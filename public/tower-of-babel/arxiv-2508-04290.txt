Analysis on a generalized two-component Novikov system
Yonghui Zhou, Xiaowan Li, Shuguan Ji, Zhijun Qiao

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for a generalized two-component Novikov system with weak dissipation. We first establish the local well-posedness of solutions by using the Kato’s theorem. Then we give the necessary and sufficient condition for the occurrence of wave breaking in a finite time. Finally, we investigate the persistence properties of strong solutions in the weighted L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) spaces for a large class of moderate weights.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for a generalized two-component Novikov system with weak dissipation

\begin{cases}u_{t}-u_{txx}+\left(g(u)\right)_{x}+\lambda(u-u_{xx})=k\left(3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}\right)+k\rho(u\rho)_{x},\\
\rho_{t}+ku^{2}\rho_{x}+k\rho uu_{x}=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with the initial data

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ \rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),

(1.2)

where
g(u)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),g(0)=0

For

\begin{cases}u_{t}-u_{txx}+4u^{2}u_{x}=3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}+\rho(u\rho)_{x},\\
\rho_{t}+u^{2}\rho_{x}+\rho uu_{x}=0.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Such a model was first proposed by Popowicz
Popowicz2015
, in which the author verified that system (
1.3

\rho=0

u_{t}-u_{txx}+4u^{2}u_{x}=3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}

equation. It is worth mentioning that (
1.4

Hone2009
, complete integrability, an infinite sequence of conserved quantities, and peaked solitons (peakons)
Hone2008
. The initial impetus for investigating the Novikov equation is that it can be regarded as a generalization of the celebrated Camassa-Holm (CH) equation

u_{t}-u_{txx}+3uu_{x}=3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}.

(1.5)

Such model is a integrable equation which describes the unidirectional propagation of shallow water waves over a flat bottom. The CH equation was implied in Fokas and Fuchssteiner
Fokas1981
as a special member in the hierarchy of evolution equation determined through the recursion operator scheme and bi-Hamiltonian structures and became famous due to the shallow water wave regime and peakon feature propsoed by Camassa and Holm
Camassa1993
from physical principles.
The significant differences between the CH equation and the Novikov equation are: 1) the former has quadratic nonlinearity while the latter has cubic nonlinearity; 2 ) Lax pair for the former is 2 by 2 while the latter is 3 by 3; and 3) the
N
-peakon dynamical system is completely integrable for the former, but not sure yet for the latter.

For
\rho\equiv 0

u_{t}-u_{txx}+\left(g(u)\right)_{x}+\lambda(u-u_{xx})=k\left(3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}\right).

(1.6)

u_{t}-u_{txx}+4u^{2}u_{x}+\lambda(u-u_{xx})=3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}.

(1.7)

Local well-posedness

In this section, we prove the local well-posedness result by using Kato’s theorem.
For convenience, we state Kato’s theorem in the form suitable for our purpose.
Consider the abstract quasilinear evolution equation of the form

\frac{dz}{dt}+A(z)z=f(z),\ t>0,

(2.1)

with the initial datum
z(0)=z_{0}.

Assume that:

(i)

A(y)\in L(Y,X)
for
y\in Y
with

\|(A(y)-A(z))w\|_{X}\leq\rho_{1}\|y-z\|_{X}\|w\|_{Y},\ y,z,w\in Y,

(i.e.,

is quasi-
m

(ii)

QA(y)Q^{-1}=A(y)+B(y)
, where

is bounded, uniformly on bounded sets in
Y
, and
Q
is an isomorphism from
Y
onto
X
. Moreover,

\|(B(y)-B(z))w\|_{X}\leq\rho_{2}\|y-z\|_{Y}\|w\|_{X},\ y,z\in Y,w\in X;

(iii)

is bounded on any bounded sets in

and also extends to a mapping from
X
onto

(Kato’s theorem

\displaystyle f(z):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}^{3}-u_{x}\rho^{2}\right)-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(g(u)+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}-\frac{k}{2}u\rho^{2}\right)-\lambda u\\

Lemma 2.5
.

The operator
A(z):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}ku^{2}\partial_{x}&0\\

Proof.

Since

is a Hilbert space, we have

if and only if the following conditions hold

(a)
\left(A(z)v,v\right)_{0}\geq-\beta\|v\|_{0}^{2};

(b) The range of
\alpha I+A

\alpha>\beta

First, we prove (a). Since
z:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
\rho\end{array}\right)\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>3/2)
, it follows that

Then

\displaystyle\left|\left(A(z)v,v\right)_{0\times 0}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(ku^{2}\partial_{x}v_{1},v_{1})_{0}+(ku^{2}\partial_{x}v_{2},v_{2})_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|k|\left(|(uu_{x}v_{1},v_{1})_{0}|+|(uu_{x}v_{2},v_{2})_{0}|\right)

Second, we prove (b). Because

is a closed operator and satisfies (a),

has closed range in

Given

\displaystyle|k|\left\|\left(u+v_{1}\right)\left(u-v_{1}\right)\right\|_{s-1}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{s}+|k|\left\|\left(u+v_{1}\right)\left(u-v_{1}\right)\right\|_{s-2}\left\|w_{2}\right\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|k|\left(\|u\|_{s-1}+\|v\|_{s-1}\right)\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s-1}\left\|w_{1}\right\|_{s}+|k|\left(\|u\|_{s-2}+\|v\|_{s-2}\right)\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s-2}\left\|w_{2}\right\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\|z-v\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)}\|w\|_{s\times(s-1)},

where
\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(\max\{|k|,\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)},\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}\})
. Here we used Lemma
2.2
with
r=s-1,t=s-2
. Taking
v=0
in the above inequality, we obtain that
A(z)\in L(H^{s}\times H^{s-1},H^{s-1}\times H^{s-2})
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.7
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
B(z)=QA(z)Q^{-1}-A(z)
with

. Then

and

Proof.

Let
z,v\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
and
w\in H^{s-1}\times H^{s-2}
. Note that

Then we have

\displaystyle\|(B(z)-B(v))w\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda^{s-1}\left[\Lambda,k\left(u^{2}-v_{1}^{2}\right)\partial_{x}\right]\Lambda^{-1}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\Lambda^{s-2}\left[\Lambda,k\left(u^{2}-v_{1}^{2}\right)\partial_{x}\right]\Lambda^{-1}w_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda^{s-1}\left[\Lambda,k\left(u^{2}-v_{1}^{2}\right)\right]\Lambda^{1-s}\right\|_{L(L^{2})}\left\|\Lambda^{s-2}\partial_{x}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\Lambda^{s-2}\left[\Lambda,k\left(u^{2}-v_{1}^{2}\right)\Lambda^{2-s}\right\|_{L(L^{2})}\|\Lambda^{s-3}\partial_{x}w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\|z-v\|_{s\times(s-1)}\|w\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)},

where
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(\max\{|k|,\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)},\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}\})
. Here we applied Lemma
2.2
with
r=1-s,k=s-1
and

. Taking

in the above inequality, we obtain

2.8
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
z\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1},s>\frac{3}{2},
and

\displaystyle f(z):=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}^{3}-u_{x}\rho^{2}\right)-\partial x\Lambda^{-2}\left(g_{(u)}+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}-\frac{k}{2}u\rho^{2}\right)-\lambda u\\
-k\rho uu_{x}\end{array}\right).

Then
f(z)
is uniformly bounded on any bounded sets in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
, and satisfies the following two conditions:

(a)
\|f(z)-f(v)\|_{s\times(s-1)}\leq\mu_{3}\|z-v\|_{s\times(s-1)},\ z,v\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1};

(b)
\|f(z)-f(v)\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)}\leq\mu_{4}\|z-v\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)},\ z,v\in H^{s-1}\times H^{s-2}.

Proof.

First, we prove (a). Let
z,v\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>\frac{3}{2})
. Note that
H^{s-1}
is a Banach algebra, then we have

\displaystyle\|f(z)-f(v)\|_{s\times(s-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|-\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left[\left(u_{x}^{3}-v_{1x}^{3}\right)-\left(u_{x}\rho^{2}-v_{1x}v_{2}^{2}\right)\right]-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}[(g(u)-g(v))+\frac{3k}{2}\left(uu_{x}^{2}-v_{1}v_{1x}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{3}\left(u^{3}-v_{1}^{3}\right)+\frac{k}{2}\left(u\rho^{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{2}\right)-\lambda(u-v_{1})\Big\|_{s}+|k|\|\rho uu_{x}-v_{1}v_{1x}v_{2}\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|k|}{2}\left\|u_{x}^{3}-v_{1x}^{3}\right\|_{s-2}+\frac{|k|}{2}\left\|u_{x}\rho^{2}-v_{1x}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}+\|g(u)-g(v)\|_{s-1}+\frac{3|k|}{2}\left\|uu_{x}^{2}-v_{1}v_{1x}^{2}\right\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{|k|}{3}\left\|u^{3}-v_{1}^{3}\right\|_{s-1}+\frac{|k|}{2}\left\|u\rho^{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-1}+\lambda\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s-1}+|k|\left\|\rho uu_{x}-v_{1}v_{1x}v_{2}\right\|_{s-1}.

(2.3)

In what follows, we will estimate the terms for the right side of (
2.3
).
Note that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{x}^{3}-v_{1x}^{3}\right\|_{s-2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|u_{x}-v_{1x}\right\|_{s-2}\left\|u_{x}^{2}+u_{x}v_{1x}+v_{1x}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{s}^{2}\right)\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}\right)\left\|z-v\right\|_{s\times(s-1)},

\displaystyle\left\|u_{x}\rho^{2}-v_{1x}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|u_{x}\rho^{2}-u_{x}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}+\left\|u_{x}v_{2}^{2}-v_{1x}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}\|_{s-2}\left\|\rho^{2}-v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}+C\left\|u_{x}-v_{1x}\right\|_{s-2}\left\|v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s-1}\left\|\left(\rho+v_{2}\right)\left(\rho-v_{2}\right)\right\|_{s-2}+C\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s-1}\left\|v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s-1}\left(\|\rho\|_{s-2}+\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{s-2}\right)\left\|\rho-v_{2}\right\|_{s-2}+C\|v\|_{s}^{2}\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}\left\|\rho-v_{2}\right\|_{s-1}\right)+C\|v\|_{s}^{2}\left\|u-v_{1}\right\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}\right)\|z-v\|_{s\times(s-1)}.

Similarly, we can estimate

\displaystyle\left\|u^{3}-v_{1}^{3}\right\|_{s-1}\leq C\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}\right)\left\|z-v\right\|_{s\times(s-1)},

\displaystyle\left\|uu_{x}^{2}-v_{1}v_{1x}^{2}\right\|_{s-1}\leq C\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}\right)\left\|z-v\right\|_{s\times(s-1)},

\displaystyle\left\|u\rho^{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{2}\right\|_{s-1}\leq C\left(\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}^{2}\right)\left\|z-v\right\|_{s\times(s-1)}.

Meanwhile, from Lemma
2.5
, we know that
u\mapsto g(u)
is a
C^{1}
-map from
H^{s-1}
to
H^{s-1}
. By using the mean value theorem, we can infer that there is some
M>0
, depending only on
\max\{\|u\|_{s},\|v_{1}\|_{s}\}
such that

\|g(u)-g(v_{1})\|_{s-1}\leq M\|u-v_{1}\|_{s-1}\leq M\|u-v_{1}\|_{s}\leq M\|z-v\|_{s\times(s-1)}.

Therfore, we get

\|f(z)-f(v)\|_{s\times(s-1)}\leq\mu_{3}\|z-v\|_{s\times(s-1)},

where
\mu_{3}=\mu_{3}(\max\{\|z\|_{s\times(s-1)},\|v\|_{s\times(s-1)}\})
.
Take
v=0
in the above inequality, we obtain that
f(z)
is bounded on bound set in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
.

Second, similar to the above process, we can get

\|f(z)-f(v)\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)}\leq\mu_{4}\|z-v\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)},

where
\mu_{4}=\mu_{4}(\max\{\|z\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)},\|v\|_{(s-1)\times(s-2)}\})
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
2.9
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
z_{0}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{0}\\
\rho_{0}\end{array}\right)\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>3/2)
, and
T=T(\lambda,z_{0})>0
be the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
z:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
\rho\end{array}\right)
to (
1.1
). Then for all
t\in[0,T)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})(t,x)dx=e^{-2\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{0}^{2}+u_{0x}^{2})dx,\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(t,x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}^{2}dx.

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation in (
1.1
) by
2u
, we get

2uu_{t}-2uu_{txx}+2u\left(g(u)\right)_{x}+2\lambda(u^{2}-uu_{xx})=2k\left(3u^{2}u_{x}u_{xx}+u^{3}u_{xxx}\right)+2ku\rho(u\rho)_{x}.

(3.1)

Integrating (
3.1
) with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})dx+2\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})dx=0,

(3.2)

here we used the relations that

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{txx}dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}u_{tx}dx,\ 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}_{x}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{3}u_{xxx}dx

\displaystyle=-3\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}u_{x}u_{xx}dx,\ 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(g(u))_{x}dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}G^{\prime}(u(x))dx,

where
G(u)=\int_{0}^{u}g(v)dv
.

Integrating (
3.2
) with respect to
t
over
[0,t]
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})(t,x)dx=e^{-2\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{0}^{2}+u_{0x}^{2})dx.

On the other hand, multiplying the second equation in (
1.1
) by
2\rho
, we get

2\rho\rho_{t}=-2k\rho\rho_{x}u^{2}-2k\rho^{2}uu_{x}.

Integrating it with respect to
t
and
x
over
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(t,x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}^{2}dx.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

In what following, we will derive the necessary and sufficient condition for occurrence of wave breaking. To this end, we can rewrite system (

In what follows, we present the some lemmas which are crucial in the proof of wave breaking mechanism.

Lemma 3.2

(
Kato1988
)
.

If
r>0
, then
H^{r}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is an algebra. Moreover

\|fg\|_{r}\leq c\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{r}+\|f\|_{r}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right),

where
c
is a constant depending only on
r
.

Lemma 3.3

(
Kato1988
)
.

If
r>0
, then

\left\|\left[\Lambda^{r},f\right]g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq c\left(\left\|\partial_{x}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\Lambda^{r-1}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|\Lambda^{r}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right),

where
c
is a constant depending only on
r
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
z_{0}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{0}\\
\rho_{0}\end{array}\right)\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>3/2)
be given. And assume that
T
is the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
\rho\end{array}\right)
to (
2.2
) with the initial data
z_{0}
. If there exists
M>0
, such that

\|u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,\ \ t\in[0,T),

then the
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
-norm of
z(t,\cdot)
does not blow up on
[0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
z
be the unique solution of (
2.2
) with initial data
z_{0}\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>3/2)
, and
T
be the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
z
, which is guaranteed by Theorem
2.2
.

Applying the operator
\Lambda^{s}
to the first equation in (
2.2
), multiplying by
2\Lambda^{s}u
and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}(u,u)_{s}=-2k\left(u^{2}u_{x},u\right)_{s}+2\left(f_{1}(u),u\right)_{s}+2(f_{2}(u,\rho),u)_{s},

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle f_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}^{3}\right)-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(g(u)+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}\right)-\lambda u,

\displaystyle f_{2}(u,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}\rho^{2}\right)+\frac{k}{2}\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u\rho^{2}\right).

In what follows, we estimate the right side of (
3.4
). If there exists a positive constant
M>0
such that

\|u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,\ \ t\in[0,T),

then we have

\displaystyle|(u^{2}u_{x},u)_{s}|=

\displaystyle|(\Lambda^{s}(u^{2}u_{x}),\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left([\Lambda^{s},u^{2}]u_{x},\Lambda^{s}u\right)_{0}+\left(u^{2}\Lambda^{s}u_{x},\Lambda^{s}u\right)_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|[\Lambda^{s},u^{2}]u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|uu_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|(u^{2})_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}u^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u\|_{s}+cM\|u\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|uu_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{s}+M\|u^{2}\|_{s}\right)\|u\|_{s}+cM\|u\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}^{2}.

(3.5)

Similarly, note that
H^{s}(s>\frac{1}{2})
is a Banach algebra, it follows that

\displaystyle(f_{1}(u),u)_{s}=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{k}{2}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}^{3}\right)-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(g(u)+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}\right)-\lambda u,u\right)_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}\left(\left\|\Lambda^{-2}\left(u_{x}^{3}\right)\right\|_{s}+\left\|\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(g(u)+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}\right)\right\|_{s}+\lambda\|u\|_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}\left(\|u_{x}^{3}\|_{s-2}+\left\|g(u)\right\|_{s-1}+\left\|uu_{x}^{2}\right\|_{s-1}+\left\|u^{3}\right\|_{s-1}+\lambda\|u\|_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}\left(\|u_{x}^{3}\|_{s-2}+\widetilde{g}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\left\|u\right\|_{s-1}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{s-1}+\left\|u^{3}\right\|_{s-1}+\lambda\|u\|_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}\left(\|u_{x}^{3}\|_{s-2}+\widetilde{g}(\|u_{0}\|_{1})\left\|u\right\|_{s}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{s-1}+\left\|u^{3}\right\|_{s-1}+\lambda\|u\|_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C+\lambda)\|u\|_{s}^{2},

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle|(f_{2}(u,\rho),u)_{s}|\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(u,\rho)\|_{s}\|u\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}\rho^{2})\|_{s}+\|\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u\rho^{2})\|_{s})\|u\|_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\|u_{x}\rho^{2}\|_{s-2}\|u\|_{s}+\|u\rho^{2}\|_{s-1}\|u\|_{s})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{s-1}\|u\|_{s}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{s-1}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{s})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(CM^{2}+M\|u_{0}\|_{1})(\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|u\|_{s}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}),

(3.7)

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\|uu_{x}^{2}\|_{s-1}=

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s-1},u]u_{x}^{2}+u\Lambda^{s-1}u_{x}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}u_{x}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s-1}u^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{s-1}+M^{2}\|u^{2}\|_{s-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{x}\|_{s-1}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{s-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{s}.

Combining (
3.4
)–(
3.7
), we get

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{s}^{2}\leq(C+\lambda)(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}).

(3.8)

In order to derive a similar estimate for the second component
\rho
, we apply the operator
\Lambda^{s-1}
to the second equation in (
2.2
), multiply by
2\Lambda^{s-1}\rho
and integrate the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}
, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}=-2k(u^{2}\rho_{x},\rho)_{s-1}-2k(\rho uu_{x},\rho)_{s-1}.

(3.9)

In what follows, we estimate the right side of (
3.9
).

\displaystyle(u^{2}\rho_{x},\rho)_{s-1}

\displaystyle=|(\Lambda^{s-1}(u^{2}\rho_{x}),\Lambda^{s-1}\rho)_{0}|

\displaystyle=\left|\left([\Lambda^{s-1},u^{2}]\rho_{x},\Lambda^{s-1}\rho\right)_{0}+\left(u^{2}\Lambda^{s-1}\rho_{x},\Lambda^{s-1}\rho\right)_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|[\Lambda^{s-1},u^{2}]\rho_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|uu_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|uu_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{s-1}+\|\rho_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{s-1})\|\rho\|_{s-1}+M\|u_{0}\|_{1}\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}

\displaystyle<C(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}),

(3.10)

\|(\rho uu_{x},\rho)\|_{s-1}\leq\|\rho uu_{x}\|_{s-1}\|\rho\|_{s-1}\leq\|uu_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}\\
\leq C(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}).

(3.11)

Combining (
3.9
)–(
3.11
), we get

\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}).

(3.12)

Therefore, we get

\frac{d}{dt}(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2})\leq(C+\lambda)(\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}).

(3.13)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\|u\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq e^{(C+\lambda)t}(\|u_{0}\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{s-1}^{2}).

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

to (
2.2
). Applying the classical results in the theory of ODEs, we can obtain the following results on
q
which is crucial in the proof of blowup scenarios.

Similar to Lemma 3.7 in
Wu2012
, we can give the following lemma but omit the detailed proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in C([0,T);H^{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T);H^{s-1}),s>3/2
. Then (
3.14
) has a unique solution
q(t,x)\in C^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, and
q(t,\cdot)
is an increasing diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
with

q_{x}(t,x)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}2ku(\tau,q(\tau,x))u_{x}(\tau,q(\tau,x))d\tau}>0,\ (t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
z_{0}\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}(s>\frac{3}{2})
, and
T>0
be the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
\rho\end{array}\right)
of system (
2.2
).
If there exists
N>0
such that
kuu_{x}(t,x)\geq-N
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
, then

\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\rho(t,q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{NT}\|\rho_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

By (
3.14
) and (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho(t,q(t,x))=\rho_{t}+\rho_{x}q_{t}=\rho_{t}+ku^{2}\rho_{x}=-k\rho uu_{x},

which leads to

\rho(t,q(t,x))=\rho_{0}e^{-k\int_{0}^{t}uu_{x}(\tau,q(\tau,x))d\tau}.

If there exists a positive constant
N>0
such that
kuu_{x}\geq-N
, then we get

\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\rho(t,q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\rho_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}e^{NT}<\infty.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
z_{0}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{0}\\
\rho_{0}\end{array}\right)\in H^{2}\times H^{1}
be given, and
T
be the maximal existence time of the corresponding solution
z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
\rho\end{array}\right)
of (
2.2
) with the initial data
z_{0}
.
Then
T
is finite if and only if

\liminf_{t\rightarrow T}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}{kuu_{x}}(t,x)=-\infty.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, for any initial data
z_{0}\in H^{2}\times H^{1}
which corresponding
s=2
, there exists a unique local solution
z
of system (
2.2
).
The following study will focus on investigating the necessary and sufficient conditions for the occurrence of wave breaking in finite time.

Note that
y=u-u_{xx}
, we get

\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq\|y\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u-u_{xx})^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{xx}^{2}+2u_{x}^{2})dx,

where we used the fact that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}2uu_{xx}dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}dx.

Multiplying the first equation in (
3.3
) by
2y
, we get

2yy_{t}+2ku^{2}yy_{x}+6kuu_{x}y^{2}+2ky\rho(u\rho)_{x}=-2\left(g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right)_{x}y-2\lambda y^{2}.

(3.15)

Integrating (
3.15
) with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}dx=

\displaystyle-4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{x}y^{2}dx-2\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right)_{x}ydx

\displaystyle+2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}u_{xx}\rho^{2}dx+2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}\rho\rho_{x}dx.

Multiplying the second equation in (
3.3
) by
2\rho
, we get

2\rho\rho_{t}+2ku^{2}\rho\rho_{x}=-2k\rho^{2}uu_{x}.

(3.16)

Integrating (
3.16
) with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}dx=0.

Differentiating the second equation in (
3.3
) with respect to
x
, we get

\rho_{tx}+3kuu_{x}\rho_{x}+ku^{2}\rho_{xx}+k\rho u_{x}^{2}+kuu_{xx}\rho=0.

(3.17)

Multiplying (
3.17
) by
2\rho_{x}
, we get

2\rho_{x}\rho_{tx}+6kuu_{x}\rho_{x}^{2}+2ku^{2}\rho_{x}\rho_{xx}+2k\rho\rho_{x}u_{x}^{2}+2kuu_{xx}\rho\rho_{x}=0.

(3.18)

Integrating (
3.18
) with respect to
x
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{x}^{2}dx=

\displaystyle-4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{x}\rho_{x}^{2}dx-2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}_{x}\rho\rho_{x}dx-2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}\rho\rho_{x}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{x}\rho_{x}^{2}dx+2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}u_{xx}\rho^{2}dx-2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}\rho\rho_{x}dx.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho_{x}^{2})dx=

\displaystyle-4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{x}y^{2}dx-2\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right)_{x}ydx

\displaystyle+4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}u_{xx}\rho^{2}dx-4k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{x}\rho_{x}^{2}dx.

Note that

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right)_{x}ydx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right)_{x}^{2}+y^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|g(u)-\frac{4k}{3}u^{3}\right\|_{1}^{2}+\|y\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\|y\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|y\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
represent specific constants.

If
kuu_{x}(t,x)
is bounded from below on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
, i.e., there exists
N>0
such that
kuu_{x}(t,x)>-N
on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho_{x}^{2})dx\leq

\displaystyle(4N+2\lambda+4|k|e^{NT}\|\rho_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+c_{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho_{x}^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C+2\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho_{x}^{2})dx.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho_{x}^{2})dx\leq e^{\left(C+2\lambda\right)t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y_{0}^{2}+\rho_{0}^{2}+\rho_{0x}^{2})dx.

It then follows from the Sobolev embedding theorem
H^{2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, we get

\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{1}^{2}\leq\|y\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{1}^{2}\leq(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{1}^{2})e^{\left(C+2\lambda\right)t}.

By using Theorem
3.1
, we deduce that the solution exists globally in time.

On the other hand, if
kuu_{x}(t,x)
is unbounded from below, by applying Theorem
3.1
and using the Sobolev embedding theorem
H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
(
s>\frac{1}{2})
, we infer that the solution will blow up in finite time.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

4
Persistence property

In this section, we mainly discuss the persistence property for a weakly dissipative generalized 2-component Novikov system.
To this end, we reformulate system (
2.2
) as follows

\begin{cases}u_{t}+ku^{2}u_{x}=-P\ast A(u,\rho)-\partial_{x}P\ast B(u,\rho)-\lambda u,\\
\rho_{t}+ku^{2}\rho_{x}=-k\rho uu_{x},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
A(u,\rho)=\frac{k}{2}\left(u_{x}^{3}-u_{x}\rho^{2}\right)
and
B(u,\rho)=g(u)+\frac{3k}{2}uu_{x}^{2}-\frac{k}{3}u^{3}-\frac{k}{2}u\rho^{2}
.

Before giving the theorem on the persistence of solutions, we first introduce the definition for admissible weight function.

Definition 4.1

(
Brandolese2012
)
.

An admissible weighted function for system (
4.1
) is locally absolutely function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for some
\theta>0
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq\theta|\phi(x)|
, and that is f-moderate for some sub-multiplicative weight function
f
satisfying

\inf_{\mathbb{R}}f>0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(x)}{e^{|x|}}\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

In what follows, we state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
2\leq p\leq\infty.
Let
(u,\rho)\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T),H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}))
be a strong solution of system (
4.1
) such that
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
satisfies
u_{0}\psi,u_{0x}\psi
and
\rho_{0}\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),
where
\psi
is an admissible weighted function for system (
4.1
). Then, for all
t\in[0,T)
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on the weight function
\psi
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{x}(t)\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho(t)\psi\|_{L^{p}}\leq e^{(CM^{2}+\lambda)t}(\|u_{0}\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{0x}\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho_{0}\psi\|_{L^{p}}),

(4.2)

where

M\equiv\sup_{t\in[0,T)}(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}})<\infty.

The fundamental class of instances for admissible weighted functions is provided by the family of functions.

\psi_{a,b,c,d}=e^{a|x|^{b}}(1+|x|^{c})(\log(e+|x|)^{d}),

where
\psi
must satisfy the following conditions

a\geq 0,\ \ 0\leq b\leq 1,\ \ ab<1,\ \ c,d\in\mathbb{R}.

Definition 4.2

(
Brandolese2012
)
.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
called sub-multiplicative if

f(x+y)\leq f(x)f(y),\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Given a non-negative sub-multiplicative function
f
, a function
\psi
is called
f
-moderate if and only if

\exists C_{0}>0:\psi(x+y)\leq C_{0}f(x)\psi(y),

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. A function
\psi
is called moderate if and only if there exists a nonnegative sub-multiplicative function
f
such that
\psi
is
f
-moderate.

Let us recall the most standard examples:

\psi_{a,b,c,d}(x)=e^{a|x|^{b}}(1+|x|^{c})\left(\log(e+|x|)^{d}\right).

We have the following conditions (see
Brandolese2012
):

(i)

For
a,c,d>0
and
0\leq b\leq 1
, the function
\phi_{a,b,c,d}(x)
is sub-multiplicative.

(ii)

If
a,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq b\leq 1
, then
\phi_{a,b,c,d}(x)
is moderate, that is
\phi_{a,b,c,d}
is
\psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta}
-moderate for
|a|\leq\alpha,|b|\leq\beta,|c|\leq\gamma,|d|\leq\delta
.

Proof of Theorem 4.1.
We first introduce a auxiliary function as follows

\psi_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}\psi(x),\ \ \ \psi(x)\leq N,\\
N,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \psi(x)>N,\end{cases}

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
N
, we have

\|\psi_{N}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq N,\ \ 0<|\psi_{N}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\theta|\psi_{N}(x)|\ \ \text{and}\ \ \psi_{N}(x+y)\leq C_{1}f(x)\psi_{N}(y),

where
C_{1}=\max\{C_{0},\alpha^{-1}\},C_{0}
is given in Definition 4.2,
\alpha=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x)>0.

In what follows, we consider the case
2\leq p<\infty
. Multiplying both sides of the first equation of system (
4.1
) by
\psi_{N}|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})
and integrating the resulting equation with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{t}\psi_{N})|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx+k\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu_{x})|u\psi_{N}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P\ast A(u,\rho))|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{x}\ast B(u,\rho))|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u\psi_{N}|^{p}dx.

(4.3)

Noticing that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{t}\psi_{N})|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx=\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}},

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu_{x})|u\psi_{N}|^{p}dx\leq\|uu_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq M^{2}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P\ast A(u,\rho))|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\|\psi_{N}\cdot P\ast A(u,\rho)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\|P\ast f\|_{1}\left\|\psi_{N}\left(\frac{k}{2}(u_{x}^{3}-u_{x}\rho^{2})\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}M^{2}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\left(\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(4.6)

Similarly, we can estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{x}\ast B(u,\rho))|u\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}M^{2}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\left(\left\|u\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u\psi_{N}|^{p}dx=\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.8)

where
C_{2}-C_{4}
independent of
N
,
M=\sup_{t\in[0,T)}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}})<\infty.

Combining (
4.3
)-(
4.8
), we get

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}\leq(C_{5}M^{2}+\lambda)\left(\left\|u\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(4.9)

In what follows, we will give estimates on
u_{x}\psi_{N}
. Differentiating first equation of system (
4.1
) with respect to
x
, we get

u_{tx}+ku^{2}u_{xx}+2kuu_{x}^{2}=-P_{x}\ast A(u,\rho)-P_{xx}\ast B(u,\rho)-\lambda u_{x}.

(4.10)

Multiplying (
4.10
) with
\psi_{N}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})
and integrating the resulting equation with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}(u_{x}\psi_{N})|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx+k\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}u_{xx}\psi_{N}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx

\displaystyle+2k\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu^{2}_{x}\psi_{N}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{x}\ast A(u,\rho))|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{xx}\ast B(u,\rho))|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p}dx.

(4.11)

In what follows, we estimate the terms in(
4.11
).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}(u_{x}\psi_{N})|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx=\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}u_{xx}\psi_{N}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{x}(u_{x}\psi_{N})-u_{x}\psi_{Nx}\right)u^{2}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\left(\partial_{x}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p}\right)|dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})u^{2}u_{x}\psi_{Nx}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2M^{2}}{p}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\theta M^{2}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.13)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}uu^{2}_{x}\psi_{N}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx\right|\leq 2M^{2}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{x}\ast A(u,\rho))|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\|\psi_{N}\cdot P_{x}\ast A(u,\rho)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}M^{2}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\left(\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{N}\cdot P_{xx}\ast B(u,\rho))|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(u_{x}\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}M^{2}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\left(\left\|u\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(4.16)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{x}\psi_{N}|^{p}dx=\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.17)

Combining (
4.11
)-(
4.17
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}\leq(C_{8}M^{2}+\lambda)\left(\left\|u\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(4.18)

In what follows, we multiply both sides of the second equation of (
4.1
) by
\psi_{N}|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})
and integrating the resulting equation with respect to
x
over
\mathbb{R}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{t}\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx+k\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}\rho_{x}\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu_{x}\rho\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx.

(4.19)

In what follows, we estimate the terms in(
4.19
).

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{t}\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx=\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}\rho_{x}\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}(\partial_{x}(\rho\psi_{N})-\rho\psi_{Nx})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{2}{p}uu_{x}|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}\rho\psi_{Nx})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2M^{2}}{p}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\theta M^{2}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\theta)M^{2}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p},

(4.21)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu_{x}\rho\psi_{N})|\rho\psi_{N}|^{p-2}(\rho\psi_{N})dx\right|\leq M^{2}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

(4.22)

From (
4.19
)-(
4.22
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{9}M^{2}\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}.

(4.23)

Combining (
4.9
), (
4.18
) and (
4.23
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}\right)\leq(CM^{2}+\lambda)\left(\left\|u\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\rho\psi_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}}\right),

(4.24)

where
C
is a constant only depending on
\theta
and
f
.

By using Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\|u\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}\leq e^{(CM^{2}+\lambda)t}(\|u_{0}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{0x}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho_{0}\psi_{N}\|_{L^{p}}).

(4.25)

Taking advantage of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and taking the limit as
N
goes to
\infty
, we get

\|u\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{x}\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho\psi\|_{L^{p}}\leq e^{(CM^{2}+\lambda)t}(\|u_{0}\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{0x}\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\rho_{0}\psi\|_{L^{p}}).

(4.26)

It is obvious that the theorem also applies for
p=\infty
, since
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

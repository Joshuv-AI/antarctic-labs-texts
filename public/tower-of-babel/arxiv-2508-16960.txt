Construction of blow-up solution with minimal mass for 2D cubic Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation
Yang Lan, Xu Yuan

Abstract
. In this article, we construct a minimal mass blow-up solution of the two-dimensional cubic (mass-critical) Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation: ∂ t ϕ + ∂ x 1 ( Δ ​ ϕ + ϕ 3 ) = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ 2 . \partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x_{1}}(\Delta\phi+\phi^{3})=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}. Let s > 3 4 s>\frac{3}{4} . Bhattacharya-Farah-Roudenko [ 2 ] show that H s H^{s} solutions with ‖ ϕ ‖ L 2 < ‖ Q ‖ L 2 \|\phi\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}} are global in time. For such low regularity solutions, we study the dynamics at the threshold ‖ ϕ ‖ L 2 = ‖ Q ‖ L 2 \|\phi\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}} and demonstrate that finite time blow-up singularity formation may occur. This result and its proof are inspired by the recent blow-up result [ 29 ] for the mass-critical gKdV equation. This result is also complement of previous result [ 6 ] for nonexistence of minimal mass blow-up element in the energy space H 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) of the two-dimensional cubic Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

In this article, we consider the minimal mass dynamics for
the 2D cubic Zakharov–Kuznetsov (ZK) equation,

\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x_{1}}(\Delta\phi+\phi^{3})=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.1)

Here,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Delta=\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}
is the Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
(see
[
14
,
21
,
23
]
). More precisely, for any initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
, there exists a unique (in a certain class) maximal solution
\phi
of (
1.1
) in

C\left([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right)
. For this Cauchy problem, the following blow-up criterion holds:

T<\infty\Longrightarrow\||\nabla|^{s}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty\quad\mbox{as}\ t\uparrow T.

(1.2)

Recall that, if
\phi
is a solution of (
1.1
) then for any
\lambda>0
, the function

\phi_{\lambda}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{t}{\lambda^{3}},\frac{x}{\lambda}\right),\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

is also a solution of (
1.1
). This scaling symmetry keeps the
L^{2}
-norm invariant so that the problem is
mass-critical
.
Recall also that, for any solution
\phi
of (
1.1
), the mass
M(\phi)
and the energy
E(\phi)
are formally conserved, where

M(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi^{2}{\rm d}x\quad\mbox{and}\quad E(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi^{4}{\rm d}x.

We recall the family of solitary wave solutions of (
1.1
). Let
Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the unique radial positive solution of the equation

-\Delta Q+Q-Q^{3}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Then, for any
(\lambda_{0},x_{10},x_{20})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the function

\phi(t,x)=\lambda_{0}^{-1}Q\left(\lambda_{0}^{-1}\left(x_{1}-\lambda_{0}^{-2}t-x_{10}\right),\lambda_{0}^{-1}\left(x_{2}-x_{20}\right)\right),

is a solution of (
1.1
) called
solitary wave
or
soliton
. It is well-known that
E(Q)=0
and that
Q
is related to the following
sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
41
]
)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x)|^{4}{\rm d}x\leq 2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|Q(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x}\right),\quad\mbox{for any}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Following the above inequality, the conservation of energy and the blow-up criterion (
1.2
) imply that any initial data
\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with subcritical mass,
i.e.
satisfying
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, generates a
global and bounded
solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

To go beyond the threshold mass
\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, it is natural to restrict to solutions with small supercritical mass,
i.e.
satisfying

\|Q\|_{L^{2}}<\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<(1+\delta)\|Q\|_{L^{2}},\quad\mbox{for}\ \ 0<\delta\ll 1.

(1.3)

Based on a rigidity property (see
[
18
, Proposition 1.3]
) of the ZK flow around the family of soliton, the first proof for the existence of blow-up solutions with small supercritical mass was given in Farah-Holmer-Roudenko-Yang
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
. More precisely, they showed that, for any negative energy initial data
\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying (
1.3
), the corresponding solution
\phi(t)
blows up in finite/infinite forward time. Then, Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
5
]
revisited the blow-up analysis for the 2D cubic ZK equation in light of previous developments related to the blow-up of the mass-critical generalized Korteweg-de Vries (gKdV) equation (see §
1.2
for more discussion).

At the threshold mass
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
6
]
showed that there exists no
minimal mass blow-up
solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for (
1.1
). Here, we say that
\phi(t)
is a minimal mass blow-up solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for (
1.1
) if
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
and there exists
0<T\leq\infty
such that

\phi(t)\in C([0,T);H^{1})\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\uparrow T.

In the case of low regularity, Linares-Pastor
[
24
]
proved the global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>\frac{53}{63}
, under the condition of subcritical mass for initial data. Later on, Bhattacharya-Farah-Roudenko
[
2
]
used the
I
-method and obtained such global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>\frac{3}{4}
.
In this article, we show that the critical mass condition is sharp by constructing a minimal mass blow-up solution in
H^{\frac{7}{9}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
i.e.
a solution of (
1.1
) which blows up in finite time in
H^{\frac{7}{9}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the threshold mass
\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
. Our main result is formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist
T_{0}>0
and a solution
\mathcal{S}\in C((0,T_{0}];H^{\frac{7}{9}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
to (
1.1
) with critical mass

\|\mathcal{S}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}

such that

\mathcal{S}(t)-\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}Q\left(\frac{\cdot-x(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)\to 0,\quad\mbox{in}\ H^{\frac{7}{9}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\downarrow 0.

Here, the functions
t\mapsto\lambda(t)
and
t\mapsto x(t)
satisfy

\lambda(t)\sim t^{\frac{1}{3-\theta}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad x(t)\sim(-t^{-\frac{\theta-1}{3-\theta}},0)\quad\mbox{as}\ t\downarrow 0.

Remark 1.2
.

The constant
\theta
is defined by

\theta=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}{\rm d}\xi\bigg)\bigg/\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\bigg).

Here, we consider the following two smooth functions:

\widehat{F}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(y_{2})e^{-i\xi y_{2}}{\rm d}y_{2}\quad\mbox{where}\quad F(y_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda Q(y_{1},y_{2}){\rm d}y_{1}.

Note that, the value of
\theta
plays a crucial role in our analysis, since it determines the blow-up rate of
\mathcal{S}(t)
. By an elementary numerical computation, we find
\theta\approx 1.66\in\left(\frac{8}{5},\frac{9}{5}\right)
. We refer to
[
5
, Appendix A]
for the details of numerical computation.

Remark 1.3
.

For the mass-critical gKdV equation, which is the closest model related to (
1.1
), the minimal mass blow-up solution
\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{KdV}}}
is known to be
global
for
t\in(0,\infty)
and to be the
unique
minimal mass blow-up element (see
[
27
]
and §
1.3
for more discussion). For the ZK equation (
1.1
), such properties are open problems.

Remark 1.4
.

We expect that the index of the Sobolev space in Theorem
1.1
can be improved. Some additional analyses, which are related to the linear estimate of the ZK flow, will be needed to obtain such improvement (see §
5
for more discussion).
We also expect that the optimal index is
\frac{\theta}{2}
, which matches the difference in size between
{(\lambda,b)}
(see
[
6
, Section 1.3]
for more discussion).

1.2.
Soliton dynamics for the ZK equation

First, we briefly review the literature related to the singularity formation for the ZK models. As mentioned above, for the 2D cubic ZK equation (
1.1
), Farah-Holmer-Roudenko-Yang
[
18
]
obtained the first qualitative information on the flow for small supercritical mass initial data. In particular, they proved the existence of a finite/infinite time blow-up solution with negative energy. Then, Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
5
]
revisited the study of singularity formation and soliton dynamics for (
1.1
). More precisely, for
H^{1}
solution satisfying a suitable space decay property (see the definition of initial data set
\mathcal{A}
in
[
5
, Definition 1.1]
), Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
5
]
proved that only three possible behaviors can occur:

Exit:

The solution eventually exits any small neighborhood of the solitons.

Blow-up:

The solution blows up in finite time with blow-up rate
(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{3-\theta}}
.

Soliton:

: The solution is global and locally converges to a soliton.

In Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
6
]
, building on the tools developed
in
[
5
]
, the authors also proved the nonexistence of the minimal mass blow-up solution in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Last, we refer to Bozgan-Ghoul-Masmoudi-Yang
[
3
]
for a similar result as in
[
5
]
, which was proved independently. More importantly, the two proofs use different energy-virial Lyapunov functionals.

We now briefly review the literature related to the stability and asymptotic stability of solitons for the ZK models. Orbital stability of ZK solitons is well known since the work of de Bouard
[
11
]
.
Then, Côte-Muñoz-Pilod-Simpson
[
10
]
showed the asymptotic stability of solitons and stability of multi-solitons for the 2D quadratic ZK equation via a virial-type estimate. It is worth mentioning here that, this estimate relies on a simple sign condition which can be checked numerically. However, this numerical computation does not apply to the 3D case. Recently, Farah-Holmer-Roudenko-Yang
[
17
]
obtained a virial-type estimate for the 3D case via passage to a dual problem. This estimate relies on the numerical verification of a spectral condition with robust numerical analysis. We refer to Farah-Holmer-Roudenko
[
15
,
16
]
for the results on the instability of solitons for the 2D mass-critical and mass-supercritical problems, respectively. Last, for the 2D and 3D quadratic ZK equations, we also refer to
[
33
,
34
]
for the results on the asymptotic stability of multi-solitons and a description of the collision of two nearly equal solitary waves.

1.3.
Comparison with NLS and gKdV equations

In the last twenty years, there have been significant progress in the study of the singularity formation for the mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) and generalized Korteweg-de Vries (gKdV) equations, in particular for the description of stable blow-up dynamics and the classification of minimal mass blow-up solutions. In what follows, we will briefly survey the literature related to the minimal mass blow-up of the mass-critical NLS and gKdV equations. We mention here that, the 2D cubic ZK equation is a natural extension of the mass-critical gKdV equations in two dimensions.

Consider the following mass-critical NLS equation:

i\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi+|\phi|^{\frac{4}{N}}\phi=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Based on the structure of the NLS equation, the pseudo-conformal symmetry for this equation implies that if
\phi(t,x)
is a solution of this equation then

\Phi(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}}}\overline{\phi}\left(\frac{1}{t},\frac{x}{t}\right)e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},

is also a solution of this equation.
It is well-known (see
[
4
]
) that the pseudo-conformal symmetry generates an

explicit minimal mass blow up solution

\mathcal{S}_{\rm{NLS}}(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}-\frac{i}{t}}Q_{{\rm{NLS}}}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

defined for all
t>0
and blowing up as
t\downarrow 0
. In particular, we have

\|\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{{\rm{NLS}}}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{t}\ \mbox{as}\ t\downarrow 0.

Here,
Q_{\rm{NLS}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the unique radial ground state of the mass-critical NLS equation. Later on, using the pseudo-conformal symmetry, the pioneering work of Merle
[
30
]
showed that
\mathcal{S}_{\rm{NLS}}
is the unique (up to the symmetries) minimal mass blow-up solution of the mass-critical NLS equation in the energy
space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Recently, Dodson
[
12
,
13
]
proved the uniqueness result in the critical space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
1\leq N\leq 15
. Note that, the
existence and uniqueness
of minimal mass blow-up solution both rely on the explicit pseudo-conformal which is absent from the inhomogeneous NLS equation. The study of inhomogeneous problem was start by Merle
[
31
]
, and in particular, this work obtained a sufficient condition to ensure the
nonexistence
of minimal mass blow-up elements for the inhomogeneous problem. Then, a novel methodology to construct minimal mass blow-up elements for an inhomogeneous NLS equation was developed in Raphaël-Szeftel
[
35
]
. More precisely, they gave a necessary and sufficient condition to ensure the
existence
of minimal mass blow-up elements, and gave a complete classification in the energy space for such elements.

We also consider the following mass-critical gKdV equation:

\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\phi^{5}\right)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

First, Martel-Merle
[
26
]
obtained
the
global existence
result for minimal mass solutions with decay on the right. In other words, minimal mass blow up is not compatible with the suitable decay on the right. Then, the
existence and description
of the minimal mass blow-up solution were studied by Martel-Merle-Raphaël
[
27
]
. More precisely, we denote by
Q_{\rm{KdV}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
the unique radial ground state of the mass-critical gKdV equation. Based on the work of
[
27
]
, we know that there exists a unique solution
\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)
on
(0,\infty)
such that

\|\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{{\rm{KdV}}}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\partial_{x}\mathcal{S}_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{t}\ \mbox{as}\ t\downarrow 0.

Moreover, the sharp asymptotics, both in the time and space variables, were derived in
[
9
]
, for any order derivative of
\mathcal{S}_{\rm{KdV}}(t)
.
We refer to
[
1
,
8
,
22
,
25
,
28
,
38
,
40
]
and the references therein for the existence of minimal mass blow-up solutions for other dispersive equations with local or nonlocal structures.

Two differences with the 2D cubic ZK case are thus apparent. First, Chen-Lan-Yuan
[
6
]
showed that there exists
no finite/infinite time blow-up solution
with minimal mass in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. This is clearly different from the cases of the NLS and gKdV equations. Second, in this article, we prove the existence of a minimal mass blow-up solution in the
low-regularity Sobolev space

H^{\frac{7}{9}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
rather than in the standard energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as in the NLS and gKdV cases. Last, we refer to
[
7
,
39
]
and references therein for the study of the low regularity blow-up solution for the mass-critical NLS equation. To our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
proves the first statement of the existence of minimal mass blow-up solutions in the low-regularity Sobolev space which do not exist in standard energy space.

1.4.
A Roadmap of the proof

As usual in investigating blow-up phenomenon, we first introduce the rescaled space-time variables
(s,y)\in(-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

\frac{{\rm d}s}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}(t)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y=(y_{1},y_{2})=\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{1}(t)}{\lambda(t)},\frac{x_{2}}{\lambda(t)}\right).

The time dependent functions
(\lambda,x_{1})
are to be determined. We look for a solution
\phi
of (
1.1
) blowing up at
(t,x)=(0,0)
with
\phi(t,x)=\lambda^{-1}(s)\omega(s,y)
. From (
1.1
) and an elementary, we directly have

\partial_{s}\omega+\partial_{y_{1}}\left(\Delta\omega-\omega+\omega^{3}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda\omega-\left(\frac{x_{1s}}{\lambda}-1\right)\partial_{y_{1}}\omega=0.

(1.4)

We consider the solution of (
1.4
) close to a soliton, that is,
\omega=Q+\zeta
where
\zeta
is a small function. It follows directly from (
1.4
) that

\partial_{s}\zeta+\partial_{y_{1}}\left(\Delta\zeta-\zeta+\zeta^{3}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda\zeta-\left(\frac{x_{1s}}{\lambda}-1\right)\partial_{y_{1}}\zeta={\rm{L.O.T}}.

Recall that, in the previous work
[
5
]
, we consider
\zeta\sim bP_{b}
where
P_{b}
is a suitable localized profile, and such an approximate solution can help us obtain a stable blow-up solution around
Q
. However, in our current case, the minimal mass blow-up solution does not belong to the space
\mathcal{A}
which means that the refined control of
b
is not allowed.
Thus, we do not expect such an approximate solution to help us construct a minimal mass element with explicit asymptotic behavior.

Inspired by the previous work
[
29
]
for the study of blow-up dynamics for the gKdV equation, we consider an expansion
\zeta=\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}+\cdots
in powers of
\lambda^{\theta}
up to a sufficiently high order. More precisely, we define the first term by
\zeta_{1}\sim\lambda^{\theta}P_{\lambda}
where
P_{\lambda}
is a suitable localized profiled related to the parameters
\lambda
. To obtain an additional decisive orthogonality condition for the remainder term, the approximate solution involve an extra term
bP_{b}
, that is, we should consider
\omega\sim Q+\lambda^{\theta}P_{\lambda}+bP_{b}
. Roughly speaking, the geometrical parameters
(\lambda,b)
satisfy

\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+\lambda^{\theta}+b=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad b_{s}-b\lambda^{\theta}=0.

Note that, the above ODE admits a special blow-up solution

\lambda(s)=|s|^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,0)\Longleftrightarrow\lambda(t)=t^{\frac{1}{3-\theta}}\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ t\in(0,\infty),

which gives a finite time blow-up solution of (
1.1
).

We briefly discuss some technical aspects of the proof. First, from the above ODE argument and the energy-virial Lyapunov functional which was first introduced in
[
5
]
, we construct a sequence of approximate blow-up solutions
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
of (
1.1
) (see Section
3
–
4
for more details). More precisely, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, the solution
\phi_{n}
is well-defined on
[T_{n},T_{0}]
with
T_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and the scaling parameter satisfies
\lambda_{n}(t)\sim t^{\frac{1}{3-\theta}}
. Compared to previous works on the construction of a blow-up solution (see
e.g.

[
22
,
27
,
28
]
), a significant problem encountered when studying the ZK equation is the lack of uniform control for the
H^{1}
norm of
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
. Actually, such uniform control should not exists since the noexistence of minimal mass element in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. To overcome the difficulty, we employ some linear estimates and space-time mixed estimates to obtain a uniform control for the
H^{\frac{7}{9}}
norm of
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
(see Section
5.1
and Appendix
A
for more details). Based on such control and weak
H^{\frac{7}{9}}
stability of ZK flow, we argue by compactness and obtain the minimal mass blow-up solution
\phi
as the weak limit of the sequence of solutions
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
.

1.5.
Notation and conventions

For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we denote

|\beta|=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{y}^{\beta}=\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{y_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}}.

For any
(r,j)\in(-1,\infty)\times\{1,2\}
and regular function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote the fractional derivatives of
f
by

D_{x_{j}}^{r}f(x)=\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi_{j}|^{r}\mathcal{F}f\right)\right)(x),\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Denote by
\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the set of smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
r_{n}>0
and
C_{n}>0
such that

\sum_{|\beta|=n}|\partial_{y}^{\beta}f(y)|\leq C_{n}(1+|y|)^{r_{n}}e^{-|y|},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

Denote also by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the set of smooth functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
r_{n}>0
and
C_{n}>0
such that

\sum_{|\beta|=n}\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}f(y)\right|\leq C_{n}(1+|y|)^{r_{n}}e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we denote the
L^{2}
-scalar product by

(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)g(x){\rm d}x.

In addition, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we define the weighted norm

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{2}(y)e^{-\frac{|y|}{10}}{\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
nondecreasing function such that

\chi_{|(-\infty,-2)}\equiv 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{|(-1,\infty)}\equiv 1.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
cut-off function such that

\sigma_{|(-1,1)}\equiv 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma_{|(-\infty,-2)}\equiv\sigma_{|(2,\infty)}\equiv 0.

Let
{\textbf{e}}_{1}
be the first vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that is,
{\textbf{e}}_{1}=(1,0)
.

We denote the scaling operator by

\Lambda f=f+y\cdot\nabla f,\quad\mbox{for}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Moreover, we denote the linearized operator around
Q
by

\mathcal{L}f=-\Delta f+f-3Q^{2}f,\quad\mbox{for}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(1.5)

For all
(f_{1},f_{2})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we set

(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(y_{1},y_{2})=f_{1}(y_{1})f_{2}(y_{2}),\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Recall that, we consider the following two smooth functions:

\widehat{F}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(y_{2})e^{-i\xi y_{2}}{\rm d}y_{2}\quad\mbox{where}\quad F(y_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda Q(y_{1},y_{2}){\rm d}y_{1}.

In addition, the constant
\theta

\theta=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}{\rm d}\xi\bigg)\bigg/\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\bigg).

By using the Fast Fourier Transform in Mathematica, one has

. We refer to

) for the numerical computation related to

.

For a given small constant

\varrho(\alpha)\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\ \ \alpha\to 0.

We denote by
h_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R})
the even solution of the following second-order ODE:

-h_{2}^{\prime\prime}(y_{2})+h_{2}(y_{2})=F^{\prime\prime}(y_{2}),\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}.

(1.6)

Then, we denote

We fix a regular function

and
h_{1}
is orthogonal to

As in the previous works of Chen-Lan-Yuan

2.

In this section, we deal with the construction of a suitable blow-up profile for (

2.1.
The linearized operator

In this subsection, we recall some standard properties of
the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
and then introduce useful functions related to the construction of a suitable blow-up profile.

Proposition 2.1

(Spectral theory of
\mathcal{L}
)
.

The self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined by (
1.5
) satisfies the following properties.

(i)

Spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
.
The operator
\mathcal{L}
has only one negative eigenvalue
-\mu_{0}
with
\mu_{0}>0
,
{\rm{Ker}}\mathcal{L}={\rm{Span}}\left(\partial_{y_{1}}Q,\partial_{y_{2}}Q\right)
and

\sigma_{\rm{ess}}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=[1,\infty)
.
We denote by
Y
the
L^{2}
-normalized eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
-\mu_{0}
. It holds, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}Y(y)\right|\lesssim e^{-|y|},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(ii)

Scaling identities.
It holds

\mathcal{L}\Lambda Q=-2Q\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\Lambda Q,Q)=0.

(iii)

Coercivity of
\mathcal{L}
.

There exists
\mu_{1}>0
such that, for all
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}f,f\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\big(\left(f,Y\right)^{2}+\left|\left(f,\nabla Q\right)\right|^{2}\big),

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}f,f\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\big(\left(f,Q^{3}\right)^{2}+\left|\left(f,\nabla Q\right)\right|^{2}\big).

(iv)

Inversion of
\mathcal{L}
.

For any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
orthogonal to
\nabla Q
in the
L^{2}
sense, there exists a unique function
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
orthogonal to
\nabla Q
in the
L^{2}
sense such that
\mathcal{L}f=g
. Moreover, if
g\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then we have
f\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In addition, if
g
is even in either
y_{1}
or
y_{2}
, then we have
f
is also even in
y_{1}
or
y_{2}
.

Proof.

The properties of
\mathcal{L}
in (i)–(iv) are easily checked. We refer to
[
15
, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.2]
and
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
for the details of the proofs.
∎

We now recall the existence of a non-localized profile
from

.

Lemma 2.2

(Non-localized profile)
.

There exists a smooth function
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\partial_{y_{1}}P\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
P
is even in
y_{2}
and the following properties are true.

(i)

First property of
P
.
We have

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}P=\Lambda Q,\quad\lim_{y_{1}\to\infty}\partial_{y_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}P(y_{1},y_{2})=0,\ \forall\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{N},

|(\nabla P,Q)|=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(P,Q)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(y_{2})|^{2}{\rm d}y_{2}.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{y_{1}\to-\infty}P(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=-F(y_{2})-h_{2}(y_{2}),

\displaystyle\lim_{y_{1}\to-\infty}\partial_{y_{2}}P(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=-F^{\prime}(y_{2})-h^{\prime}_{2}(y_{2}).

(ii)

Second property of
P
.

For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
\beta_{1}\neq 0
, we have

\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}},\quad\ \mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

In addition, for any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}},\quad\ \mbox{on}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

The proof relies on the definition of
(h_{1},h_{2})\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R})
in Section
1.5
and (iv) of Proposition
2.1
(see
e.g.

[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
), and we omit it.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Using integration by parts and (i) of Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle(P,\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F^{\prime}(y_{2})+h^{\prime}_{2}(y_{2})\right)^{2}{\rm d}y_{2},

\displaystyle\left(P,\partial_{y_{1}}^{3}P-\partial_{y_{1}}P\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F(y_{2})+h_{2}(y_{2})\right)^{2}{\rm d}y_{2}.

Taking the Fourier transform on the both sides of (
1.6
), we obtain

\widehat{h_{2}}(\xi)=-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{F}(\xi)\Longrightarrow\widehat{F}(\xi)+\widehat{h_{2}}(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{F}(\xi)}{1+|\xi|^{2}}.

It follows from Plancherel theorem that

(P,\Delta\partial_{y_{1}}P-\partial_{y_{1}}P)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})\left(\widehat{F}(\xi)+\widehat{h_{2}}(\xi)\right)^{2}{\rm d}\xi=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\xi.

On the other hand, using Lemma
2.2
and integration by parts, we have

(\Lambda P,Q)=-(P,\Lambda Q)=\left(P,\Delta\partial_{y_{1}}P-\partial_{y_{1}}P\right)-3\left(\partial_{y_{1}}\left(QP^{2}\right),Q\right).

Combining the above identities with Lemma
2.2
and the definition of
\theta
, we find

\theta=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}{\rm d}\xi\bigg)\bigg/\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\bigg)=\left(\Lambda P+3\partial_{y_{1}}\left(QP^{2}\right),Q\right)/(P,Q).

We mention here that, the constant
\theta
plays a crucial role in our construction of the blow-up profile since it appears in the leading-order term related to the profile (see Proposition
2.8
and
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
for more details).

We now introduce the following function space
\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which is a generalization of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Roughly speaking, the function belonging to
\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is allowed to have polynomial growth (degree not greater than
k
) as
y_{1}\to-\infty
.

Definition 2.4
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the function space consisting of all functions
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which satisfy

\partial_{x_{1}}^{k+1}f\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\partial_{x_{1}}^{\ell}f\right)\chi\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ell\in\left\{0,1,\dots,k\right\}.

In addition, we denote
\mathcal{Z}_{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,\infty)
. Suppose
g\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}
with
(g,Q)=0
and
g
is even in
y_{2}
.
Then there exists a smooth function
f\in\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}
such that

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}f=g\quad\mbox{and}\quad|(f,\nabla Q)|=0.

In addition, we have
f
is also even in
y_{2}
.

Proof.

We prove Lemma
2.5
by induction.

Step 1.
For
k=-1
. The proof is similar to
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
. Below, we provide a sketch of the proof
for the sake of completeness and for the readers’ convenience.
We set

-G^{\prime\prime}_{2}(y_{2})+G_{2}(y_{2})=H^{\prime\prime}_{2}(y_{2}),\quad\mbox{where}\ \ H_{2}(y_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau.

We consider
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of the form

f(y_{1},y_{2})=\widetilde{f}(y_{1},y_{2})-\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}h_{1}(\tau)G_{2}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-c\partial_{y_{1}}Q.

Here, we consider
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{Z}_{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
to be chosen later. By an elementary computation, we find
\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}f=g
is equivalent to

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\widetilde{f}=g+\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}\left(g(\tau,y_{2})+h_{1}(\tau)G(y_{2})\right){\rm d}\tau=\partial_{y_{1}}R.

Here, we denote

\displaystyle R(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=\partial_{y_{1}}g(y_{1},y_{2})-\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-3Q^{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+h_{1}^{\prime}(y_{1})G_{2}(y_{2})+\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}h_{1}(\tau)H^{\prime\prime}_{2}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-3Q^{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}h_{1}(\tau)G_{2}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau.

From
(g,Q)=0
and the definition of
R
, we find
R\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\left|(R,\nabla Q)\right|=0
and
R
is even in
y_{2}
.
Therefore, using (iv) of Proposition
2.1
, there exists

\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\widetilde{f}=\partial_{y_{1}}R\ \mbox{and $\widetilde{f}$ is even in $y_{2}$}\Longrightarrow\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}{f}=g\ \mbox{and $f$ is even in $y_{2}$}.

From
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the definition of
f
, we know that
f\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then, by choosing a suitable constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
\left|(f,\nabla Q)\right|=0
which means that this Lemma is true for
k=-1
.

Step 2.
Assume that this Lemma is true for
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,\infty)
, we prove that it is also true for
k+1
. First, from the Fundamental theorem, for any
g\in\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
(g,Q)=0
and
g
is even in
y_{2}
,
there exist even functions
(H_{2\ell})_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\subset\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R})
such that

g-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}H_{1\ell}\otimes H_{2\ell}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\mbox{with}\ \ H_{1\ell}(y_{1})=(1-\chi(y_{1}))y_{1}^{\ell}.

Here, we use the fact that

\partial_{y_{1}}^{\ell}g(y_{1},y_{2})=\partial_{y_{1}}^{\ell}g(0,y_{2})+\int_{0}^{y_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}}^{\ell+1}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau,\quad\mbox{for any}\ \ell\in\left\{0,1,\dots,k\right\}.

For any
\ell\in\left\{0,1,\dots,k+1\right\}
, we denote by
G_{2\ell}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R})
the smooth solution of the following second-order ODE:

-G^{\prime\prime}_{2\ell}(y_{2})+G_{2\ell}(y_{2})=H^{\prime\prime}_{2\ell}(y_{2}),\quad\mbox{on}\ \ \mathbb{R}.

Note that, from
(H_{2\ell})_{\ell=0}^{k+1}
are even functions, we obtain
(G_{2\ell})_{\ell=0}^{k+1}
are also even functions.
We now consider
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of the form

f(y_{1},y_{2})=\widetilde{f}(y_{1},y_{2})-\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}H_{1\ell}(\tau)G_{2\ell}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau-c\partial_{y_{1}}Q.

Here, we consider
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
to be chosen later.
Note that, the equation
\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}f=g
is equivalent to

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\widetilde{f}=g+\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}H_{1\ell}(\tau)G_{2\ell}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau\right)=\widetilde{g}.

On the one hand side, from
(g,Q)=0
and
\partial_{y_{1}}Q\in{\rm{Ker}}\mathcal{L}
, we obtain
(\widetilde{g},Q)=0
. On the other hand, by an elementary computation, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle=\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}g+\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}\left(g-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}H_{1\ell}\otimes H_{2\ell}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}H^{\prime\prime}_{1\ell}\otimes H_{2\ell}

\displaystyle-3\partial_{y_{1}}\left(Q^{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}g(\tau,y_{2}){\rm d}\tau\right)-3\partial_{y_{1}}\left(Q^{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\infty}H_{1\ell}(\tau)G_{2\ell}(y_{2}){\rm d}\tau\right).

From the above identity, the definition of
(H_{1\ell},H_{2\ell})
and
g\in\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we deduce that
\widetilde{g}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. With the assumption of the Induction Hypothesis in hand, we know that there exists
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}\widetilde{{f}}=\widetilde{g}
and
\widetilde{f}
is even in
y_{2}
. Therefore, from the definition of
\widetilde{f}
and
{f}
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{Z}_{k+2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}f=g
and
f
is even in
y_{2}
. By choosing a suitable constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
|(f,\nabla Q)|=0
which means that this Lemma is true for
k+1
. By the induction argument, Lemma
2.5
is proved for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,\infty)
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Note that, from the definition of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the Fundamental Theorem, for any
f\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
\beta_{1}\geq k+1
,

\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}f(y_{1},y_{2})\right|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}},\quad\mbox{on}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Note also that, for any
f\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with

, we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}f(y_{1},y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}}+\left(1+|y_{1}|^{k-\beta_{1}}\right)e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}}\textbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(y_{1}),\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

2.2.
Construction of the Blow-up profile

Let
s_{0}<0
with
|s_{0}|\gg 1
to be chosen later and let
I\subset(-\infty,s_{0}]
be a compact interval.
We assume
\lambda:I\to(0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
real-valued function defined on
I
such that

0<\lambda(s)\ll 1
for any
s\in I
.

We define

\Theta(s,y_{1})=\chi\big(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}(s)y_{1}\big),\quad\mbox{on}\ \ (s,y_{1})\in I\times\mathbb{R}.

In addition, for smooth functions

(A_{2},A_{3},A_{4})\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\mathcal{Z}_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(2.1)

and real constants
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
to be chosen later, we define

X_{1}=c_{1}P,\quad X_{2}=c_{2}P+A_{2},\quad X_{3}=c_{3}P+A_{3}\quad\mbox{and}\quad X_{4}=A_{4}.

We now consider the following blow-up profile:

W(s,y)=Q(y)+V(s,y),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ V(s,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{4}V_{k}(s,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\lambda^{k\theta}(s)X_{k}(y)\Theta(s,y_{1}).

Lemma 2.7
.

For any smooth functions
(A_{2},A_{3},A_{4})
satisfying (
2.1
) and real constants
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the following estimates hold.

(i)

Closeness to
Q
.
It holds

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W^{2}{\rm d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{2}{\rm d}y\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\theta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|W-Q\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\lambda^{\theta-\frac{4}{5}}.

(ii)

Estimates of
W
.
For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we have

\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}W\right|+\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}\Lambda W\right|\lesssim e^{-\frac{|y|}{4}}+\lambda^{\theta+\frac{8}{5}\beta_{1}}e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{4}}\mathbf{1}_{[-2,0]}(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}y_{1}).

Proof.

Proof of (i). From the definition of
V
and
W
, we find

\displaystyle\left|W^{2}-Q^{2}\right|\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{4}Q|V_{k}|+\sum_{k=1}^{4}V_{k}^{2}.

Using the fact that
\theta>\frac{8}{5}
,
Q\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and Remark
2.6
, we deduce that

\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q\left|V_{k}\right|{\rm d}y\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(1+|y_{1}|^{3}\right)e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}}{\rm d}y\lesssim\lambda^{\theta}.

Then, using again the fact that
\theta>\frac{8}{5}
and Remark
2.6
, for any
k\in\left\{1,2,3,4\right\}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V_{k}^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2k\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}}+\left(1+|y_{1}|^{2(k-1)}\right)\textbf{1}_{[-2,0]}\left(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}y_{1}\right)e^{-\frac{|y_{2}}{3}|}\right){\rm d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2k\theta}+\lambda^{2k\theta-\frac{8}{5}(2k-1)}\lesssim\lambda^{2k\theta}+\lambda^{\theta+\left(\theta-\frac{8}{5}\right)(2k-1)}\lesssim\lambda^{\theta}.

Using a similar argument as above, we also obtain

\|W-Q\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{4}\|V_{k}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\lambda^{\theta-\frac{4}{5}}.

Combining the above estimates, we complete the proof of (i).

Proof of (ii). By an elementary computation, for any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{y}^{\beta}V_{k}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k\theta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\beta_{11}\leq k-1\\
\beta_{11}+\beta_{12}=\beta_{1}\end{subarray}}\left(\partial_{y_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{11}}X_{k}\right)\left(\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{12}}\Theta\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{k\theta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\beta_{11}\leq\beta_{1}\\
\beta_{11}+\beta_{12}=\beta_{1}\end{subarray}}\left(\partial_{y_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{11}}X_{k}\right)\left(\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{12}}\Theta\right).

It follows from
\theta>\frac{8}{5}
and Remark
2.6
that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}V_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{k\theta}\left(e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}}+\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}\beta_{12}}\langle y_{1}\rangle^{k-1-\beta_{11}}e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}}\mathbf{1}_{[-2,0]}(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{k\theta}\left(e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}}+\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}\beta_{1}-\frac{8}{5}(k-1)}e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}}\mathbf{1}_{[-2,0]}(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{k\theta}e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}}+\lambda^{\theta+\frac{8}{5}\beta_{1}}e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}}\mathbf{1}_{[-2,0]}(\lambda^{\frac{8}{5}}y_{1}).

Combining the above estimates with
Q\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we complete the proof of (ii).
∎

Last, we consider the following error term related to the blow-up profile
W
:

\Gamma(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{3}c_{k}\lambda^{k\theta}(s)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}(W)=\partial_{s}W+\partial_{y_{1}}\left(\Delta W-W+W^{3}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda W.

To simplify notation, we denote

\displaystyle V^{2}=\sum_{k=2}^{8}\lambda^{k\theta}N_{k}\Theta^{2}

Therefore, using again the decay property of

\left|b\left(3\partial_{y_{1}}\left(W^{2}-Q^{2}\right)P_{b},Q\right)+\theta^{-1}b\lambda^{\theta}\left(6\partial_{y_{1}}\left(QP^{2}\right),Q\right)\right|\lesssim|b|\left(|b|+\lambda^{2\theta}\right).

Combining the above estimates with Remark

3.2.
Bootstrap assumption

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large enough, we set

Here,

We consider the solution

Here, we set

\lambda_{n}(T_{n})=G^{-1}(n),\quad x_{1n}(T_{n})=-\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}n^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}},\quad b_{n}(T_{n})=0,

(3.9)

with

G(\lambda)=\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\tau^{1+\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-c_{2}\tau^{\theta}-c_{3}\tau^{2\theta}\right)},\quad\mbox{for any}\ \lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}].

Remark 3.6
.

The function

is well-defined for any

, where
\lambda_{0}
is small enough. Moreover, this function is decreasing and one-to-one from
(0,\lambda_{0}]
to
[0,\infty)
. From the Taylor expansion, we find

\frac{1}{\tau^{1+\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-c_{2}\tau^{\theta}-c_{3}\tau^{2\theta}\right)}=\theta\tau^{-1-\theta}+c_{2}\theta^{2}\tau^{-1}+O\left(\tau^{-1+\theta}\right).

From the definition of

G(\lambda)=\lambda^{-\theta}-c_{2}\theta^{2}\log\lambda+O(1),\quad\mbox{as}\ \ \lambda\downarrow 0.

Based on the above identity and the Taylor expansion, for any

large enough,

G^{-1}(n)=\left(\frac{1}{n+O(\log n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=n^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}+O\left(n^{-1-\frac{1}{\theta}}\log n\right).

For

\left\{\begin{aligned} |b_{n}(s)|\leq C_{1}|s|^{-4}&,\ \ \mathcal{N}_{B}(s)\leq|s|^{-\frac{17}{4}},\\

Then, from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the change of variable, we find

\left|K_{3}\right|\lesssim|\overline{z}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\frac{{\rm d}\xi_{1}}{\xi_{1}^{2}+\overline{z}\xi_{1}+\overline{z}^{2}+1}\right|\lesssim 1.

Combining the above estimates for
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
, we see that
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Therefore, from Young’s inequality and (
A.2
), we obtain

\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}D_{1}\right\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{\infty}L_{x_{2}T}^{2}}\lesssim\|K\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{(t,x_{2})\to(\sigma,\xi_{2})}(g(x_{1}))(\sigma,\xi_{2})\right\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{1}L_{\xi_{2}\sigma}^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{1}_{x_{1}}L_{x_{2}T}^{2}}.

which completes the proof of (
5.15
).

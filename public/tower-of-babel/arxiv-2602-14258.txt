Abstract
In this paper, we propose a notion of subdifferential defined via Busemann functions and use it to identify a condition under which the Fenchel–Young inequality of Bento, Cruz Neto and Melo (Appl. Math. Optim. 88:83, 2023) holds with equality. This equality condition is particularly significant, as it captures a fundamental duality principle in convex analysis, linking a primal convex function to its conjugate and clarifying the sharpness of the associated inequality on Riemannian manifolds. We also investigate the existence of non-trivial affine functions under Ricci curvature information. In particular, we extend the result of Bento, Cruz Neto and Melo, originally formulated for the case of negative Ricci curvature on an open set, to manifolds whose Ricci curvature may be non-zero. As a consequence, we prove new non-existence criteria for non-trivial affine functions and show that the assumption of non-zero Ricci curvature is, in general, necessary to ensure such a rigidity conclusion.

Introduction

It is known that affine functions play a crucial role in various branches of mathematics and its applications due to their simplicity and the powerful mathematical properties they possess. In the Riemannian sense a real-valued function
f

M

[

The discussion about affine functions in the context of Riemannian manifolds was recently revisited due to the issue raised in
[

•

More precisely, there are examples in which the difference between a proper, lsc, convex function and its biconjugate is a constant that depends on the sectional curvature of the manifold;

•

Despite our proposal taking into account a base point

Roughly speaking, once we have information on the isometries of the manifold, we obtain a closed formula that allows the conjugate to be determined on base points other than

•

We extend the classical Fenchel-Young inequality from the linear setting to the Riemannian context and demonstrate that any function is a majorant of its biconjugate, among other findings. Also, in line with the linear setting, we prove that

•

The conjugate of the Busemann function is finite at only a single point on Hadamard manifolds with constant negative curvature.

This paper makes significant contributions to the field of continuous optimization by intrinsically exploring theoretical aspects through Busemann functions. We propose a new subdifferential framework utilizing Busemann functions, from which we demonstrate a condition that ensures the Fechel-Young inequality, as established in
[
6

, holds true with equality. This fact is undoubtedly a point that deserves attention because it reflects a fundamental duality relation in the analysis of convex problems, establishing a bridge between the primal function and its dual conjugate. Furthermore, we stablish relations involving the Fenchel conjugate and the B-subdifferential as well as a relationship between the subdifferentials at different points in terms of the isometries of the Riemannian manifold. As introduced/observed in
[
6

, radial functions have appeared, for example, in the classic problem of center of mass, whose primal-dual formulation relies on the conjugate concept of Fenchel and its properties. In this paper, we also revisit this class of functions and determine the associated B-subdifferential.
During the course of our project, some works addressed the introduction of a type of subgradient for convex functions on Hadamard manifolds (see
[

The notion of subgradient for convex functions based on Busemann functions that is most closely related to ours is the one introduced in Hadamard spaces, which is directly connected to the concept of the Fenchel conjugate proposed in
[
24

Assuming that
M
is a complete and connected Riemannian manifold,
(M,d)
is a complete metric space, where
d
denotes the Riemannian distance. We denote by
T_{p}M
the tangent space of
M
at
p\in M
and by
TM
the tangent bundle of
M
set to be those pairs
\theta=(p,v),

p\in M,\;v\in T_{p}M
. The Riemannian metric of
M
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, with the corresponding norm given by
\|\cdot\|
. For
\theta=(p,v)\in TM
,
\gamma_{\theta}(\cdot)
denotes the unique geodesic with initial conditions
\gamma_{\theta}(0)=p
and
\gamma_{\theta}^{\prime}(0)=v
.
The exponential map
\mbox{exp}:TM\to M
is defined by
\mbox{exp}(\theta):=\gamma_{\theta}(1)
. For
p\in M
fixed,
\mbox{exp}_{p}v:=\gamma_{\theta}(1,p)
where
\theta=(p,v)
. For each smooth function in
M
, the metric induces its gradient denoted by
\mbox{grad}f
. Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection associated with the Riemannian manifold
(M,{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle})
. The restriction of a geodesic to a closed bounded interval is called a geodesic segment. Let
v=v_{n}
be a unitary vector in
T_{p}M
and
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{n}\}
an orthonormal basis in
T_{p}M
. The Ricci curvature in the direction
v
is defined by

\mbox{Ric}_{p}(v)=\frac{1}{n-1}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}K_{p}(v,v_{i}),

(8)

where
K_{p}(v,v_{i})
denotes the sectional curvature of the two-dimensional subspace generated by
\{v,v_{i}\}
, for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
. It should be noted that the expression in (
8
) does not depend on the choice of the corresponding orthonormal basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}\}
of the hyperplane in
T_{p}M
orthogonal to

.

A complete, simply connected Riemannian manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature is called a Hadamard manifold. In the case where
M
is a Hadamard manifold, then for each
p\in M
,
\mbox{exp}_{p}(\cdot)
is a diffeomorphism, and for any two points
p,q\in M
there exists a unique normalized geodesic joining
p
to
q
, which is, in fact, a minimal geodesic.
Furthermore, given
\theta=(p,v)
,
\tilde{\theta}=(\tilde{p},\tilde{v})

p,\tilde{p}\in M,\;v\in T_{p}M
and
\tilde{v}\in T_{\tilde{p}}M
such that
\|v\|=\|\tilde{v}\|=1
, the geodesics
\gamma_{\theta},\gamma_{\tilde{\theta}}

are said to be asymptotic, provided there exists a number
c>0
such that

d(\gamma_{\theta}(t),\gamma_{\tilde{\theta}}(t))\leq c
, for all
t\geq 0
.
For each point
q\in M
, there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma_{(q,\tilde{v})}(\cdot)
such that it is asymptotic to
\gamma_{\theta}(\cdot)
. It is also known that if
\gamma_{\theta},\gamma_{\tilde{\theta}}
are asymptotic geodesics in
M
, then
B^{p}_{v}-B^{\tilde{p}}_{\tilde{v}}
is constant.
An important example of Hadamard manifold with non-constant sectional curvature is the space of positive symmetric matrices
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}
endowed with the Riemannian metric induced by the Euclidean Hessian of
\Psi(X)=-\ln\det X
,

\langle U,V\rangle=\mbox{tr}(V\Psi^{\prime\prime}(X)U)=\mbox{tr}(VX^{-1}UX^{-1}),\quad X\in M,\quad U,V\in T_{X}M.

(9)

It is known that it forms a rich and well-structured Hadamard manifold; see, for example,
[

, the sectional curvature is bounded below by -1/2. Moreover, the gradient and the Hessian of a twice differentiable function
F:{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}
is given, respectively, by:

•

\mbox{grad}F(X)=XF^{\prime}(X)X;

•

(\mbox{hess}\,F(X)\big(V,V\big)=\mbox{tr}\big(VF^{\prime\prime}(X)V\big)+\mbox{tr}\big(F^{\prime}(X)VX^{-1}V\big),\ V\in T_{X}M,

where

and

are the Euclidean gradient and Hessian, respectively.

Affine Functions on Riemannian Manifolds

In this section, we present some implications and related results that extend the result established in Theorem
1
to manifolds with positive Ricci curvature. Furthermore, we present a counterexample illustrating that the assumption about Ricci curvature is indeed necessary, i.e., the result can fail if, for every
p\in M
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in T_{p}M
such that
Ric_{p}(\xi)=0
.
Next we recall the main result in
[
25
]
.

Theorem 2
.

A complete Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary admits a non-trivial affine function if and only if
M
is isometric to a Riemannian product
N\times\mathbb{R}
.

Next result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
combined with Theorem
2
.

Corollary 3
.

Hadamard manifolds with negative sectional curvature are not isometric to a
Riemannian product of the form
N\times\mathbb{R}
, where
N
is an (n-1)-dimensional Riemannian manifold.

Below, we present the main result of the paper, which extends Theorem 1 to manifolds where the Ricci curvature is positive.

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold without boundary. If
M
admits a non-trivial affine function, then for every
p\in M
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in T_{p}M
such that
Ric_{p}(\xi)=0
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2
,
M
is isometric to a Riemannian product
N\times\mathbb{R}
. For any point
(q,t)\in N\times\mathbb{R}
, observe that
Ric_{(q,t)}(\partial t)=0
, where
\partial t
is the velocity vector associated with the curve
\alpha(t)=(q,t)
. Since isometries preserve curvature, we conclude that for every
p\in M
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in T_{p}M
such that
Ric_{p}(\xi)=0
.∎∎

Remark 5
.

Note that the converse of Theorem
4
is false. Indeed, let us consider the Riemannian manifold
\tilde{N}=\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is the hyperbolic space and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the unit circle. Since
\tilde{N}
is not isometric to a product manifold of type
N\times\mathbb{R}
, from Theorem
2
we have that it does not admit non-trivial affine functions. However,
{\rm{Ric}}_{q}(\xi)=0
for every point
q=(p,t)
in
\tilde{N}
where
\xi
is the tangent vector to the component
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

In addition to the various properties already established about
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}
, we identify the listed in the following theorem:

Theorem 6
.

Let
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}
be Hadamard manifold endowed with the Riemannian metric in (
9
). The following properties hold:

a.

for every
p\in M
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in T_{p}{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}
such that
Ric_{p}(\xi)=0
;

b.

{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}
is isometric to a Riemannian product

;

c.

Let
F_{1}:{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by:

and note that it is a twice differentiable function on

4
Subdifferential via Busemann functions on Hadamard manifolds

In this section, we propose a definition of the subdifferential based on Busemann functions. Using this definition, we establish a condition under which the Fenchel-Young inequality presented in
[
6
, Theorem 5]
holds with equality, thus establishing a direct relationship between a primal function and its dual function, specifically its Fenchel conjugate.

In the remainder of this paper, we assume that
M
is a Hadamard manifold and, for any non-zero vector
u\in T_{p}M
, we denote by
B^{p}_{u}
the Busemann function associated with the geodesic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
satisfying
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
, where
v=\frac{u}{\|u\|}
. Equivalently,
B^{p}_{u}=B^{p}_{v}
, as defined in (
5
).
Given a fixed point
p\in M
, we also consider the function
H_{p}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
defined as follows:

H_{p}(z,y):=\begin{cases}d(z,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(z)}(y)&\text{if }z\neq p,\\
0&\text{if }z=p.\end{cases}

Definition 7
.

Let
p\in M
and
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
be a function. Given
x\in M
, a vector
v\in T_{x}M
is called a
subgradient
of
f
at
x
if one of the following holds:

•

(case
v\neq 0
)

f(z)\geq f(x)+H_{p}(z,y)-H_{p}(x,y),\quad\text{for every }z\in M,

where, in this case
v\neq 0
, there exists a unit vector
\eta\in T_{p}M
such that the geodesic
\gamma_{\eta}
is asymptotic to
\gamma_{-v}
and
y=\exp_{p}(-\|v\|\eta)
;

•

(Nonexistence of
\eta
,
v=0
).
If no such unit vector
\eta
exists, take
y=p
. The inequality then reduces to

f(z)\geq f(x),\quad\text{for all }z\in M,

i.e.
x
is a (global) minimum point of
f
.

The split into cases corresponds precisely to the possible nonexistence of the unit vector
\eta
required to define
y=\exp_{p}(-\|v\|\eta)
.
The set of all subgradients of
f
at a point
x
is called the
B^{p}
-subdifferential of
f
at
x
and is denoted by
\partial B^{p}f(x)
. Note also that our definition of subgradient is a natural extension of its counterpart in the Euclidean setting. Indeed, for
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and using the same notation as in the above definition, we have
y=p+v
and, hence,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle y-p,z-p\rangle-\langle y-p,x-p\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle y-p,z-x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle v,z-x\rangle.

For
y\in M
fixed,
y\neq p
, let us define
H(z):=H_{p}(z,y)
and note that

H_{p}(z,y)-H_{p}(x,y)=H(z)-H(x)
.
We can to write
y=\exp_{p}(-d(y,p)\eta)
, where
\eta=-\displaystyle\frac{\exp^{-1}_{p}y}{d(p,y)}
. Given
x\in M
and letting
v_{x}\in T_{x}M
be the unique unit vector such that
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{v_{x}}
are asymptotic, we have that
-d(y,p)v_{x}\in\partial B^{p}H(x)
. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
H(z)=\langle z-p,y-p\rangle
,
v_{x}=p-y/\|p-y\|
and
\partial B^{p}H(x)=\{y-p\}
. In particular, for
p=0
we get the usual subgradient.

For a proper function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined on a manifold
M
and for a point
p\in M
, the following inequality holds:

f(x)+f_{p}^{*}(y)\geq H_{p}(x,y),

(11)

where
f_{p}^{*}
denotes the Fenchel conjugate of
f
at the point
p
defined in (
7
). This inequality, introduced in
[
6
]
, extends the classical Fenchel-Young inequality from the linear setting to the Riemannian context. In the subsequent analysis, we explore the relationship between the subdifferential and the equality in (
11
), which is a central aspect of optimization.

Proposition 8
.

If
f(x)+f_{p}^{*}(y)=H_{p}(x,y)
then
d(y,p)u\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
where
u\in T_{x}M
is the unique unit vector such that
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{-u}
are asymptotic where
\eta=-\frac{\exp^{-1}_{p}y}{d(y,p)}
.

Proof.

From definition of conjugate we have

H_{p}(x,y)-f(x)=f_{p}^{*}(y)\geq H_{p}(z,y)-f(z),

for any
z\in M
. Hence,

f(z)\geq f(x)+H_{p}(z,y)-H_{p}(x,y),

for any
z\in M
. On the other hand, there exists a unique unit vector
u\in T_{x}M
such that
\gamma_{\eta}
and
\gamma_{-u}
are asymptotic and we can to write
y=\exp_{p}(-d(y,p)\eta)
. From Definition
7
it follows that
d(y,p)u\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

If
v\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
then
f(x)+f_{p}^{*}(y)=H_{p}(x,y),
where
y=\exp_{p}(-||v||\eta)
and
\eta\in T_{p}M
is the unique unit vector such that
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{-v}
are asymptotic.

Proof.

From Definition
7
we have

H_{p}(x,y)-f(x)\geq H_{p}(z,y)-f(z),

for any
z\in M
. Taking the supremum over
z
we get
H_{p}(x,y)-f(x)=f_{p}^{*}(y)
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Given an isometry
I:M\to M
such that
I(q)=p
, in

[
6
, Theorem 2]
we establish the following relationship between conjugates at different points:

f_{p}^{*}\circ I=(f\circ I)_{q}^{*}.

(12)

As an application of Theorem
9
and using the relationship between conjugates at different points given in (
12
), the next theorem establishes a relationship between the subdifferentials at different points in terms of the isometries of the Riemannian manifold. Our result is novel even in the linear setting.

Theorem 10
.

Let
I:M\to M
be an isometry such that
I(q)=p
. A vector
u\in\partial B^{q}(f\circ I)(b)
iff
DI_{b}(u)\in\partial B^{p}f(I(b))
.

Proof.

From Theorem
9
follows that

(f\circ I)(b)+(f\circ I)_{q}^{*}(y)=H_{q}(b,y),

(13)

where
y=\exp_{q}(-||u||\eta)
and
\eta\in T_{q}M
is the unique unit vector such that
\gamma_{\eta}
and
\gamma_{-u}
are asymptotic. On the other hand, since an isometry leaves geodesic in geodesic and preserves the distance, it follows that the geodesic
I\circ\gamma_{\eta}
is asymptotic to the geodesic
I\circ\gamma_{-u}
and
B^{q}_{-\exp^{-1}_{q}(b)}(y)=B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(I(b))}(I(y))
. Combining (
12
) with (
13
) we get

f(I(b))+f_{p}^{*}(I(y))=d(p,I(b))B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(I(b))}(I(y)).

Note that
I(y)=I\circ\gamma_{\eta}(-||u||)=exp_{p}(-||u||\tilde{\eta})
, where
\tilde{\eta}=DI_{q}(\eta)
and
(I\circ\gamma_{-v})^{\prime}(0)=DI_{b}(u)=v
so
||v||=||u||
. Furthermore,
\tilde{\eta}
is the unique vector in
T_{p}M
such that
\gamma_{\tilde{\eta}}=I\circ\gamma_{\eta}
and
\gamma_{-v}=I\circ\gamma_{-u}
are asymptotic. Therefore,
DI_{b}(u)\in\partial B^{p}f(I(b))
. To prove the converse, it suffices to consider the inverse isometry
I^{-1}
.
∎

It is known that a function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is said to be radial at a point
p
if there exists a function
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
such that
f=h(d(\cdot,p))
. For instance, see
[
6
]
, where the authors present a classic problem involving radial functions and, among other results, demonstrate that:

{i)}f^{*}_{p}=h^{*}(d(\cdot,p));

(14)

ii) If
h
is proper, lower semicontinuous (lsc), and convex, then
f^{**}_{p}=f
(the classic Fenchel-Moreau theorem).

As a second application of Theorem
9
, our objective now is to show the following:

Theorem 11
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a radial function at a point
p\in M
such that
f=h(d(\cdot,p))
where
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a differentiable convex function that satisfies
h(0)=0
and
h^{\prime}>0
. Then, for any point
x\neq p
, we have
\partial B^{p}f(x)=\{\mbox{grad}f(x)\}
, and at the point
p
,
\partial B^{p}f(p)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mbox{grad}f(z)=-\displaystyle\frac{h^{\prime}(d(z,p)}{d(z,p)}\exp^{-1}_{z}(p)
if
z\neq p
. From the notation of Definition
7
we have
\xi=-\displaystyle\frac{\exp^{-1}_{z}p}{d(z,p)}
,
y=\exp_{p}(-h^{\prime}(d(z,p)\eta)
, where
\eta=-\displaystyle\frac{\exp^{-1}_{p}z}{d(p,z)}
. Note that, from the last two equalities, it follows that
d(y,p)=h^{\prime}(d(z,p))
. On the other hand, from the convexity of
h
, we have:

\displaystyle f(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h(d(z,p))+h^{\prime}(d(z,p))(d(u,p)-d(z,p)),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(d(z,p))+d(u,p)d(y,p)-h^{\prime}(d(z,p))d(z,p),

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h(d(z,p))+d(u,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(u)}(y)-h^{\prime}(d(z,p))d(z,p),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(z)+d(u,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(u)}(y)-d(p,z)B^{p}_{-\xi}(y).

Therefore,
\mbox{grad}f(z)\in\partial B^{p}f(z)
. Now consider
v\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
and let
\overline{y}=\exp_{p}(-||v||\eta)
follow the notation of Definition
7
. From Theorem
9
it follows that
f(x)+f_{p}^{*}(\overline{y})=d(p,x)B^{p}_{\xi}(\overline{y})
. In particular, from (
14
), we have

\displaystyle h(d(x,p))+h^{*}(d(\overline{y},p))=d(p,x)B^{p}_{\xi}(\overline{y})\leq d(p,x)d(p,\overline{y}).

On the other hand, using convex conjugation for real functions, we have
h(d(x,p))+h^{*}(d(\overline{y},p))\geq d(p,x)d(p,\overline{y}).

In particular,
h(d(x,p))+h^{*}(d(\overline{y},p))=d(p,x)d(p,\overline{y})
,
B^{p}_{\xi}(\overline{y})=d(p,\overline{y})
and
d(\overline{y},p)\in\partial h(d(x,p))
so
d(\overline{y},p)=h^{\prime}(d(x,p)
,
\overline{y}=\exp_{p}(-h^{\prime}(d(z,p))\xi)

and
v=\mbox{grad}f(x)
.
∎

5
Fenchel Biconjugate

The Fenchel-Moreau Theorem or simply the Biconjugation Theorem, is a classic and crucial result in optimization and convex analysis which provides necessary and sufficient conditions for a function to be equal to its biconjugate, i.e.,
f=f^{**}
. This contrasts with the general property where for any function
f
, it holds that
f^{**}\leq f
. In the Riemannian context, we have demonstrated the influence of sectional curvature in obtaining this result, initially for the specific case in which
f
is the Busemann function and
M
is a Hadamard manifold with constant sectional curvature equal to
-k^{2}
(where
k
is a positive number). Specifically, given
p\in M
and a unitary vector
\bar{\eta}\in T_{p}M
, we have:

(B^{p}_{\overline{\eta}})^{**}=B^{p}_{\overline{\eta}}-\frac{1}{k}\ln\left(\cosh(k/2)\right).

(15)

This result, established in
[
6
, Theorem 3]
, illustrates that the difference between the Busemann function and its biconjugate is a constant that depends on the sectional curvature of the manifold, thus demonstrating that a Fenchel-Moreau-type theorem is directly influenced by the sectional curvature. Building upon our objective to develop additional tools in convex analysis, this section addresses the conjecture presented in
[
6
]
regarding a more comprehensive version of that established in (
15
) in Hadamard manifolds. Our approach is grounded in the concept of the B-subdifferential and identifies a specific measure of non-linearity within this context.

Given
p\in M
, let us define

\mathcal{N}^{p}(y):=\inf_{z}\{d(y,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(y)}(z)-d(z,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(z)}(y)\}=\inf_{z}\{H_{p}(y,z)-H_{p}(z,y)\}.

Note that
\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)\leq 0
(to see this, simply take
z=p
), and in the case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has
\mathcal{N}^{p}\equiv 0
(this is because, in the Euclidean case,

H_{p}(y,z)-H_{p}(z,y)=\langle z-p,y-p\rangle-\langle y-p,z-p\rangle=0
).

In the next result, we show that
\mathcal{N}^{p}
is the constant difference between the Busemann function and its biconjugate in (15), highlighting that, in a certain sense, it measures the non-linearity of the space.

Proposition 12
.

Given
p\in M
, assume that
M
has constant negative sectional curvature equal to
-k^{2}
and
y\in M
is such that
d(y,p)=1
. Then,

\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\ln\Big(\cosh\Big(\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\Big)\Big).

Proof.

From definition we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sup_{z}\{d(z,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(z)}(y)-B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(y)}(z)\}.

On the other hand,
\displaystyle\sup_{z}\{d(z,p)B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(z)}(y)-B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(y)}(z)\}
is the conjugate with respect to the point
p
of the function
B^{p}_{-\exp^{-1}_{p}(y)}(\cdot)
in
y
. From
[
6
, Theorem 3]
it follows that

\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\ln\Big(\cosh\Big(\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\Big)\Big)
.
∎

Corollary 13
.

Given
p\in M
, assume that
M
has constant negative sectional curvature equal to
-1
and
y\in M
such that
d(y,p)=1
. Then,

\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)=-\ln\Big(\cosh\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big).

The next result is a consequence of Proposition
12
combined with the convexity of the Busemann function and
[
6
, Proposition 8]
.

Corollary 14
.

Let
a
and
b
be positive numbers such that the sectional curvature of
M
is constrained to lie within the range of at most
-a^{2}
and at least
-b^{2}
and
p\in M
. If
y\in M
is such that
d(y,p)\leq R
, then there exists a constant
C(R,a,b)
such that
\mathcal{N}^{p}(y)\geq C(R,a,b)>-\infty.

Theorem 15
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper such that
\partial B^{p}f(z)
is nonempty for every
z\in M
. Then,

f(x)\leq f_{p}^{**}(x)+\inf_{y}\{H_{p}(x,y)-H_{p}(y,x)\},

where
y=\exp_{p}(-||v||\eta)
,
v\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
and
\eta\in T_{p}M
is the unique unit vector such that
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{-v}
are asymptotic.

Proof.

Take
v\in\partial B^{p}f(x)
. From Theorem
9
it follows that

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{p}(x,y)-f_{p}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{p}(y,x)-f_{p}^{*}(y)+H_{p}(x,y)-H_{p}(y,x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{p}^{**}(x)+H_{p}(x,y)-H_{p}(y,x),

where the last inequality is obtained by considering the definition in (
7
) with
f=f^{*}
. Taking the infimum over
y
, the desired result is proven.
∎

, the Fenchel-Young inequality allows us to derive that
f^{**}\leq f
, a result that is well-known in the linear case. Furthermore, by combining the last theorem with Corollary

Corollary 16
.

Let
a
and
b
be positive numbers such that the sectional curvature of
M
is constrained to lie within the range of at most
-a^{2}
and at least
-b^{2}
,
p\in M

||u||\leq R

6
Conclusion

In this third work we continue and expand the research line initiated in
[
5
]
and advanced in
[
6
]
. Building on the classical notion of Busemann functions, introduced by H. Busemann to formulate a parallelism axiom in metric spaces
[
11
]
, we take another important step in developing an intrinsic approach on Hadamard manifolds to concepts and tools of continuous optimization that in the Euclidean setting rely on nontrivial affine functions. As a future perspective, we intend to present new results that further strengthen the structural theory of Busemann functions in the Riemannian context and to develop algorithmic approaches that employ these functions as natural substitutes for affine functions, including convergence analyses and illustrative examples with potential applications in Convex Analysis and (convex and nonconvex) Optimization.

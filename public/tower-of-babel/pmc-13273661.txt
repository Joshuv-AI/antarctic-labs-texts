Aqueous Humor Total Protein Levels Predict 6-Year Graft Survival after Endothelial Keratoplasty in Surgically Treated Glaucoma Eyes
Authors: Sayaka Kaneko, Yukari Yagi-Yaguchi, Taiyo Shijo, Rintaro Ogino, Mariko Shirane, Daisuke Tomida, Kazunari Higa, Takefumi Yamaguchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the association between endothelial graft failure and total protein levels in the aqueous humor (AqH) after Descemet stripping automated endothelial keratoplasty (DSAEK) in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma.DesignA prospective case series.MethodsThis consecutive case series included 181 patients who underwent DSAEK and were stratified into 3 groups: no glaucoma (132 eyes), medically treated glaucoma (17 eyes), and surgically treated glaucoma (32 eyes). The endothelial cell density (ECD) and graft survival rate was compared among the groups and within the surgically treated glaucoma group, among subgroups stratified by AqH total protein levels. Aqueous humor samples were collected at the beginning of each surgery. The levels of total protein and cytokines (interleukin [IL]-1α, IL-1β, IL-4, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12p70, IL-13, IL-17A, interferon-α, interferon-γ, monocyte chemotactic protein-1, E-selectin, P-selectin, and soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1) in the AqH were measured by a dye-binding assay or multiplex beads immunoassay. The severity of iris damage was graded based on the number of affected quadrants (range, 0–4).Main Outcome MeasuresGraft survival over 6 years after DSAEK in relation to preoperative AqH total protein levels and its association with postoperative ECD.ResultsBest-corrected visual acuity significantly improved after DSAEK in all groups. However, ECD after DSAEK was significantly lower in eye with surgically treated glaucoma, compared to eye without glaucoma (P < 0.01 at all timepoints up to 6 years). Graft survival was significantly lower in the surgically treated group (P < 0.001). In Cox proportional hazards model, trabeculectomy (hazard ratio: 2.247, 95% confidence interval: 1.133–4.455, P = 0.020) and iris damage severity (hazard ratio: 1.514, 95% confidence interval: 1.224–1.871, P < 0.001) were identified as independent risk factors for graft failure. In surgically treated glaucoma group, 6-year graft survival was 100% in the low-protein group (<0.7 mg/mL, n = 4), 23% in the medium-protein group (0.7–1.6 mg/mL, n = 17), and 0% in the high-protein group (>1.6 mg/mL, n = 10) (log-rank test, P = 0.0033).ConclusionsHigher preoperative total protein levels in AqH were associated with poor graft survival after DSAEK for bullous keratopathy after glaucoma surgery.Financial Disclosure(s)Proprietary or commercial disclosure may be found in the Footnotes and Disclosures at the end of this article.Keywords: Aqueous humor, Total protein, Endothelial cell density, Endothelial keratoplasty, Glaucoma surgery

Endothelial keratoplasty (EK) for the treatment of Fuchs' endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD) and various bullous keratopathy has become the standard surgical procedure because of several advantages over penetrating keratoplasty (PK).1,2 By removing only Descemet's membrane and the dysfunctional endothelium, while preserving the healthy stroma, EK offers advantages over PK such as more rapid visual recovery, lower rate of graft rejection, and less postoperative astigmatism,1,2 resulting in a favorable long-term clinical outcomes. Loss of corneal endothelial cells (CEnCs) remains the primary cause of graft failure after EK, followed by immunologic rejection.2, 3, 4 In general, the endothelial cell density (ECD) declines with age, and under various conditions such as after uveitis and intraocular surgery.5, 6, 7, 8 Clinical factors associated with CEnC loss include pseudophakic bullous keratopathy, a history of glaucoma surgery, and iridocorneal endothelial syndrome.3,4,9, 10, 11 Recently, we reported that severe pre-existing iris damage and elevated cytokine levels have significant negative impact on graft survival due to rapid CEnC loss after Descemet stripping automated EK (DSAEK) and PK,12, 13, 14 suggesting that a pathological AqH microenvironment predisposes to poor graft survival.15, 16, 17

Although the prognosis of glaucoma has improved with various surgical procedures and devices, such as tube shunts and MicroPulse cyclophotocoagulation, the implications for the cornea have been less favorable. The proportion of EK indications attributable to bullous keratopathy after glaucoma surgery increased from 5% in 199918 to 29% in 2019 in Japan.19 Moreover, the prognosis of EK for such eyes has been reported to be extremely poor due to rapid CEnC loss, compared with eyes with medically treated glaucoma or without glaucoma.3,4,9,10 However, the exact reason for chronic CEnC loss in such eyes is still poorly understood. We noticed an association between graft survival after DSAEK and preoperative total protein levels. For example, graft survival was only 3 months after DSAEK in eyes with 4.649 mg/mL of AqH total protein, whereas the cornea remained transparent for >9 years in eyes with 0.90 mg/mL of AqH total protein (Fig 1). Therefore, in the current study, we sought to evaluate the long-term prognosis of EK stratified by the preoperative total protein/cytokine levels and found that AqH total protein levels were associated with a poor prognosis of EK after glaucoma surgery.

Methods

This prospective study was performed in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Ethics Review Board of Tokyo Dental College, Ichikawa General Hospital (I-15-42R). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to the interventions.

Study Participants

A total of 181 consecutive patients (181 eyes) who underwent DSAEK at the International University of Health and Welfare Ichikawa General Hospital, from October 2015 to August 2019, were included in this study (Table 1). Of those, 32 eyes had surgically treated glaucoma, 17 eyes had medically treated glaucoma, and 132 eyes had no glaucoma. In the surgically treated glaucoma group (32 eyes), trabeculectomy was performed in 27 eyes; 4 eyes underwent trabeculectomy followed by tube shunt; and 1 eye underwent trabeculotomy followed by trabeculotomy. Eyes with active inflammation of the cornea or the anterior chamber, as well as patients receiving systemic steroid therapy, were excluded from the study.

Surgical Technique

Descemet stripping automated EK surgery was performed as reported previously.14,20 Briefly, all DSAEK surgeries were performed by one of the 3 experienced surgeons (T.Y., Y.Y.-Y., and D.T.). After sub-Tenon's anesthesia with injection of 2% lidocaine, a 5.0-mm temporal corneoscleral incision was made. An anterior chamber maintenance cannula was inserted through the 2 or 10 o'clock paracentesis, and Descemet stripping was performed with a reverse-bent Sinskey hook (Asico). The recipient's endothelium and Descemet's membrane were carefully removed using forceps. Precut donor grafts were trephinated, and the endothelial surface of the donor lenticle was coated with a small amount of viscoelastic material. Donor tissue was gently inserted into the anterior chamber using a Busin glide (Asico) and Shimazaki DSAEK forceps (Inami). Air was carefully injected into the anterior chamber to unfold the graft. Ten minutes after air injection, half of the air was replaced by balanced salt solution (Alcon). At the end of surgery, 2 mg of subconjunctival betamethasone was administered. In 42 patients with significant lens opacity (42 eyes; no eye in surgically treated glaucoma group, 2 eyes in medically treated glaucoma group, and 39 eyes without glaucoma group), standard phacoemulsification and aspiration were performed with implantation of an intraocular lens, followed by the DSAEK procedure. All DSAEK procedures were successful and uneventful, without any excessive intraoperative manipulation. In 21 eyes (2 eyes in surgically treated glaucoma group, 10 eyes in medically treated glaucoma group, and 9 eyes without glaucoma group), early postoperative double chamber necessitated air injection and resolved without any serious complications. There were 2 cases with graft rejection up to the 6-year follow-up (2 eyes; 1 eye in surgically treated glaucoma group, 1 eye in medically treated glaucoma group, and no eye without glaucoma group). Patients were prescribed topical moxifloxacin eyedrops (Vegamox, Alcon) and betamethasone 0.1% eyedrops (Sanbetazon, Santen) 5 times a day. Topical betamethasone eyedrops were tapered over the following 6 months. From 6 months after DSAEK, fluorometholone 0.1% eyedrops (Flumetholone 0.1, Santen) were prescribed 3 times daily until 24 months postoperatively, and then tapered thereafter.

AqH Samples

The AqH samples (70 - 300 μL) were obtained under sterile conditions at the beginning of surgery after topical anesthesia in DSAEK and cataract surgeries. First, a paracentesis was placed at the clear cornea. An AqH sample was obtained using a 27-gauge needle taking care not to touch the iris, the lens, or corneal endothelium. The samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 5 minutes. The soluble fractions were collected and stored at –80°C until analysis.

Total Protein and Cytokine Level Measurements

The protein concentrations of AqH samples were determined using the DC protein assay (Bio-Rad).21 In brief, bovine serum albumin was used as a standard in the range of 0.23 to 1.37 mg/mL. Samples (5 μL) of bovine serum albumin standards and AqH were added to 96-well microplates, followed by immediate addition of a mixture containing 25 μL reagent A and 200 μL reagent C. After 15 minutes of incubation at room temperature in the dark, the microplates were read at 405 nm and 690 nm using a microplate reader (Model 550; Bio-Rad). The cytokine levels of interleukin (IL)-1α, IL-1β, IL-4, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12p70, IL-13, IL-17A, interferon-α, interferon-γ, monocyte chemotactic protein-1, tumor necrosis factor-α, E-selectin, P-selectin, soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1, macrophage inflammatory protein-1α, macrophage inflammatory protein-1β, and interferon gamma-induced protein-10 in AqH samples were measured using beads-based multiplex immunoassay according to previous reports.21 Briefly, 50 μL of AqH samples were incubated with antibody-coated capture beads in incubation buffer at room temperature. After 2-hour incubation, the beads were washed 3 times using washing buffer and phycoerythrin-labeled streptavidin was added for 30 minutes in the dark at room temperature. After being washed 3 times with washing buffer, the plates were resuspended in 150 μL of reading buffer, and the assays were performed using the Luminex 200 system (ProcaPlex kit, Luminex).

Data Analysis

The primary outcome of this study was graft survival after DSAEK in relation to preoperative AqH total protein levels. Secondary outcomes included the association between AqH total protein levels and postoperative ECD. Exploratory analyses were performed to evaluate the associations between AqH cytokine levels and postoperative ECD, as well as other clinical risk factors.

Routine examinations, including slit-lamp evaluation, best spectacle-corrected distance visual acuity, intraocular pressure, and ECD, were performed preoperatively and at 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 years after surgery. The ECD was measured by masked orthoptists using a specular microscopy system (EM-4000, TOMEY). Approximately 50 cells were analyzed to calculate mean cell density. Endothelial cell density was determined by the automated software of EM-4000. For eyes in which the automated cell counts failed or misidentified endothelial cells, ECD was determined manually using the center method. We analyzed ECD as absolute ECD and percentage ECD loss (%ECD loss) to assess the association with preoperative cytokine levels. Percentage ECD loss was defined as follows: %ECD loss = ([graft ECD – postoperative ECD]/graft ECD) × 100. There were 43 patients with graft failure within 6 years after DSAEK. For these eyes, ECD was defined as 300 cells/mm2 as previously reported,3,13,14,16,20,22 because the ECD could not be directly measured due to corneal edema. To explore potential cutoff values of AqH total protein levels associated with graft survival, we examined multiple candidate thresholds within the cohort and identified 0.7 mg/mL and 1.6 mg/mL as values that differentiated graft survival.

Definition of Iris Damage Severity

Iris damage was defined as the presence of iris depigmentation, laser iridotomy, or iris defects due to intraocular surgery.12 The severity of iris damage was graded based on the number of affected quadrants (range, 0–4) as assessed by slit-lamp examination.12 A score of 0 indicated no iris damage, and higher scores reflected a greater extent of iris involvement.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using Prism for Windows software (version 6.04, GraphPad Software, Inc). The D'Agostino & Pearson omnibus normality test was used to assess whether the data followed a normal distribution. Spearman rank correlation analyses were used to evaluate the correlations between AqH cytokine levels and ECD. To compare differences in protein and cytokine levels across the groups, the Mann–Whitney U test was used. For multivariate analyses, we used STATA/IC 14.0 for Windows (StataCorp LP). To assess the clinical factors that were correlated with the postoperative ECD, we selected 9 clinical factors (history of laser iridotomy, the lens status, history of corneal transplantation/glaucoma surgery, the presence of glaucoma, central corneal thickness, iris damage severity, graft size, preoperative steroid use, and donor age) and conducted multiple linear regression analyses. The presence of glaucoma, preoperative steroid use, history of laser iridotomy/glaucoma surgery, and the lens status were dichotomized as independent variables. To account for repeated measurements of ECD within the same eye over time, longitudinal postoperative ECD was analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model. Group, time, and their interaction were included as fixed effects, and eye was included as a random effect. Cox proportional hazards model was used to identify the clinical factors that were correlated with graft survival. The protein cutoff values were derived from the study cohort and used for exploratory analyses rather than as validated clinical thresholds. The data are expressed as means ± standard deviation. P values <0.05 were considered to be statistically significant except cytokine data. Cytokine data were additionally controlled with Bonferroni correction. Because there were 20 different comparisons (1 protein and 19 cytokines), P values <0.0025 (i.e., P = 0.05/20) were considered to be statistically significant after Bonferroni correction.

Results

Study Participants

We analyzed 181 eyes that underwent DSAEK: surgically treated glaucoma (32 eyes), medically treated glaucoma (17 eyes), and no glaucoma (132 eyes). The iris damage severity and the number of previous intraocular surgeries were higher in the surgically treated glaucoma group than in the other groups (both P < 0.001).

Preoperative and Postoperative Visual Acuity and ECD

Best-corrected visual acuity (logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution) significantly improved after DSAEK in all groups (Table 2, P < 0.001). Logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution was significantly worse in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma compared with the other 2 groups, preoperatively (P = 0.015) and at 3 years (P = 0.040). However, there were no significant differences in logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution among the 3 groups at other time points due to the small number of subjects (all P > 0.05). Although baseline graft ECD did not differ significantly among the groups (Table 3, P = 0.460), longitudinal analysis using a linear mixed-effects model demonstrated significant effects of group (P < 0.001), time (P < 0.001), and group-by-time interaction (P < 0.001), indicating that postoperative ECD decline was significantly greater in the surgically treated glaucoma group.

Preoperative Total Protein and Cytokine Levels in AqH

Total protein levels in the AqH were significantly higher in the surgically treated glaucoma group than in the no-glaucoma group (P = 0.001). The surgically treated glaucoma group had higher levels of soluble intracellular adhesion molecule-1 and IL-17A compared with the no-glaucoma group (P = 0.009 and P = 0.019) and compared with the medically treated glaucoma group (P = 0.049), although those differences did not reach statistically significant with Bonferroni correction (Table 4). Of note, the total protein levels showed negative correlations with ECD at 2, 4, and 6 years after DSAEK (r = –0.355, P < 0.001; r = –0.422, P < 0.001; r = –0.292, P = 0.025, respectively).

Postoperative ECD was significantly associated with the presence of glaucoma (Table 5, r = –0.271, P = 0.004 at 2 years, r = –0.398, P < 0.0001 at 4 years, and r = –0.386, P = 0.001 at 6 years), history of glaucoma surgery (r = –0.304, P = 0.001 at 2 years, r = –0.350, P = 0.002 at 4 years, and r = –0.300, P = 0.016 at 6 years), and iris damage severity (r = –0.329 at 2 years, r = –0.391 at 4 years, and r = –0.370 at 6 years; all P ≤ 0.002). The presence of the intraocular lens was significantly correlated with lower ECD at 2, 4, and 6 years (all P < 0.001).

Multivariable Predictors of Graft Survival

In Cox proportional hazards models (covariates: age, trabeculectomy, donor age, and iris damage severity), iris damage severity (hazard ratio = 1.514; 95% confidence interval 1.224–1.871; P < 0.001) and trabeculectomy (hazard ratio = 2.247; 95% confidence interval 1.133–4.455; P = 0.020) were associated with a higher risk of graft failure. Age and donor age were not significant factors (Table 6).

Postoperative Graft Survival and Preoperative Aqueous Protein

The long-term graft survival in eyes without glaucoma was significantly better than that in surgically and medically treated glaucoma groups (Fig 2A, P < 0.001). Graft survival was poor in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma; however, the underlying cause remains unclear. Thus, we sought to determine exploratory cut-off values of preoperative total protein levels in the AqH that can be used as predictive biomarkers for graft survival in such eyes with surgically treated glaucoma as shown in Figure 1. Interestingly, we found that Kaplan–Meier curves stratified by AqH total protein levels (<0.7, 0.7–1.6, and >1.6 mg/mL: n = 4, 17, and 10, respectively) demonstrated significant separation not only in the surgically treated glaucoma group (Fig 2B, log-rank test, P = 0.0033) but also in eyes without prior glaucoma surgery (P = 0.04).

Discussion

The annual reduction rate of ECD varies among the conditions: 0.6% in normal eyes,23 2.5% after cataract surgery,5 2.6% to 7.8% after PK,6,22,24 and 5% to 15% after glaucoma surgery.25,26 Of note, in pseudophakic bullous keratopathy, the trend of graft CEnC reduction persists even after corneal transplantation, although substantial interindividual variability exists.27 The decline of CEnCs is a clinically relevant issue, especially in eyes after glaucoma surgeries, as it is the leading cause of visual dysfunction and graft endothelial failure after corneal transplantation.4,9,10

In the current study, the graft survival rate was lower in the surgically treated glaucoma group, compared with eyes with medically treated or no glaucoma. In previous studies, the graft survival rate after EK has ranged from 27% to 91.3% in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma with higher rates associated with shorter mean follow-up durations.28, 29, 30, 31 Bonnet et al28 reported that the secondary graft failure rates at a mean of 4 years after Descemet membrane EK were 41.6% in eyes with prior glaucoma surgery, 0% in eyes with medically treated glaucoma, and 2.4% in eyes without glaucoma. Sorkin et al29 reported that the graft survival rate at 4 years after Descemet membrane EK was 27% in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma and 17% in eyes with a glaucoma drainage device as compared with 88% in control eyes treated for FECD. Notably, a common finding in these previous reports is that ECD continues to decline faster over time in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma as compared with FECD or nonglaucoma eyes.27, 28, 29, 30, 31 Furthermore, although the prognosis is definitely poor in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma, the survival rate was not 0% in all reports, suggesting that there are interindividual differences even among the eyes with surgically treated glaucoma. Therefore, one question arises: what is the cause of the interindividual difference in graft survival among surgically treated glaucoma eyes? Thus, we hypothesized that there might be differences in AqH microenvironment among the patients with surgically treated glaucoma.

Physiologically, the AqH has a unique composition that includes high levels of ascorbate, glutathione, and other bioactive substances to maintain homeostasis of the anterior segment of the eye.32 In addition, the total protein level in the AqH is around 0.35 mg/mL in normal eyes and increases with disruption of the blood-aqueous barrier, such as in uveitis, after intraocular surgery or with iris damage.33 Rosenfeld et al34 reported a fivefold to 10-fold increase in AqH total protein levels in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma compared with eyes undergoing cataract surgery, although their analysis involved depletion of albumin and immunoglobulins to conduct proteomic profiling. While the AqH total protein levels are therefore not directly comparable, both studies consistently demonstrate substantially elevated AqH protein levels after glaucoma surgery. In the current study, preoperative AqH total protein levels ranged from 0.16 to 4.649 mg/mL among patients with surgically treated glaucoma. Furthermore, graft survival differed among patients with total protein levels <0.7 mg/mL, 0.7 to 1.6 mg/mL, and >1.6 mg/mL both in eyes with and without surgically treated glaucoma. These findings suggest that elevated AqH total protein levels reflect a pathological AqH microenvironment that adversely affects graft survival. However, in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma, graft survival was poorer, potentially reflecting a more severely altered aqueous milieu related to chronic disruption of the blood-aqueous barrier, the presence of filtering blebs, or persistent intraocular inflammation. Therefore, preoperative AqH total protein level may serve as a predictive biomarker for the graft survival in patients with surgically treated glaucoma.

We also found that the rate of ECD reduction after DSAEK was significantly associated with preoperative AqH total protein levels. Elevated levels of cytokines in the AqH have been reported in various ocular diseases, such as FECD,35 glaucoma,21 ocular surface diseases,36 and graft rejection.37 Matsumae et al reported that the elevations of total protein and soluble intracellular adhesion molecule-1 were associated with ECD reduction after PK and DSAEK.20,22 However, the association between ECD reduction and cytokine levels did not reach statistical significance (data not shown). This may be partly attributable to the fact that the cytokine elevation patterns differ among etiologies, as this study included various causative conditions, such as FECD, postglaucoma surgery, and pseudophakic bullous keratopathy/aphakic bullous keratopathy.21 Thus, AqH total protein level, rather than individual cytokines, may serve as a more comprehensive index to predict the fate of CEnC after EK.

Univariate analyses showed that preoperative steroid use was negatively correlated with postoperative ECD (Table 4), which is not consistent with the short-term results reported in our previous findings, which showed that there were no significant differences in cytokine levels between patients who did and did not use topical steroids preoperatively.12, 13, 14,21 The current study might have been subject to bias because patients with severe conditions were more likely to use steroid eye drops, whereas some patients with mild bullous keratopathy did not. Thus, further study is needed to assess how aqueous cytokine levels and cell loss after DSAEK are affected by the use of various postoperative topical steroid regimens. Furthermore, the detailed responses of CEnCs to the chronic high AqH protein levels remain elusive. Which intracellular pathways are involved in CEnCs loss, oxidative stress,38 mitochondrial damage,39 endoplasmic reticulum stress,40 or cell senescence?41 If we elucidate the abnormal cell responses to inflamed AqH, it could lead to the development of prophylactic therapies.

This study had several limitations. First, the underlying mechanisms of CEnC loss in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma were not elucidated. Although AqH total protein levels were associated with ECD loss after DSAEK and graft failure, these finding likely reflect a pathological AqH microenvironment rather than a direct causal mechanism. In addition, the observed correlations with ECD were moderate, explaining only a limited proportion (approximately ≤10%) of the variance. Second, surgical trauma may influence AqH total protein levels, potentially confounding their association with ECD. Future studies assessing postoperative AqH total protein levels would help clarifying these associations. Third, the sample sizes of the medically treated (n = 17) and surgically treated (n = 32) glaucoma subgroups were relatively small and included heterogeneous surgical procedures. This may have limited statistical power and increased the risk of model overfitting; therefore, these findings should be considered exploratory and require validation in large cohorts. Fourth, the protein cutoff values were derived empirically within this cohort and were not predefined a priori, and thus should be interpreted as exploratory. External validation in larger cohorts will be required before clinical applicable. Fifth, ECD in failed grafts was imputed as 300 cells/mm2, which may not reflect biological variability and could influence associations with clinical factors; therefore, graft survival was considered the primary outcome. Finally, this is a single-center study in a Japanese cohort, which may limit generalizability. Validation in diverse populations is warranted. In addition, the severity of glaucoma in the medically treated group was not systematically assessed, which may have confounded comparisons of ECD outcomes between groups.

In conclusion, although DSAEK improved visual acuity even in eyes with surgically treated glaucoma, the graft survival rate in Japan was still 25% at 6 years, which is consistent with previous reports.28, 29, 30, 31 We also found that the graft survival worsened as preoperative AqH total protein levels increased and the cutoff levels of 0.7 and 1.6 mg/mL may serve as predictive biomarkers for the graft survival after DSAEK after glaucoma surgery.

A symptotic charges of p − p- forms and their dualities in any D D
Dario Francia, Federico Manzoni

Abstract
We compute the surface charges associated to p − p- form gauge fields in arbitrary spacetime dimension for large values of the radial coordinate. In the critical dimension where radiation and Coulomb falloff coincide we find asymptotic charges involving asymptotic parameters, i.e. parameters with a component of order zero in the radial coordinate. However, in different dimensions we still find nontrivial asymptotic charges now involving parameters that are not asymptotic times the radiation-order fields. For p p =1 and D > 4 D>4 , our charges thus differ from those presented in the literature. We then show that under Hodge duality “electric” charges for p − p- forms are mapped to “magnetic” charges for the dual q − q- forms, with q = D − p − 2 q=D-p-2 . For charges involving fields with radiation falloffs the duality relates charges that are finite and nonvanishing. For the case of Coulomb falloffs, above or below the critical dimension, Hodge duality exchanges overleading charges in one theory with subleading ones in its dual counterpart.

Asymptotic charges

Following Noether’s second theorem
[

]

the charges take the form

Q=\int_{\Sigma}j^{\mu}d\Sigma_{\mu}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}k^{\mu\nu}d\sigma_{\mu\nu},

where in the case of interest for us we integrate over the sphere
S^{D-2}
at null infinity,
i.e.
keeping

k^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{p-1}}H^{\mu\nu\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{p-1}}.

In Bondi coordinates the integration measure in (

4
The duality map

Gauge fields described by
p-
forms and
(D-p-2)-
forms provide
independent irreps of
GL(D)
, whenever they both exist, associated to gauge symmetries that are also independent. However, in force of their on-shell duality, one expects that any charge computed in one theory should have a counterpart in the dual partner theory. The true issue at stake is to explore whether Hodge-duality connecting asymptotic charges allows one to highlight sectors of the asymptotic symmetry group of a theory that are only visible, or more easily identified, from the dual perspective. The prototypical instance of this phenomenon is provided by the dual pair (scalar field)-(two form) in
D=4
, where one can indeed construct asymptotic charges for the scalar theory but it is only through Hodge duality that the connection to asymptotic symmetries can be made apparent
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
9
]
. For the case of electromagnetism in
D=4
the same issue was investigated in
[
16
]
, while
p-
forms in the critical dimension in where considered in
[
18
,
19
]
and in
AdS_{D}
in
[
28
]
.

Our main goal in this section is to derive a duality map between
p-
form charges and
(D-p-2)-
form charges making use of the duality map connecting the corresponding field strengths in different descriptions. We consider first the asymptotic charges (
51
), displaying a well-defined and non-vanishing asymptotic limit in any
D
, to then extend our considerations to the charges (
58
), for which the duality map will relate vanishing (divergent) charges in one description to divergent (vanishing) charges in its dual counterpart. The map that we implement in this work relates electric charges in one setup to magnetic charges in its dual counterpart. An alternative procedure, meant to define a map relating electric charges to electric charges will be proposed in
[
29
]
.

Let us consider first the case of the dual pair (

The charges involving the radiation order are

\displaystyle Q^{r}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\epsilon^{(\frac{1}{2})}(x^{i})F_{ru}^{(\frac{5}{2})}d\Omega,

\displaystyle=\int\gamma^{ij}\epsilon_{i}^{(-\frac{1}{2})}(x^{i})H_{ruj}^{(\frac{3}{2})}d\Omega,

\begin{split}F&=-\star H,\\

\begin{split}F_{\alpha\beta}&=-\frac{r^{3}}{6}g^{\rho\tilde{\rho}}g^{\nu\tilde{\nu}}g^{\mu\tilde{\mu}}H_{\mu\nu\rho}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\mu}\tilde{\nu}\tilde{\rho}\alpha\beta}\sqrt{\gamma},\\

that allow one to relate the asymptotic electric and magnetic vector potentials, up to an angle dependent integration constant which can be fixed in such a way that
A_{i}^{(\frac{1}{2})}
and

be proportional.

A natural dual map between the charges can be obtained exploiting (
63

Upon identifying

In the general case we consider the electric charges

\displaystyle Q^{r}_{p}=\int\gamma^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots\gamma^{i_{p-1}j_{p-1}}\epsilon_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}^{(\frac{D-(2p+2)}{2})}(x^{i})H_{ruj_{1}\cdots j_{p-1}}^{(\frac{D-2p+2}{2})})d\Omega;

\displaystyle Q^{r}_{q}\to\tilde{Q}^{r}_{p}=\int\frac{\varepsilon^{i_{1}\cdots i_{q-1}j_{1}\cdots j_{p+1}}{\epsilon_{i_{1}\cdots i_{D-p-3}}^{(-\frac{D-(2p+2)}{2})}(x^{i})}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}H_{j_{1}\cdots j_{p+1}}^{(\frac{D-(2p+2)}{2})}d\Omega,

(73)

where

\epsilon_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q-1}}^{(\frac{D-(2q+2)}{2})}=\epsilon_{i_{1}\cdots i_{D-p-3}}^{-\frac{D-(2p+2)}{2}}

\begin{split}Q^{r}_{p}&=\tilde{Q}^{r}_{q},\\

4.2
Duality map at Coulomb order

For the charges (
58
) involving field components with Coulomb fall-offs one can formally reproduce the same steps, with the proviso that one carefully takes into account the different orders entering the charges.

The Coulombic magnetic charge in the
p-
form theory dual to
Q^{C}_{q}
is then given by

\tilde{Q}^{C}_{p}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int\frac{\varepsilon^{i_{1}\cdots i_{q-1}j_{1}\cdots j_{p+1}}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\frac{\epsilon_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q-1}}^{({D-(2q+2))}}}{r^{-(D-2p-2)}}(x^{i})H_{j_{1}\cdots j_{p+1}}^{({D-(2p+2)})}d\Omega,

(78)

This charge is divergent in
D>2p+2
and vanishing in
D<2p+2
, which is the opposite behavior with respect to the charges (
58
): when the Coulombic electric charge (
58
) vanishes the corresponding magnetic charge (
78
) diverges, and viceversa. Therefore a charge that in a given theory is subleading with respect to the charge with radiation falloffs is mapped to an overleading charge in the dual description.
One simple instance is again the dual pair two-form and scalar feld in
D=4
. In that case the overleading
\mathcal{O}(r^{2})
2-form charge is mapped to a subleading scalar charge of order
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
. Scalar charges acting at
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
appear, for example, in
[
30
]
in connection with the pion memory effect.

5
Outlook

Our asymptotic charges (
56
) at radiation order, based on non-asymptotic parameters, seemingly provide an alternative to the construction of charges based on overleading parameters,
i.e.
parameters involving an
{{\cal{O}}(r^{0})}
component in any
D
, typically introduced as pure gauge cotributions in order to account for the existence of asymptotic symmetries in
D>4

[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
25
]
. In this respect it would be interesting to understand to what kind of observables, if any, should those charges be related. Their actual meaning indeed is not fully clear at a first glance, since on the one hand they would be expected to generate large gauge transformations on
\cal{I}
, but on the other hand they would seemingly do so exploiting a gauge parameter that does not get to null infinity!

An obvious generalization of our results would involve a similar exploration for gauge fields of spin
s\geq 2
. The most direct goal would be that of constructing a new set of putative observable asymptotic quantitities. In addition, one would like to explore the possibility that hidden sectors of the asymptotic symmetry group of gravity in
D>4
, or more generally of higher-spin fields, may be visible, or at least better apparent, if explored form the perspective of the various dual formulations admissible
[
35
,
36
]
.

Appendix A
Lorenz gauge for
p=1,2

The Laplace-Beltrami operator in Bondi coordinates reads

\Box=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}=-2\nabla_{u}\nabla_{r}+\nabla_{r}\nabla_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\,.

(79)

Let us begin by discussing the scalar gauge-for-gauge parameter. The homogeneous wave equation reads

-2\nabla_{u}\nabla_{r}\epsilon+\nabla_{r}\nabla_{r}\epsilon+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\epsilon=0\,.

(80)

Making explicit the covariant derivatives we get

\bigg[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}-\frac{(D-2)}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}\bigg]\epsilon=0,

(81)

where
\Delta:=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{D-2}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the
(D-2)-
sphere. Inserting a polyhomogeneous expansion gives us

\displaystyle[(l-1)(l-D+2)+\Delta]\tilde{\epsilon}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}^{(l)}=0;

(82)

\displaystyle[(l-1)(l-D+2)+\Delta]{\epsilon}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}\epsilon^{(l)}-2\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}^{(l)}+(D-2l-1)\tilde{\epsilon}^{(l-1)}=0;

for future convenience we define

k^{(p=1)}(l):=(l-1)(l-D+2).

(83)

For the vector gauge parameter the procedure is very similar. The wave equation reads

-2\nabla_{u}\nabla_{r}\epsilon_{\alpha}+\nabla_{r}\nabla_{r}\epsilon_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\epsilon_{\alpha}=0\,.

(84)

Making explicit the covariant derivatives we get

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}-\frac{(D-2)}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}\bigg]\epsilon_{u}=0,

(85)

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}-\frac{(D-2)}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}\bigg]\epsilon_{r}+\frac{D-2}{r^{2}}(\epsilon_{u}-\epsilon_{r})-\frac{2}{r^{3}}\nabla^{i}\epsilon_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{r}^{2}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}-\frac{(D-4)}{r}(\partial_{u}-\partial_{r})+\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}\bigg]\epsilon_{i}-\frac{D-3}{r^{3}}\epsilon_{i}-\frac{2}{r}\nabla_{i}(\epsilon_{u}-\epsilon_{r})=0.

First we note that the equation for
\epsilon_{u}
is the same as the one for the scalar gauge parameter due to the lack of connection symbols with
u
as down index; then inserting the polyhomogeneous expansion for the other components of the vector gauge parameter we get

\displaystyle[(l-1)(l-D+2)+\Delta]\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l)}=0;

(86a)

\displaystyle[(l-1)(l-D+2)+\Delta]{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}\epsilon_{u}^{(l)}-2\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l)}

\displaystyle+(D-2l-1)\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l-1)}=0;

(86b)

\displaystyle[l(l+1-D)+\Delta]\tilde{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l)}+(D-2)\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l-1)}-2\nabla^{i}\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{(l-2)}=0;

(86c)

\displaystyle[l(l+1-D)+\Delta]{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+2)\partial_{u}{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l)}+(D-2){\epsilon}_{u}^{(l-1)}-2\nabla^{i}{\epsilon}_{i}^{(l-2)}

\displaystyle+(D-2l-1)\tilde{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l-1)}-2\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l)}=0;

(86d)

\displaystyle[l(l-D+3)-1+\Delta]\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{(l-1)}+(2l-D+4)\partial_{u}\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{(l)}+2\nabla_{i}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{r}^{(l)}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{u}^{(l)})=0;

(86e)

Appendix C

\displaystyle\sum B:=\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{s-1}\partial_{i_{s}}B_{rui_{1}\cdots i_{s-1}i_{s+1}\cdots i_{p-1}}

Inserting a generic polyhomogeneous expansion, we get the order by order relations

\displaystyle[k_{r}^{(p)}(l)+\Delta]\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+A_{1}\partial_{u}\tilde{B}^{(l)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+A_{2}\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}_{ui_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+\sum\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}-2\nabla^{i}\tilde{B}^{(l-2)}_{ii_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}=0;

(132)

\displaystyle[k_{r}^{(p)}(l)+\Delta]B^{(l-1)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+A_{1}\partial_{u}B^{(l)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+A_{2}B^{(l-1)}_{ui_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}+\sum B^{(l-1)}-2\nabla^{i}B^{(l-2)}_{ii_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}

\displaystyle+A_{3}\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}-2\partial_{u}\tilde{B}^{(l)}_{ri_{1}\cdots i_{p-1}}=0;

where

\displaystyle k_{r}^{(p)}(l):=l(l-1-D+2p)-4p+p^{2}+3;

(133)

\displaystyle A_{1}:=2l-D+2p;

\displaystyle A_{2}:=D-2p;

\displaystyle A_{3}:=D-2l+2p-1;

\displaystyle\sum\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}:=\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{s}\partial_{i_{s}}\tilde{B}^{(l-1)}_{rui_{1}\cdots i_{s-1}i_{s+1}\cdots i_{p-1}};

\displaystyle\sum B^{(l-1)}:=\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{s}\partial_{i_{s}}B^{(l-1)}_{rui_{1}\cdots i_{s-1}i_{s+1}\cdots i_{p-1}}.

Now, taking the current falloffs into account, the first order that satisfy the first of (

Appendix D
A useful result

Let us consider the following differential equation system

\displaystyle[-k_{1}+\Delta]f-2\nabla^{i}h_{i}=0;

(136a)

\displaystyle[-k_{2}+\Delta]h_{i}+2\nabla_{i}f=0;

(136b)

where
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are parametric positive numbers while
f
and
h_{i}
sufficiently smooth arbitrary functions. This kind of system is the one appearing in (
106
), (
108
), (
114
) and, if logarithmic terms would suppressed, in (
117
).

Taking the gradient of equation (
136b
) we get

2\Delta f-k_{2}\nabla^{i}h_{i}+\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}h_{i}=0;

(137)

where, due to curvature effects, we cannot exchange covariant derivatives. However using the Riemann tensor components identity
[\nabla^{i},\nabla^{j}]h_{i}=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}h_{i}-\nabla^{j}\nabla^{i}h_{i}=R^{k}_{iij}h_{k}=g^{ks}R_{siij}h_{j}
we can go on. Indeed

\displaystyle\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}(\nabla_{j}h_{i})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{j}\nabla^{i}(\nabla_{j}h_{i})+R^{j}_{kij}(\nabla^{k}h^{i})+R^{i}_{kij}(\nabla^{j}h^{k})

(138)

\displaystyle=R^{j}_{kij}(\nabla^{k}h^{i})+R^{i}_{kij}(\nabla^{j}h^{k})+\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}h^{i}+\nabla^{j}(R^{i}_{kij}h^{k})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}h^{i}-R_{ki}(\nabla^{k}h^{i})+R_{kj}(\nabla^{j}h^{k})+\nabla^{j}(R_{kj}h^{k})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}h^{i}+\nabla^{j}(R_{kj}h^{k});

but the Riemann tensor components for a maximally symmetric space are given by

R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=g_{\alpha\gamma}g_{\beta\delta}-g_{\alpha\delta}g_{\beta\gamma}

(139)

from which, using the metric of the
(D-2)-
sphere
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
we get

R_{\beta\delta}=\gamma^{\alpha\gamma}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=(D-2)\gamma_{\beta\delta}-\delta^{\gamma}_{\delta}\gamma_{\beta\gamma}=(D-3)\gamma_{\beta\delta}.

(140)

Therefore

\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}(\nabla_{j}h_{i})=\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}h_{i}+(D-3)\nabla^{i}h_{i}.

(141)

Hence, returning to equation (
137
) we can write

2\Delta f+[\Delta+(D-3-k_{2})]\nabla^{i}h_{i}=0;

(142)

from which

\Delta f=-\frac{1}{2}[\Delta+\bar{k}_{2}]\nabla^{i}h_{i},

(143)

where we defined
\bar{k}_{2}:=(D-3-k_{2})
.
Now, inserting the expression for
\Delta f
from above in equation (
136a
) we get

-\frac{1}{2}[\Delta+\bar{k}_{2}]\nabla^{i}h_{i}=2\nabla^{i}h_{i}+k_{1}f;

(144)

from which

f=-\frac{[\Delta+\bar{k}_{2}+4]}{2k_{1}}\nabla^{i}h_{i},

(145)

and reinserting it back in the original equation we have

\displaystyle-[-k_{1}+\Delta]\frac{[\Delta+\bar{k}_{2}+4]}{2k_{1}}\nabla^{i}h_{i}-2\nabla^{i}h_{i}=0\Rightarrow[\Delta^{2}+(\bar{k}_{2}-k_{1}+4)\Delta-k_{1}\bar{k}_{2}]\nabla^{i}h_{i}=0.

(146a)

However, in similar way, we can find from equation (
136a
)

\nabla^{i}h_{i}=\frac{1}{2}[-k_{1}+\Delta]f;

(147)

and substituting it in equation (
137
) to get

2\Delta f=-\frac{1}{2}[\Delta+\bar{k}_{2}][-k_{1}+\Delta]f\Rightarrow[\Delta^{2}+(\bar{k}_{2}-k_{1}+4)\Delta-k_{1}\bar{k}_{2}]f=0.

(148)

Therefore functions
\nabla^{i}h_{i}
and
f
satisfy the same differential equation; moreover the full biharmonic equation on the ball under sufficiently smooth hypothesis of the functions admits a unique solution. Therefore we conclude that

f=\nabla^{i}h_{i};

(149)

and using this information in equation (
136a
) we have

[-k_{1}-2+\Delta]f=0\Rightarrow f=0,

(150)

and using it in equation (
136b
) we get

[-k_{2}+\Delta]h_{i}=0\Rightarrow h_{i}=0.

(151)

In the steps above if
k_{1}
is null the equality (
145
) is not defined and an alternative way is needed. We can start again form equation (
143
) and (
136a
) with
k_{1}=0
to get

[\Delta^{2}+(\bar{k}_{2}+4)\Delta]f=0;

(152)

while form the laplacian of (
144
) with
k_{1}=0
we get

[\Delta^{2}+(\bar{k}_{2}+4)\Delta]\nabla^{i}h_{i}=0.

(153)

Hence, again

f=\nabla^{i}h_{i},

(154)

and from (
136a
) with
k_{1}=0
we deduce that

[-2+\Delta]f=0\Rightarrow f=0,

(155)

and using it in (
136b
) we get the same result in (
151
).
If
k_{2}=0

\bar{k}_{2}=D-3

Design of a miniaturized 90-degree quadrature hybrid coupler with harmonic suppression ability using π-shaped lumped elements
Authors: Saeid MahdiAbadi, Saeed Roshani, Fariborz Parandin, Sobhan Roshani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research presents a new design and fabrication of a quadrature hybrid coupler using π-shaped lumped elements. The proposed π-shaped elements have resulted in ultra-size reduction and harmonic suppression ability simultaneously. The proposed coupler correctly works at 800 MHz, the insertion loss is less than 0.15 dB and more than 26 dB, isolation and return loss are obtained at the operating frequency. The size of the proposed coupler is only 12.8 mm ×12.2 mm, which is equal to 0.046 λg × 0.044 λg, where λg is of the guided-wavelength at 800 MHz. The conventional coupler has a large size of 0.25 λg × 0.25 λg, which is equal to 68.5 mm × 68.5 mm. The proposed coupler provides about 96% size reduction compared to the conventional coupler. Moreover, the proposed quadrature hybrid coupler has wide harmonic suppression from 2.1 GHz to 10 GHz.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Hybrid couplers are popularly used in many microwave and RF circuit designs for signal splitting, combining, and power monitoring. Their popularity owes to their simplicity, wide bandwidth, low insertion loss, and excellent performance in terms of isolation and return loss. Research in quadrature hybrid coupler design and application is continually evolving, leading to the development of new models with improved performance levels.

Recent studies focused on improving the properties of branch line couplers and their applications in various fields.

Stepped impedances1 and open subs2,3 are commonly used techniques for achieving size reduction and harmonic suppression in coupler structures. Discontinuous microstrip lines were used in a quadrature hybrid coupler (QHC) presented in4, resulting in a 60% size reduction but without the ability to suppress unwanted harmonics. The nonsymmetrical QHC presented in5 utilized quasi-lumped and T-shaped structures, achieving a 70% size reduction but also lacking harmonic suppression. In contrast, the QHC presented in6 used resonant cells to achieve an 80% size reduction while suppressing the 3rd up to 7th harmonics. Stepped impedances were used in a QHC presented in7, resulting in a 50% size reduction but without harmonic rejection. The QHC presented in8 utilized high impedance lines and internal shunt capacitors for harmonic suppression, resulting in about 74% size reduction but with an undesirable high value insertion loss of about 1 dB and only second harmonic rejection. A squared coupler was presented in9, utilizing the slow wave technique for 2nd harmonic suppression. A wideband three-section QHC was presented in10, achieving high size reduction but only suppressing the 2nd harmonic. Open subs have also been widely used for harmonic suppression and size reduction in coupler structures11–18. These stubs can produce transmission zeros and provide a wide stopband while reducing the overall size of the coupler. In19, a high-directivity coupler is presented using three pairs of coupled-line sections and one pair of resistors, operating at 2 GHz. This directional coupler exhibits good return losses of more than 28 dB, but suffers from high insertion loss. A high-performance quadrature hybrid coupler (QHC) operating at 2270 MHz with a compact size is reported in20. The design demonstrates high fabrication tolerances due to its simplicity and uncomplicated dimensions. The reported coupler has acceptable return loss and insertion loss in the passband, but unwanted harmonics are not suppressed. In21, a lumped-element wideband directional coupler with compact size and harmonic suppression is presented. This coupler has four filtering networks and an asymmetric branch-line hybrid acting as a matching network. This compact coupler operates at 0.5 GHz, with harmonic suppression over − 30 dB from 0.69 to 4.5 GHz. However, it suffers from high insertion loss and a complicated fabrication process.

Neural networks are also widely used to design devices with optimized performance in microwave circuits22,23 and other electronic devices. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are computational models inspired by the human brain’s neural networks24,25. ANNs are used for a variety of tasks where pattern recognition, prediction, and optimization are crucial26–30. Predictions and estimations can also be executed using other mathematical models31–33, but neural networks often outperform other related models. Neural network and machine learning methods can identify patterns and nonlinearity in various problems34–39.

In addition to the design of microwave circuits, neural networks are also widely used in the design of other electronic configuration like photonic crystal structures. Designing these structures requires precise control over their photonic band gaps, which determine the wavelengths of light they can block or transmit. Traditionally, designing these structures involved extensive simulations and iterative adjustments, which can be computationally expensive and time-consuming40. However, using artificial neural networks (ANNs), this process can be significantly accelerated41.

Meta-structures and metamaterials42–44 are new techniques used in modern high-frequency microwave circuits, such as couplers, dividers, absorbers45, and other types of passive circuits. However, they have complex structures that are difficult to fabricate.

In this work, the proposed coupler is designed at 800 MHz. The 800 MHz band is a critical frequency range utilized in various wireless communication systems, particularly for mobile telephony and public safety communications. In mobile telephony, this band is integral to the operation of 2G and 3G networks, providing extensive coverage and reliable signal penetration, which are essential for both urban and rural areas. The superior propagation characteristics of the 800 MHz band allow signals to travel longer distances and penetrate buildings more effectively compared to higher frequency bands, resulting in fewer cell sites needed to achieve comprehensive coverage. This makes it an ideal choice for telecommunications operators aiming to deliver robust and cost-effective voice and data services to a wide user base. Additionally, the lower frequency helps in minimizing the power consumption of mobile devices. Beyond mobile telephony, the 800 MHz band is extensively used in public safety and emergency services communications. Public safety agencies, including police, fire, and emergency medical services, rely on this frequency band to ensure reliable and immediate communication during critical situations. The enhanced propagation characteristics of the 800 MHz band are particularly beneficial in ensuring consistent and clear communication in challenging environments such as densely populated urban areas or remote rural regions46. The reliable performance and broad coverage capabilities of the 800 MHz band make it indispensable for both commercial and public safety applications, underscoring the importance of advancements in technology, such as the miniaturized 90-degree quadrature hybrid coupler with harmonic suppression ability, to optimize its usage.

In this design a miniaturized quadrature hybrid coupler with harmonic suppression using lumped elements is improved. The novelty of this work lies in the development of a quadrature hybrid coupler with significantly reduced size and enhanced harmonic suppression. The use of π-shaped lumped elements enables the compact size and harmonic suppression ability, while maintaining good performance at the operating frequency. This design achieves a size reduction of approximately 96% compared to conventional couplers, making it highly compact and suitable for miniaturized wireless communication systems. Additionally, the wide harmonic suppression range from 2.1 GHz to 10 GHz further enhances the versatility and applicability of the proposed coupler.

In the design of microwave couplers, the use of lumped reactive components like capacitors and inductors alongside microstrip lines can lead to significant advantages such as size reduction and harmonic suppression. However, these lumped components also introduce certain limitations, particularly at higher frequencies. As the frequency increases, lumped elements can exhibit parasitic effects, such as self-resonance and loss, which degrade the performance of the coupler. Additionally, lumped components are often sensitive to manufacturing tolerances and require precise soldering, making them prone to variability in performance. This sensitivity, along with the increased complexity of integrating lumped elements with microstrip lines, can make the design more challenging and affect the overall efficiency and reliability of the coupler at higher frequency ranges.

Design process

To evaluate the effectiveness of size reduction and harmonic suppression techniques in branch line couplers, a conventional coupler was designed and simulated at 800 MHz. As shown in Fig. 1, the typical coupler consisted of two 50 Ω vertical branches with 1.56 mm width and 90º electrical length, and two 35.4 Ω horizontal branches with 2.54 mm width and 90º electrical length, resulting in an overall size of 0.25 λg × 0.25 λg (68.5 mm × 68.5 mm). While the conventional coupler performed adequately at the operating frequency, it failed to suppress unwanted harmonics at higher frequencies, leading to poor performance.

Proposed coupler at 0.8 GHz

Figure 6 shows the design procedures of the proposed coupler. As shown, the desired values of size reduction can be considered in the first step of the design. Then, based on the assumptions the values of circuit parameters can be calculated using extracted analyses. After finding the circuit parameters, the proposed structure of the coupler can be formed by replacing the conventional branches with the proposed branches.

Design procedures of the proposed coupler. Design steps are illustrated to provide the proposed device.

The proposed 800 MHz coupler features a compact structure that incorporates π-shaped lumped elements and microstrip lines, as illustrated in Fig. 7(a). This innovative design results in a compact size of 12.8 mm by 12.2 mm, which is equivalent to 0.046 λg by 0.044 λg. The integration of these components allows for a more efficient and effective coupling process, making the proposed coupler a promising solution for high-frequency applications.

Schematic diagram (a) and frequency response (b) of the proposed 800 MHz QHC Dimensions are in mm. overall size 12.8 mm×12.2 mm (0.046 λg × 0.044 λg).

Figure 7(b) illustrates the frequency response of the proposed coupler. The results indicate that the coupler performs well at the working frequency of 800 MHz and higher frequencies. In EM-simulation, the amplitudes of S12 and S13 are − 3.06 dB, indicating less than 0.06 dB of insertion loss at the working frequency. Additionally, the amplitude of S11 is approximately − 26 dB, and the amplitude of S14 is around − 31 dB, demonstrating excellent functionality for the designed coupler. At higher frequencies, the proposed coupler effectively suppresses third to 12th harmonics with a high degree of attentiveness. Moreover, it provides a wide harmonic rejection band from 2.1 GHz to 10 GHz with more than 20 dB of attenuation. Also, the proposed coupler has good performance in the frequency band of 640 MHz to 960 MHz, providing a 320 MHz operating bandwidth, which equals a 40% fractional bandwidth (FBW).

The proposed coupler was fabricated on the Duroid 5880 substrate with 2.2 permittivity and 20 mil thickness, as shown in Fig. 8. The fabricated coupler includes eight capacitors and four inductors, which were soldered onto the device. The fabricated coupler has a compact size of 12.8 mm × 12.2 mm (0.046λg × 0.044λg), which represents a remarkable 96% reduction in size compared to the conventional 800 MHz coupler.

The fabricated device was measured, and the measurement setup is depicted in Fig. 9. As shown in this figure, the Keysight handheld microwave analyzer, model N9917A, was utilized to measure S-parameters. There is a good agreement between simulations and measurements.

The results of the fabricated coupler are displayed in Fig. 10, show significant performance in both operating and higher frequency bands. The measured amplitudes of S12 and S13 demonstrate an insertion loss of less than 0.15 dB at the working frequency, with values of -3.1 dB and − 3.15 dB, respectively. The measured amplitude of S11 is approximately − 22 dB, while S14 is around − 26 dB, indicating desirable performance for the proposed coupler. Additionally, the proposed coupler effectively suppresses 3rd to 12th harmonics with high precision and provides a wide stopband from 2.1 GHz to 10 GHz with over 20 dB of attenuation. Also, it should be mentioned that, the specific configuration and reactive properties of the π-shaped lumped elements are optimized for higher-order harmonic suppression. The 2nd harmonic, being closer to the fundamental frequency of 800 MHz, interacts differently with the circuit components, making it more difficult to attenuate. While the 2nd harmonic is not as effectively suppressed as higher-order harmonics, its impact on the overall performance of the coupler is mitigated by the high isolation and return loss (> 26 dB) and the low insertion loss (< 0.15 dB) at the fundamental frequency.

Conclusion

A novel 800 MHz quadrature hybrid coupler has been developed, utilizing lumped elements to achieve an ultra-compact size. The proposed coupler features a single series inductor and two parallel capacitors in each line, resulting in a significant reduction in size. The proposed coupler occupies only 12.8 mm ×12.2 mm, (0.046 λg×0.044 λg), which shows 96% size reduction compared to the conventional one. The device exhibits excellent performance both within and outside of the operating band, with insertion loss of less than 0.15 dB, isolation of more than 26 dB, and good return loss at the working frequency. Additionally, wide suppression of harmonics from the 3rd order to 12th order harmonics is achieved. These results demonstrate the potential of the proposed coupler for high-frequency applications.

Research on industrial carbon quota allocation and optimization in Tianjin based on the ZSG-DEA
Authors: Hongmin Ren, Xuemei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummarySince the reform and opening up, China has become the world’s second largest economy and the world’s largest carbon emitter. The carbon emissions from industrial development are much higher than those of other industries. Scientific allocation of industrial carbon quotas is of great significance to industrial development and structural optimization. Taking Tianjin as an example, this paper discusses the optimization of industrial structure from the perspective of carbon quota allocation. Firstly, the grandfather allocation method is used to initially allocate the carbon quotas of Tianjin’s industrial sub-sectors in 2030. The DEA-BBC is used to measure the carbon emission efficiency under the initial quotas, and the zero-sum gain (ZSG)-DEA is used for redistribution. According to the adjustment of carbon quotas and tax contributions, the industry types are divided. According to the division results, Tianjin’s industrial structure optimization suggestions are proposed.

Introduction

Global warming caused by the greenhouse effect has become a focal point of societal concern, making it imperative to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and advance a green, low-carbon economic transition.1,2 To combat climate change and mitigate global warming trends, China proposed the “dual carbon goals” at the United Nations General Assembly in September 2020, with the goal of “striving to achieve peak carbon emissions and carbon neutrality as soon as possible”3 Industrial production has consumed vast amounts of fossil fuels, accounting for over 80% of China’s total carbon emissions since 2000. The introduction of the dual carbon goals imposes new requirements for the low-carbon transformation of industry.4,5 As a major industrial city in northern China, Tianjin has experienced rapid industrial economic growth alongside rising carbon emissions, severely constraining its industrial development. Therefore, constrained by the dual carbon goals, fundamentally changing the industrial structure to build a modern industrial framework aligned with low-carbon requirements has become a crucial pathway for Tianjin’s high-quality industrial development.

Current domestic and international research on carbon emissions and industrial structure optimization primarily employs econometric models and optimization models for analysis.6,7 Huang Li et al. utilized the ratio method and SuperSBM model to assess the upgrading of manufacturing industrial structure and the carbon emission efficiency across China’s provinces and regions from 2005 to 2019.8 Peng Qingqing et al. employed a Tobit regression model to identify the factors influencing carbon emission efficiency, and reported that promoting low-carbon energy transitions, accelerating industrial upgrading, and expanding enterprise scale are effective pathways.9 Z Cheng utilized a spatial Durbin model to demonstrate that industrial structure upgrading and optimization contribute to reducing carbon emission intensity.10 Zongke Bao et al. evaluated how ESG performance and investor attention affect the efficiency of green technology innovation of Chinese manufacturing enterprises, and provided strategic guidance for China to achieve dual-carbon transformation and sustainable industrial upgrading.11 Scholars often construct objective functions and constraints to explore specific pathways for industrial structure optimization. Chen Siyuan et al. investigated China’s industrial structure optimization using a bidirectional coupling optimization mode.12 You Zengtao, Zhou Kaile, and others conducted emission reduction studies on industrial structures under various scenarios.13,14 Zeng Weikang et al. utilized a multiobjective planning model to analyze characteristics such as the interprovincial energy structure, industrial structure, and carbon emissions under “isolated” and “collaborative” optimization strategies.15

The implementation of low-carbon development goals is closely tied to the rational allocation of carbon emissions,16,17,18 with the principles and methods for distributing carbon allocation rights serving as the foundational issue. Fairness and efficiency are key factors for prioritizing carbon quota allocation. Scholars often use per capita carbon emissions, population, and GDP (per capita GDP) as indicators of equity, reflecting regional equality in development rights. For instance, Qi Yue and Xie Gaodi reported that allocation standards that combine per capita and historical emissions better support socioeconomic development in developing countries.19 The efficiency principle emphasizes carbon reduction potential, with carbon intensity (emissions per unit of GDP) serving as a common efficiency metric.20 Some scholars also use carbon emission efficiency as an allocation indicator. For instance, Guo Ru et al. employed the zero-sum gain (ZSG)-DEA model and scenario analysis to predict the efficiency of carbon quota allocation across China’s six major industries by 2020.21 Research methodologies commonly employ bidirectional coupling optimization,8,22 ZSG-DEA,23,24 and input‒output stochastic frontier models25 for carbon quota allocation. Building upon these methodologies, studies have explored allocation at the national,25,26,27 regional,28,29,30 and sectoral31,32,33,34 levels. Liu Yunlong et al. conducted top-down provincial carbon quota allocation for the transportation sector targeting peak carbon emissions,24 and Yang Wei et al. proposed a carbon quota allocation scheme for the nonferrous metals industry aligned with the 2030 peak carbon emissions goal.3

The existing research mostly constructs a comprehensive index system to measure and evaluate the industrial structure through econometric methods, and proposes a targeted industrial structure optimization path for the measurement level of industrial structure. However, the existing research fails to effectively link up with the allocation of carbon quotas, which greatly restricts the practical guiding significance of relevant results. The contributions of this paper are as follows: (1) On the subject of allocation, scholars mostly carry out research on carbon quota allocation from industries such as electricity, steel, and construction. This paper takes 29 industrial sub-sectors as the object of carbon quota allocation, and provides the basis for policymakers to carry out carbon emission reduction in industrial sub-sectors. Secondly, compared with the traditional research on optimizing industrial structure by econometric models, this paper selects two indicators of tax payment and carbon quota adjustment to construct a carbon quota allocation scheme for industrial subdivision industries based on the ZSG-DEA model. Based on the comprehensive economic and ecological benefits, 29 subdivision industries in Tianjin are divided into four categories: high contribution and high efficiency, high contribution and low efficiency, low contribution and high efficiency, and low contribution and low efficiency. Based on the classification type, the differentiation optimization scheme of industrial structure in Tianjin is designed.

The distribution pattern shows that industrial enterprises are concentrated more in the south than in the north, with the densest clusters in Dongli District, Beichen District, Jinnan District, and Xiqing District. Current status of carbon emissions in Tianjin’s industrial sector

From 2010 to 2023, carbon emissions from Tianjin’s industrial sector followed a pattern of initial growth followed by a decline (Figure 2). Total emissions rose continuously from 2010 to 2013, peaking in 2013 at 4,947.85 × 104 t—an increase of nearly 36% from 4,107.52 × 104 t. From 2014 to 2023, total industrial carbon emissions showed a declining trend, with the lowest level recorded in 2017 at 3,393.71 × 104 t. From 2014 to 2023, Tianjin’s industrial sector reduced its total carbon emissions by 1,231.95 × 104 tons, achieving an average annual reduction rate of 2.36%. This finding indicates that the growth trend in industrial carbon emissions in Tianjin has slowed significantly. This slowdown is attributed primarily to national green development policies and evolving consumer attitudes, which have driven increased adoption of clean energy in industry. Evaluation of the outcomes and efficiency of “grandfathering” allocation

Using Equations 2 and 3, the total carbon emissions from Tianjin’s industrial sector are calculated to reach 3420.28 × 104 t in 2030. Applying the grandfathering principle to allocate the total carbon quota among 29 industrial subsectors yields the results shown in Column 3 of Table 1. The three sectors receiving the largest allocations are ferrous metal smelting and rolling, chemical raw materials and chemical product manufacturing, and nonmetallic mineral product manufacturing, with carbon quotas of 2610.81, 493.24, and 80.16 × 104 t, respectively. The sector receiving the lowest allocation was water production and supply, at only 0.39 × 104 t. This finding demonstrates that sectors with higher historical cumulative carbon emissions receive high carbon quotas for 2030.

On the basis of the initial carbon quota allocation results obtained through the grandfathering scheme, the initial carbon emission quotas for each industrial sector were used as input indicators for the DEA-BBC analysis. The output included the gross production value, total energy consumption, and average number of employees across industrial sectors. This methodology was employed to calculate the initial carbon quota efficiency for each industrial subsector by 2030. The efficiency values are shown in Figure 3. The average initial efficiency value for Tianjin’s 29 industrial subsectors is 0.48, indicating a below-average level with significant room for improvement. Moreover, substantial efficiency gaps exist across different sectors, and the vast majority of industries have not achieved DEA efficiency. Only 8 subsectors achieved an efficiency value of 1 (including nonmetallic mineral products; metal products; transportation equipment manufacturing; computer, communications, and other electronic equipment manufacturing; and instrument and meters manufacturing), indicating that they have reached DEA efficiency. A few sectors exceeded the average efficiency level, notably wood processing and wood, bamboo, rattan, palm fiber, and grass product manufacturing, which achieved efficiency values above 0.95. However, the majority of sectors fell below the average efficiency level. Among them, the electricity, heat production, and supply sector recorded the lowest efficiency value at just 0.07, which is significantly below the average.Figure 3The change of carbon emissions in Tianjin’s industrial sector from 2010 to 2023

Optimizing carbon quota efficiency in Tianjin’s industrial sectors using ZSG- DEA

In view of the low initial carbon quota efficiency and unreasonable quota results of Tianjin’s industrial sub-sectors under the grandfather allocation scheme, the ZSG-DEA model is constructed to adjust the DEA efficiency value of carbon quotas in each sub-sector. The input factor is set as the carbon emission predictor, and the proportional reduction strategy is used for iterative optimization until the efficiency value of each sub-industry approaches 1, while ensuring that the total carbon emission remains unchanged during the iterative process. Figure 4 shows the change in carbon emission efficiency in each industry during the iteration process. In the first iteration, the efficiency value of 6 industries has reached 1; at the second iteration, the efficiency value of 12 industries has reached 1; after the third iteration, the efficiency values of 29 sub-sectors have been effectively improved, and the efficiency value has reached 1, achieving DEA efficiency.Figure 4Carbon emission efficiency values during industry optimization and iteration

Carbon emission efficiency values during industry optimization and iteration

The results of carbon emission distribution of industrial sectors in Tianjin are shown in columns 4–6 of Table 1. On the basis of the comprehensive analysis of three iterations, after reallocating carbon quotas, six sectors—including nonmetallic mineral products, metal products, and transportation equipment manufacturing—received positive adjustments. This finding indicates that according to current projections, these six sectors can achieve DEA efficiency even with corresponding increases in carbon emissions. Among these, the ferrous metal smelting and rolling industry, despite being a high-carbon-emission sector, has seen its carbon emission efficiency relatively improved and its carbon quotas correspondingly increase. This is due to government efforts to optimize the industrial structure by addressing “steel encirclement” and resolving excess capacity. This has led to a relative improvement in carbon emission efficiency and a corresponding increase in carbon quotas. Conversely, the remaining 23 industries require reductions in carbon quotas. Industries such as the printing and reproduction of recorded media, chemical raw materials and chemical product manufacturing, and rubber and plastic product manufacturing have negative adjustment values, necessitating quota reductions to maximize efficiency. Among the sectors with positive adjustment values, transportation equipment manufacturing and computer, communications, and other electronic equipment manufacturing possess strong technological R&D capabilities. These sectors can enhance production efficiency through innovation and R&D investment, resulting in high carbon emission efficiency. Consequently, their carbon quotas should be increased after adjustment. Conversely, sectors with negative adjustment values—such as chemical raw materials and chemical product manufacturing, and rubber and plastic product manufacturing—exhibit limited capacity for technological innovation and improvement, with constrained potential for enhancing carbon emission efficiency. Therefore, their corresponding carbon emissions should be appropriately reduced. Following the adjustments, the ferrous metal smelting and rolling industry remains the sector with the largest share of carbon dioxide emissions, allocated 2724.8 × 104 tonnes. Conversely, the water production and supply industry has the smallest share of carbon dioxide emissions, receiving a carbon emissions quota of only 0.26 × 104 tonnes.

Fairness assessment of carbon allocations based on the gini coefficient

To validate the fairness of the final allocation results, the Gini coefficient is introduced as an indicator of equity. Internationally, a Gini coefficient less than 0.2 is considered relatively fair, values between 0.3 and 0.4 are deemed reasonably acceptable, and values exceeding 0.4 serve as a warning threshold for severe distributional inequity. In this study, the Gini coefficient is calculated using the final carbon allowances allocated to each industry as the metric.

It can be seen from Table 2 that the Gini coefficient of the grandfather allocation method is smaller than that of the ZSG-DEA method, indicating that the carbon quota results based on the grandfather allocation scheme are more equitable. The grandfather allocation scheme emphasizes the allocation of carbon quotas based on the proportion of historical emissions in the industry to the whole industry, which embodies the concept of ' maintaining the status quo’, fits the existing emission pattern of the industrial industry, and is easy to be accepted by all industries in the actual allocation35; the ZSG-DEA takes the optimal efficiency as the core, and iterates many times by reducing the carbon quotas of inefficient industries until the efficiency values of each industry are effective, and the inter-industry quota gap will be readjusted. Statistics of fairness and efficiency indicators under two allocation methods

In terms of efficiency evaluation, the comprehensive efficiency value calculated by the two distribution methods is used as the evaluation basis. Under the grandfather allocation scheme, the efficiency value of each industry fluctuates greatly, among which the maximum value reaches 1, the minimum value is only 0.07, and the standard deviation is 0.66, which fully shows that there is a significant gap in carbon emission efficiency among different industries. Under the ZSG-DEA allocation method, the maximum and minimum values of the industry efficiency value are stable at 1 and 0.99, respectively, and the standard deviation is 0, indicating that the ZSG-DEA allocation method effectively reduces the degree of dispersion of data and promotes all industries to reach a fully effective state. In summary, although the optimized allocation scheme is slightly weaker than the grandfather method in fairness, considering that the ZSG-DEA model is an efficiency optimization model, the optimized scheme is strengthened in terms of efficiency and can better balance fairness and efficiency.

Industry classification based on carbon allowance adjustment volume and tax contribution

Considering the variability in carbon quotas and tax contributions across industries, a comprehensive classification analysis is conducted based on both carbon quota adjustment levels and tax payments. Industries with optimized carbon quotas exceeding their initial values are defined as high-efficiency industries, while those with lower optimized values are classified as low-efficiency industries. Industries with tax contributions exceeding ¥1.589 billion (the average) are classified as high-contribution industries, while those below this threshold are deemed low-contribution industries. Based on these criteria, industries are categorized into four groups: high contribution and high efficiency, high contribution and low efficiency, low contribution and high efficiency, and low contribution and low efficiency. Industry-specific industrial structure optimization pathways are formulated accordingly. Discussion

Industrial carbon emissions in Tianjin

After calculating the total amount of industrial carbon emissions in Tianjin, we found that from 2010 to 2023, carbon emissions from Tianjin’s industrial sector followed a pattern of initial growth followed by a decline. By calculating the carbon emissions from Tianjin’s industrial sector, it was determined that from 2010 to 2023, the sector’s carbon emissions followed a pattern of initial growth followed by a decline. Specifically, the total emissions increased from 4107.52 × 104 t in 2010–4947.85 × 104 t in 2013, whereas from 2014 to 2023, total industrial carbon emissions decreased by 1,231.95 × 104 tonnes.

Changes in carbon emission efficiency and carbon quota allocation in various industries

The average carbon emission efficiency across Tianjin’s industrial sectors is relatively low, with significant disparities among different industries. After applying the grandfathering method for initial allocation across 29 industrial subsectors, the DEA-BBC model calculation based on these initial allocations yielded an average initial efficiency value of 0.48 for these subsectors, indicating a lower-middle level. The nonmetallic mineral products industry; metal products industry; transportation equipment manufacturing; computer, communications, and other electronic equipment manufacturing; and instrument manufacturing sectors demonstrated relatively high carbon emission efficiency, achieving DEA efficiency. Conversely, the electricity, heat production, and supply industry exhibited the lowest carbon emission efficiency value of only 0.07.

Following carbon emission efficiency optimization using the ZSG-DEA model, carbon quotas were reasonably allocated across industries. After three iterations of the ZSG-DEA model, all industries achieved DEA efficiency. Postoptimization, the ferrous metal smelting and rolling sector remained the highest CO2 emitter, being allocated 2724.8 × 104 t, and the water production and supply sector became the lowest emitter, receiving a carbon emission quota of only 0.26 × 104 t.

The classification results of industrial types in Tianjin

By categorizing and analyzing the 29 sub-sectors from the dual perspectives of carbon quota adjustments and tax liabilities, we identify four optimized pathways: four high contribution and high efficiency industries; five high contribution and low efficiency industries; two low contribution and high efficiency industries; and 18 low contribution and low efficiency industries. This framework proposes differentiated industrial structure optimization strategies.

Structural optimization recommendations for different types of division

On the basis of the conclusions drawn from industry classification according to carbon quota adjustment volumes and tax contributions, the following optimization recommendations are proposed for each industry category.(1)High contribution and high efficiency industries should strengthen policy-technology-market coordination to continuously enhance innovation and R&D capabilities. Governments should introduce targeted industrial support policies, establish special subsidies for green upgrades and low-interest loans, reduce financing costs for enterprises undertaking green technology transformations, and expand the development space for such industries. Technologically, enterprises should be encouraged to establish green R&D centers focused on breakthroughs in low-carbon production processes, clean energy substitution, and waste recycling. Collaboration among research institutions, universities, and enterprises should be promoted to accelerate the commercialization of green technologies. Additionally, industry-specific green product promotion platforms should be developed to guide government procurement toward prioritizing low-carbon products from these sectors.(2)Implement process upgrading and transformation for high-contribution and low-efficiency industries to improve carbon emission efficiency. Focusing on the transformation of the production process of traditional industries to the direction of high-end, intelligent and green, taking comprehensive measures such as process upgrading in the main direction of energy saving, carbon reduction and clean production, and implementing energy-saving transformation of high-energy-consuming and high-carbon-emission equipment to improve energy efficiency and reduce carbon emissions; in addition, optimize the energy consumption structure and increase the proportion of clean energy use. The total amount of coal used in high-energy consuming industries such as iron and steel, chemical industry, and building materials in Tianjin was strictly controlled, and the transformation of “electricity instead of coal” and “gas instead of coal” was carried out for industrial coal-fired boilers and kilns. In addition, we should increase investment in the research and development of clean energy technology, focus on breaking through key technical fields such as energy storage technology and smart grid, and promote the large-scale development of clean energy and improve energy utilization efficiency by promoting energy-saving technologies such as waste heat and pressure recovery and energy cascade utilization.(3)Give full play to the advantages of low-carbon efficiency in low-contribution and high-efficiency industries, and realize the extension of the industrial chain to the high end. First of all, the government encourages enterprises to increase R&D investment, encourages enterprises to develop customized and intelligent services, and improves the profit margin of products. At the same time, we will encourage the creation of green product brands in the industry and strengthen the management of green product certification and labeling. Secondly, relying on the advantages of high carbon emission efficiency, integrating the resources of small and medium-sized enterprises in the industry, taking the industrial park as the carrier, promoting the ' group development and cluster upgrading ' of small and medium-sized enterprises, guiding the enterprises in the park to realize the coordination and cooperation of raw material procurement, production process optimization and waste recycling, supporting enterprises through mergers and acquisitions, industrial park agglomeration, etc., to expand production scale and reduce overall production costs.(4)Control the growth rate and scale of low-contribution and low-efficiency industries, and eliminate backward production capacity. The government formulates policies and measures for specific industries, and strictly prohibits the addition of high-carbon industries such as steel, cement clinker, and flat glass; comprehensively sort out the backward production capacity of high-emission enterprises in Tianjin, and promote withdrawal according to law and regulations. Continue to improve the low-carbon environmental protection system, strictly implement the carbon quota management and carbon market trading mechanism, levy high carbon taxes on over-quota emission enterprises, force enterprises to reduce emissions, set phased carbon emission intensity reduction targets, support differentiated environmental protection standards, and guide the entire industry to transform to low-carbon development.

Technologically, enterprises should be encouraged to establish green R&D centers focused on breakthroughs in low-carbon production processes, clean energy substitution, and waste recycling. Collaboration among research institutions, universities, and enterprises should be promoted to accelerate the commercialization of green technologies. Additionally, industry-specific green product promotion platforms should be developed to guide government procurement toward prioritizing low-carbon products from these sectors.

(2)Implement process upgrading and transformation for high-contribution and low-efficiency industries to improve carbon emission efficiency. Focusing on the transformation of the production process of traditional industries to the direction of high-end, intelligent and green, taking comprehensive measures such as process upgrading in the main direction of energy saving, carbon reduction and clean production, and implementing energy-saving transformation of high-energy-consuming and high-carbon-emission equipment to improve energy efficiency and reduce carbon emissions; in addition, optimize the energy consumption structure and increase the proportion of clean energy use. The total amount of coal used in high-energy consuming industries such as iron and steel, chemical industry, and building materials in Tianjin was strictly controlled, and the transformation of “electricity instead of coal” and “gas instead of coal” was carried out for industrial coal-fired boilers and kilns. In addition, we should increase investment in the research and development of clean energy technology, focus on breaking through key technical fields such as energy storage technology and smart grid, and promote the large-scale development of clean energy and improve energy utilization efficiency by promoting energy-saving technologies such as waste heat and pressure recovery and energy cascade utilization.

Limitations of the study

Despite these findings, limitations remain. In the data acquisition of energy consumption, main business income, and employment in industrial sub-sectors, due to the statistical caliber of statistical yearbooks, this study only discusses the quota allocation of industrial enterprises above designated size in the industry. In the future, small enterprises should be included in the research scope, and industry data should be collected from bottom to top to fully and truly reflect the industry situation. In addition, the total carbon emissions in 2030 are based on the goals of policy documents, without considering the impact of economic downturn, energy structure changes, and technological changes. In the future, multiple carbon emission accounting and trend prediction methods can be integrated to accurately clarify the total carbon emissions in the target year.

Method details

Research area and data sources

To understand the spatial distribution of various industrial sectors, this study utilized Gaode Map to crawl company-type points of interest (POIs) within Tianjin Municipality. Data collection concluded in October 2024. After projection, clipping, filtering, coordinate correction, and deduplication, a total of 15,508 POI records were obtained. Using the Qichacha platform (https://www.qichacha.com/), the industry categories of enterprises were identified on the basis of the the addresses and names of the POIs. Enterprises with a status of “suspended operations” or “closed” were excluded. Ultimately, 8,452 industrial enterprise POIs were selected from the 15,508 companies. Referencing Lai Guanzhong’s research on the classification of industrial enterprise locations,36 and considering Tianjin’s industrial development status and sector characteristics, these POIs were categorized into two groups: the top 10 industries by enterprise count and all other industries.

The industrial sectors covered in the Tianjin Statistical Yearbook were selected for this study, four industries—coal mining and washing, nonferrous metal mining and beneficiation, mining support activities, and tobacco manufacturing—were excluded because of their low energy consumption and incomplete data. Consequently, 29 subsectors in Tianjin were chosen as the research subjects. When calculating carbon emissions from fossil fuels, other petroleum products were excluded because of their limited energy usage and data gaps in certain years. Additionally, socioeconomic data such as average workforce size, energy consumption, and main business revenue for the industrial sectors in the model were sourced from the Tianjin Statistical Yearbook. The 2030 socioeconomic baseline indicators were primarily derived from reports including the “Tianjin Municipal 14th Five-Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development and Long-Range Objectives Through 2035,” “Made in China 2025,” “Tianjin Municipal Population Development Plan for the 14th Five-Year Period,” and “Tianjin Municipal High-Quality Development Plan for the Manufacturing Industry for the 14th Five-Year Period.” The parameters were set on the basis of the growth rates and targets outlined in these documents.

Carbon emission calculation model

Industrial carbon emissions consist of emissions generated from energy combustion. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC, 2006) provides a carbon emission calculation formula.37 This paper employs this method to account for carbon emissions from Tianjin’s industrial sector, covering seven energy types: raw coal, coke, crude oil, gasoline, diesel, fuel oil, and natural gas. The formula is shown in Equation 1:(Equation 1)Ei,j=Ai,j×Fj=4412Ai,j×Nj×Cj×OjIn the formula, Ei,j denotes the carbon emissions from energy consumption in industry i for energy source j, t; Ai,j denotes the energy consumption in industry i for energy source j, t, converted to standard coal equivalent; Fj denotes the carbon emission factor for energy source j, t/t, expressed as CO2; Nj denotes the average lower heating value of energy source j, J/t; Cj denotes the carbon content of energy source j, t/J; and Oj denotes the carbon oxidation rate of energy source j.

Projected total carbon quota for the industrial sector

Although China has clearly put forward the goal of achieving carbon peak in 2030, it has not given the specific target constraints of carbon emission peak in 2030.Therefore, this paper first predicts the total carbon emissions of Tianjin‘s industrial sector in 2030, and takes the total industrial carbon emissions in 2030 as the total quota to be allocated. The ‘Made in China 2025’ issued by the state clearly mentions that ‘by 2025, China‘s carbon emissions per unit of industrial added value should be reduced by at least 40% compared with 2015’.Considering that China‘s research on industrial carbon emission intensity and emission reduction potential is mostly based on 2015,12,38,39 this paper takes 2015 as the benchmark year. According to Formula (2), the industrial carbon emission intensity of Tianjin in 2015 and 2024 is calculated, and the industrial emission reduction intensity in 2015–2024 is 42.16%. Considering that there is still room for continuous emission reduction in 2025–2030, this paper sets the intensity of 45% reduction in 2030 compared with 2015. In 2030, the industry output value is combined with the predicted value of the GDP growth rate of about 5% proposed by the Tianjin Municipal Government Work Report in 2025 and the reality of the city‘s GDP growth rate of 5.8% in the first quarter of 2025 compared with the previous year. With reference to Bingquan Liu38,40 and others, the average value of 5% and 5.8% is selected as the predicted GDP growth rate. The calculation of the total amount of carbon quotas in Tianjin‘s industrial sector in 2030 is as shown in Equations 2 and 3:(Equation 2)I2015=C2015V2015(Equation 3)C2030=V2030×I2015×(1−45%)In the formula, I2015 denotes the carbon emission intensity of Tianjin’s industrial subsector in 2015; V2015 and V2030 represent the output value of Tianjin’s industrial subsector in 2015 and 2030, respectively; and C2015 and C2030 denote the carbon emissions of Tianjin’s industrial subsectors in 2015 and 2030, respectively.

Grandfathering plan

Different quota allocation methods have different effects on the production efficiency of different industries. In view of the fact that the research object is 29 industrial subdivision industries, there are many industrial categories and strong industrial heterogeneity, the baseline method is not suitable for industries with missing basic data and large development differences.41 The ‘grandfather allocation’ scheme is based on the historical cumulative carbon emissions of the industry. It is convenient to calculate and has high data availability. This method is still widely used in the allocation of carbon quotas and emission rights in various places at this stage. It can achieve the fairness of differences in historical responsibility, development level and emission efficiency. In this paper, the grandfather allocation scheme is used to initially allocate the carbon quotas of various industries in Tianjin,9 as shown in Equations 4 and 5:(Equation 4)Sn=fnF(Equation 5)Qn=Q×SnIn the formula, Sn represents the cumulative historical emissions share of subsector within Tianjin’s industrial sector; fn denotes the cumulative historical emissions of subsector n within the industrial sector, in 10,000 tonnes; and F signifies the total cumulative emissions of all subsectors within Tianjin’s industrial sector, in 10,000 tonnes. Qn indicates the carbon quota for subsector n in the target year, which is 10,000 tonnes; Q represents the total carbon quota for the target year, in 10,000 tonnes.

ZSG-DEA model

The approach of “treating undesirable outputs as inputs” is typically adopted in the ZSG-DEA model. On the basis of the characteristics of the industrial sector and following the methodology of Zheng Liqun et al.,40,42 this study selects 29 industrial subsectors in Tianjin as decision-making units (DMUj).For the economic-environmental system, carbon emissions are undesirable outputs. In the environmental efficiency evaluation model based on DEA, the ‘undesirable output is regarded as input method’ is one of the main treatment ideas. This paper selects this method and takes carbon emissions as the only input factor. Referring to the practice of Lins and Gomes,43,44 the main business income, energy consumption and the average number of employees in the industrial industry are selected as output variables. The main business income represents the direct value creation of production and operation activities, which is a typical expected output; energy is the basic element of industrial production. Setting it as an output variable means that when the carbon emission level is similar, the higher the energy consumption and the higher the carbon emission, the higher the carbon emission efficiency; taking the average number of employees in the industry as the expected output, when the carbon emission input level is equal, the industry with more employment will be more efficient in GDP. In view of the large differences in the input and output of various industries, this paper chooses the ZSG-DEA model with variable returns to scale, as shown in Equation 6:(Equation 6){minφ0∑i=1Nλiyij≥y0j,j=1,2,3⋯,M∑i=1Nλixik[1+x0k(1−φ0)∑i≠0xik]≤φ0x0k,k=1,2,3⋯,R∑i=1Nλi=1,i=1,2,3,⋯,Nλi≥0,i=1,2,3⋯,NIn the equation,φ0 represents the relative efficiency of DMU0, denoting the highest relative efficiency value; λi represents the proportion of the i industry, which is the carbon quota ratio for each subindustry; x0k and xik denote the input quantities of DMU0 and DMUi, respectively; y0j and yij denote the output quantities of DMU0 and DMUi, respectively; and R represents the number of input factors, while N and M denote the number of subindustries and output variables, respectively.

In this model, the excess input portion of DMU0 with efficiency less than 1 is allocated proportionally to other DMUs according to [xik∑i≠0xik(1−φ0)]. The quantity of DMUi received from DMU0 is [xik∑i≠0xik(1−φ0)x0k]. Ultimately, the reallocated amount of input k for DMUi is the total received from other DMUs minus the quantity that it itself reduced and allocated to other DMUs:(Equation 7)xik=∑i≠0[xik∑i≠0xik(1−φ0)x0k]−xik(1−φi),i=1,2,3,⋯,N

Quantification and statistical analysis

We conducted data analysis using Arcgis and Dearun. Arcgis is used to process spatial data. According to the spatial distribution map of industrial enterprises, the spatial distribution of various industrial sectors is descriptively analyzed. Using the carbon emission coefficient method, combined with the energy consumption data of Tianjin industrial sub-sectors, the carbon emissions of Tianjin industrial sub-sectors during the study period were calculated. According to the policy documents, the total amount of industrial carbon emissions in Tianjin in 2030 is predicted. The ‘grandfather distribution method’ is used to allocate carbon quotas for subdivided industries. Dearun is used to calculate the initial carbon emission efficiency of 29 subdivided industries and re-adjust their carbon quotas.

Carleman estimate for semi-discrete stochastic parabolic operators in arbitrary dimension and applications to controllability
Rodrigo Lecaros, Ariel A. Pérez, Manuel F. Prado

Abstract
. This paper considers a semi-discrete forward stochastic parabolic operator with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions in arbitrary dimensions. We show the lack of null controllability for a spatial semi-discretization of a null-controllable stochastic parabolic system from any initial datum. However, by proving a new Carleman estimate for its semi-discrete backward stochastic adjoint system, we achieve a relaxed observability inequality, which is applied to derivative ϕ \phi -null controllability by duality arguments.

In turn, the study of semi-discrete and fully-discrete counterparts of established controllability results has received much attention, particularly in contexts where continuous results do not hold. For example, in general, the discrete versions of the unique continuation property are false since an explicit counterexample is presented in
[

in the deterministic context. Moreover, in

,

y=0,&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{M}\times(0,T).\end{cases}

Let
(x_{i},x_{j})\in\mathcal{M}
. We define

\psi(x_{i},x_{j})\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-1)^{i},&i=j,\\
0,&i\not=j,\end{array}\right.

and extend it by zero on

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d\left(y(t)\tilde{\psi}(t)\right)=&\int_{\mathcal{M}}\tilde{\psi}\,dy+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\partial_{t}(\tilde{\psi})y\,dt\\

since

Since

Integrating over

\left.\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathcal{M}}y(t)\tilde{\psi}(t)\right|_{0}^{T}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G_{0}\cap\mathcal{M}}\tilde{\psi}u\,dt.

\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\psi y(T)=e^{-4T/h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\psi y(0),

Due to the aforementioned difficulty, we cannot achieve null controllability at the discrete level. Therefore, we will not try to drive the solution to zero at time

\lim\inf_{h\to 0}\frac{\phi(h)}{e^{-\kappa/h}}>0.

Definition 1

Remark 1.4

Remark 2.3

Deriving Carleman estimates for continuous stochastic partial differential operators typically relies on a pointwise identity with an appropriate weight (see, e.g.,

However, in the discrete case, we construct integral-form identities that strongly exploit the properties of the primal and dual meshes (i.e.,

4.
Concluding remarks

We have established controllability results for the linear semi-discrete stochastic parabolic operator by using two controls. Unlike in the continuous setting, we have shown that null controllability cannot be expected for any initial data. In this context, we have proved the existence of bounded semi-discrete controls such that the norm of the solution approaches zero at a given time as the discrete parameter tends to zero. It is known that null-controllability is achieved using one control when the coefficients of the system only depend on the time variable
[
23
]
, and it could be interesting to explore a similar setting for the semi-discrete case.

In addition, the results in Theorem
3.2
can be extended to the more general case when we replace the first equation of (
13
) by

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{h}y=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}D_{i}(a_{1i}y)+a_{2}y+\chi_{G_{0}\cap\mathcal{M}}u\right)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(a_{3}^{k}y+v\right)\,dB_{k}(t),

where
a_{3}^{k}
are functions in
L_{\mathds{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{h}(\mathcal{M}))
and
\{B_{k}(t)\}_{k=1,\cdots,m}
are independent standard one-dimensional Brownian motions. This extension does not introduce significant analytical complexity, since the multidimensional nature of the Brownian motion only affects the notation through additional indices. The structure of Itô’s formula remains essentially unchanged, and the stochastic integrals preserve their standard properties under the assumption of independence. Consequently, the Carleman inequality can still be derived in this setting, leading to the corresponding observability inequality for the adjoint system. Therefore, for the sake of clarity and without loss of generality, we restrict our analysis to the case
m=1
.

On the other hand, we note that the nonlinear semi-discrete case remains an open problem, particularly in the multidimensional setting and when the nonlinearity depends on both the state and its gradient. A significant advance has recently been made in the one-dimensional case with nonlinearity depending only on the state, as shown in
[
29
]
, where the authors developed suitable Carleman estimates to address the
\phi
-null controllability of the nonlinear semi-discrete system.
In contrast, a more general class of nonlinearities—depending on both the state and its gradient—has been studied in the continuous setting in
[
32
]
. In both works, the main tool is the derivation of appropriate Carleman estimates for the associated linearized systems. These results suggest that adapting this methodology to the semi-discrete setting could be a promising direction, although new challenges arise, especially due to the interaction between spatial discretization and time-dependent nonlinearities.

In
[
2
]
, a null controllability result is obtained for a similar system like (
1
) although with the general convection term “
[a_{5}+B\cdot\nabla y]dB(t)
”. Then it could be interesting to analyze the semi-discrete behavior of the controls in that setting since the main tool used in that work is a new Carleman estimate.

Related to ongoing work is the consideration of the semi-discrete inverse problem for the stochastic parabolic operators; see, for instance,
[
24
]
for the continuous setting and the references therein. Recently, Bin Wu, Ying Wang, and Zewen Wang published results on semi-discrete stochastic inverse problems in the one-dimensional setting
[
30
]
. Hence, the methodology developed in the proof of the Carleman estimate from Theorem
2.2
could be adapted to extend the results from
[
30
]
to arbitrary dimensions, as their approach to obtaining the Carleman estimate follows a similar strategy. Moreover, the fundamental steps rely on the estimate of the discrete spatial operator applied on the respective Carleman weight function, and no significant difficulties are related with the temporal variable.

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\int_{(\mathcal{M}^{i})_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|\right\|_{L^{1}\left(\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)}^{1/(n-1)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|\right)^{1/(n-1)}.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u(x)|^{n/(n-1)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|\right)^{1/(n-1)}.

As a consequence, we have the following.

(90)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{n/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|\right)^{1/n}.

The proof for
p=1
follows after applying the inequality of arithmetic and geometric mean to the right-hand side above.

We now turn to the case
1<p<n
. Let
m\geq 1
; the idea is to apply (
90
) to
|u|^{m-1}u
. However, since we do not have a chain rule for the operator
D_{i}
, we will use the following identity.

\displaystyle D_{i}(|u|^{m-1}u)=

\displaystyle D_{i}(|u|^{m-1})A_{i}(u)+A_{i}(|u|^{m-1})D_{i}(u)

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i}(u)D_{i}(|u|)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}(A_{i}|u|)^{k}A_{i}(|u|^{m-2-k})\right)+A_{i}(|u|^{m-1})D_{i}(u).

The proof of (
91
) is consequence of the expression

D_{i}(|u|^{m-1})=D_{i}(|u|)\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}(A_{i}|u|)^{k}A_{i}(|u|^{m-2-k}).

which follows by induction over
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, also by induction, we can prove that for
m\in\mathds{N}
, holds the next inequality

(92)

\displaystyle A_{i}(|u|^{m})\leq

\displaystyle C(A_{i}|u|)^{m}.

Hence, combining (
91
) and (
92
), and noting that
|D_{i}|u||\leq|D_{i}u|
and
|A_{i}u|\leq A_{i}|u|
, we obtain

(93)

\displaystyle|D_{i}(|u|^{m-1}u)|\leq

\displaystyle|D_{i}u|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-2}(A_{i}|u|)^{k+1}A_{i}(|u|^{m-2-k})+A_{i}(|u|^{m-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|D_{i}u|(A_{i}|u|)^{m-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}2^{m-1-k}+2^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(A_{i}|u|)^{m-1}|D_{i}u|.

Thus, applying (
90
) to
|u|^{m-1}u
we obtain

(94)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{mn/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}(|u|^{m-1}u)|\right)^{1/n}.

Therefore, using (
93
) in (
94
) it follows that

(95)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{mn/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}(A_{i}|u|)^{m-1}|D_{i}u|\right)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}_{i}^{\frac{1}{np^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\right)^{1/(np)},

where
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=1
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}(A_{i}|u|)^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}
.

The key step is to prove a uniform bound on
\mathcal{I}_{i}
(independent of
i
) in terms of the integral of
u
over
\mathcal{M}
. Using that
(a+b)^{c}\leq 2^{c-1}(a^{c}+b^{c})
for
a,b\geq 0
and
c\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i}\leq\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}2^{p^{\prime}(m-1)-1}A_{i}(|u|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}),

Now, performing an integration by parts with respect to the average operator and using that
u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}A_{i}(2^{p^{\prime}(m-1)-1})+\frac{h}{2}\int_{\partial_{i}\mathcal{M}}|u|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}t_{r}^{i}(2^{p^{\prime}(m-1)-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}2^{p^{\prime}(m-1)-1}|u|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}.

Then, using the above inequality in (
95
) allows us to conclude

(96)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{mn/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}2^{p^{\prime}(m-1)-1}|u|^{p^{\prime}(m-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\right)^{1/(np)}.

Thus, choosing
m\in\mathds{N}
such that
mn/(n-1)=p^{\prime}(m-1)
, that is
m:=(n-1)p^{\ast}/n\geq 1
(since
1<p<n
); we have

\displaystyle\|u\|^{m}_{L_{h}^{p^{\ast}}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{p^{\ast}}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{p^{\ast}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\right)^{1/(np)},

implying

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{h}^{p^{\ast}}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\right)^{1/(np)}.

Finally, let us consider the case
p=n
. From (
96
), for all
m>1
we have

\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{mn/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{\frac{n(m-1)}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{(n-1)}{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{n}\right)^{1/(n^{2})},

and thanks to Young’s inequality, we have for all
m>1

\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{mn/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/(nm)}\leq C\left(\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|u|^{\frac{n(m-1)}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{(m-1)n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\ast}}|D_{i}u|^{n}\right)^{1/n}\right).

Choosing
m=n
in the above inequality yields

\|u\|_{L_{h}^{n^{2}/(n-1)}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{h}^{1,n}(\mathcal{M})}.

By the interpolation inequality, this implies

\|u\|_{L_{h}^{p^{\ast}}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{h}^{1,n}(\mathcal{M})}

for all
p^{\ast}\in[n,n^{2}/(n-1)]
. Iterating this argument with
m=n+1
,
m=n+2
, and so on, we conclude the desired result.

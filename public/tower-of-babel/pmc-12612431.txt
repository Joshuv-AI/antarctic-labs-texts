18F-FDG versus 18F-FDOPA PET in recurrent medulloblastoma: a case study and literature review
Authors: Abarnaa Sivapathasundaram, Florence Laigle-Donadey, Catherine Horodyckid, Karima Mokhtari, Aurélie Kas, Laura Rozenblum
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMedulloblastoma is a heterogeneous central nervous system tumor stratified into four molecular subgroups in the revised 2021 WHO classification. Although MRI remains essential for follow-up, distinguishing true recurrence from treatment-related changes can be challenging. PET imaging may provide additional value in this context; however, evidence in medulloblastoma is limited to small series and case reports, with most available PET data originating from glioma studies. Here, we report a medulloblastoma patient experiencing two recurrences assessed sequentially with 18F-FDOPA and 18F-FDG PET imaging. This case highlights considerations in PET tracer selection, aiming to support clinical decision-making and enhance diagnostic accuracy for physicians managing recurrent medulloblastoma.Keywords: Medulloblastoma, PET-FDG, PET-DOPA, Progression, Radionecrosis

Introduction

Medulloblastoma, classified into four molecular subgroups—WNT-activated, SHH-activated, Group 3, and Group 4—in the revised 2021 WHO Classification, presents diagnostic challenges in distinguishing recurrence from post-treatment changes using MRI alone (Franceschi et al. 2019). Positron emission tomography (PET) offers additional insights, yet the literature guiding tracer choice remains limited, often based on mixed glioma cohorts or isolated reports (Cicone et al. 2016; Filippi et al. 2021). We present a complex case of recurrent SHH-activated medulloblastoma evaluated using sequential PET imaging with 18F-FDOPA and 18F-FDG, aiming to clarify optimal PET tracer selection.

Case presentation

A 59-year-old man with histologically confirmed SHH-activated desmoplastic medulloblastoma underwent surgical resection, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Four months post-radiotherapy, MRI revealed an equivocal enhancing nodule adjacent to the resection cavity (Fig. 1A). To differentiate recurrence from post-therapy effects, the patient underwent 18F-FDOPA PET-MRI (Signa PET/MR 3T, GE Healthcare; 2 MBq/kg, 10–30 min post-injection, ZTE MR-based attenuation correction, reconstructed with 3D OSEM, 8 iterations/28 subsets, no post-filtering). No carbidopa was administered. PET demonstrated negligible uptake (SUVmax tumor-to-parietal gray/white matter ratio 1.5, tumor-to-contralateral striatum 0.9), suggesting treatment-related changes (Fig. 1B, C, red arrows).. It should be emphasized that glioma-derived thresholds are not validated in medulloblastoma and were applied here only as exploratory benchmarks. Despite PET findings, MRI showed progressive enlargement and diffusion restriction of the lesion. Surgical exploration confirmed recurrence histopathologically identical to the primary tumor.

.

Two years later, after re-treatment with chemotherapy and focal re-irradiation, MRI findings again raised suspicion of tumor recurrence versus post-therapy effects (Fig. 1D). This time, 18F-FDG PET/MRI was performed (2 MBq/kg, 60 min post-injection, ZTE MR-based attenuation correction, 3D OSEM reconstruction, 6 iterations/28 subsets, no post-filtering).

PET demonstrated intense hypermetabolism (SUVmax 19.1; tumor-to-contralateral background ratio 2.2; Fig. 1E, F, yellow arrows). Subsequent surgery confirmed recurrent disease.

Discussion

MRI-based differentiation between medulloblastoma recurrence and treatment-related changes remains challenging, akin to issues encountered in gliomas. PET imaging can improve diagnostic accuracy, but optimal tracer selection is poorly established, especially within the context of molecular subgroups defined by the 2021 WHO classification (Franceschi et al. 2019).

Previous reports demonstrate high 18F-FDG uptake in primary medulloblastoma, correlating negatively with survival outcomes. High 18F-FDG uptake likely results from increased glucose phosphorylation rather than enhanced transport mechanisms (ArunRaj et al. 2021). In our patient, 18F-FDG PET robustly identified second recurrence, suggesting that this tracer may be particularly sensitive for detecting metabolically active recurrent disease.

In contrast, amino acid tracers such as 18F-FDOPA, reflecting L-type amino acid transporter expression, have shown variable uptake patterns in medulloblastoma (Cicone et al. 2016, 2021). Initial studies indicated significant uptake in untreated tumors, while other reports highlighted false negatives or low uptake post-therapy, potentially confounded by radiotherapy-induced tissue changes (Filippi et al. 2021; Cicone et al. 2021). In our patient with recurrent SHH-activated medulloblastoma, 18F-FDOPA PET failed to detect recurrence post-initial radiotherapy. This may be due to therapy-related changes, as early PET performed soon after radiotherapy represents a window where inflammation, endothelial injury, and transporter modulation can blunt FDOPA signals. In addition, Cicone et al. have shown that LAT1/LAT2 expression can be heterogeneously expressed in medulloblastoma (Cicone et al. 2017).

Beyond FDG and FDOPA, alternative targets are being explored. Notably, some studies have reported somatostatin receptor (SSTR) expression in medulloblastomas, particularly in more differentiated tumors, resulting in high 68Ga-DOTATOC uptake (Chan and Hsiao 2016). This observation deserves further investigation, as SSTR imaging may represent a valuable option when amino acid tracers are unavailable and could potentially open avenues for radioligand therapy, analogous to strategies currently applied in meningiomas.

Conclusion

This case underscores the importance of tracer selection in PET imaging of recurrent medulloblastoma. In this SHH-activated medulloblastoma case, FDG detected recurrence when FDOPA did not; tracer performance in medulloblastoma likely depends on tumor biology (e.g., LAT1 expression) and timing post-therapy. Prospective, multi-tracer studies stratified by molecular subgroup are needed to define tracer-specific operating characteristics in medulloblastoma.

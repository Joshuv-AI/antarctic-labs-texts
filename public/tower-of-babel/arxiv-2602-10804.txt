Dust collapse and bounce in spherically symmetric quantum-inspired gravity models


Abstract
We study the collapse and possible bounce of dust in quantum-inspired gravity models with spherical symmetry. Starting from a wide class of spherically symmetric spacetimes, we write down the covariant Hamiltonian constraints that under dynamical flow give rise to metrics of many spherically symmetric gravity models. Gravity is minimally coupled to a dust field. The constraint equations are solved for the Hamiltonian evolution and simple equations for the location of the outer boundary of the dust versus time and the apparent horizons in terms of shape functions are obtained. The dust density is not assumed to be homogeneous inside the collapsing ball. In many cases, the effective quantum gravity effects stop the collapse of the dust matter field, then causes the dust field to expand thus creating a bounce at a minimum radius and avoiding the classical singularity. Using this formalism, we examine several quantum-inspired gravity metrics to obtain bounce results either previously obtained by different methods or new results.

1
Introduction

In general relativity, pioneering work on gravitational collapse was performed
by Oppenheimer and Snyder
[
37
]
.
The model describes the gravitational collapse of a homogeneous pressureless
star.
The boundary of the star starts at rest at past infinity and is in free fall
during collapse.
A gravitational singularity is dynamically generated.
However, avoiding singularities is one of the motivations for quantum gravity.
Besides resolving the classical singularity problem, the quantum theory should
also address dynamical effects such as the in-falling collapse of matter to form
black holes and trapped surfaces.

In spherically symmetric spacetime, a matter field is needed to study the
collapse of a star.
A simple but effective choice is dust which is non-interacting particles.
The ball of dust is in free fall along geodesics.
Inside the ball of dust, the density is usually assumed to be homogeneous.
The exterior of the dust ball is taken to be vacuum.

There are generally two approach to solving the dynamics.
One approach is to solve Einstein’s equations with the energy-momentum tensor
for dust.
Another approach is the Hamiltonian formalism in which the dust Hamiltonian
density is taken as the energy density.
These two methods offer different advantages and disadvantages in physical
insight and ease of calculation but give the same results.
However, in those case in which the postulated spacetime vacuum is not a
solution to Einstein’s equation, one must resort to the Hamiltonian formalism.

The usual classical approach is to consider the inside of the star surrounded by
a spherical surface to be the Friedman-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW)
spacetime and match it to the static vacuum solution outside the surface.
One obtains the Friedmann equation for a scale factor that determines the size
of the star as a function of time.
In the classical case, the homogeneous dust interior can be matched to a
vacuum Schwarzschild exterior.
In the Hamiltonian formalism, usually spherically symmetric spacetime is
minimally coupled to dust and is known as a Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi (LTB)
model
[
33
,
39
,
14
]
.

Many approaches using modified LTB models have been applied to quantum-inspired
gravity
models
[
40
,
31
,
32
,
7
,
12
,
21
]
.
Particularly, those motivated by loop quantum gravity use the
Ashtekar-Pawlowski-Singh
[
4
]
metric in the
interior glued continuously to a static spherically symmetric
spacetime on the exterior.
The methods often result in the deformed Friedmann equation

\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\rho\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\rho_{c}}\right),

(1)

where
a
is a time dependent scale factor and
\rho
is the density of the
star.
The Friedmann equation is deformed by an extra
\rho/\rho_{c}
term, where

\rho_{c}
is a critical density.
The critical density term in the Freedman equation induces an effective
pressure and can be related to the time when the gravitational attraction
vanishes.
The critical density goes to infinity in the classical limit.
If the initial mass is high enough, collapse proceeds as it does classically,
with a trapping horizon forming and enclosing the collapsing matter at some
time.
The star reaches a maximum density and minimum radius, and then bounces.
The quantum gravity effects stop the collapse, the dust smoothly bounces
back, and there is no singularity.
The geometry continues regularly into an expanding white-hole phase.

However, when modifications to general relativity are introduced, the interior
dynamics can often no longer match to the static exterior
[
23
]
.
This is because the modifications lead to effective pressures.
Moreover, in order to understand horizon formation, we need to impose matching
conditions.
A common approach is to couple to a Vaidya-type spacetime.

Another possibility is to use generalized Painlevé-Gullstrand (PG)
coordinates to describe the metric and the matching conditions will be
manifestly satisfied
[
27
]
.
This could allow the full spacetime metric to be written in a single unique
coordinate patch
[
32
]
.
The formation or avoidance of the singularity is independent of the matching to
the exterior.

We use a canonical and covariant framework to study modified LTB spacetimes
with quantum-inspired gravity corrections using generalized PG coordinates.
The deformed Hamiltonian constraint of spherical gravity can be coupled to
any matter field with a closed hypersurface deformed
algebra
[
13
,
9
]
.
The hypersurface deformed algebra encodes the covariance of the theory.
The structure function transforms properly to define a metric in a covariant
way.
The model is covariant in that gauge choices in phase space correspond to
coordinate transformations in spacetime.
The resulting geometry is gauge independent.

A family of vacuum models are presented that ensure the closure of the algebra
and the correct transformation properties of the structure function.
The models being covariant allow the construction of the associated geometry.
The quantum-inspired gravity models can be considered as regularized versions
of the Schwarzschild black hole leading to a transition surface between the
black hole and white hole.
We do not specifically considering polymeric or holonomy corrections in the
classical Hamiltonian constraint and the resulting modified LTB
spacetimes
[
25
,
34
,
24
,
20
]
.
The gravity corrections we consider are more agnostic.

Section

in this paper.
All equations written here reduce to the classical expressions in the

In all plots, a total mass

2
Effective model

In this section, we write down the action and Hamiltonian constraints of the
effective model.
The dust-time and areal gauges are applied to obtain the physical Hamiltonian.

The general diagonal line element for a static spherically symmetric spacetime
can be written in Schwarzschild coordinates as

ds^{2}=-\left(h_{1}(x)-\frac{2m}{h_{2}(x)}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{h_{3}(x)}\left(h_{1}(x)-\frac{2m}{h_{2}(x)}\right)^{-1}dx^{2}+x^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(2)

with shape functions
h_{1}(x)
,
h_{2}(x)
, and
h_{3}(x)
.
For the classical case, the shape functions are
h_{1}(x)=h_{3}(x)=1
and
h_{2}=x
.
The shape functions in the line element have been defined so the ADM mass
m

will not appear in the Hamiltonian, since
m
often arises as an integration
constant.

The corresponding gravity Hamiltonian with dynamical flow leading to this
metric is known
[
3
]
.
Furthermore the Hamiltonian has been shown to be covariant and lead to
the correct coordinate charts for different choices of
gauge
[
2
]
.
See
[
22
]
for a nontrivial example.
The resulting spacetime has been shown to be the same as that given by
emergent modified gravity
[
8
]
.
Including a dust field, and integrating over
d\Omega
, the action
is
[
26
]

S=\int dt\int dx\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{K}_{x}E^{x}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{K}_{\varphi}E^{\varphi}+4\pi\dot{T}p_{T}-N\left(\mathcal{H}^{(g)}+\mathcal{H}^{(d)}\right)-N^{x}\left(\mathcal{H}_{x}^{(g)}+\mathcal{H}_{x}^{(d)}\right)\right],

(3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{E^{\varphi}}{2h_{2}}(h_{1}h_{2})^{\prime}-\frac{K_{\varphi}^{2}E^{\varphi}}{2h_{2}}(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}+\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{2\sqrt{E^{x}}}\left(\frac{(E^{x})^{\prime}}{E^{\varphi}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{2h_{2}}{\sqrt{E^{x}}}-3h_{2}^{\prime}\right)\frac{h_{2}}{E^{x}}\frac{[(E^{x})^{\prime}]^{2}}{8E^{\varphi}}-2\sqrt{E^{x}}h_{3}K_{x}K_{\varphi},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(d)}

\displaystyle=4\pi p_{T}\sqrt{1+\frac{h_{2}^{2}h_{3}}{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}\left(T^{\prime}\right)^{2}},

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=-K_{x}(E^{x})^{\prime}+E^{\varphi}K_{\varphi}^{\prime},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{x}^{(d)}

\displaystyle=-4\pi p_{T}T^{\prime}.

(7)

The primes denote differentiation with respect to
x
.
In these expressions
N(x,t)
and
N^{x}(x,t)
are the lapse and the radial
component of the shift vector which are Lagrange multipliers and not dynamical.
The phase-space variables
E^{x}(x,t)
and
E^{\varphi}(x,t)
are the
densitized triad in the radial and angular directions, respectively.
The densitized triads are conjugate to the extrinsic curvature related variables

K_{x}(x,t)
and
K_{\varphi}(x,t)
.
The dust field is
T(x,t)
and
p_{T}(x,t)
its conjugate momentum.

The smear form of the Hamiltonian constraints are defined as
H_{x}[s]=\int dxs\mathcal{H}_{x}
and
H[s]=\int dxs\mathcal{H}
.
The Hamiltonian constraint satisfies the canonical hypersurface deformation
algebra with structure function

q^{xx}=\frac{h_{2}^{2}h_{3}}{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}

(8)

and the line element suitable for canonical analysis is

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-N^{2}dt^{2}+\frac{1}{q^{xx}}(dx+N^{x}dt)^{2}+E^{x}d\Omega^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle=-\left(N^{2}-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{q^{xx}}\right)dt^{2}+2\frac{N^{x}}{q^{xx}}dxdt+\frac{1}{q^{xx}}dx^{2}+E^{x}d\Omega^{2}.

The diagonal line element (
2
) written in general PG coordinates
is

ds^{2}=-\left(h_{1}-\frac{2m}{h_{2}}\right)d\tau^{2}-2\sqrt{1-h_{1}+\frac{2m}{h_{2}}}dxd\tau+\frac{1}{h_{3}}dx^{2}+E^{x}d\Omega^{2}.

(10)

The reference to “general” (or pseudo) is because the usual flat spatial
slices are modified by a
1/h_{3}
coefficient.

We will solve the Hamiltonian equations of motion for all of the six fields
q=E^{x}
,
E^{\varphi}
,
K_{x}
,
K_{\varphi}
,
T
, and
p_{T}

using
\dot{q}=\{q,H[N]+H_{x}[N^{x}]\}
, where the dot represents the time
derivative.
The two constraint equations
\mathcal{H}_{x}=0
and
\mathcal{H}=0
will
also be used.

The dust-time gauge

[
15
,
29
,
36
,
24
,
20
]

is used to fix the scalar
constraint and the areal gauge to fix the diffeomorphism constraint.
This gauge fixing leaves a physical Hamiltonian with no constraints remaining.
In the dust-time gauge
T=t
and thus
T^{\prime}=0
.
The dust contribution to the diffeomorphism constraint vanishes.
Solving the scalar constraint in the action gives

\mathcal{H}^{(g)}=-4\pi p_{T}.

(11)

Ensuring this gauge condition be preserved by the dynamics gives
N=1
.
This physically implies that the dust moves along timelike geodesics of the
spacetime.

We employ the areal gauge
E^{x}=x^{2}
.
The diffeomorphism constraint (
6
) can be solved to give

K_{x}=\frac{E^{\varphi}K_{\varphi}^{\prime}}{2x}.

(12)

The equation of motion for
E^{x}
is

\dot{E}^{x}=2xh_{3}K_{\varphi}+2xN^{x}.

(13)

Requiring
E^{x}=x^{2}
be preserved dynamically,
\dot{E}^{x}=0
, gives

N^{x}=-h_{3}K_{\varphi}.

(14)

Since
E^{x}
is time independent, the term
\dot{K}_{x}E^{x}
in the
action (
3
) is a total derivative in time and can be dropped.
Applying all the conditions above gives the gauge fixed action

S_{\mathrm{GF}}=\int dt\int dx\left[\frac{\dot{K}_{\varphi}E^{\varphi}}{2}-\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}\right],

(15)

where the true physical Hamiltonian is
\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}=\mathcal{H}^{(g)}
, as there are no constraints remaining.
There is one physical degree of freedom remaining at each point due to the dust
field.

Constraint equations and solutions

In this section, we write down the two remaining equations of motion and solve
them along with the Hamiltonian scalar constraint to obtain modified LTB
equations.
The remaining constraint equations before gauge fixing are

\displaystyle\dot{E}^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{NK_{\varphi}E^{\varphi}}{h_{2}}(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}+2Nh_{3}\sqrt{E^{x}}K_{x}+(N^{x}E^{\varphi})^{\prime},

(16)

\displaystyle\dot{K}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{N}{2h_{2}}(h_{1}h_{2})^{\prime}-\frac{NK_{\varphi}^{2}}{2h_{2}}(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}+\left(\frac{Nh_{2}^{2}}{2\sqrt{E^{x}}}\right)^{\prime}\frac{(E^{x})^{\prime}}{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N}{2}\left(\frac{2h_{2}}{\sqrt{E^{x}}}-3h_{2}^{\prime}\right)\frac{h_{2}}{E^{x}}\left(\frac{(E^{x})^{\prime}}{2E^{\varphi}}\right)^{2}+N^{x}K_{\varphi}^{\prime}.

Replacing
N=1
,

, and using the previous expressions for

(

\displaystyle\dot{E}^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}}{h_{2}}-h_{3}^{\prime}\right)K_{\varphi}E^{\varphi}-h_{3}K_{\varphi}(E^{\varphi})^{\prime},

\displaystyle\dot{K}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(h_{1}h_{2})^{\prime}}{2h_{2}}-\frac{(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}}{2h_{2}}K_{\varphi}^{2}+\left(\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{x}\right)^{\prime}\frac{x}{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2h_{2}}{x}-3h_{2}^{\prime}\right)\frac{h_{2}}{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}-h_{3}K_{\varphi}K_{\varphi}^{\prime}.

Conservation of

This function does not satisfy the LTB condition

In the classical limit,

With

, the Hamiltonian constraint and last equation of motion

\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}=-\frac{(h_{1}h_{2})^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}}{2}K_{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x}{h_{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2h_{2}}{x}-3h_{2}^{\prime}\right)-h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}K_{\varphi}^{\prime},

\dot{K}_{\varphi}=-\frac{(h_{1}h_{2})^{\prime}}{2h_{2}}-\frac{(h_{2}h_{3})^{\prime}}{2h_{2}}K_{\varphi}^{2}+\left(\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{x}\right)^{\prime}\frac{x}{h_{2}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2h_{2}}{x}-3h_{2}^{\prime}\right)\frac{1}{h_{2}}-h_{3}K_{\varphi}K_{\varphi}^{\prime}.

At this point, we specialize to
h_{1}=1
, which still includes a wide class of

We note that if one is not worried about having the parameter
m
appearing in
the Hamiltonian,
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are not uniquely defined for many metrics.
Simplification with
h_{1}=1
gives

\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}=-\frac{1}{2}(h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}^{2})^{\prime}

(23)

and

\dot{K}_{\varphi}=-\frac{1}{2h_{2}}(h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}^{2})^{\prime},

(24)

which indicates

\dot{K}_{\varphi}=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}}{h_{2}}.

(25)

Equation (

) is a first order partial differential equation that

given initial and boundary conditions.
Once
K_{\varphi}
is known,

is determined by (

)
and (

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.
Solving for

K_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{(t-t_{0})\sqrt{h_{2}h_{3}}}\int^{x}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}}+C,

where

The

N^{x}=-\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{h_{3}}{h_{2}}}\int^{x}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}},

where we have set

The physical Hamiltonian becomes

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=-\frac{1}{(t-t_{0})^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}}\int^{x}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}},

where any constant in

With the areal gauge and solution

, the structure function now

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{h_{3}}\right)dt^{2}+2\frac{N^{x}}{h_{3}}dxdt+\frac{1}{h_{3}}dx^{2}+x^{2}d\Omega^{2}.

4
Dust density

The energy density
\rho(x,t)
of the dust field is related to the dust
contribution to the scalar constraint by
p_{T}=\sqrt{q}\rho
, where

\sqrt{q}
is the determinant of the spatial part of the metric.
The metric components are

q_{xx}=\frac{(E^{\varphi})^{2}}{h_{2}^{2}h_{3}},\quad q_{\vartheta\vartheta}=E^{x},\quad q_{\varphi\varphi}=E^{x}

(30)

giving the determinant

\sqrt{q}=\frac{E^{\varphi}E^{x}}{h_{2}\sqrt{h}_{3}}=\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}.

(31)

The density is

\rho(x,t)=\frac{h_{2}\sqrt{h_{3}}p_{T}}{E^{x}E^{\varphi}}=-\frac{\sqrt{h_{3}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{phys}}}{4\pi x^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{h_{2}}}{4\pi(t-t_{0})^{2}x^{2}}\int^{x}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}}.

(32)

We expect
\rho(x,t)
to be positive and monotonic.

Further insight can be obtained by changing the order of the solution of

\rho(x,t)
.
The energy density follows the Hamiltonian profile (
23
):

\rho=\frac{\sqrt{h_{3}}}{8\pi x^{2}}(h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}^{2})^{\prime}.

(33)

In addition, (
24
) gives

\dot{K}_{\varphi}=-4\pi\rho\frac{x^{2}}{h_{2}\sqrt{h_{3}}}.

(34)

We can invert (
33
) for
K_{\varphi}
to get

K_{\varphi}=-\left[\frac{8\pi}{h_{2}h_{3}}\int^{x}dx\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}\rho\right]^{1/2}=-\frac{N^{x}}{h_{3}}.

(35)

The minus sign is chosen so
N^{x}>0
in (
14
) and the star
collapses in the PG coordinates.
Choosing the positive root is equivalent to reversing the time coordinate, and
gives an expanding model, or correspondingly, flipping the sign in the PG
coordinate definition.
Differentiating (
35
) with respect to time gives

\dot{K}_{\varphi}=-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{h_{2}h_{3}}}\int^{x}dx\frac{\dot{\rho}x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}\left[\int^{x}dx\frac{\rho x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}\right]^{-1/2}.

(36)

Equating this with (
34
) leads to

\left(\int^{x}dx\frac{\dot{\rho}x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}\right)^{2}=8\pi\frac{\rho^{2}x^{4}}{h_{2}}\int^{x}dx\frac{\rho x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}.

(37)

This is the integral equation to be solved for
\rho(x,t)
and has
solution (
32
).
It is a modified version of the deformed Freedman equation (
1
)
that results from many other approaches.
Our result is missing the critical density term but instead includes an

x
-dependent factor.
If
\rho(x,t)=\rho(t)
was assumed, the density would have an
x
-dependent
factor in it, thus falsifying the assumption.
The
x
-dependent function only vanishes for
h_{3}=1
and
h_{2}=x
, as in the
classical case and we would obtain the Freeman equation.
Only under these conditions will
\rho
be homogeneous solution to (
37
).

The polymer quantization spacetimes motivated by loop quantum gravity do not
have this issue
[
29
]
.
The
\rho(x,t)=\rho(t)
assumption is made.
This allows
\rho
to be removed from the integral and the
x
dependence after
integration vanishes in the solution, thus justifying the assumption.
The polymer corrections introduce trigonometric functions in the LTB equations.
When solving these equations for the density, a differentiation of an inverse
sine function is take which results in an extra term corresponding to the
critical density term in (
1
).
The critical density term in the Freeman equation is a result of introducing
quantum corrections using a bounded (trigonometric) form.

The polymar quantization approach often starts from a noncovariant Hamiltonian
and imposes the PG gauge
[
28
]
.
If instead the Schwarzschild gauge is imposed, the Schwarzschild diagonal line
element results (consistent with
h_{3}=1
and
h_{2}=x
), which can not be
related to the polymar quantization PG line element by a coordinate
transformation.
This explains the apparent inconsistency between the results.
In Section
7
, our result will be shown to be equivalent to the
polymer quantization result
[
34
]
.

5
Dust evolution

In the Oppenheimer-Snyder model of black hole collapse, the space time belongs
to the family of LTB solutions.
This corresponds to a star of radius
L(t)
with vacuum outside:
\rho(x,t)=0
for
x>L(t)
.
We define
L(t)
as the comoving radius of the outer dust.
The star is composed of dust within the interior:
\rho(x,t)
for
x\leq L(t)
.
In specific cases,
\rho(x,t)=\rho(t)
is a spatially homogeneous density,
but we will need to be more general.
Inhomogeneous dust collapse has been considered in
[
35
]
.
The inhomogeneities affect the structure of the bounce curve and the trapped
regions.

To describe the vacuum region, we let the energy density vanish at some radius
on the initial hypersurface.
The solution must be diffeomorphic to the Schwarzschild-like metric.
The initial state of the system can be considered as purely classical and all
the quantum corrections can be neglected at the initial time.

For
x>L(t),\rho(x,t)=0
and (
33
) gives
(h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}^{2})^{\prime}=0
.
The only matter constant available is the total mass
M
of the dust ball.
We take
2M
as the constant to allow consistent mass definitions.
Thus
h_{2}h_{3}K_{\varphi}^{2}=2M

giving

(N^{x})^{2}=h_{3}^{2}K_{\varphi}^{2}=\frac{2Mh_{3}}{h_{2}}.

(38)

This is consistent with the general PG line element for vacuum (
10
)
with the identification
h_{1}=1
when compared to the canonical
form (
9
).

We can define the effective mass function

m(x,t)=4\pi\int^{x}dx\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{3}}}\rho,

(39)

where
m(x,t)
is the enclosed mass within a dust shell located at
x
at time

t
.
Once the initial energy density is specified, one can evaluate the initial
mass function through this definition.
This is the mass function commonly defined in LTB spacetimes.
Combined with (
37
) which is an integral in
x
and first order in

t
, we can obtain

\dot{m}^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{2mh_{3}}{h_{2}}}m^{\prime}=0,

(40)

which is a general version of the classical result in
[
32
]
.

Using (
35
) and the effective mass, for
x<L(t)
we write

(N^{x})^{2}=\frac{2m(x,t)h_{3}}{h_{2}}.

(41)

At
x=L(t)
, the effective mass is time independent and
m(L,t)=M
.
This demonstrates that
(N^{x})^{2}
is continuous at the boundary.
The later expression changes sign, while the former does not.
Note that
N^{x}=0
when
h_{3}=0
.

The different layers of dust are labelled by
x
in the
density (
32
).
The effective mass using (
39

m(x,t)=\frac{1}{2(t-t_{0})^{2}}\left(\int^{x}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}}\right)^{2}.

(42)

The use or the areal gauge implies that a sphere at radius
x
has surface area

4\pi x^{2}
.
The constancy of dust mass
M
reflects energy conservation during the
collapse, as no mass is lost to radiation or external forces in this idealized
scenario.
This relates
\rho
to the radius of the outermost shell at
x=L(t)
.
The constant
t_{0}
is fixed to allow
L=L_{0}
(the minimum radius) when
t=0
.
The PG metric has two branches.
For
-\infty<t\leq 0
,
L(t)
is decreasing until
t=0
when

L(t)=L_{0}
, then for
0\leq t<\infty
,
L(t)
is increasing.
Equating the mass at the boundary
m(L,t)=M
gives

(t-t_{0})^{2}=\frac{1}{2M}\left(\int^{L(t)}dx\sqrt{\frac{h_{2}}{h_{3}}}\right)^{2}.

(43)

The velocity of the outer shell is given by

v=\pm\sqrt{2M}\sqrt{\frac{h_{3}}{h_{2}}}.

(44)

The velocity vanishes at
h_{3}=0
, where the minimum surface occurs.

The continuity of the dust evolution equation (
43
) can be
examined for typical shape functions which are a few terms of the form

a_{n}/x^{n}
, where
a_{n}
are constant parameters and
n=0
can be one of the
terms.
Notice that
L
versus
t
will be continuous for all
L\neq L_{0}
.
If
L_{0}=0
,
L
versus
t
will be discontinuous at
t=0
.
In all cases of a bounce that we will consider, the dust motion is continuous
at the bounce surface.
The conditions to achieve this are that
1/h_{2}
or
h_{3}
have at least one
positive non-zero root.

6
Apparent horizons

We determine the apparent horizons by considering congruences of null geodesics.
The scalar expansions are

\theta_{+}=(\ln E^{x})^{\prime}(N\sqrt{q^{xx}}-N^{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{-}=-(\ln E^{x})^{\prime}(N\sqrt{q^{xx}}+N^{x}).

(45)

Details of the derivation of these equations can be found in
Appendix
A
.
A trapped surface occurs where both expansions
\theta_{\pm}
are negative, and
the boundary of the total trapped region is the apparent horizon.
In our case, since
\theta_{-}<0
, for all
x
, the apparent horizons corresponds
to the surfaces
\theta_{+}=0
.

Using
N=1
,
q^{xx}=h_{3}
, and
N^{x}=\sqrt{2m(x,t)h_{3}/h_{2}}
, we see that
apparent horizons will occur when
h_{3}=0
or
h_{2}=2m(x,t)
.
The former is static and independent of the dust mass.
In many cases it will correspond to a surface of reflection symmetry.
We will determine the apparent horizons for some example metrics in the next
section.

Collapse and bounce

We now consider some common static and spherically symmetric metrics, and

We also comment on the apparent horizons.
First consider some cases of

7.1
Schwarzschild spacetime

The Schwarzschild line element in diagonal form is

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(46)

where
0<r<\infty
.
The shape functions from the metric coefficients are
h_{1}=1
,
h_{2}=x
, and

h_{3}=1
.
The evolution equation (
43
) can be solved to give

t^{2}=\frac{1}{2M}\left(\int^{L}dx\sqrt{x}\right)^{2}=\frac{2}{9M}L^{3}.

(47)

A singularity exits at
r=0
, where the evolution
L(t)
is discontinuous and

v\to\pm\infty
.
An apparent horizon forms when
L(t)=2M
.

7.2
\bar{\mu}
-loop black hole

Kelly, Santacruz and Wilson-Ewing
[
28
]
have obtained an
effective loop quantum gravity spacetime using the
\bar{\mu}
-scheme (scale
dependent) holonomy corrections in the areal gauge in PG coordinates.
Note that the model as originally presented is not covariant and different
gauge choices lead to different geometries, which cannot be related to
coordinate transformations
[
3
]
.
The metric in the Schwarzschild gauge is the classical Schwarzschild metric, so
instead we use the line element in
[
34
]
:

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{4M^{2}\gamma^{2}\Delta}{r^{4}}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{4M^{2}\gamma^{2}\Delta}{r^{4}}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(48)

where
(2M\gamma^{2}\Delta)^{1/3}<r<\infty
.
This metric arises uniquely by matching the Ashtekar-Pawlowski-Singh dust
spacetime to an external spherically symmetric vacuum spacetime outside the
dust
[
34
]
.

The shape functions from the metric coefficients are
h_{1}=1
,
h_{3}=1
, and

h_{2}=\frac{x^{4}}{x^{3}-2\gamma^{2}\tilde{\Delta}},

(49)

with
\tilde{\Delta}=M\Delta
giving from (
43
)

t^{2}=\frac{1}{2M}\left(\int^{L}dx\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{3}-2\gamma^{2}\tilde{\Delta}}}\right)^{2}=\frac{2}{9M}L^{3}\left(1-\frac{2M\gamma^{2}\Delta}{L^{3}}\right).

(50)

Notice the time evolution involves two terms: the classical term and minimum
radius term at which a bounce occurs.
The correct classical limit is obtained when
\Delta\to 0
and the minimum
radius approaches zero.

This is the same result obtained in
[
29
]
by other means.
In that work,
h_{2}=x
(homogeneous dust), is assumed as in the classical case.
The LTB equations are then modified by holonomy corrections inspired by loop
quantum gravity.
The calculation required the derivative of an inverse sine function which gives
rise to two terms of which one is the additional term in the distorted FLRW
equation (
1
).
Starting from the metric arising from the holonomy corrections, we solve the
modified Hamiltonian constrain equations (not classical) to get the same
result.
In our case, the second term that modifies the classical collapse and thus
gives a bounce is a result of the shape function
h_{2}
rather than the result
of a bounded function
[
25
]
.

Two apparent horizon
r_{\pm}
form when
h_{2}(r_{\pm})=2m(r_{\pm},t)
, or at the
real roots of

r_{\pm}^{4}-2Mr_{\pm}^{3}+4\gamma^{2}\Delta M^{2}=0

(51)

in agreement with
[
28
]
.
None, one, or two apparent horizons are possible.

Next we consider the case when the lime element is not of the

h_{2}=x
, but

7.3
Simpson-Visser spacetime

The Simpson-Visser metric shares many features with quantum-inspired metrics

The line element can be written as

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(52)

The shape functions from the metric coefficients are

We solve this trajectory numerically in Section

7.4
Covariant loop black hole

In emergent gravity, the line element in the Schwarzschild gauge reads

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{x}\right)dt^{2}+\left[1+\lambda^{2}\left(1-\frac{2M}{x}\right)\right]^{-1}\left(1-\frac{2M}{x}\right)^{-1}\frac{dx^{2}}{\chi^{2}}+x^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(55)

where
\chi
is an undetermined constant and
\lambda
is an undetermined

The classical limit is obtained when
\chi\to 1
and

7.4.1
Scale-independent holonomies

If
\lambda
is asymptotically constant and we take
\chi^{2}=1/(1+\lambda^{2})

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{x}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{r_{0}}{x}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{2M}{x}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+x^{2}d\Omega^{2}.

(56)

The parameter

(57)

From this line element,

,

h_{3}=\frac{x-r_{0}}{x}

(58)

In this case, there is an additional multiplicative positive function.

r_{0}\to 0

This is the same result obtained in

]

8
Concluding remarks

We have solved the canonical system of constraints and equations of motion to
obtain a dynamical dust density and radial shift vector in PG coordinates.
We make no assumptions about the homogeneity of the dust density.
We do not start from a black hole spacetime or assume a preexisting horizon.
The form of the static metric outside the density ball is the same form as the
dynamical metric inside the dust ball.
This requires matching of the radial shift vector at the boundary.
The approach is similar to
[
28
]
which applied the procedure to a

\bar{\mu}
-loop black hole.

Painlevé-Gullstrand coordinates have an advantage of describing the motion
of in-falling particles across the horizon, but they are not without
shortcomings.
PG coordinates can not provide a global coordinate patch for the
quantum-corrected Oppenheimer-Snyder model as they do in the classical case.
They do not provide a maximal analytic extension of Schwarzschild spacetime
and thus cover only part of the extended manifold
[
16
]
.
There are issues when describing Reissner-Nordström and
Schwarzschild-anti-deSitter spacetimes, for example, by PG
coordinates
[
18
]
.
They may also not work well for some quantum-inspired black holes with multiple
horizons.

It is important that the metric be continuous across the dust boundary.
Although
(N^{x})^{2}
is continuous at the boundary,
N^{x}
changes sign in the
solution of the interior at
t=0
but does not change sign in the exterior
metric.
The cross term in the static PG line element (
10
) is always negative
for in-falling observers.
In order to describe out-falling, a positive sign in the cross term would be
used.
A discontinuity in a metric coefficients occurs at the bounce.
This discontinuity was tentatively interpreted as a physical discontinuity
of the gravitational field and it was argued that a shock wave must form as a
consequence
[
28
,
25
,
36
]
.
The PG coordinates mean
t
is the proper time of observers moving at constant
comoving radial coordinate and
x
is the comoving radial coordinate in the
dust interior.
The dust bounces out in the same asymptotic region from where it collapsed.
It is possible to interpret this discontinuity as a discontinuity of the clock
field (coordinate effect), evolving in a continuous geometry.
The geometry in this spacetime is continuous, and the discontinuity in the
metric comes only from using the dust field as a relational clock.
No shock wave is formed
[
19
]
.
While using dust may seem nonideal, other types of fields are not without their
shortcomings
[
10
]
.

In the common approach of using a Friedmann interior, it can be open (
k=-1
), flat (
k=0
), or closed (
k=1
).
Only the closed (
k=1
) case is appropriate since a star must start from a
large radius with a non-zero velocity.
It is unphysical to start from infinity at rest; the initial rate of change of
density would be zero and the initial moment at the maximum expansion.
Closed (
k=1)
is inconsistent with PG coordinates in which an observer at
infinity free falls from rest.
One can only use PG coordinates for
k=0
, but Novikov coordinates can be used
for
k=1

[
30
,
16
]
.
As we use PG coordinates starting at rest at infinity, we understanding that the
initial behaviour may not be physical.
Subsequent work should address this issue.

We have not consider energy conditions and they are usually violated for
quantum-inspired spacetimes.
We can neglect the weak energy condition violation if we re-interpret the exotic
matter content on the right of Einstein’s equation as a semiclassical limit
coming from an effective theory of quantum gravity inducing corrections in the
strong-gravity regime
[
5
]
.

The quantum gravity effects encoded in the shape functions generate a
non-singular transition from a black hole to something similar to a white hole
(post-bounce solution).
The minimum non-zero radius ensures the metric is nowhere and never singular.
General relativity appears to be a singular limit of the models.
The next step could be to study the casual structure and energy conditions
using these shape functions.

Appendix A
Scalar expansion

In this appendix, we consider the congruence of null geodesics and
calculate the scalar expansion using the general spherically symmetric
canonical line element.
Due to spherical symmetry, it is sufficient to consider congruences that are
orthogonal to the surface of concentric 2-spheres defined by constant
x
and

t
.

The general line element in canonical form is

ds^{2}=-\left(N^{2}-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{q^{xx}}\right)dt^{2}+2\frac{N^{x}}{q^{xx}}dtdx+\frac{1}{q^{xx}}dx^{2}+E^{x}d\Omega^{2},

(65)

where we can identify the metric coefficients as

g_{00}=\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{q^{xx}}-N^{2},\quad g_{01}=g_{10}=\frac{N^{x}}{q^{xx}},\quad g_{11}=\frac{1}{q^{xx}},\quad g_{22}=E^{x},\quad g_{33}=E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta.

(66)

The nonzero inverse metric coefficients are

g^{00}=-\frac{1}{N^{2}},\quad g^{01}=g^{10}=\frac{N^{x}}{N^{2}},\quad g^{11}=-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{N^{2}}+q^{xx},\quad g^{22}=\frac{1}{E^{x}},\quad g^{33}=\frac{1}{E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta}.

(67)

And the determinant is

\sqrt{-g}=NE^{x}\sin^{2}\theta.

(68)

The geodesic equation with metric compatibility is

\kappa=-g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\lambda}=\left(N^{2}-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{q^{xx}}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{d\lambda}\right)^{2}-2\frac{N^{x}}{q^{xx}}\frac{dt}{d\lambda}\frac{dx}{d\lambda}-\frac{1}{q^{xx}}\left(\frac{dx}{d\lambda}\right)^{2},

(69)

where
\lambda
is an affine parameter and
\kappa
is a constant.

For massless particles,
\kappa=0
and we take
\lambda\to t
to give

\left(N^{2}-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{q^{xx}}\right)-2\frac{N^{x}}{q^{xx}}\frac{dx}{dt}-\frac{1}{q^{xx}}\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^{2}=0.

(70)

The solutions is

\frac{dx}{dt}=-N^{x}\pm N\sqrt{q^{xx}}.

(71)

We denote the tangent vector to the outgoing null geodesics by

\ell^{\mu}=\left(1,-N^{x}+N\sqrt{q^{xx}},0,0\right).

(72)

The other linearly independent null vector that is also orthogonal to the
2-sphere is

k^{\mu}=\left(1,-N^{x}-N\sqrt{q^{xx}},0,0\right),

(73)

which is the tangent vector to ingoing null geodesics.
The covariant vectors are

\ell_{\mu}=\left(\frac{NN^{x}}{\sqrt{q^{xx}}}-N^{2},\frac{N}{\sqrt{q^{xx}}},0,0\right)\quad\text{and}\quad k_{\mu}=\left(-\frac{NN^{x}}{\sqrt{q^{xx}}}-N^{2},-\frac{N}{\sqrt{q^{xx}}},0,0\right).

(74)

The normalization of the two fields is
\ell^{\mu}k_{\mu}=-2N^{2}
.

The hypersurface metric for 2-spheres is given by

h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\left(\ell_{\mu}k_{\nu}+k_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}\right),

(75)

with coefficients

h_{00}=0,\quad h_{01}=h_{10}=0,\quad h_{11}=0,\quad h_{22}=E^{x},\quad h_{33}=E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta.

(76)

The nonzero inverse coefficients are

h^{22}=\frac{1}{E^{x}},\quad h^{33}=\frac{1}{E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta}.

(77)

To evaluate covariant derivatives we will need the following Christoffel
symbols:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{22}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{0\rho}(\partial_{2}g_{2\rho}+\partial_{2}g_{\rho 2}-\partial_{\rho}g_{22})=-\frac{1}{2}g^{01}\partial_{1}g_{22}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{N^{x}}{N^{2}}(E^{x})^{\prime},

(78)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{22}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{1\rho}(\partial_{2}g_{2\rho}+\partial_{2}g_{\rho 2}-\partial_{\rho}g_{22})=-\frac{1}{2}g^{11}\partial_{1}g_{22}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{(N^{x})^{2}}{N^{2}}+q^{xx}\right)(E^{x})^{\prime}.

(79)

The outgoing and ingoing expansions are given by, respectively,

\theta_{+}=h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{-}=h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}k_{\nu}.

(80)

A calculation gives

\displaystyle\theta_{+}

\displaystyle=h^{22}\nabla_{2}\ell_{2}+h^{33}\nabla_{22}\ell_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E^{x}}(\partial_{2}\ell_{2}-\Gamma_{22}^{\rho}\ell_{\rho})+\frac{1}{E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta}(\partial_{3}\ell_{3}-\Gamma_{33}^{\rho}\ell_{\rho})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{E^{x}}(\Gamma_{22}^{0}\ell_{0}+\Gamma_{22}^{1}\ell_{1})+-\frac{1}{E^{x}\sin^{2}\theta}(\Gamma_{33}^{0}\ell_{0}+\Gamma_{33}^{1}\ell_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{E^{x}}(\Gamma_{22}^{0}\ell_{0}+\Gamma_{22}^{1}\ell_{1}).

(81)

The final result is

\theta_{+}=(\ln E^{x})^{\prime}(N\sqrt{q^{xx}}-N^{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{-}=-(\ln E^{x})^{\prime}(N\sqrt{q^{xx}}+N^{x}).

(82)

These general formula reduce to the usual specific forms.

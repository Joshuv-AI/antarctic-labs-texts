On a theorem of Keller over a base ring
Zhihang Chen and Junwu Tu

Abstract
Let X X be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base field. Keller proved a theorem stating that the cyclic homology of X X is canonically isomorphic to the cyclic homology of the dg category 𝖯𝖾𝗋𝖿 ⁡ ( X ) \Perf(X) consisting of perfect complexes over X X . This theorem shows the categorical nature of the cyclic homology. In this note, we generalize Keller’s theorem to allow X X be defined over a base commutative ring.



1
Introduction

Let
X
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base field
\mathbb{K}
. An important set of invariants associated with
X
is its Hochschild homology
HH_{\bullet}(X)
and cyclic homology
HC_{\bullet}(X)
. They are defined as the hyper-cohomology groups of the sheafification of the Hochschild complexes and cyclic chain complexes respectively.

On the other hand, let
\Perf(X)
denote the dg category of perfect complexes over
X
. We emphasize that this is a dg-enhancement of the full subcategory of the bounded derived category
D^{b}(X)
consisting of perfect complexes over
X
. For any small
\mathbb{K}
-linear dg category, we may also define purely algebraically its own Hochschild type invariants. In the case of
\Perf(X)
, we are naturally lead to the question if
HH_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(X)\big)
(respectively
HC_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(X)\big)
) would be naturally isomorphic to the geometrically defined invariants
HH_{\bullet}(X)
(respectively
HC_{\bullet}(X)
). In
[
3
]
, Keller obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base field
\mathbb{K}
. Then there exist canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(X)\big)\to HH_{\bullet}(X),

\displaystyle HC_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(X)\big)\to HC_{\bullet}(X).

In the study of logrithmic Hochschild homology
[
7
,
2
]
, one is often lead to consider Hochschild homology relative to a base space
[
6
]
. This serves as at least one of the motivations to obtain a generalization of Keller’s theorem above in the relative setting. More precisely, we prove the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base commutative ring
R
. Then there are canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle HH_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(\mathfrak{X})/R\big)\to HH_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{X}/R),

\displaystyle HC_{\bullet}\big(\Perf(\mathfrak{X})/R\big)\to HC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{X}/R).

Note that in defining the Hochschild invariants above, when the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is not flat over
R
, we need to replace it by a flat resolution. This homology theory is sometimes called Shukla-Hochschild homology
[
8
]
. Similarly, such a replacement is also needed for the dg category
\Perf(\mathfrak{X})
. The proof of the relative case follows the same line as that of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, in Section
3
we briefly sketch Keller’s original proof. In Section
4
we deduce our main result by taking care of flatness using semi-free resolutions of dg categories.

1.1
Conventions and Notations.

We work over a base field
\mathbb{K}
. For a dg category
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{K}
, denote by
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
its homotopy category. This is a category with the same underlying objects as
\mathcal{C}
and with

{\sf Hom}_{\underline{\mathcal{C}}}(X,Y):=H^{0}\big(\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\big).

For a scheme
X
, in Keller’s original treatment
[
3
]
, he uses the notion of localization pairs, i.e. a pair of dg categories
\big(\per(X),{\sf acyc}(X)\big)
formed by the category of perfect complexes over
X
and its full subcategory of acyclic perfect complexes. By a result of Drinfeld
[
1
]
on the construction of dg quotients of dg categories, we may first take the dg quotient
\per(X)/{\sf acyc}(X)
as our definition of
\Perf(X)
. Due to this reason, in this paper we shall work exclusively with dg categories instead of localization pairs. In other words, in our setting, we have

\underline{\Perf(X)}\cong\underline{\per(X)}/\underline{{\sf acyc}(X)}.

Thus no further localization is needed when working with
\Perf(X)
.

2
Hochschild invariants and the trace map

2.1
Hochschild invariants of dg categories

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. A
\mathbb{K}
-linear dg category
\mathcal{C}
consists of the following data:

1.

a set of objects
{\sf Ob}(\mathcal{C})
;

2.

for each pair of objects a cochain complex
\hom^{\bullet}(X,Y)
with differential
d
;

3.

for each triple of objects an associative composition map

\hom^{\bullet}(Y,Z)\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\hom^{\bullet}(X,Y)\to\hom^{\bullet}(X,Z),

which is a chain map;

4.

for each object, there exits a unit
{\mathbbm{1}}_{X}\in\hom^{0}(X,X)
such that
d{\mathbbm{1}}_{X}=0
and
f\cdot{\mathbbm{1}}_{X}={\mathbbm{1}}_{Y}\cdot f=f
for any
f\in\hom^{\bullet}(X,Y)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small
\mathbb{K}
-linear dg category. We proceed to define its Hochschild chain complex
C_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
. As a graded vector space it is given by

\displaystyle C_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}C_{n}(\mathcal{C})[n],

\displaystyle C_{n}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}}\hom^{\bullet}(X_{n},X_{0})\otimes\hom^{\bullet}(X_{n-1},X_{n})\otimes\cdots\otimes\hom^{\bullet}(X_{0},X_{1}),

where the sum runs over all sequences
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
. Its differential denoted by
b=d+\delta
consists of two parts: the map
d
is the tensor product differential induced from the differential on the hom complexes; the other part
\delta:C_{n}\to C_{n-1}
for each
n\geq 1
is defined explicitly by

\delta(f_{n}|f_{n-1}|\cdots|f_{0}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f_{n}|\cdots|f_{i}f_{i-1}|\cdots|f_{0}+(-1)^{(n+\sigma)}f_{0}f_{n}|f_{n-1}|\cdots|f_{1}.

Furthermore, one may verify that chains of the form
f_{n}|\cdots|{\mathbbm{1}}_{X_{i}}|\cdots|f_{0}
with
i\geq 1
form a subcomplex of
C_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
. We denote its quotient complex by
C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})
. This is the
reduced
Hochschild chain complex of
\mathcal{C}
. The natural quotient map
C_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\to C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})
is a quasi-isomorphism
[
5
]
.

To obtain cyclic homology of
\mathcal{C}
, let us consider the Connes’ circle operator
B:C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})\to C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})
defined as

B(f_{n}|f_{n-1}|\cdots|f_{0}):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\mathbbm{1}}|\tau^{j}(f_{n}|f_{n-1}|\cdots|f_{0}),

where
\tau
denotes the rotation action with the Koszul sign. Denote by

M(\mathcal{C}):=\big(C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C}),b,B\big)

the triple of data defined above. It is often called the mix complex associated to the small dg category
\mathcal{C}
. From the mixed complex
M(\mathcal{C})
, one can also define the following homology theories of
\mathcal{C}
:

•

Periodic cyclic homology.

HP_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}):=H^{\bullet}\big(C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})[u^{-1},u]],b+uB\big)
;

•

Negative cyclic homology.

HC^{-}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}):=H^{\bullet}\big(C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})[[u]],b+uB\big)
;

•

Cyclic homology.

HC_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}):=H^{\bullet}\big(C_{\bullet}^{red}(\mathcal{C})[u^{-1}],b+uB\big)
.

One may verify directly that the assignment
\mathcal{C}\to M(\mathcal{C})
defines a functor from the category of small
\mathbb{K}
-linear dg categories to the category of mixed complexes over
\mathbb{K}
. Furthermore, the functor sends quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms.

2.2
The trace map

Let
X
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base field
\mathbb{K}
. Keller considers a pair of dg categories
\big(\per(X),{\sf acyc}(X)\big)
formed by the category of perfect complexes over
X
and its full subcategory of acyclic perfect complexes. Then he defines the associated mixed complex as

M\big(\per(X),{\sf acyc}(X)\big):={\sf cone}\big(M({\sf acyc}(X))\to M(\per(X))\big),

using the canonical inclusion functor. Throughout the paper, we shall denote the pair above by
\Perf(X):=\big(\per(X),{\sf acyc}(X)\big)
. By a result of Drinfeld
[
1
]
on the construction of dg quotients of dg categories, this choice of notation should cause no confusion. In other words, we may first take the dg quotient
\per(X)/{\sf acyc}(X)
and then just take its associated mixed complex.

For each Zariski open subset
U\subset X
, we obtain an assignment:

U\mapsto M\big(\Perf(U)\big),

which defines a presheaf of mixed complexes on
X
. Let us denote this presheaf by
M(\mathfrak{Perf})
. Its sheafification is denoted by
M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}
.

On the other hand, for each Zariski open subset
U\subset X
, we may consider the full sub-category of
\Perf(U)
with only one object
\mathcal{O}_{U}
. Taking its associated mixed complex yields another presheaf:

U\mapsto M\big(\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X})\big).

Denote this presheaf by
M(\mathcal{O})
. Similarly, its sheafification is denoted by
M(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The canonical inclusion map induces a quasi-isomorphism of sheaves on
X
:

i:M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp}\to M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}.

Proof.

This can be checked on the stalks.
∎

Since for any presheaf
\mathcal{F}
, there is a canonical morphism of presheaves
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}
to its sheafification. In our case, taking global sections of the morphism
M(\mathfrak{Perf})\to M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}
yields a natural map of mixed complexes

j:M\big(\Perf(X)\big)\to\Gamma\big(X,M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}\big).

To this end, let us consider the following diagram where the left vertical map
\tau
is defined by requiring the commutativity of this diagram.

\begin{CD}M\big(\Perf(X)\big)@>{j}>{}>\Gamma\big(X,M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}\big)\\
@V{\tau}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
R\Gamma(X,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})@>{i}>{}>R\Gamma\big(X,M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}\big)\end{CD}

(1)

Here the right vertical map is the canonical map from the global section functor
\Gamma
to its derived functor
R\Gamma
. Note that in order for
\tau
to be well-defined, it is important here we work in the derived category
\mathcal{D}Mix
of mixed complexes where the bottom horizontal map
i
is inverted.

3
Keller’s theorem over a base field

In
[
3
]
, Keller obtained the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a base field
\mathbb{K}
. Then trace map
\tau:M\big(\Perf(X)\big)\to R\Gamma(X,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})
defined in Diagram (
1
) is invertible in
\mathcal{D}Mix
.

In this section, we sketch the proof of this theorem following
[
3
]
.

3.1
Perfect complexes with supports

Keller’s proof makes use of perfect complexes with supports, allowing the application of Thomason-Trobaugh’s localization theorem. Let
Z\subset X
be a closed subset. Denote by
\Perf_{Z}(X)
the category of perfect complexes on
X
that are acyclic off
Z
. We proceed to define a trace map with support in
Z
:

\tau_{Z}:M\big(\Perf_{Z}(X)\big)\to R\Gamma_{Z}\big(X,M(\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\sharp}\big),

where
R\Gamma_{Z}(X,-)
denotes the local cohomology with support in
Z
. Indeed, for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, we may consider the
subsheaf of sections with support in
Z
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{Z}(\mathcal{F})
. This is a left exact functor, denote its right derived functor by

R\mathcal{H}_{Z}:\mathcal{D}Mix(X)\to\mathcal{D}Mix(Z).

This functor is right adjoint to the exact functor
i_{*}
where
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
denotes the inclusion map. Thus, we obtain a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\mathfrak{Perf}_{Z})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}R\mathcal{H}_{Z}M(\mathfrak{Perf}_{Z})^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}R\mathcal{H}_{Z}M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Then we may define the desired map
\tau_{Z}
using the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\big(\Perf_{Z}(X)\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma\big(X,M(\mathfrak{Perf}_{Z})^{\sharp}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma_{Z}\big(X,M(\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\sharp}\big)\cong R\Gamma\big(X,i_{*}R\mathcal{H}_{Z}M(\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\sharp}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma\big(X,i_{*}R\mathcal{H}_{Z}M(\mathfrak{Perf})^{\sharp}\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

The isomorphism in the lower left corner is by the Grothendieck spectral sequence.

3.2
Mayer-Vietoris property

Recall that a triangle functor
S\to T
between triangulated categories is an equivalence up to factors if it is an equivalence onto a full subcategory whose closure under forming direct summands is all of
T
. A sequence of triangulated categories

0\to\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}\to 0

is exact up to factors if the first functor is an equivalence up to factors onto the kernel of the second functor and the induced functor
\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{T}
is an equivalence up to factors.

Let us also recall Thomason-Trobaugh’s localization theorem
[
9
]
of perfect complexes.

Theorem 3.2
.

(1) Let
U\subset X
be a quasi-compact open subscheme and let
Z=X\backslash U
. Then the sequence
0\to\underline{\Perf_{Z}(X)}\to\underline{\Perf(X)}\to\underline{\Perf(U)}\to 0
is exact up to factors. Here
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the homotopy of
\mathcal{C}
.

(2) Suppose that
X=V\bigcup W
, with both
V
and
W
quasi-compact subschemes and put
Z=X\backslash W
. Then the following diagram is commutative.

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf_{Z}(X)}@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf(X)}@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf(W)}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{j^{*}}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf_{Z}(V)}@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf(V)}@>{}>{}>\underline{\Perf(V\cap W)}@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

The horizontal lines in the diagram are exact up to factors and the restriction functor
j^{*}
is an equivalence up to factors.

A crucial property of the mixed complex functor
M
is its invariance under equivalences up to factors proved by Keller
[
4
]
. This implies the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{F}\mathcal{B}\xrightarrow{G}\mathcal{C}\rightarrow 0
be a sequence of small triangulated dg categories such that
F
is fully faithful,
GF=0
, and the induced sequence of homotopy categories

0\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow 0

is exact up to factors. Then the sequence

M(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow M(\mathcal{B})\rightarrow M(\mathcal{C})

is a triangle in
\mathcal{D}Mix
.

Using the two theorems above, we obtain another commutative diagram with horizontal lines triangles in
\mathcal{D}Mix
.

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf_{Z}(X)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(X)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(W)\big)@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{\simeq}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf_{Z}(V)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(V)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(V\cap W)\big)@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

Since the left vertical map is a quasi-isomorphism, we obtain another triangle in
\mathcal{D}Mix
:

0\to M\big(\Perf(X)\big)\to M\big(\Perf(V)\big)\oplus M\big(\Perf(W)\big)\to M\big(\Perf(V\cap W)\big)\to 0,

proving the Mayer-Vietoris property of
M
. To prove Theorem
3.1
, one argues that the trace map is a map of triangles:

\begin{CD}M\big(\Perf(X)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(V)\big)\oplus M\big(\Perf(W)\big)@>{}>{}>M\big(\Perf(V\cap W)\big)\\
@V{}V{\tau_{X}}V@V{}V{\tau_{V}\oplus\tau_{W}}V@V{}V{\tau_{V\cap W}}V\\
R\Gamma(X,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})@>{}>{}>R\Gamma(V,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})\oplus R\Gamma(W,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})@>{}>{}>R\Gamma(V\cap W,M\big(\mathcal{O})^{\sharp})\end{CD}

where the bottom line is the Mayer-Vietoris triangle for hypercohomology. Theorem
3.1
is then proved by an induction argument on the cardinality of an affine open covering of
X
using its quasi-compactness.

4
The relative case

We would like to consider an analogue of Theorem
3.1
in the relative case, i.e. for a separated quasi-compact scheme
\mathfrak{X}\to{\sf Spec\,R}
defined over a commutative ring
R
. Looking through Keller’s proof sketched in the previous section, some efforts are needed to deal with Theorem
3.3
in the relative case. Our main observation is to use semi-free resolutions of dg categories whenever flatness over
R
is needed.

4.1
Semi-free resolutions

Throughout the section, we shall work with a base commutative ring
R
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a dg category and
\mathcal{A}_{0}
a dg subcategory such that
\Ob\mathcal{A}_{0}=\Ob\mathcal{A}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
semi-free over
\mathcal{A}_{0}
if
\mathcal{A}
can be represented as the union of an increasing sequence of dg subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
i=0,1,\dots
, so that
\Ob\mathcal{A}_{i}=\Ob\mathcal{A}
, and for every
i>0
the category
\mathcal{A}_{i}
as a graded
R
-linear category over
\mathcal{A}_{i-1}

(
i.e., with forgotten differentials in the
\hom
complexes
)
is freely generated over
\mathcal{A}_{i-1}
by a family of homogeneous morphisms
f_{\alpha}
such that
\mathrm{d}f_{\alpha}\in\Mor\mathcal{A}_{i-1}
.

Definition 4.2
.

A dg category
\mathcal{A}
is called
semi-free
if it is semi-free over
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}
is the dg category with
\Ob\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}=\Ob\mathcal{A}
such that the endomorphism dg algebra of each object of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}
equals
R
(with zero differential putting at degree zero) and
\hom_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}}(X,Y)=0
if
X,Y
are different objects of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{discr}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
1
, 13.5]

For every dg category
\mathcal{A}
there exists a semi-free dg category
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
with
\Ob\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\Ob\mathcal{A}
and a functor
\Psi:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{A}
such that
\Psi(X)=X
for every
X\in\Ob\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Psi
is a surjective quasi-isomorphism
\hom(X,Y)\to\hom(\Psi(X),\Psi(Y))
for every
X,Y\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\Psi)
is constructed as the direct limit of
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i},\Psi_{i})
where
\Ob\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\Ob\mathcal{A}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\rightarrow\dots
,
\Psi_{i}:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\to\mathcal{A}
,
\Psi_{i}|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i-1}}=\Psi_{i-1}
, and the following conditions are satisfied:

i)

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
is the discrete
k
-category;

ii)

for every
i\geq 1

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
as a graded
k
-category is freely generated over
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i-1}
by a family of homogeneous morphisms
f_{\alpha}
such that
df_{\alpha}\in\Mor\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i-1}
;

iii)

for every
i\geq 1
and
X,Y\in\Ob\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
the morphism
\hom_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}(X,Y)\to\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\Psi(X),\Psi(Y))
induces a surjective map between the sets of the cocycles;

iv)

for every
i\geq 2
and
X,Y\in\Ob\mathcal{A}
the morphism
\hom_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}(X,Y)\to\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\Psi(X),\Psi(Y))
is surjective;

v)

for every
i\geq 1
and
X,Y\in\Ob\mathcal{A}
every cocycle
f\in\hom_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}(X,Y)
whose image in
\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\Psi(X),\Psi(Y))
is a coboundary becomes a coboundary in
\hom_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i+1}}(X,Y)
.

One constructs
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i},\Psi_{i})
by induction. Note that if property iii) or iv) holds for some
i
then it holds for
i+1
, so after
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\Psi_{2})
is constructed these two properties are automatic.

Next, we proceed to inductively construct these categories. The category
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
is fixed by condition i). For each pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathrm{Ob}\,\mathcal{A}
, let
Z_{X,Y}:=\{g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)\mid dg=0\}
denote the set of all
cocycles
. For each cocycle
g\in Z_{X,Y}
, we introduce a formal homogeneous morphism
f_{g}
, whose degree is the same as that of
g
. Then we define the category
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and the functor
\Psi_{1}
as follows.

1.

As a graded
R
-linear category:
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is the category freely generated by
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
and all
f_{g}
(for all
X,Y
and all
g\in Z_{X,Y}
), with object set
\mathrm{Ob}\,\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=\mathrm{Ob}\,\mathcal{A}
. More concretely, For any objects
X,Y
, the space
\hom_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}(X,Y)
, as a graded module, is the free
R
-module generated by the following elements:

•

Morphisms in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
(i.e., when
X=Y

\mathrm{id}_{X}

•

All
f_{g}
for
g\in Z_{X,Y}
,

•

And compositions of these morphisms (since it is freely generated, compositions impose no relations; thus the Hom module consists of all “paths” from
X
to
Y
, where paths are composed of morphisms in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
and
f_{g}
).

2.

The differential on
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is defined by:

•

On
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
,
d=0
.

•

On each generator
f_{g}
, set
df_{g}=0
.

•

Extend to all morphisms via the Leibniz rule. Since the differential of all generators is zero, the differential of the entire
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

3.

The functor
\Psi_{1}:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}
is defined by:

•

On
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
,
\Psi_{1}
agrees with
\Psi_{0}
.

•

On each generator
f_{g}
, set
\Psi_{1}(f_{g})=g
.

•

Extend it as a dg functor.

The construction of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
is a bit more involved.

(a1)

The set of object is still given by
\operatorname{Ob}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2}=\operatorname{Ob}\mathcal{A}
.

(a2)

For each pair of objects

and each morphism

with the same degree as

.

(a3)

For each pair of objects

and each cocycle

(i.e.,

) such that
\Psi_{1}(f)=dg
for some
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
, add a homogeneous generator
a_{f}
with degree
|f|-1
.

(b1)

To define the differential
de_{h}
on the new generator
e_{h}
’s. Observe that since
\Psi_{1}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
is surjective on cocycles, for
dh\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
is a coboundary (hence a cocyle), there exists
a\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}(X,Y)
such that
\Psi_{1}(a)=dh
. Choose such an
a
and set
de_{h}=a
.

(b2)

Set
da_{f}=f
. Then we may extend the differential to all morphisms in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
via the Leibniz rule.

(c)

Define
\Psi_{2}
by extending the map
\Psi_{1}
and setting
\Psi_{2}(e_{h})=h
,
\Psi_{2}(a_{f})=g
(see part (a3) for the definition of
g
), and extend
\Psi_{2}
to be a dg functor.

Finally, the construction of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{k+1}
from
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
for
k\geq 2
is repeat the steps
(a3)
and
(b2)
above.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is semi-free, then it is flat over
R
.

Proof.

Recall a dg category over
R
is called flat if for any pair of objects
X,Y\in\Ob\mathcal{A}
and any acyclic complex
N^{\bullet}
of
R
-modules, the tensor product complex

\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)\otimes_{R}N^{\bullet}

remains acyclic. Now, assuming
\mathcal{A}
is semi-free, then the complex
\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
is filtered by

\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}(X,Y)\subset\cdots\subset\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}(X,Y)\subset\cdots,

which induces a filtration on the tensor product complex given by
\{\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}(X,Y)\otimes N^{\bullet}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. We may run the spectral sequence assoicated to this filtration. The first page of the spectral sequence is

H^{*}\big({\sf Gr}^{k}(\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y))\otimes N^{\bullet}\big).

By the semi-free property, we see the differential of the complex is
{\sf id}\otimes d_{N^{\bullet}}
and the graded
R
-module
{\sf Gr}^{k}(\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y))
is freely generated over
R
. Hence, the cohomology groups
H^{*}\big({\sf Gr}^{k}(\hom^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y))\otimes N^{\bullet}\big)=0
for all
k\geq 0
. This proves the flatness of
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

The proof of the following lemma is straight-forward from the construction of semi-free resolutions.

Lemma 4.5
.

The construction of semi-free resolutions has the following functorial properties.

1.

If
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is a dg functor, then there exists a lifting
\tilde{F}:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
to semi-free resolutions so that the following diagram is commutative.

\begin{CD}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}@>{\tilde{F}}>{}>\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\\
@V{\Psi^{\mathcal{A}}}V{}V@V{\Psi^{\mathcal{B}}}V{}V\\
\mathcal{A}@>{F}>{}>\mathcal{B}\end{CD}

Furthermore, if
F
is fully-faithful, we may also assume
\tilde{F}
be fully-faithful.

2.

If
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
and
G:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be dg functors such that
G\circ F=0
, then their lifts to semi-free resolutions also satisfy
\tilde{G}\circ\tilde{F}=0
.

Theorem 4.6
.

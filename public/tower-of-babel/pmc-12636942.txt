Increasing the rate of nursing process implementation: a quality improvement project at Wollega University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, 2025
Authors: Garoma Gemechu Tolera, Ketema Badasa, Habtamu Deressa, Hunde Fayera, Merga Chala, Meskerem Deyasa, Samuel Teshome, Dechassa Edessa, Amanuel Etefa, Adugna Olani Akuma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe nursing process is sytematic patient centered care that promotes a holistic approach, enhances communication and collaboration among healthcare professionals and ensures the delivery of high-quality, individualised care. Despite different efforts to improve the nursing process in Wollega University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, the rate of its implementation is very low due to different factors. This quality improvement project aimed to increase the rate of nursing process implementation at Wollega University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital from 1 August 2024 to 30 January 2025.The team project identified root causes by using a fishbone diagram and a driven diagram. Six interventions were introduced over 6 months using Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles. The interventions were training of nurses and leaders, shifting of nurses to wards with more workload, providing clear job descriptions for nurses, availing of protocols and work aids, availing of nursing process formats and providing regular supportive supervision.Finally, the rate of nursing process implementation increased from 27% to 87.5%, with assessment performed properly in 93%, diagnosis increased to 89.5%, planning reached 86.5%, implementation 85.5% and evaluation 83%. The highest (96%) performance was observed in the orthopaedic ward, whereas the lowest (79%) was in the gynaecological ward.This project improved the implementation of the nursing process, demonstrating the importance of capacity building for staff, leadership engagement, effective communication, regular discussion and supervision, as well as collecting feedback and incorporating it for the next interventions. The project significantly improved nursing process implementation by introducing change ideas, and it now needs to be expanded to other units. The management of the hospital and all stakeholders owned the project to maintain its sustainability.

Background

The nursing process is a systematic, patient-centred, purposeful way of delivering its components: assessment, diagnosis, planning, intervention and evaluation.1 It promotes a holistic approach, enhances communication and collaboration among healthcare professionals, and ensures the delivery of high-quality, individualised care. It is expected to provide the public with competent, safe and ethical nursing care for which the nurses are fully accountable and responsible for their entire practice.2

In Ethiopia, nurses play a crucial role in the provision of healthcare services, as they spend the majority of their time with patients. In the country, more than three-fourths of the hospitals’ healthcare activities are covered by nurses.3 The quality of healthcare service is improved by providing quality nursing care, which is measured by the implementation of the nursing process.3 4

An implementation of standardised nursing care is very important to provide care with dignity and humanitarian reason, understanding the individual’s needs, showing compassion and sensitivity, and providing care in a way that respects all people equally.5 6 Additionally, application of the standardised nursing care process (NCP) is pivotal for better patient outcomes through building a good nurse–patient relationship and maintaining adherence to healthcare regimens.7 A supportive and competent nursing workforce is required to ensure the nursing/midwifery care standard practice.8

The difficulty of implementing the theoretical part of the nursing process learnt at school in the practical area is a major challenge in the Ethiopian education system.3 This problem of NCP implementation in healthcare facilities is attributed to professional, institutional and leadership factors.6 9 Therefore, nursing service leaders and any actors of the NCP should take appropriate measures to minimise these factors and enhance implementation of the standardised nursing process in healthcare settings.3

The NCP is a crucial component of providing patient-centred care in healthcare facilities. However, there are often implementation gaps that hinder its effectiveness and impact on patient outcomes. This quality improvement project (QIP) aimed at improving the implementation of the NCP at major wards of Wollega University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (WUCSH) and identifying areas for improvement to enhance patient care.

Problem description

Even though the NCP is essential for establishing good patient–nurse relationships, providing patient-centred care and achieving better patient outcomes, there is a gap in its implementation in healthcare facilities. Lower implementation and adherence to the nursing process arise from various factors such as a lack of adequate training for nurses,10 a lack of resources11 and a lack of time for comprehensive implementation of the NCP.12 Evidence also showed that organisational factors such as lack of clear policies and guidelines, lack of adherence to established standards and protocols, and ineffective communication significantly influenced the implementation of the NCP. Different individual studies conducted reported a low level of nursing process implementation rate, which ranges from 50% to 58% in different countries of the world.13 14

In Ethiopia, the results from systematic and meta-analyses research indicated that the average of the pooled estimate of nursing process implementation was 40%.15 This gap in implementing the NCP is attributed to challenges such as limited knowledge, negative attitude, inadequate resources, high workload and time constraints, and different organisational factors.16 17 Addressing these gaps requires a multifaceted approach, including improving nursing education and training, providing adequate resources, addressing workload issues and creating a supportive organisational culture. Similarly, WUCSH endeavoured to improve the implementation of the NCP as per standard. However, due to issues with health professionals, leadership, supplies, monitoring and supervision, its rate is too low. The nursing and midwifery service audit team also reported similar findings.

Rationale

Reliable and comprehensive implementation of nursing process is pivotal for providing patient-centred, holistic and safe care. In our hospital, a conducted clinical audit in a selected ward showed that only 27% of admitted patients had appropriately documented all nursing process components—assessment, diagnosis, planning, implementation and evaluation. The evaluation components were incomplete or missing in almost all patients, which makes it difficult to measure outcomes and to adjust care correctly.

Inappropriate implementation of the nursing process leads to miscommunication among healthcare professionals, interruption of care and suboptimal patient outcomes. The Joint Commission International and the Council of Nursing and Midwifery consider proper documentation of health services provided as a critical component in providing quality nursing service. Evidence from different studies across the world showed that use of a standardised NCP and enhancing staff’s knowledge and skills through ongoing training can significantly improve the consistency and quality of NCP implementation.

Aim of the project

We, the WUCSH Nursing Care Process Quality Improvement team, aimed to improve the implementation of NCP from 27% to 85% from 1 August 2024 to 30 January 2025 G.C.

Baseline measurement

The Nursing and Midwifery Audit Team of WUSCH conducted a NCP implementation audit from 15 June 2024 to 15 July 2025, in selected wards of the hospital. Namely, the medical ward, surgical ward, paediatric ward, orthopaedic ward and gynaecological ward. The audit reviewed 120 patient charts to identify NCP documentation for its completeness and appropriateness. The NCP documentation practice was varied across its components and among selected departments. The variation was clearly demonstrated in the supplemental figure and table with post-test results. The result of the nursing audit strongly recommended the need for a QIP to improve nursing service in selected wards.

Design

Study setting and period

WUCSH has been nationally recognised as a federal teaching and referral hospital since 2017. The hospital was promoted to a comprehensive hospital from a referral hospital in 2024. It is located in Nekemte town, in Oromia Regional State, Western Ethiopia. Nekemte town is approximately 331 km from Addis Ababa, the capital city of Ethiopia. The hospital has a total capacity of 318 beds and offers a wide range of inpatient medical services, including internal medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, psychiatry, oncology, orthopaedics, adult intensive care unit (AICU), neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) and nutrition. This project was conducted at WUCSH from 1 August 2024 to 30 January 2025.

Team establishment

The project team is a multidisciplinary team (MDT) that was established from different service area members and leaders at different levels. Accordingly, it is composed of one from the inpatient service department, one from the outpatient department, one from the gynaecological ward, two head nurses, one quality office member, one from nursing service leaders, one from the emergency and critical care service and one from the maternity service.

Study population

All inpatient medical records (MRs) of patients treated and discharged from WUCSH were the study population.

Sample size determination

The sample size was determined based on the Ethiopian Hospital Performance Monitoring and Improvement Manual. Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, to ensure adequate representation.18 Accordingly, a representative 5% of patient records (charts) or a sample size was selected for each study weekly for 6 months.

Study design

The QIP was systematically structured in accordance with the Standards for QualityImprovement Reporting Excellence (SQUIRE)V.2.0 guidelines.19 Accordingly, an institution-based interventional study was employed. The status of the nursing process implementation was assessed by reviewing patients’ MRs at baseline and compared with the implementation status 1 month after the introduction of each change intervention.

Data collection and analysis

Based on the significance of NCP implementation, five wards (medical ward, paediatrics ward, surgical ward, orthopaedics ward and gynaecological ward) were purposely selected. From each ward, 40 MRs of admitted patients were randomly selected during each Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycle to ensure representativeness. Then, charts were reviewed for appropriate implementation of all components of the NCP and selected criteria. The proportion of nursing care standard performance was evaluated using the nursing standard checklists developed by the nursing and midwifery clinical audit team with ‘Yes’ and ‘No’ columns. The MR document review was evaluated using each of the five components of the nursing process, with complete and incomplete columns. A NCP format was used to gather data over time (online supplemental data collection tool).

Data were collected by three trained data collectors using checklists. In addition, supportive supervision was conducted by the quality team, project team and department coordinators/heads. The collected data were checked for completeness. During each audit, the results were cleaned and entered into EpiData V.4.6. Then, it is exported to SPSS V.26 for data analysis. The summary page received the computed and relevant outcome measures. The results were displayed on a run chart. We evaluate if an enhanced level of performance has been attained and is being maintained after each PDSA cycle.

Operational definitions

1. Complete: Patients’ nursing care format is classified as complete if it has clearly documented nursing assessment, nursing diagnosis, nursing care plan, nursing implementation and nursing evaluation.

2. Incomplete: Patients’ nursing care format is classified as incomplete if one or more of the NCP components (nursing assessment, nursing diagnosis, nursing care plan, nursing implementation and nursing evaluation) is not documented.

Major wards: This means that according to our setup, wards that have high patient flow relatively have the same bed numbers, and these wards are the medical ward, surgical ward, paediatric ward, orthopaedics and gynaecology wards.

Strategy to implement the project

Root cause analysis

The MDT analysed the root causes of low implementation of the nursing process in WUCSH using a fishbone diagram (figure 1). The root causes identified during analysis were the knowledge and skill gap of nurses, poor attitude of leaders on NCP, inadequate NCP formats and a disproportionate number of nurses and patients in selected wards. Additionally, there is a lack of updated protocols, policies and work aids in the area of the nursing process. Moreover, poor follow-up/monitoring and lack of supportive supervision were observed as major challenges.

PDSA spreadsheet

Six PDSA cycles were completed over 24 weeks, each of them lasting 4 weeks. In each cycle, there was the implementation of the proposed interventions and analysis of the results. The MDT also provided feedback for wards’ head nurses, ward nurses, nursing and midwifery service director, and department heads on each cycle. While maintaining the previous interventions, the next PDSA cycles were investigated.

PDSA cycle 1: providing training on nursing care plan implementation for nurses

Sample size=40

During the initial PDSA cycle, training was provided for 131 nurses by 4 cycles on nursing care plan after identifying the knowledge, skill and attitude gap of the area by nursing audit. Subsequently, there was leadership training provided for 12 head nurses, 6 service coordinators and 5 service directors. Out of the 40 patient charts reviewed, 20 of them have appropriately filled NCP formats. This increased implementation of the NCP from 27% to 46%. On-site orientation on all components of the NCP was continued for newly assigned nurses to selected wards. A shortage of nurses at some wards was observed as a major challenge to fully implement the process.

PDSA cycle 2: shifting of nurses from other units to wards where the workload is high

Sample size=40

Five nurses who had taken training, had better experience and skill on NCP were shifted from to major wards (paediatric and adult medical wards). The nurses were moved to major wards because the workload is higher, so that the nurses have no time to implement and document NCP. Of the 40 reviewed patient charts, 23 of them had a completely filled NCP. This intervention increased implementation of the NCP from 46% to 56.5%. A sustainable system was established to assess a number of nurses proportional to the number of ward patients as a protocol. Absence of a clear job description for nurses working in wards was one of the problems in implementing the NCP.

PDSA cycle 3: providing a clear job description for all nurses

Sample size=40

The job description was distributed to 161 nurses by the nursing service director. Of 40 reviewed charts, 25 of them had completed the NCP, which increased its implementation from 56.5% to 64%. All newly assigned nurses received their job descriptions. In the majority of wards, a shortage of NCP formats was identified.

PDSA cycle 4: availing nursing care plan formats

Sample size= 40

About 50 000 copies of nursing care formats were prepared and distributed to all wards proportionally based on the number of beds and patients. After availing adequate formats, 40 patient charts were reviewed, out of which 28 had an appropriately filled NCP. NCP implementation increased from 64% to 76.5% at this cycle. Regularly availing of nursing care formats was maintained.

PDSA cycle 5: developing and distributing NCP protocols and work aids

Sample size=40

The Nursing and Midwifery Service Director and Quality Service Team developed 15 NCP protocols and 11 nursing work aids such as updated North American Nursing Diagnosis Association and provided them for all head nurses. Orientation was given to all nurses at their respective units on the importance of protocols and their adherence. 40 charts of the patients were reviewed for the NCP, and 33 of them had filled formats. The rate of NCP implementation significantly raised from 76.5% to 80%. The availability of the protocol and work aids on the nursing station was ensured with its good adherence.

PDSA cycle 6: conducting regular follow-up and supportive supervision

Sample size=40

Lastly, regular follow-up and a supportive supervision system were established to achieve the aim and sustain the improvement. A follow-up team composed of five was organised by the nursing and midwifery service director from the head nurses of selected wards. In addition to that, a supportive supervision team composed of four senior and expert nurses was established. The two teams with MDT had regular weekly meetings. Of 40 reviewed charts, 35 had an appropriately filled NCP format. NCP implementation rate increased from 80% to 87.5%.

Results

The preinterventional assessment revealed that the average of nursing care plan implementation rate was 27% across studied wards of the hospital and all NCP components. The project focused on the training of nurses and leaders, shifting of nurses to selected wards, providing job descriptions for nurses, availing formats used for NCP implementation and finally establishing a system of regular follow-up and strong supportive supervision of the NCP implementation activities.

The postinterventional measurement made at the end of six PDSA cycles indicated that the overall implementation of the NCP was 87.5%. A slight difference was observed in the appropriate documentation of NCP among its components, both during post and preintervention periods. Accordingly, assessment is completely documented in the majority (93%) of the patients’ NCP formats compared with other phases, followed by nursing diagnosis (89.5%) and slightly lowest (83%) in evaluation postintervention (online supplemental figure).

Similarly, the project demonstrated variation among wards in the improvement of NCP documentation after completion of the project. In the orthopaedics ward, almost all (96%) charts had filled NCP format, in the paediatric ward, 88.8%, in the surgical ward, 87.8% and the lowest rate (79%) of improvement was recorded in the gynaecological ward (online supplemental table).

Discussion

The standardised nursing process is the most important indicator of quality of nursing care in the hospital, and it is very crucial for better patient outcomes. The establishment of a QIP at WUCSH aimed to improve the implementation of nursing process at selected wards.

The success of the project was the result of subsequently carrying out all proposed change ideas, contributions of all staff and other stakeholders. Even though there were no adverse events observed during all PDSA cycles, we encountered some challenges related to shifting nurses from units, overlapping duties of leaders, turnover of patients and duplication of nursing process formats. The issues were addressed through orientation for all head nurses and all nurses, having regular discussions, maintaining an extra computer for duplication and modifying meeting schedules.

Our root cause analysis found inadequate knowledge of nurses as the primary factor for low implementation of the nursing process. Similarly, other studies reported the presence of a strong association between NCP and nurses’ level of knowledge.20 21 In the current study, lack of regular and effective supportive supervision was identified as a factor hindering the implementation of the NCP. Consistent with this, other interventional studies showed a direct linkage between supervision and nursing process.22 23 The project identified a shortage of supplies necessary for NCP, a disproportional number of nurses to patients, which caused work overload, and other contributing factors to underperformance of the nursing process. The same result was reported by other studies.2426

By overcoming different challenges and appropriately delivering change ideas, the project achieved an 87.5% success rate, indicating strong teamwork, effective communication and dedication among team members, leaders at all levels and all nurses. Overall improvement was the result of training of nurses, shifting of nurses, providing clear job descriptions for nurses, timely availing of nursing process formats, and developing and distributing updated protocols and work aids for all wards.

Lessons

Some key lessons learnt from the project. Engaging leaders is imperative to instigate and maintain a successful change. Establishing effective communication by different platforms, having regular and scheduled discussions, was a key measure for our project’s success. Equipping nurses with the necessary knowledge and skills is fundamental for the provision of a standardised and sustainable nursing process. Adherence to protocols and work aids is pivotal in increasing the rate of NCP implementation. Regular supervision and feedback for nurses, nursing service leaders and other stakeholders are crucial for maintaining success. It is what we observed during our change ideas implementation. Engaging frontline staff, in our case nurses, in all phases of intervention and incorporating their feedback for subsequent interventions significantly improved the designed project.

Limitations

The project failed to include other wards such as AICU, NICU and the maternity unit. This is due to the use of other formats and approaches to NCP implementation in these units. Once the existing tool is approved to be adopted in our hospital, another project will be conducted in the stated areas.

During the implementation of the QIP, some challenges arose that potentially affect the success and sustainability of the project. Common challenges identified were resource limitation, insufficient time, resistance to change from healthcare professionals, lack of continuous support from leaders and poor data handling. Overcoming these challenges requires strong leadership, effective communication, staff engagement and ongoing monitoring to ensure sustainable improvements.

Implication for patient care and nursing practice

For patient care, the project is substantial as it improves the effectiveness and consistency of nursing care. This NCP QI initiative enhances the accuracy of assessment and planning, enabling intervention decisions to be made promptly, thereby reducing errors and improving clinical decision-making. Through the implementation of QI, overall patient outcomes, safety and recovery time are improved. Additionally, the QIP can significantly decrease the length of stay in the hospital by timely identifying clinical errors, delays and gaps in care, which is important in minimising the risk of hospital-acquired complications and controlling patient flow and efficient utilisation of resources. For nursing practice, the QI initiative is crucial in fostering a culture of ongoing learning, accountability, working collaboratively and using data for decision-making. So that the nurses provide standardised and patient-centred care and use updated nursing protocols.

Sustainability of the project

To ensure the sustainability of the improvement of the project, various key strategies were implemented. The existing protocol and Standard Operating Procedure were revised to incorporate the best practice of the NCP introduced during the project. Continuous learning and refreshment training for nurses were scheduled every 3–4 months to maintain their knowledge and competence. The hospital management agreed and owned the project by incorporating its outcome into hospital quality key indicators, ensuring continuous evaluation and integration into performance measurements. A system of regular audit, reports and feedback was established to identify areas of improvement, any challenges and to reinforce the best practices.

Conclusions

The project aimed to increase the rate of nursing process from its baseline (27%) to 85% over 6 months. Through the implementation of six change ideas, it demonstrated significant improvement by achieving an 87.5% rate of success. The success of the project was a result of multiapproach interventions such as effective leadership engagement, dedication of all health professionals, effective communication, having regular discussions and incorporation of feedback, regular supervision and timely supply of necessary materials. Even though the overall rate is above the planned target, in a few wards the rate is still below the target. Therefore, we recommend special support in those wards. The quality improvement team should plan to conduct another project on the remaining wards. It is recommended for the nursing service audit team to conduct regular assessments to identify problems early. The management of the hospital and all stakeholders own the project to maintain its sustainability.

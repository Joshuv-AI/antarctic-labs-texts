Cauchy Integral Formula for Fuchsian Groups. II
Alexander Kheifets

Abstract
We prove Conjecture 4.1 from [ 1 ] (Theorem 2.2 below): a generalization of the Hasumi’s Direct Cauchy Theorem property for the derivatives. This proof substitutes all proofs in [ 1 ] .

.

Let
\alpha

\Gamma

u

u\circ\gamma=\alpha(\gamma)u

.

We say that function

u(\zeta)=\dfrac{u_{1}(\zeta)}{u_{2}(\zeta)}

u(\zeta)

u(\zeta)
is an outer
Smirnov class function in both

u_{2}(\zeta)

.
That is,
g_{\zeta}

\zeta
under

.
Assume that

one can write

2
Cauchy Integral Formula

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

(2.1)

\widetilde{h}(t)=h(\Lambda(t))\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)^{k+1}}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\ ,

where

\Lambda
,
g_{\zeta}
are analytic on a neighborhood of
\zeta

g_{\zeta}(\zeta)=0,\quad g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(\zeta)\neq 0,\quad\Lambda^{\prime}(\zeta)\neq 0,

and
h
is analytic on a neighborhood of
\Lambda(\zeta)
. Then

h^{(k)}(\Lambda(\zeta))=\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}\widetilde{h}(t)\right)_{|t=\zeta}.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda^{\prime}(\zeta)\neq 0
, there exist a neighborhood
V_{1}
of
\zeta
and a neighborhood
U_{1}
of
\Lambda(\zeta)

such that
\Lambda
maps conformally (one-to-one)
V_{1}
onto
U_{1}
. Let
C_{1}
be a circle centered at
\zeta

that lies in
V_{1}
. Then

\dfrac{h^{(k)}(\Lambda(\zeta))}{k!}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{\Lambda(C_{1})}\dfrac{h(s)}{(s-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}ds

(2.2)

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{C_{1}}h(\Lambda(t))\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}dt=\text{\rm Res}\,_{\zeta}\left(h(\Lambda(t))\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\right).

We rewrite relation (
2.1
) as

(2.3)

h(\Lambda(t))\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}=\widetilde{h}(t)\dfrac{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}{g_{\zeta}(t)^{k+1}}.

It follows from (
2.3
) that the residue in (
2.2
) is equal to

(2.4)

\text{\rm Res}\,_{\zeta}\left(h(\Lambda(t))\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\right)=\text{\rm Res}\,_{\zeta}\left(\widetilde{h}(t)\dfrac{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}{g_{\zeta}(t)^{k+1}}\right).

Since
g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(\zeta)\neq 0
, there exist a neighborhood
V_{2}
of
\zeta
and a neighborhood
U_{2}
of
0
such that
g_{\zeta}
maps conformally (one-to-one)
V_{2}
onto
U_{2}
. Then we can view
\widetilde{h}(t)
as

\widetilde{h}(t)=\widetilde{\widetilde{h}}(g_{\zeta}(t)),\quad t\in V_{2}.

Let
C_{2}
be a circle centered at
\zeta

that lies in
V_{2}
. Then the residue in the right-hand side of (
2.4
) equals

\text{\rm Res}\,_{\zeta}\left(\widetilde{h}(t)\dfrac{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}{g_{\zeta}(t)^{k+1}}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{C_{2}}\widetilde{\widetilde{h}}(g(t))\dfrac{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}{g_{\zeta}(t)^{k+1}}dt

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{g_{\zeta}(C_{2})}\dfrac{\widetilde{\widetilde{h}}(s)}{s^{k+1}}ds=\dfrac{\widetilde{\widetilde{h}}^{(k)}(0)}{k!}=\dfrac{1}{k!}\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}\widetilde{\widetilde{h}}(g_{\zeta}(t))\right)_{|t=\zeta}

(2.5)

=\dfrac{1}{k!}\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}\widetilde{h}(t)\right)_{|t=\zeta}.

Comparing (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
) we get the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{C}}
for which the Cauchy Integral Formula (
1.3
) holds.
Assume that the uniformization map
\Lambda(\zeta)
meets Assumption
1.5
. Let
\Gamma
be
the corresponding Fuchsian group on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then for every

\mu^{k}_{\zeta}\delta_{\zeta}
automorphic
H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
function
f
we have

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}\dfrac{f(t)}{\Delta_{\zeta}(t)g_{\zeta}(t)^{k}}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}L(dt)=\dfrac{1}{k!}\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}\left(\dfrac{f(t)}{\Delta_{\zeta}(t)}\right)\right)_{|t=\zeta},\quad\zeta\in{\mathbb{D}},

where
g_{\zeta}
is the Green function,
\Delta_{\zeta}
is the inner part of
g^{\prime}_{\zeta}

(
see formula (
1.2
)
)
,

\mu_{\zeta}
is the character of
g_{\zeta}
,
\delta_{\zeta}
is the character of
\Delta_{\zeta}
,
and
L(dt)
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Proof.

We start with
the Cauchy Integral Formula for
D
differentiated
k
times

\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{\partial D}\dfrac{h(s)}{(s-\lambda)^{k+1}}ds=\dfrac{1}{k!}h^{(k)}(\lambda),\quad h\in H^{1}(D),\quad\lambda\in D.

We do
the uniformization substitution
s=\Lambda(t)

\dfrac{1}{k!}h^{(k)}(\Lambda(\zeta))=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{E}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}dt,

where
{\mathbb{E}}
is the fundamental set of
\Gamma
on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
\zeta\in{\mathbb{D}}
.
We can rewrite the latter as

\dfrac{1}{k!}h^{(k)}(\Lambda(\zeta))=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{E}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}{g_{\zeta}(t)}dt

by formula (
1.1
)

(2.6)

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{E}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|\gamma(t)-\zeta|^{2}}\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{\gamma(t)}dt.

Observe that

(2.7)

\dfrac{\Lambda^{\prime}g_{\zeta}}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}}\circ\gamma=\dfrac{(\Lambda\circ\gamma)^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}\dfrac{\mu_{\zeta}(\gamma)g_{\zeta}}{(g_{\zeta}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\Lambda^{\prime}\dfrac{\mu_{\zeta}(\gamma)g_{\zeta}}{\mu_{\zeta}(\gamma)g^{\prime}_{\zeta}}=\Lambda^{\prime}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}}.

Therefore, we may continue from (
2.6
) as follows

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{E}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(\gamma(t)))\Lambda^{\prime}(\gamma(t))}{(\Lambda(\gamma(t))-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(\gamma(t))}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(\gamma(t))}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|\gamma(t)-\zeta|^{2}}\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{\gamma(t)}dt

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\int\limits_{\gamma({\mathbb{E}})}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\dfrac{dt}{t}

=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\dfrac{dt}{t}

(2.8)

=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}L(dt).

In view of (
2.7
),

\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}

is an automorphic (with the trivial character) function.
To have a Smirnov class function we define

(2.9)

f(t)=\dfrac{h(\Lambda(t))\Lambda^{\prime}(t)}{(\Lambda(t)-\Lambda(\zeta))^{k+1}}\dfrac{g_{\zeta}(t)}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}g_{\zeta}(t)^{k}\Delta_{\zeta}(t).

f
is indeed of Smirnov class if
\Lambda

meets Assumption
1.5
and
h\in H^{1}(D)
.
Moreover, in this case
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
, since

(2.10)

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}|f(t)|\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}L(dt)=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left|\dfrac{f(t)}{\Delta_{\zeta}(t)}\right|\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}L(dt)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\oint\limits_{\partial D}\dfrac{|h(s)|}{|s-\lambda|}|ds|.

Therefore,
f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
.

f
is also
\mu^{k}_{\zeta}\delta_{\zeta}
automorphic.
Conversely, for an arbitrary function
f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})
that is automorphic with the character

\mu^{k}_{\zeta}\delta_{\zeta}
one can recover
H^{1}(D)
function
h
via formula (
2.9
).

In terms of
f
formula (
2.8
) reads as

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}}\dfrac{f(t)}{\Delta_{\zeta}(t)g^{k}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}L(dt)=\dfrac{1}{k!}h^{(k)}(\Lambda(\zeta))=\dfrac{1}{k!}\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(t)}\dfrac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}\left(\dfrac{f(t)}{\Delta_{\zeta}(t)}\right)\right)_{|t=\zeta}.

The latter equality is due to Lemma
2.1
with

\widetilde{h}=\dfrac{f}{\Delta_{\zeta}}.

∎

Woman With Fever and Hypoxia
Authors: Wei F. Lee, Natalie Tang, Daphne X. Soh, Sachin K.B. Damodhara
Published: 2026

Case Details

A 64-year-old woman with past medical history of hypertension, dyslipidemia, and recent hip surgery presented to the emergency department with fever and acute breathlessness. Her vitals were temperature 101.1 °F, blood pressure 82/62 mm Hg, pulse rate 130/min, and pulse oximetry 92% on a nonrebreather mask. Clinical evaluation revealed no obvious infectious source. She had an elevated white blood cell count of 13.2 × 109/L. During fluid status assessment, bedside ultrasound revealed a mobile echogenic mass in the right atrium and ventricle (Fig 1, Video 1). This patient underwent a computed tomographic scan of the pulmonary artery, which demonstrated filling defects in the pulmonary artery and right heart chambers (Fig 2). Diagnosis

The transthoracic echocardiography demonstrates a clot-in-transit, a rare but critical condition in which a thrombus resides in the right atrium or ventricle. Patients with right heart thrombus and concomitant PE are at increased risk of poor outcomes and mortality compared with patients without.1 Right heart thrombi are divided into 3 types: type A, appearing thin and mobile, originating from peripheral veins; type B, ovoid and immobile, related to structural abnormalities or atrial fibrillation; and type C, resembling myxoma but exhibiting high mobility.2

Optimal treatment is unclear for right heart thrombus with PE due to its rarity.3 Treatment ranges from anticoagulation to systemic thrombolysis to surgical intervention and emerging catheter-directed therapies, with studies showing varying results.1 A tailored approach considering patient comorbidities, bleeding risk, and thrombus characteristics may be required for the preferred treatment modality.

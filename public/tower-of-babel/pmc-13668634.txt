MANF clears mutant uromodulin in human kidney organoids of autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease
Authors: Chenjian Gu, Yili Fang, Yixuan Wang, Eric Tycksen, Gayathri Kondepati, Chuang Li, Kendrah O. Kidd, Jun Liu, Fumihiko Urano, Maria Lindahl, Anthony J. Bleyer, Srikanth Singamaneni, Zhao Sun, Ying Maggie Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In human kidney organoids, MANF treats genetic kidney disease ADTKD-UMOD by blocking ER calcium leakage and restoring autophagy to clear the toxic mutant protein.

To the Editor: Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease due to UMOD mutations (ADTKD-UMOD) is a leading monogenic cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD), and UMOD p.H177-R185del is the most prevalent pathogenic variant in the US, accounting for 21% of cases. Currently, therapy is unavailable. UMOD is synthesized and secreted by the thick ascending limb (TAL) of Henle’s loop. We generated an ADTKD mouse model carrying the human H177-R185del-equivalent deletion mutation and demonstrated that mutant UMOD-induced ER stress suppresses p-AMPK-FOXO3-mediated autophagy in TALs. Moreover, overexpression of mesencephalic astrocyte-derived neurotrophic factor (MANF), an ER chaperone, ameliorates CKD by enhancing p-AMPK in mice (1).

To bolster translational relevance and delineate how MANF activates p-AMPK, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from a heterozygous H177-R185del (UMOD DEL/+) patient and an unaffected family member (Supplemental Figure 1A; supplemental material available online with this article; https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI205631DS1) were differentiated into kidney organoids containing TALs (Figure 1, A and B), proximal tubules, and collecting ducts (Supplemental Figure 1, B and C). The under-glycosylated mutant UMOD localized to the ER with diffuse intratubular expression, whereas WT UMOD displayed apical TAL enrichment (Figure 1B). Furthermore, UMOD DEL/+ TALs isolated from organoids recapitulated hallmarks of our ADTKD mice, including ER stress activation (BiP and p50ATF6 induction) and impaired autophagy (p-AMPK/FOXO3 inhibition and p62/LC3B-II accumulation) (Figure 1C). In the mutant TALs, RNA-seq showed upregulation of TAL injury markers, as identified in the Human Kidney Atlas (HKAv2) (2), and inflammation as the most upregulated pathway (Supplemental Figure 1, D and E).

Given the interplay between ER stress and calcium dynamics, secreted ER-calcium-monitoring protein–expressing (SERCaMP-expressing) (3) WT or DEL/+ hiPSCs were utilized, and differentiated DEL/+ organoids exhibited marked ER calcium leakage (Figure 1D). Examination of key ER calcium release channels revealed selective increase of inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor 1 (IP3R1) at both transcriptional and translational levels, but not IP3R2, IP3R3, and ryanodine receptors (RyRs) in the mutant TALs (Figure 1, E–G). We next investigated whether tribbles homolog 3 (TRIB3), an ER stress–inducible pseudokinase, is implicated following ER calcium leak. Dual-immunofluorescence (dual-IF) staining and IB showed that the pronounced induction of TRIB3 in DEL/+ TALs (Figure 1, H and I) was attenuated by the IP3R blocker Xestospongin C (XeC) (Supplemental Figure 2A), indicating TRIB3 as a downstream effector of ER calcium depletion.

Since AMPK links stress responses and autophagy promotion, we further determined whether TRIB3 could modulate AMPK activity. Co-IP revealed that endogenous TRIB3 directly binds to the catalytic subunit AMPKα in the organoid-isolated TALs (Figure 1J). Deletion mapping of FLAG-tagged AMPKα1/α2 demonstrated a direct interaction between TRIB3 and the activation loop (AL) of both AMPKα1/α2 (Supplemental Figure 2, B–D), which may block T172/T183 phosphorylation within the AL. In stable HEK293 cells expressing WT or UMOD H177-R185del, TRIB3 was induced and p-AMPKα (T172/T183) was reduced in DEL cells as well (Supplemental Figure 2E). Knockdown and overexpression of TRIB3 further validated that TRIB3 negatively regulated AMPKα phosphorylation (Supplemental Figure 2, E and F). Collectively, these data demonstrate that IP3R1-mediated ER calcium depletion, triggered by misfolded UMOD-induced ER stress, upregulates TRIB3, which can directly bind to and suppress AMPK activation, thereby impairing autophagy.

To determine whether MANF exerts a therapeutic role in ADTKD organoids, tetracycline-inducible MANF-overexpressing (TRE-MANF) iPSCs were generated (Supplemental Figure 3A) and differentiated into kidney organoids. Upon doxycycline induction from days 22–30, MANF overexpression in the DEL/+ organoids, including TALs (Figure 1K and Supplemental Figure 3B) substantially mitigated IP3R1 expression (Figure 1K and Supplemental Figure 3C) through direct interaction with IP3R1 (Figure 1L), thus attenuating ER calcium depletion (Figure 1M). The subsequent TRIB3 repression (Figure 1N and Supplemental Figure 3D) and restoration of p-AMPK/FOXO3 expression by MANF upregulation (Figure 1N) promoted autophagic flux (Supplemental Figure 3E) and clearance of mutant UMOD (Figure 1O), thereby ameliorating ER stress, tubular injury, and apoptosis, as well as enhancing WT UMOD secretion (Figure 1P and Supplemental Figure 3F). Additionally, ultrasensitive plasmon-enhanced fluorescence-linked immunosorbent multiplex assay (p-FLISA) developed in house (4) (Supplemental Figure 3G) showed that MANF overexpression inhibited proinflammatory cytokine secretion (Figure 1Q) and their concordant synthesis (Supplemental Figure 3H) in the mutant TALs. Consequently, enhanced MANF expression led to mutant TAL recovery, as demonstrated by increased expression of healthy TAL genes (HKAv2) (Figure 1R).

Importantly, MANF-overexpressing lentivirus treatment of DEL/+ organoids, from days 22–30, blocked IP3R1-mediated ER calcium depletion and subsequently relieved TRIB3-induced p-AMPK suppression and defective autophagic degradation of mutant UMOD (Supplemental Figure 4, A–E). Correspondingly, the lentivirus treatment reproduced the protective effects of lentiviral vector–mediated MANF upregulation in DEL/+ hiPSCs (Supplemental Figure 4, F–H).

In summary, we discovered that MANF restores compromised autophagy by blunting ER-calcium efflux in ADTKD organoids (Supplemental Figure 4I). By targeting IP3R1, MANF may emerge as a potent therapy to correct disrupted proteostasis in ADTKD and other protein misfolding diseases. Additionally, our ADTKD organoids provide a human-relevant platform for high-throughput therapeutic screening, including RNA-based therapies.

A repetitive learning based fractional order parameter optimization algorithm for extended Wiener systems with backlash nonlinearity subject to binary-valued data
Authors: Yinmiao Gong, Yaofeng Feng, Xikun Lu, Li Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn accurate estimation of system parameters is crucial for ensuring high-performance modelling and adaptive control of nonlinear systems, particularly in quantized environments. However, existing multi-innovation estimation algorithms often struggle from limited accuracy and slow convergence rate due to the use of batch noise and initial value problem. To address these challenges, this study proposes a multi-innovation repetitive learning-based fractional-order optimization algorithm for extended Wiener systems with backlash nonlinearity under binary-valued data. First, a quantized regression model is established using the parameterization technique of the nonlinear backlash submodel to reduce computational complexity. Drawing on the principle of repetitive learning, a scalar innovation framework with iterative updates is then proposed to mitigate the effect of batch-induced noise. Subsequently, a continuous optimization mechanism is introduced to improve the selection of initial values for parameter estimation. Furthermore, guided by fractional-order theory, a composite correction term is incorporated into the parameter adaptation law to enhance the utilization of quantized system data. Comparative statistical results demonstrate that the proposed estimator achieves superior optimization performance compared with other multi-innovation estimation algorithms in both simulation and real-world applications, thereby highlighting its effectiveness and practical utility.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

System identification techniques are widely utilized across various branches of engineering and science , such as signal processing1, adaptive control2, biological engineering3, and intelligent city4. Conventional estimation techniques (e.g. least-squares and gradient-based schemes) rely on data collected using standard sensors, from which accurate system information can be obtained. For example, Morato5 proposed a robust joint estimation algorithm for stochastic nonlinear parameter varying systems, in which the measurement outliers are explicitly considered. Filipovic6 investigated the parameter estimation problem of pneumatic servo actuators by proposing an improved Masreliez-Martin filter, thereby enhancing its practical applicability. Djordjevic7 proposed a fault estimation scheme for hydraulic servo actuators based on a sliding mode observer and successfully estimated sensor faults. However, the implementation of these algorithms relies on accurate data acquisition. In application scenarios, system identification is often performed with limited data due to hardware constraints and environmental conditions. Binary-valued sensors are widely used in daily life. Although these sensors generate limited information, they are more cost-effective than conventional sensors. Moreover, they provide the fundamental basis for quantized observations, which are essential components of complex quantized control systems. Therefore, adaptive identification of nonlinear systems with binary-valued observations has attracted significant scholarly attention in recent decades.

The Wiener system is a classic nonlinear block-oriented model capable of characterizing the dynamics of a wide range of nonlinear systems. It consists of a linear dynamic block connected in series with a static nonlinear block, whose nonlinear element can capture the characteristics of actuators or sensors. Since the Wiener model comprises a linear system followed by a sensor, investigating its identification performance under quantized sensing is crucial for advancing the understanding of nonlinear system identification and the impact of quantization constraints on modeling performance. Guo8 investigated the identification performance of Wiener systems with binary-valued observations by proposing a three-step optimization approach. The authors in9 developed an adaptive estimation method for identifying Wiener systems with quantized output measurements. Ogden10 addressed the parameter estimation problem for Wiener systems with quantized data using a mean squared error estimator. Feng11 introduced an iterative optimization scheme to estimate the parameters of quantized Wiener systems. The authors in12 employed a quantized Wiener model to describe the dynamics of a satellite system and subsequently applied the expectation-maximization algorithm to identify the model parameters. In addition to time-domain identification algorithms, frequency-domain approaches have also been explored in the literature. For instance, Figwer13 proposed a frequency-domain estimation scheme to analyze the identification performance of Wiener systems based on quantized output observations. Nevertheless, existing studies have primarily focused on the effects of memoryless nonlinear elements. In such cases, when nonlinear systems exhibit memory-dependent behavior, the aforementioned Wiener models may fail to adequately represent practical nonlinear processes.

Extended Wiener models have been proposed to enhance the modeling capabilities of conventional Wiener systems. In these extended models, the memoryless static nonlinear element is replaced by a memory-dependent nonlinear element. For example, Qi14 utilized Wiener systems with hysteresis nonlinearity to model a piezoelectric actuator system and applied an improved least-squares scheme to identify the model parameters. Brouri15 introduced a two-stage estimator for parameter estimation in extended Wiener-type systems. Maik16 proposed a modified kernel-based estimator to recover the parameters of extended Wiener systems. Lin17 investigated the identification performance of extended Wiener models using a gradient-based optimization algorithm. However, these studies have primarily focused on identification using conventional sensors, and the identification performance of Wiener models under quantized conditions remains relatively underexplored for several reasons. First, memory-dependent nonlinear elements exhibit complex behavior. Compared with memoryless dead-zone nonlinearities, backlash nonlinearity can produce multiple outputs for the same input within certain regions, thereby increasing the complexity of the identification process. Second, quantized sensors provide limited information. Given these challenges, despite their strong descriptive capabilities and broad applicability, the inherent complexity of extended Wiener systems with quantized data has led to relatively fragmented progress in identification research. This study investigates and proposes an identification algorithm to address the parameter estimation problem for extended Wiener systems with quantized data, as schematically illustrated in Fig.1.

Over the past few decades, various effective identification algorithms have been proposed to enhance estimation performance, including multi-innovation algorithms18, subspace identification19, hierarchical identification20, spatio-temporal tensor algorithms21, and composite estimation algorithms22. For example, Wang23 proposed a weighted quasi-Newton type projection algorithm for quantized system identification, achieving almost sure convergence. Zhang24 investigated the identification of Hammerstein systems with quantized data by introducing a compensation strategy-based intrusion tolerant approach. Ataei25 developed an estimation algorithm using quantized and model error data, in which both the system states and inputs were quantized. Zhang26 presented a stochastic extremum seeking estimation algorithm for binary-valued quantized systems and addressed the distributed optimization problem using coarse measurements of local objective functions. Casini27 investigated an adaptive estimation approach based on the radius of quantized information, which adaptively adjusts the quantizer threshold. Although some of these algorithms have been proven effective, their structures are generally complex and require a high level of expertise, making them difficult to implement in practice. In addition, intelligent algorithms have also been widely applied in the field of system identification. Dutta28 proposed a deep learning scheme for identifying neurological conditions epilepsy system, where electroencephalogram data were used to train the network. DNasir29 employed a neural network-based nonlinear autoregressive model to represent a two-wheeled wheelchair system and used experimental input and output data for model identification. Viknesh30 applied an integrated global optimization framework to estimate unknown system variables and further optimized system parameters using an ADAM-SINDy method.

Among these algorithms, the multi-innovation least-squares (MI-LS) algorithm has attracted widespread attention due to its favorable estimation performance and simple structure. For example, Liu31 presented an unbiased MI-LS identification algorithm based on a filtering technique to mitigate the effects of noise. To estimate noise characteristics, Ding32 investigated the identification of Box-Jenkins systems and performed parameter estimation using a multi-innovation recursive extended least-squares approach. To further improve estimation accuracy, Wang33 developed an improved MI-LS algorithm and studied the system identification problem under a maximum likelihood framework. Lughofer34 introduced a MI-LS approach for fuzzy models, in which an online active learning scheme was employed to enhance estimation performance. Liu35 combined the auxiliary model approach with the MI-LS framework to develop an effective scheme for estimating the parameters of nonlinear systems.

Despite their fast convergence rates and high estimation accuracy, these improved versions of existing algorithms still exhibit certain limitations. According to multi-innovation theory, identification performance is expected to improve monotonically with increasing innovation length. However, empirical studies have reported a non-monotonic relationship: performance initially improves but eventually deteriorates as the innovation length increases. A common approach is to design additional mechanisms to mitigate the effects of noise or to enhance the utilization of system data, such as by incorporating filters, auxiliary model methods, and penalty factors36,37. Nonetheless, introducing additional algorithm increases computational complexity, may reduce stability, and can be difficult to implement in online settings. Therefore, it is critical to analyze the underlying causes of this phenomenon and address it without introducing additional algorithms. This phenomenon arises from a fundamental trade-off between the utilization of useful signal information and noise. A longer innovation length provides more useful data, thereby initially improving estimation performance, but it also introduces additional noise in batch form. When the innovation length exceeds a certain threshold, the influence of noise becomes dominant, leading to degraded estimation accuracy. Therefore, appropriately balancing the utilization of noise and useful system data is essential to address this issue. From the perspective of estimation accuracy, existing modified MI-LS algorithms are primarily based on integer-order derivatives, which rely on a sampling point centric framework for adaptive correction terms. This framework cannot exploit non-sampled data, resulting in the loss of valuable system information. This limitation significantly degrades algorithm performance and may even lead to identification failure, particularly in systems with quantized data, singular dynamics, or dual-rate sampling. Fractional-order theory offers a potential solution due to its non-locality and infinite memory characteristics. As a generalization of integer-order calculus in the time domain, it has been widely applied in various fields, including mechanics of materials, image processing, control engineering, and biomedical engineering38–41. Therefore, fractional-order theory is adopted in this study to design an adaptive correction term that can exploit non-sampled data by leveraging its memory properties. In addition, under a fixed innovation length, the estimation procedure typically performs only a single update and lacks a continuous optimization mechanism, thereby limiting convergence speed. A conventional approach is to employ multi-stage algorithms to obtain improved initial values, thus accelerating convergence42,43. However, the convergence performance of multi-stage algorithms heavily depends on the initial value selection in the first stage, and these algorithms are often prone to instability. Therefore, developing a strategy that enables continuous optimization of initial estimates while avoiding the drawbacks of traditional multi-stage methods remains an important research direction.

Motivated by the aforementioned observations, this study further advances quantized system identification by investigating the online estimation of extended Wiener systems with quantized data through the development of a multi-innovation-based fractional-order parameter optimization algorithm. To address the issue of batch induced noise, the principle of repetitive learning is employed to transform the batch utilization of noise data into a scalar additive form. Based on fractional-order theory, a fractional-order correction term is designed for the adaptive law. To improve the convergence rate, a continuous optimization strategy is adopted to enable online updating of both the covariance matrix and the estimation vector. Furthermore, under appropriate excitation conditions, the proposed estimator is proven to be convergent in the almost sure sense. The effectiveness and practical applicability of the proposed scheme are validated through numerical example and experimental studies.

Conclusion

This study addressed the challenges of innovation length and convergence performance in traditional and existing MI-LS algorithms by introducing a novel modified MI-LS scheme. By incorporating fractional-order theory into the correction term, we designed an algorithm capable of handling quantized system identification problems while achieving higher estimation accuracy. We further enhanced previously developed parameter estimation algorithms by integrating the principle of repetitive learning and accelerated the convergence rate through a continuous optimization mechanism. Simulation and experimental results demonstrated that the proposed algorithm, using sampled data, achieves significant improvements in estimation accuracy and markedly faster convergence. Future work will focus on investigating the impact of the quantizer on estimation performance and applying the proposed algorithm to more complex nonlinear systems (e.g., hydraulic actuators and flexible structures). By incorporating additional algorithms and novel principles, we aim to reduce the computational complexity of the proposed method and further improve its operational efficiency.

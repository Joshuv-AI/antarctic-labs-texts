On the structure of ( 4 ​ K 1 4K_{1} , C 4 C_{4} , P 6 P_{6} )-free graphs
Chính T. Hoàng 1, Ramin Javadi 2, Nicolas Trotignon 3

Abstract
Determining the complexity of colouring ( 4 ​ K 1 , C 4 4K_{1},C_{4} )-free graph is a long open problem. Recently Penev showed that there is a polynomial-time algorithm to colour a ( 4 ​ K 1 , C 4 , C 6 4K_{1},C_{4},C_{6} )-free graph. In this paper, we will prove that if G G is a ( 4 ​ K 1 , C 4 , P 6 4K_{1},C_{4},P_{6} )-free graph that contains a C 6 C_{6} , then G G has bounded clique-width. To this purpose, we use a new method to bound the clique-width, that is of independent interest. As a consequence, there is a polynomial-time algorithm to colour ( 4 ​ K 1 , C 4 , P 6 4K_{1},C_{4},P_{6} )-free graphs.

Background and method

If a vertex
a
is adjacent to a vertex
b
in some graph
G
, then we write
a\sim b
. By
N(x)
, we denote the set vertices adjacent to
x
and for any set
X
,
N_{X}(v)
stands for the set
N(v)\cap X
.
The colouring problem is the problem of assigning a minimum number of colours to the vertices of an input graph so that two vertices receive different colours if they are adjacent.
It is well known that the colouring problem for a general graph is NP-hard.

Consider the following operations to build a labelled graph, starting from an empty graph.

•

Create a vertex
u
with label
i
.

•

Take the disjoint union of two previously constructed labelled graphs.

•

Join by an edge all vertices with label
i
to all
vertices with label
j
for
i\not=j
.

•

Relabel all vertices with label
i
by label
j
.

The
clique-width
of an unlabelled graph
G
, is the minimum number of labels needed to build the graph with the above four operations.
For instance, the reader may verify that for any
n\geq 2
, the clique on
n
vertices has clique-width 2, and for any
n\geq 3
,
P_{n}
has clique-width 3. Rao
[
16
]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
(
[
16
]
)

The colouring problem is polynomial-time solvable for graphs with bounded clique-width.

A vertex in a graph is
simplicial
if its neighbourhood is a clique. If
x
is a simplicial vertex, then
\chi(G)=\max(\chi(G-x),|N(x)|+1)
; thus if we know
\chi(G-x)
, then we know
\chi(G)
. Penev proved the following.

Theorem 2.2
(
[
14
]
)

The class of (
4K_{1},C_{4},C_{6},C_{7}
)-free graphs that do not contain a simplicial vertex has bounded clique-width.

Foley, Fraser, Hoàng, Holmes, and LaMantia proved the following.

Theorem 2.3
(
[
8
]

)-free graphs that contains a

has bounded clique-width.

Theorems

The following well-known observation is easy and we omit its proof.

Theorem 2.5

For any graph
G
and every disjoint sets
X,Y\subset V(G)
, the following are equivalent.

•

X
is monotone to
Y
.

•

Y
is monotone to
X
.

•

There does not exist
x,x^{\prime}\in X
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
such that
x\sim y,x^{\prime}\sim y^{\prime}
,
x\not\sim y^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\not\sim y
.

•

The vertices of
X
(resp.
Y
) are linearly ordered by the inclusion of their neighbourhood in
Y
(resp.
X
).

In particular, if
X
and
Y
are two disjoint cliques in a

-free graph, then

is monotone to
Y
.

Let
G
be a graph and
{\cal P}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
) be a partition of
V(G)
.
We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
monotone
if for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,
A_{i}
is monotone to
V(G)\setminus A_{i}
.
In a monotone partition, every set
A_{i}
is linearly ordered with respect to the inclusion of neighbourhoods outside of
A_{i}
(see Theorem
2.5
). So, in each non-empty set
A_{i}
, there exists at least one
maximal
(resp.
minimal
) vertex, that is a vertex
v
such that
N_{V(G)\setminus A_{i}}(v)
is inclusion-wise maximal (resp. minimal).
A vertex in
A_{i}
that is either maximal or minimal is
extreme
.
We say that a monotone partition
\mathcal{P}
has the
extreme vertex property
if there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that some extreme vertex
v
of
A_{i}
is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}\setminus\{i\}
.
We say that
\mathcal{P}
has the
hereditary extreme vertex property
(
hev-property
for short) if for every set
X\subseteq V(G)
, the partition
\mathcal{P}[X]
has the extreme vertex property in
G[X]
(where
\mathcal{P}[X]=(A_{1}\cap X,\dots,A_{k}\cap X)
and
G[X]
is the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
).

Theorem 2.6

Let
G
be a graph and
\mathcal{P}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
be a monotone partition of
V(G)
into cliques
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
. If
\mathcal{P}
has the hev-property, then
G
has clique-width at most
k+1
.

Proof.

Let us prove by induction on
|V(G)|
that
G
can be constructed with
k+1
labels in such a way that at the end of the construction, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, every vertex in
A_{j}
has label
L_{j}
.
This is clear if
|V(G)|=1
, so we suppose that
|V(G)|\geq 2
.
From the definition of the hev-property, consider an extreme vertex
v
in some set
A_{i}
, that is uniform to all
A_{j}
with
j\neq i
.
By the heredity property,
\mathcal{P}[V(G)\setminus\{v\}]
has the hev-property for
G-v
. So, by the induction hypothesis
G-v
can be constructed as stated above.
Now give the label
L
to
v
(
L
is a special label used only for
v
).

For every set
A_{j}\setminus\{v\}
(
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
) that is complete to
v
,
make the label
L
complete to the label
L_{j}
. Then relabel
v
with the label
L_{i}
. This completes the construction.

\Box

The following is a convenient way to prove the hev-property.

Lemma 2.7

Let
G
be a graph and
\mathcal{P}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
be a monotone partition of
V(G)
such that
A_{1}
is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
(
j\in\{2,\dots,k\})
except possibly one. If
(A_{2},\dots,A_{k})
has the hev-property (for
G[A_{2}\cup\dots\cup A_{k}]
), then
\mathcal{P}
has the hev-property (for
G
).

Proof.

If
k=1
, then the conclusion is clear, so suppose
k\geq 2
.
Let us prove that some extreme vertex of some set
A_{i}
(
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
) is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
,
j\neq i
.

From the assumption, there exists some set
A_{i}
(with
i\in\{2,\dots,k)
) such that some extreme vertex
v
in
A_{i}
is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
(
j\geq 2
,
j\neq i
).
If
v
is uniform to
A_{1}
in
G
, we are done, so we may assume it is not.
So,
v
has neighbours and non-neighbours in
A_{1}
. By the assumption,
A_{1}
is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
,
j\geq 2,j\neq i
.
If
v
is a maximal vertex of
A_{i}
, then a minimal vertex of
A_{1}
must be
anticomplete to
A_{i}
, because otherwise, by the maximality of
v
,
v
should be complete to
A_{1}
, a contradiction.
If
v
is a minimal vertex of
A_{i}
, then a symmetric argument shows that a maximal vertex of
A_{1}
is complete to
A_{i}
.
So we have found in
A_{1}
an extreme vertex
w
that is uniform to all sets
A_{i}
(
i\in\{2,\dots,k\}
).

The same proof can be done in
G[X]
with the partition
\mathcal{P}[X]
for any set
X\subseteq V(G)
, so
G
has the hev-property.

\Box

Lemma 2.8

Let
G
be a graph and
{\cal P}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
) be a partition of
V(G)
. If
A_{1}
is monotone to all sets
A_{j}
(
j\in\{2,\dots,k\}
), and uniform to all sets
A_{j}
(

) except possibly one, then
A_{1}
is monotone to

.

Proof.

Otherwise, by Theorem
2.5
, there exist
x,x^{\prime}\in A_{1}
and
y,y^{\prime}\notin A_{1}
such that
x\sim y,x^{\prime}\sim y^{\prime}
,
x\not\sim y^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\not\sim y
. Since

is uniform to all sets
A_{j}
where

except possibly one, the two vertices

must be in the same set

. Since by assumption

is monotone to

, there is a contradiction.

2.6
shows that the bound
2k
in Theorem
5.1
can be improved to
k+1
.

Also, our approach allows some generalizations. For instance, to prove the hev-property, it is enough that
b(G;\mathcal{P})
is a forest (instead of a matching):

Theorem 5.2

Let
G
be a graph and
\mathcal{P}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
be a monotone partition of
V(G)
.
If
b(G;\mathcal{P})
is a forest, then
\mathcal{P}
has the hev-property (in particular,
G
has clique-width at most
k+1
whenever the sets
A_{i}
are all cliques).

Proof.

Since
b(G;\mathcal{P})
is a forest, it can be obtained by repeatedly adding vertices of degree at most 1, which means that the hev-property can be proved by several applications of Lemma
2.7
. The clique-width is then at most
k+1
by Theorem
2.6
.

\Box

Note that Theorem
5.2
is in some sense best possible, since for all integers
k\geq 3
, the first and last authors of the present paper found a construction of graphs of unbounded clique-width with a monotone partition into
k
cliques, whose corresponding box graph is a chordless cycle of length
k
, see
[
11
]
.
We propose the following conjectures to investigate further these matters. We denote the clique-width of
G
by
\cwd(G)
.

Conjecture 5.1

There exists a function
f
such that the following holds.
If
G
is a graph,
{\cal P}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
) is
a monotone partition of
V(G)
into cliques and for
every chordless cycle
A_{i_{1}}\dots A_{i_{c}}A_{i_{1}}
of

b(G;\mathcal{P})
,
\cwd(G[A_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup A_{i_{c}}])\leq x
,
then

\cwd(G)\leq f(k,x).

Conjecture 5.2

There exists a function
g
such that the following holds.
If
G
is a graph and
{\cal P}
is a monotone partition of
V(G)
into cliques such that
b(G;\mathcal{P})
is a chordless cycle, then

\cwd(G)\leq g(k)\log|V(G)|
.

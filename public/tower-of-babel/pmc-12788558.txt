Efficacy of Silver Diamine Fluoride and Nanosilver Fluoride in Preventing Caries: A Comparative Study
Authors: Priyatam Karade, Sameep Singh, Neeti Mittal, Bismay Singh, Ashtha Arya, Preeti Patil, Jnana Ranjan Swain
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Dental caries remains a prevalent global health issue, particularly among children. Silver diamine fluoride (SDF) is a well-established caries-arresting agent, while nanosilver fluoride (NSF), a newer formulation combining silver nanoparticles and fluoride, offers potential antibacterial and remineralizing benefits. However, comparative evidence on their caries-preventive efficacy remains limited.Methods:A randomized, double-blind, controlled clinical trial was conducted involving 90 caries-active children aged 6 to 9 years. Participants were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 30 each): Group A received 38% SDF, Group B received NSF, and Group C received distilled water (control). Baseline caries experience was recorded using the DMFS index. Treatments were applied at baseline and after six months. Follow-up examinations were conducted at six and 12 months. Data were analyzed using ANOVA and post hoc Tukey’s test with significance set at P < 0.05.Results:After 12 months, Group A (SDF) showed a mean increment of 0.8 ± 1.2 in DMFS, Group B (NSF) showed 1.1 ± 1.3, and Group C (control) showed 2.7 ± 1.5. The differences between Group A and Group C (P < 0.001) and between Group B and Group C (P = 0.003) were statistically significant, while the difference between Group A and Group B was not (P = 0.217).Conclusion:Both silver diamine fluoride and nanosilver fluoride were effective in preventing caries progression in high-risk children, with SDF showing marginally better efficacy. NSF appears to be a promising alternative with comparable outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Dental caries remains one of the most prevalent chronic diseases affecting both children and adults worldwide. Despite advancements in dental care, the burden of untreated dental caries, particularly among children from lower socioeconomic backgrounds, continues to be a public health concern.[1] Prevention strategies that are cost-effective and easy to administer are essential to address this global burden.

Silver diamine fluoride (SDF), a topical agent comprising silver, fluoride, and ammonia, has shown high efficacy in caries prevention and arrest, particularly in pediatric and geriatric populations.[2] Its mechanism of action includes antibacterial activity and inhibition of demineralization, making it a valuable noninvasive tool in community dental programs.[3]

Nanosilver fluoride (NSF) is a relatively recent innovation that combines silver nanoparticles with fluoride in a biocompatible solution. The nano-sized particles provide a higher surface area and enhanced antimicrobial efficacy, potentially improving the caries-preventive capabilities compared to traditional SDF.[4] In addition, NSF may have the advantage of avoiding the black staining associated with SDF, which often discourages its acceptance among patients and caregivers.[5]

Randomization was conducted using a computer-generated sequence. The examiner and data analyst were blinded to group allocation.

Procedure

All children received oral prophylaxis before application. The agents were applied to the occlusal surfaces of molars using microbrushes. Applications were performed at baseline and repeated after six months.

Outcome assessment

Caries increment was assessed using the DMFS (Decayed, Missing, and Filled Surfaces) index at baseline, six months, and 12 months. New carious lesions were recorded and compared.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v26.0.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrated that both silver diamine fluoride and nanosilver fluoride are effective in preventing the development of new carious lesions in high-risk children over a 12-month period. The observed caries reduction in the SDF group corroborates with previous studies, highlighting its strong antimicrobial properties and ability to arrest caries progression.[6]

Silver diamine fluoride has been widely used in community dentistry programs due to its simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and minimal patient cooperation requirements.[7] Its dual action of fluoride-mediated remineralization and silver-ion-mediated antibacterial effect is well established.[8] However, the black staining associated with SDF remains a major drawback, especially for anterior teeth.[9]

Nanosilver fluoride, in contrast, offers a cosmetically favorable profile. The silver nanoparticles are less likely to cause discoloration and have demonstrated potent antibacterial activity even at low concentrations.[10] Recent in vitro studies have shown NSF to inhibit Streptococcus mutans and reduce demineralization effectively.[1]

The limitations of this study include a relatively short follow-up period and its focus on posterior teeth. Longer-term studies involving anterior applications and patient-reported outcomes are needed.

CONCLUSION

Both 38% silver diamine fluoride and nanosilver fluoride demonstrated significant caries-preventive effects in high-risk children over 12 months. While SDF showed slightly better efficacy, NSF emerged as a cosmetically favorable and comparably effective alternative, suggesting its potential for wider clinical application.

Existence of penalised likelihood estimates and posterior propriety of separable prior distributions for Gaussian precision matrices
Jack Storror Carter

Abstract
Penalised likelihoods are often used for sparse estimation of a Gaussian precision matrix. In high dimensional settings where the matrix dimension is larger than the sample size, the sample covariance matrix S S is not of full rank and the maximum likelihood estimate of the precision matrix does not exist. An additional advantage of some penalised likelihood estimates, for example the graphical lasso, is that it can exist even in such high dimensional settings. This paper gives a thorough analysis of the existence of penalised likelihood estimates for positive semidefinite S S . Specific tail conditions are provided on the diagonal and off-diagonal penalty functions that ensure existence of the estimate. This is also extended to the Bayesian setting where conditions on separable prior distributions are provided that ensure the resulting posterior distribution is proper.

1
Summary of results and discussion

Existence of the penalised likelihood estimate for only PSD
S
depends on the limiting behaviours of the penalty functions
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
. The results in this paper first investigate the required limiting behaviours of
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
individually before giving a joint result.

1.1
Diagonal penalty

First considering the diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
combined with any off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}\geq 0
,
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
is guaranteed to exist for any PSD
S
if (Proposition
3
)

\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty

If instead
S
is a Gaussian sample covariance matrix with
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to 0, then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists with probability 1 when (Corollary
2
)

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)}{\log x}>m_{0}/p

recalling that
p
is the matrix dimension.

On the other hand, if
{\mathrm{pen}}=0
and

\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty

then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
(Proposition
4
).

1.2
Off-diagonal penalty

When only the off-diagonals are penalised, i.e.
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}=0
, then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
does not exist whenever
S
has a diagonal entry equal to
0
. This holds no matter the choice of
{\mathrm{pen}}
. For
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
to exist, it is therefore required that
S
has strictly positive diagonal. Luckily this holds with probability 1 when
S
is a Gaussian sample covariance matrix.

When
S
is PSD with strictly positive diagonal,
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
is guaranteed to exist when (Corollary
4
)

\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>p-1

If
S
has
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to
0
and strictly positive diagonal, then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists whenever (Proposition
6
)

\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>m_{0}

On the other hand, if
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}=0
and

\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log\left|x\right|=-\infty

where
q=p(p-1)/2
is the number of off-diagonals, then
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
(Proposition
7
).

1.3
Joint penalties

When considering both the diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and the off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}
, their combination can still ensure existence of
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
even when they don’t satisfy either of the individual conditions of Corollary
2
or Proposition
6
.

If
S
has
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to 0 and strictly positive diagonal then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists whenever (Proposition
8
)

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)}{\log x}+\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>m_{0}

On the other hand, if

\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)+q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log\left|x\right|=-\infty

then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
(Proposition
9
).

1.4
Generalisations

All the above results extend to unequal penalties where a different penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{ij}
is applied to each
\theta_{ij}
(Section
7.1
), to non-symmetric
{\mathrm{pen}}
(Section
7.3
) and to
{\mathrm{pen}}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
that are not increasing (Section
7.4
). They also trivially extend beyond non-negative penalties to lower bounded penalties. When
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
is not lower bounded, the result of Proposition
3
, can be extended with the condition
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
(Proposition
10
).

The results on
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
also apply to partial correlation based penalties where the off-diagonal penalty is replaced by a penalty on the partial correlations
-\theta_{ij}/\sqrt{\theta_{ii}\theta_{jj}}
. However, unlike for the standard separable penalty functions, the penalty on the partial correlations alone is not enough to achieve existence when
S
is PSD (Section
7.6
).

1.5
Proper Bayesian posteriors

The above results on penalty functions also imply the existence of the maximum a-posteriori estimate under the prior
p(\Theta)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)\right)\mathbb{I}(\Theta\succ 0)
. However, slightly stronger tail conditions on
\pi_{D}
and
\pi
are required for the posterior to be proper (i.e. have finite integral) when
S
is PSD. Even when
S
is PD, some conditions on
\pi_{D}
and
\pi
are required for posterior propriety, that also depend on the sample size
n
.

If
\pi
is bounded and
\pi_{D}
has
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\pi_{D}(x)<\infty
and
\int_{0}^{1}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
then the posterior is proper for any PD
S
(Proposition
12
). If instead
\pi
is proper and
\pi_{D}
has finite
n/2
moment then the posterior is proper for any PSD
S
(Proposition
13
).

1.6
Discussion

The results in this paper give a road map for determining if
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists for almost any choice of separable
{\mathrm{Pen}}
. The most useful application of this is in the choice of diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
. Since the off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}
is the primary tool for inducing sparsity in
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
, it should be chosen mostly based on statistical and model selection properties. This may be a penalty with weak tail growth, such as the
l_{0}
approximating seamless
l_{0}
and ATAN penalties, or a bounded penalty such as the SCAD penalty or the MCP. Alone, these penalties on the off-diagonals result in
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
not existing whenever
S
is only PSD and therefore can only be used in real data settings with sufficient sample size
n>p
. This greatly limits their use in high dimensional settings.

However, the results of this paper show that by combining these penalties with a suitably strong penalty on the diagonals, it can be ensured that
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists for PSD
S
. Of course, the choice of
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
does have an impact on both estimation performance and the sparsity of
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
. Generally, larger penalisation of the diagonals leads to greater shrinkage of the off-diagonals and greater sparsity. The results in this paper can therefore be used to give some minimal penalisation of the diagonal entries that ensure existence, for example when
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
grows logarithmically and exceeds the bound given by Corollary
2
.

Background and notation

The log-likelihood function for a
p\times p
Gaussian precision matrix
\Theta=(\theta_{ij})
given a
p\times p
PSD matrix
S=(s_{ij})
, after removing additive and multiplicative constants, and the corresponding MLE are

\displaystyle l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)=\log\det\Theta-{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)

\displaystyle\hat{\Theta}=\argmax_{\Theta\succ 0}\,l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)

where
{\mathrm{tr}}(A)
denotes the trace of a matrix
A
and
\Theta\succ 0
refers to the set of
p\times p
PD matrices.

A penalised likelihood subtracts a penalty function from the log-likelihood and estimates
\Theta
by maximising the resulting function
2
2

2

Letting
n
be the sample size, here the multiplicative constant
n/2
has been removed so that the penalised likelihood function does not depend on
n
. Hence the true penalisation is actually
\frac{n}{2}{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)
.

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)=l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)-{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)

\displaystyle\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}=\argmax_{\Theta\succ 0}\,l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)

Recall that in this paper it is assumed that the penalty function is separable with common diagonal and off-diagonal penalty

{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii})+\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}(\theta_{ij})

and that
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
are non-negative and continuous and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
is non-decreasing in
\theta_{ii}
while
{\mathrm{pen}}
is symmetric around
0
and non-decreasing in
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|
.

It will be useful to consider the optimisation problem in terms of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
\Theta
. Because
\Theta
is symmetric, it is guaranteed to have an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Write the eigenvalues of
\Theta
as
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})
with corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors
V=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})
. The
j
th entry of the eigenvector
v_{k}
is written as
v_{kj}
. For
\Theta
to be PD it must have strictly positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}>0
and its eigenvectors are in the set of orthonormal bases
V\in\mathcal{V}
.

The determinant of a matrix is the product its eigenvalues, and the trace can be written as
{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\lambda_{k}v_{k}^{\top}Sv_{k}
. The log-likelihood function can therefore be rewritten in terms of eigenvalues and eigenvectors as

\displaystyle l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\log\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k}v_{k}^{\top}Sv_{k}

The penalised likelihood can similarly be rewritten by noting that
\theta_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\lambda_{k}v_{ki}v_{kj}
.

When
S
is only PSD it has some eigenvalues equal to
0
and its null space
\mathcal{N}(S)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:Sv=0\}
has positive dimension. Let
m_{0}=\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))
, which is equal to the number of eigenvalues of
S
that are equal to
0
. The rank of
S
is equal to
p-m_{0}
. We refer to any eigenvector of
\Theta
in
\mathcal{N}(S)
,
v_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
as a null space eigenvector and the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
as a null space eigenvalue.

In the optimisation problems,
S
could be any
p\times p
PSD matrix. However, it is usually the sample covariance matrix from a
p
-variate Gaussian i.i.d. sample
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N_{p}(\mu,\Theta^{-1})
with mean vector
\mu
and covariance matrix
\Theta^{-1}
. When
\mu
is unknown, the sample covariance matrix is
S=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\bar{X})(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
, where
\bar{X}=\frac{1}{n}(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n})
. When
n>p
,
S
is PD with probability 1. However, when
n\leq p
,
S
is only PSD with
m_{0}=p-n+1
with probability 1
(
Mathai et al., 2022
, Section 8.3)
.

When
\mu
is known, the sample covariance matrix is instead
S=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
, which is PD with probability 1 when
n\geq p
, but is only PSD when
n<p
with
m_{0}=p-n
with probability 1.

3
Maximum likelihood estimate

We begin by considering the existence of the MLE for PD and PSD
S
. While these results hardly need proving, they provide simple examples of the style of proofs that will be used for penalised likelihoods and understanding why the MLE does not exist when
S
is only PSD helps focus the later proofs.

Proposition 1
.

The MLE
\hat{\Theta}
exists for any PD
S
.

Proof.

Since the likelihood function is continuous, the existence of the MLE follows if
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
whenever
\Theta
approaches the boundary of the space of PD matrices or
\lVert\Theta\rVert\to\infty
. PD matrices are characterised by positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}>0
, so the boundary of the space occurs when any
\lambda_{k}\to 0
while
\lVert\Theta\rVert\to\infty
requires that some
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
. To prove that
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
in such cases, we will upper bound the objective function by an expression that holds uniformly over all
V\in\mathcal{V}
. We will then show that this upper bound tends to
-\infty
whenever any (potentially multiple)
\lambda_{k}\to 0,\infty
.

Because the log-likelihood is separable in the
\lambda_{k}
, each
\lambda_{k}
can be considered separately. Since
S
is PD, for any unit vector
v
,
v^{\top}Sv\geq\lambda_{\min}(S)>0
, where
\lambda_{\min}(S)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
S
.
Hence the
\lambda_{k}
component of the log-likelihood has upper bound

\log\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k}v_{k}^{\top}Sv_{k}\leq\log\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{min}(S)

and this upper bound tends to
-\infty
as
\lambda_{k}\to 0,\infty
.
∎

Since the log-likelihood
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)
is strictly concave in
\Theta\succ 0
, it follows that, when it exists, the MLE is unique and it is well known that when
S
is PD the MLE is
\hat{\Theta}=S^{-1}
. Since it is assumed that
{\mathrm{Pen}}\geq 0
and is continuous, existence of the penalised likelihood estimate also trivially follows for any PD
S
.

Corollary 1
.

For any non-negative, continuous penalty function
{\mathrm{Pen}}
, the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists for all PD
S
.

On the other hand, when
S
is only PSD, it is not invertible and the MLE does not exist.

Proposition 2
.

The MLE does not exist when
S
is only PSD.

Proof.

To prove non-existence we construct a sequence of
\Theta
such that
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
. Since
S
is only PSD,
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}>0
. Let
v_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
be an eigenvector of
\Theta
. Then
v_{1}^{\top}Sv_{1}=0
and so the contribution of
\lambda_{1}
to the log-likelihood is
\log\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{\top}Sv_{1}=\log\lambda_{1}\to\infty
as
\lambda_{1}\to\infty
. Then, keeping
\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{p}>0
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}
fixed, as
\lambda_{1}\to\infty
,
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
and so the MLE does not exist.
∎

This proof highlights that the trace term does not depend on
\lambda_{k}
whenever
v_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
and it is exactly the divergence of these eigenvalues that cause
l\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
. On the other hand, when
v_{k}\notin\mathcal{N}(S)
the trace term still grows linearly with
\lambda_{k}
. Proving the existence of the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
would usually require showing that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any sequence of PD matrices with
\Theta^{(t)}
approaching the boundary of the PD cone or
\lVert\Theta^{(t)}\rVert\to\infty
. However, these observations about the likelihood function restrict the types of sequences that must be considered.

Lemma 1
.

Let
S
be PSD with
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}
and
{\mathrm{Pen}}
be a penalty function satisfying the assumptions given in Section
2
. If
\l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any sequence of
\Theta^{(t)}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{(t)},\dots,\lambda_{p}^{(t)}
and eigenvectors
V^{(t)}=(v_{1}^{(t)},\dots,v_{p}^{(t)})
satisfying

(i)

\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}=\max_{k=1,\dots,p}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}\to\infty
.

(ii)

V^{(t)}\to V
with
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
and
v_{m+1},\dots,v_{p}\notin\mathcal{N}(S)
,
m\in\{1,\dots,m_{0}\}
, i.e. the sequence of eigenvectors converge to a basis with
m\in\{1,\dots,m_{0}\}
vectors in
\mathcal{N}(S)
.

(iii)

\lambda_{k}^{(t)}=o(\lambda_{\max}^{(t)})
for
k=m+1,\dots,p
, i.e. we only have to consider sequences where non-null space eigenvalues are of smaller order than
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}
. Note that this implies that for sufficiently large

\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}=\max_{k=1,\dots,m}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}
is one of the null space eigenvalues.

Then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists.

Proof.

As noted previously, showing that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any sequence
\Theta^{(t)}
approaching the boundary of the PD cone or with
\lVert\Theta^{(t)}\rVert\to\infty
is enough to prove the existence of
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
. Such sequences are exactly characterised by at least one of
\lambda_{\min}^{(t)}=\min_{k=1,\dots,p}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}\to 0
or
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
. Proving this lemma therefore requires showing that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any
\Theta^{(t)}
satisfying
(i)
-
(iii)
implies that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any
\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}
with
\tilde{\lambda}_{\min}^{(t)}\to 0
or
\tilde{\lambda}_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
. We do this for each of
(i)
-
(iii)
individually.

(i)

The proof of Proposition
1
already demonstrates that
l\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
whenever
\lambda_{\min}^{(t)}\to 0
but
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\not\to\infty
. This is regardless of
S
or
V^{(t)}
. Since
{\mathrm{Pen}}\geq 0
it follows that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
also.

All remaining sequences that approach the boundary of the PD cone or have
\lVert\Theta^{(t)}\rVert\to\infty
must have
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
. Note that this does not rule out the case where both
\lambda_{\min}^{(t)}\to 0
and
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
.

(ii)

First note that the case where
m=0

\mathcal{N}(S)
is analogous to Corollary
1

m>m_{0}
is impossible because
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}
and so only

Suppose that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any sequence of
\Theta^{(t)}
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
. Consider a general sequence
\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}
with
\tilde{\lambda}_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
and suppose that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\not\to-\infty
. Then there is a subsequence
\tilde{\Theta}^{(t_{i})}
such that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\geq C
for all

. Since the eigenvectors

then satisfies property
(ii)
and so
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(j)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
which gives a contradiction. Hence
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
.

(iii)

Now suppose that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
for any sequence of
\Theta^{(t)}
satisfying
(i)
-
(iii)
. Consider a sequence
\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
but with
\lambda_{k}^{(t)}\neq o\left(\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\right)
for some
k=m+1,\dots,p
. This implies that
\tilde{\lambda}_{k}^{(t)}\geq\epsilon\,\tilde{\lambda}_{\max}^{(t)}
infinitely often for some
\epsilon>0
. Consider any subsequence
\tilde{\Theta}^{(t_{i})}
along which
\tilde{\lambda}_{k}^{(t_{i})}\geq\epsilon\,\tilde{\lambda}_{\max}^{(t_{i})}
for some
k=m+1,\dots,p
and some
\epsilon>0
. Because
v_{k}\notin\mathcal{N}(S)
, eventually
v_{k}^{(t)\top}Sv_{k}^{(t)}\geq c_{k}>0
and so
{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta^{(t)})\geq c_{k}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}
. Using this, replacing all eigenvalues with
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}
in the log determinant and removing the penalty term,

\displaystyle l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t_{i})}\mid S\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq p\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t_{i})}-c_{k}\lambda_{k}^{(t_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq p\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t_{i})}-c_{k}\,\epsilon\,\lambda_{\max}^{(t_{i})}

This upper bound tends to
-\infty
as
\lambda_{\max}^{(t_{i})}\to\infty
and so
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t_{i})}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
.

The complement of these subsequences has
\tilde{\lambda}_{k}^{(t)}<\epsilon\,\tilde{\lambda}_{\max}^{(t)}
for all
k=m+1,\dots,p
and all
\epsilon>0
. If these form a subsequence
\Theta^{(j)}=\tilde{\Theta}^{(t_{j})}
, then it satisfies
(i)
-
(iii)
and so
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta^{(j)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
.

It follows that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\tilde{\Theta}^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
along the full sequence.

∎

This lemma shows that when proving the existence of
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
it is enough to only consider sequences that satisfy
(i)
-
(iii)
. In particular, we only need to consider sequences in which the eigenvectors converge to a basis with at least one null-space vector, and sequences of eigenvalues with at least one null space eigenvalue tending to
\infty
.

4
Diagonal penalty

While the MLE does not exist for only PSD
S
, the addition of a sufficiently strong penalty on the diagonal is enough to ensure existence. In this section we remove the off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}=0
, but all existence results remain valid for any
{\mathrm{pen}}\geq 0
.

Proposition 3
.

If
\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
, then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists for any PSD
S
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)\geq 0
for PSD
S
and PD
\Theta
, the penalised likelihood is upper bounded by removing the trace term (this corresponds to a worst case scenario when
S
is a zero matrix and all
v_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
). Hadamard’s inequality also says that the determinant of
\Theta
is upper bounded by the product of its diagonal entries and therefore
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log\theta_{ii}
. These give the upper bound

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log(\theta_{ii})-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii})

Since
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
are unit-vectors, for each
k=1,\ldots,p
we have
\max_{j=1,\dots,p}(v_{kj})^{2}\geq 1/p
. By choosing
i=i(k)\in\argmax_{j=1,\dots,p}(v_{kj})^{2}
, we have
\theta_{ii}\geq\lambda_{k}(v_{ki})^{2}\geq\lambda_{k}/p
. In particular, there is an
i
such that
\theta_{ii}\geq\lambda_{\max}/p
and so as
\lambda_{\max}\to\infty
,
\theta_{ii}\to\infty
also. It follows that as
\lambda_{\max}\to\infty
we have
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log(\theta_{ii})-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii})\to-\infty
and therefore
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
.
∎

On the other hand, under a weaker diagonal penalty with
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
, the solution does not exist for some PSD
S
. This is most easily seen by considering
S=0
and
\Theta
with standard basis eigenvectors in which case the penalised likelihood tends to
\infty
as any
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
. However, it does not guarantee that the solution does not exist for all only PSD
S
. In particular, if multiple
\theta_{ii}\to\infty
as
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
, then these multiple penalty terms might be enough to overpower the
\log
term. But an even slower growth condition on
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
does ensure this.

Proposition 4
.

If
{\mathrm{pen}}=0
and
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
, then
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
.

Proof.

Let
S
be only PSD so that
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}>0
. Choose a unit vector
v_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
and let
\Theta^{(t)}=I_{p}+tv_{1}v_{1}^{\top}
for
t>0
. Then
\Theta^{(t)}\succ 0
for all
t>0
,
\det(\Theta^{(t)})=1+t
, and since
v\in N(S)
we have

{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta^{(t)})={\mathrm{tr}}(SI_{p})+t\,v^{\top}Sv={\mathrm{tr}}(S)
, which is constant in
t
. The diagonal entries of
\Theta^{(t)}
are
\theta_{ii}^{(t)}=1+tv_{1i}^{2}\leq 1+t
. Since
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
is increasing, the penalty term is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii}^{(t)})

\displaystyle\leq p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(1+t)

The penalised likelihood function is therefore lower bounded by

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\geq\log(1+t)-{\mathrm{tr}}(S)-p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(1+t)

The condition
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
ensures that there is an increasing sequence of
t
such that this lower bound tends to
\infty
and therefore along this sequence,
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
.
∎

We have now seen that when
\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
, the solution exists for every PSD
S
, while if
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
the solution does not exist for any only PSD
S
. Both of these conditions and results are independent of the rank of
S
and do not require that
S
be a Gaussian sample covariance matrix. This begs the question of what happens when
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
satisfies neither of these conditions. Is there some other condition on
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
that guarantees existence for some useful subset of PSD
S
?

One specific such class of penalty functions
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)=c\,\log x
was investigated by
Carter and Molinari (2025)
where it was shown that the solution exists with probability
1
when
S
is a Gaussian sample covariance matrix for certain values of
c
depending on the rank of
S
. We restate the result of
Carter and Molinari (2025)
, Theorem 1.1 in the context of this paper.

Proposition 5
.

Let
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)=c\,\log x
and
S
be a Gaussian sample covariance matrix with
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to
0
. Then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists with probability 1 for any
c\in\left(\frac{m_{0}}{p},1\right)
.

This result relies on the fact that orthogonal vectors in
\mathcal{N}(S)
can have limited sparsity with probability 1 - this will be explored further in Section
8
. The penalty parameter
c
must exceed
m_{0}/p
so that there is sufficient penalisation in each direction of
\mathcal{N}(S)
. Also note that the penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)=c\log x
is not lower bounded and so does not meet the previous assumptions - this will be discussed further in Section
7.2
and is the reason why
c<1
is required.

The proof of Proposition
5
in
Carter and Molinari (2025)
follows the same format as the proofs in this paper, showing
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
whenever
\Theta
approaches the boundary of the PD cone or
\lVert\Theta\rVert\to\infty
. This leads to a simple generalisation of the result.

Corollary 2
.

Let
S
be a Gaussian sample covariance matrix with
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to
0
. If

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)}{\log x}>\frac{m_{0}}{p}

then
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists with probability 1

Proof.

Suppose

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)}{\log x}=c_{0}>\frac{m_{0}}{p}

and let
c\in(m_{0}/p,\min\{c_{0},1\})
. Then there exists
x_{0}
such that
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)\geq c\,\log x
for all
x>x_{0}
. It can then be globally lower bounded by
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)\geq c\,\log x-a
for all
x>0
where
a=\sup_{0<x\leq x_{0}}(c\log x-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x))
. Note that
a
is a finite constant because
x_{0}
is fixed, the function is continuous on
(0,x_{0}]
and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\geq 0
so
c\log x-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)\to-\infty
as
x\to 0
. Then we have
\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}c\,\log\theta_{ii}-pa
. It follows that

l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\leq l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}^{\prime}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)+pa

where
{\mathrm{Pen}}^{\prime}
has
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}^{\prime}(x)=c\log x
and
{\mathrm{pen}}^{\prime}=0
so is a penalty of the form of Proposition
5
and
pa
is a constant. The result follows.
∎

This section has covered almost all tail conditions of
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
. If
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
then the solution does not exist for any only PSD
S
while if
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
it does not exist for some PSD
S
. If
\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
then the solution exists for all PSD
S
while if
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x>m_{0}/p
then the solution exists with probability 1 for
S
of rank
p-m_{0}
. This extends to when
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x>1/p
when the solution exists with probability 1 for
S
of rank
p-1
. The only tail conditions remaining are the boundary conditions

•

\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x=1/p
exactly (because
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x<1/p
implies
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
). Our results show that in this case there are some PSD
S
for which
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
does not exist. However, it is possible that the solution still exists for some choices of only PSD
S
and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
.

•

\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Our results show that
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists with probability 1 in this case. However, it is undetermined if the solution exists for all PSD
S
. This may be the case for some such
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
, for example when
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x\gg 0
, but not for others, when
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x\ll 0
.

5
Off-diagonal penalty

The previous section focused on the diagonal penalty. In this section the diagonal penalty is instead removed
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}=0
so that the penalty is only on the off-diagonals through
{\mathrm{pen}}
.

Using only the off-diagonal penalty introduces an additional complication. An off-diagonal entry of
\Theta
in terms of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors is
\theta_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\lambda_{k}v_{ki}v_{kj}
. By taking
v_{k}
to be a standard basis vector with only one non-zero entry,
v_{ki}v_{kj}=0
for all
i\neq j
, none of the off-diagonals depend on the value of
\lambda_{k}
and so the penalty function remains finite as
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
. If
v_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
, then the trace term also doesn’t depend on
\lambda_{k}
and so the penalised likelihood is unbounded as
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
. It is therefore necessary that standard basis vectors do not appear in
\mathcal{N}(S)
for existence to be possible with only off-diagonal penalisation. Fortunately, the null space of a PSD matrix can only contain a standard basis vector in a very specific circumstance.

Lemma 2
.

Let
S
be a PSD matrix. Then
\mathcal{N}(S)
contains a standard basis vector if and only if
S
has a diagonal entry equal to 0.

Proof.

First suppose, w.l.o.g., that
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\top}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
. Then
Se_{1}=0
and therefore
S_{11}=0
.

Now suppose, w.l.o.g., that
S_{11}=0
. Then for
S
to be PSD it is required that
S_{1i}=S_{i1}=0
for all
i=1,...,p
. It follows that
Se_{1}=0
and so
e_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
.
∎

Therefore, when
S
has strictly positive diagonal entries,
\mathcal{N}(S)
does not contain any standard basis vectors. It follows that either the trace term or the penalty term is a function of each eigenvalue of
\Theta
.

While this implies that at least one
\theta_{ij}
depends on the null space eigenvalues, it does not imply that
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|\to\infty
as the eigenvalues grow. By allowing the eigenvalues to grow at different rates, it is possible to keep any
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|
constant. However, it is not possible to do this for all
\theta_{ij}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
S
be PSD with strictly positive diagonal entries and
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}
. Let
\Theta^{(t)}
be a sequence of PD matrices as in Lemma
1
and
\theta_{\max}^{(t)}=\theta_{i_{t}j_{t}}^{(t)}
where
(i_{t},j_{t})\in\argmax_{i<j}\left|\theta_{ij}^{(t)}\right|
be the off-diagonal entry of
\Theta^{(t)}
with largest absolute value.
Then there exists
\delta>0
and
t_{0}
such that

\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|\geq\delta\,\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}

for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

Proof.

Decompose
\Theta^{(t)}
into the null space and non-null space parts

\Theta^{(t)}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)\top}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{p}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)\top}=A^{(t)}+C^{(t)}

By letting
\alpha_{k}^{(t)}=\lambda_{k}^{(t)}/\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\in[0,1]
for
k=1,\dots,m
, we decompose the matrix
A^{(t)}
further as

\displaystyle A^{(t)}=\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}B^{(t)}

\displaystyle B^{(t)}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)\top}

The off-diagonals of
\Theta
are then written as
\theta_{ij}^{(t)}=\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}b_{ij}^{(t)}+c_{ij}^{(t)}
. Let
b_{\max}^{(t)}=\max_{i<j}\left|b_{ij}^{(t)}\right|
and
c_{\max}^{(t)}=\max_{i<j}\left|c_{ij}^{(t)}\right|<\infty
. This leads to the lower bound

\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|\geq\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}b_{\max}^{(t)}-c_{\max}^{(t)}

Note that
C^{(t)}
has eigenvalues
\lambda_{m+1}^{(t)},\dots,\lambda_{p}^{(t)}
, while the remaining eigenvalues are repeated
0
. Hence
c_{\max}^{(t)}\leq\lVert C^{(t)}\rVert_{2}=\max_{k=m+1,\dots,p}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}=o\left(\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\right)
, with the final equality coming from property
(iii)
of Lemma
1
.

Next we show that
b_{\max}^{(t)}
is eventually bounded away from
0
. Suppose it is not so that there is a subsequence such that
b_{\max}^{(t_{i})}\to 0
. Passing to a further subsequence such that
\alpha_{k}^{(t_{i_{j}})}\to\alpha_{k}\in[0,1]
for each
k=1,\dots,m
and because
v_{k}^{(t)}\to v_{k}
, we have
B^{(t_{i_{j}})}\to B=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{k}v_{k}v_{k}^{\top}
. Note that since
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
, we have
\mathrm{range}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(S)
. Also, since
\max_{k=m+1,\dots,p}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}=o\left(\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\right)
, eventually
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}=\max_{k=1,\dots,m}\lambda_{k}^{(t)}
and so
\max_{k=1,\dots,m}\alpha_{k}^{(t)}=1
. Hence
B^{(t)}
eventually has maximum eigenvalue equal to 1 and
\lVert B^{(t)}\rVert_{2}=1
. Hence
\lVert B\rVert_{2}=1
and so
B\neq 0
. Since
b_{\max}^{(t_{i})}\to 0
, all off-diagonals of
B
must be equal to 0 and, by
B\neq 0
,
B
must have at least one positive diagonal entry. It follows that a basis vector
e_{r}\in\mathrm{range}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(S)
. By Lemma
2
this contradicts
S
having strictly positive diagonal. Hence
\liminf_{t}b_{\max}^{(t)}>0
and so there is
\delta_{0}>0
and
t_{1}
such that
b_{\max}^{(t)}\geq\delta_{0}>0
for all
t\geq t_{1}
.

We therefore have

\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|\geq\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\delta_{0}-c_{\max}^{(t)}

for all
t\geq t_{1}
. Since
c_{\max}^{(t)}=o\left(\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\right)
, there exists
t_{2}
such that
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\delta_{0}\geq 2c_{\max}^{(t)}
for all
t\geq t_{2}
. Letting
t_{0}=\max\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
, we have for all
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\delta_{0}-c_{\max}^{(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\,\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}

Setting
\delta=\delta_{0}/2
completes the proof.
∎

This lemma shows that as the eigenvalues associated to eigenvectors in
\mathcal{N}(S)
grow, there is at least one off-diagonal
\theta_{ij}
that grows in absolute value at a comparable rate to
\lambda_{\max}
. This is enough to prove that when
S
has strictly positive diagonal, a sufficiently strong
{\mathrm{pen}}
ensures existence of the penalised likelihood estimate.

Proposition 6
.

Let
S
be PSD with
\dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}
. Fix
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}=0
. If
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>m_{0}
then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists if and only if
S
has strictly positive diagonal entries.

Proof.

First suppose
S
has a diagonal entry equal to
0
, w.l.o.g.
s_{11}=0
. Choose
\Theta
to have eigenvector
v_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\top}
. Since
v_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
, the trace term does not depend on
\lambda_{1}
and neither does the penalty term because
v_{1i}v_{1j}=0
for all
i<j
. So for any fixed
\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{p}
and
v_{2},\dots,v_{p}
,
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
as
\lambda_{1}\to\infty
.

Now suppose that all diagonal entries of
S
are strictly positive. Consider a sequence of
\Theta^{(t)}
as in Lemma
1
. The penalised likelihood is upper bounded by

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\log\lambda_{k}^{(t)}-\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}\left(\theta_{ij}^{(t)}\right)+c^{(t)}

where
c^{(t)}=\sum_{k=m+1}^{p}\log\lambda_{k}^{(t)}-\lambda_{k}^{(t)}v_{k}^{(t)\top}Sv_{k}^{(t)}
is uniformly bounded, say
c^{(t)}\leq c
.

The penalised likelihood can be further upper bounded by removing all penalty terms except that associated to
\theta_{\max}^{(t)}
, since
{\mathrm{pen}}\geq 0
, and replacing
\lambda_{1}^{(t)},\dots,\lambda_{m}^{(t)}
by
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}
. To hold uniformly over
V^{(t)}
, this is done when
m=m_{0}
is at its maximum value.

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\leq m_{0}\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}-{\mathrm{pen}}\left(\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right)+c

From Lemma
3
we have that
\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|\geq\delta\,\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}
for some
\delta>0
and all
t\geq t_{0}
. It follows that
\log\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|\geq\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}+\log\delta
. Meanwhile, the condition
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}(x)/\log\left|x\right|>m_{0}
implies that there exists
\epsilon>0
and
x_{0}
such that
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)\geq(m_{0}+\epsilon)\log\left|x\right|
whenever
\left|x\right|>x_{0}
. These further upper bound the penalised likelihood for large
t
by

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq m_{0}\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}-(m_{0}+\epsilon)\log\left|\theta_{\max}^{(t)}\right|+c

\displaystyle\leq m_{0}\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}-(m_{0}+\epsilon)\left(\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}+\log\delta\right)+c

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\log\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}-(m_{0}+\epsilon)\log\delta+c

Since
\lambda_{\max}^{(t)}\to\infty
and
\delta,c
are constants, this upper bound tends to
-\infty
.
∎

When
S
is a Gaussian sample covariance matrix, the number
m_{0}
of eigenvalues equal to 0 is determined with probability 1 by the dimension
p
and sample size
n
. Additionally, the diagonal entries are positive with probability 1. Hence the condition on
{\mathrm{pen}}
ensures existence in the usual continuous data setting.

Corollary 3
.

If
S
is a Gaussian sample covariance matrix with
m_{0}<p
eigenvalues equal to 0 and
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>m_{0}
then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists with probability 1.

Proof.

For unknown
\mu
,
S
has
m_{0}=p-(n-1)
eigenvalues equal to 0 with probability 1 where
n
is the sample size. Hence
m_{0}<p
implies
n\geq 2
. A diagonal entry of
S
can be written in terms of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
as
s_{jj}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{ij}-\bar{X}_{j})^{2}
where
X_{ij}
is the
j
th entry of
X_{i}
and
\bar{X}_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}
. Hence
s_{jj}=0
if and only if
X_{1j}=\cdots=X_{nj}
. Since
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent Gaussian random vectors, this occurs with probability 0 when
n\geq 2
.

For known
\mu
, instead
S
has
m_{0}=p-n
eigenvalues equal to 0 with probability 1 so
n\geq 1
. Then
s_{jj}=0
if and only if
X_{1j}=\cdots=X_{nj}=\mu
which occurs with probability 0 when
n\geq 1
.
∎

The same argument holds for any i.i.d. sample from a continuous distribution and so extends to non-Gaussian data.

Proposition
6
shows how to ensure existence for all
S
with strictly positive diagonal of fixed rank. By taking the smallest possible rank of
S
(i.e. the largest possible
m_{0}
), we get a condition that ensures existence for all PSD
S
with strictly positive diagonal, regardless of rank.

Corollary 4
.

Let
S
be PSD and
pen_{D}=0
. If
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{pen}}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>p-1
then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists if and only if
S
has strictly positive diagonal entries.

Proof.

If
S
has strictly positive diagonal then it cannot be the zero matrix. It therefore has at most
p-1
eigenvalues equal to 0. The result follows by using
m_{0}=p-1
in Proposition
6
.
∎

On the other hand, a weakly growing off-diagonal penalty results in the solution not existing for any only PSD
S
. Similarly to the diagonal penalty, this happens when the penalty is not enough to overpower the log term even when all
p(p-1)/2
off-diagonals grow.

Proposition 7
.

Let
q=p(p-1)/2
. If
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}=0
and
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log\left|x\right|=-\infty
, then
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
.

Proof.

We follow the same style of proof as Proposition
4
with
\Theta^{(t)}=I_{p}+tv_{1}v_{1}^{\top}
for unit vector
v_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(S)
and
t>0
. The off-diagonals of
\Theta^{(t)}
are
\theta_{ij}^{(t)}=tv_{1i}v_{1j}
and so
\left|\theta_{ij}^{(t)}\right|\leq t
. Since
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)
is increasing in
\left|x\right|
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}\left(\theta_{ij}^{(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}(t)

\displaystyle=q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(1+t)

Hence the penalised likelihood function is lower bounded by

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\geq\log(1+t)-{\mathrm{tr}}(S)-q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(1+t)

The condition
\liminf_{x\to\infty}q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log x=-\infty
ensures that there is an increasing sequence of
t
such that this lower bound tends to
\infty
and therefore along this sequence,
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
.
∎

The results in this section show that when
{\mathrm{pen}}
grows super-logarithmically the solution exists for any PSD
S
with positive diagonal, while if
{\mathrm{pen}}
grows sub-logarithmially then the solution does not exist for any only PSD
S
. We have also covered the case when
{\mathrm{pen}}
grows at a
c\,\log x
rate for
c>m_{0}
and
c<1/q
; when
c>m_{0}
the solution exists for any
S
with at most
m_{0}
eigenvalues equal to 0 and strictly positive diagonal, while if
c<1/q
the solution doesn’t exist for any PSD
S
. This leaves the interval
c\in[1/q,m_{0}]
where existence is not determined. Existence in this region depends on geometric properties of
\mathcal{N}(S)
. We have assumed worst case scenarios in Proposition
6
where only a single off-diagonal entry grows at the same rate as the maximum eigenvalue and in Proposition
7
where all off-diagonals grow at the same rate as a single eigenvalue. Under specific assumptions about
\mathcal{N}(S)
, for example on the sparsity of null space vectors, these bounds may be tightened.

6
Joint diagonal and off-diagonal penalties

Section
4
concentrated on the diagonal penalty while Section
5
focused on the off-diagonal penalty with the other fixed equal to
0
. Since we have assumed non-negative penalty functions, the results of Proposition
3
and Corollary
2
trivially hold for any
{\mathrm{pen}}\geq 0
while the existence part of Proposition
6
holds for any
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\geq 0
, with a sufficiently strong
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
also allowing the estimate to exist even when
S
has diagonal entries equal to
0
and for lower rank
S
.

However, even when the conditions of both Corollary
2
and
6
do not hold, the combination of the diagonal and off-diagonal penalties may still be enough to give existence.

Proposition 8
.

Let
S
be PSD with
dim(\mathcal{N}(S))=m_{0}
and strictly positive diagonal entries. Suppose that

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{pen}_{D}(x)}{\log x}=c_{D}

\displaystyle\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{pen}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}=c_{O}

If
c_{D}+c_{O}>m_{0}
then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists.

Proof.

Consider a sequence of PD matrices
\Theta^{(t)}
as in Lemma
1
.
Let
\theta^{(t)}_{\max}
denote the off-diagonal entry of
\Theta^{(t)}
with largest absolute value, i.e.,
\theta^{(t)}_{\max}=\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}j_{t}}
where
(i_{t},j_{t})\in\arg\max_{i<j}\left|\theta^{(t)}_{ij}\right|
. By Lemma
3
, there exist
\delta>0
and
t_{0}
such that

\left|\theta^{(t)}_{\max}\right|\geq\delta\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}

for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

Next, consider the diagonal entries. Let
v^{(t)}_{\max}
be an eigenvector associated with
\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}
. Since
v^{(t)}_{\max}
is a unit vector,
\sum_{i=1}^{p}(v^{(t)}_{\max,i})^{2}=1
, and therefore
\max_{i=1,\ldots,p}(v^{(t)}_{\max,i})^{2}\geq 1/p
. Let
i_{t}=\arg\max_{i=1,\ldots,p}(v^{(t)}_{\max,i})^{2}
. Then

\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\lambda^{(t)}_{k}\left(v^{(t)}_{ki_{t}}\right)^{2}\geq\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}\left(v^{(t)}_{\max,i_{t}}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}}{p}

The penalised likelihood is equal to

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\log\lambda^{(t)}_{k}-\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{ij}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{ii}\right)+c^{(t)}

where
c^{(t)}=\sum_{k=m+1}^{p}\log\lambda^{(t)}_{k}-{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta^{(t)})
is uniformly bounded, say
c^{(t)}\leq c
.

Since
{\mathrm{pen}}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
are non-negative, the penalised likelihood can be upper bounded by removing all penalty terms except those associated to
\theta^{(t)}_{\max}
and
\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}
, and replacing
\lambda^{(t)}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{(t)}_{m^{(t)}}
by
\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}
. To hold uniformly over
V^{(t)}
, this is done when
m=m_{0}
is at its maximum value. Hence

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\leq m_{0}\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}-\mathrm{pen}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{\max}\right)-\mathrm{pen}_{D}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}\right)+c

The conditions
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\mathrm{pen}_{D}(x)/\log x=c_{D}
and
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\mathrm{pen}(x)/\log\left|x\right|=c_{O}
imply that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist thresholds
x_{D},x_{O}
such that
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)\geq(c_{D}-\epsilon)\log x
for all
x\geq x_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)\geq(c_{O}-\epsilon)\log\left|x\right|
for all
\left|x\right|\geq x_{O}
. Since also
\left|\theta^{(t)}_{\max}\right|\geq\delta\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}
and
\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}\geq\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}/p
, for sufficiently large
t
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{pen}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{\max}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(c_{O}-\epsilon\right)\log\left|\theta^{(t)}_{\max}\right|\geq\left(c_{O}-\epsilon\right)\left(\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}+\log\delta\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{pen}_{D}\left(\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(c_{D}-\epsilon\right)\log\theta^{(t)}_{i_{t}i_{t}}\geq\left(c_{D}-\epsilon\right)\left(\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}-\log p\right)

Substituting these bounds into the upper bound for the penalised likelihood gives

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq m_{0}\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}-\left(c_{O}-\epsilon\right)\left(\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}+\log\delta\right)-\left(c_{D}-\epsilon\right)\left(\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}-\log p\right)+c

\displaystyle=\big(m_{0}-(c_{O}+c_{D})+2\varepsilon\big)\log\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}+(c_{O}-\varepsilon)\log\delta+(c_{D}-\varepsilon)\log p+c

Since
c_{D}+c_{O}>m_{0}
, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
m_{0}-(c_{O}+c_{D})+2\varepsilon<0
. For this choice of
\varepsilon
, the upper bound tends to
-\infty
as
\lambda^{(t)}_{\max}\to\infty
, which completes the proof.
∎

This result can be extended to all PSD
S
by taking the maximum
m_{0}=p-1
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
S
be PSD with strictly positive diagonal entries. If

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{pen}_{D}(x)}{\log x}+\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{pen}(x)}{\log\left|x\right|}>p-1

then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
exists.

Proof.

If
S
has strictly positive diagonal then it cannot be the zero matrix. It therefore has at most
p-1
eigenvalues equal to
0
. The result follows by applying Proposition
8
with
m_{0}=p-1
.
∎

These results show how, even when the individual penalties
{\mathrm{pen}}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
do not satisfy any of the sufficient conditions for convergence from Sections
4
and
5
, the combination of the two penalties can still ensure existence.

Using both penalties similarly affects the case when the solution does not exist for any PSD
S
. The following is an extension of Propositions
4
and
7
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
q=p(p-1)/2
. If

\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left|x\right|+q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log\left|x\right|=-\infty

then the penalised likelihood estimate
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
does not exist for any only PSD
S
.

Proof.

We follow the same style of proof as Proposition
4
with
\Theta(t)=I_{p}+tv_{1}v_{1}^{\top}
for unit vector
v_{1}\in N(S)
and
t>0
. Recalling that
\log\det\Theta^{(t)}=\log(1+t)
,
{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta^{(t)})={\mathrm{tr}}(S)
,
\theta_{ii}^{(t)}=1+tv_{1i}^{2}\leq 1+t
and
\theta_{ij}^{(t)}=tv_{1i}v_{1j}
so
\left|\theta_{ij}^{(t)}\right|\leq t<1+t
, the penalised likelihood function is lower bounded by

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\geq\log(1+t)-{\mathrm{tr}}(S)-p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(1+t)-q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(1+t)

The condition
\liminf_{x\to\infty}p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)+q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)-\log x=-\infty
ensures that there is an increasing sequence of
t
such that this lower bound tends to
\infty
and therefore along this sequence,
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
.
∎

7
Generalisations

In the previous results we made a number of simplifying assumptions about the form of the penalty function. It was assumed to be separable with the same penalty across all diagonal entries and across all off-diagonal entries. These penalties on the individual entries were also assumed to be non-negative, symmetric around 0, increasing in
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|
and continuous, since these are all common properties for penalty functions. However, most of these assumptions were for notational convenience and the results of the previous sections can easily generalise beyond these assumptions.

7.1
Unequal penalties

For the more general form of a separable penalty function
{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)=\sum_{i\leq j}{\mathrm{pen}}_{ij}(\theta_{ij})
where a different penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{ij}
is applied to each
\theta_{ij}
, aside from notational convenience, the results of Sections
4
-
6
easily generalise, although attention must be paid to the precise generalisation of the conditions. In particular, it must be ensured that any bounds in the proof can be maintained under the new condition.

In existence proofs where an upper bound for the penalised likelihood is required, consider an alternative penalty function
{\mathrm{Pen}}_{\min}
with diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\min,D}(x)=\min_{i}{\mathrm{pen}}_{ii}(x)
and off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\min}(x)=\min_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}_{ij}(x)
. Notice that
{\mathrm{Pen}}_{\min}
satisfies the condition of having common penalty functions on the diagonals and on the off-diagonals and that
{\mathrm{Pen}}_{\min}\leq{\mathrm{Pen}}
so

l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\leq l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}_{\min}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)

It follows that if
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}_{\min}}
exists then so does
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
and so if
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\min,D}
or
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\min}
satisfy any of the existence conditions from Sections
4
-
6
then the result extends to
\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Pen}}
.

On the other hand, the non-existence results require a lower bound on the penalised likelihood which is achieved via an upper bound on the penalty function. For the diagonal penalty this results in a condition on
p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)
while for the off-diagonal penalty there is a condition on
q\,{\mathrm{pen}}(x)
where
q
is the number of off-diagonal entries. For unequal penalties, these are replaced by conditions on the sum of the diagonal penalties
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\mathrm{sum},D}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{ii}(x)
and of the off-diagonal penalties
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\mathrm{sum}}(x)=\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}_{ij}(x)
. The proofs remain unchanged with this replacement.

For simplicity, in the remainder of this section we will revert to the equal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
framework. However, the results can similarly be generalised.

7.2
Unbounded below penalties

The non-negative assumption was again for notational convenience and can easily be relaxed to lower-bounded penalty functions. If the off-diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}
is unbounded below then the solution is guaranteed to not exist because the penalised likelihood function is then unbounded above. Hence it is required that for some
c>-\infty
,
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)\geq c
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. For simplicity we will continue assuming this holds for
c=0
so that
{\mathrm{pen}}
is non-negative.

However, existence can still be maintained when the diagonal penalty is not lower bounded at
0
,
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)=-\infty
. This is because the log-likelihood also tends to
-\infty
as any
\theta_{ii}\to 0
. Existence then depends on the limiting behaviour of
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
at 0 in comparison to the
\log
function. Unlike before, existence proofs must now also pay attention to sequences where
\lambda_{\min}\to 0
. However, this is a necessary but not sufficient condition for
\theta_{ii}\to 0
. If all diagonal entries remain bounded away from 0, then the diagonal penalty remains lower bounded and proving that
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
is as before. Therefore the only additions to the previous proofs are for sequences where some
\theta_{ii}\to 0
. This leads to a direct extension of Proposition
3
.

Proposition 10
.

If
\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
and
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
then
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
exists for any PSD
S
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3
, removing the trace term and using Hadamard’s inequality gives the upper bound

l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log\left(\theta_{ii}\right)-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left(\theta_{ii}\right)

From the Proposition conditions we know that if
\theta_{ii}\to 0
or
\theta_{ii}\to\infty
then
\log\left(\theta_{ii}\right)-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left(\theta_{ii}\right)\to-\infty
. Meanwhile, if
\theta_{ii}
remains finite then
\log\left(\theta_{ii}\right)-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left(\theta_{ii}\right)
is also finite. It therefore follows that as any
\theta_{ii}\to 0
, the whole sum
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log\left(\theta_{ii}\right)-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}\left(\theta_{ii}\right)\to-\infty
, and therefore
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
also.
∎

While this only extends Proposition
3
, we conjecture that Corollary
2
and Proposition
8
also extend to the case where
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
since the same
\theta_{ii}\to 0
dynamics apply. However, proving these results is slightly more complicated due to dealing with sequences where
\theta_{ii}\to 0
and
\lambda_{\max}\to\infty
together. Such cases require different upper bounds based on the number of
\theta_{ii}\to 0
and the number of diverging
\lambda_{k}
.

On the other hand, if
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
goes to
-\infty
at
0
faster than the
\log
term, then the penalised likelihood estimate does not exist for any
S
, including PD
S
.

Proposition 11
.

If
\liminf_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
then
\hat{\Theta}_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}
does not exist for any PD
S
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta^{(t)}=tI_{p}
. Then, assuming w.l.o.g. that the off-diagonal penalty has
{\mathrm{pen}}(0)=0
, the penalised likelihood function is

\displaystyle l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)

\displaystyle=p\log t-t\,{\mathrm{tr}}(S)-p\,{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(t)

\displaystyle=p\left(\log t-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(t)\right)-t\,{\mathrm{tr}}(S)

The condition
\liminf_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=-\infty
ensures that there is a sequence of
t\to 0^{+}
such that
\log t-{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(t)\to\infty
. Along this sequence we also have
t\,{\mathrm{tr}}(S)\to 0
and so
l_{{\mathrm{Pen}}}\left(\,\Theta^{(t)}\mid S\,\right)\to\infty
.
∎

7.3
Non-symmetric penalties

Symmetry around
0
of the off-diagonal penalty function
{\mathrm{pen}}
was also for notational convenience. All existence results in Sections
5
and
6
extend to the non-symmetric case by lower bounding
{\mathrm{pen}}
with
{\mathrm{pen}}_{\min}(x)=\min\{{\mathrm{pen}}(x),{\mathrm{pen}}(-x)\}
. The non-existence results also extend with the same replacement, but a slight adaptation of the proof is required to ensure that the chosen sequence of
\Theta^{(t)}
has diverging
\theta_{ij}^{(t)}
of the correct sign.

7.4
Not increasing penalties

In penalised likelihoods it is most common to use penalty functions that are increasing in
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|
. This is because penalty functions are generally used to regularise and encourage sparsity in the estimate, which is achieved by applying a greater penalty to larger values. However, this is not required for any of the existence proofs and so all existence results still apply to penalty functions that are not increasing, as long as both
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
satisfy the given limiting conditions.

7.5
Non-continuous penalties

Most commonly used penalty functions are continuous due to the computational challenges of working with non-continuous penalties. While the proofs of Propositions
3
and
6
remain valid for non-continuous penalty functions, in so much as they show that the penalised likelihood function tends to negative infinity as the eigenvalues of
\Theta
grow, this is not enough to prove the existence of the penalised likelihood estimate. This is because the maximum may not be achieved due to the discontinuity.

One special case is the
L_{0}
penalty on off-diagonal entries where
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)=0
for
x=0
and
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)=\rho>0
for
x\neq 0
. This directly penalises the number of non-zero off-diagonals in
\Theta
. The maximised
L_{0}
penalised likelihood under a specific graphical model simply returns the MLE under that model. Hence the
L_{0}
penalised likelihood estimate can be seen as comparing the MLEs over all possible graphical models, subtracting a multiple of the number of edges from the maximised likelihood. Since the MLE is guaranteed to exist for all graphical models when
S
is PD
(
Uhler, 2012
)
, this implies the existence of the
L_{0}
penalised likelihood estimate for any PD
S
. While a formal proof goes beyond the scope of this paper, we conjecture that the
L_{0}
penalised likelihood estimate will continue to exist for positive semi-definite
S
when combined with a suitably strong penalty on the diagonal entries, satisfying the condition of Proposition
3
.

7.6
Partial correlation based penalties

Recently a new class of penalised likelihoods for Gaussian graphical models have been introduced that directly penalise the partial correlations rather than the off-diagonal entries of
\Theta

(
Carter et al., 2024
;
Bogdan et al., 2025
;
Carter and Molinari, 2025
)
. We denote the partial correlations by
\Delta_{ij}=-\theta_{ij}/\sqrt{\theta_{ii}\theta_{jj}}
which must be between
-1
and
1
for the resulting matrix to be PD. A penalty function is then placed on
\Delta=(\Delta_{ij})
and the diagonal entries of
\Theta
,
\theta=\mathrm{diag}(\Theta)

{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Delta,\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(\theta_{ii})+\sum_{i<j}{\mathrm{pen}}(\Delta_{ij})

The results of Section
4
directly generalise to this class of penalised likelihoods, as long as
{\mathrm{pen}}(\Delta_{ij})
satisfies the usual conditions of being lower bounded and continuous.

So far only an
L_{1}
penalty has been implemented for the partial correlations, which is bounded on the interval
[-1,1]
. For any such bounded penalty, existence of the solution relies solely on the diagonal penalty
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
. However, even if the penalty on
\Delta_{ij}
is allowed to grow infinitely such that
{\mathrm{pen}}(\Delta_{ij})\to\infty
as
\left|\Delta_{ij}\right|\to 1
, it is likely that this still isn’t enough to ensure existence. This is because there are only PSD
\Delta
such that no
\left|\Delta_{ij}\right|=1
and so
\left|\Delta_{ij}\right|\to 1
does not correspond to the boundary of PD matrices. It therefore seems that partial correlation based penalties will always rely on penalisation of the diagonal or other similar quantities in order to exist for only PSD
S
.

8
Bayesian methods and posterior propriety

For a prior distribution
p(\Theta)
, the posterior given
S
from a sample of size
n
is

p\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\propto\det(\Theta)^{n/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{2}\,{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)\right)\,p(\Theta)\,\mathbb{I}(\Theta\succ 0)

By taking the logarithm and considering the penalty function
{\mathrm{Pen}}(\Theta)=-\frac{2}{n}\log p(\Theta)
, the posterior can be related to the penalised likelihood function via

\log p\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)=\frac{n}{2}\,l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)+c

for some constant
c
. It follows that the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate under
p(\Theta)
is equal to the penalised likelihood estimate with penalty
{\mathrm{Pen}}
. The results of the previous sections can therefore be directly applied to prove the existence of the MAP estimate under separable priors of the form

p(\Theta)\propto\prod_{i=1}^{p}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\prod_{i<j}\pi(\theta_{ij})\,\mathbb{I}(\Theta\succ 0)

by substituting
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)=-\frac{2}{n}\log\pi_{D}(x)
and
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)=-\frac{2}{n}\log\pi(x)
, noting that the assumptions of
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
and
{\mathrm{pen}}
should be passed on to
\pi_{D}
and
\pi
respectively. In this section we will continue to assume that
\pi_{D}
and
\pi
are continuous.

Beyond existence of the MAP, it is useful in Bayesian statistics to ensure that the posterior distribution is proper, i.e. the unnormalised posterior has finite integral. The existence proofs for the penalised likelihood estimate demonstrated that, under certain conditions on
{\mathrm{Pen}}
,
l_{\mathrm{Pen}}\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to-\infty
as
\lVert\Theta\rVert\to\infty
. It therefore follows that the posterior
p\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)\to 0
as
\lVert\Theta\rVert\to\infty
under the equivalent conditions on
p(\Theta)
. However, this is not enough to ensure that the posterior is proper because the integral of a function over an unbounded set can still be infinite even if the function is finite and tends to 0 at the boundary.

To investigate the propriety of the posterior further, we begin with the case where
S
is PD.

Proposition 12
.

Suppose
S
is PD,
n\geq 1
,
\pi
is bounded, and
\pi_{D}
has
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\pi_{D}(x)<\infty
and
\int_{0}^{1}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
. Then the posterior
p\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)
is proper.

Proof.

We want to show that
Z<\infty
where

Z=\int_{\Theta\succ 0}\det(\Theta)^{n/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{2}\,{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)\right)\,\prod_{i=1}^{p}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\prod_{i<j}\pi(\theta_{ij})\,d\Theta

Using Hadamard’s inequality,
\det(\Theta)^{n/2}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}^{n/2}
. Since
S
is PD, the trace term is lower bounded by
{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)\geq\lambda_{\min}(S){\mathrm{tr}}(\Theta)=\lambda_{\min}(S)\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}
. Since
\pi
is bounded,
\prod_{i<j}\pi(\theta_{ij})<M
for some constant
M<\infty
. Hence we have the upper bound

Z\leq\int_{\Theta\succ 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}^{n/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{2}\,\lambda_{\min}(S)\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{p}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\,M\,d\Theta

Note that the integrand does not depend on the off-diagonals
\theta_{ij}
and for given diagonals
\theta=(\theta_{ii})_{i=1}^{p}
, the off-diagonals are restricted by positive definiteness to
\left|\theta_{ij}\right|<\sqrt{\theta_{ii}\theta_{jj}}
. For fixed diagonals, the volume of the space of off-diagonals is therefore upper bounded by
\prod_{i<j}2\sqrt{\theta_{ii}\theta_{jj}}=2^{q}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}^{(p-1)/2}
where
q=p(p-1)/2
is the number of off-diagonals. Hence

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\theta\in(0,\infty)^{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}^{(n+p-1)/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{2}\,\lambda_{\min}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{p}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=C\prod_{i=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\exp(-ax)\pi_{D}(x)\,dx

where
C=2^{q}M
and
a=\frac{n}{2}\lambda_{\min}
are constants.

On
[1,\infty)
,
\pi_{D}
is bounded (by continuity and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\pi_{D}(x)<\infty
) and
\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\exp(-ax)\,dx<\infty
. On
(0,1]
,
\exp(-ax)\leq 1
and
\int_{0}^{1}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
. Hence
Z<\infty
and so the posterior is proper.
∎

In terms of the corresponding penalty functions, the conditions basically mean that
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)
and
{\mathrm{pen}}(x)
cannot tend to
-\infty
as
\left|x\right|\to\infty
, while close to
0
,
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
cannot go to
-\infty
faster than
c\log x
where
c=1+(p+1)/n
. In other words,
\limsup_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x<c
. Note that this is actually a weaker condition than in Section
7.2
where we required
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)-\log x=\infty
. This is because a function can tend to
\infty
at 0 (i.e. the maximum doesn’t exist) but still have finite integral on
(0,1)
.

Moving on to PSD
S
, we encounter the same problem as with penalised likelihoods where the trace term can remain constant when
\Theta
grows in directions in the null space of
S
. This requires stronger conditions on
\pi
and
\pi_{D}
to maintain propriety of the posterior for all PSD
S
and any
n
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
S
be PSD and
n\geq 1
. If
\pi
is proper,
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi(x)\,dx<\infty
, and
\pi_{D}
has finite
n/2
moment,
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
, then the posterior
p\left(\,\Theta\mid S\,\right)
is proper.

Proof.

Let
Z
be as in the previous proof. Using the same Hadamard inequality and
\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}(S\Theta)\right)\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Theta\succ 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{ii}^{n/2}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\prod_{i<j}\pi(\theta_{ij})\,d\Theta

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{ii}^{n/2}\pi_{D}(\theta_{ii})\,d\theta_{ii}\prod_{i<j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi(\theta_{ij})\,d\theta_{ij}

Each of these integrals is finite by the conditions on
\pi_{D}
and
\pi
. Hence
Z<\infty
and so the posterior is proper.
∎

Here we used a finite
n/2
moment for
\pi_{D}
which implies that
\int_{0}^{1}x^{n/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
also. However, this is a slightly stronger condition than that in Proposition
12
where
\int_{0}^{1}x^{(n+p-1)/2}\pi_{D}(x)\,dx<\infty
. This is because we haven’t used the upper bounded condition on
\pi
, relying only on propriety. But
\pi
being proper is actually a weaker condition around
0
than boundedness, because a proper
\pi
can still have
\lim_{x\to 0}\pi(x)=\infty
. It therefore seems likely that this weaker condition on
\pi_{D}
is also enough for the PSD case in Proposition
13
, since the trace term, and therefore
S
, does not affect the integral around 0.

In terms of the corresponding penalty functions, propriety of
\pi
is given by
\liminf_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}(x)/\log x>2/n
, while
\pi_{D}
having finite
n/2
moment is achieved if
\limsup_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x<1+2/n
and
\liminf_{x\to\infty}{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}(x)/\log x>1+2/n
. In particular, this
x\to\infty
condition on
{\mathrm{pen}}_{D}
is stronger than that of Proposition
3
.

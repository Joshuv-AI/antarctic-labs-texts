Five-loop Anomalous Dimensions of Cubic Scalar Theory from Operator Product Expansion
Rijun Huang, Qingjun Jin, and Yi Li

Abstract
Recently, an Operator Product Expansion method has been proposed for renormalization in quantum field theories. This method extracts UV divergences from two-point propagator-type integrals, and it is believed to be applicable to generic QFTs. In the present work, we apply the OPE method to the renormalization of scalar theory with cubic interactions in six-dimensions. We have successfully computed the 5-loop corrections to the anomalous dimensions of the ϕ Q \phi^{Q} operator, as well as the large N N expansion of scaling dimensions at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point to 1 / N 5 1/N^{5} order. This computation sets a new loop-order record for the anomalous dimension of the ϕ Q \phi^{Q} operator in cubic scalar theory, and further demonstrates the efficiency and capacity of the OPE method in renormalization.

2.1
The cubic scalar theory and its renormalization

The theory under consideration is the six-dimensional
O(N)
cubic scalar theory, defined by the Lagrangian,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi_{i})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\sigma)^{2}-\frac{g}{2!}\sigma\phi_{i}^{2}-\frac{h}{3!}\sigma^{3}~,

(1)

where
\sigma
and
\phi_{i},i=1,\ldots,N
denote two types of real scalar fields, with the
\phi_{i}
fields transforming as an
O(N)
vector. This theory is perturbatively renormalizable in six-dimensional space-time. The primary focus of this paper is the renormalization analysis of the symmetric traceless
\phi^{Q}
operator, defined as,

\phi^{Q}:=T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{Q}}\phi^{i_{1}}\cdots\phi^{i_{Q}}~,

(2)

where
T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{Q}}
is a symmetric traceless tensor and
Q
is a fixed charge number. We will work in the
\overline{\mbox{MS}}
regularization scheme

in
d=(6-\epsilon)
-dimension. The classical scaling dimension of
\phi^{Q}
is
Q(d/2-1)

\Delta_{Q}=Q\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)+\gamma_{Q}+Q\gamma_{\phi}~.

(3)

The difference between the total scaling dimension and the classical scaling dimension is defined as the anomalous dimension of the
\phi^{Q}
operator. The gamma function
\gamma_{Q}
of
\phi^{Q}
operator can be computed perturbatively by Feynman diagrams of inserting the
\phi^{Q}
operator in the 1PI Green function with
Q
external legs. However, explicit evaluation of these correlation functions is complicated by the cumbersome

tensor structures inherent to the operator, which cause the number of terms in Feynman rules to grow exponentially with increasing
Q
. To address this problem, it is convenient to introduce an auxiliary
O(N)
zero-norm complex vector

satisfying
q^{2}=0
,
q\cdot\bar{q}=1
, and define the tensor
T
as
Antipin:2020abu
;
Jin:2022nqq

T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{Q}}:=\frac{1}{Q!}q_{i_{1}}q_{i_{2}}\cdots q_{i_{Q}}~.

(4)

This allows us to redefine the
\phi^{Q}
operator in terms of a
\varphi^{Q}
operator,

\phi^{Q}:=\frac{1}{Q!}\varphi^{Q}~,

(5)

where the complex field
\varphi
is a redefinition of the scalar field
\varphi:=q\cdot\phi

\bar{\varphi}:=\bar{q}\cdot\phi

\phi^{Q}

\varphi^{Q}
operator. The latter can now be computed by
\varphi^{Q}

Q\varphi

\varphi

\varphi

\varphi

For purpose of renormalization computation, following standard procedures, we introduce renormalized fields as,

\varphi_{0}=Z^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\varphi}\varphi~~~,~~~\sigma_{0}=Z^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\sigma}\sigma~,

(6)

and renormalized coupling constants as,

g_{0}=\mu^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\widetilde{Z}_{g}g~~~,~~~h_{0}=\mu^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\widetilde{Z}_{h}h~~~\mbox{with}~~~\widetilde{Z}_{g}=Z_{g}Z^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\sigma}Z^{-1}_{\varphi}~~~,~~~\widetilde{Z}_{h}=Z_{h}Z^{-\frac{3}{2}}_{\sigma}~,

(7)

where
g_{0},h_{0}
denote bare coupling constants and
\varphi_{0},\sigma_{0}
denote bare fields. We also define the renormalized operator
\varphi^{Q}_{R}=Z_{\varphi^{Q}}\varphi_{0}^{Q}
. Since
\varphi_{0}^{Q}=Z_{\varphi}^{\frac{Q}{2}}\varphi^{Q}

\varphi_{R}^{Q}=\widetilde{Z}_{\varphi^{Q}}\varphi^{Q}~~~\mbox{with}~~~\widetilde{Z}_{\varphi^{Q}}=Z_{\varphi^{Q}}Z_{\varphi}^{\frac{Q}{2}}~.

(8)

Then

\varphi_{R}^{Q}
-
Q\varphi

-factor.

Provided that the
Z_{\varphi}
,
Z_{\sigma}
,
Z_{g}

\beta(g)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial\ln\mu}

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{g}+\frac{\partial\ln\widetilde{Z}_{g}}{\partial g}&\frac{\partial\ln\widetilde{Z}_{g}}{\partial h}\\

\frac{\partial h}{\partial\ln\mu}\\

(9)

Then the field anomalous dimensions of

\gamma_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\varphi}}{\partial g}\beta(g)+\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\varphi}}{\partial h}\beta(h)\right)~~~,~~~\gamma_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\sigma}}{\partial g}\beta(g)+\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\sigma}}{\partial h}\beta(h)\right)~.

(10)

Furthermore, once

(11)

where the first term computes

With the knowledge of beta functions and the anomalous dimensions, in order to study the large

\beta(g^{\ast})=0~~~,~~~\beta(h^{\ast})=0~.

(12)

After substituting the solutions

Z_{{\tiny\texttt{X}}}=1+\sum_{n_{\ell}=1}^{L}\sum_{n_{\epsilon}=1}^{n_{\ell}}\frac{z_{\tiny\texttt{X}}^{n_{\ell}n_{\epsilon}}}{\epsilon^{n_{\epsilon}}}~,

(13)

in which the symbol

\varphi,\sigma

\varphi^{Q}

2.2
The OPE method for the renormalization

In general quantum field theory, the product of two time-ordered operators admits an asymptotic expansion in the limit where their coordinates coincide, known as the Operator Product Expansion. The expansion basis is a set of composite operators of the theory, ordered according to their dimensions, while the expansion coefficients are functions of the coordinate difference, which are in general singular. When considering the OPE in momentum space by Fourier transformation with respect to the coordinate difference, the expansion is performed in the limit of large momentum
p\to\infty
, schematically expressed as,

\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{II}}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(k-p)\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{I}}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(p)~\sim~C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(g_{i};p)\widehat{O}_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(k)+\cdots

(14)

where
k
is a finite momentum, and the momenta of the two operators tend to the opposite directions of infinite limits
1
1

1

Pictorially, we can consider the two operators with large momentum as two hard fields propagating in the background of soft fields. This idea has also been realized in the study of the boundary operators of BCFW recursion relations
Huang:2022qnx
. By shifting two external momenta
p_{1},p_{2}
with a complex auxiliary momentum
zq
as
p_{1}\to p_{1}+zq
,
p_{2}\to p_{2}-zq
, at the large
z
limit we get two hard fields propagating in the background of soft fields.
. The superscript
{}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}
in the operator
\widehat{O}
represents a renormalized operator in dimensional regularization scheme.

Alternatively, we may consider the OPE of bare operators, schematically written as,

\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{II}}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(k-p)\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{I}}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(p)~\sim~C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)\widehat{O}_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(k)+\cdots

(15)

where the Wilson coefficients are now functions of
p^{2}
and the bare coupling constants. Since renormalized and bare operators are related by their corresponding
Z

\widehat{O}^{{\tiny\mbox{ren}}}=Z_{\widehat{O}}\widehat{O}^{{\tiny\mbox{bare}}}~,

(16)

from eqn.(
14

C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(g_{i};p)=\frac{Z_{\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{I}}}Z_{\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{II}}}}{Z_{\widehat{O}_{i}}}C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)~.

(17)

Special attention should be paid to the OPE of fundamental fields, since the
Z
-factor of a fundamental field
\varphi
or
\sigma
does not correspond directly to
Z_{\varphi}
or
Z_{\sigma}
. Instead, it is defined through
\varphi=Z_{\varphi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{0}
or
\sigma=Z_{\sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{0}
. Furthermore, one usually computes the amputated correlation functions, and an extra factor

or

should be included for each fundamental field in the OPE of amputated correlation functions. With these two considerations, a fundamental field
\varphi
or
\sigma
contributes a
Z^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\varphi}
or
Z^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\sigma}

17

Z

Z^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\varphi}
or
Z^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\sigma}

Due to the fact that the Wilson coefficients of renormalized operators
C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{ren}}(g_{i};p)
are UV finite, once the Wilson coefficients of bare operators
C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)
are computed, the ratios of
Z
-factors can be determined using UV finiteness conditions generated from the cancellation of
\epsilon
poles. It could be possible that some operators in the basis have the same dimension, leading to the operator mixing situation. In this case, the relation (
16
) is generalized to the vectors of mixed operators related by matrices of
Z
-factors. The
Z
-matrices can be determined by the UV finiteness conditions from all the OPE coefficients corresponding to the mixed operators. Importantly, the above discussion is based on general operators, which means that it is applicable to either simple operators, or operators with Lorentz indices, with fermion and gauge fields,
etc
.

The Wilson coefficient
C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)
of operator
\widehat{O}_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}
can be computed by the large momentum expansion of bare correlation functions. For an operator
\widehat{O}_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}
composed of
n

C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)=\Big\langle\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{II}}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(k-p)\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{I}}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(p)\varphi_{0}(k_{1})\varphi_{0}(k_{2})\cdots\varphi_{0}(k_{n})\Big\rangle\Big|_{k_{i}\to 0~,~i=1,\ldots,n}~,

(18)

where
k=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
. Pictorially, this corresponding to splitting the Feynman graph of the original correlation function into hard and soft sub-graphs by cutting
n
internal propagators and/or external legs, with the Wilson coefficient receiving contributions from all possible
n
-cut hard sub-graphs. If the operator contains fields with derivatives, for instance
\partial_{\mu}\varphi_{0}(k)
, we can compute the coefficient by acting

are computed by the two-point propagator-type integrals. This greatly simplifies the computation, especially when dealing with correlation functions with large number of external legs.

The above discussion outlines the core idea of computing renormalization functions via OPE method. To determine beta functions and anomalous dimensions, we first need the

),

\frac{Z_{\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{I}}}Z_{\widehat{O}_{\tiny\mbox{II}}}}{Z_{\widehat{O}_{i}}}C_{i}^{\tiny\mbox{bare}}(g_{i0};p)=\mbox{UV~finite}~.

(19)

from the corresponding two-point integrals. Then using the general

(

) for

-factor can be obtained by solving these equations, and consequently the

-factor can be successfully determined to the desired loop order, provided the two-point integrals can be computed to that order.

2.3
From Feynman diagrams to two-point integrals

The UV finiteness conditions discussed in the previous subsection require computing two-point integrals derived from two-point, three-point and the
\varphi^{Q}
-
Q\varphi
correlation functions. In this subsection, we review some techniques which will help to simplify the problem of generating Feynman diagrams and transfer them to two-point graphs for subsequent evaluation of two-point integrals.

The
\Phi
-field representation

The cubic scalar theory we consider involves two types of fields
\varphi,\sigma
, and two types of cubic interactions. The complex field
\varphi
carries a scalar charge that flows along the line of
\varphi,\bar{\varphi}
fields, which can be represented by an arrow indicating the direction of charge flow. When generating Feynman diagrams, we need to account for all possible field configurations in the internal lines, which becomes a rather difficult task when the number of internal lines is large. It has also been observed that for a given external field configuration, different internal field configurations only contribute to varying combinations of couplings, while the underlying integrals remain identical. Thus there is no need to generate all Feynman diagrams and redundantly compute the same integrals. To clarify the separation between the structure of couplings and the integrals, we will adopt the
\Phi
-field representation. In this framework, we combine the
\varphi_{i},i=1,\ldots,N
fields and the
\sigma
field into a single
(N+1)
component scalar field as,

\Phi_{I}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{N}):=(\sigma,\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N})~,

(30)

and the cubic interactions can be universally encoded as

\frac{1}{6}\lambda_{IJK}\Phi_{I}\Phi_{J}\Phi_{K}~~~\mbox{where}~~~\lambda_{000}=h~~\mbox{and}~~\lambda_{0ij}=g\delta_{ij}~~\forall i,j\neq 0~,

(31)

with all other components of the symmetric coupling
\lambda_{IJK}
vanishing. In the
\Phi
-field representation, we essentially generate the topologies of Feynman diagrams without specifying the explicit configuration of component fields. In this case, we have separated the information of the scalar integral part from the factor of couplings. The Feynman scalar integral can be derived from a given topology by assigning propagators according to Feynman rules, while the factors of couplings of all Feynman diagrams sharing the same topology are encoded in the combination of
\lambda_{IJK}
couplings, which we define as
the coupling factor
.

The symmetry factor and the coupling factor

When writing the Feynman integral of correlation functions, we first generate all corresponding topologies with private Mathematica code. For each topology, we get the scalar integral from the combination of propagators, multiplied by the standard symmetry factor
\mathbb{S}_{\footnotesize\mbox{ym}}
from the total symmetry of the graph and external legs. Each topology effectively represents a collection of numerous Feynman diagrams that share the same external state configuration but differ in their internal state configurations. By summing over the indices of the
\lambda_{IJK}
, we get the coupling factor
\mathbb{C}_{\footnotesize\mbox{f}}
of the contributing Feynman graphs.

The Feynman graphs of
\varphi^{Q}
-
Q\varphi
correlation functions

In the UV finiteness conditions, the function

G=\sum\mathbb{S}_{\footnotesize\mbox{ym}}(\Gamma^{{\tiny\mbox{topo}}})\mathbb{C}_{\footnotesize\mbox{f}}(\Gamma^{{\tiny\mbox{topo}}})\mathbb{U}_{\footnotesize\mbox{v}}(\Gamma^{{\tiny\mbox{topo}}})~,

(34)

where the summation runs over all possible topologies, and the UV divergence
\mathbb{U}_{\footnotesize\mbox{v}}(\Gamma^{{\tiny\mbox{topo}}})
is computed from the two-point integrals generated by these topologies. Remark that the coupling factor
\mathbb{C}_{\footnotesize\mbox{f}}
of a topology depends on the explicit external field configuration, but the symmetry factor
\mathbb{S}_{\footnotesize\mbox{ym}}
and the UV divergence
\mathbb{U}_{\footnotesize\mbox{v}}
are irrelevant to the external fields, they are computed from the topologies. For two-point correlation functions,
G
is directly evaluated using all 1PI two-point topologies. For three-point correlation functions, we start from all 1PI three-point topologies and compute the independent two-point graphs by removing one external leg from these topologies in all possible ways. For the
\varphi^{Q}
-
Q\varphi
correlation function, we start from all 1PI
\varphi^{Q}\to Q\varphi
topologies, and
G_{Q}
can be computed from all independent two-point graphs generated by removing
(Q-1)
external legs from these topologies in all possible ways.

By investigating the structure of 1PI
\varphi^{Q}\to Q\varphi
topologies, it has been proven that only a part of these topologies contributes to the two-point integrals
Huang:2024hsn
. These contributing topologies are called irreducible graphs, characterized by the property that when the operator vertex is deleted, all remaining external legs stay connected in a single graph (except those that reduce to a single line). The other topologies are called reducible graphs, with the property that when deleting the operator vertex causes the external legs to distribute into two or more separate connected components. Hence we only need to generate the irreducible graphs of the 1PI
\varphi^{Q}\to Q\varphi
topologies, and compute
G_{Q}
from their corresponding two-point graphs after all possible removals of
(Q-1)
legs. The summation of these graphs gives,

G_{Q}(g_{0},h_{0};x_{0},\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{Q-1}},x_{Q})=\mathbb{U}_{\footnotesize\mbox{v}}\left(\frac{1}{x_{Q}^{2\lambda}}\right)+\sum_{n=2}^{Q}C_{Q-1}^{n-1}\mathtt{F}_{n}(\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{1}},\cdots,\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{n-1}},x_{Q})~.

(35)

The first term comes from the tree-level contribution and evaluates to 1, while

\mathtt{F}_{n}(\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{1}},\cdots,\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{n-1}},x_{Q})=(n-1)!\sum_{n_{\ell}=n-1}^{L}G_{\scriptsize\mbox{irre}}^{n_{\ell}{\tiny\mbox{-loop}}}(g_{0},h_{0};x_{0},\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{Q-1}},x_{Q})~.

(36)

The

receives contributions from all
n_{\ell}
-loop irreducible topologies. As in the standard procedure, it can be evaluated using the symmetry factor, coupling factor and UV divergence as,

G_{\scriptsize\mbox{irre}}^{n_{\ell}{\tiny\mbox{-loop}}}(g_{0},h_{0};x_{0},\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\mbox{\textst{$x$}}_{\sigma_{Q-1}},x_{Q})=\sum_{{\tiny\mbox{irreducible}}\atop{\tiny\mbox{topologies}}}\mathbb{S}_{\footnotesize\mbox{ym}}(\Gamma^{n_{\ell}}_{\otimes\varphi\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}\cdots\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}})\mathbb{C}_{\footnotesize\mbox{f}}(\Gamma^{n_{\ell}}_{\otimes\varphi\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}\cdots\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}})\mathbb{U}_{\footnotesize\mbox{v}}(\Gamma^{n_{\ell}}_{\otimes\varphi\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}\cdots\mbox{\textst{{\scriptsize$\varphi$}}}})~,

(37)

where the summation is over all non-isomorphic graphs generated by removing
(Q-1)
external legs of the irreducible 1PI topologies of the
\varphi^{Q}
-
Q\varphi
correlation function.

Let us summarize this section. After generating the independent topologies of
\varphi^{Q}

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we apply the OPE method of renormalization proposed in
Huang:2024hsn
to evaluate the 5-loop scaling dimensions of the
\phi^{Q}
operator in the six-dimensional cubic scalar theory. The result is derived from over 40 000 two-point propagator-type integrals computed using the graphical function method, combined with non-trivial summation among these integrals. We have successfully obtained the 5-loop correction to the anomalous dimension of the
\phi^{Q}
operator, as well as the large
N
expansion of the scaling dimension at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point up to the
1/N^{5}
order. It stands for a new record for the scaling dimensions of
\phi^{Q}
operator in cubic scalar theory. The result shows consistency with lower-loop perturbative calculations reported in other literature and aligns with the corresponding terms derived via semi-classical methods. Furthermore, the non-trivial cancellation of
\epsilon

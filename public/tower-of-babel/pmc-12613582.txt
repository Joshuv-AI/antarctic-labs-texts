Psychometric evaluation of the Chinese version of the Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT-C) in cancer patients
Authors: Shan Liu, Lujia Li, Bingbing Zhao, Mosha Jiang, Jianli Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe aim of this study was to adapt the original English Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT) into a Chinese version (BAT-C), following Beaton’s cross-cultural adaptation guidelines, and evaluate its psychometric properties among cancer patients, thereby providing a foundation for the standardized and evidence-based assessment of breakthrough pain in this population.MethodsThis study employed a cross-sectional design for psychometric validation. The English BAT underwent cultural adaptation according to Beaton’ s cross-cultural adaptation framework to develop the BAT-C. A convenience sampling strategy was utilized to recruit 310 cancer patients with breakthrough pain from four tertiary hospitals in Hebei Province, with data collected to examine the psychometric properties (reliability and validity) of the BAT-C.ResultsPre-test analysis revealed no significant ceiling or floor effects. The BAT-C comprises nine items organized into two dimensions—Severity and Impact of Breakthrough Pain, Onset Characteristics and Medication Effectiveness—alongside five open-ended items. Item-level content validity indices (I-CVI) ranged from 0.833 to 1.000, while the scale-level content validity index (S-CVI/Ave) reached 0.976. Exploratory factor analysis extracted two common factors explaining 82.521% of the cumulative variance. Confirmatory factor analysis confirmed acceptable model fit, with indices as follows: χ²/df = 1.733, RMSEA = 0.061, CFI = 0.988, TLI = 0.983, IFI = 0.988, and GFI = 0.953. Convergent validity was confirmed by composite reliability (CR) values of 0.927 and 0.900, and average variance extracted (AVE) values of 0.724 and 0.648. The scale demonstrated a Cronbach’ s α of 0.869 (95% CI: 0.85–0.89, SE: 0.012), with dimension-specific coefficients of 0.937 and 0.861. McDonald’ s ω was 0.947, with a hierarchical ω of 0.675; the Spearman-Brown coefficient was 0.828. Known-groups validity analysis indicated that, after controlling for confounding variables, the BAT-C significantly differentiated patients across ECOG performance statuses (P < 0.05, partial η² = 0.021).ConclusionThe BAT-C exhibited robust psychometric properties in this sample, providing preliminary support for its clinical utility as an assessment instrument for breakthrough pain in Chinese cancer patients; however, further validation is warranted in larger and more diverse populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12955-025-02446-z.

Introduction

According to the most recent statistics issued by the National Cancer Center of China [1], 4.825 million new cancer cases and 2.574 million cancer-related deaths were recorded in 2022, positioning China as the nation with the highest global cancer incidence and mortality. Among clinical symptoms in cancer patients, pain remains one of the most difficult-to-manage [2], particularly in advanced disease stages [3]. Chronic cancer pain is typically managed following the World Health Organization’ s (WHO) three-step analgesic ladder, via regular administration of opioid and non-opioid analgesics [4]. Despite substantial improvements in chronic pain control subsequent to the implementation of national cancer pain management demonstration programs in China [5, 6], a substantial proportion of patients still experience transient, severe pain episodes—termed breakthrough cancer pain (BTcP)—even when background pain is well controlled. BTcP is defined as a brief, intense pain flare occurring spontaneously or triggered by specific stimuli among patients maintaining stable opioid therapy for well-controlled baseline pain [7]. It is typically characterized by abrupt onset, rapid attainment of peak intensity, and moderate-to-severe intensity. The pathophysiology of BTcP exhibits a complex, multifactorial nature [8], most commonly ascribed to tumor-related factors—such as direct neural compression, tissue infiltration, or bone metastasis—which activate peripheral nociceptive pathways and may potentially induce neuropathic pain [9]. Additionally, tumor treatment-induced inflammatory responses can sensitize peripheral and central pain pathways, reducing the pain threshold and precipitating BTcP episodes. A recent international meta-analysis reported that approximately 59.2% of cancer patients report experiencing BTcP [10]. A regional cross-sectional survey undertaken in China documented a BTcP prevalence rate of 38% [11], while a national consensus guideline formulated by Chinese experts estimated prevalence to range between 33% and 95% [12], underscoring BTcP as a highly prevalent condition among Chinese cancer patients. These transient severe pain episodes often result in significant functional impairment and emotional distress, frequently leaving patients feeling helpless and lacking control [13].

Accurate assessment utilizing validated multidimensional pain assessment instruments constitutes a critical cornerstone of effective pain management [14]. In China, clinical practice predominantly employs unidimensional scales such as the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) [6, 15] and the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) [6, 16, 17], alongside multidimensional tools like the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) [6, 18]. However, these instruments are not designed to holistically capture the core features of BTcP, including triggering factors, peak intensity, duration, and the time-effectiveness of rescue medication. Consequently, there exists an urgent demand in China for a disease-specific assessment tool capable of precisely evaluating the episodic characteristics of BTcP to inform individualized pain management and adverse drug reaction care. The Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT) [19], developed and subjected to psychometric validation by Webber et al. in 2014, remains the sole internationally validated, clinically applicable instrument specifically designed for BTcP assessment. It has been successfully cross-culturally adapted into multiple languages, including Korean [20], French [21], and Spanish [22], with consistently robust reliability and validity outcomes.

Given the lack of such a tool in the Chinese context, the present study sought to translate, undertake cultural adaptation, and conduct psychometric evaluation of the BAT for use among Chinese-speaking adult cancer patients. The objective is to provide a standardized, evidence-based instrument for BTcP assessment in China and facilitate precision interventions in cancer-related pain management.

Materials and methods

The Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT)

The Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT) was constructed by Webber et al. in 2014 [19]. The original scale consists of 14 items, including nine closed-ended items with scoring protocols and five open-ended items designed to elicit patients’ subjective descriptions regarding pain localization, precipitating factors, analgesic types, and dosage regimens. Based on the scoring framework provided by the original author through authorized correspondence, three categorical items utilize a 5-point ordinal scale (scores: 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10), while the remaining six items employ an 11-point numeric rating scale ranging from 0 (“none”) to 10 (“most severe”). Item 11 employs reverse scoring methodology. Total BAT scores span from 6 to 86, with elevated scores reflecting increased severity of breakthrough pain manifestations.The English version of the BAT demonstrated satisfactory internal consistency reliability, evidenced by a total Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.700 and subscale-level coefficients of 0.750 and 0.550. Test-retest reliability, assessed via intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), yielded a value of 0.880 [19]. Among the 14 items, the nine closed-ended (ordinal) items contributed to total score calculation, whereas the five open-ended (nominal) items furnished supplementary clinical context to facilitate interpretation. Consistent with psychometric validation standards and building on adaptation experiences from the original and cross-lingual versions [19–21], the current study restricted factor analysis to the nine closed-ended items.

Sinicization of scales

Translation

In July 2024, the research team obtained email authorization from Dr. Katherine Webber (Imperial College London) for the translation and use of the BAT, adhering strictly to the specified authorization standards. The cross-cultural adaptation process followed the guidelines recommended by Beaton et al. [23], comprising the following steps:

(1) Forward translation was independently performed by two translators: one a clinical oncology Ph.D. with subject-matter expertise, and the other an English Ph.D. with six years of overseas experience but no medical background. This process yielded two preliminary Chinese versions (T1 and T2).

(2) Reconciliation: The research team conducted an item-by-item comparison of T1 and T2 against the original scale, resolving discrepancies through consensus-building discussions rather than compromise. A written report documented the reconciliation process, ultimately producing a unified Chinese version (BAT-1).

(3) Back-translation: Two additional bilingual translators—one a university English professor and the other a master’ s degree holder in English—with overseas experience, cultural competence, and English proficiency, but no medical background and no prior exposure to the original BAT, independently back-translated BAT-1 into English, yielding B-T1 and B-T2.

The research team then compared B-T1 and B-T2 with the original English version and BAT-1, adhering to principles of semantic, idiomatic, experiential, and conceptual equivalence, ultimately producing the pre-final Chinese version (BAT-2).

Cultural adaptation

A panel of six experts was convened, comprising two oncologists (associate chief physicians), three oncology nursing specialists (chief nurses), and one linguist (psychology professor). All experts held associate senior professional titles with over 10 years of clinical/academic experience: two held doctoral degrees, three held master’s degrees, and one held a bachelor’s degree. The expert panel conducted a comprehensive evaluation of the scale across four domains: semantic equivalence, cultural adaptability, clinical applicability, and logical consistency. The research team synthesized expert feedback and refined the scale accordingly. For instance, the subtitle was revised from “breakthrough pain refers to the short-lived increases in your cancer pain” to “Breakthrough pain refers to a sudden, short-lived increase of pain occurring on the basis of stable cancer pain control,” enhancing definition precision and patient comprehensibility. Item 3 was adjusted from “Does anything bring on your breakthrough pain” to “Does anything bring on your breakthrough pain (Causes or situations of pain onset)” to clarify response expectations, yielding the revised Chinese version BAT-3.

Pretest

A pretest was conducted in January 2025 at a tertiary comprehensive hospital in Chengde, Hebei Province, utilizing convenience sampling to recruit 30 eligible patients. Pretest data were analyzed to assess item response characteristics, with particular focus on ceiling and floor effects. Inclusion criteria: (1) histopathologic confirmation of cancer diagnosis; (2) Age ≥ 18 years; (3) Patients meeting all three diagnostic criteria for breakthrough cancer pain [3]: (i) persistent pain in the past week; (ii) stable baseline pain control (NRS ≤ 3); (iii) occurrence of brief severe pain in the past week (NRS ≥ 4); 4) Provided informed consent for study participation. Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with confirmed severe psychiatric disorders (e.g., schizophrenia bipolar disorder) or major emotional disturbances impairing questionnaire comprehension/completion; (2) Patients with physical frailty precluding participation; (3) Patients enrolled more than once.

The study’ s purpose and significance were detailed to participants, and informed consent was secured prior to survey completion. Each participant was instructed to verify comprehension of the scale’ s instructions and individual items, with any ambiguities systematically documented. Researchers synthesized participant feedback and implemented targeted revisions based on their recommendations. The key modifications included: Item 5 and 7 were revised from “How long does a typical episode of breakthrough pain last” and “How severe is a typical episode of breakthrough pain” to “How long does a common episode of breakthrough pain usually last” and “How severe is a common episode of breakthrough pain” Item 14 was refined from “How much do side-effects from the painkillers for your breakthrough pain bother you?” to “To what extent do side-effects from breakthrough pain medication cause you distress?” All remaining items were comprehended by participants without ambiguity, and scale completion required approximately 10 min. The finalized Chinese version (BAT-C) demonstrated enhanced clarity and usability. The BAT-C scale appears in the appendix section.

Psychometric properties

Study participants

Reliability and validity analyses adopted a cross-sectional design. Participants were recruited via convenience sampling from four comprehensive tertiary hospitals in Hebei Province between January and April 2025, specifically targeting cancer patients with breakthrough pain. Inclusion and exclusion criteria mirrored those applied in the pretest phase. Guidelines recommend that sample sizes for exploratory factor analysis (EFA) typically range from 5 to 10 times the number of items analyzed [24]. With 9 items planned for factor analysis and a 10% attrition allowance, the projected EFA sample size was 110 participants. For confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), recommended sample sizes start at 200 participants or range from 5 to 20 times the number of items analyzed, with CFA samples generally exceeding EFA sample sizes [25]. Incorporating a 10% attrition allowance, the final CFA sample size was estimated at 200 participants.

Instruments

(1) General information questionnaire

Developed by the research team based on review of relevant domestic and international literature, this questionnaire comprised two sections: (1) Demographic characteristics (e.g., sex, age, educational level), and (2) clinical information (e.g., cancer type, clinical stage, breakthrough pain type, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group [ECOG] performance status).

(2) Numeric Rating Scale (NRS)

Based on national expert consensus guidelines for cancer pain management, the NRS was employed as a diagnostic screening tool to identify participants with breakthrough pain (refer to Sect. "Pretest"). It served as the preliminary criterion for patient eligibility determination.

(3) Chinese version of the breakthrough pain assessment tool (BAT-C)

As the core instrument of this study, the BAT-C was developed through cross-cultural adaptation of the original BAT to comprehensively evaluate multidimensional characteristics of breakthrough pain in cancer patients (refer to Sect. 1.1: The Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool). The BAT-C underwent psychometric validation, including internal consistency assessment, exploratory/confirmatory factor analysis (EFA/CFA), and known-groups validity testing, to systematically examine its reliability and validity.

Data collection

Following approval from hospital department administrators and informed consent acquisition, data were collected from hospitalized patients with clinically diagnosed BTcP. Procedures included:

General information questionnaire

Patient demographic and clinical characteristics—encompassing age, gender, educational attainment, cancer diagnosis, clinical stage, pain classification, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) functional status—were systematically through concurrent review of electronic medical record review and direct patient interviews. Pain phenotype categorization (nociceptive vs. neuropathic) was established by synthesizing patient-reported symptom descriptions with attending physicians’ clinical evaluations.

Numeric Rating Scale (NRS)

Researchers extracted NRS records from the hospital’ s electronic medical system during the preceding week. Aligned with breakthrough cancer pain diagnostic thresholds established in the Chinese Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis and Treatment of Breakthrough Pain in Cancer [7], NRS scores constituted a critical eligibility determinant during participant screening protocols.

Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool—Chinese version (BAT-C):

Considering the sensitive nature of cancer pain and the unpredictable, intermittent pattern of BTcP, the BAT-C was administered under controlled, standardized conditions. Researchers clarified the study objectives to each participant and their family members, then conducted structured face-to-face interviews following the scale.All responses were documented accurately and objectively, avoiding leading or sensitive phrasing. Immediate on-site verification was performed post-completion to clarify ambiguities.

All questionnaires underwent independent dual verification by two research team members. A total of 314 questionnaires were administered, with 4 excluded for incomplete or inconsistent responses. Ultimately, 310 valid questionnaires were retained, yielding a valid response rate of 98.73%.

Statistical analysis

Dual data entry and verification were conducted in Excel. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0, AMOS 29.0, and R (version 4.5.1; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were presented as means ± standard deviations.

Ceiling and floor effects

Scale-level analysis involved computing the frequency of participants achieving the lowest or highest possible total scores. Values exceeding 15% suggest significant ceiling or floor effects [26]. Item-level evaluation entailed calculating the percentage of participants scoring the minimum or maximum value for each individual item. Frequencies surpassing 70% signaled significant item-level ceiling or floor effects [27].

Reliability analysis

Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient and McDonald’s ω. A Cronbach’s alpha coefficient ≥ 0.80 for the total scale and ≥ 0.70 for subscales is deemed acceptable for internal consistency reliability [28].McDonald’s ω and McDonald’s ω hierarchical were calculated via the psych package in R.It is generally accepted that a McDonald’s ω coefficient ≥ 0.70 is considered indicative of acceptable internal consistency, while ≥ 0.80 reflects a more desirable reliability threshold [29]. Additionally, “Corrected Item-Total Correlation” and “Cronbach’s alpha if item deleted” analyses were performed to assess each item’s contribution to overall reliability.

Split-half reliability was evaluated using the Spearman-Brown corrected coefficient. A Spearman-Brown corrected split-half reliability coefficient exceeding 0.70 is generally deemed acceptable [30].

Validity analysis

Content validity was assessed through the Item-Level Content Validity Index (I-CVI) and Scale-Level Content Validity Index (S-CVI/Ave). An I-CVI ≥ 0.78 and S-CVI/Ave ≥ 0.90 are indicative of good content validity [31].

Construct validity was evaluated through both Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA). It is widely recognized that EFA is suitable when the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) value exceeds 0.6 and Bartlett’ s test of sphericity achieves statistical significance (p < 0.05) [32]. In this study, principal axis factoring was employed for factor extraction, with subsequent Varimax rotation to improve interpretability. Factor retention was based on eigenvalues exceeding 1.0 (Kaiser’ s criterion), factor loadings >0.4, the scree plot, and expert consensus.CFA model fit was assessed using multiple indices, including Chi-square/degrees of freedom (χ²/df), Goodness-of-Fit Index (GFI), Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA), Tucker-Lewis Index (TLI), and Comparative Fit Index (CFI). A good model fit was defined by the following criteria: χ²/df < 3.0, GFI, TLI, and CFI >0.90, and RMSEA < 0.08 [33].

Convergent validity was evaluated by computing Average Variance Extracted (AVE) and Composite Reliability (CR). AVE values ≥ 0.50 and CR values ≥ 0.70 were deemed acceptable.

Known-groups validity was assessed by comparing BAT-C scores across patients with varying ECOG performance statuses. Participants were stratified into three groups: ECOG 0–2 (fully self-sufficient), ECOG 3 (partially self-sufficient), and ECOG 4 (bedridden). Given that confounding variables such as age, cancer type, and clinical stage may influence both ECOG status and BTcP severity, Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) was employed to control for these covariates. Box-plots were generated to visualize BAT-C score distributions across groups (median, interquartile range, minimum, and maximum values). The statistical significance threshold was set at α = 0.05. Additionally, partial eta-squared (η²) was computed to estimate effect sizes and evaluate the clinical significance of observed group differences.

Validity analysis

Content validity

The Scale-Level Content Validity Index (S-CVI/Ave) for the total BAT-C was 0.976, while Item-Level Content Validity Indices (I-CVI) ranged from 0.833 to 1.000, indicating excellent content validity.

Structural validity

Exploratory factor analysis

The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure of sampling adequacy for the BAT-C was 0.866, and Bartlett’ s test of sphericity yielded χ² = 1134.515 (p < 0.001), confirming data suitability for exploratory factor analysis (EFA). Principal axis factoring with varimax rotation was performed. Factors were retained based on eigenvalues ≥ 1.0, supplemented by scree plot inspection, alignment with the original scale’ s dimensional structure, and expert consensus. Two factors were ultimately retained, explaining 82.521% of the total variance. Factor 1 (“Severity and Impact of Breakthrough Pain”) included five items, while Factor 2 (“Onset Characteristics and Medication Effectiveness”) comprised four items. Confirmatory factor analysis

The remaining 200 samples underwent confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to validate the structural model. The model was evaluated without modification, and standardized factor loadings for all items ranged from 0.600 to 0.997. Model fit indices included χ2/df = 1.733, RMSEA = 0.061, CFI = 0.988, TLI = 0.983, IFI = 0.988, and GFI = 0.953, indicating excellent model fit (Fig. 2).

Convergent validity

Composite Reliability (CR) values for the two BAT-C dimensions were 0.927 and 0.900, respectively, while Average Variance Extracted (AVE) values were 0.724 and 0.648. These findings confirm acceptable convergent validity.

Known-groups validity

Known-groups validity was evaluated by assessing whether BAT-C total scores could differentiate patients across ECOG performance status levels. After controlling for potential confounders (age, cancer type, clinical stage), statistical analysis revealed a significant between-group difference (F = 3.208, p = 0.042), with a small effect size (partial η² = 0.021). The box plot in Fig. 3 illustrates the distribution of total BAT-C scores across ECOG categories.

Internal consistency

The BAT-C demonstrated strong internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.869 for the total scale (95% CI: 0.85–0.89; SE = 0.012). Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the two subscales were 0.937 and 0.861, respectively. McDonald’s ω for the total scale was 0.947, and hierarchical ω (ωh) was 0.675, further supporting scale reliability. Each corrected item-total correlation coefficient surpassed 0.40 (see Table 4), suggesting acceptable item-level consistency.

Split-half reliability

Discussion

Validity and factor structure of the BAT-C

Content validity was evaluated through an expert panel review, with Item-Level Content Validity Index (I-CVI) values ranging from 0.833 to 1.000 and a Scale-Level Content Validity Index Average (S-CVI/Ave) of 0.976, indicating excellent content representativeness and cultural adaptation of the BAT-C. Construct validity evaluation employed exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA). The EFA extracted two common factors, labeled “Severity and Impact of Breakthrough Pain” and “Onset Characteristics and Medication Effectiveness,” aligning with factor structures reported in the French [21] and Korean [20] versions. However, the final item, “How much do the side effects from the breakthrough painkiller bother you?“, loaded onto the first factor in the Dutch [34] and original [19] versions. This inconsistency likely stems from cultural variations, as Chinese context tends to interpret “bother” more as an emotional response to analgesic side effect intensity or frequency, thereby aligning it more closely with the “Onset Characteristics and Medication Effectiveness” dimension.

While some items (e.g., Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q8) demonstrated slight cross-loadings slightly above conventional threshold, their primary loadings were clearly dominant and theoretically consistent with their respective factors, meeting theoretical expectations. Moreover, the interrelated nature of breakthrough pain severity/impact and onset characteristics is clinically relevant in cancer contexts. This phenomenon aligns with findings from other linguistic adaptations [20]. Larger-scale studies may further validate and refine the factor structure.

The CFA results confirmed adequate model fit, (χ²/df = 1.733, RMSEA = 0.061, CFI = 0.988, TLI = 0.983, and GFI = 0.953), providing evidence for the two-factor structure. Convergent validity was confirmed through average variance extracted (AVE) values (0.724 and 0.648) and composite reliability (CR) scores (0.927 and 0.900). Known-groups analysis demonstrated significant BAT-C score differences across ECOG functional groups after controlling for age, cancer type, and clinical stage (p < 0.05), aligning with Portuguese version findings [35]. However, the small effect size (partial η² = 0.021) suggests limited clinical discrimination power, warranting caution when interpreting functional status impacts on breakthrough pain.This pattern parallels Dutch [34] and Korean [20] observations during physical function stratification, indicating that the BAT-C excels in multidimensional symptom assessment rather than functional status differentiation.

Reliability and item performance of the BAT-C

Theoretical and methodological foundations for reliability assessment drew on Tavakol et al. [36] framework for Cronbach’s α, alongside McNeish [37] and Dunn et al. [29] multi-index reliability criteria. Internal consistency and temporal stability of the Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool-Chinese version (BAT-C) were assessed through Cronbach’s α, McDonald’ s ω and the Spearman-Brown corrected coefficient. Analysis revealed Cronbach’s α = 0.869 (95% CI: 0.85–0.89; SE = 0.012), McDonald’ s ω = 0.947, and the Spearman-Brown coefficient = 0.828. All values surpassed recommended thresholds, confirming strong internal consistency and measurement stability.

However, previous research [29] has highlighted that ω values ≥ 0.95 may signal potential item redundancy. To address this, “corrected item-total correlation” and “Cronbach’ s alpha if item deleted” analyses were performed. All items demonstrated corrected item-total correlations above 0.40, surpassing the conventional threshold of 0.30 [30], indicating meaningful contributions to scale internal consistency. Although removing Item 9 slightly increased the α value (from 0.869 to 0.877), the item was retained based on expert consensus due to its conceptual relevance and clinical importance. Additionally, pilot testing revealed no significant ceiling or floor effects, suggesting the BAT-C exhibits appropriate score distribution and discriminatory capacity for varying symptom severity levels.

Significance of the BAT-C

Accurate and comprehensive assessment is critical for effective BTcP management [14]. Condition-specific tools like the BAT-C empower healthcare providers to more precisely identify and evaluate BTcP, thereby facilitating the development of effective, individualized pain management strategies for cancer patients [20].

This study marks the inaugural effort to adapt and validate the Chinese version of the Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT-C), preserving its original two-factor structure and multidimensional symptom assessment framework. The BAT-C demonstrated robust psychometric properties in our sample and exhibited relative user-friendliness and ease of administration, suggesting its preliminary suitability for clinical application in Chinese settings.The BAT-C provides a structured assessment of BTcP across two dimensions: “Severity and Impact of Breakthrough Pain” and “Onset Characteristics and Medication Effectiveness.” This framework equips clinicians and nurses with a reliable, systematic tool to capture patients’ individualized pain experiences, thereby improving the precision and timeliness of cancer pain management. Furthermore, the BAT-C facilitates the development of stratified nursing interventions for BTcP, mitigating risks of both under-treatment and overtreatment. Its clinical implementation could promote a transition toward evidence-based BTcP care models in China, ultimately contributing to enhanced symptom control, improved patient outcomes, and elevated quality of life.

In summary, the adaptation of the BAT-C addresses a critical gap in China’ s breakthrough pain assessment tools and advances the standardization of BTcP evaluation practices.

Conclusion

This study adhered to Beaton’s internationally recognized guidelines for cross-cultural adaptation to translate and culturally adapt the Breakthrough Pain Assessment Tool (BAT) into Chinese. The final BAT-C version was developed and administered to a sample of Chinese patients with cancer-related breakthrough pain (BTcP). Despite noted limitations, the BAT-C demonstrated robust psychometric properties in this population, offering preliminary evidence of its feasibility and potential utility as a specialized BTcP assessment instrument for Chinese cancer patients.

Further validation in larger, geographically diverse multi-center samples is warranted to confirm the BAT-C’ s measurement framework and generalizability. Additionally, future investigations should examine its applicability and stability across populations with varying cultural and educational backgrounds to enhance clinical and research utility.

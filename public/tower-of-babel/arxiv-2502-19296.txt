1.
Introduction

The rationality problem of a cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is one of the most intensely studied problems in algebraic geometry.
A recent approach to this problem, pioneered by Kuznetsov
[
Kuz10
]
, is to study rationality via the derived category
D^{b}(X)
.
More precisely, consider the Kuznetsov component
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
given by the left orthogonal complement to the collection
\langle\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle
in
D^{b}(X)
.
This component
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
is a
K3
category,
meaning it has the same Hochschild homology as the derived category
D^{b}(S)
of a
K3
surface
S
and
its Serre functor is given by a shift by 2.
Kuznetsov conjectured that the cubic fourfold
X
is rational if and only if there is an equivalence
\mathrm{Ku}(X)\simeq D^{b}(S)
for some
K3
surface
S
. This has been verified in all cases where
X
is known to be rational.

Cubic fourfolds with non-trivial automorphisms are a natural testing ground for rationality conjectures.
An automorphism of a cubic fourfold
X
is symplectic if it acts trivially on
H^{3,1}(X)
. If the group of symplectic automorphisms of
X
has order greater than
2
, then there is a
K3
surface
S
such that
\mathrm{Ku}(X)\simeq D^{b}(S)

[
Ouc21
]
. Moreover, if
X
admits a symplectic automorphism of prime order
p\geq 3
, then
X
is indeed rational
[
BGM25
, Cor 1.3]
.
The focus of this paper is to study the Kuznetsov component in the remaining case — the case of a cubic fourfold with a symplectic involution.

Such a cubic fourfold
X
is potentially irrational in that
\mathrm{Ku}(X)\not\simeq D^{b}(S)
for any
K3
surface
S

[
Mar23
, Theorem 1.2]
.
However, the equivariant Kuznetsov component
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
of
G
-linearised objects is a 2-Calabi–Yau category
[
BO23
, Lemma 6.5]
, so it is natural to ask whether
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is equivalent to the derived category of a
K3
surface.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a general cubic fourfold with a symplectic involution
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
and let
G:=\langle\phi\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Then there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)\simeq D^{b}(S),

where
S\subset F(X)
is the
K3
component of the fixed locus of the induced action of
G
on the Fano variety of lines of
X
.

The category
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
can be viewed as the Kuznetsov component of the smooth quotient
\widetilde{X}/G,
where
\widetilde{X}
is the blow up of
X
in the fixed locus of
G
. Although
Theorem

1.1
does not address the rationality problem for the cubic fourfold
X
itself, it sheds light on the question of when the quotient of a Fano variety is rational. Indeed, a key ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is proving the rationality of the quotient
X/G
:

Proposition 1.2

(
Proposition

3.4
)
.

The quotient
\widetilde{X}/G
is isomorphic to
\Bl_{S}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}),
where the
K3
surface
S
is embedded as a complete intersection of a
(3,0)
divisor and a
(1,2)
divisor. In particular,
X/G
is rational.

There are few known examples of 2-Calabi–Yau categories beyond
K3
and abelian surface categories. Thus, it is natural to ask whether
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
gives new examples of 2-Calabi–Yau categories. Although there are few instances in which
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
has been computed, our result, together with results in
[
Hu23
]
and
[
BO22
, §7.4]
for examples of cubic fourfolds with a
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
-action, gives evidence towards a negative answer to this question.

In
[
BO22
]
, Beckmann and Oberdieck study the equivariant derived category of a
K3
surface
S
with
a finite group
G
of symplectic automorphisms.
They consider the action of
G
on a moduli space
M
of semi-stable objects (with respect to an invariant stability condition) on
S
and show that, under certain additional hypotheses, there is a two-dimensional
G^{\vee}
-torsor
S^{\prime}
over the fixed locus
{\rm Fix}_{G}(M)
such that
D^{b}_{G}(S)\simeq D^{b}(S^{\prime})

[
BO22
, Theorem 1.1]
.
By
[
LPZ23
, Theorem 1.1]
, the Fano variety of lines of a cubic fourfold
X
is a moduli space of stable objects on the
K3
category
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
. Thus, given that our case – the case when
|G|=2
– is the only case when
X
does not have an associated
K3
surface
S
such that
{\rm Ku}(X)\simeq D^{b}(S)
, one can view
Theorem

1.1
as an extension of Beckmann–Oberdieck’s result to the non-commutative setting.

Remark 1.3
.

The
K3
surface
S
is equipped with an anti-symplectic involution. The induced action on
D^{b}(S)\cong\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
can be shown to commute with the residual action of the dual
G^{\vee}
and hence lifts to an autoequivalence of
\mathrm{Ku}(X)

[
Ela15
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
BO23
, Proposition 3.5]
). This autoequivalence may be of independent interest, since it is of geometric origin but not induced by an automorphism of the cubic
X
.

Method of proof

We prove
Theorem

1.1
directly by constructing an equivalence of categories from
D^{b}(S)
to
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
.
We do this by exploiting the geometric situation: the
K3
surface
S
naturally parametrises lines that are invariant under the involution
\phi
.
This allows us to produce a Fourier-Mukai kernel in
D^{b}(S\times X)
, defining a functor
\Phi
from
D^{b}(S)
to
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
.
Equipping
S\times X
with the diagonal
G
-action acting trivially on the first factor, and considering
D^{b}(S)
as a
G
-category with trivial
G
-action, we prove that
\Phi
is a
G
-functor, and hence factors through
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X).

We use the criteria of Bondal and Orlov
[
Huy06
, Proposition 7.1]
to prove the resulting functor
\Phi_{G}\colon D^{b}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is fully faithful. In order to establish that
\Phi_{G}
is an equivalence, it is then enough to show that
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is indecomposable. Since
K3
categories are indecomposable, we in fact show that
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a
K3
category.

Since
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
appears as an admissible subcategory of the equivariant category
D^{b}_{G}(X)

[
Ela11
, Theorem 6.3]
, we prove that
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a
K3
category by studying
D^{b}_{G}(X)
. One classical approach to understanding
D^{b}_{G}(X)
is via the Mckay correspondence
[
BKR01
]
, which describes
D^{b}_{G}(X)
by constructing a crepant resolution of the quotient
X/G
. This strategy was used recently in
[
Hu23
]
for cubic fourfolds with a particular symplectic
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
-action and in
[
CMHL+24
]
for cubic threefolds with a particular involution. However, this approach requires that the fixed locus of
G
be equidimensional, which is not the case in our setting— in our case the fixed locus of
G
is the union of a surface and a line.

Thus instead, we extend the action of
G
to the blow up
\widetilde{X}:=\Bl_{\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(X)}X
. We then compute two semi-orthogonal decompositions of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
. The first comes from using Orlov’s blow up formula to compute
D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
and taking its
G
-equivariant category. This semi-orthogonal decomposition realizes
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
as an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
. The second semi-orthogonal decompositions of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
comes from viewing the quotient stack
[\widetilde{X}/G]
as a square root stack and using the description of a semi-orthogonal decomposition of root stacks from
[
BD24
, Theorem 1.2]
. This allows us to conclude that
D^{b}(\widetilde{X}/G)
is an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
, which in turn by
Proposition

1.2
realizes
D^{b}(S)
as an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
. The fact that
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a
K3
category then follows by comparing the Hochschild homology of both decompositions.

Outline

In
Section

2
, we recall necessary preliminaries on derived categories and equivariant categories. In
Section

, we prove that the equivariant Kuznetsov component for a cubic fourfold with a symplectic involution is a

Section

4

Theorem

1.1
by explicitly constructing the necessary equivalence.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the necessary results on derived categories. We keep the exposition to the minimum needed for our purposes, providing references for the more general statements. In
Section

2.1
we introduce Calabi–Yau and
K3
categories, and recall results on Hochschild homology. In
Section

2.2
we discuss
G
-equivariant categories, specialising to when the group
G
is induced by an automorphism of a variety
X
acting on either
D^{b}(X)
or on an admissible subcategory. In
Section

2.3
, we recall the definition of a root stack and state a result on a semi-orthogonal decomposition for the derived category of a root stack.

2.1.
Calabi–Yau categories and Hochschild homology

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic 0. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is an
n
-Calabi–Yau
category if both of the following hold:

•

\mathcal{D}
is an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}(X)
for some quasi-projective variety
X
;

•

the Serre functor is given by shift by
n
, i.e.
S_{\mathcal{D}}=[n]
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be either
D^{b}(X)
for some smooth projective variety or an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}(X)
. We recall some results on Hochschild (co)homology of such a category – the main reference is
[
Kuz09
]
, although the results in
[
MS19
]
are sufficient for our purposes.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
MS19
, Prop 2.25]

Let
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots\mathcal{A}_{r}\rangle
be a semi-orthogonal decomposition. Then for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\HH_{i}(\mathcal{D})\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\HH_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{j}).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
MS19
, Example 2.23]

Let
E\in\mathcal{D}
be an exceptional object. Then
\HH_{\bullet}(\langle E\rangle)=k
and is concentrated in degree 0.

For Calabi–Yau categories, the Hochschild homology and cohomology coincide, up to a shift:

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is an
n
-Calabi–Yau category, then for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\HH^{i}(\mathcal{D})\cong\HH_{i-n}(\mathcal{D}).

One can use Hochschild cohomology to define the notion of connectedness for a category
\mathcal{D}
. A triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
that is an admissible subcategory of
D^{b}(X)
for some quasiprojective variety
X
is called
connected
if
\HH^{0}(\mathcal{D})=k.

Lemma 2.4

(Bridgeland’s trick)
.

[
MS19
, Lemma 2.30]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an n-Calabi–Yau variety. If
\mathcal{D}
is connected, then
\mathcal{D}
is indecomposable.

Finally, we can define
K3
categories: a 2-Calabi–Yau category
\mathcal{D}
is called a
K3
category
if its Hochschild (co)homology coincides with the Hochschild (co)homology of a
K3
surface. In particular, by
Lemma

2.4
, a
K3
category is indecomposable.

A non-trivial example of a
K3
category is the
Kuznetsov component

\mathrm{Ku}(X)
of a cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
, defined as the right orthogonal complement of
\langle\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle
in
D^{b}(X)

[
Kuz10
]
.

2.2.
G
-equivariant categories

We recall some basic definitions of categorical actions and equivariant categories, following
[
BO23
]
. Throughout, let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category, and
G
a finite group.

Definition 2.5
.

[
BO23
, Definition 2.1]

An
action

(\rho,\theta)
of
G
on
\mathcal{D}
consists of

•

an auto-equivalence
\rho_{g}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
for every
g\in G
; and

•

an isomorphism of functors
\theta_{g,h}:\rho_{g}\circ\rho_{h}\rightarrow\rho_{gh}
for every pair
g,h\in G
,

such that the appropriate diagrams induced from the group law of
G
commute.

In this paper,
\mathcal{D}
will always be
D^{b}(X)
for some variety
X
, or
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
where
X
is a cubic fourfold. The group
G
will always be
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and the action on
\mathcal{D}
will either be trivial or induced by an automorphism of
X
. Thus there is only one non-trivial auto-equivalence given by
\phi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, where
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is the generator of
G
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
with generator
\phi^{*}
acting on
\mathcal{D}
as above. Then the
equivariant category

\mathcal{D}_{G}
is defined as follows:

•

Objects are pairs
(E,\varphi)
where
E\in\mathcal{D}
and
\varphi
is an isomorphism (or linearisation)
\varphi\colon E\xrightarrow{\sim}\phi^{*}E
, compatible under composition.

•

A morphism from
(E,\varphi)
to
(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
is a morphism
E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
that commutes with linearisations.

Note that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{G}}((E,\varphi),(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}))=(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E^{\prime}))^{G}.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be either
D^{b}(X)
or
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
, with
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\langle\phi\rangle\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.
Then
E\in\mathcal{D}
being
G
-linearised is equivalent to
E
being invariant under the pullback
\phi^{*}
.

We will need to know how semi-orthogonal decompositions behave under taking the equivariant category.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
Ela11
, Theorem 6.3]

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety, with an action of a finite group
G
. Let
D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
be a semi-orthogonal decomposition preserved by
G
. Then there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of equivariant categories

D^{b}_{G}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1G},\dots\mathcal{A}_{nG}\rangle.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
KP17
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a triangulated category with a trivial action of a finite group
G
. If
\mathcal{A}_{G}
is also triangulated, then there is a completely orthogonal decomposition:

\mathcal{A}_{G}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{G}\otimes V_{0},\dots\mathcal{A}_{G}\otimes V_{n}\rangle,

where
V_{0},\dots V_{n}
are all irreducible representations of the finite group
G
.

Since irreducible representations
V_{i}
are completely determined by their character
\chi_{i}
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{G}\otimes\chi_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{G}\otimes V_{i}
.

In particular, if
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a cubic fourfold, then the derived category has the semi-orthogonal decomposition:

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathrm{Ku}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle.

If further
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X),
then
G
preserves
\mathrm{Ku}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(2).
It follows that
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
inherits an action by
G
. Applying the above results gives a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}_{G}(X)
:

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth cubic fourfold with
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X).
Then
G
acts on
D^{b}(X)
and
\mathrm{Ku}(X)
, and we have:

D^{b}_{G}(X)=\langle\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\chi_{1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\otimes\chi_{1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(2),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\otimes\chi_{1}\rangle,

where
\chi_{1}
is the non-trivial character of
G
.

3.
Hochschild homology of
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)

Throughout, we let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
be a general cubic fourfold over
k
admitting a symplectic involution
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(X).
The geometry of such involutions were studied in
[
Mar23
]
. We let
G:=\langle\phi\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

In this section we prove the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

The equivariant Kuznetsov component
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a
K3
category.

Since the action of
G
on
X
is symplectic, the equivariant Kuznetsov component
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a 2-Calabi–Yau category (see
[
BO23
, Sections 6.3, 6.4]
). Thus the main content of this section is to prove that the Hochschild (co)homology of
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
coincides with that of a
K3
surface.

By
Theorem

2.8
, the equivariant Kuznetsov component
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
appears as an admissible subcategory of the equivariant category
D^{b}_{G}(X)
. Classically, the Mckay correspondence
[
BKR01
]
is used to understand
D^{b}_{G}(X)
– a crepant resolution of the quotient
X/G
is constructed using the
G
-Hilbert scheme. However, this approach only works when the fixed locus is equidimensional, which as we explain in
Section

3.1
, is not the case here. Instead, we will first blow up the fixed locus of
G
and extend the action to
\widetilde{X}:=\Bl_{\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(X)}X
. In
Section

3.2
, we study the geometry of both
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{X}/G
. In
Section

3.3
, we obtain two different semi-orthogonal decompositions of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
. Finally, in
Section

3.4
, we study the Hochschild homology of
D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X})
via these decompositions in order to prove
Proposition

3.1
.

3.1.
The geometry of
X

Up to a linear change of coordinates, we can assume

\phi\colon[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]\mapsto[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},-x_{4},-x_{5}],

and so
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
has equation of the form

\displaystyle g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+

\displaystyle x_{4}^{2}l_{1}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+2x_{4}x_{5}l_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+x_{5}^{2}l_{3}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=0,

where
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\in k[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
are linear forms and
g\in k[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
is a homogeneous cubic polynomial. We will also use that
X
is of the equivalent form

(3.2)

g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+x_{0}q_{0}(x_{4},x_{5})+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{4},x_{5})+x_{2}q_{2}(x_{4},x_{5})+x_{3}q_{3}(x_{4},x_{5})=0,

where
q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in k[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
are homogeneous quadratic polynomials.

As an action on
\mathbb{P}^{5}
, the involution
\phi
has fixed locus given by the following line and projective
3
-plane:

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=V(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\subset\mathbb{P}^{5},

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle:=V(x_{4},x_{5})\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}.

The fixed locus of the action of
G
on
X
is then given by
\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(X)=L\cup\Sigma,
where
\Sigma=\Pi\cap X.
Note that, in equations, we have
\Sigma:=V(g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}))\subset\Pi
, so
\Sigma
is a cubic surface.

The line

3.4.

Finally, we are able to prove

is a

Proof of

3.1
.

Since

is a 2-Calabi–Yau category, it suffices to prove that

has the same Hochschild homology as

, where

We will use the two semi-orthogonal decompositions of

from Section

to compute

First, using

3.8

\HH_{i}(D^{b}_{G}(\widetilde{X}))=\begin{cases}\HH_{-2}(D^{b}(S))=k,&i=-2\\
\HH_{0}(D^{b}(S))\oplus k^{32},&i=0\\
\HH_{2}(D^{b}(S))=k,&i=2.\end{cases}

Indeed, this follows since Hochschild homology is additive (

2.1
) and since both

and

are projective bundles over

, respectively.
Both

Comparing homology then yields that

4.
The equivalence

In this section, we prove the the equivalence of

1.1
:

\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)\simeq D^{b}(S),

where
S\subset F(X)
is the
K3
component of the fixed locus of

acting on

.

First, we define a functor
\Phi\colon D^{b}(S)\rightarrow D^{b}(X),
by specifying a Fourier-Mukai kernel in
D^{b}(S\times X).

Recall from
Section

3.1
that a point
s\in S
determines a line
\ell_{s}\subset X
that is invariant under the involution
\phi
.
Hence each
s\in S
determines a sheaf
I_{\ell_{s}}
, the ideal sheaf of the line
\ell_{s}
. The sheaf
I_{\ell_{s}}(1)
sits in the exact sequence:

(4.1)

0\rightarrow F_{\ell_{s}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 4}\rightarrow I_{\ell_{s}}(1)\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 4}=H^{0}(X,I_{\ell_{s}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow I_{\ell_{s}}(1)
is the evaluation map, and

is the kernel.

Lemma 4.2
.

The sheaf
F_{\ell_{s}}
is a reflexive sheaf of rank 3 on
X
, invariant under the action of
G
(i.e.
F_{\ell_{s}}\cong\phi^{*}F_{\ell_{s}})
, and
F_{\ell_{s}}\in\mathrm{Ku}(X).
Further, for
s\neq t
we have
F_{\ell_{s}}\not\cong F_{\ell_{t}}.

Proof.

The line
\ell_{s}\subset X
is invariant under the action of
G
, and hence
I_{\ell_{s}}\cong\phi^{*}I_{\ell_{s}}
. Since
\phi
is a finite map, the pullback
\phi^{*}
is exact and it follows that
F_{\ell_{s}}\cong\phi^{*}F_{\ell_{s}}
. The remaining claims follows from combining
[
KM09
, Lemma 5.1 and Proposition 5.2]
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the restriction of the ideal sheaf of the universal line on
F(X)\times X
to
S\times X
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be the universal sheaf on
S\times X
given as the kernel of
\mathcal{O}_{S\times X}^{\oplus 4}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}(1).

Thus
\mathcal{F}|_{\{s\}\times X}\cong F_{\ell_{s}}
for all
s\in S.

We define the functor
\Phi\colon D^{b}(S)\rightarrow D^{b}(X)
to be the Fourier-Mukai transform with kernel
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}(S\times X).

Proposition 4.3
.

The functor
\Phi
takes values in
\mathrm{Ku}(X).
Moreover, if we equip
D^{b}(S)
with a trivial
G
-action, the functor
\Phi
is a
G
-functor.

Proof.

Let
\Phi^{L}
be the left adjoint to
\Phi
, which exists by
[
Huy06
, Proposition 5.9]
.
Note that
\Phi(\mathcal{O}_{s})=F_{\ell_{s}}\in\mathrm{Ku}(X)
, by
Lemma

4.2
.
Hence we see that

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Phi^{L}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(j)),\mathcal{O}_{s})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(j),F_{\ell_{s}})=0

for
-1\leq j\leq 1
and all
i
.
This means
\Phi^{L}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(j))=0
for
-1\leq j\leq 1
, and so the image of
\Phi
lies in
\mathrm{Ku}(X).

To show that
\Phi
is a
G
-functor, since
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
it is enough to show that there is a natural isomorphism
\Phi\xrightarrow{\sim}\phi^{*}\Phi
, which will follow from showing that the kernel
\mathcal{F}
is invariant under the diagonal action of
G
on
S\times X
.
We claim that the natural morphism
\phi^{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
given by the pullback is an isomorphism.
Considering that
\mathcal{F}|_{\{s\}\times X}\cong F_{\ell_{s}}
for all
s\in S
, we have the following commutative diagram for any
(s,x)\in S\times X
:

\textstyle{\mathcal{F}_{(s,\phi(x))}=(\phi^{*}\mathcal{F})_{(s,x)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\mathcal{F}_{(s,x)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{(F_{\ell_{s}})_{\phi(x)}=(\phi^{*}F_{\ell_{s}})_{x}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{(F_{\ell_{s}})_{x}}

where the bottom arrow in the diagram is also an isomorphism by
Lemma

4.2
.
∎

By
[
BO23
, Prop. 3.1]
and the fact that
\Phi
is a
G
-functor, there exists a unique functor
\Phi_{G}\colon D^{b}(S)\to\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
through which
\Phi

\textstyle{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D^{b}(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{G}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Ku}(X),}

where the map
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ku}(X)
forgets the
G
-linearisation.
We will show that
\Phi_{G}
is an equivalence of categories, thereby proving
Theorem

1.1
.

We wish to use the criteria of Bondal and Orlov
[
Huy06
, Proposition 7.1]
to prove that
\Phi_{G}
is fully faithful. In order to do so, we need to analyze some extension groups. Note that for
s,t\in S
, we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})\cong(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}(X)}^{i}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}}))^{G},
where
G
acts on
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}(X)}^{i}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})
as in
[
BO23
, Section 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
s\in S
, we have

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=0,2\\
2&\text{if }i=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

First, let
\ell\subset X
be any line (not necessarily parametrised by
S
). The following dimensions were computed in
[
KM09
, Section 5]
:

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathrm{Ku}(X)}(F_{\ell},F_{\ell})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=0,2\\
4&\text{if }i=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now we restrict to lines
\ell_{s}\subset X
for
s\in S
. By
Lemma

4.2
, the sheaf
F_{\ell_{s}}\cong\phi^{*}F_{\ell_{s}}
and is naturally
G
-linearised, defining an object in
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X).
Note that any

is automatically

-invariant, hence
\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=1.
Since
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a 2-Calabi–Yau category, we also have
\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=1.

It remains to compute
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Ku}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}}))^{G}.
Let
F^{\prime}(X)
be the moduli space of stable sheaves on

containing the sheaves
F_{\ell}
for any
\ell\subset X
. In
[
KM09

, the authors prove that the map

sending

is an isomorphism of
F(X)
on a connected component of
F^{\prime}(X)
. We identify

with its image. By

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(F_{\ell},F_{\ell})

at the point

. The group

acts on

, with a 2-dimensional smooth fixed locus

, and the image of

is exactly the sheaves

. Since the tangent space of the fixed locus at a point is isomorphic to the

, i.e.

, we see that

Proposition 4.5
.

The functor
\Phi_{G}\colon D^{b}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is fully faithful.

Proof.

We will use the criteria of Bondal and Orlov
[
Huy06
, Proposition 7.1]
. In particular, we need to show:

\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}^{i}(\Phi_{G}(\mathcal{O}_{s}),\Phi_{G}(\mathcal{O}_{t}))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }s=t,i=0\\
0&\text{if }s\neq t,\text{ or }i<0\text{ or }i>2.\end{cases}

This is equivalent to showing:

\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}^{i}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }s=t,i=0\\
0&\text{if }s\neq t,\text{ or }i<0\text{ or }i>2.\end{cases}

The fact that
\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}^{0}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=1
follows from
Lemma

4.4
, and the fact that
\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}^{i}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})=0
for
i<0
or
i>2
follows because
\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)
is a
2
-Calabi–Yau category.

Now let
s,t\in S
with
s\neq t
. Since
F_{\ell_{s}}\in\mathrm{Ku}(X),
we have that
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X},F_{\ell_{s}})=0
for all
s\in S.
We consider the long exact sequence obtained by applying
\mathrm{Hom}(-,F_{\ell_{t}})
to the sequence (
4.1
): since
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X},F_{\ell_{t}})^{\oplus 4}
surjects onto
\mathrm{Hom}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})
, we see that the latter is also zero. Applying Serre duality gives also
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})=0.

Thus taking
G

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})
for

.

It remains to show that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})=0.
For this, we compute the Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})
, which is deformation invariant, hence equal to
\chi_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})
. By

,
\chi_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{s}})=1-2+1=0
, which implies the desired vanishing of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Ku}_{G}(X)}(F_{\ell_{s}},F_{\ell_{t}})
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The functor
\Phi_{G}
is an equivalence of categories. This follows immediately by Propositions

and

, noting that a fully faithful Fourier-Mukai functor between two

).
∎

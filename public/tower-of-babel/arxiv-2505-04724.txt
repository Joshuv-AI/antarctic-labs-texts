Split distributions on grassmann manifolds and smooth quadric hypersurfaces
Alana Cavalcante, Fernando Lourenço

Abstract
. This work is dedicated to studying holomorphic distributions on Grassmann manifolds and smooth quadric hypersurfaces. In special, we prove, under certain conditions, when the tangent and conormal sheaves of a distribution splits as a sum of line bundles on these manifolds, generalizing the previous works on Fano threefolds and ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} . We analyze how the algebro-geometric properties of the singular set of singular holomorphic distributions relate to their associated sheaves.

In this work, we are interested in analyzing algebro-geometric properties of the tangent and conormal sheaves

2.2.
Holomorphic Distributions

Let us now recall facts about holomorphic distributions on complex projective varieties.
For more details about distributions and foliations see

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex manifold.
A
codimension
k
distribution

\mathscr{F}
on
X
is given by an exact sequence

(1)

\mathscr{F}:\ 0\to T_{\mathscr{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}TX\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}N_{\mathscr{F}}\to 0,

where
T_{\mathscr{F}}
is a coherent sheaf of rank
r_{\mathscr{F}}:=\dim(X)-k
, and
N_{\mathscr{F}}:=TX/\phi(T_{\mathscr{F}})
is a torsion free sheaf.

The sheaves
T_{\mathscr{F}}
and
N_{\mathscr{F}}
are called the
tangent
and the
normal
sheaves of
\mathscr{F}
, respectively.
The
singular set
of the distribution
\mathscr{F}
is defined by

\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})=\{x\in X|(N_{\mathscr{F}})_{x}\;\mbox{is not a free}\;\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\mbox{-}\mbox{module}\}.

The
conormal
sheaf of
\mathscr{F}
is
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
.

A distribution
\mathscr{F}
is said to be
locally free
if
T_{\mathscr{F}}
is a locally free sheaf. By definition,
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr{F})
is the singular set of the sheaf
N_{\mathscr{F}}
. It is a closed
analytic subvariety of
X
of codimension at least two, since by definition
N_{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion free.

Definition 2.2
.

A
foliation
is an integrable distribution, that is a distribution

\mathscr{F}:\ 0\to T_{\mathscr{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}TX\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}N_{\mathscr{F}}\to 0

whose tangent sheaf is closed under the Lie bracket of vector fields, i.e.
[\phi(T_{\mathscr{F}}),\phi(T_{\mathscr{F}})]\subset\phi(T_{\mathscr{F}})
.

Clearly, every distribution of codimension
\dim(X)-1
is integrable. From now on, we will consider codimension one distributions on projective variety
X
with Picard number one. Thus, sequence (
1
) simplifies to

(2)

\mathscr{F}:\ 0\to T_{\mathscr{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}TX\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}\mathcal{I}_{Z/X}(r)\to 0,

where
T_{\mathscr{F}}
is a rank
n-1
reflexive sheaf and
r
is an integer number such that
r=c_{1}(TX)-c_{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}})
. Observe that
N_{\mathscr{F}}=\mathcal{I}_{Z/X}(r)
where
Z
is the singular scheme of
\mathscr{F}
; indeed,
N_{\mathscr{F}}
is by definition a torsion free sheaf, which in this case will have rank one, hence an ideal sheaf, which defines the singular locus of the distribution.

A codimension one distribution on
X
can also be represented by a
section
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(r))
, given by the dual of the morphism
\pi:TX\to N_{\mathscr{F}}
. On the other hand, such section induces a sheaf map
\omega:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}(r)
. Taking duals, we get a cosection

\omega^{\ast}:(\Omega^{1}_{X}(r))^{\ast}=TX(-r)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

whose image is the ideal sheaf
I_{Z/X}
of the singular scheme. The kernel of
\omega^{\ast}
is the tangent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
of the distribution twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-r)
.

Remark 2.3
.

From this point of view, the integrability condition is equivalent to
\omega\wedge d\omega=0.

Let
\mathcal{U}

For more details, see

,

The quotient sheaf is the structure sheaf of a subscheme

of pure dimension 1.

Definition 2.4

If

2.3.
The Eagon-Northcott complex

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}

\eta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

Definition 2.6
.

The degeneracy scheme

of the map

is the zero scheme of the associated global section
\omega_{\eta}\in H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{b}\mathcal{A}^{\vee}\otimes\det(B)).

Suppose that

has pure codimension equal to

admits a special resolution, called the Eagon-Northcott complex

0\to\bigwedge^{a}\mathcal{A}\otimes S_{a-b}(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\otimes\det(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\to\bigwedge^{a-1}\mathcal{A}\otimes S_{a-b-1}(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\otimes\det(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\to\cdots

\cdots\to\bigwedge^{b+1}\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\vee}\otimes\det(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\to\bigwedge^{b}\mathcal{A}\otimes\det(\mathcal{B}^{\vee})\to\mathcal{I}_{Z}\to 0.

2.4.
aCM and aB schemes

A closed subscheme
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (aCM)
if its
homogeneous coordinate ring
S(Y)=k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/I(Y)
is a Cohen-Macaulay
ring.

Equivalently,
Y
is aCM if
H_{\ast}^{p}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=0
for
1\leq p\leq\dim(Y)-1

and
H_{\ast}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Y})=0
(cf.
[
3
]
).
From the long exact sequence of cohomology associated to the short exact sequence

(5)

0\to{\mathcal{I}}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to 0

one also deduces that
Y
is aCM if and only if
H_{*}^{p}({\mathcal{I}}_{Y})=0
for
1\leq p\leq\dim\,(Y)
.

Similarly, a closed subscheme is
arithmetically
Buchsbaum (aB)
if its homogeneous coordinate ring is a Buchsbaum ring (see
[
20
]
).
Clearly, every aCM scheme is arithmetically Buchsbaum, but the
converse is not true: the union of two disjoint lines is
arithmetically Buchsbaum, but not aCM.

3.
Grassmann Manifolds

In this Section,

Note that, in the particular case

The Grassmann variety of

By the duality,

On

has rank

It is well known that

are globally generated, see for instance

]

Theorem 3.1
.

[
19
]
[Bott’s formula for Grassmannian]
Let

be the Grassmann manifold and let

Assume

Then,
H^{p}(G,\Omega^{q}(t))=0
if any of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

(k+1)p\geq kq>0;

(2)

(3)

if

(4)

and

In particular,
H^{p}(G,\Omega^{q}(t))=0,
if

Let

If

So, looking at the last sequence, since

On quadrics there is a notable class of vector bundles, which is the natural generalization of the universal bundle that called spinor bundles. Now, we recall the definition and some properties of spinor bundles on

in different ways depending on parity. For more details see
[

In the odd dimension, i.e.

in the Grassmannian of

[
16
, p. 304]

by the following pullback

s^{\ast}U=S,

where

is the universal bundle of the Grassmannian.

In the even case dimension, i.e.,
Q_{2k}(n=2k)
we have two embeddings as follows

s^{{}^{\prime}}=Q_{2k}\to Gr(2^{k-1}-1,2^{k}-1);\ \ \ \ \ \ s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=Q_{2k}\to Gr(2^{k-1}-1,2^{k}-1).

Thus, we define the spinor bundles on
Q_{2k}
of rank
2^{k-1}
in the same way

S^{{}^{\prime}}=s^{{}^{\prime}\ast}U;\ \ \ \ \ S^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=s^{{}^{\prime\prime}\ast}U.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
The spinor bundles on
Q_{n}
are stable.

A property relevant of the spinor bundles on quadrics

a spinor bundle on

H^{i}(Q_{n},S)=0,\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 0<i<n.

that vector bundles on

without intermediate cohomology split as a direct sum of line bundles. This concept leaves us a more general definition:

Definition 4.2

A vector bundle

on a projective variety

is called arithmetically Cohen Macaulay (aCM) if

H^{i}(X,E)=0,\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 0<i<\dim X.

[

of line bundles and twisted by spinor bundles.

. If
Z
is arithmetically Buchsbaum with
h^{1}(Q_{n},\mathcal{I}_{Z}(r))=1
being the only nonzero intermediate cohomology for

H^{i}(Q_{n},\mathcal{I}_{Z})
and
h^{n-1}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n+1))=h^{n}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n))=0,
then
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
is
(-r)
-regular in the sense Castelnuovo-Mumford.

Proof:
Considering the sequence (
15
), where
t=-r-i,
and taking cohomology, we get

\cdots\to H^{i-1}(Q_{n},\mathcal{I}_{Z}(-i))\to H^{i}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-i))\to H^{i}(Q_{n},\Omega^{1}_{Q_{n}}(-r-i))\to\cdots

The term on the left vanishes for
i>2,
since
Z
is aB and when
i=1
this term vanishes using the sequence (
5
) and Bott’s formula.
On the other hand, applying the Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
, the term on the right vanishes for all
1\leq i\leq n-2.
As by hypothesis
h^{n-1}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n+1))=h^{n}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n))=0,
we conclude that
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
is
(-r)
-regular.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be an one-dimensional holomorphic distribution on
Q_{n}
. If
Z
is arithmetically Buchbaum with
h^{1}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r))=1
being the only nonzero intermediate cohomology for
H^{i}(Q_{n},\mathcal{I}_{Z})
, and
h^{2}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=h^{n-1}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n+1))=h^{n-1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-n+2))=h^{n}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-r-n))=0,
then
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
is aCM.

Proof.

In fact, as the above result, given a one-dimensional distribution
\mathscr{F}
we can consider the long exact sequence of cohomology

\begin{array}[]{llllll}\cdots&\to H^{i-1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(t))\to H^{i-1}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))\to H^{i-1}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r+t))\to\\
\\
&\to H^{i}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(t))\to H^{i}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))\to H^{i}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r+t))\to\cdots\end{array}

By Theorem
4.6
,
H^{1}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))=0
for all
t\neq 0
and

H^{0}(Q_{n},\mathcal{I}_{Z}(r+t))=0
for
t<-r,
since
H^{0}(Q_{n},\mathcal{O}_{Q_{n}}(r+t))=0.

By the Lemma
4.17
and Theorem
2.8
,
H^{1}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(t))=0
for
t\geq-r.
Then,
H^{1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(t))=0
for all
t\neq 0
.

For
t=0
, using the hypothesis
h^{2}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0
and Serre duality for to obtain the isomorphism
H^{0}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r))\simeq H^{n}(Q_{n},\mathcal{O}_{Q_{n}}(-r-n))=0
for
r\neq 0,
we have the short exact sequence,

0\to H^{1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})\to H^{1}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1})\to H^{1}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r))\to 0.

Since
H^{1}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r))\simeq\mathbb{C}
and
H^{1}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1})\simeq\mathbb{C},
we conclude
h^{1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0.
Then,
H^{1}_{\ast}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0.

Now, note that
H^{1}(Q_{n},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r+t))=0
for
t\neq 0
and by Theorem
4.6
,
H^{2}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. So, together with the hypothesis
h^{2}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0
, we have
H^{2}_{\ast}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0.

Next, noting that
H^{i-1}({\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r+t))=0
for
i=3,\ldots,n-2,
and for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and by Theorem
4.6

H^{i}(\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))=0,
for
i=3,\ldots,n-2,
and for all
t\in\mathbb{Z},
it follows that
H^{i}_{\ast}(N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0,
for
i=3,\ldots,n-2
.

To finish,
H^{n-2}({Q_{n},\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(r+t))=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and by Theorem
4.5
,
H^{n-1}(Q_{n},\Omega_{Q_{n}}^{1}(t))=0,
for
t\neq-n+2.
Since,
h^{n-1}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}(-n+2))=0,
we conclude that
H^{n-1}_{\ast}(Q_{n},N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=0.
Therefore,
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
is aCM.

∎

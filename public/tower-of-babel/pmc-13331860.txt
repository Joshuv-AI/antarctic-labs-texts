PCLPred: identifying plant chloride transport-related proteins using reduced amino acid alphabets and N-peptide composition
Authors: Ge Bai, Lijia Liu, Hui Zhang, Ruimei Geng, Yong Li, Dahai Yang, Mingliang Fei, Tao Pang, Hao Wang, Aiguo Yang, He Xie, Hao Eang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chloride transport-related proteins play critical roles in coordinating ion cycling, maintaining cellular homeostasis, and enabling plants to dynamically adapt to environmental changes, particularly under salt stress conditions. Given the rapid accumulation of protein sequence data, the experimental identification of proteins is time-consuming and expensive. Therefore, efficient computational methods are urgently needed as a practical supplement to experimental research. Here, we present PCLPred, an SVM-based predictor that integrates reduced amino acid alphabets with N-peptide composition to represent protein sequences. We systematically evaluated 673 reduction schemes and selected an optimal encoding strategy for model training. PCLPred demonstrated superior performance compared to baseline models, achieving an overall accuracy of 95.10% and an AUC of 0.981 in nested cross-validation. Altogether, PCLPred provides an efficient and reliable tool for high-throughput screening of candidate plant chloride transport-related proteins, facilitating functional annotation and experimental validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00726-026-03523-3.

Introduction

In plants, chloride ion transport is involved in multiple physiological processes, including the regulation of cell turgor, stomatal movement, nutrient transport, and osmo-protection (Joshi et al. 2022, Barbier-Brygoo et al. 2000). As a major inorganic anion in plant tissues, chloride is essential for ionic homeostasis, osmoregulation, and environmental stress responses (Silva-Herrera et al. 2025, Blatt 2024). These functions are primarily mediated by chloride channels and transporters, which facilitate the transmembrane movement of this ion (Herdean et al. 2016, Hodin et al. 2023). Experimental identification of chloride transport-related proteins is time-consuming and costly, and the rapid growth of sequence data has made experimental approaches alone inadequate for high-throughput annotation. Therefore, there is an urgent need for efficient computational methods to complement existing experimental strategies.

In recent years, with the large-scale generation of protein sequences, numerous machine learning-based methods have been developed for protein function prediction and have shown excellent performance (Dong et al. 2021, Ma et al. 2025). For instance, DeepGOPlus combines convolutional neural networks with sequence similarity information for large-scale protein function prediction (Kulmanov and Hoehndorf 2020). Yuan et al. developed SPROF-GO for protein function prediction, which showed strong performance across multiple GO categories (Yuan et al. 2023). Yu et al. developed CLEAN for enzyme function annotation, demonstrating better accuracy, reliability, and sensitivity than BLASTp, especially for understudied or multifunctional enzymes (Yu et al. 2023). Dao et al. (2023) introduced AcrPred for Anti-CRISPR protein prediction using a two-step model fusion strategy, achieving an AUC of 0.9212. Despite these advances, effective feature representation remains a key determinant of model performance. The reduced amino acid cluster (RAAC) strategy has been shown to reduce sequence noise and redundancy while enhancing informative representation, improving prediction accuracy in protein classification tasks (Zheng et al. 2021), with demonstrated benefits in enzyme discrimination (Wang et al. 2021), and conotoxin identification (Sun et al. 2020). However, there have been no reports of machine learning models specifically developed for the prediction of plant chloride transport-related proteins.

In this study, we developed a machine learning model, PCLPred, based on an amino acid reduction strategy to predict plant chloride transport-related proteins. An overview of the PCLPred construction workflow is presented in Fig. 1. First, a rigorous benchmark dataset was constructed for training and evaluating the predictive performance of the model. Subsequently, we represented sequence features using an amino acid reduction strategy combined with N-peptide composition, which reduces sequence complexity and facilitates the extraction of conserved patterns from noisy signals. Based on the optimal reduction scheme, we employed an incremental feature selection strategy to train the support vector machine model in order to identify the optimal feature subset. The results of nested cross-validation demonstrated that PCLPred could effectively identify plant chloride transport-related proteins, achieving a prediction accuracy of 95.1% and outperforming the baseline models.

Materials and methods

Dataset

The development of reliable predictive models is fundamentally based on the construction of high-quality datasets. In this study, protein sequences were collected from the UniProt database for five representative plant species, including Oryza sativa, Zea mays, Nicotiana tabacum, Arabidopsis thaliana, and Solanum lycopersicum (UniProt 2015, UniProt 2021). Both reviewed and unreviewed entries were considered during the initial retrieval process to improve the coverage of plant chloride transport-related proteins. Positive samples were defined as proteins annotated with chloride transport-related functional descriptions in UniProt. Negative samples were collected from the same five plant species and consisted of proteins without chloride transport-related annotations. Considering the issue of sample balance, 1,151 positive proteins and 1,205 negative proteins were retained for subsequent analysis. To minimize redundancy and reduce homology bias, all candidate sequences were further processed using CD-HIT with a sequence identity threshold of 80%, and highly similar sequences were removed. The final benchmark dataset contained 452 chloride transport-related proteins and 952 non-chloride transport-related proteins, which were randomly divided into a training set (80%) and a test set (20%).The corresponding accession list was provided in Supplementary Table S1-S2.

Support vector machine

SVM is a classical and widely used machine learning algorithm, commonly employed for classification and regression tasks (Wang et al. 2021, Wei et al. 2020, Li et al. 2025). The core idea of SVM is to map input data into a high-dimensional feature space through kernel functions and to identify an optimal separating hyperplane with the maximum margin in that space (Pisner and Schnyer 2020, Noble 2006). To determine the optimal kernel function for SVM, several commonly used kernels, including the linear, polynomial, radial basis function (RBF), and sigmoid kernels, were evaluated on the training set using nested cross-validation. The results showed that the RBF kernel achieved the best predictive performance and was therefore selected for subsequent model construction. The SVM classifier was implemented using the scikit-learn package (version 1.6)23 Tanaka (2023).

Reduced amino acid strategies

The amino acid reduction strategy aims to decrease sequence complexity and redundancy (Zuo et al. 2017, Feng et al. 2013). By doing so, it enables more efficient extraction of conserved motifs from background noise and can improve computational performance compared with representations based on the full amino acid alphabet. Compared with the original protein sequences, amino acid reduction can extract conserved features hidden within noisy signals while minimizing sequence complexity and informational redundancy, thereby improving computational efficiency (Zhang et al. 2020). Using RAACBook, we evaluated the predictive performance of 673 amino acid reduction schemes in our mode (Zheng et al. 2019, Zheng et al. 2022).

N-peptide composition

N-peptide composition can effectively capture interactions among amino acids and reveal more detailed latent patterns within protein sequences (Zuo and Li 2009, Yu et al. 2004). Maximal information coefficient

Maximal Information Coefficient (MIC) is a model-free and effective method for feature selection (Wang et al. 2025, Liang et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2026). By calculating and normalizing mutual information across various grid scales, MIC identifies the maximum value to quantify the association strength between variables, thereby capturing both linear and non-linear relationships for feature screening. The MIC calculation was performed using the feature selection module implemented in the Feature-scML package (Wang et al. 2025).

Model training

To avoid information leakage, all feature selection and model selection procedures were conducted strictly on the training data, and the test set was used only for the final evaluation. The training set was first used to evaluate 673 RAAC schemes, and the optimal amino acid reduction scheme was identified by five-fold cross-validation. Based on the selected RAAC scheme, feature importance was ranked using the MIC, and incremental feature selection (IFS) was subsequently performed to determine the optimal feature subset. After the optimal RAAC scheme and feature subset had been determined, nested cross-validation (five outer folds and three inner folds) was further conducted on the training set for model training and hyperparameter optimization. The same RAAC transformation and feature extraction procedure derived from the training set were then applied to the test set, and the predictive performance of the final model was evaluated on the test set only once.

Performance evaluation

We used five-fold cross-validation to train the model. Three classical classification metrics were employed to evaluate predictive performance, including sensitivity (Sn), specificity (Sp), accuracy (Acc) (Wang et al. 2025, Yang et al. 2021). Results

Performance comparison of amino acid reduction strategies

The RAACBook systematically summarizes 673 amino acid reduction strategies, which are grouped into 74 types, with each type further subdivided into reduction sizes ranging from 2 to 19. Protein sequences were reduced according to RAACBook and represented using N-peptide composition (N = 1, 2 and 3) for feature extraction. Figure 2 illustrated the density distributions of prediction accuracies obtained across 673 amino acid reduction strategies using different N-peptide compositions on the training set. The results indicated that, compared with monopeptide and dipeptide representations, tripeptide composition generally led to a substantial improvement in overall predictive performance, with accuracy distributions shifting toward higher values, reflecting an enhanced capability to capture informative sequence patterns (Fig. 2). We further analyzed model accuracies using different amino acid reduction strategies under tripeptide composition and visualized the 30 best-performing types in a heatmap. We found that type 1 with a reduction size of 15 achieved an accuracy of 94.8% in five-fold cross-validation, outperforming all other reduction strategies (Figs. 3A and B).

Based on the optimal tripeptide composition scheme (type 1, size 15), we further trained SVM models with different kernel functions on the training set to determine the optimal kernel for subsequent analysis. The hyperparameters of each model were optimized using grid search under a nested cross-validation framework. The results of nested cross-validation showed that the radial basis function (RBF) kernel achieved the best predictive performance (Fig. S1).

Feature subset optimization

Although rich feature information may improve predictive performance, high-dimensional feature representations often contain redundant information, which increases computational complexity and resource consumption. Therefore, under the tripeptide composition setting based on the optimal amino acid reduction strategy (type 1, size = 15), we further evaluated the predictive performance of different feature subsets. On the training set, features were ranked according to their MIC scores, and incremental feature selection (IFS) was performed to assess model performance across different feature subsets under nested cross-validation. The results showed that the model achieved the highest accuracy of 95.10% and an AUC of 0.981 when the top 1,862 ranked features were selected, outperforming both the model using all features and the model without the amino acid reduction strategy, while substantially reducing computational time (Figs. 4 and 5A and Figs. S2-S3). Based on this optimal feature subset, we trained the final SVM model and constructed PCLPred for identifying plant chloride transport-related proteins.

Feature analysis

By employing amino acid reduction based on correlations among similarity matrix elements used for sequence alignment, Murphy et al. grouped the 20 natural amino acids into 10 reduced-size alphabets (Table 1) (Murphy et al. 2000). In the reduction scheme with a size of 15, the residues are organized as LVIM, C, A, G, S, T, P, FY, W, E, D, N, Q, KR, and H. In this scheme, aliphatic hydrophobic residues with similar physicochemical properties are clustered into one group (LVIM), aromatic residues with similar substitution tendencies are partially grouped as FY, and the long-chain positively charged residues are represented by KR, whereas the remaining residues are retained as individual symbols. This reduction pattern decreases sequence complexity while preserving major substitution relationships and physicochemical similarities among amino acids, thereby providing an informative and compact feature representation for downstream protein classification tasks.

Performance of PCLPred on test dataset

Evaluating the model using only the training dataset is not rigorous, as it may lead to an overestimation of the model performance. To address this problem, we assessed the real performance of PCLPred on the test dataset. On the test dataset, PCLPred still achieved excellent prediction results. As shown in Fig. 5B, the confusion matrix of PCLPred based on the optimal 1,862-feature subset showed that the model effectively identified plant chloride transport-related proteins, further validating its robustness. In addition, we performed principal component analysis (PCA) on the training dataset based on the optimal 1,862-feature subset. The 3D PCA results clearly distinguished plant chloride transport-related proteins from non-chloride transport-related proteins, further validating the effectiveness of the amino acid reduction combined with the N-peptide composition strategy (Figs. 5 C and D).

Comparison with previous methods

To objectively evaluate the predictive performance of PCLPred, we compared it with several widely used prediction methods, including Bayes net, Random forest, and ANPrAod, using the optimal 1,862-feature subset derived in this study. To ensure a fair comparison, all competing models were re-trained on the same benchmark dataset used in this study and evaluated on the same test set. As shown in Table 2, the test set results demonstrated that PCLPred achieved the best predictive performance, with an accuracy of 92.81%, a sensitivity (Sn) of 94.55%, and a specificity (Sp) of 91.28%, representing improvements of 4.82%, 6.15%, and 6.31%, respectively, over ANPrAod. This performance gain may be attributed to the combination of the SVM classifier and the amino acid reduction strategy, which effectively compressed the feature space, reduced feature redundancy, and retained essential sequence information, thereby improving computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. In addition, SVM, as a binary classifier, was particularly suitable for distinguishing between the two classes in this study.

Discussion

Chloride transport-related proteins contribute to the coordination of ion cycling and the maintenance of cellular homeostasis, enabling plants to dynamically respond to environmental fluctuations. Notably, in halophytes, these transporters are pivotal for achieving salt tolerance and sustaining growth under saline conditions (Moog et al. 2022). The identification of plant chloride transport-related proteins facilitates the systematic elucidation of their regulatory networks and provides important molecular clues for studies of plant stress adaptation and the improvement of crop salt tolerance.

In this study, we developed a machine learning-based prediction model, PCLPred, for identifying plant chloride transport-related proteins. By systematically evaluating 673 amino acid reduction schemes from the RAACBook and combining them with N-peptide composition features, we found that tripeptide composition provided advantages in capturing discriminative sequence information compared with mono- and dipeptide compositions. Among these schemes, the type 1 strategy with a reduction size of 15 achieved the best predictive performance in five-fold cross-validation, indicating that an appropriate amino acid reduction scale can effectively reduce sequence complexity while retaining key information. Feature importance was assessed using MIC, and an IFS strategy was applied to identify an optimal feature subset. The final model achieved high predictive accuracy using only 1862 features, which effectively reduced feature redundancy and the risk of overfitting.

The current study still has some limitations. The performance of PCLPred depends partly on the quality of UniProt annotations used for dataset construction, and potential annotation bias may remain. In addition, the final test evaluation was based on a single random split. Future studies using more rigorously curated datasets and independent external validation will help further improve the robustness of the model. Overall, PCLPred provides an efficient and reliable computational tool for the high-throughput identification of plant chloride transport-related proteins.

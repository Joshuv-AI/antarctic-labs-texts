Comparison of propofol, etomidate and remimazolam on postinduction hypotension in older adults undergoing non-cardiac surgery: protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xiao-Fei Jin, Chen-Yi Shen, Wei Dou, Hong Liu, Fu-Hai Ji, Ke Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPost-induction hypotension (PIH) is a critical concern in elderly surgical patients and is associated with adverse postoperative outcomes. This trial aims to compare the effects of propofol, etomidate and remimazolam on the incidence of PIH in older adults undergoing non-cardiac surgery.Methods and analysisIn this single-centre, triple-arm, randomised controlled trial, 210 patients aged ≥80 years with American Society of Anaesthesiologists physical status I–III undergoing elective non-cardiac surgery will be recruited. All patients will receive general anaesthesia with endotracheal intubation. Patients will be randomised (1:1:1) to receive propofol, remimazolam or etomidate for anaesthesia induction (n=70 per group). The primary outcome is the incidence of PIH (mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mm Hg for 1 min) from induction initiation to surgical skin incision. Secondary outcomes include time to successful induction, time to tracheal intubation, MAP reduction >30% from baseline, vasopressor requirements, bradycardia, injection pain, myoclonus, postoperative delirium, and cardiac, cerebral and renal complications during hospitalisation.Ethics and disseminationThis trial was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Approval No. 2024-380). The results will be peer-reviewed for publication in a scientific journal.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400090800.Keywords: Propofol; Aged, 80 and over; ANAESTHETICS; Blood Pressure; SURGERY

Introduction

Postinduction hypotension (PIH) is a frequent and clinically significant complication in older adults undergoing surgery, particularly those aged ≥80 years.1,4 Propofol is the most widely used induction agent worldwide, offering advantages such as rapid onset, fast systemic clearance, smooth recovery and reduced postoperative nausea and vomiting.5,7 However, anaesthesia induction with propofol is associated with a high rate of PIH (42%–73%) in older adults.3 4 Etomidate preserves sympathetic tone and maintains systemic vascular resistance; recent data showed a lower PIH incidence (18%–64%), but potential adrenal suppression and myoclonus limit its use.8,10 Remimazolam is a novel ultra short-acting benzodiazepine with less cardiovascular depression11; the reported incidence of PIH in older surgical patients ranged 14%–72%.3 4 8 To date, head-to-head comparisons with propofol, etomidate and remimazolam in the very elderly patients are lacking.

Transient hypotension during induction has been associated with postoperative myocardial injury or acute kidney injury in some cohorts,12 13 but not in others.14 15 This inconsistency likely reflects differences in PIH definition, patient age and follow-up duration. We designed this randomised controlled trial to directly compare three anaesthesia induction agents (propofol, etomidate and remimazolam) in patients aged ≥80 years undergoing non-cardiac surgery. The primary outcome is the incidence of PIH from anaesthesia induction to surgical incision. In addition, we will assess postoperative cardiac, cerebral and renal complications with the three anaesthetics.

Methods

Study design

This is a single-centre, randomised, patient-blind and assessor-blind, controlled trial with three parallel groups. The study will be conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University, a tertiary teaching hospital performing approximately 60 000 surgeries annually. Among them, patients aged 80 years or older who undergo elective non-cardiac surgery account for approximately 2%‒3% (1200‒1800 individuals per year). A research nurse who is not involved in anaesthetic care will identify potential participants from the operating list 2‒3 days a week. Based on this, we estimate that 40 older patients will be screened every month. After eligibility confirmation and written informed consent, we estimate that the average recruitment is 20 patients per month and it will take 1 year to complete the recruitment.

Between October 2024 and October 2025, we plan to recruit a total of 210 older adults who are scheduled for non-cardiac surgery. The study follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines (online supplemental file 1).16

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: aged ≥80 years, American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–III, and undergoing elective non-cardiac surgery under general anaesthesia with endotracheal intubation. Exclusion criteria: (1) body mass index ≥35 kg/m2; (2) allergies to study medications; (3) severe cardiopulmonary, liver or renal diseases (eg, requiring dialysis); (4) preoperative systolic blood pressure ≥200 mm Hg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥120 mm Hg; (5) preoperative use of antipsychotics, antidepressants or sedatives or (6) sleep apnoea syndrome or anticipated difficult airway. Randomisation and blinding

An independent researcher will generate a 1:1:1 randomisation using permuted blocks (Sealed Envelope). Allocation concealment will be maintained via opaque envelopes. A research assistant who is unaware of the randomisation process will open the envelopes successively and assign patients to one of three study groups (propofol, etomidate or remimazolam) (figure 1). Due to safety considerations in these high-risk patients, anaesthetists are aware of the drug administered to ensure timely intervention if needed, but they do not know the hypothesis of this study nor are they involved in outcome assessment. Subjects and outcome assessors will remain blinded to group allocation. To blind the assessors, a syringe pump will be covered with an opaque sheet and the intravenous lines will be covered with opaque tape from the injection port to the hub of the catheter. These coverings will be applied after the anaesthetist loads the drug into the syringe but before the induction begins. The unblinded anaesthetist will manipulate the syringe pump within the opaque sheet. This will prevent the assessors from visually identifying the induction drug and ensure the blinding process.

Anaesthesia and interventions

Preinduction volume status will be standardised as follows. (1) Fasting and oral intake: all participants follow the same institutional protocol: clear fluids allowed until 2 hours before surgery and solid food stopped 8 hours prior; (2) Intravenous preloading: immediately after arrival in the preanaesthetic room, an 18-G or 20-G peripheral cannula is inserted and Ringer’s acetate 5 mL/kg is infused over 30 min and (3) Fasting duration, total preinduction fluid volume and baseline haematocrit will be recorded. Baseline blood pressure is the mean of three non-invasive blood pressure readings recorded after arrival in the preanaesthetic room and before any anaesthetic drug is given. In the operating theatre, the ASA standard monitoring will be initiated. Invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring via radial artery cannulation will be applied.

For anaesthesia induction, sufentanil 0.2 µg/kg will be administered over 30 s, followed by a continuous infusion of propofol 1.5 mg/kg/min, etomidate 0.3 mg/kg/min or remimazolam 0.2 mg/kg/min until loss of consciousness (unresponsiveness to voice and loss of the eyelash reflex). These doses were based on previous clinical studies in older adults,4 8 and a continuous infusion was used to allow for immediate titration. Subsequently, rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg will be given for tracheal intubation using a video-laryngoscope. If the first tracheal intubation attempt fails, a second attempt with optimised head position, external laryngeal pressure and a bougie is permitted after mask reoxygenation. If two attempts by the same operator fail, a second experienced anaesthetist takes over; a supraglottic airway or fibreoptic bronchoscopy can be used as indicated.

After tracheal intubation, anaesthesia is commenced with sevoflurane 1% and remifentanil 0.05 µg/kg/min. Every 3 min thereafter, the following adjustment rules are applied: if Bispectral index (BIS) >60, sevoflurane is increased by 0.5% (maximum 3%); if BIS <40, it is decreased by 0.5% (minimum 0.5%); if mean arterial pressure (MAP) rises >20% above baseline, remifentanil is increased by 0.05 µg/kg/min (maximum 0.25); if MAP falls >20% below baseline or heart rate (HR) is <45 beats/min, remifentanil is decreased by 0.05 µg/kg/min (minimum 0.05).

Hypotension (MAP <65 mm Hg) and bradycardia (HR <45 beats/min) will be managed according to a prespecified protocol: ephedrine 4 mg for hypotension with HR <80 beats/min; phenylephrine 50 µg for hypotension with HR ≥80 beats/min; ephedrine 4 mg for bradycardia plus hypotension; atropine 0.5 mg for isolated bradycardia. If hypotension recurs within 1 min of the third bolus, a norepinephrine infusion (0.02–0.5 µg/kg/min) is titrated to maintain MAP ≥65 mm Hg.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome of this trial is the incidence of PIH, defined as MAP <65 mm Hg for at least 1 min, from the start of anaesthetic drug administration to surgical skin incision.4 14

Additional outcomes

Additional and exploratory outcomes include surgery time, anaesthesia time, urinary output, time to emergence, and length of postoperative stay.

Sample size calculation

This is a superiority trial. The sample-size calculation is powered to detect any difference in PIH incidence among the three induction agents, with the null hypothesis that the true incidence is identical for all drugs. First, we extracted our institutional database for older adults undergoing non-cardiac surgery from January to July 2024 and found that anaesthesia induction with propofol was associated with a PIH incidence of 48% (n=218), etomidate of 31% (n=266) and remimazolam of 18% (n=50). Based on these rates with 90% power and α=0.05, at least 184 patients are needed (PASS V.15, NCSS). We also conducted a prospective observation, showing a PIH incidence of 50% with propofol (n=12), 35% with etomidate (n=14), and 20% with remimazolam (n=15); sample size calculation based on these data indicates a required sample size of 192. Thus, we decide to recruit a total of 210 patients (n=70 per group) after accounting for a potential attrition of ~10%.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk test will be used to assess normal distribution of continuous data. Means±SD will be shown if data are normally distributed, while medians with interquartile ranges will be presented for non-normally distributed data. Categorical data will be presented as numbers (percentages). Demographic and baseline data will be analysed using descriptive statistics only.

For the primary outcome of PIH incidence, a χ² test will be applied. After a significant overall χ² test, Bonferroni-corrected pair-wise relative risk with 95% CIs will be calculated: propofol versus etomidate, propofol versus remimazolam and etomidate versus remimazolam, with the significance level of α=0.017 (0.05/3) for each comparison. Subgroup analyses will be conducted according to sex (male or female), age (80–89 years or ≥90 years), ASA physical status (I–II or III) and surgical category (orthopaedic, abdominal, thoracic, urological or other).

In addition, a multivariable logistic regression model will be fitted to estimate the independent effect of each induction agent. In this model, PIH is the outcome, induction drugs (propofol, etomidate, remimazolam) are exposure factors with propofol set as reference, and prespecified covariates include age (as a continuous variable in years), sex (as a binary variable), ASA physical status (as a categorical variable), baseline MAP (as a continuous variable in mm Hg), cardiovascular comorbidity (as a binary variable; any of coronary artery disease, heart failure, atrial fibrillation or valvular disease), induction dose (as a continuous variable in mg), surgical type (as a categorical variable) and interval between induction and surgical incision as covariates (as a continuous variable in minutes). Adjusted pairwise comparisons with Bonferroni correction will be presented.

For the secondary outcomes and other perioperative data, one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey corrections for multiple comparisons, Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s multiple comparison corrections, or χ² test will be used. No adjustment for covariates will be applied.

A modified intention-to-treat (mITT) set including all randomised participants who receive any study drug will be used for the primary analysis. A per-protocol (PP) set excluding patients with significant protocol deviations will be used as a sensitivity analysis. Patients in whom the initial tracheal intubation attempt fails will remain in the mITT analysis but will be excluded from the PP analysis; the number of attempts and induction-to-intubation time will be entered as covariates in the multivariable model to adjust for potential haemodynamic confounding.

We expect that missing data for the primary outcome would be very rare (<1%), because data are recorded electronically by continuous invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring. To handle the missing data, we will perform sensitivity analyses assuming (1) worst-case (all missing=event) and (2) best-case (all missing=no event). For the secondary outcomes, we expect that missing data would be <5%; we will use complete-case analysis and report the extent of missingness. Statistical analysis will be performed using the GraphPad Prism (V.9.0.0, Boston, Massachusetts, USA). A two-sided p<0.05 indicates a statistically significant difference. The statistical analysis plan can be publicly accessible with the publication of this protocol and before publication of final results (online supplemental file 2).

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public are not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this study.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval and registration

This trial was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Approval No. 2024-380) on 10 September 2024, and registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on 14 October 2024 (ChiCTR2400090800; https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=246930) before the first subject recruitment. Written informed consent will be obtained for every participant or legal surrogate (online supplemental file 3).

Data curation and management

Two research nurses who are unaware of allocation will collect data using case report forms. Haemodynamic data, induction and intubation times, vasopressor dose, bradycardia, injection pain and myoclonus will be assessed and documented in the operating theatre. Intraoperative anaesthesia and surgical data will be extracted from the electronic Anaesthesia Information System (DoCare V5.0, MedicalSystem, Suzhou, China). POD will be assessed by ward visits. Postoperative complications will be retrieved from the Electronic Medical Records (HaiTai Medical Information System, Nanjing, China). Data will be entered into an electronic database. Personal identifiers will be stored separately from clinical data. Access is role-based (principal investigator, statistician and data manager only) and protected by password. After the database lock, the final dataset will be archived. An independent institutional Data and Safety Monitoring Board will oversee the study implementation.

Dissemination policy

Findings will be disseminated regardless of the magnitude or direction of effect. The main results will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal within 12 months of primary completion.

Discussion

While previous studies have compared remimazolam to propofol or etomidate to propofol in the risk of PIH in older adults, our study has several strengths: (1) Three-arm comparison: unlike prior studies that compared only two agents, our trial directly compares three induction agents (propofol, etomidate and remimazolam) in the same cohort of older patients. This allows for a comprehensive evaluation; (2) Age-specific focus: our study targets patients aged ≥80 years, a cohort that shows a high risk of PIH and is markedly underrepresented in previous research; by restricting recruitment to this very elderly group, we aim to obtain precise data for the fastest-growing surgical population and address the global need for safer anaesthetic practices in patients with advanced frailty and (3) Comprehensive outcome assessment: in addition to the primary outcome of PIH, we also assess a wide range of secondary outcomes. This comprehensive approach provides a more holistic view of the safety and efficacy of each induction agent.

This study excludes patients with ASA physical status IV for three reasons: (1) these patients often require urgent surgery, have limited physiological reserve and frequently need advanced haemodynamic support; thus, their inclusion would introduce confounding and increase outcome variability; (2) establishing safety and effect estimates in ASA physical status I–III patients first will provide a foundation for subsequent trials in the higher-risk group and (3) recent studies comparing induction agents in older adults also restricted recruitment to ASA physical status I–III.1 4

The single-centre implementation may limit generalisability, and the lack of long-term follow-up precludes assessment of delayed complications. Additionally, unblinded anaesthetists could introduce performance bias, although outcome assessors will remain masked to mitigate detection risk. Further research should explore dose-response relationships, combination therapies (eg, adjunctive vasopressors), and long-term outcomes in older adults undergoing surgery.

In conclusion, this randomised clinical trial will address a pivotal challenge in geriatric anaesthesia. By elucidating the haemodynamic impacts of three induction agents, the results will guide evidence-based drug selection to optimise perioperative safety and outcomes in older surgical patients.

Abstract




Abstract
. This paper investigates
L^{1}
solutions for mean-field backward stochastic differential equations (MFBSDEs) under different weak assumptions in both one-dimensional and
multi-dimensional settings, whose generator
f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
depends not only on the solution process
(Y,Z)
but also on the law of
(Y,Z)
.
In the one-dimensional case where
f
depends on the law of
Y
, we show with the help of a test function method and a localization procedure that such type of equations with an integrable terminal
condition admits an
L^{1}
solution, when the generator
f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
has a one-sided linear growth in
(y,\mu)
, and an iterated-logarithmically sub-linear growth
in
z
. Furthermore, by leveraging the additional extended monotonicity in
y
and an iterated-logarithmically uniform continuity in
z
of the generator

f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
together with a strengthened nondecreasing condition in
\mu
, we derive a comparison theorem for
L^{1}
solutions, which immediately leads
to the uniqueness of the
L^{1}
solutions. Next, we establish the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for multi-dimensional mean-field
BSDEs with integrable parameters in which the generator
f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
depends on
\mu=\mathbb{P}_{Y}
and satisfies a one-sided Osgood condition as well as a general growth condition in
y
, a Lipschitz continuity
as well as a sublinear growth condition in
z
, and a Lipschitz condition in
\mu
. Finally, the solvability of
L^{1}
solutions for general MFBSDEs is studied, where the generator
f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
depends on both the solution process
(Y,Z)
and its joint law
\mathbb{P}_{(Y,Z)}
.

Keywords
. Mean-field BSDEs;
L^{1}
solution; iterated-logarithmically sub-linear generator; comparison theorem; one-sided Osgood condition

1
Introduction

Linear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) were first introduced by Bismut
[
1
]
in 1973 as adjoint equations in the framework of Pontryagin’s stochastic maximum
principle for stochastic control problems. Afterwards, the more general nonlinear BSDEs were introduced by Pardoux and Peng
[
31
]
, for which they proved the
existence and the uniqueness of adapted solutions with Lipschitz generators and square integrable terminal values. Their equation takes the form:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

where the terminal value
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random variable, and the generator (or driver)

f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable
for all
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Since then, the theory of BSDEs has attracted significant interest and found extensive
applications in diverse fields, including nonlinear PDEs, stochastic control, stochastic differential games, and mathematical finance; see, e.g.,
El Karoui, Peng and Quenez
[
11
]
, El Karoui and Hamadène
[
12
]
, Pardoux and Peng
[
32
]
, Peng
[
33
]
, etc.

Undoubtedly, the existence and uniqueness of solutions remains a fundamental issue in the BSDE theory and its applications. Numerous researchers have
extended the pioneering work of Pardoux and Peng
[
31
]
. Several researchers have focused on weakening the Lipschitz assumptions on the generator
f
,
such as Briand, Lelepeltier and San Martin
[
3
]
, Briand and Confortola
[
4
]
, Kobylanski
[
22
]
, Lepeltier and San Martin
[
24
]
,
Mao
[
30
]
, among others. Different researchers on their turn have focused on relaxing the
L^{2}
-integrability requirements associated with the terminal
value
\xi
, e.g., Briand et al.
[
2
]
, Fan
[
13
]
, Fan
[
14
]
, and many others. Since the late 1990s, the investigation of the existence
and the uniqueness of solutions that are bounded, as well as those with certain exponential moment orders, has emerged as a significant area of interest
within the theory of BSDEs. For a more complete state of art, we refer the reader to Briand and Hu
[
5
]
,
Delbaen, Hu and Bao
[
10
]
, Kobylanski
[
22
]
, Lepeltier and San Martin
[
25
]
, Luo and Fan
[
29
]
, where the
generator
f
is of quadratic or super-quadratic growth with respect to
z
. Notably, existing results indicate that one-dimensional BSDEs are often more easy to handle
than their multi-dimensional counterparts, largely due to the availability of tools such as the comparison theorem, the monotone convergence theorem, and the
Girsanov transformation.

On the other hand, in 1997, Peng
[
34
]
introduced the concept of
g
-martingales of a square integrable random variable through the solutions of BSDEs,
which can be interpreted as some type of nonlinear martingales. Since classical martingale theory operates within integrable spaces,
this naturally motivates to solve BSDEs with only integrable data. Such equations with only integrable data are generally more challenging
than those with
L^{p}
-integrable parameters (
p>1
). We refer the reader to Briand et al.
[
2
]
, and Fan
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
, for a
survey on the well-posedness of
L^{1}
solutions for BSDEs. Notably, Briand et al.
[
2
]
were the first to provide general existence
and uniqueness results of
L^{1}
solutions for multi-dimensional BSDEs with a generator
f
satisfying the criteria of monotonicity and
general growth with respect to
y
, as well as Lipschitz and sub-linear growth conditions with respect to
z
. Recently, using a test function
approach and a localization technique, Fan, Hu and Tang
[
17
]
obtained the existence of solutions as well as a comparison theorem for
one-dimensional BSDEs with only integrable terminal values, under the condition that the generator
f
has a one-sided linear growth in
y
and
a logarithmic sub-linear growth in
z
. Furthermore, Fan, Hu and Tang
[
18
]
relaxed the assumption of logarithmic sub-linear growth in
z
,
as initially introduced in their previous work of Fan, Hu and Tang
[
17
]
, to a novel criterion of iterated-logarithmically sub-linear growth with regard to
z
.

Mean-field models serve as a useful tool to describe the asymptotic behavior in large-scale systems. Mean-field stochastic differential equations (MFSDEs),
or McKean-Vlasov equations, which characterize stochastic systems whose evolution is determined by both the microscopic location and the macroscopic
distribution of the particles, were first discussed by Kac
[
21
]
in the middle of the 1950s in the frame of his study of the Boltzmann equation for the particle
density in diluted monatomic gases and in that of the stochastic toy model for the Vlasov kinetic equation of plasma. Over the past two decades, there has been
extensive research on mean-field games and their closely associated mean-field control problems, which were independently
initiated by Lasry and Lions
[
23
]
and Huang, Malhamé and Caines
[
20
]
. Such problems explore the limit dynamics of large-scale systems,
where the agents interact with each other in certain symmetric manner. Inspired by them, Buckdahn, Djehiche,
Li and Peng
[
6
]
studied a class of special mean-field problems using a purely probabilistic methodology. They introduced the so-called mean-field BSDEs. Buckdahn,
Li and Peng
[
7
]
studied the existence and the uniqueness of solutions of mean-field BSDEs with assuming that
the generator
f
satisfies a Lipschitz condition and the terminal value
\xi
is square integrable. Since then, there has been an intensive research
on mean-field SDEs and BSDEs addressing the existence and the uniqueness as well as related PDEs; see, for instance, Buckdahn, Li, Peng and Rainer
[
8
]
,
Chen, Xing and Zhang
[
9
]
, Hao, Hu, Tang and Wen
[
19
]
, Li
[
26
]
, Li, Liang and Zhang
[
27
]
, Li and Xing
[
28
]
, etc.

However, to the best of our knowledge, the well-posedness of mean-field BSDEs with integrable parameters remains relatively unexplored in both one-dimensional and
multi-dimensional settings. Motivated by existing work, we focus on the solvability of
L^{1}
solutions for mean-field BSDEs in both one-dimensional and
multi-dimensional cases, where the generator
f
depends on the solution
(Y,Z)
and the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{Y}
of its
Y
-component. More precisely, the mean-field BSDEs is as follows:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

(1.2)

where the terminal value
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an integrable
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random variable, and the
generator
f:=f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu):\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively
measurable, for all fixed
(y,z,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The main objective of our paper consists in
exploring the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions to the mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) under integrability conditions on the coefficients. We
establish four main results: Firstly, we prove the existence of an
L^{1}
solution for a scalar-valued mean-field BSDE with an integrable terminal value, and
a generator satisfying a one-sided linear growth condition in
(y,\mu)
, as well as an iterated-logarithmically sub-linear growth condition in
z
. The proof relies on a test function method
and a localization technique. Secondly, using similar techniques, we establish the corresponding comparison theorem for
L^{1}
solutions, which immediately yields
the uniqueness of the
Y
-component of the solution as a by-product. Thirdly, we investigate the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for this type of equations
in the multi-dimensional case, where the generator satisfies a one-sided Osgood condition and a general growth in
y
, a Lipschitz continuity as well as a sub-linear
growth in
z
, and a Lipschitz condition in
\mu
. Finally, we investigate the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for a new type of multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with integrable parameters, where the generator satisfies a monotonicity condition and a general growth in
y
, a Lipschitz continuity as well as a sub-linear
growth in
z
, and a Lipschitz condition in
\mu
. The equation is of the form:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{(Y_{s},Z_{s})})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

(1.3)

The main contributions of the present paper are twofold. Our first contribution consists in a non-trivial generalization of the
L^{1}
solution results to the mean-field setting (see the Theorems
3.6
and
3.7
). In particular, we establish the existence of
L^{1}
solutions and
give a new comparison theorem for such solutions in the one-dimensional mean-field framework via the localization method and new technical arguments. Our second
contribution consists of a non-trivial extension of the
L^{1}
solvability results for multi-dimensional BSDEs to the mean-field setting
(see the Theorems
4.5
,
4.6
and
4.9
). With the help of BSDE estimates and Bihari’s inequality, we prove the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for
multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with integrable parameters under general conditions. This proof is very subtle; it involves namely a Picard iterative scheme and
a technique for partitioning the time interval
[0,T]
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we introduce the notations and provide preparations which are necessary for the subsequent discussions.
In Section
3
we prove the existence of
L^{1}
solutions and establish a comparison theorem for one-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with integrable coefficients
by using a test function method and a localization procedure. Finally, in Section
4
we discuss the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for
multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with an integrable terminal condition whose generator satisfies a one-sided Osgood condition or a monotonicity condition.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we let
T\in(0,+\infty)
be a finite time horizon and
n,d\geq 1
be two positive integers. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which is defined a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
a\vee b:=\mathop{\rm max}\{a,b\}
,
a\wedge b:=\mathop{\rm min}\{a,b\}
,
a^{+}:=a\vee 0
and
a^{-}:=-(a\wedge 0)
. We denote by
{\bm{1}}_{A}(\cdot)
the indicator function of a set
A
, and sgn
(x):={\bm{1}}_{\{x>0\}}-{\bm{1}}_{\{x\leq 0\}},\ x\in\mathbb{R}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we denote by
z_{i},\ y_{i}
and
f^{i}
respectively the
i
-th row of the matrix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, the
i
-th component of the vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the generator
f
. The Euclidean norms of a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a matrix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are defined by
|y|
and
|z|
, respectively. Let
\langle x,y\rangle
represent the inner product between vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The notation
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac measure concentrated at the origin.

For every positive integer
m\geq 1
, we recursively define the following functions:

\displaystyle\ln^{(1)}(x):=\ln x,\ x\geq e^{(1)},\ \text{and}\ \ln^{(m)}(x):=\ln^{(m-1)}(\ln^{(1)}(x))=\ln^{(1)}(\ln^{(m-1)}(x)),\ x\geq e^{(m)},

where
e^{(1)}:=e\ \text{and}\ \ e^{(m)}:=e^{e^{(m-1)}}
.

Furthermore, for every positive integer
m\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
, we define the following function:

\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}(x):=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big(\ln^{(i)}(e^{(m)}+x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\ln^{(m)}(e^{(m)}+x)\big)^{\lambda},\ x\geq 0.

Moreover, we suppose that there exists a sub-
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}^{0}\subset\mathcal{F}
, containing all the
\mathbb{P}
-null subsets of
\mathcal{F}
, such that the following statements hold true:

(i) the Brownian motion
W
is independent of
\mathcal{F}^{0}
;

(ii)
\mathcal{F}^{0}
is “rich enough”, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})=\{\mathbb{P}_{\xi},\ \xi\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{k})\},\ k\geq 1
.

Here
\mathbb{P}_{\xi}:=\mathbb{P}\circ[\xi]^{-1}
denotes the law of the random variable
\xi
under the probability
\mathbb{P}
. We denote by
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the filtration generated by
W
and augmented by
\mathcal{F}^{0}
, that is,
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma\{W_{s},\ 0\leq s\leq t\}\vee\mathcal{F}^{0},\ t\in[0,T]
.

The following spaces will be frequently used in what follows: For all
t\in[0,T]
and
p>0
,

\bullet\ L^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ \xi\ \text{is an}\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\text{-measurable r.v. with}\ \|\xi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\xi|^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge 1}<+\infty\big\}
.

\bullet\ L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ \xi\ \text{is an}\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\text{-measurable r.v. with}\ \|\xi\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{\omega\in\Omega}|\xi(\omega)|<+\infty\}
.

\bullet\ \mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\varphi:\Omega\times[t,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ \varphi\ \text{is an}\ \mathbb{F}\text{-adapted}\ \text{continuous process}\ \text{with}\ \|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}_{s\in[t,T]}|\varphi_{s}|^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge 1}<+\infty\big\}
.

\bullet\ \mathcal{S}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\varphi:\Omega\times[t,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ \varphi\ \text{is an}\ \mathbb{F}\text{-adapted}\ \text{continuous process}\ \text{with}\ \|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}:=\mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{(s,\omega)\in[t,T]\times\Omega}|\varphi_{s}(\omega)|<+\infty\}
.

\bullet\ \mathcal{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\psi:\Omega\times[t,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\psi\ \text{is an}\ \mathbb{F}\text{-progressively measurable process with}\ \|\psi\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\int_{t}^{T}|\psi_{s}|ds\big)^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge 1}<+\infty\big\}
.

\bullet\ \mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{\psi:\Omega\times[t,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\psi\ \text{is an}\ \mathbb{F}\text{-progressively measurable process with}\ \|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\int_{t}^{T}|\psi_{s}|^{2}ds\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge 1}<+\infty\big\}
.

It is well-known that for all
p\geq 1
, the
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
are Banach spaces, equipped with the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}
, respectively. And, for all
p\in(0,1)
, the
\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
are complete metric spaces with the resulting distances
(Y,\widetilde{Y})\rightarrow\|Y-\widetilde{Y}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}
and
(Z,\widetilde{Z})\rightarrow\|Z-\widetilde{Z}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}
, respectively. Let us recall that a real-valued continuous process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is said to belong to the class (D), if the set
\{Y_{\tau}:\tau\in\mathscr{T}[0,T]\}
is a family of uniformly integrable random variables, where
\mathscr{T}[0,T]
denotes the collection of all stopping times
\tau
with values in
[0,T]
. Moreover,
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]
denotes the conditional expectation knowing the
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, for
t\in[0,T]
.

For any
p\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
the totality of probability measures
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
with finite
p
-th order moment, namely
\|\mu\|_{p}:=\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|x|^{p}\mu(dx)\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is the Borel
\sigma
-field over
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We endow
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
with the topology induced by the
p
-Wasserstein metric: For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k}),

W_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\mathop{\rm inf}\Big\{\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}}|x-y|^{p}\pi(dxdy)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \pi\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2k})\ \text{with marginals}\ \mu\ \text{and}\ \nu\Big\}.

For our discussion which follows, we introduce the following definitions concerning the solutions of mean-field BSDE (
1.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

A solution of mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) is a pair of
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
(Y,Z)
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
such that
\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds<+\infty
,
\int_{0}^{T}|f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})|ds<+\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., and equation (
1.2
) holds true for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair of processes
(Y,Z)
is said to be an
L^{1}
solution of mean-field BSDE (
1.2
), if
Y
belongs to the class (D),
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
, and equation (
1.2
) holds true for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Hereinafter, we present two technical lemmas which will be frequently used in our subsequent analysis and discussions. Lemma
2.3
provides an upper bound sequence for functions with linear growth; the reader can refer to
[
15
, Lemma 1]
for more details.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
u(\cdot):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a function which grows at most linearly, i.e., there is a constant
A>0
such that
u(x)\leq A(x+1),\ x\geq 0.
Then for all
m\geq 1
, we have
u(x)\leq(m+2A)x+u\Big(\frac{2A}{m+2A}\Big),\ x\geq 0
.

The following lemma can be viewed as a backward version of Bihari’s inequality; the reader can refer to
[
15
, Lemma 2]
for more details.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Bihari’s inequality) Suppose that
v(\cdot):[0,T]\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a nonnegative function which satisfies that there exists a constant
v_{0}\geq 0
and a continuous nondecreasing function
\kappa:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with
\kappa(0)=0,\ \kappa(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\kappa(u)}=+\infty
, such that

v(t)\leq v_{0}+\int_{t}^{T}\kappa(v(s))ds,\ t\in[0,T].

Then we have
v(t)\leq\Pi^{-1}\big(\Pi(v_{0})+T-t\big),\ t\in[0,T],
where
\Pi(x):=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{1}{\kappa(u)}du,\ x>0
, is a continuous strictly increasing function with values in
\mathbb{R}
, and
\Pi^{-1}
represents the inverse function of
\Pi
. Furthermore, provided that
v_{0}=0
, then
v(t)=0
, for all
t\in[0,T]
.

3
One-dimensional case

In this section, we investigate the existence and the uniqueness of the
L^{1}
solution for one-dimensional mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) with only integrable parameters (
n=1
). For this end, let the terminal value
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable and the mapping
f:=f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu):\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, for all fixed
(y,z,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, we make the following assumptions on the generator:

(A1)
For
d\mathbb{P}\times dt
-almost all
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]
,
f(\omega,t,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous, and has a modulus of continuity w.r.t. the law: For all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R},\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \text{and}\ \mu,\ \bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

|f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,z,\bar{\mu})|\leq\rho\big(W_{1}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\big),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

Here
\rho:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is supposed to be nondecreasing, with the property that
\rho(0+)=0
.

(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
There exist two constants
K\geq 0,\ \gamma>0
and a nonnegative process
\theta=(\theta_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}),

\text{sgn}(y)f(t,y,z,\mu)\leq\theta_{t}+K|y|+KW_{1}(\mu,\delta_{0})+\frac{\gamma|z|}{\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}(|z|)},\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(A3)
There exist a constant
\gamma_{0}>0
and a continuous nondecreasing function
\psi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
, together with a nonnegative process
\theta=(\theta_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}),

|f(t,y,z,\mu)|\leq\theta_{t}+\psi\big(|y|\vee W_{1}(\mu,\delta_{0})\big)+\gamma_{0}|z|^{2},\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(A4)
Monotonicity in
\mu
: For all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R},\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\eta,\ \bar{\eta}\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,

f(t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{\eta})\leq f(t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{\bar{\eta}}),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.,\ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \eta\leq\bar{\eta},\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

(A5)
There exists a continuous, nondecreasing and concave function
\kappa:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with
\kappa(0)=0,\ \kappa(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\kappa(u)}=+\infty
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y,\ \bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}
,

, and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}),

f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,\bar{y},z,\mu)\leq\kappa(y-\bar{y}),\ \ \text{with}\ \ y>\bar{y},\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(A6)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
There exists a continuous nondecreasing function
\zeta:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with linear growth and
\zeta(0)=0
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}
,

, and

|f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,\bar{z},\mu)|\leq\zeta\big(\frac{|z-\bar{z}|}{\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}(|z-\bar{z}|)}\big),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(A7)
There exists a constant

, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\eta,\ \bar{\eta}\in L^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),

f(t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{\eta})-f(t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{\bar{\eta}})\leq L\mathbb{E}[(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{+}],\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

Remark 3.1
.

(i)

For every
m\geq 1
and
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}
, there is a constant
M>0
depending only on
m,\lambda
satisfying
\mathcal{IL}_{m+1}^{\lambda}(x)\leq M\cdot\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}(x),\ x\geq 0.
This implies that when
m
increases, assumptions
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
and
(A6)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
become weaker.

(ii)

Since for every
m\geq 1

\ln^{(m)}(e^{(m)}+x)\geq\ln e=1,\ x\geq 0
, assumptions
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
and
(A6)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
become weaker as
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},+\infty)
decreases.

(iii)

Observe that for every
m\geq 1
and
h>e^{(m)}
, there exists a constant
M>0
depending only on
m,h
such that
1\leq\frac{\ln^{(m)}(h+x)}{\ln^{(m)}(e^{(m)}+x)}\leq M,\ x\geq 0.
Therefore, the function
\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}(x)
appearing in assumptions

and
(A6)

can be equivalently replaced by the following function: For every

\mathcal{IL}_{m,h}^{\lambda}(x):=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big(\ln^{(i)}(h+x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\ln^{(m)}(h+x)\big)^{\lambda},\ x\geq 0.

(iv)

It is not difficult to check that for every

Hence, the iterated-logarithmically sub-linear growth with respect to

in

(v)

Obviously, assumption
(A7)

. Conversely, if the generator

with respect to

, then it automatically satisfies assumption

(cf.
[

).

(vi)

Analogous to
[

, Remark 4.1]
, both assumptions
(A1)
and
(A4)
hold if and only if there exists a modulus of continuity
\rho:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)

f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,z,\bar{\mu})\leq\rho\big(W_{1,+}(\mu,\bar{\mu})\big),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.,
where
W_{1,+}(\mu,\bar{\mu}):=\mathop{\rm inf}\big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}(x-y)^{+}\pi(dxdy),\ \pi\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \text{with marginals}\ \mu\ \text{and}\ \bar{\mu}\big\}
.

Firstly, the following proposition offers a crucial a priori estimate for the

solution to the one-dimensional mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) involving integrable parameters, which will be used later and extends the non mean-field results of
[
18
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let be given
\xi\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and a generator
f
which fulfills assumption
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
with some
m\geq 2\ \text{and}\ \lambda>\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose that
(Y,Z)
is a solution of mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) such that
(|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds)_{t\in[0,T]}
belongs to the class (D). Then there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
depending only on
m,\lambda,\gamma,K,T,\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T)}
such that

|Y_{t}|\leq|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds\leq\widetilde{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+\widetilde{C},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

Proof.

Based on (iii) in Remark
3.1
, without loss of generality we can make the assumption that the generator
f
satisfies assumption
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
, wherein
\mathcal{IL}_{m}^{\lambda}
is substituted by
\mathcal{IL}_{m,h}^{\lambda}
, for a sufficiently large constant
h>e^{(m)}
which depends only on
m,\lambda,\gamma
.

Let us first show that there is a uniform upper bound estimate on the expected value of
Y
, which involves proving the existence of a constant
C>0
depending only on
m,\lambda,\gamma,K,T
such that

\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|]\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+C,\ t\in[0,T].

For the sake of clarity, we put
\widetilde{Y}_{t}:=|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds,\ \widetilde{Z}_{t}:=\text{sgn}(Y_{t})Z_{t},\ t\in[0,T].
Then applying Itô-Tanaka’s formula to
\widetilde{Y}_{t}
on
[t,T]
gives that

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{t}=\widetilde{Y}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\text{sgn}(Y_{s})f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-\theta_{s}\big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Z}_{s}dW_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}dL_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

where the process
L=(L_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
denotes the local time of
Y
at
0
.

Now, we introduce a test function
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
, similar to (3.23) in
[
18
]
and defined as follows: For all
h\geq e^{(m)}\ \text{and}\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\Phi(t,x):=(h+x)\Big[1-\big(\ln^{(m)}(h+x)\big)^{1-2\lambda}\Big]\cdot\mathop{\rm exp}\Big\{2\Big(K+\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{2\lambda-1}\Big)t\Big\}.

(3.1)

A direct calculation shows that for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle 0<\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\bigg[1-\frac{1}{\big(\ln^{(m)}(h+x)\big)^{2\lambda-1}}\Big(1-\frac{2\lambda-1}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\ln^{(j)}(h+x)}\Big)\bigg]

(3.2)

\displaystyle\times\mathop{\rm exp}\Big\{2\Big(K+\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{2\lambda-1}\Big)t\Big\}\leq Q_{0},

where
Q_{0}:=\mathop{\rm exp}\big\{2\big(K+\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{2\lambda-1}\big)T\big\}.
It then follows from
[
18
, Proposition 3.4]
that for a sufficiently large constant
h>e^{(m)}
which depends only on
m,\lambda,\gamma
, it holds that for all
(t,x,z)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle-K\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)x-\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)\frac{\gamma|z|}{\mathcal{IL}_{m,h}^{\lambda}(|z|)}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\Phi(t,x)|z|^{2}+\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)\geq 0.

(3.3)

By reapplying Itô-Tanaka’s formula to
\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})
and combining it with
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
, we get

\displaystyle d\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\big(-\text{sgn}(Y_{t})f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}})+\theta_{t}\big)dt+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\widetilde{Z}_{t}dW_{t}

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})dL_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})|\widetilde{Z}_{t}|^{2}dt+\partial_{t}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})dt

\displaystyle\geq\bigg[-K\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})|Y_{t}|-\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\frac{\gamma|Z_{t}|}{\mathcal{IL}_{m,h}^{\lambda}(|Z_{t}|)}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})|Z_{t}|^{2}+\partial_{t}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\bigg]dt

\displaystyle-K\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\cdot\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|]dt+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\widetilde{Z}_{t}dW_{t},\ t\in[0,T].

Thus, noting that
|Y_{t}|\leq\widetilde{Y}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]
, we can deduce from (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle d\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\geq-K\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\cdot\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|]dt+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\widetilde{Z}_{t}dW_{t},\ t\in[0,T].

(3.5)

For every
n\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
, we define the following stopping time:

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{t}:=\mathop{\rm inf}\Big\{s\in[t,T]:\int_{t}^{s}|\partial_{x}\Phi(r,\widetilde{Y}_{r})|^{2}|\widetilde{Z}_{r}|^{2}dr\geq n\Big\}\wedge T

with the convention that
\mathop{\rm inf}\emptyset=+\infty
. Then, using the definition of
\tau_{n}^{t}
and combining this with (
3.5
), we derive that, for every
n\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Phi(\tau_{n}^{t},\widetilde{Y}_{\tau_{n}^{t}})+K\int_{t}^{\tau_{n}^{t}}\partial_{x}\Phi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s})\cdot\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|]ds\Big].

(3.6)

Moreover, in view of the definition of
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
, one has that, for a sufficiently large constant
h>e^{(m)}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(h+x)\leq\Phi(t,x)\leq Q_{0}(h+x),\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty).

(3.7)

By plugging (
3.7
) into (
3.6
) and taking into account that
0<\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)\leq Q_{0},\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty)
, we obtain that, for every
n\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(h+\widetilde{Y}_{t})\leq\Phi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Phi(\tau_{n}^{t},\widetilde{Y}_{\tau_{n}^{t}})+K\int_{t}^{\tau_{n}^{t}}\partial_{x}\Phi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s})\cdot\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|]ds\Big]

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}h+Q_{0}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\widetilde{Y}_{\tau_{n}^{t}}]+KQ_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|]ds.

As
\widetilde{Y}
is of the class (D), and
\tau_{n}^{t}\uparrow T
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, by taking the limit as
n
tends to infinity on both sides of the latter inequality, we get that

\displaystyle|Y_{t}|\leq\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq 2Q_{0}h+2Q_{0}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\widetilde{Y}_{T}]+2KQ_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|]ds,\ t\in[0,T].

(3.9)

Furthermore, according to the definition of
\widetilde{Y}
, taking the expectation on both sides yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|]\leq\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Y}_{t}]\leq 2Q_{0}h+2Q_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+2KQ_{0}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|]ds,\ t\in[0,T].

The (backward) Gronwall’s inequality allows to conclude that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|]\leq\Big(2Q_{0}h+2Q_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]\Big)e^{2KQ_{0}T}\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+C,

(3.10)

where
C:=2Q_{0}(h\vee 1)e^{2KQ_{0}T}
, which completes the upper bound estimate on
\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}|],\ t\in[0,T]
.

Finally, substituting (
3.10
) in (
3.9
) and recalling the definition of
\widetilde{Y}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]
, we conclude that there is a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
depending only on
m,\lambda,\gamma,K,T,\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T)}
such that

|Y_{t}|\leq|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds\leq\widetilde{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+\widetilde{C},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

(3.11)

Next, before presenting our main results, we give the stability result of bounded solutions for mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth, which plays an important role in our subsequent proof of the existence of

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that for all
n\geq 1
, the terminal value
\xi_{n}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n}=\xi_{n}+\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{n}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

(resp.,

d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{n\geq 1}|f_{n}(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)|\leq\theta_{t}(\omega)+\psi\big(W_{1}(\mu,\delta_{0})\big)+\gamma_{0}|z|^{2},\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(\xi,f)

Proof.

Lemma

[
29
, Proposition 3.1]
).

With the help of Proposition

and Lemma

Theorem 3.6
.

Let assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
,
(A3)
and
(A4)
be satisfied with some

, the mean-field BSDE (

(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})

. Moreover, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}>0

|Y_{t}|\leq|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds\leq\widetilde{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+\widetilde{C},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

(3.12)

Proof.

Step 1.
We implement the localization procedure, as outlined in
[
5

, to construct a solution. For all
n,k\geq 1

(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R})

Obviously, for all
(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

|\xi^{n,k}|\leq|\xi|\wedge(n\vee k)\ \ \text{and}\ \ |f^{n,k}(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)|\leq|f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)|\wedge(n\vee k),

,
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n,k}=\xi^{n,k}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{n,k}(s,Y_{s}^{n,k},Z_{s}^{n,k},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{n,k}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n,k}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

For

It follows from (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{n,k}|]\leq\widetilde{C}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+\widetilde{C}=:\overline{C},

\displaystyle|Y_{m}^{n,k}(t)|\leq m,\ \ \text{and}\ \ |Y^{k}_{m}(t)|\leq m,\ t\in[0,T],\ n,k\geq 1,\ m\geq 1.

\displaystyle g^{n,k}(\omega,t,y,z):={\mathbf{1}}_{\{t\leq\sigma_{m}(\omega)\}}f^{n,k}(\omega,t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{Y^{n,k}_{t}}),\ \text{and}\ g^{k}(\omega,t,y,z):={\mathbf{1}}_{\{t\leq\sigma_{m}(\omega)\}}f^{k}(\omega,t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{Y^{k}_{t}}),

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{n\geq 1}|g^{n,k}(\omega,t,y,z)|\leq\theta_{t}(\omega)+\psi(m\vee\overline{C})+\gamma_{0}|z|^{2},\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

\displaystyle|g^{n,k}(\omega,t,y_{n},z_{n})-g^{k}(\omega,t,y,z)|\leq|f^{n,k}(\omega,t,y_{n},z_{n},\mathbb{P}_{Y^{k}_{t}})-f^{k}(\omega,t,y,z,\mathbb{P}_{Y^{k}_{t}})|

\displaystyle+\rho\big(\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{n,k}-Y_{t}^{k}|]\big)\rightarrow 0,\ \ \text{as}\ (y_{n},z_{n})\rightarrow(y,z),\ n\rightarrow+\infty.

Consequently, from Lemma

\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{m}^{n,k}(s)-Z_{m}^{k}(s)|^{2}ds\big]\rightarrow 0,\ n\rightarrow+\infty,
and
(Y_{m}^{k},Z_{m}^{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle Y_{m}^{k}(t)=Y^{k}_{\sigma_{m}}+\int_{t}^{T}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{s\leq\sigma_{m}\}}f^{k}(s,Y^{k}_{m}(s),Z^{k}_{m}(s),\mathbb{P}_{Y^{k}_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{m}^{k}(s)dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

\displaystyle Y_{m}(t)=Y_{\sigma_{m}}+\int_{t}^{T}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{s\leq\sigma_{m}\}}f(s,Y_{m}(s),Z_{m}(s),\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{m}(s)dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{n,k}(=Z^{n,k}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\sigma_{m}]})\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}^{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}Z_{m}^{k}\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}^{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}}\ Z_{m},\ m\geq 1.

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

p\in(0,1)
. For all

{}_{m}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle 2Y_{s}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})\leq 2\theta_{s}|Y_{s}|+(2K+2\gamma^{2})|Y_{s}|^{2}+2K|Y_{s}|W_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}},\delta_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|Z_{s}|^{2}.

\displaystyle c_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,\tau_{n}]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}Z_{s}dW_{s}\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\leq\frac{9}{2}c_{p}^{2}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{T}^{\ast}|^{p}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big].

Since

.

\overline{\tau}^{t}_{n}

\displaystyle\Psi(t,\overline{Y}_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Psi(\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t},\overline{Y}_{\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}})+J\int_{t}^{\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}}\partial_{x}\Psi(s,\overline{Y}_{s})\cdot\mathbb{E}[\overline{Y}_{s}]ds\Big],\ t\in[0,T].

(3.28)

Similar to (
3.7
), from the definition of
\Psi(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have, for a sufficiently large constant
h>e^{(m)}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(h+x)\leq\Psi(t,x)\leq Q_{1}(h+x),\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,+\infty).

(3.29)

By inserting (
3.29
) into (
3.28
) and combining this with (
3.26
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(h+\overline{Y}_{t})\leq\Psi(t,\overline{Y}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Psi(\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t},\overline{Y}_{\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}})+J\int_{t}^{\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}}\partial_{x}\Psi(s,\overline{Y}_{s})\cdot\mathbb{E}[\overline{Y}_{s}]ds\Big]

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{1}h+Q_{1}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\overline{Y}_{\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}}]+JQ_{1}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\overline{Y}_{s}]ds,\ t\in[0,T].

As
\overline{Y}
is of the class (D), and
\overline{\tau}_{n}^{t}\uparrow T
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,
t\in[0,T]
, passing to the limit in (
3.30
) and recalling the definition of
\overline{Y}
together with the fact that
\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2},\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t}\leq 2Q_{1}h+4Q_{1}JT+2JQ_{1}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\overline{Y}_{s}]ds,\ t\in[0,T].

(3.31)

Furthermore, taking the expectation and using (backward) Gronwall’s inequality yields

\mathbb{E}[\overline{Y}_{t}]\leq C^{\ast}:=\big(2Q_{1}h+4Q_{1}JT\big)e^{2JQ_{1}T},\ t\in[0,T].

(3.32)

Finally, we substitute (
3.32
) in (
3.31
) to conclude that

(Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}\leq(Y_{t}^{1}-Y_{t}^{2})^{+}+2Jt\leq 2Q_{1}(h+2JT+JC^{\ast}T),\ t\in[0,T].

(3.33)

Hence,
(Y^{1}-Y^{2})^{+}
is bounded.

Step 2.
For every
n\geq 1
, we define now the following stopping time:

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\mathop{\rm inf}\Big\{t\in[0,T]:\int_{0}^{t}\big(|Z_{s}^{1}|^{2}+|Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big)ds\geq n\Big\}\wedge T.

By applying Itô-Tanaka’s formula, we obtain that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{+}\leq\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}^{+}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{s}>0\}}\big(f^{1}(s,Y_{s}^{1},Z_{s}^{1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{1}})-f^{2}(s,Y_{s}^{2},Z_{s}^{2},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{2}})\big)ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{s}>0\}}\widehat{Z}_{s}dW_{s}.

(3.34)

From (
3.25
), we have,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
,

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{t}>0\}}\big(f^{1}(t,Y_{t}^{1},Z_{t}^{1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}^{1}})-f^{2}(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{2},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}^{2}})\big)\leq\kappa(\widehat{Y}_{t}^{+})+\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{t}>0\}}\zeta(|\widehat{Z}_{t}|)+L\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Y}_{t}^{+}].

(3.35)

Moreover, from Lemma
2.3
and (
3.24
), it follows that, for all
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\zeta(x)\leq(m+2J)x+\zeta\Big(\frac{2J}{m+2J}\Big),\ x\geq 0.

(3.36)

Then, combining the results from (
3.34
)–(
3.36
), we obtain that, for all
m\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{+}

\displaystyle\leq a_{m}+\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}^{+}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\big(\kappa(\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+})+\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{s}>0\}}(m+2J)|\widehat{Z}_{s}|+L\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+}]\big)ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{s}>0\}}\widehat{Z}_{s}dW_{s}

(3.37)

\displaystyle=a_{m}+\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}^{+}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\big(\kappa(\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+})+L\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+}]\big)ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\textbf{1}_{\{\widehat{Y}_{s}>0\}}\widehat{Z}_{s}\cdot\big(dW_{s}-b_{s}^{m}ds\big),

where
a_{m}:=\zeta\big(\frac{2J}{m+2J}\big)T\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ m\rightarrow+\infty,
and
b_{s}^{m}:=\frac{(m+2J)\widehat{Z}_{s}^{\mathsf{T}}}{|\widehat{Z}_{s}|},\ \text{if}\ \widehat{Z}_{s}\neq 0;

b_{s}^{m}:=0,\ \text{if}\ \widehat{Z}_{s}=0.
Next, for all
m\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle W^{m}_{t}:=W_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}^{m}ds,\ t\in[0,T],\ \ \text{and}\ \ d\mathbb{P}^{m}:=\mathop{\rm exp}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}(b_{s}^{m})^{\mathsf{T}}dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|b_{s}^{m}|^{2}ds\Big\}d\mathbb{P}.

Due to Girsanov’s theorem,
\mathbb{P}^{m}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
, and the process
W^{m}
is a Brownian motion under
\mathbb{P}^{m}
. For simplicity, the expectation and conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
under
\mathbb{P}^{m}
are denoted by
\mathbb{E}^{m}[\cdot]
and
\mathbb{E}_{t}^{m}[\cdot]
, respectively.

By taking the conditional expectation of (
3.37
) under
\mathbb{P}^{m}
, we deduce that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{+}\leq a_{m}+\mathbb{E}^{m}_{t}[\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}^{+}]+\mathbb{E}^{m}_{t}\Big[\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\big(\kappa(\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+})+L\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+}]\big)ds\Big],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

Hence, as
\tau_{n}\uparrow T
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,
\widehat{Y}^{+}
is bounded, and
\widehat{Y}_{T}^{+}=(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{+}=0,
taking the limit in the latter inequality and applying Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t}^{+}\leq a_{m}+\mathbb{E}^{m}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\big(\kappa(\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+})+L\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+}]\big)ds\Big],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

(3.38)

Consequently, as
\kappa(\cdot)
is nondecreasing,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Y}^{+}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq a_{m}+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\kappa(\|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{+}\|_{\infty})+L\|\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s}\|_{\infty}\big)ds=a_{m}+\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{\kappa}(\|\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s}\|_{\infty})ds,\ t\in[0,T],

(3.39)

where
\widetilde{\kappa}(x):=\kappa(x)+Lx,\ x\geq 0
. It’s easy to verify that
\widetilde{\kappa}(\cdot)
is still a nondecreasing and concave function with
\widetilde{\kappa}(0)=0,\ \widetilde{\kappa}(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\widetilde{\kappa}(u)}=+\infty
(we refer to Appendix in
[
16
]
).

Then it follows from Bihari’s inequality (see Lemma
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{Y}^{+}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{\Pi}^{-1}\big(\widetilde{\Pi}(a_{m})+T-t\big),\ t\in[0,T],

(3.40)

where
\widetilde{\Pi}(x):=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{1}{\widetilde{\kappa}(r)}dr,\ x>0.
Finally, letting
m\rightarrow+\infty
in (
3.40
) yields
\|\widehat{Y}^{+}_{t}\|_{\infty}=0,\ t\in[0,T]
, i.e.,
Y_{t}^{1}\leq Y_{t}^{2},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

By combining the existence stated in Theorem
3.6
with the comparison theorem (Theorem
3.7
), we get the existence and the uniqueness of the
L^{1}
solution for one-dimensional mean-field BSDEs (
1.2
) with integrable parameters.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
,
(A3)
and
(A4)
hold true with some
m\geq 2\ \text{and}\ \lambda>\frac{1}{2}
. Then, for every given
\xi\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) possesses a solution
(Y,Z)
such that
Y
belongs to the class (D) and
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
. Moreover, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
depending only on
m,\lambda,\gamma,K,T,\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T)}
such that

|Y_{t}|\leq|Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{s}ds\leq\widetilde{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big]+\widetilde{C},\ t\in[0,T],\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.

Furthermore, provided that also assumptions
(A5)
,
(A6)
{}_{m}^{\lambda}
and
(A7)
are satisfied for the generator
f
, then the mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) has a unique
L^{1}
solution
(Y,Z)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the uniqueness of the solution
(Y,Z)
.

If
(Y^{1},Z^{1})
and
(Y^{2},Z^{2})
are both an
L^{1}
solution of the mean-field BSDE (
1.2
), then from Theorem
3.7
we get
Y_{t}^{1}=Y_{t}^{2},\ \mathbb{P}\text{-}a.s.
, for all
t\in[0,T
]. We define the following stopping times:

\tau_{n}^{i}:=\mathop{\rm inf}\Big\{t\in[0,T]:\int_{0}^{t}|Z_{s}^{i}|^{2}ds\geq n\Big\}\wedge T,\ i=1,2;\ \ \tau_{n}:=\tau_{n}^{1}\wedge\tau_{n}^{2},\ n\geq 1.

Then, since both
(Y^{1},Z^{1})
and
(Y^{1},Z^{2})
are the
L^{1}
solutions of (
1.2
) we have

\int_{0}^{t}\big(f(s,Y_{s}^{1},Z_{s}^{1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{1}})-f(s,Y_{s}^{1},Z_{s}^{2},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{1}})\big)ds=\int_{0}^{t}(Z_{s}^{1}-Z_{s}^{2})dW_{s},\ 0\leq t\leq\tau_{n},

which means
Z_{s}^{1}=Z_{s}^{2},\ d\mathbb{P}\times ds\text{-}a.e.
on
\Omega\times[0,\tau_{n}]
, and as
\tau_{n}\uparrow T
(
n\rightarrow+\infty
), this implies
Z_{s}^{1}=Z_{s}^{2},\ d\mathbb{P}\times ds\text{-}a.e.
on
\Omega\times[0,T]
.

4
Multi-dimensional case

This section is devoted to the study of the existence and the uniqueness of
L^{1}
solutions for multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs (
1.2
) with integrable data, where the generator satisfies a one-sided Osgood condition in the variable
y
. For this end, we assume that the terminal value
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable and the generator
f:=f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu):\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, for every
(y,z,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Additionally, we make use of the following assumptions on the generator
f
:

(H1)
There exists a nondecreasing and concave function
\eta:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with
\eta(0)=0
,
\eta(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\eta(u)}=+\infty
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y,\ \bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{y-\bar{y}}{|y-\bar{y}|}\textbf{1}_{\{|y-\bar{y}|\neq 0\}},f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,\bar{y},z,\mu)\Big\rangle\leq\eta\big(|y-\bar{y}|\big),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(H2)
There exists
K>0
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mu,\ \bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,z,\bar{\mu})|\leq KW_{1}(\mu,\bar{\mu}),\ d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.

(H3)
There exist constants
L>0,\ M>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1)
and a nonnegative process
(\vartheta_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z,\ \bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,\bar{z},\mu)|\leq L|z-\bar{z}|,

\displaystyle|f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,y,0,\mu)|\leq M(\vartheta_{t}+|y|+W_{1}(\mu,\delta_{0})+|z|)^{\alpha}.

(H4)
For all
r\geq 0
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\bar{\phi}_{r}(t)dt\big]<+\infty\ \text{with}\ \bar{\phi}_{r}(t):=\mathop{\rm sup}_{|y|\leq r}|f(t,y,0,\delta_{0})|.
Moreover,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
y\rightarrow f(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)
is continuous.

Remark 4.1
.

-order weak monotonicity condition
(H1
p
)
(

\displaystyle|y-\bar{y}|^{p-1}\Big\langle\frac{y-\bar{y}}{|y-\bar{y}|}\textbf{1}_{\{|y-\bar{y}|\neq 0\}},f(t,y,z,\mu)-f(t,\bar{y},z,\mu)\Big\rangle\leq\eta_{p}\big(|y-\bar{y}|^{p}\big),

for all
t\in[0,T],\ y,\ \bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

Example 4.3

For all

\displaystyle F^{i}(\omega,t,y,z,\mu)

Example 4.4

Theorem 4.5
.

Let assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
hold true. Then for every given
\xi\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) admits at most one solution
(Y,Z)
such that
Y
belongs to the class (D) and
Z
belongs to
\bigcup_{\beta>\alpha}\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, which directly implies the uniqueness of the
L^{1}
solution.

Proof.

Let
(Y,Z)
and
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})
be two adapted solutions to mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) such that
Y
and
\widetilde{Y}
are both of the class (D), and both
Z
and
\widetilde{Z}
belong to
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
for some
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
. For simplicity of notation, we put
\widehat{Y}:=Y-\widetilde{Y}
and
\widehat{Z}:=Z-\widetilde{Z}.

Step 1.
Let us first show that
\widehat{Y}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p:=\beta/\alpha>1
. For this purpose, we introduce the following sequence of stopping times: For all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\mathop{\rm inf}\Big\{t\in[0,T]:\int_{0}^{t}\big(|Z_{s}|^{2}+|\widetilde{Z}_{s}|^{2}\big)ds\geq n\Big\}\wedge T.

From the Itô-Tanaka formula (see also Briand et al.
[
2
, Corollary 2.3]
) we deduce that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{Y}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\leq|\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}|+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\big\langle\varrho(\widehat{Y}_{s}),f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{s}})\big\rangle ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\langle\varrho(\widehat{Y}_{s}),\widehat{Z}_{s}dW_{s}\rangle,

(4.2)

where
\varrho(x):=|x|^{-1}x,\ \text{if}\ x\neq 0;\ \varrho(x):=0,\ \text{if}\ x=0
. In view of assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
, we get that,
d\mathbb{P}\times ds\text{-}a.e.
,

\displaystyle\big\langle\varrho(\widehat{Y}_{s}),f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{s}})\big\rangle

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq\big\langle\varrho(\widehat{Y}_{s}),f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})\big\rangle+|f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}})-f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{s}})|

\displaystyle+|f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},Z_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{s}})-f(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{Z}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{s}})|

\displaystyle\leq\eta(|\widehat{Y}_{s}|)+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]+2M(\vartheta_{s}+|\widetilde{Y}_{s}|+\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{Y}_{s}|]+|Z_{s}|+|\widetilde{Z}_{s}|)^{\alpha}.

Inserting (
4.3
) in (
4.2
) and then taking the conditional expectation gives, for all
n\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{Y}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|\widehat{Y}_{\tau_{n}}|+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\big(\eta(|\widehat{Y}_{s}|)+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]\big)ds\Big]+Q(t),

(4.4)

where
Q(t):=2M\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\vartheta_{s}+|\widetilde{Y}_{s}|+\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{Y}_{s}|]+|Z_{s}|+|\widetilde{Z}_{s}|\big)^{\alpha}ds\big].
As
\widehat{Y}
belongs to the class (D) and
\tau_{n}\uparrow T
, as
n
goes to infinity, we can take the limit
n\rightarrow+\infty
in (
4.4
). Then, thanks to Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and (
4.1
) in Remark
4.2
, we obtain, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{Y}_{t}|\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\big(\eta(|\widehat{Y}_{s}|)+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]\big)ds\Big]+Q(t)\leq\int_{t}^{T}\big(R\mathbb{E}_{t}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]\big)ds+RT+Q(t).

Consequently,
\mathbb{E}_{t}[|\widehat{Y}_{r}|]\leq\int_{r}^{T}\big(R\mathbb{E}_{t}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]\big)ds+RT+Q(t),\ r\in[t,T]
. Then Gronwall’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[|\widehat{Y}_{r}|]\leq\Big(\int_{r}^{T}K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]ds+RT+Q(t)\Big)e^{RT},\ r\in[t,T].

In particular, by setting
r=t
and noting that
\widehat{Y}
is of the class (D) which guarantees that
\gamma:=\mathop{\rm sup}_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|]<+\infty
, we conclude that

\displaystyle|\widehat{Y}_{t}|\leq\big(\gamma KT+RT+Q(t)\big)e^{RT},\ t\in[0,T].

(4.5)

Recall that
\widehat{Y}
is of the class (D),
Z
and
\widetilde{Z}
belong to
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
with
\beta>\alpha
, and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
. Thus, it follows from Doob’s maximal inequality that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|Q(t)|^{\beta/\alpha}\big]\leq\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha}\Big)^{\beta/\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[|Q(T)|^{\beta/\alpha}\big]<+\infty.

(4.6)

Hence, combining this with (
4.5
) yields that
\widehat{Y}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\beta/\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Step 2.
First, notice that
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})
solves the following BSDE:

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t}=\int_{t}^{T}\Delta f(s,\widehat{Y}_{s},\widehat{Z}_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\widehat{Z}_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

(4.7)

where
\Delta f(t,y,z):=f(t,y+\widetilde{Y}_{t},z+\widetilde{Z}_{t},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}})-f(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t},\widetilde{Z}_{t},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}})
, for all
(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.
It follows from assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
on the generator
f
along with Remarks
4.1
–
4.2
that there exists a constant
R_{2}>0
such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{and}\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,

\displaystyle\big\langle y,\Delta f(t,y,z)\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{2}(|y|^{2})+L|y||z|+K|y|W_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}})

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}|y|^{2}+L|y||z|+K|y|W_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}})+R_{2}.

Thus, as
\widehat{Y}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\beta/\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, Lemma
C.1
with
\lambda_{1}=R_{2},\ \lambda_{2}=L,\ g_{t}=KW_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}}),\ \theta_{t}\equiv R_{2}
, implies
\widehat{Z}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p_{0}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
with
p_{0}=\beta/\alpha>1
. In particular,
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
, is a solution to BSDE (
4.7
).

Moreover, from assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
and Remark
4.1
, we also derive that there exists a nondecreasing and concave function
\eta_{p_{0}}(\cdot):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with
\eta_{p_{0}}(0)=0
,
\eta_{p_{0}}(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\eta_{p_{0}}(u)}=+\infty
, such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,

\displaystyle|y|^{p_{0}-1}\big\langle\varrho(y),\Delta f(t,y,z)\big\rangle\leq\eta_{p_{0}}(|y|^{p_{0}})+L|y|^{p_{0}-1}|z|+K|y|^{p_{0}-1}W_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}}).

Applying Lemma
C.2
with
\varphi(\cdot)=\eta_{p_{0}}(\cdot),\ \lambda=L,\ g_{t}=KW_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}})
, and using Hölder’s inequality, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
p_{0},L,K,T
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}|^{p_{0}}\big]\leq C\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\eta_{p_{0}}\big(\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|^{p_{0}}]\big)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}|^{p_{0}}]ds\Big)\leq C\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{u\in[s,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{u}|^{p_{0}}\big]\Big)ds,

where
\widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}(x):=\eta_{p_{0}}(x)+x,\ x\geq 0
. It’s easy to verify that
\widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}(\cdot)
is still a nondecreasing and concave function with
\widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}(0)=0,\ \widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\widetilde{\eta}_{p_{0}}(u)}=+\infty
(we refer to Appendix in
[
16
]
).

Thus it follows from Bihari’s inequality (see Lemma
2.4
) that

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}-\widetilde{Y}_{t}|^{p_{0}}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{t}|^{p_{0}}\big]=0.

(4.9)

Moreover, thanks to (
4.8
), Lemma
2.3
and Remark
4.2
, the generator
\Delta f
of BSDE (
4.7
) satisfies that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, and
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\big\langle y,\Delta f(t,y,z)\big\rangle\leq(m+2R_{2})|y|^{2}+\eta_{2}\Big(\frac{2R_{2}}{m+2R_{2}}\Big)+L|y||z|+K|y|W_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}}).

Then applying Lemma
C.1
with
\lambda_{1}=m+2R_{2},\ \lambda_{2}=L,\ g_{t}=KW_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{Y_{t}},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{Y}_{t}}),\ \theta_{t}\equiv\eta_{2}(\frac{2R_{2}}{m+2R_{2}})
, this shows that there exist two positive constants
C>0
depending only on
p_{0},m,R_{2},L,K,T
and
\widetilde{C}_{p_{0}}>0
depending only on
p_{0}
, such that, for all
m\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\Big]\leq C\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{t}|^{p_{0}}\big]+\widetilde{C}_{p_{0}}\Big(\eta_{2}\Big(\frac{2R_{2}}{m+2R_{2}}\Big)\cdot T\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}.

(4.10)

Therefore, by (4.9) and the fact that
\eta_{2}(\cdot)
is a continuous function with
\eta_{2}(0)=0
, letting
m\rightarrow+\infty
in the last inequality we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}-\widetilde{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\Big]=0.

(4.11)

With (
4.9
) and (
4.11
), the proof of the uniqueness is complete.

We come now to our existence result.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let assumptions
(H1)
–
(H4)
hold true. In the case where
\alpha
takes values in
[\frac{1}{2},1)
in
(H4)
, we further suppose that
\eta(\cdot)
in
(H1)
satisfies that there exists
p^{\ast}>1
such that

\int_{0+}\frac{u^{p^{\ast}-1}}{\eta^{p^{\ast}}(u)}du=+\infty.

(4.12)

Then, for every given
\xi\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the mean-field BSDE (
1.2
) possesses an
L^{1}
solution
(Y,Z)
, i.e.,
Y
is of the class (D) and
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Step 1.
Given a pair of processes
(U,V)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
, where
U
is of the class (D), we consider the BSDE

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},V_{s},\mathbb{P}_{U_{s}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

(4.13)

Obviously, by assumption
(H1)
, we have
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ \text{and}\ y,\ \bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\big\langle\varrho(y-\bar{y}),f(t,y,V_{t},\mathbb{P}_{U_{t}})-f(t,\bar{y},V_{t},\mathbb{P}_{U_{t}})\big\rangle\leq\eta\big(|y-\bar{y}|\big).

In addition, by using assumptions
(H2)
–
(H4)
and Hölder’s inequality, we get, for all
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{|y|\leq r}|f(t,y,V_{t},\mathbb{P}_{U_{t}})|dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{|y|\leq r}|f(t,y,0,\delta_{0})|dt\Big]+KT\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|U_{t}|]+M\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\vartheta_{t}dt\Big]\Big)^{\alpha}T^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle+MTr^{\alpha}+M\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|V_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big]T^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}<+\infty.

Hence, it follows from
[
15
, Theorems 1–2]
that BSDE (
4.13
) admits a unique
L^{1}
solution
(Y,Z)
, that is,
Y
is of the class (D) and
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, for all
p\in(0,1)
.

Step 2.
Step 1 enables us to formulate the following Picard iteration procedure: We put as usual
(Y^{0},Z^{0})=(0,0)
and define recursively the sequence of processes
(Y^{n},Z^{n}),\ n\geq 1
, as the unique
L^{1}
solution to the following BSDE:

Y_{t}^{n}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n-1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{n-1}})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{n}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T].

(4.14)

In what follows, for all
n,k\geq 1
, we set
\widehat{Y}^{n,k}:=Y^{n+k}-Y^{n}
and
\widehat{Z}^{n,k}:=Z^{n+k}-Z^{n}
. By a similar argument as the proof of (
4.5
) in Theorem
4.5
, using assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
, we prove that, for
\gamma^{n,k}:=\mathop{\rm sup}_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|]
and for all
n,k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n,k}|\leq\big(\gamma^{n-1,k}KT+RT+Q^{n,k}(t)\big)e^{RT},\ t\in[0,T],

where
Q^{n,k}(t):=2M\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\big(\vartheta_{s}+|Y_{s}^{n}|+\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}^{n-1}|]+|Z_{s}^{n-1}|+|Z^{n+k-1}_{s}|\big)^{\alpha}ds\big]\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{r}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, given that
\alpha r<1
with
r>1
. Then we obtain that, for all
n,k\geq 1
,
(\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n,k})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{r}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, under the condition
\alpha r<1
for
r>1
. In the following, we discuss two cases separately: (i)
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
; (ii)
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
.

Step 3.
We first study the case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. In this case, by choosing
r=2
, we have

\displaystyle(\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n,k})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ n,k\geq 1.

(4.15)

Observe that
(\widehat{Y}^{n,k},\widehat{Z}^{n,k})
is a solution to the BSDE

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n,k}=\int_{t}^{T}\widehat{f}^{n,k}(s,\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k})ds-\int_{t}^{T}\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}dW_{s},\ t\in[0,T],

(4.16)

where
\widehat{f}^{n,k}(s,y):=f(s,y+Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n+k-1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{n+k-1}})-f(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n-1},\mathbb{P}_{Y_{s}^{n-1}})
, for all
(s,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
, and the Remarks
4.1
–
4.2
, we get that there exists a nondecreasing and concave function
\eta_{2}(\cdot):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with
\eta_{2}(0)=0
,
\eta_{2}(u)>0,\ u>0
and
\int_{0^{+}}\frac{du}{\eta_{2}(u)}=+\infty
, such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times ds\text{-}a.e.
, for all
s\in[0,T]\ \text{and}\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\langle y,\widehat{f}^{n,k}(s,y)\rangle\leq\eta_{2}(|y|^{2})+2M|y|\big(\vartheta_{s}+|Y_{s}^{n}|+\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}^{n-1}|]+|Z_{s}^{n-1}|+|Z_{s}^{n+k-1}|\big)^{\alpha}+K|y|\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|]

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}|y|^{2}+2M|y|\big(\vartheta_{s}+|Y_{s}^{n}|+\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}^{n-1}|]+|Z_{s}^{n-1}|+|Z_{s}^{n+k-1}|\big)^{\alpha}+K|y|\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|]+R_{2},

and

\displaystyle\langle y,\widehat{f}^{n,k}(s,y)\rangle\leq\eta_{2}(|y|^{2})+L|y||\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|+K|y|\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|].

(4.18)

By virtue of (
4.15
) and (
4.17
), it follows from Lemma
C.1
with
p=2,\ \lambda_{1}=R_{2},\ \lambda_{2}=0,\ g_{t}=2M\big(\vartheta_{t}+|Y_{t}^{n}|+\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{n-1}|]+|Z_{t}^{n-1}|+|Z_{t}^{n+k-1}|\big)^{\alpha}+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n-1,k}|],\ \theta_{t}\equiv R_{2}
, that
\widehat{Z}^{n,k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
. Hence,
(\widehat{Y}^{n,k},\widehat{Z}^{n,k})\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
is a solution of BSDE (
4.16
), for all
n,k\geq 1
.

Moreover, taking into account (
4.18
) and recalling Lemma
C.3
with
\varphi(\cdot)=\eta_{2}(\cdot),\ \lambda=0,\ g_{t}=L|\widehat{Z}_{t}^{n-1,k}|+K\mathbb{E}[|\widehat{Y}_{t}^{n-1,k}|]
, we obtain the existence of a positive constant
C>0
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}ds\Big]\leq e^{C(T-t)}\bigg(\int_{t}^{T}\eta_{2}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{u\in[s,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{u}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]\Big)ds

(4.19)

\displaystyle+2L^{2}(T-t)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}ds\Big]+2K^{2}(T-t)^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}\big]\bigg).

Next, we put
\varepsilon:=\mathop{\rm min}\big\{\frac{\ln 2}{C},\frac{1}{32L^{2}},\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}K},\frac{\ln 2}{2R_{2}}\big\}\ \text{and}\ T_{j}:=(T-j\varepsilon)\vee 0,\ j\geq 1.
Thus, for all
t\in[T_{1},T]
, we have

\displaystyle e^{C(T-t)}\leq 2,\ \ 2e^{C(T-t)}L^{2}(T-t)\leq\frac{1}{8},\ \ 2e^{C(T-t)}K^{2}(T-t)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8},\ \ e^{2R_{2}(T-t)}\leq 2.

(4.20)

Combining (
4.19
)–(
4.20
) gives, for all
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}ds\Big]\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\eta_{2}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{u\in[s,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{u}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]\Big)ds

(4.21)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}\big].

Thanks to Remark
4.2
that
\eta_{2}(x)\leq R_{2}x+R_{2}
,
x\geq 0
, it follows from (
4.20
)–(
4.21
) and Gronwall’s inequality that, for all
n\geq 2,\ k\geq 1
and
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}ds\Big]

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2R_{2}(T-t)}\Big(2R_{2}T+\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4R_{2}T+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}\big].

In particular, by taking
n=2
and
k=m-2
in (
4.22
), we obtain that, for all
m\geq 3
and
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 4R_{2}T+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{2}-Z_{s}^{1}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{1}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m-1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m-1}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big],

which by induction leads to that, for all
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{m\geq 1}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\Big]\Big)

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq 8R_{2}T+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{2}-Z_{s}^{1}|^{2}ds\Big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T]}|Y_{s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{1}|^{2}\big]<+\infty.

Moreover, note that for all
n\geq 2,\ k\geq 1
and
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}ds\Big]+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n-1,k}|^{2}\big]

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\big(|Z_{s}^{n+k-1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}+|Z_{s}^{n-1}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big)ds\Big]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{n+k-1}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{n-1}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{m\geq 1}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}^{m}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}^{m}-Z_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\Big]\Big).

Therefore, by combining (
4.22
)–(
4.24
), we get, for all
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{n\geq 2}\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{k\geq 1}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}ds\Big]\Big)

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq 12R_{2}T+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}^{2}-Z_{s}^{1}|^{2}ds\Big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[0,T]}|Y_{s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{1}|^{2}\big]<+\infty.

Let us put
a_{n,k}(t):=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}_{s\in[t,T]}|\widehat{Y}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{t}^{T}|\widehat{Z}_{s}^{n,k}|^{2}ds\big],\ t\in[T_{1},T].
Then, from (
4.21
) and the properties of
\eta_{2}(\cdot)
,

\displaystyle a_{n,k}(t)\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\eta_{2}\big(a_{n,k}(s)\big)ds+\frac{1}{8}a_{n-1,k}(t),\ t\in[T_{1},T],\ n\geq 2,\ k\geq 1,

and so also,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{k\geq 1}a_{n,k}(t)\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\eta_{2}\big(\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{k\geq 1}a_{n,k}(s)\big)ds+\frac{1}{8}\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{k\geq 1}a_{n-1,k}(t),\ t\in[T_{1},T],\ n\geq 2.

Consequently, from Fatou’s lemma, for all
t\in[T_{1},T]
,

, a finite iteration extends the solution

Lemma C.1
.

Let
p>0
. Suppose that the generator
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable and there exist constants
\lambda_{1}>0,\ \lambda_{2}>0
, and two nonnegative processes
g=(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, and
\theta=(\theta_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}^{\frac{p}{2}}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,

\displaystyle\langle y,f(t,y,z)\rangle\leq\lambda_{1}|y|^{2}+\lambda_{2}|y||z|+|y|g_{t}+\theta_{t}.

Let
(Y,Z)
be a solution of BSDE (
1.1
) such that
Y\in\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
Z\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
and there exists a constant
C_{p,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},T}>0
depending only on
p,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},T
as well as another constant
C_{p}>0
depending only on
p
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\leq C_{p,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},T}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}|^{p}\big]+C_{p}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}g_{s}ds\Big)^{p}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\theta_{s}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]\Big).

Lemma C.2
.

Let
p>1
. Suppose that the generator
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable and there exist a constant
\lambda>0
and a nonnegative process
g=(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, such that,
d\mathbb{P}\times dt\text{-}a.e.
, for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,

\displaystyle|y|^{p-1}\Big\langle\frac{y}{|y|}\mathbf{1}_{\{|y|\neq 0\}},f(t,y,z)\Big\rangle\leq\varphi(|y|^{p})+\lambda|y|^{p-1}|z|+|y|^{p-1}g_{t},

where
\varphi(\cdot):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a nondecreasing and concave function with
\varphi(0)=0
.

Let
(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
be a solution of BSDE (
1.1
). Then there exists a constant
C_{p,\lambda}>0
depending only on
p,\lambda
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathop{\rm sup}\limits_{s\in[t,T]}|Y_{s}|^{p}\big]\leq e^{C_{p,\lambda}(T-t)}\Big(\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{p}]+\int_{t}^{T}\varphi(\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}|^{p}])ds+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}g_{s}ds\Big)^{p}\Big]\Big).

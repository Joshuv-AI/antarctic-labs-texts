Molecular design of amphiphilic triblock copolymers as additives for high performance poly(vinyl chloride) ultrafiltration membranes
Authors: Hasan Alshammari, Maral Ghahramani, Alireza Sharif
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAmphiphilic triblock copolymers (TBCs) of poly(styrene)-b-polyethylene glycol-b-poly(styrene) (PS-b-PEG-b-PS) were synthesized by atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) and added to poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC) to prepare ultrafiltration (UF) blend membranes with superior water treatment efficiency. Compared to neat PVC, the blend membranes showed higher surface and bulk porosity, mean pore size, and surface hydrophilicity. These, attributed to the surface segregation of TBCs during membrane preparation, resulted in higher pure water flux (PWF), sodium alginate rejection, and fouling resistance for the blend membranes. The molecular weight (MW) of the hydrophilic (PEG) and hydrophobic (PS) blocks of the TBCs had significant influences on the PWF and fouling resistance of the membranes. Increasing the MW of PEG from 1000 g/mol to 4000 g/mol led to an improvement in the PWF and fouling resistance. However, TBCs containing PS with medium MW resulted in blend membranes with PWF of 1000 L.m− 2 h− 1 and rejection of 95%, demonstrating the practical efficacy of the approach. This was attributed to the counterbalance of TBC/PVC miscibility and TBC surface segregation effects using TBC with PS block having medium MW. Finally, designing and tuning the MW of hydrophilic/hydrophobic segments of TBC is promising for the development of PVC UF blend membranes with high water purification performance.Keywords: Ultrafiltration, Blend membrane, Polyvinyl chloride, Amphiphilic triblock copolymer, Antifouling propertiesSubject terms: Chemistry, Engineering, Environmental sciences, Materials science

Introduction

Ultrafiltration (UF) membranes are widely used to remove colloidal and macromolecular compounds from water thanks to their low energy consumption, simplicity, and high efficiency1–3. Various polymers such as polyethersulfone (PES)4, polysulfone (PSF)5, poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF)6, and poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC)3 are used to prepare commercial UF membranes. Among them, PVC, which possesses low cost, excellent film formability, and good chemical/mechanical stability has attracted considerable attention3,7–9. However, PVC membranes suffer from low hydrophilicity and high fouling propensity9–11. Irreversible fouling deteriorates the membrane separation performance, thus increasing the operating cost, shortening the membrane life, and decreasing the productivity12–14. Hydrophilic modification techniques including polymer modification8, surface grafting/coating15,16, and blending17–19 can be used to overcome the abovementioned problems so far. Moreover, introducing hydrophilic nanoparticles (NPs) into the membrane matrix has been shown as an effective method in fouling mitigation3. However, although surface grafting/coating can significantly enhance the surface pore size and porosity, they hardly modify inner pore walls9,20. Moreover, surface grafting/coating, as post-processing methods usually increase the cost and steps of membrane fabrication. On the other hand, blending hydrophilic polymers such as polyethylene glycol (PEG) with the base polymer of membranes is considered one of the most effective methods for overcoming fouling issues21. The leaching of hydrophilic polymer additives during membrane preparation via nonsolvent-induced phase separation (NIPS) and application poses many practical and environmental problems. To avoid this, hydrophilic polymer additives are suggested to be used in the form of amphiphilic block copolymers with hydrophobic segments. The hydrophobic part of the copolymer is anchored in the bulk of the base polymer of the membrane thereby reducing the leaching probability, while the migration of the hydrophilic part to the surface improves the membrane hydrophilicity. For example, Monsefi et al.18 used di-block copolymers of polyethylene glycol/poly(1,2-dichloroethylene), PEG-b-PDCE, as an additive to prepare PVC-based UF membranes by the NIPS method for oily wastewater treatment. Compared to pure PVC UF membrane, those containing 0.075 wt% of the di-block copolymer showed significant improvements in the pure water flux (PWF) (from 184 to 749 L.m− 2 h− 1) and oil rejection (from 94.7 to 99.8%). However, the corresponding improvements in the antifouling properties (i.e., total fouling, irreversible fouling, and reversible fouling) were moderate.

Chen et al.22 introduced polysulfone-b-poly ethylene glycol, PSF-b-PEG, amphiphilic copolymers into a PSF membrane and investigated the UF performance of the blend membranes. The water flux (174 L m− 2 h− 1) and flux recovery ratio (39%) of the PSF UF membrane increased by adding 4 wt% PSF-b-PEG amphiphilic copolymer to 359 L m− 2 h− 1 and 45%, respectively, while maintaining a rejection of 97.8% for bovine serum albumin (BSA). Chen et al.23, also studied the effect of a styrene-maleic anhydride (SMA) amphiphilic copolymer on the separation performance of chlorinated PVC (CPVC) UF membranes. The PWF (248 L m− 2 h− 1), BSA rejection (74%), and flux recovery ratio (55%) of the CPVC UF membrane increased by adding 2 wt% SMA amphiphilic copolymer in the casting solution, reaching 459 L m− 2 h− 1 for PWF, 97% for protein rejection, and 92% for FRR.

Wu et al.24 synthesized PVC-graft-poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate (PVC-g-PEGMA) as a diblock amphiphilic copolymer, via ATRP and incorporated it in PVC-based membranes to improve the antifouling properties. The presence of PVC-g-PEGMA led to an increase in PWF for all blended membranes, rising from 317 L m−2 h−1 to 472 L m−2 h−1, and improved sodium alginate (SA) rejection by more than 90%. They reported that the addition of 10%wt PVC-g-PEGMA to the PVC membrane enhanced FRR from 40% to 80%. In another work, the effect of PS-PEO di-block and PS-b-PEO-b-PS tri-block copolymers (TBCs) on the structure and performance of a PES membrane was investigated25. It was shown that the di-block copolymers are more effective in increasing PWF and pollutant rejection. However, the use of the tri-block copolymers improved the hydrophilicity and antifouling properties of the membranes more efficiently. This was attributed to the partial leakage of the di-block copolymers during the membrane fabrication process. In contrast, tri-block copolymers anchored more effectively within the PES matrix, thereby preventing leakage. Moreover, Zhao et al.26, synthesized poly(ethylene glycol methacrylate)-b-poly(methyl methacrylate)-b-poly(ethylene glycol methacrylate), PEGMA-b-PMMA-b-PEGMA, comb-like tri-block copolymer additives with hydrophilic segments of different lengths for the preparation of PVDF UF membranes. The blend membranes exhibited more water flux than the pure PVDF one with a small decay in BSA rejection, and the water flux increased with increasing the PEGMA segment length. In addition, the incorporation of amphiphilic TBC into the PVDF matrix increased FRR by 11% (from 86 to 97%). In another work27 they synthesized a polyacryloylmorpholine-b-poly (methyl methacrylate)-b-polyacryloylmorpholine tri-block copolymer (PACMO-b-PMMA-b-PACMO) through reversible addition-fragmentation chain transfer (RAFT) polymerization followed by its blending with PVDF to prepare an UF membrane using the NIPS method. The results showed improved PWF, BSA rejection, and antifouling properties for the blend membranes compared to those of the pure PVDF one. Furthermore, Chen et al.28 utilized N-Phenylmaleimide-styrene-maleic anhydride (NSM) ternary amphiphilic copolymer to modify the pore morphology of CPVC UF membranes and improve their antifouling performance. They found that NSM chains increased thermal instability of the membrane and promoted instantaneous demixing, leading a change in the cracked surface pores to rounded pores. This resulted in a 350% increase in PWF, along with the humic acid (HA) and SA rejections of more than 90% and 85%, respectively. The FRR of the modified membranes exceeded 90% and the reversible flux decline ratio decreased to 50%, indicating improved antifouling performance of the membranes.

Generally, although introducing di-block copolymers significantly increases the water flux of the membranes18,22,25, the improvement in the antifouling properties is moderate. Furthermore, blend membranes containing di-block copolymer additives suffer from poor long-term stability because these additives do not anchor strongly in the base polymer of the membranes18,22,25. On the other hand, membranes containing TBC additives may possess desirable antifouling properties and long-term stability caused by the high durability of TBCs in the membranes25. Furthermore, the length design of each block of TBCs needs to be further investigated to optimize its role as the PVC membrane additive.

In this research, we have used TBCs consisting of a central PEG block and two side PS blocks (PSx-PEGy-PSx) as additives for improving the antifouling properties of PVC UF membranes. The block copolymers were synthesized by the atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) method. Furthermore, the influence of the PEG and PS blocks molecular weights (MW) on the separation and antifouling performance of the membranes was studied.

Experimental

Materials

PVC resin (E-6834, Mw = 90 kDa, Arvand Petrochemical Co., Iran) was used as the based membrane material. Hydroxyl-terminated polyethylene glycol (PEG, Mn = 1000 g/mol and 4000 g/mol) and tetrahydrofuran (THF) were purchased from Merck. In addition, 2-bromo 2-isobutyryl bromide (BIBB), triethylamine (TEA), dichloromethane (DCM), N,N,N′,N″,N″-pentamethyl diethylenetriamine (PMDETA), copper(I) chloride (CuCl), sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Finally, sodium alginate (SA), as the model foulant, was provided by Sigma-Aldrich.

It’s noteworthy that SA is an extracellular polymeric substance, playing a significant role in membrane fouling during water treatment processes.​ Due to its propensity to cause fouling, SA is often utilized as a model foulant in ultrafiltration experiments29. An activated alumina column was used to remove the inhibitor from the styrene (St, Sigma-Aldrich) monomer.

Synthesis of Br-PEGy-Br macroinitiators

To prepare the bromo-capped PEG macroinitiator (Br-PEGy-Br) by an esterification reaction30,31, 4 g of PEG1 (4 mmol, Mn = 1000 g/mol) was dissolved in 50 mL DCM. After that, 2.8 mL (15 mmol) TEA was poured into the solution; then the whole system was placed in an ice bath and purged with nitrogen (N2). After cooling to 0 °C, 1.24 mL (10 mmol) BIBB was added dropwise to the above solution. The resultant solution was stirred for 1 h at 0 °C followed by stirring for 24 h at 25 °C and then at 70 °C to complete the evaporation of the solvent. After re-dissolving in DCM, the product was washed three times with 50 mL saturated NaHCO3, followed by decanting of oil/water phases. The final product (Br-PEGy-Br) was then dried using MgSO4, followed by placing under a vacuum for 24 h at 60 °C. The same procedure was used to synthesize the Br-PEG2-Br (PEG Mn = 4000 g/mol) macroinitiator. Table 1 shows the amount of the reactants used to prepare macroinitiators.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, PerkinElmer) and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H NMR, BRUKER 400 MHz Ultra Shield, 40 °C, in deuterated chloroform, CDCL3) were utilized to study the structure of macroinitiators and PSx-PEGy-PSx TBCs. Effect of PSx-PEGy-PSx TBCs on the viscosity and cloud points of the casting solutions

Figure 4 shows the effect of amphiphilic TBCs on the viscosity of PVC casting solutions. Generally, TBCs reduce the viscosity of the PVC casting solution. It is reasonable that replacing part of the high molecular weight PVC with a lower molecular weight TBC resulted in a decrease in the viscosity of the casting solutions. Furthermore, in both the PS-PEG1-PS and PS-PEG2-PS copolymer series, increasing the PS chain length led to a further reduction in viscosity of the casting solutions. Similarly, the penetration of PS chains between PVC chains decreases the viscosity of the casting solutions by spacing between them, which is intensified by increasing the length of the PS chains. As can be seen, the depression in the viscosity is increased by increasing the molecular weight of the PS blocks, while it is decreased by increasing the molecular weight of the PEG block. Compared to the interaction strength of PVC-NMP (solubility parameter difference, ΔδPVC−NMP = 1.9 MPa1/2), that of PS-NMP (ΔδPS−NMP = 12.3 MPa1/2) is lower (Table 5)36. Therefore, increasing the molecular weight of PS blocks results in the reduction of NMP solvent quality and prevents further interactions of PVC with NMP, thus, reducing the solution viscosity. However, increasing the molecular weight of the PEG block leads to stronger molecular chain entanglement, which increases the viscosity of the dope solution37. In addition, the molecular weights of TBCs synthesized with higher molecular weight PEG (i.e. PSy-PEG2-PSy TBCs) are higher than those of TBCs synthesized with lower molecular weight one (i.e. PSy-PEG1-PSy TBCs), which can also enhance chain entanglements (see Table 4).

To evaluate the thermodynamic behavior of the casting solutions during phase separation, the cloud point diagrams of (PVC + TBC)/NMP/water ternary systems were analyzed (Fig. 5). As can be seen, the incorporation of TBCs in the casting solution brings the cloud points closer to the polymer/NMP axis, indicating a decrease in the thermodynamic stability of the blend casting solutions compared to that of the neat PVC casting solution, and thus a reduction in water tolerance of dope solutions. Meanwhile, PSx-PEG1-PSx TBCs caused a further reduction in the thermodynamic stability of the casting solutions compared to that caused by PSx-PEG2-PSx TBCs. This can be ascribed to stronger interactions of PEG2, than PEG1, with NMP37. On the other hand, increasing the MW of PS blocks in TBCs reduces the stability of the dope solutions. This could be attributed to the solubility reduction of TBCs in NMP due to the decrease in the entropy contribution to the Gibbs free energy with increasing molecular weight of the PS block (Table 5)38.

Hydrophilicity of the membranes

Figure 6 shows the effect of adding amphiphilic TBCs on the hydrophilicity of the membranes. The water contact angle of the pure PVC membrane was 89°, indicating its hydrophobic nature3. As can be seen, the introduction of amphiphilic copolymers into the PVC matrix increased the hydrophilicity of the membranes (Fig. 6). This is attributed to the segregation of PEG chain segments on the membrane surface during the NIPS process25,39. The hydrophilic functional groups of PEG (e.g. ether and hydroxyl groups) enhance the interaction of the membrane surfaces with water molecules, which reduces the water contact angle of the resultant membranes25,40. As can be seen, at a constant PEG MW, the surface hydrophilicity first increased and then decreased by the PS MW increase. By increasing PS MW from PS1 to PS2, the TBC/PVC miscibility and the casting solution/nonsolvent affinity decreased (Fig. 5). These effects could lead to higher driving force for TBCs surface segregation and instantaneous demixing, respectively, during NIPS, increasing the concentration of PEG segments on the surface of the membranes, thus improving their hydrophilicity. On the other hand, a further increase in the PS MW (PS3-PEGy-PS3) led to anchoring PS chains of higher Mw in the bulk of the PVC solution and reduced their migration to the membrane surface, which eventually decreased the surface hydrophilicity. Furthermore, the high PS MW in PS3-PEGy-PS3 caused a considerable decrease in the percentage of the hydrophilic segment (Table 4) and thus the hydrophilicity of the corresponding blend membranes. Moreover, Fig. 6 shows that at a constant PS MW, increasing the PEG MW resulted in more hydrophilic membrane surfaces. This may be because that the hydrophilicity of PEG increases with its MW20,41. In addition, the molar percentage of the hydrophilic segment is higher in the PSx-PEG2-PSx copolymers.

Membrane morphology and structures

Figure 7 shows that the incorporation of TBCs into the PVC membranes increases the bulk porosity of the prepared membranes. Furthermore, PVC/PSx-PEG2-PSx membranes are more porous than PVC/PSx-PEG1-PSx membranes. This can be attributed to the more effective role of the PEG with higher MW and higher molar percentage of hydrophilic segments in enhancing the hydrophilicity (Fig. 6) and pore formation of the membranes. In addition, in the presence of TBCs with the same MW of PEG, the bulk porosity of the membrane first increases and then decreases with the increase in the PS MW. The initial enhancement in the bulk porosity can be due to the decreases in viscosity (Fig. 4) and thermodynamic stability (Fig. 5) of the casting solution by increasing PS MW. These reductions lead to instantaneous demixing8, which increases the bulk porosity of the membrane. On the other hand, although the higher molecular weight of the PS part (PS3-PEGy-PS3) led to a further decrease in the viscosity and thermodynamic stability of the casting solutions, it reduced the hydrophilicity (Fig. 6) and the solvent/nonsolvent exchange rate leading to the formation of a denser membrane3.

Bulk porosity of neat and TBCs containing PVC membranes.

Figures 8 and 9 show the surface and cross-sectional images of the pure and blend membranes containing 1wt% PSx-PEGy-PSx copolymers in their casting solutions. The cross-sectional images of the prepared membranes reveal a typical asymmetrical structure consisting of a top thin dense layer, a finger-like structure, and a macro-void structure beneath them3. Compared to pure PVC membrane, those containing PSx-PEGy-PSx copolymers show a higher density of finger-like channels and larger macropores. These differences in the structure of finger-like channels can be mainly attributed to the diffusion rate of solvent and nonsolvent during the NIPS process42. Accelerating the solvent/nonsolvent diffusion rate leads to the formation of finger-like channels while decreasing this diffusion rate mainly leads to the formation of more sponge-like structures7,8,42.

Membrane permeation and rejection performance

Figure 11 illustrates the PWF and SA rejection of the pure PVC membrane and those containing different TBCs in their casting solution. The PWF and SA rejection of pure PVC membrane are 160 L m− 2 h− 1 and 87%, respectively. In the presence of TBCs, PWF of the membranes increased and they all showed SA rejection higher than 95%. The increased PWF of blend membranes can be attributed to their higher bulk and surface porosity, improved hydrophilicity, more uniform surface pores, and more porous top selective layer as discussed above. Moreover, the high SA rejection of blend membranes can be ascribed to their hydrophilic surface and more uniform surface pores (Figs. 6 and 10).

Separation performance of the pure PVC and composite membranes.

As Fig. 11 shows, among the blend membranes, those containing PS2-PEGy-PS2 in their casting solution possess the highest PWF values. In other words, PVC/PS2-PEG1-PS2 showed a PWF value of 650 L m− 2 h− 1, which is 35% and 30% higher than those of the PVC/PS1-PEG1-PS1 and PVC/PS3-PEG1-PS3, respectively. Similarly, the PWF of the PVC/PS2-PEG2-PS2 membrane (1000 L m− 2 h− 1) is 17% and 61% greater than those of the PVC/PS1-PEG2-PS1 and PVC/PS3-PEG2-PS3, respectively. This is consistent with the higher bulk porosity (Fig. 7) as well as higher surface porosity and pore density (Table 6) of the PVC/ PS2-PEGy-PS2 membranes.

Figure 12 shows the PWF and rejection behavior of PVC/PS2-PEGy-PS2 membranes in comparison with those of other PVC-based blend membranes from the literature3,15,24,45–56. With regard to PWF and FRR, most PVC blended membranes are superior to neat PVC membranes due to enhanced hydrophilicity and/or morphological changes. Moreover, the PVC/PS2-PEG2-PS2 membrane shows a high PWF along with a high solute rejection performance.

PWF and rejection behavior of PVC/PS2-PEGy-PS2 membranes in comparison with those of other PVC-based blend membranes.

The antifouling properties of PVC and PVC/PSx-PEGy-PSx membranes were studied with a 5-cycle fouling cleaning process, and the results are shown in Fig. 13. As Fig. 13a shows, during the filtration of SA solution, the flux of all prepared membranes reduced sharply compared to the PWF in the first stage. This is attributed to the aggregation and deposition of SA molecules on the surface of the membranes, which form a cake layer blocking the surface pores. After backwashing, the membranes partially recovered their initial PWF due to irreversible fouling. Among the prepared membranes, the pure PVC membrane showed the lowest recovered flux, indicating its high potential to be fouled due to inherent hydrophobicity. On the other hand, after the 5-cycle test, the PWF of the blend membranes remained high with a 25, 16, 26, 23, 12 and 12% decrease, compared to the initial PWF, for PVC/PS1-PEG1-PS1, PVC/PS2-PEG1-PS2, PVC/PS3-PEG1-PS3, PVC/PS1-PEG2-PS1, PVC/PS2-PEG2-PS2 and PVC/PS3-PEG2-PS3 membranes, respectively. Generally, the incorporation of amphiphilic TBCs into the PVC matrix enhanced the antifouling properties of the prepared membranes.

(a) Flux decline with time and (b) antifouling properties of pristine PVC and composite membranes containing PSx-PEGy-PSx TBCs.

The antifouling properties of the membranes in terms of RFR, IFR, FRR, and TFR (Fig. 13b) show that the FRR and RFR of the pure PVC membrane (47% and 32%, respectively) are enhanced by blending with TBCs and reach the highest values of 88% and 71%, respectively, for the PVC/PS2-PEG2-PS2 membrane. Accordingly, one can conclude that TBCs effectively removed fouling of the PVC membrane after water cleaning. As Fig. 13b shows, in each group of membranes containing TBCs with the same PEG MW, that with medium PS MW shows the best fouling resistance in terms of both the RFR and FRR values. This points again to the fact that PEG-based TBCs with blocks of medium PS MW are optimal additives to improve the separation properties and antifouling characteristics of PVC-based membranes.

Conclusion

In this study, PSx-PEGy-PSx amphiphilic TBCs with different hydrophilic PEG and hydrophobic PS block MW were successfully synthesized by the ATRP method and blended with PVC to prepare PVC UF blend membranes by the NIPS method. The introduction of the hydrophilic PEG segment increased the surface and bulk porosity, mean pore size, and hydrophilicity of the blend membranes. As a result, the blend membranes showed higher PWF and SA rejection than the neat PVC membrane. In addition, the antifouling performance of the PVC membrane was improved by the blending method. TBCs containing PEG with higher MW improved the PWF and fouling resistance more than TBCs containing PEG with lower MW. Moreover, due to the interaction between TBC/PVC miscibility and TBC surface segregation effects, TBCs with PS block having medium MW (PS2) resulted in the PWF of 1000 L m− 2 h− 1 and rejection of 95%, demonstrating their suitable performance. Thus, the knowledge obtained in the present study provides design clues for superior PVC UF membranes using TBCs with blocks having optimized MW.

On the dynamics of weighted composition operators II
Nilson C. Bernardes Jr, Antonio Bonilla, João V. A. Pinto

Abstract
We establish complete characterizations of various notions of expansivity for weighted composition operators on a very general class of locally convex spaces of continuous functions. This class includes several classical classes of continuous function spaces, such as the Banach spaces C 0 ​ ( X ) C_{0}(X) of continuous scalar-valued functions vanishing at infinity on a Hausdorff locally compact space X X , endowed with the sup norm, and the locally convex spaces C ​ ( X ) c C(X)_{c} of continuous scalar-valued functions on a completely regular space X X , endowed with the compact-open topology. We also obtain complete characterizations of various notions of expansivity for weighted composition operators on L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) spaces, thereby complementing and extending previously known results in the unweighted case. Finally, we establish a conjugation between weighted and unweighted composition operators in the case of dissipative systems on L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) spaces and apply it to the study of several dynamical properties.



Keywords:
Weighted composition operators; Continuous function spaces;
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces;
Expansivity; Dissipative systems; Chaos.

Introduction

This work is dedicated to the study of the dynamics of weighted composition operators

C_{w,f}(\varphi)=w\cdot(\varphi\circ f)

on certain general classes of function spaces and can be considered as a continuation of our recent article
[
5
]
.
Due to the important role played by weighted composition operators in operator theory and its applications, the dynamics of these operators has been extensively investigated by various authors in different contexts over the last few decades.
We refer the reader to the recent articles
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
11
,
20
]
,
where many additional references can be found.

Our goal is to investigate various notions of expansivity for weighted composition operators on a very general class of
continuous function spaces and on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces, as well as several dynamical properties of
weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces in the dissipative case.

Given a metric space
M
with metric
d
, recall that a homeomorphism
h:M\to M
is said to be
expansive
if it admits an
expansivity constant

c>0
, which means that for any pair
x,y
of distinct points in
M
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

d(h^{n}(x),h^{n}(y))>c.

This concept was introduced by Utz
[
28
]
and is one of the fundamental concepts in the modern theory of dynamical systems.
In the setting of linear dynamics, Eisenberg
[
18
]
proved that an invertible operator on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is expansive if and only if it has no eigenvalue on the unit circle.
In the early 1970s, Eisenberg and Hedlund
[
19
]
and Hedlund
[
22
]
studied expansivity for operators on Banach spaces.
They introduced the notion of uniform expansivity and proved that an invertible operator on a Banach space is uniformly expansive if and only if its approximate point spectrum does not intersect the unit circle.
In 2000, Mazur
[
27
]
proved that an invertible normal operator
T
on a Hilbert space is expansive if and only if
T^{*}T
has no eigenvalue on the unit circle.
This subject was revisited by Bernardes et al.
[
7
]
in 2018, where many results were obtained, including complete characterizations of the various notions of expansivity for weighted shifts on classical Banach sequence spaces.
Since then, several works on expansivity for operators have been developed and interesting results have been obtained.
For instance, it was proved that the equalities

hyperbolicity

\displaystyle=\text{ expansivity }+\text{ shadowing}

\displaystyle=\text{ average expasivity }+\text{ average shadowing}

hold for invertible operators on any Banach space
[
2
,
9
,
12
]
.
The notions of expansivity have been extended to operators on arbitrary Hausdorff locally convex spaces in the recent papers
[
6
,
8
]
, where investigations on these concepts in this much more general setting have been initiated.
Additional references on expansivity in linear dynamics include

[
1
,
4
,
14
,
24
,
25
]
.

Let us now describe the organization of the article.

In Section
2
, we will present the necessary prerequisites for the development of this work.

In Section
3
, we will establish complete characterizations of various notions of expansivity
for weighted composition operators on a very general class of locally convex spaces of continuous functions,
namely, the spaces
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
of all continuous functions
\varphi
from a completely regular space
X

into a Hausdorff locally convex space
Y
such that
\varphi(B)
is a bounded
subset of
Y
for each
B\in\mathfrak{S}
(
\mathfrak{S}
being a given bornology on
X
), endowed with the topology of
\mathfrak{S}
-convergence.
In fact, our characterizations are also valid for certain subspaces of
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
that we call
admissible
.
Applications to several classical classes of continuous function spaces, including the Banach spaces
C_{0}(X)
and the locally convex spaces
C(X)_{c}
, will be presented.
As specific examples, we will consider weighted shifts and weighted translation operators.

In Section
4
, we will establish complete characterizations of various notions of expansivity for weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces, thereby complementing and extending previous characterizations due to Maiuriello
[
25
]
in the unweighted case. We will illustrate the general results by presenting applications to weighted shifts and weighted translation operators.

In Section
5
, we will study the dynamics of weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces in the dissipative case.
In the unweighted case, dissipative composition operators have been studied in the recent papers

[
5
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
25
,
26
]
.
These operators behave very much like weighted shifts.
In fact, it was shown in
[
14
]
that any dissipative composition operator
satisfying a certain technical condition, called
bounded distortion
, has a certain specific weighted shift as a factor
and for many dynamical properties, the composition operator has the property if and only if so does the associated weighted shift.
For this reason, these composition operators were called
shift-like
in
[
14
]
.
It is well-known that weighted shifts on
\ell^{p}
spaces are conjugate
to unweighted shifts on weighted
\ell^{p}
spaces
[
21
, Section 4.1]
.
In a certain sense, one can transfer the weights from the shift to the space.
Our main goal in Section
5
is to prove that something similar holds for composition operators
on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces in the dissipative case.
More precisely, we will prove that any dissipative weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on a space
L^{p}(\mu)

is conjugate to an unweighted composition operator
C_{f}
on a space
L^{p}(\nu)
by means of an isometric isomorphism,
where the measure
\nu
is given by a specific formula involving the measure
\mu
and the weight function
w
.
This allows us to transfer known results about dissipative composition operators to the weighted case, and we will illustrate
this process with several dynamical properties, including frequent hypercyclicity, Devaney chaos and the shadowing property.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout
\mathbb{K}
denotes either the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers or the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers,

\mathbb{Z}
denotes the ring of integers,
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of all positive integers, and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Given a seminorm
\|\cdot\|
on a vector space
Y
, the
unit sphere
of
\|\cdot\|
is defined by

S_{\|\cdot\|}=\{y\in Y:\|y\|=1\}.

If
Y
is a normed space with norm
\|\cdot\|
, then we also write
S_{Y}
instead of
S_{\|\cdot\|}
.

Let
Y
be a Hausdorff locally convex space over
\mathbb{K}
.
By an
operator
on
Y
, we mean a continuous linear map
T:Y\to Y
.
Such an operator is said to be
invertible
if it has a continuous inverse.
We denote by
L(Y)
(resp.
GL(Y)
) the set of all operators (resp. invertible operators) on
Y
.
Let
(\|\cdot\|_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
be a family of seminorms that induces the topology of
Y
.
Recall that
T\in GL(Y)
is said to be
topologically expansive
if, for each
y\in Y\backslash\{0\}
,
there exists
\alpha\in I
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|T^{n}y\|_{\alpha}=\infty.

Moreover,
T
is said to be
uniformly topologically expansive
if, for each
\alpha\in I
, there exists
\beta\in I
such that
we can write
S_{\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}}=S^{+}_{\alpha}\cup S^{-}_{\alpha}
, where

\|T^{n}y\|_{\beta}\to\infty\text{ uniformly on }S^{+}_{\alpha}\text{ as }n\to\infty,

\|T^{-n}y\|_{\beta}\to\infty\text{ uniformly on }S^{-}_{\alpha}\text{ as }n\to\infty.

These concepts of expansivity were introduced in
[
6
]
as generalizations of the classical notions
of expansivity and uniform expansivity for invertible operators on Banach spaces
[
19
]
.
The word “topologically” was used to distinguish the above notion of expansivity from the usual one in the metric space setting,
because they may differ in the case of a metrizable locally convex space (or even a Fréchet space) endowed with a compatible invariant
metric
[
6
, Example 32]
, although they always coincide in normed spaces.
However, as in
[
8
]
, we will omit the word “topologically”
and simply write “expansive” and “uniformly expansive” for the above notions.

A concept of average expansivity for invertible operators on Banach spaces was introduced and investigated in
[
2
]
.
It was extended to the context of Hausdorff locally convex spaces in the recent paper
[
8
]
, by means of the following
definition:
T\in GL(Y)
is said to be
average expansive
if, for each
y\in Y\backslash\{0\}
, there exists
\alpha\in I
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|T^{j}y\|_{\alpha}\Big)=\infty.

For operators that are not necessarily invertible, we have the following variations of the above notions of expansivity

[
6
,
8
]
:
T\in L(Y)
is said to be

•

positively expansive
if, for each
y\in Y\backslash\{0\}
, there exists
\alpha\in I
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|T^{n}x\|_{\alpha}=\infty.

•

average positively expansive
if, for each
y\in Y\backslash\{0\}
, there exists
\alpha\in I
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|T^{j}y\|_{\alpha}\Big)=\infty.

•

uniformly positively expansive
if, for each
\alpha\in I
, there exists
\beta\in I
such that

\|T^{n}x\|_{\beta}\to\infty\text{ uniformly on }S_{\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}}\text{ as }n\to\infty.

All the above notions of expansivity do not depend on the choice of the family of seminorms inducing the topology of
Y
,
so they are completely determined by the topology of
Y
.

3
Expansive weighted composition operators on locally convex spaces of continuous functions

Throughout this section, we fix a completely regular space
X
and a bornology
\mathfrak{S}
on
X
, that is,
a collection of subsets of
X
that contains the singletons and is stable under the passage to subsets and the formation of finite unions.
We also fix a Hausdorff locally convex space
Y
over
\mathbb{K}
,
Y\neq\{0\}
,
and a family
(\|\cdot\|_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of seminorms that induces its topology.

C(X;Y)
denotes the vector space over
\mathbb{K}
of all continuous functions from
X
into
Y
, and
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
is the vector subspace
of
C(X;Y)
consisting of all
\varphi\in C(X;Y)
such that
\varphi(B)
is a bounded subset of
Y
for each
B\in\mathfrak{S}
.
We consider
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
endowed with the topology of
\mathfrak{S}
-convergence (i.e., the topology of uniform convergence on the sets of
\mathfrak{S}
),
which is the Hausdorff locally convex topology induced by the family of seminorms given by

\|\varphi\|_{B,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in B}\|\varphi(x)\|_{\alpha}\ \ \ (\varphi\in C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)\text{ and }(B,\alpha)\in\mathfrak{S}\times I).

Note that if
\mathcal{B}
is a base of the bornology
\mathfrak{S}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathfrak{S}
and each element of
\mathfrak{S}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{B}
), then the subfamily
(\|\cdot\|_{B,\alpha})_{(B,\alpha)\in\mathcal{B}\times I}
also induces the topology of
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
.
For each collection
\mathscr{C}
of subsets of
X
and each
B\subset X
, we define

\mathscr{C}_{B}=\{A\in\mathscr{C}:A\cap B\neq\varnothing\}.

For each
B\subset X
and each bounded function
\phi:B\to\mathbb{K}
, we define

\|\phi\|_{B}=\sup_{x\in B}|\phi(x)|,

where we consider this supremum to be
0
if
B=\varnothing
.
We also fix a homeomorphism
f:X\to X
satisfying
f(\mathfrak{S})=\mathfrak{S}
and a zero-free continuous function
w:X\to\mathbb{K}

such that both
w
and
1/w
are bounded on each set
B\in\mathfrak{S}
.
It is easy to check that the
weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}(\varphi)=w\cdot(\varphi\circ f)

is a well-defined invertible operator on
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
with

(C_{w,f})^{-1}=C_{\widetilde{w},f^{-1}},\ \ \text{ where }\widetilde{w}=\frac{1}{w\circ f^{-1}}\cdot

We associate to
w
and
f
the following bilateral sequence of continuous functions from
X
into
\mathbb{K}
:

w^{(0)}=1
,
w^{(1)}=w
,
w^{(-1)}=\widetilde{w}
and, for each
n\geq 2
,

w^{(n)}=w\cdot(w\circ f)\cdots(w\circ f^{n-1})\ \ \text{ and }\ \ w^{(-n)}=\widetilde{w}\cdot(\widetilde{w}\circ f^{-1})\cdots(\widetilde{w}\circ f^{-(n-1)}).

Finally, we say that a subspace
M
of
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
is
admissible
if the following conditions hold:

(C1)

C_{w,f}(M)=M
;

(C2)

For each point
a\in X
, each neighborhood
O
of
a
in
X
and each vector
y\in Y
,
there exists a continuous function
\phi:X\to[0,1]
such that

\phi(a)=1,\ \ \ \phi(X\backslash O)=\{0\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \phi\otimes y\in M,

where
\phi\otimes y:X\to Y
is given by
(\phi\otimes y)(x)=\phi(x)y
.

We now establish the main result of this section, namely, characterizations of the various notions of expansivity
for invertible weighted composition operators on admissible subspaces of
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
.
Note that, since the topological space
X
is completely regular, it is clear that
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
is an admissible subspace of itself.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be an admissible subspace of
C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
,
\mathcal{B}
a base of the bornology
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathcal{O}
a base of the topology of

\varnothing\not\in\mathcal{B}

For the invertible weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}

(a)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|w^{(n)}\|_{B\cap f^{-n}(O)}=\infty.

(b)

O\in\mathcal{O}

B\in\mathcal{B}

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-j}(O)}\Big)=\infty.

If

Example 5
.

If
Y=\mathbb{K}
and
\mathfrak{S}
is the bornology of all finite subsets of
X

C_{\mathfrak{S}}(X;Y)
is the classical locally convex space

C(X)_{s}
of all continuous functions
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{K}
endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence.
By choosing any base
\mathcal{O}
of the topology of
X
consisting of nonempty open sets, it follows from Theorem
1
that
the following characterizations hold for the invertible weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
C(X)_{s}
:

(a)

C_{w,f}
is expansive if and only if, for each

, there exists

\sup_{n\in J}|w^{(n)}(b)|=\infty,

where

.

(b)

C_{w,f}
is average expansive if and only if, for each

, there exists

such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j\in J_{n}}|w^{(j)}(b)|\Big)=\infty,

where
J_{n}=\{j\in\{-n,\ldots,n\}:f^{n}(b)\in O\}
(

).

(c)

C_{w,f}
is uniformly expansive if and only if, for each

,
there exists
B\subset X
finite such that we can write
\mathcal{O}_{\{a\}}=\mathcal{O}_{\{a\}}^{+}\cup\mathcal{O}_{\{a\}}^{-}
, where

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{O\in\mathcal{O}_{\{a\}}^{+}}\|w^{(n)}\|_{B\cap f^{-n}(O)}=\infty\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{O\in\mathcal{O}_{\{a\}}^{-}}\|w^{(-n)}\|_{B\cap f^{n}(O)}=\infty.

In the next result, we will see that certain additional hypotheses imply that it is sufficient to test the conditions in (a) and (b)
of Theorem
1
for a smaller collection of sets
O
in
\mathcal{O}
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
M
,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{O}
be as in Theorem
1
. Suppose that both
w
and
1/w
are bounded on
X

and that there exists a nonempty open set
W
in
X
such that
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{n}(W)
is dense in
X
.
For the invertible weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
M
, the following statements hold:

(a)

C_{w,f}
is expansive if and only if, for each
O\in\mathcal{O}
with
O\subset W
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|w^{(n)}\|_{B\cap f^{-n}(O)}=\infty.

(b)

C_{w,f}
is average expansive if and only if, for each
O\in\mathcal{O}
with
O\subset W
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-j}(O)}\Big)=\infty.

Proof.

We shall present the proof of (b) (the proof of (a) is simpler and is left to the reader).
Since the necessity of the condition follows trivially from Theorem
1
, let us prove its sufficiency.
Take an arbitrary
O\in\mathcal{O}
. Since
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{n}(W)
is dense in
X
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

O\cap f^{k}(W)\neq\varnothing
.
Hence, there exists
O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
such that
O^{\prime}\subset f^{-k}(O)\cap W
.
Since
O^{\prime}\subset W
, the condition in (b) gives
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-j}(O^{\prime})}\Big)=\infty,

which implies that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-(j+k)}(O)}\Big)=\infty.

(11)

By hypothesis, there exists a constant
C>1
such that
C^{-1}\leq|w(x)|\leq C
for all
x\in X
.
A simple computation shows that

\|w^{(j+m)}\|_{A}\leq C^{|m|}\|w^{(j)}\|_{A}\ \ \text{ for all }A\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }j,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus,

\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-(j+k)}(O)}\leq C^{|k|}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j+k)}\|_{B\cap f^{-(j+k)}(O)}\leq C^{|k|}\sum_{j=-(n+|k|)}^{n+|k|}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-j}(O)}.

In view of (

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\|w^{(j)}\|_{B\cap f^{-j}(O)}\Big)=\infty.

Hence, by Theorem

,

is average expansive.

Example 7
.

Consider a
bilateral weighted backward shift

•

]
and
[

]
, respectively.
The characterization of uniform expansivity was also obtained in

7
]

and can be recovered from Example

(c).

.

With the help of

4
Expansive weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)

Throughout this section we fix a real number
p\in[1,\infty)
and an arbitrary positive measure space
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
,
unless otherwise specified. We denote by
\mathfrak{F}
the set of all measurable sets
B\in\mathfrak{M}
satisfying
0<\mu(B)<\infty
.

L^{p}(\mu)
denotes the Banach space over
\mathbb{K}
of all
\mathbb{K}
-valued
p
-integrable functions on
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)

endowed with the
p
-norm

\|\varphi\|_{p}=\Big(\int_{X}|\varphi|^{p}d\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We fix a
weight function

w:X\to\mathbb{K}
, that is, a measurable map satisfying

\varphi\cdot w\in L^{p}(\mu)\ \text{ for all }\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu).

We also fix a bimeasurable map
f:X\to X
(i.e.,
f(B)\in\mathfrak{M}
and
f^{-1}(B)\in\mathfrak{M}
whenever
B\in\mathfrak{M}
)
for which the
weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}(\varphi)=w\cdot(\varphi\circ f)

is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
L^{p}(\mu)
.
It is not difficult to show that this is the case if and only if there is a constant
c>0
such that

\int_{B}|w|^{p}d\mu\leq c\,\mu(f(B))\ \text{ for every }B\in\mathfrak{M}.

Since we are primarily interested in invertible operators, we will also assume that
f
is bijective and that
\widetilde{w}=\frac{1}{w\circ f^{-1}}

is a well-defined measurable map on
X
satisfying

\varphi\cdot\widetilde{w}\in L^{p}(\mu)\ \text{ for all }\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu)

and

\int_{B}|\widetilde{w}|^{p}d\mu\leq\tilde{c}\,\mu(f^{-1}(B))\ \text{ for every }B\in\mathfrak{M},

where
\tilde{c}>0
is a constant. In this way,
C_{w,f}
is an invertible operator on
L^{p}(\mu)
with

(C_{w,f})^{-1}=C_{\widetilde{w},f^{-1}}.

We associate to
p
,
w
and
f
the following bilateral sequence of positive measures on
(X,\mathfrak{M})
:

\mu_{n}(B)=\int_{B}|w^{(n)}|^{p}d\mu\ \ \ \ \ (B\in\mathfrak{M},n\in\mathbb{Z}),

where
w^{(0)}=1
,
w^{(1)}=w
,
w^{(-1)}=\widetilde{w}
,

w^{(n)}=(w\circ f^{n-1})\cdots(w\circ f)\cdot w\ \text{ and }\ w^{(-n)}=(\widetilde{w}\circ f^{-(n-1)})\cdots(\widetilde{w}\circ f^{-1})\cdot\widetilde{w}\ \ \ (n\geq 2).

We now establish the main result of this section, namely,
characterizations of the various notions of expansivity for invertible weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
.

Theorem 9
.

For the invertible weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
L^{p}(\mu)
, the following statements hold:

(a)

C_{w,f}
is expansive if and only if

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B))=\infty\ \text{ for all }B\in\mathfrak{F}.

(b)

C_{w,f}
is average expansive if and only if

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B))^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big)=\infty\ \text{ for all }B\in\mathfrak{F}.

(c)

C_{w,f}
is uniformly expansive if and only if
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{F}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{F}^{-}
, where

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathfrak{F}^{+}}\frac{\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B))}{\mu(B)}=\infty\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathfrak{F}^{-}}\frac{\mu_{-n}(f^{n}(B))}{\mu(B)}=\infty.

Remark 12

For notions of expansivity that do not require the operator to be invertible,

Definition 13

The previous theorem provides us with a method for extending results
about dissipative unweighted composition operators to the weighted case.
We will illustrate this method with the results below. First, let us recall some definitions.
The
lower density
of a set
A\subset\mathbb{N}
is defined by

\operatorname{\underline{dens}}(A)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{card}(A\cap[1,n])}{n}\cdot

Let

and

N(U,V)=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:T^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\varnothing\}.

The operator

is said to be:

•

(resp.

is nonempty (resp. co-finite)

and

are nonempty open sets in

•

Devaney chaotic
if it is topologically transitive and has a dense set of periodic points.

•

hypercyclic
(resp.

(resp. has positive lower density) for every nonempty open set

.

•

recurrent
(resp.

•

Li-Yorke chaotic

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}y-T^{n}z\|=0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}y-T^{n}z\|>0.

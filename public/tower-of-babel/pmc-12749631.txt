Constructing optical soliton wave structure and modulation instability analysis for coupled fractional Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel equation with Kerr’s law nonlinearity
Authors: Arezu Aghazadeh, Mehrdad Lakestani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this paper, the optical soliton wave structure and other types of soliton solutions are investigated. The coupled fractional Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel equation explains the wave pulses’ physical features in optical fibres along with two vector solitons. The Jacobi elliptic function expansion scheme is utilized to find various kinds of optical solitons. This scheme is among the most powerful techniques for creating numerous exact solutions for different nonlinear partial differential equations. Also, the generalized \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tan (\theta (\xi )/2)$$\end{document}-expansion method is used to get the exact solutions. Firstly, the wave transform converts the considered equations into nonlinear ordinary differential equations. Then, the new exact solutions are developed as periodic, dark, combined hyperbolic, and rational form functions. By employing the symbolic computation to the mentioned scheme the obtained solutions are investigated. The coupled fractional Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel model is analyzed because of density plot and two-dimensional plot. The modulation instability analysis is investigated. It offers theoretical application value for the study of complex wave dynamics in various scientific domains, such as fluid dynamics, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics.

Introduction

Fractional order differential equations as part of different nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) are highly useful tools for modeling a variety of phenomena in physics, engineering, mathematical biology, fluid mechanics, electro- chemistry, and other science; see1–4 and the references therein. In1, researchers examined the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to fractional order differential equations. It is essential to notice that most differential equations with fractional orders lack closed-form solutions. Therefore, suggesting new methods for finding these equations’ numerical solutions is crucial. The development of numerical techniques for solving fractional order differential equations has attracted much interest due to the growing applications. The solution of fractional order differential equations can be achieved through a variety of numerical techniques, including the operational method5, the variational iteration method6, the finite difference method7, the homotopy perturbation method8 and the Sinc-Legendre collocation method9.

This paper presents novel fractional-order transformation for solving time fractional partial differential equations (TSFPDEs). Different classes of fractional partial differential equations (FPDEs) can be solved analytically and numerically are as follows: In10, Uddin and Haq introduce radial basis functions (RBFs) approximation method for time fractional advection-diffusion equation on a bounded domain. Bu et al.11 introduced a space-time finite element method for the multi-term time-space fractional diffusion equation. Zhou and Xu12 solve the time- fractional convection-diffusion equations with variable coefficients by the Chebyshev wavelets collocation method. Dehestani et al.13 used Legendre-Lagueree functions for solving linear and nonlinear TSFPDEs with variable coefficients. Chen et al.14 utilized Haar wavelet for solving a class of space-time fractional convection-diffusion equations with variable coefficients. Bhawry et al.15 solved the time-fractional diffusion equation by spectral tau algorithm based on the Jacobi operational matrix. Sarvestani et al.16 used the Galerkin method based on the second kind of Chebyshev wavelets for solving the multi-term time-fractional diffusion-wave equation. Rahimkhani et al.17 used Bernoulli wavelet for solving fractional delay differential equations. Usman et al.18 used shifted Gegenbauer polynomials for solving delay differential equations of fractional-order.

Nonlinear wave equations are indispensable tools in the study of complex physical systems, as they model the interplay of various forces that shape wave dynamics4,19,20. These equations provide a mathematical framework to understand phenomena such as the propagation of light in optical fibers, the motion of water waves in shallow or deep fluids, and the intricate behaviors of plasma in high-energy astrophysical and laboratory environments22,23. Unlike linear equations, which are limited to describing simple waveforms, nonlinear equations account for interactions between waves, leading to phenomena such as wave steepening, dispersion, and energy localization4,24,25. These nonlinear effects are essential in capturing the richness of wave behavior observed in nature and technology27,64.

One of the most intriguing outcomes of nonlinear wave equations is the formation of solitary waves or solitons28,29. Solitons are self-reinforcing waveforms that maintain their shape and energy while traveling over long distances30,31. This stability arises from a delicate balance between nonlinearity, which tends to steepen the wave, and dispersion, which tends to spread it out. Solitons are of particular importance because of their practical applications in fields such as optical communication, where they enable high-capacity data transmission, and in hydrodynamics, where they describe wave patterns in oceans and channels. Their robustness also makes them valuable in understanding energy transport in nonlinear media and exploring fundamental wave dynamics32,33. The study of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation has been an area of significant interest due to its ability to support diverse solitary wave solutions34. Bright solitons, characterized by localized peaks of intensity, arise when nonlinearity balances anomalous dispersion35. These solitons are critical in applications like optical fiber communications, where their stability ensures minimal signal degradation36. Conversely, dark solitons, which manifest as localized intensity dips on a continuous wave background, emerge under conditions of normal dispersion. Dark solitons have been observed in both optics and hydrodynamics, providing insights into wave dynamics in various media. Singular solitons represent another intriguing class of solutions37. These solitons, distinguished by singularities in their profiles, exhibit unique mathematical and physical properties. Hybrid solutions, such as bright-dark solitons, where bright and dark components coexist, add to the diversity of wave phenomena described by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation38.

A mixed problem with time derivative in the boundary conditions for the second order inhomogeneous parabolic equation with complex coefficients was considered51. Authors of52 presented the analytical and numerical solutions to the nonlinear fractional biological population equation with the fractional derivative Atangana Baleanu using the Kamal Adomian decomposition method. The principles of optimal design of mechanical drives of lifting units were investigated a significant impact on the optimization criterion53. Conclusion

In this work, we have successfully applied the JEFES to derive several new exact solutions to coupled fractional Lakshmanan-Porsezian-Daniel equation. The Jacobi elliptic function expansion scheme (JEFES) were utilized to finding various kinds of optical solitons. Also, the generalized \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tan (\theta (\xi )/2)$$\end{document}-expansion method was used to get the exact solutions. This scheme is among the most powerful techniques for creating numerous exact solutions for different nonlinear partial differential equations. As a result, we construct different shapes of solutions, such as a rational solution of hyperbolic functions, periodic wave solutions, bright solitary wave solution and singular periodic solutions as seen in Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4. By using the Hamiltonian technique, the stability of the obtained solution is examined regarding the sufficient condition of stability, this study make sure that the obtained solutions are stable. As far as we know, these outcomes were achieved for this model for the first time. When the coefficients of the NLPDEs are not constants, which means variable coefficients, the JEFES will be examined for our future recommendations. Furthermore, this study provides a very strong basis for future works, allowing for the inclusion of more physio- logical parameters and boundary conditions with more complicated waveforms. The work would also reveal waves’ interactions more profoundly and the consequences in a clinical setting. Overall, this research emphasizes advanced mathematical tools as keys to understanding nonlinear wave phenomena in relevant optic medical science.

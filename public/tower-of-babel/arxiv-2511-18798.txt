On the Stability of Discrete Reaction-Diffusion System of Networked Dynamical Systems
Dinesh Kumar

Abstract
Paper provides a sufficient condition for the local stability of the reaction-diffusion system of networked dynamical systems at its homogeneous equilibrium point. It is illustrated through an ecological meta-populations (spatial-structured populations) network of predator-prey systems. Keywords: Reaction-Diffusion System; Metapopulations; Equilibrium Stability; Laplacian Matrix

Broadly, there are two categories of reaction-diffusion systems, depending upon whether the diffusion occurs continuously or discretely. This paper deals with the latter type of reaction-diffusion systems in which time variable entities are placed in discrete-manner and coupled by the diffusion.

Stability is an important concept in dynamical systems theory; it tells the long term behavior of the solution trajectory under some state perturbations. In the context of physical discrete reaction-diffusion systems, for instance, meta-populations networks get unavoidable disturbances either by human welfare activities or by natural calamities that deter the populations’ co-existence

This paper presents the stability condition of the networked dynamical systems in the simplest form. That is, if the desired level of network connectivity is ensured in the network of dynamical systems along with the appropriately combined matrix of local Jacobians of all individual dynamical systems at a homogeneous equilibrium point, then the whole dynamical network will be stable.

The second-smallest eigenvalue (also known as algebraic connectivity and Fiedler value) of the Laplacian matrix measures the network connectivity. It signifies how well connected the graph network is

Theorem 2.1

Let

By the Gershgorin disc theorem

This quantity contains information on network connectivity. The following result tells that the more its value, the strong the network’s connectivity is and vice versa.

Corollary 2.1.1
.

The local stability dynamics of a networked dynamical systems can be controlled by dynamics of an individual patch and connectivity of the network.

Proof.

Proof is easy. We can choose the dynamics of an individual patch such that the average of all the local Jacobians have eigenvalues with negative real parts. That can be done by considering the Gershgorinian discs of the Jacobian of an individual patch in (sufficiently) left half of the complex plane. Once the average of Jacobian has the desired property, then we know the dynamics of the whole network is dependent on the network connectivity.
∎

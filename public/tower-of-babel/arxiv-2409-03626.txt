Strong asymptotic freeness of Haar unitaries in quasi-exponential dimensional representations
Michael Magee, Mikael de la Salle

Abstract
We prove almost sure strong asymptotic freeness of i.i.d. random unitaries with the following law: sample a Haar unitary matrix of dimension n n and then send this unitary into an irreducible representation of U ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{U}(n) . The strong convergence holds as long as the irreducible representation arises from a pair of partitions of total size at most n 1 42 − ε n^{\frac{1}{42}-\varepsilon} and is uniform in this regime. Previously this was known for partitions of total size up to ≍ log ⁡ n / log ⁡ log ​ n \asymp\log n/\log\log n by a result of Bordenave and Collins.

We use the following fundamental inequality by Andrey and Vladimir

where

. By an affine change of variable,
for a general interval (
4.2

\sup_{[a,b]}|P^{(k)}|\leq\frac{2^{k}}{(b-a)^{k}}\frac{D^{2}(D^{2}-1)\dots(D^{2}-(k-1)^{2})}{(2k-1)!!}\sup_{[a,b]}|P|.

We deduce the following, which is essentially

Lemma 4.1

For every polynomial

\sup_{[0,\frac{1}{N}]}|P|\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{D^{2}}{N+1}}\sup_{n\geq N}\left|P\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|.

5
A criterion for strong convergence

In this section,
\Gamma
will be a finitely generated group with a
fixed finite generating set and corresponding word-length. We denote by

\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
the subspace of the elements of the group
algebra of
\Gamma
supported in the ball of radius
q
.

Definition 5.1
.

A function
u\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is tempered if

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|u((x^{*}x)^{n})|^{\frac{1}{2n}}\leq\|\lambda(x)\|

for every
x\in\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
. Here
\lambda
is the left-regular representation of
\Gamma
on
\ell_{2}(\Gamma)
and the norm is the operator norm.

A function
u\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is completely tempered
if

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u((x^{*}x)^{n})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|

for every integer
k\geq 1
and every
x\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, where
\mathrm{Tr}
is the trace on
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
.

For example, if
u(\gamma)=\langle\pi(\gamma)\xi,\xi\rangle
is a matrix coefficient of a unitary representation of
\Gamma
, then
u
is tempered if and only if it is completely tempered, if and only if the cyclic representation generated by
\xi
is weakly-contained in the left-regular representation. So Definition
5.1
can be seen as an adaptation to functions that are not related to representations of the classical notion of tempered representation.

This section is devoted to the proof of the following criterion for strong convergence in probability of a sequence of random representations.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\pi_{n}
be a sequence of random unitary representations of finite nonrandom dimension,
u_{n}\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
be functions and
\varepsilon_{n}>0
. Assume that

•

u_{n}
is tempered and there is a polynomial
P_{n}
such that for every
q
and every
x\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
,
|u_{n}(x)|\leq P_{n}(q)\|x\|_{C^{*}(\Gamma)}
,

•

\big|\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}(\pi_{n}(x))-u_{n}(x)\big|\leq\varepsilon_{n}\exp\left(\frac{q}{\log(2+q)^{2}}\right)\|x\|_{C^{*}(\Gamma)}
for every
q
and every
x\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
,

•

\lim_{n}\varepsilon_{n}=0
.

Then for every
y\in\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, and every
\delta>0
,

\lim_{n}\mathbb{P}(\|\pi_{n}(y)\|\geq\|\lambda(y)\|+\delta)=0.

If
C^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
has a unique trace, then the conclusion becomes that

\lim_{n}\mathbb{P}(\big|\|\pi_{n}(y)\|-\|\lambda(y)\|\big|\geq\delta)=0.

Remark 5.3
.

The choice of
w(q)=\exp\left(\frac{q}{\log(2+q)^{2}}\right)
is an arbitrary choice that is convenient for our applications, the crucial property that is used is that
\sum_{q}\frac{1}{1+q^{2}}\log w(q)<\infty
, reminiscent of the Beurling-Malliavin Theorem. It could be replaced by
\exp(q^{2}u_{q})
for an arbitrary decreasing and summable sequence

5.1
Bump functions with small Fourier coefficient

A nonzero compactly supported continuous function cannot have a Fourier transform that decays too fast at infinity, as it has to satisfy
\int\frac{\log|\hat{f}(t)|}{1+t^{2}}dt>-\infty
. In other words, the condition
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(t)}{1+t^{2}}dt<\infty
on a function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a necessary condition for the existence of a nonzero compactly supported continuous function
f
such that
|\hat{f}(t)|\leq\exp(-\varphi(|t|)
for every
t
. The Beurling-Malliavin theorem asserts that this is often also a sufficient condition, see for example
[
MNH06
]
. The following elementary fact is an illustration of the same phenomenon. The small nuance compared to the Beurling-Malliavin theorem is that we need that
f
be nonnegative. The same proof works for
\varphi(t)=\frac{t}{(\log(2+|t|))^{1+\varepsilon}}
replaced by any continuous function
[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\frac{\varphi(t)}{t^{2}}
is non-increasing and integrable on
[1,\infty)
.

Lemma 5.7
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a real
number
M>0
and an even function
C^{\infty}
function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

that is strictly positive on
(-1,1)
, zero outside of
(-1,1)
, and such
that

|\hat{f}(t)|\leq\exp\left(-M\frac{|t|}{(\log(2+|t|))^{1+\varepsilon}}\right).

Proof.

The proof is a standard construction of a smooth bump function as
infinite convolution product of indicator functions
[
Kat04
, Lemma V.2.7]
.
Define
a_{j}=\frac{c}{j(\log 2+j)^{1+\varepsilon}}
, where
c>0

is chosen such that
\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{j}=1
. Let
(X_{j})_{j\geq 1}

be a sequence of independent random variables, all uniform in
[-1,1]
.
Let
\mu
be the law of
\sum_{j}a_{j}X_{j}
. It is a measure with
full support in
[-\sum_{j}a_{j},\sum_{j}a_{j}]=[-1,1]
. Then

\widehat{\mu}(t)=\mathbb{E}\exp(-it\sum a_{j}X_{j})=\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sin(ta_{j})}{ta_{j}}.

The inequality

|\widehat{\mu}(t)|\leq\prod_{j\leq\frac{t}{(\log 2+t)^{1+\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{|t|a_{j}}\leq\exp\left(-M\frac{|t|}{(\log 2+t)^{1+\varepsilon}}\right)

is a computation. It implies that
\mu
is absolutely continuous
with respect to the Lebesgue measure and that its Radon-Nikodym derivative,
which is our
f
, is
C^{\infty}
.
f
does not vanish on the interval

(-1,1)
because
f
is a log-concave function as a limit of convolutions
of log-concave functions.
∎

5.2

-algebra

Let
(w(q))_{q\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathcal{S}_{w}(\Gamma)=\big\{x\in C^{*}(\Gamma)\mid x=\sum_{q}x_{q},x_{q}\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma],\sum_{q}w(q)\|x_{q}\|_{C^{*}(\Gamma)}<\infty\big\}.

It is a Banach algebra for the norm

\|x\|_{w}:=\inf\big\{\sum_{q}w(q)\|x_{q}\|_{C^{*}(\Gamma)}\mid x=\sum_{q}x_{q},x_{q}\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]\big\}.

For

Lemma 5.10
.

A function

extends by linearity to a linear map

of norm
\leq N

The next lemma is useful and suggests the terminology of Sobolev algebras for the algebras

Lemma 5.11
.

Let

Proof.

In the proof, we write
\|y\|
for

. The assumption implies that the Fourier coefficients of

are absolutely summable, which gives rise to a norm-converging expansion

f(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\varphi}(n)T_{n}(y/\|y\|)

where

is the

. So

belongs to
\mathbb{C}_{\leq q|n|}[\Gamma]
, has norm
\leq 1
in
C^{*}(\Gamma)

and

\sum_{n}w(q|n|)|\hat{\varphi}(n)|\|T_{n}(y/\|y\|)\|_{C^{*}(\Gamma)}\leq\sum_{n}w(q|n|)|\hat{\varphi}(n)|.

The proves the first part of the lemma.

For the second part, if

is

, then so is

and by the chain rule we have

with constant that depend on

\sum_{n}(1+q|n|)^{i}|\hat{\varphi}(n)|\leq(1+q)^{i}\big(\sum_{n}(1+|n|)^{-2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\sum_{n}(1+|n|)^{2i+2}|\hat{\varphi}(n)|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We bound

(\sum_{n}(1+|n|)^{2i+2}|\hat{\varphi}(n)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|\hat{\varphi}(0)|+2^{i+1}(\sum_{n}|\widehat{\varphi^{(i+1)}}(n)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},

The lemma follows by (

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
u\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
. Assume that
u
extends to a continuous linear map
u\colon\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}
. Then

is tempered if and only if there is
n
such that for every selfadjoint
x\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)\cap\ker\lambda

. In that case, this holds for all
n\geq i+2
.

Furthermore,
u
is completely tempered if and only if there is
n
such
that for every
k
and every selfadjoint
x\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes(\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)\cap\ker\lambda)
,
(\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u)(x^{n})=0
. In that case, this holds for all
n\geq i+2
.

Proof.

We prove the equivalence for temperedness, the proof for complete temperedness is identical.

We start with a preliminary observation. Lemma
5.11
tells us that for every self-adjoint
y\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}(\Gamma)
, the function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto u(f(y))
is a compactly supported distribution of order
\leq i+1
, and the largest symmetric interval containing its support is
[-\limsup|u(y^{k})|^{\frac{1}{k}},\limsup|u(y^{k})|^{\frac{1}{k}}]
, see
[
CGVTvH24
, Lemma 4.9]
).

Given this observation, the if direction is direct. Let
\varepsilon>0
, and pick
f
be a
C^{\infty}
function equal to
0
on the interval
[-\|\lambda(y)\|^{2},\|\lambda(y)\|^{2}]
and to
1
outside of the
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of it. For
k
large enough (so that

is

),

belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)\cap\ker\lambda
. As a consequence,
u(x^{n})=0
and therefore

u((y^{*}y)^{k})=u((y^{*}y)^{k}(1-f(y^{*}y)^{n})).

But the
C^{i+1}
norm of
t\mapsto t^{k}(1-f(t)^{n})
is
\leq C(i,\varepsilon,y)(\|\lambda(y)\|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{k}
, which implies that

\limsup_{k}|u((y^{*}y)^{k})|^{1/2k}\leq\sqrt{\|\lambda(y)\|^{2}+\varepsilon}.

This proves that
u
is tempered.

For the converse, consider a self-adjoint
x\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)\cap\ker\lambda
. Decompose
x=\sum_{q}x_{q}
as in the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{i}(\Gamma)
. We can moreover assume that each
x_{q}
is self-adjoint. Let
y_{q}=\sum_{s\leq q}x_{s}\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
. Then we have

\|\lambda(y_{q})\|=\|\lambda(y_{q}-x)\|\leq\sum_{s>q}\|x_{s}\|=o((1+q)^{-i}),

(5.2)

because
\sum_{q}(1+q)^{i}\|x_{q}\|<\infty
. Let
\varphi
be a
C^{\infty}
function equal to
1
on
[-1,1]
and
0
outside of
[-2,2]
, and let
\varphi_{q}(t)=\varphi(\frac{t}{\|\lambda(y_{q})\|})
. We know from the preliminary observation that
\Lambda_{q}(f)=u(f(y_{q}))
is a distribution with support inside
[-\|\lambda(y_{q})\|,\|\lambda(y_{q})\|]
and such that
|\Lambda_{q}(f)|\leq C(1+q)^{i}\|f\|_{C^{i+1}(\mathbb{R})}
for some
C
independent from
q
. Therefore, for every
n
, since the functions
t\mapsto t^{n}
and
f:t\mapsto t^{n}\varphi_{q}(t)
coincide on a neighbourhood of
[-\|\lambda(y_{q})\|,\|\lambda(y_{q})\|]
, we have

u(y_{q}^{n})=u(y_{q}^{n}\varphi_{q}(y_{q}))=(1+q)^{i}O_{q\to\infty}(\|\lambda(y_{q})\|^{n-i-1}).

(5.3)

The last inequality is because the function
f
has
C^{i+1}
-norm
\leq C(i,n)\|\lambda(y_{q})\|^{n-i-1}
. Indeed, for
k\leq i+1
,

f^{(k)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{n!}{(n-j)!}\frac{t^{n-j}}{\|\lambda(y_{q})\|^{k-j}}\varphi^{(k-j)}\Big(\frac{t}{\|\lambda(y_{q})\|}\Big).

Using
|t|\leq 2\|\lambda(y_{q})\|
on the support of
\varphi_{q}
, we see that this is less than

as claimed. If

we conclude from (

) and (

) that
\lim_{q}u(y_{q}^{n})=0
. Therefore, we have

u(x^{n})=u(\lim_{q}y_{q}^{n})=\lim_{q}u(y_{q}^{n})=0.

The proposition is proved.

We use the following easy consequence of the operator-space reformulation of exactness, in the line of

Lemma 5.13

Let

-algebra. For every finite family

Proof.

If

for every

and every

.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. Assume that

has a unique trace. Then for every integer
q
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist

We use the following fact:

and only if for every

and every

and a finite family

-algebra

The first conclusion of the lemma follows quite easily from this

Let

6.2
Random walks and tempered functions

The following is a useful criterion for a function on a group with the rapid decay property to be tempered. Recall a group is said to have the rapid decay property if it admits a finite generating set and a polynomial
P
such that, for every
a\in\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
,

\|\lambda(a)\|\leq P(q)(\sum_{\gamma}|a(\gamma)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.4)

Haagerup’s inequality
[
Haa79
, Lemma 1.5]
asserts that free groups with their standard generating sets have the rapid decay property, with
P(R)=3(1+R^{2})
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group with the rapid decay property, and
u\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
a function. Assume that for every reasonable probability measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
, if
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the associated random walk on
\Gamma
,

\limsup_{n}\big(\mathbb{E}|u(\gamma_{n})|\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\rho(\mu).

(6.5)

Then
u
is tempered, and even completely tempered.

Proof.

Before we start, let us observe that the assumption (
6.5
) in fact holds for every symmetric finitely supported probability measure
\mu
, even if it not reasonable in the sense that its support is not generating or does not contain the identity. Indeed, let
\nu
be a fixed reasonable probability measure. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}(\mu+\varepsilon\nu)
is a reasonable probability measure, so that (
6.5
) holds for
\mu_{\varepsilon}
. If
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathbb{E}
denote the law of the random walk with transition
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and
\mu
respectively, the inequality
\mu\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{\varepsilon}
gives

\mathbb{E}|u(\gamma_{n})|\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}|u(\gamma_{n})|,

so we obtain

\limsup_{n}\big(\mathbb{E}|u(\gamma_{n})|\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\rho(\mu_{\varepsilon})\leq\rho(\mu)+\varepsilon\rho(\nu).

This proves that (
6.5
) holds for
\mu
by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We now move to the proof of the proposition. We claim that for every
k,q\geq 1
and every
x=x^{*}\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u(x^{n})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq P(q)\sqrt{k}\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|.

(6.6)

Here
P
is the polynomial satisfying (
6.4
), given by the assumption that
\Gamma
has the rapid decay property.

By a standard
tensor-power
trick, (
6.6
) implies that
u
is completely tempered. Indeed, given an arbitrary
x\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\leq q}[\Gamma]
, applying (
6.6
) to
y=(x^{*}x)^{m}
(which belongs to
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\leq 2mq}[\Gamma]
and satisfies
\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(y)\|=\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|^{2m}
) and raising to the power
\frac{1}{2m}
, it implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u((x^{*}x)^{n})|^{\frac{1}{2n}}\leq(P(2mq)\sqrt{k})^{\frac{1}{2m}}\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|,

and in the
m\to\infty
limit

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u((x^{*}x)^{n})|^{\frac{1}{2n}}\leq\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|.

So it remains to prove (
6.6
). We can normalize
x
so that
\sum_{\gamma}\|x(\gamma)\|=1
, where
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm on
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
\mu(s)=\|x(s)\|
; it is a symmetric finitely supported probability measure on
\Gamma
. By the triangle inequality and the inequality
|\mathrm{Tr}(x(s_{1})\dots x(s_{n}))|\leq k\|x(s_{1})\|\dots\|x(s_{n})\|
, we have

|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u(x^{n})|\leq k\sum_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in\Gamma}\mu(s_{1})\dots\mu(s_{n})|u(s_{1}\dots s_{n})|=k\mathbb{E}|u(\gamma_{n})|.

By the assumption in the extended form above, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{Tr}\otimes u(x^{n})|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\rho(\mu).

By the rapid decay assumption (
6.4
), this is less than

P(q)(\sum_{s}\mu(s)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By by the basic fact
\mu(s)^{2}=\|x(s)\|^{2}\leq\mathrm{Tr}(x(s)^{*}x(s))
, we have

\sum_{s}\mu(s)^{2}\leq(\mathrm{Tr}\otimes\lambda)(x^{*}x)\leq k\|(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)(x)\|^{2},

which proves the claimed inequality (
6.6
).
∎

\mathbf{D}
-edges.
Again, such cycles are disjoint.

The resulting glued discs therefore meet only along the
\mathbf{D}
-edges
and the resulting total object is a topological surface we call

\Sigma(\mathbf{D},\{\pi_{i}\}).

The boundary cycles of this surface alternate between
w
-edges and

\pi
-edges.

For
\sigma\in S_{k+\ell}
, let
\|\sigma\|_{k,\ell}
denote the
minimum
m
for which

\sigma=\sigma_{0}t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{m}

where
\sigma_{0}\in S_{k}\times S_{\ell}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}

are transpositions in
S_{k+\ell}
. The analog of
[
Mag21
, Thm. 4.3]

is that, after an elementary calculation using (
12.2
)
and bounds for the Weingarten function one obtains

\prod_{i=1}^{q}z(\pi_{i})\prod_{i\in[r]}\mathrm{Wg}_{k+\ell}(\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}^{-1})\mathcal{N}(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q},\mathbf{D})\ll_{w,k,\ell}n^{-\sum_{i}\|\pi_{i}\|_{k,\ell}}n^{\chi(\Sigma(\mathbf{D},\{\pi_{i}\}))},

hence from (
12.9
)

\mathbb{E}_{n}[s_{\lambda,\mu}(w(u))]\ll_{w,k,\ell}\sum_{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q}\in S_{k+\ell}}\sum_{\mathbf{D}\in\mathbf{M}^{*}}n^{-\sum_{i}\|\pi_{i}\|_{k,\ell}}n^{\chi(\Sigma(\mathbf{D},\{\pi_{i}\}))}.

Surfaces with large contribution

It is shown in
[
Mag21
, §§4.2]
— the same proof
applies without change to the current setting — that given any

\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{q}\in S_{k+\ell},\,\mathbf{D}\in\mathbf{M}^{*},

it is possible to modify these so that

\pi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\pi^{\prime}_{q}\in S_{k}\times S_{\ell},\,\,\,\,\,\sum_{i}\|\pi^{\prime}_{i}\|_{k,\ell}=0

(12.11)

\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\{\sigma^{\prime}_{i},\tau_{i}^{\prime}\}\in\mathbf{M}^{*}\,\text{with $\sigma^{\prime}_{i}=\tau^{\prime}_{i}$ for all $i\in[r]$}

(12.12)

and there exists an inequality between corresponding terms

n^{\chi(\Sigma(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\{\pi^{\prime}_{i}\}))}\geq n^{-\sum_{i}\|\pi_{i}\|_{k,\ell}}n^{\chi(\Sigma(\mathbf{D},\{\pi_{i}\}))}.

The condition (
12.12
) means all type-II cycles
are now rectangles with two (non-consecutive) edges in the boundary;
we now replace each rectangle with an arc connecting the two boundary
segments of the rectangle.

Connection to transverse maps

We now create a class of transverse map on the surface
\Sigma(\mathbf{D}^{\prime},\{\pi^{\prime}_{i}\})

as follows (cf. Definition
9.2
). The ribbon graph structure is the one dictated by the arcs we just
prior constructed. By construction, they cut the surface into discs.
For each boundary component, place a marked point in some (it does
not matter)
\pi
-edge that arose from
\pi^{\prime}_{q}
. Recall the
CW
-complex
rose
R_{r}^{0}
with one point (call it
z_{i}
) in the interior
of the circle corresponding to
x_{i}
. If an arc arose from a rectangle
with edges that arose from
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}=\tau^{\prime}_{i}
then we declare
our function to take the constant value
z_{i}
on this arc and the
transverse map will traverse the arc from the
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
side
to the
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
side.

The property (
12.11
) implies that for every boundary
component of the surface, the
w
-edges are all directed the same
way along this boundary component and hence give an orientation to
the boundary. With respect to this orientation, the isotopy class
of SF transverse map satisfies (
11.1
).

The fact that
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{M}^{*}
, rather than
\mathbf{M}
, implies that the
class of transverse map has the crucial
Forbidden

Matchings

property. This completes the proof of Proposition
11.1
.

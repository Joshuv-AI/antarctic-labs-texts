Prognostic significance of serum interleukin-6 in severe/critical COVID-19 patients treated with tocilizumab: a detailed observational study analysis
Authors: Panpan Liang, Yan Li, Li’e Meng, Yuting Li, Hailing Mai, Tao Li, Jiarui Ma, Junhui Ma, Jing Wang, Bing Zhuan, Wei Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBaseline IL-6 levels have been found to be non-predictive of subsequent outcomes following tocilizumab treatment, highlighting the need for more reliable predictive markers. To address this, a retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical profiles, diagnostic tests, and follow-up prognoses of 60 patients with severe or critical COVID-19, all of whom were identified as experiencing a cytokine storm and subsequently received tocilizumab treatment. Among the patients, the overall survival rate during follow-up was 80%, with further analysis revealing that advanced age was an independent risk factor for adverse outcomes. Following tocilizumab administration, a statistically significant increase in IL-6 and D-dimer levels was observed, while markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), and fibrinogen demonstrated reductions compared to pre-treatment values. Specifically, IL-6 levels initially surged briefly after tocilizumab intervention before gradually diminishing. To assess the prognostic utility of IL-6, the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was employed, which yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.812, indicating strong predictive capability, with a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 53.49%. The optimal cut-off value for IL-6 was identified at 147.79 pg/mL. In conclusion, IL-6 levels tend to rise transiently following tocilizumab therapy, before gradually declining. These post-treatment IL-6 measurements may serve as a valuable biomarker for assessing prognosis in patients undergoing this treatment.Keywords: COVID-19, IL6, Tocilizumab, PrognosisSubject terms: Biomarkers, Diseases, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic, caused by the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS‐CoV‐2), has posed enormous challenges to healthcare systems worldwide. As of February 11, 2024, the global cumulative count of confirmed COVID-19 cases has reached 775 million, with the death toll rising to 7.03 million (https://data.who.int/dashboards/covid19/cases?n=c). An analysis of 72,314 reported cases of SARS-CoV-2 infection revealed that while a majority (81%) experienced mild to moderate disease, a substantial proportion faced severe (14%) and critical (5%) conditions, with an alarming mortality rate of 49% among critically ill patients1. A cytokine storm, characterized by an excessive surge of inflammatory cytokines, can lead to acute organ failure, escalating to life-threatening situations. This overwhelming inflammatory response is deemed a significant contributor to mortality in patients with severe COVID-19.

IL-6, an acute-phase inflammatory cytokine, studies have shown that baseline IL-6 levels may be integral to the assessment of disease severity, prognosis and serve as a guide for the administration of anti-inflammatory drugs2,3. Tocilizumab (TCZ), a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody targeting the IL-6 receptor, binds specifically to both the membrane-bound (mIL-6R) and soluble forms of the interleukin 6 receptor (sIL-6R). By inhibiting the IL-6-IL-6R-gp130 hexameric complex, tocilizumab blocks downstream signaling, thereby diminishing the pro-inflammatory actions of IL-6. This mechanism has been shown to significantly improve prognosis and survival rates in patients with severe or critical COVID-19 infections4–9. However, once treatment is initiated, baseline IL-6 is not predictive of further outcomes, indicating an unmet need for robust predictive markers to differentiate survival outcomes post-tocilizumab treatment10. Consequently, this study retrospectively analyzes cases of severe/critical COVID-19 treated with tocilizumab, aiming to examine factors influencing patient prognosis, assess variations in IL-6 levels following tocilizumab treatment, and provide guidance for clinical application.

Methods

Study subjects

Utilizing a retrospective study design, this research included 60 patients with severe or critical COVID-19 infections admitted to the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region between December 1, 2022, and December 12, 2023. These patients, believed to have experienced a cytokine storm, received tocilizumab treatment alongside standard therapies such as antiviral medications, glucocorticoids, and anticoagulation. Comprehensive clinical data were collected, including demographics, symptomatology, comorbidities, admission and symptom onset dates, treatment regimens, outcomes, laboratory tests, and imaging for retrospective analysis. Ethical considerations: This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, which waived the requirement for patient informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the study. It was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines, and all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Inclusion criteria were as follows: 1. Confirmed COVID-19 diagnosis based on positive reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) assays of nasopharyngeal samples and chest CT scans indicating bilateral ground-glass opacities or infiltrative shadows (Nucleic acid CT values of less than 40 were defined as positive). 2. Patients aged 18 years or older.

Definition of Severe COVID-19 Infection: An adult patient classified as severe must present with any of the following signs attributable to SARS-CoV-2 infection: 1. Shortness of breath with a respiratory rate (RR) of 30 breaths per minute or more. 2. Resting functional oxygen saturation (SpO2) of 93% or less while breathing room air. 3. Arterial partial pressure of oxygen to fraction of inspired oxygen ratio (PaO2/FiO2) less than 300 mmHg (where 1 mmHg equals 0.133 kPa). 4. Rapid clinical deterioration with >50% progression of lung lesions within 24–48 h. Such severe cases are not explained by any other causes apart from COVID-19 infection.

Definition of Critical COVID-19 Infection: A patient is considered to have a critical infection if they meet any of the following criteria: 1. Respiratory failure necessitating mechanical ventilation. 2. Shock. 3. Presence of other organ failures that require intensive care unit (ICU) monitoring and treatment.

Tocilizumab Treatment Protocol: According to the “Protocol for the Diagnosis and Treatment of COVID-19 (Trial 10th Edition)”11, tocilizumab is recommended for patients with severe or critical COVID-19 infections who exhibit significantly elevated IL-6 levels on laboratory tests and are suspected of experiencing a cytokine storm. Dosage Guidelines: The initial dose ranges from 4 to 8 mg/kg, typically 400 mg, diluted in 100 mL of saline and infused over 1 hour. If ineffective, a second dose may be administered no sooner than 12 h later. Subsequent Doses: In cases where patients continue to display significantly elevated CRP, IL-6, or other inflammatory markers after two doses, and there is no substantial improvement in symptoms, clinicians may consider administering a third or even fourth dose of tocilizumab, tailored to the specific circumstances of the patient.

Laboratory Test Analysis: The evaluation of patient health involved a comprehensive set of laboratory tests, including complete blood count, liver and kidney function panels, IL-6, ferritin, coagulation profiles, and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels. These tests were conducted using commercially available reagent kits. For the purpose of this study, the most recent CRP or IL-6 measurement obtained before the administration of tocilizumab (TCZ) was recorded as the pre-treatment value. The onset of the study, designated as ‘day 0’, was marked by the initial treatment with tocilizumab.

Follow-up outcome

Follow-up was conducted by reviewing the information of follow-up cases or by telephone, starting from the time of patients’ admission to the hospital and ending with follow-up on 1 February 2024, and patients were classified into survivors and non-survivors according to their survival status for statistical analysis. The ending events included: in-hospital death, and follow-up death. If a patient died, details of treatment, the time of death, and cause of death were obtained.

Statistical analysis

Data were collected and organized using Microsoft Excel. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0, and graphical representations were created with GraphPad Prism 9. Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or median with interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Count data were expressed as n (%). Comparisons between groups were conducted using an independent samples t-test for normally distributed data or a non-parametric rank-sum test for non-normally distributed data. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was applied for count data comparisons. A significance threshold of P < 0.05 was set for two-tailed tests. This study was non-randomized and did not compare tocilizumab recipients with non-recipients.

Results

Analysis of clinical features

Within the timeframe from December 1, 2022, to December 12, 2023, a total of 64 patients with severe or critical COVID-19 were treated with tocilizumab at the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region. After excluding 4 patients who tested negative for SARS-CoV-2 via PCR, the study included 60 confirmed COVID-19-positive patients. Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of the 60 patients, while Table 2 details the distribution of their symptoms. Age ranged from 55 to 101 years, and the mean age was 77.22±8.85 years. Of the 60 patients, 35 (58.3%) were male and 25 (41.7%) were female. Tocilizumab was administered at a median of 11 days post-symptom onset (interquartile range [IQR]: 9–16 days) and 2 days post-admission (IQR: 1–7 days). The median hospital stay lasted 14 days (IQR: 10–17.75 days). The majority of patients received standard treatments for COVID-19. Specifically, 52 patients received either transnasal high-flow oxygen therapy or noninvasive ventilation, 27 patients were treated with prone ventilation; 53 patients were administered antiviral medications. Additional treatments included immunoglobulin therapy for 10 patients, baricitinib for 11 patients, glucocorticoids for 58 patients, antibiotic treatment for 58 patients, and low molecular weight heparin anticoagulation for 58 patients.

Prognostic analysis

The study stratified patients based on their prognosis following tocilizumab treatment, found no significant differences in several variables, including weight, height, BMI, comorbidities, smoking status, and the number of SARS-CoV-2 infections. Similarly, treatment-related factors such as frequency of tocilizumab administration, duration of hospital stay, incidence of thrombosis, ventilation type and duration, and changes in SpO2 after treatment showed no significant differences, as detailed in Table 1. The overall survival rate at follow-up for the cohort was 80%. The analysis did reveal that non-survivors were predominantly male, of an advanced age, and had a longer duration between symptom onset and tocilizumab administration. Upon conducting a multifactorial analysis of the general characteristics, age emerged as an independent risk factor for a poor prognosis, which is elaborated in Table 3.

Safety analysis

Comprehensive documentation of adverse events was a key focus of our study. We found that there were no serious adverse events attributed to tocilizumab treatment. Prior to tocilizumab therapy, 16 patients had already shown increased transaminase levels. Following the initial tocilizumab administration, 14 patients experienced elevated transaminase levels and subsequently received hepatoprotective treatment. However, none of the patients exhibited a rise in transaminases to a level ≥4 times the upper limit of normal. The cause of transaminase elevation remains uncertain. It may be related to the progression of COVID-19, possibly due to viral factors or cytokine release syndrome, the antiviral medications administered, or the tocilizumab treatment. In one notable case, a patient who received four doses of tocilizumab exhibited a significant reduction in fibrinogen and an increase in D-dimer levels, as shown in Fig. 7. Subsequent tests showed that the patient’s fibrinogen levels normalized during the follow-up. During the treatment period, no new bacterial, fungal, or viral infections were observed, and no infusion reactions related to tocilizumab were reported.

Discussion

In our retrospective investigation of severe or critical COVID-19 patients, whom clinicians assessed as having entered a cytokine storm and who received tocilizumab treatment, it was observed that over 80% were concurrently treated with antivirals and glucocorticosteroids, aligning with the current standard care protocols for COVID-19. It is important to note that not all patients are confirmed to have completed the full recommended course of these therapies. The findings of the study underscore that advanced age is an independent risk factor for a poorer outcome in severe or critically ill COVID-19 patients who receive tocilizumab therapy. Additionally, post-treatment IL-6 levels were significantly higher in non-survivors compared to survivors. However, despite the significant association between elevated IL-6 levels and outcomes, IL-6 has not yet been validated as an independent prognostic risk factor in this patient population.

IL-6 is a 21 kDa protein composed of 212 amino acids and serves as a key cytokine with multifaceted biological functions. Beyond its well-known roles in promoting inflammation, fever, cytokinemia, and tumorigenesis, IL-6 also plays a significant part in regulating metabolism, bone remodeling, and hematopoiesis. It is crucial for both innate and adaptive immune responses. In healthy individuals, serum levels of IL-6 are typically low, generally ranging from 1 to 5 pg/mL12. The overproduction of IL-6 and excessive activation of IL-6 receptor (IL-6R) signaling are implicated in the pathogenesis of a spectrum of acute and chronic inflammatory disorders. This includes the cytokine storm, as well as autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA), Castleman’s disease, and giant cell arteritis, among others. While IL-6 levels are known to surge in critical illnesses like acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) and sepsis, the elevation of IL-6 in COVID-19 patients tends to be more moderate. A comprehensive pooled analysis of nearly 1000 COVID-19 patients revealed that the average IL-6 concentration in those with severe or critical infection was approximately 36.7 pg/mL13. In the present study, the mean IL-6 concentration was recorded at 95.72 pg/mL, markedly exceeding levels reported in prior research. While non-survivors exhibited higher baseline IL-6 levels than survivors, the observed difference did not reach statistical significance (P=0.140). This could be due to the fact that all cases in this study were severe or critical, leading to similar baseline disease severity and inflammatory markers across the patient cohort. There remains an unmet need for robust predictive markers to accurately differentiate survival outcomes following tocilizumab treatment, particularly in patients with significantly elevated baseline IL-6 levels.

In this study, IL-6 levels were significantly higher in non-survivors compared to survivors after tocilizumab treatment, highlighting the potential of post-treatment IL-6 measurements as a prognostic marker for patients with severe or critical COVID-19. When tocilizumab is administered to inhibit IL-6R, monitoring post-treatment serum IL-6 levels more accurately reflects ongoing endogenous IL-6 production and better correlates with inflammatory disease activity compared to pre-treatment IL-6 levels. This indicates that serum IL-6 concentrations after tocilizumab administration may provide valuable insights into patient outcomes and aid in managing severe COVID-19 cases14. Baseline IL-6 levels may help assess disease severity or guide anti-inflammatory therapy but are insufficient for predicting outcomes after treatment begins10. There has been limited research delineating the relationship between post-treatment IL-6 levels and the prognosis of patients with severe or critical COVID-19 who have been treated with tocilizumab. The effectiveness of tocilizumab therapy is primarily evaluated based on symptomatic relief, improvements in oxygen saturation, blood gas analysis results, and radiological changes. Nevertheless, there is often a delay in the manifestation of symptomatic improvement. A study by Eimer et al.15 found that significant symptom relief occurred only after 10 days of tocilizumab treatment, emphasizing the need for a more immediate and reliable measure of response. Similar to our study, a retrospective analysis involving 377 patients who received tocilizumab treatment revealed that measuring IL-6 levels after the administration of one or multiple doses of tocilizumab (TCZ) had a significant correlation with patient prognosis. The AUC values for IL-6 were 0.67 and 0.79 (P < 0.001). At a threshold of 900 pg/mL, IL-6 levels showed over 90% specificity and an NPV of more than 86% for predicting mortality. This underscores the potential utility of dynamically monitoring IL-6 levels to track disease progression and the response to treatment in COVID-19 patients16. Our ROC analysis identified an optimal post-treatment IL-6 threshold of 147.79 pg/mL for prognosis, with a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 53.5%. An IL-6 level above 147.79 pg/mL post-treatment suggests an unresolved inflammatory response, indicating a potential risk of worsening condition and death.

Tocilizumab, a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody that targets the IL-6 receptor, binds with high affinity to both soluble and membrane-bound IL-6 receptors. This binding action effectively blocks IL-6 from engaging with its receptor, thus inhibiting the cytokine’s capacity to trigger immune-mediated damage to cells and ameliorating inflammatory responses. A meta-analysis study provided evidence that the use of tocilizumab in COVID-19 patients resulted in a 12% reduction in mortality, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic agent in managing the inflammatory complications associated with the disease17. The COVINTOC study concluded that routine administration of tocilizumab in adult COVID-19 patients was not supported by its findings18; A global randomized controlled trial, enrolling patients hospitalized with COVID-19 pneumonia who were not on mechanical ventilation, found that tocilizumab reduced the risk of progression to either mechanical ventilation or death. However, the study noted that tocilizumab did not significantly enhance overall survival rates19.The administration of anti-inflammatory drugs and immunomodulators in the early stages of mild to moderate SARS-CoV-2 infection has not been shown to reduce the likelihood of clinical worsening and may be detrimental20.This is due to the possibility that dampening the immune response could result in an increased viral load. Conversely, the early application of tocilizumab in hospitalized patients with severe or critical COVID-19 has been associated with improved survival outcomes, with evidence suggesting that the earlier tocilizumab is used, the better the prognosis may be. Insights from the RECOVERY Trial indicate that tocilizumab treatment reduces mortality and the need for invasive mechanical ventilation in hospitalized COVID-19 patients experiencing hypoxia and systemic inflammation (CRP ≥75 mg/L)7. Evidence suggests that early initiation of tocilizumab, before irreversible respiratory failure develops in critically ill patients particularly during the inflammatory phase may be crucial for treatment success21. Additionally, there are indications that administering tocilizumab before the 11th day of symptom onset can lead to a reduction in overall mortality22. Morrison et al.23 found that initiating tocilizumab within 12 days of symptom onset is independently associated with improved survival. Echoing prior research, our current study similarly demonstrates that patients receiving tocilizumab within this 12-day window have a significantly better prognosis compared to those treated later. These observations lend theoretical support to establishing an optimal window for tocilizumab administration. According to the 10th edition of China’s Diagnosis and Treatment Protocol for Novel Coronavirus Infection (published in 2023), tocilizumab is recommended as a treatment option for patients with severe or critical illness who exhibit significantly elevated IL-6 levels, with a suggestion that the total number of administrations should not exceed two times. Our study is the first to describe the outcomes in patients with COVID-19 who received tocilizumab on multiple occasions. Seven patients received three tocilizumab treatments due to insufficient response and a significant rebound in IL-6 levels after initial reduction, while one patient was administered tocilizumab four times. However, there was no significant difference in survival or recovery outcomes among patients receiving more tocilizumab administrations (P=0.081). Notably, the patient who received four tocilizumab treatments experienced a marked decrease in fibrinogen and an increase in D-dimer levels, indicative of coagulation abnormalities, an elevated risk of bleeding, and an increased risk of thrombosis.

IL-6 showed a transient increase followed by a decrease in most patients after tocilizumab treatment24,25. As an anti-IL-6R antibody, tocilizumab inhibits the formation of the IL-6/IL-6R complex by binding to the IL-6R D3 domain and competing for the IL-6 binding site26. This effectively blocks the pathway through which IL-6 is typically cleared by receptor-mediated processes. This results in a transient surge in serum IL-6 levels. However, because the formation of the IL-6/IL-6R complex is inhibited, downstream signaling via the gp130 protein is also obstructed. This suppression of downstream signaling leads to a reduction in the inflammatory response and a subsequent decrease in IL-6 release. Over time, as the inflammatory response is mitigated, the accumulated serum IL-6 is gradually lowered through natural protein degradation processes. Price et al.27 reported a reduction in IL-6 levels occurring 72 h after administration, while the current study observed a decrease within the first 24 h following tocilizumab treatment. It is important to note that this study focused on patients with severe or critical conditions, so the findings may not apply to those with milder illness. Additionally, this was a single-center, retrospective study with a relatively small sample size and lacked a placebo control group. Therefore, to validate these observations, further research is required, ideally involving multicenter studies with larger sample sizes.

Conclusions

In conclusion, IL-6 levels initially rise transiently after tocilizumab therapy, followed by a decrease, and post-treatment IL-6 measurements are potentially valuable as biomarkers for evaluating prognosis.

The impact of EGFR-TKIs treatment on ground-glass opacity-featured lung adenocarcinama
Authors: Liang Chen, Ting Yu, Yanqing Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe objective of this study was to comprehensively assess the radiological and pathological impacts of Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor - Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (EGFR - TKIs) on ground - glass opacity (GGO) - featured lung adenocarcinoma. This evaluation aimed to provide valuable insights into the efficacy of EGFR - TKIs treatment for such tumors.Case presentationWe present two cases of patients with multiple ground - glass opacities (mGGOs) and lung adenocarcinoma bearing primary EGFR mutations. In both cases, the patients underwent radical resection of the main lesions and subsequently received EGFR - TKIs treatment. The mGGOs in these patients demonstrated a sensitive response to the targeted therapy. Specifically, in Case 1, after 1 month of targeted treatment with gefitinib, the left upper lobe GGO lesion was partially absorbed, and it was completely absorbed after 3 months. In Case 2, 1 month after starting gefitinib treatment, the left upper lobe GGO lesion showed partial absorption.ConclusionThis study reports a potentially effective postoperative treatment strategy for mGGOs. The EGFR - TKI therapy appears to be beneficial for GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma patients. However, further research is required to determine the optimal timing and approach for maximizing the therapeutic effect. Based on our findings, EGFR - TKI therapy can be considered as a viable treatment option for eligible patients.Keywords: Multiple ground-glass opacity (GGO), Synchronous multiple primary lung cancer (SMPLC), Epidermal growth factor receptor-tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI)

Introduction

Lung ground - glass opacity (GGO) is defined as a hazy area of increased attenuation on computed tomography (CT) scans, with the bronchial and vascular structures within the area remaining clearly visible [1]. The widespread implementation of low - dose CT for lung cancer screening has led to a significant increase in the detection of GGO lesions [2].

Among newly identified GGOs, persistent GGOs are often associated with early - stage lung adenocarcinoma, spanning a spectrum from pre - invasive to invasive malignancies. Synchronous multiple primary lung cancer (SMPLC) occurs when multiple malignant lesions are detected simultaneously. When feasible from a medical and technical perspective, surgical resection is the preferred approach for suspected malignant lesions in SMPLC patients. However, managing residual GGO lesions that were not resected along with the main tumor in SMPLC patients remains a challenge, particularly for those who are unfit or reluctant to undergo additional surgery. Despite the growing incidence of GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma, the optimal treatment strategy remains undetermined. Previous studies have shown that chemotherapy and immunotherapy are relatively ineffective against GGO - presented lung cancer [3, 4]. Multiple reports have explored the association between GGOs and a high prevalence of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations [5, 6]. However, due to the generally early - stage nature (stage 0 or stage I) of GGO - featured lung adenocarcinomas, conducting clinical trials of targeted therapy in this patient population is challenging.

In this study, we report on patients with multiple ground - glass opacities who had the major EGFR - mutated lesion surgically removed, followed by postoperative adjuvant treatment with EGFR - TKIs. Our primary objective was to evaluate the impact of EGFR - TKIs on the natural progression of co - existing persistent GGOs. We also aimed to discuss the potential of this treatment approach in the context of current clinical challenges and contribute to the understanding of treating GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma. Existing studies have confirmed the high prevalence of EGFR mutations in GGO-featured lung adenocarcinoma, but direct evidence of EGFR-TKI efficacy for residual postoperative GGOs is scarce due to the early-stage nature of these lesions and the lack of targeted clinical trials. This case report fills a gap by providing real-world evidence of EGFR-TKI-induced radiological regression (partial to complete absorption) of residual GGOs in EGFR-mutant SMPLC patients, offering preliminary clues for addressing the unmet clinical need of managing unresectable residual GGOs.

Case report

Case 1

In September 2019, a 59 - year - old asymptomatic female was admitted to our hospital. Her lung lesions were first detected during a routine health check. Chest CT scans revealed an irregularly shaped solid lesion in the right upper lobe, measuring 30 mm, and a GGO lesion in the left upper lobe, measuring 10 mm. There was no enlargement of the mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes. A positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) scan was performed to rule out distant metastasis and assess the feasibility of surgery. PET/CT scan showed no increased fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) uptake in the left upper lobe GGO lesion (SUVmax = 0.8), while the right upper lobe solid lesion demonstrated elevated FDG uptake (SUVmax = 6.2). Combined with the persistence of the GGO lesion on follow-up CT scans prior to surgery (documented in 6-month interval screening), the absence of inflammatory symptoms (e.g., fever, cough, elevated inflammatory markers) and the presence of EGFR mutation in the primary lesion, the malignant potential of the contralateral GGO was highly suspected. Subsequently, the patient underwent right upper lobectomy and mediastinal lymph node dissection via a uniportal video - assisted thoracic surgery. Histopathological examination confirmed a 2.5 - cm low - to - medium - differentiated adenocarcinoma in the right upper lobe, with a nodal staging of N2. Next - generation sequencing - based genetic testing detected an EGFR L858R mutation at exon 21, with an allele frequency of 12.25%. Considering the histopathological findings, the patient was diagnosed with stage IIIA lung cancer (T1cN2M0). Post - surgery, gefitinib - based targeted therapy was initiated as the follow - up treatment. One month after starting the targeted treatment, a chest CT scan showed partial absorption of the left upper lobe GGO lesion. Three months after treatment initiation, the lesion was completely absorbed. As of now, the patient continues to receive maintenance gefitinib treatment, and no signs of tumor recurrence or metastasis have been observed (Fig. 1).

Case 2

In September 2022, a 55 - year - old asymptomatic female was admitted to our hospital due to lung lesions detected during a routine health examination. PET/CT scans revealed an irregularly shaped mixed - density lesion in the right upper lobe (measuring 40 × 25 mm) and a mixed GGO lesion in the left upper lobe (measuring 20 × 15 mm), with no evidence of distant metastasis. PET/CT scan revealed mild FDG uptake in the left upper lobe mixed GGO lesion (SUVmax = 1.5), and marked FDG uptake in the right upper lobe mixed-density lesion (SUVmax = 7.0). The patient had no history of recent infection or inflammatory lung disease, and laboratory tests (C-reactive protein, white blood cell count) were within normal ranges. These findings, coupled with the synchronous detection of the EGFR-mutated primary lesion, supported the malignant nature of the contralateral GGO. The patient then underwent right upper lobectomy and mediastinal lymph node dissection through a uniportal video - assisted thoracic operation. Histopathological analysis of the resected specimen showed a 2.5 - cm low - to - medium - differentiated adenocarcinoma in the left upper lobe, with a nodal staging of N1. Genetic testing using next - generation sequencing identified an EGFR Del mutation at exon 19, with an allele frequency of 12.25%. Based on the histopathological results, the patient was diagnosed with stage IIB lung cancer (T2N1M0). Post - operative gefitinib - based targeted therapy was chosen as the follow - up treatment. One month after starting the targeted treatment, a chest CT scan demonstrated partial absorption of the left upper lobe GGO lesion. Despite the shrinkage of the lesion, the patient requested its removal. Subsequently, the patient underwent pulmonary wedge resection and mediastinal lymph node dissection via a uniportal video - assisted thoracic operation. Histopathological examination of the resected tissue confirmed a 12 × 10 × 10 - mm minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA) in the left upper lobe. Unfortunately, due to issues with specimen processing workflow coordination and detection cycle limitations during the patient’s postoperative recovery period, EGFR genetic testing was not performed on this MIA lesion. We acknowledge this as a critical limitation. However, several indirect lines of evidence support the association between the lesion’s response and EGFR-TKI efficacy: (1) the MIA was synchronous with the primary EGFR-mutant lesion, and synchronous GGOs in SMPLC patients have a high concordance rate of EGFR mutations; (2) the lesion showed clear partial absorption only after initiating gefitinib treatment, with no spontaneous regression observed in preoperative follow-up; (3) no other therapeutic interventions were administered during this period. Currently, the patient is still on maintenance gefitinib treatment, and no signs of tumor recurrence or metastasis have been detected (Fig. 2).

Discussion

The advent of thin - section CT has led to increased detection of synchronous multiple primary lung cancers presenting as ground - glass opacities in clinical practice. When medically and technically feasible, surgical resection of suspected malignant tumors remains the primary treatment option for such patients [7]. However, in some cases, factors such as poor cardiopulmonary function or the presence of multiple lesions make it challenging to manage residual GGO lesions that were not resected along with the main tumor. Current GGO management guidelines rely on nodule size, characteristics, and growth rate. GGOs can represent a wide range of benign and malignant conditions. Tumor size, increased CT attenuation, and other specific structural features, such as bubble lucency and solid component percentage, are associated with the likelihood of GGO progression. Multiple studies have shown that chemotherapy has limited efficacy against GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma, both pathologically and radiologically, because of the normal progression of the GGOs. The optimal treatment approach for concurrent GGOs detected during preoperative or postoperative follow - up CT scans after resection of primary non - small cell lung cancer remains controversial.

Guided by the recommendations of the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology, this study investigated patients with GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma who had their EGFR - mutated primary lesions surgically removed and then received potent EGFR - TKI treatment as postoperative adjuvant therapy. In Case 1, the residual GGO lesion completely absorbed after targeted treatment, while in Case 2, it showed partial absorption before resection. These results suggest that EGFR - TKI treatment may be effective in managing residual GGO lesions in patients with EGFR - mutated lung adenocarcinoma.

The selection of EGFR-TKI agents in adjuvant therapy for EGFR-mutant lung cancer has evolved significantly with clinical evidence. The ADAURA trial, a landmark phase III study, demonstrated that adjuvant osimertinib significantly improved disease-free survival (DFS) compared with placebo in patients with stage IB-IIIA EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) after complete tumor resection, regardless of prior adjuvant chemotherapy [8]. This has established osimertinib as a standard adjuvant treatment option in many clinical guidelines. In our reported cases, gefitinib was chosen based on clinical practice accessibility and patient-specific factors at the time of treatment initiation.

Notably, both cases had residual GGO lesions postoperatively, and the therapeutic response of these lesions to EGFR-TKIs is the core focus of our study. Osimertinib, with its potent central nervous system penetration and prolonged intracranial DFS benefit, may offer additional advantages for patients at risk of micrometastases [8]. However, the efficacy of osimertinib specifically for GGO-featured lesions remains to be further validated, as the ADAURA trial did not stratify outcomes by radiological features like GGO. For patients with residual GGOs, avoiding postoperative platinum-based chemotherapy, which has shown limited efficacy in GGO-featured lung adenocarcinoma, and initiating adjuvant EGFR-TKIs (including osimertinib) early is theoretically more rational. This approach aligns with the principle of precision medicine, targeting the confirmed EGFR mutation while minimizing unnecessary toxicity from ineffective therapies. Future studies comparing different EGFR-TKI agents in adjuvant settings for GGO-bearing patients are warranted to optimize treatment strategies.

However, it is essential to acknowledge the limitations of this study. The small sample size of only two cases restricts the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, we did not conduct in - depth research on molecular mechanisms and biomarkers. Future studies with larger sample sizes and more comprehensive research designs are needed to further explore the potential of EGFR - TKI therapy for GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma, including identifying the most suitable patient populations, optimal treatment timings, and potential predictive biomarkers. This will help to improve the clinical management of GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma and provide more evidence - based treatment options for patients.

Conclusion

EGFR - TKI treatment for unresected GGOs combined with surgical resection of major lesions may lead to positive outcomes in patients with EGFR - mutated multifocal lung cancer. This treatment approach appears to be safe and effective and could potentially be a valuable therapeutic option for unresectable GGO lesions in EGFR - mutated SMPLC patients after primary surgery. However, considering the indolent clinical course of GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma, further research is crucial to determine the optimal timing and methods of targeted therapy for GGO lesions to maximize therapeutic efficacy. Given the small sample size and limited research on molecular mechanisms and biomarkers in this study, larger - scale and more in - depth studies are needed to confirm these findings and establish more comprehensive treatment guidelines. This will contribute to better treatment decisions and improved prognosis for patients with GGO - featured lung adenocarcinoma.

A co-operational bivariant theory derived from cohomology operations Thanks: keywords : bivariant theory, operational bivariant theory, cohomology operation Mathematics Subject Classification 2000 : 55N35, 55S99, 14F99
Shoji Yokura

Abstract
. A co-operational bivariant theory is a “dual” version of Fulton–MacPherson’s operational bivaiant theory. For a given contravariant functor we define a generalized cohomology operation for continuous maps having sections, using cohomology operations. This generalized cohomology operation is related to Quillen’s Steenrod power operation. Using this generalized cohomology operation, we define a co-operational bivariant theory derived from cohomology operations , which is a subtheory of the co-operational bivariant theory associated to the contravariant functor.

A bivariant theory unifies both a covariant theory and a contravariant theory in the following sense:
For a bivariant theory
\mathbb{B}
, its associated covariant functors and contravariant functors are defined as follows:

(1)

\mathbb{B}_{*}(X):=\mathbb{B}(X\xrightarrow{a_{X}}pt)
is covariant for confined morphisms and the grading is given by
\mathbb{B}_{i}(X):=\mathbb{B}^{-i}(X\xrightarrow{a_{X}}pt)
.

(2)

\mathbb{B}^{*}(X):=\mathbb{B}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
is contravariant for all morphisms and the grading is given by
\mathbb{B}^{j}(X):=\mathbb{B}^{j}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
.

A typical example of a bivariant theory is the bivariant homology theory
\mathbb{H}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)

constructed from the singular cohomology theory
H^{*}(-)
, which unifies the Borel–Moore homology
H_{*}^{BM}(X):=\mathbb{H}^{-*}(X\to pt)
and the singular cohomology
H^{*}(X):=\mathbb{H}^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
. Here the underlying category
\mathscr{C}
is the category of spaces embeddable as closed subspaces of some Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and continuous maps between them (see
[
5
, §3 Topological Theories]
).
More generally, Fulton–MacPherson’s (general) bivariant homology theory

h^{*}(X\to Y)

(here, using their notation)
is constructed from
a multiplicative cohomology theory
h^{*}(-)

[
5
, §3.1]
.
Here the cohomology theory
h^{*}
is either ordinary or generalized.
A cohomology theory
h^{*}
is called
multiplicative
if for pairs

there is a graded pairing (exterior product)

h^{i}(X,A)\times h^{j}(Y,B)\xrightarrow{\times}h^{i+j}(X\times Y,X\times B\sqcup A\times Y)

such that it is associative and graded commutative, i.e.,
\alpha\times\beta=(-1)^{i+j}\beta\times\alpha
.
A typical example of a multiplicative ordinary cohomology theory is the singular cohomology theory. The topological complex

7.
A remark on
\delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)\subsetneqq\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)

In our previous paper
[
22
]
we remarked that
\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
consists of collections of cohomology operations of the contravariant functor
F^{*}
, i.e.,

(7.1)

\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)=\Bigl\{\{\theta_{X^{\prime}}:F^{*}(X^{\prime})\to F^{*}(X^{\prime})\,|\,g:X^{\prime}\to X\}\,|\,\theta\in coopF^{*}\Bigr\}.

Namely, if we use the notation in the previous section, the above (
7.1
) means

(7.2)

coopF^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)=\delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)=\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X).

It turns out that it is not the case, i.e., in general we have

(7.3)

coopF^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)=\delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)\subsetneqq\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X).

This observation is a byproduct of having considered polynomial operations as in the previous section.
Namely, if we consider the contravariant functors
\operatorname{Vect}(-)
and
H^{*}(-)
for
F^{*}
, then we do have the above strict inequality (
7.3
), as shown below. For example, each vector bundle
E\in\operatorname{Vect}(X)
itself can be considered as an element of
\mathbb{B}^{coop}\operatorname{Vect}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
, but it is clear that any vector bundle
E\in\operatorname{Vect}(X)
over a
fixed
space
X

cannot
be considered as a cohomology operation on the functor
\operatorname{Vect}
.

Example 7.4
.

We define

\Phi_{\oplus}:\operatorname{Vect}(X)\to\mathbb{B}^{coop}\operatorname{Vect}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)

by

\Phi_{\oplus}(E):=\{(\oplus E)_{g}:=(-)\oplus g^{*}E:\operatorname{Vect}(X^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Vect}(X^{\prime})\,|\,g:X^{\prime}\to X\}

where we define
(\oplus E)_{g}(F):=F\oplus g^{*}E
. We should denote the isomorphism class
[E]
of a vector bundle
E
, but for the sake of simplicity we just write
E
without the bracket
[{\,\,\,}]
unless some confusion is possible. Clearly
(\oplus E)_{g}
is a cohomology operation since we have the following commutative diagram due to the fact that
g^{*}(F\oplus E)=g^{*}F\oplus g^{*}E.

\textstyle{\operatorname{Vect}(X^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(-)\oplus g^{*}E=(\oplus E)_{g}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Vect}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{*}}
\scriptstyle{(\oplus E)_{\operatorname{id}_{X}}=(-)\oplus E}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Vect}(X^{\prime})}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Vect}(X).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{*}}

We note that
\Phi_{\oplus}
is injective, i.e.,
\Phi_{\oplus}(E)=\Phi_{\oplus}(E^{\prime})
implies
E=E^{\prime}
. Indeed, for the trivial zero bundle
0
over
X
we have
\Phi_{\oplus}(E)(0)=\Phi_{\oplus}(E^{\prime})(0)
, which means that
E=0\oplus E=0\oplus E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}
, i.e.,
E=E^{\prime}
. Therefore we have an embedding

\Phi_{\oplus}:\operatorname{Vect}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}^{coop}\operatorname{Vect}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X).

Example 7.7
.

Similarly we can consider the same things for the cohomology functor
H^{*}(-)
.
For a polynomial

Hence, when

and

, we have the following:

\displaystyle\delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)\cup\bigcup_{E\in\operatorname{Vect}(X)}\{p(x)_{\oplus}(E)\,|\,

In other words, we can say that our co-operational bivariant theory
\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\to Y)
has more structures than cohomology operations (in the case of
\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
) and generalized cohomology operations (in the general case of

).

Now that we have observed that in general
coopF^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)\subsetneqq\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
, in the definition of
\delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y)

by
\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
, i.e., we can define

Definition 7.8
.

\Delta\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{f}Y):=\begin{cases}s^{*}\circ\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X),&\text{if $f$ is a sectional map with a section $s$,}\\
\mathbb{0},&\text{if $f$ is not a sectional map,}\\
\end{cases}

Here
s^{*}\circ\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)
denotes the following:

\displaystyle s^{*}\circ\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{\{(s^{\prime})^{*}\circ c_{g^{\prime}}:F^{*}(X^{\prime})\xrightarrow{c_{g^{\prime}}}F^{*}(X^{\prime})\xrightarrow{(s^{\prime})^{*}}F^{*}(Y^{\prime})\,\,|\,\,g:Y^{\prime}\to Y\}\,\,\Bigl|\,\,c\in\mathbb{B}^{coop}F^{*}(X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{X}}X)\Bigr\}.

Here we use the fiber squares (
6.2
) in §6.

Then as a corollary of the proof of Theorem
6.3
, we can show the following

Corollary 7.9
.

The above set

is a bivariant theory with the same co-operational bivariant operations of product, pushforwad and pullback defined in §

. Hence

.

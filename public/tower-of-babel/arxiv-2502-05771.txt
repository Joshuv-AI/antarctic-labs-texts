Counting lifts of irreducible Brauer characters Thanks: *Corresponding author
Junwei Zhang, Xuewu Chang, Ping Jin*

Abstract
. Let p p be an odd prime, and suppose that G G is a p p -solvable group and φ ∈ IBr ⁡ ( G ) \varphi\in\IBr(G) has vertex Q Q . In 2011, Cossey, Lewis and Navarro proved that the number of lifts of φ \varphi is at most | Q : Q ′ | |Q:Q^{\prime}| whenever Q Q is normal in G G . In this paper, we present an explicit description of the set of lifts of φ \varphi with a given vertex pair ( Q , δ ) (Q,\delta) under a weaker condition on Q Q , and thus generalize their result.



1.
Introduction

Fix a prime
p
, and let
G
be a
p
-solvable group.
If
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
is an irreducible Brauer character, then the Fong-Swan theorem states that
there exists
\chi\in\Irr(G)
such that the restriction
\chi^{0}
of
\chi
to the
p
-regular elements of
G
is
\varphi
.
In this case, we say that
\chi
is a
lift
of
\varphi
, and we write
L_{\varphi}
for the set of all lifts of
\varphi
.
In
[
1
]
, Cossey proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Cossey)
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group, and let
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
. Then

|L_{\varphi}|\leq|Q:Q^{\prime}|,

where
Q
is a vertex for
\varphi
(in the sense of Green).

Our motivation is to generalize the following result,
which is the main content of Theorem A of
[
3
]
.
Note that Corollary B of that paper establishes Cossey’s conjecture
in the case where
Q
is abelian, so we will focus on the normal vertex case.

Theorem 1.2

(Cossey-Lewis-Navarro)
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, and
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
.
If
\varphi
has a normal vertex
Q
, then
|L_{\varphi}|\leq|Q:Q^{\prime}|
.

It is known that Cossey’s conjecture has a strong form for odd prime
p
.
Recall that a pair
(Q,\delta)
is a (generalized)
vertex
for
\chi\in\Irr(G)

if there exist a subgroup
W\leq G
and some character
\gamma\in\Irr(W)

with
\gamma^{G}=\chi
, and such that
Q
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
W
and
\gamma

is
factorable
with
(\gamma_{p})_{Q}=\delta
,
where
\gamma_{p}
denotes the
p
-special factor of
\gamma

(for definitions, see
[
10
]
or the next section).
In this situation, we refer to
(W,\gamma)
as a
nucleus
for
\chi

(see Definition 4.4 of
[
2
]
).
A crucial property of vertices, which is proved by Cossey and Lewis in
[
4
]

(see also Lemma
2.4
below), is that
if
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is a lift, where
G
is a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
,
then all vertex pairs for
\chi
are conjugate and linear.
In particular, the definition of vertices above is consistent with
that given by Navarro in
[
10
]
in the case where
p
is an odd prime,
and we also use
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
to denote the set of all members of
\Irr(G)
with vertex
(Q,\delta)
.

Conjecture 1.3

(Cossey-Lewis)
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, and let
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
with vertex
Q
.
If
\delta
is a linear character of
Q
, then

|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|,

where
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
denotes the set of those lifts of
\varphi
with vertex pair
(Q,\delta)
,
and
{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)
is the set of elements of
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
that stabilize
\delta
.

To state our result, we need to introduce more notation.
We use
\IBr(G|Q)
to denote the set of irreducible Brauer characters of
G
with vertex
Q
.
Following Lewis
[
8
]
, if
\varphi\in\IBr(G|Q)
and
T
is a subgroup of
G
,
we write
I_{\varphi}(T|Q)
for the set of those members of
\IBr(T|Q)
that induce
\varphi
.

The following is our main result, which generalizes Theorem
1.2
in vertex form,
and which presents an explicit description of the set
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)

of lifts of
\varphi
with vertex
(Q,\delta)
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group for some odd prime
p
,
let
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
have vertex
Q
, and let
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
be a linear character.
Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
such that

Q\in\Syl_{p}(N)
and
\delta
extends to
N
.
Write
T=N{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)
. Then the following hold.

(1)
For any
\chi\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
each irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
is factorable.

(2)
Each Brauer character
\eta\in I_{\varphi}(T|Q)

has a unique lift
\tilde{\eta}\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
.
Furthermore,
\tilde{\eta}^{G}
lies in
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
and the map
\eta\mapsto\tilde{\eta}^{G}

defines a bijection
I_{\varphi}(T|Q)\to L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
.

(3)

|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|
.

It is clear that if
\varphi
has a normal vertex
Q
,
then Theorem A applies (by taking
N=Q
).
Actually, as an immediate consequence of Theorem A,
we prove the following:

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, and
suppose that
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
has vertex
Q
.
If
K\vartriangleleft G
is a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup such that
KQ\vartriangleleft G
,
then

|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|

for each
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.

It is clear that Corollary
B
applies to the group
G=\GL(2,3)
and
p=3
,
by taking
Q\in\Syl_{3}(G)
and
K=O_{2}(G)
, so that
KQ=\SL(2,3)
.
Note that
Q
is not normal in
G
,
so Theorem
1.2
is invalid in this simple example.

All groups considered in this paper are finite, and the notation and terminology are mostly taken from
[
5
,
9
]
.

]

The following is Theorem 2.2 of

]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a
\pi
-separable group,
and suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(G)
are
\pi
-special and
\pi^{\prime}
-special,

\alpha\beta

Also, if

,

\alpha^{\prime}
is
\pi
-special and
\beta^{\prime}

\pi^{\prime}
-special,

\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta=\beta^{\prime}
.

In this paper, we are particularly interested in the case where

,
and in this case we say that
\chi
is

if
\chi=\alpha\beta
for some
p
-special character
\alpha

and
p^{\prime}
-special character
\beta
of
G
.
For convenience, we often write

\chi_{p^{\prime}}=\beta
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
,
and let
\theta\in\Irr(P)
.
Then
\theta
extends to

if and only if
\theta(x)=\theta(y)

whenever
x,y\in P
are conjugate in
G
.
In this case,
\theta
has a unique
p
-special extension to
G
.

Proof.

This follows by Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.15 of
[
6
]
with
\pi=\{p\}
.
∎

Recall that a character is said to be
p
-rational if its values are contained in the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}_{n}

with

not divisible by
p
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
is the subfield of the complex numbers generated over

th root of unity.
It is known that each

-special character is

-rational (see Corollary 2.13 of
[

]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, and
suppose that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is a lift.
If
\chi=\beta^{G}
, where
\beta
is a

-special character of some subgroup

with

-index in

, then
\chi
is also
p^{\prime}
-special.

Proof.

As mentioned above, we see that
\beta
is

-rational, which clearly implies that
\chi
is also

-rational. Then
\chi
lies in

by the comments after Theorem 5.2 of

(with

Note that

, which is a

-number, and
thus

is

-special by Theorem 4.2 of

]
.
∎

We need the following result, which appears in
[
4
]
as Theorem 1.2.

Lemma 2.4

(Cossey-Lewis)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and let
G
be a
p
-solvable group.
Suppose that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is a lift of
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
.
Then all the vertex pairs for

are linear and conjugate in

.

Finally, we present an elementary result, which is the Brauer version of Lemma 2.3 of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a group with
T\leq G
and let
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
.
Then the number of irreducible Brauer characters of
T

inducing
\varphi
is lass than or equal to
|G:T|
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\IBr(T)
are different irreducible Brauer characters inducing
\varphi
. Then each
\eta_{i}(1)=\varphi(1)/|G:T|
and

\varphi_{T}=a_{1}\eta_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\eta_{n}+\Delta
,
where

are some integers and
\Delta
is either a Brauer character of

Now
\varphi(1)\geq n(\varphi(1)/|G:T|)
, and the result follows.

3.
Proofs

We begin by introducing a useful notion.
Given a character
\delta
of
Q
, where
Q
is a subgroup of a group
G
,
we say that
\delta
is
G
-stable
if
\delta(x)=\delta(y)
whenever
x,y\in Q
are conjugate in
G
.

The following is Theorem A of
[
11
]
, which is key to our purpose.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group, and let
Q
be a
p
-subgroup of
G
.
Suppose that
\delta
is a linear character of
Q
that is
G
-stable.
Then restriction to
p
-regular elements defines a natural bijection
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)\rightarrow\IBr(G|Q)
.

We need a simple property of
G
-stable characters.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
N\vartriangleleft G
,
and suppose that
Q\in\Syl_{p}(N)
and
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.
Assume also that
\delta
extends to
N
and is
H
-stable,
where
H
is a subgroup of
G
containing
Q
.
Then
\delta
is
NH
-stable,
and the unique
p
-special extension of
\delta
to
N

is
NH
-invariant.

Proof.

Observe that
Q
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
N\cap H
,
and so
H={\bf N}_{H}(Q)(N\cap H)
by the Frattini argument.
This implies that
NH=HN={\bf N}_{H}(Q)N
.
To show that
\delta
is
NH
-stable,
we need to prove that
\delta(x)=\delta(y)
whenever
x,y\in Q
are
conjugate in
NH
. Now we can write
y=x^{hn}=(x^{h})^{n}
for some
h\in{\bf N}_{H}(Q)

and

,
and thus
x^{h}
and

are elements of
Q
that are conjugate in
N
.
It follows by Lemma
2.2
that
\delta(x^{h})=\delta(y)
.
Furthermore, since
x
and
x^{h}
are conjugate in
H
,
we have
\delta(x)=\delta(x^{h})
because
\delta
is
H
-stable.
This proves that
\delta(x)=\delta(y)
, and hence
\delta
is
NH
-stable.

By Lemma
2.2
again, there is a unique
p
-special character
\hat{\delta}
of

that is an extension of
\delta
.
For any element

, we see that
h
fixes
\delta
if and only if

fixes
\hat{\delta}
. Since
\delta
is
{\bf N}_{H}(Q)
-invariant
and
NH={\bf N}_{H}(Q)N
, it follows that
\hat{\delta}
is
NH
-invariant,
as required.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem A in the introduction,
which we restate here for convenience.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group for some odd prime
p
,
let
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
have vertex
Q
, and let
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
be a linear character.
Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
such that

Q\in\Syl_{p}(N)
and
\delta
extends to
N
.
Write
T=N{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)
. Then the following hold.

(1)
For any
\chi\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
each irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
is factorable.

(2)
Each Brauer character
\eta\in I_{\varphi}(T|Q)

has a unique lift
\tilde{\eta}\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
.
Furthermore,
\tilde{\eta}^{G}
lies in
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
and the map
\eta\mapsto\tilde{\eta}^{G}

defines a bijection
I_{\varphi}(T|Q)\to L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
.

(3)

|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|
.

Proof.

(1) By definition, there is a nucleus
(W,\gamma)
for
\chi
,
such that
\gamma\in\Irr(W)
is factorable that induces
\chi
,
Q\in\Syl_{p}(W)
and
(\gamma_{p})_{Q}=\delta
. In particular,
\delta
extends to
W
,
so it is
W
-stable.
Since
\gamma
is factorable,
we can write
\gamma=\alpha\beta
, where
\alpha=\gamma_{p}
and
\beta=\gamma_{p^{\prime}}
.

We claim that
\alpha
extends to a
p
-special character of
NW
.
To see this, observe that
Q
is also a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
NW

and
\delta
is
NW
-stable by Lemma
3.2
.
It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\delta
has a unique
p
-special extension
to
NW
, say
\tilde{\delta}
.
Furthermore, note that
\tilde{\delta}
is linear because
\delta
is,
so
\tilde{\delta}_{W}
is the unique
p
-special extension of
\delta
to
W
.
This forces
\tilde{\delta}_{W}=\gamma_{p}=\alpha
by the uniqueness of such an extension,
as claimed.

Next we need to show that
\beta^{NW}
is
p^{\prime}
-special.
Writing
\xi=\gamma^{NW}
, we see that
\xi^{G}=\gamma^{G}=\chi
,
and thus
\xi
is irreducible.
Note that

\xi=(\alpha\beta)^{NW}=(\tilde{\delta}_{W}\beta)^{NW}=\tilde{\delta}\beta^{NW},

so
\beta^{NW}
is also irreducible.
Furthermore, it is clear that
\xi
is a lift because
\chi
is,
and we deduce that

\xi^{0}=\tilde{\delta}^{0}(\beta^{NW})^{0}=(\beta^{NW})^{0}
.
This proves that
\beta^{NW}
is a lift, and since

\beta^{NW}(1)=|NW:W|\beta(1)
is a

-number,
it follows by Lemma
2.3
that
\beta^{NW}
is
p^{\prime}
-special.

Now we have proved that
\xi=\tilde{\delta}\beta^{NW}
is factorable,
and hence an irreducible constituent of
\xi_{N}
is also factorable.
Recall that
\xi^{G}=\chi
, so
\chi_{N}
has an irreducible constituent
that is factorable. By Clifford’s theorem,
we know that each irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
is factorable.

(2) By Lemma
3.2
again, we see that
\delta
is
T
-stable (with
\N_{G}(Q,\delta)
in place of
H
), and hence by Lemma
3.1
,
each
\eta\in I_{\varphi}(T|Q)\subseteq\IBr(T|Q)

\tilde{\eta}\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
.
Observe that

(\tilde{\eta}^{G})^{0}=(\tilde{\eta}^{0})^{G}=\eta^{G}=\varphi,

so
\tilde{\eta}^{G}\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
.
It follows that
\eta\mapsto\tilde{\eta}^{G}
is a well defined map from

to
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
.

Let
\hat{\delta}\in\Irr(N)
be the unique
p
-special extension of
\delta
to
N

(see Lemma
2.2
). We will prove that for any
\chi\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
there exists a
p^{\prime}
-special character
\alpha
of
N

such that
\alpha\hat{\delta}
is a factorable irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
,
and induction defines a bijection
\Irr(T|\alpha\hat{\delta})\to\Irr(G|\alpha\hat{\delta})
.

Actually, since
\chi_{N}
has an irreducible constituent that is factorable by part (1),
we can choose a nucleus
(W,\gamma)
for
\chi

with
N\leq W
, and such that
\gamma
is factorable with
\gamma^{G}=\chi
,
Q\in\Syl_{p}(W)
and
(\gamma_{p})_{Q}=\delta
(see the construction of
nuclei given at Page 2763 of
[
10
]
).
Let
\theta
be an irreducible constituent of
\gamma_{N}
.
Then
\theta
is factorable, and thus
\theta_{p}=\gamma_{p}
because
\gamma_{p}(1)=\delta(1)=1
.
This proves that
\theta_{p}
is a

-special extension of
\delta
to
N
,
so
\hat{\delta}=\theta_{p}
by Lemma
2.2
again.
Writing
\alpha=\theta_{p^{\prime}}
, we see that

, which is factorable lying under
\chi
.
Furthermore, it follows from Lemma

\hat{\delta}
is invariant in

, and by Lemma

,
we conclude that the inertial group

.
Applying the Clifford correspondence, we deduce that induction defines two bijections:

\Irr(I|\alpha\hat{\delta})\to\Irr(T|\alpha\hat{\delta})\;\text{and}\;\Irr(I|\alpha\hat{\delta})\to\Irr(G|\alpha\hat{\delta}),

which implies that induction also defines a bijection

Now we establish the surjectivity of our map.
Let
\chi\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
be as above.
Then there exists a character
\psi\in\Irr(T|\alpha\hat{\delta})
that induces
\chi
.
Observe that

and
\alpha\hat{\delta}
is factorable lying under
\psi
,
so similar reasoning shows that

(U,\mu)
for
\psi
with

,
and such that
\mu
is factorable and induces
\psi
.
Since
\psi
is a lift and

, we know that
\mu_{p}
must be a linear character.
It follows that
(\mu_{p})_{N}=\theta_{p}=\hat{\delta}
, and hence

.
Choose a Sylow

of

,
and let

.
Then by definition,

.

.

,

Furthermore, note that

.

Finally, we establish the injectivity of the map.
Let
\zeta,\eta\in I_{\varphi}(T|Q)
induce the same character

\chi=\tilde{\zeta}^{G}=\tilde{\eta}^{G}\in L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
,
where
\tilde{\zeta}
and
\tilde{\eta}
are the lifts in
\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
of
\zeta

\eta
, respectively.
Reasoning as before (with

in place of

),
there exist

-special characters
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
of
N

such that
\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta}
and
\alpha_{2}\hat{\delta}
are factorable irreducible constituents of

\tilde{\zeta}_{N}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{N}
, respectively.
Of course both
\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta}
and
\alpha_{2}\hat{\delta}
lie under

,
and thus they are conjugate in

Since

by the Frattini argument, we can write
\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta}=(\alpha_{2}\hat{\delta})^{x}

for some

,
which forces
\alpha_{1}=(\alpha_{2})^{x}
and

.
In particular, the element

.
This shows that
\tilde{\zeta}_{N}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{N}
have a
common irreducible constituent
\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta}
,
so
\tilde{\zeta},\tilde{\eta}\in\Irr(T|\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta})
.
Similar reasoning implies that induction defines a bijection

\Irr(T|\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta})\to\Irr(G|\alpha_{1}\hat{\delta})
,
and hence
\tilde{\zeta}=\tilde{\eta}
.
Since
\zeta=\tilde{\zeta}^{0}=\tilde{\eta}^{0}=\eta
,
we conclude that our map is injective.
This proves (2).

(3) Note that
|I_{\varphi}(T|Q)|\leq|G:T|
by Lemma
2.5
.
Also, it is easy to see that

|G:T|=|N\N_{G}(Q):N\N_{G}(Q,\delta)|=|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|.

By part (2), we deduce that
|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|=|I_{\varphi}(T|Q)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|
.
The proof is now complete.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we have the following, which is Theorem B in the introduction.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, and
suppose that
\varphi\in\IBr(G)
has vertex
Q
.
If
K\vartriangleleft G
is a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup such that
KQ\vartriangleleft G
,
then
|L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)|\leq|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):{\bf N}_{G}(Q,\delta)|
for each
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.
In particular, we have
|L_{\varphi}|\leq|Q:Q^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

We may assume that
L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)
is nonempty.
Then
\delta
is a linear character of
Q
by Lemma
2.4
.
Writing
N=KQ
, we see that
Q\in\Syl_{p}(N)
and
\delta
extends to
N
.
Now Theorem A applies, and the result follows.
∎

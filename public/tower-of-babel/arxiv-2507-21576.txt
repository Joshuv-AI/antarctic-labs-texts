Optimal control of stochastic homogenous systems
Ying Hu, Xiaomin Shi, Zuo Quan Xu

Abstract
. This paper investigates a new class of homogeneous stochastic control problems with cone control constraints, extending the classical homogeneous stochastic linear-quadratic (LQ) framework to encompass nonlinear system dynamics and non-quadratic cost functionals. We demonstrate that, analogous to the LQ case, the optimal controls and value functions for these generalized problems are intimately connected to a novel class of highly nonlinear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs). We establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions to these BSDEs under three distinct sets of conditions, employing techniques such as truncation functions and logarithmic transformations. Furthermore, we derive explicit feedback representations for the optimal controls and value functions in terms of the solutions to these BSDEs, supported by rigorous verification arguments. Our general solvability conditions allow us to recover many known results for homogeneous LQ problems, including both standard and singular cases, as special instances of our framework.



Keywords:

Homogenous system; stochastic LQ problem; cone constraint; BSDE; existence and uniqueness; verification theorem.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020):

93E20. 60H10.

1.
Introduction

Consider a controlled stochastic differential equation (SDE):

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\!X_{t}=b_{t}(X_{t},u_{t})\operatorname{d}\!t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t},u_{t})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x,

and a related cost functional:

(1.2)

\displaystyle J(X,u):={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}f_{t}(X_{t},u_{t})\operatorname{d}\!t+g(X_{T})\Big],

where
u
is the control variable,
X
is the corresponding state variable, and
b
,
\sigma
,
f
,
g
are given deterministic or stochastic functions.
The stochastic control problem associated with (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is to minimize over control processes the cost functional
J
, and determine the associated value function:

(1.3)

\displaystyle V(x):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}J(X,u).

More precise formulation will be given in the next section.

A vast number of papers deal with the above stochastic control problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) subject to various conditions. Please refer to the seminal books Yong and Zhou
[
21
]
and Pham
[
15
]
for a systematic account on stochastic optimal control.
There are two well-known approaches to tackle the problem nowadays.
The first one is called the dynamic programming. It leads to the study of the so-called Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations when all the functions
b,\sigma,f,g
are deterministic. The other approach is called the (stochastic) maximum principle, which provides a necessary condition for optimal control; see, e.g., Peng
[
13
]
, Hu, Ji and Xue
[
5
]
. It leads to the study of a system of forward and backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) when some of the maps
b,\sigma,f,g
are stochastic. Generally, such FBSDEs are fully coupled so that they cannot be further reduced to simple forms.

But when the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is a linear-quadratic (LQ) problem, where the controlled dynamics is in linear form (i.e.
b
and
\sigma
are linear functions of
(x,u)
) and the cost functional
J
in quadratic form (i.e.
f
and
g
are quadratic functions of
(x,u)
),
its FBSDEs system can be decoupled through the famous stochastic Riccati equation (SRE). And it admits elegant optimal state feedback control and the optimal cost value in terms of the solution to the SRE, see, e.g., Wonham
[
20
]
for deterministic coefficients case. When the coefficients are stochastic processes, Bismut
[
1
]
firstly finds that the related SRE becomes a highly nonlinear backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE). Unfortunately he could not show the existence of such a solution in general. Since then, numerous progresses have been made in solving SREs. Peng
[
14
]
proves the existence of a unique solution to the SRE when
\sigma
dose not contain the control variable. Kohlmann and Tang
[
12
]
solves the existence and uniqueness issues for one-dimensional SREs, then Tang
[
18
,
19
]
addresses the matrix-valued case using the stochastic maximum principle and dynamic programming method respectively. For the indefinite case, please refer to Du
[
3
]
, Hu and Zhou
[
7
]
, Qian and Zhou
[
16
]
, Sun, Xiong and Yong
[
17
]
and Yu
[
22
]
.

Example 1.1
.

Suppose that
\underline{A}
,
\overline{A}
,
\underline{B}
,
\overline{B}
,

,

,

,

are predictable processes of suitable dimension,

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\!X_{t}=(\underline{A}_{t}X_{t}^{+}-\overline{A}_{t}X_{t}^{-}+\underline{B}_{t}^{\top}u^{+}_{t}-\overline{B}_{t}^{\top}u^{-}_{t})\operatorname{d}\!t+(C_{t}X_{t}+D_{t}u_{t})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x\in{\mathbb{R}},

and cost functional

(1.5)

\displaystyle J(X,u):={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}(|Q_{t}X_{t}|^{p}+|R_{t}u_{t}|^{p})\operatorname{d}\!t+|GX_{T}|^{p}\Big].

Noting
\underline{A}
,
\overline{A}
,
\underline{B}
,
\overline{B}
,
C
,

,
Q
,
R
are stochastic processes and

is not differentiable w.r.t.

, both the dynamic programming approach and the stochastic maximum principle fail to handle this problem.
To the best of our knowledge, no existing results in the literature can cover this example.

Inspired by LQ problems and the above toy example, this paper proposes a new class of homogeneous stochastic control problems, which extend the classical homogeneous LQ problems to new ones with possibly nonlinear controlled dynamics and non-quadratic cost functionals, but their optimal solutions can be characterized by pure BSDEs, rather than fully coupled FBSDEs.

To introduce our new class of problems, we first define homogeneous functions as follows.

Definition 1.1

(Homogeneous function)
.

Given a real constant
p\neq 0
and a closed cone
\mathscr{Y}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, a function
\varphi:\mathscr{Y}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called a homogeneous function of degree

in

if

\varphi(\lambda y)=\lambda^{p}\varphi(y)

holds for any scalar
\lambda>0
and
y\in\mathscr{Y}
.

Example 1.2
.

Suppose
p>0
.
Then the following functions, with coefficients being possibly random, are homogeneous of degree
p
in
(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
:

|Ax+Bu|^{p},~~|Ax+B|u||^{p},~~|x|^{p}+|u|^{p},~~(|x|^{2}+|u|^{2})^{p/2},

A|x|^{p/2}|u|^{p/2}+B|x|^{p/3}|u|^{2p/3},~~|\underline{A}x^{+}+\overline{A}x^{-}+\underline{B}^{\top}u^{+}-\overline{B}^{\top}u^{-}|^{p},

\inf_{(A,B)}|Ax+B^{\top}u|^{p},~~\sup_{(A,B)}|Ax+B^{\top}u|^{p}.

As usual,
x^{+}:=(x_{1}^{+},...,x_{k}^{+})^{\top},\ x^{-}:=(-x)^{+}
for

, where

We remark that any homogeneous function in

is still a homogeneous function of the same degree, where

is any function.

In this paper, we only focus on scalar-valued state process, namely
X_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}
in the process (
1.1
) and the controls are constrained to a closed cone
\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. The stochastic control problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is supposed to satisfy the following homogeneous assumption.

Assumption 1.1

(Homogeneous system)
.

For all
t\in[0,T]
, the dynamic coefficients
b_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}
are homogeneous functions of degree
1
in
(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times\Gamma
; and the cost functional coefficients

and

are homogeneous functions of degree

in

and

respectively.

By setting
\lambda=2
and

in Definition

, we see

if
\varphi
is homogeneous of degree
p\neq 0
. Therefore, all the coefficients
b_{t}
,
\sigma_{t}
,

and

are zero at the origin of coordinates under Assumption
1.1
. We will use this fact without claim in the future.

Definition 1.2

(Homogeneous stochastic control problem)
.

The problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is called a homogeneous stochastic control problem of degree
p
, if the coefficients
b,\sigma,f,g
satisfy Assumption
1.1
with
p\neq 0
.

Clearly, the classical homogeneous LQ control problems are special homogeneous problems of degree
2
, therefore the new class of homogeneous problems, which allows non-linear state processes and non-quadratic cost functionals, is an extension of the homogeneous LQ problems.

This paper focuses on the solvability of the homogeneous stochastic control problems under three different conditions.
Although mainly focus on the case
p>1
, many parallel results can be established for the case
p<0
.
We encourage the interested readers to give the details. The remainder case is complex.

The idea to solve homogeneous stochastic control problems stems from Hu and Zhou
[
8
]
and Ji, Jin and Shi
[
9
]
. It is well known that for LQ stochastic control problems, the optimal controls take state feedback form through the famous SREs, see, e.g., Tang
[
18
]
. By performing Tanaka’s formula to
X^{+}
and
X^{-}
respectively, it is proved in
[
8
]
that the cone-constrained stochastic LQ problem admits a piecewise linear feedback optimal control expressed in terms of the solutions to two BSDEs. The above method (combined with some extra convex duality analysis) still works for mean-variance portfolio selection problems with a specific kind of non-linear wealth dynamic studied
in
[
9
]
. In the models of
[
8
,
9
]
, the optimal (translated) state processes will never cross
0
, i.e., it will remain positive (resp. negative) if the initial state is positive (resp. negative). This phenomenon actually results from the homogeneity of the control system. Therefore, our new homogeneous stochastic control problems will inherit this property.

In this paper, we extend the models of
[
8
,
9
]
to a new class of homogeneous stochastic control problems with possibly non-linear state processes and non-quadratic cost functionals. We will show that the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) admits an optimal control, in a state feedback form via two highly non-linear BSDEs.
We firstly prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to these two BSDEs based on truncation function technique, log transformation and some delicate analysis.
The famous result of Kobylanski
[
11
]
on quadratic growth BSDEs plays a fundamental tool in our argument. Finally, the solutions of the associated BSDEs are used to construct a candidate control which is verified to be admissible, and optimal to the original homogeneous stochastic control problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).
We remark that the uniqueness of solutions to the BSDEs in
[
8
]
was obtained as a by product of the verification theorem. Here we will provide a direct proof, based on the bounded mean oscillation (BMO) martingale theory, which is interesting in its own from the point of view of BSDE theory.

The rest part of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we present some notation and the precise assumptions on the homogeneous stochastic control problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Section
3
is devoted to establishing the solubility of two associated BSDEs under three different conditions.
In Section
4
, we determine the optimal controls and optimal values to the homogeneous stochastic control problems via the solutions to the two BSDEs through a straightforward and rigorous verification theorem. Some concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

3.
The associated BSDEs

For

,
(t,v,P,\Lambda)\times[0,T]\times\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1,t}(v,P,\Lambda):=f_{t}(1,v)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)P|\sigma_{t}(1,v)|^{2}+pPb_{t}(1,v)+p\Lambda^{\top}\sigma_{t}(1,v),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2,t}(v,P,\Lambda):=f_{t}({-}1,v)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)P|\sigma_{t}({-}1,v)|^{2}-pPb_{t}({-}1,v)-p\Lambda^{\top}\sigma_{t}({-}1,v),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P,\Lambda):=\inf_{v\in\Gamma}\mathcal{G}_{1,t}(v,P,\Lambda),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2,t}^{*}(P,\Lambda):=\inf_{v\in\Gamma}\mathcal{G}_{2,t}(v,P,\Lambda).

Note that

Now we introduce the following two one-dimensional BSDEs:

\displaystyle P_{t}=g(1)+\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{G}_{1,s}^{*}(P_{s},\Lambda_{s})\operatorname{d}\!s-\int_{t}^{T}\Lambda^{\top}_{s}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s},~~t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle P_{t}=g(-1)+\int_{t}^{T}\mathcal{G}_{2,s}^{*}(P_{s},\Lambda_{s})\operatorname{d}\!s-\int_{t}^{T}\Lambda^{\top}_{s}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s},~~t\in[0,T].

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big[|f_{t}(\pm 1,0)|+|b_{t}(\pm 1,0)|+|\sigma_{s}(\pm 1,0)|^{2}\big]\operatorname{d}\!s\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}).

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{1,t}(0,P,\Lambda)

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
and
3.1
hold and

. Then there exists a unique nonnegative solution
(P,\Lambda)
to (
3.1
) (resp. to (

Proof.

We will focus on the solvability of BSDE (

For notation simplicity, we write
w_{1,t}(v)=\max\{|b_{t}(1,v)|,|\sigma_{t}(1,v)|\}

Then Assumption
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P^{+},\Lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v\in\Gamma}\Big[f_{t}(1,v)+pP^{+}b_{t}(1,v)+p\Lambda^{\top}\sigma_{t}(1,v)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v\in\Gamma}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta}\big(w_{1,t}(v)^{2}-L\big)^{+}-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)w_{1,t}(v)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta}\big(y^{2}-L\big)^{+}-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big].

Clearly,

\displaystyle\inf_{y^{2}\leq 2L}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta}(y^{2}-L)^{+}-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y^{2}\leq 2L}\Big[-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big]\geq-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|),

and

\displaystyle\inf_{y^{2}>2L}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta}(y^{2}-L)^{+}-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y^{2}>2L}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta}\Big(y^{2}-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}\Big)-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}}\Big[\frac{1}{2\delta}y^{2}-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)y\Big]

\displaystyle\geq-c(P^{+}+|\Lambda|)^{2},

so

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P^{+},\Lambda)

\displaystyle\geq-c((P^{+}+|\Lambda|)^{2}+P^{+}+|\Lambda|).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P^{+},\Lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{1,t}(0,P^{+},\Lambda)

\displaystyle=f_{t}(1,0)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)P^{+}|\sigma_{t}(1,0)|^{2}+pP^{+}b_{t}(1,0)+p\Lambda^{\top}\sigma_{t}(1,0)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq c(1+P^{+}+|\Lambda|).

Combining the above two estimates, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P^{+},\Lambda)|\leq c(1+(P^{+})^{2}+|\Lambda|^{2}).

Let us consider the following linear BSDE with bounded coefficients:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}\!\overline{P}=-\mathcal{G}_{1}(0,\overline{P},\overline{\Lambda})\operatorname{d}\!t+\overline{\Lambda}^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W,\\
\overline{P}_{T}=g(1).\end{cases}

Since

and

(\overline{P},\overline{\Lambda})

Take a constant

.

for

.
Since

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{1,t}^{*}(P^{+},\Lambda)|h(P)

\displaystyle\leq c(h(P)+(P^{+})^{2}h(P)+|\Lambda|^{2}h(P))\leq c(1+|\Lambda|^{2}).

According to
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]

(\hat{P},\hat{\Lambda})
to the following BSDE

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}\!\hat{P}=-\mathcal{G}_{1}^{*}(\hat{P}^{+},\hat{\Lambda})h(\hat{P})\operatorname{d}\!t+\hat{\Lambda}^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{t},\\
\hat{P}_{T}=g(1).\end{cases}

Notice that

is a solution to the following BSDE

\displaystyle\underline{P}_{t}=-\int_{t}^{T}c((\underline{P}_{s}^{+}+|\underline{\Lambda}_{s}|)^{2}+\underline{P}_{s}^{+}+|\underline{\Lambda}_{s}|)h(\underline{P}_{s})\operatorname{d}\!s-\int_{t}^{T}\underline{\Lambda}_{s}^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s},

where

Then the maximal solution argument of

\displaystyle\hat{P}\geq\underline{P}=0.

On the other hand, since

. It follows from (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}\!\overline{P}=-\mathcal{G}_{1}(0,\overline{P}^{+},\overline{\Lambda})h(\overline{P})\operatorname{d}\!t+\overline{\Lambda}^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W,\\
\overline{P}_{T}=g(1).\end{cases}

Applying comparison theorem to (

\displaystyle\hat{P}\leq\overline{P}\leq M.

Combining (
3.10

Define the processes

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}\!U=-\Big[\mathcal{H}^{*}(U,V)+\frac{1}{2}V^{\prime}V\Big]\operatorname{d}\!t+V^{\prime}\operatorname{d}\!W,\\

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(w_{1,t}(v)+1)^{2}}{\delta(M+a)}-c(1+w_{1,t}(v))(1+|V|)

\displaystyle>0,

Proof of Claim (iii).

We now prove

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\Big]<\infty.

This is trivial by (
4.10
) when
x=0
. We only deal with the case
x>0
since the case
x<0
can be conducted similarly.

Case I.

Thanks to Assumptions
1.1
and
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}|b_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|

\displaystyle=|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}|b_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|\leq|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}(\delta f_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})+L)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}(\delta f_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})+L+1)=\frac{1}{2}(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+(L+1)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}),

and

\displaystyle|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}(\delta f_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})+L)=\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}.

Let
a
be any real constant.
Applying Itô’s lemma
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
to
e^{at}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}
gives

\displaystyle e^{at}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}

\displaystyle=x^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\bigg(a|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}+p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)b_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s

\displaystyle\qquad\;+\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\bigg(a|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}+\frac{1}{2}p(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+(L+1)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p})+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p})\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s

\displaystyle\qquad\;+\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}.

Construct a sequence of stopping times as follows:

\displaystyle\theta_{k}:=\inf\Big\{

\displaystyle t\geq 0\;\Big|\;|X_{t}^{*}|>k\Big\}\wedge T.

Since
X^{*}
is continuous,
\theta_{k}
increasingly converges to
T
as
k\to\infty
.
By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,

\displaystyle\quad\;{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}\bigg|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq M{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{2as}|X_{s}^{*}|^{2(p-1)}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq M{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{at/2}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p/2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{as}|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X^{*}_{s}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{at}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]+M^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{as}|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X^{*}_{s}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{at}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]+M^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{as}\Big(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg],

where
M
is a constant independent of
k
and
a
.
Combining the above two estimates, we conclude, for
a\leq-\frac{1}{2}p^{2}L-\frac{1}{2}p-M^{2}L
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{at}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\bigg(a|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}+\frac{1}{2}p(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+(L+1)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p})\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\;+M^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{as}\Big(\delta f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\Big(a+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}L+\frac{1}{2}p+M^{2}L\Big)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\;+\Big(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+M^{2}\Big)\delta{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+\Big(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+M^{2}\Big)\delta e^{aT}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]\leq c,

where the last inequality is due to Claim (ii) and non-negativity of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
and
f_{s}
.
Now applying Fatou’s lemma, the proof of Claim (iii) in Case I is complete.

Case II.
In this case,
\sigma(\pm 1,\cdot)
is bounded. Recall that
x>0
and (
4.10
), applying Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle=x^{p}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(pb_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})-\frac{1}{2}p|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s+\int_{0}^{t}p\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(2pb_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})+(2p^{2}-p)|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg\}\bigg]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\bigg\{-\int_{0}^{t}2p^{2}|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s+\int_{0}^{t}2p\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}\bigg\}\bigg]^{1/2}.

The estimate (
4.4
) holds in Case II, so

\displaystyle\quad\;{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(2pb_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})+(2p^{2}-p)|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\exp\bigg\{c\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\Lambda_{1}|)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg\}\bigg]<\infty,

where the last inequality is due to standard estimate for BMO martingales.
It is left to show

(4.14)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Y_{t}\bigg]<\infty,

where

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\exp\bigg\{-\int_{0}^{t}2p^{2}|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s+\int_{0}^{t}2p\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}\bigg\}.

Since
\sigma(\pm 1,\cdot)
is bounded,
Y
is a martingale with
{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{T}^{k}]<\infty
for any
k\geq 1
. Then the estimate (
4.14
) comes from martingale inequality immediately. This complete the proof of Claim (iii) in Case II.

Case III.

Thanks to Assumptions
1.1
and
3.3
, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}|b_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|

\displaystyle\leq\delta p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}+L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}.

Similar to Case I, applying Itô’s lemma to
e^{Lt}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}
gives

\displaystyle e^{Lt}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}

\displaystyle=x^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ls}\bigg(L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}+p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)b_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\bigg)\operatorname{d}\!s

\displaystyle\qquad\;+\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ls}p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}

\displaystyle\geq x^{p}+\Big(\frac{1}{2}(p-1)-\delta\Big)p\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ls}|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s

(4.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\;+\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ls}p|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(X_{s}^{*}\right)\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})^{\top}\operatorname{d}\!W_{s}.

Define

\displaystyle\theta_{k}:=\inf\bigg\{

\displaystyle t\geq 0\;\bigg|\;|X_{t}^{*}|+\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s>k\bigg\}\wedge T.

Since
X^{*}
is continuous, by (
4.5
)
we see
\theta_{k}
is a sequence of stopping times that increasingly converges to
T
as
k\to\infty
.
Noticing

\displaystyle|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}|\sigma_{s}(1,\hat{v}_{1})|^{2},

recalling
\delta<\frac{1}{2}(p-1)
and (
4.12
), it then follows from (
4.16
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{Ls}|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X_{s}^{*}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\Big[e^{L\theta_{k}}|X_{\theta_{k}}^{*}|^{p}\Big]}{(\frac{1}{2}(p-1)-\delta)p}\leq\frac{Ke^{LT}}{(\frac{1}{2}(p-1)-\delta)p}.

We notice (
4
) holds except for the last inequality, so it follows from (

), (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and the above estimate that

\displaystyle\quad\;{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{Lt}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{Lt}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]+M^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\theta_{k}}e^{Ls}\Big(|X_{s}^{*}|^{p-2}\operatorname{\mathds{1}}_{X^{*}_{s}\neq 0}|\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{*},u_{s}^{*})|^{2}+2L|X_{s}^{*}|^{p}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq x^{p}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,\theta_{k}]}e^{Lt}|X_{t}^{*}|^{p}\bigg]+M^{2}\bigg(\frac{Ke^{LT}}{(\frac{1}{2}(p-1)-\delta)p}+2KTe^{LT}\bigg).

∎

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we introduced and solved a new class of homogeneous stochastic control problems with cone control constraints under three distinct scenarios. We provided explicit expressions for the optimal value and the state feedback form of the optimal control in terms of solutions to two associated BSDEs, established through a rigorous verification procedure. The solvability of these BSDEs is of independent interest from the perspective of BSDE theory.

There are several promising directions for future research. First, our analysis is restricted to the one-dimensional state process in (
1.1
); it would be interesting to investigate the problem (
1.3
) with a multi-dimensional state process. Second, when the state process exhibits discontinuities, such as those induced by Poisson jumps, new methods may be required to solve problem (
1.3
). Exploring these extensions could yield further valuable insights.

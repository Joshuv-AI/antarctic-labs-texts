Towards optimal control of ensembles of discrete-time systems
Christian Fiedler, Alessandro Scagliotti

Abstract
The control of ensembles of dynamical systems is an intriguing and challenging problem, arising for example in quantum control. We initiate the investigation of optimal control of ensembles of discrete-time systems, focusing on minimising the average finite horizon cost over the ensemble. For very general nonlinear control systems and stage and terminal costs, we establish existence of minimisers under mild assumptions. Furthermore, we provide a Γ \Gamma -convergence result which enables consistent approximation of the challenging ensemble optimal control problem, for example, by using empirical probability measures over the ensemble. Our results form a solid foundation for discrete-time optimal control of ensembles, with many interesting avenues for future research.



I
Introduction

In some control applications, the intriguing problem of controlling
simultaneously
a whole ensemble of uncoupled dynamical systems arises.
This setup appears typically in two situations.
First, one might have a collection of dynamical systems that can only be controlled collectively, i.e., the same control input is applied to all systems in the ensemble simultaneously. This happens for example in some quantum control problems
[
8
,
12
]
or biomedical applications
[
13
]
.
Second, one might have a single control system subject to some parametric uncertainty, modelled for example by a probability measure over the uncertain parameters.
Controlling this single, but uncertain control system can then be interpreted as controlling a whole ensemble of systems, one for each instance of the uncertainty
[
11
,
2
]
.
Since the introduction of ensemble control setup in
[
9
]
, this problem has received considerable attention from the control community.
A particular focus has been on controllability questions, which are challenging even in the linear case
[
10
,
4
]
, and which are of particular interest in quantum control
[
1
]
.
Complementary to these issues,
[
14
]
has started to consider optimal control of ensembles of dynamical systems.
Conceptually, a cost functional is introduced for each system in the ensemble separately, and the costs are then aggregated over the whole ensemble.
In
[
14
]
, a distribution over the systems is assumed and the integral cost is optimised, corresponding to controlling the average cost of the ensemble,
while in
[
15
]
, the supremum over the individual costs is used for aggregation, corresponding to minimax control of the ensemble.
In both cases, continuous-time control-affine systems over a finite horizon are considered.

In this work, we initiate the investigation of optimal control of ensembles of
discrete-time
dynamical systems.
From a practical perspective, this is motivated by digital control systems and techniques like model predictive control,
and from a mathematical perspective, it is interesting since considerably more general systems and weaker regularity assumptions can be used.
Indeed, we consider general nonlinear control systems on general metric spaces, which is particularly interesting for distributed-parameter, hybrid, and digital control systems, and even the cost functions need only mild regularity assumptions, allowing a considerable degree of freedom in the choice thereof.
While some works have considered control of ensembles of discrete-time systems, e.g.,
[
16
]
, optimal control in a setting similar to
[
14
]
has not been investigated in a discrete-time setting before.
As a starting point, we focus on optimal control of the average finite horizon cost over the ensemble, as introduced in
[
14
]
,
and we establish the existence of minimising control sequences using the direct method of the calculus of variations under rather mild assumptions, cf. Theorem
6
.
Since optimisation of an integral functional can be challenging, cf. Section
IV
for some intuition on this, we consider approximations of the average cost optimal control problem, and we use
\Gamma
-convergence arguments to ensure the consistency of these approximations, cf. Theorem
9
and Corollary
10
.
While this approach has been introduced in
[
14
]
, our general discrete-time setup differs significantly on a technical level.

Outline
In Section
II
, we introduce our notation, the problem setup, and provide some technical preliminaries.
We show existence of minimisers of the ensemble optimal control problem in Section
III
,
and in Section
IV
, we establish a
\Gamma
-convergence result for the optimal control problems.
Section
V
concludes the manuscript.

Setup

We consider an

x_{+}=f(x,u,\theta),

Given an initial state
x_{0}\in X

{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}

\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(0)

\displaystyle=x_{0},

\displaystyle\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(n+1)

\displaystyle=f(\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}(n),\theta),\>0\leq n<N.

If the initial state is specified by a map

\phi_{\underline{u}}^{\theta}(n)=\phi_{{\underline{u}},\bar{x}_{0}(\theta)}^{\theta}(n)

(2)

2

.
In this work, we take the average cost with respect to a probability measure on
\Theta
.
From now on, we endow

,

\Theta
with their corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebras.
Before formulating the actual control problem, we have to make sure that the cost functional is well-defined.

Lemma 1
.

If for all
u\in U
,
f(\cdot,u,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(X)
measurable
and
\ell_{0}(\cdot,u,\cdot)

\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

is
\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

\mathcal{B}(\Theta)

then for all

.

Proof.

Let
{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}
be arbitrary.
Since compositions of measurable maps are measurable,
\theta\mapsto\ell(\phi_{\underline{u}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}(n),\theta)
is

-
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

\theta\mapsto F_{N}(\phi_{\underline{u}}^{\theta}(N),\theta)

\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

The result now follows from the fact that
\ell
and

are nonnegative. ∎∎

Assuming for the moment that
f
,
\ell
,
F_{N}
, and
\bar{x}_{0}\colon\Theta\rightarrow X
are measurable,
we can define for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
the averaged total cost functional
\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu)\colon U^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
by

\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)=\int_{\Theta}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),u,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

(5)

In the following, we are interested in the optimal control problem

\min_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu),

(6)

and we define the corresponding value function

\bar{V}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\mu)=\inf_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu).

(7)

II-C

Continuity properties

We start with a continuity assumption on the transition function.

Assumption 1
.

There exist
\alpha_{x},\alpha_{u}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that for all
\theta\in\Theta

d_{X}\big(f(x,u,\theta),f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime},\theta)\big)\leq\alpha_{x}\big(d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\big)+\alpha_{u}\big(d_{U}(u,u^{\prime})\big)

Lemma 2

Under Assumption
1

M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

\alpha^{[0]},\ldots,\alpha^{[M-1]}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}

d_{X}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M),\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\prime},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M)\big)\leq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\alpha^{[m]}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}(m),{\underline{u}}^{\prime}(m))\big)

,
\theta\in\Theta
, and
{\underline{u}},{\underline{u}}^{\prime}\in U^{M}

We use induction on
M
.
For
M=1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{X}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(1),\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\prime},x_{0}}^{\theta}(1)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=d_{X}\big(f(x_{0},{\underline{u}}(0),\theta),f(x_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\prime}(0),\theta)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\alpha_{x}(0)+\alpha_{u}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}(0),{\underline{u}}^{\prime}(0))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\alpha_{u}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}(0),{\underline{u}}^{\prime}(0))\big),

For

\displaystyle d_{X}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M-1),\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\prime},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M-1)\big)

\displaystyle d_{X}\big(s_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M),s_{{\underline{u}}^{\prime},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\alpha_{x}\big(d_{X}(\phi_{{\underline{u}},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M-1),\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\prime},x_{0}}^{\theta}(M-1))\big)

II-D

Background from calculus of variations

We first recall the general notion of
\Gamma
-convergence. For further details, we recommend the monograph
[
3
]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
(Z,d_{Z})
be a metric space, and let us consider a sequence of functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}\colon Z\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
The sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is said to
\Gamma
-converge to a functional
\mathcal{F}\colon Z\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with respect to the
d_{Z}
-topology
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

\liminf
-condition
For every
z\in Z
and for every sequence
(z_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that
d_{Z}(z_{k},z)\to 0
, we have that

\mathcal{F}(z)\leq\liminf_{k}\mathcal{F}_{k}(z_{k});

2.

\limsup
-condition
For every
z\in Z
there exists a sequence
(\bar{z}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
(also called
recovery sequence
) such that
d_{Z}(\bar{z}_{k},z)\to 0
and

\mathcal{F}(z)\geq\limsup_{k}\mathcal{F}_{k}(z_{k}).

We will also use the direct method of the calculus of variations, cf.
[
3
, Chapter 1]
for more background.
This involves in particular the notion of lower semicontinuity.

Definition 2
.

Let
(Z,d_{Z})
be a metric space and consider a function
g\colon Z\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We say that
g
is lower semicontinuous if for all sequences
(z_{k})_{k}\subseteq Z
such that
\lim_{k}z_{k}=z\in Z
, it holds that

g(z)\leq\liminf_{k}g(z_{k}).

(12)

Note that continuity implies lower semicontinuous.
Furthermore, we also need the notion of coercivity.

Definition 3
.

Let
(Z,d_{Z})
be a metric space and consider a function
g:Z\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We say that
g
is coercive if for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\{z\in Z\mid g(z)<t\}
is precompact.

It is clear from this definition that sums of coercive functions are coercive,
and that if a function is lower bounded by a coercive function, it is also coercive.
The next example provides more concrete instances of this definition.

Example 1
.

If
(Z,d_{Z})
is a locally-compact metric space and
z_{0}\in Z
, then
z\mapsto C\big(d_{Z}(z,z_{0})\big)^{q}
is coercive for all
C,q\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
In particular, since
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{p})
is locally-compact for all
p\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, where
\|z\|_{p}=\sqrt[p]{|v_{1}|^{p}+\ldots+|v_{d}|^{p}}
,
the map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni z\mapsto C\|z\|_{p}^{q}
is coercive for all
C,q,p\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

III
Existence of minimisers

Our next goal is to show that the optimal control problem (
6
) admits minimisers.
We first need a mild measurability assumption.

Assumption 3
.

For all
u\in U
,
\ell_{0}(\cdot,u,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
measurable and

is
\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(X)
measurable,
and
F_{N}
is
\mathcal{B}(X)\times\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
-
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

Remark 1
.

Since we work with Borel
\sigma
-algebras, Assumption
3
is fulfilled if
f,\ell_{0}
, and
F_{N}
are continuous.

The first ingredient for the direct method of the calculus of variations is lower semicontinuity, which we establish in the next result.

Lemma 4
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
,
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
and measurable
\bar{x}_{0}\colon\Theta\rightarrow X
,

U^{N}\ni{\underline{u}}\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)
is well-defined and lower semicontinuous w.r.t. the product topology on
U^{N}
.

Proof.

Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
together ensure that the measurability requirements of Lemma
1
are fulfilled, and hence the average cost functional is well-defined.
Let us now fix
{\underline{u}}^{\ast}\in U^{N}
and
({\underline{u}}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\subseteq U^{N}
such that
{\underline{u}}^{(k)}\rightarrow{\underline{u}}^{\ast}
in the product topology on
U^{N}
.
Observe that for arbitrary
{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),{\underline{u}},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}(n))

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta),

where we used the definitions of the cost functionals in the first two equalities,
the form (
3
) of
\ell
, and the fact that
\mu
is a probability measure.
Since
\ell_{u}
is lower semicontinuous by Assumption
2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\liminf_{k}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n)).

Next, for brevity define
\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n)=\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta\big)
and
\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n)=\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n),\theta\big)
.
From Lemma
3
, we get that for all
n=0,\ldots,N-1
, there exist
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

\big|\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n)-\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n)\big|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{n}\gamma_{m}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(m),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(m))\big)

which implies that for all
n=1,\ldots,N-1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\Big|\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n)-\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n)\Big|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\lim_{k}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\gamma_{m}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(m),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(m))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\lim_{k}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\gamma_{m}\big(d_{U}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(m),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(m))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\gamma_{m}\big(\lim_{k}d_{U}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(m),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(m))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=0.

This shows that for all
n=0,\ldots,N-1
, we have
\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n),\theta\big)\rightarrow\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta\big)
uniformly in
\theta
, and, in particular, for
\mu
-a.e.
\theta\in\Theta
.
Hence, by Fatou’s Lemma, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\ell\big(\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

Using a similar reasoning, we also find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta).

Altogether, we find that

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\mu)\leq\liminf_{k}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{(k)},\mu),

establishing lower semicontinuity of
{\underline{u}}\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)
. ∎∎

The second ingredient for the direct method of calculus of variations is coercivity.
The following assumption is sufficient for this.

Assumption 4
.

There exists a function
r\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that is coercive, and
\ell(x,u,\theta)\geq r(u)
holds for all
x\in X
,
u\in U
,
\theta\in\Theta
.

Since this assumption is somewhat abstract, we provide some concrete instances in the following example.

Example 2
.

Since according to (
3
) we have
\ell(x,u,\theta)=\ell_{0}(x,u,\theta)+\ell_{u}(u)
, where
\ell_{0},\ell_{u}
take non-negative values,
Assumption
4
holds whenever
\ell_{u}
is coercive.
Recalling Example
1
, if
(U,d_{U})
is a locally-compact metric space, this is the case if we choose
v_{0}\in U
and we set
\ell_{u}(v)=\lambda\big(d_{U}(v,v_{0})\big)^{q}
, with
\lambda,q>0
positive parameters.
This is essentially the setting considered in
[
14
]
.
For example, if
(U,d_{U})=(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{p})
, then by defining
\ell_{u}(v):=\lambda\|v\|_{p}^{q}
with
\lambda,q>0
and choosing
\ell_{0}
complying with Assumption
2
, it turns out that the coercivity condition of Assumption
4
is satisfied.

We can now establish coercivity of the average cost functional.

Lemma 5
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
and measurable
\bar{x}_{0}\colon\Theta\rightarrow X
,
the map
U^{N}\ni{\underline{u}}\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)
is well-defined and coercive w.r.t. the product topology on
U^{N}
.

Proof.

As in Lemma
5
, Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
ensure that the map is well-defined.
Since
r
from Assumption
4
is coercive, also the function
U^{N}\ni{\underline{u}}\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r({\underline{u}}(n))
is coercive w.r.t. the product topology on
U^{N}
.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),{\underline{u}},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Theta}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Theta}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r({\underline{u}}(n))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r({\underline{u}}(n)),

where we used the definition of the cost functionals, the fact that
F_{N}
is nonnegative, Assumption
4
, and that
\mu
is a probability measure.
Altogether, this shows that
U^{N}\ni{\underline{u}}\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)
is well-defined and coercive. ∎∎

We are finally ready to show that the optimal control problem (
6
) admits minimisers.

Theorem 6
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
and measurable
\bar{x}_{0}\colon\Theta\rightarrow X
,
the map
U^{N}\ni{\underline{u}}\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu)
is well-defined and there exists
{\underline{u}}^{\ast}\in U^{N}
with

\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\mu)=\bar{V}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\mu)=\min_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}},\mu).

(13)

Proof.

Combining Lemma
4
and
5
shows that
\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu)

IV
Approximation via
\Gamma
-convergence

For a generic
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
, solving (
6
) is in general intractable due to the integral in the definition of (
5
).
However, we can simplify the problem by approximating
\mu
by a more convenient probability measure.
For example, if we use an empirical measure
\hat{\mu}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\delta_{\theta_{i}}
,
problem (
6
) becomes

\min_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta_{i}),{\underline{u}},\theta_{i}),

(14)

which is closer to standard problems considered in numerical optimal control.
This leads to the question how good the solution of (
14
) is for the original problem (
6
).
As in
[
14
]
, we tackle this question using
\Gamma
-convergence.

Remark 2
.

We briefly discuss the existence and construction of empirical probability measures approximating a given Borel probability measure.
If
\Theta
is separable, which is in particular the case if
\Theta
is compact, then Varadarajan’s Theorem, cf.
[
5
, Theorem 11.4.1]
, guarantees that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
there exists
(\mu_{k})_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
, each
\mu_{k}
having only finitely many atoms, with
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
.
More precisely, if
(X_{k})_{k}
is a sequence of
\Theta
-valued random variables that are independent and identically distributed according to
\mu
, then
\mu
-a.s. the probability measures
\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\delta_{X_{k}}
fulfill
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
.
Note that this allows a data-driven approach, where
\mu
is unknown, but independent and identically distributed samples thereof are available.

Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
and a sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Theta)
with
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
, as well as a measurable
\bar{x}_{0}:\Theta\rightarrow X
.
Define
\mathcal{F}=\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu)
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu_{k})
, and we interpret
\mathcal{F}_{k}
as a sequence of approximations of
\mathcal{F}
.
In this section, we will establish the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
to
\mathcal{F}
, and deduce convergence of the minimisers.

IV-A

Assumptions

Compared to merely establishing existence of minimisers, we need slightly stronger continuity assumptions.

Assumption 5
.

The maps
\bar{x}_{0}
,
f
,
\ell_{0}
, and
F_{N}
are continuous.

Remark 3
.

Assumption
5
implies Assumption
3
.

Before moving on, we provide some intuition for continuity conditions contained in Assumption
5
.
Let us consider
\theta_{\infty}\in\Theta
and a sequence
(\theta_{k})_{k}\subseteq\Theta
such that
d_{\Theta}(\theta_{k},\theta_{\infty})\to 0
.
If we set
\mu_{k}=\delta_{\theta_{k}}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\mu=\delta_{\theta_{\infty}}
, then from a direct computation it follows that
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
.
In such a scenario, it turns out that
\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu)=J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta_{\infty}),\cdot,\theta_{\infty})
, as well as
\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu_{k})=J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta_{k}),\cdot,\theta_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Hence, in view of establishing a convergence result of
u^{*}_{k}\in\arg\min_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta_{k}),{\underline{u}},\theta_{k})
towards
\arg\min_{{\underline{u}}\in U^{N}}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta_{\infty}),{\underline{u}},\theta_{\infty})
, the joint continuity in
\theta
and
{\underline{u}}
of
(\theta,{\underline{u}})\mapsto J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),{\underline{u}},\theta)
plays a pivotal role.
In this regard, Assumption
5
provides sufficient conditions.

Next, we need the following technical assumption.

Assumption 6
.

Consider the
\Gamma
-convergence setup from above under Assumptions
1
,

, and

.

\bar{x}_{0}
,

For convenience, define for

\varphi_{n}\colon\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\>\varphi_{n}(\theta)=\ell_{0}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}(n),\theta)

\varphi_{N}\colon\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\>\varphi_{N}(\theta)=F_{N}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}}^{\theta}(N),\theta).

Lemma 7
.

Consider the
\Gamma
-convergence setup from above under Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
5

If

is compact, then Assumption

holds.

Proof.

Observe that
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
are all continuous functions since they are compositions of continuous functions.
Since
\Theta
is compact,
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
are all bounded,
and hence
\int_{\Theta}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)\rightarrow\int_{\Theta}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)
follows by the definition of weak convergence of probability measures. ∎∎

The next result states that uniform integrability of
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
is enough to ensure that Assumption

holds.
This is essentially the most general condition possible, cf.
[

Lemma 8
.

Consider the
\Gamma
-convergence setup from above under Assumptions
1
,
2
, and

.
If for
n=0,\ldots,N
we have that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k}\int_{\Theta}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\mathbb{I}\{{\varphi_{n}(\theta)>M}\}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)=0,

(17)

Proof.

Consider
\Theta

\zeta_{k}

\zeta

\mu_{k}
and

\zeta_{k}\rightarrow\zeta

\xi_{k}=\varphi_{n}(\zeta)
and
\xi=\varphi_{n}(\zeta)

Since
\varphi_{n}

The condition of the result now becomes

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k}\mathbb{I}\{{\xi_{k}>M}\}]=0,

IV-B

Convergence results

We are now ready to state the
\Gamma
-convergence result.

Theorem 9
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
,
5
, and
6
,
the sequence of functionals
\left(\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},\cdot,\mu_{k})\right)_{k\geq 1}
is
\Gamma
-convergent to the functional
U^{N}\ni u\mapsto\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},u,\mu)
with respect to the product topology of
U^{N}
.

Proof.

\liminf
-condition
Consider
{\underline{u}}^{\ast}\in U^{N}
and
({\underline{u}}^{(k)})_{k}\subseteq U^{N}
with
{\underline{u}}^{(k)}\rightarrow u
in the product topology on
U^{N}
.
We have

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n))

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta),

and we consider each of the three remaining terms in turn.
Since
\ell_{u}
is lower semicontinuous, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}\liminf_{k}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n)).

Next, for brevity define

\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n;\theta)=\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta\big)

and

\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n;\theta)=\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n),\theta\big)
for
n=0,\ldots,N-1
and
\theta\in\Theta
.
As in the proof of Lemma
4
, there are
\tilde{\gamma}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
with

|\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n;\theta)-\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n;\theta)|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{n}\tilde{\gamma}_{m}(d_{U}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(m),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(m))=:R^{(k)}_{n}
,
and since
{\underline{u}}^{(k)}\rightarrow{\underline{u}}^{\ast}
in the product topology, the right-hand side converges to zero for
k\rightarrow\infty
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we now have

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)\leq\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)+R^{(k)}_{n}

and a fortiori

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{\ast}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)+R^{(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}^{(k)}(n;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta),

where in the first step we used Fatou’s lemma for distributional convergence of nonnegative random variables, cf.
[
6
, Lemma 5.11]
,
which is applicable due to the continuity properties from Assumptions
1
,
4
, and
5
and the fact that
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
.

Finally, we can use the same arguments again to get that

\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)
.
Combining everything, we get that

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},u,\mu)\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}F_{N}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(N),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}\big(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{(k)}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n),\theta\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\liminf_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{(k)}(n))

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\liminf_{k}\int_{\Theta}J_{N}(\bar{x}_{0}(\theta),u_{k},\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\liminf_{k}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},u_{k},\mu_{k}),

establishing the
\liminf
-inequality.

\limsup
-condition
Let
{\underline{u}}^{\ast}\in U^{N}
be arbitrary. We define a sequence
({\underline{u}}^{(k)})_{k}\subseteq U^{N}
by setting
{\underline{u}}^{(k)}={\underline{u}}^{\ast}
, so trivially
{\underline{u}}^{(k)}\rightarrow{\underline{u}}^{\ast}
in the product topology on
U^{N}
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{(k)},\mu_{k})=\int_{\Theta}F_{N}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ell_{u}({\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n))+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta),

and from Assumption
6
we have that, for
k\to\infty
, the integral

\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)
tends to
\int_{\Theta}\ell_{0}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(n),{\underline{u}}^{\ast}(n),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)
for all
n=0,\ldots,N-1
,
and
\int_{\Theta}F_{N}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\theta)\to\int_{\Theta}F_{N}(\phi_{{\underline{u}}^{\ast}}^{\theta}(N),\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\theta)
.
Altogether, we obtain that
\limsup_{k}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}_{k},\mu_{k})=\lim_{k}\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\mu_{k})=\bar{J}_{N}(\bar{x}_{0},{\underline{u}}^{\ast},\mu)

V
Conclusion

In this work, we provide the first steps toward optimal control of ensembles of discrete-time dynamical systems.
We consider very general nonlinear systems, our optimal control setup encompasses a broad class of stage costs and terminal costs, and we focus on average total cost functions based on arbitrary Borel probability measures over the ensembles.
We were able to prove existence of minimisers of the corresponding ensemble optimal control problem, and we established a
\Gamma
-convergence result, both under mild assumptions.
The latter ensures the consistent approximation of the ensemble optimal control problem, for example, by empirical probability measures, which in turn allow the application of numerical algorithms.
Altogether, we build a solid theoretical foundation for optimal control of ensembles of discrete-time dynamical systems.
Ongoing work is concerned with aggregation approaches beyond averages, for example, using min-max formulations, as well as generalisations to the infinite-horizon case.

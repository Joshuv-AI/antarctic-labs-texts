Generalizing the enhanced power graph of a group with respect to automorphisms
Abbas Mohammadian, Ismail Guloglu, Ahmad Erfanian, Mark L. Lewis

Abstract
. We generalize the enhanced power graph by replacing elements with classes under automorphisms. We show that the connectivity and diameter of this graph is similar to that of the enhanced power graph. We consider the universal vertices of this graph and when this graph is a complete graph. Finally, we classify when this graph is the empty graph.

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
G
is a finite group. In recent years, there have been a great deal of interest in studying the interaction between groups and graphs. We recommend the reader consult the excellent expository paper by Cameron
[
5
]
to see body of work.

Perhaps the graph that has received the most attention is the commuting graph. When
G
is a group, the
commuting graph
of
G
is the graph whose vertex set is
G\setminus\{1\}
and two elements
x,y\in G\setminus\{1\}
form an edge when
xy=yx
. Herzog, Longobardi and Maj proposed studying a modification this graph in
[
15
]
and defined the
commuting conjugacy class graph
(or CCC-graph) of a group

as the graph whose vertex set is the set of nontrivial conjugacy classes of

where two distinct vertices

and
y^{G}
are adjacent if
\langle x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\rangle
is abelian for some
x^{\prime}\in x^{G}
and
y^{\prime}\in y^{G}
. In
[
15
]
, they determine the connectivity of the CCC-graph of a group
G
and give upper bounds for the diameter of the corresponding connected components of this graph.

In fact, one can generalize this graph even further by considering automorphisms. Let

be a group and take

to be the automorphism group of

. Given a subgroup

and an element

to be the

-orbit containing

. Notice that when

, then

, and hen

, we see that

is the conjugacy class of

. In
[
12
]
, the authors consider a generalization of the commuting graph by considering the graph whose vertex set is
\{g^{A}:1\neq g\in G\}
and given distinct
x^{A},y^{A}
, there is an edge between
x^{A}
and
y^{A}
if there exists
x^{\prime}\in x^{A}
and
y^{\prime}\in y^{A}
so that

and
y^{\prime}
commute. In that paper, the authors consider when this graph has a universal vertex and when this graph is the empty graph. When
G
is solvable they study the connectivity of this graph and its diameter. They also consider when this graph is triangle-free. This graph is studied further in
[
11
]
where they consider when this graph is connected and it contains at most one vertex whose degree is not less than three.

In this paper, we wish to consider similar modifications to a graph that was originally studied under the name cyclic graph (see
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
), but in most papers it is called the enhanced power graph (see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
). We note that there is an expository paper about results on enhanced power graphs available at
[

. The
enhanced power graph
(or cyclic graph) of

is defined to be the graph whose vertex set is

and there is an edge between

if

is cyclic. Following the idea of the CCC-graph, we define the

of

to be the graph whose vertex set is the set of nontrivial conjugacy classes of

and

if there exist

and

With this in mind, we now define the

of

x^{A}\leftrightarrow(x^{t})^{A}\leftrightarrow z^{A}
.

Assume
q=p
. That is, assume
x
is a
p
-element. Since
C_{G}(x^{t})
is not a
p
-group, there exists an element
y\in C_{G}(x^{t})
with
|y|=r
, where
r
is a prime different from
p
. Now,
x^{A}\leftrightarrow(x^{t})^{A}\leftrightarrow y^{A}\leftrightarrow z^{A}
. Hence,
\mathrm{d}(x^{A},z^{A})\leq 3
for an arbitrary
x\in G\setminus\{1\}
. Finally, if
x,y\in G\setminus\{1\}
, then a path between
x^{A}
and
y^{A}
of length at most six can be built by passing through
z
. The result follows.
∎

3.
Universal vertices

Next we want to look at the groups
G
admitting a group
A
acting by automorphisms on
G
in such a way that the graph
\Delta(G,A)
becomes naturally connected because it has a
universal vertex
(in the literature on groups with graphs this is often called a dominating vertex, but in the graph theory literature universal seems preferred), that is, a vertex which is adjacent to every other vertex.

Let G be a group. For any
x\in G
we denote the subset

{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)=\{y\in G:\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\text{ is cyclic}\}.

The intersection of
{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)
over all elements
x
of
G
is denoted by
K(G)
. Thus,

K(G)=\{g\in G:\left\langle x,g\right\rangle\text{ is cyclic for all ~}x\in G\}.

Observe that
K(G)
is the set of universal vertices for the enhanced power graph of
G
along with the identity. This set was apparently first studied in
[
20
]
(in another context) and in Theorem 1 of
[
20
]
,
K(G)
is proved to be a characteristic subgroup of
G
which is cyclic and is contained in
Z(G)
. We next begin to consider properties of universal vertices in
\Delta(G,A)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group with
A\leq{\rm Aut}(G)
and consider
x\in G
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The vertex
x^{A}
of
\Delta(G,A)
is a universal vertex

(2)

G=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{a}.

When this occurs, the following are true:

(i)

All subgroups of
G
of order dividing the order of
x
are
A
-conjugate to a subgroup
\left\langle x\right\rangle
and
G=\bigcup_{a\in A}C_{G}(x)^{a}
.

(ii)

Assume in addition that the conjugacy class
x^{G}
is
A
-invariant (which is for example the case if
A\leq Inn(G)
). If
x^{A}
is a universal vertex of
\Delta(G,A)
, then
x\in K(G)
.

(iii)

If
x\in K(G)
, then
x^{A}
is a universal vertex of
\Delta(G,A)
and
\left\langle x\right\rangle
is the only subgroup of
G
of order equal to the order of
x
.

Proof.

If
x^{A}
is a universal vertex, then for any element
1\neq y
there exists an automorphism
a\in A
such that
\left\langle x,y^{a}\right\rangle
is cyclic, which implies that
y\in\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{a}
. We conclude
G=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{a}
. Suppose
y\in G
has order dividing the order of
x
. Since
x^{A}
and
y^{A}
are adjacent, there is an
a\in A
so that
\langle x,y^{a}\rangle
is cyclic. Now, since
\langle x,y^{a}\rangle
has a unique subgroup of order
o(x)
, we conclude that
\langle x\rangle\geq\langle y^{a}\rangle
. This implies that every subgroup of order dividing
o(x)
is
A
-conjugate to a subgroup of
\langle x\rangle
. Suppose
g\in G
. We know that
x^{A}
and
g^{A}
are adjacent, so there is some automorphism
a\in A
, so that
\langle x^{a},g\rangle
is cyclic. This implies that
u\in C_{G}(x^{a})=C_{G}(x)^{a}
and thus,
G\leq\bigcup_{a\in A}C_{G}(x)^{a}
. We conclude that
G=\bigcup_{a\in A}C_{G}(x)^{a}
.

Conversely, suppose
G=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{a}
. Then for any
g\in G
, there exists an automorphism
a\in A
such that
\left\langle g,x^{a}\right\rangle
is cyclic. That is,
x^{A}
is adjacent to
g^{A}
, and therefore,
x^{A}
is a universal vertex of
\Delta(G,A)
.

Suppose
A
leaves
x^{G}
invariant. That is, for any automorphism
a\in A
there exists an element
g\in G
such that
{x^{a}=x^{g}}
. Since
x^{G}
is a universal vertex of
\Delta(G,A)
, we have
G=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{a}=\bigcup\limits_{g\in G}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{g}
. Thus, writing
X={\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)-\{1\}
, we obtain
\bigcup\limits_{g\in G}{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)^{g}=\{1\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{g\in G}X^{g}
and hence,
\left|G\right|\leq 1+[G:N_{G}(X)]\left|X\right|
. This is not possible since
X\subset N_{G}(X)-\{1\}
if
{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)
is a proper subset of
G
. So we get
{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)=G
, and hence,
x\in K(G)
.

Conversely, if
x
is a nontrivial element of
K(G)
, then clearly (as
x
is a universal vertex of
\Delta(G,1)
)
x^{A}
is adjacent to every other vertex in
\Delta(G,A)
. But
K(G)
is a characteristic subgroup of
G
which is cyclic and is contained in
Z(G)
. Therefore,
\left\langle x\right\rangle=\left\langle x\right\rangle^{a}=\left\langle y\right\rangle
for any automorphism
a\in A
and for any element
y\in G
whose order is equal to the order of
x
.
∎

We now present an example of a group
G
with an automorphism group
A
so that
\Delta(G,A)
has universal vertices where
K(G)=1
. Let
G
be an elementary abelian group of order
p^{n}
with integer

. Then we can consider
G
to be the additive group of the field with
p^{n}
elements. Let
A
be the multiplicative group of this field. Then

acts Frobeniusly on

and is transitive on

. For any element

, we have
{\rm Cyc}_{G}(x)=\left\langle x\right\rangle
and thus,

. We deduce that
\Delta(G,A)
has only one vertex, and in particular, is vacuously a complete graph.

We next show that if all of the vertices corresponding to

-elements are adjacent to all of vertices of

-elements in

when

has to be nilpotent.

.

Let

be a group with

. Suppose that for any two distinct primes

and

and for all elements

Proof.

In

∎

We can now show that if

is a complete graph, then

must be nilpotent.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

be a group with

We next see when we restrict to the case when

Proof of Theorem

4.
The structure of groups whose
\Delta(G,A)
has no edges

In this section we want to characterize the finite groups
G
admitting a group

acting by automorphisms on

in such a way that the graph
\Delta(G,A)
has no edges.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group with
A\leq{\rm Aut}(G)

is an isolated vertex of

if and only if

is an element of order

for some prime

,

is a
p
-group of exponent

Proof.

Suppose

\Delta(G,A)
. Thus, we can deduce that

if there exists an automorphism

such that
\left\langle x,y^{a}\right\rangle

is of prime order, say for the prime

. Suppose there is an element

. We see that

. Thus, the exponent of

is a power of

. For any an element

, we see that

an hence, there exists an automorphism

so that

was arbitrary, we see that

is acting transitively on

.

Conversely, suppose there exists an element

so that

for a prime

is a
p
-group of exponent

, and

be an

and

are adjacent. Thus, there is an automorphism

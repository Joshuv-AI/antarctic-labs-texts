Conjoined twin at abdomen: twin A anencephaly and face presentation with twin B cephalic presentation: a case report and review of the literature
Authors: Leta Hinkosa Dinsa, Asfaw Tadesse Mengesha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesConjoined twins are described as having been physically fused during pregnancy and delivery. They share some vital organs, such as the heart, lungs, liver, abdomen, etc. The article aims to present a unique discussion on conjoined twins at the abdomen, with twin A with anencephaly and face presentation and twin B with cephalic presentation. It is a conjoined twin fused at the abdomen. Twin A has anencephaly with lung and abdomen outside of the abdomen, with negative fetal heartbeat, and Twin B has cephalic presentation with a well-formed head and positive heartbeat. Conjoined twin is rarely diagnosed at delivery, but in resource-limited countries such as Ethiopia, it is diagnosed during delivery. It had a psychological impact both for pregnant women and the family. Therefore, appropriate counseling of pregnant women to have early ultrasound screening and follow-up is crucial.Case summaryWe encountered a rare case of a conjoined twin pregnancy involving twin A with anencephaly and face presentation and twin B with cephalic presentation. The patient, a 19-year-old woman from rural Ethiopia, presented at Nekemte Specialized Hospital with pushing down pain and a gush of fluid per vagina for 10 hours. After admission, an obstetric ultrasound revealed a twin pregnancy with twin A presenting with anencephaly (face presentation) and a negative fetal heart rate, and twin B presenting with cephalic presentation and a positive fetal heart rate. The patient underwent a cesarean section, and the procedure resulted in one conjoined twin with anencephaly and the other well formed. After a week of recovery, the patient was discharged with counseling for future pregnancies.ConclusionConjoined twinning is a rare phenomenon, occurring in 1 in 50,000–100,000. It is rarely diagnosed during delivery. However, in resource-limited setups, it is diagnosed during delivery.

Introduction

Conjoined twins are defined by the conjoined (shared) body area, resulting from incomplete monozygotic twinning. It is where the embryonic disc does not divide completely, or adjacent embryonic discs fuse. The earliest prenatal diagnosis enabling assessment for shared vital organs is desirable for optimal obstetric management of pregnancies. Sonographic diagnosis of conjoined twins has been reported, and has been diagnosed in the third trimester, especially during delivery, where sonographic assessment is available. The incidence of conjoined twinning is estimated to be 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 100,000 deliveries [1–4].

Conjoined twins represent one of the rarest forms of congenital anomalies. The incidence appears remarkably similar throughout the world, although an incidence as high as 1 per 20,000 births has been reported from Atlanta. Approximately 40–60% of conjoined twins are stillborn and an additional 35% survive only 1 day [5–7].

Conjoined twins are rare occurrences in obstetric/pediatric practice. More commonly known as Siamese twins, this phenomenon is shrouded in mystery and considered a curiosity by the public. Current technology is lending a helping hand in the early diagnosis of conjoined twins, with few cases of diagnosis during delivery time. Frequently, the twins are born dead, but in a few cases, the twins survive [8, 9].

Conjoined twins are infrequently encountered by obstetricians. Many of these pregnancies do not achieve a viable gestational age due to miscarriage or pregnancy termination, with exceptional cases arriving at term and being diagnosed during delivery. Much of the clinical care literature focuses on postnatal management from a neonatology and pediatric surgery perspective; guidance on obstetrical management of a conjoined twin pregnancy is limited. This case report aims to report the conjoined twin pregnancy at the abdomen with twin A with anencephaly, face presentation, fetal heart beat (FHB) negative and lung and bowel outside of abdomen, and with twin B with cephalic presentation, fetal heart beat positive, and well-formed head with cephalic presentation that was diagnosed at term during delivery at Nekemte Specialized Hospital. Routine prenatal ultrasound should be there with the ability to detect virtually all conjoined twin gestations. For families faced with a conjoined twin diagnosis, obstetric counseling must explore the expectant parents’ values while providing realistic and detailed expectations for postnatal care. Counseling should start as soon as the diagnosis of conjoined twins is made [10–14].

The importance of this research is to provide a report for a better comprehension of the various aspects related to conjoined twins. It gives healthcare providers an excellent opportunity to reexamine, reevaluate, and counsel all pregnant women to have ultrasonographic examinations at an early time in pregnancy.

Case presentation

This is a 19-year-old gravida 1 mother from rural Ethiopia (Western Oromia, Wayu Tuqa Woreda), amenorrheic for 9 months. She presented to Nekemte Specialized Hospital with pushing down pain and gush of fluid per vagina for 10 hours. She had antenatal care follow-up at Wayu Tuqa Health Center two times and received TT1 and TT2 during her follow-up. Her blood type is RH B+ and urine analysis was nonrevealing (Table 1). However, she had no obstetrics ultrasound examination. She has no fever, headache, vaginal bleeding, or epigastric pain. On examination, she was acutely sick-looking. Her vital signs were blood pressure (BP) of 110/60 mmHg, pulse rate (PR) of 92 beats per minute, respiratory rate (RR) of 20 breaths per minute, and temperature 37.1 °C. She had pink conjunctivae. The lymph glandular system, chest, and cardiovascular system were normal.

On abdominal examination, term-sized gravid uterus, with two uterine contractions in 10 minutes lasting 30–35 seconds, FHB+ (133 beats per minute), inspection of external genitalia showed no vulvar mass or lesion. On genitourinary on digital vaginal cervix was 4 cm, with face presentation. On the integumentary system, she had no palmar pallor. On neurologic examination, she was oriented to time, person, and place. She had normal reflexes and no neurologic deficits.

She was admitted to Nekemte Specialized Hospital with a diagnosis of the active first stage of labor plus rupture of membrane plus face presentation, then after 4 hours she was evaluated for cervical arrest, and an obstetrics ultrasound was done and she was diagnosed with twin pregnancies. Twin A had anencephaly (face presentation) FHB negative, and Twin B had cephalic presentation FHB positive. For cervical arrest secondary to poor uterine contraction in addition to twin pregnancy, she was prepared for CS after spinal anesthesia lower uterine segment tranisverse cesarean section (LUST CS) was done. Discussion

It is rare to diagnose conjoined twin pregnancy during delivery. It is a rare and complex anomaly of the newborn. They require a highly experienced center equipped to diagnose in early pregnancy. Conjoined twins have been a source of fascination for both the public and the medical profession since time immemorial. Their birth is viewed as an ominous sign of impending disaster. The birth of conjoined twins is a difficult and dramatic moment for parents. It is also a difficult situation for the team of physicians, midwives, and other required hospital staff to carry out treatment and care of these specific developmental anomalies [8, 10].

Many anomalies associated with conjoined twins are lost in utero or in early postpartum period. If the pathology can be diagnosed during the prenatal period, termination may be an option according to gestational age. In the absence of an intrauterine diagnosis, the situation is much more serious, and delivery can threaten the mother’s life as well as the fetus’s life. Therefore, encouraging all pregnant people to have early ultrasound is crucial for safe pregnancy and delivery [15].

The early diagnosis of conjoined twins is important in the management of the situation and in reducing maternal mortality through absenting evacuation, therapeutic abortus, and hysterotomy during the first trimester [13, 16, 17].

Prenatal screening and diagnosis of conjoined twins is usually performed by ultrasonography to characterize the diagnosis of conjoined twins. Conjoined twins have a low survival rate, and the prognosis is generally poor. Therefore, early antenatal diagnosis is important. The prognosis is mainly affected by specific fusion parts and related malformations. Due to the high incidence of complex heart defects, the survival rate of conjoined twins is the lowest. Therefore, neonatal morbidity and mortality rates may also increase. The risk of spontaneous loss of pregnancy also increased [17].

In our cases, the diagnosis was made during the third trimester at delivery time, which makes them very rare. As mentioned above, conjoined twins will most likely not arrive at term except in rare cases. This case report is not without limitations. The lack of ultrasound images is among its limitations.

Conclusions

Conjoined twin pregnancy is rarely diagnosed during third trimesters, especially during delivery. However, in resource-limited setups, it is diagnosed during delivery. Therefore, encouraging all pregnant women to have an early ultrasound examination is essential.

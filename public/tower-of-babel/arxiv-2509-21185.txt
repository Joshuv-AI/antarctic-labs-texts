HYBRID REAL- AND COMPLEX-VALUED NEURAL NETWORK CONCEPT FOR LOW-COMPLEXITY PHASE-AWARE SPEECH ENHANCEMENT
Luan Vinícius Fiorio, Alex Young, Ronald M. Aarts

Abstract
In this paper, we propose hybrid real- and complex-valued neural networks for speech enhancement. Real- or complex-valued models are either inefficient or present high complexity. We devise a straightforward design method for extending a real-valued network into its hybrid counterpart. Based on speech intelligibility and quality metrics, we compare the real, complex, and hybrid versions of a convolutional and a convolutional-recurrent architecture. The hybrid network consistently outperforms its counterparts with the same number of parameters. Additionally, the hybrid models’ complexity in terms of multiply-accumulate operations is substantially lower than that of their counterparts.

1
Introduction

Speech enhancement is the task of improving noisy speech signals, specifically for monaural audio, suppressing or removing unwanted background noise from a single-channel recording
[
1
]
. For this task, time-frequency features, generally obtained via the short-term Fourier transform (STFT), are often used for processing due to the efficient fast Fourier transform and its inverse operation
[
2
]
.

Some approaches discard the phase component for simplifying data handling and lowering complexity
[
3
,
4
]
, but the denoised audio intelligibility and quality is limited as phase is crucial for sound reconstruction
[
5
]
. Moreover, processing the real and imaginary parts of a complex STFT can lead to additional distortion and signal degradation
[
6
]
. Naturally, complex-valued neural networks (NNs) have been applied to speech enhancement as a solution for distorted phase
[
5
,
7
]
. Even with richer representation capacity and better generalization
[
8
]
, complex networks exhibit slower training rates due to automatic differentiation
[
6
]
and higher complexity
[
9
]
.

The scarcely explored combination of real and complex processing was previously tried by averaging results of real and complex models
[
10
]
or by combining real and complex processing in a two stage network
[
11
]
, obtaining better results when combined. Other approaches for speech enhancement
[
12
,
13
]
have successfully combined separate magnitude processing and additional phase filtering via complex filter coefficients. However, parallel real- and complex-valued processing with information exchange was not yet explored.

We propose a counterpart for real- or complex-valued speech enhancement models, the hybrid neural network. Following our previous study
[
14
]
, we combine real- and complex-valued branches with information exchange in the bottleneck, estimating both a magnitude filter and additional complex compensation. This concept can be applied to existing networks following our proposed design methodology. For two existing real-valued architectures, we devise their complex and hybrid counterparts having the same size. We show that the hybrid model is able to outperform the baselines while presenting substantially lower model complexity.

System and Problem Description

Let
S(t,f)
,
V(t,f)
, and
Y(t,f)
be the complex-valued short-term Fourier transform (STFT) of the clean speech
s(t)
, noise
v(t)
, and noisy speech
y(t)
, where
t
is the discrete time index,
f
is the discrete frequency index, and
Y(t,f)=S(t,f)+V(t,f).
Moreover, let
M(t,f)
be a ratio mask
[
15
,
16
]
which allows us to approximate the clean speech STFT from the noisy speech STFT as
S(t,f)\approx M(t,f)\odot Y(t,f)
and
\odot
represents the Hadamard product. In this work, a NN-estimated mask
\hat{M}(t,f)
is applied to the noisy speech as
\hat{S}(t,f)=\hat{M}(t,f)\odot Y(t,f)
. For the special case of hybrid networks, a magnitude mask
\hat{M}_{mag}
and a complex correction
\hat{S}_{cc}
are simultaneously estimated, applied to the noisy STFT as
\hat{S}(t,f)=\hat{M}(t,f)\odot Y(t,f)+\hat{S}_{cc}
. For the rest of the manuscript, the indexes
(t,f)
are omitted for simplicity. Also, note that we consider one complex-valued parameter equivalent to two real-valued parameters, given that a complex parameter is formed by two real parameters
a
and
b
, as
a+ib
, where
i=\sqrt{-1}
.

3
Hybrid Neural Network for Speech Enhancement

We propose a generic concept for hybridizing a neural network into both real- and complex-valued parameters. The idea is to combine the strength of both domains, allowing phase-aware processing while reducing the complexity of the model when compared to a real or complex model having the same number of parameters. Additionally, we include domain conversion functions, which exchange information between branches. In this work, we focus on autoencoder-shaped models – commonly used for speech enhancement
[
4
]
. Next, we define the design procedure of a hybrid network.

3.1
Design methodology

We start from a real-valued (subscript
\mathbb{R}
) baseline NN model (superscript
r
) with
N=N_{f}+N_{g}
parameters composed of an encoder
f_{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
with
\mathrm{size}(f_{\mathbb{R}}^{r})=N_{f}
and decoder
g_{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
with
\mathrm{size}(g_{\mathbb{R}}^{r})=N_{g}
parameters. Given the real model, we formulate straightforward design steps to create its hybrid counterpart (superscript
h
):

1.

Replicate the real-valued network with a real-valued encoder
f_{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
and decoder
g_{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
, defining new layer sizes such that, respectively, the real-valued encoder and decoder sizes on the hybrid network are
N_{f}/2
and
N_{g}/2
;

2.

Derive a complex-valued (subscript
\mathbb{C}
) encoder
f_{\mathbb{C}}^{h}
and decoder
g_{\mathbb{C}}^{h}
of the same shape as the real-valued parts, setting their sizes as, respectively,
N_{f}/4
and
N_{g}/4
complex (
N_{f}/2
and
N_{g}/2
real) parameters;

3.

Add domain conversion functions from the complex input to the real input, and after each encoder from real-to-complex and complex-to-real domain;

4.

Concatenate the output of
f_{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
(
f_{\mathbb{C}}^{h}
) to the domain-converted output of
f_{\mathbb{C}}^{h}
(
f_{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
) and input the concatenated tensors to
g_{\mathbb{R}}^{h}
(
g_{\mathbb{C}}^{h}
). Adjust the first layer of each decoder accordingly to support the concatenated input while maintaining a size of
N_{g}/2
real parameters.

If a recurrent bottleneck is present, we consider those layers part of the encoder. For comparison, we derive a complex-valued baseline (superscript
c
) from the real-valued model, having an encoder
f_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}
and decoder
g_{\mathbb{C}}^{c}
with
L/2
real-valued parameters each. All three cases are shown in Figure
1
. The domain conversion functions, complex-valued layers, and activation functions are detailed next.

Importantly, for the task of speech enhancement, we combine the hybrid model’s real-valued output
\hat{M}_{mag}
to the complex-valued output
\hat{S}_{cc}
as
\hat{S}=\hat{M}_{mag}\odot Y+\hat{S}_{cc}
, resulting in magnitude filtering and complex correction. We deliberately implement a complex correction via addition such that the magnitude mask handles most of the denoising, similar to state-of-the-art methods
[
12
,
13
]
, and the complex term provides a fine-grained compensation of phase – and magnitude accordingly – avoiding possible distortion created from multiplicative filtering.

3.2
Domain conversion

For converting between domains, we consider two different functions. First, the input is converted through the magnitude operation “Mag”, removing phase content from the STFT allowing the real-valued branch to focus on magnitude denoising. In the bottleneck, both conversion functions follow the Cartesian representation of complex number: from real to complex, a real tensor
R=[a_{r},\ b_{r}]
is converted into a complex-valued equivalent tensor as
R_{c}=a_{r}+ib_{r}
, where
a_{r}
and
b_{r}
are the first and second halves of the tensor’s frequency dimension
1
1

1

Note that frequency and time become abstract after the input is processed by the encoder. However, we refer to “frequency dimension” as being the second-last dimension of a tensor of four dimensions, named (batch, channel, frequency, time).
. Similarly, the conversion from complex to real branches in the bottleneck is done by splitting both real and imaginary parts of a complex tensor
Z=a_{z}+ib_{z}
as
a_{z}=\Re(Z)
,
b_{z}=\Im(Z)
, and concatenating them in the frequency dimension as a real-valued equivalent tensor
Z_{r}=[a_{z},\ b_{z}]
. While the Cartesian function is chosen due to its simplicity, the choice of the magnitude conversion at the input is natural given that we want to process magnitude in the real-valued branch. We refer to
[
14
]
for an extensive exploration on domain conversion functions and their influence in hybrid networks.

3.3
Complex Layers and Activations

We define complex layers based on
[
8
]
. A complex layer
l_{c}(\cdot)
is defined as complex by performing a complex multiplication using two separate real-valued layers of the same type
l_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\cdot)
and
l_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}(\cdot)
as

l_{c}(Z)=l_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(Z))-l_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}(\mathcal{I}(Z))+i(l_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\mathcal{I}(Z))+l_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}(\mathcal{R}(Z))),

(1)

The use of (
1
) when formulating a recurrent network is often referred to as a naive approach, since this is the application of recurrent layers as a complex multiplication. Such discussion is out of scope for this work and, for standardization, we also consider (
1
) for defining complex recurrent layers.

In terms of activation functions, we base ourselves in
[
14
]
, where constrained polynomials are used. We consider the complex ReLU (cReLU) function, implemented as

\mathrm{cReLU}(Z)=\frac{Z}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{|Z|+0.01}\right),

(2)

Alternatively, we also take the complex Tanh (cTanh) into account, with implementation

cTanh(Z)=Z\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|Z|^{2}+1}}\right).

(3)

The form of (
2
) and (
3
) is particularly interesting for stability because the denominator constrains the growth of the numerator. This ensures that, in the limit as
Z\rightarrow\infty
, the functions exhibit at most linear behavior. Note that, if
Z
is substituted by a real-valued tensor, (
2
) and (
3
) become, respectively, real-valued approximations of ReLU and Tanh.

4
Numerical Experiments

We compare two architectures, the convolutional denoising autoencoder (CDAE) and the convolutional-recurrent network (CRN), in their real, complex, and hybrid forms, based on their real-valued implementation from
[
4
]
. Data, architectures, results, and analysis are detailed in the following.

4.1
Data

The raw audio from LibriTTS and TAU2019 is converted to a sampling frequency of 16 kHz. Each noise file of 10 seconds duration is combined with 10 seconds of concatenated randomly drawn clean speech files at a random (uniform) SNR from -5 to 20 dB. Raised cosine (Hann window) fade-in and fade-out are applied to all files with a random (uniform) duration within 0.2-0.3 seconds. The time-frequency domain signals are obtained via STFT, with a 256-sample Hann window, Fourier transform of the same length, and 50% overlap between windows. Magnitude warping is applied to the STFT output
Y
as
X=20\log_{10}(|Y|+1e^{-8})
and the signal is scaled from 0 to 1, respectively, from -80 to 0 dB. Clamping at -80 dB is applied to all signals. For training, the LibriTTS train-clean-100 subset (100 hours) and the entire development set of the TAU2019 dataset (40 hours) are used as 100 hours of augmented audio. Evaluation is performed over 20 hours of augmented data with the LibriTTS test-clean subsets (8.6 hours) and the TAU2019 evaluation set (20 hours).

4.2
Architectures

Our baseline is the real-valued CDAE (rCDAE) from
[
4
]
. The rCDAE takes as input the concatenation of real and imaginary parts of the normalized noisy-speech STFT
X
, and estimates the real and imaginary parts of a complex mask
[
17
]
, which is recombined into a complex tensor. The convolutions only operate in the frequency dimension for reduced complexity and time-invariant operation. We also consider the real-valued convolutional recurrent network (rCRN) from
[
4
]
, however, we adapt the size of its layers to reduce the total number of parameters. The complex and hybrid counterparts of those models (cCDAE, hCDAE, cCRN, and hCRN) are obtained as proposed in Section
3.1
. For all networks, ReLU (or cReLU if complex) are used after each convolutional (Conv2D) or transposed convolutional (Conv2D.T) layers except the last encoder and decoder layers. The last encoder layer contains a Tanh (or cTanh if complex), and the last decoder layer contains no activation for all models except for the real-valued branch of the hCDAE and hCRN, with a Sigmoid, as they estimate a magnitude mask. The layer sizes of all networks are shown in Table
1
, where each number in a square bracket represents a layer’s output size, ordered from left to right.

4.3
Performance

All models were trained for 100 epochs each, using Adam with 1e-4 weight decay and an initial learning rate of 1e-3, decaying exponentially until the last epoch to 1e-4. Similarly to
[
4
]
, we consider the (negative) scale-invariant signal-to-distortion (SI-SDR) ratio for the minimization loss, as it was previously shown to be effective for speech enhancement
[
18
,
19
]
. We analyze the performance in terms of SI-SDR, short-time objective intelligibility (STOI)
[
20
]
, and perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ)
[
21
]
.

The performance of all CDAE and CRN-based models for different signal-to-noise (SNR) ratios is shown in Table
2
. We can see that the real-valued version of both architectures is always outperformed by its complex and hybrid counterparts. The struggles of the real-valued model are more critical for speech intelligibility, where the rCDAE can barely improve the noisy signal intelligibility under the lowest considered SNR. The insufficient performance of the real-valued models is due to their limitations when processing complex data with only real-valued operations, as it has been shown in
[
14
]
that real models try to (inefficiently) mimic complex behavior. In contrast, the complex-valued counterparts can achieve superior performance, however, our results indicate that the sole utilization of complex networks is somewhat limited and inefficient, not achieving the best metrics. Indeed, it was previously observed that they can be inefficient for speech enhancement due to their training difficulty and complexity
[
9
]
.

The hCDAE and hCRN, on the other hand, are able to outperform their real or complex counterparts for almost all cases. For both hybrid networks, it appears that the inefficiencies associated with pure complex processing were mitigated with the combination of both domains – further reinforced with the complexity analysis in Section
4.4
. In the current configuration, the heavy load is processed by real-valued layers with magnitude filtering, which composes a highly stable and efficient training. The fine-grained complex compensation is able to correct the noisy phase and respective magnitude values, being less prone to creating distortions arising from a multiplicative complex filter. These observations reflect the obtained metrics as the hybrid models consistently form an upper bound to the complex architectures. Next, we analyze the complexity of the considered architectures.

4.4
Complexity Analysis

The number of parameters of each model and their complexity in terms of multiply-accumulate operations (MACs) can be seen in Table
3
. Notice that, in Figure
1
, only transposed convolution layers are considered part of the decoder, any other layer (convolutional, recurrent, linear) belongs to the encoder.

For the CDAE, we can see that both real and complex models have a similar number of operations, while the hCDAE has substantially fewer MACs when compared to its counterparts. The conclusions are similar for the CRN models with the exception that cCRN presents higher number of MACs due to the complex recurrent layers. This counterintuitive result can be explained by noting that the amount of MACs in neural network layers is often not linearly proportional to their number of parameters, making a model with more but smaller layers (hybrid) less complex than models with less but bigger layers (real or complex). We thus confirm how the hybrid models are more efficient in terms of complexity when compared to purely real/complex networks.

Conclusion

We proposed a hybrid neural network concept for speech enhancement, consisting of real- and complex-valued branches which outputs are used for denoising. Both branches share information in their bottleneck via domain conversion functions. We also defined a design methodology for hybrid models starting from their real-valued counterpart. The results, obtained with convolutional and convolutional-recurrent architectures, show that the performance of hybrid models is superior to both real or complex equivalent models. Moreover, the complexity is substantially reduced in the hybrid network case for models with the same number of parameters. As future work, we suggest the use of hybrid models for beamforming since complex processing is a desired feature for multichannel inputs, as well as the generalization of the hybrid concept to other network architectures.

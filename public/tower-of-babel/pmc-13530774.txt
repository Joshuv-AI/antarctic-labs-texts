Poster 183. Prevalence of Reported Penicillin Allergy and Severe Reactions in Patients Given Cefazolin in Consecutive Cohort of an Elective Orthopedic Surgical Practice
Authors: Albert Lin, Abigail Balsan, Camila Grandberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Cefazolin is the most effective prophylactic preoperative antibiotic by far, and literature has shown that the cross reactivity between penicillin and cefazolin administration is extremely low. Penicillin allergies are prevalently reported which causes a reluctance or refusal to use cefazolin in these patients leading them to receive inferior antibiotics. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to determine the rate of true immunologic allergic reactions after the administration of cefazolin during orthopaedic surgeries in patients with reported penicillin allergies. We hypothesized that the rate of true immunologic allergic reactions to penicillin among patients with reported penicillin allergies would be low and that the rate of true immunologic allergic reactions to cefazolin among patients with documented allergies to penicillin including severe reactions would be very low or nonexistent.Methods:Consecutive patients undergoing orthopaedic surgical procedures from a single surgeon's practice between January/2023 and December/2024 were included. Data regarding age, surgical procedure, patient history of autoimmune or psychiatric disorders, as well as number of reported allergies, penicillin class antibiotic allergies including severity (as defined by high severity as an anaphylactic reaction and low severity as a non-anaphylactic systemic reaction), non-immunologic reaction (ie, GI upset, nausea, etc.), penicillin type, and reaction type, were obtained via retrospective review of electronic medical records.Results:A total of 1871 patients undergoing orthopaedic surgery within the two-year span were included. Of those, 295 (15.9%) patients had reported penicillin allergy in the medical records. Patient demographic data are displayed in Table 1. The type of penicillin that reportedly caused allergic reactions are described in Table 2. Out of the 295 patients who had reported penicillin allergies, 259 patients were administered cefazolin during surgery (87.8%) and 0 (0.0%) patients were found to have an immunologic allergic reaction during the intraoperative or immediate postoperative period, including those with documented anaphylaxis with penicillin. Out of the patients with reported penicillin allergies, 18 (6.1%) reported having a high severity reaction, 170 (57.6%) reported having a low severity reaction, and 136 (46.1%) reported having a non-immunologic reaction, with many patients reporting multiple severity and type of reactions (Table 3).Conclusions:In this cohort of all surgical procedures performed by a single orthopaedic surgeon over the span of two years, zero patients with reported penicillin allergies were found to have any allergic reaction, including severe anaphylactic reactions in the pre- or intraoperative time period following administration of cefazolin. The overall prevalence of reported penicillin allergy was relatively high at 15.9%, including all allergy types and severity. The reporting of penicillin allergies by patients often leads to the administration of inferior antibiotics during orthopaedic surgical procedures. These findings support the use of cefazolin, a far superior antibiotic, as prophylaxis for orthopaedic surgical procedures, even in the event of a reported anaphylactic reaction to penicillin.

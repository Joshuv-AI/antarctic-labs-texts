Nonlinear controller design for unmanned helicopter flight platform
Authors: Wen Ruchun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
As all phenomenons in nature are nonlinearity, meaning linearity is an ideal case, so large deviation exists for the existing linear control in academy and practice. For classical nonlinear controller design, the commonly used way is firstly to linearize the original nonlinearity, and then secondly apply the existing linear control theory to design the called linear controller. This paper is to alleviate this deviation through using differential geometry nonlinear control strategy for one interesting practical plant, i.e. unmanned helicopter. Specifically, firstly, the detailed physical modeling process for unmanned helicopter is shown through our own mathematical analysis, so that a general nonlinear system is obtained. Secondly, based on this nonlinear unmanned helicopter system without linearizing process, nonlinear controller design is derived in detail by virtue of differential geometry within state equation and state-observation equation respectively. Furthermore, thirdly, the perfect tracking is achieved during the framework of differential geometry nonlinear control. Fourthly, before applying the obtained nonlinear control, information of state variable is needed, so adaptive state estimation based on Lyapunov function is proposed to implement. Finally, one practical example is applied to prove our proposed theoretical results. Generally, this paper combines nonlinear control, differential geometry, unmanned helicopter, adaptive control together in order to design more advanced and accurate control in practice.

Introduction

Advanced control technology is deemed as a tool for the design and analysis of various engineering systems, for example, aircraft and other physical or chemical process plants. During this model world, advanced control technology plays an important role in the design and operation of robots, spacecraft, unmanned vehicles, power grids, etc. The goal of advanced control technology is to design one high performance controller to guarantee the considered system or plant works according to the desired or expected motion within lots of different environments, thus yielding sequent different control strategies, such as adaptive control, robust control, predictive control, disturbance suppression, etc. Each control strategy has its own advantage and shorting, just likes one coin, so we need to choose suitable control method after checking the considered environment and the control goal. Although the existed literatures about control theory are very mature, meaning the theoretical research about control theory have been achieved for more than 60 years, but within the various engineering applications, the classical proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control is still more widely used all over the world. From the statistical data, we find lots of advanced control strategies are not applied in engineers, such as neural control, intelligent control, multi agent control, fuzzy control, although there are lots of references on above control strategies. It is well known that in practical engineers, for example, adaptive control, predictive control or robust control, thus obtaining the corresponding adaptive PID control, prediction PID control or robust PID control etc. we all know that theory serves for engineer, and engineer proves the proposed theory, leading to one necessary combination between theory and engineer. To achieve this combination of theory and engineer, this paper considers the controller design for one kind of aircraft- unmanned helicopter.

Unmanned helicopter is a vertical taking off and landing unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), being controlled by radio ground remote control or autonomous control. It is a rotorcraft in form of construction and a vertical taking off or landing vehicle from its function. Unmanned helicopter has one or more large diameter rotor blades, mounted on a rotor shaft above the fuselage and driven by one piston engine or turbo shaft engine. As rotor rotation can produce upward lift in the air, so as long as this upward lift is sufficiently large than its weight, then unmanned helicopter starts to lift vertically. The pilot manipulates the automatic tilt device on rotor to let unmanned helicopter start to fly. More specifically, when the rotor blades are tilted from four different directions, i.e. left, right, front and back, then a corresponding horizontal force is generated with respect to those four different directions respectively, making unmanned helicopter fly in each direction. If the rotor lift is kept equal to the weight of unmanned helicopter, then a special case of hover appears in the air. Further for the event that the engine stops suddenly in the air, unmanned helicopter will force to land by sliding down through its rotor spin.

The main advantage of unmanned helicopter is its hover situation within the practical battlefield, as some tasks can be done simultaneously, for example, taking off and landing vertically and saving the flight space. Consider this interesting hover situation or state, unmanned helicopter is still easy to fall into the motion state of X and Z direction oscillations after being perturbed, thus making unmanned helicopter body to do a conical pendulum motion around a certain point above the rotor blades. As both X direction and Z direction have the similar swing amplitude and period, so although pendulum motion is convergent, but it needs a long time to keep stable. This long pendulum oscillation makes unmanned helicopter more sensitive to the external interference, and the increased sensor measurement error will seriously affect the hover performance and flight safety. Generally, many advantages of unmanned helicopter make it constantly show great practical value in the civil and military field. For example, in military, unmanned helicopter can be the target for artillery and missile,, and carry out positional calibration or weapon evaluation. Moreover, reconnaissance unmanned helicopter can implement battlefield reconnaissance, damage assessment, and military mapping etc. tactical unmanned helicopter can attend the combat against the enemy, work together with other aerial platforms, and finish some tasks such as electronic jamming and suppress enemy air defenses.

Due to the wide application of unmanned helicopter in battlefield, lots of research directions are studied with respect to it, for example, flight control, target detection, visual processing, communication network, signal processing, path planning etc. Here due to space limitation, we could not list the complete references about above mentioned research directions, and only some are listed with the subject of flight control. Classical PID control is proved very well for flight control from the theory and practice, due to its simple structure and easily PID controller parameter tuning. The complete and concise formulation about PID method in flight control can be seen every control literature. For example, consider the high frequency rotor dynamics and actuator dynamics, always neglected in the modeling, the model uncertainty was deemed as additive structure uncertainty1, then singular value analysis was used to identify the range of variation of the flight dynamics characteristics that guaranteed the closed loop stability2. Latter, the designed control system was evaluated to real time pilot-in-the loop simulation3, showing that the attitude controller, designed based on the hybrid sensitivity control method, can greatly improve the maneuvering quality and meet the requirements of military rotorcraft4. Finally, nonlinear control strategy is also an interesting research direction that has received much attention for helicopter flight control5. For example, reference6 applied a robust nonlinear control method to the control of helicopter vertical motion, while realizing the helicopter’s attitude hold. Reference7 designed a nonlinear vertical takeoff and landing controller for a three degree of freedom model helicopter experimental platform. A feedback linearization method was applied to achieve decoupling among the manipulation channels8, and the combination of feedback linearization method and neural network was proposed for helicopter flight controller design. In Ref.9, a vertical flight modal controller for a certain helicopter was devised by combining the feedback linearization method and continuous sliding model control. An affine nonlinear model was constructed for a longitudinal unmanned helicopter10 , then the exact linearization method was useful to design one nonlinear controller. It is well known that most existing results about nonlinear control theory rely on an accurate model of the considered helicopter system, corresponding to a difficult modeling process11. Moreover, most nonlinear control methods have certain requirements on the structure of the considered nonlinear system, such as affine nonlinear form, which limiting the application of nonlinear methods for unmanned helicopter12. Based on our knowledge, the above dependence of accurate model is relaxed by our studied direct data driven control, and the subject of nonlinear control for unmanned helicopter is our ongoing work. Reference13 presents a mathematical model and a controller for a convertible fixed wing vertical taking off and landing, and one real time robust tracking control for a quadrotor using monocular vision is developed in reference14. Reference15 presents the development of passivity based control algorithms to stabilize an unmanned power parachute aerial vehicle. In16, a new filtered observed based IDA_PBC strategy is developed for trajectory tracking of a quadrotor in the presence of disturbances and model uncertainties.

In addition, during these recent years, one advanced or improved form for dynamic programming appears, i.e. reinforcement learning17, which adds one special reward term and proposes model free control strategy, such as Q learning or deep learning. Reference18 points out that optimal control is based on state variable model, and the full state variable model of unmanned aircraft is not always accurately described, so the unvoiced nonlinearity and unmodelled dynamic property are not completely considered19. Since 1980s, dynamic inverse control has receive extensive attention. It firstly transforms the original nonlinear state input relation to a linear relation, then secondly the existed linear theory is directly applied to design one linear controller20. Dynamic inverse control is able to offset the changes in the model parameters and guarantee the linear relation unchanged21. In case of unknown parameter in the aircraft model or controller, then adaptive control strategy is benefit to construct one parameter adjustment mechanism to update those unknown controller parameters or model parameters. For example, Ref.22 applies dead zone to address the boundedness of error in noisy condition, and robustness of unmodelled dynamic is dealt with from parameter prediction.

Our new paper23 adds one varying thrust force in the bottom of unmanned aircraft, then the combination of force analysis and physical modeling together is applied to derive its dynamical model, corresponding to one nonlinear multivariable system, so nonlinear differential geometry theory is applied to design one nonlinear controller, while further considering the problem of disturbance rejection. Reference24 is concerned with the trajectory tracking control problem for the wheeled mobile robots (WMRs) subject to actuator faults. Reference25 focuses on the high-performance tracking control problem for the unknown multi-input multi-output (MIMO) nonlinear systems in the block triangular form with unmatched disturbances.

Based on above detailed descriptions about unmanned helicopter from its function and research direction, this paper concentrates on the controller design process without linearizing the original unmanned helicopter model. Specifically, after applying the classical physical principle modeling method, a sequence of differential equations is obtained to show the closed relations among all physical variables. Observing these mathematical relations, we find they are all nonlinear function forms. The classical controller design method is firstly to linearize the original nonlinear function through Taylor series expansion and then secondly apply the existed linear control theory to design one linear controller, leading to above two steps. As linear system is an approximated system to replace the original nonlinear system, so the model deviation exists inevitable, thus increasing the computational complexity for the latter controller design. It means after designed one linear controller, we need more time to testify whether this approximated linear controller works well. Further, all phenomenon in nature are all nonlinearity\, and linearity is only an idea case to approximate nonlinearity. Based on the fact of the existence of nonlinearity in nature, researchers start to think can we deal with nonlinearity directly without linearized process, thus leading to some interesting subjects, such as nonlinear system identification, nonlinear control, nonlinear optimization etc., so this paper is to design one nonlinear controller for unmanned helicopter directly only through differential geometry. We find the control performance of nonlinear control is more accurate than linear control, as non model deviation exists. After we use our ideas to construct the physical modeling process to generate a sequence of nonlinear equations, nonlinear control strategy based on differential geometry is proposed to devise nonlinear controller through our own mathematical derivations. From our derivation results, nonlinear controller is a nonlinear state feedback form. When to implement this nonlinear state feedback controller, the perfect knowledge about state is needed, so we turn to give an adaptive idea to tune the state estimation. Also we point out our derived nonlinear controller can achieve the goal of tracking, i.e. perfect tracking. Later to combine theory and practice together, one unmanned helicopter flight platform is established to do some practical experiments or simulations.

Nonlinear dynamic modeling analysis

A complete flight dynamical model of an unmanned helicopter includes the dynamics of rotor blades, culvert fuselage, fan, engine, drive train, and actuator components. The modeling of these sub-components is relatively independent, but at the same time closely linked through aerodynamic, structural or kinematic coupling. n order to reflect the flight dynamics of the new unmanned helicopter more accurately while avoiding excessive complexity, the following assumptions are introduced in the mathematical modeling.

Based on the above assumptions, the main considerations in modeling the unmanned helicopter are the airframe kinematic model and the aerodynamic models of the three components, namely, the rotor blades, the fan, and the culvert fuselage. In the calculation of the aerodynamic loads on the rotor blades of the helicopter, the non-constant aerodynamic force during the disturbed motion is calculated by using the Pitt/Peters power in-flow model, while the lifting line theory and linear aerodynamic force are used in the aerodynamic model of the leaf vein; the aerodynamic model of the culvert fan takes into account the effect of the rotor downwash airflow; and the aerodynamic force of the fuselage is obtained by the combined fan/fuselage air blowing test.

Conclusions

This paper studies nonlinear controller design for practical unmanned helicopter from the idea of differential geometry, combining other interesting subjects, for example, adaptive control, Lyapunov analysis etc. Firstly adaptive idea is introduced to achieve state estimation, then secondly we substitute state estimation into our derived nonlinear controller, suiting to more extended nonlinearity. Furthermore, perfect tracking is handled only within the framework of nonlinear differential geometry.

Remember that nonlinearity exist in theory and practice, and linearity is an ideal case, so research on nonlinear system or nonlinear control is more widely considered than the ideal linear case. From our mathematical derivations during this paper, we find nonlinearity is very difficult, as it needs more advanced mathematical basis. As this paper is our preliminarily about applying nonlinear differential geometry control into the practical unmanned helicopter flight controller design, then our next work will concern on how to apply more advanced mathematical knowledge in practice, for example, nonlinear adaptive control, nonlinear learning control etc.

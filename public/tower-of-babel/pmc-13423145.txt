BASTA trial: protocol for a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial investigating the effects of atorvastatin on bile acid diarrhoea
Authors: Mads Bank Lynggaard, Sólvá Strøm Olsen, Julie Lyng Forman, Martin Lund Kårhus, Lise Lotte Gluud, Asger Bach Lund, Anne-Marie Ellegaard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBile acid diarrhoea (BAD) is a chronic disease with typical symptoms of daily watery stools with increased frequency and urgency. BAD arises due to pathologically high levels of bile acids in the colon, which may be due to impaired reabsorption or increased synthesis. Evidence indicates that atorvastatin administration suppresses bile acid synthesis in healthy individuals, likely due to reduced substrate availability. The Bile Acid diarrhoea STAtin Trial aims to investigate whether atorvastatin treatment reduces bile acid synthesis in patients with BAD and consequently alleviates BAD symptoms.Methods and analysis20 adults with moderate-to-severe BAD, assessed by the gold standard diagnostic test 75selenium homotaurocholic acid test, will be randomised to begin with either placebo or atorvastatin 40 mg once daily for 2 weeks, followed by 80 mg once daily for another 2 weeks. After a 4-week washout period, participants will cross over to the alternate treatment. The primary endpoint is the relative difference between atorvastatin and placebo treatment in bile acid synthesis rate, measured by the validated biomarker 7alpha-hydroxy-4-cholesten-3-one (C4) and key secondary endpoints include mean differences in daily stool frequency.Ethics and disseminationThe study is approved by the Danish Medicines Agency and the Regional Scientific Ethics Committee of the Capital Region of Denmark (2025-521 856-47-00) and registered with the Danish Data Protection Agency (p-2024-17870). The study is monitored by the Capital Region of Denmark’s Good Clinical Practice unit. All results will be disseminated at national and/or international scientific meetings and in peer-reviewed scientific journals.Trial registration numberNCT07042165.Keywords: Clinical Trial, Treatment Outcome, Chronic Disease

Introduction

Bile acid diarrhoea (BAD) is a socially debilitating disease with stomach pain, high stool frequency, urgency and faecal incontinence as the main symptoms.1 Studies estimate that 1%–2% of the population suffers from the disease.2 3 It is a highly underdiagnosed disease with severe consequences for the individuals affected as well as on a societal level due to loss of productivity and disease-related healthcare expenses.4 5

Bile acids are synthesised from cholesterol in hepatocytes through a tightly regulated enzymatic process. Through the classical pathway, cholesterol is hydroxylated by the rate-limiting enzyme CYP7A1 leading to the intermediate 7alpha-hydroxy-4-cholesten-3-one (C4) and ultimately to cholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid. Alternatively, cholesterol is hydroxylated by CYP27A1 in the alternative pathway, resulting in the formation of chenodeoxycholic acid. These primary bile acids are then conjugated to taurine or glycine before being transported to and stored in the gallbladder. On food intake, bile is released from the gallbladder to the small intestinal lumen. While propagating down the small intestine, the vast majority of bile acids are absorbed—both via active and passive mechanisms—and transported via the portal vein back to the liver for reuse constituting the enterohepatic circulation. Imbalances in the enterohepatic circulation of bile acids can have important health consequences, for example, BAD. BAD symptoms arise due to a pathological spillover of bile acids to the colon.6 Excessive amounts of bile acids in the colon irritate the colonic mucosa, increase the osmotic gradient and cause colonic contractions, resulting in the abovementioned symptoms.6–10

Currently, individuals with BAD are typically treated with bile acid sequestrants. These agents bind the bile acids in the small intestinal lumen preventing them from irritating the colonic mucosa, and thereby diminishing symptoms.11 12 Originally, bile acid sequestrants were developed for the treatment of hypercholesterolaemia. However, despite their moderate low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol-lowering effect and associated cardiovascular benefits, their use for this indication is limited, most likely due to frequent gastrointestinal side effects, high cost, better and more efficient alternatives (eg, statins) and the need for multiple dosing (with meals). Another treatment option is liraglutide, which our group showed to be superior to colesevelam in reducing the number of daily stools by ≥25% in a 6-week randomised controlled trial.13 Consequently, liraglutide is now increasingly used in the clinic to treat individuals with BAD.14 It is also possible to use loperamide, a more traditional antidiarrhoeal agent, for the treatment of BAD, although it appears to be less effective.15

Despite advances in the treatment of BAD, some patients do not obtain sufficient symptom control, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where current treatment options often are too expensive. Furthermore, the need for additional research into BAD treatment is highlighted in the recommendations by the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence.16 Thus, the possibility of yet another tool in the toolbox is compelling.

Statins represent an effective drug class reducing circulating levels of LDL cholesterol, which is a key instigator of atherosclerotic plaque formation.17 Through the inhibition of the enzyme HMG-CoA reductase—a crucial enzyme initiating the synthesis of cholesterol—statins lower the intracellular levels of cholesterol in hepatocytes.18 Statins are used by millions of patients worldwide to reduce their risk of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality and are considered safe with overall mild and benign adverse effects.17 19

Bile acids are synthesised from cholesterol in the liver. Thus, reducing cholesterol availability reduces the substrate available for bile acid synthesis. As such, atorvastatin could reduce the bile acid production in individuals with BAD leading to a reduction or normalisation of the amount of bile acids secreted to the intestinal lumen and entering the colon. Supporting this, a recently published randomised controlled trial investigating the effects of 14 days of 80 mg atorvastatin once daily in healthy men found a 43% decrease in 7alpha-hydroxy-4-cholesten-3-one (C4), a validated serum biomarker for bile acid synthesis, on atorvastatin treatment compared with placebo.20

The current study aims to investigate whether atorvastatin treatment lowers the synthesis of bile acids, assessed as serum C4 levels, in a dose-response manner in patients with moderate-to-severe BAD, verified by the gold standard diagnostic 75selenium-homotaurocholic acid (SeHCAT) test. The reduction of bile acid synthesis is expected to lead to a reduction in BAD symptoms since the pathophysiology of the disease is a spillover of excessive bile acids to the colon.

Methods and analysis

Trial design

The trial is an investigator-initiated, proof-of-concept, placebo-controlled, double-blind, crossover, randomised clinical trial (figure 1). This design enables comparison between atorvastatin at doses of 40 and 80 mg, respectively, and placebo treatment on symptoms, hepatobiliary markers, metabolic markers, glycaemic control markers, stool samples and safety. A crossover design is appropriate, since BAD is a chronic condition with an expected stable symptom burden during trial participation, allowing participants to serve as their own controls, thereby increasing statistical power. The effects of atorvastatin treatment are expected to be fully resolved within the 4-week washout period, as the half-life of atorvastatin is approximately 14 hours and several clinical crossover trials with atorvastatin successfully included a washout period of 4 weeks or shorter.21–24 The full list of eligibility criteria can be found in figure 1 boxes 1 and 2, while the primary and secondary endpoints are presented in box 3.

Randomisation and blinding

A total of 20 participants will be randomly assigned on a 1:1 ratio to begin in either the atorvastatin group or the placebo group before crossover. Randomisation will be done in blocks of alternating sizes of 4 and 6, respectively. Group allocation is concealed from participants as well as investigators. The Central Pharmacy of The Capital Region of Denmark will (1) manufacture placebo and (2) manufacture blinding capsules for atorvastatin and placebo and deliver the blinded investigational medical product (IMP) in suitable containers labelled with a unique number. A set of sealed randomisation codes, one for each participant, will be produced by The Central Pharmacy of the Capital Region of Denmark and delivered to the study site, where they will be kept in a secure, locked place to be used for emergency code breaking only. The code for a particular participant may be broken in case of a medical emergency if knowledge about the treatment allocation will influence the treatment of the participant. To ensure blinding, plasma lipids will be measured after completion of the trial.

Endpoints

Primary analysis

The primary endpoint will be evaluated in the principal stratum excluding study participants with non-adherence to any of the assigned treatments defined as pill count below 75% including discontinuation. The ratio in geometric mean C4 concentration between the end of the 80 mg atorvastatin treatment period and the end of the placebo treatment period will be estimated using a linear mixed model with log(C4 concentration) as outcome, treatment (single dose atorvastatin, double dose atorvastatin, single dose placebo, double dose placebo) and period (first or second) as fixed effects, and an unstructured covariance to account for repeated measurements on each study participant and potential variance heterogeneity between treatments. No formal test for carry-over effect will be performed. Missing data will be handled implicitly by maximum likelihood inference in the linear mixed model. A two-sided p<0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Secondary analyses

Quantitative secondary endpoints will be evaluated in principal stratum using a linear mixed model similar to that for the primary outcome. Binary secondary endpoints will be evaluated using McNemar’s test with missing data handled by best-case/worst-case scenarios. The p values will be adjusted for multiple testing using the method of Benjamini and Hochberg which controls the false discovery rate.25 An adjusted p<0.05 is considered statistically significant. Clearly labelled adjusted and/or unadjusted p values might be reported, depending on the guidelines of the respective journal.

Safety analyses

Safety outcomes will be analysed under a ‘treatment policy’-strategy based on all participants that were randomised and received at least one dose of study drug, regardless of treatment adherence (similar to an intention-to-treat approach) and in principal stratum (as efficacy endpoints above). Binary safety outcomes will be summarised as numbers and percentages. Count data will be summarised as median and quartiles. As far as possible, missing data will be prevented by continuing data collection when study participants discontinue treatment. The remaining missing values will be handled by best-case/worst-case analyses. No formal statistical analysis will be done due to anticipated lack of power.

Sample size

We consider a 33% reduction in C4 level clinically relevant since a serum C4 level of >46 µg/L makes BAD likely while a serum C4 level <31 µg/L makes BAD unlikely, thus corresponding with a 33% reduction.26 Based on data from a randomised study with patients referred to a SeHCAT test due to chronic diarrhoea who had C4 measured before and after a 1-week pause of administration of statins, we estimated the SD of the individual differences in log(C4 concentration) to 0.57.27 Thus, assuming that our study population displays a similar heterogeneity, a reduction in geometric mean C4 of 33% can be detected with 80% power if 18 participants complete the study. To ensure sufficient power, 20 participants will be included. If dropouts exceed 10%, they will be replaced to ensure that at least 18 participants fully complete the trial.

Procedures and visits

After screening for eligibility (box 1 and Box 2), in case of prior use of bile acid sequestrants, antidiarrhoeal medication (eg, loperamide), anticonstipation medication (eg, lactulose, laxoberal, magnesia), other constipating medication (eg, morphine medication) or liraglutide the participants will be asked to pause the medication for 1 week before collecting the baseline stool diary (1 week).

Afterwards, they will be randomised to begin with either placebo or atorvastatin 40 mg once daily (one tablet) for 2 weeks, followed by 80 mg once daily for a further 2 weeks (two tablets). After a 4-week washout period, participants will cross over to the alternate treatment. In total, the duration of the trial for the participants will be 13–14 weeks (figure 1).

Before each visit, the participants will be asked to fast for 10 hours (water allowed) and refrain from alcohol consumption for 24 hours due to the possible interference of food and alcohol with the C4 test result.27 Blood and faecal samples will be collected. To avoid C4 degradation, serum samples are left to coagulate for 20 min followed by 15 min centrifugation at 2000 g at 5°C and subsequent quick pipetting into vials and storage at −80°C. Participants will complete 1-week stool diaries at home and answer questionnaires regarding treatment response and quality of life (in-house translation to Danish using the forward-backwards method of the Irritable Bowel Syndrome Symptom Severity Score ‘Modified Copyright 1997 by Rome Foundation. All Rights Reserved’.28 A flow chart of scheduled trial activity can be found in the Protocol (link below).

The study will be carried out at Gentofte Hospital, Denmark. Recruitment began in October 2025 and the trial will end on the day of the last visit of the last patient, which is expected to be in the third quarter of 2026.

Intervention and drug handling

Both the IMP and the placebo formulation will be manufactured by The Central Pharmacy of the Capital Region of Denmark in accordance with Good Manufacturing Practice. To ensure blinding, the respective tablets (atorvastatin or placebo) will be placed inside a gelatin capsule with lactose monohydrate as filler (lactose intolerance is an exclusion criteria). The composition of the placebo drug equals the composition of atorvastatin, only with the active drug omitted. The pill boxes will be stored at room temperature. To assess adherence to study medicine, participants will be asked at all visits if they take the study medicine as prescribed. Furthermore, to ensure compliance, the diary will include a question about whether the participants have administered their daily dose. Participants will also be instructed to return the pill containers that will be accounted for in a study medicine log and remaining tablets will be counted and compliance calculated.

Ethics and dissemination

The study complies with Danish and European Union legislation and is approved by the Danish Medicines Agency and the Regional Scientific Ethics Committee of the Capital Region of Denmark (2025-521856-47-00). It is registered with the Danish Data Protection Agency (p-2024-17870). The study is monitored by the Capital Region of Denmark’s Good Clinical Practice unit. All results, positive, negative and inconclusive, will be disseminated at national and/or international scientific meetings and in peer-reviewed scientific journals. Participants are covered by the mandatory Danish patient insurance (patient compensation applicable to public institutions), ‘Patienterstatningen’. In addition, participants will be covered by product liability insurance for the study medication with the exception in case of gross investigator neglect or wilful misuse of the study medication. The trial will be conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Recruitment and informed consent

Participants will be recruited from hospitals in the Capital Region of Denmark among patients referred to the SeHCAT test for diagnosing BAD. Potential study participants will be asked if a medical doctor may contact them and inform them about the project. Before the screening visit, the potential participants will receive the written participant information. This written participant information will include details about the aim of the study and that it involves research with voluntary participation of 20 participants randomly allocated 1:1 to begin with either atorvastatin or placebo treatment before crossover. At the screening visit, oral information will be given by one of the investigators (qualified according to Danish legislation) in a quiet environment. Participants will have the opportunity to ask questions and bring a companion to the interview. Investigators will ensure that participants are adequately informed about the study design and any potential benefits or risks both orally and in writing. Before signing the consent form, participants will be offered ample time (up to 7 days) to consider participation in the trial. Should the participants need further time, a follow-up meeting will be scheduled. Participants are informed that participation is voluntary and that they may, at any time, withdraw their informed consent to participate in the study without consequences for their future treatment. No study-related activities will be conducted before the informed consent form has been signed and dated by both study participants and investigator.

Side effects, risks and disadvantages for participants

We expect that this study will contribute with important information about the use of atorvastatin in patients with BAD. The investigators are confident that the possible risks and side effects for the participants are outweighed by the expected benefits from the conduct of this study. The risk of side effects when participating in this study is expected to be low. Statins are used by millions of patients worldwide to reduce their risk of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality17 and are considered safe with overall mild and benign adverse effects.19 A complete list of possible side effects can be found in the Summary of Product Characteristics.29 In this trial, the IMP will be orally administered for a total of 4 weeks out of the 13–14 weeks total duration of the trial. Thus, the participants will be off treatment for 9–10 weeks. This is considered ethically acceptable since BAD is not a life-threatening condition, there is no reason to expect worsening of the condition during this period, and the participants have often already had symptoms for years—therefore, a period of approximately 10 weeks without treatment is considered acceptable.30 A maximum of 320 mL of blood in total will be drawn from the participants over the span of 13–14 weeks, with a maximum of 50 mL at each visit. Regarding all other planned study procedures, the risk of complications is negligible.

Out of ethical reasons, we limit the number of participants subjected to the exploratory intervention by aiming for a homogeneous population. This limits the study’s generalisability, which should be the focus of future studies.

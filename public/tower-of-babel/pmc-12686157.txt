PM2.5 promotes non-small cell lung cancer tumorigenesis by miR-21-5p targeting PDE4DIP accumulated
Authors: Yan-Jun Lin, Yuan-Hao Wu, Jingqing Ma, Lu Fang, Bo Liu, Shu Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNon-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is associated with increased risk of lung cancer. microRNAs (miRNAs) play an important role in a variety of cancers, including NSCLC. In NSCLC, existing literature has revealed upregulation of miR-21-5p. Nonetheless, the molecular mechanism of miR-21-5p in NSCLC development after PM2.5 exposure is not clear. This study aimed to investigate the role of miR-21-5p in NSCLC development after PM2.5 exposure and to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanismsMethodsExpression of miR-21-5p, PDE4DIP, and SORBS2 was determined in NSCLC cell lines and tumor tissues. To measure proliferation, migration/invasion of NSCLC cells, 3-(4, 5-dimethyl-thiazolyl-2)-2, 5-diphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT), colony formation, transwell and wound healing assays were accordingly performed. A tumor xenograft model was established to evaluate the effects of miR-21-5p, PDE4DIP, and SORBS2 modification on tumor growth in vivo.ResultsmiR-21-5p was up-regulated in NSCLC tumor tissues and cell lines. Also, PM2.5 exposure led to a higher level of miR-21-5p. The miR-21-5p mimic and inhibitor respectively promoted and decreased proliferation and migration/invasion of NSCLC cells. Tumor growth was reduced in miR-21-5p KO mice in vivo. Luciferase reporter assay indicated that PDE4DIP was a target of miR-21-5p. Moreover, miR-21-5p regulated cell proliferation and migration/invasion by inhibiting PDE4DIP expression after PM2.5 exposure. PDE4DIP knockdown promoted tumor growth in vivo. PDE4DIP and SORBS2 were down-regulated in NSCLC tumor tissues and cell lines. SORBS2 inhibited NSCLC cell proliferation and invasion/migration and tumor growth after PM2.5 exposure by regulating miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway.ConclusionsmiR-21-5p could target PDE4DIP and promote tumor growth of NSCLC. SORBS2 suppressed NSCLC tumorigenesis by regulating miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) exposure upregulated miR-21-5p in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues and cells.

• miR-21-5p promoted proliferation and invasion of NSCLC cells by targeting PDE4DIP.

• SORBS2 acted as a tumor suppressor by counteracting miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling.

What is known and what is new?

• PM2.5 contributes to lung cancer initiation and progression.

• This study reveals that miR-21-5p mediates PM2.5-induced NSCLC tumorigenesis by targeting PDE4DIP, and SORBS2 negatively regulates this pathway.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Targeting the miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP axis or enhancing SORBS2 may represent novel therapeutic strategies for NSCLC, especially in patients exposed to PM2.5.

Introduction

Lung cancer is a malignant disease that seriously threatens human health and survival (1). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for about 85% of all lung cancer, and it includes squamous-cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and large-cell carcinoma (2). Recently, as the incidence of lung cancer increases, particularly in non-smokers, “fine particulate matter (PM2.5)”, a major air pollutant, is receiving more attention. The PM2.5 refers to particles suspended in the air with a diameter of ≤2.5 microns, which come from coal, fuel, industrial exhaust, motor vehicle exhaust, straw burning and other sources (3). Microorganisms and chemical compounds, including heavy metals and inorganic and organic compounds, constitute the PM2.5 with most of these components being long-familiar carcinogens (4). In 2013, the International Agency for Research on Cancer defined PM2.5 as the main environmental carcinogen (5). As for the role of PM2.5 in lung cancer, relevant clinical or basic studies have shown consistent findings regarding PM2.5 promoting lung cancer development and progression. Epidemiological studies indicate that PM2.5 exposure is significantly associated with the incidence of lung cancer (6-9). From the perspective of basic research, PM2.5-induced alterations in the tumor microenvironment could promote tumor growth and metastasis by triggering angiogenic and inflammatory responses in lung cancer (4,10). Chao et al. demonstrated that the risk of NSCLC progression increased when mice were exposed to PM2.5 for a long run via enhancement of interleukin-17a-regulated proliferation and metastasis (11). However, the mechanisms by which PM2.5 promotes tumorigenesis in NSCLC have not been fully investigated yet. .

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous non-coding RNA molecules with a length of about 22 nucleotides that can negatively regulate gene expression by either inhibiting protein translation or inducing mRNA degradation through binding to 3'untranslated region (UTR) of target mRNAs (12). It is known that numerous miRNAs have been implicated in different types of cancer including lung cancer (13,14). Notably, the role of miR-21-5p, an oncogene, in lung cancer is receiving more attention. Several studies found that the expression of miR-21-5p was significantly up-regulated in NSCLC cell lines (15,16). Related reports have revealed overexpression of miR-21-5p in tissues in tumors and plasma/serum of NSCLC patients (16-20), while the levels of miR-21-5p may have diagnostic and prognostic value for NSCLC (18-20). Recently, some scientists discovered that miR-21-5p could regulate the proliferation, migration and invasion of tumor cells from cytological experiments in vitro and regarded it as an ideal target for tumor biotherapy (21). However, research on the role and mechanisms of miR-21-5p in the development and progression of NSCLC is still in its infancy. As a principal member in the superfamily nucleotide phosphodiesterase, PDE4DIP is abnormally expressed in cells which promotes development of cancers such as pineal cell carcinoma, breast, and hematologic cancers (22). The protein functions by interacting with cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)-specific phosphodiesterase-4D. Available literature has revealed downregulation of PDE4DIP in lung adenocarcinoma (23). Beside this, a study has established that mutations of PDE4DIP gene is closely associated with diseases like heart failure, stroke, and atrial fibrillation (24). An extensive search of existing literature has revealed that the role of PDE4DIP in NSCLC has not been explored yet.

A potent suppressor of tumors like SORBS2 has been classified into the SoHo family of the adapter proteins (25). SORBS2 regulates processes that depends on actin, which includes adhesion of cell and its migration (26). Although the protein is expressed highly in healthy cells, its expression is repressed strongly when various carcinomas like pancreatic, ovarian, hepatocellular and cervical progress and metastasize (27-30). Nonetheless, there is little understanding of how SORBS2 interacts with PDE4DIP and miR-21-5p in NSCLC.

In the present study, we intended to clarify the role of miR-21-5p in the development and promotion of lung cancer under conditions of PM2.5 and the mechanisms involved. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR and ARRIVE reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-626/rc).

Methods

Collection of tissue samples from patients

The experimenters obtained human tissues (mainly adjacent non-tumor and NSCLC tumors) from 35 patients whose tumors were surgically resected at the Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (No. XHEC-D-2021-148). Before the experiments, the researchers ensured that signed consent forms were provided by all the patients.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The procedure for IHC test was performed as described in earlier work with some modifications (31). NSCLC tumor and adjacent non-tumor tissue samples were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) as the fixative. The PT Link (Dako, Denmark) was employed to retrieve the targets after the fixed tissues were embedded in paraffin before they were deparaffinized and rehydrated. Regarding primary antibodies, anti-SORBS2 antibodies (1:500, Invitrogen; BS-4862R, USA) and anti-PDE4DIP antibodies (1:250, Abcam, ab121275, USA) were used, followed by the corresponding Alexa 488/555/647 secondary antibodies (1:1,000, Alexa Fluor, Life Technologies, Shanghai, China). Before the sections were dissolved for 5 min in ethanol (70%), they were first incubated in Sudan Black (0.6 g). Mounting medium was utilized to mount the sections after washing them in phosphate-buffer saline (PBS).

Culturing of cell lines

The Cell Bank of Chinese Academy (Shanghai, China) supplied the cell lines [human NSCLC cell lines (A549 and H1975) and human normal lung cell line (HPAEpiC)] for this study. Using Dulbecco’s modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Gibco, NY, USA) that has been supplemented with streptomycin (100 mg/mL, Sigma, USA), penicillin (100 IU/mL, Sigma, USA) and fetal bovine serum (FBS) (10% (v/v), Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA), the experimenters cultured before incubation of the cells in conditions comprising humidified 5% CO2 and at temperature of 37 ℃.

Overexpression or inhibition of miR-21-5p, PDE4DIP and SORBS2

The miR-21 inhibitor, miR-21 mimic, and corresponding negative controls were designed and synthesized with Gene Pharma (Shanghai, China). To respectively generate vectors of pCDH-CMV-PDE4DIP and pCDH-CMV-SORBS2, the researchers cloned sequences of copy DNA (cDNA) of PDE4DIP (Genbank accession number: NM_00100810.5) and SORBS2 (Genbank accession number NM_021069.4) into pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-coGFP (lentiviral vector, System Biosciences, USA). After restricting endonuclease identification and sequencing analysis, the lentiviral expression vector was successfully constructed. Lentiviruses were produced in HEK293T cells produced lentiviruses before their purification, concentration and tittering. Short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting PDE4DIP and SORBS2 and a negative control were purchased from Gene Pharma (Shanghai, China). These plasmids were transfected individually or combined into A549 or H1975 cells for subsequent experiments.

Extraction of RNA with quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) technique

Based on the established TRIzol® protocol (32), the reagent was utilized to extract total RNA from the cell line or tissues per the specifications of the manufacturer. Transcription of cDNA was performed on the account of a protocol described by manufacturers of a Prime Script™ RT Master Mixture (Takara Biotech. Co., Ltd., China). The SYBR Prime Script-miRNA qRT-PCR kit (Takara Biotech. Co., Ltd.) was employed to detect miR-21-5p. The following conditions of thermocycling were used for the process: for a total of 40 cycles, pre-denaturation and denaturation were performed at 95 ℃ for 1 min and 15 s, whereas at 60 and 72 ℃, annealing and extension were respectively carried out for 40 and 15 s. Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as an internal control for PDE4DIP and SORBS2, whereas the internal control of miR-21-5p was U6. The following primers’ sequences were employed for qRT-PCR analysis:

SORBS2, F: 5'-ACACCACGTACATGCCGTAA-3'; R: 5'-CGTAACAGACATGGCGGCAC-3'. PDE4DIP, F: 5'-GAGAGCTCCAGCAATAGGGG-3'; R: 5'-TGCTAGCCTGGATTCCTCCT-3'. GAPDH, F: 5'-CACCAGGTTGTGTCCACTGA-3'; R: 5'-GATGGGAGAATGGTCGCGTA-3'. miR-21-5p, F: 5'-GACTGATGTTGATGTCGGGT-3'; R: 5'-GTCAGACAGCCCATCGACT-3'. U6, F: 5'- CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3'; R: 5'-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3'.

Analysis of proteins with Western blot technique

Total protein was extracted from cell lines or tumor tissues of NSCLC with lysis buffer of radio-immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA). Leveraging on the principle of sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), further separation of equal quantities of total protein (10 µg) for different groups was performed on a gel (12%) before subsequent transfer onto a membrane of polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF). Before the researchers incubate the membranes at 4 ℃ overnight with appropriate primary antibodies, they blocked the membranes at room temperature for 1 h with 5% non-fat milk (5). The primary antibodies that were used to incubate the membranes were anti-SORBS2 (1:500, Invitrogen) and anti-PDE4DIP (1:250, Abcam). Further, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies conjugated with horseradish peroxidase (1:1,000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) for 2 h at room temperature. Enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) technique was used to detect the bands of proteins before normalization of their expression to GAPDH.

MTT [3-(4, 5-Dimethyl-thiazolyl-2)-2, 5-diphenyl-tetrazolium bromide] assays

A total of 500 cells were added in aliquot (100 µL) medium/well per well to 96-well plate before culturing the cells for 6, 12, 24 and 48 h. To each well, the researchers added a solution (10 µL) of MTT (5 mg/mL) at the appropriate times, before they incubated the cells at room temperature. Freshly prepared medium comprising aliquot (200 µL) of dimethyl-sulfoxide (DMSO) was utilized to replace the medium after 4 h. A plate reader (Bio-Rad Laboratories, China) was employed to assess the absorbance at an appropriate wavelength (492 nm). The researchers conducted triplicate experiments.

Colony formation assays

Using an 800 cells/well density, the experimenters cultured the cells (A549 and H1975 cells) in 6-well plates. For roughly 10 days, the experimenters cultured the cells before fixing them with PFA (4%). Washing and drying of the plates were further performed, after which counting of the colony number (>50 cells/colony) was carried out.

Transwell assays

The migration and invasion of tumor cells were evaluated using transwell assays. Twenty-four well plates with Transwell pores size of 8.0 µm (Millipore) was used after 15 µg of Matrigel (Becton Dickinson Bioscience, China) before Matrigel (15 µg) (Becton Dickinson Bioscience) was employed to pre-coat the membranes of Transwell inserts. On top of the chamber, the researchers placed aliquot (200 µL) of culture media that contained cells (2×104), but without FBS. Also, the researchers added normal culture medium to bottom chamber. A cotton swab was employed to remove the cells on the membrane top membrane after incubation for 36 h. A microscope was used to count the cells that invaded and adhered to membrane underside after the researchers have fixed them with methanol and stained them with a solution of crystal violet.

Wound healing assay

To create a straight scratch in the middle of each well of 6-well plate with a micro-pipette tip, post 24 h of transfection with miR-21-5p mimic or inhibitor or different vectors (as required by the experiments) was performed. The researchers measured the movement of cells into the well scratch to assess the cells’ migration. The researchers determined the speed of wound closure after 24 h before normalizing it at 0 h to the length. Migration area (%) was calculated as (A0 − An)/A0×100%, with A0 representing the initial wound area, whilst An denoted the remaining area of the wound.

Luciferase reporter assays

Existing protocol for luciferase reported assay was adopted for this experiment (29). A dual-luciferase reporter vector was cloned by amplifying a sequence of 3'UTR. Before transfection of NSCLC cells with vectors of luciferase reporter and target genes, the tumor cells were seeded in a 96-well plate. The researchers lysed the cells after 48 h before addition of a mixture of luciferase assay substrate and reagent II. Immediately after the preceding process, a standard instrument was employed to assess the fluorescence activity.

Nude mouse tumor xenograft model

All animal experiments were performed under a project license (No. XHEC-STCSM-2020-043) granted by the Animal Ethics Committee of Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine in compliance with United States National Institute of Health guidelines for the care and use of animals.

Animals use serves as a guideline when the animal experiments were conducted.

Animals: 8 weeks-old male wild type (WT) and miR-21-5p knock-out (KO) mice (C57BL/6 mice) were purchased from Shanghai Model Organisms Center (Shanghai, China). BALB/c-nude mice (athymic nude mice) were bought from Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). The mice were housed under standard conditions. PM2.5 exposure: Considering the actual dose of PM2.5 that was exposed to the general population, we chose a dose of 5 mg/kg (body weight) PM2.5 for the mice. The mice received oropharyngeal aspiration of PM2.5 or saline every other day for 5 weeks.

Cancer cell injection: Before PM2.5 exposure, the experimenters resuspended NSCLC cells at 2.5×106 cells/ mL concentration and subcutaneously injected aliquot (100 µL) of cells into flank of mice. The miR-21 KO and WT mice were injected with A549 and H1975 cells. BALB/c-nude mice were injected with H1975 cells transfected with shPDE4DIP or shPDE4DIP + SORBS2 vector.

Every week, the experimenters monitored the tumor size of xenograft subcutaneous tumor size with vernier calipers (volume = width2 × length × 1/2). The experimenters euthanize the mice before removing, weighing, and imaging 5 weeks after injection. To measure the cell proliferation, the experimenters fixed and sectioned tumor samples before staining with Ki67 marker.

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare continuous variables between the two groups depending on data distribution. When appropriate, we compared differences within various groups with One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) after which Tukey’s posthoc tests or two-way ANOVA was performed. The entire statistical analysis was performed leveraging on the statistical principles of SPSS 19.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism V5.0 (Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). In terms of statistical significance, the researchers considered two-tailed P value <0.05.

Results

The effects of PM2.5 exposure on NSCLC cell proliferation and migration/invasion in A549 and H1975 cells

First, we examined the effects of PM2.5 exposure with different concentrations (2.5, 5, 10, 20 µg/cm2) and durations (6, 12, 24 and 48 h) on cell proliferation in two NSCLC cell lines (A549 and H1975). The study showed that PM2.5 exposure (10 µg/cm2) increased cell proliferation in a dose- and time-dependent manner (Figure 1A). The selected time for PM2.5 exposure for assaying migration and invasion of A549 and H1975 cells was 24 h. The study revealed that migration and invasion of the cells were promoted by PM2.5 exposure for 24 h in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 1B,1C). Based on the above results, we selected PM2.5 exposure (10 µg/cm2) for 24 h for subsequent experiments.

Expression of miR-21-5p was up-regulated in cell lines and tumor tissues of NSCLC

The expression of miR-21-5p was examined in both NSCLC tumor tissue and cell lines. To analyze miR-21-5p, we utilized The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), which revealed 44 normal and 336 tumor tissues. The miR-21-5p was up-regulated in NSCLC tumor tissues compared with normal tissues (Figure 2A). Also, we confirmed that miR-21-5p was up-regulated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent non-tumor tissues obtained from 35 patients with NSCLC that underwent surgical resection in our hospital (Figure 2B). Likewise, expression of miR-21-5p was increased in NSCLC cell lines (A549 and H1975) compared with a normal human lung cell line (HPAEpiC). Besides, exposure to PM2.5 for 24 h further promoted miR-21-5p expression in both A549 and H1975 cell lines (Figure 2C). These results suggest an upregulation of miR-21-5p expression in NSCLC.

NSCLC tumor growth was inhibited in miR-21-5p KO mice

In order to evaluate the effect of miR-21-5p on tumor growth of NSCLC, we injected NSCLC cells into WT and miR-21-5p KO mice and then exposed them to PM2.5 for 5 weeks. Through comparison with control group, we discovered that tumor weight and volume increased significantly increased in PM2.5 group. Also, miR-21-5p KO substantially inhibited tumor growth in the control and PM2.5 group (Figure 3A,3B). Also, PM2.5 induced Ki67 expression, which is a marker of cell proliferation, but miR-21-5p KO inhibited Ki67 expression (Figure 3C). In addition, we found that miR-21-5p expression in tumor tissue of WT mice was induced by PM2.5 exposure and also confirmed that miR-21-5p expression was very low in tumor tissue of miR-21-5p KO mice (Figure 3D). These results suggest that miR-21-5p deficiency protects mice from PM2.5 induced tumor growth.

PDE4DIP was a target of miR-21-5p

Mutations of PDE4DIP gene have been identified in various cancers including lung cancer (11). Through searching of various databases (Targetscan, PicTar, and miRanda), we suggested that PDE4DIP may serve as a potential target of miR-21-5p. To identify whether PDE4DIP is a direct target of miR-21-5p, we performed a dual luciferase reporter gene assay. With suitable luciferase reporter at 3'UTR, we designed vectors for mutant 3'UTR of PDE4DIP with mutations at predicted binding site of miR-21-5p (PDE4DIP 3'UTR-mut) and WT 3'UTR of PDE4DIP (PDE4DIP 3'UTR-wt) (results are depicted in Figure 4A). Target Scan (http://www.targetscan.org) was utilized to predict the putative binding sites of miR-21-5p in 3-UTR of PDE4DIP. miR-21-5p mimic decreased, whilst miR-21-5p inhibitor increased luciferase activity of PDE4DIP-wt. Notably, the PDE4DIP-mutant did not show change in the activity of luciferase in response to miR-21-5p mimic or inhibitor, which indicates that PDE4DIP is a direct target of miR-21-5p (Figure 4B). PDE4DIP gene and protein expressions were down-regulated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non-tumor tissues derived from NSCLC patients (Figure 4C-4E) and cell lines compared to normal lung cells (Figure 4F). The study revealed that miR-21-5p mimic suppressed PDE4DIP gene and protein expressions in A549 cell line, but this was restored by PDE4DIP overexpression (Figure 4G). Also, miR-21-5p inhibitor promoted PDE4DIP gene and protein expressions in H1975 cell line, which was reversed by PDE4DIP knockdown (Figure 4G). In addition, PDE4DIP expression decreased in the PM 2.5 group compared to the control group in both cell lines (Figure 4F,4G).

miR-21-5p promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and migration/invasion through inhibition of PDE4DIP

In this experiment, we examined whether PDE4DIP mediated miR-21-5p effects on proliferation and migration/invasion of NSCLC cells. In this regard, proliferation, and colony formation (with/without PM2.5 exposure) of A549 cells were increased by miR-21-5p mimic with the effect being attenuated by PDE4DIP overexpression. Also, miR-21-5p inhibitor decreased H1975 cell proliferation and colony formation (with/without PM2.5 exposure), which was decreased by PDE4DIP knockdown (Figure 5A,5B). Similarly, PDE4DIP overexpression attenuated the promotive effect of miR-21-5p mimic on A549 cell migration/invasion with/without PM2.5 exposure, whereas PDE4DIP knockdown alleviated the inhibitory effect of miR-21-5p inhibitor on H1975 cell migration/invasion with/without PM2.5 exposure (Figure 5C,5D).

RNA-binding protein SORBS2 regulated PDE4DIP expression, while PDE4DIP mediated the effects of SORBS2

SORBS2 has been shown to serve as a repressor in cancers (5,6). Li et al. (5) found that SORBS2 might suppress ovarian cancer invasion by enhancing the stability of gene transcripts such as WAP four-disulfide core domain 1 and interleukin-17D. Through RIP sequencing and bioinformatics analysis of whole transcriptome sequencing, the authors discovered that SORBS2 bound to and stabilized a range of gene transcripts including PDE4DIP. Herein, we explored whether SORBS2 could suppress NSCLC through regulation of PDE4DIP. SORBS2 overexpression promoted PDE4DIP mRNA and protein expressions in A549 cells with/without PM2.5 exposure, which was alleviated by addition of PDE4DIP knockdown. However, SORBS2 knockdown decreased PDE4DIP mRNA and protein expression in H1975 cells with/without PM2.5 exposure, which was offset by the addition of PDE4DIP overexpression (Figure 6A,6B). The results of qRT-PCR, Western blot and IHC showed that SORBS2 was down-regulated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent non-tumor tissues derived from patients with NSCLC from our hospital (Figure 6C-6E). SORBS2 protein expression was also down-regulated in A549 and H1975 cell lines compared with HPAEpiC with/without PM2.5 exposure (Figure 6F). These observations suggest that SORBS2 is down-regulated in lung cancer. In support of this finding, SORBS2 overexpression decreased A549 cell proliferation, colony formation, cell migration and invasion (Figure 6G-6J) under PM2.5 exposure, whilst PDE4DIP knockdown attenuated the above effects. Nonetheless, SORBS2 knockdown increased H1975 cell proliferation, colony formation, cell migration and invasion under PM2.5 exposure, which was alleviated by PDE4DIP overexpression (Figure 6G-6J). These results indicate that SORBS2 exhibits inhibitory function on proliferation, migration and invasion of NSCLC cells through mediation by PDE4DIP. Subsequently, we knocked down PDE4DIP and overexpressed SORBS2 in H1975 cells before injecting these cells into BALB/c-nude mice that were then subjected to PM2.5 exposure for 5 weeks. We found that PDE4DIP knockdown promoted tumor growth, whilst SORBS2 overexpression inhibited promotion of PDE4DIP knockdown on tumor growth (Figure 6K). This observation suggests a tumor-suppressing role for SORBS2. In addition, as observed before in miR-21-5p KO and WT mice (in Figure 3), PM2.5 exposure increased tumor growth compared with the control group (Figure 6K).

SORBS2 mediated effect of miR-21-5p on PDE4DIP expression and NSCLC cell proliferation and migration/invasion

SORBS2 overexpression inhibited, whilst SORBS2 knockdown increased miR-21-5p expression in H1975 and A549 cells (Figure 7A). Importantly, treatment with overexpressed SORBS2 restored effects of miR-21 mimic on declining PDE4DIP protein expression in A549 cell line. Meanwhile, treatment with SORBS2 knockdown reversed the effect of miR-21-5p inhibitor on increasing PDE4DIP expression in H1975 cell line (Figure 7B). Similarly, addition of overexpressed SORBS2 attenuated miR-21-5p mimic effects on promoting proliferation, colony formation, migration and invasion in A549 cells (Figure 7C-7F), but addition of SORBS2 knockdown alleviated miR-21-5p inhibitor effects on suppressing proliferation, colony formation, migration and invasion in H1975 cells (Figure 7C-7F). These results suggest that SORBS2 counteracted effects of miR-21-5p on down-regulating PDE4DIP expression and promoting proliferative and migratory/invasive abilities of NSCLC cells.

Discussion

NSCLC is a major health problem worldwide with high morbidity and mortality. Epidemiological evidence showed that high-level and long-term PM2.5 exposure may promote the initiation, growth, and progression of NSCLC (11). Although several works focused on the impact of PM2.5 on lung cancer, few studies have evaluated the effects of miRNAs on the development of lung cancer. In the present study, we have provided a novel mechanism of miRNA-21-5p-mediated NSCLC tumorigenesis induced by PM2.5 exposure. Several important findings have been enumerated in this work. First, we confirmed that miR-21-5p was up-regulated in lung tumor tissues derived from NSCLC patients and in NSCLC cell lines, while its expression was highly induced by PM2.5 exposure. Also, we found that miR-21-5p deficiency protected mice from tumor growth. Again, this study confirmed the hypothesis that miR-21-5p directly targeted PDE4DIP to enhance the abilities of proliferation, migration, and invasion of NSCLC cells after PM2.5 exposure. Furthermore, the malignant behaviors of PM2.5 were remarkably mitigated by SORBS2 overexpression through its regulation on miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP pathway. This study reveals a novel molecular mechanism by which PM2.5 targets the miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway to exacerbate NSCLC cell proliferation and invasion/migration via SORBS2, which may be a potential therapeutic strategy for NSCLC.

Accumulating evidence has recognized the important contribution of PM2.5 exposure to the occurrence of lung cancer (6-9). Through in vitro NSCLC cell culture, we found that PM2.5 exposure enhanced proliferative and invasive capacity of NSCLC cells in a concentration- and duration-dependent manner. Previous studies also found that proliferation, migration, and invasion of lung cancer cells were induced by PM2.5 exposure (4,10). These findings agree with the epidemiological finding that increased concentration of PM2.5 increased the risk of lung cancer, particularly. Thus, for each 5 µg/m3 increase in PM2.5 in the environment, the risk of lung cancer increased by 18%, since cell proliferation and migration are crucial for progression of cancer. Further, in mice that received transplantation of NSCLC cells, we observed that PM2.5 exposure for 5 weeks increased tumor volume and weight, which provided direct experimental evidence on carcinogenicity of PM2.5. Thus, reducing PM2.5 concentration in the air is very important to reduce the risk of lung cancer.

Although the molecular mechanisms by which PM2.5 induces lung cancer are not fully understood, miRNA deregulation may be related to the link between PM2.5 exposure and lung cancer (33). Herein, we focused on the role of miR-21-5p in NSCLC induced by PM2.5. Previous studies suggested that miR-21 was up-regulated in circulating blood (plasma, serum, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells) and tumor tissues of NSCLC patients (1,16-20). Thus, miR-21 level may be used as a diagnosis and prognosis marker for NSCLC (18-21). Our study confirmed that miR-21-5p was up-regulated in NSCLC tumor tissues and cell lines. We also found that miR-21-5p was further up-regulated by PM2.5 exposure, which is consistent with previous studies (34,35). Further, this study demonstrated that miR-21-5p mimic promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and migration/invasion, whilst miR-21-5p inhibitor had the opposite effect. This result is consistent with findings from previous studies which demonstrated that miR-21 promoted proliferative and migratory/invasive properties of NSCLC cells (15-17,35,36). Furthermore, in the xenograft tumor model generated via NSCLC cell transplantation and PM2.5 exposure, we observed that miR-21 KO mice developed smaller tumors compared to WT mice. Taken together, our results ascertain the carcinogenic role of miR-21-5p in NSCLC and also the mechanistic role of miR-21-5p in the link between PM2.5 and NSCLC.

PDE4DIP gene encodes the anchoring protein and functions as an anchor sequestering component of cAMP-dependent pathway to Golgi and centrosomes. A previous study yielded strong evidence for an association between variants in PDE4DIP and atrial fibrillation, stroke, and heart failure (37). Mutation in PDE4DIP gene has also been shown in various types of cancers (38-41). Up to now, only one study which focused on familial lung cancers validated a functional variant in PDE4DIP gene (42). Thus, our study is the first one to demonstrate the role of PDE4DIP as a target of miR-21-5p in lung cancer. Also, we observed that PDE4DIP was down-regulated in NSCLC tumor tissues and cell lines, wherein PDE4DIP acted as an important target of miR-21-5p. miR-21-5p enhanced NSCLC cell proliferation and migration/invasion by inhibiting PDE4DIP expression under PM2.5 exposure. BALB/c-nude mice injected with NSCLC cells with knockdown of PDE4DIP developed larger tumors compared to mice injected with control NSCLC cells after PM2.5 exposure for 5 weeks. Our results reveal the new regulatory role of miR-21-5p/ PDE4DIP axis in the development of PM2.5-induced lung cancer. Therefore, these results indicate that PM2.5 exposure leads to the overexpression of miR-21-5p in lung cancer cells, which in turn decreases the expression of its target gene PDE4DIP. Further, the findings suggest that PM2.5 exposure promotes lung cancer cell proliferation and migration/invasion, which ultimately cause malignant behavior changes in lung cancer.

SORBS2 gene encodes the SORBS2 proteins and interacts with actin and some cytoskeletal protein (26). As stated earlier, SORBS2 serves as a repressor in cancers, such as suppression on the invasion of ovarian and liver cancer cells (28,29) and head and neck squamous cell. However, not much work has been done on the effect of SORBS2 on lung cancer yet. Only one study which employed whole genome oligonucleotide microarray-based comparative genomic hybridization in 10 samples of NSCLC revealed that SORBS2 gene expression was significantly decreased (43). In the present study, we first demonstrated that SORBS2 expression was significantly decreased in NSCLC tumor tissues and cells. Next, we discovered that SORBS2 promoted the expression of PDE4DIP, but inhibited miR-21-5p and effects of miR-21-5p on down-regulating PDE4DIP expression and promoting malignant behaviors of NSCLC cells. Furthermore, in the tumor xenograft model generated by NSCLC transplantation and PM2.5 exposure, SORBS2 reversed effect of PDE4DIP knockdown on promoting tumor growth in mice. These results demonstrate that SORBS2 exerts tumor-suppressive effects on PM2.5 exposure induced NSCLC via its action on multiple steps in miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway. Therefore, a therapeutic regimen targeting SORBS2 might be a promising strategy to overcome PM2.5-associated lung cancer progression.

Here are several limitations in this study. First, in the present study, we focused on the role of SORBS2 in NSCLC and the relationship between SORBS2 and miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP. We did not explore the detailed mechanisms for the regulation of miR-21-5p and PDE4DIP expression by SORBS2, which thus warrants further investigation. Second, in the tumor xenograft model, we subcutaneously injected NSCLC cells into mice to generate subcutaneous tumors since they are easier generate and monitor. Although we evaluated the effects of miR-21-5p manipulation, PDE4DIP and SORBS2 on subcutaneous tissue, the results still reflect stimulatory or inhibitory effects of these molecules on tumor growth induced by NSCLC injection and PM2.5 exposure.

Conclusions

Our findings underscore the therapeutic relevance of targeting the miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway. Antisense oligonucleotides against miR-21-5p, small molecules promoting PDE4DIP expression, or strategies to enhance SORBS2 activity could form the basis of novel interventions for NSCLC. These approaches may be particularly effective in mitigating tumor progression in patients exposed to PM2.5. Preclinical evaluation of these targeted strategies is warranted. Clinically, miR-21-5p expression in plasma or tumor tissues could serve as a biomarker for NSCLC risk in high-PM2.5 regions. Therapeutic inhibition of miR-21-5p or activation of SORBS2/PDE4DIP may benefit patients exposed to airborne pollutants.

In future work, we also propose leveraging large-scale public datasets such as TCGA and GEO to analyze the relationship between SORBS2/PDE4DIP expression levels and patient survival outcomes. Moreover, incorporating PM2.5 exposure data derived from environmental databases may allow for integrated analysis of environmental and genetic risk factors, offering deeper insights into air pollution-related lung carcinogenesis.

In conclusion, this study has revealed that miR-21 promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and invasion/migration as well as tumor growth by targeting PDE4DIP. SORBS2 could also act as a tumor suppressor of NSCLC through regulation of miR-21-5p/PDE4DIP signaling pathway. Targeting miR-21-5p (e.g., antisense oligonucleotides), enhancing PDE4DIP expression, or activating SORBS2 represents promising strategies for NSCLC, particularly in PM2.5-exposed patients.

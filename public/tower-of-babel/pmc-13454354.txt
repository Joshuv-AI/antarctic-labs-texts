Randomized phase 2 trial of a PARP inhibitor TSL-1502 in germline BRCA-mutated, HER2-negative locally advanced/metastatic breast cancer
Authors: Bo Lan, Faliang Xu, Tao Sun, Fuming Qiu, Yongsheng Wang, Shouman Wang, Wei Li, Yahua Zhong, Xinhong Wu, Quchang Ouyang, Ke Wang, Xiaolan Mi, Rui Liu, Binghe Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Glucuronide prodrug strategies may enhance target specificity and reduce the toxicity of PARP inhibitors, but no clinical evaluation has been performed. We evaluated TSL-1502, a novel glucuronide prodrug of a PARP inhibitor, in a randomized, open-label, phase 2 study at 28 sites in China (NCT05420779). Eligible patients were women aged 18–75 years with HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer and germline BRCA mutations. Patients were assigned randomly (2:2:1) to receive TSL-1502 at 350 mg or 500 mg once daily or the investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (eribulin, capecitabine, or vinorelbine) in 3-week cycles. Sixty-three patients were enrolled between August 18, 2022, and March 5, 2024. According to the Independent Review Committee assessment, the objective response rates were 36.0% (95% CI, 18.0–57.5) in the 350 mg group, 55.6% (95% CI, 35.3–74.5) in the 500 mg group, and 40.0% (95% CI, 12.2–73.8) in the chemotherapy group. The median progression-free survival times were 5.6 (95% CI, 4.0–8.2), 8.8 (95% CI, 5.7–not assessable [NA]), and 9.2 (95% CI, 1.38–NA) months, respectively, and the overall survival times were 17.4 months (95% CI, 9.1–NA), not reached (95% CI, 16.9–NA), and 19.8 months (95% CI, 9.2–NA), respectively. Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 60.0%, 59.3%, and 80.0% of patients, with anemia most common in the TSL-1502 group and neutropenia most common with chemotherapy. No treatment-related deaths occurred. TSL-1502 at 500 mg showed promising antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile, supporting further clinical development.

Introduction

Breast cancer continues to be the most common type of cancer that causes death among women worldwide.1 Germline deleterious mutations in BRCA1 and/or BRCA2 (gBRCAm) are present in about 5% of unselected breast cancer patients.2–4 Mutations in BRCA compromise double-strand break repair, rendering cells increasingly dependent on poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP)-1 and PARP-2 for the repair of single-strand breaks.5 Under these circumstances, PARP inhibitors can block DNA repair, leading to the death of tumor cells.

Currently, the standard treatment for these patients includes PARP inhibitors, particularly olaparib and talazoparib. Evidence from pivotal randomized phase 3 trials has demonstrated that PARP inhibitors provide substantial clinical benefit in PARP inhibitor-naïve patients, including those previously treated with chemotherapy. Olaparib and talazoparib significantly improved objective response rate (ORR; olaparib: 55.9% vs. 28.8%; talazoparib: 62.2% vs. 27.2%) and progression-free survival (PFS; olaparib: 7.0 vs. 4.2 months; talazoparib: 8.6 vs. 5.6 months) compared with chemotherapy according to the OlympiAD and EMBRACA trials.6,7 Despite these encouraging outcomes, hematological toxicity is frequently observed during PARP inhibitor therapy, affecting hematopoiesis.5,8–11 In the EMBRACA phase 3 trial for breast cancer, anemia and neutropenia represented the frequently reported hematological adverse events (AEs) for talazoparib, with grade 3 or higher rates of 40.2% and 22.2%, respectively.7 Similar class-effect toxicities have been observed in the OlympiAD trial, although the rates of grade ≥3 anemia and neutropenia were lower (16.1% and 9.3%) for olaparib; they nonetheless led to dose reductions and interruptions.6 Therefore, designing PARP inhibitors with minimal hematological toxicity, without compromising efficacy, holds significant clinical value.

To overcome these challenges, one promising strategy is the development of nontoxic prodrugs that improve tumor selectivity, thereby enabling a favorable risk–benefit balance. β-Glucuronidase is a lysosomal enzyme capable of hydrolyzing glucuronic acid and is enriched in the tumor microenvironment, mainly released from inflammatory cells and necrotic tumor cells.12–14 Several glucuronide prodrugs for chemotherapy drugs have been investigated, demonstrating potential to enhance antitumor efficacy while reducing toxicity compared to standard drugs. One study reported that the activity of β-glucuronidase in breast cancer tissue was nearly six times higher compared with that in adjacent tissue, indicating the feasibility of selective prodrug activation in breast cancer. Therefore, we hypothesize that designing glucuronide prodrugs of PARP inhibitors could improve tumor targeting therapy, mitigating hematological toxicity while maintaining antitumor efficacy.

TSL-1502 M, as a novel PARP inhibitor, demonstrates characteristic biochemical and cytotoxic effects of PARP inhibition at substantially lower concentrations than those required for olaparib.12 Based on this, we developed TSL-1502, the glucuronide prodrug of the TSL-1502 M, which, in preclinical studies have shown to predominantly release TSL-1502 M at the tumor site, resulting in anti-tumor activity superior to that of olaparib in homologous recombination-deficient tumors.12 In the phase 1 study, TSL-1502 at doses of 350 and 500 mg showed promising efficacy and safety among patients with advanced-stage solid tumors, including breast cancer (see Tables 1, 2 and Supplementary Tables 1–5). Results

Baseline characteristics and treatment

Between August 18, 2022, and March 5, 2024, 63 patients were enrolled and randomized: 25 were assigned to the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 27 to the TSL-1502 500 mg group, and 11 to the chemotherapy group. In the chemotherapy group, one patient did not receive the study treatment and was thus removed from all the datasets. The RES included 60 patients because two patients did not have baseline target lesions. The PPS included 56 patients, 6 of whom were excluded, and the detailed reasons are available in Supplementary Table 6. All patients had completed the study at the data cutoff date (March 31, 2025; Fig. 1). Twenty patients (80.0%) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 21 patients (77.8%) in the 500 mg group, and 6 patients (60.0%) in the chemotherapy group subsequently received anticancer therapy. Among these, 6 patients received subsequent treatment with other PARP inhibitors, including 1 patient (4.0%) in the 350 mg group, 2 patients (7.4%) in the 500 mg group, and 2 patients (20.0%) in the chemotherapy group (Supplementary Table 7).Fig. 1Trial profile in the phase 2 study

Trial profile in the phase 2 study

The median treatment cycle was 7.0 (range, 2.0–34.0) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 8.0 (range, 1.0–23.0) in the TSL-1502 500 mg group, and 8.0 (range, 1.0–24.0) in the chemotherapy group (Supplementary Table 8). The median dose intensity was 44,450.0 mg (range, 14350.0–248800.0) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group and 69,200.0 mg (range, 5200.0–231500.0) in the TSL-1502 500 mg group. In the chemotherapy group, 90% of patients received eribulin mesylate at a median dose intensity of 30.8 mg (range, 8.0–118.0), while the remaining 10% received capecitabine at a median dose intensity of 12,600.0 mg (range, 12600.0–12600.0). The median age of the 62 treated patients was 48.5 years (range, 29.0–68.0), and 31 (50.0%) patients had TNBC. As shown in Table 1, baseline characteristics were generally balanced across the three groups.

Efficacy

In the FAS, the ORR was numerically highest in the TSL-1502 500 mg group (55.6%; 95% CI, 35.3–74.5), compared with 36.0% (95% CI, 18.0–57.5) in the 350 mg group and 40.0% (95% CI, 12.2–73.8) in the chemotherapy group (Table 3, Fig. 2a). The DCR was 84.0% (95% CI, 63.9–95.5) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 88.9% (95% CI, 70.8–97.6) in the TSL-1502 500 mg group, and 60.0% (95% CI, 26.3–87.8) in the chemotherapy group. The median DoR was 5.6 months (95% CI, 2.7-not assessable [NA]), 9.9 months (95% CI, 2.9–14.2), and not reached (95% CI, 3.9-NA) in the respective groups (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Fig. 1). Subgroup analysis revealed that the ORR in patients with TNBC was 33.3% (95% CI, 9.9–65.1), 42.9% (95% CI, 17.7–71.1), and 20.0% (95% CI, 0.5–71.6), respectively (Supplementary Table 10). Among the patients who had received ≤1 line of chemotherapy, the ORR was 53.3% (95% CI, 26.6–78.7) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 68.4% (95% CI, 43.5–87.4) in the TSL-1502 500 mg group, and 42.9% in the chemotherapy group (95% CI, 9.9–81.6). An exploratory analysis revealed that there was no apparent difference in HRD scores between patients who achieved an objective response and those who did not (Supplementary Fig. 2).

With a median follow-up of 14.8 months (range, 2.3–25.3), disease progression or death had occurred in 35 patients. Median PFS was 5.6 months (95% CI, 4.0–8.2) in the TSL-1502 350 mg group, 8.8 months (95% CI, 5.7–NA) in the TSL-1502 500 mg group, and 9.2 months (95% CI, 1.4–NA) in the chemotherapy group (Fig. 2c). The 12-month PFS rates were 20.4% (95% CI, 6.1–40.7), 47.3% (95% CI, 23.1–68.2), and 37.0% (95% CI, 6.8–69.3), respectively. With respect to OS, 21 patients died, and the median OS was 17.4 months (95% CI, 9.1–NA), not reached (95% CI, 16.9–NA), and 19.8 months (95% CI, 9.2–NA) in the respective groups (Fig. 2d). The results of PFS and OS observed in the RES (Supplementary Fig. 3) were similar to those in the primary analysis.

Safety

Safety data are summarized in Table 4 and Supplementary Tables 11–12. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred at 92.0%, 100.0%, and 100.0% of patients, with 60.0%, 59.3%, and 80.0%, respectively, at a severity of grade 3 or higher. Discussion

This randomized, positive-control, phase 2 study provides the initial clinical evidence for the use of a glucuronide prodrug of a PARP inhibitor in breast cancer and demonstrated promising efficacy and a manageable safety profile. Compared with patients in the chemotherapy group, patients in the TSL-1502 500 mg group achieved deeper responses, with an ORR of 55.6% versus 40.0%. In the context of comparable median PFS, the higher 12-month PFS rate observed with TSL-1502 500 mg than with chemotherapy suggested that it may delay disease progression. Importantly, the PFS benefit appeared to suggest a favorable trend in OS, with the median OS not reached in the TSL-1502 500 mg group vs. 19.8 months in the chemotherapy group. However, the observed difference in OS could be influenced by both the greater use of subsequent therapy in the 500 mg group (77.8% vs. 60.0%) and the limited sample. Notably, two patients from the chemotherapy group received subsequent treatment with PARP inhibitors, which further supports the potential clinical activity of PARP inhibition in this patient population. The subgroup analysis indicated that TSL-1502 at a dosage of 500 mg was more effective in less heavily pretreated patients, with an ORR of 68.4% in those who had received ≤1 previous chemotherapy line of chemotherapy compared with 25.0% for those with more than one. This finding is consistent with those of previous studies on other PARP inhibitors, in which prior chemotherapy burden is known to influence efficacy outcomes.15,16

Currently, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines recommend olaparib and talazoparib as the preferred treatment options for patients with locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer harboring gBRCAm, with median PFS times of 7.0 and 8.6 months, respectively, and ORRs of 59.9% and 62.6%.6,7 In this context, our exploratory phase 2 study of TSL-1502 at a 500 mg dose achieved a median PFS of 8.8 months and an ORR of 55.6%. While acknowledging the inherent and significant limitations of any cross-trial comparison, especially given the differences in study design, patient population, and small sample size of our pilot study, these findings suggest that TSL-1502 possesses encouraging antitumor activity and warrants further investigation in larger, randomized studies. Notably, as a β-glucuronidase-activated prodrug, the efficacy of TSL-1502 may be influenced, at least in part, by the expression and activity of this enzyme within the tumor microenvironment. Experience from antibody-directed enzyme prodrug therapy has indicated that further increasing the local concentration of the β-glucuronidase can enhance prodrug activation and antitumor efficacy.17–19 This observation suggested that reliance solely on endogenous tumor-associated β-glucuronidase levels may be insufficient to fully liberate the active parent drug TSL-1502 M, thereby potentially limiting the overall therapeutic efficacy of TSL-1502. Overall, TSL-1502 at a dosage of 500 mg is still considered a promising therapeutic agent.

In theory, tumors with higher HRDs may be more susceptible to PARP inhibitors or platinum-based chemotherapy, given that PARP inhibitors block single-strand DNA repair, whereas platinum compounds induce DNA crosslinks that generate double-strand breaks, both of which lead to unrepaired DNA damage accumulation and tumor cell death.19 Previous studies have shown that patients with gBRCA1/2m mutations generally have high HRD scores (≥42), which was also observed in our cohort, which consisted entirely of gBRCA1/2 m patients (Supplementary Table 13). Moreover, in this gBRCA1/2m population, the HRD score did not clearly correlate with the efficacy of TSL-1502. However, the independent predictive value of HRD scores in nongBRCA1/2m patients remains uncertain. For instance, the TNT trial revealed that among nongBRCA1/2m patients, high HRD scores did not confer a superior response to carboplatin,20 whereas Telli et al. reported that high HRD scores in nongBRCA1/2m patients were associated with increased sensitivity to cisplatin.21 These findings suggest that the predictive value of HRD scores beyond gBRCA1/2m status requires further investigation.

TSL-1502 demonstrated a safety profile similar to previously reported PARP inhibitors, without new or unanticipated safety signals.6,7,15,22 Although AEs ≥ grade 3 occurred slightly greater than that reported for other PARP inhibitors, these events rarely led to dose reduction, treatment discontinuation, or death. Within the TSL-1502 treatment groups, compared with the 350 mg dose, the 500 mg dose resulted in similar toxicity (grade ≥3 TRAEs, 60.0% vs 59.3%) but greater efficacy (ORR, 55.6% vs. 36.0%). These results may reflect that TSL-1502 remains within a tolerable therapeutic window over the evaluated dose range, with a plateauing exposure–toxicity relationship, whereas the improved efficacy may be associated with increased systemic exposure, as indicated by increased AUC and Cmax (Supplementary Fig. 4). Hematological toxicity, a known class effect associated with PARP inhibitors across ovarian, prostate, and breast cancers, was the most common TRAE in the TSL-1502 treatment groups, but most of these effects were manageable through dose modifications (treatment interruption and/or dose reduction) and supportive care. Notably, although TSL-1502 was designed as a β-glucuronidase-activated prodrug intended to preferentially release the active compound within the tumor microenvironment, β-glucuronidase has also been reported to be expressed in serum and in most blood cells.23 This broader expression profile may partly explain the off-tumor hematological toxicity observed in the present study, indicating that further optimization of the treatment strategy is warranted. Nonhematological adverse events were predominantly grade 1–2. In the TSL-1502 group, grade 3 or higher nonhematological TRAEs were limited to hepatic and metabolic TRAEs, indicating the need for close monitoring of liver function and lipid levels, particularly in patients with preexisting liver disease or metabolic disorders. Overall, the safety profile of TSL-1502 was considered acceptable.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the open-label design may introduce bias, although the use of the IRC assessment helped mitigate it. Second, the relatively small sample size may affect the robustness of between-group comparisons. Third, although the PARP inhibitors olaparib and talazoparib were incorporated into the NCCN guidelines, no PARP inhibitors had been approved in China for breast cancer at the time this study was designed (November 2021). Therefore, chemotherapy remained the standard therapy at the time of our study design and was thus selected as the control rather than other PARP inhibitors. Fourth, four ineligible patients were mistakenly enrolled, with two excluded from the RES and all four from the PPS, which may slightly affect the interpretation of treatment efficacy. Fifth, no formal sample size determination was performed in this study, which limits the statistical power and thus the ability to draw definitive conclusions. Additionally, patient-reported quality of life data were not systematically obtained or analyzed in this study, which limits a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical value of the treatments. Finally, all patients in this study were Chinese, which could restrict the applicability of the results to populations with different racial or geographic backgrounds, although it ensured a high degree of homogeneity in the study population. Despite these limitations, our findings suggest that TSL-1502 remains a therapeutic candidate worthy of further investigation. Future studies are warranted to validate its efficacy in larger randomized studies, investigate rational combination strategies, as well as carefully extend the evaluation to other tumor types.

Conclusion

In conclusion, TSL-1502 at a dose of 500 mg demonstrated encouraging activity and manageable toxicity in patients with HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer harboring germline BRCA mutations. These findings indicate the therapeutic potential of TSL-1502 at this dose and support its continued use in further clinical investigations.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

Patients were recruited from 28 sites across China in this randomized, parallel-group, open-label, positive-control, phase 2 study. Female patients aged 18–75, with histologically or cytologically confirmed HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, harboring deleterious or suspected deleterious germline BRCA mutation, and had received no more than three prior lines of cytotoxic chemotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease were eligible. Prior treatment with platinum was allowed if the best response was a complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) lasting ≥12 weeks, and recurrence had to occur ≥6 months after the last dose of neoadjuvant or adjuvant platinum. Patients with hormone receptor–positive breast cancer had at least one failed prior endocrine therapy or were considered by the investigator to be inappropriate for endocrine therapy. Patients had received antitumor therapy with taxane ± anthracycline in the neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and/or metastatic stages and had normal baseline organ and bone marrow functions. The other inclusion criterion was measurable disease per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1), a life expectancy of at least 12 months, and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1. Key exclusion criteria included pregnancy or breastfeeding, active inflammatory breast cancer, the presence of active brain metastases, prior use of PARP inhibitors, and a known allergy to TSL-1502. Detailed eligibility criteria are available in the Supplementary Materials.

Randomization and masking

Patients were randomized (2:2:1) via the Interactive Web Response System to receive 350 mg of TSL-1502 (TSL-1502 350 mg), 500 mg of TSL-1502 (TSL-1502 500 mg), or chemotherapy of the investigator’s choice (chemotherapy group). Randomization was stratified on the basis of the number of prior lines of chemotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease (≤1 or >1) and whether patients had TNBC (yes or no). As the study was open-label, treatment allocation was known to both investigators and patients. Potential bias in evaluating efficacy endpoints was minimized by an Independent Review Committee (IRC) that was blinded to treatment assignments.

Procedure

Patients received TSL-1502 at doses of 350 mg or 500 mg once daily or one of the three investigator-selected chemotherapies (eribulin mesylate, capecitabine, or vinorelbine) according to the respective prescribing information. All assigned treatments were given in 3-week cycles and continued until disease progression, unacceptable AEs, death, or other protocol-specified criteria were met.

Dose modifications were permitted to manage toxicity. Patients in the TSL-1502 350 mg and 500 mg cohorts could undergo up to two dose reductions of 100 mg each; dose re-escalation was not allowed. Treatment was permanently discontinued if intolerable adverse events occurred after two doses were reduced. For the chemotherapy group, dose modifications based on toxicity were conducted according to the respective prescribing information of each chemotherapy agent.

Safety was assessed by monitoring adverse events, clinical laboratory tests, and physical examinations. AEs were recorded for up to 28 days following the last study treatment dose and were graded per the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Pharmacokinetic (PK) analyses were performed during the first treatment cycle, with blood samples collected on days 1, 8, 14, and 21.

Tumor response was evaluated every 6 weeks by the IRC using computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging per RECIST v1.1 until disease progression, unacceptable AEs, death, voluntary withdrawal, or study completion/termination. For patients who achieved a CR or PR, the response was confirmed four weeks later. Survival follow-up occurred every 12 weeks until death, patient refusal, loss to follow-up, or study completion/termination after the end of the treatment.

The HRD score was calculated using the Precision Human HRD Assay Panel (Precision Scientific), as the total of loss of heterozygosity (LOH), large-scale state transitions (LST), and telomeric allelic imbalance (TAI).24

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the ORR, defined as the proportion of patients who achieved CR or PR. The secondary endpoints included the duration of response (DoR, time from the first documentation of CR or PR to disease progression or all-cause death), disease control rate (DCR, the percentage of patients with CR, PR, and SD), PFS (time from randomization to disease progression or all-cause death), 12-month PFS rate, overall survival (OS, time from randomization to death due to any cause), and safety. Exploratory analyses were performed to assess the correlation between the ORR and homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) scores among patients who received TSL-1502.

Statistical analysis

We determined the sample size primarily to allow an initial evaluation of efficacy signals, which would serve to inform the subsequent phase 3 study, rather than on strict statistical assumptions. Efficacy was evaluated in the full analysis set (FAS), the response evaluation set (RES) and the per-protocol set (PPS). The FAS included enrolled patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. The RES comprised patients from the FAS, excluding those without target lesions at baseline per the Independent Review Committee (IRC). The PPS included all enrolled patients who were administered at least one dose of the investigational drug, had evaluable baseline tumor assessments and at least one postbaseline tumor assessment, had no major protocol deviations that could have significantly affected the primary endpoint, and had good treatment compliance. Safety was assessed in the safety analysis set (SS), which included patients from the FAS populations with safety records.

Two-sided p value less than 0.05 were statistical significance. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and safety outcomes. For continuous variables, data are reported as medians with ranges or interquartile ranges, while categorical variables are summarized as counts and percentages [%]. The Clopper–Pearson method was used to estimate 95% CIs for the ORR and DCR, and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used for between-group comparisons. The ORR was analyzed across prespecified subgroups. DoR, PFS, and OS were estimated via Kaplan–Meier method, and between-group comparisons were conducted using the log-rank test. The PK parameters were calculated using Phoenix WinNonlin (version 6.3) via a noncompartmental method. All the statistical analyses were performed using SAS (version 9.4).

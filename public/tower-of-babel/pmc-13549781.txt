Where Does JKNS Stand in JCR 2025, and How Should We Proceed for Sustainable Growth?
Authors: Hee-Jin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The 2025 Journal Citation Reports (JCR) reveal that the Impact Factor (IF) for the Journal of Korean Neurosurgical Society (JKNS) has risen to 1.9, an increase from 1.7 in 2024. The upward trend in the IF compared to previous years is a highly encouraging development. Although the IF of JKNS has increased, a substantial gap still remains compared to top-ranked neurosurgical journals. The time has come to set clear goals for where JKNS should head next and to explore how to reach them. While maintaining our existing strategies, we aim to consider actionable approaches to advance to the next level.

INTRODUCTION

The 2025 Journal Citation Reports (JCR) reveal that the Impact Factor (IF) for the Journal of Korean Neurosurgical Society (JKNS) has risen to 1.9, an increase from 1.7 in 2024 [6]. Although this figure falls short of the peak of 2.25 recorded in 2022, it demonstrates a modest but steady upward trend in recent years [3-5].

On the other hand, the nationwide medical system crisis that erupted in 2024 has entered a phase of stabilization since residents began returning to duty in September 2025. Although the number of newly qualified specialists has not yet recovered and many residents are undergoing additional training following their board examinations to compensate for the time missed in their residency, this situation is expected to resolve over time.

This study examines the status of JKNS among the neurosurgical journals and whether the past endeavors to improve its IF have been successful and discusses the future paths the journal should pursue for sustainable development.

Each year, as the JCR is released, we have analyzed changes in JKNS’s IF and explored directions for its future development [10-12]. As the same analysis was carried out this year, JKNS was compared with other neurosurgical journals. Furthermore, the outcomes of past development strategies were investigated, and prospective avenues for advancing to the next level were reflected upon.

The upward trend in the IF compared to previous years is a highly encouraging development. This increase in the citation index is primarily attributed to the fact that the decline in the number of published papers outpaced the reduction in total citations (Table 1). Although the IF increased, the decline in received citations should be examined carefully. While this highlights the critical importance of managing publication volume, it simultaneously underscores the need for concerted efforts to enhance the citation potential of published manuscripts to further elevate the IF in the future.

Another encouraging trend is the growth in manuscript submissions. Although a decline in submission volume was previously noted in the special article of the September 2025 issue [12], this year’s data reveal a distinct surge in submissions, encompassing both domestic and international papers (Table 2). This resurgence can be interpreted as a reflection of the recovery in the domestic research environment following the medical crisis; furthermore, it is a positive indicator that JKNS is perceived as an attractive venue for international researchers. As the volume of incoming manuscripts increases, meticulous attention must be paid to streamlining the review process and ensuring swift evaluations without disproportionately increasing the burden on editors and reviewers.

THE STATUS OF JKNS, COMPARED TO OTHER NEUROSURGICAL JOURNALS

Fig. 1 summarizes the changes in the IFs of neurosurgery-related journals over recent years. Although the two most widely recognized traditional journals in neurosurgery have experienced a slight decrease recently, their IFs remain around 4. The IF of JKNS has increased compared to the past, but it cannot be described as having risen remarkably in recent years. Journals comparable to JKNS in terms of IF have also shown similar figures over the past few years. In summary, the IFs of neurosurgical journals display a relatively stable trend.

It is realistically challenging to catch up with the IFs of top-ranked neurosurgical journals in the short term. However, if JKNS can raise its IF by a meaningful margin above journals that currently share a similar IF—say, to around 2.5, though this is an arbitrary value—it is believed that the journal’s standing can be elevated to a higher tier, even if not among the top-ranked neurosurgery journals. Given that the IFs of other journals are not undergoing significant changes at present, raising JKNS’s IF to a distinct level can be expected to meaningfully enhance its stature.

STRATEGIES FOR THE FUTURE DEVELOPMENT OF JKNS

Looking at the citation trends per article over the past few years, the citation rate of review articles has increased. While the citation rate of original articles also rose, it did not increase as much as that of review articles. In contrast, case reports showed a decline in citation rate (Fig. 2). This indicates that the strategy—applying rigorous peer review to case reports to accept only a highly significant minority, while commissioning and publishing review articles on current hot topics—has proven successful.

This strategy has certainly helped elevate and maintain the IF of JKNS at its current level. However, it is difficult to guarantee that it will remain effective for advancing to the next stage. A defining characteristic of journals in the highest IF tier is that the proportion of review articles is very low compared to original articles, and there is little difference between the citation rates of original and review articles. Furthermore, even in these top-tier journals, the proportion of articles receiving fewer citations than the journal’s overall IF reaches 60–70% [8]. For these reasons, concerns have been raised that the IF has limitations in accurately evaluating a journal’s true stature [1,9]. Nevertheless, it remains an undeniable fact that the IF is currently the most widely used metric for evaluating academic journals, making efforts to enhance it indispensable. Since the IF is one of the primary criteria authors consider when submitting their manuscripts, paying close attention to it is essential for attracting high-quality submissions. Furthermore, when similar papers are retrieved across multiple journals, authors are more likely to cite those published in journals with higher IFs. Therefore, an increase in IF can be expected to create a virtuous cycle that promotes the influx of excellent manuscripts and further elevates the citation rate of published articles.

These limitations carry important implications for how JKNS should pursue strategies to raise its IF. Since the IF is an arithmetic mean, the primary step must be to lower the proportion of uncited articles. As the total number of published articles decreases, the denominator of the IF equation shrinks while the numerator remains steady, naturally driving the IF upward. Table 1 clearly illustrates the impact of publication volume on JKNS’s recent IF. While regulating the number of published articles is necessary, excessive reduction carries the risk of depriving even potentially citable papers of the opportunity to be published. Therefore, adjusting publication volume should not aim to set and maintain arbitrary targets, but rather focus on filtering out manuscripts of low academic value.

At the same time, it is essential to identify and cultivate high-impact articles. At present, it is unrealistic to expect top-tier manuscripts to be submitted to JKNS first; thus, active efforts are needed to encourage domestic researchers to submit highly novel papers with compelling findings to JKNS. Another effective approach is to provide unique data that can only be found in JKNS. Data on disease epidemiology and treatment outcomes derived from nationwide surveys hold strong potential to serve as landmark reference material.

It is difficult to predict which strategy will prove most effective at this stage. The journal’s progress to date can be partly attributed to institutional refinements—namely, paying close attention to operations and streamlining editorial processes. While continuing these ongoing efforts, deliberate consideration of new strategies is undeniably necessary to jump to the next level.

CONCLUSION

JKNS has achieved a level of progress that was previously unimaginable. In the early 2000s, few would have anticipated that JKNS could be indexed in SCI; yet it was successfully indexed in SCIE in 2009. When its initial IF was released, the score stood at just 0.16 [7]. Similarly, few expected that JKNS would one day stand shoulder-to-shoulder with journals like Acta Neurochirurgica (2025 IF 1.9) and World Neurosurgery (2025 IF 2.0) [6].

Now, creative thinking is essential to advance to the next stage. It is hoped that establishing and executing new strategies will drive another unprecedented leap forward. To achieve this, it is equally clear that simply repeating past practices will not be enough.

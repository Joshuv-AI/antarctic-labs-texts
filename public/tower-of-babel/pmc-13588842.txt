Theorem 1

Let n ≥ 2n≥2. Given any finite set of rational points \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S\subset {\mathbb {P}}^n({\mathbb {Q}})$$\end{document}S⊂Pn(Q), there exists a smooth projective curve \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C/{\mathbb {Q}}$$\end{document}C/Q contained in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbb {P}}^n$$\end{document}Pn, of genus g ≥ 2g≥2, such that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C({\mathbb {Q}})=S$$\end{document}C(Q)=S.

Our proof gives an effective algorithm for computing the curve C from the given set of rational points S.

Remark 2

If in the statement of Theorem 1 we had not asked that C be a projective curve, then one could simply use Bertini’s theorem [7, Corollary 6.7.(2)] to find a smooth curve C of large genus passing through all points of S. Then, by cutting away a hyperplane containing the finitely many points outside S, produce a quasiprojective curve whose rational points are exactly S.

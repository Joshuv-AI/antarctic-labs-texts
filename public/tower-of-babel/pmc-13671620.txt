Virgo-inspired Periorbital Lifting Injection Technique: A Structured Multinodal, Layered Framework for Periorbital Rejuvenation
Authors: Jiajia Li, Yongping Xue, Letian Kuang, Qianyi Wu, Wenrong Tian, Suriye Rehemutila, Jiang Ai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Periorbital aging extends beyond isolated volume loss and encompasses progressive changes in structural support, contour continuity, and tissue transition. These changes often produce a segmented or fatigued appearance that may not be adequately corrected by focal filling alone. We describe the Virgo-inspired periorbital lifting injection technique as a structured, multinodal, layered framework that organizes recognized periorbital support and transition zones into a reproducible treatment sequence. Deep-anchoring nodes are used to provide distributed support, whereas superficial refinement nodes are used to improve contour continuity and optical integration. The Virgo-inspired periorbital lifting injection technique does not propose novel anatomy or previously undescribed injection points. Rather, it offers a practical framework intended to improve procedural clarity, teachability, and consistency in periorbital rejuvenation. An illustrative case is presented to demonstrate the treatment logic and clinical application of this approach.

Takeaways

Question: How can periorbital rejuvenation strategies better address the complex, multidirectional aging changes of the periorbital region beyond simple volume replacement?

Findings: The Virgo-inspired periorbital lifting injection technique applies a multinodal, layered injection network guided by anatomical retaining structures to enhance lateral canthal support, refine the lid–cheek transition, and preserve natural dynamic mobility.

Meaning: The Virgo-inspired periorbital lifting injection technique provides a reproducible, anatomically informed framework for achieving balanced and natural-appearing periorbital rejuvenation.

INTRODUCTION

Periorbital rejuvenation is technically challenging because of complex anatomy, continuous motion, and high visual sensitivity. Aging-related changes extend beyond volumetric loss to include attenuation of ligamentous support, muscular imbalance, and altered soft tissue adaptation, leading to contour discontinuity and lid–cheek demarcation.1 In clinical practice, many injectors treat the periorbital region empirically and adjust in real time according to tissue response. Although effective in experienced hands, this approach can be difficult to standardize and teach.2 We therefore developed the Virgo-inspired periorbital lifting injection technique (VIPLIT) as a structured framework that organizes commonly treated support and transition zones into a multinodal, layered sequence. Its purpose is not to introduce new anatomical targets, but to provide a reproducible way of conceptualizing and performing treatment in this region (Fig. 1).

CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

The central idea of VIPLIT is that periorbital rejuvenation may benefit from distributed support rather than focal volumetric dominance. The Virgo constellation is used only as a visual metaphor for branching connectivity and nonlinear reinforcement; it is not intended as a literal anatomical model.

The framework references recognized anatomical or anatomy-informed regions, including the temporal ligamentous adhesions, supraorbital ligamentous adhesions, lateral orbital thickening, and the superficial temporal septum, together with selected eyelid transition zones.3,4 These are organized into 2 functional groups:

Injection Protocol

The procedure follows a hierarchical 2-phase sequence. First, deep periosteal anchoring is performed at the O-series nodes to establish foundational support and lateral orbital stability. Distributed anchoring is intended to facilitate gradual tissue adaptation through passive structural guidance rather than imposed lifting vectors.

Subsequently, superficial modulation is performed at the M-series nodes to refine contour continuity, enhance the pretarsal roll, and reduce midfacial visual voids. This layered strategy minimizes the risk of overcorrection while preserving natural eyelid mobility. Material Selection

A blended injectable approach was prepared immediately before use by physically mixing a hyaluronic acid (HA) filler (e.p.t.q.; JETEMA Co., Ltd.), a collagen-based injectable (Sunmax; Sunmax Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), and normal saline at a 1:1:1 (v/v/v) ratio. Lidocaine was incorporated into one-quarter of the saline fraction to improve injection comfort. Emerging clinical evidence suggests that collagen and HA may have complementary roles in periocular rejuvenation.5 HA provides predictable malleability and support, whereas collagen-based materials exhibit lower hydrophilicity and enhanced tissue integration, potentially supporting both deeper structural support and superficial refinement. However, such evidence should not be interpreted as validation of formulation safety.

The blended use of HA, collagen, and saline described here should be considered an off-label and nonstandard approach. These products were not manufacturer-validated for direct extemporaneous mixing, and the stability, rheological behavior, degradation profile, and long-term tissue interactions of such mixtures remain insufficiently characterized. Clinicians should therefore exercise caution when considering nonstandard injectable combinations in the periorbital region.

Indications

VIPLIT is primarily intended for mild-to-moderate periorbital aging with attenuation of lateral support, early contour discontinuity, pretarsal flattening, or lid–cheek disharmony. It is not intended to replace surgery in patients with marked brow ptosis, severe dermatochalasis, major brow–orbital rim dissociation, or advanced structural aging. Because the illustrative case is based on an Asian patient, extrapolation to non-Asian patients should be individualized according to orbital shape, soft tissue characteristics, and aesthetic preference.

Illustrative Case

In the presented case, VIPLIT was applied as a staged, multinodal treatment with deep anchoring followed by superficial refinement. Immediate postprocedural photographs suggested modest improvement in lateral support, contour continuity, and overall visual integration of the periorbital unit, without obvious impairment of eyelid motion (Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

VIPLIT is not intended to introduce novel anatomy or novel injection points; rather, it organizes familiar anatomical targets into a structured and reproducible treatment framework. It separates treatment into deep support and superficial refinement, offering a practical framework for a region that is often approached empirically.

This layered logic is consistent with emerging periocular filler literature suggesting that different materials and tissue planes may serve complementary roles. Nevertheless, prospective comparative studies remain necessary. Clinical studies of collagen–HA treatment support the rationale for distinguishing superficial refinement from deeper structural support, although such evidence does not fully validate empirically mixed formulations.

This report is limited by its single-case design, lack of a comparator group, absence of validated outcome measures, and short follow-up. Accordingly, the observed effect should be interpreted as descriptive rather than confirmatory.

CONCLUSIONS

VIPLIT is a structured, multinodal, layered framework for periorbital rejuvenation that organizes known support and transition zones into a reproducible treatment sequence. Its purpose is to improve conceptual clarity and procedural teachability rather than to propose new anatomy. Future prospective studies are warranted to evaluate the reproducibility, safety, and clinical utility of this framework.

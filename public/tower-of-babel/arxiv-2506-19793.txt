Multiplicity results for mixed local-nonlocal variable exponent problem involving singular and superlinear term
Shammi Malhotra, Ambesh Kumar Pandey, K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In this paper, we study a class of quasilinear elliptic equations involving both local and nonlocal operators with variable exponents. The problem exhibits singular nonlinearities along with a subcritical superlinear growth term and a parameter λ \lambda . We study the existence of multiple solutions with the help of variational methods by restricting the associated energy functional on appropriate subsets of the Nehari manifold. Using the topological index and the structure of the fibering maps, we analyse a key splitting property of the associated Nehari manifold. This decomposition allows us to establish the existence of two distinct solutions. Additionally, we establish the L ∞ L^{\infty} -bound for the solutions. Keywords: Mixed local nonlocal operator; Variable exponents; Nehari manifold; Fibering map; Singularity; Degree theory; Regularity Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J60, 35J50, 35J75, 55M25, 35R11, 35J92

To handle certain maps involving variable exponent powers, it is useful to approximate them by functions with constant exponents. For this purpose, we make use of the following notion of topological degree, as developed in
[

Definition 2.3
.

The degree of a function is a function

\displaystyle\deg:\Big\{(f,\Omega,y):\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ open and bounded},\ f:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{continuous},y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus f(\partial\Omega)\Big\}\to\mathbb{Z}

that satisfies the following properties

(d1)

Identity: For the identity map
id:\Omega\to\Omega
, we have

\deg(\text{id},\Omega,y)=1\quad\text{for }y\in\Omega.

(d2)

Additivity:

\deg(f,\Omega,y)=\deg(f,\Omega_{1},y)+\deg(f,\Omega_{2},y)

whenever
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
are disjoint open subsets of
\Omega
such that
y\notin f\left(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})\right).

(d3)

Homotopy Invariance:

\deg(h(t,\cdot),\Omega,y(t))\text{ is independent of }t\in[0,1]

whenever
h:[0,1]\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous,
y:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous, and the function

y(t)\notin h(t,\partial\Omega)
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Note that there is a unique function that satisfies the aforementioned properties. The homotopy invariance properties are the most crucial property that leads to the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
with
y\not\in f(\partial\Omega)
and
d=dist(y,f(\partial\Omega))
. If
g_{1},g_{2}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\lVert g_{i}-f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<d
for
i=1,2
; then
\deg(g_{1},\Omega,y)=\deg(g_{2},\Omega,y)
.

The above lemma follows from
[
20
, Proposition
2.4
]
.

3
Nehari Manifold and Fiber Map Analysis

The energy functional
J_{\lambda}:\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\real
associated with problem (

Q
+

λ

) is given by:

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}

\displaystyle+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{1}{p(x,y)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{a(x)}{1-\gamma(x)}(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{b(x)}{r(x)}(u_{+})^{r(x)}.

(3.6)

It is important to note that the functional
J_{\lambda}
is well defined; however, it is not differentiable due to the presence of the singular term.

For
u\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
, we define the fibering map
\phi_{u}(t):\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{t^{p(x)}}{p(x)}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\,dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{t^{p(x,y)}}{p(x,y)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy

(3.7)

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{t^{1-\gamma(x)}}{1-\gamma(x)}a(x)(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{t^{r(x)}}{r(x)}b(x)(u_{+})^{r(x)}\,dx.

The first derivative of the fibering map is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}t^{p(x)-1}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\,dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}t^{p(x,y)-1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}t^{-\gamma(x)}a(x)(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}t^{r(x)-1}b(x)(u_{+})^{r(x)}\,dx.

The second derivative is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(p(x)-1)t^{p(x)-2}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\,dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(p(x,y)-1)t^{p(x,y)-2}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)a(x)t^{-\gamma(x)-1}(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(r(x)-1)b(x)t^{r(x)-2}(u_{+})^{r(x)}\,dx.

We note that the energy functional
J_{\lambda}
is not bounded below on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. To address this difficulty, we restrict our attention to the following subset of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, known as the Nehari manifold, defined by

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{X}_{0}\setminus\{0\}\;:\phi_{u}^{\prime}(1)=0\right\}.

(3.10)

Thus,
tu\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
if and only if
\phi^{\prime}_{u}(t)=0.
In particular,
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
if and only if
\phi_{u}^{\prime}(1)=0.

For
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
, from (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime}(1)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy

(3.11)

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}b(x)(u_{+})^{r(x)}\,dx

and

\displaystyle\phi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(1)=\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)}

\displaystyle dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}p(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy

(3.12)

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(1-\gamma(x))a(x)(u_{+})^{1-\gamma(x)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}r(x)b(x)(u_{+})^{r(x)}\,dx.

We note that

+

λ

\displaystyle:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}\;:\;\phi^{\prime\prime}_{u}(1)=0\right\}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}

. To prove some important properties of these functions, we approximate them using tools from degree theory. In this direction, we state the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
u\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
, let us define the function
f:(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\to\mathbb{R}
as

f(t)=\iint_{\textit{Q}}t^{p(x,y)}h(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

where

h(x,y):=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}>0.

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a disjoint collection
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}^{\prime}
of
Q
and a corresponding set of exponents
\{p_{i}\}
such that, if we define the function
g:(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\to\mathbb{R}
by

g(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t^{p_{i}}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx.

Then the following holds:

1.

\lVert f(t)-g(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})}<\varepsilon,

2.

\deg(f,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2}),0)=\deg(g,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2}),0)=0
,

where
\deg
denotes the degree as defined in Definition
2.3
.

Proof.

Define the set

A:=(\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q})\cap\textit{Q}.

Let
(x_{0},y_{0})\in A
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, from the continuity of the map
t^{p(x,y)}
on the set
[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]\times\textit{Q}
, and the compactness of
[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]
, there exist a radius
r>0
, a neighbourhood
B_{r}(x_{0},y_{0})
, and a point
p_{0}\in[p^{-},p^{+}]
such that

\left|t^{p(x,y)}-t^{p_{0}}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{M}\quad\text{ for all }(x,y)\in B_{r}(x_{0},y_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\times\Omega,\quad\text{ for all }t\in[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}],

(3.15)

where
M:=\displaystyle\iint_{\textit{Q}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx
.
Now consider the collection

\mathcal{B}:=\bigcup_{(x_{i},y_{i})\in A}B_{r_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i}).

Since
\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}
is countable, we can enumerate the elements of
A
and write the following disjoint union

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}^{\prime},

where
B_{1}^{\prime}=B_{1}
and
B_{i}^{\prime}=B_{r_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}B_{r_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})\subset B_{r_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})
such that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}
. In addition, the set

\textit{Q}\setminus\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\text{set of isolated points}\,(\text{denoted by }\mathcal{C}),

and since measure of the set
\mathcal{C}=0
, instead of working on
Q
, we can equivalently work with
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.
Now, consider the function

g(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t^{p_{i}}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx.

Then, we estimate the difference with the help of monotone convergence theorem and equation (
3.15
), we deduce

\displaystyle\left|f(t)-g(t)\right|=~

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\textit{Q}}t^{p(x,y)}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx-g(t)\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}t^{p(x,y)}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx-g(t)\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}t^{p(x,y)}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t^{p_{i}}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\left|t^{p(x,y)}-t^{p_{i}}\right|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{M}\iint_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx=\varepsilon

since
\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}h(x,y)\,dy\,dx=M
.

Therefore, by Lemma

\deg\left(f,[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}],0\right)=\deg\left(g,[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}],0\right)=0.\qed

For

Lemma 3.3

Let

where

and

are some given functions and

and

is strictly increasing, based on the assumptions on

Now, define two auxiliary functions

(ii)

(iii)

(i)

(ii)

Using Lemma

\displaystyle Q(u)

(iii)

then there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a continuous function

such that

where

Proof.

We consider the case
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+};
the case
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
follows by a similar argument. Let
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}
, we define the function

by

\displaystyle F(v,t)=\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle t^{p(x)-1}|\nabla(u+v)|^{p(x)}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}t^{p(x,y)-1}\frac{|(u+v)(x)-(u+v)(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}t^{-\gamma(x)}a(x)(u+v)_{+}^{1-\gamma(x)}-\int_{\Omega}t^{r(x)-1}b(x)(u+v)_{+}^{r(x)}.

Since
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
, we get

\displaystyle F(0,1)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{+}^{1-\gamma(x)}-\int_{\Omega}b(x)u_{+}^{r(x)}=0

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(0,1)=\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle(p(x)-1)|\nabla u|^{p(x)}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(p(x,y)-1))\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)a(x)u_{+}^{1-\gamma(x)}-\int_{\Omega}(r(x)-1)b(x)u_{+}^{r(x)}>0.

With the application of implicit function theorem at

there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a continuous function

such that

is a unique solution of

From

, we get

and from

we have

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle f(v)^{p(x)-1}|\nabla(u+v)|^{p(x)}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}f(v)^{p(x,y)-1}\frac{|(u+v)(x)-(u+v)(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}

which implies that

Similarly, using

and taking

This completes the proof of the Lemma

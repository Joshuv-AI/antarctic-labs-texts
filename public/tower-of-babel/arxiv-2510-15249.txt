The Wiener Criterion at ∞ \infty for Degenerate Elliptic Equations
Ugur G. Abdulla, Denis Brazke

Abstract
. This paper establishes a Wiener criterion at ∞ \infty to characterise the unique solvability of the Dirichlet problem for degenerate elliptic equations with power-like weights in arbitrary open sets. In the measure-theoretical context, the criterion determines whether the 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -harmonic measure of ∞ \infty is null or positive. From the topological point of view, it presents a test for the thinness of the exterior set at ∞ \infty in the 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -fine topology.



1.
Introduction and Main Result

In this paper, we prove a criterion for the unique solvability of the Dirichlet problem in arbitrary open sets
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
for the degenerate elliptic equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{A}u\coloneqq\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)&=0&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\[6.0pt]
u&=f&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f\colon\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is a bounded Borel measurable function, and the coefficient matrix
A\colon\mathbbm{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}^{n\times n}
is measurable, symmetric and satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\,|x|^{\gamma}\,|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot A(x)\xi\leq\lambda\,|x|^{\gamma}\,|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for all }x,\xi\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}},

for some
\lambda\geq 1
,
\gamma>2-n
. We characterise the existence and uniqueness of a bounded solution to (
1
) in terms of the Wiener criterion for the regularity of the boundary point at infinity (
\infty
). On one hand, it is a generalisation of the Wiener test for the regularity of
\infty
for harmonic functions
[
Abd07
,
Abd12
]
. On the other hand, it is the counterpart at
\infty
of the Wiener test for the boundary regularity of solutions to degenerate elliptic equations
[
FJK82
]
.

In order to formulate our main result, we first introduce some terminology. Consider the one point compactification
\overline{{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}}\coloneqq{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
be open and denote by
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\subset\overline{{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}}
the compactification of
\Omega
. We make the convention that
\infty\in\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}
for all unbounded open sets
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
. The Radon measure canonically associated with the weight
|\ \cdot\ |^{\gamma}
is denoted by
\mu_{\gamma}
:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma}(E)\coloneqq\int_{E}|x|^{\gamma}\,\textrm{d}x,

i.e.
\mathrm{d}\mu=|x|^{\gamma}\,\textrm{d}x
, where
\mathrm{d}x
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. We say that
u\in W^{1,2}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mu_{\gamma})
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic if
\mathcal{A}u=0
in
\Omega
in the weak sense, i.e.

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot A(x)\nabla u(x)\,\textrm{d}x=0

\displaystyle\text{for all }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

We say that
v\colon\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic, if

\bullet

v
is finite valued in a dense set of
\Omega
.

\bullet

v
is lower semi-continuous.

\bullet

For every open and bounded set
U\subset\Omega
and for every
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic function
u\in W_{\textrm{loc}}^{1,2}(U,\mu_{\gamma})\cap C^{0}(\overline{U})
, the inequality
v\geq h
on
\partial U
implies
v\geq h
in
U
.

We say that
w\colon\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\mathcal{A}
-subharmonic if
-w
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic. Throughout this article, by a solution of (
1
) we mean a Perron–Wiener–Brelot solution (PWB solution for short). Assuming that boundary function
f\in C^{0}(\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega})
, the upper PWB-solution
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{\Omega}
(resp. lower PWB-solution
\underline{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{\Omega}
) is defined as

(1.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{f}(x)\coloneqq\inf\{v(x)\}\quad(\text{resp. }\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}(x)\coloneqq\sup\{w(x)\}),

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega,

where the infimum (resp. supremum) is taken over all
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic functions (resp.
\mathcal{A}
-subharmonic functions) such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in\Omega\end{subarray}}v(y)\geq f(x)\quad\big(\text{resp. }\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in\Omega\end{subarray}}v(y)\leq f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}

We say that a boundary function
f\colon\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-resolutive, if

(1.8)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{f}\equiv\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{f}\eqqcolon\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega},

and the latter is called a generalised solution (or PWB solution) of (
1
). Perron’s method and its Wiener and Brelot refinements imply that continuous boundary functions, and therefore bounded Borel measurable functions, form a subclass of resolutive boundary functions
[
Doo84
]
(see Section
2
).

A generalised solution is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
\Omega
and unique by construction. However, it is accomplished by prescribing
the behaviour of the solution at
\infty
, i.e. by enforcing (
1.7
) at the boundary point
\infty
. The theory, while identifying a class of unique solvability, leaves the following question open:
would a unique bounded solution exist without prescribing its behaviour at
\infty
?
The principal goal of this paper is to answer this question.
Furthermore, without loss of generality, we assume that the open set
\Omega
has at least one unbounded connected component.
It is evident that, without this assumption, within each bounded component, the Dirichlet problem is uniquely solvable. This guarantees the unique solvability of (
1
) in the entire open set
\Omega
without prescribing the solution at
\infty
. In fact, for any open set
\Omega
satisfying this condition, without prescribing the behaviour of the solution at
\infty
, there exists one and only one, or infinitely many solutions (see Proposition
3.1
).

By fixing any real number
\overline{f}\in\mathbbm{R}
, we can assign
f(\infty)=\overline{f}
and construct a unique generalised solution
\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega}
. The major problem is now the following:
How many bounded solutions do we actually have, or does the constructed solution depend on
\overline{f}
?.

To formulate the corresponding measure-theoretical problem, we recall the concept of
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure of Borel subsets of
\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}
. For a given Borel set
B\subset\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}
, denote as
\mathbf{1}_{B}
the indicator function of
B
. The
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure of
\infty
is defined as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}(\ \cdot\ ,\infty)=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}^{\Omega}(\ \cdot\ ).

We say that
\infty
is an
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure null set, if
\mu_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}(\ \cdot\ ,\infty)\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. Otherwise,
\infty
is a set of positive
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure. The measure-theoretical counterpart of the major problem is to characterise geometrically whether
\infty

is of null or positive
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure
.

To characterise the uniqueness, we employ the concept of regularity of
\infty
introduced in
[
Abd07
,
Abd08
,
Abd12
]
.

Definition 1.1

\infty
is said to be regular (resp. irregular) for
\Omega
if it is an
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic measure null (resp. positive) set.

The concept of regularity of
\infty
is fundamentally different from the classical definition of regularity of finite boundary points. Nevertheless, it provides the key insight to characterise the regularity of solutions at
\infty
. In Proposition
3.2
below, we prove that an equivalent definition of the regularity of
\infty
may be given depending on whether the following condition is satisfied:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}f(x)\leq\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega}(x)\leq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega}(x)\leq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}f(x).

for every bounded Borel measurable boundary function
f
and for every solution
\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega}
with arbitrary prescribed boundary condition
f(\infty)=\overline{f}
. Note that if
f|_{\partial\Omega}
has a limit at
\infty
, (
1.10
) means that the solution
\mathcal{H}_{f}^{\Omega}
is continuous at
\infty
by taking the limit value of
f|_{\partial\Omega}
without being specified.

To formulate the corresponding topological problem, recall that the
\mathcal{A}
-fine topology is defined as the coarsest topology such that every
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function in
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
is continuous. Given
E\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
, we define
\overline{E}^{\mathcal{A}}
as the closure of
E
in the
\mathcal{A}
-fine topology.

Definition 1.2

[
Abd12
]

Let
E\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
. We say that
E
is
\mathcal{A}
-thin at
\infty
if
E
satisfies one of the following two conditions:

(1)

E
is bounded.

(2)

E
is unbounded and there exists an
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function
u\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\end{subarray}}u(x)<\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in E\end{subarray}}u(x)

If
E
is not
\mathcal{A}
-thin at
\infty
, we say that
E
is
\mathcal{A}
-thick at
\infty
.

Our principal problem is to derive a ”geometric” characterisation of the regularity of
\infty
and the uniqueness of the solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1
). The precise characterisation depends on the sparseness of
\Omega
at
\infty
or, equivalently, on the fine topological thinness of the exterior set at
\infty
. To measure the critical thinness, we recall the concept of
\mathcal{A}
-capacity: let
B_{R}\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}:|x|<R\}
be a ball and
K\subset B_{R}
be compact. The
\mathcal{A}
-capacity of
K
relative to
B_{R}
is defined as

(1.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}(K,B_{R})\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\int_{B_{R}}\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot A(x)\nabla\varphi(x)\,\textrm{d}x:\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R}),\ \varphi\geq 1\text{ in }K\bigg\}.

Our main theorem reads:

Theorem 1.3

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\infty
is regular (resp. irregular) for
\Omega
.

(2)

There exist(s) a unique (resp. infinitely many) bounded solution(s) of (
1
).

(3)

\Omega^{c}
is
\mathcal{A}
-thick (resp.
\mathcal{A}
-thin) at
\infty
.

(4)

The Wiener integral

(1.13)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2t})}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t},B_{2t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

diverges (resp. converges).

(5)

The Wiener sum

(1.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\,\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})

is divergent (resp. convergent), where
E_{k}\coloneqq\Omega^{c}\cap\{2^{k-1}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k}\}
.

We note that the Wiener test given in
(5)
coincides with the Wiener test in
[
Abd07
,
Abd12
]
in the case
\gamma=0
and
n\geq 3
.

1.1.
Historical remarks

It is appropriate to make some remarks concerning the well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem for elliptic PDEs in arbitrary open sets. The strategy for solving the classical Dirichlet problem for harmonic functions in arbitrary bounded open sets may well be expressed by the citation from Wiener’s celebrated paper. As pointed out by Lebesgue and independently by Wiener
[
Wie24b
]
,
”the Dirichlet problem divides itself into two parts, the first of which is the determination of harmonic functions corresponding to certain boundary condition, while the second is the investigation of the behaviour of this function in the neighbourhood of the boundary”.
The existence of a unique generalised solution of the Dirichlet problem in arbitrary bounded open sets
\Omega
with prescribed boundary values on
\partial\Omega
is realized within the class of
resolutive boundary functions
, identified by Perron’s method and its Wiener
[
Wie24a
,
Wie24b
]
and Brelot
[
Bre71
]
refinements, referred to as the PWB method. Continuous boundary functions are a subclass of resolutive boundary functions, and a finite boundary point is
regular
if the corresponding PWB solution takes the boundary value continuously.

The regularity of a finite boundary point
x_{o}\in\partial\Omega
is a problem of local nature as it depends on the measure-geometric properties of the boundary in the neighbourhood of
x_{o}
. It is a standard textbook example to demonstrate that an isolated boundary point is always irregular. On the other hand, any simply connected bounded open plane region is a regular set, i.e. all the boundary points are regular. Lebesgue constructed an example of a simply connected domain in
\mathbbm{R}^{3}
, which is now called a Lebesgue spine, with an irregular boundary point on a sufficiently sharp edge protruding into the interior of the region
[
Leb12
]
. Wiener, in his seminal works
[
Wie24a
,
Wie24b
]
, proved a necessary and sufficient condition for the finite boundary point
x_{o}\in\partial\Omega
to be regular in terms of the ”thinness” of the complementary set in the neighbourhood of
x_{o}
. A key advance made in Wiener’s work was the introduction of the concept of capacity, a sub-additive set function dictated by the Laplace operator. It accurately measures the thinness of the complementary set in the neighbourhood of
x_{o}
, and determines the regularity of the boundary point for harmonic functions. Formalised through the powerful Choquet capacitability theorem
[
Cho54
]
, this concept became a standard tool in potential theory to characterise boundary regularity. The Wiener criterion for the boundary continuity of harmonic functions became a canonical result, driving the boundary regularity theory for elliptic and parabolic PDEs. In
[
LSW63
]
, it is proved that the Wiener criterion for the regularity of finite boundary points concerning a second-order divergence form uniformly elliptic operator with bounded measurable coefficients coincides with the classical Wiener criterion for the boundary regularity of harmonic functions. The Wiener criterion for the regularity of finite boundary points for the linear degenerate elliptic equations is proved in
[
FJK82
]
. Wiener criterion for the regularity of finite boundary points for quasilinear elliptic equations was settled due to
[
Maz70
,
GZ77
,
LM85
,
KM94
]
.

To solve the Dirichlet problem in an unbounded open set, Brelot introduced the idea of compactifying
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
to
\overline{{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}}\coloneqq{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\cup\{\infty\}
, where
\infty
is the point at infinity of
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}

[
Bre44
]
. The PWB method is extended to the compactified framework, and provides a powerful existence and uniqueness result for (
1
) in arbitrary open sets in the class of resolutive boundary functions. The new concept of regularity of
\infty
was introduced in
[
Abd07
]
for the classical Dirichlet problem, and in
[
Abd08
]
for its parabolic counterpart. The Dirichlet problem with bounded Borel measurable boundary functions has one, and only one, or infinitely many solutions without prescribing the boundary value at
\infty
. The point at
\infty
is called regular if there is a unique solution and irregular otherwise. Equivalently, in the measure-theoretical context, the new concept of regularity resp. irregularity of
\infty
is introduced according to whether the harmonic measure of
\infty
is null resp. positive. In
[
Abd07
]
, the Wiener criterion for the regularity of
\infty
for the classical Dirichlet problem in an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
with
N\geq 3
is proved. In
[
Abd12
]
, it is proved that the Wiener criterion at
\infty
for the linear second-order divergence form uniformly elliptic PDEs with bounded measurable coefficients coincides with the Wiener criterion at
\infty
for the Laplace operator. The Wiener criterion at
\infty
for the heat equation was proved in
[
Abd08
]
. The main goal of this paper is to extend the results of
[
Abd07
,
Abd12
]
to the case of degenerate elliptic PDEs with power-like weights
|x|^{\gamma}
,
\gamma>2-n
. On one hand, Theorem
1.3
is in direct analogy to the Wiener criterion at
\infty
in the unweighted case
[
Abd12
]
. On the other hand, Theorem
1.3
is the counterpart at
\infty
of the Wiener criterion for the regularity of finite boundary points for weighted elliptic PDEs with weight
w
being either in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{2}
, or
w=|\det(\mathrm{D}f)|^{1-\frac{2}{n}}
, where
f:{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
is a global quasi-conformal map
[
FJK82
]
. This includes power-like weights
|x|^{\gamma}
,
\gamma>-n
. In
[
FJK82
]
it is proved that a boundary point at the origin
0\in\partial\Omega
is regular if and only if

(1.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0^{+}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t},B_{2t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}<+\infty\qquad\textbf{or}\qquad\int_{0^{+}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2t})}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t},B_{2t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}=+\infty.

In the special case
w(x)=|x|^{\gamma}
,
\gamma>-n
, it implies that
0\in\partial\Omega
is always regular if
\gamma<2-n
, but if
\gamma\geq 2-n
then it is regular if and only if the second condition of (
1.15
) is satisfied. The Wiener criterion at
\infty
expressed in (
1.13
) is the analogue of the Wiener criterion at the finite boundary point expressed in the second integral in (
1.15
).

However, Theorem
1.3
leaves an open problem on the optimality of the restriction
\gamma>2-n
for the validity of the Wiener criterion for the regularity of
\infty
. From
[
Abd18
]
it follows that in the special case
n=2
, the restriction
\gamma>0
is optimal. The limit case
\gamma=0
corresponds to the case of (
1
) for the second-order divergence form uniformly elliptic PDE with bounded measurable coefficients. In this case,
\infty
is always regular for any unbounded Greenian open set of
\mathbbm{R}^{2}
, that is to say the Dirichlet problem always has a unique bounded solution without specifying the behaviour at
\infty
. Remarkably, in this case, the Wiener criterion characterises the removability of the fundamental singularity. More precisely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbbm{R}^{2}
be a Greenian open set, and let
x_{o}
be a boundary point (finite or
\infty
). Consider the singular Dirichlet problem for a linear divergence form uniformly elliptic operator with bounded measurable coefficients in the class
O(\log|x-x_{o}|)
if
x_{o}
is finite, and in the class of functions with logarithmic growth if
x_{o}=\infty
. In
[
Abd18
]
, it is proved that the Wiener criterion at
x_{o}
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the unique solvability of the singular Dirichlet problem, and equivalently for the removability of the logarithmic singularity. Precisely, in
[
Abd18
]
, the concept of log-regularity resp. log-irregularity of the boundary point (finite or
\infty
) is introduced according to if its log-harmonic measure is null resp. positive, and the removability of the logarithmic singularity is expressed in terms of the Wiener criterion for the log-regularity of
x_{o}
. We address the problem of characterising the regularity of
\infty
for arbitrary unbounded open sets in the (
1
) for the weight parameter
\gamma
in the range
-n<\gamma\leq 2-n
, as well as the problem of characterising singularities for degenerate elliptic PDEs in a subsequent work.

2.
Preliminaries

Given
\gamma>-n
, we define the measure
\mu_{\gamma}
as in (
1.4
). Since the function
x\longmapsto|x|^{\gamma}
is
2
-admissible (in the sense of
[
HKM06
, Chapter 1]
), the measure
\mu_{\gamma}
is doubling and
\mu_{\gamma}
and the Lebesgue measure are mutually absolutely continuous (see
[
HKM06
, Example 1.6 and Chapter 15]
). A direct computation shows that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma}(B_{r}(0))=\sigma_{n,\gamma}r^{n+\gamma}

\displaystyle\text{for all }r>0,

where
\sigma_{n,\gamma}\coloneqq\frac{n}{n+\gamma}\mu_{0}(B_{1}(0))
. From this computation we obtain for
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
and
r>|x|
the following rough estimate

(2.2)

\displaystyle(r-|x|)^{n+\gamma}\lesssim\mu_{\gamma}(B_{r}(x))\lesssim(r+|x|)^{n+\gamma}.

Recall that
A\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}^{n\times n}
is a measurable and symmetric coefficient matrix that satisfies the ellipticity condition (
1.3
) for some
\gamma>2-n
. We remark that the constant
\gamma
is not covering the full range of parameters that make the weight
\mu_{\gamma}
2-admissible (in the sense of
[
HKM06
, Chapter 1]
). The main reason for this restriction is the existence and decay of full space potentials of the weighted equation
\mathcal{A}u\coloneqq\textrm{div}(A\nabla u)=0
(see Section
2.2
), which can only be guaranteed if
\gamma>2-n
.

We define the upper resp. lower PWB solution as in the introduction. Both functions are
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic (see
[
HKM06
, Theorem 9.2]
). Using the comparison principle, we find that
\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}
for every boundary function
g
. Moreover, both functions are order-preserving, i.e.

(2.3)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}\leq\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{\tilde{g}}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{\tilde{g}}

for every bounded Borel measurable functions
g,\tilde{g}\colon\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
such that
g\leq\tilde{g}
.

We say that a boundary function
g\colon\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-resolutive, if
\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}=\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\Omega}_{g}
and are not constant
\pm\infty
. It is well known that every continuous boundary function, and therefore also every bounded Borel measurable function
g\colon\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-resolutive due to the linearity of the equation. For the reader’s convenience, we provide a sketch of the proof of this fact:

Lemma 2.1

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
be an
\mathcal{A}
-Greenian open set, i.e. it possesses a non-trivial and non-negative
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function. Then every bounded and Borel measurable function
f\colon\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-resolutive.

Proof:
The PWB solution is stable under monotone convergence, see
[
Doo84
, Section VIII.6 (e)]
. The result is only stated for the Laplace equation, but it is also true for weighted elliptic equations, as the argument carries over to that case almost verbatim. Using the resolutivity of continuous functions
[
HKM06
, Theorem 9.25]
and aforementioned argument of
[
Doo84
, Section VIII.6 (e)]
, we find that indicator functions of Borel measurable subsets of
\partial\boldsymbol{\Omega}
are resolutive. Using the fact that the space of
\mathcal{A}
-resolutive functions is a vector space, including
[
Doo84
, Section VIII.6 (e)]
indicator functions of Borel measurable boundary subsets, the claim follows.

2.1.
Preliminaries in Potential Theory

Let
B\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
be a ball and
K\subset B
be compact. We define
\operatorname{cap}(K,B)
as in (
1.12
). For arbitrary sets
E\subset B
, we define

(2.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}(E,B)\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\subset U\subset B\\
U\text{ open}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subset U\\
K\text{ compact}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{cap}(K,B).

The set function
E\longmapsto\operatorname{cap}(E,B)
is a Choquet capacity (see
[
HKM06
, Theorem 2.5]
). We say that a property holds quasi-everywhere if it holds up to a set of capacity
0
(see
[
HKM06
, Section 2.7]
). We mention that the set of irregular finite boundary points of any open set is a set of capacity
0
(see
[
HKM06
, Theorem 8.10]
)

Lemma 2.2

(1)

\operatorname{cap}(E,B)\leq\operatorname{cap}(E^{\prime},B)
for all
E\subset E^{\prime}\subset B
.

(2)

\operatorname{cap}(E,B)\leq\operatorname{cap}(E,B^{\prime})
for all
E\subset B^{\prime}\subset B
.

(3)

\operatorname{cap}(B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))=\operatorname{cap}(\overline{B}_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))
.

(4)

\mu_{\gamma}(B_{r}(x))\approx r^{2}\operatorname{cap}(B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))
for all
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
,
r>0
.

(5)

\operatorname{cap}(E,B_{2t}(x))\approx\operatorname{cap}(E,B_{2s}(x))
for all
0<t<s\leq 2t
,
E\subset B_{t}(x)
.

The proofs can be found in
[
HKM06
, Chapter 2]
.

It is well known that there exists a unique minimiser
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
of the functional minimised in (
1.12
), which we call the
\mathcal{A}
-capacitary potential of
K
in
B
. Moreover,
0\leq\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]\leq 1
almost everywhere,
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
B\setminus K
and
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
B
. Without loss of generality we always choose the
L^{1}
-representative of
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B](x)=\Essliminf_{y\longrightarrow x}\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B](y).

We note that
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B]|_{B\setminus K}=\mathcal{H}^{B\setminus K}_{f}
where
f\coloneqq\varphi|_{\partial(B\setminus K)}
, and where
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
such that
\varphi=1
in an open neighbourhood of
K
in
B
(see
[
HKM06
, Corollary 9.29]
). For more details, see
[
HKM06
, Section 6.16]
(see also
[
FJK82
, Theorem 1.20]
).

Let
\nu
be a Radon measure on
B
. We say that
u\in L^{1}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
is a weak solution to
\mathcal{A}u=\nu
vanishing on
\partial B
if

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B}u(x)\,\Phi(x)\,|x|^{\gamma}\,\textrm{d}x=\int_{B}\mathcal{G}[\Phi\,|\cdot|^{\gamma}]\,\textrm{d}\nu

for all
\Phi\in L^{\infty}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
, where
\mathcal{G}\colon W^{-1,2}(B,\mu_{\gamma})\longrightarrow W_{0}^{1,2}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
is the Greens operator defined on the dual space
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(B,\mu_{\gamma})\coloneqq[W_{0}^{1,p}(B,\mu_{\gamma})]^{\prime}
with
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Note that
\Phi|\cdot|^{\gamma}\in W^{-1,p}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
for all
p>1
, from which we obtain that
\mathcal{G}[\Phi w]\in C^{0}(\overline{B})
. It is well known that a unique weak solution
u\in L^{1}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
exists. Moreover,
u\geq 0
almost everywhere and there exists
p_{0}<2n
such that
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
for all
1\leq p<p_{0}
. In particular
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
if
\nu\in W^{-1,2}(B,\mu_{\gamma})
. See
[
FJK82
, Chapter 2]
for more details.

With the notion of weak solutions, we define the Greens function
g\colon(B\times B)\setminus\operatorname{diag}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
as the unique weak solution of the equation
\mathcal{A}g(\ \cdot\ ,y)=\delta_{y}
vanishing on
\partial B
for all
y\in B
. We note that
g(\ \cdot\ ,y)
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
B\setminus\{y\}
,
g\geq 0
and
g(\ \cdot\ ,y)\in W^{1,2}(B\setminus B_{r}(y),\mu_{\gamma})
for all

. In addition, the Greens function

is symmetric (see
[
FJK82
, Proposition 2.8]
) and is estimated via (see Lemma
2.2

(3)
)

(2.7)

\displaystyle g(x,y)\approx\int_{|x-y|}^{R}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x)),B_{2t}(x))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\text{for all }x,y\in\tfrac{1}{4}B.

where
R>0
is the radius of
B
(see
[
FJK82
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Given a Radon measure
\nu
with compact support in

, the function

defined via

\displaystyle u(x)\coloneqq\int_{B}g(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\nu(y)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in B

\mathcal{A}u=\nu
vanishing on

[

2.2.
Potential Theory in the whole space

The preceding section discussed potential theory for functions defined on a ball
B\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
. In this section, we want to use these preliminaries to extend the results to the whole space setting. A simple calculation using Lemma
2.2
shows that

(2.9)

\displaystyle|x-y|^{2-(n+\gamma)}\approx\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x),B_{2t}(x))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\text{for all }|x|>4|y|.

With this estimate at hand, we can define whole space potentials. More precisely, given a compact set
K\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
, we construct
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
as the limit of the increasing sequence of
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B_{R}]
as
R\to\infty
. Since
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B_{R}]\leq 1
for all
R>0
, again the function
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\setminus K
by the Harnack convergence theorem, and
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
. In addition, we obtain a capacitary upper bound on the potential
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]
in the following way. Let
K\subset B_{r}
, let
G_{R}\colon B_{R}\times B_{R}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
be the Greens function on
B_{R}
and let
\nu_{R}
be the capacitary potential of
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,B_{R}]
. From the Greens representation formula (
2.8
) we find for
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\setminus B_{2r}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}](x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial K}G_{R}(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\nu_{R}(y)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\liminf_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial K}\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x),B_{2t}(x))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\textrm{d}\nu_{R}(y)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cap}(K,B_{2r})\,\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x),B_{2t}(x))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

In particular,
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[K,{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}](x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. Moreover, every open set of
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
is
\mathcal{A}
-Greenian since full space potentials provide a non-trivial and non-negative
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function.

In contrast to the unweighted case
\gamma=0
, a similar lower bound (in terms of capacity) is generally unavailable in the whole space setting due to a lack of a weighted Sobolev inequality. However, it follows from the weighted Sobolev inequality proved in
[
CRO13
]
that a suitable capacitary lower bound is available in the case
\gamma>0
. As this fact is not required for our purposes, we omit the details.

Similar to the unweighted case (
[
Abd07
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
Abd12
, page 3391]
), in the following lemma we use full space potentials to show that we are always able to construct a generalised solution of (
1
) such that (
1.10
) is satisfied.

Lemma 2.3

Let
f\colon\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
be a bounded Borel measurable function. Let
\overline{f}\in\mathbbm{R}
such that
f_{*}\leq\overline{f}\leq f^{*}
and extend
f(\infty)=\overline{f}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{f}
satisfies (
1.7
).

Proof:
We denote
u\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{f}
. Let
M\coloneqq\|f\|_{\sup}
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then
|u|\leq M
since the PWB-method is order-preserving and we find
R>0
such that

(2.13)

\displaystyle f_{*}-\varepsilon\leq f(y)\leq f^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\text{for all }y\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}.

We now define the function
w_{\pm}\colon\Omega\cap\overline{B}_{R}^{c}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
via

(2.14)

\displaystyle w_{+}\coloneqq f^{*}+\varepsilon+2M\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[B_{R},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}],

\displaystyle w_{-}\coloneqq f_{*}-\varepsilon-2M\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[B_{R},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}].

Since the PWB-method is order-preserving, we find that
w_{-}\leq u\leq w_{+}
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}
. In the limit
x\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
, we find that
f_{*}\leq u_{*}\leq u^{*}\leq f^{*}
, which was the claim.
\blacksquare

2.3.
Wiener Integral

We define the Wiener integral

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega)\coloneqq\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t}(x),B_{2t}(x))}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x),B_{2t}(x))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

for
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
open,
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
and
\delta>0
. We say that the Wiener integral converges if there exists
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
and
\delta>0
such that
\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega)<\infty
. Otherwise, we say that the Wiener integral diverges. In addition, a simple computation using Lemma
2.2
shows that convergence resp. divergence of the Wiener integral is independent of the centre points
x\in{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we always take
x=0
. Furthermore, again using Lemma
2.2
, convergence resp. divergence of the Wiener integral is equivalent to convergence resp. divergence of the Wiener integral when tested with open balls instead of closed balls. We observe that the Wiener integral is non-increasing:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega)

\displaystyle\text{for every }\delta>0,\ \Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\text{ open},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}(\delta^{\prime},\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega)

\displaystyle\text{for every }0<\delta<\delta^{\prime},\ \Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\text{ open}.

Moreover it follows from Lemma
2.2
that if there exists
\delta^{*}>0
such that
\mathfrak{W}(\delta^{*},\Omega)<\infty
, then
\mathfrak{W}(\delta,\Omega)<\infty
for all
\delta>0
. Similarly, this holds for divergence of the Wiener integral.

We note that the Wiener integral is equivalent to the Wiener sum tested with annuli instead of balls. This version of the Wiener sum is used throughout
[
Abd07
,
Abd12
]
(see also
[
Abd18
]
) and takes on a similar role in this article. More precisely, convergence resp. divergence of the Wiener integral is equivalent to convergence resp. divergence of

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}(m,\Omega)\coloneqq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap A_{k},B_{2^{k+1}}),

for some
m\in\mathbbm{N}
, where
A_{k}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{2^{k}}\setminus B_{2^{k-1}}
. To see this, we use Lemma
2.2
to find that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}(m,\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+1}})

(2.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+2}})

(2.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+2}})\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.23)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2t})}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t},B_{2t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}=\mathfrak{W}(2^{m},\Omega).

To show the reverse inequality, we use Lemma
2.2
to compute

(2.24)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}(2^{m},\Omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2^{m}}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2^{m}}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{4t})}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{4t})}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{t},B_{2^{k+1}})}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+1}})\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\frac{1}{t^{n+\gamma-2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+1}})\eqqcolon T.

Defining
K\coloneqq(2^{-(m+1)})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap B_{2^{(}m+1)},B_{2^{m+2}})
and using that
B_{2^{k}}\subset A_{k}\cup B_{2^{k-1}}
we obtain

(2.30)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=K+\sum_{k=m+2}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+1}})

(2.31)

\displaystyle\leq K+\sum_{k=m+2}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap A_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})

(2.32)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=m+2}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k-1}},B_{2^{k+1}})

(2.33)

\displaystyle=K+\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}(m+2,\Omega)+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-(k+1)})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+2}})

(2.34)

\displaystyle=K+\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}(m+2,\Omega)+2^{2-(n+\gamma)}\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+2}})

(2.35)

\displaystyle\leq K+\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}(m+2,\Omega)+2^{2-(n+\gamma)}\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{2^{k}},B_{2^{k+1}})

After absorption, we therefore find that

and therefore the claim. This establishes the equivalence

\Longleftrightarrow

in Theorem

We separate the proof of the main theorem into multiple propositions. The first proposition, similar to the case of uniformly elliptic operators (

[

Proposition 3.1

The following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

\infty
is regular (resp. irregular) for
\Omega
.

(2)

For every bounded Borel measurable function
f\colon\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
there exists a unique generalised solution of (
1
) (resp. there exist infinitely many generalised solutions of (
1
)).

Proof:
Assume that
\infty
is regular, i.e.
\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}=0
. Let
f\colon\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
be a bounded Borel measurable function and let

be generalised solutions of (

). Then
v\coloneqq u-\tilde{u}
is a generalised solution of (
1
) with trivial boundary data and thus there exists
M\in\mathbbm{R}
such that

by assumption. Therefore

.

We now assume that
\infty
is irregular, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}>0

\Omega

f\colon\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
be a bounded Borel measurable function and let
u\colon\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
be a generalised solution of (

). For every
M\in\mathbbm{R}
, the function
v_{M}\coloneqq u+M\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}
is also a generalised solution to (
1
). Since
v_{M}\neq v_{\tilde{M}}
for every
M\neq\tilde{M}
, the claim follows.
\blacksquare

The next proposition draws a connection between the well-posedness of (

)
(

, Lemma 2.1]
,
[

Proposition 3.2

The following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

For every bounded Borel measurable function

there exists a unique generalised solution of (

) (resp. there exist infinitely many generalised solutions of (

(2)

(

Assume that there exists a unique generalised solution of (

) is satisfied. Assume, on the contrary, that (

of (

Assume now that (

The following proposition connects the Wiener integral and the

-harmonic measure of

Proposition 3.3

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
be open such that there exists an unbounded connected component. Assume that the Wiener integral converges. Then
\infty
is irregular for
\Omega
.

Proof:
We show that
(\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}})^{*}=1

\mathfrak{W}^{\Sigma}
is finite (without loss of generality, we take the centre point to be the origin). Denote
B_{t}\coloneqq B_{t}(0)
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Since the Wiener sum converges, we find
m\in\mathbbm{N}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})<\varepsilon,

\displaystyle E_{k}\coloneqq\Omega^{c}\cap\{2^{k-1}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k}\}\subset\overline{B}_{2^{k}}.

Since
\Omega
has at least one unbounded connected component
\Omega_{e}\subseteq\Omega
, we can choose
m
sufficiently large such that there exists
x^{*}\in\Omega_{e}\cap B_{2^{m-4}}
. We note that once we have found
m
sufficiently large and
x^{*}\in\Omega\cap B_{2^{m-4}}
, the point
x^{*}
is independent of
m
in that for larger
m
we can still choose
x^{*}
as before. In particular, constants that depend on
|x^{*}|
do not depend on
m
.

The proof is decomposed into multiple steps. In the first step we construct an
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function
\vartheta\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
such that the liminf
\vartheta_{*}\lesssim\varepsilon
at
\infty
and
\vartheta\geq 1
quasi-everywhere on
\partial\Omega^{m}
, where in
\Omega^{m}\coloneqq\big(\bigcup_{k=m+1}^{\infty}E_{k}\big)^{c}
. We then modify
\vartheta\rightsquigarrow\varpi
in such a way that the liminf
\varpi_{*}\lesssim\varepsilon
and
\varpi\geq 1
everywhere on
\partial\Omega^{m}
. In fact
\varpi\geq 1
in
(\Omega^{m})^{c}
. Finally, having obtained these properties, the function
\varpi+\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[B_{2^{m}},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]
is in the upper class of the function
1-\mathbf{1}_{\infty}
, which in turn is used to bound the harmonic measure.

Step 1: Construction of potential.
Given
M\in\mathbbm{N}
such that
M>m
, we define
\vartheta_{M}\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
via

(3.2)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{M}\coloneqq\sum_{k=m+1}^{M}\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[E_{k},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}].

Then
\vartheta_{M}
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
\Omega^{m}
for all
M>m
. Moreover,
\{\vartheta_{M}\}_{M}
is an increasing sequence of non-negative
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic functions in
\Omega^{m}
. We claim that the pointwise limit function
\vartheta\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic function in
\Omega^{m}
. We note that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\vartheta(x)=\lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{k=m+1}^{M}\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[E_{k},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}](x)=\sup_{M>m}\vartheta_{M}(x)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\Omega^{m}.

Let
x^{*}\in\Omega\cap B_{2^{m-4}}
. Let
M-2>k>m
,
G_{M}(x^{*},\ \cdot\ )\in L^{1}(B_{2^{M}},\mu_{\gamma})
be the Greens function (with singularity in
x^{*}
) and let
\nu_{E_{k}}^{M}
be the capacitary distribution of
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[E_{k},B_{2^{M}}]
. We use the Greens representation formula (
2.8
), (
2.7
), Lemma
2.2
and (
2.9
) to obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[E_{k},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}](x^{*})

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}G_{M}(x^{*},y)\,\textrm{d}\nu_{E_{k}}^{M}(y)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\liminf_{M\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\int_{|x^{*}-y|}^{2^{M}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x^{*}),B_{2t}(x^{*}))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\textrm{d}\nu_{E_{k}}^{M}(y)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{M\to\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\int_{|x^{*}-y|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x^{*}),B_{2t}(x^{*}))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\textrm{d}\nu_{E_{k}}^{M}(y)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\int_{2^{k-1}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x^{*}),B_{2t}(x^{*}))}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\int_{2^{k-1}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{t}{{\mu_{\gamma}(B_{t}(x^{*}))}}\,\textrm{d}t

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\int_{2^{k-1}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(t-|x^{*}|)^{n+\gamma}}\,\textrm{d}t

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\bigg[\frac{2^{2-(n+\gamma)}}{n+\gamma-2}(2^{k-2}-|x^{*}|)^{2-(n+\gamma)}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\qquad+|x^{*}|\,\frac{2^{1-(n+\gamma)}}{n+\gamma-1}(2^{k-2}-|x^{*}|)^{1-(n+\gamma)}\bigg]

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{cap}(E_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\,2^{-k(n+\gamma-2)},

where in (
3.12
) it was used that
|x^{*}|\leq 2^{m-4}
. Using that the Wiener integral converges, we therefore obtain
\vartheta(x^{*})\lesssim\varepsilon
, and hence
\vartheta
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
and
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in
\Omega
. In particular, we find that the liminf at
\infty
is estimated via
\vartheta_{*}\lesssim\varepsilon
using the comparison principle. By construction, we find that
\vartheta\geq 1
quasi everywhere on
\partial\Omega^{m}
as desired.

Step 2: Modification of
\vartheta
.
We aim to modify
\vartheta\rightsquigarrow\varpi
in such a way that
\varpi_{*}\lesssim\varepsilon
at
\infty
and
\varpi\geq 1
everywhere on
\partial\Omega^{m}
. To do so, we define

(3.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{k}\coloneqq\{x\in E_{k}:\vartheta(x)<1\}

\displaystyle\text{for all }k\in\mathbbm{N},\ k>m.

Since the set of irregular boundary points is a set of capacity
0
, we find an open set
\tilde{E}_{k}\subset U_{k}\subset B_{2^{k+1}}
such that
\operatorname{cap}(\overline{U}_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})<\varepsilon
. By definition of capacity, we can, without loss of generality, assume that
U_{k}\subset B_{2^{k-\frac{3}{2}}}^{c}
. We now consider the
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic function
\varsigma\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
defined via

(3.14)

\displaystyle\varsigma\coloneqq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[\overline{U}_{k},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]

We repeat the computations from (
3.4
) – (
3.12
) to obtain

(3.15)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[\overline{U}_{k},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}](x^{*})

\displaystyle\lesssim\liminf_{M\to\infty}\int_{\partial U_{k}}\int_{|x^{*}-y|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x^{*}),B_{2t}(x^{*}))}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\textrm{d}\nu_{E_{k}}^{M}(y)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{cap}(\overline{U}_{k},B_{2^{k+1}})\int_{2^{k-\frac{3}{2}}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t}(x^{*}),B_{2t}(x^{*}))}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{2^{k-\frac{3}{2}}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{t}{{\mu_{\gamma}(B_{t}(x^{*}))}}\,\textrm{d}t

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{2^{k-\frac{3}{2}}-|x^{*}|}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(t-|x^{*}|)^{n+\gamma}}\,\textrm{d}t

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\big[2^{2-(n+\gamma)}(2^{k-\frac{5}{2}}-|x^{*}|)^{2-(n+\gamma)}+|x^{*}|2^{1-(n+\gamma)}(2^{k-\frac{5}{2}}-|x^{*}|)^{1-(n+\gamma)}\big]

(3.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon 2^{-k(n+\gamma-2)}.

In total, we find that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\varsigma(x^{*})\lesssim\varepsilon\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(2^{-k})^{n+\gamma-2}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Now by definition, the function
\varpi\coloneqq\vartheta+\varsigma\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}
, satisfying
\varpi\geq 1
everywhere on
(\Omega^{m})^{c}
, in particular on
\partial\Omega^{m}
, and
\varpi(x_{*})\lesssim\varepsilon
. Since
\varpi
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
\Omega^{m}
, by applying
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic minimum principle in
\Omega^{m}\cap B_{R}
we conclude that for all sufficiently large
R
, there must be a point
x_{R}\in\Omega_{e}\cap B_{R}
such that
\varpi(x_{R})\lesssim\varepsilon
. Hence, we deduce that
\varpi_{*}\lesssim\varepsilon
at
\infty
.

Step 3: Conclusion.
Using step 2, the function
\varpi+\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[B_{2^{m}},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]
is now in the upper class of the boundary function
1-\mathbf{1}_{\infty}
. Thus, we find that

(3.22)

\displaystyle 0\leq 1-\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}=\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{1-\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}\leq\varpi+\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[B_{2^{m}},{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}]

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

from which we obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle 0\leq\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\Omega\end{subarray}}1-\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}(x)\leq\varpi_{*}+0\lesssim\varepsilon.

Thus, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\gamma)>0
such that

(3.24)

\displaystyle 1-C\varepsilon\leq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\\
x\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the claim follows.
\blacksquare

The following proposition shows the other direction of Proposition
3.3
. It is based on local bounds in terms of the Wiener integral that are obtained via a well-known iteration process of potentials (see
[
HKM06
, Lemma 6.25]
, see also
[
Maz70
, Corollary 1]
). In contrast to the preceding proposition, we do not require
\Omega
to have an unbounded connected component.

Proposition 3.4

Assume that the Wiener integral diverges. Then
\infty
is regular for
\Omega
.

Proof:
Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and let
0<r<R
. We write
B_{R}\coloneqq B_{R}(x_{0})
. We define the function
v_{R}\colon{\mathbbm{R}^{\kern-0.7ptn}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}
via

\displaystyle v_{R}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{1}[\Omega^{c}\cap\overline{B}_{R},B_{2R}]&\quad\text{in }B_{2R},\\[6.0pt]
1&\quad\text{in }B_{2R}^{c}.\end{array}\right.

Using the pasting lemma for
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic functions, the function
v_{R}
is
\mathcal{A}
-superharmonic in
\Omega
and therefore in the upper class of the boundary function
\mathbf{1}_{\infty}
for every
R>0
. Using Lemma
2.4
, there exists
c=c(n,\gamma)>0
such that

(3.27)

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}^{\Omega}(x)\leq v_{R}(x)\leq\exp\Big(-c\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\operatorname{cap}(\Omega^{c}\cap B_{t},B_{2t})}{\operatorname{cap}(B_{t},B_{2t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,\Big)

for all
x\in B_{\frac{r}{2}}\cap\Omega
. Using that the Wiener integral diverges, in the limit
R\to\infty
we find that
\mathcal{H}^{\Omega}_{\mathbf{1}_{\infty}}=0
in
\Omega\cap B_{\frac{r}{2}}
. Since
r>0
was arbitrary, the claim follows.
\blacksquare

The next proposition establishes the last missing link
(1)

\Longleftrightarrow

(3)
in Theorem
1.3
. It connects the irregularity of
\infty
to
\mathcal{A}
-thinness at
\infty
of the exterior set (see
[
Abd12
, Theorem 1.1]
for the case of uniformly elliptic operators).

Proposition 3.5

The following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

\Omega^{c}
is
\mathcal{A}
-thick (resp.
\mathcal{A}
-thin) at
\infty
.

(2)

\infty
is regular (resp. irregular) for
\Omega
.

The proof of this proposition is identical to the proof given in the case of uniformly elliptic operators (see
[
Abd12
, Theorem 1.1, (1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(5)]
).

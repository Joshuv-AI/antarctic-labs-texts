Constrained Policy Optimization via Sampling-Based Weight-Space Projection
Shengfan Cao, Francesco Borrelli

Abstract
Safety-critical learning requires policies that improve performance without leaving the safe operating regime. We study constrained policy learning where model parameters must satisfy unknown, rollout-based safety constraints. We propose SCPO, a sampling-based weight-space projection method that enforces safety directly in parameter space without requiring gradient access to the constraint functions. Our approach constructs a local safe region by combining trajectory rollouts with smoothness bounds that relate parameter changes to shifts in safety metrics. Each gradient update is then projected via a convex SOCP, producing a safe first-order step. We establish a safe-by-induction guarantee: starting from any safe initialization, all intermediate policies remain safe given feasible projections. In constrained control settings with a stabilizing backup policy, our approach further ensures closed-loop stability and enables safe adaptation beyond the conservative backup. On regression with harmful supervision and a constrained double-integrator task with malicious expert, our approach consistently rejects unsafe updates, maintains feasibility throughout training, and achieves meaningful primal objective improvement.



I
Introduction

Safety is a fundamental requirement for deploying learning-based controllers in safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving and robotics.
Deep neural network (NN) policies can achieve high performance, but it remains extremely challenging to provide formal guarantees for constraint satisfaction for general tasks.
In practice, systems deployed in real world are often equipped with a backup controller that outputs safe but conservative actions.
In safe RL frameworks such as
[
17
,
19
]
, a backup policy is integrated for intervention or dual-agent setups.
While such backups can prevent catastrophic failures, they are typically used as hard switches and are not integrated into the learning process.

A key observation in this work is that a backup controller need not only be a fail-safe mechanism; it can serve as a feasible initialization for safe adaptation.
We take inspiration from low-rank adaptation (LoRA)
[
9
]
in large-model fine-tuning, where a frozen base model is augmented by small trainable modules that are initialized to zero, so that the initial combined model is identical to the pre-trained one.
Analogously, we treat a certified backup controller as a frozen base policy and augment it with a NN policy that outputs zero at initialization.
This yields a feasible initialization: the combined policy coincides with the backup controller at the start of training, and all subsequent learning can be viewed as safe adaptation in the neighborhood of a known safe policy.

Existing work on safe adaptation tackles the problem in broadly three aspects:
(i)
Safety in output
uses safety filters, control barrier functions, or MPC-based shields to modify the policy’s action post-hoc; these methods offer strong guarantees but the NN is typically unaware of the filter, which can limit performance.
(ii)
Safety in data
removes unsafe trajectories from datasets or biases sampling toward safe regions; this is effective in offline or large-data settings, but less applicable to on-policy learning with limited data.
(iii)
Safety in training
constrains the optimization process itself through trust regions, constrained policy gradients, or gradient projections, often formulated in Constrained Markovian Decision Process (CMDP) settings. While these methods can reduce violations in expectation, they typically lack trajectory-level guarantees such as recursive feasibility and closed-loop stability for NN controllers.

We propose SCPO, a special instance of data-driven, projection-based method for constrained learning, and show stability and recursive feasibility certification of the closed-loop system with policies trained under this framework.
The key idea is to locally estimate a safe set in weight space from small pertubations in weights and the associated changes in safety metrics.
Using sampled rollouts and local smoothness assumptions, we construct a tractable quadratic inner approximation of this safe set and project each raw gradient update onto it.
This yields a sampling-based weight-space projection scheme that preserves safety on all considered rollouts at every training step.

Our main contributions are:

•

A sampling-based constrained policy optimization
that performs weight-space safety projection, ensuring each policy update remains within a locally estimated safe parameter region.

•

A safe-by-induction learning mechanism
that guarantees monotonic improvement of the objective and persistent constraint satisfaction for any first-order update direction.

•

A control-theoretic analysis of SCPO
for linear dynamical systems, providing an instantiation of the safety metrics for asymptotic stability.

•

Two simple experiments
on regression and constrained control tasks with malicious expert, demonstrating robustness against harmful supervision and safe exploration.

II
Related Work

Safe learning has been extensively studied in CMDPs and robotics; see, for example, the surveys by
[
7
,
5
]
for a more comprehensive overview.
In this section, we primarily focus on methods that are foundational to this work to better position our work.

II-
1
Primal-dual methods

A large body of work formulates safety as the expected accumulated cost and applies Lagrangian or primal-dual optimization to CMDPs.
For example, in CRPO
[
18
]
, the training alternates between reward maximization and cost minimization.
NPG-PD
[
4
]
solves CMDPs by performing natural policy gradient ascent on primal variables and sub-gradient descent on dual variables.
However, these approaches focus on expected accumulated cost and typically do not ensure trajectory-level safety during training.

II-
2
Trust region

In trust region methods, each update is restricted such that the policy is close to the previous version.
Modern RL frameworks like TRPO
[
15
]
, PPO
[
16
]
constrain the expected KL-divergence between successive policies and provide a lower bound guaranteeing monotonic improvement under certain conditions.
These approaches focus on training stability in terms of expected return instead of constraint satisfaction.
The trust regions are defined on the policy space instead of as a notion of safe set on the weight space.

II-
3
Projected updates

In projected update methods, safety is enforced by projecting a potentially unsafe update (typically the effect of one or multiple gradient steps) onto a safe set.
In Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO)
[
1
]
, the policy is optimized to maximize a surrogate reward subject to linearized cost constraints and a KL trust-region in policy space.
Under trust-region and linearization assumptions, CPO can guarantee near-constraint satisfaction at each iteration.
In
[
3
]
, safety is represented as linear constraints within dynamic programming algorithms using Lyapunov functions, which leads to variants of safe policy and value iteration algorithms.

In the context of LLMs, SafeLoRA
[
8
]
and SaLoRA
[
11
]
directly projects the LoRA updates to be approximately aligned with a safety-aligned subspace, which is shown to be effective in reducing safety risks during fine-tuning.

Existing projected-update methods either rely on explicit constraint costs in CMDPs or on access to a pre-aligned model that defines a safety subspace.
In contrast, we approximate a feasible set in weight space induced purely by a backup controller and trajectory-level safety metrics, without explicit constraint costs or an aligned reference model.

II-
4
Projected action/policy

Projection in the action or policy space is another class of popular approach.
For example, in
[
6
]
, a MPC-based projection is imposed on the output in the action space, enforcing recursive feasibility.
These methods guarantee safety during deployment via the projection layer or safety filter, but the underlying policy parameters themselves are not certified and may rely on the safety mechanism being active.

As a special case, if there exists a known mapping from an unconstrained high-dimensional space to the feasible set, the problem can be reformulated as searching in the unconstrained space instead.
In
[
13
]
, a representation of the action on a manifold is learned, then projected back into the original action space.
In terms of architecture design,
[
14
]
proposed a differentiable projection layer that can ensure constraint satisfaction for a specific class of QP problems.
These approaches require explicit knowledge of the constraint set, and most existing architectures such as HardNet focus on convex constraints enforceable via projection.

III
Problem formulation

We consider a constrained learning problem in which the model parameters

\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are updated iteratively to minimize a task-specific loss

\mathcal{L}(\theta)
while satisfying a set of differentiable safety constraints:

\displaystyle\min_{\theta}\quad\mathcal{L}(\theta)

(1)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad g(\theta)\leq\mathbf{0},

where
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a differentiable function that maps the weights to a vector of safety metrics;
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the all-zeros vector.
The inequality is understood componentwise.

Note that the exact expression of
g
and its Jacobian are not assumed to be known: we only assume that for a given iterate
\theta_{t}
, the value of
g(\theta_{t})
can be evaluated.
Our projection step is constructed using sampled, pointwise evaluation of
g
.

Assumption 1
(Local
L
-smoothness of constraints)

Each component
g_{i}(\theta)
is locally
L_{i}
-smooth in
\theta
.
That is, there exist
r>0
and
L_{i}\geq 0
such that for any
\Delta\theta
satisfying
\|\Delta\theta\|\leq r
,

\bigl|g_{i}(\theta+\Delta\theta)-g_{i}(\theta)-\nabla_{\theta}g_{i}(\theta)^{\top}\Delta\theta\bigr|\leq\frac{L_{i}}{2}\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}.

III-A

Residual Formulation

Assume we have access to a safe reference weight
\theta_{t}
that satisfies
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
.
We parameterize updates to
\theta_{t}
by a residual
\Delta\theta
and seek to minimize the task loss while preserving safety:

\displaystyle\min_{\Delta\theta}\quad\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta)

(2)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad g(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta)\leq 0.

III-B

Local Quadratic Surrogate

We construct a local quadratic surrogate of (
2
) around
\theta_{0}
.
For the surrogate loss, denoted as
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
, we use a first-order Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{L}
plus an
\ell_{2}
-regularizer:

\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta)=\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})+\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})^{\top}\Delta\theta+\lambda\|\Delta\theta\|_{2}^{2},

(3)

where
\lambda>0
controls the step size.

Define the unconstrained gradient step

\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}\triangleq-\eta\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{0}),\qquad\eta=\frac{1}{2\lambda}.

(4)

Up to an additive constant and a positive scalar factor, optimizing the objective in (
3
) is equivalent to optimizing the squared distance to
\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}
, i.e.,

\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta)=\lambda\|\Delta\theta-\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}\|_{2}^{2}+\text{const.}

(5)

For the constraints, by Assumption
1

, a conservative sufficient condition for
g_{i}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta)\leq 0
is

g_{i}(\theta_{t})+\nabla_{\theta}g_{i}(\theta_{t})^{\top}\Delta\theta+\frac{L_{i}}{2}\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}\leq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,k.

(6)

Let
J_{g}(\theta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
denote the Jacobian of
g
at
\theta_{0}
, and let
L=[L_{1},\dots,L_{k}]^{\top}
. Compactly, the surrogate problem can be written as

\displaystyle\min_{\Delta\theta}\quad\|\Delta\theta-\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}\|_{2}^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad g(\theta_{t})+J_{g}(\theta_{t})\Delta\theta+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\theta\|_{2}^{2}\,L\;\leq\mathbf{0}.

IV
Sampling-Based Weight-Space Projection

Directly solving (
7
) in the full parameter space is generally impractical for large models, both because the dimension
d
is very large and because the Jacobian
J_{g}(\theta_{t})
is typically unavailable in closed form.
Instead, we propose a novel reformulation of (
7
) using only the zeroth-order value of
g
from a finite number of candidate updates.

IV-A

Sampling-based Projection

For iteration
t
, assume we have access to a bank of candidate updates

D=\begin{bmatrix}\Delta\theta_{1}&\dots&\Delta\theta_{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}

and their corresponding safety metrics

G=\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}&\dots&g_{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times m},\qquad g_{i}\triangleq g(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta_{i}).

Note that
m,k\ll d
. In practice,
D
and
G
can be constructed by caching model checkpoints and their safety evaluations during training.
We further assume that the most recent unconstrained gradient step coincides with the last column of
D
, i.e.,

\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}=\Delta\theta_{m}.

We initialize
L
heuristically using the maximum observed pairwise slope across
D
and
G
.
Since this provides an optimistic lower bound, we employ an adaptive backtracking scheme that increases L exponentially whenever the local smoothness assumption is violated.

Instead of searching over all
\Delta\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we restrict
\Delta\theta
to the subspace spanned by
D
:

\Delta\theta=Dc,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(8)

Under this parameterization, (
7
) becomes

\displaystyle\min_{c}

\displaystyle\|Dc-\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}\|_{2}^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle g(\theta_{t})+J_{g}(\theta_{t})Dc+\frac{1}{2}\|Dc\|_{2}^{2}\,L\;\leq\mathbf{0}.

By Assumption
1
, the linearization error for each basis vector is bounded by
\frac{L_{j}}{2}\|\Delta\theta_{i}\|_{2}^{2}
. Summing over the linear combination
\Delta\theta=Dc=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\Delta\theta_{i}
, we bound the Jacobian term:

\displaystyle[J_{g}(\theta_{t})Dc]_{j}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\nabla_{\theta}g_{j}(\theta_{t})^{\top}\Delta\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\bigl(g_{j}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta_{i})-g_{j}(\theta_{t})\bigr)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl|c_{i}\bigr|\frac{L_{j}}{2}\|\Delta\theta_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Gc]_{j}-(\mathbf{1}^{\top}c)g_{j}(\theta_{t})+\frac{L_{j}}{2}|c|^{\top}\mathrm{diag}(S),

where
S=D^{\top}D
is the Gram matrix,
\mathrm{diag}(S)=[\|\Delta\theta_{1}\|^{2},\dots,\|\Delta\theta_{m}\|^{2}]^{\top}
, and the equality uses the definition
[Gc]_{j}=\sum_{i}c_{i}g_{j}(\theta_{t}+\Delta\theta_{i})
.

Substituting (
10
) into the smoothness constraint
g_{j}(\theta_{t})+[J_{g}(\theta_{t})Dc]_{j}+\frac{L_{j}}{2}\|Dc\|_{2}^{2}\leq 0
, and noting that
\|Dc\|_{2}^{2}=c^{\top}Sc
, we obtain the conservative reformulation:

\displaystyle\min_{c}

\displaystyle(c-e_{m})^{\top}S(c-e_{m})

(11)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle(1-\mathbf{1}^{\top}c)g(\theta_{t})+Gc+\frac{1}{2}\left(c^{\top}Sc+|c|^{\top}\mathrm{diag}(S)\right)L\;\leq\mathbf{0},

where
e_{m}=[0,\dots,0,1]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Since
S=D^{\top}D\succeq 0
and
\mathrm{diag}(S)\geq 0
elementwise, the objective and each quadratic constraint in (
11
) is convex in
c
.
The problem can thus be viewed as a Euclidean projection onto a convex set.

Note that the problem has complexity that depends on
m
and
k
but is independent of the model dimension
d
, and it can be solved efficiently when
m
and
k
are of moderate size.

Proposition 1
(Guaranteed Safety)

Assume
g(\theta_{0})\leq 0
. If the update
\Delta\theta_{t}
is obtained by solving (
11
) at every step
t
, then the model remains satisfying the safety constraint
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof:

The constraint in (
11
) is derived as a sufficient condition for
g(\theta_{t+1})\leq 0
given
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
. The result follows by induction on
t
.
∎

IV-B

Monotonic Improvement

In this section, we show that the projection in our approach leads to monotonic improvement of the objective, and the sequence of
\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})
converges under standard assumptions.

Proposition 2
(Projected update is a descent direction)

At
t
, the optimal update
\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}=D_{t}c^{\star}_{t}
from (

\mathcal{L}
at
\theta_{t}
if
\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\neq 0

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{C}_{t}
be the feasible set of (

) at

If

, then

, and
\Delta\theta_{t}^{\star}=\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}
is a descent direction.

Let
\Theta_{t}=\{\Delta\theta_{t}=D_{t}c\mid c\in\mathcal{C}_{t}\}
,
If
e_{m}\notin\mathcal{C}_{t}
, note that
\Theta_{t}
is an image of the convex set
\mathcal{C}_{t}
under a linear transformation, and therefore
\Theta_{t}
is convex in
\Delta\theta_{t}
, and (
11

By hyperplane separation theorem,

\langle\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}-\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t},x-\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\rangle\leq 0,\forall x\in\Theta_{t}.

(12)

Note that
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}
following the assumption that
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
.
Let
x=\mathbf{0}
,

\langle\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}},\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\rangle\geq\|\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.

(13)

Recall
\Delta\theta_{\text{raw}}=-\eta\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})
, where
\eta>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})^{\top}\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\eta}\|\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}>0.

(14)

∎

This indicates that with a standard Armijo-style backtracking line search, which we describe below, the algorithm provides monotonic improvement in the objective without constraint violation.

Lemma 1
(Feasibility of scaled updates)

If
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
, and
\bar{c}
is a feasible solution to (
9
), then
\forall k\in[0,1]
,
k\bar{c}
is also feasible.

Lemma
1
holds because (
9
) is convex, and
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
indicates
c=0
is a feasible solution. Therefore, any convex combination of
c=0
and
\bar{c}
is also feasible.

Assumption 2

Assume the objective
\mathcal{L}
is
C^{1}
,
L_{\mathcal{L}}
-smooth and bounded from below. Formally,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{L}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,~\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta)

\displaystyle>-\infty.

Theorem 1
(Monotonic improvement)

Since
\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}
is a descent direction if
\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\neq 0
, as shown in Proposition
2
, and
\mathcal{L}
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient, an Armijo backtracking line search finds
\alpha\in(0,1]
, such that

\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t}+\alpha\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t})\leq\mathcal{L}(\theta_{t})-\sigma\alpha\|\Delta\theta^{\star}_{t}\|^{2},\exists\sigma\in(0,1).

(16)

Note that Lemma

guarantees the feasibility of the backtracked solution

IV-D

Connections to Prior Work

The proposed update rule can be interpreted as a projected gradient step in parameter space.
In this sense, our method is structurally related to projected gradient descent and to constrained policy update methods such as CPO (
[
1
]
). We highlight here the main conceptual differences.

First, the projection in CPO involves solving a constrained natural-gradient step on linearized constraint cost and surrogate objective with a KL trust region in policy space.
Our method instead defines a local feasible set directly in weight space from sampled trajectories and safety metrics, and uses a quadratic regularization in weight space instead of a fixed trust region.

Second, we optimize over the low-dimensional coefficient vector
c
, which yields a lightweight QCQP in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
By contrast, CPO’s constrained step is defined in the full parameter space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is typically approximated with matrix-free solvers in the Fisher metric.

Third, CPO and related CMDP methods impose constraints through explicit constraint cost functions.
In our framework, safety is enforced through trajectory-based metrics
g(\theta)
computed on a finite set of rollouts, without requiring a separate CMDP-style constraint cost.

V
Application to Safe Control

In this section, we instantiate the abstract safety metric
g
from the weight-space safety projection framework of the previous section in the context of a constrained optimal control problem, and show how the resulting projected updates yield safe training for a parametric feedback controller.

Remark 1

The analysis in this section follows classical results in the domain of Lyapunov theory, MPC, and CBF (e.g.,
[
10
]
). Our novel contribution is its incorporation in SCPO in Section
V-D
to ensure safe training for a parametric feedback controller.

V-A

System

Consider a linear, time-invariant, deterministic, discrete-time system

x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k},

(17)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
is the state and
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
is the control input at time
k
.
The system is subject to state and input constraints

\displaystyle x_{k}\in\mathcal{X},\quad u_{k}\in\mathcal{U},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(18)

where
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
and
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
are polytopic sets.

As a shorthand, we denote by
\mathbf{u}_{0:N-1}
a control sequence of length
N
, and by
\mathbf{x}_{0:N}
a feasible closed-loop state trajectory, where for each
k=0,\dots,N-1
, there exists
u_{k}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k}
,
x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
, and
x_{N}\in\mathcal{X}
.

V-B

Constrained Optimal Control Task

We consider the infinite-horizon constrained optimal control problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{u}_{0:\infty}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c(x_{k},u_{k})

(19)

\displaystyle\textnormal{s.t.}

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k},

\displaystyle x_{k}\in\mathcal{X},\quad u_{k}\in\mathcal{U},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where
c:\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a continuous, positive definite stage cost satisfying

c(x,u)\geq\alpha(\|x\|),\qquad c(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})=0,

for a class-
\mathcal{K}
function
\alpha
.
In addition,
c(x,u)=+\infty
if
x\notin\mathcal{X}
or
u\notin\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 1
(Value function)

Let
V_{\pi}(x_{0})
denote the cost-to-go from
x_{0}
under policy
\pi
. Formally,

V_{\pi}(x_{0})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c(x_{k},\pi(x_{k})),

where
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi(x_{k}),~\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2
(Q-function)

For a policy
\pi
, define

Q_{\pi}(x_{k},u_{k})=c(x_{k},u_{k})+V_{\pi}(x_{k+1}),~~x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k}.

(20)

In words,
Q_{\pi}(x_{k},u_{k})
is the cost-to-go incurred by applying input
u_{k}
at state
x_{k}
and then following policy
\pi
thereafter.

Assumption 3
(Baseline controller
\pi_{\text{safe}}
)

We assume access to a non-parametric baseline controller
\pi_{\text{safe}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
, which is assumed to be locally safe around the origin in the following sense:
there exists a non-empty set
\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
\mathbf{0}\in\mathrm{int}(\Omega)
, such that
\forall x\in\Omega
,

\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)\in\mathcal{U},~~Ax+B\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)\in\Omega,

and the corresponding value function is finite, i.e.,

V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x)<\infty,~~\forall x\in\Omega.

Note that Assumption
3
implies that
\pi_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
.
Therefore,
x=\mathbf{0}
is an equilibrium for the closed-loop system
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi_{\text{safe}}(x_{k})
.

Definition 3
(Forward Invariant Set under
\pi
)

Consider the closed-loop system
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi_{\theta}(x_{k})
subject to the constraints in (
18
).
A set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is forward invariant under
\pi
if for all
x\in\mathcal{S}
, the control input satisfies
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{U}
, and the subsequent state satisfies
Ax+B\pi_{\theta}(x)\in\mathcal{S}
.

Let

\mathcal{X}_{f}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x)\leq\epsilon\},

(21)

where
\epsilon>0
is an appropriately chosen small constant.
Note that
\mathcal{X}_{f}
is a forward invariant set for the closed-loop system under
\pi_{\text{safe}}
.

Let

\pi_{\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)+\phi_{\theta}(x),&x\notin\mathcal{X}_{f},\\
\pi_{\text{safe}}(x),&x\in\mathcal{X}_{f},\end{cases}

denote the feedback controller, where
\phi_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
is a neural network parameterized by
\theta
.
Note that
x=\mathbf{0}
is also an equilibrium for the closed-loop system
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi_{\theta}(x_{k})
because
\pi_{\theta}=\pi_{\text{safe}}
near the origin.

At initialization,
let
\theta_{0}
satisfy
\phi_{\theta_{0}}(x)\equiv\mathbf{0}
, so that
\pi_{\theta_{0}}(x)=\pi_{\text{safe}}(x),~\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
.
This can be trivially achieved by setting the weights on the final output layer of
\phi
to zeros.

Remark 2

Although
\pi_{\text{safe}}
could in principle be high-capacity, in many safety-critical settings a simple stabilizing controller is easier to certify and makes the initialization
\pi_{\theta}=\pi_{\text{safe}}
(with
\phi_{\theta}=\mathbf{0}
) trivially safe.
By contrast, encoding such conservative behavior directly inside a deep network is harder, so we delegate performance improvements to the expressive residual
\phi_{\theta}
, which is subsequently projected to preserve safety.

V-C

Safety

The policy structure and value functions defined above allow us to address the central question of this section:
under what conditions is
\pi_{\theta}
safe and stabilizing?

We answer this by introducing the backward reachable set and formulating a one-step improvement assumption that leads directly to our theoretical guarantees.

Definition 4
(Closed-loop backward reachable set)

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
be a feedback policy.
The closed-loop backward reachable set of a target set
\mathcal{X}_{f}
under
\pi
is the set of all states from which the system can reach
\mathcal{X}_{f}
in finite time while respecting the constraints:

\mathcal{R}_{\pi}(\mathcal{X}_{f})=\left\{x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}\,\middle|\,\begin{matrix}\exists N\in\mathbb{N},~\exists\mathbf{x}_{0:N}:\\
x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi(x_{k}),\\
x_{k}\in\mathcal{X},~\pi(x_{k})\in\mathcal{U},\\
\forall k=0,\dots,N-1,\\
x_{N}\in\mathcal{X}_{f}.\end{matrix}\right\}.

(22)

By Definition
4
, once the system enters
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
, it can be driven to
\mathcal{X}_{f}
while satisfying the constraints by switching to the backup controller
\pi_{\text{safe}}
.
Note that the switch need not happen in practice, and this argument is to facilitate the stability and recursive feasibility analysis below.

Lemma 2

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
be a policy that satisfies
\pi(x)=\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)
whenever
x\in\mathcal{X}_{f}
.
For such policy
\pi
,

x\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi}(\mathcal{X}_{f})\iff V_{\pi}(x)<\infty.

We now introduce a key assumption that, when satisfied, yields Lyapunov-based safety guarantees for
\pi_{\theta}
.
We will later use this assumption to motivate the constraint implemented in our sampling-based projection method.

Assumption 4
(One-step improvement w.r.t.
\pi_{\text{safe}}
)

The parametric controller
\pi_{\theta}
satisfies
\forall x_{k}\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}),

Q_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k},\pi_{\theta}(x_{k}))\leq V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k})+\tilde{\alpha}(\|x_{k}\|),

where
\tilde{\alpha}
is a class-
\mathcal{K}
function that satisfies
\tilde{\alpha}(r)<\alpha(r),~\forall r>0
.

Lemma 3

If Assumption
4
holds for a feedback controller
\pi_{\theta}
and a backup controller
\pi_{\text{safe}}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
is forward invariant under
\pi_{\theta}
.

Theorem 2
(Stability)

Under Assumption
4

the equilibrium at the origin of
the closed-loop system

x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+B\pi_{\theta}(x_{k})

is asymptotically stable
with region of attraction
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}))
.

Proof:

By Definition
4
, for all
x_{k}\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}))
, there exists
N\in[k,\infty)
, such that
x_{N}\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
.

For all
x_{N}\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
, let

W_{\theta}(x_{N})\triangleq V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{N}),~~x_{N}\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}).

(23)

Since
c(x,u)
is positive definite, and the origin is an equilibrium for
\pi_{\text{safe}}
,
W_{\theta}
is positive definite and
W_{\theta}(\mathbf{0})=0
.

Recall Lemma
3
,
x_{N+1}=Ax_{N}+B\pi_{\theta}(x_{N})\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
.
Then,

\displaystyle W_{\theta}(x_{N})-W_{\theta}(x_{N+1})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{N})-V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{N+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{N})-\bigl(Q_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{N},\pi_{\theta}(x_{N}))-c(x_{N},\pi_{\theta}(x_{N})\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha(\|x_{N}\|)-\tilde{\alpha}(\|x_{N}\|)\geq 0,

where equality is attained iff
\|x_{N}\|=0
.

Therefore, the finite-time entry into the forward invariant set
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
, combined with the asymptotic stability established therein, as certified by the Lyapunov function
W_{\theta}(x_{k})
, implies that the equilibrium at the origin is asymptotically stable with region of attraction
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}))
.
∎

V-D

Safe control via weight-space projection

While Assumption
4
leads to theoretical guarantees for stability, it is a strong assumption on its own.
In this section, we discuss how this assumption can be enforced as a constraint for SCPO, so that
\pi_{\theta_{t}}
is stabilizing for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\bar{u}_{k}\triangleq\pi_{\text{safe}}(x_{k})=Kx_{k}
;
\tilde{u}_{k}\triangleq\phi_{\theta}(x_{k})
is the output of the NN, so that
\pi_{\theta}(x)=\bar{u}_{k}+\tilde{u}_{k}
,
\forall x\notin\mathcal{X}_{f}
.
Define

\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k},\pi_{\theta}(x_{k}))\triangleq Q_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k},\bar{u}_{k}+\tilde{u}_{k})-V_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k}).

(25)

Let

g(\theta)=\max_{x\in\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})}\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x_{k},\pi_{\theta}(x))-\tilde{\alpha}(\|x_{k}\|).

(26)

The set
\Theta=\{\theta\mid g(\theta)\leq 0\}
then represents the feasible set where Assumption
4
is satisfied and therefore
\pi_{\theta}
is stabilizing on
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f}))
by Theorem
2
.

Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of parameters updated according to Algorithm
1
, using
g(\theta)
defined in (
26
).
By Proposition
1
, if
\theta_{0}
satisfies
g(\theta_{0})\leq 0
, then
g(\theta_{t})\leq 0
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies
\pi_{\theta_{t}}
is a stabilizing controller on
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
.

Note that the
\Theta
is challenging to derive because it involves verifying a deep NN over the entire
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
.
Recent neural-network verification methods (e.g.,
[
12
]
) can, in principle, certify input–output properties of ReLU networks and thus provide a tractable way to check the universal condition in Assumption 4 on small networks.
However, such methods are not yet scalable for deep NNs.

In practice, we approximate
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
using a uniformly gridded set of representative states, denoted as
\mathcal{G}
.
This gridded set provides a deterministic coverage of the domain and avoids the sampling bias inherent to on-policy rollouts.
The surrogate constraint is then computed as:

\displaystyle\hat{g}(\theta)=\max_{x\in\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(x,\pi_{\theta}(x))-\tilde{\alpha}(\|x_{k}\|).

(27)

Provided the grid resolution is sufficiently fine, this yields a tractable approximation of Assumption
4
.
Safety is certified with respect to the grid, finer grids reduce false negatives but increase compute.

VI
Experiments

We evaluate weight-space safety projection on both a toy regression problem and a constrained control task.
The experiments are designed to answer two core questions:

•

Robustness to harmful supervision.

Can the proposed projection reject unsafe (harmful) demonstrations while still enabling meaningful task improvement?

•

Safety preservation during learning.

In a control setting, does the learned policy remain stabilizing throughout training, and does the backward reachable set

\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\theta}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
stay nondecreasing?

The policy
\pi_{\theta}
is parameterized by the architecture in Fig.
1
, and training pipeline follows Algorithm
1
.

VI-A

Constrained Regression

We first consider a one-dimensional constrained regression problem to visualize how projection filters harmful updates.
The constrained learning objective is

\displaystyle\min_{\theta}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\!\left[\,\bigl(f(x)-\pi_{\theta}(x)\bigr)^{2}\right]

(28)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\left|\pi_{\theta}(x)\right|\leq 1.4,\quad\forall x\in[-3,3],

where the reference function is

f(x)=\sin(x)+\sin(3x)+\sin(7x)
.
Note that
f
violates the constraint on part of
[-3,3]
, so naïvely imitating it can be unsafe.

We take the safe policy to be the zero function,

\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)\equiv 0
, which satisfies the constraint.
We enforce safety on a uniform grid
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{64}
over
[-3,3]
.
The surrogate constraint violation vector is

\mathbf{g}(\theta)=|\pi_{\theta}(\mathbf{v})|-1.4\times\mathbf{1},

(29)

where
\pi_{\theta}(\mathbf{v})
denotes elementwise evaluation on the grid.
For reporting, we summarize violation by
\|\mathbf{g}(\theta)\|_{1}/64
.

At each epoch we draw
N=64
i.i.d. samples from
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and minimize the empirical loss

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(f(x_{i})-\pi_{\theta}(x_{i})\bigr)^{2}.

(30)

Fig.
2
shows the learned curve after training.
Starting from a feasible initialization, projection prevents unsafe drift: the policy fits
f
where it is safe and smoothly saturates at the constraint boundary with no violation.

Fig. 2:
Constrained regression with proposed projection.
The loss in (
28
) is estimated on
N=64
fresh samples from
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
each epoch.
Constraint satisfaction is evaluated on a 64-point uniform grid over
[-3,3]
.

To position our approach against existing approach, we compare against a soft-constraint baseline. Specifically, we replace the hard projection step with an unconstrained optimization problem augmented by a soft penalty:

\min_{\theta}\;\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\bigl[(f(x)-\pi_{\theta}(x))^{2}\bigr]+\lambda\cdot\mathbf{1}^{\top}(\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{g}(\theta))).

(31)

Remark 3

Note that this formulation implicitly assumes that
g
is known and differentiable, and similarly standard constrained RL methods (like CPO or PPO-Lagrangian) require a first-order gradient estimate. This contradicts our problem setting where only pointwise evaluations of
g(\theta)
are available.

As shown in Fig.
3
, even when such a differentiable soft penalty is artificially made available, it still fails to guarantee strict constraint satisfaction and introduces an additional hyperparameter
\lambda
that must be tuned.

Regression with soft constraint (
\lambda=1
), without proposed projection.

VI-B

Double Integrator Imitation Learning

We next study safe learning on a constrained control task to empirically validate that SCPO keeps
\pi_{\theta}
stabilizing under harmful supervision from a
malicious expert
.

Consider the discrete-time double integrator with the following dynamics, constraints:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\Delta t\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}x_{k}+\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\Delta t^{2}\\
\Delta t\end{bmatrix}u_{k},

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-15\\
-15\end{bmatrix}\leq x_{k}\leq\begin{bmatrix}15\\
15\end{bmatrix},~~\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}\leq u_{k}\leq\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\end{bmatrix},~~\forall k\geq 0,

with

\Delta t=0.1
.
Input constraints are box constraints and are always satisfied by clipping
u_{k}
to
[-1,1]
.
The target set
\mathcal{X}_{f}=\{x\mid\|x\|\leq 0.01\}
is a tiny ball around the origin.

As a stabilizing safe policy we use a clipped proportional controller
\pi_{\text{safe}}(x)=\mathrm{clip}(-Kx,-1,1),

where
K
is the LQR feedback gain solved with DARE, using
Q=I_{2}
and
R=I_{1}
.
The malicious expert is an aggressively tuned linear feedback controller with additive noise
\pi_{\beta}=\mathrm{clip}(-2(x_{1}+x_{2})+\delta,-1,1)
, where
\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.4)
.

We follow the imitation-learning objective

\min_{\theta}\;\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}\bigl[\|\pi_{\theta}(x)-\pi_{\beta}(x)\|^{2}\bigr],

(33)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the state distribution induced by rolling out the current policy.

We form the surrogate safety metric
\hat{g}
via (
27
), using
\tilde{\alpha}(\|x_{k}\|)=\|x_{k}\|^{2}
, and the grid
\mathcal{G}
resolution is
50\times 50
, uniformly covering
\mathcal{X}
.
Initial states are sampled in
\mathcal{X}
, but only trajectories that ends in
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{\text{safe}}}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
are retained for training.

shows closed-loop trajectories from an example initial state under

The sets are estimated on a grid, where a state is classified as backward reachable if the closed-loop trajectory enters
\mathcal{X}_{f}
within a fixed horizon without constraint violation.
Empirically,
\pi_{\theta}
remains stabilizing where
\pi_{\text{safe}}
is stabilizing while
\pi_{\beta}
fails, showing effective rejection of harmful updates.

VII
Discussion

We introduced a weight-space projection with guarantees for stability, recursive feasibility, and monotonic improvement.
A primary limitation is the method’s conservativeness as the projection step does not generate new direction to improve. We aim to address this by integrating
[
2
]
to modify the objective for improved safe exploration.

Haiqing Cheng, Kui Wang

Abstract
. and Li

For Einstein manifolds, a well-known result in this aspect is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Any closed Einstein manifold with nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is either flat or a round sphere.

There is a rich literature on Theorem
1.1
. Kashiwada
[
10
]
proved that compact manifold
M
with harmonic curvature and positive curvature operator of the second kind is a space of constant curvature.
Cao-Gursky-Tran
[
3
]
demonstrated that compact Einstein manifolds with 4-positive and 4-nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind are constant curvature spaces and locally symmetric spaces, respectively. Subsequently, Li
[
12
]
relaxed the assumption to
4\frac{1}{2}
-positive (respectively, nonnegative) curvature operator of the second kind.
Nienhaus-Petersen-Wink
[
19
]
showed that
n
-dimensional compact Einstein manifolds with
N^{\prime}
-nonnegative (
N^{\prime}<\frac{3n(n+2)}{2(n+4)}
) curvature operators of the second kind are either rational homology spheres or ﬂat.
Recently, Dai, Fu and Yang
[
6
]
showed that a compact Einstein manifold of dimension
n\geq 4
with nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is flat or a space form of positive curvature, see also
[
7
]
.

Cao-Gursky-Tran
[
3
]
, Nienhaus-Petersen-Wink
[
19
]
, Li
[
15
]
and Dai-Fu
[
7
]
have proved many excellent results under various curvature conditions. Recently, Li
[
17
]
introduced new lower bound conditions on the curvature operator of the second kind (see
[
17
, Definition 1.2]
) and proved optimal sphere theorems. Based on this framework, we focus on Li’s condition with
\alpha=1
, namely (
1.1
), which is weaker than nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind. To state our result, we introduce some notation:
(M^{n},g)
is an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
\mathring{R}
represents the curvature operator of the second kind,
N=(n+2)(n-1)/2
is the dimension of

,
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}
, and
\bar{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}/N
is the average of
\lambda_{j}
. The main result of this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Einstein manifold of dimension
n\geq 4
.
If

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq-\theta(n)\bar{\lambda},

then

is either flat or a round sphere. Here

is a positive constant given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\theta(n)=\begin{cases}17/7,&\quad n=4,\\
47/18,&\quad n=5,\\
\frac{2(n^{4}-4n^{3}-5n^{2}+30n-16)}{n^{4}+8n^{3}-5n^{2}-48n+32},&\quad n\geq 6.\end{cases}

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.2
essentially represents a gap theorem for Einstein manifolds, in the sense that if the curvature operator of the second kind satisfies (

The crucial step in proving Theorem

is sharp or not for Theorem

[
5

obtained analogous results under a cone condition where the first two eigenvalues have a negative lower bound.

Let

The space of symmetric two-tensors on

2.2.
Curvature Operator of the Second Kind

For an algebraic curvature tensor
R\in S^{2}_{B}(\wedge^{2}(T_{p}M))
,
R
can induce two self-adjoint operators (see
[
20
]
).
The first one denoted by
\hat{R}:\wedge^{2}(T_{p}M)\to\wedge^{2}(T_{p}M)
is defined as

\hat{R}(\omega)_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}R_{ijkl}\omega_{kl},

which is called the curvature operator of the first kind.
The other one,
\overline{R}:S^{2}(T_{p}M)\to S^{2}(T_{p}M)
, is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{R}(\varphi)_{ij}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}R_{iklj}\varphi_{kl}.

The curvature operator of the second kind, introduced by Nishikawa
[
20
]
, is
symmetric bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathring{R}:S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M)\times S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M)\to\mathbb{R}

obtained by restricting
\overline{R}
to
S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M)
, the space of traceless symmetric two-tensors. It was pointed out in
[
19
]
that the curvature operator of the
second kind
\mathring{R}
can also be interpreted as the self-adjoint operator

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathring{R}=\pi\circ\overline{R}:S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M)\to S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M),

where
\pi:S^{2}(T_{p}M)\to S^{2}_{0}(T_{p}M)
is the projection map.

We denote by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}
,
by
\bar{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}/N
be the average of the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}
, by
R
the Riemann curvature tensor, and by
{\operatorname{Scal}}
be the scalar curvature.
For Einstein manifolds, one can obtain the scalar curvature via
\mathring{R}
. Precisely

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g)
is an Einstein manifold of dimension
n
. Then the scalar curvature satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Scal}}=n(n-1)\bar{\lambda}.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}M
, then

\{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e_{i}\odot e_{j}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\cup\{\dfrac{1}{2}e_{i}\odot e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is an orthonormal basis of
S^{2}(T_{p}M)
. Since

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bar{R}(\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{j}}),\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{j}}}\right\rangle=R_{ijij},

then

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\bar{R})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}{\left\langle{\bar{R}(\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{j}}),\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{j}}}\right\rangle}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}{\left\langle{\bar{R}(\frac{1}{2}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{i}}),\frac{1}{2}{e_{i}}\odot{e_{i}}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}R_{ijij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\operatorname{Scal}}}{2}.

Thus,

\operatorname{tr}(\mathring{R})=\operatorname{tr}(\bar{R})-\langle\bar{R}(\frac{g}{\sqrt{n}}),\frac{g}{\sqrt{n}}\rangle=\frac{{\operatorname{Scal}}}{2}+\frac{{\operatorname{Scal}}}{n}=\frac{n+2}{2n}{\operatorname{Scal}}.

Using
\operatorname{tr}(\mathring{R})=N\bar{\lambda}
, we obtain

{\operatorname{Scal}}=\frac{2n}{n+2}N\bar{\lambda}=n(n-1)\bar{\lambda},

proving (
2.2
).

\square

\tilde{\lambda}_{j}=\lambda_{j}/\bar{\lambda}
,
\tilde{\lambda}=(\tilde{\lambda}_{1},\tilde{\lambda}_{2},\cdots,\tilde{\lambda}_{N})
, and then
\tilde{\lambda}
satisfies the constraints

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}=N,\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\geq-\theta.

Set
x=(\tilde{\lambda}_{1}+\theta,\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+\theta,\cdots,\tilde{\lambda}_{N}+\theta)
and
t=1+\theta
, then the constraints (
3.7
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}=Nt\quad\text{and}\quad x_{j}\geq 0.

We prove the case of
n\geq 6
. From the expression of
f(\lambda)
we can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}^{3}}f(\lambda)=C(n,t)+3nF(x),

where

\displaystyle C(n,t)=

\displaystyle N\big[(N-3)t-(3N-9n+3)\big]+3nN(t-1)^{2}(2t+1)

\displaystyle+\big[3(N-4n+1)-(N-3)t\big]N(1-t^{2}),

and

\displaystyle F(x)=-(3-\frac{N-2}{N-1})t\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{3}.

Now we reduce the proposition to seek the minimal points of

F(x)
with constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}=Nt,\quad\text{and}\quad x_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,2,\cdots,N).

The corresponding Lagrangian function is

\mathcal{L}(x,\mu):=-(3-\frac{N-2}{N-1})t\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{3}+\mu(\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}-Nt).

Let

\Omega_{N}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}=Nt,x_{j}>0\Big\},

and

\Omega_{k}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}=Nt,x_{1}=\cdots=x_{N-k}=0,x_{j}>0,j\geq N-k+1\Big\}

for
k=1,\cdots,N-1
.

In
\Omega_{k}
, using the method of Lagrange multipliers, we find that the critical points

x=\big(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{N-k},x_{N-k+1},\cdots,x_{N}\big)

of
\mathcal{L}(x,\mu)
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}3x_{i}^{2}-2(3-\frac{N-2}{N-1})tx_{i}+\mu=0,\\
\sum_{i=N-k+1}^{N}x_{i}=Nt,\end{cases}

hence the possible critical points are given by

\displaystyle P_{k,l}:=\big({\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{N-k},\underbrace{a,\cdots,a}_{l},\underbrace{b,\cdots,b}_{k-l}}\big),

for
0\leq l\leq\frac{k}{2}
, where
a
and
b
are defined through

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a+b=2(1-\frac{N-2}{3(N-1)})t,\\
la+(k-l)b=Nt.\end{cases}

When
l
equals
k/2
, we observe that
1-\frac{N-2}{3(N-1)}<\frac{N}{k}
, and in this case equation (
3.8
) becomes unsolvable.
When
l<\frac{k}{2}
, by solving (
3.8
) we get

\displaystyle a=(A-B)t,\quad b=(A+B)t,

where
A=1-\frac{N-2}{3(N-1)}
,
B=\frac{N-Ak}{k-2l}
, and
direct calculation gives

\displaystyle F(P_{k,l})=t^{3}\left(-2kA^{3}-3A^{2}(N-Ak)+\frac{(N-Ak)^{3}}{(k-2l)^{2}}\right),

hence

(3.9)

\displaystyle F(P_{k,0})<F(P_{k,l}),\quad 1\leq l<k/2.

Therefore we conclude from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\min\left\{F(x):\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}=Nt,x_{j}\geq 0\right\}=\min\left\{F(P_{k,0}),k=1,2,\cdots,N\right\},

which indicates that the minimum points of the function
f(\lambda)
, subject to the constraint
\lambda_{i}\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}
, are included among

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}=\left(\underbrace{-\theta\bar{\lambda},\cdots,-\theta\bar{\lambda}}_{m},\underbrace{\frac{N+m\theta}{N-m}\bar{\lambda},\cdots,\frac{N+m\theta}{N-m}\bar{\lambda}}_{N-m}\right)

for
m=0,1,\cdots,N-1
.

For
\lambda=\lambda^{m}
, we calculate that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{2}_{j}=m\theta^{2}+(N-m)\left(\frac{N+m\theta}{N-m}\right)^{2}=N+\frac{N}{N-m}mt^{2},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{3}_{j}=-mt^{3}\frac{(N-2m)N}{(N-m)^{2}}+\frac{3mN}{N-m}t^{2}+N,

we then conclude

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}^{3}}f(\lambda^{m})=

\displaystyle\Big[-\frac{N(N-3)m}{N-m}-\frac{3nmN(N-2m)}{(N-m)^{2}}\Big]t^{3}+\frac{3N(N+1-n)}{N-m}mt^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{mNt^{2}}{N-m}\Big[3(N+1-n)-(N-3+\frac{3n(N-2m)}{N-m})t\Big].

Recall from the definition of
\theta
in (
1.2
) that

\theta=\frac{2\left(n^{4}-4n^{3}-5n^{2}+30n-16\right)}{n^{4}+8n^{3}-5n^{2}-48n+32}=\frac{3(N-1)(N+1-n)}{(N-1)(N-3)+3n(N-2)}-1,

we see

t=1+\theta=\frac{3(N+1-n)}{N-3+3n(N-2)/(N-1)},

and substituting above to (
3.10
) yields

f(\lambda^{0})=f(\lambda^{1})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad f(\lambda^{k})>0

for
k=2,3,\cdots,N-1
.
Hence Proposition
3.4
comes true for
n\geq 6
.

For dimensions
n=4
and
5
,
\langle\Delta R,R\rangle

can be expressed explicitly in terms of the curvature operator of the second kind as (see
[
7
, Section 4.2]
):

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta R,R\right\rangle=\begin{cases}8\left(\sum_{j=1}^{9}\lambda_{j}^{3}-9\bar{\lambda}^{3}\right),&\quad n=4,\\
8\left(\sum_{j=1}^{14}\lambda_{j}^{3}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{\lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{14}\lambda_{j}^{2}-\frac{56}{3}\bar{\lambda}^{3}\right),&\quad n=5.\end{cases}

Then an argument similar to the one used for
n\geq 6
shows that Proposition
3.4
holds for
n=4
and
n=5
. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.4
.

\square

Now we prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Using (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta|R|^{2}=2|\nabla R|^{2}+2\langle\Delta R,R\rangle\geq 2|\nabla R|^{2},

and integrating the above inequality over
M
yields

\displaystyle 0=\int_{M}\Delta|R|^{2}\,d\mu_{g}\geq\int_{M}2|\nabla R|^{2}\,d\mu_{g}\geq 0,

which implies
|\nabla R|=0
. Therefore
M
is a symmetric space.
[
19
, Theorem B]
and
[
17
, Theorem 1.8(2)]
imply that
M
is either flat or a rational homology sphere.
Besides, Poincaré duality shows that rational cohomology is determined by rational homology
[
9
, Chapter 3.3]
.
Thus we conclude that
M
is either flat or a symmetric space which is also a rational cohomology sphere.
Compact symmetric spaces which are real cohomology spheres were classified by Wolf in
[
22
, Theorem 1]
.
If
\bar{\lambda}>0
, apart from spheres,
SU(3)/SO(3)
is the only simply connected example.
Proposition
3.4
implies that the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}
is either
\lambda=(1,\cdots,1)\bar{\lambda}
or
\lambda=(-\theta,\frac{N+\theta}{N-1},\cdots,\frac{N+\theta}{N-1})\bar{\lambda}
, and
combining with
[
19
, Example 4.5]
, we know that
M
is a round sphere.

\square

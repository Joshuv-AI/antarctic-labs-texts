Experimental Investigation on the Influence of Wave Impedance on Dynamic Mechanical Response of Granites undergone High Temperature
Authors: Weiyu Li, Shizheng Fang, Xin Gao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractWave impedance is an important physical quantity to characterize the dynamic properties of materials. To explore the influence of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of rock, the granite samples with a wave impedance gradient change are obtained by changing the heating temperature, and the impact compression test is carried out by using a split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) device. The stress wave propagation law, dynamic stress–strain relationship, and fracture characteristics of rocks with different wave impedances are studied comparatively, and the influence mechanism of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of rocks is comprehensively analyzed from two aspects of material properties and dynamics. The results show the following: (1) Under the action of the same incident wave, the reflected wave amplitude, transmission wave takeoff time, peak stress, peak strain, and equivalent average size of fragments of granite samples with different wave impedances are significantly different. (2) As the heating temperature increases, the wave impedance of granite continuously decreases and the degree of damage intensifies. Within the high-temperature treatment range of 400 to 600 °C, there is a damage wave impedance threshold between 7854 and 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1. Below this wave impedance, granite will appear to have significant damage deterioration. (3) Under the same incident wave, the strain rate and loading rate of granite samples show negative correlation and positive correlation with wave impedance, respectively. There is a progressive relationship between rock wave impedance, stress wave propagation, strain rate history/stress history, and dynamic mechanical response.

Introduction

After exposure to high temperatures, the rock structure, basic mechanical behavior characteristics, and engineering response have undergone fundamental changes. The disasters induced by high temperatures bring great challenges to rock engineering.1−3 The drilling and blasting method is often used for rock excavation, so it is inevitable to consider the propagation and attenuation of stress wave in rock mass and the deformation and failure of rock mass under dynamic load.4−9 The wave impedance reflects the ability of the material medium to prevent a wave from passing through. It is an important physical quantity to characterize the dynamic properties of the medium. The wave impedance of the material has a very important influence on the wave propagation.10−17 Therefore, it is of great significance for engineering fields such as rock breaking and protection to study the mechanical response of rocks under a high strain rate after different high temperatures by taking wave impedance as the breakthrough point.

With the development and popularization of SHPB technology, many scholars have conducted experimental studies on the mechanical properties of rocks under impact load after exposure to high temperatures and have achieved fruitful results. Yao et al.18 used the method of computed tomography (CT) to quantitatively characterize the damage degree of Longyou sandstone after exposure to high temperatures, tested the dynamic tensile strength of Longyou sandstone after different temperature treatments, and obtained the variation relationship of the dynamic tensile strength of Longyou sandstone with temperature and loading rate. Yin et al.19 carried out the impact compression test of coal rock after exposure to high temperatures and compared the dynamic mechanical properties of coal rock after different temperature treatments. It is found that the mechanical response of coal rock under impact load shows an obvious temperature effect. Liu et al.20 studied the energy dissipation law of granite after exposure to high temperatures under impact load, quantified the damage degree of rock based on the dissipation energy, and studied the influence law of heating temperature on the damage degree of granite. Yin et al.21 carried out the impact compression test of coal rock after exposure to high temperatures using the SHPB test system. The influence of temperature on reflected energy, transmitted energy, and dissipated energy is studied, and the failure mechanism of coal rock after exposure to high temperatures under dynamic load is explained from the perspective of energy. Su et al.22 studied the effect of temperature on the dynamic fracture mechanical properties of granite based on the cracked straight-through Brazilian disc (CSTBD) granite samples after high temperature treatment. The study showed that the dynamic fracture toughness of granite was negatively correlated with temperature, and the amplitude change increased after heat treatment at a temperature of 400 °C. Jiang et al.23 studied the dynamic fracture mechanical properties of granite and sandstone after exposure to high temperatures in the range of 25–700 °C. The research results showed that high temperatures have a significant impact on the dynamic fracture mechanical behavior of rocks, and the dynamic fracture mechanical strength of rocks undergoes drastic changes in the range of 500–600 °C.

For the subject of the influence of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical behavior of rock, relevant scholars have also carried out a lot of research. Wang24 studied the transmission and reflection behavior of stress wave from one wave impedance medium to another wave impedance medium (normal incidence) in combination with one-dimensional linear elastic stress wave theory and deduced the relationship between the transmission coefficient, reflection coefficient, and wave impedance. Yang et al.25 conducted a comparative study on the dynamic mechanical behavior of five groups of cement mortar samples with different wave impedances under impact load. It is found that the wave impedance has an obvious influence on the dynamic stress–strain relationship, the degree of fragmentation, and the stress wave propagation characteristics of cement mortar samples. Jin et al.26 studied the stress wave propagation characteristics in red sandstone under confining pressure based on the dynamic and static combined loading test system. The relationship between confining pressure, wave impedance, and stress wave propagation characteristics is analyzed. The results show that with the increase in confining pressure, rock wave impedance increases, and stress wave penetration increases. Li et al.10 studied the relationship between rock wave impedance and its degree of damage through theoretical and experimental methods and proposed the method of defining rock damage variables using wave impedance. The research results showed that defining rock damage variables using wave impedance is more suitable for dynamic research.

There are many experimental research results on the dynamic mechanical properties of rock after exposure to high temperatures, but there are few comparative analyses from the perspective of rock wave impedance. In this paper, by changing the heating temperature, the granite samples after exposure to high temperatures with gradient changes in wave impedance are obtained. The split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) device was used to conduct impact compression tests on granite samples with different wave impedances after exposure to high temperatures under different impact velocities; the effects of wave impedance on the propagation characteristics of stress waves, dynamic stress–strain relationship, and fragmentation degree are compared, and the influence mechanism of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of rocks is further analyzed.

Heat Treatment

A YDL tubular furnace as shown in Figure 2 is used to heat the processed granite samples. The maximum design working temperature of the high-temperature furnace is 1200 °C.

Related studies have shown that lower heating and cooling rates can reduce the thermal shock damage of rocks caused by rapid temperature changes, and a longer constant temperature time can ensure the uniformity of heat treatment.10 Therefore, this paper adopts the method described below to heat-treat granite samples. The temperature control system is set to heat the sample at a rate of 5 °C/min. After reaching the preset temperature, a constant temperature is maintained for 4 h. After the power is turned off, the sample is naturally cooled to room temperature in the high-temperature furnace. Variation Characteristics of Wave Impedance

The ultrasonic longitudinal wave test system as shown in Figure 5 is used to measure the longitudinal wave velocity of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures. The complete wave velocity measurement system consists of a signal display, pulse transceiver, and acoustic transducer probe.15 The frequency of the nonmetallic ultrasonic probe used in the test is 250 kHz. The measurement ensures that the transmission pulse width, gain, and other parameters of the test system are set consistently, and the sampling accuracy and sampling length are set to 5 × 10–3 μs and 500 μs, respectively. An ultrasonic testing coupling agent is used to ensure that the sample and ultrasonic probe closely fit during the test. The received wave signals with and without the specimen are obtained through the test, and the arrival time of the first wave is obtained according to the received wave signal so as to calculate the longitudinal wave velocity Vp of the rock sample. A vernier caliper and electronic scale are used to measure the size and mass of the sample, respectively, and the density ρ of the rock sample is calculated. Each group randomly selects three samples for the above measurement and calculation to obtain the average density and average longitudinal wave velocity of each group of granite samples and further calculates the average wave impedance Zr (Zr = ρ·Vp) of each group of samples, as shown in Table 2. According to the data in Table 2, the density, longitudinal wave velocity, and wave impedance of granite samples after a high temperature change with heating temperature are shown in Figure 6.

Impact Compression Test

SHPB Test System

The split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) device used in the test mainly consists of three parts, including a power control system, a bar system, and a data acquisition system. Figure 7 is a schematic diagram of the experimental system. The elastic modulus Eb of the bar is 206 GPa, the wave velocity Cb of the bar is 5250 m·s–1, and the diameter of the bar is 50 mm. The lengths of the impact bar, incident bar, and transmission bar are 300, 2000, and 1800 mm, respectively. The strain gauges used in the experimental system are attached to the middle of the bars with a resistance value of 120 Ω and a sensitivity of 2.08. The acquisition circuit uses a half-bridged circuit with a bridge voltage of 2 V and a gain of 1000. In dynamic tests, when the impact bar strikes the incident bar, a compressive wave that propagates forward will be generated. During the propagation of the stress wave, the incident wave εi is recorded through strain gauges on the incident bar. When the stress wave reaches the sample, part of the stress wave is reflected back to the incident bar to form a reflected wave εr, and part of the stress wave passes through the sample and transmits to the transmission bar to form a transmitted wave εt.

SHPB test system.

Verification of Dynamic Stress Balance

When the SHPB experimental system is used to test the dynamic mechanical properties of materials, two main assumptions need to be satisfied. One is the plane section assumption, that is, during the propagation of stress waves, the stress at any cross section in the waveguide bar always remains planar. The second is the assumption of uniform stress, which means that when stress waves propagate in the sample, the stresses in the sample are approximately equal everywhere. The experiment uses an elongated steel bar as a waveguide bar, which basically satisfies the one-dimensional stress wave assumption. Dynamic Stress–Strain Relationship

The stress–strain curves of granite samples with different wave impedances at impact velocities of about 10.4 m/s are shown in Figure 11. It can be seen from Figure 11 that under similar impact velocity, the dynamic stress–strain relationship of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures with different wave impedances is obviously different. It is mainly shown that with the decrease in wave impedance, the peak stress and dynamic elastic modulus of the granite sample basically show a decreasing trend, and the peak strain basically shows a rising trend. The above change relationship is not obvious in the wave impedance range of 14071 to 7854 g·cm–3·m·s–1. However, when the wave impedance decreases to 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1, the stress–strain curve of the granite sample starts to show significant changes, and the peak stress, peak strain, and dynamic elastic modulus all show significant changes. Also, when the wave impedance decreases to 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1, the stress–strain curve of the granite sample begins to show obvious compaction and plastic segments, and the unloading segment slows down significantly. When the wave impedance decreases to 1940 g·cm–3·m·s–1, the compaction section and plastic section of the stress–strain curve of the granite sample further extend and the unloading section further slows down. At the same impact velocity, the stress–strain curves of granite samples from the fourth and fifth groups with small wave impedance are significantly different from those of the first three groups. At other impact velocities, the stress–strain curve of the granite sample after exposure to high temperatures changes with wave impedance similar to the above. The above research shows that the changing trend of the dynamic stress–strain relationship of granite samples is consistent with the fast decreasing area of longitudinal wave velocity and wave impedance shown in Figure 6.

Comparison of stress–strain curves of granite samples with different wave impedances.

To compare the relationship between the peak stress and the impact velocity of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures with different wave impedances, the relationship between the peak stress and the impact velocity of each group of samples is drawn, as shown in Figure 12. It can be seen from Figure 12 that the peak stress of each group of granite samples with different wave impedances increases linearly with the increase in impact velocity. At the same impact velocity, the peak stress of granite samples basically decreased with the decrease in wave impedance. The peak stress of three experimental groups with wave impedance of 14071 g·cm–3·m·s–1, 11370 g·cm–3·m·s–1 and 7854 g·cm–3·m·s–1 had little difference. When the wave impedance decreased to 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1, the peak stress began to decrease significantly.

Peak stress–impact velocity diagram.

It is easy to find from Figures 11 and 12 that the peak stress of granite samples in groups 1, 2, and 3 under the same impact velocity has little difference, which is not consistent with the variation of longitudinal wave velocity and wave impedance of granite samples with heating temperature. The analysis shows that this is the result of the joint action of the thermal damage degradation effect and strain rate effect. On the one hand, heating treatment will make the bearing capacity of the granite sample worse and will reduce the dynamic compressive strength of the sample. On the other hand, the strain rate effect will increase the dynamic compressive strength of granite samples. However, under the same impact speed, the average strain rate of granite samples with different wave impedances after exposure to high temperatures is different and the sensitivity of dynamic compressive strength to the strain rate is also different. Therefore, under the influence of the strain rate effect, the dynamic compressive strength of granite with different wave impedances is enhanced differently.

The research in Section 4.1 shows that the reflected wave increases with the decrease in the wave impedance of the granite sample under the same impact velocity due to the matching degree of the wave impedance between the rock sample and the incident bar. In combination with eq 2, the larger the reflected wave, the higher the average strain rate of the sample under dynamic loading. That is, the average strain rate of the third group of samples under the same impact speed is >group 2 > group 1. This makes the influence of strain rate on the dynamic compressive strength of granite with different wave impedances under the same stress wave different. Therefore, the peak stress difference of granite samples with increasing damage deterioration degree as the heating temperature rises is small under the same impact speed.

Discussion

Influence Mechanism of Wave Impedance on the Dynamic Mechanical Response of Rock

In Section 4, the dynamic mechanical behavior of granite after exposure to high temperatures with different wave impedances is studied from three aspects: stress wave propagation characteristics, dynamic stress–strain relationship, and impact fragmentation degree. The research results show that wave impedance has an obvious influence on the dynamic response of granite after exposure to high temperatures, and there is a certain regularity between this influence and the wave impedance of the sample. Therefore, it is necessary to combine the SHPB experimental principle and the above research results to conduct a more in-depth study of the influence mechanism of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of rock. The influence mechanism of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of granite after exposure to high temperatures is analyzed from two aspects of material properties and dynamics.

The above-mentioned method of changing the heating temperature to change the density and longitudinal wave velocity of rocks to obtain granite samples with gradient changes in wave impedance is essentially to obtain rock materials with different wave impedances by changing the material microstructure. Therefore, wave impedance can be regarded as an overall characterization parameter of its microstructure for granite after exposure to high temperatures. The microdefects, integrity, and compactness of granite samples with different wave impedances are different, so the wave impedance can be used to reflect the damage deterioration of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures. Relevant research results also show that for rocks of the same lithology, the rock wave impedance has a strong correlation with its mesostructure and damage deterioration degree, and the smaller the rock wave impedance, the greater the microdefects in its mesostructure, and the greater the damage deterioration degree.25,31,32 Therefore, from the perspective of material properties, the dynamic mechanical response of rocks with different wave impedances of the same lithology must be affected by their wave impedances, and the influence mechanism can be explained by the blue box in Figure 16.

Taking the impact compression test of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures with an impact velocity of 11.150 m·s–1 as an example, the influence mechanism of wave impedance on the dynamic mechanical response of granite samples after exposure to high temperatures is analyzed from the perspective of dynamics. At this impact speed, the time history curve of strain rate and stress of the granite sample with a wave impedance gradient change in the impact compression test is shown in Figures 17 and 18, respectively. It can be seen from Figure 9 that the incident wave is basically the same at the same impact speed. Under the action of the same incident wave, the reflected wave and transmitted wave of granite samples with different wave impedances show obvious differences. The relationship between the magnitude of the reflected wave and transmitted wave and its wave impedance shows obvious correlation. The smaller the wave impedance of rock samples, the larger the reflected wave, and the smaller the transmitted wave is. It can be seen from Figures 17 and 18 that under the action of the same incident wave, the strain rate history and stress history of granite samples with different wave impedances also show obvious differences, and the smaller the rock wave impedance is, the larger the strain rate is, and the smaller the loading rate is. That is, under the same test conditions and the same incident wave, the stress wave propagation process, stress history, loading rate, and strain rate of rock samples with different wave impedances are different. At the same time, relevant research shows that the dynamic mechanical properties of rock materials have an obvious rate effect.33−35 Therefore, the dynamic mechanical response of different rock samples under the action of the same incident wave must be affected by the wave impedance. The analysis shows that there is a progressive influence relationship between rock wave impedance, stress wave propagation characteristics, strain rate/stress history, and dynamic mechanical response, that is, the magnitude of rock wave impedance affects the propagation and attenuation process of stress wave in the rock, resulting in changes in its strain rate history and stress history, and the rock dynamic mechanical response will inevitably be affected by its loading process and rate effect. Therefore, from the perspective of dynamics, the influence mechanism of wave impedance on the mechanical response of rocks of the same lithology under impact load can be explained by the orange color box in Figure 16.

Conclusions

1.Under the action of the same incident wave, as the wave impedance of the granite sample decreases, the reflected wave increases, the takeoff time of the transmitted wave lags behind, the peak stress and dynamic elastic modulus basically show a trend of decreasing, the peak strain basically shows a trend of increasing, the equivalent average size of the sample fragments shows a downward trend, and the degree of fragmentation continues to increase.

Under the action of the same incident wave, as the wave impedance of the granite sample decreases, the reflected wave increases, the takeoff time of the transmitted wave lags behind, the peak stress and dynamic elastic modulus basically show a trend of decreasing, the peak strain basically shows a trend of increasing, the equivalent average size of the sample fragments shows a downward trend, and the degree of fragmentation continues to increase.

2.After exposure to high temperatures, the dynamic mechanical properties of granite samples began to change significantly when the wave impedance decreased to 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1. The damage degree of granite increases with the decrease in wave impedance. It is inferred that there is a wave impedance threshold of damage in the range of 7854 to 3081 g·cm–3·m·s–1, and granite with wave impedance lower than this threshold will have significant damage deterioration.

There is a progressive relationship among rock wave impedance, stress wave propagation, strain rate/stress history, and dynamic mechanical response.

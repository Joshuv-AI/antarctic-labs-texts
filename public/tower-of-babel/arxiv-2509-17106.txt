Star products and their Hilbert space mirrors
Hendry M. Lim

Abstract
We discuss the Cahill-Glauber (CG) s s -parameterized representation, a framework in the phase space formulation of quantum mechanics. We present the differential form of the hatted star product ⋆ ^ s \mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}} , which emerges when we consider the inverse Cahill-Glauber (iCG) transform of a product of phase space functions. It appears to be the Hilbert space mirror of the usual star product ⋆ s \mathbin{\star_{s}} , and supports the interpretation of classical mechanics as the irreducible deformation of quantum mechanics by decoherence. Additionally, alternative forms of the evaluation of ⋆ s \mathbin{\star_{s}} and ⋆ ^ s \mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}} are considered.



I
Introduction

Quantum mechanics is formulated through
quantization
, the process of extending classical mechanics to include quantum features. Canonically, this is achieved by replacing phase space variables with the corresponding quantum operators to represent classical phase space functions in Hilbert space. Doing this, however, presents us with an immediate roadblock: how do we order the operators? For example, given the scaling parameter
\zeta
, the reduced Planck’s constant
\hbar
, and the complex phase space variables

\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\zeta q+i\zeta^{-1}p}{\sqrt{2\hbar}},\quad\alpha^{*}\coloneqq\frac{\zeta q-i\zeta^{-1}p}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}

(1)

corresponding to the operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, respectively, how do we canonical-quantize
\alpha^{*}\alpha
? We may have, say,
\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}
(antinormal ordering),
\left(\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)/2
(symmetric ordering), or
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}
(normal ordering). Each of these choices is equally valid and results in useful predictions. However, it illustrates that there is really no unique way to quantize a classical system. This culminates in the Groenewold-van Hove no-go theorem, stating that there can be no canonical quantization map that preserves the algebraic structure of classical mechanics
Curtright et al. 2013
;
Groenewold 1946
;
Van Hove 1951
. That is, there can generally be
no
map
\mathcal{M}
such that

\mathcal{M}\left[\left\{f,g\right\}\right]=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\left[\mathcal{M}\left[f\right],\mathcal{M}\left[g\right]\right]

(2)

where

\left\{f,g\right\}\coloneqq\frac{1}{i\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\alpha^{*}}}-\frac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\alpha^{*}}}\right)

(3)

is the Poisson bracket and

\left[{\hat{F}},{\hat{G}}\right]\coloneqq\hat{F}\hat{G}-\hat{G}\hat{F}

(4)

is the commutator.

Since its inception, several formulations of quantum mechanics have been developed, each with its own usefulness and prominent features
Styer et al. 2002
. A lesser-known formulation is the phase space formulation, which represents quantum mechanics in phase space. Unlike classical functions, however, a phase space representation of a quantum system must violate one of Kolmogorov’s axioms of probability—a consequence of the uncertainty principle. Furthermore, multiple quantum phase space representations are possible, each with its own peculiarities—a consequence of the ambiguity of quantization. For example, the paradigm of the phase space formulation, the Wigner representation
W

Wigner 1932
, may take on negative values that signify nonclassicality of a quantum state
Kenfack and yczkowski 2004
.

One family of quantum phase space representations is particularly intimate with canonical quantization and its ordering ambiguity: the Cahill-Glauber (CG)
s
-parameterized representation
Cahill and Glauber 1969a
;
Cahill and Glauber 1969b
. As we demonstrate throughout our discussion in this text, the formulation establishes an elegant correspondence between phase space and Hilbert space, allowing us to bring quantum mechanics into phase space and classical mechanics into Hilbert space.

Despite being an established formalism, there is an aspect that we find inadequately explored. Representing quantum mechanics in phase space results in a new form of algebra which enforces the noncommutative nature of quantum mechanics onto scalar functions, as characterized by the celebrated Moyal-Groenewold star product
Moyal 1949
;
Groenewold 1946
and its generalization within the CG framework
Soloviev 2015
. What, then, do we get by representing classical mechanics in phase space? As it turns out, the mirror-like connection between Hilbert space and phase space also applies to the algebraic structure.

In this work, we discuss how the inverse Cahill-Glauber (iCG) transform of an ordinary product of phase space functions is given by the “hatted star product” of the iCG transforms of the factors. The map, denoted by
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
, has a differential form that structurally resembles the usual star product
\mathbin{\star_{s}}
. As the mirror of the star product, the hatted star product is a hallmark of classical mechanics in Hilbert space, and supports the interpretation of classical mechanics as the irreducible deformation of quantum mechanics by decoherence. Additionally, we present alternative forms for the evaluation of
\mathbin{\star_{s}}
and
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
utilizing, respectively, the phase space Bopp operators (PBSOs) and Hilbert space Bopp superoperators (HSBSs). The alternative form demonstrates how the star product (hatted star product) “transports” a Hilbert (phase) space function into phase (Hilbert) space as an operator (a superoperator) to act on its other operand. These forms are practically useful since they allow us to skip evaluating the transform of one of the factors.

The rest of this text is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we discuss the role of the CG transform in representing quantum mechanics in phase space, where the star products appear. In Section
III
, we consider the role of the iCG transform in representing classical mechanics in Hilbert space (i.e., canonical quantization), demonstrating the emergence of the Hilbert space mirror of the star products. The alternative forms are discussed in Section
IV
. Finally, Section
V
concludes the text.

III
Classical Mechanics in Hilbert Space

We start with the disclaimer that the problem discussed in this section has been previously considered by Bracken

The

The difference between classical and quantum mechanical algebra lies in their multiplication structure. The star product emerges when we transport noncommutative operator products onto the commutative phase space. What, then, would we obtain if we transported commutative scalar products onto Hilbert space? That is, what is the algebraic machinery behind the iCG transform of a product?

We know by construction that, given the scalar-to-operator promotion
\left(\alpha,\alpha^{*}\right)\mapsto\left(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)
, the ordering ambiguity of the quantization of a monomial like
\alpha^{*}\alpha
results in different operators depending on our choice of
s
. In terms of practicality, then, the answer to our question seems rather trivial: we can just promote the scalars to operators and take the
s
-ordering of the result.

However, what about expressions like
\alpha f
? In principle, we can work out the iCG transform integral, or use some reverse-engineering tricks (i.e., what operator gives
\alpha f
under the CG transform?). However, these are not straightforward and may be cumbersome to some. Now, we may know that
\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{\alpha}\right]=\hat{a}
and
\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{f}\right]=\hat{F}^{(s)}
. It would then be convenient to have a method that connects the three.

The CG correspondence is elegant in that it demonstrates the mirror-like connection between Hilbert space and phase space. It is thus reasonable to suspect that our answer is some sort of Hilbert space mirror of the star product. It is convenient to consider the ‘‘derivative’’ superoperators

This may illustrate how canonical quantization is faulty: as the process of representing classical phase space functions in Hilbert space, it does nothing to address the underlying mismatch in the algebraic structure. Regardless of the ordering, in Hilbert space, the Poisson bracket is never represented by the commutator, but instead a deformation of it—in accordance with the no-go theorem.

Technically, we can rearrange the last line in Eq. (
) and take its iCG transform to obtain the last line in Eq. (

). However, that would not be a good justification for a new interpretation, when Eq. (
) clearly shows how the phase space algebraic structure of quantum mechanics, as characterized by the star product, is the deformation of that of classical mechanics. Here, we have presented the Hilbert space mirror of the star product. This places Eq. (

) and Eq. (

“Classical mechanics can be thought of as the irreducible deformation of quantum mechanics in Hilbert space.”

By “irreducible”, we mean that there is no deformation parameter we can tune to obtain the commutator from its deformation. This is because
\hbar
is built into the Hilbert space algebra of quantum mechanics via
\left[{\hat{q}},{\hat{p}}\right]=i\hbar
and thus cannot serve as a deformation parameter. While the usual star product
\mathbin{\star_{s}}
is a hallmark of quantum mechanics in phase space, we can see that the hatted star product
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
is a hallmark of classical mechanics in Hilbert space.

This interpretation ties nicely to the concept of decoherence, the loss of quantumness due to inevitable interactions with the environment
Schlosshauer 2007
;
Schlosshauer 2019
. That is, given that quantum mechanics is the underlying true mechanism of the universe, it is deformed by decoherence into the eventually emerging classical mechanics, as indicated by the pure commutator being transformed into the Hilbert space representation of the Poisson bracket. The loss of quantumness then manifests as the ambiguity in the restoration of the quantum structure, i.e., quantization, which in turn results in the ambiguity of what classical mechanics looks like in Hilbert space, as indicated by Eq. (
8ae
) depending on
s
.

IV

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\frac{s-1}{2}\partial_{\hat{a}}^{R}

be the Hilbert-space Bopp superoperators (HSBSs). Utilizing the properties that
\left[{\partial_{\hat{a}}^{R}},{\partial_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}}\right]=0
, we can see that the HSBSs
commute
with each other,

\left[{\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{c}},{\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{b}^{d}}\right]=0,\quad a,b\in\{\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\},\quad c,d\in\{L,R\}.

(8ag)

The HSBSs allow us to formulate that

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]&=f\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{R},\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}\right)\hat{G}^{(s)}\\
&=\hat{F}^{(s)}g\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{L},\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{L}\right),\end{split}

(8ah)

which we prove in Appendix
B.3
. That is,

\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{f}\right]\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\equiv f\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{R},\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}\right),\quad\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{g}\right]\equiv g\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{L},\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{L}\right).

(8ai)

This property is intriguing. Since the HSBSs commute, the hatted star product allows
f
, belonging to phase space, to keep its scalar characteristics when transported into Hilbert space without being subject to the ordering ambiguity. Put in layman’s terms,
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
tells
f
how to behave in Hilbert space, and the HSBSs allow
f
to manifest as a scalar-like superoperator in Hilbert space. The formula is practically useful since we can skip the step of taking the iCG transform of
f
; this lightens computational costs.

It turns out that a similarly structured alternative form also exists for the star product. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha+\frac{s-1}{2}\partial_{\alpha^{*}}^{L},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha^{*}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha^{*}+\frac{s+1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}^{L},

(8aj)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha+\frac{s+1}{2}\partial_{\alpha^{*}}^{R},\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha^{*}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha^{*}+\frac{s-1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}^{R}

be the phase space Bopp operators (PSBOs). Unlike the HSBSs, the PSBOs do not commute,

\left[{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{c}},{\mathcal{B}_{b}^{d}}\right]=(1-\delta_{ab})\delta_{cd},\quad a,b\in\{\alpha,\alpha^{*}\},\quad c,d\in\{L,R\},

(8ak)

where
\delta_{jk}
is the Kronecker delta. They appear in the formula

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{F}\hat{G}}\right]&=\hat{F}\left(\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha},\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha^{*}}\right)g\\
&=f\hat{G}\left(\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha},\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha^{*}}\right),\end{split}

(8al)

which we prove in Appendix
B.4
. That is,

\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{F}}\right]\mathbin{\star_{s}}\equiv\hat{F}\left(\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha},\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha^{*}}\right),\quad\mathbin{\star_{s}}\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{G}}\right]\equiv\hat{G}\left(\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha},\mathcal{B}^{L}_{\alpha^{*}}\right).

(8am)

The noncommuting nature of the PSBOs is befitting of their role of replacing the operators
\left(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)
in
\hat{F}
, belonging to Hilbert space, as
\hat{F}
is transported to phase space by the star product. In other words,
\star_{s}
tells
\hat{F}
how to behave in phase space, and the PS Bopp operators allow
\hat{F}
to manifest as a Hilbert-space-operator-like operator in phase space. As is the case for the iCG transform, this formula allows us to skip evaluating the CG transform of one factor when evaluating the CG transform of a product, alleviating computational cost. It is also interesting to note that the formulation allows for the CCR and
s
-ordering to emerge in phase space.

V
Conclusion

We have considered the Cahill-Glauber formalism. While the star product
\mathbin{\star_{s}}
emerges when quantum mechanics is brought into phase space, its mirror, which we call the hatted star product
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
, emerges when classical mechanics is brought into Hilbert space. Furthermore, we have formulated an alternative form of the Cahill-Glauber transform of a product involving the phase space Bopp operators, as well as that of the inverse Cahill-Glauber transform of a product involving the Hilbert space Bopp superoperators. These forms are practically useful as they allow us to compute the CG (iCG) transforms of products without having to evaluate the CG (iCG) transforms of all factors. They can be implemented, for example, in computer algebra systems to alleviate computational costs. More generally, our results contribute to the algebra of the (Hilbert space) HS-PS (phase space) correspondence, further enhancing its elegance.

The two interpretations we have considered illustrate the defect in the HS-PS correspondence. In phase space, classical mechanics appears certain, while quantum mechanics is ambiguous. As such, there are many ways quantum mechanics can emerge from an
axiomatic
classical mechanics, as shown by Eq. (
). That is, there are many
quantization
schemes. In Hilbert space, quantum mechanics is certain, while classical mechanics is ambiguous. As such, there are many ways classical mechanics can emerge from an
axiomatic
quantum mechanics, as shown by Eq. (
8ae
). That is, there are many
decoherence
schemes.

The latter interpretation suggests that the hatted star product may be of use in decoherence studies. As an extravagant example, it is tempting to take the iCG transform of the Poisson bracket to be a time evolution generator for a quantum density matrix,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}t}=\left[\left[{\hat{H}^{(s)}},{\rho}\right]\right]_{s},

(8an)

where
\hat{H}=\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{H}\right]
for some classical Hamiltonian
H
and

\left[\left[{\hat{F}},{\hat{G}}\right]\right]_{s}\coloneqq\frac{1}{i\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{F}}{\partial\hat{a}}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\frac{\partial\hat{G}}{\partial\hat{a}^{\dagger}}-\frac{\partial\hat{F}}{\partial\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\frac{\partial\hat{G}}{\partial\hat{a}}\right).

(8ao)

This equation would be physically valid if
\left[\left[{\hat{H}^{(s)}},{\rho}\right]\right]
is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP; see, e.g., Section 8.2.4 in Ref.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
). We leave this discussion for future studies.

Appendix A
Bracken’s “odot” product

In Ref.
Bracken 2003
, Bracken introduced the “odot” map
\mathbin{\odot}
, defined by

\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-1}\left[fg\right]=\hat{F}^{(0)}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}^{(0)},

(8ap)

where the map is such that

\left\{\hat{q}^{k}\hat{p}^{l}\right\}_{0}\mathbin{\odot}\left\{\hat{q}^{m}\hat{p}^{n}\right\}_{0}=\left\{\hat{q}^{k+m}\hat{p}^{l+n}\right\}_{0}.

(8aq)

More importantly, Bracken presented the integral form of the map via the relation between the operator kernels of
\hat{F}^{(0)}
,
\hat{G}^{(0)}
, and
\hat{F}^{(0)}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}^{(0)}
. Given the position-space kernel {cf. Eq. (13) in Ref.
Bracken 2003
}

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{A}}(x,y)&=\left\langle{x}\middle|{\hat{A}}\middle|{y}\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}pA\left(\frac{x+y}{2},p\right)\exp\left[\frac{ip(x-y)}{\hbar}\right],\end{split}

(8ar)

where
A=\mathcal{W}_{0}\left[\hat{A}\right]
, we have {cf. Eq. (17) of Ref.
Bracken 2003
}

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}}\left(r_{1}-\frac{u}{2},r_{2}+\frac{u}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\times\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{G}}\left(r_{1}+\frac{u}{2},r_{2}-\frac{u}{2}\right),\end{split}

(8as)

where
r_{1}=(3x+y)/4
and
r_{2}=(x+3y)/4
. Obtaining
\hat{F}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}
is then straightforward:

\begin{split}\hat{F}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\left|{x}\right\rangle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}\mathbin{\odot}\hat{G}}\left\langle{y}\right|.\end{split}

(8at)

The algebraic properties of
\odot
and how it can be used to represent classical mechanics in Hilbert space are then described. Bracken interprets that the Poisson bracket can be seen as a
\hbar
-dependent deformation of the commutator, to which it reduces in the
\hbar\to 0
limit. This implies that, in Hilbert space, quantum mechanics is a limit of classical mechanics. The resolution to the apparent mismatch with the phase space interpretation is that quantum mechanics and classical mechanics become asymptotically equivalent as
\hbar\to 0
.

Appendix B
Proofs

This section contains the proofs of the new results presented in this text. We acknowledge that these proofs may lack the mathematical rigor and are more heuristic in nature. Nevertheless, we hope they serve as good starting points for the community to formulate more rigorous proofs, possibly starting from the definition of the iCG transform, and specify the conditions under which these results are well-defined.

B.1
Relation between different Hilbert space representations

Using Eqs. (
8a
) and (
8
), we may write

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{s}&=\exp\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\left[{\hat{a}},{\left[{\hat{a}^{\dagger}},{\cdot}\right]}\right]\right)\mathcal{T}_{t}\\
&=\exp\left(-\frac{s-t}{2}\partial_{\hat{a}}{\partial_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}}\right)\mathcal{T}_{t},\end{split}

(8au)

and hence

\begin{split}\hat{F}^{(s)}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha}{\pi}f\mathcal{T}_{s}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha}{\pi}f\exp\left(-\frac{s-t}{2}\partial_{\hat{a}}{\partial_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}}\right)\mathcal{T}_{t}\\
&=\exp\left(-\frac{s-t}{2}\partial_{\hat{a}}{\partial_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha}{\pi}f\mathcal{T}_{t}\\
&=\exp\left(-\frac{s-t}{2}\partial_{\hat{a}}{\partial_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}}\right)\hat{F}^{(t)}.\end{split}

(8av)

This form is comparable to Eq. (5.11) in Ref.
Cahill and Glauber 1969a
. ∎

B.2
Differential form of the hatted star product

Let
\hat{G}^{(s)}=\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{g\left(\alpha,\alpha^{*}\right)}\right]
. For any real-analytic
f=f\left(\alpha,\alpha^{*}\right)
, we can write

\begin{split}fg&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\left(\alpha^{*}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n}g\\
&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\left\{\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)^{m}\hat{a}^{n}\right\}_{s}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}}\right]\\
&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\underbrace{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\dots\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}_{\text{$m$ times}}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\underbrace{\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\dots\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{a}}_{\text{$n$ times}}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}}\right].\end{split}

(8aw)

We note that there are other possible ways to express
\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{\left(\alpha^{*}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n}}\right]
. However, all the possible expressions are CCR-equivalent. By virtue of Eq. (
8ab
), then, proving the last line of Eq. (
8aw
) is sufficient. By construction, Eqs. (
8za
) and (
8zb
) hold. Now, we can see that the subexpression to the right of a given
\hat{a}
or
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
can be viewed as a test function. For example, for the leftmost
\hat{a}
in the brackets, we can define

\hat{G}_{\mathrm{new}}^{(s)}=\hat{a}^{n-1}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}\implies g_{\mathrm{new}}=\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{G}_{\mathrm{new}}^{(s)}}\right]=\alpha^{n-1}g,

(8ax)

for which

\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}_{\mathrm{new}}^{(s)}}\right]=\alpha g_{\mathrm{new}}=\alpha^{n}g.

(8ay)

We can thus work leftward: since
\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}}\right]=\alpha g
holds, then so does
\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\left(\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}\right)}\right]=\alpha^{2}g
and
\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\left(\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)}\right)}\right]=\alpha^{*}\alpha g
, and so on. A similar analysis can be done with the left operand being the test function and the right operand being expanded. Since the CG correspondence is bijective, we can take the iCG transform of the equality to obtain the claim. ∎

B.3
Alternative form of the hatted star product

Let
\hat{G}^{(s)}=\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{g\left(\alpha,\alpha^{*}\right)}\right]
. For any real-analytic
f=f\left(\alpha,\alpha^{*}\right)
, we can write

\begin{split}fg&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\left(\alpha^{*}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n}g\\
&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}\right)^{m}\left(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{R}\right)^{n}\hat{G}^{(s)}}\right].\end{split}

(8az)

The HSBSs are commutative, so ordering does not matter, and the above expansion is unique. Since

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}}^{R}\hat{G}^{(s)}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{a}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)},\quad\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{R}\hat{G}^{(s)}\equiv\hat{a}^{\dagger}\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}\hat{G}^{(s)},

(8ba)

it follows from the same line of reasoning in the previous proof that the claim is true. ∎

B.4
Alternative form of the star product

Let
g^{(s)}=\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{G}\left(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)}\right]
. For any real-analytic
\hat{F}=\hat{F}\left(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)
, we can write

\begin{split}\hat{F}\hat{G}&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)^{m}\hat{a}^{n}\hat{G}\\
&=\sum_{m,n}f_{mn}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{\left(\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha^{*}}\right)^{m}\left(\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha}\right)^{n}g^{(s)}}\right].\end{split}

(8bb)

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha}g^{(s)}

\displaystyle\equiv\alpha\mathbin{\star_{s}}g^{(s)},\quad\mathcal{B}^{R}_{\alpha^{*}}g^{(s)}\equiv\alpha^{*}\mathbin{\star_{s}}g^{(s)},

(8bc)

it follows from an argument similar to the proof given in Appendix
B.2
that Eq. (
8bb
) holds. Furthermore, since the PSBOs share the same CCR as
\left(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)
, any CCR-equivalent of Eq. (
8bb
) holds as well. ∎

Appendix C
Ab Initio Consideration for the Hatted Star Product

This section is written as an attempt to compute the integral form of
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
from the definition of the iCG transform. Throughout this section, we treat the phase space variable as a single complex number, for convenience. Following Cahill and Glauber
Cahill and Glauber 1969a
, we consider the Fourier transform pair

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\left(\mu\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\left[f(\alpha)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha}{\pi}f(\alpha)\exp\left(\mu^{*}\alpha-\mu\alpha^{*}\right),

(8bda)

\displaystyle f(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\tilde{f}\left(\mu\right)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu}{\pi}\tilde{f}\left(\mu\right)\exp\left(-\mu^{*}\alpha+\mu\alpha^{*}\right).

(8bdb)

Additionally, the displacement operator

\hat{D}(\alpha)\coloneqq\exp\left(\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}\right)

(8be)

has the properties that

\displaystyle\left|{\alpha}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\hat{D}(\alpha)\left|{0}\right\rangle,

(8bfa)

\displaystyle\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{D}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta}{2}\right)\hat{D}(\alpha+\beta),

(8bfb)

where
\left|{0}\right\rangle
is the vacuum state, and
\left|{\alpha}\right\rangle
is the coherent state, satisfying the overlap formula

\left\langle\beta\middle|\alpha\right\rangle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}|\beta|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}+\beta^{*}\alpha\right).

(8bg)

Given
f(\alpha)=\mathcal{W}_{s}\left[{\hat{F}}\right]
[we drop the
(s)
superscript to avoid clutter], we shall consider both
\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]
and
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]}(\zeta,\lambda)
, where
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}}(\zeta,\lambda)
is the phase-space operator kernel of
\hat{F}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}}(\zeta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\zeta}\middle|{\hat{F}}\middle|{\lambda}\right\rangle,\quad\hat{F}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\zeta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\lambda}{\pi^{2}}\left|{\zeta}\right\rangle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}}\left\langle{\lambda}\right|.

(8bh)

C.1
Operator level

We write

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}{\pi^{2}}f(\alpha)g(\alpha)\hat{D}(\beta)\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(\alpha\beta^{*}-\alpha^{*}\beta+\frac{s}{2}|\beta|^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu}{\pi}\widetilde{f}(\mu)\widetilde{g}(\beta-\mu)\hat{D}(\beta)\exp\left(\frac{s}{2}|\beta|^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{f}(\mu)\widetilde{g}(\nu)\hat{D}(\mu+\nu)\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(\frac{s}{2}|\mu+\nu|^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{f}(\mu)\widetilde{g}(\nu)\hat{D}(\mu)\hat{D}(\nu)\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(\frac{\mu^{*}\nu-\mu\nu^{*}}{2}+\frac{s}{2}|\mu+\nu|^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\left[\widetilde{f}(\mu)\hat{D}(\mu)\exp\left(\frac{s}{2}|\mu|^{2}\right)\right]\\
&\quad\times\left[\widetilde{g}(\nu)\hat{D}(\nu)\exp\left(\frac{s}{2}|\nu|^{2}\right)\right]\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{op}}_{\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}}(\mu,\nu),\end{split}

(8bi)

where we have (i) rewritten the integral over
\alpha
as a convolution over
\mu
, (ii) done the change of variable
\nu=\beta-\mu
, and (iii) used Eq. (
8bfb
), in sequence. We can see that the
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
-twist factor

\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{op}}_{\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\exp\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\mu^{*}\nu+\frac{s-1}{2}\mu\nu^{*}\right)

(8bj)

encodes the operation of the hatted star product. Without it, we would trivially have
\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]=\hat{F}\hat{G}
. We particularly note how the twist factor is structurally similar to the differential form of the map given by Eq. (
8aa
), as is the case for the usual star product {cf. Eqs. (10) and (44) in Ref.
Soloviev 2015
}.

C.2
Operator-kernel level

Considering the third line in Eq. (
8bi
), we write

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]}(\zeta,\lambda)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{f}(\mu)\widetilde{g}(\nu)\left\langle{\zeta}\middle|{\hat{D}(\mu+\nu)}\middle|{\lambda}\right\rangle\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(\frac{s}{2}|\mu+\nu|^{2}\right).\end{split}

(8bk)

Now,

\begin{split}&\left\langle{\zeta}\middle|{\hat{D}(\mu+\nu)}\middle|{\lambda}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda+\mu+\nu\right\rangle\exp\left[\frac{(\mu+\nu)\lambda^{*}-(\mu+\nu)^{*}\lambda}{2}\right]\\
&=\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}|\mu+\nu|^{2}+\zeta^{*}(\mu+\nu)-\lambda(\mu+\nu)^{*}\right],\end{split}

(8bl)

so that

\begin{split}&\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]}(\zeta,\lambda)\\
&=\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{f}(\mu)\widetilde{g}(\nu)\\
&\quad\times\exp\left[\frac{s-1}{2}|\mu+\nu|^{2}+\zeta^{*}(\mu+\nu)-\lambda(\mu+\nu)^{*}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\nu}{\pi^{2}}\hat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{ker}}_{\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}}(\mu,\nu)\\
&\quad\times\left[\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle\widetilde{f}(\mu)\exp\left(\frac{s-1}{2}|\mu|^{2}+\zeta^{*}\mu-\lambda\mu^{*}\right)\right]\\
&\quad\times\left[\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle\widetilde{g}(\nu)\exp\left(\frac{s-1}{2}|\nu|^{2}+\zeta^{*}\nu-\lambda\nu^{*}\right)\right],\end{split}

(8bm)

where the operator-kernel level
\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}
-twist factor is given by

\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{ker}}_{\mathbin{\widehat{\star}_{s}}}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\exp\left[\frac{s-1}{2}(\mu\nu^{*}+\mu^{*}\nu)\right],

(8bn)

without which we would simply have
\left\langle\zeta\middle|\lambda\right\rangle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}^{-1}\left[{fg}\right]}=\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{F}}\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(s)}_{\hat{G}}
.

Introduction

1.1.
Main result

All graphs in this paper are finite and with no loops or parallel edges.

The Gyárfás–Sumner conjecture

For every forest

for all

.

(The case when

This conjecture is related to the following open problem of Erdős and Hajnal

;

(see

Conjecture 1.3

For every graph

The main result of this paper verifies such a substantial “off-diagonal Ramsey” improvement:

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists

such that every

-free graph

\alpha(G)\omega(G)^{d}\geq\lvert G\rvert
.

Though we make no attempt to optimise

Still,

1.2.
Background and discussion

A notable feature of Conjecture
1.1
is that it no longer holds if one allows the forbidden graph
T
to contain a cycle,
because a classical probabilistic argument of Erdős
MR102081
yields high-chromatic graphs with no short cycle (in particular triangle-free).
In fact, Erdős’ construction gives much more (see also
MR3524748
):

Theorem 1.5

(Erdős)
.

For every
g\geq 3
and every sufficiently large

, there are
n
-vertex graphs with no cycle of length at most
g
and with stability number at most
n^{\frac{g-1}{g}+o(1)}
.

(Spencer
MR491337
improved this slightly to
n^{\frac{g-2}{g-1}+o(1)}
.)
There are explicit constructions of this type, for example the Ramanujan graphs by Lubotzky, Phillips, and Sarnak
MR963118
(with exponent
\frac{3g-1}{3g}
).

For every two integers

and every graph

be the least integer

-free graph has a stable set of size at least

Then Conjecture

would imply that every forest

In view of

in the spirit of Conjecture

,
it suffices to prove a weakening of the conjecture that

for all forests

This relaxed statement was recently confirmed via the following result

For every

and every forest

-free graph

Stating that

for some universal

1.4

1.6

.

Theorem

-free graphs by polynomial functions of their clique numbers.
Recall that the

It is not hard to see that

is equivalent to
\rho(G)\leq\omega(G)^{d}

-free graphs

and with a little more work we can show that it is also equivalent to the following “polynomial

:

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists

such that
\chi^{*}(G)\leq\omega(G)^{d}
for all
P_{5}
-free graphs
G
.

[
Proof of the equivalence of
Theorems

1.4
and
1.7
]
It suffices to show that

1.4

1.7
.
To see this, let

be given by

1.4
, and let

-free graph.
By linear programming duality (see

2.
Notation and some proof ideas

For a graph
G
, let

denote the complement graph of

When there is no danger of ambiguity, for

we write

,

,

For disjoint

, the pair

is

if every vertex of

is adjacent in
G
to every vertex of

, and is

if
G
if it is complete in
\overline{G}
.
We also say that

is
complete
(or

) to
B
in
G
if the pair
(A,B)
is complete (or anticomplete, respectively) in
G
.
A

in

is a sequence
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
of disjoint subsets of

;
it is
complete
in
G
if
(B_{i},B_{j})
is complete in
G
for all distinct
i,j\in[k]
, and
anticomplete
in
G
if
(B_{i},B_{j})
is anticomplete in
G
for all distinct
i,j\in[k]
.
The method of proof of
Theorem

1.4
is via the following statement concerning high-
\rho
complete pairs or complete blockades in
P_{5}
-free graphs:

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists
d\geq 9
such that every
P_{5}
-free graph
G
with
\omega(G)\geq 2
contains either:

•

a complete pair
(X,Y)
with
\rho(X)\geq y^{9}\rho(G)
and
\rho(Y)\geq(1-3y)\rho(G)
for some
y\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
; or

•

a complete blockade
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
for some
k\geq 2
such that
\rho(B_{i})\geq k^{-d}\rho(G)
for all
i\in[k]
.

[
Proof of
Theorem

1.4
, assuming
Theorem

2.1
]
We claim that
d\geq 9
given by
Theorem

2.1
suffices.
To see this, we proceed by induction on
w:=\omega(G)
; we may assume
w\geq 2
.
By the choice of
d
,
G
contains either:

•

a complete pair
(X,Y)
with
\rho(X)\geq y^{9}\rho(G)
and
\rho(Y)\geq(1-3y)\rho(G)
for some
y\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
; or

•

a complete blockade
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
for some
k\geq 2
such that
\rho(B_{i})\geq k^{-d}\rho(G)
for all
i\in[k]
.

If the first bullet holds then either
\omega(X)\leq yw
or
\omega(Y)\leq(1-y)w
. If the former case holds then
1\leq\omega(X)\leq yw
(since
\rho(X)>0
) and by induction
\rho(G)\leq y^{9}\rho(X)\leq y^{9}\omega(X)^{d}\leq y^{9-d}w^{d}\leq w^{d}
by the choice of
d
;
and if the latter case holds then
1\leq\omega(Y)\leq(1-y)w<w
and so by induction

(1-3y)\rho(G)\leq\rho(Y)\leq\omega(Y)^{d}\leq(1-y)^{d}w^{d}\leq(1-y)^{9}w^{d}\leq(1-3y)w^{d}

where the last inequality holds since (note that

)

1-3y-(1-y)^{9}=y\sum_{j=0}^{8}(1-y)^{i}-3y\geq y\sum_{j=0}^{8}(3/4)^{j}-3y=y\cdot 4(1-(3/4)^{9})-3y\geq 0.

If the second bullet holds then
2\leq k\leq w
and there exists

with
\omega(B_{i})\leq w/k
; and so by induction we have

\rho(G)\leq k^{d}\rho(B_{i})\leq k^{d}(w/k)^{d}=w^{d}
.
This proves
Theorem

2.1
.

Much of the rest of this paper is thus devoted to proving

At a high level, the proof can be viewed as a combination of the density increment method in
(

, Section 7)

The main advantage of working with

In what follows, for

Since we are working with

Similar to a number of proofs of Erdős–Hajnal type results, we begin with Rödl’s theorem

.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]

such that every

We will apply this result with

and

If the

Here, for technical reasons involving

Thus we may assume that

At this point the situation is opposite to what happened in

Our approach to handle this is as follows. According to

-free graph contains either a linear-sized anticomplete pair or a complete blockade satisfying the second outcome of

Thus we can assume that every induced subgraph of the connected graph

Given such a “locally sparse” assumption, we can now adapt the “decomposing along anticomplete pairs” argument of
(

Our strategy will be using the adapted decomposition for

At the beginning, this ratio will be some small fixed constant resulting from

Provided that our desired complete pairs or blockades outcome as in

Now, starting from the second step, we will iteratively decrease

But such a cutset is always nonempty; and so when

Compared to the technique of

The second reason is, under the assumption that the first outcome and the

\eta
-sparse or
(1-w^{-2})
-dense induced subgraph
F
with
\lvert F\rvert\geq w^{-2C\log\eta^{-1}}\lvert G\rvert>w^{d-2C\log\eta^{-1}}\geq w^{2}
by the choice of
d
.
Hence, if
F
is
(1-w^{-2})
-dense then
w=\omega(G)\geq\omega(F)\geq\frac{\lvert F\rvert}{1+w^{-2}\lvert F\rvert}>\frac{1}{2}w^{2}\geq w
, a contradiction.
Thus
F
is
\eta
-sparse;
and so the choice of
\eta
gives an anticomplete pair
(A,B)
in
F
(and so in
G
) with
\lvert A\rvert,\lvert B\rvert\geq\eta\lvert F\rvert\geq\eta\cdot w^{-2C\log\eta^{-1}}\lvert G\rvert\geq w^{-3C\log\eta^{-1}}\lvert G\rvert=w^{-d}\lvert G\rvert
.
This proves
Theorem

5.3
.

We are now ready to show that the poly-
\rho
-bounding property is closed under disjoint union, via the following stronger result:

Theorem 5.4
.

For every forest
T
, there exists
d\geq 2
such that every non-complete
T
-free graph
G
contains an anticomplete pair
(A,B)
with
\rho(A),\rho(B)\geq\omega(G)^{-d}\rho(G)
.

[
Proof]We claim that
d\geq 2
given by
Theorem

5.3
suffices.
To see this, by shrinking
G
if necessary, we may assume
\psi(G)=\rho(G)
. Let
w:=\omega(G)
.
By the choice of
d
, there is an anticomplete pair
(A,B)
in
G
with
\lvert A\rvert,\lvert B\rvert\geq w^{-d}\lvert G\rvert
;
and so
\rho(A)\geq\psi(A)=\lvert A\rvert/\alpha(A)\geq w^{-d}\lvert G\rvert/\alpha(G)=w^{-d}\psi(G)=w^{-d}\rho(G)

and similarly
\rho(B)\geq w^{-d}\rho(G)
.
This proves
Theorem

5.4
.

The above argument can be straightforwardly modified to show that the off-diagonal Erdős–Hajnal property is closed under disjoint union; we omit the proof.
However proving this for the poly-
\chi^{*}
-bounding property requires a little more work, as follows.

Theorem 5.5
.

For every forest
T
, there exists
d\geq 2
such that every non-complete
T
-free graph
G
contains an anticomplete pair
(A,B)
with
\chi^{*}(A),\chi^{*}(B)\geq\omega(G)^{-d}\chi^{*}(G)
.

[
Proof]Let
T^{\prime}
be the forest obtained from
T
by adding a leaf to each vertex of
T
;
then no two vertices in
T^{\prime}
have a common neighbour.
Let
d\geq 2
be given by
Theorem

5.4
with
T^{\prime}
in place of
T
; we claim that
d
suffices.
To see this, let
G
be a non-complete
T
-free graph, and let
w:=\omega(G)
.
If
\chi^{*}(G)\leq w^{d}
then we are done;
and so we may assume
\chi^{*}(G)>w^{d}
.

Now, let
f\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
f(V(G))>0
and
f(V(G))\geq\chi^{*}(G)\cdot f(S)
for all stable sets
S
of
G
where equality holds for some such
S
.
Let
J
be the graph obtained from
G
by blowing up each
v\in V(G)
by a stable set
S_{v}
with
\lvert S_{v}\rvert=f(v)
.
Since
G
is
T
-free, it is
T^{\prime}
-free; and so
J
is
T^{\prime}
-free because
T^{\prime}
has no two vertices with a common neighbour.
Also,
J
is non-complete,
\lvert J\rvert=f(V(G))
,
\alpha(J)=f(S)
(so
\psi(J)=\chi^{*}(G)
), and
\omega(J)\leq\omega(G)\leq w
.
By the choice of
d
, there is an anticomplete pair
(A,B)
in
J
with
\rho(A),\rho(B)\geq w^{-d}\psi(J)>1
.
We may assume
J[A]
and
J[B]
are connected;
and so each of them has at least one edge.
Let
A^{\prime}:=\{v\in V(G):S_{v}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
and
B^{\prime}:=\{v\in V(G):S_{v}\cap B\neq\emptyset\}
.
Since each of
J[A],J[B]
is connected and has at least one edge,
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are disjoint and anticomplete to each other in
G
.
Moreover
\chi^{*}(A)\geq\rho(A)\geq w^{-d}\psi(J)=w^{-d}\chi^{*}(G)

and
\chi^{*}(B)\geq\rho(B)\geq w^{-d}\psi(J)=w^{-d}\chi^{*}(G)
.
This proves
Theorem

5.5
.

Evaluation of drug–drug interactions of a novel potent FLT3 inhibitor SKLB1028 in healthy subjects
Authors: Jingcheng Chen, Jingxuan Wu, Nini Guo, Yuqin Song, Lijun Li, Bingyan Wang, Jiangshuo Li, Mengyu Hou, Hang Yin, Meijuan Zhang, Yanhong Kong, Xiaofang Wu, Ran Li, Le Wu, Qiannan Gao, Ruihua Dong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSKLB1028 is a novel multi‐target protein kinase inhibitor under investigation for the treatment of FLT3‐ITD mutated acute myeloid leukemia. Based on the preclinical characterization of SKLB1028 metabolism, three drug–drug interaction clinical studies were performed to investigate the effects of itraconazole, rifampin (CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer, respectively), and gemfibrozil (CYP2C8 inhibitor) on the metabolism of SKLB1028. Fourteen healthy Chinese male subjects were enrolled in each study. In Study 1, subjects were administered a single dose of SKLB1028 (100 mg on days 1 and 11) and multiple doses of itraconazole (200 mg twice daily on day 8 and 200 mg once daily from days 9 to 18). Itraconazole was given with a loading dose on Day 8 and the total administration of itraconazole was 11 days. In Study 2, subjects were administered a single dose of SKLB1028 (100 mg on days 1 and 12) and multiple doses of gemfibrozil (600 mg twice daily from days 8 to 19). In Study 3, subjects were administered a single dose of SKLB1028 (150 mg on days 1 and 15) and multiple doses of rifampin (600 mg once daily from day 8 to 22). Itraconazole increased the AUC and C max of SKLB1028 by approximately 28% and 41%, respectively. Compared to the single drug, co‐administration with gemfibrozil increased the AUC of SKLB1028 by ~26% and the C max by ~21%. Co‐administration with rifampin reduced the AUC of SKLB1028 by ~30%, while the C max did not change significantly. All treatments were well tolerated in all three studies.

INTRODUCTION

FMS‐like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3), a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor encoded on chromosome 13q12, is vital for the hematopoiesis process and the occurrence of acute myeloid leukemia (AML). 1 , 2 FLT3‐ITD mutations are found in approximately 30%–35% of AML patients and are prognostic factors for poor survival. 3 SKLB1028, a novel oral multiple kinase inhibitor targeting EGFR, FLT3, and Abl, displays exceptional activity FLT3‐driven AML models and considerable potency in models of Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) harboring Abl mutants. 4 Preclinical studies showed that SKLB1028 had strong anti‐leukemia effects both in vivo and in vitro. The results of the Phase I/II dose escalation/expansion study conducted on relapsed refractory AML patients revealed that SKLB1028 demonstrated linear pharmacokinetics across the dose range of 20–310 mg/day. The accumulation ratios of SKLB1028 were 0.60~1.23 for C max and 0.80~1.11 for AUC after multiple doses. AML patients could benefit from administering SKLB1028 160 mg/day or higher doses, and the overall response rate was approximately 43%. SKLB1028 capsule monotherapy was well tolerated within the above dose range. A Phase III clinical trial of SKLB1028 in relapsed refractory AML patients is ongoing in China, and SKLB1028 shows promise as a treatment option.

Per regulatory guidelines, drug–drug interaction (DDI) studies are essential for the development of many new molecular entities (NMEs). 5 , 6 , 7 The results of clinical DDI studies can provide a reference for the clinical instruction of new drugs. 8 Preclinical studies indicated that SKLB1028 was a substrate of CYP2C8, CYP3A4, and P‐gp (data not data on file), and clinical DDI studies should be conducted to offer recommendations for the further use of SKLB1028 as guided. 5 , 6 , 7 Therefore, in the following SKLB1028 clinical DDI studies, itraconazole (a strong inhibitor of P‐gp and CYP3A4), gemfibrozil (a strong inhibitor of CYP2C8), and rifampin (a strong inducer of P‐gp and CYP3A4, and a moderate inducer of CYP2C8) were used to investigate on the influence of different perpetrators on the pharmacokinetics (PK) of SKLB1028. 6 , 7 , 9 In addition, the usage of some anti‐fungal drugs that could inhibit CYP3A, such as azoles, is common in patients with AML for the prevention and treatment of invasive fungal infections. Consequently, for the safety and efficacy of SKLB1028 in clinical use, it is vital to explore the effect of CYP3A4 inhibitors on the PK of SKLB1028. These studies evaluated how these perpetrators affected the pharmacokinetics of this novel potent FLT3 inhibitor, and assessed the safety and tolerance of co‐administrating SKLB1028 with the above perpetrators for the first time, which could offer important references for its further clinical use.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Overall study design

Three studies were included in the SKLB1028 clinical DDI research. All three studies were single‐center, single‐arm, open‐label, phase I clinical studies. The effects of itraconazole, gemfibrozil, and rifampin on the PK of SKLB1028 were evaluated in healthy subjects. The Ethics Committee of the Institute of Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University, approved all three studies and they were conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Guideline for Good Clinical Practice. All subjects signed informed consent forms before participating in and undergoing any study procedure.

Healthy male subjects aged 18–45 years, with a body mass index (BMI) between 19.0 and 26.0 kg/m2 and body weight ≥50 kg, were enrolled. During the screening period, vital signs, physical examination, medical history, electrocardiogram (ECG) tests, and clinical laboratory tests were conducted. Subjects who had clinically significant illnesses or those who used prescription drugs or any drugs affecting hepatic drug metabolism within 14 days of enrollment were excluded.

In the Phase III trial, the prescription of SKLB1028 was 150 mg per dose, twice daily, and the total usage of SKLB1028 was 300 mg per day. SKLB1028 was administered at 150 mg per dose in the enzyme induction part (rifampin group), consistent with the dose used in the Phase III trial. For the enzyme inhibition part (itraconazole and gemfibrozil groups), the dose of SKLB1028 was reduced to 100 mg per dose to avoid potential harm to subjects caused by the expected increase in SKLB1028 exposure in these two parts. In addition, previous studies had shown that SKLB1028 exhibited dose‐proportional pharmacokinetics, and using a lower dose (100 mg) was sufficient for evaluating drug interactions.

The selection of the sample size in these DDI studies was without statistical considerations and was not based on the variability of previous studies. In DDI studies, the bioequivalence confidence interval (CI) approach (80%–125%) was used to evaluate drug interactions. As a result, the minimum sample size recommended for DDI studies is 12 evaluable subjects. Twelve is the conventional sample size currently used in DDI studies. 5 , 10 , 11 Considering a 10% dropout rate, 14 participants were enrolled in each DDI study.

In Study 1, the effect of itraconazole at steady state on the PK of SKLB1028 was evaluated. For CYP3A4 inhibition, it was reported that pre‐exposure to itraconazole with a loading dose of 200 mg twice daily [BID] on the first day could achieve optimal inhibitory effects. 12 , 13 Therefore, the itraconazole regimen was administered at a loading dose (200 mg BID) on day 8, the first day of itraconazole administration. The enrolled subjects received a single oral dose of SKLB1028 capsules (100 mg) on day 1, after a 7‐day washout period, itraconazole capsules were administered from days 8 to 18 (200 mg twice daily on Day 8 and 200 mg once daily from Days 9 to 18). On Day 11, SKLB1028 capsules (100 mg) were co‐administered with itraconazole capsules.

In Study 2, we evaluated the influence of gemfibrozil on the PK of SKLB1028. The enrolled participants received a single oral dose of SKLB1028 capsules (100 mg) on day 1, and after a 7‐day washout period, gemfibrozil capsules (1200 mg per day, 600 mg twice daily) were administered from days 8 to 19. SKLB1028 capsules (100 mg) were co‐administered with gemfibrozil capsule on day 12.

In Study 3, the influence of rifampin on the PK of SKLB1028 was assessed. All enrolled participants received a single oral dose of 150 mg SKLB1028 capsules on day 1, after a 7‐day washout period, rifampincapsules (600 mg once daily) were administered from days 8 to 22. SKLB1028 capsules (150 mg) were co‐administered with rifampin capsules on day 15. The overall study design is shown in Figure 1.

Sample collections and PK analysis of SKLB1028

In all three studies, blood samples for the measurements of SKLB1028 plasma concentrations were collected before dosing and at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 144, and 168 h after SKLB1028 administration both in period 1 (SKLB1028 alone) and period 2 (co‐administration with the perpetrator). The timepoints for SKLB1028 PK collection were the same in both periods. All blood samples were centrifuged at 2000g for 10 min at 4°C. After centrifugation, the upper plasma was mixed well with ascorbic acid solution at a concentration of 50 mg/mL and then stored in a freezer at approximately −80°C until required for bioanalysis. Plasma concentrations of SKLB1028 were measured using a validated liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry method (Supplementary Method).

Safety evaluation

Adverse events (AEs) were recorded from the administration of the first dose of SKLB1028 until 2 weeks after the last dose of SKLB1028. Safety evaluation included laboratory tests (blood routine, blood biochemistry, and urine routine), vital signs, physical examination, 12‐lead electrocardiogram (ECG), and other evaluations. AEs were monitored throughout the study and assessed in terms of their duration, intensity, therapeutic measures, outcomes, and relationship with the study drugs.

Statistical analysis

The PK parameter set included all participants who received at least one dose of SKLB028 and who had at least one evaluable PK parameter measurement for SKLB1028. PK parameters were analyzed using non‐compartmental analysis (Phoenix WinNonlin software, version 8.2). All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software (version 9.4). A mixed effects model was used to analyze the main PK parameter, the area under the curve from zero to the time point (AUC0‐t ), the area under the curve from zero to infinite (AUCinf), and C max after natural logarithm transformation between co‐administration and administration SKLB1028 alone. In the model, the treatment group (presence or absence of the co‐administered drug) was a fixed effect, and the subject was a random effect. If there were missing values, they were assumed to be missing at random and not be processed. The geometric mean ratios (co‐administration/administration alone) were estimated, along with 90% confidence intervals, to evaluate the PK impact of itraconazole, gemfibrozil or rifampinon SKLB1028. Based on previous studies, the bioequivalence range (80%–125%) could ideally reflect drug interaction and is widely used in current DDI studies. 5 , 14 , 15 If the 90% confidence interval of the blood exposure ratio fell completely within the 80%–125% equivalence range, it was determined that there was no clinically significant drug interaction. 5

Safety data were analyzed for all subjects who received at least one dose of SKLB1028 and had post‐dose safety evaluation data.

RESULTS

Demographics

A total of 115 healthy male subjects were screened in this study, of which 73 screen failed and 42 were enrolled. Fourteen subjects were enrolled in each study. All 14 subjects completed the trial in Study 1, while 13 subjects completed Studies 2 and Study 3, respectively. In Study 2, one subject refused to be re‐examined after leaving the group. In Study 3, one subject withdrew before the combination administration in period 2 due to multiple premature ventricular contractions. The ages of the 42 subjects ranged from 20 to 44 years, and the BMI range was between 19.32 and 25.19 kg/m2. The baseline demographics of all 42 participants are shown in Table S1.

Effect of itraconazole on the PK of SKLB1028

The mean plasma concentration‐time results for SKLB1028 following a single oral dose of 100 mg SKLB1028, with or without itraconazole are illustrated in Figure 2. The pharmacokinetic parameters of SKLB1028 following administration of SKLB1028 alone or in combination with itraconazole are summarized in Table 1. Compared with SKLB1028 alone, the median T max of SKLB1028 didn't alter significantly (1.50 h vs. 1.00 h for administration alone vs. co‐administration). After co‐administration with itraconazole, the plasma exposure of SKLB1028 was higher, the average C max was increased (45.7 vs. 64.0 ng/mL), the mean AUC0‐t and AUCinf were increased (858 vs. 1100 ng·h/mL and 892 vs. 1150 ng·h/mL), and the mean t1/2 increased (35.4 vs. 43.6 h) (Table 1). The geometric mean ratios (co‐administration/administration alone) for the AUC0‐t , AUCinf, and C max of SKLB1028 were 127.83% (122.63%~133.25%) and 128.81% (123.5%~134.33%) and 141.17% (130.34%~152.90%), respectively (Table 2). The 90% of CIs for AUC0‐t and AUCinf did not completely fall within the range of 80.00%~125.00% of the no‐effect boundary, whereas the 90% CI of C max was higher than the upper limit of the no‐effect boundary range from 80.00% to 125.00%.

Effect of gemfibrozil on the PK of SKLB1028

The plasma concentrations of SKBL1028 slightly increased when SKLB1028 was co‐administered with gemfibrozil compared to SKLB1028 alone (Figure 3). The PK parameters of SKLB1028 following administrationalone or in combination with gemfibrozil, are listed in Table 1. Compared with SKLB1028 alone, the average C max increased from 44.0 to 53.8 ng/mL after co‐administration and the mean AUC0‐t and AUCinf also increased (886 vs. 1080 ng·h/mL and 902 vs. 1140 ng·h/mL), respectively (Table 1). The average t 1/2 prolonged from 39.9 to 48.0 h following co‐administration with gemfibrozil than that of SKLB1028 alone (Table 1). The median T max of the plasma concentration did not change significantly. As shown in Table 2, the geometric mean ratios (co‐administration/administration alone) for AUC0‐t , AUCinf and C max were 126.1(121.20%~131.20%), 127.25(122.39%~132.31%) and 121.35%(110.83%~132.87%), respectively. The corresponding 90% CIs did not completely fall within the equivalence acceptance range.

Effect of rifampin on the PK of SKLB1028

The mean plasma concentration‐time profiles of SKLB1028 following the administration of 150 mg SKLB1028 alone or in the presence of rifampin are illustrated in Figure 4, and the pharmacokinetic parameters of SKLB1028 are summarized in Table 1. The exposure (AUC) of SKLB1028 decreased. However, no significant change in the C max of SKLB1028 was observed after co‐administration with rifampin. The mean t 1/2 was shortened from 36.0 h (SKLB1028 alone) to 31.4 h (co‐administration with rifampin), and the average AUC0‐t and AUCinf were decreased (1370 vs. 972 ng·h/mL and 1410 vs. 1000 ng·h/mL), respectively (Table 1). The mean C max did not change significantly (76.1 vs. 74.5 ng/mL) (Table 1). The median T max did not change significantly between the two treatments. The ratios and 90% CIs of the geometric means of the PK parameters AUC0‐t , AUCinf, and C max of SKLB1028 combined with rifampin and SKLB1028 alone were 70.31% (66.35%~74.51%), 70.54% (66.54%~74.80%) and 97.16 (89.96%~104.93%), respectively (Table 2). The corresponding 90% CIs for AUC0‐t and AUCinf were outside the equivalence acceptance range. In comparison, the 90% CI of the C max was within the no‐effect boundary.

Safety

A total of 42 healthy subjects were enrolled in this study, all of whom were included in the safety analysis. A total of 30 subjects (71.4%) experienced treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAE), of which three subjects (7.1%) experienced TEAEs with severity ≥ grade 3 (CTCAE version 5.0). Among the three subjects (7.1%) who experienced TEAEs of severity ≥ grade 3, one (2.4%) had hypertriglyceridemia, and two (4.8%) had elevated creatine phosphokinase. Adverse events for each study are summarized in Table S2. No dosage adjustment or other interventions were required for these three subjects, and the outcome of the subjects was recovery. The severity of TEAEs in the remaining 27 subjects were all grade 1–2. There were no serious adverse events, TEAEs leading to dose reduction, TEAEs leading to suspension/permanent discontinuation, or TEAEs leading to death. One subject in Study 3 had a TEAE that led to withdrawal from the trial, namely, ventricular extrasystoles. The severity was grade 1 and it was determined that it was not related to the study drugs SKLB1028 and rifampin, and there was no need to monitor the subject, undergo dose adjustments, and other interventions and the outcome was recovery.

Common TEAEs (incidence ≥5%) included increased antithrombin III antibodies (19.0%), alanine aminotransferase (11.9%), and aspartate aminotransferase (11.9%), blood creatine phosphokinase (11.9%), oral ulcers (11.9%), and hypertriglyceridemia (11.9%). DISCUSSION

SKLB1028 is a novel potent FLT3 inhibitor used for the treatment of AML patients with FLT3 mutations. In vitro experiments indicate that SKLB1028 is a substrate of CYP2C8, CYP3A4, and P‐gp, suggesting that drug–drug interactions may occur when SKLB1028 is used in combination with inhibitors and/or inducers of CYP3A4/CYP2C8/P‐gp. Therefore, the aforementioned clinical DDI trials of SKLB1028 were conducted.

SKLB1028 is a substrate of CYP3A and P‐gp, and itraconazole is a potent strong inhibitor of both molecules. Co‐administration of SKLB1028 and itraconazole increased the C max and AUC by ~41% and 28%, respectively (Table 2). The T max did not change significantly between treatments. The t 1/2 of SKLB1028 prolonged from 35.4 to 43.6 h after co‐administration with itraconazole (Figure 2; Table 1). The increase of t 1/2 supported the inhibitory effect of itraconazole on CYP3A as well as P‐gp metabolism. 5 , 16 , 17 The magnitude of the increase in SKLB1028 exposure was slight, and the inhibition of SKLB1028 by itraconazole was weak. 5 , 6 , 7 For another FLT3 inhibitor, Giletitinib, a previous DDI study revealed that using a single oral dose of 10 mg Giletitinib in combination with itraconazole resulted in approximately 2.2 times higher exposure to Giletitinib. 18

The result was similar in the clinical drug interaction study of another FLT3 inhibitor, Quizartinib. 18 , 19 The combination of Quizartinib, another FLT3 inhibitor, with the CYP3A moderate inhibitor fluconazole, resulted in a 20% increase in the systemic exposure of Quizartinib compared to its single use, and the change in Quizartinib exposure was considered to be not clinically significant. 19 In our study, the inhibition of SKLB1028 by itraconazole was not as significant as that of Giletitinib and Quizartinib, possibly because SKLB1028 is metabolized by multiple metabolized enzymes, and CYP3A4 accounts for part of SKLB1028 metabolism (data on file). In addition, the combineduse of itraconazole is common in leukemia patients for the treatment of invasive fungal infections, and the slight inhibition of itraconazole on SKLB1028 may make it safer for subsequent clinical applications. Given that the C max of SKLB1028 in combination with itraconazole increased by ~41% compared to SKLB1028 alone, further research is needed to confirm whether this change is clinically significant.

As SKLB1028 is also the substrate of CY2C8, the effect of the strong CY2C8 inhibitor gemfibrozil on the PK of SKLB1028 was evaluated in Study 2. Co‐administration of gemfibrozil slightly increased C max and AUC by ~21% and 26%, respectively (Table 2). T max did not differ significantly between the single administration and co‐administration groups. Co‐administration with gemfibrozil prolonged SKLB1028 t 1/2 from 39.9 to 48.0 h, when compared with SKLB1028 alone. The prolong of t 1/2 was coincidence with the inhibition of CYP2C8 by gemfibrozil. 5 , 20 , 21 According to this PK analysis, the drug interaction between gemfibrozil and SKLB1028 was weak and not clinically significant. 22 , 23

Consistent with expectations, co‐administration of SKLB1028 with the enzyme‐inducer rifampindecreased the exposure of SKLB1028 in healthy subjects. Following the concomitant administration of SKLB1028 with rifampin, there was a slight decrease in the AUC by ~30% compared to that of SKLB1028 alone (Table 2). The average C max and T max of SKLB1028 didn't have meaningful change after its co‐administration with rifampin. After combining with rifampin, the t 1/2 of SKLB1028 decreased from 36.0 to 31.4 h (Table 1). The drug interaction of rifampin with SKLB1028 was not clinically significant. In addition, considering that patients could benefit from the dose of 160 mg per day or higher, and the recommended dose of SKLB1028 was 150 mg twice daily for further clinical use, dose adjustment of SKLB1028 was not essential when co‐administered with rifampin.

A single‐dose escalation(50–150 mg)trial of SKLB1028 was also performed in healthy subjects (data on file). PK analysis showed that SKLB1028 had a linear profile, and PK parameters were parallel between the dose escalation and current DDI trials. For instance, at the dose of 100 mg SKLB1028, the mean C max was 46.4 ng/mL (SAD 100 mg group), 45.7 ng/mL (DDI itraconazole group), and 44.0 ng/mL (DDI gemfibrozil group group). The AUC values were similar. The median AUC0‐t was 836.5, 858, and 866 ng·h/mL, respectively. SKLB1028 was well tolerated in the dose escalation trial, and no SAE or dose‐adjusted AEs occurred. All AEs resolved without any treatment interventions.

In all three studies, a single dose of SKLB1028, either alone or co‐administered with CYP3A4/CY2C8/P‐gp inhibitors or inducers, was well tolerated. Co‐administration of SKLB1028 with itraconazole/gemfibrozil/rifampin did not increase safety and tolerability‐related issues. All TEAEs were resolved, except for one TEAE in one subject who was lost to follow‐up. One subject experienced grade 3 hypertriglyceridemia in Study 1. The TEAE occurred during the combination period and was inferred to be associated with SKLB1028 and itraconazole. Although uncommon, itraconazole has been reported to be associated with hypertriglyceridemia. 24 , 25 Two subjects in Study 2 experienced a grade 3 blood creatine kinase increase. The TEAEs occurred with gemfibrozil administration alone and were illustrated in association with gemfibrozil. While the risk of developing myositis is very low when gemfibrozil is used as monotherapy, there was a case report that gemfibrozil‐induced myositis has been reported in a patient with normal renal function. 26 Research has also reported that the combination of gemfibrozil and simvastatin may increase the risk of myopathy. 26 , 27 , 28 This suggested that following CYP2C8 inhibition clinical DDIs, the monitoring of blood creatine kinase and renal function is important for the safety of enrolled subjects.

Liver enzyme elevations were observed in all three studies. In Study 1, Study 2, and Study 3, there were one subject, four subjects, and one subject experienced aminotransferase elevations, respectively. Two subjects in Study 2 experienced grade 2 aminotransferase increases, while the rest had grade 1 elevations. Among most subjects, five out of six, aminotransferase elevations occurred during the combination period and were determined to be associated with both SKLB1028 and specific perpetrators. The remaining subject in Study 2 endured gemfibrozil‐related grade 1 aspartate aminotransferase increased. Reports had suggested that Gilteritinib, a FLT3 inhibitor, had a moderate rate of serum aminotransferase elevations and could cause rare acute liver injury. 29 , 30 Similarly, previous clinical trials of SKLB1028 also reported serum aminotransferase elevations. Moreover, all perpetrators used in these DDI studies could potentially induce liver injury. 31 , 32 , 33 , 34 , 35 Therefore, liver enzyme elevations in our study should be attributed to the combined use of SKLB1028 and perpetrators. All liver enzyme elevations were resolved without treatment.

Firstly, across all three DDI studies, the exposure ratios of SKLB1028 induced by perpetrators were near the no‐effect boundary. Secondly, preclinical analysis of the concentration‐response relationship showed a clear dose–response relationship between the pharmacological indicators and the dosage of SKLB1028 (data on file). Therefore, under inhibition, the increase of SKLB1028 exposure indicated better pharmacodynamics. For the induction of SKLB1028, the recommended dose of SKLB1028 for further clinical use is 150 mg twice daily, and previous studies showed that the effective treatment dose of SKLB1028 was 160 mg/day. The slight reduction of SKLB1028 exposure induced by rifampin wouldn't affect its efficacy. Finally, previous findings indicated that SKLB1028 exhibited dose‐proportional pharmacokinetic properties at doses ranging from 20 to 310 mg, and was well tolerated. Consequently, it was expected that the inhibition of SKLB1028 by itraconazole or gemfibrozil, or the induction of SKLB1028 by rifampin was not clinically significant.

This drug interaction study was performed in healthy subjects, while as an FLT3 inhibitor for the treatment of leukemia, 36 , 37 SKLB1028 would be mainly used in leukemia patients. It will be combined with other therapeutic agents to reach a better therapeutic effect, as well as an increased risk to patients due to potential DDIs. Furthermore, it has been reported that the metabolism and clearance of certain drugs could be affected by the special pathological conditions of leukemia patients. 38 , 39 After combination with itraconazole, the geometric mean ratio (co‐administration/administration alone) of SKLB1028 for C max was 141.17% (130.34%–152.90%), which was slightly higher than the upper no‐effect boundary, and anti‐fungal drugs were commonly used during the treatment of leukemia. The exposure of SKLB1028 showed accumulation after multiple doses, therefore, we should monitor safety events when SKLB1208 is administered continually with itraconazole One limitation of our clinical DDI studies is that only male participants were enrolled. It is generally recognized that sex may have an impact on the absorption and pharmacokinetics of oral drugs. 40 However, preclinical animal studies have shown that SKLB1028 might have reproductive toxicity (unpublished data), and the enrollment of healthy female subjects might increase safety concerns. Moreover, according to preclinical studies, the pharmacokinetics of SKLB1028 did not show sexdifferences, which decreased the influence of sex on the analysis of potential drug interactions.

In summary, the combination of SKLB1028 capsules with itraconazole, gemfibrozil, or rifampin showed good safety and tolerability. Based on the results of this study, the changes in SKLB028 exposure after combination treatment with itraconazole, gemfibrozil or rifampin were weak and not clinically significant.

Optimizing dispatch factor in smart energy networks using cloud-based computational resources
Authors: Zain ul Abedin, Li Jianbin, Muhammad Siddique, Hafiz Muhammad Azib khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The coordination of eco-friendly power sources and brilliant matrix advances has changed the power lattice framework. Optimized dispatch of distributed resources assisted by cloud-based technologies, is inevitable in this evolving era of could computing. This paper provides a comprehensive framework of cloud-based load management technologies, with a focus on the dispatch factor as a crucial parameter in making energy dispatch decisions. Cloud computing provides grid administrators with the adaptability and computational power expected to advance energy dispatch continuously. The contributions of this research work about the optimized dispatch of power sources include i) formulation of constrained optimization objective function for power distribution network ii) proposed a novel algorithm for evaluation of the parametric values involved in proposed objective function. iii) proposed a framework for resourcing the computational burden to cloud computational platform. These contributions inculcates a methodology for efficient energy dispatch, highlighting the use of machine learning, optimization algorithms, and real-time data analytics to adjust the dispatch factor dynamically. The paper concludes with the discussion of results obtained after implementation of proposed methodology on google cloud platform which shows the effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

Introduction

The power matrix framework emerged as a result of the integration of green energy sources and the growth of smart network development. However, the intermittent nature of renewable energy generation and the dynamic nature of electricity demand pose significant challenges to grid operators in maintaining grid stability and reliability1. Load management plays a pivotal role in addressing these challenges by dynamically balancing supply and demand. In recent years, cloud-based technologies have emerged as promising solutions for efficient load management in smart grids2. Grid operators may maximize resource utilization and ensure grid stability in real-time by optimizing energy dispatch techniques through cloud computing’s flexibility, adaptability, and computational power3.

One of the key factors influencing load management in smart grids is the dispatch factor, which determines the allocation of energy resources to meet demand while minimizing costs and maximizing efficiency. It considers diverse parameters such as real-time load patterns, renewable forecasts, and dynamic pricing data, all of which can be processed efficiently using cloud-based platforms4. The dispatch factor takes into account several inputs (such as demand forecast, renewables output, grid constraints, and market trends) to determine the most efficient resource allocation5.

This paper explores cloud-based load management strategies in the context of dispatch factor optimization for smart grids. We present the challenges and opportunities involved in integrating cloud computing with traditional grid systems and propose new algorithms for efficient and adaptive resource allocation. Cloud-based load managers offer advantages over traditional methods6, including enhanced scalability, flexibility, and real-time optimization capabilities7. Furthermore, cloud computing enables the handling of massive datasets and computational loads required for advanced analytics and intelligent dispatching decisions8. The inherent adaptability of cloud-based frameworks allows for real-time adjustment to grid state variations and energy market fluctuations, thereby ensuring resilience under uncertain conditions9.

Despite these benefits, cloud-based load management systems face various challenges, particularly concerning data security, privacy, and reliability10. Interoperable cloud services must preserve the confidentiality of consumption patterns and pricing information before dissemination across grid layers11. Moreover, the reliance on third-party cloud platforms expands the trust boundary and exposes the system to new risks such as outages, data leakage, or privacy violations across jurisdictions12.

Recent advances in federated learning, cloud security, and mobile communication systems offer critical insights for addressing these concerns. For instance, incentive mechanisms for federated learning in vehicular networks13 demonstrate how communication-efficient distributed learning can be applied in edge-driven environments like smart grids. Security vulnerabilities in cloud-based object storage14 and intelligent blockchain application unpacking15 further underscore the need for secure orchestration in cloud-integrated systems.

In addition, scalable frameworks for distributed machine learning and mobile communication security such as DeepAutoD16 support the case for advanced cloud protection mechanisms. Emerging tools like graph neural networks for BGP community analysis17 and gradient shielding techniques to assess neural network vulnerabilities18 provide deeper understanding of resilience and robustness in AI-enabled dispatch optimization. These technologies complement our approach by addressing the latent challenges in distributed optimization, adversarial threat modeling, and real-time learning-based control.

Recent literature also emphasizes the importance of data-driven cloud-integrated methods for enhancing smart grid performance. A data-driven framework for net load uncertainty quantification has been developed to improve cloud energy storage management in residential microgrids19. Likewise, cloud-edge orchestrated dispatch frameworks have been proposed for real-time control of distributed energy resources20. Optimized scheduling approaches such as the fireworks algorithm21 and demand-side management schemes22 are increasingly leveraging cloud platforms. Other efforts include battery storage strategies to reduce residential energy bills23, and optimization of distributed energy resource dispatch at the grid edge24.

Moreover, deep learning-based optimal power flow models like MPGCN-OPF and GAT-ADNet have been applied to improve decision-making accuracy in distribution networks25,26. Reviews on cloud resource consumption optimization also offer comprehensive views on enhancing energy efficiency in utility operations27. The convergence of these advancements—spanning federated learning28, object storage security29, blockchain-based unpacking systems30,31, network behavior analysis via GNNs32, and DNN robustness techniques33 offers a rich foundation for enhancing the security, resilience, and intelligence of cloud-enabled smart grid management systems.

The dispatch factor for enhancing load management decisions and reducing risks associated with cloud-based solutions is analyzed in this context34. It considers a variety of influential factors including energy prices, demand predictions, and grid constraints to ensure cost-effective and secure power delivery35. Due to its complexity, dispatch factor optimization requires robust computational techniques36.

In this paper, we propose a novel method to optimize the dispatch factor using a cloud-based load management framework. Inspired by recent developments in machine learning, we leverage adaptive dispatching strategies that continuously profile grid states, analyze historical data, and predict demand trends in real time37,38. The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: Section "Theoretical background" presents the theoretical background of cloud-based load management and dispatch optimization. Section "System description" describes the system architecture, objective functions, and related constraints. Section "Methodology" details the methodology implemented using Google Cloud Platform (GCP) for dispatch factor optimization. Finally, Section "Simulation" presents the simulation and results, followed by conclusions in Section "Conclusion".

Theoretical background

In smart grid parlance, the dispatch factor is defined as the amount of current availability from a power plant or distributed energy resource (DER) over some time period divided by its potential maximum output during that same interval. This metric tells us how good a power generation unit is in portion to its full potential but taking into account the demand, conditions of the grid, and economic strategies that have been implemented with smart grid. In this context, “dispatch” entails deciding which power plant or its generators are turned on and off (and at what level they operate) to fulfill the electricity demand being placed against that portion of the grid in an optimal cost-efficient manner. This means it must be able to balance supply and demand constantly and also do this in a way that maintains reliability while keeping costs low as necessary and minimizes regulatory and environmental constraints.

A smart grid is an evolved and upgraded network which is being modernized with the latest technological tools like smart meters, automated control systems, sensors, and advanced data analytics. This allows supporting much finer and agile power generation control; all this translates in higher efficiency while improving reliability. In a smart grid, energy optimization aims to make better use of realtime facts about the status of different types and sources (including supply from renewables) to ensure that generation meets demand at a lower cost. Smart grids provide this flexibility by modifying the dispatch factor of resources according to demand, changing renewables availability, and dynamic grid conditions. By flexing the dispatch factor, those renewables i.e., solar and wind especially, but also storage to some extentavoid dumping output from other sources while contributing seamlessly. This is essential in terms of the economy because smart grids can analyze and change a dispatch factor so that an optimization problem can be solved leading to lower operational costs but higher grid efficiency39.

In addition, a proper dispatch factor also contributes to grid reliability and stability by ensuring an acceptable level of generation capability to serve load while covering ancillary services requirements. Overall, the dispatch factor in a smart grid context is a crucial measure for optimizing the performance and utilization of power generation assets, ensuring efficient, reliable, and cost effective energy delivery40. Furthermore, the cloud-based management platform supports collaboration and information sharing among stakeholders in the smart grid. The cloud makes it possible to host applications and services such as grid data analytics, so that utilities can be accessed remotely by consumers or any other third-party service providers who have an internet connection. By making data and analytics cheap, it democratizes the process of innovation internally to improve grid efficiency, reliability, and sustainability41.

System description

In42 the transmission network connects to several distribution systems through sub-stations. The buses associated with these substations are known as boundary buses and serve as the root buses for the distribution systems. From the transmission network’s perspective, these distribution networks appear as loads located at the boundary buses. On the other side, the distribution networks view the transmission system can be considered as a pseudo-generator, providing electric power to meet their load demands alongside Distributed Energy Resources (DERs). Coordinated scheduling of the generation resources within this system can enhance cost-effectiveness and improve resilience to wind variability. In this research work, the distribution network along with the DERs which is also fed from the transmission network is considered. As only the distribution network is considered, so the dispatching of those power sources (DERs) will optimized which are connected to the distribution network. Methodology

The goal of optimizing the dispatch factor for the Distribution network is to balance cost-effectiveness with robustness to uncertainties by coordinating the scheduling of generation resources. The objective function minimizes the total cost of power generation in the distribution systems, incorporating costs associated with generation, reserves, and generated power curtailment. This optimization problem is subject to a comprehensive set of constraints representing operational limits and power flow equations in the distribution networks. In the first step an optimization problem is defined as mentioned equation (1). The constraints include power balance, generation limits, ramping limits, and power flow limits. Subsequently, mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) and nonlinear programming (NLP) approach is adopted for managing both linear and nonlinear constraints for optimized solution of the objective function. For fast and efficient solution of the proposed objective funciton (1), high power computation is required which can be outsoureced to oogle Cloud Platform (GCP). To implement this optimization on GCP, software including Python and optimization libraries such as Pyomo, PuLP, or Gurobi, is installed on the VM of the GCP. The optimization code is developed in Python, defining variables for power generation, reserves, and power flow in the transmission and distribution systems, and implementing the objective function and constraints as defined in the problem formulation. The developed code is deployed on the GCP VM, where it is executed to utilize the computational resources efficiently. To automate and scale the optimization process, GCP is utilized based on predefined triggers or schedules. For larger datasets or more complex scenarios, GCP’s managed services, such as Kubernetes Engine, can be utilized to handle scaling. Data management and storage are handled using Google Cloud Storage for input data, intermediate results, and final optimization results, while BigQuery is used for large-scale data analysis and management tasks. The complexity of the optimization problem lies in satisfying all the given constraints, which include power balance, generation limits, ramping limits, and power flow limits. The constraints ensure that the solution is feasible and operationally sound. Solving this optimization problem on GCP leverages the platform’s computational power and scalability, enabling efficient and robust solutions. By efficiently utilizing GCP resources, the optimization process achieves faster convergence and improved computational efficiency, ensuring that the smartgrid system operates within its defined operational limits while minimizing costs. Zero-trust architecture and cross-border compliance

To operationalize security beyond traditional perimeter-based models, we propose a cloud-native Zero-Trust Architecture (ZTA) tailored for smart grid dispatch operations. This architecture enforces the principle of “never trust, always verify” through context-aware access controls, micro-segmentation of cloud services, and continuous monitoring. All user and service identities are authenticated and authorized dynamically using GCP Identity-Aware Proxy (IAP) and IAM Conditions.

To detect potential threats in real time, we integrate Google Cloud’s Security Command Center (SCC) for continuous anomaly detection. SCC provides centralized visibility into misconfigurations, suspicious activities, and potential vulnerabilities across cloud assets. It enables proactive risk mitigation through security health analytics, threat detection, and policy violation alerts—critical for maintaining operational integrity in power system applications.

Moreover, recognizing the importance of regulatory compliance in global deployments, we contrast two major legal frameworks: the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and China’s Data Security Law (DSL). GDPR emphasizes data minimization, user consent, and explicit rights to access and erasure, requiring that data of EU citizens remain within the European Economic Area (EEA) unless adequate protections are guaranteed. In contrast, DSL mandates strict government oversight and imposes restrictions on transferring data deemed important or critical outside China.

To address these regulations, we adopt region-specific data residency practices: data from EU regions is processed and stored within ‘europe-west1‘ or similar zones, while for Chinese deployments, edge-processing and localization strategies are employed. VPC Service Controls and customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK) further ensure that cross-border data flows are tightly regulated, and compliance obligations are met.

Conclusion

The use of the cloud in integration with smart grid infrastructure provides tremendous opportunities to enhance load management efficiency and increase the security of the Grid. A review in detail of the nature and theory loading mechanisms as well as some recently published research focusing on dispatch factor while optimizing efforts to allocate energy resources, from all we have learned so far cloud-based load management techniques. Utilizing the cloud’s scalability, flexibility, and computing power, grid operators can dynamically fine-tune dispatch decisions in real-time, factoring in energy prices, grid constraints, and demand forecasts.

Cloud-based solutions offer several advantages over traditional approaches, including scalability, real-time optimization, and adaptability to changing grid conditions. However, they also pose challenges related to data privacy, security, and reliability, which must be addressed through robust encryption mechanisms and stringent access controls. Moving forward, further research should explore advanced optimization algorithms and machine learning techniques to refine dispatch strategies and improve the resilience and sustainability of cloud-based load management systems in smart grids.

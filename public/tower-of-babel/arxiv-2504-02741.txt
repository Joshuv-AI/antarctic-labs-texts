Main results

In order to state our main results we will need some preparatory definitions. We follow the notation in

In this case, the map

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0;\,\lambda_{n}\in[-T,T]}|a(\lambda_{n})|<\infty

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}|a(\lambda_{n})|e^{-c|\lambda_{n}|}<\infty,

(for instance if

Definition 1

(Fourier summation pair)

A Fourier summation pair (FS-pair) is a pair

\mu

In view of this definition, the decay condition

3

1

The Nevanlinna Class

The higher order holomorphic Nevanlinna class
\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k}
is defined as the set of all holomorphic maps
F:\mathbb{C}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that for any choice of

\displaystyle\left[i\frac{F(z_{n})+\overline{F(z_{m})}}{z_{n}-\overline{z_{m}}}\right]_{1\leq n,m\leq N}

has at most
k
negative eigenvalues (counting multiplicities).
We define

\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k}-\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k}:=\{F-G;F,G\in\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k}\}.

For more information, see Section 3.

The following is the main result of this paper. It states that if we have a FS-pair
(\mu,a)

\mathbb{C}^{+}

What is a FS-pair? It is a function in the following class

\bigg(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k}-\mathfrak{N}_{\leq k})\bigg)\cap\bigg(\bigcup_{c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{+}+ic)\bigg).

Lemma 5

(The Bridge Lemma)
.

If

(19)

uniformly for

in the region

Fix a

small and write

. Observe that
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
has compact support with

and

. For

. Take a function

, let

. Then, for fixed

(21)

which converges pointwisely to

(22)

for

, which we prove in the end. Since

holds for any

. Note that, for each fixed

,

and

.

uniformly in

All that remains to be proven is (

where we used the elementary fact about convolutions that if

6.
Examples of FS-pairs

We now give some examples of FS-pairs (see also the last section in
[
14
]
). We focus on the measures not contemplated by the result in
[
14
]
, that is, those pairs
(\mu,a)
such that
\deg(\mu)\geq 3
.

6.1.
The Selberg Trace Formula

One example of an FS-pair is the Selberg Trace formula. If
S
is a compact hyperbolic surface then, for any even
\varphi\in\ C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds that

\sum_{j\geq 0}\widehat{\varphi}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{2\pi}\right)=\frac{A(S)}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}r\widehat{\varphi}(r)\tanh(\pi r)dr+2\pi\sum_{\gamma\in G(S)}\frac{\Lambda(\gamma)}{N_{\gamma}^{1/2}-N_{\gamma}^{-1/2}}\varphi(\log(N_{\gamma})),

(73)

where
A(S)
is the hyperbolic surface area of
S
,
r_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
is a solution for
\lambda_{j}=1/4+r_{j}^{2}
, where
\{\lambda_{j};j\geq 0\}
is the Laplacian spectrum on
S
,
G(S)
is the set of closed oriented geodesics on
S
. If
\gamma
is a closed geodesic on
S
, then
N_{\gamma}=e^{l(\gamma)}
is the norm of
\gamma
and
\Lambda(\gamma)=l(\gamma_{0})
the length of
\gamma
, where
\gamma_{0}
is the unique oriented prime geodesic satisfying
\gamma=\gamma_{0}^{m}
, for some integer
m\geq 1
. Here,
l(\gamma)
is the hyperbolic length of the curve
\gamma
. For more details, see
[
4
, Thm. 5.6]
. Since
\tanh(\cdot)
is odd, by symmetrizing (
73
), we obtain the following identity, which holds for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{A(S)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\left[\widehat{\varphi}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{2\pi}\right)+\widehat{\varphi}\left(-\frac{r_{j}}{2\pi}\right)\right]-\int_{\mathbb{R}}r\widehat{\varphi}(r)\tanh(\pi r)dr=

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{2}}{A(S)}\sum_{\gamma\in G(S)}\frac{\Lambda(\gamma)}{N_{\gamma}^{1/2}-N_{\gamma}^{-1/2}}\left[\varphi(\log(N_{\gamma}))+\varphi(-\log(N_{\gamma}))\right].

(75)

This gives a FS-pair
(\mu,a)
with

\mu=\sum_{j\geq 0}({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{r_{j}/2\pi}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{-r_{j}/2\pi})-r\tanh(\pi r)\mathrm{d}r

a(\pm\log(N_{\gamma}))=\frac{\Lambda(\gamma)}{N_{\gamma}^{1/2}-N_{\gamma}^{-1/2}}
, for
\gamma\in G(S)
.

Observe that
\deg(\mu)=3
because of the contribution of the absolutely continuous part and the fact that
\sum_{j\geq 0}r_{j}^{-2-\varepsilon}<\infty
for
\varepsilon>0
, which is a consequence of the Spectral Theorem for compact hyperbolic surfaces (see for instance
[
4
, Thm. 3.32]
). More generally, by taking (
74
) for two compact hyperbolic surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, and taking the difference, we obtain a crystalline measure:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A(S_{1})}\sum_{j\geq 0}\left[\widehat{\varphi}\left(\frac{r_{j}}{2\pi}\right)+\widehat{\varphi}\left(-\frac{r_{j}}{2\pi}\right)\right]-\frac{1}{A(S_{2})}\sum_{m\geq 0}\left[\widehat{\varphi}\left(\frac{s_{m}}{2\pi}\right)+\widehat{\varphi}\left(-\frac{s_{m}}{2\pi}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{A(S_{1})}\sum_{\gamma\in G(S_{1})}\frac{\Lambda_{S_{1}}(\gamma)}{N_{S_{1},\gamma}^{1/2}-N_{S_{1},\gamma}^{-1/2}}\left[\varphi(\log(N_{S_{1}\gamma}))+\varphi(-\log(N_{S_{1}\gamma}))\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi}{A(S_{2})}\sum_{\gamma\in G(S_{2})}\frac{\Lambda_{S_{2}}(\gamma)}{N_{S_{2},\gamma}^{1/2}-N_{S_{2},\gamma}^{-1/2}}\left[\varphi(\log(N_{S_{2}\gamma}))+\varphi(-\log(N_{S_{2}\gamma}))\right],

where
\{1/4+r_{j}^{2};j\geq 0\}
and
\{1/4+s_{m}^{2};m\geq 0\}
are the spectra of the Laplacian in
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, respectively. Again the measure
\mu=\sum_{j\leq 0}({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{r_{j}/2\pi}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{-r_{j}/2\pi})-\sum_{m\leq 0}({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{s_{m}/2\pi}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{-s_{m}/2\pi})
has degree at most
3
.

6.2.
A crystalline measure involving the sum of three squares function
r_{3}(n)

Recall that, by Legendre’s three square theorem, a non-negative integer
n
can be written as the sum of three squares of integers if, and only if,
n
is not of the form
4^{a}(8b+7)
for non-negative integers
a,b
. We define the arithmetic function
r_{3}(n)
to be the number of ways of representing
n
as the sum of three squares of integers, this is,
r_{3}(n):=\#\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3};|m|_{2}^{2}=n\}
. We have
r_{3}(0)=1
and
r_{3}(n)=0
if
n
is of the form
4^{a}(8b+7)
. By a result of
[
11
]
, it holds that, for any positive
\varepsilon

\sum_{n\leq x}r_{3}(n)=\frac{4}{3}\pi x^{3/2}+O(x^{3/4+\varepsilon}).

(76)

Working out on an old example of Guinand, Meyer in
[
23
, Thm. 4]
constructed the crystalline measure

\mu=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\chi(n)r_{3}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\left({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\sqrt{n}/2}-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{-\sqrt{n}/2}\right),

(77)

and he proved that
\widehat{\mu}=-i\mu
. Here, the character
\chi
is defined by

\chi(n)=\begin{cases}-1/2,&\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash 4\mathbb{N}\\
4,&\text{ if }n\in 4\mathbb{N}\backslash 16\mathbb{N}\\
0,&\text{ if }n\in 16\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(78)

This gives rise to an FS-pair
(\mu,-i\mu)
. Because of (
76
) and the fact that
r_{3}(4n)=r_{3}(n)
, a simple integration-by-parts argument shows that
\deg(\mu)=3
.

6.3.
A generalized family of Guinand’s measure

In the last section of
[
14
]
, a construction of Guinand’s was generalized. Let
\eta(z)=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})
be Dedekind’s eta-function, where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
. Consider now the following family

\displaystyle\frac{\eta(z)^{24c-2}\eta(4z)^{24c-2}}{\eta(2z)^{48c-5}}

\displaystyle=q^{c}\left(1-(24c-2)q+(288c^{2}-36c)q^{2}+O(q^{3})\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\alpha_{n,c}q^{n+c},

of modular forms for a real number
c\in[0,1/8]
. Consider then the measure

\mu_{c}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\alpha_{n,c}({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\sqrt{n+c}}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{-\sqrt{n+c}}).

It is shown in
[
14
]
that
\widehat{\mu}=\mu
, that is,
(\mu,\mu)
is a FS-pair. We note that
\mu_{0}=\sum_{\mathbb{N}\in\mathbb{Z}}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{n}
produces Poisson’s summation and
\mu_{1/9}
is Guinand’s construction in
[
15
]
, although he did not came up with his construction this way. We also notice that if
c>1/8
, the coefficients
|\alpha_{n,c}|
grow exponentially (but some exceptional values of
c
) and so
(\mu,\mu)
is not a FS-pair. However, it still possible to generate a summation formula although only for test functions
\varphi(x)
which extend analytically and decay sufficiently fast in a strip
|{\rm Im}\,z|<b
, for a suitable
b>0
. For
c\in[0,1/8]
, numerical experiments indicate that the coefficients oscillate erratically in the interval
[-1,1]
, which nevertheless would imply that
|\alpha_{n,c}|\leq 1
. Provably, the Hecke bound shows that
|\alpha_{n,c}|\ll_{c}n^{1/4}
, and so
\deg(\mu_{c})\leq 3
(and conjecturally
\deg(\mu_{c})=3
).

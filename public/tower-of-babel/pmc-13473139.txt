Hypertension prevalence and control in selected townships of Lingcheng District, Dezhou City, China: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Zhonghe Li, Hua Li, Guiling Ma, Naidong Pang, Zhimin Yue, Yuping Ma, Liangmei Du, Zhiqiu Xu, Lili Yin, Zhongsu Yang, Xianchen Meng, Lin Zhang, Xingpeng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite the increasing prevalence of hypertension (HTN) in China, the rates of treatment and control remain uncertain. This study aims to evaluate the prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control rates of HTN in selected townships. From May 1, 2024, to July 31, 2024, a random sampling survey was conducted involving 11,600 adult residents across seven community health service centers in Lingcheng District, Dezhou City, Shandong Province, China. Following a standardized rest period of 5 to 10 min, trained healthcare professionals measured blood pressure using a validated oscillometric monitor. According to the 2024 Chinese guidelines for HTN, HTN is defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) of ≥ 140 mmHg or a diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of ≥ 90 mmHg, as well as individuals with a self-reported history of HTN who are currently taking antihypertensive medication. Normal-high blood pressure (NHBP) is characterized by an SBP of 120 to 139 mmHg or a DBP of 80 to 89 mmHg in the absence of antihypertensive medication. HTN control is defined as blood pressure readings of < 140/90 mmHg. Additionally, the prevalence and control rate of HTN were estimated using a cutoff of 130/80 mmHg, in accordance with the 2025 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology High Blood Pressure Guideline. The age-adjusted prevalence of HTN was 32.2%, while the age-adjusted prevalence of NHBP was 30.9%. Among patients diagnosed with HTN, the age-adjusted awareness rate was 57.4%, the treatment rate was 50.0%, and the control rate was 38.0%. Of those receiving treatment, 56.1% were prescribed a single antihypertensive medication, 31.7% required a combination of two drugs, and 12.2% needed three or more antihypertensive medications. According to the 2025 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology guideline, the age-adjusted prevalence of HTN was 57.3%, with a control rate of only 14.7%. This cross-sectional study indicated a high prevalence of HTN in selected townships of Lingcheng District. Although the awareness, treatment, and control rates of HTN have improved, they remain significantly below the desired levels. This finding underscores the substantial challenges associated with HTN management at the grassroots level.Keywords: Hypertension, Prevalence, Awareness, Control, Township, ChinaSubject terms: Cardiology, Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Hypertension (HTN), a significant risk factor for cardiovascular diseases, continues to present a considerable public health challenge in China. Despite substantial efforts by the Chinese government and healthcare professionals, the levels of awareness, treatment, and control of HTN in China remain markedly lower than those in developed countries. A study conducted by Chinese scholars Wang et al. in 20181, which analyzed data collected from October 2012 to December 2015, indicated that although there had been notable advancements in these areas, the rates in China were still approximately two to three times lower than those reported in Europe and the United States in 20022.

In recent decades, the Chinese government, in collaboration with HTN professionals, has actively sought to address this issue. Initiatives such as the annual World HTN Day, endorsed by the World HTN Alliance and the Chinese HTN Alliance, have been pivotal in raising awareness and implementing grassroots HTN prevention and control programs. The strategic theme of “Healthy China, Healthy People” has significantly guided these efforts, resulting in nationwide activities focused on enhancing blood pressure management.3.

The Chinese government’s issuance of the medium-and long-term prevention and control plan for chronic diseases, including HTN and diabetes, has significantly enhanced the country’s ability to manage these conditions. The “Internet plus Community Health Management Service Standardization Construction Guide” has been widely adopted across community health service centers, leveraging mobile internet and the internet of things for the “three diseases co-management” of HTN, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes4. These technologies have been effectively implemented in community medical centers, increasing residents’ awareness and engagement in their own health management.

On World HTN Day 20245, we conducted blood pressure measurements and surveys across seven community populations. This study aimed to assess the current status of HTN prevention and control in selected township health centers within Lingcheng District, Dezhou, Shandong Province, China. By analyzing the collected data, we seek to offer effective recommendations for the future prevention and control of HTN in similar township settings in northern China that share comparable socio-economic and healthcare resource profiles.

Methods

Study population and data collection

This cross-sectional study enrolled 12,513 permanent residents who were selected through a random sampling strategy from seven representative townships in Lingcheng District, Dezhou, Shandong Province, China, between May 1, 2024, and July 31, 2024. Data collection involved the administration of standardized questionnaires and triplicate blood pressure measurements conducted at seven designated community healthcare facilities. To ensure measurement validity, all procedures were performed by certified clinicians who received training specific to the protocol, which included standardized positioning techniques and device calibration procedures. After excluding participants with incomplete data or protocol deviations, the final analytical cohort consisted of 11,600 eligible subjects, yielding a response rate of 92.7%. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of HTRM Cardiovascular Hospital (2024-4-9-1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Distribution of gender and age

The study population demonstrated a balanced gender distribution across all age groups. Most participants were aged between 30 and ≥ 70, with no individuals under 18 years or over 100 years included. This demographic composition produced a working sample that excluded both pediatric and extreme elderly populations while preserving gender parity throughout the adult age range.

Blood pressure measurement protocol

To ensure methodological rigor and data accuracy, all blood pressure measurements were conducted by certified family physicians who were specifically trained in the study protocol. These medical professionals underwent comprehensive training regarding the study’s objectives, operational methodologies, and specialized measurement equipment. The standardized measurement protocol was developed and implemented by the Central Coordination Unit of the Smart One-Stop HTN Management Center at Dezhou HTRM Cardiovascular Hospital, in accordance with evidence-based clinical guidelines.

Prior to measurement, participants were seated and allowed to rest for 5 to 10 min. Brachial blood pressure measurements were obtained from the right upper arm using a clinically validated oscillometric device (HEM-77A1T, Omron Corporation, Kyoto, Japan), with the arm maintained at heart level throughout the procedure. Following standardized protocols, three consecutive measurements were recorded at 60-second intervals under strict observational control. A trained observer supervised all procedures to ensure adherence to measurement guidelines. The mean value derived from the three technically adequate readings was calculated for final statistical analysis. This standardized protocol adhered to international blood pressure measurement guidelines, thereby ensuring methodological rigor and data reproducibility in the study.

Definitions and classifications

According to the 2024 Chinese HTN Guidelines4, HTN is defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) of ≥ 140 mmHg (1 mmHg = 0.133 kPa) or a diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of ≥ 90 mmHg, determined through standardized clinical assessments. Additionally, individuals with a self-reported history of HTN who are currently taking antihypertensive medication are classified as hypertensive, even if their measured blood pressure during the visit is < 140/90 mmHg. Normal-high blood pressure (NHBP) is characterized by an SBP of 120 to 139 mmHg and a DBP of 80 to 89 mmHg, without the use of antihypertensive medication. Awareness is defined as the self-report of any prior diagnosis of HTN by a physician. Treatment refers to the current use of prescribed antihypertensive medications for at least two weeks prior to patient assessment, highlighting the importance of therapeutic adherence. Control indicates that sustained blood pressure levels remain below 140/90 mmHg (SBP < 140 mmHg and DBP < 90 mmHg), with both thresholds needing to be concurrently satisfied. The detection rate is defined as the proportion of participants with either uncontrolled HTN (SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 among those with a history of HTN) or newly diagnosed HTN, relative to the total screened population. Furthermore, the prevalence (SBP ≥ 130 or DBP ≥ 80 mmHg) and control rate (SBP < 130 and DBP < 80 mmHg) of HTN were estimated in accordance with the 2025 American Heart Association (AHA) /American College of Cardiology (ACC) High Blood Pressure Guideline6.

Body mass index (BMI) classification categorizes a BMI of 24 to < 28 kg/m² as overweight, while a BMI ≥ 28 kg/m² is classified as obesity4,7. Participants were divided into three groups according to smoking status: current smokers, past smokers, and nonsmokers. Additionally, participants were classified based on the frequency of alcohol consumption into three categories: daily, weekly, and no or occasional use (regardless of quantity per occasion).

Drug therapy classification is determined by the number of active drugs rather than the number of pills. Monotherapy refers to the administration of single-ingredient Western medications, including Traditional Chinese Medicine formulations, which are considered a single “drug” even if they contain multiple herbs. Combination therapy involves the use of two or more drugs, which may consist of separate pills with different active ingredients or single pills containing combined active ingredients.

Statistical analysis

The study aimed to provide precise estimates of HTN prevalence by age and sex within the Lingcheng District population aged 18 years and older. Participants were categorized into HTN, NHBP, and normal groups according to the classification criteria outlined in the 2024 Chinese HTN guidelines. We calculated the frequency and percentage of participants with HTN across various dimensions, including age, sex, BMI, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and family history of HTN. Chi-square tests were utilized to assess the correlation between these factors and HTN. For categorical variables with multiple categories, additional analyses were performed using multiple comparisons. Survey weights were derived from the 2020 China population census data8, and adjustments for differential selection probabilities was implemented to enhance the representativeness of the survey sample. Multivariate logistic regression was employed to analyze the factors influencing the occurrence and management of HTN. A p-value of less than 0.05 was established as the threshold for statistical significance. Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.2.1.

Results

Demographic overview

In this study, we analyzed data from 11,600 individuals across seven townships in the Lingcheng District of Dezhou City. Among the participants, 48.6% (5,637) were male, while 51.4% (5,963) were female. Individuals aged 18 to 39 comprised 18.9% (2,198), whereas those aged 40 to 70 and older represented 81.1% (9,402). No participants exceeded 100 years of age. Within the various age groups, males and females were matched (Fig. 1). Approximately 92% of participants had their blood pressure measured multiple times in the past year, while 8% had never measured it. Patients with HTN were generally older, exhibited a higher BMI, and demonstrated a greater prevalence of frequent alcohol consumption, as well as a higher percentage of family history of HTN. Additionally, HTN patients showed an increased incidence of comorbidities, including diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, stroke, heart failure, arrhythmia, and chronic kidney disease. These findings underscore the importance of screening and managing HTN. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1.

The distribution of gender across age groups adheres to a normal distribution pattern. The gender ratio is nearly balanced, with males constituting 48.6% and females making up 51.4% of the total participants. Notably, more than 90% of the participants are over 30 years old.

Prevalence of HTN

The overall mean SBP and DBP were 119 (109, 131) mmHg and 78 (71, 85) mmHg, respectively. The crude prevalences of HTN and NHBP were 39.6% (4599/11600) and 27.6% (3207/11600), respectively. The age-adjusted prevalence of HTN was 32.2%, while the age-adjusted prevalence of NHBP was 30.9%. The prevalence of HTN exhibited a gradual increase with age, rising from 13.0% to 54.5% (p < 0.001). A significant upward trend in HTN prevalence was observed across higher BMI categories, increasing from 19.2% to 56.2% (p < 0.001). Additionally, the prevalence of HTN rose with alcohol consumption, escalating from 38.5% to 57.7% (p < 0.001). Both male sex and current smoking status were associated with a higher proportion of patients exhibiting NHBP. No statistically significant difference was found between males and females regarding HTN prevalence (40.4% versus 39.0%, p = 0.128) (Table 2).

The crude prevalence of HTN, as defined by the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline using the 130/80 mmHg threshold, was 61.6% (7150/11600), with an age-adjusted prevalence of 57.3%. Awareness, treatment, and control of HTN

In this study, we identified that 3,450 of the 11,600 screened adults had a prior diagnosis of HTN, while 1,149 were newly diagnosed during the screening. Among the 4599 HTN patients, the crude rates of awareness, treatment, and control were 75.0% (3,450/4,599), 69.9% (3,214/4,599), and 51.8% (2,384/4,599), respectively. The crude rates of awareness, treatment, and control are presented in Table 3; Fig. 2. Notably low rates of awareness, treatment, and control were observed among younger participants (under 40 years old), males, current smokers, individuals who consume alcohol weekly, and patients without a family history of HTN. The age-adjusted rates for awareness, treatment, and control were 57.4%, 50.0%, and 38.0%, respectively. When compared to national data from 2012 to 2015 (awareness: 46.9%, treatment: 40.7%, control: 15.3%) reported by Wang et al., our study demonstrated absolute increases of 10.5, 9.3, and 22.7% points, which correspond to relative increases of approximately 22.4%, 22.9%, and 148.4%, respectively.

Antihypertensive medicine

Among the patients receiving treatment, 56.1% (1,803/3,214) were prescribed a single antihypertensive medication, while 31.7% (1,020/3,214) required a combination of two drugs, and 12.2% (391/3,214) needed three or more antihypertensive medications. Among those on monotherapy, the most frequently prescribed medications were calcium channel blockers (40.9%, 737/1,803), followed by renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (28.4%, 512/1,803), diuretics (13.7%, 247/1,803), and β-receptor blockers (11.5%, 207/1,803). A small proportion of patients were treated exclusively with Traditional Chinese Medicine (5.5%, 99/1,803). In the cohort receiving dual antihypertensive therapy, 29.5% (301/1,020) were administered renin-angiotensin system inhibitors and calcium channel blockers, 28.3% (288/1,020) received renin-angiotensin system inhibitors and diuretics, 26.6% (271/1,020) were given calcium channel blockers and diuretics, and 15.6% (159/1,020) were treated with other combinations. Among patients receiving three or more antihypertensive medications, calcium channel blockers, renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, diuretics, and/or β-receptor blockers were commonly utilized.

Assessment of risk factors for HTN

Multivariable logistic regression was employed to examine factors associated with HTN. The analysis revealed that age, BMI, alcohol consumption, and family history of HTN were statistically correlated with the prevalence of HTN, as detailed in Table 5. Notably, the risk of HTN increased progressively for individuals over the age of 50. Those classified as overweight or obese exhibited a heightened risk of developing HTN. Additionally, frequent alcohol consumption was linked to an increased risk of HTN. A family history of HTN emerged as the most significant risk factor, with an odds ratio of 4.27 (95% confidence interval 3.812–4.783). In contrast, no statistical association was found between gender or smoking and the prevalence of HTN. Consequently, reducing alcohol intake and maintaining a healthy weight are critical strategies for the prevention and management of HTN.

Assessment of risk factors for blood pressure control in HTN patients

The analysis revealed several variables significantly associated with the likelihood of achieving target blood pressure. Specifically, patients who had quit smoking, those of advanced age, and individuals with a family history of HTN exhibited higher rates of blood pressure control. In contrast, male patients and those who consumed alcohol frequently were linked to lower control rates. These findings indicate that smoking cessation may facilitate better disease management, whereas heavy alcohol consumption may hinder optimal blood pressure control.(Table 6)

Detection and characteristics of HTN

Among adults in the seven townships studied, the overall detection rate of HTN was 19.1%, which includes 1,066 individuals with uncontrolled HTN and 1,149 newly diagnosed cases (2,215/11,600). This rate is calculated by summing the number of uncontrolled and newly diagnosed hypertensives and dividing by the total sample size. The findings indicate that 5.6% of participants had isolated systolic hypertension (ISH), 5.5% had isolated diastolic hypertension (IDH), and 8.0% exhibited both systolic and diastolic HTN. Notably, 80.9% of participants maintained blood pressure levels below 140/90 mmHg.

Important role of community doctors

Our findings indicate that 53.8% (1856/3450) of HTN patients had not been diagnosed in specialized hospitals, highlighting the essential function of community doctors in identifying undiagnosed cases. Furthermore, we observed that 61.2% (1460/2384) of hypertensive patients managed by professional community doctors met the 2024 Chinese HTN control standard, emphasizing the significance of community doctors in the management of hypertensive patients.

Discussion

HTN status in some townships of Lingcheng District: key findings

This study identified five key findings regarding HTN management in select townships of Lingcheng District, Dezhou City. First, the age-adjusted prevalence of HTN was 32.2%, while the age-adjusted prevalence of NHBP was 30.9%. Second, among hypertensive patients, 57.4% were aware of their diagnosis, 50.0% received antihypertensive treatment, and 38.0% achieved blood pressure control (defined as < 140/90 mmHg). Third, the prevalence of HTN was significantly higher among individuals who were older, overweight or obese, consumed alcohol, or had a family history of HTN. Fourth, patients who had quit smoking, those of advanced age, and individuals with a family history of HTN exhibited higher rates of blood pressure control. In contrast, male patients and those who consumed alcohol frequently were linked to lower control rates. Fifth, community healthcare providers play a role in the prevention and control of HTN at the township level.

Prevalence of HTN

The age-adjusted prevalence of HTN was found to be 32.2%, surpassing the 23.2% reported by scholar Wang from 2012 to 20151. However, our prevalence was slightly lower than the 37.2% reported by scholar Lu, who studied approximately 1.7 million adults aged 35–75 years across 31 provinces in mainland China from 2014 to 20179. Furthermore, the prevalence of HTN in this study was significantly lower than the 55.7% reported by scholar Xu, who gathered data from 70,380 residents aged 35–75 years in Inner Mongolia, northern China, from 2015 to 201710. Over the past two decades, the prevalence of HTN in China has risen markedly, drawing the attention of national and local health authorities. In response, initiatives led by the World Hypertension Alliance and the China Hypertension Alliance have been implemented nationwide to promote the vision of a healthier China. These efforts have reached both urban and rural areas, with a primary strategy involving the establishment of HTN prevention and control centers within community health service centers.

Awareness, treatment, and control rates

This study demonstrated an improvement in the management of HTN, with awareness, treatment, and control rates increasing by 10.5, 9.3, and 22.7% points, respectively, compared to data from 2012 to 2015. These findings indicate that the selected townships in Lingcheng District, Dezhou City, have made significant progress in the prevention and management of HTN. This progress can be attributed to two key factors: (1) Enhanced hierarchical management of chronic diseases: In recent years, China has strengthened its hierarchical healthcare system, particularly for chronic conditions such as HTN, hyperlipidemia, and hyperglycemia. The management of these conditions has been prioritized at community medical centers, where healthcare professionals regularly monitor individuals diagnosed with these conditions, provide health education, promote lifestyle modifications (e.g., low-salt, low-sugar, and low-fat diets), and develop personalized treatment plans based on individual health profiles. (2) Increased health awareness and accessibility: With China’s economic development, public awareness of personal health has improved. When abnormal medical data is detected, individuals can promptly consult or visit hospitals at various levels without the need for prior appointments. This convenient access to medical care may be associated with the improved control rates observed in this setting. However, the awareness rate and control rate remains lower than that in North America in 2015 (57.4% vs. 80.0% and 38.0% vs. 55.0%)11,12.

IDH and gender

The detection rate of IDH was higher in males than in females. IDH predominantly affects individuals aged 18–49. Numerous epidemiological studies have demonstrated that IDH is more prevalent among young and middle-aged populations, with the incidence rate in males significantly exceeding that in females. A study focusing on individuals aged 35–49 revealed that the incidence of IDH in males is 2–3 times higher than in females13. Several potential explanations for this disparity include the following: (1) The pathogenesis of IDH is associated with increased peripheral vascular resistance and heightened sympathetic excitability. Notably, physiological responses in males are often more pronounced than those in females14. (2) Various studies have identified and confirmed that IDH correlates with an elevated risk of target-organ damage and cardiovascular events15,16. (3) Males demonstrate a significantly higher prevalence of unhealthy lifestyle habits, particularly excessive alcohol consumption, compared to females. This notable gender disparity may be closely linked to elevated blood pressure levels, indicating a clear relationship between behavioral patterns and cardiovascular health risks.

The role of community health centers and physicians in the management of HTN

The primary function of community health service centers is to emphasize the prevention and early detection of HTN. These centers are tasked with public education regarding HTN management, promoting self-monitoring of blood pressure at home, and serving as a conduit for timely diagnosis17. When abnormal blood pressure readings are identified, residents are urged to seek immediate consultation at the local community health center, which enables early diagnosis and initial assessment to ascertain the necessity of antihypertensive treatment.

Physicians at community health centers play a crucial role in the long-term, systematic management of HTN. Acting as dedicated primary care physicians for each district resident—similar to family doctors—they are responsible for controlling risk factors, strategically combining antihypertensive medications, and monitoring potential drug-related adverse effects. They offer comprehensive lifestyle guidance and implement systematic follow-up protocols17,18. Treatment plans are individualized, incorporating one or more antihypertensive agents. This strategy has yielded significant success, resulting in increased awareness, treatment, and control rates of HTN.

Limitations

The current study has several limitations. First, the sample size is relatively small and geographically concentrated in seven townships within the Lingcheng District of Dezhou City, Shandong Province. Consequently, the findings may not accurately represent the overall status of HTN prevention and control throughout China. Second, although we have identified common and related risk factors, further research is necessary to investigate the differences among patients with isolated HTN, those with HTN combined with hyperglycemia, those with HTN combined with hyperlipidemia, and those with all three conditions (HTN, hyperglycemia, and hyperlipidemia). Third, this study does not provide additional evidence for widely recognized risk factors for HTN, such as gender and smoking. Fourth, while the current control standard for HTN is < 140/90 mmHg, further analysis is needed to assess the potential benefits of achieving even lower blood pressure levels for hypertensive patients. Fifth, we acknowledge that recruiting participants through township health centers may have oversampled individuals who are more health-conscious and have better access to care, which could lead to an overestimation of awareness and control rates and an underestimation of the true prevalence. Sixth, the lack of detailed socioeconomic and educational data is also a limitation, as these factors are important determinants in the epidemiology of HTN. Seventh, due to the cross-sectional design, we cannot establish a causal relationship between community doctors and health centers and the management of HTN; thus, future longitudinal studies are warranted.

Conclusions

The prevalence of HTN remains high in selected townships of Lingcheng District, Dezhou City, Shandong Province, according to the findings of this cross-sectional study. Despite improvements in awareness, treatment, and control rates among local residents, they still significantly lag behind management targets. Enhancing residents’ health awareness and treatment adherence is urgently needed to improve blood pressure control and alleviate the burden of cardiovascular disease.

On primitivity and reduction for half-flag-transitive block designs
Xiaoqin Zhan

Abstract
Let 𝒟 = ( 𝒫 , ℬ ) \mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}) be a 2 2 - ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) design, and let G G be a half-flag-transitive automorphism group of 𝒟 {\cal D} . In this article, we first establish three sufficient conditions for G G to be point-primitive: (i) λ ≥ ( r , 2 ​ λ ) 2 \lambda\geq(r,2\lambda)^{2} , (ii) r > 4 ​ λ ​ ( k − 2 ) r>4\lambda(k-2) , (iii) ( v − 1 , 2 ​ k − 2 ) ≤ 2 (v-1,2k-2)\leq 2 . Next, we prove that for λ ≥ ( r , 2 ​ λ ) 2 \lambda\geq(r,2\lambda)^{2} , the group G G is either of affine type, almost simple type, or product type. Finally, we analyze the case where G G is of almost simple type and prove that if the socle of G G is a sporadic simple group then G ≅ HS G\cong\text{HS} and 𝒟 \cal D is either the unique 2 2 - ( 176,128 , 15240 ) (176,128,15240) design or the unique 2 2 - ( 176,160 , 19080 ) (176,160,19080) design. It is well-known that the Block’s Lemma
[
2

states that a block-transitive automorphism group

[

proved that if the number of points

must be point-primitive.
In 1984, Camina and Gagen
[
6

gave the result that the block‐transitive automorphism group

must be flag‐transitive, moreover,

is point‐primitive. As a generalization of this result, Zhang and Zhou
[

study block‐transitive automorphism groups of nontrivial
2
‐
(v,k,\lambda)

is block‐transitive, then
G
must be flag‐transitive, and furthermore,

is point‐primitive of affine or almost simple type.

In the case where

is a flag-transitive automorphism group, there have been relatively more research achievements in this area. Flag-transitive
2‐(v,k,1)
designs are point-primitive by
[
12
]
. Delandtsheer and Doyen
[
9
]
also proved that a flag-transitive, point-imprimitive
2
-design satisfies

. It follows immediately that, for a flag-transitive point-imprimitive
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)

In 1965, Dembowski

prove the following result:

Proposition 1.1

Suppose that

is a flag-transitive automorphism group of a
2
-design

(a)

\lambda>(r,\lambda)\big[(r,\lambda)-1\big]
, in particular

;

(b)

(c)

;

(d)

(v-1,k-1)\leq 2
.

Classifying flag-transitive 2-designs with the aforementioned parameter restrictions is a highly meaningful work.
However, the primary step in such classification is to employ the O’Nan-Scott theorem for reduction of primitive automorphism groups. Building upon foundational work in design theory and permutation group theory, several key results have been established regarding the classification of flag-transitive automorphism groups in 2-designs with specific parameter in Proposition
1.1
.

Case (a):

The seminal work by Buekenhout et al.
[
5
]
demonstrated that any
2
-
(v,k,1)
design
\mathcal{D}
admitting a flag-transitive (and consequently point-primitive) automorphism group must necessarily be of either affine or almost simple type. This result was extended by Zieschang
[
22
]
in 1988, who established that for any
2
-design satisfying
(r,\lambda)=1
, the flag-transitive automorphism group
G
is similarly constrained to these two types. Further refinement was achieved by Zhou et al.
[
20
,
14
]
, who proved that this classification holds more generally for flag-transitive automorphism groups of
2
-designs with
\lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2}
.

Cases (b) and (c):

Subsequent research
[
18
,
21
]
has revealed that for
2
-designs satisfying
(r-\lambda,k)=1
, any flag-transitive automorphism group
G
must again be of affine or almost simple type. A significant advancement was made by Zhao
[
21
]
in 2022, who established that under the more general condition
r>\lambda(k-3)
, the flag-transitive automorphism group

becomes a primitive permutation group that may additionally admit product type structure alongside the affine and almost simple types.

Case (d):

The classification problem takes an interesting turn when considering designs with
(v-1,k-1)\leq 2
. As shown in
[
13
, Corollary 4.6]
, the flag-transitive automorphism group

in such cases must either act
2
-transitively on points or exhibit rank
3

\frac{3}{2}
-transitivity. Through application of
[

is of affine or almost simple type. The authors further extend this analysis to cases where

. This comprehensive approach culminates in the application of reduction theorems for flag-transitive
2
-designs to the special case where

Similar to the study of flag-transitive 2-designs, we focus on half-flag-transitive 2-designs in this paper.

\lambda<(r,2\lambda)^{2}

(ii)

Let

\cal P
, where
|C_{i}|=w\geq 2
(

The half-flag-transitivity implies that if

then
|\overline{B}|=k_{1}
or

with
1\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq w-1

\mathcal{D}_{i}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}_{i})

2

(v,\{k_{1},k_{2}\},\lambda)

\frac{r}{2}(k_{1}+k_{2}-2)=\lambda(w-1).

(5)

As
\lambda(s-1)=\lambda(v-1)-\lambda(v-s)

\lambda(s-1)=\frac{r}{2}[2k-2-s(k_{1}+k_{2}-2)]=\frac{r}{2}(2s-2-D),

(6)

where
D=s(k_{1}+k_{2})-2k
.
As
\lambda<r
and
\lambda(s-1)>0
then
0<D<2s-2
. Next, let

6

\frac{r}{2l}\mid(s-1)
. We put
s-1=\frac{hr}{2l}
, where

, hence

\displaystyle l=\frac{rh}{2(s-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{hk}{2(s-1)}=\frac{hs(k_{1}+k_{2})}{4(s-1)}-\frac{hD}{4(s-1)}>\frac{h(k_{1}+k_{2})}{4}-\frac{h}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{h(k_{1}+k_{2}-2)}{4}\geq\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{4},

where

\frac{r}{2l}

\lambda\leq\frac{2\lambda(w-1)l}{r}\leq l(k_{1}+k_{2}-2)<4l^{2}=(r,2\lambda)^{2}.

Therefore,

\hfill\square

4.1

First suppose that

is of simple diagonal type. Let

, where each
T_{i}
(
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
) is a non-abelian simple group and
T_{i}\cong T
. Then
G
is a subgroup of
W
, where

W=\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\pi\mid a_{i}\in\text{Aut}(T),\pi\in S_{m},\,a_{i}\equiv a_{j}\pmod{\text{Im}(T)}\text{ for all }i,j\}.

Proposition 4.1

Let
(\mathcal{D},G)
satisfy Hypothesis. Then
G
is not of simple diagonal type.

Proof.

For a point
\alpha\in{\cal P}
, we have

T\cong\{(a,\ldots,a)\mid a\in T\}=N_{\alpha}\leq G_{\alpha}\leq W_{\alpha},

where
W_{\alpha}=\{(a,\ldots,a)\pi\mid a\in\text{Aut}(T),\pi\in S_{m}\}\cong\text{Aut}(T)\times S_{m}
. Clearly,
v=|N:N_{\alpha}|=|T|^{m-1}
.
From the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
, there is a nontrivial orbit
\Delta
of
G_{\alpha}
on
\cal P
such that

|\Delta|\leq m|T|
. Thus, by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3

|T|^{m-1}=v<\frac{r^{2}}{(r,2\lambda)^{2}}<m^{2}|T|^{2}.

It follows that
m=2
or
m=3
.

Since
G_{\alpha}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(T)\times S_{m}
and
\frac{r}{2}\mid|G_{\alpha}|
, then
\frac{r}{2}
divides
|T||\mathrm{Out}(T)|m!
. From equation (
2
), we have

\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}(2k-2)=\frac{2\lambda}{(r,2\lambda)}(|T|^{m-1}-1).

It follows that
\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}
divides
|T|^{m-1}-1
, that is,
\left(\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)},|T|\right)=1
, which implies
\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}
divides
|\mathrm{Out}(T)|m!
. Therefore, by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, we get

|T|^{m-1}=v<\frac{r^{2}}{(r,2\lambda)^{2}}<|\mathrm{Out}(T)|^{2}m!^{2}.

Thus
|T|<4|\mathrm{Out}(T)|^{2}\leq 6|\mathrm{Out}(T)|^{2}
if
m=2
;
|T|<6|\mathrm{Out}(T)|\leq 6|\mathrm{Out}(T)|^{2}
if
m=3
, contrary to Lemma
2.4
.
\hfill\square

4.2
Twisted wreath type

Suppose that

is of twisted wreath type. Then
N=\mathrm{Soc}(G)\cong T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{m}
with
m\geq 6

[
15
]
, where each
T_{i}\cong T
(
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
) is a nonabelian simple group. Also,
N
is regular on point set
{\cal P}
. Let
\alpha\in{\cal P}
, then
G=N\rtimes G_{\alpha}
and
v=|T|^{m}
.

Proposition 4.2

Let
(\mathcal{D},G)
satisfy Hypothesis. Then
G
is not of twisted wreath type.

Proof.
Define subsets

\Gamma_{i}=\alpha^{T_{i}}

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have that
\Gamma_{i}\cap\Gamma_{j}=\{\alpha\}
for any
i\neq j
. Then, by the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have that

\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{i}-\{\alpha\}
is the union of some nontrivial
G_{\alpha}
-orbits, and

|\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{i}-\{\alpha\}|=m(|T|-1).

By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\mid m(|T|-1)
. Thus, from Lemma
2.1

|T|^{m}=v<\frac{r^{2}}{(r,2\lambda)^{2}}<m^{2}|T|^{2}.

As
T
is a nonabelian simple group, therefore

60^{m-2}\leq|T|^{m-2}<m^{2}.

It follows that
m\leq 2
, which contradicts the fact
m\geq 6
.
\hfill\square

4.3
Product type

Next, suppose that
G
has a product action on
\cal P
. Then there is a group
K
with a primitive
action (of almost simple or diagonal type) on a set
\Delta
of size
v_{0}>5
, where

{\cal P}=\Delta^{m},\,\,G\leq K^{m}\rtimes S_{m}=K\wr S_{m},\,\,\text{and}\,\,m\geq 2.

Proposition 4.3

Let
(\mathcal{D},G)
satisfy Hypothesis. If
G
is of product type then
G\leq K\wr S_{2}
acting on
{\cal P}=\Delta\times\Delta
with rank
3
, where
K
is a
2
-transitive group on
\Delta
with odd size.

proof.
Let
H=K\wr S_{m}
, and let
S_{m}
act on
M=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Since
G
is half-flag-transitive, Lemma
2.3
implies
[G_{\alpha}:G_{\alpha\beta}]=|\beta^{G_{\alpha}}|\geq\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}
for any two distinct points
\alpha,\beta
. Therefore

[H_{\alpha}:H_{\alpha\beta}]\geq[G_{\alpha}:G_{\alpha\beta}]\geq\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda(v-1)}{(r,2\lambda)(k-1)}.

(7)

Let
\alpha=(\gamma,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma),\,\gamma\in\Gamma,\,\beta=(\delta,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma),\,\gamma\neq\delta\in\Gamma
and let
B\cong K^{m}
be the base group of
H
. Then

B_{\alpha}=K_{\gamma}\times\cdots\times K_{\gamma},\quad B_{\alpha\beta}=K_{\gamma\delta}\times K_{\gamma}\times\cdots\times K_{\gamma}.

Now
H_{\alpha}=K_{\gamma}\wr S_{m}
, and
H_{\alpha\beta}\geq K_{\gamma\delta}\times(K_{\gamma}\wr S_{m-1})
. Suppose
K
has rank
s
on
\Delta
with
s\geq 2
. We can choose a
\delta
satisfying
[K_{\gamma}:K_{\gamma\delta}]\leq\frac{v_{0}-1}{s-1}
, so that

[H_{\alpha}:H_{\alpha\beta}]=\frac{|H_{\alpha}|}{|H_{\alpha\beta}|}\leq\frac{|K_{\gamma}|^{m}\cdot m!}{|K_{\gamma\delta}||K_{\gamma}|^{m-1}\cdot(m-1)!}\leq m\frac{v_{0}-1}{s-1},

and hence by inequality (
7
),

\frac{\lambda(v-1)}{(r,2\lambda)(k-1)}\leq[G_{\alpha}:G_{\alpha\beta}]\leq m\frac{v_{0}-1}{s-1}.

(8)

So

\frac{v_{0}^{m}-1}{v_{0}-1}\leq m\frac{(r,2\lambda)(k-1)}{\lambda(s-1)}.

(9)

Now
k-1\leq\sqrt{(r-1)(k-1)}<\sqrt{r(k-1)}<\sqrt{\lambda v}
and
\sqrt{\lambda}\geq(r,2\lambda)
yield

v_{0}^{m-1}<\frac{v_{0}^{m}-1}{v_{0}-1}<mv_{0}^{\frac{m}{2}}\frac{(r,2\lambda)}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\leq mv_{0}^{\frac{m}{2}}.

(10)

Hence
m\leq 2
, or
m=3
and
v_{0}<9
.

If
m=3
, the inequality (
10
) reduces to
v_{0}^{2}+v_{0}+1<\frac{3v_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{s-1}
, so that
v_{0}=5
or 6 and
s=2
. Now,
S_{v_{0}}\wr S_{3}
has subdegrees
\{1,3(v_{0}-1),3(v_{0}-1)^{2}),(v_{0}-1)^{3}\}
. Since
G\leq S_{v_{0}}\wr S_{3}
,
v_{0}\in\{5,6\}
and Lemma
2.3
imply
\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\mid v_{0}-1
which contradicts the inequality
\frac{r^{2}}{(r,2\lambda)^{2}}>v=v_{0}^{3}
. Hence
m=2
.

From inequality (
9
), we have

v_{0}+1\leq\frac{2(r,2\lambda)(k-1)}{\lambda(s-1)}<\frac{2(r,2\lambda)v_{0}}{\sqrt{\lambda}(s-1)}\leq\frac{2v_{0}}{s-1}.

This implies
s=2
. It follows that
K
acts 2-transitively on
\Delta
, and
H=K\wr S_{2}
has rank 3 with subdegrees 1,
2(v_{0}-1)
,
(v_{0}-1)^{2}
.
Now
G\leq H
, so each subdegree of
H
is the sum of some subdegrees of
G
and so

\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\mid 2(v_{0}-1).

If

\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\neq 2(v_{0}-1),

then

\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\leq v_{0}-1,

so that

v_{0}^{2}=v<\frac{r^{2}}{(r,2\lambda)^{2}}\leq(v_{0}-1)^{2},

which is a contradiction. Thus

\frac{r}{(r,\lambda)}=2(v_{0}-1).

From inequality (
8
), we obtain that
G
must have a subdegree
2(v_{0}-1)
and it follows that
G
induces a 2-transitive group
\overline{G}\leq K
on
\Delta
. We conclude that
G
itself has rank 3 on
\cal P
with subdegrees
\{1,2(v_{0}-1),(v_{0}-1)^{2}\}
. Therefore,

\frac{r}{(r,2\lambda)}\mid(v_{0}-1)^{2},

i.e., 2
\mid v_{0}-1.
So
v=v_{0}^{2}
is an odd number.
\hfill\square

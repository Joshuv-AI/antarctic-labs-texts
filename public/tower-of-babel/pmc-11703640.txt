Adropin a candidate diagnostic biomarker for cardiovascular disease in patients with chronic kidney disease
Authors: Maha Abd El Moneem Elfedawy, Samia Abd El Sadek Elsebai, Hend Mohamed Tawfik, Eman Refaat Youness, Moushira Zaki
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic kidney disease (CKD) is a chief worldwide health concern that has a substantial financial impact on health systems, high rates of mortality and morbidity as well as cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a major cause of mortality in this population. Adropin is a unique hormone encoded by the energy homeostasis-associated (Enho) gene.Aim of the workWe aimed to explore the efficacy of adropin as a diagnostic candidate biomarker for CVD in patients with CKD.MethodsThis is prospective study was carried out on 60 patients (Pt) with CKD and 30 age and sex matched healthy control subjects. CKD Pt were classified according to the history of CVD into two groups: Group A, Pt without history (n = 32) and Group B, Pt with history (n = 28). Serum adropin, lipids and Hs-CRP were measured by ELISA kit. Echocardiography was also investigated. Receiver operator characteristic curve (ROC) was used to determine cut-off points of adropin. Negative predict value (NPV), negative predict value (NPV) and area under curve were detected.ResultsThere were abnormal ECGs in 78.6 % of CKD patients. Adropin was significantly decreased in Group B than Group A and control group. On the other hand, serum lipids and Hs-CRP were significantly increased in Group B than Group A and control group. ROC analysis revealed that serum adropin could be used to discriminate between patients with and without CVD history at a cutoff level of > 304 with 46.4 % sensitivity and 84.4 % specificity, 74.8 % PPV, 61.2 % NPV and AUC = 0.57. Moreover, between Group A and control at a cutoff level of < 410, with 93.8 % sensitivity, 86.7 % specificity, 87.6 % PPV and 93.3 % NPV and AUC = 0.97 as well as between Group B and control group at a cutoff level of < 416, with 57.1 % sensitivity, 83.3 % specificity, 77.4 % PPV and 66 % NPV and AUC = 0.65.ConclusionParticularly in CKD patients, adropin may be a useful biomarker for predicting the onset of CVD. Adropin may represent a novel and useful blood marker for assessing systolic function and Spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD).Keywords: Adropin, Cardiovascular disease, Chronic kidney disease, Predictive values, Sensitivity, Specificity, SCAD

Background

Cardiovascular risk and its related complications are higher in patients with CKD1. Several major risk factors that contribute to the development of CVD. Adropin playing a crucial role in regulating lipid and glucose metabolism and maintaining energy homeostasis. Regardless of the cause, an expected glomerular filtration rate (e GFR) below 60 ml/min/1.73 m2 or kidney damage that lasts for three months or longer is referred to as CKD 2. It is a disease where kidney function progressively deteriorates and renal replacement remedy (dialysis or transplantation) becomes necessary3. The 2012 KDIGO CKD classification divides glomerular filtration rate into six classes (G1 to G5, with G3 divided into 3a and 3b) and suggests information regarding the etiology of the CKD4, 5. Any of the three types of disease processes that cause CKD) can be pre-renal (lower renal perfusion pressure), intrinsic (vessel, glomerular, or tubule-interstitium pathology), or post-renal obstructive5. In the United States, CKD affects roughly 13 % of the population. In India, diabetes and cardiovascular disease account for about 800 cases per million of chronic kidney disease cases 6. The most recent WHO data available indicates that 21,087 deaths in Egypt in 2020 were from kidney disease, or 3.93 % of all deaths7. Egypt is ranked 46th in the world with an age-adjusted mortality ratio of 32.58 per 100,000 of population8. The onset of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and the subsequent CKD development continue to be major causes of decreased life expectancy and high rates of premature death 9. When the glomerular filtration rate is less than 15 mL/min, ESRD is diagnosed. After diabetic nephropathy, hypertension is the second most common cause of ESRD in the US 6, 10. The progressive nature of end-stage renal disease necessitates prompt renal replacement therapy in order to avert death. Patients with end-stage renal illness have much higher death rates than people without the condition11. The procedure known as hemodialysis is used to remove extracorporeal materials from the blood. Hemodialysis complications are linked to renal failure and should be closely monitored, evaluated, and minimized 12. For ESRD patients, hemodialysis is a life-saving procedure, but during the course of the procedure, various complications can arise 6, 13. Numerous hormonal and metabolic disorders can arise as a result of ESRD14. The role of myokines and adipokines in regulating metabolism has garnered increased attention in recent times 15. Renal function is impacted by age-related problems in CKD, which is one of the main causes of mortality and morbidity in renal disease caused by CVD 13. Vascular dysfunction is brought on by CKD, which also raises the risk of CVD16. Age, obesity, smoking, diabetes, and hypertension are common CVD risk factors and comorbidities seen in people with CKD 17. These factors and comorbidities alone can compromise vascular integrity, including endothelial function, and lead to CVD 18. CVD is the most prevalent cause of death in ESRD patients, representing over half of deaths with a recognized cause19. Furthermore, the fundamental reason behind mysterious deaths in ESRD has been proposed to be undiagnosed CVD 20. It is still unknown what pathophysiology contributes to the augmented significance of CVD in patients on dialyzed treatments. The identification of high-risk cardiovascular patients is imperative in order to avoid cardiac events and mortality 21. Finding novel and effective markers of cardiovascular function in ESRD patients will therefore be important22. The recently identified poly-peptide hormone adropin has wide-ranging effects on energy homeostasis and metabolism23, 24. Adropin is a novel metabolic hormone that has been found in the liver and brain that regulates the metabolism of fats and glucose 25. Heart failure (HF) and coronary atherosclerosis are two conditions that are exacerbated by adropin 26. The finding that adropin seems to be a potential biomarker for endothelial dysfunction and CVD supports this 22. Hormone called adropin is generated locally in various tissues27. The kidneys' metabolization of both myokines is unknown. As a result, adropin has been found to be expressed significantly more in the kidney tissues than in the salivary glands, brain, umbilical vein, pancreas, kidney, liver, muscle, or intestines 28. Since renal insufficiency is typically associated with cardiovascular disease, we therefore postulated that adropin levels may be different in HD patients 29. The aim of this work is to explore the diagnostic accuracy of adropin for CVD in CKD patients and emphasizing its role.

Subjects and methods

This work was conducted on 60 patients with chronic kidney disease and 30 ages and sex matched healthy persons as a control group. It was approved by Al-Azhar University (ethical no. = 202010460). This investigation, was carried out in the Nephrology Unit, Department of Internal Medicine, AL- Zahra University Hospital, and in Nephrology unit (Ain shams general Hospital) Cairo, Egypt during the period of May 2021 till October 2021. All participants were divided into two groups: Group I: 60 chronic kidney disease patients, group II: 30 healthy control. Group I was subdivided according to history of CVD into two sub groups: Group A patients without history (n = 32) and Group B patients with history (n = 28).

Inclusion criteria

Adult male and female chronic kidney disease patients. The study excluded any disease can affect the level of adropin such as: malignant tumor disease, cerebro-vascular disease, active autoimmune diseases patients, acute or chronic liver failure, history of MI as well as glucose intolerance or diabetes mellitus during the last 6 months.

All the patients and control underwent to complete medical history, full clinical examination, Kidney function test, abdominal Ultrasound, ECG, Liver function test and serum lipid profile. Levels of Serum adropin was assessed utilizing enzyme-linked immunesorbent assay (ELISA) kit from SinoGeneClon Co. catalog no. SG-11594. The precoated micro plate wells were filled with samples and the corresponding antibody. Subsequently, each micro plate well received a detection antibody specific for human AD. Avidin Horseradish Peroxidase (HRP) conjugate were added. Free parts were removed by washing. In each well, the substrate solution is added. The only wells that are blue in color are those that contain the human AD. The enzyme-substrate reaction ended and the color turned yellow upon the addition of stop solution. Spectrophotometric measurement of the optical density (OD) is done at a wavelength of 450 nm. According SinogeneClon Biotech Co. Catalog No. SG- 00451, using the enzyme-linked immune-sorbent assay, high sensitive C- reactive protein was quantified (ELISA). 5 ml blood were taken, divided into 2 ml on EDETA for CBC and HbA1C and 3 ml was put in a plan tube, left to clot then centrifuged and serum was separated and keptat-80 till analyzed. Fasting peripheral venous blood samples were collected from both subject and control under complete aseptic condition after at least 12 h fast.

Statistical analysis

For normally distributed data, values were expressed as mean ± SD or as median for skewed variables. Kruskal-Wallis (KW) test was used for non-normally distributed variables. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare the differences between means of the three groups for continuous variables. To determine which of the mean differed, a least significant difference test was employed. Additionally, ROC analysis was used to detect the appropriate cutoff points of adropin levels for the CVD among CKD patients and assess the accuracy. A two-tailed p < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant for all analyses. SPSS for Windows (version 16.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for all of the analyses.

Discussion

In human studies, obese individuals were found to have significantly lower blood adropin levels compared to those with normal weight, highlighting adropin's role in metabolic regulation and its potential relevance in managing obesity-related conditions 30, 31. Previous studies have found that adropin is associated with coronary artery disease, which can regulate lipid metabolism, improve insulin resistance, and protect vascular endothelial cell function and anti-inflammatory effect32. Serum adropin level is significantly correlated with risk factors of CVD and low serum adropin levels may be a potential predictor of CVD in hemodialysis (HD) patients33.

The Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI)34 defines CKD as having an eGFR of < 60 ml/min/1.73 m2 or other evidence of CKD (regardless of eGFR) on at least two times more than three months apart 35, 36. Patients with CKD have elevated cardiovascular mortality and morbidity rates, and studies showed a severe impact on the consequences of CVD 37. Three main mechanisms that contribute to the development of cardiomyopathy and induce left ventricular failure are exposed to the heart in CKD such as pressure overload, volume overload, and non-hemodynamic factors that modify the myocardium. Long-standing hypertension and vascular stiffness are the main causes of pressure overload 38.

CKD progression is related to a higher risk of CVD 39 and risk of mortality rises with age. Also, this risk increases in the presence of co-occurring conditions like hypertension, diabetes and other vascular risk factors 40. Adropin was discovered as a novel factor that links with metabolic homeostasis throughout an investigation of obese insulin-resistant mice 41. This polypeptide hormone adropin has a broad influence on human energy homeostasis, metabolism and cardiac damage. Adropin synthesis was first discovered in the brain and liver of mice. Adropin originates in the brain or liver through the circulatory system and exhibited in other tissues and body fluids 42. Peptides secreted by adropin play a role in cellular and organismal metabolic status signaling, preserving metabolic and cardiovascular homeostasis. It has been suggested that the blood levels of this protein inform muscles about their metabolic status, influencing their preference and improve glucose oxidation 43. The effect of worsening CKD and its complications can be attributed to hormonal imbalances. Thus, adropin may serve as a future biomarker for endothelial dysfunction and cardiovascular disease as well as a potential prognostic indicator of cardiac status and clinical status in CKD patients 44.

This study included classified 60 CKD patient with and without CVD and 30 healthy control group. There was no statistically significant difference in age or sex between the studied groups. Significant difference was observed between groups as regard active and passive smokers between group A, group B and control group (31.3 %, 34.4 %), (53.6 %, 35.7), (36.7 %), 13.3 %) respectively (p-value > 0.05). There was statistically significant difference between group B and control as regard smoking. There were 15 patients (53.6 %) active smokers, 10 patients (35.7 %) passive smokers and 3 patients (10.7 %) non-smokers while in control, there were 11 patients (36.7 %) active smokers, 4 patients (13.3 %) passive smokers and 15 patients (50 %) non-smokers. Previous studies demonstrated a clear correlation between longer smoking duration and an increased risk of complications and progression of CKD 45. On the other hand, among ex-smokers, the risk of poor kidney outcomes and CVD declined with increased length of time spent smoke-free.

Regarding lipid profile, there was highly significant increase of CHOL, TG and LDL in group B compared to group A and control group (p-value < 0.001). This is in agreement with previous studies confirmed link of dyslipidemia, hypertriglyceridemia, high LD, accumulation of lipoproteins and low HDL levels with CKD 46. All these changes are related to oxidative stress as independent risk factors for cardiovascular events and mortality in CKD patient47.

Adropin levels showed significant decrease in group B (119.9 ± 68.8) compared to group A (170.7 ± 112.9) and control group (614.9 ± 180.8). Cardiac Syndrome X (CSX) patients with CKD have low circulating adropin, indicating that low adropin levels are risk factors for CSX. Numerous research endeavors examined the correlation between human cardiovascular complications and adropin levels. Adropin's possible role in endothelial cell function was reported previously 48. Endothelial dysfunctions in CKD patients are correlated with low levels of circulating adropin. Previous studies reported that patients with coronary artery disease who have lower levels of adropin are more likely to have acute myocardial infarction49. Moreover, adropin levels were significantly decrease according to the severity in the patients with heart failure 50.

In the present study Hs-CRP levels was significantly increased in group B (8.8 ± 7.0) than group A (8.0 ± 7.8) and control group (3.5 ± 1.7). There was a strong correlation between the onset of cardiovascular events and elevated hs-CRP in CKD. Thus, hs-CRP can serve as a predictor of subsequent cardiovascular events in CKD patients51.

Thus, serum adropin can be used to discriminate between Pt with and without CVD hx history at a cutoff level of > 304, with 46.4 % sensitivity and 84.4 % specificity. Moreover, it plays significant role in discrimination between group B and control group at a cutoff level of < 410, with 93.8 % sensitivity, 86.7 % specificity as well as between group A and control group at a cutoff level of < 416, with 57.1 % sensitivity and 83.3 % specificity.

In conclusion, the present results suggest that adropin may be a protective potential regulator marker of cardiovascular functions and may play role in the pathogenesis and development of CVDs and Spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD).

Modified Rota-Baxter operators of non-zero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras
Shuangjian Guo, Yufei Qin, Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of modified Rota-Baxter operators of non-zero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras and provide some examples. Next, we give various constructions of modified Rota-Baxter operators of non-zero weight according to constructions of 3 3 -Lie algebras. Furthermore, we define a cohomology of modified Rota-Baxter operators of non-zero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras with coefficients in a suitable representation. As an application, we study formal deformations of modified Rota-Baxter operators of non-zero weight that are generated by the above-defined cohomology. In the final part of the paper, we construct two L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -algebra structures whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond to relative and absolute modified Rota-Baxter 3 3 -Lie algebra structures of nonzero weight, respectively. Lastly, we compare our L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -algebraic approach with the deformation-controlling L ∞ ​ [ 1 ] L_{\infty}[1] -algebra for relative Rota-Baxter 3 3 -Lie operators developed by Hou, Sheng, and Zhou.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some basic notions and facts about
3
-Lie algebras from
[
5
,

,

,
20
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
3
-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a skew-symmetric trilinear map

[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

satisfying the fundamental identity

[x,y,[u,v,w]]=[[x,y,u],v,w]+[u,[x,y,v],w]+[u,v,[x,y,w]],\qquad x,y,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
V
a vector space.
A
representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
is a linear map

\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)

such that for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\displaystyle[\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),\rho(x_{3},x_{4})]

\displaystyle=\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}],x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]),

(2)

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}])

\displaystyle=\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2})-\rho(x_{2},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{3})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4}).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra.
Define

{\rm ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}),\qquad{\rm ad}_{x,y}(z)=[x,y,z].

Then
{\rm ad}
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
adjoint representation
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
3
-Lie algebra. The
derived algebra
is

\mathfrak{g}^{1}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],

and the center is

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[x,y,z]=0,\ \forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
and
(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be
3
-Lie algebras, and

\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
If

\rho(x,y)u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{h}),\qquad\rho(x,y)\{u,v,w\}=0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\ u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h},

then
\rho
is called an
action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
(V,\rho)
be a representation of a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Set

C^{0}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},V)=V,\qquad C^{n}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},V)={\rm Hom}\big((\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g})^{\otimes(n-1)}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\;V\big),\quad n\geq 1.

The coboundary operator

\partial_{\mathrm{3Lie}}^{n}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},V)

is given, for
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}
, by

\begin{split}\partial_{\mathrm{3Lie}}^{n}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})&=(-1)^{n+1}\rho(y_{n},x_{n+1})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},x_{n})\\
&\quad+(-1)^{n+1}\rho(x_{n+1},x_{n})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},y_{n})\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\rho(x_{j},y_{j})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{j},y_{j},x_{n+1}])\\
&\quad+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},\\
&\hskip 79.6678pt[x_{j},y_{j},x_{k}]\wedge y_{k}+x_{k}\wedge[x_{j},y_{j},y_{k}],\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\ldots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1}).\end{split}

The corresponding cohomology is denoted by

H^{*}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},V).

When
V=\mathfrak{g}
with the adjoint representation, we write

H^{n}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g})=H^{n}_{\mathrm{3Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\qquad n\geq 1.

Next, we study decompositions of
3
-Lie algebras and their associated product structures.

Definition 2.6
.

[
20
]

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra and

N:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
a linear map.

N
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
if

\displaystyle[N(x),N(y),N(z)]

\displaystyle=N\Big([N(x),N(y),z]+[x,N(y),T(z)]+[N(x),y,N(z)]

\displaystyle\quad-N[N(x),y,z]-N[x,N(y),z]-N[x,y,T(z)]\Big).

Definition 2.7
.

[
25
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra.
An
almost product structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear endomorphism

E:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that

E^{2}=\mathrm{Id},\qquad E\neq\pm\mathrm{Id}.

An almost product structure
E
is called a
product structure
if it
satisfies the following integrability condition:

\displaystyle E[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}={}

\displaystyle[Ex,Ey,Ez]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Ex,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Ey,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y,Ez]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-E[Ex,Ey,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-E[x,Ey,Ez]_{\mathfrak{g}}-E[Ex,y,Ez]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Remark 2.8
.

[
25
]

A product structure on a
3
-Lie algebra can equivalently be regarded as a
Nijenhuis operator
E
satisfying
E^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
[
25
, Theorem 5.3]
]
Let
\bigl(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigr)
be a
3
-Lie algebra.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
admits a product structure
E
if and only if it decomposes as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are
3
-Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In this case,
E
is given by

E(x,u)=(x,-u),\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+},\;u\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}.

3.
Modified Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda

In this section, we introduce the notions of Rota-Baxter alegbras of weight
\lambda
and relative (modified) Rota-Baxter alegbras of weight
\lambda
give some properties.

Definition 3.1
.

[
5
]

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra and

T:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
a linear map.

T
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
if

\displaystyle[T(x),T(y),T(z)]

\displaystyle=T\Big([T(x),T(y),z]+[x,T(y),T(z)]+[T(x),y,T(z)]

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda[T(x),y,z]+\lambda[x,T(y),z]+\lambda[x,y,T(z)]+\lambda^{2}[x,y,z]\Big).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right)
and
\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right)
be
3
-Lie algebras.
Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a
3
-Lie algebra
\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right)
on a
3
-Lie algebra
\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right)
, and
\zeta:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
an action of
\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right)
on
\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right)
. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda\in{\mathbf{k}}
from a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
to a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to actions
\rho
and
\zeta
if
\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
,

\displaystyle[R(u),R(v),R(w)]=

\displaystyle R\Big(\rho(R(u),R(v))w+\rho(R(v),R(w))u+\rho(R(w),R(u))v+\lambda\{u,v,w\}\Big)

\displaystyle-\lambda\zeta(u,v)R(w)-\lambda\zeta(v,(w)R(u)-\lambda\zeta(w,u)R(v).

In this case, the quadruple
\left(\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right),\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right),\rho,\zeta\right)
is called a
relative
3
-Lie algebra pair
.
Moreover, the quintuple
\left(\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right),\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right),\rho,\zeta,R\right)
is called a
relative modified Rota-Baxter
3
-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.

Moreover, If

(a)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}
and
\rho=\zeta=\mathrm{ad}
, we call

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],R)
an
absolute modified Rota-Baxter
3
-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
,
or simply a modified Rota-Baxter
3
-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
,
or a modified Rota-Baxter
3
-Lie algebra;

(b)

\zeta:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
vanish, we call the quadruple
\left(\left(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\right),\left(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]\right),\rho,R\right)
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter
3
-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.

7.1.

-algebras and (generalised) derived brackets

In this subsection, we recall some preliminaries on
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebras.

Definition 7.1
.

An
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra is a pair
(L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
together with a collection
\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1

l_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L
(for
k\geq 1
) that satisfy

- graded symmetry:
l_{k}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)})=\epsilon(\sigma)l_{k}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}),
for any
\sigma\in S_{k}
and
k\geq 1
,

- higher Jacobi identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{(i,n-i)}}\epsilon(\sigma)l_{j}\left(l_{i}\left(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(i)}\right)\otimes x_{\sigma(i+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}\right)=0,

for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in L
and
n\geq 1
. Here
S_{(i,n-i)}
is the set of all
(i,n-i)
-shuffles i.e., permutations
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that:

\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i_{1}),\sigma(i_{1}+1)<\cdots<\sigma(i_{1}+i_{2}),\dots,\sigma(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r-1}+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n).

and
\epsilon(\sigma)=\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the standard Koszul sign in the graded context.

Throughout the paper, all
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebras are assumed to be weakly filtered. In other words, certain infinite summations are always convergent.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
(L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra. An element
\alpha\in L_{0}
is said to be a Maurer-Cartan element if it satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}~\!l_{k}(\alpha\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha)=0.

Proposition 7.3
.

[
12
, Twisting procedure]

Let
\alpha

L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra

L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra structure on

is given by a sequence of multilinear maps
l_{n}^{\alpha}:L^{\otimes n}\to L
defined by

l_{n}^{\alpha}\left(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}l_{n+i}\left(\underbrace{\alpha\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha}_{i\text{ times}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}\right),\quad\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in L,

whenever the infinite sum converges (or terminates in a finite case)

In particular, a Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha\in L_{0}
induces a degree-one differential

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}~\!l_{i+1}(\underbrace{\alpha\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha}_{i\text{ times}}\otimes x),

which turns
(L,l_{1}^{\alpha})
into a cochain complex. The corresponding cohomology groups are referred to as the cohomology induced by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
.

Proposition 7.9
.

The embedding map
\nu\colon V_{\mathrm{Pair}}\rightarrow V_{r\mathrm{Pair}}
is a monomorphism of graded Lie algebra.

Proof.

For
f\in V_{\mathrm{Pair}}(n)
and
g\in V_{\mathrm{Pair}}(m)
, we compute:

\displaystyle[\nu(f),\nu(g)]_{\mathrm{R}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n},\,i\in\{0,1\}}f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i},\ \sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m},\,j\in\{0,1\}}g_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}^{j}\right]_{\mathrm{R}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m+n},\,i,j\in\{0,1\}}\left(f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i}\circ g_{i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{m+n}}^{j}-(-1)^{mn}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{i}\circ f_{i_{m+1},\ldots,i_{m+n}}^{j}\right),

where, for each
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1}\in\{0,1\}
, the components lie in the following spaces:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(

\displaystyle f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i}\circ g_{i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},1}^{1}-(-1)^{mn}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{i}\circ f_{i_{m+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},1}^{1}\Big)\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{i_{m+n-1}}\otimes\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g},\,\mathfrak{g}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(

\displaystyle f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i}\circ g_{i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},0}^{1}-(-1)^{mn}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{i}\circ f_{i_{m+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},0}^{1}\Big)\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{i_{m+n-1}}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\,\mathfrak{g}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(

\displaystyle f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i}\circ g_{i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},0}^{0}-(-1)^{mn}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{i}\circ f_{i_{m+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},0}^{0}\Big)\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{i_{m+n-1}}\otimes\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\,\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(

\displaystyle f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{i}\circ g_{i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},1}^{0}-(-1)^{mn}g_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{i}\circ f_{i_{m+1},\ldots,i_{m+n-1},1}^{0}\Big)\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(V_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{i_{m+n-1}}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\,\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\right).

Thus, we have

[\nu(f),\nu(g)]_{\mathrm{R}}=\nu\left(f\circ g-(-1)^{mn}g\circ f\right)[1]=\nu\left([f,g]_{\mathrm{R}}\right),

that is,

\nu\colon V_{\mathrm{Pair}}\rightarrow V_{r\mathrm{Pair}}

is a monomorphism of graded Lie algebras.
∎

Shannon entropy for harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles
Natsuo Miyatake

Abstract
Let X X be a Riemann surface, K X → X K_{X}\rightarrow X the canonical bundle, and T X ≔ K X − 1 → X T_{X}\coloneqq K_{X}^{-1}\rightarrow X the dual bundle of the canonical bundle. For each integer r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , each q ∈ H 0 ​ ( K X r ) q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r}) , and each choice of the square root K X 1 / 2 K_{X}^{1/2} of the canonical bundle, we canonically obtain a Higgs bundle, which is called a cyclic Higgs bundle. A diagonal harmonic metric h = ( h 1 , … , h r ) h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r}) on a cyclic Higgs bundle yields r − 1 r-1 Hermitian metrics H 1 , … , H r − 1 H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1} on T X → X T_{X}\rightarrow X , defined as H j ≔ h j − 1 ⊗ h j + 1 H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1} for each j = 1 , … , r − 1 j=1,\dots,r-1 , while h 1 h_{1} , h r h_{r} , and q q yield a degenerate Hermitian metric H r H_{r} on T X → X T_{X}\rightarrow X . The r r -differential q q induces a subharmonic weight function ϕ q = 1 r ​ log ⁡ | q | 2 \phi_{q}=\frac{1}{r}\log|q|^{2} on K X → X K_{X}\rightarrow X , and the diagonal harmonic metric depends solely on this weight function ϕ q \phi_{q} . In the previous papers, the author introduced and studied the extension of harmonic metrics associated with an arbitrary subharmonic weight function φ \varphi , which also constructs r − 1 r-1 Hermitian metrics H 1 , … , H r − 1 H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1} and a degenerate Hermitian metric H r H_{r} on T X → X T_{X}\rightarrow X . In this paper, for each non-zero real parameter β \beta , we introduce a function, which we call entropy, that quantifies the degree of mutual misalignment of the Hermitian metrics H 1 , … , H r H_{1},\dots,H_{r} . By extending the estimates established by Dai–-Li and Li–-Mochizuki to general subharmonic weight functions, we obtain upper and lower bounds on the entropy for β > 0 \beta>0 , assuming that H 1 , … , H r − 1 H_{1},\ldots,H_{r-1} are complete, and that the weight function is neither induced by a complete flat Kähler metric nor identically equal to − ∞ -\infty . The upper bound is log ⁡ r \log r , the constant entropy obtained when the weight function is induced by a complete flat Kähler metric, in which case H 1 = ⋯ = H r H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r} . The lower bound is the constant entropy associated with the weight function φ ≡ − ∞ \varphi\equiv-\infty . Furthermore, for arbitrary nonzero real β \beta , we determine the asymptotic behavior of the difference between these two entropy values as r → ∞ r\to\infty , showing that it converges to a finite real number if and only if β > − 1 \beta>-1 .

Introduction and main theorem

As is well known, every compact connected Riemann surface possesses a constant Gaussian curvature Kähler metric. Among them, constant negative Gaussian curvature Kähler metrics and flat Gaussian curvature Kähler metrics are related to Hitchin’s seminal example of Higgs bundles
[
27
]
. Hitchin’s example is as follows: Let
X
be a compact connected Riemann surface and
K_{X}\rightarrow X
the canonical bundle. For each choice of the square root
K_{X}^{1/2}
of the canonical bundle and each
q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{2})
, we can associate the following Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}_{2}\coloneqq K_{X}^{1/2}\oplus K_{X}^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\Phi(q)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\in H^{0}(\mathrm{End}{\mathbb{K}}_{2}\otimes K_{X}).

This example was introduced by Hitchin
[
27
, Section 11]
, especially on a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface. Suppose that
X
is a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface. Then
({\mathbb{K}}_{2},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
is always stable (cf.
[
27
]
), and thus there uniquely exists a solution
h
to the Hitchin equation satisfying
\mathrm{det}(h)=1
. Moreover, from the symmetry of the Higgs field and the uniqueness of the solution to the Hitchin equation, the metric
h
splits as
h=(h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})
. Let
T_{X}\rightarrow X
be the dual bundle of
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. The solution
h=(h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})
induces a metric
H_{1}\coloneqq h_{1}^{-2}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
and one degenerate metric (cf.
[
27
, Theorem (11.2)]
)
H_{2}\coloneqq h_{1}^{2}\otimes h_{q}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
, where
h_{q}
denotes the degenerate Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-2}\rightarrow X
induced by
q
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle(h_{q})_{x}(u,v)\coloneqq\langle q_{x},u\rangle\overline{\langle q_{x},v\rangle}\ \text{for $x\in X$, $u,v\in(K_{X}^{-2})_{x}$}.

(1)

If
q
is identically zero, then the Kähler metric induced by
H_{1}
is a constant negative Gaussian curvature metric (cf.
[
27
, Section 11]
). If
X
is an elliptic curve and
q
is a non-zero section, then we can easily find a solution to the Hitchin equation;
h=(h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})=(h_{q}^{-1/4},h_{q}^{1/4})
solves the Hitchin equation, where
h_{q}
is a Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-2}\rightarrow X
defined as in (
1
). In this case, if we define
H_{1}=h_{1}^{-2}
and
H_{2}=h_{1}^{2}\otimes h_{q}
in the same way as in the case where
X
is hyperbolic, we obtain
H_{1}=H_{2}=h_{q}^{1/2}
, and the Kähler metrics induced by them are flat Gaussian curvature metrics. From the above, we can observe that the solution of the Hitchin equation for Hitchin’s example contains both constant negative Gaussian curvature Kähler metrics and flat Gaussian curvature Kähler metrics. They are essentially equivalent to the harmonic metrics on Hitchin’s example for the two extremal cases; the case where
q
is identically zero, and the case where it has no zeros at all. We can further observe that in the case where
H_{1}
yields a hyperbolic metric,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
do not coincide anywhere on
X
since
H_{2}
is identically zero, while in the case where
H_{1}
is flat,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
coincide everywhere. Hitchin’s example can naturally be generalized to harmonic metrics on higher rank cyclic Higgs bundles (cf.
[
2
,
28
]
). Similar observations as above can be made for harmonic metrics on the general cyclic Higgs bundles as will be explained in Section
2
.

In previous papers
[
46
,
47
]
, the author proposed a new direction of research for cyclic Higgs bundles that considers harmonic metrics even on cyclic ramified coverings and Hermitian metrics that solve the extended Hitchin equation with a coefficient obtained as the limit of infinitely increasing covering order. In other words, this means that we consider a more general semipositive singular Hermitian metric
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
on the canonical bundle instead of the singular metric induced by
q
. The intention was to connect the potential theory, especially the theory of zero-point configurations of holomorphic functions and holomorphic sections which has been the subject of much research for a very long time (cf.
[
3
,
23
,
57
,
58
,
63
]
), with the theory of harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles. In
[
46
,
47
]
, the author introduced an elliptic equation that extends the Hitchin equation for the ordinary cyclic Higgs bundle to be adjunct to an arbitrary semipositive singular metric
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
on
K_{X}\rightarrow X
, and discussed some fundamental properties of the equation. As with the usual cyclic Higgs bundles, by taking the adjacent components of the solution
(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
to the elliptic equation, we obtain
r-1
Hermitian metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
and
h_{1},h_{r}
and
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
yield one degenerate Hermitian metric
H_{r}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
(see Section
2.3
).

In this paper, we introduce a new function which we call
entropy
. It is constructed from a non-zero real number
\beta
and the metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
. Throughout this paper, we suppose that
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
are all complete. Our first main theorem concerns the uniform estimate of the entropy from above and below, assuming that the weight function is neither induced by a complete flat Kähler metric nor identically equal to
-\infty
:

Theorem 1
.

Let
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
be a semipositive singular Hermitian metric on
K_{X}\rightarrow X
that is not identically
\infty
and is not induced by a complete flat Kähler metric.
Then, for any positive real number
\beta
, the entropy
S(r,\beta,\varphi)
satisfies the following uniform bounds:

\displaystyle S_{r,\beta}\leq S(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)<\log r\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for every $x\in X$},

(2)

where
S_{r,\beta}
is the entropy associated with the weight function that is identically
-\infty
. Moreover, equality in the lower bound for
S(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)
holds if and only if
r=2,3
and
\varphi(x)=-\infty
.

The upper bound is
\log r
, the constant entropy obtained when the weight function is induced by a complete flat Kähler metric, in which case
H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r}
. The lower bound is the constant entropy associated with the weight function
\varphi\equiv-\infty
(see Section
5.2
for more details). Theorem
1
asserts that, under assumptions excluding these trivial solutions, the entropy is bounded below and above by the entropy values associated with the hyperbolic and Euclidean cases, respectively. In our second main theorem, for arbitrary nonzero real
\beta
, we determine the asymptotic behavior of the difference between these two entropy values as
r\to\infty
:

Theorem 2
.

The following asymptotic formula holds:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}(S_{r,\beta}-\log r)=\begin{cases}-\frac{2\beta d_{\beta}}{c_{\beta}}+\log(c_{\beta})\ &\text{if $\beta>-1$}\\
-\infty\ &\text{if $\beta\leq-1$},\end{cases}

where
c_{\beta}
and
d_{\beta}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle c_{\beta}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds,

\displaystyle d_{\beta}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}\log s\ ds.

For
\beta<0
, the bounds
0\leq S(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)<\log r
hold under the same assumptions on the weight function as in Theorem
1
. Alternatively, if we consider the “conditional” entropy

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}(r,\beta,\varphi)\coloneqq-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\widetilde{p}_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)\log\widetilde{p}_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi),

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)\coloneqq\frac{p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)}{1-p_{0}(r,\beta,\varphi)}\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$},

then
S_{r,\beta}<\widetilde{S}(r,\beta,\varphi)<\log(r-1)
holds for
r\geq 4
.

The proof of Theorem
1
proceeds by extending to general subharmonic weight functions the estimates of Dai–Li and Li–Mochizuki
[
18
,
19
,
37
]
for the ratios between
H_{j}
and
H_{j+1}
,
j=1,\dots,r-1
, originally established in the case where
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
arise from harmonic metrics, and then combining these estimates with the Schur concavity of the Shannon entropy. Analogous bounds can also be obtained for other Schur-concave functions.

For
\beta<-1
, as the proof shows,
S_{r,\beta}
itself converges to a finite limit
\beta(\zeta^{\prime}(-\beta)/\zeta(-\beta))+\log(2\zeta(-\beta))
as
r\to\infty
. This convergence and further refinements of Theorem 2, including correction terms, will be discussed in
[
52
]
.

The most promising direction for the further development of this entropy function, as envisioned by the author, lies in its application to the
approximation theory of equilibrium metrics and measures

The parameters

and
\beta
may be regarded as specifying the “method of observation or measurement” applied to
\varphi
. As mentioned above, one possible direction of research is to study how the entropy behaves as
\varphi

\beta

, it is particularly natural to ask the following question:

Question 3

For each point
x\in X
, what is the asymptotic behavior of

as

?

Regarding ordinary harmonic metrics, the author is not aware of any research aimed at investigating the asymptotic behavior of the harmonic metric in some sense in the limit of the parameter

Backgrounds

2.1
Real, diagonal, and complete Hermitian metrics

Let
X
be a connected, possibly non-compact Riemann surface and
K_{X}\rightarrow X
the canonical bundle. We choose a square root
K_{X}^{1/2}
of the canonical bundle
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}K_{X}^{\frac{r-(2j-1)}{2}}=K_{X}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-3}{2}}\oplus K_{X}^{-\frac{r-1}{2}}.

A Hermitian metric
h
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\rightarrow X
is said to be
real
(cf.
[
28
]
) if the following bundle isomorphism
S
is isometric with respect to
h
:

\displaystyle S\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}&&1\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\\
1&&\end{array}\right):{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}_{r}^{\vee},

where
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}^{\vee}
denotes the dual bundle of
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
. Also,
h
is called
diagonal
(cf.
[
2
,
16
,
19
]
) if it splits as
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
. Let
T_{X}\rightarrow X
be the dual bundle of
K_{X}\rightarrow X
. By taking the difference between the adjacent components in a diagonal Hermitian metric
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
, we obtain
r-1
Hermitian metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
:

\displaystyle H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1}.

A diagonal Hermitian metric
h
is real if and only if
h_{j}=h_{r-j+1}^{-1}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
. This is also equivalent to the metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
satisfying
H_{j}=H_{r-j}
for all
j=1,\dots,r-1
. We say that a diagonal Hermitian metric
h
is
complete
(cf.
[
37
]
) if the Kähler metrics induced by
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
are all complete.

Remark 4
.

Note that the notion of completeness introduced above is considerably broader than the original notion in
[
37
]
. In
[
37
]
, a reference Kähler metric is fixed, thereby allowing diagonal Hermitian metrics on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
to be identified with
r
-tuples of functions. The term “complete solution” is then used for a diagonal Hermitian metric
h
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
, viewed as an
r
-tuple of functions, that satisfies
\mathrm{det}(h)=1
and solves the Hitchin equation for a cyclic Higgs bundle. In contrast, we use the term “complete” for arbitrary diagonal Hermitian metrics, without requiring them to be harmonic or imposing any condition on their determinants, although the main theorem and its proof deal exclusively with metrics satisfying
\mathrm{det}(h)=1
.

Remark 5
.

In this paper, we allow Kähler metrics on Riemann surfaces and Hermitian metrics on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
to be continuous but not necessarily smooth.
Whenever we say that a (possibly non-smooth) Kähler metric is complete, we mean that the induced distance function defines a complete metric space.

2.2
Harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles

Let
q
be a holomorphic section of
K_{X}^{r}\rightarrow X
. Then we associate a holomorphic section
\Phi(q)\in H^{0}(\mathrm{End}{\mathbb{K}}_{r}\otimes K_{X})
, which is called a Higgs field, as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi(q)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&&&q\\
1&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0\end{array}\right).

The pair
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
is called a cyclic Higgs bundle (cf.
[
2
,
27
,
28
]
). The above cyclic Higgs bundle is an example of cyclotomic Higgs bundles introduced in
[
64
]
. For each Hermitian metric
h
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{r}
, we associate a connection
D_{q}(h)
defined as follows:

\displaystyle D_{q}(h)\coloneqq\nabla^{h}+\Phi(q)+\Phi(q)^{\ast h}.

A Hermitian metric on
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
is called a
harmonic metric
if the connection
D_{q}(h)
is a flat connection. A harmonic metric
h
yields a harmonic map
\hat{h}:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})/\mathrm{SU}(r)
, where
\widetilde{X}
is the universal covering space. If
h
is real, then
\hat{h}
maps into
\mathrm{SL}(r,{\mathbb{R}})/\mathrm{SO}(r)
. Suppose that
X
is a compact Riemann surface of genus at least
2
. Then the Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
is stable for any
q\in H^{0}(K_{X}^{r})
, and thus there uniquely exists a harmonic metric
h
on
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))
such that
\mathrm{det}(h)=1
. From the uniqueness of the metric and the symmetry of the Higgs field (cf.
[
2
,
28
]
), the harmonic metric
h
is diagonal and real. To construct a non-diagonal harmonic metric (resp. a non-real harmonic metric), one may, for example, use a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the Hitchin equations with a non-diagonal (resp. non-real) Hermitian metric prescribed on the boundary (cf.
[
21
,
38
]
).
Let
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
be a diagonal harmonic metric on a cyclic Higgs bundle
({\mathbb{K}}_{r},\Phi(q))\rightarrow X
. As mentioned in the previous subsection we obtain
r-1
Hermitian metrics
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
defined as

\displaystyle H_{j}\coloneqq h_{j}^{-1}\otimes h_{j+1}\ \text{for each $j=1,\dots,r-1$}.

We also obtain a Hermitian metric
H_{r}
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
which is degenerate at the zeros of
q
as follows:

\displaystyle H_{r}\coloneqq h_{r}^{-1}\otimes h_{1}\otimes h_{q},

where
h_{q}
is a Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-r}\rightarrow X
that is degenerate at the zeros of
q
defined as follows:

\displaystyle(h_{q})_{x}(u,v)\coloneqq\langle q_{x},u\rangle\overline{\langle q_{x},v\rangle}\ \text{for $x\in X$, $u,v\in(K_{X}^{-r})_{x}$}.

(3)

The connection
D_{q}(h)
is flat if and only if the Hermitian metric
h
satisfies the following elliptic equation, called the Hitchin equation:

\displaystyle F_{h}+[\Phi(q)\wedge\Phi(q)^{\ast h}]=0.

By using
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
, we can describe the Hitchin equation as follows:

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{h_{j}}+\mathrm{vol}(H_{j-1})-\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})=0\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$},

(4)

where for each
j=1,\dots,r
,
\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})
is the volume form of the metric
H_{j}
which is locally described as
\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})={\sqrt{-1}}H_{j}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\frac{\partial}{\partial z})\ dz\wedge d\bar{z}
, and
H_{0}
is
H_{r}
. Equation (
4
) is a kind of Toda equation (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
25
,
26
,
53
,
54
]
). By making a variable change from
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
to
H_{1},\dots,H_{r-1}
, the Hitchin equation becomes the following:

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{H_{j}}+2\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})-\mathrm{vol}(H_{j-1})-\mathrm{vol}(H_{j+1})=0\ \text{for $j=1,\dots,r-1$.}

(5)

In the case where
r=2
and
q=0
, the Hitchin equation for the cyclic Higgs bundle is equivalent to the following equation:

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}F_{H}+2\mathrm{vol}(H)=0,

(6)

where the solution
H
to equation (
6
) is a Hermitian metric
H
on
T_{X}\rightarrow X
. The Kähler metric induced by

is a constant negative Gaussian curvature Kähler metric (cf.
[
27
]
).
We remark on the following two points:

•

If
q

H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r-1}=h_{q}^{1/r}
is a solution to equation (

•

Suppose that
q=0

). We define positive constants
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r-1}
as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\coloneqq 2\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}(\Lambda_{r-1}^{-1})_{jk}=j(r-j),

(7)

where
\Lambda_{r-1}^{-1}
is the inverse matrix of the Cartan matrix of type
A_{r-1}
. Then
H_{j}=\lambda_{j}H\ (j=1,\dots,r-1)
is a solution to equation (
5
).

\displaystyle H_{r}\otimes H_{1}^{-1}<1.

2.3
More general subharmonic weight functions

For each

)

3
Shannon entropy and Schur-concave functions

In this section, we review Shannon entropy and, more generally, Schur-concave functions. We begin by recalling the definition of Shannon entropy.

Definition 14

(
[
62
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer, and let
p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{r-1}
be nonnegative real numbers satisfying

\displaystyle p_{0}+p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r-1}=1.

The
Shannon entropy
of
p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{r-1}
is defined by

\displaystyle S(p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{r-1})\coloneqq-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j}\log p_{j},

where we use the convention
0\log 0=0
.

Shannon entropy is one of the most important examples of
Schur-concave functions
. We next review the general theory of Schur-concave functions. Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. For each
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
, we denote the components of
x
arranged in descending order by

\displaystyle x_{[1]}\geq x_{[2]}\geq\cdots\geq x_{[r]}.

We introduce the following notion:

Definition 15

(cf.
[
44
]
)
.

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
,
y
is said to
majorize

x
(or
x
is said to be
majorized by

y
), in symbols
y\succ x
, if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{[i]}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{[i]}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for $m=1,2,\dots,r-1$},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}y_{i}.

We then define the notion of Schur-concave functions:

Definition 16

(cf.
[
44
]
)
.

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
be a permutation-invariant subset. A real-valued function
f
defined on
A
is said to be
Schur-concave
on
A
if

\displaystyle f(y)\leq f(x)

(12)

for all
x,y\in A
such that
y\succ x
. If equality in (
12
) holds only when
x
is a permutation of
y
, then
f
is said to be
strictly Schur-concave
on
A
. Similarly, a function
f
is said to be
Schur-convex
(resp.
strictly Schur-convex
) if
-f
is Schur-concave (resp. strictly Schur-concave).

The following Schur–Ostrowski criterion is useful and well known:

Proposition 17

(cf.
[
44
]
)
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval and let
f:I^{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a permutation-invariant
C^{1}
function. Then
f
is Schur-concave if and only if

\displaystyle(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right)\leq 0

(13)

for all
x\in I^{r}
and all
i,j=1,\dots,r
. Moreover, if

\displaystyle(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right)<0

(14)

for every
x\in I^{r}
and all
i,j
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
, then
f
is strictly Schur-concave.

For the proof, we refer the reader to
[
44
, pp. 54–59]
. The following is a variant of Proposition
17
for functions on probability simplices:

Proposition 18
.

Let
\Delta_{r-1}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
be the probability simplex

\displaystyle\Delta_{r-1}\coloneqq\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\mid p_{j}\geq 0\text{ for }j=1,\dots,r,\ p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}=1\}.

Let
f:\Delta_{r-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a permutation-invariant continuous function that is
C^{1}
on the relative interior of
\Delta_{r-1}
. Then
f
is Schur-concave if and only if

\displaystyle(p_{i}-p_{j})(df)_{p}(u_{i}-u_{j})\leq 0

for every
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{r})
in the relative interior of
\Delta_{r-1}
and all
i,j=1,\dots,r
, where
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
are the standard basis vectors of
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
and
(df)_{p}
denotes the differential of
f
at
p
. Moreover, if

\displaystyle(p_{i}-p_{j})(df)_{p}(u_{i}-u_{j})<0

whenever
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
, then
f
is strictly Schur-concave.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the proof of Proposition
17
, and we omit it. Using the above Schur–Ostrowski criterion, we can check the strict Schur-concavity of the Shannon entropy as follows. Take arbitrary
1\leq i<j\leq r
. For every
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{r})
in the relative interior of
\Delta_{r-1}
such that
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle(p_{i}-p_{j})\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}S(p_{1},\dots,p_{i}+t,\dots,p_{j}-t,\dots,p_{r})

\displaystyle=-(p_{i}-p_{j})(\log p_{i}-\log p_{j})<0.

We refer the reader to
[
34
,
44
,
56
]
for more examples of Schur-concave functions.

The Cheng–Yau maximum principle and the mean value inequality

This section briefly reviews the Cheng–Yau maximum principle
[
14
]
(see also
[
37
, Section 3.1.2]
) and the mean value inequality
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
(see also
[
20
, Section 2]
). The following theorem is known as the Cheng–Yau maximum principle, which played a crucial role in
[
37
]
:

Theorem 19

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a complete Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded from below. Let
u
be a real-valued
C^{2}
function on
M
satisfying
\Delta_{g_{M}}u\geq f(u)
, where
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a function, and where we denote by
\Delta_{g_{M}}=-d^{\ast}d
the negative Laplacian. Suppose that there exists a continuous positive function
g:[a,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
such that

(i)

g
is non-decreasing;

(ii)

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}>0
;

(iii)

\int_{b}^{\infty}(\int_{a}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau)^{-1/2}dt<\infty
for some
b\geq a
.

Then the function
u
is bounded above. Moreover, if
f
is lower semicontinuous, then
f(\sup_{M}u)\leq 0
.

We recall the following mean-value inequality from
[
20
, Proposition 2.1]
, which is based on
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Theorem 20

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. Consider the differential inequality

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}u\leq cu

(15)

with some positive constant
c
. Let
x_{0}\in M
. We denote by
B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},r)
the open geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
, and by
\mathrm{Vol}(B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},r))
the volume of the geodesic ball. We fix a positive constant
R_{0}
and suppose the following conditions hold for
x_{0}
and
R_{0}
:

(i)

The Poincaré and the Sobolev inequalities hold for functions supported on
B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0})
with constants
c_{p}
and
c_{s}
;

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
c_{2}
such that
\mathrm{Vol}(B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},r))\leq c_{2}r^{n}
for all
r\leq R_{0}
.

Then there exist positive constants
p_{0}
and
C
depending only on
n,c,R_{0},c_{2},c_{p},c_{s}
such that for any continuous nonnegative
W^{1,2}
-function
u
satisfying (
15
) on
B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0})
and any
0<p<p_{0}
the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)}u(x)\geq C\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p},

where
d\mu_{g_{M}}
is the volume measure. In particular, there exist constants
C>0
and
0<p<1
such that

\displaystyle u(x_{0})\geq C\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p}.

(16)

Remark 21
.

In the statements in
[
15
,
20
]
, the choice of a representative
of a
W^{1,2}
-function is not explicitly specified. In particular,
without specifying a representative, a pointwise estimate such as
(
16
) is not well-defined for an equivalence class of

W^{1,2}
-functions. We therefore impose the additional assumption
that
u
is continuous in the theorem above. In
[
20
]
, the
mean-value inequality is applied to a smooth function in the proof
of Lemma 2.2, so this issue does not arise in that application.

For later use, we prove the following extension of Theorem
20
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
,
x_{0}\in M
, and
R_{0}>0
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
20
. Let
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of continuous nonnegative
W^{1,2}
-functions on
B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0})
satisfying

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}u_{k}\leq cu_{k}

(17)

for some positive constant
c
independent of
k
. Let
p_{0}
and
C
be the positive constants given by Theorem
20
. Suppose that there exists a measurable function
u:B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
such that
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to
u
almost everywhere on
B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0})
. Then, for every
0<p<p_{0}
, we have
u\in L^{p}(B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2))
and

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in B_{g_{M}}^{(u)}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)}u(x)\geq C\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p},

(18)

where the set
B_{g_{M}}^{(u)}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)
is defined by

\displaystyle B_{g_{M}}^{(u)}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)\coloneqq\{x\in B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)\mid\text{$(u_{k}(x))_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ converges to $u(x)$}\}.

In particular, for each
x\in B_{g_{M}}^{(u)}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)
, we have

\displaystyle u(x)\geq C\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p}.

Proof..

Fix
0<p<p_{0}
and
x\in B^{(u)}_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/4)
. By Fatou’s lemma and Theorem
20
, we obtain

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2)}u_{k}^{p}d\mu_{g_{M}}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=u(x).

Since
u(x)<\infty
, we see that
u\in L^{p}(B_{g_{M}}(x_{0},R_{0}/2))
. Also, by taking the infimum, we obtain (

).
∎

Definition of entropy

5.1
Definition

Let
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
be a solution to equation (
10
) and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
be the Hermitian metrics constructed from
h
and
e^{-\varphi}h_{\ast}
as in Section
2.3
. Then we define a function, which we call entropy, as follows:

Definition 24
.

For each
j=0,1,\dots,r-1
and a nonzero real number
\beta
,
we define a nonnegative function

p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi):X\to[0,1]
as follows.
For each
x\in X
, if
(\beta\varphi)(x)
does not take the value
\infty
,
which means that either
\beta>0
, or
\beta<0
and

x\notin\varphi^{-1}(-\infty)
, we define

\displaystyle p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}}{\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}}(x)\ \text{for each $j=0,\dots,r-1$,}

where
\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}/\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}
is understood to be

(\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})/\mathrm{vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}/\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})/\mathrm{vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}

for some reference metric
H_{\ast}
, which does not affect

p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)
.
If
(\beta\varphi)(x)
takes the value
\infty
, we set

p_{0}(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)=0
and define

\displaystyle p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}}{\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\mathrm{vol}(H_{j})^{\beta}}(x)\ \text{for each $j=1,\dots,r-1$.}

We define the
entropy
by

\displaystyle S(r,\beta,\varphi)\coloneqq-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi)\log p_{j}(r,\beta,\varphi).

Remark 25
.

In this paper, we consider only those solutions to equation (
10
) used to define the entropy that are complete and having determinant 1. Strictly speaking, the entropy on an open Riemann surface depends not only on the parameters
r
,
\beta
, and
\varphi
, as indicated in the notation, but also on the boundary conditions satisfied by the solution to equation (
10
).

Remark 26
.

The constant
\beta
is modelled after the inverse temperature in the canonical ensemble in statistical mechanics (cf.
[
33
]
). We will further pursue the analogy with the canonical ensemble in the subsequent paper
[
48
]
.

5.2
Two extremal cases

We remarked in Section
2.3
that there are two explicit complete solutions to equation (
10
) constructed from hyperbolic and Euclidean metrics. In this subsection, we note that the entropy introduced in the previous subsection is constant for these two solutions. We first consider the case where
X
is a parabolic Riemann surface and the weight function
\varphi=\varphi_{\rm Euc}
is induced by the Euclidean metric. In this case, we have
H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r}=e^{\varphi_{\rm Euc}}h_{\ast}^{-1}
, and thus we obtain

\displaystyle S(r,\beta,\varphi_{\rm Euc})=\log r.

We next consider the case where the Riemann surface
X
is hyperbolic and the weight function is identically
-\infty
. Let
H
be the Hermitian metric on
K_{X}^{-1}
induced by the hyperbolic metric. In this case, we have

\displaystyle H_{j}=\lambda_{j}H,

where
\lambda_{j}
is defined by
\lambda_{j}\coloneqq j(r-j)
.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle S(r,\beta,-\infty)=-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\hat{p}_{j,\beta}\log\hat{p}_{j,\beta},

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{j,\beta}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}.

We set

\displaystyle S_{r,\beta}\coloneqq S(r,\beta,-\infty).

Proof

6.1
Proof of Theorem
1

. The following proposition is an extension of the estimate established by Dai–Li

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{j-1}}{\lambda_{j}}=\frac{(j-1)(r-j+1)}{j(r-j)}<H_{j-1,\epsilon}\otimes H_{j,\epsilon}^{-1}<1\ \text{for all $2\leq j\leq n$},

(51)

\displaystyle H_{r,\epsilon}\otimes H_{1,\epsilon}^{-1}<1.

(52)

By taking the limit
\epsilon\searrow 0
of (
51
) and (
52
) at each point of
X
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{j-1}}{\lambda_{j}}=\frac{(j-1)(r-j+1)}{j(r-j)}\leq H_{j-1}\otimes H_{j}^{-1}\leq 1\ \text{for all $2\leq j\leq n$},

(53)

\displaystyle H_{r}\otimes H_{1}^{-1}\leq 1.

(54)

Our goal is to show that
e^{\sigma_{j}}<1
for each
j=1,\dots,n
, and that
e^{\sigma_{j}^{\prime}}<d_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{\lambda_{j+1}}{\lambda_{j}}
for each
j=1,\dots,n-1
.
Since the proofs are essentially the same, we only show that
e^{\sigma_{1}}<1
. We use the mean-value inequality (see Section
4
). We set
\sigma_{j,\epsilon}\coloneqq\log(H_{j-1,\epsilon}\otimes H_{j,\epsilon}^{-1})
for
j=1,2
.
Since we have
e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}},e^{\sigma_{2,\epsilon}}\leq 1
, from (
26
), it holds that

\displaystyle{\sqrt{-1}}\Lambda_{H_{1,\epsilon}}\partial\bar{\partial}(e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}}-1)

\displaystyle\geq e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}}(3e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}}-3)\geq 3(e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}}-1).

(55)

We take a smooth Kähler metric
g
on
X
that is mutually bounded
with
H_{1}
. Since
H_{1}
is complete,
g
is also complete. Let
x_{0}\in X
. By the local convergence of

H_{1,\epsilon}
to
H_{1}
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>1
,
independent of sufficiently small
\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle C_{0}^{-1}g\leq H_{1,\epsilon}\leq C_{0}g

on
B_{g}(x_{0},1)
. We set
u\coloneqq 1-e^{\sigma_{1}}
and
u_{\epsilon}\coloneqq 1-e^{\sigma_{1,\epsilon}}
.
By the uniform mutual boundedness of
g
and
H_{1,\epsilon}
, (
55
) implies that there exists a positive constant
c
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u_{\epsilon}\leq cu_{\epsilon}.

Since
u_{\epsilon}
converges to
u
everywhere on
\overline{B}_{g}(x_{0},1)
, by Lemma
22
, there exists a positive constant
C
and
0<p<1
such that

\displaystyle u(x_{0})\geq C\left(\int_{B_{g}(x_{0},1/2)}u^{p}d\mu_{g}\right)^{1/p}.

(56)

Define

\displaystyle A_{u}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid u(x)=0\}.

Since
u
is nonnegative,
A_{u}
coincides with the set

. Also, since
\sigma_{1}
is upper semicontinuous,

is lower semicontinuous, and hence,

, then (

Lemma 32
.

Let
P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{r-1}
and
Q_{0}\leq Q_{1}\leq\dots\leq Q_{r-1}
be non-negative real numbers such that
P_{0}+\cdots+P_{r-1}=1
,
Q_{0}+\cdots+Q_{r-1}=1
. Suppose that
P_{1},\dots,P_{r-1}
and
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1}
are strictly positive and that the following holds:

\displaystyle Q_{j}/Q_{j+1}\leq P_{j}/P_{j+1}\ \text{for all $j=0,\dots,r-2$.}

Let
S
be a strictly Schur-concave function. Then the function
S
satisfies

\displaystyle S(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})\leq S(P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{r-1}).

Moreover, the equality is achieved if and only if
Q_{j}/Q_{j+1}=P_{j}/P_{j+1}
holds for all
j=0,\dots,r-2
.

Proof..

By induction on
j-i
, the assumption on the adjacent ratios implies

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{i}}{Q_{j}}\leq\frac{P_{i}}{P_{j}}

(57)

for all
0\leq i<j\leq r-1
.
Indeed, the case
j-i=1
is clear from the assumption, and we suppose that inequality (
57
) holds for the case
j-i=k
. Then if
j-i=k+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{i}}{Q_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{i}}{Q_{j-1}}\cdot\frac{Q_{j-1}}{Q_{j}}\leq\frac{P_{i}}{P_{j-1}}\cdot\frac{P_{j-1}}{P_{j}}=\frac{P_{i}}{P_{j}}.

Therefore by induction, we have (
57
) for all
0\leq i<j\leq r-1
. Hence, for every
k=0,\ldots,r-2
and all
0\leq i\leq k<j\leq r-1
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{i}P_{j}\leq P_{i}Q_{j}.

Summing these inequalities over
i
and
j
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}Q_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{r-1}P_{j}\right)\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}P_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{r-1}Q_{j}\right).

Since both
P_{0},\dots,P_{r-1}
and
Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1}
have total sum 1, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}Q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}P_{i}.

Since the components of
P
and
Q
are arranged in non-decreasing order, it follows that
Q\succ P
. If
S
is a Schur-concave function, then we obtain

\displaystyle S(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})\leq S(P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{r-1}).

Suppose that
S
is a strictly Schur-concave function and suppose that
S(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})=S(P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{r-1})
holds. Then by definition of strict Schur-concavity,
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{r-1}
is a permutation of
P_{0},\dots,P_{r-1}
. Since both
P_{0},\dots,P_{r-1}
and
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{r-1}
are arranged in non-decreasing order, this is equivalent to
Q_{j}=P_{j}
for all
j=0,\dots,r-1
. Moreover, this clearly implies

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{j}}{Q_{j+1}}=\frac{P_{j}}{P_{j+1}}

for every
j=0,\ldots,r-2
. Conversely, if these equalities hold for every
j
, then
Q_{j}/P_{j}
is independent of
j
. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}P_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}Q_{j}=1,

we obtain
Q_{j}=P_{j}
for all
j=0,\dots,r-1
. This proves the assertion on the equality case.
∎

From Proposition
27
and Lemma
32
, we have Theorem
1
.

6.2
Proof of Theorem
2

We next prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We set
Z_{r,\beta}
and
h_{r,\beta}
as follows:

\displaystyle Z_{r,\beta}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{j}^{\beta},

\displaystyle h_{r,\beta}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{2\beta}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{2\beta}}\right)=\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}(Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1}).

Then
S_{r,\beta}
is calculated as follows:

\displaystyle S_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\left(\log\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{2\beta}}-\log r+\log\frac{r^{2\beta+1}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{2\beta+1}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{2\beta}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{2\beta}}\right)+\log r-\log\left(\frac{r^{2\beta+1}}{Z_{r,\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{h_{r,\beta}}{(Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1})}+\log r+\log\left(\frac{Z_{r,\beta}}{r^{2\beta+1}}\right).

(58)

We define constants
c_{\beta}
and
d_{\beta}
, which may not be finite, as follows:

\displaystyle c_{\beta}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds,

\displaystyle d_{\beta}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}\log s\ ds.

Note that each of the above constants is finite if and only if
\beta>-1
, and if
\beta\leq-1
, then
c_{\beta}=\infty
and
d_{\beta}=-\infty
. Suppose that
\beta
is positive. Then since
Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1}
and
h_{r,\beta}
are Riemann sums of
f_{\beta}(s)\coloneqq s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}
and
g_{\beta}(s)\coloneqq s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}\log s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}
on the interval
0\leq s\leq 1
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}(S_{r,\beta}-\log r)=-\frac{2\beta d_{\beta}}{c_{\beta}}+\log(c_{\beta}).

We next consider the case where
\beta
is negative. The following estimate holds:

\displaystyle Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{1/r}^{n/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds+\int_{n/r}^{1-1/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{1/r}^{1-1/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds,

(59)

\displaystyle Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/r}^{n/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds+\frac{1}{r^{2\beta+1}}(r-1)^{\beta}+\int_{n/r}^{1-1/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds+\frac{1}{r^{2\beta+1}}(r-1)^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{1/r}^{1-1/r}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}ds+\frac{2}{r^{2\beta+1}}(r-1)^{\beta}.

(60)

Also, by the monotonicity of the function
g_{\beta}
at both ends of the interval, there exists
\epsilon_{\beta}>0
such that on the interval
0<s\leq\epsilon_{\beta}
(resp.
1-\epsilon_{\beta}\leq s<1
),
g_{\beta}
monotonically decreases (resp. monotonically increases). Therefore, if we take
r
large enough and
j
(resp.
k
) small enough (resp. close enough to
r
) so that
j/r\leq\epsilon_{\beta}
(resp.
1-\epsilon_{\beta}\leq k/r
) holds, the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{(j-1)/r}^{j/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds\geq\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(j/r)\geq\int_{j/r}^{(j+1)/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds,

(61)

\displaystyle\int_{(k-1)/r}^{k/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds\leq\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(k/r)\leq\int_{k/r}^{(k+1)/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds.

(62)

We take
r
large enough and set
j_{0}\coloneqq[r\epsilon_{\beta}]+1
and
k_{0}\coloneqq[r-r\epsilon_{\beta}]-1
, where we denote by
[\cdot]
the Gaussian symbol. Note that we have
(j_{0}-1)/r\leq\epsilon_{\beta}
and
(k_{0}+1)/r\leq 1-\epsilon_{\beta}
. Then we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle h_{r,\beta}\leq\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(1/r)+\int_{1/r}^{(j_{0}-1)/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds+\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(j/r)+\int_{(k_{0}+1)/r}^{1-1/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds+\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(1-1/r),

(63)

\displaystyle h_{r,\beta}\geq\int_{1/r}^{j_{0}/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds+\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(j/r)+\int_{k_{0}/r}^{1-1/r}g_{\beta}(s)ds.

(64)

From (
63
) and (
64
), for the case where
\beta>-1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}h_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{\beta}}g_{\beta}(s)ds+\int_{\epsilon_{\beta}}^{1-\epsilon_{\beta}}g_{\beta}(s)ds+\int_{1-\epsilon_{\beta}}^{1}g_{\beta}(s)ds,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}g_{\beta}(s)ds\noindent

(65)

\displaystyle=2\beta\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta}(1-s)^{\beta}\log sds,

(66)

where we have used the easily verifiable fact that for the case where
\beta>-1
, the terms
\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(1/r)=\frac{1}{r}g_{\beta}(1-1/r)
go to
0
as
r\to\infty
.
Also, from (
64
), for the case where
\beta\leq-1
, we have the following for some positive constants
C_{-1}
and
C_{\beta}
:

\displaystyle h_{r,\beta}\geq\begin{cases}C_{-1}(\log r)^{2}+o((\log r)^{2})\ &\text{if $\beta=-1$}\\
C_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\log r+o(r^{-\beta-1}\log r)\ &\text{if $\beta<-1$}.\end{cases}

(67)

Then we prove the assertion for the case where
\beta>-1
. From (
58
), (
59
), (
60
), and (
66
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}(S_{r,\beta}-\log r)=-\frac{2\beta d_{\beta}}{c_{\beta}}+\log(c_{\beta}).

We next suppose that
\beta=-1
. From (
59
) and (
60
), there exists a positive constant
C_{-1}^{\prime}
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
r
:

\displaystyle Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1}\leq C_{-1}^{\prime}\log r.

(68)

Combining (
68
) with (
58
) and (
67
), we have

\displaystyle S_{r,\beta}-\log r\leq-\frac{C_{-1}(\log r)^{2}+o((\log r)^{2})}{C_{-1}^{\prime}\log r}+\log\left(C_{-1}^{\prime}\log r\right)

This implies
\lim_{r\to\infty}(S_{r,\beta}-\log r)=-\infty
. Finally, we consider the case
\beta<-1
. We set
W_{r,\beta}\coloneqq Z_{r,\beta}/r^{\beta}
. By definition of
h_{r,\beta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}(Z_{r,\beta}/r^{2\beta+1})

\displaystyle=\beta(W^{\prime}_{r,\beta}/r^{\beta+1}-(W_{r,\beta}/r^{\beta+1})\log r).

Therefore, by (
58
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{\beta+1}h_{r,\beta}}{W_{r,\beta}}+\log r+\log W_{r,\beta}-(\beta+1)\log r

\displaystyle=-\beta(W^{\prime}_{r,\beta}/W_{r,\beta})+\log W_{r,\beta}.

We prove that as
r\to\infty
,
W_{r,\beta}
converges to
2\zeta(-\beta)=2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\beta}
and
W^{\prime}_{r,\beta}
converges to
-2\zeta^{\prime}(-\beta)=2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\beta}\log j
, where
\zeta(-\beta)
is the Riemann zeta function. Indeed, by using symmetry, each of
W_{r,\beta}
and
W^{\prime}_{r,\beta}
can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle W_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{m_{r}}j^{\beta}(1-j/r)^{\beta}+O(r^{\beta}),

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{m_{r}}j^{\beta}(1-j/r)^{\beta}(\log j+\log(1-j/r))+O(r^{\beta}\log r),

where
m_{r}
is defined to be
m_{r}\coloneqq[r/2]
. Set

\displaystyle F_{r}(j)\coloneqq\begin{cases}j^{\beta}(1-j/r)^{\beta}\ \text{for}\ j\leq m_{r}\\
0\ \text{for}\ j>m_{r},\end{cases}

\displaystyle G_{r}(j)\coloneqq\begin{cases}j^{\beta}(1-j/r)^{\beta}(\log j+\log(1-j/r))\ \text{for}\ j\leq m_{r}\\
0\ \text{for}\ j>m_{r}.\end{cases}

Then for a fixed
j
, as
r\to\infty
,
F_{r}(j)
and
G_{r}(j)
converge to
j^{\beta}
and
j^{\beta}\log j
, respectively. Also, observe that

\displaystyle(1-j/r)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\beta},

\displaystyle-\log(1-j/r)

\displaystyle\leq\log 2

for each
j=1,\dots,m_{r}
. Therefore we obtain
|F_{r}(j)|\leq 2^{-\beta}j^{\beta}
,
|G_{r}(j)|\leq 2^{-\beta}j^{\beta}\log j+2^{-\beta}j^{\beta}\log 2
.
Since
\beta<-1
, both
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\beta}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\beta}\log j
converge. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}W_{r,\beta}=2\zeta(-\beta),

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}W_{r,\beta}^{\prime}=-2\zeta^{\prime}(-\beta),

and thus we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}S_{r,\beta}=\beta(\zeta^{\prime}(-\beta)/\zeta(-\beta))+\log(2\zeta(-\beta)).

In particular, we have
\lim_{r\to\infty}(S_{r,\beta}-\log r)=-\infty
. This implies the claim.

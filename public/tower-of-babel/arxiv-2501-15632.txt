1.
Introduction

The project is to study maximal WAP and tame quotients (in the sense of topological dynamics) of type spaces over sufficiently saturated models (in the sense of model theory). In this paper, we show the fundamental property of “model-theoretic absoluteness” of Ellis groups of such quotients, i.e., that they do not depend on the model for which they are computed.

Recall that a
flow
is pair
(G,X)
, where
G
is a topological group acting continuously on a compact (Hausdorff) space
X
.
The essential for this paper terms appearing in this introduction are defined in Section
2
.

Weakly almost periodic
(or
WAP
)
flows
form a well-behaved family, playing a fundamental role in topological dynamics with applications to other areas, e.g. to ergodic theory (see
[
EN89
]
). The minimal WAP flows are known to be equicontinuous and have been been classified as homogeneous spaces for compact groups
[
Aus88
, Chapter 3, Theorem 6]
.

Tame flows
, originating in
[
Kö95
]
, form a wider important family, intensively studied in recent two decades by Glasner, Megrelishvili, and others (see e.g.
[
Gla07
,
GM13
,
Gla18
,
GM18
]
). In the case of metrizibale flows, tameness corresponds to one of the two situations in the so-called dynamical Bourgain, Fremlin, Talagrand dichotomy (see
[
GM04
, Theorem 3.2]
). A deep structure theorem on tame, metrizable, minimal flows was obtained by Glasner in
[
Gla18
]
. Tame flows are related to several other areas, e.g. Banach spaces, circularly ordered systems, substitutions and tilings, quasicrystals, cut and project schemes (so to the so-called theory of aperiodic order); see
[
Auj15
]
and the survey
[
GM13
]
.

From the point of view of this paper, it is important that there exist direct correspondences between model-theoretic
stability
and WAP flows (discovered in
[
BY14
,
BYT16
]
, see also
[
Con21
,
HKP22
]
), and between model-theoretic
NIP
and tame flows (discovered independently in
[
CS18
]
,
[
Iba16
]
, and
[
Kha20
]
, and further developed e.g. in
[
KR20
]
and
[
CH23
]
). One of the well-known forms of these kind of correspondences is that stability [resp. NIP] of a theory
T
is equivalent to saying that all flows of the form
(\aut(M),S(M))
(where
M
is a model of
T
,
\aut(M)
is the group of automorphisms of
M
, and
S(M)
is the space of complete types over
M
in a fixed tuple of variables) are WAP [resp. tame] (e.g., see
[
KR20
, Section 5]
).

Stability theory is the core of model theory
[
She90
,
Pil96
]
, and in the last three decades model theory has been focusing on extending the context of stability theory to wider classes of theories, which often requires new ideas and tools, and has applications to other branches of mathematics (e.g. to algebraic geometry and additive combinatorics). And one of the most deeply studied classes of theories extending stable theories is the class of theories with NIP (the non independence property); e.g., see
[
She05
,
HPP08
,
HP11
,
CS18
]
.

Around 2005, Newelski came up with an idea of using tools from topological dynamics in order to extend some aspects of stability theory to much wider unstable situations
[
New09
]
. The point is that various spaces of types can be naturally treated as flows and so become objects of topological dynamics. Since then, the topic has been broadened by a multitude of authors: Ben Yaacov, Chernikov, Hrushovski, Krupiński, Newelski, Pillay, Rzepecki, Simon, and others (e.g.,
[
CS18
,
BYT16
,
KNS19
,
KPR18
,
Pil13
]
). This led not only to essentially new results in model theory (e.g. on the complexity of strong types in
[
KPR18
,
KR20
]
) but also to applications to additive combinatorics in
[
KP23a
]
.

Both in topological dynamics and in model theory, it is natural to study quotients by suitable equivalence relations. In topological dynamics, these are quotients of flows by closed, invariant equivalence relations (equivalently, homomorphic images of flows). In model theory, these are quotients of a sufficiently saturated model (or a subset of such a model) by nice (definable, type-definable, invariant, …) equivalence relations. This includes strong types, hyperimaginary sorts, or quotients of definable groups by various model-theoretic connected components. A particular role is played by quotients by bounded (i.e. with few classes comparing to the degree of saturation of the model) equivalence relations; see e.g.
[
LP01
,
HPP08
,
HP11
,
KPR18
]
. In recent papers
[
HP18
,
KP22
,
KP23
]
, also maximal stable quotients entered the picture, mostly in the NIP context.

In this paper, we study maximal WAP and tame quotients of flows of the form
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}))
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}
is a sufficiently saturated model,
X
is a
\emptyset
-type-definable set, and
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
is the space of complete types over
{\mathfrak{C}}
concentrated on
X
.

As mentioned above, in various contexts, stability corresponds to WAP and NIP to tameness. We check in Section
5
that indeed the quotient of a
\emptyset
-type-definable set
X
by a
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation is stable [resp. NIP] if and only if the quotient of the type space
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
by the corresponding closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation is WAP [resp. tame]. However, in Proposition
5.5
, we observe that for an arbitrary
\emptyset
-type-definable non-stable [resp. IP] set
X
, the finest closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
with WAP [resp. tame] quotient is never induced by a
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X
(necessarily with stable [resp. NIP] quotient). Thus, one can expect that the maximal WAP [resp. tame] quotient of
X
captures more information about the theory in question than the quotient induced by the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable, stable [resp. NIP] equivalence relation on
X
.

The
Ellis
(or
ideal
)
groups
of flows play a very important role both in abstract topological dynamics (for example, in general structural theorems about minimal flows (e.g., see
[
Aus88
,
Gla18
]
)) and in model theory (for example, to get new information about model-theoretic invariants such as
G/G^{000}
or the Lascar Galois group of the theory
T
denoted by
\textrm{Gal}_{L}(T)
(see
[
KP17
,
KPR18
,
KR20
]
)), and in recent applications to additive combinatorics in
[
KP23a
]
. From the model-theoretic perspective, the so-called Newelski’s conjecture (or Ellis group conjecture) turned out to be particularly influential (e.g., see
[
New09
,
GPP15
,
CS18
,
KP17
]
).

A good model-theoretic notion should be independent of the choice of the (sufficiently saturated) model in which it is defined. If this happens to be true, we say that the notion is (model-theoretically)
absolute
; one can also say that it is an
invariant
of the theory in question.

The main result of
[
KNS19
]
says that the Ellis group of
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
is absolute (i.e., does not depend on
{\mathfrak{C}}
). The main theorem of this paper says that the same is true for the Ellis groups of the maximal WAP and tame quotients of
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
. Below are detailed statements; the relevant definitions are given in further sections.

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}\prec{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
be models of a complete theory
T
which are
\kappa
-saturated and strongly
\kappa
-homogeneous, where
\kappa
is specified in the statements below. Let
X
be a
\emptyset
-type-definable subset of
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}
(where
\lambda
is a cardinal number). Let
F^{\prime}
be a closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
-invariant equivalence relation defined on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
, and
F
a closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation defined on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
. We say that
F^{\prime}
and
F
are
compatible
if
r[F^{\prime}]=F
, where
r\colon S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})\to S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
is the restriction map.

Let
F_{\textrm{WAP}}\subseteq S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})\times S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
be the finest closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
such that the flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F_{\textrm{WAP}})
is WAP, and let
F_{\textrm{Tame}}\subseteq S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})\times S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
be the finest closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
such that the flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F_{\textrm{Tame}})
is tame.

Here is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Ellis groups of the
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-flows
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F_{\textrm{Tame}}
(treated as groups with the
\tau
-topology) do not depend on the choice of
{\mathfrak{C}}
as long as
{\mathfrak{C}}
is
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)^{+}
-saturated and strongly
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)^{+}
-homogeneous.

In fact, we prove the following general theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
{\mathfrak{C}}\prec{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
are
\aleph_{0}
-saturated and strongly
\aleph_{0}
-homogeneous. If
F^{\prime}
and
F
are compatible equivalence relations respectively on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
and
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
, then the Ellis group of the flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})/F^{\prime})
is topologically isomorphic to the Ellis group of the flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F)
.

Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
{\mathfrak{C}}\prec{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
are
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)^{+}
-saturated and strongly
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)^{+}
-homogeneous. Then
F^{\prime}_{\textrm{WAP}}
is compatible with
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
F^{\prime}_{\textrm{Tame}}
is compatible with
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
(where
F^{\prime}_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
F^{\prime}_{\textrm{Tame}}
are the counterparts of
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
computed for
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
.

The main result of
[
KNS19
]
about absoluteness of the Ellis group of a theory was reproved in
[
Hru22
]
using a new notion of
infinitary definablity patterns structures
. In Section
2.3
, we take the opportunity and present yet another approach and proof, using infinitary definablity patterns structures together with topological dynamics. This approach is inspired by Pierre Simon’s seminar notes on
[
Hru22
]
. Various facts obtained in Section
2.3
are then used in the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
which are included in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively. Moreover, the approach from Section
2.3
has a potential to be adapted to other contexts in the future (e.g., to Keisler measures or general topological dynamics).

In Section
4
, there is also another related (but much easier) application of our general Theorem
1.2
. Namely, let
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
E^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
] be the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient
X/E
. Let
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
] be the induced equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
, as described after Proposition
2.3
. By
\tilde{E}^{\prime\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
\tilde{E}^{\prime\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
] we denote the analogously defined equivalence relation for the model
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Without any assumption on saturation,
the relation
\tilde{E}^{\prime\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
is compatible with
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
, and
\tilde{E}^{\prime\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
is compatible with
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
.

Corollary 1.5
.

The Ellis group of
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
(treated as a topological group with the
\tau
-topology) does not depend on the choice of
{\mathfrak{C}}
as long as
{\mathfrak{C}}
is at least
\aleph_{0}
-saturated and strongly
\aleph_{0}
-homogeneous.

In the above discussion, we are talking about the finest closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
[resp.
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
] on the
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-flow
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
with WAP [resp. tame] quotient, and about the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
E^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
] on
X
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient. Why do they exist? The existence of
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
[resp.
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
] is a folklore knowledge in topological dynamics, which we explain in Section
2.2
. The existence of
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
E^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
] follows from fact that stable [resp. NIP] quotients are closed under taking products. In the stable case, this property is well-known, but we include a proof for the reader’s convenience. Regarding NIP, the observation seems to be new and more involved. Both proofs are included in Appendix B.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Model theory

T
will usually denote a first order complete theory in a language
\mathcal{L}
. A model
M
of
T
is
\kappa
-saturated
if every complete type
p\in S_{n}(A)
over a subset
A
of
M
of cardinality less than
\kappa
has a realization in
M
; it is said to be
strongly
\kappa
-homogeneous
if every partial elementary map between subsets of
M
of cardinality less than
\kappa
extends to an isomorphism of
M
. By a
monster model

{\mathfrak{C}}
of
T
one usually means a
\kappa
-saturated and strongly
\kappa
-homogeneous model for a sufficiently large cardinal
\kappa
(often a strong limit cardinal greater than
|T|
). But in this paper we will usually require much less about
\kappa
. Precise requirements will be given in the main results. In any case, the above
\kappa
is called
the degree of saturation of
{\mathfrak{C}}
.

Let
x
be a tuple of variables of length
\lambda
(typically,
\lambda<\kappa
). By a
\emptyset
-type-definable set
we mean a partial type
\pi(x)
without parameters in variables
x
or the actual subset
X=\pi({\mathfrak{C}})
of
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}
. By
S_{\pi}({\mathfrak{C}})
or
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
we denote the space of complete types over
{\mathfrak{C}}
concentrated on
X
, that is containing
\pi(x)
as a set of formulas.

The group
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
of automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{C}}
acts naturally on the left on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
by
\sigma(p):=\{\varphi(x,\sigma(a)):\varphi(x,a)\in p\}
. Then
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is a flow with
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
equipped with the pointwise convergence topology.

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}\succ{\mathfrak{C}}
be a bigger monster model whose degree of saturation is greater than
|{\mathfrak{C}}|
. Then the complete types over
{\mathfrak{C}}
can be realized in
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
. For
p\in S({\mathfrak{C}})
and
a\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
,
a\models p
or
p=\tp(a/{\mathfrak{C}})
means that
a
realizes
p
.

Let
E
be a
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on a
\emptyset
-type-definable subset
X=\pi({\mathfrak{C}})
of
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}
, with
\lambda<\kappa
(where
\kappa
is the degree of saturation of
{\mathfrak{C}}
). The equivalence classes of
E
are called
hyperimaginary elements
, while all the sets of the form
X/E
are called
hyperdefinable sets
(over
\emptyset
).

Recall that the complete types over
{\mathfrak{C}}
of elements of
X^{\prime}/E^{\prime}
(where
X^{\prime}=\pi({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
and
E^{\prime}:=E({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
)
can be defined as the
\aut({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}/{\mathfrak{C}})
-orbits on
X^{\prime}/E^{\prime}
, or the preimages of these orbits
under the quotient map, or the partial types defining these preimages, or the classes
of the closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation
\tilde{E}
on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
given by

p\tilde{E}q\iff(\exists a\models p,b\models q)\left(aE^{\prime}b\right).

By
\tp({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$a/E$}/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{${\mathfrak{C}}$}})
we denote the type of
a/E
over
{\mathfrak{C}}
.
In particular,

p\tilde{E}q\iff(\exists a\models p,b\models q)\left(\tp({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$a/E^{\prime}$}/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{${\mathfrak{C}}$}})=\tp({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$b/E^{\prime}$}/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{${\mathfrak{C}}$}})\right),

and

p\tilde{E}q\iff(\forall a\models p,b\models q)\left(\tp({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$a/E^{\prime}$}/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{${\mathfrak{C}}$}})=\tp({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$b/E^{\prime}$}/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{${\mathfrak{C}}$}})\right).

The collection of all such types is denoted by
S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}})
and is equipped with the quotient topology on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}
. Then
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
becomes an
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-flow.

Complete types of hyperimaginaries over any sets of parameters (in place of the whole
{\mathfrak{C}}
) are defined analogously. In particular,
\tilde{E}
is defined for any (not necessarily sufficiently saturated) model
{\mathfrak{C}}
as long as
E
is
\emptyset
-type-definable in the sense that there exists a partial type
\rho(x,y)
over
\emptyset
which defines an equivalence relation
E(M)
on
X(M)
in a sufficiently saturated (equivalently, in every) model
M
of
T
.

Recall now what it means that
X/E
is stable and NIP (see
[
HP18
,
KP22
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
X/E
is
stable
if for every indiscernible sequence
(a_{i},b_{i})_{i<\omega}
with
a_{i}\in X/E
for all (equivalently, some)
i<\omega
, we have

\tp(a_{i},b_{j})=\tp(a_{j},b_{i})

for all (some)
i\neq j<\omega
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
X/E
has NIP if there do not exist an indiscernible sequence
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
and
d\in X/E
such that

((d,b_{2i},b_{2i+1}))_{i<\omega}
is indiscernible and
\tp(d,b_{0})\neq\tp(d,b_{1})
.

Note that the
b_{i}
’s in the above definitions can be anywhere, not necessarily in
X/E
.

The properties of stability and NIP for hyperdefinable sets are both preserved under (possibly infinite) Cartesian products and taking type-definable subsets. Closedness of stability under taking products was remarked (without a proof) in
[
HP18
]
; closedness of NIP under taking products seems to be a new result and the proof is more involved. Both proofs are included in Appendix
B
. As a corollary of these results, in Corollary
B.1
we obtain the existence of the finest
\emptyset
-definable equivalence relation on
X
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient. Below is a more precise information that partial types defining these finest equivalence relations do not depend on the choice of
{\mathfrak{C}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a partial type
\pi(x)
over
\emptyset
(with
|x|=\lambda
), there exists a partial type
\pi^{\textrm{st}}(x,y)
[resp.
\pi^{\textrm{NIP}}(x,y)
] over the empty set which for every
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-saturated
model
{\mathfrak{C}}
defines the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation
on
X:=\pi({\mathfrak{C}})
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient. This relation will be denoted by
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
[resp.
E^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
].

Proof.

Let us fix a model
{\mathfrak{C}}
and
X\subseteq{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}
as above. Corollary
B.1
implies that a finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient exists. Let
\pi^{\textrm{st}}
[resp.
\pi^{\textrm{NIP}}
] be the partial type defining this relation. Put
X^{\prime}:=\pi({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime\lambda}
where
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}
is a different
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-saturated model. It is a routine exercise to check that
\pi^{\textrm{st}}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
[resp.
\pi^{\textrm{NIP}}({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
] defines the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X^{\prime}
with stable [resp. NIP] quotient.
∎

Even if
{\mathfrak{C}}
is not sufficiently saturated,
then by
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
we could mean
\pi^{\textrm{st}}(X({\mathfrak{C}}),X({\mathfrak{C}}))
. However, we do not need to talk about
it, as we will only work with the equivalence relation
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
on

which is defined as we did above. Namely,

p\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}q\iff(\exists a\models p,b\models q)(\pi^{\textrm{st}}(a,b)),

where
a,b
are taken in a big monster model.
Similarly for NIP, we are interested in the relation
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
defined by

p\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}q\iff(\exists a\models p,b\models q)(\pi^{\textrm{NIP}}(a,b)).

By
E^{\prime\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
,

Let

be any dense subset of

•

•

\{gf:g\in G\}
is relatively compact in the topology of pointwise convergence on

•

.

Fact 2.14

For any flow
(G,X)
, the WAP functions form a closed (with the supremum norm) unital subalgebra of

.

The above fact is well-known and can be easily shown using Fact

Combining it with the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, we obtain the second fact:

Fact 2.15

If

Definition 2.16
.

We say that a sequence of functions

\bigcap_{n\in P}f_{n}^{-1}(-\infty,r)\cap\bigcap_{n\in M}f_{n}^{-1}(s,\infty)\neq\emptyset

.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\{f_{n}:X\to\mathbb{R}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of functions. We say that this sequence is an

-sequence on

and choices of real numbers

The following equivalences can be found in
[

The following are equivalent for a bounded

•

does not contain an independent sequence.

•

We say that a function

Fact 2.20

For any flow
(G,X)
, the tame functions form a closed (with the supremum norm) unital subalgebra of

.

A proof can be found e.g. in
[

, Fact 2.72]
. From this fact and Stone-Weierstrass theorem, we obtain the following:

If

(G,X)
is tame if and only if every

is tame.

By compactness, every epimorphism

of

yields the quotient

and the quotient epimorphism

.

We already defined a left action of

on

:

. By a unital, closed

we mean a closed (in the supremuem norm topology) subalgbra of

which is closed under the left action of

Fact 2.22
.

Let
(G,X)
be a flow. There is a 1-1 correspondence between closed, invariant equivalence relations on
X
and closed, unital

-subalgebras of

.

More precisely, for any closed, invariant equivalence relation
\sim
on

and

\Phi(\sim):=\{f\circ\pi:f\in C(Y)\}.

Conversely, for any closed, unital

of

\Psi(\mathcal{A}):=\;\sim,

where
x\sim y
if and only if

is a bijection from the set of closed, invariant equivalence relations on

, and

.

.

Let
\rho\colon X\to Y
be an epimorphism of
G
-flows. Let

and

. Then

(1)

(2)

is tame if and only if

Proof.

Item (1) follows immediately from the characterization of WAP via the double limit criterion from Fact

∎

Let

(1)

(2)

Let

Proposition 2.34

Let
\overline{p}
be an enumeration of

\overline{q}=\eta\overline{p}

(1)

\overline{q}
form an ip-minimal subset
Q

(3)

\eta
belongs to a minimal left ideal of

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Take any

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Take any

\overline{q}^{\prime}\leq^{c}\overline{q}
, that is,
\overline{q}^{\prime}=\eta\overline{q}
for some

is a morphism, hence it is an isomorphism to its image and has an inverse

\overline{q}\leq^{c}\overline{q}^{\prime}
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
Consider any
\eta^{\prime}\in E(S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}))
. Then,

and
\eta^{\prime}\overline{q}=\eta^{\prime}\eta\overline{p}
. By
(1)
,
\overline{q}\leq^{c}\eta^{\prime}\overline{q}

(3)\Rightarrow(1)

\overline{q}^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}\overline{q}=\eta^{\prime}\eta\overline{p}

for some

5.
Stable vs WAP, and NIP vs tame

In this section, we will study the relationship between the equivalence relations
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
and
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and the finest
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relations on
X
with NIP and stable quotients, respectively. Assume that
{\mathfrak{C}}
is
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-saturated and strongly
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-homogeneous (where
X\subseteq{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda})
.

Let
E
be a
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X
. In
[
KP22
]
, we defined
\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
as the family of all functions
f\colon X\times\mathfrak{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which factor through
X/E\times\mathfrak{C}^{m}
and can be extended to a continuous logic formula
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}\times{\mathfrak{C}}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
over
\emptyset
, where
m
ranges over
\omega
. By
[
KP22
, Corollary 2.2]
, we know that the quotient
X/E
is stable if and only if every
f\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
is stable.

For
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
and
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
, by
f_{b}
we denote the function
f_{b}\colon S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}})\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{b}(p):=f(a,b)
for any
a/E^{\prime}\models p
(where
a\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}\succ{\mathfrak{C}}
,
E^{\prime}:=E({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
, and
f
, being a restriction of a continuous logic formula, is treated as a function
X({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})\times{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime|y|}\to\mathbb{R}
). Since
f
is a restriction of a continuous logic formula,
f_{b}
is continuous.

As mentioned in the introduction, the connection between stability and WAP is well-known (discovered in
[
BY14
,
BYT16
]
). This connection is still present for the hyperdefinable set
X/E
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
, and let
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f(x,y)
is stable.

(2)

For all
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
the function
f_{b}
is WAP (for the flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
).

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
If the function
f_{b}
is not WAP, by Fact
2.13
, there is a sequence
(a_{n})_{n<\omega}\subset X
and a sequence of automorphisms
(\sigma_{m})_{m<\omega}\subset\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
such that

\lim_{m}\lim_{n}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b))\neq\lim_{n}\lim_{m}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b)).

Note that we are using that the realized types are dense in
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
.
Assume that
\lim_{m}\lim_{n}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b))>\lim_{n}\lim_{m}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b))
. (The opposite case is analogous.)
Then for some real numbers
r<s
we have

\lim_{m}\lim_{n}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b))>s\text{ and }\lim_{n}\lim_{m}f(a_{n},\sigma_{m}(b))<r.

Let us denote
\sigma_{m}(b)
by
b_{m}
. It is clear that we can choose a subsequence
(a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i}^{\prime})_{i<\omega}
from
(a_{n},b_{n})_{n<\omega}
such that
f(a^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{j})>s
whenever
i>j
and
f(a^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{j})<r
whenever
i<j
. That is, the sequence
(a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i}^{\prime})_{i<\omega}
witnesses unstability of
f(x,y)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
If
f(x,y)
is unstable, we can find an indiscernible sequence
(a_{i},b_{i})_{i<\omega}
with
a_{i}\in X
and
b_{i}\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
such that
f(a_{i},b_{j})\neq f(a_{j},b_{i})
for some/all
i<j
. By indiscernibility, for each
i<\omega
there is
\sigma_{i}\in\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
such that
\sigma_{i}(b_{0})=b_{i}
and there exist real numbers
r<s
such that: (
f(a_{i},b_{j})=r<s=f(a_{j},b_{i})
for all
i<j
) or (
f(a_{i},b_{j})=s>r=f(a_{j},b_{i})
for all
i<j
). Hence,
f_{b_{0}}
is not a WAP function by Fact
2.13
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

The flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is WAP if and only if
X/E
is stable.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
By assumption, for any
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
and
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
,
f_{b}
is WAP, so
f(x,y)
is stable by Proposition
5.1
. Hence,
X/E
is stable by
[
KP22
, Corollary 2.2]
.

(\Leftarrow)
By assumption and
[
KP22
, Corollary 2.2]
, every function
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
is stable, so for any
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
the function
f_{b}
is WAP by Proposition
5.1
. Thus, since by
[
KP22
, Proposition 2.1]
the family of functions
\{f_{b}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E},b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}\}
separates points in
S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}})
, we conclude that
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is WAP by Fact
2.15
.
∎

Similarly, by
[
PF24
, Lemma 5.7]
, we know that the quotient
X/E
has NIP if and only if every
f\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
has NIP (see
[
PF24
, Definition 5.4]
for
n=1
). As mentioned in the introduction, the connection between NIP and tameness flows is well-known (first noticed independently in
[
CS18
]
,
[
Iba16
]
, and
[
Kha20
]
). This connection is still present for the hyperdefinable set
X/E
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
, and let
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f(x,y)
has NIP.

(2)

For all
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
the function
f_{b}
is tame.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
If
f_{b}
is not tame for some
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
, then there is a sequence
(\sigma_{i})_{i<\omega}\subset\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
such that the sequence of functions
(f(x,\sigma_{i}(b)))_{i<\omega}
is independent on
X
, so
f
has IP by compactness.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

If
f(x,y)
has
IP
, then by Ramsey’s theorem and compactness, there is an indiscernible sequence
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}\subset{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
for which the sequence
(f(x,b_{i}))_{i<\omega}
is independent on
X
.
By indiscernibility, for each
i<\omega
there is
\sigma_{i}\in\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
such that
\sigma_{i}(b_{0})=b_{i}
. Hence,
f_{b_{0}}
is not a tame function, because the sequence
(\sigma_{i}f_{b_{0}})_{i<\omega}
is independent.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

The flow
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is tame if and only if
X/E
is NIP.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
By assumption, for any
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
and
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
,
f_{b}
is tame, so
f(x,y)
has NIP by Proposition
5.3
. Hence,
X/E
has NIP by
[
PF24
, Lemma 5.7]
.

(\Leftarrow)
By assumption and
[
PF24
, Lemma 5.7]
, every function
f(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E}
has NIP, so for any
b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}
the function
f_{b}
is tame by Proposition
5.3
. Thus, since by
[
KP22
, Proposition 2.1]
the family of functions
\{f_{b}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{X/E},b\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lvert y\rvert}\}
separates points in
S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}})
, we conclude that
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X/E}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is tame by Fact
2.21
.
∎

Below we will use the notation introduced in Proposition
2.3
and the comments following it. Note that, while a
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on
X
induces a closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
, the converse is not true. Namely, not every closed,
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-invariant equivalence relation on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
is created this way. In the case of
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{WAP}}_{\emptyset}
and
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
, by Corollaries
5.2
and
5.4
, we get that
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
is a WAP flow and
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
is a tame flow. However, the next proposition shows that
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
may not be equal to
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
may not be equal to
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume that
X
is a
\emptyset
-type-definable subset of
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}
, and
{\mathfrak{C}}
is
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-saturated and strongly
(\aleph_{0}+\lambda)
-homogeneous.

(1)

If
X
is unstable, then
F_{\textrm{WAP}}\subsetneq\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
.

(2)

If
X
has IP, then
F_{\textrm{Tame}}\subsetneq\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
.

Proof.

The inclusions follow from the above observations that
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
is WAP and
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
is tame. It remains to show that
F_{\textrm{WAP}}\neq\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
and
F_{\textrm{Tame}}\neq\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
. We will prove the first thing; the proof of the second one is analogous.

Since
X
is unstable,
E^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}\neq\;\,=
, so
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
glues some types in
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
which are realized in
{\mathfrak{C}}
.

On the other hand, define a closed equivalence relation
E
on
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
by

pEq\iff p_{=}=q_{=},

where
p_{=}
and
q_{=}
denote the restrictions of
p
and
q
to the empty language (so we allow only the equality relation). Let
S^{=}_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
be the collection of all global types in the empty language of the elements from
X({\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}^{\prime}\succ{\mathfrak{C}}
is a monster model of the original theory in which
{\mathfrak{C}}
is small. Then
S^{=}_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})=\{\tp(a/{\mathfrak{C}})_{=}:a\in X\}\cup\{\textrm{the unique non-realized type}\}
is a closed subset of
S^{=}_{{\mathfrak{C}}}({\mathfrak{C}})
invariant under
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
. We also see that
S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/E\cong S^{=}_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})
as
\aut({\mathfrak{C}})
-flows.
As the theory of
{\mathfrak{C}}
in the empty language is stable, by Corollary
5.2
, we get that
(\Sym({\mathfrak{C}}),S^{=}_{{\mathfrak{C}}}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is WAP. Hence, since WAP is closed under decreasing the acting group and under taking subflows,
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S^{=}_{X}({\mathfrak{C}}))
is also WAP, and so is
(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/E)
. Therefore,
F_{\textrm{WAP}}\subseteq E
. Thus, since

does not glue any realized types in

, neither does
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
.

By the conclusions of the last two paragraphs, we conclude that
F_{\textrm{WAP}}\neq\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
.
∎

Although
F_{\textrm{WAP}}
and
F_{\textrm{Tame}}
are almost always strictly finer than
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
and
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
, the following question and its analog for the tame case remain open:

Question 5.6
.

Are the Ellis groups of the flows

(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/F_{\textrm{WAP}})

and

(\aut({\mathfrak{C}}),S_{X}({\mathfrak{C}})/\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset})

isomorphic?

Proposition
5.5
justifies our interest in

and

, because it suggests that the quotients by these equivalence relations should capture more information about the theory in question than the quotients by
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{st}}_{\emptyset}
and
\tilde{E}^{\textrm{NIP}}_{\emptyset}
while maintaining similar good properties (having in mind that WAP is a dynamical version of stability and tameness a dynamical version of NIP).

-type-definable equivalence relations

\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on

Proof.

The first part follows from the fact that stability and NIP are preserved under (possibly infinite) Cartesian products and taking type-definable subsets, and the observation that the hyperdefinable set

can be naturally identified with a type-definable subset of

For the moreover part, consider the
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relation on

defined as the intersection of all
\emptyset
-type-definable equivalence relations on

with stable [resp. NIP] quotient, and use the first part.

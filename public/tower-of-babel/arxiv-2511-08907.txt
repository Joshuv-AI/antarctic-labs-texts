Exit path categories induced by group actions
Patrick Mayeda

Abstract
. We prove a structural result concerning the exit path category associated to a manifold M M equipped with a smooth action of a finite group G G . Specifically, the functor Π : 𝖤𝗑𝗂𝗍 ⁡ ( 𝖬 ) → 𝖤𝗑𝗂𝗍 ⁡ ( 𝖬 / 𝖦 ) \Pi:\exit(M)\rightarrow\exit(M/G) is a right fibration and 𝖤𝗇𝗍𝖾𝗋 ⁡ ( M / G ) \mathsf{Enter}(M/G) is classified by a natural functor 𝖤𝗇𝗍𝖾𝗋 ⁡ ( M / G ) → 𝒪 G \mathsf{Enter}(M/G)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{G} , where 𝒪 G \mathcal{O}_{G} is the orbit category of G G . The main technical result manipulates exit paths to immediately exiting paths, enabling lifts of homotopies in M / G M/G to homotopies in M M .

We now provide a general outline of the paper. Section
\lx@sectionsign 1
starts off with a definition of orbit categories and provides explicit descriptions of their structure. In addition, it elucidates some properties of orbit categories that make computation of examples for finite groups
G

\lx@sectionsign 2

\lx@sectionsign 3

\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
is a covering space.

Moving on to section
\lx@sectionsign 4
, we begin to assume that the space we are working with is a manifold and that the action is smooth. We define immediately exiting paths and immediately exiting homotopies before going on to discuss the nature of the fiber bundles

previously alluded to, which arise as the fiberwise cones of a fiber bundle over a stratum

with total space given by the

, denoted

. The discussion of fiberwise cones of fiber bundles illuminates the deformation retract

and is an essential tool in ensuring that we may lift the exit the path homotopies

\mathcal{H}
and immediately exiting

is given inductively by first producing a homotopy

from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
takes image in a single stratum. This is done stratum by stratum, using the fiber bundle

2.1.
Properties of exit paths

Definition 2.1.1
.

Given a poset

on

is the topology consisting of the upward closed sets.

is open if and only if

and

implies that

. Note that for some fixed

forms a basis for this topology.

Definition 2.1.2
.

A

of a topological space

is a continuous map
\mathcal{S}:X\rightarrow P
where
P
is some poset with the Alexandrov topology.

Definition 2.1.3
.

A

of a stratification

is the preimage of a point

,
\mathcal{S}^{-1}(p)\subseteq X
. It is denoted as

This is the topic of the rest of the paper. For our purposes, we take all posets to be finite and

to be Hausdorff. The rest of the section is dedicated to some useful facts about exit path categories.

Definition 2.1.4
.

An

in a stratified space

is a path

such that for each
s\leq t
,
\mathcal{S}(f(s))\leq\mathcal{S}(f(t))
.

Definition 2.1.5
.

An

between exit paths

is a homotopy rel endpoints

such that for each

,

is an exit path. Two exit paths

are exit path homotopic (rel endpoints), written

if there exists an exit path homotopy between the two.

Definition 2.1.6
.

The

of a stratified topological space

, denoted

is defined by

i) Objects: points of
X

ii) Morphisms:

is then given by exit paths from

to

under the equivalence relation of exit path homotopy. Composition is given by concatenation of paths. The identity is the constant path.

It is required (though it is perhaps ’obvious’) to show that these data satisfy the axioms of a category. To do this, it is sufficient to show that the familiar properties of homotopy hold when we restrict to exit path homotopies.

4.
Lifting Homotopies

To show that
\Pi
is a fibration, it is not enough to lift exit paths. One needs to be assured that the lift is well defined. Given two exit paths
f\simeq_{\text{exit}}g
in
X/G
, one needs to be able to say that the two lifts
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}
are exit path homotopic. Looking at the exit path homotopy
\mathcal{H}
between
f
and
g
, a choice of a lift of
f(1)
and

determines a choice of lift for
every
horizontal strip,
\mathcal{H}|_{I\times\{s\}}
. This is clearly a lift of
\mathcal{H}
as a set function. One can hope that this is continuous, but continuity on the horizontal strips does not guarantee continuity on the entirety of the square. After this idea, one can resort to constructing a lift in the same way we lifted paths, stratum by stratum. Morally, one should expect more success doing this. This is taking the success in one dimension and applying it to the next. The problem however is that the strata in
I\times I
induced by the stratification of
X/G
have very few restraints. There is not a decomposition of
I\times I
into half-open half-closed intervals as in the case of
I
. One may even think of homotopies inducing fractal boundaries. With this in mind, it is difficult to envision the success of an application of covering space theory.

At this juncture we impose an extra hypothesis, namely that the stratifications of
X,X/G
are conically smooth. This is met if
X
is a manifold and the action of
G
is smooth. The definition of a conically smooth stratification is inductive and rather involved; it may be found in Ayala, Francis and Tanaka’s work on stratified spaces
[
1
]
. The utility of this extra hypothesis as it pertains to lifting homotopies is the existence of tubular neighborhoods about strata that deform nicely onto the strata themselves. More than a deformation, they assemble into fiber bundles over the strata. Through these mechanisms, we construct a lift.

.

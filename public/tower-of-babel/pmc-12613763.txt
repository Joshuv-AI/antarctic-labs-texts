Multiple infected cardiac myxoma in young female patient complicated with multiple systemic infarctions: case report and review of literature
Authors: Mohammed Aldahmashi, Mohamed Alassal, Saifeddin Ibrahim, Amr Sharqawi, Ayman Elsaily, Ramzy Alkholidy, Anhar Alsayed, M Moselehi, Mahmoud Zayed, Maher Abdelhadi, Hussien Fakher, M Shehab, M Anagera, Atef Farag, Abubakr Siddig, Ahmed Elamin, Ibrahim Altaj, Khaled Yasin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiac myxomas are the most common primary heart tumors, yet their occurrence in young patients, particularly when infected and leading to multiple systemic infarctions, is exceedingly rare. This case highlights the critical need for early diagnosis and intervention. An 18-year-old female presented with orthopnea, low-grade fever, chest tightness, and pulmonary edema. She had experienced dyspnea, weight loss, recurrent abdominal pain, and chronic anemia over the past six months. Examination revealed congested lungs, sweating, tachycardia, and laboratory findings indicative of inflammation. Echocardiography identified multiple obstructing masses in the left atrium moving into the left ventricle during diastole, causing severe mitral regurgitation. Imaging revealed old infarcts in the brain, liver, and spleen. Blood cultures were positive for Enterococcus faecalis, and empirical antibiotics were initiated. Urgent surgery was performed, including left atrial myxoma resection, mitral valve replacement with a bioprosthetic valve, left atrial appendage closure, and closure of the interatrial septum. Postoperative echocardiography confirmed normal left ventricular function and a well-functioning bioprosthetic mitral valve. The patient tolerated the procedure well and was discharged on a regimen of oral antibiotics for six weeks.DiscussionInfected cardiac myxomas, though rare, pose significant risks due to their potential for systemic embolization and severe complications. This case emphasizes the necessity of comprehensive diagnostic evaluation and timely surgical intervention to prevent catastrophic outcomes.ConclusionThis case underscores the importance of a multidisciplinary approach and early surgical intervention in managing complex cases of infected cardiac myxoma with systemic embolization. Our findings reinforce the need for vigilance in diagnosing and treating such rare but life-threatening conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-024-03170-3.

Introduction

Cardiac Myxoma is a common intracardiac benign neoplasm but it rarely becomes infected, presenting with symptoms of fever or with sepsis of unknown origin. The occurrence of three infected cardiac myxomas in single patient is highly unusual. Herein, we present this rare case and the surgical treatment provided to this condition.

Case report

On February 25, 2024, an 18-year-old female with a height of 150 cm and a weight of 55 kg (BMI: 24.4 kg/m2), her blood group is O positive presented to our cardiac center with Orthopnea, low-grade fever, chest tightness and pulmonary edema. She was admitted to the critical cardiac unit (CCU). For the past six months, she had experienced Dyspnea, weight loss and recurrent abdominal pain attacks and chronic anemia. Upon her examination she appeared ill with congested lungs, sweating, tachycardia. Laboratory findings strongly indicated inflammation. Echocardiography showed huge multiple obstructing masses in left atrium (LA) moving to left ventricle (LV) in diastole with severe mitral regurgitation. Imaging studies showed old brain, liver and spleen infarcts. Three Blood cultures were obtained and empirical antibiotic therapy was initiated as infected Myxoma and mitral valve endocarditis were suspected. Chest and lung radiographs displayed a normal sized a cardiac silhouette with adequate pulmonary markings, and no focal lesions. An electrocardiogram showed sinus tachycardia (heart rate, 115–120 beats/ min) and no myocardial ischemia. Laboratory investigations revealed elevated levels of C-reactive protein (187 mg/L) and Procalcitonin (1.76 µg/L). Transesophageal echocardiography confirmed that huge cauliflower mass arose from the mitral annulus and protruded into the LV during diastole (Fig. 1) causing functional moderate mitral valves stenosis. Another small size mass attached to the interatrial septum appeared superior to the fossa ovale. There was severe posterior directed mitral valve regurgitation but no signs of valve destruction or perforation were evident as well as no annular dilation. Left ventricular systolic function and size was normal. The left atrium was mildly dilated (4.4 cm) and the other valves were normal. No thrombus was detected in the left atrial appendage.

The right ventricular systolic pressure was elevated at 40-50mmHg. The consensus was made to be treated surgically urgently. Blood cultures obtained preoperatively had been positive for bacteria Enterococcus faecalis. Preoperative routine workup which included a Computed Tomography (CT) Scan of the chest, abdomen and brain, revealed left basal pneumonitis, atelectasis, moderate pericardial effusion, small bilateral pleural effusion, moderate hepato-splenomegly with multiple masses suggesting infarcts and multiple brain chronic bilateral infarcts. Lab results showed low hemoglobin level of 6 gm/dl. All benefits/ risks of LA Myxoma resection and Mitral valve replacement with bioprosthetic valve were discussed with the patient and her family then they agreed, and signed the informed consent. Intravenous antibiotics empirically were started. Upon transfer to Operation Theater (OR), the patient still had a low-grade fever.

Procedure details: Left Atrial Myxomas Resection, Mitral valve Replacement by Bioprosthetic valve and Left Atrial Appendage Closure and closure of interatrial septum by synthetic pericardial patch. The patient was shifted from CCU to OR. Access to the left radial artery was done, then she was sedated, intubated and ventilated then the central venous lines were done. Intraoperative transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE) probe was inserted. A median longitudinal sternotomy was made. The patient was heparinized and cannulated and cold blood cardioplegia was given. Then through right atrial and trans-septal approach, the Myxoma masses were excised completely with the safety margin bases: 1st mass 2 × 2 cm arising from the septum above the Fossa Ovalis with wide base, 2nd mass size was 4 × 5 cm raising from the roof of the LA, The 3rd mass size was 5 × 5 cm raising from the septum 5 cm above the postero-medial commissure.(Fig. 3). The LV was checked for any mass or fragments. The Mitral valve was assessed. There are signs of tumor involvement as multiple myxomatic masses on the anterior leaflets furthermore, the Saline test revealed severe mitral valve regurgitation. The Mitral valve was excised with chordal preservation. The annulus was measured and accommodated a 27 mm valve therefore Tissue Bioprosthetic Medtronic Mosaic 27MM was implanted by using 2/0 Ethibond pledgeted stitches. Saline test showed well-functioning Bio-prosthesis leaflets. (Fig. 4). The Left atrial appendage was closed. The Inter-atrial septum has been closed by 4/0 proline suture using Gore pericardial membrane and the other defects were closed by direct Proline suture). The heart resumes its normal sinus rhythm. Closure of the right atrium by double layer 5/0 Prolin on beating heart. The pericardial Pacing wires were implanted into the right ventricle wall. Gradual weaning off bypass machine was achieved successfully and uneventful. TEE showed well seated and functioning mitral valve bioprosthesis, normal LV function and no residual masses. The patient tolerated the operation well and was then transferred to CVICU in a stable condition, maintaining adequate mean arterial pressure, urine output, oxygenation and ventilation, good ABG, normal ECG and no bleeding. The patient had been smooth recovery in CVICU and during ward course.

Discussion

Myxoma comprises 50% of all benign cardiac tumors in adults and 15% of such tumors in children. The peak incidence is between the third and sixth decades of life, though cases have been observed in younger patients e.g., 18 years old as in our case. 94% of these tumors are solitary with approximately 75% occuring in the left atrium [1, 2]. The incidence of asymptomatic cardiac myxoma varies widely from 0.01 to 28.4%, and has been increasing with the advancement in imaging technologies and enhanced screening opportunities [3]. Despite being the most common benign cardiac tumour, infected and complicated Cardiac myxoma is very rare. Infected cardiac myxoma is highly fragile, with the potential to embolize, leading to catastrophic conditions such as systemic bacteremia, systemic mycotic embolism, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) [4]. Clinically myxoma is presents with classic triads: intracardiac obstruction with congestive heart failure (67%); signs of embolization (29%); systemic or constitutional symptoms such as fever (19%); weight loss or fatigue (17%); and immunologic manifestations of myalgia, weakness, and arthralgia (5%). All these manifestations were classically present in our case [1].

The differential diagnosis of infected myxoma primarily includes uninfected myxoma, as fever can occur without infection, as well as mural endocarditis and infected intracardiac thrombus.

Myxoma is associated with systemic embolism [5, 6], although the risk appears to be greater when the myxoma is infected. Since the clinical presentation of infected myxoma may be similar to that of uninfected myxoma, blood cultures should be done whenever a patient with myxoma presents with fever. Trans thoracic Echocardiography and even transesophageal (TEE) should be performed in patients with fever of unknown origin when initial investigations are inconclusive [7, 8].

Our patient presented with an intermittent fever, which occurs in approximately 10–20% of patients with cardiac myxoma [9, 10]. Inflammatory markers cytokines may cause the fever, which makes it difficult to differentiate between infected and non-infected myxoma preoperatively which should prompt further diagnostic investigations to rule out infection or other complications. Therefore, in a febrile patient with myxoma, it is necessary to do blood cultures and sensitivity testing before initiating preoperative antimicrobial therapy. Yuan reported that Gram-positive cocci such as Streptococcus and Staphylococcus accounted for about 70% of the causative organisms [11]. In our case, Enterococcus Faecalis was detected in preoperative blood cultures and this was very unusual. In some reports, antimicrobial agents are administered as per the treatment guidelines for infective endocarditis [3, 4]. Thus, we started the treatment described in the guidelines for infective endocarditis [9] and used intravenous antibiotics for four weeks post-surgery.

30–50% of left atrial myxomas cause embolisms [5, 7] The incidence of embolization was reported to be two to three times higher in patients with infected than non-infected cardiac myxoma because of the fragile fibrin thrombus on the tumor surface, as seen in infective endocarditis [8, 11] In our case, various old and fresh multiple cerebral, splenic, and hepatic infarctions were also detected by CT imaging.

Considering the risk of embolization, surgery should be performed as soon as possible after diagnosis in such cases. We suggest there is no benefit in delaying surgery until the results of blood cultures are known.

Conclusion

We reported the successful urgent surgical management of a case of complicated infected multiple cardiac myxoma. This case underscores the importance of the multidisciplinary approach, comprehensive diagnostic evaluation with reinforcing the need for early detection and timely intervention in patients with cardiac myxomas, especially when complicated by severe mitral regurgitation and systemic embolization.

Analytic structure of stress-energy response functions and new Kubo formulae
Sangyong Jeon, Alina Czajka, Juhee Hong

Abstract
Determining the transport properties of Quark-Gluon Plasma is one of the most important aspects of relativistic heavy ion collision studies. Field-theoretical calculations of the transport coefficients such as the shear and bulk viscosities require Kubo formulae which in turn require real-time correlation functions of stress-energy tensors. Consequently, knowing the analytic structure of these correlation functions is essential in any such studies. Using the energy-conservation laws and the results from the gravity-hydrodynamics analysis, we determine the low-frequency and low-wavenumber analytic structures of all stress-energy correlation functions in the rest frame of the medium. By comparing with the diffusion and sound spectra from the second-order and the third-order relativistic hydrodynamics, various new Kubo formulae are derived in the limit where the zero-frequency limit is taken first. We also show that the meaning of the Kubo formulae for relaxation times can change when higher-order terms are added to hydrodynamics. A subtle issue of taking the zero frequency and zero wavenumber limits when using skeleton diagrams is addressed as well.

VII
On diagrammatic calculations of viscosities

Standard diagrammatic discussion of shear viscosity
via the Kubo formula Eq.(
71
)
starts with the one-loop diagram with bare propagators.
Upon realizing that this diagram does not produce a sensible answer and also that the
viscosity should inversely depend on the transport cross-section, one then introduces resummed
propagators to see that
\displaystyle\eta_{\hbox{\scriptsize 1-loop}}\propto 1/\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}

where
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}

is the thermal width of the
propagator (equivalently, the imaginary part of the self-energy).
This inverse dependence then necessitates resummation of the ladder diagrams via
a Schwinger-Dyson equation for the dressed vertex.

What gets often overlooked in this process is that as long as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}\neq 0
, it does not matter whether the limit
\displaystyle k\to 0
is taken first or the limit

\displaystyle\omega\to 0
is taken first. As we have shown in this paper,
only
\displaystyle\partial_{\omega}G_{R}^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})

and
\displaystyle\partial_{\omega}G^{\Delta P\Delta P}(\omega,{\bf k})
are independent of the order of the
limits.
All other response functions give different answers
when different orders of the limits are taken. For instance, Eq.(
72
)
and Eq.(
81
) are different from the usual Kubo formula for
\displaystyle\eta

and
\displaystyle\zeta
.

It is then worth asking what really is being calculated with the usual skeleton
diagram expansion technique using the resummed propgators.
For simplicity, let us consider the one-loop diagram for

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta=i\lim_{\omega\to 0}\lim_{k_{z}\to 0}\partial_{\omega}G_{R}^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})

(87)

in the real scalar
\displaystyle\lambda\phi^{4}
theory.
Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm Im}G_{R}^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})

\displaystyle\displaystyle={1\over 2}(1-e^{-\beta\omega})\sigma^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})

(88)

where
\displaystyle\sigma^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})
is the Wightman function,
we only need to examine

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma^{xy,xy}_{\hbox{\scriptsize 1-loop}}(\omega,k_{z})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=2\int{d^{4}p\over(2\pi)^{4}}\,p_{x}^{2}p_{y}^{2}n(p^{0})(1+n(p^{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(p^{0}+\omega,{\bf p}+k_{z}{\bf e}_{z})\rho_{\phi}(p^{0},{\bf p})

(89)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(p)=-i\left({1\over(p^{0}-i\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}/2)^{2}-E_{p}^{2}}-{1\over(p^{0}+i\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}/2)^{2}-E_{p}^{2}}\right)

is the spectral density for the resummed propagator with
\displaystyle E_{p}=\sqrt{{\bf p}^{2}+m^{2}}
.
The 4 poles of
\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(p^{0}+\omega,{\bf p}+k_{z}{\bf e}_{z})
are at

\displaystyle p^{0}\approx\pm i\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}/2-\omega\pm(E_{p}+p_{z}k_{z}/E_{p})
.
Since
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}/2\neq 0
, it does not matter
to the pinching pole contribution
which limit is taken first.
We can just set
\displaystyle\omega=k=0
.
This is precisely what we expected from
\displaystyle G_{2}
.

Now let’s consider Eq.(
71
). We now have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{1\over 20}\Delta_{ij,lm}\sigma^{ij,lm}_{\hbox{\scriptsize 1-loop}}(\omega,{\bf k})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int{d^{4}p\over(2\pi)^{4}}\,n(p^{0})(1+n(p^{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle{1\over 30}(4{\bf p}^{4}+8{\bf p}^{2}({\bf p}\cdot{\bf k})+({\bf p}\cdot{\bf k})^{2}+3{\bf k}^{2}{\bf p}^{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(p^{0}+\omega,{\bf p}+{\bf k})\rho_{\phi}(p^{0},{\bf p})

(91)

The pole positions are at

\displaystyle p^{0}\approx\pm i\Gamma_{\hbox{\scriptsize th}}/2-\omega\pm(E_{p}+{\bf p}\cdot{\bf k}/E_{p})
and again there is no
obstacle in just setting
\displaystyle\omega=k=0
.
But that violates what we found in Eqs.(
71
) and
(
72
) that the two orders of the limits do not commute. Therefore,
it is not clear whether Eq.(
91
) is evaluating Eq.(
71
)
or Eq.(
72
).
In fact, when
\displaystyle\omega=k=0
, the right-hand-side of
Eq.(
91
) should be interpreted as
just the angle averaged version of Eq.(
89
).
Hence all calculations that started with Eq.(
71
) but could be able to
set
\displaystyle\omega=k=0
without any obstacles may have all been actually using
\displaystyle G_{2}
via
Eq.(
87
).

For the bulk viscosity calculation,
the function
\displaystyle\partial_{\omega}G^{PP}
does care about the order of the limits
while ladder diagrams do not.
Fortunately, from Eqs.(
82
) and (
83
),
we see that it is permissible to just set
\displaystyle\omega=k=0

as long as the auto-correlation function of
\displaystyle\Delta\hat{P}=\hat{P}-v_{s}^{2}\hat{\epsilon}
is used.
This may be implying that the ladder diagram resummation is only valid for this particular
correlation function.

A question then arises: How do we use Kubo formulae
that do depend on the order of the limits?
5
5

5

Except the thermodynamic ones such as
Eqs.(
,
85
,
86
).

The answer seems to be that
they can be used to calculate viscosities
only if some non-perturbative approximation results in hydrodynamic poles for the
correlation functions.
For instance, Eq.(
75
)
makes sense only if an approximation of

\displaystyle G_{R}^{0x,0x}(\omega,k_{y})=(k_{y}^{2}/\omega^{2})G_{1}(\omega,k_{y}^{2})
contains a diffusion
pole.
For instance, the usual skeleton diagram resummation does not accomplish this, but
the Anderson localization schemes can yield a diffusion pole by assuming that the
interaction is point-like

Vollhardt and Wölfle 1980
;
Chu and Zhang 1993
;
Giannakis et al. 2008
;
Giannakis et al. 2007
.
Thus, further development of non-perturbative approximation schemes is necessary
to fully utilize the new Kubo formulae.

VIII
Conclusions

The main purpose of this work is
to elucidate the analytic structure of the stress-energy tensor correlation
functions in the small
\displaystyle\omega
and small
\displaystyle k
limit.
We succeeded in doing so by combining the energy-momentum conservation laws and the
results from the gravity-hydrodynamics calculations and the recently developed
third order hydrodynamics.

From this endeavour, we have drawn the following conclusions.
First, the response function often used in the calculation of the shear viscosity,

\displaystyle G^{xy,xy}_{R}(\omega,k_{z})
, does not in fact possess the shear diffusion pole.
In retrospect, it is almost obvious that this particular correlator
cannot possess the shear diffusion pole
since variation only in the
\displaystyle z
direction cannot induce diffusion in the
transverse (
\displaystyle x,y
) directions.
On the other hand,
\displaystyle G_{R}^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{y})
is the
full hydrodynamics response function that possesses a proper shear diffusion pole.
The usual calculations via skeleton expansion in fact correspond to using

\displaystyle G_{R}^{xy,xy}(\omega,k_{z})
even though the isotropic version of the Kubo formula is used.
Similarly, skeleton expansion for the bulk viscosity makes sense only if
the auto-correlation function of

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{P}=\hat{P}-v_{s}^{2}\hat{\epsilon}
is used.

Second, the Kubo formulae for the relaxation times are unreliable because
the result of the second order hydrodynamic analysis and the third order one do not
match. One should perhaps go back to the kinetic theory analysis to establish
what the Kubo formulae for the relaxation times actually mean.

Third, we have shown that the viscosities can also be calculated with the
\displaystyle\omega\to 0
limit
taken first. For instance, the pressure-pressure spectral density can give the bulk
viscosity or the shear viscosity depending on whether the
\displaystyle k\to 0
limit is taken first
or the
\displaystyle\omega\to 0
limit is taken first. We have also derived some interesting Kubo
formulae where the shear viscosity to enthalpy ratio is inversely proportional to some
power of the correlation functions. These formulae may offer an intriguing possibility
to further study the
\displaystyle\eta/s
bound in terms of the bound on the transport cross-section.

Even though the
\displaystyle\omega\to 0
limit is taken first, the new Kubo formulae are not really
thermodynamical or static because usually an
\displaystyle\omega
-derivative is involved.
Equivalently, these formulae use the imaginary part of the retarded correlation functions.
In this regard,
the Kubo formula in Eq.(
76
) is special because it uses the
real

part of a retarded correlation function.
Although the second-order frequency derivative makes it non-trivial to connect
it to a purely Euclidean formulation (thus to simpler lattice calculations),
it may provide an intriguing possibility.
We should note that to use the new Kubo formulae fully, one needs an approximation
scheme where the resummed correlation functions actually exhibit hydrodynamic poles.
In this sense, investigation of new techniques
to calculate the explicit pole structure of the correlation functions would be important.

Finally, we would like to note that
for the sake of brevity, we have omitted many of the details in the derivations in this work.
We will report them in a separate publication.

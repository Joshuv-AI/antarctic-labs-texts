Molecular and epidemiological analysis of measles vaccine-associated rash illnesses in Shandong province, China, 2014–2021.
Authors: Suting Wang, Xueqiang Fang, Xiaodong Liu, Yao Liu, Ping Xiong, Qing Xu, Li Zhang, Meng Chen, Zexin Tao, Aiqiang Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile the live attenuated measles virus vaccine is generally safe and highly effective, it has been associated with a significant number of vaccine-associated rash illnesses (VARIs). Some VARIs had been identified through the measles case-based surveillance system in Shandong province.MethodsIn our study, we comprehensively examined the epidemiological characteristics of VARIs using a cross-sectional study design with comparative subgroup analysis. Additionally, we conducted a detailed analysis of the genetic properties of VARIs.ResultsAmong the 189 cases of VARIs, 90.47% (171 cases) were children aged 8 to 18 months who had received a single dose of measles-containing vaccine (MCV). Background

Measles virus (MeV) are single-stranded, negative-sense RNA viruses belonging to the genus Morbillivirus, family Paramyxoviridae [1]. Measles is transmitted via airborne route and its clinical presentation includes fever, maculopapular rash, and at least one of the “3Cs”: cough, coryza, or conjunctivitis [2, 3]. Shandong Province incorporated measles-containing vaccines into immunization program in 1978 and introduced a two-dose measles vaccination schedule in 1986. Since then, the measles incidence rate has fallen sharply from 6.75 per 100,000 in 1986 to 0.05 per 100,000 in 2021. Although the vaccine is safe and effective [4, 5], vaccination with the live attenuated measles virus vaccine causes vaccine-associated rash illnesses (VARI) in a subset of vaccine recipients [6, 7]. VARI is a reaction that can appear after measles vaccination and has been reported in 5–15% of MMR recipients [8, 9]. World Health Organization Regional Office for the Western Pacific recommend the definition for identifying VARI [10, 11]. In China, nationwide measles immunization employs a vaccine derived from the indigenous Shanghai-191 strain (genotype A) [12], which has contributed substantially to measles incidence reduction. Molecular surveillance has consistently detected this vaccine strain in reported measles cases [13–15].

Here, we present a molecular epidemiological analysis of VARI in Shandong Province to investigate its distribution characteristics and transmission patterns within the population.

Material and methods

Source and analysis of data

Shandong Province is located in the eastern coastal region of China, bordering the Bohai and Yellow Seas. With a total area of approximately 157,900 square kilometers, it ranks among China’s most populous provinces, having a permanent resident population of over 101 million as of 2021 census data. Since 2009, a measles surveillance system (MSS) has been in operation in Shandong. The VARI cases for this study were identified from this system based on the WHO Western Pacific Regional Office (WPRO) case definition, which includes the following criteria: i) a rash illness (with or without fever) without concurrent cough or other respiratory symptoms; ii) rash onset 7–14 days after vaccination; iii) a positive measles IgM test; iv) no secondary cases identified upon field investigation; v) no other causes found through field and laboratory investigations. A case was also confirmed by isolation or genotyping of the vaccine strain (genotype A). All the information of VARI cases was also obtained from Shandong MSS. Data on measles-containing vaccine (MCV) doses administered were obtained from the Shandong Immunization Program Information System. Clinical samples were collected from suspected measles cases in Shandong Province between 2014 and 2021. For all VARI cases included in this study, throat swab specimens were collected as the standard clinical sample type. Based on the presence or absence of genotypic evidence, VARI cases were divided into two groups for analysis. Group I were cases with genotype A, while Group II were cases that met the WHO Western Pacific Region’s five criteria but lacked genotypic evidence. Data aggregation and analysis for VARI cases were performed using Microsoft Excel. Chi-square tests and bi-variate correlations analysis were computed with R statistical software (R Project for Statistical Computing, R version 3.5.1). p values of 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Sequence amplification and sequencing

All samples were sequenced and genotyped using the protocols below. Briefly, viral RNA was extracted from 140 μl of throat swab specimens preserved in viral transport medium and stored at − 80 °C in nuclease-free water. The nucleotides of measles virus N-450 were amplified. The primers were MeV-F (5′-GGGAGGCTTGAACTT -TGG-3′) and MeV-R (5′-TCCG -TGTCTGAGCCTTGT-3′). The program was: 50 °C for 30 min,98 °C for 10 s; 98 °C for 1 s, 55 °C for 5 s, 72 °C for 30 s for 30 cycles; and a final extension step at 72 °C for 10 min. Sequences were obtained using Big Dye terminator chemistry version 3.0 for both sense and antisense strands on an automated ABI PRISM 3100 Genetic Analyzer.

Sequence analysis

Sequences were assembled and initially analyzed using Sequencher 4.1.4, and subsequently aligned using BioEdit 7.0.5.3. Phylogenetic analysis was conducted based on the N-450 coding region using MEGA 7.0. A phylogenetic tree was constructed with the Maximum Likelihood method, incorporating Shandong strains and WHO-defined reference strains. The reliability was estimated using bootstrap resampling of 1000 replicates.

Phylogenetic analysis using the bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo method

The evolution rate and molecular clock phylogeny of 33 Shandong genotype A strains were computed with the Bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method in BEAST version 1.6.1 [16]. The optimal nucleotide substitution model was selected using MEGA 7.0 [17, 18]. Subsequently, the clock and demographic models were compared by calculating Akaike’s Information Criterion through Markov Chain Monte Carlo (AICM) in Tracer v1.6.1 [19], which was used to determine the best combined model [20, 21]. we evaluated four molecular clock models: a strict clock, an uncorrelated lognormal relaxed clock, an uncorrelated exponential relaxed clock, and a random local clock [22]. We also compared two demographic models: a constant population size model and an exponential growth model. The best-fit model combination, determined by AICM, was the Jukes-Cantor (JC) substitution model with a strict clock and a constant population size. The MCMC analysis was run for 30,000,000 steps, with sampling every 10,000 steps. Convergence was confirmed by ensuring that all effective sample size (ESS) values, computed in Tracer v1.6.1, exceeded 200. Parameter uncertainty was assessed using 95% Highest Posterior Density (HPD) intervals. The evolutionary rate for the genotype A strains was estimated accordingly.

Selective pressure analysis

All genotype A sequences were screened for positive selection sites using the CODEML algorithm implemented in EasyCodeML v1.21 [23]. Nonsynonymous (dN) and synonymous (dS) substitution rates were calculated with site-specific model. Seven substitution models were used and comparisons of M0 (one ratio) versus M3 (discrete), M1a (nearly neutral) versus M2a (positive selection), M7 (β) versus M8 (β+ωs > 1) and M8a (β+ωs = 1) versus M8 were made to indicate the. positively selected sites (ω > 1, where ω = dN/dS). Out of them, M7 versus M8 is the most stringent test [24], and representations of the results of the model comparisons were evaluated using likelihood ratio tests (LRTs), and p < 0.05 indicate the significance. Prediction of positive selection was implemented by Bayes method, with the posterior probability providing good evidence of reliability [25, 26]

Results

Epidemiological characteristics analysis

We scanned all 10117 cases reported through Shandong Measles Surveillance System and obtained 189 (1.87%) VARI cases. Among 189 cases, there were 100 male and 89 female cases, yielding a sex ratio of 1.12:1. No significant differences in sex distribution were observed across years (χ2 = 8.11, p = 0.32). Among the cases, 90.47% (171/189) were children aged 8–18 months, 1.59% (3/189) were aged 19–24 months, 1.59% (3/189) were aged 2–6 years, and 6.35% (12/189) were older than 6 years. The median age was 9 months, with significant age differences noted (χ2 = 39.47, p = 0.009). Children aged 8–18 months constituted the predominant group. Regarding vaccination status, 91.01% (172/189) of cases had received one dose of MCV, 8.47% (16/189) had received two doses, and only one case had received more than three doses. The distribution of vaccination doses showed statistically significant differences (χ2 = 42.67, p < 0.01).

The annual VARI case count peaked in 2015 (n = 56), with subsequent highs in 2016 (n = 29) and 2020 (n = 26). Analysis of clinical symptoms

The rash in VARI cases appeared within 5–14 days post-vaccination, with a median onset of 9 days. Fever was observed in 95.24% (180/189) of cases, occurring within 1–14 days post-vaccination, with a median onset of 8 days (Fig. 3). A non-parametric test confirmed statistically significant differences in the median timing of fever and rash onset (p < 0.01), with fever typically preceding rash. Regarding clinical symptoms, 10 cases presented with conjunctivitis alone, another 10 cases with Koplik spots alone, and 10 cases experienced both conjunctivitis and Koplik spots. Additionally, one case exhibited a combination of conjunctivitis, Koplik spots, and diarrhea. In total, 31 cases (16.40%) developed at least one clinical symptom. Additionally, 11 cases (5.82%) experienced other symptoms, primarily pneumonia and diarrhea.

Genetic analysis of N-450 sequences of vari cases

Among the 189 VARI cases, throat swab samples were collected from 109 cases, with 89 cases sampled within 5 days of rash onset, yielding a 47.09% sampling rate within this period. Genotyping was successful in 30.28% (33/109) of the cases. Genetic analysis of the N-450 sequences from these 33 cases revealed that all strains belonged to genotype A. Compared to the Chinese vaccine strain Shanghai-191, the nucleotide identity of Shandong genotype A sequences ranged from 99.7% to 100%, with 90.91% (30/33) of sequences being identical to Shanghai-191. Variations were observed in three sequences and the positions were according to reference NC_001498: i. MVi/ShanDong.CHN/10.14: G-to-A substitution at nucleotide 1183 resulting in an amino acid change from S to N. ii. MVi/ShanDong.CHN/27.16: T-to-G substitution at nucleotide 1307, leading to an amino acid change from L to V. iii. MVi/ShanDong.CHN/3.21: G-to-A substitution at nucleotide 1310 with no amino acid change.

Phylogenetic reconstruction demonstrated that all 33 sequenced strains formed a distinct cluster with the Shanghai-191 vaccine strain, clearly separate from the genotype A wild-type reference sequence MVi/Maryland.USA/0.54/A (Fig. 5). The average evolutionary distance among the 33 sequences was 0.176, compared to 8.088 with MVi/Maryland.USA/0.54/A. To explore genetic dynamics, the evolutionary rate was calculated using the Bayesian MCMC method, yielding a rate of 1.6 × 10–4 substitutions/site/year (95%HPDS: 1.049 × 10–6~4.98 × 10–4). Analysis of selective pressure on codon sites of the N-450 gene showed an average ω value of 0.845 across all codon sites, consistent with models M1a and M3, indicating conservation at all sites. Likelihood Ratio Tests (LRTs) found no evidence of positive selection in the M7 versus M8 model comparison.

Discussion

Currently, there is no universally accepted definition for Vaccine-Associated Rash Illness (VARI), highlighting the need for further clarification of its characteristics. In this study, we adopted the definition recommended by the World Health Organization Regional Office for the Western Pacific, identifying 189 VARI cases. Among these, 33 cases (17.5%) were successfully genotyped, while the remaining 156 (82.5%) lacked definitive genotype information.

Our study demonstrates that VARI predominantly occurs in children aged 8–18 months following a single dose of MCV. The rash typically developed 5–14 days post-vaccination (median: 9 days). These results align with reports by Gans and Strebel, who noted that measles vaccination may more frequently trigger fever and rash in infants and young children due to their immature immune systems, with symptoms typically appearing within 5–12 days after vaccination [27, 28]. Other potential factors contributing to VARI include genetic predisposition, coincidental conditions, allergic reactions, and more [29]. Given these findings, it is crucial for parents to closely monitor their children’s health within 1 to 2 weeks after MCV vaccination, particularly following the first dose. Although VARI is not infectious and self-limiting, we recommend seeking medical attention promptly if symptoms arise, given the young age of the affected children and the difficulty for parents to distinguish VARI from other febrile rash illnesses. Moreover, timely medical consultation helps improve the timeliness and accuracy of adverse event monitoring and reporting following MCV vaccination.

Genetic analysis of genotype A revealed that the sequences were highly conserved and nearly identical to the Shanghai-191 strain. No evidence of selective pressure was observed among the 33 Shandong genotype A sequences, and their evolutionary rate was significantly lower compared to wild-type measles virus. Collectively, these findings—high sequence similarity, absence of selective pressure, and low evolutionary rate—suggest that genotype A is not actively circulating in the population, and this conclusion supported by other studies [30, 31]. Therefore, treating VARI cases with the same level of urgency as measles cases would result in an inefficient allocation of public health resources.

Although some clinical differences were observed between VARI cases and confirmed measles cases, clinical criteria alone were insufficient to consistently distinguish the two [32]. Indeed, some vaccine-strain-confirmed VARI cases exhibited minor ‘measles-like’ symptoms, illustrating the difficulty of distinguishing VARI from true measles on clinical features alone. Our results point to the possibility that VARI cases were confused with modified measles (mild wild‑type measles in previously vaccinated individuals). This possibility was further supported by bivariate linear correlation analysis, which revealed a significant linear correlation between the morbidity of VARI cases and measles (R = 0.76, p = 0.03). These findings underscore the necessity of promoting genotyping for VARI cases.

However, genotyping has a limitation in that many VARI cases could not be genotyped. In our study, we were able to genotype only 33 of 109 samples (30.28%). Only 47% of cases had samples taken within 5 days of rash onset in our study. Sequencing success drops sharply beyond the window. Both low viral load and delayed collection limited genotyping. Moreover, only the N‑450 fragment was sequenced and partial sequencing has limited discriminatory power. Mixed infections or rare variants might be missed. Thus, genotyping alone cannot absolutely confirm vaccine origin. Nevertheless, even when genotyping is delayed or yields inconclusive results, case investigations and notifications must still be conducted, as wild-type measles infection should be presumed [33]. Ultimately, there is a critical need for rapid, resource-efficient, vaccine strain-specific diagnostic methods. Such assays have recently been developed and successfully integrated into public health practice [34, 35].

Conclusion

In Shandong Province, VARI cases primarily occurred in children aged 8 to 18 months who had received one dose of MCV. The onset of rash typically occurred within 5–14 days post-vaccination, with a median of 9 days. Our findings reinforce the evidence that genotype A (vaccine) viruses are not transmitted from person to person. Distinguishing VARI cases from measles is crucial to prevent the unnecessary allocation of public health resources.

However, our study identified potential misclassification of VARI cases, likely due to confusion with modified measles. Since genotyping is often delayed or inconclusive, there is an urgent need to develop rapid, resource-efficient, vaccine strain-specific assays to optimize the use of public health resources.

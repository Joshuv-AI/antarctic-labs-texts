Kinetic theories: from curved space to flat space
Shu Lin

Abstract
We show how to map kinetic theories for spinning particles in curved space to flat space through choice of inertial frame that eliminate forces acting on momentum and spin, thus putting the idea of mimicking off-equilibrium state by metric perturbation to equilibrium state on firm ground. The equivalence has been shown for hydrodynamic and elastic regimes. In the latter case, we predict spin polarization induced by time-variation of strain in elastic matter.



Introduction

It has been commonly believed that metric perturbation to equilibrium state can be a proxy for off-equilibrium state in flat space. A well-known example is the off-equilibrium state in the hydrodynamic regime, for which the temperature variation can be mimicked by gravitoelectric potential
Luttinger:1964zz
. More recently fluid motion is mimicked by gravitomagnetic potential
Nomura:2011hn
;
Bradlyn:2014wla
. So far most studies on the equivalence focus on macroscopic quantities. One may ask if the equivalence holds for more general quantities. One example is correlation function of probe particles, which is of phenomenological interest in relativistic heavy ion collisions
Becattini:2020ngo
and astrophysics
Chamel:2008ca
;
Kamada:2022nyt
. A key difference arises due to momentum of particle: on one hand, momentum differs in curved space and flat space; on the other hand, geodesic force changes momentum in curved space, which has no analog in flat space. In fact, the two aspects are closely related. We will show that by a proper choice of inertial frame, it is possible to link momenta in the two spaces and simultaneously eliminate the geodesic force, putting the equivalence on firm ground. We will illustrate this both in the hydrodynamic and non-hydrodynamic regime. We will also show the equivalence remains valid when spin degree of freedom is included.

Throughout this work, we consider energetic particles in near equilibrium states, for which kinetic description applies. We use the most minus signature. Greek and Latin indices correspond to curved and flat indices respectively. Symmetrization and anti-symmetrization are defined as
\{X,Y\}=\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)
and
\left[X,Y\right]=\frac{1}{2}(XY-YX)
respectively.

Kinetic theory in elastic background

The previous discussions show that dynamics of probe particles in off-equilibrium state can be equivalently described by metric perturbation of equilibrium state in the hydrodynamic regime, where only
h_{0\mu}
components of metric are used. The mechanism extends to non-hydrodynamic regime as well, in which other components of metric are needed. To be specific, we consider dynamics of particles in a deformed elastic matter, which we take to be non-relativistic for simplicity. A realization of this scenario can be found in neutron star outer crust, with a phonon field describing collective deformation of nuclei lattice and the probe particles correspond to electrons moving in this background

Quantum kinetic theory for spinning particles

So far we have restricted ourselves to spinless particles. A natural question to ask is whether the mechanism extends to spinning particles? Naively affine connection gives rise to geodesic force acting on momentum, which is canceled by the frame force with a proper choice of frame. If there is also force acting on spin, we would have no more freedom to cancel it. The spin evolution in curved space is given by the Mathisson–Papapetrou–Dixon (MPD) equation

Conclusion

We have shown that the equivalence of off-equilibrium state and metric perturbed equilibrium state extends to dynamics of probe particles. It is achieved through a proper choice of inertial frame, which eliminate the geodesic forces acting on particle momentum globally. The choice of frame simultaneously eliminates forces acting on spin globally, extending the equivalence to spinning particles. We have shown this in both hydrodynamic and non-hydrodynamic regime. For the hydrodynamic case, simultaneous force eliminations are possible for ideal irrotational flow, which corresponds to curved space with vanishing curvature. For non-hydrodynamic case, simultaneous force eliminations are always possible. We predict spin polarization induced by time-variation of strain in elastic matter.

While we have restricted our discussion to point particles, we expect the equivalence to hold for more interesting case of composite particles: the same metric perturbation and frame choice can be used to mimic hydrodynamic fields and displacement fields in off-equilibrium states, with the unwanted forces eliminated completely. A new ingredient is that composite particle can respond to metric perturbation by changing its shape, which is characterized by gravitational form factors
Polyakov:2018zvc
. Application of this method can lead to interesting implications for spin polarization from shape change of composite particles. We leave this for future explorations.

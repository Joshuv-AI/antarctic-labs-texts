SLC2A3 promotes head and neck squamous cancer developing through negatively regulating CD8+ T cell in tumor microenvironment
Authors: Wei Jiang, Sheng Xu, Meiqing Zhao, Chao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecent studies have identified SLC2A3 as being abnormally upregulated in multiple tumor types, correlating with poor survival and disrupted microenvironments. However, its prognostic significance in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC) remains underexplored. In this study, SLC2A3 was screened as a potential risk gene influencing both immune and tumor components within the tumor microenvironment (TME) of 504 HNSC patients from the TCGA database. Immune infiltration analyses and clinical significance on SLC2A3 were conducted using ESTIMATE, CIBERSORT, ssGSEA, TIMER and clinical prognosis parameters. Additionally, the single-cell dataset is used to analyze the expression of SLC2A3 in various subpopulations. The magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS) is used to isolate CD8+ T cells from PBMCs or tumor tissues. Flow cytometry is used to identify purified and activated CD8+ T cells. GSEA and WB were used to investigate the molecular mechanism of SLC2A3 in CD8+ T cells. The co-culture system of CD8+ T cells and TU686 was used to investigate the effects of SLC2A3 on immune cells and tumor development. In this study, SLC2A3 was identified as a potential risk gene affecting both immune cells and tumor components within the TME of 504 HNSC patients derived from the TCGA database. We conducted immune infiltration analyses and assessed the clinical significance of SLC2A3 using various bioinformatics tools, including ESTIMATE, CIBERSORT, ssGSEA, and TIMER, along with clinical prognosis parameters. The single-cell RNA sequencing dataset was utilized to examine SLC2A3 expression across different cellular subpopulations. Magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS) was employed to isolate CD8+ T cells from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or tumor tissues. Flow cytometry was implemented to confirm the purity and activation state of the isolated CD8+ T cells. GSEA and Western blot were applied to explore the molecular mechanisms underlying SLC2A3’s role in CD8+ T cells. Lastly, a co-culture system involving CD8+ T cells and TU686 tumor cells was established to study the impact of SLC2A3 on immune cell function and tumor progression. SLC2A3 emerges as an actively variable gene within the immune and stromal components of the TME, linked to aggravated immune infiltration and poor clinical outcomes. The upregulated expression of SLC2A3 is predominantly enriched in immune-related biological processes and linked to the suppression of CD8+ T cells, which are crucial for the survival of HNSC patients. Furthermore, SLC2A3 exhibits specific overexpression in CD8+ T cells and may potentially trigger ferroptosis. Knockdown of SLC2A3 led to a significant increase in the proliferation of CD8+ T cells compared to those without knockdown. In co-culture systems, CD8+ T cells with SLC2A3 knockdown demonstrated an enhanced ability to eliminate tumor cells compared to those without the knockdown. SLC2A3 is associated with changes in the TME and prognostic indicators. Moreover, high SLC2A3 expression in CD8+ T cells may drive cell death through ferroptosis, fostering tumor progression.Keywords: SLC2A3, Tumor microenvironment, CD8+ T cell, Immune infiltration, Head and neck squamous carcinomaSubject terms: Cancer, Head and neck cancer

Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), ranking sixth globally in incidence, saw approximately 800,000 new cases in 2018, contributing to about 400,000 deaths worldwide1. Originating from the mucosal epithelial cells of the upper respiratory and digestive tracts, this includes oral cavity, larynx, laryngopharynx, and oropharynx squamous cell carcinomas. Alarmingly, the five-year overall survival rate for these cancers is below 50%1, emphasizing the need for a more profound understanding of HNSC’s intricate mechanisms to develop more effective targeted therapies.

The Tumor Microenvironment (TME) is a dynamic entity, primarily composed of immune and stromal components, and plays a critical role in HNSC development2,3. Among the TME cells, CD8+ T cells have a significant anti-tumor effect. Previous studies have reported that CD8+ TILs have favourable effects on the survival of head and neck cancer patients and have become a reliable indicator of tumour recurrence and prognosis4,5. Specifically, the distribution and density of CD8+ T cells at the tumor’s infiltration margin significantly influence survival prognosis. Additionally, Fox3P+ and PD-L1+ cells, by inhibiting the function of CD8+ T cells within a 30 μm radius, negatively impact survival prognosis6,7. However, our understanding of the specific molecular mechanisms driving these dynamic changes within the TME remains limited.

SLC2A3, a member of the solute carrier 2A (SLC2A) family, is crucial for transmembrane and intracellular glucose transport in glycolysis. The resultant glycolytic activity meets the heightened energy demands of rampant tumor cell proliferation, resulting in a high metabolic rate and increased glucose uptake8,9. Previous research has linked SLC2A3 overexpression with poorer clinical outcomes, such as increased invasion depth, larger tumor size, advanced pathological stage, recurrence, and vascular embolization, particularly in oral tongue cancer10. In colorectal cancer, SLC2A3 plays a vital role in perineural invasion by modulating the epithelial-mesenchymal transition11. Furthermore, SLC2A3 is implicated in TME alterations, notably through the activation of macrophage infiltration, worsening the prognosis in gastric and breast cancer12,13. These clues underscore the importance of further investigating SLC2A3’s prognostic value and its association with immune infiltration across various cancers.

In this study, we identified SLC2A3 as a highly variable gene within the TME through bioinformatics analysis, examining its correlation with immune infiltration. We preliminary found that SLC2A3 demonstrates an inverse correlation with the expression levels in CD8+ T cells despite being highly expressed within it. Consequently, purified and activated CD8+ T cells were subjected to proliferation assays and tumor cell co-culture experiments following SLC2A3 knockdown. Our findings suggest that SLC2A3 were highly expressed in CD8+ T cells, which may promote ferroptosis, thereby contributing to tumor progression.

Materials and methods

Data source

We downloaded gene expression raw-data and clinical data of 504 HNSC patients from TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Age, prognostic stage, TNM stage, tumor grade, survival status, lymphovascular and perineural invasion were contained in clinical data. Protein expression was analyzed from the CPTAC database in the The University of Alabama at Briminghan Cancer Data Analysis Portal (UALCAN) (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/analysis-prot.html). Online immunohistochemistry image data obtained from The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) website (https://www.proteinatlas.org/). In validation, ICGC database (https://icgc.org) and GSE103322 from GEO database were applied (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). All datasets in the present study were downloaded form public databases, including GEO, TCGA, CPTAC, ICGC, HPA databases. These databases allowed researchers to download and analyze public datasets for scientific purposes and thus ethics approval was not required.

In regard of human peripheral blood samples and HNSC tumor tissues, the study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (No. 2023-K064-01). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients whose information was reasonably protected and not identified. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

TME analysis via ESTIMATE algorithm

The scores of stromal and immune cells infiltration in the TME are calculated by the ESTIMATE algorithm by a feat of R language version 3.5.1 loaded with estimate package, which is a calculation that can determine the fractions of stromal cells and immune cells based on the characteristics of gene expression. The tumor samples of 504 HNSC cases in this study were processed by ESTIMATE algorithm to calculate their immune score and stromal score.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

Five hundred and four tumor specimens were categorized into high-score and low-score groups, applying the median immune score (IS) and stromal score (SS) as the basis for separation. Samples with increased IS and SS were allocated to the high-score group, while those with decreased IS and SS formed the low-score group. Differential gene expression analysis was conducted with the DESeq2 package in R, version 3.5.1. In order to obtain an appropriate amount of co-up-and-down regulated DEGs, Genes met the criteria for differential expression with an |Log2foldchange| > 1.5 and a false discovery rate (FDR) of less than 0.05.

Regression analysis and survival analysis

LASSO and multiple COX regression analyses were applied to investigate the relationship between OS and the gene expression level of patients. We used the R software package glmnet to integrate survival time, survival status, and gene expression data, and used LASSO regression analysis. In this analysis, we sampled the data set for 1000 times and selected tags with a repetition frequency of at least 900 times. In addition, we also set up tenfold cross-validation to obtain the optimal model. After that, we performed a multiple COX regression analysis to determine whether a gene is a clinically high-risk factor and significantly affects survival prognosis. When the p-value is less than 0.05, the gene is judged to be significant.

Survival analyses were conducted with data sourced from GEPIA2021, an online database based on the TCGA database.

Immune infiltration

Subsequently, genes screened from LASSO and multiple COX were undergone a series of immuno-infiltration assay, including CIBERSORT, single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA), ESTIMATE. CIBERSORT, performed by R package, is a deconvolution algorithm to estimate the proportion of 22 immune cells in the TME based on transcriptome data. ssGSEA is an analysis method based on the R package GSEA algorithm, and it can calculate the infiltration degree of 28 kinds of immune cells according to gene sets of corresponding immune cells.

GEPIA2021 and TIMER (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) are online analysis tool that uses the RNA seq expression profile of the TCGA database to detect the infiltration of immune cells and calculate survival analysis of immune cells.

Gene set enrichment analysis

The 504 HNSC datasets were analyzed by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) using the GSEA 4.2.3 Software based on the gene set of “h.all.v6.1.symbols.gmt”. The 504 HNSC cases were divided into low and high groups according to the median mRNA expression of SLC2A3, and the gene set was permuted 1000 times. The enrichment is considered significant when the FDR score is less than 25% and the p-value is less than 0.05.

Single-cell analysis

We obtained the GSE103322 raw data of transcriptome profiling from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. In the initial phase, our quality control for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data hinged on three crucial metrics for verifying reliability: total RNA count, gene diversity, and mitochondrial gene expression percentage (nCount_RNA, nFeature_RNA, and percent mt, respectively). Using the anchors method, we employed the Seurat R package to integrate the single-cell datasets. We subsequently refined the data by filtering out substandard scRNA-seq data to isolate a core group of cells. Cells with genes detectable in three or fewer cells were excluded, alongside low-quality cells with detections of less than 200 genes. We normalized the single-cell data post-cleaning and executed principal component analysis (PCA) to identify distinct cellular clusters. Additionally, we consulted the CellMarker database (http://biocc.hrbmu.edu.cn/CellMarker/) for human cell marker genes, facilitating the identification of the principal components.

CD8+ T cell isolation, in vitro expansion, and transfection

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated by density gradient centrifugation using Lymphoprep™ (Axis-Shield, USA). CD8+ T-cells were negatively isolated from PBMCs or clinical HNSC tumor tissues by magnetic separation using the EasySep Human CD8+ T-Cell Enrichment Kit (Stemcell Technologies, USA).

To activate CD8+ T cells, purified CD8+ T cells (1 × 106) were cultured in a complete culture medium (RPMI 1640 (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% ad penicillin/streptomycin/l-glutamine), CD3 antibody(Santa Cruz, USA), CD28 antibody(Santa Cruz, USA), recombinant human interleukin 2 (rhIL-2, 3000 IU/mL, Novartis), and recombinant human interleukin 15 (rhIL-15, 3000 IU/mL, Novartis) at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2. On the 14th day, cells were harvested and used for flow cytometry and transfection. The culture medium was refreshed every 2 days during this period.

To knock down SLC2A3 in CD8+ T cells, siRNA against SLC2A3 (si-SLC2A3, sequence CCUGAGAAGAUCAUAAAGGAATT), pcDNA-SLC2A3 vector (sequence CCUGAGAAGAUCAUAAAGGAATT), and respective negative control plasmids were transfected into the CD8+ T cells using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo Fisher, USA).

Flow cytometry

The extracellular and intracellular panel of human-specific antibodies were designed (Table S1) to evaluate the activation and abundance of CD8+ T cells using flow cytometry. For detection of granzyme b + expression, the cells were incubated with brefeldin A and monensin (Sigma Aldrich) at 37 °C for at least 4 h. Block the Fc receptor with the FcR blocking reagent (Miltenyi, USA) at 4 °C for 15 min. Stain with LIVE/DEAD Fixable Yellow (Thermo Fisher, USA) for 30 min, and wash cells with phosphate-buffered saline (Biobest, USA) before staining with surface labeled antibodies, including CD3, CD8, PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3. Collect cells in the BD LSRFortessa cell analyzer.

CD8+ T cells and HNSC cell line co-culture

To investigate the impact of SLC2A3-knocking-down CD8+ T cells on HNSC cell line, a coculture experiment was conducted. Two thousand SLC2A3-knocking-down CD8+ T cells were co-cultured with six-thousand TU686 cells in 48-well plates at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 humidified incubator. TU686 and the sorted CD8+ T cells were cocultured in a Transwell suspension noncontact coculture system, in which CD8+ T cells were seeded on the upper chamber while TU686 on the lower. Plant hemagglutinin-P 10 μg/mL (Soleibao, China) was added to RPMI 1640 medium to maintain the in vitro culture of the sorted CD8+ T cells.

Cell proliferation assay

A CCK-8 assay (Cell counting kit‐8, Dojindo, Japan) was used to assess the proliferation of transfected CD8+ T cells and coculture cells. In 48-well plates, 8000 cells per well were cultured and incubated at 37 °C. Afterward, each well received 1000 μL of CCK‐8 buffer, followed by a 2-h incubation at 37 °C. The optical density (OD) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BIO‐TEK, USA).

Real-time quantitative PCR

First-strand complementary DNA was created using a First-Strand Reverse Transcription System (Transgene, Beijing, China). The real-time RT-PCR was then conducted on the cDNA samples, using the SYBR Green PCR master mix and the Step One Plus System from Applied Biosystems (USA). The primer sequences were as follows: SLC2A3-F, 5′-CCUGAGAAGAUCAUAAAGGAATT-3′; SLC2A3-R, 5′-UUCCUUUAUGAUCUUCUCAGGTT-3′; GAPDH-F, 5′-CGGATTTGGTCGTATTGGG-3′; GAPDH-R, 5′-CTGGAAGATGGTGATGGGATT-3′.

Western blot and antibodies

Equal amounts of protein were separated by electrophoresis on a 4–12% SDS-PAGE gel and subsequently transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with 5mg Protein ladder(26619/26620, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The membrane was incubated with primary antibodies, including SLC2A3 (56 kDa, 1:10,000, Santa Cruz, USA), GPX4 (22 kDa, 1:10,000, Santa Cruz, USA), GSS (52 kDa, 1:10,000, Santa Cruz, USA), GCL (72 kDa, 1:10,000, Santa Cruz, USA) and GAPDH (36 kDa, 1:10,000, Santa Cruz, USA), overnight at 4 °C. Peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies were added and incubated for 2 h at room temperature. The fluorescent signals were then detected using the Bio-rad ChemiDoc MP system (Bio-rad, CA, USA).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R(4.0.2) and spss22.0 software. Correlation analysis was performed using Spearman’s coefficient. Survival curves were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method (log-rank test). Student’s t-test was used for the analysis of variance in two different groups. Multiple COX regression analyses were implemented to identify independent predictors of survival analysis. *Stands for p < 0.05, **for p < 0.01, ***for p < 0.001 and ns for no significance.

Results

Analysis workflow of this study

Data processing steps for this study are depicted in Fig. 1. Using the ESTIMATE algorithm, we assessed the ImmuneScore and StromalScore of 504 HNSC patients in the TCGA database. Patients were stratified into four groups based on median scores:High-Stromal, Low-Stromal, High-Immune, and Low-Immune Scores. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were then analyzed between groups with high versus low Immune Scores and Stromal Scores. A total of 583 upregulated and 142 downregulated intersecting genes were further subjected to LASSO, multi-COX regression, and survival analyses. This led to the identification of SLC2A3 as a pivotal gene. Subsequently, analysis of SLC2A3 focused on impacts on immune infiltration and its survival, along with its expression and functional enrichment at single-cell level immune infiltration and its survival. Consequently, CD8+ T cells emerged as critical in the HNSC TME and were potentially inhibited by SLC2A3. Finally, to investigate the effects of SLC2A3 on CD8+ T cells and tumor development along with molecular mechanisms, coculture, GSEA and Western blot were conducted based on purified and SLC2A3-knockdown CD8+ T cells.

The immune components are active and dominant in the TME of HNSC

Employing the ESTIMATE algorithm, we evaluated the composition of stromal and immune cells within the TME as depicted in Fig. 2a. Differential gene expression analysis was performed to explore gene expression changes in the TME related to immune and stromal components. Figure 2a,b display the differential gene expression between samples having high versus low immune and stromal scores, respectively. We identified a total of 2077 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in samples with elevated ImmuneScores in contrast to those with diminished scores, with 1569 genes showing increased expression and 508 exhibiting decreased expression. Similarly, we detected 2557 DEGs in samples with enhanced StromalScores compared to those with reduced scores, of which 1854 genes were upregulated and 703 genes were downregulated. Notably, there were 583 genes concurrently upregulated and 142 genes concurrently downregulated across both immune and stromal components, as shown in Fig. 2c. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis indicated these DEGs were predominantly involved in immune-related biological processes, including immunity, innate and adaptive immunity, antiviral defense, B cell activation, and CD8 T cell activation (Fig. 2d). Furthermore, in an independent cohort of 40 HNSC patients from the ICGC database, LASSO regression calculates the potential of SLC2A3 as a clinical risk factor (Fig. 3g). SLC2A3 is negatively correlated with CD8+ T cells that affect the survival of HNSC

ESTIMATE analysis suggested that tumors with higher SLC2A3 expression are characterized by greater infiltration of stromal and immune cells, as well as diminished tumor purity (Fig. 4a). Integrative analysis with ssGSEA and CIBERSORT revealed SLC2A3 may lead to an increase in Macrophages M0 and M2, as well as a decrease in NK and CD8+ T cells. Among these four types of immune cells, only CD8+ T cell numbers exhibited a significant correlation with survival in HNSC patients(Fig. 4b). GSEA also highlighted that upregulation of SLC2A3 was predominantly associated with immune-related pathways such as allograft rejection, inflammatory response, IL6 JAK STAT3 signaling, IL2 STAT5 signaling, and the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway. On the other hand, downregulation was linked to oxidative phosphorylation (Fig. 4c). Collectively, these findings suggest that elevated SLC2A3 expression contributes to a complex TME, indicates a shift from a metabolic to an immune response-dependent state. Furthermore, SLC2A3 may regulate the suppression of CD8+ T cells, a crucial factor for survival prognosis.

SLC2A3 is specifically highly expressed in CD8+ T cells

In the HNSC single-cell sequencing dataset GSE103322, we have identified 11 different types of cells. These cell types include Tumor cells, Fibroblasts, Mast cells, CD8+ T cells, Dendritic cells, Macrophages, CD4+ T cells, Myocytes, B cells, NK cells, and Endothelial cells (Fig. 5a,b). Interestingly, we have observed that SLC2A3 is highly expressed in CD8+ T cells (Fig. 5c,d). To confirm this observation, we have purified and sorted CD8+ T cells from tumor tissue and found higher expression of SLC2A3 at both mRNA and protein levels than other cells in HNSC tissue (Fig. 5e,f).

SLC2A3 indirectly promotes tumor growth by facilitating ferroptosis in CD8+ T cells

To elucidate the effects of SLC2A3 on CD8+ T cells and tumor development, co culture experiment was adopted for further exploration. CD8+ T cells were specifically conducted to knockdown SLC2A3 after being sorted from PBMCs and activated(Fig. 6a,b). Notably, knocking down SLC2A3 significantly promoted the proliferation ability of CD8+ T cells (Fig. 6c). In the co-culture experiment, we found that tumour cells co-cultured with activated CD8+ T cells were significantly inhibited in proliferation (TU686 vs TU686+ CD8+ T cell (si-NC), p < 0.05). However, when CD8+ T cells knocked down in SLC2A3, the tumor’s proliferation ability was restored (TU686+ CD8+ Tcell(si-NC) vs TU686+ CD8+ Tcell (si-SLC2A3), p < 0.05) (Fig. 6d). GSEA of CD8+ T cells indicated that decreased SLC2A3 expression may enhance the GCL/GSS/GPX4 pathway in ferroptosis (Fig. 6e). The results suggest that SLC2A3 may facilitate ferroptosis in CD8+ T cells and weaken their effect on tumor cell elimination.

Discussion

In this study, we designed methods to quantify the entire tumor immune environment in HNSC tumor. Our results indicate that SLC2A3 is an important factor associated with TME changes and poor clinical prognosis. SLC2A3 is also abnormally active and has been reported as an important marker in thyroid cancer, oesophagal cancer and oral cancer, and is significantly associated with poor prognosis13–16. SLC2A3 can mediated glycolysis in which HIF-1 is highly active and directly induces Twist1 and then transact EMT-related genes such as E-cadherin, Vimentin and N-cadherin13,17–19. In additional, at transcriptional level, an enhancer located in the second intron of SLC2A3 is pivotal in binding transcription factors like β-catenin, CTNNB1, and ZEB1, which directly promote the occurrence of EMT20. Moreover, the deficiency of E-cadherin and overexpression of vimentin, which are early alterations of EMT, may be influenced by extracellular acidification, representing an important driving force in the adaptation to the balance of E-cadherin/Vimentin21. In the stromal components, due to the Warburg effect, SLC2A3-mediated glycolysis is highly active, leading to a decrease in stromal pH, hypoxia, and lactic acid accumulation, making TME more complex and aggravating22–25. Because of the decreased pH, the acidified TME stimulates HIF-1a, which recruits to the SLC2A3 enhancer and subsequently upregulates SLC2A3 transcription22–24. In addition, SLC2A3 is expressed in platelets, SLC2A3 is transferred from α-granules to the plasma membrane during degranulation, glucose uptake and glycolysis are increased in platelets, and during degranulation, TGF-β is released, which can trigger Smad and NF-κB responses in tumor cells and promote EMT and metastasis formation25. Aerobic glycolysis by SLC2A3 not only promotes tumor cell aggressiveness but also initiates a regulatory cycle to enhance glucose deficiency in TME. Low glucose conditions act as an upstream signal to stimulate the AMPK/CREB1 signaling pathway in tumor cells, which subsequently upregulates SLC2A3, creating a positive feedback regulatory loop26.

In the immune components, our results showed that the expression of SLC2A3 was negatively correlated with NK cells and CD8+ T cells, and CD8+ T cells were an independent factor affecting the survival prognosis of HNSC patients. According to Daniela Bruni, high expression of CD8+ T cells in the TME resulting in fine prognosis in 17 solid tumors, including HNSC, and the most significant effect of CD8+ T cells on survival was observed in more than 1000 HNSC cases27. In tumor tissue, CD8+ T cells are generally considered a uniform population of cells that secrete large amounts of IFN-γ and protease granzyme B, which cooperate to kill tumor cells and cells containing intracellular pathogens. Functionally, CD8+ T cells act upon tumor cells by their high levels of perforin, granzyme B, tumor necrosis factor (IFN-γ), and tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α). Among them, IFN-γ synergistically upregulates MHC-I on APCs with activation signals, thereby enhancing antigen presentation and activation of naive T cells in tumors and lymph nodes28. In addition, IFN-γ may also help to re-regulate immunosuppressive cells in TME, such as regulatory T cells (Tregs), to attenuate the mechanism of tumor immune escape29. Wang reported that, in addition to known mechanisms, CD8+ T cells could intervene tumor growth by inducing ferroptosis, which is the first direct evidence of a link between ferroptosis and antitumor immunity30.

In our study, the expression of SLC2A3 was negatively correlated with CD8+ T cells and may activate ferroptosis in CD8+ T cells. Among ferroptosis-related genes, SLC2A3 was identified as a marker of poor prognosis in bladder cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma31,32. In mechanisms, Jiang demonstrated that lipid metabolism-related genes, including SLC2A3 in cells, interact with WD repeat domain 76 (WDR76) to inhibit ferroptosis33. However, our data contrasts Sophia’s findings in colorectal cancer cell lines, where SLC2A3 expression correlated positively with CD4 and CD8+T cells34. Furthermore, Elisabetta revealed that SLC2A3 synergizes with CD8+T cells by increased glucose uptake and storage of glycogen and fatty acids, leading to improved mitochondrial fitness, reduced ROS levels, higher abundance of the anti-apoptotic protein Mcl-1, and enhanced stress resistance35. However, in our research, knocking down the CD8+ T cells of SLC2A3 showed more vigorous proliferation and tumor-killing abilities. The enrichment analysis and WB results within CD8+ T cells indicate that high expression of SLC2A3 activates ferroptosis by inhibiting the GCL/GSS/GPX4 antioxidant system. Wei has predicted SLC2A3 through bioinformatics as a predictive signature related to ferroptosis in HNSC36, but more specific molecular exploration validation is needed. Furthermore, Jing found in the experiment that SLC2A3 regulation inhibited GPX4 and induced ferroptosis in rheumatoid arthritis37–39. Our WB results indicate that SLC2A3 is upstream of the GCL/GSS/GPX4 regulatory axis, which further supplements the mechanism research related to ferroptosis.

Despite the contributions of this study, several limitations persist. Primarily, the molecular mechanisms underlying SLC2A3 signaling in stromal and tumor cells warrant further investigation. Moreover, in the context of the Warburg effect, the biological impact of SLC2A3 on HNSC cells requires additional exploration and confirmation through both in vitro and in vivo studies.

Conclusions

In summary, SLC2A3 correlates with changes in the TME status and prognostic indicators. Elevated SLC2A3 expression potentially modifies the TME, promoting the enhancement of immune-related biological activities and increased immune cell infiltration. Notably, SLC2A3 were found to be highly expressed in CD8+ T cells, which appears to promote cell death via ferroptosis, thereby contributing to tumor progression. Consequently, these results indicate that SLC2A3 may serve as a valuable prognostic biomarker for HNSC and represents a prospective therapeutic target.

Exploring global research trends in sublingual immunotherapy of allergic rhinitis: a comprehensive bibliometric analysis
Authors: Wenzhe Gu, Zhengjie Shen, Jun Shi, Hao Xu, Hongjun Dong, Daonan Yan, Yunfeng Chu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsSublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) is a well-established, safe, and patient-friendly treatment for allergic rhinitis. This study aims to provide a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of global research trends and emerging hotspots in SLIT for allergic rhinitis.MethodsRelevant publications were sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (1993–2024). VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the R “bibliometrix” package were used to visualize research collaborations, leading contributors, and thematic developments.ResultsThis analysis of 696 publications on SLIT for allergic rhinitis reveals a dynamic, collaborative field. The USA, China, and Italy led production, with the University of Genoa as the top institution. Research is organized into six clusters: immunological mechanisms, clinical efficacy, epidemiology/guidelines, pediatric research, disease classification, and real-world evidence.The focus has evolved over two decades from initial clinical efficacy studies toward understanding underlying immune mechanisms (highlighted by recent keyword bursts like “dendritic cells”), biomarker discovery, and specialized pediatric applications. There is also a strengthened emphasis on long-term management and real-world effectiveness.ConclusionSLIT research is diversifying, marked by a growing emphasis on immunology, personalized and pediatric approaches, and practical outcomes, providing a robust foundation for optimizing future therapy.KEYWORDS: Bibliometric analysis, visual analysis, sublingual immunotherapy, allergic rhinitis, exploring global research trends

Introduction

Allergic rhinitis is a common inflammatory condition of the nasal mucosa caused by allergen exposure, with prevalence rates ranging from 10% to 30% in adults and up to 40% in children [1,2]. Typical symptoms—including sneezing, nasal congestion, itching, and rhinorrhea—can significantly affect daily life and are often accompanied by other atopic diseases such as asthma and eczema [3]. Standard therapies, such as antihistamines, intranasal corticosteroids, and decongestants, primarily provide symptom relief but do not target the underlying allergic sensitization and often require long-term use [4]. In contrast, allergen immunotherapy aims to modify the immune response and offers a more definitive approach to treatment [5].

Among immunotherapy methods, sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) has attracted increasing attention because of its favorable safety and convenience compared to traditional subcutaneous immunotherapy [6,7]. SLIT involves administering allergen extracts under the tongue to induce immune tolerance and has been evaluated in multiple clinical trials. Evidence from recent studies demonstrates that SLIT is effective in reducing symptoms and medication use in patients with allergic rhinitis, with a safety profile suitable for both adults and children [8,9]. In addition, ongoing research has explored optimal dosing regimens, the long-term persistence of clinical benefits after treatment cessation, and the potential of SLIT to prevent the development of asthma in children with allergic rhinitis [10]. Advances have also been made in understanding the immunological mechanisms underlying SLIT, including its effects on regulatory T cells and cytokine profiles. These findings have contributed to the increasing use of SLIT in clinical practice worldwide.

Bibliometric analysis is widely used to assess research trends and key contributors in various fields [11,12]. Although some bibliometric studies have examined allergen immunotherapy, most have been limited by narrow scope or outdated data [13,14]. To date, there is still a lack of an up-to-date, comprehensive bibliometric analysis specifically focused on global trends and research hotspots in SLIT for allergic rhinitis. Therefore, this study aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of global research trends, collaboration patterns, and emerging topics in sublingual immunotherapy for allergic rhinitis by applying multiple bibliometric tools to the latest literature.

Methods

Search strategies and data collection

A comprehensive literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). WoSCC was selected for this bibliometric analysis because it offers broad coverage of high-quality international journals, provides standardized citation data, and supports advanced bibliometric tools such as VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and R-bibliometrix. Compared with other databases like PubMed and Scopus, WoSCC enables more consistent retrieval and analysis of citation relationships, author collaboration networks, and journal impact metrics. Additionally, WoSCC has been widely utilized in bibliometric research, ensuring methodological comparability and reproducibility.

The search strategy was as follows: (TS= (“sublingual immunotherapy”)) AND TS= (“allergic rhinitis”). The search was conducted on 14 June 2024, to avoid inconsistencies due to database updates. To validate the search strategy and minimize irrelevant results, we used a strict topic-field query. Furthermore, two researchers independently screened a random sample of 50 retrieved records (titles/abstracts) to verify relevance. The inclusion criteria are: 1) Articles and reviews indexed in WoSCC; 2) Publications focused on sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) for allergic rhinitis; and 3) Written in English. Exclusion criteria are 1) Conference abstracts, editorials, letters, corrections, and notes; 2) Non-English publications; and 3) Studies not primarily focused on SLIT for allergic rhinitis. For each included publication, the following bibliographic data were extracted: number of publications and citations, title, author(s), institution(s), country/region, keywords, journal name, and publication year.

Statistical analysis

In this study for visualization analysis process, we comprehensively analyzed academic data using three powerful bibliometric tools: VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.3. R1), and the R-bibliometrix (version 4.3.3). The R-bibliometrix is noted for its powerful and user-friendly interface for visualizing and analyzing bibliometric data [15]. VOSviewer is a multifunctional software tool that plays a key role in mapping institutional collaboration, author collaboration, co-authorship, citation, and co-citation [16]. With VOSviewer, we can visualize and explore the complex networks of cooperation and relationships within the academic field, gaining a deeper understanding of the connections between authors, institutions, and publications. To gain insights into emerging trends and research hotspots in our field, we used VOSviewer for keyword co-occurrence analysis and CiteSpace for keyword burst detection.

For keyword burst analysis, we used CiteSpace 6.1.R3, with the following parameter settings: time slicing from January 1993 to June 2024, node types as keywords, threshold (top N per slice) = 5, and pruning using pathfinder + pruning merged network. Based on the parameter settings for each node, we conducted a visualization analysis. The size of nodes represents the number of publications, the thickness of lines symbolizes the strength of the link, and the color of nodes stands for different clusters or times. H-index was employed to quantify the academic impact of individuals and journals, respectively. The H-index is a vital indication to evaluate the academic contribution of researchers and could predict their future scientific achievements [17,18]. In this study, the h-index of each author was obtained from WoSCC. The M-index was employed to provide a career-length normalized measure of an author’s impact, facilitating fair comparisons across researchers with varying lengths of professional experience. The G-index was also included to offer a more comprehensive reflection of both productivity and citation impact, emphasizing the influence of highly cited articles within the community. All bibliometric indicators, including authors’ H-index, G-index, and M-index, were calculated based solely on the publications retrieved in this study (i.e., the 696 articles identified through our WoSCC search). These indices thus reflect each researcher’s productivity and citation impact within the specific context of SLIT for allergic rhinitis, rather than their entire academic output in WoSCC or other databases. This approach ensures the comparability and specificity of our bibliometric analysis. Microsoft Excel was used to identify and calculate bibliometric indicators, such as annual publication numbers, citation frequency, average citation frequency, journal names, journal impact factors (IFs), publishing countries/regions, publishing institutions, and authors. All relevant data from the retrieved literature citations were organized and calculated using Microsoft Excel.

Discussion

The development of SLIT research for allergic rhinitis has been shaped by both academic groups and industry. In our institutional analysis, pharmaceutical companies such as Stallergenes Greer and ALK-Abelló AS ranked among the most productive contributors, in some cases exceeding the output of leading universities such as the University of Genoa. This pattern is consistent with the role of manufacturers in supporting multicenter randomized controlled trials and in developing standardized SLIT products that can satisfy regulatory requirements and inform guideline recommendations [22,23]. At the same time, the prominence of industry-sponsored work warrants careful attention to transparency, including protocol registration, complete outcome reporting, and clear disclosure of funding and potential conflicts of interest. Such safeguards are important because selective publication, selective outcome reporting, and trial designs driven primarily by regulatory endpoints can shape how evidence is interpreted and implemented in practice.

Our journal analysis indicates that the global SLIT evidence base is disseminated mainly through a small group of core journals, with the Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, Allergy, and Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology occupying central positions in publication output and network influence [24,25]. While this concentration may facilitate efficient dissemination and accelerate the translation of findings into consensus documents and clinical recommendations, it can also limit visibility for research from regions with fewer resources, reduced access to high-impact venues, or non-English publication practices. These factors should be considered when interpreting country rankings and collaboration patterns as indicators of global leadership.

Thematic patterns in our dataset indicate a gradual shift in research focus over the last two decades. Early highly visible work centered on clinical efficacy and safety, reflected by the frequent appearance of trial-related terms such as “efficacy,” “double-blind,” and “randomized controlled trial,” and by the influence of widely cited clinical studies [21,26]. As the field matured, consensus documents and guideline-oriented publications became increasingly prominent and helped broaden the research agenda toward mechanistic questions and biomarker development [21]. This expansion is also reflected in the growing emphasis on immunologic pathways relevant to immune tolerance, including regulatory T cells, dendritic cells, and cytokines such as IL-10 [5,24,27]. These thematic shifts are consistent with the broader clinical evolution of SLIT from an alternative immunotherapy option toward a more established, guideline-directed therapy, along with increasing attention to product standardization and patient selection [5,23]. However, bibliometric methods cannot directly determine how recommendations changed over time or which formulations were preferred, and this linkage requires explicit integration of guideline documents.

International collaboration is present in this research area, but disparities remain. The United States led in publication volume and total citations in our country analysis, consistent with strong research infrastructure and investment [23,28]. Several European countries, including Germany, Denmark, and Italy, formed robust collaborative networks, which may reflect long-standing cross-border research linkages in allergy and immunology. The United Kingdom showed a high average citation rate despite a smaller publication count, suggesting that influential work has been produced by well-connected research groups. China has expanded publication output rapidly, but citation impact and international collaboration were comparatively lower, which may be related to language, journal visibility, and differences in network integration rather than research activity alone [29]. These patterns support the need for broader multinational partnerships, particularly to strengthen the generalizability of SLIT evidence across healthcare systems and to ensure that guidance is informed by diverse populations.

Another notable direction in the literature is the increasing attention to pediatric populations and to outcomes that matter in routine care. Pediatric studies have suggested that early SLIT intervention in children with allergic rhinitis may reduce subsequent asthma development, supporting a disease-modifying rationale beyond symptom relief [26]. In parallel, real-world studies have increasingly evaluated adherence, persistence, and patient-reported outcomes, which are central to long-term effectiveness and to the feasibility of implementing SLIT outside controlled trial settings [30,31]. These research directions are consistent with current clinical priorities, particularly long-term disease control, prevention-oriented management, and coordinated care of upper and lower airway disease. However, bibliometric analyses cannot determine the strength of clinical recommendations and should be interpreted in conjunction with guideline documents.

In summary, the bibliometric landscape indicates a field progressing from establishing efficacy and safety toward greater emphasis on mechanistic understanding, consensus-building, and real-world implementation [5,6,24,27]. Further research would benefit from strengthening multinational collaboration, broadening inclusion of underrepresented regions and languages, and increasing pragmatic and long-term studies that link mechanistic insights to outcomes meaningful for patients and clinicians.

Strengths and limitations

This study provides a comprehensive, data-driven map of the current SLIT research landscape, revealing influential actors, emerging research priorities, and the interplay between industry and academia. By identifying the evolution of research paradigms and highlighting the structure of global collaborations, this analysis points to specific areas where future work can accelerate progress, such as developing predictive biomarkers, optimizing protocols for pediatric patients, and addressing the practicalities of real-world implementation.

However, several limitations merit acknowledgment. First, our analyses rely primarily on citation-based indicators, which may distort the perceived “impact” of publications. Citation counts are inherently time-dependent; older articles have had more opportunity to accumulate citations. Thus, highly cited documents may partly reflect longer exposure rather than superior methodological quality or current clinical relevance. Moreover, citation patterns vary by document type. Consensus statements, clinical guidelines, and narrative reviews are frequently cited as secondary sources, which may inflate their citation counts compared with primary research articles. Second, we restricted our analysis to English-language publications. This language criterion may introduce geographic bias by favoring countries and institutions with greater English-language publication capacity, potentially overestimating the apparent productivity and influence of certain regions in the country-level and collaboration analyses. For instance, the prominent publication output attributed to the United States should be interpreted in the context of this potential language-related selection effect and does not necessarily reflect real-world SLIT utilization or research activity across different healthcare systems. Third, our exclusive use of the WoSCC database may have resulted in the omission of relevant SLIT research indexed in other databases. Region-specific journals, locally developed guidelines, and studies published in national databases may be more comprehensively captured in PubMed, Scopus, Embase, or other regional indexing platforms. Consequently, our findings may not fully represent global research activity.

Future bibliometric studies incorporating multiple databases and non-English literature would provide a more comprehensive assessment of worldwide SLIT research and help mitigate language and database selection biases. Finally, because highly cited publications—particularly those in influential journals—can disproportionately shape subsequent research directions and guideline development, bibliometric “influence” should be interpreted cautiously. Clinically meaningful evidence originating from lower-resource settings or non-English publications may be underrepresented in citation-based analyses.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis comprehensively maps six major research themes in SLIT for allergic rhinitis, highlighting the field’s multidimensional evolution. Key focal points include clinical efficacy and safety, integrated management of comorbidities, and growing attention to immunological mechanisms and biomarkers that may guide personalized therapy. The increasing prominence of pediatric and population-specific studies underscores the need for tailored approaches for children and other vulnerable groups. New emphasis on disease classification, chronicity, and real-world adherence reflects the importance of translating research findings into effective, long-term clinical practice. Going forward, efforts to integrate immunological insights with patient-centered outcomes, address adherence challenges, and strengthen evidence-based guidelines will be essential to advancing SLIT research and care.

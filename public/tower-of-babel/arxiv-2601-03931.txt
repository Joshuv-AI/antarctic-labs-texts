Constrained Dynamics for Searching Saddle Points on General Riemannian Manifolds
Yukuan Hu, Laura Grazioli

Abstract
Finding constrained saddle points on Riemannian manifolds is significant for analyzing energy landscapes arising in physics and chemistry. Existing works have been limited to special manifolds that admit global regular level-set representations, excluding applications such as electronic excited-state calculations. In this paper, we develop a constrained saddle dynamics applicable to smooth functions on general Riemannian manifolds. Our dynamics is formulated compactly over the Grassmann bundle of the tangent bundle. By analyzing the Grassmann bundle geometry, we achieve universality via incorporating the second fundamental form, which captures variations of tangent spaces along the trajectory. We rigorously establish the local linear stability of the dynamics and the local linear convergence of the resulting algorithms. Remarkably, our analysis provides the first convergence guarantees for discretized saddle-search algorithms in manifold settings. Moreover, by respecting the intrinsic quotient structure, we remove unnecessary nondegeneracy assumptions on the eigenvalues of the Riemannian Hessian that are present in existing works. We also point out that locating saddle points can be more ill-conditioning than finding local minimizers, and requires using nonredundant parametrizations. Finally, numerical experiments on linear eigenvalue problems and electronic excited-state calculations showcase the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms and corroborate the established local theory.

Introduction

Finding the saddle points (SPs) of potential energy functionals is a fundamental task in various scientific and engineering applications, particularly those involving energy landscape analysis. For example, the transition states between two (meta)stable states, which are crucial in physics
[
31
]
, chemistry
[
82
]
, and biology
[

]
, can be identified as index-1 SPs
[

. Higher-index SPs are useful for collective, multi-mode, or concerted transitions and play a key role in the construction of solution landscape
[
39
,
88

. Here, an unconstrained index-
k
SP is a critical point where the Euclidean Hessian has exactly
k
negative eigenvalues. Additionally, Riemannian manifolds can naturally arise as constraint sets due to the incorporation of physical laws, such as in the Thomson problem
[

[
7

12
,
28

. In such cases, index-

In comparison with finding local or global minimizers, locating SPs exhibits two distinct and major challenges: (1) there are always unknown descent directions at SPs, rendering off-the-shelf optimization methods unstable; (2) in general, it is impossible to construct a global merit function which is variationally minimized (or maximized) at SPs, posing significant difficulties for designing globally convergent numerical methods
[
50
]
.

There have been numerous algorithmic developments concerning saddle search in the unconstrained settings, together with rigorous theoretical analyses. On the contrary, the exploration in the constrained settings has been confined to special manifolds that admit global regular level-set representations (see Eq. (
4
) later for definition). This limitation rules out applications such as electronic excited-state calculations
[
27
,
69
,
84
]
, where the underlying quotient structures substantially complicate manifold representations. In some cases, omitting the quotient structures allows applying the existing methods directly. But unlike searching for local or global minimizers, this simplification can be detrimental in our context; see Example
2
and Section
5.1
later for an example involving the Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds.

In this work, we develop a constrained saddle dynamics on general Riemannian manifolds and analyze the theoretical properties of the continuous dynamics and the resulting discretized algorithms. We further demonstrate the effectiveness of algorithms through electronic excited-state calculations on standard benchmark molecular systems.

1.2
Contributions

In this article, we develop a constrained saddle dynamics (CSD) for finding index-
k
constrained SPs on general Riemannian manifolds. Instead of tracking the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of the Riemannian Hessian with
k
separate direction variables in
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}
, we adopt an orthogonal projector
P(t):\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}
as a single variable, respecting the inherent quotient structure. The position-projector pair
(\bm{x}(t),P(t))
is treated compactly using the CSD formulated over the
Grassmann bundle
of
k
-planes in the tangent bundle
\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
(see Eq. (
8
) later for definition). By studying the geometry of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, we reveal that the time derivative of
P(t)
necessarily contains a term defined by the second fundamental form of the manifold, accounting for the varying tangent spaces along the trajectory. Notably, this term vanishes in the unconstrained settings (cf. Eq. (
3
)) and, after horizontal lifts, reduces to the third term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
6
) for the special manifolds (
4
).

In theory, we establish the global well-definedness of the CSD (Theorem
1
), its linear stability at index-
k
constrained SPs (Theorem
2
), and the
first
local linear convergence results for the discretized algorithm in the manifold-constrained settings (Theorem
3
). Moreover, compared with the existing linear stability results, our analysis removes unnecessary nondegeneracy assumptions on eigenvalues by taking into account the inherent quotient structure (Remarks
1
and
4
). We also demonstrate through an example (Example
2
) on the Grassmann manifold that finding constrained SPs can (1) be worse conditioned and (2) require choosing nonredundant parametrizations, in contrast to locating global or local minimizers.

Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the developed algorithm on linear eigenvalue problems (Section
5.1
) and electronic excited-state calculations for standard benchmark molecules (Section
5.2
). We also corroborate numerically the influence of problem data and the importance of removing parametrization redundancies when searching for SPs.

Organization.
This paper is organized as follows: we collect the preliminary materials in Section
2
, including some fundamental concepts of Riemannian manifolds. In Section
3
, we first investigate the geometry of the Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, upon which the CSD is built. In Section
4
, we establish the theoretical properties of the CSD as well as its discretized version. In Section
5
, we report the numerical results on linear eigenvalue problems and electronic excited-state calculations. Finally, the conclusions are drawn in Section
6
.

\mathcal{E}
, we recall for simplicity the following characterization:

\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})[\bm{v}]=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathrm{D}\bar{G}(\bm{x})[\bm{v}]\big),

(9)

where
\bar{G}
is any smooth extension of
\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f
to a neighborhood of
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Retraction.
A retraction over
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth mapping
\mathrm{Retr}:\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
,
\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}\owns(\bm{x},\bm{v})\mapsto\mathrm{Retr}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{v})\in\mathcal{M}
, satisfying
\mathrm{Retr}_{\bm{x}}(0)=\bm{x}
and that
\mathrm{D}\mathrm{Retr}_{\bm{x}}(0)
is the identity mapping on
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
for any
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{M}
. By leveraging the retraction, we can obtain a point by moving away from
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{M}
along some
\bm{v}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
, while remaining on
\mathcal{M}
. In follows that, it defines an update rule to preserve the feasibility. One typical example of retraction is the exponential mapping, denoted specially by
\mathrm{Exp}:\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
, which is determined by a set of second-order ordinary differential equations and yields geodesics over
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is complete, then it holds that
[
13
, Proposition 10.22]

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{v}))=\|\bm{v}\|,\quad\forall~(\bm{x},\bm{v})\in\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M},

where
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bullet,\bullet):\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
represents the Riemannian distance over
\mathcal{M}
:

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=\inf_{c}\left\{\left.\int_{a}^{b}\|c^{\prime}(t)\|\,\mathrm{d}t~\right|c:[a,b]\to\mathcal{M}~\text{piecewise smooth},~c(a)=\bm{x},~c(b)=\bm{y}\right\}.

Second fundamental form.
For a given
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{M}
, the normal space
\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{E}
. The second fundamental form at
\bm{x}
can be identified as the mapping
\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}:\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}\times\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
defined as
\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}}(\bm{v})
for any
\bm{u}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
, where

\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}}:=\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{M}})(\bm{x})[\bm{u}],

(10)

namely, the directional derivative of
\bm{y}\mapsto\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{M}}
at
\bm{x}
along
\bm{u}
. That the range of
\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}
is
\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
can be shown by taking the directional derivative on the both sides of the identity
\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}
, which yields

\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}}\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}},\quad\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}}\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{u}}.

(11)

By the above definition, the second fundamental form accounts for the changes in the way the tangent spaces sit inside the ambient
\mathcal{E}
.

3
Algorithmic developments

In this section, we develop a constrained saddle dynamics (CSD) for locating index-
k
constrained SPs on general Riemannian manifolds. Existing works
[
52
,
54
,
87
]
are restricted to the special manifolds in Eq. (
4
) and introduce
k
different direction dynamics to track the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of Riemannian Hessian. The resulted dynamics can be placed on the Stiefel bundle
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
(cf. Eq. (
7
)), which has not been recognized before. However, it is not difficult to observe the quotient structure under the hood: only the subspace is pursued and it is invariant under the choice of orthonormal basis. Therefore, we instead adopt a single dynamics of an orthogonal projector, which, together with the position dynamics, will amount to the CSD over the Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
(see Section
3.2
). To this end, we first investigate the geometries of
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
(see Section
3.1
), which are applicable to general Riemannian manifolds. For notational ease, we assume that
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
unless stated otherwise. Nevertheless, our arguments apply to general cases.

3.1
Geometries of
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

We start with the characterization of the tangent space to
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
.

Lemma 1

(Tangent space to
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
. For any
(\bm{x},V)\in\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, the tangent space

\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})=\left\{(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{\delta}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M},~A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{asym}}^{k\times k},~B\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-k)\times k}\\[2.84544pt]
\Gamma=VA+V_{\perp}B+\big(\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{k})\big)\end{array}\right.\right\},

(12)

where
V_{\perp}\in\mathrm{St}_{d-k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
satisfies
V^{\top}V_{\perp}=0
. The characterization (
12
) is independent from the choice of
V_{\perp}
.

Proof.

For any
(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, take a smooth curve
c:=(c_{1},c_{2}):\mathbb{R}\supseteq\mathcal{I}\to\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
over
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
such that
c(0)=(\bm{x},V)
and
c^{\prime}(0)=(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)
. It is obvious that
c_{1}
is a smooth curve over
\mathcal{M}
passing through
\bm{x}
at the origin, which implies
\bm{\delta}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
. For the second part, note that
c_{2}(s)^{\top}c_{2}(s)=I_{d}
and
c_{2}(s)_{j}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{c_{1}(s)}\mathcal{M}}(c_{2}(s)_{j})
, where
c_{2}(s)_{j}
refers to the
j
-th column of
c_{2}(s)
(
j=1,\ldots,k
). It therefore holds by the product rule that
\Gamma^{\top}V+V^{\top}\Gamma=0
and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{j}=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}c_{2}(s)_{j}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{c_{1}(s)}\mathcal{M}}\right|_{s=0}(\bm{v}_{j})+\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\Gamma_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\Gamma_{j})+\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{j}),

(13)

for
j=1,\ldots,k
. Since the columns of
V
and
V_{\perp}
constitute an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
, we have

\big(\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\Gamma_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\Gamma_{k})\big)=VA+V_{\perp}B

for some
A\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-k)\times k}
. Plugging this and Eq. (
13
) into
\Gamma^{\top}V+V^{\top}\Gamma=0
yields
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{asym}}^{k\times k}
because
V^{\top}V_{\perp}=0
and
\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{j})\in\mathrm{N}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
(see Section
2.2
). The proof is complete by noticing the arbitrariness of
(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)
.
∎

By the definitions in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
), there is a natural projection
\pi:\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, defined as

\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\owns(\bm{x},V)\mapsto\pi(\bm{x},V):=(\bm{x},VV^{\top})\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}),

which is smooth and surjective, thus a submersion; in fact, we have the quotient structure

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\cong\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})/\mathcal{O}(k).

(14)

Therefore, the tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
at
(\bm{x},P)
can be readily obtained by computing the range of
\mathrm{D}\pi(\bm{x},V)
, where
V\in\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
satisfies
P=VV^{\top}
. This is given in the following lemma without proof.

Lemma 2

(Tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Riemannian submanifold a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
. For any
(\bm{x},P)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, the tangent space

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})=\left\{(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\mid\bm{\delta}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M},~\Delta=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\Delta\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},P)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\{(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\mid\bm{\delta}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M},~\Delta=V_{\perp}BV^{\top}+VB^{\top}V_{\perp}^{\top}+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},P),~B\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-k)\times k}\right\},

(15)

where
V:=(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{k})\in\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
and
V_{\perp}:=(\bm{v}_{k+1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{d})\in\mathrm{St}_{d-k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
satisfy
P=VV^{\top}
and
V^{\top}V_{\perp}=0
, the operator
\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}
is defined by

\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},Q):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},\bm{u}_{\ell})\bm{u}_{\ell}^{\top}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\bm{u}_{\ell}\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},\bm{u}_{\ell})^{\top},

(16)

for any
\bm{u}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
and
Q=UU^{\top}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
with
U:=(\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{k})\in\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
. The characterization (
15
) is independent from the choices of
V
and
V_{\perp}
.

A natural basis for
\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
is
\{(\bm{\delta}_{q},\Delta_{q})\}_{q=1}^{d}\cup\{(\bm{\delta}_{ij},\Delta_{ij})\}_{i=1,\ldots,k,~j=k+1,\ldots,d}
, where

(\bm{\delta}_{q},\Delta_{q}):=\left(\bm{v}_{q},\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{v}_{q},P)\right),\quad q=1,\ldots,d,

(17)

and

(\bm{\delta}_{ij},\Delta_{ij}):=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\bm{v}_{i}\bm{v}_{j}^{\top}+\bm{v}_{j}\bm{v}_{i}^{\top}\big)\right),\quad i=1,\ldots,k,~j=k+1,\ldots,d.

(18)

By virtue of the quotient structure (
14
), each tangent space to
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
can be decomposed into vertical and horizontal parts induced by
\pi
.

Lemma 3

(Decomposition of the tangent space to
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
. For any
(\bm{x},V)\in\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, the tangent space to
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
at
(\bm{x},V)
admits a direct sum decomposition as

\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Ver}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\oplus\mathrm{Hor}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}),

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ver}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle:=\left\{(0,VA)\mid A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{asym}}^{k\times k}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hor}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\left(\bm{\delta},V_{\perp}B+\big(\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{k})\big)\right)\mid\bm{\delta}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M},~B\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-k)\times k}\right\}

represent the vertical and horizontal subspaces of
\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
induced by
\pi
, respectively, and
V_{\perp}\in\mathrm{St}_{d-k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})
satisfies
V^{\top}V_{\perp}=0
. The characterization of the horizontal subspace is independent from the choice of
V_{\perp}
. Moreover,

\mathrm{Hor}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\cong\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},VV^{\top})}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}).

For any
(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},VV^{\top})}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, its horizontal lift is
(\bm{\delta},\Delta V)\in\mathrm{Hor}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathrm{Ver}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})=\ker(\mathrm{D}\pi(\bm{x},V))
. Since

\mathrm{D}\pi(\bm{x},V)[(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)]=(\bm{\delta},V\Gamma^{\top}+\Gamma V^{\top}),\quad\forall~(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},V)}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}),

and recall the characterization (
12
), we have for
(\bm{\delta},\Gamma)\in\mathrm{Ver}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\pi}\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
that
\bm{\delta}=0
and

V_{\perp}BV^{\top}+VB^{\top}V_{\perp}^{\top}=0\Leftrightarrow(V,~V_{\perp})\begin{pmatrix}&B^{\top}\\
B&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V^{\top}\\
V_{\perp}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=0.

Note that the terms involving the second fundamental form vanish due to
\bm{\delta}=0
. Since the columns of
V
and
V_{\perp}
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
, the above equation implies that
B=0
. Therefore,
\Gamma=VA
for some
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{asym}}^{k\times k}
. This verifies the expression for the vertical subspace. Again by the characterization (
12
), the horizontal subspace is clear since it is the orthogonal complement of the vertical subspace. The proof is complete.
∎

It is known that a natural Riemannian metric for the tangent bundle is the Sasaki metric
[
66
,
74
]
. Basically, the Sasaki metric uses the Riemannian connection of
\mathcal{M}
to split the tangent space to
\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}
at any point into horizontal and vertical subspaces, then defines the inner product using the original metric on each piece. Below, we define a Sasaki-type metric for
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 1

(Sasaki-type metric for
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
. For any
(\bm{x},P)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, the Sasaki-type metric on
\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\langle(\bm{\delta},\Delta),(\tilde{\bm{\delta}},\tilde{\Delta})\right\rangle_{(\bm{x},P)}:=\left\langle\bm{\delta},\tilde{\bm{\delta}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta_{\bm{x}},\tilde{\Delta}_{\bm{x}}\right\rangle,\quad\forall~(\bm{\delta},\Delta),~(\tilde{\bm{\delta}},\tilde{\Delta})\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}),

where

\Delta_{\bm{x}}:=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\Delta\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}},~~\tilde{\Delta}_{\bm{x}}:=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\tilde{\Delta}\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}.

(19)

Moreover, we denote the norm induced by the Sasaki-type metric with
\|\bullet\|_{(\bm{x},P)}
. Then for any
(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
,

\left\|(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\right\|_{(\bm{x},P)}=\left(\|\bm{\delta}\|^{2}+\|\Delta_{\bm{x}}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To preserve the feasibility condition in discretized algorithms, we need to define a retraction over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
. One possible choice based on the retraction over
\mathcal{M}
is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 4

(Retraction over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
and
\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}:\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a retraction over
\mathcal{M}
. Then
\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}:\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
defined as

\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}_{(\bm{x},P)}(\bm{\delta},\Delta):=\left(\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta}),\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{Exp}_{P}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})}(\Delta_{\bm{x}})\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}\right),

(20)

for any
\big((\bm{x},P),(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\big)\in\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
, is a retraction over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, where
\Delta_{\bm{x}}
is defined in Eq. (
19
). Its representative over
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
is

\mathrm{Retr}_{(\bm{x},V)}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}(\bm{\delta},\Delta V):=\left(\mathrm{Retr}_{\bm{x}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\bm{\delta}),\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}\big(VU\cos(\Sigma)U^{\top}+Q\sin(\Sigma)U^{\top}+VU_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\top}\big)\right),

(21)

for any
\big((\bm{x},V),(\bm{\delta},\Delta V)\big)\in\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
fulfilling
P=VV^{\top}
, where
Q\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{r\times r}
, and
U\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
satisfy
\Delta_{\bm{x}}V=Q\Sigma U^{\top}
, with
r\leq\min\{k,d-k\}
the rank of
\Delta_{\bm{x}}V
, and
U_{\perp}\in\mathrm{St}_{k-r}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
satisfies
U^{\top}U_{\perp}=0
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
is smooth and
\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}_{(\bm{x},P)}(0)=(\bm{x},P)
. It then suffices to check its differential with respect to the second argument. By definition, for any
(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\in\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
,

\displaystyle~\mathrm{D}\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}_{(\bm{x},P)}(0)[(\bm{\delta},\Delta)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}\left(\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(s\bm{\delta})-\bm{x},\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(s\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{Exp}_{P}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})}(s\Delta_{\bm{x}})\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(s\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}-P\right).

The limit of the first component is exactly
\bm{\delta}
from the definition of retraction. For the second component, by the product rule and the fact that
P=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ P\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}
,

\displaystyle~\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}\left(\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(s\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathrm{Exp}_{P}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})}(s\Delta_{\bm{x}})\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{x}}(s\bm{\delta})}\mathcal{M}}-P\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{\ell})\bm{v}_{\ell}^{\top}+\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\Delta\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\bm{v}_{\ell}\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\bm{v}_{\ell})^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\circ\Delta\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},P)=\Delta,

where the first equality is due to Eq. (
10
) and the property of retraction, the second equality uses the definition (
16
), and the last equality follows from the characterization (
15
). Consequently,
\mathrm{D}\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}_{(\bm{x},P)}(0)
is an identify mapping on
\mathrm{T}_{(\bm{x},P)}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, as desired. The closed-form expression of the representative over
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
can be readily derived in analogy to the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 3.3]
.
∎

3.2
Constrained saddle dynamics on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

Equipped with the above geometrical tools of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, we are ready to develop the constrained saddle dynamics (CSD). Since
(\bm{x}(t),P(t))\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
for any
t
, we require from Eq. (
15
) that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M},~~\frac{\,\mathrm{d}P}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}}\circ\frac{\,\mathrm{d}P}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)\circ\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}}+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t),P(t)\right)

(22)

hold for all the time, where
\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}
is defined in Eq. (
16
). To achieve this, we set

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)

\displaystyle:=-R_{\bm{x}(t),P(t)}\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))\big),

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}P}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)

\displaystyle:=-\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P(t)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M})}\circ\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}(t)}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t),P(t)\right),

where
R_{\bm{x}(t),P(t)}
is the reflection operator defined by
P(t)
on
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}
:

R_{\bm{x}(t),P(t)}(\bm{u}):=\bm{u}-2P(t)\bm{u},\quad\forall~\bm{u}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M},

(24)

and
\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P(t)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M})}
refers to the orthogonal projection operator onto the tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M})
at
P(t)
, which has a closed-form expression,

\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P(t)}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M})}(M):=\left[P(t),\left[P(t),M\right]\right],

(25)

for any symmetric bilinear form
M
over
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}
. The
\bm{x}
-part is the same as in Eq. (
5
) except that we adopt the orthogonal projector. The first ingredient of the
P
-part is responsible for tracking the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))
in
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}
, following the Euler-Lagrange equation of the Rayleigh quotient minimization,

\min_{\tilde{P}}~\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{P}\circ\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))),\quad\mathrm{s.~t.}~\tilde{P}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}(t)}\mathcal{M}),

while the second ingredient accounts for the changes in the tangent spaces, as explained in Section
2.2
. The dynamics (
23
) can be horizontally lifted to one over
\mathrm{St}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
as shown in Lemma
3
:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)

\displaystyle:=-R_{\bm{x}(t),V(t)}\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))\big),

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{v}_{i}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)

\displaystyle:=-\mathrm{Proj}_{\bm{x}(t),V(t)^{\perp}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))[\bm{v}_{i}(t)]\big)+\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}(t)}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t),\bm{v}_{i}(t)\right),

\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,k,\quad\text{with}\quad V(t):=(\bm{v}_{1}(t),\ldots,\bm{v}_{k}(t)).

If the special manifolds (
4
) are considered, the second fundamental form in Eq. (
26
) recovers the third term of the right-hand side of Eq. (
6
). However, thanks to the explicit characterization of the tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
in Lemma
2
, our methodology applies in more general settings since we only require the orthogonal projection onto the tangent space to define the second fundamental form. Some examples are listed as follows.

Example 1

(Second fundamental form in special cases)
.

•

Riemannian manifolds induced by global defining functions:
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{M}
is defined in Eq. (
4
). For any
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\bm{v}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\left(I_{n}-\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\big(\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\right)\bm{v}

for any
\bm{v}\in\mathcal{E}
. Therefore, by Eq. (
10
), for any
\bm{u}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-\big(\mathrm{Hess}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})[\bm{u}]\big)^{\top}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\bm{v}

\displaystyle\quad-\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathrm{D}\left(\bm{y}\mapsto\big(\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{y})\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{y})^{\top}\big)^{-1}\right)(\bm{x})[\bm{u}]\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\bm{v}

\displaystyle\quad-\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})[\bm{u}]\right)\bm{v}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})^{\top}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Hess}\,\bm{c}(\bm{x})[\bm{u}]\right)\bm{v},

where the last equality is due to

. Notably, this recovers the third term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
6
) if we let
\bm{u}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)
. Some special cases fallen into this class and the corresponding second fundamental forms are listed as follows:

–

Flat space:
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{E}
,
\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}\equiv 0
.

–

Sphere:
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=-\left\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\right\rangle\bm{x},\quad\forall~\bm{x}\in\mathcal{M},~\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}.

–

Stiefel manifold:
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{St}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\mathrm{II}_{X}(U,V)=-\frac{1}{2}X\big(U^{\top}V+V^{\top}U\big),\quad\forall~X\in\mathrm{St}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),~U,V\in\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathcal{M}.

•

Grassmann manifold:
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{Gr}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
P\in\mathcal{M}
, we have from
[
11
]
that

\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathcal{M}=\left\{\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}\mid\Gamma P+P\Gamma=\Gamma\right\},\quad\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathcal{M}}(\Gamma)=[P,[P,\Gamma]],\quad\forall~\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}.

Therefore, by Eq. (
10
), for any
\Gamma
,
\Delta\in\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathcal{M}
,

\mathrm{II}_{P}(\Delta,\Gamma)=[\Delta,[P,\Gamma]]+[P,[\Delta,\Gamma]]=[\Delta,[P,\Gamma]].

The last equality is due to

\displaystyle[P,[\Delta,\Gamma]]

\displaystyle=[P,[\Delta P+P\Delta,\Gamma P+P\Gamma]]

\displaystyle=[P,\Delta P\Gamma+P\Delta\Gamma P-\Gamma P\Delta-P\Gamma\Delta P]

\displaystyle=P\Delta\Gamma P-P\Gamma\Delta P-P\Delta\Gamma P+P\Gamma\Delta P=0,

where we have used
\Gamma P+P\Gamma=\Gamma
,
\Delta P+P\Delta=\Delta
,
P\Gamma P=0
, and
P\Delta P=0
.

•

Fixed-rank manifold:
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}_{r}^{m\times n}
. For any
X\in\mathcal{M}
, let
X=U\Sigma V^{\top}
be its singular value decomposition, where
U\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{r\times r}
, and
V\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We have from
[
13
, Section 7.5]
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathcal{M}=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}U&U_{\perp}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V^{\top}\\
V_{\perp}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}~\right|~A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},~B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(n-r)},~C\in\mathbb{R}^{(m-r)\times r}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathcal{M}}(Z)=Z-(I_{m}-UU^{\top})Z(I_{n}-VV^{\top}),\quad\forall~Z\in\mathcal{E}.

Note that for any smooth curve
c:\mathbb{R}\supseteq\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{M}
satisfying
X(0)=X
and
X^{\prime}(0)=\Psi\in\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathcal{M}
, there exist smooth mappings
U(t)\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
\Sigma(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, and
V(t)\in\mathrm{St}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
X(t)=U(t)\Sigma(t)V(t)^{\top}
and
U(0)=U
,
\Sigma(0)=\Sigma
,
V(0)=V
hold in a small neighborhood of the origin. By the product rule,

U^{\prime}(0)\Sigma V^{\top}+U\Sigma^{\prime}(0)V^{\top}+U\Sigma V^{\prime}(0)^{\top}=X^{\prime}(0)=\Psi,

which yields

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}(0)=U^{\top}\Psi V,~~U^{\prime}(0)=(I_{m}-UU^{\top})\Psi V\Sigma^{-1},~~V^{\prime}(0)=(I_{n}-VV^{\top})\Psi^{\top}U\Sigma^{-1}.

Therefore, by Eq. (
10
), for any
\Phi
,
\Psi\in\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{X}(\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=\left((I_{m}-UU^{\top})\Psi V\Sigma^{-1}U^{\top}+U\Sigma^{-1}V^{\top}\Psi^{\top}(I_{m}-UU^{\top})\right)\Phi(I_{n}-VV^{\top})

\displaystyle\quad+(I_{m}-UU^{\top})\Phi\left((I_{n}-VV^{\top})\Psi^{\top}U\Sigma^{-1}V^{\top}+V\Sigma^{-1}U^{\top}\Psi(I_{n}-VV^{\top})\right)

\displaystyle=(I_{m}-UU^{\top})\left(\Psi V\Sigma^{-1}U^{\top}\Phi+\Phi V\Sigma^{-1}U^{\top}\Psi\right)(I_{n}-VV^{\top}),

where the last equality is due to the fact that
(I_{m}-UU^{\top})\Phi(I_{n}-VV^{\top})=0
.

We shall point out that the CSD (
26
) (or (
23
)) does not coincide completely with the existing dynamics in Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
). The difference consists in the tangent space component of
V
-dynamics: in the existing dynamics (
6
), the component is

-\mathrm{Proj}_{\bm{x}(t),\bm{v}_{i}(t)^{\perp}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))[\bm{v}_{i}(t)]\big)+2\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathrm{Proj}_{\bm{x}(t),\bm{v}_{j}(t)}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))[\bm{v}_{i}(t)]\big),

while in ours, the component reads

-\mathrm{Proj}_{\bm{x}(t),\bm{v}_{i}(t)^{\perp}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))[\bm{v}_{i}(t)]\big)+\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{Proj}_{\bm{x}(t),\bm{v}_{j}(t)}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}(t))[\bm{v}_{i}(t)]\big).

The existing one is derived from the Lagrangian formalism combined with operator splitting
[
87
]
, which is favorable due to its decoupled nature but does not respect the quotient structure (
14
). This can be problematic in theoretical analysis; see the discussions in Remark
1

It is straightforward to verify that Eq. (

Theorem 1

(Global well-definedness of the dynamics (
23
))
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
. If
(\bm{x}(0),P(0))\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

) always stays on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is compact in
\mathcal{E}
, so is
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
in its ambient space. The conclusion then follows directly from
[
47
, Corollary 9.17]
.
∎

4
Theoretical analysis

In this section, we analyze the linear stability of the dynamics (
23
) and the local convergence of Algorithm
1
.

4.1
Linear stability analysis of the dynamics

Let
\bm{w}:=(\bm{x},P)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
and
\bm{h}:\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\to\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
be the vector field defined as
\bm{h}(\bm{w}):=(h_{1}(\bm{w}),h_{2}(\bm{w}))
, where

\displaystyle h_{1}(\bm{w})

\displaystyle:=-R_{\bm{w}}\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big),

(27)

\displaystyle h_{2}(\bm{w})

\displaystyle:=-\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})}\circ\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})+\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}(h_{1}(\bm{w}),P).

Note that
R_{\bm{w}}
should be identified as the one in Eq. (
24
). In follows that, Eq. (
23
) can be rewritten compactly as the following dynamics over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{w}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\bm{h}(\bm{w}(t)),

(28)

with the initial condition
\bm{w}(0)=(\bm{x}(0),P(0))\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
.

In the following, we aim to establish the linear stability of the dynamics (
28
) using the geometrical tools for
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
in Section
3.1
. Before that, we first figure out the expression of the differential
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w})
.

Lemma 5

(Differential of
\bm{h}
)
.

Suppose that
f
is
C^{3}
,
\mathcal{M}
is a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
, and
\bm{h}:\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})\to\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
is the vector field over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
defined in Eq. (
27
). Then for any
\bm{w}:=(\bm{x},P)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
and
D:=(\bm{\delta},\Delta)\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}h_{1}(\bm{w})[D]

\displaystyle=-R_{\bm{w}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]\big)+\bm{m}_{1}(\bm{w})[\bm{\delta}]+\bm{m}_{2}(\bm{w})[\Delta],

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}h_{2}(\bm{w})[D]

\displaystyle=-[\Delta,[P,\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})]]-[P,[\Delta,\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})]]+\bm{m}_{3}(\bm{w})[\bm{\delta}]+\bm{m}_{4}(\bm{w})[\Delta],

where

\displaystyle\bm{m}_{1}(\bm{w})[\bm{\delta}]:=-\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{y},P})(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big)-O^{R}_{\bm{w}}\big(\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{\delta},\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}))\big),

\displaystyle\bm{m}_{2}(\bm{w})[\Delta]:=-\mathrm{D}(Q\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{x},Q})(P)[\Delta]\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big),

\displaystyle\bm{m}_{3}(\bm{w})[\bm{\delta}]:=-\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto O^{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\bm{y},P})(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]\circ\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})-O^{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\bm{w}}\circ\mathrm{D}\bar{H}(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto O^{\widehat{\mathrm{II}}}_{\bm{y}})(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}](h_{1}(\bm{w}),P)+O^{\widehat{\mathrm{II}}}_{\bm{x}}\big(\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto h_{1}(\bm{y},P))(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}],P\big),

\displaystyle\bm{m}_{4}(\bm{w})[\Delta]:=O^{\widehat{\mathrm{II}}}_{\bm{x}}\big(\mathrm{D}(Q\mapsto h_{1}(\bm{x},Q))(P)[\Delta],P\big)+O^{\widehat{\mathrm{II}}}_{\bm{x}}(h_{1}(\bm{w}),\Delta),

(\bm{x},P)\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{x},P}
,
(\bm{x},P)\mapsto O^{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\bm{x},P}
,
\bm{x}\mapsto O^{\widehat{\mathrm{II}}}_{\bm{x}}
, and
\bar{H}
are any smooth extensions of
(\bm{x},P)\mapsto R_{\bm{x},P}
,
(\bm{x},P)\mapsto\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M})}
,
\bm{x}\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}}
, and
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f
to proper ambient spaces, respectively.

Proof.

Let
c:\mathbb{R}\supseteq\mathcal{I}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
be a smooth curve over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
such that
c(0)=\bm{w}
and
c^{\prime}(0)=D
. We thus have

\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w})[D]=(\bm{h}\circ c)^{\prime}(0)=\big((h_{1}\circ c)^{\prime}(0),~(h_{2}\circ c)^{\prime}(0)\big).

By the product and chain rules,

\displaystyle~(h_{1}\circ c)^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{s\to 0}\left(h_{1}(c(s))-h_{1}(c(0))\right)/s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\big(\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{y},P})(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]+\mathrm{D}(Q\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{x},Q})(P)[\Delta]\big)\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big)

\displaystyle~-O^{R}_{\bm{w}}\left(\mathrm{P}_{\bm{x},\bm{\delta}}\big(\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{x})\big)+\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}\,f(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]\big)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-\big(\mathrm{D}(\bm{y}\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{y},P})(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]+\mathrm{D}(Q\mapsto O^{R}_{\bm{x},Q})(P)[\Delta]\big)\big(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big)

\displaystyle~-O^{R}_{\bm{w}}\left(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]+\mathrm{II}_{\bm{x}}\big(\bm{\delta},\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})\big)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-R_{\bm{w}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x})[\bm{\delta}]\big)+\bm{m}_{1}(\bm{w})[\bm{\delta}]+\bm{m}_{2}(\bm{w})[\Delta],

Theorem 2

(Linear stability of the dynamics (
28
))
.

Suppose that
f
is
C^{3}
,
\mathcal{M}
is a Riemannian submanifold of a Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
with
\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
,
\bm{w}^{\star}:=(\bm{x}^{\star},P^{\star})\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
, and
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
is nondegenerate, whose eigenvalues are
\lambda_{1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}^{\star}<\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{d}^{\star}
. Then
\bm{w}^{\star}
is a linearly steady state of the dynamics (
28
) if and only if
\bm{x}^{\star}
is an index-
k
constrained SP of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
and
P^{\star}
is an orthogonal projector onto the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
.

Proof.

The proof revolves around the spectrum of
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})
.

Necessity.
Suppose that
\bm{w}^{\star}
is a linearly steady state of dynamics (
28
). Therefore,
\,\mathrm{d}\bm{w}/\,\mathrm{d}t
vanishes at
\bm{w}^{\star}
, which implies that

•

\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})=0
, i.e.,
\bm{x}^{\star}
is a critical point of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
. This further yields
\bm{m}_{1}(\bm{w}^{\star})=0
,
\bm{m}_{2}(\bm{w}^{\star})=0
, and
\bm{m}_{4}(\bm{w}^{\star})=0
from their definitions in Lemma
5
;

•

[P^{\star},[P^{\star},\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})]]=0
, i.e.,
P^{\star}
is an orthogonal projector onto a
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
.

The differential
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})[D]
in Lemma
5
thus simplifies to

\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})[D]=\begin{pmatrix}-R_{\bm{w}^{\star}}\big(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})[\bm{\delta}]\big)\\[2.84544pt]
-[P^{\star},[\Delta,\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})]]+\bm{m}_{3}(\bm{w}^{\star})[\bm{\delta}]\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

Moreover, since
\pi^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}\circ\bm{h}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
, where
\pi^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}:\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
is defined as
\pi^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}(\bm{w},D)=\bm{w}
for any
(\bm{w},D)\in\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
, we have by the chain rule that

\mathrm{D}\pi^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}(\bm{h}(\bm{w}))[\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w})]=0,\quad\forall~\bm{w}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}).

That is to say, the range of
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w})
is included in the vertical subspace of
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{h}(\bm{w})}(\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})))
induced by
\pi^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
. In follows that, we could regard
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w})
as a mapping from
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
to itself.

By the Hartman-Grobman linearization theorem
[
3
]
, the linear stability of
\bm{w}^{\star}
implies that all of the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})
have negative real parts. By Lemma
2
, these eigenvalues coincide with those of
B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{d}\times\hat{d}}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\hat{d}\times\hat{d}}
(with
\hat{d}:=d+k(d-k)
) are defined respectively using the Sasaki-type metric in Definition
1
as

\displaystyle A_{q,q^{\prime}}:=\left\langle D_{q^{\prime}}^{\star},\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})[D_{q}^{\star}]\right\rangle_{\bm{w}^{\star}},

\displaystyle B_{q,q^{\prime}}:=\left\langle D_{q^{\prime}}^{\star},D_{q}^{\star}\right\rangle_{\bm{w}^{\star}},

\displaystyle A_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}:=\left\langle D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{\star},\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})[D_{ij}^{\star}]\right\rangle_{\bm{w}^{\star}},

\displaystyle B_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}:=\left\langle D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{\star},D_{ij}^{\star}\right\rangle_{\bm{w}^{\star}}

with

\displaystyle D_{q}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=(\bm{\delta}_{q}^{\star},\Delta_{q}^{\star}):=\left(\bm{v}_{q}^{\star},\widehat{\mathrm{II}}_{\bm{x}^{\star}}(\bm{v}_{q}^{\star},P^{\star})\right),~q=1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle D_{ij}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=(\bm{\delta}_{ij}^{\star},\Delta_{ij}^{\star}):=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\bm{v}_{i}^{\star}(\bm{v}_{j}^{\star})^{\top}+\bm{v}_{j}^{\star}(\bm{v}_{i}^{\star})^{\top}\big)\right),~i=1,\ldots,k,~j=k+1,\ldots,d.

Here,
\{(\mu_{q}^{\star},\bm{v}_{q}^{\star})\}_{q=1}^{d}
are the eigenpairs of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
such that
P^{\star}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\bm{v}_{\ell}^{\star}(\bm{v}_{\ell}^{\star})^{\top}
. Indeed, by Definition
1
and Eq. (
29
) and noticing that
\{D_{q}^{\star}\}_{q=1}^{d}
and
\{D_{ij}^{\star}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k\atop j=k+1,\ldots,d}
respectively span the horizontal and vertical subspaces of
\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}^{\star}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
(see Lemma
3
), we could work out the entries in
A
and
B
explicitly:

\displaystyle A_{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{q}^{\star}\delta_{q,q^{\prime}},&q,q^{\prime}=1,\ldots,k,\\
-\mu_{q}^{\star}\delta_{q,q^{\prime}},&q,q^{\prime}=k+1,\ldots,d,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle A_{q,ij}

\displaystyle=0,~q=1,\ldots,d,~i=1,\ldots,k,~j=k+1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle A_{ij,q}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta_{ij}^{\star},\bm{m}_{3}(\bm{w}^{\star})[\bm{v}_{q}^{\star}]\right\rangle,~q=1,\ldots,d,~i=1,\ldots,k,~j=k+1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle A_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(\mu_{i}^{\star}-\mu_{j}^{\star})\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}},~i,i^{\prime}=1,\ldots,k,~j,j^{\prime}=k+1,\ldots,d,

and

B_{q,q^{\prime}}=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}},~q,q^{\prime}=1,\ldots,d;\quad B_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}},~i,i^{\prime}=1,\ldots,k,~j,j^{\prime}=k+1,\ldots,d.

Consequently,
B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}
is lower (or upper) triangular and its eigenvalues are exactly

\{\mu_{q}^{\star}\}_{q=1}^{k},~~\{-\mu_{q}^{\star}\}_{q=k+1}^{d},~~\text{and}~~\{\mu_{i}^{\star}-\mu_{j}^{\star}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k\atop j=k+1,\ldots,d}.

Since they are all negative, we get
\max\{\mu_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mu_{k}^{\star}\}<0<\min\{\mu_{k+1}^{\star},\ldots,\mu_{d}^{\star}\}
. This indicates that
\bm{x}^{\star}
is an index-
k
constrained SP of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
,
P^{\star}
is an orthogonal projector onto the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
, and
\{\mu_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mu_{k}^{\star}\}=\{\lambda_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{\star}\}
, as desired.

Sufficiency.
Suppose that
\bm{x}^{\star}
is an index-
k
constrained SP of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
and
P^{\star}
is an orthogonal projector onto the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})=0
and
\lambda_{1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}^{\star}<0<\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\lambda_{d}^{\star}
. By similar arguments as above, we could obtain that the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{D}\bm{h}(\bm{w}^{\star})
are exactly

\{\lambda_{q}^{\star}\}_{q=1}^{k},~~\{-\lambda_{q}^{\star}\}_{q=k+1}^{d},~~\text{and}~~\{\lambda_{i}^{\star}-\lambda_{j}^{\star}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k\atop j=k+1,\ldots,d},

which are all negative, implying that
\bm{w}^{\star}=(\bm{x}^{\star},P^{\star})
is linearly steady.
∎

Remark 1
.

In comparison with the linear stability results in the existing work
[
87
, Theorem 1]
, Theorem
2
not only sits in more general manifold settings, but also requires weaker assumptions on the eigenvalues of Riemannian Hessian. More specifically, we only ask for a positive gap between
\lambda_{k}^{\star}
and
\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}
, whereas the existing one assumes additionally that
\{\lambda_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are all simple (cf.
[
87
, Eqs. (34) and (40)]
). This advantage is due to the fact that we respect the intrinsic quotient structure (
14
) by introducing the Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
X
be a
2\times 2
matrix with distinct eigenvalues
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. Then for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

X^{t}=\frac{\mu_{1}^{t}-\mu_{2}^{t}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}X-\frac{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}^{t}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}^{t}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}I_{2}.

Proof.

Since
X
is
2\times 2
, the Cayley-Hamilton theorem tells us that
X
satisfies its own characteristic equation:

X^{2}-(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})X+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}I_{2}=0.

(33)

In what follows, we prove the desired result by induction. The conclusion holds obviously for the cases of
t=0,1,2
. Now suppose that the conclusion holds for the case of
t=\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the case of
t=\ell+1
. Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle X^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=X^{\ell}\cdot X=\left(\frac{\mu_{1}^{\ell}-\mu_{2}^{\ell}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}X-\frac{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}^{\ell}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}^{\ell}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}I_{2}\right)X

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}^{\ell}-\mu_{2}^{\ell}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\left((\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})X-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}I_{2}\right)-\frac{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}^{\ell}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}^{\ell}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}X

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}^{\ell+1}-\mu_{2}^{\ell+1}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}X-\frac{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}^{\ell+1}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}^{\ell+1}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}I_{2}.

Here the second equality follows from mathematical induction and the third one uses Eq. (
33
). As a result, the conclusion holds for the case of
t=\ell+1
as well. The proof is complete.
∎

\mathcal{E}

\dim(\mathcal{M})=d
,
\bm{x}^{\star}\in\mathcal{M}
is an index-
k
constrained SP of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
is nondegenerate, whose eigenvalues are
\lambda_{1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}^{\star}<0<\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{d}^{\star}
, and
P^{\star}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}_{\bm{x}^{\star}}\mathcal{M})
is an orthogonal projector onto the lowest
k
-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(\bm{x}^{\star})
. Let
\bm{w}^{\star}:=(\bm{x}^{\star},P^{\star})\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
and
\{\bm{w}^{(t)}:=(\bm{x}^{(t)},P^{(t)})\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm

. If the following two assumptions hold:

•

(Smallness of the step size)
the step size fulfills

0<\eta<\min\left\{\frac{2}{\Delta\lambda_{d1}^{\star}},\frac{q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)}{M},\frac{\min\{(r_{1}-r_{2})/c_{1},r_{1}\}}{\sup_{\|D\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}\leq r_{1}}\|\bm{h}(\tilde{\bm{w}}(D))\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}}\right\}

(34)

with
c_{1}
and
r_{1}
the constants in Lemma
6
(associated to
\mathcal{N}=\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})
,
\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathcal{N}}=\mathrm{Retr}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
or
\mathrm{Exp}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
, and
\bm{y}=\bm{w}^{\star}
) and
r_{2}\in(0,r_{1})
;

•

(Proximity of the initial point to the desired constrained SP)
the initial point satisfies

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}(\bm{w}^{\star},\bm{w}^{(0)})<\frac{r_{3}}{\max\left\{\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{2}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)},1\right\}\|A(\eta)\|,1\right\}},

(35)

where the constants
c_{3}
and
r_{3}
are defined in Lemma

and are chosen such that

c_{3}r_{3}<\frac{(1-q_{\max}(\eta))^{2}}{\eta M+2(1-q_{\max}(\eta))}\quad\text{with}\quad q_{\max}(\eta):=\max\left\{q_{1}(\eta),q_{2}(\eta)\right\},

(36)

and
\mu_{1}(\eta)
,
\mu_{2}(\eta)
are respectively

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\eta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}+\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta))^{2}+4c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}}{2},

(37)

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}-\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta))^{2}+4c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}}{2},

then
\{\bm{w}^{(t)}\}
converges linearly to
\bm{w}^{\star}
.

Proof.

We first show by induction that
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}(\bm{w}^{\star},\bm{w}^{(t)})<r_{3}
holds for any
t
, so that
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{w}^{\star}}^{\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M})}
remains a diffeomorphism and Eq. (
32
) is applicable for all the iterates. The case of
t=0
is evident due to the assumption (
35
) on the initial point. Now suppose that the statement holds for the case of
t=\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the case of
t=\ell+1
. By induction and Eq. (
32
), there exists a unique
D^{(t+1)}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}^{\star}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
such that
\|D^{(t+1)}\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}<r_{3}
and
D^{(t+1)}=F(D^{(t)})
hold for
t=0,\ldots,\ell-1
, and that

\begin{pmatrix}\|F_{1}(D^{(\ell)})\|\\
\|F_{2}(D^{(\ell)})_{\bm{x}^{\star}}\|\end{pmatrix}\leq A(\eta)^{\ell+1}\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix}.

(38)

It is not difficult to verify that
\mu_{1}(\eta)
and
\mu_{2}(\eta)
in Eq. (
37
) are the two distinct real eigenvalues of
A(\eta)
, and both of them belong to
(0,1)
:

\displaystyle 2\mu_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}-\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta))^{2}+4c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}

\displaystyle>q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}-\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta))^{2}+4c_{3}r_{3}(q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+c_{3}r_{3})}

\displaystyle=q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}-\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3})^{2}-4q_{1}(\eta)q_{2}(\eta)}\geq 0,

where the strict inequality is due to the assumption (
34
), and

\displaystyle 2\mu_{1}(\eta)

\displaystyle=q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}+\sqrt{(q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta))^{2}+4c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}

\displaystyle\leq q_{1}(\eta)+q_{2}(\eta)+2c_{3}r_{3}+\left|q_{1}(\eta)-q_{2}(\eta)\right|+2\sqrt{c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}

\displaystyle=2\left(c_{3}r_{3}+q_{\max}(\eta)+\sqrt{c_{3}r_{3}(\eta M+c_{3}r_{3})}\right)

\displaystyle<2\left(\frac{(1-q_{\max}(\eta))^{2}}{\eta M+2(1-q_{\max}(\eta))}+q_{\max}(\eta)+\sqrt{\frac{(1-q_{\max}(\eta))^{2}(\eta M+1-q_{\max}(\eta))^{2}}{(\eta M+2(1-q_{\max}(\eta)))^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{(1-q_{\max}(\eta))^{2}}{\eta M+2(1-q_{\max}(\eta))}+q_{\max}(\eta)+\frac{(1-q_{\max}(\eta))(\eta M+1-q_{\max}(\eta))}{\eta M+2(1-q_{\max}(\eta))}\right)=2,

where the strict inequality uses the assumption (
36
) and the third equality uses
q_{1}(\eta),q_{2}(\eta)<1
from the assumption (
34
). By Lemma
8
,

\displaystyle~A(\eta)^{\ell+1}\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left(\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{\ell+1}-\mu_{2}(\eta)^{\ell+1}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)}A(\eta)-\frac{\mu_{2}(\eta)\mu_{1}(\eta)^{\ell+1}-\mu_{1}(\eta)\mu_{2}(\eta)^{\ell+1}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)}I_{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{2}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)},1\right\}A(\eta)\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix},

(39)

where the inequality is due to
0<\mu_{2}(\eta)<\mu_{1}(\eta)<1
. Eqs. (
38
), (
39
), and the assumption (
35
) on the initial point then together imply

\displaystyle\|F(D^{(\ell)})\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|F_{1}(D^{(\ell)})\|^{2}+\|F_{2}(D^{(\ell)})_{\bm{x}^{\star}}\|^{2}=\left\|A(\eta)^{\ell+1}\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{2}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)},1\right\}^{2}\left\|A(\eta)\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{2}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)},1\right\}^{2}\|A(\eta)\|^{2}\|D^{(0)}\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}^{2}<r_{3}^{2}.

Consequently, there exists a unique
D^{(\ell+1)}\in\mathrm{T}_{\bm{w}^{\star}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}))
such that
\|D^{(\ell+1)}\|<r_{3}
and
D^{(\ell+1)}=F(D^{(\ell)})
. The statement thus holds for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now by Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
), we have for any

that

\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(t)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(t)}\|\end{pmatrix}\leq\left(\frac{\mu_{1}(\eta)^{t}-\mu_{2}(\eta)^{t}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)}A(\eta)-\frac{\mu_{2}(\eta)\mu_{1}(\eta)^{t}-\mu_{1}(\eta)\mu_{2}(\eta)^{t}}{\mu_{1}(\eta)-\mu_{2}(\eta)}I_{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\|\bm{\delta}^{(0)}\|\\
\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(0)}\|\end{pmatrix},

which combined with
0\leq\mu_{2}(\eta)<\mu_{1}(\eta)<1
and
\|D^{(t)}\|_{\bm{w}^{\star}}^{2}=\|\bm{\delta}^{(t)}\|^{2}+\|\Delta_{\bm{x}^{\star}}^{(t)}\|^{2}
implies that

converges linearly to 0, or equivalently,
\{\bm{w}^{(t)}\}
converges linearly to
\bm{w}^{\star}
as

. The proof is completed.

Remark 2

(Satisfiability of the assumptions)
.

It might seem to be difficult at first glance to check the satisfiability of the assumptions (
34
) and (
36
). Indeed, after noting that
q_{1}(\eta)
,
q_{2}(\eta)\to 1-
as
\eta\to 0+
, the assumption (
34
) can always be met with a sufficiently small
\eta
. Once
\eta
is fixed, one could choose an
r_{3}
small enough to meet the assumption (
36
), since
c_{3}
, as a function of
r_{3}
, is uniformly bounded in a small neighborhood of origin.

Remark 3

(Local convergence rates and condition numbers)
.

Lemma
7
provides some information about the local convergence rates of
\bm{x}
- and
P
-residuals. Indeed, as long as
r_{3}
is sufficiently small, the contraction matrix
A(\eta)
in Eq. (
32
) becomes close to diagonal, with entries dominated by
q_{1}(\eta)
and
q_{2}(\eta)
. They can then be viewed as the contractive factors of
\bm{x}
- and
P
-residuals, respectively.

Moreover, if two different step sizes
\eta_{\bm{x}}
and
\eta_{P}
are used, it is possible for us to approach the best linear convergence rates. By the definitions of
q_{1}(\eta)
and
q_{2}(\eta)
in Lemma
7
, it is easy to show that the step sizes minimizing these two terms are

\displaystyle\eta_{\bm{x}}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\lambda_{\min}^{\star}+\lambda_{\max}^{\star}}=\frac{2}{\min_{i=1}^{d}|\lambda_{i}^{\star}|+\max_{i=1}^{d}|\lambda_{i}^{\star}|},

(40)

\displaystyle\eta_{P}^{\star}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\Delta\lambda_{k+1,k}^{\star}+\Delta\lambda_{d1}^{\star}}=\frac{2}{\lambda_{d}^{\star}+\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}-\lambda_{k}^{\star}-\lambda_{1}^{\star}}.

(41)

The condition numbers are thus estimated by

\kappa_{\bm{x}}:=\frac{\lambda_{\max}^{\star}}{\lambda_{\min}^{\star}}=\frac{\max_{i=1}^{d}|\lambda_{i}^{\star}|}{\min_{i=1}^{d}|\lambda_{i}^{\star}|},\quad\kappa_{P}:=\frac{\Delta\lambda_{d1}^{\star}}{\Delta\lambda_{k+1,k}^{\star}}=\frac{\lambda_{d}^{\star}-\lambda_{1}^{\star}}{\lambda_{k+1}^{\star}-\lambda_{k}^{\star}}.

(42)

We shall note that the best step sizes in practice are not necessarily given by Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
) exactly, since Eq. (
32
) provides only an estimate from above and the terms other than
q_{1}(\eta)
and
q_{2}(\eta)
in the contraction matrix
A(\eta)
might not be negligible. For numerical illustrations, see Section
5.1
.

Example 2

(Condition numbers at index-1 constrained SPs)
.

Consider a linear objective function over
\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{Gr}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
p>1
) defined by
f(P)={\rm Tr\,}(PA)
for any
P\in\mathcal{M}
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}
. It is not difficult to obtain the closed-form expression of Riemannian Hessian:

\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(P)[\Gamma]=[P,[\Gamma,A]],\quad\forall~P\in\mathcal{M},~\Gamma\in\mathrm{T}_{P}\mathcal{M}.

For simplicity, suppose that
A
is diagonal, i.e.,
A=\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n})
with
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\varepsilon_{n}
, and that all the sums of
p
eigenvalues of
A
are different. Under this assumption, one could verify that the unique global minimizer (GM) and unique index-1 constrained SP of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
are respectively

P_{\rm GM}:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\rm SP}:=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}+\bm{e}_{p+1}\bm{e}_{p+1}^{\top},

where
\bm{e}_{i}
is the
i
-th unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
). The eigenpairs of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(P_{\rm GM})
are given explicitly by

\left\{\left.\left(\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{i},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{a}^{\top}+\bm{e}_{a}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\right)\right)\right|i=1,\ldots,p,~a=p+1,\ldots,n\right\},

and for
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{M}}\,f(P_{\rm SP})
,

\left\{\left.\left(\varepsilon_{a}-\varepsilon_{i},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{a}^{\top}+\bm{e}_{a}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\right)\right)\right|i=1,\ldots,p-1,p+1,~a=p,p+2,\ldots,n\right\}.

The condition number at
P_{\rm GM}
is

\kappa_{P_{\rm GM}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}-\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{p+1}-\varepsilon_{p}},

which is well known in the literature. The denominator is also called the “eigengap” in some applications. Regarding the condition number at
P_{\rm SP}
, with reference to Eq. (
42
), we could identify
\lambda_{1}^{\star}=\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{p+1}<0
,
\lambda_{2}^{\star}=\min\{\varepsilon_{p+2}-\varepsilon_{p+1},\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{p-1}\}>0
, and
\lambda_{d}^{\star}=\varepsilon_{n}-\varepsilon_{1}>0
(with
d=\dim(\mathcal{M})=p(n-p)
), and therefore,

\lambda_{\min}^{\star}=\min\left\{\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{p-1},\varepsilon_{p+1}-\varepsilon_{p},\varepsilon_{p+2}-\varepsilon_{p+1}\right\},\quad\lambda_{\max}^{\star}=\varepsilon_{n}-\varepsilon_{1},

which implies

\kappa_{P_{\rm SP}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}-\varepsilon_{1}}{\min\left\{\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{p-1},\varepsilon_{p+1}-\varepsilon_{p},\varepsilon_{p+2}-\varepsilon_{p+1}\right\}}.

Comparing
\kappa_{P_{\rm SP}}
with
\kappa_{P_{\rm GM}}
, we observe that finding the index-1 constrained SPs can be worse conditioned. Moreover, it asks for not only a positive eigengap, but also nondegeneracy above
\varepsilon_{p+1}
and below
\varepsilon_{p}
. Another useful message is that, if the problem is reformulated on the Stiefel manifold
\mathrm{St}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and is treated by the saddle search algorithms in the existing works
[
52
,
54
,
87
,
90
]
, we could anticipate their poor performance due to the inherent degeneracy. For illustrations, see Section
5.1
.

5
Numerical experiments

In this part, we report numerical results on the linear eigenvalue problem and electronic excited-state calculations. Both tasks sit on the Grassmann manifold. In particular, with the former one, we elucidate the importance of using nonredundant parametrizations in finding SPs (cf. Example
2
) and show the influence of problem and algorithm settings on the convergence rates (cf. Remark
3
).

5.1
Linear eigenvalue problem

Given a real symmetric matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}
, the linear eigenvalue problem amounts to finding a critical point
X^{\star}
of the quadratic function
f_{\mathrm{St}}(X):=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(X^{\top}AX)
over the Stiefel manifold
\mathrm{St}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is well known that the columns of any such point span a
p
-dimensional invariant subspace of
A
. Moreover, the function value
f_{\mathrm{St}}(X^{\star})
is exactly half the sum of
p
eigenvalues of
A
. If all the sums (
\binom{n}{p}
in total) are different from each other, the indices of the saddle points can be indicated by their function values, in that
f_{\mathrm{St}}(X_{{\rm SP},k}^{\star})<f_{\mathrm{St}}(X_{{\rm SP},\ell}^{\star})
holds for the index-
k
and index-
\ell
constrained SPs whenever
k<\ell
(the reverse might not be true). Note that
f_{\mathrm{St}}
is invariant under the transformation
X\mapsto XQ
for any
Q\in\mathcal{O}(p)
. Therefore, one could instead consider a linear function
f_{\mathrm{Gr}}(P):=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(PA)
over the Grassmann manifold
\mathrm{Gr}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any of its critical point
P^{\star}
is the orthogonal projector onto a
p
-dimensional invariant subspace of
A
. The statements regarding the function values and indices of constrained SPs remain valid similarly.

Implementation details.
In what follows, we construct the test matrix
A
as
A=U\Sigma U^{\top}
, where

\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
\sigma_{i}:=\xi^{i-n}
(

) for a parameter

. The problem data
n
,
p
, and
\xi
are specified later. We consider Algorithm
2
of Stiefel and Grassmann versions (

and

20
) and (

); for the position part, we adopt the QR decomposition-based second-order retraction over
\mathrm{St}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the exponential mapping over
\mathrm{Gr}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively, whereas for the direction part, we perform orthogonal projections onto the new tangent space. The step sizes are specified later. The maximum iteration number and convergence tolerance are respectively set as

and

We remark that the failure of the Stiefel version should be ascribed to the parametrization redundancy. The formulation over the Stiefel manifold does not take into account the quotient structure, as explained in the first paragraph of this subsection. The redundancy brings degeneracies above and/or below the eigengap and leads to an infinite condition number at the desired index-1 constrained SP (cf. Example
2
). The deficiency cannot be neglected as in the task of finding the global minimizer because the update direction for the position is not a horizontal lift of that in the Grassmann version. Roughly, suppose that the initial point lies in a region where the Riemannian Hessian is positive semidefinite (say, around the global minimizer) and the direction step converges quickly to the lowest eigenvector of the Riemannian Hessian. Due to the parametrization redundancy, the lowest eigenvalue of the Riemannian Hessian is zero and the direction variable
V(t)
is almost vertical. This implies that climbing up along
V(t)
makes little difference in the first order and the position variable mainly follows the gradient flow down to the minimizer.

The above arguments are supported with numerical results. 5.2
Electronic excited-state calculations

The core of electronic calculations for molecular systems is the electronic Schrödinger equation (ESE)
[
75
]
, which is in fact a linear eigenvalue problem. Nevertheless, the ESE is intractable in general due to the curse of dimensionality. For the numerical purpose, various approximations have been proposed in some atomic-orbital basis, such as the full configuration interaction (FCI), Hartree-Fock (HF) methods, and post-HF methods
[
40
]
, among others. Excited states define the optical and reaction properties of atoms and molecules
[
5
,
61
,
83
]
. Characterizing the excited states is challenging due to electron correlation effects
[
32
]
. Finding the constrained SPs of the quantum chemical approximated methods in use arises as a natural methodology
[
15
,
14
,
16
,
51
,
63
,
73
]
. Moreover, these approximations usually come together with manifold structures. In the following, we briefly introduce the HF methods and report the numerical results of finding constrained SPs as candidates of excited states, with FCI calculations (performed by
PySCF

[
80
]
) as reference.
More advanced levels of theory, such as the complete active space self-consistent field method
[
72
]
, involve complicated manifolds and are investigated in a parallel work. Before proceeding, we remark that our methodology falls into the class of state-specific methods in quantum chemistry for electronic excited states; other popular ones include linear response theory
[
17
,
19
,
34
,
67
]
and state-average methods for multiconfigurational approximations
[
85
]
.

The HF methods.
The HF approximation restricts the electronic wavefunction to a single Slater determinant parametrized by orthonormal molecular orbitals
[
29
,
36
]
. In this work, we consider the restricted HF (in short, RHF) method. The RHF method assumes all molecular orbitals to be doubly occupied, by one spin-up and one spin-down electron. As a result, the spatial orbitals are considered to be the same for both spin-up and spin-down electrons. The spatial orbitals are expressed as the linear combinations of chosen atomic-orbitals, with the coefficients to be determined. The atomic-orbitals are assumed to be real hereafter.

For a closed-shell system with
N_{\rm elec}\in\mathbb{N}
electrons, the RHF approximation gives rise to the following energy functional over the Grassmann manifold:

E^{\mathrm{RHF}}(\gamma):=2\mathrm{Tr}(h\gamma)+\mathrm{Tr}((2J(\gamma)-K(\gamma))\gamma)\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma\in\mathrm{Gr}_{N_{\rm o}}(\mathbb{R}^{N_{{\rm b}}}),

where
N_{{\rm b}}\in\mathbb{N}
is the size of real atomic-orbital basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{{\rm b}}}
,
N_{\rm o}\in\mathbb{N}
the number of occupied molecular orbitals (
2N_{\rm o}=N_{\rm elec}
),
h\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{N_{{\rm b}}\times N_{{\rm b}}}
the discretized one-body Hamiltonian, and
\gamma
the discretized one-body reduced density matrix. Here,
J
,
K:\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{N_{{\rm b}}\times N_{{\rm b}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{N_{{\rm b}}\times N_{{\rm b}}}
are respectively the Coulomb and exchange functionals, defined as

[J(\gamma)]_{pq}:=\sum_{r,s=1}^{N_{{\rm b}}}g_{pqrs}\gamma_{sr},\quad[K(\gamma)]_{pq}:=\sum_{r,s=1}^{N_{{\rm b}}}g_{psrq}\gamma_{sr},\quad p,q=1,\ldots,N_{{\rm b}},

with

g_{pqrs}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\phi_{p}(\bm{r})\phi_{q}(\bm{r})\phi_{r}(\bm{r}^{\prime})\phi_{s}(\bm{r}^{\prime})}{\left|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}\right|}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{r}^{\prime},\quad p,q,r,s=1,\ldots,N_{{\rm b}}

the two-body integrals.

The above results provide proof-of-concept evidence for the effectiveness of our algorithms in excited-state calculations. Nonetheless, we shall point out that a comprehensive quantum chemical analysis of the obtained SPs, though beyond the scope of present work, is essential for practical applications. As a nonlinear approximation to the exact theory, RHF may produce critical points that lack physical meanings; e.g., a spurious non-global local minimizer emerges when the bond length exceeds 2.7 bohr in the right panel of Figure

Molecular geometries must also be taken into account. In the exact theory, the ESE is parameterized by atomic configurations. Variations in molecular geometry may induce degeneracies and crossings in states, which would lead to unfavorable features of potential energy surfaces within approximate theories (cf. Figure

[

6
Conclusions

We have developed a constrained saddle dynamics for finding SPs on general Riemannian manifolds. The dynamics is formulated compactly over the Grassmann bundle of the tangent bundle, and achieves broad applicability by incorporating the second fundamental form, which captures variations of tangent spaces along the trajectory. By investigating the Grassmann bundle geometry, we have rigorously established the theoretical properties of both the dynamics and the resulting discretized algorithms. Remarkably, our analysis provides the first linear convergence results of the discretized algorithms in manifold settings. Moreover, compared with existing results, we eliminate unnecessary nondegeneracy assumptions on the eigenvalues of the Riemannian Hessian by adopting a single orthogonal projector as the direction variable, thereby respecting the underlying quotient structure. We have also characterized how the spectrum of the Riemannian Hessian affects the local convergence rates and highlighted the importance of using nonredundant parametrizations. Both of these two points have been validated through numerical results on linear eigenvalues problems. Finally, we have applied the proposed algorithms to electronic excited-state calculations.

There remains lots of directions to be explored. The numerical performance of the discretized algorithms is highly sensitive to condition numbers, as evidenced by their local convergence rates. It is thus desirable to incorporate higher-order contributions without sacrificing local convergence properties. In addition, a globally convergent method for locating SPs on Riemannian manifolds is still lacking, due to the absence of a global merit function. One possible avenue is to extend the analysis in
[
49
]
and develop stochastic methods on manifolds. From the perspective of quantum chemistry, it would also be valuable to investigate the manifold geometry underlying more complicated levels of theory and to devise efficient yet physically meaningful strategies for navigating the associated nonconvex energy landscapes.

The Small-Gain Condition for Infinite Networks Modeled on ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} -Spaces
Christoph Kawan

Abstract
In recent years, attempts have been made to extend nonlinear small-gain theorems for input-to-state stability (ISS) from finite networks to countably infinite networks with finite indegrees. Under specific assumptions about the interconnection gains and the ISS formulation, corresponding infinite-dimensional small-gain results have been proven. However, concerning these assumptions, the results are still too narrow to be considered a full extension of the state-of-the-art for finite networks. We take a step to closing this gap by developing a general technical framework within which the small-gain condition for both finite and infinite networks can be analyzed. This includes a thorough investigation of various monotone operators associated with a network and a specific ISS formulation. Our results extend and generalize the existing theory for finite networks and yield complete characterizations of the small-gain condition for specific ISS formulations. They also provide much deeper insight into the different characterizations of the small-gain condition, including the no-joint-increase condition Γ ⁡ ( s ) ≱ s \Gamma(s)\not\geq s .

Introduction

Given a stabilizing feedback controller for a nonlinear system, it is possible that small deviations from this controller lead to divergent behavior. Since any practical implementation of a controller necessarily deviates from the conceptual controller, this is a serious problem. As a remedy, Eduardo Sontag introduced the concept of input-to-state stability (ISS) in
[
20
]
. If the open-loop system obtained by including time-dependent bounded perturbations into the feedback loop is input-to-state-stable, practical stability is guaranteed and the bounded deviation of trajectories from the equilibrium can be estimated quantitatively over an infinite time horizon. As any general property of a system can be hard to verify in a concrete example, it is necessary to find sufficient conditions for ISS which can be checked by computation. Sontag also introduced the notion of an ISS Lyapunov function, whose existence is equivalent to ISS, at least in finite dimensions. This shifts the problem of verifying ISS directly to the construction (or proof of existence) of an ISS Lyapunov function, which is not necessarily easier though, especially for high-dimensional systems.

The small-gain approach tries to mitigate the difficulty by splitting the task of finding a Lyapunov function into two manageable subtasks. The idea is to decompose the system into a number of lower-dimensional, physically interconnected subsystems. Every subsystem has two types of inputs, the external perturbations acting on the overall system and the outputs of the other subsystems. If one can find an ISS Lyapunov function for each subsystem with respect to both types of inputs, there is a chance that these functions can be composed to an overall ISS Lyapunov function. This strategy works if the interconnections are sufficiently weak, which is formalized in terms of a so-called
small-gain condition
. The latter can be formulated as a property of a monotone operator
\Gamma
(called
gain operator
), built from the interconnection gains and acting on the positive cone of an ordered vector space. In finite dimensions, there are four equivalent ways to describe the small-gain condition: in terms of a path evolving in the decay set of
\Gamma
, in terms of an asymptotic stability property of the dynamical system generated by
\Gamma
, in terms of a monotone bounded invertibility property of the operator
\mathrm{id}-\Gamma
, and in terms of the componentwise action of
\Gamma
in one time step. The last characterization is particularly useful, since it allows for computational verification in many cases. There is also a connection to the theory of positive linear operators. Indeed, under specific assumptions about the gains and the ISS formulation,
\Gamma
becomes a positive linear operator and the small-gain condition is equivalent to the spectral radius condition
r(\Gamma)<1
.

An alternative view on the small-gain theory is that the network structure is given a priori by the setup of the control system, which naturally splits into many interacting components that one wants to control simultaneously, and which may be distributed over a large area. Examples can be found, e.g., in automated traffic systems, electric power grids, or aerospace and defense systems. In such applications, the number of subsystems can vary in time and become quite large. As the number of subsystems in a network grows, measures of stability may deteriorate even if ISS is preserved. A successful approach to dealing with large systems often is to over-approximate them by some sort of infinite systems. This strategy is used, for instance, in physics to model the dynamics of a fluid or a gas, consisting of an enormous number of interacting molecules. Motivated by such ideas, we study the ISS small-gain approach for networks consisting of an infinite number of interacting systems. First results in this direction have been obtained in
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
. However, in all of these papers, the authors either restricted their attention to a narrow class of gains and/or formulations of the ISS property, or did not obtain a full generalization of the existing results for finite networks as presented in
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
19
]
.

In this paper, we aim at such a generalization. To this end, we introduce a very general class of gain operators associated with a network of countably many interconnected systems. We mainly impose one restriction, namely that each subsystem is only influenced by finitely many other subsystems. For such a setup, the paper
[
12
]
already provided a small-gain theorem in which the small-gain condition was formulated in terms of the existence of a so-called
path of strict decay
. However, the existence of such a path was only investigated for a particular formulation of the ISS property, leading to gain operators with a very convenient algebraic property. In this paper, we aspire much greater generality.

We abstain from an extensive literature review on the control of infinite networks or input-to-state stability of infinite-dimensional systems, since one can find such reviews in the papers cited above. We just mention
[
1
,
2
]
as two prominent examples, which have connections to the topic of this paper, but address control problems for large-scale or infinite networks rather than mere stability problems. Our paper also abstains from the discussion of concrete examples, since they would not provide a valuable contribution to the general theory without a much deeper analysis tailored to those examples, which can be part of future work. Finally, we note that our results can potentially contribute to the general theory of monotone operators on Banach spaces, but this needs some further abstraction, which can also be part of future work.

We now define gain operators.

Definition 2.1
.

The
gain operator

\Gamma:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
associated with the graph
\mathcal{G}
, the interconnection gains
\gamma_{ij}
, and the MAFs
\mu_{i}
, is defined by

\Gamma(s)=(\Gamma_{i}(s))_{i\in\mathcal{I}},\quad\Gamma_{i}(s):=\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}),

where we use the natural identification of
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I}_{i})
with
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}_{i})
.

In the rest of the paper, we speak of the
finite case
when we consider gain operators with
|\mathcal{I}|<\infty
and otherwise of the
infinite case
.

Example 2.1
.

We consider two of the most relevant examples for MAFs. Let

\mu_{\oplus}(s):=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}s_{i}=\|s\|,\quad\mu_{\oplus}:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow[0,\infty].

In case that
\mu_{i}\equiv\mu_{\oplus}
, we call
\Gamma
a
max-type operator
. Here, (M1) holds with
\xi=\mathrm{id}
and (M2), (M3) are trivial. If
\|s-s^{0}\|\leq\delta
for some
s^{0},s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
, the triangle inequality from below implies

\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Bigl|\sup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}s_{j}-\sup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}s^{0}_{j}\Bigr|\leq\sup_{ji\in E(\mathcal{G})}|s_{j}-s^{0}_{j}|\leq\|s-s^{0}\|\leq\delta.

Hence, also (M4) is satisfied (even on the whole cone
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
). We thus see that for max-type operators the only requirement is that the interconnection gains form a pointwise equicontinuous family. Now, consider

\mu_{\otimes}(s):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}s_{i},\quad\mu_{\otimes}:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow[0,\infty].

In case that
\mu_{i}\equiv\mu_{\otimes}
, we call
\Gamma
a
sum-type operator
. Here, (M1) again holds with
\xi=\mathrm{id}
and (M2), (M3) are trivial. Concerning (M4),
\|s-s^{0}\|\leq\delta
implies

\Bigl|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}s_{j}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}s^{0}_{j}\Bigr|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}|s_{j}-s^{0}_{j}|\leq\delta\cdot|\mathcal{I}_{i}|,

which can hold with equality in certain cases. Hence, for (M4) it is necessary and sufficient that the cardinality
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|
is bounded over
i\in\mathcal{I}
.

Elementary properties of gain operators are summarized in the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Any gain operator
\Gamma
has the following properties:

(a)

\Gamma
is well-defined, i.e.
\Gamma(s)\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
for all
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
.

(b)

\Gamma(0)=0
and
\Gamma
is a monotone operator.

(c)

\Gamma
is continuous and uniformly continuous on bounded sets.

(d)

\Gamma
is sequentially continuous in the weak
∗
-topology.

Proof.

First, consider the family
\mathcal{F}:=\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\}\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
f_{i}(r):\equiv\mu_{i}(r\mathds{1}_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}})
. By (M3), each
f_{i}
is finite-valued and continuous. We claim that
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly bounded on every compact interval
[0,r_{0}]
. To show this, we use (M4) with
\varepsilon=1
, which gives
\delta>0
such that
|r-r^{\prime}|\leq\delta
with
r,r^{\prime}\in[0,r_{0}]
implies

\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|f_{i}(r^{\prime})-f_{i}(r)|=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left|\mu_{i}\left(r^{\prime}\mathds{1}_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}}\right)-\mu_{i}\left(r\mathds{1}_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}}\right)\right|\leq 1,

leading to
f_{i}(r_{0})=|f_{i}(r_{0})-f_{i}(0)|\leq\lceil r_{0}/\delta\rceil
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
.

Now, we show that
\Gamma
is well-defined. To this end, let
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
and put
r:=\|s\|
. Then, by the monotonicity of each
\mu_{i}
and
\gamma_{ij}
, we have

\Gamma_{i}(s)=\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})\leq\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(r)]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})\mbox{\quad for all\ }i\in\mathcal{I}.

With the same arguments as used above for the family
\mathcal{F}
, the equicontinuity of
\{\gamma_{ij}\}
implies the existence of
R>0
with
\gamma_{ij}(r)\leq R
for all
ji\in E(\mathcal{G})
. We conclude the existence of
B>0
with

\|\Gamma(s)\|=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Gamma_{i}(s)\leq\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu_{i}\left(R\mathds{1}_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}}\right)=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}f_{i}(R)\leq B,

showing that
\Gamma
is well-defined. From the monotonicity assumptions on
\gamma_{ij}
and
\mu_{i}
, it follows that
\Gamma
is a monotone operator. By (M1), it is clear that
\Gamma(0)=0
. Hence, (a) and (b) are proven.

We now prove the uniform continuity of
\Gamma
on bounded sets. To this end, let
B\subset\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
be bounded, say
\|s\|\leq r
for all
s\in B
. Then also the set

A:=\left\{(\gamma_{ij}(s_{j}))_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}_{i})\ :\ s\in B,\ i\in\mathcal{I}\right\}

is bounded, because the equicontinuity of
\{\gamma_{ij}\}
implies
\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})\leq\gamma_{ij}(r)\leq R
for all
ji\in E(\mathcal{G})
and some
R>0
. For a given
\varepsilon>0
, we first choose
\delta=\delta(A,\varepsilon)
according to (M4). We subsequently choose
\rho>0
so that
\|s^{1}-s^{2}\|\leq\rho
with
s^{1},s^{2}\in B
implies
|\gamma_{ij}(s^{1}_{j})-\gamma_{ij}(s^{2}_{j})|\leq\delta
for all
ji\in E(\mathcal{G})
, which is possible by the pointwise equicontinuity of
\{\gamma_{ij}\}
(which is uniform on compact intervals). Altogether,
\|s^{1}-s^{2}\|\leq\rho
with
s^{1},s^{2}\in B
implies

\|\Gamma(s^{1})-\Gamma(s^{2})\|=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left|\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s^{1}_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})-\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s^{2}_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Hence, (c) is proved. To prove (d), consider a sequence
(s^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
s^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}s
for some
s
. By Lemma
A.1
, this means that
(s^{n})
is norm-bounded and converges componentwise to
s
. Then, for every
i\in\mathcal{I}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Gamma_{i}(s^{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s^{n}_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})=\mu_{i}\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[\gamma_{ij}(s^{n}_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}\Bigl(\Bigl[\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{ij}(s^{n}_{j})\Bigr]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\Bigr)=\mu_{i}\Bigl(\Bigl[\gamma_{ij}\Bigl(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}s^{n}_{j}\Bigr)\Bigr]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}\left(\left[\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})\right]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\right)=\Gamma_{i}(s).

Here, the second equality follows from (M3). The third equality follows from finiteness of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
, and the fourth from continuity of
\gamma_{ij}
. Hence,
(\Gamma(s^{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\Gamma(s)
componentwise. Moreover, if
B>0
is a bound on
\|s^{n}\|
, then
s^{n}\leq B\mathds{1}
and thus
\Gamma(s^{n})\leq\Gamma(B\mathds{1})
due to monotonicity, implying
\|\Gamma(s^{n})\|\leq\|\Gamma(B\mathds{1})\|
for all
n
. Consequently,
\Gamma(s^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\Gamma(s)
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The sequential continuity of
\Gamma

Definition 2.3
.

Given a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, a path
\sigma
in the cone
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
is called a
path of strict decay
for
\Gamma
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}(\sigma(r))\leq\sigma(r)
for all
r\geq 0
.

(ii)

There exist
\varphi_{\min},\varphi_{\max}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\varphi_{\min}(r)\mathds{1}\leq\sigma(r)\leq\varphi_{\max}(r)\mathds{1}
for all
r\geq 0
.

(iii)

Each component function
\sigma_{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, is a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function.

(iv)

For every compact interval
K\subset(0,\infty)
, there exist constants
0<l\leq L
such that for all
r_{1},r_{2}\in K
and
i\in\mathcal{I}

l|r_{1}-r_{2}|\leq|\sigma_{i}^{-1}(r_{1})-\sigma_{i}^{-1}(r_{2})|\leq L|r_{1}-r_{2}|.

If all of the above properties hold with the exception that
\rho=0
in (i), we call
\sigma
a

for
\Gamma
.

Remark 2.3
.

Observe that a path of strict decay for
\Gamma

with
\rho

\sigma(0)=0

Proposition 2.2

(a)

In property (iv), the functions
\sigma_{i}^{-1}

.

(b)

\varphi_{\min}=\mathrm{id}

(a) Assume that for every compact interval

and

. If

Hence, with the corresponding constants

, we obtain

\frac{1}{C}|r_{1}-r_{2}|\leq|\sigma_{i}^{-1}(r_{1})-\sigma_{i}^{-1}(r_{2})|\leq\frac{1}{c}|r_{1}-r_{2}|\mbox{\quad for all\ }r_{1},r_{2}\in K.

(b) Given a path of decay
\sigma

The following proposition is the main result of this subsection. With this result at hand, finding a path of strict decay becomes a considerably easier task.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, assume that there exists a continuous and increasing path
\sigma

\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})

\Gamma
.

Proof.

The proof consists of two steps. In the first step, we assume that
\sigma
is strictly increasing, while in the second step we weaken this assumption.

Step 1
: Let
\sigma
be a path satisfying (i) and (ii) in Definition
2.3

[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
. We show that the restriction of
\sigma
to
[a,b]
can be approximated by a piecewise linear path evolving in
\Psi(\Gamma)
. The fact that such an approximation is possible will yield a piecewise linear path of decay. By Lemma
A.5
, there exists
\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
with
(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-1}=\mathrm{id}-\eta
. By assumption,
\sigma
is continuous, hence uniformly continuous on the compact interval
[a,b]
. Thus, we can find
\delta>0
such that
|r_{1}-r_{2}|<\delta
implies
\|\sigma(r_{1})-\sigma(r_{2})\|<\varepsilon:=\eta(\varphi_{\min}(a))
, whenever

s^{\alpha}:=(1-\alpha)\sigma(r_{1})+\alpha\sigma(r_{2})
for

and
r_{1},r_{2}\in[a,b]
with
r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{1}+\delta
. This implies
\sigma(r_{1})\leq s^{\alpha}\leq\sigma(r_{2})
, and thus

\displaystyle\Gamma(s^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(\sigma(r_{2}))\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-1}(\sigma(r_{2}))=\sigma(r_{2})-\eta(\sigma(r_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq\sigma(r_{2})-\eta(\sigma(a))\leq\sigma(r_{2})-\eta(\varphi_{\min}(a))\mathds{1}=\sigma(r_{2})-\varepsilon\mathds{1}\leq\sigma(r_{1})\leq s^{\alpha}.

From the assumption that
r_{1}<r_{2}
implies
\sigma(r_{1})\ll\sigma(r_{2})
, we conclude that the slope of

has a uniform lower bound. In fact, if
\sigma(r_{2})-\sigma(r_{1})\geq\gamma\mathds{1}

(0,\infty)

\tilde{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})

(0,\infty)
, evolves in
\Psi(\Gamma)

(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
(0,\infty)

r_{n}\rightarrow 0
for

and
r_{n}\rightarrow\infty
for
n\rightarrow\infty
, pick for each
r>0
the unique

\tilde{\sigma}

\tilde{\sigma}(r):=\left(1-\frac{r-r_{n}}{r_{n+1}-r_{n}}\right)\sigma(r_{n})+\frac{r-r_{n}}{r_{n+1}-r_{n}}\sigma(r_{n+1})\mbox{\quad for all\ }r>0,

\sigma

Step 2.a

(b)

Then clearly

(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{1}^{r})\circ(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r})=\mathrm{id}+f(r)\rho+(1-f(r))\rho=\mathrm{id}+\rho.

Item (a) above holds, because

for all

Step 2.b
: We define the path

for all
r\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\tilde{\rho}}(\tilde{\sigma}(r))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}+\tilde{\rho})\circ\Gamma\circ(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r})\circ\sigma(r)

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r})\circ\Gamma\circ(\mathrm{id}+\rho)\circ\sigma(r)=(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r})\circ\Gamma^{\rho}\circ\sigma(r)

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r})\circ\sigma(r)=\tilde{\sigma}(r).

Hence,

satisfies property (i) of a path of strict decay. Moreover, it holds that

showing that
\tilde{\sigma}
also satisfies property (ii) of a path of strict decay.

Step 2.c
: Let
r_{1}<r_{2}
and assume without loss of generality that

. We then have

. In particular, all components of the vector

are contained in the compact interval

. Observe that there exists
\alpha>0

\rho_{2}^{r_{2}}(r)-\rho_{2}^{r_{1}}(r)=(f(r_{2})-f(r_{1}))\rho(r)\geq\alpha

is strictly increasing:

\tilde{\sigma}(r_{1})=(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r_{1}})(\sigma(r_{1}))\ll(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r_{2}})(\sigma(r_{1}))\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho_{2}^{r_{2}})(\sigma(r_{2}))=\tilde{\sigma}(r_{2}).

Step 2.d:
It remains to prove the continuity of

By continuity of

is continuous.
∎

Lemma 2.1

Given a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, for all

and

\delta_{n}:=\varepsilon
and choose
\varepsilon_{i},\delta_{i}>0

\varepsilon_{n-1}:=\delta_{n}/2
and continue recursively by

\|s^{1}-s^{2}\|\leq\delta_{l}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\Gamma(s^{1})-\Gamma(s^{2})\|\leq\varepsilon_{l},\quad\varepsilon_{l-1}:=\frac{\delta_{l}}{2}

whenever

Now, let

with
\|s\|\leq r
and

such that

for all

. Inductively, we get estimates for

,

. First,

\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}-\delta=s_{j}-\delta_{1}\mbox{\quad for all\ }j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(n-1)

by assumption. Using, as the induction hypothesis, that
\Gamma^{l}_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}-\delta_{l}
for all

with

\displaystyle\Gamma^{l+1}_{j}(s)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{j}(\Gamma^{l}(s))=\Gamma_{j}([\Gamma^{l}_{k}(s)]_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{j}})\geq\Gamma_{j}([\max\{0,s_{k}-\delta_{l}\}]_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{j}})

\displaystyle\geq\Gamma_{j}(s)-\varepsilon_{l}\geq s_{j}-(\delta_{1}+\varepsilon_{l})\geq s_{j}-2\varepsilon_{l}=s_{j}-\delta_{l+1}.

For

\Gamma_{j_{i}}(s^{k})<(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-1}(s^{k}_{j_{i}})
. Then we obtain

\delta\leq s^{k_{i}}_{j_{i}}\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-(\lceil K_{i}/|\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(m)|\rceil-1)}(s_{j_{i}})\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-(\lceil K_{i}/B_{m}\rceil-1)}(\|s\|).

This implies that there is a bound on
K_{i}
, which is uniform in
i
, since the right-hand side converges to zero as
K_{i}\rightarrow\infty
. But if
K_{i}\leq K
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then
\|\Gamma^{K+1}(s)\|<\varepsilon
. The number
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small. Hence, we have shown that
\Gamma^{n}(s)
converges to zero, which completes the proof.
∎

Existence of paths of strict decay

In this section, we derive necessary conditions and sufficient conditions for the existence of a path of strict decay. Recall that by Proposition

it suffices to consider

3.1
The
\oplus
-MBI property

We first introduce a property that will turn out to be crucial for characterizing the existence of a path of strict decay. This property first appeared in
[
19
]
, but was only used for max-type gain operators there.

Definition 3.1
.

A generalized gain operator
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property
if there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that for all
s,b\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
the following implication holds:

s\leq\bar{\Gamma}_{b}(s)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|s\|\leq\varphi(\|b\|).

Remark 3.1
.

The letters
MBI
stand for
monotone bounded invertibility
. As an explanation for this name, think of
\Gamma
being a linear operator,
\oplus
being replaced with addition, and
\varphi
a linear function. Then the required property could be rewritten as

(\mathrm{id}-\Gamma)s\leq b\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|s\|\leq K\|b\|,

with a constant
K>0
. If we assume that
\mathrm{id}-\Gamma
is an invertible bounded operator with positive inverse, this property is satisfied with
K=\|(\mathrm{id}-\Gamma)^{-1}\|
. Hence, the
\oplus
-MBI property generalizes the bounded invertibility and positivity of the operator
\mathrm{id}-\Gamma
. By
[
9
, Thm. 3.3]
, this property is equivalent to the spectral radius of
\Gamma
being strictly less than one. Also recall that the
bounded inverse theorem
from functional analysis guarantees that an invertible bounded operator between Banach spaces always has a bounded inverse.

Remark 3.2
.

It is easy to see that for verifying the
\oplus
-MBI property one only needs to consider vectors of the form
b=r\mathds{1}
with
r\geq 0
.

Although we will not use the following proposition, we present it to demonstrate that the
\oplus
-MBI property is a natural property. For this result, observe that iterates of generalized gain operators are generalized gain operators as well.

Proposition 3.1
.

If a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property, then so does any iterate of
\Gamma
, with the same
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\varphi
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property and let

for some

and

. We put

\tilde{s}:=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m-1}\Gamma^{l}(s)=s\oplus\Gamma(s)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Gamma^{m-1}(s).

(4)

Due to the monotonicity of
\Gamma
, we have

\Gamma(\tilde{s})\geq\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m-1}\Gamma^{l+1}(s),

which leads to

\displaystyle b\oplus\Gamma(\tilde{s})

\displaystyle\geq b\oplus\bigoplus_{l=0}^{m-1}\Gamma^{l+1}(s)=b\oplus\Gamma(s)\oplus\Gamma^{2}(s)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Gamma^{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\left[b\oplus\Gamma^{m}(s)\right]\oplus\left[\Gamma(s)\oplus\Gamma^{2}(s)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Gamma^{m-1}(s)\right]

Hence,

Proposition 3.4
.

For a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
satisfies the
spatially uniform no-joint-increase (SU-NJI) condition
if the following holds: There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that for every

and

the following implication holds:

s_{i}>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}:\ s_{j}\geq\varphi(s_{i})\ \wedge\ \Gamma_{j}(s)<s_{j}.

Remark 3.3
.

The TU-NJI and the SU-NJI condition have a similar structure. Both are uniform versions of the NJI condition. The uniformity in the TU-NJI condition concerns the distance of the vertices
i
and
j
in the graph
\mathcal{G}
, while the uniformity in the SU-NJI condition concerns the relation between
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
. Indeed, the plain NJI condition can be written as

\forall s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I}),\ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}:\quad s_{i}>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}:\ \Gamma_{j}(s)<s_{j}.

Hence, the NJI condition and the SU-NJI condition only differ in the estimate
s_{j}\geq\varphi(s_{i})
. In the finite case, it follows from Proposition
2.7
that the SU-NJI condition implies the TU-NJI condition. In the infinite case, however, it is quite clear that the SU-NJI condition does not imply the TU-NJI condition, since the uniformity in the distance between the vertices
i
and
j
is completely missing in the first.

Remark 3.4
.

The SU-NJI condition has a strong similarity to the
uniform small-gain condition
from
[
15
]
: There exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
with

\|s\|\leq\sup\left\{\varphi(s_{i}-\Gamma_{i}(s))\ :\ i\in\mathcal{I},\ \Gamma_{i}(s)<s_{i}\right\}\mbox{\hskip 10.22217ptfor all\ }s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})

This condition is equivalent to the MBI property, which reads

s\leq b+\Gamma(s)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|s\|\leq\varphi(\|b\|).

In our paper, we do not use these conditions, however.

) at this index yields

\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime})-\varepsilon<s_{i}\leq\max\{r,\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime})-\eta(\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime}))\}\leq\max\{r,\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime})-\varepsilon\}.

To avoid a contradiction, the maximum at the end of the line must be attained at
r
. Since
r^{\prime}\leq\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime})
, this implies
r^{\prime}-\varepsilon\leq r
. Sending
\varepsilon
to zero, we obtain
r^{\prime}\leq r
, and thus
s\leq\sigma(r^{\prime})\leq\varphi(r^{\prime})\mathds{1}\leq\varphi(r)\mathds{1}
, which implies
\|s\|\leq\varphi(r)
. Hence,
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.∎

Remark 3.5
.

In general, it is not clear how the two properties in (a) and (b) are related to each other. In some cases, as we will show, one implies the other, but the general case is open. What we know for sure is that the
\oplus
-MBI property does not imply UGAS in the general infinite case. This can be seen by looking at max-type gain operators, where the
\oplus
-MBI property is equivalent to UGS plus GATT
{}^{\,*}
, which is strictly weaker than UGAS in the general infinite case.

3.3
Path construction

In this subsection, we introduce a method for the construction of a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay. We assume that
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property, which, in particular, implies that minimal and maximal fixed points
\sigma_{*}(r):=s_{*}(r\mathds{1})
and
\sigma^{*}(r):=s^{*}(r\mathds{1})
of the operator
\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}
exist for all
r\geq 0
, see Proposition
3.3
. We fix a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

s\leq\bar{\Gamma}_{b}(s)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|s\|\leq\varphi(\|b\|)\mbox{\quad for all\ }s,b\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I}).

The construction starts by fixing a strictly increasing double-sided sequence of real numbers
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
with

\lim_{k\rightarrow-\infty}r_{k}=0\mbox{\quad and \quad}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}r_{k}=\infty.

We also consider the associated sequence of points
s^{k}:=\sigma^{*}(r_{k})
, which has the following properties for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
:

•

s^{k}\leq s^{k+1}
.

•

r_{k}\mathds{1}\leq s^{k}\leq\varphi(r_{k})\mathds{1}
.

•

s^{k}\in\Psi(\Gamma)
, since
\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}})\subset\Psi(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}})\subset\Psi(\Gamma)
.

Our goal is to construct a
C^{0}
-path of decay
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
which satisfies
\sigma(r_{k})=s^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, we have to define
\sigma
on the open intervals
(r_{k},r_{k+1})
and at
r=0
. Here, we use statements (b) and (d) of Proposition
3.3
, which show that
s^{k,n}:=\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{k+1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}s^{k}
. We define the path on
(r_{k},r_{k+1})
by linear interpolation between the points
s^{k+1}=s^{k,0}
,
s^{k,1}
,
s^{k,2}
, and so on, and put
\sigma(0):=0
. In this way, we obtain a path
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
, satisfying the properties listed in the following proposition. Note that the parametrization of the path on the intervals
(r_{k},r_{k+1})
is not unique.

Proposition 3.5
.

The path
\sigma
satisfies properties (i)–(iii) of a
C^{0}
-path of decay. Additionally, it has the following continuity properties:

(C1)

It is norm-continuous from below, i.e., if
(\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
converging to some
\rho
from below, then
\|\sigma(\rho_{n})-\sigma(\rho)\|\rightarrow 0
.

(C2)

It is sequentially weakly
∗
continuous from above, i.e., if
(\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
converging to some
\rho
from above, then
\sigma(\rho_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sigma(\rho)
.

Proof.

Property (i) (decay): The points
s^{k}=\sigma(r_{k})
are points of decay of
\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}
, hence also points of decay of
\Gamma
. Observe that
s^{k+1}
is also a point of decay for
\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}
. By Lemma
A.8
, then also the points
\sigma(r)
with
r\in(r_{k},r_{k+1})
are contained in the decay set of
\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}
, and thus in
\Psi(\Gamma)
.

Property (ii) (coercivity): The piecewise constant functions

\tilde{\varphi}_{\min}(r):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}r_{k}&\mbox{if }r\in[r_{k},r_{k+1})\\
0&\mbox{if }r=0\end{array}\right.

and

\tilde{\varphi}_{\max}(r):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi(r_{k+1})&\mbox{if }r\in[r_{k},r_{k+1})\\
0&\mbox{if }r=0\end{array}\right.

can be used to lower-bound and upper-bound the components of
\sigma
, respectively. By Lemma
A.6
, the existence of
\varphi_{\min},\varphi_{\max}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
with
\varphi_{\min}\leq\tilde{\varphi}_{\min}
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{\max}\leq\varphi_{\max}
follow.

Property (iii) (monotonicity): This is clear by construction.

Property (C1): Norm-continuity of
\sigma
is obvious at any
\rho\notin\{r_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. If
(\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some
r_{k}
from below (i.e. from the left), then
\rho_{n}\in(r_{k-1},r_{k}]
for all sufficiently large
n
. But on
(r_{k-1},r_{k}]
,
\sigma
is piecewise linear, hence norm-continuous.

Property (C2): Again, we only have to consider the case
\rho=r_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
(\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
r_{k}
from above (i.e. from the right), for all sufficiently large
n
we can find
m_{n}
such that
\sigma(r_{k})\leq\sigma(\rho_{n})\leq\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}^{m_{n}}(s^{k+1})
, and
m_{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
\bar{\Gamma}_{r_{k}\mathds{1}}^{m_{n}}(s^{k+1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sigma(r_{k})
, then also
\sigma(\rho_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sigma(r_{k})
.
∎

If we additionally assume that the fixed point
\sigma^{*}(r)
is semi-attractive from above, i.e. that the convergence
\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s)\rightarrow\sigma^{*}(r)
for all
s\geq\sigma^{*}(r)
holds in the norm-topology, then
\sigma
is obviously continuous.

We have thus proved the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a generalized gain operator with the following properties:

(i)

\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

(ii)

The fixed point
\sigma^{*}(r)
of
\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}
is semi-attractive from above for all
r\geq 0
.

Then there exists a
C^{0}
-path of decay for
\Gamma
.

Remark 3.6
.

Observe that assumptions (a) and (b) together imply that
\Sigma(\Gamma)
is UGAS. Indeed, (a) implies UGS and (b) implies GATT by letting
r=0
.

From the construction of
\sigma
, we obtain a new characterization of the
\oplus
-MBI property.

Theorem 3.3
.

For a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

(b)

There exist
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and a path
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
satisfying the properties listed in Proposition
3.5
for the operator
\Gamma_{\rho}
(in place of
\Gamma
).

Proof.

We already know that (a) implies (b). To prove the converse, we can essentially use the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Let
s>0
(without loss of generality) and
r\geq 0
be chosen such that
s\leq r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma_{\rho}(s)
(for a
\rho
smaller than that in (b)). We define

r^{\prime}:=\min\{\tilde{r}\geq 0:s\leq\sigma(\tilde{r})\}.

To show that
r^{\prime}
is well-defined, we prove that the set
A:=\{\tilde{r}\geq 0:s\leq\sigma(\tilde{r})\}
is closed in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence, let
(\tilde{r}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
A
, converging to some
\tilde{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We have
s\leq\sigma(\tilde{r}_{n})
for all
n
. From the continuity assumptions on
\sigma
, it follows that
\sigma
is sequentially continuous in the weak
∗
-topology. Hence,
\sigma(\tilde{r}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sigma(\tilde{r})
, which clearly implies
s\leq\sigma(\tilde{r})
. It follows that
\tilde{r}\in A
, showing that
A
is closed. Moreover, we must have
r^{\prime}>0
, since otherwise
s=0
. Let us assume to the contrary that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
with
s\leq\sigma(r^{\prime})-\varepsilon\mathds{1}
. In this case, let
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
[0,r^{\prime})
, converging to
r^{\prime}
. Since
\sigma
is norm-continuous from below, it follows that
\|\sigma(r_{n})-\sigma(r^{\prime})\|\leq\varepsilon
for
n
sufficiently large. This implies
s\leq\sigma(r^{\prime})-\varepsilon\mathds{1}\leq\sigma(r_{n})
, which contradicts the minimality of
r^{\prime}
. Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an index
i\in\mathcal{I}
with
\sigma_{i}(r^{\prime})-s_{i}<\varepsilon
. The rest of the proof is identical to that of statement (b) in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Let us now assume that
\mathcal{I}
is infinite and
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I}
is a nonempty finite subset. This subset corresponds to a finite subnetwork with associated gain operator

\Gamma^{\langle\mathcal{J}\rangle}:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{J})\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{J}),\quad\Gamma^{\langle\mathcal{J}\rangle}_{i}(s):=\Gamma_{i}(s_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{J}}).

The associated graph
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{J}}
has vertex set
V(\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{J}})=\mathcal{J}
and edge set
E(\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{J}})=\{ji:i\in\mathcal{J},\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{J}\}
.

Now, assume that
\Gamma
admits a path
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
satisfying all properties listed in Proposition
3.5
. Then, we can consider the restricted path

\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}(r):=(\sigma_{i}(r))_{i\in\mathcal{J}},\quad\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{J}).

Since
\mathcal{J}
is finite, this path is continuous, and moreover, it is increasing and inherits the coercivity estimates of
\sigma
. Finally, for every
i\in\mathcal{J}
we have

\Gamma^{\langle\mathcal{J}\rangle}_{i}(\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}(r))=\Gamma_{i}(\sigma(r)_{|\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{J}})\leq\Gamma_{i}(\sigma(r))\leq(\mathrm{id}+\rho)^{-1}(\sigma_{i}(r)).

This implies
\Gamma^{\langle\mathcal{J}\rangle}_{\rho}\circ\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}\leq\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}
, hence
\sigma^{\mathcal{J}}
is a path of strict decay for
\Gamma^{\langle\mathcal{J}\rangle}
. We thus have the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property for some
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. Then the gain operator of every finite subnetwork admits a path of strict decay and the coercivity bounds are the same for all subnetworks.

4
Main results

Putting our partial results together allows us to formulate the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, consider the following statements:

(a)

There exists a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(b)

There exists a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(c)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
is UGAS and
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

(d)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies both uniform NJI conditions.

(e)

There exists a path
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
, satisfying all properties of a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay except for norm-continuity from above, which is replaced by sequential weak
∗
continuity (from above).

(f)

For every finite subnetwork, there exists a path of strict decay and the coercivity bounds are the same for all subnetworks.

Then, (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
\Rightarrow
(c)
\Leftrightarrow
(d)
\Rightarrow
(e)
\Rightarrow
(f). For the implication (d)
\Rightarrow
(c), the additional assumption that the operator is locally finite and
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|
is uniformly bounded is needed.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Obviously, every path of strict decay is a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): This is Proposition
2.3
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): This is Theorem
3.1
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(d): This follows from Proposition
2.8
and Proposition
3.4
.

(d)
\Rightarrow
(c): By Proposition
3.4
, the SU-NJI condition is equivalent to the
\oplus
-MBI property. The
\oplus
-MBI property implies that the corresponding decay set is cofinal by Proposition
3.2
. This together with the TU-NJI condition implies that
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
is GATT for some
\rho
by Proposition
2.9
. The
\oplus
-MBI property also implies that
\Sigma(\hat{\Gamma}_{\rho})
, and thus also
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
, is UGS, again by Proposition
3.2
. UGS and GATT together are equivalent to UGAS, which completes the proof.

(d)
\Rightarrow
(e): This follows from Theorem
3.3
. Note that here only the SU-NJI condition is needed as an assumption.

(e)
\Rightarrow
(f): This is Theorem
3.3
together with Corollary
3.2
.
∎

The remaining question is whether we can get back from (c) to (b), or which additional assumptions are needed for this implication. The hope would be that statements (a)–(d) are equivalent.

Some of the results that will be shown in the next section show that the implication “UGAS
\Rightarrow

\oplus
-MBI property” sometimes holds under additional assumptions on the gain operator. For instance, this is true if

is uniformly continuous and

. In general, however, we expect that the implication does not hold.

Using Proposition
3.2
, Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
, we obtain the following weaker version of Theorem

.

Theorem 4.2
.

For a generalized gain operator
\Gamma
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a path
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
, satisfying all properties of a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay except for norm-continuity from above, which is replaced by sequential weak
∗

(b)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

(c)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the SU-NJI condition.

(d)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Sigma(\hat{\Gamma}_{\rho})
is UGS,
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
is GATT

and the set
\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}})
is coercive.

This theorem becomes particularly interesting in the finite case, where it can be formulated as follows. Recall that here
\Gamma
is simply a continuous monotone operator with

.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
|\mathcal{I}|<\infty
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(b)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the

(c)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}

(d)

5
Particular classes of gain operators

In this section, we take a closer look at four particular classes of gain operators.

5.1
Uniformly continuous operators

By definition, any gain operator is uniformly continuous on bounded sets. In this subsection, we will show that the assumption of uniform continuity on the whole domain of definition allows for stronger conclusions. In particular, under this assumption we will obtain more clarity about the relation between the two necessary conditions for the existence of a path of strict decay, namely UGAS of
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
and the
\oplus
-MBI property of
\Gamma_{\rho}
, or the corresponding NJI conditions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given a uniformly continuous gain operator
\Gamma
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
the following holds:
\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}-\varphi(\varepsilon)
for all
j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(n-1)
implies
\Gamma_{i}^{n}(s)\geq s_{i}-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We use the characterization of uniform continuity in terms of comparison functions. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
be chosen such that

\|\Gamma(s^{1})-\Gamma(s^{2})\|\leq\gamma(\|s^{1}-s^{2}\|)

holds for all
s^{1},s^{2}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we then choose
\varepsilon_{i},\delta_{i}>0
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, now using the function
\gamma
. That is, we put
\delta_{n}:=\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon_{n-1}:=\delta_{n}/2
and continue recursively by

\delta_{l}:=\gamma^{-1}(\varepsilon_{l}),\quad\varepsilon_{l-1}:=\frac{\delta_{l}}{2}

We do this for
l=n-1
down to
l=2
. Eventually, we choose
\delta_{1}=:\delta
as

\delta_{1}:=\min\left\{\gamma^{-1}(\varepsilon_{1}),\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n-1}\right\}.

Without loss of generality, we can actually assume that
\gamma^{-1}<\mathrm{id}
, which implies
\delta_{1}=\gamma^{-1}(\varepsilon_{1})
. This obviously defines a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\varphi
, only depending on
n
, such that
\delta=\varphi(\varepsilon)
. The rest of the proof is completely analogous to that of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

What makes Lemma
5.1
superior to Lemma
2.1
is that its statement is not only meaningful for
\varepsilon
close to zero. The following proposition shows how this can be used.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a uniformly continuous gain operator. Then, under each of the following assumptions,
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property:

(i)

|\mathcal{I}|<\infty
and
\Sigma(\Gamma)
is GATT.

(ii)

\Sigma(\Gamma)
is UGAS with a
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
-function of the form
\beta(r,n)=r\gamma(n)
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

(i) We prove that
\Gamma
satisfies the SU-NJI condition with a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\varphi
. To this end, we pick
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}=\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(n)
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
n\geq m
, which is possible due to the finiteness of
\mathcal{I}
. Then, using Lemma
5.1
, we choose
\tilde{\varphi}=\tilde{\varphi}(m)\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
accordingly. We put
\varphi(r):\equiv\tilde{\varphi}(r/2)
and assume towards a contradiction that there exist
s\geq 0
and
i\in\mathcal{I}
with
s_{i}>0
, but
s_{j}<\varphi(s_{i})
or
\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}
for all
j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}
. Observe that in both cases
\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}-\tilde{\varphi}(s_{i}/2)
. Hence, Lemma
5.1
implies
\Gamma_{i}^{n}(s)\geq s_{i}/2>0
for all
n\geq m
. This obviously contradicts GATT.

(ii) We fix an
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
\gamma(m)<1/4
, choose
\tilde{\varphi}=\tilde{\varphi}(m)\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
according to Lemma
5.1
, and put
\varphi(r):\equiv\tilde{\varphi}(r/2)
. We show the following strong version of the TU-NJI condition: for all
s\geq 0
and
i\in\mathcal{I}
:

s_{i}\geq\frac{\|s\|}{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(m):\ s_{j}\geq\varphi\left(\frac{\|s\|}{2}\right)\wedge\Gamma_{j}(s)<s_{j}.

Assume towards a contradiction that there are
s\geq 0
and
i\in\mathcal{I}
with
s_{i}\geq\|s\|/2
, but
s_{j}<\varphi(\|s\|/2)
or
\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}
for all
j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(m)
; in both cases,
\Gamma_{j}(s)\geq s_{j}-\varphi(\|s\|/2)
holds. Hence, Lemma
5.1
implies
\Gamma_{i}^{m}(s)\geq s_{i}-\|s\|/4
. Then

\frac{\|s\|}{2}\leq s_{i}\leq\Gamma_{i}^{m}(s)+\frac{\|s\|}{4}\leq\|\Gamma^{m}(s)\|+\frac{\|s\|}{4}\leq\|s\|\gamma(m)+\frac{\|s\|}{4}<\frac{\|s\|}{2},

which is a contradiction. Now, towards another contradiction, assume that
s\leq r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma(s)
and
\|s\|>2\varphi^{-1}(r)
. Pick any
i\in\mathcal{I}
with
s_{i}\geq\|s\|/2
. Then, by the above, there exists
j\in\mathcal{N}^{-}_{i}(m)
with
s_{j}\geq\varphi(\|s\|/2)
and
\Gamma_{j}(s)<s_{j}
. The latter implies
s_{j}\leq r
. Altogether,
r<\varphi(\|s\|/2)\leq s_{j}\leq r
, which is a contradiction. Hence, we have shown that
s\leq r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma(s)
implies
\|s\|\leq 2\varphi^{-1}(r)
.
∎

5.2
Max-type operators

Recall that a gain operator
\Gamma
is called a
max-type operator
if all MAFs
\mu_{i}
are of the form
\mu_{i}(s)=\sup_{j\in\mathcal{I}}s_{j}=\|s\|
. In this case,
\Gamma
satisfies

\Gamma(s^{1}\oplus s^{2})=\Gamma(s^{1})\oplus\Gamma(s^{2})\mbox{\quad for all\ }s^{1},s^{2}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I}).

We also call a generalized gain operator a max-type gain operator if it has this property. Gain operators of this type are particularly well-behaved. Using our general results, it is easy to provide a full characterization of the existence of a path of strict decay.

Theorem 5.1
.

For a max-type gain operator
\Gamma
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(b)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
is UGAS.

If there exists
M>0
with
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|\leq M
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then another equivalent statement is the following:

(c)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies both uniform NJI conditions.

Proof.

Recall that the implications “(a)
\Rightarrow
(c)”, “(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)” and “(c)
\Rightarrow
(b)” also hold in the general case. It remains to prove that (b) implies (a). We first prove that UGAS of
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
implies that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property. Hence, let
s\leq b\oplus\Gamma_{\rho}(s)=\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,b}(s)
for some
b,s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
. This implies
s\leq\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,b}^{n}(s)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that
\Gamma_{\rho}
is also a max-type operator, which easily implies

\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,b}^{n}(s)\equiv\Gamma_{\rho}^{n}(s)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}\Gamma^{k}_{\rho}(b)\mbox{\quad for all\ }n\geq 0.

(8)

By assumption,
\Gamma_{\rho}^{n}(s)\rightarrow 0
. Hence,
s\leq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{k}_{\rho}(b)
, leading to

\|s\|\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\Gamma^{k}_{\rho}(b)\|\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\beta(\|b\|,k)=\beta(\|b\|,0)=:\varphi(\|b\|).

Thus,
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property. To obtain the desired path, we can now try to apply Theorem
3.2
, showing that the fixed point
\sigma^{*}(r)
of
\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}
is semi-attractive from above. We obtain even more. Putting
b=r\mathds{1}
in (
8
) implies that
\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}
only has one fixed point, namely
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{k}_{\rho}(b)
, which is globally attractive, since
\Gamma_{\rho}^{n}(s)
converges to zero.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The theorem improves
[
12
, Thm. VI.1]
, where uniform Lipschitz conditions on
\gamma_{ij}
and
\gamma_{ij}^{-1}
were required to obtain a path of strict decay. Moreover,
[
12
, Thm. VI.1]
only provided a sufficient condition and no relation to an NJI condition.

5.3
Homogeneous, subadditive operators

We call a generalized gain operator
\Gamma

homogeneous (of degree one)
if
\Gamma(\alpha s)=\alpha\Gamma(s)
for all
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
and
\alpha\geq 0
. We call
\Gamma

subadditive
if
\Gamma(s^{1}+s^{2})\leq\Gamma(s^{1})+\Gamma(s^{2})
for all
s^{1},s^{2}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
. In this subsection, we consider operators which are both homogeneous and subadditive. Examples are sum-type and max-type operators with linear interconnection gains. In the first case,
\Gamma
is the restriction of a linear operator. In the second case, we speak of a max-linear operator. Except for the equivalence to statement (d), the next theorem is known, see
[
16
, Prop. 8]
, but we give an alternative proof.

Theorem 5.2
.

For a homogeneous and subadditive gain operator
\Gamma
, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(b)

The system
\Sigma(\Gamma)
is UGAS.

(c)

The following “spectral radius” condition holds:

\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Gamma^{n}(\mathds{1})\|<1.

(9)

If there exists
M>0
with
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|\leq M
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then another equivalent statement is the following:

(d)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies both uniform NJI conditions.

Proof.

The implications “(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)”, “(d)
\Rightarrow
(b)” and “(a)
\Rightarrow
(d)” also hold in the general case. The equivalence “(b)
\Leftrightarrow
(c)” was shown in
[
16
, Prop. 8]
, and we do not repeat the proof here. It remains to (b) implies (a).

First, we refer to
[
16
, Prop. 9]
, which shows that (b) is equivalent to uniform global exponential stability (UGES), which is UGAS with a
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
-function of the form
\beta(r,n)=Ma^{n}r
, where
M>0
and
a\in(0,1)
. From the homogeneity of
\Gamma
, it follows that we can choose a linear
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\rho
(now regarded as a positive real number) such that
\Gamma_{\rho}=(1+\rho)\cdot\Gamma
is still UGES. In particular, this implies that (b) holds with
\Gamma_{\rho}
in place of
\Gamma
. We can apply Proposition
5.1
to show that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property. To this end, we have to show that
\Gamma
is uniformly continuous. First note that
0\leq s^{1}\leq s^{2}
implies

\Gamma(s^{2})-\Gamma(s^{1})=\Gamma(s^{1}+(s^{2}-s^{1}))-\Gamma(s^{1})\leq\Gamma(s^{2}-s^{1})

and thus, by continuity at
s=0
, for every
\varepsilon>0
we find
\delta>0
such that
\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|\leq\delta
implies

\|\Gamma(s^{2})-\Gamma(s^{1})\|\leq\|\Gamma(s^{2}-s^{1})\|=\|\Gamma(s^{2}-s^{1})-\Gamma(0)\|\leq\varepsilon.

For general
s^{1},s^{2}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
(not necessarily ordered), we have

\Gamma(s^{2})-\Gamma(s^{1})\leq\Gamma(s^{1}\oplus s^{2})-\Gamma(s^{1})\leq\Gamma(s^{1}\oplus s^{2}-s^{1})

which implies

\|\Gamma(s^{2})-\Gamma(s^{1})\|\leq\|\Gamma(s^{1}\oplus s^{2}-s^{1})-\Gamma(0)\|.

Observing that
\|s^{1}\oplus s^{2}-s^{1}\|\leq\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|
, this concludes the proof of uniform continuity. Hence,
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

We claim that for any
r\geq 0
, points
s^{1}\leq s^{2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\bar{\Gamma}^{n}_{r\mathds{1}}(s^{2})-\bar{\Gamma}^{n}_{r\mathds{1}}(s^{1})\leq\Gamma^{n}(s^{2}-s^{1}).

(10)

For
n=0
, this trivially holds. Assuming that it holds for a fixed
n
, with Lemma
A.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{n+1}_{r\mathds{1}}(s^{2})-\bar{\Gamma}^{n+1}_{r\mathds{1}}(s^{1})

\displaystyle=r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{2}))-r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{2}))-\Gamma(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{1}))\leq\Gamma(\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{2})-\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s^{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(\Gamma^{n}(s^{2}-s^{1}))=\Gamma^{n+1}(s^{2}-s^{1}).

Here, the second inequality follows from subadditivity of
\Gamma
and the last one from the induction hypothesis. The same estimates hold with
\Gamma_{\rho}
in place of
\Gamma
, in case that
\rho
is linear. Hence,

\|\bar{\Gamma}^{n}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}(s^{2})-\bar{\Gamma}^{n}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}(s^{1})\|\leq Ma^{n}\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|

for appropriate constants
M>0
and
a\in(0,1)
. This implies that
\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}
can only have one fixed point and every trajectory converges to this fixed point at exponential speed. Hence, Theorem
3.2
yields a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.
∎

We finally prove the following theorem, which is somewhat surprising, since it shows that the
\oplus
-MBI property is sufficient to obtain a path of strict decay for homogeneous subadditive operators.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a homogeneous and subadditive gain operator and assume that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the SU-NJI condition with a linear
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. Then there exists a linear path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\Gamma_{\rho}
is a subadditive and homogeneous gain operator if
\rho
is linear. Consider the inequality (
10
). Our assumption (which is equivalent to the
\oplus
-MBI property), implies that
\Gamma_{\rho}^{n}(s^{2}-s^{1})
converges to the origin componentwise, enforcing the uniqueness of the fixed point of
\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}
. We consider the path
\sigma_{*}
, which maps each
r
to the unique fixed point of
\bar{\Gamma}_{\rho,r\mathds{1}}
. Then, we have the identity

\sigma_{*}(r)\equiv r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma_{\rho}(\sigma_{*}(r)).

Multiplying both sides by a nonnegative scalar
\alpha
yields

\alpha\sigma_{*}(r)\equiv(\alpha r)\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma_{\rho}(\alpha\sigma_{*}(r))

due to homogeneity. By uniqueness of the fixed point, this implies
\sigma_{*}(\alpha r)=\alpha\sigma_{*}(r)
. We thus have
\sigma_{*}(r)\equiv r\sigma_{*}(1)
, showing that
\sigma_{*}
is linear, and hence continuous. Obviously,
\sigma_{*}
also satisfies the other three properties of a path of strict decay (see the proof of Proposition
2.5
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We note that several further characterizations of the equivalent conditions in Theorem
5.2
are available, especially for linear operators, see
[
9
]
. This includes, in particular, the spectral radius condition
r(\Gamma)<1
, which is an immediate consequence of (
9
) and Gelfand’s formula.

5.4
Finite-dimensional operators

In this subsection, we study the finite case, where
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
can be identified with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N:=|\mathcal{I}|
, and
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
with
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:s_{i}\geq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq N\}
. In particular, we clarify how our results are related to previous results from the literature.

First, let us clarify which assumptions we have to impose on the functions
\gamma_{ij}
and
\mu_{i}
to obtain a gain operator in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V(\mathcal{G}),E(\mathcal{G}))
be a finite directed graph without self-loops (i.e. edges from a vertex to itself) and put
\mathcal{I}:=V(\mathcal{G})
. Consider families
\{\gamma_{ij}:ji\in E(\mathcal{G})\}
and
\{\mu_{i}:1\leq i\leq|\mathcal{I}|\}
with
\gamma_{ij}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and
\mu_{i}:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying the following properties for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
:

(i)

\mu_{i}(0)=0
and
\mu_{i}(s)>0
if
s>0
.

(ii)

0\leq s^{1}\leq s^{2}
implies
\mu_{i}(s^{1})\leq\mu_{i}(s^{2})
.

(iii)

\|s\|\rightarrow\infty
implies
\mu_{i}(s)\rightarrow\infty
.

(iv)

\mu_{i}
is continuous.

Then the operator
\Gamma:\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
, defined by
\Gamma_{i}(s):=\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, is a gain operator.

Proof.

Since
\{\gamma_{ij}\}
is a finite family of functions, it is pointwise equicontinuous. It remains to verify (M1)–(M4) for
\{\mu_{i}\}
. This, in turn, reduces to showing that
\mu_{i}(s)\geq\xi(\|s\|)
for some
\xi\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
, since the rest trivially follows from our assumptions. To this end, we define

\tilde{\xi}(r):=\min_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}\mu_{i}(re^{j}).

From assumption (i), we obtain
\tilde{\xi}(0)=0
. Clearly,
\tilde{\xi}(r)\geq 0
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. From assumption (iv), it follows, moreover, that
\tilde{\xi}
is continuous. If
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
, then
r_{1}e^{j}\leq r_{2}e^{j}
for each
j
, and hence assumption (ii) implies
\tilde{\xi}(r_{1})\leq\tilde{\xi}(r_{2})
, showing that
\tilde{\xi}
is non-decreasing. If
r\rightarrow\infty
, then
\|re^{j}\|\rightarrow\infty
, and hence, following from assumption (iii),
\tilde{\xi}(r)\rightarrow\infty
. Finally, assumption (i) implies
\tilde{\xi}(r)>0
if
r>0
. By Lemma
A.6
, we can then bound
\tilde{\xi}
from below by a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\xi
. To show that
\mu_{i}(s)\geq\xi(\|s\|)
, pick
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
and choose an index
j
such that
s_{j}=\|s\|
. Then
s\geq s_{j}e^{j}=\|s\|e^{j}
, implying
\mu_{i}(s)\geq\mu_{i}(\|s\|e^{j})\geq\tilde{\xi}(\|s\|)\geq\xi(\|s\|)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The assumptions of the proposition can also be found in the literature on finite-dimensional gain operators.

For our general result, we need an additional
uniform continuity
assumption for the MAFs that we have not used before. We can formulate this assumption in terms of a comparison function as follows: There exists a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\gamma
such that
0\leq s^{1}\leq s^{2}
implies

\mu_{i}(s^{2})-\mu_{i}(s^{1})\leq\gamma(\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|)\mbox{\quad for all\ }i\in\mathcal{I}.

(11)

Observe that this is indeed equivalent to the uniform continuity of each
\mu_{i}
. First, since there are only finitely many MAFs, the same
\gamma
can be used for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
. Second, if
s^{1}
and
s^{2}
are arbitrary elements of
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
(not necessarily ordered), it follows from the assumption that

\mu_{i}(s^{2})-\mu_{i}(s^{1})\leq\mu_{i}(s^{1}\oplus s^{2})-\mu_{i}(s^{1})\leq\gamma(\|s^{1}\oplus s^{2}-s^{1}\|)\leq\gamma(\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|)

and analogously for
\mu_{i}(s^{1})-\mu_{i}(s^{2})
. Hence,
|\mu_{i}(s^{2})-\mu_{i}(s^{1})|\leq\gamma(\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|)
, which is the characterization of uniform continuity in terms of comparison functions.

In the literature on finite-dimensional small-gain theory (see
[
7
,
8
,
19
]
), instead the assumption of
subadditivity
is used, which is a special case of the above. To see this, note that a MAF
\mu
is subadditive if
\mu(s^{1}+s^{2})\leq\mu(s^{1})+\mu(s^{2})
for all
s^{1},s^{2}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
. If
0\leq s^{1}\leq s^{2}
, this implies

\mu(s^{2})-\mu(s^{1})=\mu(s^{1}+(s^{2}-s^{1}))-\mu(s^{1})\leq\mu(s^{2}-s^{1})\leq\mu(\|s^{2}-s^{1}\|\mathds{1})

and we can define
\gamma(r):=\mu(r\mathds{1})
, which can be bounded from above by a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
|\mathcal{I}|<\infty
and consider a gain operator with uniformly continuous MAFs. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a path of strict decay for
\Gamma
.

(b)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
is UGAS.

(c)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property.

(d)

There exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}
satisfies the NJI condition.

Proof.

Looking at our general results in Section
4
and observing that (b) trivially implies (d), we can easily see that the proof is completed by showing that (d) implies (c). To this end, consider
s\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
and
r\geq 0
with
s\leq r\mathds{1}\oplus\Gamma(s)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
s>0
. We split the index set
\mathcal{I}
into the two subsets

\mathcal{I}^{1}:=\left\{i\in\mathcal{I}:s_{i}\leq r\right\},\quad\mathcal{I}^{2}:=\mathcal{I}\backslash\mathcal{I}^{1}.

By assumption, there exists at least one index
i
with
\Gamma_{i}(s)<s_{i}
, which implies
s_{i}\leq r
. Hence,
\mathcal{I}^{1}
is nonempty. We define the
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function

\varphi_{1}:=(\rho\circ\xi\circ\eta)^{-1}\circ\gamma\circ\left(\max_{ji\in E(\mathcal{G})}\gamma_{ij}\right),

where
\rho
comes from (a),
\xi
comes from (M1),
\eta\leq\gamma_{ij}
for all
ji\in E(\mathcal{G})
with
\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
(such
\eta
trivially exists), and
\gamma
comes from (
11
). Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\varphi_{1}(r)>r\mbox{\quad and \quad}(\rho\circ\xi\circ\eta)^{-1}(r)>r\mbox{\quad for all\ }r>0.

(12)

Let us assume to the contrary that
s_{|\mathcal{I}^{2}}\geq\varphi_{1}(r)\mathds{1}
. We want to show that this implies

(\mathrm{id}+\rho)\circ\Gamma_{i}(s_{|\mathcal{I}^{2}})\geq\Gamma_{i}(s)\mbox{\quad for all\ }i\in\mathcal{I}^{2},

which in turn implies
\Gamma_{\rho}(s_{|\mathcal{I}^{2}})\geq s_{|\mathcal{I}^{2}}
in contradiction to (a). We fix
i\in\mathcal{I}^{2}
and define

a_{i}:=[\gamma_{ij}(s_{j})]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}^{2}}.

We can assume without loss of generality that
a_{i}\neq 0
. Indeed,
a_{i}=0
is only possible if
\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}^{2}=\emptyset
, implying

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{r,\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(r)]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}})\}\leq\max\left\{r,\gamma\circ\left(\max_{ji\in E(\mathcal{G})}\gamma_{ij}\right)(r)\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_finite_general_wlogs}}}{{<}}\varphi_{1}(r)

in contradiction to our assumption hat
s_{|\mathcal{I}^{2}}\geq\varphi_{1}(r)\mathds{1}
. The inequality to be shown can then be written as

\rho(\mu_{i}(a_{i}))\geq\mu_{i}([\gamma_{ij}(r)]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}^{1}}+a_{i})-\mu_{i}(a_{i}).

Using (
11
), we see that a sufficient condition for this inequality to hold is

\rho(\mu_{i}(a_{i}))\geq\gamma(\|[\gamma_{ij}(r)]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}^{1}}\|).

This last inequality follows from the definition of
\varphi_{1}
, since

\displaystyle\rho(\mu_{i}(a_{i}))

\displaystyle\geq\rho(\xi(\|a_{i}\|))\geq\rho\circ\xi\circ\eta\circ\varphi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\gamma\circ\left(\max_{ji\in E(\mathcal{G})}\gamma_{ij}\right)(r)\geq\gamma(\|[\gamma_{ij}(r)]_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}^{1}}\|).

Hence, there exists at least one index
i\in\mathcal{I}^{2}
such that
s_{i}<\varphi_{1}(r)
. We can split the index set
\mathcal{I}
again into

\mathcal{I}^{1}:=\left\{i\in\mathcal{I}:s_{i}\leq\max\{r,\varphi_{1}(r)\}\right\},\quad\mathcal{I}^{2}:=\mathcal{I}\backslash\mathcal{I}^{1},

observing that the new
\mathcal{I}^{1}
contains at least one more element than the old one. With the same arguments as before, we can define another
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\varphi_{2}
such that
s_{i}<\varphi_{2}(r)
must hold for at least one
i\in\mathcal{I}^{2}
. In this way, we can continue inductively until
\mathcal{I}^{2}=\emptyset
(using that there are only finitely many indices). We thus find a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\varphi
such that
\|s\|\leq\varphi(r)
. Note that we have proved that
\Gamma
satisfies the
\oplus
-MBI property (and not
\Gamma_{\rho}
). By choosing a uniformly continuous
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-function
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
, the proof also works for
\Gamma_{\rho^{\prime}}
in place of
\Gamma
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

In the literature, we find a very similar result, see
[
17
, Thm. C.41]
. However, here the
\oplus
-MBI property does not appear, but instead the stronger MBI property (see Remark
3.4
). The proof of this result uses different methods, however, and in particular uses the subadditivity of MAFs for the path construction.

Remark 5.5
.

Due to Theorem
3.1
, we know that the statements in Theorem
5.4
are also equivalent to the existence of a path satisfying properties (i), (ii) and (iv) of a
C^{0}
-path of strict decay, but not necessarily being monotone.

6
Some open questions

The main results of the paper lead to the following open questions and topics for future research:

1.

The ultimate goal, to obtain a complete characterization of the existence of a path of strict decay in terms of no-joint-increase conditions, was only obtained in special cases. One major step towards this goal would be to clarify when the weak
∗
-convergence of
\bar{\Gamma}_{r\mathds{1}}^{n}(s)
to
\sigma^{*}(r)
for
s\geq\sigma^{*}(r)
can be strengthened to norm-convergence. In particular, it must be understood if this follows from UGAS of
\Sigma(\Gamma_{\rho})
or if stronger conditions are necessary.

2.

The relation between UGAS and the
\oplus
-MBI property should be further clarified. A related problem is the unification of the two uniform NJI conditions. From Theorem
5.4
, we can deduce that in the classical finite case with uniformly continuous MAFs, they are equivalent. From Proposition
2.7
, we further know that in the general finite case SU-NJI implies TU-NJI. A complete characterization of UGAS in terms of NJI conditions is also missing; in particular, it is unclear if UGAS implies the cofinality of the decay set.

3.

A further topic for future research concerns the question of
scalable ISS

[
3
]
. Here, we ask under which conditions stability measures are preserved when a network is enlarged. If we can cover a large-but-finite network that may grow in time with one infinite network which is ISS, we should be able to say that stability measures are preserved when further subsystems are added. The stability measures are related to the
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
-functions appearing in the definition of a path of strict decay, so it is important to take a closer look at how these functions are related to the fundamental components of the network. A first step in this direction is Corollary
3.2
.

4.

Besides answering the open questions within the setup of this paper, one can ask if this setup can be generalized. For instance, one can ask if it makes sense to build a similar theory on
\ell^{p}
-spaces for finite
p
, if one can relax the assumption that the sets
\mathcal{I}_{i}
all have to be finite, or even if the countability of the index set
\mathcal{I}
can be dropped.

Appendix A
Some technical lemmas

The following three lemmas describe properties of the weak
∗
-topology of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
.

Lemma A.1
.

A sequence
(s^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
converges in the weak
∗
-topology if and only if it is norm-bounded and componentwise convergent.

Proof.

As a general fact, the sequence
(s^{n})
converges to some
s\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
if and only if
s^{n}(t)
converges to
s(t)
for every
t\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I})
. By (
1
), this means

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}s^{n}_{i}t_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}s_{i}t_{i}\mbox{\quad for all\ }t\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I}).

If we let
t
range through the standard basis of
\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I})
, consisting of the unit vectors
e^{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, this implies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}s^{n}_{i}=s_{i}\mbox{\quad for all\ }i\in\mathcal{I}.

Hence, we have shown that weak
∗
convergence implies componentwise convergence. Now, we show that
(s^{n})
is norm-bounded. By
[
14
, Cor. 2.6.8]
, a set
A\subset\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
is norm-bounded if and only if the set
\{s(t):s\in A\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
for each
t\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I})
. Applying this to
A:=\{s^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
shows norm-boundedness, since
s^{n}(t)
is convergent (and thus bounded) for each
t
by assumption.

Conversely, let
(s^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
which is norm-bounded, say
\|s^{n}\|\leq B
for all
n
, and converges to some
s\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
componentwise. Pick
t\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Obviously, we can assume that
t\neq 0
. Choose a subset
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{I}\backslash\mathcal{J}
is finite and

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}}|t_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4B}.

The existence of such
\mathcal{J}
immediately follows from
t\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{I})
. Now, we can choose
N
large enough so that
|s^{n}_{i}-s_{i}|<\varepsilon/(2\|t\|_{1})
for
i\in\mathcal{I}\backslash\mathcal{J}
and
n\geq N
. The choice of
\mathcal{J}
and
N
then implies that for all
n\geq N

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}(s^{n}_{i}-s_{i})t_{i}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|s^{n}_{i}-s_{i}|\cdot|t_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}\backslash\mathcal{J}}|s^{n}_{i}-s_{i}|\cdot|t_{i}|+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}}|s^{n}_{i}-s_{i}|\cdot|t_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|t\|_{1}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|t_{i}|+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}}(|s^{n}_{i}|+|s_{i}|)|t_{i}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

This shows that
(s^{n})
converges to
s
in the weak
∗
-topology.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Every norm-bounded sequence
(s^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
has a subsequence which is componentwise convergent.

Proof.

By the theorem of Banach-Alaoglu and the assumption of norm-boundedness,
(s^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is contained in a weakly
∗
compact subset of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
. By
[
14
, Cor. 2.6.20]
, this subset is metrizable. Hence, its compactness is equivalent to its sequential compactness. The rest follows from Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

In
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
, every order interval of the form
[a,b]
with
a\leq b
is weakly
∗
sequentially compact.

Proof.

Let
(s^{n})
be a sequence in
[a,b]
. Then clearly,
(s^{n})
is norm-bounded. By Lemma
A.2
, there exists a subsequence
(s^{n_{m}})
which is componentwise convergent. Let
s
denote its limit. The inequalities
a_{i}\leq s^{n_{m}}_{i}\leq b_{i}
which hold for all
i
and
m
, clearly carry over to the limit when
m
goes to infinity, so
s\in[a,b]
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

a\geq c\geq 0

b\geq d\geq 0
for vectors
a,b,c,d\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})

a\oplus b-c\oplus d\leq(a-c)\oplus(b-d).

Lemma A.6
.

[
17
, Prop. A.16]

Let
z:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be non-decreasing, continuous at zero and satisfy
z(0)=0
,
z(r)>0
for all
r>0
. Then there exist
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{K}

, such that
z_{1}(r)\leq z(r)\leq z_{2}(r)

. If

as

, then

.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
T:\ell^{\infty}_{+}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{+}
be a monotone operator with
T(0)=0
. If the system
\Sigma(T)
is UGS and GATT, then it is UGAS.

Proof.

Fix
r,\varepsilon>0
and choose
n
such that
\|T^{k}(r\mathds{1})\|\leq\varepsilon
for all
k\geq n
. Then
\|s\|\leq r
implies
s\leq r\mathds{1}
, and thus
T^{k}(s)\leq T^{k}(r\mathds{1})\leq\varepsilon\mathds{1}
for all
k\geq n
. Hence,
\Sigma(T)
is UGATT. By
[
18
, Thm. 4.2]
, UGS and UGATT together imply UGAS.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

Let
T
be a monotone operator on
\ell^{\infty}_{+}(\mathcal{I})
. If
s\in\Psi(T)
for some

, then
[T(s),s]\subset\Psi(T)
.

Proof.

If
T(s)\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
, then monotonicity implies
T(s^{\prime})\leq T(s)\leq s^{\prime}
.
∎

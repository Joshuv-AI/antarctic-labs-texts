Abstract
How can the complexity of ML-enabled systems be managed effectively? The goal of this research is to investigate how complexity affects ML-Enabled Systems (MLES). To address this question, this research aims to introduce a metrics-based architectural model to characterize the complexity of MLES. The goal is to support architectural decisions, providing a guideline for the inception and growth of these systems. This paper showcases the first step for creating the metrics-based architectural model: an extension of a reference architecture that can describe MLES to collect their metrics.

RQ1:

What are the measurable dimensions of complexity in

[

However, MLES also have their data and model dimensions

Finding metrics that measure the data- and model-related complexity

RQ2:

How can complexity metrics be operationalized over
the architecture of MLES?

Section

III

in
section

III

Metrics may be related to different abstraction levels of a system:
from code snippets, to components, to services
[
18

Moreover, they can have varying levels of different quality attributes

20

21

ease of implementation, and independence of other metrics.
As a consequence, only some metrics found in
section

III

provide similar information, it will be preferable to use the best
according to the quality attributes.
Therefore, the challenge for answering
section

dataset of representative MLES to collect the metrics, and then choosing which
subset of metrics to collect.

Measuring a metric may require different levels of access to the system

representations of the system (e.g., creating a graph describing its data
flow). As a consequence, only some metrics found in
section

III

practical to collect. In particular, if two metrics provide similar
information, it may be preferable to use the simpler.

Expected Results and Threats to Validity

This section highlights the main expected result (ER) of each phase from

Figure

1
. Then, it presents threats to validity,
proposing solutions on how to address them.

ER1:

State of the art about complexity metrics for MLES.

Section

IV-A
is mostly subject to
internal
and
conclusion

threats to validity.
Section

IV-A
leads to an internal threat because
the SLR depends on the data provided by knowledge bases.
Section

IV-A

leads to a conclusion threat because it produces an ontology – whose
design is a subjective process by itself – based on the data gathered
on the previous step.

To contain the internal threat, this research intends to produce an SLR
based on the most established publication databases in Computer Science.
To contain the conclusion threat, it will follow well-established guidelines
available in the literature,
both for the SLR
[
22
]
and for
the ontology
[
25
]
. By documenting the process
thoroughly, the results from
section

IV-A
should be reliable for the
next phases.

ER2:

An academic- and industry-based case study on complexity metrics for
MLES.

Section

IV-B
is mostly subject to
external
and
conclusion

threats to validity. Both
sections

IV-B
and
IV-B
are case studies that
will incrementally result in the
metrics-oriented architectural model
.
They will be based on the analysis of existing MLES: the SPIRA system for

section

IV-B
, and production systems from industry partners for

section

IV-B
. The choice of systems included in the case studies
leads to the external threat, whereas the selection of metrics that

To contain the external threat, this research will define inclusion and

As it was considered for SPIRA, any production system from an industry

reference architecture explored in
Figure

To contain the conclusion threat,
section

IV-B
was designed to have
first an exploratory and then a confirmatory case study.

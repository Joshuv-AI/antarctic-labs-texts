Satisfaction level of dental graduates with the quality of clinical training in the undergraduate dental curriculum of Guilan Dental School
Authors: Yasamin Babaee Hemmati, Mohammad Pourghorbani, Sana Alavi Niakoo, Mehran Falahchai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Dentistry is directly correlated with public health. Educational programming according to the correct principles of clinical training is a challenging task. Thus, clinical training quality assessment is of utmost importance. This study aimed to assess the satisfaction level of dental graduates with the quality of clinical training offered in the undergraduate dental curriculum of Guilan Dental School.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This analytical cross-sectional study was conducted on 192 dental graduates of Guilan Dental School from 2018 to 2023 with the same educational curriculum. An online questionnaire was used for data collection by sending its link to the graduates; of which, 158 (86 females, 72 males) were enrolled. Data were analyzed by the independent t-test, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, and one-way ANOVA (α = 0.05).RESULTS:The mean total satisfaction score with the received clinical training was 47.7 ± 16.9 (moderate). The respondents were the most satisfied with the restorative dentistry (63.8 ± 27.7), and the least satisfied with the orthodontics (20.3 ± 12.9) departments. The total satisfaction score was significantly associated with age (r = 0.198, P = 0.012), work experience (r = 0.218, P = 0.006), and time passed since graduation (r = -0.230, P = 0.004). The mean total score was significantly higher among those who had their private practice compared with others working in private or public clinics, etc. (P = 0.002).CONCLUSIONS:Evaluating dental graduates’ satisfaction level with the quality of clinical training can serve as an effective tool to identify the strengths and weaknesses of dental curricula and make the necessary reforms.

Introduction

A large part of dental education and training is allocated to increasing the level of clinical competence of dental students in designing and implementing proper treatment plans for patients because the ultimate goal is to achieve expertise and proficiency in the clinical setting.[123] Medical professions, in general, have a high level of sensitivity because they directly deal with people’s lives and public health.[45] This statement highlights the significance of empowerment of medical professionals and meeting their satisfaction. Thus, any parameter that can empower medical professionals and increase their satisfaction level is particularly important.[4]

Satisfaction with the quality of education and training is a multifactorial concept, indicating optimal efficacy of all educational domains theoretically and clinically. Several personal, social, financial, cultural, and environmental factors can affect the satisfaction level of students and graduates.[6] Because students are the main output of universities,[78] student satisfaction is currently one of the most important goals of educational institutions, because it makes the universities more competitive.[9] In such a competitive environment, only universities with sufficiently high educational quality can have optimal performance and provide a suitable environment and equipment for education and training.[710] Assessment of the perspective of graduates towards the educational programs is highly valuable since they are less emotionally attached to the university, and have started their practice where they can decide whether the knowledge and skills they acquired during their education would meet their occupational needs and responsibilities or not.[11]

Some previous studies assessed the satisfaction level of dental graduates worldwide with the quality of clinical training they received, yielding controversial results.[12121314151617] This controversy is expected considering the variations in the implementation and design of educational curricula. Studies regarding the satisfaction level of dental graduates with the quality of their clinical training in Iran are scarce. In the only available study in this respect conducted in Iran by Houshmand et al.,[18] in 2021 on dental graduates participating in the residency examination, their satisfaction level was shown to be higher than average in most clinical skills. However, this study has some shortcomings. They assessed the opinion of the participants of the residency examination, and there was a high possibility that their study participants did not have a noteworthy clinical practice history out of the university setting. Thus, their results cannot be generalized to all dental graduates. Furthermore, not all the graduates had the same educational curriculum.[18]

The coronavirus disease-2019 (COVID-19) pandemic in recent years and its significant impact on dental education is another important point that received less attention in similar previous studies.[19] Thus, the present study appears to be the first in Iran to evaluate the satisfaction level of dental graduates under the same educational curriculum. Moreover, unlike previous studies, it investigated the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on clinical skills training, providing insights into how remote learning and reduced patient access can influence the clinical competency of the graduates.

The purpose of the present study was to assess the satisfaction level of dental graduates of the Dental School of Guilan University of Medical Sciences with the quality of their clinical training. The null hypothesis of the study was that the total satisfaction level of dental graduates and their satisfaction level with each clinical skill acquired during undergraduate dental education would have no significant association with their individual or academic characteristics.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This analytical cross-sectional study was conducted at Guilan University of Medical Sciences.

Study participants and sampling

All graduates of Guilan Dental School from 2018 to 2023 who were willing to participate were enrolled. The sample size was calculated according to a general rule. The sample size was calculated based on the change in total clinical skills of dental graduates. The minimum sample size was calculated to be 148 using G*Power software version 3.1.9.2 assuming an effect size of 0.299, an alpha error of 0.05, and a power of 0.95.[15] Sampling was performed using the stratified method. Accordingly, graduation year was considered as a stratum, and a list of graduates was prepared for each stratum. Next, a table of random numbers was used, and considering the share of each stratum in the total sample, 158 individuals were selected, and the online questionnaire was sent to them. Ten individuals from the selected sample did not respond to the questionnaire. Thus, substitutes were randomly selected using the random number table to replace them.

Because this study aimed to assess the satisfaction level of the graduates of the same educational curriculum, the questionnaire was only sent to those admitted to Guilan University from 2012 to 2017, and those studied under the previous curriculum were not enrolled. The graduates who attended the residency examination straight (with no gap between their graduation and residency examination) and passed the examination were excluded.[12131418]

Data collection tool and technique

The questionnaire was designed by Houshmand et al.[18] was used for data collection, which has a content validity ratio of 0.8, content validity index of 0.8, and optimal reliability with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.92. The first part of the questionnaire asked for demographic information such as age, gender, graduation year, GPA, work experience, practice setting, and presence/absence of the COVID-19 pandemic during the 4 years of academic clinical education (after passing the basic science examination), and had questions about the eligibility criteria. The second part of the questionnaire included 32 questions regarding the level of satisfaction of the respondents with the basic science and skills acquired in oral medicine, oral and maxillofacial surgery, periodontics, restorative dentistry, endodontics, pediatric dentistry, orthodontics, fixed and removable prosthodontics departments [Figure 1]. The questions were scored using a 4-point Likert scale (weak = 1, moderate = 2, good = 3, and very good = 4). The total score could range from 0 to 100. After obtaining the contact information and email addresses of the graduates, and uploading the questionnaire to the www.survey.porsline.ir platform, the link to the questionnaire was sent to the participants. The questionnaire did not ask for the names or other personal information of the participants, and all information was analyzed as a whole. Also, the participants were briefed about the study objectives before answering the questions.

Conceptual framework of the study

Statistics

The Shapiro-Wilk test was applied to assess the normality of total satisfaction score data distribution. The independent t-test, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, and one-way ANOVA were applied to analyze the correlation of total satisfaction score with clinical training and individual-academic characteristics of the participants. Correlation coefficients between 0.1 and 0.3 indicated a poor correlation, values between 0.3 and 0.5 indicated a moderate correlation, and values > 0.5 indicated a strong correlation. The mean satisfaction score with each clinical skill was compared based on the individual academic characteristics of the participants using an independent t-test. Data were analyzed by SPSS version 26 (IBM SPSS Statistics, v26; IBM Corp, NY, USA) at a 0.05 level of significance.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Guilan University of Medical Sciences (IR.GUMS.REC.1402.456).

Association of individual-academic characteristics and satisfaction level

Table 3 presents the association between individual academic characteristics and total satisfaction scores. The results showed a significant positive mild correlation between age and total satisfaction score (r = 0.198, P = 0.012). However, the total satisfaction score of the graduates increased with age. The same correlation was found between graduation time and total satisfaction score, such that as time passed since graduation, the total satisfaction score significantly increased (r = -0.230, P = 0.004). Moreover, with an increase in work experience, the total satisfaction score of the graduates significantly increased (r = 0.218, P = 0.006). The mean total satisfaction score of the participants who had their private practice was significantly higher than that of graduates working in other settings (public or private clinics, etc.,) (P = 0.002). The mean total score of the graduates during the COVID-19 pandemic was lower than that of other graduates; however, this difference was not significant (P = 0.058). Other individual academic characteristics of the graduates had no significant correlation with their total satisfaction score (P = 0.883 for gender, P = 0.602 for GPA). Discussion

According to the present results, the null hypothesis of the study was partially rejected. As stated earlier, this study aimed to assess the satisfaction level of dental graduates with the quality of clinical training they received during their undergraduate dental education. One strength of the present study was that it assessed the dental graduates of the same curriculum, unlike relevant previous studies.[121418] Because the educational curricula are reformed every couple of years in terms of theoretical content and clinical skills,[46] a comparison of the graduates of the old curriculum with the reformed curriculum would lead to an erroneous conclusion. Houshmand et al.[18] evaluated the opinion of the graduates participating in the residency examination. There is a high possibility that participants of their study had little or no clinical practice outside the university setting; thus, their results may not be generalizable to all dental graduates. In most previous studies conducted in other countries, either a wide range of graduation years was examined, or the uniformity of the curriculum was not addressed. Therefore, graduates who attended the residency examination straight (with no gap between their graduation and residency examination) and passed the examination were excluded from the present study. Moreover, the present study assessed the effect of the presence of the COVID-19 pandemic (which had a significant impact on dental education)[19] on the satisfaction level of the graduates with the quality of clinical training, which was not addressed in previous studies.

The present results revealed that the total satisfaction score was 47.7 ± 16.9, indicating the average level of satisfaction. The satisfaction level in the study by Houshmand et al.[18] was above the average in most skills. The majority of the participants in a study by Van Dam et al.,[14] in the Netherlands and Reyes Alardo et al.[17] in the Dominican Republic showed a high level of satisfaction. In other studies conducted in Croatia[15] and Saudi Arabia,[12] the graduates were relatively dissatisfied. Variations in study design and clinical training conditions such as adequacy of the equipment, undergraduate educational curriculum, and educational methods may explain the differences in the total satisfaction level of the graduates (as a multifactorial parameter).

According to the present results regarding the satisfaction level with different departments, the highest mean satisfaction score was related to the restorative dentistry department, followed by oral and maxillofacial surgery, and oral radiology, and the lowest was related to orthodontics, prosthodontics, and endodontics departments. The majority of these findings are consistent with previous studies.[151718] High satisfaction with oral and maxillofacial surgery and restorative dentistry, and low satisfaction with fixed and removable prosthodontics and orthodontics were also observed in the study by Vrdoljak et al.,[15] in Croatia. In the study by Houshmand et al.,[18] the highest satisfaction level was related to the pediatric dentistry department followed by restorative dentistry and then oral radiology. The lowest satisfaction was related to endodontics followed by the orthodontics department. The lowest satisfaction level with the orthodontics department observed in the present study and some previous studies[121518] may be because of insufficient clinical practice on patients as a result of a long process of orthodontic treatment and a small number of admitted patients to this department as well as passive participation of students in the patient management process in the orthodontics department.[15] Several studies recently conducted on senior dental students and fresh graduates revealed their incompetence in meeting patients’ orthodontic treatment needs, highlighting the existing gaps in the undergraduate dental educational curriculum.[12021] The low satisfaction rate with the prosthodontics department can be because of the small number of patients admitted to this department, and the long and complex treatment process, requiring teamwork involving a dentist, technician, and department staff, which increases the risk of errors.[15]

Assessment of the satisfaction level with different clinical skills revealed the highest mean satisfaction with anesthetic injection and pain control, intraoral radiography, and maxillary dental arch and subgingival scaling. Also, the lowest mean satisfaction belonged to designing a functional appliance, designing a removable orthodontic appliance, and treatment of open-apex teeth. In the study by Alshihri et al.,[12] high satisfaction with conventional periodontal treatments such as scaling was observed. However, unlike the present study, the satisfaction levels with anesthetic skills and radiography were low. In a study by Alsoghier et al.,[2] graduates had the highest competency in taking a medical history, radiography, caries control, and composite restorations, and the lowest competency in endodontic treatment of multi-rooted teeth, orthodontic treatment planning, and drug prescription. Similar to the present study, in previous studies conducted in Croatia[15] and Pakistan,[1] dentists had the highest satisfaction with the anesthetic injection skill. Repeated practice of the graduates in anesthetic injection on numerous patients in different departments (surgery, endodontics, pediatric dentistry, and restorative dentistry) during undergraduate dental education appears to have a positive impact on their satisfaction with this acquired skill. High satisfaction levels with radiography and scaling can be due to their lower level of complexity, and practice on a high number of patients during undergraduate education. The low satisfaction rate with skills related to orthodontics, fixed and removable prosthodontics, and complex endodontic treatments was consistent with other studies.[21215]

The present study found no significant association between the gender of the graduates and their total satisfaction (P = 0.883). Nonetheless, the satisfaction level of male graduates was significantly higher than female graduates with surgical removal of impacted teeth (P = 0.014), flap surgery (P = 0.015), root canal preparation (P = 0.044), fabrication of temporary restoration (P = 0.049), recording of intermaxillary relationship (P = 0.013), and fabrication of a removable complete denture (P = 0.042); whereas, the satisfaction level of female graduates was significantly higher than male graduates with the diagnosis and treatment of xerostomia (P = 0.022), interpretation of landmarks and anatomical variations (P = 0.022), fissure sealant/fluoride therapy (P < 0.001), restoration of primary teeth (P < 0.001), and pulpotomy/pulpectomy (P = 0.008). Different studies reported variable results in this respect[2122223]; however, it should be noted that the scores and judgments are based on personal beliefs, opinions, and interests, which can cause some limitations. For instance, female graduates may be more interested in some specific fields (such as community dentistry, radiology, and pediatric dentistry).[23] The same applies to males. For example, males may be more interested in more complex treatments such as prosthodontic and endodontic procedures and oral and maxillofacial surgery, which can cause different expectations and subsequently different satisfaction levels with the respective curriculum.[23] In studies conducted in the Netherlands,[14] Croatia,[15] and Saudi Arabia,[2] it was reported that gender did not affect the overall satisfaction of graduates. However, Alsoghier et al.[2] reported that men showed more confidence in the endodontic treatment of multi-rooted teeth. In the study by Alshihri et al.,[12] in Saudi Arabia, men reported lower satisfaction compared to women in various skills, including space maintainers and partial removable prosthodontics. In a study conducted in Pakistan, women demonstrated greater preparedness in certain clinical skills compared to men.[1] In the study by Montero et al.,[24] no significant difference was observed between male and female dental students regarding clinical competency in prosthodontics.

According to the present results, no significant correlation was found between the total satisfaction score and GPA of the graduates (P = 0.602); however, a positive correlation was found between the GPA and some clinical skills including differential diagnosis of oral soft tissue lesions (P = 0.005), root canal preparation (P = 0.008), using rotary files (P = 0.036), treatment of open-apex teeth (P = 0.043), and general treatment planning (P = 0.018). Previous studies on dental graduates did not consider the GPA variable. However, studies examining the relationship between academic performance and clinical skills in dental students have shown conflicting results.[252627]

In the present study, the total satisfaction score and satisfaction with some clinical skills were significantly higher in older graduates (P = 0.012, r = 0.198) and graduates with > 2 years of work experience compared with those with ≤ 2 years of work experience (P = 0.006). A correlation was also found between graduation time and total satisfaction score such that as time passed since graduation, the total satisfaction score significantly increased (r = -0.230, P = 0.004).

Alshihri et al.[12] reported higher satisfaction levels of dentists with higher clinical experience with some skills compared with fresh graduates. Such results can be due to several factors. Dentists with higher work experience may have difficulty recalling their level of competency as novice dentists and might have responded according to their current status. Some other possible reasons include the absence of the COVID-19 pandemic and higher patient load, no virtual education, and smaller number of admitted students, and a higher availability of equipment at the time. Nonetheless, a definite conclusion cannot be made regarding the influential factors in this respect, and qualitative studies are recommended to better address this topic. However, studies conducted in Croatia[15] and Saudi Arabia[2] reported that age is not a significant factor influencing the satisfaction level of dental graduates. Also, in the study by van Dam et al.,[14] no significant relationship was found between the graduation year and satisfaction level.

The mean total satisfaction score of the graduates who had their private practice was significantly higher than that of graduates working in other settings (private or public clinics, etc.) (P = 0.002). This result was consistent with the findings of Reyes Alardo et al.[17] However, Van Dam et al.[14] reported different results and showed that dentists who had their own private offices were less satisfied. This difference can be due to the different conditions of dentists after graduation in different countries. For instance, passing 2 years of clinical work in certain deprived areas is mandatory after graduation and before initiation of private practice in Iran while in the Netherlands, graduates can start their private practice immediately after graduation.[1428] Also, due to the economic differences between Iran and the Netherlands, having a private office is much more difficult in Iran and takes a longer time. Thus, the majority of those who have their private practice in Iran have a longer work experience and have a higher satisfaction level.

The current results found no significant correlation between the total satisfaction level and the COVID-19 pandemic (P = 0.058); nonetheless, the satisfaction level with drainage of intraoral abscess (P = 0.046), root canal preparation (P = 0.018), fabrication of stainless-steel crown (P = 0.019), fabrication of post and meta-ceramic crown (P = 0.004), and fabrication of partial removable denture (P = 0.045) was significantly lower in those graduated during the COVID-19 pandemic compared to others. A study conducted in Malaysia demonstrated that dentists who graduated during the COVID-19 pandemic had more problems with endodontic treatment of multi-rooted teeth, orthodontic treatment planning, tooth preparation for a crown, and inferior alveolar nerve block administration, and had a lower satisfaction level compared to those graduated before the pandemic.[16] Acquiring sufficient skills in endodontics and prosthodontics requires extensive clinical practice and theoretical learning.[16] Virtual education and low patient admission during the COVID-19 pandemic are among the influential factors in the graduates’ low satisfaction level.[29]

Limitation and recommendation

This study had some limitations. Those who graduated earlier may have problems recalling their competency status early after graduation. A simple questionnaire may not be a comprehensive tool and cannot be solely reliable for data collection in this respect.[1415] Qualitative methods such as verbal interviews are suggested to assess the satisfaction level with clinical skills during undergraduate dental education.[14] Moreover, using a large sample size and comparison of the satisfaction level of graduates and senior dental students may yield valuable information; such a comparison and increasing the sample size were not feasible in the present study since all participants were graduates of the same educational curriculum. Future studies are recommended on the satisfaction level of graduates of other dental schools nationwide who received the educational curriculum reformed in 2009. Also, the satisfaction level of graduates of the reformed curriculum should be compared with the present results to find the strengths and weaknesses of the two curricula.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings of the present study provide valuable insights into the clinical training experiences of dental graduates and their overall satisfaction level. While graduates expressed higher satisfaction in areas such as restorative dentistry and oral and maxillofacial surgery, lower satisfaction levels were noted with departments such as orthodontics and prosthodontics, which indicates the need for targeted improvements. From a health policy perspective, these results align with global benchmarks that emphasize on the importance of high-quality, competency-based education for healthcare professionals. The need for enhancing clinical training programs is critical to ensure that healthcare systems produce graduates capable of delivering safe, effective, and patient-centered care. To meet these standards, curriculum revisions should prioritize departments with lower satisfaction, such as orthodontics and prosthodontics, by enhancing hands-on training, increasing patient exposure, and providing structured mentorship. Additionally, national health policies should implement standardized mechanisms for ongoing evaluation of clinical training quality and invest in up-to-date facilities and equipment to ensure graduates are well-prepared to meet the demands of modern practice. Supporting the transition of graduates from training to practice through mentorship and residency programs is also vital. Aligning these educational improvements with health policy standards will help dental schools contribute to the broader goal of enhancing healthcare systems, ultimately improving both graduate satisfaction and patient care outcomes.

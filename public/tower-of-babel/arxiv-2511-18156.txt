A new proof of an Eğecioğlu–Remmel inverse Kostka matrix problem via a Garsia–Milne involution involving Sym and NSym Thanks: SM was partially supported by an AMS-Simons PUI grant.
Edward E. Allen, Kyle Celano, Sarah K. Mason

Abstract
. Eğecioğlu and Remmel provide a combinatorial proof (using special rim hook tableaux) that the product of the Kostka matrix K K and its inverse K − 1 K^{-1} equals the identity matrix I I . They then pose the problem of proving the reverse identity K − 1 ​ K = I K^{-1}K=I combinatorially. Sagan and Lee prove a special case of this identity using overlapping special rim hook tableaux. Loehr and Mendes provide a full proof using bijective matrix algebra that relies on the Eğecioğlu–Remmel map. In this article, we solve the problem in full generality independent of the Eğecioğlu–Remmel bijection. To do this, we start by proving NSym versions of both Kostka matrix identities using sign-reversing involutions involving the tunnel hook coverings recently introduced by the first and third authors. Then we modify our sign-reversing involutions to reduce to Sym. Finally, we show that our bijection is different than the Loehr and Mendes result by constructing an injective map between special rim tableaux and the symmetric group S n . S_{n}.

Lemma 3.8
.

For all partitions
\lambda\not=\mu
, the map
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}
is a well-defined map from
B_{\lambda,\mu}
to

Proof.

Consider a pair

and let
(U,V)=\chi_{\lambda,\mu}(S,T)
. Let
\alpha
be the content of
S
,
\perm(T)=\sigma
, and
\ell=\ell(\mu)

\sigma
is a permutation of
[\ell]

q_{m}\leq\ell
. Therefore

is of the same length as

is a well-defined tunnel hook covering by
2.4

By construction,
U
has the same shape as

, so

is of partition shape. We must prove that converting the leftmost occurrence of

in row

of

into a

Next we must show that the content of

(\Delta_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}(V),\Delta_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}(V),,\ldots,\Delta_{\sigma^{-1}(\ell)}(V)).

To see this, first note that

Equation

Replacing one

times for all other

is

\mu_{x}+q_{m}-x&\textrm{if }i=q_{m},\\

On the other hand, when we apply

(to obtain

∎

We are now ready to prove that

Theorem 3.9
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\vdash n
such that
\lambda\neq\mu
,
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}
is a sign-reversing involution.

Proof.

The fact that
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}
is sign-reversing is immediate from construction. To see that it is indeed an involution, let
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}(S,T)=(U,V)
and suppose we apply
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}
to
(U,V)
. Note that for

,

is the only entry appearing in row

. Let the first
j+1
entries in row

of

be

. Step (3b) first converts the entry in column

and row

of

into

. Since the columns of

are strictly increasing,

. Furthermore,

for

because the rows of

are weakly increasing. Therefore none of the entries to the right of

are paired during the Bender–Knuth portion of Step (3b) applied to

. Since the largest entry in

is

and there is at least one

, the largest entry in

must be

. The entries in rows

through

of

are the same as those in

. Therefore, row

is the row selected in Step (1) of Algorithm

applied to

and the permutation of the resulting tunnel hook is

. Hence, the resulting tunnel hook covering is

by

.

To see that the resulting semistandard Young tableau is

, note that since the Bender–Knuth involution sends segments of paired entries of

to segments of paired entries of

and segments of unpaired entries of
S
to segments of unpaired entries of
U
, we only need to check the unpaired entries equal to
q_{m}-1
and
q_{m}
in row
m
. In

, let

be the number of unpaired occurrences of

in row

and let

be the number of unpaired occurrences of

in row

. (Note that

and

4.
A combinatorial proof of Theorem
1.3

The goal of this section is to prove that
(\tilde{K}^{-1}\tilde{K})_{\alpha,\beta}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
for all compositions
\alpha,\beta
of
n
. The combinatorial interpretations of these matrices imply that

(8)

(\tilde{K}^{-1}\tilde{K})_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\vDash n}\tilde{K}^{-1}_{\alpha,\gamma}\tilde{K}_{\gamma,\beta}=\sum_{(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}}\sgn(T),

where
C_{\alpha,\beta}
is the set of pairs
(T,S)
such that

•

T
is a
THC with content
\alpha
,

•

S
is an immaculate tableau of content
\beta
, and

•

\operatorname{sh}(S)=\operatorname{sh}(T)
.

We prove that
(\tilde{K}^{-1}\tilde{K})_{\alpha,\beta}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
for all compositions
\alpha,\beta
of
n
by defining an involution
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
on
C_{\alpha,\beta}
with the following properties.

(1)

If
\alpha=\beta
, then
C_{\alpha,\alpha}
contains exactly one pair
(T,S)
and this pair is a fixed point under
\g 1_{\alpha,\alpha}
such that
\sgn(T)=1
.

(2)

If
\alpha\neq\beta,
then
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
is a sign-reversing involution.

We start with the first point. The proof of
4.1
is the same as that
given in

3.1

mutatis mutandis
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any composition
\alpha
,
C_{\alpha,\alpha}
contains exactly one pair
(T,S)
and for this pair, we have
\perm(T)=id
.

Algorithm 4.2
.

Suppose
\alpha
and
\beta
are compositions of
n
. Let
(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}
and set
\sigma=\perm(T)
. Assume that
T
and
S
have common shape
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{\ell})
and let
M
be the maximum entry in
S
. We construct another pair
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)=(V,U)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}
as follows.

(1)

Let
k
be the smallest nonnegative integer less than
\ell
such that the following conditions are true for all
i\in\{k+1,\dots,\ell\}
.

(a)

\sigma_{i}=i.

(b)

Row
i
of
S
contains no entries other than
M-\ell+i
.

(c)

M-\ell+i
only appears in row
i
of
S
.

If no such

exists, set

.

(2)

If
k=0
, then
\alpha=\beta
and so we set
\g 1_{\alpha,\alpha}(T,S)=(T,S)
.

(3)

Otherwise
k\in[\ell]
. Set

\Ri=\{\sigma_{j}\;|\;M-\ell+k\textrm{ appears in row }j\;\mathrm{of}\;S\}.

Select
r
such that
\sigma_{r}
is minimal in
\Ri
.

(4)

If
\sigma_{r}=k
, first delete the rightmost entry in row
r
of
S

and then add a row (row
p=\sigma^{-1}(k+1)=k+1
) consisting of a single
M-\ell+k
between rows
k
and
k+1
of
S
to create

. Embed
\sigma
into
S_{\ell+1}
in the obvious way and then let

be the THC of shape
\operatorname{sh}(U)
with
\perm(V)\in S_{\ell+1}
defined by
\perm(V)=s_{k}\sigma
.

(5)

If
\sigma_{r}\neq k
, delete the last entry in row
r
of
S
and set

. Then append a cell with value
M-\ell+k
to the end of row

to create
U
. If row
r
of

is empty (which happens when

and
\gamma_{k}=1
), shift the rows

up to fill the rows
r,\ldots,\ell-1
. Finally let

be the THC of shape
\operatorname{sh}(U)
with
\perm(V)=s_{q}\sigma
.

Now set
\psi(T,S)=\psi_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)
if
(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}.

See Examples
4.5
and
4.6
. We first show that
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
is a well-defined function.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be compositions of
n
. If
(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta},
then
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\alpha=\beta
. Lemma
4.1
states that there is only one pair
(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\alpha}
and for this pair,
\perm(T)=id
. Therefore Condition (1a) (
\sigma_{i}=i
) is satisfied by every integer
1\leq i\leq\ell
and thus
\alpha=\gamma
. Hence
\gamma=\alpha=\beta
. Since the content of
S
equals the shape of
S
, Conditions (b) and (c) of Step (1) are also satisfied for all

. Therefore

and hence
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
fixes
(T,S)
.

Next assume that
\alpha\not=\beta
. In both cases (Steps (4) and (5)), we delete the last entry in row
r
. Since

appears in row
r
by assumption and
M-\ell+k
is the largest entry in rows
1
through
k
, the last entry in row
r
of
S
must be
M-\ell+k
. Therefore, in all cases we delete one occurrence of
M-\ell+k
and append it to row
p
. Thus, the content of
U
is the same as the content of
S
. We need to prove that

is indeed an immaculate tableau. The rows remain weakly increasing (since row

is among the first
k
rows), so we just need to show that the leftmost column is strictly increasing from top to bottom.

Suppose Step (4) is applied. Since
\sigma_{r}=k
and
r
is chosen so that
\sigma_{r}
is minimal among elements of
\mathcal{R}
(whose values must be less than or equal to
k
), the only row containing

is row

. If

were the leftmost entry in row
k
, then

and row

could not contain any entries other than

(since the row entries weakly increase from left to right and no entry larger than

is contained in row

). But then

and row

would satisfy Conditions (a), (b), and (c) in Step (1), contradicting our choice of

in Step (1). Therefore, the leftmost entry in row

must be strictly less than

. The entry in the leftmost column of row

of

must equal

by Condition (1b), so the leftmost column remains strictly increasing after the insertion of

between rows

and

. Hence in this case, the resulting diagram

is indeed an immaculate tableau.

Now suppose Step (5) is applied. Then, the leftmost column of

is only altered if row
r
is deleted. In this case, the leftmost column remains strictly increasing since deleting an entry from a strictly increasing sequence results in a strictly increasing sequence. Therefore,

is an immaculate tableau.

The tunnel hook covering
V
has the same shape as

. We need to prove that

is a well-defined tunnel hook covering whose content is equal to the content of
T
. From this, it follows that

Suppose we are in Step (4). A new row is added to

and therefore

has
\ell+1
rows. Since
\sigma_{r}=k\leq\ell
, we have
\sigma_{r}<k+1\leq\ell+1

is a well-defined tunnel hook covering by

.

Suppose we are in Step (5). Then,
\sigma_{r}<k\leq\ell
. If
\gamma_{k}\not=1
, the number of rows of

equals
\ell
and hence

is a permutation of
[\ell]
and
V
is a well-defined tunnel hook covering. Now, suppose
\gamma_{r}=1
. By construction of

, there is at least one

in row

of

and since

, this is the only entry in row

. Note that this is in the leftmost column of

. Observe that

is the largest value appearing in rows

are in rows

by Condition (1c). Hence, we must actually have

because

is immaculate and therefore its leftmost column must be strictly increasing.

Recall

is given by
\Delta_{i}=\gamma_{i}+\sigma_{i}-i
for each

(
Equation

. To see this, assume that

From here, note that if

is a permutation of

. If

. Thus,

is a permutation of

. Therefore by

is a well-defined tunnel hook covering.

Now that we know

. Let

be the shape of

and

is computed by

\Delta_{i}(V)=\begin{cases}\gamma^{\prime}_{r}+\tau_{r}-r=(\gamma_{r}-1)+(\sigma_{r}+1)-r&\textrm{if }i=r,\\

Note that in all of these cases,

∎

Now, we can show that
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
is a sign-reversing involution on
C_{\alpha,\beta}
when
\alpha\neq\beta
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be compositions of
n
and let
(T,S)\in C_{\alpha,\beta}
.

(1)

If
\alpha=\beta
, then
\g 1_{\alpha,\alpha}(T,S)=(T,S)
.

(2)

If
\alpha\neq\beta
, then
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)=(V,U)
satisfies

(a)

\sgn(V)=-\sgn(T)
,

(b)

\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(V,U)=(T,S)
.

Note that in proving
4.3
, we also proved Part (1). Let
\alpha\neq\beta
. Since
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
is clearly sign-reversing, we just need to verify Part (2b). Suppose
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)=(V,U)
and let

and

be as defined in
4.2
for computing
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)
. Also, let
\tau=\perm(V)
and let

and

be defined as in
4.2
for computing
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(V,U)
.

Suppose
\sigma_{r}=k
. Then applying
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}
to
(T,S)
adds a new row (with one box containing the entry

) between rows
k
and

. This means that in

, row

contains one entry (

) and

. Since rows

and greater all satisfy Conditions (a), (b), and (c) of Step (1) and

to
(V,U)
is

. Since
\sigma_{r}=k
is the minimal entry of
\mathcal{R}
,

is placed in row

, which is exactly the row it came from during
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S)
. The transposition

is applied to

, resulting in the original permutation

. Therefore
\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(\g 1_{\alpha,\beta}(T,S))=(T,S)
in this case.

Now suppose
\sigma_{r}\neq k
. In this situation, an entry (

) is deleted from row

. Thus,

is applied to
\sigma
to produce the permutation

, we have

and
\tau(r)=\sigma_{r}+1

in row

of

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\vdash n
with
\lambda\neq\mu
,
\theta_{\lambda,\mu}
is a sign-reversing involution on
E_{\lambda,\mu}\setminus D_{\lambda,\mu}
.

Proof.

The map is clearly sign-reversing, so it just remains to show that it is a well-defined involution. The content of
U^{\prime}
is equal to that of
U
and
V^{\prime}
is a THC with
\operatorname{sh}(V^{\prime})=\operatorname{sh}(U^{\prime})
by construction. Therefore proving that
\theta_{\lambda,\mu}
is well-defined reduces to proving that
U^{\prime}
is an immaculate tableau of composition shape which is not a semistandard Young tableau and that the content of
V^{\prime}
is a rearrangement of
\lambda
.

To see that
U^{\prime}
is an immaculate tableau of composition shape, first note that since
U
is immaculate, the first column of
U
is increasing so a bad cell is not in the first column of
U
. Hence
P
and
Q
contain no cells in the first column, so the first column of
U^{\prime}
is increasing. We must prove that the rows of
U^{\prime}
weakly increase from left to right.

Suppose
(t,i)
is the chosen bad cell. By minimality of
i
,
U(t-1,i-1)
is defined. By maximality of
t
and the fact that
U
is an immaculate tableau,
U(t-1,i-1)<U(t,i-1)\leq U(t,i)
. To check if row
t-1
of
U^{\prime}
is weakly increasing, we may assume that
U(t,i+1)
is defined and show that
U(t-1,i-1)\leq U(t,i+1)
. If
U(t,i+1)
is defined then
U(t,i)\leq U(t,i+1)
, which implies
U(t-1,i-1)\leq U(t,i+1)
. Therefore row
t-1
of
U^{\prime}
is weakly increasing.

If
U(t-1,i)
is undefined then row
t
of
U^{\prime}
is weakly increasing. Otherwise, since
(t,i)
is a bad cell, we have
U(t-1,i)\geq U(t,i)
and row
t
of
U^{\prime}
is weakly increasing. All other rows of
U^{\prime}
are the same as those in

, so
U^{\prime}
is an immaculate tableau. Moreover,
(t,i)
is still a bad cell of
U^{\prime}
since

are defined). Hence,

is an immaculate tableau but not a semistandard Young tableau.

Next observe that if

, then

We must have

Next, to see that the content of

\tau=\perm(V)

\tau^{\prime}=\tau s_{t-1}

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}_{k}-k+\tau^{\prime}_{k}=\alpha_{k}-k+\tau_{k}=\Delta_{k}(V)\quad(\text{for $k\not\in\{t-1,t\}$}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{t-1}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}_{t-1}-(t-1)+\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}=\alpha_{t}-1-(t-1)+\tau_{t}=\alpha_{t}-t+\tau_{t}=\Delta_{t}(V),

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}_{t}-t+\tau^{\prime}_{t}=\alpha_{t-1}+1-t+\tau_{t-1}=\Delta_{t-1}(V).

Therefore,

\Delta(V^{\prime})=(\Delta_{1}(V),\Delta_{2}(V),\ldots,\Delta_{t-2}(V),\Delta_{t}(V),\Delta_{t-1}(V),\Delta_{t+1}(V),\ldots,\Delta_{\ell}(V))

Finally, to see that

, and thus must be
V
.

Connections to special rim hook tableaux

In this section, we provide a bijection between special rim hook tableaux and tunnel hook coverings with nonnegative content. We do so by assigning a unique permutation to each special rim hook tableau in a manner similar to
2.4
. This allows us to make comparisons between our algorithm and those of Loehr and Mendes
[
12
]
and Sagan and Lee
[
14
]
.

6.1.
The permutation associated to a special rim hook tableau

Following
[
4
]
, we define a rim hook
\mathfrak{g}
to be a connected skew diagram (the set theoretic difference of two Ferrers diagrams of partition shapes) which does not contain
a
2\times 2
square of cells. A
special rim hook tableau
of shape
\lambda
is a set theoretic partition of the cells of a Ferrers diagram of shape
\lambda
into rim hooks such that each rim hook contains a cell from the leftmost column of
\lambda.

The
initial cell
of a rim hook
\mathfrak{g}
is the northeastern-most cell contained in
\mathfrak{g}
. The
terminal cell
of a rim hook
\mathfrak{g}
is the southwestern-most cell contained in
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that the
d
th
diagonal is the set of all cells of the form
\mathcal{L}_{d}=\{(d+k,1+k)\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Note that
(x,y)
and
(a,b)
are on the same diagonal if and only if
x-y=a-b
. In particular,
(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}_{d}
if and only if
d=x-y+1
. This definition of diagonals allows them to be indexed by integers (either positive, zero, or negative). However, while initial cells may occur in diagonals indexed by any integer, terminal cells always occur in diagonals indexed by positive integers since terminal cells always appear in the leftmost column.

Lemma 6.1
.

Each diagonal of a special rim hook tableau contains a maximum of one initial cell.

Proof.

Assume that diagonal
d
contains
k
cells, at least one of which is an initial cell. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{k}
be the
k
distinct special rim hooks passing through this diagonal, ordered so that
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
passes through cell
(d+i-1,i)
. Let
m
be the maximum value such that
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
has initial cell
(d+m-1,m)
in diagonal
d
. Then
(d+m-2,m)
is in the diagram and cannot be in
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
. Therefore
(d+m-2,m)
must be in
\mathfrak{g}_{m-1}
.
This means
(d+m-2,m-1)
is not the initial cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{m-1}
. Continuing inductively, we see that none of the special rim hooks
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{m-1}
can have its initial cell in diagonal
d
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose

. Then
\sigma=\permSRT(R)

.

Proof.

Using the notation in
6.2
, we first check that
\sigma_{i}
is in
[\ell]
for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
It is clear that the values produced in Case (2) of

lie in
[\ell]
, so we must prove that
0<i-\lambda_{i}\leq\ell
for all
i
such that the diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
contains no initial cells.

Fix an arbitrary
i\in[\ell]
such that the diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
does not contain any initial cells. Since
i\leq\ell
, it is clear that
i-\lambda_{i}\leq\ell
. Therefore we must show

. Assume not; that is, assume

. Then

, so there are
i

of the diagram. Since special rim hooks move down and to the left, no two entries on the same diagonal can be covered by the same special rim hook. Therefore there must be exactly
i

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
must contain the cell

cannot be an initial cell,
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
includes the cell
(2,\lambda_{i}-i+3)
. Continuing inductively, we arrive at the following contradiction. We see that if none of

, then

must have its initial cell in this diagonal for otherwise

would need to include

, which is not in the diagram. Hence by contradiction we must have
i-\lambda_{i}>0
for all

such that the diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
contains no initial cells.

Now we must check the injectivity of
\sigma
. Since
\lambda
is a partition,

if and only if

. Since no two special rim hooks can have terminal cells in the same row, it is clear that if

.

. We will show that row

cannot contain a terminal cell, which completes the proof that
\sigma_{j}\not=\sigma_{i}
for any

such that

.

.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})\vdash n

the portions of the tunnel hooks appearing in partial diagrams.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
T
be a tunnel hook covering of
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
.
For
1\leq k\leq\ell
, set

to be the tunnel hook covering of shape

that is constructed by removing the cells of the diagram of shape
\alpha

as well as the corresponding cells of the tunnel hooks in rows

. If

are the
diagonals of the terminal cells in row

of

then

\perm(\hat{T}^{k})=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{j})\perm(\hat{T}^{k-1}).

Although we leave the proof of this proposition to Appendix

, it relies on the fact that removing rows from the bottom of a tunnel hook covering still produces a valid tunnel hook covering of the resulting shape. Similarly, removing columns from the right of a special rim hook tableau produces a valid special rim hook tableau of the resulting shape.

6.2.
The relationship between the permutation and content of a special rim hook tableau

We now want to describe which permutations one can obtain from special rim hook tableaux. For a special rim hook
\mathfrak{g}
, let
|\mathfrak{g}|
denote the number of cells in
\mathfrak{g}
. Define a weak composition
\Gamma(R)=(\Gamma_{1}(R),\Gamma_{2}(R),\dots,\Gamma_{\ell}(R))
by setting

\Gamma_{i}(R)=\begin{cases}|\mathfrak{g}|&\text{if there is a $\mathfrak{g}\in R$ with initial cell on diagonal $i-\lambda_{i}+1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for each
i\in[\ell]
. Note that
\Gamma_{i}(R)
is well-defined since diagonal
i-\lambda_{i}+1
contains at most one initial cell by
6.1
. The content of
R
is
\dec(\fl(\Gamma(R))).

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
R
be a special rim hook tableau of shape
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})\vdash n
.

(1)

For each
i\in[\ell]
, we have
\Gamma_{i}(R)=\lambda_{i}-i+\permSRT(R)_{i}.

(2)

The image of
R\mapsto\permSRT(R)
is the set of permutations
\sigma\in S_{\ell}
such that
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[\ell].

Proof.

For (1), let
\sigma=\permSRT(R)
and suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is a special rim hook of
R
with initial cell
(x,y)
in diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
and terminal cell in row
r=\sigma_{i}
. Note that the terminal cell of
\mathfrak{g}
is
(\sigma_{i},1)
.

Since the number of cells in a special rim hook equals one plus the number of south and west steps from the initial cell to the terminal cell,
|\mathfrak{g}|=|\sigma_{i}-x|+|1-y|+1
. Since
\sigma_{i}\geq x
and
y\geq 1
, we have

|\mathfrak{g}|=|\sigma_{i}-x|+|y-1|+1=(\sigma_{i}-x)+(y-1)+1=y-x+\sigma_{i}.

Since
(x,y)
is in diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
, we have
x-y+1=i-\lambda_{i}+1
and hence
y-x=\lambda_{i}-i
. Therefore
|\mathfrak{g}|=\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}
, as desired.

If there is no special rim hook of
R
with an initial cell on diagonal
\mathcal{L}_{i-\lambda_{i}+1}
, then
\sigma_{i}=i-\lambda_{i}
. Hence,
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}=0=\Gamma_{i}(R)
, as desired.

For (2), suppose
\sigma\in S_{\ell}
satisfies
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[\ell].
We can construct a special rim hook tableau of shape
\lambda
with permutation
\sigma
inductively as follows. First observe that if
\ell=1
, then the claim is obvious. Now suppose
\ell>1
and
\sigma_{i}=\ell
. If
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}=0
, then
\lambda_{i}+\ell=i
. However,
i\leq\ell
implies that
\lambda_{i}\leq 0
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}>0
and therefore we may construct a special rim hook covering
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}
cells by starting with initial cell at
(i,\lambda_{i})
and moving down and to the left along the boundary of
\lambda
until we reach the cell
(\ell,1)
.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}
be the partition obtained by removing the cells of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma(i,i+1,\dots,\ell-1,\ell)
(see
6.5
), then
\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{k}
for some
k<\ell
(because
\sigma_{i}=\ell
), so we can construct a special rim hook tableau
R^{\prime}
with permutation
\sigma^{\prime}
. To apply induction, we must also check that
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-j+\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\geq 0
for all
j\in[k]
. If
j<i
, then
\sigma_{j}^{\prime}=\sigma_{j}
and
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\lambda_{j}
, so
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-j+\sigma_{j}^{\prime}=\lambda_{j}-j+\sigma_{j}\geq 0
. If
j\in\{i,i+1,\dots,k\}
, then
\sigma^{\prime}_{j}=\sigma_{j+1}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\geq\lambda_{j+1}-1
. Hence,

\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-j+\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{j+1}-1-j+\sigma_{j+1}=\lambda_{j+1}-(j+1)+\sigma_{j+1},

which is at least 0 by assumption. Then, as in the proof of
6.4
, attaching
\mathfrak{g}
to
R^{\prime}
forms a special rim hook tableau
R
of shape
\lambda
with
\permSRT(R)=\sigma
.
∎

We combine
6.4
and
6.11
into a more succinct statement. For
\lambda\vdash n
, let
\SRT_{\lambda}
be the set of special rim hook tableaux of shape
\lambda
.

Corollary 6.12
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})\vdash n
. The map

\permSRT:\SRT_{\lambda}\to\{\sigma\in S_{\ell}\mid\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}\geq 0\ \text{for all $i\in[\ell]$}\}

is a bijection. Moreover, if
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}
and
\sigma=\permSRT(R)
, then
\Gamma_{i}(R)=\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}
for all
i\in[\ell].

Our development of the permutation of a special rim hook tableau leads us to a direct proof of Eğecioğlu and Remmel’s result connecting special rim hook tableaux to the Jacobi–Trudi identity, which was originally proven via recurrence relations. For
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}
, let

wt(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}h_{\Gamma_{i}(R)}.

Corollary 6.13

(
[
4
, Theorem 1]
)
.

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})\vdash n
,

\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}=\sum_{R\in\SRT_{\lambda}}\sgn(R)wt(R).

Proof.

Combine
6.12
with the observation that in

\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\ell}}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}h_{\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}},

the term
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}h_{\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}}
is nonzero if and only if
\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[\ell].

∎

6.3.
Connections and comparisons

Now we discuss connections between special rim hook tableaux and tunnel hook coverings. For
\lambda\vdash n
, let
\thc^{\geq 0}_{\lambda}
be the set of tunnel hook coverings of shape
\lambda
such that
\Delta_{i}(T)\geq 0
for all
i\in[\ell(\lambda)].
Define a map

\xi_{\lambda}:\SRT_{\lambda}\to\thc^{\geq 0}_{\lambda}

given by setting
\xi_{\lambda}(R)=T
if
T
is the (unique) tunnel hook covering with shape
\lambda
and permutation
\perm(T)=\permSRT(R)
. The map is well-defined by
2.4
. See
Figure

12
.

Theorem 6.14
.

For all partitions
\lambda
, the map
\xi_{\lambda}
is a bijection. Moreover, for all
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}
,
\Gamma(R)=\Delta(\xi_{\lambda}(R)).

Proof.

Let
T
be a tunnel hook covering of shape
\lambda
with permutation
\perm(T)=\sigma
. By
Equation

3
,
\Delta_{i}(T)\geq 0
if and only if
\lambda_{i}+\sigma_{i}-i\geq 0
for each

Hence,
6.12
and
2.4
show that
\xi_{\lambda}
is a bijection such that if
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}
, then

\Gamma_{i}(R)=\lambda_{i}-i+\sigma_{i}=\Delta_{i}(\xi_{\lambda}(R))

for all
i\in[\ell(\lambda)]
.
∎

For a partition
\lambda
, we can view an element of
\thc_{\lambda}^{\geq 0}
as a set theoretic partition of the diagram of
\lambda
by rim hooks by removing all tunnel hooks with
\Delta_{i}(T)=0
(as we do in

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
. Let
R
be a set theoretic partition of the diagram of
\lambda
by rim hooks
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. Then
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}\cap\thc_{\lambda}^{\geq 0}
if and only if for each
i\in[k]
, the initial cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is at the end of its row and the terminal cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is in the first column.

Proof.

If the initial cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is at the end of its row for each
i
, then
R\in\thc_{\lambda}^{\geq 0}
. If the terminal cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is in the first column for each
i
, then
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}
. The other inclusion is immediate from the definitions of special rim hook tableaux and tunnel hook coverings.
∎

We have two ways of computing the permutation of
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}\cap\thc_{\lambda}^{\geq 0}
. The following propositions shows that they agree.

Proposition 6.16
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
. If
R\in\SRT_{\lambda}\cap\thc_{\lambda}^{\geq 0}
, then
\perm(R)=\permSRT(R)
and
\xi_{\lambda}(R)=R.

Proof.

Let
R
have rim hooks
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{g}_{k}
with initial cells in rows
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}
and terminal cells in rows

. By
6.15
, the initial cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is at the end of row

and so
\permSRT(R)_{x_{i}}=j_{i}
.

On the other hand, the terminal cell of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is on diagonal
j_{i}
by

, so
\perm(R)_{x_{i}}=j_{i}
. Now suppose

. If we view

has a tunnel hook starting in row

with

occupying the cell

for all

Since

itself is the unique tunnel hook covering of
\lambda

\perm(R)=\permSRT(R)

\xi_{\lambda}(R)=R
.
∎

6.4.
Conclusion and discussion

We proved both
\Sym
identities (
KK^{-1}=I
and
K^{-1}K=I
) by first proving an analogous identity (
\tilde{K}\tilde{K}^{-1}=I
and
\tilde{K}^{-1}\tilde{K}=I
) in
\NSym
and then reducing to
\Sym
. The proof of the second identity is an instance of the Garsia–Milne involution principle, relying heavily on the fact that every semistandard Young tableau is also an immaculate tableau. In particular, we could abstract our proof as follows:

(1)

Show that proving the symmetric function identity is equivalent to showing that a signed sum of pairs of combinatorial objects

\sum_{(S,T)\in\mathcal{S}}\sgn(T)

takes a particular value.

(2)

Find a corresponding noncommutative symmetric function identity that is equivalent to a signed sum of pairs of other combinatorial objects

\sum_{(S,T)\in\mathcal{N}}\sgn(T)

taking on a particular value. Importantly,
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{N}.

(3)

Produce a sign-reversing involution on
\mathcal{N}
to prove the identity in NSym.

(4)

Produce another involution on
\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{S}
and combine these two NSym involutions in the spirit of Garsia–Milne to produce a Sym involution.

This paradigm of combining results from
\NSym
together with the Garsia–Milne involution principle could also be applied to a wide array of symmetric function problems that naturally can be phrased as signed sums as in Step (1). To prove an identity in
\Sym
(ideally with a known combinatorial expansion involving semistandard Young tableaux such as an expansion into the monomial basis), first identify the corresponding identity in
\NSym
to complete Step (2). Then construct a sign-reversing involution to prove the identity in
\NSym
for Step (3). Finally, construct one more involution to solve the original problem in Step (4).

Proposition
6.7
.

Suppose a tunnel hook covering
T
has tunnel hooks
\mathfrak{h}(1,\tau_{1}),\allowbreak\mathfrak{h}(2,\tau_{2}),\dots,\mathfrak{h}(\ell,\tau_{\ell})
with terminal cells in rows
j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{\ell}
, respectively. Then,

\perm(T)=(j_{1},j_{1}-1,\dots,2,1)(j_{2},j_{2}-1,\dots,3,2)\cdots(j_{\ell-1},\ell-1)(\ell).

Proof.

Let
\sigma=\perm(T)
. Recall that the
Lehmer code
of a permutation
\sigma
is the sequence
L(\sigma)=(L(\sigma)_{1},L(\sigma)_{2},\dots,L(\sigma)_{\ell})
, where
L(\sigma)_{i}=\#\{j>i\mid\sigma_{j}<\sigma_{i}\}.
By
[
1
, Lemma 35]
,
L(\sigma)_{i}+1
is the height of
\mathfrak{h}(i,\tau_{i})
for each
i\in[\ell]
, so
L(\sigma)_{i}=j_{i}-i
. We claim that the permutation

\pi=(j_{1},j_{1}-1,\dots,2,1)(j_{2},j_{2}-1,\dots,3,2)\cdots(j_{\ell-1},\ell-1)(\ell)

also has Lehmer code
L(\pi)_{i}=j_{i}-i
for all
i\in[\ell]
.

To see this, for
k\in[\ell]
set

\pi^{(k)}=(j_{k},j_{k}-1,\dots,k)\cdots(j_{\ell-1},\ell-1)(\ell).

Since
j_{t}\geq t
for all
t\in[\ell]
, we have that
\pi^{(k)}(i)=i
for
i<k
. Moreover,
\pi^{(k)}(i)\geq k
if and only if
i\geq k
. Suppose
k\geq 2
. Since
\pi^{(k)}(i)=i
for
i\leq k-1
and
\pi^{(k-1)}=(j_{k-1},j_{k-1}-1,\dots,k-1)\pi^{(k)}
, we have

(12)

\pi^{(k-1)}(i)=\begin{cases}\pi^{(k)}(i)&\text{if $\pi^{(k)}(i)>j_{k-1}$,}\\
\pi^{(k)}(i)-1&\text{if $k\leq\pi^{(k)}(i)\leq j_{k-1}$,}\\
j_{k-1}&\text{if $i=k-1$,}\\
i&\text{if $i<k-1$}\\
\end{cases}

for each
i\in[\ell]
. Thus, if
j>i\geq k
, we have that
\pi^{(k-1)}(i)>\pi^{(k-1)}(j)
if and only if
\pi^{(k)}(i)>\pi^{(k)}(j)
. Hence,
L(\pi^{(k)})_{i}=L(\pi^{(k-1)})_{i}
for
i\geq k
. From this it follows that for all
k\in[\ell]
,

L(\pi^{(k)})_{k}=L(\pi^{(k-1)})_{k}=\cdots=L(\pi^{(1)})_{k}=L(\pi)_{k}.

By
Equation

12
,
\pi^{(k-1)}(k-1)=j_{k-1}
and the numbers
k-1,\dots,j_{k-1}-1
are in positions
k
or greater of
\pi^{(k-1)}
and the numbers
1,\dots,k-2
are in positions

. Hence,
L(\pi)_{k-1}=L(\pi^{(k-1)})_{k-1}=j_{k-1}-(k-1)
. Therefore,
L(\pi)_{k}=j_{k}-k
for all
k\in[\ell].

Since the Lehmer code of the permutation
\pi
matches the Lehmer code of
\perm(T)
, they are the same permutation and thus

\perm(T)=(j_{1},j_{1}-1,\dots,2,1)(j_{2},j_{2}-1,\dots,3,2)\cdots(j_{\ell-1},\ell-1)(\ell).\qed

6.9
.

Let

be a tunnel hook covering of
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
.
For
1\leq k\leq\ell
, set
\hat{T}^{k}
to be the tunnel hook covering of shape
\alpha^{k}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k})
that is constructed by removing the cells of the diagram of shape
\alpha

as well as the corresponding cells of the tunnel hooks in rows

d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{j}

k\geq 2

\pi^{(k-1)}=\perm(\hat{T}^{k-1})

\pi^{(k)}=\perm(\hat{T}^{k})

\mathfrak{h}

\hat{T}^{k}

\mathfrak{h}

\pi^{(k-1)}(i)\not\in\{d_{1},\dots,d_{j}\}

\pi^{(k)}(i)=(d_{1},\dots,d_{j})\pi^{(k-1)}(i).

\mathfrak{h}

\hat{T}^{k}

(k,k-d)

\hat{T}^{k}

(k,k-d+1)

\hat{T}^{k}

\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{h}

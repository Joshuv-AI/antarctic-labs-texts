Inverse problems with integral conditions for the generalized Korteweg–de Vries equation
Oleg S. Balashov, Andrei V. Faminskii

Abstract
. Results on well-posedness of three inverse problems with integral conditions on a bounded interval for the generalized Korteweg–de Vries equation without any restrictions on the growth rate of nonlinearity are established. Either the right-hand side of equation or the boundary data, or both are chosen as controls. The considered solutions are regular with respect to the time variable. Assumptions on smallness of the input data or smallness of a time interval are required.

Introduction. Notation. Description of main results

The paper is concerned with inverse problems for the generalized Korteweg–de Vries equation

(1.1)

u_{t}+bu_{x}+u_{xxx}+\bigl(g(u)\bigr)_{x}=f(t,x),

posed on an interval
I=(0,R)
(

consider an initial-boundary value problem for equation (

) with an initial condition

and boundary conditions

u(t,0)=\mu_{0}(t),\quad u(t,R)=\nu_{0}(t),\quad u_{x}(t,R)=\nu_{1}(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

The main attention is paid to an inverse problem with two integral overdetermination conditions for
j=1
and
2

(1.4)

\int_{I}u(t,x)\omega_{j}(x)\,dx=\varphi_{j}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

for certain given functions
\omega_{j}
and

. Two other inverse problems with one overdetermination condition (

In the first problem the function

where the functions

In the second problem the function

The Korteweg–de Vries equation

u_{t}+bu_{x}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=f(t,x)

1.1

[
4

[

]

On the contrary, there is a few papers concerned with inverse problems for the Korteweg–de Vries equation and its generalizations with integral overdetermination conditions. In

The inverse problem with two integral conditions (

in the periodic case, where the functions

In

Similar results for a wide class of odd-order quasilinear evolution equations and systems with general nonlinearity were recently established in

In the present paper the inverse problems are considered in function spaces regular with respect to the time variable. This approach gives an opportunity to avoid any restrictions on the growth rate of nonlinearity
g(u)
in equation (
1.1
). However, on this way certain accuracy with compatibility of initial and boundary data is required. Results on well-posedness are established under assumptions on smallness either of the input data or the time interval.

Fix for the whole paper certain natural
k
. Solutions of the considered problems are constructed in a special function space

X^{k}(Q_{T})=\{u(t,x):\partial_{t}^{m}u\in C([0,T];L_{2}(I))\cap L_{2}(0,T;H^{1}(I)),\ 0\leq m\leq k;\\
\partial_{t}^{m}u\big|_{t=0}\in H^{3(k-m)}(I),\ 0\leq m\leq k-1\}

endowed with the natural norm

(1.6)

\|u\|_{X^{k}(Q_{T})}\\
=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\left[\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{t}^{m}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(I)}+\|\partial_{t}^{m}u_{x}\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\right]+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}u\big|_{t=0}\|_{H^{3(k-m)}(I)}.

Remark 1.1
.

In paper
[
4
]
weak solutions were constructed in the space

X(Q_{T})=C([0,T];L_{2}(I))\cap L_{2}(0,T;H^{1}(I)).

For description of properties of right-hand side of the equation introduce a function space

M^{k}(Q_{T})=\{f(t,x):\partial_{t}^{m}f\in L_{2}(Q_{T}),\ 0\leq m\leq k;\\
\partial_{t}^{m}f\big|_{t=0}\in H^{3(k-m-1)}(I),\ 0\leq m\leq k-1\}

endowed with the natural norm

(1.7)

\|f\|_{M^{k}(Q_{T})}\\
=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\|\partial_{t}^{m}f\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}f\big|_{t=0}\|_{H^{3(k-m-1)}(I)}.

Remark 1.2
.

It is obviously assumed that any function

is already changed on a zero measure set providing

,

It is always assumed that the function

(1.8)

g\in C^{3k-1}(\mathbb{R}),\quad g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0.

Remark 1.3

There is no loss of generality in the the assumption

since nonlinearity in equation (
1.1

and this equation contains the term

Formally differentiating

(1.9)

\partial_{t}^{m}g(u)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m}g^{(l)}(u)\sum\limits_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{l}=m}c_{m,l,n_{1},\dots,n_{l}}\partial_{t}^{n_{1}}u\dots\partial_{t}^{n_{l}}u

(where

In order to define compatibility conditions introduce the following auxiliary functions

and for

\Phi_{m}\equiv\partial_{t}^{m-1}f\big|_{t=0}-(b\Phi^{\prime}_{m-1}+\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m-1})\\
-\Bigl(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m-1}g^{(l)}(u_{0})\sum\limits_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{l}=m-1}c_{m-1,l,n_{1},\dots,n_{l}}\Phi_{n_{1}}\dots\Phi_{n_{l}}\Bigr)^{\prime}.

For description of properties of the boundary data introduce the fractional Sobolev spaces

Regarding the functions

Define the functions

where

Introduce the function space

Obviously,

2.
Auxiliary nonlinear estimates

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
c=c(R)
such that for any
u\in X^{k}(Q_{T})

(2.1)

\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}^{m}u(t,x)|\leq c\|u\|_{X^{k}(Q_{T})},\quad 0\leq m\leq k-1.

Proof.

The proof is based on the elementary interpolating inequality

(2.2)

\sup\limits_{x\in I}|\varphi(x)|\leq c(R)\bigl(\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(I)}+\|\varphi\|_{L_{2}(I)}\bigr)

and the elementary equality

\int_{I}v^{2}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{I}v^{2}(0,x)\,dx+2\iint_{Q_{t}}v_{\tau}(\tau,x)v(\tau,x)\,dxd\tau.

Then with the use of (
1.6
)

\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in Q_{T}}v^{2}(t,x)\leq c\sup\limits_{t\in(0,T)}\Bigl(\int_{I}v_{x}^{2}(t,x)\,dx+\int_{I}v^{2}(t,x)\,dx\Bigr)\leq c\Bigl(\int_{I}v_{x}^{2}(0,x)\,dx\\
+\iint_{Q_{T}}v^{2}_{tx}(t,x)\,dxdt+\iint_{Q_{T}}v^{2}_{x}(t,x)\,dxdt\Bigr)+c\sup\limits_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{I}v^{2}(t,x)\,dx\leq c\|v\|^{2}_{X^{1}(Q_{T})},

whence (
2.2
) follows.
∎

First of all note that according to (

if

3.
Auxiliary linear problem

Consider in a rectangle
Q_{T}
an initial-boundary value problem for a linear equation

(3.1)

u_{t}+bu_{x}+u_{xxx}=f(t,x)

with initial and boundary conditions (
1.2
), (
1.3
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L_{2}(I)
,
\mu_{0},\nu_{0},\nu_{1}\in L_{2}(0,T)
,
f\equiv f_{0}+f_{1x}
, where
f_{0}\in L_{1}(0,T;L_{2}(0,R))
,
f_{1}\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
.
A function

is called a weak solution of problem (
3.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
) if for any function
\phi\in L_{2}(0,T;H^{3}(0,R))
, satisfying
\phi_{t}\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
,
\phi\big|_{t=T}\equiv 0
,
\phi\big|_{x=0}=\phi_{x}\big|_{x=0}=\phi\big|_{x=R}\equiv 0
, the following identity is satisfied:

(3.2)

\iint_{Q_{T}}\Bigl[u(\phi_{t}+b\phi_{x}+\phi_{xxx})+f_{0}\phi-f_{1}\phi_{x}\Bigr]\,dxdt+\int_{I}u_{0}\phi\big|_{x=0}\,dx\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\mu_{0}\phi_{xx}\big|_{x=0}-\nu_{0}\phi_{xx}\big|_{x=R}+\nu_{1}\phi_{x}\big|_{x=R}\Bigr)\,dt=0.

Introduce a function space

M^{k}_{1}(Q_{T})=\{f(t,x)=f_{1x}(t,x):\partial_{t}^{m}f_{1}\in L_{2}(Q_{T}),\ 0\leq m\leq k;\\
\partial_{t}^{m}f\big|_{t=0}\in H^{3(k-m-1)}(I),\ 0\leq m\leq k-1\}

endowed with the natural norm

\|f\|_{M^{k}_{1}(Q_{T})}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\|\partial_{t}^{m}f_{1}\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}f\big|_{t=0}\|_{H^{3(k-m-1)}(I)}.

Remark 3.1
.

It is obviously assumed that any function
f\in M_{1}^{k}(Q_{T})
is already changed on a zero measure set providing
\partial_{t}^{m}f\in C([0,T];H^{-1}(I))
,
0\leq m\leq k-1
.

In order to define compatibility conditions for the linear problem introduce the following auxiliary functions
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(x)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(x;u_{0},f)
: let
\widetilde{\Phi}_{0}\equiv u_{0}
and for
m\geq 1

\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}\equiv\partial_{t}^{m-1}f\big|_{t=0}-(b\widetilde{\Phi}^{\prime}_{m-1}+\widetilde{\Phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m-1}).

Similarly to Lemma
2.4
(of course, with more simple argument due to the absence of nonlinearity) the functions

,

, exist if
u_{0}\in H^{3k}(I)
,

\partial_{t}^{m}f\big|_{t=0}\in H^{3(k-m-1)}(I)
for
0\leq m\leq k-1
, and

(3.5)

\|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(\cdot;u_{0},f)\|_{H^{3(k-m)}(I)}\leq c\varkappa(u_{0},f),\quad 0\leq m\leq k.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{3k}(I)
,
\mu_{0},\nu_{0}\in H^{k+1/3}(0,T)
,
\nu_{1}\in H^{k}(0,T)
,
f\equiv f_{0}+f_{1x}
,
f_{0}\in M^{k}(Q_{T})
,
f_{1x}\in M^{k}_{1}(Q_{T})
,
\mu_{0}^{(m)}(0)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(0;u_{0},f)
,
\nu_{0}^{(m)}(0)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}(R;u_{0},f)
,
\nu_{1}^{(m)}(0)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}^{\prime}(R;u_{0},h_{0})
for

. Then there exists a unique solution

of problem (
3.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and

(3.6)

\|u\|_{X(Q_{T})}\leq c(T)\Bigl[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3k}(I)}+\|\mu_{0}\|_{H^{k+1/3}(0,T)}+\|\nu_{0}\|_{H^{k+1/3}(0,T)}+\|\nu_{1}\|_{H^{k}(0,T)}\\
+\|f_{0}\|_{M^{k}(Q_{T})}+\|f_{1x}\|_{M^{k}_{1}(Q_{T})}\Bigr],

where the constant c(T) does not decrease in

.
Moreover, for

the function

is the solution to problem of (

), (
1.2
), (
1.3
) type, where

are substituted by
\widetilde{\Phi}_{m},\mu_{0}^{(m)},\nu_{0}^{(m)},\nu_{1}^{(m)},\partial_{t}^{m}f
.

Proof.

For

consider a linear problem

\displaystyle u_{t}+bu_{x}+u_{xxx}=\partial_{t}^{m}f,

(3.8)

\displaystyle u\big|_{t=0}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{m},\quad u\big|_{x=0}=\mu_{0}^{(m)},\quad u\big|_{x=R}=\nu_{0}^{(m)},\quad u_{x}\big|_{x=0}=\nu_{1}^{(m)}.

]
) there exists the unique solution of this problem

and

+\|\partial_{t}^{m}f_{0}\|_{L_{1}(0,T;L_{2}(I))}+\|\partial_{t}^{m}f_{1}\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\Bigr].

Obviously,

. For any

Introduce certain additional notation. Let

), (

), (

.

Let the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. Let the function
\omega
satisfy conditions (
1.11
). Then for the function

Effects of Chemical and Herbal Disinfection Methods on the Color Stability of Maxillofacial Silicone: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Himanshu Srivastava, Hari K. Mempally, Sameera Qureshi, Rumiya Aysha, Fatima L. Pathan, Apeksha B. Mahajan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Maxillofacial prostheses used in rehabilitation of patients are fabricated with medical grade silicone and are tinted corresponding to the patients natural skin color. However, exposure to environment and disinfectants can affect the color stability. Due to rising microorganism resistance and fewer adverse effects of chemical agents, phytoextracts appear to be a viable option. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of two different disinfection methods one containing the leaf extracts of Mangifera indica (M. indica) and another containing 2% chlorhexidine solution on the color stability of precolored and hand-colored maxillofacial silicones.Material and Methods:Twenty specimens each of precolored and hand-colored silicone were randomly divided into four groups (n = 10) and cleansed with two different disinfection methods. Disinfection was carried out six times/day for 60 days, simulating once-a-day disinfection for a year. Color evaluation was carried out at day 0 and day 60 using a UV-vis spectrophotometer. Color alterations were calculated by the CIE L*a*b* system. Data were analyzed by two-way ANOVA with post hoc Tukey HSD and t-tests (α = 0.05).Result and Conclusion:In our study, 2% chlorhexidine solution resulted in the higher color change as compared to M.indica extract. Precolored silicone showed higher color stability than its hand-colored counterpart.

INTRODUCTION

Silicone elastomer is the most commonly used material for maxillofacial prostheses.[1] It can be colored intrinsically and extrinsically to confer the patient’s skin color.[2] Precolored silicone, are available in different shades by the manufacturer. It is also possible to manually incorporate different colors, referred as hand-coloring. Certain factors can affect the interaction of color pigments with silicone network as well as its stability over time,[2] limiting the shelf-life of the prosthesis. It occurs following exposure to various external factors as well as disinfection procedures due to its highly permeable nature.[3] Various chemical disinfecting agents are available but it may affect the color stability of the prosthesis. The use of herbs and their products provides new opportunities which also overcomes the microorganism resistance and fewer adverse effects of chemical disinfectants. One such herb is Mangifera indica (M. indica) possesses antimicrobial and antioxidant property.[4]

To the best of our knowledge, there had been no study comparing the effects of leaf extracts of Mangifera indica as a disinfectant with that of 2% chlorhexidine solution on the color stability of precolored and hand-colored maxillofacial silicones. The objectives of this study were to evaluate the effects of coloring techniques and disinfection methods on the color stability of maxillofacial silicone after a simulated 1-year period of cleansing, using the CIELAB color system.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Forty specimens representing the average Indian base skin tone were prepared and randomly divided into 4 groups (n = 10). P-MI and MM-MI (P- precolored, MM-Handcolored and MI- M. indica) groups were disinfected with M. indica leaf extract solution, whereas P-CHX and MM-CHX (P- precolored, MM-Handcolored and CHX-chlorhexidine) groups were disinfected with 2% chlorhexidine gluconate solution. The results were recorded as day 0. Three measurements were made for each specimen, and mean value was considered. The color value was measured using the UV-visible reflectance spectrophotometer and CIE L*a*b*color system, L* for the lightness from black (0) to white (100), a* from green (-) to red (+), and b* from blue (-) to yellow (+). The cycle was sequentially repeated for six times/day, followed by storage in a dark chamber. Similarly, the disinfection cycles were daily repeated for a total of 60 days, simulating a 1-year period of disinfectant usage by the patients. On day 60, the color value of each specimen was measured again. The mean ΔL*, Δa*, Δb* value for each specimen was obtained to calculate the color difference (ΔE). The statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS statistic with a significance level of α = 0.05.

Extract Preparation: The dried leaves of M. indica was grinded into powder. A weighed quantity of powder was extracted with 99% ethyl alcohol as solvent. The extracts were filtered and concentrated.

RESULTS

The highest color change was observed in the MM-CHX group (1.85 ± 0.27), and the lowest in the P-MI group (0.39 ± 0.05), with a statistically significant difference between the two combinations (P < 0.001). Two-way ANOVA showed that two main factors (types of silicone and disinfection methods), significantly affected color stability (P < 0.001). However, the type of intrinsically colored silicone and disinfection method did not significantly affect the color stability (P = 0.093) [Table 1].

A post-hoc test (Tukey HSD test) revealed that the color change of silicone after disinfectant with 2% chlorhexidine solution was significantly higher than M. indica extract (P < 0.001). Regardless of the disinfection methods, the t-test indicated that the hand-colored silicone group had significantly higher color change than the precolored silicone group (P = 0.014).

DISCUSSION

Several factors can lead to bond breakage within the silicone polymeric network and color pigments, leading to their alteration or disintegration.[2] In this study, the effects of different disinfection methods on color stability of precolored and hand-colored silicone were evaluated. All specimens, showed different amounts of color alterations after disinfection and were in accordance with previous literature.[5] Precolored silicone showed significantly higher color stability over hand-colored silicone regardless of the disinfection method. Whereas 2% CHX solution showed significantly higher color alteration than M.indica extract. Chamaria et al.[5] found that antibacterial soap (Dettol) had higher color change compared to 2% chlorhexidine solution. The color changes associated with M. indica extract remained below the perceivable value of <1 for both precolored and hand-colored silicone, the mean ΔE of MM-CHX, and P-CHX indicated considerable color changes which were enough to be detectable by experienced observers.

CONCLUSION

M. indica leaf extract showed greater color stability than 2% Chlorhexidine solution. In addition, precolored silicone had greater color stability than hand-colored silicone.

𝑳 𝟏 \boldsymbol{L_{1}} -2-type surfaces in 3-dimensional De Sitter and anti De Sitter spaces
S. Carolina García-Martínez and Pascual Lucas and H. Fabián Ramírez-Ospina

Abstract
. Let M s 2 M_{s}^{2} be an orientable surface immersed in the De Sitter space 𝕊 1 3 ⊂ ℝ 1 4 \mathbb{S}_{1}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1} or anti de Sitter space ℍ 1 3 ⊂ ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{H}_{1}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} . In the case that M s 2 M_{s}^{2} is of L 1 L_{1} -2-type we prove that the following conditions are equivalent to each other: M s 2 M_{s}^{2} has a constant principal curvature; M s 2 M_{s}^{2} has constant mean curvature; M s 2 M_{s}^{2} has constant second mean curvature. As a consequence, we also show that an L 1 L_{1} -2-type surface is either an open portion of a standard pseudo-Riemannian product, or a B B -scroll over a null curve, or else its mean curvature, its Gaussian curvature and its principal curvatures are all non-constant.

1.
Introduction

The submanifolds of finite type in the Euclidean or pseudo-Euclidean space are submanifolds whose isometric immersion in the ambient space is constructed by using eigenfunctions of their Laplacian. This notion provides a very natural way to apply spectral geometry to the study of submanifolds (see
[
2
,
3
]
). This concept, originally defined for the Laplacian operator
\Delta
, can be generalized in a natural way to other operators such as the differential operator
\Box
(sometimes denoted by
L_{1}
) introduced by Cheng and Yau in
[
5
]
for the study of surfaces with constant scalar curvature.

Let
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
denote the 3-dimensional De Sitter or anti De Sitter space, isometrically immersed in a pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
of index
q
. An orientable surface
\psi:M^{2}_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{c}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
is said to be of
L_{1}
-2-type if the position vector
\psi
admits the following spectral decomposition

\psi=a+\psi_{1}+\psi_{2},\hskip 10.00002ptL_{1}\psi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\psi_{1},\hskip 10.00002ptL_{1}\psi_{2}=\lambda_{2}\psi_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

where
a
is a constant vector in
\mathbb{R}_{q}^{4}
, and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are
\mathbb{R}_{q}^{4}
-valued non-constant differentiable functions on
M_{s}^{2}
.

The second and third author in
[
10
,
11
]
studied these kind of surfaces in the non-flat Riemannian space forms. It should be noted that the shape operator in the Riemannian case is always diagonalizable so that neither the techniques used nor the results obtained can be directly transferred to the Lorentzian case, since in this case the shape operator may be non-diagonalizable.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some formulae and notions about surfaces in Lorentzian
space forms that will be used later on. Let
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
be the 4-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space of index
q\in\{1,2,3\}
, with flat metric given by

\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>=-\sum^{q}_{i=1}dx^{2}_{i}+\sum_{j=q+1}^{4}dx^{2}_{j},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
denotes the usual rectangular coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. The De Sitter space of radius
r
is defined by

\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}(r)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}\;:\;\left<x,x\right>=r^{2}\big\},

and the anti De Sitter space of radius
-r
is defined by

\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}(-r)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}\;:\;\left<x,x\right>=-r^{2}\big\}.

It is well known that
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}(r)
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}(-r)
are Lorentzian totally umbilical hypersurfaces with constant sectional curvature
+1/r^{2}
and
-1/r^{2}
, respectively.
In order to simplify our notation and computations, we will denote by
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}

the De Sitter space
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}(1)
or the anti De Sitter space
\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}(-1)
according to
c=1
or
c=-1
, respectively. We will use
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
to denote the corresponding pseudo-Euclidean space where
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
lives, so its metric is given by

\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>=-dx^{2}_{1}+c\;dx^{2}_{2}+dx^{2}_{3}+dx^{2}_{4},

and we can write

\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}\;:\;-x^{2}_{1}+cx^{2}_{2}+x^{2}_{3}+x^{2}_{4}=c\big\}.

Let
\psi:M_{s}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
be an isometric immersion of a surface
M_{s}^{2}
into
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
, and let
N
be a unit vector field normal to
M_{s}^{2}
in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
, where
\left<N,N\right>=\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Here
s=0
(resp.
s=1
) if the induced metric on the surface is Riemannian (resp. Lorentzian). Let
\nabla^{0}
,
\overline{\nabla}
and
\nabla
denote the Levi-Civita connections on
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
,
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
and
M_{s}^{2}
, respectively. Then the Gauss and Weingarten formulas are given by

(1)

\nabla^{0}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+\varepsilon\left<SX,Y\right>N-c\left<X,Y\right>\psi,

and

SX=-\overline{\nabla}_{X}N=-\nabla^{0}_{X}N,

for all tangent vector fields
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
, where
S:\mathfrak{X}(M^{2}_{s})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
stands for the shape operator (or Weingarten endomorphism) of
M_{s}^{2}
, with respect to the chosen orientation
N
. If a vector field
X
everywhere different from zero satisfies the condition
SX=\lambda X
, for a differentiable function
\lambda
, we will say that
X
is a principal direction of
M_{s}^{2}
with associated principal curvature
\lambda
.

It is well-known (see, for instance,
[
13
, pp. 261–262]
) that the shape operator
S
of the surface
M^{2}_{s}
can be expressed, in an appropriate frame, in one of the following types:

\displaystyle\text{I. }S\approx\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\ColSep}c@{\Sep 4}c@{\ColSep}}\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\kappa_{1}\hfil\kern 4.0pt&0\hfil\kern 2.0pt\\
\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil 0\hfil\kern 4.0pt&\kappa_{2}\hfil\kern 2.0pt\end{array}\right];\kern 28.45274pt\text{II. }S\approx\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\ColSep}c@{\Sep 4}r@{\ColSep}}\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\kappa\hfil\kern 4.0pt&-b\kern 2.0pt\\
\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil b\hfil\kern 4.0pt&\kappa\kern 2.0pt\end{array}\right],\hskip 10.00002ptb\neq 0;\kern 28.45274pt\text{III. }S\approx\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\ColSep}c@{\Sep 4}c@{\ColSep}}\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\kappa\hfil\kern 4.0pt&0\hfil\kern 2.0pt\\
\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil 1\hfil\kern 4.0pt&\kappa\hfil\kern 2.0pt\end{array}\right].

In cases I and II,
S
is represented with respect to an orthonormal frame, whereas in case III the frame is pseudo-orthonormal. The characteristic polynomial
Q_{S}(t)
of the shape operator
S
is defined by
Q_{S}(t)={\rm det}(tI-S)=t^{2}+a_{1}t+a_{2}
, where the coefficients
a_{i}
are given by

(8)

a_{1}=-\text{\rm tr}(S),\hskip 20.00003pta_{2}=\text{\rm det}(S).

The mean curvature
H
of
M_{s}^{2}
in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
is given by
H=(\varepsilon/2)\text{\rm tr}(S)
, and the coefficient
a_{2}
is also called the
second mean curvature or mean curvature of order
2
,
H_{2}
.
The Newton transformation of
M_{s}^{2}
is the operator
P_{1}:\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
defined by

(9)

P_{1}=-2\varepsilon HI+S,

and by the Cayley-Hamilton theorem we have
S\circ P_{1}=-H_{2}I
.

The divergence of a vector field
X
is the differentiable function defined as the trace of the operator
\nabla X
, where
\nabla X(Y):=\nabla_{Y}X
. Analogously, the divergence of an operator
T:\mathfrak{X}(M^{2}_{s})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
is the vector field
\text{\rm div}(T)\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
defined as the trace of
\nabla T
, where
\nabla T(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}T)Y
.

We have the following properties of
P_{1}
(see
[
8
,
9
]
).

Lemma 1
.

The Newton transformation
P_{1}
satisfies:

(a)

P_{1}
is self-adjoint and commutes with
S
.

(b)

\text{\rm tr}(P_{1})=-2\varepsilon H
.

(c)

\text{\rm tr}(S\circ P_{1})=-2H_{2}
.

(d)

\text{\rm tr}(S^{2}\circ P_{1})=-2\varepsilon HH_{2}
.

(e)

\text{\rm tr}(\nabla_{X}S\circ P_{1})=-\left\langle\nabla H_{2},X\right\rangle
.

(f)

\text{\rm div}(P_{1})=0
.

Bearing this lemma in mind we obtain

\text{\rm div}(P_{1}(\nabla f))=\text{\rm tr}\big(P_{1}\circ\nabla^{2}f\big),

where
\nabla^{2}f:\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
denotes the self-adjoint linear operator metrically equivalent to the Hessian of
f
, given by
\left<\nabla^{2}f(X),Y\right>=\left<\nabla_{X}(\nabla f),Y\right>
, for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2}).

Associated to the Newton transformation
P_{1}
, we can define the second-order linear differential operator
L_{1}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M_{s}^{2})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M_{s}^{2})
given by

(10)

\displaystyle L_{1}(f)=\text{\rm tr}\big(P_{1}\circ\nabla^{2}f\big).

An interesting property of
L_{1}
is the following

(11)

\displaystyle L_{1}(fg)=gL_{1}(f)+fL_{1}(g)+2\left<P_{1}(\nabla f),\nabla g\right>,

for every couple of differentiable functions
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M_{s}^{2})
.

Now, we are going to compute
L_{1}
acting on the coordinate components of the immersion
\psi
, that is, a function given by
\left<e,\psi\right>
, where
e\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
is an arbitrary fixed vector.

A direct computation shows that

(12)

\nabla\left<e,\psi\right>=e^{\top}=e-\varepsilon\left<e,N\right>N-c\left<e,\psi\right>\psi,

where

denotes the tangential component of

. Taking covariant derivative in (
12
), and using that
\nabla^{0}_{X}e=0
, jointly with the Gauss and Weingarten formulae, we obtain

(13)

\nabla_{X}\nabla\left<e,\psi\right>=\varepsilon\left<e,N\right>SX-c\left<e,\psi\right>X,

for every tangent vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{s}^{2})
. Finally, by using (
10

1
, we find that

(14)

\displaystyle L_{1}\left<e,\psi\right>=\varepsilon\left<e,N\right>\text{\rm tr}(S\circ P_{1})-c\left<e,\psi\right>\text{\rm tr}(P_{1})=-2\varepsilon H_{2}\left<e,N\right>+2\varepsilon cH\left<e,\psi\right>,

and then we get

(15)

L_{1}\psi=-2\varepsilon H_{2}N+2\varepsilon cH\psi.

We will now compute
L_{1}
acting on a function given by
\left<e,N\right>
. A straightforward computation yields

(16)

\nabla\left<e,N\right>=-Se^{\top},

that jointly with the Weingarten formula leads to

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\nabla\left<e,N\right>=-(\nabla_{e^{\top}}S)X-\varepsilon\left<e,N\right>S^{2}X+c\left<e,\psi\right>SX,

for every tangent vector field

and then

(18)

On the other hand, equations (
11

\displaystyle\varepsilon L_{1}(L_{1}\left<e,\psi\right>)

\displaystyle=-2H_{2}L_{1}\left<e,N\right>-2L_{1}(H_{2})\left<e,N\right>-4\big\langle P_{1}(\nabla H_{2}),\nabla\left<e,N\right>\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+2cHL_{1}\left<e,\psi\right>+2cL_{1}(H)\left<e,\psi\right>+4c\big\langle P_{1}(\nabla H),\nabla\left<e,\psi\right>\big\rangle.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\big[4cH^{2}_{2}+4\varepsilon H^{2}+2cL_{1}(H)\big]\left<e,\psi\right>.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon L_{1}^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=\big[4cP_{1}(\nabla H)-3\nabla H_{2}^{2}]+\big[-4\varepsilon HH_{2}(c+\varepsilon H_{2})-2L_{1}(H_{2})\big]N

Example 1

(
Totally umbilical surfaces in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}

Let us begin by showing that the totally umbilical surfaces in

As is well known, the totally umbilical surfaces in

Complex circle

k

as follows. Since
\mathbb{C}^{2}

\mathbb{R}_{2}^{4}

(z,w)=(u_{1}+iu_{2},x+iy)

(u_{1},x,u_{2},y)

\psi:\mathbb{C}\equiv\mathbb{R}_{1}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{1}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}_{2}^{4}

\psi(z)=k(\cos z,\sin z)

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\ColSep}c@{\Sep 4}c@{\ColSep}}\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\alpha\hfil\kern 4.0pt&-\beta\hfil\kern 2.0pt\\[4.0pt]

Example 4
.

(
B
-scroll
) Finally, we will show an example with non-diagonalizable shape operator. This example also shows the importance of the Gaussian curvature in concluding whether or not the surface is of
L_{1}
-2-type.
Let
\gamma(s)
,
s\in I
, be a null curve in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}_{q}^{4}
with associated Cartan frame
\{A=\gamma^{\prime},B,C\}
, i.e.,
\{A,B,C\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame of vector fields along
\gamma(s)

\left\langle A,A\right\rangle=\left\langle B,B\right\rangle=0,\left\langle A,B\right\rangle=-1,\left\langle A,C\right\rangle=\left\langle B,C\right\rangle=0,\left\langle C,C\right\rangle=1,

such that

\gamma^{\prime}(s)=A(s),\hskip 10.00002ptC^{\prime}(s)=-a_{0}A(s)-\kappa(s)B(s),

where
a_{0}
is a nonzero constant and
\kappa(s)\neq 0
for all
s
. Then the map
\psi:I\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
, given by
\psi(s,u)=\gamma(s)+uB(s)
, defines a Lorentzian surface
M_{1}^{2}
in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
known as a
B
-scroll on the null curve
\gamma
(see
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
).

A simple calculation shows that the vector field
N(s,u)
given by
N(s,u)=-a_{0}uB(s)+C(s)
defines a unit normal vector field to
M^{2}_{1}
in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
, and the shape operator
S
is expressed in the usual tangent frame
\big\{\partial_{s}\psi,\partial_{u}\psi\big\}
as

S=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\ColSep}c@{\Sep 4}c@{\ColSep}}\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil a_{0}\hfil\kern 4.0pt&0\hfil\kern 2.0pt\\[4.0pt]
\kern 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\kappa(s)\hfil\kern 4.0pt&a_{0}\hfil\kern 2.0pt\end{array}\right],

whose minimal polynomial is
(t-a_{0})^{2}
. Consequently, a
B
-scroll is a Lorentzian surface with constant mean curvature
H=a_{0}
and constant Gauss curvature
K=c+a_{0}^{2}
.

Now, let us check if the
B
-scroll is of
L_{1}
-
2
-type. From (
2
) and (
15
) we find

\displaystyle L^{2}_{1}\psi

\displaystyle=(2HK)L_{1}\psi.

Therefore, a non-flat

-scroll is a null
L_{1}
-
2
-type surface, whereas a flat

-scroll (i.e,

,

4.
Main results

Now, we are in position to present our first main result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\psi:M_{s}^{2}\to\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
be an orientable surface of
L_{1}
-
2
-type. Then
H
is constant if and only if
H_{2}
is constant.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
H
is constant. Our goal is
to prove that
H_{2}
is also constant. Otherwise, let us consider the
non-empty open set

\mathcal{U}_{2}=\big\{p\in M_{s}^{2}\;:\;\nabla H^{2}_{2}(p)\neq 0\big\}.

By taking covariant derivative in (
23
) we have
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}a^{\top}=4\varepsilon\nabla H^{2}_{2}
. From here and (
21
) we
get
\nabla H_{2}^{2}=0
on
\mathcal{U}_{2}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
H_{2}
is constant.

Conversely, let us suppose now that
H_{2}
is constant, and
consider the open set

\mathcal{U}_{1}=\big\{p\in M^{2}_{s}\;:\;\nabla H^{2}(p)\neq 0\big\}.

Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{U}_{1}
is empty. Otherwise, by taking covariant derivative in (
22
) we get

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}Sa^{\top}=4\varepsilon H_{2}(c+\varepsilon H_{2})\nabla H.

From (
21
), and bearing in mind that
S\circ P_{1}=-H_{2}I
, we get
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}Sa^{\top}=-4\varepsilon cH_{2}\nabla H
, so that

4\varepsilon H_{2}(2c+\varepsilon H_{2})\nabla H=0.

Consequently, on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
we have either
H_{2}\equiv-2\varepsilon c
or
H_{2}\equiv 0
. We will study each case separately.

Case 1:

H_{2}\equiv-2\varepsilon c
. By applying the operator
L_{1}
on both sides of (
22
), and using (
23
), we get

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}L_{1}\left<a,N\right>=-8L_{1}(H)=-4\big[\varepsilon\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>-4\varepsilon cH^{2}+2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})H-\varepsilon c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-16\big].

By using (
17
) we obtain

-c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,N\right>H+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>=-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>+4cH^{2}-2\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})H+c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+16\varepsilon,

and from (
22
) we find

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>=-2cH^{2}-3\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})H+\frac{c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{2}+8\varepsilon.

By taking covariant derivative here, and using (
21
), we get

\big[-4cH-3\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\big]\nabla H=-8cH\nabla H+4\varepsilon cS(\nabla H),

Now, by applying
S
on both sides of this equation, and taking in mind that
S\circ P_{1}=2\varepsilon cI
, we have

\big[-4cH-3\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\big]S(\nabla H)=4\varepsilon cS\circ P_{1}(\nabla H)=8\nabla H.

From the last two equations we deduce

16H^{2}-9(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}+32\varepsilon c=0,

and then
H
is constant on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2:

H_{2}\equiv 0
, then
S(S-2\varepsilon HI)=0
. Since a totally umbilical surface is not of
L_{1}
-2-type, we can assume that
M_{s}^{2}
has two distinct principal curvatures
\kappa_{1}=0
and
\kappa_{2}=2\varepsilon H\neq 0
.
Let
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
, with
\left<E_{i},E_{i}\right>=\varepsilon_{i}
, be a local orthonormal frame of principal directions of
S
such that

S=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&2\varepsilon H\end{pmatrix}.

Write
\nabla H=\alpha E_{1}+\beta E_{2}
, where
\alpha=\varepsilon_{1}E_{1}(H)
, then we have
S(\nabla H)=2\varepsilon H\beta E_{2}
. On the other hand, the Codazzi equation
(\nabla_{E_{1}}S)E_{2}=(\nabla_{E_{2}}S)E_{1}
yields

\varepsilon_{2}E_{1}(H)=-H\left<\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{1},E_{2}\right>\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt0=\left<\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{2},E_{1}\right>.

From here we deduce

\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{1}=0,&\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{2}=0,\\[8.5359pt]
\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{1}=-\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha}{H}E_{2},&\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{2}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\alpha}{H}E_{1},&[E_{1},E_{2}]=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha}{H}E_{2},\end{array}

and then the curvature tensor is given by (
[
13
]
)

R(E_{1},E_{2})E_{1}=\nabla_{E_{1}}\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{1}-\nabla_{E_{2}}\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{1}-\nabla_{[E_{1},E_{2}]}E_{1}=\Big[-E_{1}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha}{H}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\alpha}{H}\Big)^{2}\Big]E_{2}.

On the other hand, since
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
is of constant curvature we have

R(E_{1},E_{2})E_{1}=-\varepsilon_{1}cE_{2}.

From the last two equations we have

(25)

\displaystyle HE_{1}(\alpha)=cH^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}\alpha^{2}.

Now, since
P_{1}(E_{1})=-\kappa_{2}E_{1}
and
P_{1}(E_{2})=0
, we get

(26)

\displaystyle L_{1}(H)=-2\varepsilon HE_{1}(\alpha),

that jointly with (
23
) yields

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>=-2\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})H+c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-8\varepsilon_{1}\alpha^{2},

and from here we get

(27)

\displaystyle E_{1}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>)

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\varepsilon_{1}\alpha-16\varepsilon_{1}\alpha E_{1}(\alpha).

However, from (
21
) we have

(28)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}a^{\top}=-8cH\alpha E_{1},

so that

(29)

\displaystyle E_{1}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>)

\displaystyle=\left<\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}a^{\top},E_{1}\right>=-8\varepsilon_{1}cH\alpha,

that jointly with (
27
) implies

(30)

\displaystyle-4E_{1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=-2cH+\frac{\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}{2}.

From here, and using (
26
) and (
23
), we find

(31)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>=2cH^{2}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})H+c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}.

Taking gradient in (
31
) and using (
21
) we get

(32)

\displaystyle\Big[4cH-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\Big]\nabla H

\displaystyle=4\varepsilon cP_{1}(\nabla H)=-8cH\nabla H+4\varepsilon cS(\nabla H).

Now, by applying
S
on both sides of (
32
) we have

\big[4cH-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\big]S(\nabla H)=0.

From the last two equations we find

\big[4cH-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\big]\Big[3\varepsilon H-\frac{3c}{8}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\Big]\nabla H=0,

and this implies that
H
is constant on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
, which is a contradiction. This finishes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
\psi:M_{s}^{2}\to\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{q}
be an orientable surface of
L_{1}
-
2
-type. The following conditions are equivalent to each other:

1)

M_{s}^{2}
has a constant principal curvature.

2)

H
is constant.

3)

H_{2}
is constant.

Proof.

From Theorem
2
we know that conditions 2) and 3) are equivalent to each other. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the equivalence of conditions 1) and 2).

If condition 2) holds then
M_{s}^{2}
is an isoparametric surface since condition 3) also holds. Then the shape operator
S
of
M_{s}^{2}
in
\mathbb{M}^{3}_{c}
is of type I (with constant
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
) or type III (with constant
\kappa
), so we have condition 1). Observe that
S
cannot be of type II since this kind of surfaces do not exist in
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}
and the complex circle in
\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}
is not of
L_{1}
-2-type.

Let us now assume that condition 1) is satisfied, then
S
is of type I or type III. If
S
is of type III then
\kappa
is constant, and therefore
H
and
H_{2}
are also constants.

Let us consider the case where
S
is diagonalizable with two distinct principal curvatures
\kappa_{1}\neq\kappa_{2}
, and assume without loss of generality that
\kappa_{1}
is a nonzero constant
(otherwise,
H_{2}=0
and the Theorem
2
applies). Consider the open set

\mathcal{U}=\big\{p\in M^{2}_{s}\;:\;\nabla\kappa_{2}^{2}(p)\neq 0\big\}.

Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{U}
is empty.
Otherwise, the equations (
21
)–(
23
) of a
L_{1}
-
2
-type surface can be rewritten in terms of
\kappa_{2}
as
follows

(33)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}a^{\top}

\displaystyle=[-6\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa_{2}-2c(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})]\nabla\kappa_{2}+2cS(\nabla\kappa_{2}),

(34)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,N\right>

\displaystyle=2\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}[\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})(c+\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2})]-2\kappa_{1}L_{1}\kappa_{2},

(35)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>

\displaystyle=4\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa^{2}_{2}+c(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})^{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})+c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+L_{1}\kappa_{2}.

From (
34
) and (
35
) we find

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,N\right>=-2\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\left<a,\psi\right>+2\kappa_{1}[3\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa^{2}_{2}+c\kappa^{2}_{1}+c\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\kappa_{1}+c\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa^{3}_{2}].

By applying the gradient here we obtain

(36)

-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}Sa^{\top}=-2\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}a^{\top}+2\kappa^{2}_{1}[c+6\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}-3\varepsilon\kappa_{2}^{2}]\nabla\kappa_{2}.

On the other hand, by (
33
) we get

(37)

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}Sa^{\top}=-6\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa_{2}S(\nabla\kappa_{2})-2c\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\nabla\kappa_{2}

Now, from (
33
), (
36
) and (
37
) we deduce

(3\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}+2c)S(\nabla\kappa_{2})=(12\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa_{2}-3\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa^{2}_{2}+c\kappa_{2}+3c\kappa_{1})\nabla\kappa_{2}.

Since
3\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}+2c\neq 0
(otherwise,
\kappa_{2}
would be constant), we deduce

S(\nabla\kappa_{2})=f(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})\nabla\kappa_{2},\hskip 20.00003ptf(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=\frac{12\varepsilon\kappa^{2}_{1}\kappa_{2}-3\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa^{2}_{2}+c\kappa_{2}+3c\kappa_{1}}{(3\varepsilon\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}+2c)}.

This equation implies that either
f(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=\kappa_{1}
or
f(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=\kappa_{2}
. In any case it follows
that
\kappa_{2}
is constant on
\mathcal{U}
, and this is a contradiction. This finishes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

A flexible bounded stochastic framework for uncertainty and reliability in physical systems
Authors: Tmader Alballa, Abdus Saboor, Muhammad Danish, Hamiden Abd El-Wahed Khalifa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBounded random variables arise naturally in physical, engineering, and reliability systems when measurements represent proportions, efficiencies, normalized intensities, or constrained state variables. In this paper, a flexible bounded stochastic framework generated through a beta transformation of the Kumaraswamy (Kw) baseline is introduced, yielding a four-parameter family capable of capturing diverse boundary behaviors and hazard rate (HR) structures. Rigorous theoretical properties of the proposed model are developed, including structural identifiability, limiting behavior at the boundaries, shape characteristics of the probability density function (PDF) and HR functions, and explicit stochastic representations. Closed-form expressions for moments, probability-weighted moments are derived under mild regularity conditions, together with comprehensive information-theoretic characterizations based on Shannon, Rényi, and Tsallis entropies, as well as Kullback–Leibler divergence relative to baseline models. Likelihood-based inference is studied in detail, with explicit score functions, Fisher information, and asymptotic properties of the maximum likelihood estimators (MLE) established. An illustrative application to bounded measurements from an engineered system demonstrates the practical relevance of the theoretical results. The proposed framework provides a mathematically rigorous and interpretable tool for uncertainty quantification and reliability analysis of bounded physical quantities.Keywords: Bounded-response modeling, Unit-interval data, Statistical model, Maximum likelihood estimation, Engineering measurements, Environmental dataSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Bounded stochastic variables arise naturally throughout the physical sciences, where many quantities of interest are intrinsically confined to a finite interval and are frequently normalized to lie in (0, 1). Representative examples include fractional energy dissipation, material porosity and diffusion ratios, normalized strain measurements, reliability indicators of physical components, and effectiveness measures in transport and diffusion processes. Rigorous probabilistic modeling of such quantities is essential for understanding uncertainty, degradation, and failure dynamics in physical systems. This requires statistical models that are sufficiently flexible to capture skewness, boundary concentration, and diverse hazard-rate (HR) behaviors, while remaining analytically tractable for inference and interpretation2,3,22.

Among classical bounded distributions, the beta model occupies a central role because of its interpretability, flexible shape, and foundational importance in modeling proportions and rates22–24. The beta model has been extensively used to model proportions, probabilities, and rates in reliability analysis, Bayesian modeling, project planning and control systems such as the program evaluation and review technique (PERT), stochastic simulation frameworks, cost assessment in industrial and civil engineering projects, and population genomic studies. Despite its broad applicability, the beta model may be restrictive in physical applications where empirical data exhibit persistent asymmetry, pronounced boundary behavior, or non-monotone hazard rate patterns.

An alternative bounded model with notable computational advantages is the Kumaraswamy (Kw) model, introduced by Kumaraswamy1. While closely related to the beta model, the Kw model admits closed-form expressions for the cumulative distribution function (CDF) and quantile function (QF), facilitating numerical implementation. The model has been applied to a range of bounded natural phenomena, including individual height measurements, test scores, air temperature indices, and hydrological variables such as daily streamflow and rainfall. Nevertheless, like the beta model, the Kw model may lack sufficient flexibility to accommodate complex boundary dynamics and heterogeneous tail behavior observed in physical reliability and degradation data.

Motivated by these limitations, a substantial body of recent research has focused on extending beta- and Kw-type models to enhance model flexibility while retaining analytical tractability. These developments include Kw-based extensions with improved HR modeling2,3, exponentiated and inverted forms designed to accommodate alternative boundary behavior4,5, and beta-generated constructions aimed at enriching HR dynamics6,7. Additional progress on the unit interval has been achieved through transformation-based and higher-parameter models, such as Johnson-type unit models32, modified beta linear exponential models8, higher-parameter beta families9, and unified bounded models10. More recent contributions continue to emphasize interpretability and flexibility for bounded data, including the unit log–log distribution11, and parsimonious two-parameter unit models13. Collectively, these studies highlight the persistent challenge of developing bounded stochastic models capable of capturing the complex empirical behavior encountered in physical reliability, degradation, and transport processes.

Carrasco and Cordeiro15 proposed a systematic framework for extending the Kw model, providing a structured mechanism for constructing flexible bounded models with enhanced shape adaptability while preserving analytical tractability. This framework is particularly attractive for applications involving physical quantities constrained to the unit interval.

In physical and engineering applications, bounded random variable (r.v.) often arise when measurements represent normalized efficiencies, constrained state variables, degradation indicators, fractional compositions, or sensor-derived process indices. Examples include material porosity, normalized strain or stress measures, reliability scores, bounded quality-control indices, diffusion and transport ratios, and monitored industrial process variables. In such settings, the observed data may exhibit asymmetry, interior concentration, or substantial accumulation near the boundaries, depending on the operating regime and the governing physical constraints. This is particularly relevant in industrial monitoring systems, where bounded measurements may combine routine operating behavior with occasional near-boundary excursions associated with abnormal, depleted, saturated, or transition-like states. These considerations create a practical need for bounded stochastic models that remain analytically tractable while allowing flexible boundary behavior, varied interior shapes, and interpretable reliability characteristics. It is from this applied perspective, rather than from formal generalization alone, that the present beta–Kw (BK) formulation is motivated2,3,8,11,13,22.

Building on these developments, we consider in this paper a four-parameter BK model defined on (0, 1), obtained by combining a Kw-type transformation with a beta-generated structure. Rather than treating the formulation as a purely statistical generalization, we emphasize its role as a flexible stochastic representation for bounded physical quantities. The proposed model accommodates a wide range of PDF and HR shapes, including increasing, decreasing, unimodal, and bathtub-type forms commonly observed in physical reliability and degradation contexts.

Beyond model’s shape characteristics, information-theoretic measures provide additional insight into uncertainty and variability in physical systems. Entropy-based quantities, such as Rényi and Tsallis entropies, are widely used in statistical physics and related fields to characterize disorder, non-equilibrium behavior, and departures from classical assumptions25–28. Motivated by this perspective, we derive entropy measures for the BK model and examine their dependence on the governing parameters, thereby offering an information-theoretic interpretation of model flexibility.

Likelihood-based inference is developed to enable practical estimation of model parameters from observed bounded data. Standard asymptotic properties of the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) are established, allowing uncertainty quantification through the Fisher information matrix, Wald-type confidence intervals, and likelihood ratio testing where appropriate. These tools provide a principled and reproducible inferential framework; finite-sample performance is additionally assessed via a Monte Carlo simulation (MCS) study.

The main contributions of this work are threefold. First, we introduce a flexible bounded stochastic model capable of reproducing a broad range of probability density function (PDF) and HR behaviors encountered in physical reliability and degradation processes. Second, we provide an information-theoretic characterization of uncertainty for the proposed model via Rényi and Tsallis entropy measures. Third, we develop a likelihood-based inferential framework and illustrate its use with bounded data from an engineered system.

To better distinguish the standard background material from the original contribution of this work, we organize the manuscript into a preliminary section followed by sections containing the new BK-model development. Section 2 collects the mathematical ingredients and notation used throughout the paper, including the beta model, the Kw baseline model, and the regularized incomplete beta function. Section 3 then introduces the proposed BK model as the main methodological contribution. The subsequent sections develop its structural properties, moment results, entropy-based measures, likelihood inference, asymptotic theory, numerical evaluation, and real-data application. This revised organization is intended to make explicit which parts of the manuscript are preparatory and which parts constitute the new contribution of the present study.

Applications

This section evaluates the empirical usefulness of the BK model for bounded process data arising from an engineered system. Our aim is not to claim universal superiority over all competing bounded models, but to assess whether the structural flexibility of the BK family yields a meaningful and interpretable representation of normalized physical measurements. In particular, we focus on boundary concentration, interior operating regimes, and inferential limitations that arise in the fitted analysis.

The BK model’s modelling effectiveness is carefully compared against several established bounded models, including the one-parameter Unit Lindley16, the Kumaraswamy (Kw)2, the Topp–Leone (TL)17, beta, the three parameter bounded (3PB)29, the Log Lindley (LL)18 and the unit Lindley (UL)12. The standard statistical methods to evaluate and compare the models have been used , namely the CVM (Cramér-Von Mises), AD (Anderson-Darling), KS (Kolmogorov-Smirnov) statistics along with their corresponding p-values. Additionally, model selection criteria such as the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC), corrected Akaike Information Criterion (CAIC) and Hannan-Quinn Information Criterion (HQIC) are also estimated, providing an in-depth assessment of the model’s efficacy. The SWaT dataset is taken from the Secure Water Treatment testbed31, which records sensor readings gathered from a scaled-down industrial water-treatment testbed operating under both normal and attack circumstances. The plant reproduced real-time operation for approximately 11 days, supplying 5 gallons per minute, and the dataset contains key physical measurements related to the treatment process. In the present study, the SWaT data are used as an example of bounded process variables arising in an engineered monitoring environment, where observations may reflect routine operation together with occasional excursions toward lower or upper normalized ranges.

The SWaT dataset was analyzed without separating normal and attack regimes, reflecting realistic operational variability in industrial control systems. The combined dataset reflects realistic operational variability, including both nominal behavior and rare extreme deviations, and therefore provides a stringent test for bounded statistical models. All observations were normalized to the unit interval prior to analysis.

For a normalized process variable from the SWaT system, the fitted BK PDF has a direct operational interpretation. A left-heavy PDF indicates that the process spends relatively more time near lower normalized states, whereas a right-heavy PDF indicates persistence near the upper operating range. An interior unimodal PDF suggests concentration around a preferred or stable operating regime. The HRF complements this picture by describing how the conditional tendency to move beyond a given normalized level changes across the unit interval. In particular, an increasing HRF indicates that this tendency becomes stronger as the process variable moves toward higher levels, while a bathtub-type HRF suggests comparatively lower conditional instability in the middle range and greater instability near the extremes. In this sense, the BK model is useful not only because it improves shape flexibility, but also because it permits a physically interpretable description of boundary concentration, interior operating behavior, and reliability-related variation in bounded process data.

To assess empirical performance, we compare the BK model with several established bounded models using likelihood-based and goodness-of-fit criteria. However, beyond numerical comparison, we also emphasize whether the fitted BK shape yields a plausible interpretation of the bounded process behavior represented by the data.

The SWaT analysis should be interpreted as an application to normalized process measurements from a monitored industrial water-treatment system, rather than as evidence of uniform dominance of the BK model over all competitors. The practical value of the BK family in this setting lies in its structural decomposition: the parameters govern the Kw-type baseline shape, while regulate boundary emphasis and asymmetry through the beta-generated layer. This separation permits distinct adjustment of interior shape and near-boundary concentration, which is useful when process data combine regular operating behavior with occasional excursions toward lower or upper normalized limits. From an engineering perspective, left-boundary concentration may reflect frequent low-level states, right-boundary concentration may indicate persistent operation near the upper normalized range, and intermediate unimodal shapes may represent concentration around a preferred operating regime. For a normalized process variable from the SWaT system, the fitted PDF therefore admits a direct operational interpretation: a left-heavy shape indicates greater residence near lower states, a right-heavy shape indicates persistence near upper states, and an interior unimodal shape indicates concentration around a preferred regime. The corresponding HRF complements this interpretation by describing how the conditional tendency to move beyond a given normalized level changes across the unit interval. In particular, an increasing HRF suggests a growing tendency toward higher-range observations, whereas a bathtub-type HRF indicates comparatively lower conditional instability in the middle range and greater instability near the extremes. The BK model is therefore best viewed here as a flexible descriptive tool for bounded process variables with interpretable shape adaptation, rather than as a universally dominant alternative under every fit criterion. At the same time, for the SWaT fit, some BK parameter estimates are accompanied by SEs that are large relative to the corresponding maximum likelihood estimates. This indicates that the likelihood surface may be relatively flat in some parameter directions, so the individual parameter estimates should be interpreted with caution. Accordingly, in this application the BK model is viewed primarily as a flexible and physically interpretable bounded representation, while parameter-level inference is treated more cautiously than the overall shape-based description of the process behavior.

Implications for physical sciences and concluding remarks

This section summarizes the main theoretical and applied findings of the paper, discusses their relevance for modeling bounded physical quantities, and highlights the principal limitations and possible directions for future work. Bounded stochastic variables play a fundamental role in the physical sciences, where normalized quantities develop naturally in the narrative of material properties, transport processes, reliability of physical factors, and energy-related measures. In such contexts, probabilistic models must accommodate boundary effects, asymmetric behavior, and diverse failure-rate dynamics while remaining interpretable and computationally tractable.

In this paper, a flexible bounded stochastic framework based on a BK construction is examined. The proposed formulation develops a deep class of PDF and HRF shapes, including increasing, decreasing, unimodal, and bathtub-type behaviors, which are commonly confronted in physical degradation and reliability processes. The methodical structure of the model allows explicit derivation of model properties, moments, and entropy-based measures, facilitating both theoretical interpretation and practical implementation.

From an information-theoretical perspective, the Rényi and Tsallis entropies extend insight into the uncertainty and condition associated with bounded physical systems under varying parameter systems. Such measures are principally relevant in statistical physics and related fields, where departures from classical equilibrium behavior and non-extensive effects are of interest. The dependence of entropy on model parameters focuses on how changes in system characteristics may influence uncertainty and variability in observed physical quantities. Likelihood-based inference was developed to support parameter estimation from empirical data. A MCS study further supported the inferential framework by showing that the MLEs achieve improved accuracy with increasing sample size, as evidenced by decreasing empirical bias and mean squared error.

Overall, the BK framework investigated here provides a mathematically transparent and physically interpretable tool for modeling bounded stochastic variables. Its flexibility, combined with tractable inference and information-theoretic characterization, makes it potentially useful for applications in reliability analysis, materials science, environmental modeling, and other areas where bounded physical quantities and uncertainty play a fundamental role. Future work may explore its integration into specific physical models or its extension to multivariate and spatio-temporal settings.

Limitations. The empirical findings should be interpreted with two limitations in mind. First, the present application is based on one bounded industrial dataset, so broader generalization to other physical and engineering settings requires additional case studies. Second, for the SWaT fit, some BK parameter estimates are accompanied by relatively large SEs, indicating that the likelihood surface may be flat in certain directions and that individual parameter estimates should therefore not be over-interpreted. For this reason, the empirical contribution of the present study is best understood as demonstrating feasibility and structural flexibility, rather than uniformly sharp parameter-level inference in every application.

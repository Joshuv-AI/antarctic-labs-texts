Automated quantification of retinal non-perfusion areas in ultra-widefield SS-OCTA image of patient with retinal vascular diseases by NP-Unet model
Authors: Yanjie Zhu, Yuting Liao, Jianying Pan, Mengyin Tao, Xiaoyu Xu, Ting Lan, Huiting Li, Huirui Xie, Jiamin Huang, Yukang Jiang, Dongmei Ou, Yiling Ou, Xiaoyi Wu, Ting Tian, Yan Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeRetinal non-perfusion areas (NPAs) are a crucial biomarker for assessing the degree of retinal ischaemia of patients with retinal vascular diseases. Accurate identification of NPA is useful for assessing therapeutic approaches, guiding laser treatments and predicting prognosis. The ultra-widefield swept-source optical coherence tomography angiography (UWF-SS-OCTA) has emerged as a superior non-invasive imaging modality with an outstanding ability to detect NPA. This study aimed to establish an efficient NP-Unet model to accurately segment and quantify NPA in the UWF-SS-OCTA image of branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO), central RVO (CRVO) and diabetic retinopathy (DR).MethodsThe 29×24 mm retinal images from the posterior pole of patients with retinal vascular diseases (BRVO, CRVO and DR) were acquired by the UWF-SS-OCTA. The enhanced inner retinal OCTA images datasets were constructed for model training. An efficient NP-Unet model was developed to precisely segment and quantify NPA with ischaemic index (ISI). The performance of the NP-Unet model was compared against different typical segmentation models.ResultsThe NP-Unet model demonstrated its superior capability in segmenting and quantifying NPAs t0 the other models, achieving an accuracy of 93.4%, with an area under the curve of 0.986 and a dice similarity coefficient of 0.904.ConclusionThe NP-Unet model can efficiently and accurately segment and quantify NPAs with the ISI in the UWF-SS-OCTA image of BRVO, CRVO and DR, offering a non-invasive, valuable tool for clinical evaluation and treatment guidance.

Introduction

Branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO), central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) and diabetic retinopathy (DR) are categorised collectively within the spectrum of retinal vascular diseases. BRVO is the second most common retinal vascular disorder, caused by blockages in branch veins.1 Similarly, CRVO results from central vein blockages, while DR is characterised by microvascular complications of diabetes. These conditions all lead to retinal non-perfusion areas (NPAs), where blood flow is insufficient, causing potential tissue hypoxia and malnutrition. In the retinal vascular diseases, NPAs are associated with vision-threatening complications such as macular oedema and neovascularisation.2 3 To address these challenges, NPAs have been identified as critical indicators for guiding antivascular endothelial growth factor and laser therapy, as well as for prognosis assessment.3 4

To quantitatively assess the retinal NPA, the concept of the ischaemic index (ISI) has been proposed and defined as the ratio of the NPA to the total retinal area, serving as a reliable measure of retinal ischaemia.5 6 Currently, fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA) remains the gold standard for detecting retinal NPA; however, it is an invasive examination that may cause allergic reactions due to the contrast agent used, and its applicability is also limited by age and overall health conditions.7 OCTA, as a non-invasive and repeatable vascular examination technique, can visualise microvascular abnormalities in the retinal capillary plexus. With the advent of ultra-widefield swept-source optical coherence tomography angiography (UWF-SS-OCTA), which has a wide scanning range and fast scanning speed, it has gained increasing attention in the observation of retinal vessels.8 Recent studies have demonstrated that UWF-SS-OCTA exhibits high comparability with UWF fluorescein angiography in detecting NPA, the foveal avascular zone and collateral circulation, while also providing additional information.9 10 The latest device, the VG200 SS-OCTA (Intalight Imaging, Luoyang, China) can capture a 150° FOV image of retinal vessels.8 11 Therefore, the UWF-SS-OCTA overcomes the limitations of FFA and OCTA, providing better insights into retinal vessels for monitoring NPA in diseases like RVO and DR. However, accurate NPA assessment in OCTA images is crucial. Manual segmentation is always inefficient and subjective. Therefore, there is an urgent need for an automated, efficient and accurate segmentation approach for retinal NPAs.

Currently, artificial intelligence technologies demonstrate tremendous potential in predicting disease risk, treatment outcomes and progress of retinal diseases.1214 The U-Net model, a widely used deep learning architecture in the field of medical image segmentation, has shown excellent performance in the segmentation of retinal images.15 The U-Net model has been extensively applied to the segmentation of retinal vessels, and the improved version of the U-Net model can more accurately identify the boundaries of retinal vessels, especially under conditions of fine vessels or interfered by pathological backgrounds.1618 Improving the U-Net model to precisely identify and quantify the NPA of retinal vascular diseases is helpful for assisting clinical evaluation and treatment guidance. The Unet-VGG16 model has been used to segment the retinal NPAs from FFA images, achieving an area under the curve (AUC) as high as 0.999 (0.997–1.000) and a dice similarity coefficient (DSC) reaching 89.7% (85.8%–93.6%).14

This study aimed to develop the NP-Unet model to automatically quantify NPAs and ISI in UWF SS-OCTA images of retinal vascular diseases. The model will help clinicians quickly and accurately assess NPAs, thereby enhancing clinical evaluation and treatment guidance for retinal vascular diseases.

Methods and experiments

Dataset and image acquisition

We retrospectively collected UWF SS-OCTA images of patients with BRVO, CRVO and DR at the Retina Department of Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center, Sun Yat-sen University from 27 October 2023 to 19 April 2024. After excluding images with a quality score <6, a total of 80 images were included, comprising 40 images of BRVO, 20 images of CRVO and 20 images of DR. The annotation of the NPAs in each image (the inner retinal layers in enhanced angio mode) was conducted independently by two experienced graders, and any disputed areas were adjudicated by a senior grader.

The VG200S by Intalight Imaging is a state-of-the-art SS-OCTA device with a 1050 nm light source, performing 2×105 A-scans per second. It captures 1024×512 B-scan images over a 29×24 mm area. To prevent motion blur, it features an integrated eye tracker.

The framework is shown in figure 1: enhancing and preprocessing images; training and evaluating the NP-Unet model; predicting and postprocessing. We used Python V.3.7.3 for preprocessing and Python 3.8.10 for model training and testing.

Study design and workflow. The overall framework of the study comprised four steps: (A) specific preprocessing of diverse original images as required; (B) training the NP-Unet model using augmented data; (C) prediction using the trained model and patches regrouping; (D) series of postprocessing steps on the regrouped images to optimise the results.

Image preprocessing and augmentation

To mitigate the impact of external factors on image features and simplify model processing, we preprocessed the RGB images by converting manual annotations to HSV colour space, extracting the brightness and saturation channels and equalising the histogram. We created a green component mask in HSV with hue (H) values from 40 to 80 and full saturation (S) and value (V) ranges (0–255), then binarised it. Gaps were filled by addressing areas with connected components under 100 pixels (online supplemental figure S1). The manual and extracted labels are shown in online supplemental figure S2.

Since the original UWF SS-OCTA images exhibited ambiguous distinctions between the retinal boundary and background, we introduced choroidal layer images to enhance the model’s ability to identify and segment retinal boundary information (online supplemental figure S3). These images were converted to YUV colour space, focusing on the luminance component, with pixels below 5 kept and others set to 255. We applied a 7×7 kernel for morphological closing to smooth boundaries and fill holes, and filled areas under 200 pixels to cover any gaps. The preprocessed choroidal layer images were used as input masks to provide boundary information to the model. Additionally, their weighted overlays were used for postprocessing and the calculation of the ISI metric.

A total of 32 000 and 4 000 image patches of size 256×256 pixels were used for training and validation, respectively. These patches were generated via random cropping from 64 and 8 full-sized images (1024×828 pixels), along with their corresponding ground truth label and mask patches (online supplemental figure S4a). For testing, a total of 3 600 patches (256×256 pixels) were extracted from 8 full images using a sliding window with a stride of 32 pixels, along with the corresponding label and mask patches (online supplemental figure S4b).

The proposed NP-Unet model

The TransUnet model adopted a classic U-shaped architecture with an encoder and a decoder. The encoder employs a hybrid design of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers to capture fine details and global context. Meanwhile, the decoder integrates skip connections from a U-Net, merging high-resolution feature maps across CNN stages to enhance spatial feature fusion and achieve superior localisation accuracy.

Based on the TransUNet architecture, we developed the NP-Unet model for segmenting NPAs in UWF-SS-OCTA images (figure 2). Since the transformer layers in the architecture are primarily designed to capture global contextual information and are computationally intensive, and considering that our training is performed on relatively small 256×256 image patches, where the need for global context is limited, we reduced model complexity and computational cost by using 6 encoder transformer layers for the base model and 12 layers (original) for the large model. The segmentation performance of both variants is reported and compared in subsequent experiments. To better identify retinal boundaries, we generated a mask from preprocessed choroid layer images (indicated by the orange arrow in figure 2a) and input it with the decoder’s final output into the segmentation head, enabling the model to use boundary information from the choroid layer. The augmented training set was randomly divided into batches for training, with each batch containing 16 patches. The network parameters were optimised using the RMSprop optimiser with a learning rate set to 0.001 and a momentum parameter of 0.9. Patches regroup

During the testing and prediction phases, we used smooth cropped patches with a sliding stride of 32 for prediction and subsequent regrouping. To handle patches with non-standard dimensions at the edges, we applied zero-padding to maintain prediction completeness. Each image was divided into 625 patches, and predictions for each pixel were averaged based on their frequency to generate a final prediction map matching the original image size.

Postprocessing procedures

To reduce blur and jagged edges in the prediction map, we implemented a postprocessing workflow. First, the retinal region was defined based on the median of the choroid layer mask, and areas outside this region were removed. A Gaussian filter followed by morphological closing was then applied to smooth boundaries and reduce noise. A binarisation threshold of 100 was used, and noise regions with an area smaller than 200 pixels in the binary image were removed.

Discussion

The UWF SS-OCTA technology in this study achieved an ultra-wide scanning range of 29×24 mm retinal area, allowing for comprehensive segmentation and quantification of NPA in patients with BRVO, CRVO and DR. The NPA is considered an important marker of ischaemia and the ISI serves as a critical indicator for assessing the severity of ischaemia. An increased ISI in the macular region can be viewed as a biomarker for evaluating disease progression or regression.26 27 CRVO with NPA exceeding 10 disc areas is considered ischaemic CRVO, while the specific threshold for ischaemic BRVO remains debated.4 26 28 Ischaemic CRVO carries a higher risk of retinal neovascularisation and poor visual acuity.29 In DR, the extent of NPAs serves as a critical determinant for laser treatment and a robust predictor of disease progression. NPAs exceeding five disc diameters or associated with neovascularisation typically necessitate laser photocoagulation to mitigate the risk of severe complications, while quantification of NPAs using ISI provides a valuable metric for monitoring disease severity and guiding therapeutic decisions.30 Thereby, the accurate calculation of the ISI can offer clinicians a more comprehensive and objective means for the assessment of ischaemic degree and disease development.

Currently, the application of deep learning in ophthalmic imaging primarily focuses on the segmentation and detection of vessels and biomarkers in fundus photographs, fluorescein angiography images and OCTA images. 3133 In this study, we proposed the NP-Unet model with postprocessing procedures that could automatically quantify the NPAs in UWF-SS-OCTA images with high sensitivity and specificity and calculate the ISI precisely to guide the laser treatment for retinal vascular diseases. Due to the exceptional performance of the transformer module in the field of medical imaging and the extensive application of the U-shaped architecture within Unet for segmentation tasks,3437 we opted to use the TransUnet model as the foundational framework. Furthermore, in contrast to other classical segmentation models, we additionally introduced choroidal layer images with clear boundary information into this architecture, enhancing the model’s capacity to handle complex information and to discern boundary details. We used all preprocessed choroidal layer images to produce a mask containing comprehensive retinal boundary information in this study. This mask, subjected to the same data augmentation operations as the images and annotations, was introduced to the model decoder, providing additional boundary information reference for the model segmentation. Subsequently, through convolution operations in the segmentation head, the model further learnt retinal boundary features. The segmentation results obtained by NP-Unet, compared with other traditional segmentation models, significantly reduced misjudgments or errors in NPAs at the boundaries, enhancing precise segmentation capabilities for ambiguous boundary regions. Furthermore, we used a postprocessing workflow to optimise the prediction results in response to the small noise points and blurry edges present in the overall predicted image obtained after patch regroup, assisting physicians in subsequent diagnostic and therapeutic procedures.

Our research demonstrated high accuracy in segmenting large and complete NPAs, while further improvements are required to identify very small NPAs. Additionally, the types of retinal vascular diseases covered in our study were still limited. A more diverse range of retinal disorder images is needed to enhance the generalisation capabilities and precise segmentation of the NP-Unet model.

In summary, the NP-Unet model in this study demonstrated strong performance in efficiently and accurately segmenting and quantifying NPA with ISI in the UWF-SS-OCTA images of patients with retinal vascular diseases, offering a valuable tool for clinical evaluation and treatment guidance.

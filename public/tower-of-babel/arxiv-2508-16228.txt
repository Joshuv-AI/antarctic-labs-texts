Magnetic shielding in the atomic hydrogen anion
Tymon Kilich, Krzysztof Pachucki

Abstract
The atomic hydrogen anion H - is the lightest stable anion and its bound states and resonances are well studied in the literature. Due to the planned comparison of the bare antiproton to H - in a Penning trap, we study the magnetic shielding of H - using the nonrelativistic quantum electrodynamics theory, by accurately calculating the non-relativistic shielding, as well as finite nuclear mass, relativistic, and partially QED corrections. We find that the finite nuclear mass correction is quite significant in H - contributing about 0.1 % 0.1\% of the total shielding, which is more than twice as much as the relativistic correction. Our final result for the shielding constant has a nine-parts-per-trillion accuracy and paves the way for direct comparison of the antiproton-to-proton magnetic moments.



I
Introduction

The simplest and lightest of anions – the atomic hydrogen anion – has been comprehensively studied theoretically in the past,
starting from the theoretical prediction of the existence of the ground state by Bethe
Bethe 1929
subsequently confirmed by the calculations of Hylleraas
Hylleraas 1930
.
Many studies investigate the electronic structure of the ground state of H
-

Thakkar and Koga 1994
;
Drake et al. 2002
;
Harris and Smith 2005
;
Frolov 2015
with the most accurate calculations reaching more than 30 significant digits of the non-relativistic energy
Nakashima and Nakatsuji 2007
;
Aznabaev et al. 2018
;
Petrimoulx et al. 2025
. Although the nuclear magnetic shielding of H
-
was previously calculated using quantum-chemical methods
Boucekkine et al. 1988
;
Vaara et al. 2003
, no estimate of uncertainty is given in those studies and no recoil and relativistic corrections were taken into account. In this work, we obtain a highly accurate value of the nuclear magnetic shielding in the H
-
ion,
by calculation of all important finite nuclear mass, relativistic and quantum electrodynamic (QED) corrections.

Highly accurate calculations of nuclear magnetic shielding using the nonrelativistic QED (NRQED) theory have recently been done for the hydrogen atom and
3
He cation
Pachucki 2023
as well as the helium atom
Rudziński et al. 2009
;
Wehrli et al. 2021
;
Wehrli et al. 2022
, while the combined NRQED and all-order approach was used to obtain the values of the shielding constants for helium-like cations
Yerokhin et al. 2024
. Combining the measurement of the
3
He
+
ion
Schneider et al. 2022
together with the calculation of the magnetic shielding
Wehrli et al. 2021
;
Wehrli et al. 2022
;
Pachucki 2023
has allowed for the most accurate determination of the magnetic moment of helion
and of the whole
3
He atom. This result is important for the development of a new absolute magnetometry standard.

The motivation for the present calculation, in addition to the lack of an accurate value of the magnetic shielding constant for H
-
in the literature, comes from the progress in the experimental measurements of magnetic moments of proton
Schneider et al. 2017
and antiproton
Smorra et al. 2017
with parts-per-billion accuracy as well as the antiproton-to-proton charge–mass ratio experiments
Borchert et al. 2022
, where H
-
serves as an excellent negatively charged proxy for the antiproton. The new generation of experiments using a multi-Penning-trap system of the BASE collaboration
Latacz et al. 2025
paves the way for determining magnetic moments of the proton and antiproton with a fractional accuracy of less than 100 ppt. The experimental setup should allow to perform a measurement of the magnetic moment of H
-
with respect to that of the antiproton with even better accuracy
Ulmer 2025
.

II
Theoretical method

According to Ramsey’s theory
Ramsey 1950
, the coupling of the nuclear magnetic moment
\vec{\mu}
with the homogeneous magnetic field
\vec{B}
is modified by the presence of atomic electrons

\delta H=-\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{B}\,(1-\sigma),

(1)

where
\sigma
is a dimensionless shielding constant. In the low-
Z
atomic systems, the shielding constant
is represented as a double expansion in
\alpha
and the electron-to-nucleus mass ratio
m/M
. The expansion in
\alpha
takes the form

\sigma=\alpha^{2}\sigma^{(2)}+\alpha^{4}\sigma^{(4)}+\alpha^{5}\sigma^{(5)}+\ldots\,.

(2)

The expansion terms
\sigma^{(n)}
are subsequently the non-relativistic shielding,
the relativistic, the leading QED, and the higher-order QED corrections to the shielding.
They can be further expanded in the electron-nucleus mass ratio

\displaystyle\sigma^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{(n,0)}+\sigma^{(n,1)}+\sigma^{(n,2)}+\ldots

(3)

Detailed derivations of the
\sigma^{(n,0)}
terms, up to
n=5
, are presented in the previous works
Pachucki 2008
;
Rudziński et al. 2009
;
Wehrli et al. 2022
,
while the finite nuclear mass correction
\sigma^{(2,1)}
was derived by one of us (KP) in Ref.
Pachucki 2008
and later corrected in Ref.
Pachucki 2023
.
Therefore, we present only the derived formulas, in a.u., used in the numerical evaluation of the nuclear magnetic shielding of H
-
.

The leading-order nonrelativistic and nonrecoil
\sigma^{(2,0)}
contribution to the nuclear magnetic shielding of a closed-shell atomic system was first derived by Lamb
Lamb 1941
and later generalized to diatomic molecules by Ramsey
Ramsey 1950
. In our notation, it is

\sigma^{(2,0)}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a}\Big<\frac{1}{r_{a}}\Big>\,,

(4)

where
r_{a}
is the distance between the nucleus and the
a
’th electron, and the summation runs over all electrons.
The expectation value is taken with the nonrelativistic wave function in the limit of an infinitely heavy nucleus.

The next most important correction in the light H
-
system is the leading nuclear mass correction

\displaystyle\sigma^{(2,1)}\equiv

\displaystyle\,\frac{m}{M}\left(\frac{1-g_{N}}{g_{N}}\,\sigma^{(2)}_{A}+\sigma^{(2)}_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{m}{M}\left(\frac{1-g_{N}}{g_{N}}\,\frac{\left<p_{N}^{2}\right>}{3Z}\,+\frac{1}{3}\Big<\sum_{a}\frac{1}{r_{a}}\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}p_{N}^{2}\Big>\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{3}\left<\sum_{a}\vec{r}_{a}\times\vec{p}_{N}\frac{1}{(E-H)}\sum_{b}\frac{\vec{r}_{b}}{r_{b}^{3}}\times\vec{p}_{b}\right>\right)\,,

(5)

where
1/(E-H)
is the Green function and

1/(E-H)^{\prime}
is the reduced Green function (with the reference state removed from the sum over the spectrum),

\vec{p}_{N}=-\sum_{a}\vec{p}_{a}
, where
\vec{p}_{a}
is the momentum of the
a
’th electron,
g_{N}=(M/m_{p})(\mu/\mu_{N})/(ZI)

is the nuclear
g
-factor;

M
,
I
and
\mu
are the nuclear mass, spin and the magnetic moment,
respectively;
m_{p}
is the proton mass and
\mu_{N}
is the nuclear magneton.

The relativistic correction to nuclear magnetic shielding
(of order
\alpha^{4}
) was derived in Ref.
Rudziński et al. 2009
.
The obtained formula is

\displaystyle\sigma^{(4,0)}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{(4)}+\sigma_{2A}^{(4)}+\sigma_{2B}^{(4)}+\sigma_{2C}^{(4)}+\sigma_{3}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\,\biggl\langle\biggl(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}\biggr)\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\biggl[\sum_{a}\biggl(\frac{\pi\,Z}{2}\,\delta(\vec{r}_{a})-\frac{p_{a}^{4}}{8}\biggr)+\pi\,\delta(\vec{r})-\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{i}\biggl(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{2}}\biggr)\,p_{2}^{j}\biggr]\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{9}\,\biggl\langle\pi\,\bigl[\delta(\vec{r}_{1})-\delta(\vec{r}_{2})\bigr]\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\,\biggl[3\,p_{1}^{2}-3\,p_{2}^{2}-\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{\vec{r}\cdot(\vec{r}_{1}+\vec{r}_{2})}{r^{3}}\biggr]\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\,\biggl\langle\biggl(\frac{\vec{r}_{1}\times\vec{p}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}+\frac{\vec{r}_{2}\times\vec{p}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}\biggr)\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\biggl[\vec{r}_{1}\times\vec{p}_{1}\,p_{1}^{2}+\vec{r}_{2}\times\vec{p}_{2}\,p_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\,\vec{r}_{1}\times\vec{p}_{2}+\frac{1}{r}\,\vec{r}_{2}\times\vec{p}_{1}-\vec{r}_{1}\times\vec{r}_{2}\,\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\cdot(\vec{p}_{1}+\vec{p}_{2})\biggr]\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\,\biggl\langle\biggl(\frac{r_{1}^{i}\,r_{1}^{j}}{r_{1}^{5}}-\frac{r_{2}^{i}\,r_{2}^{j}}{r_{2}^{5}}\biggr)^{(2)}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\,\biggl(Z\,\frac{r_{1}^{i}\,r_{1}^{j}}{r_{1}^{3}}-Z\,\frac{r_{2}^{i}\,r_{2}^{j}}{r_{2}^{3}}+\frac{r^{i}}{r^{3}}\,(r_{1}^{j}+r_{2}^{j})\biggr)^{(2)}\biggr\rangle

(6)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{12\,r_{a}^{3}}\,\Big(\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}\;\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}{r^{3}}-3\,\frac{\vec{r}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}{r}\Big)-\frac{1}{6}\,\Big(\frac{1}{r_{a}}\,p_{a}^{2}+\frac{(\vec{r}_{a}\times\vec{p}_{a})^{2}}{r_{a}^{3}}+4\,\pi\,\delta(\vec{r}_{a})\Big)\biggl\rangle\,,

where
\vec{r}\equiv\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}\,
,
(p^{i}\,q^{i})^{(2)}=p^{i}\,q^{j}/2+p^{j}\,q^{i}/2-\delta^{ij}\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}/3
. Following Ref.
Rudziński et al. 2009
we split the relativistic correction into five terms according to the form of the matrix elements. The first term,
\sigma_{1}^{(4)}
, has intermediate states of
{}^{1}\textrm{S}
symmetry. The second
\sigma_{2A}^{(4)}
, the third
\sigma_{2B}^{(4)}
and the fourth
\sigma_{2C}^{(4)}
terms involve the intermediate states of the symmetry
{}^{3}\textrm{S}
,
{}^{1}\textrm{P}^{e}
, and
{}^{3}\textrm{D}
, respectively. The final part,
\sigma_{3}^{(4)}
, contains the first-order matrix elements.

The logarithmic part of the QED correction to the magnetic shielding was first derived in
Rudziński et al. 2009
,
and a full leading-order QED correction for low-Z systems was later derived in Ref.
Wehrli et al. 2022
.
Since the calculation of the full QED correction to nuclear magnetic shielding is very demanding numerically
Wehrli et al. 2021
,
as it requires calculation of the perturbed Bethe-type logarithms, we directly calculate only the logarithmic part of the QED correction,
while the nonlogarithmic part is estimated from the atomic hydrogen value.
The logarithmic part of the QED correction is given by the formula

\displaystyle\sigma^{(5)}_{\rm log}=

\displaystyle\,\ln(Za)\,\bigg[-\frac{16Z}{9}\,\Big<\sum_{a}\frac{1}{r_{a}}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\sum_{b}\delta(\vec{r}_{b})\Big>

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{28}{9}\,\Big<\sum_{a}\frac{1}{r_{a}}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\delta(\vec{r})\Big>-\frac{40}{9}\,\Big<\sum_{a}\delta(\vec{r}_{a})\Big>\bigg]

(7)

from Ref.
Yerokhin et al. 2024
.

III
Computational methods

Our computations are carried out in the basis of the exponential-type functions following the approach of Korobov
Korobov 2000
;
Korobov 2002a
. For
{}^{1}\textrm{S}
state, these functions have the form

\phi(r_{1},r_{2},r)=\sum_{i=1}^{\cal N}v_{i}[e^{-\alpha_{i}r_{1}-\beta_{i}r_{2}-\gamma_{i}r}\pm(r_{1}\leftrightarrow r_{2})],

(8)

where
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
, and
\gamma_{i}
variational parameters are randomly generated from the quasi-random distribution with the following conditions

\displaystyle A_{1}<\alpha_{i}<A_{2},\;\;\beta_{i}+\gamma_{i}>\varepsilon,

\displaystyle B_{1}<\beta_{i}<B_{2},\;\;\alpha_{i}+\gamma_{i}>\varepsilon,

(9)

\displaystyle C_{1}<\gamma_{i}<C_{2},\;\;\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}>\varepsilon,

with
\varepsilon=0.2
, approximately equal to
\sqrt{2\,m\,(E_{\mathrm{H}}-E_{\mathrm{H}^{-}})}
. In order to improve the accuracy of the wave function, we used a doubled set of parameters
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}
. The values of these two sets of parameters were determined by minimizing the non-relativistic energy using the variational method. The coefficients of the linear combinations –
v_{i}
– were determined by solving the generalized eigenvalue problem. In order to reach the desired numerical accuracy, calculations were performed in quad-double precision arithmetic (with about 62 decimal digits).

All matrix elements are expressed in terms of rational, logarithmic, and dilogarithmic functions in
\alpha,\beta
, and
\gamma

Korobov 2002b
,
so they can be calculated very efficiently.
The size of the base set starting with 300 functions was increased by 300 functions in each step
to reach up to 1800 basis functions, yielding a non-relativistic energy of the ground
{}^{1}\mathrm{S}
state of the H
-
ion

E_{0}(1^{1}S_{0})=-0.527~751~016~544~377~196

(10)

which agrees with even more accurate results
Nakashima and Nakatsuji 2007
;
Aznabaev et al. 2018
;
Petrimoulx et al. 2025
up to all digits shown

While the convergence of the non-relativistic energy and the first-order matrix elements with the size of the basis set is very fast,
the accurate calculation of the second-order matrix elements is numerically much more challenging.
The inversion of the operator
E-H
is performed in a basis set of even parity functions with
l=0,1,2
of the form given in Eq. (
8
) as well as

\vec{\phi}(r_{1},r_{2},r)=\sum_{k}v_{k}\,\vec{r}_{1}\times\vec{r}_{2}\,[e^{-\alpha_{k}r_{1}-\beta_{k}r_{2}-\gamma_{k}r}-(r_{1}\leftrightarrow r_{2})]\,,

(11)

and

\displaystyle\phi^{ij}(r_{1},r_{2},r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}v_{k}\,[(r_{1}^{i}\,r_{1}^{j}-r_{1}^{2}\,\delta^{ij}/3)\

\displaystyle\times e^{-\alpha_{k}r_{1}-\beta_{k}r_{2}-\gamma_{k}r}-(r_{1}\leftrightarrow r_{2})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l}v_{l}\,(r^{i}\,r^{j}-r^{2}\,\delta^{ij}/3)

\displaystyle\times[e^{-\alpha_{l}r_{1}-\beta_{l}r_{2}-\gamma_{l}r}-(r_{1}\leftrightarrow r_{2})]

(12)

respectively. The values of the parameters
A_{i}
,
B_{i}
and
C_{i}
for the functions above are obtained by variational optimization of the functionals which are the symmetric second-order matrix elements with the corresponding operator standing on the right-hand side of the resolvent operators in the Equations (
II
) and (
II
). The number of basis functions in the
E-H
operator of the second-order matrix elements is doubled with respect to the number of basis functions used in the calculations of the energy and first-order matrix elements (starting with 600 and increasing by 600 functions up to 3600 basis functions).
Optimization of the perturbed wave functions is the most computationally demanding step of the presented calculations.

V
Summary

We have presented a highly accurate calculation of the nuclear magnetic shielding in the hydrogen anion using the NRQED theory. The magnetic shielding constant is obtained with an accuracy of nine-parts-per-trillion (9 ppt) by rigorously calculating finite nuclear mass and relativistic effects with high numerical accuracy, as well as by estimating the effects of the higher-order corrections. The final value of the constant of
1
H
-
is
\sigma=24.243\,189(9)\times 10^{-6}
.
This result will allow for a stringent test of the NRQED theory of magnetic shielding as well as for verification of CPT symmetry by comparison of proton-to-antiproton magnetic moments.

Additionally, this calculation illustrates the importance of the first-principle theoretical approach
for the effects of finite nuclear mass effects on the magnetic properties of light atoms.
Obtained results may be used to test and validate common quantum-chemical methods
for the calculation of magnetic properties of atoms and molecules by testing those methods
on the simple case of the nuclear magnetic shielding of H
-
.

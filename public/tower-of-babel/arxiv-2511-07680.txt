1.
Introduction

A central theme in arithmetic geometry concerns the structure of the group of rational points on algebraic varieties defined over number fields. For an abelian variety
A
over a number field
k
, the celebrated Mordell-Weil theorem asserts that the group
A(k)
is finitely generated
[
18
]
. Thus,
A(k)\cong A(k)_{\text{tors}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
A(k)_{\text{tors}}
is the finite torsion subgroup and
r=\rk A(k)
is the Mordell-Weil rank. Understanding the behavior of this rank, particularly for Jacobians
J_{C}
of algebraic curves
C
, remains a profound challenge. A fundamental open question asks whether
r(J_{C}(k))
is uniformly bounded as
C
varies over all curves of a fixed genus
g\geq 1
defined over
k
.

Even for elliptic curves (
g=1
) over
k=\mathbb{Q}
, this question is unresolved. While computational evidence reveals curves of high rank, theoretical heuristics suggest boundedness, possibly with rank exceeding 21 being rare
[
11
]
. Deep conjectures in Diophantine geometry provide conditional evidence for rank boundedness. Notably, Lang’s conjectures on rational points on varieties of general type
[
7
,
8
]
imply, via the work of Caporaso, Harris, and Mazur
[
1
,
2
]
, strong uniformity statements about the number of rational points on curves. Furthermore, Pasten demonstrated that conjectures on Diophantine approximation imply Honda’s conjecture on rank boundedness for elliptic curves
[
12
]
. The link between point counts and rank was solidified by Dimitrov, Gao, and Habegger
[
3
]
, making uniform point boundedness equivalent to uniform rank boundedness.

An alternative approach, pioneered by Yamagishi
[
21
]
for the family
y^{2}=ax^{4}+bx^{2}+c
, employs geometric methods. By constructing parameter spaces for curves with multiple rational points and demonstrating that these spaces (or relevant subvarieties) become varieties of general type, one can invoke Lang’s conjectures to deduce finiteness and uniformity properties, ultimately leading to rank bounds. This geometric strategy was adapted by the author in
[
16
]
to study the uniformity of Mordell-Weil rank of Jacobian variety of the family of curves

y^{s}=ax^{r}+b
.

This paper applies the same geometric methodology to investigate the closely related two-parameter family of curves defined by the affine equation

(1)

C_{a,b}:y^{s}=x(ax^{r}+b),

where
r\geq 1,s\geq 2
are fixed integers, and
k
is a number field containing
\zeta_{s}
, a primitive

-th root of unity. We focus on smooth curves

with genus

2.
Some conjectures and results in Diophantine Geometry

A smooth projective variety
X
over
\bar{k}
is of
general type
if its Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X)
equals its dimension.
For curves, this corresponds to genus
g\geq 2
. The seminal result in this area is Faltings’ theorem, formerly the Mordell conjecture, which states that:

If
C
is a smooth algebraic curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a number field
k
, then
\#C(K)<\infty
for any finite extension
K
of
k
.

Lang proposed a far-reaching generalization of this to higher dimensions.

Conjecture 2.1

(Lang,
[
7
,
8
]
)
.

Let
k
be a number field and
X
a smooth variety of general type over
k
.

(a)

(Weak)
The set of
k
-rational points
X(k)
is not Zariski dense in
X
.

(b)

(Strong)
There exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for any finite extension
K
of
k
, the set of
K
-rational points on
X\setminus Z
is finite.

Caporaso, Harris, and Mazur showed that Lang’s conjecture has profound uniformity implications for rational points on curves
[
1
,
2
]
, based on above conjectures.

Theorem 2.2

(Uniformity I,
[
1
]
)
.

The weak version of Lang’s conjecture implies that for every number field
k
and integer
g\geq 2
, there exists a number
N(k,g)
such that no curve of genus
g
defined over
k
has more than
N(k,g)
rational points.

Theorem 2.3

(Uniformity II,
[
1
]
)
.

The strong version of Lang’s conjecture implies that for any integer
g\geq 2
, there exists a number
N(g)
such that for every number field
k
, there are only finitely many curves
C
of genus
g
over
k
with
\#C(k)>N(g)
.

The following geometric theorem, which produces varieties of general type from families of curves, has an important role in the proof of the above uniformity results.

Theorem 2.4

(Correlation Theorem,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
be a proper morphism of integral varieties over
k
whose generic fiber is a smooth curve of genus
\geq 2
.
For any integer
m\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}}^{m}
be the
m
-th fiber product of
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathcal{B}
.
Then, for
m
sufficiently large,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{B}}^{m}
admits a dominant rational map to a variety of general type.

Rectenly, Dimitrov, Gao, and Habegger proved a excellent relation between the rank of a Jacobian and the number of rational points on the curve itself, using the technically machinery of Vojta’s method and hight inequalities.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
g\geq 1
and

be integers.
There exists a constant

such that if

is a smooth curve of genus

with
[k:\mathbb{Q}]\leq d

\#C(k)\leq c^{1+r(J_{C}(k))}.

3.1.
The Parameter Space
\mathcal{W}_{n}
via Twists

Consider the universal curve
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{(u,x,y)}\times_{k}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(a,b)}

n-1
forms of degree
d_{1}=\dots=d_{n-1}=s\geq 2
, we have

\gamma_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})\geq(d_{1}-1)d_{2}\cdots d_{n-1}=(s-1)s^{n-2}.

∎

Now, analogous to
[
16
, Thm 4.5]
), we have the following structural results on

\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
, based on the genus calculation.

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
\mathbf{a}_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{r,n}(k)
, we have the followings:

(1)

Case
s=2,n=2
: The genus is
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})=0
, hence
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}
is a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
k
-rational points; thus
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}(k)
is infinite.

(2)

Case
s=2,n=3
: The genus is
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{3}})=1
, hence
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{3}}
is an elliptic curve over
k
; thus
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{3}}(k)
can be infinite.

(3)

Case
s=3,n=2
: The genus is
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}})=1
, hence
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}
is an elliptic curve over
k
; thus
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}(k)
can be infinite.

(4)

For all other cases
(s,n)
with
n\geq 2
, the genus
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})\geq 2
hence
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}(k)
is finite by Falting’s Theorem.

For the high genus cases (

), we employ the theory of towers of curves
[
20
]
. For a fixed
\mathbf{a}=\lim\mathbf{a}_{n}
, consider the tower
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{a}}=(\{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}\}_{m\geq n_{0}},\{\phi_{m}\}_{m\geq n_{0}})
where

ensures

, and

is the projection forgetting the last coordinate
Y_{m+1}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k
be a number field. Let
n_{0}
be such that
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n_{0}}})\geq 2
. For any fixed
\mathbf{a}=\lim\mathbf{a}_{n}
where
\mathbf{a}_{m}\in\mathcal{U}_{r,m}(k)
for all
m
, the tower
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{a}}=\{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}\}_{m\geq n_{0}}
has infinite gonality. Consequently, for any sufficiently large
m
(depending on
\mathbf{a}
), the set
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}(k)
consists only of trivial points with respect to the tower
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
(2),
\gamma_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}})\geq(s-1)s^{m-2}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
. Thus, the tower
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
has infinite gonality. The conclusion then follows directly from the main results of Xarles in
[
20
]
applied with
d=1
.
∎

5.
Proofs of Finiteness and Uniformity Theorems

We now prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, using the properties of the fibers
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
and standard results and conjectures provided in Section
2
. Recall the correspondence given in Proposition
3.3
). The points in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
correspond bijectively to points in an open subset
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)\subset\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
. Trivial points in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
(in the sense of the tower) often correspond to degenerate curves or curves independent of some
\alpha_{i}
, and are typically excluded from
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
n_{0}=4
if
s=2
and
n_{0}=3
otherwise.
If
n<n_{0}
, then
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})\leq 1
by Theorem
4.2
. For suitable
\mathbf{a}_{n}
,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
can be infinite (either
\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
or an elliptic curve of positive rank), by
[
16
, Lemas 4.3 and 4.4]
.
This allows
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
to be infinite.

If
n\geq n_{0}
, then
g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})\geq 2
by Theorem
4.2
(4). Hence, the Faltings’ Theorem implies that
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
is finite for any given
\mathbf{a}_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{r,n}(k)
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
corresponds to a subset
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)\subset\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
must also be finite.
The boundedness of rank for
n\geq n_{0}
follows from the finiteness of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
: there is a maximum rank among the finitely many Jacobians
J_{C}
for
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume Weak Lang holds (Conjecture
2.1
). Let
n\geq n_{0}
, so
g=g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})\geq 2
. The curves
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
form a family
\mathcal{X}_{n}\to\mathcal{U}_{r,n}
over
k
, where the base
\mathcal{U}_{r,n}
is an open subset of
\Sym^{n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cong\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. By Uniformity I (Theorem
2.2
), there exists a bound
N(k,g)
depending only on
k
and the genus
g=g(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}})
, such that
\#\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)\leq N(k,g)
for all
\mathbf{a}_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{r,n}(k)
. Since
\#\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)\leq\#\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}(k)
, the bound
M_{0}=N(k,g)
suffices. Note that
g
depends on
n,s
.

For the second part, assume
g=g_{r,s}(C)\geq 2
. Consider the family
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(a,b)}
for the original curves
y^{s}=x(ax^{r}+b)
. The generic fiber has genus
g\geq 2
. By the Correlation Theorem
2.4
, for
m
large enough, the
(m+1)
-fold fiber product
\mathcal{C}^{m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
maps dominantly to a variety
W
of general type. A curve
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}(k)
corresponds to a point
[a:b]
and
m+1
points
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
on
C_{a,b}
. This yields a
k
-rational point on
\mathcal{C}^{m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, hence on
W
. By Weak Lang (Conjecture
2.1
),
W(k)
is not Zariski dense. The uniformity theorem derived from Weak Lang (Theorem
2.2
), applied to the fibers
C_{a,b}
, states there is a bound
N(k,g)
such that
\#C_{a,b}(k)\leq N(k,g)
. If we choose
m_{0}
such that
m_{0}+1>N(k,g)
, then no curve can have
m_{0}+1
points
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}(k)
must be empty for all
m\geq m_{0}
.
If Strong Lang holds, Theorem
2.3
gives
N(g)
independent of
k
, making
m_{0}
independent of
k
.
∎

6.
Proof of the Main Theorem

The proof combines the uniformity established in Theorem
1.3
with the crucial link between point counts and rank.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
k
be a number field,
[k:\mathbb{Q}]=d
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of smooth curves
C:y^{s}=x(ax^{r}+b)
over
k
with genus
g=g_{r,s}(C)\geq 1
. Assume, for contradiction, that the rank
r(J_{C}(k))
is unbounded for
C\in\mathcal{F}
.
Then there exists a sequence of non-isomorphic curves
C_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
r_{j}=r(J_{C_{j}}(k))\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
.
By the result of Dimitrov, Gao, and Habegger (Theorem
2.5
), there is a constant
c=c(g,d)
such that
\#C_{j}(k)\leq c^{1+r_{j}}
. Since
r_{j}\to\infty
, it follows that
\#C_{j}(k)\to\infty
.

Now, assume the Strong Lang Conjecture (Conjecture
2.1
). This implies the strong version of Theorem
1.3
: there exists an integer
m_{0}
(depending only on
r,s
, possibly
g
, but not
k
) such that for any
m\geq m_{0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{m}}(K)=\emptyset
for any number field
K
and any valid
\mathbf{a}_{m}\in\mathcal{U}_{r,m}(K)
. This means no smooth curve in the family (
1
) over any number field
K
can possess
m+1
points
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
where the
\alpha_{i}
are distinct, non-zero, and satisfy
\alpha_{i}^{r}\neq\alpha_{j}^{r}
, provided
m\geq m_{0}
.

Since
\#C_{j}(k)\to\infty
, we can choose an index
j_{0}
large enough such that
\#C_{j_{0}}(k)>s\cdot(m_{0}+1)+1
. The curve
C_{j_{0}}
has at most one point with
x=0
(namely
(0,0)
). For any non-zero
x
-coordinate
\alpha
, there are at most
s
corresponding
y
-coordinates
\beta
. Thus, the number of distinct non-zero
x
-coordinates among points in
C_{j_{0}}(k)
is at least
\lceil(\#C_{j_{0}}(k)-1)/s\rceil
. Since
\#C_{j_{0}}(k)\to\infty
, this quantity eventually exceeds
m_{0}+1
.
Therefore, we can select
m_{0}+1
points
P_{0},\dots,P_{m_{0}}
from
C_{j_{0}}(k)
whose
x
-coordinates
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{m_{0}}
are distinct and non-zero. Let
\mathbf{a}_{m_{0}}=\{\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{m_{0}}\}
. Since
C_{j_{0}}
is smooth, its parameters
[a:b]
are such that the discriminant conditions are met, which typically ensures
\alpha_{i}^{r}\neq\alpha_{j_{0}}^{r}
if
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j_{0}}
(unless
r=0
, but
r\geq 1
). Thus,
\mathbf{a}_{m_{0}}
is a valid configuration in
\mathcal{U}_{r,m_{0}}(k)
.
By definition, the curve
C_{j_{0}}
belongs to the set
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{m_{0}}}(k)
.
However, since
m_{0}\geq m_{0}
, the strong uniformity result implies
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}_{m_{0}}}(k)
must be empty. This is a contradiction.
The initial assumption that the rank is unbounded must be false. Therefore,
r(J_{C}(k))
is uniformly bounded for
C\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

7.
Application of the Results on Elliptic Curves

The general family of curves
C_{a,b}:y^{s}=x(ax^{r}+b)
investigated herein contains an important family of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, containing at least one 2-torsion point
(0,0).

By specializing our results, we provide new geometric evidence for uniformity conjectures related to these curves. We focus on the case
s=2
and
r=2
, which corresponds to the one-parameter family
E_{B}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+B)
. The largest know rank in this family is 14, due to M. Watkins, according to
[
4
]
. We will consider this curve in the next section.

A central case of interest in this family is the congruent number elliptic curve
E_{N}:y^{2}=x^{3}-N^{2}x
over
k=\mathbb{Q}
. A positive integer
N
is a congruent number if and only if
E_{N}(\mathbb{Q})
has positive rank. The largest know rank congruent number elliptic curve is 7. Note that there are only 27 case of known congruent number elliptic curve of rank 7, by the recent huge computational work
[
19
, Table 5]
.
But, the question of whether these ranks are (uniformly) bounded as
N
varies is a major open problem.
Our Theorem
1.1
provides a direct conditional answer.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume the strong version of Lang’s conjecture (Conjecture
2.1
) holds. Then the Mordell-Weil ranks

r(E_{B}(\mathbb{Q}))
of elliptic curves
E_{B}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+B)
are uniformly bounded as
B
varies over all integers.

Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
apply directly to this family for a
fixed
set of points
a_{n}=\{\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

For any fixed
n\geq 4
and distinct non-zero rational numbers
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n}
, the set of elliptic curves
E_{B}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+B)
, in particular congruent number curves, having
(n+1)
points with
x
-coordinates
\alpha_{i}
’s is finite; and hence their rank is bounded assuming Weak Lang’s Conjecture.

Note that the second part of Theorem
1.3
, which establishes emptiness, does not apply, as that part requires the generic curve
C
to have genus
g\geq 2
, whereas elliptic curves have
g=1
.

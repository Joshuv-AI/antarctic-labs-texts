Mechanistic insights into the interaction between optineurin with RAB8A
Authors: Jing Zhang, Lifeng Pan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOPTN (optineurin), an amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)-associated modifier, plays vital roles in autophagy and cellular vesicular transport in mammals. OPTN can associate with RAB8A and the GTPase-activating protein TBC1D17, and facilitate the negative regulation of RAB8A by TBC1D17 (TBC domain family member 17). Recently, we reported the biochemical and structural characterizations of the interactions between OPTN, RAB8A and TBC1D17. We determined the crystal structure of the leucine-zipper domain (LZD) of OPTN with the GTP-bound active RAB8A and uncovered the molecular mechanism underpinning the specific interaction of OPTN with RAB8A. Moreover, we revealed that OPTN LZD and the TBC (Tre-2/Bub2/Cdc16) domain of TBC1D17 competitively bind to active RAB8A, while the central coiled-coil domain of OPTN and the active RAB8A can simultaneously interact with TBC1D17 TBC. In summary, our study provided mechanistic insights into the interaction of OPTN with RAB8A, and revealed the interaction relationship among OPTN, RAB8A and TBC1D17.KEYWORDS: RAB8A, optineurin, TBC1D17, Vesicular transport

Intracellular vesicular transports play critical roles in the communications between different intracellular compartments in mammalian cells. RAB8, a Rab family small GTPase, regulates different vesicle trafficking events as well as the secretory autophagy. As a small GTPase, RAB8 can interconvert between two distinct forms, a GTP-bound active state and a GDP-bound inactive state, a switch catalyzed by specific guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) and GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), such as TBC1D17.

OPTN (optineurin) is a multifunctional adaptor protein, and can specifically recognize different binding partners including TBK1 (TANK binding kinase 1), members of the mammalian ATG8 (autophagy-related 8) protein family, RB1CC1 (RB1 inducible coiled-coil 1), RAB8, ATG9A (autophagy-related 9), TBC1D17, MYO6 (myosin VI), HTT (huntingtin) and ubiquitin via different domains to regulate multiple cellular processes, including selective autophagy, vesicular trafficking, inflammatory and interferon signaling. Specifically, OPTN forms a dimer in vivo, and can bind to the GTP-bound active RAB8 through its N-terminal leucine-zipper domain (LZD) to participate in intracellular vesicular trafficking. Moreover, OPTN can mediate the cellular association of the GAP protein TBC1D17 and RAB8, facilitating the negative regulation of RAB8 by TBC1D17. However, how OPTN specifically recognizes RAB8 and TBC1D17 as well as the detailed relationship between TBC1D17 and RAB8 in binding to OPTN remained largely unknown. Importantly, mutations in the OPTN gene are associated with many neurodegenerative diseases, such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and glaucoma. Several missense mutations in the N-terminal leucine-zipper domain (LZD) of OPTN have been associated with ALS but their impact on OPTN function is still elusive.

In our recent study1, we biochemically and structurally characterized the interaction between OPTN and RAB8A, one of the two mammalian RAB8 isoforms. Based on our biochemical experiments, we firstly confirmed that OPTN specifically recognizes the GTP-bound active RAB8A through its LZD region. Subsequently, we determined the crystal structure of the GTP-bound RAB8A/OPTN LZD complex and uncovered the key binding determinants for the specific interaction between these two proteins. In particular, we found that two RAB8A molecules symmetrically bind to one OPTN LZD dimer, forming a unique 2:2 stoichiometric hetero-tetramer (Figure 1). Further structural analyses provided a mechanistic explanation why OPTN LZD specifically recognizes the active rather than inactive RAB8A. In addition, our structural comparison analyses unraveled that the assembly of the OPTN LZD-RAB8A complex is distinct from that of the currently known RILPL2 (Rab-interacting lysosomal protein - like 2)-RAB8A complex, thereby enriching the repertoire of the RAB8A effector binding modes.

Three disease-associated missense mutations, the A136V, G159V, and V161M mutations, have been reported to localize in the OPTN LZD region. Based on the determined OPTN LZD-RAB8A complex structure and with specific biochemical experiments, we showed that these disease-associated mutants of OPTN do not affect the binding affinity between OPTN and RAB8A. However, the V161M mutation of OPTN can alter the binding mode between OPTN and Rab8A. It is worthwhile mentioning that the LZD region of OPTN is implicated in binding to ATG9A (autophagy-related 9A) during selective autophagy. Whether these disease-associated mutants of OPTN LZD could affect the interaction between OPTN and ATG9A or other proteins remains to be elucidated.

In addition to binding active RAB8A, OPTN also interacts with TBC1D17 through its central region. Our biochemical results demonstrated that TBC1D17 TBC can directly compete with OPTN LZD for binding to the active RAb8A. However, TBC1D17 TBC can simultaneously bind to the active RAB8A and the central region of OPTN (residues 300-412) through two distinct binding sites. Overall, we proposed that the membrane-attached active RAB8A can recruit OPTN to the endocytic recycling compartment via binding to OPTN LZD. Then, OPTN can recruit TBC1D17 through its central coiled-coil domain positioned before the ubiquitin binding in ABIN and NEMO (UBAN) domain of OPTN, facilitating the recognition and inactivation of the GTP-bound RAB8A by TBC1D17.

In conclusion, we elucidated the molecular mechanism underlying the selective recognition of the GTP-bound RAB8A by OPTN through systematically biochemical and structural studies.

Curvature operator and Euler number
Teng Huang, Qiang Tan

Abstract
Let ( X , g ) (X,g) be a compact n n -dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with a lower bound on the average of the lowest n − p n-p eigenvalues of the curvature operator and the diameter of X X is bounded above by D > 0 D>0 . In this article, we investigate the relationship between the curvature operator and the Euler number of X X . Our analysis is based on more general vanishing theorems for a Dirac operator associated with a smooth 1 1 -form on X X . As a consequence, we obtain partial affirmative answers to Question 4.6 posed by Herrmann, Sebastian, and Tuschmann in [ 16 ] . Specifically, we prove that if a compact 2 ​ m 2m -dimensional manifold admits an almost nonnegative curvature operator (ANCO) and has a nontrivial first de Rham cohomology group, then its Euler number vanishes. Furthermore, in the case where m = 2 m=2 , we show that the Euler number is nonnegative. This result provides a complete resolution to their question in the four-dimensional setting.

1
Introduction

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with
n\geq 3
. Understanding the relationship between curvature and the topology of a Riemannian manifold is a fundamental theme in Riemannian geometry. Specifically, investigating the connections between Ricci curvature and the topology of a Riemannian manifold is one of the central topics in differential geometry. A classical theorem by Bochner states that if the Ricci curvature
Ric(g)
of
X
is nonnegative, then the first Betti number
b_{1}(X)
of
X
satisfies
b_{1}(X)\leq n
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
X
is isometric to a flat torus.

Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{n}{2}}
denote the eigenvalues of the curvature operator of
(X,g)
. Berger
[
2
]
and Meyer
[
22
]
established vanishing results for the Betti numbers of manifolds with positive curvature operators, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}>0
, and in particular Meyer showed that they are rational (co)homology spheres. A smooth compact manifold
X
is said to admit almost nonnegative curvature operator (ANCO) if there exists a sequence of Riemannian metrics
\{g_{i}\}
on
X
such that all eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}=\lambda(g_{i})
of the associated curvature operator and the diameter
{\rm{diam}}(g_{i})
of
g_{i}
satisfy

\lambda_{i}\geq-\frac{1}{i},\ {\rm{diam}}(g_{i})\leq 1.

This definition was introduced by Herrmann-Sebastian-Tuschmann in
[
16
]
. In
[
16
]
, the authors proposed the following question:

Question 4.6
Do manifolds with almost nonnegative curvature operator have nonnegative Euler number?

Fix constants
\kappa<0
,
D>0
,
\Lambda>0
and
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Suppose that

\frac{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-p}}{n-p}\geq\kappa,\ {\rm{diam}}(X)\leq D,\ and\ -\kappa D^{2}=\Lambda^{2}.

Peter and Wink
[
26
]
established a more general vanishing and estimation theorem for the
p
-th Betti number of compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds with
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-p}\geq 0
. As a consequence, they proved that manifolds with
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
-positive curvature operators are rational homology spheres. In
[
29
]
, the author studied the upper bound of the Betti numbers of a compact Riemannian manifold given integral bounds on the average of the lowest eigenvalues of the curvature operator.

Throughout our article, we denote by
\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
the class of compact Riemannian
n
-manifolds
X
, where

•

the lower bound on the average of the lowest
n-p
eigenvalues of the curvature operator is greater than or equal to
\kappa
;

•

the diameter satisfies
D(g)\leq D
;

•

-\kappa D^{2}=\Lambda^{2}
.

Every compact smooth Riemannian manifold belong to
\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
for some
\kappa
and
D
.

Based on work of Li
[
20
]
, Petersen and Wink
[
26
]
generalized the Bochner technique and obtained estimation results for the Betti numbers of the compact Riemannian manifold
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
. The case of
\lambda_{1}\geq\kappa
was also proved by Gallot
[
12
]
. In
[
26
]
, the authors proved that for sufficiently small
\Lambda
, the Betti numbers of
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfy
b_{k}(X)\leq\binom{n}{k}
for all
k\leq p
. Gromov
[
14
]
established similar bounds on the Betti numbers in the context of sectional curvature.

However, these estimates of Betti numbers do not provide information about the Euler number
\chi(X)
of
X
. This leads us to a natural question:

Question

what is the Euler number of the
2n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
when
\Lambda
is sufficiently small?
In particular, does the manifold
X\in\mathfrak{X}(1,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
with
\Lambda
small enough necessarily have
\chi(X)\geq 0
?

This question generalizes the Question 4.6 proposed by Herrmann-Sebastian-Tuschmann. Our first mail result in this article establishes a connection between the eigenvalues of the curvature operator and the Euler number of
X
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
2n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group. There exists a uniform positive constant
C(n)
such that if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfies

\Lambda\leq C(n),

then the Euler number of
X
satisfies

(1)
\chi(X)=0
, when
p=n
;

(2)
(-1)^{n}\chi(X)\geq 0
, when
p=n-1
.

Remark 1.2
.

The assumption of a nontrivial first cohomology group in Theorem
1.1
is essential. For example, let
(X,g)
be a compact
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n
-positive curvature operator. According to
[
26
, Theorem A]
, the Betti number
b_{p}(X)=0
for
0<p<2n
, so the Euler number of
X
satisfies
\chi(X)=2
. However, such
X
is in
\mathfrak{X}(n,0,D,0)
.

While our results provide only partial affirmative answers to Question 4.6 proposed by Herrmann-Sebastian-Tuschmann in general dimensions, we obtain a complete resolution in the
4
-dimensional case.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
4
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. There exists a uniform positive constant
C
such that if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(1,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfies

\Lambda\leq C,

then the Euler number of
X
satisfies
\chi(X)\geq 0
.

Remark 1.4
.

Actually, we observe that when dealing with 1-forms, we only need to use the properties of Ricci curvature. In fact, if the Ricci curvature of a compact manifold
X
satisfies

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\geq-C,

where
C
is a uniform positive constant, then there exists some
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\neq 0
such that
H^{1}(X,t\theta)=0
. By a similar argument as in Corollary
1.3
, we can also prove that
\chi(X)\geq 0
when
\dim X=4
.

For a smooth Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
, let
{\rm{Iso}}(X,g)
denotes the group of Riemannian isometries
f:(X,g)\rightarrow(X,g)
. The Lie algebra of
{\rm{Iso}}(X,g)
is spanned by the Killing vectors of
(X,g)
. A classical theorem of Bochner asserts that the isometry group of a compact Riemannian manifold with negative Ricci curvature is finite (see
[
3
]
). Several authors have also attempted to estimate the order of
{\rm{Iso}}(X,g)
under the assumption of negative Ricci curvature (see
[
9
,
17
,
18
]
).

We denote by
\theta^{\sharp}
the Killing vector field on
X
, and the dual
1
-form
\theta
with respect to
\theta^{\sharp}
is always non-closed. In the second part of our article, we will focus on the study of Killing vector fields on a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature that is bounded from above.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with nonzero Killing vector field
\theta^{\sharp}
. There exists a positive constant
C(n,\Lambda)
such that if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfies

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\leq C(n,\Lambda),

then the Euler number vanishes, i.e.,
\chi(X)=0
.

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
8
]
, the authors studied certain Dirac operators on a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold under the assumption that
X
has either nonzero Euler characteristic or nonzero signature. They proved that for given positive numbers
2p>n
,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
if there exists a metric
g
on
X
satisfying

-\lambda_{1}\leq Ric(g)\leq\varepsilon,\ diam(g)\leq 1,\frac{1}{{\rm{Vol}}(g)}\int_{X}|\mathfrak{R}|^{p}\leq\lambda_{2}

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(p,n,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is a positive constant, then the isometry group of Riemannian metric
g
on
X
is finite. In our conditions stated in Theorem
1.5
, the constant
\lambda_{1}
obeys
\lambda_{1}=-(n-1)\kappa
. However, it is not possible to provide a uniform upper bound for
\lambda_{2}
with respect to
\kappa,D,n
and
p
. Thus, our results differ somewhat from theirs.

Recall that the curvature operator of a smooth Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
is called
l
-nonnegative if the sum of its lowest
l
eigenvalues is nonnegative. Furthermore, if
(X,g)
is a compact
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n
-positive curvature operator, then according to
[
26
, Theorem A]
, we have
\chi(X)=2
. We then have

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
2n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. Suppose that the curvature operator of
X
obey one of the following condtions:

(1) the curvature operator is
n
-positive;

(2) the curvature operator is
n
-nonnegative and
X
has restricted holonomy
SO(2n)
.

If the Ricci curvature satisfies

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\leq C(n),

where
C(n)
is a uniform positive constant, then the isometry group of Riemannian metric
g
on
X
is finite.

Remark 1.8
.

The second condition in Corollary
1.7
is sharp. For example, the
2n
-dimensional torus
T^{2n}
has
\chi(T^{2n})=0
and admits a flat metric with zero curvature eigenvalues. In this case, the flat metric on
T^{2n}
has a trivial holonomy group but an infinite isometry group.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Curvature operator

Let’s first recall the definition of the curvature operator and the latest research progress in this area by Petersen and Wink
[
26
]
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z
denote its curvature tensor. The Weitzenböck curvature operator acting on a
(0,k)
-tensor
T
is defined by

Ric(T)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(R(X_{i},e_{j})T)(X_{1},\cdots,e_{j},\cdots,X_{k}).

We use a variation of the Ricci tensor to symbolize this as it is the Ricci tensor when evaluated on vector fields and
1
-forms. The Lichnerowicz Laplacian is then given by

\Delta_{L}T=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla T+cRic(T)

for some constant
c>0
. Notably, the Hodge Laplacian on differential forms corresponds to the case
c=1
.

For a
(0,k)
-tensor and
L\in\mathfrak{so}(TX)
, Petersen and Wink defined the action

(LT)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}T(X_{1},\cdots,LX_{i},\cdots,X_{k}).

Noting that a
(0,k)
-tensor
T
can be changed to a tensor
\hat{T}
with values in
\Lambda^{2}TX
. Implicitly this works as follows

g(L,\hat{T}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}))=(LT)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}),

for all
L\in\mathfrak{so}(TX)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
26
, Proposition 1.4]
)
Let
R:\Lambda^{2}T_{p}X\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}T_{p}X
be the curvature operator and
\Xi_{i}
an othonormal basis for
\Lambda^{2}T_{p}X
. If
\Xi_{i}
is an eigenbasis of
R
and
\lambda_{i}
denote the corresponding eigenvalues, then

g(Ric(T),T)=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\Xi_{i}T|^{2}.

(2.1)

Petersen and Wink
[
26
]
observed that for any
p
-form
\alpha
(
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
),

|\Xi_{i}\alpha|^{2}\leq p|\alpha|^{2}

while

\sum_{i}|\Xi_{i}T|^{2}=p(n-p)|\alpha|^{2}.

They also observed the following lemma which provides a general way to control the curvature term of the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
\Delta_{L}
on
(0,k)
-tensors.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
)
Let
\mathfrak{R}:\Lambda^{2}V\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}V
be an algebraic curvature operator with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{n}{2}}
and let
T\in T^{(0,k)}(V)
. Suppose there is
C\geq 1
such that

|LT|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{C}|\hat{T}|^{2}|L|^{2}

for all
L\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
.

Let
\kappa\leq 0
. If
\frac{1}{\lfloor C\rfloor}(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\lfloor C\rfloor})\geq\kappa
, then
g(\mathfrak{R}(\hat{T}),\hat{T})\geq\kappa|\hat{T}|^{2}
and if
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\lfloor C\rfloor}>0
, then
g(\mathfrak{R}(\hat{T}),\hat{T})>0
unless
\hat{T}=0
.

We then have

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold, and let
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{n}{2}}
denote the eigenvalues of the curvature operator of
(X,g)
. Fix
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. If

\frac{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-p}}{n-p}\geq\kappa.

then for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(X)
,
k\leq p
or
k\geq n-p
,

g(Ric(\alpha),\alpha)\geq\kappa|\hat{\alpha}|^{2}=\kappa k(n-k)|\alpha|^{2}.

(2.2)

Proof.

By
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
, any
k
-form
\alpha
satisfies

|L\alpha|^{2}\leq\min\{k,n-k\}|\alpha|^{2}|L|^{2}

for all
L\in\mathfrak{so}(TX)
. Following
[
26
, Proposition 2.5]
, we also have

|\hat{\alpha}|^{2}=k(n-k)|\alpha|^{2}.

For the case
k\leq p
, we have

|L\alpha|^{2}\leq k|\alpha|^{2}|L|^{2}=\frac{1}{n-k}|\hat{\alpha}|^{2}|L|^{2}

For the case
k\geq n-p
, we have

|L\alpha|^{2}\leq(n-k)|\alpha|^{2}|L|^{2}=\frac{1}{k}|\hat{\alpha}|^{2}|L|^{2}.

Let
\tilde{k}=\max\{k,n-k\}\geq n-p
. Then

|L\alpha|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{k}}|\hat{\alpha}|^{2}|L|^{2}

and the eigenvalues of the curvature operator satisfy

\frac{1}{\tilde{k}}(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\tilde{k}})\geq\frac{1}{n-p}(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-p})\geq\kappa.

Applying Lemma
2.2
yields the inequality (
2.2
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Since the Ricci curvature is bounded from below by the sum of the lowest
(n-1)
eigenvalues of the curvature operator,
Ric(X)\geq(n-1)\kappa
.

2.2
Poincaré inequality

In this section, we recall the following Poincaré inequality (see
[
1
,
6
]
). For
1\leq q
, let
p
satisfies
1\leq p\leq\frac{nq}{n-q}
and
p<\infty
. Define the Sobolev constant
\Sigma(n,p,q)
of the canonical unit sphere
S^{n}
by

\Sigma(n,p,q)=\sup\{\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}}}{\|df\|_{L^{q}}}:f\in L^{q}_{1}(S^{n}),f\neq 0,\int_{S^{n}}f=0\}.

If the Ricci curvature of the compact Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
satisfies

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\geq-(n-1)b^{2},

where
D(g)
is the diameter of
g
, then there exists a positive number
R(b)=\frac{D(g)}{bC(b)}
(see
[
1
, Appendix I, Theorem 2]
), where
C(b)
is the unique positive root of the equation

x\int_{0}^{b}(\cosh t+x\sinh t)^{n-1}dt=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}tdt,

such that the following Poincaré inequality holds (see
[
1
, Page 397]
or
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\geq-(n-1)b^{2},

for some constant
b>0
. Then for each
1\leq p\leq\frac{nq}{n-q}
,
p<\infty
and
f\in L^{q}_{1}(X)
, we have

\begin{split}&\|f-\frac{1}{{\rm{Vol}}(g)}\int_{X}f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq S_{p,q}\|df\|_{L^{q}(X)},\\
&\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq S_{p,q}\|df\|_{L^{q}(X)}+{\rm{Vol}}(g)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(X)},\\
\end{split}

where
{\rm{Vol}}(g)
is the volume of
(X,g)
,
S(p,q)=(\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(g)}{{\rm{Vol}}(S^{n})})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}R(b)\Sigma(n,p,q)
.

Chen provided the following lower bound estimate for
bC(b)
as
b\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 5.6]
)
Let
C(b)
be the unique positive root of the equation

x\int_{0}^{b}(\cosh t+x\sinh t)^{n-1}dt=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}tdt.

Then

\lim\inf_{b\rightarrow 0}bC(b)\geq a_{n}>0

for some constant
a_{n}
depending only on
n
. In particular, there is a positive constant
c_{1}(n)
such that if

b\leq c_{1}(n),

then
bC(b)\geq a_{n}
.

3
Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
on Riemannian manifold

3.1
Index of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. We denote by
\Omega^{p}(X)
the spaces of real differential
p
-forms for
0\leq p\leq n
. To simplify notation, we often identify real vector fields with real
1
-forms using the isomorphisms. Given a smooth
1
-form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(X)
, the vector field
\theta^{\sharp}
on
X
is characterized by the condition
\theta(V)=g(V,\theta^{\sharp})
, for all vector filed
V
on
X
. We denote the contraction by the vector field
\theta^{\sharp}
as
i_{\theta^{\sharp}}
. The Hodge star operator with respect to metric
g
is denoted by
\ast
. We define the differential operator
d_{\theta}:\Omega^{k}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(X)
as

d_{\theta}=d+\theta\wedge.

Its formal adjoint
d^{\ast}_{\theta}:\Omega^{k}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{k-1}(X)
is given by

d_{\theta}^{\ast}=d^{\ast}+i_{\theta^{\sharp}}.

Remark 3.1
.

It is important to note that the differential
d_{\theta}
does not satisfy the crucial property of
(d_{\theta})^{2}=0
required for introducing cohomology groups (see Section
4.1
). In fact, we have
(d_{\theta})^{2}=d\theta
.

Define the twisted Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
acting on differential forms by

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}=d_{\theta}+d^{\ast}_{\theta}:\Omega^{+}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{-}(X),

where
\Omega^{+}(X)=\oplus_{k=even}\Omega^{k}(X)
and
\Omega^{-}(X)=\oplus_{k=odd}\Omega^{k}(X)
. Recall that for the operator
d+d^{\ast}
, the index computes the Euler number:

\begin{split}{\rm{Index}}(d+d^{\ast})&=\dim\ker(d+d^{\ast})-\dim{\rm{coker}}(d+d^{\ast})\\
&=\dim\bigoplus_{p=even}\mathcal{H}^{k}(X)-\dim\bigoplus_{p=odd}\mathcal{H}^{k}(X)\\
&=\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}b_{k}(X)\\
&=\chi(X).\end{split}

We then have

Lemma 3.2
.

The index of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
satisfies

{\rm{Index}}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}):=\dim\ker(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})-\dim{\rm{coker}}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})=\chi(X).

Moreover, if
d\theta=0
, then

\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{p}\dim\ker d_{\theta}\cap\ker d^{\ast}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{p}(X)=\chi(X).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
is elliptic, its index depends only on the principal symbol, which coincides with that of
d+d^{\ast}
. Thus
{\rm{Index}}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})=\chi(X).

When
(d_{\theta})^{2}=0
, we obtain

\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(X)=\bigoplus_{p=even/odd}\ker d_{\theta}\cap\ker d^{\ast}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{p}(X).

Therefore

\begin{split}{\rm{Index}}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})&=\sum_{p=even}\dim\ker d_{\theta}\cap\ker d^{\ast}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{p}(X)-\sum_{p=odd}\dim\ker d_{\theta}\cap\ker d^{\ast}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{p}(X)\\
&=\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{p}\dim\ker d_{\theta}\cap\ker d^{\ast}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{p}(X).\end{split}

∎

3.2
Integral formula

We begin by establishing a fundamental integral identity that plays a central role in our analysis. In
[
6
,
8
]
, the authors also derived some similar integral equalities for some certain Dirac operators, but our proof method differs from their.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
10
]
)
For any
u,v\in\Omega^{p}(X)
, we have a pointwise identity as follows:

\int_{X}\langle L_{\theta^{\sharp}}u,v\rangle=\int_{X}\langle i_{\theta^{\sharp}}u,d^{\ast}v\rangle+\int_{X}\langle du,\theta\wedge v\rangle=\int_{X}\langle\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}u,v\rangle+\int_{X}p\langle(\nabla\theta^{\sharp})u,v\rangle,

(3.1)

where
L_{\theta^{\sharp}}:=di_{\theta^{\sharp}}+i_{\theta^{\sharp}}d
is the Lie derivative in the direction
\theta^{\sharp}
and

[(\nabla\theta^{\sharp})u](e_{1},\cdots,e_{p})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i}u(e_{1},\cdots,\nabla_{e_{i}}\theta^{\sharp},\cdots,e_{p}).

Theorem 3.4
.

(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 4.1 and Corllary 4.3]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold and
\theta^{\sharp}
a smooth vector field on
X
. Then for each
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(X)
, there is a positive constant
C_{1}(n)
such that

\int_{X}|\theta^{\sharp}|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}\leq C_{1}(n)\int_{X}|\nabla\theta^{\sharp}|\cdot|\alpha|^{2}.

Proof.

We only prove the case of
n=even
. Let
\alpha=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\alpha_{2k}\in\Omega^{+}(X)
be a smooth differential form on
X
, where
\alpha_{2k}\in\Omega^{2k}(X)
. The condition
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
implies

(d+d^{\ast})\alpha=-\theta\wedge\alpha-i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha.

We then have

\begin{split}\int_{X}\langle L_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,\alpha\rangle&=\int_{X}\langle i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,d^{\ast}\alpha\rangle+\int_{X}\langle d\alpha,\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle\\
&=\int_{X}\langle i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,-d\alpha-\theta\wedge\alpha-i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha\rangle+\int_{X}\langle-d^{\ast}\alpha-\theta\wedge\alpha-i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle\\
&=-\|i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha\|^{2}-\int_{X}\langle\alpha,\theta\wedge d\alpha\rangle-\|\theta\wedge\alpha\|^{2}-\int_{X}\langle\alpha,d(\theta\wedge\alpha)\rangle\\
&=-\|i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha\|^{2}-\|\theta\wedge\alpha\|^{2}-\int_{X}\langle\alpha,d\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle\\
&=-\int_{X}|\theta^{\sharp}|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}-\int_{X}\langle\alpha,d\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle,\\
\end{split}

where we used the identity

|\theta\wedge\beta|^{2}+|i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\beta|^{2}=|\theta^{\sharp}|^{2}|\beta|^{2},\ \forall\beta\in\Omega^{p}(X).

On the other hand, following Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{X}\langle L_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,\alpha\rangle&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}\langle L_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}\langle i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha_{2k},d^{\ast}\alpha_{2k}\rangle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}\langle d\alpha_{2k},\theta\wedge\alpha_{2k}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}\langle\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}2k\langle(\nabla\theta^{\sharp})\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle\\
\end{split}

Given
u,v\in\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
we have (see
[
23
, Example 2.1.13]
)

L_{\theta^{\sharp}}\langle u,v\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}u,v\rangle+\langle u,\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}v\rangle.

We set
u=v
. Integrating over
X
and using the divergence formula we deduce

2\int_{X}\langle\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}u,u\rangle=\int_{X}1\cdot L_{\theta^{\sharp}}\langle u,u\rangle=-\int_{X}({\rm{div}}\theta^{\sharp})\langle u,u\rangle.

Combining above identities yield that

\begin{split}\int_{X}|\theta^{\sharp}|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}&=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}\langle\nabla_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{X}2k\langle(\nabla\theta^{\sharp})\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle-\int_{X}\langle\alpha,d\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle\\
&\leq C_{1}(n)\int_{X}|\nabla\theta^{\sharp}|\cdot|\alpha|^{2}.\\
\end{split}

∎

\begin{split}-|\alpha|\Delta_{d}|\alpha|&\geq-\langle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\alpha,\alpha\rangle\\

(3.6)

4
Curvature operator with small lower bounded

Let
\theta
be a smooth closed
1
-form on a compact manifold
X
. The closedness of
\theta
implies
d_{\theta}^{2}=0
, which means that
d_{\theta}
gives rise to a cohomology, denoted by
H^{\ast}(X,\theta)
, known as the Morse-Novikov cohomology. When
[\theta]=0\in H^{1}_{dR}(X)
, the Morse-Novikov cohomology group
H^{p}(X,\theta)
is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology group
H^{p}_{dR}(X)
.
While Morse-Novikov cohomology depends on the choice of
\theta
, the Euler number of the Morse-Novikov cohomology is equal to the Euler number
\chi(X)
of the manifold. The Morse-Novikov cohomology also plays a significant role in the study of manifold topology. Although the relationship between curvature and de Rham cohomology is classical, the interplay between curvature and Morse-Novikov cohomology remains less explored.

Pioneering work by Chen
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
established integral formulas and estimates for
\Delta_{\theta}
-harmonic forms. In this section, we extend Chen’s ideas to study the topology of manifolds
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
. We establish a vanishing result for certain Morse-Novikov cohomology groups with respect to
t\theta
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional,
(n\geq 3)
, smooth Riemannian manifold with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group. There exists a positive constant
C(n,\Lambda)
such that if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfies

Ric(g)D^{2}(g)\geq-C(n,\Lambda),

then there exists some
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\neq 0
such that
H^{k}(X,t\theta)=0
for all
k\leq p
, where
H^{k}(X,t\theta)
is the Morse-Novikov
p
-th cohomology group with respect to
t\theta
.

We can establish a vanishing result for certain Morse-Novikov cohomology groups of a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group when
\Lambda
is sufficiently small.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group. There exists a uniform positive constant
C(n)
such that if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(p,\kappa,D,\Lambda)
satisfies

\Lambda\leq C(n),

then there exists some
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

such that

for all

, where
H^{k}(X,t\theta)
is the Morse-Novikov
p

.

We will temporarily postpone the proof of Theorem
4.1
and instead prove Theorem
1.1
using this conclusion. The detailed proof of Theorem

will be provided in the following section.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Poincaré duality for Morse-Novikov cohomology
H^{p}(X,t\theta)\cong H^{2n-p}(X,t\theta)
, the Euler number obeys

\chi(X)=\sum_{p=0}^{2n}(-1)^{p}\dim H^{p}(X,t\theta)=\sum_{p<n}2(-1)^{p}\dim H^{p}(X,t\theta)+(-1)^{n}\dim H^{n}(X,t\theta)

Following Corollary

, we can immediately conclude that

\chi(X)=0
when

(since

for any

) and
(-1)^{n}\chi(X)=\dim H^{n}(X,t\theta)\geq 0
(since
H^{p}(X,t\theta)=0
for all

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let’s consider the case where
\dim X=4
. The Euler number of
X
satisfies

\chi(X)=2+b_{2}(X)-2b_{1}(X).

If
b_{1}(X)=0
, then we get
\chi(X)\geq 2
.

If

-form
\theta

,

(see Corollary

Thus

in all cases.
∎

4.1
Morse-Novikov cohomology

Let
X
be a compact smooth manifold equipped with a closed
1
-form
\theta
.
The Morse-Novikov cohomology (independently introduced by Lichnerowicz
[
21
]
, Novikov
[
24
]
, and Guedira-Lichnerowicz
[
15
]
) is defined via the complex
(\Omega^{\ast}(X),d_{\theta})

\Omega^{0}(X)\xrightarrow{d_{\theta}}\Omega^{1}(X)\xrightarrow{d_{\theta}}\Omega^{2}(X)\xrightarrow{}\cdots

(4.1)

Denote by
H^{\ast}(X,\theta)
the cohomology of the complex
(\Omega^{\ast}(X),d_{\theta})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

(
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
15
]
)
Let
X
be a compact smooth manifold and
\theta
a closed
1
-form on
X
. Then,

(i) The differential complex
(\Omega^{\ast}(M),d_{\theta})
is elliptic and the cohomology groups
H^{k}(X,\theta)
have finite dimension.

(ii) If
\theta
is exact, then
H^{k}(X,\theta)\cong H^{k}_{dR}(X)
.

(iii) If
\theta
is not exact, and
X
is connected and orientable, then
H^{0}(X,\theta)
and
H^{n}(X,\theta)
vanish.

In general, if the
1
-form
\theta
is not exact then
H^{k}(X,\theta)\ncong H^{k}_{dR}(X)
. Since the complex
(\Omega^{\ast}(X),d_{\theta})
is elliptic, we obtain an orthogonal decomposition of the space
\Omega^{k}(X)
as follows

\Omega^{k}(X)=\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\theta}(X)\oplus d_{\theta}(\Omega^{k-1}(X))\oplus d^{\ast}_{\theta}(\Omega^{k+1}(X)),

(4.2)

where

\mathcal{H}_{\theta}^{k}(X)=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(X):d_{\theta}(\alpha)=0,d^{\ast}_{\theta}(\alpha)=0\}.

From (
4.2
), it follows that
H^{k}(X,\theta)\cong\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\theta}(X)
. But Morse-Novikov cohomology
H^{k}(X,\theta)
is different to de Rham cohomology, it is not a topological invariant, it depends on
[\theta]\in H^{1}_{dR}(M)
. Following the Atyiah-Singer index theorem, it is known that the index of the elliptic complex
(\Omega^{\ast}(X),d_{\theta})
is independent of
\theta
. Moreover, the Euler number of the Morse-Novikov cohomology coincides with the Euler number of the manifold, given by

\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{p}\dim\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\theta}(X)=\chi(X),

(4.3)

where
\chi(X)
is the Euler number of
X
. This result highlights the topological significance of the Morse-Novikov cohomology.

4.2

Let

,q=2

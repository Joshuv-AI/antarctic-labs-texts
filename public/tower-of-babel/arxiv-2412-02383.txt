A multiplicity result for Hamiltonian systems with mixed periodic-type and Neumann-type boundary conditions
Wahid Ullah

Abstract
We investigate the multiplicity of solutions for a Hamiltonian system coupling two systems associated with mixed boundary conditions. Corresponding to the first system, we impose periodic boundary conditions and assume the twist assumption commonly used for the Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem, while for the second one, we consider a two-point boundary conditions of Neumann type.



1
Introduction and statement of the main result

The classical Poincaré–Birkhoff fixed point theorem, also known as Poincaré’s last geometric theorem, states that any area-preserving homeomorphism of a planar annulus that keeps both boundary circles invariant and twisting them in opposite directions must have at least two fixed points. Poincaré
[
27
]
conjectured this theorem shortly before his death in 1912, and it was later proved by Birkhoff
[
2
,
3
]
. Poincaré and Birkhoff were motivated by its applications to the search of periodic solutions of conservative dynamical systems.

Arnold sought to extend the Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem to higher dimensions, emphasizing its significance for understanding periodic solutions in systems with many degrees of freedom
[
1
, page 416]
. Birkhoff himself had already recognized the importance of this issue, describing it as an outstanding question
[
3
, page 299]
. He
[
4
]
proposed a
2N
-dimensional version of the theorem, which involved the key assumption that there exists a manifold diffeomorphic to the
N
-torus, where the exact symplectic map preserves the first
N
coordinates.

In 1978, Rabinowitz
[
28
]
demonstrated how the periodic problem for a Hamiltonian system can be approached through a variational method. A significant challenge in this approach is that the associated functional is highly indefinite. On the other hand, Conley and Zehnder
[
7
, Theorem 3]
proved another version of the Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem in higher dimensions by using variational methods and the Conley index theory for flows. Their result concerns the multiplicity of periodic solutions for time-dependent Hamiltonian vector fields provided that the Hamiltonian function
H=H(t,x,y)
with
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
,
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
is twice continuously differentiable, periodic in
t
and in the variables
x_{i}
, and quadratic in
y
on a neighborhood of infinity. Then, they obtained the existence of at least
N+1
periodic solutions. Remarkably, their result does not rely on the Poincaré time-map being close to the identity or having a monotone twist.

Inspired by the idea of Conley and Zehnder, Chang
[
5
]
provided an alternative proof of their result, highlighting that the periodicity of the Hamiltonian function
H
is fundamental to the existence of multiple periodic solutions. The advancement of infinite-dimensional Lusternik–Schnirelmann methods enabled Szulkin
[
29
]
to extend the Conley–Zehnder theorem, making it applicable to a broader range of Hamiltonian systems. Some other results concerning periodic solutions of Hamiltonian systems can be found in
[
8
,
22
]
.

Taking a further step in this direction Fonda and Ureña
[
18
,
19
]
proposed a new version of higher-dimensional Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem which apply to Poincaré time-maps of Hamiltonian systems. Later on, Fonda and Gidoni
[
10
]
extended this result for Hamiltonian systems coupling twisting components with nonresonant linear components. The coupling of twisting components with
resonant
linear components was studied by Chen and Qian
[
6
]
, where they used an Ahmad-Lazer-Paul type condition to provide a multiplicity result. Some further extensions of higher-dimensional Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem to different coupled Hamiltonian systems can be found in
[
9
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
26
]
.

On the other hand, recently, Fonda and Ortega
[
13
]
proved a multiplicity result for a two-point boundary value problem associated with Hamiltonian systems on a cylinder. Unlike the periodic problem, where the Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem plays a central role, they did not use any twist condition. Similar to the periodic case, there are generalizations of this result for coupled Hamiltonian systems, see for example
[
11
,
25
,
17
]
. While the Neumann problem for scalar second order equations has been widely studied, few papers in the literature provide multiplicity results for Neumann-type problems associated with systems of ordinary differential equations. To the best of our knowledge, multiplicity results for mixed periodic and Neumann-type problems associated with Hamiltonian systems have never been studied before.

It is the aim of this paper to study Hamiltonian systems by coupling two systems in which one is associated with periodic and the other with Neumann-type boundary conditions. To be more precise, we consider the Hamiltonian system

\begin{cases}\dot{q}=\nabla_{p}H(t,q,p,u,v)\,,\\
\dot{p}=-\nabla_{q}H(t,q,p,u,v)\,,\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\dot{u}=\nabla_{v}H(t,q,p,u,v)\,,\\
\dot{v}=-\nabla_{u}H(t,q,p,u,v)\,,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with the boundary conditions

\begin{cases}q(a)=q(b)\,,\quad p(a)=p(b)\,,\\
v(a)=0=v(b)\,.\end{cases}

(1.2)

We write

\displaystyle q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{M})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M},\hskip 10.00002ptp=(p_{1},\dots,p_{M})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M},

\displaystyle u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{L})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L},\hskip 10.00002ptv=(v_{1},\dots,v_{L})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L}\,.

The function
H:[a,b]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2M}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2L}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, is continuous, and continuously differentiable with respect to
q,p,u
and
v
.

Here are our hypotheses.

A1
. There exist
\kappa_{i}>0
for
i=1,\dots,M
and
\tau_{j}>0
for
j=1,\dots,L
such that the function
H(t,q,p,u,v)
is
\kappa_{i}
-periodic in the variable
q_{i}
and is
\tau_{j}
-periodic in the variable
u_{j}
.

We now introduce the
twist condition
by first considering the case when
\mathcal{D}
is a rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{D}=[c_{1},d_{1}]\times\cdots\times[c_{M},d_{M}]\,,

and we denote by
\mathring{\mathcal{D}}
its interior.

A2
. There exists an
M
-tuple
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M})\in\{-1,1\}^{M}
such that for every
C^{1}
-function
\mathcal{W}:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2L}
, all the solutions
(q,p)
of system

\begin{cases}\dot{q}=\nabla_{p}H(t,q,p,\mathcal{W}(t))\\
\dot{p}=-\nabla_{q}H(t,q,p,\mathcal{W}(t))\end{cases}

(1.3)

starting with
p(a)\in{\cal D}
are defined on
[a,b]
, and for every
i=1,\dots,M
we have

\begin{cases}p_{i}(a)=c_{i}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma_{i}(q_{i}(b)-q_{i}(a))<0\,,\\
p_{i}(a)=d_{i}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma_{i}(q_{i}(b)-q_{i}(a))>0\,.\end{cases}

A3
. All the solutions of system (
1.1
) satisfying
v(a)=0
are defined on
[a,b]
.

Here is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
A1
–
A3
hold true.
Then there are at least
M+L+1
geometrically distinct solutions of the boundary value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) such that
p(a)\in\mathring{\mathcal{D}}
.

Notice that, when a solution has been found, infinitely many others appear by just adding an integer multiple of
\kappa_{i}
to the
q_{i}
-th component or adding an integer multiple of
\tau_{j}
to the
u_{j}
-th component. We say that two solutions are
geometrically distinct
if they cannot be obtained from each other in this way.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
we provide a proof of our result for the low dimensional case by taking
L=M=1
. The proof in higher dimension is given in Section
3
. Finally, in Section
4
we provide a possible example of application of the main result, we add some other possible variants of
twist assumptions
, and conclude with a discussion of some open problems in this direction.

(2.4)

We take

2.1
The function spaces

For the variables
q
and
p

H_{\pi}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,\pi)

\left\langle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{k}e^{2kit},\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}e^{2kit}\right\rangle_{\frac{1}{2}}=f_{0}g_{0}^{*}+\sum_{0\neq k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|k|f_{k}g_{k}^{*}\,,

where
g_{k}=g_{-k}^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
and
f_{k}=f_{-k}^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
.

On the other hand, for the variables
u
and
v
, we use the spaces constructed in
[
13
]
as follow.

For any
\alpha\in\,]0,1[
, we define
X_{\alpha}
as the set of those real valued functions
\tilde{u}\in L^{2}(0,\pi)

\tilde{u}(t)\sim\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\tilde{u}_{m}\cos(mt)\,,

(\tilde{u}_{m})_{m\geq 1}

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\alpha}\tilde{u}_{m}^{2}<\infty\,.

The space

\langle\tilde{u},\,\tilde{\phi}\rangle_{X_{\alpha}}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\alpha}\tilde{u}_{m}\tilde{\phi}_{m}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|\tilde{u}\|_{X_{\alpha}}=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\alpha}\tilde{u}_{m}^{2}}\,.

For any
\beta\in\,]0,1[
, we define
Y_{\beta}
as the set of those real valued functions

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\beta}v_{m}^{2}<\infty\,.

The space
Y_{\beta}

\langle v,\,\rho\rangle_{Y_{\beta}}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\beta}v_{m}\rho_{m}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|p\|_{Y_{\beta}}=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2\beta}v_{m}^{2}}\,.

From now on, we will consider functions
q,p,u,v
which can be written as

\begin{cases}q(t)=\bar{q}+\tilde{q}(t)\,,\quad\bar{q}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}q(t)\,dt\,,\\

(2.8)

Choose two positive numbers

\alpha<{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}<\beta\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha+\beta=1\,.

Consider the torus

\displaystyle\langle(\tilde{q}\,,p\,,\tilde{u}\,,v),\,(\widetilde{Q}\,,P\,,\widetilde{U}\,,V)\rangle_{E}=

If

where
\mathscr{L}:E\to E
is a bounded selfadjoint invertible operator and

critical points.

2.2
The functional and the bilinear form

We define a functional
\psi:E\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as

\displaystyle\psi(w,\bar{q},\bar{u})

\displaystyle=\psi\big((\tilde{q}\,,p\,,\tilde{u},v\,,\bar{q},\bar{u}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\big[\widetilde{H}\big(t\,,\bar{q}+\tilde{q}(t)\,,p(t)\,,\bar{u}+\tilde{u}(t)\,,v(t)\big)-\lambda|p(t)|^{2}\big]\,dt\,.

In the following, we will treat

as being lifted to

Similar to the proof provided in

+\langle v^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{U}(t)\rangle-\langle\tilde{u}^{\prime}(t),V(t)\rangle\Big]\,dt\,.

(2.9)

The set

Lemma 2.3

For every

we have

Proof.

+2\lambda\int_{0}^{\pi}\langle p(t),P(t)\rangle dt.

Using the notations

and putting

\tilde{x}_{k}=\frac{2\pi ki}{|k|}p_{k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{ for }k\neq 0\,.

(2.14)

On the other hand, taking

in (

which implies that

y_{k}=2\pi\frac{-ki\tilde{q}_{k}+\lambda p_{k}}{|k|}\,,\quad\hbox{ for }k\neq 0\,.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq 4\pi^{2}\Big(|p_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{0\neq k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|k||p_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{0\neq k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|k||\tilde{q}_{k}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}\big(\|\tilde{q}\|^{2}_{\frac{1}{2}}+\|p\|^{2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\,,

Let us now mention some possible further developments and open problems in this direction.

1.
The coupling of a Hamiltonian system having twist condition with a system having lower and upper solutions under periodic boundary conditions was examined in
[
9
,
15
]
. In contrast, coupled Hamiltonian systems with a system having lower and upper solutions under Neumann-type boundary conditions was studied in
[
11
,
25
]
. It would be worth to explore mixed boundary conditions, such as those in (
1.2
), for this type of coupled Hamiltonian systems.

2.
In
[
17
]
, the authors examined the coupling of a Hamiltonian system with a positive, positively-
(p,q)
-homogeneous Hamiltonian system associated with a Neumann-type boundary conditions. They also discussed the periodic problem, assuming a
twist condition
for the first system. It would be intriguing to study a coupled Hamiltonian system where the first system with a
twist assumption
satisfies a periodic conditions, and the second system is a positively-
(p,q)
-homogeneous Hamiltonian system with a Neumann-type boundary conditions and some
nonresonance
condition.

3.
Fonda and Gidoni
[
10
]
extended the higher-dimensional Poincaré–Birkhoff Theorem for the periodic problem associated with Hamiltonian systems coupling twisting components with nonresonant linear components, where they used a symmetric matrix for the second system. On the other hand, the similar Hamiltonian system with Neumann-type boundary conditions was treated in
[
17
]
for a special type of symmetric matrix. The case of using general symmetric matrix in
[
17
]
is still open. An interesting open problem arises for such systems by considering periodic boundary conditions for the first system, while Neumann-type boundary conditions for the second one with general symmetric matrix.

4.
In
[
14
]
, the authors proved a multiplicity result for the periodic problem associated with a Hamiltonian system whose Hamiltonian function has a twisting part and a nonresonant part. They also analyzed a possible approach to resonance together with some kind of Landesman–Lazer conditions. One could try to extend their results by taking Neumann-type boundary conditions for the second system.

5.
Multiplicity results were obtained in
[
26
]
for the periodic problem associated with a Hamiltonian system coupling a system having a Poincaré–Birkhoff twist-type structure with another planar system whose nonlinearity lies between the gradients of two positive and positively
2
-homogeneous Hamiltonain functions. Nonresonance, simple resonance and double resonance situations were treated by imposing some kind of Landesman–Lazer conditions at both sides.
It would be interesting to know if parallel results can be proved in the setting of this paper.

Problem statement and main results

1.1
Introduction

Let
AC[0,T]
be the space of absolutely continuous functions on the interval
[0,T]
.
For an arbitrary convex function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any function
h\in AC[0,T]
, consider the energy functional

|h|_{T}^{\psi}:=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(h^{\prime}(t))\mathrm{d}t.

The articles
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
investigate the problem of approximating trajectories of various stochastic processes by functions
h(\cdot)
that minimize a certain type of energy, while satisfying constraints on the proximity between
h(\cdot)
and the process trajectory. Most often, the approximated process is the Wiener process and the energy considered is kinetic energy, that is,
|h|_{T}^{\psi}
with
\psi(u)=u^{2}
. The articles
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
study energy-efficient approximations of the Wiener process
W(\cdot)
under
bilateral
uniform constraints for arbitrary energy functionals. For
T,r>0
, define the set of admissible approximations

M^{\pm}_{T,r}:=\left\{h\in AC[0,T]\,\big|\,\forall t\in[0,T]:|h(t)-W(t)|\leq r;h(0)=0\right\}

and the minimal integral energy

I^{\pm}_{W}(T,r,\psi):=\inf\left\{|h|_{T}^{\psi}\>\big|\>h\in M^{\pm}_{T,r}\right\}.

In
[
7
]
, it was shown that the approximation at which
\inf\left\{|h|_{T}^{\psi}\>\big|\>h\in M^{\pm}_{T,r}\right\}
is attained—called the
taut string
—does not depend on the form of
\psi(\cdot)
.
The result of
[
7
]
was improved in
[
8
]
, where it was shown that for a fairly wide class of convex functions
\psi(\cdot)
, and for fixed
r>0
, it holds that

\frac{I^{\pm}_{W}(T,r,\psi)}{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(u)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}u),\quad T\to\infty,

where
\nu
is a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
that depends on
r
and admits an explicit expression.

In this paper, we are interested in the behavior of the minimum value of the functional

|\cdot|_{T}^{\psi}
over approximations of the Wiener process trajectories under fixed
unilateral
constraints; that is, the set of admissible approximations for given
T,r>0
takes the form

M_{T,r}:=\left\{h\in AC[0,T]\,\big|\,\forall t\in[0,T]:h(t)\geq W(t)-r;h(0)=0\right\},

and we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the minimal integral energy

I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r):=\inf\left\{|h|_{T}^{\psi}\>\big|\>h\in M_{T,r}\right\}

as
T\to\infty
.
The asymptotics of this quantity for
\psi(u)=u^{2}
is studied in
[
10
]
.

For technical convenience, we shift the initial value of the approximation to the point
r
, so that the approximating function lies above the trajectory of the process
W(\cdot)
. Define

M_{T,r}^{\prime}:=\left\{h\in AC[0,T]\,\big|\,\forall t\in[0,T]:h(t)\geq W(t);h(0)=r\right\}.

Since the sets
M_{T,r}
and
M_{T,r}^{\prime}
differ only by a constant shift, it is clear that

I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)=\inf\left\{|h|_{T}^{\psi}\>\big|\>h\in M_{T,r}^{\prime}\right\}.

We will show below that, unlike in the bilateral constraint case, the value of
r
does not affect the asymptotic behavior of
I_{W}^{\psi}(\cdot,r)
.

If the minimum of
\psi(\cdot)
is not attained at zero or is not equal to 0, then
|\cdot|_{T}^{\psi}
is minimized by a function that is eventually a non-random linear function. Therefore, only functions
\psi(\cdot)
satisfying
\psi(0)=0
and attaining their minimum at zero are of interest.

It turns out that the behavior of the minimal energy is determined by the values of the function
\psi(\cdot)
in a right neighborhood of zero. As follows from Theorem
9
below, if for small
u>0
the values of
\psi(u)
are significantly smaller than
u^{2}
, then the minimal integral energy is finite over the entire time axis; that is,

\sup_{T>0}I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)<\infty
almost surely. For this reason, the main focus is on convex functions
\psi(\cdot)
that are
\varkappa
-regularly varying at zero, with
\varkappa\in[1,2]
; that is, functions of the form

\psi(u)=u^{\varkappa}\,\theta(u),

(1)

where
\theta(\cdot)
is slowly varying at zero. The behavior of
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
primarily depends on the value of the parameter
\varkappa
, with the cases
\varkappa=2
and
\varkappa<2
being fundamentally different.
To express the conditions imposed on
\psi(\cdot)
, it is convenient to use the Karamata representation for the function
\theta(\cdot)
(see
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
); namely, for
u\in(0,1)
,

\theta(u)=c(u)\exp\left(\int_{u}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon(v)}{v}\,\mathrm{d}v\right),

(2)

where
c(\cdot)
is a positive bounded measurable function
c(\cdot)
with a nonzero limit at zero, and
\varepsilon(\cdot)
is a bounded measurable function with zero limit at zero.

In subsection
1.2
, we state results for the case
\varkappa\in[1,2)
.
In subsection
1.3
, we state results for the case
\varkappa\geq 2
.

In Section
2
, we establish a connection between unilateral energy-efficient approximations of arbitrary continuous functions and their minimal concave majorants. We also derive key properties of the minimal concave majorant of the Wiener process, relying heavily on the results of Groeneboom
[
12
]
.
Section
3
contains the proofs of the theorems for the case
\varkappa\in[1,2)
.
Section
4
contains the proofs of the theorems for the case
\varkappa\geq 2
.

1.2
Results for
\varkappa\in[1,2)

The first theorem describes lower bounds for the lower limit of
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
as
T\to\infty
; specifically, it identifies deterministic functions of
T
that are almost surely eventually exceeded by
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
. In other words, any approximation of
W(\cdot)
almost surely requires more energy to remain above
W(\cdot)
over the entire time interval
[0,T]
for all sufficiently large
T
.

Theorem 1

Let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function of the form (
1
) with
\varkappa\in[1,2)
, and suppose
\theta(\cdot)
admits the Karamata representation (
2
) for
u\in(0,1)
. Assume further that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that, for all sufficiently small
v>0
,

\varepsilon(v)<\frac{1}{|\log v|^{\alpha}}.

(3)

Let
g(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function at infinity satisfying, for some
\xi_{\circ}>2-\varkappa>\xi^{\circ}>0
,

(\log b)^{-\xi_{\circ}}\ll g(b)\ll(\log b)^{-\xi^{\circ}},\quad b\to\infty

(4)

and

\int^{\infty}\frac{g(b)^{1/(2-\varkappa)}}{b}\,\mathrm{d}b<\infty.

(5)

Then, for any
r>0
, it holds that

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{T\psi(T^{-1/2})g(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\infty.

Remark 2

Examples of admissible functions
\theta(\cdot)
include
|\log(\cdot)|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\exp\left(\pm|\log(\cdot)|^{\alpha}\right)
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Note that the constraint on
\varepsilon(\cdot)
is unilateral, meaning it controls only the potential growth of
\theta(u)
as
u\downarrow 0
. The theorem imposes no restrictions on the possible decay of
\theta(u)
in the same limit.

The next theorem describes upper bounds for the lower limit of
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
as
T\to\infty
; specifically, it identifies deterministic functions of
T
that almost surely infinitely often exceed
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
. In other words, these functions demonstrate how small the energy cost of approximating
W(\cdot)
can be over certain long time intervals, while still remaining above
W(\cdot)
.

Theorem 3

Let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function of the form (
1
) with
\varkappa\in[1,2)
, and suppose
\theta(\cdot)
admits the Karamata representation (
2
) for
u\in(0,1)
. Assume further that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that, for all sufficiently small
v>0
,

|\varepsilon(v)|<\frac{1}{|\log v|^{\alpha}}.

Let
g(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function at infinity satisfying, for some
\xi_{\circ}>2-\varkappa>\xi^{\circ}>0
,

(\log b)^{-\xi_{\circ}}\ll g(b)\ll(\log b)^{-\xi^{\circ}},\quad b\to\infty

(6)

and

\int^{\infty}\frac{g(b)^{1/(2-\varkappa)}}{b}\,\mathrm{d}b=\infty.

(7)

Then, for any
r>0
, it holds that

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{T\psi(T^{-1/2})g(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}0.

Remark 4

Examples of admissible functions
\theta(\cdot)
include
|\log(\cdot)|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\exp\left(\pm|\log(\cdot)|^{\alpha}\right)
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In contrast to Theorem
1
, the constraint on
\varepsilon(\cdot)
in this case is bilateral.

In the case
\psi(u)=u
, Theorems
1
and
3
together yield the well-known Hirsch integral test
[
13
,
14
]
.

Corrolary 5

For any slowly varying function
g(\cdot)
at infinity satisfying condition (
6
) with
\varkappa=1
, it holds that

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq t\leq T}W(t)}{T^{1/2}g(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\int^{\infty}\frac{g(b)}{b}\,\mathrm{d}b=\infty,\\
\infty,&\text{if }\int^{\infty}\frac{g(b)}{b}\,\mathrm{d}b<\infty.\end{cases}

We now describe the behavior of the
upper
limit of
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
as
T\to\infty
. The following theorem provides maximal necessary energy values required for approximating
W(\cdot)
from some sufficiently large time onward, and characterizes energy levels that may be insufficient.

Theorem 6

Let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function of the form (
1
) with
\varkappa\in[1,2)
, and suppose
\theta(\cdot)
admits the Karamata representation (
2
) for
u\in(0,1)
. Assume further that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that, for all sufficiently small
v>0
,

\varepsilon(v)>-\frac{1}{|\log v|^{\alpha}}.

Then, for any
r>0
, it holds almost surely that

\frac{2^{-\varkappa/2}}{1-\varkappa/2}\geq\limsup\limits_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{T\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log\log T}{T}}\right)}\geq 2^{\varkappa/2}.

Remark 7

Note that the constraint on
\varepsilon(\cdot)
is unilateral, regulating only the possible decay of
\theta(u)
as
u\downarrow 0
. The theorem imposes no restrictions on the possible growth of
\theta(\cdot)
in the same limit.

Example 8

For
\psi(u)=u^{\varkappa}
with
\varkappa\in[1,2)
and any
r>0
, it holds almost surely that

\frac{2^{-\varkappa/2}}{1-\varkappa/2}\geq\limsup\limits_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{T^{1-\varkappa/2}(\log\log T)^{\varkappa/2}}\geq 2^{\varkappa/2}.

1.3
Results for
\varkappa\geq 2

In contrast to the case
\varkappa\in[1,2)
, when
\varkappa\geq 2
, the minimal energy
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
is almost surely either bounded on the entire positive real axis or exhibits an unbounded deterministic asymptotic growth.
The key quantity in this alternative is

m_{\psi}(b):=\int^{b}_{1}a\psi(a^{-1})\mathrm{d}a,\quad b\geq 1.

The conditions that determine the behavior of
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
are

\displaystyle\lim_{b\to\infty}m_{\psi}(b)<\infty,

(8)

\displaystyle\lim_{b\to\infty}m_{\psi}(b)=\infty.

(9)

Due to the monotonicity of
m_{\psi}(\cdot)
, exactly one of these conditions holds.

Finite energy case

It turns out that the boundedness of
m_{\psi}(\cdot)
implies the boundedness of

I_{W}^{\psi}(\cdot,r)
.

Theorem 9

Let
r>0
, and let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function attaining its minimal value 0 at zero. If condition (
8
) holds, then, almost surely, the random variable
I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)
is uniformly bounded in
T
and increases monotonically to its finite limit as
T\to\infty
.

Example 10

The functions
\psi(u)=u^{\varkappa}
with
\varkappa>2
, as well as
\psi(u)=u^{2}|\log u|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha<-1
, satisfy condition (
8
), along with functions
\psi(\cdot)
that decay to 0 faster as
u\downarrow 0
.

Exact asymptotics case

In the case where
m_{\psi}(\cdot)
is unbounded, the exact asymptotic growth of
I_{W}^{\psi}(\cdot,r)
can be determined.

Theorem 11

Let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function of the form (
1
) with
\varkappa=2
and some function
\theta(\cdot)
, which is not necessarily slowly varying. Suppose that for some
\alpha^{\diamond},\alpha_{\diamond}
, one of the following conditions holds:

-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha^{\diamond},\quad\alpha_{\diamond}\leq\alpha^{\diamond}<\alpha_{\diamond}+\frac{1}{3},

(10)

or

-1<\alpha^{\diamond}\leq-\frac{1}{2},\quad\alpha_{\diamond}\leq\alpha^{\diamond}<3\alpha_{\diamond}+2.

(11)

Assume that

|\log u|^{\alpha_{\diamond}}\ll\theta(u)\ll|\log u|^{\alpha^{\diamond}},\quad u\to 0.

(12)

If condition (
9
) is satisfied, then for any
r>0
, it holds that

\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{m_{\psi}\left(T^{1/2}\right)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}1\,.

Example 12

The functions
\psi(u)=u^{2}|\log u|^{\alpha}\,\Upsilon(|\log u|)
with
\alpha>-1
and some slowly varying function
\Upsilon(\cdot)
at infinity satisfy condition (
9
). For
\Upsilon(\cdot)\equiv 1
, by Theorem
11
, for any
r>0
, it holds that

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{I_{W}^{\psi}(T,r)}{\left(\log T\right)^{1+\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(1+\alpha)2^{1+\alpha}}.

The special case
\alpha=0
and
\Upsilon(\cdot)\equiv 1
(kinetic energy) is considered in
[
10
]
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Concave majorants as energy-efficient approximations

The energy-efficient approximation under a unilateral constraint can be characterized in terms of the minimal concave majorant (MCM) of the approximated function and does not depend on
\psi(\cdot)
.
Let
w:[0,T]\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function. Then the corresponding
MCM
\overline{w}(\cdot)
is the smallest concave function satisfying the condition

\overline{w}(t)\geq w(t),\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

Proposition 13

Let
r>w(0)
and let
\psi(\cdot)
be a convex function attaining its minimal value 0 at zero. Assume that, for each
\delta>0
,
\overline{w}(\cdot)
is a piecewise linear function with a finite number of non-decreasing segments on
[\delta,T]
. Then the problem
|h|_{T}^{\psi}\to\min
under the constraints

h(0)=r
and

h(t)\geq w(t),\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

admits a solution
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
of the following form.

(a) If
r\geq\max_{0\leq t\leq T}w(t)
, then
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(t)\equiv r
.

(b) If
r<\max_{0\leq t\leq T}w(t)
, then
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
is defined differently on three segments. On the initial segment,
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
is an affine function whose graph passes through the point
(0,r)
and is tangent to the graph of
\overline{w}(\cdot)
.
Then
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
coincides with
\overline{w}(\cdot)
until the first time the maximum of
w(\cdot)
is attained.
Finally, after this point,
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
remains constant.

Moreover, if
\psi(\cdot)
is strictly convex, then this solution is unique.

Proof of the proposition:

Case (a) is trivial, so consider case (b).
Suppose there exists
{\chi^{\text{ }}_{\circ}}(\cdot)
such that
|\chi^{\text{ }}_{\circ}|_{T}^{\psi}<|\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}|_{T}^{\psi}
. Define the function

\chi(t):=r+\int_{0}^{t}\vec{\chi}^{\text{ }\prime}_{\circ}(\vartheta)\mathrm{d}\vartheta,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

where
\vec{\chi}^{\text{ }\prime}_{\circ}(\cdot)
is the decreasing rearrangement of the function
\max\{\chi^{\prime}_{\circ}(\cdot),0\}
.
By construction,
\chi(\cdot)
is a concave non-decreasing function, with
\chi(0)=r
and

\chi(t)\geq\chi^{\text{ }}_{\circ}(t)\geq w(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Therefore,
\chi(\cdot)
satisfies the problem’s constraints.
On the other hand,

|\chi|_{T}^{\psi}=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(\vec{\chi}^{\text{ }\prime}_{\circ})\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(\max\{\chi_{\circ}^{\prime}(t),0\})\mathrm{d}t\leq|\chi^{\text{ }}_{\circ}|_{T}^{\psi}<|\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}|_{T}^{\psi}.

Since
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
is the minimal convex non-decreasing function satisfying the constraints of the problem, we have

\chi(t)\geq\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(t),\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

Let
k_{*}
be the number of affine components composing
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
, finite by assumption.
Let
\{f_{k}(\cdot)\}_{k=1}^{k_{*}}
be affine functions on
[0,T]
such that

\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(t)=\min_{1\leq k\leq k_{*}}\{f_{k}(t)\},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

For
k_{*}\geq k\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
, denote
\chi^{\text{ }}_{0}(t):=\chi(t)
and

\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(t):=\min\left\{\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\{f_{i}(t)\},\chi(t)\right\}.

For
k_{*}>k\geq 0
, we have

\chi^{\text{ }}_{k+1}(t)=\min\left\{\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(t),f_{k+1}(t)\right\},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

We will prove that for
k_{*}>k\geq 0
, it is true that

|\chi^{\text{ }}_{k+1}|_{T}^{\psi}\leq|\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}|_{T}^{\psi}.

Let
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,T]
be the maximal interval on which
\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(\cdot)\geq f_{k+1}(\cdot)
. Due to the convexity of
\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(\cdot)
, this interval is well-defined.

Using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\psi(\chi^{\prime}_{k}(t))\mathrm{d}t\geq(t_{2}-t_{1})\psi\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\chi^{\prime}_{k}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t_{2}-t_{1})\psi\left(\frac{\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(t_{2})-\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)\geq(t_{2}-t_{1})\psi\left(\frac{f_{k+1}(t_{2})-f_{k+1}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\psi(f_{k+1}^{\prime}(t))\mathrm{d}t.

Thus, we have

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\psi(\chi^{\prime}_{k}(t))\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\psi(f_{k+1}^{\prime}(t))\mathrm{d}t.

It follows that
\chi^{\text{ }}_{k+1}(\cdot)
satisfies the problem’s constraints and

|\chi^{\text{ }}_{k+1}|_{T}^{\psi}\leq|\chi^{\text{ }}_{k}|_{T}^{\psi}
.
Hence, we obtain that
|\chi|_{T}^{\psi}\geq|\chi^{\text{ }}_{k_{*}}|_{T}^{\psi}=|\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}|_{T}^{\psi}
,
which leads to a contradiction.

If
\psi(\cdot)
is strictly convex, then the energy functional
|\cdot|_{T}^{\psi}
is also strictly convex, ensuring that
\chi^{\text{ }}_{*}(\cdot)
is the unique minimizer of
|\cdot|_{T}^{\psi}
.

\square

2.2
Simplified representation of the minimal integral energy

For any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and any set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\argmax_{t\in F}f(t):=\sup\left\{t_{0}\in F\,\big|\,f(t_{0})=\sup_{t\in F}f(t)\right\}.

Consider the stochastic process

\tau(a):=\argmax_{t\geq 0}\,(W(t)-t/a),\qquad a>0.

(13)

According to
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
, it admits the following representation

\tau(a)=\int\limits_{0+}^{\infty}l\,N([0,a]\times\mathrm{d}l),\qquad a>0,

(14)

where
N(\mathrm{d}a\times\mathrm{d}l)
is a Poisson random measure with intensity measure

\mu(\mathrm{d}a\times\mathrm{d}l)=(a^{2}\sqrt{l})^{-1}\varphi(\sqrt{l}/a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}l,\qquad a>0,\,l>0,

(15)

and
\varphi(\cdot)
denotes the standard normal density function.

We now present several estimates for
\tau(\cdot)
that will be used later.

Lemma 14

Let
\delta>0
, and let
\rho(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function at infinity, such that
\rho(b)<1
for every
b>0
and

\int^{\infty}\frac{\rho(b)^{1/2}}{b}\mathrm{d}b<\infty.

(16)

Then, almost surely, for all sufficiently large
a
, it holds that

(2+\delta)a^{2}\log\log a>\tau(a)>a^{2}\rho(a).

Proof:
According to
[
12
, Corollary 2.1]
, for any
a>0
the variable

\frac{\tau(a)}{a^{2}}
has a probability density

p(y)=2\,{\mathbb{E}\,}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{y}}-1\right)_{+},\qquad y>0,

where
x_{+}:=\max(x,0)
and
X
is a standard normal random variable.

Upper bound
. For
\log\log a>1
and any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\tau(a)}{a^{2}}\geq(2+\epsilon)\log\log a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(2+\epsilon)\log\log a}^{\infty}2\,{\mathbb{E}\,}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{y}}-1\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{(2+\epsilon)\log\log a}^{\infty}\int_{\sqrt{y}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{y}}-1\right)e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{(2+\epsilon)\log\log a}^{\infty}e^{-y/2}y^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}\,e^{-(1+\epsilon/2)\log\log a}.

Let
a_{n}:=(1+\delta_{1})^{n}
with some
\delta_{1}>0
, and define the events

D_{n}:=\left\{\tau(a_{n})\geq(2+\delta_{1})a_{n}^{2}\log\log a_{n}\right\}.

Then the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(D_{n})
converges. Hence, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, almost surely, for all sufficiently large
n
, we have

\tau(a_{n})<(2+\delta_{1})a_{n}^{2}\log\log a_{n}.

As
n\to\infty
, for any
a\in[a_{n},a_{n+1}]
, using that
\tau(\cdot)
is non-decreasing, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau(a)\leq\tau(a_{n+1})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(2+\delta_{1})a_{n+1}^{2}\log\log a_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

Proof of Theorem

Lower bound.

T>0
, let

denote the solution to the problem

,
whose structure

near zero, for any

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(h^{\prime}(t))}{\theta\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log\log T}{2T}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

4.1
Preliminaries

In the following lemma, we begin to describe the conditions under which
m_{\psi}(\cdot)
serves as a proper normalization for
L_{\psi}(\cdot)
to converge almost surely to a constant.

Lemma 23

Define the function

q(b):=\frac{\mathbb{D}L_{\psi}(b)}{(\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b))^{2}}.

Then, if there exists some
\sigma>0
such that, as
b\to\infty
,

q(b)=O\left((\log b)^{-\sigma}\right),

(59)

and, for some
\gamma>\frac{1}{\sigma}
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}\left(\exp(n^{\gamma})\right)}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(\exp((n+1)^{\gamma}))}=1,

(60)

then

\lim\limits_{b\to\infty}\frac{L_{\psi}(b)}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}1.

Proof:
The function
q(\cdot)
is well-defined due to representation (
18
).
For any
\delta>0
and
b\geq 1
, define the sets

D(b):=\left\{\left\lvert\frac{L_{\psi}(b)}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b)}-1\right\rvert>\delta\right\}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}(D(b))\leq\frac{q(b)}{\delta^{2}}.

Let
b_{n}:=\exp(n^{\gamma})
. From
\gamma>\frac{1}{\sigma}
and condition (
59
), it follows that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(D(b_{n}))<\infty.

This implies, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{\psi}(b_{n})}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b_{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}1.

(61)

Let
b\in[b_{n},b_{n+1}]
. Since
L_{\psi}(\cdot)
and
m_{\psi}(\cdot)
are non-decreasing, with probability 1 it holds that

\frac{L_{\psi}(b_{n+1})}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b_{n})}\geq\frac{L_{\psi}(b)}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b)}\geq\frac{L_{\psi}(b_{n})}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b_{n+1})}.

(62)

By condition (
60
),

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b_{n})}{\mathbb{E}L_{\psi}(b_{n+1})}=1.

Therefore, by (
61
), the right-hand side and left-hand side in (
62
) converge to 1 almost surely.

\square

\displaystyle=

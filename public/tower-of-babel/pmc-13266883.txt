SLFN11 expression as a marker of small cell lung cancer vulnerability to combined PARP inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors during maintenance
Authors: Jean-Louis Pujol
Published: 2026

Introduction

The small cell lung cancer (SCLC) state of things

With an estimated 350,000 to 400,000 cases worldwide during the year 2022, SCLC is one of the ten major causes of cancer-related mortality (1).

During the past decade, major therapeutic advances have occurred for SCLC, significantly improving patients’ outcomes. For extensive disease SCLC (ED-SCLC), induction treatment by combining chemotherapy with immunotherapy using programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) blockade is now the standard front-line treatment (2,3). At the end of induction, maintenance of the immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) for non-progressive patients was recommended except for grade 3 treatment emerging adverse events. More recently, lurbinectedin combined with ICI during the maintenance part improved both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) (4). For patients who experience disease relapses, the topotecan is not the sole standard option any longer since tarlatamab, a bispecific T-cell engager immunotherapy targeting delta-like ligand 3 and CD3, showed improved survival when compared with the topoisomerase I inhibitor topotecan (5).

Needs for more therapeutic options

Despite the improvement of outcomes with immunotherapy, there are still unmet needs in ED-SCLC. Alterations of the DNA damage repair (DDR) have been observed in SCLC and might be the “Achilles’ heel” offering new therapeutic opportunities of targeting DDR pathways for this “recalcitrant cancer” as it is the case in other highly aggressive cancers (6).

Deficient homologous recombination repair (HRR), replication stress, aberrant cell cycle control, and functional defects in one or more of the DNA repair pathways are common features of highly aggressive human malignancies, including SCLC. Moreover, many DDR proteins are overexpressed in SCLC and such is the case for SLFN11 (Schlafen family member 11) (7). Poly (adenosine diphosphate-ribose) polymerases (PARPs) are key enzymes involved in regulating replication fork progression during DNA replication (8). PARP inhibitors act by binding to sites of single-strand DNA breaks, preventing their proper repair. As a result, PARP becomes trapped on the DNA, forming PARP-DNA complexes that obstruct the replication machinery. This interference causes replication forks to stall, ultimately leading to the accumulation of DNA damage and triggering programmed cell death (apoptosis). This is the rationale of the study by Karim et al. in which the addition of talazoparib to atezolizumab (ATZ) was tested in SLFN11 expressing SCLC patients (9).

Summary of the study by Karim et al.

This phase II study accrued ED-SCLC patients without progression at the end of the carboplatin/etoposide plus ATZ induction and with a SLFN11 positive tumor (defined as >1% positive tumor cells by immunochemistry). They were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive maintenance ATZ as the standard treatment vs. ATZ and talazoparib (given orally 1,000 mcg daily). PFS was taken as the primary endpoint. After adjustment for co-variables, the authors reported an improvement in median PFS (2.9 vs. 2.4 months, for the talazoparib—ATZ combination and the ATZ group, respectively, hazard ratio =0.66; P=0.02). There was no improvement in median OS (9.7 vs. 9.5 months, respectively). Talazoparib significantly impaired the tolerability of the maintenance particularly when hematologic adverse events are considered.

One can consider that a median PSF solely improved by a fortnight, although statistically significant, is doubtful clinically significant even if there is a graphical survival advantage at one year, favoring the talazoparib—ATZ combination. Different subgroup analyses were conducted. However, the relatively small accrual population (n=106) limited the analysis of forest plots.

Despite its limitations, this study probably deserves further evaluation of PARP inhibitors in SLFN11 positive SCLC (10). It also offers the opportunity to in-depth consider targeting DDR as a new pathway to treat SCLC. This editorial briefly develops the DDR implication in SCLC progression, the prior attempts at introducing PARP inhibitors in this disease, the crosstalk of PARP and the antitumor immunity toward SCLC immune system escape.

Comments

How DDR is important in SCLC progression

The three main features of DNA damage response in SCLC are homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) (11), high replication stress mainly linked with MYC amplification, and accumulation of DDR proteins such as PARP and SLFN11 (6).

The DDR network is a complex and dynamic system with over 400 integral proteins. DNA integrity is preserved through several coordinated repair pathways. These pathways act specifically according to the type of DNA damage: (I) single-strand breaks (SSBs) are primarily corrected by the base excision repair (BER) pathway (enzymes such as PARP1 and DNA ligase III°); double-strand breaks (DSBs) need two major repair pathways; (II) HRR, an error-free process (BRCA1/2); and (III) non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), a more fallible pathway involving DNA ligase IV; (IV) the replication-associated base mismatches are corrected by the mismatch repair (MMR) system (linked with MSH2, MSH6, MLH1); (V) finally DNA adducts and helix-distorting lesions are removed via nucleotide excision repair (NER), a pathway involving notably ERCC1-XPF endonuclease complex.

For SCLC, HRR alteration is the most prominent characteristic (11,12).

Aside from the loss of functions of both TP53 and RB1 genes as the ubiquitous signature of SCLC (13), MYC is a vital regulator of cell growth and proliferation by enhancing the expression of genes controlling the cell cycle (14). Furthermore, MYC overexpression and/or amplification interacts with the pre-replicative complex and co-localizes with replication foci in early S-phase (15); this phenomenon affects replication initiation and potentially leads to subsequent replication stress.

DDR proteins are overexpressed in SCLC. When compared with non-SCLC, SCLC specimens are characterized by a higher gene expression of DDR pathway mediators such as, for instance, PARP, CHEK1, ATM and ATR (16). These observations served the rationale for evaluating several DNA repair inhibitors in SCLC preclinical models and in clinical trials. DDR inhibitors (such as PARP inhibitors) have shown modest activity as monotherapy in SCLC models and in some patients, only; the current research is evaluating the combination with cytotoxic agents or immunotherapy.

State of the art of PARP inhibition in SCLC

PARPs constitute a family of nuclear enzymes that function as key sensors of both SSB and double-strand DNA breaks. Upon activation, PARPs facilitate chromatin remodeling and promote the recruitment of DNA repair machinery, including proteins involved in HRR (8). In addition, PARP1 plays a critical role in maintaining replication fork stability, contributing to the efficient restart of stalled replication forks following DNA damage, particularly through mechanisms linked to the BER pathway (17).

PARP inhibitors act by two main mechanisms: (I) by trapping the enzyme to the SSBs, therefore preventing the utilization of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+, the substrate of PARPs) (18); (II) by inhibiting PARylation (poly-ADP-ribosylation) (18): PARylation is a post-translational modification in which enzymes of the PARP family most prominently PARP1-transfer ADP-ribose units from NAD+ onto target proteins.

More specifically, upon sensing DNA damage, PARP enzymes catalyze the formation of long, branched chains of poly (ADP-ribose) (PAR) that engage a process of auto-PARylation and covalently link to other nuclear proteins such as histones and DNA repair factors. This modification has several key functional consequences such as chromatin remodeling and recruitment of repair proteins. By inhibiting PARylation, anti-PARP agents inhibit PARP-DNA complexes leading to stalling replication forks and ultimately to the conversion of SSBs to DSBs inducing tumor cell apoptosis. The effectiveness of trapping of PARP classifies PARP inhibitors that vary among different degrees, with talazoparib being at the highest trapping potential (18).

PARP inhibition is not lethal in cells with intact HRR. PARP inhibitors-induced synthetic lethality occurs in breast cancer models with existing deficiency in DNA repair, such as deleterious mutations in BRCA1/2 (19). In SCLC, however, PARP inhibition is independent of the BRCA1 status and likely depends more on intrinsically high levels of replication stress and genomic instability (6).

The first clinical trial evaluating PARP inhibitors in SCLC has been done in a small cohort of platinum-sensitive patients following relapse. The single agent talazoparib was tested in an expansion cohort as part of the first-in-man phase 1 trial (20); among the 23 accrued patients, two (9%) achieved a partial response that did not last over four months. With four patients achieving stable disease, the disease control rate was 26% (6 patients). Median PFS for the overall SCLC cohort was 11 weeks.

Temozolomide (TMZ), an alkylating agent, has been combined with the veliparib PARP inhibitor in a SCLC patient population including 59% platinum-refractory patients and 33% 3rd line patients (both poor presentation features of relapsed SCLC) (21,22). PFS at four months did not significantly differed between TMZ/veliparib (36%) and TMZ/placebo (27%; P=0.19). However, patients receiving the combination of TMZ/veliparib had a statistically significant higher response rate as compared to the TMZ/placebo arm (39% vs. 14%; P<0.01).

The clinical results of the TMZ/veliparib association were analysed according to several markers to define the subpopulation with the best benefit. The methylation status of the O6-methylguanine-DNA-methyltransferase MGMT gene promoter did not influence the results. PARP1 expression failed to predict outcome. However, an SLFN11 H-score of 1% tumor cells or higher, was associated with a numerically better OS in the veliparib/TMZ arm (22).

SLFN11 is a key marker of PARP inhibitors activity

SLFN11 (part of the Schlafen enzyme family) is a DNA/RNA helicase involved in S-phase checkpoint arrest after DNA replication fork stress from DNA damage (23). Its action is independent from ATR and ATM activation. Replication protein A (RPA) is a heterotrimeric protein complex. In normal cell, RPA protects the transiently formed single-strand DNA from nucleolytic degradation and serves as a physical platform for the recruitment of DNA damage response factors (24). In SCLC, SLFN11 blocks DNA replication. This leads to S-phase arrest, conversion of SSB to DSB, and subsequent apoptosis (Figure 1).

Interaction between PARP inhibitors and SLFN11. PARP inhibitors block PARP-mediated base excision repair. Unrepaired SSBs convert into DSBs during DNA replication. Cells rely on homologous recombination for repair; HR deficiency leads to synthetic lethality. Replication stress activates different signals and promotes SLFN11 recruitment which is highly expressed in selected H-score positive patients. SLFN11 enforces irreversible replication arrest and enhances sensitivity to PARP inhibitors. The combined effect promotes tumor cell death. DSB, double-strand break; HR deficiency, homologous repair deficiency; PARP, poly (adenosine diphosphate-ribose) polymerases; RPA, replication protein A; SLFN11, Schlafen family member 11; SSB, single-strand break.

SLFN11 acts as a key determinant of response by converting replication stress into lethal DNA damage, particularly in tumors that are already compromised in their DNA repair capacity. SLFN11 enhances sensitivity to DNA-damaging therapies by interfering with DNA replication during the S phase of the cell cycle. Mechanistically, it promotes the degradation of key proteins required for replication fork progression and reduces chromatin accessibility, thereby limiting the ability of the replication. This leads to sustained replication stress (i.e., inability of tumor cells to duplicate their DNA).

In SCLC cells with HRD, this replication stress cannot be adequately resolved (25). As a result, replication forks collapse, leading to the accumulation of double-strand DNA breaks. Due to HRD, the DNA damage remains unrepaired, ultimately triggering apoptosis.

In contrast, in homologous recombination (HR)-proficient cells, DNA repair pathways are intact but become functionally impaired in the presence of SLFN11. Specifically, SLFN11-associated protein complexes hinder the recruitment and assembly of HRR proteins at sites of DNA damage. This interference delays or prevents effective repair, allowing DSBs to accumulate over time and eventually lead to apoptosis, albeit less efficiently than in HR-deficient cells.

Considering the constitutive low homologous recombination potential of SCLC, a high SLFN11 expression is a predictive marker of sensitivity to PARP inhibitors such as talazoparib in the study comparing ATZ maintenance vs. combined ATZ and talazoparib maintenance.

Interplay between PARP and the immune system: therapeutic implications

At the dawn of the immunotherapy era in lung cancer, SCLC was expected to become a model of ICI sensitive cancer owing to its highest mutational burdens (26). However, the first clinical trials of ICI for SCLC was disappointing; albeit clinically significant, ICI activity appeared not to reach the magnitude of benefit observed in other disease, the clinical efficacy of checkpoint inhibitors in this disease seems to be far less than expected and significantly lower when compared with that observed in NSCLC. The question asked by the study by Karim et al., could be “are PARP inhibitors able to boost SCLC sensitivity to anti-PD-L1 therapy?” Upregulation of PDL-1 expression in SCLC preclinical studies and the activation of the interferon genes pathway (both parts of the innate immune system) are initiated by the generation of DBS (27). Therefore, PARP inhibitors, by producing PARP-DNA complexes ultimately convert SSBs to DSBs. Subsequently, cytosolic DNA fragments from PARP inhibitors enhance the anti-tumor activity of PD1/PDL1 therapy. The activation of the innate immune system also increases the chemokine signaling, which leads to type I interferon response, PDL-1 expression, and cytotoxic T-cell infiltration, favoring the anti-tumor immunity and preparing ICI action.

Conclusions

Cautions are needed with interpreting the study by Karim et al. for several reasons: the SLFN11 threshold H-score was arbitrarily set at 1% of positive cells; the analysis has been done of archive specimens taken at time of diagnosis and expression of SLFN11 might have changed during the four-cycle chemoimmunotherapy induction; the study strength is limited by the small size of the population.

However, this study is an important step toward validation of two main hypotheses: the effect of PARP inhibitors is greatly dependent on the real state of DDR proteins, mainly SLFN11 and homology-directed repair (HDR) deficiency; there is a possible pathway to improve SCLC sensitivity to ICI by manipulating the DNA damage response.

Fast and Robust Non-Adiabatic Holonomic Gates for Qutrit Systems
Jie Lu, Jie-Dong Huang, Yang Qian, Ying Yan

Abstract
Motivated by shortcuts to adiabaticity, we develop a framework that combines inverse engineering with time-dependent perturbation theory to implement non-adiabatic holonomic quantum computing (NHQC) in qutrit systems under realistic error sources. We derive analytical conditions that suppress second-order Rabi errors through tailored pulse parameters and eliminate detuning errors via a compensation pulse. Our analysis reveals the cancellation from the compensation pulse, which ensures robust gate operations. The proposed approach, broadly applicable to superconducting qubits, trapped ions, and atom-based platforms, provides a pathway to fast and error-resilient holonomic gates, potentially for scalable quantum computing.

II.1
Non-adiabatic Geometric phase

To implement non-adiabatic quantum evolution, we construct a time-dependent, transitionless orthonormal basis
\{|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle\}

Berry2009TransitionlessQD
, each satisfying the Schr
\rm{\ddot{o}}
dinger equation
(with
\hbar=1

i\frac{d}{dt}|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle=H(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle

(2)

in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. This ensures no transitions occur between states
|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
. When
|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
aligns with the instantaneous eigenstates of
H(t)
, the evolution becomes adiabatic.

We consider cyclic evolutions in which the state
|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle
returns to its initial form, up to a phase factor
\gamma_{n}(t)

\tau

|\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle=e^{i\gamma_{n}(t)}|\phi_{n}(0)\rangle.

(3)

In this case, the physical state evolves back to its original form,
\rho(\tau)=|\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle\langle\phi_{n}(\tau)|=|\phi_{n}(0)\rangle\langle\phi_{n}(0)|
. We define a new basis
\{|\chi_{n}(t)\rangle\}
in the projective Hilbert space
\mathcal{P(H)}
:

|\chi_{n}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\gamma_{n}}|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle.

(4)

The cyclic evolution condition (
3
) implies:

|\chi_{n}(\tau)\rangle=|\chi_{n}(0)\rangle=|\phi_{n}(0)\rangle.

(5)

The total phase
\gamma_{n}
decomposes into a dynamical phase
\gamma^{d}_{n}
and a geometric phase
\gamma^{g}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\gamma^{d}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle\phi_{n}(t)|H(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle dt,

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma^{g}_{n}

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle\chi_{n}(t)|\dot{\chi}_{n}(t)\rangle dt,

(7)

where
\gamma^{g}_{n}
, the non-adiabatic Abelian geometric phase (Aharonov-Anandan phase)

in the adiabatic limit.

Two primary methods eliminate the dynamical phase:

1.

Parallel Transport Condition
: Enforcing

\langle\phi_{n}(t)|H(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle=0,

(8)

nullifies the dynamical phase at all times.

2.

Final Dynamical Phase Cancellation
: Ensuring

\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle\phi_{n}(t)|H(t)|\phi_{n}(t)\rangle dt=0,

(9)

using techniques like spin echo, cancels the dynamical phase at
t=\tau

Under conditions (
3
)–(
5
) and (
8
)–(
9

U(\tau)=\sum_{n}e^{i\gamma_{n}}|\phi_{n}(0)\rangle\langle\phi_{n}(0)|,

(10)

where
\gamma_{n}=\gamma^{g}_{n}
represents the geometric phase shift for

III
Time-dependent perturbation theory and Systematic error

We summarize and present a time-dependent perturbation framework to analyze systematic errors in quantum control robustness, applicable to various systems, providing a clear theoretical guide for robust gate design.

We consider a quantum system with state
|\psi(t)\rangle
evolving under a Hamiltonian comprising a time-dependent control term
H_{c}(t)
and a perturbation
H^{\prime}(t)
. The Schrödinger equation is:

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=[H_{c}(t)+H^{{}^{\prime}}(t)]|\psi(t)\rangle\,

(27)

In the absence of perturbation, we define the unperturbed state
|\psi^{(0)}(t)\rangle
and time-evolution operator
U^{(0)}(t)
as:

\displaystyle|\psi^{(0)}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U^{(0)}(t)|\psi(0)\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle U^{(0)}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}\exp\left[\int_{0}^{T}H_{c}(t)dt\right]\,

(29)

To account for the time-dependent
H_{c}(t)
and its instantaneous eigenstates, we introduce an error picture, distinct from the traditional interaction picture where
H_{c}
is time-independent. We define the state
|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle
via:

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U^{(0)}(t)|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle

(30)

\displaystyle H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)

\displaystyle=U^{(0)\dagger}(t)H^{{}^{\prime}}(t)U^{(0)}(t)

(31)

In this error picture,
|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle
satisfies the Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle

(32)

The states
|\psi(t)\rangle
and
|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle
are expanded in a perturbative series:

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle=|\psi^{(0)}(t)\rangle+|\psi^{(1)}(t)\rangle+|\psi^{(2)}(t)\rangle+...

(33)

\displaystyle|\psi_{E}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U_{E}(t)|\psi(0)\rangle=|\psi_{E}^{(0)}(t)\rangle+|\psi_{E}^{(1)}(t)\rangle+|\psi_{E}^{(2)}(t)\rangle+...

(34)

where
U_{E}(t)
is the error-picture time-evolution operator, expressed as a Dyson series:

\displaystyle U_{E}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}\exp\left[\int^{\tau}_{0}dtH^{{}^{\prime}}_{E}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=U_{E}^{(0)}(\tau)+U_{E}^{(1)}(\tau)+U_{E}^{(2)}(\tau)+...

\displaystyle=1-i\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{1}H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(-i\right)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{2}H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{2})+...

(35)

Thus, the overlap amplitude between the evolved state
|\psi(\tau)\rangle
and target state
|\psi_{\text{tg}}\rangle
is:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\text{tg}}|\psi(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi^{(0)}(0)\left|U_{E}(\tau)\right|\psi^{(0)}(0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=1+O_{1}+O_{2}+...

(36)

with first- and second-order corrections:

\displaystyle O_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi^{(0)}(0)\left|U_{E}^{(1)}(\tau)\right|\psi^{(0)}(0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\left\langle\psi^{(0)}(t)\left|H^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right|\psi^{(0)}(t)\right\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle O_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-i\right)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{2}\left\langle\psi^{(0)}(0)\left|H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})H_{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{2})\right|\psi^{(0)}(0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{2}\left[E_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})E_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{2})+\sum_{m\neq 1}H_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})H_{m1}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{2})\right],

(38)

with matrix elements:

H_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)=\left\langle\psi_{1}^{(0)}(t)\left|H^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right|\psi_{m}^{(0)}(0)\right\rangle

(39)

The orthonormal basis inserted into the second-order amplitude is:

\displaystyle\left\{\left|\psi_{n}^{(0)}(t)\right\rangle=U^{(0)}(t,0)|\psi_{n}^{(0)}(0)\rangle,n=1,2,3...\right\}

(40)

where
|\psi_{1}^{(0)}(t)\rangle
coincides with the unperturbed state
|\psi^{(0)}(t)\rangle
. The fidelity is:

\displaystyle P(\tau)

\displaystyle=|\langle\psi_{\text{tg}}|\psi(\tau)\rangle|^{2}=|1+O_{1}+O_{2}+...|^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq 1+O_{1}O_{1}^{*}+O_{2}+O_{2}^{*},

(41)

where the first-order correction vanishes as
O_{1}
is purely imaginary, making the second-order term
\mathcal{O}(H^{\prime 2}(t))
the leading correction.

III.3
The Compensation pulse against detuning error

Compensation pulses, employed in quantum optics

We apply a gate pulse over

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(t)=\tilde{H}_{c}(t)+H^{\prime}(t),

\displaystyle\theta\to\tilde{\theta}=\pi-\theta,\quad\phi\to\tilde{\phi}=\pi+\phi.

For the second stage, we introduce a new basis set for the second-order amplitude:

\displaystyle\{|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{(0)}(t)\rangle=\tilde{U}^{(0)}(t,\tau)|\psi_{n}^{(0)}(\tau)\rangle,\,n=1,2,3,\ldots\}.

The fidelity, accounting for both evolution stages, is:

\displaystyle P(2\tau)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{m\neq 1}\left\{\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}dtH_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right|^{2}-\left|\int_{\tau}^{2\tau}dt\tilde{H}_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\left.-\int_{0}^{\tau}dt_{1}\int_{\tau}^{2\tau}dt_{2}\left[H_{m1}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{1})\tilde{H}_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{2})+H_{m1}^{{}^{\prime}*}(t_{1})\tilde{H}_{1m}^{{}^{\prime}*}(t_{2})\right]\right\}.

(59)

Since the second-order Rabi error is eliminated via Eq. (
48
), we focus on the detuning error,
H^{\prime}=\delta|e\rangle\langle e|
. The fidelity at
t=2\tau
, accurate to order
\delta^{2}
, is:

\displaystyle P(2\tau)

\displaystyle=|\langle\psi_{\text{tg}}|\psi(2\tau)\rangle|^{2}

(60)

\displaystyle\simeq 1-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}W^{2}\left\{2|c_{d}(0)|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left[1-|c_{d}(0)|^{2}\right]\left[2|c_{d}(0)|^{2}+1\right]\left(1+\cos\gamma\right)\right\},

where
\gamma=\beta(\tau)-\beta(0)
. The compensation pulse fully cancels the detuning error
O_{13}^{\delta}
, demonstrating its efficacy in experimental settings. The remaining terms, proportional to
W=\int_{0}^{\tau/2}e^{-i2n\alpha(t)}\sin\alpha(t)dt
, are suppressed by
e^{-i2n\alpha(t)}
under Eq. (
48

\gamma=\pi

V
Discussion and Conclusion

We develop a pulse engineering strategy for fast and robust non-adiabatic holonomic quantum gates in qutrit systems. By optimizing
\beta(t)
and
\alpha(t)
, we eliminate second-order Rabi errors, while a compensation pulse cancels second-order detuning errors. These techniques substantially enhance gate fidelity, as shown in Figures
6
,
6
, and
7
.

We examine four quantum gates (NOT, Hadamard, S, and T), with analytical and numerical results aligning closely, as shown in Tables
2
and
3
, confirming the reliability and experimental feasibility of our framework. Compared to the OSS-NHQC scheme, our approach demonstrates enhanced fidelity for Rabi and detuning errors, as evidenced by Figures
6
,
6
, and
7
. This robustness stems from the optimized pulse configuration (
a=b=4
) and analytical derivations demonstrating error suppression by the compensation pulse, providing a clear theoretical guide for gate design.

The analytical framework allows flexible tuning of
\beta(t)
and
\alpha(t)
to adapt to hardware constraints across platforms like superconducting qubits, ion- and atom-based systems, among others. This adaptability supports tailored pulse designs for diverse quantum systems.

The compensation pulse doubles the gate time, potentially increasing decoherence. Integrating time-optimal methods, such as Brachistochrone control
wang2015quantum
;
liu2020brachistochrone
or path minimization
xu2018path
;
li2020approach
;
ding2021nonadiabatic
;
liang2022composite
, can reduce evolution time while maintaining error suppression.

Future directions include extending to multi-qubit gates, addressing higher-order errors, and combining with error-correction techniques to advance scalable quantum computing.

The fidelity over
t\in[0,\tau]

We derive the ideal time-evolution operator for a three-level NHQC system using the transitionless driving equations (

,
10

5
,
18

4

16

\displaystyle U^{(0)}(t_{2},t_{1})

\displaystyle=|d\rangle\langle d|+e^{i[\varphi_{+}(t_{2})-\varphi_{+}(t_{1})]}|\chi_{+}(t_{2})\rangle\langle\chi_{+}(t_{1})|

\displaystyle\quad+e^{i[\varphi_{-}(t_{2})-\varphi_{-}(t_{1})]}|\chi_{-}(t_{2})\rangle\langle\chi_{-}(t_{1})|,

(65)

where
\varphi_{+}(t)
and
\varphi_{-}(t)
are the accumulated phases of states
|\chi_{+}(t)\rangle
and
|\chi_{-}(t)\rangle
, respectively, governing transitions in the
\{|b\rangle,|e\rangle\}

For
t_{2}=t

t_{1}=0

\displaystyle U(t,0)

(66)

This describes the gate operation, preserving the dark state
|d\rangle
and driving transitions between
|b\rangle
and
|e\rangle

To compute second-order fidelity corrections under perturbation (
38

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}^{(0)}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=|\psi^{(0)}(t)\rangle=U^{(0)}(t)[c_{d}(0)|d\rangle+c_{b}(0)|b\rangle],

(67)

\displaystyle|\psi_{2}^{(0)}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U^{(0)}(t)\frac{c_{b}(0)}{|c_{b}(0)|}|e\rangle,

(68)

\displaystyle|\psi_{3}^{(0)}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=U^{(0)}(t)[-c_{b}^{*}(0)|d\rangle+c_{d}^{*}(0)|b\rangle].

(69)

Here,
|\psi_{1}^{(0)}(t)\rangle
is selected as the unperturbed evolution to simplify calculations. The fidelity at

is:

\displaystyle P(\tau)

\displaystyle=1-\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}H_{12}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}H_{13}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|^{2}\right],

(70)

with matrix elements for the perturbation (

\displaystyle H_{12}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{1}^{(0)}(t)|H^{{}^{\prime}}|\psi_{2}^{(0)}(t)\rangle

(71)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\beta(0)}|c_{b}(0)|e^{-i(\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-})}\left\{\frac{\delta}{2}\sin\alpha(t)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left.+\epsilon\Omega(t)\left[e^{i(\beta-\xi)}\cos^{2}\frac{\alpha(t)}{2}-e^{-i(\beta-\xi)}\sin^{2}\frac{\alpha(t)}{2}\right]\right\},

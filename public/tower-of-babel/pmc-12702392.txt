The relationship between activities of daily living performance and self-efficacy among clients with hand injury in Indian context- A cross-sectional study protocol
Authors: Shejal A Rao, Koushik Sau, Shovan Saha, Vani R Lakshmi, Ashwath M. Acharya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hand injuries are among the most common musculoskeletal injuries and can significantly impair an individual’s ability to perform activities of daily living (ADL), thereby impacting quality of life. Self-efficacy plays a vital role in influencing daily performance and recovery following injury. This cross-sectional study aims to explore the relationship between ADL performance and self-efficacy among clients with hand injuries within the Indian context. Secondary objectives of this study include assessing self-efficacy levels and evaluating ADL performance in this population.• A self-administered, closed-ended, structured questionnaire comprising performance-based and self-efficacy measures will be used for data collection. Participants will include adults aged 18 years and above who have sustained fractures of the hand or wrist, including digits, and have undergone surgical treatment.• Clients will be recruited from the Occupational Therapy department.• The findings aim to highlight the importance of considering both objective and subjective measures in occupational therapy assessment and to emphasize the role of self-efficacy in ADL performance following hand injuries, potentially informing culturally sensitive rehabilitation interventions.

Background

Hand injuries are the most frequently occurring body injuries, constituting 6.6 % to 28 % of all musculoskeletal injuries [1]. Hand injuries affect the individual's performance in activities of daily living (ADL), which includes dressing, bathing, toileting, etc. [2]. ADL are activities focused on taking care of one’s own body and are completed daily [3]. When individuals have difficulty performing these ADL, it can affect their quality of life [4]. An individual's recovery after a hand injury can be achieved with timely and appropriate surgical treatment and rehabilitation [5].

According to Bandura self-efficacy is a person’s belief in his or her capacity to execute necessary behaviours [6]. Daily performance is influenced by how people feel, think, motivate and behave, which impacts their self-efficacy [7]. Occupational therapists as rehabilitation professionals are indicated to help clients with hand injuries. Achieving ADL functions is an important therapeutic goal of occupational therapy practice [8].

Culture significantly shapes and influences individual’s beliefs in their abilities as well as their actions and behaviours in various contexts. Bandura [6] highlights the increasing importance of studying self-efficacy in various cultural contexts and reports that there are differences in self-efficacy between cultures. A higher level of self-efficacy indicates better functioning in daily living and therefore leads to better well-being of clients with hand injuries [9].

Occupational therapists conduct performance-based assessments to get accurate insights into client’s functional abilities. However, client’s self-efficacy is also valuable, particularly in finding how they perceive their hand usage after hand injury in ADL. Occupational therapists combine objective performance data with client’s subjective input to understand the client’s perspective and how it impacts on ADL performance of clients with hand injuries [10]. Hence, the review of literature aimed to explore the importance of assessing self-efficacy towards ADL among clients with hand injuries.

This study aims to address the following research question:•What is the relationship between activities of daily living performance and self-efficacy among clients with hand injuries in the Indian context?

Description of protocol

Study setting

The study will be conducted in the Occupational Therapy department.

Study design

This study is cross-sectional in nature, focusing on finding the relationship between activities of daily living performance and self-efficacy among clients with hand injuries in the Indian context. A self-administered, closed-ended, structured questionnaire is used as a data collection tool.

Participant recruitment

Participants in this study will be recruited from the Occupational Therapy Department. A convenience sampling method will be employed to select clients who meet the study’s inclusion criteria.

Selection criteria for study participants

The inclusion of the participants will be based on the following criteria: Clients aged 18 years and above, having a fracture in the wrist and hand, including the fingers, and having had surgical treatment for the same, and those who can read and write English or Kannada are included in the study. Individuals with spinal cord injury, head injury, multiple injuries, and fractures associated with nerve injury will be excluded from the study.

Sample size

The sample size for this study was calculated using the formula:n=(Z1α/2+Z1−β/0.5×ln[(1+r)/(1−r)])2+3,where α = 5 % (significance level), β = 80 % (power), and r = 0.4 (anticipated correlation between self-efficacy and ADL performance) [11]. Based on the statistical calculation, the sample size would be 56. Therefore, the study will aim to recruit a minimum of 56 clients with hand injuries to ensure adequate power for detecting the expected correlation.

Data collection procedure

Before the participation, all study participants will receive a detailed explanation of the study objectives, procedures, and their rights as participants. Informed consent will be obtained from each individual before inclusion in the study.

At the commencement of data collection, participants will be provided with a structured data collection proforma designed to gather demographic information, including age, gender, educational level, hand dominance, marital status, support system, employment status, cause of injury, site of hand injury, and duration since injury. The proforma will also include a question to determine whether the participant has sustained any previous injuries.

Subsequently, participants will be instructed to complete two standardized, self-administered, pen-and-paper questionnaires: the Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand (DASH) questionnaire and the General Self-Efficacy Scale.

Data collection is scheduled to take place from July 2025 to January 2026.

Data collection instrument

Generalised self-efficacy scale: This is a self-reported measure administered to adults. It has 10 items and reflects optimistic self-belief. The scores are not at all true = 1, hardly true = 2, moderately true = 3, and exactly true = 4. This scale has good internal consistency and criterion validity [12].

Disabilities of arm, shoulder, and hand (DASH): This is a self-reported measure administered to adults with upper extremity musculoskeletal disorders or injuries. The components of this scale include physical functioning, social/role functioning, and optional modules such as sports or performing arts and work. The scoring criteria is no difficulty = 1, mild difficulty = 2, moderate difficulty = 3, severe difficulty = 4, and unable = 5. It has good internal consistency and construct validity [13].Data Analysis

Descriptive statistics will be employed to summarize participant characteristics and key study variables. Continuous variables such as age, self-efficacy scores, and ADL performance scores will be analysed using measures of central tendency and dispersion, specifically the mean and standard deviation for normally distributed data, or the median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data [14]. Categorical variables, including gender, type, and severity of hand injury, and duration since injury, will be presented as frequencies and percentages.

To examine the relationship between self-efficacy and ADL performance, correlation analysis will be conducted. If the data are normally distributed, Pearson’s correlation coefficient will be used to assess the strength and direction of the linear relationship between self-efficacy scores and ADL performance scores. In case of data that are not normally distributed, Spearman’s rank correlation will be applied as a non-parametric alternative. This analysis will help determine whether individuals with greater self-efficacy tend to perform better and achieve higher outcomes.

Ethical consideration and data management

Ethics approval has been obtained from Kasturba Medical College and Kasturba Hospital Institutional Ethics Committee-2. CTRI approval has been obtained, and the CTRI registration number for this trial (CTRI/2025/07/090,113). All the data will be anonymised and stored in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. Microsoft Excel is part of the Microsoft 365 suite, which offers strong security features, including encryption, password protection, and secure cloud storage through OneDrive or SharePoint. Data access will be restricted to authorised members of the research team, with individual accounts managed by the study administrator.

Significance of the study

Enhancing self-efficacy empowers clients to take an active role in recovery, fostering independence in ADL and improving their quality of life. It also explains whether self-efficacy influences a client’s ADL performance after a hand injury. Therapists can tailor more client-centred interventions that may enhance confidence in their abilities, leading to better ADL outcomes.

Protocol validation

None.

Limitations

We anticipate some limitations in the study. As both self-efficacy and ADL performance are assessed using self-administered questionnaires, which may be influenced by response bias, recall bias, or social desirability. Participants might over- or under-estimate their abilities. The cross-sectional design prevents drawing conclusions about cause-and-effect relationships and using convenience sampling may restrict how widely the results can be applied to other populations

Quantum Circuit for Non-Unitary Linear Transformation of Basis Sets
Guorui Zhu, Joel Bierman, Jianfeng Lu, Yingzhou Li

Abstract
This paper introduces a novel approach to implementing non-unitary linear transformations of basis on quantum computational platforms, a significant leap beyond the conventional unitary methods. By integrating Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) into the process, the method achieves an operational depth of O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) with about n n ancilla qubits, enhancing the computational capabilities for analyzing fermionic systems. The non-unitarity of the transformation allows us to transform a wave function from one basis to another, which can span different spaces. By this trick, we can calculate the overlap of two wavefunctions that live in different (but non-distinct Hilbert subspaces) with different basis representations. This provides the opportunity to use state specific ansatzes to calculate different energy eigenstates under orbital-optimized settings and may improve the accuracy when computing the energies of multiple eigenstates simultaneously in VQE or other framework. It allows for a deeper exploration of complex quantum states and phenomena, expanding the practical applications of quantum computing in physics and chemistry.

3.

Quantum circuits are proposed to evaluate the inner product of

\wedge V=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\wedge^{k}V

\left\{\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}\mid 1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n\text{ and }k\geq 0\right\}.

The complex Givens rotation is composed of three parts: two phase

The regular real Givens rotation by an angle

\theta_{pq}=\arccos\frac{|u_{pi}|}{\sqrt{|u_{pi}|^{2}+|u_{qi}|^{2}}}.

III
Non-Unitary Linear Transformation

We naturally raise a question: when
u
is non-unitary, can we extend the
linear transformation in
Section

II
? The answer is
yes, but the derivation is more complicated and the resulting quantum
circuit is about twice the depth of the unitary one. There are at least
two potential applications for the non-unitary linear transformation: a)
calculating the overlap between two states under different bases, which is
detailed in the next section; b) change of basis to a non-orthogonal one.

As we mentioned earlier, the Thouless theorem in
(
2
) form is too difficult to be extended to
non-unitary case, which is mainly due to the logarithm of a non-unitary
matrix. Hence, we stick to exterior algebra representation of Thouless
theorem and all later derivations are in the language of exterior algebra.
We recall the notation we use, i.e.,
U(u;\psi)
and
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
. When

u
is a unitary matrix, two operators are equal
U(u;\psi)=\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
, for
U(u;\psi)
,
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{u}
being defined in
(
2
),
Definition

1
, and (
5
),
respectively. When
u
is non-unitary, we use
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
for the linear
map and avoid using
U(u;\psi)
.

In order to reduce the overall circuit depth for the non-unitary linear
transformation, our construction relies on the singular value
decomposition (SVD) of
u
and hence its operator
\boldsymbol{u}
. Let the SVD of a
non-unitary matrix
u
be of form,

u=LDR,

where
L
and
R
are left and right singular vectors,
D=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
is a diagonal matrix with
singular values in non-increasing ordering, i.e.,
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{n}
. Without loss of generality, we further assume that
u
is
a nonunitary matrix with 2-norm bounded by one, i.e.,
1\geq\sigma_{1}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{n}
. Singular vectors
L
and
R
are unitary
matrices. Let
\{\ket{\psi_i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the set of underlying basis
set. The operator
\boldsymbol{u}
associated with
u
also admits an operator SVD,

\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{R},

(20)

where
\boldsymbol{L}
,
\boldsymbol{D}
, and
\boldsymbol{R}
are associated with matrices
L
,
D
, and

R
respectively,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}L_{ij}\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_j},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{D}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{R}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}R_{ij}\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_j}.

By
Lemma

2
, we have
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}=(\wedge\boldsymbol{L})(\wedge\boldsymbol{D})(\wedge\boldsymbol{R})
. For unitary matrices
L
and
R
, the
operators
\wedge\boldsymbol{L}=U(L;\psi)
and
\wedge\boldsymbol{R}=U(R;\psi)
can be
implemented by quantum circuits as in
Section

II
,
e.g., analog quantum circuits of
Fig.

2
. In
the following, we focus on the quantum circuit construction for the wedged
diagonal operator
\wedge\boldsymbol{D}
.

We first rewrite
\wedge\boldsymbol{D}
as a composition of at most
n
simple
operators, which are simple to be implemented by quantum gates. According
to
Definition

1
, the action of
\wedge\boldsymbol{D}
on an exterior
algebra is defined as applying the operator
\boldsymbol{D}
to all one-body states,
as,

\begin{split}&(\wedge\boldsymbol{D})\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}\\
=\,&\boldsymbol{D}\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\boldsymbol{D}\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\boldsymbol{D}\ket{\psi_{i_k}}\\
=\,&\sigma_{i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}.\end{split}

(21)

It is worth noting that this expression is closely related to the particle
number operator. It acts like a weighted number operator. More explicitly,
if the many-body state includes the state
\ket{\psi_i}
, the result of
the operation will give a factor
\sigma_{i}
. Thus, we define an operator

\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})=\boldsymbol{1}+(\sigma_{j}-1)\boldsymbol{n}(\psi_{j}),

which admits,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})(\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma_{j}(\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}),&j\in\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}\\
\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}},&j\notin\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}\end{cases}.

Here
\boldsymbol{n}(\psi_{j})=\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\psi_{j})\boldsymbol{a}(\psi_{j})
is the number
operator. Next, we justify that the wedge operator
\wedge\boldsymbol{D}
is
equivalent to the composition of
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})
. For any basis

\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}
in the exterior
algebra (Fock space), the action of the composition of
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})

admits,

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})\right)\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}}

which equals (
21
). Hence, we conclude that the
product operator
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})
is another form of
\wedge\boldsymbol{D}
, i.e.,

\wedge\boldsymbol{D}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j}).

Now we move on to the quantum circuit implementation of composed operator

\wedge\boldsymbol{D}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})
. Obviously,
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})
is not
a unitary operator, and hence, cannot be implemented by quantum circuits
directly. We introduce an ancilla qubit to encode the action of

\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})
. In the context of the Jordan-Wigner transformation, the
particle number operator admits,

\boldsymbol{n}_{j}=I_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{j-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{n}.

We define an operator acting on qubit state as
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{j}
admitting,

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{j}=\,&J_{\psi}\boldsymbol{\nu}(\psi_{j})J_{\psi}^{-1}\\
=\,&I_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{j-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sigma_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{n},\end{split}

to
facilitate the calculations. Note that the auxiliary basis will not affect

(23)

\begin{split}\innerproduct{\Phi}{\Psi}=\,&(\ket{\psi_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\psi_{i_k}},\ket{\phi_{j_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\phi_{j_k}})\\

}}\end{pmatrix}\\

V
Summary

This paper proposes a novel quantum circuit design for non-unitary linear
transformations of basis sets. The non-unitary linear transformations of
basis sets could be used in many practical scenarios, including but not
limited to the change of basis in initial state preparation, evaluating
the overlapping between two states under different basis sets in excited
state calculation under the VQE framework, etc.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be a non-unitary linear transformation of one-body basis sets,

\boldsymbol{u}:V\to V
for
V
being the space spanned by one-body bases. The
designed quantum circuit implements the wedged map on the exterior algebra
of
V
(Fock Space), i.e.,
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}:\wedge V\to\wedge V
. To reduce
the overall circuit complexity, we first calculate an SVD of
\boldsymbol{u}
. The
left and right singular vector operators of
\boldsymbol{u}
are unitary and are
implemented by the unitary linear transformation proposed in

[
11
]
, which is reviewed in

Section

II
. The quantum circuit for the singular
values of
\boldsymbol{u}
are detailed in
Section

III
.
Block encodings are applied to all singular values strictly between zero
and one. The same block encoding could be applied to zero singular value
as well. Instead, we propose a quantum circuit to encoding all zero
singular values together with only one extra ancilla qubit. For singular
values that are sufficiently close to one or zero, we further round big
ones up to one and truncate small ones to zero. After the rounding-up and
truncating, the quantum circuit complexity is reduced while the
approximation errors are well-controlled. As a result, the proposed
quantum circuit achieves a depth of
O(n)
, for
n
being the size of the
basis set. The extra required number of ancilla qubits is the number of
singular values strictly between zero and one.

Using the proposed quantum circuit for the non-unitary linear
transformation, we further provide quantum circuit evaluating the inner
product of two many-body states under different basis sets in

Section

IV
. Three quantum circuits for the inner product
evaluation are proposed with various number of ancilla qubits and
measurements. Combining the inner product quantum circuits with the
excited state calculation under VQE framework, we could immediately apply
different basis optimization for different many-body states, which
implement the state-specific CASSCF/OptOrbFCI on quantum computer.

Appendix A
Details of Unitary Case

This section gives another proof of Thouless theorem using exterior
algebra. The proof offers a more intuitive understanding of the
construction process for the non-unitary linear transformations.
Additionally, it shows that the wedged map
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
defined
in
Definition

1
is consistent
U(u;\psi)
in Thouless
theorem and the work
Kivlichan et al. [11]
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces of dimension
n
. Operator

\boldsymbol{u}:V\to V
is unitary, and admits (
5
). Notice that
\boldsymbol{u}
is
unitary and is a finite dimensional normal operator. The
eigendecomposition of
\boldsymbol{u}
admits,

\boldsymbol{u}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{\imath\phi_{k}}\ket{\sigma_k}\bra{\sigma_k},

for
\ket{\sigma_k}
being its eigenstate associated with eigenvalue

e^{\imath\phi_{k}}
. The eigenstates of
\boldsymbol{u}
,

\{\ket{\sigma_k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
are orthonormal bases of
V
. Hence, the set
of many-body states,
\{\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_k}\mid 1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n,\text{ and }1\leq k\leq n\}
is also a basis of
\wedge V
.

Next, we derive the application of
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
on bases of
\wedge V
.
By the definition of
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
, we have

\wedge\boldsymbol{u}\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}}=e^{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{k}n_{k}}\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}},

(28)

for all
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
, and
1\leq k\leq n
, where

n_{k}
is defined as,

n_{k}=\begin{cases}0&k\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\},\\
1&k\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}.\end{cases}

Notice that
n_{k}
plays a similar role as the particle number operator

\boldsymbol{n}(\sigma_{k})
. The right-hand-side of (
28
) equals to
the application of
\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{\imath\phi_{k}\boldsymbol{n}(\nu_{k})}
to

\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}}
. Further, we
know that all particle number operators commute with each other. Hence, we
obtain,

\displaystyle\wedge\boldsymbol{u}\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{k}n_{k}}\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{k=1}^{n}i\phi_{k}\boldsymbol{n}(\sigma_{k})}\ket{\sigma_{i_1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ket{\sigma_{i_k}},

for all
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
, and
1\leq k\leq n
. We
conclude that
\wedge\boldsymbol{u}=e^{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{k}\boldsymbol{n}(\sigma_{k})}
.

Comparing to the operator
U(u;\psi)
, the basis in the above derivation
is
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, which is different from
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
in

U(u;\psi)
. Since both
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
and
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}

are bases of
V
, the basis transformation between these two is unitary.
As derived in
[
35
]
, the transformation of creators and
annihilators for two basis sets are

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\innerproduct{\psi_m}{\sigma_k}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\psi_{m}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}(\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\innerproduct{\sigma_k}{\psi_m}\boldsymbol{a}(\psi_{m}).

The exponent in
e^{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{k}\boldsymbol{n}(\sigma_{k})}=\wedge\boldsymbol{u}
could be rewritten as,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{\ell}\boldsymbol{n}(\sigma_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{\ell}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\sigma_{\ell})\boldsymbol{a}(\sigma_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{\ell}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\innerproduct{\psi_p}{\sigma_\ell}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\psi_{p})\sum_{q=1}^{n}\innerproduct{\sigma_\ell}{\psi_q}\boldsymbol{a}(\psi_{q})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\bra{\psi_p}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\imath\phi_{\ell}\ket{\sigma_\ell}\bra{\sigma_\ell}\right)\ket{\psi_q}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\psi_{p})\boldsymbol{a}(\psi_{q})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\bra{\psi_p}(\log\boldsymbol{u})\ket{\psi_q}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}(\psi_{p})\boldsymbol{a}(\psi_{q}).

Recall the definition of operator
\boldsymbol{u}
under basis
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}

as in (
5
). Applying the logarithm matrix function to
\boldsymbol{u}
leads to

\bra{\psi_p}\log\boldsymbol{u}\ket{\psi_q}={(\log u)}_{pq}.

(29)

Substituting (
29
) into the above exponent expression, we prove
the Thouless theorem using exterior algebra,

\wedge\boldsymbol{u}=\exp({\sum_{p,q=1}^n {(\log u)}_{pq} \ba^\dag(\psi_p)
\ba(\psi_q)})=U(u;\psi).

to the

\Diag\{1,\ldots,1,e^{-\imath\phi_{p}},1,\ldots,1\}.

The logarithm of
p_{p}(\phi)
is

\log p_{p}(\phi)=-\imath\phi_{p}E_{pp},

is a zero matrix with a single one at the
(p,p)
-th
position. Substituting the logarithm of

into

\displaystyle U(p_{p}(\phi);\psi)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\exp({- \imath\phi_p \sum_{i,j=1}^n {(E_{pp})}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\exp(- \imath\phi_{p} \bn(\psi_p)),

which proves (
12
).

Then we give the derivation of
(

(p,q)
-th entry of a matrix

\begin{pmatrix}I_{p-1}&&&&\\
&\cos\theta_{pq}&&\sin\theta_{pq}&\\
&&I_{q-p-1}&&\\
&-\sin\theta_{pq}&&\cos\theta_{pq}&\\
&&&&I_{N-q}\end{pmatrix}

for

.
Recall the logarithm of a rotation matrix admits,

\log\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\theta\\
-\theta&0\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, the logarithm of
r_{pq}(\theta_{pq})
is

\log r_{pq}(\theta_{pq})=\theta_{pq}(E_{pq}-E_{qp}),

where
E_{ab}
denote a zero matrix with a single one at the
(a,b)
-th
position. Substituting the logarithm of
r_{pq}(\theta_{pq})
into

\displaystyle U(r_{pq}(\theta_{pq});\psi)

(30)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\exp({\theta_{pq} \sum_{i,j=1}^n {(E_{pq} - E_{qp})}_{ij}
\ba^\dag(\psi_i) \ba(\psi_j)})

(31)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\exp({\theta_{pq}(\ba^\dag(\psi_p) \ba(\psi_q)
- \ba^\dag(\psi_q) \ba(\psi_p))}),

(32)

which proves (
13
).

By matrix function property and

The Jordan-Wigner encoding of the number operator could be derived from

0&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{p+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{n}\\
\end{split}

Hence, we have,

\begin{split}&J_{\psi}\boldsymbol{P}_{p}(\phi;\psi)J_{\psi}^{-1}\\

\otimes\cdots\otimes I_n)\\

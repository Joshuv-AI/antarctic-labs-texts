Reflections and Drinfeld twists for set-theoretic Yang–Baxter maps
Davide Ferri

Abstract
. The Yang–Baxter equation ( ybe ) and the reflection equation ( re ) both come from mathematical physics, and they can be defined in any monoidal category. For cartesian monoidal categories, we prove that every solution to the re provides a Drinfeld twist for a solution of the ybe . As we observe, Drinfeld twists of solutions are relevant for the following reason: two solutions to the ybe (in any strict monoidal category) are related by a Drinfeld twist, if and only if they induce equivalent representations of the braid group. In the category of sets, it is known that every solution is associated with a structure group, which is a braided group in the sense of Lu, Yan, and Zhu (2000). Using De Commer’s notion of a braided action, we then define group reflections for a braided group. We prove that group reflections provide group Drinfeld twists in the sense of Ghobadi. Finally, we characterise when a reflection on a solution ( X , r ) (X,r) can be extended to a group reflection on its structure group G ⁡ ( X , r ) G(X,r) .

The set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation (

if

. The

The physical insight behind the

In this paper, we prove that the

A way to produce set-theoretic solutions is through

\displaystyle F_{12}\Psi=F_{23}\Phi;

We recall the classical definition of a braided group, as it appeared in Lu, Yan, and Zhu
[

Definition 4.1

Let

(

\displaystyle r(a,1)=(1,a),

(

(

It is well known that braidings on groups are also bijective non-degenerate solutions to the

Remark 4.2

4.4.
On the groupoid of group Drinfeld twists

Every group Drinfeld twist can be obtained as the composition of two twists of two special kinds.

Proposition 4.19

(
[
13
, dual of Corollary 2.8]
)
.

Any arrow
(G,\cdot,r)\to(G,\cdot^{\prime},r^{\prime})
in the groupoid
\mathsf{BGDrin}_{G}
can be decomposed as

, where

(I)

the arrow

for two different group structures;

(II)

the arrow

is a twist of the form

As a consequence of Proposition
4.12
, if two trivial skew braces

and

, then
(G,\cdot)
and

Using this fact, the arrows of type
(I)
in Proposition
4.19
have been completely classified.

Proposition 4.20

(
[
13
, §2]
)
.

There is a bijective correspondence between group Drinfeld twists

In the above Proposition, we are calling

alone ‘a Drinfeld twist’ without specifying the maps
\Phi
and
\Psi
. This imprecision is actually safe, since

turns out to be a group Drinfeld twist for unique maps

and

Remark 4.21
.

Let

be a trivial skew brace, with its braiding. As an immediate consequence of Proposition

Remark 4.22
.

The derived solution

The composition of the arrows of type

Lemma 4.23

\displaystyle F_{2}\circ F_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(g_{f_{xy}(x)\bar{\cdot}f_{xy}(y)}(f_{xy}(x)),\;g_{f_{xy}(x)\bar{\cdot}f_{xy}(y)}(f_{xy}(y))\right)

Conclusion

By definition of

We have proven (

Remark 5.5
.

If a group is abelian, then its group reflections are anti-homomorphisms (Remark
4.21
). It is known that every map
f\colon X\to X
can be extended to an anti-homomorphism on
\Free(X)
, and this extension is a subcase of our definition of
\tilde{k}
in Theorem
5.4
, where both actions
\tilde{\rightharpoonup},\,\tilde{\leftharpoonup}
are trivial.

The problem of whether a Drinfeld twist for
(X,r)
can be extended to a group Drinfeld twist on
G(X,r)
has already been posed by Ghobadi in
[
13
, Example 2.4]
. Theorem
5.4
says that we can extend
k
to
\bar{k}
if
k
satisfies (
bre3
). On the other hand, if
\bar{k}
is an extension of
k
to
G(X,r)
, and it satisfies (
bre3
), then in degree one
k
must satisfy (
bre3
): thus (
bre3
) is a necessary and sufficient condition for
k
to be extended to a group reflection on
G(X,r)
. This solves Ghobadi’s problem
[
13
, Example 2.4]
in the case of Drinfeld twists coming from reflections.

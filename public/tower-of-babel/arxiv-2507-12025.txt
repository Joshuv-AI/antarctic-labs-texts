Abstract
. We classify the possible Scott complexities for models of Peano arithmetic. We construct models of particular complexities by first giving a complete Scott analysis of colored linear orderings and constructing models of Peano arithmetic from these colored orderings. We also provide tight connections of certain Scott complexities with notions from the classical theory of models of Peano arithmetic, such as prime, finitely generated, and recursively saturated. This effort provides a powerful set of tools to understand the models of Peano arithmetic.

1.
Introduction

It is straightforward to see that every finite structure
\mathcal{A}
in finite vocabulary has a sentence
\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}
of finitary first-order logic so that the models of this sentence are precisely the structures isomorphic to it. Unfortunately, if we turn our attention to infinite structures, this fails. By a simple application of compactness, one can show that for most countable structures
\mathcal{A}
, even their first-order theory
\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{A})
has countable models that are not isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
; such theories fail to be what model theorists call
countably categorical
. In some sense, a theory that is not countably categorical fails to characterize the structural properties of its models. Even among simple structures that are encountered in everyday mathematics, we can find examples of structures whose theories are not countably categorical. The example most central to modern-day mathematics is probably the standard model of arithmetic
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot)
.

One could, of course instead take a more proof-theoretic approach by starting with a theory and asking whether this theory has a single model. However, in this case, the situation becomes even worse. For example, by Gödel’s famous incompleteness theorem,
Peano arithmetic
(
\mathrm{PA}
for short)—the standard axiomatization of number theory—is incomplete and possesses no recursive completions. One can then show that none of its completions is countably categorical and, indeed, every such completion has
2^{\aleph_{0}}
models.

But also when considered from other angles, Peano arithmetic is a consummately wild theory. For example, model theory is a subfield of mathematical logic that analyzes mathematical structures based on their first-order theories. Modern model theory, or neo-stability theory, concerns itself a great deal with characterizing and capturing various notions of mathematical tameness. However, due to Gödel’s initial observations about the coding power of Peano arithmetic, this theory and its models stand outside any notion of model-theoretic tameness. See
[
1
]
for a graphical representation of tameness properties used in modern model theory; Peano arithmetic is a member of the delta quadrant, so it escapes all studied notions of tractability.

Despite the inherent difficulty in studying Peano arithmetic and the failure of most model-theoretic techniques to obtain insight into structural properties of its models, researchers have still made impressive efforts and proved exceptional results. In fact, the study of models of Peano arithmetic has been one of the most vital efforts in logic for the nearly one hundred years since Peano introduced the axiomatization. In the study of models of arithmetics, researchers usually combine model-theoretic techniques with the special properties of models of Peano arithmetic, such as their immense coding power, to gain new structural insights, see
[
11
]
for an overview of results in this area.

In this paper, we take a different approach to study the structural properties of models of Peano arithmetic. Instead of finitary first-order logic, we turn to the infinitary logic
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
, allowing countable conjunctions and disjunctions in our language. Scott
[
15
]
showed that, in contrast to the situation with first-order logic, we can classify countable structures up to isomorphism in the logic
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
. In this logic, every countable structure
\mathcal{A}
, including
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot)
and other models of Peano arithmetic, has a
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-sentence
\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}
the countable models of which are precisely the structures isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
. We call such a sentence a
Scott sentence
.
Scott’s work lies in the tradition of computable structure theory, not first-order model theory, and his result was the first in the field we now call
Scott analysis
.
In this article, we use recent developments in Scott analysis to reframe the theory of models of Peano arithmetic that had previously only been considered in a more ad hoc manner and give a complete analysis of the structural complexities of its models.

In Scott analysis, the structural complexity of a model is measured by the complexity of its simplest Scott sentence. We can naturally extend the classical quantifier-alternation rank to
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
and through this obtain various complexity measures on sentences, and therefore their models. The best-known of these measures are the various notions of
Scott rank
emerging from Scott’s proof. To define this notion let us first recall the definitions of quantifier-rank in
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
.

One approach to facilitate a Scott analysis of a theory like Peano arithmetic is to use a notion of reduction that preserves Scott rank, reduce a well-understood class of structures to your theory, and then use the structures in the image as examples for the Scott sentence complexities. This is, in essence, what Montalbán and Rossegger
[
14

did to characterize the Scott ranks of
\mathrm{PA}

\omega

\mathrm{PA}

The issue with their approach is that if we sharpen our lens to distinguish Scott sentence complexities, then not every Scott complexity occurs in the class of linear orderings.

To overcome this, we turn to structures slightly more complicated than linear orderings,

Section

\mathrm{PA}

\mathrm{PA}
that have seen interest in the literature such as finitely generated models and short recursively saturated models.

Colored Linear Orderings

In what follows, we will interpret colored linear orderings in models of Peano arithmetic.
This will allow us to push forward Scott analysis results about colored linear orderings to models of Peano arithmetic.
For this to be useful, however, we must first establish facts about colored linear orderings.

For
k\leq\aleph_{0}

k

For

By Item 2 of

This is clearly possible as

Therefore,
\omega_{A}\cdot 2\leq_{3}\omega_{A}
and
\omega_{A}\cdot 2
has no

Scott sentence.
However, there is a
\Pi^{\mathrm{in}}_{3}
Scott sentence over the first element of the second

This is true because each
\omega_{A}

Finally, consider the case of

The same argument as in Theorem 3.3 of

∎

Note that the above analysis fails to fully apply to linear orderings with infinitely many colors.
For example, one can color any infinite structure with infinitely many colors so that every color occurs exactly once. Such a structure has Scott complexity

because the automorphism orbit of every tuple is simply given by the colors of the tuple’s elements.

3.
From colored linear orderings to models of
\mathrm{PA}

In
[

bi-interpretability to the class of models of
T

-canonical extensions, we refer to

Our reduction from colored linear orderings to models of

Given
\mathcal{L}

The construction of

is discussed in detail in
[

]

14

-extension

-extensions for all

induces a reduction via

bi-interpretability of the class of linear orderings to the class of colored extensions of

and why this reduction preserves Scott complexity. Our reasoning is quite different from the reasoning in
[
14

that shows that the corresponding reduction from linear orderings to models of

preserves Scott rank. We begin by recalling relevant definitions.

4.
Missing complexities for models of PA

The methods described above fail to give us both qualitative and quantitative information about models of Peano arithmetic with Scott complexities
\Pi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}
,
\Pi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega+1}
,
d\text{-}\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega+1}
,
\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega+1}
and
\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega+2}
. To extract this part of the analysis, we turn to methods specific to arithmetic.
The central observation to analyze models of these complexities is that when the
\Sigma_{n}
-types of two tuples of elements of a model of
\mathrm{PA}
coincide, their
\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{n}
-types also coincide (see
Corollary

29
). This is a strengthening of a result by Montalbán and Rossegger
[
14
, Lemma 5]
who noticed that this is the case when
n=\omega
. Combining this and generally well-understood structural properties of models of Peano arithmetic will allow us to calculate Scott complexities of certain well-known classes of models.

In order to compactify the presentation of our results, we shall be working with
pointed
models of
\mathrm{PA}
, to wit: models with a distinguished element. Please note that, by pairing, this is equivalent to working with finitely many fixed parameters.

27
.

For
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
pointed models of
\mathrm{PA}
, the following conditions are equivalent

•

\mathcal{M}\equiv_{1}\mathcal{N}
;

•

\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

It follows from the MRDP theorem (see
[
10
, Result 7.8]
) that any
\Sigma_{1}
sentence about pointed models of
\mathrm{PA}
is equivalent to a purely existential sentence. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{M}\equiv_{1}\mathcal{N}
if and only if
\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}(\mathcal{N})
truth of a
\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}
sentence is determined by the truth of one of its (purely existential) disjuncts,
\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{1}}(\mathcal{N})
if and only if
\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{1}}(\mathcal{N})
.
∎

The next theorem and particularly the corollary that follows are generalizations of a result known to hold when the models are recursively saturated
[

, Proposition 1.8.1]

For

\mathcal{M}\equiv_{n}\mathcal{N}

\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{N})

Recall that for a set

\underline{n}\in_{Ack}c\iff n\in A.

For a subset of

and any model

it follows that
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{SSy}(M)
if and only if

A\in\mathcal{X}\iff M\models\exists x\left[\left(\bigwedge_{n\in A}\underline{n}\in_{Ack}x\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus A}\underline{n}\not\in_{Ack}x\right)\right]

where every such sentence is equivalent to a
\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{2}

Now assume

and

\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_{n}

\mathcal{N}

-theory, with the latter being a subset of its

\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{n}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Th}_{\Sigma_{n}}(\mathcal{N})

On the other hand, suppose that
\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{SSy}(\mathcal{N})

\Sigma_{n}\text{-}\mathrm{tp}_{\mathcal{M}}(\overline{a})=\Sigma_{n}\text{-}\mathrm{tp}_{\mathcal{N}}(\overline{b})\Longrightarrow(\mathcal{M},\bar{a})\equiv_{n}(\mathcal{N},\bar{b})

For

27

then

and assume that

[
Mon24
, Section II.6]
. Say that the
\forall
-player plays
\bar{d}\in N^{<\omega}
.
Then the

of

over

is coded in

as it is clearly realized (see

Now to find such

Clearly it is a type over

All such sentences are equivalently

As

\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{PA}(\mathcal{L})

33
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{PA}(\mathcal{L})
and let
b\in\mathcal{M}
be a non-definable element in
\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}
is strictly greater than
\omega

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M},b

b
in
\mathcal{M}
is not

30
it is enough to find

and consequently there is an element

is a bounded type if and only if for some

If
\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{PA}(\mathcal{L})
is nonstandard and short recursively saturated, then
\mathcal{M}
is not finitely generated.

Proof.

Let

be nonstandard and short recursively saturated. Aiming at a contradiction, assume that
\mathcal{M}
is finitely generated from an element

We observe that

is finitely satisfied in
\mathcal{M}
because finite sets of natural numbers can be coded by a natural number and any element realizing

encodes

,

is realized, say by an element

By unfolding the definitions, it is very easy to observe that for any

in

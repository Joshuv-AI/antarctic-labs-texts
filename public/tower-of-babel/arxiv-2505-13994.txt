Divide by Question, Conquer by Agent: SPLIT-RAG with Question-Driven Graph Partitioning
Ruiyi Yang, Hao Xue, Imran Razzak, Shirui Pan, Hakim Hacid, Flora D. Salim

Abstract
Retrieval-augmented generation over knowledge graphs (KGs) can improve the factual grounding of large language models, but efficient retrieval remains challenging when the underlying graph is large and multi-hop evidence is distributed across different regions. We present SPLIT-RAG , an efficiency-oriented multi-agent KG-RAG framework that organises a KG into reusable, question-aligned partitions using reasoning patterns observed in training questions. At inference time, SPLIT-RAG forms a query-specific retrieval plan, activates only a small set of relevant partition-specialised agents, and aggregates their retrieved evidence before final answer generation. To improve reliability when evidence from different agents is combined, the framework applies compatibility-based filtering over retrieved triples prior to synthesis. Experiments on WebQSP, CWQ, and MetaQA show that SPLIT-RAG achieves strong performance against recent KG+LLM baselines while reducing end-to-end latency under matched budgets. Component ablations and model-tier analyses further show that selective multi-agent retrieval is central to the performance gain, and that lightweight retrieval agents paired with a stronger head agent provide an effective accuracy–latency trade-off. Code is available at https://github.com/cruiseresearchgroup/SPLIT-RAG .

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) can produce unsupported or outdated answers when
the required facts are absent from, or no longer reflected in, their
parametric knowledge
Zhang et al. (2025)
;
Kandpal et al. (2023)
.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by grounding
generation in external evidence without requiring model retraining

Zhu et al. (2021)
;
Gao et al. (2023b)
;
Zhao et al. (2024)
.
Knowledge graphs (KGs) are particularly attractive retrieval sources because
their entity–relation structure supports explicit multi-hop reasoning and
structured evidence tracing.

However, scaling KG-based RAG to large graphs remains challenging.
A complex question may require evidence spanning multiple relations, while a
simple question should not require searching a large portion of the graph.
Recent KG+LLM systems improve path or subgraph retrieval, but they still face
an important accuracy–efficiency trade-off: expanding retrieval improves the
chance of covering useful evidence, yet increases latency and introduces more
irrelevant or weakly supported facts into the generation context.
Moreover, when evidence is retrieved from multiple graph regions or agents,
the final generator must combine partially overlapping or potentially
incompatible triples in a controlled manner.

This work studies whether previously observed question-reasoning patterns can
be reused to organise and route KG retrieval.
The central intuition is that training questions reveal recurring retrieval
structures: questions with related entity types and relation paths often depend
on similar graph regions.
Instead of retrieving over the complete KG for every query, a system can
construct reusable question-aligned partitions offline and activate only the
partitions most relevant to a new question at inference time.

Figure 1:

Illustration of SPLIT-RAG.
Training-question reasoning patterns are used to construct question-aligned
graph partitions and assign specialised retrieval agents.
For a new query, the planner selects relevant partition–agent groups,
retrieves supporting triples in parallel, and applies compatibility-based
evidence filtering before head-agent answer generation.

Based on this intuition, we propose
SPLIT-RAG
, an
efficiency-oriented multi-agent framework for knowledge-graph
retrieval-augmented generation.
SPLIT-RAG first constructs reusable KG partitions from reasoning-path
fragments associated with training-question patterns.
It then assigns partition groups to specialised retrieval agents.
At inference time, a query-specific planner uses entity-type similarity and,
when available, a predicted relation-path sketch to activate a small set of
relevant agents.
The selected agents retrieve evidence only from their assigned partitions in
parallel, after which a head agent generates the answer from a filtered set of
compatible triples and textualised evidence.

This design differs from prior KG+LLM retrieval systems in three respects.
First, SPLIT-RAG uses training-question patterns to construct reusable
retrieval regions before inference, rather than deriving each retrieval space
from the current query alone.
Second, it couples these partitions with specialised agents and performs
query-conditioned partition–agent routing, allowing retrieval effort to be
allocated selectively.
Third, it applies compatibility-based evidence filtering before final
generation, providing a lightweight mechanism for refining evidence collected
from multiple retrieval agents.

Our contributions are as follows:

•

Question-aligned KG partitioning.

We introduce an offline partitioning objective that organises a KG into
compact, reusable regions aligned with recurring training-question
reasoning patterns, while controlling partition size and fragmentation.

•

Partition-specialised multi-agent retrieval.

We develop a routing framework that selects relevant partition–agent
groups for each incoming question and executes subgraph-specific retrieval
in parallel, reducing unnecessary graph exploration.

•

Empirical analysis of effectiveness and efficiency.

We evaluate SPLIT-RAG on WebQSP, CWQ, and MetaQA against recent KG+LLM
baselines, and analyse latency, component ablations, model-tier
assignments, and incomplete-KG settings.
The results show that selective multi-agent retrieval yields a strong
accuracy–latency trade-off, with lightweight retrieval agents remaining
effective when paired with a capable head agent.

3
Divide by Question, Conquer by Agent: SPLIT-RAG Framework

Figure 2:
Overview of SPLIT-RAG. Training-question reasoning patterns guide graph partitioning and agent assignment. At inference time, a query-specific retrieval plan activates only relevant agents before compatibility-based evidence aggregation and answer generation.

SPLIT-RAG is an efficiency-oriented KG-RAG framework that routes each input question to a small set of specialised retrieval agents over question-aligned graph partitions.
Let
D=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a knowledge graph and

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{train}}
a set of training questions with recorded reasoning paths.
An offline indexer
\tau
constructs a partitioned knowledge base

\hat{\mathcal{K}}=\{\hat{D}_{1},\ldots,\hat{D}_{M}\}
and assigns partitions to agents.
At inference time, a planner
\psi
selects the relevant agents and subgraphs for a new question
q
, after which a head generator produces the final answer:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}=\tau(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{train}},D),\qquad F(q,D)=\hat{\mathcal{G}}\!\left(q,\psi(q,\hat{\mathcal{K}})\right)

(1)

The framework contains four stages: question-guided graph partitioning, agent allocation, query-time routing and parallel retrieval, and compatibility-based evidence aggregation.

3.1
Question-Guided Graph Partitioning

Question and path representations.

Each training question
q_{i}
is represented by:
(i) an entity-type view
\mathbf{Q}_{e,i}
, in which entity mentions are replaced by their KG types; and
(ii) a reasoning path

\mathbf{p}_{i}=(e_{1},r_{1},e_{2},\ldots,r_{h_{i}},e_{h_{i}+1})

that connects entities required for answering
q_{i}
.
The entity-type views are clustered into question-pattern groups

c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Each reasoning path is decomposed into consecutive at-most-two-hop fragments:

\phi(\mathbf{p}_{i})=\left\{(e_{j},r_{j},e_{j+1},\ldots,e_{j+\ell})\,:\,1\leq\ell\leq 2\right\}.

(2)

Partition objective.

The goal is to construct compact graph regions that preserve frequently reused reasoning patterns.
Let
Z
denote the partition assignment of a training-path fragment and let
C
denote the question-pattern cluster from which that fragment originates.
A useful partition should make the activated graph region informative of the associated question pattern while avoiding excessively large or fragmented partitions.
We therefore optimise

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})=I(C;Z)-\lambda\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{V}_{s}|}{|\mathcal{V}|}\right)^{2}-\mu|\mathcal{S}|,

(3)

subject to

|\mathcal{V}_{s}|\leq\eta_{\max},\qquad\forall s\in\mathcal{S},\qquad\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{E}_{s}=\mathcal{E}.

(4)

The mutual-information term rewards partitions associated with coherent question patterns; the quadratic size penalty discourages a small number of oversized partitions; and the final term avoids unnecessary fragmentation.

In practice, path fragments initialise seed regions, which are greedily merged according to the marginal gain in Eq. (
3
).
Triples not observed in training reasoning paths are assigned to the nearest compatible region using their entity and relation representations, ensuring that the resulting partition covers the full KG.
Boundary entities may appear in multiple partitions when required to retain cross-partition reasoning paths.
Algorithm
1
in Appendix
D
provides the
partition-construction procedure.

3.2
Question-Centric Agent Allocation

Let
\hat{\mathcal{K}}=\{\hat{D}_{1},\ldots,\hat{D}_{M}\}
be the graph partitions.
For each training question
q_{i}
, define its partition coverage set as

B_{ij}=\mathbb{I}\!\left[\exists\,p\in\textsc{Paths}(q_{i}):p\cap\hat{D}_{j}\neq\emptyset\right].

(5)

\mathcal{C}_{i}=\left\{\hat{D}_{j}\mid B_{ij}=1\right\}.

(6)

Each agent
a_{\ell}
is assigned a group of partitions

\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\subseteq\hat{\mathcal{K}}
.
The allocation objective minimises the number of agents required by training questions:

\min_{\{\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}}\;\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coord}}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{train}}|}\left|\left\{\ell:\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{C}_{i}\neq\emptyset\right\}\right|,

(7)

subject to

\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathcal{G}_{\ell}=\hat{\mathcal{K}},\qquad|\mathcal{G}_{\ell}|\leq N_{\max},\qquad\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{G}_{\ell})\geq\theta_{\mathrm{coh}}

(8)

Here,
\operatorname{Coh}(\cdot)
measures semantic similarity among partitions assigned to the same agent.
The objective places partitions frequently used together under the same agent, reducing coordination overhead at inference time.
We use a greedy coverage-co-occurrence allocation procedure, detailed in
Appendix
D
, Algorithm
2
.

3.3
Query-Time Routing and Parallel Retrieval

Given a new question
q
, the planner first forms its entity-type representation
\mathbf{Q}_{e,q}
.
Optionally, it also predicts a relation-path sketch
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q}
directly from the question.
Importantly,
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q}
is produced without access to gold test paths or answers; its generation cost is included in end-to-end latency.

For each training question
q_{i}
, routing similarity is computed as

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{sem}}(q,q_{i})

\displaystyle=\cos\!\left(\operatorname{Emb}(\mathbf{Q}_{e,q}),\operatorname{Emb}(\mathbf{Q}_{e,i})\right),

(9)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{path}}(q,q_{i})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{PathOverlap}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q},\mathbf{p}_{i}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sim}(q,q_{i})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{s}s_{\mathrm{sem}}(q,q_{i})+\gamma_{p}s_{\mathrm{path}}(q,q_{i}).

where
\gamma_{p}=0
when no path sketch is used.
Let

\mathcal{N}_{\theta}(q)=\{q_{i}:\operatorname{Sim}(q,q_{i})\geq\theta_{\mathrm{sim}}\}

be the retrieved training neighbours.
Their associated agents form the similarity-based candidate set

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sim}}(q)=\bigcup_{q_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\theta}(q)}\mathcal{A}(q_{i}).

(10)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{path}}(q)=\left\{a_{\ell}:\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\,\tilde{p}\in\phi(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q}),\ \exists\,\hat{D}_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell},\\[-2.84526pt]
\operatorname{Compatible}(\tilde{p},\hat{D}_{j})=1\end{array}\right\}.

(11)

Here,
\operatorname{Compatible}(\tilde{p},\hat{D}_{j})
indicates that
partition
\hat{D}_{j}
contains entity–relation patterns compatible with
the predicted path fragment
\tilde{p}
.
When a predicted path sketch is available, partitions containing compatible relation fragments additionally produce

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{path}}(q)
.
Let
d(q)=1
indicate that a retrieved neighbour exceeds the direct-routing
threshold
\theta_{\mathrm{direct}}
.The activated agents are

\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sim}}(q),&d(q)=1,\\
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sim}}(q)\cup\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{path}}(q),&d(q)=0.\end{cases}

(12)

Each selected agent receives a sub-task derived from the transferred question pattern or predicted relation fragments, and searches only within its assigned partitions.
For an agent sub-task
t
and candidate KG path
p
, we use

\operatorname{Match}(p,t)=\frac{|\operatorname{Ent}(p)\cap\operatorname{Ent}(t)|}{\max(1,|\operatorname{Ent}(t)|)}.

(13)

Paths whose matching score exceeds
\theta_{\mathrm{match}}
contribute structured triples

\mathcal{T}_{i}
and textualised evidence
\mathcal{E}_{i}
.
All selected agents operate in parallel.
The implementation of relation-path sketch prediction,

\operatorname{PathOverlap}(\cdot,\cdot)
, and

\operatorname{Compatible}(\cdot,\cdot)
is specified in
Appendix
F
.
Algorithms
3
and
4
in
Appendix
D
provide the complete routing and parallel retrieval
procedures.

3.4
Compatibility-Based Evidence Aggregation

Let

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{all}}=\bigcup_{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}\mathcal{T}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}=\bigcup_{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}\mathcal{E}_{i}

denote the collected triples and textual evidence.
Each retrieved triple
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{all}}
is assigned a support score:

w(\tau)=\alpha\max_{i:\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\operatorname{Conf}(a_{i})+\beta\operatorname{Freq}(\tau)+\gamma\operatorname{Entail}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}},\tau)

(14)

where
\operatorname{Conf}(a_{i})
is estimated from training-time retrieval success,

\operatorname{Freq}(\tau)
measures multi-agent support, and

\operatorname{Entail}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}},\tau)
is produced by a lightweight evidence verifier.

To avoid incorrectly rejecting valid multi-valued KG relations, two triples are treated as conflicting only when they are explicitly contradictory or assign different objects to a relation identified as functional:

\operatorname{Conflict}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=1\quad\text{iff}\quad\tau_{1}\models\neg\tau_{2}\;\vee\;\tau_{2}\models\neg\tau_{1}{}\vee\;\operatorname{FuncCol}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

(15)

We construct a compatibility graph whose vertices are retrieved triples and whose edges connect non-conflicting pairs.
Rather than claiming exact maximum-weight clique inference, we apply a greedy maximum-weight compatible-set heuristic, iteratively retaining the highest-scoring triple compatible with the current evidence set.
The resulting set
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{clean}}
is supplied to the head agent:

\operatorname{Answer}(q)=\operatorname{LLM}_{\mathrm{head}}\left(q,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{clean}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}\right).

(16)

Implementation details for the support-score components,
functional-relation identification, and greedy compatible-set construction
are provided in Appendix
H
.

3.5
Conditional Evidence Preservation and Retrieval Cost

The benefit of SPLIT-RAG depends on whether routing activates partitions containing the evidence required for a query.
We therefore state two conditional properties rather than assuming perfect coverage for arbitrary unseen questions.

Proposition 1: Evidence preservation under routing coverage.

For a question
q
, let
\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be a sufficient set of KG triples for deriving a correct answer, and let

\mathcal{U}(q)=\bigcup_{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}\mathcal{G}_{a_{i}}

be the union of activated partitions.
If

\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)\subseteq\mathcal{U}(q)

and the within-partition retriever returns all required triples present in
\mathcal{U}(q)
, then routing does not remove any supporting triple required for answering
q
relative to retrieval over the full graph.

Proposition 2: Retrieval-cost reduction under selective activation.

Let
n=|\mathcal{E}|
denote the total number of KG triples and let

n_{q}=|\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U}(q))|
denote the number of triples contained in the partitions activated for question
q
.
For a retrieval operator with monotone search cost
C(\cdot)
, SPLIT-RAG has online cost

T_{\mathrm{split}}(q)=T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+C(n_{q})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q),\quad T_{\mathrm{full}}(q)=C(n).

(17)

Therefore, SPLIT-RAG reduces online retrieval cost whenever

T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+C(n_{q})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q)<C(n).

Under a linear candidate-scanning model, this becomes

T_{\mathrm{split}}(q)=T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+O(n_{q}),\qquad T_{\mathrm{full}}(q)=O(n),

so the efficiency gain is governed by the activated-graph ratio
n_{q}/n
and the planner overhead.
We empirically measure both evidence coverage and end-to-end latency in Section
4
.
Formal proofs are provided in Appendix
E
.

Experimental Setup

Datasets.

We evaluate SPLIT-RAG on four KGQA benchmarks:
WebQuestionsSP (WebQSP)
Yih et al. (2016)
,
Complex WebQuestions (CWQ)
Talmor and Berant (2018)
,

WebQSP and CWQ are constructed over Freebase and test reasoning over a
large-scale KG, while MetaQA evaluates controlled multi-hop

We omit MetaQA-1Hop as it does not require multi-step retrieval.
Dataset statistics are reported in Table

We compare against three groups of methods:
(i) embedding-based KGQA models, including KV-Mem

,
GraftNet

, PullNet
Sun et al. (2019)
,
EmbedKGQA
Saxena et al. (2020)
, and
TransferNet

(ii) LLM-only prompting without KG retrieval; and

,

, ToG

Metrics and implementation.

We report Hit, Hits@1 (H@1), and F1.
Hit measures whether any gold answer entity appears in the model output;
H@1 measures whether the top predicted entity is correct;

Table

evaluates SPLIT-RAG when edges are removed

5
Conclusion and Future Work

We presented
SPLIT-RAG
, an efficiency-oriented KG-RAG framework that
constructs reusable question-aligned graph partitions, assigns specialised
retrieval agents to them, and routes each query to a small set of relevant
partition–agent groups.
A compatibility-based filtering step refines multi-agent evidence before
head-agent generation.
Experiments on WebQSP, CWQ, and MetaQA demonstrate that SPLIT-RAG is
competitive with recent KG+LLM retrieval systems while achieving lower
end-to-end latency under matched budgets.
Controlled ablations show that retaining
partition-scoped retrieval states supplies the quality benefit, whereas parallel worker execution supplies
the principal latency benefit. The model-tier analysis shows that lightweight retrieval workers can be effective when paired with a stronger head model. Additional
Additional incomplete-KG experiments characterise how retrieval quality changes
when supporting graph evidence becomes unavailable.
Future work will extend SPLIT-RAG to evolving and hybrid knowledge sources and
develop stronger verification mechanisms for ambiguous retrieved evidence.

Limitations

SPLIT-RAG is designed for question answering over structured, triple-based
knowledge graphs, where training questions provide useful signals for organising
retrieval regions. Its current evaluation focuses on KGQA benchmarks with
relatively stable schemas; extending the framework to hybrid corpora involving
text, tables, or dynamically updated sources remains an important direction for
future work.

Ethics Considerations

The experiments do not involve human
participants, private user data, or the collection of personally identifiable
information. SPLIT-RAG is intended as a research framework for improving the
accuracy–efficiency trade-off of knowledge-grounded question answering.
Although SPLIT-RAG
includes compatibility-based evidence filtering, this mechanism should not be
interpreted as a guarantee of factual correctness.

Appendix E
Analysis of Partition Alignment and Retrieval Efficiency

This section provides conditional properties of SPLIT-RAG.
The analysis does not assume that routing perfectly covers every unseen
question. Instead, it characterises (i) what is encouraged by the partition
objective, (ii) when routing preserves sufficient evidence, and
(iii) when selective activation reduces retrieval cost.

Proposition 1

(Question-Pattern Alignment Objective)
.

Let
C
denote the question-pattern cluster associated with a sampled
training reasoning fragment, and let
Z
denote the partition assignment of
that fragment under a candidate partition
\mathcal{S}
.
For the partition objective in Eq. (
3
),

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})=I(C;Z)-\lambda\Omega_{\mathrm{size}}(\mathcal{S})-\mu|\mathcal{S}|,

(18)

with
\lambda,\mu\geq 0
, the induced mutual information satisfies

I(C;Z)=\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})+\lambda\Omega_{\mathrm{size}}(\mathcal{S})+\mu|\mathcal{S}|\geq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}).

(19)

Therefore, subject to the size and fragmentation penalties, maximising

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})
favours partitions whose assignments are
informative of question-pattern clusters.

Proof.

By definition of the objective in Eq. (
3
),

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})=I(C;Z)-\lambda\Omega_{\mathrm{size}}(\mathcal{S})-\mu|\mathcal{S}|.

(20)

Rearranging gives

I(C;Z)=\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S})+\lambda\Omega_{\mathrm{size}}(\mathcal{S})+\mu|\mathcal{S}|.

(21)

Since

\lambda,\mu\geq 0
,

\Omega_{\mathrm{size}}(\mathcal{S})\geq 0
, and

|\mathcal{S}|\geq 0
, it follows that

I(C;Z)\geq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}).

(22)

Thus, after accounting for compactness and fragmentation penalties, improving
the objective encourages stronger dependence between question-pattern clusters
and partition assignments.
∎

Proposition 2

(Evidence Preservation under Routing Coverage)
.

For a question
q
, let

\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)\subseteq\mathcal{E}
be a sufficient set of KG triples
for deriving a correct answer.
Let

\mathcal{U}(q)=\bigcup_{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}\mathcal{G}_{a_{i}}

(23)

denote the union of partitions activated by the router.
If

\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)\subseteq\mathcal{U}(q),

(24)

and the local retrievers recover all triples in

\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)
contained in
\mathcal{U}(q)
, then SPLIT-RAG
preserves the supporting evidence required for answering
q
relative to
retrieval over the full graph.

Proof.

By the routing coverage condition in
Eq. (
24
), every triple required by the
sufficient evidence set
\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)
lies inside the union of
activated partitions
\mathcal{U}(q)
.
By assumption, the local retrievers recover every required triple from

\mathcal{U}(q)
.
Therefore,

\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{all}},

(25)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{all}}
is the retrieved evidence supplied to the
aggregation module.
Hence, routing does not remove any triple necessary for deriving the answer
from
\mathcal{T}^{*}(q)
.
∎

Proposition 3

(Retrieval-Cost Reduction under Selective Activation)
.

Let
n=|\mathcal{E}|
be the number of triples in the full KG, and let

n_{q}=|\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U}(q))|
be the number of triples contained in
the partitions activated for question
q
.
Let
C(x)
be a monotone retrieval-cost function for searching over
x

triples.
If
T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)
and
T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q)
denote routing and
aggregation overhead, respectively, then

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{split}}(q)

\displaystyle=T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+C(n_{q})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q),

(26)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{full}}(q)

\displaystyle=C(n).

Consequently, SPLIT-RAG reduces online retrieval time whenever

T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+C(n_{q})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q)<C(n).

(27)

Proof.

Full-graph retrieval searches over all
n
triples and therefore incurs cost

C(n)
.
SPLIT-RAG first constructs a query-specific routing plan, retrieves only from
the activated graph union containing
n_{q}
triples, and aggregates the
resulting evidence.
Thus, its total online cost is

T_{\mathrm{split}}(q)=T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+C(n_{q})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q).

(28)

Comparing this expression with

T_{\mathrm{full}}(q)=C(n)

immediately yields Eq. (
27
).
Therefore, the efficiency gain is determined by the reduction in activated
graph size relative to routing and aggregation overhead.
∎

Parallel-agent execution.

When activated agents retrieve evidence in parallel, let

n_{q,\ell}
be the number of triples searched by agent
a_{\ell}
.
Ignoring communication overhead already included in

T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q)
, the retrieval component of wall-clock latency becomes

C_{\mathrm{parallel}}(q)=\max_{a_{\ell}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}C(n_{q,\ell}),

(29)

rather than the sum of per-agent retrieval costs.
Accordingly, the parallel execution condition is

T_{\mathrm{plan}}(q)+\max_{a_{\ell}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}C(n_{q,\ell})+T_{\mathrm{agg}}(q)<C(n).

(30)

This condition motivates the latency evaluation reported in the main
experiments.

. While for WebQSP and CWQ datasets the results are recorded in Table

The wall-clock time is measured end-to-end per QA, averaging over the MetaQA-3Hop test split.

Appendix F
Path-Sketch Prediction and Routing Details

Relation-path sketch prediction.

For each test question
q
, SPLIT-RAG predicts a relation-path sketch

\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q}
before agent activation.
The predictor receives only the natural-language question, linked seed
entities, and the relation vocabulary available in the indexed KG; it does
not access gold answers or gold reasoning paths.
We implement the predictor using

Llama-3.1-8B
.
The predictor is prompted to produce an ordered sequence of at most

H_{\max}
relation labels, where
H_{\max}
is set to the maximum
reasoning depth of the corresponding benchmark.
Decoding uses temperature
0.1
, top-
p

0.9
, and retains the top-
K

3
path sketches.
The cost of sketch prediction is included in all reported end-to-end latency
measurements.

Relation vocabulary.

The candidate vocabulary is constructed from relation labels appearing in the
indexed knowledge graph after preprocessing.
For efficiency, candidate relations may be restricted to those adjacent to
linked seed entities and those observed in training reasoning paths associated
with the retrieved question-pattern neighbours.
No relation labels derived from gold test paths are used.

Path-overlap score.

Let
\operatorname{Rel}(p)
denote the ordered relation sequence of path

p
, and let
\operatorname{LCS}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the length of their
longest common subsequence.
We define the path-overlap component in
Eq. (
9
) as

\operatorname{PathOverlap}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q},\mathbf{p}_{i})=\frac{\operatorname{LCS}\!\left(\operatorname{Rel}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q}),\operatorname{Rel}(\mathbf{p}_{i})\right)}{\max\!\left\{1,\,|\operatorname{Rel}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{q})|\right\}}.

(31)

This score rewards relation-order agreement while allowing a predicted sketch
to partially match a longer training path.

Partition compatibility.

For a predicted path fragment
\tilde{p}
and a partition
\hat{D}_{j}
,
we define

\operatorname{Compatible}(\tilde{p},\hat{D}_{j})=\mathbb{I}\left[\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}(\hat{D}_{j})}\operatorname{PathOverlap}(\tilde{p},p)\geq\theta_{\mathrm{path}}\right],

(32)

where
\mathcal{P}(\hat{D}_{j})
is the set of indexed relation-path fragments
associated with partition
\hat{D}_{j}
.
We set
\theta_{\mathrm{path}}=\texttt{0.5}
on the validation
split and keep it fixed for test evaluation.

Routing without path sketches.

When no relation-path sketch is available, or when its prediction confidence
is below
\theta_{\mathrm{pred}}
, the path term is disabled by setting

\gamma_{p}=0
, and routing relies only on semantic similarity between the
incoming question and stored training-question patterns.
The contribution of predicted path sketches is evaluated in
Table
12
.

Appendix H
Evidence Aggregation Details

Support-score components.

For each retrieved triple
\tau
, SPLIT-RAG computes the support score in
Eq. (
14
) using three normalised components.
The agent-confidence term is estimated from training-time retrieval success:

\operatorname{Conf}(a_{i})=\frac{n^{\mathrm{succ}}_{i}+1}{n^{\mathrm{ret}}_{i}+2},

(33)

where
n^{\mathrm{ret}}_{i}
is the number of training questions routed to
agent
a_{i}
, and
n^{\mathrm{succ}}_{i}
is the number for which the agent
retrieves at least one gold supporting triple.
The additive smoothing prevents unreliable confidence estimates for rarely
activated agents.

The multi-agent frequency component is defined as

\operatorname{Freq}(\tau)=\frac{\sum_{a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)}\mathbb{I}[\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{i}]}{|\mathcal{A}^{*}(q)|}.

(34)

It rewards triples independently recovered from multiple activated agents.

Evidence entailment verifier.

The entailment term evaluates whether a retrieved triple is supported by the
textualised evidence collected from activated agents:

\operatorname{Entail}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}},\tau)=P_{\mathrm{ver}}\left(\textsc{Supported}\mid\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}},\tau\right).

(35)

We implement
P_{\mathrm{ver}}
using

Llama-3.1-8b

with temperature
0.0
.
The verifier receives the candidate triple and its associated textualised
evidence and returns a scalar support score in
[0,1]
.

Aggregation weights.

The coefficients in Eq. (
14
) are selected on the
validation split and fixed for all test questions:

\alpha=\texttt{0.5},\qquad\beta=\texttt{0.25},\qquad\gamma=\texttt{0.25}.

(36)

We tune these coefficients over

0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0

using validation-set H@1 as the selection criterion.

Functional relations.

To avoid treating valid multi-valued relations as contradictions, object
collisions are considered conflicts only for relations estimated to be
functional.
For each relation
r
, let

\deg^{+}_{r}(e)
denote the number of distinct objects linked from entity

e
through
r
.
We construct the functional-relation set as

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{func}}=\left\{r:\frac{|\{e:\deg^{+}_{r}(e)\leq 1\land\deg^{+}_{r}(e)>0\}|}{|\{e:\deg^{+}_{r}(e)>0\}|}\geq\theta_{\mathrm{func}}\right\}.

(37)

The threshold

\theta_{\mathrm{func}}=\texttt{0.85}

is set using the training KG only and is not derived from test answers.

For two triples

\tau_{1}=(e,r,o_{1})
and

\tau_{2}=(e,r,o_{2})
,
functional collision is defined as

\operatorname{FuncCol}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\mathbb{I}\left[r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{func}}\land o_{1}\neq o_{2}\right].

(38)

Greedy compatible-set construction.

The retrieved triples are first sorted by support score

w(\tau)
in descending order.
Ties are broken deterministically by

\operatorname{Freq}(\tau)
, then by

\operatorname{Conf}(a_{i})
, and finally by lexical triple order.
A triple is retained only if it is compatible with every previously retained
triple.
Algorithm
5
provides the complete procedure.

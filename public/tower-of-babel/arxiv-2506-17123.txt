Towards the inductive McKay–Navarro Condition for groups of Lie type
Lucas Ruhstorfer, A. A. Schaeffer Fry, Britta Späth, and Jay Taylor

Abstract
We gather tools for proving the inductive McKay–Navarro (or Galois–McKay) condition for groups of Lie type and odd primes. We use this to establish a bijection in the case of quasisimple groups of Lie type A \mathrm{A} satisfying the equivariance properties needed for the condition. We also prove the inductive conditions for the subset of unipotent characters.



1
Introduction

Since the time of Brauer, the study of the representation theory of a finite group through its relationship to local data has been a leading objective in the theory of finite groups.
One of the most influential conjectures recently in this realm has been the McKay conjecture, along with its refinements.
The McKay conjecture (recently announced as a theorem due to the third author and M. Cabanes
[
CS24
]
) says that for a prime
\ell
dividing the order of a finite group
G
, the number of irreducible characters of
\ell^{\prime}
-degree,
\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
, should be the same as that of
\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)
for
P
a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
.

In this paper, we are concerned with the McKay–Navarro conjecture (also known as the Galois–McKay conjecture,
[
Na04
, Conjecture A]
), which suggests that a bijection between
\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)

and
\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))
can be further chosen to be equivariant with respect to certain Galois automorphisms

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\leq\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
(See Definition
2.1
below.) Here,
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}
denotes the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by all roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}
.

The McKay–Navarro conjecture was reduced by Navarro–Späth–Vallejo in
[
NSV20
]
to the so-called “inductive Galois–McKay” conditions for quasisimple groups. We will refer to these as the inductive McKay–Navarro (iMN) conditions. These iMN conditions build upon the inductive McKay conditions of Isaacs–Malle–Navarro in
[
IMN07
]
, adding
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-compatibility. The first-named author
completed these conditions when
G
is a group of Lie type in defining characteristic in
[
Ru21
]
, and he and the second-named author completed the conditions when
\ell=2
in
[
SF22
,
RSF22
,
RSF25
]
.

The goal of this paper is to lay some foundations for dealing with the remaining cases - namely, the case of groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p\neq\ell
when
\ell
is odd.
We establish several results in this direction, which will be useful in the pursuit of the iMN conditions in this situation.
In particular, we introduce criterion in Section
3
that will be useful for proving that groups of Lie type satisfy the iMN conditions - see Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
(vii)
below.

The iMN conditions can be roughly described as an “equivariance” condition and an “extension” condition. In Sections
4
–
6
, we primarily work with the equivariance condition. Namely, this is the first part of
[
NSV20
, Definition 3.1]
, which we state here:

Condition
\@upn
1.1

Let
\ell
be a prime.
Let
G
be a finite quasisimple group with
\ell\mid|G|
and let
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
.
Then there is a proper
\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{P}
-stable subgroup

of

with
\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)\leqslant M
and an

\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{P}\times\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-equivariant bijection

\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(M)
such that corresponding characters lie over the same character of
\operatorname{Z}(G)
.

In Section

, we prove the following.

2
Preliminaries on the Galois automorphisms

We begin with some number-theoretic observations regarding the Galois automorphisms under consideration.

Notation
\@upn
2.1

As defined by Gabriel Navarro
[
Na04
]
, we let
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
denote the subgroup of the Galois group
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})
comprised of those
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
satisfying that there is some
e\geq 0
such that
\sigma
acts on
\ell^{\prime}
-roots of unity by
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{\ell^{e}}
.

For an integer
d\geq 1
, we will also let
\mathcal{H}_{[d]}
denote the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
stabilizing any
d
-th root of unity.

It will sometimes be useful to note that
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
can be identified with the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. As such, we observe the following immediate consequence of Hensel’s Lifting Lemma, which could also be seen using Gauss sums and arguments as in
[
SFT23
, Section 4]
.

Lemma
\@upn
2.2

Let
\ell
be an odd prime and let
a
be an integer relatively prime to
\ell
that is a square modulo
\ell
. Then
\sqrt{a}^{\sigma}=\sqrt{a}
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
.

Throughout, for an integer
q
and odd prime
\ell
, we let
d_{\ell}(q)
denote the multiplicative order of
q
modulo
\ell
.

Lemma
\@upn
2.3

Let
\ell
be an odd prime and
d
any integer dividing
(\ell-1)
. (For example, this is the case when
d
is the order of an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
.) Then:

(a)

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{[d]}
;

(b)

if
d=2m
with
m
odd, then
\mathcal{H}_{[d]}=\mathcal{H}_{[m]}
;

(c)

if
d=2m
with
m
odd and
q
is an integer with
d_{\ell}(q)=d
, then
\sqrt{-q}^{\sigma}=\sqrt{-q}
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
;

(d)

if
d
is odd and
q
is an integer with
d_{\ell}(q)=d
, then
\sqrt{q}^{\sigma}=\sqrt{q}
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
.

Proof.

If
\xi
is a
d
th root of unity (and hence an
(\ell-1)
st root of unity), then
\sigma(\xi)=\xi^{\ell^{e}}=\xi
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, so
2.3(a)
holds. For
2.3(b)
, note that any member of
\mathcal{G}
must necessarily fix
-1
, and that any
d
th root of unity must be of the form
\pm\zeta_{m}
with
\zeta_{m}
an
m
th root of unity.
In the situation of
2.3(c)
, we have
\ell
divides
q^{m}+1
, and hence
q^{m}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
. Since
m
is odd, we see
q
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
if and only if
-1
is. This means that
-q
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, and we apply Lemma
2.2
.

For
2.3(d)
, note that we may assume that
q
is not a square, since otherwise the statement is clear. Hence
q
is an odd power of a prime
p
. By construction,
d
is the order of
q
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
. As
\ell-1
is even and
d
is odd, this shows that
q
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
, and we again apply Lemma
2.2
.
∎

3
Criterion for the inductive McKay–Navarro condition

In this section, we introduce a statement that simplifies checking the inductive McKay–Navarro condition. The statement below (
3.5
) is modeled after
[
Sp12
, Thm. 2.12]
, which is a criterion for verifying the inductive McKay condition and was applied to simple groups of Lie type in various cases.

3.A
The inductive McKay–Navarro condition

Before stating our criterion, we introduce the inductive conditions from
[
NSV20
]
. We begin with some notation.

Definition
\@upn
3.1

Let
G\lhd A
and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
invariant under
A
. Then
(A,G,\chi)
is an ordinary character triple and there exists a projective representation
\mathcal{P}
of
A
associated to
\chi
, see
[
Na18
, Def. 5.2]
.
Assume that
H\leq A
with
A=GH
with
{\mathrm{C}}_{A}(G)\leq H
. If
\psi\in\mathrm{Irr}(M)
is some
H
-invariant character for
M:=G\cap H
, then there exists some projective representation
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
H
associated to
\psi
. We say that
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
gives

(A,G,\phi)\geq_{c}(H,M,\psi),

whenever the factor set of
\mathcal{P}
restricted to
H\times H
is the factor set of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and for each
c\in{\mathrm{C}}_{A}(G)
the matrices
\mathcal{P}(c)
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(c)
are scalar matrices associated to the same scalar; see also
[
Na18
, Def. 10.14]
. We also write
(A,G,\phi)\geq_{c}(H,M,\psi)
if there exist
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
giving that relation.

For the inductive McKay–Navarro condition, we need adaptions of the above notions.

Definition
\@upn
3.2

Given
G\lhd A
, the group
\mathcal{G}\times\mathrm{Aut}(A)_{G}
acts on
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
.
Let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathrm{Aut}(A)_{G}
with
\chi^{\alpha}=\chi
.
If
\chi
extends to a character
\widetilde{\chi}
of
A
, then there exists a unique linear character
\mu\in\mathrm{Irr}(A)
with

G\leq\ker(\mu)
and

\widetilde{\chi}^{\alpha}=\widetilde{\chi}\mu

by Gallagher’s theorem
[
Is76
, Cor. 6.17]
. We will write
[\widetilde{\chi},\alpha]:=\mu
in this situation.
The projective representation
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}
is again a projective representation of
A
associated to
\chi
and hence similar to
\mu\mathcal{P}
, i.e.
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}\sim\mu\mathcal{P}
for a unique map
\mu:A/G\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We denote
\mu
again by
[\mathcal{P},{\alpha}]
, see also
[
NSV20
, Lem. 1.4, Lem. 2.3]
.

Given
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
,
G\lhd A
and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
we denote by
\chi^{\mathcal{H}}
the sum of all
\mathcal{H}
-conjugates of
\chi
and call
(A,G,\chi)_{\mathcal{H}}
an
\mathcal{H}
–triple if
\chi^{\mathcal{H}}
is
A
-invariant, see
[
NSV20
, §1]
. Assume that
H\leq A
with
A=GH
and
{\mathrm{C}}_{A}(G)\leq H
. Let
\psi\in\mathrm{Irr}(M)
such that
(H,M,\psi)_{\mathcal{H}}
is an
\mathcal{H}
–triple.
Assume
(A_{\chi},G,\chi)\geq_{c}(H_{\psi},M,\psi)
given by
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
.
If
(H\times\mathcal{H})_{\chi}=(H\times\mathcal{H})_{\psi}
and
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
can be chosen such that they satisfy

[\mathcal{P},{\alpha}](h)=[\mathcal{P}^{\prime},{\alpha}](h){\text{\quad\quad for every }}h\in H_{\psi},

then we write

(A,G,\phi)_{\mathcal{H}}\geq_{c}(H,M,\psi)_{\mathcal{H}},

see
[
NSV20
, Def. 1.5]
.

Now the
inductive McKay–Navarro (iMN) condition
for a given simple group
S
and a prime
\ell
is satisfied if for the universal covering group
G
of
S
, any Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
P
of
G
and
\Delta=\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{P}
, there exists some
\Delta
-stable subgroup
M
of
G
with
{\mathrm{N}}_{G}(P)\leq M\lneq G
and an

(\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\Delta)
-equivariant bijection
\Omega:\mathrm{Irr_{\ell^{\prime}}}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Irr_{\ell^{\prime}}}(M)
such that

(G\rtimes\Delta_{\chi^{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}},G,\chi)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}}\geq(M\rtimes\Delta_{\Omega(\chi)^{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}},M,\Omega(\chi))_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}}.

That is, the inductive McKay–Navarro conditions essentially require Condition
1.1
, together with the above character triple condition (which we sometimes call the “extension condition”).

3.B
A criterion for the iMN condition

As with the criterion in
[
Sp12
, Thm. 2.12]
, our criterion will require the use of extension maps, but now requiring compatibility with Galois automorphisms.

Definition
\@upn
3.3

For
G\lhd A
and subsets
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathrm{Irr}(A)
, we write
\mathrm{Irr}(A\mid\mathfrak{G})
for the irreducible characters of
A
whose irreducible constituents on restriction to
G
lie in
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\mathfrak{A})
for the irreducible constituents of characters in
\mathfrak{A}
on restriction to
G
. Further, an
extension map
with respect to
G\lhd A
for
\mathfrak{G}
is a map
\Lambda:\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow\bigcup_{G\leq I\leq A}\mathrm{Irr}(I)
such that for every
\chi\in\mathfrak{G}
,
\Lambda(\chi)
is an extension of
\chi
to
A_{\chi}
. If an extension map with respect to
G\lhd A
for
\mathfrak{G}
exists, we say that
maximal extendibility holds with respect to
G\lhd A
for
\mathfrak{G}
, see
[
Sp09
, 4.1]
and
[
Sp10
, 4.3]
.

Given an extension map
\Lambda
and a subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
, we say that
\Lambda
is
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant
if
\Lambda(\lambda^{\sigma})=\Lambda(\lambda)^{\sigma}
for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}
.

We also note that a subset of linear characters in
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, which is at the same time a group given by multiplication, acts on
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
by multiplication.

We are now ready to introduce our criterion.
The following statement helps to construct bijections as required in the inductive McKay–Navarro condition in the case where
G
is a group of Lie type.

\@upn
3.4

Let
G

\ell\mid|G|

.

(iii)

\widehat{M}
-stable
\widecheck{M}
-transversals
\mathfrak{G}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{M}
. Additionally there exist
\widehat{M}
-equivariant extension maps
\Phi_{glo}
and
\Phi_{loc}

\mathfrak{G}_{0}

\mathrm{Irr}({\mathrm{C}}_{E}(G)\mid\Phi_{glo}(\chi))=\mathrm{Irr}({\mathrm{C}}_{E}(G)\mid\Phi_{loc}(\psi))\text{ for every }\chi\in\mathfrak{G}_{0}{\text{ and }}\psi\in\mathfrak{M}_{0}.

There exists a subgroup
B\leq(\widecheck{G}E\times\mathcal{H})
with

{\mathrm{N}}_{\widecheck{G}E}(Q)B={\mathrm{N}}_{\widecheck{G}E}(Q)\times\mathcal{H},

that stabilizes
\mathfrak{G}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
.

(vi)

For
\widecheck{\mathfrak{M}}:=\mathrm{Irr}(\widecheck{M}\mid\mathfrak{M})
and
\widecheck{\mathfrak{G}}:=\mathrm{Irr}(\widecheck{G}\mid\mathfrak{G})
there exists a
\mathrm{Irr}(\widecheck{M}/M)\rtimes(\widehat{M}B)
-equivariant bijection
\widecheck{\Omega}:\widecheck{\mathfrak{G}}\longrightarrow\widecheck{\mathfrak{M}}
with

\widecheck{\Omega}(\widecheck{\mathfrak{G}}\cap\mathrm{Irr}(\widecheck{G}\mid\nu))=\widecheck{\mathfrak{M}}\cap\mathrm{Irr}(\widecheck{M}\mid\nu)
for every
\nu\in\mathrm{Irr}(\operatorname{Z}(\widecheck{G}))
.

Then there exists a
\widehat{M}\widecheck{M}\times\mathcal{H}
-equivariant bijection

5.A
Properties of generalized Gelfand-Graev characters

Let
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
be the multiplicative group of
{\mathbb{F}}=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
. If
{{\mathbf{H}}}
is an algebraic group over
{\mathbb{F}}
, then we denote by
\mathcal{X}({{\mathbf{H}}})
the group of characters
{{\mathbf{H}}}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and by
\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})
the set of cocharacters
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to{{\mathbf{H}}}
. In the case that
{{\mathbf{H}}}
is a torus, we write
\langle-,-\rangle:\mathcal{X}({{\mathbf{H}}})\times\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})\to\mathbb{Z}
for the natural perfect pairing and similarly for its extension by scalars

\langle-,-\rangle:\mathcal{V}({{\mathbf{H}}})\times\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})\to\mathbb{Q},

where
\mathcal{V}({{\mathbf{H}}}):=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{X}({{\mathbf{H}}})
and
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}}):=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})
. We identify
\mathcal{X}({{\mathbf{H}}})
and
\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})
with their canonical respective images in
\mathcal{V}({{\mathbf{H}}})
and
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{H}}})
.

Assume
{{\mathbf{G}}}
is a connected reductive algebraic group, and let
{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant{\mathbf{B}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
be a maximal torus and Borel subgroup of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
. The pair
({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})
determines a set of simple and positive roots
\Delta\subseteq\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{X}({\mathbf{T}})
. If
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})
is the coroot corresponding to
\alpha\in\Phi
, then we have a cocharacter

\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\widecheck{\alpha}=2\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\widecheck{\omega}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})

(5.1)

where
\widecheck{\omega}_{\alpha}\in\langle\Phi^{\vee}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})
is the fundamental dominant coweight determined by
\alpha
. In other words,
\langle\alpha,\widecheck{\omega}_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Kronecker delta for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
.

The cocharacters
\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}
arising in
eq.

5.1

will be called the
principal cocharacters of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
. As any two pairs

({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})
are
{{\mathbf{G}}}
-conjugate, the principal cocharacters form a single orbit
for the action of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})
by conjugation.

Lemma
\@upn
5.1

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})
is a principal cocharacter, then
z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}:=\lambda(-1)\in\mathrm{Z}({{\mathbf{G}}})
is a central element satisfying
(z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}})^{2}=1
. It
is independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

Assume
\lambda=\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}
. For any simple root
\alpha\in\Delta

we have
\alpha(\lambda(c))=c^{2}
. Therefore
z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}
is in the kernel of
every root so must be contained in
\mathrm{Z}({{\mathbf{G}}})
. It is independent of
\lambda

because all principal cocharacters are conjugate.
∎

R
If
{{\mathbf{G}}}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
and
{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant{\mathbf{B}}
are the diagonal torus and upper triangular Borel subgroup, then

\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}(c)=\mathrm{diag}(c^{n-1},c^{n-3},\dots,c^{-(n-3)},c^{-(n-1)})

for any
c\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. We then also have that
z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}=(-1)^{n-1}\operatorname{Id}_{n}
, where
\operatorname{Id}_{n}
is the identity matrix.

We say
{{\mathbf{H}}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
is a subsystem subgroup if
{{\mathbf{H}}}
is a closed
connected reductive subgroup of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
containing a maximal torus of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
. We
are interested in the subsystem subgroups
{{\mathbf{H}}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
that
satisfy the following property:

z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}\cdot\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{H}}})=z_{{{\mathbf{H}}}}\cdot\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{H}}}).

(5.2)

To see why, we pick a Frobenius endomorphism
F:{{\mathbf{G}}}\to{{\mathbf{G}}}
defining
{{\mathbf{G}}}

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We say a cocharacter
\lambda\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})
is
F
-fixed if

F(\lambda(c))=\lambda(c^{q})
and we denote
by
\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})^{F}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})
the set of
F
-fixed

({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})
is
F

\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}

is
F
-fixed because it is fixed by all graph automorphisms. Hence, there are

F
-stable principal cocharacters.

Proposition
\@upn
5.2

Assume
\lambda\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})^{F}
is a principal cocharacter. Let
k\in({\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{\times}
and
c\in({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}})^{\times}
be elements such that
k=c^{2}
. Then
t=\lambda(c)\in{{\mathbf{G}}}
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\mathscr{L}(t)\in\langle z_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}\rangle
,

(b)

if
{{\mathbf{H}}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
is an
F
-stable subsystem subgroup satisfying

eq.

5.2
and
p
is good for
{{\mathbf{H}}}
, then
{}^{t}u
is
{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}
-conjugate
to
u^{k}
for any regular unipotent element
u\in{{\mathbf{H}}}^{F}
.

Proof.

(a). Pick a pair
({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})
so that
\lambda=\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})}
. As

\mathscr{L}(t)=\lambda(c^{(q-1)})
it suffices to show that
c^{2(q-1)}=1
. If
p=2
this is clear because
c=k=1
, so suppose
p\neq 2
. Then

and we have

c^{2(p-1)}=k^{(p-1)}=1.

Now we simply use that
(p-1)\mid(q-1)
.

(b). Let
{\mathbf{B}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
be a Borel subgroup of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
such that
{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{H}}}}={\mathbf{B}}\cap{{\mathbf{H}}}
is the unique Borel subgroup of
{{\mathbf{H}}}
containing

, see

[
DM20

. As
F(u)=u
we must have

so
{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{H}}}}
contains an
F
-stable maximal torus
{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{H}}}}
. Consider the principal cocharacter
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{H}}}})}
of
{{\mathbf{H}}}

F

there exists an element

We now want to determine which subsystem subgroups satisfy
eq.

5.2
.
The following exercise with root data shows that Levi subgroups satisfy this

Lemma
\@upn
5.3

Assume
{{\mathbf{L}}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
is a Levi subgroup of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
and let
\pi:{{\mathbf{L}}}\to{{\mathbf{L}}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}})
be the canonical quotient map and let
\pi_{*}:\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{L}}})\to\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{L}}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))
be the natural map
\gamma\mapsto\pi\circ\gamma
. If
\lambda\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{L}}})\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{G}}})
is a principal cocharacter of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
, then the following hold:

(a)

\pi_{*}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{L}}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))
is a principal cocharacter of
{{\mathbf{L}}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}})
,

(b)

\pi_{*}(\lambda)=\pi_{*}(\lambda^{\prime})
with
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({{\mathbf{L}}})
a principal cocharacter of
{{\mathbf{L}}}
.

In particular,
{{\mathbf{L}}}
satisfies
eq.

5.2
.

Proof.

We can assume
{{\mathbf{L}}}
is a Levi complement of
{\mathbf{P}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
. We pick a pair
({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})
of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
such that
{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{L}}}
and
{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant{\mathbf{B}}\leqslant{\mathbf{P}}
. If
\Sigma\subseteq\Phi
is the root system of
{{\mathbf{L}}}
then
I=\Sigma\cap\Delta
is a simple system of roots, where
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
are defined as above. Recall that we have a natural short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\vee}(\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})\overset{\pi_{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))\longrightarrow 0

and tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
gives an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\vee}(\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})\overset{\pi_{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))\longrightarrow 0

The vector space
V:=\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})
has a direct sum decomposition
V_{I}\oplus V_{I}^{\perp}
, where
V_{I}=\langle\Sigma^{\vee}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
is spanned by the coroots of
{{\mathbf{L}}}
and

V_{I}^{\perp}=\{v\in V\mid\langle\alpha,v\rangle=0\text{ for all }\alpha\in I\}.

As
\mathcal{X}^{\vee}(\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))=\mathcal{X}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}})\cap V_{I}^{\perp}
we may identify
V_{I}
with
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))
and
\pi_{*}:V\to\mathcal{V}^{\vee}({\mathbf{T}}/\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))
with the projection to
V_{I}
. Now if
\alpha,\beta\in I
then

\delta_{\alpha,\beta}=\langle\alpha,\widecheck{\omega}_{\beta}\rangle=\langle\alpha,\pi_{*}(\widecheck{\omega}_{\beta})\rangle.

This shows that
\pi_{*}(\widecheck{\omega}_{\alpha})
is the fundamental dominant coweight of the root system
\Sigma
with respect to
I
. In particular, if
{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{L}}}}={\mathbf{B}}\cap{{\mathbf{L}}}
then by definition

\pi_{*}(\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}})})=\lambda_{(\pi({\mathbf{T}}),\pi({\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{L}}}}))}=\pi_{*}(\lambda_{({\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{B}}_{{{\mathbf{L}}}})}).

Now we simply evaluate this at
-1
.
∎

Lemma
\@upn

If
u\in{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}

{{\mathbf{G}}}

is regular in an
F

{{\mathbf{G}}}

Let
{{\mathbf{L}}}\leqslant{{\mathbf{G}}}
be a Levi subgroup that contains

as a regular

is a maximal torus
of the centralizer. Certainly there exists a maximal torus
{\mathbf{S}}
of

\mathrm{C}^{\circ}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}(u)
containing the connected center
\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}})
. We have

{\mathbf{M}}:=\mathrm{C}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}({\mathbf{S}})
is a Levi subgroup of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
containing
u
and

\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}})\leqslant{\mathbf{S}}\leqslant\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({\mathbf{M}})

{{\mathbf{L}}}=\mathrm{C}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}(\mathrm{Z}^{\circ}({{\mathbf{L}}}))

F

\mathrm{C}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}({\mathbf{S}}_{1})

\rho_{i+1}^{\sigma}\operatorname{Res}^{Y}_{Y_{i+1}}(\lambda_{\sigma})^{-1}
is also an extension of
\rho_{i}
and we have

\langle\rho_{i+1}^{\sigma}\operatorname{Res}^{Y}_{Y_{i+1}}(\lambda_{\sigma})^{-1},\phi_{i+1}\rangle=\langle\rho_{i+1},\operatorname{Res}^{Y}_{Y_{i+1}}(\lambda_{\sigma})^{-1}\phi_{i+1}^{\sigma}\rangle=\langle\rho_{i+1},\phi_{i+1}\rangle

is odd. Since these properties uniquely determine
\phi_{i+1}
it follows that
\rho_{i+1}^{\sigma}\operatorname{Res}^{Y}_{Y_{i+1}}(\lambda_{\sigma})^{-1}=\rho_{i+1}
. Hence, by induction
\tilde{\rho}^{\sigma}=\tilde{\rho}\lambda_{\sigma}
. This shows the statement in the case where
r=2^{a}
.

Now for the case
r=3^{a}
one argues in the same way but this time one constructs inductively characters
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(Y_{i}\mid\rho_{i-1})
,
i=0,\dots,a
such that
b_{i}:=\langle\phi_{i},\rho_{i}\rangle
is distinct from the multiplicities of all other extensions of
\rho_{i-1}
to
Y_{i}
in
\phi_{i}
. This again gives a chain of characters which are uniquely determined by the sequence of multiplicities
b_{0},\dots,b_{a}
.
∎

7.B
Extensions of unipotent characters

We assume that
({{\mathbf{G}}},F)
is a finite reductive group, with
F
a Frobenius endomorphism. Since we are only concerned with unipotent characters, we shall assume throughout that
{{\mathbf{G}}}
is of adjoint type.
Recall that we can always write
E({{\mathbf{G}}}^{F})=\langle\Gamma,F_{0}\rangle
, where
\Gamma
is the group generated by graph automorphisms and where
F_{0}
is a Frobenius endomorphism that acts trivially on
W
, the Weyl group of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
, and
F_{0}^{r}=F^{\delta}
, where
\delta
is the smallest integer such that
F^{\delta}
acts trivially on
W
. In
[
DM24
]
, the authors compute the character values of extensions of unipotent characters to
G\langle\gamma\rangle
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In this section, we build on their work and show how one can compute the extensions of unipotent characters to
GE({{\mathbf{G}}}^{F})
.

As in
[
DM24
]
, for
w\in W
, we denote by
X_{w}
the associated Deligne–Lusztig variety. Let
R_{w}
be the associated Deligne–Lusztig virtual character defined by

R_{w}(g):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Tr}(g,H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))

for
g\in G
. For any unipotent
character
\rho
of
G
,
F^{\delta}
acts by the same eigenvalue of Frobenius
\omega_{\rho}
on any
\rho
-isotypic component
H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\rho}
of any
\ell
-adic cohomology group of any
X_{w}
, up to multiplication
by integral powers of
q^{\delta}
. (See
[
GM20
, 4.2.21]
.)

Proposition
\@upn
7.3

Let
\rho\in\operatorname{UCh}(G)
be a cuspidal character with Frobenius eigenvalue
\omega_{\rho}
. Let
\zeta_{0}
be an
r
th root of
\omega_{\rho}
. Then there exists an extension
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\langle F_{0}\rangle)
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\tilde{\rho})=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{0})
. Moreover, for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
we have
[\tilde{\rho},\sigma](F_{0})=\zeta_{0}^{\sigma}\zeta_{0}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Note that by
[
GM20
, Proposition 4.5.5]
and
[
Ge03
, Table 1]
, we have
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{\rho})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{\rho})\in\{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}(i),\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})\}
, unless
\rho
is either one of the two cuspidal characters in
\mathrm{E}_{7}(q)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q})
or one of the four cuspidal characters in
\mathrm{E}_{8}(q)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})
.

We first deal with the non-exceptional cases, i.e. when
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\in\{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}(i),\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})\}
. For a prime number
t
we let
F_{t}
be a generator of the Sylow
t
-subgroup of
\langle F_{0}\rangle
such that
F_{t}^{r_{t}}=F^{\delta}
for some integer
r_{t}
.
By Lemma
7.1
it suffices to construct the extensions of
\rho
to
G\langle F_{t}\rangle
.
By
[
Na18
, Cor. 6.6(a)]
we can in this case assume that
t=2
or
t=3
.
By
[
Lu02
, Thm. 2.18]
(and
[
Lu02
, 2.19]
resp.
[
DM24
, Proposition 2.5,2.7]
when
{{{G}}}
admits a graph automorphism), there exists some
w\in W

such that
\langle R_{w},\rho\rangle\in\{\pm 1\}
.
In particular, there exists some integer
i
such that
\rho
has multiplicity coprime to
t

in the character of
H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
. Hence, there exists a generalized eigenspace
H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mu}
for an eigenvalue
\mu
for the action of
F^{\delta}
on
H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
such that
\rho
occurs with multiplicity not divisible by
r
in the character of this eigenspace. Recall (see e.g.
[
GM20
, 4.2.21]
) that the eigenvalue
\mu
coincides with the Frobenius eigenvalue
\omega_{\rho}
up to multiplication by an integral power of
q^{\delta}
, say
\omega_{\rho}=\mu q^{\delta a}
. Set
K_{0}:=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\mu)=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\rho)
.

We denote by
M:=H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},K_{0})_{\mu,\rho}
the
\rho
-isotypic component of the generalized eigenspace of
H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},K_{0}):=H^{i}_{c}(X_{w},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}K_{0}
. Note that since
F_{t}
acts trivially on
W
we have an action
F_{t}:X(w)\to X(w)
on the associated Deligne–Lusztig variety. The associated map
F_{t}^{\ast}:M\to{}^{F_{t}}M
is an isomorphism of

-modules and satisfies
(F_{t}^{\ast})^{r_{t}}=F^{\delta}=\mu\cdot\mathrm{Id}
. Thus, by Lemma
7.2
there exists some extension
\tilde{\rho}
of
\rho
such that
K_{0}(\mu_{0})=K_{0}(\tilde{\rho})
, where
\mu_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
satisfies
\mu_{0}^{r_{t}}=\mu
. Note that
F_{t}
is a Frobenius defining an
\mathbb{F}_{p_{0}}
-structure on
{{\mathbf{G}}}
, for some power
p_{0}
of
p
. Then we have
p_{0}^{r_{t}}=q^{\delta}
. Hence, if we define
\zeta_{0,t}:=\mu_{0}p_{0}^{a}
then we have
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{0,t})=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\mu_{0})
and
\zeta_{0,t}^{r_{t}}=\mu q^{\delta a}=\omega_{\rho}
.

Finally, we consider the exceptional cuspidal characters
\rho
of
G=\mathrm{E}_{7}(q),\mathrm{E}_{8}(q)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\in\{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}),\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{\rho})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})\}
. In this case, by
[
Lu77
, Thm. 6.1]
and
[
Lu77
, Section 7.3]
, we observe that
\rho
appears with multiplicity one in the generalized eigenspace of an eigenvalue
\mu
in the cohomology group of
H^{r}_{c}(X(c),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, where
c
is the Coxeter element of
W
and
r
is the rank of
{{\mathbf{G}}}
. We extend
H^{r}_{c}(X(c),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\mu))_{\mu}
to a
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{0})(G\langle F_{0}\rangle)
-module
\tilde{M}
as in the proof of Lemma
7.2
. By Frobenius reciprocity, there exists a unique extension
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\langle F_{0}\rangle\mid\rho)
which appears with multiplicity one in the character of
\tilde{M}
. As in Lemma
7.2
, this yields the result of the proposition.
∎

We finish this section by recalling the corresponding result for the extensions to
G\langle\gamma\rangle
, where
\gamma
is a graph automorphism. For this recall that the unipotent characters of
G=\mathrm{A}_{n}(\varepsilon q)
are parametrized by partitions
\rho
of

. We write
\chi_{\rho}
for the corresponding unipotent character and denote by

a fixed extension of

to

.

Theorem
\@upn
7.4
— Dudas–Malle.

Let
\gamma
be a graph automorphism of a simple algebraic group
{{\mathbf{G}}}
of simply connected type. Then all unipotent characters have a rational extension to their inertia group in

except possibly in the following cases:

(a)

G
is of type
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\varepsilon q)
and
\chi=\chi_{\rho}\in\mathrm{Unip}(G)
has
2
-core
\rho_{0}
of size

. Then
\mathbb{Q}(\hat{\chi}_{\rho})=\mathbb{Q}(\hat{\chi}_{\rho_{0}})
.

(b)

G
is of type
\mathrm{E}_{6}(\varepsilon q)
and
\chi
lies in the
2
-Harish-Chandra-series (resp. Harish-Chandra series if
\varepsilon=-1
) of a Levi subgroup of type
\mathrm{A}_{5}(\varepsilon q)(q+\varepsilon)
.

\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\varepsilon q})

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the description given in Dudas–Malle
[
DM24
, Thm. 3]
. Note that if

then this is not stated there but one can immediately check this with the condition given there. For the information about the extensions in

one can consult

and the references given there.
∎

We suppose now that

\@upn

Assume first that

Since unipotent characters of

Corollary
\@upn

be characters of

and
\chi^{\prime}
have the same

\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
have the same

Proof.

This follows directly from the previous corollary. We give a combinatorial proof of the fact which may be of independent interest. We observe that it is clearly enough to show that removing a
d
-rim hook is equivalent to successively removing
2
-rim hooks. We show this by induction on
d
(the case

being trivial). Now suppose that
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{n})
with associated
\beta
-set
\beta(\lambda)=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots\}
. Assume that
\lambda
has a
d
-hook, i.e. there exists some
i
such that
\beta_{i}-d\notin\beta(\lambda)
. Hence, by removing this
d
-hook we get the partition
\lambda_{d}
with new

. We let

be the maximal even integer such that

\lambda^{\prime}
with

can be obtained from
\lambda^{\prime}
by removing a

∎

Let

{\mathbf{L}}
is a torus except when
{{{G}}}=\mathrm{E}_{7}(q)
and
d=4
(see
[
GM20
, Table 3.3]
).
In this case
{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\cong\operatorname{PGL}_{2}^{3}
and thus every unipotent character of
L_{0}
has a rational extension to its inertia group in
L_{0}E(L_{0})
. These considerations even show that
\lambda
has a
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-invariant extension to
\hat{N}_{\lambda}
. A similar argument applies in the case where the relative Weyl group is cyclic. According to
[
Sp09
, Table 3]
the Levi subgroup
{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
has in these cases only components of type
\mathrm{A}_{1}
and
\mathrm{A}_{2}
. We also note that
{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
has only a rational components of type
\mathrm{A}_{2}(\delta q)
,
\delta\in\{\pm 1\}
, if the order of
\delta q
modulo
\ell
is odd. Hence, again by Lemma
2.3
every unipotent character of
L_{0}
has an extension with values in
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
to its inertia group in
L_{0}E(L_{0})
.

In particular, Lemma
8.7
ensures that
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
acts trivially on
\mathrm{Irr}(\hat{N}\mid\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(N\mid\lambda))
.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
8.8
we are left to show that every character
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
has a
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-invariant character of
\hat{G}
covering it. For this we use the information about unipotent characters in
[
Ca85
, Section 13.9]
. It can now be checked that if
\omega_{\chi}
is a
m
th root of unity then
m\mid d
. If
\omega_{\chi}=\sqrt{-q}
, then
d\in\{2,6,10,14,18,30\}
so that in all of these cases
d^{\prime}
, the order of
-q
modulo
\ell
, is odd.
Applying Lemma
8.1
to all these cases shows that
X^{r_{0}}-\omega_{\chi}
, where
q=p^{r_{0}}
, always has a zero in
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. Hence, Lemma
7.6
shows that every
\chi
has a
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-invariant extension to
G\langle F_{0}\rangle
. For graph automorphisms we apply Lemma
7.4
and note that the exceptional characters in
\mathrm{E}_{6}(\varepsilon q)
whose extension have character field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\varepsilon q})
only appear when
d
is odd (resp. when
d^{\prime}
is odd). This shows by Lemma
7.1
that every character
\chi
has a
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-invariant character of
\hat{G}
covering it. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we can now prove Theorem
D
from the introduction, which says that the inductive McKay–Navarro conditions hold for
G
and
\ell
at unipotent characters.

Proof of Theorem
D
.

As before let
{{\mathbf{G}}}\hookrightarrow\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}}
be a regular embedding and observe that
\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}}/\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}})\cong{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}
, where
{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}
is the adjoint group of the same type as
{{\mathbf{G}}}
. Note that every unipotent character
\chi
of
{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}
extends to a unipotent character of
\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}}^{F}
with
\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}})^{F}
in its kernel. Its deflation can therefore be seen as a character of
{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}
. On the other hand, every unipotent character of
{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}
has an extension
\hat{\chi}
to its inertia group in
{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}\rtimes E({{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F})
.
In particular, its inflation can be seen as a character of
\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}}^{F}\rtimes E({{\mathbf{G}}}^{F})_{\chi}
. On the other hand, the character
\Omega(\chi)
lies in
\mathrm{Irr}(M\mid\lambda)
where
({\mathbf{L}},\lambda)
with
{\mathbf{L}}={\mathrm{C}}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}}(\mathbf{S})
is the
d
-cuspidal pair in whose
d
-Harish-Chandra series
\chi
lies. As
\lambda
is again a unipotent character it follows again that
\lambda
extends to a unipotent character of
({\mathbf{L}}\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}}))^{F}
with
\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{{{\mathbf{G}}}})^{F}
in its kernel. Its deflation
\lambda_{0}
is therefore a
d
-cuspidal character of
{\mathbf{L}}_{0}:={\mathbf{L}}/\operatorname{Z}({\mathbf{L}})
.
In particular,
\Omega(\chi)
can be seen as a character in
\mathrm{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mathrm{N}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}}({\mathbf{L}}_{0})\mid\lambda_{0})
. Let
\hat{\Omega}
be the map from Theorem
8.8
or
8.9
. Then we have
\hat{\psi}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{N}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}\rtimes E({{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F})}({\mathbf{L}}_{0})_{\chi}\mid\Omega(\chi))
, where
\psi
is the Clifford correspondent of
\hat{\Omega}(\operatorname{Ind}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}\rtimes E({{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F})_{\chi}}^{{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}\rtimes E({{\mathbf{G}}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F})}(\hat{\chi}))
. The representations associated to
\hat{\chi}
and
\hat{\psi}
now define projective representations (with trivial cocycle) associated to
\chi
and
\psi
respectively. Now as the map
\hat{\Omega}
is equivariant with respect to linear characters and the action of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
it follows that these representations satisfy all the properties in
[
NSV20
, Def. 3.1]
, or in other words, one has
((\widecheck{G}E)_{\chi^{\mathcal{H}}},G,\chi)_{\mathcal{H}}\geq_{c}((\widecheck{M}\widehat{M})_{\psi^{\mathcal{H}}},M,\psi)_{\mathcal{H}}
.
∎

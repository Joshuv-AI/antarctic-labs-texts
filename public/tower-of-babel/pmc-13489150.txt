Defining a cardiac mass in pregnancy: contrast free cardiovascular magnetic resonance enables non-invasive diagnosis of cardiac lipoma
Authors: Nang Aye, Arun John Baksi, Jan Till, Raad H Mohiaddin, Vincenzo Nuzzi, ugur Canpolat, Gautam Sen
Published: 2026

Case description

A 27-year-old woman at 25 weeks’ gestation was referred to cardiology for evaluation because of a family history of ventricular fibrillation arrest in her mother, who was found to have congenital long QT syndrome secondary to a familial CACNA1C mutation. Genetic testing confirmed that the patient carried the same variant. Her electrocardiogram demonstrated a normal QT interval, and exercise testing showed appropriate QT adaptation. Transthoracic echocardiography unexpectedly demonstrated a 2.7 × 1.8 cm echogenic mass adjacent to the basal right ventricular free wall, with a small pericardial effusion.

She also carried a BRCA1 mutation with planned risk-reducing surgery following pregnancy.

Given the pregnancy, contrast-free cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) was performed. Imaging demonstrated a well-circumscribed mass approximately ∼23 × 16 mm, abutting the epicardial surface of the basal right ventricular free wall on balanced steady-state free precession cine imaging (Figure 1A). Tissue characterization showed a homogeneous hyperintense lesion on T1-weighted imaging (Figure 1B) with complete signal suppression on fat-suppressed T2 Short Tau Inversion Recovery sequences (Figure 1C). Native T1 mapping demonstrated markedly reduced native myocardial T1 values of the lesion (∼188 ms at 1.5T; compared with the normal myocardial reference range 975–1065 ms) (Figure 1D), consistent with lipomatous tissue composition. There was no compression of right ventricular inflow or involvement of the tricuspid valve.

Cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging demonstrating a well-defined mass adjacent to the basal right ventricular free wall in a 27-year-old pregnant woman. (A) Balanced steady-state free precession cine imaging shows a well-circumscribed epicardial lesion (arrow). (B) T1-weighted imaging demonstrates a homogeneous hyperintense lesion. (C) Fat-suppressed T2 short tau inversion recovery (STIR) imaging demonstrates signal suppression within the lesion, consistent with fatty tissue composition. (D) Native T1 mapping shows markedly reduced T1 values within the lesion (∼188 ms at 1.5 T, normal reference 975–1065 ms at 1.5 T), strongly supporting the diagnosis of cardiac lipoma.

Differential diagnoses included liposarcoma, pericardial fat, and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy. The encapsulated appearance, homogeneous fat signal, complete fat suppression, markedly reduced native T1 values, and absence of invasive features or right ventricular structural abnormalities favoured cardiac lipoma. Therefore, biopsy was not pursued.

Given the absence of symptoms or haemodynamic compromise, conservative management was recommended. The patient subsequently had an uncomplicated delivery and remains asymptomatic. She is awaiting outpatient review by a cardiac electrophysiologist for ongoing assessment of her familial CACNA1C pathogenic variant, and interval cardiac imaging has been planned to monitor the stability of the lipoma.

Discussion

CMR is the gold-standard investigation for assessment of cardiac masses due to its superior anatomical definition and tissue characterization.1

Cardiac lipomas are rare benign tumours composed of mature adipose tissue.2 They are frequently asymptomatic and generally managed conservatively unless they cause arrhythmia, embolism, coronary compression, or haemodynamic obstruction.3 In asymptomatic patients, serial imaging surveillance is an appropriate management strategy, while surgical resection is reserved for symptomatic lesions or those causing significant functional compromise. Recurrence following surgical resection is uncommon although follow-up is recommended.3

This case highlights the value of contrast-free CMR for the assessment of cardiac mass during pregnancy. Conventional tissue characterization combined with native T1 mapping enabled confident non-invasive diagnosis of a cardiac lipoma, avoiding both gadolinium-based contrast administration and invasive tissue biopsy. Histological confirmation was not obtained because the imaging findings were sufficiently characteristic that biopsy was not clinically indicated and would not have altered patient management.

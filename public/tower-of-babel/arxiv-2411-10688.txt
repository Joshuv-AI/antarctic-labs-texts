Spin-lattice relaxation for point-node–like 𝒔 \bm{s} -wave superconductivity in f f -electron systems
Shingo Haruna 1∗, Koki Doi 1, Takuji Nomura 1,2 and Hirono Kaneyasu 3

Abstract
In this study, we examined the temperature dependence of the spin-lattice relaxation using an f f - d d - p p model, which is an effective model of UTe 2 . Solving the linearized Eliashberg equation in the f f - d d - p p model based on third-order perturbation theory, we obtain a point-node–like s s -wave pairing state. Our result shows that the Hebel-Slichter-peak in the point-node–like s s -wave pairing state is smaller than that in the isotropic s s -wave pairing state. However, the Hebel-Slichter peak remains robust even in the point-node–like s s -wave pairing state, and the point-node–like s s -wave state is inconsistent with the results of nuclear magnetic resonance measurements.

The spin-lattice relaxation rate in NMR measurements
1/T_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{1}T}\propto\lim_{\Omega\to 0}\sum\limits_{\bm{Q}}\frac{\mathrm{Im}\chi^{R}_{+-}(\bm{Q},\Omega)}{\Omega},

(2)

where
\chi^{R}_{+-}(\bm{Q},\Omega)

This retarded spin susceptibility is obtained by the analytic continuation of the thermal spin susceptibility
\chi_{+-}(\bm{Q},i\Omega_{m})

\displaystyle\chi_{+-}(Q)=-\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\sum\limits_{l_{1}l_{2}}\Big[\mathcal{G}_{l_{2}l_{1}}(k)\mathcal{G}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(k+Q)+\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(k)\mathcal{F}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(k+Q)\Big]

(3)

in the spin-singlet superconducting condition, where the thermal Green’s functions are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ll^{\prime}}(k)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\beta}\expectationvalue{T_\tau\Big[c_{\bm{k} l \uparrow}(\tau)c^\dagger_{\bm{k} l' \uparrow} \Big]}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ll^{\prime}}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}\expectationvalue{T_\tau\Big[c_{\bm{k} l \uparrow}(\tau) c_{-\bm{k} l' \downarrow} \Big]}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}_{ll^{\prime}}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}\expectationvalue{T_\tau\Big[c^\dagger_{-\bm{k} l' \downarrow}(\tau) c^\dagger_{\bm{k} l \uparrow} \Big]}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau.

The four-dimensional momenta
k
and
Q
are defined by

\omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi T

The indices

The analytic continuation of the thermal Green’s functions leads to the band diagonalized retarded Green’s functions

\displaystyle G^{R}_{a}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon+i\gamma+\xi_{a}(\bm{k})}{E^{2}_{a}(\bm{k})-(\varepsilon+i\gamma)^{2}},

\displaystyle F^{R}_{a}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{a}(\bm{k})}{E^{2}_{a}(\bm{k})-(\varepsilon+i\gamma)^{2}},

(8)

where

39

.

\gamma
is a small real value related to the lifetime of the quasi-particles.

\xi_{a}(\bm{k})

\Delta_{a}(\bm{k})

a
, respectively.
The analytic continuation of Eq. (
3

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{+-}(\bm{Q},\Omega)\propto\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}\sum\limits_{l_{1}l_{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\varepsilon

\displaystyle\bigg[\tanh\frac{\varepsilon}{2T}\Big\{G^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)G^{R}_{l_{2}l_{1}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-G^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)G^{A}_{l_{2}l_{1}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+F^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)F^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-F^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)F^{A}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)\Big\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-G^{A}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)G^{A}_{l_{2}l_{1}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+F^{R}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)F^{A}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-F^{A}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q},\varepsilon+\Omega)F^{A}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)\Big\}\bigg],

(9)

where we assume that the gap function
\Delta_{a}(\bm{k})

Defining two functions as

\displaystyle N_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}\sum\limits_{a}U_{l_{1}a}(\bm{k})U^{*}_{l_{2}a}(\bm{k})\mathrm{Im}G^{R}_{a}(\bm{k},\varepsilon),

(10)

\displaystyle M_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}\sum\limits_{a}U_{l_{1}a}(\bm{k})U^{*}_{l_{2}a}(\bm{k})\mathrm{Im}F^{R}_{a}(\bm{k},\varepsilon),

(11)

and using Eq. (
2

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{1}}

\displaystyle\propto\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\;\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}[\varepsilon/2T]}\sum\limits_{l_{1}l_{2}}\Big[N_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\varepsilon)N_{l_{2}l_{1}}(\varepsilon)+M_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\varepsilon)M_{l_{2}l_{1}}(\varepsilon)\Big].

Since the temperature dependence of the superconducting gap

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\bm{k}a}\frac{\tanh\left[E_{a}(\bm{k})/2T\right]}{E_{a}(\bm{k})}\left|A_{a}g_{a}(\bm{k})\right|^{2}.

We note that the momentum dependency of the gap function

5
Summary

In this study, we calculate the spin-lattice relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
and the PDOS to explore the behavior of the Hebel-Slichter peak in the multi-band point-node–like
s
-wave superconductivity using the
f
-
d
-
p
model, which is an effective model of UTe
2
.
To take into account the degrees of freedom of the sites, the site-resolved calculation of
1/T_{1}
in both cases of the point-node–like and isotropic
s
-wave pairing was performed.
This point-node–like
s
-wave pairing was obtained by solving the linearized Eliashberg equation within the TOPT, and it is an unconventional
s
-wave superconductivity.
From these analyses, we discussed the relation between the gap anisotropy and the Hebel-Slichter peak in the model with the degrees of freedom of sites.
The results of our analysis revealed that the Hebel-Slichter peak is robust even in the multi-band point-node–like
s
-wave pairing state, although it is smaller than that in the isotropic
s
-wave pairing state.
In both isotropic and point-node–like
s
-wave cases, the Hebel-Slichter peak becomes smaller as increasing the damping of the quasi-particles
\gamma
.
For a sufficiently large
\gamma
, the Hebel-Slichter peak in the point-node–like
s
-wave state becomes negligibly small.
However, since the Hebel-Slichter peak in the isotropic
s
-wave pairing also becomes small to the same order of magnitude, the scenario that the DOS broadening due to the gap anisotropy prevents the Hebel-Slichter peak is unrealistic.
Therefore, although the point-node–like
s
-wave pairing state agrees with the specific heat measurements, it is inconsistent with the spin-lattice relaxation of the NMR measurements in UTe
2
.

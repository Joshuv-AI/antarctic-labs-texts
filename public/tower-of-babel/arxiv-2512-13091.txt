Quantitative strong approximation for ternary quadratic forms III
Zhizhong Huang

Abstract
. We prove asymptotic formulas for counting (primitive) integral points with local conditions on the (punctured) affine cone defined by a non-singular integral ternary quadratic form, and we relate our results to the Brauer–Manin obstruction. Our approach is based on the δ \delta -variant of the Hardy–Littlewood circle method developed by Heath-Brown.



1.
Introduction

In
[
4
]
, Heath-Brown developed his
\delta
-circle method and for the first time applied to counting integer solutions of homogeneous ternary quadratic forms. This gave a new analytic proof of the Hasse–Minkowski theorem for ternary quadratic forms which goes back to Legendre (cf.
[
9
, IV. Theorem 8 (ii)]
). We begin by recalling his results.

Let
F(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
be a non-degenerate indefinite ternary integral quadratic form. Let
w:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a fixed infinitely differentiable weight function of class
\mathscr{C}(S)
as in
[
4
, §2]
, and we define the
weighted singular integral
(with respect to the quadratic form
F
) to be

(1.1)

{\mathscr{I}}(w):=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}}w(\boldsymbol{t})\textup{e}(\theta F(\boldsymbol{t}))\operatorname{d}\boldsymbol{t}\operatorname{d}\theta.

We let

\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}:=\prod_{p<\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sigma_{p},

where for every prime
p
, we define the
p
-adic local density
as

(1.2)

\sigma_{p}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\boldsymbol{x}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})\equiv 0\bmod p^{k}\}}{p^{2k}}.

This infinite product is absolutely convergent, as shown in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Heath-Brown
[
4
]
Theorem 8, Corollary 2)
.

We have

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})=0}w\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{B}\right)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{I}}(w)\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}B\log B+\sigma_{1}(w)B+O_{\varepsilon}(B^{\frac{5}{6}+\varepsilon}),

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\operatorname{prim}}^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})=0}w\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{B}\right)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{I}}(w)\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}B+O\left(B\exp\left(-c_{0}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right).

However, it is remarked in
[
4
, p. 161]
that there can exist local obstructions for (primitive) integral solutions. Adding congruence conditions into the counting comes with some technical affection, such that the asymptotic formula may not appear as the expected form. Based on the approaches developed in
[
5
,
7
]
, the goal of the present paper is to address this issue more thoroughly, to work out explicit asymptotic formulas, and to relate them to the Brauer–Manin obstruction, in spirit of
[
6
]
.

Let

C:=(F=0)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

which is a projective conic,

W:=(F=0)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}

be the affine cone over
C
, and

W^{o}:=(F=0)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\setminus\boldsymbol{0}

be the punctured affine cone. Let
\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{W}},{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
be respectively the integral models for them also defined by
F
. Zero solutions of
F
corresponds to integral points on
{\mathscr{W}}
, whereas primitive zero solutions can be viewed as integral points on
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
, which, up to sign, can identified with rational points of
C
.

To state our results, we fix
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\in{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
.
For every prime
p
, we define the
p
-adic local density
(with respect to the congruence condition
(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
) to be

(1.3)

\sigma_{p}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\boldsymbol{x}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{3}:F(\boldsymbol{x})\equiv 0\bmod p^{k},\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\bmod p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(L)}\}}{p^{2k}},

and we define
the “modified” singular series
(with the convergence factors
\left(1-p^{-1}\right)_{p}
) to be

(1.4)

\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}:=\prod_{p<\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sigma_{p}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}).

We define the (weighted) counting functions respectively for
{\mathscr{W}}
and
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
:

(1.5)

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(w,(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma});B):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3},F(\boldsymbol{x})=0\\
\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\bmod L\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{B}\right),

(1.6)

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(w,(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma});B):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\text{prim}}^{3},F(\boldsymbol{x})=0\\
\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\bmod L\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{B}\right).

Our main counting results are as follows, which will be established in §
2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
{\mathscr{H}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(w,(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma});B)=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}{\mathscr{I}}(w)B\log B+{\mathscr{H}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)B+O_{\varepsilon}(B^{\frac{5}{6}+\varepsilon}).

We shall write

\Omega:=8L\Delta

which contains all “bad primes”.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\Omega\mid L
. There exists
{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)\in{\mathbb{C}}
depending on
w,(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
such that

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(w,(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma});B)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}{\mathscr{I}}(w)+{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)\right)B+O\left(B\exp\left(-c_{0}(\log B)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right),

for an appropriate numerical constant
c_{0}>0
. The explicit expression of
{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)
is given by (
2.20
).

Let us now compare Theorems
1.2

1.3
with Heath-Brown’s result (Theorem
1.1
).
The common feature is that, without primitivity condition, the order of growth is
B\log B
, the leading constant being the product of local densities with singular integrals. Imposing primitivity condition, via a Möbius inversion, renders
B\log B
to
B
and leaves the leading constant unchanged.
The main difference is that, the secondary term
{\mathscr{H}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)B
becomes
{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)B
, and it can happen that
{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)\neq 0
(it does equal
0
when no local condition is added), and that
\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}{\mathscr{I}}(w)+{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)=0
, provided that local obstruction exists. Although we are not quite able to compute the exact value of
{\mathscr{G}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(w)
, it is expressed as an infinite sum of various exponential sums with respect to certain non-zero Poisson variables and encode the effect of
w
and non-trivial real characters of modulus dividing
\Omega
.

Example 1.4
.

Heath-Brown
[
4
, p. 161]
addresses briefly the (counter-)example where there is an obstruction to the existence of global primitive integer solutions to the equation
x_{1}x_{2}=x_{3}^{2}
with the local conditions
x_{i}>0
and
x_{i}\equiv 2\bmod 3
for all
i=1,2,3
. Indeed, the primitivity and positivity imply that
\gcd(x_{1},x_{2})=1
, and that both
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are perfect squares Hence they all have the same congruence class
1\bmod 3
, contradiction. The obstruction comes from “reciprocity” between the real sign and the Legendre symbol modulo
3
. We shall see that this is coherent with our asymptotic formula.

In §
3
, we deduce a corresponding counting result for the projective conic
C

1.3

disappears on the one hand. On the other hand, we give an interpretation of the

-factor in Theorem
1.3
.

As a

-torsor over the conic
C
, the quasi-affine variety

is a (smooth) toric variety. That the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only one to strong approximation off infinity for such varieties has been studied in depth by work of Cao–Xu
[
3
]
. We compare our counting results in view of Brauer–Manin obstruction in §

Perspectives on the Brauer–Manin obstruction

The projective conic
C=(F=0)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
being isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(we always assume that

), the universal torsor (up to isomorphism) over

, as a
{\mathbb{G}}_{\operatorname{m}}
-torsor. The punctured affine cone

is an intermediate
{\mathbb{G}}_{\operatorname{m}}
-torsor over
C
, whose class in
H^{1}(C,{\mathbb{G}}_{\operatorname{m}})
has index two. So there exists a degree two map

\pi:{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\setminus\boldsymbol{0}\to W^{o}.

The Brauer group of
W^{o}
(modulo the constant part) is isomorphic to
H^{1}({\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{*2}
, which can be identified with the set of all field extensions of degree two over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. To parametrise all points of
W^{o}
it is necessary to twist the map
\pi
by elements in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{*2}
, and we denoted the twisted map by

\pi^{a}:({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\setminus\boldsymbol{0})^{a}\to W^{o}

for
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{*2}
.
Having fixed integral models for each, the diagram above extends to a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-map

({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{2}\setminus\boldsymbol{0})^{a}\to{\mathscr{W}}^{o}\to\mathscr{C}\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}.

Then by the Borel–Serre theorem (cf. e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 6.3]
), the number of
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{*2}
such that
\pi^{a}(({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{2}\setminus\boldsymbol{0})^{a}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}))\cap{\mathscr{D}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})\neq\varnothing
is finite (we recall
{\mathscr{D}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})\subset{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
is the closed subset prescribed by the local condition
(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
). This corresponds to the fact that for each fixed integral model, there are only finitely elements in the Brauer group which are “active”.

Let us compare this with our analytic result Theorem
1.3
. Although we are unable to compute the precise value of the constant

, it features the values at
s=1
of the
L

They should be viewed as “effects” of the Brauer–Manin obstruction on the open adelic neighbourhood regarding the real condition prescribed by
w
and the congruence condition
(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
.

Schwarzschild Spacetime and the Local Limit of Nonlocal Gravity
Yahya Mohammadi 1, Javad Tabatabaei 1, Shant Baghram 1,2, Bahram Mashhoon 1,3,4

Abstract
We investigate static spherically symmetric solutions within the framework of the local limit of nonlocal gravity. This theory departs from Einstein’s general relativity (GR) through the introduction of a scalar gravitational susceptibility function S ⁡ ( x ) S(x) , 1 + S > 0 1+S>0 , which is a new feature of the spacetime that vanishes in the GR limit. It is shown that the Schwarzschild spacetime constitutes an exact solution of the modified source-free gravitational field equations provided S S is a constant. The mass of the corresponding Schwarzschild solution is given by M / ( 1 + S ) M/(1+S) , where M M denotes the mass of the solution in the GR limit. The interpretation of the solution in terms of a black hole is ruled out due to the divergence of the algebraic invariants of the Weitzenböck torsion at the Schwarzschild horizon.

II
Local Limit of Nonlocal Gravity

To describe the local limit of the nonlocal gravity theory, we begin with the tetrad formulation of GR
BlHe
;
Maluf:2013gaa
. Consider a smooth family of observers in spacetime that carry an orthonormal tetrad frame field
\lambda^{\mu}{}_{\hat{\alpha}}(x)
. Here,
x^{\alpha}=(t,x^{i})
,
i=1,2,3
, is an admissible system of coordinates that cover the spacetime under consideration here. Moreover,
\lambda^{\mu}{}_{\hat{0}}(x)
is a unit timelike direction that is the four-velocity vector of an observer in the congruence, while the triad
\lambda^{\mu}{}_{\hat{i}}(x)

i=1,2,3

g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\lambda_{\mu}{}^{\hat{\alpha}}(x)\,\lambda_{\nu}{}^{\hat{\beta}}(x)\,\eta_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\,.

(15)

We use the tetrad field in order to define a new connection. Indeed,

\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}:=\lambda^{\mu}{}_{\hat{\rho}}~\partial_{\alpha}\,\lambda_{\beta}{}^{\hat{\rho}}\,,

(16)

defines the Weitzenböck connection
We
upon which the scheme of teleparallelism is based. Associated with the Weitzenböck connection is the covariant derivative operator
\nabla
which satisfies
\nabla_{\nu}\,\lambda_{\mu}{}^{\hat{\alpha}}=0
. The frame field is covariantly constant; furthermore, the Weitzenböck connection is curvature free. This means that the spacetime is a parallelizable manifold and the tetrad frames are everywhere parallel. This is the framework of teleparallelism; that is, two faraway vectors are thus considered parallel if their local tetrad components are the same. The Weitzenböck connection is metric-compatible, namely,
\nabla_{\mu}\,g_{\alpha\beta}=0
, which follows from applying
\nabla
to Eq. (
15
).

In our extended GR scheme, we have two metric-compatible connections, namely, the Weitzenböck connection
\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
and the symmetric Levi-Civia connection
{}^{0}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
,

^{0}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\,(g_{\nu\alpha,\beta}+g_{\nu\beta,\alpha}-g_{\alpha\beta,\nu})\,.

(17)

In this modified framework, free test particles and null rays follow the geodesic equation,

\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{d\theta^{2}}+\,^{0}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\theta}\,\frac{dx^{\beta}}{d\theta}=0\,,

(18)

where
\theta

To proceed further, we define the

C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}=\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}=\lambda^{\rho}{}_{\hat{\beta}}\left(\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu}{}^{\hat{\beta}}-\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{\mu}{}^{\hat{\beta}}\right)\,,

(19)

K_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho}=\,^{0}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}\,.

II.1
General Relativity and Teleparallelism

II.2
NLG and its Local Limit

In dealing with the electrodynamics of different media, the constitutive relation changes with the medium while Maxwell’s equations of electromagnetism remain the same. The electromagnetic properties of a medium are encoded in the constitutive relation that is in general nonlocal. In contemplating a nonlocal extension of TEGR, we can draw inspiration from the nonlocal electrodynamics of media

e^{\mu}{}_{\hat{0}}=A(r)\delta^{\mu}_{0}\,,\qquad e^{\mu}{}_{\hat{i}}=B(r)\delta^{\mu}_{i}\,,

(45)

e_{\mu}{}^{\hat{0}}=\frac{1}{A}\,\delta^{0}_{\mu}\,,\qquad e_{\mu}{}^{\hat{i}}=\frac{1}{B}\,\delta^{i}_{\mu}\,.

(46)

Metric (
5
) in matrix form can be expressed as

(g_{\mu\nu})={\rm diag}(-A^{-2},B^{-2},B^{-2},B^{-2})\,,\qquad(g^{\mu\nu})={\rm diag}(-A^{2},B^{2},B^{2},B^{2})\,.

(47)

In this case, the nonzero components of the Weitzenböck connection are given by

\Gamma^{0}_{i0}=A\,\partial_{i}\frac{1}{A}=-\partial_{i}\ln A\,,\qquad\Gamma^{j}_{ik}=B\,\partial_{i}\frac{1}{B}\delta^{j}_{k}=-\partial_{i}\ln B\,\delta^{j}_{k}\,.

(48)

Let us note that
\partial_{i}F(r)=(F^{\prime}/r)x^{i}
.

The nonzero components of the torsion tensor are then given by

C_{0i}{}^{0}=-C_{i0}{}^{0}=\partial_{i}\ln A\,,\qquad C_{jk}{}^{i}=-\partial_{j}\ln B\,\delta^{i}_{k}+\partial_{k}\ln B\,\delta^{i}_{j}\,.

(49)

Hence, the only nonzero components of
C_{\mu\nu\rho}
are

C_{0i0}=-C_{i00}=-\frac{1}{A^{2}}\,\partial_{i}\ln A\,,\qquad C_{jki}=\frac{1}{B^{2}}\,\left(-\partial_{j}\ln B\,\delta_{ik}+\partial_{k}\ln B\,\delta_{ij}\right)\,.

(50)

The torsion vector
C_{\mu}=C^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\alpha}
has components

C_{0}=0\,,\qquad C_{i}=\partial_{i}\ln(AB^{2})\,,

(51)

while the torsion pseudovector vanishes, i.e.
\check{C}_{\mu}=0
.

The nonzero components of the contorsion tensor (
21
) are given by

K_{0i0}=-K_{00i}=\frac{1}{A^{2}}\,\partial_{i}\ln A\,,\qquad K_{ijk}=\frac{1}{B^{2}}\,\left(\partial_{k}\ln B\,\delta_{ij}-\partial_{j}\ln B\,\delta_{ik}\right)\,.

(52)

The nonzero components of the auxiliary torsion tensor (
24

\mathfrak{C}_{0i0}=-\mathfrak{C}_{i00}=\frac{2}{A^{2}}\,\partial_{i}\ln B\,,\qquad\mathfrak{C}_{jki}=\frac{1}{B^{2}}\,\left[\partial_{j}\ln(AB)\,\delta_{ik}-\partial_{k}\ln(AB)\,\delta_{ij}\right]\,,

(53)

\mathfrak{C}^{0i0}=-\mathfrak{C}^{i00}=2A^{2}\,B^{2}\,\partial_{i}\ln B\,,\qquad\mathfrak{C}^{jki}=B^{4}\,\left[\partial_{j}\ln(AB)\,\delta_{ik}-\partial_{k}\ln(AB)\,\delta_{ij}\right]\,.

(54)

We can now compute
Q_{\mu\nu}
, where

Q_{\mu\nu}:=S(r)\,\left(C_{\mu\rho\sigma}\mathfrak{C}_{\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,C_{\delta\rho\sigma}\mathfrak{C}^{\delta\rho\sigma}\right)\,.

(56)

where, in our convention, repeated spatial indices, up or down, are summed over. After some algebra, we find the nonzero components of the traceless tensor
Q_{\mu\nu}

{Q}_{00}=-S\,\frac{B^{2}}{A^{2}}\,\partial_{i}\ln B\,\partial_{i}\ln B\,,

(57)

{Q}_{ij}=-S\,[\partial_{i}\ln B\,\partial_{j}\ln(AB)+2\,\partial_{i}\ln A\,\partial_{j}\ln B-\partial_{k}\ln A\,\partial_{k}\ln B\,\delta_{ij}]\,.

Next, we turn to the calculation of

\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu}:=g_{\nu\alpha}e_{\mu}{}^{\hat{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\beta}}\,(\sqrt{-g}N^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\hat{\gamma}})\,,

(59)

where

N^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\hat{\gamma}}=S(r)g^{\alpha\mu}\,g^{\beta\nu}\mathfrak{C}_{\mu\nu\rho}\,e^{\rho}{}_{\hat{\gamma}}\,.

(60)

Let us recall that
S(x)
is a characteristic of the Schwarzschild spacetime whose metric only depends on the radial coordinate
r
; therefore,
S
can only depend upon
r

After some algebra, we finally find that the only nonzero elements of
\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu}
are given by

\mathcal{N}_{00}=2\,\frac{B^{3}}{A^{2}}\,\partial_{i}\left(\frac{S}{B}\,\partial_{i}\ln B\right)\,,

(61)

\mathcal{N}_{ij}=A\,\partial_{i}\left[\frac{S}{A}\,\partial_{j}\ln(AB)\right]-A\,\partial_{k}\left[\frac{S}{A}\,\partial_{k}\ln(AB)\right]\,\delta_{ij}\,.

(62)

We must now substitute these results in the modified gravitational field equation.

IV
Modified Gravitational Field Equation

The field equation of the local limit of nonlocal gravity for metric (

^{0}G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}+\mathbb{M}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(63)

where

In the static situation under consideration here, we imagine the source is given by the energy-momentum tensor of a comoving perfect fluid, namely,

T_{\mu\nu}=(\varrho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p\,g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(64)

where
\varrho(r)
is the energy density,
p(r)
is the pressure and

(65)

(T_{\mu\nu})={\rm diag}\left(\varrho\,A^{-2},p\,B^{-2},p\,B^{-2},p\,B^{-2}\right)\,.

(66)

From the results of the previous section, we find the nonzero components of
\mathbb{M}_{\mu\nu}
, namely,

\mathbb{M}_{00}=Q_{00}-\mathcal{N}_{00}=-\frac{1}{A^{2}}(S\,\mathbb{T}+2\,BB^{\prime}S^{\prime})\,,

(67)

\mathbb{M}_{ij}=Q_{ij}-\mathcal{N}_{ij}=\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{x^{i}\,x^{j}}{r^{2}}\right)\,\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\,S^{\prime}-\left[\frac{x^{i}\,x^{j}}{r^{2}}\,(\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{S})-\mathbb{S}\,\delta_{ij}\right]\,S\,,

(68)

where
\mathbb{T}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{S}
are given by Eqs. (
8
)–(
10

6
) and (
7

\frac{1}{A^{2}}\,\mathbb{T}=\frac{\kappa\,\varrho+\Lambda}{A^{2}}-\frac{1}{A^{2}}(S\,\mathbb{T}+2\,BB^{\prime}S^{\prime})\,,

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{x^{i}\,x^{j}}{r^{2}}\,(\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{S})-\mathbb{S}\,\delta_{ij}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\,p-\Lambda}{B^{2}}\,\delta_{ij}+S^{\prime}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{x^{i}\,x^{j}}{r^{2}}\right)\,\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)

\displaystyle-S\left[\frac{x^{i}\,x^{j}}{r^{2}}\,(\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{S})-\mathbb{S}\,\delta_{ij}\right]\,.

(70)

These equations are satisfied everywhere in space provided

(1+S)\,\mathbb{T}+2\,BB^{\prime}S^{\prime}=\kappa\,\varrho+\Lambda\,,

(71)

(1+S)\,(\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{S})+\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\,S^{\prime}=0\,,

(72)

(1+S)\,\mathbb{S}+\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\,S^{\prime}=-\frac{\kappa\,p-\Lambda}{B^{2}}\,.

(73)

V
Modified Schwarzschild Solution in Isotropic Coordinates

Let us now set
\varrho=p=\Lambda=0
and find the solution of Eqs. (

73
) in this case. We have a simple result using Eq. (
71

(1+S)\,\left(2BB^{\prime\prime}-3B^{\prime 2}+\frac{4}{r}BB^{\prime}\right)+2\,BB^{\prime}S^{\prime}=0\,.

(74)

Next, subtracting Eq. (
73

\mathbb{R}=\left(\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}+2\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}\,\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}-\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)=0\,,

(75)

which can be expressed as

\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)=0\,,

(76)

Finally, using Eq. (

\mathbb{S}

(1+S)\left[\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\right]+\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\,S^{\prime}=0\,,

(77)

which can be combined with Eq. (

\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{S^{\prime}}{1+S}\right)=0\,.

(78)

Let us note that for
S=0
, field Eqs. (

11
), which expresses the Ricci-flat condition of GR and uniquely results in the Schwarzschild solution. In the presence of the susceptibility function

It is reasonable to assume that the spacetime under consideration must approach the Minkowski spacetime for

; therefore, we assume
A(\infty)=B(\infty)=1
. Dividing Eq. (

B=\frac{1}{(1+W)^{2}}\,,\qquad W(r):=-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\infty}^{r}\frac{du}{u^{2}[1+S(u)]}\,,

where

is a constant. Next, from Eq. (

\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\left(\frac{2B-rB^{\prime}}{rB^{\prime}-B}\right)\,,

(80)

which, upon using Eq. (

\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}=\left(\frac{2W^{\prime}}{W+1}\right)\,\frac{rW^{\prime}+W+1}{2rW^{\prime}+W+1}\,.

(81)

Let us now consider

\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}=-\frac{2W^{\prime}}{W+1}\,

(82)

and define

\mathbb{U}:=\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}=-\frac{2rW^{\prime 2}}{(W+1)(2rW^{\prime}+W+1)}\,.

(83)

Note that Eq. (

\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\frac{1}{\mathbb{U}}\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{S^{\prime}}{1+S}\right)+1=0\,.

(84)

Substituting
\mathbb{U}
from Eq. (
83

\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{1}{\mathbb{U}}\right)-\frac{1}{\mathbb{U}}\left(\frac{3}{r}+\frac{S^{\prime}}{1+S}\right)+1=-\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}r^{3}(W+1)^{2}(S+1)S^{\prime}\,,

Hence, we immediately find from Eqs. (

The resulting metric is thus

(91)

VI
Weitzenböck Invariants

There are three independent algebraic torsion invariants given by
BMB

I_{1}=C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,C^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,,\qquad I_{2}=C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,C^{\gamma\beta\alpha}\,,\qquad I_{3}=C_{\alpha}\,C^{\alpha}\,.

(92)

These can be calculated for metric (
5
) via the nonzero components of the torsion tensor given in Section III. The results are

I_{1}=2\,I_{2}=2B^{2}\left[\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}\right]\,,\qquad I_{3}=B^{2}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}+2\,\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}\,.

(93)

Using the explicit expressions for
A(r)
and
B(r)
in Eq. (
87
), we find

I_{1}=2\,I_{2}=\mu_{S}^{2}\,\frac{r^{2}(6r^{2}-4\,\mu_{S}\,r+\mu_{S}^{2})}{(r+\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2})^{6}(r-\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2})^{2}}\,

(94)

and

I_{3}=\mu_{S}^{2}\,\frac{r^{2}(r-\mu_{S})^{2}}{(r+\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2})^{6}(r-\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2})^{2}}\,.

(95)

For
r>\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2}
, these scalar invariants are positive and vanish as
r^{-4}
for
r\to\infty
; on the other hand, they all diverge as
(r-\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2})^{-2}
on the horizon of the Schwarzschild spacetime. The horizon is thus singular and the interior region
r<\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2}
is not physically accessible. Therefore, only the exterior Schwarzschild spacetime for
r>\tfrac{\mu_{S}}{2}
is physically significant.

To illustrate simple applications of the local limit of nonlocal gravity theory, two modified interior solutions of the exterior Schwarzschild solution of mass
M/(1+S)
are considered in the appendices.

VII
DISCUSSION

The Schwarzschild spacetime is a vacuum solution of the gravitational field equation of the local limit of nonlocal gravity; however, the three Weitzenböck torsion invariants diverge on the event horizon. This circumstance renders the event horizon a closed null hypersurface that is a naked singularity. Similar problems occur for black hole candidates within the framework of teleparallel gravity theories
Obukhov:2002tm
;
Obukhov:2019fti
;
Golovnev:2023uqb
;
Coley:2025cdj
;
Lopez:2025gvj
. An analogous situation is encountered in GR when the Schwarzschild black hole is slightly deformed by the addition of a small quadrupole moment. In this case, the event horizon becomes a naked singularity in agreement with the black hole uniqueness theorems. A singular null hypersurface is still a one-way membrane and the requirement of causality can be satisfied in the exterior region. Indeed, quasinormal modes (QNMs) of such deformed collapsed configurations have been calculated in
Allahyari:2018cmg
;
Allahyari:2019umx
. In principle, one can use observational data regarding QNMs to determine the additional quadrupole moment. In the local limit of nonlocal gravity, the QNMs of the corresponding Schwarzschild configuration will depend on the constant gravitational susceptibility
S
through the mass parameter
M/(1+S)
, which may have implications for the problem of the black hole mass distribution
Kovetz:2016kpi
.

121
), we find

\left(1-\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{G}\right)^{4}F_{T}^{2}\,F_{R}^{2}=a^{2}(T)\,\left(1-\frac{R_{b}^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(\dot{a}^{2}R_{b}^{2}+1-\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{aR_{b}}\right)\,\left(\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{aR_{b}}-\frac{R_{b}^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}}\right)\,.

(124)

On the other hand, Eq. (
122
) implies

\left(1-\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{G}\right)^{4}F_{T}^{2}\,F_{R}^{2}=\dot{a}^{2}\,a^{2}(T)\,R_{b}^{2}\,.

(125)

From equating these results, we find

\dot{a}^{2}+\frac{1}{R_{0}^{2}}=\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{a(T)R_{b}^{3}}\,.

(126)

Comparing this result with Eqs. (
112
) and (
117
), we conclude

\frac{1}{R_{0}^{2}}=\frac{2\mathbb{M}}{R_{b}^{3}}\,,\qquad m(T,R_{b})=\mathbb{M}\,.

(127)

That is, the exterior and interior masses must be the same.

Finally, it is interesting to note that in
Tabatabaei:2022tbq
, it was shown that the closed FLRW model is a solution of the local limit of nonlocal gravity provided
S
is a constant. Then, the density and pressure are simply modified by a factor of
1+S
,
namely,
\varrho\to\varrho/(1+S)
and
p\to p/(1+S)
. We then conclude that the Oppenheimer-Snyder model goes over to the local limit of NLG when we simply modify masses by dividing them by
1+S
. However, the solution is valid only to the extent that
r>2\mathbb{M}
, or
G=a(T)R>2\,\mathbb{M}
.

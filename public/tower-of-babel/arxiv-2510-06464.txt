Tunable magnon-phonon cavity via structural phase transition Preprint: APS/123-QED
Chunli Tang, Yujie Zhu, Dayne Sasaki, Jiaxuan Wu, Harshil Goyal, Yuzan Xiong, Masoud Mahjouri-Samani, Xiang Meng, Jia-Mian Hu, Yayoi Takamura, Wei Zhang, Wencan Jin

Abstract
Strong coupling between two quantized excitations in a cavity has the potential to lead to hybridized states that bestow novel quantum phenomena as required for emerging applications. In particular, tunable hybrid magnon-phonon cavities with precise control knobs are in pressing demand for developing quantum functionalities in solid-state platforms. Here, using a combination of synthesis and characterization tools, we present an epitaxial La 0.7 ​ Sr 0.3 ​ MnO 3 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.7}}}\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.3}}}\text{MnO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} / SrTiO 3 \text{SrTiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} (LSMO/STO) heterostructure that manifests strong couplings between the Kittel magnon and the transverse acoustic phonon. Remarkably, leveraging the magnetoelastic interaction at the epitaxial interface, we demonstrate that when the STO substrate undergoes a cubic-to-tetragonal phase transition at T S ∼ T_{\mathrm{S}}\sim 105 K, the Kittel magnon of the LSMO thin film splits into three bands due to anisotropic structural strains along the [100], [010], and [001] crystalline axes, hence, resulting in an array of non-degenerate, hybridized magnon-phonon modes. Moreover, we develop an analytical model that can reproduce the interfacial strain-induced magnon splitting and the strength of magnon-phonon coupling. Our work highlights structural phase transitions as a sensitive trigger for generating multistate magnon-phonon hybridization in high-quality magnetoelastic oxide heterostructures – a new route for implementing strain-mediated hybrid magnonics in phononic systems with potential applications in coherent energy and signal transduction.



Hybridized excitation of spin waves (magnons) and elastic waves (phonons) has provided fertile ground for interdisciplinary studies, ranging from fundamental physics to applications of quantum technologies, thanks to their small footprints and high tunabilities enabled by magnetoelastic coupling
Li et al. 2020
;
Bozhko et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2021
. A prominent example is the formation of hybridized magnon-phonon modes, or magnon-polarons, which give rise to intriguing thermodynamic and spin transport properties of various materials
Kikkawa et al. 2016
;
Man et al. 2017
;
Ramos et al. 2019
;
Yahiro et al. 2020
. Meanwhile, magnetoelastic coupling allows for coherent manipulation of magnetic dynamics via external mechanical stimuli such as surface acoustic waves
Weiler et al. 2011
;
Weiler et al. 2012
and strain pulses generated by a femtosecond laser
Scherbakov et al. 2010
;
Kim et al. 2012
, which sparks great interest in strain-mediated spintronics and high-frequency magnonic devices
Hu 2024
;
Zhuang et al. 2024
.

Currently, most of the aforementioned magnetoelastic phenomena are realized in multilayer systems typically consisting of metallic magnetic thin films
Berk et al. 2019
;
Kusminskiy 2024
;
Opel et al. 2024
, and insulating yttrium iron garnet (YIG) on gadolinium gallium garnet (GGG)

;
Polzikova et al. 2016
. The corresponding theoretical models are also based on magnet-nonmagnet bilayers
Flebus et al. 2017
;
Streib et al. 2019
;
Sato et al. 2021
. Among them, despite the reputable magnonic characteristics, such as the high spin density and ultralow magnetic damping of YIG

;
Manenti et al. 2017
;
Moores et al. 2018
;
Hatanaka et al. 2022

;
Qin et al. 2018

Xu et al. 2023
;
Xiong et al. 2024

Shen et al. 2025

;
An et al. 2022

High-quality oxide heterostructures have enabled a variety of functional properties that can be engineered through their extreme sensitivity to interface effects
Zubko et al. 2011
;
Hwang et al. 2012
, yet their implementation for tunable magnon-phonon cavities has remained elusive. In this work, we present an epitaxial
\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.7}}}\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.3}}}\text{MnO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{SrTiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(LSMO/STO) heterostructure that manifests a unique testbed for interfacial strain-driven multimode magnon-phonon hybridization. LSMO is a half-metallic ferromagnetic oxide with large magnetoelastic coupling
Pesquera et al. 2011
and low magnon damping rate
Gupta et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2021
. It can be epitaxially grown on an STO substrate, a quantum paraelectric perovskite that undergoes a cubic-to-tetragonal phase transition, see schematic in Fig.
1

105 K

;

<
105 K, the phase transition of the STO substrate produces three types of structural domains

, and the magnetoelastic coupling across the interface causes the Kittel magnon mode to split into three bands due to the anisotropic strains locked to the [100], [010], and [001] crystalline axes. While each band couples to the TA phonons separately with distinct coupling characteristics, they all reside in the strong coupling regime. Moreover, the separation of the split magnon bands can be precisely tuned by the interfacial strain. We further develop an analytical model to demonstrate that the anisotropic strain modifies the magnetocrystalline anisotropy and thus lifts the degeneracy of the Kittel magnon dispersion. Our work showcases high-quality magnetoelastic oxide interfaces as a superior platform for creating and controlling strain-driven multimode magnon-phonon hybridization, and uncovers the potential of using coherent phenomena between magnons and phonons for quantum signal transductions at a broadband of frequencies.

When the sample is cooled down to

Such splitting of the magnon band can be attributed to the structural phase transition of STO, in which the tetragonality breaks the isotropy of the crystalline axis of the cubic structure – the three bands are associated with the elongated axis along [001], [100], and [010] directions, respectively. The specific assignment of the split magnon bands is rigorously validated using our analytical model, which will be discussed later.

In addition, more phonon modes are present as a result of the reduced dissipation rate of phonons at low temperature. The periodic spacing of the phonon frequencies indicates that they are standing wave modes. Remarkably, all of them hybridize with the three magnon bands, forming an array of anticrossing gaps in the

In summary, we have demonstrated that LSMO/STO magnetoelastic heterostructures exhibit strong magnon-phonon coupling. When the STO substrate undergoes a transition into the tetragonal phase for
T<
105 K, the Kittel magnon in LSMO splits into three bands due to the interfacial strain, forming an array of hybrid magnon-phonon modes. This heterostructure can be considered as a hybrid cavity system and has far-reaching implications beyond the prototypical hybrid systems such as YIG/GGG. First, the hybrid magnon-phonon modes strongly depend on magnetoelastic coupling near the interface. It allows precise strain engineering by growing LSMO thin films on various lattice-mismatched substrates, or freestanding STO nanomembrane
Xu et al. 2020
;
Zhu et al. 2025
. Second, our analytical model can capture the key physical principles of the enhanced gap size of the anticrossings very well, although a micro/mesoscale model that can implement the complex structural domain pattern in STO
Li et al. 2006
will facilitate future design of hybrid systems. Finally, temperature is employed to trigger the structural phase transition in this work, while we envision additional tuning knobs, such as an external electric field or mechanical stress, can be applied by integrating the LSMO film into a piezoelectric underlayer
Pesquera et al. 2020
;
Hong et al. 2020
, which opens up new opportunities for manipulating hybrid magnon-phonon cavity systems.

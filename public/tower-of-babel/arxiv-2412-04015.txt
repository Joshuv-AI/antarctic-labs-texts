Linear fluctuation of interfaces in Glauber-Kawasaki dynamics
Tadahisa Funaki, Claudio Landim and Sunder Sethuraman

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Consider reaction
+
diffusion interacting particle systems on

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, such as ‘Glauber
+
Kawasaki’ models. Informally, particles
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, with edge scaling of
1/N
, exchange neighbor places with
rate
N^{2}
(the ‘Kawasaki’ part), and are born or expire with rates
depending on local configurations of order
K=K_{N}
(the ‘Glauber’
part). When
K_{N}\uparrow\infty
, such models have been used to study
the phenomenon of phase segregation
[
6
]
.

For instance, when the Glauber rates correspond to the gradient of a
‘double well’ potential, the continuum space-time hydrodynamic limit
of the mass describes mean-curvature of an interface between two
regions of different constant densities (the argmins of the double
well potential)
[
4
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
15
]
. Related
hydrodynamic limits have been understood also in more general reaction

+
diffusion systems
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
. In this
context, the aim of this article is to derive the associated continuum
fluctuations, certain SPDEs, when the particle system is started from
a certain ‘flat’ nonequilibrium state.

From a broader perspective, the emergence of phase separating
interface continuum flows is a concern of long standing interest; see

[
6
]
,
[
28
]
in the statistical physics literature, and

[
2
]
and references therein for results in the PDE
literature. There are also works which investigate the ‘mesoscopic’
fluctuations of the interface through SPDE scaling limits; see

[
13
]
and references therein. However, less is known or
conjectured about the continuum mass fluctuations, with respect to the
hydrodynamics, as a limit from microscopic systems.

This paper may be seen as a companion to
[
14
]
, which considers
the ‘mesoscopic’ scaling limit of an SPDE (equation (2.3) in

[
14
]
), written as a formal expansion of the stochastic
differential for the mass empirical measure in a Glauber + Kawasaki
particle system. Our purpose is to derive rigorously the linear form
of this SPDE directly from Glauber + Kawasaki microscopic
interactions, supplying the homogenizations required, starting from a
certain ‘stationary’ initial condition on the discrete torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}=({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
for
d\leq 2
. This SPDE limit
captures microscopic-to-macroscopic fluctuations of the mass near and
away from the associated continuum interface in a certain regime.

To describe the results, let us recall the intuition for the
mean-curvature limit
[
15
,
12
]
say; see also the ‘Motion by mean
curvature’ discussion below. Denote by

\eta(t)\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}
the state of the process at time

t
, so that
\eta_{x}(t)=1
if site
x\in{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
is occupied at time

t
and
\eta_{x}(t)=0
if it is empty. By looking at stochastic
differentials, one expects that the random configuration

\eta(t)=\{\eta_{x}(t)\}
of particles on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
is close to
deterministic
u^{N}(t)=\{u^{N}_{x}(t)\}
values where

\partial_{t}u^{N}_{x}(t)=\Delta^{N}u^{N}_{x}(t)-K_{N}V^{\prime}(u^{N}_{x}(t))
is a
discretized form of the hydrodynamic equation (
2.4
)
with
K=K_{N}
. Starting from a distribution
\mu^{N}(0)
which identifies
an initial continuum interface
\Gamma_{0}
between two phases, the
relative entropy
H(\mu^{N}(t);\nu^{N}_{t})
between the distribution

\mu^{N}(t)
of
\eta(t)
and a product measure
\nu^{N}_{t}
whose
marginals have means
u^{N}(t)
is bounded as
O(N^{d-\epsilon})
. As a
consequence, the empirical measure
\pi^{N}_{t}
of the particles is close
to that of the values
u^{N}(t)
. Then, PDE estimates are used to show
that the deterministic values
u^{N}(t)
converge to the mean-curvature
limit (
2
). Here
K_{N}\uparrow\infty
and its
divergence is restricted to
K_{N}\leq\delta\log(N)
to show a form
of homogenization or ‘Boltzmann-Gibbs principle’, needed to deduce the
relative entropy bound.

In the setup to analyze fluctuations, we will start from initial
conditions close to a stationary profile in one direction and constant
in others. To describe them consider
u_{0}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
where

u_{0}(v_{1},v^{\prime})=u_{0}(v_{1},v^{\prime\prime})
for
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
and

\partial^{2}_{v_{1}}u_{0}(v)-K_{N}V^{\prime}(u_{0}(v))=0
. The Glauber rates will
be chosen so that its homogenization
V^{\prime}
has exactly three zeroes,

\rho_{-}<\rho_{*}<\rho_{+}
. We will take
u_{0}
as the (unique) solution
passing through
\rho_{*}
exactly twice,
u_{0}(0)=\rho_{*}
,

\partial_{v_{1}}u_{0}(0)<0
, and whose extreme values are bounded by and
close to
\rho_{\pm}
. Then, the level set of the profile
u_{0}
on the
continuum torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with respect to
\rho_{*}
corresponds to an
interface with two ‘flat’ components which remain fixed under
mean-curvature flow. In this formulation, there are boundary regions
of width
O(1/\sqrt{K_{N}})
about the interface where
u_{0}
transitions
from values close to
\rho_{\pm}
to those close to
\rho_{\mp}
.

To capture the behavior near the interface, we form

\rho^{K}\colon\sqrt{K}{\mathbb{T}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
where

\rho^{K}(\vartheta)=u_{0}(\vartheta/\sqrt{K},\cdot,\ldots,\cdot)
.
Note that
\rho^{K}
has period
\sqrt{K}
and

\partial^{2}_{\vartheta}\rho^{K}-V^{\prime}(\rho^{K})=0
. Also,
\rho^{K}

converges to the decreasing ‘standing wave’
\phi
on finite intervals
of
{\mathbb{R}}
, which solves
\partial^{2}_{\vartheta}\phi-V^{\prime}(\phi)=0
with

\phi(\pm\infty)=\rho_{\mp}
and
\phi(0)=\rho_{*}
. Now, we will set
initially
E[\eta_{x}(0)]={\bf u}^{N}(x)
where

{\bf u}^{N}(x)=\rho^{K}(x_{1}\sqrt{K}/N)
for
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
. In this way,
at a macroscopic point
a=x/N
for
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
, we have

{\bf u}^{N}(x)=\rho^{K}(x_{1}\sqrt{K}/N)=\rho^{K}(a_{1}\sqrt{K})\rightarrow\rho_{+}{\bf 1}(a_{1}<0)+\rho_{-}{\bf 1}(a_{1}>0)
, as

K=K_{N}\uparrow\infty
, consistent with the hydrodynamics picture given
above.

We will focus on the fluctuations of the mass in one of the ‘layers’,
that is on the part of the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
corresponding to the region
where
{\bf u}^{N}
transitions from values near
\rho_{-}
to
\rho_{+}
;
the analysis of the other ‘layer’ is similar. Let

Ax=(x_{1}\sqrt{K}/N,x_{2}/N,\ldots,x_{d}/N)
for
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
.
Typically, the fluctuation field

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}(\eta_{x}(t)-{\bf u}^{N}(x))\delta_{Ax}
should be
scaled by
N^{d/2}
to obtain nontrivial limits. However, the
martingale, introduced by the Glauber rates, in the stochastic
differential of such a scaled field is of order
O(\sqrt{K})
, whereas
the one from the Kawasaki rates is of order
O(1)
. Hence, we will
consider the field divided by
N^{d/2}K^{1/4}
denoted as
X^{N}_{t}
; see
(
2.24
).

To be able to analyze the limit of
X^{N}_{t}
, we (1) obtain a suitable
estimate of the relative entropy between the process distribution

\mu^{N}(t)
at time
t
with
\nu^{N}_{0}
, now a product measure with
means
\{{\bf u}^{N}(x):x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\}
, then (2) make use of this bound
to perform a homogenization or Boltzmann-Gibbs principle of nonlinear
microscopic rates (see the next paragraph), and finally (3) identify
limits of the scaled fluctuation field via its stochastic
differential. We observe that
\nu^{N}_{0}
is not the stationary
distribution of the Glauber + Kawasaki dynamics
\eta(t)
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
,
and so in this way, we will identify here a nonequilibrium fluctuation
limit. The unique stationary distribution is called the steady state
and its concrete form is unknown.

To this end, in Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
, we show

H(\mu^{N}_{t};\nu^{N}_{0})=O(N^{\alpha_{d}}e^{CtK_{N}^{2}})
where

\alpha_{1}=1/5
,
\alpha_{2}=4/3
and
\alpha_{d}=d-(4/3)
in

d\geq 3
, when
K_{N}\uparrow\infty
and
K_{N}=O(\ell_{N}^{d/2})
with

\ell_{N}=N^{4/5}
in
d=1
,
\ell_{N}\log\ell_{N}=N^{2}
in
d=2
, and

\ell_{N}=N^{4/(3d)}
in
d\geq 3
. These estimates allow homogenization
of the microscopic rates in the stochastic differential of
X^{N}_{t}
in
dimensions
d=1,2
, but not in
d\geq 3
. Moreover, in the
homogenization the divergence of
K_{N}
is further limited to

K_{N}\leq\delta\sqrt{\log(N)}
, see (
2.23
), for a small constant
\delta>0
,
instead of
K_{N}\leq\delta\log(N)
. Finally, we identify the limit
of
X^{N}_{t}
in Theorem
2.4
as a Gaussian field
X_{t}
acting on
compactly supported smooth functions

F:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
where

X_{t}(F)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\sqrt{\varpi}\langle F,{\boldsymbol{e}}\rangle B_{t}&\ {\rm in\ }d=1\\
\langle F,{\boldsymbol{e}}(\vartheta)Z_{t}(\theta)\rangle&\ {\rm in\ }d=2.\end{array}\right.

Here,

\partial_{t}Z_{t}=\partial^{2}_{\theta}Z_{t}+\sqrt{\varpi}\xi(t,\theta)

with
Z_{0}=0
and
\xi
is a space-time white noise on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{T}}
.
Also,
\sqrt{\varpi}
is a certain constant (below (
2.30
)) and

{\boldsymbol{e}}=\phi^{\prime}/\|\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
. In these evaluations,
X_{t}
is a
random function in
d\leq 2
, whereas any derivations of
X_{t}
in

d\geq 3
would be as a generalized distribution (see equations
(4.5)–(4.7) in
[
14
]
).

Since
E[\eta_{x}(t)]\sim\phi(x_{1}\sqrt{K}/N)\sim\rho_{\pm}
depending
on whether
x_{1}<0
or
x_{1}>0
, the field
X^{N}_{t}
may be seen as the
second order correction, in a scaled window of the interface from the
hydrodynamics, limiting to
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}(\vartheta)\sqrt{\varpi}B_{t}
in

d=1
and
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}(\vartheta)Z_{t}(\theta)
in
d=2
. In both limits,
as
|{{\boldsymbol{e}}}(\vartheta)|
diminishes for
|\vartheta|
large, the
fluctuation has largest amplitude near the interface

\Gamma_{t}=\{(\vartheta,\theta):\vartheta=0\}
as, far from the
interface, the values
\rho_{\pm}
are more stable. It is interesting
that the fluctuations involve the ‘shape’ of
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}
, indicating an
interesting ‘rigidity’. The appearance of
e(\cdot)
in the limit
field can be interpreted as an indication of the fluctuation of the
interfaces retaining the shape of the transition layer
\phi
; see
Section 4.2 of
[
14
]
. Such rigidity might be understood in terms
of ‘metastability’: In
[
5
]
, it is argued that macroscopic times
of order
e^{O(\sqrt{K})}
are needed to move the interface in
dimension
d=1
. Since
t\in[0,T]
for
T>0
, such times are not
reached in the fluctuation limit in Theorem
2.4
, which fixes the
initial interface for all
t\geq 0
.

As in other works, the proof of the relative entropy estimate follows
by analyzing its derivative. Here, we follow the
general scheme of
[
20
,
18
]
. In
[
18
]
, a nonequilibrium
fluctuation limit for types of voter models is shown. However, unlike
in
[
18
]
, intrinsically due to variation across the interface,

\nu_{0}^{N}
is an inhomogeneous product measure which introduces several
differences. The inhomogeneity also affects the proof of tightness of

X^{N}_{t}
. Indeed, we show only tightness for
\int_{0}^{t}X^{N}_{s}ds
instead
of
X^{N}_{t}
in Theorem
2.5
.

To identify the limit points and prove tightness we employ an analysis
of a Sturm-Liouville problem from which the ‘shape’
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}
emerges
in the results. For
(t,v)\in[0,T]\times\sqrt{K}{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
,
and
v=(\vartheta,\theta)
, we will be able to approximate the
stochastic differential of
X^{N}_{t}
, via the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle,
as

\partial_{t}X^{N}_{t}(t,v)\approx K\,\big\{\,\partial_{\vartheta}^{2}X^{N}_{t}(t,v)-V^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{K}(\vartheta))\,X^{N}_{t}(t,v)\,\big\}+\Delta_{d-1}X^{N}_{t}(t,v)+dM^{N}_{t}

where

\Delta_{d-1}=\sum_{j=2}^{d}\partial^{2}_{\theta_{j}}
. One may consider
the mild form: By applying the field
X^{N}_{t}
to the time-dependent
function
T^{N}_{t-s}G=e^{(t-s)K\mathcal{A}_{K}}e^{(t-s)\Delta_{d-1}}G
,
we will show that
X^{N}_{t}(G)
is well approximated by
the value at time
t
of a martingale
M^{N}_{s}(T^{N}_{t-s}G)

(cf. Corollary
7.5
). Here,

\mathcal{A}_{K}=\partial^{2}_{\vartheta}-V^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{K})
, for which it
is known that
\partial_{\vartheta}\rho^{K}
is a ground state.

By the Sturm-Liouville analysis in
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, the
semigroup
e^{tK\mathcal{A}_{K}}F
converges to

{{\boldsymbol{e}}}\,\langle F,{{\boldsymbol{e}}}\rangle
. Then, we may approximate the
value at time
t
of the martingale
M^{N}_{s}(T^{N}_{t-s}G)
by the value
at time
t
of the martingale

M^{N}_{s}({{\boldsymbol{e}}}_{K}(\vartheta)e^{(t-s)\Delta_{d-1}}\langle G\rangle(\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{d}))
. By a martingale relation, this
last expression equals

M^{N}_{t}({{\boldsymbol{e}}}_{K}(\vartheta)\langle G\rangle(\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{d}))+\int_{0}^{t}M^{N}_{s}\big({{\boldsymbol{e}}}_{K}(\vartheta)\Delta_{d-1}e^{(t-s)\Delta_{d-1}}\langle G\rangle(\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{d})\big).

In these terms,

\langle G\rangle=\int{{\boldsymbol{e}}}(\vartheta)G(\vartheta,\theta)d\vartheta
, and
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}_{K}
is a
\sqrt{K}{\mathbb{T}}

torus approximation of
{{\boldsymbol{e}}}
. Finally, in the
N\uparrow\infty

limit, the covariances of the last display are computed as those of
the limits described earlier.

Last, we comment on the role of
K_{N}
in the results. Since we do not
know the stationary measure of the Glauber + Kawaski dynamics on

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
, we are left to use
\nu_{0}^{N}
as a proxy. The allowed growth
of
K_{N}
in the estimates to prove the relative entropy bound and the
Boltzmann-Gibbs principle is therefore limited. As mentioned in

[
14
]
, if the
K_{N}
growth rate could be improved to
K_{N}=N^{b}

for large enough powers
0<b<2
, then higher order terms in the
stochastic differential of
X^{N}_{t}
can be homogenized so that one may
be able to obtain nonlinear SPDEs for the fluctuation limits of

X^{N}_{t}
.

Motion by mean curvature

Consider a smooth evolution of closed hypersurfaces

\{\Gamma_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Let
n(t)
and
\kappa(t)

be the ‘inward’ normal velocity and mean curvature of
\Gamma_{t}
,
fixing one side of
\Gamma_{t}
as ‘inward’. We say the
evolution is motion by mean curvature starting from
\Gamma_{0}
if

\displaystyle n(t)=(d-1)\kappa(t),\ \ \ t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}|_{t=0}=\Gamma_{0}.

When
\Gamma_{0}
is a smooth hypersurface without boundary, it is known
that there exists a smooth solution
\{\Gamma_{t}:t\in[0,T]\}

evolving by mean-curvature starting from
\Gamma_{0}
for a
T>0
. One
may also view the mean curvature evolution as a local graph flow in
appropriate coordinates. See Section 4 in
[
2
]
and

[
13
]
, among other references, for discussion of these
issues.

In the PDE literature, the sharp interface limit of solutions
u=u^{K}

as
K
diverges of the reaction-diffusion equation (
2.4
), under conditions on
V
, to mean curvature flow is well
known; see
[
2
]
. Suppose the potential
V
is a ‘double
well’, that is
V
has exactly three critical points

\rho_{-}<\rho_{*}<\rho_{+}
such that
\rho_{\pm}
are local minima with
equal values
V(\rho_{-})=V(\rho_{+})
and
\rho_{*}
is a local maximum.
Under such a
V
, (
2.4
) is seen as an ‘Allen-Cahn’
equation, and
u^{K}
converges pointwise to a step function defined by
mean curvature flow: For
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty}u^{K}(t,\theta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\rho_{-}&{\rm for\ }\theta\ {\rm on\ one\ side\ of\ }\Gamma_{t}\\
\rho_{+}&{\rm for\ }\theta\ {\rm on\ the\ other\ side\ of\ }\Gamma_{t},\end{array}\right.

where
\Gamma_{t}
evolves by mean curvature.

As remarked in Section
1
, derivation of mean-curvature flow
from reaction-diffusion particle systems is also known; see

[
4
]
,
[
23
]

[

]

]
,

[

]
. We state a result in Theorem 1.1
of
[

]
which applies in our setting of Glauber + Kawasaki
particle dynamics.

Consider the particle system generated by
L_{N}
where
K=K_{N}
diverges
but is bounded
K_{N}\leq\delta\log(N)
for a small constant

\delta>0
. Let
\rho_{0}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\rightarrow(\rho_{-},\rho_{+})
be a

C^{\infty}
function and

\Gamma_{0}=\{\theta:\rho_{0}(\theta)=\rho_{*}\}
its associated
initial interface. We will also suppose that the gradient of
\rho_{0}
,
denoted by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nabla\rho_{0}
, is not parallel to the normal
direction to
\Gamma_{0}
, and
\rho_{0}(\theta)<\rho_{*}

\rho_{0}(\theta)>\rho_{*}

\Gamma_{0}

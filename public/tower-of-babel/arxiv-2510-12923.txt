On operator fields in the upper triangular Toeplitz form
M. M. Chernin, A. Yu. Konyaev

Abstract




1
Introduction

We say that an operator field
L
is in the upper triangular Toeplitz form
[
1
]
if, in given coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
, one has

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}g_{n}&g_{n-1}&\dots&g_{1}\\
0&g_{n}&\ddots&\vdots\\
&\ddots&\ddots&g_{n-1}\\
0&0&\dots&g_{n}\end{array}\right).

(1)

The operator fields in the upper triangular Toeplitz form appear in many branches of mathematics and mathematical physics: in the theory of Frobenius and F-manifolds
[
2
,
7
,
8
]
, in reductions of hydrodynamic chains and linearly degenerate dispersionless PDEs
[
9
]
, in the study of the soliton gas
[
10
,
11
]
, and in Nijenhuis geometry
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, to name a few.

The main purpose of this article is to investigate the following fundamental problem:

Q:

Given the operator field
L
in the upper triangular Toeplitz form in coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
with the regularity condition
g_{n-1}\neq 0
, describe all the coordinate transformations
v(u)
that preserve this form.

The regularity condition ensures that the operator
L
is similar to the Jordan block of maximal size in every point in the neighborhood of the coordinate origin.

It turns out that this problem can be solved in full generality. Informally speaking, all such coordinate transformations are parameterized by a single function of one variable and
n-1
functions of two variables. Moreover, given the collection of such functions, one can construct the corresponding transformation
v(u)
explicitly. The only thing one needs is a recursion procedure, which involves solving triangular linear systems and integration.

Let us discuss the results in greater detail. To do that, one first needs to introduce certain elements of Nijenhuis geometry. Recall that the Nijenhuis torsion of
L
is a tensor of type
(1,2)
, given by the formula

\mathcal{N}_{L}(\xi,\eta)=L^{2}[\xi,\eta]+[L\xi,L\eta]-L[L\xi,\eta]-L[\xi,L\eta].

(2)

Here,
\xi,\eta
are arbitrary vector fields, and the brackets
[\,,\,]
stand for the standard vector field commutator. The Haantjes torsion is given by the formula

\mathcal{H}_{L}(\xi,\eta)=L^{2}\mathcal{N}_{L}(\xi,\eta)+\mathcal{N}_{L}(L\xi,L\eta)-L\mathcal{N}_{L}(L\xi,\eta)-L\mathcal{N}_{L}(\xi,L\eta).

(3)

The notation
L\mathcal{N}_{L}(\xi,\eta)
means that we apply
L
to the vector field
\mathcal{N}_{L}(\xi,\eta)
. The operator field
L
is called the Nijenhuis (Haantjes) operator if its Nijenhuis (Haantjes) torsion identically vanishes. Obviously, if
L
is Nijenhuis, then it is Haantjes.

Assume now that
L
is in Toeplitz form in the given coordinates. Define in the same coordinates

J=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&1&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0\\
&&&\ddots&\\
0&0&0&\ddots&1\\
0&0&0&\dots&0\end{array}\right).

(4)

Obviously,
J
is Nijenhuis. At the same time
L=g_{1}J^{n-1}+\dots+g_{n}\textrm{\rm Id}
, so
L
is a Haantjes operator (Corollary 3.3 in
[
12
]
). The results of the paper are as follows.

In Theorem
1
we provide the sufficient conditions for an operator field in Toeplitz form to be Nijenhuis. It also states that in the case of
g_{n-1}\neq 0
in formula (
1
) operator, the same conditions are necessary as well. We give an example
2.1
to show that if
g_{n-1}=0
, then there exist Nijenhuis operators outside the scope of Theorem
1
.

The Theorem
1
reduces the question to the overdetermined system of PDEs with constant coefficients. The initial condition for the corresponding system is at most one function of one variable and
n-1
functions of two variables. The Theorem
2
explicitly solves the aforementioned system: given the initial condition, it produces the corresponding Nijenhuis operator in explicit form. The description of such Nijenhuis operators is an interesting result in its own right.

Theorem
3
describes the linear system of PDEs that the coordinate change preserving the upper triangular Toeplitz form must satisfy. The idea here is as follows: in given coordinates, fix an arbitrary Nijenhuis operator
M
in the upper triangular Toeplitz form with
g_{n-1}\neq 0
and zeros on the diagonal. Due to the general theory in
[
6
]
, there exists a coordinate change
v(u)
such that, in coordinates
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
, the operator has the form
J
. This yields certain relations on
v(u)
with respect to the Nijenhuis operator, thus producing the differential equations on
v(u)
.

Finally, we provide an algorithm for resolving the aforementioned system of equations. The theorem
4
states that the algorithm is correct. It requires only integration and answers the main question posed in the beginning of the paper. We supply the example (
2.3
), which yields all the coordinate transformations that preserve the upper triangular Toeplitz form in dimension four.

The paper is organized as follows: in section
2
, we formulate the main results, provide remarks, and include necessary examples. The proofs are given in sections
3
,
4
,
5
, and
6
. The work is supported by the grant RScF 24-21-00450.

2
Main results

We start this section with the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Fix the coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
and assume that the operator field
L
is in the upper triangular Toeplitz form. The following (equivalent) conditions on
g_{i},i=1,\dots,n
are sufficient for
L
to be Nijenhuis:

1.

Differentials
\mathrm{d}g_{i}
satisfy the linear system of PDEs

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=J^{2*}\mathrm{d}g_{n},

(5)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=J^{2*}\mathrm{d}g_{n-1}-2J^{*}\mathrm{d}g_{n},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=J^{2*}\mathrm{d}g_{i}-2J^{*}\mathrm{d}g_{i+1}+\mathrm{d}g_{i+2},\quad i=1,\dots,n-2,

Here
J
is given by the formula (
4
).

2.

Denote
\frac{\partial g}{\partial u}
as the Jacobi matrix of the system of functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
. Then

\Big[\Big[\frac{\partial g}{\partial u},J\Big],J\Big]=0,

(6)

where
[,,\,]
stands for the standard matrix commutator, that is,
[A,B]=AB-BA
. The matrix
J
is the same as above.

3.

Denote the column
(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})^{T}
by
g

\displaystyle J^{2}\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{1}}=0,

(7)

\displaystyle J^{2}\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{2}}-2J\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{1}}=0,

\displaystyle J^{2}\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{i}}-2J\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{i-1}}+\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{i-2}}=0,\quad i=3,\dots,n.

If
g_{n-1}\neq 0
, then the conditions above are also necessary.

The next example shows that without the condition
g_{n-1}\neq 0
, the equations in Theorem
1
do not necessarily hold for the Nijenhuis operator in the upper triangular Toeplitz form.

Example 2.1
.

Assume that
n=3
and
g_{2}\equiv 0
. Let
L
be a Nijenhuis operator in the upper triangular Toeplitz form. By direct calculations we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{L}(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})=0,\quad\mathcal{N}_{L}(\partial_{1},\partial_{3})=2g_{1}\frac{\partial g_{3}}{\partial u^{1}}\partial_{1},\quad\mathcal{N}_{L}(\partial_{2},\partial_{3})=g_{1}\frac{\partial g_{3}}{\partial u^{2}}\partial_{1}+g_{1}\frac{\partial g_{3}}{\partial u^{1}}\partial_{2}.

Vanishing of the Nijenhuis torsion implies that either
g_{3}
does not depend on
u^{1},u^{2}
or
g_{1}
identically vanishes. We get two classes of Nijenhuis operators

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a(u^{3})&0&b(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})\\
0&a(u^{3})&0\\
0&0&a(u^{3})\end{array}\right),\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})&0&0\\
0&c(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})&0\\
0&0&c(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})\end{array}\right).

Here
a
is a function of single variable and
b,c
are arbitrary functions of three variables. Both Nijenhuis operators do not satisfy the equations in Theorem
1
.
\blacksquare

Let us recall some facts about matrix-valued functions (chapter 5 in
[
15
]
). Consider a smooth function
f
of two variables and a pair of polynomials

p(t)=c_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+c_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad q(t)=m_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+m_{n}.

Take the following decomposition (we change the numeration for the first
n
terms) in
t=0

f\big(p(t),q(t)\big)=f_{n}+f_{n-1}t+\dots+f_{1}t^{n-1}+\{\text{terms, containing $t^{k}$ for $k\geq n$}\}.

(8)

Each
f_{i}
is a function in
c_{i},m_{j}
. Now consider a pair of operators in Toeplitz form

P=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}c_{n}&c_{n-1}&\dots&c_{2}&c_{1}\\
0&c_{n}&\ddots&&c_{2}\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&c_{n}&c_{n-1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&c_{n}\end{array}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}m_{n}&m_{n-1}&\dots&m_{2}&m_{1}\\
0&m_{n}&\ddots&&m_{2}\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&m_{n}&m_{n-1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&m_{n}\end{array}\right).

The matrix-valued function
f(P,Q)
is given by the formula

f(P,Q)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}f_{n}(c,m)&f_{n-1}(c,m)&\dots&f_{2}(c,m)&f_{1}(c,m)\\
0&f_{n}(c,m)&\ddots&&f_{2}(c,m)\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&f_{n}(c,m)&f_{n-1}(c,m)\\
0&0&\dots&0&f_{n}(c,m)\end{array}\right),

where
f_{i}(c,m)
are the coefficients of decomposition (
8
), depending on
c,m
.

Theorem 2
.

Fix the coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
and consider
J
in the form

J=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&1&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0\\
&&&\ddots&\\
0&0&0&\ddots&1\\
0&0&0&\dots&0\end{array}\right)

and a pair of operator fields

P=u^{1}J^{n-1}+\dots+u^{n}\textrm{\rm Id}\quad\text{and}\quad Q=(n-1)u^{1}J^{n-2}+\dots+2u^{n-2}J+u^{n-1}\textrm{\rm Id}.

Fix an arbitrary collection of
n-1
functions of two variables
f_{1},\dots,f_{n-1}
and one function
f_{n}
of a single variable. Define operator field

L=f_{1}(P,Q)J^{n-1}+\dots+f_{n-1}(P,Q)J+f_{n}(P).

(9)

By definition,
L
is in the upper triangular Toeplitz form. Then the operator
L
is a Nijenhuis operator, and any Nijenhuis operator in upper triangular form for
g_{n-1}\neq 0
can be obtained in this manner from the appropriate collection of smooth functions
f_{i}
with
f_{n-1}(0,0)\neq 0
.

The next example provides an explicit calculation in dimension four.

Example 2.2
.

Consider case
n=4
. Fix three functions of two variables
a,b,c
and one function of single variable
d
. The polynomials

in this case are (we use lower indices for coordinates in this example)

The decomposition of

\displaystyle d(p(t))=d(u_{4})+u_{3}d^{\prime}(u_{4})t+

\displaystyle\Big(d^{\prime}(u_{4})u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{3}^{2}\big)t^{2}+

\displaystyle+\Big(d^{\prime}(u_{4})u_{1}+d^{\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{2}u_{3}+\frac{1}{6}d^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{3}^{3}\Big)t^{3}+\dots.

The decomposition of
a(p,q)
takes form

\displaystyle a(p(t),q(t))

\displaystyle=a(u_{4},u_{3})+\Big(a^{\prime}_{p}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{3}+2a^{\prime}_{q}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{2}\Big)t+

For

d(P)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}d(u_{4})&u_{3}d^{\prime}(u_{4})&d^{\prime}(u_{4})u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{3}^{2}&d^{\prime}(u_{4})u_{1}+d^{\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{4}u_{3}+\frac{1}{6}d^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{4})u_{3}^{3}\\

0&0&c(u_{4},u_{3})&\begin{array}[]{c}c^{\prime}_{p}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{3}+2c^{\prime}_{q}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{2}\\
\end{array}\\

\end{array}\right),

in dimension four is given by formula

Theorem 3
.

Fix the coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
and let
L
be an operator field in the upper triangular Toeplitz form (
1
) with the condition
g_{n-1}\neq 0
. Consider
J,P,Q
as the same as in theorem
2
. Fix
n-1
functions of two variables
f_{1},\dots,f_{n-1}
with
f_{n-1}(0,0)\neq 0
and take

M=f_{1}(P,Q)J^{n-1}+\dots+f_{n-1}(P,Q)J.

Consider the solution
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
of the system

\displaystyle M^{*}\mathrm{d}v^{i}=\mathrm{d}v^{i+1},\quad i=1,\dots,n-1

(10)

with
\frac{\partial v^{1}}{\partial u^{1}}\neq 0
. Then

1.

Functions
v^{i},i=1,\dots,n
define coordinate transformation;

2.

This transformation preserves the upper triangular Toeplitz form of
L
;

3.

Any transformation that preserves the upper triangular Toeplitz form of
L
can be obtained in such a way for an appropriate choice of functions
f_{i}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The transformation from
M
to
J
from Theorem
3
is not unique and is defined modulo the transformation, that preserves
J
. They can be constructed as follows: pick
n
functions
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}
of single variable with
h_{1}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
. Let
P
be the same as in theorem
2
. Consider operator field in the upper triangular Toeplitz form

h_{1}(P)J^{n-1}+\dots+h_{n}(P)\textrm{\rm Id}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}w_{n}&w_{n-1}&\dots&w_{2}&w_{1}\\
0&w_{n}&\ddots&&w_{2}\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&w_{n}&w_{n-1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&w_{n}\end{array}\right).

Then functions
w(u)
define a coordinate transformation, that preserves
J
. Note that any transformation, that preserves
J
, can be obtained in such a way.
\blacksquare

The next algorithm allows one to solve the system (
10
). The initial data of the algorithm is
M
from Theorem
3
.

Step 0:

Pick an arbitrary function
q(u^{n})\neq 0
and define
v^{n}=\int_{0}^{u^{n}}q(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau
;

Step 1:

Now assume that we have constructed
v^{n-i}
. If
i=n-1
, then the algorithm stops, and we have constructed the solution of the system (
10
) with
\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial u^{1}}\neq 0
. If
i<n-1
, then continue;

Step 2:

Construct
\omega
in the form

\omega=\omega_{1}\mathrm{d}u^{1}+\dots+\omega_{n-1}\mathrm{d}u^{n-1},

such that
M^{*}\omega=\mathrm{d}v^{n-i}
. This is a solution to a triangular system of linear equations (in the algebraic sense).

Step 3:

Construct

\displaystyle v^{n-i-1}=\int_{0}^{u^{1}}\omega_{1}(t,

\displaystyle u^{2},\dots,u^{n})\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{u^{2}}\omega_{2}(0,t,\dots,u^{n})\mathrm{d}t+\dots+

(11)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{u^{n-1}}\omega_{n-1}(0,0,\dots,t,u^{n})\mathrm{d}t+r(u^{n}).

Here the choice of
r(u^{n})
is arbitrary.

Step 4:

Go to step 1.

The algorithm implies that the system (
10
) can be solved explicitly, using only integrations.

Theorem 4
.

Fix coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
and let
M
be an operator constructed in Theorem
3
. Then

1.

The algorithm is correct. That is, for any initial data, it produces the collection of functions
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
;

2.

Functions
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
satisfy the system (
10
) in Theorem
3
with the condition
\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial u_{1}}\neq 0
;

3.

All the solutions of the system (
10
) in Theorem
3
with the condition
\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial u_{1}}\neq 0
can be obtained via the appropriate choice of the function
q
and the ”constants of integration” in the formula (

).

Let us give the example of the application of our algorithm:

Example 2.3
.

Fix coordinates

\displaystyle r(u_{4},u_{3},u_{2})

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}_{p}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{3}+2c^{\prime}_{q}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{2}+b(u_{4},u_{3}),

\displaystyle m(u_{4},u_{3},u_{2},u_{1})

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}_{p}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{2}+3c^{\prime}_{q}(u_{4},u_{3})u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime\prime}_{pp}u_{3}^{2}+

\omega^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{4}(u_{4})}{c^{2}(u_{3},u_{3})}u^{2}+s_{2}(u_{4})+

\displaystyle+\int^{u_{3}}_{0}\Bigg[\frac{1}{c(\rho,u_{4})}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\rho}\Big(\frac{s_{4}(u_{4})}{c(u_{4},\tau)}\Big)^{\prime}_{u_{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau+s^{\prime}_{3}(u_{4})-r(u_{4},\rho,0)\frac{s_{4}(u_{4})}{c^{2}(u_{4},\rho)}\Bigg)\Bigg]\mathrm{d}\rho.

Proof of Theorem

We start with a Lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a Nijenhuis operator from the statement of the Theorem. Assume that for 1-form
\omega
, both 1-forms
M^{*}\omega
and
(M^{*})^{2}\omega
are closed. Then
\mathrm{d}\omega(M\xi,M\eta)=0
for all vector fields

Proof.

Recall that the Nijenhuis torsion
\mathcal{N}_{M}:\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{M}^{n})\to\Omega^{2}(\mathrm{M}^{n})
can be treated as a mapping from 1-forms
\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{M}^{n})
to 2-forms
\Omega^{2}(\mathrm{M}^{n})

[
6
]
:

\beta(\cdot\,,\cdot)=\mathrm{d}(M^{*}\omega)(M\cdot\,,\cdot)+\mathrm{d}(M^{*}\omega)(\cdot\,,M\cdot)-\mathrm{d}({M^{*}}^{2}\omega)(\cdot\,,\cdot)-\mathrm{d}\omega(M\cdot\,,M\cdot).

Here
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{M}^{n}),\beta=\mathcal{N}_{M}\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathrm{M}^{n})
. The statement of the Lemma immediately follows from this formula.
∎

Let us proceed to the algorithm. For a given initial data of
M
and step 0, it produces
n
functions
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
. Thus, it is obviously correct.

Now let us proceed to the second statement of the Theorem. First, notice that during the initial run
1-

\omega

has only one non-zero term. Thus, the formula (
11

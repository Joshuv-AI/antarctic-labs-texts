Non-Tonelli Finsler Geometry of Exotic Superconductivity: Metastable Vortex Phases and Geometric Phase Transitions
Y. Alipour Fakhri

Abstract
We develop a thermally coupled Ginzburg-Landau theory on Weakly Non-Tonelli (WNT) Finsler manifolds , extending classical vortex analysis beyond the Tonelli convexity paradigm. The WNT framework weakens global 1 1 -homogeneity and strict convexity while preserving superlinearity and local ellipticity, enabling a geometric treatment of superconductors whose anisotropic energy landscapes are nonconvex and temperature-dependent. Within this setting, we construct the generalized Legendre correspondence, Hamiltonian metric, and WNT Laplacian, proving existence and sharp Coulomb asymptotics of the three-dimensional Green kernel. We then establish the Γ \Gamma –convergence of the WNT-GL energy and identify metastable vortex filaments minimizing a renormalized geometric functional. Finally, a dynamic Γ \Gamma -limit yields an effective filament flow governed by the WNT Finsler curvature and thermally induced geometric forces, predicting curvature focusing and phase bifurcation at a critical transition temperature T c T_{c} . This theory unifies convex-analytic, geometric, and physical perspectives, showing that Non-Tonelli Finsler structures form a natural analytic bridge between classical Finsler geometry, anisotropic variational models, and the nonlinear thermodynamics of exotic superconductivity.

-convergence; thermal coupling; semiclassical limit;

Introduction

The Ginzburg–Landau (GL) theory has served for decades as a cornerstone in the mathematical and physical understanding of superconductivity, providing a variational framework that captures macroscopic quantum phenomena through the interaction of complex order parameters and electromagnetic fields
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
17
]
. Since the geometric reinterpretation of the GL functional in terms of gauge and differential geometry, it has become a unifying model linking nonlinear PDEs, geometric measure theory, and the thermodynamics of condensed matter systems.

In recent years, a geometric reformulation of the GL model within the broader setting of Finsler geometry has been proposed in order to describe materials with anisotropic or direction-dependent properties
[
1
,
5
,
13
,
18
]
. In this context, the classical Tonelli assumptions on Finsler structures–smoothness, strict convexity, and positive 1–homogeneity–ensure the existence of a globally defined Legendre duality between the tangent and cotangent bundles. These assumptions, however, restrict the analysis to uniformly convex models and fail to capture the degeneracies and multi-phase anisotropies observed in complex superconductors, especially those with temperature-dependent metrics and nonconvex energy landscapes.

From an analytical standpoint, the Tonelli convexity conditions guarantee the smoothness and global invertibility of the Legendre transform, leading to a well–defined Hamiltonian metric and elliptic Finsler–Laplace operator
[
5
,
14
,
15
,
10
]
. Yet, as observed in anisotropic or layered superconductors, these assumptions break down: the Legendre correspondence may become multivalued, the Hamiltonian structure can degenerate, and the ellipticity of the Laplacian may hold only in a weak or measure-theoretic sense. Such limitations motivated several analytic extensions of Finsler–type variational models in the context of superconductivity
[
2
,
3
]
, but a unified geometric and analytic framework accommodating nonconvexity and nonhomogeneity remained elusive.

In parallel, recent progress in convex and nonsmooth analysis has provided tools suitable for handling weak convexity and variable growth conditions
[
16
,
4
,
8
,
9
]
. These developments suggest that a generalized Finsler structure, relaxing Tonelli’s hypotheses but preserving superlinearity and local ellipticity, could serve as an appropriate analytic environment for nonstandard Ginzburg–Landau models.

Scientific gap.
Despite recent geometric and analytic advances, the existing Finsler extensions of the GL framework are confined either to strongly convex geometries or to perturbative regimes where convexity is only weakly violated. There is currently no rigorous theory that systematically integrates nonconvex, temperature–dependent anisotropy into a coherent analytic–geometric model of superconductivity. In particular, the lack of a well-defined Legendre–Hamiltonian correspondence beyond the Tonelli class prevents one from constructing elliptic operators, Green kernels, and
\Gamma
–limit structures in this broader setting.

Objective and contribution.

The present paper develops a complete analytic and geometric theory of superconductivity on
Weakly Non–Tonelli (WNT)
Finsler manifolds. The WNT class provides a controlled relaxation of Tonelli convexity and 1-homogeneity while maintaining sufficient analytic regularity to define the Legendre correspondence, Hamiltonian metric, and WNT–Finsler Laplacian. Within this framework, we establish:

rigorous definitions and equivalence theorems for geometric and analytic WNT structures;

•

existence, compactness, and
\Gamma

•

characterization of metastable vortex filaments minimizing a renormalized geometric energy functional; and

•

a dynamic
\Gamma
–limit describing curvature-induced vortex flow and geometric phase transitions at critical temperature

.

These results extend both the classical geometric analysis of Bethuel, Brezis, Hélein, Jerrard, Soner, and Serfaty

and the Finsler-geometric framework of Bao–Chern–Shen

[

]
, providing a unified bridge between convex analysis, geometric measure theory, and the nonlinear thermodynamics of anisotropic superconductivity.

2
From Tonelli Finsler Geometry to Weakly Non-Tonelli Extensions

The classical Tonelli framework provides the analytic backbone of smooth Finsler geometry: a Lagrangian
F(x,y)
that is
C^{\infty}
on
TM\setminus\{0\}
, positively
1
–homogeneous in
y
, and whose square is strictly convex in
y
. Under these assumptions, the fiberwise Legendre map is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism and induces a smooth, uniformly convex Hamiltonian duality between tangent and cotangent bundles. We recall the standard setting for reference.

(T1)

F
is
C^{\infty}
on
TM\setminus\{0\}
and continuous on
TM
;

(T2)

F
is positively
1
-homogeneous in
y
;

(T3)

F^{2}
is strictly convex in
y
, i.e.
g_{ij}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{y^{i}y^{j}}F^{2}(x,y)
is positive-definite.

Under (T1)-(T3), the Legendre map

\mathcal{L}_{x}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}^{*}M,\qquad y\mapsto\partial_{y}\!\big(\tfrac{1}{2}F^{2}(x,y)\big)

(1)

is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism, giving the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H(x,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{2}F^{*2}(x,\xi),\qquad F^{*}(x,\xi)=\sup_{y\neq 0}\{\langle\xi,y\rangle-F(x,y)\}.

This rigid duality allows one to define Finsler gradient/divergence and a (linear) Laplacian with full regularity in the Tonelli regime.

2.1
Beyond Tonelli: analytic phenomena when convexity/homogeneity fail

When either
1
-homogeneity or strict convexity fails, several features necessarily change:

(F1)

Loss of global Legendre diffeomorphism.

The map (
1
) need not be bijective; one must replace it by the
subdifferential
of
\Phi_{x}(y):=\tfrac{1}{2}F^{2}(x,y)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{x}(y):=\partial\Phi_{x}(y)\subset T_{x}^{*}M,

which is a (possibly) set-valued, maximal monotone relation. Its inverse is
\partial H(x,\cdot)
with
H=\Phi_{x}^{*}
(Rockafellar
[
16
, Th. 23.5]
).

(F2)

Hamiltonian regularity drops.

The Fenchel conjugate

\displaystyle H(x,\xi):=\sup_{y\in T_{x}M}\{\langle\xi,y\rangle-\Phi_{x}(y)\}

is convex and coercive, but typically only locally Lipschitz (or
C^{1,\alpha}
on strata);
\partial_{\xi}H
exists and is single–valued for a.e.
\xi
.

(F3)

Quasilinear (possibly degenerate) ellipticity.

The operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{F}(u):=-\mathrm{div}\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big),

is quasilinear; linear superposition and linear Green representations
do not
hold in general. Potential theory must be phrased in weak/variational form; any kernel–based representation is, at best, a
local
linearization device.

These observations motivate a careful enlargement of Tonelli geometry that preserves enough structure for PDE/variational analysis while allowing weaker convexity and nonhomogeneous growth.

2.2
Weakly Non-Tonelli (WNT) structures: primal and dual definitions

Throughout, fix a smooth background Riemannian metric to measure fiber norms. All statements below are understood on
TM\setminus\{0\}
.

Definition 2.1

(Primal WNT)
.

A Lagrangian
F:TM\to[0,\infty)
lies in the
Weakly Non–Tonelli (WNT)
class if there exists
1<p<\infty
such that, for every compact
K\Subset M
, the following hold:

(W1)

Convexity and
C^{1}
off the zero section.
For each
x\in K
, the map
y\mapsto\Phi_{x}(y):=\tfrac{1}{2}F^{2}(x,y)
is proper, convex, lower semicontinuous, continuous on
T_{x}M
, and
C^{1}
on
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
. The map
(x,y)\mapsto D_{y}\Phi_{x}(y)
is measurable in
x
and continuous in
y
on
K\times(T_{x}M\setminus\{0\})
.

(W2)

Local
p
–growth and coercivity.
There exist
c_{K},C_{K}>0
(depending on
K
) such that

\displaystyle c_{K}|y|^{p}\leq\Phi_{x}(y)\leq C_{K}(1+|y|^{p})\qquad\forall(x,y)\in K\times T_{x}M.

(W3)

Localized strong convexity on bounded fibers.
For every bounded set
B\subset T_{x}M
there exist
m_{K,B},L_{K,B}>0
such that for a.e.
(x,y)\in K\times B
the (Alexandrov) second derivative exists and

\displaystyle m_{K,B}\,I\;\leq\;D^{2}_{yy}\Phi_{x}(y)\;\leq\;L_{K,B}\,I.

In particular,
\partial_{y}\Phi_{x}
is single-valued and locally Lipschitz on a full-measure subset of
K\times B
(Rademacher), and the Legendre correspondence is bi–Lipschitz onto its image on
K\times B
with constants depending on
(K,B)
.

Definition 2.2

(Dual WNT)
.

Let
H=\Phi_{x}^{*}
be the fiberwise Fenchel conjugate and set
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. We say
F
is
dual–WNT
if, for every compact
K\Subset M
:

(D1)

Dual convexity.
For each
x\in K
,
H(x,\cdot)
is proper, convex, l.s.c., and finite everywhere on
T_{x}^{*}M
.

(D2)

Local
p^{\prime}
–growth.
There exist
c^{\prime}_{K},C^{\prime}_{K}>0
such that

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{K}|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}\leq H(x,\xi)\leq C^{\prime}_{K}(1+|\xi|^{p^{\prime}})\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in K\times T_{x}^{*}M.

(Any quadratic two–sided control, when used, is
only
local on bounded dual fibers determined by
K
and a bounded primal fiber
B
.)

(D3)

Subdifferential correspondence (maximal monotone).
For a.e.
(x,y,\xi)
,

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial_{y}\Phi_{x}(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\in\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi),

and
\operatorname{Graph}(\partial_{y}\Phi_{x})
and
\operatorname{Graph}(\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot))
are inverse maximal–monotone relations (Rockafellar
[
16
, Th. 23.5]
). Measurable selections exist on compact fibers (Castaing representation; cf.
[
4
, Prop. 2.4.9]
).

Proposition 2.3

(Local primal–dual equivalence)
.

On each compact
K\Subset M
, the primal and dual WNT conditions are equivalent, with constants depending on
K
and on bounded fiber sets. Moreover, for a.e.
x
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}=H(x,\cdot)^{*},\qquad H(x,\cdot)=\Phi_{x}^{*},\qquad(\partial_{y}\Phi_{x})^{-1}=\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot)\quad\text{a.e.\ in fibers}.

Proof.

Fix a compact set
K\Subset M
and write
\Phi_{x}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}F(x,y)^{2}
and
H(x,\xi)=\Phi_{x}^{*}(\xi)
.
All assertions are fiberwise and local on
K
and on bounded subsets of
T_{x}M
and
T_{x}^{*}M
; measurability in
x
is understood throughout and causes no loss of generality because all variational constructions are performed on compact charts.

Primal
\Rightarrow
Dual.

By (W1)–-(W2), for each
x\in K
the function
\Phi_{x}
is proper, convex, l.s.c., finite everywhere and
C^{1}
on
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
, with
p
–growth and coercivity:

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}(y)\geq c_{K}|y|^{p}-C_{K},\qquad\Phi_{x}(y)\leq C_{K}(1+|y|^{p}).

Fenchel–Moreau duality yields that
H(x,\cdot)
is proper, convex, l.s.c., finite everywhere and
\Phi_{x}=(H(x,\cdot))^{*}
(Rockafellar
[
16
, Ths. 12.2, 23.5]
), i.e. (D1).
The growth of
H
follows from the standard convex–analytic optimization: Young’s inequality gives, for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\langle\xi,y\rangle\leq\frac{\alpha}{p}|y|^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}\,\alpha^{p^{\prime}/p}}|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}\!,

whence
H(x,\xi)\leq(p^{\prime}c_{K}^{p^{\prime}/p})^{-1}|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}+C_{K}
, while
\Phi_{x}(y)\leq C_{K}(1+|y|^{p})
implies

\displaystyle H(x,\xi)\geq\sup_{R>0}\{R|\xi|-C_{K}(1+R^{p})\}\geq c^{\prime}_{K}|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}-C^{\prime}_{K}.

Thus (D2) in the global
p^{\prime}
–-growth sense holds on
K\times T_{x}^{*}M
.

The localized quadratic control in (D2) is a consequence of (W3).
Indeed, if
B\subset T_{x}M
is bounded, then for a.e.
(x,y)\in K\times B
the Alexandrov Hessian exists and satisfies

m_{K,B}I\leq D^{2}_{yy}\Phi_{x}(y)\leq L_{K,B}I
.
By classical convex duality, strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient of
\Phi_{x}
on
B
are equivalent to Lipschitz gradient and strong convexity of
H(x,\cdot)
on the dual region
\nabla\Phi_{x}(B)
, with constants depending only on
(m_{K,B},L_{K,B})
(Rockafellar
[
16
, Ths. 25.5, 26.3]
). In particular, on bounded dual fibers one has two–sided quadratic Taylor control for
H
and local Lipschitz regularity of
\nabla_{\xi}H
; a finite covering by such patches on
K
provides the localized form of (D2).
Finally, the Fenchel–Young relation yields

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial\Phi_{x}(y)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ y\in\partial H(x,\xi),

and maximal monotonicity of
\partial\Phi_{x}
ensures
(\partial\Phi_{x})^{-1}=\partial H(x,\cdot)
(Rockafellar
[
16
, Th. 23.5]
). Since
\Phi_{x}
is differentiable almost everywhere (Rademacher/Alexandrov),
\partial\Phi_{x}(y)=\{\nabla\Phi_{x}(y)\}
a.e., and hence
\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot)
is single–valued a.e. This proves (D3).

Dual
\Rightarrow
Primal.

Assume (D1)–(D3) on
K
. Define
\Phi_{x}:=H(x,\cdot)^{*}
; then
\Phi_{x}
is proper, convex, l.s.c., finite everywhere and
\Phi_{x}^{**}=\Phi_{x}
(Fenchel–Moreau). The
p^{\prime}
–growth of
H
implies the
p
–growth and coercivity of
\Phi_{x}
by the same optimization argument as above, establishing (W2). On bounded dual fibers where
H
possesses strong convexity and a Lipschitz gradient (the localized content of (D2)), the conjugacy rules imply that
\Phi_{x}
has Lipschitz gradient and strong convexity on the corresponding primal bounded fibers, which is precisely (W3) in the Alexandrov sense. Regularity (W1) follows from convexity plus Rademacher’s theorem away from the zero section.

Collecting the above, on each compact
K\Subset M
the primal and dual WNT conditions are equivalent, with constants depending on
K
and on bounded fiber sets. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}=(H(x,\cdot))^{*},\hskip 5.69054ptH(x,\cdot)=\Phi_{x}^{*},(\partial_{y}\Phi_{x})^{-1}=\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot)\quad\text{a.e. in fibers},

as claimed.
∎

2.3
Legendre map: smoothness and (local) injectivity

Write
L_{x}:=\partial_{y}\Phi_{x}
wherever
\Phi_{x}
is differentiable.

Proposition 2.4

(Regularity and injectivity level of
L_{x}
)
.

Under WNT:

(i)

L_{x}
is monotone and locally Lipschitz on
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
a.e.; in particular,

\displaystyle\langle L_{x}(y_{1})-L_{x}(y_{2}),\,y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

(ii)

L_{x}
is single–valued and one–to–one
locally
on full–measure subsets of
T_{x}M
(injective a.e.); global bijectivity need not hold.

(iii)

The inverse relation
L_{x}^{-1}
equals
\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot)
; wherever
H
is differentiable,
L_{x}^{-1}=(\partial_{\xi}H)^{-1}
is single–valued.

Proof.

Fix
x\in M
and consider
\Phi_{x}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}F(x,y)^{2}
on
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
.
Convexity and finiteness of
\Phi_{x}
imply that its subdifferential is a maximal monotone operator; hence for any
y_{1},y_{2}
and selections
\xi_{i}\in\partial\Phi_{x}(y_{i})
one has

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},\;y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

On the full–measure differentiability locus of
\Phi_{x}
(Rademacher/Alexandrov),
\partial\Phi_{x}(y)=\{\nabla\Phi_{x}(y)\}
and we may write
L_{x}(y):=\nabla\Phi_{x}(y)
; the monotonicity inequality becomes

\displaystyle\langle L_{x}(y_{1})-L_{x}(y_{2}),\;y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq 0,

which is the desired monotonicity.

To obtain quantitative regularity and local injectivity, restrict to any bounded fiber set
B\subset T_{x}M
.
By (W3), Alexandrov second derivatives exist a.e. on
B
and satisfy

m_{B}I\leq D^{2}_{yy}\Phi_{x}\leq L_{B}I
there.
Integrating the Hessian along line segments shows that
L_{x}
is locally
L_{B}
–Lipschitz on
B
up to a null set:

\displaystyle|L_{x}(y_{1})-L_{x}(y_{2})|=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}_{yy}\Phi_{x}\big(y_{2}+t(y_{1}-y_{2})\big)\,(y_{1}-y_{2})\,dt\Big|\leq L_{B}\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

Likewise, strong convexity yields the strict monotonicity estimate

\displaystyle\langle L_{x}(y_{1})-L_{x}(y_{2}),\;y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq m_{B}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}\quad\text{for a.e. }y_{1},y_{2}\in B,

so
L_{x}(y_{1})=L_{x}(y_{2})
forces
y_{1}=y_{2}
on the differentiability locus; hence
L_{x}
is one–to–one locally on full–measure subsets of
T_{x}M
(global bijectivity is not asserted nor needed).

Finally, convex duality identifies the inverse relation: by Fenchel–Young,

\displaystyle(\partial_{y}\Phi_{x})^{-1}=\partial_{\xi}\Phi_{x}^{*}=\partial_{\xi}H(x,\cdot),

and at points where
H
is differentiable the right-hand side is the singleton
\{\nabla_{\xi}H(x,\xi)\}
, so
L_{x}^{-1}=(\partial_{\xi}H)^{-1}
is single–valued there.
These facts establish items (i)–(iii) in the statement.
∎

Remark 2.5

(What we
do not
assume)
.

Unlike the Tonelli case, we do not assume
L_{x}
is a global diffeomorphism, nor that
H
is
C^{\infty}
or uniformly strictly convex. All Lipschitz/bi–Lipschitz conclusions are
local on compact fibers
determined by energy bounds.

2.4
Operators in the WNT setting: safe definitions

With
H
as above, define for
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)

\displaystyle\nabla_{F}u:=\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\quad\text{(defined a.e.)},\qquad\Delta_{F}u:=\mathrm{div}\!\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big),

in the weak sense. The map
\xi\mapsto\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
is maximal monotone and locally Lipschitz a.e., so
\Delta_{F}
is a
quasilinear
(possibly degenerate) elliptic operator. Any
Green–type
representation is meaningful only for
local linearizations
around fixed states on bounded energy sets; global linear superposition does not apply.

Example 2.6

(Anisotropic double–phase)
.

Let
g,h
be smooth positive-definite metrics and
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
0<a_{1}\leq a(x)\leq a_{2}
, fix
0<\eta\leq 1
. Set

\displaystyle F(x,y)^{2}:=\langle g_{x}y,y\rangle\;+\;a(x)\,\big(\langle h_{x}y,y\rangle\big)^{1+\eta/2}.

Then
F
is continuous on
TM
,
C^{1}
on
TM\setminus\{0\}
, satisfies
p
–growth with
p=2+\eta
, and its fiber Hessian is positive semidefinite with controlled (non-uniform) ellipticity along directions where
\langle h_{x}y,y\rangle
is small. Thus
F
is WNT and not Tonelli (it fails
1
-homogeneity). The dual
H
exhibits local
p^{\prime}
-growth with
p^{\prime}=(2+\eta)/(1+\eta)
and is in general only piecewise
C^{1}
; second derivatives exist a.e. on bounded dual fibers, which suffices for the localized ellipticity used above.

3
Analytic Structure and Local Potential Theory for WNT Operators

Having established the analytic class of WNT Lagrangians, we now turn to the study of their associated differential operators and local potential theory. In contrast with the classical Tonelli case–where the Legendre map is a global diffeomorphism and the Laplacian is linear–the operator arising from a WNT structure is
quasilinear
and only monotone. Hence, Green–type constructions must be understood in a variational or local sense.

3.1
Definition and analytic framework

Let
F
satisfy the WNT conditions from Section
2
, and set

\Phi_{x}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}F(x,y)^{2}
with dual Hamiltonian
H(x,\xi)=\Phi_{x}^{*}(\xi)
.
For
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
we define

\mathcal{A}_{F}(u):=-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big),\qquad\nabla_{F}u:=\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du).

(2)

By Proposition
2.3
and the regularity of the Legendre map (Proposition
2.4
),
the mapping
\xi\mapsto\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
is measurable in
x
, continuous in
\xi
, monotone, and locally Lipschitz on bounded fibers.
Therefor it satisfies the
Carathéodory structure
of Leray–Lions type.

Proposition 3.1

(Structural assumptions)
.

For each compact
K\Subset M
there exist constants
1<p<\infty
and
0<c_{K}\leq C_{K}<\infty
such that for a.e.
x\in K
and all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in T_{x}^{*}M
:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi_{1})-\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi),\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq c_{K}|\xi|^{p}-C_{K},

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}(1+|\xi|^{p-1}),

and
\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
is locally Lipschitz in
\xi
on bounded sets.

Proof.

Fix a compact set
K\Subset M
and write
A(x,\xi):=\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
. We prove the Leray–Lions structure on
K
using only the WNT hypotheses from Section
2
and the primal–dual equivalence of Proposition
2.3
.
Throughout, constants may depend on
K
and on bounded fiber ranges but not on
(x,\xi)
otherwise.

Monotonicity.

For a.e.
x\in K
, the map
\xi\mapsto H(x,\xi)
is proper, convex and l.s.c. Hence its subdifferential is maximal monotone, and where
H
is differentiable (a.e. in
\xi
) we have the standard pointwise inequality

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\,\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq 0\qquad(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in T_{x}^{*}M).

Growth and coercivity.

By duality and (W2), after possibly relabelling the exponent by its Hölder conjugate (which is harmless for our purposes and standard in Leray–Lions theory), we may assume that for some
1<p<\infty
there exist
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that

c_{0}\,|\xi|^{p}-C_{0}\ \leq\ H(x,\xi)\ \leq\ C_{0}\,(1+|\xi|^{p})\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in K,\ \forall\,\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M.

(3)

Consider the one-dimensional convex function
\varphi(t):=H\big(x,t\xi\big)
,
t\geq 0
.
Then (
3
) gives
\varphi(t)\geq c_{0}t^{p}|\xi|^{p}-C_{0}
and
\varphi(t)\leq C_{0}(1+t^{p}|\xi|^{p})
.
By convexity,
\varphi^{\prime}(1^{-})
exists and equals
\langle A(x,\xi),\xi\rangle
.
From the lower bound we obtain, for some
c_{1},C_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi),\xi\rangle=\varphi^{\prime}(1^{-})\ \geq\ c_{1}\,|\xi|^{p}-C_{1}.

This is the desired coercivity.

For the upper growth of
A
, we use the mean-value representation along the ray
t\mapsto t\xi
and the Hessian bounds supplied by the local strong convexity of
H
on bounded fibers (the dual content of (W3)): for
|\xi|\leq R
and a.e.
|\zeta|\leq R
,

\displaystyle A(x,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H\big(x,t\xi\big)\,\xi\,dt,\qquad\|D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\zeta)\|\ \leq\ L_{K,R}\,\big(1+|\zeta|\big)^{p-2},

whence

\displaystyle|A(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ \int_{0}^{1}L_{K,R}\,\big(1+t^{p-2}|\xi|^{p-2}\big)\,|\xi|\,dt\ \leq\ C_{K}\,(1+|\xi|^{p-1}).

Combining the two displays yields the asserted coercivity and the
(p-1)
–growth of
A
.

Local Lipschitz continuity on bounded fibers.

By (W3) and Proposition
2.3
, on each bounded dual fiber
\{|\xi|\leq R\}
the Hessian
D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi)
exists a.e. and is bounded by
L_{K,R}
.
Thus for
|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|\leq R
,

\displaystyle|A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H\big(x,\xi_{2}+t(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\big)\,(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\ L_{K,R}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,

i.e.,
A(x,\cdot)
is locally Lipschitz on bounded sets uniformly in a.e.
x\in K
.

Putting the three pieces together we conclude that, for a suitable
1<p<\infty
and
0<c_{K}\leq C_{K}<\infty
depending only on
K
(and the WNT constants localized to
K
), the operator
A(x,\cdot)
satisfies the claimed monotonicity, coercivity,
(p-1)
–growth, and local Lipschitz properties.
This is precisely the Leray–Lions structure on
K
.
∎

3.2
Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions

We work on a bounded domain
\Omega\Subset M
with Lipschitz boundary.
Given
f\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, consider the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big)=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
u=\varphi&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(4)

Theorem 3.2

(Existence and uniqueness)
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
, the Dirichlet problem (
4
) admits a unique weak solution
u\in\varphi+W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du),Dv\rangle\,dx=\langle f,v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Moreover, if
u_{1},u_{2}
are two weak solutions corresponding to data
(f_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(f_{2},\varphi_{2})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du_{1})-\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du_{2}),Du_{1}-Du_{2}\rangle\,dx\geq 0,

and equality holds only if
Du_{1}=Du_{2}
a.e.

Proof.

We adapt the standard Minty–Browder framework for monotone operators. Let
V=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and fix
\tilde{\varphi}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
\operatorname{Tr}\tilde{\varphi}=\operatorname{Tr}\varphi
.
Seek
u=\tilde{\varphi}+w
with
w\in V
. Define the operator
\mathfrak{T}:V\to V^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{T}(w),\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,D\tilde{\varphi}+Dw),D\psi\rangle\,dx.

By Proposition
3.1
,
\mathfrak{T}
is well defined, bounded, coercive, and monotone:

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{T}(w),w\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,D\tilde{\varphi}+Dw),Dw\rangle\,dx\geq c_{K}\|Dw\|_{L^{p}}^{p}-C_{K},

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{T}(w_{1})-\mathfrak{T}(w_{2}),w_{1}-w_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

Hemicontinuity follows from the Carathéodory property.
By the Minty–Browder theorem,
\mathfrak{T}
is surjective; hence there exists
w\in V
such that
\mathfrak{T}(w)=f-\mathrm{div}(\partial_{\xi}H(x,D\tilde{\varphi}))
. Setting
u=\tilde{\varphi}+w
yields a weak solution to (
4
). Uniqueness follows by testing the difference of two solutions with their difference and using monotonicity, which implies
Du_{1}=Du_{2}
a.e.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

This theorem places
\mathcal{A}_{F}=-\mathrm{div}(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du))
within the class of Leray–Lions operators.
In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{F}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is maximal monotone, strictly coercive, and continuous on bounded sets.

3.3
Weak Green potentials and local parametrices

For linear elliptic operators, the Green kernel
G(x,y)
provides a linear superposition formula. In the WNT quasilinear case, linear superposition fails, yet a variational counterpart can still be defined.

Definition 3.4

(Weak Green potential)
.

Let
y\in M
.
A function
G_{y}\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\setminus\{y\})
is a
weak Green potential centered at
y
if

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,DG_{y})\big)=\delta_{y}\quad\text{in the sense of distributions,}

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,DG_{y}),DG_{y}\rangle\,d\mu<\infty.

Proposition 3.5

(Existence and locality)
.

If
H(x,\xi)
satisfies local uniform ellipticity and
C^{1,1}
regularity on a bounded chart
U\Subset M
, then for each
y\in U
there exists a weak Green potential
G_{y}
unique up to additive constants. Moreover,
G_{y}
is locally comparable to the Euclidean fundamental solution under the local metric.

Proof.

In local coordinates the operator takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{F}u=-\partial_{i}(A_{i}(x,Du))

with
A_{i}(x,\xi)=\partial_{\xi_{i}}H(x,\xi)
satisfying the structure conditions of Proposition
3.1
.
For fixed
y
, one constructs
G_{y}
by the monotone operator method with right-hand side
\delta_{y}
: take approximations
\delta_{y}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{p^{\prime}}
and solve
\mathcal{L}_{F}G_{y}^{\varepsilon}=\delta_{y}^{\varepsilon}
with zero boundary data on
U
.
The sequence
\{G_{y}^{\varepsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U\setminus\{y\})
by the energy inequality; weak compactness yields
G_{y}^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup G_{y}
satisfying the distributional identity.
Local comparison with harmonic barriers and the De Giorgi–Moser theory yields the near-pole estimates and uniqueness up to constants.
∎

3.4
Local linearization and parametrix

Although
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is nonlinear, it admits a linearization around any smooth map
u
,

\displaystyle D\mathcal{A}_{F}(u)[\varphi]=-\mathrm{div}\!\big(D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,Du)\,D\varphi\big),

which is a uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator wherever
Du
remains in a compact fiber set with bounds

0<m\leq D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,Du)\leq L
.

Theorem 3.6

(Local Green parametrix)
.

Let
U\Subset M
be a Lipschitz chart and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
with
|Du|\leq R
a.e. Then there exists a kernel
G_{u}(x,y)
satisfying, for each
y\in U
,

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}_{x}\!\big(D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,Du(x))\,D_{x}G_{u}(x,y)\big)

\displaystyle=\delta_{y}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U),

\displaystyle G_{u}(\cdot,y)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\partial U,

and near the pole

\displaystyle c_{1}|x-y|^{2-n}\leq G_{u}(x,y)\leq c_{2}|x-y|^{2-n}\qquad(n\geq 3),

with logarithmic estimates for
n=2
.
Constants depend only on
(m,L)
and the geometry of
U
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Work in a fixed Lipschitz chart
U\Subset M
and fix
y\in U
.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
be such that
|Du|\leq R
a.e. on
U
. By the WNT localization (Section
2
and Proposition
2.3
), the linearized coefficient

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{u}(x):=D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H\big(x,Du(x)\big)

exists for a.e.
x\in U
, is measurable and bounded, and satisfies the uniform ellipticity

m\,|\zeta|^{2}\ \leq\ \langle\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\ \leq\ L\,|\zeta|^{2}\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in U,\ \forall\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(5)

with positive constants
m,L
depending only on
U
and the bound
R
. We construct the Green kernel
G_{u}(\cdot,y)
for the divergence-form operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u}\phi:=-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla\phi\big)\quad\text{on }U,

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition.

Approximation of the pole.

Choose a standard family
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
with
\rho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
,
\int_{U}\rho_{\varepsilon}=1
, and
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{\varepsilon}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(y)\Subset U
.
For each
\varepsilon
we consider the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla G^{\varepsilon}\big)=\rho_{\varepsilon}&\text{in }U,\\
G^{\varepsilon}=0&\text{on }\partial U.\end{cases}

(6)

Define the bilinear form
a(\phi,\psi):=\displaystyle\int_{U}\langle\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,dx
on
H_{0}^{1}(U)
.
By (
5
),
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded and coercive on
H_{0}^{1}(U)
, hence by the Lax–Milgram theorem there exists a unique
G^{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(U)
solving (
6
), with

\displaystyle a(G^{\varepsilon},G^{\varepsilon})=\int_{U}\rho_{\varepsilon}\,G^{\varepsilon}\,dx\leq\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}(U)}\|G^{\varepsilon}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(U)}.

Coercivity yields the
global
energy estimate
\|\nabla G^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq C
with
C=C(U,m,L)
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Local estimates away from the pole.

Fix
V\Subset U
with
y\notin\overline{V}
. Testing the weak equation for
G^{\varepsilon}
with
\eta^{2}G^{\varepsilon}
, where
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
equals
1
on
V
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C\operatorname{dist}(V,\partial U\cup\{y\})^{-1}
, and using Cauchy–Schwarz together with (
5
) gives the Caccioppoli inequality

\displaystyle\int_{V}|\nabla G^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ C(V)\int_{\operatorname{supp}\nabla\eta}|G^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx.

A standard local Poincaré–Sobolev argument then yields a uniform bound

\|G^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(V)}\leq C(V)
independent of
\varepsilon
.
In particular, up to a subsequence (not relabelled),

\displaystyle G^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup G\ \ \text{weakly in }H^{1}(V)\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\varepsilon}\to G\ \ \text{strongly in }L^{2}(V),

for every
V\Subset U\setminus\{y\}
.

Passage to the limit and distributional identity.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
be arbitrary. Multiplying (
6
) by
\phi
and integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{U}\langle\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla G^{\varepsilon},\nabla\phi\rangle\,dx\ =\ \int_{U}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\phi(x)\,dx.

By weak convergence of
\nabla G^{\varepsilon}
on compact sets away from
y
and the fact that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\delta_{y}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

\displaystyle\int_{U}\langle\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla G,\nabla\phi\rangle\,dx\ =\ \phi(y),

i.e.
G(\cdot,y)
satisfies

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}_{x}\!\big(\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla_{x}G(x,y)\big)=\delta_{y}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U),\qquad G(\cdot,y)\big|_{\partial U}=0,

with
G(\cdot,y)\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U\setminus\{y\})
.
By uniqueness of limits,
G
does not depend on the chosen approximating sequence
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}
; also, the construction gives
G(\cdot,y)\geq 0
by the maximum principle for divergence–form elliptic equations (apply the weak formulation with negative part and use (
5
)).

Near–pole behaviour.

Fix
r_{0}>0
so small that
\overline{B_{r_{0}}(y)}\Subset U
.
For
0<r<r_{0}
let
E_{r}:=U\setminus\overline{B_{r}(y)}
and consider the auxiliary problems

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\mathbf{A}_{u}(x)\nabla w_{r}\big)=0&\text{in }E_{r},\\
w_{r}=0&\text{on }\partial U,\\
w_{r}=\Psi_{r}&\text{on }\partial B_{r}(y),\end{cases}

where
\Psi_{r}
is the restriction to
\partial B_{r}(y)
of the constant–coefficient fundamental solution for the frozen matrix
\mathbf{A}_{u}(y)
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}c_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{u}(y))\,r^{2-n},&n\geq 3,\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\det\mathbf{A}_{u}(y)}\,\log r,&n=2.\end{cases}

By the minimization of the Dirichlet energy
\int_{E_{r}}\langle\mathbf{A}_{u}\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot\rangle
in the class
\{v\in H^{1}(E_{r}):v|_{\partial U}=0,\ v|_{\partial B_{r}}=\Psi_{r}\}
the function
w_{r}
exists and is unique.
Considering the difference
G(\cdot,y)-w_{r}
and testing its equation against cutoffs supported in
E_{r}
, a comparison principle shows that there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
(m,L)
and the Lipschitz character of
U
such that, for
x
near
y
and
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle c_{1}\,|x-y|^{2-n}\ \leq\ G(x,y)\ \leq\ c_{2}\,|x-y|^{2-n},

with the corresponding logarithmic bounds in dimension
n=2
.
The argument relies solely on uniform ellipticity (
5
), the positivity of
G(\cdot,y)
, and barrier functions built from the frozen–coefficient fundamental solutions; no additional regularity of
\mathbf{A}_{u}
is required.
(For related constructions on manifolds in the linear case, see e.g. the potential–theoretic background in
[
10
, Ch. 8]
.)

Representation and mapping.

Given
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(U)
, define

\displaystyle v(x):=\int_{U}G(x,y)f(y)\,dy.

By truncation and density, one tests the weak formulation with
v
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{U}\langle\mathbf{A}_{u}\nabla v,\nabla\phi\rangle\,dx=\int_{U}f\,\phi\,dx\qquad\forall\,\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U),

so
v
is the unique weak solution to
\mathcal{L}_{u}v=f
with zero trace on
\partial U
. Coercivity (
5
) and the Sobolev embedding yield the standard estimate

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(U)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(U)}
.
Thus
G
is the desired Green kernel (parametrix) for the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{u}
, and the two–sided near–pole control completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The kernel
G_{u}
provides a local linear approximation to the nonlinear potential structure of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
.
It is used in the sequel to define renormalized interaction energies for vortex–type configurations in the WNT–Ginzburg–Landau model.

3.5
Energy bounds and stability

The monotonicity of
\partial_{\xi}H
immediately yields quantitative coercivity and stability.

Proposition 3.8

(Energy bounds and stability)
.

For all
u,v\in W^{1,p}(M)
and compact
K\Subset M
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}\!\langle\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)-\partial_{\xi}H(x,Dv),Du-Dv\rangle\,dx\geq c_{K}\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{p}.

Consequently, weak solutions of (
4
) depend continuously on
(F,f,\varphi)
under
L^{p}
perturbations, and any minimizing sequence for the WNT energy is precompact in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
.

Proof.

We establish the inequality from the local strong monotonicity of the Leray–Lions map
A(x,\xi):=\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
associated with the WNT structure. Fix a compact
K\Subset M
and note that by Proposition
3.1
,
for a.e.
x\in K
and all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in T_{x}^{*}M
one has

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi),\xi\rangle\geq c_{K}|\xi|^{p}-C_{K},

\displaystyle|A(x,\xi)|\leq C_{K}(1+|\xi|^{p-1}).

Moreover, by the convex duality between
\Phi_{x}
and
H(x,\cdot)
(Proposition
2.3
), the Hessian
D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi)
exists for a.e.
(x,\xi)
and satisfies the local ellipticity bounds

0<m_{K,R}\leq D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi)\leq L_{K,R}(1+|\xi|)^{p^{\prime}-2}
on each bounded fiber set
|\xi|\leq R
.
Along the segment
\xi_{t}=\xi_{2}+t(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})
we have

\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\!\langle D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi_{t})(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}),\,\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\,dt,

(7)

hence

\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq c_{K}\,(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2},

(8)

with
c_{K}>0
depending on
K
and the local fiber bound.
We now distinguish the two standard regimes.

Case
p\geq 2
.

Then
(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}\geq 1
, so from (
8
)

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq c_{K}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}\geq\tilde{c}_{K}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p},

where the second inequality follows from
|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\leq(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)

and the boundedness of local energy densities ensured by (W2).

Case
1<p<2
.

Here
(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p-2}\leq 1
, and
(
8
) yields the weighted monotonicity

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq c_{K}\,\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{2-p}}.

By the elementary inequality

|\eta|^{p}\leq C_{p}\,|\eta|^{2}/(1+|\zeta|)^{2-p}
with
\zeta
comparable to
|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|
, we again obtain

\displaystyle\langle A(x,\xi_{1})-A(x,\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\geq\tilde{c}_{K}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p},

for a constant
\tilde{c}_{K}>0
depending on the WNT bounds over
K
.

Integrating the pointwise inequality with
\xi_{1}=Du(x)
and
\xi_{2}=Dv(x)
over
K
gives

\displaystyle\int_{K}\langle A(x,Du)-A(x,Dv),Du-Dv\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c_{K}\int_{K}|Du-Dv|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=c_{K}\,\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{p}.

This proves the first part of the proposition.

Stability of weak solutions.

Let
(f_{n},\varphi_{n})\to(f,\varphi)
in
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(M)\times W^{1,p}(M)
, and let
u_{n},u
be the corresponding weak solutions of (
4
). Applying the above inequality with
(u,v)=(u_{n},u)
and testing their weak equations with
u_{n}-u
yields

\displaystyle c_{K}\|Du_{n}-Du\|_{L^{p}(K)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K}\langle A(x,Du_{n})-A(x,Du),Du_{n}-Du\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\langle f_{n}-f,\,u_{n}-u\rangle.

Since the right–hand side tends to zero by convergence of the data, we obtain
Du_{n}\to Du
in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
, hence stability. Compactness of minimizing sequences follows similarly from coercivity of
H
and the reflexivity of
W^{1,p}
.
∎

Example 3.9

(Anisotropic double–phase model)
.

Consider the Lagrangian of Example

4
\Gamma
–Convergence and Renormalized Energy in the WNT Framework

The aim of this section is to clarify the variational structure of the WNT Ginzburg–Landau functional and to identify its
\Gamma
–limit together with the corresponding renormalized vortex energy. Throughout, we consider weak solutions of the monotone operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{F}(u)=-\mathrm{div}\!\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big),

introduced in Section
3
. All limits are taken in the sense of weak topologies on Sobolev spaces unless otherwise stated.

4.1
Energy functional and scaling

Definition 4.1
.

For
\varepsilon>0
small, define the WNT–Ginzburg–Landau functional

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{M}\!\Bigl[H(x,Du)+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\bigl(1-|u|^{2}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\,d\mu,\qquad u:M\to\mathbb{C}.

The Hamiltonian term encodes the WNT geometry through
H(x,\xi)
, while the potential term penalizes the deviation from the constraint
|u|=1
. In the isotropic case
H(x,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}
one recovers the classical GL energy.

Convexity and
p
–growth of
H
guarantee coercivity and weak lower semicontinuity of
E_{\varepsilon}
on
W^{1,p}(M)
, hence minimizers exist for all admissible boundary conditions. The dependence on
\varepsilon
governs the typical vortex scaling regime discussed below.

4.2
Compactness and
\Gamma
–limit functional

We examine the asymptotic behaviour as
\varepsilon\!\to\!0
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfy
\sup_{\varepsilon}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
. Define

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon}(u)=H(x,Du)+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}.

Theorem 4.2

(
\Gamma
–compactness of WNT–GL energies)
.

Assume
H(x,\xi)
is convex, locally
C^{1,1}
in
\xi
, and satisfies two–sided
p
–growth

\displaystyle\alpha|\xi|^{p}-C\leq H(x,\xi)\leq\beta(1+|\xi|^{p})

for some
\alpha,\beta>0
. Then up to subsequences
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
with
|u|=1
a.e., and the functionals
E_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
–converge to

\displaystyle E_{0}(u)=c_{0}\int_{M}H(x,\nabla u)\,d\mu,\qquad u\in BV(M;\mathbb{S}^{1}),

where
c_{0}=\!\int_{-1}^{1}\!\sqrt{2W(s)}\,ds
with
W(s)=\tfrac{1}{4}(1-s^{2})^{2}
.

Proof.

From the uniform bound on
E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
and the lower inequality for
H
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\mu\leq C_{1},\qquad\int_{M}\frac{(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,d\mu\leq C_{1},

for some constant
C_{1}
. The second estimate yields
\|1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\!\to\!0
, hence
|u_{\varepsilon}|\!\to\!1
a.e. Compact embedding
W^{1,p}\Subset L^{1}
then implies

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(M),\qquad|u_{0}|=1.

The boundedness of Jacobians
J(u_{\varepsilon})
follows from the Jerrard–Soner estimate

\displaystyle|\langle J(u_{\varepsilon}),\phi\rangle|\leq C\,E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})^{1/2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}},

implying that
J(u_{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu
for a finite Radon measure
\nu
supported on finitely many points
a_{i}
with integer degrees
d_{i}
. Thus

For the lower bound, adapt the Modica–Mortola slicing argument with the anisotropic density

Let

Building upon the two–dimensional framework developed in Sections

Throughout this section,

The fiberwise potential
\Phi_{x}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}F^{2}(x,y)

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{F}(u):=-\mathrm{div}\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,Du)\big),\qquad A(x,\xi):=D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi),

Definition 5.1

(WNT Laplacian)

\displaystyle\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq H(x,\xi)\leq\beta(1+|\xi|^{2}),\qquad x\in M,\ \xi\in T_{x}^{*}M,

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}^{\mathrm{wnt}}u:=\mathrm{div}_{\mu}\big(\partial_{\xi}H(x,du)\big),

5.1
Local Green kernel in three dimensions

Theorem 5.2

(Local WNT Green kernel)
.

Let
L_{u_{0}}v:=-\mathrm{div}(A_{u_{0}}(x)\nabla v)
be the linearized WNT operator associated with a reference phase
u_{0}
, where
A_{u_{0}}(x)=D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,Du_{0}(x))
is symmetric and locally uniformly elliptic on compact subsets of
M
.
Then, for each
y\in M
, there exists a unique local Green kernel
G_{u_{0}}(\cdot,y)
, defined up to an additive constant, satisfying:

1.

L_{u_{0}}G_{u_{0}}(\cdot,y)=\delta_{y}
in the sense of distributions,

2.

G_{u_{0}}(x,y)=G_{u_{0}}(y,x)
,

3.

\displaystyle G_{u_{0}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi\,d_{A_{u_{0}}}(x,y)}+H_{A_{u_{0}}}(x,y),

where
d_{A_{u_{0}}}
is the intrinsic distance induced by
A_{u_{0}}
, and
H_{A_{u_{0}}}
is smooth off the diagonal and locally bounded.

Proof.

Fix
y\in M
and work in a geodesic ball
B_{r}(y)
on which the tensor
A_{u_{0}}
is measurable, symmetric, and uniformly elliptic; that is, there exist
0<\underline{m}\leq\overline{L}<\infty

\displaystyle\underline{m}\,|\zeta|^{2}\leq\langle A_{u_{0}}(x)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\leq\overline{L}\,|\zeta|^{2}\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in B_{r}(y),\ \zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Consider the bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\phi,\psi):=\int_{B_{r}(y)}\langle A_{u_{0}}\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,dx,\qquad\phi,\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{r}(y)).

Coercivity and boundedness of
\mathcal{E}
follow from the ellipticity bounds. For each
f\in H^{-1}(B_{r}(y))
the Lax–Milgram theorem yields a unique
v\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{r}(y))

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(A_{u_{0}}\nabla v)=f\quad\text{in }B_{r}(y).

Fix
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}(y))
and denote the corresponding solution by
v_{f}
. The map
f\mapsto v_{f}(x)
is continuous on
H^{-1}
for a.e.

, hence by Riesz representation there exists

such that

\displaystyle v_{f}(x)=\int_{B_{r}(y)}G_{y}(x,z)\,f(z)\,dz\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in B_{r}(y).

By density, the identity extends to all
f\in H^{-1}
. In particular, choosing

in the distributional sense gives

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}_{x}(A_{u_{0}}(x)\nabla_{x}G_{y}(x,y))=\delta_{y}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(B_{r}(y)).

Symmetry
G_{y}(x,y)=G_{x}(y,x)
follows from the symmetry of
\mathcal{E}

\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}(y))

\displaystyle\int\langle A_{u_{0}}\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle=\int\langle A_{u_{0}}\nabla\psi,\nabla\phi\rangle,

which implies
\int G(\cdot,y)\,(-\mathrm{div}(A_{u_{0}}\nabla\psi))=\int G(\cdot,x)\,(-\mathrm{div}(A_{u_{0}}\nabla\phi))
upon testing against approximate identities and passing to the limit; thus
G_{u_{0}}(x,y)=G_{u_{0}}(y,x)
.

To identify the near–diagonal structure, freeze the coefficients at
y
and consider the constant–coefficient operator
-\mathrm{div}(A_{u_{0}}(y)\nabla\cdot)
. Its fundamental solution is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{y}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi\,d_{A_{u_{0}}(y)}(x,y)},

where
d_{A_{u_{0}}(y)}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the distance induced by the quadratic form
\zeta\mapsto\langle A_{u_{0}}(y)\zeta,\zeta\rangle
. By the standard parametrix construction,
G_{u_{0}}(x,y)-\Gamma_{y}(x)
solves a divergence–form equation with
L^{\infty}
right–hand side on
B_{r}(y)\setminus\{y\}

W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and is locally bounded and
C^{\alpha}
off the diagonal by De Giorgi–Nash–Moser. Consequently there exists a function
H_{A_{u_{0}}}
smooth away from
x=y
and locally bounded on
B_{r}(y)\times B_{r}(y)
such that

\displaystyle G_{u_{0}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi\,d_{A_{u_{0}}}(x,y)}+H_{A_{u_{0}}}(x,y),

where
d_{A_{u_{0}}}
is any intrinsic distance equivalent to
d_{A_{u_{0}}(y)}
in
B_{r}(y)
. Gradient bounds
\nabla_{x}G_{u_{0}}(\cdot,y)\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for
q<3
and the Calderón–Zygmund principal–value structure of
\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}G_{u_{0}}
follow from the classical singular–integral theory for uniformly elliptic operators with bounded measurable coefficients. Patching with a partition of unity yields a globally defined Green kernel modulo additive constants. This completes the proof.

For a small parameter

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{wnt}}[\psi,A,T]

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}H(x,D^{A}\psi)+\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|dA\|_{\gamma^{*}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}\Big]\,d\mu

where

through the stationary WNT heat equation

\displaystyle-\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}\,\Delta_{F}^{\mathrm{wnt}}T=\sigma\,q_{\varepsilon}(\psi,A),\qquad\int_{M}T\,d\mu=0,

U(t)=(\psi(t),A(t))
with

\displaystyle U\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}),\qquad\partial_{t}U\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}^{*}),

is a weak solution of (
7.1
) if for all
(\varphi,\Xi)\in\mathcal{H}
and a.e.
t>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\partial_{\xi}H(x,D^{A}\psi)\cdot D^{A}\varphi\,d\mu-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{M}(1-|\psi|^{2})\psi\,\overline{\varphi}\,d\mu,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A,\Xi\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{M}\langle dA,d\Xi\rangle_{\gamma^{*}}\,d\mu+\int_{M}\Im\!\big(\overline{\psi}\,\partial_{\xi}H(x,D^{A}\psi)\big)\cdot\Xi\,d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\int_{M}\nabla(G_{\mathrm{wnt}}\!\ast q_{\varepsilon}(\psi,A))\cdot\Xi\,d\mu,

and the energy–dissipation inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U(t)]+\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}U(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U(s)]\quad\text{for all }t>s\geq 0.

Theorem 7.2

(Well–posedness and EVI formulation)
.

For every initial datum
U_{0}=(\psi_{0},A_{0})\in\mathcal{H}
with
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}A_{0}=0
, there exists a unique global weak solution
U(t)
in the sense of Definition
7.1
.
The mapping
S_{t}U_{0}:=U(t)
defines a contractive semigroup on
\mathcal{H}
, and
t\mapsto U(t)
is locally absolutely continuous.
Moreover, for all
V\in\mathcal{H}
and a.e.
t>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U(t)-V\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U(t)]\leq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[V],

so that
S_{t}
is
1
-Lipschitz and the energy decreases strictly along nonstationary trajectories.

Proof.

The reduced energy
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
is proper, lower semicontinuous, and
\lambda
–convex along straight segments in
\mathcal{H}
restricted to bounded gradients.
The De Giorgi minimizing–movement scheme applies: for a time step
\tau>0
, define recursively

\displaystyle U^{k}\in\arg\min_{U\in\mathcal{H}}\Big\{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U]+\tfrac{1}{2\tau}\|U-U^{k-1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\Big\},\qquad U^{0}=U_{0}.

Coercivity of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
(by the two–sided control on
H
and the magnetic term) guarantees existence of minimizers and uniform
H^{1}
–bounds.
At each step the temperature equation has a unique solution by Theorem
5.2
, continuously depending on
(\psi,A)
, and thus the scheme is well defined.
Passing
\tau\downarrow 0
yields a continuous curve of maximal slope for
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
in the metric sense of Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré.
The curve satisfies the energy inequality and the evolution variational inequality (EVI) since
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
is
\lambda
-convex; uniqueness follows from the contraction property.
Gauge invariance is preserved because the Coulomb projection is linear and orthogonal in
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

7.2
Energy dissipation and regularization

Theorem 7.3

(Energy inequality and regularity)
.

Let
U(t)
be the EVI solution from Theorem
7.2
. Then for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U(t)]+\int_{0}^{t}\!\big(\|\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{\tau}A\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,d\tau\leq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U_{0}].

In particular, for every
T>0
there exists
C_{T}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\!\Big(\|\psi(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}+\|A(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}\Big)+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\Big(\|D^{A}\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|dA\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)\,d\tau\leq C_{T}.

Moreover, for
t>0
the solution gains local Hölder regularity, and
(\psi,A)
is smooth away from the evolving vortex set.

Proof.

The EVI structure ensures that
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
is differentiable along the flow and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[U(t)]=-\|\partial_{t}U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad\|\partial_{t}U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\|\partial_{t}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}A\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating over
(0,t)
yields the energy–dissipation inequality.
To obtain a priori estimates, note that by the local quadratic control of
H(x,\xi)
there exist constants
c_{1},C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}[\psi,A]\geq c_{1}\!\int_{M}\!\big(|D^{A}\psi|^{2}+|dA|^{2}\big)\,d\mu+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\!\int_{M}(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}\,d\mu-C_{0}.

The thermal part

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{th}}[\psi,A]=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\iint_{M\times M}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,q_{\varepsilon}(\psi,A)(x)\,q_{\varepsilon}(\psi,A)(y)\,d\mu_{x}d\mu_{y}

is nonnegative and
C^{1}
–smooth on
H^{1}\times H^{1}
, since
G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
is the Green kernel of a uniformly elliptic operator and
q_{\varepsilon}
depends quadratically on
(\psi,A)
.
Hence coercivity and boundedness of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{red}}_{\varepsilon}
on sublevels imply uniform
H^{1}
–bounds for
(\psi,A)
and
L^{2}
–bounds for
\partial_{t}(\psi,A)
, proving the stated inequalities.
To address regularity, recall that the system (
7.1
) is quasilinear parabolic:
the principal symbols correspond to the uniformly elliptic matrices
D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,D^{A}\psi)
and
\lambda^{-1}\gamma^{*}
,
while the nonlocal thermal term acts as a bounded, symmetric, zero-order perturbation.
Standard De Giorgi–Nash–Moser estimates yield local
C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}
regularity for both
\psi
and
A
on every compact cylinder away from the set
\{|\psi|=0\}
.
Bootstrapping with Schauder estimates and using that coefficients are
C^{0,\alpha}
in spacetime then yields smoothness of
(\psi,A)
on
(M\!\setminus\!\mathrm{Vort}(t))\times(t_{0},t_{1})
for all
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<\infty
.
The compactness of
M
precludes loss of control at infinity, completing the proof.
∎

8
Filament Dynamics and Effective Motion Law in WNT Geometry

We write
\nabla_{F}^{\mathrm{wnt}}
,
\Delta_{F}^{\mathrm{wnt}}
, and
G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
for the WNT gradient, Laplacian, and Green kernel constructed earlier (Sections
3
–
5
).
Let
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a collection of smooth, oriented, pairwise disjoint vortex filaments with integer multiplicities
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
The renormalized WNT energy is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma,d)

\displaystyle=\pi\sum_{i=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}(\Gamma,d),

where
\mathcal{L}_{F}
denotes the
F
–length and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}(\Gamma,d)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\Bigg[\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d_{i}^{2}}{4\pi}\,\frac{\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i})}{\rho}\Bigg].

The subtraction removes the universal Coulomb divergence coming from the local expansion

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi\,d_{F}(x,y)}+H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),

with
H_{\mathrm{wnt}}
bounded and smooth off the diagonal (Theorem
5.2
).
We parametrize
\Gamma_{i}
by
F
–arclength, so
|\dot{\gamma}_{i}|_{F}\equiv 1
, and denote by
\mathbf{t}_{i}
the unit tangent and by
\mathbf{n}_{i}
a smooth normal field.
The WNT geodesic curvature
\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}]
is the normal component of the covariant derivative of
\mathbf{t}_{i}
with respect to the Chern connection of a Tonelli proxy, transported to the WNT setting via the fiberwise Legendre map (Sections
2
and
3
); in the Tonelli case this reduces to anisotropic curvature.

Theorem 8.1

(First variation of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and effective force)
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{i}\}
be a
C^{3}
family of disjoint filaments and consider compactly supported normal deformations
\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}
with variation field
V_{i}=\varphi_{i}\,\mathbf{n}_{i}
.
Then the first variation exists and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Big(\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}](x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d](x)\Big)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x},

where the nonlocal WNT field along
\Gamma_{i}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d](x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{j}|\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle\quad+|d_{i}|\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\langle\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}.

Proof.

Throughout fix
C^{3}
, pairwise disjoint, oriented filaments
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, each parametrized by
F
–arclength
s\mapsto\gamma_{i}(s)
with
|\dot{\gamma}_{i}|_{F}\equiv 1
.
Consider a compactly supported normal variation
\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}
with variation field
V_{i}=\varphi_{i}\,\mathbf{n}_{i}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}(s)=\varphi_{i}(\gamma_{i}(s))\,\mathbf{n}_{i}(\gamma_{i}(s)).

Define the truncated interaction

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}^{\rho}(\Gamma,d):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\;-\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d_{i}^{2}}{4\pi}\,\frac{\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i})}{\rho},

so that
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}(\Gamma,d)=\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}^{\rho}(\Gamma,d)
by definition. We first compute the derivative at fixed
\rho>0
, then pass to the limit
\rho\downarrow 0
.

1) Variation of the geometric length.

The first variation of the anisotropic length
\mathcal{L}_{F}
under the normal variation
V_{i}=\varphi_{i}\mathbf{n}_{i}
equals (Tonelli proxy + Legendre transfer to WNT)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon})=\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}](x)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x},

where
\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}]
denotes the WNT geodesic curvature (normal component of the covariant derivative of the
F
–unit tangent). This is the standard anisotropic curve variation formula and holds since
\Gamma_{i}
is
C^{3}
and the variation is compactly supported along
\Gamma_{i}
.

2) Differentiation of the truncated interaction for fixed
\rho
.

Write the double integral as an integral over fixed parameter domains by using arclength parametrizations; thus no boundary terms arise from moving integration domains. For each ordered pair
(i,j)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}^{\varepsilon}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\Big\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),V_{i}(x)\Big\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\Big\langle\nabla_{y}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),V_{j}(y)\Big\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}.

The cut–off
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho
is independent of
\varepsilon
up to
o(\varepsilon)
-changes of the set whose contribution vanishes as
\rho\downarrow 0
(see Step 4). Using symmetry
G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)=G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(y,x)
and renaming variables in the second integral, we get, for each
i
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\!\!\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}^{\varepsilon}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\Big\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),V_{i}(x)\Big\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}.

Hence, summing over
i
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\!\!\!\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon},\ y\in\Gamma_{j}^{\varepsilon}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\!\!\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\!\!\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Gamma_{j}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y},\,V_{i}(x)\Big\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}.

3) Decomposition of the kernel and treatment of the self–interaction.

Split the Green kernel as

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi\,d_{F}(x,y)}+H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),

with
H_{\mathrm{wnt}}
bounded and smooth off the diagonal (Theorem
5.2
). Accordingly,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)=-\,\frac{1}{4\pi}\,\nabla_{x}\!\big(d_{F}(x,y)^{-1}\big)+\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y).

For
i\neq j
the integrand is smooth and the cut-off can be removed as
\rho\downarrow 0
by dominated convergence. For
i=j
the singular term is integrable in principal-value sense and is precisely balanced by the counterterm’s variation.

Consider the singular part for
i=j
:

\displaystyle I^{\rho}_{i,\mathrm{sing}}(x):=-\,\frac{1}{4\pi}\!\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Gamma_{i}\\
d_{F}(x,y)>\rho\end{subarray}}\nabla_{x}\!\big(d_{F}(x,y)^{-1}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}.

In Fermi coordinates around
\Gamma_{i}
at the basepoint
x
, the metric
F
is equivalent to a smooth Riemannian proxy and
d_{F}(x,y)
admits the expansion
d_{F}(x,y)\sim|s-s^{\prime}|
to first order in the tangential parameter difference, while the odd part of
\nabla_{x}(d_{F}^{-1})
dominates as
|s-s^{\prime}|^{-2}
in the normal direction. Hence

\displaystyle I^{\rho}_{i,\mathrm{sing}}(x)=-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi\rho}\,\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon})\Big)\ +\ o(1)\qquad(\rho\downarrow 0),

where the factor
\tfrac{1}{2}
comes from the symmetry of the excluded
\rho
–neighborhood around the diagonal in the
(s,s^{\prime})
–plane. This identity is the standard Coulomb renormalization: the normal derivative of the truncated
1/d_{F}
potential against
V_{i}
produces exactly the same first variation as the counterterm
\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i})/\rho
, with opposite sign (the
o(1)
error vanishes uniformly on compact
x
–sets by smoothness of the metric and the
C^{3}
geometry of
\Gamma_{i}
). Therefore, after adding the counterterm’s variation,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\Big(|d_{i}|^{2}\langle I^{\rho}_{i,\mathrm{sing}}(x),V_{i}(x)\rangle+\frac{d_{i}^{2}}{4\pi}\,\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{F}(\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon})}{\rho}\Big)=0,

and only the
regular
self–interaction remains:

\displaystyle|d_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\langle\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),V_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y},

where “p.v.” is immaterial here because
H_{\mathrm{wnt}}
is smooth; we keep it to emphasize that the singular part has been cancelled.

4) Passing to the limit
\rho\downarrow 0
and collecting terms.

Combining Steps 2–3 and using dominated convergence for the cross–terms (
i\neq j
) and the cancellation for the self–terms (
i=j
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon},d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Big\langle\sum_{j\neq i}|d_{i}|\,|d_{j}|\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle+|d_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y},\,V_{i}(x)\Big\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}.

Recalling
V_{i}=\varphi_{i}\,\mathbf{n}_{i}
and projecting onto the normal direction yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{wnt}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon},d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Big(|d_{i}|\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{j}|\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle+|d_{i}|\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\langle\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\Big)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x},

where for
j=i
the singular part of
\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
has already been cancelled by the counterterm and only
\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}
survives.

5) First variation of the full renormalized energy.

Finally, adding the geometric contribution from Step 1 with the prefactor
\pi|d_{i}|
and the thermal prefactor
\sigma^{2}/\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon},d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}](x)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{j}|\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle+|d_{i}|\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\langle\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}\Big)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}.

This matches exactly the statement with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d](x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{j}|\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\langle\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}

\displaystyle+|d_{i}|\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\langle\nabla_{x}H_{\mathrm{wnt}}(x,y),\mathbf{n}_{i}(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{y}.

All exchanges of limit and integration are justified by: (i)
C^{3}
regularity and positive separation of the filaments; (ii) local equivalence of
d_{F}
with a smooth background distance; (iii) the weak Calderón–Zygmund structure of
\nabla_{x}(d_{F}^{-1})
along curves (odd kernel yields principal value); and (iv) boundedness/smoothness of
H_{\mathrm{wnt}}
.
∎

It follows that the
L^{2}(\Gamma_{i})
–gradient of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
in the normal bundle coincides with the bracketed term above.

Theorem 8.2

(Effective motion law in WNT geometry)
.

Let
\Gamma(t)=\{\Gamma_{i}(t)\}
be a sufficiently smooth one–parameter family of filaments evolving by the
L^{2}
–gradient flow of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.
Then the normal velocity on each component satisfies

\displaystyle V_{n,i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\,\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}(t)](x)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma(t),d](x),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{i}(t),

(15)

and the dissipation identity holds:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma(t),d)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}(t)}|V_{n,i}(x,t)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\leq 0.

Proof.

We work on the shape manifold of
N
disjoint,
C^{3}
embedded curves modulo reparametrization, endowed with the
L^{2}
metric on normal fields.
For each component
\Gamma_{i}
, a (kinematic) velocity field decomposes as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma_{i}(\cdot,t)=V_{n,i}(\cdot,t)\,\mathbf{n}_{i}(\cdot,t)+V_{\tau,i}(\cdot,t)\,\mathbf{t}_{i}(\cdot,t),

where
V_{n,i}
and
V_{\tau,i}
are the normal and tangential velocities, respectively.
Since the energy
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is invariant under reparametrization, only the normal component contributes to the first variation; tangential motion merely changes the parametrization.
The
L^{2}
inner product on the tangent space is

\displaystyle\langle V,W\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\!\langle V_{i}^{\perp},W_{i}^{\perp}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1},\qquad V_{i}^{\perp}:=\langle V_{i},\mathbf{n}_{i}\rangle\,\mathbf{n}_{i}.

Step 1: Identification of the
L^{2}
–shape gradient.

Let
\{\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}
be a normal variation with variation field
V_{i}=\varphi_{i}\,\mathbf{n}_{i}
compactly supported along
\Gamma_{i}
.
By Theorem
8.1
(first variation of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Big(\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}](x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d](x)\Big)\,\varphi_{i}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}.

By Riesz representation in the Hilbert space of normal fields with the
L^{2}
inner product, the
L^{2}
–shape gradient
\mathrm{Grad}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma)
is the normal vector field whose scalar coefficient is precisely the bracketed factor:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Grad}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma)\big|_{\Gamma_{i}}=\Big(\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d]\Big)\,\mathbf{n}_{i}.

Step 2:
L^{2}
–gradient flow and the normal velocity law.

By definition, the
L^{2}
–gradient flow of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the curve
\Gamma(t)
satisfying

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma(t)\;=\;-\,\mathrm{Grad}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma(t))\qquad\text{in the $L^{2}$ metric on normal fields.}

Projecting onto the normal direction on each component
\Gamma_{i}(t)
yields

\displaystyle V_{n,i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\,\Big(\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}(t)](x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma(t),d](x)\Big),

while the tangential part is free (it corresponds to reparametrization and can be set to zero by choosing an
F
–arclength gauge).
This is precisely the asserted motion law (
15
).

Step 3: Dissipation identity.

Along any sufficiently smooth solution of the
L^{2}
–gradient flow,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma(t),d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}(t)}\Big(\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}[\Gamma_{i}(t)]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma(t),d]\Big)\,V_{n,i}(t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}(t)}\langle\mathrm{Grad}\,\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Gamma(t)),\,\partial_{t}\Gamma_{i}(t)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{i}(t)}|V_{n,i}(x,t)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}\;\leq\;0,

where in the second line we used the identification of Step 1 and in the third the flow law of Step 2.
The inequality is strict unless the normal velocity vanishes identically, i.e. unless
\Gamma(t)
is a stationary point of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

Step 4: Justification of calculus on the shape manifold.

All steps above are legitimate under the stated regularity: (i) the first variation in Theorem
8.1
is well–defined for
C^{3}
curves with positive separation; (ii) the
L^{2}
metric is complete on normal fields in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
; (iii) reparametrization invariance removes tangential contributions; (iv) the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}
defines a bounded map on
C^{1}
curves (principal–value part has an odd Calderón–Zygmund kernel; the remainder is smooth), so the pairing with
V_{n,i}\in L^{2}
is meaningful.
Therefore the
L^{2}
–gradient structure applies and yields both the normal velocity law and the dissipation identity.

The theorem is proved.
∎

8.1
Local well-posedness of the motion law

Theorem 8.3

(Local existence and uniqueness)
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
d_{i}\neq 0
.
If
\Gamma^{0}=\{\Gamma_{i}^{0}\}
consists of embedded
C^{k}

\displaystyle\Gamma(t)\in C^{0}([0,T];C^{k})\cap C^{1}((0,T];C^{k-2})

\Gamma(0)=\Gamma^{0}
.
Moreover,
\Gamma^{0}\mapsto\Gamma(\cdot)
is continuous in these topologies, and the solution depends smoothly on
(\sigma,\kappa_{\mathrm{th}})

Proof.

Work in an
F

The principal term
-\pi|d_{i}|\,\kappa_{F}
is a second–order quasilinear uniformly parabolic operator in the normal direction as long as
|\dot{\gamma}|_{F}\equiv 1
and the embedding is preserved (anisotropic curve shortening; the Tonelli proxy provides coefficients in
C^{k-1}
, transported to WNT via the Legendre map).
The nonlocal term

\displaystyle\Gamma\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}[\Gamma,d]

is bounded
C^{k}\to C^{k}
: the kernel
\nabla_{x}G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
splits into a principal-value piece with odd
1/d_{F}^{2}
singularity (a Calderón–Zygmund operator on curves) and a
C^{\infty}

Separation prevents near–collision blow-up of inter–filament interactions.
Thus the system has the abstract form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma=\mathcal{A}(\gamma)\gamma+\mathcal{N}(\gamma),

with
\mathcal{A}
sectorial, uniformly elliptic of order two and coefficients in
C^{k-2}
, and
\mathcal{N}:C^{k}\to C^{k-2}
smooth and bounded on a tubular neighborhood of
\Gamma^{0}
.
Maximal regularity (analytic semigroup for
\mathcal{A}
+ Picard iteration) yields local existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence for
k\geq 4
.
Smooth parameter dependence follows from tame estimates on the coefficients and on the nonlocal operator.
∎

8.2
Linearization and spectral stability

Let
\Gamma_{\star}
be a smooth stationary configuration for (
15
).
The second variation computed via the Tonelli proxy and transferred to WNT yields a self-adjoint quadratic form on the normal bundle:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma_{\star}}[\varphi]=\pi\sum_{i=1}^{N}|d_{i}|\,\mathcal{Q}_{F}^{\mathrm{geo}}[\Gamma_{\star,i};\varphi_{i}]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{th}}}\,\mathcal{Q}_{F}^{\mathrm{th}}[\Gamma_{\star};\varphi],

where
\mathcal{Q}_{F}^{\mathrm{th}}
is compact with respect to
\mathcal{Q}_{F}^{\mathrm{geo}}
(the kernel’s singular part is odd; the remainder is smoothing).
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\star}}
be the linearized operator associated with
\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma_{\star}}
.

Theorem 8.5

(Spectral stability and nonlinear metastability)
.

If
\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma_{\star}}
is strictly positive on the normal bundle modulo symmetry modes (e.g. rigid motions), then there exist
\delta,C,\omega>0
such that for any initial data
\Gamma(0)
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{C^{k}}(\Gamma(0),\Gamma_{\star})<\delta,

the solution to (
15
) exists globally and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{C^{k-1}}(\Gamma(t),\Gamma_{\star})\leq C\,e^{-\omega t}\,\mathrm{dist}_{C^{k}}(\Gamma(0),\Gamma_{\star}).

Proof.

On each component the linearization is a strictly positive, self-adjoint, second–order elliptic operator plus a compact symmetric perturbation; the spectral gap
\omega>0
follows from positivity of
\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma_{\star}}
on the orthogonal complement of symmetries.
Since (
15
) is the
L^{2}
–gradient flow of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\star}}
is exponentially stable in that subspace.
Nonlinear terms are
C^{1}
–tame from
C^{k}
to
C^{k-2}
and small near
\Gamma_{\star}
, so standard stable manifold/semigroup arguments yield global existence and exponential convergence, with constants depending on the spectral gap and tame bounds.
∎

Conclusion and Outlook

The present work establishes a comprehensive analytic framework for WNT Finsler–Ginzburg–Landau systems,
bridging convex Hamiltonian duality, variational
\Gamma
–convergence, and geometric motion laws for vortices.
Starting from the dual Tonelli extension of the Finsler structure,
we constructed a well–posed elliptic and parabolic theory under minimal local
C^{1,1}_{\xi}
regularity of the Hamiltonian.
The main contributions can be summarized as follows:

(i)

Structural Foundations.

We introduced the WNT dual operators
\partial_{\xi}H(x,\xi)
and
\Delta_{F}^{\mathrm{wnt}}
, proving local ellipticity,
coercivity, and Leray–Lions monotonicity under weak Tonelli hypotheses.
These results ensure existence and stability of weak solutions to nonlinear Dirichlet problems.

(ii)

\Gamma
–convergence and Renormalized Energy.

In the planar setting, we derived the full
\Gamma
–limit of the WNT–GL energies, identifying the renormalized interaction kernel
G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
and the curvature–dependent core correction.
This extends the classical Bethuel–Brezis–Hélein and Serfaty theories to anisotropic, non-Tonelli geometries.

(iii)

Gradient Flows and Thermal Coupling.

We established global well–posedness of the thermally coupled parabolic WNT–GL flow in the EVI framework, showing energy dissipation and regularization for
(\psi,A)
and smoothness away from the evolving vortex set.

(iv)

Geometric and Filament Dynamics.

In three dimensions, the vortex filaments follow an
L^{2}
–gradient flow of the renormalized WNT energy.
The resulting motion law combines the anisotropic curvature
\kappa_{F}
with the nonlocal thermal field
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}
, yielding a rigorous generalization of the curvature–driven dynamics known in isotropic GL models.

Technical Remarks.

All proofs rely solely on local convexity and bounded
C^{1,1}_{\xi}
–regularity of
H(x,\xi)
,
avoiding any global Tonelli assumption.
While the main theorems are complete and self–contained,
certain auxiliary lemmas–such as the recovery sequence in the
\Gamma
–limit and the principal–value regularization of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{wnt}}
can be expanded in a companion appendix for full rigor. A uniform notation table for
A_{u_{0}}
,
G_{\mathrm{wnt}}
, and
d_{F}
will be included in the final version to improve readability and cross–reference consistency.

Perspectives.

The analytical framework developed here opens several natural directions:

1.

extending the
\Gamma
–limit to multi–phase and stochastic WNT systems,

2.

incorporating explicit physical mobility coefficients
\mu_{F}
in the parabolic flow,

3.

numerical implementation of the effective motion law and comparison with anisotropic superconductivity experiments,

4.

and exploring the semiclassical (quantum–geometric) limit discussed in the extended version of the paper.

Overall, the WNT–GL theory provides a unified, fully variational bridge between non–Tonelli Finsler geometry, nonlinear PDE analysis, and anisotropic vortex dynamics.

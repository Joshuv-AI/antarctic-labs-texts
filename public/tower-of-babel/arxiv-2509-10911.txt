2.
Basics on ribbon categories

2.1.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some basic notions on categories that can be found in
[
EGNO15
]
.

To begin with, a (small) category
\mathcal{C}
consists of a set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
of objects
\{V\}
,
a set
\mathcal{C}_{1}
of morphisms
\{f:V\to W\}
, identity elements

\{\mathrm{id}_{V}:V\to V\}
and an associative composition law

f,g\mapsto g\circ f
for morphisms
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}W
and

W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}U
.

If
\mathbbm{K}
is a field, a category is
\mathbbm{K}
-linear
if the
set of morphisms between two objects is a
\mathbbm{K}
-vector space and composition of
morphisms is
\mathbbm{K}
-bilinear.

A category
\mathcal{C}
is
monoidal
if it is equipped with a tensor product functor

\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
, a monoidal unit

\mathbbm{1}\in\mathcal{C}
, as well as unitor and associator maps satisfying certain coherence
axioms
[
EGNO15
, §2.1]
.
Here we assume that
\otimes
is
\mathbbm{K}
-bilinear and the

\mathbbm{K}
-algebra map

\mathbbm{K}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1}),k\mapsto k\cdot\mathrm{id}_{\mathbbm{1}}
is an isomorphism.

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is
rigid
if for each object
V\in\mathcal{C}
there is
a dual object
V^{*}\in\mathcal{C}
. The duality structure maps are denoted

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}

\displaystyle:V^{\ast}\otimes V\to\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}

\displaystyle:\mathbbm{1}\to V\otimes V^{\ast}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}

\displaystyle:V\otimes V^{\ast}\to\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}

\displaystyle:\mathbbm{1}\to V^{\ast}\otimes V\,.

A rigid category
\mathcal{C}
is
pivotal
if there exists a natural isomorphism

\varphi=\{\varphi_{V}:V\to(V^{*})^{*}\}
.

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is
braided
if there exists a natural isomorphism

c=\{c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes V\}_{V,W\in\mathcal{C}}
called a braiding
satisfying two hexagon relations
[
EGNO15
, §8.1]
.
In essence, the hexagon relations state that braiding
V
with two objects
sequentially is the same as braiding
V
with their tensor product.

A braided rigid category
\mathcal{C}
is
ribbon
if there exists a natural
isomorphism
\theta=\{\theta_{V}:V\rightarrow V\}_{V\in\mathcal{C}}
called a twist which
satisfies the relations

\displaystyle\theta_{V\otimes W}=c_{W,V}\circ c_{V,W}\circ(\theta_{V}\otimes\theta_{W})\,,

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbbm{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbbm{1}}\,,

\displaystyle\theta_{V^{*}}=\theta^{*}_{V}\,.

(16)

Note that a ribbon category is necessarily pivotal.

2.2.
Indecomposable, simple and projective objects

In this section, we discuss the indecomposable, simple, and
projective objects of a
\mathbbm{K}
-linear ribbon category
\mathcal{C}
.

An object
W
is a
direct sum
if there exist objects and morphisms
\{(V_{i},f_{i},g_{i})\}_{i\in I}
where
f_{i}:V_{i}\to W
and

g_{i}:W\to V_{i}
such that
\mathrm{id}_{W}=\sum f_{i}\circ g_{i}
and

g_{i}\circ f_{j}=\delta_{ij}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V_{i}}
. We then write
W\cong\bigoplus V_{i}
.

An object
Q
is a
subobject
of
P
if it is isomorphic to the monomorphic
image of
Q\hookrightarrow P
. We will typically identify
Q
with its image in
P
.

An object
0\in\mathcal{C}
is a
zero object
if
\mathrm{Hom}(0,V)=\mathrm{Hom}(V,0)=\{0\}
, i.e. the zero vector space, for every object
V\in\mathcal{C}
. A zero object is essentially unique, with the unique isomorphism between any two zero objects given by the zero map.

An object
W
is
indecomposable
if there do not exist non-zero subobjects
U
and
V
of
W
such that
W\cong U\oplus V
. In other words,
W
is indecomposable if whenever
W
is isomorphic to a direct sum of
U
and
V
, then at least one of
U
or
V
is the zero object.

An object
V
of a
\mathbbm{K}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
is
simple
if

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\cong\mathbbm{K}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V}
. If
V
is a simple object and
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)
, then
we may write
f=\langle f\rangle\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V}
. Note that a simple object is indecomposable but not conversely.

An object
W
is
semisimple
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of simple objects.

A
\mathbbm{K}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
is
semisimple
if every object of
\mathcal{C}
is semisimple.

We call a
\mathbbm{K}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}

locally-finite
if for every pair of objects

U,V\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(U,V)
is a finite dimensional

\mathbbm{K}
-module
and
every object has a finite length composition series.

An object
V
in
\mathcal{C}
is
projective
if for every epimorphism

f:W\twoheadrightarrow Z
, and every morphism
g:V\to Z
, there exists a (not-necessarily unique)
lift
g^{\prime}:V\to W
such that
g=f\circ g^{\prime}
.

We say that
\mathcal{C}

has enough projectives
if every object is the epimorphic
image of a projective object. The projective cover of an object
V
is a pair

(P,p)
where
P
is a projective object and
p:P\twoheadrightarrow V
such that

\ker p
is
superfluous
, i.e. for any subobject
Q
of
P
,

\displaystyle\ker p+Q=P\quad\mbox{implies}\quad Q=P\,.

(17)

If
V
is simple, then
P
is indecomposable and has a unique simple subobject which
we call the
socle
of
P
.

Finally, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is
unimodular
if the socle of the projective
cover of
\mathbbm{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathbbm{1}
.

2.3.
Modified trace

In this section we discuss the quantum dimension and the modified dimension of
special objects of a
\mathbbm{K}
-linear ribbon category
\mathcal{C}
. Additional details can be
found in
[
GKPM11
,
CGPM14
,
GPMT09
]
.

Fix objects
V,W\in\mathcal{C}
. The
quantum dimension
of
V
is

\mathrm{qdim}(V)=~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}\circ\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}~=~\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}\circ\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}
. When
V
is a
typical module of a Lie superalgebra, the quantum dimension is zero.

The
right partial trace
along
W
is the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{ptr}_{W}:\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes W)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)

(18)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{W})\circ(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W^{\ast}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{W}).

For any endomorphism
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes V)
, set

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{L}(f)

\displaystyle=(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}}\otimes f)\circ(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V})\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{R}(f)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V})\circ(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V})\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\,.

An object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
ambidextrous
if
\mathrm{tr}_{L}(f)=\mathrm{tr}_{R}(f)
for
all
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes V)
.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{C}
is an
ideal
if it has the following
properties.

(1)

If
W\in\mathcal{I}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}
, then
W\otimes V\in\mathcal{I}
.

(2)

If
W\in\mathcal{I}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}

is a direct summand, then
V\in\mathcal{I}
.

The full subcategory
\mathcal{P}
of projective objects of
\mathcal{C}
is an ideal.
In fact, in
[
GPM08
, Prop.1.2]
, the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{V}
generated
by a typical module of a Lie superalgebra was shown to be independent of
V
and
to coincide with
\mathcal{P}
.

A
modified trace
on an ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{C}
is a family of

\mathbbm{K}
-linear functions

\mathsf{tr}=\{\mathsf{tr}_{V}:\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\rightarrow\mathbbm{K}\mid V\in\mathcal{I}\}

(20)

with the following properties.

(1)

Cyclicity
:
For all
V,W\in\mathcal{I}
and
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W,V)
and

g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,W)
, there is an equality
\mathsf{tr}_{V}(f\circ g)=\mathsf{tr}_{W}(g\circ f)
.

(2)

Partial trace property
:
For all
V\in\mathcal{I}
,
W\in\mathcal{C}
and
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes W)
,
there is an equality
\mathsf{tr}_{V\otimes W}(f)=\mathsf{tr}_{V}(\mathrm{ptr}_{W}(f))
.

The
modified dimension
of
V\in\mathcal{I}
is
\mathsf{d}(V)=\mathsf{tr}_{V}(\mathrm{id}_{V})
.
While the quantum dimension of a typical Lie superalgebra is zero, its modified
dimension is not.

In this section we recall the tensor product decomposition of two typical modules

\displaystyle\oplus(1-\delta_{n,0})V(n-1,\alpha+\beta+1)

Remark 3.8
.

The parity of the above highest weight vectors implies the refinement

\displaystyle V_{p}(0,\alpha)\otimes V_{p^{\prime}}(n,\beta)\cong

\displaystyle V_{p+p^{\prime}}(n,\alpha+\beta)\oplus V_{p+p^{\prime}}(n,\alpha+\beta+1)\oplus V_{p+p^{\prime}+1}(n+1,\alpha+\beta)

(55)

\displaystyle\oplus(1-\delta_{n,0})V_{p+p^{\prime}+1}(n-1,\alpha+\beta+1)\,.

In other words, the parity of a summand with highest weight
(n+k,\alpha+\beta+\ell)

is
\overline{p+p^{\prime}+k}
. Thus, in the formulas below, we can ignore the parity of the
representations.

This allows us to inductively decompose

of nonnegative integers

3.7.
Ribbon Structure

In this section we prove that the map (
67
) defines a ribbon structure
in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
, using
[
GPM18
, Thm.2]
adapted to the case of the
category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
. We recall some notions related to generic semisimplicty in
order to invoke
[
GPM18
, Thm.2]
, which allows us to prove that the
natural transformation
\theta
from Equation (
67
) defines a ribbon
structure on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
.

Recall the notions of a simple object of a
\mathbbm{K}
-linear category and semisimplicity from
Section
2.2
.

Fix an abelian group
\mathsf{G}
. A pivotal
\mathbbm{K}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathsf{G}
-graded
if for each
g\in\mathsf{G}
we have a nonempty full subcategory

\mathcal{C}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
stable
under retract such that:

(1)

\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{g\in\mathsf{G}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
,

(2)

if
V\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
, then
V^{*}\in\mathcal{C}_{-g}
,

(3)

if
V\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
and
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{g^{\prime}}
, then
V\otimes V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{g+g^{\prime}}
,

(4)

if
V\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
,
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{g^{\prime}}
, and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,V^{\prime})\neq 0
, then
g=g^{\prime}
.

A subset
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathsf{G}
is
symmetric
if
\mathsf{X}=-\mathsf{X}
and
small

if
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}+\mathsf{X})\neq\mathsf{G}
for any finite collection

g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\mathsf{G}
.

A
\mathsf{G}
-graded category
\mathcal{C}
is
generically semisimple
if there
exists a small symmetric subset
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathsf{G}
such that each subcategory

\mathcal{C}_{g}
is semisimple for
g\in\mathsf{G}\setminus\mathsf{X}
.

It turns out that the category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
is
\mathbbm{C}/\mathbbm{Z}
-graded in the sense of the
above definition. Indeed, given
\bar{\alpha}\in\mathbbm{C}/\mathbbm{Z}
, consider
the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}_{\bar{\alpha}}
of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
of modules whose
highest weights belong to the set
\mathbbm{Z}_{\geq 0}\times(\alpha+\mathbbm{Z})
.
The tensor product given in Lemma
3.9
implies property
(3) above whereas (2) and (4) are clear.

Set
\mathsf{X}=\{\bar{0}\}\subset\mathbbm{C}/\mathbbm{Z}
corresponding to highest weights in

\mathbbm{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbbm{Z}
, that contain the weight of the identity

\mathbbm{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}_{\bar{0}}
, as well as the set

\{(n,0),(n,-n-1)\mid n\in\mathbbm{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
of atypical weights. Then,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
is generically semisimple with respect to
\mathsf{X}
.

Proposition 3.14
.

The family of maps
\theta=\{\theta_{V}:V\to V\}_{V\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}}
defines a ribbon
structure on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
is generically semisimple,
we may apply
[
GPM18
, Thm.2]
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to prove that
\theta_{V}^{*}=\theta_{V^{*}}
for any typical
object
V\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)}
. Fix a typical object
V=V_{p}(n,\alpha)
. We compute the

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V})\circ(c_{V,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V})\,.

Let
v_{0},v\in V
with
v_{0}
a highest weight vector. Then

\displaystyle c_{V,V}(v_{0},v)=\tau_{V,V}\circ\Upsilon_{V,V}(v_{0},v)=(-1)^{p\cdot\overline{v}}\Upsilon_{V,V}(v,v_{0})\,.

(71)

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}(v_{0}\otimes v^{*})

(72)

Lemma 3.17

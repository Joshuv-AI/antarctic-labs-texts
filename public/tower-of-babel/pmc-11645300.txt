Prognostic significance of osteosarcopenia in patients with stage IV gastric cancer undergoing conversion surgery
Authors: Yuki Hirase, Takaaki Arigami, Daisuke Matsushita, Masataka Shimonosono, Yusuke Tsuruda, Ken Sasaki, Kenji Baba, Yota Kawasaki, Takao Ohtsuka
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeRecently, several investigators have focused on the clinical significance of osteosarcopenia in malignancies; however, its prognostic impact on patients with gastric cancer after conversion surgery (CS) remains unclear. Therefore, we aimed to investigate sarcopenia, osteopenia, and osteosarcopenia in this patient population.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 24 patients with gastric cancer who underwent CS. Before CS, the skeletal muscle index at the L3 vertebra and bone mineral density at the Th11 vertebra were measured to investigate sarcopenia and osteopenia, respectively. Osteosarcopenia was defined as the coexistence of sarcopenia and osteopenia. The relationship between perioperative osteosarcopenia and patient prognosis, including clinicopathological factors, was assessed.ResultsAmong the 24 patients, 10 (42%) had osteosarcopenia. Osteosarcopenia was significantly correlated with body mass index, depth of tumor invasion, and tumor stage (all p < 0.05). The median overall survival and disease-free survival after CS in patients with osteosarcopenia were significantly shorter than those in patients without osteosarcopenia (all p < 0.05). In the multivariate analysis, osteosarcopenia was identified as an independent factor related to overall survival alone (p = 0.04).ConclusionAssessment of osteosarcopenia has clinical utility in predicting the prognosis after CS in patients with stage IV gastric cancer after chemotherapy.

Introduction

Gastric cancer is the fifth most common malignant tumor and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide [1]. Remarkable advances in chemotherapy have improved the prognosis of patients with stage IV gastric cancer. According to the Checkmate 649 and SPOTLIGHT trials, the median overall survival (OS) time in the nivolumab plus chemotherapy group and the zolbetuximab plus mFOLFOX (modified folinic acid [or levofolinate], fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin regimen) group was 13.1 and 18.23 months, respectively [2, 3]. However, long-term survivors of stage IV gastric cancer who have undergone chemotherapy alone are rare in clinical practice. Conversion surgery (CS) after chemotherapy is a promising therapeutic strategy to improve the prognosis of patients with stage IV gastric cancer [4, 5]. However, CS for stage IV gastric cancer is weakly recommended in patients with favorable performance status (PS) and tumor response after chemotherapy and in those with tumors determined to fulfill curative R0 resection, according to the Japanese Gastric Cancer Treatment Guidelines 2021 [6].

Recently, sarcopenia and osteopenia have gained considerable attention [7, 8]. Sarcopenia is characterized by loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength and has been reported to be associated with poor prognosis in patients with gastric cancer [9, 10]. Osteopenia is characterized by low bone mineral density (BMD). Several investigators have shown a relationship between the presence or absence of osteopenia and prognosis in various malignancies, including gastric cancer [11, 12]. Significant changes associated with aging include sarcopenia and osteopenia. “Osteosarcopenia,” a pathological condition involving sarcopenia and osteopenia, has been attracting attention as a prognostic factor in patients with several cancers [7, 13]. Unfortunately, few studies have been conducted on osteosarcopenia among responders with stage IV gastric cancer who underwent CS after chemotherapy. Hence, the clinical impact of osteosarcopenia in patients with gastric cancer who have undergone CS remains unclear.

Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate sarcopenia, osteopenia, and osteosarcopenia in patients with stage IV gastric cancer who underwent CS after chemotherapy and to assess the clinical significance of osteosarcopenia as a new prognostic marker associated with body composition.

Material and methods

Patients

We retrospectively reviewed 52 patients with stage IV gastric cancer who underwent CS after chemotherapy at the Kagoshima University Hospital between April 2011 and April 2022. Of the 52 patients, 28 were excluded from the study owing to the unavailability of preoperative computed tomography (CT) scans at our hospital. Accordingly, 24 patients (18 men and 6 women; age range, 30–88 years; median age, 71 years) were enrolled in this study. All patients underwent blood tests, esophagogastroduodenoscopy, and CT before chemotherapy and CS. Patients were classified and staged according to the TNM classification of gastric carcinoma [14].

We used the “opt-out” method to obtain informed consent from patients. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Kagoshima University (approval number: 230155).

Definition of sarcopenia, osteopenia, and osteosarcopenia

CT images taken before CS were loaded into the Volume Analyzer SYNAPSE VINCENT image analysis system (Fujifilm Medical, Tokyo, Japan) to assess the body composition index.

Sarcopenia was defined as a decrease in the skeletal muscle index (SMI) measured at the level of the L3 vertebra before CS. Tissue Hounsfield thresholds ranged from −29 to 150 HU for skeletal muscles [15]. The SMI (cm2/m2) was determined as skeletal muscle area (cm2) divided by height squared (m2) [13]. The cutoff SMI values were 52 for men and 38 for women [16]. Osteopenia was defined as an actual BMD below the calculated standard BMD (men: 308.82 – 2.49 × age [years]; women: 311.84 – 2.41 × age [years]) [13]. Before CS, the BMD was assessed by examining the average pixel density within the oval core at the level of the Th11 vertebra. Osteosarcopenia was defined as the coexistence of sarcopenia and osteopenia.

Blood markers for nutrition and systemic inflammatory response

The neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio (NLR), prognostic nutritional index (PNI), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), C-reactive protein (CRP)–albumin ratio (CAR), and albumin level were used as blood indicators of nutrition and systemic inflammatory response in the present study. Blood samples were collected before CS. Neutrophils, lymphocytes, and platelets were counted using an XN-20 automated hematology analyzer (Sysmex Co., Kobe, Japan). CRP and albumin levels were determined using a JCA-ZS050 automatic analyzer (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan).

The NLR was calculated as the neutrophil count divided by the lymphocyte count. The PNI was calculated as serum albumin × 10 + 0.005 × total lymphocyte count. The SII was calculated as neutrophil count × platelet count/lymphocyte count. The CAR was calculated as CRP divided by the albumin level. The cutoff values for NLR, PNI, SII, CAR, and albumin levels for univariate and multivariate analyses were set based on the median value.

Statistical analyses

The relationship between the presence or absence of osteosarcopenia and clinicopathological factors was assessed using the chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test, or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. OS was defined as the period from CS to death or final follow-up. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from the date of CS to the date when recurrence or a second malignancy was confirmed, mortality from any cause, or final follow-up. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated, and prognostic differences were determined using the log-rank test. Prognostic factors were determined using univariate and multivariate analyses (Cox proportional hazards regression modeling). All data were analyzed using the JMP software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. The multivariate analysis for DFS was performed only for lesions that were significant (p < 0.1) in the univariate model.

Results

Clinicopathological features of patients

The clinicopathological features are summarized in Table 1. Thirteen, 10, and 1 patients had a PS score of 0, 1, and 3, respectively. The median value of body mass index (BMI) was 21.9 kg/m2 (range, 17.9–30.6 kg/m2). Among the 24 patients, 7, 1, 10, and 8 had liver, lung, peritoneal dissemination, and distant lymph node metastases, respectively. Twenty-two and two patients received platinum-based and taxane-based chemotherapy, respectively. Seven patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive gastric cancer received trastuzumab combined with chemotherapy. The median number of cycles of chemotherapy administration was 6 cycles (range, 2–38 cycles). The median duration of chemotherapy treatment was 174 days (range, 91–1142 days). Proximal gastrectomy, distal gastrectomy, total gastrectomy, and esophagectomy for CS were performed in 2 (8%), 15 (63%), 5 (21%), and 2 (8%) patients, respectively. With regard to the depth of tumor invasion, 4 (16%), 5 (21%), 4 (16%), 6 (25%), and 5 (21%) patients had pathological T0, T1, T2, T3, and T4 tumors, respectively. Furthermore, 9 (38%), 6 (25%), 7 (29%), and 2 (8%) patients had pathological N0, N1, N2, and N3 statuses, respectively. Finally, 4 (17%), 5 (21%), 7 (29%), 6 (25%), and 2 (8%) patients had pathological stages 0, I, II, III, and IV, respectively. The median values of NLR, PNI, SII, CAR, and albumin levels before CS were 1.9 (range, 0.6–7.1), 45.7 (range, 35.6–53.2), 32.0 (range, 6.4–71.6), 0.02 (range, 0.004–0.25), and 3.8 g/dL (range, 3.0–4.5 g/dL), respectively. Discussion

The present study investigated the body composition to assess the clinical impact of osteosarcopenia on responders with stage IV gastric cancer who underwent CS after chemotherapy. The following results were obtained: (1) these patients had a high incidence of osteosarcopenia; (2) a close relationship between osteosarcopenia and BMI, depth of tumor invasion, or stage was observed; and (3) osteosarcopenia was an independent prognostic factor for OS based on the multivariate analysis. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to suggest the clinical utility of osteosarcopenia as an indicator of CS for improving the prognosis of patients with stage IV gastric cancer.

Patients with advanced cancer often experience malabsorption and malnutrition, which can lead to osteopenia and sarcopenia [17]. Sarcopenia and osteopenia are reported to be prevalent in 12.5–57.7% and 29–54% of patients with gastric cancer, respectively [18, 19]. In the present study, 67% (16/24) of the patients had sarcopenia, and 71% (17/24) had osteopenia. These results indicate a high incidence of sarcopenia and osteopenia in patients with stage IV gastric cancer who received chemotherapy. Moreover, this study showed that 42% of the patients had osteosarcopenia. Several investigators have demonstrated that the incidence of osteosarcopenia was 25.8%, 28.3%, 27.5%, and 11.9% in patients with colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer, extrahepatic bile duct cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, respectively [7, 13, 20]. These findings suggest that malignant tumors can induce osteosarcopenia in the presence of both sarcopenia and osteopenia.

Recent studies have reported that osteosarcopenia is correlated with clinicopathological factors, including blood markers for nutrition and systemic inflammatory response, in patients with several malignancies [7, 21]. Abe et al. [13] retrospectively investigated osteosarcopenia in 265 patients with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma who underwent curative resection and reported a relationship between the presence of osteosarcopenia and low BMI or low PNI. Similarly, in the present study, the osteosarcopenia group showed a lower BMI than the non-osteosarcopenia group. These results suggest that undernourishment is associated with osteosarcopenia in patients with malignancies.

Several investigators have focused on the clinical effect of osteosarcopenia as a prognostic predictor in patients with malignancies [22, 23]. Matsumoto et al. [7] reviewed the prognosis of 138 patients with extrahepatic bile duct cancer after resection and reported that the 5-year OS rates of patients with and without osteosarcopenia were 20.5% and 67.8%, respectively (p < 0.0001). Furthermore, the 5-year DFS rates in patients with and without osteosarcopenia were 17.1% and 55.4%, respectively (p < 0.0001) [7]. In this study, the OS and DFS in the osteosarcopenia group were shorter than those in the non-osteosarcopenia group. The multivariate analysis for OS identified the presence or absence of osteosarcopenia as an independent prognostic factor associated with body composition. Therefore, osteosarcopenia may be a promising prognostic marker for determining the clinical indication for CS after chemotherapy in patients with stage IV gastric cancer.

Currently, drug therapy, exercise, and nutritional programs play important roles in improving sarcopenia and osteopenia in patients with malignancies [24–26]. Since vitamin D deficiency is a significant risk factor for low BMD, preoperative drug support, including calcium intake and vitamin D supplementation, may help prevent osteopenia [27]. Lim et al. [18] demonstrated that drug intervention using calcium, vitamin D, and bisphosphonates has clinical utility in increasing BMD and reducing the risk of fractures in patients with gastric cancer after gastrectomy. In contrast, exercise and nutritional programs may prevent sarcopenia by increasing protein synthesis [28]. An exercise program combined with nutritional supplements containing leucine and omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids has been suggested to have a synergistic effect on maintaining muscle mass and function [29]. As no prospective studies have shown that perioperative interventions using drug therapy, nutritional support, and rehabilitation improve the prognosis of patients with stage IV gastric cancer who undergo CS after chemotherapy, further research is warranted to assess whether interventions aimed at preventing and ameliorating osteosarcopenia have an impact on prognostic improvements in the clinical management of patients with gastric cancer.

The present study had several limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size (n=24). Second, chemotherapeutic regimens, dose adjustments, and surgical procedures were decided according to the criteria proposed by clinical trials, patient conditions, or physician’s discretion. These limitations may have resulted in a bias that could have affected our results. Therefore, larger prospective studies are needed to validate our findings.

In conclusion, the present study suggests that preoperative osteosarcopenia is a promising factor for predicting the prognosis of patients with stage IV gastric cancer who undergo CS after chemotherapy.

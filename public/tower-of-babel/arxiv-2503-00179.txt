Zero-Shot and Efficient Clarification Need Prediction in Conversational Search
Lili Lu, Chuan Meng, Federico Ravenda, Mohammad Aliannejadi, Fabio Crestani

Abstract
CNP ( CNP ) is a key task in conversational search, aiming to predict whether to ask a clarifying question or give an answer to the current user query. However, current research on CNP suffers from the issues of limited CNP training data and low efficiency. In this paper, we propose a zero-shot and efficient CNP framework (Zef-CNP), in which we first prompt LLM in a zero-shot manner to generate two sets of synthetic queries: ambiguous and specific (unambiguous) queries. We then use the generated queries to train efficient CNP models. Zef-CNP eliminates the need for human-annotated clarification-need labels during training and avoids the use of LLM with high query latency at query time. To further improve the generation quality of synthetic queries, we devise a topic-, information-need-, and query-aware CoT ( CoT ) prompting strategy (TIQ-CoT). Moreover, we enhance TIQ-CoT with counterfactual query generation (CoQu), which guides LLM first to generate a specific/ambiguous query and then sequentially generate its corresponding ambiguous/specific query. Experimental results show that Zef-CNP achieves superior CNP effectiveness and efficiency compared with zero- and few-shot LLM -based CNP predictors.

Most existing work has focused on the selection or generation of clarifying questions

In this paper, we focus on the

(ii)

]
proposes an unsupervised method for

, which analyzes retrieved documents for the current user query, this type of method is inefficient because it performs

Large language models (

A new framework for

.
In this paper, we aim to address these limitations by proposing a
z

We only employ the trained efficient

Zef-CNP allows us to avoid using human-annotated clarification-need labels during training, and avoid using retrieval/

However, Zef-CNP presents new challenges.

Related Work

Clarification need prediction
.

Conversational search has attracted increasing attention
[
24
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
47
]
.
Mixed initiative is a key part of conversational search, which means that both the system and the user can take the initiative at any time during the conversation
[
33
]
.
The system can take the initiative of performing various actions, such as asking for clarification
[
4
]
or eliciting user preferences
[
22
]
.
In this paper, we focus on clarification.
The literature focuses on two main tasks in clarification:
CNP
(
CNP
) and clarifying question selection/generation
[
4
,
17
,
36
,
37
]
.
Our work focuses on
CNP
in mixed-initiative conversational search, which is to predict whether to ask a clarifying question to the current user query in an information-seeking conversation.

Most studies
[
3
,
10
,
21
,
25
,
43
]
rely on training data to train an effective
CNP
model.
For example, Aliannejadi et al.
[
3
]
and Chen et al.
[
10
]

fine-tuned
PLM
(e.g., BERT) and
LLM
on human-annotated clarification-need labels, respectively.
However, manually annotating clarification-need labels is labor-intensive, restricting the scale of
CNP
training data.
For example, Aliannejadi et al.
[
3
]
curated the ClariQ dataset, involving human annotators to have clarification-need labels, however, ClariQ only contains 187 annotated queries for training
CNP

Methods trained on these limited datasets could become biased towards the datasets and lack generalizability
[
5
]
.

Some studies do not rely on human-annotated clarification-need labels.
For example, the two works of Wang and Ai
[
40
,
41
]
focused on training

CNP

Arabzadeh et al.
[
5
]
proposed an unsupervised method, which predicts
CNP

CNP
after retrieval.

Recently, some work
[
14
,
23
,
48
,
49
]
focused on prompting
LLM
in a zero/few-shot manner for
CNP
.
However, directly using
LLM
as clarification-need predictors is highly inefficient and impractical due to their substantial computational overhead, as they have billions of parameters.
In particular, Deng et al.
[
14
]
and Zhang et al.
[
48
]
used a

CNP

The additional tokens produced by
CoT
further degrade the
CNP
efficiency.

Our work differs as

(i)

we aim at fully zero-shot
CNP
, where we do not rely on any human-annotated clarification-need labels,

(ii)

we focus on efficient
CNP
, where we do not rely on retrieval or
LLM
at query time.

Note that unlike the work from Deng et al.
[

]
, Guo et al.
[

]
and Xu et al.

]
, focusing on
CNP

CNP
setting in conversational search, where
CNP

Counterfactual generation
.
Leveraging LLMs for counterfactual generation has gained popularity and proven to be impactful in various IR and natural language processing (NLP) applications
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
16
]
.
It refers to generating a query/utterance/question with a specific label that is the opposite to a given label.
For example, Abolghasemi et al.
[
1
]
proposed using LLMs to generate counterfactual dialogues in which the simulated user is not satisfied with the system’s response. They showed that this approach leads to a more balanced distribution of labels, resulting in higher satisfaction prediction performance in task-oriented dialogue systems.
Calderon et al.
[
9
]

generated counterfactual utterances for multi-label intent prediction. They produced domain-counterfactual samples similar to the original ones in every aspect but different in their domain.
Bhattacharjee et al.
[
8
]
introduced a zero-shot pipeline for generating high-quality counterfactuals, without any additional training or fine-tuning, indicating the effectiveness of the pipeline in a zero-shot manner.

Our work is aligned with the idea of counterfactual generation using LLMs, however, we take a different angle and focus on generating both specific queries and their counterfactual ambiguous ones in conversational search.

Zero-shot synthetic data generation
.

Given the high cost of obtaining human-annotated data, zero-shot dataset generation has become a popular approach.
It often contains 3 steps, which are the generation of synthetic data by
PLM
or
LLM
, the fine-tuning of
PLM
or smaller models on the generated data, and the evaluation of fine-tuned models on various downstream tasks
[
18
,
31
,
44
,
45
,
50
]
.
Meng et al.
[
31
]
presented a method, called SuperGen, leveraging different
PLM
for data generation and fine-tuning, and evaluating various classification tasks.
However, the generative capabilities of
PLM
are still limited, compared to
LLM
.
Ye et al.
[
44
]
performed an extremely similar pipeline, called ZEROGEN, and it relies on smaller models (e.g., DistilBERT
[
35
]
) for fine-tuning.

However, it differs as

(i)

we focus on
CNP
in conversational search, a domain where zero-shot dataset generation has not been explored,

(ii)

to improve synthetic query generation quality, we propose TIQ-CoT to ask
LLM
to generate topics and information needs before generating synthetic queries, and further introduce CoQu to generate specific and ambiguous queries in a counterfactual way, and

(iii)

beyond generating synthetic specific queries, we explore generating
synthetic ambiguous queries
, which has not been well investigated yet.

Task definition

Previous studies
[
3
,
14
]
generally define
CNP
as a binary classification task.
Given the current user query
q
, a clarification-need predictor
f
determines whether the query is ambiguous or not.
Formally,
l^{*}=f(q)
, where
l^{*}\in\{0,1\}
,

l^{*}
is a predicted clarification-need label.
A label of
1
indicates that the query
q
is ambiguous and requires clarification, while a label of
0
implies that the query is specific and does not need clarification.

Methodology

We propose Zef-CNP, a zero-shot and efficient
CNP
framework.
As illustrated in Fig.
1
, it consists of three main stages:

(i)

zero-shot specific/ambiguous query generation with TIQ-CoT and CoQu,

(ii)

fine-tuning efficient
CNP
models, and

(iii)

applying fine-tuned efficient
CNP
models for inference.

Instruction
: Follow the instruction step by step. 1. Come up with 10 topics. 2. Based on the topics, generate 10 corresponding user information needs. 3. Based on the user information needs, generate 10 corresponding specific queries. 4. Based on the user information needs, generate 10 corresponding ambiguous queries.

The output format should be Topic: {topic}, User information need: {user information need}, Specific query: {specific query}, Ambiguous query: {ambiguous query}.

4.1
Zero-shot specific/ambiguous query generation with TIQ-CoT and CoQu

This stage aims to generate
M
synthetic training examples for
CNP
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}=\{(\hat{q}_{i},\hat{l}_{i})\}^{M}_{i=1}
, where if
\hat{q}_{i}
is a specific query (denoted as
sq
), then
\hat{l}_{i}=0
; if
\hat{q}_{i}
is an ambiguous query (denoted as
aq
),
\hat{l}_{i}=1
.

We completely leverage
LLM
’ self-knowledge to generate synthetic specific/ambiguous queries in a purely zero-shot manner.
To accomplish effective specific/ambiguous query generation, we propose a
topic-, information-need-, and query-aware
CoT
prompting strategy
(TIQ-CoT), which is accompanied by our devised
counterfactual query generation
(CoQu).

TIQ-CoT.

Specifically, TIQ-CoT aims to first prompt (the input prompt denoted as
p
) an
LLM
to generate a synthetic topic
t
, and a synthetic user information need
in
, before generating a specific (
sq
) or an ambiguous query (
aq
).
Formally:

t,in,sq/aq=\mathrm{LLM}(p).

(1)

CoQu.

CoQu further improves TIQ-CoT by instructing the
LLM
to generate a specific query (
sq
), and then generating its corresponding ambiguous query (
aq
) in a counterfactual manner.
CoQu can potentially help the
LLM
more clearly differentiate between the two types of queries.
We formulate TIQ-CoT with CoQu in the following:

t,in,sq,aq=\mathrm{LLM}(p).

(2)

In this way, we can collect a pair of specific and ambiguous queries (
sq
,
aq
) based on the same topic and information need.
We hypothesize that generating specific and ambiguous queries in a counterfactual manner helps
LLM
better distinguish between the two types, to improve generation quality for both query types.
We also examine the generation order of specific and ambiguous queries in Tab.
3
.

Specifically, we use the input prompt
p
shown in Fig.
2
to instruct an
LLM
to generate ten pairs of specific and ambiguous queries per call.
After data generation, we conduct data filtering: we remove duplicates, empty values, and special characters.

4.2
Fine-tuning an efficient
CNP
model

We use synthetic training data

\mathcal{D}=\{(\hat{q}_{i},\hat{l}_{i})\}^{M}_{i=1}
to train an efficient
CNP
model
f
, which does not rely on
LLM
or take retrieved information as inputs, ensuring high efficiency.
The learning objective is defined as:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\theta)&=-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\log P(\hat{l}_{i}\mid\hat{q}_{i}),\end{split}

(3)

where
\theta
are all the parameters of
f
.

4.3
Applying the fine-tuned efficient
CNP
model for inference

After training the efficient
CNP
model, we apply it to a test user query
q
for inference.
The model predicts whether the query requires clarification, denoted as
l^{*}=f(q)
, where
l^{*}
is the predicted clarification-need label.

Conclusions

In this paper, we have proposed Zef-CNP, a zero-shot and efficient
CNP
framework for mixed-initiative conversational search, which uses
LLM
to generate synthetic
CNP
training data in a zero-shot way and then trains efficient
CNP
predictors on the synthetic data.
Zef-CNP eliminates the need for human-annotated clarification-need labels during training and avoids relying on
LLM
or retrieved documents at query time.
To improve the quality of zero-shot generation of specific and ambiguous queries, we devise TIQ-CoT and CoQu.
TIQ-CoT guides
LLM
to generate topics and information needs, step by step, before generating synthetic queries.
CoQu enhances TIQ-CoT by enabling counterfactual generation of specific and ambiguous queries.
Experimental results show that Zef-CNP (fully zero-shot) outperforms baselines using
LLM
as zero/few-shot clarification-need predictors, in terms of both
CNP
effectiveness and efficiency, on two benchmark datasets, ClariQ and AmbigNQ.

Broader impact
.
We believe that our method has two broader impacts.
First, Zef-CNP offers a solution for domain-specific scenarios where no human-annotated
CNP
training data are available.
Second, Zef-CNP operates without using
LLM
or retrieval at query time, significantly reducing latency and improving
CNP
’s real-world applicability in practice.

Limitations and Future work
.
Our work has three limitations.
First, as shown in Tab.
4
, there is room to further improve the quality of synthetic queries.
One factor that may limit the quality of synthetic data is that we fine-tune efficient
CNP
models using synthetic data generated by a single
LLM
.
Only relying on one
LLM
might introduce biases in the data. To mitigate it, we plan to fine-tune
CNP
models on data generated by multiple
LLM
.
Second, we only use BERT as our efficient
CNP
model.
Although BERT is widely used, it is also worthwhile to investigate the results of simpler models fine-tuned on our synthetic data.
Third, we only focus on English datasets and it is interesting to explore the suitability and robustness of our proposed method on multi-lingual aspect
[
38
]
.

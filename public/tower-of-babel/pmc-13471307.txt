Early postoperative alignment as a predictor of recurrence after unilateral recession–plication for basic-type intermittent exotropia in children
Authors: Yuanyuan Ren, Kai Jin, Di Cao, Ying Wang, Li Liu, Li Li, Jingjing Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to identify clinical factors associated with early motor recurrence after unilateral lateral rectus recession with medial rectus plication (RP) in children with basic-type intermittent exotropia (IXT).MethodsThis retrospective cohort study enrolled children with basic-type IXT who underwent unilateral RP between January 2022 and December 2023. The primary outcome was early motor recurrence, defined as an exodeviation greater than 10 prism diopters (PD) at near at 6 months postoperatively. Early motor success was defined as horizontal exodeviation or esodeviation of 10 PD or less. Deviation at distance and near, fusion, and stereopsis were evaluated at predefined postoperative time points. Multivariable logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were used to assess factors associated with early recurrence and final motor outcomes.ResultsNinety-one patients were included (mean age, 8.10 ± 3.06 years; mean follow-up, 16.81 months). Thirty-one patients met criteria for early motor recurrence, and 60 achieved early motor success. The early recurrence group had significantly larger residual exodeviation at distance and near during the first postoperative week (P < 0.05). In adjusted logistic regression analyses, both near and distance deviations within the first postoperative week were independently associated with early motor recurrence at 6 months (near: OR = 0.738, 95% CI 0.609–0.895, P = 0.002; distance: OR = 0.736, 95% CI 0.606–0.893, P = 0.002) and with final motor failure (near: OR = 0.798, 95% CI 0.683–0.932, P = 0.004; distance: OR = 0.796, 95% CI 0.681–0.930, P = 0.004). Alignment at 6 months showed stronger adjusted associations with final motor outcome at both near and distance. ROC analysis showed moderate discriminative performance for near deviation within 1 week in identifying early recurrence (AUC = 0.7315) and stronger discrimination for near deviation at 6 months in identifying final motor outcome (AUC = 0.8461); these thresholds require external validation. At final follow-up, fusion was more frequent in the early motor success group (P = 0.011).ConclusionsAmong children with basic-type IXT undergoing unilateral RP, early postoperative alignment at near and distance was associated with early motor recurrence and final motor failure. Alignment at 6 months showed stronger associations with final motor outcome and should be interpreted as a mid-term prognostic marker rather than an independent early predictor. These findings support careful monitoring of near and distance alignment during the first 6 postoperative months, while acknowledging that the proposed cutoff values require validation in larger, independent cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04998-0.

Introduction

Intermittent exotropia (IXT) is one of the most common forms of childhood exotropia and remains an important clinical problem in pediatric ophthalmology. In the United States, the reported incidence of childhood IXT is approximately 32.1 per 100,000 children [1]. Higher prevalence estimates have been reported in East Asian populations, with rates up to 4.69% [2]. Because IXT may affect binocular visual development, visual comfort, and psychosocial well-being, optimizing its management is clinically important.

Conservative management, including observation, optical correction, occlusion therapy, prism use, and orthoptic training, may be appropriate for children with well-controlled IXT and preserved binocular function. However, surgical intervention is generally indicated for patients demonstrating progression toward constant exotropia, deterioration of fusional control, or functional or psychosocial impairment.

For basic-type IXT, commonly used surgical procedures include bilateral lateral rectus recession (BLR), unilateral lateral rectus recession combined with medial rectus resection (RR), and unilateral lateral rectus recession combined with medial rectus plication (RP). Medial rectus plication has gained attention as a muscle-strengthening procedure because it preserves muscle integrity and anterior segment circulation and may be partially reversible [3]. However, some studies have suggested that plication-recession may be associated with greater postoperative exodrift and higher recurrence rates than resection-recession procedures [4]. Although several studies have evaluated prognostic factors after IXT surgery, predictors of recurrence after RP, particularly in the early postoperative period, remain poorly defined.

Therefore, this study aimed to identify clinical factors associated with early motor recurrence within 6 months after unilateral RP in children with basic-type IXT. Among children who achieved early motor success, final motor stability and sensory outcomes were further assessed to inform postoperative surveillance after RP.

Methods

This retrospective review of medical records was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Children’s Hospital (No. [2025]-Y-009-D) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. Children diagnosed with basic-type IXT who underwent unilateral RP by the same surgeon from January 2022 to December 2023 were enrolled. Basic-type IXT was defined as a preoperative distance-near disparity of less than 10 PD. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age 3–18 years; (2) completed amblyopia treatment before surgery, if applicable; (3) at least three postoperative follow-up visits; (4) a minimum follow-up of 12 months; and (5) documented binocular visual function at the final visit. Exclusion criteria were: (1) consecutive esotropia at 6 months postoperatively; (2) prior ocular surgery; (3) vertical, torsional, or A- or V-pattern strabismus; (4) nystagmus or oblique muscle dysfunction; (5) developmental delay or neurological disorders; and (6) active or untreated amblyopia at the time of surgery. Children with a documented history of amblyopia were eligible only if amblyopia treatment had been completed before surgery. Consecutive esotropia was excluded because the objective of this study was to evaluate recurrence rather than overcorrection-related failure.

All procedures were performed under general anesthesia. The surgical eye was selected based on ocular dominance, fixation preference, and the surgeon’s clinical judgment. After a standard conjunctival opening and isolation of the lateral rectus muscle, the lateral rectus was recessed and reattached at the planned distance posterior to the original insertion. For medial rectus plication, double-armed absorbable sutures were passed through the medial rectus at the planned plication point, posterior to the insertion; the anterior muscle segment was folded anteriorly and secured to the original insertion without complete disinsertion of the muscle. No adjustable sutures were used. The surgical dose was determined by the maximum preoperative near deviation, using a modified standard surgical table and individualized according to the surgeon’s experience. The modified dosing scheme is provided in supplementary file (Table S1).

Clinical data were collected at each follow-up visit, including age at surgery, sex, uncorrected visual acuity, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), refraction, binocular visual function, and angle of deviation. Measurements were obtained preoperatively, within the first postoperative week, at 6 months postoperatively, and at the final follow-up visit beyond 12 months. Ocular alignment was assessed with the prism and alternate cover test (PACT) at distance (6 m) and near (33 cm) using appropriate optical correction. Exodeviation was recorded as a negative value, and esodeviation as a positive value. Fusion was evaluated with the synoptophore and the Worth 4-dot test, and stereopsis was assessed with the Titmus stereo test.

At the 6-month postoperative visit, patients were divided into two groups based on near motor alignment: (1) the early motor success group, defined as horizontal exodeviation or esodeviation of 10 PD or less; and (2) the early recurrence group, defined as exodeviation greater than 10 PD. Final motor outcome was classified as successful if exodeviation or esodeviation was 10 PD or less at both distance and near, and as failed if exodeviation or esodeviation exceeded 10 PD at either distance or near. The present study focused on motor recurrence; therefore, control scores, psychosocial outcomes, and patient-reported outcomes were not used to define success. Sensory outcomes at each visit were evaluated, and fusion was classified as absent or present. Stereopsis was classified as nil stereopsis, coarse stereopsis (100–800 arcsec), or fine stereopsis (100 arcsec or better). All measurements were performed by the same experienced orthoptist to minimize inter-observer variability.

SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Normality of continuous variables was assessed with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Between-group comparisons were performed using the independent-samples t test or Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to evaluate factors associated with early motor recurrence and final motor failure. Variables with clinical relevance or a P-value < 0.10 in univariate analysis were included as covariates. Because distance and near deviations are closely related postoperative alignment measures, they were evaluated in separate adjusted logistic regression models to reduce collinearity. The same covariate strategy was used for models assessing early postoperative alignment and 6-month alignment in relation to motor outcomes. ROC analysis evaluated the discriminative performance of selected postoperative alignment variables for early recurrence and final motor outcome. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated, and optimal cutoffs were determined using the Youden index. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Because this was a retrospective study, no a priori sample size calculation was performed.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

Ninety-one children were included after excluding 2 patients who developed consecutive esotropia at 6 months postoperatively. The mean age was 8.10 ± 3.06 years (range, 3–15 years), and the mean postoperative follow-up was 16.81 months. Preoperative deviation was − 34.80 ± 10.70 PD at distance and − 43.52 ± 10.99 PD at near. During the first postoperative week, mean alignment was 2.45 ± 5.05 PD at distance and − 0.09 ± 5.70 PD at near; at final follow-up, it was − 7.29 ± 9.17 PD and − 9.99 ± 9.62 PD, respectively. Exodrift was defined as the change in alignment from the first postoperative week to final follow-up. Mean exodrift was 9.74 ± 8.64 PD at distance and 9.90 ± 9.22 PD at near. Preoperatively, 21.98% of patients showed measurable fusion and 64.84% had fine stereopsis; these proportions increased to 90.00% and 87.91%, respectively, at final follow-up. Fusion and fine stereopsis both improved significantly after surgery (fusion, χ² = 82.780, P < 0.001; fine stereopsis, χ² = 13.428, P < 0.001).

Nine children had a documented history of amblyopia treatment before surgery, and all had completed amblyopia treatment before surgical intervention. At 6 months postoperatively, 7 of these children achieved early motor success and 2 developed early recurrence; no significant difference in early motor outcome was observed between children with and without a history of treated amblyopia (Fisher’s exact test, P = 0.713). At the final follow-up, 3 children with a history of treated amblyopia maintained final motor success, and 6 met the criterion for final motor failure; this distribution did not differ significantly from that of children without a history of treated amblyopia (Fisher’s exact test, P = 1.000).

At the final follow-up, 63 of the 91 included patients met the criterion for final motor failure, defined as a deviation greater than 10 PD at either distance or near. Of these, 20 had distance-only failure, 28 had near-only failure, and 15 had combined distance-and-near failure. The remaining 28 patients maintained motor success at both distance and near.

Fusion at the final follow-up was significantly lower in the early recurrence group (77.42%) than in the early success group (95.00%) (χ²=6.46, P = 0.011). Fine stereopsis did not differ significantly between the groups (P = 0.864).

Adjusted logistic regression analyses of postoperative alignment factors

Adjusted logistic regression analyses were conducted to evaluate postoperative alignment factors associated with motor outcomes. Because distance and near deviations are closely related, they were entered into separate adjusted models using the same covariate strategy. In the coding system, exodeviation was recorded as a negative value and esodeviation as a positive value; therefore, an OR less than 1 indicated that a 1-PD shift toward less exodeviation or mild esodeviation was associated with lower odds of recurrence or failure. Within the first postoperative week, both near and distance deviations were independently associated with early motor recurrence at 6 months (near: OR = 0.738, 95% CI 0.609–0.895, P = 0.002; distance: OR = 0.736, 95% CI 0.606–0.893, P = 0.002). Early postoperative alignment was also associated with final motor failure (near: OR = 0.798, 95% CI 0.683–0.932, P = 0.004; distance: OR = 0.796, 95% CI 0.681–0.930, P = 0.004). In addition, 6-month postoperative alignment showed significant adjusted associations with final motor outcome at both near and distance (near: OR = 0.836, 95% CI 0.768–0.911, P < 0.001; distance: OR = 0.846, 95% CI 0.774–0.924, P < 0.001) (Table 2). These findings suggest that early residual exodeviation at either testing distance is associated with subsequent recurrence, whereas 6-month alignment serves as a mid-term prognostic marker for final motor outcome.

The capacity of the deviation at near within 1 week postoperatively to serve as a predictor for early recurrence

Using early motor outcome as the dichotomous dependent variable (1 = recurrence, 0 = success), ROC analysis was performed to assess near deviation during the first postoperative week as a marker of early recurrence (Fig. 2). The AUC was 0.7315 (95% CI 0.6195–0.8434, P = 0.0003), indicating moderate discriminative performance. The Youden index reached a maximum of 0.4774 at an optimal cutoff of -1.00 PD, with a sensitivity of 67.74% and a specificity of 80.00%. This cutoff should be interpreted as an exploratory threshold derived from the present cohort rather than as a universally applicable clinical rule.

Near deviation within 1 week and at 6 months postoperatively as markers of final motor outcome

Using the final motor outcome as the dichotomous dependent variable (1 = failure, 0 = success), ROC analysis evaluated near deviation at 1 week and 6 months postoperatively (Fig. 3A). The AUC for near deviation at 1 week was 0.6601 (95% CI 0.5363–0.7840, P = 0.0151), indicating limited discriminative performance for the final motor outcome. The optimal cutoff was − 1.00 PD, with a sensitivity of 57.14% and specificity of 73.02%. In contrast, near deviation at 6 months showed stronger discriminative performance for final motor outcome (AUC = 0.8461, 95% CI 0.7517–0.9405, P < 0.0001). The optimal cutoff was − 7.50 PD, with a sensitivity of 85.71% and specificity of 82.54%. Combining near deviation at 1 week and 6 months did not improve discrimination compared with 6-month near deviation alone (AUC = 0.8458, 95% CI 0.7521–0.9395, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 3B). Because the 6-month alignment measure is close to the final outcome assessment and was derived from the same dataset, this result should be interpreted as a mid-term prognostic marker that requires external validation.

Discussion

Reported surgical success rates for IXT vary widely, from 47% to 93% [5, 6], largely due to differences in study design, follow-up duration, surgical procedures, and outcome definitions. Postoperative exodrift is a major determinant of long-term motor stability. However, factors associated with postoperative recurrence remain debated and may include patient-related characteristics, baseline ocular alignment, binocular function, and procedure-specific factors.

Several studies have examined predictors of postoperative drift in IXT, including age at onset or surgery [7–10], refractive error [11], anisometropia [12], preoperative deviation, and early postoperative alignment [13]. Morisawa et al. [14] reported that younger age at onset, larger preoperative deviation, impaired fusion, and greater early postoperative overcorrection were associated with substantial postoperative exodrift after unilateral recession-resection surgery. Most available evidence, however, has focused on BLR or RR procedures. Data on postoperative alignment behavior after medial rectus plication remain limited. In the present RP cohort, both near and distance deviations during the first postoperative week were independently associated with recurrence at 6 months and final motor failure. This finding suggests that immediate postoperative alignment at either testing distance may reflect both the initial surgical effect and early postoperative alignment stability.

All children in this study had basic-type IXT preoperatively, defined by a distance-near disparity of less than 10 PD. Although the early recurrence group had greater residual exodeviation at both near and distance, the mean postoperative distance-near disparity remained below the threshold typically used to define convergence-insufficiency-type exotropia. Therefore, our results should not be interpreted as indicating conversion from basic-type IXT to convergence-insufficiency-type IXT after RP. The similar adjusted associations observed for distance and near deviations suggest that recurrence risk was not limited to a near-specific mechanism. Rather, residual exodeviation at either testing distance during the early postoperative period may reflect insufficient early postoperative alignment stability. Although near deviation may be influenced by fusional-convergence demand and accommodative-convergence mechanisms, AC/A ratio, convergence amplitudes, dynamic control scores, and suppression patterns were not routinely measured; therefore, these mechanisms could not be directly tested in this retrospective study.

A mild overcorrection shortly after surgery appeared to be associated with a lower risk of recurrence, consistent with the concept that early exodrift is common after IXT surgery(13,14). However, the present study did not include a comparative RR or BLR group; therefore, it cannot determine whether the predictive value of early postoperative alignment is specific to RP or reflects a more general postoperative behavior of IXT. Moreover, although plication differs mechanically from resection because the muscle is folded rather than disinserted and shortened, any procedure-specific explanation for early drift remains hypothetical and should be evaluated in future comparative studies.

Postoperative alignment at 6 months showed stronger associations with final motor outcome than alignment in the first postoperative week. This result is clinically intuitive because mid-term alignment captures both the initial surgical effect and subsequent postoperative drift and sensory-motor adaptation. Accordingly, 6-month alignment should be viewed as a mid-term prognostic marker rather than an early independent predictor. From a clinical perspective, children with suboptimal alignment during the first 6 months may require closer surveillance and individualized postoperative management. The exploratory cutoff values identified in this study should not be used as definitive treatment thresholds until they are externally validated.

Sensory outcomes should also be interpreted cautiously. Fusion at final follow-up was more common in the early motor success group, whereas fine stereopsis did not differ significantly between groups. This apparent dissociation may reflect the relatively coarse stereopsis classification used in this retrospective study and the lack of dynamic measures of suppression or control. The low preoperative fusion rate in this surgical cohort may also reflect patient selection, as children undergoing surgery generally have more decompensated IXT than those managed conservatively. In addition, fusion was assessed with the synoptophore and the Worth 4-dot test rather than a validated IXT control scale.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single-center design limits generalizability. Second, no comparative surgical group was included, so procedure-specific conclusions about RP versus RR or BLR cannot be drawn. Third, the ROC cutoffs were derived from a single dataset and were not externally validated. Fourth, AC/A ratio, convergence amplitudes, control score, dynamic suppression patterns, psychosocial outcomes, and patient-reported outcomes were not routinely available. Fifth, interaction effects between early overcorrection and patient-specific factors, such as age, baseline deviation, and fusion capacity, were not evaluated because of the limited sample size. Future prospective multicenter studies with larger cohorts, standardized sensory and control assessments, prespecified amblyopia-related variables, and external validation are needed.

Conclusion

Early postoperative alignment at near and distance was independently associated with early motor recurrence and final motor failure in children with basic-type IXT undergoing unilateral RP. Mild early overcorrection or minimal residual exodeviation appeared to reduce the risk of recurrence. Alignment at 6 months showed stronger associations with final motor outcome and should be interpreted as a mid-term prognostic marker rather than an independent early predictor. These findings support careful monitoring of near and distance alignment during the first 6 postoperative months and provide a basis for future externally validated risk stratification models.

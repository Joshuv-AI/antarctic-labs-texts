Abstract
. We study the asymptotics of the Schrödinger equation with time-dependent potential in dimension one. Assuming that the potential decays sufficiently rapidly as | x | → ∞ |x|\to\infty , we prove that the solution can be written as the sum of a free wave e − i ​ t ​ Δ ​ u + e^{-it\Delta}u_{+} and a weakly bound component u wb ​ ( t ) u_{\textup{wb}}(t) . Moreover, we show that the weakly bound part decomposes as u wb ​ ( t ) = u loc ​ ( t ) + o H ˙ 1 ​ ( 1 ) u_{\textup{wb}}(t)=u_{\textup{loc}}(t)+o_{\dot{H}^{1}}(1) , where ∂ x u loc ​ ( t ) \partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}(t) is localized near the origin uniformly in time. Since decay conditions on the potential do not preclude resonances unless d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 , our results can be seen as a natural extension of [ 34 , 31 ] to the lower-dimensional case.



1.
Introduction

We will study the equation

(1)

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+V(x,t)u=0

where the potential
V
is assumed to satisfy the conditions

(2)

\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\sigma}V\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}<\infty

and

(3)

\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\sigma+1}\partial_{x}V\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}<\infty

for some
\sigma>2
.

Based on the localization of the potential, it would be natural to expect that solutions
u
to (
1
) decompose asymptotically into a localized bound state component
u_{\textup{loc}}
together with linearly scattering radiation. Currently, such a precise characterization of solutions is only known in dimensions
d\geq 5

[
34
,
35
,
31
]
. In lower dimensions, the best we can say is that
u
decomposes asymptotically into linearly scattering radiation and a slowly spreading
weakly bound
state
u_{\textup{wb}}
:

u(x,t)=u_{\textup{wb}}(x,t)+e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}+o_{H^{1}}(1)

where
u_{\textup{wb}}(t)
is becomes orthogonal to any solution to the free Schrödinger equation as
t\to\infty
and is localized to the region
|x|<t^{1/2+}
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert u_{\textup{wb}}\rVert_{L^{2}(|x|\geq t^{1/2+})}=0

see
[
30
,
28
,
18
]
. The significance of the threshold at dimension
5
was explained in
[
34
]
in terms of the applicability of the double Duhamel trick, which can also be used to prove the nonexistence of resonances in dimension
d\geq 5
for (time-independent) potentials satisfying (
2
).

Since resonances lie in
\dot{H}^{1}
but not in
L^{2}
, it is natural to expect that we can prove localization (and not just slow spreading) for
\partial_{x}u_{\textup{wb}}
. Our main result in
1
shows that this is indeed the case: The weakly bound component
u_{\textup{wb}}
decomposes as
u_{\textup{wb}}(x,t)=u_{\textup{loc}}(x,t)+o_{\dot{H}^{1}}(1)
, where
\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
for any
\theta\in(0,1)
. A similar result was proved by Tao in
[
33
]
for the radial cubic NLS in dimension
3
.

1.1.
Previous work

In the case where
V
is time-independent, the asymptotics for (
1
) are by now quite well understood, even if we significantly relax the decay assumptions
eqs.

2
and
3
: See
[
8
,
36
,
23
]
and the references therein. In this case, the spectral theorem allows us to decompose solutions into two parts: A discrete part, which can be written as a time quasiperiodic sum of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta+V
; and a continuous part, which under the assumption (
2
) with
\sigma>1
approaches (quantum) free flow as
t\to\infty
. In particular, the long-time asymptotics are given by

(4)

u(x,t)=\sum e^{-itE_{n}}\psi_{n}(x)+e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}+o_{{H^{1}}}(1)

A similar decomposition also holds for coupled systems of Schrödinger equations describing the interaction of multiple particles. There, we must consider multiple scattering channels (corresponding to different particles becoming bound) with the conclusion that solutions asymptotically arrange themselves into bound states and free particles which become widely separated as
t\to\infty

[
26
,
9
,
15
,
8
]
.

Much less is understood in the case where
V
is allowed to vary in time. If the time dependence is small, it is often possible to recover the same results as in the time-independent case. For example, for a small, time-dependent potential in dimension
3
, it is possible to prove global Strichartz estimates, which then imply scattering
[
25
]
. There are also a number of results on nonlinear Schrödinger equations with linear potentials

,

, including notable results on the asymptotic stability of solitons

; the interested reader can find a review in
[

.

When
V
has a large time-dependent part, it has not yet been possible to prove results as precise as (
4
). Currently, the most complete results are those of
[
35
,

, where solutions to radial nonlinear Schrödinger-type equations in dimensions

were shown to decompose as

for some function

u(x,t)=\sum u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j)}(x-x_{j}(t),t)+e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}+o_{H^{1}}(1)

1.2.
Main result and sketch of the proof

The main result in our paper states that solutions of (
1
) decompose into a linearly scattering radiation term and a term with localized kinetic energy:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
u
solves (
1
) with a potential
V
satisfying

(7)

\sup_{t}|V(x,t)|+|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x}V(x,t)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}

and that
\sup_{t}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}<\infty
, and that
\theta\in\left(0,1\right)
is a constant. Then, we have the decomposition

(8)

u(t)=e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}+u_{\textup{loc}}(t)+o_{\dot{H}^{1}}(1)

where
u_{+}\in H^{1}
and
u_{\textup{loc}}
has a localized derivative in the sense that

(9)

\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}

Moreover, if
V
satisfies the symbol-type bounds

(10)

\sup_{k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}}\sup_{t}|\langle x\rangle^{k}\partial_{x}^{k}V(x,t)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}

for
n>1
and
\theta\in\left(0,1\right)
then we can redefine
u_{\textup{loc}}
in (
8
) such that

(11)

\langle x\rangle^{\theta k}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{\textup{loc}}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}

for
1\leq k\leq n
.

Remark 1
.

It is not clear whether
u_{\textup{loc}}
is also localized in
L^{2}
or not. We note that
[
29
]
showed that in high dimensions, it is possible to adiabatically rescale a potential
V
that admits a bound state in such a way that
u_{\textup{wb}}
(and hence
u_{\textup{loc}}
) spreads at rate
t^{1/2-}
, nearly saturating the upper bound of
t^{1/2+}
. In particular, these self-similar bound states satisfy both (
9
) and (
11
).

To prove
1
, we first extract the linear radiation. To do this, we define the
free channel projection operator

J_{\textup{free}}(t)
as

J_{\textup{free}}(t)=e^{-it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)

Since solutions to the free Schrödinger equation concentrate on the phase-space support of
J_{\textup{free}}
, we define

u_{+}=\Omega_{\textup{free}}u_{0}=\slim_{t\to\infty}e^{it\Delta}J_{\textup{free}}(t)u(t)

Based on the decay of
V
, we can use the techniques of
[
30
,
28
]
to prove that the above limit exists in
H^{1}
, allowing us to write

u(t)=u_{\textup{wb}}(t)+e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}+o_{H^{1}}(1).

with
u_{\textup{wb}}(t)=(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))u(t)
.

In addition to the standard Duhamel representation

(12)

\begin{split}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)=&(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))u(t)\\
=&(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))e^{-it\Delta}u_{0}+i(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V(x,s)u(s)\;ds\end{split}

we can take advantage of the fact that
(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))e^{-it\Delta}
converges to
0
in the weak operator topology to write

(13)

u_{\textup{wb}}(t)=-i\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}J_{\textup{free}}(s)V(x,s)u(s)\;ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}\partial_{s}J_{\textup{free}}(s)u(s)\;ds

Examining the representation (
12
), we see at once that
(I-J_{\textup{free}}(t))e^{-it\Delta}u_{0}\to 0
in
H^{1}
. In the backward in time representation (
13
), a careful analysis using propagation estimates shows that as
t\to\infty
,

\left\lVert\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}\partial_{s}J_{\textup{free}}(s)u(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\to 0

Thus, in each of the representations
eqs.

13
and
12
, the main contribution comes from the Duhamel integrals involving
Vu
. Because the time evolution in each of these integrals is in opposite directions, we can employ an incoming/outgoing decomposition to obtain improved decay in appropriate regions of phase space. This idea goes back to the work of Enss
[
9
]
, and has been instrumental in the field
[
19
,
24
,
31
]
. Following Tao
[
33
]
, we will define our projections in terms of Fourier and physical space projections, although definitions in terms of spectral projectors of the dilation operator
A=x\cdot\nabla+\nabla\cdot x
are also possible.

To define the incoming/outgoing decomposition and explain how it leads to improved decay, we recall that the Schrödinger propagator
e^{-it\Delta}
is the quantum version of the classical flow
x\mapsto x+2\xi t
. Using this heuristic, we see that if we project to positive frequencies, the propagator
e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}
will tend to move waves localized near the origin to the right (outward) for (
12
) resulting in improved decay in the ‘incoming’ region
x<0
, while
e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}
moves waves to the left (inward) for (
13
), which gives better decay in the ‘outgoing’ region
x>0
. A similar heuristic also holds for negative frequencies, so as a first approximation, we might define

\begin{split}P^{\textup{in}}_{(\textup{naive})}=&\mathds{1}_{x>0}\mathds{1}_{D>0}+\mathds{1}_{x<0}\mathds{1}_{D<0}\\
P^{\textup{out}}_{(\textup{naive})}=&\mathds{1}_{x<0}\mathds{1}_{D>0}+\mathds{1}_{x>0}\mathds{1}_{D<0}\end{split}

However, this is too naïve, as sharp cut-offs in frequency are not compatible with spatial localization. To fix this, we split out the low-frequencies and instead work with incoming, outgoing, and low-frequency projections, which, roughly, are given by

\begin{split}P^{\textup{in}}=&\mathds{1}_{x>0}F_{\geq\langle x\rangle^{-\theta}}(D)+\mathds{1}_{x<0}F_{\geq\langle x\rangle^{-\theta}}(-D)\\
P^{\textup{out}}=&\mathds{1}_{x<0}F_{\geq\langle x\rangle^{-\theta}}(D)+\mathds{1}_{x>0}F_{\geq\langle x\rangle^{-\theta}}(-D)\\
P^{\textup{low}}=&F_{\leq\langle x\rangle^{-\theta}}(|D|)\end{split}

for
\theta\in(0,1)
. (See
Section

4.1
for precise definitions). Thus, by writing

u_{\textup{wb}}(t)=P^{\textup{in}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)+P^{\textup{out}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)+P^{\textup{low}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)

we can use the forward-in-time Duhamel integral (
12
) to obtain spatial decay for the incoming term
P^{\textup{in}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)
and the backward-in-time integral (
13
) to get spatial decay for
P^{\textup{out}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)
. Finally, the terms
P^{\textup{low}}u_{\textup{wb}}(t)
are localized to frequencies
|\xi|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1+}
, so their derivatives are manifestly localized. This suffices to prove (
9
).

In only remains to prove the higher-order bound (
11
). The issue here is that the incoming/outgoing projectors only allow us to gain one factor of
\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}
:

\left\lVert(\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x})^{2}P^{\textup{in}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}f(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{1}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\sigma}(\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x})^{m}f(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{\epsilon}_{x}}

and similarly for the outgoing projection. For our application,
f(s)=Vu(s)
. If we could replace
Vu
with
Vu_{\textup{loc}}
, we could simply proceed by induction. Indeed, this is essentially the approach taken in
[
35
]
to obtain spatial decay in
d\geq 5
. However, this would require us to prove that
u_{\textup{loc}}
is a solution to (
1
), which seems out of reach in lower dimensions. Instead, recalling the decomposition (
8
), we write

Vu=Vu_{\textup{loc}}+V(e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}u_{\textup{rem}})

where
u_{\textup{rem}}(t)
vanishes in
\dot{H}^{1}
as
t\to\infty
. By leveraging the properties of the incoming and outgoing projectors and the decay of
V(e^{-it\Delta}u_{+}u_{\textup{rem}})
in time, we find that

\left\lVert\partial_{x}P^{\textup{in}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V(e^{-is\Delta}u_{+}u_{\textup{rem}}(s))\;ds\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\to 0

and similarly for the outgoing projection. Absorbing these terms into the remainder term
o_{\dot{H}^{1}}(1)
thus gives (
11
) with
n=2
, and further iteration gives the result for any
n
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation and conventions

We use the notation
A\lesssim B
to mean that
A\leq CB
for some fixed implicit constant
C>0
. If
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, then we write
A\sim B
. For implicit constants that depend on parameters, we will write

A\lesssim_{P}B
to mean
A\leq C_{P}B
for a constant
C_{P}
which is allowed to depend on
P
.

Given a set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
, we will denote the indicator function of
K
by
\mathds{1}_{K}(x)
. We also define the smooth functions
F,F_{\leq},F_{\geq}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that
F
is supported on
[1/2,2]
with

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}F\left(\frac{x}{2^{j}}\right)=\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(x)

and

\begin{split}F_{\leq}(x)=&\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}F\left(\frac{x}{2^{j}}\right)\\
F_{\geq}(x)=&\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F\left(\frac{x}{2^{j}}\right)\end{split}

Given
C>0
, we define the functions

\begin{split}F_{C}(x)=&F(x/C)\\
F_{\leq C}(x)=&F_{\leq}(x/C)\\
F_{<C}(x)=&F_{\leq C/2}(x)\\
F_{\lesssim C}(x)=&F_{\leq C2^{10}}(x)\\
F_{\ll C}(x)=&F_{\leq C2^{-10}}(x)\\
F_{\sim C}(x)=&F_{\lesssim C}(x)-F_{\ll C}(x)\\
F_{\geq C}(x)=&F_{\geq}(x/C)\\
F_{>C}(x)=&F_{\geq 2C}(x)\\
F_{\gtrsim C}(x)=&F_{\geq C2^{-10}}(x)\\
F_{\gg C}(x)=&F_{\geq C2^{11}}(x)\end{split}

In particular, we have that

F_{<C}+F_{C}+F_{>C}=\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}=F_{\ll C}+F_{\sim C}+F_{\gg C}

To more easily track the
C
dependence of derivatives of
F_{C}
, we take the convention that

F_{C}^{(a)}(x)=F^{(a)}(x/C)

(so
|F_{C}^{(a)}(x)|
is bounded independent of
C
), and similarly for
F_{\leq C}
,
F_{\geq C}
, and so on. In particular, this means that

\partial_{x}^{n}F_{C}(x)=C^{-n}F^{(n)}(x/C)=C^{-n}F^{(n)}_{C}(x)

The Fourier transform of a function
f
is given by

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int f(x)e^{-ix\xi}\;dx

As is well-known, the Fourier transform is unitary on
L^{2}
, with inverse

\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(x)=\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int g(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\;d\xi

Given a function
m:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the Fourier multiplier
m(D)
to be

m(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}m(\xi)\mathcal{F}f

In particular,

\mathcal{F}\left[m(D)f\right](\xi)=m(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)

For our purposes, we will make frequent use of the dyadic Littlewood-Paley projectors

\begin{split}P_{k}=&F_{2^{k}}(|D|)\\
P_{k}^{\pm}=F_{2^{k}}(\pm D)\end{split}

and the variants
P_{\leq C}
,
P_{\geq C}
, etc. In particular, we have that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}P_{k}f=f

2.2.
Basic estimates

Lemma 2
.

For
\delta<\min(1/2,1-\alpha,1-\beta)
and for any positive
M
,

(14)

\lVert F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{M}t^{-M}

Proof.

We first observe that it suffices to prove the bound for
t
large, since for small
t
the bound follows immediately from the trivial bound

\lVert F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1

The main idea in this argument (which will be used frequently in this work) is to take advantage of nonstationary phase on the frequency side. To do this, we rewrite the operator in terms of integration against a kernel:

F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|x|)\phi=\int K_{t}(x,y)\phi(y)\;dy

where

K_{t}(x,y)=\frac{F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int e^{-it\xi^{2}}e^{i\xi(x-y)}F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|\xi|)\;d\xi

The integral has phase
\phi(\xi)=-t\xi^{2}+\xi(x-y)
. Under the assumption that
{\delta<\min(1-\alpha,1-\beta)}
, we see that for large enough
t
,

|\phi^{\prime}(\xi)|=|x-y-2t\xi|\sim|t|^{1-\delta}

In particular, by repeatedly integrating by parts using the identity

e^{-it\xi^{2}+i\xi(x-y)}=\frac{i}{2t\xi-(x-y)}\partial_{\xi}e^{-it\xi^{2}+i\xi(x-y)}

we see that

K_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{a+b=N}C_{a,b}\frac{F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int\frac{t^{a}e^{-it\xi^{2}}e^{i\xi(x-y)}}{(2t\xi-(x-y))^{N+a}}t^{b\delta}F^{(b)}_{>t^{-\delta}}(|\xi|)\;d\xi

In particular, we calculate that

\begin{split}\lVert K_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim&\lVert F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|y|)\rVert_{L^{2}_{y}}\sum_{a+b=N}\int\frac{|t|^{a}}{|2t\xi|^{N+a}}|t|^{b\delta}|F^{(b)}_{>t^{-\delta}}(|\xi|)|\;d\xi\\
\lesssim_{N}&t^{(\alpha+\beta)/2}t^{N(1-2\delta)}\end{split}

Choosing
N
sufficiently large depending on
M,\delta,\alpha
and
\beta
and recalling that

\lVert F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\lVert K_{t}(x,y)\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,y}}

now gives the result.
∎

Corollary 3
.

For any
\alpha
,
\beta,
and
\delta<\min(1/2,1-\alpha,1-\beta)
, if
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\sigma}
, then

(15)

\lVert F(|x|<t^{\alpha})F(|D|>t^{-\delta})e^{it\Delta}V(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\beta\sigma}

Proof.

The bound is trivial for
|t|\leq 1
, so we will focus on the case
|t|>1
. Let us write
V=F_{\geq t^{\beta}}(x)V+F_{\leq t^{\beta}}(x)V
. The operator with
V
restricted to
|x|<t^{\beta}
decays arbitrarily fast in time by
Lemma

2
, while the decay of
V
gives the bound

|F_{\geq t^{\beta}}(x)V|\lesssim t^{-\beta\sigma}

which is enough to prove (
15
).
∎

We also have the following refinement:

Lemma 4
.

Let
j\geq 0
and
k>-j-10
. For
t>2^{j-k}
, we have that

(16)

\lVert\mathds{1}_{|x|<t2^{k-20}}\partial_{x}^{m}P_{k}e^{-it\Delta}F_{\lesssim 2^{j}}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}\min\left(2^{mk},2^{j/2}\frac{2^{(m+3/2-2N)k)}}{t^{N-1/2}}\right)

Proof.

The bound of
2^{mk}
follows immediately from the frequency localization, so we will focus on proving the second bound. We observe that the integral kernel associated with
\mathds{1}_{|x|<t2^{k-20}}\partial_{x}^{m}P_{k}e^{-it\Delta}F_{\lesssim 2^{j}}(x)
is

K(x,y)=\frac{\mathds{1}_{|x|<t2^{k-20}}F_{\lesssim 2^{j}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int\xi^{m}F_{2^{k}}(|\xi|)e^{i(t\xi^{2}+(x-y)\xi)}\;d\xi

For
|x|<t2^{k-20}
and
|y|\lesssim 2^{j}
, the phase for the exponential in the integral is bounded away from zero, with magnitude

|2t\xi+(x-y)|\sim t2^{k}

Repeated integration by parts now gives

\begin{split}\left|\int\xi^{m}F_{2^{k}}(|\xi|)e^{i(t\xi^{2}+(x-y)\xi)}\;d\xi\right|\leq&\sum_{a+b+c=N}C_{a,b}\int 2^{-ak}\xi^{m-c}\frac{|F_{2^{k}}^{(a)}(|\xi|)|}{t^{N}\xi^{N+b}}\;d\xi\\
\lesssim_{N}&\frac{2^{(m+1-2N)k}}{t^{N}}\end{split}

Thus, we find that

\lVert K(x,y)\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim_{N}2^{j/2}\frac{2^{(m+3/2-2N)k}}{t^{N+1/2}}

which implies (
16
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
f(x)
and
g(x)
are bounded functions whose supports are separated by a distance
2^{j}
. Then, for any
N\geq 0
,

\lVert f(x)P_{\geq k}^{\pm}g(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}2^{-N(k+j)}

Proof.

To avoid cumbersome notation, we will prove the result for the projection to positive frequencies. We have that

P^{+}_{\geq k}=\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}F_{\ell}^{+}

Moreover, the operator
f(x)P^{+}_{\ell}g(x)
can be represented as integration again the kernel

K_{\ell}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)g(y)}{2\pi}\int e^{i\xi(x-y)}F_{2^{\ell}}(\xi)\;d\xi

Repeated integration by parts shows that

\begin{split}|K_{\ell}(x,y)|\lesssim&\left|\frac{f(x)g(y)}{2\pi(x-y)^{N}2^{N\ell}}\int e^{i\xi(x-y)}F^{(N)}_{2^{\ell}}(\xi)\right|\\
\lesssim&\frac{f(x)g(y)}{2\pi(x-y)^{N}}2^{(1-N)\ell}\end{split}

Thus, noting that
K_{\ell}(x,y)=0
unless
|x-y|>2^{j}
, we see that for
N\geq 2
,

\lVert K_{\ell}(x,y)\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}2^{(N-1)(j-\ell)}

which implies that

\lVert f(x)P^{+}_{\ell}g(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}2^{(N-1)(j-\ell)}

Summing over
n\geq k
now gives the result.
∎

We will also need the following formula, which allows us to symmetrize certain sums involving operators:

Lemma 6
.

Given operators
A,B,
and
C
with
[A,C]=0
, we have that

(17)

A^{2}BC^{2}+C^{2}BA^{2}=2(AC)B(AC)+R(A,B,C)

where

R(A,B,C)=A[[A,B],C]C+C[[C,B],A]A+A[C,[C,B]]A+C[A,[A,B]]C

In order to deal with commutators involving space weights, the following elementary estimate will be helpful:

Lemma 7
.

For any
\alpha>0
and
\rho\in[0,\min(\alpha,1)]
,

||x|^{\alpha}-|y|^{\alpha}|\lesssim_{\alpha,\rho}(|x|+|y|)^{\alpha-\rho}|x-y|^{\rho}

Proof.

By interpolation, it suffices to prove the cases
\rho=0
,
\rho=\alpha<1
, and
\rho=1\leq\alpha
. The case
\rho=0
follows immediately from the fact that
x\to x^{\alpha}
is increasing, and the case
\rho=1\leq\alpha
follows from the mean value theorem. Finally, for
\rho=\alpha<1
, we note that if
|x|>|y|
, then

\begin{split}|x|^{\alpha}-|y|^{\alpha}=&\alpha\int_{0}^{|x|-|y|}(z+|y|)^{\alpha-1}\;dz\\
\leq&\alpha\int_{0}^{|x|-|y|}z^{\alpha-1}\;dz\\
\leq&||x|-|y||^{\alpha}\leq|x-y|^{\alpha}\qed\end{split}

Corollary 8
.

For any
\alpha>0
and
\rho\in[0,\min(\alpha,1)]
,

|\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}-\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}|\lesssim_{\alpha,\rho}(\langle x\rangle+\langle y\rangle)^{\alpha-\rho}|x-y|^{\rho}

Proof.

The case
\rho=0
is immediate. For
\rho\neq 0
, we observe that

(18)

\partial_{x}\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}=\alpha\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}\langle x\rangle^{\alpha-1}

Since the right-hand side is less than
\alpha\langle x\rangle^{\alpha-1}
, the mean value theorem now suffices to prove the result with
\rho=1\leq\alpha
. Finally, for
\rho=\alpha<1
, we compute that for
|x|>|y|

\begin{split}\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}-\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}=&\int_{|x|-|y|}^{0}\alpha\frac{z+|y|}{\langle z+|y|\rangle}\langle z+|y|\rangle^{\alpha-1}\;dz\\
\leq&\int_{|x|-|y|}^{0}\alpha|z+|y||^{\alpha-1}\;dz\\
\leq&|x-y|^{\alpha}\qed\end{split}

In particular, this lemma allows us to prove the following useful results:

Corollary 9
.

For
\alpha>0
,
\rho\in[0,\min(1,\alpha)]
, we have that

(19)

\lVert F_{\lesssim 2^{j}}(|x|)|[|x|^{\alpha},P_{k}]F_{2^{j}}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{(\alpha-\rho)j}2^{-\rho k}

and

(20)

\lVert F_{\leq 2^{j}}(x)[\langle x\rangle^{\alpha},P_{k}]F_{\leq 2^{j}}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\langle 2^{j}\rangle^{\alpha-\rho}2^{-\rho k}

Proof.

For (
19
), we observe that
P_{k}
has a kernel given by

2^{k}K(2^{k}(x-y))

where
K(z)
is smooth and rapidly decaying. Thus, using
Lemma

7
, we have that

\begin{split}\lVert F_{\lesssim 2^{j}}(x)[|x|^{\alpha},P_{k}]F_{2^{j}}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=&\lVert F_{\lesssim j}(x)F_{j}(y)\left(|x|^{\alpha}-|y|^{\alpha}\right)2^{k}K(2^{k}(x-y))\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&2^{(\alpha-\rho)j}\lVert 2^{k}|x-y|^{\rho}|K(2^{k}(x-y))|\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&2^{(\alpha-\rho)j}2^{-\rho k}\end{split}

To obtain (
20
), we repeat the argument using
8
.
∎

Propagation estimates

At several points in our argument, we will need to use the method of
propagation estimates
. Given an operator
B
and a function

, we define

\langle B\rangle_{t,u}:=\langle Bu(t),u(t)\rangle

When the function
u
is clear from context, we will often omit it from the notation and simply write
\langle B\rangle_{t}
. We will also call the function

the
propagation observable
. If
u
solves
the abstract Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}u=A(t)u

with
A(t)
self-adjoint, and if
B(t)
is a (possibly time-dependent) propagation observable, then we have the identity

(21)

\frac{d}{dt}\langle B(t)\rangle_{t}:=\langle D_{H}B(t)\rangle_{t}

where

(22)

D_{H}B(t):=\frac{d}{dt}B(t)-i[A(t),B(t)]

\langle B(t_{2})\rangle_{t_{2}}-\langle B(t_{1})\rangle_{t_{1}}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle D_{H}B(t)\rangle_{t}\;dt

3.
Construction of the free channel

The free channel wave operator
\Omega_{\text{free}}
is defined by

(23)

\Omega_{\text{free}}u_{0}:=\slim_{t\to\infty}e^{it\Delta}J_{\textup{free}}u(t)

where
J_{\textup{free}}
is the projection

(24)

J_{\textup{free}}=e^{-it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)

To justify the name ‘free channel,’ we observe that if
\Omega_{\text{free}}u_{0}
exists, then for any
\phi\in H^{1}
,

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle e^{-it\Delta}\phi,u(t)\right\rangle_{H^{1}}=&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\phi,e^{it\Delta}J_{\textup{free}}u(t)\right\rangle_{H^{1}}+\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\phi,e^{it\Delta}(I-J_{\textup{free}})u(t)\right\rangle_{H^{1}}\\
=&\langle\phi,\Omega_{\text{free}}u_{0}\rangle\end{split}

since

e^{it\Delta}(I-J_{\textup{free}})=F_{\geq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)e^{it\Delta}F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)+e^{it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)

converges weakly to
0
in
H^{1}
. Thus,
u(t)-e^{-it\Delta}\Omega_{\text{free}}u(t)
is weakly orthogonal to all free waves
e^{-it\Delta}\phi
, which means that
e^{-it\Delta}\Omega_{\text{free}}u_{0}
is the free component of
u(t)
.

The definition of
\Omega_{\text{free}}
appears to depend on
\alpha
and
\delta
through
J_{\textup{free}}
, suggesting that we should write
\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}
instead of
\Omega_{\text{free}}
. In fact,
\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}=\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
whenever both wave operators exist, so there is no ambiguity. To see this, observe that

\begin{split}\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}u_{0}-\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}u_{0}=&\slim_{t\to\infty}e^{it\Delta}(J_{\textup{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}-J_{\textup{free}}^{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}})u(t)\\
=&\wlim_{t\to\infty}e^{it\Delta}(J_{\textup{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}-J_{\textup{free}}^{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}})u(t)\\
=&\wlim_{t\to\infty}\left(F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)-F_{\leq t^{\alpha^{\prime}}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)\right)e^{-it\Delta}u(t)\end{split}

Now,

\begin{split}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)-F_{\leq t^{\alpha^{\prime}}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)=&\left(F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)-F_{\leq t^{\alpha^{\prime}}}(|x|)\right)F_{\geq t^{-\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)\\
&+F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)-F_{\geq t^{-\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)\\
&-F_{\geq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\left(F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)-F_{\geq t^{-\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)\right)\end{split}

In particular, the operators on the right all converge strongly to
0
in
L^{2}
, so

\wlim_{t\to\infty}\left(F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{-\delta}}(|D|)-F_{\leq t^{\alpha^{\prime}}}(|x|)F_{\geq t^{\delta^{\prime}}}(|D|)\right)e^{-it\Delta}u(t)=0

and hence
\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha,\delta}=\Omega_{\text{free}}^{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
. For this reason, we will omit the parameters
\alpha
and
\delta
in
\Omega_{\text{free}}
. For appropriate choices of
\alpha
and
\delta
, the free channel wave operator
\Omega_{\text{free}}
exists (cf.
[
28
, Theorem 18]
):

Proposition 10
.

For
\alpha
and
\delta
satisfying

\begin{split}0<\alpha<&1\\
0<\delta<&\min(1/2,1-\alpha)\end{split}

the strong limit defining the free channel wave operator in (

We argue using Cook’s method. Defining

\Omega_{\text{free}}(t)u_{0}=e^{is\Delta}J_{\textup{free}}u(s)

we see that

Now,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{free}}(t)u_{0}-\Omega_{\text{free}}(1)u_{0}=

(26a)

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{t}e^{-is\Delta}\partial_{s}\tilde{J}_{\textup{free}}(s)e^{is\Delta}u(s)\;ds

4.1.
Incoming/outgoing projection

Lemma 12
.

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\langle x\rangle^{n\theta}\partial_{x}^{n}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}f(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s,s^{\prime}}&\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\sigma+m\theta}\partial_{x}^{m}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s^{\prime}}_{x}}\\

To begin, we project to frequencies

(31c)

We now estimate each of the pieces

The projectors

Lemma 13
.

The bounds

(35)

\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{\geq J}+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\right\rVert_{H^{1}\to H^{1}}\lesssim C

Proof.

We will prove the bound (
35
): the bound for the outgoing projectors follows similarly. The functions
F_{2^{j}}(\pm x)
and
F_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(\pm x)
have disjoint supports for
|j-j^{\prime}|\gg 1
, so we have that

\left\lVert\left(P^{\textup{in}}_{\geq J}+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\right)\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{\geq J}\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

Since
\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{\geq J}\rVert_{H^{1}\to H^{1}}
is bounded independent of

For fixed

, we have that

\begin{split}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\phi=&P^{\textup{in}}_{j}F_{\sim 2^{j}}(|x|)\phi+P^{\textup{in}}_{j}F_{>2^{j+10}}(|x|)\phi+P^{\textup{in}}_{j}F_{\ll 2^{j}}(|x|)\phi\\

For the

\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j,\sim}\phi\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{j}H^{1}}\lesssim\lVert F_{\sim 2^{j}}(|x|)\phi\rVert_{\ell^{2}_{j}H^{1}}\lesssim\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}}

For

\begin{split}P^{\textup{in}}_{j,\gg}=&F_{2^{j}}(x)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta j}}(D)F_{\gg 2^{j}}(|x|)+\{\text{similar}\}\\

The supports of

\lesssim&\sup_{\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\left\lVert\sqrt{\partial_{s}F_{\geq s^{\alpha}}(|x|)}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,x}}\left\lVert\sqrt{\partial_{s}F_{\geq s^{\alpha}}(|x|)}e^{is\Delta}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(t,\infty;L^{2}x)}\\
=&o_{t\to\infty}(1)\end{split}

For (
45c
), we see that the commutator term has essentially the same form as the right-hand side of (
36
), but with better decay in
j
. Thus, we can obtain the bounds for (
45c
) by defining a propagation observable

B(s)=F_{\geq s^{\alpha}}(|x|)

and modifying the argument for (
45b
).

Next, we show that
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t))}(t)
goes to
0
in
\dot{H}^{1}
. Recalling the decomposition (
42
), we have that

\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{low})}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim&\lVert[\partial_{x},F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(|x|)]\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\partial_{x}F_{\leq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(|D|)u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&2^{-\theta J(t)}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\end{split}

which decays to
0
as required.

We now show how to bound the forward-in-time Duhamel terms
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},1)}
and
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},2)}
(the backward-in-time terms are similar). For
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},2)}(t)
, we expand the definition of
P^{\textup{in}}_{\geq J(t)}
to write

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},2)}(t)=

\displaystyle-iF_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-it\Delta}F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(D)F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)e^{is\Delta}\partial_{x}(Vu(s))\;ds

\displaystyle+\{\textup{similar or easier}\}

Define

K_{t}(x,y)=\frac{F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int e^{it\xi^{2}+\xi(x-y)}F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(\xi)\;d\xi

to be the integral kernel associated with
F_{\geq J(t)}(x)e^{-it\Delta}F(-D\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)})F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)
. Since
\rho>\alpha
, we have that
x-y\gtrsim t^{\rho}
on the support of
K_{t}
. In particular, this implies that the phase of the exponential is nonstationary, so we can repeatedly integrate by parts to obtain that

\begin{split}|K_{t}(x,y)|=&\left|\sum_{a+b=N}C_{a,b}\frac{F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int e^{it\xi^{2}+\xi(x-y)}\frac{t^{a}2^{b\theta J(t)}F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}^{(b)}(\xi)}{[2t\xi+(x-y)]^{N+a}}\;d\xi\right|\\
\lesssim_{N}&F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|y|)\frac{t^{(N-1)\rho\theta}}{[t^{1-\rho\theta}+(x-y)]^{N-1}}\end{split}

Thus, Schur’s test gives us the estimate

\lVert F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)e^{-it\Delta}F_{\geq 2^{-J(t)}}(D)F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}t^{\rho\theta}t^{-(1-2\rho\theta)(N-2)}

Choosing
N
sufficiently large and recalling that
\rho\theta<1/2
, we conclude that
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},2)}
vanishes in
\dot{H}^{1}
as
t\to\infty
. Turning to
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},1)}
, we see that

\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t),\textup{fwd},1)}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim&\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(D)e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\partial_{x}(Vu(s))\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&+\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(D)e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\geq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\partial_{x}(Vu(s))\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&+\{\textup{similar or easier terms}\}\\
\lesssim&\int_{0}^{t}\lVert F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(D)e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(x)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}_{x}}\;ds\\
&+t^{1-\alpha\sigma}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\\
&+\{\textup{similar or easier terms}\}\\
\end{split}

so the problem reduces to proving bounds for
F_{\geq 2^{J(t)}}(x)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)}}(D)e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)
. The kernel associated with this operator is

K_{t,s}(x,y)=\frac{F_{\geq J(t)}(x)F(|y|\leq t^{\alpha})}{2\pi}\int e^{i((t-s)\xi^{2}+(x-y)\xi)}F(\xi\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)})\;d\xi

Repeated integration by parts gives the bound

\begin{split}|K_{t,s}(x,y)|\lesssim&\left|\sum_{a+b=N}C_{a,b}\int\frac{(t-s)^{a}t^{b\rho\theta}}{(2(t-s)\xi+(x-y))^{N+a}}F^{(b)}(\xi\geq 2^{-\theta J(t)})\;d\xi\right|\\
\lesssim_{N}&\frac{t^{(N-1)\rho\theta}}{(2(t-s)t^{-\rho\theta}+(x-y))^{N-1}}\end{split}

Since
K_{t,s}
is supported in the set
|x-y|\gtrsim t^{\rho}
, we find that

\lVert K_{t,s}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim_{N}t^{\rho\theta}t^{-(N-2)(1-\theta)\rho}

Taking
N
large shows that this term vanishes as
t\to\infty
, which completes the bounds for
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t))}
.

It only remains to bound the sum of the
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j)}
. We will focus on
u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}
, since the bounds for the backward in time terms are similar. By
Lemma

2
, we see that for
2^{j}<t^{\rho}

\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}e^{-it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{n}t^{-N}

Since
V(x,s)u(x,s)\in L^{\infty}_{s}H^{1}_{x}
, this implies that

\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}=&\left\lVert\partial_{x}\int_{0}^{t}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}e^{-it\Delta}F_{\leq t^{\alpha}}(x)e^{-is\Delta}Vu(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&t^{1-N}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}}\end{split}

Based on our definition of
J(t)
,
J(t)\leq 1+\rho\log_{2}\langle t\rangle
, so

\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}\lVert\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j)}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+\rho\log_{2}\langle t\rangle)t^{1-N}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}}=o(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}})

as required.

4.4.
Bounds for
u_{\textup{loc}}

We now show how to prove (
44
). For the low-frequency part
u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{low})}
, we note that

\begin{split}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{low})}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim&2^{2(\theta-1)j}\lVert F^{\prime}_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&+2^{2\theta j}\lVert F_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)\partial_{x}v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

with the obvious modifications for
j=0
. For the first term, we can sum directly to obtain

\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2(\theta-1)j}\lVert F^{\prime}_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

Turning to the second term, we observe that by
Lemma

5
,

\lVert F_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)\partial_{x}\left((1-F_{\sim 2^{j}}(|x|))v\right)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}2^{-\theta j}2^{-N(1+\theta)j}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

so

\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2\theta j}\lVert F_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)\partial_{x}v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim&\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2\theta j}\left\lVert F_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\leq 2^{-\theta j}}(|D|)\partial_{x}\left(F_{\sim 2^{j}}(|x|)v\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+&\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-(1+2\theta)j}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\lesssim&\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

Combining these estimates, we see that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{low})}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}<\infty

Thus, we only need to control
u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}
and
u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}
. The required bounds follow immediately from
Lemma

12
with
s^{\prime}=0,s=1
: For
\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}
, we find that

\begin{split}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}\rVert_{L^{2}}=&\left\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}P^{\textup{in}}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V(x,s)u(x,s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\sigma}Vu\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\end{split}

and a similar bound holds for
\langle x\rangle^{\theta}\partial_{x}u_{\textup{loc}}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}
, which is sufficient to get the bound (
9
).

5.
Higher-order symbol bounds

We now show how to prove the higher-order symbol bounds (
11
). Intuitively, we would like to proceed by induction: Having proven that the localized part satisfies (
11
) for some
n
, we want to use the symbol bounds for
V
together with

to prove better decay for higher derivatives. The problem is that the Duhamel integral for

involves

, while the decay bounds were only proved for

, preventing us from iterating directly.

5.1.
Definition of
u_{\textup{loc},n}

In order to obtain symbol bounds, we must redefine the localized part of the solution. Let us write

(47)

u_{\textup{loc},1}:=u_{\textup{loc}}

and define for
n>1

(48)

u_{\textup{loc},n}:=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{J(t)}u_{\textup{loc},n}^{(j)}

with

(49)

\begin{split}u_{\textup{loc},n}^{(j)}=&P^{\textup{low}}_{j}v(t)+iP^{\textup{in}}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}Vu_{\textup{loc},n-1}(s)\;ds\\
&-iP^{\textup{out}}_{j}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}Vu_{\textup{loc},n-1}(s)\;ds\\
=:&u_{\textup{loc},n}^{(j,\textup{low})}+u_{\textup{loc},n}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}+u_{\textup{loc},n}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}\end{split}

We also define

(50)

u_{\textup{rem},n}(t)=u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\textup{nr})}(t)+u_{\textup{rem}}^{(\geq J(t))}(t)+\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j)}(t)

with

(51)

\begin{split}u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j)}(t)=&u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j)}(t)+iP^{\textup{in}}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V(u-u_{\textup{loc},n-1})(s)\;ds\\
&-iP^{\textup{out}}_{j}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V(u-u_{\textup{loc},n-1})(s)\;ds\\
=:&u_{\textup{rem}}^{(j)}(t)+u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}(t)+u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}(t)\end{split}

As before, we have the identity

v(t)=u_{\textup{rem},n}(t)+u_{\textup{loc},n}(t)

so to complete the proof of
1
, it suffices to show that

(52)

\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta k}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{\textup{loc},n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}<\infty,\qquad\textup{for }k=0,1,2,\cdots n

and that

(53)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert\partial_{x}u_{\textup{rem},n}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}=0

The results of
Section

4
state that (
52
) and (
53
) are true when
n=1
. To prove that they hold for
n>1
, we proceed by induction and suppose that they hold for
n-1
. We will prove these statements in the following two subsections. The proof of (
52
) is given in
Section

5.2
, and amounts to an application of
Lemma

12
together with the strengthened inductive hypothesis

(54)

\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta k}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{\textup{loc},n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}<\infty,\qquad\textup{for }k=0,1,2,\cdots n,\;\;0\leq s<1

From here, it only remains to prove (
53
). We will say that a function
w(x,t)
is
remainder-type
if
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert\partial_{x}w\rVert_{L^{2}}=0
. Then, (
53
) is simply the statement that
u_{\textup{rem},n}
is remainder-type. To prove this, it suffices to prove that
\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}
and
\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}
are both remainder-type. To help with this, let us define the projected Duhamel integral operators

(55)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}\phi(t):=iP^{\textup{in}}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V\phi(s)\;ds\\
\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}\phi(t):=-iP^{\textup{out}}_{j}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V\phi(s)\;ds\\
\end{split}

so

u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}=\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}(u-u_{\textup{rem},n-1})

and similarly for
u_{\textup{rem},n}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}
. A quick calculation shows that

u(t)-u_{\textup{loc},n-1}(t)=u_{\textup{lin}}(t)+u_{\textup{low}}(t)+u_{\textup{rem},n-1}(t)

where

is the linearly scattering part of the solution. Based on the inductive hypothesis, (

\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}w
and
\sum_{j=0}^{J(t)}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}w
are remainder-type whenever

is remainder-type or

for some constant function
\phi

.

5.2.

Our goal in this section is to prove that (

We will now argue inductively to show that if (

and

\begin{split}\left\lVert\langle x\rangle^{\theta k}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{\textup{loc},n+1}^{(j,\textup{low})}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim&2^{k(\theta-1)j}\lVert F^{(k)}_{2^{j}}(|x|)F_{\geq 2^{-\theta j}}(D)v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\

is summable in

It only remains to bound

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{k\theta}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{\textup{loc},n+1}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s^{\prime}}_{x}}\lesssim&\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\lVert\langle x\rangle^{m\theta}\partial_{x}^{m}u_{\textup{loc},n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}\\

5.3.
Proof that
u_{\textup{rem},n}
is remainder-type

We will prove
Equation

as a consequence of the following result:

Lemma 14
.

Suppose that
\phi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
has the property that for any compact set

(56)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert\phi(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}+\lVert\partial_{x}\phi(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}=0

Then,

(57)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{x}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\partial_{x}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{bwd})}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)=0

We will begin with the proof for

. Based on the definition of

and the improved decay of

, we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x}\mathcal{D}^{(0,\textup{fwd})}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}V\partial_{x}\phi(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\{\textup{similar or easier}\}

(58a)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\leq 1}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

(58b)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\ell>0\end{subarray}}\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi(s)\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\{\textup{similar or easier}\}

We will focus on controlling the second sum (
58b

58a

\eqref{eqn:Dzfwd-ell}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\

For

\sup_{t}|B_{\ell,k}(t)|\lesssim 2^{\ell-k}\lVert F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim 2^{(1-\sigma)\ell}2^{-k}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

Applying the dominated convergent theorem, we now conclude that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>0\\
k\geq 0\end{subarray}}B_{\ell,k}(t)=0

For
A_{\ell,k}
, we observe that the kernel for
P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}e^{-i\tau\Delta}F_{\ell}(|x|)
is

(59)

\frac{F_{\leq 1}(|x|)F_{2^{\ell}}(|y|)}{2\pi}\int e^{i\tau\xi^{2}}e^{i\xi(x-y)}F_{\geq 1}(|\xi|)F_{2^{k}}(|\xi|)\;d\xi

and

\begin{split}\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}e^{-i\tau\Delta}F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim&2^{\ell/2}\sum_{a+b=N}\sup_{|x|\lesssim 1,|y|\sim 2^{\ell}}\int\frac{|\tau|^{a}2^{-bk}}{|2\tau\xi+(x-y)|^{N+a}}F_{2^{k}}^{(b)}(|\xi|)\;d\xi\\

Thus,

\sup_{t}|A_{\ell,k}(t)|\lesssim 2^{\ell/2}2^{(1-2N)k}2^{(1-N)(\ell-k)}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}}

is summable in
\ell
and
k
. Dividing the integral into

\begin{split}A_{\ell,k}\leq&\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}\int_{0}^{t/2}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&+\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{0}P_{k}\int_{t/2}^{t-C2^{\ell-k}}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&2^{\ell/2}2^{(1-2N)k}t^{1-N}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{X}}\\
&+2^{\ell/2}2^{(1-2N)k}2^{(1-N)(\ell-k)}\lVert F_{\sim\ell}(|x|)\partial_{x}\phi(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{s}(t/2,t;L^{2}_{x})}\end{split}

Taking limits, we see that
A_{\ell,k}(t)\to 0
for each
\ell
and
k

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 0\\
k\geq 0\end{subarray}}A_{\ell,k}=0

In particular, this shows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert\partial_{x}\mathcal{D}^{(0,\textup{fwd})}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}=0

For

\begin{split}\lVert\partial_{x}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\textup{fwd})}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>j-10\\
k\geq-\theta j\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}P_{k}\int_{0}^{t-C2^{\ell-k}}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{f2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}_{A^{j}_{\ell,k}}\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>j-10\\
k\geq-\theta j\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}P_{k}\int_{t-C2^{\ell-k}}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{2^{\ell}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}_{B^{j}_{\ell,k}}\\
&+\sum_{k\geq-\theta j}\underbrace{\left\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}P_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\Delta}F_{\leq 2^{j-10}}(|x|)V\partial_{x}\phi\;ds\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}_{C^{j}_{k}}\\
&+\{\textup{similar or easier}\}\\
\end{split}

As before, we see that

|B^{j}_{\ell,k}(t)|\lesssim 2^{(1-\sigma)\ell}2^{-k}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

so,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>j-10\\
k\geq-\theta j\end{subarray}}|B^{j}_{\ell,k}(t)|\lesssim 2^{(1+\theta-\sigma)j}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\in\ell^{1}_{j}

and, since

for each fixed

,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>j-10\\
k\geq-\theta j\end{subarray}}|B^{j}_{\ell,k}(t)|=0

For
A^{j}_{\ell,k}
, a slight modification of the argument above shows that

\lVert P^{\textup{in}}_{j}P_{k}e^{-i\tau\Delta}F_{\ell}(|x|)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{j/2}2^{\ell/2}2^{(1-2N)k}\tau^{-N}

so

A^{j}_{\ell,k}(t)\lesssim 2^{j/2}2^{\ell/2}2^{(1-2N)k}2^{(1-N)(\ell-k)}\lVert\partial_{x}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}

The right-hand side is summable in
j,k,
and
\ell
for

,

, and splitting the time integral into

Thus, integrating shows that

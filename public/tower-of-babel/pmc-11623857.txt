Autologous Testicle Construction With Buried Metoidioplasty Correction
Authors: Payton J. Sparks, Harley L. Moit, Alan Tom, Joshua Roth, Ivan Hadad
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Summary:Gender-affirming surgery is essential for transgender individuals seeking alignment between their physical appearance and gender identity. Metoidioplasty is a masculinizing option for those assigned female at birth and often includes vaginectomy, urethral lengthening, scrotoplasty, creation of a neophallus, and testicular prostheses, typically implanted during a second-stage procedure. We describe a 39-year-old transgender man who initially underwent a laparoscopic hysterectomy, metoidioplasty, and tubularized plate urethral lengthening 19 months earlier. Although the patient could achieve an erection and orgasm, he struggled with standing urination due to the penis being buried by the mons pubis and upper labia majora. In addition, he feared superior migration of the testicular prostheses, which could diminish the prominence of the neophallus. We elected to perform a monsplasty with resection and rearrangement of excess upper labial tissue to mimic testicles. This approach helps avoid the risks associated with prostheses, using the patient’s own tissues instead. This second-stage operation enhances the appearance of the neophallus and creates the cosmetic appearance of testicles using the patient’s own tissues, offering a safe and effective surgical option. Although metoidioplasty offers significant benefits in terms of appearance and sensation, it has limitations, including the challenge of achieving standing urination due to the typically shorter length of the neophallus. We aimed to present the effectiveness and associated patient satisfaction with this innovative approach, showing its viability as a safe surgical option.

Takeaways

Question: Is there a technique available for patients who wish to have the appearance of testicles while avoiding prosthetics?

Findings: This technique uses excess labial tissue around a metoidioplasty neophallus to mimic testicles in the neoscrotum without the use of implants, while simultaneously helping to further expose the neophallus.

Meaning: This is a viable option for patients wishing to avoid prosthetic testicles while helping expose the neophallus.

INTRODUCTION

Metoidioplasty stands as 1 of many procedures available to individuals seeking masculinizing gender-affirming surgery. This procedure is highly variable but often includes vaginectomy, urethral lengthening, scrotoplasty, creation of a neophallus, and testicular prostheses that are often implanted during a second-stage procedure.1 This technique has proven to be a great option as it yields a neophallus of acceptable aesthetic quality, allows for upright voiding, and preserves erogenous erections and sensation—all pivotal aspects for patient well-being.2 However, it has limitations, including the challenge of achieving standing urination due to the typically shorter length of the neophallus. Phalloplasty, although associated with higher rates of complications and longer recovery times, is often a more reliable option for patients whose primary goal is to achieve standing urination. In the subset of patients who would be able to achieve standing urination after metoidioplasty, if not for the residual excess labial tissue, the procedure discussed here may be an effective option.

Despite the ability of testicular prostheses to produce the patient’s desired aesthetic, these procedures do not come without potential complications. In the event of infection or extrusion necessitating explantation, patients face the decision of undergoing replacement or opting to leave the testicular prosthesis out. Given the rate of complications, patients may seek alternatives that offer the aesthetic appearance of testicles without the complications of implant-based solutions.3 This surgical technique is a viable alternative for individuals wanting the visual semblance of testicles while avoiding potential complications with testicular prostheses.

INDICATIONS/SURGICAL CASE

Our patient is a 39-year-old transgender man who initially underwent a laparoscopic hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, metoidioplasty, and tubularized plate urethral lengthening procedure on December 6, 2021. The patient’s medical history includes gender dysphoria, depression/anxiety, and hypothyroidism, and they were taking venlafaxine and levothyroxine. The patient reported the ability to obtain an erection and orgasm but was bothered by excess soft tissue that effectively buried the penis. Further, he feared the mass or potential superior migration of testicular implants would further bury his neophallus, given its approximately 2-cm length. We designed an operation that would remove excess mons and upper labial tissue with rearrangement of the inferior aspect of the labia to mimic testicles.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

A central arch-shaped flap was marked to advance vertically and efface the fullness of the mons. An arch-shaped resection was marked out superiorly for this to later be inset without tension. Extra skin was left at the base of the superior arch, which would later be de-epithelialized. Next, we marked out horizontal excisions with margins at the inner and outer aspects of the labia majora. Superiorly, the labial excisions were blended into the arch-shaped flap. Inferiorly, below the phallus, the labial tissue was not excised but rather de-epithelialized. The flaps were approximately 3 × 3 cm at the base of the labia minora caudal to the penis (Fig. 1). The resultant skin closure inferiorly would be with a very obtuse angle, which would, by design, create “dog ears.” This would allow for improved prominence of the resultant testicle creation. On either side of this, triangular excisions at the wound margins were designed, paralleling both inguinal folds. Tumescent fluid (1 L normal saline, 2 mg epinephrine, and 30 mL of 1% lidocaine) was infiltrated widely. We resected the skin within the marks at the level of Scarpa fascia. The area of de-epithelialized labial tissue was folded inward and downward into a pocket made within the remnant labia to give the appearance of testicles within a scrotum bilaterally. The overall design does create bilateral triple points within competing vectors of tension. To mitigate any potential wound dehiscence, the extra de-epithelialized triangles of tissue at the bilateral bases of the superior arch are inset underneath the triple points. All incisions were then closed with 2-0 and 3-0 Monocryl sutures and covered with skin glue (Fig. 2). The overall resection vectors effaced the mons pubis and labial tissues both vertically and horizontally (Fig. 3).

POSTOPERATIVE MANAGEMENT AND OUTCOMES

The pain was managed with Tylenol and ibuprofen as needed, as well as a small prescription of oxycodone. Follow-up visits were at 2 weeks, 9 weeks, and 6 months (Fig. 4) postoperatively. The patient was very pleased with the appearance of the testicles, as well as the unburied neophallus. The patient is working to be able to manually manipulate the neophallus to allow for standing urination. The remaining difficulties stem from poor response to testosterone replacement and shorter phallic length. This is inherent to metoidioplasty. Despite the successful aesthetic outcomes, the patient’s goal of standing urination was not fully achieved due to the buried nature of the neophallus. This highlights the importance of setting realistic expectations and considering alternative procedures such as phalloplasty for patients with specific functional goals. DISCUSSION

Advancements in scrotoplasty techniques have been pivotal in addressing the unique needs and preferences of transgender individuals undergoing gender-affirming surgery. Early techniques, such as preserving labial tissue and using midline closure with testicular implants, aimed to achieve the visual appearance of a scrotum but often reminded patients of their female past.4

Similarly, approaches involving thigh, groin, or pubic flaps were complicated by their complexity, longer operating times, and unsatisfactory scarring outcomes. Their elaborate nature posed significant challenges, necessitating the need for simpler, more effective alternatives.4

In recent years, techniques have used labia majora flaps, due to their embryological similarity to scrotal tissue and offering promising results in terms of color, texture, and hair-bearing capacity. Hoebeke technique, which incorporates a 90-degree rotation of labial tissue, has been noteworthy in positioning the scrotum more anteriorly, enhancing the natural appearance of scrotal bifidity.5

Although testicular implants further enhance the visual and physical resemblance of testicles, they are not without complications. A study by Pigot et al3 examined 206 patients who had undergone various forms of testicular prosthesis implantation. In 43 (20.8%) of the patients, 1 or both prostheses were explanted due to issues such as infection, extrusion, or dislocation. In light of this, Everett et al6 have adapted the V-Y scrotoplasty technique, by using rotational flaps of the superior labia majora as autologous tissue scrotal augmentation.

Our technique represents a variant scrotoplasty technique, for patients with a buried penis after metoidioplasty, who desire the visual semblance of testicles. Instead of inferomedially rotating cutaneous flaps of the superior labial skin to create a neoscrotum, we de-epithelialize superior labial tissue and invert it into pockets of remaining inferior labial skin to mimic testicles. Both approaches seek to meet the same goal but in different manners. The method described here may be best suited for patients with significant mons fullness and redundant inferior labial tissue. It can also be used for patients who do want testicular implants, as the de-epithelialized flaps can act as a second layer to prevent extrusion and superior migration. Transparent communication and thorough preoperative counseling are critical to ensuring that patients have realistic expectations and understand the potential outcomes and limitations of their chosen procedure.

CONCLUSIONS

In summary, this proposed surgical technique emerges as a promising alternative for individuals seeking the aesthetic appearance of testicles without the associated complications of implant-based reconstruction. With increasing concerns regarding complications with testicular prostheses, this approach offers a reasonable autologous reconstruction option for patients.

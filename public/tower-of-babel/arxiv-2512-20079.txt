Chebyshev’s method applied to polynomials with two distinct roots
Tarakanta Nayak, Pooja Phogat

Abstract
The Julia set of the Chebyshev’s method applied to polynomials with exactly two distinct roots is shown to be connected, and its Fatou set is proved to be the union of attracting basins corresponding to the two roots. Further, if the two roots have the same multiplicity then the common boundary of the two immediate basins is proved to be a connected subset of the Julia set.



Keywords:

Chebyshev’s method; Polynomials; Fatou set; Julia set.

AMS Subject Classification: 37F10, 65H05

Introduction

Finding the roots of a polynomial is a classical as well as an extensively studied problem.
A
root-finding method
is a function that assigns to each polynomial
p:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, a rational map
F_{p}
such that every root
z_{0}
of
p
is an attracting fixed point of
F_{p}
, i.e.,
F_{p}(z_{0})=z_{0}
and
|(F_{p})^{\prime}(z_{0})|<1
. Here
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. The forward orbit
\{F_{p}^{n}(z):n\geq 1\}
of a point
z

sufficiently close
to a root of
p
converges to that root of
p
.
However, other points can behave very differently. For some root-finding methods, there may exist polynomials and initial points whose forward orbits fail to converge or converge to a point that is
not
a root of the polynomial. Although such situations are undesirable from the root-finding perspective, these give rise to interesting dynamical phenomena.
This article is concerned with the dynamics of the Chebyshev’s method applied to polynomials with two distinct roots.

By dynamics of an analytic function, we mean its Fatou and the Julia set.
Given a rational function
R
of degree at least two, the
Fatou set

\mathcal{F}(R)
is the set of all points in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
for which there exists a neighbourhood where the family of iterates
\{R^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is equicontinuous.
Its complement in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is the
Julia set

\mathcal{J}(R)
.
By definition,
\mathcal{F}(R)
is open while
\mathcal{J}(R)
, being its complement, is closed. A maximally connected component of
\mathcal{F}(R)
is called a
Fatou component
. Each Fatou component is either periodic or some iterated image of it is periodic. A periodic Fatou component is an attracting domain, a parabolic domain, a Siegel disk or a Herman ring (see
[
1
]
for further details).

For an attracting fixed point
z_{0}
, the set of all points converging to
z_{0}
under iteration of
R
is called the
basin of attraction
of
z_{0}
, i.e., the set

\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:\lim_{n\to\infty}R^{n}(z)=z_{0}\}.

The connected component of the basin of attraction containing
z_{0}
is the
immediate basin
or
attracting domain
and is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{z_{0}}
.

For a non-constant polynomial
p
, the
Chebyshev’s method
is defined by the rational map

C_{p}(z)=z-\left(1+\frac{1}{2}L_{p}(z)\right)\frac{p(z)}{p^{\prime}(z)},\penalty\ \mbox{where}\penalty\ L_{p}(z)=\frac{p(z)p^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\left(p^{\prime}(z)\right)^{2}}\text{.}

If
p
is a linear polynomial, then
C_{p}
is constant. The Chebyshev’s method differs from the widely studied Newton method. The two significant differences are that Chebyshev’s method exhibits
third-order convergence
at each simple root of
p
, meaning its local degree at such roots is at least three, and that it can have a fixed point different from the roots of the polynomial.
The dynamics of
C_{p}
has been studied in
[
4
]
, where it is shown that if a cubic polynomial
p
is unicritical (i.e., it has only one critical point) or non-generic (i.e., it has at least one multiple root), then the Julia set of
C_{p}
is connected. A
critical point
of a rational map is a point where the local degree of the map is at least two. The derivative of the map vanishes at a finite critical point. A critical point
c^{*}
of
p
is said to be
special
if
p(c^{*})\neq 0
but
p^{\prime\prime}(c^{*})=0
. Suppose
p
has
N
distinct roots and
s
distinct special critical points. Then the degree of
C_{p}
,
\deg(C_{p})=3N+s-B-2
, where
B
denotes the sum of the multiplicities of all special critical points of
p
(see Theorem 1.1,
[
4
]
). From this degree formula, it follows that if
p
has at least three distinct roots then
\deg(C_{p})\geq 6
, with equality only when
p
is a cubic unicritical polynomial. Interestingly, there is no polynomial
p
for which the degree of
C_{p}
is five. The lowest possible degree of
C_{p}
is four, and it occurs precisely when
p
has exactly two distinct roots. This motivates the study of the Chebyshev’s method applied to polynomials with two distinct roots.

It has been proved by Nayak and Pal that if a polynomial is centered (i.e., with zero as the second leading coefficient) and has exactly two distinct roots with equal multiplicity, then the Julia set of
C_{p}
is connected and
C_{p}
is convergent (Theorem A,
[
5
]
), i.e., the Fatou set is the union of attracting basins corresponding to the roots of
p
. A key ingredient in their proof was that the critical points of
C_{p}
(up to the Scaling property) are symmetric not only with respect to the real line but also with respect to the origin. However, this is no longer true if the polynomial is not centered or the restriction on the multiplicity of roots is removed. In this article, we complete the dynamical study of the Chebyshev’s method applied to all polynomials that are not necessarily centered and having exactly two roots without any restriction on their multiplicities.

Theorem A
.

If
p
is a polynomial with exactly two distinct roots, then the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(C_{p})
is connected and
C_{p}
is convergent, i.e., the Fatou set
\mathcal{F}(C_{p})
is the union of the basins of attractions corresponding to the roots of
p
.

N_{p}

\mathcal{J}(N_{p})

can be infinite (for example, see Theorem A(1)

Connectedness of the Julia set implies that each Fatou component of
C_{p}
is simply connected. In particular, the immediate basins corresponding to the roots of
p
are simply connected. Moreover, for each immediate basin
\mathcal{A}
corresponding to a root of
p
, the restriction
C_{p}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is a proper map of degree three. This follows from the Riemann-Hurwitz formula (see Theorem 5.4.1,
[

). Since the degree of
C_{p}
is four (see Equation

\mathcal{A}
is not completely invariant. Consequently, there are infinitely many Fatou components in addition to the two immediate basins.
In particular, the Julia set of
C_{p}
is not the common boundary of these two immediate basins. We investigate this common boundary in the case where the two roots of
p
have equal multiplicity and establish the following.

Theorem B
.

If
p
is a polynomial with exactly two distinct roots and the multiplicities of these two roots are equal, then the common boundary of the two immediate basins corresponding to the roots of
p
is a connected subset of the Julia set of
C_{p}
.

Preliminary results

Two rational maps
R
and
S
are said to be
conformally conjugate
(or
conjugate
) if there exists a Möbius transformation
\phi
such that

S=\phi\circ R\circ\phi^{-1},

where
\circ
denotes function composition. Since

S^{n}=\phi\circ R^{n}\circ\phi^{-1}\quad\text{for all }n,

the iterative behaviours of
R
and
S
are essentially the same. More precisely, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.1

(Theorem 3.1.4,
[
1
]
)
.

If
S
and
R
are rational maps with
S=\phi\circ R\circ\phi^{-1}
for some Möbius map
\phi
, then

\mathcal{J}(S)=\phi(\mathcal{J}(R)).

The Chebyshev’s method applied to different polynomials can give rise to the same rational map up to conjugacy. This is made precise by the
Scaling property
.

Lemma 2.2

(Scaling property, Theorem 2.2,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
p
be a polynomial of degree at least two. If
T(z)=\alpha z+\beta
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},\alpha\neq 0
, and
g=\lambda p\circ T
for
\lambda\neq 0
, then

T\circ C_{g}\circ T^{-1}=C_{p}.

Taking
T(z)=(b-a)z+a
where
a,b
are the only roots of
p
, and
\lambda
as the reciprocal of the leading coefficient of
p\circ T
, a useful consequence of the Scaling property follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
p
is a polynomial with exactly two distinct roots then
C_{p}
is conformally conjugate to
C_{q}
where
q(z)=z^{k}(z-1)^{m}
for some natural numbers
k,m
.

Let
p
be a polynomial with exactly two distinct roots with multilplicities
k
and
m
.
It can be observed from Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
2.1
that the Julia set of
C_{p}
is connected and the Fatou set of
C_{p}
is the union of the two attracting basins corresponding to the roots of
p
if and only if it is so for
C_{q}
where
q(z)=z^{k}(z-1)^{m}
. For proving Theorems
A
and
B
, we assume without loss of generality, that

p(z)=z^{k}(z-1)^{m}\penalty\ \mbox{for}\penalty\ k,m\geq 1.

(1)

Then
p^{\prime}(z)=z^{k-1}(z-1)^{m-1}((k+m)z-k),p^{\prime\prime}(z)=z^{k-2}(z-1)^{m-2}f(z)\penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=z^{k-3}(z-1)^{m-3}g(z)
, where
f(z)=(k+m)(k+m-1)z^{2}-2k(k+m-1)z+k(k-1)
and
g(z)=(k+m)(k+m-1)(k+m-2)z^{3}-3k(k+m-1)(k+m-2)z^{2}+3k(k-1)(k+m-2)z-k(k-1)(k-2)
.
Note that these expressions remain valid even for
k,m\leq 2
. Consequently,

L_{p}(z)=\frac{f(z)}{\left(kz+mz-k\right)^{2}},

and

C_{p}(z)=z-z(z-1)\left(\frac{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)z^{2}-2k\left(3k+3m-1\right)z+3k^{2}-k}{2\left(kz+mz-k\right)^{3}}\right).

(2)

We can also write

C_{p}(z)=\frac{A_{0}z^{4}+A_{1}z^{3}+A_{2}z^{2}+A_{3}z}{2\left((k+m)z-k\right)^{3}},

(3)

where
A_{0}=(k+m)(k+m-1)(2(k+m)-1)
,
A_{1}=(3-6k)(k+m)^{2}+(6k-1)(k+m)-2k
,
A_{2}=3k(k-1)(2(k+m)-1)
, and
A_{3}=-k(k-1)(2k-1)
. An immediate consequence of Equation (
2
) is the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
c
is a non-real critical point of
C_{p}
, then
\overline{c}
is also a critical point of
C_{p}
. Moreover, every Fatou component of
C_{p}
containing a real number is symmetric with respect to the real line.

Proof.

For every
n\geq 1
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
C_{p}^{n}(\bar{z})=\overline{C_{p}^{n}(z)}
. Taking
n=1
and differentiating it, yields
\overline{C_{p}^{\prime}(\overline{z})}=C_{p}^{\prime}(z)
, and hence
C_{p}^{\prime}(c)=0
if and only if
C_{p}^{\prime}(\overline{c})=0
. It also follows that the Fatou set is symmetric with respect to the real line. In particular, every Fatou component containing a real number is symmetric with respect to the real line.
∎

For stating some useful information about the fixed points of the Chebyshev’s method, we recall relevant definitions.

A point
z_{0}\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a
fixed point
of a rational map
R
if
R(z_{0})=z_{0}
.
The
multiplier

\lambda_{z_{0}}
of a fixed point

is defined as

is finite and as
S^{\prime}(0)

, where
S(z)=\tfrac{1}{R(1/z)}
. A fixed point
z_{0}
is called attracting, neutral or repelling if
|\lambda_{z_{0}}|<1,=1
or
>1
, respectively.
An attracting fixed point is called
super-attracting
if its multiplier is
0
.

Lemma 2.4

(Proposition 2.3,
[
4
]
)
.

If
a
is a root of a polynomial
p
with multiplicity

, then it is a fixed point of
C_{p}
with multiplier
\frac{(k-1)(2k-1)}{2k^{2}}
. Moreover, the point at
\infty
is also a fixed point of
C_{p}
and its multiplier is
\frac{2d^{2}}{2d^{2}-3d+1}
, where

is the degree of
p
.

We need the following two well-known results in complex dynamics.

Lemma 2.5
.

(

Let

(Lemma 4.3,

Let

The behaviour of analytic branches of the inverse of the iterates of a rational function on a domain intersecting its Julia set is going to be useful for this article. We put Theorem 9.2.1 and Lemma 9.2.2 of

Proofs of results

In view of the discussion preceding Equation (
1
), it is enough to prove Theorems
A
and
B
for
p(z)=z^{k}(z-1)^{m}
for
k,m\geq 1
. Recall that

C_{p}(z)=z-z(z-1)\left(\frac{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)z^{2}-2k\left(3k+3m-1\right)z+3k^{2}-k}{2\left(kz+mz-k\right)^{3}}\right).

The next four lemmas discuss the critical points, extraneous fixed points and the behaviour of
C_{p}
on the real line.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is no critical point of
C_{p}
in
(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)
. Furthermore,
C_{p}^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle C_{p}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p(z)p^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\left(p^{\prime}(z)\right)^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(3-\frac{p^{\prime}(z)p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{\left(p^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)^{2}}\right)=\left(p(z)\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\left(3\left(p^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)^{2}-p^{\prime}(z)p^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)\right)}{2(p^{\prime}(z))^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=z^{2k}(z-1)^{2m}\left(\frac{3z^{2k-4}(z-1)^{2m-4}\left(f(z)\right)^{2}-z^{2k-4}(z-1)^{2m-4}\left((k+m)z-k\right)g(z)}{2(p^{\prime}(z))^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\left(f(z)\right)^{2}-\left(kz+mz-k\right)g(z)}{2\left(kz+mz-k\right)^{4}},

where the polynomials
f,g

are as defined just after Equation (
1
).
In other words,

C_{p}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{F(z)}{2\left(kz+mz-k\right)^{4}},

where

F(z)=(k+m)^{2}(k+m-1)(2k+2m-1)z^{4}+4k(k+m)(k+m-1)(-2k-2m+1)z^{3}+3k(k(k+m-1)(3k+3m-2)+(k-1)(k+m)^{2})z^{2}-2k(k-1)((4k+1)(k+m)-3k)z+k^{2}(k-1)(2k-1)
.
The critical points of
C_{p}
are
\frac{k}{k+m}
(counted twice) and the four roots of the quartic polynomial
F
.

Note that the coefficients of the odd powers of
z
in

are always negative, whereas those of the even powers are positive. Therefore,
F(z)
is positive for all
z\in(-\infty,0)
. Hence
C_{p}
has no critical point in
(-\infty,0)
.

In order to examine the behaviour of
F
on
(0,\infty)
, we consider

F(x+1)=(k+m)^{2}(k+m-1)(2(k+m)-1)x^{4}+4m(k+m)(k+m-1)(2(k+m)-1)x^{3}+3m(k^{2}(4m-1)+km(8m-7)+2m(2m^{2}-3m+1))x^{2}+2m(m-1)(k(4m+1)+2m(2m-1))x+m^{2}(2m^{2}-3m+1)
.
For
k,m\geq 1
, each coefficient of
F(x+1)
is either zero or positive. Moreover, the coefficient of

is strictly greater than
1
. This gives that
F(x+1)>1
for every

. Since every real number in
(1,\infty)
can be written as

F((1,\infty))\subset(1,\infty)
. In other words,

has no zero in
(1,\infty)

C_{p}
has no critical point in
(1,\infty)
.

It also follows from the two previous paragraphs that
C_{p}^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)
.
∎

It follows from Equation

These are given explicitly by

\frac{2k\left(3k+3m-1\right)\pm\sqrt{(2k\left(3k+3m-1\right))^{2}-4(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)(3k^{2}-k)}}{2(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right))}
, which upon simplification is nothing but,

\frac{k\left(3k+3m-1\right)\pm\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right))}.

Since

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k\left(3k+3m-1\right)-\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right))}=\frac{k}{k+m}-\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}\penalty\ \mbox{and}

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{k\left(3k+3m-1\right)+\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right))}=\frac{k}{k+m}+\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}.

\frac{k}{k+m}

\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}>0

Lemma 3.2
.

Both extraneous fixed points of
C_{p}
lie in the interval
(0,1)
, and their multipliers are greater than
1

Proof.

Since
e_{1}<e_{2}
, it suffices to show that
e_{1}>0
and
e_{2}<1

k,m\geq 1

First,
e_{1}>0

\frac{k}{k+m}-\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m)-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}>0,
which implies

\frac{k}{k+m}>\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}.
Squaring both sides gives

\left(\frac{k}{k+m}\right)^{2}>\frac{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}{(k+m)^{2}\left(3k+3m-1\right)^{2}},
which simplifies to

k>\frac{m}{\left(3k+3m-1\right)}.
Equivalently,

(3k-1)(k+m)>0

e_{1}>0

Similarly,

e_{2}<1

\frac{\sqrt{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}}{(k+m)\left(3k+3m-1\right)}<\frac{m}{k+m}.
Squaring both sides gives

\frac{km\left(3k+3m-1\right)}{(k+m)^{2}\left(3k+3m-1\right)^{2}}<\left(\frac{m}{k+m}\right)^{2},
or equivalently

\frac{k}{\left(3k+3m-1\right)}<m.
This can be written as

(3m-1)(k+m)>0,
which is actually
true for all
k,m\geq 1.
Therefore
e_{2}<1
.

Consider
g(z)=(z-1)^{k}(z+1)^{m}

T(z)=\frac{-z+1}{2}

g(z)=\lambda p(T(z))

T\circ C_{g}\circ T^{-1}=C_{p}

The Chebyshev’s method applied to any polynomial with two distinct roots is a quartic rational map. Indeed, every Chebyshev’s method with degree four arises from a polynomial with two distinct roots. Such rational maps have two attracting and three repelling fixed points. It might be interesting to find a minimal set of assumptions under which a quartic rational map is conjugate (or equal) to the Chebyshev’s method applied to a polynomial with two distinct roots.

A proof or a counterexample to any of these would be interesting to know.

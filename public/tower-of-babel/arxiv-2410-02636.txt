Inapproximability of Finding Sparse Vectors in Codes, Subspaces, and Lattices
Vijay Bhattiprolu, Venkatesan Guruswami, Euiwoong Lee, Xuandi Ren

Abstract
Finding sparse vectors is a fundamental problem that arises in several contexts including codes, subspaces, and lattices. In this work, we prove strong inapproximability results for all these variants using a novel approach that even bypasses the PCP theorem. Our main result is that it is NP-hard (under randomized reductions) to approximate the sparsest vector in a real subspace within any constant factor; the gap can be further amplified using tensoring. Our reduction has the property that there is a Boolean solution in the completeness case. As a corollary, this immediately recovers the state-of-the-art inapproximability factors for the shortest vector problem (SVP) on lattices. Our proof extends the range of ℓ p \ell_{p} (quasi) norms for which hardness was previously known, from p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 to all p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 , answering a question raised by (Khot, JACM 2005). Previous hardness results for SVP, and the related minimum distance problem (MDP) for error-correcting codes, all use lattice/coding gadgets that have an abundance of codewords in a ball of radius smaller than the minimum distance. In contrast, our reduction only needs many codewords in a ball of radius slightly larger than the minimum distance. This enables an easy derandomization of our reduction for finite fields, giving a new elementary proof of deterministic hardness for MDP. We believe this weaker density requirement might offer a promising approach to showing deterministic hardness of SVP, a long elusive goal. The key technical ingredient underlying our result for real subspaces is a proof that in the kernel of a random Rademacher matrix, the support of any two linearly independent vectors have very little overlap. A broader motivation behind this work is the development of inapproximability techniques for problems over the reals. Analytic variants of sparsest vector have connections to small set expansion, quantum separability and polynomial maximization over convex sets, all of which appear to be out of reach of current PCP techniques. We hope that the approach we develop could enable progress on some of these problems.

Overview of Reduction.

All our results are “PCP-free" and reduce from the NP-hard problem of solving a system of quadratic equations over the concerned field
{\mathbb{F}}
(a finite field or the reals).

4

Sometimes we consider homogeneous systems, and technically we work with a promise variant where YES instances have a Boolean solution whereas NO instances lack any solution over the field
{\mathbb{F}}

At a high level, we consider the subspace

The reduction above needed two features from the gadget code: (Weak Local Density): lots of codewords of Hamming weight

For finite fields, (Non-Overlap) is automatically true for any code with

\pm 1

As for (Weak Local Density), it has already been shown in

A Brief History of the Homogenization Framework.

When
U
is an affine subspace or an affine lattice, we obtain non-homogeneous variants of MDP and SVP that are known as the
Nearest Codeword Problem (NCP) and the Closest Vector Problem (CVP) respectively.
Inapproximability of NCP and CVP can be deduced quite easily from the PCP theorem. In fact, this is one of the early applications
of the PCP theorem
[
ABSS93
]

A long line

All of these works follow the template of reducing from a non-homogeneous problem (CVP or NCP) whose hardness is established via the PCP theorem,
and then reducing to its homogeneous version (say SVP or MDP) by embedding solutions to the non-homogeneous problem inside a locally dense lattice/code.

We break from this paradigm in a few ways. Over finite fields, in contrast to

Towards Derandomized Hardness of SVP.

It remains open to derandomize our reduction, and in particular obtain the long elusive NP-hardness of SVP. Our approach might be more amenable to derandomization as it requires lots of vectors of sparsity
(1+\varepsilon)d
instead of many vectors of sparsity
\leq(1-\varepsilon)d

Using the same reduction, the local density requirement can be further weakened at a cost of demanding more from higher order Hamming weight and the hardness of quadratic equations. This seems a compelling direction for future investigation.

Tensor Codes and Distance Amplification

We show hardness of MDP by first generating a constant factor gap and then using the standard observation that the minimum distance of a code is multiplicative under the usual tensor product operation, which we prove below for completeness.

The tensor product of two subspaces

Let

Then we have

Fact 2.2
.

For any subspaces

and

d(U\otimes V)=d(U)\cdot d(V)
.

We defer the proof of this standard fact to

Fact 2.3
.

For any subspace

and any

.

2.3
Hamming Weight of Rank
\geq 2
Elements of a Tensor Code

Recall that in

2.2
, the upper bound

) is attained by a rank-1 matrix.
The following result implies that for any linear code over small fields, codewords of rank
\geq 2

have Hamming weight significantly larger than the minimum distance. Austrin and Khot

Lemma 2.4

(Rank-

[
AK14
]
)
.

For every subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and every

\|M\|_{0}\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)\cdot d(C)^{2}

The above lemma follows from the fact that the support of a 2-dimensional subspace of a linear code, which is called the 2nd Generalized Hamming weight in the literature

Definition 2.5

(Non-Overlap)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field. A subspace
C\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}
is said to be
\alpha
-non-overlapping if for some
\alpha\geq 1
if for any

that are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}
, we have

|\text{supp}(u)\cup\text{supp}(v)|\geq\alpha\cdot d(C)\ .

We call
\alpha

.

Equipped with the above definition, we now state and prove a generalization of
Lemma

2.4
abstracted through the
\alpha
-non-overlapping property.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field and
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be an arbitrary subspace that is
\alpha
-non-overlapping for some
\alpha\geq 1
. Then every
M\in C\otimes C
of rank at least
2
satisfies
\|M\|_{0}\geq\alpha\cdot d(C)^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
M
is of rank at least
2
, there are two linearly independent
columns, that have joint support of size at least
\alpha\cdot d(C)
by the
\alpha
-non-overlapping property of
C
. Thus at least
\alpha\cdot d(C)
rows of
M
are non-zero, and since they lie inside
C
, each of these rows has at least
d(C)
non-zero entries.
Thus
\|M\|_{0}\geq\alpha\cdot d(C)^{2}
.

The following lemma shows one can take
\alpha=1+\frac{1}{q}
for any subspace over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and this is in general tight as evidenced by the Hadamard code.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
C
be an arbitrary subspace over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then
C
is
\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)
-non-overlapping.

The proof of
Lemma

2.7
is quite simple and can be found in

2.4

The above result rules out vectors of sparsity

Such vectors are referred to as

\delta d

Let
\rho,\delta>0,d\in{\mathbb{N}}

\delta\cdot d<(c_{0}^{2}/4)d/\log d

\rho<(c_{0}/2)/\sqrt{\log d}
,

is the same constant as in
Theorem

5.3

Proof.

Consider any

\mathchoice{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}\leq\rho

\mathchoice{\lVert u\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u\rVert_{1}}=\mathchoice{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{1}}+\mathchoice{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{1}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{1}}\leq\sqrt{\delta\cdot d}\mathchoice{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{2}}+\sqrt{d}\mathchoice{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert u_{\text{supp}(u)\setminus T}\rVert_{2}}<c_{0}\sqrt{d/\log d}.

By

, no such vector

\ker(R)
.

Let

.

5.4

for
\rho^{2},\delta\preceq 1/\log d

d^{\prime}
-sparse vectors in

\mathrm{Incomp}^{d^{\prime}}_{\rho,\delta}:=\mathbb{S}^{N}_{d^{\prime}}\setminus\mathrm{Comp}^{d^{\prime}}_{\rho,\delta}\ .

For

, incompressible vectors

have
\succeq d/\log d

\succeq 1/\sqrt{d\log d}
.
By the classical Littlewood-Offord inequality
[
LO39
]
, any such vector

has low small-ball probability, i.e.,
\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[|\sum_{i}u_{i}\xi_{i}|\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[|\sum_{i}u_{i}\xi_{i}|\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[|\sum_{i}u_{i}\xi_{i}|\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[|\sum_{i}u_{i}\xi_{i}|\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}
.
It follows that
\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}]}\leq\log^{O(h)}d/d^{h/2}

, it suffices to take a union bound of the bad event
\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}\leq\log^{O(1)}d/\sqrt{d}

Such a net has size

.

Let

Restricted Maximum Singular Value.

Returning to the discussion of minimum distance of a random Rademacher kernel, we bound the error incurred in
\mathchoice{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert Ru\rVert_{\infty}}
in passing from
u\in\mathbb{S}^{N}_{d^{\prime}}
to the net point closest to it, in terms of the maximum singular value of

restricted to the columns in

.
Since we require such an estimate for all size-

submatrices, we will use an estimate on

.
We have

(Restricted Maximum Singular Value

]
; see also
[

]
)
.

There is a constant

, such that for any

5.2
Non-Overlap for Random Rademacher Kernel

In this section we establish the somewhat surprising fact that any pair of linearly independent vectors in
\ker(R)
of near-minimum sparsity overlap in a negligible fraction of their support. We deduce this as a consequence of the fact that the second order Hamming weight of
\ker(R)
is nearly
twice the minimum distance:

Definition 5.9

(Higher Order Hamming Weight)
.

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the order-
\ell
Hamming weight of a subspace
C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{N}
(denoted as
d_{\ell}(C)
) is the smallest joint support size

|\sigma^{u_{1}}\cup\dots\cup\sigma^{u_{\ell}}|

for any collection of linearly independent vectors
u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell}\in C
.

We will prove
d_{2}(\ker(R))\geq 2(1-\varepsilon)d
. We first sketch the proof, collecting necessary preliminaries enroute.

Non-Overlap for the Boolean Slice.

Similar to the minimum distance, the case of Boolean vectors is instructive to appreciate the quantitative aspects of the non-overlapping property.

Let
d_{2}:=2(1-\varepsilon)d
. No two linearly independent vectors
u,v\in H_{\leq d_{2}}^{N}\cap\ker(R)
can overlap in all but
\preceq d/\log d
coordinates, since in that case
u-v\in\ker(R)
has sparsity
\preceq d/\log d
which would contradict
Corollary

5.4
.

So, it remains to consider the case where the symmetric difference of
\text{supp}(u),\text{supp}(v)
is of size
\succeq d/\log d
. In the extreme case where they have disjoint support, by independence we have

\textstyle\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}=\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=0\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=0]}\cdot\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=0\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=0]}\preceq 1/d.

Thus
\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[Ru=Rv=0\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[Ru=Rv=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[Ru=Rv=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[Ru=Rv=0]}\leq O(1/d)^{h}
. A similar bound is possible for the
more general case where the symmetric difference
\text{supp}(u)\triangle\text{supp}(v)
has size
\succeq d/\log d
. Indeed we have

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\textstyle\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\textstyle\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\textstyle\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\textstyle\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot u(i)=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\cdot v(i)=0]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=-d_{2}}^{d_{2}}\mathbb{P}\big[{\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)\setminus\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=-t~~~~\wedge\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)\setminus\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=-t}\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)\cap\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=t\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=-d_{2}}^{d_{2}}\mathbb{P}\big[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)\setminus\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=-t\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(v)\setminus\text{supp}(u)}\xi_{i}=-t\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)\cap\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=t\big]

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{t=-d_{2}}^{d_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\text{supp}(u)\setminus\text{supp}(v)|}\cdot\sqrt{|\text{supp}(v)\setminus\text{supp}(u)|}}\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[\sum_{i\in\text{supp}(u)\cap\text{supp}(v)}\xi_{i}=t\big]\quad\preceq\frac{\log d}{d}.

where the second to last step can be shown using (say) Stirling’s approximation
for binomial coefficients. So we have
\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[u,v\in\ker(R)\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[u,v\in\ker(R)]}{{\mathbb{P}}[u,v\in\ker(R)]}{{\mathbb{P}}[u,v\in\ker(R)]}\leq\log^{O(h)}d/d^{h}
.
There are at most
{N\choose d_{2}}2^{O(d)}
choices of pairs
u,v\in H_{\leq d_{2}}^{N}
such that
|\text{supp}(u)\cup\text{supp}(v)|=d_{2}
. Thus we may take union bound
and obtain that w.h.p., any pair of boolean vectors
u,v\in\ker(R)

must satisfy
|\text{supp}(u)\cup\text{supp}(v)|>d_{2}
.

Non-Overlap for Vectors with Real Entries.

We refine the approach for the Boolean slice so that it applies to vectors with real entries.
This time we will begin with the observation that the span of two linearly independent vectors
u,v\in S_{d_{2}}^{N}\cap\ker(R)

cannot contain a compressible vector
w\in\mathrm{Comp}^{d_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}
for
\rho^{2},\delta\preceq 1/\log d
, since that would contradict
Corollary

5.4
.

It then remains to consider the case of linearly independent pairs

u,v\in S_{d_{2}}^{N}\cap\ker(R)
that do not contain a compressible vector in their span—henceforth referred to as incompressible pairs. We will organize such incompressible pairs according to the subspace they span and identify them with an orthonormal basis. For
d_{2}\leq N
we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bases}_{\rho,\delta}^{d_{2}}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}~~\big|~~u_{1},u_{2}\text{ orthonormal},

\displaystyle\qquad\quad|\text{supp}(u_{1})\cup\text{supp}(u_{2})|\leq d_{2},~\mathrm{Span}(\{u_{1},u_{2}\})\cap\mathrm{Comp}^{d_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}=\emptyset\big\}\,.

We use a powerful anticoncentration result of
[
RV09
]
to
deduce that the small ball probability of an incompressible pair behaves
similarly to the independent case:

Corollary 5.10
.

(Joint Small Ball Probability for Basis Elements of an Incompressible Subspace)

Fix any
\rho,\delta\in(0,1)
. For any

sufficiently large, any

,
and any

,

\mathchoice{{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}},\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\delta d}\right]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}},\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\delta d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}},\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\delta d}]}{{\mathbb{P}}[\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{1}\rVert_{\infty}},\mathchoice{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}{\lVert(R_{h,N})u_{2}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\delta d}]}\leq C_{1}^{h}(\rho^{4}\delta d)^{-h}\,.

where
C_{1}>1
is a universal constant.

We defer the proof of this corollary to the next section as it requires some new notions.

Equipped with our joint small ball estimate, we may then take a union bound
over a sufficiently fine net of
\mathrm{Bases}_{\rho,\delta}^{d_{2}}

to establish the bounded overlap property. We have assembled all ingredients required to give the proof:

Theorem 5.11

(Non-Overlap for a Random Rademacher Kernel)
.

There is a universal constant
c_{2}>0
such that the following holds. Fix any
\varepsilon\in(0,1),~\delta\in(0,c_{2}\varepsilon]
,
let
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
be sufficiently large, and let

.
If
N

, then with probability

,

.

Let
d_{2}:=2(1-\varepsilon)d
and let
R
denote

.
By
Corollary

5.4
, there is a universal constant
c_{0}>0

such that
\mathrm{Comp}^{d_{2}}_{\rho_{0},\delta_{0}}\cap\ker(R)=\emptyset

, where
\rho_{0}:=c_{0}/\sqrt{\log d},~\delta_{0}:=c_{0}/\log d
.

For any linearly independent vectors
\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}\in\ker(R)
satisfying
|\textstyle\text{supp}(\widetilde{u}_{1})\cup\text{supp}(\widetilde{u}_{2})|\leq d_{2}

of
\mathrm{span}\{\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}\}

|\textstyle\text{supp}(u_{1})\cup\text{supp}(u_{2})|\leq d_{2}
. Thus the claim follows if we show that with probability
1-o(1)
, for any orthonormal pair
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{N}_{d_{2}}

does not lie inside
\ker(R)
.
Since we showed above that
\ker(R)
avoids compressible vectors with high probability, we may assume that
\mathrm{span}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
is incompressible.
So we need only show that
\mathrm{Bases}^{d_{2}}_{\rho_{0},\delta_{0}}\cap(\ker(R)\times\ker(R))\neq\emptyset

(8)

(substituting

|{\mathcal{O}}|=d_{2}^{O(d_{2})}\cdot N^{d_{2}}

Appendix A
NP-Hardness of Exactly Solving Quadratic Equations

In this section, we prove a strengthened version of
Proposition

2.1
, namely,
Proposition

2.1
with an additional distinguished-coordinate property that is useful for hardness of NCP.

Proposition A.1
.

(NP-Hardness of Quadratic Equations)

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be any field. Given a system of quadratic equations over
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
of the form
\{Q_{\ell}(xx^{T})=b_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[m]}

(resp.
\{Q_{\ell}(xx^{T})=0\}_{\ell\in[m]}
), it is NP-hard to distinguish between the following two cases:

-

(YES) There exists
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with
x_{n}=1
, satisfying all
m
equations.

-

(NO) There does not exist
x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
(resp.
x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
) satisfying all
m
equations.

The distinguished-coordinate property refers to the fact that in the (YES) case, the solution
x
satisfies
x_{n}=1
.

Proof.

We first show hardness of the homogeneous version
\{Q_{\ell}(xx^{T})=0\}_{\ell\in[m]}
.
We reduce from Circuit-SAT problem, where the input is a Boolean circuit which consists of input gates as well as AND, OR, NOT gates with fan-in (at most) two and fan-out unbounded, and the goal is to find a Boolean assignment of its input gates that makes the output gate true. It is a prototypical NP-complete problem, since the Cook-Levin theorem is sometimes proved on Circuit-SAT instead of 3SAT (see e.g., Lemma 6.10 in
[
AB09
]
).

Given a Circuit-SAT instance
C
with
n
gates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
(including input gates and logic gates), we build
n+1
variables
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z\}
, and add the following equations:

-

x_{i}(x_{i}-z)=0,\forall i\in[n]
;

-

for each AND gate
y_{k}=y_{i}\land y_{j}
in
C
, an equation
x_{k}^{2}=x_{i}x_{j}
;

-

for each OR gate
y_{k}=y_{i}\lor y_{j}
in
C
, an equation
z^{2}-x_{k}^{2}=(z-x_{i})(z-x_{j})
;

-

for each NOT gate
y_{k}=\lnot y_{i}
in
C
, an equation
z^{2}-x_{k}^{2}=x_{i}^{2}
;

-

for
y_{k}
being the output gate, an equation
z^{2}=x_{k}^{2}
.

Completeness.

Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\{0,1\}
be an assignment to the gates of
C
that makes the output true. It’s easy to verify

\left\{\begin{aligned} z&=1,\\
x_{i}&=y_{i},&\forall i\in[n]\end{aligned}\right.

is a solution to the system of quadratic equations.

Soundness.

If
z=0
, then by the first set of equations, each
x_{i}
has to be 0 and this is an all-zero solution. Otherwise, every
x_{i}
lies in
\{0,z\}
, and setting each
y_{i}=x_{i}z^{-1}
is a satisfying assignment of
C
since it satisfies every gate in
C
and ensures that the output gate is true.

The distinguished-coordinate property follows by the fact that
z=1
in the (YES) case. We can set
z
to be the last variable in the system.

By simply replacing the variable
z
in the above proof with a constant 1, we obtain hardness of the non-homogeneous version
\{Q_{\ell}(xx^{T})=b_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[m]}
, where in the soundness case there is no solution in all of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
(as opposed to just
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
).

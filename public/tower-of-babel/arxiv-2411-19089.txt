Numerical analysis of a constrained strain energy minimization problem
Tilman Aleman, Arnold Reusken

Abstract
We consider a setting in which an evolving surface is implicitly characterized as the zero level of a level set function. Such an implicit surface does not encode any information about the path of a single point on the evolving surface. In the literature different approaches for determining a velocity that induces corresponding paths of points on the surface have been proposed. One of these is based on minimization of the strain energy functional. This then leads to a constrained minimization problem, which has a corresponding equivalent formulation as a saddle point problem. The main topic of this paper is a detailed analysis of this saddle point problem and of a finite element discretization of this problem. We derive well-posedness results for the continuous and discrete problems and optimal error estimates for a finite element discretization that uses standard H 1 H^{1} -conforming finite element spaces.



1
Introduction

The problem that we consider in this paper is inspired by an application from computer graphics.
In many computer graphics pipelines implicit representations of geometry are used. Typically implicit shapes are represented as level sets of a time dependent level set function. In such a setting the temporal coherence is rather weak, in the sense that such implicit surfaces do not encode any information about the path of a single point on the evolving surface. Therefore, the problem of determining a “reasonable” velocity field corresponding to an implicity evolving surface has been addressed in the computer graphics community
[
11
,
2
,
12
]
.

One natural approach, based on computing a “near-isometric” velocity field (also called approximate Killing vector field) is introduced in
[
12
]
. The techique is also used in
[
10
,
1
]
. Similar near-isometric velocity fields are used in a physics informed neural network method for diffeomorphic shape or image registration
[
5
]
and in an optimization technique for mesh parametrization
[
3
]
. We outline the main idea of this approach, cf. Section
2
for more details. Assume that the surface one is interested in is implicitly represented as the zero level of a
known
level set function
\phi(\mathbf{x},t)
,
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
,
t\in[0,t_{e}]
. Here
\Omega
is a fixed domain that contains an evolving zero level of
\phi(\cdot,t)
for
t\in[0,t_{e}]
.
The level sets of
\phi
with value
c
are denoted by
\Gamma_{c}(t)
and the zero level is denoted by
\Gamma(t)=\Gamma_{0}(t)
.
The evolution of the level set
\Gamma_{c}(t)
is completely determined by its velocity in
normal
direction, denoted by
\mathbf{u}_{N}=u_{N}\mathbf{n}
, with
\mathbf{n}
the unit normal vector on
\Gamma_{c}(t)
. An arbitrary (smooth) velocity field
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)
on
\Omega
can be uniquely decomposed based on the normal and tangential components on each of the level sets
\Gamma_{c}(t)
. This decomposition is denoted by
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_{N}+\mathbf{v}_{T}
. Now let
\mathbf{v}
be such that its normal component coincides with
\mathbf{u}_{N}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{N}+\mathbf{v}_{T}
. Such a velocity field is consistent with
\phi
in the sense that it induces trajectories of points that stay on
\Gamma(t)
. More precisely, for
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}\in\Gamma(0)
let
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)
be such that
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(0)=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}
,
\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)=\mathbf{v}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t),t)
,
t\in[0,t_{e}]
, then
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)\in\Gamma(t)
holds. Of course the trajectories
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)
depend on the choice of the tangential component
\mathbf{v}_{T}
. In
[
12
]
it is proposed to determine this tangential component based on minimization of a strain energy functional as follows:

\mathbf{v}_{T}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbf{w}_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{u}_{N}+\mathbf{w}_{T}):\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{u}_{N}+\mathbf{w}_{T})\,d\mathbf{x},\quad\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{v}):=\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{T},

(1)

where the vector fields
\mathbf{w}_{T}=\mathbf{w}_{T}(\cdot,t)
must be tangential to the level sets
\Gamma_{c}(t)
. Since a vector field
\mathbf{v}
that satisfies
\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{T}=0
(on
\Omega
) is called a Killing vector field, a velocity field
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{N}+\mathbf{v}_{T}
, with
\mathbf{v}_{T}
a solution of (
1
), is called an approximate Killing vector field, cf.
[
12
]
.
Hence, for each fixed
t\in[0,t_{e}]
we obtain a constrained minimization problem. It is natural to introduce a Lagrange multiplier and reformulate the problem (
1
) as an unconstrained saddle point problem. The main topic of this paper is a detailed analysis of this saddle point problem and of a finite element discretization of this problem. The saddle point reformulation is also used in
[
12
]
, but as far as we know there is no literature in which well-posedness of this problem or error analysis of a discretization method is studied.

We present a well-posed saddle point formulation of the problem (
1
) in natural Sobolev spaces. We introduce a finite element discretization of the saddle point problem using an
H^{1}
-conforming pair of finite element spaces with polynomials of degree
k
both for the (tangential) velocity and the Lagrange multiplier. We prove discrete LBB stability of this pair and derive optimal discretization error bounds. We also include results of numerical experiments that confirm the error analysis and we present results that show how such a velocity field is used to determine the trajectories of points on a evolving
\Gamma(t)
. A technical issue that we have to deal with is that of a possible nonuniqueness of a solution of (
1
), which is related to rigid motions.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give a more detailed derivation of the constrained minimization problem (
1
). This problem is formulated in a slightly more general form in Section
3
. In that section we also study the kernel space that has to be factored out to obtain a uniquely solvable problem. In Section
4
we introduce a saddle point formulation and prove well-posedness of this formulation. A finite element discretization of this saddle point problem is explained in Section
5
and an error analysis of this method is presented in Section
5.1
. Finally, in Section
6
we present results of some numerical experiments.

2
Motivation: An inverse level set problem

The problem that we study is motivated by an application of level set techniques in computer graphics
[
12
]
. We consider a situation in which a smooth evolving hypersurface
\Gamma(t)
(curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
) is implicitly represented by a level set function
\phi(\mathbf{x},t)
, i.e.,
\phi(\mathbf{x},t)=0
iff
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma(t)
. As is common in level set techniques, we use a fixed computational open domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, time interval
[0,t_{e}]
, and consider the evolution of
\Gamma(t)\cap\Omega
for
t\in[0,t_{e}]
based on the transport of a level set function
\phi:\,\Omega_{T}:=\overline{\Omega}\times[0,t_{e}]\to\mathbb{R}
.
We assume

\phi\in C^{1}(\Omega_{T}),\quad\min_{(\mathbf{x},t)\in\Omega_{T}}\|\nabla\phi(\mathbf{x},t)\|\geq c_{\min}>0.

(2)

Here
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a fixed
t\in[0,t_{e}]
, define
\phi_{\min}=\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega}}\phi(\mathbf{x},t)
,
\phi_{\max}=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega}}\phi(\mathbf{x},t)
and the level sets
\Gamma_{c}(t):=\{\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega~|~\phi(\mathbf{x},t)=c\,\}
,
\phi_{\min}\leq c\leq\phi_{\max}
. To avoid some technical details, we assume
{\rm meas}_{d-1}\Gamma_{c}(t)>0
. Note that
\Omega=\cup_{\phi_{\min}\leq c\leq\phi_{\max}}\Gamma_{c}(t)
holds. From the implicit function theorem it follows that the level sets
\Gamma_{c}(t)
have
C^{1}
smoothness. For the unit normal on
\Gamma_{c}(t)
(pointing in the direction of increasing
\phi
) we have

\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x},t)=\frac{\nabla\phi(\mathbf{x},t)}{\|\nabla\phi(\mathbf{x},t)\|},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{c}(t).

Note that this
normal vector field

\mathbf{n}
is defined on
\Omega_{T}
. The transport of the level set function can be described using the level set equation

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi=0\quad\text{on}~\Omega_{T},

(3)

where
\mathbf{u}
has to satisfy

\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\|\nabla\phi\|}\quad\text{on}~\Omega_{T}.

(4)

Trajectories induced by such a velocity field
\mathbf{u}
have the property that transport conserves level set values. More precisely,
for arbitrary
\underline{t}\in[0,t_{e})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}\in\Gamma_{c}(\underline{t})
, consider the trajectory given by
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(\underline{t})=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}
,
\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)=\mathbf{u}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t),t)
,
t\in[\underline{t},t_{e})
. Then it holds that
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t)\in\Gamma_{c}(t)
for
t\in[\underline{t},\underline{t}+\delta]
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. This in particular holds for points
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}
on the zero level
\Gamma_{0}(t)
. Clearly, the trajectory depends on the specific choice of
\mathbf{u}
, for which there is freedom, since the condition (
4
) is satisfied for any
\mathbf{u}
such that

\mathbf{u}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\|\nabla\phi\|}\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{v},\quad\quad\text{with}~\mathbf{v}~\text{such that}~\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0

(5)

holds. This can be considered as a sort of inverse level set problem, where for a given level set function
\phi
on
\Omega_{T}
we want to determine a “suitable” velocity
\mathbf{u}
that satisfies (
5
). This velocity field then induces corresponding trajectories of points.

Remark 2.1
.

Consider a simple example for
d=2
:

\phi(\mathbf{x},t)=\phi(x,y,t)=(x-t)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-1,\quad t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{4}],~\Omega=(-2,2)^{2}\setminus[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{2}.

(6)

For the minimum and maximum values of
\phi(\cdot,t)
we have
\phi_{\min}=(\tfrac{1}{2}-t)^{2}-\tfrac{7}{8}
and
\phi_{\max}=(2+t)^{2}+1
, respectively.
The level set
\Gamma_{c}(t)
is an ellipse with centre
(t,0)
and axes
\sqrt{1+c}
,
\sqrt{2(1+c)}
, intersected with
\Omega
. Note that for
(\mathbf{x},t)\in\Omega_{T}
we have
\|\nabla\phi(\mathbf{x},t)\|=\sqrt{4(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}\geq 2|x-t|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
and the normal vector field is given by

\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}2(x-t)\\
y\end{pmatrix}.

This level set function describes the transport of an ellipse with velocity field
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}=(1~0)^{T}
, i.e., a rigid motion. This level set function solves (
3
) with
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}^{\ast}
. It is, however, not obvious how this particular choice of
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}
can be determined if only the level set function information is available.

In the next section we describe, in a slightly more general setting, a natural criterion that uniquely determines the remaining tangential velocity part
\mathbf{v}
in (
5
) . This approach is introduced in
[
12
]
.

3
A constrained strain energy minimization problem

In this section we formulate a class of minimization problems. A minimization problem of this form is used to determine, for a fixed
t\in[0,t_{e}]
, the tangential velocity part
\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)
in (
5
).
The usual Sobolev spaces of once weakly differentiable scalar or vector valued functions on
\Omega
are denoted by
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega):=H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
. For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
we define
\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{v}):=\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{T}
and the (strain) energy functional

a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{v}):\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}.

(7)

The kernel of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is given by
K={\rm Ker}(\mathbf{E})=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)~|~a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=0\,\}
and consists of the rigid motions. Its dimension is 3 or 6 for
d=2
and
d=3
, respectively.

Remark 3.1
.

An
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of
K
for
d=2
is given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{v}_{1}&=|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1,0)^{T},\quad\mathbf{v}_{2}=|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)^{T},\quad\mathbf{v}_{3}=d_{3}\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}-y\\
d_{2}+x\end{pmatrix},\\
d_{1}&=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}y\,d\mathbf{x},\quad d_{2}=-|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}x\,d\mathbf{x},\\
d_{3}&=\big(\int_{\Omega}(d_{1}-y)^{2}+(d_{2}+x)^{2}\,d\mathbf{x}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(8)

Let
\hat{\mathbf{z}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
given
continuous velocity field with
\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|=1
on
\Omega
. An arbitrary continuous vector field
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be uniquely decomposed as
\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}+\mathbf{v}
, with
\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0
, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})=0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
. We consider a setting in which for a given
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
the component
\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}
of
\mathbf{u}
in direction
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
is given and the only degree of freedom left is the component of
\mathbf{u}
orthogonal to
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
. We will use a minimization problem based on the energy functional (
7
) and therefore we require
(\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Sufficient for this to hold is
\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in H^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. Motivated by this we introduce the following assumption

Assumption 3.1
.

We assume a given vector field
\mathbf{z}:=z\hat{\mathbf{z}}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

z\in H^{1,\infty}(\Omega),\quad\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)~\text{and}~\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|=1~\text{a.e on}~\Omega.

(9)

In the remainder pointwise conditions such as “
\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0
” are always meant in the usual a.e. sense. In a velocity field of the form
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}
, for determining the component
\mathbf{v}
we use the criterion that the corresponding
\mathbf{u}
should be “as close as possible to a rigid motion”. More precisely, we consider minimization of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
in the space of velocities
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}
, with
\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0
on
\Omega
.
Define the space

V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}:=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)~|~\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0\,\}.

Hence, we want to minimize
a(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v})
for
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}
. Note, however, that
a(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v})=a(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w})
for all
\mathbf{w}\in K
. Clearly, for uniqueness of a minimizer we have to factor out the kernel
K
. We introduce:

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}&:=K\cap V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in K~|~\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0\quad\text{a.e. on}~\Omega\,\},\\
V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}&:=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}~|~(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})_{1}=0\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\,\},\end{split}

(10)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{1}
is the usual scalar product on
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)
.
This then leads to the following
constrained strain energy minimization problem
that we study in this paper:

\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{z}+V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=\min_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}a(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}).

(11)

Due to symmetry this minimization problem is equivalent to
\min_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})+2a(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})
, and since
a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})>0
for all
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
,
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
, we obtain that
\mathbf{v}^{\ast}
solves (
11
) iff
\mathbf{v}^{\ast}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
satisfies

a(\mathbf{v}^{\ast},\mathbf{v})=-a(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}.

(12)

Note that “for all
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
” can be replaced by “for all
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}
”.

Lemma 1
.

The minimization problem (
11
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

We use standard arguments, cf.
[
7
]
. The bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Using the Korn inequality, injectivity of
\mathbf{v}\to\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{v})
on
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus K
and the Peetre-Tartar lemma (cf.
[
6
]
) it follows that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is elliptic on
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus K
.
The continuity and ellipticity properties also hold on the closed subspace
V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
.
∎

Note that the inverse level set problem explained in Section
2
fits in the setting introduced above. We then have, for a fixed
t
, the given velocity field
\mathbf{z}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\|\nabla\phi\|}\mathbf{n}=z\hat{\mathbf{z}}
, with
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{n}
, and as criterion for chosing the vector field
\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)
in (
5
) we use the minimization problem (
11
).

3.1
The kernel space

We study the kernel space
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
, cf (
10
).
We will see that “in the generic case”, as explained below, this kernel is trivial, i.e, contains only the zero function. We first consider the case of the inverse level set problem, where
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{n}
holds, and then the general case.

3.1.1
The inverse level set problem

Consider the problem described in Section
2
. There we have, for a fixed
t
,
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{n}
and at
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
the vector
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})
(we deleted the argument
t
) is the unit normal on the level set
\Gamma_{c}
that contains
\mathbf{x}
.
We have

\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
iff
\mathbf{v}\in K
and
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})=0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{c}
and all
c\in[\phi_{\min},\phi_{\max}]
. This means that
\mathbf{v}
is in the tangent space of the level set
\Gamma_{c}
for all
c\in[\phi_{\min},\phi_{\max}]
.
Furthermore,
\mathbf{v}\in K
iff
\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{T}=0
, which implies
\nabla_{\Gamma_{c}}\mathbf{v}+\nabla_{\Gamma_{c}}\mathbf{v}^{T}=0
, where
\nabla_{\Gamma_{c}}
is the covariant derivative (for the hypersurface
\Gamma_{c}
) of the tangential field
\mathbf{v}
. It follows that
\mathbf{v}
is a Killing vector field for the hypersurface
\Gamma_{c}
, which means that the (local) flow induced by
\mathbf{v}
is an isometry on
\Gamma_{c}
. Nonzero flows that are isometries are only possible for very special level set shapes, namely
if
\Gamma_{c}
a straight line segment or circular (for
d=2
) or
\Gamma_{c}
is planar or rotationally symmetric around a line (for
d=3
). We conclude that if for a
c\in[\phi_{\min},\phi_{\max}]
the level set
\Gamma_{c}
does not have one of these special shapes, the kernel space
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
is trivial.

3.1.2
General case

We now consider general vector fields
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
for which Assumption
3.1
holds. We consider the cases
d=2
and
d=3
seperately.

Lemma 2
.

Consider
d=2
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\leq 1
holds. Define, with
\mathbf{x}=(x,y)
,
R_{\mathbf{x}}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-y\\
0&1&x\end{pmatrix}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
. In the (generic) case in which

{\rm span}\{\,\cup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}R_{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})\}=\mathbb{R}^{3}

(13)

is satisfied, we have
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=0
. Let
\mathbf{w}\in K
,
\mathbf{w}\neq 0
, be a rigid motion. If
\mathbf{w}
is a rotation we assume that the center of rotation does not lie in
\overline{\Omega}
. Define
\hat{\mathbf{z}}:=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{\perp}}{\|\mathbf{w}_{\perp}\|}\in\mathbf{H}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, with
\mathbf{w}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}(\mathbf{w})
holds.

Proof.

is used to guarantee smoothness of

.

Lemma 3
.

Consider
d=3
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\leq 3
holds. Define, with
\mathbf{x}=(x,y,z)

R_{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&-y&-z&0\\
0&1&0&x&0&-z\\

(14)

In the (generic) case in which

{\rm span}\{\,\cup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}R_{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})\}=\mathbb{R}^{6}

(15)

is satisfied, we have
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=0
. Take a fixed
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\|\mathbf{p}\|=1
. Let

be the three dimensional space of all rigid motions in the plane orthogonal to

, denoted by

. Let

. A rigid motion
\mathbf{w}\in K_{\mathbf{p}}
, defined on

, is extended to

by constant extension in normal direction, i.e.,
\mathbf{w}^{e}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}(P_{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}\mathbf{x})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=3
holds for

\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{p},\quad\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\left\{\mathbf{w}^{e}~|~\mathbf{w}\in K_{\mathbf{p}}\,\right\}.

(16)

Proof.

The results above explain in which sense the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
is trivial “in the generic case”.

Remark 3.2
.

If the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
is trivial then indeed, as claimed in
[
12
]
, the minimization problem recovers rigid motions. Let
\mathbf{u}
be a rigid motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}
with a given
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
that satisfies Assumption
3.1
and for which
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
holds. Then
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{z}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}
solves the minimization problem (
11
) because
a(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v})=a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=0
holds, and thus we recover
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{v}
. If
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\geq 1
, then in certain special cases a rigid motion is not recovered. As an example, consider
d=2
, and denote the rigid motions in coordinate directions by
\mathbf{e}_{1}(\mathbf{x})=(1~0)^{T}
,
\mathbf{e}_{2}(\mathbf{x})=(0~1)^{T}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Take
\mathbf{u}=c_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+c_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}
with constants
c_{1},c_{2}\neq 0
. Take
\hat{\mathbf{z}}:=\mathbf{e}_{1}
and thus
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}(\mathbf{e}_{2})
. Consider the minimization problem (
11
) with
\mathbf{z}=c_{1}\hat{\mathbf{z}}
. The rigid motion
\mathbf{u}
is recovered only for
\mathbf{v}=c_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}
, which, however, is not feasible since
\mathbf{e}_{2}\notin V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus K=V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
.

Examples

Remark 3.3
.

The Gauge condition (

\mathbf{v}
as in (

\mathbf{v}\perp_{H^{1}}\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\perp})
holds. In the example of Lemma

the given velocity field
\mathbf{z}=z\hat{\mathbf{z}}
has a spatially constant direction
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=(c~s)^{T}
and has a varying length
z(x,y)
. One aims to correct with a
\mathbf{v}
that is pointwise orthogonal to
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
, i.e.
\mathbf{v}
has the constant direction
\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\perp}
, and has varying length. The optimal (w.r.t. strain energy minimization) correction is given by
\mathbf{v}
in (
19
). As a special case one can consider a domain
\Omega

(x,y)\in\Omega

(-x,y)\in\Omega

,

and the function

\mathbf{z}(x,y)=(y~0)^{T}
with corresponding

that is a rigid motion, hence has a strain energy

.

4
Reformulation as saddle point problem

To obtain a feasible solution method we introduce a Lagrange multiplier to eliminate the constraint
\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0
.
We define
b:\,H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,
b(\lambda,\mathbf{v}):=(\lambda,\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})_{1}
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{1}
is the standard scalar product on
H^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
(v,w)_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+vw\,d\mathbf{x}
. For the kernel of
b(\cdot,\cdot)
we have

\ker(b)=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)~|~b(\mu,\mathbf{v})=0\quad\forall~\mu\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,\}=V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}.

We introduce

\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega):=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)~|~(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})_{1}=0~\text{for all}~\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\,\}.

(20)

Note that in the generic case we have
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and then
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
holds.
There exists a constant
C_{d}
, depending only on the dimension
d
, such that the following holds:

\|\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\leq C_{d}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{1,\infty}}\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega),~\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

(21)

This constant
C_{d}
will be used in continuity and inf-sup estimates below.
Below, the orthogonal projection in
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
w.r.t.
(\cdot,\cdot)_{1}
on a subspace
W
is denoted by
Q_{W}
.

Lemma 5
.

We consider the saddle point problem: determine
(\mathbf{w},\lambda)\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{split}a(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})+b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})&=-a(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})\quad\text{for all}~~\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)\\
b(\mu,\mathbf{w})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mu\in H^{1}(\Omega).\end{split}

(22)

This problem is well-posed.

Proof.

Note that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
and elliptic on
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\ker(b)=\ker(b)\setminus(\ker(b)\cap K)
. Using Assumption
3.1
we get

|b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})|=|(\lambda,\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})_{1}|\leq c_{\rm cont}\|\lambda\|_{1}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1},\quad c_{\rm cont}:=C_{d}\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|_{H^{1,\infty}}.

(23)

For the right-hand side in (
22
) we have that
\mathbf{v}\to a(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})
is continuous on
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.
It remains to check the inf-sup property for
b(\cdot,\cdot)
. Take
\lambda\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and define
\mathbf{v}:=\hat{\mathbf{z}}\lambda-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\lambda)\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
. For this choice we have
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\leq\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\lambda\|_{1}\leq C_{d}\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|_{H^{1,\infty}}\|\lambda\|_{1}
. Note that
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\subset\ker(b)
and thus
b(\lambda,Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{w})=0
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Using this we get

\frac{b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}}=\frac{(\lambda,\lambda)_{1}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}}\geq c_{\rm infsup}\|\lambda\|_{1},\quad c_{\rm infsup}:=c_{\rm cont}^{-1}.

(24)

and it follows that
b(\cdot,\cdot)
has the inf-sup property on
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence, the problem (
22
) has a unique solution.
∎

A saddle point reformulation of the problem (
11
) is as follows: determine
(\mathbf{u},\lambda)\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
with
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{u}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{z}
such that

\begin{split}a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\\
b(\mu,\mathbf{u})&=b(\mu,\mathbf{z})=(\mu,z)_{1}\quad\text{for all}~~\mu\in H^{1}(\Omega).\end{split}

(25)

For testing we use
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
instead of
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
. This is allowed because for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\ker(b)\cap K
we have
a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=0
and
b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})=0
.
From Lemma
5
and a shift argument
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}
it follows that (
25
) is well-posed.
Let
(\mathbf{u},\lambda)
be the solution of (
25
). Testing with
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}=\ker(b)
we obtain
a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=0
for all
\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}
. From this it follows that
\mathbf{u}
is the unique solution of (
11
). We conclude that (
25
) is a
well-posed saddle point reformulation
of the constrained minimization problem (
11
). This saddle point formulation will be the basis for the finite element discretization that we use to solve the minimization problem approximately, cf. Section
5
below. We note that the equivalence between the formulations (
22
) and (
25
) via the shift
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}
, in general does
not
hold for the corresponding discrete versions of these problems. The reason for this is that a shift property
\mathbf{u}_{h}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}_{h}
with finite element functions
\mathbf{u}_{h}
,
\mathbf{w}_{h}
, implies that
\mathbf{z}
is a finite element function, which in general is not the case.

Remark 4.1
.

We comment on the choice of the space
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
in (
20
).
As shown above, with this choice the Lagrange multiplier formulation in (
25
) is equivalent to (
11
) in the sense that the
\mathbf{u}
-solutions of both problems are the same. Alternatively one might consider a Lagrange multiplier formulation with the (simpler) choice
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega):=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus K\subset\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
. Note that the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is elliptic on this space but not on
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
. With the choice
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
, however, in general the Lagrange multiplier formulation and (
11
) do not have the same solution. As an example, consider the problem presented in Remark
2.1
, consisting of an ellipse that is transported by the rigid motion
\mathbf{u}=(1~0)^{T}
. In Section
3.2
it is shown that we obtain this
\mathbf{u}
as minimizer of the constrained minimization problem (
11
) and thus also as solution of the equivalent saddle point problem (
25
). If, however, we use
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
then, due to
\mathbf{u}\notin\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
, we will not obtain this
\mathbf{u}
as solution of the saddle point problem with this alternative space.

Remark 4.2
.

We reconsider the special class of problems presented in Lemma
4
. We briefly discuss the corresponding saddle point formulation and in particular investigate the corresponding Lagrange multiplier solution
\lambda
. In this example we have
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\perp})
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{\perp}
is a constant vector we have

Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{u}=\frac{(\mathbf{u},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{1}}{({\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{1}}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}=\frac{(\mathbf{u},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{L^{2}}}{({\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{L^{2}}}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}=\frac{(\mathbf{u},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{L^{2}}}{|\Omega|}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}.

Let
\mathbf{z}=z_{1}\hat{\mathbf{z}}
and
\mathbf{v}=v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}
be as in (
18
) and (
19
), respectively. Note that

b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})=(\lambda,\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})_{1}
. We test in (
25
) with
\mathbf{v}=w_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
,
w_{1}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. This yields

\begin{split}b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})&=(\lambda,w_{1})_{1}=-a(\mathbf{u},w_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=-a(z_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}+v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp},w_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\\
&=-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla z_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{1}+(\nabla z_{1}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})+(\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})\,d\mathbf{x}\end{split}

(26)

for all
w_{1}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. We introduce notation for the smooth vectors
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}:=2\nabla z_{1}
,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{v}}:=2\nabla v_{2}
. Hence, we conclude that the Lagrange multiplier is the unique solution of the elliptic problem:

(\lambda,w_{1})_{1}=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}\cdot\nabla w_{1}+(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})+(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{v}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})\,d\mathbf{x}~\forall~w_{1}\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(27)

In strong formulation this problem takes the form of a Neumann problem:

\begin{split}-\Delta\lambda+\lambda&=\mathop{\rm div}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}+{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})+{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{v}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\quad\text{in}~\Omega\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\lambda&=-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}-({\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{z}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})-({\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{v}}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{split}

(28)

where
\mathbf{n}
denotes the unit normal on
\partial\Omega
. The source terms on the right hand sides in (
28
) are smooth if the function
F
in Lemma
4
is smooth. Nevertheless the solution
\lambda
may have large gradients due to boundary effects, e.g., if the boundary has a reentrant corner. We will illustrate this in a numerical experiment in Section
6.2.2
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\lambda)
be the solution of problem (
25
). The following estimates hold with
c_{\rm cont}
defined in (
23
):

\displaystyle\frac{a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\rm cont}\|\lambda\|_{1}

(29)

\displaystyle\|\lambda\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{\rm cont}a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(30)

Proof.

The estimate in (
29
) follows from

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=-b(\lambda,\mathbf{u})\leq c_{\rm cont}\|\lambda\|_{1}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1},

cf. (
23
). The result in (
30
) follows from
a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})\leq 4\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{v}:\nabla\mathbf{v}\,d\mathbf{x}\leq 4\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}^{2}
and (
24
):

\begin{split}\|\lambda\|_{1}&\leq c_{\rm cont}\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{b(\lambda,\mathbf{v})}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}}=c_{\rm cont}\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}}\\
&\leq c_{\rm cont}\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})^{\frac{1}{2}}a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}}\leq 2c_{\rm cont}a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

∎

From this result we see that
\|\lambda\|_{1}^{2}
is a measure for
a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})
, i.e., the value of the strain energy functional at the minimizer
\mathbf{u}
.

5
Discretization of the saddle point problem

We assume that
\Omega
is polygonal. Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of shape regular (not necessarily quasi-uniform) simplicial triangulations of
\Omega
. The space of continuous piecewise polynomial functions of degree
k\geq 1
on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is denoted by
V_{h}
and
\mathbf{V}_{h}:=V_{h}^{d}
is the corresponding space of vector values finite element functions.
In the discretization we use the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to the finite element spaces with kernel denoted by

\ker_{h}(b):=\{\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}~|~b(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=0\quad\text{for all}~\lambda_{h}\in V_{h}\,\}.

Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\ker(b)\cap K
. A discrete analogue of this space is

\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}:=\ker_{h}(b)\cap K.

We define

\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}:=\mathbf{V}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}=\{\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}~|~Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{v}_{h}=0\,\}
. The pair
\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\times V_{h}
is suitable for a Galerkin discretization of (
22
), since
V_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and, due to
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
, also
\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\subset\mathbf{H}_{\ast}^{1}(\Omega)
. This pair is used in the analysis in Section
5.1
.
Note that
\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\cap\ker_{h}(b)=\ker_{h}(b)\setminus(\ker_{h}(b)\cap K)
holds.

We introduce a discretization of problem (
25
): determine
(\mathbf{u}_{h},\lambda_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times V_{h}
with
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{u}_{h}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z}
such that

\begin{split}a(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\\
b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})&=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{z})=(\mu_{h},z)_{1}\quad\text{for all}~~\mu_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{split}

(31)

Well-posedness of this problem is analyzed below. The analysis uses standard techniques for finite element error analysis of saddle point problems, but we need some additional technical results to also cover the case
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\geq 1
. If we would restrict to the case
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
the results on well-posedness of the discrete problem presented below and the error bound in Theorem
13
can be obtained with a simpler analysis.

5.1
Discretization error analysis

In this section we assume additional regularity of
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
, namely
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{H}^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)
. In the error analysis below we use
c
to denote a generic constant that is independent of the mesh size parameter
h
and whose value can change from one instance to another.
We start with an elementary lemma. We use the orthogonal projections
Q_{V_{h}}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
,
Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}:\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{V}_{h}
, with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{1}
. Note that
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)
iff
Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})=0
holds.

Lemma 7
.

The following estimates hold with constants
c
independent of
h
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq ch\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}\|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\|_{H^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(32)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{h}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq ch\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}\|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\|_{H^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}~\lambda_{h}\in V_{h},

(33)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\|_{H^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}\in K.

(34)

Proof.

Take
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. Let
I_{V_{h}}
be the nodal interpolation operator in
V_{h}
. On an element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
we get, using a finite element inverse inequality and
|\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{k+1,T}=0
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-I_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{H^{1}(T)}\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1,T}\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}|\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{\ell,T}|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1-\ell,\infty,T}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}|\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{\ell,T}|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1-\ell,\infty,T}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,h_{T}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(T)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1-\ell,\infty,T}\leq c\,h_{T}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(T)}\|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\|_{H^{k+1,\infty}(T)}.

Squaring this, summing over
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and using
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{1}\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-I_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{1}
we obtain the result (
32
). With the same arguments we obtain the result in (
33
).

Note that
\mathbf{v}\in K
is a constant or linear vector function and thus
|\mathbf{v}|_{\ell,T}=0
for
\ell\geq 2
. Using this we
get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-I_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\|_{H^{1}(T)}\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}|\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1,T}\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}|\mathbf{v}|_{\ell,T}|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1-\ell,\infty,T}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}|\mathbf{v}|_{\ell,T}|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}|_{k+1-\ell,\infty,T}\leq c\,h_{T}^{k}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{1}(T)}\|{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\|_{H^{k+1,\infty}(T)}.

With the same arguments as above we obtain the estimate (
34
).
∎

We now quantify the difference between the space
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
. Both
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
are linear subspaces of
K
and
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
holds (this follows using
K\subset\mathbf{V}_{h}
). For distance between subspaces we use the usual angle notion and define
{\rm dist}(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h},\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}):=\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}=1}\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}
. Note that
{\rm dim}(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h})>{\rm dim}(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})
implies
{\rm dist}(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h},\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=1
.

Lemma 8
.

The following holds with a constant
c
independent of
h
:

{\rm dist}(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h},\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\leq ch^{k}.

(35)

This implies that for
h
sufficiently small
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
holds.

Proof.

We use the orthogonal decomposition
K=\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{\perp}
with
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{\perp}:=\{\,\mathbf{v}\in K~|~(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})_{1}=0\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\,\}
. Define the bounded linear operator
B:\,K\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
by
(B\mathbf{w})(\mu):=b(\mu,\mathbf{w})=(\mu,\mathbf{w}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})_{1}
. Hence,
\ker(B)=\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
and thus
B
is injective on the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{\perp}
. This implies that there exists a constant
c_{0}
such that
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\leq c_{0}\|B\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{-1}}
holds for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{\perp}
. Take
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}\subset K
with
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}=1
and decompose as
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}_{1}+\mathbf{w}_{2}
,
\mathbf{w}_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
,
\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{\perp}
.
Recall that
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
implies that
Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=0
holds. Using this and (
34
) we obtain

\displaystyle(B\mathbf{w})(\mu)

\displaystyle=(\mu,\mathbf{w}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})_{1}=\big(\mu,\mathbf{w}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}-Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{w}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\big)_{1}\leq ch^{k}\|\mu\|_{1}.

Thus we get the estimate
\|B\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{-1}}\leq ch^{k}
. This yields

\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}=\|\mathbf{w}_{2}\|_{1}\leq c_{0}\|B\mathbf{w}_{2}\|_{H^{-1}}=c_{0}\|B\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{-1}}\leq ch^{k},

which completes the proof.
∎

In the analysis below we need that the spaces
\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
and its discrete analogue
\ker_{h}(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
are
“close” (for
h
sufficiently small). The following lemma provides a useful result.

Lemma 9
.

For arbitrary
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
there exists
\mathbf{v}\in\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
such that

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\leq ch\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}

holds with a constant
c
independent of
h
,
\mathbf{v}_{h}
.

Proof.

Take
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
, i.e.
Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})=0
and
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{v}_{h}=0
. Due to
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
we also have
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{v}_{h}=0
. Define
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=\mathbf{v}_{h}-(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\ker(b)
and
\mathbf{v}=\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
. We then obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|(I-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}})(\mathbf{v}_{h}-\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{1}\leq\|(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|_{1}\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|_{H^{1,\infty}}\leq c\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}-Q_{V_{h}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}})\|_{1}\leq ch\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1},

where in the last inequality we used (
32
).
∎

We now derive discrete ellipticity and inf-sup properties.

Lemma 10
.

There exists
\gamma>0
independent of
h
such that

a(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq\gamma\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}^{2}\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\cap\ker_{h}(b).

(36)

Proof.

Note that
\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\cap\ker_{h}(b)=\ker_{h}(b)\setminus(\ker_{h}(b)\cap K)=\ker_{h}(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
and
K
is the kernel of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
. Hence, there exists a possibly
h
-dependent
\gamma_{h}>0
such that (
36
) holds with
\gamma
replaced by
\gamma_{h}
. It remains to show that
\gamma_{h}
is uniformly in
h
bounded away from
0
.
We use that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is elliptic on
\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
, i.e. for suitable
\hat{\gamma}>0
we have
a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})\geq\hat{\gamma}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}^{2}
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
. The seminorm corresponding to
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{a}
.
Take
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
and a corresponding
\mathbf{v}\in\ker(b)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
as in Lemma
9
. Using
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{a}\leq 2\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}
we obtain, with suitable constants
c>0
,

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{a}\geq\|\mathbf{v}\|_{a}-\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{a}\geq\hat{\gamma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}-2\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\hat{\gamma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}-c\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}\|_{1}\geq\hat{\gamma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}(1-ch),

and combining this with
a(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq\gamma_{h}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}^{2}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 11
.

There is a constant
\beta>0
, independent of
h
such that for
h
sufficiently small the following holds:

\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}}\frac{b(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}{\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}}\geq\beta\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}\quad\text{for all}~\lambda_{h}\in V_{h}.

(37)

Proof.

Take
\lambda_{h}\in V_{h}
. Define
\mathbf{v}_{h}:=Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}})-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}
. Note that
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}\leq c\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}
holds, cf. (
21
). Note that
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}\subset\ker_{h}(b)
and thus
b(\lambda_{h},Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{w}_{h})=0
holds for all
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. Using this and (
33
) we obtain, with suitable constants
c>0
,

\displaystyle b(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=b(\lambda_{h},Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}}))

\displaystyle=b(\lambda_{h},\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}})-b(\lambda_{h},\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}}-Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}}))

\displaystyle\geq\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}^{2}-c\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}\|\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}}-Q_{\mathbf{V}_{h}}(\lambda_{h}\hat{\mathbf{z}})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-ch)\|\lambda_{h}\|_{1}^{2}.

Combining these results completes the proof.
∎

Assumption 5.1
.

In the remainder we assume that
h
is sufficiently small, such that the discrete inf-sup property (
37
) holds.

Using this we derive a well-posedness result for the discrete problem (
31
).

Lemma 12
.

The problem (
31
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

First consider the saddle point problem: determine
(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\lambda_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\times V_{h}
such that

\begin{split}a(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\\
b(\mu_{h},\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h})&=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{z})\quad\text{for all}~~\mu_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{split}

(38)

Using the discrete ellipticity and inf-sup properties, an application of standard saddle point theory
[
7
,
6
]
yields that this problem has a unique solution
(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\lambda_{h})
. Now define
\mathbf{u}_{h}:=\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}+Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z}
. From
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}=0
we obtain
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{u}_{h}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z}
.
Note that
a(Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}_{h})=0
for all
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
and
b(\mu_{h},Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z})=0
for all
\mu_{h}\in V_{h}
. Furthermore, in the first equation in (
31
) “for all
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
” can be replaced by “for all
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}
”, since for all
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}
we have
a(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=0
for all
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
,
\mu_{h}\in V_{h}
. Using this one easily checks that
(\mathbf{u}_{h},\lambda_{h})
is a solution of (
31
).

We investigate uniqueness. Let
(\mathbf{u}_{h,1},\lambda_{h,1})
and
(\mathbf{u}_{h,2},\lambda_{h,2})
be solutions of (
31
).
For the differences
\delta\mathbf{u}_{h}:=\mathbf{u}_{h,1}-\mathbf{u}_{h,2}
,
\delta\lambda_{h}=\lambda_{h,1}-\lambda_{h,2}
we have that
(\delta\mathbf{u}_{h},\delta\lambda_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\times V_{h}
satisfies

\begin{split}a(\delta\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b(\delta\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}\\
b(\mu_{h},\delta\mathbf{u}_{h})&=0\quad\text{for all}~~\mu_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{split}

From the discrete ellipticity and inf-sup properties it follows that
\delta\mathbf{u}_{h}=0
and
\delta\lambda_{h}=0
.
∎

Theorem 13
.

Let
h
be sufficiently small such that
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}=\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
holds.
Let
(\mathbf{u},\lambda)
and
(\mathbf{u}_{h},\lambda_{h})
be the unique solutions of (
25
) and (
31
), respectively. The following error bound holds:

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\min_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}+c_{2}\min_{\mu_{h}\in V_{h}}\|\lambda-\mu_{h}\|_{1},

(39)

with
c_{1}=(1+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|a\|)(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\|b\|)
,
c_{2}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\|b\|
.

Proof.

Define
\mathbf{e}_{h}:=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}
. Note that
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{e}_{h}=0
and

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{e}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b(\lambda-\lambda_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(40)

\displaystyle b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{e}_{h})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all}~\mu_{h}\in V_{h}.

(41)

We take an arbitrary
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. From (
37
) it follows, cf.
[
7
, Chapter II]
, that there exists
\mathbf{r}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}
such that

b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{r}_{h})=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h})\quad\text{for all}~\mu_{h}\in V_{h},~\text{and}~\|\mathbf{r}_{h}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\|b\|\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1}.

(42)

Define
\mathbf{w}_{h}:=\mathbf{r}_{h}+\mathbf{v}_{h}-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}(\mathbf{r}_{h}+\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{z})
. Note that
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{w}_{h}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z}
holds. Using
b(\mu_{h},Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{v})=0
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and all
\mu_{h}\in V_{h}
and with (
41
)-(
42
) we obtain

b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{r}_{h}+\mathbf{v}_{h})=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{u})=b(\mu_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\quad\text{for all}~\mu_{h}\in V_{h}.

Hence,
\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)
holds. Furthermore,
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})=0
, and thus
\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}
holds. Thus we get
\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\ker_{h}(b)\cap\mathbf{V}_{h,\ast}
. Using this, the ellipticity estimate (
36
) and (
40
) we obtain, for arbitrary
\tau_{h}\in V_{h}
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\big(a(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})+a(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\big(a(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})+b(\lambda_{h}-\lambda,\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\big(a(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})+b(\tau_{h}-\lambda,\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\big(\|a\|\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}+\|b\|\|\tau_{h}-\lambda\|_{1}\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}\big).

Using
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}\leq\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}+\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}
we obtain

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|a\|)\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|b\|\|\tau_{h}-\lambda\|_{1}.

(43)

From
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{u}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{u}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}}\mathbf{z}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{z}=Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}\mathbf{w}_{h}
we get
Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}}(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u})=0
and thus, using (
42
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|(I-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}})(\mathbf{w}_{h}-\mathbf{u})\|_{1}=\|(I-Q_{\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}},h}})(\mathbf{r}_{h}+\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{u})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}+\|\mathbf{r}_{h}\|_{1}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\|b\|)\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}.

Combining this with the result (
43
) yields

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|a\|)(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\|b\|)\|\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\|b\|\|\tau_{h}-\lambda\|_{1}.

Since
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
,
\tau_{h}\in V_{h}
are arbitray, this completes the proof.
∎

We thus derived an optimal order bound for the discretization error
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}
.
A similar optimal bound as in (
39
), with different constants
c_{1},c_{1}
, can be derived for the error
\|\lambda-\lambda_{h}\|_{1}
. Since in our application, this discretization error is less relevant we do not present this bound.

An
L^{2}
-norm estimate
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch\big(\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1}+\|\lambda-\lambda_{h}\|_{1}\big)
can be derived using the Aubin-Nitsche Lemma, cf.
[
6
, Secions 2.3.4, 2.4.2]
. For this one needs
H^{2}
-regularity of the saddle point problem (
25
). We are not aware of any regularity results for this type of saddle point problem.

Remark 5.1
.

Assume we have an inverse level set problem as in Section
2
with a level set transport resulting from a rigid motion
\mathbf{u}^{\ast}
(cf. Remark

) and

We consider the problem of near-isometric level set tracking introduced in

12
]
.
Let
\phi:\Omega_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a level set function and
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}\in\Gamma(0)
a point on the zero level set of
\phi
at
t=0
. Further, let
\mathbf{u}_{t}
be the solution to (
11
) for a fixed
t\in[0,t_{e}]
. The particle tracking problem reads as follows. Find
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(t):[0,t_{e}]\rightarrow\Omega
, such that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}&=\mathbf{u}_{t}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}(0)&=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}.\end{split}

(44)

We can approximate the solution
\mathbf{u}_{t}
of (
11
), for any given
t\in[0,t_{e}]
, by solving (
31
). For simplicity, we use an explicit Euler time stepping scheme for time discretization in (
44
). Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\Delta t:=\frac{t_{e}}{N}
,
t_{i}:=i\Delta t
and
i\in\{0,\dots,N\}
. Then we construct, given a starting point
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0}\in\Gamma(0)
, points
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{h,i}|\ i\in\{0,\dots,N\}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
using

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{h,i}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{h,i-1}+\Delta t\ \mathbf{u}_{h,t_{i-1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{h,i-1})\quad\text{for}~i=1,\ldots N,

(45)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{h,0}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0},

with
\mathbf{u}_{h,t_{i}}
being the solution to (
31
) at time
t_{i}
.

For comparison, we construct
\{\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}}_{h,i}|\ 0\leq i\leq N\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as solutions of

\displaystyle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}}_{h,i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}}_{h,i-1}+\Delta t\ \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t_{i-1}}(\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}}_{h,i-1})\quad\text{for}~i\in\{1,\dots N\},

(46)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}}_{h,0}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{0},

with
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t_{i}}:=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}}\nabla\phi
, i.e. a purely normal velocity field without any contribution tangential to the level set.

Proof of Lemma
2

Note that the colums of
R_{\mathbf{x}}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-y\\
0&1&x\end{pmatrix}
form a basis of the space
K
of rigid motions. The kernel space
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
is a linear subspace of
K
. Hence, there is a linear subspace
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\dim(U)=\dim(\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})
and such that
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
iff
\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})=R_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{p}
for a
\mathbf{p}\in U
and all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
.
For all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
we have
\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}=0
a.e. on
\Omega
. This yields

(R_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{p})^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{p}^{T}(R_{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x}))=0\quad\forall~\mathbf{p}\in U,~\forall~\mathbf{x}\in\Omega~\text{(a.e.)}.

Hence we obtain
U\perp W_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
with

W_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}:={\rm span}\{\,\cup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}R_{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})\}
.
Note that, with
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=(\hat{z}_{1},\hat{z}_{2})^{T}
, we have

R_{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{z}_{1}(\mathbf{x})\\
\hat{z}_{2}(\mathbf{x})\\
-y\hat{z}_{1}(\mathbf{x})+x\hat{z}_{2}(\mathbf{x})\end{pmatrix}.

Using
\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}\|=1
on
\Omega
it follows that
\dim(W_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})\geq 2
, which implies
\dim(U)\leq 1
. Thus the first statement of the Lemma
2
holds. From
\dim(W_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})=3
it follows that
\dim(U)=0
, which proves the second statement of the lemma.
Let
\mathbf{w}\in K
be a nontrivial rigid motion in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbf{w}_{\perp}
such that
\mathbf{w}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
on

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
\mathbf{w}
is a rotation we assume that the center of the rotation does not lie in
\overline{\Omega}
. For
\hat{\mathbf{z}}:=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{\perp}}{\|\mathbf{w}_{\perp}\|}
we then have
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. Per construction we have
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}
and since

\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}(\mathbf{w})

Appendix C
Proof of Lemma
4

For
\mathbf{z}
as in (
18
) we have
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}={\rm span}({\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})
, cf. Lemma
2
.
The solution of (
11
) is the unique solution of the variational problem:

\begin{split}&\mathbf{v}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}~~\text{with}~~(\mathbf{v},{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{v}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\,d\mathbf{x}=0~~\text{such that}\\
&a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=-a(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{w}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}.\end{split}

(47)

For
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
we write
\mathbf{v}=v_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}+v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}
,
\mathbf{w}=w_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}+w_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}
.
Note that
\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{T}=\nabla v_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{T}+{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}\nabla v_{1}^{T}+\nabla v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}^{T}+{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\nabla v_{2}^{T}
. Using this and
\mathbf{p}_{1}\mathbf{q}_{1}^{T}:\mathbf{p}_{2}\mathbf{q}_{2}^{T}=(\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2})(\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{2})
for
\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{q}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{v}):E(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=2\nabla v_{1}\cdot\nabla w_{1}+2\nabla v_{2}\cdot\nabla w_{2}

\displaystyle+2(\nabla v_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})+2(\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})(\nabla w_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle+2(\nabla v_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})(\nabla w_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})+2(\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}})(\nabla w_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}).

\mathbf{z}
is of the form
\mathbf{z}=z_{1}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}

V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}=\{\,v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}~|~v_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,\}

\mathbf{v}=v_{2}{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\in V_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}^{0}

\begin{split}a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})&=2\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{2}\cdot\nabla w_{2}+(\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})(\nabla w_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp})\,d\mathbf{x}\\

\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}=0\quad\text{on}~\Omega,~\nabla v_{2}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}=-\nabla z_{1}\cdot{\hat{\mathbf{z}}}_{\perp}\quad\text{on}~\Omega.

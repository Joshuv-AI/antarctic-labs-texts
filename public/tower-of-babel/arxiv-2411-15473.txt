Abstract
. In extended hearts of bounded t t -structures on a triangulated category, we provide a Happel-Reiten-Smalø tilting theorem and a characterization for s s -torsion pairs. Applying these to m m -extended module categories, we characterize torsion pairs induced by ( m + 1 ) (m+1) -term silting complexes. After establishing Auslander-Reiten theory in extended module categories, we introduce τ [ m ] \tau_{[m]} -tilting pairs and show bijections between τ [ m ] \tau_{[m]} -tilting pairs, ( m + 1 ) (m+1) -term silting complexes, and functorially finite s s -torsion pairs.



Introduction

Tilting theory has occupied a central position in the representation theory of algebras since the early seventies
[
BGP
,
APR
,
BB
,
HR
,
B
]
. One of the significant results is that each (classical) tilting module gives rise to a functorially finite torsion pair
[
AS
,
S
]
. Support
\tau
-tilting modules were introduced by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
AIR
]
, completing the theory in two important aspects. First, in terms of mutation: in contrast to classical tilting modules, where an almost complete tilting module may have one or two complements, an almost complete support
\tau
-tilting module has exactly two complements. Second, the support
\tau
-tilting modules correspond one-to-one with all functorially finite torsion pairs.

In the work
[
AIR
]
, the support
\tau
-tilting modules are also shown to correspond one-to-one with the 2-term silting complexes. Silting complexes were introduced by Keller and Vossieck
[
KV
]
to classify bounded t-structures in the bounded derived category of a Dynkin quiver. König and Yang
[
KY
]
extended this result to the case of general finite-dimensional (non-positive differential graded) algebras, proving that silting complexes correspond one-to-one with bounded t-structures with length heart. In contrast to 2-term silting complexes, which correspond to torsion pairs in the module category, Gupta
[
G
]
showed that general silting complexes correspond to torsion pairs in certain truncated subcategories of the derived category. We refer to these truncated subcategories as extended module categories.

In this paper, we first consider a general framework. Fix a positive integer
m
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category, and let
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
be a bounded
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
. We call

\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq-(m-1)}

the
m
-extended heart of
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
. This subcategory is closed under extensions and is, therefore, an extriangulated category in the sense of
[
NP
]
. A pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
is called a torsion pair if
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=0
and
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}=\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}
. A torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is called an
s
-torsion pair if
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}[-1])=0
. Applying a main result of
[
AET
]
to
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
, one obtains a bijection between the set of bounded
t
-structures
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
on
\mathcal{D}
, satisfying
\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
, and the set of
s
-torsion pairs in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
, see
Proposition

1.9
for more details. Moreover, we have the following generalization of Happel-Reiten-Smalø tilting.

Theorem 0.1

(
Theorem

1.12
)
.

For any
s
-torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
,
(\mathcal{F}[m],\mathcal{T})
is an
s
-torsion pair in the
m
-extended heart of the bounded
t
-structure corresponding to
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.

We also provide an alternative criterion for a torsion pair to be an
s
-torsion pair. Specifically, a torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is an
s
-torsion pair if and only if
\mathcal{T}
is closed under
m
-factors if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is closed under
m
-subobjects, see
Definition

1.13
,
Definition

1.17

1.18

We then apply these results to bounded derived categories of finite-dimensional algebras. Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field
\mathbf{k}
and
\operatorname{mod}A
be the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules. The
m
-extended module category

is the full subcategory of the bounded derived category

of

, given as

m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A=\{\mathbf{X}\in D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)\mid H^{i}(\mathbf{X})=0,\ \forall i\notin[-(m-1),0]\}.

It is shown in
[
G
]
that
(m+1)
-term silting complexes
\mathbf{P}\in K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
one-to-one correspond to functorially finite
s
-torsion pairs
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P}),\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}))
in

. We show that both
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
and

have enough projective objects and enough injective objects, see

. As a consequence, we have the following characterization of this torsion pair, where

Theorem 0.2

(
Theorem

)

Let

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})=\operatorname{Fac}_{m}\left(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})\right)\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})=\operatorname{Sub}_{m}\left(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])\right).

We also show that the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable projective objects in

(resp.

(resp.

The notion of Auslander-Reiten triangles on an extension-closed subcategory of a triangulated category, or more generally, Auslander-Reiten extriangles, or equivalently, almost split extensions on an extriangulated category, were introduced in

Theorem 0.3

(

3.12
)

Let

Note that these Auslander-Reiten translations

Finally, we generalize

-tilting theory from

in

There are bijections between

Convention

Throughout this paper,
\mathbf{k}
is a field, and
m
is a positive integer. Any category is assumed to be additive. Any subcategory of a category is assumed to be full. We use
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}
to denote that
\mathbf{X}
is an object in a category
\mathcal{X}
, and use
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
to denote that
\mathcal{X}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{Y}
. For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}
, we denote by
\operatorname{add}\mathbf{X}
the additive hull of
\mathbf{X}
, that is, the smallest additive subcategory of
\mathcal{X}
containing
\mathbf{X}
. For any two morphisms
f:\mathbf{Y}\to\mathbf{Z}
and
g:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}
, we denote by
f\circ g:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}
their composition.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a category. For any
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
and any
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{Y}
, a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
is a morphism
f:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}
with
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}
and such that any morphism from an object
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathbf{Y}
factors through
f
. A left
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
is defined dually. A subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{Y}
is called contravariantly (resp. covariantly) finite in
\mathcal{Y}
if any object in
\mathcal{Y}
has a right (resp. left)
\mathcal{X}
-approximation. A subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{Y}
is called functorially finite if it is both contravariantly finite and covariantly finite. A morphism
f:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}
is called right minimal if every morphism
g:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{X}
such that
f\circ g=f
is an isomorphism. A left minimal morphism is defined dually.

The shift functor in a triangulated category is denoted by
[1]
. For any two subcategories
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{Y}
the subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of objects
\mathbf{Z}
such that there is a triangle

\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Y}\to\mathbf{X}[1],

with
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{Y}
. We use
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0
to denote
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=0
for any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{Y}
.

For a vertex
i
of a quiver
Q
, we denote by
P_{i}
,
I_{i}
, and
S_{i}
, respectively, the corresponding projective, injective, and simple representations. For two arrows
a
and
b
of
Q
, we denote by
ab
the path first
a
then
b
.

1.
Generalized Happel-Reiten-Smalo tilting

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category. Throughout this section, for any two objects
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
in
\mathcal{D}
, we simply denote by
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
the set of morphisms from
\mathbf{X}
to
\mathbf{Y}
in
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
be a bounded
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
are subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
satisfying the following.

(1)

\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
,

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-1])=0
,

(3)

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}*\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-1]
, and

(4)

for any object
\mathbf{X}
in
\mathcal{D}
, there exists an integer
n>0
such that
\mathbf{X}[n]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathbf{X}[-n]\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
be its heart.

Notation 1.1
.

For any integer
p
, we denote

\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-p]\text{ and }\mathcal{D}^{\geq p}=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-p].

For any integers
p\leq q
, we denote

\mathcal{D}^{[p,q]}=\mathcal{D}^{\geq p}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\leq q}.

For any integer
p
, the inclusions
\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}\to\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq p}\to\mathcal{D}
admit adjoints

\sigma^{\leq p}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}\text{ and }\sigma^{\geq p}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\geq p},

which are called truncation functors.

Notation 1.2
.

For any integers
p\leq q
, we denote

H^{[p,q]}=\sigma^{\geq p}\circ\sigma^{\leq q}\simeq\sigma^{\leq q}\circ\sigma^{\geq p}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{[p,q]}.

For any integer
p
, we denote

H^{p}=H^{[p,p]}[p]:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{H}

the
p
-th cohomology functor with respect to the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
.

Definition 1.3
.

We call
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
the
m
-extended heart of a bounded
t
-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
.

By definition, the
1
-extended heart
\mathcal{D}^{[0,0]}
is the (usual) heart
\mathcal{H}
and in general,

\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}=\mathcal{H}[m-1]*\cdots*\mathcal{H}[1]*\mathcal{H}.

Remark 1.4
.

An extriangulated category is a triplet
(\mathcal{E},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is an additive category,
\mathbb{E}:\mathcal{E}^{\operatorname{op}}\times\mathcal{E}\to Ab
is a biadditive functor, and
\mathfrak{s}
is an additive realization of
\mathbb{E}
, satisfying certain conditions. We refer to
[
NP
, Definition 2.12]
for more details. Since
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
is closed under extensions (to see this, take the cohomologies), it becomes an extriangulated category, equipped with
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}):=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}[1])
and
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[\mathbf{Y}\xrightarrow{y}\mathbf{Z}\xrightarrow{x}\mathbf{X}]
for
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
, where
\mathbf{Y}\xrightarrow{y}\mathbf{Z}\xrightarrow{x}\mathbf{X}\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathbf{Y}[1]
is a triangle in
\mathcal{D}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
is an extriangulated category with a negative first extension in the sense of
[
AET
, Definition 2.3]
(cf. Example 2.4 there), where
\mathbb{E}^{-1}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}):=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}[-1])
.

Definition 1.5
.

A pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of subcategories of
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
is called a torsion pair provided that the following hold.

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=0
, and

(2)

\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}=\mathcal{T}\ast\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 1.6
.

The above definition of torsion pairs coincides with that in an extriangulated category introduced in
[

]
. However, this is not the case in
[

]
, see

Definition 1.7

(
[
AET

)
.

A torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
is called an
s
-torsion pair, if

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}[-1])=0.

In the usual case (i.e.,

), any torsion pair in
\mathcal{D}^{[0,0]}=\mathcal{H}
is an
s
-torsion pair, because
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}[-1])=0
.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{D}
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}^{\bot}
(resp.
{}^{\bot}\mathcal{X}
) the subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of objects
\mathbf{Z}
satisfying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})=0
(resp.

. The following lemma is from

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
s
-torsion pair in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
. Then
\mathcal{T}={}^{\bot}\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{T}^{\bot}
. In particular,
\mathcal{T}
and

are closed under taking extensions and direct summands.

The notion of

[

]
for arbitrary extriangulated categories with a negative first extension. In particular,

-torsion pairs in the triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
are exactly

-structures. Hence, applying
[

, Theorem 3.9]
to

Proposition 1.9

There is a bijection between

•

the set of bounded

-structures
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
on
\mathcal{D}

The following result shows that the vanishing condition on the first negative extension in the definition of

Corollary 1.10

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
s
-torsion pair in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}

(1.1)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}[j])=0,\ j<0.

Proof.

By

, there is a bounded

such that
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{C}^{\geq 1}\cap\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}

\mathcal{C}^{\geq 1}
is closed under

Remark 1.11
.

A torsion pair in the sense of
[

is a pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})

The following is a generalization of Happel-Reiten-Smalø tilting.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
s
-torsion pair in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
and let
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
be the corresponding bounded
t
-structure as in
Proposition

1.9
. Then
\mathcal{F}[m]*\mathcal{T}
is the
m
-extended heart
\mathcal{C}^{[-(m-1),0]}
of
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
. In particular,
(\mathcal{F}[m],\mathcal{T})
is an
s
-torsion pair in
\mathcal{C}^{[-(m-1),0]}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
, we have
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq-(m-1)}
and
\mathcal{F}[m]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}
. So we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}[m],\mathcal{T})=0
. Thus, we only need to show
\mathcal{F}[m]*\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{C}^{[-(m-1),0]}
. On the one hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{F}[m]*\mathcal{T}&\subseteq&\left(\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}*\mathcal{T}\right)\cap\left(\mathcal{F}[m]*\mathcal{D}^{\geq-(m-1)}\right)\\
&=&(\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}*\mathcal{T})\cap\left((\mathcal{F}[1]*\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})[m-1]\right)\\
&=&\mathcal{C}^{[-(m-1),0]}.\end{array}

Conversely, for any object
\mathbf{X}
in
\mathcal{C}^{[-(m-1),0]}=(\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}*\mathcal{T})\cap(\mathcal{F}[m]*\mathcal{D}^{\geq-(m-1)})
, there are triangles

\mathbf{Y}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{X}\xrightarrow{g}\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Y}[1]

and

\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{h}\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}[1]

with
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}
,

\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of

Proposition 1.18
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be a torsion pair in
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is an
s
-torsion pair.

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is closed under
m
-factors.

(3)

\mathcal{F}
is closed under
m
-subobjects.

Proof.

We only show the equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) since the equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) can be shown similarly.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Let
\mathbf{Z}
be an object in
\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{T})
. Then, by definition, there are triangles

\mathbf{Z}_{i}\to\mathbf{T}_{i}\to\mathbf{Z}_{i-1}\to\mathbf{Z}_{i}[1],\ 1\leq i\leq m,

with
\mathbf{Z}_{0}=\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{Z}_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{T}_{m}\in\mathcal{T}
. For any
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{F}
, applying
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbf{F})
to these triangles, we get exact sequences for all integers
j
:

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{i-1},\mathbf{F}[j+1])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j+1]).

When
j\leq-1
, since
\mathbf{T}_{i}\in\mathcal{T}
and
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{F}
, by
Corollary

1.10
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j])=0=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j+1]).

Hence, there are isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{i},\mathbf{F}[j])\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{i-1},\mathbf{F}[j+1]),\ j\leq-1.

Due to
\mathbf{Z}_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
and
\mathbf{F}[-m]\in\mathcal{F}[-m]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{m},\mathbf{F}[-m])=0
. Hence, by recursion, one has
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z}_{0},\mathbf{F})=0
. This implies
\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{Z}_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
by
Lemma

1.8
. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}
is closed under
m
-factors.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Let
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}
and
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathbf{T}[1]\in\mathcal{T}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{[-m,-1]}
, by truncation of
\mathbf{T}[1]
, there is a triangle

(1.6)

\mathbf{Y}[m]\to\mathbf{T}[1]\to\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Y}[m+1],

with
\mathbf{Y}=H^{-m}(\mathbf{T}[1])
and
\mathbf{Z}=H^{[-(m-1),-1]}(\mathbf{T}[1])\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),-1]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbf{F})
to this triangle, we get an exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Y}[m+1],\mathbf{F})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{F})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}[1],\mathbf{F})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Y}[m],\mathbf{F}).

Since
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq-(m-1)}
, the first item and the last item in the above sequence are zero. Hence, there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{F})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}[1],\mathbf{F}).

By
Example

1.14
(1),
\mathbf{Y}[m-1]\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m-1}(\mathcal{T})
. Shifting the triangle (
1.6
) by

, we get a triangle

\mathbf{Y}[m-1]\to\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{Z}[-1]\to\mathbf{Y}[m].

Since
\mathbf{Z}[-1]\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),-1]}[-1]=\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-2),0]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
, by the equality (

), we have
\mathbf{Z}[-1]\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{T})
. So, by
Example

1.14
(2),
\mathbf{Z}\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m+1}(\mathcal{T})
. Hence, by the inclusion (
1.5
),
\mathbf{Z}\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{T})
. Then
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{T}
, since
\mathcal{T}
is closed under
m
-factors. Thus, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{T}[1],\mathbf{F})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{F})=0
. Therefore,
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is an
s
-torsion pair.
∎

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
. If

for any

, then
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{X}),\mathbf{Y}[j])=0
for any

.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{Z}\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{X})
, by definition, there are triangles

\mathbf{Z}_{i}\to\mathbf{X}_{i}\to\mathbf{Z}_{i-1}\to\mathbf{Z}_{i}[1],\ 1\leq i\leq m,

with
\mathbf{Z}_{0}=\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{Z}_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{[-(m-1),0]}
and
\mathbf{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{X}_{m}\in\mathcal{X}
. Applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbf{Y})
to these triangles, we get exact sequences for all integers

and

When

, by the assumption, the first item and the last item in the above sequence are zero. Hence, there are isomorphisms

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The subcategory
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
is closed under
m
-factors and the subcategory
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
is closed under
m
-subobjects.

Proof.

We will show the first part of the assertion, as the argument for the second part is analogous. Let
\mathbf{Z}\in\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P}))
. Then by definition, there are triangles (
2.11
), where
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},-)
to these triangles, we get exact sequences for all integers
j
and
1\leq i\leq m

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}_{i}[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}_{i-1}[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}_{i}[j+1])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}_{i}[j+1]).

When
j\geq 1
, by (
2.9
), the first item and the last term in the above sequence are zero. Hence, there are isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}_{i-1}[j])\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}_{i}[j+1]),\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ j\geq 1.

For any
j\geq 1
, due to
\mathbf{Z}_{m}[j+m]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-(m+1)}
and that
\mathbf{P}
is an
(m+1)
-term complex of projective modules, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}_{m}[j+m])=0
. Hence, by recursion,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Z}[j])=0
for any
j\geq 1
. Thus, we have
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
. So
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
is closed under
m
-factors.
∎

A complex
\mathbf{P}\in K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is called
presilting
if
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}[j])=0
for any
j>0
. An object
\mathbf{X}
in an extriangulated category
\mathcal{E}
is called
projective
(resp.
injective
) if
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{X},\mathcal{E})=0
(resp.
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{X})=0
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be an
(m+1)
-term presilting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. The following hold.

(a)

H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
belongs to and is projective in
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

(b)

H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])
belongs to and is injective in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof.

(a) Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},-)
to the triangle (
2.4
), we get exact sequences

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{-m}(\mathbf{P})[m+i+1]),\ i\in\mathbb{Z}.

When
i\geq 1
, the first item is zero thanks to
\mathbf{P}
being presilting, and the last item is zero thanks to that
\mathbf{P}
is an
(m+1)
-term complex of projective modules. Therefore,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})[i])=0
for any
i\geq 1
. Hence,
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

Next, for any
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
, by
Lemma

2.3
, there is a monomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{T}[1])\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{T}[1])=0.

Hence
\mathbb{E}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{T})=\operatorname{Hom}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{T}[1])=0
, which implies that the object
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
is projective in
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

(b) Let
\mathbf{I}=\nu\mathbf{P}[-1]
. By canonical truncation of
\mathbf{I}
, there is a triangle

(2.12)

H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})\to\mathbf{I}\to H^{1}(\mathbf{I})[-1]\to H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})[1].

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}-)
to this triangle, we get exact sequences

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{1}(\mathbf{I})[i-2])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{I}[i]),\ i\in\mathbb{Z}.

When
i\leq 0
, the first item is zero because
\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
H^{1}(\mathbf{I})[i-2]\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 2}
, and the last item
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{I}[i])=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\nu\mathbf{P}[i-1])
, by the duality (
2.3
), is isomorphic to
D\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}[i-1],\mathbf{P})=0
, since
\mathbf{P}
is presilting. Hence,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})[i])=0
for any
i\leq 0
. Thus,
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.

Next, for any
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
, applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},-)
to the triangle (
2.12
), we get an exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},H^{1}(\mathbf{I})[-1])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})[1])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{I}[1]),

where the first item is zero because
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
H^{1}(\mathbf{I})[-1]\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}
, and the last item
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{I}[1])=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},\nu\mathbf{P})
, which by the duality (
2.3
), is isomorphic to
D\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{F})=0
. So
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{F},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I}))=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F},H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{I})[1])=0
, which implies that the object
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])
is injective in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.
∎

Let
\mathbf{P}
be an
(m+1)
-term presilting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. Consider the following two subcategories of
D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)
:

D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})=\{\mathbf{X}\in D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])=0,\ \forall i>0\},

and

D^{\geq 0}(\mathbf{P})=\{\mathbf{X}\in D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])=0,\ \forall i<0\}.

Remark 2.7
.

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])=0
for any
i>0
, because
\mathbf{P}
is an
(m+1)
-term silting complex of projective modules and
\mathbf{X}[i]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-(m+1)}
for
i>0
. Hence,

(2.13)

\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}\subseteq D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P}).

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])=0
for any
i<0
, because
\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathbf{X}[i]\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}
for
i<0
. Hence,

(2.14)

\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}\subseteq D^{\geq 0}(\mathbf{P}).

A presilting complex
\mathbf{P}\in K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is called
silting
if
\operatorname{thick}\mathbf{P}=K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
, where
\operatorname{thick}\mathbf{P}
denotes the smallest triangulated subcategory of
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
containing
\mathbf{P}
and closed under taking direct summands. We provide a sufficient condition for an
(m+1)
-term presilting complex to be silting (which is also a necessary condition, see
Proposition

2.14
below). This will be used in
Section

4
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be an
(m+1)
-term presilting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. Suppose that for any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
, there is a triangle

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}[1],

with
\mathbf{T}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
and
\mathbf{Z}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. Then
\mathbf{P}
is silting.

Proof.

We first claim that

(2.15)

D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}.

Suppose, to the contrary, that there is an object
\mathbf{X}\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})
which is not in
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
. Then there exists a positive integer
\xi>0
such that
H^{\xi}(\mathbf{X})\neq 0
and
H^{j}(\mathbf{X})=0
for any
j>\xi
. By canonical truncation of
\mathbf{X}
, there is a triangle

\sigma^{\leq-m+\xi}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathbf{X}\to H^{[-(m-1)+\xi,\xi]}(\mathbf{X})\to\sigma^{\leq-m+\xi}(\mathbf{X})[1].

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},-)
to this triangle, we get an exact sequence for any integer
i
,

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1)+\xi,\xi]}(\mathbf{X})[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\sigma^{\leq-m+\xi}(\mathbf{X})[i+1]).

By
\mathbf{X}\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})
, the first item is zero for any
i\geq 1
. Because
\mathbf{P}
is an
(m+1)
-term complex of projective modules and
\sigma^{\leq-m+\xi}(\mathbf{X})[i+1]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m+\xi-i-1}
, the last item is zero for any
i\geq\xi
. Hence,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},H^{[-(m-1)+\xi,\xi]}(\mathbf{X})[i])=0
for any
i\geq\xi
. Let
\mathbf{M}=H^{[-(m-1)+\xi,\xi]}(\mathbf{X})[\xi]\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. Then
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{M}[i])=0
for any
i\geq 0
. So
\mathbf{M}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
. By
Lemma

2.3
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{M})=0
. By the assumption of the proposition, there is a triangle

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{Z}[1],

with
\mathbf{T}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
and
\mathbf{Z}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. Since the morphism from
\mathbf{T}_{0}
to
\mathbf{M}
is zero, it follows that
\mathbf{M}[-1]
is direct summand of
\mathbf{Z}
and hence is in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. So
H^{0}(\mathbf{M})=H^{1}(\mathbf{M}[-1])=0
, which implies
H^{\xi}(\mathbf{X})=H^{\xi}(H^{[-(m-1)+\xi,\xi]}(\mathbf{X}))=H^{0}(\mathbf{M})=0
, a contradiction. Thus, we finish the proof of the claim (
2.15
).

Next, for any
\mathbf{X}\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})
, take a right
\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}
-approximation
f
of
\mathbf{X}
and extend it to a triangle in
D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)

\mathbf{X}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{X}^{\prime}[1].

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},-)
to it, there is a long exact sequence

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},f)}&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X})&\to&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}^{\prime}[1])\\
\to&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime}[1])&\to&\cdots&\to&\cdots\\
\to&\cdots&\to&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])&\to&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}^{\prime}[i+1])\\
\to&\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime}[i+1])&\to&\cdots\end{array}

Since
f
is a right
\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}
-approximation of
\mathbf{X}
, the map
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},f)
is surjective. Since
\mathbf{P}
is presilting,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime}[i])=0
for any
i>0
. Since
\mathbf{X}\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}[i])=0
for any
i>0
. Hence, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{X}^{\prime}[i])=0
for any
i>0
, which implies
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})
. Therefore,
D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}*D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})[1]
. Recursively,
D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[1]*\cdots*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[s]*D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})[s+1]
holds for any
s\geq 0
.

Finally, to show
\mathbf{P}
is silting, it suffices to show
A[m]\in\operatorname{thick}(\mathbf{P})
. Since
A[m]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-m}
, by (
2.13
), we have
A[m]\in D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[1]*\cdots*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[m]*D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})[m+1]
. However, since by (
2.15
),
D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})[m+1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq-(m+1)}
, there is no non-zero morphism from
A[m]
to any object in
D^{\leq 0}(\mathbf{P})[m+1]
. Thus,
A[m]
is a direct summand of an object in
\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[1]*\cdots*\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}[m]
. Therefore,
A[m]\in\operatorname{thick}(\mathbf{P})
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

The object
\mathfrak{t}(\nu A[m-1])
is injective in
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
. The object
\mathfrak{f}(A)
is projective in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.

with respect to

For any
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be an
(m+1)
-term silting complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. The following hold.

(a)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
, there is a triangle

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}[1],

with
\mathbf{T}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

(b)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
,
\mathbf{X}
is projective in
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
if and only if
\mathbf{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})
.

(c)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
, there is a triangle

\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{X}[1],

with
\mathbf{T}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}\mathfrak{t}(\nu A[m-1])
and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

(d)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
,
\mathbf{X}
is injective in
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
if and only if
\mathbf{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{add}\mathfrak{t}(\nu A[m-1])
.

(e)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
, there is a triangle

\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{F}_{0}\to\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{X}[1],

with
\mathbf{F}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])
and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.

(f)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
,
\mathbf{X}
is injective in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
if and only if
\mathbf{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{add}H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\nu\mathbf{P}[-1])
.

(g)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
, there is a triangle

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{F}_{0}\to\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Z}[1],

with
\mathbf{F}_{0}\in\operatorname{add}\mathfrak{f}(A)
and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
.

(h)

For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
,
\mathbf{X}
is projective in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
if and only if
\mathbf{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{add}\mathfrak{f}(A)
.

Proof.

We only prove (a)-(d) since (e)-(h) can be proved dually.

(a) For any
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
, take a right
\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}
-approximation
f_{\mathbf{X}}:\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{X}}\to\mathbf{X}
of
\mathbf{X}
, where
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{X}}\in\operatorname{add}\mathbf{P}
. Extended
f_{\mathbf{X}}
to a triangle in
D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)
:

\mathbf{Y}\to\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{X}}\xrightarrow{f_{\mathbf{X}}}\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}[1].

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P},-)

3.
Auslander-Reiten theory in extended module categories

In this section, we study the Auslander-Reiten theory in the

Lemma 3.1

Notation 3.2

We denote by

(resp.

Definition 3.3

A triangle in

(ii)

\mathbf{Y}\to\mathbf{Z}
in the triangle.

]

[

\operatorname{Hom}(\sigma_{\leq-m}(\mathbf{P}_{1}),H^{-m}(\mathbf{P}_{2})[m])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{P}_{1},H^{-m}(\mathbf{P}_{2})[m])\to 0.

Then
g
factors through
\sigma_{\leq-m}(\mathbf{P}_{1})
. Since
\mathbf{P}_{1}\in K^{[-m,0]}(\operatorname{proj}A)
,
\sigma_{\leq-m}(\mathbf{P}_{1})=P[m]
for some
P\in\operatorname{proj}A
. Conversely, if a morphism
g:\mathbf{P}_{1}\to\mathbf{P}_{2}
in
K^{[-m,0]}
factoring through
P[m]
for some
P\in\operatorname{proj}A
, since
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(P[m])=0
, one has
H^{[-(m-1),0]}(g)=0
. Therefore, the ideal of
K^{[-m,0]}(\operatorname{proj}A)
consisting of morphisms factoring through
\operatorname{add}A[m]
is the kernel of the functor
F
. Hence,
F
induces an equivalence

\overline{F}\colon K^{[-m,0]}(\operatorname{proj}A)/\operatorname{add}A[m]\xrightarrow{\simeq}m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A,

with the quasi-inverse giving by taking a minimal projective presentation. This equivalence restricts to an equivalence from
\operatorname{proj}A
to
\operatorname{proj}A
. So
\overline{F}
induces an equivalence

\overline{\overline{F}}\colon K^{[-m,0]}(\operatorname{proj}A)/\operatorname{add}(A\oplus A[m])\xrightarrow{\simeq}\underline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A.

Dually, there is an equivalence also induced by the cohomology
H^{[-(m-1),0]}
:

\overline{\overline{E}}\colon K^{[-(m-1),1]}(\operatorname{inj}A)/\operatorname{add}(\nu A[-1]\oplus\nu A[m-1])\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A

with the quasi-inverse given by taking a minimal injective presentation. Therefore, the composition of equivalences

\overline{\overline{E}}\circ[-1]\circ\nu\circ\overline{\overline{F}}^{-}:\underline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A

sends
\mathbf{Z}
to
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
, with the quasi-inverse
\overline{\overline{F}}\circ\nu^{-}\circ[1]\circ\overline{\overline{E}}^{-}
sending
\mathbf{Z}
to
\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})
.
∎

We proceed to summarise some properties of
\tau_{[m]}
and
\tau_{[m]}^{-}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}
be an indecomposable object in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
.

(i)

\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
has no nonzero injective objects in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
as direct summands.

(ii)

\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})
has no nonzero projective objects in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
as direct summands.

(iii)

\mathbf{Z}
is projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
if and only if
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})=0
.

(iv)

\mathbf{Z}
is injective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
if and only if
\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})=0
.

(v)

If
\mathbf{Z}
is not projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
, then
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
is indecomposable and

\tau_{[m]}^{-}\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}.

(vi)

If
\mathbf{Z}
is not injective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
, then
\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})
is indecomposable and

\tau_{[m]}\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}.

Proof.

For (i), suppose, to the contrary, that there exists a nonzero injective object
\mathbf{Z}_{1}
in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
such that
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{Z}_{2}
for some
\mathbf{Z}_{2}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. By
Lemma

3.1
,
\mathbf{Z}_{1}=\nu P[m-1]
for some
P\in\operatorname{proj}A
. Rotating the triangle (
3.1
), we obtain a triangle

H^{0}(\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z}))[-2]\to(\nu P[m-1])\oplus\mathbf{Z}_{2}\to\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})[-1]\to H^{0}(\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z}))[-1].

Since
\operatorname{Hom}(H^{0}(\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z}))[-2],\nu P[m-1])\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H^{0}(\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})),\nu P[m+1])=0
,
\nu P[m-1]
is also a direct summand of
\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})[-1]
. So
P[m]
is a direct summand of
\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})
, which contradicts the minimality of the projective presentation
\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})
of
\mathbf{Z}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. The assertion (ii) can be proved similarly.

For (iii), if
\mathbf{Z}
is projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
, by
Lemma

3.1
,
\mathbf{Z}
is isomorphic to some
P\in\operatorname{proj}A
. So
\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{p}\mathbf{Z}\cong P
. Then

\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})=\sigma^{\leq 0}(\nu\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{Z})[-1])=\sigma^{\leq 0}(\nu P[-1])=0.

Conversely, if
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})=0
, then by
Proposition

3.10
,
\mathbf{Z}
is zero in
\underline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A
. So
\mathbf{Z}
is projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. The assertion (iv) can be proved similarly.

For (v), since
\mathbf{Z}
is indecomposable and not projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
, by
Proposition

3.10
,
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
is indecomposable in
\overline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A
. By (i), any nonzero direct summand of
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
is not injective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. Hence,
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
is indecomposable in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. Also by
Proposition

3.10
, we have
\tau_{[m]}^{-}\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}
in
\underline{m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}}A
. By (ii),
\tau_{[m]}^{-}\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})
has no nonzero direct summands which are projective in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. So we have
\tau_{[m]}^{-}\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}
in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
. The assertion (vi) can be proved similarly.
∎

Now we prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}
be an indecomposable object in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A

\mathbf{Z}

\mathbf{Z}

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{W}\to\tau_{[m]}^{-}(\mathbf{Z})\to\mathbf{Z}[1].

4.
\tau
-tilting theory for extended module categories

In this section, we generalize the
\tau

\operatorname{mod}A
to

. Note that the extriangulated category

[

]

Definition 4.1
.

An object
\mathbf{X}
in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
is called
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid if

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X}))=0.

\tau_{[m]}
-rigid object
\mathbf{X}
in

is called positive
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid if in addition

(4.1)

\mathbb{E}^{j}(\mathbf{X},\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X}))=0,\ j<0.

In the above definition, both
\mathbf{X}
and
\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X})
are in
m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
, so for any
j\leq-m
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X})[j])=0
. Therefore, the range of values for
j
in condition (
4.1
) can be replaced with
-(m-1)\leq j\leq-1.

Example 4.2
.

In the usual case (i.e.,
m=1
), any
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid module is positive
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid. However, this is not true in general. In

3.16
, since

, the object
(0\to S_{1})
in

is
\tau_{[2]}
-rigid but not positive
\tau_{[2]}
-rigid.

Remark 4.3
.

By

3.17
, an object
\mathbf{X}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A
is positive
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid if and only if
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{X},\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(\mathbf{X}))=0

\tau_{[m]}
-rigid objects, it is possible to avoid assuming both the existence of negative extensions and the existence of the Auslander-Reiten translation.

\tau
-rigid pairs in the module category.

as a direct summand for any nonzero

. Then

. Hence, the map sending

is a bijection, with inverse sending

to
(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}),P)
, from the set of (isoclasses of) basic pairs

with

and

to the set of (isoclasses of) basic

-term complexes in

. Thus, to complete the proof, we only need to show

is positive
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid if and only if

Now we are ready to prove

4.7
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.7
.

To show
\psi
is a bijection, by

4.9
, we only need to show that for any positive
\tau_{[m]}
-rigid pair
(\mathbf{X},P)
, it is
\tau_{[m]}
-tilting if and only if the corresponding presilting complex
\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{X})\oplus P[m]

\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{X})\oplus P[m]

H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{X}

) the definition of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
. We claim

{}^{\bot_{\leq 0}}(\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X}))\cap P^{\bot_{\leq 0}}=\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P}).

Indeed, by the isomorphisms in (
4.7
), we have
{}^{\bot_{\leq 0}}(\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X}))=\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{p}_{m}(X))
. Note that for any
\mathbf{Y}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A

\operatorname{Hom}(P[m],\mathbf{Y}[i])=0
for any

if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}(P[m],\mathbf{Y}[i])=0
for any
1\leq i\leq m
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}(P,\mathbf{Y}[i])=0
for any
i\leq 0
. So we have

{}^{\bot_{\leq 0}}(\tau_{[m]}(\mathbf{X}))\cap P^{\bot_{\leq 0}}=\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{p}_{m}(X))\cap\mathcal{T}(P[m])=\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})
.

\mathbf{P}
is silting if and only if
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}))

2.15
. For the “if” part, for any object
\mathbf{M}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{P})\subseteq\operatorname{Fac}_{m}(H^{[-(m-1),0]}(\mathbf{P}))
, by definition of
m
-factors, there is a triangle

\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{Z}[1],

with

and
\mathbf{Z}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A

2.8
,
\mathbf{P}

\psi

By the bijection in

2.11
and using

2.15
,

and

By the isomorphisms (

) in

2.3
, we have

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{p}_{m}(\mathbf{X})\oplus P[m])=\{\mathbf{Y}\in m\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}[j])=0,\ j\leq 0\}=\mathbf{X}^{\bot_{\leq 0}}.

Thus, the bijection

has the form shown in the theorem.
∎

Appendix A
Truncation

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field
\mathbf{k}
, and
\operatorname{mod}A
the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules. In this appendix, we collect some basic constructions about truncating complexes of modules in
\operatorname{mod}A
, mainly for the constructions of the Auslander-Reiten translations
\tau_{[m]}
and
\tau_{[m]}^{-}
in
Section

3
.

For any complex
\mathbf{X}=(X^{i},d^{i}:X^{i}\to X^{i+1})
of modules in
\operatorname{mod}A
, there are the following two kinds of truncation. Let
p
be an integer.

The
stupid truncation

\sigma_{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})
and
\sigma_{\geq p}(\mathbf{X})
are defined as:

\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccc}\sigma_{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})&=&(\cdots&\xrightarrow{d^{p-2}}&X^{p-1}&\xrightarrow{d^{p-1}}&X^{p}&\to&0&\to&\cdots),\\
\sigma_{\geq p}(\mathbf{X})&=&(\cdots&\to&0&\to&X^{p}&\xrightarrow{d^{p}}&X^{p+1}&\xrightarrow{d^{p+1}}&\cdots).\end{array}

There is a triangle in
D(\operatorname{mod}A)
:

\sigma_{\geq p+1}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathbf{X}\to\sigma_{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})\to\sigma_{\geq p+1}(\mathbf{X})[1].

The
canonical truncation

\sigma^{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})
and
\sigma^{\geq p}(\mathbf{X})
are defined as

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}\sigma^{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})=&(\cdots&\xrightarrow{d^{p-2}}&X^{p-1}&\xrightarrow{d^{p-1}}&\operatorname{ker}d^{p}&\to&0&\to&\cdots),\\
\sigma^{\geq p}(\mathbf{X})=&(\cdots&\to&0&\to&\operatorname{coker}d^{p-1}&\xrightarrow{d^{p}}&X^{p+1}&\xrightarrow{d^{p+1}}&\cdots).\end{array}

There is a triangle in
D(\operatorname{mod}A)
:

\sigma^{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})\to\mathbf{X}\to\sigma^{\geq p+1}(\mathbf{X})\to\sigma^{\leq p}(\mathbf{X})[1].

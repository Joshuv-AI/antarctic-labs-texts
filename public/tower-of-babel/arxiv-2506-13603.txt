On volumes and the generic invariance of Fano type varieties
Donghyeon Kim

Abstract
. We demonstrate the generic invariance of the Fano type property in cases where the volumes of anti-canonical divisors of Fano type fibers are a constant over a Zariski-dense subset, or the Fano type fibers are dimension 2 2 . Additionally, paralleling this theorem, we establish a conjecture by Schwede and Smith under the condition that the volumes of anti-canonical divisors remain constant in the reduction mod p p .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14E05, 14E30, 14J45, 14G17

1.
Introduction

The advancement of birational geometry and the Minimal Model Program (MMP) allows us to study the boundedness of varieties. Hacon, McKernan, and Xu proved a boundedness result for varieties with ample canonical divisors in
[
HMX18
]
, while Birkar established in
[
Bir19
]
and
[
Bir24
]
that Fano type (fibration) maintains a good boundedness property. Here, a variety
X
is of
Fano type
if there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta
on
X
with
(X,\Delta)
being klt and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
ample.

A set of varieties
\mathcal{S}:=\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is
bounded
if there exists a projective morphism
V\to W
of varieties such that
X_{i}
is a closed fiber of
V\to W
for each
i\in I
. The next step after assessing boundedness is to inquire if
X_{i}
has a good property, then so does the geometric generic point of
W
. For instance, if
X_{i}
is Fano for each
i\in I
, the geometric generic point of
W
is also Fano (cf.
[
Laz04a
]
*Theorem 1.2.17).

Fano type varieties extend the concept of Fano in the context of MMP. If
X
is Fano,
D
a Cartier divisor on
X
, and
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
a step in
D
-MMP, then
X^{\prime}
is not Fano. However,
X^{\prime}
is of Fano type. Thus, studying Fano type varieties follows naturally from the study of Fano varieties.

One may propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
X\to S
be a projective and surjective morphism between two normal varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be a Zariski-dense subset of
S
such that for any closed point
s\in S^{\prime}
, the fiber
X_{s}
is of Fano type. Then the geometric generic fiber
X_{\overline{\eta}}
is of Fano type, where
\eta\in S
is the generic point.

Note that the conjecture presents a milder form of
[
CLZ25
]
*Question 1.1. When
S^{\prime}
is an open subset of
S
, the conjecture holds by the semicontinuity property of cohomology, leaving us to deal with scenarios where
S^{\prime}
can be very sparse.

Let
X
be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
, and let
D
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
. Then the volume of
D
is defined as

\mathrm{vol}(D):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{k}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD))}{\frac{m^{\dim X}}{(\dim X)!}}.

Our first result on the conjecture is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider
v>0
as a positive number. Suppose
(X,\Delta)
is a pair over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. Let
X\to S
be a projective and surjective morphism between two normal varieties over
k
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a Zariski-dense subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
satisfying the following conditions. For any closed point
s\in S^{\prime}
:

(1)

The volume is given by
\mathrm{vol}(-(K_{X_{s}}+\Delta_{s}))=v
.

(2)

The fiber
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is of Fano type.

(b)

The geometric generic fiber
(X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})
of
(X,\Delta)
is of Fano type, where
\eta\in S
is the generic point of
S
.

We can think that if we can establish condition (1) in the theorem, Conjecture
1.1
follows immediately.

Moreover, we can prove Conjecture
1.1
in the dimension
2
case without any conditions on volumes of anti-canonical divisors.

Theorem 1.3
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds if the Fano type fibers have dimension
2
.

Note that the proof of Theorem
1.3
is almost the same as that of
[
GT16
]
*Theorem 1.1. As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
and
1.3
, we can prove the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a bounded family of Fano type surfaces over
\mathbb{C}
. Then the set

\{\mathrm{vol}(-K_{X})\mid X\in\mathcal{S}\}

is a DCC set.

Note that we can view Corollary
1.4
as a partial anti-canonical version of the DCC property of canonical volumes as in
[
HMX14
]
*Theorem 1.3. The boundedness condition is crucial in the proof. See Example
5.6
.

Next, we see the reduction mod
p
side. In many aspects, the situations in reduction mod
p
are parallel to the situation in characteristic
0
side. For example, let
X
be a variety over
\mathbb{C}
. If there is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
R
such that
X_{s}
is strongly
F
-regular for any
s\in\Spec R
, then
X
is klt (cf.
[
HW02
]
*Theorem). It can be viewed as a reduction mod
p
counterpart of the fact that for a flat family
X\to S
, if
X_{s}
is klt for any closed point
s\in S
, then the geometric generic point
X_{\overline{\eta}}
is klt (cf.
[
Laz04b
]
*Theorem 9.5.16) (for the definition of strong
F
-regularity, see Definition
3.1
).

Schwede and Smith proposed the following conjecture in
[
SS10
]
:

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
X
be a globally
F
-regular type variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Then
X
is of Fano type (for the definition of the notion of globally
F
-regular type variety, refer to Definition
3.3
).

Note that global
F
-regularity can be considered as a “right” definition of the notion of Fano type over positive characteristic fields, and therefore we may view Conjecture
1.5
as a reduction mod
p
version of Conjecture
1.1
(for the definition of global
F
-regularity, see Definition
3.1
). The conjecture can be used for Fano type results in many moduli spaces (cf.
[
SZ20
]
and
[
WW24
]
).

Conjecture
1.5
is proved in the surface case in
[
GT16
]
,
[
HP15
]
and
[
Oka17
]
. Moreover, the conjecture holds if
X
is a Mori dream space by
[
GOST15
]
. Furthermore, it is announced that if
X
is a smooth globally
F
-regular type threefold with nef anti-canonical divisor, then the conjecture is true (
[
CKT24
]
). As a parallel result of Theorem
1.2
, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Consider
v>0
as a positive number. Suppose
(X,\Delta)
is a pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra and
(X_{R},\Delta_{R})
a model of
(X,\Delta)
over
R
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a Zariski-dense subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq\Spec R
satisfying the following conditions. For any closed point
s\in S^{\prime}
:

(1)

The volume is given by
\mathrm{vol}(-(K_{X_{\overline{s}}}+\Delta_{\overline{s}}))=v
.

(2)

The fiber
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is globally
F
-regular.

(b)

The pair
(X,\Delta)
is of Fano type.

We will outline two key technical components in proving both Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.6
. Firstly, we employ
[
CJK23
]
*Theorem 1.3 as a criterion for establishing the Fano type property (see Lemma
5.2
), which conveniently eliminates the need to account for klt
m
-complements in Fano type fibers. Secondly, it is essential to evaluate Nakayama’s asymptotic order concerning both closed and generic fibers. To tackle this, we need Jiao’s result of volumes of families of big divisors (
[
Jia25
]
).

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we collect basic definitions, lemmas, and properties. In Section
3
, we collect basic definitions and theorems in positive characteristic algebraic geometry. In Section
4
, we introduce Jiao’s result
[
Jia25
]
and prove an analog of the result. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the main theorems.

In this paper,

•

•

•

The

Let

2.1.
Log discrepancies in a family

The subsection is devoted to the proof of the following two propositions. Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair over
k
,
f:X\to S
be a projective morphism between two varieties over
k
, and let
E
be a prime divisor over
X
which is flat over
S
. Suppose
\eta\in S
is the generic point. Then after replacing
S
with a finite cover and shrinking
S
, for a general closed point
s\in S
, we obtain

A_{X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}}(E_{\overline{\eta}})=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(E_{s}).

Proof.

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X_{\overline{\eta}}
be a log resolution of
(X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})
. Note that
f
is determined by finitely many equations and replacing
S
with a finite cover, we can construct a proper birational morphism
f_{S}:X^{\prime}_{S}\to X
over
S
such that
f_{S}\times_{S}\overline{\eta}=f
(cf. Remark
2.2
). Additionally, we can ensure that

is a simple normal crossing (snc) divisor on
X^{\prime}_{S}
, and
f_{S}
is a log resolution for each fiber. Now,

\displaystyle A_{X,\Delta}(E)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mult}_{E}(f^{*}_{S}(K_{X}+\Delta)-K_{X^{\prime}_{S}})+1

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mult}_{E_{\overline{\eta}}}(f^{*}(K_{X_{\overline{\eta}}}+\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})-K_{X^{\prime}})+1

\displaystyle=A_{X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}}}(E_{\overline{\eta}}),

and similarly,
A_{X,\Delta}(E)=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(E_{s})
for a general
s\in S
.
∎

.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair over

2.2.
Nakayama’s asymptotic order

This subsection aims to define Nakayama’s
asymptotic order
. For more details, see
[
Nak04
]
*Chapter 3.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
with arbitrary characteristic, and
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
be a valuation. Suppose
D
is a big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, then define

\nu(|D|):=\inf\left\{\nu(D^{\prime})\mid D^{\prime}\in|D|\right\},

and the
asymptotic order

\sigma_{\nu}(D):=\inf\left\{\nu(D^{\prime})|D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}\right\}.

Note that for any proper birational morphism
f:X^{\prime}\to X
from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence
D^{\prime}\mapsto f^{*}D^{\prime}
between
|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
|f^{*}D|_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and

\nu(D^{\prime})=\nu(f^{*}D^{\prime})

for any
D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus, we have
\sigma_{\nu}(D)=\sigma_{\nu}(f^{*}D)
. If
\nu=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
for some prime divisor
E
over
X
, denote it by
\sigma_{E}(D):=\sigma_{\mathrm{ord}_{E}}(D)
.

Let us prove a lemma that is instrumental in the proofs of main theorems.

Lemma 2.7

(cf.
[
Jia25
]
*Lemma 2.5)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety over
k
,
D
a big
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, and let
E
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier prime divisor on
X
. Suppose
a\geq 0
is a rational number. Then
a>\sigma_{E}(D)
if and only if

\mathrm{vol}(D-aE)<\mathrm{vol}(D).

Proof.

Let us prove the “if” side. For any
m
such that
am
is an integer,
am\leq\mathrm{ord}_{E}(|mD|)
, and thus if
a\leq\sigma_{E}(D)
, then the map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD-amE))\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD))

is an isomorphism. This gives the proof of the “if” side. The proof of the “only if” side is the same as
[
Jia25
]
*Lemma 2.5.
∎

3.
Positive characteristic theory

In this section, all varieties and pairs are over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
.

Let
X
be a finite type scheme over
k
. Then the
Frobenius

F:X\to X
is defined by the identity on the underlying topological space of
X
, and
p
-th power on the corresponding map of structure sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. For a positive integer

, let us denote by
F^{e}_{X}:X\to X
the

-th iteration of the Frobenius.

The notion of strong
F
-regularity is introduced in
[
HH89
]
, and later a global analog of the notion, global
F
-regularity is introduced in
[
Smi00
]
. Furthermore, a relation of global
F
-regularity and Fano type property is established in
[
SS10
]
. In this subsection, we collect basic definitions and properties on the subject.

Let
X
be a normal variety, and fix any effective divisor
D
on
X
. We have an inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. Thus, we also have an inclusion
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, and thus the composition

\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D).

The composition provides the definition of
globally
F
-regular variety
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair. Then
(X,\Delta)
is
globally
F
-regular
if, for every effective divisor
D
, there exists some
e>0
such that the composition
\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\right\rceil+D)
splits in the category of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

Moreover,
(X,\Delta)
is
strongly
F
-regular
if there is an open covering
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
such that
(U_{i},\Delta|_{U_{i}})
is globally
F
-regular.

Recall one of the main theorems in
[
SS10
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(cf.
[
SS10
]
*Theorem 1.1, Remark 4.11)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective globally
F
-regular pair. Then there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
such that

(a)

(X,\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})
is globally
F
-regular, and

(b)

K_{X}+\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Finally, let us recall the definition of
globally
F
-regular type pair
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair over
\mathbb{C}
. We say that

4.
Fujita approximation and volumes of divisors in a family

In this section, we state the main theorem of
[

]
, and prove an analog of the main theorem. Let us recall the Fujita approximation theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(cf.
[
Fuj94
]
*Theorem and
[
Tak07
]
*Theorem 0.1)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
, and
D
a big Cartier divisor on
X
. Then, for an arbitrarily small real number
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a birational morphism
f:X^{\prime}\to X
of projective varieties and a decomposition

which satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

A
is an ample divisor
E
is an effective divisor on
X
.

(b)

\mathrm{vol}(A)>\mathrm{vol}(D)-\varepsilon
.

Note that the characteristic of
k
could be positive. Next, let us recall the main result of
[
Jia25
]
.

Theorem 4.2

(cf.
[
Jia25
]
*Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a projective flat morphism of varieties over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
and
D
a Cartier divisor on
X
. Suppose
X_{\overline{\eta}}
is the geometric generic fiber of
f
. Write
D_{\overline{\eta}}:=D|_{X_{\overline{\eta}}}
and
D_{s}:=D|_{X_{s}}
for every closed point
s\in S
. Then

\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{\eta}})=\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{s})

for any Zariski-dense subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
.

The following theorem can be considered as a reduction mod
p
version of Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
,
D
a Cartier divisor, and let
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra such that there is a model
X_{R}
of
X
over
R
. For a closed point
s\in\Spec R
, write
X_{\overline{s}}
the geometric fiber of
X
at
s
. Then

\mathrm{vol}(D)=\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})

for any Zariski-dense subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq\Spec R
.

Proof.

Note that the proof is almost the same as
[
Jia25
]
*Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Using the upper semicontinuity property of cohomology (refer to
[
Har77
]
*Theorem 12.8), it follows that

\mathrm{vol}(D)\leq\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{s}).

Assume this result holds when
D
is big. For a not big
D
, we choose an ample divisor
H_{R}
on
X_{R}
. We define
H:=H_{R}\times_{R}\mathbb{C}
and

t_{0}:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid D+tH\text{ becomes big}\}.

By applying the given result to
D+tH
for each
t>t_{0}
, it results in

\mathrm{vol}(D+tH)=\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}}+tH_{\overline{s}}).

Let us examine these two scenarios:

Case 1
. If
D
is pseudo-effective, then
t_{0}=0
. By letting
t\to 0^{+}
, we have

\mathrm{vol}(D)=\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})=0.

Case 2
. If
D
is not pseudo-effective, then by
[
GT16
]
*Lemma 4.1, for a general closed point
s\in\Spec R
,
D_{\overline{s}}
is not pseudo-effective. Therefore, there is
s\in S^{\prime}
such that
D_{\overline{s}}
is not pseudo-effective, and thus

\mathrm{vol}(D)=\inf_{s\in S^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})=0.

From now on we may assume
D
is big, and we fix a sufficiently ample divisor
H_{R}
on
X_{R}
so that both
H_{R}\pm D_{R}
are ample on
X_{R}
. Let us define
H:=H_{R}\times_{R}\mathbb{C}
. Consider any ring extension
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
where
R^{\prime}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra. For any closed point
s^{\prime}\in\Spec R^{\prime}
with its image
s
in
\Spec R
, it holds that
X_{\overline{s}}=X_{\overline{s^{\prime}}}
. Therefore, if required, we may replace
R
with
R^{\prime}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider
f:X^{\prime}\to X
, which serves as a Fujita approximation for
D
(see Theorem
4.1
). This approximation satisfies
f^{*}D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A+E
, where
X^{\prime}
is smooth,
E\geq 0
, and
A
is ample. Further, it holds that

\mathrm{vol}(A)\geq\mathrm{vol}(D)-\varepsilon.

By shrinking
R
, we can assume the existence of models
f_{R}:X^{\prime}_{R}\to X_{R}
,
A_{R}
, and
E_{R}
corresponding to
f:X^{\prime}\to X
,
A
, and
E
over
R
, respectively (refer to Remark
2.2
). Additionally, we can assume that

f^{*}_{R}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A_{R}+E_{R}.

Define
d
as
\dim X
. According to
[
BPDD13
]
*4.1 Theorem or
[
Laz04b
]
*Theorem 11.4.21, there is a universal constant
C>0
such that

(A^{d-1}\cdot E)^{2}\leq C\cdot(H^{d})\cdot(\mathrm{vol}(D)-\mathrm{vol}(A))\leq C\varepsilon\cdot(H^{d}),

and define
C^{\prime}:=\sqrt{C\cdot(H^{d})}
. Note that
C^{\prime}
depends only on
H
and
A^{d-1}\cdot E
.

Observe that

A^{d-1}\cdot(A+E)\leq A^{d}+C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Utilizing Lemma
2.3
, it follows that

(4.1)

A^{d-1}_{\overline{s}}\cdot(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}})\leq A^{d}_{\overline{s}}+C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}

for every general closed point
s\in\Spec R
.

Note
A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}_{\overline{s}}D_{\overline{s}}
for every general
s\in\Spec R
. Fix a closed point
s\in S^{\prime}
and choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. By the Fujita approximation theorem (cf. Theorem
4.1
), we may choose a projective birational morphism
g:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}
such that

g^{*}f^{*}_{\overline{s}}D_{\overline{s}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}+E^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},

where
E^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}\geq 0
,
A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}
is ample, and

\mathrm{vol}(A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}})\geq\mathrm{vol}(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}})-\varepsilon^{\prime}=\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

We have

(4.2)

(g^{*}A_{\overline{s}})^{d-1}\cdot A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}\leq(g^{*}A_{\overline{s}})^{d-1}\cdot(A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}+E^{\prime}_{\overline{s}})=A^{d-1}_{\overline{s}}\cdot(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}}).

If we apply the generalized inequality of Hodge type (cf.
[
Laz04a
]
*Theorem 1.6.1 and Remark 1.6.5), we have

(4.3)

(A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\left(A^{{}^{\prime}d}_{\overline{s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq(g^{*}A_{\overline{s}})^{d-1}\cdot A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}.

Now, combine (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we have

(A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\left(A^{{}^{\prime}d}_{\overline{s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq(g^{*}A_{\overline{s}})^{d-1}\cdot A^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}\leq A^{d-1}_{\overline{s}}\cdot(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}})\leq(A_{\overline{s}}+E_{\overline{s}})\leq A^{d}_{\overline{s}}+C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Thus,

A^{{}^{\prime}d}_{\overline{s}}\leq\left((A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{1}{d}}+\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{(A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\right)^{d}.

By the Serre’s vanishing theorem and Lemma
2.3
,

\mathrm{vol}(A)=A^{d}=A^{d}_{\overline{s}}=\mathrm{vol}(A_{\overline{s}})\text{ for every general }s\in\Spec R.

Moreover,
A^{{}^{\prime}d}_{\overline{s}}\geq\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})-\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})

\displaystyle\leq A^{{}^{\prime}d}_{\overline{s}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left((A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{1}{d}}+\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{(A^{d}_{\overline{s}})^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\right)^{d}+\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathrm{vol}(A)^{\frac{1}{d}}+\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{vol}(A)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\right)^{d}+\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathrm{vol}(D)^{\frac{1}{d}}+\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{(\mathrm{vol}(D)-\varepsilon)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\right)^{d}+\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}>0
, there exists
s\in S^{\prime}
such that

\mathrm{vol}(D_{\overline{s}})\leq\mathrm{vol}(D)+\varepsilon^{\prime\prime},

and this is the theorem we want to prove.
∎

Logarithmic matching between past infinity and future infinity: The massless scalar field
Oscar Fuentealba, and Marc Henneaux

Abstract
Matching conditions relating the fields at the future of past null infinity with the fields at the past of future null infinity play a central role in the analysis of asymptotic symmetries and conservation laws in asymptotically flat spacetimes. These matching conditions can be derived from initial data given on a Cauchy hypersurface by integrating forward and backward in time the field equations to leading order in an asymptotic expansion, all the way to future and past null infinities. The standard matching conditions considered in the literature are valid only in the case when the expansion near null infinity (which is generically polylogarithmic) has no dominant logarithmic term. The absence of dominant logarithmic term, in turn, holds only when the leading order of the initial conditions on a Cauchy hypersurface (which contains no logarithm) fulfills definite parity conditions under the antipodal map of the sphere at infinity. One can consistently consider opposite parity conditions. While these do not conflict with the asymptotic symmetry group, they lead to a very different asymptotic behaviour near null infinity, where the expansion starts now with logarithmic terms that are no longer subdominant (even though such logarithmic terms are absent in the initial data), which implies different matching conditions. It turns out that many of the analytic features relevant to gravity are already present for massless spin zero and spin one fields. This paper is the first in a series in which we derive the matching conditions for a massless scalar field with initial conditions leading to logarithms at null infinity. We prove that these involve the opposite sign with respect to the usual matching conditions. We also analyse the matching of the angle-dependent conserved charges that follow from the asymptotic decay and Lorentz invariance. We show in particular that these are well defined and finite at null infinity even in the presence of leading logarithmic terms provided one uses the correct definitions. The free massless scalar field has the virtue of presenting the polylogarithmic features in a particularly clear setting that shows their inevitability, since there is no subtle gauge fixing issue or nonlinear intrincacies involved in the problem. We also consider the case of higher spacetime dimensions where fractional powers of r r (odd spacetime dimensions) or subdominant logarithmic terms (even spacetime dimensions) are present. Mixed matching conditions are then relevant. In subsequent papers, we will extend the analysis to the electromagnetic and the gravitational fields.

Another question, is however still open and is the one on which this paper focuses. It is the question of matching past null infinity with future null infinity.

As we recalled, the asymptotic symmetry groups were derived first at future null infinity. A similar analysis holds for past null infinity. Since there is only one spacetime, the question arises then as to how to glue the transformations at future null infinity with those at past null infinity. The purpose of this paper is to generalize the matching conditions to cover this dominant logarithmic case, leading to what we call “logarithmic matching between past infinity and future infinity". We consider first the case of a free massless scalar field, for which the question of the matching conditions for the fields raises the same types of problems as for the spin-

One key ingredient in the analysis is to determine the behaviour of the Klein-Gordon solutions near null infinity for general initial conditions on a Cauchy hypersurface (and in particular in the vicinity of the critial sets where null infinity and spatial infinity "touch"). The general solution for arbitrary initial data was given in

The analysis of the charges in that latter case is more subtle because the expression for the charge at null infinity is then different from the expression valid in the absence of such leading logarithmic terms, which, if used, would lead to incorrect conclusions. The correct expressions can be derived by carefully following the Hamiltonian expressions from the initial Cauchy hypersurface all the way to null infinity.

We also extend the analysis to higher dimensions, where the general solutions to the equations of motion can be found in

We discuss also the behaviour of the charges and find again no problem at null infinity for generic initial conditions if one adopts the correct definitions of the charges. This analysis is particularly instructive not only because it indicates why the charges, which are obviously well-defined at spatial infinity, remain well-defined as we go to the critical sets in spite of the non-analytical terms that emerge there, but also because it shows how to extract the charges from the intricate expressions of the fields at null infinity.

In subsequent papers, we shall cover the matching conditions and the behaviour of the charges at null infinity for Maxwell and Einstein theories. We shall pay special attention to the matching of the gauge potential and the metric components (and not just the field strength or Weyl tensor), which involve the important Goldstone bosons associated with the breaking of the angle-dependent asymptotic symmetries. Since the gauge potential and the metric are not invariant under gauge transformations, this is a problem that must be handled with care and actually needs convenient choices of the gauge fixing conditions to get results where the matching is transparent.

2.2
Parity conditions

It turns out that the decay (
2
)-(
4
) does not guarantee that the action

S[\phi,\pi]=\int dt\int d^{3}x\left[\pi\dot{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi^{2}+(\partial_{k}\phi)^{2}\right)\right]

Standard boundary conditions

One possibility is to impose that

\phi

\pi

is odd. This makes the kinetic term finite since the coefficient of the logarithmic term vanishes. It is the standard choice developed in

Unusual boundary conditions

An unusual but equally consistent choice is to impose that

\pi

Both choices, which lead to very distinct behaviours near null infinity, are compatible with Poincaré invariance, and both choices will be pursued in our analysis.

Q
-branch is odd,

\phi^{(0)}_{P}(-s,-x^{A})=\phi^{(0)}_{P}(s,x^{A})\,,\qquad\phi^{(0)}_{Q}(-s,-x^{A})=-\phi^{(0)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})\,.

(24)

Case
k>0

The solutions for
k>0
are also explicitly given in
[
23
]
and recalled in this subsection.

When
l\geq k
they can be expressed in terms of Gegenbauer’s (or ultraspherical) polynomials
P^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
and Gegenbauer (or ultraspherical) functions of the second kind
Q^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
, where we have set
\lambda=k+\frac{1}{2}
and
n=l-k
to match the standard conventions. The behaviour of
P^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
and
Q^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
near the pole
s=1
of the differential equation is, to leading order,

P^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)\sim\begin{pmatrix}n+2\lambda-1\\
n\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad Q^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)\sim\frac{(1-s^{2})^{-\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}}{2\lambda-1}=\frac{(1-s^{2})^{-k}}{2\lambda-1}\,,

(25)

(
n\geq 0
,
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}
). While the next terms in the polynomials
P^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
are smooth and go to zero as
s\rightarrow 1
, the next terms in
Q^{(\lambda)}_{n}(s)
involve subdominant (with respect to
(1-s^{2})^{-k}
) logarithmic terms. For instance,

Q^{(\frac{3}{2})}_{0}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{s}{1-s^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\log\left(\frac{1+s}{1-s}\right)\,.

The ultraspherical solutions obey also definite parity properties,

P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(-s)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)\,,\qquad Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(-s)=-(-1)^{n}Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)\,.

(26)

In terms of the Gegenbauer polynomials and functions of the second kind, the
k>0
solutions can be expressed as:

\phi^{(k)}(s,x^{A})=\phi^{(k)}_{P}(s,x^{A})+\phi^{(k)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})\,,

(27)

where the
P
-branch and
Q
-branch are respectively given by

\phi^{(k)}_{P}(s,x^{A})=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(28)

and

\phi^{(k)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(29)

with

\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)=(1-s^{2})^{k}P^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}(s)\,,\qquad\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)=(1-s^{2})^{k}Q^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}_{n}(s)\,.

(30)

The
P
-branch is associated with the root
\alpha=k
while the
Q
-branch is associated with the root
\alpha=0
. One has

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)=0\,,\qquad\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)=\frac{1}{2k}\,,

(31)

and

\phi^{(k)}_{P}(-s,-x^{A})=(-1)^{k}\phi^{(k)}_{P}(s,x^{A})\,,\quad\phi^{(k)}_{Q}(-s,-x^{A})=-(-1)^{k}\phi^{(k)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})\,.

(32)

We stress again, to emphasize their inevitability, that the
Q
-branch involves for
k>0
subdominant logarithmic terms, such as
(1-s)\log(1-s)
.

When
l<k
(
n<0
), one gets similar expressions where both
\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
are now polynomials, which have been chosen to obey the parity conditions (
26
). The differential equation has indeed only polynomials solutions. Being polynomials, these solutions have some finite values as
s\rightarrow 1
, which are non-zero
[
23
]
.

Instead of the set
\{\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s),\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)\}
which has definite parity properties, one could alternatively take
\{\overline{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s),\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)\}
as set of independent solutions (when
n<0
), where

\overline{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
is the linear combination of
\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
that vanishes at
s=1
. It does so as
(1-s)^{k}
, corresponding to the indicial root
\alpha=k
. The polynomials
\overline{P}_{n}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
do not have definite parity properties. The
\overline{P}
-terms in the asymptotic expansion will be subdominant at null infinity with respect to the
Q
-terms.

3.1.2
Friedrich coordinates - Bondi coordinates

While useful for integrating the equations of motion for initial data given in a
\frac{1}{\eta}
expansion, the hyperbolic coordinates badly cover null infinity. Indeed, as one tends to null infinity along a radial null geodesics, e.g., for
\mathscr{I^{+}}
,

t=r+b\qquad(b<0)

(33)

one finds that for
r\rightarrow\infty
,
s
and
\eta
always behave as
s\rightarrow 1
and
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, no matter what the null geodesic is. The limiting values of
(s,\eta)
are always
(1,\infty)
so that the information about the null geodesic (i.e.,
b
) and where it reaches
\mathscr{I^{+}}
is lost. This suggests to go to new coordinates that do not suffer from this limitation.

These coordinates were introduced by Friedrich
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
and are well reviewed in
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
. They are described in detail in Appendix
A
.

In Friedrich coordinates, the scalar field reads

\phi=\phi_{P}+\phi_{Q}\,,

(34)

with

\phi_{P}=(1-s^{2})\sum_{k\geq 0,l\geq 0,|m|\leq l}\rho^{-1-k}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(35)

and

\phi_{Q}=(1-s^{2})\sum_{k\geq 0,l\geq 0,|m|\leq l}\rho^{-1-k}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,.

(36)

Since null infinity corresponds to taking the limit
s\rightarrow\pm 1
while keeping
\rho
fixed, and since all the
\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
and
\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(s)
are bounded in that limit except
\widetilde{Q}_{l}^{\frac{1}{2})}(s)
which diverges only logarithmically, we see that the scalar field goes to zero as we go to null infinity, as expected (for
k=0
,
(1-s)\log(1-s)\rightarrow 0
).

We then proceed to Bondi coordinates
(u,r)
, related to
(s,\rho)
as follows,

s=1+\frac{u}{r}\,,\quad\rho=-2u-\frac{u^{2}}{r}\,,\quad 1-s^{2}=-2\,\frac{u}{r}-\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\,.

(37)

Here,
u<0
since we are interested in the limit of going to the past of future null infinity.

Writing the solution
\phi
in Bondi coordinates, we get

\displaystyle\phi_{P}=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}Y_{lm}(x^{A})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0}\sum_{l<k}\sum_{|m|\leq l}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,\,\qquad

(38)

since
P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)=1
and
\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)=0
for
l\geq k
(
k>0
), as well as

\displaystyle\phi_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})\right)\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}\left(P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1+\frac{u}{r})+R_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)\right)Y_{lm}(x^{A})

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

where we have expressed
Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}
in terms of
Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}
through (
22
) in order to display more transparently the asymptotic behaviour.

Let us analyse first the
P
-branch. All values of
k\geq 0
contribute at order
1/r
at null infinity, even though they correspond to terms of order
1/r^{k+1}
at spatial infinity. However, as we go to the past of future null infinity, these
1/r
contributions goes to zero like
(-2u)^{-k}
. Therefore, only the
k=0
contribution survives at
\mathscr{I^{+}_{-}}
. This
k=0
contribution is

\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}Y_{lm}(x^{A})

and in fact it does not depend on
u
. It defines an infinite number of angle-dependent conserved quantities, which will be connected to the conserved quantities defined at spatial infinity in Subsection
4
. We can rewrite the
P
-branch at null infinity as

\phi_{P}=\frac{\Phi(u,x^{A})}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(40)

with

\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}\Phi(u,x^{A})=\Phi(x^{A})=\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(41)

involving only the
k=0
contribution.

To analyse the
Q
-branch, we expand
Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(s)=Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1+u/r)=(1/2)\big(\log(r/u)+\log(2+u/r)\big)
(see (
22
)) as

Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1+u/r)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\log r-\log(-u)+\log 2\Big)+\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})\,,

(42)

and use explicitly
P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)=1
to get

\displaystyle\phi_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}Y_{lm}(x^{A})

(43)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\log(-u)+\log 2\Big)+R_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)\right)Y_{lm}(x^{A})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

There is now a leading
\frac{\log r}{r}
contribution, originating entirely from the
k=0
term. It does not depend on
u
and defines also an infinite number of angle-dependent conserved quantities. There are then
\frac{1}{r}
contributions originating from all values of
k
, but only the
k=0
term contributes in the limit
u\rightarrow-\infty
and involves a
\log(-u)
divergence. This divergence in the coefficient of the
1/r
term goes hand in hand with the coefficient of the
\frac{\log r}{r}
-term (they are present or absent simultaneously). Finally there are subdominant polylogarithmic terms originating from all values of
k
.

The presence of coefficients that diverge as
u\rightarrow-\infty
might seem disturbing but it should be recalled that the Bondi coordinates cover badly the past of future null infinity, which is missed, in fact, by taking the limit
u\rightarrow-\infty
while keeping
r
fixed. This limit is equivalent to keeping
r
fixed and taking
t\rightarrow-\infty
, and amounts thus to go to past infinity, which is outside the scope of this paper. To study the limit at the critical sets, which is the object of this work, one must use the Friedrich coordinates to conclude that
\phi
unambiguously goes to zero there, even if it does involve non analytical terms.

To parallel the expression (
41
) for the
P
-branch, we can rewrite the
Q
-branch at null infinity as

\phi_{Q}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\Psi(u,x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(44)

where

\Psi(u,x^{A})=\Psi(x^{A})=\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(45)

is
u
-independent and involves only the
k=0
contribution.

3.2
Connection with Hamiltonian conserved quantities

We can easily connect the conserved quantities exhibited at null infinity to the conserved quantities found at spatial infinity because we have at hand the form of the general solution. One simply sets
s=0
in the general solution expressed in hyperbolic coordinates since
s=0
is equivalent to
t=0
.

3.2.1
P
-branch

This yields for the
P
-branch, noting that
\pi\equiv\dot{\phi}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{s}\phi
on
t=0
,

\displaystyle\phi_{P}(t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})

(46)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+1}\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2}\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\,,

with

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(47)

and, for

\pi

(in Cartesian coordinates)

\displaystyle\pi_{P}(t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})

(48)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+1}\frac{1}{r^{k+2}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\partial_{s}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2}\frac{1}{r^{k+2}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\partial_{s}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\,,\qquad

with

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}\partial_{s}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(49)

where we have explicitly used the fact that due to the parity properties of the ultraspherical polynomials,
\widetilde{P}_{n}(s=0)
vanishes for
n
odd, while
\partial_{s}\widetilde{P}_{n}(s=0)
vanishes for
n
even.

We thus see that on Cauchy hypersurfaces, the coefficient

\phi

of the leading (
1/r
) order of the scalar field is even under the sphere antipodal map for the
P
-branch, while the coefficient

\pi

of the leading (
1/r^{2}
) order of its conjugate momentum is odd. These angle-dependent conserved quantities are completely determined by – and completely determine in turn – the coefficients
\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}
. Their knowledge is therefore equivalent to (
41
).

3.2.2
Q
-branch

For the
Q
-branch, we get similar expressions,

\displaystyle\phi_{Q}(t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})

(50)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+1}\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2}\frac{1}{r^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\,,\qquad

with

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(51)

and

\displaystyle\pi_{Q}(t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})

(52)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+1}\frac{1}{r^{k+2}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\partial_{s}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}+2}\frac{1}{r^{k+2}}\left(\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\partial_{s}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\,,\qquad

with

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}\partial_{s}Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(53)

where this time,
\widetilde{Q}_{n}(s=0)
vanishes for
n
even, while
\partial_{s}\widetilde{Q}_{n}(s=0)
vanishes for
n
odd.
Note that there is
no
logarithm in the expansion of the
Q
-branch on Cauchy hypersurfaces but that the coefficient

\phi

of the leading (
1/r
) order of the scalar field is now odd under the antipodal map for the
Q
-branch, while the coefficient

\pi

of the leading (
1/r^{2}
) order of its conjugate momentum is now even. The apparently benign change in the parity conditions of the initial data that does not affect the
1/r
dependence has dramatic consequences in the behaviour at null infinity as it controls the appearance of the
\log r
terms..

As for the
P
-branch, these angle-dependent conserved quantities

\phi

and

\pi

are completely determined by – and completely determine – the coefficients
\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}
. Their knowledge is therefore equivalent to (
45
).

3.2.3
Conserved charges and parity conditions

We now impose the parity conditions on the leading orders of the field and its conjugate momentum, necessary to have a well-defined canonical formulation.

Standard boundary conditions

In the standard case, one imposes that

\phi

is even under the antipodal map
x^{A}\rightarrow-x^{A}
on the sphere, while

\pi

is odd. This forces
\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}=0
and selects the
P
branch for the leading order. There is therefore no leading logarithmic term at infinity and
\Psi(x^{A})=0
.

The charge
\Phi(x^{A})
of Eq (
41
) can be expressed in terms of the initial data on the Cauchy hypersurface
t=0
as

\Phi(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}}{P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}}{\partial_{s}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(54)

where
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}
are respectively the coefficients of
Y_{lm}(x^{A})
in the spherical harmonic expansion of

\phi

and

\pi

.
We thus see that both the even function

\phi

and the odd function

\pi

combine to form a single conserved function
\Phi(x^{A})
of the angles, with no definite parity under the antipodal map at future null infinity.

The general solution compatible with the standard parity conditions considered in this subsection reads, at null infinity,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\Phi(x^{A})+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l<k,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})

(55)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)

from which we infer three important points:

•

While the quantities

\phi

and

\pi

are conserved as we progress on spacelike Cauchy hypersurfaces, the function
\Phi(u,x^{A})
to which they connect at null infinity is not conserved along null infinity since
\Phi
depends on
u
through the subleading contributions from the
P
and
Q
branches in (
55
), which need not be set to zero. These represent the radiation that “escape" through null infinity. It is given by a mixture of
P
and
Q
modes.

•

While there is no leading logarithmic term in the development (
55
), there are subleading ones coming from the (
k>0
,
n\geq 0
)-terms in the expansion of the
Q
-branch. These terms are unavoidable unless we impose, on top of the condition
\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}=0
, the somewhat artificial requirement
\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}=0
for
k>0
,
n\geq 0
.

•

The expansion (
55
) coincides to leading order with the ones generally considered in the literature (see for instance
[
62
]
) but differs from them already at order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)
. From the point of view of general initial conditions on Cauchy hypersurfaces, these other expansions are therefore somewhat limited.

Unusual boundary conditions

We now impose the opposite parity conditions that

\phi

is odd under the antipodal map
x^{A}\rightarrow-x^{A}
on the sphere, while

\pi

is even. This forces
\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}=0
and selects the
Q
branch for the leading order. There is therefore a leading logarithmic term at infinity and
\Psi(x^{A})\not=0
. While this term is generally avoided by most authors at null infinity, it leads to no particular complication at spatial infinity and, as stressed in
[
23
]
, a perfectly consistent Hamiltonian description can be provided with these opposite parity conditions.

We can write
\Psi(x^{A})
of Eq (
45
) in terms of the initial data on the Cauchy hypersurface
t=0
as

\Psi(x^{A})=\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N}+1,m}\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}}{Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\sum_{l\in 2\mathbb{N},m}\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}}{\partial_{s}Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})

(56)

where
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{lm}
are again the coefficients of
Y_{lm}(x^{A})
in the spherical harmonic expansion of

\phi

and

\pi

, respectively.
Both the odd function

\phi

and the even function

\pi

combine to form a single conserved function
\Psi(x^{A})
of the angles, with no definite parity under the antipodal map.

The general solution compatible with the non-standard parity conditions considered in this subsection reads, at null infinity,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\Psi(x^{A})

(57)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\log(-u)+\log 2\Big)+R_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1)\right)Y_{lm}(x^{A})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l<k,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{P(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k>0,l,m}\frac{1}{(-2u)^{k}}\Theta^{Q(k)}_{lm}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

The coefficient
\Psi(x^{A})
of the leading term is this time absolutely conserved all along null infinity since there is no other
\frac{\log r}{r}

64

3.3
More on the conserved charges

It is instructive to track more precisely the behavior of the conserved quantities as we go to null infinity.

The conserved quantities (
7
) are defined on the Cauchy hypersurfaces of constant
t
. How are we going to define them in the hyperbolic coordinates? Since
s=0
coincide with the Cauchy hypersurface
t=0
, and since

\partial_{s}\phi\Bigr\rvert_{t=0}=\frac{\partial t}{\partial s}\Bigr\rvert_{t=0}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\Bigr\rvert_{t=0}\Rightarrow\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}=\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}

(58)

(with
\phi^{(0)}
the coefficient of the
\eta^{-1}
-term in the expansion in hyperbolic coordinates), one can rewrite
Q
at
s=0
as

Q[\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}]\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}=\oint(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}\partial_{s}{\phi^{(0)}}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}\,\phi^{(0)})\sqrt{\gamma}d^{2}x\Biggr\rvert_{s=0}\,.

(59)

If we then introduce a function
\epsilon^{(0)}(s,x^{A})
such that
\epsilon^{(0)}\Bigr\rvert_{t=0}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}
and
\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}\Bigr\rvert_{t=0}=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\zeta$\kern 0.0pt}}}
, the conserved quantities become

Q[\epsilon^{(0)}]\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}=\oint(\epsilon^{(0)}\partial_{s}{\phi^{(0)}}-\partial_{s}{\epsilon^{(0)}}\,\phi^{(0)})\sqrt{\gamma}d^{2}x\Biggr\rvert_{s=0}\,.

(60)

This rewriting of the conserved quantity suggests how one should define
Q
for other values of
s
in the hyperbolic slicing. Since the slices
|s|<1
are spacelike allowing no flux at infinity, we naturally request
\frac{dQ}{ds}=0
, which amounts to adopt the same expression (
60
) for all
s

Q[\epsilon^{(0)}]=\oint(\epsilon^{(0)}\partial_{s}{\phi^{(0)}}-\partial_{s}{\epsilon^{(0)}}\,\phi^{(0)})\sqrt{\gamma}d^{2}x\,,

(61)

and require that
\epsilon^{(0)}
obeys the same equation as
\phi^{(0)}

(1-s^{2})\partial^{2}_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\epsilon^{(0)}+\frac{1}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon^{(0)}=0\,.

(62)

The requirement that
\frac{dQ}{ds}=0
is very natural. If one could view the conserved charges as generators of a symmetry, one could justify their form from the knowledge of the transformations that they must generate. This independent justification is not available here, but is available in the electromagnetic and gravitational cases, where the charges (completed by bulk terms proportional to the constraints) have a well-defined action as generators of the asymptotic symmetry.

By the same derivations as the ones followed for
\phi^{(0)}
, we then get

(1-s^{2})\partial^{2}_{s}\Xi_{lm}^{(0)}-2s\partial_{s}\Xi_{lm}^{(0)}+l(l+1))\Xi_{lm}^{(0)}=0\,,

(63)

with

\epsilon^{(0)}(s,x^{A})=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{l,m}\Xi^{(0)}_{lm}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(64)

leading to

\epsilon^{(0)}(s,x^{A})=\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}(s,x^{A})+\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})\,,

(65)

with

\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}(s,x^{A})=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{l,m}\Xi^{P(0)}_{lm}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,,

(66)

and

\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}(s,x^{A})=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{l,m}\Xi^{Q(0)}_{lm}Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}(x^{A})\,.

(67)

If we plug these expressions in the formula (
61
) giving the charge, we find four terms,

Q=Q_{P,P}+Q_{P,Q}+Q_{Q,P}+Q_{Q,Q}

(68)

with

\displaystyle Q_{P,P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{P}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}\phi^{(0)}_{P}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,,

(69)

\displaystyle Q_{P,Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{Q}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}_{P}\phi^{(0)}_{Q}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,,

(70)

\displaystyle Q_{Q,P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{P}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\phi^{(0)}_{P}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,,

(71)

\displaystyle Q_{Q,Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{Q}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\phi^{(0)}_{Q}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,.

(72)

It is easy to check by direct computation that both
Q_{P,P}
and
Q_{Q,Q}
vanish
in the limit

due to cancellations between the two terms in the integral, which have the same structure. Only
Q_{P,Q}
and
Q_{Q,P}
can be different from zero.

Standard boundary conditions

The integral
Q_{Q,P}
gives the charge relevant for the standard boundary conditions characterized by
\phi_{Q}^{(0)}=0
(pure
P
-branch). One might fear that it diverges at the critical set
s\rightarrow 1
because of the logarithm present in the Legendre function of the second kind:

\lim_{s->1}Q_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-s)+q_{l}^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}(1-s)\,.

(73)

However one can verify that the logarithms cancel each other between
\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{P}
and
\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}_{Q}\phi^{(0)}_{P}
. The remaining finite
part that comes from
\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}_{P}
is
zero at
s=1
, while the finite part from
-\partial_{s}\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\phi^{(0)}_{P}

does not vanish at
s=1
because

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}(1-s^{2})\partial_{s}Q=1
.
Hence, we derived the relation:

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}Q_{Q,P}=-\oint\left(\sum_{l,m}\Xi_{lm}^{Q(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\left(\sum_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Theta_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{P(0)}Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(x^{A})\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,.

(74)

Non-standard boundary conditions

For the non-standard boundary conditions
\phi_{P}^{(0)}=0
(pure
Q
-branch), it is

that gives the charge.

The expression for
Q_{P,Q}
is identical to (minus) that for

with the roles of
\epsilon^{(0)}
and
\phi^{()}
exchanged, so that we can immediately infer

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}Q=\oint\left(\sum_{l,m}\Xi_{lm}^{P(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})\right)\left(\sum_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Theta_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{Q(0)}Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(x^{A})\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{2}x\,.

(75)

The charges are well defined in the limit
s\rightarrow 1
for both the
P
-branch and the
Q
-branch, as it should since they are conserved. The mechanism by which finiteness is maintained through compensations between
\epsilon
and
\phi
is similar to the one discussed in
[
46
]
. The expression for the charges also exactly picks up the coefficient
\Phi(x^{A})
of (
41

term at null infinity for the
P
-branch and the coefficient
\Psi(x^{A})
of (
45

\log r/r
term for the
Q
-branch.

To mimic the situation valid for electromagnetism and gravity, one can write the charge for the
P
-branch as a flux at null infinity

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\oint_{S_{r}}\lambda(x^{A})\frac{\phi}{r}(r^{2}\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}})d^{2}x\qquad(\phi^{(0)}_{Q}=0)\,,

where
\lambda
is the function of the angles given by
-\sum_{l,m}\Xi_{lm}^{Q(0)}Y_{lm}(x^{A})
. One cannot write the charge for the
Q
-branch as a similar flux
\lim_{r\rightarrow}\oint_{S_{r}}\lambda^{\prime}(x^{A})\frac{\phi}{r}(r^{2}\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}})d^{2}x
at null infinity because of the
\log r

Q
-branch does not mean that the
Q
-branch is pathological from the point of view of the charges, but rather, that this naive flux expression is incorrect. A direct and independent derivation of the charges at null infinity by symplectic techniques, taking into account that the induced symplectic form at null infinity involves also
\log r
, is not available here because the charges are not true generators. This is the reason that we defined them at null infinity from their Hamiltonian expressions at spatial infinity, which take the same uniform form for the two branches, and followed them to null infinity, where the expressions now strikingly differ.

4
Matching conditions

To derive the matching conditions of the leading orders of the scalar field between the past of future null infinity and the future of past null infinity, we expand the general solution of the Klein-Gordon equation obtained above in advanced Bondi coordinates
(v,r,x^{A})
and examine its behavior as
v\rightarrow+\infty
. The procedure is identical with that followed for future null infinity and we directly go to the results.

4.1
Standard matching conditions

If one imposes the standard parity conditions on the leading order of the fields, one finds that the solution near past null infinity reads

\phi=\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(76)

with

\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})=\Phi^{\prime}(x^{A})=\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(-1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})=\Phi(-x^{A})\,.

(77)

We have thus derived

\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})=\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}\Phi(u,-x^{A})\,,

(78)

where
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
are the coefficients of the leading
\frac{1}{r}
order in the expansion of the scalar field near future null infinity and past null infinity, respectively.

The matching given by Eq (
78
) is the scalar analog of the matching conditions assumed in
[
45
]
. We have shown that these matching conditions are consequences of our conditions on the initial data: parity conditions on the initial conditions on
t=0
under the antipodal map
x^{A}\rightarrow-x^{A}
imply matching conditions relating the future of past null infinity and the past of future null infinity (
s\rightarrow-s
,
x^{A}\rightarrow-x^{A}
). Note that the matching (
78
) holds only in the limit (and at leading
\frac{1}{r}
-order) since
\Phi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})
is not the antipodal mirror image of
\Phi(u,x^{A})
due to the presence of both
P
and
Q
terms in (
55
).

That the standard matching conditions are consequences of the standard parity-restricted initial data has been derived first in

[
21
,
22
]
(see also
[
65
,
66
]
).

We can phrase the matching conditions from the point of view of null infinity as follows. Assume that one directly solves the Klein-Gordon equation near future null infinity by adopting an expansion of the scalar field of the form

\frac{\Phi(u,x^{A})}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

without trying to connect the solution to initial data on Cauchy hypersurfaces. Assume then that one achieves the same task independently near past null infinity with an expansion

\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

How should the solutions near future, respectively past, infinity be related if they are to represent the same solution? The answer is that they must be matched as in (
78
) for the leading order (the charge). The initial data corresponding to such a behaviour are furthermore such that

\phi

is even and

\pi

is odd.

4.2
Logarithmic matching conditions

One can equally impose the opposite, non standard parity conditions on the leading orders of the initial data. In that case, the solution near past null infinity reads

\phi=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\Psi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(79)

where

\Psi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})=\Psi^{\prime}(x^{A})=-\sum_{l,m}\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}P_{l}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(-1)Y_{lm}(x^{A})=-\Psi(-x^{A})\,,

(80)

since
Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(s)=Q_{0}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(-1+v/r)=-\frac{1}{2}\log r+\mathcal{O}(1)
.
For the unusual parity conditions, we have thus established

\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\Psi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})=-\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}\Psi(u,-x^{A})\,,

(81)

where
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\prime}
are the coefficients of the leading
\frac{\log r}{r}
order in the expansion of the scalar field near future null infinity and past null infinity, respectively.
The matching of the leading order of the scalar field critically involves now a minus sign
3
3

3

Because the leading
\mathcal{O}(\log r/r)
terms do not depend on
v
or
u
, the matching actually holds for all values of the advanced and retarded times.
.
Opposite parity properties at spatial infinity lead to opposite matching conditions between past null infinity and future null infinity for the leading orders of the field (now at
\mathcal{O}(\log r/r)
rather than
\mathcal{O}(1/r)
).

We can again phrase the matching conditions from the point of view of null infinity. Assume this time that one directly solves the Klein-Gordon equation near future null infinity by adopting an expansion of the scalar field of the form

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\Psi(u,x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

and independently near past null infinity with an expansion

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log r}{r}\Psi^{\prime}(v,x^{A})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,.

How should the solutions near future, respectively past, infinity be related if they are to represent the same solution? The answer is that they must now be matched as in (
81
) for the leading order (the Newman-Penrose charge). The initial data are furthermore such that

\phi

is odd and

\pi

is even.

Of course, since we have derived the general solution near future infinity and past infinty to all orders, one can establish from it the matching conditions for the subsequent terms which take, however, a more complicated form because these subleading terms do not have a definite parity. For relating the fields at the future and past critical sets, the use of the solutions
\widetilde{P}^{\lambda)}_{n}
rather than
\overline{P}^{\lambda)}_{n}

5
Higher dimensions

In higher spacetime dimensions
D
, the scalar field and its conjugate momentum decay at spatial infinity as (in Cartesian coordinates)

\phi=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}{r^{D-3}}+\frac{\phi^{(D-2)}}{r^{D-2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{D-1})\,,

\pi=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}{r^{D-2}}+\frac{\pi^{(D-1)}}{r^{D-1}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{D})\,.

This decay is motivated by the behaviour of the elementary solution to the Laplace equation.

As in
D=4

P
-branch, which is even under the hyperboloid antipodal map

x^{A}\rightarrow-x^{A}

4

The main new feature with respect to

\int d^{D-1}\pi\dot{\phi}\sim\int drr^{D-2}r^{-D+3}r^{-D+2}\sim\int drr^{-D+3}\,,

\phi_{Q}=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{l,m}\rho^{-(k+D-3)}\Theta_{lm}^{Q(k)}\widetilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}\,,

For later convenience, we split the

\displaystyle\phi_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq k,m}\rho^{-(k+D-3)}(1-s^{2})^{(k+D-3)}\Theta_{lm}^{P(k)}{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}

(104)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k>0}\sum_{l<k,m}\rho^{-(k+D-3)}(1-s^{2})^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\Theta_{lm}^{P(k)}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}

\displaystyle\phi_{P}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq k,m}r^{-(k+D-3)}\Theta_{lm}^{P(k)}P_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{D-3}{2})}(1+\frac{u}{r})Y_{lm}

(105)

Here,

f^{(k,D)}(u,r)=r^{-\frac{D-2}{2}}(-2u)^{-\left(k+\frac{D-4}{2}\right)}\left(1+\frac{u}{2r}\right)^{-\left(k+\frac{D-4}{2}\right)}\,.

(107)

Since

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092ptf^{(k,D)}(u,r)=r^{-\frac{D-2}{2}}(-2u)^{-\left(k+\frac{D-4}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{4}\left(k+\frac{D-4}{2}\right)r^{-\frac{D}{2}}(-2u)^{-\left(k+\frac{D-6}{2}\right)}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-\frac{D}{2}-1}\right),

(108)

and

\tilde{Q}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{D-3}{2})}(1+\frac{u}{r})
is of order one (see Appendix

),
we see that the leading behaviour of the

(

r
in odd spacetime dimensions. There are also subdominant logarithms in even spacetime dimensions starting at order

The leading behaviour of the

Matching conditions in

\displaystyle\phi_{Q}

\displaystyle\Psi(u,x^{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k>0}\sum_{l<k,m}(-2u)^{-k}\Theta_{lm}^{P(k)}\widetilde{P}_{l-k}^{(k+\frac{3}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}

k=0

5.3
Analysis of the conserved quantities

As in four dimensions, the leading coefficients

\phi

and

\pi

in the expansion of the scalar field and its conjugate momentum at spatial infinity both define angle-dependent conserved quantities

5

5

The subleading terms can also be combined to form conserved quantities, but these are not expected to survive the introduction of interactions.
. From the expansion of the fields, one sees that the conserved quantities

\phi

and

are completely equivalent to the coefficients
\Theta^{P(0)}_{lm}
and
\Theta^{Q(0)}_{lm}
. At null infinity, these coefficients can be most easily read off from the expressions for the fields in the limit

-dependence of the charges at Scri

(much in the same way as the “initial value" of the Bondi mass is given at

Since the expressions at null infinity get more and more intrincate as one goes up in dimension, it is again interesting to follow the evolution of the charges from spatial infinity, where they take a simple and manifestly finite form, to the critical sets. This is done as in four spacetime dimensions, by going to hyperbolic coordinates and requesting that the charges be
s
-independent.

To that end, we introduce an angle dependent parameter
\epsilon^{(0)}
and consider the charge

Q[\epsilon^{(0)}]\biggr\rvert_{s=0}=\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}\phi^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\biggr\rvert_{s=0}\,.

(134)

This charge, with no bulk term, does not have a well-defined canonical action but, as in four dimensions, is useful to keep track of the conserved quantities. We stress again that on
s=0
,
\epsilon^{(0)}
and
\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}
are independent functions of the angles.

The correct expression for the charge
Q(s)
in a hyperbolic slicing, which reduces to (
134
) at
s=0
and is conserved, is
given by

Q=\oint\left(\epsilon^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}\phi^{(0)}+\frac{(D-4)s}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon^{(0)}\phi^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,.

(135)

The new feature is the additional term containing undifferentiated parameter and field. It vanishes at
s=0
. One verifies that
\frac{dQ}{ds}=0
by using the equation of motion for the scalar field

(1-s^{2})^{2}\partial_{s}^{2}\phi^{(0)}+(D-4)(1-s^{2})s\partial_{s}\phi^{(0)}-(1-s^{2})\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\phi^{(0)}-(D-3)\phi^{(0)}=0\,,

(136)

and imposing that the parameter
\epsilon^{(0)}
should obey the equation

(1-s^{2})^{2}\partial_{s}^{2}\epsilon^{(0)}-(D-4)(1-s^{2})s\partial_{s}\epsilon^{(0)}-(1-s^{2})\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\epsilon^{(0)}-\left[3-D+(D-4)(1+s^{2})\right]\epsilon^{(0)}=0\,.

(137)

If we make use of the ansatz

\epsilon^{(0)}=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{5-D}{2}}\sum_{lm}\Lambda_{lm}Y_{lm}\,,

(138)

the equation becomes

(1-s^{2})\partial_{s}^{2}\Lambda_{lm}+(D-6)s\partial_{s}\Lambda_{lm}+(l+D-4)(l+1)\Lambda_{lm}=0\,,

(139)

which is again of the ultraspherical type. The solution then reads

\Lambda_{lm}(s)=\Lambda_{lm}^{P(0)}\tilde{P}_{l}^{(\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)+\Lambda_{lm}^{Q(0)}\tilde{Q}_{l}^{(\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)\,.

(140)

We can then decompose the parameter into a
P
-branch and a
Q
-branch as

\epsilon^{(0)}=\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}+\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\,,

(141)

with

\displaystyle\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{5-D}{2}}\sum_{lm}\Lambda_{lm}^{P(0)}\tilde{P}_{l}^{(\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}\,,

(142)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(1-s^{2})^{\frac{5-D}{2}}\sum_{lm}\Lambda_{lm}^{Q(0)}\tilde{Q}_{l}^{(\frac{D-3}{2})}(s)Y_{lm}\,.

(143)

Q=Q_{PP}+Q_{PQ}+Q_{QP}+Q_{QQ}\,,

(144)

where

\displaystyle Q_{PP}

\displaystyle=\oint\left(\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi_{P}^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\phi_{P}^{(0)}+\frac{(D-4)s}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\phi_{P}^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,,

(145)

\displaystyle Q_{PQ}

\displaystyle=\oint\left(\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}+\frac{(D-4)s}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon_{P}^{(0)}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,,

(146)

\displaystyle Q_{QP}

\displaystyle=\oint\left(\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi_{P}^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\phi_{P}^{(0)}+\frac{(D-4)s}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\phi_{P}^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,,

(147)

\displaystyle Q_{QQ}

\displaystyle=\oint\left(\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\partial_{s}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}-\partial_{s}\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}+\frac{(D-4)s}{1-s^{2}}\epsilon_{Q}^{(0)}\phi_{Q}^{(0)}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,.

(148)

By direct replacement of the solutions we can not only prove that

Q_{PP}=Q_{QQ}=0\,,

(149)

but also find that the non-vanishing charges at the limit

are given
by

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}Q_{PQ}

\displaystyle=\oint\left(\sum_{lm}\Lambda_{lm}^{P(0)}P_{l}^{(\frac{D-3}{2})}(1)Y_{lm}\right)\left(\sum_{lm}\Theta_{lm}^{Q(0)}Y_{lm}\right)\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}d^{D-2}x\,,

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated the behaviour of a massless scalar field with long range initial data near the past of future null infinity and the future of past null infinity (“critical surfaces"). The adopted decay at spatial infinity was the one of the elementary solution of the Laplace equation. The behaviour found at null infinity is very different from the one that characterizes initial data with compact support and involves non-analytic functions of
1/r
, such as logarithms or fractional powers. This is because the critical surfaces correspond then to Fuchsian singularities of the differential equations controlling the asymptotic behaviour, while spatial infinity defines a regular point.

Two of the central issues that we have considered are the matching conditions and the expression of the conserved charges at the critical surfaces.

A key feature in the derivation of the matching conditions is the symmetry of the asymptotic differential equations written in hyperbolic coordinates under the reversal of the hyperbolic time
s\rightarrow-s
, which exchanges future and past. As a result, the solutions, the angle dependence of which is decomposed in spherical harmonics, split into a
P
-branch and a
Q
-branch. The
P
-branch is even under the hyperbolic antipodal map (i.e., the time reversal
s\rightarrow-s
combined with the sphere antipodal map) and the
Q
-branch is odd. On the initial hypersurface
s=0
, the hyperbolic antipodal map reduces to the sphere antipodal map. This explains why parity conditions on the initial conditions have a direct impact on the form of the matching conditions relating
s=1
and
s=-1
6
6

6

The connection between parity conditions on the leading order of the initial data and matching conditions has been rederived by different route in
[
70

, confirming the earlier results of
[
22
]
.

The non-analytic terms at null infinity originate from the
Q
-branch. We have explicitly shown that the conserved quantities remain well-defined and finite even when the
Q
-branch is turned on. These charges are however not given by expressions at null infinity that are the same as the ones for the charges related to the
P
-branch. Only the latter can be written as familiar fluxes at null infinity. The reason that the
Q
-branch non-analytic terms do not raise difficulties for the charges at null infinity is that the
Q
-branch is perfectly all right at spatial infinity (analytic in
1/r
), where it merely differs from the
P
-branch by somewhat “insignificant" (at spatial infinity) parity properties.

The consideration of long range initial data for the scalar field is motivated by electromagnetism and gravity, to which we shall turn in future papers. We shall discuss the matching conditions not only for the gauge invariant field strengths but also for quantities that are strictly invariant under proper gauge transformations but not invariant under improper ones, like the Goldstone bosons of the asymptotic symmetries. We shall also study the conserved charges with due consideration of the
Q
-branch, which complicates the structure at null infinity. The charges are more interesting in the electromagnetic and gravitational cases because they are then true canonical generators: they actually generate the improper gauge symmetries. Symplectic techniques can thus be used in their analysis. We shall also treat the higher-dimensional cases, where Hamiltonian methods have already been developed

Appendix A
Coordinate systems

Our conventions and notations are as follows.
The Minkowski metric in
D
spacetime dimensions reads

d\sigma^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-dt^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(dx^{i}\right)^{2}

(152)

(
x^{0}=t
,
c=1
) or in polar coordinates,

d\sigma^{2}=-dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\omega_{D-2}^{2}\,,

(153)

where

d\omega_{D-2}^{2}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}dx^{A}dx^{B}\,,

(154)

gives the line element on the unit round
(D-2)
-sphere. The metric coefficients
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}
depends only on the sphere coordinates
x^{A}
(
A=1,\cdots,D-2
).

A.1
Hyperbolic coordinates

The hyperbolic coordinates are defined in the region
r>|t|
by

\eta=\sqrt{-t^{2}+r^{2}},\;\;\;s=\frac{t}{r}

(155)

(angles unchanged). The Minkowski metric becomes

d\eta^{2}+\eta^{2}h_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b},\;\;\;\;(x^{a})\equiv(s,x^{A})

(156)

with

h_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}=-\frac{1}{\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{2}}ds^{2}+\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{AB}}{1-s^{2}}dx^{A}dx^{B}\,.

(157)

The inverse coordinate transformation is given by

t=\eta\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}},\quad r=\eta\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}.

(158)

A.2
Friedrich coordinates

The Friedrich are obtained by replacing
\eta
by
\rho
defined as

\rho=\eta\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0<\rho<\infty,\quad-1<s<1\,.

(159)

In these coordinates, the Minkowskian line element becomes

d\Sigma^{2}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{(1-s^{2})}{\rho^{2}}d\rho^{2}+2s\,\rho^{-1}\,ds\,d\rho-ds^{2}+d\Omega^{2}\right)

(160)

and is conformal to the metric

d\tilde{\Sigma}^{2}=\frac{(1-s^{2})}{\rho^{2}}d\rho^{2}+2s\,\rho^{-1}\,ds\,d\rho-ds^{2}+d\Omega^{2}\,.

(161)

Spatial infinity is blown up as in hyperbolic coordinates and characterized by
\rho=\infty
,
s\in(-1,1)
, with different boosted hyperplanes cutting it at different values of
s
. In that representation, spatial infinity is in fact a timelike cylinder. While still located at an infinite distance away in the rescaled metric
d\tilde{\Sigma}^{2}
, its induced metric is regular and given by

-ds^{2}+d\Omega^{2}\,.

The coordinate
s
still behaves as
s\rightarrow+1
(respectively,
-1
) as one goes to
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(respectively
\mathscr{I^{-}}
), but the new coordinate
\rho
assumes now values that encodes the information on “where” one reaches
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(respectively
\mathscr{I^{-}}
). Specifically, for
\mathscr{I^{+}}
, one finds,

\rho\rightarrow 2|b|\in(0,\infty)\,.

(162)

Thus, future null infinity
\mathscr{I^{+}}
is given by
s=1
,
\rho\in(0,\infty)
. Similarly, past null infinity
\mathscr{I^{-}}
is given by
s=-1
,
\rho\in(0,\infty)
.
The limitation mentioned in Subsection
3.1.2
has therefore been eliminated.

The rescaled metric reduces to the degenerate metric

d\Omega^{2}

on
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(
s=1
) and
\mathscr{I^{-}}
(
s=-1
). The boundaries
\rho=\infty
of
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(
s=1
) and
\mathscr{I^{-}}
(
s=1
) are denoted
\mathcal{I}^{+}\equiv\mathscr{I^{+}_{-}}
and
\mathcal{I}^{-}\equiv\mathscr{I^{-}_{+}}
. They are the critical surfaces (spheres). They are also the upper and lower boundaries of the cylinder representating spatial infinity. Matching conditions connect limiting values of fields at the critical surfaces.

Note that
s=1
,
\rho\rightarrow\infty
corresponds to going first to
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(
s=1
,
\rho>0
) and then taking the limit to the past of
\mathscr{I^{+}}
(i.e., going to spatial infinity “from above” along null infinity). Similarly,
s=-1
,
\rho\rightarrow\infty
corresponds to going first to
\mathscr{I^{-}}
(
s=-1
,
\rho>0
) and then taking the limit to the future of
\mathscr{I^{-}}
(i.e., going to spatial infinity “from below” along null infinity). Note also the fact that increasing
\rho
corresponds to going to spatial infinity in both cases - hence to the past for
\mathscr{I^{+}}
and to the future for
\mathscr{I^{-}}
.

Conversely, the limit
\rho\rightarrow\infty
,
s\in(-1,1)
followed by
s\rightarrow 1
(respectively
-1
) corresponds to going to future null infinity (respectively past null infinity) from spatial infinity.

Appendix B
Gegenbauer functions of the second kind

As we have seen in the text, the relevant differential equations controlling the asymptotic dynalmics of the scalar field are

(1-s^{2})\partial_{s}^{2}Y_{n}^{(\lambda)}+(2\lambda-3)s\partial_{s}Y_{n}^{(\lambda)}+(n+1)(n+2\lambda-1)Y_{n}^{(\lambda)}=0\,,

(163)

with

\lambda=k+\frac{D-3}{2}\,,\qquad n=l-k\,,

(164)

(see (
89
)).

For
n\geq 0
, the solution can be written as

Y_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)=A\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)+B\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)\,,

(165)

where

\widetilde{P}_{n}^{(\lambda)}=(1-s^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}P_{n}^{(\lambda)}\,,\qquad\widetilde{Q}_{n}^{(\lambda)}=(1-s^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}\,.

(166)

Here,
P_{n}^{(\lambda)}
is an ultraspherical (Gegenbauer) polynomial
and
Q_{n}^{(\lambda)}
is an ultraspherical function of second kind. As recalled in the appendix A of
[
23
]
these functions can be constructed using the recurrence relation

nQ_{n}^{(\lambda)}(s)=2(n+\lambda-1)sQ_{n-1}^{(\lambda)}(s)-(n+2\lambda-2)Q_{n-2}^{(\lambda)}(s)\,,

(167)

with starting point

Q_{0}^{(\lambda)}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}dx\,,\qquad Q_{1}^{(\lambda)}(s)=2\lambda sQ_{0}^{(\lambda)}(s)-\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{-\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(168)

To be more explicit on the
s
-dependence of
Q_{0}^{(\lambda)}(s)
, we note that if we set
\alpha=\cos^{-1}x
, the integrals become

\int_{0}^{s}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{-p}dx=\int_{\arccos s}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\sin\alpha)^{-(2p-1)}d\alpha\,,

(169)

with
p=\lambda+\frac{1}{2}
integer (even dimensions) or half-integer (odd dimensions).
Let us set

m=2p-1\,,\qquad J_{m}=\int(\sin\alpha)^{-m}\,d\alpha\,.

(170)

For
p
integer,
m
is odd, while for
p
half-integer,
m
is
even.

The above integral can be written as

\displaystyle\int(\sin\alpha)^{-m}d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-1)}\sin\alpha d\alpha

\displaystyle=-(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-1)}\cos\alpha-(m-1)\int(\cos\alpha)^{2}(\sin\alpha)^{-m}d\alpha

\displaystyle=-(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-1)}\cos\alpha-(m-1)\int(\sin\alpha)^{-m}d\alpha+(m-1)\int(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-2)}d\alpha\,.

From the latter, we obtain that

m\int(\sin\alpha)^{-m}d\alpha=-(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-1)}\cos\alpha+(m-1)\int(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-2)}d\alpha\,.

(171)

or

J_{m}=-\frac{1}{m}(\sin\alpha)^{-(m-1)}\cos\alpha+\frac{(m-1)}{m}J_{m-2}\,.

(172)

For the integrals with
p
half-integer in (
169
),
we compute the lowest case. Explicit computation shows that

J_{2}(s)=\int_{\arccos s}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\sin\alpha)^{-2}d\alpha=\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\,.

(173)

Then, by virtue of the recursion integral
formula (
172
),
J_{m}
will not possess logarithmic
terms for all
m\geq 4
even (
p
half-integer), which corresponds
to the case of odd dimensions.

For the integrals with
p
integer in (
169
), we only
must compute the lowest case to show the presence of logarithmic terms
in this class of integrals. Explicit computation shows that

J_{3}=\int_{\arccos s}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\sin\alpha)^{-3}d\alpha=\frac{x}{2(1-x^{2})}+\frac{1}{4}\log\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)\,.

(174)

Thus,
J_{m}
will possess logarithmic terms for all
m\geq 5
odd
(
p
integer), which only happens in even dimensions.

The appearance of fractional powers of
r
(and no logarithm) in odd dimension, as well as the appearance of logarithms in even dimensions, of course perfectly agrees with the general theory of linear second order differential equations near Fuchsian singularities and the computation of the roots of the indicial equation.

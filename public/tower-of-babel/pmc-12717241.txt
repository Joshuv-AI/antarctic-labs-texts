Secure federated transfer learning with enhanced secure multiparty computation for privacy preserving smart EHR systems
Authors: Tae Hoon Kim, C. Rohith Bhat, Temesgen Engida Yimer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Federated Learning and Artificial Intelligence (AI) are two most intriguing and leading technologies in the intelligent healthcare business. Data must be collected, stored and analyzed from various companies. Patient data processing, particularly in the medical industry, promises substantial breakthroughs in customized health care. For example, different hospitals preserve electronic health records (EHR) for different patient populations, which are difficult to communicate between hospitals due to their concern. As electronic health record (EHR) systems have developed more widespread, privacy and security concerns have increased significance. Sharing and analyzing sensitive health data across multiple organizations and stakeholders requires the application of sophisticated privacy-protection strategies. This study offerings a Secure Federated Transfer Learning (SFTL) structure with an improved system to address privacy and security issues in smart EHR systems. While preserving the privacy of individual patient records, the SFTL framework enables various healthcare providers to interact and collaboratively train machine learning (ML) models using their dispersed EHR data. SMPC enhances privacy guarantees by securely calculating statistical metrics and model parameters across multiple participants without disclosing their contributions. Based on a real-world smart EHR dataset, we evaluate the proposed SFTL -SMPC architecture and compare its performance to existing privacy-preserving techniques. The results demonstrate that our strategy, SFTL-SMPC, balances data privacy and model precision, outperforming traditional federated learning approaches while maintaining patient data security. In addition, we conduct a comprehensive security analysis to validate that our SFTL-SMPC implementation is resilient against various attack scenarios.

Introduction

The Smart electronic health record (HER) System is more than a digital medical record repository; it is a dynamic, intelligent, and networked solution designed to improve the entire healthcare ecosystem. The days of time-consuming paper records and information silos are passed. Both healthcare practitioners and patients may benefit from a more streamlined and efficient healthcare journey with the implementation of this game-changing technology. Contemplate a future in which patient information is securely available at the touch of a button, approving healthcare consultants to make knowledgeable decisions in real-time. Because of the combination of AI and ML, the Smart EHR System can analyze massive amounts of data. This enables doctors to correctly identify patients and establish personalized treatment regimens with outstanding speed and precision. Furthermore, patients have greater control over their health data, encouraging a more collaborative doctor-patient relationship. Individuals can utilize the Smart EHR System to path their development, check their health metrics, and aggressively contribute in their treatment journey, resulting in better health outcomes and increased patient consummation.

By 2030, the World Health Organization (WHO) has designed to acquire universal health attention1. This inventiveness’s principal goal is to guarantee that everyone, everywhere, has access to high-quality healthcare that is both economical and available, regardless of where they living. A collaborative effort with the backing of WHO is preparing to launch the National Health Insurance (NHI) project in nations like South Africa to improve access to first-rate medical care2. To ensure a smooth transition and the achievement of the final goal, the NHI project must build an electronic health record system. This system should quickly enroll and track patients interacting with various healthcare providers. The EHR system is vital for accurate diagnosis and treatment since it is a depository for patient-specific material. This excellent tool is made available to a wide range of healthcare facilities3. The EHR system organizes complete patient health data, including treatment specifics, medical evaluations, allergies, and test results, using electronic medical records (EMR)4. EMR system installation continues to benefit healthcare facilities and the patients they serve, despite some ongoing management problems5. Currently, localized databases hold a sizable amount of the EMR data used in healthcare organizations. Even while EMR systems have been widely deployed throughout many countries, there is still cause for worry over the underuse of advanced system functionalities6. Patients’ private information is at risk due to this condition from cyberattacks, incidental data loss, and deliberate alteration. Centralized AI training on IoT data risks privacy breaches due to aggregation of sensitive device records. Federated learning addresses this by enabling local training, sharing only encrypted model updates instead of raw data. Secure aggregation techniques like SMPC or differential privacy ensure privacy, efficiency, and regulatory compliance.7. A hierarchical federated learning (FL) framework distributes training across edge devices and secure cloud nodes to balance scalability and privacy. Edge devices train locally on private data and share only protected updates, aggregated securely using robust methods. The cloud then combines updates and redistributes the global model, ensuring efficient and privacy-preserving collaborative learning.

From an access perspective, healthcare institutions already manage EMRs often. Regular patients do not have the authority to manage their EMR, either. Patients cannot control who might have restricted access to and use their information. EMRs are operated by various healthcare organizations, each of which has its own set of EMR standards, which affects how hospitals and patients can share data. Patients, mainly those critically ill, could experience considerable difficulties when trying to piece together disparate health records from different sources in the required time8. These EMRs make it challenging to network with colleagues because they contain public and private information about healthcare companies and patients.

Tools and procedures known as privacy-improving technologies (PETs) were developed to growth the security and privacy of data conversation and online interactions. Pets are equipped with several capabilities that help protect users’ sensitive data and private information against unauthorized access and tracking. ML-based models and other data-driven technologies can be given a secure path while maintaining privacy with the help of PET development. Pets are a collection of procedures, methods, and approaches that help to reduce security and privacy issues with sensitive data while still extracting value from it9. PETs are essential, particularly in industries that generate and analyze sensitive data regularly, such as unmanned aerial vehicles and the healthcare industry10. Researchers and healthcare workers can use patient data to identify ailments, develop medications, and promote public health. The importance of statistics to public health was demonstrated, for instance, by studies on vaccine development during the COVID-19 outbreak11.

SFTL-SMPC architecture guarantees that a raw patient information does not reside outside of the local hospital systems and the EHRs remain decentralized and safe. Using Secure Multiparty Computation, intermediate computations will be encrypted, and they cannot be accessed by unauthorised parties across institutions. Federated learning allows training models simultaneously and only sending encrypted updates to preserve confidentiality. Moreover, the existence of strong verification, aggregation, and compliance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability (HIPAA) and General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) introduces additional complexity to the operations. offer privacy and data integrity that could be audited. To guarantee collaborative and individual healthcare learning, SFTL framework combines a number of elements. Local model training maintains patient data in a decentralized state, whereas transfer learning utilizes the common knowledge in pre-trained models to improve the performance. The SMPC allows aggregated updates to be encrypted across institutions without sensitive records being revealed. An accountability, regulatory compliance and a balance between accuracy and strict data protection layer are enforced through a privacy layer. The paper is researching the concept of integrating enhanced Secure Multiparty Computation and Secure Federated Transfer Learning to healthcare. This study establishes a new and strong model through integrating these sophisticated technologies that will enable health professionals, researchers and authorities to make more informed decisions, foster revolutionary medical findings, and eventually enhance patient care coupled with the highest privacy of confidential health information. In this paper, we shall discuss the hypothetical SFTL-SMPC foundations, its benefits, likely obstacles and existing practical applications to demonstrate the gigantic potential of our proposed Smart Electronic Health Record System. Hope to see a new age of healthcare innovation where there are real patient priorities through the unity of the knowledge and skills of various organizations in addition to keeping privacy of data confidential.

Literature survey

Chatterjee et al.12 obtainable a safe access control strategy that includes biometric authentication and a user password in their study. When compared to typical password-based systems, this approach provides increased security. Amin et al.13 developed an anonymous user authentication framework for electronic healthcare systems to assure patient privacy and unlikability. They also presented a solution for anonymous user authentication in wireless medical sensor networks for monitoring patient health.

Chen et al.14 developed a method of enhancing storage, usage and sharing of medical information resources in hospitals. The aim of this strategy is to enhance efficiency in the use of medical resources, as well as patient care. It guarantees integrity and non-repudiation of message, it generates mutual authentication among concerned parties, it attests medical resource sharing authentication through cloud computing and it safeguards against various known attacks.

Ndzimakhwe et al.15 came up with an HLF-based architecture that may be used as an alternative to the existing system. The challenging environment of patient data management was what motivated them. Their solution, which is based on Hyperledger Fabric, solves the problem of the current data management practices in the industry. The component pieces of the system can be modified to fit the established design requirements. Its flexibility can assist researchers and clinicians in creating scenarios that resemble real-life experiences and learn about the challenge of maintaining the health record of a patient.

Samarin et al.16 consider the idea to use a private cloud storage system and give patients access to their medical records only. The patients now have complete control of medical records. Such a strategy should take into account the need to share medical data with other parties, including healthcare providers. Also, a deposit box with the records to a third party would resolve the interoperability problem. The notion presented in this piece of writing fails to take into account situations where a physician might be forced to conceal medical details even to the patient. Moreover, this plan must resolve the problem of the security of medical records.

According to Fatokun et al.17, blockchain technology can be used to improve EHR systems. They also discussed how our proposed approach could address data exchange, security, and privacy concerns. We created a smart contract on the Ethereum consortium blockchain to construct our patient-centric EHR application. Because blockchain records are immutable and cannot be removed, they provide security, privacy, and integrity. Medical records sharing requires patients’ permission, and patient information from various healthcare providers is kept consistently to promote interoperability. Over time, data will continue to increase. Another issue with the suggested approach is the additional expense caused by the bandwidth resources needed to mine a new block and the communication broadcast to all network nodes.

Nowrozy et al.18 presented a privacy model in light of changing technologies and privacy legislation. More in-depth studies and actual testing are needed to properly verify the efficacy and dependability of our approach, as is the case in our study. The scalability and interoperability of our approach with current EHR and healthcare IT systems have yet to be thoroughly studied. We should periodically review and change our methodology to keep up with new technological developments and creative privacy-enhancing strategies. Research is still being done to address ethical issues, such as possible biases in ML algorithms, data ownership, and consent management.

Kayes et al.19 suggested creating a context-aware access control system based on fog. This system had an aim of enhancing scale and flexibility of security. The authors state that their method, which incorporates dynamic and context-sensitive access control, can enhance the security of fog computing systems. The proposal of the CAC system was well described in the essay. Nevertheless, both the performance and scalability of the proposed method was not tested empirically in the publication addition. The possibility of challenges and limitations to the implementation of such a system was not considered. The proposed plan has potential, and it should be supported by more researches to reveal its efficiency and feasibility.

The context-aware access control system on cloud-based data resources recently proposed by Kayes et al.20 has the ability to accommodate incorrect context information. The approach of the authors consists in the use of fuzzy logic applied to the data of the context in order to control its ambiguity. Consequently, a whole context-driven access control model was created. Their proposed model was relatively limited in the evaluation since it did not have a deep analysis of such aspects as performance testing, scalability, and the comparison to other innovative methods. Also, the way the proposed framework could be utilized had to be outlined.

Adil et al.21 provide a thorough examination of the current literature on the security threats and countermeasures of next-generation industrial cyber-physical systems (NC-ICPS) technology, with a particular emphasis on AI, ML, and DL-enabled techniques. To assess these methodologies, we define their advantages and disadvantages in comparison to conventional authentication and data preservation techniques. Furthermore, we deliberated on the review articles that were published on this subject in order to recognize their contributions and constraints. Hussen et al.22 this study introduces a machine-learning system to optimize mobile app feature addition and performance decisions. NLP was used to preprocess textual information and develop an Enhanced Random Forest (ERF) framework to assess and forecast ratings for multifunctional apps and investigate the relationships between features and user ratings using an Apple AppStore dataset.

Ahmad et al.23 used smart contracts to construct a system for remotely monitoring patients and notifying medical workers of crises. Three actors and three functions make up the proposed system. Patients and doctors can register on the website first. They use a smartphone to access it, securely entering their ID, name, age, and other facts. The IoT sensor data will be processed for patient monitoring and saved on the blockchain using smart contracts. Doctors can now follow the progress of their patients in real-time. Concerning commercial and medical devices, the final function is affected. A smart contract is created among the vendor and the customer when a device is purchased to be registered in their name.

Jamil et al.24 developed a system that monitors key indications to allay patient data privacy and openness worries. The core of this platform is the blockchain technology known as Hyperledger Fabric, along with smart contracts. The suggested method entails obtaining data from medical sensors and then making this data accessible over the blockchain network. Smart hospital staff members track and process these data using a web application created using JavaScript and HTML5 that allows data access. The security and traceability of the patient’s vital parameters provide performance assurance. The performance of the suggested prototype is strong because of features including low latency, an easy-to-use UI, and quick transactions.

Proposed methodology

The secure multiparty computation (SMC) approach is primarily used to determine the shared function of participants with access to secret information. In this paradigm, only the calculation’s result is shared with the participants; the remaining inputs are only visible to the Manager or other authority supervising the entire transaction. Since private patient data shouldn’t be shared with others, this approach’s implementation fits well with the e-healthcare concept. Instead, it should be used to make diagnoses by contrasting them with information from other patients. Due to its effectiveness, affordability, and dependability, the public cloud storage solution is chosen due to the huge amount of data, including past medical reports.

Due to the masks’ strength, randomness and secrecy protect the information from the opposite side. At each training level, A learns its slopes. Because it is impossible to solve n equations with more than n unknowns, more is needed for A to learn anything from B. In other words, A can have the same gradients by changing B in unlimited ways. A cannot teach B anything, just as B cannot teach A anything. The suggested protocol is secure as long as the encryption method keeps its security. With every sample evaluation, A obtains a forecast of the anticipated result from B. Since this is a scalar product, A cannot deduce any private information about B. B gains knowledge of A’s name; no other details about A’s personal life are revealed.

After the training process, neither party (A nor B) is aware of the data structure of the other party. They are only aware of the model parameters for their features. The two parties must cooperate during reasoning to determine the outcomes of the predictions. Note that the process does not apply if one or both parties attempt to cause harm. A could determine the value of uBi at a given input junction if it generates false inputs and only displays one non-zero information. Both xBi and B still need to be explained to it, and neither can accurately forecast the future.

This section delves into the fundamental idea that underpins our secure MPC protocol. We develop a protocol based on addition and multiplication to ease multi-party arithmetic calculation over the field F_k_p. The following are some of this protocol’s primary benefits:

Privacy and Confidentiality: Encryption ensures information protection by only allowing the user’s input secret and output values to be revealed.

Publicly evident: The operation of computations continues to be unrestricted and is visible to all. Any computing step can be verified in the open.

Our method is a two-part process that includes an off-chain preprocessing phase and an on-chain compute phase, similar to SPD. These phases represent the offline and online stages of SPD. We combine the Paillier cryptosystem with additive secret sharing to ensure privacy and secrecy. Based on parallelizable encrypted shares, the computations are performed. The verifiability of calculations and information sharing is also confirmed using the Pedersen technique.

We start with a publicly reliable processor (PRP), which may be used with either a trustworthy entity or a trusted execution environment (TEE). The PRP efficiently manages inputs, executes calculations, and accurately produces results. The PRP always acts transparently, and all its decisions may be substantiated.

(F)Gradient noise injection for privacy preservation

(F)Gradient noise injection for privacy preservation

Gradient noise injection for privacy preservation

Gradient noise injection is used to curb membership inference attacks in sensitive applications like workplace surveillance to ensure that membership information is deterred. Membership inference attacks are based on the idea that the slight variation between model output or gradients can be used to identify whether a specific sample was included in the training set, and thus endanger the privacy of employees28. The information leakage is reduced with model utility being preserved by injecting calibrated random noise into the gradient updates. Mathematically, having the loss gradient at iteration t, i.e.

where η is the learning rate. The method is compatible with the concepts of differential privacy, in which the stochasticity guarantees that the data of any individual employee cannot be differentiated in the aggregate gradients. In workplace surveillance situations where the abuse of inferred membership may reveal sensitive behavioral patterns this mechanism gives a mathematically justified trade off between privacy and predictive performance.

(A)Experimental setup

(A)Experimental setup

Experimental setup

The Fabric code was written in Java chain code, whereas the MPC library was created in C++ . We used JNI technology to elegantly integrate the MPC library into Java chain code, allowing Java to call C++ functions. This integration was possible to a specially created Docker container called Fabric-javaenv. Our infrastructure comprised Hyper ledger Fabric 1.4.0 running under Ubuntu 18.04 on an Intel Core i5-6400 processor running at 2.70 GHz with 16 GB of RAM. In distinct Docker containers on the same machine, Peers and Chain code were both active simultaneously. We were able to communicate with the blockchain network more easily to the Hyper ledger Fabric SDK’s Java 1.4.0 release. Each organization had a single peer in each of our experiments, and those peers belonged to different participants. To guarantee the same number of patients as computing records, we chose “all organizations” as the endorsement standard. In this research using the existing systems are Decentralized Efficient and Privacy-Enhanced Federated Edge Learning (DEEP-FEL)29, Communication Efficient and Enhanced Privacy with federated learning (CEEP-FL)30, server less privacy edge intelligence-based federated learning (SPEI-FL)31, convolutional neural network-based secure multi-party computation (CNN-SMPC)32, Practical and effective privacy-enhanced federated learning (PEPFL)33.

The smart EHR real-world data that is utilized to test the SFTL-SMPC architecture is gathered in numerous hospitals and clinics, which makes it diverse in terms of patient demographics, medical conditions, and treatments. It has structured (laboratory tests, vital signs, medications, and diagnostic codes) and unstructured (clinical note, discharge note, and imaging metadata) data. The data is horizontally fragmented among the institutions and does not centralize patient records to model a realistic federated learning scenario. It is also capable of combining both temporal and multimodal data that allows the framework to model longitudinal trends and cross-modes. The dataset is secure in multiparty computation, with anonymized identifiers and compatibility with privacy-preserving protocols, and allows an evaluation rigorous to determine the predictive accuracy, robustness, and privacy resilience. The suggested framework is resistant to homomorphic encryption (HE), which enables training and inference using encrypted data and therefore the raw inputs are not visible at any point. With the combination of SMPC, the model updates are divided into cryptographic shares, so that no particular machine can restore sensitive information. This will block gradient inversion or membership inference attacks by adversaries. The cryptographic design has semantic security to ensure that even partial information leakage is computationally infeasible. Resistant key management and encrypted channels of communication also prevents eavesdropping. These mechanisms are combined to form a powerful theoretical foundation that discourages privacy violations and keeps models useful.

Scalability and computational analysis

We analyze the computation and communication costs of the proposed SFTL-SMPC framework per federated round to assess its scalability across participating healthcare institutions. Using Paillier-based homomorphic encryption (HE) for additions and scalar multiplications, and Beaver-triple secret sharing (SS) for secure multiplications, the per-round client payload scales with the number of secure multiplications \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M$$\end{document} and model dimension \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d$$\end{document}: bytes/round = O(Md2) for SS operations and O(d) for HE vectors operations. The proposed hierarchical multiplication gate, with logarithmic depth \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( {{\text{log}}m} \right)$$\end{document}, reduces interactive communication rounds while preserving verifiability through Pedersen commitments. On the experimental infrastructure described in §Experimental Setup (Intel i5-6400 @ 2.70 GHz, 16 GB RAM, Docker-based Hyperledger Fabric network), the median computational latencies were approximately 0.84 s for key generation, 1.21 s for encryption, 2.73 s for secure matrix multiplication, and 0.67 s for decryption per federated round. Communication overhead per client increased linearly with the number of participants due to ciphertext payload growth, ranging from 28 MB (5 clients) to 131 MB (20 clients). Under strong-scaling experiments (total samples fixed, varying client count \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K$$\end{document}), the time to reach AUC ≥ 0.95 decreased sub-linearly from 92 s @ K = 5 to 54 s @ K = 20, while overall throughput improved by nearly 39% owing to parallelized encryption and aggregation. Conversely, in weak-scaling tests (samples per client fixed, increasing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K$$\end{document}), accuracy remained stable within Δ ≤ 1.8% up to K = 20, indicating robust convergence despite increased cryptographic overhead. These findings indicate that SFTL-SMPC has an effective computational performance and scalable communication overhead, which allows it to be deployed in practice in multi-institutional EHR federated environments without affecting security or model fidelity.

Comparative evaluation

Compared to FedMUP (malicious-user prediction in FL clouds) and FedMBC (personalized FL with non-IID clients), SFTL-SMPC has similar or better AUC and F1-score results on the Smart-EHR dataset and provides ciphertext-level privacy with homomorphic encryption (HE) and secure multiparty computation (SMPC), as well as verifiable aggregation. FedMUP reports significant gains over previous baselines in detection metrics, in comparison our results indicate that the addition of HE and SMPC adds about 710 MB of extra communication per round and a mean latency overhead of 6.8 percent but predictive accuracy is within 1.2 percent of FedMUP with the same non-IID data partitions34. Compared to FedMBC that uses personalized local heads to address the statistical heterogeneity, SFTL-SMPC successfully reduces the non-IID performance gap without involving client-specific models, with the convergence and variance being stabilized and decreased during training rounds. Although FedMBC still has its benefits when the distribution of clients is highly skewed, our integrated architecture has shown better scalability, higher privacy, and balanced generalization of the model across heterogeneous hospital nodes. These findings point to the fact that SFTL-SMPC represents a viable trade-off between privacy, communication effectiveness, and predictive utility, and it can be considered a good candidate in real-world federated healthcare environment35.

There are a number of challenges in implementation of SFTL-SMPC in real life healthcare situation. The lack of data heterogeneity in different hospitals, incomplete data and irregular formatting may impede effective transfer learning. Computational and communication costs are also higher in the cryptographic overhead of SMPC, which reduces scalability and applicability in real-time. Though privacy is secured, threats of data leakage and adversarial attack are still present, which poses a trade-off between the utility and security. Furthermore, the complexity of the integration with the existing healthcare systems and adherence to stringent regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR increase operational complexity.

Conclusion

Our main priority is to ensure the secure storage of patient-owned sensitive medical information in third-party or public cloud platforms. In today’s technology-determined society, patients can receive medical advice from doctors automatically. On the other hand, patients are practically concerned about the security of their personal information when distribution it with the medical system. In this paper, proposed constructing an electronic medical advising system with Secure Multiparty Computation capabilities to address this risk. This device would capture patient symptoms and compare them to data vaults in the healthcare system. A homomorphic encryption technique model would be employed to ensure the security of the stored data. To implement the partial homomorphic encryption approach, the Paillier encryption algorithm would be employed. Creating the SFTL framework will enable varied healthcare professionals to collaborate and train machine learning models using their electronic health record data, all while protecting each patient’s privacy. By securely calculating statistical measures and model parameters across several participants without revealing their contributions, SMPC improves privacy assurances. We assess the proposed SFTL -SMPC architecture and compare it to the Performance of accuracy with the existing privacy-preserving methods like SPAKE (Secure Password Authentication-Based Key Exchange), HCBE (Hierarchical Comparison-Based Encryption), BSF (Blockchain Security Framework), LR (Logistic Regression), and SAEs (stacked auto-encoders) methods. It has an accuracy of 74.98%, 89.34%, 83.33%, 66.99%, and 94.67%, respectively, while our proposed SFTL -SMPC way has 99.89% with 600 records using a real-world smart EHR dataset. Further inclusion of patients and other stakeholders in healthcare into the federated learning structure, in addition to healthcare providers, may result in a more holistic and patient-centered approach to healthcare. This would entail finding a way to address special issues related to data access and data security and create an incentive to utilize the system. Further studies need to be directed towards creating personalized federated learning tools, which can be customized to suit different patient groups in various hospitals. The SMPC protocols will need to be optimised to make them scalable and low-latency, so that real-time clinical decision-making is possible. Also, incorporation of multimodal healthcare information, including imaging, laboratory and clinical notes into secure FL can provide additional predictive accuracy. In addition, effective defense systems are needed to protect federated environments against adversarial threats such as gradient inversion and data poisoning attacks.

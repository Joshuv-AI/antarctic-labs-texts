Distinguishing Siegel modular forms
Arvind Kumar and Ariel Weiss

Abstract
. Let f f and f ′ f^{\prime} be genus 2 2 cuspidal Siegel paramodular newforms. We prove that if their Hecke eigenvalues a p a_{p} and a p ′ a_{p}^{\prime} satisfy a non-trivial polynomial relation P ⁡ ( a p , a p ′ ) = 0 P(a_{p},a_{p}^{\prime})=0 for a set of primes p p of positive density, then f f and f ′ f^{\prime} are twists of each other by a Dirichlet character. In particular, if both forms have trivial nebentypus, then they differ by a quadratic twist. This result extends the strong multiplicity one theorem, which handles the case P ⁡ ( x , y ) = x − y P(x,y)=x-y , to arbitrary polynomial relations. Our proof analyses the image of the product Galois representation attached to the pair ( f , f ′ ) (f,f^{\prime}) : we show that this image is as large as possible, unless f f is a twist of f ′ f^{\prime} . Our results also apply to elliptic modular forms. They therefore provide a unified method for distinguishing both elliptic and Siegel modular forms based on their Hecke data, including their Hecke eigenvalues, Satake parameters, Sato–Tate angles, and the coefficients of their L L -functions. We apply our methods to recover and generalise a range of existing results and to prove new ones in both the elliptic and Siegel settings.

1.
Introduction

Let
f
and
f^{\prime}
be genus
2
, cuspidal Siegel paramodular newforms. Suppose that for almost all primes
p
, their Hecke eigenvalues
a_{p}
and
a_{p}^{\prime}
satisfy a fixed polynomial relation
P(a_{p},a_{p}^{\prime})=0
.
What is the relationship between
f
and
f^{\prime}
?

If
P(x,y)=x-y
, then this question is answered by the
strong multiplicity one
theorem
[
33
,
34
,
10
]
, which states that
a_{p}=a_{p}^{\prime}
for almost all primes
p
if and only if
f
is a scalar multiple of
f^{\prime}
. But for other polynomials, this conclusion fails. For example, a form and its quadratic twists have Hecke eigenvalues satisfying
a_{p}^{2}={a_{p}^{\prime}}^{2}
for all
p
, yet are not scalar multiples.

In this paper, we show that twists are the only possible exceptions: if
a_{p}
and
a_{p}^{\prime}
satisfy a non-trivial polynomial relation for a positive proportion of primes, then
f
and
f^{\prime}
must be twists of each other by a Dirichlet character. This result can be viewed as a refinement of the strong multiplicity one theorem: even partial algebraic information about a form’s Hecke eigenvalues is sufficient to determine it up to twist.

We now state our result more precisely. Assume that
f
and
f^{\prime}
have weights
(k_{1},k_{2})
and
(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
—i.e. weights
\Sym^{k_{1}-k_{2}}\det^{k_{2}}
and
\Sym^{k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{2}^{\prime}}\det^{k_{2}^{\prime}}
—with
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 2
and
k_{1}^{\prime}\geq k_{2}^{\prime}\geq 2
, levels
N
and
N^{\prime}
, and characters
\epsilon
and
\epsilon^{\prime}
. Suppose that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are of general type (type
(G)
in the notation of
[
33
]
), and that neither has complex multiplication (CM), real multiplication (RM), or arises as a symmetric cube lift. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be the corresponding cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P(a,a^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[a,a^{\prime}]
be any non-zero polynomial. Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

P(a_{p},a_{p}^{\prime})=0.

Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
. In particular, if
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}=1
then
f
is a scalar multiple of a quadratic twist of
f^{\prime}
.

More generally, we show that an algebraic relation among
any
of the Hecke eigenvalues of a Siegel modular form
f
is sufficient to determine it up to twist. The Hecke algebra of
f
is generated by
T(p)
,
T(p^{2})
, and the diamond operator
\Delta(p)
. The
T(p)
-Hecke eigenvalues
a_{p}
of a Siegel modular form
f
are the coefficients of its
spinor
L
-function

(1.1)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{spin}}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}L_{p,\mathrm{spin}}(f,s)=L(\epsilon^{2},2s-u+1)\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}n^{-s}

where
u=k_{1}+k_{2}-3
, and for
p\nmid N
, by
[
2
]
*(5.64) and
[
6
]
*pp. 1499, we have

L_{p,\mathrm{spin}}(f,s)=(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+(a_{p}^{2}-a_{p^{2}}-\epsilon(p^{2})p^{u-1})p^{-2s}-a_{p}\epsilon(p^{2})p^{u}p^{-3s}+\epsilon(p^{4})p^{2u}p^{-4s}))^{-1}.

Let

(1.2)

b_{p}=\frac{a_{p}^{2}-a_{p^{2}}}{\epsilon(p^{2})p^{u}}-\frac{1}{p}-1.

Then
b_{p}
satisfies the equation

(b_{p}+1)\epsilon(p^{2})p^{u}=a_{p}^{2}-a_{p^{2}}-\epsilon(p^{2})p^{u-1}

and is the coefficient of the
standard

L
-function of
f
,

L_{\std}(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}n^{-s}.

We prove the following more general result, from which
Theorem

1.1
follows immediately:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

P(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
be a non-zero polynomial. Assume that
P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
, where
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
are integers defined in (
1.3
).
Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

P(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3},a_{p},b_{p},a_{p}^{\prime},b_{p}^{\prime})=0.

Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
. In particular, if
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}=1
then
f
is a scalar multiple of a quadratic twist of
f^{\prime}
.

The coprimality condition on
P
is necessary to rule out trivial polynomial relations that can arise from the weights and characters of
f
and
f^{\prime}
.

This result strengthens the observation in
[
26
,
10
]
on the strong multiplicity one theorem, where the authors note that it is “remarkable” that the eigenvalues
a_{p}
alone suffice to determine
f
, despite the fact that the Hecke algebra is not generated by

. We show that an algebraic relation involving
any
of the Hecke eigenvalues for just a positive proportion of primes suffices to determine the form up to twist.

Remark 1.3
.

The conclusion that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
can presumably be strengthened to
f=f^{\prime}\otimes\chi
, but we are not aware of a reference that constructs arbitrary twists of paramodular newforms. However, if
f
and
f^{\prime}
both have trivial nebentypus character, then the conclusion
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
implies that
f
is a scalar multiple of a
quadratic
twist of
f^{\prime}
. Indeed, in this case
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have trivial central character, so
\chi^{2}=1
, and the construction of quadratic twists of paramodular newforms is given in
[
15
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

If the levels
N
and
N^{\prime}
are squarefree, then the conclusion
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
implies that
\chi
is trivial, and hence that
f
is a scalar multiple of
f^{\prime}
.

1.1.
Galois representations attached to Siegel modular forms

To prove
Theorem

1.2
, we reinterpret algebraic relations between Hecke eigenvalues as relations occurring in the images of Galois representations attached to Siegel modular forms.

For each prime
\ell
, there exists a semisimple Galois representation

\rho_{\ell}\colon\Gal(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/{\mathbf{Q}})\to\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})

associated to
f
. This representation is unramified outside
\ell N
, and if
p\nmid\ell N
, then the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p})
agrees with the Euler factor of
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(f,s)
at
p
. In particular, we have

a_{p}=\Tr\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p}),

\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3}=\simil\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p}),

and

b_{p}=\Tr\std\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p})=\frac{\tr\wedge^{2}\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p})}{\simil\rho_{\ell}(\Frob_{p})}-1,

where
\simil\colon\GSp_{4}\to\mathbf{G}_{m}
is the similitude character, and
\std\colon\GSp_{4}\to\PGSp_{4}\cong\SO_{5}\hookrightarrow\GL_{5}
is the standard representation (via the exceptional isomorphism
\PGSp_{4}\cong\SO_{5}
).
We similarly define

\rho_{\ell}^{\prime}\colon\Gal(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/{\mathbf{Q}})\to\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})

attached to
f^{\prime}
, and consider the product representation

R_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell}\times\rho_{\ell}^{\prime}\colon\Gal(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/{\mathbf{Q}})\to\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\times\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}).

The vanishing of a polynomial
P
as in
Theorem

1.2
for a set of primes of positive density implies the vanishing of the function

(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\mapsto P(\simil(\gamma),\simil(\gamma^{\prime}),\tr(\gamma),\tr(\gamma^{\prime}),\tr\std(\gamma),\tr\std(\gamma^{\prime}))

on a large part of the image of
R_{\ell}
. We prove
Theorem

1.2
by studying the image of
R_{\ell}
.

1.2.
Images of Galois representations

The similitude relation between
\rho_{\ell}
and
\rho_{\ell}^{\prime}
ensures that the image of
R_{\ell}
lies inside the subgroup

\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})=\left\{(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\in\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\times\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}):\simil(\gamma)^{\kappa}=\simil(\gamma^{\prime})^{\kappa^{\prime}}\right\},

where

\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
are the smallest positive integers such that

(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3})^{\kappa}=(\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3})^{\kappa^{\prime}}

for all primes
p
. Explicitly, we have

(1.3)

(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})=\ord\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2(k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3)/n}}{(\epsilon^{\prime})^{2(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)/n}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3}{n},\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-3}{n}\right),

where

n=\gcd(k_{1}+k_{2}-3,k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3).

We view
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}
as an affine algebraic group over
\Spec\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
: it is the fibre product of
\GSp_{4}
with itself, fibred over the similitude maps
\simil^{\kappa}
and
\simil^{\kappa^{\prime}}
. In particular, we have

\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}=\Spec\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}),

where
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})
is the ring of global functions of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}
. In
Section

3.3
, we give an explicit description of the
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})
and its connected components.

Recall that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are of general type, and that neither has CM, RM, or is a symmetric cube lift. By work of Dieulefait
[
8
]
, Dieulefait–Zenteno
[
9
]
, and the second author
[
40
,
41
]
, the images of
\rho_{\ell}
and
\rho_{\ell}^{\prime}
are generically large. In particular, there is a set of primes
\mathcal{L}
of Dirichlet density
1
such that if
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, then the Zariski closures of the images of
\rho_{\ell}
and
\rho_{\ell}^{\prime}
in
{\GSp_{4}}_{/\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}}
are
\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
(
Corollary

3.4
).

Let
\Gamma_{\ell}
denote the Zariski closure of the image of
R_{\ell}
in
{\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}}_{/\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}}
. Using the fact that both projections of
\Gamma_{\ell}
onto
\GSp_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
are surjective, we show that
\Gamma_{\ell}
is strictly contained in
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
if and only if
f
and
f^{\prime}
are related. More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 1.5
.

Exactly one of the following two conditions holds:

(i)

For all primes
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, we have
\Gamma_{\ell}=\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
.

(ii)

There exists a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

To deduce
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
, it remains to relate the existence of a polynomial
P
satisfying the vanishing hypothesis to the strictness of the inclusion
\Gamma_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
.

Let
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})^{\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}}
denote the subring of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})
consisting of functions that are invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}
on itself by conjugation. Note that if
P
is a polynomial as in
Theorem

1.2
, then the function

(1.4)

\phi\colon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\mapsto P(\simil(\gamma),\simil(\gamma^{\prime}),\tr(\gamma),\tr(\gamma^{\prime}),\tr\std(\gamma),\tr\std(\gamma^{\prime}))

is an element of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})^{\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}}
.

By combining
Theorem

1.5
with Rajan’s algebraic version of the Chebotarev density theorem
[
27
]
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\phi
be an element
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}})^{\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}}
that does not vanish on any connected component of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}
.
Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density, we have

\phi(R_{\ell}(\Frob_{p}))=0.

Then there exists a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
. In particular, if
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}=1
then
f
is quadratic twist of
f^{\prime}
.

Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
follow from
Theorem

1.6
. Indeed, we show that the hypothesis in
Theorem

1.2
that
P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
exactly ensures that the corresponding function
\phi
defined in (
1.4
) does not vanish on any connected component of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}
.

1.3.
Results for a single Siegel modular form

While our results concern relations between pairs of Siegel modular forms, they can also be used to prove results about the Hecke eigenvalues of a single Siegel modular form.

Suppose that
f
is a Siegel modular form satisfying the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
P(s,a,b)\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},a,b]
be a non-zero polynomial. Then the set of primes

\left\{p:P(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},a_{p},b_{p})=0\right\}

has Dirichlet density
0
.

Note that
Theorem

1.7
follows immediately from
Theorem

1.2
by viewing
P
as an element of
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
and choosing
f^{\prime}
to be any Siegel modular form that is not a twist of
f
.

It is also possible to prove
Theorem

1.7
directly, without appealing to
Theorem

1.2
, by applying our techniques to a single Siegel modular form. We include
Theorem

1.7
here to emphasise that
Theorem

1.2
captures such statements uniformly.

1.4.
Results for elliptic modular forms

Our methods equally apply to elliptic modular forms. In this setting, we replace
Theorem

1.5
with the classical results of Ribet and Momose on the images of Galois representations attached to modular forms
[
31
,
22
,
32
,
20
]
. We state the corresponding analogue of
Theorem

1.2
in this context and note that our applications in
Section

2
all have analogues in this setting.

Let
f
and
f^{\prime}
be cuspidal newforms of weights
k,k^{\prime}\geq 2
, levels
N,N^{\prime}
, and nebentypus characters
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}
. For each prime
p
, let
a_{p}
and
a_{p}^{\prime}
denote their Hecke eigenvalues. Assume that
f
and
f^{\prime}
do not have complex multiplication. Similarly to (
1.3
), define
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
to be the smallest positive integers such that

(\epsilon(p)p^{k-1})^{\kappa}=(\epsilon^{\prime}(p)p^{k^{\prime}-1})^{\kappa^{\prime}}

for all primes
p
. Then we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
P(s,s^{\prime},a,a^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,a^{\prime}]
. Assume that
P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
.
Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

P(\epsilon(p)p^{k-1},\epsilon^{\prime}(p)p^{k^{\prime}-1},a_{p},a_{p}^{\prime})=0.

Then
k=k^{\prime}
and there is a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
f=f^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
P(s,a)\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},a]
be a non-zero polynomial. Then the set of primes

\left\{p:P(\epsilon(p)p^{k-1},a_{p})=0\right\}

has density
0
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, the Hecke eigenvalue
a_{p^{n}}
can be expressed as a polynomial in
a_{p}
and
\epsilon(p)p^{k-1}
. Hence, as a corollary to
Theorem

1.8
, we immediately deduce the following result:

Corollary 1.10
.

Fix
n\in\mathbf{N}
and suppose that
a_{p^{n}}=a^{\prime}_{p^{n}}
for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density. Then
k=k^{\prime}
and there is a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
f=f^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

In fact, we can show that if
P\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[a,a^{\prime}]
is any polynomial such that
P(a_{p^{n}},a^{\prime}_{p^{n}})=0
for a positive density of
p
, then

is a twist of
f^{\prime}
(see
Remark

2.18
).

2.1.
Distinguishing eigenforms by their Hecke eigenvalues

Let
n
and
m
be positive integers. Taking
P(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a^{n}-{a^{\prime}}^{n})(b^{m}-{b^{\prime}}^{m})
in
Theorem

1.2
, we immediately deduce the following corollary:

Corollary 2.1
.

Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density, either
a_{p}^{n}={a_{p}^{\prime}}^{n}
or
b_{p}^{m}={b_{p}^{\prime}}^{m}
. Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Remarks 2.2
.

(i)

If
f
and
f^{\prime}
both have level
1
, then
\chi
must be trivial and we conclude that
f
is a scalar multiple of
f^{\prime}
. Thus,
Corollary

2.1
generalises
[
17
]
*Thm. 1.5 to Siegel modular forms of arbitrary level and character.

(ii)

If
f
and
f^{\prime}
have trivial character, then the character
\chi
must be quadratic, and we deduce that
f
is a scalar multiple of
f\otimes\chi
. Thus,
Corollary

2.1
generalises
[
39
]
*Thm. 1.2 to arbitrary level, extends it to include the case
k_{2}=2
(c.f.
[
39
]
*Rem. 2), and weakens the hypothesis by requiring only that
b_{p}=b_{p}^{\prime}
for a positive density of primes, rather than for almost all primes.

(iii)

The analogue of
Corollary

2.1
for elliptic modular forms recovers
[
27
]
*Cor. 1 when
n=1
, the main result of
[
29
]
when
n=2
, and the main result of
[
28
]
in general.

We also deduce the following result, which is the analogue of
[
25
]
*Cor. 1.1 for Siegel modular forms (see also
[
16
]
*Thm. 2):

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose that for a positive density of primes
p
, there exists a positive integer
n_{p}
such that
a_{p}^{n_{p}}={a_{p}^{\prime}}^{n_{p}}
. Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is a Dirichlet character such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Proof.

If
a_{p}^{n_{p}}={a_{p}^{\prime}}^{n_{p}}
, then
a_{p}/a_{p}^{\prime}
is a root of unity. Let
E
be the number field containing all the
a_{p}
’s and
a_{p}^{\prime}
’s. Since
E
contains only finitely many roots of unity, it follows that there is an integer

such that
a_{p}^{n}={a_{p}^{\prime}}^{n}
for a positive density of primes
p
. The result follows from
Corollary

2.1
.
∎

2.2.
Distinguishing eigenforms by their normalised eigenvalues

For each prime
p
and each positive integer
n
, let

\lambda_{p^{n}}=\frac{a_{p^{n}}}{p^{n(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)/2}}

and

\lambda_{p^{n}}^{\prime}=\frac{a_{p^{n}}^{\prime}}{p^{n(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-3)/2}}

be the normalised Hecke eigenvalues of
f
and
f^{\prime}
at
p^{n}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

P(x,y)\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[x,y]
be any non-zero polynomial, and suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

P(\lambda_{p},\lambda_{p}^{\prime})=0.

Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

We will make frequent use of the following lemma:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
R
be a
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
-algebra, let
f(x)\in R[x]
be a non-zero polynomial, and let
d
be a positive integer. Let

F(x)=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{d}}P(\zeta x).

Then there exists a non-zero polynomial
Q
such that
F(x)=Q(x^{d})
.
In particular, there exists a polynomial
Q\in R[x]
such that
P(x)\mid Q(x^{d})
.

Proof.

Clearly
P(x)\mid F(x)
. Since
F(\zeta x)=F(x)
for all
\zeta\in\mu_{d}
, it follows that
F(x)=Q(x^{d})
for some polynomial
Q(x)\in R[x]
. Since
P(\zeta x)
is non-zero for all
x
,
F(x)
is non-zero, and hence
Q
is non-zero.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.4
.

Let
d
be an integer such that
\epsilon^{2d}={\epsilon^{\prime}}^{2d}=1
. By the construction of
Lemma

2.5
, there exists a polynomial
Q\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[x,y]
such that
P(x,y)\mid Q(x^{2d},y^{2d})
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=Q(\frac{a^{2d}}{s^{d}},\frac{{a^{\prime}}^{2d}}{{s^{\prime}}^{d}})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
.
Since

\lambda_{p}^{2d}=\frac{a_{p}^{2d}}{p^{d(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)}}=\frac{a_{p}^{2d}}{(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3})^{d}}

and similarly for
{\lambda_{p}^{\prime}}^{2d}
, we see that

\mathcal{P}(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3},a_{p},b_{p},a_{p}^{\prime},b_{p}^{\prime})=Q(\lambda_{p}^{2d},{\lambda^{\prime}}_{p}^{2d})=0

for a positive proportion of
p
.

Now,
\mathcal{P}
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{P}
had a common factor with
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
, then
\mathcal{P}(s^{2},{s^{\prime}}^{2},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
would have a common factor with
s^{2\kappa}-s^{2\kappa^{\prime}}
. But

\mathcal{P}(s^{2},{s^{\prime}}^{2},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=Q((a/s)^{2d},(a^{\prime}/s^{\prime})^{2d}),

and by the construction in
Lemma

2.5
, the right-hand side is a product of non-zero polynomials of the form
P\left(\zeta(a/s),\zeta^{\prime}(a^{\prime}/s^{\prime})\right)
with
\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}\in\mu_{2d}
. After the change of variables
a\mapsto sa
,
a^{\prime}\mapsto s^{\prime}a^{\prime}
, each of these polynomials become independent of
s
and
s^{\prime}
, and thus coprime to
s^{2\kappa}-s^{2\kappa^{\prime}}
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-s^{\kappa^{\prime}}
, and the result follows from
Theorem

1.2
.
∎

Taking
P(x,y)=x^{n}-y^{n}
in
Theorem

2.4
, we immediately deduce the following result:

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density,
\lambda_{p}^{n}={\lambda_{p}^{\prime}}^{n}
. Then there is a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi.

The analogue of
Corollary

2.6
for elliptic modular forms recovers
[
23
]
*Thm. 1 and
[
24
]
*Thm. 1 when
n=1
.

2.3.
Distinguishing eigenforms by the absolute values of their Hecke eigenvalues

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

P(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
be a non-zero polynomial.
Assume that
P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
.
Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

P(p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3},|a_{p}|,|b_{p}|,|a_{p}^{\prime}|,|b_{p}^{\prime}|)=0.

Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Proof.

Applying the properties of the Peterson inner product (
[
1
]
*Prop. 1.8), we have

a_{p}=\epsilon(p)\overline{a_{p}}\quad{\rm and}\quad a_{p^{2}}=\epsilon(p^{2})\overline{a_{p^{2}}},

where
\overline{z}
denotes the complex conjugate of the complex number
z
. It follows that the arguments of
a_{p}
and
a_{p^{2}}
are rational multiples of
\pi
. Consequently, the arguments of
{a_{p}^{2}}/\epsilon(p^{2})
and
{a_{p^{2}}}/{\epsilon(p^{2})}
, and hence of
b_{p}
, are as well (see (
1.2
)). Since the same is true for
a_{p}^{\prime}
and
b_{p}^{\prime}
, there exists a positive integer
m
such that
a_{p}^{m},b_{p}^{m},{a_{p}^{\prime}}^{m},{b_{p}^{\prime}}^{m}\in\mathbf{R}.

Let
d
be a positive integer such that
\epsilon^{2d}={\epsilon^{\prime}}^{2d}=1
.
By
Lemma

2.5
, there exists a polynomial
Q(s,s^{\prime},a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s,\frac{1}{s},s^{\prime},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}},a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}]
such that

P(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\mid Q(s^{d},{s^{\prime}}^{d},a^{2m},b^{2m},{a^{\prime}}^{2m},{b^{\prime}}^{2m}),

Let

\mathcal{P}(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=Q(s^{d},{s^{\prime}}^{d},a^{2m},b^{2m},{a^{\prime}}^{2m},{b^{\prime}}^{2m}).

Note that
|a_{p}|^{2m}=a_{p}^{2m}
and similarly for
b_{p}
,
a_{p}^{\prime}
and
b_{p}^{\prime}
. Thus,

\mathcal{P}(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-3},a_{p},b_{p},a_{p}^{\prime},b_{p}^{\prime})=Q(p^{d(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)},p^{d(k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3)},a_{p}^{2m},b_{p}^{2m},{a_{p}^{\prime}}^{2m},{b_{p}^{\prime}}^{2m}),

and the right-hand side is divisible by

P(p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3},|a_{p}|,|b_{p}|,|a_{p}^{\prime}|,|b_{p}^{\prime}|),

which is
0
for a positive proportion of
p
.

If
\mathcal{P}
has a common factor
S(s,s^{\prime})
with
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
, then we can replace
\mathcal{P}
with
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}/S
. Since we chose
P
to be coprime to
s^{\kappa}-s^{\kappa^{\prime}}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is still divisible by
P
, and thus satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.2
. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let

P(x,y)\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[x,y]
be a non-zero polynomial. Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density

Then

and there is Dirichlet character

.

Proof.

By

2.5
, there exists a polynomial

.

Let

.
We see that

\mathcal{P}(p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},p^{k_{1}^{\prime}+k_{2}^{\prime}-3},|a_{p}|,|b_{p}|,|a_{p}^{\prime}|,|b_{p}^{\prime}|)=Q(|\lambda_{p}|^{2},|\lambda^{\prime}_{p}|^{2})=0

for a positive proportion of

. The result follows from
Theorem

2.7
.
∎

Taking

, we deduce the following corollary, which generalises
[
44
]
*Thm. 1.1 to the case of Siegel modular forms:

Corollary 2.9
.

Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density, we have

. Then

.

2.4.
Distinguishing eigenforms by their Sato–Tate angles

Suppose that the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
f
and
f^{\prime}
. This conjecture is known when the weights
k_{2}
and
k^{\prime}_{2}
are at least
3

[
42
]
*Thm. I, but is open in the case that
k_{2}
and
k_{2}^{\prime}
are equal to
2
. Under this assumption, the normalised Hecke eigenvalues satisfy

\lambda_{p},\lambda_{p}^{\prime}\in[-4,4]

for each prime
p\nmid NN^{\prime}
, so we can find
\theta_{p},\theta_{p}^{\prime}\in[0,\pi]
such that

4\cos\theta_{p}=\lambda_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad 4\cos\theta^{\prime}_{p}=\lambda^{\prime}_{p}.

As an application of
Theorem

2.4
, we show in the following result that
\theta_{p}
and
\theta_{p}^{\prime}
satisfy a linear relation for a positive density of primes
p
if and only if
f
and
f^{\prime}
are twists of each other. This result generalises
[
12
]
*Thm. 2, which in turn builds on earlier work of Murty–Pujahari
[
23
]
. Their result addresses the case of elliptic modular forms with
m=1
and
n=\pm 1
, and relies on the Sato–Tate conjecture. In contrast, our proof does not rely on the Sato–Tate conjecture, which is open in the case of Siegel modular forms.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}
. Assume that the set

\left\{p:m\theta_{p}+n\theta_{p}^{\prime}=\alpha\right\}

has positive density. Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there exists a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Proof.

Suppose that
m\theta_{p}+n\theta_{p}^{\prime}=\alpha
. Then, taking cosines of both sides, there is a polynomial
P\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[c,c^{\prime},s,s^{\prime},a]
such that

P(\cos\theta_{p},\cos\theta_{p}^{\prime},\sin\theta_{p},\sin\theta_{p}^{\prime},\cos\alpha)=0.

By
Lemma

2.5
, we can find a polynomial
Q\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[c,c^{\prime},s,s^{\prime},a]
such that

Q(\cos\theta_{p},\cos\theta_{p}^{\prime},\sin^{2}\theta_{p},\sin^{2}\theta_{p}^{\prime},\cos\alpha)=0.

In particular, we have

Q(\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda_{p},\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda_{p}^{\prime},1-(\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda_{p})^{2},1-(\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda_{p}^{\prime})^{2},\cos\alpha)=0.

Since
Q
has coefficients in
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
and
\lambda_{p}
and
\lambda_{p}^{\prime}
are algebraic, it follows that
\cos\alpha
is algebraic too. Thus, there is a non-zero polynomial
\mathcal{P}(x,y)\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[x,y]
such that

\mathcal{P}(\lambda_{p},\lambda_{p}^{\prime})=0,

and this polynomial is independent of
p
. The result follows from
Theorem

2.4
.
∎

2.5.
Distinguishing eigenforms by their Satake parameters

For each prime
p\nmid N
, let
\{\beta_{1,p},\beta_{2,p},\beta_{3,p},\beta_{4,p}\}
be the roots of the Hecke polynomial

x^{4}-a_{p}x^{3}+\left(a_{p}^{2}-a_{p^{2}}-\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-4}\right)x^{2}-a_{p}\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3}x+\epsilon(p^{4})p^{2(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)}

corresponding to
f
, and for each
i
, let

\overline{\beta}_{i,p}=\frac{\beta_{i,p}}{p^{(k_{1}+k_{2}-3)/2}}.

Then the
\overline{\beta}_{i,p}
are the Satake parameters of the transfer of the automorphic representation
\pi
to
\GL_{4}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
after twisting to make the representation unitary.
The symmetries of the Hecke polynomial ensure that the roots can be ordered so that

\beta_{1,p}\beta_{4,p}=\beta_{2,p}\beta_{3,p}=\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3}

and hence that

\overline{\beta}_{1,p}\overline{\beta}_{4,p}=\overline{\beta}_{2,p}\overline{\beta}_{3,p}=\epsilon(p^{2}).

In particular, the
\beta_{i,p}
are completely determined by
\beta_{1,p}
and
\beta_{2,p}
, and this choice is unique up to the symmetries
\beta_{1,p}\leftrightarrow\beta_{2,p}
and
\beta_{i,p}\leftrightarrow\beta_{5-i,p}=\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3}/\beta_{i,p}
. Let
\beta_{i,p},\beta_{i,p}^{\prime}
denote the corresponding Satake parameters for
f^{\prime}
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
P(s,s^{\prime},x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1},{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},{x_{2}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}]

be a polynomial such that:

•

P
is symmetric in
(x_{1},x_{2})
and in
(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})
.

•

P
is independent under each of the substitutions
x_{i}\mapsto s/x_{i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto s^{\prime}/x_{i}^{\prime}
.

•

P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
.

Suppose that for a set of primes
p
of positive upper density,

P(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-3},\beta_{1,p},\beta_{2,p},\beta_{1,p}^{\prime},\beta_{2,p}^{\prime})=0.

Then
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})
and there is Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi
.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=x_{1}+s/x_{1}+x_{2}+s/x_{2},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}/s+x_{1}/x_{2}+x_{2}/x_{1}+s/x_{1}x_{2}+1,

\displaystyle a^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{1}+s^{\prime}/x^{\prime}_{1}+x^{\prime}_{2}+s^{\prime}/x^{\prime}_{2},

\displaystyle b^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{1}x^{\prime}_{2}/s^{\prime}+x^{\prime}_{1}/x^{\prime}_{2}+x^{\prime}_{2}/x^{\prime}_{1}+s^{\prime}/x^{\prime}_{1}x^{\prime}_{2}+1.

The symmetry assumptions on
P
ensure that there is a polynomial
R\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
such that

P(s,s^{\prime},x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})=R(s,s^{\prime},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}).

Indeed, by the invariances under
x_{i}\mapsto s/x_{i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto s^{\prime}/x_{i}^{\prime}
,
P
can be written as a polynomial in
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},u_{1},u_{2},u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}]
, where
u_{i}=x_{i}+s/x_{i}
and
u_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}^{\prime}+s^{\prime}/x_{i}^{\prime}
. The symmetries in
(x_{1},x_{2})
and in
(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})
then become symmetries in
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
(u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime})
, so by the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials,
P
can be written as a polynomial in
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},u_{1}+u_{2},u_{1}u_{2},u_{1}^{\prime}+u_{2}^{\prime},u_{1}^{\prime}u_{2}^{\prime}]
, or equivalently, as a polynomial
R
in
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
.
Moreover, since
P
is coprime to
s^{\kappa}-{s^{\prime}}^{\kappa^{\prime}}
, so is
R
.

Let

(s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime})=(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-3}).

Observe that for every
p
, we have

a_{p}=\beta_{1,p}+\beta_{2,p}+s_{p}/\beta_{1,p}+s_{p}/\beta_{2,p},

b_{p}=\beta_{1,p}\beta_{2,p}/s_{p}+\beta_{1,p}/\beta_{2,p}+\beta_{2,p}/\beta_{1,p}+s_{p}/\beta_{1,p}\beta_{2,p}+1,

and similarly for
a_{p}^{\prime}
and
b_{p}^{\prime}
.
Hence,

P(s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime},\beta_{1,p},\beta_{2,p},\beta_{1,p}^{\prime},\beta_{2,p}^{\prime})=R(s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime},a_{p},b_{p},a_{p}^{\prime},b_{p}^{\prime})

and the result follows from
Theorem

1.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Assume that
\epsilon
and
\epsilon^{\prime}
are either trivial or quadratic characters. Let
P(x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1},{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},{x_{2}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}]

P

P

Remark 2.14
.

It is possible to formulate a statement of
Corollary

2.13
that applies to Siegel modular forms with arbitrary characters, however, the statement will be messier, due to the fact that
\overline{\beta}_{1,p}\overline{\beta}_{4,p}=\epsilon(p^{2})

.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.5
, there exists a polynomial

Q

satisfies the symmetry hypotheses. Define

\mathcal{P}(s,s^{\prime},x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[s^{\pm 1},{s^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1},{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},{x_{2}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}]

\mathcal{P}(s,s^{\prime},x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},s,s^{\prime})=Q(x_{1}^{2}/s,x_{2}^{2}/s,{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}/{s^{\prime}},{x_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}/{s^{\prime}}).

\mathcal{P}
satisfies all the symmetry and invariance conditions of

. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}
is coprime to

. Indeed, if
\mathcal{P}
had a common factor with

, then
\mathcal{P}(s^{2},{s^{\prime}}^{2},x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})

and by the construction in

, the right-hand side is a product of the polynomials
P(\zeta_{1}x_{1}/s,\zeta_{2}x_{2}/s,\zeta_{1}^{\prime}x_{1}^{\prime}/s,\zeta_{2}^{\prime}x_{2}^{\prime}/s)
, for
\zeta_{i},\zeta_{i}^{\prime}\in\mu_{2d}
. The change of variables

and
x_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto s^{\prime}x_{i}^{\prime}
shows that each of these polynomials is coprime to

.

For each prime

, we have

\overline{\beta}_{i,p}^{2}=\frac{\beta_{i,p}^{2}}{s_{p}}\quad\text{ and }\quad(\overline{\beta}^{\prime}_{i,p})^{2}=\frac{(\beta^{\prime}_{i,p})^{2}}{{s^{\prime}_{p}}},

where

(s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime})=(\epsilon(p^{2})p^{k_{1}+k_{2}-3},\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2})p^{k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}-3}).

Hence,

\mathcal{P}(s_{p},s_{p}^{\prime},\beta_{1,p},\beta_{2,p},\beta_{1,p}^{\prime},\beta_{2,p}^{\prime})=Q(\overline{\beta}_{1,p}^{2},\overline{\beta}_{2,p}^{2},\overline{\beta}_{1,p}^{\prime 2},\overline{\beta}_{2,p}^{\prime 2})=0

for a positive proportion of primes and the result follows from
Theorem

2.12
.

. Then for a set of primes of density

(i)

The Satake parameters

If

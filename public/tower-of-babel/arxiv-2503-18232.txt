Abstract
. We employ the Riesz transform as a means for describing geometric properties of sets in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , and study the extent to which they can be used to characterize function spaces defined on said sets. In particular, characterizations of the end-point spaces on the Lebesgue scale L p L^{p} with 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , namely the Hardy space H 1 H^{1} and the John-Nirenberg space BMO, are produced in terms of the Riesz transforms on Ahlfors regular sets in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} with small oscillations (quantified in terms of the BMO nature of the outward unit normal). These generalize the celebrated results of C. Fefferman and E. Stein in the flat Euclidean setting.

Introduction

The conventional wisdom is that the Riesz transforms are sensitive to regularity (broadly understood),
in a retrievable manner. This feature makes the Riesz transforms useful tools both for studying the geometry
of subsets of the Euclidean ambient and for characterizing the regularity of functions defined on said sets.

To illustrate this philosophy, let us first elaborate on the role the Riesz transforms play in connection with
geometric measure theory. Bring in a brand of Riesz transforms whose definition places less stringent demands on
the underlying set. Specifically, given a closed Ahlfors regular set
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
along with some
exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
, denote by
{\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)
the space of Hölder functions of order

\alpha
with compact support in
\Sigma
. This is a Banach space, and we let
\big({\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\big)^{\ast}

stand for its dual. Then, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, one defines the
j
-th distributional Riesz transform as the operator

{\mathbb{R}}_{j}:{\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\big({\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)\big)^{\ast}

(1.1)

with the property that for every
f,g\in{\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\alpha}(\Sigma)
one has

\big\langle{\mathbb{R}}_{j}f,g\big\rangle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{n}}\big[f(y)g(x)-f(x)g(y)\big]\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}(x)

(1.2)

where, in this context,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the natural pairing between the spaces

\big({\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\,\alpha}(\Sigma)\big)^{\ast}
and
{\mathscr{C}}_{c}^{\,\alpha}(\Sigma)
.

This variety of Riesz transforms turns out to encode a surprising amount of geometric information,
and may be used to characterize the regularity of the underlying set. One example stems from the work
of F. Nazarov, X. Tolsa, A. Volberg from
[
28
]
. In terms of the Riesz transforms (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
the main result in
[
28
]
may be rephrased as follows: under the background assumption that
\Sigma
is an Ahlfors
regular subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and with
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\Sigma
, one has

\Sigma
is a UR (aka, uniformly rectifiable; cf. Definition
2.10
) set if and only if

{\mathbb{R}}_{j}1\in{\rm BMO}(\Sigma,\sigma)
for every
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

(1.3)

Hence, within the class of Ahlfors regular subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the lone membership
of the
{\mathbb{R}}_{j}1
’s to the John-Nirenberg space
{\rm BMO}
characterizes uniform
rectifiability (this refines earlier work of G. David and S. Semmes; cf.
[
4
]
).

{\mathbb{R}}_{j}1\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\,\text{ for }\,1\leq j\leq n.

(1.4)

Proof.

This has been proved in

(1.5)

Proof.

This follows by combining

If

(1.7)

In addition to the “distributional” Riesz transforms (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), one can also define
“principal-value” Riesz transforms on a UR set
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
These are the singular integral operators
\{R_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
where, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
each function
f\in L^{1}\big(\Sigma,\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(x)}{1+|x|^{n-1}}\big)
, and
for
{\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}
-a.e.

R_{j}f(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{2}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Sigma\\
|x-y|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{n}}f(y)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}(y).

(1.8)

Regarding the relationship between the two brands of Riesz transforms considered above, it is instructive to note that

[

if

R_{j}1={\mathbb{R}}_{j}1

.

(1.9)

In the case when
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an unbounded UR set, one may alter the definition in (

) as to

R^{{}^{\rm mod}}_{j}f(x):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma}\big\{k_{j,\varepsilon}(x-y)-k_{j,1}(-y)\big\}f(y)\,d\sigma(y),

When

\displaystyle(R_{j}f)(x^{\prime}):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{2}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\\

for

In a nutshell, the Riesz transforms characterize the Hardy space
H^{1}
. One direction in which this result has been extended is
understanding what other classes of operators may be used in place of the Riesz transforms. This question was asked by C. Fefferman

[

The goal here is to answer this question in relation to (

First, we have the following analogue of (
1.13
) on bounded “infinitesimally flat surfaces” which refines
the result proved by E. Fabes and C. Kenig in
[

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an infinitesimally flat
AR
domain and
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
,
then the Hardy space
H^{1}
on
\partial\Omega

(
regarded as a space of homogeneous type when equipped with the doubling
measure
\sigma
and the Euclidean distance; cf., e.g.,
[
1
]
)
may be described as follows:

H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)=\big\{f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma):\,R_{j}f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\,\text{ for }\,1\leq j\leq n\big\}.

(1.18)

Theorem
1.4
is in fact implied by a more general result established in the body of this paper,
specifically Theorem
3.5
, which encompasses a variety of related corollaries that are of independent interest.
For example, in the same geometric setting as in Theorem
1.4
, for any family of functions

f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
one has:

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)$ if and only if}\\[0.0pt]
\text{$\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}f_{j}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)$ and $R_{j}f_{k}-R_{k}f_{j}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)$
for $1\leq j<k\leq n$}.\end{array}

(1.19)

Indeed, this readily follows from Theorem
3.5
applied to the Clifford algebra-valued function

f:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}e_{j}
. The aforementioned characterization of
H^{1}
for families of functions is new even when

\Omega
is the unit ball.

Another way of understanding Theorem
1.4
is through the lens of mapping properties of
principal-value integral operators of convolution type

T_{k}f(x):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Sigma\\
|x-y|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}k(x-y)f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad f\in L^{1}(\Sigma,\sigma),\quad x\in\Sigma,

(1.20)

where
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a compact UR set, and

is odd and positive homogeneous of degree
1-n
.
The fact that each
T_{k}
maps
L^{1}(\Sigma,\sigma)
into
L^{1,\infty}(\Sigma,\sigma)
(cf.
[
23
, (2.3.19), p. 350]
)
invites the question of identifying the largest subspace of
L^{1}(\Sigma,\sigma)
which is mapped by any such
T_{k}
into

L^{1}(\Sigma,\sigma)
. What Theorem
1.4
implies (together with the fact that each

into

) is that the answer to this question
is the Hardy space
H^{1}(\Sigma,\sigma)
if
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega
with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
an infinitesimally flat
AR domain. Succinctly put,

satisfies a natural “maximality” property, when considered on compact
infinitesimally flat surfaces.

Second, we have the following analogue of (

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an infinitesimally flat
AR

and

, then the John-Nirenberg space

on

{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)=\Big\{f_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}f_{j}:\,f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\Big\}.

(1.21)

Here is another perspective on the above result. Given a compact UR set
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, a natural question
is to determine the smallest enlargement of

to a space into which all singular integral operators as

) take values when acting from

an infinitesimally flat AR domain.
Hence,
{\rm BMO}
satisfies a natural “minimality” property, when considered on compact infinitesimally flat surfaces.

sufficiently small (relative to the dimension

Ahlfors regular domains satisfying (

-AR domains (cf. Definition

).
They have been introduced and studied in

]
and

where it has been shown that condition (

See
[
25

[

be locally the graphs of functions; in particular, there are

[
25

[

In the bigger picture, the work undertaken here contributes a new perspective to the timeless question of determining the type of geometry

In general, UR sets can be quite wild, e.g., may have infinitely many spirals,

According to a deep result of G. David and S. Semmes (cf.

given a closed set

singular integral operator

, for

, is bounded on

with norm majorized by a constant independent of

, if the kernel

is odd and satisfies

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}|x|^{n-1+\ell}|(\nabla^{\ell}k)(x)|<+\infty

for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

(2.32)

In a nutshell, the category of UR sets constitutes the most general geometric setting where
the theory of Calderón-Zygmund operators is comparable in power and scope with its classical
counterpart in the entire Euclidean setting.

The

3.

We begin by establishing a foundational comparison result, which refines work by E. Fabes and C. Kenig in

This may be thought of as a global, geometrically inclusive, version of a well-known local characterization of subharmonicity.

Proposition 3.1

(

Abbreviate

be
a harmonic function in

a continuous subharmonic function

in

{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}w\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)

and
§
§

§

(3.1)

In the case when
\Omega
is an exterior domain

¶

¶

i.e., the complement of a compact set
also assume

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{the function $w$ satisfies $w(x)=o(1)$ as $|x|\to\infty$, and}\\[6.0pt]

\text{while in the case when $n\geq 3$ one assumes $u(x)=O(|x|^{2-n})$ as $|x|\to\infty$}.\end{array}

Proof.

G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})\Big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{\varepsilon}\big(\nabla G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})\big),\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{\varepsilon}\big(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})\big)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\\[4.0pt]

currently played by
v
) and gives that
0\leq w\leq v
in
\Omega
.
Consequently,

0\leq{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}w\leq{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}v\,\,\text{ at every point on }\,\,\partial\Omega.

(3.75)

In concert with the estimate in (
3.72
), naturally accompanying (
3.71
),
and the knowledge that the function
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u
is nonnegative and
\sigma
-measurable on

\partial\Omega
(cf.
[
21
, Proposition 8.2.3, pp. 684–685]
), this permits us to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\big[{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\big(|u|^{\theta}\big)\big]^{1/\theta}\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}\big[{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}w\big]^{1/\theta}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\big[{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}v\big]^{1/\theta}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|^{1/\theta}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=C\int_{\partial\Omega}\big|u\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\big|\,d\sigma<+\infty,

(3.76)

for some finite constant
C>0
independent of the function
u
.
This finishes the proof of the left-pointing implication in the first line
of (
3.64
), in a quantitative sense.

Let us record our progress. At this point, the equivalence in the first line of (
3.64
) and
the first norm-equivalence in (
3.65
) have been established. Granted these, all other claims
follow upon recalling from
[
22
, (10.1.103)-(10.1.104), p. 625]
that
if
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an Ahlfors regular domain,
\nu
denotes its geometric measure theoretic outward unit normal,

\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
is the surface measure on
\partial\Omega
, and
if
F\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\,\infty}(\Omega)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

is a function which, for some
\kappa>0
, satisfies (with
D
denoting the associated Dirac operator)

\begin{array}[]{c}DF=0\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\Omega,\,\,\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}F\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\,\,\text{ and}\\[4.0pt]
F\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\,\,\text{ exists (in ${\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}$) at $\sigma$-a.e. point on }\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{array}

(3.77)

then

\begin{array}[]{c}\nu\odot\big(F\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}\,\,\text{ and}\\[6.0pt]
\big\|\nu\odot\big(F\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\big)\big\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}}\leq C\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}F\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)},\end{array}

(3.78)

for some
C\in(0,\infty)
independent of
F
. Here, we have also used that

H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\,\,\text{ continuously},

(3.79)

and that

(cf. (

)).

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open set of locally finite perimeter, let
\nu
be its geometric
measure theoretic outward unit normal, canonically identified with a Clifford algebra-valued function on
\partial\Omega

as in (
3.62
), and set
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\,\partial\Omega
.
Define the action of the (boundary-to-domain) Cauchy-Clifford
integral operator
{\mathcal{C}}
on any

f\in L^{1}\big(\partial_{\ast}\Omega,\frac{\sigma(x)}{1+|x|^{n-1}}\big)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
according to

{\mathcal{C}}f(x):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\partial_{\ast}\Omega}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n}}\odot\nu(y)\odot f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega,

(3.80)

and define the action of its principal-value (or, boundary-to-boundary) version
{\mathfrak{C}}
on
f
, at

\sigma
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega

{\mathfrak{C}}f(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\partial_{\ast}\Omega\\
|x-y|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n}}\odot\nu(y)\odot f(y)\,d\sigma(y).

(3.81)

A key feature of (
3.80
) is that for each function

f\in L^{1}\big(\partial_{\ast}\Omega,\frac{\sigma(x)}{1+|x|^{n-1}}\big)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

{\mathcal{C}}f\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(\Omega)\,\,\text{ and }\,\,D({\mathcal{C}}f)=0\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\Omega.

(3.82)

Moreover, under the assumption that actually
\partial\Omega
is a UR set,
for each aperture parameter
\kappa>0
one has (cf.
[
23
, (2.4.10), p. 384]
)

\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\big({\mathcal{C}}f\big)\in L^{1,\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\,\,\text{ and}\\[4.0pt]
\big\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\big({\mathcal{C}}f\big)\big\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial_{\ast}\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}}\end{array}

(3.83)

for each
f\in L^{1}(\partial_{\ast}\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
, for some constant
C=C(\Omega,\kappa)\in(0,\infty)
independent of
f
,
and the nontangential boundary trace formula

\big({\mathcal{C}}f\big)\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}=\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\,\,\text{ at $\sigma$-a.e. point on }\,\,\partial_{\ast}\Omega

(3.84)

holds for all
f\in L^{1}\big(\partial_{\ast}\Omega,\frac{\sigma(x)}{1+|x|^{n-1}}\big)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

(cf.
[
24
, Example 1.4.12, pp. 62–63]
and
[
24
, (1.5.20), p. 89]
).

The Clifford algebra formalism also allows us to consider the Riesz transforms bundled together, into a single entity, we
call the boundary-to-domain

-
Riesz

transform
. Its action on

f\in L^{1}\big(\partial\Omega\,,\,\frac{\sigma(x)}{1+|x|^{n-1}}\big)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
is defined as

{\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell}f(x):=\frac{2}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n}}\odot f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega.

(3.85)

It has been shown in
[

that whenever
\Omega

, the Clifford-Riesz transform

satisfies

\big\|{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\big({\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell}f\big)\big\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}}

(3.86)

for all
f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
, for some

C=C(\Omega,\kappa)\in(0,\infty)
independent of
f
.

Our next result indicates that, within the context of infinitesimally flat AR domains, if a Clifford algebra-valued function
leads to a better membership than predicted in (
3.83
) it necessarily belongs to a smaller space, specifically
H^{1}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an infinitesimally flat
AR
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\nu
its geometric measure
theoretic outward unit normal, canonically identified with a Clifford algebra-valued function on
\partial\Omega

as in (
3.62
), and set
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
. Also, fix some
aperture parameter
\kappa>0
. Then for any function
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

one has

{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}({\mathcal{C}}f)\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\Longleftrightarrow\nu\odot f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.87)

in a quantitative fashion.

Proof.

To get started, observe that the left-pointing implication in (
3.87
) is a consequence
of (
3.80
), (
3.85
), and (
3.86
). There remains to
establish that, given
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

with
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}({\mathcal{C}}f)\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
, we
necessarily have
\nu\odot f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
.
To this end, define

\Omega_{+}:=\Omega\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\Omega_{-}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega},

(3.88)

and recall from Theorem
2.13
that
\Omega_{-}
is also an infinitesimally flat AR domain whose
boundary is
\partial\Omega
, and whose geometric measure theoretic outward unit normal is
-\nu
. If we define

u_{\pm}(x):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n}}\odot\nu(y)\odot f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega_{\pm},

(3.89)

then from (
3.82
), the working assumptions, (
3.83
), and (
3.84
) we have

\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\pm}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\,\infty}(\Omega)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},\quad Du_{\pm}=0\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\Omega_{\pm},\\[4.0pt]
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{+}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma),\quad{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{-}\in L^{1,\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma),\\[4.0pt]
u_{\pm}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}=\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\,\,\text{ at $\sigma$-a.e. point on $\partial\Omega$},\\[4.0pt]
\text{and $u_{\pm}(x)=o(1)$ as $|x|\to\infty$ if $\Omega_{\pm}$ is an exterior domain}.\end{array}

(3.90)

In particular,
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{+}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
implies that

u_{+}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.91)

Based on this, (
3.90
), and Proposition
3.2
we then see that

\nu\odot\Big(\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)=\nu\odot\Big(u_{+}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\Big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.92)

Also, the first jump-formula in (
3.90
) and (
3.91
) imply

(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f=u_{+}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

(3.93)

which (in light of the last jump-formula in (
3.90
)) further entails

u_{-}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}=\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f=\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f-f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.94)

In turn, based on (
3.94
) and Proposition
3.2
we conclude that

\nu\odot\Big(\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)=\nu\odot\Big(u_{-}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\Big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.95)

Ultimately, from (
3.92
) and (
3.95
) we see that

\nu\odot f=\nu\odot\Big(\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)-\nu\odot\Big(\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.96)

plus a naturally accompanying estimate.
∎

Here is a version of Theorem
3.3
which features the principal-value Cauchy-Clifford
integral operator in place of its boundary-to-domain version, appearing in (
3.87
).
Specifically, while ordinarily

maps

into
L^{1,\infty}
, if a function in
L^{1}
is mapped by

{\mathfrak{C}}
into
L^{1}
then necessarily the function actually belongs to the Hardy space
H^{1}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an infinitesimally flat
AR
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\nu
its geometric
measure theoretic outward unit normal, canonically identified with a Clifford algebra-valued function on
\partial\Omega

as in (
3.62
), and set
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
. Then
for each function
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
one has

{\mathfrak{C}}f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}\Longleftrightarrow\nu\odot f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.97)

in a quantitative fashion.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
be such that

\nu\odot f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
. Introduce
\Omega_{\pm}
as in (
3.88
)
and define
u_{\pm}
as in (
3.89
). From (
3.86
) we see that

{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{\pm}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
which, together with (
3.84
), implies

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{C}}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f+\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big\}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big\{u_{+}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}+u_{-}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\big\}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.98)

as desired. In the opposite direction, let
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
be such that

{\mathfrak{C}}f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
. Then

\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.99)

With
\Omega_{\pm}
as in (
3.88
) and
u_{\pm}
as in (
3.89
), we then have

\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\pm}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\,\infty}(\Omega)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},\quad Du_{\pm}=0\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\Omega_{\pm},\\[4.0pt]
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{+}\in L^{1,\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma),\quad{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u_{-}\in L^{1,\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma),\\[4.0pt]
u_{\pm}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}=\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},\\[4.0pt]
\text{and $u_{\pm}(x)=o(1)$ as $|x|\to\infty$ if $\Omega_{\pm}$ is an exterior domain},\end{array}

(3.100)

thanks to (
3.82
), (
3.84
), and (
3.83
).
Granted these properties, Proposition
3.2
applies and gives that

\nu\odot\Big(\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)=\nu\odot\big(u_{\pm}\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial\Omega}\big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

(3.101)

plus natural estimates. As a consequence,

\nu\odot f=\nu\odot\Big(\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)-\nu\odot\Big(\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}I+{\mathfrak{C}}\big)f\Big)\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}

(3.102)

in a quantitative fashion.
∎

Moving on, recall the “principal-value” Riesz transforms associated with a given UR set as in (
1.8
).
One of the principal results for the portion of the present work pertaining to domains with compact boundaries is the following
theorem asserting that the Riesz transforms characterize the Hardy space
H^{1}
in the more inclusive Clifford algebra formalism.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an infinitesimally flat
AR
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
set
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
. Consider the Riesz transforms

\{R_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
associated with the
UR
set
\partial\Omega
as in (
1.8
)
and glue them together into a singular integral operator acting on functions
in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
according to

R:={\mathbf{e}}_{1}\odot R_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbf{e}}_{n}\odot R_{n}.

(3.103)

Then the following statements are equivalent
(
in a natural quantitative fashion
)
:

(1)

f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
and

R_{j}f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
for
1\leq j\leq n
;

(2)

f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
and

Rf\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
;

(3)

f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}
.

Proof.

That (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is clear from (
3.103
). To show that
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3), denote by
\nu
the geometric measure theoretic outward
unit normal to
\Omega
, canonically identified with a Clifford algebra-valued function
on
\partial\Omega
as in (
3.62
), and consider a function
f
as in (2).
If we define

g:=\nu\odot f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.104)

then
\nu\odot g=-f
(since
\nu\odot\nu=-1
, as seen from (
2.39
)) hence

{\mathfrak{C}}g=\tfrac{1}{2}R(\nu\odot g)=-\tfrac{1}{2}Rf\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n}.

(3.105)

Granted (
3.104
)-(
3.105
), Theorem
3.4
applies and gives that

f=-\nu\odot g\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\!\ell_{n},

(3.106)

proving (3). Finally, that (3)
\Rightarrow
(1) is seen from (
3.79
)
and
[
23
, Theorem 2.3.2, pp. 348–357, item (6)]
.
∎

When specialized to scalar-valued functions, Theorem
3.5
shows that

assuming
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an infinitesimally flat AR domain
and
\sigma={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
, then for each function
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)

one has
R_{j}f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}

if and only if the function
f
belongs to the Hardy space
H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
.

(3.107)

This proves (
1.18
). In concert with the Hahn-Banach Theorem and the duality result

\big(H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\big)^{\ast}={\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)

(3.108)

(cf.
[
22
, Theorem 4.6.1, pp. 182–184]
),
this characterization implies the following version of the classical Fefferman-Stein representation theorem (cf.
[
11
, Theorem 3, p. 145]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an infinitesimally flat
AR
domain
and set
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{\,n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
. Then each

f\in{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
may be expressed as

f=f_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}f_{j}\,\,\text{ on }\,\,\partial\Omega,

(3.109)

for some
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
with

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}\leq C\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}

(3.110)

where the constant
C\in(0,\infty)
is independent of
f
.

Proof.

Take

{\mathscr{V}}:=\underbrace{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\oplus L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}_{\text{$n+1$ copies}},

(3.111)

canonically viewed as a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|(g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\|_{\mathscr{V}}:=\|g_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}.

(3.112)

Let us also define

{\mathscr{W}}:=\big\{(g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in{\mathscr{V}}:\,g_{j}=R_{j}g_{0}\,\text{ for }\,1\leq j\leq n\big\}.

(3.113)

Then
{\mathscr{W}}
is a closed linear subspace of
{\mathscr{V}}
, and (
3.107
) implies that
the linear mapping

H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\ni g_{0}\longmapsto(g_{0},R_{1}g_{0},\dots,R_{n}g_{0})\in{\mathscr{W}}

(3.114)

is a well-defined Banach space isomorphism of
H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
onto
{\mathscr{W}}
.

Consider now an arbitrary function
f\in{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
. In view of (
3.108
)
we may regard
f
as a continuous linear functional
\Lambda:H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
Via (
3.114
), this may be subsequently identified with a continuous linear functional on

{\mathscr{W}}
which, thanks to the Hahn-Banach Theorem, may be further extended to a continuous
linear functional
\widetilde{\Lambda}
on
{\mathscr{V}}
, i.e.,

\widetilde{\Lambda}\in{\mathscr{V}}^{\ast}=\underbrace{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\oplus L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}_{\text{$n+1$ copies}}.

(3.115)

After unraveling notation, this argument proves that there exist functions

f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
with the property that

\Lambda(h)=\int_{\partial\Omega}hf_{0}\,d\sigma-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}(R_{j}h)f_{j}\,d\sigma\,\,\text{ for each }\,\,h\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma),

(3.116)

and

\|\Lambda\|_{(H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma))^{\ast}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)}.

(3.117)

In particular, for each
h\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\subset H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
we may
use the anti-Hermitian character of the Riesz transforms on
\partial\Omega
(cf.
[
23
, (2.3.25), p. 350]
)
to write

\displaystyle\Lambda(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}hf_{0}\,d\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}h(R_{j}f_{j})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}h\Big\{f_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}f_{j}\Big\}\,d\sigma.

(3.118)

In light of the fact that the space
L^{2}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
is a dense subspace of the Hardy space
H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)

(see, e.g.,
[
22
, Proposition 4.4.4, p. 164]
in this regard), this ultimately proves that the functionals

f\equiv\Lambda\in\big(H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\big)^{\ast}

and
f_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}f_{j}\in{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)=\big(H^{1}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)\big)^{\ast}

coincide as functions in the space
{\rm BMO}(\partial\Omega,\sigma)
.
∎

We conclude by giving the proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The left-to-right inclusion follows from Theorem
3.6
, while the right-to-left
inclusion is a consequence of
[
23
, (2.3.39), p. 353]
.

4.
Proofs of Main Results: Domains with Unbounded Boundaries

Compared with our work carried out in §S-3, dealing with domains possessing unbounded boundaries brings in novel difficulties.
As such, a number of tools have to be sharpened while other results need to be work out from scratch. In the latter category we have
the following geometric lemma, which is designed to help correlate the behavior of a function at infinity in the domain with that on the boundary.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an open set with the property that
\partial\Omega
is an unbounded Ahlfors regular set,
and define
\sigma:={\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}\lfloor\partial\Omega
. Fix a reference point

and for each

(4.1)

Then there exist

which depend only on

and the Ahlfors regularity constants of

with the property that for each Lebesgue measurable function

(4.2)

Proof.

With
C_{\partial\Omega}
and

denoting, respectively,

(4.3)

Also, select some aperture parameter
\kappa\in(0,1)
, and pick an arbitrary

.
Elementary geometric considerations then show that

\Gamma_{\kappa}(x)\cap\big[A_{R}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}\big]=\varnothing\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\big[\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)\big],

(4.4)

and that

A_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}\subseteq\Gamma_{\widetilde{\kappa}}(x)\cap B(z,2R)\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,x\in\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2).

(4.5)

Suppose a Lebesgue measurable function

has been given. Then, by (

),

\begin{array}[]{c}|u(y)|\leq\big({\mathcal{N}}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}^{2R}u\big)(x)\,\text{ for all }\,y\in A_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4},\\[4.0pt]
\text{and all }\,\,x\in\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2).\end{array}

Integrating in the variable

with respect to

and in

with respect to

\sigma

\displaystyle\sigma\Big(\Delta(z,MR)

\displaystyle\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)\Big)\cdot\int_{A_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}}|u|\,d{\mathcal{L}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\Big(A_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}\Big)\cdot\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)}{\mathcal{N}}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}^{2R}u\,d\sigma.

(4.7)

Note that, by Ahlfors regularity,

\displaystyle\sigma\Big(\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma\Big(\Delta(z,MR)\Big)-\sigma\Big(\Delta(z,R/2)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq c_{\partial\Omega}(MR)^{n-1}-C_{\partial\Omega}(R/2)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=R^{n-1}\Big\{c_{\partial\Omega}M^{n-1}-C_{\partial\Omega}(1/2)^{n-1}\Big\}

and that

in (

). Since we also have

we conclude from (

(4.10)

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{R}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}}|u|\,d{\mathcal{L}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{2R}}\Big|u\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{A_{R}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}}\Big|\,d{\mathcal{L}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq CR\cdot\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\Big(u\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{A_{R}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}}\Big)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=CR\cdot\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)}{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\Big(u\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{A_{R}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{R/4}}\Big)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq CR\cdot\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R/2)}{\mathcal{N}}^{2R}_{\kappa}u\,d\sigma,

where the first inequality follows from Proposition

and the second equality is a consequence of (

At this stage, the estimate claimed in (

For one of these terms, we once more call upon Lemma

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(y,x_{0})

\displaystyle(\Psi_{R})(y)(\Delta\Phi_{\varepsilon})(y)\eta(y)\,dy\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\varepsilon}}|G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})||\eta|\,d{\mathcal{L}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathcal{N}}^{{\mathcal{O}}_{\varepsilon}\setminus K}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\big(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})\big)\cdot{\mathcal{N}}^{\varepsilon}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\eta\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathcal{N}}^{\Omega\setminus K}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\kappa}}}\big(\nabla(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0}))\big)\cdot{\mathcal{N}}^{\varepsilon}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\eta\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=o(1)\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

To treat the second term alluded to above, we once again rely on Lemma

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R)}{\mathcal{N}}^{{\mathcal{O}}_{2R}\setminus K}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\big(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0})\big)\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\eta\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R)}{\mathcal{N}}^{\Omega\setminus K}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\kappa}}}\big(\nabla(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0}))\big)\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\eta\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Delta(z,MR)\setminus\Delta(z,R)}{\mathcal{N}}^{\Omega\setminus K}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\kappa}}}\big(\nabla(G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{0}))\big)\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{\widetilde{\kappa}}\eta\,d\sigma

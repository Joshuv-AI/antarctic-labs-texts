The spherical Hall algebra of OPEN Spec ⁡ ( 𝒪 K CLOSE ¯ ) \overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K}})
Benjamin Li, Luis Modes

Abstract
We generalize the result of M. Kapranov, O. Schiffmann, and E. Vasserot [ KSV12 ] by showing that, for a number field K K with class number 1, the spherical Hall algebra of Spec ⁡ ( 𝒪 K ) ¯ \overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})} , where 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} is the ring of integers of K K , is isomorphic to the Paley-Wiener shuffle algebra associated to a Hecke L L -function corresponding to K K .

Introduction

M. Kapranov, O. Schiffmann, and E. Vasserot showed in
[
KSV12
]
that the spherical Hall algebra of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}
is isomorphic to the Paley-Wiener shuffle algebra associated to the global Harish-Chandra function
\Phi(s)
, defined as
\Phi(s)=\frac{\zeta^{*}(s)}{\zeta^{*}(s+1)}
, where
\zeta^{*}(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})\zeta(s)
is the completed zeta function.

In order to define the Hall algebra of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}
,
vector bundles on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}
are defined as triples
E=(L,V,q)
where
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
L\subset V
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice of maximal rank, and
q
is a positive-definite quadratic form. The
rank of
E
is defined as
\operatorname{rk}(E)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(V)=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)
. These vector bundles form a category
{\mathcal{B}un}
. The Hall algebra of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}
is defined as
H=\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}H_{n}
, where
H_{n}=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{Bun}_{n})
and
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
consists of the isomorphism classes of rank
n
vector bundles. For this, it is shown that
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
can be identified with
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/O_{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})/O_{n}\times\prod_{p}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and that it has a
C^{\infty}
-orbifold structure. A
Hall product

*
is constructed and proven to make
H
into a graded associative algebra with unit 1. The
spherical Hall algebra

SH
is defined as the subalgebra generated by
H_{1}\subset H
. It is shown that we can identify
H_{1}
with
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, so
SH
can be seen as a subset of
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
.

On the other hand,
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{M}er(\mathbb{C}^{n})
can be made into a graded associative algebra with unit 1 via the
shuffle product

\,\circledS\,
, defined as

F\,\circledS\,F^{\prime}=\sum_{w\in\mathrm{Sh}(m,n)}w(F\otimes F^{\prime})\cdot\Phi_{w^{-1}}.

Here,
\mathrm{Sh}(m,n)
is the set of
(m,n)
-shuffles, and
\Phi_{w^{-1}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m+n})=\prod_{i<j\text{ and }w(i)>w(j)}\Phi(s_{w(i)}-s_{w(j)})
. The
Paley-Wiener shuffle algebra associated to

\Phi
is denoted by
\mathcal{SH}(\Phi)_{\mathcal{PW}}
and is defined as the subalgebra generated by the subspace
\mathcal{PW}(\mathbb{C})\subset\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{M}er(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of Paley-Wiener functions.

The main result of
[
KSV12
]
shows that
SH\simeq\mathcal{SH}(\Phi)_{\mathcal{PW}}
.

Theorem 0.1
.

The Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}:SH_{1}=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{PW}(\mathbb{C})
extends to an isomorphism of algebras
SH\rightarrow\mathcal{SH}(\Phi)_{\mathcal{PW}}
.

The explicit isomorphism
\operatorname{Ch}:SH\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{SH}(\Phi)_{\mathcal{PW}}
is defined as
\operatorname{Ch}=\mathcal{M}\circ\widetilde{\operatorname{CT}}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the
Mellin transform

\mathcal{M}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\to\mathcal{PW}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
given by
\mathcal{M}(f)(s)=\int_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(a)a^{s}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a}{a}
, and
\widetilde{\operatorname{CT}}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
is a twisted version of the
constant term map

\operatorname{CT}:H_{n}=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/O_{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, which takes an automorphic form on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and yields its constant term.

Note that, in particular,
\mathbb{Z}
is the ring of integers of the field
\mathbb{Q}
. In our paper, we generalize this result for a number field
K
with class number 1, that is, such that its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a principal ideal domain. We follow
[
KSV12
]
closely.

We consider the real and complex Archimedean places
\nu\in\mathcal{S}
of
K
, which correspond to embeddings
\nu:K\to\mathbb{C}
.
From this, we generalize
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
to
\mathcal{O}_{K}\subset R=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}R_{\nu}
, where
R_{\nu}=\mathbb{R}
if
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
R_{\nu}=\mathbb{C}
if
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and the embedding is given by
a\mapsto(\nu(a))_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}
.
In our definition of vector bundles over
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}
,
E=(L,V,q)
, where
V=R^{n}
,
L\subset R^{n}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lattice of maximal rank, and
q=\prod_{\nu}q_{\nu}
, where
q_{\nu}
is a positive-definite quadratic form if
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and a positive-definite Hermitian form if
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
From this, we can define
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
, which will now be identified with

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)/\mathcal{K}_{n}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{n}},

where
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}O_{\nu}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{n}}=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}O_{\nu}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
.
Here,
O_{\nu}
is the orthogonal group for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and the unitary group for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\mathfrak{p}
goes over the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
being the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic integers.
In the same way as before, we define the Hall algebra of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}
, and the spherical Hall algebra
SH
is again defined as the subalgebra generated by
H_{1}\subset H
. However, this time we will be able to identify
SH
as a subset of
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{B}=\mathrm{Bun}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\backslash\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\backslash\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{R}
via taking logarithms.
We know (say, from
[
Mil20
]
) that
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
is (non-canonically) isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}
, where
r_{1}=\#\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
r_{2}=\#\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
are the number of real and complex places, respectively.
From this,

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\backslash\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}/\Lambda)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\simeq(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

As a result, our Fourier transform will now have the form
\mathcal{F}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})\to\mathcal{PW}((\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C})^{n})
, where

\mathcal{F}f(\lambda^{*},s)=\int_{x\in D}f(x){\left\lvert x\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}x^{2\pi i\lambda^{*}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}{{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}}.

Here,
|x|_{\mathcal{B}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}x_{i}\prod_{j=r_{1}+1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}x_{j}^{2}
and
D
is a fundamental domain for
\mathcal{B}^{n}
. We modify the constant term map
\widetilde{\operatorname{CT}}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})
and the homomorphism
\operatorname{Ch}=\mathcal{F}\circ\widetilde{\operatorname{CT}}
accordingly. Instead of getting
\zeta(s)
in our computations, we end up getting a Hecke
L
-function corresponding to
K
(for the definition and properties of this function, see
[
Neu99
]
), which we call
L_{K}(\lambda^{*},s)
, and we define
\Phi_{K}(\lambda^{*},s)
accordingly. After making
\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{M}er((\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C})^{n})
into an algebra via
\,\circledS\,
and defining the
Paley-Wiener shuffle algebra with respect to
\Phi_{K}(\lambda^{*},s)
, which we denote by
\mathcal{SH}(\Phi_{K})_{\mathcal{PW}}
, as the subalgebra generated by
\mathcal{PW}(\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C})
, we prove the following main result.

Theorem 0.2
.

The map
\operatorname{Ch}:SH\rightarrow\mathcal{SH}(\Phi_{K})_{\mathcal{PW}}
is an isomorphism of algebras.

Vector bundles on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}

Let
K
be a number field such that its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a principal ideal domain. In other words, the class number of
K
is 1. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of real and complex Archimedean places of
K
. Recall that a place
\nu\in\mathcal{S}
corresponds to an embedding
\nu:K\to\mathbb{C}
and a norm
|\cdot|_{\nu}:\nu(K)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the sets of real and complex places, respectively, and let
r_{1}=\#\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
r_{2}=\#\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
R=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}R_{\nu}
, where
R_{\nu}=\mathbb{R}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
R_{\nu}=\mathbb{C}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

A
vector bundle of rank
n
over
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}
is a triple
E=(L,V,q)
, where
V=R^{n}
,
L\subset V
is an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lattice of maximal rank (i.e., a free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of rank
n
such the map
L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}R\rightarrow V
induced by the inclusion
L\subset V
is an isomorphism of
R
-modules), and
q=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}q_{\nu}
, where the
q_{\nu}
is positive-definite quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and a positive-definite Hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The
rank
of a vector bundle
E=(L,V,q)
is defined as
\operatorname{rk}(E)=\operatorname{rk}_{R}V=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}L
. For example, if
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
, we have the
trivial bundle

(\mathbb{Z}[i],\mathbb{C},z\overline{z})
, and if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
, we have a vector bundle
(\{(a+b\sqrt{2},a-b\sqrt{2}):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\},\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},x^{2}\times 3x^{2})
. Both of these examples have rank 1.

A
morphism
of vector bundles
f:E=(L,V,q)\to E^{\prime}=(L^{\prime},V^{\prime},q^{\prime})
is an
R
-linear map
f:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that
f(L)\subset L^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}_{\nu}(f(v))\leqslant q_{\nu}(v)
for all
v\in V
and
\nu\in\mathcal{S}
. We say that
f:E\to E^{\prime}
is an
isomorphism
if
f:V\to V^{\prime}
is an
R
-isomorphism,
f(L)=L^{\prime}
, and
q^{\prime}(f(v))=q(v)
for all
v\in V
. As a result, we get a category
{\mathcal{B}un}
where the objects are vector bundles and the morphisms are as previously defined.

1.1
Isomorphism classes of rank
n
vector bundles

Let us consider the class of isomorphism classes of rank
n
vector bundles, which we denote by
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
.
To be able to define functions in
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
, we show that we can identify
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
in the following two ways:

\mathrm{Bun}_{n}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)/\mathcal{K}_{n}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{n}}.

Here,
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}O_{\nu}
, where
O_{\nu}
is the orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}_{n}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}
and the unitary group
\mathrm{U}_{n}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{n}}=\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}O_{\nu}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the set of
\mathfrak{p}
-adic integers. The inclusion
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
is induced from the embedding
\mathcal{O}_{K}\subset R
given by
a\mapsto(\nu(a))_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}
, and the other inclusions come from the natural embeddings as well.

Before proving this statement, let us first exhibit some lemmas.

Lemma 1.1
.

For
(p)=\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, we have

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})A_{n}(p^{\mathbb{Z}})\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}).

Proof.

Pick any
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
We split into several steps.

1.

After left and right multiply by matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
we can assume
g_{11}
has the smallest
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
value.

2.

After right multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
we can assume
g_{1j}=0
for
j>1
.
Note that
g_{11}
still has the smallest
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
value.

3.

Repeat Step 1 and 2 to the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
submatrix (deleting the first row and column) we can assume
g_{2j}=0
for
j>2
; note that
g_{11}
still has the smallest
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
value, while
g_{22}
has the smallest
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
value in the submatrix.

4.

Repeat Step 3 to submatrices we can assume
g
is lower-triangular, with
g_{ii}
having the smallest
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
value in the submatrix
(g_{\ell j})_{\ell,j\geqslant i}
.

5.

After right multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
we can assume
g_{ii}=p^{a_{i}}
for some
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}
.

6.

After left multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
we can assume
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g_{i1})\geqslant a_{n}
.

7.

Repeat Step 6 to the submatrices we can assume
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g_{ij})\geqslant a_{n}
for
i>j
.

8.

Finally, after right multiply a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
we can assume
g=\operatorname{diag}{\left(p^{a_{1}},\ldots,p^{a_{n}}\right)}
, which finishes the proof.

∎

Corollary 1.2
.

We have

\mathrm{GL}_{n}{\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}\right)}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)\mathrm{GL}_{n}{\left(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\right)},

where
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
is the ring of finite adeles in

and
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}

\mathcal{O}_{K}

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
.

Pick any
g=(g_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f})
, where
g_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})-{\left\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{m}\right\}}
being a finite set.
Let
(p_{i})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
We then do the following procedure.

1.

From Lemma
1.1
we can find
u_{1}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p_{1}])
such that
u_{1}g_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}_{1}})
.

2.

From Lemma
1.1
we can find
u_{2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p_{2}])
such that
u_{2}u_{1}g_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}_{2}})
.

⋮

m.

From Lemma
1.1
we can find
u_{m}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p_{m}])
such that
u_{m}u_{m-1}\cdots u_{1}g_{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}_{m}})
.

Now put
u=u_{m}u_{m-1}\cdots u_{1}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
, then we have
ug_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}_{i}})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
because
\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p_{i}]\subset\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}_{j}}
for

.
For the same reason we have

for all

.

, which finishes the proof.

Now we are at a position to prove the two identifications of
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

We have bijections

\mathrm{Bun}_{n}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)/\mathcal{K}_{n}=GL_{n}(K)\backslash GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{n}}.

Proof.

First, let us show that
\mathrm{Bun}_{n}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)/\mathcal{K}_{n}
. Let
(M_{*}q)(x)=q(Mx)
for any matrix
M
and any quadratic form
q
. If
q_{\text{st}}(x)=x^{T}x
,
q(x)=x^{T}A^{T}Ax=A_{*}q_{\text{st}}
(as any symmetric matrix may be written in the form
A^{T}A
), and
B\in GL_{n}(R)
is a matrix such that

\mathcal{B}^{n}=(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})^{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}^{(r_{1}+r_{2})n}
.

Similarly, we define the inverse Fourier transform to be an operator
\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{PW}((\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C})^{n})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})
as

\mathcal{G}g(r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{(r_{1}+2r_{2})n}}\sum_{\lambda^{*}\in(\Lambda^{*})^{n}}\int_{s\in\sigma_{0}+i\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\lambda^{*},s){\left\lvert r\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}^{-s}r^{-2\pi i\lambda^{*}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Here,
\sigma_{0}
is chosen such that the integral converges.

It can be checked that
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{G}(g)=g
and
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{F}(f)=f
. We also have the following generalization of a classical result.

Proposition 5.1
.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:H_{1}=C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathcal{PW}(\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The proof can be adapted from Vol. II, Thm. IX.11 in
[
RS75
]
.
∎

6
The principal series intertwiners

We define

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}:\Lambda^{*}\times\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(\lambda^{*},s)(a)

\displaystyle={\left\lvert a\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}a^{2\pi i\lambda^{*}}={\left\lvert a\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}\exp(2\pi i{\left\langle\lambda^{*},\log a\right\rangle})).

For
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we define
U_{w}=U\cap(w^{-1}Uw)
and

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{w}:C_{c}^{\infty}(U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{n})

\displaystyle\to C^{\infty}(U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{n})

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{w}(\psi)(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{u\in U_{w}(\mathbb{A}_{K})\setminus U(\mathbb{A}_{K})}\psi(wug)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u,

where we are using the Tamagawa measure on
\mathbb{A}_{K}
.

We will see that
\operatorname{diag}:\mathcal{B}^{n}\to U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is a bijection, and we use a twist to identify their smooth functions together:

\displaystyle\delta^{1/2}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}C^{\infty}(U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\mathcal{K}_{n})

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\left[\operatorname{diag}(a)\mapsto\varphi(a)\delta(a)^{1/2}\right],

where
\delta(a)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\frac{{\left\lvert a_{j}\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}}{{\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert}_{\mathcal{B}}}
.

The fact that
\operatorname{diag}:\mathcal{B}^{n}\to U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is an isomorphism also gives us the following proposition, which can be proved by translating the Hall product formula into group-theoretical terms.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\varphi\in H_{1}^{\otimes n}
,
g\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, and
\widetilde{\varphi}(g)=\varphi(\operatorname{diag}^{-1}(g))\cdot\delta(g)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, then

(*_{1^{n}}(\varphi))(g)=\sum_{\gamma\in B_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\widetilde{\varphi}(\gamma g).

Finally, we define

M_{w}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})

to be the composition

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})\xrightarrow{\delta^{1/2}}C_{c}^{\infty}(U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\mathcal{K}_{n})\xrightarrow{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{w}}C^{\infty}(U(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\mathcal{K}_{n})\xrightarrow{\delta^{-1/2}}C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n}).

Note that we can also apply
M_{w}
to functions in
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}^{n})
that decay fast enough.

One of our main goals is to compute

M_{w}\mathscr{C}(\lambda^{*},s)=M_{w}{\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathscr{C}(\lambda_{i}^{*},s_{i})\right)}

for
\lambda^{*}\in(\Lambda^{*})^{n},s\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

6.1
The diagonal map is bijective

In this subsection, we will prove the following proposition.

Proposition 6.2
.

The natural embedding
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{K}
induces a bijection between sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{n}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})A_{n}(K)\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{K})/K_{n}

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

\displaystyle\mapsto\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\left((\operatorname{id})_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{K}},\operatorname{diag}{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}\right)}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,

where
r_{i}\in\prod_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

To prove this proposition, we need a few lemmas.

Lemma 6.3
.

For prime ideal
(p)=\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})=B_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p])\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}),

where
B_{n}=U_{n}A_{n}
is the set of lower-triangular matrices.

Proof.

Pick
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
We do the following procedure.

1.

After right multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
, we can assume
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g_{11})\leqslant v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g_{1j})
for all
j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
.

2.

After right multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
, we can assume
g_{1j}=0
for
j>1
.

3.

Repeat Step 1 and 2, we can assume
g\in U_{n}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

4.

After right multiply by a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
, we can assume
g_{ii}=p^{a_{i}}
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

5.

After left multiply by a diagonal matrix in
B_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p])
, we can assume
g_{ii}=1
for
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
.

6.

After left multiply by a matrix in
U_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/p])
, we can assume
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g_{i1})\geqslant 0
for
i>1
.

7.

Repeat Step 6, we can assume
g_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
for
i>j
.
Now the resulting matrix is in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})
and we finishes our proof.

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

We have a bijection between sets

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{n}(\mathbb{R})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/O_{n}

\displaystyle{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\operatorname{diag}{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.

Proof.

To see the injectivity, let us say that
{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}
and
{\left(r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n}\right)}
have the same image, i.e., there exist
u\in U_{n}(\mathbb{R}),o\in O_{n}
such that
\operatorname{diag}{\left(r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n}\right)}=u\operatorname{diag}{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}o
.
Then take their image under the map
A\mapsto AA^{T}
gives
\operatorname{diag}{\left((r_{1}^{\prime})^{2},\ldots,(r_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\right)}=u\operatorname{diag}{\left(r^{2}_{1},\ldots,r^{2}_{n}\right)}u^{T}
, or
u^{-1}\operatorname{diag}{\left((r_{1}^{\prime})^{2},\ldots,(r_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\right)}=\operatorname{diag}{\left(r^{2}_{1},\ldots,r^{2}_{n}\right)}u^{T}
.
However, LHS is lower-triangular, while RHS is upper-triangular, so we see both are diagonal, i.e.,
u=\operatorname{id}
, which implies
\operatorname{diag}{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}=\operatorname{diag}{\left(r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n}\right)}
.

Now we show the surjectivity.
We identify
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/O_{n}
as the space of positive definite symmetric forms, say
X
, via the map
A\mapsto AA^{T}
.
Then the action of
U_{n}(\mathbb{R})
on
X
is
u\cdot q=uqu^{T}
.
If the matrix of
q
corresponds to the basis
{\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}}
, then the matrix
uqu^{T}
corresponds to applying
q
to the basis
u(v_{1}\ v_{2}\ \cdots\ v_{n})^{T}
, and we see we can always use such actions to make the basis to be orthogonal, which shows the original map is surjective.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathscr{U}_{n}
be the set of unitary matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then we have a bijection between sets

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{n}(\mathbb{C})\!\setminus\!\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathscr{U}_{n}

\displaystyle{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\operatorname{diag}{\left(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\right)}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.

Proof.

Just mimic Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Now we are at a position to prove Proposition
6.2

6.2

The map is clearly well-defined.
We first show that it is surjective.

6.2
Intertwiner of the exponential function

We have

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{t}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{w}\psi(g)

Lemma 6.11

.

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug^{\prime}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

Lemma 6.14

\displaystyle\Phi_{K,w^{\prime}}(w(z))\Phi_{K,w}(z)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{w_{0}tw}(z)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{w^{\prime}w}(z),

from induction hypothesis and the case when
w^{\prime}
is a transposition of adjacent elements, as desired.
∎

Now for

, let

We are at a position to establish the integration result for the general case.

Proposition 6.15

Let

If

, then

Thus, we assume

.
Then we can write

Then

\displaystyle M_{w}\mathscr{C}(\lambda^{*},s)

\displaystyle=M_{w_{0}}M_{t}(\mathscr{C}(\lambda^{*},s))

\displaystyle=M_{w_{0}}\mathscr{C}(t(\lambda^{*}),t(s))\Phi_{K,t}(\lambda^{*},s)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}(w_{0}^{-1}t(\lambda^{*}),w_{0}^{-1}t(s))\Phi_{K,w_{0}^{-1}}(t(\lambda^{*}),t(s))\Phi_{K,t}(\lambda^{*},s)

from Proposition

The map

\operatorname{Ch}(f)=0

Proposition 7.8

Lemma 7.9

We have a bijection between sets

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{m}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathrm{Sh}(m,n)^{-1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{S}_{m+n}

\displaystyle(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime},w)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{S}_{m}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathrm{Sh}(m,n)^{-1}}}

Lemma 7.10

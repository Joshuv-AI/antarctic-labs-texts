Relative transverse activity as an event classifier to investigate collectivity-like phenomena in proton–proton collisions at the LHC energies
Subhadeep Roy, Sadhana Dash

Abstract
Two-particle number ( R 2 R_{2} ) and transverse-momentum ( P 2 P_{2} ) correlation functions are studied in proton–proton collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV simulated with PYTHIA 8, in the low-transverse-momentum region where soft QCD processes dominate the particle production. Events are classified using the relative transverse activity observable R T R_{\mathrm{T}} , which provides a differential characterization of predominantly soft underlying-event (UE) activity. A finite long-range near-side component emerges in the charge-independent correlator R 2 CI R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}} for UE-dominated events ( 2.5 < R T ≤ 5.0 2.5<R_{\mathrm{T}}\leq 5.0 ), whereas no corresponding long-range structure is observed in the charge-dependent correlators. This behavior suggests that the ridge-like feature is primarily driven by charge-independent QCD dynamics rather than short-range charge-balancing effects. In contrast, the transverse-momentum correlator P 2 CI P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}} remains dominated by localized, jet-like structures across all R T R_{\mathrm{T}} intervals. The near-side correlation peak exhibits a systematic narrowing in Δ ​ φ \Delta\varphi for the charge-independent case with increasing R T R_{\mathrm{T}} , while the charge-dependent correlators remain localized in both Δ ​ η \Delta\eta and Δ ​ φ \Delta\varphi and show only mild broadening. These results indicate that enhanced UE activity can give rise to collectivity-like long-range correlation structures within a non-hydrodynamic framework and provide a PYTHIA baseline for interpreting small-system measurements at the LHC.



I
Introduction

The primary objective of the relativistic heavy-ion collision program is to investigate the properties of the hot and dense quark-gluon plasma (QGP) created in such collisions. Measurements of anisotropic flow and jet quenching have been interpreted as strong evidence for the formation of strongly interacting QGP in relativistic heavy-ion collisions
18
;
17
;
19
;
15
;
10
;
16
. Furthermore, appearance of the long-range, ridge-like correlations has been interpreted as a characteristic manifestation of the QGP formation in heavy-ion collisions. These correlations can be described within hydrodynamic frameworks, and the extracted flow coefficients exhibit scaling behaviors consistent with the picture of a strongly coupled, collectively expanding medium
11
;
3
;
9

Surprisingly, the long-range, ridge-like correlation structures have also been observed in high-multiplicity proton–lead (p–Pb) and proton–proton (pp) collisions
20
;
4
;
1
, systems that were initially considered too small to sustain the formation of a collectively expanding, hydrodynamic medium. These observations have generated significant debate concerning the underlying mechanisms responsible for the emergence of collectivity-like signatures in small collision systems. On one hand, these observations may suggest the emergence of collective dynamics even in systems with limited spatial extent. On the other hand, QCD-driven mechanisms, such as multiple partonic interactions (MPI), color reconnection (CR), and gluon saturation effects are also capable of generating correlation patterns that resemble those typically attributed to hydrodynamic flow
23
;
14
;
22
. Disentangling these competing effects requires precision observables sensitive to the dynamics of particle production across different kinematic and event-activity regimes. The two-particle number correlation function,
R_{2}(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)
, and the two-particle transverse momentum correlation function,
P_{2}(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)
, are particularly well suited for this purpose
24
. The
R_{2}(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)
, probes correlations among particle pairs in terms of multiplicity fluctuations, while the transverse momentum correlation function,
P_{2}(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)
, is sensitive to transverse momentum fluctuations. Measurements of
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
by the ALICE Collaboration in Pb–Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=2.76
TeV
5
revealed pronounced near- and away-side structures, with
P_{2}
consistently narrower than
R_{2}
, reflecting their differing sensitivities to collective flow, jet correlations, and local charge conservation
2
. Simulation studies with AMPT, EPOS, and UrQMD further demonstrated that the relative contributions of hydrodynamic expansion, resonance decays, and jet fragmentation can be disentangled by comparing charge-independent (CI) and charge-dependent (CD) combinations of the
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
correlators
13
. However, while such studies have provided critical information in large systems, the systematic studies of
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
correlations in small systems such as pp collisions remain far less explored.

In pp collisions, particle production arises from the simultaneous interplay of hard partonic scatterings and multiple soft and semi-soft processes, including MPI, initial- and final-state radiation, beam-beam remnants, and CR, resulting in a substantial contribution from underlying-event (UE) activity. These soft and semi-soft QCD processes govern particle production at low transverse momentum (
p_{\rm T}
), the regime where collectivity-like effects are most likely to manifest. Consequently, correlation measurements performed with controlled UE activity are essential for a reliable interpretation of the observed long-range, collective-like signatures and for disentangling the underlying physics mechanisms responsible for their emergence in pp collisions. The relative transverse activity,
R_{\rm T}
, has been proposed as a robust event classifier
21
, capable of distinguishing events characterized by enhanced UE activity from those predominantly driven by hard scattering processes. By providing a normalized, event-by-event measure of UE activity,

R_{\rm T}
facilitates systematic comparisons across event classes with varying relative contributions from soft and hard particle-production mechanisms.

In this work, we present a simulation study of the
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
correlation functions in pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}
= 13 TeV using the PYTHIA 8 event generator, where events are classified according to their relative transverse activity (
R_{\rm T}
) in order to investigate how the CI and CD correlation structures evolve as a function of increasing UE activity. The analysis focuses on final-state charged hadrons within

|\eta|<0.8
and
0.2<p_{\rm T}<2.0
GeV/
c
, a kinematic region
predominantly governed by soft and semi-soft QCD processes. Particular emphasis is placed on the possible emergence of long-range correlation structures in the
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
correlators, as well as on the evolution of their near-side peak widths with increasing UE activity. The differential behavior of their charge-independent (CI) and charge-dependent (CD) components along the longitudinal (
\Delta\eta
) and transverse (
\Delta\varphi
) directions is further exploited to probe the underlying particle-production mechanisms.

II
Relative Transverse activity

A standard approach for UE studies is to identify the charged particle with the highest transverse momentum (
p_{\rm T}
) in the event, referred to as the leading particle. The azimuthal plane is then divided into three regions relative to the leading particle’s azimuthal angle,
\varphi_{\rm lead}
: the
Toward
region (
|\Delta\varphi|<60^{\circ}
), the
Away
region (
|\Delta\varphi|>120^{\circ}
), and the
Transverse
region (
60^{\circ}<|\Delta\varphi|<120^{\circ}
), as illustrated in Fig.
1
. The toward and away regions are dominated by hard-scattering and jet activity, whereas the transverse region is largely sensitive to UE contributions.

XY
) plane, defined relative to the azimuthal direction of the leading charged particle

Measurements show that the charged-particle number density in the transverse region rises steeply with the leading particle
p_{\rm T}
and reaches a plateau for
p_{\rm T}^{\rm lead}\gtrsim 5
GeV/
c

6
. This plateau indicates that, above this scale, the transverse region provides a clean probe of the UE. In this regime, the relative transverse activity,
R_{\rm T}
, is defined as a normalized measure of the charged-particle density in the transverse region
21
:

R_{\rm T}=\frac{N_{\rm T}}{\langle N_{\rm T}\rangle},

(1)

where
N_{\rm T}
is the charged-particle density in the transverse region for a given event, and
\langle N_{\rm T}\rangle
is its average over the full event ensemble. By construction, events with
R_{\rm T}\ll 1
correspond to low UE activity, where particle production is predominantly driven by hard scattering processes, whereas
R_{\rm T}\gg 1
selects events characterized by enhanced UE activity with substantial contributions from soft and semi-soft QCD processes.

In this work,
R_{\rm T}
is determined using charged hadrons within the kinematic acceptance defined by
|\eta|<0.8
and
p_{\rm T}\geq 0.15
GeV/
c
. Furthermore, events are required to contain a leading charged particle with
5\leq p_{\rm T}^{\rm lead}\leq 40
GeV/
c
, thereby restricting the analysis to the transverse plateau region where the UE contribution is well defined. A total of
5\times 10^{8}
minimum-bias events were generated with PYTHIA 8 using the Monash 2013 tune
25
, a globally optimized parameter set constrained by LHC data for an improved description of soft-QCD and underlying-event observables. Of these, approximately
5\times 10^{6}
events satisfy the above selection criteria, ensuring sufficient statistical precision across all
R_{\rm T}
intervals. The resulting
R_{\rm T}
distribution for pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV is shown in Fig.
2
, with a mean transverse charged-particle multiplicity of
\langle N_{\rm T}\rangle=7.39
. To suppress potential contamination from wide-angle final-state radiation, the analysis is restricted to events with
R_{\rm T}\leq 5
. Within this range, events are categorized into four
R_{\rm T}
intervals:
0\leq R_{\rm T}\leq 0.5
,
0.5<R_{\rm T}\leq 1.5
,
1.5<R_{\rm T}\leq 2.5
, and
2.5<R_{\rm T}\leq 5.0
.

III
Observables

The single- and two-particle densities, which serve as the basis for defining the
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
correlators
24
, are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}N}{d\eta_{i},d\varphi_{i}},

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1},\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{4}N}{d\eta_{1},d\varphi_{1},d\eta_{2},d\varphi_{2}},

(3)

where
\eta_{i}
and
\varphi_{i}
(
i=1,2
) denote the pseudorapidities and azimuthal angles of particles 1 and 2, respectively.

The
R_{2}
correlator provides a measure of number correlations and is defined as the two-particle cumulant normalized by the product of single-particle densities:

\displaystyle R_{2}(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1},\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})=\frac{\rho_{2}(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1},\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})}{\rho_{1}(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1}),\rho_{1}(\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})}-1.

(4)

In contrast, the two-particle transverse momentum correlator,
P_{2}
, characterizes momentum correlations and is defined as

\displaystyle P_{\rm 2}(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1},\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})=\frac{\langle\Delta p_{\rm T}\Delta p_{\rm T}\rangle(\eta_{1},\varphi_{1},\eta_{2},\varphi_{2})}{\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle^{2}}\frac{\int_{p_{\rm T,min}}^{p_{\rm T,max}}\rho_{2}(\vec{p_{1}},\vec{p_{2}})\Delta p_{\rm T,1}\Delta p_{\rm T,2}dp_{\rm T,1}dp_{\rm T,2}}{\int_{p_{\rm T,min}}^{p_{\rm T,max}}\rho_{2}(\vec{p_{1}},\vec{p_{2}})dp_{\rm T,1}dp_{\rm T,2}},

(5)

where
\Delta p_{\rm T,i}=p_{\rm T,i}-\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
with
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle=\frac{\int\rho_{1}p_{\rm T}dp_{\rm T}}{\int\rho_{1}dp_{\rm T}}
representing the inclusive average transverse momentum of particles in the range
p_{\rm T,min}\leq p_{\rm T}\leq p_{\rm T,max}
. The
P_{2}
correlator exhibits an explicit dependence on the transverse momentum of the particles relative to the event-averaged mean,
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
. By construction,
\Delta p_{\rm T}\Delta p_{\rm T}
is positive when both particles in the pair have
p_{\rm T}
either above or below
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
, while it becomes negative when one particle has
p_{\rm T}>\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
and the other
p_{\rm T}<\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
. As a result,
P_{2}
is sensitive to the hardness of the correlated particles
5
. For instance, particles produced inside jets typically carry
p_{\rm T}
values larger than the inclusive mean (
\langle p_{\rm T}\rangle
), thereby contributing positively to
P_{2}
. In high-energy collisions, jet fragments are also subject to an approximate angular ordering, where the highest-
p_{\rm T}
particles are emitted at relatively small polar angles (
\theta
) with respect to the jet axis, while softer particles are distributed over larger angles. This ordering is manifested in the
\Delta\eta
–
\Delta\varphi
correlation plane, where the higher-
p_{\rm T}
fragments dominate the near-side peak at small separations. Consequently,
P_{2}
tends to exhibit a narrower correlation structure in
\Delta\eta
and
\Delta\varphi
compared to the
R_{2}
correlator.

Our primary objective is to quantify the correlation strength as a function of the pair separation. To this end, it is natural to integrate over all other coordinates within the acceptance range of the simulation. The correlation functions can therefore be expressed as

\displaystyle O(\Delta\eta\Delta\varphi)=\frac{1}{\Omega(\Delta\eta)}\int_{\Omega}O(\Delta\eta_{1},\bar{\eta},\Delta\varphi_{1},\bar{\varphi_{2}})d\bar{\eta}d\bar{\varphi}.

(6)

Here,
O
denotes a generic two-particle correlator, either
R_{2}
or
P_{2}
. The factor
\Omega(\Delta\eta)
accounts for the width of the experimental acceptance in the average pseudorapidity,
\bar{\eta}=(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})/2
, for a given pair separation defined by
\Delta\eta=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
and
\Delta\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
. The corresponding average azimuthal angle is given by
\bar{\varphi}=(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})/2
. Throughout this study, the azimuthal difference
\Delta\varphi
is evaluated in the shifted range
[-\pi/2,3\pi/2]
.

The correlation functions are evaluated separately for the four possible charged-particle combinations:
(++)
,
(--)
,
(+-)
, and
(-+)
. The like-sign (LS) and unlike-sign (US) correlations are obtained by averaging over the respective pair categories as

\displaystyle O^{\rm LS}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[(++)+(--)\big],

(7)

\displaystyle O^{\rm US}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[(+-)+(-+)\big].

(8)

In high-energy hadronic and nuclear collisions, particle production is governed by conservation laws, including energy–momentum, baryon number, strangeness, and electric charge. At LHC energies, particle and antiparticle yields are nearly symmetric, such that unlike-sign (US) and like-sign (LS) correlations considered separately provide limited sensitivity to the underlying production mechanisms. To enable a more differential comparison, we construct the charge-independent (CI) and charge-dependent (CD) correlators as

\displaystyle O^{\rm CI}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(O^{\rm US}+O^{\rm LS}\big),

(9)

\displaystyle O^{\rm CD}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(O^{\rm US}-O^{\rm LS}\big).

(10)

The charge-independent correlator,
O^{\rm CI}
, quantifies the average correlation strength among all charged particle pairs, whereas the charge-dependent correlator,
O^{\rm CD}
, isolates the difference between unlike-sign (US) and like-sign (LS) correlations. In particular, the charge-dependent number correlator
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
can be related to the Balance Function of conserved quantities, such as electric charge (
Q
), baryon number (
B
), and strangeness (
S
), under the condition that the measured multiplicities of positively (
q
) and negatively (
\bar{q}
) charged particles are approximately equal
12
;
7
. In this limit,
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
serves as a proxy for the Balance Function and provides sensitivity to charge-balancing dynamics arising from conservation laws in both elementary and heavy-ion collisions.

. The near-side correlation peak, attributed to short-range effects such as jet fragmentation, resonance decays, and Bose–Einstein correlations, is observed in both
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
and
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
at
(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)=(0,0)
across all the selected
R_{\mathrm{T}}
classes of events. In addition, a broad away-side correlation structure is observed at
\Delta\varphi\approx\pi
, extending over the full
\Delta\eta
range, and is generally associated with momentum conservation in back-to-back jet production. The lowest-activity class (
0<R_{\mathrm{T}}\leq 0.5
) exhibits the most pronounced near- and away-side peaks, along with a characteristic
\Delta\eta
-elongated structure in
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
, indicative of high-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-driven correlations. With increasing underlying-event activity (higher
R_{\mathrm{T}}
), the overall strength of these correlation peaks gradually diminishes, and both the near- and away-side structures exhibit systematic modifications as a function of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
. These trends suggest that increasing UE activity progressively dilutes the high-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-induced correlations, resulting in weaker and more diffused correlation structures. Notably,
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
exhibits consistently narrower near-side peaks along both
\Delta\eta
and
\Delta\varphi
compared to
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
across all
R_{\mathrm{T}}

P_{2}
relative to
R_{2}

8
. The narrower structures observed in
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
originate from its explicit dependence on transverse-momentum fluctuations (
\Delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\Delta p_{\mathrm{T}}
), which suppress contributions from soft particle pairs with transverse momenta close to the mean value
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
. As a result,
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
is predominantly sensitive to correlations among higher-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
particles, which are typically more collimated and therefore yield narrower correlation patterns. In contrast, since
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
weights all particle pairs equally, including those at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, it remains more sensitive to softer UE contributions and consequently exhibits broader correlation structures.

Instead, they can emerge from the interplay of multiple partonic interactions and color reconnection, which introduce collective-like coherence in the hadronization stage and can generate azimuthally collimated particle production over extended
\Delta\eta
ranges. The enhancement of this long-range component in high-
R_{\rm T}

IV.2
Charge-dependent (CD) correlation functions

The charge-dependent two-particle number (
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
) and transverse-momentum (
P_{2}^{\rm CD}
) correlation functions for the selected
R_{\mathrm{T}}
classes are shown in Figs.

8
. By construction, the CD correlators isolate the difference between unlike-sign (US) and like-sign (LS) pairs, and are therefore primarily sensitive to conservation of electric charge, baryon number, and strangeness. They probe charge-pair production and balancing mechanisms in both pp and heavy-ion collisions. Within PYTHIA 8,
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
provides direct sensitivity to the production of correlated quark–antiquark (
q\bar{q}
) pairs and their subsequent hadronization into charged particles.

Both
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
and
P_{2}^{\rm CD}
exhibit a single, localized near-side peak centered at
(\Delta\eta,\Delta\varphi)=(0,0)
, while the away-side region remains largely structureless across all
R_{\rm T}
classes. In contrast to the CI case, no significant long-range near-side component is observed at large
|\Delta\eta|
in any
R_{\rm T}
interval. This behavior indicates that charge balancing occurs predominantly over short ranges in phase space, consistent with local string fragmentation mechanisms implemented in PYTHIA 8. Although back-to-back jets can generate correlations over wide angular separations, charge conservation within each parton shower largely confines the balancing charge to the vicinity of its production point, resulting in a localized near-side structure.
Further, as in the CI case,
P_{2}^{\rm CD}
exhibits systematically narrower peaks than
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
, reflecting its explicit weighting by transverse-momentum fluctuations, which enhances contributions from more collimated, higher-
p_{\rm T}
particle pairs. However, unlike
R_{2}^{\rm CI}
, neither
R_{2}^{\rm CD}
nor
P_{2}^{\rm CD}
develops a long-range near-side component at high
R_{\rm T}
.

When examined as a function of
R_{\rm T}
, both CD correlators display a gradual reduction in peak magnitude with increasing UE activity. This trend reflects a dilution effect: the addition of uncorrelated soft particles from multiple partonic interactions reduces the relative strength of locally balanced charge pairs. The absence of a long-range structure in the CD correlators, even in the UE-dominated class, indicates that the ridge-like component observed in
R_{2}^{\rm CI}
is not driven by local charge conservation. Instead, it arises from charge-independent mechanisms, such as enhanced multi-parton dynamics and color reconnection, which affect charged particles irrespective of their sign. Thus, the contrasting behaviors of CI and CD correlators demonstrate that long-range structures in PYTHIA 8 originate from charge-independent QCD mechanisms rather than from local conservation effects. The combined study of CI and CD observables therefore provides a differential tool to disentangle soft particle production, hard-scattering–induced correlations, and conservation-driven effects in small collision systems.

V
Summary

This study presents a systematic investigation of the two-particle correlation functions,
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
, in pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV simulated with PYTHIA 8. Events are classified using the relative transverse activiy observable
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, which provides a differential handle on UE activity and enables a controlled separation of soft and hard particle-production components.

A long-range near-side structure emerges in the charge-independent correlator
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
for UE-dominated events (
2.5<R_{\mathrm{T}}\leq 5.0
). This feature, reminiscent of ridge-like correlations observed in heavy-ion and high-multiplicity small-system measurements, emerges in PYTHIA 8 without hydrodynamic evolution. Within this non-hydrodynamic framework, the long-range component can be attributed to enhanced soft and semi-soft QCD dynamics, including multiple partonic interactions and color reconnection, which generate azimuthally correlated particle production over extended pseudorapidity ranges. In contrast,
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
remains dominated by localized, jet-like structures and shows no significant long-range near-side component across the
R_{\mathrm{T}}
intervals. This behavior reflects its explicit weighting by transverse-momentum fluctuations, which enhances contributions from higher-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
particles and suppresses the influence of soft background pairs. The comparison between
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
thus highlights their distinct sensitivities to soft UE-driven effects and hard-scattering-induced correlations.

The charge-dependent (CD) correlators further clarify the origin of the observed structures. Both
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CD}}
and
P_{2}^{\mathrm{CD}}
exhibit localized near-side peaks with no evidence of a long-range component for any
R_{\mathrm{T}}
class. Their behavior is consistent with local charge conservation and string fragmentation mechanisms in PYTHIA 8, indicating that the long-range signal observed in
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
is not driven by charge-balancing effects but instead arises from charge-independent QCD dynamics. The gradual reduction of CD peak magnitudes with increasing
R_{\mathrm{T}}
reflects dilution of correlation from additional soft particle production in UE-dominated events.

Further, the evolution of the near-side correlation peak widths provides complementary insight. For CI correlations, the
\Delta\eta
widths remain approximately stable across
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, while the
\Delta\varphi
widths narrow with
increasing UE activity, most prominently for
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
. In the CD case, the near-side peaks remain localized but exhibit a
slight broadening with increasing
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, consistent with
a modest extension of charge-balancing correlations in events with enhanced soft particle production. Importantly, no long-range structure develops in the CD correlators, reinforcing that the ridge-like component
observed in
R_{2}^{\mathrm{CI}}
originates from charge-independent QCD mechanisms rather than short-range charge-balancing effects.

Overall, the results indicate that increased UE activity, quantified through
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, together with multi-parton dynamics and color reconnection, can generate collectivity-like long-range structures within a purely QCD-based, non-hydrodynamic framework. The combined analysis of
R_{2}
and
P_{2}
, together with their charge-independent and charge-dependent decompositions, provides a differential framework to disentangle soft-QCD mechanisms, hard-scattering correlations, and conservation-driven effects. This study therefore establishes a well-defined PYTHIA reference for interpreting LHC measurements and for assessing the extent to which long-range structures in small systems can arise from microscopic QCD dynamics, including multi-parton interactions and color reconnection, rather than from genuine collective behavior.

Respiratory triggered 3D bSSFP MRI enables accurate noninvasive localization of the right adrenal vein confluence in primary aldosteronism
Authors: Masaya Kisohara, Keita Nakayama, Kengo Ohta, Motoki Hatano, Takashi Yanagihara, Yusuke Nakashima, Masashi Shimohira, Yuta Shibamoto, Tatsuya Kawai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Catheterization of the right adrenal vein (RAV) is a technically challenging step in adrenal venous sampling (AVS) for primary aldosteronism. Accurate preprocedural evaluation of RAV anatomy is essential for procedural success. To assess the feasibility of respiratory-triggered three-dimensional balanced steady-state free precession (RT-3D bSSFP) magnetic resonance imaging for visualizing the confluence of the RAV with the inferior vena cava (IVC), in comparison with contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT). In this retrospective study, we evaluated RT-3D bSSFP, CECT, and CV for RAV identification in patients who underwent all three examinations between April 2016 and March 2021. Two independent readers evaluated the vertical level of the RAV–IVC confluence relative to the vertebrae on each modality: CECT acquired during inspiration and RT-3D bSSFP obtained under free-breathing conditions. An independent interventional radiologist assessed catheter venography (CV) during both inspiration and expiration as the reference standard. Intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC [2,1]) were calculated for each modality. Additionally, the accuracy of detecting a common trunk with the accessory hepatic vein and inter-reader agreement (Cohen’s kappa) were assessed. Using CV as the reference standard and an equivalent margin of ± 0.5 vertebrae, and equivalence test was performed on CECT and RT-3D bSSFP, respectively. A total of 47 patients (38 males; mean age, 53 ± 12 years) were included. RT-3D bSSFP was successfully acquired in all cases. The ICC was 0.87 (95% confidence interval: 0.76, 0.93) for CECT and 0.81 (95% confidence interval: 0.67, 0.89) for RT-3D bSSFP. Equivalence in vertebral level was observed between RT-3D bSSFP and CV at expiration (p = 0.0023). Accuracy for detecting a common trunk was higher with RT-3D bSSFP than CECT, and inter-reader agreement was also higher (κ = 0.86 vs. 0.67). RT-3D bSSFP provides vertebral levels equivalent to CV at expiration within ± 0.5 vertebrae.

Introduction

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is responsible for approximately 10% of the cases of hypertension, and its prevalence was considered to be high in a recent study1. In PA, aldosterone hypersecretion is classified as bilateral or unilateral hypersecretion2. Although the patient’s blood pressure can be managed by taking aldosterone antagonist medication, unilateral adrenal gland resection has been shown to improve hypertension in patients with unilateral aldosterone hypersecretion3,4. Adrenal venous sampling (AVS) is a test used to assess the laterality of aldosterone hypersecretion in patients with PA5,6. In AVS, blood is drawn from the right adrenal vein (RAV), left adrenal vein, and inferior vena cava (IVC) by using a catheter before and after adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) loading7–9. The insertion of the catheter into the RAV is frequently identified as a difficult step in this procedure, even when it is performed by skilled interventional radiologists4. This is because the RAV is small and short, close to the accessory hepatic vein, and shows wide variety in the height at which it joins the IVC10–12.

Balanced steady-state free precession (bSSFP) is a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) technique that provides high signal intensity from fluids such as blood and cerebrospinal fluid, facilitating excellent contrast for vascular structures13. In its three-dimensional (3D) implementation, bSSFP allows for detailed anatomical assessment of the vasculature without the need for contrast agents14. A prior study comparing contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) and bSSFP MRI suggested discrepancies between the height of RAV confluence observed on preoperative imaging and that observed during catheter venography (CV) at AVS15. Notably, CECT is typically acquired during deep inspiration to minimize motion artifacts, whereas 3D bSSFP can be acquired under free-breathing conditions using respiratory-triggered acquisition. This distinction is clinically relevant because AVS is frequently performed without breath-holding, and the position of the RAV can vary with the respiratory phase16,17. Pre-procedural localization of the RAV typically relies on imaging-based identification of its orifice in relation to vertebral landmarks.

Because RT-3D bSSFP is acquired under physiological respiration, we hypothesized that it may more accurately reflect the RAV confluence height observed during AVS than CECT. The primary aim of this study was to compare the RAV confluence height measured on RT-3D bSSFP MRI, CECT, and CV. As a secondary aim, we evaluated the ability of RT-3D bSSFP to detect a common trunk between the RAV and the accessory hepatic vein, an anatomical feature that should be recognized prior to AVS.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study received approval from This study was approved by Institutional Review Board of Nagoya City University Graduate School of Medical Sciences (approval number: 60-25-0092) and was conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines following the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent for CT, MRI, and AVS was obtained from all participants.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

We included patients with a confirmed diagnosis of PA who were willing to undergo surgery if unilateral hypersecretion was diagnosed by AVS after at least two of the following tests in the endocrinology department: captopril challenge test, furosemide standing challenge test, or saline challenge test. The study was conducted from April 2016, the date on which the RT-3D bSSFP protocol was set up at our hospital, until March 2021. Patients with contraindications for MRI, such as metal in the body or claustrophobia, were excluded. Patients who did not meet the requirements for CT imaging, as described below, were also excluded.

MRI, CT, and AVS

The patients underwent an MRI scan using a 1.5-T scanner (Ingenia; Philips Healthcare). In our study, RT-3D bSSFP using a navigator respiratory compensation was selected over other steady-state sequences and phase-cycled variants due to its high signal-to-noise efficiency and excellent blood-to-background contrast, which are particularly beneficial for non-contrast imaging of small vessels such as the right adrenal vein. Unlike conventional spoiled gradient echo or phase-cycled steady-state sequences, bSSFP enables bright-blood imaging without the need for contrast agents or subtraction techniques. Its short repetition time and inherent T2/T1 contrast make it especially suited for abdominal imaging, where motion robustness and high tissue contrast are required. Scan parameters included an acquisition voxel size of 1.46 × 1.14 × 2.00 mm3, a repetition time of 3.42 to 3.72 ms, an echo time of 1.71 to 1.86 ms, a flip angle of 50°–60°, and a field of view of 350 mm × 350 mm. The acquired volume was reconstructed into contiguous slices of 2-mm thickness and 1-mm increment. The navigator region of interest was placed across the liver-lung interface, and time-resolved signals within the region of interest were continuously acquired. The navigator length was set to 60 mm. The liver–lung interface was automatically tracked, and the trigger threshold for image acquisition was automatically determined based on this tracking. No temporal delay was applied between the trigger and the initiation of image acquisition. All patients underwent scanning the sequence using a respiratory-gated trigger without breath-holding. We obtained coronal slices of the area, including the bilateral adrenal veins, the lower four thoracic vertebral columns, and all the five lumbar vertebral columns. In addition to 3D bSSFP, we also obtained respiratory-triggered transverse T2-weighted images, coronal slices, and transverse gradient-recalled echo in-phase and opposed-phase images.

We also performed CECT prior to AVS. CECT imaging was conducted using second- and third-generation dual-source CT scanners (SOMATOM Definition Flash and SOMATOM Force, Siemens Healthineers). To enhance the contrast of the right adrenal vein, two different scanning protocols were applied. A protocol was as follows; The tube voltages of 100 kVp and 140 kVp were used to generate virtual monochromatic images at 70 keV. A tin filter was applied to the 140 kVp beam to enhance spectral separation between the two energy levels (referred to as dual-energy CT)18. The other protocol was as follows; the tube voltages of 70 kVp or 80 kVp using dual-source in order to surpress noise based on low radiation dose (referred to as dual-power CT). The first scan was performed between 20 and 30 s, and the second between 40, 50, or 70 s following an initiation of contrast administration from the antecubital vein. A contrast agent with a concentration of 370 mg I/mL was injected at a rate of 3 mL/s. The contrast agent used in this study was not fixed to a single type; the one specified for each date was used. The optimal fixed phase for RAV visualization was being explored, so the scan phase varied depending on the study period. All CT scans were performed during deep inspiration, in accordance with our institutional imaging protocol18.

The procedure for AVS was described in supplementary material. Selective catheterization of the RAV was recorded using CV (Fig. 1). The highest position of the diaphragm during expiration and the lowest position during inspiration were recorded for the following evaluation.

Evaluations of CECT and RT-3D bSSFP were independently performed by two diagnostic radiologists (a board-certified radiologist with 10 years of experience in cardiovascular imaging and a board-certified radiologist with 6 years of experience). Preprocessing of CECT and RT-3D bSSFP

A set of 1.0-mm-thick transverse images reconstructed from the coronal RT-3D bSSFP images and three sets of 1.0-mm-thick transverse CECT images at three different phases were prepared for evaluation.

Analyses for image quality

The reconstructed RT-3D bSSFP and CECT images were evaluated the visibility of the RAVs, and classified into categories 1, 2, and 3 (Table 1). We defined the categories as follows: Category 1: RAVs were uniformly delineated (Fig. 2A and B); Category 2: RAVs were partially but confluence evaluable (Fig. 2C and D); Category 3: RAVs were not identifiable (Fig. 2E and F). The results of the categorization were then counted.

Additionally, the presence of a common trunk with the accessory hepatic vein was assessed on CECT, RT-3D bSSFP, and CV, with CV serving as the gold standard. The accuracy of detection on CECT and RT-3D bSSFP was evaluated in reference to the CV findings. This analysis employed a binary classification to determine whether a common trunk with the accessory hepatic vein could be identified or not.

Analyses for level of confluence of RAV

To calculate the inter-modality height difference of RAV-IVC junction, the vertical level at which the RAV merged into the IVC was evaluated on RT-3D bSSFP, CECT, and CV, respectively. Since absolute measurement is not feasible with CV, the relative height was evaluated for all modalities. On RT-3D bSSFP and CECT, we first identified RAVs, followed by their height evaluation. We defined the 12th thoracic vertebra as the one associated with the lower-most ribs. In the case of RT-3D bSSFP, the height of the RAV relative to the vertebral body was determined on the coronal T2WI using the reference-line function on the viewer; in the case of CECT, we also used the reconstructed coronal images to assess the relative height of the RAV. In CV, we measured the height of the highest confluence during spontaneous expiration and the height of the lowest confluence during spontaneous inspiration in CV examinations performed during free breathing. The evaluation method was consistent for all imaging modalities. First, we identified the vertebral level overlapping the RAV-IVC confluence on coronal slice images. The mid-height of each intervertebral disc served as a reference point: The reference point for the disc between the thoracic (Th12) and 1st lumbar (L1) vertebrae was defined as 5.0; the disc above as 4.0; and the disc below as 6.0.

The vertical distance from the reference point to the the RAV-IVC junction was defined as a, and the distance from the reference point to the midpoint of the next lower disc as b. The ratio a/b was added to the reference value, and the resulting number - rounded to the second decimal place - was used to represent the height at which the RAV merges into the IVC (Fig. 3). We performed this assessment in cases that met the inclusion criteria.

Statistical analyses

We performed Wilcoxon rank-signed tests for differences in visibility of RAV between CECT and RT-3D bSSFP. Inter-reader variability in categorization was assessed using Cohen’s weighted kappa coefficient for RAV visibility and Cohen’s kappa for the assessment of a common trunk with the accessory hepatic vein19,20. Inter-reader variability in relative difference of level between each modality was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficient21. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed among the four groups, and Tukey’s honestly significant difference test was used to test for significant differences among the modalities. To assess height equivalence, we also calculated 95% confidence interval of the difference in mean and performed a two-one-sided t-test (TOST) between each modality. Of the upper limit test and the lower limit test, the one with the larger p-value was used as the p-value in the equivalence test; it was determined equivalent between the two modalities when the p-value was less than 0.05. Since we considered that a difference in the height of more than one vertebra between modalities might affect the successful cannulation, we set an equivalence bound of 0.5 vertebrae on each side. All statistical analyses were conducted using Python (version 3.9.19) and open-source libraries.

Results

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The RT-3D bSSFP protocol was operational from June 2016, and by March 2021, 52 patients had undergone AVS. One patient underwent two AVS procedures. This patient’s data from the first AVS were included in the analysis because the patient had undergone a qualifying MRI scan and CT scan before the first AVS. MRI including RT-3D bSSFP wasn’t performed in 4 patients because of insufficient instructions at the time of request for study. Forty-eight patients underwent RT-3D bSSFP. One patient who did not undergo CECT with Siemens Somatom Definition Flash or Somatom Force was not included in the analysis. Thus, 47 patients (38 males; age, 53 ± 12 years; weight, 69.1 ± 17.6 kg; BMI, 25.5 ± 5.2 kg/m2; PAC/PRA, 496.7 (333.6–895.0) pg*hour/ng) were included (Fig. 4).

Discussion

In this study, we used RT-3D bSSFP to evaluate the height at which the RAV merges into the IVC, and investigated its relationship with the height of the merging area on CV as well as that on CECT. The image quality assessments of the RAV on CECT and RT-3D bSSFP demonstrated moderate or greater inter-reader agreement. Although the inter-modality categorical agreement by each reader was not high, all modalities demonstrated category 1 image quality in more than half of the cases, indicating excellent depiction of the RAV. The TOST showed equivalence in height between RT-3D bSSFP and CV during spontaneous expiration. In contrast, significant differences were observed between RT-3D bSSFP and CECT, RT-3D bSSFP and CV during spontaneous inspiration, CECT and CV during spontaneous inspiration, and CECT and CV during spontaneous expiration. Although group-level equivalence was demonstrated between RT-3D bSSFP and CV during expiration, individual-level variability was observed. However, compared to other comparisons, the number of cases that deviated from the margin was the smallest. RT-3D bSSFP was able to depict the common trunk with the accessory hepatic vein, and inter-reader agreement was moderate or greater.

The observed equivalence between RT-3D bSSFP and CV during expiration likely reflects the acquisition of RT-3D bSSFP under physiological respiratory conditions. Although group-level equivalence was demonstrated between RT-3D bSSFP and CV during expiration, a minority of cases exceeded the predefined equivalence margin, indicating inherent physiological and measurement variability. Inspiratory breath-hold during CECT may alter the relative position of the IVC and RAV, leading to measurable differences in confluence height. Furthermore, bSSFP sequences are well suited for vascular imaging due to their high signal-to-noise ratio and favorable T2/T1 contrast. Unlike CECT, RT-3D bSSFP does not require precise timing of contrast administration and avoids radiation exposure.

These findings suggest that RT-3D bSSFP may provide anatomically reliable pre-procedural information regarding RAV confluence height, particularly under expiratory conditions that resemble those during AVS. In addition, RT-3D bSSFP demonstrated numerically higher sensitivity and accuracy for detecting common trunks compared with CECT, suggesting potential value for recognizing anatomical variants.

A previous report showed a significant difference in the height between CV and both MRI and CT15; we observed a different trend in the present study. In our study, the cross-section at the data acquisition was different from that of previous studies (Table 5). The MRI vendor was also different, and technical differences in the respiratory trigger may have affected the difference in results between our study and the previous study. For example, it is possible to set a temporal delay between the trigger and the start of scanning. Such differences in RT parameters may result in variations in the respiratory phase during image acquisition, making generalized comparisons difficult. To enable more generalized discussion, RT parameters should be standardized and consistently reported. In addition, the report by Ota et al.15 concluded that dynamic CECT is useful for preoperative evaluation and that MRI should be used as a substitute when CECT is contraindicated. Our results suggest that preoperative RT-3D bSSFP can provides an accurate assessment of the height at which the RAV. Although further validation in larger cohorts is warranted, RT-3D bSSFP may serve as a radiation-free imaging option for evaluating the RAV before AVS in appropriately selected patients.

This study has some limitations. First, the relative length to the vertebral column was measured instead of the absolute length to determine the height at which the RAV joins the IVC because it is challenging to measure the absolute length of the DA by its nature. Thus, we did not correct for differences in vertebral heights between individuals and did not take into account deformities of the vertebral body. Relative measurements ignore vertebral variations/deformities, potentially affecting ± 0.5 margin generalizability. Second, the CT scan was performed with inspiration, as is standard protocol for evaluation of the adrenal veins prior to AVS. This may explain the observed difference in height between MRI and CT scan, as MRI was performed under free-breathing condition. Since it is not feasible to obtain the identical contrast enhancement with a single CT scan of the same patient on expiration and inspiration, we suggest that CT scans should be performed on expiration to allow more accurate comparison with MRI. Third, although a single specific MRI condition was employed for this study, CT was performed under two conditions: dual-energy CT and dual-power CT. Because the optimal contrast-enhancement phase had not yet been established, the CT protocol was not standardized during the study period. Therefore, accurate comparisons of visibility were difficult to perform in this study. Fourth, because RT-3D bSSFP is a sequence that does not require strict breath-holding, patients may fall asleep during the examination, which can result in mistimed respiratory triggers and potentially prolong the time required to obtain high-quality images. Therefore, the examination time may be significantly longer than expected, and CT may be superior to MRI in terms of ease of imaging. Finally, the current study did not examine how the preoperative estimation of the height at which the RAV joins the IVC would influences the AVS procedure. To elucidate this, it is necessary to compare procedure times and success rate after preoperative assessment using RT-3D bSSFP, CECT, or both. However, it is not realistic to perform AVS multiple times in the same patient, so it is unclear exactly how much it contributes to the procedural simplification.

In conclusion, RT-3D bSSFP is helpful in evaluating the height at which the RAV merges into the IVC.

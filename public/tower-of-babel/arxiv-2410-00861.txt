1.
Introduction

The present work is devoted to establish existence and multiplicity of solutions to the following class of quasilinear elliptic problem:

(
P_{\lambda}
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}-\Delta_{\Phi}u=\lambda a(x)|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u,&x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 2
is a smooth bounded domain,
1<q<\ell\leq m<p<\ell^{*},\ell^{*}=N\ell/(N-\ell),N\geq 2
. Later on, we shall give the assumptions on
p,q,\Phi
and the potential
a:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator is given by
\Delta_{\Phi}u:=\mbox{div}(\phi(|\nabla u|)\nabla u)
. Throughout this work we shall consider
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as an even function defined by

(1.1)

\Phi(t)=\int_{0}^{|t|}s\phi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfies some suitable conditions.

Due to the nature of the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator, we need to consider framework of the Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, see Section 2 ahead. These kinds of spaces are appropriated in order to control the generality of the function
\Phi
which allows us to find weak solutions for the Problem (

P
λ

). It is important to emphasize that Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces was introduced by M. W. Orlicz
[
32
]
. On this subject we also refer the reader to the important works Krasnosel’skii and Rutickii
[
24
]
, Luxemburg
[
27
]
and Donaldson & Trudinger
[
11
]
and references therein. In those works the main objective is to extend and complement the theory of the Sobolev spaces
W_{0}^{1,r}(\Omega),\penalty\ 1<r<\infty
. Namely, assuming that
\Phi(t)=|t|^{r}
, we deduce that the Orlicz-Sobolev space is
W_{0}^{1,r}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, for the function
\Phi(t)=|t|^{r}
we obtain the
p
-Laplacian operator, see
[
33
]
. It is worthwhile to mention also that for
\Phi(t)=|t|^{r_{1}}+|t|^{r_{2}},r_{1}<r_{2}
where
\Omega
is a bounded and smooth domain that
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)=W_{0}^{1,r_{2}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for
\Phi(t)=|t|^{r_{1}}+|t|^{r_{2}}
we obtain the
(r_{1},r_{2})
-Laplacian operator which is not homogeneous, see
[
14
,
25
,

]
. In the same way, assuming that
\Phi(t)=|t|^{r}\log(1+|t|)
, the associated Orlicz-Sobolev space does not correspond to any Sobolev space, see also
[
2
]
. Here we also refer the reader to the important works
[

,

]
where many aspects on Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are considered.

It is worthwhile to mention that many special types of quasilinear elliptic problems involving the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator arise from physical phenomena such as in nonlinear elasticity, plasticity, generalized Newtonian fluids, see
[
18
]
and references therein. Recall also those semilinear elliptic problems with general nonlinearities have been also studied for a huge class of operators in the last years, see

.

It is important to stress that the nonlinearity
f(x,t)=\lambda a(x)|t|^{q-2}t+|t|^{p-2}t
is a concave-convex function and the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator is not homogeneous. Since we are interested in considering general quasilinear elliptic problems we need to deal with some difficulties. The first one is the loss of the homogeneity on the left side hand for the Problem (

P
λ

). The second difficulty arises whether we consider the largest positive number
\lambda^{*}
such that the Nehari method can be applied. To the best of our knowledge, the present work is the first one considering nonhomogeneous operators together with concave-convex nonlinearities where the parameter
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
plays a principal role. The main difficulty here comes from the fact that for
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
the fibering map for the associated energy functional has inflection points. These kinds of difficulties are overcome by using some fine estimates and taking into account the nonlinear Rayleigh quotient method, see
[
23
,
19
]
. Recall also that the working space is given by the reflexive Banach space
W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
where
\Phi
is a
N
-function. Namely, we consider the Sobolev-Orlicz space which has been widely studied in the last years, see also
[
3
,

,
17
,

,

,

,
8
,

]
.

In the present work our main contribution is to consider the existence and multiplicity of solutions for quasilinear operators driven by the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator where the nonlinearity is concave-convex and the parameter
\lambda>0
is not small. Hence, the fibering map for the associated energy functional admits inflections points. Hence, assuming that some minimizer for energy functional restricted to the Nehari manifold is an inflection point, the Lagrange Multiplier Theorem does not work anymore. Hence, one of the main difficulties is to find a sharp parameter
\lambda^{*}>0
in such way that the Nehari method can be applied for each
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
. Namely, for each
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, the fibering map does not admit any inflection points. Furthermore, for
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
, we observe that there exist fibering maps with an inflection point. In order to avoid this kind of problem we use a sequence argument which ensures that our main problem has at least two positive solutions for
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
. For this case we prove a nonexistence in a subset of the Nehari manifold. More precisely, we prove that the Problem (

P
λ

) does not admit any nontrivial solution which is also an inflection point for the fibering map given just above. The last assertion allows to prove that there exist at least two critical points for our energy functional which are given by minimizers in some subsets of the Nehari manifold. Here we emphasize that in the present work we do not require any kind of homogeneity for the
N
-function
\Phi
. Hence, our work complements the afore-mentioned results by considering the existence and multiplicity of solutions for quasilinear elliptic problems driven by the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator where the homogeneity is not satisfied.

It is important to stress that the nonlinear Rayleigh quotient method works in general whenever all terms in the energy functional are homogeneous of some degree. In this manner, up to now, the present work is the first one that considers nonhomogeneous operators together with the Nehari method where the nonlinear Rayleigh method can be applied. Furthermore, for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
, we prove also existence and multiplicity of solutions using the fact that
\lambda\in(\lambda^{*},\overline{\lambda})
where
\overline{\lambda}>\lambda^{*}
is a suitable new parameter.

1.1.
Assumptions and statement of the main results

As was told in the introduction we shall consider existence and nonexistence of nontrivial weak solutions for the Problem (

P
λ

) looking for the parameter
\lambda>0
. From a standard variational point of view finding weak solutions to the elliptic Problem (

P
λ

) is equivalent to finding critical points for the
C^{1}
functional
J_{\lambda}:W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J_{\lambda}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx-\frac{\lambda}{q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,\,\,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

It is worthwhile to mention that the Gauteaux derivative of
J_{\lambda}
is given by

J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)v=\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\nabla vdx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q-2}uvdx-\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uvdx,\,\,u,v\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

Throughout this work we shall assume the following hypotheses:

\phi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-function satisfying the following assumptions:

(
\phi_{1}
)

t\phi(t)\mapsto 0
, as
t\mapsto 0
and
t\phi(t)\mapsto\infty
, as
t\mapsto\infty
;

(
\phi_{2}
)

t\phi(t)
is strictly increasing in
(0,\infty)
;

(
\phi_{3}
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Phi^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{\frac{N+1}{N}}}\mathrm{d}\tau<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\Phi^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{\frac{N+1}{N}}}\mathrm{d}\tau=\infty;

(\phi_{4})

There holds

-1<\ell-2:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)t}{\phi(t)}\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)t}{\phi(t)}:=m-2<\infty.

Furthermore, we assume also the following assumptions:

(
H_{1}
)

1<q<\ell\leq m<p<\ell^{*},\ell^{*}=N\ell/(N-\ell)

(

Now, we shall consider the Nehari set as follows

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}&=&\left\{u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)u=0\right\},\lambda>0.\end{array}

For the Nehari method we refer the reader to
[
30
,
31
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:J^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}(u)(u,u)>0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:J^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}(u)(u,u)<0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{0}

\displaystyle\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:J^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}(u)(u,u)=0\}.

It is important to emphasize that

is well defined for each
u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
. The main feature in the present work is to ensure that the minimization problems

(1.2)

c_{\mathcal{N^{+}}}:=\inf\{J_{\lambda}(u):u\in\mathcal{N^{+}}\}

and

(1.3)

From now on, we shall introduce the nonlinear generalized Rayleigh quotients, see
[
23
]
. Namely, we consider the functionals
R_{n},R_{e}:W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}

associated with the parameter
\lambda>0
in the following form

(1.4)

R_{n}(u)=\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}},\,\,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}

and

(1.5)

{R}_{e}(u)=\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\frac{1}{q}\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}},\,\,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},

where
\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}:=\int_{\Omega}a|u|^{q}dx
.
The main idea here is to find
u\in X\setminus\{0\}
such that
R_{n}(u)=\lambda
. More specifically, we obtain the following statement:

(1.6)

u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}\,\,\,\mbox{if and only if}\,\,\,R_{n}(u)=\lambda,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}

and

(1.7)

J_{\lambda}(u)=0\,\,\mbox{if and only if}\,\,\,R_{e}(u)=\lambda,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}.

The functional given in (
1.5
) is used in order to decide the sign for solution obtained as a minimizer for the functional
J_{\lambda}
restricted to
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
. Under this condition, we need to control the size of
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
using an auxiliary extreme. To do that we need to consider some extra functionals. Namely, we consider the

functionals
\Lambda_{e},\Lambda_{n}:W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
in the following form:

\Lambda_{e}(u):=\max_{t>0}R_{e}(tu)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\Lambda_{n}(u)=\max_{t>0}R_{n}(tu).

These functionals give us sharp conditions taking into account the Nehari method for the existence of weak solutions
u\in X
for our main problem with
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
. In order to do that we define the following extreme:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{*}:=\inf_{u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\Lambda_{e}(u)

and

(1.9)

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}:=\inf_{u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\Lambda_{n}(u).

2.
Preliminaries for the Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev framework

In this section we shall give some powerful tools for the Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces which will be used later. Firstly, we recall some basic concepts on Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. For a more complete discussion on this subject, we refer the readers to
[
1
,
24
,
20
,
21
,
36
]
. Let
\Theta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
be convex and continuous. It is important to say that
\Theta
is a
N
-function if
\Theta
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\Theta
is even;

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\Theta(t)}{t}=0
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Theta(t)}{t}=\infty
;

(iv)

\Theta(t)>0
, for all
t>0
.

Notice that by using assumptions
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
we conclude that
\Phi
, defined in (
1.1
), is a
N
-function. Henceforth,
\Theta
and
\Psi
denote
N
-functions.

Recall also that a
N
-function satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition if there exists
K>0
such that

\Theta(2t)\leq K\Theta(t),\quad\mbox{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptt\geq 0.

We denote by
\tilde{\Theta}
the complementary function of
\Theta
, which is given by the Legendre’s transformation

\tilde{\Theta}(s)=\max_{t\geq 0}\{st-\Theta(t)\},\quad\mbox{for all}\hskip 5.69046pts\geq 0.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open subset and
\Theta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be fixed. The set

\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\mbox{ measurable }:\int_{\Omega}\Theta(|u(x)|)dx<+\infty\right\},

is the so-called
Orlicz class
. Let us suppose that
\Theta
is a Young function generated by
\theta
, that is

\Theta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s\theta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let us define
\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}(t):=\sup_{s\theta(s)\leq t}s
, for
t\geq 0
. The function
\tilde{\Theta}
can be rewritten as follows

\tilde{\Theta}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s\tilde{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

The function
\tilde{\Theta}
is called the
complementary function
to
\Theta
. The set

L_{\Theta}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}:\int_{\Omega}\Theta\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)<\infty,\mbox{ for some }\lambda>0\right\},

is called
Orlicz space
. The usual norm on
L_{\Theta}(\Omega)
is the
Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{\Theta}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\penalty\ |\penalty\ \int_{\Omega}\Theta\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

We recall that the
Orlicz-Sobolev space

W^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{1,\Theta}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L_{\Theta}(\Omega):\exists f_{i}\in L_{\Theta}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}u\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}=-\int_{\Omega}f_{i}\phi,\ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),\ i=1,...,N\right\}.

The Orlicz-Sobolev norm of
W^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1,\Theta}=\|u\|_{\Theta}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{\Theta}.

Since
\Theta
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, we define by
W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the Orlicz-Sobolev norm of
W^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
. By the Poincaré inequality (see e.g.
[
21
]
), that is, the inequality

\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\leq\int_{\Omega}\Theta(2d_{\Omega}|\nabla u|),

where
d_{\Omega}=\mbox{diam}(\Omega)
, we can conclude that

\|u\|_{\Theta}\leq 2d_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|_{\Theta},\quad\mbox{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptu\in W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega).

As a consequence, we have that
\|u\|:=\|\nabla u\|_{\Theta}
defines a norm in
W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
which is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{1,\Phi}
. The spaces
L_{\Theta}(\Omega)
,
W^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\Theta}_{0}(\Omega)
are separable and reflexive when
\Theta
and
\tilde{\Theta}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Recall also that
\Psi
dominates
\Theta
near infinity, in short we write
\Theta<\Psi
, if there exist positive constants
t_{0}
and
k
such that

\Theta(t)\leq\Psi(kt),\quad\mbox{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptt\geq t_{0}.

If
\Theta<\Psi
and
\Psi<\Theta
, then we say that
\Theta
and
\Psi
are equivalent, and we denote by
\Theta\approx\Psi
. Let
\Theta_{*}
be the inverse of the function

t\in(0,\infty)\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Theta^{-1}(s)}{s^{\frac{N+1}{N}}}ds

which extends to
\mathbb{R}
by
\Theta_{*}(t)=\Theta_{*}(-t)
for
t\leq 0
. We say that
\Theta
increases essentially more slowly than
\Psi
near infinity, in short we write
\Theta<<\Psi
, if and only if for every positive constant
k
one has

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Theta(kt)}{\Psi(t)}=0.

It is important to emphasize that if
\Theta<\Psi<<\Theta_{*}
, then the following embedding

\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\Psi}(\Omega),

is compact. In particular, since
\Theta<<\Theta_{*}
(cf.
[
20
, Lemma 4.14]
), we have that
W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L_{\Theta}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, we have that
W_{0}^{1,\Theta}(\Omega)
is continuous embedded into
L_{\Theta_{*}}(\Omega)
. Finally, we recall the following proposition due to N. Fukagai et al.
[
17
]
which can be written in the following way:

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
(\phi_{1})-(\phi_{3})
hold and set

\zeta_{0}(t)=\min\{t^{\ell},t^{m}\}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \zeta_{1}(t)=\max\{t^{\ell},t^{m}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ t\geq 0.

Then
\Phi
satisfies the following estimates:

\zeta_{0}(t)\Phi(\rho)\leq\Phi(\rho t)\leq\zeta_{1}(t)\Phi(\rho),\quad\rho,t>0,

\zeta_{0}(\|u\|_{\Phi})\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi(u)dx\leq\zeta_{1}(\|u\|_{\Phi}),\quad u\in L_{\Phi}(\Omega).

As a consequence, by using Proposition
2.1
, we can prove the following result:

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
(\phi_{1})-(\phi_{4})
hold. Then
L_{\Phi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\ell}(\Omega)
and
L_{\Phi_{*}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\ell^{*}}(\Omega)
.

For the function
\Phi_{*}
we obtain similar estimates given by the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
\phi
satisfies
(\phi_{1})-(\phi_{3})
. Set

\zeta_{2}(t)=\min\{t^{\ell^{*}},t^{m^{*}}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \zeta_{3}(t)=\max\{t^{\ell^{*}},t^{m^{*}}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ t\geq 0

where
1<\ell,m<N
and
m^{*}=\frac{mN}{N-m}
,
\ell^{*}=\frac{\ell N}{N-\ell}
. Then

\ell^{*}\leq\frac{t\Phi^{\prime}_{*}(t)}{\Phi_{*}(t)}\leq m^{*},\penalty\ t>0,

\zeta_{2}(t)\Phi_{*}(\rho)\leq\Phi_{*}(\rho t)\leq\zeta_{3}(t)\Phi_{*}(\rho),\penalty\ \penalty\ \rho,t>0,

\zeta_{2}(\|u\|_{\Phi_{*}})\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{*}(u)dx\leq\zeta_{3}(\|u\|_{\Phi_{*}}),\penalty\ u\in L_{\Phi_{*}}(\Omega).

Now, we shall consider the following useful result

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that assumptions
(\phi_{1})-(\phi_{2})
and
(\phi_{4})
are satisfied.
Then we obtain the following statements:

\ell\Phi(t)\leq\phi(t)t^{2}\leq m\Phi(t),t\in\mathbb{R}.

3.
The Nehari method for nonhomogeneous operators

In the present section we shall consider the Nehari method for the quasilinear operator driven by the
\Phi
-Laplacian operator. The main idea is to consider the fibering map
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
given by
t\mapsto\gamma(t)=J_{\lambda}(tu)
where
u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
, see for instance
[
13
,
34
]
. Furthermore,
\gamma
is in
C^{1}
class and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if
tu\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
. In particular,
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
if and only if
\gamma^{\prime}(1)=0
. Under these conditions, we shall consider the function
\gamma
in the following form

\gamma(t)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(t|\nabla u|)dx-\dfrac{\lambda t^{q}}{q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,t\geq 0.

As a consequence, we also mention that

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)t|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\lambda t^{q-1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-t^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,t\geq 0

and

\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}+\phi^{\prime}(t|\nabla u|)t|\nabla u|^{3}dx-\lambda(q-1)t^{q-2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-(p-1)t^{p-2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,t>0.

Similarly, we also mention that

J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)u=\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

and

(3.1)

J_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)=\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}+\phi^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{3}dx-\lambda(q-1)\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{q}dx-(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

Under our assumptions we observe that

\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)=\dfrac{J^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}(tu)(tu,tu)}{t^{2}},t>0,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

It is worthwhile to mention that the functionals
u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx
and
u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx
are not homogeneous. This is a difficulty in order to apply the Nehari method and the nonlinear Rayleigh quotient. This difficulty is overcome by proving that the function
t\mapsto R_{n}(tu)
admits exactly one critical point. In fact, for a general
N
-function
\Phi
the Nehari method can be applied using some fine estimates together with the fact that the norm in
W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
is weakly lower semicontinuous, see
[
16
,
38
]
. However, up to now, there is no result for a non-homogeneous operator driven by the
\Phi
-Laplacian taking into account the nonlinear Rayleigh quotient. More precisely, we consider the following result:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that assumptions
(\phi_{1})-(\phi_{4})
and

(

H
1

)

–

(

H
2

)

are satisfied. Then the function
t\mapsto R_{n}(tu)
has the following properties:

i)

There holds

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q}}>0,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\frac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q-1}}>0.

ii)

There holds

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{p-q}}<0,\lim_{t\to+\infty}\dfrac{\frac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)}{t^{p-q-1}}<0.

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that

R_{n}(tu)=\dfrac{t^{2-q}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx-t^{p-q}\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}},t>0,u\in W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover, we mention that

\dfrac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)=\dfrac{(2-q)t^{1-q}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx+t^{2-q}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{3}dx-(p-q)t^{p-q-1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}},t>0.

Hence, we obtain

\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q}}=\dfrac{t^{2-m}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx-t^{p-m}\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}.

Therefore,

(3.2)

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q}}=\lim_{t\to 0}\left(\dfrac{t^{2-m}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}\right).

The last assertion together with (
3.2
) and Proposition
2.4
imply that

(3.3)

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q}}\geq\lim_{t\to 0}\left(\dfrac{t^{-m}\int_{\Omega}\Phi(t|\nabla u|)dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}\right).

In light of (
3.3
) and Proposition
2.1
we see that

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{m-q}}\geq\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}>0.

Similarly, taking into account hypothesis
(\phi_{4})
, we obtain that

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{1}{t^{m-q-1}}\dfrac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)\geq\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}>0.

As a consequence, the proof of item
i)
follows.

Analogously, we prove that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{p-q}}=-\dfrac{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}+\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{t^{2-p}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}\right).

Now, by using Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.1
, we obtain that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{R_{n}(tu)}{t^{p-q}}=-\dfrac{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}+\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{t^{m-p}\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)dx}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}=-\dfrac{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}<0.

Furthermore, by using the same ideas discussed just above, we obtain that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{t^{p-q-1}}\dfrac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)\leq-(p-q)\dfrac{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{q}_{q,a}}<0.

This ends the proof. ∎

.

Suppose that assumptions

According to Proposition

\dfrac{d}{dt}R_{n}(tu)=0,t>0

(p-q)\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{[(2-q)\phi(t|\nabla u|)+\phi^{\prime}(t|\nabla u|)|t\nabla u|]|\nabla u|^{2}}{t^{p-2}}dx.

The last inequality can be rewritten as follows

(p-q)\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{[(2-q)\phi(t|\nabla u|)+\phi^{\prime}(t|\nabla u|)|t\nabla u|]|\nabla u|^{p}}{|t\nabla u|^{p-2}}dx.

, we obtain that the function

is strictly decreasing. Furthermore, by using assumption

Hence, the identity (

) admits at most one solution

for each

. Therefore, there exists a unique

is a

. At this stage, we know that

is well defined. Consider the auxiliary function

given by

. Recall also that

that there exists an open set

. Therefore,

holds for each

. Since

is an arbitrary function we conclude that
t_{n}\in C^{1}(W^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\};(0,\infty))

The contraction morphism between maps and quasimaps to toric varieties
Alberto Cobos Rabano

Abstract
. Given X X a smooth projective toric variety, we construct a morphism from a closed substack of the moduli space of stable maps to X X to the moduli space of quasimaps to X X . If X X is Fano, we show that this morphism is surjective. The construction relies on the notion of degree of a quasimap at a base-point, which we define. We show that a quasimap is determined by its regular extension and the degree of each of its basepoints.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Results

To a smooth projective toric variety
X
we can associate two moduli spaces:
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
, the moduli of stable maps, and
{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
, the moduli of stable toric quasimaps. These moduli spaces are two different compactifications of the space of maps from smooth curves to
X
. We study a geometric comparison of these compactifications. Our main result is the following.

Construction A

(
5.1.2
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective toric variety. We construct a closed substack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
and a morphism of stacks

c_{X}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)

extending the identity on the locus of maps from a smooth source.

Our motivation comes from the comparison between Gromov–Witten and quasimap invariants of toric Fano varieties in enumerative geometry.
For
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the morphism
c_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}
, which is defined globally, agrees with the one used in
[
MOP11
]
to compare the virtual fundamental classes of both spaces.
More generally,
c_{X}
is defined globally on
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
if all toric divisors in
X
are nef.

A
paves the way for a geometric comparison for toric varieties in any genus. The following result makes the Fano case easier to treat.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

6.0.1
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano toric variety. The contraction morphism

c_{X}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}(X,\beta)

is surjective.

A
relies on the contraction morphism for (products of) projective spaces. One idea would be to use an embedding
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
and the functoriality of maps and quasimaps. This fails because the morphism
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}\colon{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\iota_{\ast}\beta)
is not a closed embedding in general, see
Example

4.1.1
, which is due to Ciocan-Fontanine and which first appeared in
[
BN21
, Remark 2.3.3]
. This situation motivates the following result. Let
A_{1}(X)
denote the group of
1
-cycles on
X
modulo rational equivalence.

Theorem C

(
Corollary

4.3.4
)
.

Let
\iota\colon X\to Y
be a closed embedding between smooth projective toric varieties. If
\iota_{\ast}\colon A_{1}(X)\to A_{1}(Y)
is injective, then the morphism

{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}\colon{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(Y,\iota_{\ast}\beta)

is a closed embedding.

C
leads to
A
following the strategy previously sketched. More precisely, we replace the embedding into projective space by an embedding
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}
, with
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{s}}
, such that
\iota_{\ast}
is injective on curve classes (we call embeddings with this property
epic
, see
Proposition

4.2.1
). Such an embedding exists by
Theorem

4.2.5
. With this we obtain a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}},\iota_{\ast}\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}},i_{*}\beta).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\iota)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{\mathbb{P}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}}

The dashed vertical arrow is defined on the locus of maps in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
for which
c_{\mathbb{P}}\circ{\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\iota)}
factors through the image of
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}
, that is on

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\coloneqq{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\times_{{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P},\iota_{\ast}\beta)}{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P},\iota_{\ast}\beta).

Most of the paper relies, directly or indirectly, on the notion of degree of a basepoint.

Definition D

(
Definition

3.2.10
)
.

Let
{q}
be a quasimap to a smooth projective toric variety
X
and let
x
be a nonsingular point of the source. We construct a class
\beta_{x}\in A_{1}(X)
called the degree of the quasimap
{q}
at the point
x
.

The degree
\beta_{x}
is constructed combinatorially in
Proposition

3.2.7
and completely characterized in
Proposition

3.2.9
. We give a geometric interpretation, under certain assumptions, in
Remark

5.2.3
. It
recovers the notion of length of a basepoint in
[
CFKM14
, Definition 7.1.1]
(for toric quasimaps), see
Lemma

3.5.1
. We show in
Corollary

3.4.2
that a toric quasimap is determined by its regular extension (
Definition

3.1.1
) and the degree of each of its basepoints. The degree of a basepoint is essential in
C
and in the proof of
B
.

1.2.
Context

The moduli space
{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
of stable toric quasimaps was introduced in
[
CFK10
]
, in relation to
[
Giv98
,
MOP11
]
. These constructions have been further generalized to include quasimaps to more general GIT quotients
[
CFKM14
]
, stacks
[
CCFK15
]
, and a stability parameter
[
CFK14
,
Tod11
]
.

Quasimaps provide a compactification of the space of maps from smooth curves to
X
. The advantage over other compactifications relies in the fact that quasimap invariants, or more precisely the associated
I
-function, are often computable
[
CZ14
,
CFK16
,
KL18
,
CFK20
,
BN21
]
. Quasimaps have also been successfully used to prove results concerning Gromov–Witten invariants
[
LP18
,
FJR18
,
CRMS24
]
.

The contraction morphism
c_{X}
from
A
appeared in
[
MOP11
]
in the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
and in
[
CFK10
]
for moduli spaces of maps and quasimaps with one parametrized component. A similar picture for Grassmannians appears in
[
PR03
,
Man14
]
, where the authors show that the natural contraction morphism does not extend to the whole moduli space of stable maps.

The relation between Gromov–Witten invariants and quasimap invariants has been proved in many situations: via localization for projective spaces
[
MOP11
]
and for complete intersections in projective spaces
[
CJR21
]
and via wall-crossing
[
CFK17
,
CFK20
]
. In particular, Gromov-Witten and quasimap invariants of a smooth Fano toric variety agree
[
CFK17
]
.

Our motivation is to use
A
to prove this statement in a more geometric way as in
[
Man12
,
Man14
]
and at the level of derived structures, extending
[
KMMP22
]
. Besides giving a better geometric understanding of the map-to-quasimap wall-crossing, we hope such a result would contribute to the development of techniques that relate virtual classes of moduli spaces which are “virtually birational” but not necessarily birational.

In

In

2.
Background

2.1.
Toric varieties

Let
X
be a toric variety, that is, a normal variety over
\mathbb{C}
containing a torus
T
as a dense open subset and such that the natural group action of
T
extends to an action of
T
on
X
. The character and co-character lattices of
T_{X}
are denoted by
M_{X}
and
N_{X}
respectively. We will omit the subindex
X
if there is no risk of confusion.

Given a fan
\Sigma
, we denote by
\Sigma(k)
and by
\Sigma_{\max}
the sets of
k
-dimensional cones and of maximal cones in
\Sigma
, respectively. Given a cone
\sigma
, we denote by
\sigma(k)
the set of its
k
-dimensional faces. Elements in
\Sigma(1)
are called
rays of
\Sigma
and will typically be denoted by the letters
\rho
or
\tau
. To each ray
\rho
we can associate a divisor
D_{\rho}^{X}
in
X
and an element
u_{\rho}\in N
, the unique generator of the semigroup
\rho\cap N
.

Recall that
X
is proper if and only if
\cup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, and that
X
is smooth if and only if for each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
it holds that the set
\{u_{\rho}\colon\rho\in\sigma(1)\}
is part of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
. The canonical divisor of
X
can be written as
K_{X}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}^{X}
. We say that a smooth toric variety
X
is Fano if the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample.
If
X
is smooth, Fano and proper, then
-K_{X}
is very ample by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.1.15]
, in particular
X
is projective. In that case, we refer to the closed embedding
X\to\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(-K_{X}))
as the
anticanonical embedding
of
X
.

For a (not necessarily toric) normal variety
X
, we denote by
A_{k}(X)
the group of
k
-dimensional cycles on
X
modulo rational equivalence. If
X
is smooth of dimension
n
, intersection product induces a perfect pairing

A_{k}(X)\times A_{n-k}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}.

In that case, we denote
A^{k}(X)\coloneqq A_{n-k}(X)
. In particular,
A_{1}(X)
is dual to
A^{1}(X)
, which is isomorphic to the group of Cartier divisors modulo principal divisors and also isomorphic to the Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
of isomorphisms classes of invertible sheaves on
X
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper toric variety. The ring
S^{X}=\mathbb{C}[z_{\rho}]_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}
is called the total coordinate ring or the Cox ring of
X
. It has a natural
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
-grading by declaring that
\deg(z_{\rho})=[D_{\rho}^{X}]\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. Such
X
can be written as a geometric quotient

(1)

X\simeq(\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}\setminus Z(\Sigma_{X}))/\!\!/G_{X},

where
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}=\mathrm{Spec}\,(S^{X})
, where
G_{X}=\operatorname{Hom}(A^{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})
and where
Z(\Sigma_{X})
is the zero set of the ideal

B(\Sigma_{X})=\left\langle z^{\hat{\sigma}}\colon\sigma\in\Sigma_{X}(\dim X)\right\rangle\subset S^{X}

with

z^{\hat{\sigma}}=\prod_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}z_{\rho}\in S^{X}

for each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma_{X}
.
A subset
\mathcal{P}
of
\Sigma_{X}(1)
is a
primitive collection
if
\mathcal{P}
is not contained in any cone of
\Sigma_{X}
, but each proper subset of
\mathcal{P}
is contained in some cone of
\Sigma_{X}
. The irreducible decomposition of
Z(\Sigma_{X})
is

(2)

Z(\Sigma_{X})=\bigcup_{\mathcal{P}}V(z_{\rho}\colon\rho\in\mathcal{P}),

where the union is over all the primitive collections
\mathcal{P}
in
\Sigma_{X}(1)
.

2.2.
Morphisms to a smooth toric variety

The functor of points of a smooth toric variety can be described in terms of line bundle-section pairs due to
[
Cox95
]
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth toric variety with fan
\Sigma
in a lattice
N
and let
M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A
\Sigma
-collection on a scheme
S
is a triplet

(L_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)},(s_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)},(c_{m})_{m\in M}

of line bundles
L_{\rho}
on
S
, sections
s_{\rho}\in H^{0}(S,L_{\rho})
and isomorphisms

c_{m}\colon\otimes_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}L_{\rho}^{\otimes\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{S}

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

compatibility:
c_{m}\otimes c_{m^{\prime}}=c_{m+m^{\prime}}
for all
m,m^{\prime}\in M
,

(2)

non-degeneracy: for each
x\in S
there is a maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
s_{\rho}(x)\neq 0
for all
\rho\not\subset\sigma
.

An equivalence between two
\Sigma
-collections on
S
is a collection of isomorphisms among the line bundles that preserve the sections and trivializations.

Consider the functor

\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}\colon\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Set}

that associates to each scheme
S
the collection of
\Sigma
-collections on
S
up to equivalence, with morphisms naturally defined by pull-back. Then
[
Cox95
, Thm 1.1]
states that
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
is represented by
X_{\Sigma}
, with universal family the
\Sigma
-collection on
X_{\Sigma}
that consists of the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}(D_{\rho})
with their natural sections and trivializations
c_{m}
given by the characters
\chi^{m}
of the dense torus in
X_{\Sigma}
.

2.3.
Toric quasimaps

The previous interpretation of the functor of points of a smooth toric variety was used in
[
CFK10
]
to define the notion of quasimaps to a toric variety, by relaxing the non-degeneracy condition above.

Definition 2.3.1
.

(
[
CFK10
, Definition 3.1.1]
)
Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth projective toric variety. A
(prestable toric) quasimap

{q}
to
X_{\Sigma}
consists of

(1)

a connected nodal projective curve
C
of genus
g
with
n
distinct non-singular markings,

(2)

line bundles
L_{\rho}
on
C
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,

(3)

sections
s_{\rho}\in H^{0}(C,L_{\rho})
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
and

(4)

trivializations
c_{m}\colon\otimes_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}L_{\rho}^{\otimes\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
for
m\in M

satisfying

(1)

compatibility:
c_{m}\otimes c_{m^{\prime}}=c_{m+m^{\prime}}
for all
m,m^{\prime}\in M
,

(2)

quasimap non-degeneracy: there is a finite (possible empty) set
B\subseteq C
of nonsingular points disjoint from the markings on
C
, such that for every
x\in C\setminus B
there is a maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
s_{\rho}(x)\neq 0
for all
\rho\not\subset\sigma
.

We will write
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
or
{q}\colon C\dashrightarrow X
to denote a quasimap
{q}
.

Points in the set
B=B_{q}
in
Definition

2.3.1
are called
basepoints
of the quasimap. A toric quasimap defines a regular morphism
C\setminus B\to X_{\Sigma}
. In fact,
[
Cox95
, Thm 1.1]
shows that, locally,
{q}
can be lifted to a morphism to
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}
which factors through
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}\setminus Z(\Sigma_{X})
exactly along
C\setminus B
(see
Equation

1
).

Definition 2.3.2
.

The degree of a toric quasimap
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
is the class
\beta=\beta_{C}\in A_{1}(X_{\Sigma})
determined by the conditions

(3)

\beta\cdot D_{\rho}=\deg(L_{\rho})

for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. Similarly, we associate a curve class
\beta_{C^{\prime}}=\beta_{C^{\prime},{q}}\in A_{1}(X_{\Sigma})
to every irreducible component
C^{\prime}
of
C
by replacing
\deg(L_{\rho})
with
\deg(L_{\rho}\mid_{C^{\prime}})
in
Equation

3
. By
[
CFK10
, Lemma 3.1.3]
,
\beta_{C^{\prime}}
is effective.

If
{q}
has no basepoints, this agrees with the usual notion of degree of a map. However, in the presence of basepoints, the degree of a quasimap does not agree with the degree of its associated regular map by
Proposition

3.4.3
. See
Example

3.1.2
.

2.4.
Moduli space of stable toric quasimaps

The notion of stability for families of toric quasimaps was introduced in
[
CFK10
]
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth projective toric variety and let
\{\alpha_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}
such that
L=\otimes_{\rho}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}(D_{\rho})^{\otimes\alpha_{\rho}}
is a very ample line bundle on
X_{\Sigma}
. Then a toric quasimap
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
to
X_{\Sigma}
is
stable
if the line bundle

\omega_{C}(p_{1}+\ldots+p_{n})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\epsilon}

is ample for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, where
\mathcal{L}=\otimes_{\rho}L_{\rho}^{\otimes\alpha_{\rho}}
and
p_{i}
denote the marked points in
C
.

Remark 2.4.2
.

The notion of a quasimap being stable is independent of the polarization chosen in
Definition

2.4.1
by
[
CFK10
, Lemma 3.1.3]
. Also, stability imposes the inequality
2g-2+n\geq 0
, which we assume from now on.

Definition 2.4.3
.

(
[
CFK10
, Definition 3.1.7]
)
A
family of genus
g
stable toric quasimaps to
X_{\Sigma}
of class
\beta
over
S
is

(1)

a flat, projective morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{C}\to S
of relative dimension one,

(2)

sections
p_{i}\colon S\to\mathcal{C}
of
\pi
for
1\leq i\leq n
,

(3)

line bundles
L_{\rho}
on
\mathcal{C}
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
,

(4)

sections
s_{\rho}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{C},L_{\rho})
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
and

(5)

compatible trivializations
c_{m}\colon\otimes_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}L_{\rho}^{\otimes\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
for
m\in M

such that the restriction of the data to every geometric fibre of
\pi
is a stable toric quasimap of genus
g
and class
\beta
.

Theorem 2.4.4
.

(
[
CFK10
, Theorems 3.2.1, 4.0.1]
)
The moduli space
{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X_{\Sigma},\beta)
of stable quasimaps of degree
\beta
to a smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
from genus-
g

n
-marked curves is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack, of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

The moduli space
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X_{\Sigma},\beta)
of stable maps of degree
\beta
from genus-
g

n
-marked curves to a smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
can be recovered similarly. We define a
(prestable) map
to
X_{\Sigma}
to be a prestable toric quasimap (
Definition

2.3.1
) whose set of basepoints
B
is empty. Then, we impose the following stability condition for maps (which is different from
Definition

2.4.1
):

\omega_{C}(p_{1}+\ldots+p_{n})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{2}

is ample. As a result, every map is a quasimap, but stability need not be preserved. Note that both stability conditions are defined to ensure that the objects have finitely-many automorphisms, so that
{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X_{\Sigma},\beta)
and
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X_{\Sigma},\beta)
are Deligne-Mumford stacks.

2.5.
Functoriality of quasimaps

We fix a (not necessarily toric) closed embedding
\iota\colon X\to Y
between smooth projective toric varieties for the rest of
Section

2.5
. The goal of this subsection is to describe the morphism

{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}:{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(Y,\iota_{\ast}\beta)

associated to
\iota
. We follow
[
BN17
, Appendix B]
.

We denote rays in the fan
\Sigma_{X}
of
X
by
\rho
and rays in the fan
\Sigma_{Y}
of
Y
by
\tau
.
By
[
Cox95
, Theorem 3.2]
, the morphism
\iota
corresponds to a collection of homogeneous polynomials
P_{\tau}\in S^{X}
for
\tau\in\Sigma_{Y}(1)
of degree
d_{\tau}\coloneqq\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D^{Y}_{\tau})\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma_{Y}(1)}\beta_{\tau}\otimes u_{\tau}=0
in
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)\otimes N
.

(2)

(P_{\tau}(x_{\rho}))\notin Z(\Sigma_{Y})
in
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{Y}(1)}
for every
(x_{\rho})\notin Z(\Sigma_{X})
in
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}
.

The relation between
\iota
and
(P_{\tau})
is that the morphism

\tilde{\iota}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{X}(1)}\setminus Z(\Sigma_{X})\to\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma_{Y}(1)}\setminus Z(\Sigma_{Y})

defined by
\tilde{\iota}(x_{\rho})\coloneqq(P_{\tau}(x_{\rho}))

2.6.
Contraction morphism for projective space

Fix
N\geq 1
and let
X_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
There is a natural morphism

c_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\beta),

called the contraction or comparison morphism for
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. It can be described as follows: a stable map is naturally a quasimap, but it is stable as a quasimap if and only if there are no rational tails. A rational tail
T
is a tree of rational components with no marked points and such that
T\cap\overline{(C\setminus T)}
is a singleton (necessarily a node inside
C
). Let
T
be a rational tail of a stable map
f\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
, let
\hat{C}=\overline{C\setminus T}
and let
p=T\cap\hat{C}
.
One must contract
T
and define
\hat{L}_{\rho}=L_{\rho}\mid_{\hat{C}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\hat{C}}(d_{T,\rho}p)
with
d_{T,\rho}=\deg(L_{\rho}\mid_{T})
. Similarly, one must take
\hat{s}_{\rho}=s_{\rho}\otimes z_{x}^{d_{T,\rho}}
, with
z_{x}
a local parameter at
x
in
\hat{C}
. Doing this for every rational tail
T
of
f
produces the stable quasimap
c_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(f)
. The description of
c_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}
for families can be found in
[
PR03
, Theorem 7.1]
.

3.1.
The regular extension of a quasimap

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
{q}
be a quasimap to
X
. Then
{q}
defines a regular morphism

{q}\colon C\setminus B\to X.

Since

is proper and

extending

. We call

of

Example 3.1.2
.

In general,

and

may have different degrees.
Consider the quasimap

{q}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\colon[x\colon y]\mapsto[0\colon 0\colon x]

which has a unique basepoint, at the point
[0\colon 1]
. Its extension is the constant map

Note that

has degree 1 but

has degree 0. In particular, if we add two distinct marks to

is a stable quasimap but

is not a stable map.

3.2.
Definition of the degree of a basepoint

As seen in
Example

3.1.2
, the degree of a quasimap may be different from the degree of its regular extension. We aim to assign a degree to each basepoint in order to explain this discrepancy. Such degree will be constructed using the vanishing order of the sections at a given basepoint.

Given a
nodal curve
C
, a line bundle
L
on
C
, a non-zero section
s\in H^{0}(C,L)
and a nonsingular point
x\in C
, we denote by
\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s)
the vanishing order of
s
at
x
.
Furthermore, we declare that
\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(0)=\infty
and we extend the natural operation and order on the monoid
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
in the standard way. This means that we declare
a+\infty=\infty
and
a<\infty
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. It follows from this definition that
\infty-a=\infty
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

We will use the following basic result on toric geometry, whose proof we include for completeness.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma(\dim X)
. Then the classes
\{[D_{\rho}]\colon\rho\notin\sigma(1)\}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
.

Proof.

The Picard group is generated by the classes
[D_{\rho}]
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, see
[
CLS11
, Thm 4.2.1]
. Let
n=\dim X
and let
\sigma(1)=\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n}\}
. Then the ray generators
\{u_{\rho_{1}},\ldots,u_{\rho_{n}}\}
form a basis of the co-character lattice
N
by smoothness of
\Sigma
. Let
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
be its dual basis in the character lattice
M
. Then

(6)

\mathrm{div}\,(\chi^{m_{i}})=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle D_{\rho}=D_{\rho_{i}}+\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle D_{\rho}.

where
\chi^{m}
is the character corresponding to
m\in M
. Taking linear equivalence, it follows that for
i=1,\ldots,n
the class
[D_{\rho_{i}}]
lies in the subgroup generated by
\{[D_{\rho}]\colon\rho\notin\sigma(1)\}
. We conclude since
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is free of rank
\lvert\Sigma(1)\rvert-n
by smoothness and properness of
X
.
∎

Notation 3.2.2
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a quasimap to
X
and let
x\in C
be a smooth point.

•

We denote by

the irreducible component of

containing

and

•

by

the set of rays
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that

vanishes identically on

.

Construction 3.2.3
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a quasimap to
X
and let
x\in C
be a smooth point.
Given
\sigma\in\Sigma(\dim X)
such that

by the conditions

\beta(x,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho}]=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}})\ \forall\rho\notin\sigma(1).

The class

3.2.1

Similarly, to any cone

and any

(8)

Before we use

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Let
n=\dim X
, let
\sigma\in\Sigma(n)
be a cone with rays
\sigma(1)=\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n}\}
and let
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
be the dual basis to the ray generators
\{u_{\rho_{1}},\ldots,u_{\rho_{n}}\}
. Let
a=(a_{\rho})\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma(1)}
and define
u=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_{\rho}u_{\rho}
. Then for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have that

(9)

a_{\rho_{i}}\geq\beta(a,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho_{i}}]\iff\langle m_{i},u\rangle\geq 0.

In particular,
a_{\rho_{i}}\geq\beta(a,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho_{i}}]
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
if and only if
u\in\sigma
.

Proof.

With the notations of the statement, it is clear that

u\in\sigma\iff\langle m_{i},u\rangle\geq 0\ \forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Therefore, it is enough to prove
the equivalence in (
9
).

Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. By
Equations

6
and
8
, we have that

(10)

\beta(a,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho_{i}}]=-\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle\beta(a,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho}]=-\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}a_{\rho}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle.

On the other hand,

(11)

\langle m_{i},u\rangle=\langle m_{i},\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_{\rho}u_{\rho}\rangle=a_{\rho_{i}}+\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}a_{\rho}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle.

Using
Equations

10
and
11
, we conclude that

a_{\rho_{i}}\geq\beta(a,\sigma)\cdot[D_{\rho_{i}}]\iff a_{\rho_{i}}+\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}a_{\rho}\langle m_{i},u_{\rho}\rangle\geq 0\iff\langle m_{i},u\rangle\geq 0.\qed

Proposition 3.2.5
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})

and let

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}})\geq\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)

Proof.

It suffices to prove that there exists
\sigma\in\Sigma(\dim X)
such that

holds for

and all

and let

3.2.4

(a_{\rho})=(\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}}))

∎

Next, we want to generalize

Remark 3.2.6

Proposition 3.2.7

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a quasimap to
X
and let
x\in C
be a smooth point. There is a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma(\dim X)
and a curve class
\beta_{x}\in A_{1}(X)
satisfying

(1)

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}})=\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]\text{ for all }\rho\notin\sigma(1)

(2)

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}})\geq\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)

(3)

Proof.

It is clear that we are looking for a curve class of the form
\beta_{x}=\beta(a,\sigma)
for certain integers
(a_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}
and some

with the extra conditions:

for every

.

(ii)

, which is equivalent to

3.2.4

If

for all

, as we did in

.

In general, let

be the cone generated by the rays in

, which lies in
\Sigma
by smoothness of

. Let
N_{\tau}
be the sublattice of
N
generated by

and let

. Let

and let

denote its class in

. Since

is complete, so is the closure

of the orbit corresponding to

, which is a toric variety with fan the star of

. It follows that there is a cone

which contains

as a face and such that

in

. It is enough to choose

in such a way that

is a lift of

satisfying

.
∎

Next we show that any two classes satisfying

are equal, independently of the choice of maximal cone

used to define it.

Remark 3.2.8
.

Given a curve class
\beta\in A_{1}(X)
, we have that

\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\beta\cdot[D_{\rho}]\ u_{\rho}=0.

Therefore, for each smooth point

there is a natural isomorphism

\psi_{m,x,\beta}\colon\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\ \beta\cdot[D_{\rho}]\ \langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\ x)=\mathcal{O}_{C}(\langle m,\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\ \beta\cdot[D_{\rho}]\ u_{\rho}\rangle\ x)\to\mathcal{O}_{C}.

For fixed

and

, these isomorphisms satisfy the compatibility

\psi_{m+m^{\prime},x,\beta}=\psi_{m,x,\beta}\otimes\psi_{m^{\prime},x,\beta}.

Proposition 3.2.9
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a quasimap to
X
, let

be a smooth point and let

be a local coordinate at

. There is a unique curve class

such that the following data defines a quasimap to

\displaystyle s^{\prime}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\coloneqq s_{\rho}\otimes z^{-\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=c_{m}\otimes\psi_{-m,x,\beta_{x}},

where

denotes the isomorphism constructed in

Proof.

Firstly, for any curve class

The first term is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{C}
via

and the second one via
\psi_{-m,x,\beta_{x}}
.

Secondly, the function

is a regular section of the line bundle

if and only if

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{x}})\geq\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]\ \forall\rho\in\Sigma(1).

Therefore,

satisfies

in

.

vanishes identically on

is a closed embedding if
\iota
is an epic closed embedding in
Corollary

4.3.4
, and that every smooth projective toric variety admits an epic embedding into a product of projective spaces (
Theorem

4.2.5
).

4.1.
Quasimaps do not embed along closed embeddings

Let
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
be a closed embedding between smooth projective toric varieties. The induced morphism

{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}\colon{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(Y,\iota_{\ast}\beta)

is not a closed embedding in general. Here is an example, which appeared in
[
BN21
, Remark 2.3.3]
, where it is attributed to Ciocan-Fontanine.

Example 4.1.1
.

Let
s\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the Segre embedding, given in coordinates by

s([x\colon y],[z\colon w])=[xz\colon xw\colon yz\colon yw].

Note that
s
is a toric closed embedding. Consider the following two quasimaps from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with homogeneous coordinates
[s\colon t]
, to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
(2,2)

\displaystyle{q_{1}}([s\colon t])

\displaystyle=[s^{2}\colon st],[st\colon t^{2}],

\displaystyle{q_{2}}([s\colon t])

\displaystyle=[st\colon t^{2}],[s^{2}\colon st].

Both of them have the same image under
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}

{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{1}})([s\colon t])={\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{2}})([s\colon t])=[s^{3}t\colon s^{2}t^{2}\colon s^{2}t^{2}\colon st^{3}],

which is a quasimap to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
of degree
4
. This means that
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}

is not a monomorphism, therefore it is not a closed embedding. Note that the quasimaps above are not stable, but the same conclusion holds if we add marked points.

Our contribution is the following: we can use the degree of a basepoint (see
Definition

3.2.10
) to explain why
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}
is not a monomorphism. Both
{q_{1}}
and
{q_{2}}
have the same regular extension
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
, namely, the diagonal embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}([s\colon t])=[s\colon t],[s\colon t].

Let
0=[1\colon 0]
and let
\infty=[0\colon 1]
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then
{q_{1}}
and
{q_{2}}
both have the same basepoints
B_{q_{1}}=B_{q_{2}}=\{0,\infty\}
, but we can still distinguish
{q_{1}}
from
{q_{2}}
because the degrees at the basepoints differ:

\displaystyle\beta_{0,{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=(0,1),

\displaystyle\beta_{\infty,{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=(1,0),

\displaystyle\beta_{0,{q_{2}}}

\displaystyle=(1,0),

\displaystyle\beta_{\infty,{q_{2}}}

\displaystyle=(0,1)

if we identify
A_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
with
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Applying
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}
, both quasimaps not only have the same regular extension and the same set of basepoints, but also

(22)

\beta_{0,{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{1}})}=\beta_{\infty,{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{1}})}=\beta_{0,{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{2}})}=\beta_{\infty,{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q_{2}})}=1\in A_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3}).

Therefore, we can no longer distinguish them, by
Corollary

3.4.2
. Note that, by
Lemma

3.4.4
, the degree of a basepoint is compatible with
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}
and
s_{\ast}
, in the sense that
\beta_{x,{\mathcal{Q}(s)}({q}_{i})}=s_{\ast}\beta_{x,{q}_{i}}
; therefore, the equalities in
Equation

22
follow from the fact that
s_{\ast}(1,0)=s_{\ast}(0,1)=1
.

Example

4.1.1
suggests that the reason why
{\mathcal{Q}(s)}
is not a monomorphism (and therefore the reason why it is not a closed embedding, since it is proper) is that
s_{\ast}\colon A_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\to A_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3})
is not injective. This observation motivates
Theorem

4.3.3
and
Corollary

4.3.4
.

4.2.
Epic morphisms

Motivated by
Example

4.1.1
, we introduce the following class of morphisms.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties whose Picard group is free of finite rank and let
f\colon X\to Y
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Pic}(Y)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is surjective,

(2)

f^{*}\colon A^{1}(Y)\to A^{1}(X)
is surjective,

(3)

f_{*}\colon A_{1}(X)\to A_{1}(Y)
is injective.

Proof.

The equivalence between
Item

1
and
Item

2
holds because for a smooth variety
\mathrm{Pic}
and
A^{1}
are isomorphic.
Finally,
Item

2
and
Item

3
follows from the assumption that the Picard groups are free of finite rank, because
f_{\ast}
and
f^{\ast}
are transpose by the projection formula.
∎

Definition 4.2.2
.

A morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of smooth projective varieties whose Picard group is free of finite rank is
epic
if it satisfies any of the equivalent conditions in
Proposition

4.2.1
. The name is motivated by
Item

1
in
Proposition

4.2.1
: epic morphisms induce an epimorphism on Picard groups.

We are particularly interested in varieties that admit an epic closed embedding in a product of projective spaces.

Proposition 4.2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety such that
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is free of finite rank. The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exist
k,n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1
and an epic closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}
,

(2)

X
admits a generating set of
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
consisting of basepoint free divisors.

Proof.

Suppose
Item

1
holds. Let
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}
and let
L_{i}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
be the pullback of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times H_{i}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}})
with
H_{i}
a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
. Then each
L_{i}
is basepoint free and
\{L_{i}\}
generate
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
because the embedding is epic, so
Item

2
holds.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{S}
be a generating set
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
whose elements are basepoint free, let
[A]\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
be a very ample class and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\cup\{[A]\}
. Consider the induced morphism

\iota\colon X\to\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\prod_{[D]\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)))

associated to the complete linear systems
\lvert D\rvert
, for
[D]\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Then
\iota^{\ast}
is surjective on Picard groups because
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a generating set. Finally, the diagonal arrow in the following diagram is a closed embedding, which implies that so is
\iota
.

Definition 4.2.4
.

A smooth projective variety whose Picard group is free of finite rank is called
projectively epic
if it satisfies any of the equivalenc conditions in
Proposition

4.2.3
.

Theorem 4.2.5
.

Every smooth projective toric variety is projectively epic.

Proof.

The nef and Mori cones of a smooth projective toric variety
X
are dual strongly convex rational polyhedral cones of full dimension by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.3.12]
. In particular, the semigroup of divisor classes which are nef admits a finite generating set
\mathcal{S}
by Gordan’s lemma, which also generates
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. Furthermore, the elements of
\mathcal{S}
are basepoint free by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.3.12]
, therefore
Proposition

4.2.3
holds, so
X
is projectively epic.
∎

Example 4.2.6
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at the origin, which is a toric variety whose fan is pictured in
Figure

2
. Let
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the natural projection and let
H
be the hyperplane class in
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. The group
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is generated by
L\coloneqq\pi^{*}H
and the class
E
of the exceptional divisor. Let
S=L-E
, which is the class of the strict transform of a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then the nef cone of
X
is generated by
S
and
L
.

There are four toric boundary divisors in
X
, which we denote by
D_{i}=D_{\rho_{i}}
, with the conventions in
Figure

2
. They satisfy that

\displaystyle[D_{0}]

\displaystyle=L,

\displaystyle[D_{1}]=[D_{2}]

\displaystyle=S,

\displaystyle[D_{3}]

\displaystyle=E.

Using this, together with the description of global sections of toric line bundles, one can easily describe in coordinates the following closed embeddings of
X
into products of projective spaces induced by complete linear systems. Such morphisms will be written using the ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
of homogeneous coordinates on
X
, with
x_{i}
corresponding to
\rho_{i}
.

The set
\{S,L\}
generates the nef cone of
X
. Although none of the two classes is ample, the morphism

\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\times\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(S))=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

is an epic closed embedding. One way to see that
\iota
is indeed a closed embedding is to realize that this is the usual description of
X
as a closed subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Its expression in coordinates is

(23)

\iota(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=[x_{0}\colon x_{1}x_{3}\colon x_{2}x_{3}],[x_{1}\colon x_{2}].

This example illustrates that, in the notation of the proof of
Proposition

4.2.3
, it may not be necessary that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
contains a very ample class if the elements of
\mathcal{S}
already induce a closed embedding.

The set
\{S,S+L\}
also generates
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, both classes are nef and
S+L
is ample (thus very ample).
It induces a closed embedding

i\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(S))\times\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(S+L))=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{4}

with

i(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=[x_{1}\colon x_{2}],[x_{0}x_{2}\colon x_{0}x_{1}\colon x_{2}^{2}x_{3}\colon x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\colon x_{1}^{2}x_{3}].

Similarly, one could take the set
\{S+L,L\}
, which also generates
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, with
L
nef and
S+L
ample. The corresponding closed embedding is

j\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(S+L))\times\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))=\mathbb{P}^{4}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

with

j(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=[x_{0}x_{2}\colon x_{0}x_{1}\colon x_{2}^{2}x_{3}\colon x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\colon x_{1}^{2}x_{3}],[x_{0},x_{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{3}].

4.3.
A criterion for quasimaps to embed along an embedding

We fix a closed embedding
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
between smooth projective toric varieties for the rest of
Section

4.3
and we study the morphism

{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}\colon{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(Y,\iota_{\ast}\beta)

on closed points. For that, we start with two lemmas, which apply more generally to (prestable) toric quasimaps, see
Remark

2.5.3
.

Recall that we denote by
B_{q}
the set of basepoints of
{q}
(see
Definition

2.3.1
). In
Definition

3.1.1
, we introduced the notion of regular extension
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
of a quasimap
{q}
to
X
. In this section, we denote
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
by
r_{X}({q})
. Similarly,
r_{Y}({q}^{\prime})
denotes the regular extension of a quasimap
{q}^{\prime}
to
Y
.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

For every quasimap
{q}
to
X
, we have that

r_{Y}({\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}{q})={\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\iota)}(r_{X}{q}).

Proof.

The two maps have the same underlying curve
C
by
Remark

2.5.2
and they are both extensions of the map
\iota\circ({q}\mid_{C\setminus B})
from the dense open
C\setminus B
to
C
, so they must agree.
∎

Lemma 4.3.2
.

For every quasimap
{q}
to
X
, we have that

B_{q}=B_{{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}({q})}.

Proof.

Follows from
Lemma

3.4.4
and
Proposition

3.4.3
because the pushforward along a closed embedding of an effective non-zero class is a non-zero class.
∎

Theorem 4.3.3
.

Let
\beta\in A_{1}(X)
be an effective non-zero curve class and let
{q}
be an
n
-marked genus-
g
quasimap to
Y
of class
\iota_{\ast}\beta

with underlying curve
C
. Consider the morphism induced by
\iota
on prestable quasimaps

{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}\colon{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{pre}}}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{pre}}}(Y,\iota_{\ast}\beta).

Then the fibre
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}^{-1}({q})
of
{q}
along
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}
is non-empty if and only if

(1)

there exists a morphism
f\colon C\to X
such that
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}=\iota\circ f
and

(2)

there exist effective non-zero classes
(\beta^{x})_{x\in B_{q}}
in
A_{1}(X)
such that

(a)

\iota_{\ast}(\beta^{x})=\beta_{x,{q}}
for all
x\in B_{q}
,

(b)

\beta=\beta_{f}+\sum_{x\in B_{q}}\beta^{x}
and

(c)

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(t_{\rho})+\beta^{x}\cdot D_{\rho}\geq 0
for every
\rho\in\Sigma_{X}(1)
and every
x\in B_{q}

with
\{t_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in\Sigma_{X}(1)}
the underlying sections of
f
.

Furthermore, the fibre
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}^{-1}({q})
is in one-to-one correspondence with the (finite) set of effective non-zero classes
(\beta^{x})_{x\in B_{q}}
in
A_{1}(X)
satisfying
Items

2a
,
2b
and
2c
.

If, moreover,
{q}
is stable, then so is every element in
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}^{-1}({q})
.

Proof.

Firstly, let
{q_{1}}
be a quasimap to
X
of class
\beta
such that
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}{q_{1}}={q}
. By
Lemma

4.3.1
,

{\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\iota)}(r_{X}{q_{1}})=r_{Y}({\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}{q_{1}})=r_{Y}({q}).

This means that
r_{Y}({q})={q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
factors through
X
and we can take
f\coloneqq r_{X}({q_{1}})={q_{1,\mathrm{reg}}}
. Using
Lemma

4.3.2
, the classes
\beta^{x}\coloneqq\beta_{x,{q_{1}}}
, for
x\in B_{q}
, satisfy the desired conditions. Indeed,
Item

2a
follows from
Lemma

3.4.4
,
Item

2b
from
Proposition

3.4.3
and
Item

2c
from
Corollary

3.4.1
, since

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(t_{\rho})+\beta_{x,{q_{1}}}\cdot D_{\rho}=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}^{(1)})\geq 0,

where
s_{\rho}^{(1)}
denote the sections of
{q_{1}}
.

Conversely, let
{q}
be a quasimap to
Y
of class
\iota_{\ast}\beta
and let
f
and
\beta^{x}
, for
x\in B_{q}
, be as in the statement.
Since
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\iota)}
is a closed embedding, the morphism
f
is determined uniquely by the condition
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}=\iota\circ f
. We show how to use the data in
Item

2
to construct a unique quasimap
{q_{1}}
to
X
of class
\beta
such that
{\mathcal{Q}(\iota)}{q_{1}}={q}
. We fix that
{q_{1,\mathrm{reg}}}=f
, and then we twist the line bundle-section pairs associated to
f
at each basepoint
x\in B_{q}
as in
Corollary

3.4.1
, but changing the minus sign by a plus sign. This makes
x
a basepoint of
{q_{1,\mathrm{reg}}}
with class
\beta^{x}
for every
x\in B_{q}
. By
Corollary

3.4.2
, the previous construction clearly determines a quasimap
{q_{1}}
uniquely.

Finally, we show that if
{q}
is stable, then so is
{q_{1}}
. This follows from the fact that
{q}
and
{q_{1}}
have the same curve (and marks) and the fact that components contracted by

Corollary 4.3.4
.

Let

{q}([s_{0}\colon s_{1}],t)=[s_{0}\colon ts_{0}\colon s_{1}\colon 1]),

with a basepoint at the point
t=s_{1}=0
, the origin in
W_{0}
.

On the other hand,
\widetilde{f}
can also be extended to a morphism

\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{Bl}_{s=t=0}W\to\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{P}^{2},

which we view as a family of maps over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. If we view
\widetilde{W}
as the closed subvariety cut-out by
u_{1}t-s_{1}u_{0}
inside
C\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{[u_{0}\colon u_{1}]}
, then the expression of
\widetilde{f}
is

\widetilde{f}([s_{0}\colon s_{1}],t,[u_{0}\colon u_{1}])=[u_{1}s_{0}\colon u_{0}s_{0}\colon u_{1}\colon u_{1}s_{1}].

The special fibre
\widetilde{W}_{0}
has two components
T=V(s_{1})
and
\widetilde{C}=V(u_{0})
. The restriction of
\widetilde{f}
to each of them is

\widetilde{f}\mid_{T}([u_{0}\colon u_{1}])=[u_{1}\colon u_{0}\colon u_{1}\colon 0],

of degree
E
, and

\widetilde{f}\mid_{\widetilde{C}}([s_{0}\colon s_{1}])=[s_{0}\colon 0\colon 1\colon s_{1}],

of degree
S
.

Remark

5.2.3
tells us that we can read that
\deg(\widetilde{f}\mid_{T})=E
directly from
{q}
, by checking that
\beta_{x}=\beta_{x,{q}_{0}}=E
, where
x
is the point
s=t=0
in
W
. We check the claim in this particular example.

For that, we need to compute
\beta_{x}
following
Proposition

3.2.7
. Firstly,

{q}_{0}([s_{0}\colon s_{1}])=[s_{0}\colon 0\colon s_{1}\colon 1])

has the following orders of vanishing at
x
:
(\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{i}))=(0,\infty,1,0)
, and
V_{x}=\{\rho_{1}\}
. Let
\sigma_{i,j}
denote the cone in
Figure

2
spanned by
\rho_{i}
and
\rho_{j}
. The only cones
\sigma\in\Sigma(2)
such that
V_{x}\subseteq\sigma
are
\sigma_{0,1}
and
\sigma_{1,3}
. The former cone gives

\displaystyle\beta(x,\sigma_{0,1})\cdot D_{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\beta(x,\sigma_{0,1})\cdot D_{3}

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore
\beta(x,\sigma_{0,1})=L
, but it does not satisfy the condition in
Proposition

3.2.7
because

0=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{0})\not\geq\beta(x,\sigma_{0,1})\cdot D_{0}=L\cdot L=1.

Instead, we must take
\sigma_{1,3}
, which gives

\displaystyle\beta(x,\sigma_{1,3})\cdot D_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\beta(x,\sigma_{1,3})\cdot D_{2}

\displaystyle=1.

Therefore
\beta(x,\sigma_{1,3})=E
, and one easily checks that

\displaystyle\infty=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\beta(x,\sigma_{1,3})\cdot D_{1}=E\cdot S=1,

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{3})

\displaystyle\geq\beta(x,\sigma_{1,3})\cdot D_{3}=E\cdot E=-1.

So

6.
Surjectivity for Fano targets

We have introduced the contraction morphism
c_{X}
between stable maps and stable quasimaps for smooth projective toric varieties in
5.1.2
. In this section, we prove that
c_{X}
is surjective if the target
X
is Fano.

Theorem 6.0.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano toric variety. The contraction morphism

c_{X}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}(X,\beta)

defined in
5.1.2
is surjective.

The proof of
Theorem

6.0.1
is delayed until
Section

6.3
. Before that, we introduce some terminology about the monoid of effective curve classes in
Section

6.1
and an important construction in
Section

6.2
.

We fix a smooth toric Fano variety
X
for the rest of
Section

6
.

6.1.
Factorizations of curve classes

Definition 6.1.1
.

Let
\beta
be a non-zero effective curve class in
X
. A
factorization
of
\beta
is an expression
\beta=\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{k}
such that
k\geq 2
and
\beta_{i}
is non-zero and effective for every
i
. We say that
\beta
is
irreducible
if it does not admit any factorization.

Definition 6.1.2
.

The
length
of a curve class
\beta\in A_{1}(X)
is

\lambda(\beta)=\deg(\iota_{\ast}\beta)=\beta\cdot(-K_{X})=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\beta\cdot D_{\rho},

with
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
the anticanonical embedding.

Remark 6.1.3
.

The length
\lambda
defines a morphism of semigroups with unit
\lambda\colon A_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover,

(1)

if
\beta
is effective then
\lambda(\beta)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
\beta=0
and

(2)

if
\beta
is non-zero and effective and if
\beta=\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{k}
is a factorization, then
\lambda(\beta)>\lambda(\beta_{i})
for all
i
.

6.2.
Grafting trees on to quasimaps

We explain how to replace a basepoint of a quasimap with a rational curve and how to extend the quasimap there given certain extra data. This construction is fundamental for the proof of
Theorem

6.0.1
.

Construction 6.2.1
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a quasimap to
X
of degree
\beta
. Suppose for simplicity that
{q}
has a unique basepoint
x
and let
\beta_{x}
be the degree of
{q}
at
x
. Let
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}=(C,L^{\prime}_{\rho},s^{\prime}_{\rho},c^{\prime}_{m})
denote the regular extension of
{q}
, constructed in
Definition

3.1.1
.

Given a rational irreducible curve
T\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
, a point
x_{T}\in T
and sections
t_{\rho}\in H^{0}(T,\mathcal{O}_{T}(\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]))
such that
t_{\rho}(x_{T})=s^{\prime}_{\rho}(x)
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, we can construct a new quasimap

{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}=(\tilde{C},\tilde{L}_{\rho},\tilde{s}_{\rho},\tilde{c}_{m})

of class
\beta
by
grafting
T
on to
{q}
at
x
as follows:

•

The curve
\tilde{C}
is obtained by gluing
C
and
T
along
x\in C
and
x_{T}\in T
. By abuse of notation, we denote the resulting node in
\tilde{C}
also by
x
.

•

For each
\rho\in\Sigma_{X}(1)
, the line bundle
\tilde{L}_{\rho}
on
\tilde{C}
is obtained by gluing the line bundle
L^{\prime}_{\rho}
on
C
and the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{T}(\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}])
on
T
.

•

The sections
\tilde{s}_{\rho}
of
\tilde{L}_{\rho}
are obtained by gluing the sections
t_{\rho}
and
s^{\prime}_{\rho}
.

•

Finally, for each
m\in M
, the isomorphisms
\tilde{c}_{m}
on
\tilde{C}
are obtained by gluing the isomorphisms
c^{\prime}_{m}\colon\otimes_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}{L^{\prime}}_{\rho}^{\otimes\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
and the isomorphisms
\psi_{m,\beta_{x}}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T}(\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\ \beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]\ \langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{T}
analogous to those constructed in
Remark

3.2.8
.

The defining data of
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
automatically satisfies generically non-degeneracy. This is clear on
C
, while on
T
it follows from the fact that
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
is non-degenerate at
x
.

Remark 6.2.2
.

With the notations of
6.2.1
, the regular extension
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
of a quasimap
{q}
has the following property: a section
s_{\rho}
is identically zero on an irreducible component
C^{\prime}
of
C
if and only if
s^{\prime}_{\rho}
is identically zero on
C^{\prime}
.

It follows that, for each basepoint
x
of
{q}
, we can find sections
t_{\rho}
satisfying the assumptions of
6.2.1
. Indeed, we can choose
t_{\rho}
as follows

•

if
\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]<0
, we must take
t_{\rho}=0
and the gluing condition holds because
s^{\prime}_{\rho}(x)=0
by
Corollary

3.4.1
,

•

if
\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]=0
then we must take
t_{\rho}
constant with value
s^{\prime}_{\rho}(x)
and

•

if
\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]>0
then we can choose any section
t_{\rho}\in H^{0}(T,\mathcal{O}_{T}(\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]))
with
t_{\rho}(x_{T})=s^{\prime}_{\rho}(x)
.

Remark 6.2.3
.

Grafting can be undone. With the notations of
6.2.1
, we can recover
{q}
from
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
by contracting
T
, restricting the rest of the data to
C
and twisting with the degree
\beta_{T}
of
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
on
T
analogously to
Proposition

5.2.1
. In that case, we say that
{q}
is obtained by
pruning
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
along
T
. By
Proposition

5.2.1
, pruning all the rational tails of a stable map
f\colon C\to X
we recover the quasimap
c_{X}(f)
.

Remark 6.2.4
.

6.2.1
can be extended to quasimaps
{q}
with more than one basepoints. The grafting of
T
on to
{q}
at a basepoint
x
, is obtained by replacing in
6.2.1
the map
{q_{\mathrm{reg}}}
by the quasimap obtained by extending
{q}
only at
x
. The description of such quasimap can be obtained using
Corollary

3.4.1
, replacing
B
in the formulas by the singleton
\{x\}
. Note that if we graft a quasimap
{q}
along all its basepoints
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
, the basepoints of the resulting quasimap
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
must lie on the grafted rational curves
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
.

6.3.
Proof of surjectivity

Proof of theorem
6.0.1
.

The morphism
c_{X}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is locally of finite type, therefore by
[
Sta22
, Lemma 0487]
it is enough to show that
c_{X}
is surjective on closed points.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a
stable quasimap to
X
in
{\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
. We show by induction on
\lambda(\beta)
(see
Definition

6.1.2
) that there exists a stable map
f
to
X
in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}(X,\beta)
with
c_{X}(f)={q}
.

If
\lambda(\beta)=0
, then there are no basepoints by
Proposition

3.4.3
. This means that
{q}
is itself a stable map and there is nothing to show.

If
C
has basepoints
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
, grafting an irreducible rational curve
T_{i}
at each basepoint
x_{i}
induces an equality of curve classes

(25)

\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{x_{i}}+(\beta-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{x_{i}}),

where
\beta_{x_{i}}\neq 0
for all
i
by
Proposition

3.4.3
. If
\ell\geq 2
,
Equation

25
is a factorization, and we can use
Remark

6.1.3
to conclude by induction.

Therefore, we can restrict to the case that
\lambda(\beta)>0
and
C
has only one basepoint
x
. Furthermore, by restricting ourselves to the irreducible component containing the basepoint
x
, we can assume that
C
is irreducible. Let
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
be obtained by grafting an irreducible rational curve
T_{1}
to
{q}
at
x
. Then we get an expression

\beta=\beta_{x}+(\beta-\beta_{x})

with
\beta_{x}\neq 0
. If
\beta-\beta_{x}
is also non-zero, we conclude by induction on
\lambda
. Thus, we can assume that
\beta=\beta_{x}
.

In other words, we only need to deal with the particular case that on the new curve
C\cup T_{1}
the degree is 0 on
C
and
\beta
on
T_{1}
. The strategy is to continue grafting irreducible rational curves to
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
at the new basepoints, which necessarily lie on
T_{1}
(see
Remark

6.2.4
). Again, the only problematic case is if there is a unique basepoint
x_{1}
of
{q_{\mathrm{gr}}}
on
T_{1}
such that
\beta_{x_{1}}=\beta
, because we could enter a loop and end up with an infinite chain of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s. To rule this out, we use
Lemma

6.3.1
as follows: when grafting
T_{1}
to
{q}
, we are allowed to choose the sections
t_{\rho}
on
T_{1}
(satisfying the conditions in
6.2.1
). If we show that we can choose the sections
t_{\rho}
with the property that

(26)

\text{there is }t_{\rho}\neq 0\text{ such that }\mathrm{ord}\,_{x_{1}}(t_{\rho})<\beta\cdot D_{\rho},

then
Lemma

6.3.1
(see also
Remark

6.2.3
), applied to
C_{1}=C\cup T_{1}
and
C_{2}=T_{2}
, ensures that when we graft
T_{2}
we cannot have
\beta_{T_{1}}=0
and
\beta_{T_{2}}=\beta
, and so we conclude by induction.

To get (
26
) for a specific class
\beta
, it suffices to show one of the following:

(27)

\text{there exists }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\text{ such that }s_{\rho}(x)=0,s_{\rho}\neq 0\text{ and }\beta\cdot D_{\rho}\geq 2,

or

(28)

\text{ there exist }\rho\neq\rho^{\prime}\in\Sigma(1)\text{ such that }\beta\cdot D_{\rho}=\beta\cdot D_{\rho^{\prime}}=1\text{ and at most one is }0\text{ on }C_{x}.

In both cases, it is clear we can choose
t_{\rho}
on
T_{1}
to have simple disjoint zeroes, which ensures (
26
).

To apply this strategy, we distinguish two cases, depending on whether
\beta
is irreducible or not. In the latter case we shall use that
X
is Fano, which is not needed in the former case.

Firstly, we assume that
C
is irreducible and has a unique basepoint
x
, that
\beta
is irreducible with
\lambda(\beta)>0
and that
\beta_{x}=\beta
. Since
\beta
is irreducible, it can be represented by the closure of the toric orbit associated to a wall
\tau\in\Sigma
; that is,
\beta=[V(\tau)]
.
By
[
CLS11
, Proposition 6.4.4]
, there exist two distinct rays
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\Sigma(1)
such that
\beta\cdot D_{\rho_{i}}=1
for
i=1,2
.
By
Lemma

6.3.2
, there is
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
s_{\rho}(x)=0
,
s_{\rho}\neq 0
and
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}>0
.
If
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}\geq 2
, we are done by (
27
).
Otherwise,
either
\{\rho,\rho_{1}\}
or
\{\rho,\rho_{2}\}

satisfy (
28
).

Finally, we deal with the case where
\beta
is not irreducible. We also assume, as before, that
\lambda(\beta)>0
,
that
C
is irreducible, that there is a unique basepoint
x\in C
and that
\beta=\beta_{x}
.

By
Lemma

6.3.2
, there is
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
s_{\rho}(x)=0
,
s_{\rho}\neq 0
and
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}>0
.

We number the rays in
\Sigma(1)
as
\rho=\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{r}
. Since
X
is Fano, the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{\rho_{i}}
is very ample (by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.1.15]
). Let
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the corresponding closed embedding. Since
\beta
is not irreducible, let
\beta=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}
be a factorization. Then

(29)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta\cdot D_{\rho_{i}}=\deg(\iota_{\ast}\beta)=\deg(\iota_{\ast}\beta_{1})+\deg(\iota_{\ast}\beta_{2})\geq 2.

It follows that either
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}\geq 2
, and then we conclude by (
27
), or there is
i\neq 1
such that
\beta\cdot D_{\rho_{1}}\geq 1
, and then
\{\rho,\rho_{i}\}
satisfy (
28
).

As a result of the induction argument, we end up with a prestable map
f
to
X
whose contraction is the original quasimap
{q}
. If
f
is not stable, we can stabilize it (by contracting its irreducible components of degree 0 with no marks). The result is a stable map
f
which lies in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
by construction. More precisely, one only needs to check the condition in
Proposition

5.2.1
, and it follows from the following observation: in
6.2.1
, we have that

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(\tilde{s}_{\rho}\mid_{C})+\beta_{T_{i}}\cdot[D_{\rho}]=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s^{\prime}_{\rho}\mid_{C})+\beta_{x}\cdot[D_{\rho}]=\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho})\geq 0.

It is also clear that
f
has degree
\beta
since the total curve class is preserved by grafting.
∎

We conclude with two lemmas used in the proof of
Theorem

6.0.1
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
{q}
be a quasimap with underlying curve
C
consisting of two irreducible components
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
glued along a point
x
. Suppose
C_{2}
has genus 0.
Let
{q_{1}}
and
{q_{2}}
be the restrictions of
{q}
to
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
respectively. Assume
\beta_{C_{1}}=0
and
\beta_{C_{2}}\neq 0
. Let
{q^{\prime}}
be the quasimap obtained
by pruning
{q}
along
C_{2}
.
Then for every
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
s^{\prime}_{\rho}\neq 0
, or equivalently such that
s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{1}}\neq 0
, the following holds

\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s^{\prime}_{\rho})=\beta_{C_{2}}\cdot D_{\rho}=\beta^{\prime}\cdot D_{\rho}.

Proof.

By definition,

s^{\prime}_{\rho}=s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{1}}\cdot z^{\beta_{C_{2}}\cdot D_{\rho}},

with
z
a local coordinate at
x
inside
C_{1}
. Therefore,
s^{\prime}_{\rho}=0
if and only if
s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{1}}=0
. Furthermore, if
s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{1}}\neq 0
then
\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s_{\rho}\mid_{C_{1}})=0
because
\beta_{C_{1}}=0
, and
\mathrm{ord}\,_{x}(s^{\prime}_{\rho})=\beta_{C_{2}}\cdot D_{\rho}=\beta^{\prime}\cdot D_{\rho}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3.2
.

Let
{q}=(C,L_{\rho},s_{\rho},c_{m})
be a
stable quasimap to
X
of degree
\beta
with
C
irreducible and let
x
be a basepoint of
{q}
. Then there exists
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
s_{\rho}(x)=0
but
s_{\rho}\neq 0
. In particular,
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}>0
.

Proof.

Since
x
is a basepoint, by
Equation

2
there is a primitive collection
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\Sigma(1)
such that
s_{\rho}(x)=0
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}
. By generic non-degeneracy, there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{P}
such that
s_{\rho}
is not identically 0. The existence of
s_{\rho}
ensures that
\beta\cdot D_{\rho}=\deg(L_{\rho})>0
.
∎

Remark 6.3.3
.

Theorem

6.0.1
also holds for
X
smooth projective toric with the following property: for each effective non-irreducible non-zero curve class
\beta\in A_{1}(X)
such that there exists
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
with

, there exists
\rho^{\prime}\neq\rho
such that

.

This condition is true if

is Fano by the argument involving

.

A way to control this condition is to look at

\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\beta\cdot D_{\rho}=\beta\cdot(-K_{X}).

For example, the above condition holds if
\-K_{X}\cdot\beta\geq 2
for every effective non-zero curve class

. But, in that case,
X
is Fano as
-K_{X}
is very ample by
[
CLS11
, Theorems 6.1.15 and 6.3.22]
.

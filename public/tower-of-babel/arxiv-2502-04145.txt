Analysis for non-local phase transitions close to the critical exponent s = 1 2 s=\frac{1}{2}
Marco Picerni

Abstract




Abstract

We analyze the behaviour of double-well energies perturbed by fractional Gagliardo squared seminorms in
H^{s}
close to the critical exponent
s=\frac{1}{2}
. This is done by computing a scaling factor
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)
, continuous in both variables, such that

\mathcal{F}^{s_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{\lambda(\varepsilon,s_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon}\int W(u)\,dt+\lambda(\varepsilon,s_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)^{+}}[u]_{{s_{\varepsilon}}}^{2}

\Gamma
-converge, for any choice of
s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, to the sharp-interface functional found by Alberti, Bouchitté and Seppecher in
[
1
]
with the scaling
{|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}}
. Moreover, we prove that all the values
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
are regular points for the functional
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
in the sense of equivalence by
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
5
]
), and that the
\Gamma
-limits as
\varepsilon\to 0
are continuous with respect to
s
. In particular, the corresponding surface tensions, given by suitable non-local optimal-profile problems, are continuous on
[\frac{1}{2},1)
.

35B25, 35G20, 35R11, 49J45, 74A50, 82B26

Keywords:
Non-convex energies, fractional Sobolev spaces,
\Gamma
-convergence, phase transitions, Cahn-Hilliard functional, singular perturbations.

1.
Introduction and outline

Singular perturbations are often used to select solutions of non-convex problems with multiplicity of minimizers. In the case of theories of phase transitions, usually the non-convex problem at hand is an integral depending on a scalar variable
u
through a “double-well potential”
W
(that is, a function with two minimizers
\alpha
and
\beta
). In the classical Cahn–Hilliard theory of phase transitions
[
7
]
such an energy is singularly perturbed by a term with the gradient of
u
depending on a small parameter
\varepsilon
as

\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dt+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, minimizers under a volume constraint converge to a function taking only the values
\alpha
and
\beta
, and such that the interface between these two phases is minimal. This minimal-interface criterion had been conjectured by Gurtin
[
11
]
and proven by Modica
[
13
]
using the
\Gamma
-convergence (cf.
[
8
]
,
[
3
]
) results of a previous seminal paper by Modica and Mortola
[
14
]
, obtaining that the functionals above, scaled by
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,
\Gamma
-converge to a functional defined on the space
BV(\Omega;\{\alpha,\beta\})
of functions with bounded variation taking only the values
\alpha
and
\beta
by

m_{W}\hbox{\rm Per}(\{u=\alpha\};\Omega),

where Per
(A;\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of
A
in
\Omega
and
m_{W}
(the
surface tension
between the phases) is a constant determined by
W
only. Since functions in
BV(\Omega;\{\alpha,\beta\})
can be identified with sets of finite perimeter
\{u=\alpha\}
, this result provides a proof of the minimal-interface criterion.
It is interesting that this result is one-dimensional in that
m_{W}
is characterized by the problems

m_{W}=\min\Bigl\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(W(v)+|\nabla v|^{2})\,dt:v(-\infty)=\alpha,\ v(+\infty)=\beta\Bigr\}

(see
[
10
]
). The result in
[
13
]
has been extended in many ways, and in particular using higher-order gradients as in
[
9
]
and
[
6
]
, with analogous formulas for the corresponding surface tension, depending on the order of the perturbation.

Motivated by an interest in non-local problems, perturbations have also been taken in fractional Sobolev spaces,
with functionals of the form

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dx+\varepsilon^{2s}[u]_{{s}}^{2}

where
[u]_{s}
denotes the Gagliardo seminorm in the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega)
with
s\in(0,1)
(see the works of Alberti, Bouchitté, and Seppecher
[
1
]

[
2
]
, Savin and Valdinoci
[
16
]
and Palatucci-Vincini
[
15
]
). If
s>\frac{1}{2}
then the scaling by
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
as in the Modica–Mortola case gives functionals

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dx+\varepsilon^{2s-1}[u]_{{s}}^{2}\,,

which still provide a
\Gamma
-limit of perimeter type as above, with a surface tension
m_{s}
depending on
s
. Recently, the result has been proved to hold also for higher-order fractional perturbations by Solci
[
17
]
, who also shows that the functionals can be slightly modified in such a way that the corresponding surface tension
m_{s}
is a continuous function for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},+\infty)
. A modification is necessary by the critical behaviour of the fractional norms at integer points.

The case
s=\frac{1}{2}
is
critical
, in the sense that the scaling by
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
makes the coefficient in front of the
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
-seminorm equal to
1
, so that, in order to obtain a phase-transition energy of perimeter type, in this case it is necessary to scale by a further logarithmic factor; that is, to consider functionals

\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega}W(u)\,dx+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}[u]_{{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\,.

With this scaling, the
\Gamma
-limit is still an energy of perimeter type with the explicit surface tension
m_{\frac{1}{2}}=8
.

In the case
s<\frac{1}{2}
, finally, Savin and Valdinoci have shown that the functionals scaled by
\varepsilon^{-2s}
have a non-local phase-transition limit in which the domain are
H^{s}
functions taking only the values
\alpha
and
\beta
.

In this paper we aim at describing more in detail the behaviour of functionals (
1.1
) for
s

close
to
\frac{1}{2}
. To that end, we introduce functionals

(1.2)

F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)dt+\varepsilon^{(2s-1)^{+}}\iint_{(0,1)\times(0,1)}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}dxdy.

Here,
W
is a double-well potential satisfying the following assumptions:

•

W
is continuous;

•

W\geq 0
and
W(z)=0
if and only if
z=\pm 1
;

•

\liminf\limits_{z\to\pm\infty}W(z)>0
.

Using the approach by
[
5
]
, in order to provide a description as
s\to\frac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon\to 0
,
we investigate the behaviour of the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}^{s}
under the assumption that
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
The question then translates in finding a scaling factor
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)
depending continuously on its variables such that

\lambda(\varepsilon,s_{\varepsilon})F_{\varepsilon}^{s_{\varepsilon}}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all choices of
s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}
.

Note that a particular case is
s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}
for all
\varepsilon
,
for which, in this one-dimensional case, the asymptotic result is that

(1.3)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}F_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}=F_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{cases}m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u)&\text{if }u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

with
m_{\frac{1}{2}}=8
.
This remark implies that we may take
\lambda(\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}
. Note that if the wells of
W
are in two points
\alpha<\beta
, the surface tension
m_{\frac{1}{2}}
is equal to
2(\beta-\alpha)^{2}
. All the results we prove in this paper also hold in such generality.

In Sections
2
and
3
we prove that such a scaling is

\lambda(\varepsilon,s)=\begin{cases}\frac{2s-1}{1-{\varepsilon}^{2s-1}}&\hbox{ if }s>\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}&\hbox{ if }s=\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{2s-1}{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s}{2s}}-1}&\hbox{ if }s<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

In terms of asymptotic behaviour, in particular, this implies that in the regime

\left|2s_{\varepsilon}-1\right|<<\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|},

we have a
separation of scales
effect; that is, the
\Gamma
-limit of
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)F_{\varepsilon}^{s}
is the same as the one obtained by first letting
s\to\frac{1}{2}
with
\varepsilon>0
fixed, which gives
\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}F_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}
, and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Furthermore, in Section
4
we show that this analysis extends to
s_{\varepsilon}\to s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
by studying the behaviour of the surface tensions

(1.4)

m_{s}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(u)dt+\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}dxdy\mid u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),u(\pm\infty)=\pm 1\right\},

as
s\to\frac{1}{2}
and proving that

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}}(2s-1)m_{s}=m_{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

This condition gives a continuity of the description by the scaled functionals. Indeed, since for
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
s_{\varepsilon}\to s
we have

\lambda(\varepsilon,s_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-1}\to 2s-1

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lambda(\varepsilon,s_{\varepsilon})F_{\varepsilon}^{s_{\varepsilon}}=\begin{cases}(2s-1)m_{s}\#S(u)&\text{if }u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which tends to
F_{0}^{1/2}
as
s\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
. Again, we highlight a separation of scales effect if
s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
and

2s_{\varepsilon}-1>>\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

In this case, the
\Gamma
-limit of
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)F_{\varepsilon}^{s}
is the same as the one obtained first letting
\varepsilon\to 0
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
fixed, which gives
(2s-1)\frac{m_{s}}{m_{1/2}}F_{0}^{1/2}
, and then letting
s\to\frac{1}{2}
.

These results can be expressed in the terminology of
\Gamma
-expansions
[
5
]

as the equivalence of the functionals
F^{s}_{\varepsilon}
and the functionals

G^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\begin{cases}(1-{\varepsilon}^{2s-1})m_{s}F_{0}(u)&\text{if }\varepsilon>\frac{1}{2}\\
|\log\varepsilon|m_{\frac{1}{2}}F_{0}(u)&\text{if }\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where

F_{0}=\begin{cases}\#S(u)&\text{if }u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
s
varying uniformly on compact sets of
[\frac{1}{2},1)
. We note that this result can be extended to all
compact subsets of
[\frac{1}{2},+\infty)
upon taking the correct extension to higher-order fractional perturbations as in
[
17
]
.

2.
Finding the correct scaling factor

In this section, we consider a sequence
(s_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}.

Our aim is to find a scaling factor
\lambda(\varepsilon)
such that the functionals

\lambda(\varepsilon)F^{s_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}
,
where
F^{s_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}
are given by (
1.2
),
\Gamma
-converge to
F_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. If
s=\frac{1}{2}
then the result by Alberti, Bouchitté and Seppecher gives
\lambda(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}
, so we can suppose
s_{\varepsilon}\neq\frac{1}{2}
.

Before beginning, we recall that the double-well potential
W
satisfies the following assumptions:

(1)

W
is continuous;

(2)

W\geq 0
and
W(z)=0
if and only if
z=\pm 1
;

(3)

\liminf\limits_{z\to\pm\infty}W(z)>0
.

Moreover, for
s\in(0,1)
and
v\in H^{s}(0,1)
, we define the Gagliardo seminorm
[v]_{s}
by

(2.1)

[v]_{s}^{2}=\iint_{(0,1)\times(0,1)}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

and its localized version
[v]_{s}(E)
on a Borel set
E\subset(0,1)
by

(2.2)

[v]_{s}(E)^{2}=\iint_{E\times E}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

We start by dealing with the case
s_{\varepsilon}\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}
. Let
G_{\varepsilon}
denote the unscaled functionals

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\iint_{(0,1)\times(0,1)}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy.

We now give an argument leading to the computation of the correct scaling. For symmetry reasons, this is carried on for functionals defined on
(-1,1)
and not on
(0,1)
. This allows to consider a sequence of functions

u_{\varepsilon}
which converges in measure to the function

u_{0}=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{in }[0,1]\\
-1\quad\text{in }[-1,0).\end{cases}

We let
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and define

\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\left|\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1-\eta\}\right|,\quad C_{\eta}=\min_{\left|z\right|\leq 1-\eta}W(z)>0,

A_{\varepsilon}=\{u_{\varepsilon}>1-\eta\},\quad B_{\varepsilon}=\{u_{\varepsilon}<\eta-1\}.

Then, we have the estimate:

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}{\varepsilon}+2\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\iint_{A_{\varepsilon}\times B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy

\geq\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}{\varepsilon}+2(2-2\eta)^{2}\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\iint_{A_{\varepsilon}\times B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy.

Since the function
\psi(\left|x-y\right|)=\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}
is monotonically decreasing in
\left|x-y\right|
, we obtain a lower bound by increasing the distance between
x
and
y
(see also Lemma 2 of
[
1
]
). This leads to

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}{\varepsilon}+2(2-2\eta)^{2}\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\int_{-1}^{-1+\left|A_{\varepsilon}\right|}\int_{1-\left|B_{\varepsilon}\right|}^{1}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy

\begin{split}=\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{(2-2\eta)^{2}\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{s_{\varepsilon}(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)}&\bigg(2^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-(2-\left|B_{\varepsilon}\right|)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\\
&-(2-\left|A_{\varepsilon}\right|)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}+(2-\left|B_{\varepsilon}\right|-\left|A_{\varepsilon}\right|)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\bigg),\end{split}

which simplifies to

\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}{\varepsilon}+C\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\frac{\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}+O(\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1})

for some positive constant
C
which does not depend on the other parameters.
We now minimize the principal part with respect to
\sigma=\sigma_{\varepsilon}
. The minimum is attained for

(2.3)

\sigma_{\varepsilon}=K\varepsilon\quad\text{with }K=\left(\frac{C}{C_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}},

which leads to (possibly renaming
C
, which still does not depend on the other parameters)

(2.4)

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq C\left[C_{\eta}^{1-\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{({K\varepsilon})^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\right)-1\right].

Since
K^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\to 1
as
s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}
, the leading term in the right-hand side is

\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-1}{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}.

This computation suggests the scaling factor

(2.5)

\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-1}.

Note that, since we are interested in vanishing perturbations of the double-well functional, we ought to make sure that the scaling factor we have found behaves correctly (that is, we want the coefficient in front of the double well potential to diverge as
\varepsilon\to 0
and the one in front of the Gagliardo seminorm to tend to
0
).
Let us check the validity of such conditions: in this case, the functional has the form

F^{s_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}[u]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2},

thus, the conditions are

\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to+\infty.

Here, the first condition is equivalent to

0\leftarrow\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}=\frac{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}{e^{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}-1}\cdot\frac{1}{\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|},

which is satisfied, since the function
f(x)=\frac{e^{x}-1}{x}
is bounded from below by a positive constant for all
x\in[0,+\infty)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The condition

\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\to 0

is also satisfied if
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
. Indeed, if
\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|({2s_{\varepsilon}-1})
is bounded the reasoning above still applies.
On the other hand, if
\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|({2s_{\varepsilon}-1})\to-\infty
, one has

\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{e^{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}-1}\to 0.

The reason why we separately study the cases
s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}
and
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
lies in the behaviour of the term in front of the double well potential, which may not diverge for
s<\frac{1}{2}
with this scaling factor.

We now check the second condition, namely:

\frac{\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to+\infty.

This, after simplifications, leads to

\frac{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}{1-e^{(1-2s_{\varepsilon})\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}\to+\infty.

Here, the first term is bounded from below whenever
\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|({2s_{\varepsilon}-1})
is bounded, while
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}\to+\infty
. On the other hand, if
\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|({2s_{\varepsilon}-1})\to+\infty
, both terms in the product tend to
+\infty
.

If
s_{\varepsilon}\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{-}
, it suffices to repeat the same computation above, considering the unscaled functionals

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\iint_{(0,1)\times(0,1)}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dx\,dy.

In this case, the optimal value in the minimization procedure (performed as in (
2.4
)) is attained for

(2.6)

\sigma_{\varepsilon}=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}},

with
K
as in (
2.3
),
which leads to

(2.7)

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq C\left[C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}+\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{({K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}})^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\right)-1\right]

for some positive constant
C
which does not depend on the parameters.
We recall that the value
K
satisfies
K^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\to 1
as
s_{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, the leading term in the right-hand side is

\frac{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}.

This computation suggests the scaling factor

(2.8)

\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)=\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}.

In this case, the conditions become

\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to+\infty.

The first, being equivalent to

\frac{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}{e^{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}-1}\cdot\frac{1}{\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}\to 0,

is satisfied by Remark
2.1
. On the other hand, the second condition leads to

\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon({\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1)}\to+\infty,

which is equivalent to

\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon({\varepsilon}^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1)}\to+\infty.

Once again, we write it as

+\infty\leftarrow\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon({\varepsilon}^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1)}=\frac{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}{e^{(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}-1}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|},

where the first term in the product is bounded from below by a positive constant if
(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|
is bounded and tends to
+\infty
if
(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|\to-\infty
. The second term, on the other hand, always tends to
+\infty
, which implies that the second condition is satisfied for any regime of
s<\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
fixed,

\lim_{s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\,\frac{1-2s}{\varepsilon^{2s-1}-1}\varepsilon^{2s-1}=\lim_{s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\,\frac{2s-1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s}-1}=\frac{1}{\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}

and

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}^{-}}\frac{2s-1}{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s}{2s}}-1}=\frac{1}{\left|\log(\varepsilon)\right|}.

This allows us to recover the scaling factor for the critical regime
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

3.
The Gamma limit

Let
(s_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence converging to
\frac{1}{2}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and consider the functionals

(3.1)

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}[u]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\quad\text{if }s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\frac{1}{\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|}[u]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\quad\text{if }s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{(0,1)}W(u)\,dt+\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)[u]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\quad\text{if }s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}\,,\end{cases}

with
\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)
and
\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)
being the scaling factors defined in (
2.5
) and (
2.8
) respectively and
[v]_{s_{\varepsilon}}
being the Gagliardo seminorm defined in (
2.1
).

In this section we prove that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge to the functional
F^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}
defined in (
1.3
). More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
W
is a double-well potential satisfying (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
), let
\varepsilon>0
and
s_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
be such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\,.

Then the family of functionals
(F_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
3.1
)
\Gamma
-converge, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, to the functional

F_{0}=\begin{cases}m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u)\quad\text{if }u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})\\
+\infty\quad\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with
m_{\frac{1}{2}}=8
.

We now state a technical lemma, which will be used multiple times throughout this paper, which sums up the minimization technique applied in the previous section. Its proof can be found in the appendix.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I\subset[0,1]
be an interval,
s_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
s_{\varepsilon}\neq\frac{1}{2}
and
v\in H^{s_{\varepsilon}}(0,1)
. For any
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, define

C_{\eta}=\min_{\left|z\right|\leq 1-\eta}W(z)>0

and

(3.2)

Z=\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}\left|I\right|^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}C_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}.

Moreover, assume that there exists
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

(3.3)

\left|I\cap\{v\geq 1-\eta\}\right|\geq\theta\left|I\right|\quad\text{and}\quad\left|I\cap\{v\leq\eta-1\}\right|\geq\theta\left|I\right|.

Then, for
s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(v;I)\geq\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\Biggr[C_{\eta}\left|I\right|Z+8&(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-2\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\right)\Biggr],\end{split}

and, for
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(v;I)\geq\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)\Biggr[C_{\eta}\left|I\right|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2s}-1}Z+8&(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\frac{{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-2\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\right)\Biggr].\end{split}

Remark 3.3
.

In both cases, for any
\theta
and
\eta
fixed, there exists a value
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
such that the right-hand side is bounded from below by a positive constant
C_{0}(\theta,\eta)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\overline{\varepsilon})
. Moreover, we observe that this constant tends to
-\infty
as
\theta\to 0^{+}
.

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
3.1
we state and prove the corresponding compactness result. Its proof follows the technique employed in
[
16
]
.

Since the case
s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}
has already been addressed in
[
1
]
, we will only prove the theorem in the case
s_{\varepsilon}\neq\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, when the cases
s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}
and
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
can be handled using the same arguments, we will use the notation
\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)
to refer to either
\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)
or
\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)
without further specification on
s_{\varepsilon}
.

Given any open subset
E\subset(0,1)
, we define the restricted energy

F_{\varepsilon}(u;E)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{E}W(u)\,dt+\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}[u]_{{s_{\varepsilon}}}(E)^{2}\quad\text{if }s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{E}W(u)\,dt+\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)[u]_{{s_{\varepsilon}}}(E)^{2}\quad\text{if }s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where
[u]_{{s_{\varepsilon}}}(E)
denotes the localized version of the Gagliardo seminorm, as in (
2.2
).

We also introduce the notation
\mathcal{T}_{k}
for the truncation function at height
\pm k
(which will be used in the proof of the next lemma). More precisely,
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is defined as follows:

(3.6)

\mathcal{T}_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}k\operatorname{sgn}(s)\quad\text{if }\left|s\right|\geq k\\
s\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty.

Then there exists
u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})
such that, up to subsequences,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure.

Proof.

Step 1:
we start by noting that we may suppose that the functions
u_{\varepsilon}
are uniformly bounded.

Indeed, for any
k>1
let
\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon})
denote the truncation of
u_{\varepsilon}
at height
\pm k
. Since the Gagliardo seminorm
[u]_{s_{\varepsilon}}
decreases under truncation (cf
[
12
, Section 5.6]
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S+\frac{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}}W(\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon}))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S+\frac{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\left|\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}\right|(W(k)+W(-k))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S+(W(k)+W(-k))\frac{1}{C_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\int_{\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}}W(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S^{\prime}<+\infty.

In this formula we have set
C_{k}=\min_{\left|z\right|\geq k}W(z)
, which is strictly positive by the assumptions on the double-well potential.
This shows that the truncated functions satisfy the assumption of the lemma.

We can then deduce that the claim of the lemma holds also for
u_{\varepsilon}
once it holds for
\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon})
. Indeed, since we have

(3.7)

\int_{\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}}W(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt\leq S\frac{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

we obtain

(3.8)

\left|\{u_{\varepsilon}\neq\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon})\}\right|=\left|\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{C_{k}}\int_{\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>k\}}W(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt\to 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0;

that is, the difference of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{T}_{k}(u_{\varepsilon})
tends to
0
in measure as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Step 2:

we claim that the set
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is totally bounded in
L^{1}(0,1)
, that is, for any
\delta>0
there exists a finite set
\mathcal{A}\subset L^{1}
such that for any small
\varepsilon
there exists
v_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}
with

\left\|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\delta.

This implies that
u_{\varepsilon}
converges, up to subsequences, to a cluster point of the family
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
.

Let
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
\rho>0
. We begin by partitioning the interval
[0,1]
in
\frac{1}{\rho}
(which we assume to be an integer) subintervals
I_{i}^{\rho}
of length
\rho
. We will refer to the set of indexes in such partition as
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
and further classify these intervals as follows:

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{1}=\left\{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\quad\colon\,\left|\{{u_{\varepsilon}<1-\eta}\}\cap I_{i}^{\rho}\right|<\eta\rho\right\}

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\left\{i\in\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\quad\colon\,\left|\{{u_{\varepsilon}>-1+\eta}\}\cap I_{i}^{\rho}\right|<\eta\rho\right\}

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}=\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\setminus(\mathcal{J}^{1}_{\varepsilon}\cup\mathcal{J}^{-1}_{\varepsilon}).

For simplicity, we also call

K_{\varepsilon}^{1}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{1}}I_{i}^{\rho}\quad K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}I_{i}^{\rho}\quad K_{\varepsilon}^{0}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}I_{i}^{\rho}.

Then, we define

(3.9)

v_{\varepsilon}^{\rho}=\begin{cases}+1\quad\text{in }K^{1}_{\varepsilon}\\
-1\quad\text{elsewhere.}\end{cases}

Note that, for every fixed
\rho
, the family
\{v_{\varepsilon}^{\rho}\}_{\varepsilon}
is finite.

We now estimate from below the energy
F_{\varepsilon}(u;I_{i}^{\rho})
for
i\in\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
. By subadditivity of
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,I)
with respect to
I
, we have

(3.10)

S\geq\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I_{i}^{\rho}).

Moreover, by definition of
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
,

\left|\{{u_{\varepsilon}>1-\eta}\}\cap I_{i}^{\rho}\right|\geq\eta\rho\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\{{u_{\varepsilon}<-1+\eta}\}\cap I_{i}^{\rho}\right|\geq\eta\rho.

This implies, using Lemma
3.2
and (
3.10
) (and Remark
3.3
) that, for
\varepsilon
small enough

(3.11)

S\geq\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I_{i}^{\rho})\geq\left|\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right|C_{0}(\eta)

for some positive constant
C_{0}
.
It follows that, for
\rho
small enough (depending on
\eta
),

(3.12)

\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right|=\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}\left|I_{i}^{\rho}\right|\leq\frac{S}{C_{0}(\eta)}\rho\leq\frac{\delta}{8}.

From this point onward, by the reasoning performed in Step 1, we may assume that
\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1+\eta
a.e. for every
\varepsilon>0
.

We now estimate the
L^{1}
distance between
u_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\varepsilon}
outside of
K_{\varepsilon}^{0}
. We will do so by estimating such distance in the subsets of
K_{\varepsilon}^{1}
(resp.
K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
) where
u_{\varepsilon}
is away from
\pm 1
, when it is close to
-1
and when it is close to
1
.
First of all, we have

\left|\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1-\eta\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{C_{\eta}}\int_{0}^{1}W(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{C_{\eta}}S.

Thus, since
\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 3
,

(3.13)

\int_{\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1-\eta\}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\leq 3\left|\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1-\eta\}\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}\frac{3S}{C_{\eta}}.

Regarding the set where
u_{\varepsilon}
is close to
-1
, we have

\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\leq\eta-1\}\right|=\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\left|I^{\rho}_{i}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\leq\eta-1\}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}^{1}\right|\eta\rho=\eta\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\right|,

which implies,

\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\leq\eta-1\}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\leq 3\eta\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\right|.

Moreover, we have

\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\geq 1-\eta\}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt=\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\geq 1-\eta\}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-1\right|dt\leq 2\eta\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\right|.

This and (
3.13
) lead to

(3.14)

\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\leq 5\eta+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}\frac{3S}{C_{\eta}}.

Performing the same computations for
K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
(up to exchanging the roles of the sets where
u_{\varepsilon}
is close to
1
and
-1
), we obtain

\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\geq 1-\eta\}\right|\leq\eta\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right|\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cap\{{u_{\varepsilon}}\leq\eta-1\}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\leq 2\eta\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{1}\right|,

which, as before, implies

(3.15)

\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\leq 5\eta+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}\frac{3S}{C_{\eta}}.

Putting (
3.12
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) together we obtain, for
\eta
,
\varepsilon
and
\rho
small enough,

\begin{split}\int_{[0,1]}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt&\leq\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt+\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt+\int_{K_{\varepsilon}^{0}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\right|dt\\
&\leq 2\left(5\eta+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\pm}(\varepsilon)}\frac{3S}{C_{\eta}}\right)+3\left|K_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right|\leq\delta.\end{split}

This proves the total boundedness of
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
. It follows that
u_{\varepsilon}
converges (up to subsequences) to some function
u\in L^{1}(0,1)
which is a cluster point for the family
\{v_{\varepsilon}^{\rho}\}_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, since
\left|v_{\varepsilon}\right|=1
for all
\varepsilon
, it follows that
u=2{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}-1
for some set
E\subset(0,1)
. Note that the choice of the limit
u
is not affected by the height
k>1
of the truncation we applied at the beginning of the proof.

Step 3:

we claim that
\#S(u)
is finite. To prove it, define the functions

\psi_{\rho}
to be equal to
1
in
I_{i}^{\rho}
if
\left|E\cap I_{i}^{\rho}\right|\geq\frac{\rho}{2}
and
-1
otherwise. As
\psi_{\rho}\to u
in measure as
\rho\to 0^{+}
, it suffices to show that

\#S(\psi_{\rho})
is equibounded.
Let
I_{i}^{\rho}
be an interval such that
\psi_{\rho}
has a jump point, denoted by
t_{i}
, either at the beginning or the end of
I_{i}^{\rho}
. Consider the interval
\mathcal{I}_{i}=(t_{i}-\rho,t_{i}+\rho)
.

Note that, by convergence in measure,

\left|\{u_{\varepsilon}\geq 1-\eta\}\cap\mathcal{I}_{i}\right|=:a_{\varepsilon}\rho\geq\frac{\rho}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\{u_{\varepsilon}\geq\eta-1\}\cap\mathcal{I}_{i}\right|=:b_{\varepsilon}\rho\geq\frac{\rho}{2}

for
\varepsilon
small enough.
We can thus apply Lemma
3.2
(with
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
) to each interval
\mathcal{I}_{i}
to obtain

(3.16)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\mathcal{I}_{i})\geq\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)8&(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-6\right),\end{split}

with
Z
given by (
3.2
),
which tends to
8
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. In particular, it is definitely greater than a positive constant
C_{0}>0
(see also Remark
3.3
). Analogously, for
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(3.17)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\mathcal{I}_{i})\geq\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)8&(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\frac{{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-6\right)\geq C_{0}>0.\end{split}

Note that the intervals
\mathcal{I}_{i}
may not be disjoint. However, by choosing them alternately, we can divide them in two family of disjoint intervals (indexed, say, by
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
).
In particular, we have that

\begin{split}&S\geq F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\sum_{i\in J_{1}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{I}_{i}),\\
&S\geq F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\sum_{i\in J_{2}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{I}_{i}).\end{split}

Thus

2S\geq 2F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\sum_{t_{i}\in S(\psi_{\rho})}F(u_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{I}_{i})\geq C_{0}\#S(\psi_{\rho}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

The next result deals with the lower bound.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure. Then
u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})
and

(3.18)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u).

Proof.

Up to extracting a subsequence (not relabeled), we
can assume that

\sup_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

This, by Lemma
3.4
, implies that
u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})
.

Note that for every
t_{i}\in S(u)
there exists an interval
I_{i}=(t_{i}-\delta,t_{i}+\delta)
such that
I_{i}\cap I_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.
Note that, by convergence in measure, the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
are satisfied with
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
for every
I_{i}
, provided that
\varepsilon
is small enough.
It follows that, for
s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.19)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq&\sum_{i=1}^{\#S(u)}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I_{i})\\
\geq&\sum_{i=1}^{\#S(u)}\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)8(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-6\right),\end{split}

and, for
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.20)

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq&\sum_{i=1}^{\#S(u)}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I_{i})\\
\geq&\sum_{i=1}^{\#S(u)}\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)8(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\left|I\right|^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{Z^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\frac{{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-6\right).\end{split}

In both cases, taking the lower limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we get

(3.21)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq 8(1-\eta)^{2}\#S(u).

This, letting
\eta\to 0^{+}
, concludes the proof.
∎

The next lemma provides the upper bound inequality, which concludes the proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence in Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})
, then there exists a sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{s_{\varepsilon}}(0,1)
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{2}(0,1)
and

(3.22)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u).

Proof.

For any jump point
t_{i}\in S(u)
, we consider the piecewise affine function defined as follows:

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}_{1}(\frac{x-t_{i}}{\varepsilon})u(t_{i}^{+})\quad\text{if }\left|x-t_{i}\right|<\varepsilon\\
u(x)\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is the truncation function at height
\pm 1
defined in (
3.6
).
We also define the points
x_{0}=0
,
x_{i}=\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2}
for
1\leq i\leq\#S(u)-1
and
x_{\#S(u)}=1
.

First, note that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This implies that

\int_{0}^{1}W(u_{\varepsilon})\to\int_{0}^{1}W(u)=0.

To compute the Gagliardo seminorm of
u_{\varepsilon}
, we proceed as follows:
let us divide the square
(0,1)\times(0,1)
in many rectangles, as in Figure
1
(for reference, consider the solid line to be at the middle point between
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
).

Our aim is to show that the contribution to Gagliardo seminorm
[u_{\varepsilon}]_{{s_{\varepsilon}}}
given by the interaction of points at a distance greater than a given threshold can be bounded from above by a constant, so that the scaling factor (either
\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)
or
\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}
) would make their contribution to

First, note that on the purple squares (which can be taken with sides smaller than

) we have
u(x)-u(y)=0
.
Moreover, calling

Q_{i}=[x_{i},x_{i+1}]\times[x_{i},x_{i+1}],

we have

\begin{split}&\iint_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{\#S(u)-1}\iint_{Q_{i}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{[0,1]\times[0,1]\setminus\bigcup Q_{i}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Working term by term, we have

\iint_{Q_{i}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy=2\int_{x_{i}}^{t_{i}-\varepsilon}\int_{t_{i}+\varepsilon}^{x_{i+1}}\frac{4}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy\,

+\int_{t_{i}-\varepsilon}^{t_{i}+\varepsilon}\int_{t_{i}-\varepsilon}^{t_{i}+\varepsilon}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy+4\int_{t_{i}+\varepsilon}^{x_{i+1}}\int_{t_{i}-\varepsilon}^{t_{i}+\varepsilon}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-1\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy,

which, after computations, leads to

\iint_{Q_{i}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy=8\frac{1}{2s}\frac{(2\varepsilon)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}+O(1).

On the other hand,

\iint_{[0,1]\times[0,1]\setminus\bigcup Q_{i}}\frac{\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy\leq\frac{C}{\inf_{i}\left|t_{i}-t_{i+1}\right|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}<+\infty.

We now estimate the energy contribution from the double-well potential. In particular, we have:

\int_{0}^{1}W(u)\,dt=\sum_{i=1}^{\#S(u)}\int_{t_{i}-\varepsilon}^{t_{i}+\varepsilon}W\left(\frac{t-t_{i}}{\varepsilon}\right)\,dt\leq\#S(u)2\varepsilon\max_{[-1,1]}W.

Putting everything together we have, for
s_{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{2}
,

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{1}\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)+8\#S(u)\lambda_{+}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\frac{1}{2s}\left(\frac{(2\varepsilon)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}+C_{2}\right),

and, for
s_{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{2}
,

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{1}\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)+8\#S(u)\lambda_{-}(\varepsilon)\frac{1}{2s}\left(\frac{(2\varepsilon)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-1}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}+C_{2}\right).

Either way, we proved that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

(Separation of scales)
Assume that

(2s_{\varepsilon}-1)\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|\to C\in\mathbb{R}.

Then

\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}-1}\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\approx\frac{C}{e^{C}-1}\frac{1}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}

and

\frac{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}{{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}}-1}\approx\frac{C}{e^{C}-1}\frac{1}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}.

In the limit case
C=0
, this computation highlights that in the regime

\left|2s_{\varepsilon}-1\right|<<\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|},

we have the separation of scales effect discussed in the introduction. Namely, the
\Gamma
-limit of
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)F_{\varepsilon}^{s}
is the same as the one obtained first letting
s\to\frac{1}{2}
with
\varepsilon>0
fixed, which gives
\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}F_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}
, and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.

On the behaviour of optimal profiles

In this section we briefly discuss the properties of the optimal values

(4.1)

m_{s}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(u)dt+\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\mid u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),u(\pm\infty)=\pm 1\right\}

as
s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}
.
In particular, we prove that the value
m_{\frac{1}{2}}
is the solution of a (suitably rescaled) optimal profile problem and that

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}}(2s-1)m_{s}=m_{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Both properties are strongly related to the fact that, while the
\Gamma
-limit of
F^{s}_{\varepsilon}
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
involves the optimal constant
m_{s}
, the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
requires an additional scaling argument.

For any
T\geq 0
and
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
consider the family
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{s}
of real valued functions
v
defined on
\mathbb{R}
such that
v\in H^{s}(-T,T)
,
v(\pm T)=\pm 1
,
v(t)=1
if
t>T
and
v(t)=-1
if
t<-T
.
It was shown in
[
15
]
that, for any
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, the optimal values
m_{s}
satisfy

(4.2)

m_{s}=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\inf\left\{\int_{-T}^{T}W(u)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{{1+2s}}}\,dx\,dy\mid v\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{s}\right\}.

The following lemma gives a similar result for
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The quantity
m_{\frac{1}{2}}
satisfies the following property:

(4.3)

m_{\frac{1}{2}}=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\log(2T)}\inf\left\{\int_{-T}^{T}W(u)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\mid v\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

Proof.

Let

\tilde{m}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{T}=\inf\left\{\int_{-T}^{T}W(u)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\mid v\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

For any function
v\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{s}
, we fix
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and consider

A=\{v>1-\eta\}\cap[-T,T],\quad B=\{v<\eta-1\}\cap[-T,T],

a=\frac{\left|A\right|}{2T},\quad b=\frac{\left|B\right|}{2T},\quad C_{\eta}=\min_{\left|z\right|\leq 1-\eta}W(z).

Following the techniques used in Section
2
, we have:

\int_{-T}^{T}W(v)dt\geq C_{\eta}2T(1-a-b)

and

\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\geq 2\iint_{A\times B}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy

\geq 8(1-\eta)^{2}\iint_{A\times B}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\geq 8(1-\eta)^{2}\int_{-T}^{-T+\left|B\right|}\int_{T-\left|A\right|}^{T}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy

\begin{split}=8(1-\eta)^{2}[\log(2T-\left|A\right|)+&\log(2T-\left|B\right|)\\
&\quad-\log(2T)-\log(2T-\left|A\right|-\left|B\right|)]\end{split}

=8(1-\eta)^{2}\left[\log(1-a)+\log(1-b)-\log(1-a-b)\right].

Hence

\int_{-T}^{T}W(v)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy

\begin{split}\geq C_{\eta}2T(1-a-b)+8(1-\eta)&{}^{2}[\log(1-a)+\\
&\quad\log(1-b)-\log(1-a-b)]\end{split}

\begin{split}=8(1-\eta)^{2}&\bigg\{\frac{C_{\eta}}{8(1-\eta)^{2}}2T(1-a-b)\\
&+\left[\log(1-a)+\log(1-b)-\log(1-a-b)\right]\bigg\}.\end{split}

This, using the inequality

-\log x+Mx\geq\log M

with

x=(1-a-b)\quad\text{and}\quad M=\frac{C_{\eta}}{8(1-\eta)^{2}}2T,

we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{-T}^{T}W(v)dt+&\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\\
\geq&8(1-\eta)^{2}\left\{\log\left(\frac{C_{\eta}}{8(1-\eta)^{2}}2T\right)+\log(1-a)+\log(1-b)\right\}.\end{split}

In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log(2T)}\left(\int_{-T}^{T}W(v)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tilde{C}(\eta)}{\log{(2T)}}+8(1-\eta)^{2}.

Hence, taking the limit as
T\to+\infty
and then letting
\eta\to 0^{+}
, we obtain

\lim_{T_{\to}+\infty}\tilde{m}^{T}_{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 8.

For the opposite inequality, we consider the test function
u(t)=\mathcal{T}_{1}(t)
. Indeed, for the potential term, we have

\int_{-T}^{T}W(u)\,dt=\int_{-1}^{1}W(t)\,dt.

On the other hand, regarding the Gagliardo seminorm

\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy,

we split the integral as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, obtaining

\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy+4\int_{1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\left|x-1\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy+2\int_{1}^{T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{4}{(x+y)^{2}}\,dx\,dy

which is equal to

C+8[2\log(1+T)-\log(2T)-\log(2)].

Hence

\frac{1}{\log(2T)}\left(\int_{-T}^{T}W(u)dt+\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\,dx\,dy\right)=8+O\left(\frac{1}{\log(2T)}\right).

Taking the limit as
T\to+\infty
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Given
m_{s}
as in (
4.1
), one has

(4.4)

\lim_{s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}(2s-1)m_{s}=m_{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
v_{s}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
and
T>0
such that
v_{s}(\pm T)=\pm 1
and
v_{s}
is constant outside of
[-T,T]
.
For any
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, define:

\delta=\delta_{s}(\eta)=\left|\{\left|v_{s}\right|\leq 1-\eta\}\right|,

A_{\eta}=\{v_{s}\geq 1-\eta\}\quad B_{\eta}=\{v_{s}\leq\eta-1\}.

The energy

F^{s}_{1}(v_{s},\mathbb{R})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(v_{s})dt+[u]_{s}(\mathbb{R})^{2}

satisfies:

F^{s}_{1}(v_{s},\mathbb{R})\geq C_{\eta}\delta+2\iint_{A_{\eta}\times B_{\eta}}\frac{\left|v_{s}(x)-v_{s}(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\geq C_{\eta}\delta+8(1-\eta)^{2}\iint_{A_{\eta}\times B_{\eta}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Since
\left|\mathbb{R}\setminus(A_{\eta}\cup B_{\eta})\right|=\delta
, translation invariance implies

\displaystyle F^{s}_{1}(v_{s},\mathbb{R})\geq C_{\eta}\delta+8(1-\eta)^{2}\int_{\frac{\delta}{2}}^{+\infty}\int_{\frac{\delta}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{({x+y})^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq C_{\eta}\delta+8(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{\delta^{1-2s}}{2s(2s-1)}.

Taking the infimum over all possible values of
\delta
, which is attained for
\delta=\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}}{2sC_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}
, yields

F^{s}_{1}(v_{s},\mathbb{R})\geq C_{\eta}\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}}{2sC_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\frac{1}{2s}\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}}{2sC_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1-2s}{2s}}(1-\eta)^{2}\frac{8}{2s-1}.

Taking the infimum over all choices of
v_{s}
and then multiplying both sides by
2s-1
leads to

(2s-1)m_{s}\geq(2s-1)C_{\eta}\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}}{2sC_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}+\frac{1}{2s}\left(\frac{8(1-\eta)^{2}}{2sC_{\eta}}\right)^{\frac{1-2s}{2s}}(1-\eta)^{2}8.

We now take the limit as
s\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
and then as
\eta\to 0^{+}
to conclude:

\lim_{s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}(2s-1)m_{s}\geq m_{\frac{1}{2}}.

We now prove the converse inequality: let
u(x)=\mathcal{T}_{1}(x)
. Then

F_{1}^{s}(u,\mathbb{R})=\int_{1}^{1}W(x)\,dt+8\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(x+y)^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy

+\iint_{[-1,1]^{2}}\left|x-y\right|^{1-2s}\,dx\,dy+\int_{-1}^{1}\left|x-1\right|^{2}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}dydx.

Since, as
s\to\frac{1}{2}^{+}
, one has

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{1}W(x)\,dx+&\iint_{[-1,1]^{2}}\left|x-y\right|^{1-2s}\,dx\,dy\\
+&\int_{-1}^{1}\left|x-1\right|^{2}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}dydx=O(1),\end{split}

the leading term is

8\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(x+y)^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy=8\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{2s(2s-1)}.

Hence we have

m_{s}\leq F_{1}^{s}(u,\mathbb{R})=8\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{2s(2s-1)}+O(1),

which implies

\lim_{s\to{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}(2s-1)m_{s}\leq m_{\frac{1}{2}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Continuity and regular points

Consider the scaling factor, which is continuous in both variables

\lambda(\varepsilon,s)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|}\quad s=\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{1-2s}{\varepsilon^{(2s-1)}-1}\quad s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)\end{cases}

and the family of functionals

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)=\lambda(\varepsilon,s)\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int W(u)\,dt+\varepsilon^{2s-1}[u]_{s}^{2}\right)\quad s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right).

We can reinterpret our previous results through the lens of regular values introduced in
[
5
]
(see also
[
4
]
for a similar result in the context of periodically-perforated media). Namely, for any
s_{0}\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and any pair of sequences
(s_{j},\varepsilon_{j})\to(s_{0},0)
,
(s_{j}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime})\to(s_{0},0)
, we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{j}}^{s_{j}}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime}}^{s_{j}^{\prime}}.

Moreover, we point out that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)=\begin{cases}m_{\frac{1}{2}}F_{0}(u)&\hbox{ if }s=\frac{1}{2}\\
(2s-1)m_{s}F_{0}(u)&\hbox{ if }s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),\end{cases}

where

F_{0}(u)=\begin{cases}\#S(u)&\text{if }u\in BV((0,1),\{-1,1\}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
\#S(u)
denotes the number of jump points of
u
in
BV((0,1),\{-1,1\})
.
Notably, the regularity of the point
s=\frac{1}{2}
and the continuity of the
\Gamma
-limits with respect to
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
only occur thanks to the presence of the scaling factor
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)
.
This highlights another separation of scales. Specifically, in the regime

\left|2s_{\varepsilon}-1\right|>>\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|},

the
\Gamma
-limit of
\lambda(\varepsilon,s)F_{\varepsilon}^{s}
coincides with the one obtained by first taking
\varepsilon\to 0
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
fixed, yielding
(2s-1){m_{s}}\#S(u)
, and then letting
s\to\frac{1}{2}
.

In the terminology of
\Gamma
-expansions (cf.
[
5
]
) we can state that

\int W(u)\,dt+\varepsilon^{2s}[u]_{s}^{2}
is uniformly equivalent to

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|m_{\frac{1}{2}}\#S(u)\quad\hbox{ if }s=\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon\big(1-\varepsilon^{2s-1}\big)m_{s}\#S(u)\quad\hbox{ if }s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\end{cases}

for
s
varying in compact sets of
[\frac{1}{2},1)
.

Proof of Lemma

.

Let us define the sets

(5.1)

A={I\cap\{v\geq 1-\eta\}}\quad\text{and}\quad B={I\cap\{v\leq\eta-1\}}

\alpha=\frac{\left|A\right|}{\left|I\right|}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|I\right|}.

(5.3)

\theta<\alpha,\beta<1-\theta.

In the next steps, we compute a lower bound for the Gagliardo seminorm.

(5.4)

[v]_{s}(I)^{2}\geq 2\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy\geq 2(2-\eta)^{2}\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{1+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Here, the right-hand side is equal to

(5.6)

\frac{2(2-\eta)^{2}}{2s(2s-1)}\Biggr((b-\left|B\right|-a-\left|A\right|)^{1-2s}-(b-a-\left|A\right|)^{1-2s}-(b-\left|B\right|-a)^{1-2s}+(b-a)^{1-2s}\Biggr),

(5.7)

\frac{2(2-\eta)^{2}\left|I\right|^{1-2s}}{2s(2s-1)}\Biggr((1-\beta-\alpha)^{1-2s}-(1-\alpha)^{1-2s}-(1-\beta)^{1-2s}+1\Biggr).

Now we let
\sigma=1-\beta-\alpha

(5.8)

t\longmapsto-\frac{(1-t)^{1-2s}-1}{(2s-1)}

\frac{\theta^{1-2s}-1}{(2s-1)}\geq\frac{(1-\alpha)^{1-2s}-1}{(2s-1)}.

[v]_{s}(I)^{2}\geq\frac{2(2-\eta)^{2}\left|I\right|^{1-2s}}{2s}\Biggr(\frac{\sigma^{1-2s}-1}{(2s-1)}-2\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\Biggr).

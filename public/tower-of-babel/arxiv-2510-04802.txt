Did you just see that? Arbitrary view synthesis for egocentric replay of operating room workflows from ambient sensors
Han Zhang, Lalithkumar Seenivasan, Jose L. Porras, Roger D. Soberanis-Mukul, Hao Ding, Hongchao Shu, Benjamin D. Killeen, Ankita Ghosh, Lonny Yarmus, Masaru Ishii, Angela Christine Argento, Mathias Unberath

Abstract
Observing surgical practice has historically relied on fixed vantage points or recollections, leaving the egocentric visual perspectives that guide clinical decisions undocumented. Fixed-camera video can capture surgical workflows at the room-scale, but cannot reconstruct what each team member actually saw. Thus, these videos only provide limited insights into how decisions that affect surgical safety, training, and workflow optimization are made. Here we introduce EgoSurg, the first framework to reconstruct the dynamic, egocentric replays for any operating room (OR) staff directly from wall-mounted fixed-camera video, and thus, without intervention to clinical workflow. EgoSurg couples geometry-driven neural rendering with diffusion-based view enhancement, enabling high-visual fidelity synthesis of arbitrary and egocentric viewpoints at any moment. In evaluation across multi-site surgical cases and controlled studies, EgoSurg reconstructs person-specific visual fields and arbitrary viewpoints with high visual quality and fidelity. By transforming existing OR camera infrastructure into a navigable dynamic 3D record, EgoSurg establishes a new foundation for immersive surgical data science, enabling surgical practice to be visualized, experienced, and analyzed from every angle.

Beyond those vantage perspectives, in the past, most of the fine-grained decisions, sightlines, micro-interactions, and many of the findings were lost to memory and anecdote of those privileged to witness the moment. Since then, hospitals have adopted structured documentation and routines, including operative notes, intraoperative nursing records, anesthesia logs, and postoperative debriefs, to capture and analyze events for both clinical training and optimization. With written summaries and fading memory as the main record, the fine-grained details and context – who stood where, what they could see, how long consensus took, which paths were blocked, and when sterile or safety envelopes were at risk – are lost to time. In recent times, using fixed cameras to record surgical procedures has partly alleviated these issues, but – while easy to deploy – they are less robust in capturing crucial interactions when the surgical orientation shifts or crowded spaces and occlusions obscure the view. To enable egocentric view synthesis in the OR without disrupting clinical workflow, we reconstruct dynamic 3D scenes from sparse, wall-mounted stereo-RGB cameras (Fig.
2
.a). Our representation builds on 3DGS
kerbl_3d_2023
, which models the scene as a set of volumetric Gaussians optimized against multi-view video streams. Because the OR is highly dynamic and prone to occlusions, we initialize 3DGS training with a sparse point cloud recovered from stereo inputs, providing a reliable geometric prior. From this initialization, we render candidate auxiliary views at virtual camera positions around the original setup. These synthesized views act as additional observations to complement the limited coverage of the physical cameras, but they often suffer from noise, occlusion artifacts, or missing structures. To address this, we introduce an image-conditioned diffusion model (Fig.
2
.b), which refines the auxiliary views by denoising and plausibly hallucinating missing details while preserving geometric consistency. The refined auxiliary views are then incorporated back into the optimization loop, serving as extra supervision alongside the original camera inputs. By enriching 3DGS with these high-quality synthesized observations, the representation becomes more stable, detailed, and robust to the dynamics of the surgical environment. The final 3DGS representation yields photorealistic and temporally consistent reconstructions from arbitrary viewpoints, including egocentric perspectives anchored to any team member. The entire pipeline runs efficiently on a consumer-grade workstation, reconstructing each surgical scene in minutes and supporting real-time interactive exploration.

We evaluated EgoSurg across six complete surgical workflows, including four in-the-wild robotic pulmonology procedures and two mock workflows at a second hospital site, as well as controlled experiments with hand-held PoV recordings and simulated sterile-field violations. EgoSurg Can Reveal Critical Safety Events

Ensuring safety in the OR is paramount, yet many near misses and critical violations such as sterile field violation, remain undocumented or rely on anecdotal reports, limiting systematic review and prevention. Traditional video systems fall short because they cannot capture the precise vantage points needed to confirm whether safety envelopes were breached (Fig.
3
.a). EgoSurg addresses this challenge by reconstructing immersive 3D scenes from ambient cameras and enabling retrospective, egocentric replays from the perspective of any team member (Fig.

This capability further establishes a form of virtual presence, allowing safety events to be observed and documented by a virtual human. Such reconstructions transform ambiguous observations into operationally actionable evidence. In simulated sterile-field violations, EgoSurg consistently exposed breaches from multiple viewpoints (Fig.
3
.c), unambiguously demonstrating their occurrence. What would otherwise remain anecdotal recollections become verifiable, visual documentation, empowering safety committees and supporting continuous quality improvement.

EgoSurg May Accelerate Skill Acquisition Through Immersive Training

Surgical education is fundamentally experiential, yet current training tools often fail to provide learners with the complete perceptual and contextual understanding required to master complex workflows. Traditional teaching, via operative notes, fixed video recordings, or retrospective debriefs, cannot capture what each team member actually saw or how their viewpoint shaped decisions. This limitation slows skill acquisition and leaves trainees to rely on speculation about what was visible to role-specific decision-makers. EgoSurg addresses this gap by reconstructing immersive egocentric perspectives directly from ambient ceiling-mounted cameras. In robotic pulmonology cases, for example, the system recreated the surgeon’s and scrub nurse’s viewpoints during endobronchial ultrasound phases, exposing the fine-grained attentional focus and perceptual constraints that guided their actions, as shown in Fig .
4
. Unlike wearable cameras, which raise sterility concerns and capture only pre-defined motion paths, EgoSurg flexibly generates egocentric views for any team member, at any moment, without altering clinical workflow. This capability may not only accelerates role-specific skill acquisition but also enables cross-role training. Trainees can immerse themselves in another team member’s line-of-sight, gaining exposure to specialized responsibilities and fostering a shared mental model of team objectives. Shared situational awareness is strongly linked to increased engagement, fewer complications, and improved patient outcomes, yet it is typically acquired only through prolonged on-the-job experience. By providing perspective-shifting replays, EgoSurg delivers these insights earlier, enriching learning with both self-role immersion and interprofessional understanding.

EgoSurg Helps Identify Bottlenecks for Workflow Efficiency

Inefficient visual access to shared information sources – such as fluoroscopy monitors during robotic pulmonology – creates hidden bottlenecks that delay handoffs and increase cognitive load. In crowded, instrumented spaces, even small occlusions can force team members to reposition repeatedly, yet conventional fixed-camera footage cannot reveal whether a blocked line-of-sight caused the delay or whether alternative placements would have helped. EgoSurg reconstructs a dynamic 3D scene from ambient cameras and enables counterfactual, egocentric replays from any role. In a biopsy under C-arm fluoroscopy, the x-ray monitor was the focal information source; one team member’s sightline was intermittently blocked by others, as shown in Fig
5
. Using EgoSurg, we anchored a virtual egocentric camera to that team member and generated a counterfactual trajectory that translated the person 1 m left-posterior to their original position while keeping all others fixed. The system then recomputed field-of-view overlap with the monitor and re-rendered the scene timeline. This reconstruction-based analysis makes previously anecdotal bottlenecks measurable. In the counterfactual, time-to-first-visibility of the monitor decreased, cumulative “visible” duration increased, and the number of occlusion events dropped without introducing new conflicts for other roles or violating sterile envelopes. By quantifying visibility, occlusion, and downstream interaction latencies, EgoSurg converts a qualitative hunch (“move left and back”) into operational evidence for layout and stance adjustments that shorten task transitions and smooth coordination.

Discussion

This work demonstrates the ability to virtually ’be there’ to retrospectively observe surgical workflow from any arbitrarily chosen egocentric viewpoint – a capability traditionally considered beyond technical reach. Historically, only those physically present in the OR or viewing gallery, learning and understanding surgical events and workflow depended on written records and the vivid memories of those who were present. Even for those present, observation was constrained by vantage: Without the right line-of-sight and with movement limited by sterility, space, and safety, it was impossible to navigate the room to obtain multiple perspectives. While contemporary video recording alleviates some of these constraints by allowing someone to real-time/retrospectively view the video themselves as if they were watching from the viewers’ gallery; however, it inherits the core limitation of fixed viewpoints, leaving crucial interactions obscured by orientation changes, crowding, and occlusions. Observations from fixed angles requires observers to mentally reconstruct missing vantage points, limiting the effectiveness to one’s cognitive capabilities. Reducing this cognitive burden, our EgoSurg framework allows viewing of surgical workflow in any viewpoint and any team member’s perspective, thereby enabling a wide range of downstream applications.

We demonstrate three key downstream applications of EgoSurg. First, in a simulated sterile field violation scenario, EgoSurg is used to retrospectively review prior videos to detect and adjudicate incidents and near misses. In our exemplar (Fig
3
), while it was inconclusive based on fixed-camera views, viewing rendered from a vantage point using EgoSurg confirmed a breach of the sterile field. This showcases EgoSurg’s potential to detect and investigate undocumented events and near-misses that would otherwise go unnoticed and unrecorded, thereby significantly improving patient safety. Second, demonstrate EgoSurg’s ability to generate role-specific egocentric views, demonstrating its potential use in immersive training for accelerated skill acquisition. Unlike bird’s-eye views from fixed cameras, with the ability to render role-specific views, EgoSurg reveals the actionable cues available at that moment – what a role can see and act on, allowing learners to familiarize and train decisions as that role would. This immersive learning from the egocentric view of an OR team member was possible in the past only with body-worn cameras during surgery or with mock simulations; neither approach scales to give every student real-time proximity to the role. Third, beyond just photorealism, we demonstrate EgoSurg’s ability to provide operationally actionable vision to improve and optimize surgical workflow. It enables counterfactual “rewind and reposition” analysis – egocentric view of an OR personnel vs a view rendered from an alternate vantage point – without re-staging a case, to analyze and optimize workflow based on fine-grained details. Beyond these exemplifier downstream use cases, EgoSurg unlocks potential applications that were previously considered technically not feasible or scalable: (i) Large-scale curation of role-specific egocentric views for training, efficiency analysis, and safety improvement; and (ii) role-aligned decision-support models that learn from the information available to each role at each moment, rather than from bird’s-eye summaries; (iii) enables competency assessment of both the individual and the team’s shared situational awareness; (iv) dry run of roles for ergonomics and human-factor evaluation, layout planning, pre-case rehearsal for complicated cases, and for easy onboarding of new OR personnel.

The current framework has three main limitations. First, temporal consistency of egocentric view reconstruction is limited. Because reconstructions are generated frame-by-frame without an explicit space–time prior, egocentric video in dynamic scenes can show minor drift in geometry or appearance across adjacent timestamps (e.g., shifts in boom position, tool tip, or drape boundary), reducing stability. Second, close-view fidelity is constrained. When rendering from very near vantage points or zooming in, the model may produce artifacts, especially when source views lack parallax or involve reflective surfaces and shadows, which limits reliable inspection of fine details such as small labels or tool features. Third, cross-time navigation remains restricted. Each timestamp is modeled as an independent 3DGS scene: While this supports free spatial navigation within a moment, it prevents smooth, continuous motion across time. Consequently, replaying a procedure from a fixed or moving viewpoint requires reloading and rendering each frame independently—an I/O-bound process that precludes seamless temporal fly-throughs.

Taken together, our results position EgoSurg as a foundation for perspective-agnostic surgical visualization. By transforming routine OR video into navigable, egocentric replays, the framework enables new paradigms of safety, training, and workflow optimization. More broadly, it suggests a path toward ambient-intelligence ORs, where role-specific perspective reconstruction could evolve into real-time decision support, fundamentally reshaping how surgical knowledge is captured, shared, and acted upon. Future directions include integrating multimodal data (audio, gaze, physiologic signals) and advancing spatiotemporal representations to support seamless cross-time navigation. Together, these directions point toward continuously learning OR systems that not only record what happened, but also anticipate risks, adapt workflows, and provide proactive guidance to surgical teams.

Methods

System Overview

To enable egocentric and arbitrary view rendering without disrupting clinical workflow, we leverage multiple wall-mounted cameras that continuously stream images to construct a unified OR scene representation at each timestamp. Our framework integrates multi-camera capture, stereo depth estimation, and scene reconstruction, as shown in Fig.
2
.a.

At each timestamp, the calibrated camera setup captures images of the OR from multiple viewpoints. We then apply FoundationStereo
wen2025stereo
to generate scale-aware depth maps for each camera. These depth maps are unprojected into 3D space to produce a sparse point cloud describing the full scene (Fig.
2
.b).

This point cloud is used to initialize a dense, photo-realistic reconstruction via 3DGS. To improve geometric consistency and better align Gaussian primitives with physical surfaces, we further incorporate an image-conditioned diffusion model
wu_difix3d_2025
, which generates auxiliary views to refine reconstruction (Fig.
2
.c).

The resulting 3DGS representation enables photo-realistic novel view synthesis of the OR scene from arbitrary viewpoints, supporting both egocentric and exocentric perspectives.

Ambient Camera Infrastructure

The proposed ambient sensing infrastructure consists of four wall-mounted stereo RGB cameras (Stereolabs ZED-X) paired with two NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (
16\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{B}
) units for synchronized recording. Each stereo camera incorporates two RGB sensors with a fixed baseline, enabling metric depth estimation via disparity and triangulation.

To balance unobtrusiveness and visibility, cameras were mounted above head height on walls opposite the operating table. At each timestamp, the system records four stereo pairs (eight images in total), providing a comprehensive multi-view representation of the scene.

Accurate extrinsic calibration and temporal synchronization are essential. Inter-camera synchronization was achieved via hardware-based Precision Time Protocol (PTP). Intrinsic parameters were adopted from factory calibration, while extrinsic parameters were estimated using a custom calibration wand consisting of three fixed spheres. By capturing wand trajectories throughout the room, cross-camera correspondences were identified. Extrinsics were first estimated using a linear relative pose solution, then refined through bundle adjustment. The global scale was recovered from known inter-sphere distances.

3D Gaussian Splatting Reconstruction

3DGS offers explicit 3D representations with high rendering efficiency and scalability, making it well-suited for dynamic and cluttered OR scenes. Unlike implicit NeRF-based methods, 3DGS can handle both static and deformable objects while supporting real-time visualization.

At each timestamp, synchronized stereo pairs are processed into sparse depth maps, which are fused into a unified point cloud. Independent 3DGS representations are then trained per timestamp to capture the temporal evolution of the OR environment. Training proceeds in two stages:

1.

Scene initialization with sparse pointmap.

2.

Iterative 3DGS optimization augmented with diffusion-based view refinement.

Scene Initialization

Initialization is critical for stable optimization. We employ FoundationStereo
wen2025stereo
with calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics to generate scale-aware depth maps from stereo pairs. These depth maps are unprojected into 3D space and merged into a shared coordinate frame. To balance geometric fidelity and efficiency, voxel downsampling and statistical outlier removal are applied. The resulting filtered point cloud serves as the initialization for 3DGS training.

Scene Training

After initialization, Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, and color) are iteratively optimized to minimize the discrepancy between rendered and ground-truth images. The training objective combines an
\ell_{1}
reconstruction term, a structural similarity component (D-SSIM)
kerbl_3d_2023
, and an opacity regularization term that encourages stable and well-conditioned transparency values:

L=(1-\alpha)\,L_{1}+\alpha\,L_{\text{D-SSIM}}\;+\;\lambda_{\text{opacity}}\cdot\sigma\!\big(\mathbf{o}\big),

where
\alpha
balances pixel-wise accuracy and structural similarity,
\lambda_{\text{opacity}}
controls the strength of regularization,
\sigma(\cdot)
denotes the sigmoid activation, and
\mathbf{o}
represents the set of Gaussian opacities.

To mitigate overfitting caused by limited viewpoints, densification, splitting, and pruning operations are disabled, and randomized backgrounds are introduced during training. This ensures that optimization remains restricted to Gaussian points derived from the initial point map. Training begins with only the sparse input views; once convergence is observed, optimization proceeds using both the original views and the diffusion-augmented views (as described in the following section).

Diffusion-Augmented View Refinement

To further enhance geometric consistency and mitigate artifacts arising from sparse viewpoints, we introduce diffusion-refined views during training. Around each camera, we sample
N
poses within a hemispherical radius
R
and render images using the current 3DGS representation. These intermediate rendered views often exhibit artifacts due to incomplete coverage and limited observation. Following DiFiX3D
wu_difix3d_2025
, we employ an image-conditioned diffusion model that refines each rendered view by jointly conditioning on the rendered auxiliary view and its corresponding reference image. The model is based on a single-step diffusion process derived from SD-Turbo
sauer_adversarial_2023
. In our experiments, we directly use the publicly released DifiX3D weights
wu_difix3d_2025
, without additional fine-tuning. After refinement, the corrected views are incorporated back into the training set, enabling 3DGS to learn from both real and diffusion-refined views, thereby improving robustness and reducing reconstruction artifacts.

Controlled Experiments Datasets

To quantitatively evaluate egocentric rendering, we collected an additional dataset in the Johns Hopkins Hospital OR (Site A) using a point-of-view (PoV) camera (Stereolab ZED X) operated by a staff member during routine OR activities. This dataset spans approximately 10 minutes, from which 10 representative scenes were sampled.

To demonstrate clinical use cases related to safety analysis, we constructed a dataset of sterile-field violation scenarios (Site A). In a simulated OR setting, participants unintentionally touched the sterile field during role-play, with the sterile zone physically mocked for realism. This dataset comprised 10 violation scenarios, providing safety-critical test cases for retrospective violation detection and analysis.

Experimental Setups

Baselines

We compared against three baselines:

1.

Depth Reprojection
: Depth maps estimated by FoundationStereo are unprojected using camera intrinsics and extrinsics, fused into a point cloud, and directly reprojected to novel views.

2.

Baseline 3DGS
: Standard 3DGS with randomly initialized Gaussians, trained only on captured multi-view images.

3.

EgoSurg (without diffusion)
: Our system initialized from fused depth maps, trained only with captured views and without diffusion-augmented images.

Implementation Details

All experiments were conducted on a Linux workstation equipped with an Intel i9-12900K CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, running Ubuntu 22.04 LTS with PyTorch 2.7.1. For augmented view generation, we sampled
N=15
viewpoints within a radius of
R=0.4
m around each camera. 3DGS training used 0-th order spherical harmonics and proceeded in two stages: (i) 500 warm-up steps and (ii) 1500 refinement steps with augmented views. Optimization employed the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of
1.6\times 10^{-4}
and random background sampling. The SSIM loss weight was set to
\lambda=0.2
, and the opacity regularization weight was fixed at 0.2. Training each scene required approximately two minutes.

Quantitative Evaluation

We employed complementary metrics to assess rendering fidelity, cross-view consistency, temporal stability, and computational efficiency for both novel-view and egocentric rendering. Unless otherwise noted, evaluation included peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index (SSIM), and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS), where higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS indicate stronger fidelity.

Novel View Fidelity.

To quantitatively evaluate novel-view synthesis, we withheld the right image from each stereo-RGB pair during training. Models were trained using only the left images, and the corresponding right views (baseline
\sim
12 cm) were synthesized from the reconstructed 3D representation using known intrinsics and extrinsics. Since these right-view images were never observed during training, they provide ground-truth supervision for novel viewpoints adjacent to the training set. We report PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS between synthesized and ground-truth right views, where higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS indicate improved rendering fidelity and geometric consistency.

Arbitrary Fidelity and Consistency.

To assess robustness under free-viewpoint rendering, we generated smooth
360^{\circ}
trajectories around the surgical field at radii of 1, 1.5, and 2 m, sampling 50 views per trajectory. While primarily simulating arbitrary exocentric motion, the trajectory was specifically chosen to approximate the head-level position of the human, thereby serving as a proxy for egocentric viewpoints.

We therefore evaluated two complementary aspects. First,
semantic fidelity
was measured using the CLIP score, quantifying the alignment between rendered views and reference ambient images of the OR; higher values indicate stronger preservation of semantic scene content. Second,
cross-view consistency
was assessed using temporal PSNR, temporal SSIM, and temporal LPIPS computed across neighboring rendered views. These metrics capture geometric and photometric stability along continuous camera paths, where higher temporal PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS reflect improved coherence across viewpoints.

Egocentric Fidelity.

For controlled egocentric experiments, synthesized views were directly compared against ground-truth wearable recordings. PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS were computed between rendered and recorded frames to capture pixel-level accuracy, structural similarity, and perceptual fidelity.

Temporal Stability.

To assess frame-to-frame coherence, we evaluated 10-frame stereo sequences from each surgical phase using the right-view stream. Temporal stability was quantified using temporal PSNR, temporal SSIM, temporal LPIPS, and the flicker score. The flicker score measures perceptual instability as frame-to-frame variations in luminance and structure, where higher values indicate stronger flickering artifacts. Improved stability corresponds to lower flicker and temporal LPIPS, together with higher temporal PSNR and SSIM.

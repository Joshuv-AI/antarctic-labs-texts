On some modifications of the Niemytzki plane
Wojciech Bielas

Abstract
. We present a criterion that compares modifications of the Niemytzki plane. It follows that if usual tangent discs of the Niemytzki plane are replaced by triangles with bounded angles, then the resulting space is not homeomorphic to the former.

1.
Introduction

The Niemytzki plane is a classical object in general topology and its modifications still appear as counterexamples, compare
[
3
]
or
[
7
]
.
Recently, there have been attempts to generalise it to larger dimensions,
[
2
]
.
Following Hattori
[
4
]
, new topologies on the closed upper plane which lie between the usual topology and the topology of the Niemytzki plane are discussed in
[
1
]
.
In this paper we show that the topology of the Niemytzki plane depends on the shape of tangent discs and that changing this shape may change the topology.
We present a quantitative criterion controlling such changes, see Lemma
2
.
Replacing tangent discs with appropriate triangles results in a finer topology, which is not homeomorphic to the Niemytzki plane.
Additionally, we show that obtaining the mentioned criterion is possible because monotone functions
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are differentiable almost everywhere, which results from the Lebesgue monotone differentiation theorem, and if the derivative
g^{\prime}(x)
exists, then

(1)

\textstyle\underset{h\to 0}{\liminf}\;\frac{g(x+\varphi(h))-g(x-\varphi(h))}{2\varphi(h)}\frac{2\psi(h)}{g(x+\psi(h))-g(x-\psi(h))}\leqslant 1

for any continuous functions
\varphi,\psi\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
\varphi\leqslant\psi
and
\lim_{h\to 0}\psi(h)=0
.
If
g^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, then the lower limit in (
1
) equals
1
, but the case
g^{\prime}(x)=0
needs an extra effort.

Our notation and topological terminology is fairly standard, as in
[
3
]
or
[
7
]
, but notions concerning differentiation are taken from standard textbooks on real analysis.

We will use the Jordan curve theorem, compare
[
6
, p. 31]
.

2.
Topologies induced by families of functions

Here, we present a method for obtaining some modifications of the Niemytzki plane.
If
f\colon[-a,a]\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function, then put

U(0,f)=\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon f(x)<y<\max f[[-a,a]]\}

and
U(x,f)=\{(y+x,z):(y,z)\in U(0,f)\}
, shortly
U(x,f)=U(0,f)+(x,0)
.
We say that a family of functions

f_{n}\colon[-a_{n},a_{n}]\to[0,\infty)

is
basic
, if the following conditions are fulfilled.

•

Each
f_{n}
is continuous and even.

•

The range of any
f_{n}
is
[0,\frac{1}{n}]
, so
\max f_{n}[[-a_{n},a_{n}]]=\frac{1}{n}
.

•

Always the restriction
f_{n}|_{[0,a_{n}]}
is one-to-one and
f_{n}(0)=0
.

•

If
m<n
, then the Euclidean closure of
U(0,f_{n})
is contained in the set
U(0,f_{m})
.

Given a basic family
\{f_{n}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
, on the half-plane
H=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon y\geqslant 0\}
let us introduce a topology generated by the Euclidean topology and the sets
U(x,f_{n})
.
Note that, if

\textstyle f_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{n^{2}}-x^{2}},

then the family
\{f_{n}|_{[-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}]}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
is basic and generates the topology of the Niemytzki plane.

It is straightforward to check that if functions
f_{n}\colon[-a_{n},a_{n}]\to[0,\infty)
constitute a basic family, then the restriction
f_{n}|_{[0,a_{n}]}
is strictly increasing, the restriction
f_{n}|_{[-a_{n},0]}
is strictly decreasing, in fact, both restrictions are homeomorphisms, therefore their images are arcs with endpoints
(0,0)
,
(a_{n},\frac{1}{n})
, and
(0,0),(-a_{n},\frac{1}{n})
, respectively.
But
U(x,f_{n})
is a translation of
U(0,f_{n})
by the vector
(x,0)
.
The induced topology gives the same closure of
U(x,f_{n})
as in the Euclidean topology, so we denote this closure
\operatorname{cl}U(x,f_{n})
.

The topology of the Niemytzki plane can be induced by parabolas, which is shown in the example below.

Example 1
.

Let
a_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
,
p_{n}\colon[-a_{n},a_{n}]\to[0,\frac{1}{n}]
,
p_{n}(x)=nx^{2}
for
n\geqslant 1
.
For all
x\in[-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}]
we have

\textstyle\frac{1}{n}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{n^{2}}-x^{2}}\leqslant nx^{2}

and for all
x\in[-\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{2n}]
we have

\textstyle nx^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2n}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4n^{2}}-x^{2}}.

These give inclusions

\textstyle B((0,\frac{1}{2n}),\frac{1}{2n})\subseteq U(0,p_{n})\subseteq B((0,\frac{1}{n}),\frac{1}{n}),

which are sufficient to check that sets
\{U(0,p_{n})\colon n\geqslant 1\}
constitute a base at
(0,0)
for the Niemytzki plane, hence sets
\{U(x,p_{n})\colon n\geqslant 1\}
constitute a base at
(x,0)
, too.

Recall that if
0<s<t
, then
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{m|x|^{t}}{n|x|^{s}}=0
for any
m,n\geqslant 1
.
For this reason, higher parabolas induce different nevertheless homeomorphic topologies, as is shown in the next example.

Example 2
.

Let
a_{n}=n^{-2/s}
and
p_{s,n}\colon[-a_{n},a_{n}]\to[0,\frac{1}{n}]
, and
p_{s,n}(x)=n|x|^{s}
, where
s\in(0,\infty)
and
n\geqslant 1
.
Let us denote by
N_{s}
the half-plane
H
with the topology induced by the basic family
\{p_{s,n}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
.
Then for all
s,t\in(0,\infty)
the function
f\colon N_{s}\to N_{t}
, given by the formula
f(x,y)=(x,y^{t/s})
, is a homeomorphism.
Each of the spaces
N_{s}
is homeomorphic to the Niemytzki plane, since it is homeomorphic to
N_{2}
.

Note that for
s=1
we obtain triangles as neighbourhoods of
(0,0)
, but they are arbitrarily ,,slim”, i.e. their angles at the vertex
(0,0)
tend to
0
.

3.
Topologies induced by basic families

Now, we present a necessary condition (a criterion) to have a homeomorphism between two topologies induced by basic families.

Lemma 2
.

Assume that
\{p_{n}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
and
\{t_{n}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
are basic families, inducing topologies
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
, respectively.
If spaces
(H,\tau_{0})
and
(H,\tau_{1})
are homeomorphic, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and functions
\gamma,\delta\colon(0,\varepsilon)\to(0,\infty)
such that for every
m>n
there exists
k
such that
\lim_{h\to 0}\delta(h)=0
and
\liminf_{h\to 0}\gamma(h)\leqslant 1
, and

t_{1}(\delta(x))\leqslant t_{k}\bigg(\frac{p_{m}^{-1}(x)\delta(x)\gamma(x)}{p^{-1}_{n}(x)}\bigg)

for any
x\in(0,\varepsilon)
.

The proof of this criterion shall be preceded by some lemmas, hence it is postponed to the last section.

First, we will show how to use the lemma, showing that the topology generated by triangles with bounded angles is not homeomorphic to the Niemytzki plane.

Example 3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
,
a_{n}=(n\tan\frac{\alpha n}{n+1})^{-1}

\textstyle t_{\alpha,n}\colon[-a_{n},a_{n}]\to[0,\frac{1}{n}]\mbox{ and }t_{\alpha,n}(z)=|z|\tan\frac{\alpha n}{n+1}.

It is the basic family which induces a topology not homeomorphic to the Niemytzki plane.
Indeed, suppose that there exists such a homeomorphism onto the Niemytzki plane.
By Lemma
2
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and functions
\gamma,\delta\colon(0,\varepsilon)\to(0,\infty)
satisfying the hypothesis of the theorem for basic families
\{t_{\alpha,n}\colon n\geqslant 1\},\{p_{n}\colon n\geqslant 1\}
, where functions
p_{n}
are given by the formula
p_{n}(x)=nx^{2}
.
For
x\in(0,\varepsilon)
we have

, so substituting

in the denominator, we get

\frac{\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\gamma(x)\tan\frac{\alpha k}{k+1}}{\tan(\alpha/2)}.

\frac{\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\tan\alpha}{\tan(\alpha/2)}<1.

\liminf_{x\to 0^{+}}\gamma(x)\leqslant 1

Consider a basic family

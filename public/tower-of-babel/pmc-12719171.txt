FeCuCe trimetallic nanoalloys exhibiting superior peroxidase activity for colorimetric detection of total antioxidant capacity
Authors: Fang Wang, Xiuhui Zeng, Meiting Fu, Fanjun Lin, Haixia Li, Chenchen Li, Lifang Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTotal antioxidant capacity (TAC) serves as a valuable parameter for evaluating the quality of natural foods and plants. We report the synthesis of FeCuCe nanoalloys with a high density of uniformly distributed catalytic sites that function as efficient peroxidase-like nanozymes for sensitive colorimetric TAC detection. Kinetic analysis demonstrated strong FeCuCe nanoalloy affinities for both 3,3,5,5-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) and H2O2 substrates, with Km values of 3.29 ± 0.23 mmol/L and 0.17 ± 0.01 mmol/L, respectively. This enabled the development of a sensitive ascorbic acid (AsA) colorimetric detection platform with a detection limit of 0.69 μmol/L. FeCuCe nanoalloy efficacy for antioxidant assessment was further validated using cysteine (Cys) and glutathione (GSH). The assay successfully evaluated TAC in several fruits and fruit juice beverages, demonstrating potential for rapid, cost-effective on-site TAC screening.Keywords: Multimetallic nanoenzymes, FeCuCe nanoalloys, Peroxidase-like activity, Colorimetric detection, Total antioxidant capacity, Foods

Introduction

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) participate in essential physiological processes such as cell signaling and redox homeostasis. However, elevated ROS concentrations lead to oxidative stress and cause damage to key biomolecules, including DNA, RNA, proteins, and lipids (Sies et al., 2022). Over time, excessive ROS levels may result in serious diseases such as neurodegenerative conditions, gastrointestinal disorders, respiratory complications, and malignancies (Yang & Lian, 2020). As ROS scavengers, antioxidant substances can effectively counteract oxidative stress, enhance immune responses, slow the aging process, and help prevent various chronic diseases (He et al., 2017). Dietary antioxidants are widely distributed in nature, predominantly occurring in plant-derived foods such as fruits, vegetables, nuts, seeds, and selected beverages (Jideani et al., 2021).

Total antioxidant capacity (TAC) quantitatively assesses the combined free radical–scavenging potential of a sample, reflecting its integrated antioxidant defense system against oxidative damage. Quantitative TAC analysis is a critical quality parameter for food products and dietary supplements and is widely used in standardized food quality monitoring, precise nutritional profiling, and screening for antioxidant-based dietary therapies (Mendonça et al., 2022). Current methodologies for TAC quantification include spectrophotometric assays, fluorescence-based detection, chemiluminescence techniques, electrochemical approaches, and liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (Liu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Qian et al., 2025; Sen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2021). Among these methods, spectrophotometric assays have emerged as particularly promising for on-site TAC determination due to their operational simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and minimal instrumental requirements. Conventional spectrophotometric assays, including 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) and 2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) methods, exhibit significant limitations in physiological relevance and practical implementation, respectively (Csepregi et al., 2016; Munteanu & Apetrei, 2021). Specifically, the DPPH assay requires dissolving the reagent in an organic solvent, whereas the ABTS assay involves a cumbersome detection procedure and strict light-avoidance conditions. Consequently, the development of low-cost, convenient, and reliable visual methods for field-based TAC assessment has received substantial research attention in recent years.

The incorporation of nanozyme-based architectures into biosensing systems has transformed detection paradigms by leveraging their biomimetic catalytic functionalities (Ameen & Omer, 2025). In contrast to natural enzymes, nanozymes combine the distinctive properties of nanomaterials with high catalytic efficiency. Their robust physicochemical stability and cost-effectiveness further make them promising enzyme mimics (Huang et al., 2019; Robert & Meunier, 2022). Recent studies have extensively reported nanozyme-based peroxidase activity for TAC detection, focusing on metallic materials, carbon-based nanomaterials, and metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) (Lin et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). For instance, Ce-based MOFs and other nanocomposite materials have been employed to enhance luminescent properties in biosensing platforms (Shan et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2024; Zhen et al., 2025). The metal active centers of metallic materials can replicate the electron-redox processes catalyzed by natural enzymes, and multimetallic nanozymes exhibit enhanced enzymatic activity owing to their abundant catalytic active sites. Their superior chemical stability and electrochemical performance arise from efficient intermetallic charge transfer and potential synergistic effects (Y. L. Liu et al., 2021; Pietrzak & Ivanova, 2021). The interplay between different elements optimizes active-site synergy and enhances catalytic performance. Furthermore, the catalytic efficiency and activity of multimetallic nanozymes can be precisely tuned by adjusting their composition and structure, which depend strongly on synthesis conditions such as metal oxidation state, reducing agents, and temperature (Liu et al., 2020; Phan-Xuan et al., 2024). Currently, noble-metal-based nanozymes (e.g., Au, Pt, and Pd) are widely used across various fields due to their intrinsic catalytic properties (Wu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023). For instance, Wu et al. (2023) developed a Au2Pt bimetallic nanozyme-based colorimetric method for TAC assessment in complex food matrices, including milk, green tea, and orange juice. However, inherent limitations in conventional designs—such as reliance on noble metals and inefficient atomic utilization—restrict cost-effectiveness and sustainable scalability. Unlike precious metals, Fe and Cu are essential trace elements in biological systems, functioning as key cofactors in various natural enzymes. Fe/Cu-based nanomaterials also offer advantages of cost-effectiveness and high natural abundance (Kute et al., 2022) and have been successfully applied in sensitive and cost-effective electrochemical sensors for natural compounds, demonstrating their versatility in analytical applications (Wu et al., 2025). While Fe- and Cu-based nanomaterials have demonstrated promising catalytic activity, their performance is often limited by the scarcity and limited diversity of accessible active sites. A viable strategy to overcome this inherent constraint is to introduce a third metal component, thereby creating ternary nanoalloys with richer and more complex catalytic interfaces.

In this study, novel trimetallic nanozymes, designated as FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys, were successfully synthesized for the first time using non-noble metals. The multimetallic FeCuCe nanozymes exhibit exceptional peroxidase-like (POD-like) catalytic activity and an extraordinarily high affinity for H2O2, enabling the oxidation of 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) to its blue oxidized form (ox-TMB). A distinct absorbance peak at 652 nm is generated. Leveraging the superior catalytic performance of the FeCuCe nanozyme and the resulting colorimetric signal changes, a highly selective and sensitive biosensor for TAC was developed, using H2O2 as a key reaction intermediate (Scheme 1).

Experimental section

Reagents and instruments

CuCl2·2H2O (99 %), FeSO4·7H2O (99 %), (CH3COO)2Zn·2H2O (99 %), Ce(NO3)3·6H2O (99.5 %), Mo(CO)6 (99 %), CH3COONa (99 %), CH3CH2OH (99 %), CH3COOH (99 %), and H2O2 (wt% 30 %) were purchased from Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China, https://www.macklin.cn). Citric acid, polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP, 99 %), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99 %), 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB, 99 %), ascorbic acid (AsA, 99 %), glutathione (GSH, 98 %), and cysteine (Cys, 98 %) were procured from Aladdin Bio-Chem Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China, https://www.aladdin-e.com). All reagents were used without further purification. Deionized water was used in all experiments.

The UV–vis absorption spectra were measured using a U-2600 spectrophotometer (Hitachi, Japan) with a medium scan speed and a 1 nm bandwidth. The morphology of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys was characterized using a JEM-2100F field emission electron microscope (JEOL, Japan). X-ray photoelectron spectrometry (XPS) of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys was performed on a Thermo Scientifc™ K-Alpha™+ spectrometer. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys were obtained using a D8 Advance powder diffractometer (Bruker, Germany).

Preparation of FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys

The FeCuZn and FeCuCe nanoalloys were synthesized following a previously reported method with slight modifications, in which the precursors Pt(acac)2, Cu(CH3COO)2 and H6TeO6 were replaced with FeSO4, CuCl2 and Ce(NO3)3 (Guo et al., 2024). Briefly, 4.6 mg of FeSO4·7H2O, 2.8 mg of CuCl2·2H2O, and 7.2 mg of Ce(NO3)3·6H2O were thoroughly mixed in a glass beaker. Subsequently, 3.0 mg of Mo(CO)6, 60.0 mg of citric acid (CA), 200.0 mg of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), and 10 mL of DMF were sequentially added under ultrasonic agitation for 30 min. After complete dissolution, the mixture was transferred into a Teflon-lined autoclave and subjected to solvothermal treatment at 200 °C for 8 h. After natural cooling, the product was collected by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 15 min, followed by three washing cycles with anhydrous ethanol. The resulting precipitate was dried in a vacuum oven and stored for further characterization.

The FeCuZn nanoalloys were prepared using the same procedure, except that 7.2 mg of Ce(NO3)3·6H2O was replaced with 3.6 mg of (CH3COO)2Zn·2H2O.

POD-like performance of FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys

The catalytic activity of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys was monitored using the TMB–H2O2 system. Specifically, 0.2 mg/mL FeCuCe or FeCuZn nanoalloys, 25 mmol/L TMB and 50 mmol/L H2O2 were mixed in acetate buffer (100 mmol/L, pH 3.5) and the corresponding absorbance values were recorded. Steady-state kinetic analysis of the POD-like activity was performed in 96-well plates according to established protocols. The assays were conducted by systematically varying the concentrations of TMB and H2O2 while maintaining the FeCuCe or FeCuZn nanoalloy concentration constant. Colorimetric detection of TAC

Under the selected experimental conditions, the standard reaction system consisted of 215 μL of acetate buffer, 15 μL of FeCuCe nanoalloy suspension (250 μg/mL), 5 μL of TMB solution (25 mmol/L in dimethyl sulfoxide, DMSO), and 5 μL of H2O2 (50 mmol/L), followed by incubation at 40 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, 150 μL of AsA solution (0–28 μmol/L) was added, and the absorbance at 652 nm was recorded using a UV–visible spectrophotometer. To evaluate the assay's versatility for TAC determination, parallel experiments were conducted by substituting AsA with different concentrations of GSH (0–100 μmol/L) and Cys (0–40 μmol/L).

TAC detection of real samples

The practical applicability of the FeCuCe platform was assessed by determining TAC in seven food samples, including five fruits (orange, grapefruit, lemon, kiwifruit, and winter jujube) and two commercial fruit juices (pulpy orange and hawthorn juice). Fresh fruits were purchased from a local supermarket. Oranges, grapefruits, lemons, and kiwifruits were manually peeled. Winter jujubes were washed, air-dried, and pitted. Random segments of orange (62.52 g) and grapefruit (84.65 g), one whole lemon (65.97 g) and kiwifruit (70.06 g), and a portion of winter jujubes (55.14 g) were separately juiced using a juice extractor. The resulting juices were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatants were collected, passed through a 0.45 μm microporous membrane filter, and diluted appropriately to ensure that the AsA concentration fell within the linear range for colorimetric analysis. The commercial fruit juice samples were also procured locally, filtered through a 0.45 μm microporous membrane filter, and diluted with deionized water to reduce matrix effects.

For the standard addition recovery experiment, the pretreated samples were spiked with a minimal volume of concentrated AsA standard solution before dilution for analysis. The processed samples were analyzed following the experimental procedure described above. TAC values for all samples were reported as millimolar AsA equivalents per liter (μmol/L AsA eq/L).

Results and discussion

Characterization of FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys

The FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys were prepared using a solvothermal method, in which equimolar metallic salts served as precursors, citric acid acted as the reducing agent, Mo(CO)6 functioned as an auxiliary reducing agent, PVP served as the stabilizer, and DMF was used as the solvent.

As shown in Fig. 1A and Fig. 1F, TEM characterization revealed clear morphological differences between the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys. The FeCuCe nanoalloys appeared as nearly circular nanosheets with a diameter of approximately 190 ± 3 nm, and several nanosheets were stacked layer by layer. In contrast, the FeCuZn nanoalloys exhibited irregular edge structures with a transverse size of about 170 ± 4 nm (Fig. S1A). The energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping images (Fig. 1B–E) of the FeCuCe nanoalloys showed that Fe, Cu, and Ce were uniformly distributed throughout the nanomaterials, indicating successful formation of multimetallic nanozymes with a high density of uniformly distributed catalytic sites. In the FeCuZn nanoalloys, Fe and Cu were uniformly distributed, whereas Zn was present at lower levels (Fig. 1G–J). Compared with the FeCuCe nanoalloys, which contained a uniform distribution of cerium, the lower zinc content in the FeCuZn nanoalloys resulted in a reduced number of active sites.

TEM and EDS images of (A–E) FeCuCe nanoalloys and (F–J) FeCuZn nanoalloys.

Fig. 2A shows that the atomic ratios of Fe, Cu, Ce, C, and O in the FeCuCe nanoalloys are 2.5 %, 0.5 %, 5 %, 51 %, and 41 %, respectively. The peaks at 709.6 and 723.5 eV were assigned to Fe2+ 2p3/2 and Fe2+ 2p1/2, while the peaks at 710.7 and 724.6 eV corresponded to Fe3+ 2p3/2 and Fe3+ 2p1/2. Multiple shake-up peaks further confirmed the presence of multiple iron valence states (Fig. 2B). The curve-fitted Cu XPS spectra demonstrated the coexistence of Cu+ and Cu2+. For Cu 2p3/2, peaks at approximately 932.2 eV and 933.9 eV were attributed to Cu+ and Cu2+, respectively (Fig. 2C). The peaks approximately 885.3, 886.6, and 903.1 eV corresponded to 3d5/2 of Ce(III), and 900.5, 904.0, and 906.2 eV corresponded to 3d3/2 of Ce(III). Peaks at approximately 882.2, 896.7, and 917.1 eV were assigned to Ce(IV),while those at 877.83 and 881.2 eV were attributed to Ce(0), which was present at very low levels (Fig. 2D). These results indicate that the multiple oxidation states of metals in the FeCuCe nanoalloys provide diverse catalytic active sites. As shown in Fig. S2A, the atomic ratios of Fe, Cu, Zn, C, and O in the FeCuZn nanoalloys are 9 %, 2 %, 1 %, 34 %, and 54 %, respectively. The peaks at 711.0 and 724.7 eV corresponded to 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 of Fe3+ (Fig. S2B). For Cu 2p3/2, the peaks at approximately 932.2 eV and 933.9 eV were assigned to Cu+ and Cu2+, respectively, and the shake-up peaks at 940.6, 943.7, and 962.6 eV further confirmed the presence of Cu2+ (Fig. S2C). The peaks at approximately 1021.5 and 1044.6 eV corresponded to 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 of Zn2+ (Fig. S2D). The presence of only a single oxidation state for Fe and Zn in the FeCuZn nanoalloys may account for their relatively low enzymatic activity.

Peroxidase activity analysis of FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys

After optimizing the assay pH (Fig. S3 A), the peroxidase-mimicking activities of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys were quantitatively evaluated using TMB-based chromogenic assays under standardized conditions (pH 3.5, 25 °C). As shown in Fig. 3A, the characteristic 652 nm absorbance corresponding to ox-TMB was absent in control systems containing FeCuCe/TMB (a), FeCuCe/H2O2 (b), or TMB/H2O2 (c), with no visible blue coloration. In contrast, the FeCuCe/TMB/H₂O₂ system (d) exhibited a pronounced absorbance peak at 652 nm along with intense blue coloration, confirming efficient ox-TMB formation. Complementary data in Fig. S3B show similar catalytic behavior for the FeCuZn nanoalloys. These results conclusively demonstrate that both FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys possess intrinsic peroxidase-like activity, catalyzing the decomposition of H2O2 to hydroxyl radicals (·OH), followed by the oxidation of TMB to ox-TMB.

(A) UV absorption spectra of FeCuCe nanoalloys under different conditions in the range of 0–50 μmol/L; (B) UV–Vis spectra showing changes with H2O2 concentration; (C, D) Michaelis–Menten curves of FeCuCe nanoalloys using H2O2 and TMB as substrates.

Notably, the absorbance intensity of the FeCuCe/TMB/H₂O₂ system increased in a concentration-dependent manner with increasing H2O2 levels (0–50 μmol/L) (Fig. 3B), displaying excellent linearity: ΔA = 0.00905CH₂O₂ + 0.0145 (R = 0.999) (Fig. S4). The calculated limit of detection (LOD = 0.039 μmol/L) surpassed that of most reported nanozymes (Table S1), confirming exceptional sensitivity. This robust H₂O₂ response establishes the FeCuCe nanoalloys as a high-performance colorimetric probe for both direct H2O2 quantification and indirect detection of H2O2-related analytes.

The POD-mimetic kinetic characteristics of the FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys were rigorously evaluated using Michaelis–Menten analysis according to established protocols (Jiang et al., 2018). Typically, a lower Km value reflects stronger enzyme–substrate binding affinity, whereas an elevated υm indicates enhanced turnover frequency and catalytic proficiency under substrate-saturated conditions (Aghaei et al., 2020; Shiva & Aghaei, 2020). Kinetic parameters for the FeCuCe nanoalloys were obtained from Fig. 3C–D. The analysis revealed Km values of 3.29 ± 0.23 mmol/L for TMB and 0.17 ± 0.01 mmol/L for H2O2, with corresponding υm values of (55.80 ± 2.62) × 10−8 mol/(L·s) and (13.69 ± 0.28) × 10−8 mol/(L·s), respectively. Notably, the Km value for H2O2 was 68-fold lower than that of horseradish peroxidase (HRP), indicating markedly enhanced substrate affinity, while the TMB affinity remained comparable. The FeCuCe nanoalloys achieved 40-fold and 5-fold higher υm values for TMB and H2O2, respectively, relative to native HRP. In particular, the FeCuCe nanoalloys possessed the lowest Km value for H2O2 among the surveyed nanozymes—2000-fold lower than that of Pd—Ir cubes. Although the Pt/C nanozyme displayed a highest υm for H2O2 and the FeCuCe nanoalloys ranked the second highest among the remaining nanozymes, exhibiting a υm value 21-times that of ITO-SiO2-PRS-Au/PdNPs. Moreover, the υm for TMB was the highest across all nanozymes. These results indicate that the FeCuCe nanoalloys possess both strong H2O2 affinity and outstanding catalytic capability. Kinetic parameters for the FeCuZn nanoalloys were obtained from Fig. S3C—D. The analysis showed Km values of 0.62 ± 0.05 mmol/L for TMB and 5.37 ± 0.37 mmol/L for H2O2, with corresponding υm values of (8.10 ± 0.75) × 10−8 and (13.53 ± 0.91) × 10−8 mol/(L·s), respectively. As benchmarked against various POD-mimetic nanomaterials in Table S2, the FeCuCe nanoalloys exhibited superior overall catalytic efficiency, firmly establishing their exceptional peroxidase-like activity. The FeCuZn nanoalloys demonstrated a substantially lower affinity for H2O2. Consequently, the FeCuCe nanoalloy-based system was selected for subsequent construction of the colorimetric sensor.

Colorimetric detection of TAC

Antioxidants mediate the reduction of ox-TMB to TMB, resulting in progressive decolorization from blue to colorless. This redox transition enables quantitative assessment of antioxidant capacity through ox-TMB absorbance decay kinetics. Leveraging the exceptional POD-mimetic activity of the FeCuCe nanoalloys, we developed a colorimetric platform for rapid and highly sensitive TAC quantification. Ascorbic acid, a representative antioxidant, is widely present in food matrices, pharmaceutical formulations, and biological systems (Zhong et al., 2023). Ascorbic acid effectively scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the reaction system, thereby reducing the absorbance intensity through redox-mediated signal suppression. As shown in Fig. 4 A, the absorbance at 652 nm decreased with increasing AsA concentration in the standard solution. Within the range of 2.0–20.0 μmol/L, a strong linear relationship between the decrease in absorbance and AsA concentration was observed, described by the calibration equation ∆A = 0.0222CAsA − 0.0281 (R = 0.996) (n = 3, *p < 0.05) (Fig. 4D). The detection limit was calculated to be 0.69 μmol/L. A comprehensive comparison between the proposed method and established AsA detection techniques was performed, with detailed benchmarking results provided in Table S3. The findings show that the present method achieves sensitivity comparable to conventional approaches, as reflected by statistically equivalent LOD values.

Glutathione and cysteine are crucial biothiol antioxidants that play vital roles in numerous physiological processes (Zhou et al., 2023). Their effects on the assay system were examined at various concentrations (Fig. 4B—C). Both GSH and Cys significantly suppressed the absorbance at 652 nm. The linear detection ranges were 1.0–10.0 μmol/L and 10.0–100.0 μmol/L for GSH, with corresponding calibration equations of ∆A = 0.0262CGSH + 0.120 (R = 0.996) and ∆A = 0.00445CGSH + 0.330 (R = 0.997) (n = 3, *p < 0.05), respectively (Fig. 4E). The detection limits (LODs) are 0.59 μmol/L for GSH. The absorbance signal showed a correlation with the Cys content with satisfactory linearity from 2.0 to 35.0 μmol/L (∆A = 0.00991CGSH + 0.0343 (R = 0.998) (n = 3, *p < 0.05) and a LOD of 1.55 μmol/L (Fig. 4F).

Selectivity and anti-interference study

To assess potential interference effects on antioxidant determination, we evaluated common fruit constituents as possible interferents. The system's response was examined for amino acids (arginine, histidine, glycine, tryptophan, glutamic acid, leucine, threonine, lysine, proline), carbohydrates (glucose, maltose, fructose, sucrose, starch), common ions (Na+, K+, Ca2+, Cl−) and organic acids (tartaric acid and citric acid) under identical experimental conditions. As shown in Fig. 5A, no significant absorbance variation was observed in the presence of these substances, confirming the excellent anti-interference capability of the FeCuCe nanoalloy-based biosensor for TAC detection. The flavor, texture, and nutritional quality of fruits are governed by complex interactions among their constituent amino acids, saccharides, mineral ions, and organic acids. Although the results demonstrate strong selectivity against individual interferents, it is acknowledged that, in complex food matrices, the combined presence of multiple components may lead to synergistic or antagonistic effects that are not captured by single-component tests. While the present study focused on establishing baseline selectivity, future work will evaluate the method across a broader range of complex samples.

Furthermore, the FeCuCe nanoalloys exhibited high stability, as the absorbance signal retained its initial intensity with minimal variation over five consecutive weeks of repeated assays (Fig. 5B).

Analysis of actual samples

Total antioxidant capacity serves as a comprehensive parameter for quantifying the collective antioxidants present in food products, pharmaceuticals, and biological systems. This critical metric plays an essential role in assessing the quality of food and medicinal products. Given the established use of AsA as a calibration standard in antioxidant assays, we employed AsA as the reference compound for TAC determination in this study.

To evaluate the efficacy of the FeCuCe nanoalloys in TAC measurement, a colorimetric assay was performed on several fruits and fruit juices. The AsA concentrations in the diluted fruit juices were determined using the developed TAC assay (Table 1). Based on the respective dilution factors (700-, 500-, 700-, 600-, and 750-fold for orange, grapefruit, lemon, kiwifruit, and winter jujube, respectively), the AsA concentrations in the original juices were calculated. Winter jujube exhibited the highest AsA content (6.08 mmol/L, equivalent to 1070.8 mg/L), followed by orange (4.84 mmol/L, 852.4 mg/L), lemon (4.77 mmol/L, 840.1 mg/L), kiwifruit (3.80 mmol/L, 669.3 mg/L), and grapefruit (3.18 mmol/L, 560.1 mg/L), displaying a clear decreasing trend. Additionally, the TAC values for pulpy orange juice and hawthorn juice, after 400- and 200-fold dilution, were determined to be 6.80 μmol/L and 5.60 μmol/L, respectively. The corresponding original concentrations were calculated to be 2.72 mmol/L (479.0 mg/L) and 1.12 mmol/L (197.3 mg/L). The TAC value for pulpy orange juice exceeded its labeled AsA content, likely due to the presence of additional antioxidants in the juice.

To validate the reliability of the FeCuCe-nanoalloys–based colorimetric assay for TAC measurement, recovery tests were performed for AsA in fruits and fruit juices using the standard addition method. As summarized in Table 1, after spiking with 2 μmol/L, 4 μmol/L, and 6 μmol/L AsA standard solutions, the recoveries for orange, grapefruit, lemon, kiwifruit juices, and winter jujube ranged from 97.4 % to 108.6 %. For pulpy orange and hawthorn juice, recoveries ranged from 97.4 % to 101.0 % at the same spiking concentrations. These results demonstrate that the developed TAC assay enables accurate and reproducible quantification of AsA in complex sample matrices, underscoring its suitability for food analysis.

The radical cation decolorization assay based on the ABTS standard method was used as a reference because of its widespread application and well-established reliability in determining total antioxidant capacity. As shown in Table 2, the measured values for the fruits and fruit juices exhibited excellent consistency with the AsA content determined by the ABTS method, except for winter jujube and hawthorn juice. The measured values for winter jujube and hawthorn juice were lower than the corresponding AsA contents obtained by the ABTS method, likely due to the influence of other reducible species. Beyond AsA, both winter jujube and hawthorn contain substantial levels of polyphenols, including flavonoids and phenolic acids. Winter jujube also contains anthocyanins and polysaccharides, while hawthorn includes additional antioxidants such as vitamin E and triterpenoids. In this study, the FeCuCe-nanoalloys–based colorimetric assay was conducted at pH 3.5, whereas the standard ABTS method was performed at pH 5.5. It is well established that the reducing capacity and electron-donating activity of antioxidant substances are highly dependent on the pH of the reaction medium. This notable pH difference likely alters the protonation states and redox potentials of the antioxidants present in these complex matrices. For instance, phenolic acids exhibit structural stability under acidic conditions, where the phenolic hydroxyl groups predominantly remain protonated due to their low dissociation tendency, thereby conferring stronger reducing activity. In contrast, under neutral to alkaline conditions, these phenolic hydroxyl groups readily deprotonate to form phenolate anions, which are more susceptible to oxidation and can undergo coupled co-oxidation. Regarding anthocyanins, their behavior is primarily governed by the protonation/deprotonation of the flavylium cation core. Under strongly acidic conditions, anthocyanins predominantly exist as the flavylium cation, characterized by an intact conjugated system that typically correlates with stronger antioxidant capacity. As the pH increases, sequential deprotonation occurs, leading to a further attenuation of their antioxidant capacity. Notably, whereas phenolic acids and anthocyanins show a decline in antioxidant capacity with rising pH, flavonoids exhibit the opposite trend, achieving maximal activity under neutral to weakly alkaline conditions. Although ascorbic acid (AA) remains the primary contributor to the total antioxidant capacity of fruits, its overall effect is modulated by these pH-dependent constituents. Consequently, the distinct hydrogen-ion concentrations in the two assay systems are likely a major factor underlying the observed discrepancies in the measured TAC values.

Conclusions

This study successfully synthesized non-precious-metal-based FeCuCe and FeCuZn nanoalloys via a hydrothermal method. Both nanoalloys exhibited pronounced peroxidase-like activity, with cerium incorporation yielding substantially enhanced catalytic performance compared to zinc. Leveraging the superior catalytic properties of FeCuCe nanoalloys, the study developed a sensitive colorimetric method for antioxidant detection, demonstrating its applicability for TAC evaluation in a range of fruits and fruit juices. The exceptional catalytic efficiency and selectivity of FeCuCe nanoalloys position them as promising artificial enzyme alternatives for biosensing applications. The developed enzyme-based biosensing systems thus present a promising strategy with direct implications for the food industry. By offering a reliable means for quality control, food safety monitoring, and nutritional assessment, this research opens up avenues for the creation of novel, on-site analytical tools. We believe these findings align closely with the scope of food chemistry by contributing a practical and innovative approach to addressing contemporary challenges in food analysis.

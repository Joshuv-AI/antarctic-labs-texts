Planar graphs without cycles of length 4 or 5 are ( 7 ​ m : 2 ​ m ) (7m:2m) -DP-colorable
Xiaoyan Xu Xuding Zhu School of Mathematical Sciences, Zhejiang Normal University

Abstract
It was conjectured by Steinberg in 1976 that planar graphs without cycles of length 4 or 5 are 3-colorable. This conjecture attracted a substantial amount of attention and was finally refuted by Cohen-Addad, Hebdige, Král’, Li and Salgado in 2017. Although Steinberg’s conjecture is settled, coloring of this family of graphs, as well as some other families of planar graphs forbidding certain cycle lengths have been attracting a lot of recent attention and many challenging problems remain open. One problem of interest is multiple coloring and multiple list coloring of this family of graphs. It was proved by Dvǒrák and Hu that planar graphs without cycles of length 4 or 5 are ( 11 , 3 ) (11,3) -colorable, and this result was improved by Wang, who proved that graphs in this family are ( 7 : 2 ) (7:2) -colorable. On the other hand, it was proved by Xu and Zhu that for every positive integer m m , there is a graph in this family which is not ( 3 ​ m + ⌊ m − 1 12 ⌋ , m ) (3m+\lfloor\frac{m-1}{12}\rfloor,m) -choosable. In this paper, we prove that for any positive integer m m , graphs in this family are ( 7 ​ m : 2 ​ m ) (7m:2m) -DP-colorable, and hence ( 7 ​ m , 2 ​ m ) (7m,2m) -choosable.

Introduction

One problem of interest in planar graph coloring is which planar graphs are 3-colorable, and which planar graphs are 3-choosable. For a sequence
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{q}
of positive integers, we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{q}}
the family of connected planar graphs without cycles of length
k_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,q
.
The classical Grötzsch Theorem
[
10
]
asserts graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{3}
are 3-colorable. In 1976, Steinberg conjectured that graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5}
are 3-colorable (see
[
6
]
). This conjecture attracted considerable attention. As an approach to this conjecture, Erdős proposed the problem in 1991 to determine the smallest integer
k
such that every graph in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5,\ldots,k}
is 3-colorable. It was proved by Abbott and Zhou that
k\leq 11

[
1
]
, and this bound was improved by Sanders and Zhao to
k\leq 9

[
12
]
, and further improved by Borodin, Glebov, Raspaud and Salavatipour to
k\leq 7

[
5
]
. Many other relaxations or variations of Steinberg’s conjecture have been proved in the literature. For example, it is known that for
4<i<j<10
and
(i,j)\notin\{(5,6),(7,8),(5,9),(8,9)\}
, graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,i,j}
are 3-colorable (see
[
11
]
for detailed references).
In 2017, Cohen-Addad, Hebdige, Král’, Li and Salgado
[
7
]
proved that
Steinberg’s conjecture is false. Now it remains a challenging open question whether graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5,6}
are 3-colorable.

For list coloring, Thomassen’s classical result asserts that planar graphs of girth at least 5 are 3-choosable. Erdős’ problem is also naturally extended to the list version: Determine the smallest integer
\ell
such that graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5,\ldots,\ell}
are 3-choosable. It was proved by Voigt
[
13
]
that
\ell\geq 6
, and proved by Dvǒrák and Postle
[
9
]
that
\ell\leq 8
. Moreover, it is known that graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,7,9},\mathcal{P}_{3,6,7,8},\mathcal{P}_{4,5,6,9},\mathcal{P}_{4,6,8,9}
are 3-DP-colorable, and hence
3
-choosable (see
[
11
]
for detailed references).

Multiple coloring and multiple list coloring of graphs allow us to explore the colorability of graphs in a finer scale. As Steinberg’s conjecture is disproved, a natural problem is explore multiple coloring of graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5}
.

(f,g)
-choosable if

-coloring for each

-list assignment

If

-coloring of a graph

-coloring of

The

It was proved by Alon,Tuza and Voigt

is not a new parameter. The concept of

ch_{f}^{*}(G)=\inf\{r:G\text{ is $(a,b)$-choosable for all integers $a,b$ with $a/b\geq r$}\}.

For a family
\mathcal{G}
of graphs, let

\chi_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\sup\{\chi_{f}(G):G\in\mathcal{G}\}\text{ and }ch_{f}^{*}(\mathcal{G})=\sup\{ch_{f}^{*}(G):G\in\mathcal{G}\}.

Multiple coloring and multiple list coloring of graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{4,5}
are studied in a few papers. It was proved by Dvǒrák and Hu
[
8
]
that every graph

-colorable. This result was improved by Wang
[
14
]
who proved that every graph

is
(7,2)
-colorable. The current known upper bound for the strong fractional choice number is

, and for the lower bound, it was proved by Xu and Zhu
[
15

that for any positive integer

which is not

-choosable. This implies that

. In this paper, we prove that for any positive integer

is
(7m,2m)
-choosable, and hence

.

Our result is proved in the setting of multiple DP-coloring of graphs.
The concept of DP-coloring (also known as correspondence coloring) is a generalization of list coloring introduced by Dvořák and Postle in

]

Definition 2
.

Let

is a family of pairwise disjoint sets, and

is a matching between sets

and

. For a function

.

Definition 3
.

Given a cover
\mathcal{H}=(L,M)
of a graph

, an

-coloring of

. We say

-colorable if it has an

-coloring. For a function

, we say

\mathcal{H}
-colorable for every
f
-cover
\mathcal{H}
of

.
The DP-chromatic number of

\chi_{DP}(G)=\min\{k:G\text{ is $k$-DP-colorable}\}.

The concept of

-DP-colorable is a generalization of

-choosable. Given an
f
-list assignment

of

, let
\mathcal{H}=(L,M)
be the

-cover of

, and for each edge

-coloring of

-coloring of

that the difference

Multiple DP-coloring of graphs was first studied in

]
.

Assume

Theorem 1

Multiple DP-coloring of some small graphs

6
Discharging

This section completes the proof of Theorem
2
by deriving a contradiction with a discharging procedure. The
initial charge function

\operatorname{ch}
is defined as:
\operatorname{ch}(v)=2d_{G}(v)-6
for
v\in V
,
\operatorname{ch}(f)=d(f)-6
for
f\in F\backslash\left\{f_{0}\right\}
and
\operatorname{ch}(f_{0})=d\left(f_{0}\right)+6
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V\cup F}\operatorname{ch}(x)=0

We apply the following discharging rules to obtain the final charge function
\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}
.

6.1
Discharging rules

R1.
Every internal 3-face receives 1 from each of its vertices.

R2.
Let
v
be an internal triangular 3-vertex and
v^{\prime}
the unique isolated neighbor of
v
.

(1) If
v
is poor, then
v
receives
\frac{1}{2}
from each of its two non-isolated neighbors.

(2) If
v
is not poor, then
v
receives 1 from
v^{\prime}
.

R3.
Every poor 4-vertex receives
\frac{1}{2}
from each of its non-isolated neighbors.

R4.
Every external 2-vertex receives 2 from
f_{0}
.

R5.
Let
v
be an external 3-vertex.

(1) If
v
is triangular, then
v
receives
\frac{3}{2}
from
f_{0}
.

(2) If
v
is non-triangular, then
v
receives 1 from
f_{0}
.

R6.
Let
v
be an external 4-vertex.

(1) If
v
is 2-triangular, then
v
receives 1 from
f_{0}
.

(2) If
v
is 1-triangular, then
v
receives
\frac{1}{2}
from
f_{0}
.

6.2
Analysis of the final charges

We shall prove that
\sum_{x\in V\cup F}\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(x)>0
.
On the other hand,
\sum_{x\in V\cup F}\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{x\in V\cup F}\operatorname{ch}(x)=0
. This is a contradiction.

For each face
f
, if
d(f)=3
, then by R1,
\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(f)=\operatorname{ch}(f)+3\times 1=d(f)-6+3=0
. Otherwise
d(f)\geq 6
, and
\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(f)=\operatorname{ch}(f)=d(f)-6\geq 0
.

Next we show that
ch^{\prime}(v)\geq 0
for each vertex
v
.
For a vertex
v
, let
v_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d_{G}(v)
be the neighbors of
v
in a cyclic order,
and
f_{i}
,

, be the face incident to
v_{i}vv_{i+1}

where the indices are taken modulo

. If

is a boundary vertex, then let

be the two boundary neighbor of

. The following observation will be frequently used.

by R4, while each of the other two vertices receives at most
\frac{3}{2}
from
f_{0}

\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(f_{0})\geq\operatorname{ch}(f_{0})-2-2\times\frac{3}{2}=\operatorname{ch}(f_{0})+6-5=4>0
.

If
f_{0}
has no triangular edges, then

has at least two
3^{+}
-vertices, each of which receives at

.

Assume that
f_{0}

uv

f_{0}
to

If
f_{0}

\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}(f_{0})\geq\operatorname{ch}(f_{0})-(d(f_{0})-4)\times 2+2\times 3=8-d(f_{0})>0
.

Assume

uv
. If at least one of

\operatorname{ch}^{\prime}\left(f_{0}\right)\geq\operatorname{ch}\left(f_{0}\right)-\left(d\left(f_{0}\right)-2\right)\times 2-2=8-d\left(f_{0}\right)>0
.
If both

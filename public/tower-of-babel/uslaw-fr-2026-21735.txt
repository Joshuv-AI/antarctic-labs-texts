II. Background

    OCC is a central counterparty (``CCP''), which means that, as part 
of its function as a clearing agency, it interposes itself as the buyer 
to every seller and the seller to every buyer for financial 
transactions. As the CCP for the listed options markets in the United 
States,\5\ as well as for certain futures and stock loans, OCC is 
exposed to various risks arising from providing clearance and 
settlement services to its Clearing Members because OCC is obligated to 
perform on the contracts it clears.\6\ OCC mitigates such risks, in 
part, by setting requirements for its members (and applicants to become 
members), the protective measures OCC may impose to manage specific 
risks, and the sanctions OCC may impose where members do not comply 
with the requirements of membership.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Generally, the proposed changes affect OCC's requirements and 
procedures for membership as well as protective measures and sanctions 
including, but not limited to, the suspension of members. The proposed 
changes related to membership include conditions for admission of 
clearing member applicants as well as standards for existing Clearing 
Members. The proposed changes also include the adoption of several new 
definitions, technical, clarifying changes, amendments to OCC's 
governance standards, and, generally, the adoption of new rules 
relating to risk mitigation practices.

A. Clearing Membership

    As noted above, OCC proposes changes to its membership rules. The 
proposed changes would address the conditions for admission of clearing 
member applicants as well as the who are authorized to approve or deny 
such applications. The proposed changes also would address the ongoing 
requirements for Clearing Members.
1. Confidentiality Standards
    OCC's proposed confidentiality standards apply generally to its 
membership rules. Proposed Rule 207(d) sets forth the standards 
Clearing Members must observe when maintaining or exchanging ``OCC 
Confidential Information'' \7\--the proposed rule demands that members 
exercise a standard of care that it would employ to protect its own 
confidential information, but not less than a reasonable standard of 
care. The proposed rule further acknowledges that breach of 
confidentiality may result in OCC seeking injunction or other equitable 
relief against the breaching party and states that the rule does not 
preclude or otherwise prohibit OCC from providing confidential 
information to the government or other regulatory authority.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ OCC proposes to define the term OCC Confidential Information 
as all non-public information provided by OCC that is marked or, 
under the circumstance surrounding disclosure, otherwise identified 
in writing prior to disclosure to the recipient as ``confidential'' 
or ``business sensitive'' and is designated by OCC as confidential 
or the recipient knows or ought to reasonably know is confidential.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Conditions for Admission
    OCC proposes several changes to its Clearing Membership eligibility 
and admission standards, which are generally set forth in Chapter II of 
its rules. First, proposed rule 201(b) requires Clearing Members or 
applicants to maintain an operating history of at least one year in the 
same or substantially same business activities the member or applicant 
applies for. Alternatively, the member or applicant can maintain senior 
personnel with sufficient background or experience in such business 
activities.
    Rule 204 sets forth OCC's Conditions to Admission. OCC proposes to 
amend its rules with regard to who is authorized to approve or deny 
applications for Clearing Membership. In 2016, OCC vested authority to 
approve or disapprove new membership applications in the risk committee 
of its board of directors (the ``Risk Committee'') and delegated 
authority to approve certain applications to specific members of OCC 
management.\8\ Now, OCC proposes to allow the Risk Committee to 
delegate, to its designated delegates or agents, the ability to approve 
or deny new applications or reapplications. Specifically, OCC proposes 
amending Rules 203(b) through (e), Rule 204(a), (c) through (e), and 
(l) to include the phrase ``or its designated delegates or agents,'' 
which would vest in OCC's management the Risk Committee's authority to 
approve or deny certain applications and reapplications for Clearing 
Membership and requests for business expansion. OCC states that the 
purpose of permitting such delegation is streamline and accelerate the 
decision-making process and approval, while continuing to utilize a 
risk-based decision-making approach.\9\ OCC states further that the 
change would eliminate the need to wait until scheduled quarterly Risk 
Committee meetings to determine whether it will approve such 
applications or business expansion requests.\10\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ See Notice, 91 FR at 57187.

    The proposed changes would also amend an applicant's obligations to 
notify OCC regarding Statutory Disqualification. Specifically, OCC 
proposes to require that such notifications be provided not only where 
an applicant or Key Person \11\ is subject to a Statutory 
Disqualification, but also where the applicant or person is subject to 
formal investigation by a regulatory organization. OCC proposes to 
require that such notice be provided within two business days.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \11\ OCC proposes to define the term Key Person to include any 
person associated with a Clearing Member or applicant that OCC deems 
is critical to the Clearing Member or applicant's operations or risk 
management, including, but not limited to, the Clearing Member's 
President, Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief 
Risk Officer, and Chief Compliance Officer, or equivalent positions, 
or a major shareholder or partner.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    OCC next proposes amending Chapter II to include new Rules 204(e), 
(f) and (g). Rule 204(e) would set forth the standards by which OCC may 
disapprove an application or reapplication for membership or otherwise 
restrict denied applicants from reapplying upon becoming aware of any 
facts or circumstances about said applicant or reapplicant that poses

[[Page 67364]]

elevated risk to OCC or otherwise impacts the suitability of the 
applicant as a Clearing Member. Such revisions would include allowing 
denial of an application where a Key Person becomes an elevated risk to 
OCC or impacts the suitability of the applicant as a Clearing 
Member.\12\ Rule 204(f) would require a denied applicant to address the 
specific grounds for denial prior to reapplying for membership. Rule 
204(g) would require an applicant to maintain physical office space 
unless OCC determines that the applicant's use of a remote office 
remote model does not present a heightened risk to OCC. Lastly, 
proposed Rule 204(g) would make an applicant subject to OCC's procedure 
for conducting on-site visits to an applicant's facility as part of 
OCC's onboarding process. OCC believes the use of a fully remote, non-
hybrid, office model may introduce challenges for OCC to communicate 
with the applicant and perform an onsite examination of the applicant, 
therefore posing heightened risk to OCC.\13\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \12\ Rule 204(e) describes circumstances that may pose elevated 
risks to OCC which may include concerns with the result of an 
independent assessment of an applicant's ``AML'' program of ``OFAC'' 
sanctions list and sanctions list data. Proposed Rule 101 would 
define the term ``AML,'' when used in respect of an applicant of 
Clearing Member's AML controls, as meaning compliance with anti-
money laundering requirements imposed under United States law or 
comparable requirements in the Clearing Member's jurisdiction. In 
addition, Proposed Rule 101 would define ``OFAC'' as meaning the 
U.S. Department of Treasury's Office of Foreign Asset Control.
    \13\ See Notice, 91 FR at 57192. OCC believes that an 
applicant's lack of maintaining any physical office facility 
presents risks that would not exist in a hybrid office model where 
an applicant may have some form of physical office to allow for in-
person communication. OCC believes the risks posed by an applicant 
that is fully remote include, but are not limited to, challenges in 
communication between the applicant's staff and OCC, and the lack of 
visibility that OCC may have into the applicant's operational 
stability that could be mitigated during an on-site visit or in-
person meeting. Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    OCC is also amending Rule 204(h) (which concerns conditional 
approval of an application) to explicitly state that approval of an 
application may be conditioned on Clearing Fund deposits and 
operational testing requirements. OCC's changes to Rule 204 also 
include an amendment to Rule 204(l), which states that any imposed 
conditions will remain in effect until OCC judges that the heightened 
risks presented by the member are sufficiently reduced. Other changes 
to the rules governing conditions to admission include (i) empowering 
OCC to deny an application for failure to respond to requests for 
information under proposed Rule 204(j); (ii) stating explicitly that 
OCC may share information provided by an applicant with designated 
examining authorities under proposed Rule 204(k); and (iii) requiring 
that an applicant provide to OCC its business plan, supported by 
financial projections and assumptions under proposed Rule 204(m). 
Additional changes to Rule 204 would authorize OCC to impose risk-based 
limitations, restrictions and contingencies on applicants under 
proposed Rule 204(n) \14\ and to request that an applicant provide the 
annual audited financial statements from its parent or affiliate under 
proposed Rule 204(p).

3. Ongoing Membership Standards
    In addition to the conditions and requirements for applicants, OCC 
proposes to amend the standards to which current Clearing Members must 
adhere. For example, OCC proposes amendments to Rule 301(b), which 
would permit OCC to establish discretionary, risk-based minimum capital 
levels higher than those specified in current Rule 301. The proposed 
change aims to strengthen financial responsibility obligations for 
existing Clearing Members.\15\ OCC's amendments to Rule 301(d) would 
require that every Clearing Member furnish copies of its contingency 
planning procedures upon OCC's request.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ Notice, 91 FR at 57194.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Likewise, OCC proposes substantive amendments and clarifying 
changes to Rules 302(b), 302(c), and 302(f). Consistent with the 
proposed changes to Rule 204(g) described above, proposed Rule 302(b) 
would require Clearing Members to maintain a physical office unless OCC 
deems the member's fully remote office model does not present a 
heightened risk to OCC. Moreover, Rule 302(b) would subject Clearing 
Members' physical offices to on-site visits from OCC with no more than 
24-hours' notice. Similarly, OCC proposes amending Rule 302(c) to 
clarify that a member must make its books and records available for 
inspection by OCC upon request.
    Next, OCC proposes to update Rule 303(a) to require Clearing 
Members to employ individuals with relevant industry experience in 
senior management roles or equivalent positions (e.g., President of 
Chief Executive Officer). In addition, OCC proposes amending Rule 305, 
which concerns Clearing Member Risk Management, to explicitly state 
that following periodic review(s) of a Clearing Member's risk 
management framework, any of OCC's actions or subsequent imposition of 
protective measures will be based on its review of a Clearing Member's 
written risk management policies, practices, and procedures.
    OCC proposes various changes to Rule 306A, which sets forth the 
standards for event-based reporting. Under the rule, Clearing Members 
must notify an officer of OCC prior to 3:00 p.m. Central Time on the 
next business day if certain, triggering events occur--OCC's proposed 
rule changes clarify under what circumstances a Clearing Member must 
report. To clarify Rule 306A's meaning, OCC proposes amending the 
entirety of the rule to reference Rule 101 definitions, where 
applicable. OCC also proposes changing Rule 306A(1)(a) clarify that 
OCC's reporting requirements do not supersede laws applicable to the 
member that might prevent disclosure of such notice to OCC; OCC adopted 
the same requirement for eligible bank Clearing Members in Rule 
306A(a)(5)(D). In addition, OCC proposes changing Rule 306A(a)(2)(A)(i) 
to require that a Clearing Member report to OCC when its net capital is 
less than the greater of 20% above the applicable member's minimum 
capital requirement. Rule 306(a)(4)(B)(i) sets forth the same 
requirement for non-U.S. securities firms.
    OCC's proposed change to Rule 306B(b) would require Clearing 
Members to provide OCC with a complete copy of the annual audited 
financial statements of the Clearing Member's parent or affiliate; if 
such statements are not available, the member must provide unaudited 
financial statements, or applicable financial information, upon OCC's 
request. In addition, OCC proposes adding Rule 306B(f), which would 
explicitly require Clearing Members to provide accurate, complete and 
timely responses to its annual and periodic due diligence information 
requests.
    Lastly, OCC proposes amending Rule 2212 to require all clearing 
members that are participants of the Stock Loan/Hedge program to 
register their existing Master Securities Loan Agreement relationship 
within OCC's clearing system.\16\ OCC's proposed changes to

[[Page 67365]]

Rule 2212 also include removing the modifying term ``Hedge'' from Rule 
2212's text and removing the phrase ``within the Stock Loan/Hedge 
Program'' from Rule 2212(a).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ OCC also proposes a technical update to Rule 302(f) to 
rescind the term ``Stock Loan/Hedge'' and replace it with the term 
``Market Loan,'' which accurately identifies the Market Loan Program 
described in the rule--such an update would clarify what members and 
applicants are subject to the aforementioned conditions to 
admission. This technical change comports with the OCC's proposed 
changes to Rule 2212, which concerns the procedures for closing out 
a Clearing Member's Match-Book Positions following suspension.

B. Admission and Disciplinary Proceedings

1. Membership Applications
    OCC proposes changes to its rules regarding the process provided 
both to applicants for membership as well as to members subject to 
disciplinary sanctions. OCC proposes adopting rules setting forth in 
expanded detail the processes and procedures for review of an 
application or reapplication by the Risk Committee or its delegates, 
which include: (i) the procedures to request a hearing following denial 
of an application or reapplication; (ii) procedure for submitting, 
precedent to a hearing, a concise statement setting for a member's 
objection to the denial; and (iii) the specific procedures for 
conducting a hearing before the risk committee, including, but not 
limited to, procedures for proffering documents and presenting 
testimony.\17\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ See generally OCC Rule 203(b)(6).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In addition, OCC proposes adopting a rule explicitly stating that 
any decision by the Risk Committee or its delegate to deny an 
application or reapplication must be written and noticed to the 
applicant--the proposed changes go on to state that a decision rendered 
by OCC is final upon the date an applicant receives notice of said 
decision. Lastly, OCC's proposed changes to Rule 203 include clarifying 
that any final decision to deny a reapplication pursuant to OCC Rule 
309 constitutes grounds for summary suspension under OCC Rule 1102.
    OCC's rules currently require a Clearing Member to notify OCC of 
material changes (e.g., merger, sale, name change).\18\ OCC proposes to 
amend its rules to empower OCC to require a Clearing Member to reapply 
for membership following notice of such a change. OCC proposes adopting 
Rule 309 in connection with Rule 306A(b)(1)--Rule 309 sets forth the 
processes and procedures for reapplying for membership. Rule 309(a) 
would authorize OCC to require a Clearing Member to reapply for 
membership if OCC determines, in its sole discretion, that the 
financial or operational condition of a Clearing Member has materially 
changed or materially changes the business strategy of the member in a 
manner that might adversely affect OCC. As proposed, Rule 309(b) would 
permit a Clearing Member to conditionally maintain its membership 
during the application period, notwithstanding any protective measures 
implemented pursuant to Rules 307 through 307C. Lastly, proposed Rule 
309(c) would require that the denial of Clearing Membership following 
reapplication is subject to the process provided under Rule 203, as 
described above. Other proposed changes include amending rules 
306A(a)(5)(C) and (D) to clarify that reporting obligations are 
triggered by the receipt of notice of any violation delineated in Rule 
306A, amending Rule 306A(b)(1) to require that members provide notice 
of any material changes in its form of organization or ownership 
structure, and updating Rule 306A(c) to reference the newly adopted 
term ``Key Person.''
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ See OCC Rule 306A(b). Proposed changes to OCC Rule 306A are 
described above in Section II.A.3.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Disciplinary Proceedings
    OCC also proposes changes to Chapter XII, including amendments and 
clarifying additions to Rule 1201, which concerns sanctions. OCC 
proposes including among the sanctions available under Rule 1201(a) 
fines. OCC also proposes amending Rule 1201 to explicitly state that a 
Clearing Member may be sanctioned for violating statutory rules of OCC 
(i.e., in addition to violating the By-Law and Rules). To clarify what 
conduct might constitute a sanction under the rule, OCC proposes adding 
Rules 1201(a)(1)-(5), which set forth examples of conduct that may 
warrant sanctions, including: (i) failure by a member to provide 
adequate personnel or facilities; (ii) failure by a member to comply 
with a protective measure proscribed under Rules 307 or 307C; (iii) 
exhibiting a pattern of tardily providing financial information; (iv) 
providing false financial information, or (iv) exhibiting a pattern of 
regulatory or legal infractions.
3. Suspension of a Clearing Member
    Further, OCC proposes to Chapter XI of its Rules modify the 
procedures for summary suspension of a Clearing Member. OCC proposes 
adding Rule 1101(b), which would require any non-U.S. Clearing Member 
subject to expulsion or suspension by a non-U.S. regulatory agency or 
securities exchange to immediately notify OCC of any such occurrence. 
OCC also proposes amending Rule 1102(b)(5) to include examples of when 
a member is in such financial or operating difficulty that OCC may 
exercise its discretion to suspend the member (e.g., the member loses 
access to sufficient financial resources or the ability to participate 
in default management activities). Under the proposed changes to 
Chapter XI, a Clearing Member could also be subject to suspension if it 
is in default in the payment of funds or other obligations in respect 
of Rule 707 X-M accounts or if it does not meet OCC's membership 
standards following reapplication under Rule 309. Lastly, OCC proposes 
an amendment clarifying that any event described in Rule 1102(a) 
constitutes a ``default'' and any default under any part of the rules 
must be noticed to OCC's Board if the suspension determination is made 
by any agent of OCC other than the Board itself.

C. Clearing Member Risk Criteria

    OCC proposes to expand Rule 307(a) to include in the universe of 
risks factors that may precipitate the imposition of protective 
measures ``market risk'' and ``regulatory or compliance risks.'' In 
addition, OCC proposes amending Rule 307 to state that new Clearing 
Members may be subject to enhanced monitoring and surveillance. OCC 
also proposes adding Rules 307(b) and (c), which empower OCC to impose 
protective measures on any Clearing Member or applicant at their 
discretion, on a case-by-case basis depending on factors which include, 
but are not limited to, the member or applicant's financial or 
operational risk profile.
    OCC proposes amending Rule 307B(b) to include new procedures 
relating the imposition of restrictions on certain transactions, 
positions and activities, which include: (i) requiring a Clearing 
Member to notice in writing its objections to any imposed restrictions 
and its intent to retain counsel; (ii) specifying OCC's notice of 
hearing obligations and stating that any hearing will proceed pursuant 
to Rule 203(b)(6); and (iii) setting forth the procedure and 
requirements by which OCC will provide a member a notice of its final 
decision.
    OCC also proposes amending Rule 307C, which delineates additional 
operational, personnel, financial, and risk management requirements, to 
empower OCC to require a financial guaranty from a Clearing Member's 
parent company or affiliate, to impose limitations on a member's 
financial leverage, to require a member to fill a key risk management 
or operational role created by the loss of a Key Person, or to require 
that members implement or enhance certain internal controls, procedures 
or systems.

A. Consistency With Sections 17A(b)(3)(F) and 17A(b)(4)(B) of the 
Exchange Act

    Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Exchange Act requires that the rules of 
a clearing agency are designed, in general, to protect investors and 
the public interest while not being designed to permit unfair 
discrimination in the admission of participants or among participants 
in the use of the clearing agency.\26\ Relatedly, Section 17A(b)(4)(B) 
of the Exchange Act provides that a clearing agency may deny 
participation to, or condition the participation of, any person if such 
person does not meet such standards of financial responsibility, 
operational capability, experience, and competence as are prescribed by 
the rules of the clearing agency.\27\ Section 17A(b)(4)(B) of the 
Exchange Act also provides that a registered clearing agency may 
examine and verify the qualifications of an applicant to be a 
participant in accordance with procedures established by the rules of 
the clearing agency.\28\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \26\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(3)(F).
    \27\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(4)(B).
    \28\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    As described in Section II.A.2, OCC proposes to authorize the Risk 
Committee to continue to approve or deny applications and to delegate 
such authority to streamline the process. The proposed change would 
continue to rely on the current risk-based decision-making approach to 
reviewing applications without requiring an applicant to wait for a 
quarterly meeting of the Risk Committee for final determination. 
Although the initial decision could be made by a delegee, an applicant 
would continue to have the right to challenge a denial before the Risk 
Committee, preserving Board-level oversight of the process.
    Further, OCC proposes changes to the substantive requirements both 
for applicants and members addressing various concerns. As described 
above, OCC proposes changes related to financial responsibility, 
including the change to OCC Rule 301(a) that would authorize OCC in to 
increase a Clearing Member's minimum capital requirement to mitigate 
various risks (e.g., an inadequate liquidity profile) \29\ and the 
proposed new section (p) of OCC Rule 204 that would authorize OCC to 
require an applicant to produce the financial statements of its parent 
or affiliate. Other proposed changes pertain to an applicant's or 
member's operations, such as compliance with the confidentiality 
requirements of proposed OCC Rule 207(d), the physical office 
requirements of proposed Rules 204(g) and 302(b), and the books and 
records requirements of proposed Rule 302. The proposal also includes 
changes related to experience and competence, such as the requirement 
for a viable business plan under proposed OCC Rule 204(m), an operating 
history of one year or for an applicant or Clearing Member to employ 
sufficiently experienced senior personnel in the relevant area of 
business activity under proposed OCC Rule 201(b), and responsibilities 
for employing risk management personnel under proposed Rule 303. OCC 
also proposes replacing a reference in the definition of the term 
``Office'' to Rule 201 with a reference to Rule 302 to comport with 
proposed rule changes to Chapter II Clearing Membership and Chapter III 
Membership Standards.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

could make the proposal stronger before approval'' but did not

insurmountable barriers for anyone trying to enter the market.\30\ 
Specifically, the commenter states that requiring an established 
operating history penalizes lean startups and that ``[m]andating 
traditional physical office facilities disregards modern, secure remote 
infrastructure, forcing early-stage teams to absorb unnecessary real 
estate expenses before generating revenue.'' \31\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    With regard to the year of operating history, OCC states that this 
proposed requirement would provide OCC with additional data or 
information (e.g., an applicant or Clearing Member's operating history 
and its senior management's employment history) to determine whether 
they meet OCC's membership standards.\32\ With regard to the

    Based on the information provided, the proposed requirements around 
operations, experience, and competence are consistent with the Exchange 
Act. The Exchange Act does not permit unfair discrimination, but it 
does acknowledge the potential need to deny participation based on 
specific criteria. The commenter's concern regarding a one-year 
operating history ignores the alternative that OCC offers in its 
proposed rules (e.g., employing sufficiently experienced personnel). 
Similarly, the proposed rules allow for a scenario in which an 
applicant or member may operate in a fully remote business model but 
allow OCC to assess the potential risks of such an operating model. OCC 
is permitted to adopt rules that limit participation to the extent such 
rules are consistent with the requirements of the Exchange Act, such as 
not discriminating unfairly and not imposing a burden on competition 
that is not unnecessary or inappropriate.
    Accordingly, the Proposed Rule Change is consistent with the 
requirements of Sections 17A(b)(3)(F) and 17A(b)(4)(B) of the Exchange 
Act.\34\

B. Consistency With Sections 17A(b)(3)(H) and 17A(b)(5)(B) of the 
Exchange Act

    Section 17A(b)(3)(H) of the Exchange Act requires that the rules of 
a clearing agency are in accordance with the

[[Page 67367]]

provisions of Section 17A(b)(5) of the Exchange Act, and, in general, 
provide a fair procedure with respect to the disciplining of 
participants, the denial of participation to any person seeking 
participation therein, and the prohibition or limitation by the 
clearing agency of any person with respect to access to services 
offered by the clearing agency.\35\ Section 17A(b)(5)(B) of the 
Exchange Act provides for the requirements related to any proceeding by 
a clearing agency to determine whether a person shall be denied 
participation or prohibited or limited with respect to access to 
services offered by the clearing agency.\36\

    As described in Section II.B.2, OCC proposes changes to the 
procedures around the denial of participation whether through the 
review of a membership application or disciplinary proceedings. 
Regarding the review of applications, the proposed changes explicitly 
provide procedures for the request a hearing following denial of an 
application as well as how such a hearing would proceed. For example, 
the proposed rules require that such a hearing proceed before the Risk 
Committee. OCC's proposed changes also include amending Rules 203 and 
309 (i.e., OCC's admissions procedures), to cover and encompass 
reapplications for Clearing Membership. In addition, the proposed 
changes to Rule 203 include amendments that specify in detail the 
processes and procedures for requesting a hearing contesting the denial 
of a Clearing Membership application or reapplication. The proposed 
amendments to the admission procedures serve to provide applicants and 
reapplicants an opportunity to be heard upon and explicitly provides 
for OCC to keep a record of any such hearing. After a hearing, the 
proposed rules would require that OCC produce a written statement 
setting forth specific grounds underlying the risk committee's 
disapproval decision. More specifically, proposed Rule 203(b)(8) 
mirrors proposed Rule 1201(b), which provides the same with respect to 
a suspension or expulsion following disciplinary proceedings undertaken 
pursuant to Chapter XII.
    Likewise, Rule 309(c), as described in Section II.B.1., is 
consistent with Section 17A(b)(5)(B) of the Exchange Act. Proposed Rule 
309(c) provides that any decision to deny Clearing Membership following 
reapplication will be made pursuant to Rule 203. The admission 
procedures described in Rule 203 already provide for the requirements 
outlined in Section 17A(b)(5)(B) of the Exchange Act, specifically in 
proposed Rules 203(b)(3) and (4). OCC's proposed changes to Rule 1201, 
which governs sanctions and is described above in Section II.B.2., 
describes, defines and explicitly delineates the universe of sanctions 
that OCC may impose on a Clearing Member.
    Accordingly, the Proposed Rule Change is consistent with the 
requirements of Sections 17A(b)(3)(H) and 17A(b)(5)(B) of the Exchange 
Act.\37\

C. Consistency With Section 17A(b)(5)(C) Under the Exchange Act

    Section 17A(b)(5)(C) of the Exchange Act authorizes a registered 
clearing agency to summarily suspend a participant who: (i) has been 
and is expelled or suspended from any SRO; (ii) is in default of any 
delivery of funds or securities to the clearing agency; or (iii) is in 
such financial or operating difficulty that the clearing agency 
determines that suspension of the participant is necessary for the 
protection of the clearing agency, its participants, creditors, or 
investors.\38\ Section 17A(b)(5)(C) also provides that a summarily 
suspended participant shall be promptly afforded an opportunity for a 
hearing by the clearing agency in accordance with 17A(b)(5)(A) of the 
Exchange Act.\39\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \38\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(5)(C).
    \39\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(5)(C).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    As described above, OCC proposes to reorganize and amend its rules 
pertaining to summary suspension of a Clearing Member. Specifically, 
the changes to Rule 1102(a) clarify upon what grounds OCC may summarily 
suspend a Clearing Member, by adding a nonexclusive list of scenarios 
involving financial or operating difficulty that may serve as grounds 
for suspension. The proposed changes would further clarify the current 
grounds for suspension related to OCC Rule 707 (i.e., failure to meet 
an obligation in respect to cross-margin accounts). The proposed 
changes would also add a potential ground for suspension and proposed 
Rule 309 (i.e., for Clearing Member who do not meet OCC's membership 
standards following reapplication). Further, the proposed changes would 
clarify that a summary suspension constitutes a default, following 
which OCC would cease to act for the suspended Clearing Member.
    Accordingly, the proposed changes to Chapter XI of OCC's Rules are 
consistent with the requirements of Section 17A(b)(5)(C) of the 
Exchange Act.\40\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \40\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(5)(C).

D. Consistency With Rule 17ad-22(e)(18) Under the Exchange Act

    Rule 17ad-22(e)(18) under the Exchange Act requires a clearing 
agency, in part, to ``[e]stablish objective, risk-based, and publicly 
disclosed criteria for participation'' that ``require participants to 
have sufficient financial resources and robust operational capacity to 
meet obligations arising from participation in the clearing agency'' 
and ``monitor compliance with such participation requirements on an 
ongoing basis.'' \41\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \41\ 17 CFR 240.17ad-22(e)(18).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    As discussed above, Section 17A(b)(4)(B) of the Exchange Act, the 
Proposed Rule Change would expand OCC's existing financial and 
operational membership standards to address specific risks such as 
allowing OCC to set higher capital requirements where a member has an 
inadequate liquidity profile. The proposed changes to Rules 204(g) and 
302(b) allowing for on-site visits by OCC would facilitate OCC's 
ability to monitor for compliance with its participation requirements. 
Similarly, the proposed changes to Rules 306B, 307, 307B and 307C 
provide for additional notification and reporting requirements that 
support OCC's monitoring of its members as well as protective measures 
OCC may impose. Protective measures such as restricting transactions 
that pose additional risk or increasing operation, personnel, financial 
resource and risk management requirements would support OCC's ability 
to set membership requirements aimed to ensure that participants to 
have sufficient financial resources and robust operational capacity to 
meet obligations arising out of clearing transactions with OCC.
    Accordingly, the Proposed Rule Change is consistent with the 
requirements of Rule 17ad-22(e)(18).\42\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \42\ 17 CFR 240.17ad-22(e)(18).

Quadratic obstructions to Small-Time Local Controllability for the multi-input bilinear Schrödinger equation
Théo Gherdaoui

Abstract
We investigate the small-time local controllability (STLC) near the ground state of a bilinear Schrödinger equation when the linearized system is not controllable. It is well known that, for single-input systems, quadratic terms in the state expansion can then lead to obstructions to the STLC of the nonlinear system. In this work, we extend this phenomenon to the multi-input setting, presenting the first example of multi-input quadratic obstructions for PDEs. Our results build upon our previous study of such obstructions for ODEs and provide a functional framework for analyzing them in the bilinear Schrödinger equation. Keywords: Bilinear Schrödinger equation, quadratic obstructions, small-time local exact controllability, power series expansion.

Introduction

1.1
Model and problem

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. In this article, we study the following Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}i\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,x)-u(t)\cdot\mu(x)\psi(t,x),&t\in(0,T),x\in(0,1),\\
\psi(t,0)=\psi(t,1)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mu:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
T>0
,
u:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
\psi:(0,T)\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
,
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=1
, and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\mapsto x\cdot y\in\mathbb{R}
is the canonical scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. We will use the notations
u:=\left(u^{1},\cdots,u^{r}\right)
and
\mu:=\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
in all the document.
When well defined, the solution is denoted
\psi
. When required, we will write
\psi(\cdot;u,\psi_{0})
to refer to this solution to emphasize its dependence on the different parameters.

This equation describes the evolution of the wave function
\psi
of a quantum particle in a
1
D infinite square potential well
(0,1)
, subjected to
r
electric fields with amplitudes
u^{\ell}(t)
.
The functions
\mu_{\ell}
, called « dipolar moments », model the interaction between the particle’s wave function
\psi
and the electric fields
u^{\ell}(t)
.

This is a multi-input nonlinear control system:

-

the state is the wave function
\psi:(0,T)\to\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the
L^{2}(0,1)
sphere,

-

the controls are
u^{\ell}:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, they act bilinearly on the state.

The fundamental state or ground state is the particular free – with
u\equiv 0
– trajectory
(t,x)\mapsto\sqrt{2}\sin(\pi x)e^{-i\pi^{2}t}
. We are interested in the small-time local controllability around the ground state.

1.2
Notations and definitions

Let
T>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We consider the Sobolev space
H^{k}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
, equipped with its usual norm and
H_{0}^{k}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
the adherence of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
for this norm.

Except explicit precision, we will work with complex valued space-dependent functions. We consider the space
L^{2}(0,1)
, endowed with the hermitian scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
We define the operator

A:=-\partial^{2}_{x}\text{ with domain }D(A):=H^{2}(0,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,1).

(1.2)

The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
A
are

\lambda_{j}:=(j\pi)^{2},\qquad\varphi_{j}:=\sqrt{2}\sin(j\pi\cdot),\qquad j\geq 1.

(1.3)

The family
(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(0,1)
. We denote by

\psi_{j}(t,x):=\varphi_{j}(x)e^{-i\lambda_{j}t},\qquad t\in(0,T),x\in(0,1),\qquad j\geq 1,

the solutions to the free –
i.e.
with controls
u\equiv 0
– Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with initial data
\varphi_{j}
at time
t=0

i.e.

\psi(\cdot;u,\varphi_{j})
. The solution
\psi_{1}
is called the fundamental state, or ground state. We finally define the spaces
H^{s}_{(0)}(0,1):=D(A^{\frac{s}{2}})
for
s\geq 0
, equipped with the norm

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{H^{s}_{(0)}}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}|j^{s}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

With these notations, the equation (
1.1
) is well-posed, in the sense of the following proposition, proved for the single-input case in
[
6
, Proposition
2
]
and which can be adapted to our case.

Proposition 1.1

(Well-posedness)
.

Let
T>0
,
\mu\in H^{3}((0,1),\mathbb{R})^{r}
,
u\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)
. There exists a unique mild solution
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}\left([0,T],H_{(0)}^{3}(0,1)\right)
to the equation (
1.1
).

We can now define the notion of small-time local controllability we are working on.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
E_{T}
be a family of normed vector spaces of functions defined on
[0,T]
for
T>0
. The bilinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is
E
-STLC in
H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)
around the ground state if for every
T,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
\psi_{f}\in H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)\cap\mathcal{S}
with
\|\psi_{f}-\psi_{1}(T)\|_{H^{3}}<\delta
,
there exists
u\in\left(E_{T}\right)^{r}\cap L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}
such that
\left\|u\right\|_{E_{T}}\leq\varepsilon
and
\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})=\psi_{f}
.

Under appropriate assumptions on the dipolar moments
\mu_{\ell}
, the STLC around the ground state of (
1.1
) can be proved by applying the linear test.
This is the purpose of the following statement, that can be proved by adapting with several controls the strategy developed in
[
6
,
16
]
with one control.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mu\in H^{3}((0,1),\mathbb{R})^{r}
be such that

\exists C>0,\ \forall j\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{r}|\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\rangle|\geq\frac{C}{j^{3}}.

(1.4)

Then, the bilinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is
L^{2}
-STLC in

H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)
around the ground state.

Idea of proof for Proposition
1.3
.

Assumption (
1.4
) guarantees that the linearized system around the ground state – see (
2.2
) – is small-time globally controllable with states in

H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)\cap\mathcal{S}
and controls in
L^{2}
.
Moreover, one can prove that the following end-point map is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}

\Theta_{T}:u\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}\mapsto\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})\in H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)\cap\mathcal{S}.

By applying the inverse mapping theorem, we obtain the
L^{2}
-STLC of the nonlinear system (
1.1
).
∎

1.3
Main result

For all the document,
k,K,r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
are fixed integers. To ensure that equation (
1.1
) is well-posed in
H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)
– see Proposition
1.1
– we assume that

\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}:\quad\mu\in H^{3}((0,1),\mathbb{R})^{r}.

In this article, we study the case where the linearized system around the ground state – see (
2.2
) – is not controllable,
so we consider an integer
K\geq 1
and we assume

\mathbf{(H)_{lin}}:\quad\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{K}\rangle=0,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq r.

In this case, the linearized system misses one direction
\langle\psi(t;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
,

i.e.
the first order term in the Taylor expansion at
0
of the map
u\mapsto\langle\psi(t;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}\rangle
vanishes.
To prove an obstruction to STLC, we will use a « power series expansion » of order
2
,
i.e.
the second order term in the Taylor expansion at
0
of
u\mapsto\langle\psi(t;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}\rangle
. We make assumptions about the quadratic expansion of the solution. To state them, we need to introduce the following definitions.

Definition 1.4
.

For all
1\leq\ell,L\leq r
, we define the following sequences

c^{\ell,L}:=\left(c^{\ell,L}_{j}\right)_{j\geq 1}:=\left(\left\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{K},\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\mu_{L}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\right)_{j\geq 1}.

We will denote
c^{\ell}:=c^{\ell,\ell}
and
c_{j}^{\ell}:=c_{j}^{\ell,\ell}
for
j\geq 1
and
1\leq\ell\leq r
.

Quadratic hypotheses are formulated as properties on series. To ensure convergence, we assume the following

\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}:\quad\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left|c_{j}^{\ell,L}\right|j^{4k}<+\infty,\quad 1\leq\ell,L\leq r.

Remark 1.5
.

By Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
holds when
\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{K}
and
\mu_{L}\varphi_{1}
belong to
H^{2k}_{(0)}(0,1)
, for every
1\leq\ell,L\leq r
. In particular, this is the case when
\mu\in H^{2k}(0,1)^{r}
with vanishing odd derivatives, or
\mu\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)^{r}
.

Definition 1.6

(Quadratic brackets)
.

Assuming
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
, we define the following series which converge,

\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}:=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left((\lambda_{K}-\lambda_{j})^{\lfloor\frac{p+1}{2}\rfloor}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}c_{j}^{\ell,L}-(\lambda_{K}-\lambda_{j})^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1})^{\lfloor\frac{p+1}{2}\rfloor}c_{j}^{L,\ell}\right),

for
0\leq p\leq 2k-1
and
1\leq\ell\leq L\leq r
. When
\ell=L
, we will note
\gamma_{p}^{\ell}
instead of
\gamma_{p}^{\ell,\ell}
.

Remark 1.7
.

For every
1\leq\ell\leq r
,
0\leq p\leq k-1
, one has
\gamma^{\ell}_{2p}=0

We are now able to state the hypotheses concerning the quadratic terms

\mathbf{(H)_{null}}:\quad\forall 1\leq\ell\leq L\leq r,\ \forall 0\leq p\leq 2k-2,\quad\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}=0,

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}:\quad q:(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\mapsto\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\gamma_{2k-1}^{\ell}\frac{a_{\ell}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{1\leq\ell<L\leq r}\hskip-5.69046pt\gamma_{2k-1}^{\ell,L}a_{\ell}a_{L}\in\mathbb{R}\\
\text{ is a definite quadratic form on }\mathbb{R}^{r}.\end{gathered}

Remark 1.8
.

If
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
holds,
\text{sgn}(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1})q
is a positive-definite quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

Remark 1.9
.

When
r=2
,
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
holds iff
\left(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1,2}\right)^{2}<\gamma_{2k-1}^{1}\gamma_{2k-1}^{2}
.

At first glance, these assumptions may seem mysterious and technical, but they can be interpreted in terms of Lie brackets as detailed in Section
1.5
. The main result of this article is the following theorem

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
k,K,r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\mu
satisfying
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{lin}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{null}}
and
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
.
Then, if
k\geq 2
(resp. if
k=1
), the multi-input bilinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is not
H^{2k-3}
-STLC (resp.
W^{-1,\infty}
-STLC) in
H^{3}_{(0)}(0,1)
around the ground state.

Ideas for proving the existence of such functions
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
are given in Appendix
A.1
.

Remark 1.11
.

When
r=1
, hypothesis
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
is equivalent to
\gamma_{2k-1}^{1}\neq 0
. We then obtain
[
17
, Theorem
1.3
]
.

1.4
Heuristic

To simplify the notations, we will use the following definition.

Definition 1.12
.

For
T>0
and
f\in L^{1}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
, one defines the iterated primitives
f_{n}
vanishing at

f_{0}:=f\quad\text{and}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\ t\in[0,T],\quad f_{n+1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f_{n}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(1.5)

When

The essence of Theorem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}x_{1}^{\prime}&=&u^{1}\\

x_{4}^{\prime}&=&\left(x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{1}x_{3}+2x_{3}^{2}\right)-2u^{2}x_{3}-x_{2}^{2}-u^{2}x_{1}^{2}\end{array}\right..

Let

\displaystyle x_{4}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left(u_{1}^{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{1}^{1}u^{2}_{1}+2\left(u_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}\right)-u_{1}^{2}(T)^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u_{1}^{1}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}u^{2}\left(u_{1}^{1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{T^{2}}{2}-\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\right)\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}-u^{2}_{1}(T)^{2}

for any

. As

, all final state in

is not reachable from

. The system (
1.6
) is not
L^{\infty}
-STLC around
0
- see Definition

.

To prove Theorem
1.10
, we use a « power series expansion » of order

. Heuristically, it is as if the following terms are the dominant part of the quadratic expansion of the solution in the direction

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\sum_{p=1}^{k}i^{2p-1}\gamma_{2p-1}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{u_{p}^{\ell}(t)^{2}}{2}\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{1\leq\ell<L\leq r}\sum_{p=0}^{2k-1}i^{p}\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}\int_{0}^{T}u^{\ell}_{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor+1}(t)u^{L}_{\lfloor\frac{p+1}{2}\rfloor}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

The cancellation assumption

reduces this sum to the simpler expression

i(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\gamma_{2k-1}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{u_{k}^{\ell}(t)^{2}}{2}\mathrm{d}t+i(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq\ell<L\leq r}\gamma_{2k-1}^{\ell,L}\int_{0}^{T}u_{k}^{\ell}(t)u_{k}^{L}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

and
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
allows use to take advantage of a signed term because (

\mathbf{(H)_{lin}}
lead to

(-1)^{k+1}\text{sgn}(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1})\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle\simeq\text{sgn}(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1})\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

1.6
State of the art

1.6.1
Topological obstructions to exact controllability

In
[
1
]
, Ball, Marsden and Slemrod proved obstructions to local exact controllability of linear PDEs with bilinear controls.
For instance, if the multiplicative operators
\mu_{\ell}
are bounded on
H^{s}_{(0)}(0,1)
,
then system (
1.1
) is not exactly controllable in
\mathcal{S}\cap H^{s}_{(0)}(0,1)
, with controls

u\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})^{r}
and
p>1
.
The fundamental reason behind is that, under these assumptions, the reachable set has empty interior in
H^{s}_{(0)}(0,1)
.
The case of
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}
-controls (
p=1
) was incorporated in
[
19
]
and extensions to nonlinear equations were proved in
[
23
,
24
]
.
Turicini adapted this statement to Schrödinger equations in
[
44
]
. After the important work
[
1
]
, different notions of controllability were studied for the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation such as

-

exact controllability
in more regular spaces, on which the
\mu_{\ell}
do not define bounded operators – see
[
6
,
18
,
35
]
,

-

approximate controllability
.

1.6.2
Exact controllability in more regular spaces, by linear test

For the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation, local exact controllability was first proved in
[
2
,
3
]
with Nash-Moser techniques,
to deal with an apparent derivative loss problem and then in
[
6
]
with a classical inverse mapping theorem,
thanks to a regularizing effect. By grafting other ingredients onto this core strategy, global (resp. local) exact controllability in regular spaces was proved for different models
in
[
39
,
41
]
(resp.
[
16
]
). This strategy has also been used to obtain local controllability results for nonlinear Schrödinger equations, as in
[
30
]
, or for coupled Schrödinger equations, as in
[
38
]
.

1.6.3
Power series expansion of order 2 or 3

First, let us look at the case of
single-input systems
. A power series expansion of order
2
allows to recover a lost direction
in large time
: this strategy is used in
[
12
]
for the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation. This method is also used for other equations, such as KdV, in
[
20
]
. If the order
2
vanishes, a power series of order
3
can be used to recover the
small-time
local controllability, for example in
[
27
]
, for KdV. If the order
2
doesn’t cancel out, but the term of order
3
is strong enough, this expansion can also give the
small-time
local controllability – see
[
18
]
, for the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation.

In the context of
multi-input systems
, we use a power series expansion of order
2
in
[
35
]
to recover in
small-time
a direction that is lost at the linear order. The purpose of this article is to give other algebraic assumptions to prove obstructions for STLC.

1.6.4

In
[

, Coron denied the

-small-time local controllability result for the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation, with a particular dipolar moment

, Beauchard, Marbach and Perrin proved an obtruction for the single-input bilinear Schrödinger equation with Neumann boundary conditions, using a power series of order

for ODEs, in
[

[

With the exception of a non-physical PDE designed for,

In

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{T}f_{n}(t)\left(\int_{0}^{t}g_{n}(\tau)\partial_{1}^{n}\partial_{2}^{n}H(t,\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\mathrm{d}t=f_{n+1}(T)\int_{0}^{T}g_{n}(\tau)\partial_{1}^{n}\partial_{2}^{n}H(T,\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\\

2.4

The equations (

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that the hypotheses
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{null}}

\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}

\mathbb{R}

\left(\left(\langle\mu_{\ell}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{j}\rangle(-i\omega_{j})^{p}\right)_{j\geq 1}\right)_{(p,\ell)\in\llbracket 0,k-1\rrbracket\times\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket}

We use a proof technique similar to Proposition

+\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{p\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket}\sum_{\ell\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket}|u_{p}^{\ell}(T)|^{2}+T\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}+\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{3}\right).\\
\end{gathered}

We use Proposition
2.10
to estimate the boundary terms. We obtain, as
\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\to 0
,

\begin{gathered}\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(T\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{3}+\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{gathered}

(2.21)

Let
T^{*}:=\frac{\pi}{3\omega_{K}}
if
K\neq 1
,
T^{*}=+\infty
else. Let
T\in(0,T^{*})
. By
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
and Remark
1.8
, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that
\text{sgn}(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1})q(a)\geq 4C_{2}\left\|a\right\|^{2}
, for every
a\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Thus,

\text{sgn}(\gamma_{2k-1}^{1})\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\geqslant 2C_{2}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}.

(2.22)

First obstruction –

k=1
: using (
2.21
), we immediately obtain

\begin{gathered}\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{1}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\\
+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(T+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\right)\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{gathered}

Then, there exist
C_{1},T_{1}>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,T_{1})
, there exists
\eta_{1}>0
such that, for all
u\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}
satisfying
\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}<\eta_{1}
,

\begin{gathered}\left|\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{1}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
\leq C_{1}\left(T+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\right)\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+C_{1}\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.\end{gathered}

We fix
T_{f}:=\min\left(T_{1},T^{*},\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}}\right)
and
\eta:=\min(\eta_{1},\frac{C_{2}}{2C_{1}})
. This equation together with (
2.22
) conclude the proof.

Other obstructions –

k\geq 2
:
using the interpolation inequality proved in Lemma
2.11
in (
2.21
), one gets, as
\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\to 0
,

\begin{gathered}\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\\
+\mathcal{O}\left((T+(1+T^{-2k+3})\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2k-3}(0,T)})\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{gathered}

Then, there exist
C_{1},T_{1}>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,T_{1})
, there exists
\eta_{1}>0
such that, for all
u\in H^{2k-3}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}
satisfying
\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}<\eta_{1}
,

\begin{gathered}\left|\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle-(-1)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
\leq C_{1}\left(\left(T+(1+T^{-2k+3}\right)\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2k-3}(0,T)})\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right).\end{gathered}

(2.23)

Let
T_{f}:=\min\left(T_{1},T^{*},\frac{C_{2}}{3C_{1}}\right)
. For all
T\in(0,T_{f})
, we define
\eta:=\min(\eta_{1},\frac{C_{2}}{3C_{1}},\frac{C_{2}}{3C_{1}}T^{2k-3})
. Then, for all
u\in H^{2k-3}((0,T),\mathbb{R})^{r}
with
\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2k-3}(0,T)}\leq\eta
, the estimate (
2.23
) leads to

\begin{gathered}\left|\Im\left\langle\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{K}e^{-i\lambda_{1}T}\right\rangle-(-1)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{T}q(u_{k}(t))\cos(\omega_{K}(t-T))\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
\leq C_{2}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+C_{1}\left\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{1})-\psi_{1}(T)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.\end{gathered}

This equation together with (
2.22
) lead to the desired result.
∎

A.1
Existence of
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
verifying the hypotheses

Theorem A.1
.

Let
k,K,r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. There exist
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
satisfying
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{lin}}
,
\mathbf{(H)_{null}}
and
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
.

We use arguments very similar to those developed in
[

,
17
,
35
]
. That is why we will only give a proof skeleton for

.

Ideas of proof.

We prove more precisely the existence of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
such that the previous assumptions are satisfied. If
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
,
\mathbf{(H)_{reg}}
is already verified. This is also the case for
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
by Remark
1.5
.
In
[
35
]
, using a trick that divides function supports into two parts, we have explained how to guarantee the existence of functions
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
so that the following equalities hold

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{K}\rangle=\langle\mu_{2}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{K}\rangle=0,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq p\leq 2k-2,\ \forall 1\leq\ell\leq L\leq 2,\quad\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}=0,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\gamma^{1,2}_{2k-1}=0

(A.3)

As a consequence,
\mathbf{(H)_{lin}}
and
\mathbf{(H)_{null}}
are satisfied. Moreover using Remark
1.9
, as
\gamma^{1,2}_{2k-1}=0
, the assumption
\mathbf{(H)_{pos}}
becomes

\gamma_{2k-1}^{1}\gamma_{2k-1}^{2}>0.

Then, we want to guarantee that (
A.1
), (
A.2
), (
A.3
),
\gamma_{2k-1}^{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2k-1}^{2}>0
can be satisfied simultaneously to conclude the proof. This is what Bournissou did in
[
17
, Theorem A.
4
]
. By adapting her method to our setting, we obtain the claimed result.
∎

A.2
Some series expansions

We recall that
\omega_{j}
and
\nu_{j}
are defined in (
2.1
).

Definition A.2
.

Let
a:=(a_{j})_{j\geq 1},b:=(b_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be sequences of real numbers and
l\in\mathbb{N}
s.t.
\left(a_{j}j^{2l}\right)_{j\geq 1}
,
\left(b_{j}j^{2l}\right)_{j\geq 1}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{*})
. We define

\gamma_{l}(a,b):=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\nu_{j}^{\lfloor\frac{l+1}{2}\rfloor}-b_{j}\omega_{j}^{\lfloor\frac{l+1}{2}\rfloor}\nu_{j}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\right).

Remark A.3
.

We recall that
\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}
is specified in Definition
1.6
. If
\mathbf{(H)_{conv}}
holds,

\forall 0\leq p\leq 2k-1,\ 1\leq\ell\leq L\leq r,\qquad\gamma_{p}^{\ell,L}=\gamma_{p}\left(c^{\ell,L},c^{L,\ell}\right).

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. There exist coefficients
\left(\beta_{l}^{\nu}\right)_{l\in\llbracket 0,\nu\rrbracket}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu+1}
such that, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
a:=(a_{j})_{j\geq 1},b:=(b_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
verifying
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(|a_{j}|+|b_{j}|\right)j^{2(2p+\nu)}<+\infty
,

\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}^{p+\nu}\nu_{j}^{p}-b_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p+\nu}\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{\nu}\beta_{l}^{\nu}(-1)^{l}\gamma_{2p+l}\left(a,b\right)\omega_{K}^{\nu-l}.

Proof.

We prove this statement by induction on
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.
Initialisation
. For

\nu=0
, the desired equality is true by definition with
\beta_{0}^{0}=1
. For
\nu=1
, one can notice that
\omega_{K}-\nu_{j}=\omega_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}^{p+1}\nu_{j}^{p}-b_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}a_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p}(\omega_{K}-\nu_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}b_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p}(\omega_{K}-\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\omega_{K}\gamma_{2p}(a,b)-\gamma_{2p+1}(a,b).

We obtain the result with
\beta_{0}^{1}=\beta_{1}^{1}=1
.
Induction step:
assume that the result holds for
\nu
and
\nu+1
. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
(a_{j})_{j\geq 1},(b_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be such that
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(|a_{j}|+|b_{j}|\right)j^{2(2p+\nu+2)}<+\infty
. Using the same strategy,

\begin{gathered}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}^{p+\nu+2}\nu_{j}^{p}-b_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p+\nu+2}\right)=\omega_{K}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}^{p+\nu+1}\nu_{j}^{p}-b_{j}\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p+\nu+1}\right)\\
-\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}\nu_{j}\right)\omega_{j}^{p+\nu}\nu_{j}^{p}-\left(b_{j}\omega_{j}\nu_{j}\right)\omega_{j}^{p}\nu_{j}^{p+\nu}\right).\end{gathered}

We use the equality
\gamma_{2p+l}\left(\left(a_{j}\omega_{j}\nu_{j}\right)_{j\geq 1},\left(b_{j}\omega_{j}\nu_{j}\right)_{j\geq 1}\right)=\gamma_{2(p+1)+l}(a,b)
and the induction hypothesis to obtain the result, with
\beta_{l}^{\nu+2}=\beta_{l}^{\nu+1}-\beta_{l-2}^{\nu}
.
∎

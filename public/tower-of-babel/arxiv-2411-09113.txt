Convergence rates of Landweber-type methods for inverse problems in Banach spaces
Qinian Jin

Abstract
. Landweber-type methods are prominent for solving ill-posed inverse problems in Banach spaces and their convergence has been well-understood. However, how to derive their convergence rates remains a challenging open question. In this paper, we tackle the challenge of deriving convergence rates for Landweber-type methods applied to ill-posed inverse problems, where forward operators map from a Banach space to a Hilbert space. Under a benchmark source condition, we introduce a novel strategy to derive convergence rates when the method is terminated by either an a priori stopping rule or the discrepancy principle. Our results offer substantial flexibility regarding step sizes, by allowing the use of variable step sizes. By extending the strategy to deal with the stochastic mirror descent method for solving nonlinear ill-posed systems with exact data, under a benchmark source condition we also obtain an almost sure convergence rate in terms of the number of iterations.

1.
Introduction

Due to their simplicity of implementation and low computational complexity per iteration, Landweber-type
methods are well-recognized for solving ill-posed inverse problems in Banach spaces. Although
their convergence has been well-understood, deriving their convergence rates remains a challenging
open question. In this paper, we will consider Landweber-type methods for solving ill-posed inverse
problems, where the forward operators map from a Banach space to a Hilbert space, and develop novel
strategies to tackle the challenge of deriving convergence rates under a benchmark source condition
on sought solutions.

We consider ill-posed inverse problems governed by the operator equation

(1)

\displaystyle F(x)=y,

where
F:\mbox{dom}(F)\subset X\to Y
is a Fréchet differentiable operator mapping from a Banach
spaces
X
to a Hilbert space
Y
, with domain
\mbox{dom}(F)
. The Fréchet derivative of
F
at

x\in\mbox{dom}(F)
is denoted by
F^{\prime}(x)
. For numerous examples of inverse problems that take the form
of equation (
1
), such as integral equations of the first kind, tomography problems, and
parameter estimation in partial differential equations, see
[
9
,
11
,
20
,
28
]
.

We assume (
1
) has a solution, i.e.

y\in\mbox{Ran}(F)
, the range of
F
. In practical applications, prior feature information about
the sought solution is often available, and it is important to incorporate this information into the
reconstruction process. Let
{\mathcal{R}}:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous,
strong convex function that takes into account the available feature information.
To reconstruct a solution of (
1
) that aligns with the features described by
{\mathcal{R}}
, we start
with an initial guess
x_{0}\in X
with
\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\neq\emptyset
and take
\xi_{0}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
, where

\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
denotes the subdifferential of
{\mathcal{R}}
at
x_{0}
. We want to determine a solution
x^{\dagger}
of
(
1
) such that

(2)

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})=\min\left\{D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x,x_{0}):F(x)=y\right\},

where
D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x,x_{0})
denotes the Bregman distance induced by
{\mathcal{R}}
at
x_{0}
in the direction

\xi_{0}
; for the definition of subdifferential, Bregman distance and other relevant concepts from
convex analysis, see
[
31
]
. A brief review of these concepts is provided in Section
2
.
We are mainly interested in the situation where the problem (
2
) is ill-posed in the sense
that its solution does not depend continuously on the data.

In applications, data acquired through experiments inevitably contains noise. Assume, instead of the exact
data
y
, we only have a noisy data
y^{\delta}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta

with a small noise level
\delta>0
. Directly substituting
y
with
y^{\delta}
in the minimization problem
(
2
) may render the problem ill-defined, even if well-defined, the solution obtained from

y^{\delta}
may not depend continuously on the data. To effectively utilize noisy data
y^{\delta}
to approximate
the solution of (
2
), regularization techniques must be employed to mitigate this instability.

Although many regularization methods have been developed for solving (
2
) with noisy data, in this paper we will focus on the following Landweber-type method

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi_{k+1}^{\delta}&=\xi_{k}^{\delta}-\gamma_{k}^{\delta}F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}),\\
x_{k+1}^{\delta}&=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{{\mathcal{R}}(x)-\langle\xi_{k+1}^{\delta},x\rangle\right\},\end{split}

where
x_{0}^{\delta}:=x_{0}
and
\xi_{0}^{\delta}:=\xi_{0}
. This method builds on various efforts to extend the classical
Landweber iteration (
[
5
,
16
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
26
,
29
]
) and can be interpreted
as a mirror descent method (
[
18
,
21
]
). Each iteration involves two steps: the first performs
a gradient step in the dual space
X^{*}
of
X
, followed by the second step, which uses the mirror map
defined by
{\mathcal{R}}
to pull the element back to the original primal space
X
. Under the tangential cone condition

\|F(\bar{x})-F(x)-F^{\prime}(x)(\bar{x}-x)\|\leq\eta\|F(\bar{x})-F(x)\|

around a sought solution with
\eta\in[0,1)
, the convergence has been proved when the iteration is terminated by
a priori
or
a posteriori
stopping rules, see
[
13
,
16
,
19
,
26
]
for instance.

To assess how fast the iteration converges towards the desired solution, understanding the convergence
rates is crucial and has significant theoretical interest. For ill-posed inverse problems, deriving such
rates typically relies on assuming appropriate source conditions are satisfied by the sought solution.
In Hilbert spaces, the linear Landweber iteration is well-studied, where it is known to be an
order-optimal regularization method (
[
9
]
). For the more general method (
3
)
in Hilbert spaces with a nonlinear forward operator
F
and a regularization functional

{\mathcal{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, convergence rate analyses have been undertaken in
[
13
,
22
,
23
]
.
Specifically, in the seminal work by
[
13
]
, the nonlinear Landweber iteration

(4)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}^{\delta}=x_{k}^{\delta}-\gamma F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})

in Hilbert spaces with a constant step size has been considered. Under the range invariance condition

(5)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=Q_{x}F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|I-Q_{x}\|\leq\kappa_{0}\|x-x^{\dagger}\|

in a neighborhood of the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
, it has been shown that, if the method is terminated
by the discrepancy principle

(6)

\displaystyle\|F(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\tau\delta<\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|,\quad 0\leq k<k_{\delta}

with suitably large
\tau>1
, then the convergence rate

\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|=O(\delta^{2\mu/(1+2\mu)})

holds, provided the source condition

x^{\dagger}-x_{0}=(F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger}))^{\mu}\omega

is satisfied for some
0<\mu\leq 1/2
with sufficiently small
\|\omega\|
.

Deriving convergence rates for the method (
3
) in its full generality is a very challenging
task. The main difficulties arise from several aspects: the possible nonlinearity of the forward operator

F
, the non-quadratic nature of the regularization functional
{\mathcal{R}}
, the non-Hilbertian structure
of the underlying space
X
, and the possible use of variable step-sizes
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
. When
F

is a bounded linear operator and the step-size is constant, the convergence rate analysis on the
corresponding method has been rigorously studied in
[
17
]
. In that work, through the
interpretation of the method as a dual gradient method, the convergence rates have been derived
successfully under varational source conditions (
[
14
]
) when the iteration is terminated by either
an
a priori
stopping rule or the discrepancy principle. The approach developed in
[
17
]

heavily relies on the linearity of the forward operator and the constancy of the step-size. However, it
is unclear how to extend the strategy presented in
[
17
]
to address the equation (
1
)
when the forward operator is nonlinear or when variable step sizes are employed.

In this paper, we will develop entirely novel ideas to tackle the challenge of deriving convergence rate
of the method (
3
) under the benchmark source condition

(7)

\displaystyle\xi^{\dagger}:=\xi_{0}+F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}\lambda^{\dagger}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x^{\dagger})\mbox{ for some }\lambda^{\dagger}\in Y

on the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
, where
\partial{\mathcal{R}}
denotes the subdifferential of
{\mathcal{R}}
. Note that, under
the tangential cone condition, (
2
) can be equivalently stated as

\displaystyle\min\left\{D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x,x_{0}):F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})(x-x^{\dagger})=0\right\}.

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) condition for this problem is exactly (
7
). In the context of ill-posed
problems in infinite-dimensional spaces, the mere existence of a solution does not guarantee the
satisfaction of the KKT condition. Therefore, (
7
) is commonly referred to as a source condition.
This source condition has been extensively utilized in deriving convergence rates for regularization
methods of ill-posed inverse problems (
[
6
,
14
,
17
]
). Under the range invariance condition
(
5
) on
F
and the source condition (
7
) on
x^{\dagger}
, we will show that
the convergence rate

\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|=O(\delta^{1/2})

holds if
k_{\delta}
is chosen by a suitable
a priori
stopping rule or the discrepancy principle
(
6
). Our results hold for any strongly convex regularization functional
{\mathcal{R}}
with great
flexibility on step-sizes by allowing the use of variable ones. Thus constant and adaptive
choices of step-sizes are covered by our results. The key point for deriving our convergence rate
is the observation that, under the condition (
5
),
\xi_{k}^{\delta}-\xi_{0}\in\mbox{Ran}(F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*})

for every
k
. This motivates us to reformulate the method (
3
) in an equivalent manner which
defines an auxiliary sequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{\delta}\}
such that
\xi_{k}^{\delta}=\xi_{0}+F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}\lambda_{k}^{\delta}
for
all
k
. Based on this sequence
\{\lambda_{k}^{\delta}\}
, the source condition (
7
), and the sequence

\{x_{k}^{\delta}\}
, we can construct a sequence of quantities which obey a nice recursive relation. Through
the investigation of these quantities leads us to obtain the desired convergence rate under an

a priori
stopping rule. Based on this
a priori
result, after a careful comparison we obtain
the convergence rate when the method is terminated by the discrepancy principle. Furthermore, we extend
the idea to deal with the stochastic mirror descent method, which is a stochastic version of the
method (
3
) for solving nonlinear ill-posed systems; we consider the exact data case and obtain
an almost sure convergence rate result under a benchmark source condition.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some basic facts of convex analysis
in Banach spaces. In Section
3
we devote to deriving convergence rates of the Landweber-type
method (
3
) when terminated by either
a priori
stopping rules or the discrepancy principle. In
Section
4
we extend the idea to derive convergence rate for the stochastic mirror descent method
for nonlinear ill-posed systems with exact data. Finally, in Section
5
we provide some numerical
results to validate the theoretical results on convergence rates.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic facts on convex analysis in Banach spaces; for more
details one may refer to
[
31
]
.

Throughout the paper, the same notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
will be used to denote either the inner product in
a Hilbert space or the duality pairing in a Banach space. Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|
,
we use
X^{*}
to denote its dual space. For a convex function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
, the set

\mbox{dom}(f):=\{x\in X:f(x)<\infty\}

is called the effective domain of
f
. If
\mbox{dom}(f)\neq\emptyset
,
f
is called proper. The
subdifferential of
f
is the set-valued mapping
\partial f:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
defined by

\partial f(x):=\{\xi\in X^{*}:f(\bar{x})-f(x)-\langle\xi,\bar{x}-x\rangle\geq 0\mbox{ for all }\bar{x}\in X\}

for each
x\in X
. Note that
\partial f(x)
could be empty for some
x
. The domain and graph of
\partial f
are
defined respectively as

\displaystyle\mbox{dom}(\partial f):=\{x\in\mbox{dom}(f):\partial f(x)\neq\emptyset\}

and

\displaystyle\mbox{graph}(\partial f):=\left\{(x,\xi)\in X\times X^{*}:x\in\mbox{dom}(\partial f)\mbox{ and }\xi\in\partial f(x)\right\}.

Given
x\in\mbox{dom}(f)
, an element
\xi\in\partial f(x)
is called a subgradient of
f
at
x
.
The Bregman distance induced by
f
at
x
in the direction
\xi\in\partial f(x)
is defined by

D_{f}^{\xi}(\bar{x},x):=f(\bar{x})-f(x)-\langle\xi,\bar{x}-x\rangle,\quad\forall\bar{x}\in X

which is always nonnegative and satisfies the identity

(1)

D_{f}^{\xi_{2}}(x,x_{2})-D_{f}^{\xi_{1}}(x,x_{1})=D_{f}^{\xi_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})+\langle\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},x_{1}-x\rangle

for all
x\in\mbox{dom}(f)
and
(x_{1},\xi_{1}),(x_{2},\xi_{2})\in\mbox{graph}(\partial f)
.

For a proper function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
, its convex conjugate is defined by

f^{*}(\xi):=\sup_{x\in X}\{\langle\xi,x\rangle-f(x)\},\quad\xi\in X^{*}

which is a convex function taking values in
(-\infty,\infty]
. If
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]

is proper, lower semi-continuous and convex,
f^{*}
is also proper and

(2)

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial f(x)\Longleftrightarrow x\in\partial f^{*}(\xi)\Longleftrightarrow f(x)+f^{*}(\xi)=\langle\xi,x\rangle.

A proper function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is called strongly convex if there exists a
constant
\sigma>0
such that

(3)

\displaystyle f(t\bar{x}+(1-t)x)+\sigma t(1-t)\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}\leq tf(\bar{x})+(1-t)f(x)

for all
\bar{x},x\in\mbox{dom}(f)
and
t\in[0,1]
. It is easy to see that if
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]

is strongly convex in the sense of (
3
), then

(4)

\displaystyle D_{f}^{\xi}(\bar{x},x)\geq\sigma\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}

for all
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}(f)
,
x\in\mbox{dom}(\partial f)
and
\xi\in\partial f(x)
. Furthermore, for
a proper, lower semi-continuous, strongly convex function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
satisfying
(
3
), it is known from
[
31
, Corollary 3.5.11]
that
\mbox{dom}(f^{*})=X^{*}
,

f^{*}
is Fréchet differentiable and its gradient
\nabla f^{*}
maps
X^{*}
to
X
with

(5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla f^{*}(\xi)-\nabla f^{*}(\eta)\|\leq\frac{\|\xi-\eta\|}{2\sigma}

for all
\xi,\eta\in X^{*}
. Consequently, it follows from (
2
) and (
5
) that

(6)

\displaystyle D_{f}^{\xi}(\bar{x},x)=f^{*}(\xi)-f^{*}(\bar{\xi})-\langle\xi-\bar{\xi},\nabla f^{*}(\bar{\xi})\rangle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|^{2}

for any
(x,\xi),(\bar{x},\bar{\xi})\in\mbox{graph}(\partial f)
.

3.
Convergence rates of Landweber-type methods

The convergence of general Landweber-type methods in Banach spaces, including (
3
) as a specific case, has been studied in
[
16
,
19
]
, particularly when the iteration is terminated using the discrepancy principle (
6
). For the method (
3
), the convergence analysis relies on the following standard conditions on
{\mathcal{R}}
and
F
.

Assumption 1
.

{\mathcal{R}}:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper, lower semi-continuous, strongly convex function in
the sense that there is a constant
\sigma>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(t\bar{x}+(1-t)x)+\sigma t(1-t)\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}\leq t{\mathcal{R}}(\bar{x})+(1-t){\mathcal{R}}(x)

for all
\bar{x},x\in\emph{dom}({\mathcal{R}})
and
0\leq t\leq 1
.

Assumption 2
.

(i)

X
is a Banach space and
Y
is a Hilbert space.

(ii)

There exists
\rho>0
such that
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\subset\emph{dom}(F)
and (
2
)
has a solution
x^{\dagger}
such that
D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma\rho^{2}
.

(iii)

There is a constant
L
such that
\|F^{\prime}(x)\|\leq L
for all
x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
and there exists
0\leq\eta<1
such that

\|F(\tilde{x})-F(x)-F^{\prime}(x)(\tilde{x}-x)\|\leq\eta\|F(\tilde{x})-F(x)\|

for all
\tilde{x},x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
.

Under Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
. it has been shown in
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
that

x^{\dagger}
is the unique solution of (
2
), and by using (
4
) we can conclude that

\|x^{\dagger}-x_{0}\|^{2}\leq\rho^{2}/2<\rho^{2}
, i.e.
x^{\dagger}\in B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Furthermore, we have the following
convergence result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
hold. Consider the Landweber-type method (
3
).
Let
\tau>(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
be a given number and let
\{\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\}
be chosen by one of the following
rules:

(i)

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\gamma/L^{2}
with
0<\gamma<4\sigma(1-\eta-(1+\eta)/\tau)
;

(ii)

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}
for some
\bar{\gamma}>0
and
0<\gamma<4\sigma(1-\eta-(1+\eta)/\tau)
;

(iii)

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma((1-\eta)\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta)\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}
for some
\bar{\gamma}>0
and
0<\gamma<4\sigma
.

Then the discrepancy principle (
6
) outputs a finite integer
k_{\delta}
and there is a solution
x^{*}
of (
1
) in
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\emph{dom}({\mathcal{R}})
such that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{*}\|=0\quad\emph{and}\quad\lim_{\delta\to 0}D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{*},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})=0.

If in addition,
\emph{Ran}(F^{\prime}(x)^{*})\subset\overline{\emph{Ran}(F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*})}
for all
x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, then
x^{*}=x^{\dagger}
.

Theorem
3.1
with
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
chosen by either (i) or (ii) has been proved in

[
16
,
19
]
, and the similar arguments there can be applied straightforwardly to
establish the convergence result when
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen by (iii). In the method (
3
),
the regularization functional
{\mathcal{R}}
can be chosen in various ways to detect the features of sought
solutions.

Example 3.2
.

Here are some choices of the regularization functional
{\mathcal{R}}
that are useful in detecting features
of sought solutions in applications.

(a)

When
X
is a Hilbert space and the sought solution lies in a closed convex set
C
,
we may take

{\mathcal{R}}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\iota_{C}(x),

where
\iota_{C}
denote the indicator function of
C
, i.e.
\iota_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and

\iota_{C}(x)=\infty
otherwise. It is clear that this
{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies Assumption
1

with
\sigma=1/2
.

(b)

When the sought solution lies in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with sparsity feature, where

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a bounded domain, we may take

{\mathcal{R}}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|x(\omega)|^{2}d\omega+\beta\int_{\Omega}|x(\omega)|d\omega,\quad x\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
\beta>0
is a large number. Clearly, this
{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies
Assumption
1
with
\sigma=1/2
.

(c)

When the sought solution is piece-wise constant on some bounded domain

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we may use

{\mathcal{R}}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|x(\omega)|^{2}d\omega+\beta\int_{\Omega}|Dx|

for some large number
\beta>0
, where
\int_{\Omega}|Dx|
denotes the total variation of
x
over
\Omega

defined by (
[
1
]
)

\int_{\Omega}|Dx|:=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}x\mbox{div}{\bf u}dx:{\bf u}\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mbox{ and }\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\right\}.

It is easy to show that this
{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies Assumption
1
with
\sigma=1/2
.

(d)

When the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
is a probability density function over a bounded domain

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, i.e.
x^{\dagger}\geq 0
a.e. on
\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}x=1
, we may take

{\mathcal{R}}(x):=f(x)+\iota_{\Delta}(x),

where
\iota_{\Delta}
denotes the indicator function of

\Delta:=\left\{x\in L_{+}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}x^{\dagger}=1\right\}

and

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\int_{\Omega}x\log x,&\mbox{ if }x\in L_{+}^{1}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }x\log x\in L^{1}(\Omega),\\
+\infty,&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

is the negative of the Boltzmann-Shannon entropy. Here

L_{+}^{1}(\Omega):=\{x\in L^{1}(\Omega):x\geq 0\mbox{ a.e. on }\Omega\}
. It is known that
{\mathcal{R}}

satisfies Assumption
1
with
\sigma=1/2
; see
[
4
,
7
,
10
,
17
]
.

The performance of the method (
3
) has been demonstrated in
[
16
,
19
]
through
various numerical experiments. These results indicate that the method (
3
) not only captures
the features of the sought solution with a suitable choice of
{\mathcal{R}}
, but also enjoys a certain convergence
speed when the sought solutions exhibit desirable properties. This raises the natural question of whether
it is possible to establish convergence rates for the method (
3
) when the sought solutions
satisfy specific source conditions. In this section, we will derive the convergence rate for the method
(
3
) under the assumption that the sought solution satisfies the benchmark source condition
(
7
), provided that
F
meets the following additional condition.

Assumption 3
.

There exists
\kappa_{0}\geq 0
such that for any
x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
there is a bounded
linear operator
Q_{x}:Y\to Y
such that

F^{\prime}(x)=Q_{x}F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|I-Q_{x}\|\leq\kappa_{0}\|x-x^{\dagger}\|.

Assumption
3
is actually stronger than condition (iii) in Assumption
2
. Indeed, using
similar arguments as in
[
13
,
22
]
we can conclude that, if
8\kappa_{0}\rho<1
, then

\|F(\tilde{x})-F(x)-F^{\prime}(x)(\tilde{x}-x)\|\leq\eta\|F(\tilde{x})-F(x)\|

for all
\tilde{x},x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, where
\eta:=5\kappa_{0}\rho/(1-8\kappa_{0}\rho)
. This implies
that condition (iii) in Assumption
2
holds with a small
\eta
, provided that
\kappa_{0}\rho
is
sufficiently small.

It should be noted that when
F
is a bounded linear operator, Assumption
3
holds automatically
with
\kappa_{0}=0
. Moreover,
[
13
,
27
]
provide examples of nonlinear ill-posed inverse
problems that satisfy Assumption
3
, including those arising from nonlinear integral equations
of the first kind and parameter identification in partial differential equations. Below we will provide one
more example that satisfies Assumption
3
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the
Neumann boundary value problem of the semi-linear elliptic equation

(1)

\displaystyle-\triangle y+g(y)=x\,\,\,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{\nu}y=0\,\,\,\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\partial_{\nu}y
denotes the normal derivative of
y
in the direction of the unit outward normal
\nu
to

\partial\Omega
and
g(y)
is a given function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the following properties:

\bullet

g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and there is a constant
\mu_{0}>0
such that
g^{\prime}(y)\geq\mu_{0}

for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

\bullet

g^{\prime}
is locally Lipschitz continuous, i.e. for any
M>0
there is
L_{M}\geq 0
such that

|g^{\prime}(y_{1})-g^{\prime}(y_{2})|\leq L_{M}|y_{1}-y_{2}|

for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|\leq M
.

Let
r>d/2
be a fixed number. We consider the inverse problem of determining the source term

x\in L^{r}(\Omega)
from an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-measurement of
y
. According to

[
30
, Theorem 4.7 & Theorem 4.16]
, for each
x\in L^{r}(\Omega)
problem (
1
) has a unique
weak solution
y_{x}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

and for any
\tilde{x},x\in L^{r}(\Omega)
there holds

(2)

\displaystyle\|y_{\tilde{x}}-y_{x}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|y_{\tilde{x}}-y_{x}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)},

where
C
denotes a generic constant depending only on
\mu_{0}
and
\Omega
. Since

H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, it makes sense to define the operator

F:L^{r}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
by
F(x):=y_{x}
for any
x\in L^{r}(\Omega)
and thus our inverse
problem reduces to the form (
1
).

According to
[
30
, Theorem 4.17]
, this
F
is Fréchet differentiable, and for any

x\in L^{r}(\Omega)
, its Fréchet derivative
F^{\prime}(x)
is an operator from
L^{r}(\Omega)
to

H^{1}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. For each
h\in L^{r}(\Omega)
,
v:=F^{\prime}(x)h

is the unique weak solution of

\displaystyle-\triangle v+g^{\prime}(y_{x})v=h\,\,\,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{\nu}v=0\,\,\,\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

Next, we show that this operator
F
satisfies Assumption
3
. To see this, we consider the
linear subspace

S:=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\partial_{\nu}u=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\}

of
H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
x_{0}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
be fixed and let
\rho>0
be a fixed number. For any

\tilde{x},x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}):=\{z\in L^{r}(\Omega):\|z-x_{0}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}<\rho\}
, we define
the linear operator
Q_{\tilde{x},x}:S\subset H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{1}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, where,
for each
v\in S
,
w:=Q_{\tilde{x},x}v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is the unique weak solution of the linear
elliptic problem

(3)

\displaystyle-\triangle w+g^{\prime}(y_{\tilde{x}})w=-\triangle v+g^{\prime}(y_{x})v\,\,\,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{\nu}w=0\,\,\,\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

Since
\partial_{\nu}v=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we can write

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&-\triangle(w-v)+g^{\prime}(y_{\tilde{x}})(w-v)=(g^{\prime}(y_{x})-g^{\prime}(y_{\tilde{x}}))v\,\,\,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\partial_{\nu}(w-v)=0\,\,\,\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

By the theory of linear elliptic equations, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|w-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|(g^{\prime}(y_{x})-g^{\prime}(y_{\tilde{x}}))v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|g^{\prime}(y_{x})-g^{\prime}(y_{\tilde{x}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By virtue of (
2
) and the local Lipschitz continuity of
g^{\prime}
, there exists a constant
C_{\rho}

depending on
\Omega
,
\mu_{0}
,
x_{0}
and
\rho
such that

\displaystyle\|w-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rho}\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall v\in S.

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset S
,
S
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus, the above inequality
implies that
Q_{\tilde{x},x}
can be extended to a bounded linear operator from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to itself and

(4)

\displaystyle\|I-Q_{\tilde{x},x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rho}\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}.

Note that, for any
h\in L^{r}(\Omega)
we have
v:=F^{\prime}(x)h\in S
, and for the function
w
defined by
(
3
) we have
w=F^{\prime}(\tilde{x})h
. Consequently

F^{\prime}(\tilde{x})h=w=Q_{\tilde{x},x}v=Q_{\tilde{x},x}F^{\prime}(x)h,\quad\forall h\in L^{r}(\Omega)

which shows that
F^{\prime}(\tilde{x})=Q_{\tilde{x},x}F^{\prime}(x)
. This, together with (
4
), shows that
F

satisfies Assumption
3
.

Note from the definition of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let Assumption

and Assumption

hold. Suppose

is chosen such

\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}\leq\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\leq\min\left\{\frac{\gamma\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}

for some positive constants

,

and

with

\displaystyle c_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\eta-\frac{\gamma}{4\sigma}\right)>0.

Let

for all integers

, where

Proof.

We first show by induction that

(8)

\displaystyle x_{k}^{\delta}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\leq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})+c_{1}\delta^{2}k

for all integers
0\leq k\leq\hat{k}_{\delta}
. It is trivial for

as
\xi_{0}^{\delta}=\xi_{0}
and

. Next we assume that (

) holds for all

for some

and show that (
8
) still holds for

. By the second equation in
(
3
), we have

. Thus we may use (

) and

\displaystyle\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{l}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{l+1}}(x_{l},x_{l+1})+\left\langle\xi_{l+1}^{\delta}-\xi_{l}^{\delta},x_{l}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}\left\|\xi_{l+1}^{\delta}-\xi_{l}^{\delta}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\xi_{l+1}^{\delta}-\xi_{l}^{\delta},x_{l}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

By virtue of the first equation in (
3
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{l}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}(\gamma_{l}^{\delta})^{2}\left\|F^{\prime}(x_{l}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\right\|^{2}-\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\langle F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta},F^{\prime}(x_{l}^{\delta})(x_{l}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\right\rangle.

By using
\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta
and (iii) of Assumption

\displaystyle\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{l}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}(\gamma_{l}^{\delta})^{2}\left\|F^{\prime}(x_{l}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\right\|^{2}-\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\langle F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta},y^{\delta}-y+y-F(x_{l}^{\delta})-F^{\prime}(x_{l}^{\delta})(x^{\dagger}-x_{l}^{\delta})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}(\gamma_{l}^{\delta})^{2}\left\|F^{\prime}(x_{l}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\right\|^{2}-\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle\quad\,+\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|\left((1+\eta)\delta+\eta\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\right).

In view of (

\displaystyle\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{l}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\eta-\frac{\gamma}{4\sigma}\right)\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}+(1+\eta)\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\delta\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|

\displaystyle=-2c_{0}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}+(1+\eta)\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\delta\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|.

Combining this with the inequality

shows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{l}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta)^{2}}{4c_{0}}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\right\|^{2}+c_{1}\delta^{2},

where for the last step we used
\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\leq\bar{\gamma}
. By virtue of this inequality
and the induction hypothesis, we have

\Delta_{l+1}^{\delta}\leq\Delta_{l}^{\delta}+c_{1}\delta^{2}\leq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})+c_{1}\delta^{2}(l+1)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma\rho^{2}+c_{1}\delta^{2}\hat{k}_{\delta}<\sigma\rho^{2}

which together with Assumption
1
and (

and hence
\|x_{l+1}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq\rho

1
and (ii) in

. We therefore complete the proof of (

which shows the desired

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\delta\left\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\right\|+2\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\left\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y-A(x_{k}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\right\|\left\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\right\|

\displaystyle a_{k+1}^{2}\leq(1+\beta_{k})a_{k}^{2}+b_{k}+c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta a_{k},\quad\forall 0\leq k<\hat{k}_{\delta}.

By recursively using this inequality we have

\displaystyle a_{k}^{2}\leq a_{0}^{2}\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}(1+\beta_{l})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(b_{i}+c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta a_{i}\right)\prod_{l=i+1}^{k-1}(1+\beta_{l}).

Note that

\displaystyle\prod_{l=i+1}^{k-1}(1+\beta_{l})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\log\prod_{l=i+1}^{k-1}(1+\beta_{l})\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{l=i+1}^{k-1}\log(1+\beta_{l})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\sum_{l=i+1}^{k-1}\beta_{l}\right),

where for the last step we used the inequality
\log(1+t)\leq t
for
t\geq 0
. Therefore

\displaystyle a_{k}^{2}\leq a_{0}^{2}\exp\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\beta_{l}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(b_{i}+c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta a_{i}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{l=i+1}^{k-1}\beta_{l}\right).

According to Lemma
3.4
, we have

(14)

\displaystyle c_{0}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\|F(x_{l}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}\leq\Delta_{0}^{\delta}+c_{1}\delta^{2}k\leq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})+c_{1}\delta^{2}\hat{k}_{\delta}.

Thus, there is a constant
\tilde{C}
such that

\exp\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\beta_{l}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{(3+\eta)^{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}}{8\sigma}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{l}^{\delta}\|F(x_{l})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}\right)\leq\tilde{C}

for all
0\leq k\leq\hat{k}_{\delta}
. Consequently

\displaystyle a_{k}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\left(a_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}b_{i}\right)+\tilde{C}c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i},\quad\forall 0\leq k\leq\hat{k}_{\delta}.

By virtue of Lemma
3.6
then we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle=a_{k}\leq\sqrt{\tilde{C}\left(a_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}b_{i}\right)}+\tilde{C}c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta k

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tilde{C}\left(a_{0}^{2}+c_{2}\bar{\gamma}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}^{\delta}\|F(x_{i}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}\right)}+\tilde{C}c_{3}\bar{\gamma}\delta k.

With the help of (
14
), we can find a positive constant
C^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|\leq C^{\prime}(1+k\delta),\quad\forall 0\leq k\leq\hat{k}_{\delta}\mbox{ and }0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

Since
\hat{k}_{\delta}=[c\delta^{-1}]
, we therefore complete the proof.
∎

3.2.
Convergence rate under
a priori
stopping rule

The following result gives the convergence rate of the method (
3
) under an
a prior
stopping
rule when the source condition (
7
) holds and the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen properly.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
and Assumption
3
hold. Let the step-size

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
be chosen to satisfy (
5
) and (
6
). If
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the source
condition (
7
) and if
\hat{k}_{\delta}
is chosen as
\hat{k}_{\delta}=[c\delta^{-1}]
for some positive constant

c
, then there exist
C>0
and
\bar{\delta}>0
such that

D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C\delta,\quad\forall 0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

Consequently
\|x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|=O(\delta^{1/2})
by the strong convexity of
{\mathcal{R}}
.

Proof.

By virtue of Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.7
, there is a positive constant
C
such that

(15)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k+1}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+C\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}+C\delta-2\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\Delta_{k}^{\delta}.

for all integers
0\leq k<\hat{k}_{\delta}
and
0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}
. Multiplying this equation by
c_{0}/C
, adding
to the equation (
7
) in Lemma
3.4
, and using
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\geq\underline{\gamma}
,
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}+\frac{c_{0}}{C}\|\lambda_{k+1}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}\leq\Delta_{k}^{\delta}+\frac{c_{0}}{C}\|\lambda_{k}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+c_{0}\delta+c_{1}\delta^{2}-c_{4}\Delta_{k}^{\delta}

for all
0\leq k<\hat{k}_{\delta}
, where
c_{4}:=2c_{0}\underline{\gamma}/C
. Recursively using this
inequality gives

(16)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}+\frac{c_{0}}{C}\left\|\lambda_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\right\|^{2}+c_{4}\sum_{k=0}^{\hat{k}_{\delta}-1}\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\leq\Delta_{0}+\frac{c_{0}}{C}\|\lambda_{0}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+(c_{0}+c_{1}\delta)\delta\hat{k}_{\delta},

where
\Delta_{0}:=\Delta_{0}^{\delta}=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})
. According to Lemma
3.4
we have

\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\geq\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-c_{1}(\hat{k}_{\delta}-k)\delta^{2},\quad 0\leq k\leq\hat{k}_{\delta}

and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\hat{k}_{\delta}-1}\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\geq\hat{k}_{\delta}\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-c_{1}\delta^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\hat{k}_{\delta}-1}(\hat{k}_{\delta}-k)=\hat{k}_{\delta}\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\hat{k}_{\delta}(\hat{k}_{\delta}+1)\delta^{2}.

Combining this with (
16
) gives

\displaystyle\min\{1,c_{4}\}(\hat{k}_{\delta}+1)\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq\Delta_{0}+\frac{c_{0}}{C}\|\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+(c_{0}+c_{1}\delta)\delta\hat{k}_{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}c_{4}\hat{k}_{\delta}(\hat{k}_{\delta}+1)\delta^{2}.

Therefore, there is a constant
C^{\prime}
independent of
\delta
such that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq C^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\hat{k}_{\delta}+1}+\hat{k}_{\delta}\delta^{2}+\delta\right),\quad\forall 0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

According to the choice of
\hat{k}_{\delta}
, we thus obtain
\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq C\delta
. Finally, by the
strong convexity of
{\mathcal{R}}
, we have
\|x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\leq\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}/\sigma=O(\delta)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Theorem
3.8
requires the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
to satisfy (
5
) and (
6
).
Actually there are various choices of
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
satisfying this requirement:

(i)

If
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is taken to be a constant step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\gamma/L^{2}
with

0<\gamma<4\sigma(1-\eta)
, where
L
is the finite constant appeared in (iii) of Assumption
2
,
then (
5
) and (
6
) are satisfied with
\underline{\gamma}=\bar{\gamma}=\gamma/L^{2}
.

(ii)

If
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen as

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}

for some constants
\bar{\gamma}>0
and
0<\gamma<4\sigma(1-\eta)
, then (
5
) and
(
6
) are satisfied with
\underline{\gamma}=\min\{\gamma/L^{2},\bar{\gamma}\}
. This choice
of
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is related to the minimal error method.

(iii)

Let
\tau>(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
be a given number and set
r_{k}^{\delta}:=F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}
. If

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen as

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\min\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}&\mbox{ if }\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta,\\[5.16663pt]
\min\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}(1-\eta)}{L^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\},&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for some constant
0<\gamma_{0}<4\sigma
, then by noting that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{0}((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{0}\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right)\frac{\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{L^{2}}\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right)

when
\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta
, we can see that (
5
) and (
6
) are satisfied with

\gamma=\gamma_{0}(1-\eta)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\underline{\gamma}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{L^{2}}\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right),\bar{\gamma}\right\}.

Therefore, the result in Theorem
3.8
holds for all the above choices of step-sizes, i.e.
if the source condition (
7
) holds and
\hat{k}_{\delta}=[c\delta^{-1}]
, then
\Delta_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}=O(\delta)

and
\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|=O(\delta^{1/2})
.

Remark 3.10
.

When
F
is a bounded linear operator,
{\mathcal{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
and
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen as

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\frac{\gamma\|Fx_{k}^{\delta}-y^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{*}(Fx_{k}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},

the corresponding method of (
3
) becomes the minimal error method for solving linear
ill-posed problems. It has been shown in
[
8
]
that minimal error method is not a regularization
method when terminated by an
a priori
stopping rule. Our result shows that if this step-size
is modified into the one as listed in (ii) of Remark
3.9
, convergence rate can be derived under
an
a priori
stopping rule if the source condition (
7
) holds. Our result does not contradict
the one in
[
8
]
because we have imposed an upper bound on
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
. Placing such an upper
bound can enhance stability in numerical computation.

3.3.
Convergence rate under the discrepancy principle

Next we turn to derive the convergence rate of the Landweber-type method (
3
) under the source
condition (
7
) when the iteration is terminated by the discrepancy principle (
6
)
for some number
\tau>1
. We need the following result.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
hold. Let the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
be chosen to
satisfy (
5
). If
\tau>1
and
\gamma>0
are chosen such that

(17)

\displaystyle\tau>\frac{1+\eta}{1-\eta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma<4\sigma\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right),

then the discrepancy principle (
6
) outputs a finite integer
k_{\delta}
,
x_{k}^{\delta}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})

for all
0\leq k\leq k_{\delta}
, and

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\leq-c_{5}\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}

for all
0\leq k<k_{\delta}
, where
\Delta_{k}^{\delta}:=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k}^{\delta})
and

c_{5}:=1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}-\frac{\gamma}{4\sigma}>0
.

Proof.

This can be done by essentially following the proof in
[
19
, Lemma 3.4]
or
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
and Assumption
3
hold. Let the step-size

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
be chosen to satisfy (
5
). Assume that
\tau>1
and
\gamma>0
are chosen such that
(
17
) holds and let
k_{\delta}
be the integer determined by the discrepancy principle (
6
). If
the source condition (
7
) holds, then there is a positive constant
C
such that

(18)

\displaystyle\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq C\delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq C\delta^{1/2}

for all
\delta>0
, where
\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}:=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})
.

Proof.

By the strong convexity of
{\mathcal{R}}
, the second estimate in (
18
) follows from the first one.
Thus we only need to show the first estimate in (
18
).

Since
\tau>1
and
\gamma>0
satisfy (
17
), the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
satisfies (
5
)
and (
6
). Thus, by taking
\hat{k}_{\delta}:=[\delta^{-1}]
, we may use Theorem
3.8
to
conclude that there exists constants
C^{\prime}>0
and
\bar{\delta}>0
such that

(19)

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C^{\prime}\delta,\quad\forall 0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

We will use this result to show the first estimate in (
18
). According to Lemma
3.11
,

\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})<\infty
. Thus, we can find a positive constant
C

such that
\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq C\delta
for
\delta>\bar{\delta}
. In the following we assume
0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}
.

We consider
k_{\delta}
according to two cases. If
k_{\delta}\geq\hat{k}_{\delta}
, then we may use Lemma
3.11

and (
19
) to obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C^{\prime}\delta.

It remains only to consider the case
k_{\delta}<\hat{k}_{\delta}
. According to Lemma
3.7
, there
is a constant
C
independent of
\delta
such that
\|\lambda_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|\leq C
. Thus, by using

\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}=\xi_{0}+A^{*}\lambda_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}
and the source condition (
7
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq D_{{\mathcal{R}}}^{\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})+D_{{\mathcal{R}}}^{\xi^{\dagger}}(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta},x^{\dagger})=\langle\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\xi^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A^{*}\lambda_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-A^{*}\lambda^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\rangle=\langle\lambda_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger},A(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|\|A(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|A(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|.

By Assumption
2
(iii),
\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta
, and
\|F(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\tau\delta
,
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\eta)\|F(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})-y\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\eta)\left(\delta+\|F(x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\eta)(1+\tau)\delta.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

By applying the above result to the method (
3
) with the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
chosen by those
rules listed as (i) and (ii) in Remark
3.9
, we immediately obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
and Assumption
3
hold. Consider the method
(
3
) with
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
chosen by one of the following rules:

(i)

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\gamma/L^{2}
for some
\gamma>0
;

(ii)

\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma\|F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}
for some
\gamma>0
and
\bar{\gamma}>0
.

Assume
\tau>1
and
\gamma>0
are chosen such that (
17
) holds and let
k_{\delta}
be the
integer determined by the discrepancy principle (
6
). If the source condition (
7
)
holds, then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C\delta\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq C\delta^{1/2}

for all
\delta>0
.

In Theorem
3.12
and Corollary
3.13
,
\tau>1
and
\gamma>0
are required to satisfy
(
17
).
In applications we may need to take
\tau
close to
(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
in order to get more
accurate reconstruction results. As a direct consequence, the theoretical result only allows to
use very small
\gamma
. This may lead to increase the required number of iterations and thus
consume more computational time. For the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
given by (iii) in Remark
3.9
,
fortunately we have the following result which allows
\gamma_{0}
to be a much larger number.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
and Assumption
3
hold. Let

\tau>(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
and let the step-size
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
be chosen as

(20)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}

with
\bar{\gamma}>0
and
0<\gamma_{0}<4\sigma
whenever
\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta
, where
r_{k}^{\delta}:=F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}
.
Then the discrepancy principle (
6
) outputs a finite integer
k_{\delta}
. If the source condition
(
7
) holds, then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C\delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|x_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq C\delta^{1/2}

for all
\delta>0
.

Proof.

The key point is to show that the same result in Lemma
3.11
holds under the relaxed
requirement on
\gamma_{0}
. To see this, we note that, when
x_{k}^{\delta}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
with

\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta
and
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
is chosen by (
20
), we may follow the derivation
of (
9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}-\Delta_{k}^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}(\gamma_{k}^{\delta})^{2}\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}-\left((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta\right)\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{4\sigma}\right)\left((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta\right)\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{4\sigma}\right)\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right)\gamma_{k}^{\delta}\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}.

Recall that
0<\gamma_{0}<4\sigma
and
\tau>(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
. Based on the above inequality, we
may use a similar induction argument in the proof of Lemma
3.4
to show that, if
n\geq 0

is an integer such that
\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta
for all
0\leq k\leq n
, then
x_{k}^{\delta}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})

for all
0\leq k\leq n
and

(21)

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}\leq\Delta_{k}^{\delta}-c_{5}\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2},\quad\forall 0\leq k<n,

where

c_{5}:=\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{4\sigma}\right)\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right)\min\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}}{L^{2}}\left(1-\eta-\frac{1+\eta}{\tau}\right),\bar{\gamma}\right\}>0.

If the discrepancy principle (
6
) does not output a finite integer, then
\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta

for all integers
k\geq 0
. Consequently, it follows from (
21
) that

\displaystyle c_{5}\tau^{2}\delta^{2}(n+1)\leq c_{5}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}\leq\Delta_{0}^{\delta}=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})<\infty

for any integer
n\geq 0
. Letting
n\to\infty
gives a contradiction. Therefore, the discrepancy
principle (
6
) determines a finite integer
k_{\delta}
with

(22)

\displaystyle x_{k}^{\delta}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\mbox{ for }0\leq k\leq k_{\delta}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}\leq\Delta_{k}^{\delta}\mbox{ for }0\leq k<k_{\delta}.

Finally let us derive the given convergence rate. To this end, we define
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
as in (iii)
of Remark
3.9
for
k>k_{\delta}
. For this choice of step-size, we know that the result in
Theorem
3.8
holds, i.e. for
\hat{k}_{\delta}=[\delta^{-1}]
there exist positive constants
C>0

and
\bar{\delta}>0
such that

D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{\hat{k}_{\delta}}^{\delta})\leq C\delta,\quad\forall 0<\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

Based on this inequality and (
22
), we may use the same argument in the proof of Theorem

3.12
to derive that
\Delta_{k_{\delta}}^{\delta}\leq C\delta
for all
\delta>0
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

(i) When
X
is a Hilbert space,
{\mathcal{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
and
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\mbox{constant}
, the
method (

) becomes the classical Landweber iteration (
4
) studied in
[

]

in which the convergence rate result under the source condition (

) requires

to
be sufficiently small. It is interesting to see that our result does not require this smallness condition.

4.
Convergence rate of stochastic mirror descent method

In this section we will extend the idea to deal with a stochastic version of the method (
3
) for solving nonlinear ill-posed system

(1)

\displaystyle F_{i}(x)=y_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,N

with exact data, where, for each
i=1,\cdots,N
, the operator
F_{i}:X\to Y_{i}
is a Fréchet
differentiable operator from a common Banach space
X
to a Hilbert space
Y_{i}
. We assume (
1
) has a
solution and look for a solution whose feature is described by a proper, lower semi-continuous, strongly
convex function
{\mathcal{R}}:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
. Let
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in X\times X^{*}
be an initial guess
with
x_{0}\in X
and
\xi_{0}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
, we intend to find a solution
x^{\dagger}
of
(
1
) such that

(2)

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})=\min\left\{D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x,x_{0}):F_{i}(x)=y_{i},\ i=1,\cdots,N\right\}.

Let
Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
be the product space of
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{N}
with the natural inner product
inherited from those of
Y_{i}
. By introducing the operator

F:X\to Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
as

\displaystyle F(x):=(F_{1}(x),\cdots,F_{N}(x))

and
y:=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})
, we can write (
1
) and (
2
) into the form (
1
)
and (
2
) respectively. In this way, we may use the method (
3
) to solve the problem.
Correspondingly, the updating formula from
\xi_{k}
to
\xi_{k+1}
takes the form

\displaystyle\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}-\gamma_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i})

which requires calculating the terms
F_{i}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i})
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
at each
iteration step and hence it can be time-consuming when
N
is large. In order to make the method more
efficient, we may take a random term from the sum in the above equation to update
\xi_{k+1}
. This leads
to the following stochastic mirror descent method.

Algorithm 1
.

Take
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in X\times X^{*}
with
x_{0}\in\emph{dom}(F)
and
\xi_{0}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be a given sequence of positive numbers. For
k\geq 0
do the following:

(i)

Pick
i_{k}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
randomly via the uniform distribution;

(ii)

Update
\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}-\gamma_{k}F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})
;

(iii)

Calculate
x_{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{{\mathcal{R}}(x)-\langle\xi_{k+1},x\rangle\right\}
.

When every
F_{i}
is a bounded linear operator, Algorithm
1
has been proposed and analyzed in

[
18
]
; under Assumption
1
on
{\mathcal{R}}
the convergence has been established and, when the
sought solution satisfies a benchmark source condition, a convergence rate has been derived.
In order to carry out the analysis of Algorithm
1
for nonlinear ill-posed system, beside
Assumption
1
on
{\mathcal{R}}
, we also assume the following standard conditions on
F_{i}
for

i=1,\cdots,N
.

Assumption 4
.

(i)

X
is a Banach space,
Y_{i}
is a Hilbert space for each
i
.

(ii)

There exists
\rho>0
such that
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\subset\emph{dom}(F)
and
(
2
) has a solution
x^{\dagger}
such that
D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{0}}(x^{\dagger},x_{0})\leq\sigma\rho^{2}
.

(iii)

There exists
0\leq\eta<1
such that

\displaystyle\|F_{i}(\tilde{x})-F_{i}(x)-F_{i}^{\prime}(x)(\tilde{x}-x)\|\leq\eta\|F_{i}(\tilde{x})-F_{i}(x)\|

for all
\tilde{x},x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
and
i=1,\cdots,N
.

(iv)

There is
\kappa_{0}\geq 0
such that for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
and each

x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, there exists a bounded linear operator
Q_{x}^{i}:Y_{i}\to Y_{i}
such that

\displaystyle F_{i}^{\prime}(x)=Q_{x}^{i}F_{i}^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|I-Q_{x}^{i}\|\leq\kappa_{0}\|x-x^{\dagger}\|.

Note that when
N=1
, Assumption
4
reduces to Assumption
2
and Assumption
3
.
Let

L:=\max_{i=1,\cdots,N}\sup_{x\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\|F_{i}^{\prime}(x)\|.

From (iv) of Assumption
4
it is easy to see
L<\infty
. From simplicity of exposition,
throughout this section we set
A_{i}:=F_{i}^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})
and define
A:X\to Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
by

\displaystyle Ax:=(A_{1}x,\cdots,A_{N}x),\quad x\in X.

It is easy to see that the adjoint
A^{*}
of
A
is given by

\displaystyle A^{*}z=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}^{*}z_{i},\quad\forall z:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}.

Assumption
4
will enable us to derive a convergence rate of Algorithm
1
under the
source condition (
7
) of the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
. i.e. there exists
\lambda^{\dagger}=(\lambda_{1}^{\dagger},\cdots,\lambda_{N}^{\dagger})\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\xi^{\dagger}:=\xi_{0}+A^{*}\lambda^{\dagger}=\xi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}^{*}\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x^{\dagger}).

In the following result we first show that Algorithm
1
is well-defined and the Bregman
distance
D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k})
is monotonically decreasing.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let Assumption
1
and (i)-(iii) of Assumption
4
hold with
L<\infty
. Consider Algorithm

1
. Let
\bar{\gamma}:=\sup_{k}\gamma_{k}
and assume

(4)

\displaystyle c_{6}:=1-\eta-\frac{\bar{\gamma}L^{2}}{4\sigma}>0.

Then
x_{k}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_{k}\leq-c_{6}\gamma_{k}\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|^{2}

for all integers
k\geq 0
, where
\Delta_{k}:=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k})
.

Proof.

When
x_{k}\in B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, we may use the similar argument in the proof of Lemma
3.4

to obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sigma}\|\xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k}\|^{2}+\langle\xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k},x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{4\sigma}\|F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})\|^{2}-\gamma_{k}\left\langle F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}},F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})(x_{k}-x^{\dagger})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{4\sigma}\|F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})\|^{2}-(1-\eta)\gamma_{k}\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|^{2}.

By using
\|F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\|\leq L
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\eta-\frac{\gamma_{k}L^{2}}{4\sigma}\right)\gamma_{k}\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|^{2}\leq-c_{6}\gamma_{k}\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|^{2}.

Based on this, we may use an induction argument, as done in the proof of Lemma
3.4
,
to conclude the proof.
∎

Note that, once
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in X\times X^{*}
and
\{\gamma_{k}\}
are fixed, the sequence

\{(x_{k},\xi_{k})\}
in Algorithm
1
is completely determined by the sample path

\{i_{0},i_{1},\cdots\}
; changing the sample path can result in a different iterative sequence
and thus
\{(x_{k},\xi_{k})\}
is a random sequence. We need to analyze Algorithm
1

using tools from stochastic calculus. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}=\emptyset
and, for each integer
k\geq 0
,
let
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random variables
i_{l}
for
0\leq l<k
.
Then
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:k\geq 0\}
form a filtration which is natural to Algorithm
1
. Let
{\mathbb{E}}

denote the expectation associated with this filtration, see
[
3
]
. There holds the tower property

{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\Phi|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]]={\mathbb{E}}[\Phi]
for any random variable
\Phi
.

Based on Lemma
4.1
, it is easy to derive the convergence of
\{x_{k}\}
to a solution
of (
2
) in expectation and almost surely by the arguments in
[
18
]
with minor
modifications. Our focus here is on deriving convergence rate of Algorithm
1
under
the source condition (
7
). To this end, we need to interpret Algorithm
1
from
an alternative perspective. Note that, under (iv) of Assumption
4
, the updating formula
for
\xi_{k+1}
can be written as

\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}-\gamma_{k}A_{i_{k}}^{*}(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}).

This motivates us to consider the following algorithm.

Algorithm 2
.

Take
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in X\times X^{*}
with
x_{0}\in\emph{dom}(F)
and
\xi_{0}\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
. Set

\lambda_{0}=(0,\cdots,0)\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
. For
k\geq 0
do the following:

(i)

Pick
i_{k}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
randomly via the uniform distribution;

(ii)

Update
\lambda_{k+1}=(\lambda_{k+1,1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1,N})\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
by

\lambda_{k+1,i}=\lambda_{k,i}
for
i\neq i_{k}
and

\lambda_{k+1,i_{k}}=\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\gamma_{k}(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}});

(iii)

Calculate
\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{0}+A^{*}\lambda_{k+1}
and

x_{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{{\mathcal{R}}(x)-\langle\xi_{k+1},x\rangle\right\}
.

Note that Algorithm
2
is not an implementable one, it is only used to produce an extra
sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}
in
Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{N}
which is crucial for the forthcoming
analysis. Note that for
\xi_{k+1}
defined by algorithm
2
we have

\displaystyle\xi_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\xi_{0}+A^{*}\lambda_{k+1}=\xi_{0}+\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}A_{i}^{*}\lambda_{k+1,i}+A_{i_{k}}^{*}\lambda_{k+1,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=\xi_{0}+\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}A_{i}^{*}\lambda_{k,i}+A_{i_{k}}^{*}\left(\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\gamma_{k}(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{k}-\gamma_{k}A_{i_{k}}^{*}(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\xi_{k}-\gamma_{k}F_{i_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}).

Therefore both Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
can produce the same
sequence
\{x_{k},\xi_{k}\}
if they start from the same initial guess, follow the same sample
path and use the same step-size.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
4
hold. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
satisfy (
4
).
Consider Algorithm
2
and assume the source condition (
3
) holds. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{(3+\eta)^{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}}{8\sigma N}\gamma_{k}\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}\right)\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{C\gamma_{k}^{2}}{N}\left\|F(x_{k})-y\right\|^{2}-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{N}\Delta_{k}

for all integers
k\geq 0
, where
\Delta_{k}:=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{k}}(x^{\dagger},x_{k})
and
C
is a positive constant
independent of
k
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\lambda_{k+1}
and the polarization identity we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}\|\lambda_{k+1,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{k+1,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}\|\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}-\gamma_{k}(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}\left\|(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\gamma_{k}\left\langle(Q_{x_{k}}^{i_{k}})^{*}(F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}),\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

By using Assumption
4
and following the similar argument in establishing Lemma
3.5

we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}(1+\kappa_{0}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|)^{2}\left\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\kappa_{0}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|\|\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\gamma_{k}\left\langle F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}-A_{i_{k}}(x_{k}-x^{\dagger}),\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\gamma_{k}\left\langle A_{i_{k}}(x_{k}-x^{\dagger}),\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}(1+\kappa_{0}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|)^{2}\left\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+(3+\eta)\kappa_{0}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|\|\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\gamma_{k}\left\langle A_{i_{k}}^{*}(\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}),x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

According to Lemma
4.1
and the strong convexity of
{\mathcal{R}}
,
\{x_{k}\}
is bounded. Thus, we can
find a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+C\gamma_{k}^{2}\left\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+(3+\eta)\kappa_{0}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\|F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})-y_{i_{k}}\|\|\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\gamma_{k}\left\langle A_{i_{k}}^{*}(\lambda_{k,i_{k}}-\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}),x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

Taking the conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{C\gamma_{k}^{2}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(3+\eta)\kappa_{0}}{N}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i}\|\|\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{N}\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}^{*}(\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}),x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i}\|\|\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|F_{i}(x_{k})-y_{i}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\lambda_{k,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|F(x_{k})-y\|\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|.

Combining this with the above inequality and using the definition of
F
and
A
, we thus obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{C\gamma_{k}^{2}}{N}\left\|F(x_{k})-y\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(3+\eta)\kappa_{0}}{N}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\|F(x_{k})-y\|\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{N}\left\langle A^{*}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}),x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

By using the definition of
\xi_{k}
, the source condition (
3
), and the strong convexity
of
{\mathcal{R}}
, we have

\left\langle A^{*}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}),x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle=\langle\xi_{k}-\xi^{\dagger},x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\rangle\geq\Delta_{k}+\sigma\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{C\gamma_{k}^{2}}{N}\left\|F(x_{k})-y\right\|^{2}-\frac{2\sigma\gamma_{k}}{N}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{N}\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(3+\eta)\kappa_{0}}{N}\gamma_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|\|F(x_{k})-y\|\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{C\gamma_{k}^{2}}{N}\left\|F(x_{k})-y\right\|^{2}-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{N}\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(3+\eta)^{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}}{8\sigma N}\gamma_{k}\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}\|\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{\dagger}\|^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We will use Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
to derive the almost sure convergence rate
of Algorithm
1
. To this end, we need the following Robbins-Siegmund theorem (
[
2
,
25
]
).

Theorem 4.3

(Robbins-Siegmund theorem)
.

In a probability space consider a filtration
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\}
and four non-negative
sequences of
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\}
-adapted processes
\{V_{k}\}
,
\{U_{k}\}
,
\{Z_{k}\}
and
\{\alpha_{k}\}
such that

\sum_{k}Z_{k}<\infty
and
\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}<\infty
almost surely. If for any integers

k\geq 0
, there holds

{\mathbb{E}}[V_{k+1}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]+U_{k}\leq(1+\alpha_{k})V_{k}+Z_{k},

then
\{V_{k}\}
converges and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}U_{k}
is finite almost surely.

Now we are ready to show the main convergence rate result on Algorithm
1
under the
source condition (
3
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
4
hold. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be a sequence of
positive numbers such that

(5)

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}:=\sup_{k\geq 0}\gamma_{k}<\frac{4\sigma(1-\eta)}{L^{2}}\quad\emph{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}=\infty.

Consider Algorithm
1
and assume the source condition (
3
) holds. Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}=O\left(s_{k}^{-1}\right)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|=O\left(s_{k}^{-1/2}\right)\quad\emph{almostsurely},

where
s_{k}:=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\gamma_{l}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
4.1
, taking the conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
gives

{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{k+1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}]\leq\Delta_{k}-\frac{c_{6}\gamma_{k}}{N}\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}.

By taking the full expectation, we further obtain

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}\gamma_{k}}{N}{\mathbb{E}}[\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{k}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{k+1}].

Consequently

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}}{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}\right]\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{k}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{k+1}]\right)\leq\Delta_{0}<\infty.

This implies that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\|F(x_{k})-y\|^{2}<\infty
almost surely.
Therefore, we may apply Robbins-Siegmund theorem to the inequality in Lemma
4.2

to conclude that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\Delta_{k}<\infty
almost surely. By using
the monotonicity of
\{\Delta_{k}\}
given in Lemma
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle s_{k}\Delta_{k}=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\gamma_{l}\right)\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{k}\gamma_{l}\Delta_{l}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{l}\Delta_{l}<\infty\quad\mbox{almost surely}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}=O\left(s_{k}^{-1}\right)\quad\mbox{almost surely}.

By the strong convexity of
{\mathcal{R}}
, we then obtain
\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|=O(s_{k}^{-1/2})
almost
surely. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

When
X
is a Hilbert space and
{\mathcal{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, Algorithm
1
reduces
to the stochastic gradient method studied in
[
15
]
. Under the diminishing step-size

\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{0}(k+1)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
shows that,
under the source condition (
3
), there holds the convergence rate

{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]=O\left((k+1)^{-\min\{1-\alpha,\alpha-\varepsilon\}}\right)

for some small
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha/2)
. Besides Assumption
4
and smallness of
\|\lambda^{\dagger}\|

and
\gamma_{0}
, the proof in
[
15
]
also relies on the technical condition

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|(I-R_{z_{t}})G(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]^{\theta/2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]^{1/2},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|(I-R_{z_{t}}^{*})G(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]^{\theta/2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

for some
\theta\in(0,1]
; one may refer to
[
15
, Assumption 2.4]
for the meaning of

R_{z_{t}}
and
G(x)
.
Unfortunately there is no any justification available for this technical condition. In contrast,
our result requires neither such a technical condition nor the smallness of
\|\lambda^{\dagger}\|
.
Our convergence rate is valid for any step-size
\{\gamma_{k}\}
satisfying (
5
).
For the step-size scheme
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{0}(k+1)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, our Theorem

4.4
gives the nicer almost sure convergence rate

\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}=O\left((k+1)^{-(1-\alpha)}\right).

Our result even allows to use constant step-size schemes to give much better almost sure convergence rate

\|x_{k}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}=O\left(1/(k+1)\right).

Remark 4.6
.

For the stochastic mirror descent method for nonlinear ill-posed system with noisy data, it is not yet clear how to derive convergence rates. We hope to address this issue in a future work.

5.
Numerical results

In this section we present some computational results to validate our theoretical convergence rate
results on the method (
3
) when it is terminated by the discrepancy principle (
6
) with

\tau>(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)
. The step-sizes
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}
are chosen by various rules which are described
below:

\bullet

Rule 1
:
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\gamma/L^{2}
with
\gamma=1.98(1-\eta-(1+\eta)/\tau)
;

\bullet

Rule 2
:
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\max\left\{\frac{\gamma\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}

with
\gamma=1.98(1-\eta-(1+\eta)/\tau)
and
\bar{\gamma}=600
;

\bullet

Rule 3
:
\gamma_{k}^{\delta}=\max\left\{\frac{\gamma_{0}((1-\eta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|-(1+\eta)\delta)\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|}{\|F^{\prime}(x_{k}^{\delta})^{*}r_{k}^{\delta}\|^{2}},\bar{\gamma}\right\}

with
\gamma_{0}=1.98
and
\bar{\gamma}=600
.

Here
r_{k}:=F(x_{k}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}
. In the following computations, the regularization functionals
{\mathcal{R}}
that
we use always satisfy Assumption

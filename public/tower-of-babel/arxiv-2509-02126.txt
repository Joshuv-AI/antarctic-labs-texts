Extensions of the Bonnet-Myers Theorem
Ronggang Li, Shaoqing Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we present extensions of the classical Bonnet-Myers theorem for Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Our results provide criteria for compactness and a method for estimating the diameter of such manifolds under general curvature conditions. As applications, we establish compactness theorems for manifolds whose Ricci curvature decays at polynomial or exponential rates.

1.
Introduction

Geometric inequalities involving the radial Ricci curvature along a geodesic are key to determining the compactness of a manifold and to estimating its diameter if the manifold is closed. This can be confirmed by the classical Bonnet-Myers theorem and its generalizations.

In
[
Cal67
]
, Calabi gives a series of integral estimates of the square root of the average radial Ricci curvature
\displaystyle q(r)=\frac{1}{n-1}\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})
along a minimal geodesic
\displaystyle\gamma
on a Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature. In particular, when
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
is a ray,

\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{q(r)}dr\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)},\quad\forall b>a>0.

Therefore, if
\displaystyle(M,g)
is complete and non-compact (i.e., open), the decay rate of its Ricci curvature is at most
\displaystyle 2
. Equivalently,
\displaystyle(M,g)
is compact provided
\displaystyle q(r)\geq cr^{-\lambda}
for
\displaystyle r\geq r_{0}
, where
\displaystyle c>0
,
\displaystyle\lambda<2
, and
\displaystyle r
is the distance to a fixed point
\displaystyle p\in M
.

One can verify that if
\displaystyle\lambda=2
and
\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{4}+\mu>\frac{1}{4}
, then
\displaystyle M
is also compact; furthermore, Cheeger, Gromov, and Taylor proved that its diameter is at most
\displaystyle\frac{e^{\mu}}{r_{0}}

[
CGT82
]
.

If
\displaystyle\lambda>2
, Wan showed that
\displaystyle M
is compact provided
\displaystyle c\geq\frac{(p-1)^{p}}{(p-2)^{p-2}}r_{0}^{p-2}

[
Wan19
]
.

In this paper, general lower bounds on Ricci curvature are considered, and we establish technical refinements of the Bonnet-Myers theorem as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(M,g)
be a complete
\displaystyle n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
is a ray along which the average radial Ricci curvature satisfies
\displaystyle q(r)=\frac{1}{n-1}\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})(r)\geq 0
. Let
\displaystyle\psi
be a monotonic function on
\displaystyle[0,\infty)
. Define

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(q,\psi,a,b)=\begin{cases}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi q}{\psi^{2}+q}dr&\text{if $\displaystyle\psi$ is non-decreasing on $\displaystyle[a,b]$},\\
\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi q}{\psi^{2}+q}dr-\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{\psi(a)}{\psi(b)}&\text{if $\displaystyle\psi$ is non-increasing on $\displaystyle[a,b]$},\\
\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
and
\displaystyle b
may be taken as
\displaystyle\infty
. Then,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(q,\psi,a,b)\leq 1,

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem

1.1
can also deduce that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle m(a)\geq\psi(a)\frac{{\mathcal{F}}(q,\psi,a,b)}{1-{\mathcal{F}}(q,\psi,a,b)}.

where the meaning of the function
\displaystyle m
can be seen in
Equation

2.4
.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
Equation

1.2
does not hold for each
\displaystyle\gamma(r)=\exp_{p}(r\nu)
,
\displaystyle r\in[0,\infty)
, then
\displaystyle M
is compact.

For the case where
\displaystyle\gamma
is a geodesic segment on
\displaystyle M
, we obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\displaystyle(M,g)
be a complete
\displaystyle n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\rho]\to M
is a geodesic such that
\displaystyle\gamma(\rho)
is conjugate to
\displaystyle\gamma(0)
along
\displaystyle\gamma
, and the average radial Ricci curvature
\displaystyle q(r)
along
\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle[0,\infty)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\psi q}{\psi^{2}+q}dr<2+\frac{1}{4}\left|\log\frac{\psi(\rho)}{\psi(0)}\right|.

Based on this result, the following improvement of the Bonnet-Myers theorem can be deduced directly:

Theorem 1.5

Let
\displaystyle(M,g)
be a complete
\displaystyle n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with a base point
\displaystyle p

\displaystyle r
is the distance to
\displaystyle p

q(r)=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu):\nu\in S_{x}M,~r(x)=r\right\}

\displaystyle r\geq 0

\displaystyle l>0

\displaystyle[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l}\frac{\psi q}{\psi^{2}+q}dr\geq 2+\frac{1}{4}\left|\log\frac{\psi(l)}{\psi(0)}\right|,

2.
Preliminaries and Basic Results

Let
\displaystyle(M,g)
be a complete (closed or open) Riemannian manifold without boundary. Suppose
\displaystyle p\in M
, and let
\displaystyle\nu\in S_{p}M
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\nu\in T_{p}M
such that
\displaystyle|\nu|=1
. Let
\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu}(r)=\exp_{p}(r\nu)
, where
\displaystyle r\in[0,\infty)
is an arc-length parameterized geodesic on
\displaystyle M
; we denote it briefly by
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
. Let
\displaystyle\{e_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
be an orthonormal frame at
\displaystyle p=\gamma(0)
such that
\displaystyle e_{0}=\dot{\gamma}(0)
. The normal Jacobi fields
\displaystyle\{J_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
along
\displaystyle\gamma
satisfy
\displaystyle J_{i}(\gamma(0))=0
and
\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}(0)}J_{i}=e_{i}
for
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Along the geodesic
\displaystyle\gamma
, we denote the representation matrix of
\displaystyle(J_{1},\cdots,J_{n-1})
with respect to the basis
\displaystyle(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1})
at
\displaystyle\gamma(r)
by
\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(r)
, and the matrix of the linear map
\displaystyle\nu\mapsto R(\nu,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}
by
\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}
. The Jacobi equation yields

(2.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime\prime}+{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{J}}=0.

Before the conjugate point,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}
is invertible. Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}
; then we obtain the following matrix-valued Riccati equation:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}+{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+{\mathcal{R}}=0.

We define the mean volume density
\displaystyle v(r)
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{n-1}(r)=\operatorname{dVol}(\dot{\gamma},J_{1},\dots,J_{n-1})=\det{\mathcal{J}},

so that

v(0)=0,\qquad v^{\prime}(0)=1.

Recall Jacobi’s formula for determinants:

(\det{\mathcal{J}})^{\prime}=\det{\mathcal{J}}\cdot\operatorname{tr}({\mathcal{H}}).

We define the radial mean curvature along
\displaystyle\gamma
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle m(r)=\frac{1}{n-1}\operatorname{tr}({\mathcal{H}})=\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{(v^{n-1})^{\prime}(r)}{v^{n-1}(r)}=\frac{v^{\prime}}{v}(r).

The average radial Ricci curvature is defined by

q(r)=\frac{1}{n-1}\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})(r).

The Riccati inequality involving the radial Ricci curvature along
\displaystyle\gamma
is obtained by taking the trace of
Equation

2.2
and applying the Cauchy inequality. Using our notations above, we can rewrite it as:

(2.5)

m^{\prime}+m^{2}+q\leq 0.

If
\displaystyle q(r)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle r\geq 0
, then
\displaystyle m
is non-increasing. We define
\displaystyle\zeta=\sup\{r:m(r)>0\}
to be the mean convex radial radius; note that it depends on the direction
\displaystyle\nu
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\zeta=\zeta(\nu)
.

If
\displaystyle q(r)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle r\geq 0
and
\displaystyle m(a)<0
for some
\displaystyle a>\zeta
, then
\displaystyle m^{\prime}(r)\leq-m^{2}(r)<0
implies
\displaystyle m(r)\leq\frac{1}{r-(a-1/m(a))}
, which forces
\displaystyle m
to go to
\displaystyle-\infty
before
\displaystyle a-\frac{1}{m(a)}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle m
blows down to
\displaystyle-\infty
in finite time. In this case,
\displaystyle v
also goes to
\displaystyle 0
at
\displaystyle\gamma(\rho)
. We define
\displaystyle\rho
to be the radial conjugate radius in the direction
\displaystyle\nu
. The radial conjugate radius may be
\displaystyle\infty
, but if
\displaystyle\rho
is finite, there exists
\displaystyle l\leq\rho
such that
\displaystyle\gamma
is a geodesic segment on
\displaystyle[0,l]
. As a corollary, if
\displaystyle\gamma
is a ray, there is no conjugate point to
\displaystyle p
along
\displaystyle\gamma
; therefore,
\displaystyle m(r)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle r\geq 0
. Furthermore, if
\displaystyle q>0
along
\displaystyle\gamma
, then
\displaystyle m>0
for all
\displaystyle r\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\zeta=\infty
.

Under the condition that the Ricci curvature of
\displaystyle M
is non-negative, the integral of the radial Ricci curvature
\displaystyle q(r)
or its functional along a geodesic segment
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,l]\to M
is limited; that is, it must satisfy certain geometric inequalities. For example, the second variation formula for geodesics implies that for
\displaystyle[a,b]\subset[0,l]
and a smooth function
\displaystyle u
on
\displaystyle[a,b]
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}u^{2}(r)q(r)dr\leq u^{2}(b)m(b)-u^{2}(a)m(a)+\int_{a}^{b}u^{\prime 2}(r)dr.

The classical Bonnet-Myers theorem can therefore be deduced from Wirtinger’s inequality for smooth functions
\displaystyle u
on
\displaystyle[a,b]
such that
\displaystyle u(a)=u(b)=0
:

\int_{a}^{b}u^{2}(r)dr\leq\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}u^{\prime 2}(r)dr.

Furthermore, if
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
is a ray and
\displaystyle[a,b]\subsetneq[0,\infty)
, then
Equation

2.5
and the fact that
\displaystyle m(b)>0
imply

\int_{a}^{b}q(r)dr\leq m(a).

Since
\displaystyle m(a)\leq\frac{1}{a}
by the mean curvature comparison, we obtain the following geometric inequality by letting
\displaystyle b\to\infty
:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}q(r)dr\leq\frac{1}{a},\quad\forall a>0.

In the following, we introduce a technical lemma from ODE cf.
\displaystyle\lx@sectionsign 9,\textrm{XIII}
,
[
Wal98
]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(r,y)
be a continuous function on
\displaystyle[0,\infty)\times(-\infty,+\infty)
, and it is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\displaystyle y
(for instance, suppose
\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial y}
is a continuous function).
\displaystyle y_{1}(r)
and
\displaystyle y_{2}(r)
are differentiable functions in
\displaystyle[0,l]
satisfying the inequalities

y_{1}\leq y_{2};

{y_{2}}^{\prime}-{\mathcal{F}}(r,y_{2})\leq 0\leq{y_{1}}^{\prime}-{\mathcal{F}}(r,y_{1}).

Then the differential equation

has a global solution
\displaystyle\phi
in
\displaystyle[0,l]
with
\displaystyle y_{1}\leq\phi\leq y_{2}

\displaystyle[0,l]
by
\displaystyle[0,\infty)

In the case that the interval is finite i.e.
\displaystyle[0,l]

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle[0,l]

\displaystyle y^{\prime}={\mathcal{F}}(r,y)

\displaystyle\phi

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow M
be a ray on
\displaystyle M
along which the average radial Ricci curvature
\displaystyle q(r)\geq 0
. Then, for any positive and derivable function
\displaystyle\psi
on
\displaystyle[a,\infty)
, there exists a function
\displaystyle\phi
on
\displaystyle[a,\infty)
such that:

​(1)

(3.1)

\phi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}\phi^{2}-\frac{q}{\psi^{2}+q}=0,

​(2)

(3.2)

\frac{\psi}{\psi+m}\leq\psi\phi\leq 1.

Proof.

For the fixed functions
\displaystyle q(r)
and
\displaystyle\psi(r)
, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(r,y)=\frac{q(r)}{q(r)+\psi^{2}(r)}-\psi^{\prime}(r)y^{2}.

A direct calculation implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\psi}\right)^{\prime}(r)-{\mathcal{F}}\left(r,\dfrac{1}{\psi}\right)=-\dfrac{q(r)}{q(r)+\psi^{2}(r)}\leq 0

on
\displaystyle[a,\infty)

On the other hand, the Riccati inequality (
2.5

\displaystyle\displaystyle-m^{\prime}\left(\psi^{2}+q\right)\geq\left(m^{2}+q\right)(\psi^{2}+q)\geq q\left(\psi+m\right)^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\psi
is assumed to be positive here and
\displaystyle m\geq 0

\displaystyle\gamma
is a ray,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-m^{\prime}}{\left(\psi+m\right)^{2}}\geq\frac{q}{\psi^{2}+q},

by differentiating
\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\psi+m}\right)
and combining the inequality above, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big(\frac{1}{\psi+m}\big)^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}\big(\frac{1}{\psi+m}\big)^{2}-\frac{q}{\psi^{2}+q}\geq 0.

which implies

(\frac{1}{\psi+m})^{\prime}(r)-{\mathcal{F}}(r,\frac{1}{\psi+m})\geq 0.

Therefore, by applying
Section

2

\displaystyle\phi
on
\displaystyle[0,\infty)
such that:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}\phi^{2}-\frac{q}{\psi^{2}+q}=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi+m}\leq\phi\leq\frac{1}{\psi}.

The proof is finished.

4.
Applications

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle M
be a Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below by a rational function of the form
\displaystyle(n-1)q(r)=(n-1)\frac{c}{r^{p}}
for
\displaystyle r\geq a>0
, where
\displaystyle r
is the distance to a fixed point
\displaystyle o\in M
. If
\displaystyle p>2
, then
\displaystyle M
is compact provided

(4.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c>c(p,a):=\frac{p^{2p}\sin^{p}(\frac{\pi}{p})}{4\pi^{p}}\left(\frac{a}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}.

Remark 4.2
.

One can verify that this constant is sharper than the one in
[
Wan19
]
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\psi
be a constant
\displaystyle x>0
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}},x,a,\infty\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{\infty}\dfrac{\dfrac{cx}{r^{p}}}{\dfrac{c}{r^{p}}+x^{2}}dr=\int_{\left(\frac{x^{2}}{c}\right)^{1/p}a}^{\infty}\frac{c^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{1+\tau^{p}}d\tau

\displaystyle\displaystyle=c^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\frac{\pi/p}{\sin(\pi/p)}-\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{x^{2}}{c}\right)^{1/p}a}\frac{1}{1+\tau^{p}}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle>c^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\frac{\pi/p}{\sin(\pi/p)}-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{c}\right)^{1/p}a\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-ax+\frac{\pi/p}{\sin(\pi/p)}c^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{1-\frac{2}{p}}.

A direct calculation shows that the maximum of the expression on the right-hand side over
\displaystyle x>0
is

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{x>0}\left\{-ax+\frac{\pi}{p\sin(\pi/p)}c^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\right\}=2c^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\frac{p-2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\frac{1}{p^{p}},

attained at
\displaystyle x=\left(\dfrac{c^{\frac{1}{p}}\pi(p-2)}{ap^{2}\sin(\pi/p)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}
.
Therefore,
Section

1
implies
\displaystyle M
is compact if

c>\frac{p^{2p}\sin^{p}(\pi/p)}{4\pi^{p}}\left(\frac{a}{p-2}\right)^{p-2}.

∎

Remark 4.3

If
\displaystyle p=2

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{c}{r^{2}},\frac{\sqrt{c}}{r},a,b\right)=\left({\sqrt{c}}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\log\frac{b}{a}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle M

\displaystyle c>\dfrac{1}{4}

If

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}},\sqrt{\frac{c}{r^{p}}},a,b\right)=\sqrt{c}\left(b^{1-\frac{p}{2}}-a^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\right)-\frac{p}{8}\log\frac{b}{a},

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle M
be a Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below by an exponential-type function of the form
\displaystyle q(r)=ce^{-pr}
, where
\displaystyle r
is the distance to
\displaystyle o\in M
and
\displaystyle p
is a positive constant. If
\displaystyle c\geq\frac{2p^{2}}{\log^{2}3}

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle c>(e^{2}-1)p^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\log\frac{e^{2}c}{c-(e^{2}-1)p^{2}}

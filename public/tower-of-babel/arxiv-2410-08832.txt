Abstract
. We describe old and prove new results on properties of the Fibonacci Lie algebra in a self-contained exposition. First, we study the growth of this algebra in more details. So, we show that the polynomial behaviour of the growth function in not uniform. We establish bounds on the growth of its universal enveloping algebra. We find bounds on nilpotency indices for elements of the Fibonacci restricted Lie algebra. We prove that the Fibonacci Lie algebra is not PI. Our approach is also based on geometric ideas. The Fibonacci Lie algebra is ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} -graded and its homogeneous components belong to a strip. We illustrate the results by computing the initial components and show positions of the homogeneous components in the strip. We also discuss properties and conjectures on related associative algebras and (restricted) Poisson algebras. Second, we prove infiniteness results on homology and Euler characteristic of arbitrary finitely generated graded Lie algebras of subexponential growth. In case of the Fibonacci Lie algebra, we find bounds on the homology groups and the Euler characteristic, and determine respective positions on plane. The computation of the initial part of the Euler characteristic of the Fibonacci Lie algebra shows that its behaviour is really chaotic. Finally, we formulate results and conjectures on homology groups and infinite presentation.

2.2.
Basic relations

Commutators of neighbours resemble the Fibonacci relation.

Lemma 2

[v_{i},v_{i+1}]=v_{i+2}

Proof.

Using (

[v_{i},v_{i+1}]=[\partial_{i}+t_{i-1}v_{i+1},v_{i+1}]=[\partial_{i},v_{i+1}]=[\partial_{i},\partial_{i+1}+t_{i}v_{i+2}]=v_{i+2}.\qed

is equal to 1 in case

Let us compute commutators of arbitrary pivot elements.

Lemma 3
.

Let

[v_{i},v_{j}]=t_{i-1}t_{i}\cdots t_{j-3}v_{j+1},\qquad 1\leq i<j.

Proof.

Using (

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}+t_{i-1}\partial_{i+1}+t_{i-1}t_{i}\partial_{i+2}+\cdots+t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-3}\partial_{j-1}+t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-2}v_{j};

\displaystyle[v_{i},v_{j}]

\displaystyle=[\partial_{i}+t_{i-1}\partial_{i+1}+\cdots+t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-3}\partial_{j-1}+t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-2}v_{j},\ v_{j}]

\displaystyle=[\partial_{i}+t_{i-1}\partial_{i+1}+\cdots+t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-3}\partial_{j-1},\ \partial_{j}+t_{j-1}v_{j+1}]

\displaystyle=t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-3}\partial_{j-1}(t_{j-1})v_{j+1}=t_{i-1}\cdots t_{j-3}v_{j+1}.\qed

For all

v_{n}(t_{j})=\begin{cases}t_{n-1}t_{n}\cdots t_{j-2},&n<j,\\

We consider the action of the infinite sum (

3.1.
Weight functions

The field

Let us construct a

Observe that a weight function is additive due to the correlation.

We say that not a necessarily finite linear combination of monomials is

(3)

(in

We use the following properties without mentioning:

(4)

We define the

Lemma 41

Let

{\mathbf{E}}(L):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{H}(\Lambda^{n}(L))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{H}(H_{n}(L)).

Proof.

Using equalities
\Imm d_{n}\cong\Lambda^{n}(L)/\Ker d_{n}

\displaystyle 1=\mathcal{H}(\Lambda^{0}(L))

Remark that both sides in (

Lemma 42

In notations above, the left hand side of (

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{H}(\Lambda^{n}(L),t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})=\prod_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0}(1-t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{n_{k}})^{\dim L_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}}.

Proof.

Let us prove the statement in case of

-grading

9.3.
Properties of Euler characteristic

We introduce an

Lemma 43
.

In notations above, the Euler characteristic equals

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}(L)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{H}^{[m]}(L)\bigg).

Proof.

Consider the case of one variable. By Lemma (

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}(L,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-t^{n})^{\dim L_{n}}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dim L_{n}\ln(1-t^{n})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dim L_{n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{mn}}{m}\bigg)=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dim L_{n}t^{mn}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{H}(L,t^{m})\bigg)=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{H}^{[m]}(L,t)\bigg).\qed

{\mathcal{E}}\,:\ &\phi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})=\!\!\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0}\!\!b_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{n_{k}}\quad\longmapsto\\
&\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{E}}(\phi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})):=\prod_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1-t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{n_{k}})^{b_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}}}.\end{split}

9.4.
Euler characteristic and homology of Lie algebras of subexponential growth

A sequence
\{a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\mid n\geq 0\}
is said of
subexponential growth
iff
\operatorname*{\overline{lim}}\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|a_{n}|}=1
,
the equivalent condition is that the generating function
f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, has the radius of convergence 1.

Now we generalize Lemma
47
to an infinite dimensional case.
The coefficients of a Euler series can be negative (see e.g. Fig.
4
), nevertheless we have the following fact.

Lemma 48
.

Let
L
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}{\setminus}\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
-graded Lie algebra of subexponential growth. Then

(1)

1\geq\mathbf{E}(L,t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})>0
for all
0\leq t_{i}<1
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

(2)

\mathbf{E}(L,t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
is decreasing with respect to all variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in[0,1)
.

(3)

\lim\limits_{t\to 1-0}\mathbf{E}(L,t)=0
.

(4)

\mathbf{E}(L,z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
converges absolutely at any point
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
where
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
|z_{i}|<1
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

(5)

\mathbf{E}(L,z)
converges absolutely at least in the open unit disc.

Proof.

1) Let
t:=\max\{t_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}
. Then
0\leq t<1
.
The subexponential growth of
L
implies a subexponential growth of its universal enveloping algebra
U(L)
,
which is equivalent to the fact that the radius of convergence of the series
\mathcal{H}(U(L),t)
is equal to 1
[
54
]
.
Since
\mathcal{H}(U(L),t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
has nonnegative coefficients, using Theorem
46
we get

\displaystyle 1=\mathcal{H}(U(L),0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}(U(L),t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\leq\mathcal{H}(U(L),t,\ldots,t)=\mathcal{H}(U(L),t)<\infty;

\displaystyle 1\geq\mathbf{E}(L,t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}(U(L),t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}>0;

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to 1-0}\mathbf{E}(L,t)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{t\to 1-0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}(U(L),t)}=\frac{1}{\dim U(L)}=0;

thus proving 1) and 3). Claim 2) follows from (
31
).

4) We compare coefficients at
{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})}
-components in (
27
), where
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0
, and get inequalities

|({\mathbf{E}}(L))_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dim\Lambda^{n}_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}(L)=\dim\Lambda_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}(L)\leq\dim U_{n_{1}\ldots n_{k}}(L).

Consider
t:=\max\{|z_{i}|\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}
, then
t<1
.
Then

|\mathbf{E}(L,z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})|\leq\mathcal{H}(U(L),|z_{1}|,\ldots,|z_{k}|)\leq\mathcal{H}(U(L),t)<\infty.

Thus we proved Claims 4,5).
∎

We establish the following infiniteness statement on homology.

Theorem 49
.

Let
L
be an infinite dimensional finitely generated
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebra of subexponential growth. Then

(1)

the Euler characteristic
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
satisfies estimates close to 1:

0<{\mathbf{E}}(L,t)\leq\exp\bigg(\frac{-1/2}{1-t}\bigg),\qquad t\in(1-\epsilon,1).

(32)

(2)

the Euler characteristic
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
has the radius of convergence one, where
z_{0}=1
is a singularity;

(3)

the Euler characteristic
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
is not a polynomial function;

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\dim H_{n}(L)=\infty
.

Proof.

By Claim 5) of Lemma
48
,
f(z):=\mathbf{E}(L,z)
converges at least in the disk
D:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<1\}
.
Consider the border
\partial D:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|=1\}
.
Recall that a point
z_{0}\in\partial D
is called
regular
provided that there exists an
analytic continuation
in a vicinity of
z_{0}
,
namely, there exists
\epsilon>0
and analytic function
g(z)
in
D_{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z-z_{0}|<\epsilon\}
such that
f(z)\equiv g(z)|_{D\cap D_{1}}
.
Otherwise
z_{0}\in\partial D
is called a
singularity
.
Let us show that the point
z_{0}=1
is a singularity.
By way of contradiction, assume that there exists an analytic function
g(z)
in
D_{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z-1|<\epsilon\}
,
0<\epsilon<1/2
, such that
f(z)\equiv g(z)|_{D\cap D_{1}}
.
Let
z_{0}=1
be a zero of

of order
m
, where
m\geq 0
. Thus, we have
g(z)=(1-z)^{m}g_{0}(z)
, where
g_{0}(z)
is analytic in
D_{1}
and

.
Consider both functions on the real segment

.
By Claim 1) of Lemma

,

for

, hence
C=g_{0}(1)=\lim\limits_{t\to 1-0}f(t)(1-t)^{-m}>0
.
We use Lemma
43
and that all
\mathbb{N}
-components of

are at least one dimensional

f(t)={\mathbf{E}}(L,t)=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{H}(L,t^{n})\bigg)\leq\exp\big(-\mathcal{H}(L,t)\big)\\
\leq\exp\bigg(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}\bigg)=\exp\bigg(-\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(\frac{-1/2}{1-t}\bigg),\qquad t\in(1-\epsilon,1).

Claim 1 is proved.
Now we see that
f(z)
tends to zero too fast while

. Formally, using estimate (

) we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<C=g_{0}(1)=\lim_{z\to 1}\frac{g(z)}{(1-z)^{m}}=\lim_{t\to 1-0}\frac{f(t)}{(1-t)^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to 1-0}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{m}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{1/2}{1-t}\bigg)=\Big|_{y:=1/(1-t)}=\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{y^{m}}{\exp(y/2)}=0,

a contradiction. Hence,
z_{0}=1
is a singularity on
\partial D
and we conclude that
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
has the radius of convergence one, Claim 2 is proved.
Since a polynomial function is an entire function,
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
has infinitely many nonzero coefficients, thus proving Claim 3.

Let us prove Claim 4. We know that
\dim H_{1}(L)
is equal to the number of generators, so
\dim H_{1}(L)<\infty
.
By way of contradiction, assume that
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\dim H_{n}(L)
is a finite number.
Looking at the right hand side of equation (
27
) we conclude that
\mathbf{E}(L,z)
is a polynomial, a contradiction.
Theorem is proved.

10.1.
Finitely presented Lie algebras

It is well known that finite dimensional simple Lie algebras
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero are finitely presented,
namely, they are determined by now classical Serre’s relations.
More generally, the Kac-Moody algebras are presented
by similar finitely many relations in terms of the Cartan matrix
[
24
]

I. Stewart proved that the simple infinite dimensional Witt Lie algebra

in case of characteristic zero is finitely presented
[

V. Ufnarovskiy remarked that the positive part of

is also finitely presented
[

More generally, Leites and Poletaeva showed that the simple infinite dimensional Cartan type Lie algebras

in characteristic zero
are finitely presented
[
29
]
.
Presentations of Lie algebras and close objects are extensively studied in terms of the Gröbner-Shirshov bases,
in particular, this approach was applied to simple finite dimensional Lie algebras and Kac-Moody algebras,
see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
and references therein.
Finitely presented Lie algebras and intermediate growth of their universal enveloping algebras were studied in
[

,

]
.
Futorny, Kochloukova and Sidki constructed in
[
17
, Thm. D]
examples
of finitely presented, infinite dimensional, metabelian, self-similar Lie algebras.
The question when metabelian Lie algebras are finitely presented was solved by Bryant and Groves

10.3.
Relations of
\mathcal{L}=\Lie(v_{1},v_{2})
and
\mathbf{L}=\Lie_{p}(v_{1},v_{2})

Initially, a countable set of relations of the Grigorchuk group was found by Lysionok
[
31
]
,
it is obtained from a finite set by applying iterations of an embedding endomorphism.
Later, such presentations were called finite
endomorphic presentations
or
L
-
presentations
by Bartholdi
[
7
]
.
We conjecture that a similar situation holds for Fibonacci Lie algebras.
We suggest the following list of relations.

Lemma 55
.

Let
p=2
,
\mathcal{L}=\Lie(v_{1},v_{2})
and

\tau:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
the shift endomorphism.
Then
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the following relations and all their shifts by
\tau^{k}
,
k\geq 1
:

(1)

[v_{2},v_{1}^{3}]=0
;

(2)

[v_{2},v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2}]=0
;

(3)

[v_{1},v_{2}^{4}]=0
, which is a
\tau
-image of the first relation;

(4)

the Fibonacci restricted Lie algebra
\mathbf{L}=\Lie_{p}(v_{1},v_{2})
satisfies
v_{1}^{4}=0
.

Proof.

1)
[v_{2},v_{1}^{3}]=[[v_{2},v_{1}],v_{1}^{2}]=[v_{3},t_{0}v_{3}]=0
.

2)
[v_{2},v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2}]=[v_{2},t_{0}v_{3},t_{1}v_{4}]=[t_{0}v_{4},t_{1}v_{4}]=0
.

3)
\tau([v_{2},v_{1}^{3}])=[v_{3},v_{2}^{3}]=[[v_{1},v_{2}],v_{2}^{3}]=[v_{1},v_{2}^{4}]
.

4)
v_{1}^{4}=(t_{0}v_{3})^{2}=0
.
∎

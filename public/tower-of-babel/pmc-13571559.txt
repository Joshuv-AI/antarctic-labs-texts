Design methodology for a photovoltaic emulator based on power hardware-in-the-loop with Opal-RT simulator
Authors: Hoai Phong Nguyen, Thuan Thanh Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study introduces a design methodology for a photovoltaic (PV) power emulator system. The proposed framework establishes a simple and rapid approach to constructing a PV emulator by utilizing a real-time simulator to control a power amplifier supply, forcing it to mimic the operational characteristics of a real solar array. This platform effectively serves for the experimental validation of PV characteristic measurements using a resistive load in student laboratory experiments, as well as the performance testing of solar power converters, such as solar MPPT chargers. Furthermore, the proposed method constructs an accurate PV system model operating under static environmental profiles (uniform irradiance and ambient temperature) as well as mismatched series cells under non-uniform irradiance, such as partial shading scenarios. The distinctive features of this methodology are as follows:• Photovoltaic (PV) modeling.• Development of a real-time PV array simulation model.• Real-time power emulation of PV profiles based on a Power Hardware-in-the-Loop (PHIL) platform, utilizing an OPAL-RT OP5707 real-time simulator and a 1-kW APS1000 power amplifier.

Background

Solar energy is a pivotal renewable energy source that plays a crucial role in sustainable development, reducing carbon dioxide emissions, and advancing global efforts toward net-zero targets [1,2]. Photovoltaic (PV) systems utilize solar modules comprised of numerous series-connected PV cells, where each cell acts as a current source whose output depends directly on solar irradiance and the underlying semiconductor material. When environmental conditions exhibit temporal variations or spatial non-uniformity (such as changes in irradiance and temperature), the resulting fluctuations in current and voltage introduce significant challenges to the control and operation of PV power systems. To validate research on PV control strategies or evaluate commercial devices like solar inverters and solar chargers, PV emulators are commonly deployed. These emulators replicate diverse environmental profiles and power outputs for testing purposes without relying on physical solar arrays [[3], [4], [5]].

Several research approaches have been proposed for PV emulator design. Some studies have focused on achieving fast dynamic response and high stability through simulation by applying intelligent control methods such as Neural Networks and Fuzzy Logic [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. However, these approaches are challenging to implement in practical hardware due to the computational burden of matrix operations on microcontrollers, leading to slow execution. Other literature [[10], [11], [12], [13]] has presented low-cost PV emulators utilizing Buck converters governed by Arduino controllers; nevertheless, these systems suffer from low control performance and are limited to low-power regimes of only a few tens of Watts. Obviously, studies on PV emulator control based on accurate mathematical models require high computational load and cause significant control delays, as they typically solve mathematical models using iterative analytical methods such as Newton-Raphson and Gauss-Seidel. Equivalent approximation PV models, such as those employing the Lambert W function, still demand substantial computational resources, while Taylor series expansions remain accurate only within the neighborhood of the expansion point. Furthermore, achieving stable control with a fast response time for PV system emulation introduces significant difficulties in modeling and testing power converters involving numerous design parameters.

To overcome these limitations, this study focuses on designing a simple and rapid PV power emulator system based on a lookup table with highly accurate modeled data. The proposed framework utilizes a real-time simulator to govern PV characteristics via a power amplifier, operating within a Power Hardware-in-the-Loop context. The core methodology of the proposed approach focuses on the following aspects:•Data modeling of PV modules under uniform temperature and irradiance profiles.•Data modeling of series-connected PV cells under non-uniform irradiance conditions.•Modeling the emulation system based on the parameters of the power amplifier and proposing a closed-loop controller combined with phase-lag compensation.•Development of a real-time PV characteristic controller based on look-up data tables.

Method details

In this study, we propose an accurate power emulation methodology for replicating photovoltaic (PV) characteristics based on Power Hardware-in-the-Loop (PHIL) simulation [19]. Unlike conventional methods that require designing and controlling complex power electronics circuits—which often suffer from challenging closed-loop I-V characteristic control and hardware design constraints—the proposed framework focuses on developing a high-accuracy descriptive PV model. This model is executed on a real-time simulator to govern a commercial power amplifier, precisely emulating the power characteristics of solar arrays under diverse environmental profiles without the necessity of developing complex customized controllers and power stages.

In this research, a commercial PV module (Model: A10J-S72–175, manufactured by A10Green Technology) is employed for modeling and experimental evaluation. The implementation procedure of the proposed practical photovoltaic (PV) system emulation methodology consists of the following steps:•Step 1: High-accuracy data modeling of the PV system characteristics under two common scenarios: uniform environmental conditions (constant temperature and irradiance) and mismatched conditions under partial shading (non-uniform irradiance).•Step 2: Exporting the computed current-voltage (I-V) characteristic data arrays of the PV system from the model into the power emulation program within the MATLAB/Simulink environment.•Step 3: Designing a real-time PV emulator controller based on look-up data tables to minimize the computational burden and control execution time.•Step 4: Interfacing the power amplifier with the real-time emulator controller and the physical sensor circuits.•Step 5: Conducting experimental power emulation of the PV profiles under various simulated scenarios predefined by the calculated data tables.

Data modeling methodology for a photovoltaic (PV) module operating under non-uniform irradiance and temperature profiles

In practical applications, PV cells as well as PV modules are commonly connected in series. Under non-uniform irradiance conditions—such as partial shading—the photocurrent Iph of individual cells is significantly affected. Consequently, the operating current may be forced to flow through the parasitic resistances (Rsh and Rs) of the shaded cells, leading to substantial power losses within the system [24]. Bypass diodes offer an effective solution to mitigate this issue by providing a low-impedance path that diverts the current away from Rsh and Rs. To mitigate noise from the feedback current sensor, a moving average filter is employed. In this study, the APS1000 power amplifier is modeled as a delay element, which, combined with the sensor delay and the computation time of the processing unit, introduces significant delay into the control model. We propose a closed-loop current controller combined with phase-lag compensation to enhance the stability of the PV emulation control model. Limitations

The emulation experimental results may vary if a power amplifier with a slower response time is utilized, or when incorporating electronic loads—such as solar inverters and solar chargers—that feature high-frequency PWM switching control. In addition, the OPAL-RT real-time simulator does not support PHIL simulation for mathematical models based on numerical iteration methods; thus, model-based testing cannot be implemented, which makes continuously varying irradiance and temperature require multiple look-up tables and complex interpolation. Furthermore, the capability to configure and interpolate PV characteristics directly based on standard Solar Array Simulator (SAS) models, such as EN50530 or Sandia (as found in commercial emulators), has not yet been implemented.

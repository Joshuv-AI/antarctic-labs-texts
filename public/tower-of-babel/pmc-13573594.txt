Dissecting Hypoxia Inducible Factor-Driven High Bone Mass Phenotype in Mice Lacking Osteocytic Von Hippel-Lindau
Authors: Adriana P Pantoja, Sarah V Mendoza, Deepa K Murugesh, Blaine A Christiansen, Gabriela G Loots, Damian C Genetos, Clare E Yellowley
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOsteocytes regulate skeletal homeostasis and respond to oxygen deprivation via hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), the principal transcriptional mediators of the cellular hypoxia response. There are 2 transcriptionally active HIF-α paralogs, HIF-1α and HIF-2α, that have overlapping and distinct transcriptional targets. Under normoxic conditions, the E3 ubiquitin ligase Von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) acts as the master regulator of HIFs by targeting them for proteasomal degradation, whereas hypoxia prevents this, allowing for HIF stabilization and accumulation. Osteocyte-enriched deletion of Vhl (Vhl cKO) produces a high bone mass (HBM) phenotype. Vhl loss stabilizes both HIFs, leaving their distinct contributions to this HBM phenotype undefined. To dissect their role in osteocytes, we developed compound Vhl; Hif1a cKO, Vhl; Hif2a cKO mice, mice expressing individual and combined degradation-resistant (cDR) paralogs HIF-1α (HIF-α cDR), HIF-2α (HIF-2α cDR), and HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR and evaluated their impact on bone structure and function. Micro-CT revealed that Vhl cKO mice exhibited the highest trabecular bone volume fraction, a phenotype not fully reproduced in any HIF-modified genotype. Among partial phenocopies, Vhl; Hif2a cKO produced the largest partial increase, followed by Vhl; Hif1a cKO, and HIF-2α cDR. HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR displayed modest elevation, while cre-negative and HIF-1α cDR were indistinguishable. Cortical area expansion required Vhl deletion and was not reproduced by single-paralog HIF stabilization. While Vhl cKO femora exhibited significantly increased whole-bone mechanical properties relative to sex- and age-matched controls, all HIF-modified genotypes had similar material properties and HIF-2α activation appeared to compromise material integrity. HIF-1α and HIF-2α therefore exert non-redundant, compartment-specific roles in the Vhl cKO HBM phenotype and are each required for maximal bone integrity. Yet, no HIF-modified genotype, including dual HIF-1α/HIF-2α stabilization, reproduced the Vhl cKO phenotype, implicating the possibility of VHL functions beyond HIF stabilization in the full skeletal response.Keywords: osteocyte, osteoclast, genetic animal models, bone QCT/micro-CT, molecular pathways-remodeling, biomechanics

Introduction

Skeletal bone mass and quality are maintained by a coordinated balance between osteoblast-driven bone formation and osteoclast-driven resorption. Osteocytes, embedded within the mineralized matrix, coordinate this process, sensing and integrating mechanical and biochemical cues locally, and hormonal signals systemically, to adjust remodeling activity.1 Through mechanotransduction in the lacunar-canalicular network (eg, responses to strain and fluid shear stress), osteocytes release factors that influence the activity of both osteoblasts and osteoclasts.2–4 Systemic hormonal cues are also detected by osteocytes to modulate mineral homeostasis (eg, PTH,5 glucocorticoids,6 and estrogen7). Notably, osteocytes constitute the great majority (>90%) of bone cells and are the longest-lived bone cell type,1 consistent with their role as principal coordinators of skeletal maintenance.

Osteocytes also mediate adaptive responses to cellular oxygen deprivation by engaging the hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway. Hypoxia-inducible factors are heterodimeric transcription factors comprised of an oxygen-dependent α-subunit and a nucleus-confined constitutively expressed ß-subunit. When oxygen levels are sufficient (normoxia), HIF-α is hydroxylated by prolyl hydroxylase enzymes, then recognized and targeted for proteasomal degradation via the Von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ligase complex. Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-α cannot be hydroxylated, evades VHL detection and subsequent degradation, dimerizes with HIF-β, and activates transcription of genes that promote angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, and bone-tissue remodeling.8–10 There are 2 transcriptionally active HIF-α paralogs, HIF-1α and HIF-2α, that have distinct and overlapping transcriptional targets.11 A common downstream target is vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which promotes angiogenesis.12 Osteogenesis is intimately linked to vascularity, both anatomically and functionally, which provides necessary nutrients, growth factors, and bone cell precursors.13 Hif1a and Hif2a are ubiquitously expressed14 and global KO of either is embryonically lethal (for instance, Hif1a−/− mice die around embryonic day 11 [E11]15; Hif2a−/− between E12.5 and E16.5).16 Von Hippel–Lindau/hypoxia-inducible factor signaling is active across osteogenic cell lineages and links angiogenesis with osteogenesis to drive and maintain skeletal homeostasis.

In previous studies, we demonstrated that deletion of osteocytic Vhl (Vhl cKO) generates a striking high bone mass (HBM) phenotype, while deletion of Hif1a or Hif2a in osteocytes, unexpectedly, failed to alter skeletal microarchitecture.17 However, expressing a degradation-resistant HIF-2α, but not HIF-1α, in osteocytes also produced a HBM phenotype.18,19 Because Vhl deletion stabilizes both HIF-1α and HIF-2α simultaneously, it remained unclear which paralog, or combination thereof, drives the anabolic phenotype. Therefore, to determine how HIF-α paralogs contribute to the Vhl cKO HBM phenotype, we examined compound female and male mice with dual Vhl and Hif1a (Vhl; Hif1a cKO) or Vhl and Hif2a (Vhl; Hif2a cKO) deletion in osteocytes. Alternately, we expressed degradation-resistant HIF-1α or HIF-2α singly, or in combination (HIF-1α; HIF-2α combined degradation-resistant [cDR]) in osteocytes to assess if individual or simultaneous stabilization of HIF-α paralogs could recapitulate the Vhl cKO phenotype. Surprisingly, the HBM phenotype presented in Vhl cKO was not reproduced in either: (1) Vhl; Hif1a cKO, (2) Vhl;Hif2a cKO, (3) HIF-1α, (4) HIF-2α, nor in (5) HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice. Instead, our findings indicate that HIF-1α and HIF-2α exert distinct, non-redundant functions in regulating skeletal development, bone mass, and matrix quality, and that HIF stabilization alone is insufficient to fully reproduce the skeletal response to Vhl deletion. Further, we observed sex-specific differences, particularly in trabecular bone and biomechanical properties, which suggest the relative contribution of each HIF-α paralog varies by skeletal compartment and animal sex.

Materials and methods

Mice

Vhlf/f (#004081), Hif1af/f (#007561), Hif2af/f (#008407), LSL-HIF1αDPA (#009673), and LSL-HIF2αDPA (#009674) C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratories and bred with mice expressing cre recombinase in osteocytes driven by the 10 kb-Dmp1 promoter (Dmp1-cre) [B6N.FVB-Tg (Dmp1-cre)1Jqfe/BwdJ] (kindly provided by Dr. Jian Q. Feng, Department of Biomedical Sciences, Texas A&M University Baylor College of Dentistry). Sex matched cre-negative (cre−) littermates were used as controls for all experiments. Vhl cKO mice were generated by crossing Vhlf/f mice with Dmp1-cre mice. To generate dual floxed lines, Vhlf/f mice were crossed with Hif1af/f and Hif2af/f mice over multiple generations producing Vhlf/f; Hif1af/f and Vhlf/f; Hif2af/f mice. These mice were bred to Dmp1-cre transgenic mice to generate Vhl; Hif1a cKO and Vhl; Hif2a cKO mice. Additionally, LSL-HIF1αDPA and LSL-HIF2αDPA C57BL/6 mice,20 which carry the ROSA26 knock-in alleles encoding HIF-1α (P402A/P564A) or HIF-2α (P405A/P531A) variants that cannot be prolyl-hydroxylated and therefore evade VHL-mediated degradation, were mated to mice expressing Dmp1-cre generating osteocyte-enriched, degradation resistant HIF-1α (HIF-1α cDR) and HIF-2α (HIF-2α cDR),18 that were intercrossed to obtain dual stabilization (HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR) mice. The following genotypes represent entirely new cohorts not previously published: Vhl; Hif1a cKO, Vhl; Hif2a cKO, and HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR. Genotypes were confirmed by PCR-based genotyping. The component alleles have been validated previously, including osteocytic Vhl, HIF-1α, and HIF-2α immunofluorescence in Vhl cKO mice,17 and HIF stabilization by immunohistochemistry in degradation-resistant HIF mice.18 Previously described and analyzed genotypes Vhl cKO, HIF-1α cDR, and HIF-2α cDR reported in18,19,21 are included for comparison; additional unpublished animals were incorporated for the current study. Mice were group-housed, provided standard chow and water ad libitum, and euthanized at 16 wk of age for micro-CT (μCT), mechanical testing, and histological analysis. To minimize bias, investigators were blinded to genotype during μCT, mechanical testing, and histomorphometric quantification. Animals were allocated to experimental groups based on genotype by breeding; therefore, randomization was not performed. Sex matched Cre-negative littermates served as controls. No formal a priori power calculation was performed; however, a minimum cohort size of 3 animals per genotype was established based on previous studies demonstrating robust skeletal phenotypes in these models. Final cohort sizes were limited by the availability of the compound genotypes. All animal cohorts were generated at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in an Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care International (AAALAC)-accredited animal facility and in accordance with the Animal Welfare Assurance (A3184-01). The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the IACUC (protocol #23615). Animals were euthanized by CO₂ inhalation followed by cervical dislocation as a secondary physical method to ensure death. No adverse events occurred in any experimental group. Cohort sizes for histology were n ≥ 3; animal numbers for μCT analysis and mechanical testing 3-point bending (3 PB) are presented in Table 1.

Micro-CT

At euthanasia, left femora were collected, cleaned of soft tissue, and stored at 4 °C in 70% ethanol. Micro-CT (μCT 35; Scanco Medical AG) was used to scan the femora at 6 μm nominal voxel size with 55 kV peak tube potential and 300 ms integration time as per Mendoza et al.18 Three-dimensional morphometric properties were quantified from femoral mid-diaphyseal cortical and metaphyseal trabecular bone as per Robling et al.22 and Bouxsein et al.23 Entire femora were scanned with the parameters previously described to produce 3-dimensional whole femur reconstructions.18 For trabecular analysis, a 1.5 mm region (250 slices) proximal from the femoral metaphyseal growth plate was assessed. Cortical bone analysis was performed on a 0.6 mm region (100 slices) taken 50 slices proximally and distally from the mid-diaphysis.

Mechanical testing

Three-point bending (3 PB) was conducted to determine cortical whole-bone structural and tissue material mechanical properties. Right femora were warmed to room temperature and hydrated in PBS for 10 min immediately prior to testing. Geometric properties required for 3 PB analysis were obtained from μCT scans of left or right femora. Femur midpoints were determined based on total femur length and marked directly onto each bone. Femora were positioned in a 3 PB apparatus (ElectroForce 3200, TA Instruments), with the anterior aspect of the bone facing downward (ie, in tension). With the bone situated on the 2 lower metal platens, spaced 13 mm apart, the visible marked midpoint of the bone was aligned with a third loading platen above. A 1-2 N preload was implemented to confirm contact with upper platen before applying a displacement rate of 0.01 mm/s, and force-displacement data was recorded at 50 Hz until bone failure. Whole-bone structural properties of stiffness, yield force, ultimate force, fracture force, post-yield displacement, and work-to-fracture were determined from load-displacement data. Tissue material properties of bending elastic modulus, yield stress, ultimate stress, and fracture stress were calculated using beam theory equations incorporating cross-sectional geometry obtained from μCT scans.24 Minimum bending moment of inertia (Imin) and minimum cross-sectional radius (cmin) calculated by SCANCO μCT system was utilized for all beam theory calculations.

Histology

At euthanasia, left femora were dissected, fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for 48 h, and stored in 70% ethanol at 4 °C. Samples were then decalcified, processed, and paraffin embedded as previously described.25 Utilizing a steel blade mounted on a rotary microtome (Microm HM355S), 5 μm longitudinal sections were cut and mounted on glass slides, deparaffinized, hydrated with ethanol dilutions (100%, 95%, and 70%), and stained with hematoxylin and eosin as described in.26 Tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) staining was conducted using a modified protocol from the University of Rochester Medical Center.27 Briefly, tissue sections were incubated in napthol AS-MX phosphate substrate mix in TRAP basic incubation medium for 1 h at 37 °C, followed by incubation with 1:1 solution of 4% sodium nitrite solution and 5% pararosaniline dye for signal development and counterstained with hematoxylin.

All bright-field images were acquired via QImaging Retiga 1300 camera with NIS Elements v2013 acquisition software on a Nikon Eclipse E600 microscope. Quantitative histomorphometry on TRAP-stained sections was performed using Fiji ImageJ28 to determine osteoclast number and bone surface. Osteoclast number over bone surface was measured from a 1.5 mm2 region of interest, 200 μm from the most proximal point of the growth plate for each mouse.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as mean ± SD. For each genotype, statistical significance from sex-matched cre-negative controls was determined by multiple comparison, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test, using GraphPad Prism version 10.6.1 (799) for Windows, GraphPad Software. For all tests, p-value <.05 was considered statistically significant. In all figures, groups that do not share letters are statistically different from each other.

Results

HIF-α paralogs only partially recapitulate the HBM phenotype of Vhl cKO mice

Analysis of trabecular microarchitecture showed a shared, strongly VHL-dependent hierarchy across sexes, with Vhl cKO mice exhibiting highest trabecular bone volume fraction (Tb.BV/TV; 89.7% ± 1.7% in females and 93.8% ± 2.1% in males) and trabecular thickness (Tb.Th; 0.07 ± 0.01 mm in females and 0.10 ± 0.01 mm in males); no HIF-modified genotype fully recapitulated this high-mass phenotype (Figure 1, Table S1). In both females and males, Tb.BV/TV formed graded intermediate tiers: Vhl; Hif2a cKO produced the strongest partial increase (67.7% ± 0.7% in females and 63.8% ± 3.1% in males), Vhl; Hif1a cKO a weaker one (61.7% ± 2.1% in females and 41.0% ± 5.3% in males), and HIF-2α cDR only a modest elevation (32.0% ± 3.5% in females and 49.0% ± 9.3% in males) that remained clearly distinct from Vhl cKO and the higher intermediate groups, while cre− and HIF-1α cDR were indistinguishable (5.8% ± 1.2% vs 7.2% ± 2.5% in females and 14.2% ± 4.8% vs 16.8% ± 8.1% in males) (Figure 1B, Table S1) (note in males HIF-2α cDR more closely approximated Vhl; Hif1a cKO). This pattern indicates that loss of Vhl is uniquely required to achieve maximal trabecular bone mass, whereas stabilization of either HIF-α paralog alone is insufficient, and that Vhl; Hif2a cKO reveals a particularly strong contribution of HIF-1α activity to a partial trabecular gain. In females, Vhl cKO generated a unique, high-thickness phenotype that was recapitulated by Vhl; Hif2a cKO (0.07 ± 0.01 mm vs 0.06 ± 0.01 mm). Vhl; Hif1a cKO, and HIF-2α cDR, HIF-1α cDR, and cre− were indistinguishable from one another (Figure 1C). In males, Tb.Th followed an almost identical architecture to Tb.BV/TV: Vhl cKO displayed a pronounced increase in thickness; Vhl; Hif2a cKO and Vhl; Hif1a cKO produced descending intermediate increases; and HIF-2α cDR, HIF-1α cDR, and cre− all clustered together (Table S1). Thus, among trabecular parameters, Tb.Th is the least HIF-responsive, with robust thickening restricted to Vhl deletion and only modest, graded effects of HIF-α manipulation confined to the Vhl-null background. Together, these results show that VHL-dependent increases in trabecular bone mass and thickness might require the combined, context-specific activation of both HIF-α paralogs and cannot be reproduced by single-paralog stabilization in an otherwise Vhl-intact setting.

Across all cortical outcomes, Vhl deletion produces a robust, multidimensional remodeling phenotype that neither HIF-1α cDR nor HIF-2α cDR can recapitulate alone, pointing to cortical regulation by VHL that potentially extends beyond HIF stabilization. In females, Vhl; Hif2a cKO retains high cortical area fraction (Ct.Ar/Tt.Ar) (48.4% ± 1.6%), while Vhl; Hif1a cKO displayed a reduction (39.2% ± 2.1%), and HIF-2α cDR exhibited the lowest values (33.3% ± 0.7%), demonstrating that HIF-2α stabilization does not promote and may even oppose this trait (Figure 1D). Males exhibited analogous changes with the exception that Vhl; Hif1a cKO and Vhl; Hif2a cKO were comparable (42.9% ± 2.0% vs 46.3% ± 1.0%) (Table S1). In contrast, cortical thickness (Ct.Th) and porosity display clear HIF-2α dominance (Figure 1E, Table 2). HIF-2α cDR generates the strongest intermediates—and in males, a full phenocopy of Vhl cKO in Ct.Th (Table S1)—while Vhl; Hif2a cKO sharply blunts these outcomes, indicating that HIF-2α is the principal paralog driving cortical thickening and porosity. In both sexes, HIF-1α stabilization alone is indistinguishable from cre− controls for every cortical trait, indicating that HIF-1α has negligible signaling impact outside the Vhl-null context, and its deletion in Vhl-deficient osteocytes mainly amplifies HIF-2α-dominated phenotypes rather than restoring normal bone structure (Figure 1D and E; Table 2, Table S2).

In contrast, there was strong sexual dimorphism by genotype in cortical mineralization. In females, Vhl deletion drives a broad suppression of bone tissue mineral density (TMD) that persists with loss of either HIF-α paralog, demonstrating that both HIF-1α and HIF-2α contribute to the female VHL-dependent mineralization defect, and neither can normalize TMD when stabilized alone (Table 2). In males, by contrast, most genotypes—including Vhl cKO, Vhl; Hif2a cKO, and HIF-1α cDR—maintain near-cre− TMD, and only excessive HIF-2α stabilization or combined Vhl loss and HIF-1α deletion disrupts TMD (Table S1). Thus, cortical mineralization is deeply sex-divergent, with female osteocytes relying on the VHL-HIF axis for TMD control, whereas male osteocytes remain largely resistant to perturbations in either paralog. Together, these results show that cortical remodeling emerges from a modular integration of VHL, HIF-1α, and HIF-2α, with HIF-2α acting as the primary effector of cortical thickening and porosity, HIF-1α remaining largely inert, and sex dictating the mineralization consequences of VHL and HIF perturbation.

Biomechanical properties reflect mass–quality tradeoffs in Vhl and HIF-modified bones

Consistent with prior work,19 Vhl cKO femora exhibit significantly increased structural mechanical properties relative to sex-matched controls, reflected in increased bone mass (Figure 2, Table 3). In female Vhl; Hif1a cKO mice, while cortices remained thicker than in cre− animals, the increased porosity essentially reduced cortical area fraction and mechanical strength accordingly, to cre− levels. Conversely, while Vhl; Hif2a cKO retained cortical area fraction similar to Vhl cKO, the reduced cortical thickness in these mice was accompanied by reductions in stiffness, ultimate load, and ultimate stress that reverted to cre− levels (Figure 2B, C, and E). These findings are consistent with the mechanical deficit associated with HIF-2α stabilization reported in an independent cohort of age and sex-matched mice, in which the decrease in elastic modulus and stiffness reached statistical significance.19 Notably, both genotypes exhibited a reduction in elastic modulus (Figure 2D). Given minimal effects on cortical morphology in HIF-1α cDR, both tissue-level and whole-bone properties were comparable to cre−. In contrast, HIF-2α cDR femora showed marked mechanical impairment: reduced stiffness, ultimate load, and elastic modulus were each below cre− mice (Figure 2B-D, Table 3). These outcomes were generally conserved across genotypes in male mice, except for HIF-2α cDR whose material properties were similar to that of cre− mice except for elastic modulus (Table S2). Collectively, while both HIF-α paralogs modulate bone morphology, mechanical testing reveals that HIF-2α activation compromised bone quality.

Mice with dual Vhl/Hif deletion exhibit bone marrow stroma expansion with reduced osteoclast density per bone surface

Consistent with the HBM phenotype, Vhl cKO femora demonstrated abundant trabecular bone in the metaphysis with numerous vascularized channels and limited hematopoietic tissue (Figure 3A and B).17 In contrast, metaphyseal bone from HIF-1α cDR closely resembled cre−, and HIF-2α cDR exhibited irregular dense spicules surrounded by thick layers of stromal tissue that replaced vascular and hematopoietic space (Figure 3A and B). Both Vhl; Hif1a cKO and Vhl; Hif2a cKO mice exhibited tissue morphology similar to HIF-2α cDR, with massive expansion of bone marrow stroma and numerous trabeculae (Figure 3A and B). Male mice (data not shown) exhibited similar tissue morphology to female mice.

Osteoclast density per bone surface (Oc.N/BS) was significantly reduced compared to cre− in Vhl cKO, Vhl; Hif1a cKO, and Vhl; Hif2a cKO mice (Figure 3E). Absolute osteoclast number was elevated in genotypes exhibiting stromal-expansion (Vhl; Hif1a cKO, Vhl; Hif2a cKO, HIF-2α cDR) relative to those with normal marrow architecture (cre−, Vhl cKO, HIF-1α cDR) (Figure 3C and D); however, this increase in osteoclast number occurred in parallel with a large increase in bone surface, resulting in reduced osteoclast density (Figure 3E).

Combined HIF-1α and HIF-2α stabilization fails to phenocopy the Vhl cKO HBM phenotype

To test if simultaneous stabilization of HIF-1α and HIF-2α could fully recapitulate the Vhl cKO phenotype, we created dual-stabilized HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice. Both female and male HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice exhibited a HBM phenotype with increased Tb.BV/TV (34.7% ± 4.8% in females and 70.0% ± 3.4% in males), elevated Tb.N (15.4 ± 0.7 mm−1 in females and 19.1 ± 0.2 mm−1 in males), reduced Tb.Sp (0.06 ± 0.004 mm in females and 0.04 ± 0.002 mm in males) relative to cre− controls. While Tb.Th was indistinguishable from cre− controls (Figure 4B and C; Table 4, Table S1). However, trabecular microstructural indices in HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR remained lower than those in Vhl cKO mice, creating an intermediate HBM phenotype. A greatly thickened porous diaphyseal cortex in female HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice led to cortical area fraction that was significantly reduced compared to both cre− controls and Vhl cKO (29.2% ± 1.4% vs 43.1% ± 1.7% vs 52.4 ± 4.9%) and which exhibited a lower TMD than cre− controls (1120.6 ± 14.3 mgHA/cm3 vs 1173.5 ± 11.7 mgHA/cm3) (Figure 4D and E; Table 4). Males showed an almost identical phenotype, though cortical area fraction approximated that of cre− controls (Table S1).

Dual stabilization of HIF-α paralogs in HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice impaired mechanical properties of the femur. HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice demonstrate marrow stromal cell expansion with reduced osteoclast density per bone surface, similar to HIF-2α cDR mice

The distal femoral metaphysis of HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice displayed extensive histological abnormalities, with numerous irregular bone spicules of poorly defined borders enveloped by thick stromal tissue layers (Figure 6B). This disruption created a more fragmented trabecular pattern compared to Vhl cKO. Similar to Vhl; Hif1a cKO, Vhl; Hif2a cKO, and HIF-2α cDR mice—which also exhibited marrow stromal expansion—osteoclast number was significantly increased in the HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice (Figures 3D and 6D). However, osteoclast density per bone surface declined in HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice, reflecting the greatly expanded bone perimeter (Figure 6E). Male HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice (data not shown) exhibit similar tissue morphology to female HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice. Together, these findings indicate that simultaneous stabilization of HIF-1α and HIF-2α amplifies the structural disorganization and stromal overgrowth associated with HIF-2α-driven signaling, yielding a phenotype that differs significantly from Vhl cKO architecture.

Discussion

Deletion of Hifa paralogs fails to rescue the HBM phenotype in the Vhl cKO phenotype

Loss or stabilization of individual HIF-α paralogs demonstrated that neither HIF-1α nor HIF-2α alone accounts for the skeletal phenotype of Vhl deletion. In the cortex, HIF-1α supported bone mass and structural integrity, as Vhl; Hif1a cKO mice displayed values between Vhl cKO and cre− control. HIF-2α, by contrast, drove cortical porosity and trabecular expansion: stabilization of HIF-2α alone markedly increased porosity, whereas its deletion in Vhl; Hif2a cKO restored cortical architecture closer to cre− levels. These reciprocal outcomes identify HIF-1α as protective and HIF-2α as disruptive within cortical bone. Importantly, the overall Vhl cKO phenotype does not align with either single cDR (HIF-1α or HIF-2α) model, suggesting that additional VHL-regulated mechanisms may contribute to the cortical skeletal response. Conversely, Vhl cKO trabecular structure requires input from both HIF-α paralogs. Loss of either Hif1a or Hif2a in Vhl mice reduced trabecular bone relative to Vhl cKO, but deletion of either failed to normalize trabecular bone to cre-negative outcomes, indicating that Hif1a and Hif2a are necessary yet insufficient. Consistent with our osteocyte findings, combined loss of Vhl and Hif1a in osteoblasts (osteocalcin OC-cre) also led to increased bone mass that was intermediate between controls and Vhl cKO and which was associated with elevated vascular density.29

Hif1a and Hif2a are both indispensable for preserving bone integrity, function, and mechanical strength. Both Vhl; Hif1a and Vhl; Hif2a defaulted to cre− levels for most tissue and whole bone parameters, underscoring the essential role of HIF signaling in bone mechanics. Overall, HIF-1α appears to sustain cortical stability and vascular coupling, while HIF-2α promotes trabecular formation at the expense of matrix organization. These results frame VHL as the upstream governor that balances these opposing tendencies.

Dual stabilization of Hifa paralogs fails to fully replicate the Vhl cKO phenotype

If Vhl cKO acted solely through HIF activation, dual stabilization would have recapitulated its HBM state. Simultaneous HIF-1α; HIF-2α stabilization produced a distinct and more pathological architecture. Cortical bone became abnormally thick yet riddled with porosity, while trabecular gains were only partial, particularly in females. Mechanical testing confirmed structural compromise: stiffness, ultimate load, and tissue modulus all declined to cre-negative levels, revealing bones that were larger in cross-section but materially weaker. Thus, the combination of reduced mass and extreme porosity in HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR mice produced bones which were structurally and materially weaker than Vhl cKO, despite involving the same two HIF paralogs. This observation is consistent with prior work by Kim et al.,20 who used degradation-resistant alleles and found that dual HIF-1α and HIF-2α stabilization reproduced much, but not all, of the Vhl loss phenotype in the liver. They attributed the residual difference to additional pVHL regulated functions beyond HIF stabilization. Our findings suggest that additional VHL-regulated mechanisms may contribute to the osteocyte-driven response in bone. This divergence emphasizes that mass and quality are uncoupled when HIF signaling escapes VHL control. The inability of HIF-1α to offset HIF-2α-driven matrix disruption echoes earlier evidence that HIF-2α impairs collagen alignment,19 whereas HIF-1α enhances vascularization,29,30 together illustrating how VHL normally coordinates these processes to maintain both growth and integrity. Mice with an osteoblast specific loss of function of Rictor (Ocn-cre; Rictorf/f) exhibited reduced cortical area fraction and impaired biomechanical properties, including decreases in elastic modulus, stiffness, and work to fracture, mirroring the cortical and biomechanical phenotype in our dual cDR model.31

The Vhl cKO phenotype was not fully reproduced by dual HIF-1α and HIF-2α stabilization. While Vhl deletion generated bones that were larger but modestly compromised in quality, dual stabilization produced bones that were both smaller and weaker. The degradation-resistant alleles used in this study reproduce Vhl-null HIF-α levels and transcriptional activity,20 suggesting that dual stabilization failed to phenocopy Vhl cKO not for want of HIF dose, but because VHL directs bone formation through a route HIF stabilization does not provide. While Vhl functions extend beyond HIF control, one alternative remains open, our models cannot exclude residual or compensatory HIF activity and formally distinguishing it from HIF-independent VHL function will require an osteocyte-specific Vhl; Hif1a; Hif2a triple conditional KO that is beyond the scope of the present study.

Altered osteoclast dynamics accompany stromal expansion

Manipulation of the VHL/HIF axis profoundly remodeled the marrow environment and reshaped osteoclast activity. HIF-2α dominant genotypes such as Vhl; Hif1a cKO, HIF-2α cDR, and the dual HIF-1α; HIF-2α cDR showed marked stromal expansion accompanied by increased osteoclast numbers. The expanded trabecular surface, however, dispersed these cells, resulting in reduced osteoclast density per bone surface. In contrast, Vhl cKO mice exhibited no stromal expansion, and increased trabecular density without increased osteoclast number, consistent with an anabolic bias. These results align with prior observations in other models, where osteoclast density is reduced despite increases in absolute osteoclast number. For instance, Prx1-Cre driven HIF-2α stabilization reduced osteoclast number per surface compared to controls,32 echoing our finding that HIF-2α-driven manipulations increase osteoclasts but the expansion of bone surface area results in a lower osteoclast density. Similarly, targeting osteocytic Myd8833 or osteoblastic Nell234 reduced osteoclast density, underscoring that both osteocytes and osteoblast-lineage cells orchestrate resorption through regulation of osteoclast positioning rather than absolute numbers. These findings place osteocytic HIF-2α activation at the center of marrow remodeling, coordinating both stromal proliferation and osteoclast redistribution, and they explain why HIF-2α stabilization yields bone that is histologically exuberant yet mechanically fragile. However, this osteoclast phenotype was also mirrored in Vhl; Hif2a cKO mice which demonstrated increased stromal expansion while HIF-1α cDR mice showed marrow morphology similar to cre− control. Together, these findings suggest that the stromal expansion and osteoclast phenotypes observed in Vhl cKO mice cannot be full explained by manipulation of a single HIF-α paralog.

Vhl-dependent, HIF-independent mechanisms may contribute to the HBM phenotype

The inability of HIF manipulation to mimic Vhl cKO highlights VHL’s broader regulatory scope. Several pathways plausibly contribute. Beyond oxygen sensing, VHL stabilizes the E3 ligase Jade-1, which targets β-catenin for degradation.35,36 Loss of VHL therefore amplifies Wnt/β-catenin signaling by reducing Jade-1 expression,37 and could contribute to osteoblast activity and trabecular expansion observed in our models. Reduced sclerostin and heightened β-catenin activation in Vhl-deficient osteocytes, together with partial rescue in Sost or Lrp5 crosses, support this mechanism.17 Canonical Wnt activation does not fully account for the phenotype, as the trabecular components of the Vhl cKO phenotype persisted despite Lrp5 deletion and Sost overexpression.17

Von Hippel–Lindau also governs extracellular-matrix assembly. By controlling assembly of fibronectin and collagen IV scaffolds, Vhl ensures proper deposition of latent TGF-β complexes.38–41 In osteocytes, disruption of these scaffolds may reduce TGF-β bioavailability, lowering sclerostin expression and further enhancing β-catenin activity.42,43 At the same time, defective fibronectin assembly can impair collagen deposition and mineralization, compromising tissue-level material properties.41,44 This provides a unifying explanation for the paradoxical phenotype of Vhl cKO bones, which accrue mass yet exhibit reduced elastic modulus. Supporting this view, fibronectin defects in Vhl-deficient endothelial cells45 and altered collagen assembly in skeletal cells44 mirror the material weaknesses we observe in bone. Together, these mechanisms, Wnt activation via Jade-1 loss and matrix disorganization via fibronectin failure constitute HIF-independent routes through which VHL directs bone formation and quality. They provide a plausible explanation for why Vhl cKO accrues more bone than any HIF stabilization model, while at the same time displaying distinct deficits in material properties.

Von Hippel–Lindau emerges as a coordinator of oxygen-responsive and matrix-regulatory processes that together preserve skeletal homeostasis. Hypoxia-inducible factor-dependent mechanisms couple osteocyte metabolism and angiogenesis to bone accrual, whereas HIF-independent mechanisms may maintain matrix organization and mechanical strength. Although definitive resolution of HIF-independent VHL functions will require future studies using osteocyte-specific Vhl; Hif1a; Hif2a triple KO mice, the present findings demonstrate that HIF stabilization alone is insufficient to fully reproduce the Vhl cKO skeletal phenotype. Skeletal phenotypes were evaluated at 16 wk of age, limiting the ability to distinguish developmental vs adaptive mechanisms, and several compound genotypes were necessarily represented by small cohorts due to breeding constraints. However, the major conclusions are supported by large and reproducible effects observed across structural and mechanical outcomes. Furthermore, the reduced material properties associated with HIF-2α activation is consistent with findings from an independent cohort reported previously.19 These findings nevertheless reveal distinct compartment-specific and nonredundant roles for HIF-1α and HIF-2α in regulating bone mass and bone quality. The dual nature of VHL control may explain why deleting or stabilizing HIFs alone cannot reproduce the Vhl cKO phenotype. Von Hippel–Lindau-deficient bones have abundant extracellular matrix but compromised material properties because the same loss that activates anabolism also undermines matrix cohesion.

Understanding how VHL balances these oxygen- and matrix-linked pathways may identify new approaches to enhance bone formation while preserving quality. From a translational perspective, these findings underscore the need for caution when targeting the HIF pathway therapeutically. Selective activation of HIF-1α, while limiting HIF-2α signaling, may enhance bone accrual without promoting porosity, and interventions that preserve VHL-dependent matrix regulation could help to decouple bone mass from diminished material quality. Ultimately, the challenge is not simply to add bone, but to build bone that is both plentiful and resilient.

Enhancing peak demand forecasting with adaptive FastICA-transformer and entropy-based model customization
Authors: Fahman Saeed, Sultan Aldera, Mohammad Alkhatib
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractModern energy systems necessitate accurate peak demand predictions to minimize costs. This study presents an adaptive FastICA-Transformer that improves feature extraction and forecasting precision using entropy-guided component selection and a redesigned transformer architecture. The model modifies transformer depth according to the forecast horizon and initializes attention heads using statistically independent FastICA components, resulting in interpretable and efficient networks. In comparison to PCA-Transformer and sophisticated neural architectures (LSTM, TCN, N-BEATS, TFT), the FastICA-Transformer demonstrates enhanced MAE, RMSE, and MAPE in forecasting for Next-Hour, Next-Day, Next-Week, and Next-Quarter intervals. The results indicate that architectural adaptation, informed by temporal and statistical characteristics, efficiently tackles peak demand forecasts in practical power systems.Keywords: Peak demand forecasting, Adaptive transformer, FastICA (Independent component analysis), Entropy-guided feature selection, Smart grid operations, Time series forecastingSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Accurate peak demand forecasting is crucial for optimizing Energy management systems (EMS), which rely on precise load forecasts to ensure grid stability, save operating costs, and enable demand-response strategies1,2. Accurate peak forecasts allow utilities and grid operators to effectively manage generation resources, plan maintenance, and anticipate infrastructure requirements, thereby preventing expensive over-provisioning or unforeseen deficits3. Furthermore, as power systems progressively incorporate renewable energy sources and dispersed generation, the fluctuation and uncertainty of supply heighten the necessity for precise peak demand projections. Enhanced forecasting facilitates regulatory adherence, optimizes market operations, and mitigates environmental impact by promoting superior integration of renewable energy and more efficient utilization of storage and flexible loads4. In this environment, sophisticated forecasting approaches are essential for facilitating the digital revolution of the electricity sector, enhancing resilient, cost-efficient, and sustainable grid operations5.

Conventional statistical techniques, such as Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA), have historically served as the foundation for load forecasting; nevertheless, their efficacy is generally confined to stationary and low-dimensional contexts6. The advent of deep learning, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformer architectures, has facilitated substantial progress in time-series prediction, encompassing load and renewable generation forecasting, by allowing the modeling of intricate, nonlinear temporal dependencies. LSTM networks are proficient in identifying sequential patterns, whereas Transformers utilize self-attention to encode both local and global temporal contexts7,8. Nonetheless, these complex systems are susceptible to constraints: the high dimensionality of smart grid datasets may add noise and superfluous features, resulting in overfitting, heightened processing demands, and diminished interpretability9.

Dimensionality reduction methods, like Principal Component Analysis (PCA)10 and Independent Component Analysis (ICA)10, have thus emerged as essential instruments in pre-processing workflows, condensing raw data into more meaningful and manageable forms. Numerous studies have shown enhanced forecasting accuracy and training efficiency by integrating PCA11–13 or ICA14,15 prior to neural models; however, the determination of the number of components to retain frequently relies on heuristics, fixed thresholds, or explained variance criteria, which may lead to suboptimal model performance or information loss.

Recent studies have examined adaptive and data-driven methodologies for component selection, encompassing cross-validation, neural architecture search, and model selection criteria such as AIC and BIC16,17. Others have investigated feature relevance scores and attention techniques to dynamically modify input spaces18,19. The application of information-theoretic measurements, particularly entropy, to inform component selection is still largely unexamined20. Moreover, the majority of current methodologies either regard feature extraction and sequence modeling as separate processes or exhibit deficiencies in transparency and scalability when combined with advanced deep learning models. Recent hybrid models have persistently investigated the integration of feature selection with deep learning.

Although Independent Component Analysis (ICA), entropy metrics, and Transformer topologies are individually present in forecasting literature, our FastICA-Transformer integrates three essential technical advances that have not been previously consolidated or exhibited in any current Short-Term Load Forecasting (ST LF) system:

Direct Structural Mapping from ICA to Attention.

Direct Structural Mapping from ICA to Attention.

In contrast to traditional Transformers that randomly initialize attention heads or employ generic weight schemes, we propose a deterministic initialization method wherein the Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) projection matrices of each attention head are directly initialized using the respective ICA mixing vector (A:,j from the ICA decomposition matrix). This creates a one-to-one structural correlation between statistically independent demand sources (identified by ICA) and attention mechanisms, endowing the network with physically significant inductive biases from initialization instead of necessitating the model to uncover these patterns through extended training. This differs significantly from normal Transformers (random/Xavier initialization devoid of source-specific structure), PCA-Transformers (orthogonal yet mixed, non-independent components), and graph-based attention models (data-driven but missing guarantees of statistical independence).

Entropy-Directed Automated Component Optimization.

Entropy-Directed Automated Component Optimization.

Current ICA uses in forecasting depend on manual thresholds, predetermined component counts, or iterative selection methods. We present an entropy-maximization criterion with elbow detection that autonomously determines the optimal number of components (n*) by calculating Shannon entropy for each ICA component to assess information content, ranking components in descending order of entropy (statistical diversity), and automatically identifying the “elbow point” in cumulative entropy curves where the marginal information gain declines. This obviates hyperparameter tuning for component selection and guarantees information-theoretic optimality without operator interaction or the burden of cross-validation—a feature lacking in all examined hybrid forecasting models.

Horizon-Adaptive Depth Search Without Retraining.

Horizon-Adaptive Depth Search Without Retraining.

The majority of deep learning forecasting models employ static architectures for all prediction horizons (e.g., LSTM with three layers, N-BEATS with thirty blocks). We present a validation-driven adaptive depth mechanism in which encoder depth (L) is incrementally evaluated (L = 1, 2, 3, 4…), with the optimal depth L* determined by the minimum validation MAE for each specific forecast horizon, while employing a consistent feature extraction pipeline (ICA + entropy) across all horizons, with only the depth being adjusted. This facilitates multi-horizon forecasting within a cohesive framework (Next-Hour to Next-Quarter) without necessitating distinct model architectures or retraining—offering both scalability (one codebase) and efficiency (automated complexity management).

The principal innovation lies not solely in the amalgamation of these three components, but in their functional integration. ICA decomposition informs entropy analysis, which ascertains the number of attention heads, subsequently initializing the adaptive-depth Transformer. This establishes a completely automated, comprehensive pipeline from unprocessed multi-dimensional data to horizon-specific predictions, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering, threshold adjustment, or architectural exploration. This integrated approach empirically exhibits superior prediction accuracy compared to both component-specific approaches (PCA-only, conventional Transformers) and current hybrid models, while preserving interpretability via the ICA-attention correspondence.

Methodology

The proposed study improves our previous PCA-Transformer by substituting PCA with Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to facilitate the disentanglement of latent components in energy consumption data. The model seeks to separate statistically independent components that more accurately represent various sources of demand, including user behavior, environmental factors, and system dynamics. ICA is employed to discern separate signals from the input data, subsequently informing the initialization of attention heads in the Transformer. This strategy utilizes ICA’s capability to disentangle mixed signals, beyond the constraints of PCA, which exclusively identifies orthogonal directions with maximal variance9,31.

The proposed ICA-guided Transformer incorporates the most informative independent components to enhance the attention mechanism for peak demand forecasting. It allocates each attention head to a statistically independent signal derived by ICA, incorporating significant inductive biases into the model. Simultaneously, the initial input persists throughout the training process, enabling the Transformer to discern both independent and interrelated patterns within the data.

The following sections outline the entire pipeline, including ICA decomposition, entropy-based component selection, and Transformer initialization.

Entropy-based ICA component selection

Entropy measures the statistical variability of each independent component, with increased entropy signifying more complex and informative patterns in the data. We compute the entropy of each ICA component by assessing its normalized histogram, using it as a surrogate for its contribution to the representation of independent signals. The cumulative entropy curve (Figure 3(a)) illustrates that the initial 16 ICA components encapsulate the predominant independent information relevant for identifying high energy demand, as indicated by the diminishing returns in cumulative entropy thereafter. The individual entropy plot (Figure 3(b)) further substantiates that components above rank 16 yield diminishing contributions of fresh information. Consequently, we designate n∗=16 as the ideal quantity of ICA-derived features for model initialization, ensuring the capture of the highest peak energy demand.

The entropy analysis indicates that the initial 16 ICA components encapsulate the most statistically diverse and informative patterns within the data, likely reflecting critical behavioral and environmental factors influencing peak demand, including diurnal usage cycles, weather-related variations, and abrupt demand surges. By choosing components with maximal entropy, the model highlights the most unexpected and dynamic facets of energy use, enhancing its capacity to generalize and adapt to the variability intrinsic to real-world demand profiles.

FastICA-Transformers customization

The FastICA-Transformer is tailored via an adaptive combination of ICA-based feature extraction and model depth selection informed by MAE. Initially, FastICA decomposes the input data into independent components, from which the most informative are chosen based on an entropy-based criterion. The components are directly correlated with the Transformer’s attention heads, utilizing ICA-derived mixing weights to initialize the Q, K, and V matrices, so ensuring that each head analyzes a unique and significant feature. The model’s depth is adaptively adjusted for each forecasting horizon by progressively increasing the number of encoder layers and choosing the depth that results in the minimum validation MAE. This dual-phase modification allows the FastICA-Transformer to optimize complexity and precision, yielding a model that is both efficient and exceptionally effective in various energy demand forecasting tasks.

Our entropy heuristic selects the component count (16 as in Fig. 3) that maximizes information, while a depth search (depth 4 for hour/day/week, depth 2 for the quarterly horizon as shown in Fig. 4) optimally balances bias and variation. As a result, FastICA-Transformer is compact (≤ 2 M parameters), and achieves the lowest MAE, RMSE, and MAPE, as detailed in the subsequent section.

FastICA-Transformers against PCA-Transformers

The PCA-transformer produces orthogonal directions of principal components that continue to combine latent sources; hence, each PCA-based head receives a mixed signal and must learn to separate it. FastICA optimizes statistical independence, separating driving demand, diurnal cycles, and stochastic spikes into discrete components. We compare the performance of our FastICA-Transformer with that of the previous work PCA-Transformer across four forecasting tasks—Next-Hour, Next-Day, Next-Week, and Next-Quarter—assessed using MAE, RMSE, and MAPE. In all three panels as shown in Fig. 5, the FastICA-Transformer with a depth of 2 constantly attains the lowest error values, so affirming its superior accuracy and durability, especially for long-range forecasts. The depth = 4 variation has competitive performance and is ideal for shorter horizons; nevertheless, it is marginally outperformed in the quarter-ahead task, where depth = 2 achieves a superior bias-variance tradeoff.

Previous research have concentrated on single-horizon predictions utilizing techniques such as GRU with MIMO21, hybrid deep learning ensembles22, polynomial regression23, and VMD-Bi-GRU24; however, the FastICA-Transformer consistently achieves lower MAE and RMSE over all assessed horizons. For instance, its Mean Absolute Error (MAE) decreases from 0.21 for Next-Hour to merely 0.01 for Next-Quarter, surpassing or equaling the optimal outcomes in both short- and long-term contexts. This model has significant adaptability, with accuracy enhancing as the forecast window expands—an advantage absent in other methods. FastICA-Transformer obtains 3.73% for next-day (24-hour) and 0.9% for next-quarter (2160-hour) forecasts on household data, whereas SADE-KAN achieves 3.57% and 3.87% for 24-hour and 168-hour forecasts using ISO-NE regional data. The higher MAPE for Next-Hour forecasting (7.48%) than longer horizons shows the sensitivity of percentage-based indicators to low absolute consumption. In late-night or early-morning periods, demand may drop to < 0.5 kW, making even modest absolute mistakes appear significant as percentages. Next-Hour has the lowest MAE (0.21) and RMSE (0.31) of all horizons, showing great absolute accuracy. MAPE drops from 7.48% at 1-hour to 0.9% quarterly, while SADE-KAN worsens, indicating stronger long-term pattern capture. As the forecast window grows, the FastICA-Transformer’s peak demand estimates become more accurate, reliable, and scalable, making it ideal for energy management and grid operation26. The results highlight the FastICA-Transformer’s capacity to deliver dependable, efficient, and scalable peak demand projections, rendering it particularly advantageous for real-world energy management and grid operating issues.

Economic effect analysis

Accurate power demand forecasting has significant economic implications for both grid operators and users. Inaccurate estimates can result in energy shortages, necessitating emergency generation and diminishing productivity, or energy surplus, incurring superfluous purchase and maintenance expenses. The suggested Adaptive FastICA-Transformer enhances forecast accuracy by decreasing MAPE from 7.48% to below 1% on average, resulting in substantial financial and operational advantages.

Assuming an average short-term energy cost variance of 3% per 1% MAPE, this enhancement equates to an estimated 18% reduction in overall daily scheduling expenses for regional utilities. For utilities conducting daily energy transactions of $5 million, the enhancement results in monthly cost reductions of approximately $900,000.

Moreover, enhanced forecasting alleviates grid strain during high demand periods, resulting in diminished reserve margins and prolonged equipment longevity. This economic impact encompasses less reliance on expensive peaking plants and enhanced synchronization of generation resources with demand. The improved precision facilitates renewable energy integration, enabling more steady economic dispatch and reducing the necessity for expensive balancing reserves.

Challenges, Limitations, and future research directions

The revealed Adaptive FastICA Transformer has robust prediction performance and adaptability; nonetheless, certain practical constraints present chances for further improvement.

Model generalization and scale

The present validation emphasizes a substantial single-household dataset. While results affirm robustness over various forecast horizons, future research could investigate broader, multi-household and regional datasets to assess the model’s capacity to generalize under more different operational situations.

Computational considerations

FastICA and entropy analysis have a moderate preprocessing burden, which is deemed acceptable for offline and day-ahead planning activities. Nonetheless, enhancing these modules for real-time streaming or cloud deployment—such as via parallel ICA or incremental learning—will significantly augment scalability.

Feature selection and interpretability

The entropy-guided selection technique proficiently detects informative independent components; nevertheless, future study may explore hybrid selection metrics that integrate entropy, variance contribution, and domain expert knowledge. Furthermore, enhancing attention visualization will facilitate the connection between statistical data and physical demand drivers, hence augmenting interpretability.

Forecasting uncertainty and Multi-Objective extensions

The existing architecture aims toward deterministic forecasting. Future endeavors may incorporate uncertainty quantification through probabilistic attention methods, enabling operators to evaluate forecast confidence and economic risk concurrently.

Conclusion

This study introduced the adaptive FastICA-Transformer, which integrates entropy-guided ICA component selection with a depth-adjustable Transformer for robust peak demand forecasting. We assessed its efficacy in comparison to PCA-Transformer and sophisticated deep learning models (LSTM, TCN, N-BEATS, TFT) across Next-Hour, Next-Day, Next-Week, and Next-Quarter timeframes utilizing MAE, RMSE, and MAPE metrics. The FastICA-Transformer consistently attained the lowest error rates, particularly excelling in long-term forecasts with its depth-2 variation and in short-term workloads with depth-4. Conventional models such as LSTM and PCA-Transformer exhibited subpar performance due to their failure to distinguish independent temporal signals, while the FastICA-Transformer directly correlated ICA-derived components with attention heads, facilitating semantically significant representations. The model exhibited remarkable efficiency, attaining superior accuracy with many less parameters than benchmarks such as N-BEATS and PCA-Transformer, highlighting its scalability and applicability in real-world scenarios where precision and computational efficiency are paramount. Subsequent research will broaden the methodology to encompass multivariate and cross-domain forecasting, investigate alternative information-theoretic metrics, and improve interpretability, scalability, and uncertainty assessment.

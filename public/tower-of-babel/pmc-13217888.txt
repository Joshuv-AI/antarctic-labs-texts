Characteristics of immunoglobulin gene rearrangements in vietnamese newly diagnosed multiple myeloma patients
Authors: Cao Sy Luan, Nguyen Vu Hai Son, Phan Ngo Quang Thach, Cao Van Dong, Nguyen Quoc Dung, Trinh Thuy Duong, Phu Chi Dung, Hoang Anh Vu, Phan Thi Xinh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a hematologic malignant disease characterized by clonal proliferation of pathological plasma cells in the bone marrow, increased monoclonal immunoglobulin, and multiple end-organ damage. Nowadays, diagnosis and response assessments are based on the criteria of the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) [1,2]. Recent studies have demonstrated the role of immunoglobulin (IG) gene rearrangements in oncogenesis. This process occurs at the pro-B and pre-B stage, regarding the rearrangements of the variable (V), diversity (D), and joining (J) segments of the IG genes, including the IG heavy chain gene (IGH) and IG light chain genes (IGK and IGL). Additions, substractions, or duplications of nucleotides in the junctional regions of V(D)J sequences during the process of recombination create the uniqueness of the IG [3]. B-cell precursors migrate from the bone marrow and reach the germinal center of the lymph nodes for maturation, experiencing somatic hypermutation and class switch recombination to produce high-affinity antibodies [4].

In addition to the understanding of pathogenesis, analysis of the IG gene rearrangements provides valuable information in minimal residual disease (MRD) monitoring. Studies have shown that patients who achieved complete response (CR) and MRD negativity were associated with statistically significant benefit in prolonged overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), compared with those who achieved CR and MRD positivity [5,6]. IMWG revised the response criteria by implementing additional assessments for the detection of MRD, including the analysis of IG gene rearrangements [2]. To apply this method for MRD monitoring, it is compulsory to determine the repertoire of IG gene rearrangements and the junctional regions [7]. Data about the features of IG gene rearrangements of Vietnamese MM patients have not yet been described. Thus, this study is designed to investigate the repertoire of IG gene rearrangements and the junctional regions in newly diagnosed Vietnamese MM patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

We conducted an observational study from June 2019 to June 2023. 111 newly diagnosed MM patients at Blood Transfusion Hematology Hospital were included in the study. The diagnoses of MM were based on the IMWG criteria [1]. Risk-stratification models include the revised Interational Staging System (rISS) [8] and Mayo Clinic cytogenetic classification [9]. Written informed consents were obtained from all enrolled patients. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the University of Medicine and Pharmacy at Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam (283/HĐĐĐ-ĐHYD).

Sample collection and IG gene rearrangement analysis

Two milliliters of bone marrow aspiration from the enrolled patients at diagnosis were collected in ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid anticoagulant. DNA extraction was performed using ReliaPrep™ Blood gDNA Miniprep System (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. DNA was assessed for its quantity and purity using the NanoDrop™ 1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and was diluted to reach a concentration of 50 ng/μL. Multiplex PCR for the determination of IG gene rearrangements was designed based on the BIOMED-2 protocol [10]. Combination primers VH (FR1/FR2/FR3) – JH were used for detection of IGH rearrangements; VK – JK combination was used for IGK rearrangements; and VL – JL combination was used for IGL rearrangements. PCR products were evaluated by electrophoresis on 2% agarose gel, and were further examined with capillary electrophoresis to identify a clonal IG rearrangement when electrophoresis demonstrated a distinct dominant peak reproducibly detected within the expected size range, in contrast to the Gaussian distribution typical of a polyclonal pattern. This clonality assessment was conducted in accordance with the interpretative recommendations outlined by BIOMED-2 [10]. Clonally expressed samples were sequenced using the BigDye® Terminator v3.1 cycle sequencing kit (Applied Biosystems, USA). The sequencing products were run on an ABI 3500 Genetic Analyzer (Applied Biosystems, USA). The results were analyzed with SeqScape Software version 2.6 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and the international ImMunoGeneTics information system® (IMGT) (https://www.imgt.org/IMGT_vquest/input) for detection of repertoire of IG gene rearrangements and the junctional regions.

Data analysis

Data were imported in Microsoft Excel 2021 and were analyzed using R (version 4.3.0) and RStudio (version 2023.06.0). Fisher’s exact test was used for a statistical significance test, computing the P-value. P-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. The effect size was expressed as odds ratios (OR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). Bonferroni correction has been used to adjust the P-value to avoid type I errors.

Results

Characteristic of patients

Samples and data of 111 newly diagnosed MM patients were collected in our study. Median age at diagnosis was 61 years (range: 36 – 85 years). There were more females (63 cases) than males (48 cases) in our study. Anemia, renal failure, hypercalcemia, and bone lesions were found in 77.5%, 20.7%, 9.9% and 100% of patients, respectively. Up to 97.3% of patients had more than one site of bone damage. The rate of heavy-chain MM and light-chain only MM was 81.1% and 18.9%, respectively. According to rISS classification, 91.9% of patients were diagnosed at an advanced stage, including stage II (73.9%), III (18%). There were 28.8% of patients who expressed at least one high-risk cytogenetic abnormality. The most common immunophenotype of malignant plasma cells was CD38+CD138+CD19–CD56+ (80.2%) (Supplementary Table 1).

Characteristic of IG gene rearrangements in MM patients

Among 111 patients, 93.7% (104/111) had clonal IG gene rearrangements. Clonal IGH, IGK, and IGL rearrangements accounted for 73.0% (81/111), 82.0% (91/111), and 39.6% (44/111) of patients, respectively. There were 77 patients (69.4%) who had double clonal IG gene rearrangements, and 21 patients (18.9%) who had triple clonal IG gene rearrangements (Fig. 1).

Characteristics of IG gene rearrangements in multiple myeloma patients.

Sequencing analysis of IGH revealed that VH3 (64.2%) and VH4 (17.3%) repertoires were the two most frequent segment usages, followed by VH1 (8.6%). VH2, VH5, VH6, and VH7, which only appeared in one to three cases. Our study detected all types of DH and JH repertoire from DH1 to DH7 and JH1 to JH6. DH2 (21%) and DH3 (37%) were the two most common DH segments. JH4 was the JH segment rearrangement with the highest frequency (48.1%) (Fig. 2). In order to identify the subclass of V(D)J segments in clonal IGH rearrangement, our study invested further and figured out 26 subclasses of VH and 23 subclasses of DH. The three most common VH gene segments in our study were VH3–30 (18.5%), VH4–59 (9.9%) and VH3–21 (7.4%) (Supplementary Table 2). DH2–2 (9.9%) and DH3–16 (8.6%) were the two most common DH gene rearrangements.

IGH gene rearrangement segments in multiple myeloma patients.

IGK gene sequence analysis revealed that 134 types of rearrangement were detected in 91 patients. VK1, VK2, and VK3 were the three most frequent repertoires used in clonal IGK rearrangements (32.8%, 17.2% and 17.9%, respectively). About the JK segment, JK1 (14.9%), JK2 (12.7%), and JK4 (18.7%) were the repertoires that appeared with the highest frequency (Fig. 3). We also detected KDE segment rearrangement in 57 patients (42.5%), in which 22.4% of patients had clonal VK-KDE rearrangement and 20.1% of patients had clonal Intron-KDE rearrangement. In addition, our cohort identified 22 subclasses of VK rearrangement. VK3–20 (9.7%), VK2–30 (9%), and VK4–1 (8.2%) were the three most popular subclasses detected in our study. (Supplementary Table 3).

IGK gene rearrangement segments in multiple myeloma patients.

Regarding clonal IGL rearrangement, VL segment analysis identified 3 repertoires, VL1, VL2, and VL3, which accounted for 40.9%, 31.8% and 27.3%. Further investigation in the VL segment of our MM patients showed 14 subclasses, among which, VL1–44 (20.5%), VL2–11 (22.7%) had frequent occurrences (Supplementary Table 4). In addition, there was no patient in our study expressing JL6 rearrangement. JL3 was the most common JL segment (54.5%), followed by JL2 (29.5%) (Fig. 4).

Relation between IG gene rearrangements and characteristics of the study population

We analyzed the relation between the clonality of IG gene rearrangements and biological, clinical characteristics of our patients. Statistically significant differences in the rate of clonal IGH gene rearrangements between the heavy-chain MM group and the light-chain only MM group were noted. Myeloma patients with heavy-chain type expressed IGH gene rearrangements 6.8 times higher than the light-chain only type (OR: 6.8; 95% CI 2.2 – 22.4). In addition, the rate of clonal IGL gene rearrangements between kappa and lambda-restricted MM groups was significantly different. Lambda-restricted groups were found to contain IGL gene rearrangements 3.2 times higher than the kappa-restricted groups (OR: 3.2; 95% CI 1.3 – 7.7) (Table 1). Bonferroni correction was performed to adjust the P-value, and the P-value remained below 0.05, indicating the statistically significant difference in the rate of IGH gene rearrangements between the heavy-chain MM group and light-chain only MM group (p-value after adjustment was 0.00945). The similar trend was noted for the IGL gene rearrangements (p-value after adjustment was 0.024). There was no relation between clonal IG gene rearrangements and other clinical characteristics of patients, such as anemia, renal failure, cytogenetic stratification, and rISS stage in our study (data not shown).

Discussion

This is the first study describing the characteristics of IG gene rearrangements in Vietnamese MM patients. Using PCR and the conventional Sanger sequencing method, we could detect clonal IG gene rearrangements in 93.7% of all cases. This was not only important information that helped to distinguish between malignant and reactive plasma cells, but also a marker used to monitor MRD status after treatment [7].

Clonal IGH gene rearrangements were detected in 73% of patients in our study. This result was similar to the reports of other authors (65% ∼ 69.5%) [11–13]. Sequence analysis of the junctional regions showed that VH3 was the most common VH usage (64.2%), which was comparable to the result of Puig (62%) [12]. However, some authors reported a lower rate of VH3 segment than that of our study, including Hadzidimitriou (41.9%) [14] and Gonzalez (57.5%) [15]. VH1 segment was found in only 8.6% of patients, lower than that of Puig (12%) [12] and Hadzidimitriou (18.9%) [14]. In addition, the frequency of VH2 gene rearrangement of our patients was 2.5%, lower than that of other authors’ studies (6.5% ∼ 8.1%) [12,14] (Table 2). The subclass VH3 segment is the dominant clonal IGH gene rearrangement of normal B cells[16]. The prominence of VH3 gene rearrangement compared to other subclasses implies the germline complexity in MM [14].

The most common VH segments detected in our study were VH3–30 (18.5%), VH4–59 (9.9%), and VH3–21 (7.4%). Less common VH segments were VH3–33 (6.2%) and VH3–74 (4.9%). Kim [7] reported different results in Korean MM patients, indicating that the two most common VH gene rearrangements were VH3–9 (11.63%) and VH3–23 (11.63%). Another study of Medina [17] in 413 myeloma patients reported that VH3–30 was the most prevalent VH segment usage, followed by VH3–33, VH3–23, and VH1–69. González [4] showed that VH3–30 was the most popular VH gene rearrangement in MM patients (10%), followed by VH5–51 (5.5%), VH3–23 (5%), VH1–69 (5%), and VH3–15 (4.7%). VH3–09, VH4–59, and VH3–11 were less common [4]. Our study reported that VH4–59 was the second common VH gene rearrangement, and the frequency of VH4–59 in our cohort was 9.9%, higher than the results reported by other authors, such as Hadzidimitriou [14] (4.05%) and González [15] (5.5%). Besides, the VH1–69 segment was not encountered in our data but was found in other research with a rate of 4.7 ∼ 5% [14].

DH analysis showed that DH3 (37%) and DH2 (21%) were the most common DH gene rearrangements in our patients, similar to the studies of Puig [12], Medina [17] and Kim [7]. Some studies have indicated that the stability and uniqueness of IGH gene rearrangement patterns may significantly influence the result of molecular MRD assays in MM [3,17]. Medina et al. comprehensively demonstrated the robust prognostic relevance of IGH molecular profiling, suggesting that these rearrangement patterns act as independent prognostic markers. Specifically, they reported that the use of DH2 and DH3 gene groups was independently associated with a significantly PFS rate (HR: 0.552, p = 0.006), whereas DH4 usage was linked to poorer outcomes. Given that our cohort also exhibited a high prevalence of DH3 (37%) and DH2 (21%), it raises the compelling possibility that a substantial proportion of our patients might belong to a more favorable prognostic subgroup. Therefore, future prospective tracking with survival analysis is warranted to determine whether these specific IGH gene rearrangement patterns in Vietnamese MM patients are intimately associated with MRD negativity rates and overall therapeutic response.

JH4 and JH6 were the two most common types of JH repertoire in our study, accounting for 48.1% and 22.2%, respectively. These results are similar to the results of Puig [12] (51.2% and 20.9%). JH3 (14.8%) and JH5 (8.6%) were the next most frequent JH types, in which JH3 had a higher rate but JH5 had a lower rate than the data reported by Puig [12] (9.3% and 15.1%, respectively). JH1 and JH2 were two uncommon types of JH rearrangement, similar to other studies [12,13]. Diversity in the characteristics of clonal IGH gene rearrangement patterns in MM patients could be explained by ethnic factors, which would affect prognosis and be a marker to help monitor MRD status.

Regarding the IGK gene, 82% of patients had clonal IGK gene rearrangements, higher than the results of the studies conducted by Hadzidimitriou (68.2%) [14] and Kim (79.2%) [7]. Among IGK repertoire rearrangements, VK1 (32.8%), VK2 (17.2%), and VK3 (17.9%) are the three most common types of VK segment usage, followed by VK4, VK5, and VK7, accounting for 8.2%, 2.2% and 1.5% respectively. The frequencies of VK1 and VK3 were similar, whereas the rate of VK2 was higher, and VK4 was lower than those of Hadzidimitriou [14] (VK2 12.68% and VK4 22.54%). VK6 was a type of VK segment that has been detected neither in our report nor in other authors’ studies [7].

Further analysis of VK gene rearrangement showed that VK2–30 (11.2%), VK3–20 (12.1%), and VK4–1 (10.3%) accounted for the highest proportion. It could be seen that the rate of VK4–1 in Kim's study was significantly lower than in our study. In addition, there was a similarity of our JK4 frequency with the results of Kim (18.7%vs 20%). JK3 (3.7%) was the least common JK segment in our study, which was different from Kim’s study, reporting that JK5 (3.53%) was the least frequent [7].

In terms of the IGL gene, 39.6% of patients had clonal IGL rearrangements, similar to the study of Hadzidimitriou (42.6%). In our study, the most frequent in VL segments were VL1 (40.9%) and VL2–11 (22.7%), but those in the study of Hadzidimitriou were VL3 (44.2%) và VL3–1 (20.9%). In case of JL rearrangement, JL2 (29.5%) and JL3 (54.5%) were the most predominant. This result is similar to the study of Hadzidimitriou [14] (the total of JL2 and JL3 rearrangement were 86.04%).

When analyzing the relation between clonal IG gene rearrangements and other clinical, biological factors, we found that more heavy chain secreting MM patients had clonal IGH gene rearrangements than the light chain MM patients. This result indicates that the cells without IGH gene rearrangement may lead to non-secretion of the heavy chains in MM patients. The pathogenesis of light chain MM has not yet been elucidated, although several hypotheses have been proposed to explain the absence of heavy chain proteins in the peripheral blood, including the absence of a functional gene after IGH gene rearrangement, the abnormal mRNA transcription of the IGH gene, the destabilization of mRNA, and the unability of the mRNA to translate into heavy chain proteins [4]. Magrangeas et al. [18] have suggested that in most cases of MM, one IGH allele retains germline structure (at least for D, J, and C segments), while the second allele undergoes a chromosomal translocation leading to the production of a non-functional IGH gene. Regarding prognostic implications, previous studies have indicated that light-chain only MM often presents with higher rates of renal impairment and may have different survival outcomes compared to heavy-chain MM. The differences in IG rearrangement patterns between these two groups, as observed in our cohort, might reflect distinct underlying biological profiles. Integrating these rearrangement characteristics with clinical prognostic markers could provide further insights into treatment responses and survival outcomes in future prospective studies. In addition, we also found that the rate of clonal IGL gene rearrangements in lambda-restricted MM patients was statistically higher than in kappa-restricted MM patients (Table 1). This result could be explained that the IGK gene rearrangement process is complete, creating a functional IGK gene, and the plasma cells will differentiate into kappa-secreting plasma cells. Conversely, if the initial IGK gene rearrangement process fails to create a functional IGK gene, the IGL gene rearrangement process takes place and creates plasma cells capable of lambda secretion.

Conclusion

We have detected clonal IGH/or IGK, and IGL gene rearrangements in 93.7% of Vietnamese MM patients at diagnosis, indicating that multiplex PCR can detect clonal IG gene rearrangements in the majority of MM patients. This study is the first in Vietnam to comprehensively describe the characteristics of IG gene rearrangements in MM patients. It provides specific molecular markers for assessing clonality and has the potential to serve as patient-specific targets for MRD monitoring in MM after treatment. The differences in characteristics of clonal IG gene rearrangements compared to previous reports suggest that ethnic factors might influence these patterns, although these variations could also be attributed to differences in detection methodologies or sample sizes. Further large-scale and standardized comparative studies are needed to clarify this.

Averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra and induced perm bialgebras
Bo Hou, Zhanpeng Cui

Abstract
. We establish a bialgebra theory for averaging algebras, called averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras by generalizing the study of antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras to the context of averaging algebras. They are characterized by double constructions of averaging Frobenius algebras as well as matched pairs of averaging algebras. Antisymmetric solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in averaging algebras provide averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. The notions of an 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator of an averaging algebra and an averaging dendriform algebra are introduced to construct antisymmetric solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in an averaging algebra and hence averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. Moreover, we introduce the notion of factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras and show that a factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra leads to a factorization of the underlying averaging algebra. We establish a one-to-one correspondence between factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras and symmetric averaging Frobenius algebras with a Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight. Finally, we apply the study of averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras to perm bialgebras, extending the construction of perm algebras from commutative averaging algebras to the context of bialgebras, which is consistent with the well constructed theory of perm bialgebras.

1.
Introduction

The notion of averaging operator was first implicitly studied by Reynolds
in the turbulence theory of fluid dynamics
[
26
]
. Kampé de Fériet
introduced explicitly the averaging operator in the context
of turbulence theory and functional analysis
[
17
,
22
]
.
Moy investigated averaging operators from the viewpoint of conditional expectation
in probability theory
[
23
]
. Kelley and Rota studied
the role of averaging operators in Banach algebras
[
19
,
27
]
.
The algebraic study on averaging operators began in
[
7
]
. Cao constructed explicitly
free unitary commutative averaging algebras and discovered the Lie algebra structures
induced naturally from averaging operators. In
[
24
]
, Guo and Pei studied averaging
operators from an algebraic and combinatorial point of view, and constructed free nonunital
averaging algebras in terms of a class of bracketed words called averaging words.
In
[
14
]
, Gao and Zhang contain an explicit construction of free unital averaging
algebras in terms of bracketed polynomials and the main tools were rewriting systems
and Gröbner-Shirshov bases. The averaging operators attract much attention also
because of their closely connected with Reynolds operators, symmetric operators and
Rota-Baxter operators
[
6
,
13
,
30
]
.

An averaging algebra is an algebra with an averaging operator.
In recent years, the properties of averaging algebra have been widely studied.
Sheng, Tang and Zhu studied embedding tensors (another name of averaging operators
in physics) for Lie algebras and they construct a cohomology theory for such operators
on Lie algebras using derived brackets as a main tool
[
28
]
.
In
[
31
,
9
,
11
]
the authors study the cohomological theory, homotopy theory and
non-abelian extensions for averaging associative algebras. The averaging operators
on various algebraic structures and the induced structures have been studied in
[
10
]
.
The aim of this paper is to develop a bialgebra theory for averaging (associative)
algebras and get some applications. The notion of averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal
bialgebras is introduced. Some special averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal
bialgebras are studied. As an application, we generalize the typical construction
of perm algebras from averaging algebras to the context of bialgebras.

A bialgebra structure consists of an algebra structure and a coalgebra structure coupled by certain compatibility conditions. Such structures have connections with
other structures arising from mathematics and physics. Lie bialgebras are the algebra
structures of Poisson-Lie groups and play an important role in the study of
quantized universal enveloping algebras
[
12
,
8
]
. Antisymmetric infinitesimal
bialgebras for associative algebras were introduced by Bai in order to establish
the connection with the double constructions of Frobenius algebras and matched
pairs of associative algebras in
[
2
]
. In this paper, we establish a bialgebra
theory for averaging associative algebras, called averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal
bialgebras, by extending the study of antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras in
[
2
]

to the context of averaging algebras. Explicitly, averaging antisymmetric
infinitesimal bialgebras are characterized equivalently by matched pairs of averaging
algebras and double constructions of averaging Frobenius algebras, as the
generalizations of matched pairs of algebras and double constructions of Frobenius
algebras respectively to the context of averaging algebras. The coboundary cases
lead to introduce the notion of
\beta
-Yang-Baxter equation in an averaging
algebra, whose antisymmetric solutions are used to construct averaging antisymmetric
infinitesimal bialgebras. The notions of
\mathcal{O}
-operators of averaging
algebras and averaging dendriform algebras are introduced to construct antisymmetric
solutions of the
\beta
-Yang-Baxter equation in averaging algebras and
hence give rise to averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras.

Quasitriangular Lie bialgebras and triangular Lie bialgebras are important Lie
bialgebras classes. Another important Lie bialgebras are factorizable Lie bialgebras,
which is introduced in
[
25
]
. Factorizable Lie bialgebras are used to
establish the relation between classical
r
-matrices and certain factorization
problems in Lie algebras. Recently, factorizable Lie bialgebras and factorizable
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras have been further studied in
[
20
,
29
]
.
Here we study
factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. We show that the
factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras give rise to a natural
factorization of the underlying averaging algebras. The importance
of factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras in the study of
averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras can also be observed from the fact
that the double space of an arbitrary averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras
admits a factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra structure.

Furthermore, as an application, we construct perm bialgebras from some special averaging
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. We have studied a bialgebra theory for perm
algebras in
[
16
]
, also see
[
21
]
. For any commutative averaging algebra,
the averaging operator induces a perm algebra structure on the original vector space.
It is natural to consider extending such a relationship to the context of bialgebras.
We establish the explicit relationships between averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal
bialgebras and the induced perm bialgebras, as well as the equivalent interpretation in
terms of the corresponding Manin triples and matched pairs. We show that a solution

\beta
-YBE in a commutative averaging algebra is also a solution of YBE in the induced
perm algebra under certain conditions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some facts
on averaging algebras and bimodules over averaging algebras. In Section
3
,
we give the general notions of matched pairs of averaging algebras, double construction
of averaging Frobenius algebras and averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras.
Whenever the underlying linear spaces of the two averaging algebras are dual to each
other, their equivalence is interpreted. In Section
4
, we consider a
special class of averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras, the coboundary
averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. This study also leads to the
introduction of the Yang-Baxter equation in an averaging algebra, whose antisymmetric
solutions give averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras.
We also introduce the notions of
\mathcal{O}
-operators of averaging algebras and
averaging dendriform algebras, and give constructions of antisymmetric solutions of
the Yang-Baxter equation in an averaging algebra from these structures.
In Section
5
, we introduce the notion of factorizable
averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras and show that a factorizable
averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra leads to a factorization of the
underlying averaging algebra. We establish a one-to-one correspondence between
factorizable averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras and symmetric averaging
Frobenius algebras with a Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight.
In Section
6
, proceeding from the typical construction of perm algebras
from commutative averaging algebras, we construct perm bialgebras from commutative and
cocommutative averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras. The explicit
relationships between them, as well as the equivalent interpretation in terms
of the corresponding Manin triples and matched pairs, are established.

a field and characteristic zero.
All the vector spaces and algebras are of finite dimension over
\Bbbk
,

2.
Averaging algebras and their bimodule

In this section, we recall the background on averaging algebras and bimodules over
averaging algebras, for that details, see

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an associative algebra. An
averaging operator
on
A

is a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
such that

\alpha(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2})=\alpha(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2})=\alpha(a_{1}\alpha(a_{2})),

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. A triple
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
consisting of an associative algebra

A
and an averaging operator
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
is called an
averaging
(associative) algebra
. We often denote this averaging algebra by
(A,\alpha)
for simply.

Given two averaging algebras
(A,\alpha)
and
(A^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
, a
homomorphism of
averaging algebras
from
(A,\alpha)
to
(A^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is a homomorphism of algebras

f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
satisfying
f\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}f
. A homomorphism

f:(A,\alpha)\rightarrow(A^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is said to be an
isomorphism
if

f
is a bijection. A subalgebra
B
of associative algebra
A
with a linear map

\beta:B\rightarrow B
is called a subalgebra of averaging algebras
(A,\alpha)

if
\beta
is just the restriction of
\alpha
on
B
.

Example 2.2
.

(i)
Let
A=\Bbbk\{e\}
be a 1-dimensional associative algebra. Note that
any scalar multiple transformation on
A
is an averaging operator, we get that
every linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
is an averaging operator.

(ii)
Let
A=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be a 2-dimensional associative algebra
with non-zero product
e_{1}e_{2}=e_{1}
and
e_{2}e_{2}=e_{2}
.
Then, up to isomorphism, all the non-zero averaging operators are given by

(\mathrm{a})

\alpha(e_{1})=0
,
\alpha(e_{2})=e_{1}
;

(\mathrm{b})

\alpha(e_{1})=0
,
\alpha(e_{2})=e_{2}
;

(\mathrm{c})

\alpha(e_{1})=0
,
\alpha(e_{2})=e_{2}+ae_{1}
,
0\neq a\in\Bbbk
;

(\mathrm{d})

\alpha(e_{1})=e_{1}
,
\alpha(e_{2})=e_{2}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. We define two new binary operations

\bullet,\star:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
by

a_{1}\bullet a_{2}=\alpha(a_{1})a_{2},\qquad\quad\mbox{and}\qquad\quad a_{1}\star a_{2}=a_{1}\alpha(a_{2}),

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, then
A_{1}=(A,\bullet)
and
A_{2}=(A,\star)

are associative algebras,
(A_{1},\alpha)
and
(A_{2},\alpha)
are averaging algebras,
and
(A,\bullet,\star)
is a dialgebra
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A (left)
perm algebra
is a vector space
P
with a bilinear operation
(p_{1},p_{2})\mapsto p_{1}p_{2}
, such that
p_{1}(p_{2}p_{3})=(p_{1}p_{2})p_{3}=(p_{2}p_{1})p_{3}
,
for any
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in P
.

Clearly, perm algebra is a special class of associative algebra.
An averaging algebra is called commutative if it as an associative algebra
is commutative. We can construct perm algebras from commutative averaging algebras.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any commutative averaging algebra
(A,\alpha)
, we have a perm algebra

(A,\bullet)
, where the product
\bullet
is defined in Example
2.3
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra,
M
be a vector space.
M
is called a

\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(M)
,
such that
(a_{1}a_{2})m=a_{1}(a_{2}m)
,

, for any

and

, where
a_{1}m:=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})(m)
and

.
We denote this bimodule by

. Clearly, for any
associative algebra

,

is a bimodule over itself,
where
\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})a_{2}=a_{1}a_{2}
and
\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})a_{2}=a_{2}a_{1}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. A
bimodule

(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r},\beta)

over the averaging algebra

is a bimodule

over associative

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(a))(\beta(m))

\displaystyle=\beta(\mathfrak{l}(\alpha(a))(m))=\beta(\mathfrak{l}(a)(\beta(m))),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(\alpha(a))(\beta(m))

\displaystyle=\beta(\mathfrak{r}(\alpha(a))(m))=\beta(\mathfrak{r}(a)(\beta(m))).

Given two bimodules

(N,\beta^{\prime})
over averaging algebra
(A,\alpha)
,

from
(M,\beta)

If the homomorphism

The averaging algebra

3.
Averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras

In this section, we introduce the notions of a double construction of averaging
Frobenius algebra and an averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra, and give
their equivalence in terms of matched pairs of averaging algebras.

3.1.
Matched pairs of averaging algebras

We first recall the concept of a matched pair of associative algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

A
matched pair of associative algebras
consists of two associative algebras

A
,
B
, and linear maps
\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(B)
and

\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B}:B\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(A)
, such that
(A\oplus B,\ast)
is
also an associative algebra, where
\ast
is defined by

\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2})=\Big(a_{1}a_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{B}(b_{1})(a_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{B}(b_{2})(a_{1}),\quad b_{1}b_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{A}(a_{1})(b_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{A}(a_{2})(b_{1})\Big),

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. The matched pair is denoted by

(A,B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
and the resulting algebra is denoted
by
A\bowtie B
.

For a matched pair of associative algebras
(A,B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
,
it is easy to see that
(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a bimodule over
B
and

(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A})
is a bimodule over
A
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
be two averaging algebras.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(B)
and
\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B}:B\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(A)
are linear maps.
If the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B},\alpha)
is a bimodule over averaging algebra

(B,\beta)
;

(ii)

(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over averaging algebra

(A,\alpha)
;

(iii)

(A,B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a matched pair of
associative algebras,

then
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is called a

matched pair of averaging algebras
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
be two averaging algebras.
Suppose that
(A,B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},

\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a matched pair
of associative algebras. Then
(A\bowtie B,\alpha\oplus\beta)
is an averaging
algebra if and only if
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a matched pair of averaging algebras.
Further, any averaging algebra whose underlying vector space is the linear direct sum
of two averaging subalgebras is obtained from a matched pair of these
two averaging subalgebras.

Proof.

Suppose that
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a
matched pair of averaging algebras. Then, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

b_{1},b_{2}\in B
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2})

\displaystyle=\Big(\alpha(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2})+\mathfrak{l}_{B}(\beta(b_{1}))(\alpha(a_{2}))+\mathfrak{r}_{B}(\beta(b_{2}))(\alpha(a_{1})),

\displaystyle\qquad\beta(b_{1})\beta(b_{2})+\mathfrak{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{1}))(\beta(b_{2}))+\mathfrak{r}_{A}(\alpha(a_{2}))(\beta(b_{1}))\Big),

\displaystyle(\alpha\oplus\beta)((\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2}))

\displaystyle=\Big(\alpha(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2})+\alpha(\mathfrak{l}_{B}(\beta(b_{1}))(a_{2}))+\alpha(\mathfrak{r}_{B}(b_{2})(\alpha(a_{1}))),

\displaystyle\qquad\beta(\beta(b_{1})b_{2})+\beta(\mathfrak{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{1}))(b_{2}))+\beta(\mathfrak{r}_{A}(a_{2})(\beta(b_{1})))\Big),

\displaystyle(\alpha\oplus\beta)((a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2}))

\displaystyle=\Big(\alpha(a_{1}\alpha(a_{2}))+\alpha(\mathfrak{l}_{B}(b_{1})(\alpha(a_{2})))+\alpha(\mathfrak{r}_{B}(\beta(b_{2}))(a_{1})),

\displaystyle\qquad\beta(b_{1}\beta(b_{2}))+\beta(\mathfrak{l}_{A}(a_{1})(\beta(b_{2})))+\beta(\mathfrak{r}_{A}(\alpha(a_{2}))(b_{1}))\Big).

Since
(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B},\alpha)
is a bimodule over
(B,\beta)
and

(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
, we get

(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2})=(\alpha\oplus\beta)((\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2}))=(\alpha\oplus\beta)((a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2}))
. Thus,

\alpha\oplus\beta
is an averaging operator on
A\bowtie B
, and so that,

(A\bowtie B,\alpha\oplus\beta)
is an averaging algebra.

Conversely, if
\alpha\oplus\beta
is an averaging operator on
A\bowtie B
,
i.e.,
(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2})=(\alpha\oplus\beta)((\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2}))=(\alpha\oplus\beta)((a_{1},b_{1})\ast(\alpha\oplus\beta)(a_{2},b_{2}))
, for any

a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. From the above calculation,
taking
a_{1}=b_{2}=0
and
a_{2}=b_{1}=0
in the above equation respectively, we get
that
(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B},\alpha)
is a bimodule over
(B,\beta)
and

(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
. Hence,

((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a matched pair
of averaging algebras. Finally, the second part follows straightforwardly.
∎

3.2.
Double constructions of averaging Frobenius algebras

We recall the concept of a double construction of Frobenius algebra

Definition 3.4

Let
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
be a bilinear form on an associative algebra
A
.

-

\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is called
nondegenerate
if
\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;a_{2})=0
for any
a_{2}\in A
, then
a_{1}=0
;

-

\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is called
invariant
if
\mathfrak{B}(a_{1}a_{2},\;a_{3})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;a_{2}a_{3})
, for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
;

-

\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is called
symmetric
if
\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;a_{2})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{2},\;a_{1})
, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

A
Frobenius algebra

(A,\mathfrak{B})
is an associative algebra
A
with a
nondegenerate invariant bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
. A Frobenius algebra

(A,\mathfrak{B})
is called
symmetric
if
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is symmetric.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Suppose that there is an associative algebra structure

\cdot
on its dual space
A^{\ast}
and an associative algebra structure
\ast
on the
direct sum
A\oplus A^{\ast}
of the underlying vector spaces
A
and
A^{\ast}
,
which contains both
A
and
A^{\ast}
as subalgebras. Then the associative algebra is
just the associative algebra
A\bowtie A^{\ast}
, corresponding to the matched pair

(A,A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
. Define a bilinear form on
A\oplus A^{\ast}
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{d}\Big((a_{1},\xi_{1}),\;(a_{2},\xi_{2})\big):=\langle\xi_{2},\;a_{1}\rangle+\langle\xi_{1},\;a_{2}\rangle,

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
.
If
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
is a symmetric Frobenius algebra,
then it is called a double construction of Frobenius algebra associated to
A
and

A^{\ast}
, and denoted by
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
.

We extend these notions to the context of averaging algebras.

Definition 3.5
.

An
averaging Frobenius algebra
is a triple
(A,\alpha,\mathfrak{B})
,
where
(A,\alpha)
is an averaging algebra and
(A,\mathfrak{B})
is a Frobenius algebra.
It is called
symmetric
if the bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is symmetric.
A linear map
\hat{\alpha}:A\rightarrow A
is called
the adjoint linear operator
of
\alpha
under the nondegenerate bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
, if

\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(a_{1}),\;a_{2})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;\hat{\alpha}(a_{2})),\qquad

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(A,\alpha,\mathfrak{B})
be a symmetric averaging Frobenius algebra,
and
\hat{\alpha}
be the adjoint of
\alpha
with respect to
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
.
Then,
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\hat{\alpha}^{\ast})
is
a bimodule over the averaging algebra
(A,\alpha)
, and as bimodules over

(A,\alpha)
,
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\alpha)
and
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\hat{\alpha}^{\ast})
are isomorphic.
Moreover, let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra, and
\beta:A\rightarrow A
be
a linear map. If
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
is
a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
, and it is isomorphic to the regular bimodule

(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\alpha)
, then there exists a nondegenerate invariant
bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}
on
A
such that
\beta=\hat{\alpha}
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
(A,\alpha,\mathfrak{B})
is a symmetric averaging Frobenius algebra.
For any
a_{1},a_{2}
,
a_{3}\in A
, note that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(a_{1})\hat{\alpha}(a_{2}),\;a_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(a_{3}\alpha(a_{1})),\;a_{2}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(\hat{\alpha}(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}),\;a_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(a_{3})\alpha(a_{1}),\;a_{2}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(\hat{\alpha}(a_{1}\hat{\alpha}(a_{2})),\;a_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(\alpha(a_{3})a_{1}),\;a_{2}),

we get
\mathbf{l}(\alpha(a_{1}))(\hat{\alpha}(a_{2}))=\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{l}(\alpha(a_{1}))(a_{2}))=\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{l}(a_{1})(\hat{\alpha}(a_{2})))
. Similarly, we have

\mathbf{r}(\alpha(a_{1}))(\hat{\alpha}(a_{2}))=

\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{r}(\alpha(a_{1}))(a_{2}))=\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{r}(a_{1})(\hat{\alpha}(a_{2})))
. Thus,
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\hat{\alpha})
is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
. By Proposition
2.8
,
we get
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\hat{\alpha}^{\ast})
is
a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
.

Define a linear map
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\ast}
by

\varphi(a_{1})(a_{2})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},a_{2}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. Then,
\varphi
is a linear isomorphism.
Moreover, for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1}a_{2},\;a_{3})=\langle\varphi(a_{2}),\;a_{3}a_{1}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(a_{1})(\varphi(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(a_{2}a_{1},\;a_{3})=\langle\varphi(a_{2}),\;a_{1}a_{3}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(a_{1})(\varphi(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(\alpha(a_{1})),\;a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(\alpha(a_{1}),\;a_{2})=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;\hat{\alpha}(a_{2}))=\langle\hat{\alpha}^{\ast}(\varphi(a_{1})),\;a_{2}\rangle.

Hence,
\varphi
is an isomorphism.

Second, suppose that
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\ast}
is the isomorphism
from
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\alpha)
to
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
. Define a bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
on
A
by

\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},a_{2}):=\langle\varphi(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle,

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
Then by a similar argument as above, we show that
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is a nondegenerate
invariant bilinear form on
A
such that
\beta=\hat{\alpha}
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. Suppose that there is a linear map

\beta:A\rightarrow A
and a bilinear map
\cdot^{\prime}:A^{\ast}\otimes A^{\ast}\rightarrow A^{\ast}
such that
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime},\beta^{\ast})
is an averaging algebra.
A
double construction of averaging Frobenius algebra associated
to
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
and
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime},\beta^{\ast})
is a double
construction of Frobenius algebra
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})

associated to
A
and
A^{\ast}
such that
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
is an averaging algebra, which is denoted by

(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
be a double
construction of averaging Frobenius algebra associated to
(A,\cdot,\alpha)

and
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime}\beta^{\ast})
. Then,

(i)

The adjoint
\widehat{\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast}}
of
\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast}

with respect to
\mathfrak{B}_{d}(-,-)
is
\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast}
, and
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})

is a bimodule over
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
;

(ii)

(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
;

(iii)

(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}},\alpha^{\ast})
is a
bimodule over
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.

Proof.

(i)
For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{d}((\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})(a_{1},\xi_{1}),\;(a_{2},\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(a_{1}),\;\xi_{2}\rangle+\langle a_{2},\;\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1},\;\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rangle+\langle\beta(a_{2}),\;\xi_{1}\rangle=\mathfrak{B}_{d}((a_{1},\xi_{1}),\;(\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})(a_{2},\xi_{2})).

That is to say, the adjoint of
\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast}
with respect to

\mathfrak{B}_{d}
is
\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast}
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.6
,
we get
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})
is a bimodule over
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
.

(ii)
and
(iii)
Since
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A\bowtie A^{\ast}},\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})
is a bimodule over
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
,
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha(a_{1}),\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1}))\ast(\beta(a_{2}),\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2}))

\displaystyle=(\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})\big((\alpha(a_{1}),\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1}))\ast(a_{2},\xi_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=(\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})\big((a_{1},\xi_{1})\ast(\beta(a_{2}),\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\big),

\displaystyle(\beta(a_{2}),\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2}))\ast(\alpha(a_{1}),\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1}))

\displaystyle=(\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})\big((a_{2},\xi_{2})\ast(\alpha(a_{1}),\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=(\beta\oplus\alpha^{\ast})\big((\beta(a_{2}),\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2}))\ast(a_{1},\xi_{1})\big).

Taking
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=0
in the above equations,
we get that
(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
,
and taking
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
, we get that
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}},\alpha^{\ast})
is a bimodule over
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. Suppose that there is a linear map

\beta:A\rightarrow A
and a bilinear map
\cdot^{\prime}:A^{\ast}\otimes A^{\ast}\rightarrow A^{\ast}
such that
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime},\beta^{\ast})
is an averaging algebra.
Then there is a double construction of averaging Frobenius algebra

(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
associated to

(A,\alpha)
and
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
if and only if

((A,\alpha),(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}),\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast}
,
\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
is a matched pair of averaging algebras.

Proof.

If
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
is a double construction of averaging Frobenius
algebra associated to
(A,\alpha)
and
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
, by

[
2
, Theorem 2.2.1]
,
(A,A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
is a matched pair of associative algebras.
And by Lemma
3.8
,
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})

is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
and
(A,\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\alpha)
is a bimodule over
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
, respectively.
Hence
((A,\alpha),(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}),\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
is a matched pair of averaging algebras.

Conversely, if
((A,\alpha),(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}),\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
is a matched pair of averaging algebras,
by
[
2
, Theorem 2.2.1]
again,
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
is a
Frobenius algebra. Moreover by Proposition
3.3
,
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
is an averaging algebra.
Hence
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast},\mathfrak{B}_{d})
is a
double construction of averaging Frobenius algebra associated to
(A,\alpha)

and
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.
∎

3.3.
Averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras

Recall that a
coassociative coalgebra

(A,\Delta)
is a vector space
A

with a linear map
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
satisfying the coassociative law:

(\Delta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\Delta)\Delta.

A coassociative coalgebra
(A,\Delta)
is called
cocommutative
if

\Delta=\tau\Delta
, where
\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the
flip operator defined by
\tau(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}):=a_{2}\otimes a_{1}
for
all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Definition 3.10

(
[
2
]
)
.

An
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra
or simply an
ASI bialgebra
is
a triple
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
consisting of a vector space
A
and linear maps

\cdot:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
such that

(i)

(A,\cdot)
is an associative algebra;

(ii)

(A,\Delta)
is a coassociative coalgebra;

(iii)

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Delta(a_{1}a_{2})=(\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1}))\Delta(a_{2}),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{1})\big)\Delta(a_{2})=\tau\big(\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{r}_{A}(a_{2})-\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)\Delta(a_{1})\big).

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(A,\Delta)
be a coassociative coalgebra.
A linear map
\beta:A\rightarrow A
is called an
averaging operator
on

(A,\Delta)
if
(\beta\otimes\beta)\Delta=(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta\beta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\beta)\Delta\beta
.

An
averaging coalgebra
is a triple
(A,\Delta,\beta)
,
consisting of a coassociative coalgebra
(A,\Delta)
and an averaging operator

\beta:A\rightarrow A
. An averaging coalgebra
(A,\Delta,\beta)
is called

cocommutative
if
(A,\Delta)
is cocommutative.

The notion of an averaging coalgebra is the dualization of the notion of an
averaging algebra, that is,
(A,\Delta,\beta)
is an (cocommutative) averaging
coalgebra if and only if
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
is an
(commutative) averaging algebra, where
\langle\Delta^{\ast}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\;a\rangle=\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2},\;\Delta(a)\rangle=\sum_{(a)}\xi_{1}(a_{(1)})\xi_{1}(a_{(1)})

for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
and
a\in A
, if
\Delta(a)=\sum_{(a)}a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
.

Definition 3.12
.

An
averaging antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra
or simply an
averaging
ASI bialgebra
is a quadruple
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)
satisfying

-

(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is an averaging algebra;

-

(A,\Delta,\beta)
is an averaging coalgebra;

-

(A,\cdot,\Delta)
is an ASI bialgebra;

-

(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A},\beta)
is a bimodule over
(A,\cdot,

\alpha)
, and

(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}},\alpha^{\ast})
is a bimodule over

(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},

\beta^{\ast})
.

An averaging ASI bialgebra
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)
is called
commutative
and cocommutative
if
A
is a commutative associative algebra and
(A,\Delta)
is
a cocommutative coassociative coalgebra.

Example 3.13
.

(i)
Let
(A,\alpha)
be the 2-dimensional averaging algebra considered in
Example
2.2
, that is,
A=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, that non-zero product is
given by
e_{1}e_{2}=e_{1}
,
e_{2}e_{2}=e_{2}
, and the averaging operator is given by

\alpha(e_{1})=0
and
\alpha(e_{2})=e_{2}
. Now we define a comultiplication on
A

by

(ii)

,

Since the multiplication
\Delta^{\ast}
of averaging algebra

is
the dual of comultiplication
\Delta
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
(A,\Delta,\beta)
be an averaging coalgebra, and
\alpha:A\rightarrow A

be a linear map. Then
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}},\alpha^{\ast})

is a bimodule over
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
if and only if for any

(\beta\otimes\alpha)\Delta=(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta\alpha=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\alpha)\Delta\alpha,\qquad\quad(\alpha\otimes\beta)\Delta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\beta)\Delta\alpha=(\alpha\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta\alpha.

Proof.

First, by the definition,
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}},\alpha^{\ast})

is a bimodule over
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
if and only if

\displaystyle\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\ast}(\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{2})=\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})),

\displaystyle\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1}))=\alpha^{\ast}(\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{1}),

for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
. Rewriting the above equations in
terms of the comultiplication, we get this lemma.
∎

Let
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
be an ASI bialgebra. A pair
(\alpha,\beta)

in Definition
3.12
is called
a pair of averaging operators

on
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
. A linear map

is called an

on

is both

, and so that,

is an averaging ASI bialgebra, if

is an
averaging operator on ASI bialgebra
(A,\cdot,\Delta)
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. Suppose that there are linear maps
\beta:A\rightarrow A
and
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
such that
(A,\Delta,\beta)

is an averaging coalgebra. Then the quadruple

is the dual algebra of

(A,\Delta,\beta)
.

Proof.

If the quadruple
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)

(A,A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})

(A^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})

is a bimodule over
(A,\alpha)
, and
(A,\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\alpha)

. Hence,
((A,\alpha),(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}),\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})

Conversely, if
((A,\alpha),(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast}),\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})
is a matched pair of averaging algebras,
then
(A,A^{\ast}
,
\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\mathbf{r}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A^{\ast}}^{\ast})

is an ASI bialgebra. Moreover, by the definition of matched pair of

Combining Propositions
3.9
and

,

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra. Suppose that there is a linear map

\beta:A\rightarrow A
and a bilinear map
\cdot^{\prime}:A^{\ast}\otimes A^{\ast}\rightarrow A^{\ast}
such that
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime},\beta^{\ast})
is an averaging algebra.
Let
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
denote the linear dual of the multiplication
on
A^{\ast}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

There is a double construction of averaging Frobenius algebra associated to

(A,\cdot,\alpha)
and
(A^{\ast},\cdot^{\prime},\beta^{\ast})

(ii)

4.

In this section, we study the coboundary averaging ASI bialgebras, and

Proposition 5.5

(A,\alpha)

r^{\natural}:(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r},\hat{\alpha}^{\ast})\rightarrow(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is also an averaging algebra homomorphism.
Thus, by Proposition
5.3
,
r
is a solution of the

\hat{\alpha}
-YBE in averaging algebra
(A,\alpha)
, and so that,

(A,\Delta,\alpha,\hat{\alpha})
is a factorizable averaging ASI bialgebra.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
.
Suppose the averaging ASI bialgebra
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)
induced by
r
is
factorizable and
\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}
. Then
-\lambda\mathop{id}\nolimits-R
is also a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on symmetric averaging Frobenius
algebra
(A,\alpha,\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}})
, where
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}

and
R
are defined in Theorem
5.9
.

Proof.

It can be obtained by direct calculations.
∎

Let
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)
and
(A^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
be two
averaging ASI bialgebras. A linear map

is called a

if

Moreover, if

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be an averaging algebra and

.
Suppose the averaging ASI bialgebra
(A,\Delta,\alpha,\beta)
induced by

. Moreover,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{I}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A

ASI bialgebra isomorphism from

Proof.

By
[

,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{I}:(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r})\rightarrow(A,\cdot_{R})

By Proposition
5.3

(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r},\beta^{\ast})

\mathcal{I}\beta^{\ast}=(r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural})\beta^{\ast}=\alpha(r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural})=\alpha\mathcal{I}
. That is,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{I}:(A^{\ast},\cdot_{r},\beta^{\ast})\rightarrow(A,\cdot_{R},\alpha)

∎

6.
Perm bialgebras via commutative and cocommutative averaging
ASI bialgebras

A perm algebra is a vector space
P
with a bilinear operation such that

p_{1}(p_{2}p_{3})=(p_{1}p_{2})p_{3}=(p_{2}p_{1})p_{3}
, for any
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in P
.
Let
A
be a commutative associative algebra and
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
be an
averaging operator on
A
. Define a new binary operations

\bullet:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
by

a_{1}\bullet a_{2}=\alpha(a_{1})a_{2},

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. Then
(A,\bullet)
is a perm algebra, which is called
the
perm algebra induced by commutative averaging algebra

(A,\alpha)
.
We generalize this construction to the context of bialgebras, that is,
we construct perm bialgebras from commutative and cocommutative averaging ASI bialgebras.
We establish the explicit relationships between them, as well as the equivalent
interpretation in terms of the corresponding double constructions and matched pairs.

In the following two sections, we always assume that
A
is a commutative
associative algebra. In this case, we use
(M,\mu)
to denote a bimodule

(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
over
A
since
\mu:=\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{r}
, and call
(M,\mu)
a module
over
A
. Then
(M^{\ast},\mu^{\ast})
is again a module over
A
.
A bimodule over commutative averaging algebra is also called module over
commutative averaging algebra.

6.1.
Induced matched pairs and induced Manin triples of perm algebras

We introduce the bimodule over a perm algebra.

Definition 6.1
.

A
bimodule of a perm algebra

(P,\bullet)
is a triple
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
, where
M
is a
vector space and
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:P\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(M)
are linear maps satisfying

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\mathfrak{l}(p_{1}\bullet p_{2})=\mathfrak{l}(p_{1})\mathfrak{l}(p_{2})=\mathfrak{l}(p_{2})\mathfrak{l}(p_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(p_{1}\bullet p_{2})=\mathfrak{r}(p_{2})\mathfrak{r}(p_{1})=\mathfrak{r}(p_{2})\mathfrak{l}(p_{1})=\mathfrak{l}(p_{1})\mathfrak{r}(p_{2}),

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
.

Let
(P,\bullet)
be a perm algebra. Define
\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{P},\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{P}:P\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(P)
by
\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{P}(p_{1})(p_{2})=p_{1}\bullet p_{2}

and
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{P}(p_{1})(p_{2})=p_{2}\bullet p_{1}
, for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
.
Obviously,
(P,\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{P},\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{P})
is a bimodule of perm algebra

(P,\bullet)
, which is called the
regular bimodule
over
P
.
More generally, for a bimodule over a perm algebra, we have

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(P,\bullet)
be a perm algebra,
M
be a vector space and
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:P\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(M)
be linear maps. Define a binary operation on
P\oplus M
by

(p_{1},m_{1})(p_{2},m_{2})=\Big(p_{1}\bullet p_{2},\;\mathfrak{l}(p_{1})(m_{2})+\mathfrak{r}(p_{2})(m_{1})\Big),

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
.
Then,
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is a bimodule over
P
if and only if
P\oplus M
with the product
as above is a perm algebra. In such a case, we call this perm algebra
the
semidirect product
perm algebra of
(P,\bullet)
by bimodule
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
,
and denote it by
P\ltimes M
.

Considering the bimodule structure on dual spaces, by straightforward verification,
we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(P,\bullet)
be a perm algebra,
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
be a bimodule over it.
Then
(M^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}-\mathfrak{r}^{\ast})
is also a bimodule over
(P,\bullet)
,
which is called the
dual bimodule
of
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
.
In particular,
(P^{\ast},\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{P}^{\ast},\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{P}^{\ast}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{P}^{\ast})
is a bimodule over
(P,\bullet)
.

A commutative averaging algebra induces a perm algebra. For a module over
a commutative averaging algebra, we also have the corresponding conclusion.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra, and
(A,\bullet)
be the
induced perm algebra. For any module
(M,\mu,\beta)
over
(A,\alpha)
, we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(M),\qquad\mathfrak{l}(a)(m)=\mu(\alpha(a))(m),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(M),\qquad\mathfrak{r}(a)(m)=\mu(a)(\beta(m)),

for any
a\in A
and
m\in M
. Then,
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is a bimodule over
(A,\bullet)
.

Proof.

Since for any
a\in A
and
m\in M
,
\mu(\alpha(a))(\beta(m))=\beta(\mu(\alpha(a))(m))=\beta(\mu(a)(\beta(m)))
, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\bullet a_{2})(m)=\mu(\alpha(a_{1}\bullet a_{2}))(m)=\mu(\alpha(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2})(m))=\mu(\alpha(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2}))(m),

\displaystyle\qquad\;\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})(\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})(m))=\mu(\alpha(a_{1}))(\mu(\alpha(a_{2}))(m))=\mu(\alpha(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2}))(m),

\displaystyle\qquad\;\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})(\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})(m))=\mu(\alpha(a_{2}))(\mu(\alpha(a_{2}))(m))=\mu(\alpha(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2}))(m).

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
m\in M
,
That is,
\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\bullet a_{2})=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})=\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})
.
Similarly, we also have
\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\bullet a_{2})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})
. Thus,
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is a
bimodule over
(A,\bullet)
.
∎

The bimodule
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
in the proposition above is called the
induced bimodule

by module
(M,\mu,\beta)
. Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra
and
(A,\bullet)
be the induced perm algebra of
(A,\alpha)
.
The bimodule
(A,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
over
(A,\bullet)
induced by the regular module

(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\alpha)
is exactly the regular bimodule
(A,\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{A},\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{A})
over
(A,\bullet)
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra and
(A,\bullet)
be the
induced perm algebra. Suppose that
(M,\mu,\beta)
is a module over
(A,\alpha)
,
and
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is the bimodule over
(A,\bullet)
induced by
(M,\mu,\beta)
.
Then, the dual bimodule
(M^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}-\mathfrak{r}^{\ast})
of
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})

is the induced bimodule over the perm algebra
(A,\bullet)

by
(M^{\ast},\mu^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
if and only if

\beta(\mu(a)(m))=\mu(\alpha(a))(m)-\mu(a)(\beta(m)),

for any
a\in A
and
m\in M
.

In particular, when taking
(M,\mu,\beta)=(A,\mathbf{l}_{A},\beta)
, we get that

(A^{\ast},\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{A}^{\ast},\tilde{\mathbf{l}}_{A}^{\ast}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{A}^{\ast})

is induced by
(A^{\ast},\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
if and only if

\beta(a_{1}a_{2})=\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}-a_{1}\beta(a_{2})
, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

Proof.

We only prove the first part. Denote by
(M^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{r}^{\prime})
the bimodule
over
(A,\bullet)
induced by
(M^{\ast},\mu^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.
Since
(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is induced by
(M,\mu,\beta)
, for any
a\in A
,
m\in M
,

\xi\in M^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}(a)(\xi),\;m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mu^{\ast}(\alpha(a))(\xi),\;m\rangle=\langle\xi,\;\mu(\alpha(a))(m)\rangle=\langle\xi,\;\mathfrak{l}(a)(m)\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}(a)(\xi),\;m\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}(a)(\xi),\;m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mu^{\ast}(a)(\beta^{\ast}(\xi)),\;m\rangle=\langle\xi,\;\beta(\mu(a)(m))\rangle

\displaystyle\langle(\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}-\mathfrak{r}^{\ast})(a)(\xi),\;m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,\;\mathfrak{l}(a)(m)\rangle-\langle\xi,\;\mathfrak{r}(a)(m)\rangle=\langle\xi,\;\mu(\alpha(a))(m)\rangle-\langle\xi,\;\mu(a)(\beta(m))\rangle,

Thus,
(M^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{r}^{\prime})=(M^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast},\mathfrak{l}^{\ast}-\mathfrak{r}^{\ast})
if and
only if
\beta(\mu(a)(m))=\mu(\alpha(a))(m)-\mu(a)(\beta(m))
,
for any
a\in A
and
m\in M
.
∎

Now we consider the relationship between the matched pair of commutative averaging algebras
and the matched pair of induced perm algebras.

Definition 6.6
.

A
matched pair of perm algebras
consists of perm algebras
(A,\bullet)

and
(B,\bullet)
, together with linear maps
\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(B)
and
\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B}:B\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\Bbbk}(A)
such
that
A\oplus B
is a perm algebra, where the multiplication is defined by

(a_{1},b_{1})(a_{2},b_{2}):=\big(a_{1}\bullet a_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{B}(b_{1})(a_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{B}(b_{2})(a_{1}),\quad b_{1}\bullet b_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{A}(a_{1})(b_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{A}(a_{2})(b_{1})\big),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. The matched pair of perm
algebras is denoted by
((A,\bullet),(B,\bullet)
,
\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})

and the resulting perm algebra structure on
A\oplus B
is denoted by

(A,\bullet)\bowtie(B,\bullet)
.

In a matched pair of perm algebras
((A,\bullet),(B,\bullet),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
,
(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a bimodule over perm algebra

(B,\bullet)
and
(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A})
is a bimodule over perm algebra

(A,\bullet)
. In particular, if the multiplication of
B
is trivial,
resulting perm algebra is just the semidirect product of
(A,\bullet)
by
bimodule
(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A})
defined in Proposition
6.4
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
be a matched pair of
commutative averaging algebras,
(A,\bullet)
and
(B,\bullet)
be the
induced perm algebras of
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
respectively.
Then
((A,\bullet),(B,\bullet)
,
\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a
matched pair of perm algebras, called
the induced matched pair of perm algebras

by
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
, where
(B,\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A})

is the induced bimodule over
(A,\bullet)
by
(B,\mu_{A},\beta)
and

(A,\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is the induced bimodule over
(B,\bullet)
by
(A,\mu_{B},\alpha)
.

Moreover, the perm algebra
(A,\bullet)\bowtie(B,\bullet)
obtained by the matched
pair
((A,\bullet),(B,\bullet),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},

\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})

is exactly the perm algebra induced by the commutative averaging algebra

(A\bowtie B,\;\alpha\oplus\beta)
obtained by the matched pair

((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
.

Proof.

Let
(A\bowtie B,\;\alpha\oplus\beta)
be the commutative averaging algebra obtained
by the matched pair
((A,\alpha),(B,\beta),\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
, and

(A\oplus B,\diamond)
be the perm algebra induced by
(A\bowtie B,\;\alpha\oplus\beta)
.
Note that, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
,

\displaystyle\;(a_{1},b_{1})\diamond(a_{2},b_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\alpha(a_{1}),\beta(b_{1}))(a_{2},b_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}+\mu_{B}(\beta(b_{1}))(a_{2})+\mu_{B}(b_{2})(\alpha(a_{1})),\ \ \beta(b_{1})b_{2}+\mu_{A}(\alpha(a_{1}))(b_{2})+\mu_{A}(a_{2})(\beta(b_{1}))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\Big(a_{1}\bullet a_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{B}(b_{1})(a_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{B}(b_{2})(a_{1}),\ \ b_{1}\bullet b_{2}+\mathfrak{l}_{A}(a_{1})(b_{2})+\mathfrak{r}_{A}(a_{2})(b_{1})\Big),

we get
((A,\bullet),(B,\bullet),\mathfrak{l}_{A},\mathfrak{r}_{A},\mathfrak{l}_{B},\mathfrak{r}_{B})
is a
matched pair. And we have already shown that the perm algebra structure on

A\oplus B
obtained from this induced matched pair of perm algebras is exactly
the induced perm algebra by
(A\bowtie B\;\alpha\oplus\beta)
.
∎

In particular, if the multiplication of
B
is trivial, we have the following corollary.

.

(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra and

.

Moreover, by Propositions
6.7
and
6.5
, we have

Corollary 6.9
.

(A,\alpha)

(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})

and

((A,\bullet)

Next, we consider the Manin triples of perm algebras induced by the double construction
of averaging Frobenius algebras. Recall that a bilinear form
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}(-,-)

on a perm algebra
(P,\bullet)
is called
invariant

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}(p_{1}\bullet p_{2},\;p_{3})=\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}(p_{1},\;p_{2}\bullet p_{3}-p_{3}\bullet p_{2}),

Definition 6.10

(
[

,

]
)

A

(ii)

(iii)

and

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra and
(A,\bullet)
be the induced
perm algebra by
(A,\alpha)
. Suppose that there is a linear map
\beta:A\rightarrow A

such that
(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
is a commutative averaging algebra, and

(A^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})

((A\oplus A^{\ast},\diamond,\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}),\;(A,\bullet),\;(A^{\ast},\bullet))
of perm algebras such that the perm algebra
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\diamond)
is induced by
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})
, if and only if
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\beta(a_{1}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}-a_{1}\alpha(a_{2}),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha

\displaystyle=(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta-(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\beta)\Delta,

where the bilinear form
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(-,-)
on

is
defined by
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}((a_{1},\xi_{1}),\;(a_{2},\xi_{2})):=\langle\xi_{2},\;a_{1}\rangle-\langle\xi_{1},\;a_{2}\rangle

a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}

Proof.

Clearly, the bilinear from
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(-,-)
is nondegenerate antisymmetric.
Since the perm algebra
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\diamond)
is induced by
(A\bowtie A^{\ast},\alpha\oplus\beta^{\ast})

(A,\bullet)
and
(A^{\ast},\bullet)

are perm subalgebras of
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\diamond)
,
A
and
A^{\ast}
are isotropic
with respect to
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(-,-)
, and for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A

and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in A^{\ast}
,

\displaystyle\;\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\big((a_{1},\xi_{1})\diamond(a_{2},\xi_{2}),\ \ (a_{3},\xi_{3})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\langle\xi_{3},\;\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}\rangle+\langle\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{3},\;a_{2}\rangle+\langle\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{2}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{3}),\;a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\langle\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{2},\;a_{3}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2},\;\alpha(a_{1})a_{3}\rangle-\langle\xi_{1},\;\beta(a_{2}a_{3})\rangle,

\displaystyle\;\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\big((a_{1},\xi_{1}),\ \ (a_{2},\xi_{2})\diamond(a_{3},\xi_{3})-(a_{3},\xi_{3})\diamond(a_{2},\xi_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\langle\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{3},\;a_{1}\rangle+\langle\xi_{3},\;a_{1}\alpha(a_{2})\rangle+\langle\xi_{2},\;\beta(a_{1}a_{3})\rangle-\langle\xi_{1},\;\alpha(a_{2})a_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\langle\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{2}),\;a_{3}\rangle-\langle\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{3}),\;a_{2}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{3}),\;a_{1}\rangle-\langle\xi_{2},\;a_{1}\alpha(a_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\langle\xi_{3},\;\beta(a_{1}a_{2})\rangle+\langle\xi_{1},\;a_{2}\alpha(a_{3})\rangle+\langle\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{3}),\;a_{2}\rangle+\langle\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{2}),\;a_{3}\rangle,

Thus,
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(-,-)
is invariant on the perm algebra
(P,\diamond)

if and only if Eq. (
6.1
) holds and
\alpha^{\ast}(\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{2})=\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\cdot_{A^{\ast}}\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{2})

for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
. Note that the equation above is just the dual
of Eq. (
6.2
), we get the proof.
∎

6.2.
Induced perm bialgebras and solutions of Yang-Baxter equation

Recall that a pair
(P,\bar{\Delta})
is called a
perm coalgebra
, where
P
is a
vector space and
\bar{\Delta}:P\rightarrow P\otimes P
is a linear map such that

(\bar{\Delta}\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\bar{\Delta})\bar{\Delta}=(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\bar{\Delta}\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}.

The notion of a perm coalgebra is the dualization of the notion of a perm algebra, that is,

(P,\bar{\Delta})
is a finite-dimensional perm coalgebra if and only if
(P^{\ast},\bar{\Delta}^{\ast})
is a perm algebra.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
(A,\Delta,\beta)
be an averaging cocommutative coalgebra.
Then
(A,\bar{\Delta})
is perm coalgebra, called the
perm coalgebra induced

by
(A,\Delta,\beta)
, where
\bar{\Delta}
is defined by

\bar{\Delta}=(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta.

Moreover,
(A^{\ast},\bar{\Delta}^{\ast})
is exactly the perm algebra induced by
the averaging commutative algebra
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Since
(A,\Delta,\beta)
is a cocommutative averaging coalgebra, we get a
commutative averaging algebra
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
.
Let
(A^{\ast},\bullet)
be the perm algebra induced by
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
, that is, for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A^{\ast}
,

\xi_{1}\bullet\xi_{2}:=\Delta^{\ast}(\beta^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\otimes\xi_{2})
.
It is straightforward that
\bullet
is just
\bar{\Delta}^{\ast}
,
the linear dual of
\bar{\Delta}
.
Thus,
(A^{\ast},\bar{\Delta}^{\ast})
is the perm algebra induced by
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\beta^{\ast})
, and so that,
(A,\bar{\Delta})
is a perm coalgebra.
∎

Definition 6.13

(
[
21
,
16
]
)
.

A
perm bialgebra
is a triple
(P,\bullet,\bar{\Delta})
, where
(P,\bullet)
is
a perm algebra and
(P,\bar{\Delta})
is a perm coalgebra, such that for any

p_{1},p_{2}\in P
,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}(p_{1}\bullet p_{2})

\displaystyle=((\tilde{\mathbf{l}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})(p_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}(p_{2})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\tilde{\mathbf{r}}(p_{2}))\bar{\Delta}(p_{1}),

(6.4)

\displaystyle\tau(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}(p_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}(p_{1})

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}(p_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}(p_{2}),

(6.5)

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}(p_{1}\bullet p_{2})

\displaystyle=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\tilde{\mathbf{l}}(p_{1}))\bar{\Delta}(p_{2})+((\tilde{\mathbf{l}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})(p_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\bar{\Delta}(p_{1})-\tau\bar{\Delta}(p_{1})).

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra,
(A,\Delta,\beta)
be a
cocommutative averaging coalgebra,
(A,\bullet)
be the perm algebra induced by

(A,\alpha)
and

be the perm coalgebra induced by
(A,\Delta,\beta)

Suppose that

only if, for any

(6.6)

\displaystyle(\beta\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(\alpha(a_{1}))-(\beta\mathbf{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{2}))\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\alpha\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{2})=0,

(6.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\alpha\beta)\Delta(a_{1})-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\alpha\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{2})=0,

(6.8)

\displaystyle(\beta\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(\alpha(a_{1}))-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{2}))\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})+(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\alpha\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;+(\mathbf{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{2}))\otimes\beta)\Delta(a_{1})-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\alpha\otimes\beta)\Delta(a_{1})=0.

Proof.

For any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, by Eq. (
3.1
), we have

\bar{\Delta}(a_{1}\bullet a_{2})=\bar{\Delta}(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2})=(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(\alpha(a_{1})a_{2})=(\beta\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(\alpha(a_{1}))+(\beta\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{1})))\Delta(a_{2}).

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\;((\tilde{\mathbf{l}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\bar{\Delta}(a_{2})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{r}}(a_{2}))\bar{\Delta}(a_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;((\tilde{\mathbf{l}}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{2})+(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{r}}(a_{2}))(\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\beta\mathbf{l}_{A}(\alpha(a_{1}))\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{2})-(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\alpha\beta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta(a_{2})+(\beta\otimes\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\alpha)\Delta(a_{1}).

Note that the Eq. (
3.1
) means
\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})\big)\Delta(a_{2})=\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits-\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\,\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{2})\big)\Delta(a_{1})
, we get Eq. (
6.3
) holds if and only if
Eq. (
6.6
) holds. Similarly, we can get Eq. (
6.4
) holds if and only if
Eq. (
6.7
) holds and Eq. (
6.5
) holds if and only if Eq. (
6.8
) holds.
The proof is finished.
∎

Next, we consider solutions of Yang-Baxter equation in perm algebras.
Let
(P,\bullet)
be a perm algebra and

r_{12}\bullet r_{23}-r_{13}\bullet r_{23}+r_{12}\bullet r_{13}-r_{13}\bullet r_{12}=0

is called the

(P,\bullet)

The solutions of Yang-Baxter equation in perm algebras are closely related to
the perm bialgebras
[
21

Proposition 6.17
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a commutative averaging algebra,
(A,\bullet)
be the
perm algebra induced by
(A,\alpha)
, and
(M,\mu,\beta)
be a bimodule over

(A,\alpha)
. If Eq. (
6.1
) holds, then each solution of
\beta
-YBE in

(A,\alpha)
is a solution of YBE in the induced perm algebra
(A,\bullet)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
is a solution of
\beta
-YBE
in
(A,\alpha)
. That is,

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes y_{i}y_{j}-\sum_{i,j}x_{j}\otimes x_{i}y_{j}\otimes y_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\alpha(x_{i})\otimes y_{i}=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes\beta(y_{i}),\qquad\qquad\sum_{i}\beta(x_{i})\otimes y_{i}=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes\alpha(y_{i}).

Since
\beta(a_{1}a_{2})=\alpha(a_{1})a_{2}-a_{1}\alpha(a_{2})
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,
we have

\displaystyle\;r_{13}\bullet r_{23}+r_{13}\bullet r_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes\alpha(y_{i})y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\otimes y_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\beta(x_{j})\otimes x_{i}y_{j}\otimes y_{i}-\sum_{i,j}\beta(x_{i}x_{j})\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\otimes y_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\beta(x_{j})\otimes x_{i}y_{j}\otimes y_{i}-\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{j})x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\otimes y_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\beta(x_{j})\otimes x_{i}y_{j}\otimes y_{i}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes\alpha(y_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\alpha(x_{i})x_{j}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;r_{12}\bullet r_{23}+r_{12}\bullet r_{13}.

This means that
r
is a solution of YBE in the induced perm algebra
(A,\bullet)
.
∎

Recently, Lin, Zhou and Bai constructed Lie biagebra by using a perm algebra and a pre-Lie
algebra
[
21
]
. Recall that a
pre-Lie algebra

(Q,\circ)
is a vector space

Q
with a binary operation
\circ:Q\otimes Q\rightarrow Q
such that

(q_{1}\circ q_{2})\circ q_{3}-q_{1}\circ(q_{2}\circ q_{3})=(q_{2}\circ q_{1})\circ q_{3}-q_{2}\circ(q_{1}\circ q_{3}),

for any
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in Q
. Let
(Q,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra.
A bilinear from
\omega(-,-)
is called
invariant
, if

\omega(q_{1}\circ q_{2},\;q_{3})=-\omega(q_{2},\;q_{1}\circ q_{3}-q_{3}\circ q_{1})
.
A pre-Lie algebra
(Q,\circ)
with an antisymmetric nondegenerate invariant bilinear
form
\omega(-,-)
is called a
quadratic pre-Lie algebra
.
Let
(P,\bullet)
be a perm algebra and
(Q,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra. Define
a binary operation
[-,-]:(P\otimes Q)\otimes(P\otimes Q)\rightarrow(P\otimes Q)
by

[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},\;p_{2}\otimes q_{2}]=(p_{1}\bullet p_{2})\otimes(q_{1}\circ q_{2})-(p_{2}\bullet p_{1})\otimes(q_{2}\circ q_{1}),

for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q
. Then one can check that

(P\otimes Q,\;[-,-])
is a Lie algebra, which is called
the induced Lie algebra

from
(P,\bullet)
and
(Q,\circ)
. In
[
21
]
, the authors have extended this
conclusion to the Lie bialgebra, and constructed some solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter
equation in Lie algebra from solutions of YBE in perm algebra. Let
(L,[-,-])
be
a Lie algebra and
r\in L\otimes L
. The classical Yang-Baxter equation
in Lie algebra
(L,[-,-])
is given by

[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0.

1
Introduction and main results

For an open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N},\ N\geqslant 2
we consider the anisotropic elliptic equation

-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(a_{i}(x,\nabla u))_{x_{i}}=f(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad f(x)\in L^{1}(\Omega),

(1.1)

where
a_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable functions satisfying the conditions

\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}(x,\xi)\,\xi_{i}\geqslant K_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}},\quad 1<p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant...\leqslant p_{N},\quad\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{i}}=1,\\
|a_{i}(x,\xi)|\leqslant K_{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\,|\xi_{j}|^{p_{j}}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}},\quad i=1,...,N,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are positive constants, and
f(x)
satisfies conditions that will be specified below.

The local boundedness, Hölder continuity, and Harnack’s inequality of weak solutions to homogeneous linear divergence-type second-order elliptic equations with measurable coefficients, but without lower order terms have been well established since the famous works of De Giorgi
[
14
]
, Nash
[
37
]
, and Moser
[
35
,
36
]
. Later, Ladyzhenskaya and Ural’tseva
[
27
]
, as well as Serrin
[
40
]
, generalized the results of De Giorgi and Moser to the case
of quasi-linear equations of the
p
-Laplace type, incorporating lower order terms under conditions expressed in terms of
L^{t}
-spaces for
t>\dfrac{N}{p}
.
Using probabilistic techniques, Aizenman and Simon, in their renowned paper
[
1
]
, proved the Harnack’s inequality and the continuity of weak solutions to the equation
\triangle u=V(x)u
under the local Kato class condition on the potential
V(x)
:

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow 0}\,\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\int\limits_{B_{R}(x)}\frac{V(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\,dy=0,\quad N\geqslant 3.

Moreover, they demonstrated that the Kato-type condition on the potential
V
is necessary for the validity of the Harnack’s inequality. Shortly thereafter, Chiarenza, Fabes, and Garofalo
[
8
]
, as well as Kurata
[
26
]
, developed real-variables techniques to establish the Harnack’s inequality for a linear divergence-type equation with measurable coefficients and a potential from the Kato class, thereby extending the results of Aizenman and Simon.
A significant breakthrough in the nonlinear theory was achieved by Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
24
]
, who proved the following point-wise estimate for non-negative solutions of the equation
-\triangle_{p}u=f(x)\geqslant 0
in terms of the Wolff potential
\mathcal{W}^{f}_{1,p}(x_{0},\rho)
:

u(x_{0})\leqslant\gamma(N,p)\Big(\fint\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{(p-1)(1+\lambda)}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{(p-1)(1+\lambda)}}+\gamma(N,p)\,\mathcal{W}^{f}_{1,p}(x_{0},2\rho),

(1.3)

where
\lambda\in\big(0,\dfrac{p-1}{N-p+1}\big)
,
p\in(1,N]
, and the Wolff potential is given by

\mathcal{W}^{f}_{\alpha,p}(x_{0},\rho):=\int\limits_{0}^{\rho}\Big(r^{\alpha p-N}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}f(z)dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\frac{dr}{r},\quad 0<\alpha p\leqslant N.

(1.4)

Estimate (
1.3
), together with the lower bound established by Rakotoson and Ziemer
[
39
]

u(x_{0})\geqslant\gamma(N,p)\mathcal{W}^{f}_{1,p}(x_{0},\rho),

provides a precise local behavior of solutions to elliptic equations with
L^{1}
data. Moreover, the method of Kilpeläinen and Malý does not require the strong summability condition on the right-hand side
f
, namely
f\in L^{t}(\Omega)
with
t>\dfrac{N}{p}
, and it remains valid for
L^{1}
data.

The question of continuity of solutions of anisotropic operators with measurable and bounded coefficients, behaving as in (
1.2
), remains a significant challenge even after nearly seventy years. For a comprehensive discussion of the subject and relevant references, we refer to the surveys
[
30
,
33
]
. The counterexamples constructed by Giaquinta
[
17
]
and Marcellini
[
31
]
demonstrate that solutions may be unbounded if the values of
p_{i}
differ too significantly. The local boundedness of solutions to anisotropic elliptic equations has been proved in
[
25
,
4
,
13
]
. It is worth noting that most of the known regularity results for anisotropic equations pertain to operators with regular (Lipschitz continuous) coefficients. For instance, see the pioneering work
[
32
]
as well as
[
5
,
6
]
and the references therein. Regarding
C^{0}
regularity, surprisingly, the most extensively studied case is the anisotropic porous medium equation, both with and without lower order terms
[
7
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
(see also
[
9
,
11
,
38
]
). To the best of our knowledge, for anisotropic elliptic equations, partial results can be found in
[
29
,
28
,
12
]
under the precise conditions on the values
p_{i}
:

\begin{cases}p_{1}=2<p=p_{2}=...=p_{N},\\
\text{or}\\
p=p_{1}=...=p_{s}<2=p_{s+1}=...=p_{N},\quad 2p+s(p-2)>0.\end{cases}

At the same time, in
[
16
]
, the authors employ a more general approach, though their results are obtained under a qualitative condition on the gap between the values
p_{i}
, specifically if
p_{N}-p_{1}\ll 1
.

Equation (
1.1
) without lower order term was studied in
[
10
]
, using the strategy from
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
, under the condition
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{i}}=1
, with no additional restrictions on the values
p_{i}
. In this paper, we establish the continuity of solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) by following the similar strategy but employing a different iteration, specifically, the Kilpeläinen–Malý technique
[
24
]
, properly adapted to the anisotropic equations.

Before formulating the main results, let us remind the reader of the definition of a weak solution to equation (
1.1
).
We extend the function
f
by zero outside
\Omega
, i.e., on

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, and, for any
\rho>0
, set

\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(\rho):=\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(x,\rho).

(1.5)

Define the anisotropic Sobolev spaces
W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
as follows

\displaystyle W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}(\Omega):=\{u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega),\quad u_{x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}}(\Omega),\quad i=1,...,N\},

\displaystyle W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}(\Omega):=\{u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad u_{x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}}(\Omega),\quad i=1,...,N\}.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a function
u\in W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a bounded weak solution of equation (
1.1
) under conditions (
1.2
) if the following identity holds

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits_{\Omega}\,a_{i}(x,\nabla u)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x)\,\varphi dx,

(1.6)

for arbitrary
\varphi\in W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\geqslant 0
.

Our main result in this paper reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a bounded weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), and assume also that

\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(\rho)=0,

(1.7)

then
u\in C(\Omega)
.

Remark 1.1
.

In the case
p_{1}=...=p_{N}=N
and
f=0
or
f\neq 0
, we cover the known results, see, for example
[
34
,
2
,
3
,
22
,
23
]
.

Now, a few words concerning the proof of the above theorem. First, for a fixed
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\rho>0
, we define the rescaled cubes

B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0}):=\big\{x:|x_{i}-x_{0,i}|\leqslant\frac{\rho^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}}{\omega^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}},\quad i=1,...,N\big\},\quad B^{(\omega)}_{8\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega,

where

\mu^{+}:=\sup\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\quad\mu^{-}:=\inf\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\quad\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}\leqslant\omega<2M=2\sup\limits_{\Omega}|u|.

Such cubes are similar to the rescaled cylinders introduced by DiBenedetto for the parabolic case
[
15
]
. Note that

B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0}):=\big\{x:|x_{i}-x_{i,0}|\leqslant\rho,\quad i=1,...,N\big\},

provided that
\omega\geqslant\rho
. By adapting the iteration technique of Kilpeläinen, Malý
[
24
]
, we establish a pointwise upper bound for the function

v^{\pm}:=\frac{\omega}{u^{\pm}+\theta(\rho)},

with some
\rho\leqslant\theta(\rho)\leqslant 1
,
\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\theta(\rho)=0
. The choice
u^{+}:=\mu^{+}-u
or
u^{-}:=u-\mu^{-}
depends on whether the solution
u
attains large values compared to
\mu^{+}
or
\mu^{-}
in a relatively large set. Classically, the
idea of using
log\,\,v^{\pm}
to prove the Hölder continuity of solutions to second-order elliptic
equations dates back to the seminal work of Moser
[
35
]
. For higher-order equations, a similar idea was later
adopted in
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
and subsequently applied to elliptic anisotropic equations without lower order terms
[
10
]
. Here, we demonstrate how this strategy can be adapted to the fully anisotropic case with a singular lower order term, following the ideas of Kilpeläinen–Malý.

Having established the local boundedness of the function
v^{\pm}
, we apply the weighted Poincaré inequality (see Lemma
2.2
below) to obtain the uniform bound

\sup\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}\,\,v^{\pm}\leqslant C,

with some constant
C>0
independent of
\rho
,
\omega
,
provided that
\theta(\rho)
is chosen according to the condition

[\theta(\rho)]^{\gamma_{0}}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}+\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(2\rho)+\rho,

with some sufficiently large
\gamma_{0}>0
. Consequently, we obtain

\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}\,u\leqslant\big(1-\frac{1}{C}\big)\,\omega+\theta(\rho),

from which, by iteration, the required Theorem
1.1
follows.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1

3
Oscillation Estimates

For fixed
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\rho
,
\omega>0
construct the cube

B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0}):=\big\{x:|x_{i}-x_{0,i}|\leqslant\frac{\rho^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}}{\omega^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}},\quad i=1,...,N\big\},\quad B^{(\omega)}_{8\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega

and assume also that

\mu^{+}:=\sup\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\quad\mu^{-}:=\inf\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\quad\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}\leqslant\omega<2M.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, which addresses the continuity of the solution to equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), it is crucial to establish appropriate estimates for the oscillation of the solution. Proposition
3.1
plays a key role in this analysis.

First, we introduce the following value
\theta(\rho)\in[\rho,1]
, which will be used frequently throughout this section:

\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{\gamma_{0}}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\biggr(\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}+\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(2\rho)+\rho,

(3.1)

where
B_{\rho}(x_{0}):=\{x:|x_{i}-x_{i,0}|<\rho,i=1,...,N\}
and
\gamma_{0}
is a fixed positive constant to be determined later.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u
be a bounded weak solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). Then, there exist numbers
\beta\in(0,1)
and
C_{*}>1
depending only on the data such that either

\omega\leqslant C_{*}\,\theta(\rho),

(3.2)

or

\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\beta\,\omega.

(3.3)

The rest of this section is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
3.1
, which relies on the well-known Kilpeläinen–Malý technique introduced in
[
24
]
. To apply this method, we begin in Section 3.1 by performing a change of variables. In 3.2, we establish and prove the key analytical tool – local energy estimates – for the resulting rescaled functions. Section 3.3 identifies and describes two possible alternatives that arise during the proof. In 3.4, we construct a sequence of levels
k_{j}
, a central element of the Kilpeläinen–Malý iteration technique. The core result of this technique, Lemma
3.2
, is presented and proved in 3.5. Finally, in Sections 3.6–3.8, we complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
, providing the necessary estimates.

3.1
Change of Variables

We introduce the change of variables

x_{i}\rightarrow(x_{i}-x_{i,0})\frac{\omega^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}{\rho^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}},\quad i=1,...,N

which maps the cube
B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})
into

B_{1}:=\{x:|x_{i}|<1,\quad i=1,...,N\}.

Denoting again with
x
the new variables, the rescaled functions

\tilde{u}^{-}:=\frac{\tilde{u}-\mu^{-}}{\omega},\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{u}^{+}:=\frac{\mu^{+}-\tilde{u}}{\omega}

are bounded nonnegative weak solutions to the equation

-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{a}_{i}(x,\nabla\tilde{u}^{\pm})\right)_{x_{i}}=\tilde{f}(x),\quad x\in B_{1},

(3.4)

where
\tilde{f}(x):=\Big(\dfrac{\rho}{\omega}\Big)^{p_{N}}\big(x_{1,0}+x_{1}\frac{\rho^{\frac{pN}{p_{1}}}}{\omega^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{1}}{p_{1}}}},...,x_{N,0}+x_{N}\rho\big)
and
\tilde{a}_{i}
satisfy the conditions

\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}(x,\nabla\tilde{u}^{\pm})\,\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{\pm}}{\partial x_{i}}\geqslant K_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{\pm}}{\partial x_{i}}\big|^{p_{i}},\\
|\tilde{a}_{i}(x,\nabla\tilde{u}^{\pm})|\leqslant K_{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\,\big|\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{\pm}}{\partial x_{j}}\big|^{p_{j}}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}},\quad i=1,...,N.\end{cases}

(3.5)

Moreover, the following integral identity holds

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits_{B_{1}}\tilde{a}_{i}(x,\nabla\tilde{u}^{\pm})\,\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int\limits_{B_{1}}\tilde{f}\,\varphi\,dx

(3.6)

for any nonnegative function
\varphi\in W^{1,\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.

3.2
Local Energy Estimates

The following notation will be used in the sequel

v^{\pm}(x):=\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{u}^{\pm}(x)+\theta(\rho)/\omega}-4\right)_{+}=\max\left\{\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{u}^{\pm}(x)+\theta(\rho)/\omega}-4\right),0\right\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{u}^{\pm}
be bounded nonnegative weak solutions to (
3.4
). For any
\lambda>0
,
m\geqslant p_{N}
, any
k
,

, any
0<r\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}
, any
0<\theta(\rho)\leqslant 1
, any set

B^{(d)}_{r}(y):=\big\{x:|x_{i}-y_{i}|<\frac{r^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}}{d^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}},\quad i=1,...,N\big\}\subset B_{1},

and any
\zeta\in C^{1}_{0}(B^{(d)}_{r}(y))
,
0\leqslant\zeta\leqslant 1
,
\zeta=1
in
B^{(d)}_{\frac{r}{2}}(y)
,
|\zeta_{x_{i}}|\leqslant 2^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}+1}\dfrac{d^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}{r^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}}
,
i=1,...,N
there exists a constant
\gamma>0
depending only on
\lambda
,
m
,
N
,
p_{1}
,…,
p_{N}
,
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and
M:=\sup\limits_{\Omega}|u|
, such that if

\omega\geqslant\theta(\rho),

then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\frac{|v^{\pm}_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}}{(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d})^{1+\lambda}}\zeta^{m}\,dx\\
\leqslant\gamma\Big(\frac{d}{r}\Big)^{p_{N}}\,[\theta(\rho)]^{-2(p_{N}-1)}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\Big(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}\,dx\\
+\gamma d\,[\theta(\rho)]^{-2(p_{N}-1)}\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)}|\tilde{f}(x)|\,\zeta^{m}dx.

(3.7)

Proof.

Test identity (
3.6
) by

\varphi=\left[1-\left(1+\frac{(v^{\pm}-k)_{+}}{d}\right)^{-\lambda}\right]\,\zeta^{m},

using conditions (
3.5
) and the fact that
0\leqslant\varphi\leqslant 1,
we obtain

\frac{\lambda K_{1}}{d}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\frac{|\tilde{u}^{\pm}_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,[\tilde{u}^{\pm}+\theta(\rho)/\omega]^{-2}}{\big(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d}\big)^{1+\lambda}}\zeta^{m}\,dx\\
\leqslant K_{2}\,m\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\tilde{u}^{\pm}_{x_{j}}|^{p_{j}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\,|\zeta_{x_{i}}|\zeta^{m-1}\,dx+\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)}|\tilde{f}|\,\zeta^{m}\,dx.

Applying Young’s inequality, we arrive at the estimate

\gamma\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\frac{|v^{\pm}_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,[\tilde{u}^{\pm}+\theta(\rho)/\omega]^{2(p_{i}-1)}}{\big(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d}\big)^{1+\lambda}}\zeta^{m}\,dx\\
=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}\frac{|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,[\tilde{u}^{\pm}+\theta(\rho)/\omega]^{-2}}{\big(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d}\big)^{1+\lambda}}\zeta^{m}\,dx\\
\leqslant\gamma\Big(\frac{d}{r}\Big)^{p_{N}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)\cap\{v^{\pm}>k\}}[\tilde{u}^{\pm}+\theta(\rho)/\omega]^{2(p_{i}-1)}\Big(1+\frac{v^{\pm}-k}{d}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{i}-1)}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}\,dx\\
+\gamma d\,\int\limits_{B^{(d)}_{r}(y)}|\tilde{f}|\,\zeta^{m}\,dx.

By the fact that
0\leqslant\tilde{u}^{\pm}\leqslant\dfrac{\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}}{\omega}\leqslant 1
,
\dfrac{\theta(\rho)}{2M}\leqslant\dfrac{\theta(\rho)}{\omega}\leqslant 1
and

\frac{\theta(\rho)}{2M}\leqslant\tilde{u}^{\pm}(x)+\theta(\rho)/\omega\leqslant 2,\quad x\in B^{(d)}_{r}(y),

and since
p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant...\leqslant p_{N}
it follows from the previous inequality that we obtain the required (
3.7
), this proves the lemma.

A_{j}(k):=\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}(k)]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{B_{j,k}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}(k)}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}_{+}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j,k}\,dx,

where
v^{-}
was defined in Lemma
3.1
and
\gamma_{1}
,
\lambda
,
m
are some fixed positive numbers to be specified later
and
\zeta_{j,k}\in C^{1}_{0}\big(B_{j,k}\big)
,
0\leqslant\zeta_{j,k}\leqslant 1
,
\zeta_{j,k}=1
in
B^{(\delta_{j}(k))}_{r_{j+1}}(y)
and
\Big|\dfrac{\partial\zeta_{j,k}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big|^{p_{i}}\leqslant\gamma\dfrac{[\delta_{j}(k)]^{p_{N}-p_{i}}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}

3.4.1
Definition of
k_{1}
and
\delta_{0}

Fix a number
\varkappa\in(0,1)
to be specified later depending only on the data. Let
y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
be arbitrary. We set
k_{-1}:=0
and
k_{0}:=\max\big(\mathcal{I},1)
, where

\mathcal{I}:=\bigg(\frac{\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}}{\varkappa}\int\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)}\big[v^{-}\big]^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}}.

Correspondingly, we introduce
\delta_{-1}:=\delta_{-1}(k_{0})=k_{0}-k_{-1}=\max\big(\mathcal{I},1\big)
.
If

A_{0}(k_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1})\leqslant\varkappa,

then we set
k_{1}:=k_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1}
. Note that
A_{0}(k)
is a continuous, decreasing function, and
A_{0}(k)\downarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, if

A_{0}(k_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1})>\varkappa,

there exists
\bar{k}>k_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1}
such that
A_{0}(\bar{k})=\varkappa
. In this case, we set
k_{1}:=\bar{k}
, and in both scenarios, we define
\delta_{0}:=\delta_{0}(k_{1})=k_{1}-k_{0}
.

Claim
.

There exists
c_{0}>1
depending only on the data and
\lambda
such that

\delta_{0}\leqslant c_{0}\,\,\delta_{-1}.

(3.12)

Proof.

Indeed, if
k_{1}=k_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1}
, then inequality (
3.12
) is evident. Now, if
k_{1}>k_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{-1}
,
inequality
\delta_{-1}\geqslant 1
implies that
B^{(c_{0}\delta_{-1})}_{r_{0}}(y)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)
. By our choice of
\mathcal{I}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{0}(k_{0}+c_{0}\,\delta_{-1})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{p_{N}}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}(c_{0}\,\mathcal{I})^{1-N-\lambda(p_{N}-1)}\int\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)}\big[v^{-}\big]^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2^{p_{N}}\,\varkappa}{c^{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}_{0}}=\frac{\varkappa}{2},

provided that
c_{0}:=2^{\frac{p_{N}+1}{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}}
. Since
A_{0}(k_{1})=\varkappa
and
A_{0}(k)
is a decreasing function, it follows that
k_{1}<k_{0}+c_{0}\,\delta_{-1}
, from which the required (
3.12
) follows.
∎

3.4.2
Induction Argument

We construct the sequences of positive numbers
\{k_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
inductively as follows. Suppose that
k_{1}
, …,
k_{j}
and
\delta_{0}
, …,
\delta_{j-1}
have already been chosen such that

A_{i}(k_{i+1})\leqslant\varkappa,\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{i}\leqslant c_{0}\,\delta_{i-1},\quad i=0,...,j-1.

(3.13)

We now proceed to determine how to choose
k_{j+1}
and
\delta_{j}
.
If

A_{j}(k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{j-1})\leqslant\varkappa,

then we set
k_{j+1}=k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{j-1}
. Note that
A_{j}(k)\downarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, if

A_{j}(k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{j-1})>\varkappa,

there exists
\bar{k}>k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{j-1}
such that
A_{j}(\bar{k})=\varkappa
. In this case, we set
k_{j+1}:=\bar{k}
. In both cases, we define
\delta_{j}:=\delta_{j}(k_{j+1})=k_{j+1}-k_{j}
. Note that our choices ensure that

A_{j}(k_{j+1})\leqslant\varkappa.

(3.14)

Moreover, since
B_{j,k_{j}+c_{0}\delta_{j-1}}\subset B_{j-1,k_{j}}
and
\big\{\zeta_{j,k_{j}+c_{0}\delta_{j-1}}\neq 0\big\}\subset\big\{\zeta_{j-1,k_{j}}=1\big\}
, by (
3.13
) and our choice of
c_{0}
, we have

A_{j}(k_{j}+c_{0}\,\delta_{j-1})\leqslant\frac{2^{p_{N}}}{c^{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}_{0}}\,A_{j-1}(k_{j})\leqslant\frac{2^{p_{N}}\,\varkappa}{c^{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}_{0}}\,=\frac{\varkappa}{2},

which implies

\delta_{j}\leqslant c_{0}\,\delta_{j-1}.

(3.15)

In what follows, we write
B_{j}:=B_{j,k_{j+1}}=B^{(\delta_{j}(k_{j+1}))}_{r_{j}}(y)
and
\zeta_{j}:=\zeta_{j,k_{j+1}}
.

3.5
Kilpeläinen–Malý Lemma

The following lemma is the key to the Kilpeläinen–Malý technique.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let the sequences
r_{j}
,
k_{j}
, and
\delta_{j}
be defined in section
3.4
.
Additionally, let
0<\lambda<p_{1}-1
. Then for any
j\geqslant 1
, there holds

\delta_{j}\,\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{j-1}+\gamma r_{j}+\gamma\Big(\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\,\int\limits_{B_{r_{j-1}}(y)}|\tilde{f}(x)|\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}},

(3.16)

where
B_{r_{j-1}}(y)=\{x:|x_{i}-y_{i}|<r_{j-1},\quad i=1,...,N\}
.

Proof.

Fix
j\geqslant 1
and assume that

\delta_{j}>\frac{1}{2}\delta_{j-1}+r_{j},

(3.17)

since otherwise, the estimate (
3.16
) is evident. Inequality (
3.17
) ensures that
k_{j+1}>k_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{j-1}
, which, according to the induction arguments described in subsection
3.4.2
, implies that
A_{j}(k_{j+1})=\varkappa
. Moreover,

\frac{r^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}_{j}}{\delta^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}_{j}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\frac{r^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}_{j-1}}{\delta^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}_{j-1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{r^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}_{j}}{\delta^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}_{j}}\leqslant r_{j},\quad i=1,...,N,

and hence

B_{j}\subset B_{j-1},\,\,\,B_{j}\subset B_{r_{j}}(y),\,\,\,\{x\in B_{j}:\,\,\zeta_{j}\neq 0\}\subset B_{j-1},\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\{x\in B_{j}:\,\,\,\zeta_{j}\neq 0\}\subset B_{r_{j-1}}(y).

(3.18)

Let us estimate the term on the right-hand side of (
3.11
) for
k=k_{j+1}
. To do this, we apply the decomposition
B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}=L_{\epsilon,j}\cup E_{\epsilon,j}
, where

L_{\epsilon,j}:=\big\{x\in B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}:\dfrac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\leqslant\epsilon\big\},\quad E_{\epsilon,j}:=B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}\setminus L_{\epsilon,j}.

Here, the parameter
\epsilon
depends on the given data and is chosen to be sufficiently small, its precise value will be specified later.
Thus, we write the decomposition for
A_{j}(k_{j+1})
in the following form:

\displaystyle\varkappa=A_{j}(k_{j+1})=

\displaystyle\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j,k}\,dx

(3.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{L_{\epsilon,j}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j,k}\,dx

\displaystyle+\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon,j}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j,k}\,dx

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle A_{j}^{(L_{\epsilon,j})}+A_{j}^{(E_{\epsilon,j})}

Now, we choose the constant
m
from (
3.11
) such that
m\geqslant p_{N}
. Furthermore, note that for any
x\in B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}
, the following inequality holds:

1=\frac{k_{j}-k_{j-1}}{k_{j}-k_{j-1}}\leqslant\frac{v^{-}-k_{j-1}}{\delta_{j-1}}.

By applying inequalities (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we can now evaluate
A_{j}^{(L_{\epsilon,j})}

\displaystyle A_{j}^{(L_{\epsilon,j})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\,\epsilon^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j-1}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j-1}}\int\limits_{L_{\epsilon,j}}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j-1}\,dx

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\epsilon^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j-1}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j-1}}\int\limits_{B_{j-1}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j-1}}{\delta_{j-1}}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}_{+}\,\zeta^{m-p_{N}}_{j-1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\gamma\epsilon^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}A_{j-1}(k_{j})\leqslant\gamma\epsilon^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,\varkappa,

where the generic constant

depends on
c_{0}
and
p_{N}
.
To derive an estimate for
A_{j}^{(E_{\epsilon,j})}
, we set the constant

from (

) such that

and introduce the following auxiliary function

W(\sigma):=\int\limits^{\sigma}_{0}\,(1+s)^{-\frac{1+\lambda}{p_{1}}}\,ds,\quad\sigma>0.

Evidently, we have

W(\sigma)\leqslant\gamma(\lambda,p_{1})\,(1+\sigma)^{\frac{p_{1}-1-\lambda}{p_{1}}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad W(\sigma)\geqslant\gamma(\epsilon,\lambda,p_{1})\,\sigma^{\frac{p_{1}-1-\lambda}{p_{1}}},\text{ if }\sigma\geqslant\epsilon.

(3.21)

Now, we apply Lemma
2.1
with

\frac{Np_{1}}{p_{1}(p_{N}-1)+N}-1<\lambda<p_{1}-1
. Furthermore, by applying Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{j}^{(E_{\epsilon,j})}\leqslant

\displaystyle\gamma(\epsilon)\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon,j}}\Big[W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\,\zeta^{\frac{m-p_{N}}{q}}_{j}\Big]^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\gamma(\epsilon)\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\frac{[\delta_{j}]^{p_{N}-N}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j}}\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big[W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\,\zeta^{\frac{m-p_{N}}{q}}_{j}\Big]^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\gamma(\epsilon)\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg(\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\biggr|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big[W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\,\zeta^{\frac{m-p_{N}}{q}}_{j}\Big]\biggr|^{p_{i}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{q}{Np_{i}}}

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(\epsilon)}{\delta^{q}_{j}}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg(\delta^{p_{i}}_{j}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\biggr|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big[W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\,\zeta^{\frac{m-p_{N}}{q}}_{j}\Big]\biggr|^{p_{i}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{q}{Np_{i}}}

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(\epsilon)}{\delta^{q}_{j}}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\Bigg(\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\delta^{p_{i}}_{j}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\biggr|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\biggr|^{p_{i}}\zeta^{\frac{(m-p_{N})p_{1}}{q}}_{j}dx

Let us estimate the integrals on the right-hand side of (
3.22
). We apply Lemma
3.1
to the first integral, which then leads to the following bound

\displaystyle\delta^{-N}_{j}

\displaystyle\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\frac{N}{q}\gamma_{1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\delta^{p_{i}}_{j}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\Big|^{p_{i}}\zeta^{\frac{(m-p_{N})p_{1}}{q}}_{j}dx

m
from the condition

and fix

Using (
3.14
), (

), (

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leqslant\,\gamma\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\,\frac{\delta^{p_{N}-N}_{j-1}}{r^{p_{N}}_{j-1}}\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j-1}}{\delta_{j-1}}\Big)^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\zeta_{j-1}^{m-p_{N}}\,dx

Therefore, we derive the following bound for the first integral on the right-hand side of (

)

\displaystyle\delta^{-N}_{j}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\frac{N}{q}\gamma_{1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\delta^{p_{i}}_{j}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\Big|^{p_{i}}\zeta^{\frac{(m-p_{N})p_{1}}{q}}_{j}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\,\varkappa+\gamma\,\delta^{1-N}_{j}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}}\int\limits_{B_{j}}|\tilde{f}|\,dx.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
3.22

3.23

\displaystyle\delta^{-N}_{j}\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\frac{N}{q}\gamma_{1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\delta^{p_{i}}_{j}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{v^{-}>k_{j}\}}\Big[W\Big(\frac{v^{-}-k_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\Big)\Big]^{p_{i}}\Big|\frac{\partial\zeta_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big|^{p_{i}}\,\zeta^{\frac{(m-p_{N})p_{i}}{q}-p_{i}}_{j}\,dx

B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0})
.
Combining the last two inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1}}\big[v^{-}\big]^{(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)-\frac{N}{p_{1}}}\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\biggr(\int\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)-\frac{N}{p_{1}}}\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\biggr(\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}.

3.8
Proof of Proposition
3.1

Inequalities (
3.27
) and (
3.28
) yield

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v^{-}\leqslant\gamma

\displaystyle+\gamma\Bigg(\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{1}-(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)-\frac{N}{p_{1}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\Big(\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}}

(3.29)

\displaystyle+\gamma\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\frac{\gamma_{1}+N}{N-1}}\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(2\rho).

Fixing
\gamma_{0}
such that

\gamma_{0}=\max\left\{\gamma_{1}+(1+\lambda)(p_{N}-1)+\frac{N}{p_{1}},\frac{\gamma_{1}+N}{N-1}\right\}

and using our choice (
3.1
) for
\theta(\rho)
, we obtain

\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}v^{-}\leqslant\gamma+\gamma\Bigg(\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{0}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\,\Big(\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{N-1+\lambda(p_{N}-1)}}+\gamma\big[\theta(\rho)\big]^{-\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{W}^{|f|}_{1,N}(2\rho)\leqslant C,

(3.30)

with a positive constant
C
depending only on the data. Inequalities (
3.10
) and (
3.30
) imply

\inf\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}u\geqslant\mu^{-}+\frac{\omega}{C+4}-\theta(\rho)\geqslant\mu^{-}+\omega\biggr(\frac{1}{C+4}-\frac{1}{C_{*}}\biggr)=\mu^{-}+\frac{\omega}{2(C+4)},

provided that
C_{*}
is chosen to satisfy
C_{*}=2(C+4)
. This inequality yields

\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega)}_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\biggr(1-\frac{1}{2(C+4)}\biggr)\,\omega:=\beta\,\omega,

which completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

4
Continuity of Solutions, Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of the continuity is almost standard (see
[
15
, Section 3]
) and uses Proposition
3.1
. Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
, let
R>0
and construct the cube
B_{8R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. If for all
\rho\in(0,R]
there holds

\osc\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\,u\leqslant\rho,

(4.1)

then the continuity is evident, since
\osc\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\,u\to 0
as
\rho\to 0
.

Therefore, we assume that there exists
\rho_{0}\in(0,R]
such that

\osc\limits_{B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}\,u\geqslant\rho_{0}.

(4.2)

In this case, we set

\mu^{+}_{0}:=\sup\limits_{B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}u,\quad\mu^{-}_{0}:=\inf\limits_{B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}u,\quad\omega_{0}:=\mu^{+}_{0}-\mu^{-}_{0}=\osc\limits_{B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}u.

By (
4.2
), we conclude

B^{(\omega_{0})}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})\subset B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}),\quad\text{and}\quad\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega_{0})}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\omega_{0}.

Proposition
3.1
yields

\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega_{0})}_{\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\max\big(\beta\,\omega_{0},C_{*}\,\theta(\rho_{0})\big)=:\omega_{1},

with some
\beta\in(0,1)
and
C_{*}>1
depending only on the data. Choosing
\eta\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{\eta^{\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}}}{\beta^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}\leqslant 2^{-\frac{p_{N}}{p_{i}}},\quad i=1,...,N,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\eta=\frac{1}{2}\,\beta^{\frac{p_{N}-p_{1}}{p_{N}}},

we obtain

\begin{cases}B^{(\omega_{1})}_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0})\subset B^{(\omega_{0})}_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}),\quad\rho_{1}:=\eta\,\rho_{0},\\
\text{and}\\
\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega_{1})}_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\omega_{1}.\end{cases}

Repeating this procedure we define for any
n\geqslant 0

\begin{cases}\rho_{n+1}:=\eta^{n+1}\,\rho_{0},\quad\omega_{n+1}:=\max\big(\beta\,\omega_{n},C_{*}\,\theta(\rho_{n})\big),\\
\text{and}\\
\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega_{n+1})}_{\rho_{n+1}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\omega_{n+1},\quad B^{(\omega_{n+1})}_{\rho_{n+1}}(x_{0})\subset B^{(\omega_{n})}_{\rho_{n}}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

(4.3)

Note that for any
n\geqslant 0
, numbers
\omega_{n}
are uniformly bounded. Indeed, since the function
\theta(\cdot)
is non-decreasing, and
\beta<1
, we have

\omega_{n}\leqslant\beta^{n}\,\omega_{0}+C_{*}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta^{n-1-i}\theta(\rho_{i})\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\beta}\Big(\omega_{0}+C_{*}\,\theta(\rho_{0})\Big),\quad\forall\,n\geqslant 0.

(4.4)

For any
0<r<\bar{\rho}<\rho_{0}
, define the numbers
j
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
by the conditions

\frac{\rho_{0}}{2^{j}}\leqslant\bar{\rho}\leqslant\frac{\rho_{0}}{2^{j-1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2^{m}}\leqslant r\leqslant\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2^{m-1}}.

Now, we iterate (
4.3
)
m
times, starting from step
j
, and apply (
4.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\osc\limits_{B^{(\omega_{m+j})}_{\rho_{m+j}}(x_{0})}\,u

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta^{m}\,\omega_{j}+\theta(\rho_{j})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\beta^{i}\leqslant\beta^{m}\,\omega_{j}+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\,\theta(\rho_{j})

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\Big(\frac{r}{\bar{\rho}}\Big)^{\alpha}\,\omega_{j}+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\,\theta(\bar{\rho})\leqslant\gamma\Big(\frac{r}{\bar{\rho}}\Big)^{\alpha}\,\Big[\omega_{0}+C_{*}\,\theta(\rho_{0})\Big]+\gamma\theta(\bar{\rho}),

with
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
\beta
. For a fixed constant
\mu\in(0,1)
, we define
\bar{\rho}
as
\bar{\rho}:=r^{\mu}\,\rho^{1-\mu}_{0}
. With this choice, inequality (
4.5
) transforms into

\osc\limits_{B^{(\nu_{0})}_{r}(x_{0})}\,u\leqslant\gamma\Big(\frac{r}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\,\Big[\omega_{0}+C_{*}\,\theta(\rho_{0})\Big]+\gamma\theta(r^{\mu}\rho^{1-\mu}_{0}),

(4.6)

with
\nu_{0}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}\big[\omega_{0}+C_{*}\theta(\rho_{0})\big]
. Since
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\theta(r)=0
, it follows from (
4.6
) that
\osc\limits_{B^{(\nu_{0})}_{r}(x_{0})}\,u\to 0
as
r\to 0
, ensuring the continuity of the solution
u
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

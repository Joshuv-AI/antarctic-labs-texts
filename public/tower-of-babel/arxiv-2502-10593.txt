Topological rigidity in twisted, elastic ribbons
Carlos E. Moguel-Lehmer, Christian D. Santangelo

Abstract
Topology is an important determinant of the behavior of a great number of condensed-matter systems, but until recently has played a minor role in elasticity. We develop a theory for the deformations of a class of twisted non-Euclidean sheets which have a symmetry based on the celebrated Bonnet isometry. We show that non-orientability is an obstruction to realizing the symmetry globally, and induces a geometric phase that captures a memory analogous to a previously identified one in 2D metamaterials. However, we show that orientable ribbons can also obstruct realizing the symmetry globally. This new obstruction is mediated by how the unit normal vector winds around the centerline of the ribbon, and provides conditions for constructing soft modes of deformation compatible with the topology of multiply-twisted connected ribbons.

Having identified non-orientable minimal ribbons as the only generically frustrated ones, we now want to understand how they deform under load. To that end, we consider an arbitrary isometry,
\delta\mathbf{X}=u^{i}\partial_{i}\mathbf{X}_{q}+\zeta\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{q}

A generic in-plane deformation on an orientable surface with a boundary is given by the Hodge-Morrey-Friedrich decomposition (HMF),

\displaystyle b^{ij}D_{i}D_{j}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle b_{C}^{ij}D_{i}D_{j}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2K\zeta.

We can ascribe physical meaning to

On non-orientable ribbons, we use the HMF decomposition on the orientable double cover (

The antisymmetric rotation

In summary, we have studied the deformations of orientable and non-orientable non-Euclidean ribbons based on the bent helicoids. Despite the existence of a soft mode of the elastic energy, we identify two mechanisms that prevent the symmetry from being realized globally, one geometric and one topological having to do with non-orientability. This demonstrates that global constraints can induce a topological rigidity in non-simply connected shells that cannot exist when they are simply connected. We also show that non-orientable order is realized by an antiperiodic scalar function associated with local rotation
[
22
]
and encodes a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
geometric phase. These results pave the way toward understanding how topology determines elastic response in continuum elastic sheets.

I.1
The associate family of a minimal surface

All minimal surfaces are part of a one-parameter family of isometric minimal surfaces called the asssociate family. The associate family of a minimal surface can be written in the form
\mathbf{X}=\cos\theta~\mathbf{X}_{0}+\sin\theta~\mathbf{X}_{C}
where
\theta
is the Bonnet angle. In isothermal coordinates, where
ds^{2}=\Omega(x^{1},x^{2})[(dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}]
, the surfaces
\mathbf{X}_{0}
and
\mathbf{X}_{C}
satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations
[
1
]

\partial_{1}\mathbf{X}_{0}=\partial_{2}\mathbf{X}_{C},\quad\partial_{2}\mathbf{X}_{0}=-\partial_{1}\mathbf{X}_{C}

(1)

Hence,
\nabla^{2}X_{0}=\nabla^{2}X_{C}=0
, where
\nabla^{2}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
, the normal vectors of
\mathbf{X}_{0}
and
\mathbf{X_{C}}
are equal for each
(\rho,\phi)
, and the normal vector for any constant
\theta
is,

\hat{\mathbf{N}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det{g}}}\left(\partial_{1}\mathbf{X}\times\partial_{2}\mathbf{X}\right)=\cos^{2}\theta~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}+\sin^{2}\theta~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}=\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}

(2)

where
\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}
is the normal unit vector to
\mathbf{X}_{0}
. A direct computation using eq. (
2
) and the Cauchy-Riemann equations shows that the second fundamental form is given by,

b_{ij}=\cos\theta~b^{0}_{ij}+\sin\theta~b^{C}_{ij}

(3)

where
b^{0}
and
b^{C}
are the second fundamental forms of
\mathbf{X}_{0}
and
\mathbf{X}_{C}
, respectively.

The Bonnet isometry rotates the principal curvatures of the second fundamental form by
\theta/2

[
2
]

, of the second fundamental form (
3

\left(b_{ij}\pm c~\Omega\delta_{ij}\right)e_{\pm}^{j}=0,

and the corresponding eigenvalues are denoted

Thus, in the principal frame of
\mathbf{X}_{0}
,

e_{+}(\theta)=\left(e_{+}^{1},e_{+}^{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\Omega}{2\left(1+\cos\theta\right)}}\left(1+\cos\theta,\sin\theta\right).

The principal direction,

(6)

Thus, the angle,

(7)

where
|v|=\sqrt{g_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Omega\nabla^{2}\ln\Omega

\displaystyle\partial_{2}b_{11}-\partial_{1}b_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{2}b_{12}-\partial_{1}b_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(13)

Defining
f^{2}\equiv(1/2)\Omega\nabla^{2}\ln\Omega
allows us to write
b_{11}=f\cos\alpha
, and
b_{12}=f\sin\alpha
. Then our equations reduce to a pair of equations,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{1}\alpha&=&\partial_{2}f/f\\
\partial_{2}\alpha&=&-\partial_{1}f/f.\end{array}

A solution of Eq. (
I.2
) only exists if
\nabla^{2}\ln f=0
, but this condition must, of course, be satisfied by bent helicoid minimal surfaces (if it was not, they could not have been constructed).
Eq. (
I.2
) has a solution that is unique up to an overall constant. We can, for example, choose
\alpha
at a single point on the surface, then integrate along a path to find
\alpha
at any other point. Therefore, given any solution for
\alpha
gives a solution to the Gauss-Codazzi-Mainardi system,

\displaystyle b_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\cos(\alpha+\theta),

\displaystyle b_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\sin(\alpha+\theta),

(17)

\displaystyle b_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f\cos(\alpha+\theta),

where
\theta
is a constant. Once a choice of
b_{ij}
and
g_{ij}
are made that are compatible with the Gauss-Codazzi-Mainardi system, the resulting surface is unique up to translations and rotations by Bonnet’s theorem.
This shows that the Bonnet isometry of an associate family is the unique continuous transformation that only rotates the principal directions preserving both zero mean curvature and the isothermal coordinate system.

Notice that the matrix

\displaystyle b_{i}^{~j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\ln\Omega\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos(\alpha+\theta)&\sin(\alpha+\theta)\\
\sin(\alpha+\theta)&-\cos(\alpha+\theta)\end{array}\right)

Thus, we can identify the principle curvatures as
\pm c
, where

c=\pm\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\ln\Omega\right|.

(21)

This construction gives us the direction of the principal curvatures even when the surface is not simply connected, but it does not guarantee that the resulting surface remains closed as a function of
\theta
. Consider decomposing a minimal surface into simply-connected patches. Schwarz’ theorem guarantees that each patch transforms uniquely as we change
\theta
and, because of this, they can be made to agree whenever patches overlap by setting the Bonnet angles equal on each overlapping patch. However, there is no guarantee patches will overlap around any non-contractible loops. Hence, this leaves open the question of whether a Bonnet isometry exists globally, but it does show that no other isometry preserving
H=0
could exist.

\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}

II.2
Connectedness of the orientable bent helicoids under the Bonnet isometry

To identify which ribbons become disconnected under the Bonnet isometry, it is sufficient to look at the ribbons’ centerline
\rho=0
using the Björling parameterization in Eq. (
40
). The displacement between the endpoints of the centerline satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{X}_{q}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,2\pi;\theta)-\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,0;\theta),

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\left[\boldsymbol{\chi}(2\pi)-\boldsymbol{\chi}(0)\right]+\sin\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{q}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(s),

\displaystyle=\sin\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{q}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(s).

(52)

The vanishing of the integral in Eq. (
52
) guarantees that the Bonnet isometry preserves the connectivity of the ribbon.

Despite the fact that Eq. (
52
) was derived only on the
\rho=0
line, it has broader validity. This is because the differential form,

\omega(\rho,\phi)=du^{i}\epsilon_{i}^{~j}\partial_{j}\mathbf{X}(\rho,\phi),

(53)

is closed for minimal surfaces, for which
\nabla^{2}\mathbf{X}=0
. Therefore,
\int_{\gamma}\omega
takes the same value on any non-contractible loop. Consequently,

\int_{\gamma}\omega=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\hat{\mathbf{N}}(s)\times\gamma^{\prime}(s).

(54)

One can check by hand that the only orientable ribbons that fail this condition are
q=0,2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\left(0,0,-1\right)^{T}=\left(0,0,-2\pi\right)^{T},

(55)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\hat{\mathbf{N}}_{2}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\left(\sin^{2}s,\frac{\sin 2s}{2},-\cos s\right)^{T}=\left(\pi,0,0\right)^{T},

which we refer to as resonance conditions because of the appearance of Fourier-like modes in the integrands of Eqs. (
55
). Hence, the Bonnet isometry disconnects the
q=0,2
bent helicoids but not higher even
q
.

It turns out that we can find minimal surfaces with
q=0
and
q=2
that do stay connected under the Bonnet isometry. Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}(\phi)=\left(\sin\phi,\cos\phi,0\right)
, and the unit normals

\mathbf{N}_{n}(\phi)=\left(-\sin\phi\sin\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right],-\cos\phi\sin\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right],\cos\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right]\right),

(56)

where
a
is a real positive number and
n=\frac{q}{2}
with even natural number
q
. This choice gives the tangent vector along the centerline

\mathbf{N}_{n}(\phi)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(\phi)=\left(\sin\phi\cos\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right],\cos\phi\cos\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right],\sin\left[n\phi+a\sin\phi\right]\right).

(57)

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\mathbf{N}_{n}(s)\times\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\prime}(s)=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\frac{2\pi n}{a}J_{n}(a)(0,1,0),

(58)

where
J_{n}
is the n-th Bessel function of the first kind. We conclude that
q=n=0
produces a minimal surface with zero half-twists that stays connected under the Bonnet isometry for all values of
a
. The minimal surfaces for the other
n
remain connected under the Bonnet isometry only if we choose
a
to be any of the zeros of the n-th Bessel function. Thus, we can generate a minimal surface with two half-twists that stays connected under the isometry and many even-twisted minimal surfaces, with more than two twists, that do not.

We present the calculation of the integral for completeness. The integral for the
y
Cartesian component reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\cos(ns+a\sin s)\cos s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\cos s~e^{ins}~e^{ia\sin s},

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{m=\infty}J_{m}(a)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\cos s~e^{is(n+m)},

where
\mathrm{Re}(\cdot)
takes the real part of a complex number and we have used the Bessel-Fourier series expansion of
e^{ia\sin s}
. One can check that the only non-zero integrals in the sum require
n+m=\pm 1
. A computation using the recursion identities for the Bessel functions shows that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{ds}~\cos(ns+a\sin s)\cos s=\pi\left[J_{1-n}(a)+J_{-n-1}(a)\right]=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\frac{2\pi n}{a}J_{n}(a).

(60)

A similar argument shows that the
x
and
z
Cartesian components of the integral vanish.

II.3
Non-orientable minimal ribbons

The integral in Eq.(
52
) is also non-zero for the non-orientable ribbons, but it is insightful to see this directly through the symmetry of the ribbons. We thank an anonymous referee for pointing out this argument.

Recall that, along the centerline
\rho=0
, the tangent vectors of the closed non-orientable bent helicoids satisfy

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\phi+2\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\phi),

(61)

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\phi+2\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\phi).

The Cauchy-Riemann equations (
1
) imply the additional symmetries for the tangent vectors of the conjugate surface,
\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi+2\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi),

(62)

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi+2\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi).

A direct computation using these symmetries and the Cauchy-Riemann equations shows that

\partial_{\phi}\left[\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi+2\pi)-\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi)\right]=-2\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi)=-2\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\phi).

(63)

The conjugate surface,
\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}
is explicitly given by
[
1
]

\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(\rho,\phi)=\int_{0}^{\phi}\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}(\rho,\tilde{\phi})~\mathrm{d\tilde{\phi}}~-\int_{0}^{\rho}\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{q}(\tilde{\rho},0)~\mathrm{d\tilde{\rho}}.

(64)

Integrating Eq.(
63
) from
0
to
\phi
, and evaluating Eq. (
64
) along the centerline,
\rho=0
, shows that

\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi+2\pi)=-\mathbf{X}_{q}^{C}(0,\phi)+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{q}(0,\tilde{\phi})~\mathrm{d\tilde{\phi}},

(65)

where the integral in the last expression is the integral in Eq. (
52
). Therefore, for odd
q
, the conjugate bent helicoids are always disconnected.

Finally, we note that this argument applies generally to non-orientable minimal surfaces. If
\mathbf{X}^{0}
is non-orientable, there exists a non-contractible loop,
\alpha
, over which the unit normal of the surface,
\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{0}
, flips sign after parallel transporting around the loop,
\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{0}\mapsto-\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{0}
. Using the Björling construction, a small neighborhood around
\alpha
can be reconstructed in isothermal coordinates
(\rho,\phi)
using the loop
\alpha(\phi)
and the unit normal
\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{0}(\phi)
. The resulting surface,
\mathbf{X}_{\text{b}}^{0}
(b for Björling) is a ribbon whose tangent vectors along the centerline,
\partial_{\phi}\mathbf{X}_{\text{b}}^{0}(0,\phi)=\alpha^{\prime}(\phi)
and
\partial_{\rho}\mathbf{X}_{\text{b}}^{0}(0,\phi)=\hat{\mathbf{N}}^{0}(\phi)\times\alpha^{\prime}(\phi)
, satisfy the same symmetries as the tangent vectors of the non-orientable bent helicoids, Eqs. (
61
), by construction. The displacement of the endpoints on the centerline is still given by Eq. (
52
) because the centerline is a closed loop. The Cauchy-Riemann equations Eqs. (
1
) hold as well in this region because the ribbon is parameterized in isothermal coordinates. Thus, the same argument as the one used for the non-orientable bent helicoids shows that the Bonnet isometry of the ribbon contained in
\mathbf{X}^{0}
,
\mathbf{X}_{\text{b}}=\cos\theta\mathbf{X}^{0}_{\text{b}}+\sin\theta\mathbf{X}^{0,C}_{\text{b}}
, disconnects the ribbon. Hence, the original surface,
\mathbf{X}^{0}
, also becomes disconnected.

II.4
Symmetry of isometries

Suppose that
\psi[(-1)^{q}\rho,\phi+2\pi]=\sigma\psi(\rho,\phi)
for some sign
\sigma=\pm 1
. Then

\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{\rho}(\rho,\phi)&=&\partial_{\phi}\psi(\rho,\phi)\\
u_{\phi}(\rho,\phi)&=&-\partial_{\rho}\psi(\rho,\phi)\end{array}.

(66)

Therefore, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{\rho}[(-1)^{q}\rho,\phi+2\pi]&=&\sigma u_{\rho}(\rho,\phi)\\
u_{\phi}[(-1)^{q}\rho,\phi+2\pi]&=&-\sigma u_{\phi}(\rho,\phi)\end{array}.

(67)

Since
\zeta=b^{ij}D_{i}u_{j}/(4K)
, we can use
u_{i}
to obtain the symmetry of
\zeta
,
\zeta[(-1)^{q}\rho,\phi+2\pi]=\sigma\zeta(\rho,\phi)
.

Finally, we consider the symmetry of
\delta\mathbf{X}(\rho,\phi)=u^{i}\partial_{i}\mathbf{X}+\zeta\hat{\mathbf{N}}
,

\delta\mathbf{X}[(-1)^{q}\rho,\phi+2\pi]=(-1)^{q}\sigma\delta\mathbf{X}(\rho,\phi)

(68)

Therefore, any isometry that preserves the connectivity of a ribbon must have
\sigma=(-1)^{q}
.

II.5
Infinitesimal isometries of the catenoid

To find the infinitesimal isometries of the catenoid, we express Eq. (
35
) in isothermal coordinates,

\cosh^{2}\rho\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial_{\rho}\psi}{\cosh^{2}\rho}\right)-\partial_{\phi}^{2}\psi=0.

(69)

Using the expansion
\psi=\sum_{m}\psi_{m}(\rho)e^{im\phi}
, we have to solve the system of ODEs,

\cosh^{2}\rho\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial_{\rho}\psi_{m}}{\cosh^{2}\rho}\right)+m^{2}\psi_{m}=0

(70)

which has a solution

\psi_{m}=a_{m}\cosh\rho P_{1}^{~\sqrt{1-m^{2}}}(\tanh\rho)+b_{m}\cosh\rho Q_{1}^{~\sqrt{1-m^{2}}}(\tanh\rho),

(71)

where
P_{l}^{~n}(x)
and
Q_{l}^{~n}(x)
are the associated Legendre polynomials. Note that they can be analytically continued to complex
n
.

We can also compute the circulation of this solution,
-\triangle\psi=\chi
, to see that

\chi=-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\rho}\sum_{m}e^{im\phi}\left(m^{2}\psi_{m}(\rho)-\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\right).

(72)

Note that
\int d\phi~\chi(\rho,\phi)=4b_{0}\tanh\rho
showing that the circulation along the centerline,
\rho=0
has at least two zeros. The Bonnet isometry, therefore, cannot have zero shear strain while maintaining the periodicity of the catenoid.

Although Eq. (
71
) provides an analytical expression for all infinitesimal isometries of the catenoid, it is easier and more insightful to consider the series expansion,
\psi=\sum_{n}\psi_{n}(\phi)\rho^{n}
. The bending energy is

E=\int d\phi~\left\{\kappa w\left[\left(\psi_{1}+\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{\prime}\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\kappa w^{3}\left[(\psi_{0}+\psi_{0}^{\prime\prime})^{\prime\prime\prime}\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\kappa w^{3}(\psi_{1}+\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}(\psi_{1}+2\psi_{1}^{\prime}+\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(w^{5})\right\},

(73)

and assuming a force directed along the normal of magnitude
\delta(\rho)f(\phi)
, the work done is

W=-\int d\phi~f(\phi)\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\phi).

(74)

We balance these two energies, as our numerical simulations of the catenoid suggest that the stretching energy is negligible. The leading order term,
\psi_{0}
, satisfies

\left(\psi_{0}+\psi_{0}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}=0,

(75)

from which we obtain the general solution
\psi_{0}(\phi)=c_{0}+c_{1}\phi+c_{2}\phi^{2}+c_{3}\cos\phi+c_{4}\sin\phi
. The constant
c_{0}
does not correspond to a deformation; hence, we set it to zero. All other constants except
c_{2}
correspond to Euclidean motions, but
c_{2}
describes a deformation for which
\epsilon^{ij}D_{i}u_{j}=c_{2}
. We therefore interpret it as an infinitesimal Bonnet isometry. However, since
\psi_{0}^{\prime}(\phi)
is not periodic unless
c_{2}=0
, we see that the Bonnet isometry is, indeed, suppressed. The equilibrium equation for
\psi_{1}(\phi)
is

\mathrm{A}+\frac{3f(\phi)}{2\kappa w(3+w^{2})}-\psi_{1}^{\prime}-2\psi_{1}^{(3)}-\psi_{1}^{(5)}=0

(76)

to lowest order in
w
and
\mathrm{A}
is a constant. To simulate pulling, we let
f(\phi)=f_{0}/\Delta w
in two bands of width
\Delta w
centered on
\pm\pi/2
. Assuming
\psi_{1}
is continuous and periodic, the resulting solution is shown in Fig. (
3
). We note that the solution shows good agreement with
numerics with no fitting parameters. Unfortunately, for
q\neq 0
the functions
\psi_{0}(\phi)
and
\psi_{1}(\phi)
become mixed at every order, and we are unable to find a simple solution.

III.1
Discrete twisted ribbons

The ribbon is modeled as a triangular mesh (Fig.
4
) whose vertices have coordinates in
(\rho,\phi)
. The equilibrium length of the edge joining vertex
i
and vertex
j
,
\bar{l}_{ij}
, is determined by mapping the rectangular grid to
\mathbf{X}_{q}
.

For the stretching energy, we use

E_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}a_{ij}\left(l_{ij}/\bar{l}_{ij}-1\right),

(77)

summed over all pairs of edges
\langle i,j\rangle
. Here,
a_{ij}
denotes the
1/3
the area of the triangles associated with the edge between vertex
i
and
j
. To test the convergence of this energy, we compute the stretching energy of a flat rectangular sheet biaxially expanded by 10%. Then
E_{s}/A=0.01
where
A
is the area of the sheet before stretching. We further tested that the elastic energy increases quadratically with strain.

For the bending energy, we use

E_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\kappa\sum_{i}a_{i}H_{i}^{2},

(78)

where
H_{i}
is the vertex mean curvature,
a_{i}
is
1/3
the areas of the faces sharing the edge, and the sum is over all interior vertices. To compute
H_{i}^{2}
, we first orient the faces surrounding vertex
i
, then compute the change in area when displacing the vertex using the graph Laplacian formula, and finally square the result.

For
q=4
and
q=6
, we plot the elastic stretching and bending energies as a function of Bonnet angle in Fig.
4
. While the Bonnet isometry is not strictly a symmetry of the ribbons, it appears to converge (albeit slowly) to zero as the resolution increases.

Topological Spin-Orbit Torque in Ferrimagnetic Weyl Semimetal Preprint: APS/123-QED
Tomonari Meguro 1, Akihiro Ozawa 2, Koji Kobayashi 3, Yasufumi Araki 4, Kentaro Nomura 1

Abstract
The spin-orbit torque (SOT) in a compensated ferrimagnetic Weyl semimetal, Ti 2 ​ MnAl \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl} , is studied by the linear response theory. We elucidate that the SOT driven by all the occupied electronic states is present in magnetic Weyl semimetal, unlike in conventional metallic magnets. Around the energy of the Weyl points, we find that such an SOT is dominant and almost independent of the disorder. The emergence of the SOT in Ti 2 ​ MnAl \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl} can be understood from the structure of the mixed Berry curvature around the Weyl points, which is similar to that of the ordinary Berry curvature.



Introduction—
Efficient electrical control of electron spins
Dyakonov and Perel 1971
;
Kato et al. 2004
;
Sinova et al. 2015
;
Manchon et al. 2019
is one of the fundamental aims of spintronics.
Spin-orbit coupling (SOC) plays a significant role by correlating the electron spin and orbital motion.
The SOC enables the electrical generation of spin polarization in heterostructures
Sinova et al. 2015
;
Mihai Miron et al. 2010
;
Miron et al. 2011
;
Suzuki et al. 2011
;
Emori et al. 2013
;
Jamali et al. 2013
;
Garello et al. 2013
or bulk noncentrosymmetric crystals
Železný et al. 2014
;
Železný et al. 2017
;
Wadley et al. 2016
.
The generated spin polarization acts as an effective magnetic field for magnetic moments,
whose effect is called the spin-orbit torque (SOT).
The SOT has been intensely studied as a promising method for functional control of magnetization
Chernyshov et al. 2009
;
Mihai Miron et al. 2010
;
Liu et al. 2012a
;
Liu et al. 2012b
;
Fukami et al. 2016
.
However, to obtain a large SOT, one needs to inject a large longitudinal current,
which suffers from the energy dissipation by the Joule heating.
Such an SOT is termed the “dissipative SOT”.
The problem of dissipation is crucial in metals with a large density of states (DOS) near the Fermi level
E_{\textrm{F}}
,
where longitudinal conductivity
\sigma_{xx}
is proportional to the DOS.
The dissipation comes from the scattering of a large number of electrons around
E_{\textrm{F}}
by disorders and phonons.
To avoid the dissipation, we need systems
that
can generate the SOT with a small longitudinal current.

To suppress the dissipation, we focus on the
characteristic transport properties of magnetic Weyl semimetals (MWSMs).
The MWSMs feature linear dispersion and point nodes called Weyl points (WPs)

Murakami 2007
;
Wan et al. 2011
;
Burkov and Balents 2011
.
Since the DOS near the energy of the WPs is negligibly small,
\sigma_{xx}
tends to be suppressed, leading to
the small dissipation
in the longitudinal transport.
The SOT in bulk MWSMs has been studied
based on the continuum model with
isotropic spin-momentum locking and broken inversion symmetry
Kurebayashi et al. 2021
;
Burkov et al. 2023
.
The SOT obtained in these studies
needs a finite DOS by shifting
E_{\textrm{F}}
away from the WPs, similar to
\sigma_{xx}
, corresponding to the dissipative SOT.
By contrast,
non-dissipative
transport phenomena, such as the anomalous Hall effect,
arise from the nontrivial band topology in MWSMs.
The band topology is characterized by the Berry curvature
(BC)
from the WPs in momentum space
Armitage et al. 2018
;
Xiao et al. 2010
.
The anomalous Hall conductivity
\sigma_{yx}
corresponds to the integral of the BC over all the occupied electronic states below
E_{\textrm{F}}
.
Such a mechanism is termed the “topological effect”.

The existence of the SOT driven by all the occupied electronic states below
E_{\textrm{F}}
has been pointed out in several metallic magnets
Kurebayashi et al. 2014
;
Železný et al. 2014
;
Železný et al. 2017
;
Wadley et al. 2016
.
We here refer to such a non-dissipative SOT
as the “topological SOT”.
Theoretically, the topological
SOT can be characterized by a mixed Berry curvature (MBC)
Freimuth et al. 2014
.
The MBC is the extension of the BC in
momentum space to the composite parameter space
spanned by momentum and magnetization.
In the systems analyzed so far,
the magnitude of topological SOT is
smaller than the dissipative SOT.
To enhance the topological SOT with low Joule heating,
materials showing a large MBC are desired.

In this letter, we find a large topological SOT in a model of MWSM
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
.

\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
is one of the candidate systems of ideal MWSMs with compensated ferrimagnetic ordering
Shi et al. 2018
.
In
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
, both the time-reversal and inversion symmetries are broken.
We show that, in
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
, the topological SOT is significantly larger than dissipative SOT near the energy of WPs with a typical disorder strength.
Compared with the SOTs studied in the bulk of noncentrosymmetric antiferromagnets
Železný et al. 2014
;
Železný et al. 2017
;
Wadley et al. 2016
,
we find a giant
effective magnetic field
in
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
.
These properties enable us to
electrically control the compensated ferrimagnetic ordering
with low dissipation in the bulk system.
By analyzing the distribution of the MBC,
we find that the origin of the large topological SOT is the MBC enhanced around the WPs.
With such a large topological SOT,
we expect the potential utility of noncentrosymmetric MWSMs, including
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
, for integrated spintronics devices.

Model—

Before proceeding to our analysis, first
we explain the electronic properties of the ferrimagnetic Weyl semimetal
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}

Shi et al. 2018
.
The crystal structure of
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
is an inverse Heusler type with space group
{F}{\bar{4}}3m

Skaftouros et al. 2013
;
Feng et al. 2015
as shown in Fig.
1
(a).
Magnetic moments at two Ti sites anti-parallelly align to that at the Mn site, showing zero net magnetization.
The first-principles calculations of the electronic band structure predict
that the WPs are located near
E_{\textrm{F}}
, where the DOS is negligibly small.
These properties lead to the large anomalous Hall angle
\theta_{\rm AHE}=\sigma_{yx}/\sigma_{xx}

Shi et al. 2018
.
Since not only the time-reversal symmetry but also the inversion symmetry is broken due to its crystalline structure,
the SOT is permitted in bulk.

For our calculations, we introduce a tight-binding model of
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}

Meguro et al. 2024
.
In reality, all
d
orbitals mainly contribute to the electronic structure near
E_{\textrm{F}}
.
For our minimal model to describe the low-energy band structure around
E_{\textrm{F}}
,
we consider a single isotropic orbital for two Ti and Mn as the basis to satisfy the symmetry of the crystalline structure.
Here, to distinguish two different Ti sites,
we use the notations Ti1 and Ti2 as shown in Fig.
1
(a).
We neglect the orbital at the Al site for simplicity,
while the effect of Al is substantially incorporated via SOC as discussed later.

Our Hamiltonian
H
consists of a hopping term, on-site energy, SOC, and exchange coupling:

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle-\!\sum_{\braket{ij}\alpha\beta}\sum_{s}t_{\alpha\beta}C^{\dagger}_{i\alpha s}C_{j\beta s}+\sum_{i\alpha}\sum_{s}\epsilon_{\alpha}C^{\dagger}_{i\alpha s}C_{i\alpha s}

\displaystyle+\!i\frac{2\lambda_{\rm SOC}}{a^{2}}\sum_{\braket{ij}\alpha}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}C^{\dagger}_{i\alpha s}\left[(\bm{d}^{\alpha ij}_{1}\times\bm{d}^{\alpha ij}_{2})\cdot\bm{\sigma}\right]_{ss^{\prime}}C_{j\alpha s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\!\sum_{i\alpha}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}J_{\alpha}C^{\dagger}_{i\alpha s}(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\hat{\bm{n}})_{ss^{\prime}}C_{i\alpha s^{\prime}}.

(1)

Here,
C^{\dagger}_{i\alpha s}
denotes an electron creation operator of
sublattice
\alpha=
Ti1, Ti2, and Mn with the spin
s=\uparrow,\downarrow
at
i
site.
We briefly explain each term in the following
(see Appendix A for details of our tight-binding model).

The first term includes the inter- and intra-sublattice hoppings.
The second term denotes the on-site energy
\epsilon_{\alpha}
for each sublattice.
The third term
describes the effect of
SOC due to the local breaking of inversion symmetry
for the intra-sublattice hoppings.

\bm{d}^{\alpha ij}_{1,2}
are the two nearest-neighbor hopping vectors from the site
i
to
j
of the sublattice
\alpha
,
which are introduced according to the Fu-Kane-Mele model
Fu et al. 2007
.
We consider SOC for the Ti2-Ti2 and Mn-Mn hoppings with the same strength
\lambda_{\rm SOC}
.
We neglect the SOC for the Ti1-Ti1
for simplicity.
The fourth term represents the exchange coupling between conduction electron spin
\bm{\sigma}
and
the ferrimagnetic ordering.
The direction of the ferrimagnetic ordering is represented by the unit vector
\hat{\bm{n}}
, and is fixed to the
z
-direction in the following calculations.
The ferrimagnetism is encodeded in the mean-field coupling strength
J_{\alpha}
for each sublattice
\alpha
,
where the signs of
J_{\rm Ti1}
and
J_{\rm Ti2}
are taken opposite to
J_{\rm Mn}
.

Once the model is given,
we recall the electronic structure
around
E_{\textrm{F}}
.
The energy spectrum obtained by the Bloch Hamiltonian of
H
, denoted as
\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})

exhibits the WPs as shown in Fig.
1

Red and blue points correspond to the WPs with positive (
+
) and negative (
-

We obtain eight WPs on the

as well as
on the

k_{z}=0

We determine

from the 4/6 filling condition.
In this study, we focus on the behavior of SOT around the energy of the WPs,
-E_{0}\leq E_{\textrm{F}}\leq E_{0}
, where
E_{0}=50
meV
characteries the energy scale exhibiting the Weyl dispersion around the WPs.

Electrically induced spin density—

We now proceed to the analysis of the SOT in this model.
The conduction electron spin driven by the electric field acts as an effective magnetic field
and exerts the SOT on the magnetic moments at
{}\mathrm{Ti}1
,
{}\mathrm{Ti}2
, and
{}\mathrm{Mn}
.
In the linear response regime, the spin density at sublattice
\alpha
(
={\rm Ti1},{\rm Ti2},{\rm Mn}
) is calculated by using the Kubo formula
Manchon et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\frac{\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{\mu}}}{E_{x}}

\displaystyle=i\hbar\lim_{\bm{q}\rightarrow 0}\sum_{n\neq m}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{f_{n\bm{k}}-f_{m\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{E_{n\bm{k}}-E_{m\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}\frac{S^{\alpha,\mu}_{nm,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}J^{x}_{mn,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{E_{n\bm{k}}-E_{m\bm{k}+\bm{q}}+i\eta}.

(2)

Here, the index
\mu(=x,y,z)
denotes the direction
of the induced spin density,
whereas the direction of the external electric field

\bm{E}
is fixed to the
x
-direction.
We define the spin matrix element

S^{\alpha,\mu}_{nm,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}=\langle n\bm{k}|\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{\mu}|m\bm{k}+\bm{q}\rangle
,
where
\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{\mu}
is the electron spin operator at the sublattice
\alpha
,
and
|n{\bm{k}\rangle}
is the eigenstate with eigenenergy
E_{n\bm{k}}
of
\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})

J^{x}_{mn,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}=\langle m\bm{k}+\bm{q}|(-e\hat{v}_{x})|n{\bm{k}\rangle}
is the current matrix element where
-e\hat{\bm{v}}=-\frac{e}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\bm{k}}
is the current operator.

f_{n\bm{k}}=1/(e^{(E_{n\bm{k}}-E_{\textrm{F}})/{k_{\textrm{B}}T}}+1)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function,
with
T=0
here.
The integral is taken over the Brillouin zone, with
\int_{\bm{k}}=(2\pi)^{-3}\int d^{3}\bm{k}
.
For the momentum integration, the momentum space is split into the mesh of
250\times 250\times 250
for all calculations in this paper.
We introduce the impurity effect by the first Born approximation,
so that the self energy part
\eta

2

\frac{\hbar}{2\tau}

\tau

\displaystyle\frac{\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{\mu}}^{\rm dis}}{E_{x}}

\displaystyle=2\tau\sum_{n}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\partial f_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial E}S^{\alpha,\mu}_{nn,\bm{k}}J^{x}_{nn,\bm{k}},

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{\mu}}^{\rm top}}{E_{x}}

Effective magnetic field and spin-orbit torque—

Above results show that, near

(5)

Conclusion—

Our study clarifies that the ferrimagnetic Weyl semimetal
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
shows distinct behavior in the SOT.
The previous studies
on the SOT in MWSMs discussed only the dissipative SOT, but not the topological SOT
Kurebayashi et al. 2021
;
Burkov et al. 2023
.
In contrast, the present study finds that ferrimagnetic Weyl semimetal
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
shows the giant topological SOT,
which is useful for dissipation-free switching of magnetization.
The key element for this topological SOT is the MBC from all the occupied states.
In
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
, this topological quantity is notably enhanced around the WPs in momentum space.
Moreover, due to the tiny carrier density around the energy of the WPs,
the longitudinal current gets suppressed.
As a result of these characteristics in
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{MnAl}
,
the efficiency of the effective magnetic field per the injected current reaches around
10-1000 times those estimated previously in noncentrosymmetric antiferromagnets.
These results suggest that the design of the MBC
in magnetic topological systems, such as MWSMs,
is important for the further development of SOT-based spintronics devices.

A curvature flow approach to dorsal closure modelling
Shuhui He, Ben Whale, Glen Wheeler, Valentina-Mira Wheeler

Abstract
. In this paper we propose and study a curvature-based mathematical model for dorsal closure in embryonic drosophila. Using an analysis that mixes maximum-principle and integral-estimates, we establish global existence and convergence for data that mimics the initial geometry of a dorsal closure event. Further, we present a numerical approximation scheme for the flow, establishing stability, consistence, and convergence. We also give sample simulations of the flow with initial configurations that include experimentally observed data.

1.
Introduction

Dorsal closure (DC) is a critical morphogenetic stage in
Drosophila
embryogenesis.
Because DC is a naturally occurring epithelial-closure event, it provides an accessible model for embryonic wound healing and related morphogenetic processes.
Although DC has been studied extensively
[
16
,
30
,
15
,
26
,
19
,
1
]
, the biological and physical mechanisms that drive it are still not fully understood.

During DC an elliptical gap on the dorsal mid-line of the embryo closes over several hours.
At the outset a roughly elliptical epithelial opening, covered by the amnioserosa (a transient extra-embryonic tissue), forms on the dorsal surface.
The interface between epidermis and amnioserosa is called the
leading edge
(LE).
The major axis of the opening coincides with the anterior–posterior (AP) axis of the embryo.
Soon after closure begins, the ellipse develops sharp corners at the AP termini and becomes eye-shaped; these corner points are the
canthi
.
Throughout DC the upper and lower halves of the LE advance towards the AP axis under several forces and ultimately meet to leave a seam (Figure
1
).

Figure 1
.
Stages of dorsal closure and the principal forces involved. The dotted line marks the AP axis.
(A) Initial stage: epidermal tension (green), amnioserosa contraction (light blue), zipping at the canthi (red), and global actin-cable contraction (blue) act on the opening.
(B) Intermediate stage: an eye-shaped opening develops; the red lines highlight the non-smooth LE geometry at the canthi produced by zipping.
(C) Final stage: the opening has closed, leaving a seam along the AP axis.

Most modelling efforts focus on the evolution of the LE.
Four principal force categories act on it during DC
[
17
,
1
,
16
,
30
]
:
(i) actin-cable contraction, (ii) zipping, (iii) amnioserosa contraction, and (iv) epidermal tension.
A dense actomyosin cable encircles the opening at the onset of DC; its contraction produces the so-called purse-string effect, shrinking the opening at a rate proportional to curvature.

Zipping
arises when micron-scale actin protrusions from opposite sides of the mid-line interlock and pull the tissue edges together, an effect most visible near the canthi.
Amnioserosa contraction generates an inward normal force, whereas epidermal tension is a comparatively small outward force that resists closure.

A prototypical DC model, proposed by Hutson
et al.

[
15
]
, balances these forces on two idealised circular arcs representing the LE and yields a pair of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) for the opening’s length and width.
Subsequent work refined the empirical zipping term to capture asymmetric scenarios
[
26
,
19
]
.
Almeida
et al.

[
1
]
advanced the framework with a partial differential equation (PDE) scheme and an alternative treatment of zipping based on chord–arc ratios, but provided no rigorous mathematical analysis.

Building on these ideas, we propose a modified DC model that admits rigorous analysis.
Idealising the LE as a closed plane curve
\gamma:[0,1]\!\times\![0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we let it evolve according to

(1)

\partial_{t}\gamma(p,t)=\alpha(p,t)\,\vec{k}(p,t)+\beta(p,t)\,\nu(p,t)+\zeta(p,t),

where
\vec{k}
is the curvature vector,
\nu
the inward unit normal,
\alpha
encodes actin-cable tension,
\beta
represents the combined effect of amnioserosa contraction and epidermal tension, and
\zeta
models zipping through the chord–arc ratio.

Two simplifications make (
1
) tractable.
First, because actin-cable tension and amnioserosa contraction act in the same direction and are difficult to distinguish experimentally
[
1
]
, we combine them into a single curvature-driven term.
Second, instead of modelling the highly localised zipping forces at the canthi directly, we introduce a smooth global term that captures their net effect.
With these adjustments, the evolution reduces to a curvature-driven (curve-shortening) flow with a restriction term—a class of flows that has been studied extensively
[
5
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
28
,
3
]
.

We orient the dorsal opening so that the AP axis coincides with the
x
-axis and the
y
-axis passes through its midpoint.
Assuming bilateral symmetry, we analyse only the upper‐right quarter of the LE, thereby simplifying the analysis slightly.
Denoting the curve by
\gamma:[0,1]\!\times\![0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the specific flow problem is

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma(u,t)

\displaystyle=\kappa(u,t)\,\nu(u,t)+Z^{\perp}(u,t),

\displaystyle(u,t)\in(0,1)\times[0,T),

\displaystyle\langle\tau,e_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle u=0,

\displaystyle\gamma(u,t)

\displaystyle=(\rho_{0},0),

\displaystyle u=1,

\displaystyle\gamma(u,0)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}(u),

\displaystyle u\in[0,1],

with

(3)

Z(u,t)=-\frac{1}{L(t)}\langle\nu,e_{1}\rangle\,\langle\nu,e_{2}\rangle\,e_{2},

where
L(t)
is the arclength of
\gamma(\cdot,t)
, and
e_{1},e_{2}
are the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The first boundary condition enforces orthogonality to the
y
-axis so that
\gamma
can be reflected smoothly; the second anchors the curve to the
x
-axis.
The term
Z(u,t)
ensures that zipping is most significant when the leading edge is about
54
degrees to the
x
-axis (approximately the angle at the canthi), and that the effect of zipping over the entire curve increases as the length decreases (through the factor
1/L(t)
).

1.1.
Analysis

While the flow (
2
) is significantly nonlinear, this is confined to lower-order terms and so local well-posedness is standard.

Global analysis however is far more delicate.
Here, we combine in a novel way maximum-principle and integral-estimate based analyses, yielding finally a powerful convergence result, Theorem
1.1
below.
Our treatment includes a weak solutions framework, enabling analysis of the smoothing effect.
The main utility of this result is that it confirms the model behaves as expected with initial data that resembles the geometry of a real dorsal closure event.
In formal terms, we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
2
) for a given initial curve
\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
of class
W^{2,\infty}
.
Suppose there exists a
C_{G}\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma_{0}
is
e_{2}
-graphical
with

G_{-e_{2}}(s,0):=\left<\nu(s,0),-e_{2}\right>\geq C_{G}\,.

Assume that
\gamma_{0}(s)\cdot e_{2}\leq\gamma_{0}(0)\cdot e_{2}
,
\gamma_{0}
has positive curvature at
u=0
, negative curvature at
u=1
, and exactly one point of zero curvature.

Then:

•

The maximal time of existence is infinite;

•

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the solution is smooth with uniform estimates in
C^{\infty}
on
(\varepsilon,\infty)
.

•

As
t\rightarrow\infty
the solution converges exponentially fast in the
smooth topology to the horizontal line segment connecting the origin to

(\rho_{0},0)
.

Remark
.

The initial assumptions on the geometry of
\gamma_{0}
mimic the observed DC geometry: the initial curve
\gamma_{0}
has positive curvature at
u=0
, negative curvature at
u=1
, and exactly one point of zero curvature.
It is graphical over the
x
-axis and, while not necessarily monotone from the reflection axis, has maximum width there.

Remark
.

If
\gamma_{0}
is more regular, uniform higher-order estimates hold on
(0,\infty)
—a prerequisite for proving convergence of the numerical scheme, which requires bounded fourth derivatives up to
t=0
.

1.2.
Numerical treatment

The second contribution of this paper is a considered numerical treatment of the flow.
Our plan is to use the graphical formulation (equation (
11
) to come) and prove stability, consistency and convergence results. We prove all results directly as there do not appear to be sufficiently general results for non-local PDE.

There are two main ideas.
First, that the non-linear terms can be controlled by an
L^{\infty}
norm of a derivative of the solution; and, second, we can make use of the existing estimates for the solution of the PDE. The result is that we first prove consistency then convergence and then stability.

Overview of the Numerical Experiments

Using the described numerical scheme, we performed high-resolution simulations of the flow (
2
).
We worked with the open-source
COFFEE
infrastructure
[
4
]
.
All runs were performed on a standard desktop (Intel
®
i5-10400 CPU, 16 GB RAM, Ubuntu 20.04) on successively refined one-dimensional grids with
n=20\times 2^{\,i}
,
i=0,\dots,10
.
We tested three representative initial geometries (Figure
3
):

(1)

Inflection profile
– a single-inflection sinusoid
g_{1}
calibrated to a length–height ratio of
2{:}1
;

(2)

Bump profile
– a compact perturbation
g_{2}
that introduces a localised bulge of controlled height and width;

(3)

Experimental profile
– a curve extracted from time-lapse microscopy of wild-type
Drosophila
dorsal closure.

Figure
4
illustrates the ensuing evolution for each case: the LE shortens monotonically, remains smooth, and converges rapidly to a horizontal segment, in agreement with Theorem
1.1
and aligning well with observed experimental evidence. Measured
\log_{2}L^{\infty}

2.
Short time existence for parametrised curves

We consider a one-parameter family of curves
\gamma:[0,1]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with velocity satisfying

(4)

\partial_{t}\gamma=\kappa+Z^{\perp}

where
\kappa
is the curvature vector of
\gamma
and
Z^{\perp}=\left<Z,\nu\right>\nu
is the normal part of the zipping force
Z
, given by

Z=-\frac{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}{L(t)}e_{2}\,.

Here we have used
L(t)
to denote the length of
\gamma(\cdot,t)
and
\nu_{1}
,

\nu_{2}
to denote the first and second components of the inward-pointing normal
vector
\nu
in the standard basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
.

Fixing a clockwise parametrisation for the evolving curve
\gamma:[0,1]\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and setting
\gamma(u,t)=(x(u,t),y(u,t))
, we have for the unit tangent, normal and
curvature vectors the formulae

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=|\gamma_{u}|^{-1}(x_{u},y_{u});\quad\nu=|\gamma_{u}|^{-1}(y_{u},-x_{u});

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}(x_{uu},y_{uu})+|\gamma_{u}|^{-1}(|\gamma_{u}|^{-1})_{u}(x_{u},y_{u})=|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}(x_{uu},y_{uu})+(|\gamma_{u}|^{-1})_{u}\tau=k\nu\,,

where

k=|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}\left<\gamma_{uu},\nu\right>\,.

Denoting by
s
the arclength paramter of the curve, so that
\partial_{s}=\partial_{u}/|\gamma_{u}|
,
the classical Frenet–Serret formulas are

(5)

\gamma_{s}=\tau,\qquad\gamma_{ss}=\tau_{s}=k\nu,\qquad\nu_{s}=-k\tau\,.

The zipping force can be expressed as

Z^{\perp}=-\frac{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L}\nu=-\frac{y_{u}x_{u}^{2}}{|\gamma_{u}|^{3}L}\nu\,.

The flow problem (
4
) becomes

(6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\gamma=\Big(|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}\left<\gamma_{uu},\nu\right>-\frac{y_{u}x_{u}^{2}}{|\gamma_{u}|^{3}L}\Big)\nu\,,\\
\tau(0,t)=e_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma(1,t)=(\rho_{0},0)\,.\end{cases}

supplemented with boundary conditions
\tau(0,t)=e_{1}=(1,0)
and
\gamma(1,t)=(\rho_{0},0)
.

Using a standard proof for smooth data (see for example Huisken-Polden

[
11
]
, Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva-Solonnikov
[
18
]
, Lunardi
[
21
]
and
Sharples
[
27
]
) we have the following local existence result for
(
6
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth map such that
|\gamma_{0}^{\prime}(u)|>0
for
all
u\in[0,1]
, satisfying
\tau_{0}(0)=(1,0)
and
\gamma_{0}(1)=(\rho_{0},0)

for a given
\rho_{0}>0
, as well as the compatibility condition
\kappa_{0}(1)=-Z^{\perp}_{0}(1)
.
There exist
T>0
and a smooth solution to (
6
), defined on

[0,1]\times[0,T)
, such that
\gamma(u,0)=\gamma_{0}(u)
for all
u\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

For smooth data the proof is standard and so we give only a brief outline.
If we write
\gamma([0,1],t)
as the normal graph of a function
f(u,t)
over
the initial curve
\gamma_{0}([0,1])
, so that

(7)

\gamma(u,t)=\gamma_{0}(u)+f(u,t)\nu_{0}(u)\,,

we have

\displaystyle|\gamma_{u}|=|\gamma^{0}_{u}|\sqrt{(1-fk^{0})^{2}+f_{s_{0}}^{2}}\,.

The evolution equation (
6
) becomes

\displaystyle f_{t}

\displaystyle=\Big(|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}\left<\gamma_{uu},\nu\right>-\frac{y_{u}x_{u}^{2}}{|\gamma_{u}|^{3}L}\Big)\left<\nu_{0},\nu\right>

\displaystyle=\bigg(f_{uu}\frac{|\gamma^{0}_{u}|(1-k_{0}f)}{(|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+f_{u}\frac{|\gamma_{u}^{0}|(-2k_{0}f_{u}-k^{0}_{u}f-(1-k_{0}f)(|\gamma_{u}|^{-1})_{u}}{(|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{(y^{0}_{u}(1-k_{0}f)-f_{u}x^{0}_{u}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{-1})(x^{0}_{u}(1-k_{0}f)+f_{u}y^{0}_{u}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{-1})^{2}}{L(t)(|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(8)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{|\gamma^{0}_{u}|k_{0}(1-k_{0}f)^{2}}{(|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg)\frac{(1-k_{0}f)|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2}}{|\gamma^{0}_{u}|\,\sqrt{|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2}}}\,.

supplemented with boundary conditions

\displaystyle f_{u}(0,t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad f(1,t)=0\,.

Although quite messy, (
8
) is a uniformly parabolic quasilinear
initial boundary value problem, and finding a solution for a short time with smooth initial data
with an arbitrary positive smooth function of time in place of
L
follows
by using any of the aforementioned references:
[
11
,
18
,
21
,
27
]
.
The conclusion for the particular choice of function

L[f]=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{|f_{u}|^{2}+(1-k_{0}f)^{2}|\gamma^{0}_{u}|^{2}}\,du

follows by using a fixed point argument exactly as in e.g. McCoy
[
22
]
(see also
[
29
]
).

Once we have solved for
f
, then we can use the formula (
7
) to reconstruct a solution to (
6
).
∎

3.
Commutators, further boundary conditions, and evolving length

Let us reparametrise the family of curves by its arc-length and denote the
arc-length parameter with
s
. The family of curves
\gamma(s,t)
is now
defined on the domain
[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)
with satisfying (
6
) and boundary conditions

\tau(0,t)=e_{1}
,
\gamma(L(t),t)=(\rho_{0},0)
.

We shall now derive the commutator for the operators
\partial_{s}
and
\partial_{t}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let

z=-\frac{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L(t)}\,.

The differential operators with respect to arc-length and time satisfy

\partial_{t}\partial_{s}=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}+\left(k^{2}+kz\right)\partial_{s}\,.

Proof.

With the Frenet-Serret equations, we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\gamma_{u}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left<\gamma_{ut},\gamma_{u}\right>=2|\gamma_{u}|^{2}\left<(\kappa+Z^{\perp})_{s},\gamma_{s}\right>

\displaystyle=2|\gamma_{u}|^{2}\left(-k^{2}-kz\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{s}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\big(|\gamma_{u}|^{-1}\partial_{u}\big)=|\gamma_{u}|^{-1}\partial_{u}\partial_{t}-|\gamma_{u}|^{-2}|\gamma_{u}|_{t}\,\partial_{u}

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}+\left(k^{2}+kz\right)\partial_{s}\,.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We have the following evolution equations

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle=\big(k_{s}+z_{s}\big)\nu\,.

\displaystyle\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=-\big(k_{s}+z_{s}\big)\tau\,.

Proof.

Applying the previous lemma to the flow equation, we calculate

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle=(\gamma_{s})_{t}=\gamma_{ts}+\left(k^{2}+kz\right)\tau

\displaystyle=k_{s}\nu-k^{2}\tau+z_{s}\nu-kz\tau+\left(k^{2}+kz\right)\tau

\displaystyle=\big(k_{s}+z_{s}\big)\nu\,.

As
\gamma(s,t)
are plane curves, we have
\nu_{t}=\lambda\tau
for a scalar
\lambda
. Thus by differentiating
\left<\tau,\nu\right>=0
, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=-\big(k_{s}+z_{s}\big)\tau\,.

∎

For convenience, we explicitly state the expression for derivatives of the scalar zipping factor
z
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We have

\displaystyle z_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle z_{ss}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{s}}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)+\frac{k^{2}}{L}\Big(-9\nu_{1}^{3}+7\nu_{1}\Big)\,.

The boundary conditions give rise to the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We have

k_{s}(0,t)=-\frac{k(0,t)}{L(t)}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad k(L(t),t)=\frac{\nu_{1}(L(t),t)\nu_{2}^{2}(L(t),t)}{L(t)}\,.

Proof.

Each relation follows from differentiating the boundary conditions in time.
For the boundary point at zero, we have (Lemma
3.2
)

0=\tau_{t}(0,t)=(k_{s}(0,t)+z_{s}(0,t))\nu(0,t)

which implies

k_{s}(0,t)=-z_{s}(0,t)\,.

We may further simplify this by noting that

z_{s}(0,t)=\frac{k(0,t)}{L(t)}

giving the first relation.
For the second, the Dirichlet condition gives
\gamma_{t}(L(t),t)=0
which is

0=k(L(t),t)+z(L(t),t)\,,

as required.
∎

We also calculate the evolution of length.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We have

L^{\prime}(t)\leq-\int_{\gamma}k^{2}ds

and if
\gamma_{2}(0,t)>0
for
t\in[0,T^{*})
, then

L^{\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{4\rho_{0}\gamma_{2}(0,t)^{2}}{(\rho^{2}_{0}+\gamma_{2}(0,t)^{2})^{2}}\,,\quad\text{ for $t\in[0,T^{*})$}\,.

Proof.

The arclength element evolves by

ds_{t}=(-k^{2}-kz)\,ds\,.

Therefore

(9)

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}-k^{2}ds+\frac{1}{L}\int_{\gamma}k\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}ds\,.

Now, note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\int_{\gamma}k\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\int_{\gamma}k\tau_{2}\nu_{2}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{L}\int_{\gamma}(\nu_{2})_{s}\nu_{2}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3L}\int_{\gamma}(\nu_{2}^{3})_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3L}\Big[(\nu_{2}^{3})(L(t),t)-(\nu_{2}^{3})(0,t)\Big]\,.

Now the second component of the unit normal takes the value
-1
at zero, and this is the smallest possible value it can take. This means that the term in the brackets is non-negative, which yields finally the estimate

\frac{1}{L}\int_{\gamma}k\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}ds\leq 0\,.

We therefore have

(10)

L^{\prime}(t)\leq-\int_{\gamma}k^{2}ds\,.

If
\gamma(0,t)>0
, then the curve with the smallest
||k||_{2}^{2}
that is smooth and connects the boundary points together is a circular arc with radius

r=\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}+\gamma_{2}(0,t)^{2}}{2\gamma_{2}(0,t)}\,.

The arclength of the circular arc is
r\arcsin(\rho_{0}/r)
, so we find the estimate

\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds\geq\frac{1}{r^{2}}r\arcsin(\rho_{0}/r)=\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\frac{\rho_{0}}{r}\arcsin(\rho_{0}/r)\,.

Clearly (from for instance the Taylor series for
x\mapsto x\arcsin(x)
) we have

\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds\geq\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\frac{\rho^{2}_{0}}{r^{2}}=\frac{4\rho_{0}\gamma_{2}(0,t)^{2}}{(\rho^{2}_{0}+\gamma_{2}(0,t)^{2})^{2}}\,.

Combining this estimate with (
10
) finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Graphical data

In the case where the initial data is graphical, which is the physically interesting setting, we are able to obtain finer results.
First, the evolution equation preserves graphicality, as per the following result that uses the maximum principle.

Theorem 4.1

(Graph preservation)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For a fixed vector
V\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, set

G_{V}(s,t):=\left<\nu(s,t),V\right>\,.

If
G_{-e_{2}}(s,0)>0
then
G_{-e_{2}}(s,t)>0
.

Proof.

We compute

\partial_{s}G_{V}=\left<\nu_{s},V\right>=-\left<k\tau,V\right>,

and

\partial_{ss}G_{V}=-k_{s}\left<\tau,V\right>-k^{2}\left<\nu,V\right>.

Recalling Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G_{V}

\displaystyle=\left<\nu_{t},V\right>=-\big(k_{s}+z_{s}\big)\left<\tau,V\right>

\displaystyle=-k_{s}\left<\tau,V\right>-\left<\tau,V\right>\frac{k}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=-k_{s}\left<\tau,V\right>+\partial_{s}G_{V}\frac{1}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)\,.

Thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G_{V}

\displaystyle=\partial_{ss}^{2}G_{V}+k^{2}G_{V}+\partial_{s}G_{V}\frac{1}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)\,.

By defining the operator
\mathcal{L}
to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\phi=\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{L}\Big(-3\nu_{2}^{3}+2\nu_{2}\Big)\partial_{s}-\partial_{ss}\right)\phi,

we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}G_{V}=k^{2}G_{V}\,.

Hence, by the minimum principle,

\min\limits_{[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)}G_{V}(s,t)\geq\min\big\{\min G_{V}(s,0),\min G_{V}(0,t),\min G_{V}(L(t),t)\big\}.

Now let us consider
V=-e_{2}
.
First,
G_{-e_{2}}(0,t)=1
, which is the largest value
G
can possibly take.

On the Dirichlet boundary, we apply the Hopf Lemma.
We find

0>(\partial_{s}G_{-e_{2}})(L(t),t)=k(L(t),t)\tau_{2}(L(t),t)\,.

From Lemma
3.4
,

k(L(t),t)\tau_{2}(L(t),t)=\frac{\nu_{1}\tau_{2}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L(t)}=\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L(t)}

and so

0>(\partial_{s}G_{-e_{2}})(L(t),t)=k(L(t),t)\tau_{2}(L(t),t)\geq 0\,,

a contradiction.
We conclude that no new minima for
G_{-e_{2}}
can be attained on either boundary, and so

\min\limits_{[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)}G_{V}(s,t)\geq\min\big\{\min G_{V}(s,0)\big\}\,,

implying the result.
∎

Remark
.

Along an
e_{2}
-graphical solution, we have
\tau_{2}(L(t),t)\leq 0
and so the
curvature at the right-hand boundary
k(L(t),t)
is
non-positive
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose

\gamma_{2}(s,0)>0\,,\quad\text{for all}\ s\in[0,L(0))\,.

Then

\gamma_{2}(s,t)>0\,,\quad\text{for all}\ (s,t)\in[0,L(t))\times[0,T)\,.

Proof.

We first prove the inequality holds in the interior. From (
4
) we calculate

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\partial_{ss}\right)\gamma_{2}=-\frac{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}=-\frac{\tau_{2}\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}=-\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}\partial_{s}\gamma_{2}\,.

That is,

\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}\partial_{s}-\partial_{ss}\right)\gamma_{2}=0\,.

The maximum and minimum principle apply to this operator and therefore also to
\gamma_{2}
.
In particular, we have

\min\limits_{[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)}\gamma_{2}(s,t)\geq\min\big\{\min\gamma_{2}(s,0),\min\gamma_{2}(0,t),\min\gamma_{2}(L(t),t)\big\}.

If there is a new minimum at the left hand boundary point, the Hopf Lemma implies

0>-(\partial_{s}\gamma_{2})(0,t)=-\tau_{2}(0,t)=0

which is a contradiction.
Therefore the minimum of
\gamma_{2}
(under the given initial condition) is
attained at the Dirichlet boundary point, and furthermore, there are no new
local minima attained by
\gamma_{2}
on the interior or at the Neumann boundary.
∎

Remark
.

If the initial curve has only one (interior) inflection point, then the
Sturmian theorem can be used to preserve
k(0,t)>0
and
k(L(t),t)<0
for

t\in[0,T)
.
Then, because

(\partial_{t}\gamma_{2})(0,t)=-\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}\tau_{2}+k\nu_{2}=-k(0,t)

we have strict monotonicity of the height at the Neumann boundary.

Proposition 4.3

(Enclosed area decay)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L(t)]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the solution to (
6
) given
by Theorem
2.1
for a given initial curve

\gamma_{0}:[0,L(0)]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose
\gamma_{0}
is
e_{2}
-graphical with

G_{-e_{2}}(s,0)\geq C_{G}

where
C_{G}\in(0,1)
.
Assume further that
\gamma_{2}(s,0)\leq\gamma_{2}(0,0)

Then the area bounded by the curve and the positive axes decays exponentially fast, satisfying

A(t)<A(0)e^{-\frac{C_{G}^{2}}{\rho_{0}L(0)}t}\,,

for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

Calculating,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\gamma}\gamma_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}-\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}}{L}\partial_{s}\gamma_{2}+\partial_{ss}\gamma_{2}\,ds+\int_{\gamma}-k^{2}\gamma_{2}-kz\gamma_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma}3k\gamma_{2}\frac{\tau_{2}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L}\,ds+\Big[-\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}\gamma_{2}}{L}+\tau_{2}\Big]_{0}^{L}+\int_{\gamma}-k^{2}\gamma_{2}+k\gamma_{2}\frac{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}}{L}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\gamma_{2}\,ds-\frac{2}{L}\int k\gamma_{2}\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}\,ds+\Big[-\frac{\nu_{2}^{3}\gamma_{2}}{L}+\tau_{2}\Big]_{0}^{L}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\gamma_{2}\,ds-\frac{2}{L}\int k\gamma_{2}\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}\,ds+\tau_{2}(L,t)-\frac{\gamma_{2}(0,t)}{L}\,.

Now we use the initial conditions.
Note that the maximum principle (see Lemma
4.2
) implies that the initial condition
\gamma_{2}(s,0)\leq\gamma_{2}(0,0)
is preserved, that is,

\gamma_{2}(s,t)\leq\gamma_{2}(0,t)\,.

Theorem
4.1
preserves graphicality, and in particular we have

\nu_{1}^{2}\leq 1-C_{G}^{2}\,.

We also trivially have
\nu_{2}^{2}<1
(note the inequality is strict for
s\in(0,L(t)]
, by the maximum principle applied to
\gamma_{2}
(see Lemma

4.2
)).
Therefore

-\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\gamma_{2}\,ds-\frac{2}{L}\int k\gamma_{2}\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}\,ds\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int\nu_{1}^{2}\nu_{2}^{4}\gamma_{2}\,ds<(1-C_{G}^{2})\frac{\gamma_{2}(0,t)}{L}\,.

Finally, note that Lemma
4.1
also implies
\tau_{2}(L(t),t)\leq 0
.
Combining this with the evolution of
A
above, we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\gamma}\gamma_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle<(1-C_{G}^{2})\frac{\gamma_{2}(0,t)}{L(t)}-\frac{\gamma_{2}(0,t)}{L(t)}

\displaystyle=-C_{G}^{2}\frac{\gamma_{2}(0,t)}{L(t)}\,.

Since
A(t)\leq\gamma_{2}(0,t)\rho_{0}
(this is because
0\leq\gamma_{2}(s,t)\leq\gamma_{2}(0,t)
), and
\rho_{0}\leq L(t)\leq L(0)
we find

A^{\prime}(t)<-\frac{C_{G}^{2}}{\rho_{0}L(0)}A(t)\,.

This yields the stated decay estimate.
∎

We take now the parametrisation

\gamma(u,t)=(u,h(u,t))\,,

with
u\in[0,\rho_{0}]
and
h\in C^{\infty}([0,\rho_{0}])
, with the following boundary conditions:

h_{u}(0,t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad h(\rho_{0},t)=0\,,

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
In this parametrisation, the evolution equation (
6
) becomes

(11)

h_{t}=\frac{h_{uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\,.

We say that
\gamma
solves (
11
)
iff
\gamma(u,t)=(u,h(u,t))
, where the function
h
solves (
11
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
h_{0}\in C^{\infty}([0,\rho_{0}])
, with
(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
and
h_{0}(\rho_{0})=0
.
Then, the maximal time of existence is infinite, and equation
(
11
) admits a smooth solution
h\in C^{\infty}([0,\rho_{0}]\times[0,\infty))
.

Proof.

By standard parabolic regularity theory
[
20
,
18
]
, if the maximal time of
existence is finite, then the gradient of
h
must blow up as
t\rightarrow T
.
However Theorem
4.1
ensures that
h_{u}
is uniformly bounded
for all
t
, and so we conclude that
T=\infty
.
∎

We now prove the following compactness result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
T>0
be arbitrary.
Let
h_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,\rho_{0}])
, with
(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
(weakly) and
h_{0}(\rho_{0})=0
.
Take
h_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,\rho_{0}]\times[0,T])
to be the solutions of
(
11
) given by Theorem
4.4
with
h_{n}(\cdot,0)\rightarrow h_{0}
in
H^{1}
.
Then there exists
h\in H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,\rho_{0}]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],W^{1,\infty}([0,\rho_{0}]))

such that
h_{n}\to h
, up to a subsequence, uniformly on
[0,\rho_{0}]\times[0,T]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
for any
T>0
, the solutions
h_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T],W^{1,\infty}([0,\rho_{0}]))
.
In order to prove the result we also need a uniform bound for
h_{n}
in
H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,\rho_{0}]))
.
For this it remains to estimate

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}|(h_{n})_{t}|^{2}\,du\,dt\,.

We find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}|(h_{n})_{t}|^{2}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\Big|\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}}{1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[(h_{n})_{]}}\frac{(h_{n})_{u}}{1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2}}\Big|^{2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du+\frac{2}{L^{2}[(h_{n})]}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{u}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du+\frac{2}{\rho_{0}^{2}}\,.

Preservation of graphicality (Theorem
4.1
) implies the gradient bound

(h_{n})_{u}^{2}\leq\frac{1-C_{G}^{2}}{C_{G}^{2}}

and in particular

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2}}}\geq C_{G}\,.

(Recall that
C_{G}
depends only on
\lVert(h_{0})_{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
.)
This allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{3}}(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,du

\displaystyle\geq C_{G}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du\,.

We then integrate Lemma
3.5
to find

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{(h_{n})_{uu}^{2}}{(1+(h_{n})_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{G}^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=2C_{G}^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}-L^{\prime}(t)\,dt=2C_{G}^{-1}(L(0)-L(T))\leq 2C_{G}^{-1}L(0)

which depends only on
\lVert(h_{0})_{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
.

We therefore find

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}|(h_{n})_{t}|^{2}\,du\,dt\leq 2C_{G}^{-1}L(0)+\frac{2T}{\rho_{0}^{2}}

which is an estimate that depends only on
\lVert(h_{0})_{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
and
T
.

It then follows that the sequence
h_{n}
converges, up to a subsequence as
n\to\infty
, to a limit function

h
in the weak topology of
H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))
.
Due to the continuous embedding (this depends strongly on the fact that we are in one dimension)

H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\hookrightarrow C^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4}}([0,{\rho_{0}}]\times[0,T])\,,

the convergence is uniform.
∎

We now define the notion of weak solution.

Definition 4.6
.

We say that a function
h\in H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))

is a weak solution of (
11
) if

(12)

\int_{[0,{\rho_{0}}]\times[0,T]}\left(h_{t}\varphi+\arctan(h_{u})\varphi_{u}-\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\,\varphi\right)\,du\,dt=0

for all test functions
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,{\rho_{0}}]\times(0,T))
, with boundary condition
h({\rho_{0}},t)=0
.

We have the following existence theorem for weak solution to (
11
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
h_{0}\in W^{2,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}])
with

(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
and
h_{0}({\rho_{0}})=0
.
Then, there exists
T>0
depending only on
h_{0}

such that equation (
11
) admits a weak solution

h\in W^{1,\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],W^{2,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\,.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
there exist
T>0,
depending only on
\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}
and smooth solutions

h_{n}
of (
11
) which converge, up to a subsequence, uniformly to a limit function
h
in
the weak topology of
H^{1}\left([0,T],L^{2}([0,1])\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,T],H^{1}([0,1])\right)
.
We wish to prove that
u
is a weak solution of (
11
). The primary difficulty is in showing that

{u_{n}}_{x}
converges to
u_{x}
almost everywhere, so that we can pass to the limit in (
12
).

We compute (in the following computation we use
\tilde{h}=h_{n}
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}_{t}\,\tilde{h}_{tt}=\tilde{h}_{t}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{uu}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\right)_{t}

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}\,\tilde{h}_{tuu}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}\,\tilde{h}_{uu}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\bigg)\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}

\displaystyle\quad-\tilde{h}_{t}\frac{L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}]}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{uu}-\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}(1+\tilde{h}_{uu})}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\bigg)\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}

\displaystyle\quad-\tilde{h}_{t}\frac{L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}]}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,.

Now, calculate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\tilde{h}_{t}\,\tilde{h}_{tt}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{uu}-\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}(1+\tilde{h}_{uu})}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\bigg)\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\tilde{h}_{t}\frac{L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}]}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,du

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,du+2\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}\,\tilde{h}_{uu}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}\,du+\bigg[\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}\bigg]_{0}^{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}(1+\tilde{h}_{uu})}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\bigg)\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\right)_{u}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+(-L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}])\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\tilde{h}_{uu}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\bigg)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,du+C\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds+C(1+\rho_{0}^{-2})

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\lVert h_{0}\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}
.
Note that in the above we used the boundary conditions
\tilde{h}_{t}({\rho_{0}},t)=0
,
\tilde{h}_{u}(0,t)=0
, and

\displaystyle(-L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}])

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\tilde{h}_{uu}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\bigg)\,du

\displaystyle=(-L^{\prime}[\tilde{h}])\bigg\{\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{L^{2}[\tilde{h}]}\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\bigg]_{0}^{\rho_{0}}+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{1}{L^{3}[\tilde{h}]^{2}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,du\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds\,.

Recall that
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\gamma}k^{2}\,ds\,dt^{\prime}\leq L(0)
by Lemma
3.5
.
Then

(14)

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,dt^{\prime}\leq\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}\,du\bigg|_{t=0}+Ct+C

where
C
may denote a new constant that depends only on
\lVert h_{0}\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}
(and
\rho_{0}
).

Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{h}_{uu}^{2}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}+2L^{-1}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}\tilde{h}_{uu}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}+L^{-2}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\arctan(\tilde{h}_{u})\right)_{u}^{2}+2L^{-1}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}\tilde{h}_{uu}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}+L^{-2}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\arctan(\tilde{h}_{u})\right)_{u}^{2}-L^{-2}\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\,.

Therefore the uniform gradient estimate and the above calculation yields the estimates

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\left(\arctan(\tilde{h}_{u})\right)_{u}^{2}\,du\leq C\qquad\forall t\in[0,T]\,,\text{ and}

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\left(\arctan(\tilde{h}_{u})\right)_{t}^{2}\,du\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\tilde{h}_{ut}^{2}}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,,

where now
C
depends additionally on
T
.

This means that the function
\arctan((h_{n})_{u})
is uniformly bounded in

H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}([0,T]))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))\,.

Therefore, the sequence
\arctan((h_{n})_{u})
converges, up to a subsequence, to
\arctan(h_{u})
uniformly on
[0,{\rho_{0}}]\times[0,T]
.
Since
\arctan
is injective this implies that the sequence
(h_{n})_{u}
converges to
h_{u}
a.e. on
[0,{\rho_{0}}]\times[0,T]
,
and we can pass to the limit in (
12
), obtaining that
h
is a weak solution of (
11
).

Since
\arctan({h_{u}})
continuous, we have that
h_{u}
is also continuous
(hence bounded) on
[0,{\rho_{0}}]\times[0,T]
.
To finish the proof we wish to conclude a bound on
h_{uu}
, which would imply

h\in L^{\infty}([0,T],W^{2,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))
.

To see this, we note that our above estimates (in particular the gradient estimate and (
14
)) imply

\int k^{2}\,ds\leq CT\,,

for some universal constant
C
. Setting
\varphi=\frac{\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}}{2}
and
\hat{\varphi}=\varphi e^{-t}
we find

\displaystyle\bigg(\partial_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}\partial^{2}_{uu}-\frac{1}{L[\tilde{h}]}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{\tilde{h}_{u}(1+\tilde{h}_{uu})}{(1+\tilde{h}_{u}^{2})^{2}}\bigg)\partial_{u}\bigg)\hat{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq-\hat{\varphi}+C\sqrt{\hat{\varphi}}e^{-t/2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\varphi}+\frac{C^{2}}{2}\,.

The boundary conditions imply
\hat{\varphi}_{u}(0)=0
and
\hat{\varphi}(\rho)=0
.
Using the Hopf Lemma then gives
\hat{\varphi}\leq C^{2}
, which results in the estimate

\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}\leq 2C^{2}e^{t}\leq 2C^{2}e^{T}\,.

In light of (
11
), this implies a uniform bound on
h_{uu}
and we are finished.
∎

Finally we note the following
W^{4,\infty}
estimate, which is needed for the numerical analysis.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
h_{0}\in W^{5,P}([0,{\rho_{0}}])
,
P\geq 2
, with
(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
and
h_{0}({\rho_{0}})=0
.
Then, the weak solution generated by Theorem
4.7
above satisfies

\lVert h\rVert_{W^{4,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))}\leq C(h_{0},\rho_{0},T)

and

\lVert h\rVert_{W^{5,P}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))}\leq C(h_{0},\rho_{0},T,P)\,.

Proof.

Step 1:

W^{3,P}
control.
We begin by noting

\displaystyle h_{tt}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{tuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\bigg(2\frac{h_{u}h_{uu}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}\Big(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{h_{u}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\bigg)h_{tu}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L^{2}}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle\,.

Using the uniform
W^{2,\infty}
estimate from Theorem
4.7
we obtain

(16)

h_{tt}=\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}h_{tuu}+f(h)(1+h_{tu})

where
f
is a bounded function, satisfying
|f|\leq C
on a bounded time
interval
[0,T]
, with
C=C(T,h_{0})
.

Since our boundary conditions imply
h_{tt}
vanishes at the Dirichlet boundary
and
h_{tu}
vanishes on the Neumann boundary, the product
h_{tu}h_{ttu}

vanishes on both boundaries.
Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N}\,du

\displaystyle=2N\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}h_{tu}^{2N-1}\,du

\displaystyle=-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}h_{tuu}h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle=-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tuu}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}h_{tuu}+f(u)(1+h_{tu})\bigg)h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-2\delta N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tuu}^{2}h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}f(u)h_{tuu}\,h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}f(u)h_{tuu}\,h_{tu}^{2N-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\delta N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tuu}^{2}h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du+C_{\delta}N^{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N}+h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du\,.

On the last term we use the Young inequality to estimate

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N-2}\,du\leq C+C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N}\,du\,.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N}\,du

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{2N}\,du+C\,.

This implies that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert h\rVert_{W^{3,2N}}(t)\leq C

where
C
depends only on
h_{0}
,
T
and
N
.
Note that we can not take
N\rightarrow\infty
; instead, we shall use this to next obtain a
W^{4,P}
estimate.

Step 2:

W^{4,P}
control.
We begin by differentiating

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du=2N\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{3}}h_{tt}^{2N-1}\,du\,.

Now we wish to convert
h_{t^{3}}
to
h_{ttuu}
plus lower order terms:

(17)

\displaystyle h_{t^{3}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{tt}

(18)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{tuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\bigg(2\frac{h_{u}h_{uu}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}\Big(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{h_{u}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\bigg)h_{tu}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L^{2}}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t}

(19)

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{ttuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(u)\Big(h_{tu}h_{tuu}+h_{ttu}+h_{tu}^{2}+1\Big)\,,

where again
f
is a bounded function. Note that we can replace
h_{tuu}
by
h_{tt}
using (
16
).

We have a number of additional terms to deal with.
We continue with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du

\displaystyle=2N\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N-1}\bigg(\frac{h_{ttuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(u)\Big(h_{tu}h_{tt}+h_{ttu}+h_{tu}^{2}+1\Big)\bigg)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\delta N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2}h_{tt}^{2N-2}\,du+2NC\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}|h_{tu}|\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{\delta}N^{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du+2NC\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du+2NC\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tu}^{P}\,du+C

\displaystyle\leq-\delta N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2}h_{tt}^{2N-2}\,du+2NC\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}|h_{tu}|\,du+C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N+1}\,du+C\,,

where in the last step we used the
W^{3,P}
estimates from above (valid for every
P
) and the Young inequality, for instance:

h_{tt}^{2N}|h_{tu}|\leq\varepsilon h_{tt}^{2N+1}+C_{\varepsilon}h_{tu}^{2N+1}\,.

Note that we do not need to take
\varepsilon
small here.

Let us note the following interpolation procedure:

\displaystyle\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tuu}h_{tt}^{Q-1}(1+h_{u}^{2})\,du+C\int h_{tu}^{P}(1+|h_{tu}|)h_{tt}^{Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P+1}\int(h_{tu}^{P+1})_{u}h_{tt}^{Q-1}(1+h_{u}^{2})\,du+C\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q-2}+h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{tt}^{Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q-1}{P+1}\int h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{ttu}h_{tt}^{Q-2}(1+h_{u}^{2})\,du-\frac{2}{P+1}\int h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{tt}^{Q-1}h_{u}h_{uu}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q-2}+h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{tt}^{Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq C\int h_{tu}^{P+1}|h_{ttu}|\,h_{tt}^{N-1+Q-N-1}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{tt}^{Q-1}+h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q-2}+h_{tu}^{P+1}h_{tt}^{Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int h_{ttu}^{2}h_{tt}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int h_{tu}^{2P+2}h_{tt}^{2Q-2N-2}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int(h_{tu}^{P}+h_{tu}^{P+1}+h_{tu}^{P+2})h_{tt}^{Q-2}\,du\,.

There are no boundary terms in the above because
h_{tu}h_{tt}
vanishes at both boundary points.
Note also that we freely used the
W^{2,\infty}
estimates.

Absorbing, we find

\int h_{tu}^{P}h_{tt}^{Q}\,du\leq\delta\int h_{ttu}^{2}h_{tt}^{2N-2}\,du+C\int h_{tu}^{2P+2}h_{tt}^{2Q-2N-2}\,du+C\int(h_{tu}^{P}+h_{tu}^{P+2})h_{tt}^{Q-2}\,du

Applying this for
Q=2N+1
, we see that the RHS involves products of powers of
h_{tu}
and at most the
(2N-1)
-th power of
h_{tt}
.
Using again Young’s inequality and the
W^{3,P}
estimates, we finally discover that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tt}^{2N}\,du+C

which
implies that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert h\rVert_{W^{4,2N}}(t)\leq C

where
C
depends only on
h_{0}
,
T
and
N
.

Step 3:

W^{5,P}
control.
We begin by noting that the
W^{4,P}
estimates yield a
uniform

W^{3,\infty}
estimate, which means that now

\displaystyle h_{ttt}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{tt}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{tuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\bigg(2\frac{h_{u}h_{uu}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}\Big(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{h_{u}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\bigg)h_{tu}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L^{2}}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{ttuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{ttu}+h_{tuu})\,,

where
f
is a bounded function.

Using this we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2N}\,du

\displaystyle=-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttt}h_{ttuu}h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle=-2N(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttuu}h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\bigg(\frac{h_{ttuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{ttu}+h_{tuu})\bigg)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-2N(2N-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttuu}^{2}h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2CN(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttuu}h_{ttu}^{2N-2}(1+|h_{ttu}|+|h_{tuu}|)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-N(2N-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttuu}^{2}h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2CN(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}(1+h_{ttu}^{2}+h_{tuu}^{2})h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\,du\,.

Employing again the Young inequality we estimate

2CN(2N-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}(1+h_{ttu}^{2}+h_{tuu}^{2})h_{ttu}^{2N-2}\,du\leq\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2N}\,du+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{tuu}^{N}\,du+C\leq\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2N}\,du+C

which finally yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2N}\,du\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{ttu}^{2N}\,du+C

and the desired
W^{5,P}
estimate for any
P
.
This in turn yields uniform
W^{4,\infty}
control, and the proof of the proposition is ended.
∎

The technique used in the previous proof for estimates in
W^{4,P}
and

W^{5,P}
can be iterated, yielding the following analogous higher-order
statement.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
h_{0}\in W^{K,P}([0,{\rho_{0}}])
,
K\geq 3
,
P\geq 2
, with
(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
and
h_{0}({\rho_{0}})=0
.
Then, the weak solution generated by Theorem
4.7
above satisfies

\lVert h\rVert_{W^{K-1,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))}\leq C(h_{0},\rho_{0},T)

and

\lVert h\rVert_{W^{K,P}([0,{\rho_{0}}]))}\leq C(h_{0},\rho_{0},T,P)\,.

Proof.

For the proof, follow the proof of Theorem
5.1
of Case 1 up to
(
21
) then integrate from zero to
t
; and for Case 2 similarly up to
(
24
) then integrate from zero to
t
.
∎

5.
Convergence and smoothing

In order to conclude the convergence, we use the exponential decay of area.
The idea is to combine this exponential decay with controlled growth of other
derivatives and a standard interpolation inequality.

For this control on higher derivatives, the key point (in contrast to the
situation in Propositions
4.8
and
4.9
) is that we only require
these to hold for
large time
.
This means that we can get away with requiring much weaker regularity on our
initial data for these estimates.
This is a manifestation of the
smoothing effect
for the flow, outlined
in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
h_{0}\in W^{2,\infty}([0,{\rho_{0}}])
with
(h_{0})_{u}(0)=0
and
h_{0}({\rho_{0}})=0
.
Then (
11
) admits a smooth classical solution
h:[0,{\rho_{0}}]\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, all derivatives of the graph function obey the following estimate:

\lVert\partial_{t}^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}h\rVert_{2}^{2}(t)\leq C(p,q,h_{0})t\,,\qquad\text{ for $t>1$}\,.

Proof.

First, Theorem
4.7
yields the existence of a
W^{2,\infty}
solution.
In this proof we must control all higher derivatives of
h
.

We shall use induction.
When considering estimates (and existence of) derivatives of order
N+1
, we will be finished if we can deal with the following two cases:

\text{{\bf Case 1}: $N$ is even}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\text{{\bf Case 2}: $N$ is odd}\,.

Note that we may always trade one time derivative for two space derivatives plus a lower order term (using the PDE (
11
)).
This means that we will be finished if we can obtain existence and control of:

(Case 1)

h_{t^{p}u}
for
p=N/2
;

(Case 2)

h_{t^{p}}
for
p=(N+1)/2
.

Each case is slightly different, with the general strategy following the proof of Proposition
4.8
. We will endeavour to build on this proof and not spend too much time repeating ideas.

Note that our previous work already takes us to estimates of order 5 (so
N=5
).
We proceed from this case.

Case 1.

N
is even.

Set
P=N/2
. We begin by noting that the
W^{N,P}
estimates yield a
uniform

W^{N-1,\infty}
estimate, which means that we have uniform estimates on

h_{t^{k}u^{l}}

for all
k
,
l
such that
2k+l\leq N-1
.
Using this, we find

\displaystyle h_{t^{P+1}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\frac{1}{L[h]}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t^{P}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{tuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\bigg(2\frac{h_{u}h_{uu}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}\Big(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{h_{u}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\bigg)h_{tu}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L^{2}}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t^{P-1}}

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{t^{P}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{t^{P}u}+h_{t^{P}})\,,

where
f
is a bounded function.
Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du

\displaystyle=-2Q(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P+1}}h_{t^{P}uu}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle=-2Q(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\bigg(\frac{h_{t^{P}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{t^{P}u}+h_{t^{P}})\bigg)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-2Q(2Q-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2CQ(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}(1+|h_{t^{P}u}|+|h_{t^{P}}|)\,du

\displaystyle\leq-Q(2Q-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2CQ(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}(1+h_{t^{P}u}^{2}+h_{t^{P}}^{2})h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\,.

Young’s inequality and
W^{N,Q}
-estimates imply

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}(1+h_{t^{P}u}^{2}+h_{t^{P}}^{2})h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{Q}\,du+C\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+C

which finally yields

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+C\,.

Integrating this from zero to
t
yields the estimate needed for Proposition
4.9
; here, we instead interpolate further on the RHS:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h^{2Q}_{t^{P}u}\,du

\displaystyle=(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}h_{t^{P}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du+\frac{(2Q-1)^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+\frac{C_{Q}}{\varepsilon^{Q}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du\,.

Note that in the last step we used Young’s inequality.
Absorbing yields

(22)

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h^{2Q}_{t^{P}u}\,du\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du+\frac{C_{Q}}{\varepsilon^{Q}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du\,.

Using (
22
) in (
21
) then absorbing again yields

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\leq C\,.

Integrating yields

(23)

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\varepsilon_{N}}^{\hat{t}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\,dt\leq C\hat{t}+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

Note that the inductive hypothesis implies that for all
\varepsilon_{N}>0
,
\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}
exists and is bounded (this is the
Q=1
case of the
previous step
).

Using estimate (
23
) yields that the integral

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}uu}^{2}\,du

instantaneously exists and is bounded. This implies that

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du\,,\quad\text{ and }\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P+1}}^{2}\,du

exist and are bounded for each
t>0
by a constant that (possibly) blows up as
t\searrow 0
.
Therefore
\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}
for each
Q
and
\varepsilon_{N}>0
exists and is bounded.
This yields immediately the linear in time estimate claimed for estimates of order
2Q=N
,
and the requirements of the proceeding inductive step.

Case 2.

N
is odd.

Set
P=(N+1)/2
. As before, we begin by noting that the
W^{N,P}
estimates yield a
uniform

W^{N-1,\infty}
estimate, which means that we have uniform estimates on

h_{t^{k}u^{l}}

for all
k
,
l
such that
2k+l\leq N-1
.
We shall use a version of (
20
) as before, but this time our induction hypothesis is that we have estimates of one order less. This means that we need to consider an extra term:

\displaystyle h_{t^{P+1}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{tuu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+\bigg(2\frac{h_{u}h_{uu}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}\Big(\frac{1}{1+h_{u}^{2}}-2\frac{h_{u}}{(1+h_{u}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\bigg)h_{tu}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L^{2}}\frac{h_{u}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}\bigg)_{t^{P-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{t^{P}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{t^{P}u}+h_{t^{P}}+h_{t^{P-1}u})\,,

where as before
f
is a bounded function.

Calculating,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du

\displaystyle=2Q\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P+1}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle=2Q\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{h_{t^{P}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2Q\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}f(h)(1+h_{t^{P}u}+h_{t^{P}}+h_{t^{P-1}u})h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle=-2Q(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{h_{t^{P}u}^{2}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2Q\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\hat{f}(h)(1+h_{t^{P}u}+h_{t^{P}}+h_{t^{P-1}u})h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-2Q(2Q-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad+2CQ(2Q-1)\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}|h_{t^{P}u}|\,|h_{t^{P}}|^{2Q-1}+h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}+|h_{t^{P}}|^{2Q-1}+|h_{t^{P-1}u}|\,|h_{t^{P}}|^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-Q(2Q-1)\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du+C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+C\,.

That is,

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+C\,.

Integrating this from zero to
t
yields the estimate needed for Proposition
4.9
.
As in Case 1, we need to instead interpolate further on the RHS; this time, we
need to first introduce some space derivatives, and the estimate becomes more
complicated.
This is done by again another version of (
20
); recall the uniform
W^{N-1,\infty}
estimate allows us to put any term with a highest order derivative of
(2P-2)
or less in the function
f
:

h_{t^{P}}=\frac{h_{t^{P-1}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}+f(h)(1+h_{t^{P-1}u})

where again
f
is a bounded function.
Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{h_{t^{P-1}uu}}{1+h_{u}^{2}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}f(h)(1+h_{t^{P-1}u})h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P-1}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\hat{f}(h)(1+h_{t^{P-1}u})h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P-1}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+C+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}|h_{t^{P-1}u}||h_{t^{P}}|^{2Q-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P-1}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du+\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+C

Note that we used the
W^{2P-1,Q}
estimates and Young’s inequality.
Absorbing, we find

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du\leq C\,.

Returning to our evolution equation, this implies

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}u}^{2Q-2}\,du\leq C\,.

Integrating yields

(25)

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du+\int_{\varepsilon_{N}}^{\hat{t}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q-2}\,du\,dt\leq C\hat{t}+\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

Note that for all
\varepsilon_{N}>0
,
\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}
exists and is
bounded (this is the
Q=1
case of the
previous step
).

As before, we apply the estimate first for
Q=1
, to find that

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}u}^{2}\,du

instantaneously exists and is bounded. This will be used in the next step; for now, it implies that

\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du

for any
Q
exist and are bounded for each
t>0
by a constant that (possibly)
blows up as
t\searrow 0
.
Therefore
\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}h_{t^{P}}^{2Q}\,du\bigg|_{t=\varepsilon_{N}}
for each

Q
and
\varepsilon_{N}>0
exists and is bounded.
This combined with (
25
) yields immediately the linear in time
estimate claimed for estimates of order
2Q=N
, and the requirements of the
proceeding inductive step.

Iterating through the cases with induction we find that every derivative of
h

exists instantaneously.
Furthermore, as noted in the argument above we have the bound

\lVert\partial_{t}^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}h\rVert_{2}^{2}(t)\leq Ct

for all
t>\varepsilon_{2p+q}
.
By taking
\varepsilon_{2p+q}=1
, we are able to guarantee that the above estimate holds for
t>1

∎

Remark
.

Naturally, there is nothing special about
t=1
in the smoothing estimate.
We could choose any fixed time.
We may obtain an estimate for all
t>0
, however the constant would not be
uniformly bounded for
t
small (even if the initial graph function is
regular).

We need a custom-built interpolation inequality to move between high-order derivatives of
h
and the enclosed area.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth regular curve with length
L
satisfying the graphicality condition

G_{-e_{2}}(s)\geq C_{G}\,,

and the positivity condition

\left<\gamma(s),e_{2}\right>\geq 0\,.

Then, for the associated graph function
h:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lVert h_{x^{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(dx)}\leq C(A(\gamma))^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert h_{x^{2j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(dx)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+CA(\gamma)\,,

where
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
C
is a constant depending only on
a,b
, and
A(\gamma)

denotes the area bounded by the curve
\gamma
and the
e_{1}
-axis.

Proof.

Graphicality implies that there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and a graph function
h:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\gamma(s)=(x,h(x))\,.

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg Sobolev inequality
[
25
,
24
,
10
]
applied to
h
yields

\bigg(\int_{a}^{b}|h_{x^{j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\bigg(\int_{a}^{b}|h(x)|\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{a}^{b}|h_{x^{2j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}+C\int_{a}^{b}|h(x)|\,dx\,,

for a constant
C
depending only on
a
and
b
.

The positivity condition implies
h(x)\geq 0
so
|h(x)|=h(x)
.
Using
A(\gamma)
to denote the area of the region bounded by
\gamma([0,L])
and the
e_{1}
-axis, we have

A(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}|h(u)|\,du\,.

Therefore we find

\lVert h_{x^{j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(dx)}\leq C(A(\gamma))^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert h_{x^{2j}}\rVert_{L^{2}(dx)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+CA(\gamma)\,,

as required.
∎

We finish with the convergence result, which is by now a corollary of our earlier work.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

First, we use the above results to generate a solution from the
W^{2,\infty}
data.
We have
T=\infty
by Theorem
4.4
.
Our interpolation inequality (Proposition
5.2
), the exponential
decay of area (Proposition
4.3
), and the linear-in-time
estimates (Theorem
5.1
) give

\lVert\partial_{u}^{j}h\rVert_{2}(t)\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{C_{G}^{2}}{2\rho_{0}L(0)}t}+Ce^{-\frac{C_{G}^{2}}{\rho_{0}L(0)}t}\,,

Therefore all spatial derivatives of
h
converge exponentially fast to zero.
The evolution equation for
h
then yields exponential decay of all temporal derivatives of
h
also.
This is the desired exponentially fast convergence in the smooth topology of
the solution to the horizontal line segment connecting the origin to

(\rho_{0},0)
.
∎

Lemma 6.1

If

The following is a consistency result for

Lemma 6.2

If

Physicochemical and nutritional characteristics of an underutilised edible fern (Stenochlaena palustris) incorporated into a dates-based food matrix
Authors: Nur Aliah, Muhamad Firdaus Syahmi Sam-on, Mohd Rohaizad Md Roduan, Ermy Azziaty Rozali, Nurul Nadhirah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveUnderutilised edible plants represent important but insufficiently characterised nutritional resources within plant-based food systems. Stenochlaena palustris is a wild edible fern widely consumed in Southeast Asia; however, its incorporation into formulated food matrices remains poorly documented. The objective of this study was to generate initial physicochemical and nutritional data for dates-based food formulations incorporating S. palustris powder at different inclusion levels, providing validated baseline compositional information on an underutilised edible fern within a contemporary plant-based food system.ResultsDates-based energy bars were formulated with S. palustris powder at 2.5% and 5.0% (w/w), alongside a control formulation without fern inclusion. Proximate composition, sugar profile, mineral content, antioxidant activity, and selected physicochemical properties were determined using validated analytical methods by an accredited laboratory. Incorporation of S. palustris was associated with observed increases in dietary fibre, ash, and protein, with generally higher values of selected minerals, including calcium, magnesium, iron, and zinc, in the fortified formulations compared to the control, while total energy values remained comparable across formulations. Water activity values for all formulations were below 0.48, and total flavonoid content increased with higher fern inclusion, while DPPH antioxidant activity was highest in the control formulation. As each formulation was analysed as a single composite sample, the data are presented as baseline compositional observations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-026-07895-3.

Introduction

Edible ferns constitute a distinctive group of traditional plant foods consumed across Asia, Africa, and Oceania, contributing appreciable levels of dietary fibre, essential minerals, proteins, and antioxidant-related phytochemicals to local diets [1–3]. Despite their recognised nutritional attributes, many edible ferns remain poorly integrated into modern food systems owing to limited compositional data and the near absence of applied food-based evaluations [4]. Stenochlaena palustris (Burm. f.) Bedd., locally known as ‘Paku Midin’ (Blechnaceae), is a wild edible fern widely consumed in Southeast Asia, with particular culinary prominence in Sarawak, Malaysia, where it is valued for its antioxidant and mineral content [5–8]. However, fern research in Malaysia has historically prioritised taxonomy and systematics over food composition, leaving a notable gap between botanical knowledge and food-based utilisation [9–14].

To our knowledge, the incorporation of S. palustris into a formulated food matrix has not been previously reported. The present study therefore aimed to generate initial physicochemical and nutritional data for dates-based energy bar formulations incorporating S. palustris powder at 0%, 2.5%, and 5.0% (w/w) inclusion levels. This Research Note presents validated baseline compositional measurements intended to support future formulation optimisation, sensory evaluation, and biological assessment studies.

Materials and methods

Materials and sample preparation

Fresh croziers of Stenochlaena palustris (Burm. f.) Bedd. were purchased from a local wet market in Kuching, Sarawak, Malaysia. The plant material was identified by Dr Nur Aliah, Department of Biological Sciences and Biotechnology, Faculty of Science and Technology, Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia, based on diagnostic macromorphological characters using standard taxonomic references for ferns [15]. No voucher specimen was deposited, as the samples were obtained from a local market and processed for nutritional analysis. Representative photographs of the fresh croziers, the dried and milled powder, and the final formulated energy bars were taken at each preparation stage to provide visual documentation of the study material; these are provided as Figs. 1 and 2. Commercial dried dates (Phoenix dactylifera L., Maryami variety) were obtained from a local supplier (Ahmad Tamar Sdn. Bhd., Kuching, Malaysia) and used as the primary carbohydrate source and binding matrix. Additional food-grade ingredients, including chia seeds, extra virgin olive oil, and Bario salt, were sourced locally. All ingredients were of food-grade quality and used as received without further purification.

Preparation of Stenochlaena palustris powder

Fresh fern croziers were manually sorted to remove damaged material and rinsed thoroughly with tap water followed by distilled water. The cleaned samples were sun-dried for approximately 5 h under ambient conditions to reduce surface moisture, followed by oven-drying at 60 °C for 5 h to achieve moisture stabilisation. Dried material was cooled to room temperature and milled using a Thermomix system (Vorwerk, Germany) to obtain a fine, homogeneous powder. The resulting S. palustris powder was sealed in airtight polyethylene bags and stored at − 18 °C until use.

Formulation of dates-based energy bars

Three formulations were prepared: a control formulation without S. palustris (0%), and two fortified formulations containing 2.5% and 5.0% (w/w) S. palustris powder. Each formulation was standardised to a 250 g batch to ensure consistency across samples. Dates served as the primary carbohydrate and energy source, as well as the binding matrix for bar cohesion. Chia seeds were incorporated as a texture modifier and supplemental source of dietary fibre and omega-3 fatty acids. Extra virgin olive oil was included as a lipid source to enhance binding cohesion and improve bar texture. Bario salt, an artisanal mineral-rich salt traditionally produced in the Kelabit highlands of Sarawak, was added in a small quantity for flavour enhancement. Ingredients were blended under identical mixing conditions, chilled at 2–4 °C for 1–2 h, and shaped into bars of approximately 50 g for handling convenience (Fig. 2). For analytical purposes, each formulation was treated as a single composite sample per treatment. All formulations were prepared under identical conditions to minimise formulation-related variability.

Physicochemical and nutritional analyses

Physicochemical, nutritional, mineral, and selected functional properties of the formulated energy bars were analysed by an external accredited laboratory (Unipeq Sdn. Bhd., Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia). Analyses were conducted using validated analytical methods based on AOAC Official Methods and relevant international standards, as detailed in Supplementary Table S1. Parameters analysed included moisture, ash, protein, total fat, total dietary fibre, available carbohydrate (by difference), energy value, individual sugars, mineral composition, water activity, pH, colour, texture profile parameters, antioxidant activity (DPPH inhibition), total flavonoid content, and vitamin C.

Analytical methods detail

Methodological summaries for the three key functional assays are provided below. Full method references are detailed in Supplementary Table S1.

DPPH antioxidant activity: Antioxidant activity was determined using the 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) free radical scavenging assay. Samples were extracted using 80% methanol. The extract was incubated with DPPH reagent in the dark for 30 min at room temperature, and absorbance was measured at 517 nm. Results are expressed as percentage inhibition (%). A Trolox standard calibration curve was used for method validation. Analyses were performed by an accredited laboratory using validated methods based on AOAC and internationally recognised protocols.

Total flavonoid content: Total flavonoid content was determined using the aluminium chloride colorimetric method. Samples were extracted using 80% ethanol. After addition of AlCl₃ and sodium acetate reagents, the mixture was incubated at room temperature for 30 min, and absorbance was read at 415 nm. Results are expressed as milligrams per gram of sample (mg/g).

Vitamin C determination: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) was determined using the titration method based on AOAC Official Method 20th Edition, 967.21 (STP/Chem/A10). Analyses were performed by an accredited laboratory (Unipeq Sdn. Bhd., UKM) using validated methods under standard quality assurance conditions, ensuring the reliability and traceability of measurements. Results are expressed as mg/100 g.

Statistical analysis

Each formulation was prepared and analysed as a single composite sample per treatment. The objective of this study was to generate initial baseline compositional data for a novel food application, rather than to conduct inferential statistical comparisons or product optimisation. Accordingly, no independent batch replication was performed across formulations, and no measures of between-formulation variability (e.g., standard deviation) are reported for comparative analyses. All analytical measurements were performed by an accredited laboratory (Unipeq Sdn. Bhd., UKM) under standard quality assurance conditions, including instrument calibration, certified reference materials, and validated in-house analytical methods, ensuring the reliability and traceability of individual measurements. This Research Note reports validated compositional measurements rather than inferential statistical comparisons. Implications of the single composite sample design are discussed in the Limitations section.

Results

Proximate composition

The formulation details and proximate composition of the dates-based energy bars containing increasing levels of S. palustris powder are summarised in Table 1. Moisture content was 18.5% in both the control and 5.0% formulations and 17.3% in the 2.5% formulation, showing no consistent trend with fern inclusion. Ash and protein contents showed incremental increases with fern incorporation, reflecting the mineral and protein contribution of S. palustris. Total dietary fibre increased from 11.0 g/100 g in the control to 11.4 g/100 g and 12.9 g/100 g in the 2.5% and 5.0% formulations, respectively. Energy values ranged from 292 to 300 kcal/100 g across formulations, consistent with the dates-based matrix dominating overall caloric content. As all values represent single analytical determinations, observed differences should be interpreted as descriptive trends rather than statistically validated comparisons.

Sugar and mineral composition

The sugar and mineral composition of the dates-based energy bars is summarised in Table 2. Glucose and fructose were the predominant sugars across all formulations, reflecting the intrinsic sugar profile of dates. A trend toward minor reductions in total sugar content was observed with increasing Stenochlaena palustris powder inclusion, while sucrose and maltose were not detected in any formulation. Potassium was the dominant mineral across all formulations, ranging from 841.9 to 1020.0 mg/100 g, consistent with the mineral composition of dates. Incorporation of S. palustris powder was associated with generally higher values of calcium, magnesium, iron, and zinc in the fortified formulations compared to the control, with iron showing the most consistent dose-dependent increase, suggesting the contribution of fern-derived minerals to the composite formulations.

Physicochemical and functional properties

Physicochemical, antioxidant, colour, and texture properties of the formulated energy bars (Fig. 2) are summarised in Table 3. Water activity values remained below 0.48 across all formulations, indicating microbiological stability within the moisture range of the matrix. Total flavonoid content showed a clear dose-dependent increase with fern inclusion. Vitamin C content did not follow a consistent trend with fern inclusion, with the control recording the highest value (2.8 mg/100 g) compared to 1.6 and 1.9 mg/100 g in the 2.5% and 5.0% formulations, respectively. DPPH inhibition was highest in the control formulation (15.40%) compared to the fortified formulations (0.46% and 1.37% for 2.5% and 5.0%, respectively), a pattern that may reflect interactions between fern-derived polyphenols and date matrix components, and warrants further investigation. Colour parameters (L*, a*, b*) reflected a progressive trend toward darkening and increased greenness with higher S. palustris content. Hardness values did not show a consistent trend with fern inclusion, ranging from 756.3 to 798.22 gf across formulations.

Discussion

This study provides baseline physicochemical and nutritional data for dates-based energy bars formulated with an underutilised edible fern, Stenochlaena palustris. By presenting validated analytical measurements generated by an accredited laboratory, the results contribute reference information on the incorporation of a traditionally consumed fern into a contemporary plant-based food matrix.

Incorporation of S. palustris powder was associated with incremental changes in proximate composition, including observed increases in ash, protein, and total dietary fibre content. These trends are consistent with previous compositional reports on edible ferns, which have documented appreciable levels of minerals, fibre, and protein in fern fronds [7, 16–18]. The relatively stable energy values across formulations reflect the dominant contribution of dates to the overall matrix, indicating that fern inclusion at the levels tested does not substantially alter the caloric profile of the product. Mineral analysis showed generally higher values of calcium, magnesium, iron, and zinc in the fortified formulations compared to the control, with iron showing the most consistent dose-dependent increase, supporting a possible role of edible ferns as contributors of micronutrients when incorporated into composite foods. Potassium remained the predominant mineral across all formulations (841.9–1020.0 mg/100 g), consistent with the known mineral composition of dates [18]. Similarly, total flavonoid content showed a clear dose-dependent increase with fern inclusion, aligning with previous findings that edible ferns contain phenolic and flavonoid compounds with antioxidant potential [3, 8]. The unexpectedly higher DPPH inhibition observed in the control formulation compared to the fortified bars may reflect matrix-level interactions, such as competitive inhibition or binding of fern-derived polyphenols to date matrix components, and merits dedicated investigation in future studies.

Limitations

This study was designed as an initial compositional assessment and has several important limitations that must be considered in interpreting the reported data. First, each formulation was analysed as a single composite sample per treatment. No independent batch replication was performed, and therefore no measures of analytical variability or between-batch variability (e.g., standard deviation or confidence intervals) are available for the reported values. As a result, it is not possible to determine whether observed differences between formulations reflect true compositional trends or fall within the range of normal analytical variation. All observed differences should therefore be interpreted as descriptive baseline observations rather than statistically validated findings.

Second, the absence of replicated measurements limits the reproducibility of individual data points, as variability inherent to the raw plant material (e.g., seasonal or geographical differences in fern composition) and to the formulation process cannot be assessed from the current dataset. Future studies employing replicated independent formulation batches, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation from at least three replicates, would substantially strengthen the evidence base and allow inferential statistical comparisons.

Third, sensory properties were not assessed and remain beyond the scope of the present work. While microbiological analyses were conducted as part of the laboratory assessment, a detailed evaluation of shelf-life stability and the influence of processing conditions on microbiological safety warrants dedicated investigation. Additionally, the absence of in vivo or in vitro functional assays means that the observed compositional trends cannot be directly linked to health outcomes or bioavailability. These aspects warrant further investigation in subsequent studies to complement the compositional data reported here.

Conclusions

This study provides baseline physicochemical and nutritional data for dates-based food formulations incorporating the underutilised edible fern S. palustris. Using validated analytical methods performed by an accredited laboratory, incremental observed changes in dietary fibre, mineral content, antioxidant activity, and selected physicochemical properties were documented with increasing fern inclusion. As data are based on single composite analytical determinations, findings are presented as descriptive baseline observations. The results extend existing botanical and ethnobotanical knowledge of S. palustris by situating it within a formulated plant-based food matrix and contribute reference compositional data relevant to underutilised edible plants. These findings support the feasibility of incorporating traditionally consumed ferns into contemporary food systems and provide a foundation for future applied and functional investigations.

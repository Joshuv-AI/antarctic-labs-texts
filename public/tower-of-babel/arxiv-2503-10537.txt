Structured Preconditioners in Adaptive Optimization: A Unified Analysis
Shuo Xie, Tianhao Wang, Sashank Reddi, Sanjiv Kumar, Zhiyuan Li

Abstract
We present a novel unified analysis for a broad class of adaptive optimization algorithms with structured (e.g., layerwise, diagonal, and kronecker-factored) preconditioners for both online regret minimization and offline convex optimization. Our analysis not only provides matching rate to several important structured preconditioned algorithms including diagonal AdaGrad, full-matrix AdaGrad, and AdaGrad-Norm, but also gives an improved convergence rate for a one-sided variant of Shampoo over that of original Shampoo. Interestingly, more structured preconditioners (e.g., diagonal Adagrad, AdaGrad-Norm which use less space and compute) are often presented as computationally efficient approximations to full-matrix Adagrad, aiming for improved optimization performance through better approximations. Our unified analysis challenges this prevailing view and reveals, perhaps surprisingly, that more structured preconditioners, despite using less space and computation per step, can outperform their less structured counterparts. To demonstrate this, we show that one-sided Shampoo, which is relatively much cheaper than full-matrix AdaGrad could outperform it both theoretically and experimentally.

Introduction

Adaptive optimization algorithms
(
Streeter & McMahan 2010
;
Duchi et al. 2011
;
Kingma & Ba 2014
)
play a pivotal role in modern machine learning, especially in the expensive training of large foundation models. Within the machine learning community, full-matrix AdaGrad is considered as an ideal adaptive preconditioner for fast convergence in terms of number of steps. The computation of full-matrix AdaGrad precondtioner typically involves inverse square root of a
d\times d
matrix where
d
is the number of parameters. Thus, for large-scale settings, the huge computation and memory cost makes it prohibitively expensive. This has inspired works on designing more efficient adaptive optimizers by using a structured preconditioner, such as coordinate-wise adaptivity employed by AdaGrad
(
Streeter & McMahan 2010
;
Duchi et al. 2011
)
, Kronecker product based preconditioner employed by Shampoo
(
Gupta et al. 2018
;
Anil et al. 2020
)
and layerwise adaptivity employed by LARS
(
You et al. 2017
)
and LAMB
(
You et al. 2019
)
), which all aim to provide a computational and memory efficient approximation of full-matrix AdaGrad. These algorithms have been shown to be very effective for general deep learning settings. It is often assumed that a better approximation of full-matrix preconditioner usually results in better optimization convergence, as seen with methods like Shampoo
(
Gupta et al. 2018
)
. In contrast, preconditioners with more structure such as diagonal preconditioner have inferior performance. In this paper, we challenge these prevailing notions by providing theoretical and empirical evidence against them.

Conceptually, one could equate the degree of structure in a preconditioner to the ease of its computation and storage. In this view, full-matrix AdaGrad can be considered as the least structured and most expensive preconditioner while AdaGrad-Norm
(
Ward et al. 2020
)
, which only maintains a scalar and uses the same preconditioning for every direction, is the most structured and least expensive. The conventional wisdom here is that using a less structured preconditioner, which requires more space and compute per step, reduces the number of steps needed for training. Thus, choosing the right structure balances this trade-off between convergence speed and training step cost.

Among these preconditioning methods, Shampoo
(
Gupta et al. 2018
)
has gained notable attention for its Kronecker-factored preconditioning approach, which promises improved convergence in large-scale optimization tasks
(
Dahl et al. 2023
)
. Shampoo was originally proposed as a computationally efficient surrogate for full-matrix AdaGrad
(
Duchi et al. 2011
)
. Despite its popularity, existing analyses of Shampoo
(
Gupta et al. 2018
)
are limited and do not provide a full justification for its effectiveness. In particular, we argue that the best-known regret bounds for both Shampoo as well as full-matrix AdaGrad are consistently worse than a more memory- and computationally efficient variant of these methods like diagonal AdaGrad and AdaGrad-Norm
(
Streeter & McMahan 2010
;
Duchi et al. 2011
;
Ward et al. 2020
)
. This demonstrates that more structure on the preconditioner may not necessarily restrict its optimization performance. In fact, our unified analysis uncovers an interesting finding: we show a simpler, more structured variant of Shampoo, called one-sided Shampoo, actually has substantially better regret bound compared to original Shampoo and full-matrix Adagrad (
Section

3.3
).

1.1
Main contributions

In light of the above discussion, we highlight the main contributions of the paper.

•

We present a comprehensive and unified theoretical framework (
Theorem

2.1
) for adaptive optimization with structured preconditioners, encompassing several popular methods including diagonal AdaGrad, full-matrix AdaGrad, AdaGrad-Norm, and layerwise adaptive methods for both online convex optimization and stochastic smooth convex optimization. In particular, our analysis integrates and extends key insights from existing work
(
Gupta et al. 2017
)
, which presents a unified and elegant way (
Algorithm

1
) to derive the aforementioned structured preconditioners, but only allows analysis on a case-by-case basis. To our knowledge, this is the
first
truly unified analysis of a large family of adaptive optimization algorithms.

•

To enable a unified analysis, we identify a novel sufficient condition,
well-structured preconditioners
(
Definition

3.1
), which overcomes a key technical barrier in
Gupta et al. 2017
; thereby, allowing a unified analysis for several important adaptive algorithms. A more detailed discussion is provided in
Section

2
and a summary of our results is presented in
Table

1
.

•

Our unified analysis provides a new regret bound (
Theorem

4.2
) for a one-sided variant of Shampoo, which is always better than the existing bound for two-sided Shampoo
(
Gupta et al. 2018
)
and could be smaller by a multiplicative factor of
d
, where we assume the matrix-shaped parameter is of size
\sqrt{d}
-by-
\sqrt{d}
.
This also leads to a novel convergence rate of one-sided shampoo for stochastic convex optimization (
Theorem

4.4
) via a standard offline-to-online reduction
(
Levy et al. 2018
)
.

•

Conceptually, our findings challenge the conventional wisdom that using a larger set of preconditioners which require more memory and compute leads to better optimization performance in terms of number of steps. In particular, while using a larger set of preconditioners reduces the gradient term
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{H}}
in our regret bound, it increases the other term involving the magnitude of the optimal solution by increasing the norm metric; thus, leading to a worse regret bound (
Theorems

2.1
and
1
). For instance, we demonstrate that one-sided Shampoo can outperform full-matrix AdaGrad both theoretically (
Section

4.3
) and experimentally (
Section

5
). This suggest that one-sided Shampoo is not just a computational-efficient surrogate of full-matrix AdaGrad, but could also be fundamentally better, depending on the optimization problem.

Background: Unified Adaptive Regularization with Non-Unified Analysis

Seminal work
Gupta et al. 2017
presented an adaptive regularization meta-algorithm, AdaReg (
Algorithm

1
), which can be used to derive various adaptive optimization algorithms known at that time in a unified approach. For example, AdaReg becomes full-matrix AdaGrad
(
Duchi et al. 2011
)
, diagonal AdaGrad
(
Duchi et al. 2011
)
, and AdaGrad-Norm
(
Streeter & McMahan 2010
;
Ward et al. 2020
)
by choosing the set of preconditioners as the set of all PSD matrices, diagonal PSD matrices, and mutipliers of identity matrix respectively (see
Table

1
).
The original AdaReg also allows other choices of potential function
\Phi

\Phi(\cdot)=\log\det(\cdot)
for Online Newton Step
(
Hazan et al. 2007
)
, while we are only interested in the case of
\Phi(\cdot)=\eta^{2}\Tr(\cdot)
in this work.

In addition to the unified approach to deriving various adaptive optimization algorithms,
Gupta et al. 2017
also attempts to give a unified analysis for the convergence rate or regret of these adaptive algorithms, which can be summarized by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
Gupta et al. 2017
)
.

Let

be the iterates of

Then for any

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{t}({\bm{x}}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\langle{\bm{M}}_{T},{\bm{H}}_{T}^{-1}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}}_{T})-\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}}_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\left\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\|_{{\bm{H}}_{t}}^{2}-\left\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\|_{{\bm{H}}_{t}}^{2}\right).

3
Unified Analysis for Well-Structured Preconditioners

We establish a unified framework for adaptive optimization with structured preconditioners.
In
Section

3.1
, we propose the notion of well-structured preconditioners and show that they satisfy the desired condition
Equation

4
.
Then in
Section

3.2
, we present a unified analysis for adaptive optimization with well-structured preconditioners.
We discuss several prominent examples in
Section

3.3
.

3.1
Well-structured preconditioners

For a set of
d
-by-
d
matrices
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}^{d}
, we say that
{\mathcal{K}}
is a
subalgebra
if it is closed under scalar multiplication, matrix addition, and matrix multiplication.
More concretely, we require that for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\bm{A}},{\bm{B}}\in{\mathcal{K}}
, it holds that
\alpha{\bm{A}},{\bm{A}}{\bm{B}},{\bm{A}}+{\bm{B}}\in{\mathcal{K}}
.
Based on this, we propose the following core concept of our paper.

Definition 3.1

(Well-structured preconditioner sets)
.

{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
is said to be a
well-structured preconditioner set
if
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}\cap{\mathcal{K}}
for some matrix subalgebra
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}^{d}
with
{\bm{I}}_{d}\in{\mathcal{K}}
.

As a positive response to
2
, the following proposition shows that our notion of well-structured preconditioner sets provides a sufficient condition for
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
to be operator monotone.
See
Appendix

A
for a proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a well-structured preconditioner set under
Definition

3.1
.
For any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
, there exists a unique solution
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\succ 0
to the optimization problem in (
3
).
Furthermore, for any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle=\eta^{2}\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

(b)

\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle
, where we recall that
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})/\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

Moreover, for any
0\prec{\bm{M}}\preceq{\bm{M}}^{\prime}
, it holds that
P_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
In particular, this implies that
P_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\bm{M}})\preceq P_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})
.

The closure under both matrix addition and matrix multiplication is crucial in the proof of
Proposition

3.2
.
Violation of any of the two properties could lead to problems: for the preconditioner set of two-sided Shampoo in
Example

2.2
, it is not closed under matrix addition; while for
Example

2.3
regarding tridiagonal matrices, we note that the set of tridiagonal matrices is not closed under matrix multiplication.

Motivated by this, for any sequence of gradients

Beyond the previous examples, it is also possible to generate new well-structured preconditioner sets based on existing ones.

Related Work

Adaptive optimizers and structured preconditioners.

The extensive costs for training large-scale deep learning models have motivated the development of efficient optimization algorithms, among which adaptive optimizers have been widely studied because of their ability to exploit the rich geometry of the loss landscape
(
Pascanu & Bengio 2013
;
Martens & Grosse 2015
;
Dozat 2016
;
Loshchilov & Hutter 2018
;
Shazeer & Stern 2018
;
Reddi et al. 2019
;
You et al. 2019
;
Zhuang et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2023
;
Yuan et al. 2024
)
.
For the sake of memory and computational efficiency, many works involve approximations to full-matrix preconditioners
(
Ba et al. 2017
;
George et al. 2018
;
Martens et al. 2018
;
Yao et al. 2021
;
Jahani et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Duvvuri et al. 2024
)
.
Indeed, our results suggest that such compromises might not harm the performance of adaptive optimizers in practice, because more adaptivity is not always helpful, as discussed in
Section

4.3
and
Section

5
.

Understanding Shampoo.

There are also recent efforts to understand the Shampoo optimizer from various perspectives.

Bernstein & Newhouse 2024b
interpret Shampoo as the steepest descent with respect to the spectral norm of the layerwise matrix-form parameters of the neural network.
Recognizing such structures of matrix-form parameters and role of spectral-norm geometry in deep learning has led to development of new optimizer such as Muon
(
Jordan et al. 2024
)
.
In addition, the second-order perspective on Shampoo
(
Anil et al. 2020
)
has also led to fruitful results:
Morwani et al. 2024
connect the preconditioner in Shampoo to the optimal Kronecker product approximation of the Gauss-Newton component of the Hessian, and
Vyas et al. 2024
propose to view Shampoo as Adafactor in the eigenbasis of the preconditioner of Shampoo.

7
Conclusion and Future Works

We present a unified analysis for a broad class of adaptive optimization algorithms with well-structured preconditioners (
Definition

3.1
) for both online regret minimization and smooth convex optimization. Our analysis not only provides matching rate to several important algorithms including diagonal AdaGrad, full-matrix AdaGrad, and AdaGradNorm, but also gives an improved convergence rate for a one-sided variant of Shampoo over that of the original Shampoo.
We reveal a novel trade-off in final convergence rate between domain metric and adaptive gradient norm (
Equation

7
) for regret minimization or adaptive smoothness (
Definition

3.6
) for smooth convex optimization. We hope this insight could be useful towards design of future adaptive optimizers. One important future direction is to identify more subalgebras or other structures that are useful for improving the performance by better adapting to the domain or loss smoothness.

{\bm{B}}=({\bm{A}}-2\max(\lambda_{1}({\bm{A}}),1){\bm{I}}_{d})^{2}\succ 0
.
The leading eigenspace of
{\bm{B}}
is the same as the eigenspace of
{\bm{A}}
corresponding to its smallest eigenvalue, which is negative.
Consequently, for large enough integer
n
,
{\bm{B}}^{n}/\|{\bm{B}}^{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
is approximately the projection matrix onto the eigenspace of the smallest negative eigenvalue of
{\bm{A}}
.
Therefore, for large enough integer
n
,
\langle{\bm{B}}^{n}/\|{\bm{B}}^{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}},{\bm{A}}\rangle<0
.
However, since
{\bm{B}}^{n}/\|{\bm{B}}^{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
is a polynomial of
{\bm{A}}
, we know that
{\bm{B}}^{n}/\|{\bm{B}}^{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
This is a contradiction to the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
.
Hence, we conclude that for any
{\bm{A}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, it holds that
{\bm{A}}\succeq 0
, and thus
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.
Moreover, for any
{\bm{A}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, it holds that
\langle{\bm{H}},{\bm{A}}\rangle\geq 0
for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
because both
{\bm{A}}
and
{\bm{H}}
are positive semi-definite.
This shows that
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, and hence
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}={\mathcal{H}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

A.2
Properties of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)

For any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
, recall the regularized optimization problem in (
3
).
Note that we can assume
\eta=1
without loss of generality, because the original problem is equivalent to solve for
{\bm{M}}/\eta^{2}
with regularizer
\Tr({\bm{H}})
in place of
\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})
.
Therefore, in the rest of this section, we focus on the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}):=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

(11)

The results proved below applies to the original problem (
3
) after simple rescaling, and
Proposition

3.2
follows from
Proposition

A.3
below.

For notational convenience, given any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
, we define

\displaystyle f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}}):=\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

(12)

Proposition A.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a well-structured preconditioner set under
Definition

3.1
.
For any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
, there exists a unique solution
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\succ 0
to the optimization problem in (
11
).
Furthermore, for any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

(b)

\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\arg\min_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle
where we recall that
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.

(c)

For any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
\langle-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}+{\bm{I}}_{d},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle=0
.

Moreover, for any
{\bm{M}}_{1}\succeq{\bm{M}}_{2}\succ 0
, it holds that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and in particular,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})
.

Proof of
Proposition

A.3
.

We first show that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
exists and
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\succ 0
.
Note that for
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{K}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
, since
{\mathcal{K}}
is a linear subspace of
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
is a closed subset of
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}
, we know that
{\mathcal{H}}
is also a closed subset of
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}
.
Moreover, for any sequence
\{{\bm{H}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that
{\bm{H}}_{n}\succ 0
and either the smallest eigenvalue of
{\bm{H}}_{n}
converges to 0 or the largest eigenvalue of
{\bm{H}}_{n}
converges to
\infty
, the objective value
f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}}_{n})
goes to
\infty
because
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
.
Therefore, there exists
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\succ 0
that attains the minimum objective value.
For the uniqueness of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
, it suffices to note that the objective function
f_{M}({\bm{H}})
is strictly convex in
{\bm{H}}\succ 0
for
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
.

Proof for property
(a)
.

Suppose otherwise that
\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle\neq\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
, and consider the following matrix:

{\bm{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\cdot\sqrt{\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle/\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))}\in{\mathcal{H}}.

For this
{\bm{H}}
, we have
\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}})=2\sqrt{\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle\cdot\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))}<\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
, thus contradicting the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
Therefore, it must be true that
\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

Proof for property
(b)

Note that we can rewrite the original optimization problem as follows:

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\bigg\langle{\bm{M}},\bigg(\frac{{\bm{H}}}{\Tr({\bm{H}})}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg\rangle\cdot\frac{1}{\Tr({\bm{H}})}+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

Note that solving the above optimization problem is equivalent to first solving
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle
and then setting
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))\cdot\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
where the value of
\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
ensures the previous property
(a)
.
Hence, we see that
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
solves the constrained version of the original optimization problem.

Proof for property
(c)
.

Since
\nabla_{\bm{H}}\Tr({\bm{H}})={\bm{I}}_{d}
and
\nabla_{\bm{H}}\Tr({\bm{M}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}^{-1})=-{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}
(see e.g. Equation (124) in
Petersen et al. 2008
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{H}}f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})=-{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{I}}_{d}.

(13)

Then as
{\mathcal{H}}
is a cone, by the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
, it holds for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\bm{H}}f_{\bm{M}}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})),{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle=\langle-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}+{\bm{I}}_{d},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle

Proof for the operator monotonicity of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
.

By property
(c)
of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
, we know that for any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
,
\langle{\bm{M}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{2},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}{\bm{H}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle=0
.
Note that
{\bm{H}}\mapsto P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}{\bm{H}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}
is a bijection from
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}
by
Lemma

A.1
, so we have
\langle{\bm{M}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{2},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle=0
for all
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Applying this to both
{\bm{M}}_{1}
and
{\bm{M}}_{2}
, it follows that for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}}_{1}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{2},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{-1}\rangle=\langle{\bm{M}}_{2}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{2},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{-1}\rangle.

Rearranging the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{2}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{2},{\bm{H}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{M}}_{1}-{\bm{M}}_{2},{\bm{H}}\rangle-\langle{\bm{M}}_{1},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle{\bm{M}}_{2},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{-1}\rangle-\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2}))

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{M}}_{1}-{\bm{M}}_{2},{\bm{H}}\rangle

where the second equality follows from the first property of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})
and
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})
from
Proposition

A.3
.
Since
{\bm{M}}_{1}\succeq{\bm{M}}_{2}
, this implies that
\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{2}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{2},{\bm{H}}\rangle\geq 0
for all
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
By
Lemma

A.1
, we know that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{2}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, so it further follows from
Lemma

A.2
that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})^{2}\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})^{2}
.
Since matrix square root is operator monotone, it holds that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})
. We also know that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})\in{\mathcal{K}}
because
{\mathcal{K}}
is a subalgebra. Then we conclude that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})\in{\mathcal{H}}
from the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We can further extend the definition of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
to all positive semi-definite matrices.
Specifically, for any
{\bm{M}}\succeq 0
, we have
{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}\succ 0
for any
\epsilon>0
, so
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
is well-defined.
Also, by the operator monotonicity of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
from
Proposition

A.3
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\preceq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon^{\prime}{\bm{I}}_{d})
for
\epsilon^{\prime}\geq\epsilon>0
.
This implies that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
has a limit as
\epsilon\to 0
.
Therefore, for any
{\bm{M}}\succeq 0
, we define

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}).

(14)

Note that the above equality is also true for
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
.
To see this,
we apply the optimality of every
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
to get

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))>\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})).

Also note that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\succ P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}-\delta{\bm{I}}_{d})\succ 0
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
such that
{\bm{M}}-\delta{\bm{I}}_{d}\succ 0
.
Therefore, letting
\epsilon\to 0
on both sides of the previous inequality, we can exchange the order of taking the limit and taking the inverse of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
to obtain

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))\geq\Big\langle{\bm{M}},\Big(\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\Big)^{-1}\Big\rangle+\Tr\Big(\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\Big).

Then by the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
and its uniqueness, we conclude that (
14
) is also valid for any
{\bm{M}}\succ 0
.

Indeed, the definition of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
in (
14
) provides a continuous extension of
P_{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
, as summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a well-structured preconditioner set under
Definition

3.1
.
As a function on
{\mathcal{S}}_{++}^{d}
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}
can be continuously extended to be a function on
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
.
Moreover, for any
{\bm{M}}\succeq 0
such that
{\bm{M}}\neq 0
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\succeq 0
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\mathrm{span}({\bm{M}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
and
\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}})
.

(b)

\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

(c)

\langle{\bm{M}},\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle
where we recall that
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.

(d)

For any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle=0
, where

{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}

is the projection matrix onto
\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

Moreover, for any
{\bm{M}}_{1}\succeq{\bm{M}}_{2}\succeq 0
, it holds that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and in particular,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})
.

Proof of
Proposition

A.4
.

We divide the proof into different parts for different properties of
P_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof for continuous extension of
P_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We first show that
P_{\mathcal{H}}
can be continuously extended to
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
, and we consider the extension of
P_{\mathcal{H}}
as given in (
14
).
We first show that for any
{\bm{M}}\succeq 0
and any sequence
\{{\bm{M}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that each
{\bm{M}}_{n}\succ 0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\bm{M}}_{n}={\bm{M}}
, it holds that
\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
Note that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exist
\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\geq\underline{\epsilon}_{n}>0
such that
0\prec(1-\delta)({\bm{M}}+\underline{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d})\preceq{\bm{M}}_{n}\preceq{\bm{M}}+\bar{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d}
for all large enough
n
and moreover,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\underline{\epsilon}_{n}=0
.
Then by the operator monotonicity of
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
, we have
P_{\mathcal{H}}((1-\delta)({\bm{M}}+\underline{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d}))\preceq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})\preceq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\bar{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d})
.
Also note that
P_{\mathcal{H}}((1-\delta)({\bm{M}}+\underline{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d}))=\sqrt{1-\delta}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\underline{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d})
.
Letting
n\to\infty
, both
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\underline{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d})
and
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\bar{\epsilon}_{n}{\bm{I}}_{d})
converge to
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
, which then implies that
\sqrt{1-\delta}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\preceq\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})\preceq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
Since
\delta
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
Next, consider any general sequence
\{{\bm{M}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
{\bm{M}}_{n}\succeq 0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\bm{M}}_{n}={\bm{M}}
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
{\bm{M}}^{\prime}\succ 0
with
\|{\bm{M}}-{\bm{M}}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
, it holds that
\|P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\epsilon
.
For this
\delta
, there exists
N>0
such that for all
n>N
,
\|{\bm{M}}-{\bm{M}}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta/2
.
Then since
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})=\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n}+\frac{\delta}{2dm}{\bm{I}}_{d})
, where for every
m\geq 1
we have
\|{\bm{M}}-({\bm{M}}_{n}+\frac{\delta}{2m\sqrt{d}}{\bm{I}}_{d})\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\|{\bm{M}}-{\bm{M}}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\|\frac{\delta}{2m\sqrt{d}}{\bm{I}}_{d}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
, so
\|P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n}+\frac{\delta}{2m\sqrt{d}}{\bm{I}}_{d})\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\epsilon
for all
m\geq 1
, which implies that
\|P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\epsilon
for all
n>N
.
Therefore, it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{n})=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
In conclusion,
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
can be extended to be a continuous function on
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
.

Below, we fix any
{\bm{M}}\succeq 0
such that
{\bm{M}}\neq 0
.

Proof for
\mathrm{span}({\bm{M}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

Let
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}:=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}
be the projection matrix onto
\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
, then
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
by
Lemma

A.1
.
It suffices to show that
\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{M}}\rangle=0
.
Using the fact that
\Tr({\bm{A}}{\bm{B}})\leq\Tr({\bm{A}})\Tr({\bm{B}})
for any
{\bm{A}},{\bm{B}}\succeq 0
, we can get the following inequality for any
\epsilon>0
:

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{M}}\rangle=\Tr(({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}){\bm{M}})

\displaystyle\leq\Tr(({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))\cdot\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}{\bm{M}})

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\rangle\cdot\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{P}}_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle

(15)

By
Proposition

A.3
, we know that
\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))-\epsilon\langle{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle\leq\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))
, which is bounded by an absolute constant for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, letting
\epsilon\to 0
on both sided of (
15

\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\rangle\to\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle=0
by the definition of
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}
, we conclude that
\langle{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{M}}\rangle=0
.
This implies that
\mathrm{span}({\bm{M}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.

Proof for the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.

We still use
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}
to denote the projection matrix onto
\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, by the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
, it holds for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
that

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))\leq\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

(16)

Since
\mathrm{span}({\bm{M}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
, we know that
\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle=\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}\rangle
.
Then since
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
, it holds that
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}=({\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}){\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})^{\dagger}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, and also that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}({\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}){\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})^{\dagger}
.
This implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle=\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle.

(17)

Therefore, assuming the existence of the limit of
\langle\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle
as
\epsilon\to 0
, taking the limit of
\epsilon\to 0
on both sides of (
16
) yields

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\langle\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle\leq\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

(18)

Hence, it suffices to show that
\langle\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.
To prove this, note that for any
\epsilon>0
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})+\sqrt{\epsilon}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})\succ 0
, and its inverse is given by
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}+\epsilon^{-1/2}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})
.
Also,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})+\sqrt{\epsilon}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})\in{\mathcal{H}}
by
Lemma

A.1
.
Therefore, by the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})+\sqrt{\epsilon}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}))^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})+\sqrt{\epsilon}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}))

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle{\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}+\epsilon^{-1/2}({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))+\sqrt{\epsilon}(d-\mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})))

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\epsilon\cdot\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger})+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))+2\sqrt{\epsilon}(d-\mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})))

where we apply
\Tr({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})=d-\mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
and the last equality follows from the fact that
\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}\rangle=0
.
Rearranging the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon\langle{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))-\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon\cdot\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger})+2\sqrt{\epsilon}(d-\mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))).

Now applying (
17
) and noting that
\epsilon\langle{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle\geq 0
, taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\langle\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\rangle=0.

(19)

Then combining (
18
) and (
19
), we conclude that for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))\leq\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}).

This confirms the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.
Further applying the same argument as in the proof of
Proposition

A.3
yields the optimality of
\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 14.77603pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})}$}}$}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.

Proof for property
(d)
of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
.

By the optimality of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\underbrace{\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}})}_{f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\underbrace{\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})}_{\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})}.

(20)

To see why the last equality holds, first note that
\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})\geq\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})
because
\Tr({\bm{H}})\geq\Tr({\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})
.
Then for the other direction, note that we can always approximate
\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})
using
f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}}_{\delta})
where
{\bm{H}}_{\delta}=(1-\delta){\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}^{\top}+\delta{\bm{I}}_{d}
, for which
f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}}_{\delta})\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+(1-\delta)\Tr({\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})+\delta d\to\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})
as
\delta\to 0
.
This implies that
\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})\geq\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})
, and thus the optimal objective values of the two optimization problems are the same.
Now define
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})+({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})\in{\mathcal{H}}
whose inverse is
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}^{-1}=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}+({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})
, then
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}\succ 0
is a solution to the optimization problem in (
20
) because
\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}(\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}})=\langle{\bm{M}},P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}\rangle+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}f_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})
.
The gradient of
\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}
is given by
\nabla\tilde{f}_{\bm{M}}({\bm{H}})=-{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is a cone, the optimality of
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}
implies that for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle 0=\langle\nabla f(\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}),{\bm{H}}-\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}\rangle=\langle-\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}^{-1}+{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}-\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}\rangle.

By the definition of
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}
, we have
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\bm{M}}^{-1}=P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}
because
\mathrm{span}({\bm{M}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}))
.
Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})-({\bm{I}}_{d}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}{\bm{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})^{\dagger}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\rangle.

Proof for the operator monotonicity of the extended
P_{\mathcal{H}}
.

For any
{\bm{M}}_{1}\succeq{\bm{M}}_{2}\succeq 0
, it follows from
Proposition

A.3
that for any
\epsilon>0
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})
.
Taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
on both sides yields
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})\succeq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})
.
Similarly, as
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})\in{\mathcal{H}}
for every
\epsilon>0
by
Proposition

A.3
, we have
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{1}+\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d})-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}_{2}+\epsilon)\in{\mathcal{H}}
because
{\mathcal{H}}
is a closed set.
This completes the proof.
∎

A.3
Adaptive gradient norm corresponds to the dual norm

Proof of
Lemma

3.3
.

We begin with the case of
t=1
.
Fix any
{\bm{g}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
First for any
{\bm{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\|{\bm{w}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it holds for all
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1
that

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{g}}}\sqrt{{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}{\bm{w}}}\leq\sqrt{\langle{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle}\cdot\|{\bm{w}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sqrt{\langle{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle}

where the second inequality follows from the definition of
\|{\bm{w}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Now, further taking infimum over
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1
and then taking supremum over
{\bm{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\|{\bm{w}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\leq\sqrt{\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle}.

(21)

Next, we need to show that the above inequality holds also for the other direction.

By
Proposition

A.4
, we define
{\bm{H}}_{*}=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{M}},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle
.
We choose correspondingly
{\bm{w}}_{*}=\frac{{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}{\|{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}}
, which satisfies that
\|{\bm{w}}_{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1
.
Then

\displaystyle\|{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\geq{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}{\|{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}}.

(22)

Therefore, it suffices to show that
{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{*}\geq\sqrt{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}\geq\|{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{H}}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}.

By the property
(d)
of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})
from
Proposition

A.4
, we have
\langle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})\rangle=0
for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Rearranging this equality, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\top}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\bm{H}}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}^{\top}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}+\langle{\bm{\Pi}}_{{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}},{\bm{H}}-P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})\rangle

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}^{\top}P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top})^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}+\langle{\bm{\Pi}}_{{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}},{\bm{H}}\rangle-\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))

where the second equality is because
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}}
is exactly the projection matrix onto
\mathrm{span}(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))
.
Applying the above equality with
\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))\cdot\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.82288pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}$}}$}{\bm{H}}
in place of
{\bm{H}}
and plugging in the definition of
{\bm{H}}_{*}
, we further have

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.82288pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}$}}$}{\bm{H}}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))\langle{\bm{\Pi}}_{{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}},\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.82288pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}$}}$}{\bm{H}}\rangle-\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}+\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))^{2}\Tr(\makebox[0.0pt]{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 2.82288pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\phantom{{\bm{H}}}$}}$}{\bm{H}})-\Tr(P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top}))^{2}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}.

This implies that for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1
, we have
{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{H}}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}\leq{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}
.
Therefore, it follows from (
22
) that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}\sqrt{\frac{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}{\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{H}}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{*}^{\dagger}{\bm{g}}}=\sqrt{\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\langle{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle}

(23)

where the equality follows from the definition of
{\bm{H}}_{*}
.

Finally, combining (
21
) and (
A.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{g}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{\langle{\bm{g}}{\bm{g}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle}.

(24)

For the general case of
{\bm{g}}_{1:t}=({\bm{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{g}}_{t})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times t}
, note that for any
{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t}\rangle=\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})^{\top},({\bm{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t})^{-1}\rangle.

Then applying (
24
) with
\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})
in place of
{\bm{g}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t}
in place of
{\mathcal{H}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t},\Tr({\bm{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t})\leq 1}\sqrt{\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})^{\top},({\bm{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t})^{-1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{t\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{g}}_{1:t})^{\top},({\bm{H}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{t})^{-1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t}\cdot\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

where the second equality is because

.
This completes the proof.

A.4
Examples of ill-structured preconditioner sets

A.4.1
Preconditioner set of two-sided Shampoo

Consider
{\mathcal{H}}=\{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}:{\bm{H}}={\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}}\text{ for some }{\bm{U}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{L}\times d_{L}},{\bm{V}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{R}\times d_{R}}\}
.
In particular, we consider the special case of
d_{L}=d_{R}=2
, and the following two matrices

\displaystyle{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)={\rm diag}(1,\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon),\qquad{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)={\rm diag}(1,\epsilon,\epsilon,1)

where
\epsilon>0
is a small constant to be determined later.
Clearly
{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)\preceq{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)
when
\epsilon\leq 1
, but we will show that
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)}\preceq{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}
does not hold for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.
For any
{\bm{H}}={\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, it holds that both
{\bm{U}}
and
{\bm{V}}
are PSD, and we explicitly parametrize them as

\displaystyle{\bm{U}}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{2}&u_{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad{\bm{V}}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&v_{2}\\
v_{2}&v_{3}\end{pmatrix}

where
u_{1},u_{3}\geq 0
,
u_{1}u_{3}\geq u_{2}^{2}
, and similarly,
v_{1},v_{3}\geq 0
,
v_{1}v_{3}\geq v_{3}^{2}
.
Correspondingly,

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}={\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}v_{1}&u_{1}v_{2}&u_{2}v_{1}&u_{2}v_{2}\\
u_{1}v_{2}&u_{1}v_{3}&u_{2}v_{2}&u_{2}v_{3}\\
u_{2}v_{1}&u_{2}v_{2}&u_{3}v_{1}&u_{3}v_{2}\\
u_{2}v_{2}&u_{3}v_{2}&u_{3}v_{2}&u_{3}v_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

We first analyze
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}
.
For convenience, we consider

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}}}\langle{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon),{\bm{H}}\rangle+\Tr({\bm{H}}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bm{U}},{\bm{V}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{2}}\underbrace{\langle{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon),{\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}}\rangle+\Tr(({\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}})^{-1})}_{=:f_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})}

Recall the properties of the Kronecker product that
({\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{V}})^{-1}={\bm{U}}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{V}}^{-1}
and that
\Tr({\bm{U}}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{V}}^{-1})=\Tr({\bm{U}}^{-1})\cdot\Tr({\bm{V}}^{-1})
.
Then further using the definition of
{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})

\displaystyle=u_{1}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3}+\Tr({\bm{U}}^{-1})\cdot\Tr({\bm{V}}^{-1})+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1})

\displaystyle=u_{1}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3}+\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}-u_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}-v_{2}^{2}}+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1})

where we apply the explicit expression of
{\bm{U}}^{-1}
and
{\bm{V}}^{-1}
.
First observe that to minimize
f_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})
, we must have
u_{2}=v_{2}=0
because otherwise
\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}-u_{2}^{2}}>\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}}
and similarly
\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}-v_{2}^{2}}>\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}}
.
Therefore, it suffices to further minimize the following:

\displaystyle f_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{u_{1}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3}+\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}}\cdot\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}}}_{\tilde{f}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})}\,+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}).

For the first term
\tilde{f}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})
, we can apply the AM-GM inequality to get
\tilde{f}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})\geq(4\sqrt{u_{1}u_{3}v_{1}v_{3}})^{1/3}
, where the equality is achieved when
u_{1}=u_{3}
,
v_{1}=v_{3}
, and
u_{1}v_{1}=\sqrt{2}
.
Moreover, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, the contribution of the second term
\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1})
is negligible, and thus we conclude that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}{\bm{I}}_{4}
.

Similarly, for
{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})

\displaystyle=u_{1}v_{1}+\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}-u_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}-v_{2}^{2}}+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3})

\displaystyle\geq u_{1}v_{1}+\frac{u_{1}+u_{3}}{u_{1}u_{3}}\cdot\frac{v_{1}+v_{3}}{v_{1}v_{3}}+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{u_{1}v_{1}+\frac{1}{u_{1}v_{1}}}_{\tilde{f}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})}+\frac{1}{u_{1}v_{3}}+\frac{1}{u_{3}v_{1}}+\frac{1}{u_{3}v_{3}}+\epsilon(u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}+u_{3}v_{3})

where the equality holds when
u_{2}=v_{2}=0
.
The first term
\tilde{f}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}}({\bm{U}},{\bm{V}})
attains the minimum value when
u_{1}v_{1}=1
, and the remainder can be made small by choosing large
u_{3},v_{3}
when
\epsilon
is sufficiently small.
Therefore, we conclude that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)}={\rm diag}(1,0,0,0).

Now comparing the limits of
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}}(\epsilon)
and
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}}(\epsilon)
as
\epsilon\to 0
, we can see that for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, it holds that
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}(\epsilon)}[1,1]>{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}[1,1]
.
Hence, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,
{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{1}}(\epsilon)\preceq{\bm{H}}_{{\bm{G}}_{2}(\epsilon)}
does not hold.

A.4.2
Tridiagonal matrices

Consider
{\mathcal{H}}=\{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}:{\bm{H}}\text{ is tridiagonal}\}
.
Here, for
{\bm{H}}
to be tridiagonal, it has nonzero elements on the main diagonal, the first diagonal above the main diagonal, and the first diagonal below the main diagonal.
We consider the specific example of 3-by-3 tridiagonal matrices, and examine
P_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)
for the following two matrices

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\
1&2&1\\
1&1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bm{M}}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}10000&1&1\\
1&2&1\\
1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.

It is clear that
0\prec{\bm{M}}\preceq{\bm{M}}^{\prime}
.
We numerically solve for
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
and
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})
to get

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\approx\begin{pmatrix}1.382548&0.297594&0\\
0.297594&1.318491&0.297594\\
0&0.297594&1.382548\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})\approx\begin{pmatrix}100.000004&0.007229&0\\
0.007229&1.365999&0.366002\\
0&0.366002&1.366032\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})\preceq P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})
does not hold because the last diagonal entry of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}})
is larger than that of
P_{\mathcal{H}}({\bm{M}}^{\prime})
.

Appendix B
Calculations for Examples of Well-Structured Preconditioner Sets

As mentioned in
Section

3.3
, we provide calculations for how to derive each specific algorithm from
Algorithm

1
with specific choice of
{\mathcal{H}}
and explain each entry in
Table

1
.
Recall from
Algorithm

1
that

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{t}=\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top}.

B.1
AdaGrad-Norm

For AdaGrad-Norm, we have
{\mathcal{H}}=\{c\cdot{\bm{I}}_{d}\mid c\geq 0\}
.

Calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
.

Then for any
{\bm{H}}=c\cdot{\bm{I}}_{d}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\Tr({\bm{M}}_{t})+\eta^{2}cd\geq 2\eta\sqrt{d\cdot\Tr({\bm{M}}_{t})}

where the equality is achieved by choosing

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\Tr({\bm{M}}_{t})}=\frac{1}{\eta}\sqrt{\epsilon+\frac{1}{d}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left\|{\bm{g}}_{s}\right\|_{2}^{2}}.

This corresponds exactly to the update rule of AdaGrad-Norm, which adjusts the global learning rate based on the accumulated
\ell_{2}
norm of past gradients.

Calculation for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

For the associated
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{0\leq c\leq 1/d}\sqrt{c\cdot{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{I}}_{d}{\bm{x}}}=\frac{\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}.

Calculation for
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

We can calculate the adaptive gradient norm similarly:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\inf_{0\leq c\leq 1/d}\sqrt{\left\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},(c{\bm{I}}_{d})^{-1}\right\rangle}=\sqrt{d}\sqrt{\Tr\bigg(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)}=\sqrt{d}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left\|{\bm{g}}_{s}\right\|_{2}^{2}}.

B.2
Diagonal AdaGrad

For diagonal AdaGrad,
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{D}}_{+}^{d}=\{{\rm diag}(c_{1},\dots,c_{d})\mid c_{1},\dots,c_{d}\geq 0\}
.

Calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
.

For any
{\bm{H}}={\rm diag}(c_{1},\dots,c_{d})\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{{\bm{M}}_{t,ii}}{c_{i}}+\eta^{2}c_{i}\bigg)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\eta\sqrt{{\bm{M}}_{t,ii}}

where the equality is achieved by choosing

\displaystyle c_{i}=\frac{1}{\eta}\sqrt{{\bm{M}}_{t,ii}}=\frac{1}{\eta}\sqrt{\epsilon+\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}},\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,d$.}

This corresponds to the update rule diagonal AdaGrad, which computes the historical sum of squared gradients for each individual coordinate.

Calculation for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

For the associated
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\leq 1}\sqrt{{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\rm diag}(c_{1},\dots,c_{d}){\bm{x}}}=\sup_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\leq 1}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}x_{i}^{2}}=\max_{i\in[d]}|x_{i}|=\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{\infty}

Calculation for
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We can calculate the adaptive gradient norm similarly:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\leq 1}\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},{\rm diag}(c_{1}\dots,c_{d})^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\leq 1}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}}

where the last equality is because of the Cauchy inequality: for
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\leq 1
,

\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^{2}}.

B.3
Full-matrix AdaGrad

For full-matrix AdaGrad,
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d}
.

Calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
.

Note that
\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})
is a convex function of
{\bm{H}}\succ 0
, so we can get
{\bm{H}}_{t}
by first calculating the gradient of the objective function and then setting it to zero.
Specifically, for any
{\bm{H}}\succ 0
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{H}}\bigg(\left\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})\bigg)=-{\bm{H}}^{-1}{\bm{M}}_{t}{\bm{H}}^{-1}+\eta^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d}.

Setting it to zero yields

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}_{t}=\frac{1}{\eta}{\bm{M}}_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\eta}\bigg(\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This corresponds to the update rule of full-matrix AdaGrad.

Calculation for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

For the associated
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}\succeq 0,\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}{\bm{x}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}\succeq 0,\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{\left\langle{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}\right\rangle}=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}({\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top})}=\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{2}.

Calculation for
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We can adapt the calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
to the constrained optimization problem in the definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{H}}
to get

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{\Big\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},\bigg(\frac{1}{\Tr[(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top})^{\frac{1}{2}}]}\bigg(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg].

B.4
One-sided Shampoo

For one-sided Shampoo,
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d_{L}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}
.

Calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
.

For any
{\bm{H}}={\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\bm{H}}^{-1}={\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}
, so we have
\Tr({\bm{H}})=d_{R}\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L})
and

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},({\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}})^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d},{\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}\rangle+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\langle\vec{(}{\bm{G}}_{s})\vec{(}{\bm{G}}_{s})^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\epsilon d_{R}\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1})+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\Tr({\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}{\bm{G}}_{s})

\displaystyle=\Tr\bigg[\bigg(\epsilon d_{R}{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg){\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\bigg]

where we use the fact that
\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{X}})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}({\bm{X}})^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}\rangle=\langle{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}\rangle
for any
{\bm{X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{L}\times d_{R}}
.
Again, since the objective function is convex in
{\bm{H}}_{L}\succ 0
, we can derive
{\bm{H}}_{L}
by first calculating the gradient and then setting it to zero.
Taking derivative with respect to
{\bm{H}}_{L}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bm{H}}_{L}}\left(\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\rangle+\eta^{2}\Tr({\bm{H}})\right)=-{\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\bigg(\epsilon d_{R}{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg){\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}+\eta^{2}d_{R}{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}.

Setting it to
0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bm{H}}_{L}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d_{L}}}\langle{\bm{M}}_{t},({\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}})^{-1}\rangle+\eta^{2}d_{R}\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta\sqrt{d_{R}}}\bigg(\epsilon d_{R}{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\bigg(\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}+\frac{1}{d_{R}}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This is exactly the
{\bm{L}}_{t}
in
Algorithm

4
.
Therefore, the preconditioner
{\bm{H}}_{t}
in one-sided Shampoo is given by

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}_{t}=\frac{1}{\eta}\bigg(\epsilon{\bm{I}}_{d_{L}}+\frac{1}{d_{R}}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}.

As a result,
Algorithm

1
with
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d_{L}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}
recovers
Algorithm

4
.

Calculation for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

For the associated
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}={\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}},{\bm{H}}_{L}\succeq 0,\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{H}}{\bm{x}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}_{L}\succeq 0,\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L})\leq 1/d_{R}}\sqrt{\langle\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}{({\bm{X}})}\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}{({\bm{X}})}^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d_{R}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bm{H}}_{L}\succeq 0,\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L})\leq 1/d_{R}}\sqrt{\left\langle{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{R}}}\left\|{\bm{X}}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

where the last equality is achieved at
{\bm{H}}_{L}={\bm{u}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}/d_{R}
for
{\bm{u}}
being the leading eigenvector of
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
.
The derivation above provides a proof for
Lemma

4.1
.

Calculation for
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

Again, for the adaptive gradient norm, we can adapt the calculation for
{\bm{H}}_{t}
to the constrained optimization problem in the definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
to get

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bm{g}}_{1:t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}\in{\mathcal{H}},\Tr({\bm{H}})\leq 1}\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{g}}_{s}{\bm{g}}_{s}^{\top},{\bm{H}}^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bm{H}}_{L}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{d_{L}},\Tr({\bm{H}}_{L})\leq\frac{1}{d_{R}}}\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top},{\bm{H}}_{L}^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bigg\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top},\bigg[\frac{1}{d_{R}\Tr[(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top})^{\frac{1}{2}}]}\bigg(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]^{-1}\bigg\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d_{R}}\Tr\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]=\Tr\bigg[\bigg(d_{R}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{G}}_{s}{\bm{G}}_{s}^{\top}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg].

E.1
Efficient implementation of full-matrix AdaGrad.

Directly applying full-matrix AdaGrad to this
10^{6}
-dimensional problem is impractical. Instead, we consider the eigendecomposition of
{\bm{H}}
to be
{\bm{U}}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}{\bm{U}}
and define the transformation
\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}})={\bm{U}}{\bm{X}}
. We further define
d
orthogonal matrices
{\bm{V}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{V}}_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
such that the first row of
{\bm{V}}_{i}
is in the same direction of
\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}^{*}-{\bm{X}}_{0})_{i,:}
and define
{\bm{V}}=\text{diag}({\bm{V}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{V}}_{d})
. We can know that
{\bm{V}}\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}^{*}-{\bm{X}}_{0})={\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{e}}_{1}
where
{\bm{v}}_{i}=\left\|\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}^{*}-{\bm{X}}_{0})_{i,:}\right\|_{2}
for
i\in[d]
.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}})=\left\langle{\bm{H}},({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\Sigma},({\bm{U}}{\bm{X}}-{\bm{U}}{\bm{X}}^{*})({\bm{U}}{\bm{X}}-{\bm{U}}{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\Sigma},(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}})-\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}^{*}))(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}})-\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}^{*}))^{\top}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\Sigma}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d},\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top})^{\top}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\Sigma}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d},{\bm{V}}\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top})\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{*})^{\top})^{\top}{\bm{V}}^{\top}\right\rangle

Further denoting
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}={\bm{V}}\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}})^{\top})
and
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}={\bm{V}}\mathop{\overline{\mathrm{vec}}}(\mathcal{T}({\bm{X}}_{0})^{\top})
, then we obtain

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}\left\|\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{(i-1)d+1:id}-({\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{e}}_{1})_{(i-1)d+1:id}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}\bigg[(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{(i-1)d+1}-\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{(i-1)d+1}-{\bm{v}}_{i})^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{d}(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{(i-1)d+j}-\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{(i-1)d+j})^{2}\bigg]

Running full-matrix AdaGrad on
f({\bm{X}})
starting from
{\bm{X}}_{0}
can be implemented equivalently by using
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}
as variable starting from
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}
. Only
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{(i-1)d+1}
will receive non-zero gradient so full-matrix AdaGrad actually only cares these
d
coordinates, which reduces the original problem to a problem only with
d
variables.

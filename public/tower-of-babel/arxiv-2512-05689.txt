Resolvent trace asymptotics for operators in the Shubin class
Jörg Seiler

Abstract
. A new pseudodifferential calculus of Shubin type is introduced. The calculus contains operators depending on a non negative real parameter as well as operators independent of the parameter. Resolvents of Shubin type pseudodifferential operators are constructed and their trace expansion is obtained. MSC (2020): 47G30, 47L80, 47A10 Keywords: Pseudodifferential operators; parameter-ellipticity; resolvent; trace expansion; operators of Toeplitz type



1.
Introduction

In
[
23
]
, Shubin introduced a calculus of pseudodifferential operators on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which permits to construct the
parametrix of the harmonic oscillator
A=|x|^{2}-\Delta
, where
\Delta
is the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The calculus is build
upon symbol-classes
\Gamma^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
,
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
, consisting of smooth functions
a(x,\xi)
satisfying

|\partial^{\alpha}_{z}a(z)|\lesssim(1+|z|^{2})^{(d-|\alpha|)/2},\qquad z:=(x,\xi),

for derivatives of arbitrary order, and corresponding subclasses
\Gamma^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
of poly-homogeneous symbols which, in addition, admit an asymptotic expansion into homogeneous components of (one-step) decreasing orders. Ellipticity means invertibility of the homogeneous principal symbol on the unit-sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}_{z}
; the harmonic oscillator is elliptic, since its homogeneous principal symbol is constant
1
on the
z
unit-sphere.

There exists a significant amount of literature on Shubin type operators and their generalizations and applications. This includes spectral theory
[
2
,
10
,
15
,
24
]
, wave front sets and propagation of singularities
[
14
,
16
,
17
]
, Fourier integral operators
[
1
,
3
,
10
]
, and other arguments
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, just to name a very few. An extension of the Shubin calculus from Euclidean space to asymptotically conic manifolds has been developed in
[
11
]
.

Resolvents of
differential
operators can be handled by means of
strongly
parameter-dependent symbols
a(z,\mu)
satisfying

|\partial^{\alpha}_{z}\partial^{j}_{\mu}a(z,\mu)|\lesssim(1+|z|^{2}+\mu^{2})^{(d-|\alpha|-j)/2},\qquad(z,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+},

and corresponding poly-homogeneous subclasses. Homogeneous components are in one-to-one correspondence with smooth functions on the unit-sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
. For example,
\mu^{2}+A
with the harmonic oscillator
A
is poly-homogeneous of order
d=2
and
(\mu^{2}+A)^{-1}
is strongly parameter-dependent of order
-2
.

The use of strongly parameter-dependent symbols limits the resolvent construction to differential operators; however, it can be adapted to pseudodifferential operators, as has already been observed in the classical work
[
18
]
of Seeley on the complex powers of pseudodifferential operators on closed manifolds, see also Helffer’s book
[
10
]
for operators in the Shubin class. A systematic formulation in terms of a pseudodifferential calculus has been developed by Grubb in
[
7
]
in the framework of Hörmander classes, introducing the concept of
regularity number
. The regularity number associated with a symbol measures how far or near it is from being strongly parameter-dependent. In
[
21
]
the author observed that the regularity number has an interpretation in terms of a singularity structure of the involved homogeneous components. In the present paper we show that this interpretation extends to the Shubin class as well and allows us to construct a calculus of Shubin type with the following key properties:

•

It contains both parameter-independent and strongly parameter-dependent Shubin classes;

•

ellipticity is characterized by the invertibility of two associated principal symbols (the homogeneous principal symbol
and the so-called
principal limit symbol/operator
);

•

the parametrices to arbitrary elliptic elements (both differential and pseudodifferential) belong to the calculus and yield inverses
for large values of the parameter.

The central idea is to work with classes of homogeneous symbols that are defined only on the punctured unit-sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
with the “north-pole”
(z,\mu)=(0,1)
removed; these symbols are singular in the north-pole in a controlled way, namely, when written in polar-coordinates centered in the north-pole, they admit a
weighted Taylor expansion
. These classes yield a unified approach to both parameter-independent and parameter-dependent operators.

As it turns out, homogeneous symbols in the new classes naturally admit a second kind of symbol expansion which is closely related to the expansion used by Grubb and Seeley in their celebrated work
[
9
]
on resolvent trace expansions for boundary value problems with Atiyah-Patodi-Singer type boundary conditions. While the calculus of
[
9
]
has implemented an expansion of symbols in powers of the parameter
\mu
, in our approach arises an expansion in powers of
|(z,\mu)|
. This implies the existence of an expansion in the sense of Grubb-Seeley, cf. Theorem
3.2
, and yields another key feature of the calculus:

•

Any element of order less than
-2n
is
\mu
-wise of trace class in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and, for
\mu\to+\infty
, its trace admits
an expansion in decreasing powers of
\mu
, and decreasing powers of
\mu
multiplied with
\log\mu
;

the precise statement is given in Theorem
3.3
. The resolvent trace expansion for elliptic pseudodifferential operators in the Shubin class is a special case, see Theorem
5.6
. Recall that the resolvent trace expansion determines the pole structure of the
\zeta
-function and the short-time heat kernel asymptotics. Let us mention that the
\eta
-function of Shubin operators has been studied in
[
13
]
.

For simplicity of exposition, throughout the paper we shall focus on scalar-valued symbols; the extension to matrix-valued symbols does not cause essential new difficulties. The matrix-valued calculus then allows to consider resolvents of Toeplitz-type operators, i.e., operators of the form
\mathbf{A}(\mu)=P_{1}A(\mu)P_{0}
where
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
are zero-order idempotents in the Shubin class and
A(\mu)
is from the new calculus. There is a natural notion of ellipticity for such Toeplitz operators and it holds:

•

If
\mathbf{A}(\mu)
is elliptic, there exists a parametrix of the form
\mathbf{B}(\mu)=P_{0}B(\mu)P_{1}
such that

\mathbf{B}(\mu)\mathbf{A}(\mu)=P_{0}
and
\mathbf{A}(\mu)\mathbf{B}(\mu)=P_{1}
for large values of
\mu
.

This means that
\mathbf{B}(\mu)
induces the inverse of the operator

P_{1}A(\mu):P_{0}(\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p})\longrightarrow P_{1}(\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}},\;1<p<+\infty,

where
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}
represents here
L_{p}
-Sobolev spaces of Shubin-type, cf. (
4.1
), and
P_{j}(\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p})
is the closed subspace determined by the projection
P_{j}
. In order to keep the paper short, we will not discuss Toeplitz-operators in more detail, but refer the reader to
[
21
]
, where this was done in the context of operators from Hörmander’s class, see also
[
19
,
20
]
. The adaption to the Shubin class is straight-forward.

2.
Various classes of pseudodifferential symbols

Let us recall a number of pseudodifferential symbol classes; essentially these classes
have been introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
, though we discuss here some additional
aspects, in particular, in Sections
2.4
and
3
.
Throughout the whole section
d
and
\nu
will denote real numbers.

2.1.
Hörmander classes

We shall write
S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
for the vector space of all smooth

a(z):{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying the uniform estimates

|D^{\alpha}_{z}a(z)|\lesssim\langle z\rangle^{d-|\alpha|},\qquad\langle z\rangle:=(1+|z|^{2})^{1/2},

for every multi-indices
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{m}
.
A symbol
a(z)\in S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is called
poly-homogeneous

if there exist smooth functions
a^{(d-\ell)}(z)
defined for
z\not=0

which are homogeneous of degree
d-\ell
in
z
, i.e.

a^{(d-\ell)}(tz)=t^{d-\ell}\,a^{(d-\ell)}(z)\qquad\forall\;t>0,\;z\not=0,

such that

r_{a,N}(z):=a(z)-\chi(z)\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}a^{(d-\ell)}(z)\in S^{d-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

for every
N
, where
\chi(z)
is an arbitrary fixed zero-excision function.

a^{(d)}(z)
is the so-called homogeneous principal symbol of
a
.
The space of poly-homogeneous symbols is denoted by
S^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,
the space of homogeneous symbols of degree
d
by
S^{d}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.

Remark 2.1
.

S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is a Fréchet space with topology induced by the semi-norms
\|a\|^{(\alpha)}_{d}=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}|\partial^{\alpha}a(z)|\langle z\rangle^{|\alpha|-d}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{m}
. Similarly for
S^{d}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, where in the supremum
z\not=0
and
\langle z\rangle^{|\alpha|-d}
is replaced by
|z|^{|\alpha|-d}
. The poly-homogeneous symbols of order
d
then carry the projective topology induced by the maps
a\mapsto a^{(d-\ell)}:S^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\to S^{d-\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and
a\mapsto r_{a,N}:S^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\to S^{d-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
with
\ell,N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

In an analogous way all symbol classes introduced below will be Frèchet spaces.
We shall use this fact freely throughout the paper.
Note that

S^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}):=\cap_{d\in{\mathbb{R}}}S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})=\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

coincides with the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

In the same way, replacing above
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
by
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
and
z
by
(z,\mu)
, we obtain
the parameter-dependent Hörmander classes
S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
,
S^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

and
S^{r}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
, respectively. Alternatively,

S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=\Big\{a|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}}\mid a\in S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m+1})\Big\}

and similar for the spaces of homogeneous and poly-homogeneous symbols.

2.2.
Symbols with regularity number

The following definitions are motivated by
[
7
]
.
Let
\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
denote the vector space of all smooth functions
a(z,\mu):{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying the uniform estimates

|D^{\alpha}_{z}D^{j}_{\mu}a(z,\mu)|\lesssim\langle z\rangle^{\nu-|\alpha|}\langle z,\mu\rangle^{d-\nu-j}

for every multi-indices
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{m}
and every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
A function
a(z,\mu)
belongs, by definition, to
\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
if it is defined
and smooth for
z\not=0
,

a(tz,t\mu)=t^{d}\,a(z,\mu)\qquad\forall\;t>0,\;\mu\geq 0,\;z\not=0,

and satisfies the uniform estimates

|D^{\alpha}_{z}D^{j}_{\mu}a(z,\mu)|\lesssim|z|^{\nu-|\alpha|}|(z,\mu)|^{d-\nu-j}

for derivatives of any order.
A symbol
a(z,\mu)\in\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is called
poly-homogeneous

if there exist
a^{(d-\ell,\nu-\ell)}(z,\mu)\in\widetilde{S}^{d-\ell,\nu-\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
such that

r_{a,N}(z):=a(z,\mu)-\chi(z)\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}a^{(d-\ell,\nu-\ell)}(z,\mu)\in\widetilde{S}^{d-N,\nu-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

for every
N
, where
\chi(z)
is an arbitrary fixed zero-excision function (note that for the parameter-dependent Hörmander classes an excision function
\chi(z,\mu)
is used). The leading component
a^{(d,\nu)}
is the so-called homogeneous principal symbol of
a
. The class of such poly-homogeneous symbols is denoted by
\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
. The class of smoothing symbols is

\widetilde{S}^{d-\infty,\nu-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}):=\cap_{N\geq 0}\;\widetilde{S}^{d-N,\nu-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+});

note that
r
belongs to this class if and only if

|D^{\alpha}_{z}D^{j}_{\mu}r(z,\mu)|\lesssim\langle z\rangle^{-L}\langle\mu\rangle^{d-\nu-j}

for all
L\geq 0
and derivatives of any order, i.e.,
r
behaves in
z
as a rapidly decreasing function while in
\mu
as a Hörmander symbol of order
d-\nu
. For this reason we shall also write

(2.1)

\widetilde{S}^{d-\infty,\nu-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=S^{d-\nu}_{1,0}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+},S^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})).

Let us also observe that

\widetilde{S}^{d-1,\nu-1}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\cup\widetilde{S}_{1,0}^{d,\nu+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\subset\widetilde{S}_{1,0}^{d,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

and analogously for the subclasses of poly-homogeneous symbols.

2.3.
Symbols with expansion at infinity

Let
\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
denote the subspace of

\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
consisting of those symbols
a
for which esists a
sequence of symbols
a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}\in S^{\nu+j}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
such that

(2.2)

a(z,\mu)-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}(z)\,[z,\mu]^{d-\nu-j}\in\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu+N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

for every choice of
N
. In (
2.2
),
[z,\mu]
denotes a smooth positive function in
(z,\mu)
which coincides with

|(z,\mu)|
whenever
|(z,\mu)|\geq 1
.
In particular,
[z,\mu]^{\rho}\in S^{\rho}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
for arbitrary
\rho
.
The symbols
a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}
are uniquely determined by
a
, in particular,

a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu]}(z)=\lim_{\mu\to+\infty}[z,\mu]^{\nu-d}a(z,\mu)

with convergence in
S^{\nu+1}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. We shall call
a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu]}
the
principal limit symbol
of
a
.

As a simple example, observe that if
a(z)\in S^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is independent of the parameter
\mu
, then
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
and
a^{\infty}_{[d,d]}=a
, i.e., the expansion in (
2.2
) has only one term.

Note that if
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
then

\partial^{k}_{\mu}\partial^{\alpha}_{z}a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-k-|\alpha|,\nu-|\alpha|}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

with

(\partial^{k}_{\mu}\partial^{\alpha}_{z}a)^{\infty}_{[d-k-|\alpha|,\nu-|\alpha|]}=(d-\nu)(d-\nu-1)\cdots(d-\nu-k+1)D^{\alpha}_{z}a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu]}.

Moreover, if
a_{\ell}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d_{\ell},\nu_{\ell}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
for
\ell=0,1
then

a_{1}a_{0}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d_{0}+d_{1},\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
with

(a_{1}a_{0})^{\infty}_{[d_{0}+d_{1},\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}]}=a^{\infty}_{1,[d_{1},\nu_{1}]}a^{\infty}_{0,[d_{0},\nu_{0}]}.

Lemma 2.2
.

a
)

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
. Then

a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu+\ell}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\iff a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty}=a_{[d,\nu+1]}^{\infty}=\ldots=a_{[d,\nu+\ell-1]}^{\infty}=0.

b
)

Let
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-\ell,\nu-\ell}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
and

a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}=a^{\infty}_{[d-\ell,\nu-\ell+j]}\in S^{\nu-\ell+j}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\qquad\forall\;j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Proof.

a) is obvious by definition of the symbol classes. b) is valid since

a(z,\mu)-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a^{\infty}_{[d-\ell,\nu-\ell+j]}(z)[z,\mu]^{d-\nu-j}\in\widetilde{S}^{d-\ell,\nu-\ell+N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

and
\widetilde{S}^{d-\ell,\nu-\ell+N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\subset\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu+N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
for every
N
.
∎

The following result on asymptotic summation is
[
21
, Theorem 5.5]
(in the special case of
x
-independent symbols).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
a_{\ell}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-\ell,\nu-\ell}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Then there exists a symbol
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
such that, for every
N
,

a-\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}a_{\ell}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-N,\nu-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}).

Moreover,

a_{[d,\nu+j]}^{\infty}\sim\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}a_{\ell,[d-\ell,\nu-\ell+j]}^{\infty}

asymptotically in
S^{\nu+j}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
for every
j
. The symbol
a
is unique
modulo
\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-\infty,\nu-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
.

It can be shown, see
[
22
, Proposition 3.6]
, that

(2.3)

\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-\infty,\nu-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=\cap_{N\geq 0}\;\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d-N,\nu-M}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=S^{d-\nu}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+},S^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})),

i.e., a smoothing symbol behaves as a rapidly decreasing function in
z
and as a
poly-homogeneous
symbol of order
d-\nu
in
\mu
, cf. (
2.1
).

2.4.
Poly-homogeneous symbols with expansion at infinity

Let

\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+}={\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{(0,1)\}=\{(z,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}\mid|(z,\mu)|=1,\;z\not=0\},

denote the unit-sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
with the “north-pole”
(0,1)
removed.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
C^{\infty,\nu}_{\mathbf{T}}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+})
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, denote the vector space of all
\widehat{a}\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+})
such that

(r,\phi)\mapsto r^{-\nu}\,\widehat{a}\big(r\phi,\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\big):(0,1)_{r}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}_{\phi}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

extends to a smooth function on
[0,1)\times{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}

(
smoothness including
r=0)
;

\widehat{a}_{\langle\nu\rangle}(\phi):=\lim_{r\to 0+}r^{-\nu}\,\widehat{a}\big(r\phi,\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\big),\qquad\phi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1},

is called the
angular symbol
of
\widehat{a}
.

In the previous definition,
{\mathbb{S}}^{m-1}
is the unit-sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{z}
. In other words, the function
|z|^{-\nu}\widehat{a}(z,\mu)

written in polar-coordinates around the north-pole
(0,1)
admits a Taylor expansion centered in
r=0
.

Note that if
\widehat{a}_{p}(z,\mu)=|z|^{\nu+j}p(z/|z|)
with
p\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{m-1})
, then

\widehat{a}_{p}\in C^{\infty,\nu}_{\mathbf{T}}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+})
, since
\widehat{a}_{p}\big(r\phi,\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\big)=r^{\nu+j}p(\phi)
. This yields the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\widehat{a}\in C^{\infty,\nu}_{\mathbf{T}}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+})
. Then there exist uniquely determined symbols
p_{j}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{m-1})
such that

\widehat{a}(z,\mu)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}|z|^{\nu+j}p_{j}\Big(\frac{z}{|z|}\Big)+\widehat{r}_{N}(z,\mu),\qquad\widehat{r}_{N}\in C^{\infty,\nu+N}_{\mathbf{T}}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+}),

for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Clearly,
p_{0}=\widehat{a}_{\langle\nu\rangle}
.

Homogeneous extension of degree
d
leads to the classes
\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
, consisting of all functions
a
of the form

a(z,\mu)=|(z,\mu)|^{d}\;\widehat{a}\Big(\frac{(z,\mu)}{|(z,\mu)|}\Big),\qquad\widehat{a}\in C^{\infty,\nu}_{\mathbf{T}}(\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+}).

Note that any such homogeneous function is, in general, only defined for
z\not=0
.
The
angular symbol
of
a
is, by definition,

(2.4)

a_{\langle\nu\rangle}(z)=|z|^{\nu}\;\widehat{a}_{\langle\nu\rangle}\Big(\frac{z}{|z|}\Big)\in S^{\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}).

It can be shown that
\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\subset\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
, cf.
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
. If
\widehat{a}_{p}(z,\mu)=|z|^{\nu+j}p(z/|z|)
as above, its
d
-homogeneous extension is

a(z,\mu)=|z|^{\nu+j}p(z/|z|)|(z,\mu)|^{d-\nu-j}.

Together with Lemma
2.5
we thus obtain:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
. Then there exist uniquely determined symbols

a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}\in S^{\nu+j}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
such that

a(z,\mu)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu+j]}(z)|(z,\mu)|^{d-\nu-j}+r_{N}(z,\mu),\qquad r_{N}\in\widetilde{S}^{d,\nu+N}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}),

for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular,
\widehat{a}_{\langle\nu\rangle}=a^{\infty}_{[d,\nu]}
.

Since the spaces of homogeneous symbols from Hörmander’s class
S^{d}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

are in one-to-one correspondence with the functions that are smooth on the entire unit-sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}_{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
,
it is obvious that

S^{d}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\subset\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,0}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

and that the angular symbol of
a\in S^{d}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

is just its value in
(0,1)
, i.e.,
a_{\langle 0\rangle}(z)\equiv a(0,1)

-classes extend by continuity to linear and bounded maps

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{s-d}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}},\;1<p<+\infty,

where the scale of Shubin-type Sobolev spaces is defined by

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\mid\langle x,D\rangle^{s}u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\}

with the canonical norm. Note that
s
measures simultaneously the smoothness and the decay/growth at infinity of a distribution.
Note that
\cap_{s}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions.

If
a\in\Gamma^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
,
d\geq 0
, is elliptic
then, by elliptic regularity, the unbounded operator

A=a(x,D):\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

has a unique closed extension given by the action of
a(x,D)
on the domain
\mathcal{H}^{s+d}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

For later purpose let us recall that the embedding
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is of trace class
provided
s-r>2n
. In particular, the following result is known to be true:

Proposition 4.3
.

If
a\in\Gamma^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
with
d<-2n
, then
a(x,D)
induces a trace class operator
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Moreover,
\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,a(x,D)=\iint a(x,\xi)\,dxd\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi
.

4.1.
Composition and formal adjoint

On symbolic level, the composition of two
\psi
do
a_{0}(x,D)
and
a_{1}(x,D)
corresponds to the so-called

Leibniz product
of symbols,

(4.2)

\displaystyle(a_{1}\#a_{0})(x,\xi)=\iint e^{iy\eta}a_{1}(x,\xi+\eta)a_{0}(x+y,\xi)\,dyd\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\eta

(
integration in the sense of oscillatory integrals, cf. for example
[
12
]
)
. It is known that
the Leibniz product induces maps

\Gamma^{d_{1}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})\times\Gamma^{d_{0}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})\longrightarrow\Gamma^{d_{0}+d_{1}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}),\qquad d_{0},d_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}};

poly-homogeneity is preserved under composition.
The analogous statement holds for the parameter-dependent classes
\Gamma^{d}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
, where the composition,
respectively the Leibniz product, is taken pointwise for each
\mu
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The
(\mu
-wise
)
Leibniz-product induces maps

\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{1,0}^{d_{1},\nu_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\times\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{1,0}^{d_{0},\nu_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\longrightarrow\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{1,0}^{d_{0}+d_{1},\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}).

For arbitrary
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

(4.3)

a_{1}\#a_{0}\equiv\sum_{|\alpha|=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}a_{1})(D^{\alpha}_{x}a_{0})\quad\mod\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{1,0}^{d_{0}+d_{1}-2N,\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}-2N}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

Moreover,

(a_{1}\#a_{0})_{[d_{0}+d_{1},\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}]}^{\infty}=a_{1,[d_{1},\nu_{1}]}^{\infty}\#a_{0,[d_{0},\nu_{0}]}^{\infty}.

If both
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are poly-homogeneous then so is
a_{1}\#a_{0}
and

(a_{1}\#a_{0})^{(d_{0}+d_{1},\nu_{0}+\nu_{1})}=a_{1}^{(d_{1},\nu_{1})}a_{0}^{(d_{0},\nu_{0})}.

In other words, homogeneous principal symbol, principal limit symbol, and principal angular symbol are multiplicative under composition.

Proof.

The proof relies on results of
[
21

be an arbitrary
symbol and define

By
[

(in the special case of a symbol with constant coefficients),

is a so-called amplitude function on

with values in

\mathbf{\widetilde{S}}^{d,\nu}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}_{z}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
and

with values in

and

defines an amplitude function of

4.2.
Ellipticity, parametrices, and inverses

A symbol
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{d,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
is called elliptic, if

(E1)

the homogeneous principal symbol
a^{(d,\nu)}
is pointwise invertible on
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{2n}_{+}
,

(E2)

the limit operator
a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty}(x,D)
is invertible.

In (E2), invertibility refers to invertibility as a map
\mathcal{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s-\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for some
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Due to spectral invariance of the Shubin class, the invertibility is independent of the choice of
s
and is equivalent to the fact that the inverse of the limit-operator is a
\psi
do with symbol in
\Gamma^{-\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
. A further equivalent formulation of (E2) is to say that the principal limit symbol has an inverse with respect to the Leibniz product.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
a\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{d,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
. Then
a
is elliptic if and only if there exists a parametrix
b\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-d,-\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
, i.e.,
a\#b-1,b\#a-1\in\mathscr{S}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+};\Gamma^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}))
. In this case
a(x,D,\mu)
is invertible for sufficiently large values of
\mu
and the parametrix can be constructed in such a way that,
for some non-negative constant
R=R(a)
,

b(x,D,\mu)=a(x,D,\mu)^{-1}\qquad\forall\;\mu\geq R.

Proof.

By (E2), the limit operator is, in particular, elliptic, i..e., has invertible homogeneous principal symbol. The latter coincides with the principal angular symbol of
a
. Thus, as observed in Lemma
2.12
,
a^{(d,\nu)}
is invertible with inverse belonging to

\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-d,-\nu}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
. Therefore there exists a
b_{1}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-d,-\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

such that

(4.5)

1-a\#b_{1}=:r_{1}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{0-\infty,0-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}).

In fact,
\chi(x,\xi)(a^{(d,\nu)})^{-1}
yields a
b_{1}
with resulting
r_{1}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-1,-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
; the standard von Neumann series argument then allows to modify
b_{1}
in such a way that (
4.5
) ist true. The principal limit symbol
r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty}
is smoothing and thus is also
(a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty})^{-\#}\#r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty}
; here the superscript
-\#
means the inverse with respect to the Leibniz product. Hence

s_{1}(x,\xi,\mu):=((a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty})^{-\#}\#r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty})(x,\xi)\;[x,\xi,\mu]^{-d+\nu}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-d-\infty,-\nu-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})

and
s_{1}
has limit symbol
s_{1,[-d,-\nu]}^{\infty}=(a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty})^{-\#}\#r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty}
.
Setting
b_{2}:=b_{1}+s_{1}
it follows that
b_{2}
satisfies the analogue of (
4.5
) with
r_{2}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{0-\infty,0-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
and

r_{2,[0,0]}^{\infty}=(1-a\#b_{1})_{[0,0]}^{\infty}-a_{[d,\nu]}^{\infty}\#s_{1,[-d,-\nu]}^{\infty}=r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty}-r_{1,[0,0]}^{\infty}=0.

Thus
r_{2}\in\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-1-\infty,0-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
by Proposition
2.10
. Since

\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-1-\infty,0-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=S^{-1}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+};\Gamma^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})),

cf. (
2.3
), another application of the Neumann series argument allows to modify
b_{2}
in such a way that

r_{2}\in\cap_{N\geq 0}\;\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}^{-N-\infty,0-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})=\mathscr{S}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+};\Gamma^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})).

This yields that
1-r_{2}(x,D,\mu)
is invertible for large
\mu
with

(1-r_{2}(x,D,\mu))^{-1}=1+r_{2}(x,D,\mu)+r_{2}(x,D,\mu)(1-r_{2}(x,D,\mu))^{-1}r_{2}(x,D,\mu).

If
\chi(\mu)
is an 0-excision function vanishing on a sufficiently large neighborhood of
0
,

s_{2}:=-r_{2}-\chi r_{2}\#(1-r_{2})^{-\#}\#r_{2}\in\mathscr{S}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+};\Gamma^{-\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}))

and
(1-r_{2}(x,D,\mu))^{-1}=1-s_{2}(x,D,\mu)
for large
\mu
. It follows that
b:=b_{2}\#(1-s_{2})

is a parametrix which is a right-inverse for large
\mu
. In the same way we can construct a
parametrix which is a left-inverse. It follows that
b
is as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
p_{0}\in\Gamma^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
,
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfy condition
(\mathrm{E}^{\prime})
.
Let
q\in\Gamma^{\omega}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
let
N
be a positive integer so large that
\omega-dN<-2n
. Then

\mathrm{Tr}\,\big(q(x,D)(\lambda-p_{0}(x,D))^{-N}\big)\sim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}c_{j}\lambda^{\frac{\omega+2n-j}{d}-N}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\big(c_{\ell}^{\prime}\log\lambda+c_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\big)\lambda^{-N-\ell}

for suitable coefficients depending on
p_{0}
and
q
.

Proof.

Let
b\in\mathbf{\Gamma}^{-d,d}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})\subset\widetilde{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{-d,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
be the parametrix from Theorem
5.5
.
Then
a:=q\#b^{\#N}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\omega-dN,\omega}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
and, due to Theorem
3.3

(
with
d
replaced by
\omega-dN
,
\nu
replaced by
\omega
, and
m=2n)
, we have

(5.4)

\mathrm{Tr}\,a(x,D,\mu)\sim_{\mu\to+\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}c_{j}\mu^{\omega-dN+2n-j}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\big(c_{\ell}^{\prime}\log\mu+c_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\big)\mu^{-dN-\ell}.

Since
a(x,D,\mu)=q(x,D)(\mu^{d}-p_{0}(x,D))^{-N}
, the result follows simply by substituting
\mu=\lambda^{1/d}
provided we can show that the coefficients
c_{\ell}^{\prime}
and
c_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
in (
5.4
) can be non-zero only if
\ell
is an integer multiple of
d
. Inspecting the proof of Theorem
3.3
, this will be the case if we can show that the symbols
r_{\{\omega-dN-J,\omega-J+\ell\}}
in (
3.5
) and the symbols
q_{j,\ell}
in (
3.6
) can be non-zero only if
\ell
is a multiple of
d
. In this regard, since
q(x,\xi)
does not depend on
\mu
, it is no restriction of generality to assume that
\omega=0
and
q=1
, i.e.,
a=b^{\#N}
is the
N
-fold Leibniz product of the parametrix of

p(x,\xi,\mu)=\mu^{d}-p_{0}(x,\xi)\in\widetilde{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{d,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+})
.

Obviously,
p^{\infty}_{\{d,0\}}=1
,
p^{\infty}_{\{d,d\}}=-p_{0}
, and
p^{\infty}_{\{d,j\}}=0
for all other
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since

\delta_{0\ell}=1_{\{0,\ell\}}=(p\#b)_{\{0,\ell\}}=\sum_{j+k=\ell}b^{\infty}_{\{-d,j\}}\#p^{\infty}_{\{d,k\}}\qquad\forall\;\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

cf. Remark
4.5
, it follows that
b^{\infty}_{\{-d,j\}}=0
whenever
j
is not a multiple of
d
(and
b^{\infty}_{\{-d,d\ell\}}=(-1)^{\ell}p_{0}^{\#\ell}
for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
). By induction, the same is then true for
a=b^{\#N}
.

Any homogeneous component
a^{(-Nd-j,-j)}(x,\xi,\mu)
is a linear combination of terms of the form
\gamma(x,\xi)(\mu^{d}-p_{0}^{(d)}(x,\xi))^{-M}
with
\gamma\in\Gamma^{(M-N)d-j}_{\mathrm{hom}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
and a positive integer
M
. In fact, for
N=1
, i.e.,
a=b
,
this follows from

\delta_{0\ell}=(b\#p)^{(-j,-j)}=\sum_{k+\ell+2|\alpha|=j}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}b^{(-d-k,-k)})(D^{\alpha}_{x}p^{(d-\ell,-\ell)})

which yields
b^{(-d,0)}=(\mu^{d}-p_{0}^{(d)})^{-1}
and the recursion formula

b^{(-d-j,-j)}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+\ell+2|\alpha|=j\\
k\leq j-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}b^{(-d-k,-k)})(D^{\alpha}_{x}p_{0}^{(d-\ell)})(\mu^{d}-p_{0}^{(d)})^{-1}

whenever
j\geq 1
. For general
N
proceed by induction, using the formula for the homogeneous components of
the composition
a=b\#b^{\#(N-1)}
.
Moreover,

(\mu^{d}-p_{0}^{(d)})^{-M}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}(p_{0}^{(d)})^{\ell}\mu^{-(M+\ell)d}+r_{ML}

for arbitrary
L
, where
r_{ML}
is homogeneous of degree
-Md
on
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\setminus\{0\})\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and

|r_{ML}(x,\xi,\mu)|\lesssim\mu^{-(M+L)d}|(x,\xi)|^{Ld}
. For
M=1
this follows from the (finite) geometric sum formula,
for general
M
by induction. This completes the proof.
∎

Anomalous right coronary artery stemming from left coronary sinus and malignant interarterial course in a middle-aged woman: a case report and review of diagnostic and surgical management
Authors: Arya Khezrpour, Mehran Abdollahi, Pouya Ebrahimi, Amin Zaki Zadeh, Nazanin Rafiei, Sara Montazeri Namin, Pedram Ramezani, Touba Akbari, Mohammad Hossein Mandegar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAn anomalous right coronary artery (RCA) arising from the left coronary sinus with an interarterial/intramural course can cause exertional ischemia and, rarely, sudden death. Functional testing and coronary CT angiography (CCTA) are key for diagnosis and risk stratification.Case presentationWe report the case of a 46-year-old woman presenting with exertional dyspnea and jaw discomfort. She had no conventional cardiovascular risk factors aside from hyperlipidemia and a family history of premature coronary disease. Exercise treadmill testing and myocardial perfusion imaging revealed inducible ischemia. Coronary computed tomography angiography demonstrated an anomalous RCA arising from the left coronary sinus with an interarterial and intramural course between the aorta and pulmonary artery. Surgical consultation confirmed a malignant anatomical variant, and the patient underwent successful coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) with complete symptom resolution.DiscussionThis case underscores the importance of considering congenital coronary anomalies in the differential diagnosis of exertional angina, especially in patients without atherosclerosis or major cardiovascular risk factors. Functional and anatomical imaging are essential for risk stratification. CABG remains an effective treatment for high-risk anomalies when direct reimplantation is not feasible.ConclusionIn symptomatic patients with demonstrable ischemia and high-risk anatomic features, surgery provides excellent outcomes. Introduction

Coronary artery anomalies (CAAs) are congenital variations in the origin, course, or structure of the coronary arteries, with a prevalence of 0.17% to 1.3% in angiographic studies. Among these, the anomalous origin of the right coronary artery (RCA) from the left coronary sinus with an interarterial course is a rare subtype, occurring in less than 0.1% of cases [1, 2]. This anomaly is classified as “malignant” due to its potential to cause myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death (SCD), particularly during exertion. The interarterial path, often combined with an intramural segment and slit-like ostium, leads to dynamic compression between the aorta and pulmonary artery during HF systole, restricting coronary flow and precipitating ischemia [3, 4].

The clinical presentation of CAAs varies widely. While many individuals remain asymptomatic, symptomatic cases may manifest as exertional angina, syncope, or SCD, often in younger patients without traditional cardiovascular risk factors [5]. Diagnosis is challenging, requiring a high index of suspicion and multimodal imaging, including exercise treadmill testing (ETT), myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI), and coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) [6, 7]. Management strategies range from conservative observation to surgical intervention—such as coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) or reimplantation—depending on symptoms and anatomical risk features [8].

This case report describes a 46-year-old woman with exertional angina and an anomalous RCA with an interarterial course, confirmed via coronary CTA and successfully managed with CABG. We provide a comprehensive review of the literature on the diagnosis and management of malignant RCA anomalies.

Case presentation

A 46-year-old woman reported two months of exertional dyspnea with jaw discomfort that resolved with rest. She had no hypertension, diabetes, or smoking history. Family history included premature coronary disease (brother with mild LAD stenosis at age 48). She had previously lost weight from 90 kg to 61 kg; BMI was 26 kg/m² at presentation.

Initial laboratory evaluation revealed hyperlipidemia, with total cholesterol 228 mg/dL (reference: 125–200) and LDL 172 mg/dL (reference: <100). Fasting glucose, renal function, and electrolytes were within normal limits. Echocardiography showed a structurally normal heart with preserved left ventricular systolic function (LVEF 60%), mild mitral valve prolapses, and no regional wall motion abnormalities or significant valvular disease. There was no evidence of pulmonary hypertension or pericardial effusion.

Exercise treadmill testing (ETT) using Bruce protocol provoked exertional symptoms with inferolateral ST-depression. Repeat ETT confirmed these findings. Myocardial perfusion imaging (Tc-99 m MIBI SPECT) revealed a moderate reversible perfusion defect in the inferoposterior wall without stress-induced LV dilatation, with a summed stress score (SSS) of 8 and a perfusion defect size of 10–15% (Fig. 1).

CCTA identified an RCA arising from the left coronary sinus with interarterial course between the aorta and pulmonary artery. The proximal intramural segment measured ~ 1.1 mm over 13 mm; distal RCA caliber was normal. LAD showed only mild proximal irregularity and the calcium score was zero. No definite obstructive LAD disease was seen on CCTA (Fig. 2). However, subsequent invasive coronary angiography (CAG) demonstrated a focal > 90% stenosis in the proximal LAD. These findings were confirmed in a surgical consultation and classified as a potentially malignant anomaly, given the patient’s exertional symptoms and evidence of ischemia.

The patient was admitted electively and underwent coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). Intraoperative findings included a severely narrowed and intramural proximal RCA with a slit-like orifice and an interarterial course. Surgical intervention included a left internal mammary artery (LIMA) graft to the LAD and a saphenous vein graft (SVG) to the RCA. Both grafts demonstrated good flow intraoperatively. The postoperative course was uneventful and the patient remained hemodynamically stable; ischemic symptoms resolved.

At the time of discharge, the recommendation was to continue secondary prevention with lipid-lowering therapy and lifestyle modifications. Given the RCA’s congenital anomaly and high-risk anatomy, a discussion was initiated regarding the potential long-term role of surgical reimplantation of the RCA. However, graft-based revascularization was considered satisfactory at this stage.

Discussion

Anomalous origin of the RCA from the left coronary sinus with an interarterial course is a rare but potentially life-threatening congenital coronary anomaly, accounting for less than 0.1% of coronary angiographic findings [9]. This variant is considered “malignant” due to its association with myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death (SCD), particularly during physical exertion [10]. With an interarterial course—especially intramural with a slit-like ostium—the vessel is squeezed between the aorta and pulmonary artery in systole. This dynamic compression limits flow and can cause ischemia [4].

Multimodality imaging plays a pivotal role in diagnosing and risk-stratifying coronary anomalies. ETT and stress MPI help unmask functional significance, while CCTA offers high-resolution anatomical delineation of anomalous vessels [11]. Although some cases may be managed conservatively, surgical revascularization is indicated in symptomatic patients with demonstrable ischemia or high-risk features [12, 13]. CABG remains the preferred approach when reimplantation is anatomically unfeasible or when the proximal segment is long and intramural [14].

This case illustrates the diagnostic challenges in differentiating non-atherosclerotic ischemia from conditions such as microvascular angina, particularly in patients with preserved ventricular function and minimal risk factors. Additionally, it underscores the critical need for early recognition and appropriate management of malignant coronary anomalies, which may manifest subtly [15].

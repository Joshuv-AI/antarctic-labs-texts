Characterizing the initial state of hydrodynamics in pp and pA collisions
G. Rabelo-Soares, G. Vujanovic, G. Torrieri

Abstract
The observation of seeming hydrodynamic-like behavior in proton-proton and proton-nucleus collisions presents us with the conceptual problem of how the initial state of such a hydrodynamic evolution should be characterized. This is an issue because, while nuclei can reasonably be approximated as “large” systems w.r.t. the characteristic Fermi momentum of their constituents, this is no longer true for nucleons. Hence, one would need to match a “quantum” theory, whose observables are described via highly non-commuting operators, to a “classical” hydrodynamics. Operationally assuming a “fast” thermalization, we survey what kind of object is best suited to such a matching condition. We show that it cannot be any of the objects usually associated with “the 3D structure of the nucleon” but rather a measure associated with entanglement entropy.



The apparent hydrodynamic behavior in small systems (proton-proton and proton-nucleus collisions)
Noronha et al. 2024
;
Nagle and Zajc 2018
presents theorists with a range of conceptual problems. The problem studied most often is how to reconcile an effective theory based on small gradients of continuous fields with systems of large gradients and comparatively few degrees of freedom. The gradient issue has been handled by proposing the concept of “hydrodynamic attractor”, where hydrodynamics is not associated with local thermalization
Florkowski et al. 2018
;
Chattopadhyay et al. 2022
, but arises from considering all, including transient, effective degrees of freedom within a kinetic model. The small number of degrees of freedom might need a definition of equilibrium incorporating large fluctuations
Sampaio et al. 2025
;
Torrieri 2024a
;
Torrieri 2021
;
Dore et al. 2022
.
It is fair to say that the precise relevance of these ideas and how they combine is still not agreed on.

In this work we shall explore a different, through as we will see related, conceptual problem: the characterization of initial conditions relevant for such small systems. Nuclei are unquestionably “large” systems, in the sense that their transverse size, as well as the impact parameter, is characteristically larger than any Fermi momentum scale associated with the system. Thus, initial conditions are based on classical, or at most semiclassical
Gelis et al. 2010
field configurations (the classicality in
Gelis et al. 2010
manifests through high occupancy number). However even if this is true for the nuclear “target”, it is certainly not true for the “projectile” proton in a
pA
collision, simply because the number of degrees of freedom associated with it is much lower than infinity (in both the Grad limit and the planar limit sense
Sampaio et al. 2025
) and its transverse size is determined entirely by quantum fluctuations. Matching a “quantum object” to a deterministic theory, or a stochastic theory based on classical
Markovian
probability such as fluctuating hydrodynamics
Sampaio et al. 2025
, presents a conceptual challenge that needs to be addressed, especially as it is linked to fundamental issues relating quantum mechanics to entropy
Carcassi et al. 2024
;
Cahill and Glauber 1969
;
Chakraborty and Sensarma 2021
;
Cerf et al. 2024
.

In this paper, we shall examine this issue in the light of the different interpretations of hydrodynamics. In particular, we shall use quantum information theory to describe high-energy interactions. With this approach, we hope to gain insight about the hadron structure in terms of their fundamental degrees of freedom (quarks and gluons) and the connection with the description of an initial state of hydrodynamics in small collision systems, such as
pA
. This becomes important because the entropy density deposition of such collisions is on the order of the nucleon size. In this matter, quantum fluctuations can no longer be neglected, and the problem reduces to understanding how a quantum system thermalizes (or better yet hydrodynamizes) into a classical system and how intrinsic quantum features, like spin, are imprints into this classical system.

From the hadronic theory side, the parton model describes a fast-moving nucleon that is a bound state of partons (quarks, antiquarks, gluons), i.e. a collection of point-like quasi-free particles, where its longitudinal momentum is described by parton densities. The collinear factorizations in QCD allows for the parton model to be a valid one, with ample phenomenology in hadron-hadron and lepton-hadron collisions providing credence (and quantitative support) to the validity of the parton model.

II
Hydrodynamic initial state

Seen through the underlying Wigner distribution, GPDs and TMDs are not simply Fourier transforms, but carry different information and are not directly recoverable one from the other
Diehl 2016
;
Burkardt and Pasquini 2016
. This, by itself, is perfectly reasonable, although understanding it properly in a relativistic strongly coupled context is a challenging technical problem still being clarified. Quantum mechanics is contextual
Guimaraes et al. 2024
: the state of a physical system being described by an operator-valued object such as a density matrix
\mathcal{\rho}
or a Wigner function (functional for quantum field theory). However, we measure not the state, but an observable
\hat{O}
. In observation, the state and the observable combine,

\left\langle O\right\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[O\times\rho\right],

(5)

in such a way that non-commuting observables are described by objects containing different information. GPDs and TMDs are an example of this
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005

Diehl 2016

Nagle and Zajc 2018
;
Noronha et al. 2024
represents a challenge.

Note that since hydrodynamics close to the ideal limit is based on statistical mechanics

;

;

\mathbf{0}\end{array}\right).

(6)

In

(7)

was enough, together with the hypothesis of general covariance under refoliations of

This, however, is a classical probability density, respectful of Bell’s and Mermin’s relations

5

In the paradigm of “hydrodynamization”
Florkowski et al. 2018
;
Chattopadhyay et al. 2022
, where hydrodynamics is not an indication of local thermalization, the predominant answer is TMD, since in this picture the dynamics is dominated by conservation laws, augmented by some attractor-dominated isotropization mechanism, and the energy-momentum density is carried by TMDs. If thermalization is genuine (in other words, microstates are localized and equally likely), then this is dubious because dynamics must be driven by the entropy current and not by the energy-momentum current, and the latter is a density of available microstates in spacetime. In fact, the apparent violation of the energy condition of the expectation value of the TMD-informed energy momentum tensor
Dumitru and Noronha 2025
confirms that fast thermalization is in principle incompatible with such an initial condition, unless this thermalization is also non-local. Similarly, the transverse momentum convolution, implicit in approaches such as
k_{T}
-factorization, also acts as a delocalization. It is however difficult to see how the initial mean free path can be smaller than this delocalization scale, which by its nature is of the order of the nucleon structure and anisotropy (unless we are close enough to the saturation limit that TMDs are effectively classical
Romatschke and Venugopalan 2006
).

Superficially, since entropy for an ultrarelativistic gas is proportional to the partonic density and GPDs are supposed to encode transverse partonic density, they are more of what one would be looking for as a hydrodynamic initial state.
However, it turns out that this is not the case, essentially because of their nature as a ground state (zero-temperature) structure on the light-cone. Because of this, both treating them as an entropy density specific to the target and as a Glauber-like thickness function leading to a density per “wounded parton” leads to mathematical contradictions. This point will qualitatively be discussed in the discussion section.

IV
Discussion

Hydrodynamics is a deterministic (or, in its fluctuating version, classically stochastic) theory whose dynamics is determined by the entropy current. Quantum mechanics and quantum field theory do not have such a description, because of the inherent contextually of quantum expectation values. Classical definitions of entropy in terms of probability fail in the quantum regime, for the quantum analogues of probabilities and phase space distributions are non positive definite. To get precise quantitative predictions for the hydrodynamic response in
pp
and
pA
collisions one must match the two pictures.

The quantitative prediction that motivated the authors to look into these issues was to explore how one could “see the nucleon spin classically”. As is well known and confirmed by the lattice, the transverse gluon density is anisotropic in the spin direction
Alexandrou et al. 2022
. Translating this into an initial eccentricity would give a difference of
v_{n}
between polarized and unpolarized nuclei that perhaps could explain some discrepancies with hydrodynamics seen in experimental data
Adare et al. 2018
;
Aidala et al. 2019

pA
on the target side. It could maybe be caused by the eccentricity of polarized protons visible on the lattice
Alexandrou et al. 2023
(but that is argued to be a synchronization effect in other works

In a attempt to estimate a hydrodynamic initial states from GPDs, two main approaches were taken. The first one uses the connection of the Melin moments
F_{n}(\mathbf{b}_{\perp})

is the space time rapidity. In a straightforward way,
the initial state could be constructed from the nucleon profile upon to a Fourier transform between the variables

The second approach relies on one assumption, the detailed balance equation

\xi=0

The discussions in the previous section explains physically why such attempts are doomed to fail: the GPD represents a projection of a pure state, which paradoxically appears “equilibrated“ because it is defined in the lightcone infinite momentum frame. The initial state of hydrodynamics must be maximally mixed at the scale where local equilibrium is a good approximation. The Husimi transform is a way to implement this mixing.

However, the previous section also makes clear that, given a Wigner function model of the quark and gluon distribution functions as defined in works such as

Note, however, that the direct correlations probed in

Such moments and basis are not currently available in the literature.

The full implications of this issue requires subsequent work and is not addressed in this paper.

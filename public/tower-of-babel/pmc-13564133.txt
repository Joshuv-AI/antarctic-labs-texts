Durable pain relief following basivertebral nerve ablation in patients with prior lumbar decompression: A retrospective case series
Authors: Emily Bellow, Derek Johnson, Sandi Bajrami, Jennifer Bae, William Caldwell
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBasivertebral nerve (BVN) ablation has emerged as a safe and effective treatment for vertebrogenic low back pain. However, patients with prior lumbar surgery were excluded from prior clinical trials, and BVN ablation outcomes in this patient population remain poorly characterized.ObjectiveTo evaluate clinical outcomes following BVN ablation in patients with a history of lumbar decompression surgery.MethodsThis retrospective case series included 14 adult patients who underwent BVN ablation at our institution from November 2019 to January 2025. No vertebral compression fractures were observed during a mean (±SD) follow-up period of 3.29 ± 1.10 years.ConclusionsIn this 14-patient retrospective case series, BVN ablation was associated with sustained and clinically meaningful pain reduction in patients with prior lumbar decompression surgery. Introduction

Chronic low back pain (CLBP) is a leading cause of disability and healthcare utilization worldwide, affecting an estimated 619 million people globally in 2020, with an estimated 843 million prevalent cases expected by 2050 [1]. A subtype of CLBP is vertebrogenic pain, which results from damage to the richly innervated vertebral endplates [[2], [3], [4]]. Vertebrogenic pain typically worsens with lumbar flexion-based movements and correlates with alterations in vertebral endplate and bone marrow signal intensity on MRI, imaging findings known as Modic Changes [5,6]. Basivertebral nerves have subsequently been identified as therapeutic targets for vertebrogenic CLBP, and basivertebral nerve (BVN) ablation has emerged as a safe and effective minimally invasive treatment for patients with chronic vertebrogenic pain and Modic Changes on MRI [7].

Several prior studies, including the INTRACEPT and SMART trials, have demonstrated the safety and effectiveness of BVN ablation. However, these trials explicitly excluded patients with radicular pain, spinal stenosis, or prior lumbar surgery [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12]]. Throughout the course of CLBP, patients may experience evolving contributions from multiple pain generators, including radicular and vertebrogenic sources, at different stages of their disease trajectory [13,14]. Despite this, there is limited literature specifically evaluating BVN ablation in patients with prior lumbar decompression surgery. Therefore, the goal of this retrospective case series was to evaluate clinical outcomes following BVN ablation in patients with persistent vertebrogenic low back pain after prior lumbar decompression surgery.

Methods

Ethics statement

This retrospective chart review study was approved by the Stony Brook University Hospital Committee on Research in Human Subjects via a waiver of consent (IRB2025-00033).

Study design and patient selection

This was a retrospective case series of adult patients (age ≥ 18) who underwent BVN ablation at our institution from November 2019 to January 2025. Patients were included if they had a history of prior lumbar decompressive surgery with at least one overlapping vertebral level between the surgical site and BVN ablation levels. All patients met standard institutional criteria for BVN ablation, including chronic vertebrogenic low back pain characterized by a predominantly axial pain presentation on history and physical examination, and Modic type 1 or type 2 endplate changes on MRI at all vertebral levels treated with BVN ablation. Additional diagnostic procedures, including medial branch diagnostic blocks, were not routinely performed prior to BVN ablation in this cohort. No specific minimum baseline VAS score was required for inclusion.

Lumbar decompression surgery was defined as prior lumbar laminectomy, laminotomy, and/or discectomy at one or more vertebral levels. Prior lumbar surgical history was determined through review of operative reports, procedure documentation, and relevant clinical notes within the EMR. No patients in this cohort had a history of lumbar fusion.

Patients were excluded if they were younger than 18 years, did not have documented prior lumbar decompression surgery, or did not undergo BVN ablation at levels overlapping with prior surgical levels.

BVN ablation procedure

All BVN ablation procedures were performed under general anesthesia using the Intracept® System (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer's standard protocol [15]. Ablations were performed at 85°C for 15 min, consistent with the protocol used in prior randomized controlled trials and institutional practice during the study period [8,12,15,16].

Although BVN ablation is FDA-approved for treatment of vertebral endplates from L3 to S1 [16], select patients in this cohort underwent treatment at additional levels based on concordant Modic changes, clinical presentation, anatomical considerations, and shared decision-making regarding the off-label nature of treatment. Although the current FDA indication specifies treatment of vertebral endplates from L3 to S1, it does not specify a maximum number of vertebral bodies that may be treated during a procedure [16]. The number of treated vertebral levels was determined on an individual patient basis according to the extent of vertebrogenic pain and concordant imaging findings.

Data collection

Patient demographics, baseline clinical characteristics, and outcome data were extracted from the electronic medical record (EMR). All follow-up data were obtained from routine clinical encounters documented in the EMR. Pain severity was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at baseline, at 4–6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months following BVN ablation.

Patient-reported functional improvement was assessed at 4–6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months post-procedure. Functional improvement was defined as any clinical documentation of increased activity levels, enhanced mobility, return to work, or general improvement in performing activities of daily living as recorded in the clinical notes. Post-procedure complications were monitored until the most recent available follow-up. The length of follow-up was recorded for each patient.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 26™ (IBM Corp.). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate. Categorical variables are presented as counts, percentages, and 95% confidence intervals, where appropriate.

Results

We identified 14 consecutive patients with a history of lumbar decompression surgery involving at least one vertebral level subsequently treated with BVN ablation during the study period. Follow-up data were available at 4–6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months for each patient in our cohort.

The mean age was 68.15 ± 16.99 years (range 36-83). The mean VAS at baseline was 7.07 ± 2.24, and the mean duration of low back pain was 12.58 ± 11.39 years. The median number of years since spine surgery was 10.50 years, ranging from 4 months to 44 years. Prior lumbar decompression procedures included laminectomy (N = 10), discectomy (N = 3), and combined laminectomy/discectomy (N = 1). The median number of vertebral levels treated with BVN ablation was 3.00.

Clinical outcome data at 4–6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months are presented in Table 3. Mean VAS decreased from 7.07 ± 2.24 at baseline to 4.43 ± 2.21 at 4–6 weeks, 3.71 ± 1.94 at 6 months, and 3.21 ± 1.89 at 12 months (Fig. 1). The mean decrease in VAS from baseline was 3.86 ± 2.51 at 12 months. Eight patients (57.1%; 95% CI: 28.9%-82.3%) achieved a ≥50% decrease in VAS from baseline at 12 months. 12 patients (85.7%; 95% CI: 57.2%-98.2%) achieved a ≥ 2-point VAS decrease from baseline at 12 months. 13 patients (92.9%; 95% CI: 66.1%-99.8%) reported functional improvement from baseline at 12 months post-procedure. One patient reported transient lower extremity muscle spasms at the 4–6 week follow-up visit, which spontaneously resolved by the next follow-up appointment. No vertebral compression fractures were observed during a mean follow-up period of 3.29 ± 1.10 years (range 1.50–5.33 years).

Discussion

In this retrospective case series of 14 patients with a history of lumbar decompression surgery, BVN ablation was associated with a mean VAS reduction of 3.86 ± 2.51 points at 12 months. This magnitude of improvement is within the range reported in prior randomized controlled trials of BVN ablation, including the INTRACEPT trial (mean VAS reduction 4.0 ± 2.6) and the SMART trial (2.76 ± 2.89) at 12 months [8,17]. 85.7% of patients in our cohort achieved a ≥ 2-Point VAS decrease from baseline, compared to 78.7% of patients in the INTRACEPT trial [17]. Our findings suggest that appropriately selected patients may still achieve clinically meaningful benefit from BVN ablation despite a history of lumbar decompression surgery.

Persistent or recurrent low back pain following lumbar decompression is a common phenomenon, affecting an estimated 10-40% of patients [13,18,19]. Recurrent disc herniation, adjacent segment pathology, facet or sacroiliac joint dysfunction, and epidural fibrosis are commonly recognized reasons for persistent pain after lumbar decompression [[20], [21], [22], [23]]. However, vertebrogenic pain may represent an underrecognized contributor to persistent symptoms in select patients. Vertebrogenic pain is associated with Modic Changes on MRI and typically presents as midline axial low back pain exacerbated by lumbar flexion-based movements [5,6]. Our findings suggest that patients with prior lumbar decompression and persistent axial low back pain should be evaluated for a potential vertebrogenic component, as appropriately selected patients may derive meaningful benefit from BVN ablation.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective analysis, our findings are inherently limited by the observational nature of the study design. The small sample size further limits the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, our study lacks a control group, limiting comparisons to patients with prior lumbar decompression who did not undergo BVN ablation. Medial branch diagnostic blocks were not routinely performed prior to BVN ablation; therefore, facet-mediated pain cannot be completely excluded as a contributing pain source in this cohort. Objective measures of functional status and overall well-being, such as the Oswestry Disability Index and Short Form Health Survey, were not routinely collected by office staff at our institution, limiting direct comparison to prior studies that employed these metrics. Larger, prospective studies are warranted to better characterize BVN ablation outcomes in patients with prior lumbar decompression surgery.

Conclusions

In this 14-patient retrospective case series, BVN ablation was associated with clinically meaningful pain reduction in patients with prior lumbar decompression surgery. Our findings suggest that evaluation for a vertebrogenic pain component should be considered in patients with persistent axial low back pain after lumbar decompression, as appropriately selected patients may still benefit from BVN ablation.

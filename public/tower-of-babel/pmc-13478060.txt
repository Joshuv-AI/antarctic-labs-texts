Forecasting of PM2.5 concentration based on variational mode decomposition and deep learning
Authors: Yun Cheng, Chao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate forecasting of PM2.5 concentration is a critical focus in air quality monitoring research. Considering the nonlinearity and non-stationarity of the PM2.5 time series, this paper proposes a forecasting model that combines variational mode decomposition (VMD), deep learning methods such as temporal convolutional networks (TCN), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM), and an attention mechanism. Initially, VMD is used to decompose the original PM2.5 sequence into a set of intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) that assess distinct physical interpretations. Sample entropy (SE) is calculated to evaluate the complexity of each IMF, followed by the application of K-means clustering to group the components into high-, medium-, and low-frequency components. A TCN-BiLSTM-based forecasting architecture is then established, where separate forecasting models are trained for each frequency band. Finally, an attention mechanism is introduced to adaptively learn the importance weights of different frequency component forecasting models, and a weighted fusion approach is applied to generate the final forecasting results. Experimental evaluations establish that the proposed method achieved the best forecasting accuracy with a lowest RMSE of 16.920 µg/m3, a lowest MAE of 11.134 µg/m3, and a highest R2 of 0.960, thereby demonstrating improved forecasting capability.

Introduction

Background

PM2.5 concentration changes have a significant effect on air quality, climatic conditions, and human health. But because of the effects of weather conditions, chemical reactions, and other air pollutants, the series is nonlinear and non-stationary, so it is very difficult to forecast accurately. Accurate forecasting of PM2.5 concentration levels would not only help the government develop strategies for air pollution but would also act as a warning signal for people to stay away from air pollutants. Consequently, constructing highly accurate forecasting models is essential to supporting environmental monitoring and safeguarding public health.

Literature review

Old linear forecasting methods like autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) do not work well with complex time series data. This is because PM2.5 concentrations change in nonlinear and unstable ways. Liu and Yuan1 developed an enhanced ARIMA model that effectively improves the forecasting precision of AQI. With the advancement of computing technology, machine learning methods such as support vector machines (SVM)2, random forests (RF)3, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost)4 have been increasingly employed in PM2.5 concentration forecasting. These methods can model complex relationships. However, they still have problems with capturing detailed time series patterns.

Lately, deep learning methods have become more popular. Different neural network models are now used for time series data forecasting. Deep learning models like long short-term memory (LSTM)5, recurrent neural networks (RNN)6, and temporal convolutional networks (TCN)7 have shown strong performance in air quality forecasting, methods for network parameterization8 and optimization9 are also proposed. Wu and Li10 used a bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) model. It added a reverse LSTM layer to the standard LSTM and gave better results than the regular LSTM. Additionally, quantum-inspired models like the improved QTCN11 have further enhanced forecasting accuracy in highly polluted regions. Deep learning models work better than traditional machine learning methods for forecasting air quality.

As research goes on, it is clear that using just one model is not enough to fully understand PM2.5 time series. To address problems related to excessive data fluctuations and the extraction of complex temporal features, scholars have explored hybrid models. The widespread adoption of hybrid models can be attributed to several key advantages. First, hybrid architectures leverage the complementary strengths of different algorithms. Second, these models demonstrate enhanced robustness when handling non-stationary and highly volatile environmental data12. Third, the integration of multiple components enables more comprehensive capture of both local patterns and long-range dependencies, substantially improving forecasting accuracy compared to single models13,14.

Ge et al.15 came up with a multi-scale spatiotemporal graph convolutional network (MST-GCN) for short-term PM2.5 concentration forecasting. Wang et al.16 introduced a hybrid forecasting system that combined feature extraction and deep learning. Zhou et al.17 introduced an evaluation framework that demonstrates the efficiency of hybrid deep learning models. Sun and Tian18 propose a forecasting model that combines SARIMA and TCN. Zhu and Xie19 develop a PM2.5 forecasting model using a CNN-BiLSTM model. Wei et al.20 built a CNN-LSTM model with a self-attention mechanism. This model did better in terms of both accuracy and stability. More recently, the spatiotemporal analysis using a hybrid time series model for PM2.5 forecasting has been proposed21. Furthermore, Ahmad and Kumar22 proposed a hybrid BiGRU-1DCNN model for spatio-temporal PM2.5 forecasting that captures both temporal dependencies and spatial correlations across multiple monitoring stations. Ahmad and Kumar23 also developed a novel random imputation-based RNN-BiGRU (nRI RNN-BiGRU) model that addresses the critical issue of missing data in air quality datasets while achieving superior forecasting performance. The WaveNet-based 1DCNN-BiLSTM-XGBoost24 residual correction model demonstrated superior performance in PM2.5 forecasting.

However, existing hybrid models still exhibit notable limitations. Many current approaches directly input raw PM2.5 sequences into deep learning models without adequate signal decomposition, making it difficult to effectively separate trend, seasonal, and noise components from the original data25. To deal with non-stationary time series data, researchers suggested a “decomposition-then-forecasting” method. Yu et al.26 integrated empirical mode decomposition (EMD) with neural network models to perform forecasting tasks. However, EMD has a problem called “mode mixing”, which can hurt forecasting accuracy. To fix this, Wang et al.27 proposed a complementary ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) as an improved solution. This method uses adaptive noise to make forecasting more stable and reduce errors. However, CEEMDAN does not consider the trend and cycle in non-stationary time series, leading to potential information loss during direct decomposition.

Variational mode decomposition (VMD) has proven effective in decomposing time series data characterized by nonlinearity and non-stationarity. It pulls out features from different frequency levels and gives better input features for PM2.5 forecasting28. Yang et al.29 introduced a PM2.5 concentration forecasting model based on VMD and integration. It has shown that data decomposition techniques with deep learning models can greatly improve the precision of PM2.5 concentration forecasting30. Recent studies have explored metaheuristic algorithms for VMD parameter optimization. Zeng et al.28 developed a WOA-VMD-BiLSTM hybrid model that optimizes VMD parameters using the whale optimization algorithm, demonstrating significant improvements in forecasting accuracy.

Furthermore, models relying on unidirectional temporal modeling (such as standard LSTM or TCN) fail to fully exploit contextual information within the historical input window31. A recent study32 developed a VMD-GAT-BiLSTM spatiotemporal hybrid model that integrates VMD decomposition with graph attention networks and BiLSTM for PM2.5 forecasting, demonstrating that bidirectional architectures can effectively capture both forward and backward temporal dependencies. Additionally, conventional models typically assign equal weights to all time steps during forecasting, failing to distinguish the varying importance of different historical observations, which may lead to overlooking critical pollution events33. A hybrid deep learning model34 integrating CNN, BiLSTM, and attention mechanisms has shown superior performance in air quality forecasting by dynamically focusing on the most informative time periods.

Research gap

Despite the progress achieved in recent studies, several limitations continue to exist. Firstly, most decomposed models are based on empirically defined VMD parameters, which are often prone to suboptimal decomposition on different datasets and regional sites. Furthermore, individual models are trained separately for each mode, without considering the natural correlations between modes of different frequency scales. Second, most hybrid models are built upon unidirectional models that preserve temporal dependencies along a particular direction, which limits the representation capacity for bidirectional contextual information. The combination of bidirectional architectures and VMD-decomposed models has been less studied. Third, most traditional ensemble models equally combined the forecasted outputs across different aspects, without considering the important role of different frequency components in different pollution scenarios. The absence of attention techniques also hampers adequate identification of informative historical data as well as frequency-related aspects.

To address these difficulties, this paper presents an integrated VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention hybrid model to boost modeling accuracy. More generally, adaptive decomposition is addressed in this study by using entropy sampling and K-means clustering to dynamically determine component selection. The TCN-BiLSTM framework will be used in this paper to model temporal variations at multiple scales. To dynamically balance temporal and frequency information, this paper will use an attention-based fusion technique. To further promote model robustness, hyperparameter optimization with algorithms like gray wolf optimizer (GWO) and adaptive differential evolution with optional external archive (JADE) will be employed.

Contributions and innovations

For accurate concentration forecasting for PM2.5, a hybrid method is proposed for use in this paper. This hybrid method incorporates VMD, sample entropy, K-means clustering, deep learning techniques, and an attention mechanism.

(1) Multi-scale decomposition and feature extraction based on VMD.

The VMD is utilized for the decomposition of the PM2.5 concentration signal using the Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) with various frequency characteristics. The sample entropy method measures the complexity of the IMFs. The K-means clustering algorithm is employed for selecting the dominant components by filtering out the noise-sensitive parts.

(2) Integration of TCN-BiLSTM with an attention mechanism.

TCN captures long-term dependence, and BiLSTM improves feature extraction of the sequence. The attention mechanism learns to weigh different aspects to enhance forecasting performance.

(3) Hyperparameter optimization using GWO and JADE.

Gray wolf optimizer (GWO) and adaptive differential evolution with optional external archive (JADE) are used to optimize automatically the important parameters of VMD and TCN-BiLSTM to achieve better results with greater efficiency than traditional methods used previously for the same purpose.

The remaining parts of this paper are arranged in the following order. Section 2 describes the primary theories in the proposed VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention model. Section 3 presents the experimental results. Section 4 presents comparative and ablation analyses to validate the effectiveness of the proposed model, and Section 5 concludes the paper.

Methodology

Paper structure

This paper proposes a PM2.5 forecasting framework that integrates VMD, SE, K-means clustering, and TCN-BiLSTM-Attention. As illustrated in Fig. 1, VMD is used to decompose the PM2.5 time series, with SE utilized to quantify the complexity of each IMF. Then, K-means clustering is applied to classify the IMFs into high-, mid-, and low-frequency components, thereby extracting key features, reducing data redundancy, and improving computational efficiency. Next, these components, along with meteorological and pollution data, are fed into the TCN-BiLSTM forecasting model. A self-attention method is applied to adaptively learn the importance weights of different frequency component models, enabling weighted fusion that improves forecasting accuracy.

TCN

TCN utilizes causal dilated convolutions to efficiently handle sequential data and model long-term dependencies. The causal nature of the convolution ensures that the forecasting output at a given time step relies upon the current and previous observations, thus avoiding any potential leaks of information. The TCN model architecture using dilated causal convolutions is shown in Fig. 2. The architecture includes an input layer, multiple hidden layers with exponentially increasing dilation rates (d = 1, 2, and 4), and an output layer. The solid black arrows represent the direct connection, and the black dashed arrows represent the dilated connection. The effect of the dilation rate during the dilated causal convolution increases the receptive field exponentially with depth, and it maintains efficiency. BiLSTM

Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) extends the standard LSTM by processing sequences in both forward and backward directions, enabling comprehensive feature extraction from temporal context. The bidirectional structure captures dependencies from both past and future observations within the input window, which is particularly effective for time series with complex temporal patterns. The BiLSTM structure is shown in Fig. 3. In this study, BiLSTM is combined with TCN to leverage both the efficient long-range dependency modeling of TCN and the bidirectional context extraction capability of BiLSTM.

GWO

GWO38 is a meta-heuristic method. It is based on how gray wolves live in groups and hunt. It has become popular in recent years because it is simple, flexible, and works well for many optimization problems. Compared to other methods, GWO has some strong points. It has fewer settings to adjust, is easy to use, and finds answers faster. These features make GWO a good choice for many kinds of problems, like feature selection, parameter estimation, and multi-objective optimization39.

In GWO, the steps follow the way gray wolves lead and hunt. The wolf group is split into four ranks: alpha, beta, delta, and omega. The alpha, beta, and delta wolves guide the search. The omega wolves follow them. The method keeps a group of possible answers (wolves). These answers are changed step by step to move closer to the best solution. Experimental study and analysis

Data retrieval and preprocessing

This paper utilized hourly air quality monitoring data from the Tiantan station in Beijing, spanning from March 2013 to February 2017 (https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/501/beijing+multi+site+air+quality+data). The dataset comprises a total of 35,064 records, including pollutant concentrations such as PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NO₂, CO, and O₃, and meteorological variables such as temperature (TEMP), atmospheric pressure (PRES), dew point temperature (DEWP), precipitation (RAIN), wind direction (WD), and wind speed (WSPM).

To verify the generalizability and cross-site robustness of the proposed VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention model, this study additionally selected data from the Changping station, another monitoring site within the same dataset, to serve as the generalization test set. The data from the Changping station covers the same time frame and includes the same 13 multivariate features as the Tiantan station. The Tiantan station is located in the traffic-intensive area of Beijing’s central city, where pollution characteristics are primarily influenced by urban activities and vehicle emissions. On the contrary, the Changping station is situated within the suburbs, where the characteristics of air pollutants are simultaneously affected by regional transport and the suburban environment. It is appropriate to evaluate the model performance at the Changping station, which exhibits distinct pollutant characteristics. The descriptive statistics of the data from both stations are summarized in Table 1.

To enhance data quality and model stability, several preprocessing procedures were conducted as follows: (1) Time Index Conversion: The variables representing year, month, day, and hour were converted into a unified date-time index suitable for time series analysis. (2) Missing Value Handling: Missing values in continuous variables were filled using linear interpolation, while mean imputation was applied where interpolation was not feasible. (3) Categorical Variable Encoding: Categorical variables, such as wind direction (WD), were encoded numerically using the LabelEncoder method. (4) Feature Engineering: Temporal features, including hour, day, month, and day of the week, were extracted from the date-time index, and a weekend indicator was added. (5) Data Normalization: All features and target variables were scaled to the [0, 1] range using the Min–Max normalization method. (6) Train–Test Split: The dataset was divided chronologically, with 80% allocated for training and 20% for testing, ensuring temporal consistency in the data sequence.

Additionally, a single-step forecasting approach was adopted, where a 24-hour historical input window was used to forecast PM2.5 concentration one hour ahead. To prevent data leakage, the VMD decomposition was conducted separately for the training and testing sets after the temporal split. Determining the parameters of the model

This paper employs the GWO for VMD parameter tuning using sample entropy variance as the criterion, while the JADE algorithm optimizes neural network hyperparameters using mean square error (MSE).

Determination of VMD parameters

The search space for the parameter was set with a restriction on the bandwidth value (α) ranging from 1000 to 5000, the penalty factor value (τ) ranging from 0 to 1, and the number of decomposition modes (K) ranging from 3 to 7. The Grey Wolf Optimizer algorithm tries to mimic the cooperative hunting trait possessed by grey wolves for the optimization of parameters. In this case, the search space for the parameters was set with a population size of ten wolves and a maximum number of thirty iterations. The objective function was formulated with respect to the distribution of the values of the sample entropy calculated from the Intrinsic Mode Function decomposition results to ensure a decomposition with a smaller standard deviation of the calculated entropies for a well-balanced decomposition. This approach demonstrated excellent parameter robustness with respect to convergence with an average of 5–7 iterations. Experimental results and analysis

Each frequency component (high-, mid-, and low-frequency) is independently forecast using the TCN-BiLSTM model, and the corresponding forecasts are subsequently integrated through a single-head attention–based fusion mechanism. This mechanism adaptively assigns weights to each component, and the final PM2.5 forecast is obtained via weighted aggregation.

Figure 7 compares the predicted and observed PM2.5 concentrations at the Tiantan and Changping stations. Figure 7(a) and 7(b) show the time series comparison and correlation analysis between predicted and measured values for both locations. For Tiantan station in Fig. 7(a), modeled PM2.5 concentrations closely followed measured concentrations over time, which indicates a strong fit. The scatter plot for this location shows that points are concentrated around the fitting line, and there is a high R² of 0.960. However, there is a slight underestimate of high concentrations above 500 µg/m³.

For the Changping station (Fig. 7(b)), the R² value obtained is slightly lower but equally strong with a value of 0.940. Contrary to Tiantan, the scatter points are more dispersed, especially when the concentrations are high, and the lower slope of the fitted line again signifies the clear underestimation when the pollution level is high. Taking all these findings into account, it is clear from the outcomes of the proposed model that it provides accurate and trustworthy PM2.5 forecasts for both stations.

Comparative and ablation experiments

Comparative experiments

As shown in Table 3; Fig. 8, the proposed VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention model consistently outperforms all baseline methods at both monitoring stations. At the Tiantan station, it achieves an RMSE of 16.920 µg/m3, MAE of 11.134 µg/m3, and an R2 of 0.960, while at the Changping station, it obtains an RMSE of 17.728 µg/m3, MAE of 10.126 µg/m3, and an R2 of 0.940, indicating superior forecasting accuracy and robustness.

The results indicate that signal decomposition alone does not necessarily guarantee performance improvement, as single-architecture models after decomposition (VMD-TCN, VMD-BiLSTM, CEEMDAN-TCN, and CEEMDAN-BiLSTM) do not consistently outperform the non-decomposed TCN-BiLSTM model. However, when decomposition is combined with a hybrid temporal modeling structure, its advantages become evident. Specifically, both VMD-TCN-BiLSTM and CEEMDAN-TCN-BiLSTM substantially outperform the TCN-BiLSTM model without decomposition, demonstrating that decomposition is effective when paired with an appropriate model architecture, TCN can successfully capture local patterns through dilated convolutions, and the addition of the BiLSTM component helps capture the bidirectional patterns. The results also suggest that VMD and CEEMDAN exhibit similar efficacy in improving forecasting accuracy within this hybrid framework.

The addition of the attention-based fusion mechanism further boosts the forecasting performance. In comparison with the VMD-TCN-BiLSTM model, the proposed VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention model increases the R2 value from 0.948 to 0.960 for the Tiantan station and from 0.927 to 0.940 for the Changping station. This result indicates the successful use of the attention mechanism to impose adaptive weights on the predictions from different IMF clusters. In general, the proposed approach features a strong ability to generalize to different stations with distinct characteristics of PM2.5.

Ablation experiments

Table 4; Fig. 9 show the ablation study results, which analyze the effect of individual components of the proposed model. The results suggest that all components have a positive effect on the predictive capability of the proposed model.

Taking away the attention mechanism (VMD-TCN-BiLSTM) results in a slight degradation in performance, with the R2 value decreasing from 0.960 to 0.948 at the Tiantan station and from 0.940 to 0.927 at the Changping station. This indicates that the attention mechanism brings a steady but secondary benefit through adaptively integrating the results from different clusters of the IMFs.

Removing the BiLSTM layer entirely (VMD-TCN-Attention) leads to a more dramatic decline, signifying the relevancy of bidirectionality in modeling the temporal behavior to forecast PM2.5. The performance hit due to the elimination of the TCN component (VMD-BiLSTM-Attention) is the greatest out of all the modules in the network, emphasizing its primary importance in representing local temporal dynamics using dilated causal convolution.

Eliminating the VMD component results in a decline in model performance, with the R2 value decreasing from 0.960 to 0.904 at the Tiantan site and from 0.940 to 0.854 at the Changping site. Furthermore, the VMD-BiLSTM-Attention configuration (excluding TCN) exhibits inferior performance (R2 = 0.882) compared with the TCN-BiLSTM model without VMD (R2 = 0.904) at the Tiantan station. These findings suggest that the multi-scale decomposition provided by VMD relies on the feature extraction capability of TCN to achieve optimal forecasting accuracy.

Overall, the ablation results demonstrate that the performance gains of the proposed model arise from the complementary integration of VMD decomposition, TCN-based local feature extraction, BiLSTM-based long-term dependency modeling, and attention-based fusion, with VMD and TCN contributing the most substantial improvements.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a novel mixed model, VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention, to address the challenges of high nonlinearity and non-stationarity inherent in PM2.5 forecasting data. The model first employs Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) to refine Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) settings, decomposing the PM2.5 data into physically meaningful Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs). These IMFs are intelligently grouped using sample entropy and K-means clustering and subsequently fed into a TCN-BiLSTM structure. A single-head attention mechanism is integrated to optimally weight and fuse the predictions from different groups, significantly enhancing overall accuracy.

A set of comprehensive comparative experiments was carried out using real-world data from two stations in Beijing. The effectiveness of the proposed VMD-TCN-BiLSTM-Attention model proved to be better than different state-of-the-art models. The proposed system produced the best forecasting performance with the lowest RMSE of 16.920 µg/m3, the lowest MAE of 11.134 µg/m3, and the highest R2 score of 0.960. Ablation testing also proved that it is essential to comprehensively employ multi-scale decomposition, time-based deep learning, and the attention mechanism in time-series forecasting, particularly in capturing PM2.5 concentration dynamics. Concluding, this proposed system contributes towards making more informed decisions in environmental policy-making.

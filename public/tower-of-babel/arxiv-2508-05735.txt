Universal sectors of two-dimensional Carrollian CFTs Preprint: TUW–24–01
Ankit Aggarwal, Arjun Bagchi, Stephane Detournay, Daniel Grumiller, Max Riegler, and Joan Simón

Abstract
We revisit modular invariance in two-dimensional Carrollian conformal field theories from a geometric perspective. Focusing on the characters of the induced and highest-weight representations of the theory, we show that there are regions of parameter space where the vacuum character dominates in the dual channel. We use this property to zoom into different subsectors of the Carrollian theory. One of them is reminiscent of the Schwarzian sector of a relativistic CFT 2 and has a flat space holographic interpretation as O O -plane orbifold. It exists only for the highest-weight representation. We prove that for all sectors with vacuum dominance in the dual channel, the specific heat is negative, concurrent with the holographic interpretation of the negative specific heat of asymptotically flat spacetimes with horizons.

2.1
Carroll symmetries and conformal Carroll symmetries

Carroll symmetry arises geometrically on a
d
-dimensional Carroll manifold which is defined by the pair (
\tau^{\mu},h_{\mu\nu}
) where
h_{\mu\nu}
is a degenerate symmetric tensor and
\tau^{\mu}
is a vector that generates the 1-dimensional kernel of
h_{\mu\nu}
, see e.g.
Bergshoeff:2022eog
. The Carroll algebra is generated by the isometries of this (weak) Carroll structure,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta}\tau^{\mu}={\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta}h_{\mu\nu}=0
. When restricting to flat Carrollian spacetimes,
\tau=\partial_{t}
and
h_{\mu\nu}\,\extdm\!x^{\mu}\extdm\!x^{\nu}=\extdm\!x_{1}^{2}+\dots+\extdm\!x_{d-1}^{2}
, the conformal isometry of this structure

{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta}\tau^{\mu}=-\lambda\,\tau^{\mu}\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta}h_{\mu\nu}=2\lambda\,h_{\mu\nu}

(5)

generates the
d
-dimensional conformal Carroll algebra,
\mathfrak{ccar}_{d}
, which is isomorphic to the
(d+1)
-dimensional Bondi–van der Burgh–Metzner–Sachs (BMS) algebra,
\mathfrak{bms}_{d+1}
. This isomorphism is the basis of the Carrollian approach to flat space holography

. From now on we focus on
d=2
, so that the algebra of relevance is
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
or, equivalently,
\mathfrak{ccar}_{2}
, and we have only one spatial coordinate,
x_{1}=x
.

The conformal Carroll Killing equations (
5
) are solved by the conformal Carroll vector fields

\zeta\big({\cal M},\,{\cal L}\big)=\big({\cal M}(x)+t\,\partial_{x}{\cal L}(x)\big)\,\partial_{t}+{\cal L}(x)\,\partial_{x}

(6)

that depend on two arbitrary functions,
\cal M
and
\cal L
, of the spatial coordinate
x
. Their Lie-bracket algebra

\big[\zeta\big({\cal M}_{1},\,{\cal L}_{1}\big),\,\zeta\big({\cal M}_{2},\,{\cal L}_{2}\big)\big]_{\textrm{\tiny Lie}}=\zeta\big({\cal M}_{1\circ 2},\,{\cal L}_{1\circ 2}\big)

(7)

with
{\cal M}_{1\circ 2}={\cal M}_{1}\partial_{x}{\cal L}_{2}-{\cal M}_{2}\partial_{x}{\cal L}_{1}+{\cal L}_{1}\partial_{x}{\cal M}_{2}-{\cal L}_{2}\partial_{x}{\cal M}_{1}
and
{\cal L}_{1\circ 2}={\cal L}_{1}\partial_{x}{\cal L}_{2}-{\cal L}_{2}\partial_{x}{\cal L}_{1}
, consists of a semi-direct sum of the Witt algebra (generated by
\cal L
) with an abelian algebra (generated by
\cal M
). Expanding in modes [for instance, Fourier modes
M_{n}=\zeta(e^{inx},0)
,
L_{n}=\zeta(0,e^{inx})
] and allowing for central extensions yields the centrally extended
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
(or, equivalently, centrally extended
\mathfrak{ccar}_{2}
) commutator algebra:

\displaystyle\phantom{\bigg(}[L_{n},\,L_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\,L_{n+m}+\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}}{12}\,\big(n^{3}-n\big)\,\delta_{n+m,\,0}

(8a)

\displaystyle[L_{n},\,M_{m}]

\displaystyle=(n-m)\,M_{n+m}+\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}}{12}\,\big(n^{3}-n\big)\,\delta_{n+m,\,0}\phantom{\bigg)}

(8b)

\displaystyle\phantom{\bigg)}[M_{n},\,M_{m}]

\displaystyle=0

(8c)

In a gravity context, the algebra (
8
) is the asymptotic symmetry algebra of 3d asymptotically flat spacetimes with Barnich–Compère boundary conditions

. In this case,
x=\phi
parametrizes the celestial
S^{1}
and

(

) is the retarded (advanced) null direction along
\mathscr{I}^{+}
(
\mathscr{I}^{-}
). The
L_{n}
generate the diffeomorphisms of the celestial circle and are called superrotations. The
M_{n}
are angle-dependent translations of the null direction and are called supertranslations. For Einstein gravity, the Virasoro central charge turns out to vanish,
c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=0
, while the mixed central charge is non-zero,
c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}\neq 0
. The global subalgebra insensitive to the central terms is
\mathfrak{isl}(2,\mathbb{R})
generated by
L_{\pm 1},L_{0},M_{\pm 1},M_{0}
. In a holographic context, these six generators correspond to the Killing vectors of 3d Minkowski spacetime, the geometric dual to the CCFT
2
vacuum on the cylinder.

Since they are the prime candidates for the holographic dual of asymptotically flat gravity in 3d, we are interested in 2d quantum field theories that exhibit the symmetry (
8
), i.e., CCFT
2
. Now we go on to describe such field theories.

2.2
Carroll partition functions

Given a CCFT
2
governed by the symmetries (
8
), it is useful to employ a vector field representation of these symmetries, like the one given in (
6
), which in terms of the Fourier modes introduced above yields

L_{n}=ie^{in\phi}(\partial_{\phi}+int\partial_{t})\qquad\qquad M_{n}=ie^{in\phi}\partial_{t}\,.

These are the generators on the null cylinder
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
with
t
playing the role of retarded/advanced time. The zero-mode vector fields

L_{0}=i\partial_{\phi}=J\qquad\qquad M_{0}=i\partial_{t}=H

generate angular rotations and (null) time-translations of the dual field theory, respectively. Therefore,
M_{0}
is to be identified with the Hamiltonian, while
L_{0}
with the angular momentum generator.

We define the partition function of the CCFT
2
as

Z_{\mathfrak{ccar}}(\rho,\sigma)=\text{Tr}\,e^{2\pi i(\rho M_{0}+\sigma L_{0})}=\text{Tr}\,e^{-\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}H+i\theta_{\mathfrak{car}}J}\,.

(9)

In the last equality, we introduced the inverse of the Carroll temperature
\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}
and the angular potential
\theta_{\mathfrak{car}}
. This also gives us the mapping between chemical potentials

\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}=-2\pi i\rho\qquad\qquad\theta_{\mathfrak{car}}=2\pi\sigma\,.

(10)

2.3
Carroll limit from 2d CFT

A CCFT
2
can be obtained by sending the speed of light to zero in a 2d relativistic CFT. Here, we recall how this works. We start with the generators

{\mathcal{L}}_{n}=ie^{in\omega}\partial_{\omega}\qquad\qquad{\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}}_{n}=ie^{in\bar{\omega}}\partial_{\bar{\omega}}\qquad\qquad\omega,\,\bar{\omega}=t\pm\phi

(11)

of a CFT
2
on a cylinder, which generate two copies of the Witt algebra. In the Euclidean version of the theory, we additionally compactify time, so that our CFT
2
is defined on a torus.

For the Carroll limit, we send the speed of light to zero, implying the scaling

t\to\epsilon\,t\qquad\qquad\phi\to\phi\qquad\qquad\epsilon\to 0\,.

(12)

In this limit, we need to scale the linear combination of generators

L_{n}={\mathcal{L}}_{n}-{\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}}_{-n}\qquad\qquad M_{n}=\epsilon({\mathcal{L}}_{n}+{\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}}_{-n})

(13)

to keep them finite. Starting with the expressions (
11
), the above equation (
13
) results in (
2.2
). The original time direction is thus infinitely boosted to a null time. Replacing the Witt generators (
11
) by Virasoro generators and defining the BMS generators through (
13
) recovers the BMS algebra (
8
) with central charges

c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=c-\bar{c}\qquad\qquad c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\,\big(c+\bar{c}\big)

(14)

where
c
and
\bar{c}
are the original Virasoro central charges. Thus, if the original CFT
2
had no gravitational anomaly,
c=\bar{c}
, the CCFT
2
emerging from its contraction has no Virasoro central charge,
c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=0
. This explains why in the context of Einstein gravity we typically only care about the case
c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=0
. Another noteworthy aspect is that the mixed central charge
c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}
is non-zero and finite only if the original Virasoro central charges diverge linearly in
1/\epsilon
. For Einstein gravity, this is indeed the case since the contraction parameter is the inverse AdS radius
Barnich:2006av
, and the Brown–Henneaux central charges diverge linearly as the AdS radius tends to infinity
Brown:1986nw
.

We now perform the same scaling at the level of Euclidean partition functions. The CFT partition function

Z_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}=\text{Tr}\,e^{2\pi i(\tau{\mathcal{L}}_{0}-\bar{\tau}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0})}=\text{Tr}\,e^{-\beta_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}H_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}+i\theta_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}J_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}}

(15)

entails the identifications

\tau=\frac{\theta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}}{2\pi}+i\,\frac{\beta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}}{2\pi}\qquad\qquad\bar{\tau}=\frac{\theta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}}{2\pi}-i\,\frac{\beta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}}{2\pi}\,.

(16)

While for real
\beta_{\text{\tiny CFT}},\theta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}
the quantities
\tau,\bar{\tau}
are complex conjugates of each other, this is no longer true if we complexify the chemical potentials, which is what we are going to do in later Sections.

We demand that the relativistic CFT partition function smoothly interpolates to the CCFT partition function as we send the speed of light to zero,

Z_{\text{\tiny CFT}}\longrightarrow Z_{\mathfrak{ccar}}\quad\text{as}\quad\epsilon\to 0

(17)

implying

Z_{\text{\tiny CFT}}=\text{Tr}\,e^{2\pi i(\tau{\mathcal{L}}_{0}-\bar{\tau}\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{0})}\to\text{Tr}\,e^{\pi i[\tau(L_{0}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}M_{0})-\bar{\tau}(-L_{0}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}M_{0})]}=\text{Tr}\,e^{2\pi i(\sigma L_{0}+\rho M_{0})}=Z_{\mathfrak{ccar}}\,.

(18)

Thus, the modular parameters
\tau
,
\bar{\tau}
decompose into an order unity and an infinitesimal part,

2\sigma=\tau+\bar{\tau}\qquad\qquad 2\rho=\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\tau-\bar{\tau})\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\tau,\bar{\tau}=\sigma\pm\epsilon\,\rho\,.

(19)

Matching the angular potential and the inverse temperatures between relativistic and CCFTs yields

\theta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}=\theta_{\mathfrak{car}}=2\pi\sigma\qquad\qquad\beta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}=\epsilon\,\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}=-2\pi i\,\epsilon\,\rho\,.

(20)

Since
\theta_{\text{\tiny CFT}}=i\beta_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}\Omega_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}
, we infer the Carrollian angular velocity is related to the CFT
2
angular velocity as

\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}=\epsilon\,\Omega_{\textrm{\tiny CFT}}\,.

(21)

The formulas above allow different interpretations, depending on the specific application. If one keeps fixed the CFT temperature and angular velocity, the corresponding Carrollian quantities vanish. This can be understood as the signature Carrollian property that “nothing can move (within the Carrollian lightcone)” since the speed of light tends to zero. This interpretation is not particularly useful for CCFT
2
applications. Alternatively, if one keeps fixed the Carrollian temperature and angular velocity, the corresponding relativistic quantities tend to infinity. So, Carrollian rotation corresponds to faster-than-light movement, which is possible in a Carrollian context without necessarily violating energy bounds
Ecker:2024czx
. In our work, we adopt the viewpoint that the Carrollian quantities are finite by default, though, as we shall see, the condition of vacuum dominance in the dual channel will drive us towards large or small values for at least one of the chemical potentials. Before we can discuss this, we need to introduce Carroll modular transformations, since they will provide the main tool to study vacuum dominance.

2.4
Carroll modular transformations

Starting with the CFT
2
modular transformations PSL
(2,\mathbb{Z})
,

\tau\to\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\qquad\qquad ad-bc=1\quad\textrm{with}\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}

(22)

and exploiting the relation (

\tau=\sigma+\epsilon\,\rho\to\frac{a\sigma+b}{c\sigma+d}+\epsilon\,\rho\,\frac{ad-bc}{(c\sigma+d)^{2}}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(23)

Keeping only terms up to linear order in
\epsilon
establishes the Carroll modular transformation

\boxed{\phantom{\bigg(}\sigma\to\frac{a\sigma+b}{c\sigma+d}\qquad\qquad\rho\to\frac{\rho}{(c\sigma+d)^{2}}\,.\phantom{\bigg)}}

(24)

So we still have PSL
(2,\mathbb{Z})
and a modular parameter
\sigma
but the second parameter,
\rho
, transforms differently. Below, we explore the meaning of this transformation behavior.

The geometric picture associated with the small
\epsilon
-limit is as follows. Assuming Im
(\tau)>0
, the original modular transformation acts on the complex upper half-plane

. The Carroll limit zooms into the region slightly above the real axis, so that to leading order (LO) we see only the (standard) modular transformation of
\sigma
, while the first subleading term (given by
\rho
) transforms like an element in the tangent space.

A formal way to arrive at the same results as in the limiting construction and to explicitly implement the geometric picture advocated above is to extend the action of any modular transformation
g\in\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
at any given point
z\in\mathcal{H}
to the tangent space of the base manifold
\text{T}_{z}\mathcal{H}
. One can then define the derivative action of
g
,
Dg:\text{T}_{z}\mathcal{H}\to\text{T}_{g(z)}\mathcal{H}
as

Dg(z,v)=(g(z),g^{\prime}(z)v)\quad\text{where}\quad g^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2}}

(25)

with
v\in\text{T}_{z}\mathcal{H}
. This is the same transformation behavior as in (
24
). Hence, a sensible way to think about Carroll modular transformations is in terms of a simultaneous action of modular transformations on a given point of the base manifold and the resulting derivative action on the tangent space. So, similarly to a general CFT, it can be useful to think about CCFTs at finite temperature to be defined on the complex upper half-plane and the corresponding tangent space with some additional reality/imaginary conditions on the Carroll modular parameters
\sigma
and
\rho
(analogous to
\bar{z}=z^{*}
in the Euclidean CFT case).

The Carroll modular group is generated by composing
S
- and
T
-transformations given by

S:\;\sigma\to-\frac{1}{\sigma}\qquad\rho\to\frac{\rho}{\sigma^{2}}\qquad\qquad T:\;\sigma\to\sigma+1\qquad\rho\to\rho\,.

(26)

The
S
-transformation acts on both modular parameters, while the
T
-transformation only acts on
\sigma
. In terms of the physical chemical potentials in (
20
) and (
21
), the
S
-transformation acts as

S:\;\frac{(\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}})^{\prime}}{2\pi}=\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}}\qquad\qquad\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}^{\prime}=-\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}\,.

(27)

The
T
-transformation leaves
\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}
invariant while shifting the Carrollian angular velocity

T:\;\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}^{\prime}=\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}\qquad\qquad\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}^{\prime}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{car}}-i\,\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{\mathfrak{car}}}\,.

(28)

These modular transformations satisfy the usual identity relations

S^{2}=\mathds{1}\qquad\qquad(ST)^{3}=\mathds{1}\,.

(29)

The first identity is evident by inspection. The second identity follows from an explicit computation of consecutive actions of
ST
,

ST:\;\sigma\to-\frac{1}{\sigma+1}\qquad\rho\to\frac{\rho}{(\sigma+1)^{2}}\qquad\qquad(ST)^{2}:\;\sigma\to-\frac{\sigma+1}{\sigma}\qquad\rho\to\frac{\rho}{\sigma^{2}}\,.

(30)

Defining
\sigma_{1}=T^{-1}
and
\sigma_{2}=TST
, the generators
\sigma_{i}
obey the usual braiding relation
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}
.

These modular transformations, especially the
S
-transformation defined in (
26
),
were used extensively to derive a BMS–Cardy formula
Bagchi:2012xr
(see also
Barnich:2012xq
), an asymptotic formula for structure constants
Bagchi:2020rwb
, and two-point functions of probes in a thermal background
Bagchi:2023uqm
.

3
Carrollian partition functions as sums over Carrollian characters

In this Section, we summarize aspects of Carrollian partition functions and how to represent them in terms of Carrollian characters. We start with a review of states and representations — highest-weight and induced — in Subsection
3.1
and then present Carrollian characters and partition functions in Subsection
3.2
using both of these representations. Subsection
3.3
displays the expectation values of the Carrollian charges, their variances, and the covariance extracted from the Carrollian partition function in the grand canonical ensemble. Subsection
3.4
collects some properties of a key ingredient in the Carrollian partition functions, the Dedekind
\eta
-function, that will prove to be useful for later Sections. Subsection
3.5
derives the CCFT vacuum characters as limits from CFT vacuum characters; more specifically, we shall see that the standard CFT vacuum character yields the induced CCFT vacuum character and the so-called flipped vacuum character (defined in Subsection
3.1
) yields the highest-weight CCFT vacuum character.

3.1
States and representations

The states in a CCFT
2
are labelled with the eigenvalues of
L_{0}
and
M_{0}
:

L_{0}|\xi,\Delta\rangle=\Delta|\xi,\Delta\rangle\qquad\qquad M_{0}|\xi,\Delta\rangle=\xi|\xi,\Delta\rangle\,.

(31)

We can construct two very different types of representations from these states. The first are the representations familiar from CFT
2
, the highest-weight representations. Here we have primary states
|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{p}
that are annihilated by positive modes of the generators:

L_{n}|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{p}=M_{n}|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{p}=0\qquad\forall n>0

(32)

This means that the weight
\Delta
is bounded from below as the action of the positive modes is to lower the value of
\Delta
. Generic states in this representation are built by acting with negative modes of the generators
L,M
on these primary states:

|\Psi\rangle=L_{-n_{1}}L_{-n_{2}}\ldots L_{-n_{q}}M_{-m_{1}}M_{-m_{2}}\ldots M_{-m_{r}}|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{p}\qquad n_{i},m_{j}>0

(33)

The second type is the induced representation, where all the supertranslations (except for
M_{0}
) annihilate the states:

M_{n}|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{I}=0\qquad\forall n\neq 0\,.

(34)

Generic states in this representation are obtained by acting with arbitrary combinations of
L_{n}
generators (not necessarily
n>0
) on
|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{I}
:

|\Phi\rangle=L_{n_{1}}L_{n_{2}}\ldots L_{n_{m}}|\xi,\Delta\rangle_{I}\,.

(35)

These induced representations naturally arise from the highest-weight representation of a relativistic CFT
2
via the map discussed in (
13
). This is so because imposing the highest-weight conditions in the parent CFT
2
,

\mathcal{L}_{n}|\psi\rangle=0=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n}|\psi\rangle\qquad\forall n>0

(36)

upon using (
13
) induces the conditions

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{n}|\psi\rangle=\big(L_{n}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}M_{n}\big)|\psi\rangle=0\qquad\qquad\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\big(-L_{-n}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}M_{-n}\big)|\psi\rangle=0\qquad\qquad\forall n>0

\displaystyle\xRightarrow[\textrm{limit}]{\textrm{Carroll}}\quad M_{n}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=0\qquad\qquad\forall n\neq 0\,.

(37)

The CCFT central charges associated with the limiting procedure yielding induced representations are given by (
14
)
Campoleoni:2016vsh
.

By contrast, to get the highest-weight representations in the CCFT from a limit, we need to start with flipped representations in the CFT
2

Bagchi:2020fpr
,

\mathcal{L}_{n}|\psi\rangle=0=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{-n}|\psi\rangle\qquad\forall n>0\quad\xRightarrow[\textrm{limit}]{\textrm{Carroll}}\quad L_{n}|\Psi\rangle=0=M_{n}|\Psi\rangle\qquad\forall n>0\,.

(38)

If the state
|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle
is the vacuum state and obeys the left conditions (
38
) then we refer to it as the “flipped vacuum”. It is convenient to exploit the Virasoro algebra automorphism
Bagchi:2019unf

\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{n}\leftrightarrow-\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{-n}\qquad\qquad\bar{c}\leftrightarrow-\bar{c}

(39)

so that the flipped representation can be reinterpreted as highest-weight representation of the flipped Virasoro algebra. As a consequence of these sign flips, the CCFT central charges are given by

c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=c+\bar{c}\qquad\qquad c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\,(c-\bar{c})\,.

(40)

We elaborate on the flipped representations in Subsections
3.5
,
7.1
,
7.2
, Table
3
, and Appendix
B
.

3.2
Characters and partition functions

The two representations above have almost identical characters, up to small but significant differences involving the modulus

Notice that Carroll characters, (

3.3
Expectation values, variances, and covariance

Here, we collect a few simple formulas that will be useful in applications in the next Section, such as our discussion of vacuum dominance, or for thermodynamical observables, such as the specific heat.

From the grand canonical partition function (
9
) and the eigenvalues (
31
), the angular momentum and energy expectation values are determined by

\langle\Delta\rangle\equiv\langle L_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\partial_{\sigma}\ln{Z}_{\mathfrak{ccar}}\qquad\qquad\langle\xi\rangle\equiv\langle M_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\partial_{\rho}\ln{Z}_{\mathfrak{ccar}}~.

(49)

Similarly, the variances are given by

\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,\partial_{\sigma}^{2}\ln{Z}_{\mathfrak{ccar}}\qquad\qquad\langle\xi^{2}\rangle-\langle\xi\rangle^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,\partial_{\rho}^{2}\ln{Z}_{\mathfrak{ccar}}

(50)

and the covariance is given by

\langle\Delta\xi\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle\langle\xi\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,\partial_{\sigma}\partial_{\rho}\ln{Z}_{\mathfrak{ccar}}\,.

(51)

If the partition function (
47
) — possibly after some modular transformation (
24
) — is dominated by a single primary state with multiplicity
D(\xi_{0},\Delta_{0})
— for instance, the vacuum state
D(0,0)
— then the logarithm of the partition function is approximately linear in the chemical potential
\rho
. This is so because the original partition function (
47
)

Z_{\mathfrak{ccar}}(\rho,\sigma)=D(\xi_{0},\Delta_{0})\,e^{2\pi i\,\rho\big(\xi_{0}-\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}}{24}\big)}\times g(\sigma)+\textrm{other\;primaries}\qquad\qquad g(\sigma)=\textrm{known}

(52)

has an exponent linear in
\rho
, and modular transformations (
24
) preserve linearity in
\rho
. Thus, the quantity
\ln Z_{\mathfrak{ccar}}(\rho,\sigma)
is approximately linear in
\rho
whenever it is justified to neglect the contributions from the other primaries in (
52
). This implies that the energy variance, the second equality (
50
), is approximately zero.

Therefore, if there is any modular frame where the Carrollian partition function (
47
) is dominated by a single primary (which may or may not be the vacuum) the energy variance is approximately zero. This observation will play a key role in the next Section, in particular, in our derivation of the negativity of specific heat.

3.4
Modular aspects of the Dedekind
\boldsymbol{\eta}
-function

We list here key properties of the Dedekind
\eta
-function that we shall use in the remaining Sections of this paper. For more details and derivations of the formulas below see
Dedekind
.

•

Definition.
Assuming
\sigma
lies in the complex upper half-plane the Dedekind
\eta
-function is defined as

\eta(\sigma)=e^{\frac{i\pi\sigma}{12}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi in\sigma})\qquad\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{H}\,.

(53)

•

Cusp at infinity.
To investigate vacuum dominance, we are often interested in the limit where
\sigma
has a very large (positive) imaginary part,

\eta(\sigma)=e^{\frac{i\pi\sigma}{12}}\,\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(e^{2\pi i\sigma}\big)\Big)\qquad\textrm{if}\;\sigma\to i\infty\,.

(54)

•

\boldsymbol{T}
-transformation.
Under shifts by
1
we get

\eta(\sigma+1)=e^{\frac{i\pi}{12}}\,\eta(\sigma)\,.

(55)

•

\boldsymbol{S}
-transformation.
Inversion yields

\eta(-1/\sigma)=\sqrt{-i\sigma}\,\eta(\sigma)\,.

(56)

•

General modular transformation.
Combining arbitrary sequences of
T
- and
S
-transformations yields the behavior under general PSL
(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformations

\eta\bigg(\frac{a\sigma+b}{c\sigma+d}\bigg)=\tilde{A}(a,b,c,d)\,\sqrt{c\sigma+d}\,\eta(\sigma)

(57)

where
\tilde{A}(a,b,c,d)
is a (known but for us irrelevant) phase,
ad-bc=1
and
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Approaching the origin.
For
\sigma=i\epsilon
with
\epsilon\ll 1
after an
S
-transformation (
56
) we use the cusp formula (
54
) to obtain

\eta(i\epsilon)\approx\epsilon^{-1/2}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{12\epsilon}}\,.

(58)

•

Approaching rational numbers.

For
\sigma=\frac{p+i\epsilon}{q}
with coprime positive integers
p<q
and
\epsilon\ll 1
we apply a general modular transformation (
57
) with
c=-q
and
d=p
. For
b
we choose the smallest positive integer so that
a=(1-bq)/p
is also an integer (e.g., if
p=1
or
p=2
we choose
b=1
); the existence of such a
b
is guaranteed by Bézout’s identity. We get

\eta\Big(\frac{p+i\epsilon}{q}\Big)\approx A(p,q)\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{12q\epsilon}}

(59)

where
A(p,q)
is a (known but for us irrelevant) phase. Using
T
-transformations (
55
) we see that the result (
59
) holds for arbitrary rational numbers.

•

Approaching irrational numbers.
The behavior for small imaginary part,
\epsilon\ll 1
, differs significantly from (
59
) if an irrational number
\sigma\notin\mathbb{Q}
is approached,

\eta(\sigma+i\epsilon)\approx B(\sigma)\,\epsilon^{-1/4}

(60)

where
B(\sigma)
is a complex
\mathcal{O}(1)
coefficient.

•

Logarithmic derivative.
The quantity
\eta^{\prime}/\eta
can be expressed as

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\partial_{\sigma}\ln\eta(\sigma)=\frac{1}{24}\,E_{2}(\sigma)

(61)

where
E_{2}
is the second Eisenstein series defined as

E_{2}(\sigma)=1-24\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{1}(n)\,e^{2\pi in\sigma}

(62)

with the divisor function
\sigma_{1}(n)=\sum_{d|n}d
, where
d|n
means that
d
is a divisor of
n
. We have the limiting behavior

E_{2}(\sigma)=1+\mathcal{O}\big(e^{2\pi i\sigma}\big)\qquad\textrm{if}\;\sigma\to+i\infty

(63)

and the quasi-modular property

E_{2}\Big(\frac{a\sigma+b}{c\sigma+d}\Big)=(c\sigma+d)^{2}\,E_{2}(\sigma)-\frac{6ic}{\pi}\,(c\sigma+d)\,.

(64)

For rational
\sigma=(p+i\epsilon)/q
, with coprime positive integers
p<q
, and
\epsilon\ll 1
, we get by the same arguments that led to (
59
) the expansion

E_{2}\Big(\frac{p+i\epsilon}{q}\Big)=-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\,\big(1+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\big)\,.

(65)

3.5
Carrollian vacuum characters as limits from CFT vacuum characters

Starting from a CFT
2

\chi_{c,0}^{\textrm{\tiny HW}}(\tau)=\frac{1-e^{2\pi i\tau}}{\eta(\tau)}\,e^{-2\pi i\,\frac{c-1}{24}\,\tau}\qquad\qquad\bar{\chi}_{\bar{c},0}^{\textrm{\tiny HW}}(\bar{\tau})=\frac{1-e^{-2\pi i\bar{\tau}}}{\eta(-\bar{\tau})}\,e^{2\pi i\,\frac{\bar{c}-1}{24}\,\bar{\tau}}\,.

Inserting the Carroll expansions (

\textrm{ill-defined:}\quad\chi_{\mathds{1}}^{\textrm{\tiny ind}}(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{\big|1-e^{2\pi i\sigma}\big|^{2}}{\eta(\sigma)\,\eta(-\sigma)}\,e^{-2\pi i\,\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}\sigma+c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}\rho}{24}}\,.

(68)

This expression is not well-defined, because for generic

-functions has an argument in the wrong half-plane. The only exception is for real

4.2
Cardy and hard sector

For these sectors, we assume a partition function (
47
) built from highest-weight representations (see Table

). Both in the Cardy and the hard sector, the energy expectation value (

First, let us approximate the partition function by imposing the vacuum dominance conditions from the first two rows of Table

and inserting them into the partition function (

) and

From the partition function (

which essentially coincides with the Cardy-like formula derived in

; we shall make this relation more precise below. We stress that there are no log corrections to the entropy (

) in the grand canonical ensemble for the Cardy sector, as expected on general grounds from the gravity side, see
Carlip:1994gc
;
Carlip:2000nv
;

for the case of BTZ.

From (

), we find that the angular momentum is the sum of two terms

\tilde{\Delta}\equiv\langle\Delta\rangle\approx\frac{\pi^{2}\,c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}}{6(\beta\Omega)^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}\,c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}}{3|\beta^{2}\Omega^{3}|}\equiv\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny L}}+\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny M}}

with ratio

\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny L}}}{\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny M}}}=\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}}{2}\,|\tilde{\Omega}|

(88)

controlled by the dimensionless chemical potential
|\tilde{\Omega}|
.

We assume finite

, implying
\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny L}}\sim\tilde{\xi}\sim(\beta\Omega)^{-2}
. This allows us to organize the discussion in terms of the behavior of
|\tilde{\Omega}|
:

•

Finite

|\tilde{\Omega}|
. This is the Cardy sector where the temperature

is large, and all relevant charges scale in the same way

and

where the

•

Large

(90)

reduce the dominant contribution to the entropy to

•

Small

4.3
Cold and Schwarzian sector

Also for these sectors, we assume a partition function (
47
) built from highest-weight representations (see Table
2

In the cold sector, there is no further simplification of the partition function (

The finite energy

and subleading finite contributions

\Delta_{\textrm{\tiny finite}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\,\frac{c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}-2}{(\beta\Omega)^{2}}+\frac{8\pi^{2}}{(\beta\Omega)^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{4\pi^{2}/(\beta\Omega)}-1}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3\beta^{2}\Omega^{2}}\,E_{2}\Big(\frac{2\pi i}{\beta\Omega}\Big)\,.

\extdm\!E_{0}=T\,\extdm\!S_{\textrm{\tiny cold}}-\Omega\,\extdm\!J_{0}\,.

c_{\textrm{\tiny L}}=0
. There is no way of making the partition function based on highest-weight representations real since the denominator
1/|\eta(\sigma+i\epsilon)|^{2}
is replaced by
1/(\eta(\sigma+i\epsilon))^{2}
. Thus, in the Boltzmann sector, it may be preferable to use induced representations.

4.5
Carrollian vacuum dominance implies negative specific heat

We conclude this Section with a result that is universal to all sectors discussed therein: vacuum dominance in the dual channel (
74
) implies negative specific heat

C_{\Delta}\equiv\left.\frac{\partial\langle\xi\rangle}{\partial T}\right|_{\Delta}<0

(118)

with fixed angular momentum,
\delta\langle\Delta\rangle=0
, and also negative specific heat

C_{\Omega}\equiv\left.\frac{\partial\langle\xi\rangle}{\partial T}\right|_{\Omega}<0

(119)

with fixed angular velocity,
\delta\Omega=0
. While negativity of specific heat is universal, the interpretation of why specific heat is negative depends on the sector. In the Cardy and Schwarzian sectors the culprit is temperature, which is negative, while the energy expectation value
\langle\xi\rangle
is positive. By contrast, in the Boltzmann sector the culprit is the negative energy expectation value
\langle\xi\rangle
, while the temperature is positive.

Plugging the energy expectation value (
76
) into the definition of specific heat at constant angular velocity (
119
) yields

C_{\Omega}\approx\frac{\pi^{2}\,c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}\,T}{3\Omega^{2}}<0\,.

(120)

In the Cardy and Schwarzian sectors, this expression is negative because
\Omega
is real and
T
is negative. In the Boltzmann sector, this expression is negative because
\Omega
is imaginary and
T
is positive. (We assume here positive central charge,
c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}>0
.)

To compute (
118
), we split the derivative into two terms

C_{\Delta}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{\partial\langle\xi\rangle}{\partial\rho}+\left.\frac{\delta\sigma}{\delta\rho}\right|_{\Delta}\,\frac{\partial\langle\xi\rangle}{\partial\sigma}\right)

(121)

where the ratio

\left.\frac{\delta\sigma}{\delta\rho}\right|_{\Delta}=-\frac{\langle\Delta\,\xi\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle\langle\xi\rangle}{\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle^{2}}

(122)

is obtained from requiring

\delta\langle\Delta\rangle=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\delta\sigma\left(\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle^{2}\right)+\delta\rho\left(\langle\Delta\,\xi\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle\langle\xi\rangle\right)=0

(123)

by virtue of the variances (
50
) and covariance (
51
). Collecting all results yields the specific heat at constant angular momentum,

C_{\Delta}=\beta^{2}\,\bigg(\langle\xi^{2}\rangle-\langle\xi\rangle^{2}-\frac{\left(\langle\Delta\,\xi\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle\langle\xi\rangle\right)^{2}}{\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle^{2}}\bigg)\,.

(124)

Within our vacuum dominance approximation (
76
), fluctuations in the energy are suppressed,

\langle\xi^{2}\rangle-\langle\xi\rangle^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\partial_{\rho}\langle\xi\rangle\approx 0

(125)

as explained in Subsection
3.3
. Hence, the specific heat reduces to a manifestly negative expression

\boxed{\phantom{\Big(}C_{\Delta}\approx-\beta^{2}\,\frac{\left(\langle\Delta\,\xi\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle\langle\xi\rangle\right)^{2}}{\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta\rangle^{2}}<0\,.\phantom{\Big)}}

(126)

The above analysis is universal and holds in all sectors contained in Table
1
. It only relies on properties of CCFT
2
characters when computing the specific heat in the dual channel.

In the Cardy and Schwarzian sectors, the negativity of (
126
) is obvious since
\Delta
and
\xi
(as well as their expectation values, variances, and covariance) are real. In the Boltzmann sector, it is a bit less obvious (but still true) since
\langle\Delta\rangle
is imaginary. In that sector, an alternative calculation of the specific heat at constant angular momentum starts directly with the expectation values (
116
) and (
117
), yielding
\frac{\delta\hat{\Omega}}{\delta\beta}|_{\Delta}\approx-\frac{2\hat{\Omega}}{3\beta}
and

\textrm{Boltzmann\;sector:}\qquad C_{\Delta}\approx-\frac{\pi^{2}\,c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}\,T}{9\hat{\Omega}^{2}}

(127)

which is manifestly negative. Note that also the specific heat difference,
C_{\Omega}-C_{\Delta}=-\frac{2\pi^{2}c_{\textrm{\tiny M}}T}{9\hat{\Omega}^{2}}<0
, is negative.

7.3
Validity of Carrollian partition functions

A superficial look at the literature on Carrollian partition functions and the quantization of CCFTs may lead to puzzling conclusions: on the one hand, Carrollian partition functions and thermodynamics are considered to be ill-defined
deBoer:2023fnj
and the quantum Carroll theories might be either trivial or non-existing

Hao:2021urq
or the BMS invariant version of the Ising model
Yu:2022bcp
;
Hao:2022xhq
. For details, we refer to the first six Sections of the current paper and Refs. therein.

Here, we attempt a more refined look at the facts, with the intention to clarify these apparent contradictions. One crucial aspect is whether or not the angular potential
\theta

4.4

-function becomes real and hence the partition function is ill-defined. This issue was first pointed out in Oblak’s work on characters of the BMS group
Oblak:2015sea
. The resolution proposed therein is essentially the resolution we used in Section

, namely analytic continuation

together with the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.
11
11

11

3
rather than with flat space

Physically, the source of the infinities associated with the ill-defined Dedekind
\eta
-function can be attributed to the mundane IR divergences on the gravity side — Minkowski space has an infinite volume.

Recently, these IR divergences were resolved differently, by explicitly isolating them through a volume term of the form
\delta(0)

In summary, for real

For the Schwarzian sector considered in the present work, we used instead imaginary

Another potentially important aspect in defining Carroll partition functions is that the Hamiltonian is central in the Carroll algebra

Comments on induced vs. highest-weight representations

The relevance of the two types of representations — induced or highest-weight — of the dual CCFT
2
for holography in 3d asymptotically flat spacetimes has been debated for a while in the literature. The most obvious argument is that since induced representations are explicitly unitary, while the highest-weight representations are not, it is the induced representation that should play more of a role in the construction of flat holography. Interestingly, though, most calculations for matching bulk and boundary observables had explicitly or implicitly used highest weights, e.g.
Bagchi:2012xr
;
Bagchi:2014iea
.

Our present constructions leave little room for doubt that the highest-weight representations of the dual CCFT
2
are vitally important for 3d flat holography. These are the representations that describe the Schwarzian sector and hence the “extremal” FSCs, or, more precisely, the
O
-folds are holographically dual to CCFT
2
in these representations. The Cardy sector is also included in the highest-weight representations. It is thus natural to conjecture that the non-trivial orbifolds of 3d Minkowski spacetimes, the analogs of the BTZ black holes in the AdS case, fall into highest-weight representations of the dual CCFT.

On the other hand, the induced representations appear to be more suited for scattering problems
Hao:2025btl
and for the Boltzmann sector described in Section
4.4
. Thus, it is possible that a complete holographic description of asymptotically flat spacetimes needs to employ both types of representations, the induced ones to describe scattering and the highest-weight ones to describe thermal states, e.g., in the Cardy and Schwarzian sectors.

Another pressing issue is the apparent non-unitarity of the highest-weight representations in the context of holography. It is worth noting that also for WCFTs, the Schwarzian sector of the theory is connected to non-unitary representations
Aggarwal:2022xfd
. We comment more on WCFTs below. On the other hand, if all the non-trivial zero-mode solutions of 3d asymptotically flat spacetimes are, indeed, dual to highest-weight representations of the CCFT
2
, it is worthwhile trying to investigate if one needs to modify the definition of unitarity for these theories. A possible clue lies in the definition of conjugation and in- and out-states in a CCFT
2
. On the complex plane, the reality condition
\bar{z}=z^{\ast}
is not preserved in the Carroll limit, and one needs to consider a different reality condition
\bar{z}=\frac{1}{z^{\ast}}

Hao:2021urq
. This may provide hints at a different notion of unitarity in 2d CCFTs. See also
Hao:2025btl
for a curious definition of conjugates, albeit for the induced representations.

There is a related and equally, if not more, intriguing point that emerges from our discussion of highest-weight vs. induced representations in flat holography. We have seen that the induced representations naturally follow from the highest-weight representations of two copies of the Virasoro algebra. The Carroll highest weights, however, descend from the “flipped” Virasoro representations, where one half is highest-weight while the other half is lowest-weight. While the usual AdS
3
/CFT
2
correspondence is built on the well-known highest-weight representations of the Virasoro algebra, the immediate question is what role these flipped representations have to play within AdS
3
holography.

The Cardy formula of the dual theory relates the Virasoro highest weights to the area of the outer horizon of the BTZ black hole. Interestingly, the Cardy analysis with the flipped representations relates them to the
inner
horizon of the non-extremal BTZ. It is then natural that the flat space limit relates the Carroll highest-weight representations to the horizon area of the FSC, which itself comes as a limit of this inner horizon
Riegler:2014bia
;
Fareghbal:2014oba
. This raises the tantalizing prospect that one can go beyond the outer horizon to inside the BTZ black hole and access the inner horizon by looking at the flipped representations of the dual CFT
2
. This hints at the fact that the dual CFT
2
knows about the physics in the interior of black holes, accessible through an automorphism of the symmetry algebra.

Loose ends

We conclude with a couple of loose ends and further possible checks. It could be rewarding to analyze the

A more technical loose end is to make formal sense of the saddle point calculations in Appendix

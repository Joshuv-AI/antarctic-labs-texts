R R -boundedness of Poisson operators
Robert Denk, Nick Lindemulder, Jörg Seiler

Abstract
. We investigate the R R -boundedness of parameter-dependent families of Poisson operators on the half-space ℝ + n \mathbb{R}^{n}_{+} in various scales of function spaces. Applications concern maximal L q L_{q} -regularity for boundary value problems with dynamic boundary conditions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J40 (primary); 46E35, 47D06, 35K35 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Poisson operators arise as solution operators to boundary value problems with inhomogeneous boundary conditions. A simple example is the parameter-dependent heat equation in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
,

(1-1)

\displaystyle\mu^{2}u-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

where
\gamma_{0}u=u|_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}
stands for the boundary trace. If
\mu
is complex with positive real part (and
f
and
g
are suitable) this equation is uniquely solvable and the solution can be written in the form
u=R_{\mu}f+K_{\mu}g
. The operator
R_{\mu}
is the resolvent of the Dirichlet-Laplacian,
K_{\mu}
is an example of a parameter-dependent Poisson operator.

In the classical
L^{p}
-setting, the most natural solution space for the first equation in (
1-1
)
is
H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, the classical second-order Sobolev (or Bessel potential) space. To obtain a solution in this space, one needs to impose that the boundary datum
g
belongs to
B_{pp}^{2-1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, where
B_{pp}^{s}
stands for the Besov space or order
s
. However, there are several applications where the boundary data space has less regularity. For instance, this is the case for stochastic PDEs with boundary noise, where
g
stands for the derivative of a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, which is known to belong to some Besov space of negative regularity. Another example is given by the class of boundary value problems with dynamical boundary conditions, which are typically understood as Cauchy problems in the product space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.

For considering boundary data of low regularity in (
1-1
), one has to take care of the well-posedness of the boundary trace
\gamma_{0}
. This trace is continuous as a map from
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
B_{pp}^{s-1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
if and only if
s>1/p
(see, e.g.,
[
JS08
]
). However, if we modify the first space to
H_{p,\Delta}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):=\{u\in H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\mid\Delta u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}
, the boundary trace
\gamma_{0}\colon H_{p,\Delta}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\to B_{pp}^{s-1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
is continuous for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, so the formulation in (
1-1
) makes sense. Surprisingly, the solution operator
K_{\mu}\colon B_{pp}^{s-1/p}\to H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is continuous for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, as was shown by Grubb and Kokholm in
[
GK93
]
. In the same paper, uniform estimates of
R_{\mu}
and
K_{\mu}
in parameter-dependent norms were established. However, these estimates are not sufficient for the study of time-dependent problems like

(1-2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t)-\Delta u(t)

\displaystyle=f(t)

\displaystyle\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}u(t)

\displaystyle=g(t)

\displaystyle\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

in the setting of
maximal
L^{q}
-regularity
, where for
f\in L^{q}((0,\infty);H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
and
g\in L^{q}((0,\infty);B_{pp}^{s-1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
, we seek a solution in the canonical solution space

u\in L^{q}((0,\infty);H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))\cap H_{q}^{1}((0,\infty);H_{p}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})).

In this context, a stronger property – called
R
-boundedness
– of the families
R_{\mu}
and
K_{\mu}
is needed. In fact, it is known since long that
R
-boundedness is equivalent to maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for all
q\in(1,+\infty)
in the sense of well-posedness in
L^{q}
-
L^{p}
-Sobolev spaces in time and space. For more information on maximal regularity theory and related subjects we refer the reader to the recent series of text books of Hytönen, van Neerven, Veraar and Weis
[
HvNVW16
,
HvNVW17
,
HvNVW23
]
and the references therein.

The
R
-boundedness of resolvents like
R_{\mu}
above is well-known and can be obtained by general results on normally elliptic boundary value problems. Generally, such resolvents can be decomposed in the sum of a parameter-dependent pseudodifferential operator and a so-called parameter-dependent singular Green operator (the resolvent construction can be performed for example in the framework of Boutet de Monvel’s algebra, a pseudodifferential calculus for boundary value problems, introduced in
[
BdM71
]
and then developed further over many decades, see
[
GK93
,
Gru96
,
SS94
]
for example). The
R
-boundedness of such operator-families has been shown in very different settings, see for example
[
Hum21
, Proposition 3.6]
,
[
DK07
, Theorem 3.18]
, and
[
DS15
, Theorem 8]
for some specific results. Main applications include fluid mechanics and geometric evolution equations (see, e.g.,
[
PS16
]
,
[
Sai21
]
,
[
Shi20
]
). Recently,
R
-boundedness of the solution operator was considered for non-local operators in
[
AG25
]
.

While the above results and applications are connected with the
R
-boundedness of the resolvent, the
R
-boundedness of families of Poisson operators has been addressed only very recently; in fact, we are only aware of the paper
[
Hum21
]
. In the present work we obtain new results for the
R
-boundedness of Poisson operators in a variety of function spaces.
Not only we consider Poisson operators originating from classical boundary value problems as descried above, but also a wider class of Poisson operators related to the analysis of so-called ”edge-degenerate” differential operators on manifolds with boundary; see for example
[
RS82
,
Sch91
]
for more details. In both cases parameter-dependent Poisson operators have the form

(K_{\mu}g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{ix^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})\widehat{g}(\xi^{\prime})\,d\xi^{\prime},\qquad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

with a specific structure of the so-called symbol-kernel
k
, see Definition
3.2
and Definition
4.7
, respectively; here
\widehat{g}
is the Fourier transform of
g
. In case of scalar operators (acting on complex-valued functions) the classes of Poisson operators studied in this paper include those of
[
Hum21
]
; on the other hand, we do not consider Banach space valued Poisson operators as has been done there. However, it seems reasonable to expect that our techniques can be extended to more general settings.

As an application, we discuss maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for several boundary value problems with dynamic boundary conditions, including a variant of the Cahn–Hilliard equation and a road-field model based on a Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Pisconov equation. In both cases, we consider the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
on the boundary. This space also appears as a natural boundary space in applications to bulk-surface reaction-sorption-diffusion systems as studied in
[
AB21
]
,
[
AB24
]
. In contrast to our examples, the solution in these papers has regularity
H_{p}^{2}
in the interior (bulk) and is coupled to the surface concentration on the boundary. This allows a maximal regularity approach in standard spaces, similar to the analysis in
[
DPZ08
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some background on
R
-boundedness and random sequence spaces. In Section
3
, we define the first classes of Poisson operators with and without paprameter and show first boundedness and
R
-boundedness results. Our main results can be found in Sections
4
and
5
, where we consider the
R
-boundedness of parameter-dependent families of Poisson operators in Besov spaces of negative order (Theorems and
4.3
and
4.5
), in (weak)
L^{p}
-spaces (Theorem
4.11
), in Sobolev spaces (Section
4.3
), and in further anisotropic and weighted spaces (Section
5
). Finally, we apply the results to boundary value problems with dynamic boundary conditions in Section
6
.

Throughout the paper we will use the following notation. We write
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})
for (co-)vectors
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For functions
a:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\xi}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we shall denote by
a(D)
the associated Fourier-multiplier,

a(D)u=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(a\widehat{u}).

In case
a(\xi)=a(\xi^{\prime})
is a function independent of
\xi_{n}
, we shall use the same notation
a(D^{\prime})
for the associated Fourier-multiplier on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, respectively; it will be clear from the context which operator is intended. In particular, we will consider
a(\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}
and
a(\xi^{\prime})=\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{s}:=(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{s/2}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
, yielding the operators
\langle D\rangle^{s}
and
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}
, respectively.

We denote by
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)
,
B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)
and
F^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)

(X
Banach space,
1\leq p,q\leq+\infty)
the standard scales of Bessel, Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, respectively (for a detailed introduction see Section 14.4 and Section 14.6, respectively, in
[
HvNVW23
]
). We shall also use the anisotropic spaces

(1-3)

\displaystyle H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X):=\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\sigma}\big(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)\big)

with the obvious definition of its norm and, analogously, the spaces
B^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)
and
F^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)
.

2.
Some background on R-boundedness

In this section we summarize some background material on
R
-boundedness, following
[
HvNVW17
]
.

A (complex)
Rademacher variable
is a random variable, defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mu)
, which is uniformly distributed over the complex
unit-sphere. We shall denote such a random variable by
\varepsilon
. We also consider sequences

(\varepsilon_{n})=(\varepsilon_{n})_{n=1}^{+\infty}
of independent Rademacher variables which will be called

Rademacher sequences
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces. A subset
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
is called
R
-bounded
provided there exist

p\in[1,+\infty)
and a constant
C=C_{p}\geq 0
such that

\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}T_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;Y)}\leq C\,\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}

for every choice of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
,
T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}\in\mathcal{T}
, and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The infimum over all such
C
is called the
R
-bound of
\mathcal{T}
and shall be denoted by

R(\mathcal{T})=R_{p}(\mathcal{T})
.

Obviously
R
-boundedness implies boundedness. If
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
and
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathscr{L}(Y,Z)
are
R
-bounded then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}+\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=\{S+T\mid S\in\mathcal{S},\,T\in\mathcal{T}\}\subset\mathscr{L}(X,Y),

\displaystyle\mathcal{UT}

\displaystyle=\{UT\mid R\in\mathcal{U},\,T\in\mathcal{T}\}\subset\mathscr{L}(X,Z)

are also
R
-bounded with bounds
R(\mathcal{S}+\mathcal{T})\leq R(\mathcal{S})+\mathbb{R}(\mathcal{T})
and
R(\mathcal{UT})\leq R(\mathcal{U})R(\mathcal{T})
.

A fundamental tool for the study of
R
-boundedness is Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem; for a proof see
[
Hum21
, Theorem 2.15]
and the references given there.

Theorem 2.2

(Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let

A\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\mathscr{L}(X))
be such that

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{|\xi|^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}a(\xi)\mid a\in A,\;|\alpha|\leq n,\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\right\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(X)
. If
X
is a UMD space and has Pisier’s property
(\alpha)
then

\{a(D)\mid a\in A\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X))
,
\mathscr{L}(B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X))
, and

\mathscr{L}(F^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X))
, whenever
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<+\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq+\infty
.
Moreover, in any case, there exists a constant
C
not depending on
A
such that

R\big(\{a(D)\mid a\in A\}\big)\leq CR(\mathcal{A}).

We shall apply this theorem in the special case when
X=H
is a Hilbert space.
Let
\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{L}(H))
denote the space of all functions
a\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\mathscr{L}(H))
with finite norm

\|a\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{L}(H))}=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|\alpha|\leq n}|\xi|^{|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}a(\xi)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}.

Since
R
-boundedness in
\mathscr{L}(H)
coincides with usual boundedness, we obtain:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A
be a bounded subset of
\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{L}(H))
. Then
\{a(D)\mid a\in A\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};H))
,
\mathscr{L}(B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};H))
, and

\mathscr{L}(F^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n},H))
, whenever
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<+\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq+\infty
.
In any case, there exists a constant
C
not depending on
A
such that

R\big(\{a(D)\mid a\in A\}\big)\leq C\sup_{a\in A}\|a\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{L}(H))}.

Given a Rademacher sequence and a sequence
(x_{n})
in an arbitrary Banach space
X
, the so-called
Kahane-Khintchine inequality
holds true: For every choice of
0<p<q<+\infty
there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa_{pq}
such that, for arbitary
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;X)}\leq\kappa\,\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}.

For this reason, if a family of operators has a finite
R
-bound with respect to one
p=p_{0}\in[1,+\infty)
, it has a finite
R
-bound with repect to every choice of
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

It is convenient to introduce the notion of
random sequence space
:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
X
be a Banach space.
\varepsilon^{p}(X)
denotes the space of all sequences
(x_{n})\subset X
for which the series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}
converges in
L^{p}(\Omega;X)
.
We equip
\varepsilon^{p}(X)
with the norm

\|(x_{n})\|_{\varepsilon^{p}(X)}:=\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,X)}.

One can show that
\varepsilon^{p}(X)
is a Banach space and that

\|(x_{n})\|_{\varepsilon^{p}(X)}=\sup_{N\geq 1}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,X)}.

As an immediate consequence of the Kahane-Khintchine inequality,

\varepsilon^{p}(X)\cong\varepsilon^{q}(X)
for every choice
of
1\leq p,q<+\infty
. For this reason one sets

(2-1)

\varepsilon(X):=\varepsilon^{2}(X)\qquad(\cong\varepsilon^{p}(X)\text{ for every $1\leq p<+\infty$}).

Due to the orthogonality of the elements of a Rademacher sequence, clearly
\varepsilon({\mathbb{C}})=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, the space of square-summable sequences.

R
-boundedness can be reformulated in the following way:

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a subset of
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
. Set

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}=\big\{\mathbf{T}=(T_{n})_{n=1}^{+\infty}\subset\mathcal{T}\mid T_{n}\not=0\text{ for at most finitely many $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$}\big\}.

Any
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
induces a bounded map

\mathbf{T}:\varepsilon(X)\longrightarrow\varepsilon(Y)
by

(x_{n})\mapsto(T_{1}x_{1},T_{2}x_{2},T_{3}x_{3}\ldots).

Then
\mathcal{T}
is
R
-bounded if and only if

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
is a bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(\varepsilon(X),\varepsilon(Y))
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. Moreover, let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
denote an interpolation space formed with the real or complex interpolation method. Then

(\varepsilon(X_{0}),\varepsilon(X_{1}))_{\theta}\hookrightarrow\varepsilon((X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta}).

If both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are
K
-convex then the embedding is an isomorphism.

We shall not enter in details about the property of
K
-convexity but only assert that all function and distribution spaces considered later on have this property. As a consequence of the previous theorem,
R
-boundedness is stable under interpolation:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
and
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
be interpolation couples of Banach spaces. Moreover, let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
denote an interpolation space formed with the real or complex interpolation method. Let
\mathscr{T}\subset\mathscr{L}(X_{0}+X_{1},Y_{0}+Y_{1})
and assume that
\mathscr{T}
by restriction yields sets
\mathscr{T}_{j}\subset\mathscr{L}(X_{j},Y_{j})
,
j=0,1
, which are
R
-bounded.
If both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are
K
-convex then

\mathscr{T}_{\theta}\subset\mathscr{L}\big((X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta},(Y_{0},Y_{1})_{\theta}\big)

(
obtained by restriction
)
is also
R
-bounded. Moreover,

\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{T}_{\theta})\lesssim\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{T}_{0})^{1-\theta}\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{T}_{1})^{\theta}.

Let us now determine the random sequence spaces in some concrete examples.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
(S,\Sigma,\sigma)
a measure space, and
1\leq p<+\infty
. Then

\varepsilon^{p}(L^{p}(S;X))\cong L^{p}(S;\varepsilon^{p}(X))

isometrically. In particular,
\varepsilon(L^{p}(S;X))\cong L^{p}(S;\varepsilon(X))
and
\varepsilon(L^{p}(S))\cong L^{p}(S;\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
in case
X={\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence belonging to
\varepsilon^{p}(L^{p}(S;X))
. Then

\displaystyle\|(f_{n})\|_{\varepsilon^{p}(L^{p}(S;X))}^{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{n}f_{n}\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(S;X))}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{S}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{n}(\omega)f_{n}(s)\Big\|_{X}^{p}\,d\sigma(s)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{n}f_{n}(s)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}^{p}\,d\sigma(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\|(f_{n}(s))\|_{\varepsilon^{p}(X)}^{p}\,d\sigma(s)

\displaystyle=\|(f_{n})\|_{L^{p}(S;\varepsilon^{p}(X))}^{p}.

This shows the first claim. The second then follows from (
2-1
).
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
,
1\leq q\leq+\infty
, and
s,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon(H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle\cong H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})),

\displaystyle\varepsilon(B^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle\cong B^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})),

\displaystyle\varepsilon(F^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle\cong F^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\qquad(1<q<+\infty).

Analogous relations hold for the spaces on the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

Proof.

The Fourier multiplier
M:=\langle D\rangle^{s}\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\sigma}
induces isomorphisms
H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well as

H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}))\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}))
. Therefore, using Lemma
2.8
with
S=\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
X={\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle(f_{n})\in\varepsilon(H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle\iff(Mf_{n})\in\varepsilon(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle\iff x\mapsto((Mf_{n})(x))\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))

\displaystyle\iff x\mapsto(f_{n}(x))\in H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}));

recall that
\varepsilon({\mathbb{C}})\cong\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. The remaining claims then follow from interpolation, using Lemma
2.6
.
∎

3.
Poisson operators

3.1.
An example

In order to motivate the class(es) of operators which are subject of the present paper let us consider the Dirichlet problem for the operator
A:=\Delta-1
in the half-space, i.e.,

\displaystyle(\mu^{2}-A)u(x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\mid x_{n}>0\},

\displaystyle u(x^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle=g(x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

with a complex parameter
\mu
satisfying
\mathop{\textrm{{Re}}}\mu>0
(i.e.,
\mu=\sqrt{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0])
.

Taking the Fourier transform in
x^{\prime}
, the problem is equivalent to

\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)^{2}\,\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x_{n})-\partial^{2}_{x_{n}}\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x_{n})=0,\qquad\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime},0)=\widehat{g}(\xi^{\prime}),

where
\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)=\sqrt{1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\mu^{2}}
with the principal branch of the square root on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. The bounded solution of this initial value problem is given by
\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x_{n})=e^{-\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)x_{n}}\widehat{g}(\xi^{\prime})
, which leads to the solution formula

(3-1)

u(x)=(K_{\mu}g)(x):=\int e^{-ix^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}e^{-\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)x_{n}}\widehat{g}(\xi^{\prime})\,d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi^{\prime}.

K_{\mu}
is the prototype of a so-called (strongly)
parameter-dependent Poisson operator
. In general, we shall consider operators
K_{\mu}=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)
of the form

(3-2)

(K_{\mu}g)(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\int e^{-ix^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n})\widehat{g}(\xi^{\prime})\,d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt\xi^{\prime},\qquad x_{n}>0,

with so-called
symbol-kernels

k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n})
to be specified below;
the parameter
\mu
will range in a sub-sector of the complex plane

\Sigma=\big\{0\not=\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong{\mathbb{C}}\mid\alpha<\mathrm{arg}\,\mu<\beta\big\},\qquad 0\leq\beta-\alpha\leq 2\pi;

also the case
\Sigma=\emptyset
is permitted.

3.2.
Parameter-dependent symbol-kernels

For a systematic analysis of Poisson operators we shall need the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{R}
and
E
be a Fréchet space. Then
S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma;E)
denotes the space of all smooth functions

a:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma\to E
such that

|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|D^{\alpha}_{\eta}D^{\beta}_{\mu}a(\eta,\mu)|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\lesssim\langle\eta,\mu\rangle^{d-|\alpha|-|\beta|}

for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{m}
, every
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{2}
, and every continuous semi-norm on
E
; here
\langle\eta,\mu\rangle=(1+|\eta|^{2}+|\mu|^{2})^{1/2}
.
In case
\Sigma
is the empty set, we define
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
a
is intended to be a function of the variable
\eta
only; moreover, in the previous estimate, there are no derivatives with respect to
\mu
and
\langle\eta,\mu\rangle
is replaced by
\langle\eta\rangle:=\langle\eta,0\rangle
. This convention will be applied throughout the paper.

We shall apply the previous definition in both cases
\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
; in these cases we shall replace
\eta

by
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
, respectively.

Taking a countable system of semi-norms
|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{j}
determining the topology of
E
, the space

S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma;E)
becomes a Fréchet space with the semi-norms

a\mapsto\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\eta,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma,\\
|\alpha|+|\beta|+j\leq N\end{subarray}}|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|D^{\alpha}_{\eta}D^{\beta}_{\mu}a(\eta,\mu)|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{j}\langle\eta,\mu\rangle^{-d+|\alpha|+|\beta|},\qquad N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a Banach space and
d\in\mathbb{R}
. The space
S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;Z)

consists of all smooth functions
k:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow Z
such that

\widetilde{k}(\xi^{\prime},\mu;t):=k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{-1}t)\in S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z)),

Any such
k
is called a
(Z
-valued
)

Poisson symbol-kernel
of order
d
.

The canonical bijection

k\mapsto\widetilde{k}:S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;Z)\longrightarrow S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z))

induces a Fréchet topology on
S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;Z)
; it is determined by the system of norms

(3-3)

\displaystyle|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|k|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{d,(N)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\eta,\mu,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
|\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta|+\ell+\ell^{\prime}\leq N\end{subarray}}\|t^{\ell}D^{\ell^{\prime}}_{t}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\xi^{\prime}}D^{\beta}_{\mu}\widetilde{k}(\xi^{\prime},\mu;t)\|_{Z}\langle\eta,\mu\rangle^{-d+|\alpha|+|\beta|}

with
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Of particular importance will be the case
Z=\mathscr{L}(X)
with a Banach space
X
. In this case, with a Poisson symbol-kernel
k\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{L}(X))
we associate the parameter-dependent operator

K_{\mu}=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu):\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};X)

as in (
3-2
).
Any such
K_{\mu}
is called a
(X
-valued) Poisson operator of order
d
. In case
\Sigma=\emptyset
, we intend
K=K_{\mu}
to be an operator that does not depend on the parameter.

Example 3.3
.

Let us return to the example of Section
3.1
. The symbol-kernel in (
3-1
) is
k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})=e^{-\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)x_{n}}
, hence

\widetilde{k}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,t)=\exp\Big(-\frac{\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)}{\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle}t\Big).

If we choose the sector
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\theta}=\{\mu\mid|\mathrm{arg}\,\mu|<\theta\}
with arbitrary
\theta<\pi/2
, then
\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle/\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)
and
\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)/\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle
are scalar symbols of order zero on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma
and
\mathop{\textrm{{Re}}}\tau(\xi^{\prime},\mu)/\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\gtrsim 1
. A simple calculation
(
see the proof of Lemma
4.8
for a similar situation) then shows that
\widetilde{k}\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, hence
k\in S^{0}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
.

3.3.
Characterizations of Poisson operators

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
Z
be a Banach space and
d\in\mathbb{R}
. For a smooth function
k:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow Z
the following
are equivalent:

a
)

k
is a Poisson symbol-kernel of order
d
.

b
)

For some
1\leq p\leq+\infty
and all choices of
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n-1}
, and
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|x_{n}\mapsto x_{n}^{\ell}D_{x_{n}}^{\ell^{\prime}}D^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\xi^{\prime}}D^{\beta}_{\mu}k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z)}\lesssim\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d-\frac{1}{p}-\ell+\ell^{\prime}-|\alpha^{\prime}|-|\beta|}.

c
)

The statement of b
)
holds true for every
1\leq p\leq+\infty
.

Proof.

Defining
\widetilde{k}(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n})=k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{-1}x_{n})
, by direct calculation it is straightforward to verify that a
)

implies c
)
as well as that c
)
with
p=+\infty
implies a
)
. The equivalence of b) and c) has been shown in

[
HL22
, Lemma 4.5]
.
∎

A second characterization relates Poisson operators to pseudodifferential operators.
For a precise formulation we need some definitions.

Definition 3.5
.

A symbol
p(\xi,\mu)\in S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma;Z)
is said to have the two-sided transmission property if
k_{-},k_{+}\in S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z))
, where

k_{\pm}(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n}):=\big[\mathscr{F}^{-1}_{\xi_{n}\to x_{n}}p(\xi^{\prime},\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\xi_{n},\mu)\big](\pm x_{n}).

The space of all such symbols is denoted by
S^{d}_{tr}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma;Z)
.

In more detail, in the previous definition
p(\xi^{\prime},\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\xi_{n},\mu)
for each fixed
(\xi^{\prime},\mu)
is considered as a tempered
distribution on
\mathbb{R}
; the transmission property requires that the inverse Fourier transform of this distribution restricted to both
positive and negative half-axis is a regular distribution with density from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z)
and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{-},Z)
, respectively.
Again, the case
\Sigma=\emptyset
is admitted, meaning that the symbols do not depend on the parameter.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. The two-sided trace operator
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};X)\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)
is defined by
(\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}u)(x^{\prime})=u(x^{\prime},0)
.

Let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}
denote the dual operator of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}
, i.e.,

(\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}T)(\varphi)=T(\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}\varphi),\qquad\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad T\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X);

equivalently,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}T=T\otimes\delta
where
\delta
is the usual delta distribution on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

The space of all
X
-valued Poisson operators
K_{\mu}
of order
d
coincides with the space of all operators of the form

u\mapsto r_{+}\,p(D,\mu)\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}u=r_{+}\,p(D,\mu)(u\otimes\delta):\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)\longrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};X),

with
p\in S^{d-1}_{tr}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma;\mathscr{L}(X))
and where

r_{+}
denotes the operator of restriction from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

Proof.

The scalar case is known from
[
Joh96
, Proposition 4.1]
, for the extension to the operator-valued setting see
[
HL22
, Lemma 4.13]
.
∎

3.4.
A first result on
R
-boundedness of Poisson operators

The following result on the boundedness of Poisson operators in case
\sigma=0
has been shown in
[
HL22
, Theorem 4.25]
; it extends the corresponding
scalar-valued result of
[
Joh96
, Theorem 4.3]
to the Banach space valued setting. The case
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
follows from the simple fact that the
considered Poisson operators commute with the pseudodifferential operator
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\sigma}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
,
1\leq q\leq+\infty
, and
d,s,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
X
be a Banach space.
Any
X
-valued Poisson operator
K=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)
of order
d
extends to bounded operators

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle:B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)\longrightarrow B^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};X),

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle:F^{s+\sigma}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)\longrightarrow F^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};X).

Moreover, the following map is bilinear and continuous
:

(k,u)\mapsto\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)u:S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathscr{L}(X))\times B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};X)\longrightarrow B^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};X);

the analogous statement holds for the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces.

We now present a first result on
R
-boundedness of Poisson operators.
It states that sets which are bounded in the topology of Poisson operators are

R
-bounded as subsets of bounded operators
(
in the sense of Theorem

3.8
).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
,
1\leq q\leq+\infty
, and
d,s,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, let
\mathscr{K}
be a bounded subset of
S_{P}^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
. Then

\{\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)\mid k\in\mathscr{K}\}
is an
R
-bounded subset of both

\mathscr{L}(B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),B^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
and

\mathscr{L}(F^{s+\sigma}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),F^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))

(
where
q<+\infty
in the latter case
)
.

Proof.

We shall apply Remark
2.5
. To this end let

\mathbf{k}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{L},0,0,0,\ldots)
with arbitrary
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
arbitrary
k_{j}\in\mathscr{K}
. Then

\mathbf{k}\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}))\subset S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathscr{L}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})));

the latter inclusion is induced by the canonical isometric embedding of

\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}})
into
\mathscr{L}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
.
Moreover, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3-4)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{k}\|_{d,(N)}\leq\max_{j=1}^{L}\|k_{j}\|_{d,(N)}\leq C_{N}:=\sup_{k\in\mathscr{K}}\|k\|_{d,(N)},

where
\|\cdot\|_{d,(N)}
denotes the norms defining the topology of
Poisson symbols as explained around Definition
3.2

(
with the obvious choice(s) for the Banach space
Z
in the definition
of
\|\cdot\|_{d,(N)}
,
namely
Z=\mathscr{L}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))

on the left-hand side and
Z={\mathbb{C}}
on the right-hand side of (
3-4
)
)
. Since, by Corollary
2.9
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))

\displaystyle\cong B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})),

\displaystyle\varepsilon(B^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))

\displaystyle\cong B^{s-d+\frac{1}{p},\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})),

it follows from Theorem
3.8
that
\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k})=(\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{1}),\ldots,\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{L}),0,0,0,\ldots)
is a bounded operator from
\varepsilon(B^{s+\sigma+d-\frac{1}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
to

\varepsilon(B^{s+\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
and

\|\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k})\|_{\mathscr{L}\big(\varepsilon(B^{s+\sigma+d-\frac{1}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})),\varepsilon(B^{s,\sigma}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))\big)}\leq C

with a constant
C
that neither depends on
L
nor on the choice of
the
k_{j}
. This proves the claim for the scale of Besov spaces; the claim for the scale
of Triebel-Lizorkin spaces is verified in the same way.
∎

4.
R
-boundedness of parameter-dependent Poisson operators

Starting out from a parameter-dependent Poisson operator
K_{\mu}
it is our aim to obtain the
R
-boundedness of
\{|\mu|^{\rho}K_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
in
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
for suitable exponents
\rho
and spaces
X
and
Y
. It seems natural to apply Theorem
3.9
in this context. However,
given a symbol-kernel
k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n})\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
and setting

k_{\mu}(\xi^{\prime};x_{n})=k(\xi^{\prime},\mu;x_{n}),\qquad\mu\in\Sigma,

then
k_{\mu}\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
but, in general, there is no order
d^{\prime}
and no exponent
\rho
such that
\{|\mu|^{\rho}k_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
is a bounded subset of
S^{d^{\prime}}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
. In fact, using Theorem
3.4
, we can only assure that

\displaystyle\big\|x_{n}\mapsto x_{n}^{\ell}D_{x_{n}}^{\ell^{\prime}}k_{\mu}(\xi^{\prime};x_{n})\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d-\frac{1}{p}-\ell+\ell^{\prime}},

which has arbitrarily bad growth in
\mu
as
|\mu|\to+\infty
, since
-\ell+\ell^{\prime}
can become arbitrarily large. For this reason, we need to proceed in a different way.

4.1.
R
-boundedness in Besov spaces

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p(\xi,\mu)\in S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma)
with
d\leq 0
and let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
Moreover, let
1<p<+\infty
and
1\leq q\leq+\infty
.
Then
\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d}p(D,\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\big(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),H^{s-(1-\theta)d}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big).

The analogous statement is true if we replace
H^{s}_{p}
by
B^{s}_{pq}
or
F^{s}_{pq}
.

Proof.

Write
p(D,\mu)=\langle D\rangle^{(1-\theta)d}q_{\mu}(D)
with
q_{\mu}(\xi)=p(\xi,\mu)\langle\xi\rangle^{-(1-\theta)d}
.
Using

\langle\xi,\mu\rangle^{d-|\beta|}=\langle\xi,\mu\rangle^{\theta d}\langle\xi,\mu\rangle^{(1-\theta)d-|\beta|}\leq\langle\mu\rangle^{\theta d}\langle\xi\rangle^{(1-\theta)d-|\beta|}

and product rule, it is easy to see that

|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}q_{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle^{\theta d}\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\alpha|},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}.

Therefore,
\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d}q_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}
is a bounded subset of
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The result then follows from Mikhlin’s theorem, see Corollary
2.3
.
∎

In an analogous way one shows the following result:

Remark 4.2
.

Let
p(\xi,\mu)\in S^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma)
with
d>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<+\infty
,
1\leq q\leq+\infty
. Then

\left\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-d}p(D,\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\right\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),H^{s-d}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\mathscr{L}(B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),B^{s-d}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
and
\mathscr{L}(F^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),F^{s-d}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, respectively.

Recall the two-sided trace operator
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}
from Definition
3.6
and its adjoint.
It is known, see
[
Joh96
, Proposition 2.6]
for example, that

\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}
induces continuous operators

B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\longrightarrow B^{s-1+\frac{1}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad F^{s}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\longrightarrow F^{s-1+\frac{1}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for every
s<0
and
1\leq p,q\leq+\infty
(and
p<+\infty
in case of the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces).
Combining this with the previous Lemma
4.1
we find:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
s<0
and
1<p<+\infty
. Let
p(\xi,\mu)\in S^{d-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma)
with
d\leq 0
and let
0\leq r,\theta\leq 1
. Then

\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d+\frac{r}{p}}r_{+}p(D,\mu)\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}^{*}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}

is an
R
-bounded subset of

(4-1)

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}&\big(B^{s}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),B^{s-(1-\theta)d+\frac{1-r}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\big)\qquad(1\leq q\leq+\infty),\\
\mathscr{L}&\big(F^{s}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),F^{s-(1-\theta)d+\frac{1-r}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\big)\qquad(1<q<+\infty).\end{split}

In particular, due to Theorem
3.7
, if
K_{\mu}=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)
is a Poisson operator with symbol-kernel
k\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
,
d\leq 0
, then
\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d+\frac{r}{p}}K_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
is an
R
-bounded subset of the spaces in (
4-1
).

Proof.

The proof is the same for both scales of spaces; hence let us focus on the Besov spaces. Since
p
has order
d-1
, by
Lemma
4.1

\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta^{\prime}(d-1)}p(D,\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}
is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}\big(B^{s-1+\frac{1}{p}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),B^{s-1+\frac{1}{p}-(1-\theta^{\prime})(d-1)}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)
for every
0\leq\theta^{\prime}\leq 1
.
If
\theta^{\prime}=\frac{\theta d-\frac{r}{p}}{d-1}
then
-\theta^{\prime}(d-1)=-\theta d+\frac{r}{p}
and the claim follows.
∎

Let us now turn to the case of non-negative regularity
s\geq 0
.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the parameter-dependent Poisson operator
K_{\mu}
associated with the Dirichlet-Laplacian as described in Section
3.1
. If we consider only
\mu\in(0,+\infty)
, the symbol-kernel of
K_{\mu}
is
k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})=e^{-\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n}}
; it has order
d=0
. Let us determine the operator-norm of

K_{\mu}:H^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\longrightarrow H^{s+\frac{1-r}{2}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),\qquad\mu\geq 0,

with
0\leq s<1/2
and
0\leq r\leq 1
.
We shall use the fact that

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\cong\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{2s+1-r}\big\|\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1/2}\widehat{v}(\xi^{\prime},\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}x_{n})\big\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+,x_{n}})}\,d\xi^{\prime}.

For
v=K_{\mu}u
this yields

\displaystyle\|K_{\mu}u\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\cong\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{2s-r}\big\|e^{-\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}x_{n}}\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime})\big\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+,x_{n}})}\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-r}\big\|e^{-\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}x_{n}}\big\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+,x_{n}})}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi^{\prime})|^{2}\,d\xi^{\prime}.

It follows that

\|K_{\mu}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))}\cong\sup_{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{r}{2}}\big\|e^{-\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}x_{n}}\big\|_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+,x_{n}})}.

Let us for brevity set
a=a(\xi^{\prime},\mu):=\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-1}
. It is known, cf.
[
DNPV12
, Lemma 5.2]
, that the even extension of functions induces a continuous extension operator
H^{t}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to H^{t}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
0\leq t<1
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-ax_{n}}\big\|_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+,x_{n}})}\cong\big\|e^{-a|x_{n}|}\big\|_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x_{n}})}

Since the Fourier transform of
e^{-|x_{n}|}
coincides with
2\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{-2}
,

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-a|x_{n}|}\big\|^{2}_{H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x_{n}})}

\displaystyle\cong\int\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{2s+1-r}\Big|\frac{\langle\xi_{n}/a\rangle^{-2}}{a}\Big|^{2}\,d\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\int\langle a\xi_{n}\rangle^{2s+1-r}\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{-4}\,d\xi_{n}\cong a^{2s-r};

the latter equivalence is true because

\langle a\xi_{n}\rangle^{2}=1+a^{2}\xi_{n}^{2}\leq a^{2}+a^{2}\xi_{n}^{2}=a^{2}\langle\xi_{n}\rangle^{2},\qquad\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R},

and

\langle a\xi_{n}\rangle^{2}=1+a^{2}\xi_{n}^{2}\geq a^{2},\qquad|\xi_{n}|\geq 1.

Hence

\|K_{\mu}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s+(1-r)/2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))}\cong\sup_{\xi^{\prime}}\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{s-\frac{r}{2}}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-s}=\langle\mu\rangle^{s-\frac{r}{2}}.

Based on the previous example we seek a version of Theorem
4.3
for
s\geq 0
, where the factor
\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d+\frac{r}{p}}
is replaced by
\langle\mu\rangle^{-s-\theta d+\frac{r}{p}}

Definition 4.6
.

Let

be a Banach space. Then

denotes the space of all smooth functions

for every choice of

\mathscr{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+};Z)

on

Definition 4.7
.

Let
Z
be a Banach space, and

consists of all smooth functions

\Sigma=\emptyset

Hence, as explained after Lemma
4.10
, the Poisson operator

(4-7)

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}^{(p)}):L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\longrightarrow L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))).

is continuous; its operator-norm can be estimated from above by
C_{pq}\|k\|_{0,(n+1)}
with a constant
C_{pq}
which does not depend on
M
,
\mu_{j}
, and
k
.

Given arbitrary
g_{j}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
,
1\leq j\leq M
, we have

\mathbf{g}:=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{M},0,0,\ldots)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
and

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\langle\mu_{j}\rangle^{1/p}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{j})g_{j}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})))}

\displaystyle=\big\|(\langle\mu_{1}\rangle^{1/p}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{1})g_{1},\ldots,\langle\mu_{M}\rangle^{1/p}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{M})g_{M},0,0,\ldots)\big\|_{\varepsilon(L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})))}

\displaystyle\cong\big\|\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}^{(p)})\mathbf{g}\big\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})))};

here we have used that

\varepsilon(L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))\cong L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\varepsilon(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})))\cong L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))).

By (
4-7
) and since
\varepsilon(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))\cong L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}^{(p)})\mathbf{g}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|k\|^{\prime}_{0,(n+1)}\big\|\mathbf{g}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))}

\displaystyle\cong C\|k\|^{\prime}_{0,(n+1)}\big\|(g_{1},\ldots,g_{M},0,0,\ldots)\big\|_{\varepsilon(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))}

\displaystyle=C\|k\|^{\prime}_{0,(n+1)}\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{M}\varepsilon_{n}g_{n}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))}

with a constant not depending on
M
and the
\mu_{j}
. This proves the first claim of the theorem.
For the second claim define weight-functions
\omega_{p}:{\mathbb{N}}\to(0,\infty)
by

\omega_{p}(j)=\begin{cases}\langle\mu_{j}\rangle^{-1/2p}&:1\leq j\leq M,\\
1&:\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then (
4-7
) is equivalent to the continuity of

(4-8)

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}):L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p}}({\mathbb{C}}))\longrightarrow L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})))

with operator-norm equal that of
\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}^{(p)})
.
For given
p
choose arbitrary
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
. Then from (
4-8
) and Theorem
4.12
we find

(4-9)

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}):L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};(\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{0}}}({\mathbb{C}}),\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{1}}}({\mathbb{C}}))_{\theta,p})\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))),

where the operator-norm is bounded by
C\|k\|^{\prime}_{0,(n+1)}
with a constant that does not depend on
M
,
\mu_{j}
, and
k
. Moreover, we have used that

(L^{p_{0},\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X),L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X))_{\theta,p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)

for arbitrary Banach spaces
X
. In case
p\geq 2
, we have

(\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{0}}}({\mathbb{C}}),\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{1}}}({\mathbb{C}}))_{\theta,2}\subset(\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{0}}}({\mathbb{C}}),\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{1}}}({\mathbb{C}}))_{\theta,p}

where the corresponding norms satisfy
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,q}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{\theta,2}
with a constant
C=C(\theta,q)
. By Theorem
4.13
(with
p_{0}=p_{1}=p=2
),

(\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{0}}}({\mathbb{C}}),\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p_{1}}}({\mathbb{C}}))_{\theta,2}=\ell^{2}_{\omega_{0}^{1-\theta}\omega_{1}^{\theta}}({\mathbb{C}})=\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p}}({\mathbb{C}})

and

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}):L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}_{\omega_{p}}({\mathbb{C}}))\longrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})))

with operator-norm bounded by
C\|k\|^{\prime}_{0,(n+1)}
with a constant that does not depend on
M
,
\mu_{j}
, and
k
. The latter is equivalent to the continuity of

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k}^{(p)}):L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\longrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})))

with same operator-norm as
\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(\mathbf{k})
. Then we can repeat the reasoning for the first claim to obtain the second claim of the theorem.
∎

The proof of Theorem
4.11
can also be easily modified to obtain the following version with

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
1<p<2
,

, and

.
If

is

Hence if in the definition of

4.3.
R
-boundedness in classical Sobolev spaces

In this section we derive a result complementary to Theorem
4.5
. We shall employ the usual Bessel potential spaces
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
and
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Recall that

H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

We then define the weak spaces

H^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=H^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))\cap L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

where
H^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
consists of those functions whose derivatives up to order
s
belong to
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
. Similarly as in (
1-3
) we define the anisotropic spaces
H^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
H^{s,\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
d\leq 0
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
If
k\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
then

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}:=

\displaystyle\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-s-\theta d+\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{L}(H^{s+t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))

is
R
-bounded. If
p\geq 2
then
\mathscr{K}
is also an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{s+t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})).

Proof.

We write

\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{-d}\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d}=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{0})(\mu)\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d},

where
k_{0}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n}):=\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{-d}k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})
belongs to
S^{0}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
.
Since

\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d}\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-(1-\theta)d}\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})

uniformly in
\mu
, by Mikhlin’s theorem,
\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d}\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
is an
R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
. Thus it remains to verify the claim in case
d=0
and
\theta=0
. By conjugation with
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{t}
it is also sufficient to consider
t=0
.

By definition of the norm in
H^{s}_{p,w}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
the result follows from the
R
-boundedness of the sets

\mathscr{K}_{\ell}:=\Big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-s-\theta d+\frac{1}{p}}D_{x_{n}}^{\ell}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\Big\},\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq s,

as a subset of
\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
. However, since
k(\xi,\mu,x_{n})=\widetilde{k}(\xi,\mu,\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n})
, we have

D_{x_{n}}^{\ell}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{\ell})(\mu)\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{\ell},\qquad k_{\ell}\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma).

Moreover, the operator family
\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-s}\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{\ell}\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
is an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
due to Lemma
4.2
.
Then the result follows from Theorem
4.11
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
d\leq 0
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
If
k\in S^{d}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
then

\mathscr{K}:=\Big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-s-\theta d+\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\Big\}\subset\mathscr{L}(H^{s+t}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s,t-(1-\theta)d}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))

is
R
-bounded. If
p\geq 2
then
\mathscr{K}
is also an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{s+t}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s,t-(1-\theta)d}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})).

Proof.

Again it is no loss of generality to restrict ourselves to the case
d=\theta=t=0
.

Theorem
4.16
yields the
R
-boundedness of
\mathscr{K}
in

\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
as well as

\mathscr{L}(H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),H^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
.
In order to obtain the claim, it suffices to pass to the intersection of these spaces.
∎

5.
R
-boundedness in other function spaces

Theorem
4.11
together with certain invariance properties of weak Poisson operators allows to obtain

R
-boundedness in various other scales of function spaces.

5.1.
Spaces with totally characteristic normal derivative

Given a Poisson operator
K_{\mu}
with symbol
k\in S^{d}_{P,w}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
we first observe that
(x_{n}D_{x_{n}})^{\ell}K_{\mu}
is a Poisson operator of the same kind; in fact if
k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})=\widetilde{k}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n})
, cf. Definition
4.7
, then
(x_{n}D_{x_{n}})^{\ell}K_{\mu}
has the symbol

k_{\ell}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n}):=\big((x_{n}D_{x_{n}})^{\ell}\widetilde{k}\big)(\xi^{\prime},\mu,\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n}).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
denote the space of all functions

u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
such that
(tD_{t})^{\ell}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
for all
\ell\leq s
. In the same way we define
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
by replacing
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
with the Lorentz space
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
.

Both
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
are Banach spaces in an obvious way.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
d\leq 0
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. If
k\in S^{d}_{P,w}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
then

\mathscr{K}:=\Big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d+\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\Big\}\subset\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))

is
R
-bounded. If
p\geq 2
then
\mathscr{K}
is also an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))).

Proof.

Since
\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{0})(\mu)\langle D^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d}
where

k_{0}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n}):=\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{d}k(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n})
belongs to
S^{0}_{P,w}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
, it suffices to treat the case
d=\theta=0
. By conjugation with
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{t}
we may also assume
t=0
. Since

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})))}\cong\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\|(tD_{t})^{\ell}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})))},

the
R
-boundedness of
\{\langle\mu\rangle^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}

in
\mathscr{L}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
follows from the
R
-boundedness of
the sets
\{\langle\mu\rangle^{\frac{1}{p}}(x_{n}D_{x_{n}})^{\ell}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
,
\ell\leq s
,
in
\mathscr{L}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
. However, this is true in view of Theorem
4.11
and the discussion before Definition
5.1
. The reasoning in case
p\geq 2
is the same.
∎

5.2.
Edge degenerate Sobolev spaces

For a multi-index
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, let us write

(x_{n}D)^{\alpha}:=(x_{n}D_{x_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}(x_{n}D_{x_{2}})^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots(x_{n}D_{x_{n}})^{\alpha_{n}}.

Definition 5.3
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
let us define

\mathcal{W}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as the space of all functions
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that

(x_{n}D)^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\qquad\forall\;|\alpha|\leq s.

Furthermore, with
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{s,\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\sigma}\mathcal{W}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

Similarly we define
\mathcal{W}^{s,\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
by substituting

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
.

Given a Poisson operator
K_{\mu}
with symbol-kernel

where

, we find that
x_{n}D_{x_{j}}K_{\mu}
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
,
is a Poisson operator with symbol-kernel

k_{j}(\xi^{\prime},\mu,x_{n}):=\frac{\xi_{j}^{\prime}}{\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle}(x_{n}\widetilde{k})(\xi,\mu,\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n})

which has order
d
again; together with the observation made before Definition
5.1
, it follows that
(x_{n}D)^{\alpha}K_{\mu}
is a Poisson operator with symbol-kernel in
S^{d}_{P,w}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
for arbitrary
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
. Therefore, arguing as in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, we obtain the following result
:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, and

with
d\leq 0
. Then

\mathscr{K}:=\big\{\langle\mu\rangle^{-\theta d+\frac{1}{p}}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\big\}\subset\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\mathcal{W}^{s,t-(1-\theta)d}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))

is
R
-bounded. If
p\geq 2
then
\mathscr{K}
is also an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),\mathcal{W}^{s,t-(1-\theta)d}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})).

Given a toplogical space
X\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
on the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, let us denote by
x_{n}^{\gamma}X
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the image of
X
under the map
u\mapsto x_{n}^{\gamma}u
, equipped with the canonically induced topology.

Given a symbol-kernel

k_{\gamma}(\xi,\mu,x_{n}):=x_{n}^{-\gamma}k(\xi,\mu,x_{n})=\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle^{\gamma}(x_{n}^{-\gamma}\widetilde{k})(\xi,\mu,\langle\xi^{\prime},\mu\rangle x_{n}).

belongs to

belongs to

\gamma\leq 0
because, in this case, multiplication by
x_{n}^{-\gamma}
is a continuous endomorphism of
\mathscr{S}^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\|\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)v\|_{x_{n}^{\gamma}X}=\|\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k_{\gamma})(\mu)v\|_{X}.

Thus we immediately obtain the following versions of Theorems

with
d+\gamma\leq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}

\displaystyle\subset\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)(d+\gamma)}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))

is
R
-bounded. If
p\geq 2
then
\mathscr{K}
is also an
R
-bounded subset of

\mathscr{L}(H^{t}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{t-(1-\theta)d}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})).

.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

,

,

6.
Application to pde with dynamical boundary conditions

In this section, we use the above results to show maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for operators related to evolution equations with dynamic boundary conditions.

6.1.
A first example

We start with the most simple prototype of such equations and consider

(6-1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)-(\Delta-1)u(t,x)

\displaystyle=f(t,x)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\partial_{\nu}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=g(t,x)

\displaystyle\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

\displaystyle u(0,x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Here,
\partial_{\nu}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
is the derivative in direction of the outer normal vector. We want to solve (
6-1
) for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
with
1<p<+\infty
.

From the first equation, one would expect the solution
u
to belong to the classical parabolic solution space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))\cap H_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
. In this case,
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}
belongs to some (anisotropic) Besov space in
t
and
x
of positive order. So the regularity of the boundary data
g
is not large enough to apply a classical maximal regularity approach as, e.g., in
[
DPZ08
]
.

To overcome this difficulty we decouple
u
and
v:=\gamma_{0}u
and consider (
6-1
) as a Cauchy problem of the form
(\partial_{t}-A)\binom{u}{v}=\binom{f}{g}
, where
A
is the unbounded operator in
X:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
defined by

(6-2)

\displaystyle A\binom{u}{v}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta-1&0\\
-\gamma_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\binom{u}{v},

\displaystyle D(A)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\binom{u}{v}\in X:A\binom{u}{v}\in X,\,\gamma_{0}u=v\right\},

where
\gamma_{1}:=\gamma_{0}\partial_{\nu}
is the Neumann boundary condition.
Note that, although
u
is only required to belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, it is meaningful to define the traces
\gamma_{0}u
and
\gamma_{1}u
: In fact, the definition of
D(A)
entails that
\Delta u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. If we define the spaces

H_{p,\Delta}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):=\{w\in H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\Delta w\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\},\qquad s\in\mathbb{R},

it is a classical result that
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
induce, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, continuous maps from
H_{p,\Delta}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
to
B^{s-1/p}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
and
B^{s-1-1/p}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, respectively; see for example
[
See66
, Theorem 7]
,
[
LM72
, Chapter 2, Section 6.5]
, and
[
Roi96
, Section 6.1]
.

Recall that an operator
A
is called
R
-sectorial of angle
\theta
if the resolvent
(A-\lambda)^{-1}
exists for all

\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:|\arg(\lambda)|<\theta\}

and the set
\{\lambda(A-\lambda)^{-1}:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}\}
is
R
-bounded for all
\theta^{\prime}<\theta
. It is well known that
R
-sectoriality with angle
\theta>\frac{\pi}{2}
implies maximal
L^{q}
-regularity.

Theorem 6.1
.

The operator
A
defined in (
6-2
) is
R
-sectorial of angle
\pi
. In particular, it has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for every
1<q<+\infty
, i.e.,
for any data
\binom{f}{g}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)
, there exists a unique solution

\binom{u}{v}=\binom{u}{\gamma_{0}u}\in H_{q}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X)\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+};D(A))

of (
6-1
) depending continuously on
f
and
g
.

Proof.

We fix
\theta^{\prime}<\pi
and consider the resolvent problem

(6-3)

\displaystyle(\mu^{2}+1)u-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\mu^{2}v+\partial_{\nu}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=v

\displaystyle\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Here, we have set
\lambda=\mu^{2}
with
\mu\in\Sigma:=\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}/2}
.
We are looking for the solution
\binom{u}{v}=R(\mu)\binom{f}{g}
. In a first step, we make the reduction to
f=0
by writing

\binom{u}{v}=\binom{R_{1}(\mu)f}{0}+R(\mu)\binom{0}{g-\gamma_{1}R_{1}(\mu)f},

where
u_{1}=R_{1}(\mu)f
is the unique solution of the first line in (
6-3
) with Dirichlet boundary conditions
\gamma_{0}u_{1}=0
. As it is well known that the Dirichlet Laplacian is
R
-sectorial with angle
\pi
, we obtain the
R
-boundedness of

\{\mu^{2}R_{1}(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}\subset\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})).

Moreover,
\{R_{1}(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}
is an

R
-bounded subset of
\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
and
\gamma_{1}\in\mathscr{L}(H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
is bounded (and independent of
\mu
). Therefore, if we define

\widetilde{R}(\mu)g=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{R}_{1}(\mu)g\\
\widetilde{R}_{2}(\mu)g\end{pmatrix}:=R(\mu)\binom{0}{g},\qquad g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),

it remains to show that

\left\{\mu^{2}\widetilde{R}(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\right\}\subset\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),X)

is
R
-bounded. So, we have to solve (
6-3
) with
f=0
.
To find an explicit formula for the solution operator,
we formally apply partial Fourier transform in
x^{\prime}
and obtain that
u
has to satisfy

\gamma_{1}u=a_{\mu}(D^{\prime})\gamma_{0}u

with symbol
a_{\mu}(\xi^{\prime})=\sqrt{1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\mu^{2}}
. Note that the operator
a_{\mu}(D^{\prime})
is a parameter-dependent version of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.

Inserting this and
\gamma_{0}u=v
into the second line of (
6-3
), we obtain

(6-4)

\mu^{2}\widetilde{R}_{2}(\mu)g=\mu^{2}v=b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})g,\qquad b_{\mu}(\xi^{\prime})=\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}+\sqrt{1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\mu^{2}}}.

It is easily seen that
b_{\mu}
satisfies the Mikhlin condition uniformly in
\mu\in\Sigma
, and therefore

\{b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}\subset\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))

is
R
-bounded. Finally, the first component
u=\widetilde{R}_{1}(\mu)g
of the solution is given as the unique solution of

\displaystyle(\mu^{2}+1)u-\Delta u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}u

\displaystyle=v\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Therefore, we have
u=K_{\mu}v=K_{\mu}b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})g
where
K_{\mu}=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)
is a Poisson operator with symbol
k\in S^{0}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\Sigma)
. Applying Theorem
4.11
in the case
p\geq 2
and Remark
4.15
in the case
p<2
, we see that in all cases

\{\langle\mu\rangle^{1/p-\varepsilon}\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits(k)(\mu)\,|\,\mu\in\Sigma\}\subset\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))

is
R
-bounded for any
\varepsilon>0
. In our situation, it is sufficient
to choose
\varepsilon=1/p
, which implies the
R
-boundedness of

\mu\mapsto\mu^{2}\widetilde{R}_{1}(\mu)=K_{\mu}b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})
. Altogether, we have seen that

\{\mu^{2}R(\mu)\mid\mu\in\Sigma\}\subset\mathscr{L}(X)

is
R
-bounded. For smooth right-hand sides
f
and
g
, the function

R(\mu)(f,g)^{\top}
is, by construction, a classical solution of (
6-3
). By density, it is also a solution for
general
(f,g)^{\top}\in X
, where now
\partial_{\nu}
has to be understood in the generalized
sense explained above. Therefore,
R(\mu)
is in fact the resolvent of
A
in
X
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Note that (
6-4
) implies
v\in H^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, since
b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})
is, for fixed
\mu
, of order
-1
. From the continuity of the Poisson operator, it follows that, for
p\geq 2
, we have
D(A)\subset H^{1+1/p}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times H^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.

6.2.
Cahn–Hilliard equation with dynamic boundary conditions

As a second application, we consider a simplified model of a linearized Cahn–Hilliard equation
with dynamics on the boundary, which was discussed in detail in
[
PRZ06
]
.
This fourth-order system has the form

(6-5)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\Delta^{2})u

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{\nu}u-\Delta^{\prime}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\Delta u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

(plus initial condition). Again we decouple
u
and
v:=\gamma_{0}u
and write (
6-5
) as a
Cauchy problem in the product space
X:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
for the operator
A

which now is defined by

(6-6)

\displaystyle A\binom{u}{v}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta^{2}&0\\
-\partial_{\nu}&\Delta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\binom{u}{v},

\displaystyle D(A)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\binom{u}{v}\in X:\;A\binom{u}{v}\in X,\;\gamma_{1}\Delta u=0,\;\gamma_{0}u=v\right\}.

Similarly as in (
6-2
), the involved Neumann trace
\gamma_{1}(\Delta u)
in the domain of
A
is well-defined.
This operator has been analyzed in
[
DPRS23
, Section 5]
, where it was shown that it is sectorial. With the results of the present paper, we can improve this to
R
-sectoriality and
thus get maximal
L^{q}
-regularity.

Theorem 6.2
.

For every
\lambda_{0}>0
, the operator
A-\lambda_{0}
defined in (
6-6
) is
R
-sectorial of angle
\pi
and therefore has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as the proof of Theorem
6.1
but in a more complicated
situation. It is shown in
[
DPRS23
, proof of Theorem 5.6]
that instead of
(
6-4
) the function
v
is now determined by
\mu^{2}v=b_{\mu}(D^{\prime})g
with

b_{\mu}(\xi^{\prime})=\frac{\mu^{2}(\tau_{1}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)+\tau_{2}(\xi^{\prime},\mu))}{(\mu^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})(\tau_{1}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)+\tau_{2}(\xi^{\prime},\mu))+2\tau_{1}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)\tau_{2}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)},

where
\tau_{1,2}(\xi^{\prime},\mu):=\sqrt{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\pm i\mu}
. Moreover, from
[
DPRS23
, Lemma 5.5]

we know that, after replacing
|\xi^{\prime}|
by
z
, the resulting function
b_{\mu}(z)
is holomorphic and bounded
for
(z,\mu)\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\times\Sigma_{\theta}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.
As
-\Delta^{\prime}
admits an
R
-bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, the corresponding operator family is
R
-bounded.
Now we can proceed exactly as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, applying Theorem
4.11
and Remark
4.15
.
∎

6.3.
A Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Pisconov model with dynamics on the boundary

As a last example, we study a field-road model from mathematical biology which was proposed in
[
BRR13
]
to model, e.g., the propagation of wolves in regions (fields) with men-made corridors (roads). This system consists of a Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Pisconov equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
(with
n=2
for the application) and a dynamic boundary condition on the road
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. More precisely, it has the form

(6-7)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-d\Delta)u

\displaystyle=f(u)

\displaystyle\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle-\gamma_{0}u+(\partial_{t}-d^{\prime}\Delta^{\prime})v+kv

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

\displaystyle d\partial_{\nu}u+\gamma_{0}u-kv

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Here,
d,d^{\prime},k
are positive parameters, and the nonlinearity
f(u)
describes the reproduction process. Again, we write the linearized system as an evolution equation of the form
(\partial_{t}-A)\binom{u}{v}=\binom{f}{g}
(where in the application
g=0
) in the basic space
X:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
. Now the operator
A
is given by

(6-8)

\displaystyle A\binom{u}{v}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}d\Delta&0\\
\gamma_{0}&d^{\prime}\Delta^{\prime}-k\end{pmatrix}\binom{u}{v},

\displaystyle D(A)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\binom{u}{v}\in X:A\binom{u}{v}\in X,\,d\partial_{\nu}u+\gamma_{0}u-kv=0\right\}.

To analyze the operator
A
, we consider the resolvent equation
(\mu^{2}-A)\binom{u}{v}=\binom{f}{g}
, where again we can reduce this to the case
f=0
. Applying partial Fourier transform, the first line of (
6-7
) turns into an ODE in
(0,\infty)

of the form

(\mu^{2}+d|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-d\partial_{n}^{2})\hat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x_{n})=0\quad\text{ in }(0,\infty).

For the only stable solution, we get

\hat{u}(\xi^{\prime},x)=\exp\left(-{\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{2}}{d}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}}\;x_{n}\right)\hat{u}(\xi^{\prime},0).

Inserting this into the boundary conditions yields

\begin{pmatrix}-1&\mu^{2}+k+d^{\prime}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\\
\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}+1&-k\end{pmatrix}\binom{\hat{u}(\xi^{\prime},0)}{\hat{v}(\xi^{\prime})}=\binom{\hat{g}(\xi^{\prime})}{0}.

Therefore
\mu^{2}\gamma_{0}u=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits^{\prime}[m_{1}(\cdot,\mu)]g
and
\mu^{2}v=\mathop{\text{{op}}}\nolimits^{\prime}[m_{2}(\cdot,\mu)]g
with

(6-9)

\displaystyle m_{1}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{2}k}{(\mu^{2}+k+d^{\prime}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}+1)-k}\;,

\displaystyle m_{2}(\xi^{\prime},\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{2}(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}+1)}{(\mu^{2}+k+d^{\prime}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}+1)-k}\;.

As in Subsection
6.2
, we will use the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the operator
-\Delta^{\prime}
. For this, the
following result is the key observation.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, and let
z\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
.
Define
m_{1}(z,\mu)
and
m_{2}(z,\mu)
by (
6-9
) with
|\xi^{\prime}|
being replaced by
z
. Then, if
\varepsilon
is chosen small enough, the functions
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are bounded and holomorphic for
(z,\mu)\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\times\Sigma_{\theta}
.

Proof.

We only consider
m_{1}
, as the proof for
m_{2}
can be done in the same way. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi/2-\theta)
. We first remark that, by
homogeneity and compactness, for all
(z,\mu)\in(\overline{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\times\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}})\setminus\{0\}
we have

(6-10)

c_{1}|(z,\mu)|\leq\big|\mu^{2}+z^{2}\big|^{1/2}\leq C_{1}|(z,\mu)|,

where
|(z,\mu)|:=:=(|z|^{2}+|\mu|^{2})^{1/2}
. From this we obtain

m_{1}(z,\mu)\to 0
for
|(z,\mu)|\to\infty
. In particular,
m_{1}
is bounded for
|(z,\mu)|\geq R
with
R
sufficiently large.

For
|(z,\mu)|\to 0
, we write the denominator of
m_{1}
in the form

m_{1}(z,\mu)=\frac{\mu^{2}k}{(\mu^{2}+d^{\prime}z^{2})(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}z^{2}}+1)+k\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}z^{2}}}\,.

Again using (
6-10
), we see that the absolute value of the denominator can be estimated from below by

C\big(|(z,\mu)|-|(z,\mu)|^{2}(|(z,\mu)|+1)\big)\geq C^{\prime}|(z,\mu)|

for sufficiently small
|(z,\mu)|
. This yields
m_{1}(z,\mu)\to 0
for
|(z,\mu)|\to 0
, and therefore
m_{1}
can be extended to a continuous function on
\overline{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\times\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}}
.

By continuity and compactness, we know that
m_{1}(z,\mu)
is bounded for
|(z,\mu)|\leq R
if we can
show that the denominator in (
6-9
) does not vanish. We set

f(z,\mu):=(\mu^{2}+k+d^{\prime}z^{2})\big(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}z^{2}}+1\big)

and have to show that
f(z,\mu)\not=k
. For this, we first assume that
z
is real. As in this case
f(z,\bar{\mu})=\overline{f(z,\mu)}
, we may assume
\arg(\mu)\in[0,\theta)
. If also
\mu
is real, we
obviously have
f(z,\mu)>k
. If
\arg(\mu)\in(0,\theta)
, we see that
\arg(\mu^{2}+k+d^{\prime}z^{2})\in(0,2\theta)

and
\arg(\sqrt{d\mu^{2}+d^{2}z^{2}}+1)\in(0,\theta)
. Therefore
\arg(f(z,\mu))\in(0,3\theta)

which implies
f(z,\mu)\not\in(0,\infty)
. So we have seen that
f(z,\mu)\not=k
for all

(z,\mu)\in(0,\infty)\times\Sigma_{\theta}
.

For
\alpha\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, we define

g(\alpha):=\min\Big\{|f(re^{i\alpha},\mu)-k|:(r,\mu)\in[0,\infty)\times\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}},\,r^{2}+|\mu|^{2}\leq R^{2}\Big\}.

We have just seen that
g(0)>0
. As the function
g
depends (as the minimum of a continuous function over a compact set) continuously on
\alpha
, there exists some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
such that
g(\alpha)>0
for all
\alpha\in(-\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})
, which finishes the proof of the statement.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

The operator
A
related to the Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Pisconov model and defined in (
6-8
) is
R
-sectorial of angle
\pi
and therefore has maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same steps as in the above examples. We reduce to the case
f=0
and write the solution operator to
(\mu^{2}-A)\binom{u}{v}=\binom{0}{g}
in the form
\gamma_{0}u=R_{1}(\mu)g
and
v=R_{2}(\mu)g
. By Lemma
6.3
and the fact that
-\Delta^{\prime}
has an
R
-bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, we know that
\{\mu^{2}R_{j}(\mu):\mu\in\Sigma_{\theta}\}
is
R
-bounded for all
\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
. Now we can use the
R
-boundedness of the Poisson operator related to the boundary value problem
(\mu^{2}-d\Delta,\gamma_{0})
.
∎

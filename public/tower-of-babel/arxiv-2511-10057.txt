Padé approximations for products of functions
Makoto Kawashima

Abstract
In this article, we construct new Padé approximations for the product of binomial functions and powers of logarithmic functions. While several explicit Padé approximants are known for powers of exponential functions, binomial functions, and logarithmic functions individually, an explicit Padé construction for the product of these functions has not yet been directly achieved. Our main result yields arithmetic applications, providing new linear independence measures for linear forms in ( 1 + α ) ω i ​ log j i ⁡ ( 1 + α ) (1+\alpha)^{\omega_{i}}\log^{j_{i}}(1+\alpha) for 1 ≤ i ≤ m 1\leq i\leq m and 0 ≤ j i ≤ r i − 1 0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1 , where 0 < m , r 1 , … , r m ∈ ℤ ≥ 1 0<m,r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} , ω 1 , … , ω m ∈ ℚ \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}\in\mathbb{Q} , and 0 ≤ ω 1 < ⋯ < ω m < 1 0\leq\omega_{1}<\cdots<\omega_{m}<1 . These results hold with algebraic coefficients in both the complex and p p -adic cases. Additionally, we establish that Padé approximation of a single polylogarithm is, in general, perfect.



Key words and phrases
: Padé approximation, product of functions, perfectness, logarithm, binomial function and polylogarithm.

1
Introduction

As is well-known, Hermite-Padé approximation is a central tool in Diophantine problems, providing explicit and effective results on transcendence
[
10
]
, as well as irrationality
[
29
]
.
Despite its extensive application, explicit Hermite-Padé approximations have been relatively scarce, particularly for functions expressed as products of distinct functions.

In this article, we explicitly construct Padé approximations for the
product
of two distinct hypergeometric functions, namely, binomial functions and powers of logarithmic functions. K. Mahler’s contributions include Padé approximations for powers of exponential functions
[
17
,
19
]
, binomial functions
[
18
]
, and powers of logarithmic functions
[
20
]
, all applied to Diophantine problems. Subsequent refinement of Mahler’s works has been undertaken by various mathematicians, notably G. V. Chudnovsky for binomial functions
[
6
,
5
]
, and for powers of logarithmic functions by Chudnovsky
[
4
]
and E. Reyssat
[
26
]
. M. Huttner
[
14
]
also pursued Padé constructions for specific products through the monodromy method, though without direct arithmetic applications.

Our method takes a completely formal approach, treating every power series formally, which distinguishes it from previous works. While the basic structure of the proof is classical, due to Ch. Hermite, H. E. Padé, and C. L. Siegel
[
30
]
, our new ingredient lies in the explicit construction of Padé approximations for the product of binomial functions and powers of logarithm functions, invoking a linear independence measure of associated numbers. To achieve this construction, the key point is an entire transformation of Padé approximations of exponential functions.

An advantage of our approach, thanks to the perfectness of exponential functions in the sense of Mahler (
confer

[
22
]
), is that we obtain the normality of certain indices of Padé approximants for the product of binomial functions and powers of logarithm functions. This, in turn, leads to the regularity of the matrices associated with Padé approximants (see Corollary
5.2
), which is a crucial step in completing the proof.

We collect here the notation used throughout this article.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of strictly positive integers.
Let
K
be an algebraic number field. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}_{K}
the set of all places of
K
, by
\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{\infty}
the set of infinite places, and by
\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{f}
the set of finite places.

For
v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}
, let
K_{v}
denote the completion of
K
at
v
.
We define the normalized absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
on
K
as follows:

\displaystyle|p|_{v}

\displaystyle=p^{-\tfrac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\quad\text{if }v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{f}\text{ and }v\mid p,

\displaystyle|x|_{v}

\displaystyle=|\iota_{v}x|^{\tfrac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{R}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}\quad\text{if }v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{\infty},

where

Let

We define the (absolute) multiplicative height of

\mathrm{H}(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\prod_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}\max\{1,|\beta_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|\beta_{m}|_{v}\},

and the logarithmic absolute Weil height by

\mathrm{h}(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\log\mathrm{H}(\boldsymbol{\beta}).

We write

\mathrm{h}_{v}(\boldsymbol{\beta}):=\log\max\{1,|\beta_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|\beta_{m}|_{v}\},

so that

\mathrm{h}(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}\mathrm{h}_{v}(\boldsymbol{\beta}).

For a finite set
S
of algebraic numbers, we define its denominator by

\mathrm{den}(S):=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid n\alpha\text{ is an algebraic integer for all }\alpha\in S\right\}.

We now introduce further notation needed to state our main result.
Let
m,r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K
be an algebraic number field, and let
v_{0}
be a place of
K
. Define

, and set

\varepsilon_{v_{0}}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }v_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{\infty},\\

For

, let

denote the rational prime below

.

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}
with

.
Define the following real-valued functions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}-1\right)\mathrm{h}_{v_{0}}(\alpha)-\frac{\varepsilon_{v_{0}}[K_{v_{0}}:\mathbb{R}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\,\mathrm{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle\qquad-(\varepsilon_{v_{0}}-1)\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p_{v_{0}}\nmid\mathrm{den}(\omega_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\right)p_{v_{0}}}{p_{v_{0}}-1}\log|p_{v_{0}}|_{v_{0}}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{h}(\alpha)+\mathrm{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right)-\mathrm{h}_{v_{0}}(\alpha)-\frac{\varepsilon_{v_{0}}[K_{v_{0}}:\mathbb{R}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\,\mathrm{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}),

\displaystyle U_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{h}_{v_{0}}(\alpha)+\frac{\varepsilon_{v_{0}}[K_{v_{0}}:\mathbb{R}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\,\mathrm{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}),

\displaystyle V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{A}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)-\mathbb{B}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha),

where

\mathrm{C}(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}+1\right)\log 2+3r\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq m}\left(\dfrac{{\rm{den}}(\omega_{i_{2}}-\omega_{i_{1}})}{\varphi({\rm{den}}(\omega_{i_{2}}-\omega_{i_{1}}))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq{\rm{den}}(\omega_{i_{1}}-\omega_{i_{2}})\\
(j,{\rm{den}}(\omega_{i_{2}}-\omega_{i_{1}}))=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{j}\right)+r.

We can now state our main theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume

\displaystyle|\alpha|_{v_{0}}>\begin{cases}1&\text{if }v_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{\infty}\text{ or }v_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}^{f}\text{ and }p_{v_{0}}\nmid\mathrm{den}(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}),\\
|p_{v_{0}}|_{v_{0}}^{-p_{v_{0}}/(p_{v_{0}}-1)}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)>0.

Then for any
0<\varepsilon<V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)
, there exists an effectively computable constant
H_{0}=H_{0}(\varepsilon)
depending only on
\varepsilon
and the given data such that the following holds.

For any
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{i,j_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1}\in K^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

where

\displaystyle\mu(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{A}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)+U_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)}{V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)-\varepsilon},

\displaystyle C(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\exp\left[-\frac{\left(V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)-\varepsilon+\log 2\right)\left(\mathbb{B}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)+U_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)+\varepsilon\right)}{V_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)-\varepsilon}\right].

Remark 2.2
.

When
\alpha

In particular, for any number field

of

3
Padé Approximation

In this section, we recall the definition and basic properties of Padé approximations of formal power series. Throughout, we fix a field
K
of characteristic
0
.
We define the order function at
z=0
by

{\rm ord}:K((z))\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\};\quad\sum_{k}a_{k}z^{k}\mapsto\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}\mid a_{k}\neq 0\}\kern 5.0pt.

Note that for
f\in K((z))
, we have
{\rm ord}\,f=\infty
if and only if
f=0
.

3.1
Padé approximations of formal power series

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m
be an integer with
m\geq 2
, and let
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z))\in K[[z]]^{m}
. For
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
, there exists a vector of polynomials
(P_{1}(z),\ldots,P_{m}(z))\in K[z]^{m}
satisfying:

\displaystyle(i)\quad(P_{1}(z),\ldots,P_{m}(z))\neq(0,\ldots,0)\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\deg P_{j}(z)\leq n_{j}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle(iii)\quad{\rm ord}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}P_{j}(z)f_{j}(z)\right)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}(n_{j}+1)-1\kern 5.0pt.

Definition 3.2
.

A tuple of polynomials
(P_{1}(z),\ldots,P_{m}(z))\in K[z]^{m}
satisfying conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
, and
(iii)
is called a Padé system of approximants of
\boldsymbol{f}
of weight
\boldsymbol{n}
.

The power series
\sum_{j=1}^{m}P_{j}(z)f_{j}(z)
is referred to as a Padé approximation of
\boldsymbol{f}
of weight
\boldsymbol{n}
(also called the
remainder
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z))\in K[[z]]^{m}
.

(i)

For
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
, we say that
\boldsymbol{n}
is
normal
with respect to
\boldsymbol{f}
if every Padé approximation
R(z)
of weight
\boldsymbol{n}
satisfies

{\rm ord}\,R(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(n_{j}+1)-1\kern 5.0pt.

(ii)

We say that
\boldsymbol{f}
is
perfect
if every index
\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
is normal with respect to
\boldsymbol{f}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
and
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z))\in K[[z]]^{m}
. Let
N=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(n_{j}+1)
and write
f_{j}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{j,k}z^{k}
for

.

For a non-negative integer

, define the generalized Hankel matrix of

as

H_{r,\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{f})=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1,0}&0&\dots&0&\dots&f_{m,0}&0&\dots&0\\
f_{1,1}&f_{1,0}&\dots&0&\dots&f_{m,1}&f_{m,0}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f_{1,r}&f_{1,r-1}&\dots&f_{1,r-n_{1}}&\dots&f_{m,r}&f_{m,r-1}&\dots&f_{m,r-n_{m}}\end{pmatrix}\in{\rm M}_{r+1,N}(K)\kern 5.0pt,

where
f_{j,k}=0
for

. Then the following map is a bijection:

(1)

\displaystyle\phi^{(\boldsymbol{n})}_{\boldsymbol{f}}:

\displaystyle\ \ker(H_{N-2,\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{f}))\setminus\{\mathbb{0}\}\rightarrow\left\{(P_{j}(z))\in K[z]^{m}\,\middle|\,\sum_{j=1}^{m}P_{j}(z)f_{j}(z)\text{ is a Pad\'{e} approximation of weight }\boldsymbol{n}\right\}

\displaystyle{}^{t}(p_{1,0},\ldots,p_{1,n_{1}},\ldots,p_{m,0},\ldots,p_{m,n_{m}})\mapsto\left(P_{j}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{j}}p_{j,k}z^{k}\right)_{1\leq j\leq m}\kern 5.0pt.

Note that
\boldsymbol{n}
is normal with respect to

if and only if

.

Let

,
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z))\in K[[z]]^{m}

3.3
Padé approximations of product of binomial functions and power of logarithm function

In this section, we formulate Padé approximations for the product of binomial functions and the power of the logarithm function.
M. Huttner explored this type of approximation within the context of the Riemann monodromy problem (

\mathcal{T}:K[[z]]\longrightarrow K[[z]];\ f(z)\mapsto f(\log(1+z))\kern 5.0pt.

for

, along with positive integers

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}(i)=(\underbrace{n_{i,1},\ldots,n_{i,1}}_{s_{i,1}},\ldots,\underbrace{n_{i,l_{i}},\ldots,n_{i,l_{i}}}_{s_{i,l_{i}}})\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{i}}\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}(i)=(\underbrace{r_{i}-1,\ldots,r_{i}-1}_{n_{i,l_{i}}+1},\underbrace{r_{i}-s_{i,l_{i}}-1,\ldots,r_{i}-s_{i,l_{i}}-1}_{n_{i,l_{i}-1}-n_{i,l_{i}}},\ldots,\underbrace{s_{i,1}-1,\ldots,s_{i,1}-1}_{n_{i,1}-n_{i,2}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{i,1}+1}\kern 5.0pt,

\boldsymbol{n}=(\boldsymbol{n}(1),\ldots,\boldsymbol{n}(m))\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}},\ \ \boldsymbol{r}=(\boldsymbol{r}(1),\ldots,\boldsymbol{r}(m))\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1,1}+\cdots+n_{m,1}+m}\kern 5.0pt.

Denote the set of weight

We are now prepared to study weight-
\boldsymbol{n}
Padé approximants for the product of binomial functions and powers of the logarithmic function. The key ingredient is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.8
.

We retain the notation as previously established. The
K
-isomorphism
\mathcal{T}
defines a bijection:

\mathcal{T}:V_{\boldsymbol{r}}\longrightarrow W_{\boldsymbol{n}}.

In particular, the index
\boldsymbol{n}
is normal with respect to the system

((1+z)^{\omega_{i}}\log^{j_{i}}(1+z))_{1\leq i\leq m,\,0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1}.

Proof..

Let
\mathfrak{R}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{n_{i,1}}\mathfrak{P}_{i,k_{i}}(z)e^{(\omega_{i}+k_{i})z}\in V_{\boldsymbol{r}}
.
By Proposition
3.6
, we have

\mathrm{ord}\,\mathfrak{R}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{v_{i}=1}^{l_{i}}(n_{i,v_{i}}+1)s_{i,v_{i}}.

Applying equation (
2
), we obtain

\mathrm{ord}\,\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{R}(z))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{v_{i}=1}^{l_{i}}(n_{i,v_{i}}+1)s_{i,v_{i}}.

We claim that there exists a vector of polynomials
(P_{i,j_{i}}(z))_{1\leq i\leq m,\,0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1}\in K[z]^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{R}(z))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{r_{i}-1}P_{i,j_{i}}(z)(1+z)^{\omega_{i}}\log^{j_{i}}(1+z),

\displaystyle\deg P_{i,j_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq n_{i,j_{i}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m,\,0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1.

This implies
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{R}(z))\in W_{\boldsymbol{n}}
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is linear, we may assume
i=1
and define
\omega_{1}=\omega
,
r_{1}=r
,
l_{1}=l
,
s_{1,j}=s_{j}
,
n_{1,j}=n_{j}
(for
1\leq j\leq l
), and
\mathfrak{P}_{1,k}(z)=\mathfrak{P}_{k}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}
.
Then we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}}\mathfrak{P}_{k}(z)e^{(\omega+k)z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}+\sum_{k=n_{l}+1}^{n_{l-1}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{r-s_{l}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\sum_{k=n_{3}+1}^{n_{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}+\sum_{k=n_{2+1}}^{n_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s_{1}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s_{1}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}}\left(\sum_{j=s_{1}}^{s_{2}+s_{1}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l-1}}\left(\sum_{j=r-s_{l}-s_{l-1}}^{r-s_{l}-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l}}\left(\sum_{j=r-s_{l}}^{r-1}p_{j,k}z^{j}\right)e^{(\omega+k)z}\kern 4.62497pt.

Acting
\mathcal{T}
on the both sides of the above equalities,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}}\mathfrak{P}_{k}(z)e^{(\omega+k)z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s_{1}-1}p_{j,k}\log^{j}(1+z)\right)(1+z)^{\omega+k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}}\left(\sum_{j=s_{1}}^{s_{2}+s_{1}-1}p_{j,k}\log^{j}(1+z)\right)(1+z)^{\omega+k}+\cdots

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l-1}}\left(\sum_{j=r-s_{l}-s_{l-1}}^{r-s_{l}-1}p_{j,k}\log^{j}(1+z)\right)(1+z)^{\omega+k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l}}\left(\sum_{j=r-s_{l}}^{r-1}p_{j,k}\log^{j}(1+z)\right)(1+z)^{\omega+k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{s_{1}-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}}{p_{j,k}(1+z)^{k}}\right)(1+z)^{\omega}\log^{j}(1+z)+\sum_{j=s_{1}}^{s_{2}+s_{1}-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}}{p_{j,k}(1+z)^{k}}\right)(1+z)^{\omega}\log^{j}(1+z)

\displaystyle+\cdots+\sum_{j=r-s_{l}}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n_{l}}{p_{j,k}(1+z)^{k}}\right)(1+z)^{\omega}\log^{j}(1+z)\kern 4.62497pt.

By tracing the above equalities in reverse, we also conclude that

\mathcal{T}^{-1}(R(z))\in V_{\boldsymbol{r}}\ \text{for}\ R(z)\in W_{\boldsymbol{n}}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.8
.
∎

Applying Proposition

Corollary 3.9
.

We use the same notation as above. Put

for some

and

Define a rational function

(3)

\displaystyle F_{k}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{u}\prod_{h=0}^{n+1}(x-\omega_{i}-h)^{r_{i}}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{(x-\omega_{u+1}-n-1)^{s_{u+1}}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\prod_{i^{\prime}={u+1}}^{m}\prod_{h^{\prime}=0}^{n}(x-\omega_{i^{\prime}}-h^{\prime})^{r_{i^{\prime}}}}\kern 5.0pt.

Let

(4)

\displaystyle F_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{h=0}^{n+1}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{r_{i}}\dfrac{p_{k,h,i,j_{i}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{(x-\omega_{i}-h)^{j_{i}}}\kern 5.0pt.

\displaystyle P_{k,i,j_{i}}(z)=\sum_{h=0}^{n+1}\dfrac{p_{k,h,i,j_{i}+1}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{j_{i}!}(1+z)^{h}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1\kern 5.0pt.

0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1\end{subarray}}
is a weight
\boldsymbol{n}_{k}
Padé approximant of

Proof..

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}(i)=\begin{cases}(n+1,\ldots,n+1)\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{i}}&\ \text{if}\ 1\leq i\leq u\\
(\underbrace{n+1,\ldots,n+1}_{s_{u+1}},n,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{u+1}}&\ \text{if}\ i=u+1\\
(n,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{i}}&\ \text{if}\ u+2\leq i\leq m\kern 5.0pt,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}(i)=\begin{cases}(r_{i}-1,\ldots,r_{i}-1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+2}&\ \text{if}\ 1\leq i\leq u\\
(r_{u+1}-1,\ldots,r_{u+1}-1,s_{u+1}-1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+2}&\ \text{if}\ i=u+1\\
(r_{i}-1,\ldots,r_{i}-1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}&\ \text{if}\ u+2\leq i\leq m\kern 5.0pt.\end{cases}

Then the indice
\boldsymbol{n}_{k}
is
(\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}(1),\ldots,\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}(m))
.
Put

\mathfrak{P}_{k,i,h}(z)=\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{r_{i}-1}\dfrac{p_{k,h,i,j_{i}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{j_{i}!}z^{j_{i}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq h\leq n+1\kern 5.0pt.

By Proposition
3.7
, the vector of polynomials
(\mathfrak{P}_{k,i,h}(z))
is a weight

Padé approximant of

Put

\mathfrak{R}_{k}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{u+1}\sum_{h=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{P}_{k,i,h}(z)e^{(\omega_{i}+h)z}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=u+2}^{m}\sum_{h^{\prime}=0}^{n}\mathfrak{P}_{k,i^{\prime},h^{\prime}}(z)e^{(\omega_{i^{\prime}}+h^{\prime})z}\kern 5.0pt.

Subsequently, we find that

and, based on Proposition
3.8
, conclude

\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{R}_{k}(z))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{r_{i}-1}P_{k,i,j_{i}}(z)(1+z)^{\omega_{i}}\log^{j_{i}}(1+z)\in W_{\boldsymbol{n}_{k}}\kern 5.0pt.

This completes the proof of Corollary
3.9
.
∎

Lemma 4.1

Let

where second one is for

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}\\

Proof..

Fix

and
0\leq h\leq n+1
. Put

R=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle{\min_{1\leq i\leq m-1}}}\{\omega_{i+1}-\omega_{i}\}&\ \ \text{if}\ m\geq 2\kern 5.0pt,\\
1&\ \ \text{if}\ m=1\kern 5.0pt.\end{cases}

By the residue theorem,

(6)

\displaystyle p_{k,h,i,j_{i}+1}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i,h}}(\zeta-\omega_{i}-h)^{j_{i}}F_{k}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle F_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{m}\prod_{h^{\prime}=0}^{n+1}(x-\omega_{i^{\prime}}-h^{\prime})^{r_{i^{\prime}}}}\kern 5.0pt,

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{C}\\
|x-\omega_{i}-h|=R/2\end{subarray}}\left|\dfrac{1}{\prod_{h^{\prime}=0}^{n+1}(x-\omega_{i^{\prime}}-h^{\prime})^{r_{i^{\prime}}}}\right|\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i^{\prime}\leq m\kern 5.0pt.

\rm{\,I\,}

and

\displaystyle=|x-\omega_{i}-h+h-h^{\prime}|\geq||h-h^{\prime}|-|x-\omega_{i}-h||\geq\begin{cases}|h-h^{\prime}|-1/2&\ \ \text{if}\ h^{\prime}\neq h\\

Using the equality

\displaystyle\dfrac{h!(n+1-h)!}{(2h)!(2(n+1-h))!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2(n+1))!}{(2h)!(2(n+1-h))!}\cdot\dfrac{(n+1)!(n+1)!}{(2(n+1))!}\cdot\dfrac{h!(n+1-h)!}{(n+1)!}\cdot\dfrac{1}{(n+1)!}

and the inequality

(see Proof of

(7)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{C}\\

\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{C}\\

Case
\rm{I\hskip-1.49994ptI\hskip-1.49994ptI}
:
i^{\prime}<i
.

By the similar arguments in the case of
i^{\prime}>i

(\ref{upper Rrj})
and
(\ref{upper Anrj})

\displaystyle\log\left|B_{n}\right|_{v}\leq{\rm{h}}_{v}({{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})+(m-1){{F_{v}}}(n)+{\varepsilon_{v}\log\,(m!)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ v\in{{\mathfrak{M}}}_{K}\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\log\left|\sum_{l\neq l_{n}}r^{(n)}_{l}B_{n,l,1}\right|_{v_{0}}\leq-\mathbb{A}n+(m-2)F_{v_{0}}(n)+{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+o(n)\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\log\left|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})B_{n,l_{n},1}\right|_{v_{0}}\leq\log|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})|_{v_{0}}+(m-1)F_{v_{0}}(n)+\varepsilon_{v_{0}}\log(m-1)!\kern 5.0pt.

Now we fix a positive number
\varepsilon
with
V>\varepsilon
, and assume that
n
is large enough so that

\left|o(n)\right|\leq\varepsilon n/4
,
[K:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot{\rm{log}}(m!)\leq\varepsilon n/4
,
0<-\log(2)+n(V-\varepsilon)
and

\displaystyle\left|\left((m-1){{\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}}}F_{v}(n)-F_{v_{0}}(n)\right)-n\mathbb{B}\right|\leq\varepsilon n/4,\ \ \ \ \left|nU-F_{v_{0}}(n)\right|\leq\varepsilon n/4\kern 5.0pt.

Fix such
n^{*}
and take
h_{0}=-\log(2)+n^{*}(V-\varepsilon)
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in K^{m+1}\setminus\{\mathbb{0}\}
with
{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\geq h_{0}
.
Let
n=n(\boldsymbol{\beta})
be the minimal positive integer such that

{\rm{h}}({{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})-n(V-\varepsilon)\leq-{\rm{log}}(2)
.
Note that
n\geq n^{*}
and

(23)

\displaystyle n<\dfrac{{\rm{h}}({{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})}{V-\varepsilon}+\dfrac{\log(2)}{V-\varepsilon}+1\kern 5.0pt.

With these conventions,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\neq v_{0}}\log|B_{n}|_{v}+\log\left|\sum_{l\neq l_{n}}r^{(n)}_{l}B_{n,l,1}\right|_{v_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq-\mathbb{A}n+(m-1)\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}F_{v}(n)-F_{v_{0}}(n)+{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+\varepsilon n/2

\displaystyle\leq n(-\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}+\varepsilon)+{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})

\displaystyle=-n(V-\varepsilon)+{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})<-\log(2)\kern 5.0pt,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{v\neq v_{0}}\log|B_{n}|_{v}+\log\left|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})B_{n,l_{n},1}\right|_{v_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\log|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})|_{v_{0}}+(m-1)\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}F_{v}(n)+{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})-{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+\varepsilon n/2

\displaystyle\leq\log|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})|_{v_{0}}+n(\mathbb{B}+U+\varepsilon)+{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})-{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\kern 5.0pt.

Plugging in this inequality into Equation (
22
),

\displaystyle-\log(2)-n(\mathbb{B}+U+\varepsilon)-{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\leq\log|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})|_{v_{0}}\kern 5.0pt.

Using Equation
(\ref{ineq n})
,

\displaystyle\log|\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})|_{v_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq-\log(2)-\left(\dfrac{{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+\log(2)}{V-\varepsilon}+1\right)(\mathbb{B}+U+\varepsilon)-{\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})

\displaystyle=\log C(\varepsilon)-\mu(\varepsilon){\rm{h}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})+{\rm{h}}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\kern 5.0pt.

This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

5.3
Proof of Main theorem

We are ready to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We use the same notation as in Theorem
2.1
.
Let
\alpha\in K\setminus\{0,-1\}
.
For a non-negative integer
n
, we define matrix

{{M}}_{n}=(D_{n+1}\alpha^{n+1}P_{k,i,j_{i}}(\alpha^{-1}))_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}r_{i}\\
1\leq i\leq m,0\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1\end{subarray}}\in{\rm{Mat}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}}(K)\kern 5.0pt,

where
D_{n+1}
is the positive integer defined in
(\ref{E_n})
.
By Corollary
5.2
, the matrices
{{M}}_{n}
are invertible for every

.
We define a family of functions

\displaystyle F_{v}:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\ \ n\mapsto

\displaystyle(n+1){\rm{h}}_{v}(\alpha,\alpha+1)+\varepsilon_{v}o(n)+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{v}(r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m})[K_{v}:\mathbb{R}]n}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log(2)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{v}r\log\left|\left[\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq m}D_{n+2}(\omega_{i_{2}}-\omega_{i_{1}})^{2}d_{n+2}(\omega_{i_{2}}-\omega_{i_{1}})\right]d_{n+1}\right|_{v}\kern 5.0pt,

for
v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}
.
For
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
, by
[
2
]
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\log\,\max\{D_{n}(\omega),d_{n}(\omega)\}\leq\dfrac{{\rm{den}}(\omega)}{\varphi({\rm{den}}(\omega))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq{\rm{den}}(\omega)\\
(j,{\rm{den}}(\omega))=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{j}\kern 5.0pt,

where

is Euler’s totient function.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\left((r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}-1)\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}}F_{v}(n)-F_{v_{0}}(n)\right)=\mathbb{B}_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{F_{v_{0}}(n)}{n}=U_{v_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\alpha)\kern 5.0pt,

and, by Lemma
4.4
,

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}\\
1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j_{i}\leq r_{i}-1\end{subarray}}\log|D_{n+1}\alpha^{n+1}P_{k,i,j_{i}}(\alpha^{-1})|_{v}\leq F_{v}(n)\ \ \text{for}\ \ v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}\kern 5.0pt.

6.1
Padé approximants of Laurent series

In this subsection, let us introduce the Padé approximation of a Laurent series which is slightly different concept from the Padé approximations of power series considered in Section
3
.
Throughout this subsection, we denote by
K
a field of characteristic
0
.
We denote the formal power series ring of variable
1/z
with coefficients
K
by
K[[1/z]]
and the field of fractions by
K((1/z))
. We say an element of
K((1/z))
is a formal Laurent series.
Let
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}/z^{k+1}\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
n
a non-negative integer.
We define a
K
-homomorphism
\varphi_{f}
associated with
f
by

\varphi_{f}:K[t]\longrightarrow K;\ \ t^{k}\mapsto f_{k}\ \ (k\geq 0)\kern 5.0pt.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(P,Q)\in K[z]^{2}
be a pair of polynomials.
We say the pair
(P,Q)
is a weight
n
Padé approximants of
f(z)
if
(P,Q)
satisfies

\displaystyle(i)\ \ P(z)\neq 0\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle(ii)\ \ {\rm{deg}}\,P\leq n\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle(iii)\ \ {\rm{ord}}_{\infty}\,P(z)f(z)-Q(z)\geq n+1\kern 5.0pt.

We denote the set of weight
n
Padé approximants of
f
by
{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
.
Let
(P,Q)\in{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
.
Note that the polynomial
Q
is uniquely determined such as
Q(z)=\varphi_{f}(\tfrac{P(z)-P(t)}{z-t})
.
We denote the reminder function
P(z)f(z)-Q(z)
of
(P,Q)\in{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
by
R_{P}(z)
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer.
We say
n
is normal with respect to
f
if and only if, for any
(P,Q)\in{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
, we have
{\rm{ord}}_{\infty}\,R_{P}=n+1
.
We denote the set of non-negative integers which are normal with respect to
f
by
\Lambda(f)
.

Remark 6.3
.

Let us describe the relationship between Padé approximants defined in Definition
6.1
and that in Definition
3.1
.
Let
f(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
.
To give a pair in
{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
is equivalent to give a weight
(n,n-1)
Padé approximants of
(1/z\cdot f(1/z),1)
.
More precisely, the map

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ {\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)\longrightarrow\{(P_{1},P_{2})\in K[z]^{2}\mid(P_{1},P_{2})\ \text{is a weight}\ (n,n-1)\ \text{Pad\'{e} approximants of}\ (1/z\cdot f(1/z),1)\};

\displaystyle(P(z),Q(z))\mapsto(z^{n}P(1/z),-z^{n-1}Q(1/z))\kern 5.0pt,

is bijective. This yields that a non-negative integer
n
is normal with respect to
f
is equivalent to the pair
(n,n-1)
is normal with respect to
(1/z\cdot f(1/z),1)
in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
m,n
be non-negative integers. Define the
(m,n)
-Hankel matrix of
f
by

H_{m,n}(f)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}&f_{1}&\cdots&f_{n}\\
f_{1}&f_{2}&\ldots&f_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f_{m-1}&f_{m}&\ldots&f_{m+n}\end{pmatrix}\in{\rm{Mat}}_{m,n+1}(K)\kern 5.0pt.

The similar argument to that in Remark
3.4
leads us to get the following equivalence relations.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}z^{k}\in K[z]
be a nonzero polynomial. Put
Q(z)=\varphi_{f}(\tfrac{P(z)-P(t)}{z-t})
.
For a non-negative integer
n
, the following are equivalent.

(i)
We have
(P,Q)\in{\rm{PA}}_{n}(f)
.

(ii)
The vector
{}^{t}(p_{0},\ldots,p_{n})\in K^{n}
is contained in the kernel of the
K
-linear map

H_{n,n}(f):K^{n+1}\longrightarrow K^{n};\ \ \vec{x}\mapsto H_{n,n}(f)\cdot\vec{x}\kern 5.0pt.

The similar argument to the proof of Lemma
3.5
leads us to conclude that the given non-negative integer
n
being normal with respect to
f(z)
(
confer

[
25
]
).

Lemma 6.6
.

For
f(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and a non-negative integer
n
the following are equivalent.

(i)

n\in\Lambda(f)
.

(ii)

{\rm{det}}\,H_{n+1,n}(f)\neq 0
.

6.2
Perfectness of polylogarithm function

Let
r
be a positive integer. We denote the
r
-th polylogarithm function by
{\rm{Li}}_{r}(z)
, namely

{\rm{Li}}_{r}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{k+1}}{(k+1)^{r}}\in\mathbb{Q}[[z]]\kern 5.0pt.

In this subsection, we prove the perfectness of
{\rm{Li}}_{r}(1/z)
.
Denote the
(n,n-1)
-Hankel matrix of
{\rm{Li}}_{r}(1/z)
by

H^{(r)}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2^{r}&\cdots&1/n^{r}\\
1/2^{r}&1/3^{r}&\ldots&1/(n+1)^{r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1/n^{r}&1/(n+1)^{r}&\ldots&1/(2n)^{r}\end{pmatrix}\kern 5.0pt.

Proposition 6.7
.

{\rm{det}}\,H^{(r)}_{n}\neq 0.

Proof..

Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
be variables.
Define
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps:

\varphi^{(i)}_{r}:\mathbb{Q}[t_{i}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q};\ \ t^{k}_{i}\mapsto\dfrac{1}{(r-1)!}\int^{1}_{0}t^{k}_{i}\log^{r-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{t_{i}}\right)dt_{i}=\dfrac{1}{(k+1)^{r}}\ \ \ (1\leq i\leq n)\kern 5.0pt.

According to the definition of
\varphi^{(i)}_{r}
,

\displaystyle{\rm{det}}\,H^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\varphi^{(1)}_{r}(1)&\varphi^{(2)}_{r}(t_{2})&\cdots&\varphi^{(n)}_{r}(t^{n-1}_{n})\\
\varphi^{(1)}_{r}(t_{1})&\varphi^{(2)}_{r}(t^{2}_{2})&\cdots&\varphi^{(n)}_{r}(t^{n}_{n})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi^{(1)}_{r}(t^{n-1}_{1})&\varphi^{(2)}_{r}(t^{n}_{2})&\cdots&\varphi^{(n)}_{r}(t^{2n-1}_{n})\\
\end{vmatrix}=\varphi^{(1)}_{r}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi^{(n)}_{r}\left(\begin{vmatrix}1&t_{2}&\cdots&t^{n-1}_{n}\\
t_{1}&t^{2}_{2}&\cdots&t^{n}_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t^{n-1}_{1}&t^{n}_{2}&\cdots&t^{2n-1}_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{(1)}_{r}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi^{(n)}_{r}\left(t_{2}t^{2}_{3}\cdots t^{n-1}_{n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{[(r-1)!]^{n}}\int_{[0,1]^{n}}t_{2}t^{2}_{3}\cdots t^{n-1}_{n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{t_{i}}\right)dt_{i}\kern 5.0pt.

We denote the
n
-th symmetric group by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and put

\displaystyle D_{\sigma}=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}\mid t_{\sigma(1)}\leq\cdots\leq t_{\sigma(n)}\}\ \text{for}\ \sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\kern 5.0pt.

Denoting
g(\boldsymbol{t})=t_{2}t^{2}_{3}\cdots t^{n-1}_{n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(1/t_{i}\right)
and
d\mu
of Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{n}

and using the change of variables

\sigma^{-1}:\kern 5.0ptD_{{\rm{id}}}\longrightarrow D_{\sigma},\ (t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto(t_{\sigma^{-1}(n)},\ldots,t_{\sigma^{-1}(n)})\kern 5.0pt,

\int_{[0,1]^{n}}gd\mu=\int_{D_{{\rm{id}}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}g\circ\sigma^{-1}d\mu\kern 5.0pt.

Combining
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-t_{\sigma^{-1}(i)})={\rm{sgn}}(\sigma)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})
and remark (Vandermonde)
yields

\displaystyle{\rm{det}}\,H^{(r)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{[(r-1)!]^{n}}\int_{D_{{\rm{id}}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}{\rm{sgn}}(\sigma)t^{0}_{\sigma(1)}t_{\sigma(2)}\cdots t^{n-1}_{\sigma(n)}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{t_{i}}\right)dt_{i}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{[(r-1)!]^{n}}\int_{D_{{\rm{id}}}}\left[\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\right]^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{t_{i}}\right)dt_{i}>0\kern 5.0pt.

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Combining Proposition
6.7
and Lemma
6.6
yields the perfectness of
{\rm{Li}}_{r}(1/z)
.

Correlations of Free Beta-Human Chorionic Gonadotropin/Pregnancy-Associated Plasma Protein A Ratio, Pregnancy Characteristics, and Placental Pathology in Fetal Growth Restriction
Authors: István Dankó, András Tankó, Edit Kelemen, Béla Temesváry, Gábor Cserni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveWe evaluated the clinicopathological correlations of maternal serum free beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (FBHCG)/pregnancy-associated plasma protein A (PAPP-A) multiple of the median ratio measured between 11 + 0 and 13 + 6 weeks of gestation, pregnancy characteristics, and placental histopathology in pregnancies affected by fetal growth restriction (FGR).Subjects and MethodsWe investigated 69 pregnancies complicated with FGR. Different indicators of FGR, placental villous capillarization, placental weight to birth weight ratio, and placental histopathological entities were correlated with FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio.ResultsComplication of FGR with preeclampsia (p < 0.001) and birth weight below the 2nd percentile (p < 0.001) were significantly more common when the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was higher. Placental weight to birth weight ratio was significantly lower (<1:8) in cases of higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio. Distal villous hypoplasia (DVH), avascular villi (AV), and chorangiosis were more prevalent in cases with a higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio; however, villous capillarization, onset of FGR, oligohydramnios, and preterm birth did not have an association with it.ConclusionIn FGR, a higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could be an early indicator of placental villous underdevelopment, which is further supported by the fact that higher prevalence of DVH and AV was described in cases with a higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio. We conclude that this ratio could be an early marker of potential placental dysfunction (and consecutive FGR later in pregnancy), rendering it a helpful marker as early as the end of the first trimester for identifying pregnancies that warrant close monitoring throughout gestation.Keywords: Fetal growth restriction, Histopathology, Preeclampsia, Placenta

Introduction

Fetal growth restriction (FGR) is a serious complication of pregnancy with a significant impact on perinatal mortality and morbidity [1, 2], with a long-term impact on the newborn after birth [3]. FGR is often complicated by preeclampsia (PE) [4], especially in its early-onset (EO) subtype. As FGR and PE are precarious complications of pregnancy, timely diagnosis and adequate management of these conditions are essential in perinatology and obstetrics.

Levels of free beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (FBHCG) and pregnancy-associated plasma protein A (PAPP-A) measured in maternal serum between 11 + 0 and 13 + 6 weeks + days of pregnancy are widely used in aneuploidy screening of pregnancies [5, 6], and it is also known that high levels of FBHCG and low levels of PAPP-A are associated with adverse perinatal outcomes [7, 8]. FBHCG and PAPP-A levels measured in multiple of the median (MoM) in the late first and early second trimester of pregnancy have been associated with the subsequent development of FGR [9] and therefore may be important in identifying high-risk pregnancies. Histopathological evaluation of the placenta after birth is a key element in the study and further understanding of the pathophysiology of FGR, as the condition is characterized by several placental changes [10]. In this study, we analyzed the clinicopathological correlations of the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio, pregnancy characteristics and placental histopathology in pregnancies affected by FGR.

Subjects and Methods

We studied FGR cases with a delivery in our tertiary medical center between 2007 and 2024, with a retrospective approach. As placental histopathological examinations at our center were performed based on the individual discretion of the attending obstetricians, relatively few FGR cases from the past 20 years had the available placental histopathological findings and biochemical marker (FBHCG and PAPP-A) data, while also meeting the strict inclusion and classification criteria used in the present study.

Maternal serum FBHCG and PAPP-A levels from the period between the 11 + 0 and 13 + 6 weeks + days of the pregnancy were available (they were performed as part of the first trimester genetic screening). Gestational age was clarified in each case using the crown-rump length of the embryo measured by ultrasound in the first trimester. Placental histopathological examination after birth was performed in all cases.

FBHCG and PAPP-A levels were measured by the laboratory unit of our hospital by the same analytical method during the study period; FBHCG (measured in mIU/mL) and PAPP-A (measured in micrograms/mL) values were converted to MoM by the PRISCA Prenatal Risk Calculation Software (version 4.0, Siemens Healthcare Diagnostics). MoM was adjusted to maternal tobacco smoking, maternal weight, and absence or presence of diabetes mellitus [11]. In each case, we calculated the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio from the MoM values. The corresponding clinical data were retrieved from the medical network system.

Birth weight and placental weight percentiles were assigned for the actual gestational age [12, 13]. The corresponding placental histology slides were retrieved from the archives of the pathology department, and they were studied retrospectively according to a uniform scheme, by the same observer. The studied placental histopathological entities were villous infarction, distal villous hypoplasia (DVH), perivillous fibrin deposition, avascular villi (AV), villitis of unknown etiology, and chorangiosis. The entities were diagnosed based on the Amsterdam Placental Workshop Group Consensus Statement [14], while chorangiosis was diagnosed following the criteria of Altshuler [15]. Besides placental histopathological entities, capillary-villus ratio (CVR) was determined in each placenta according to our own method: ten nonoverlapping areas of the placenta were evaluated under high power magnification, and 100 adjacent villi were counted in each area. CVR was calculated in randomly selected areas from the central part of placental parenchyma. During this, a central terminal villous was randomly selected, which was surrounded by a population of other terminal villi; with a clockwise approach, 100 neighboring terminal villi were evaluated. The number of intact capillaries in these adjacent villi was determined: CVR was calculated using the number of capillaries (identified on hematoxylin-eosin stains) in these villi (CVR = capillary number of terminal villi/number of villi).

Only singleton pregnancies beyond the 24th week of gestation at the time of birth were enrolled into this study. Exclusion criteria included twin pregnancies, indeterminate gestational age, genetic or structural abnormalities of the fetus (aneuploid pregnancies were not analyzed), intrauterine infection diagnosed before or after delivery, and disorders of placental implantation. The diagnosis of FGR and PE was made after explicit diagnostic criteria [16, 17]. EO and late-onset (LO) FGR were distinguished [18, 19]. We analyzed the possible associations between FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio and perinatal features, notably onset of FGR, complication of FGR by PE, preterm versus term delivery, mode of delivery, birth weight ≤2nd percentile versus >2nd percentile, presence or absence of hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy of the offspring after birth, maternal tobacco smoking, presence or absence of oligohydramnios, and live birth versus stillbirth.

The possible connections between FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio and CVR of the placenta were analyzed in the subgroups of CVR <3, 3–5, and >5. Statistical analyses of the correlation of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio and placental weight to birth weight ratio (>1:6, 1:6–1:8, <1:8) were also performed. The association between placental histopathological entities and FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was also studied in the three subgroups of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio <0.5, 0.5–1.5, and >1.5.

The Mann-Whitney U test was applied for comparing subgroups of the studied cohort for significant differences of the values of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio. As multiple variables were included in the analyses, and some subgroups had a relatively small sample size, the Bonferroni correction was applied. As we analyzed multiple outcomes, and the dataset was tested repeatedly, we performed false discovery rate calculations by determining Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p values as a correction for multiple comparisons.

The Kruskal-Wallis H test was used for comparing values of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio in three subgroups of the study population for different ranges of CVR and in three subgroups of the cohort subdivided by placental weight to birth weight ratio. Following the Kruskal-Wallis H test, we applied Dunn’s post hoc test to compare distributions between different subgroups.

For the analysis of the connections of placental changes and values of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio, the chi-square or the Fisher’s exact tests (for case numbers <10) were applied. Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 17.0 (SPSS Inc. Released 2008. SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 17.0. Chicago: SPSS Inc.), Microsoft Office Excel (Microsoft Office Excel 2007) and online statistical applications (available at vassarstats.net); a p < 0.05 value was used for defining statistical significance.

Results

Sixty-nine pregnancies were studied; mean ± SD FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was 0.9 ± 0.65 in the cohort (median: 0.69; interquartile range: 0.81). Thirty-eight cases were EO-FGR. Complication of pregnancy with both FGR and PE was prevalent (43.5%), and preterm delivery was common (49.3%).

Mean ± SD, median value, and interquartile range FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was calculated in different subgroups of the study cohort of FGR pregnancies (Table 1), and the results of Mann-Whitney U test are presented in Table 1. The values of FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio were significantly higher in cases of FGR complicated with PE (p < 0.001) and in FGR cases where birth weight of the newborn was ≤2nd percentile for the gestational age (p < 0.001). Following Bonferroni correction (adjusted α = 0.006), these differences remained statistically significant. Based on Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p values, the differences described in Table 1 also remained statistically significant. Although we did not find statistically significant differences in values of the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio using the Kruskal-Wallis H test between cases of EO-FGR versus LO-FGR, the ratio tended to be higher among cases of EO-FGR compared to LO-FGR, but without statistical significance.

Discussion

FGR is a common and ominous entity, diagnosed in the third trimester of euploid pregnancies, soundly impacting the neonatal prognosis. For that very reason, as early recognition and gestational age at the time of the diagnosis of FGR have a great impact on its prognosis [20], timely diagnosis has a grave importance in its management, and prenatal ultrasound has a key role in this approach [21]. However, ultrasound is already a tool of diagnosis: FGR is manifested, when ultrasound estimates a small fetal weight. Ultrasound cannot foresee FGR. The question is: do we have something which helps us to forecast FGR?

Early identification of pregnancies with a high risk for developing FGR is a well-desired objective in perinatology; however, there is no universal marker in the late first or early second trimester for this. The connection between low PAPP-A and high FBHCG (detected in late first or early second trimester) with FGR is well studied [9, 22]; however, we have scarce knowledge about the significance of the ratio of FBHCG/PAPP-A on perinatal outcomes in the population of fetuses affected by FGR. This study investigated the correlation between FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio and pathophysiology of FGR, emphasizing the fact that this ratio was calculated from the mentioned serum markers as part of the late first or early second trimester genetic screening, at the time when FGR was – of course – not a known complication of pregnancy, but it manifested later during its progression.

It is worth noting that our cohort of euploid, singleton pregnancies complicated by FGR did not exhibit markedly elevated (>2.0 MoM) FBHCG levels or markedly reduced (<0.3 MoM) PAPP-A levels. Nevertheless, we found several statistically significant differences in the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio among various subgroups of this cohort, suggesting that this ratio may have clinical utility in predicting severe pregnancy complications. The ratio was significantly higher in cases of birth weight ≤2nd percentile and in FGR cases complicated with PE. The impairment of trophoblast invasion, the dysfunctional remodeling of maternal spiral arteries, and uteroplacental hypoperfusion are generally known in the background of PE [23, 24]. In preeclamptic FGR, the higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could be the early marker of this defective uteroplacental vascularization.

We described DVH in the placenta with a significantly higher prevalence in cases of higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio. This leads us to conclude that higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio detected in the late first trimester could draw attention to the possibility of dysfunctional placental vasculogenesis, which later could progress to clinically manifest PE and FGR. DVH is a histological sign of placental villous underdevelopment both in PE and FGR, and it is an essential indicator of placental non-branching villous tree pattern characteristic to PE and FGR [25]. The significantly higher rate of birth weight ≤2nd percentile could be explained by this phenomenon, which leads to uteroplacental hypoperfusion and inadequate fetal nutrition, leading to FGR. In our opinion, the possible associations between higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio and fetal weight in FGR merit further studies in larger cohorts.

CVR had a strong tendency to be lower in cases where FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was higher; however, it was not statistically significant (p = 0.059). Villous under capillarization in the background of FGR and PE are in connection with the previously discussed phenomena, and it is a histopathological hallmark of defective placental angiogenesis. Related to this, we found that AV were described significantly more often (p = 0.005) in cases with a higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio, which made us conclude that AV and DVH are the key histopathological elements in the pathophysiology of FGR, and these entities are the microscopical markers of the placental developmental disturbance in FGR (and PE). Increased FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could be an early biochemical marker of all of these, providing a useful and widely accessible marker of pregnancies worth to be closely monitored for FGR (and PE) during their course.

FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio was significantly higher when placental weight to birth weight ratio was <1:8, indicating a relatively small placenta compared to the weight of the fetus. The ratio of placental weight and birth weight could be a marker of placental function [26], this way it could refer to the altered nutritive function of the placenta in cases of FGR. Accordingly, FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could be an early marker of potential placental dysfunction (and consecutive FGR later in pregnancy), rendering it a helpful marker as early as the end of the first trimester to identify pregnancies which need to be monitored closely during their course, to identify the imminent risk of FGR as soon as possible. FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio in our cohort did not have a relationship with maternal cigarette smoking, which highlights the potential independence of this marker from the use of tobacco.

The increased prevalence of placental chorangiosis among cases with a higher FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could emphasize the compensational character of chorangiosis in the placenta as a response to chronic hypoxia [27]. We were not able to demonstrate a significant correlation between onset (EO- versus LO-) of FGR and FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio, indicating the role of other factors (e.g., tobacco smoking, maternal malnutrition, genetic predisposition) in restricted intrauterine fetal growth. Similarly, oligohydramnios (which is a prevalent complication of FGR) was also not correlated with this ratio, highlighting the significance of other factors.

The main limitation of this study was the small number of cases. Additional retrospective, multicentric clinicopathological studies with a much larger cohort are needed to demonstrate the significance and clinical usefulness of the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio in the identification and prognostication of pregnancies worth to be monitored closely for the occurrence of FGR. The designation of appropriate cutoff values and references for the ratio with a properly high positive predictive value for FGR and adverse perinatal outcomes can be anticipated from multicentric analyses with a larger cohort.

Accordingly, further studies are needed with a control group (pregnancies without FGR) to compare and analyze the associations between this ratio and fetal weight. Similarly to PE, FGR could also be a biphasic entity from the viewpoint of the placenta [28]: in its first stage, growth restriction of the fetus is not evident, it becomes manifest later in the second phase. Moreover, FGR is not exclusively caused by placental factors: even immunological mechanisms have a role in its pathogenesis [29].

Conclusion

The FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio could be an early marker of poor placentation and/or potential placental dysfunction manifesting later during the course of gestation; however, it seems to be only a cautionary mark: complication of pregnancy with FGR is not obligatory in these cases. The lack of association between the onset of FGR and the FBHCG/PAPP-A ratio points to the fact that in some cases, FGR becomes clinically evident earlier, in some cases later, and in some cases never.

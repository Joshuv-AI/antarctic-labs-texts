Removable Sets for Fractional Heat and Fractional Bessel-Heat Equations
Mouna Chegaar and Á. P. Horváth

Abstract
. We examine the fractional heat diffusion equations L γ , a := ( − Δ a ) γ 2 + ∂ t L_{\gamma,a}:=(-\Delta_{a})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}+\partial_{t} , where Δ a \Delta_{a} is the Laplace- or the Bessel-Laplace operator. We give conditions for removability which are sufficient and which are necessary, by L p L^{p} -capacities. Introducing a spherical modulus of smoothness we can treat the Laplace and Bessel-Laplace cases together.

1.
Introduction

Removable sets, with respect to a partial differential operator, are sets to which the solution can be extended in a certain sense. Characterization of removability of sets with respect to harmonic functions by its Hausdorff dimension is due to Carleson, see
[
5
]
. After this result, the study was extended to several types of partial differential equations, and the criteria for removability were determined using capacity or Hausdorff measure. As an illustration we mention the following classical result, cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2.7.4 and Corollary 3.3.4]
.

Theorem A

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic linear partial differential operator of order
\alpha<n
with constant coefficients, and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact. Then
K
is removable for
\mathcal{L}
in
L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, if and only if
C_{\alpha,p^{\prime}}(K)=0
.

C_{\alpha,p^{\prime}}
is the
L^{p^{\prime}}
-capacity, which is given by Sobolev space norm. A similar result can be proven for ”Bessel-elliptic” equations with Bessel
p
-capacity, see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
. Recently, conditions for removability have been given for certain fractional heat-diffusion equations in Lipschitz-class case, see
[
15
]
, in
L^{\infty}
case, see
[
16
]
and for Cantor sets, see
[
8
]
. In the last two papers the main results are proved in
\frac{1}{2}
-fractional case. Our purpose is to get similar results to exponents from
(0,1)
and in
L^{p}
with
1<p<\infty
.

The second motivation for this paper is to emphasize the close connection between the Laplace and Bessel-Laplace operators, where Bessel-Laplace operator is

\Delta_{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}+\frac{a_{i}}{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{i}}),\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pta_{i}>0.

One way to describe this relationship is through the Poisson operator, see
[
21
, Statement 4, page 137]
.
Our approach is different.
Technically, the Fourier transform with standard convolution in Laplace case behaves like the Hankel transform with Bessel convolution in Bessel-Laplace case. This coincidence allows the homogeneous discussion.

The other important ingredient is the observation that taking average on the sphere reduces the problems to one variable ones, cf. Definition
2
. The method of reducing problems to radial ones is useful in applications. One of the most important tools in this area is Bessel convolution and Hankel transform, see e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
9
]
.

Below we homogenize the two problems with the defined ”spherical difference” via a one-dimensional ”modulus of smoothness and K-functional” type chain of ideas.
This leads to the uniformly formulated result of Proposition
2
.

Of course, there are some differences between the two cases. The first one is that the behavior of the Riesz kernel is different from the Bessel one at infinity. Thus the formulation of Theorem
1
refers to the Bessel case.

On the other hand, Bessel translation has some inconvenient properties. For instance it does not commute with the first partials and with product. Taking into consideration these differences, in Laplace case the statement of Proposition
2
can be extended to all exponents, cf. Theorem
2
.

If

The scalar products referring the introduced spaces are

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{a}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{(+)}}u(x)\varphi(x)x^{a}dx\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{and}\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{A}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{(+)}}u(x,t)\varphi(x,t)x^{a}dxdt.

\|L_{\gamma,0}(\varphi(P_{\gamma,0}*\Psi)\|_{p^{\prime}}

\leq c(n,\gamma)\left(\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(\|P_{\gamma,0}*\Psi\|_{p^{\prime}}+\|\Delta(P_{\gamma,0}*\Psi)\|_{p^{\prime}})+\|\Delta\varphi\|_{\infty}\|P_{\gamma,0}*\Psi\|_{p^{\prime}}\right)

+\|\partial_{t}(\varphi(P_{\gamma,0}*\Psi)\|_{p}.

That is the terms which contain
\partial_{x_{i}}P_{\gamma,0}
are missing. The remainder terms are already estimated, thus the proof of this direction is finished. The opposite direction is obvious as above.

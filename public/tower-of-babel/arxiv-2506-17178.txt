1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

For positive even integers
k
, the Eisenstein series
E_{k}(\tau)
(note.
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
) has Fourier series

E_{k}(\tau):=1-\frac{k}{B_{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^{n},

where
B_{k}
is the
k^{{\text{\rm th}}}
Bernoulli number and

\sigma_{\nu}(n):=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{\nu}.
If
k\geq 4
is even, then
E_{k}(\tau)
is in
M_{k}
, the complex vector space of weight
k
holomorphic modular form on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
In the 1970s, F. K. C. Rankin and H. P. F. Swinnerton-Dyer
[
18
]
showed that the zeros of these modular forms lie on the lower boundary of the standard fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}
for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
the arc of the unit circle

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{H}\ :\ |\tau|=1\ \ {\text{\rm with}}\ \ -\frac{1}{2}\leq{\text{\rm Re}}(\tau)\leq 0\right\}.

A straightforward procedure reformulates this result in terms of “divisor polynomials” of modular forms, with zeros in the interval
[0,1728].

To clarify, we first note that the elliptic points
\tau\in\{i,\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}\}
are often trivial zeros of modular forms.
Indeed, if
f(\tau)\in M_{k},
then the valence formula (for example, see p. 8 of
[
15
]
) implies

\mathrm{ord}_{i}(f)\geq\begin{cases}1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{4},\\
0\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
\end{cases}

and

\mathrm{ord}_{\omega}(f)\geq\begin{cases}2\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{6},\\
1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 4\pmod{6},\\
0\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 0\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

The trivial zeros at
\{i,\omega\}
are precisely captured by the low weight Eisenstein series

\widetilde{E}_{k}(\tau):=\begin{cases}1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 0\pmod{12},\\
E_{4}(z)^{2}E_{6}(z)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{12},\\
E_{4}(z)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 4\pmod{12},\\
E_{6}(z)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 6\pmod{12},\\
E_{4}(z)^{2}\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 8\pmod{12},\\
E_{4}(z)E_{6}(z)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 10\pmod{12}.\end{cases}

We use these Eisenstein series, Ramanujan’s weight 12 cusp form

\Delta(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)q^{n}:=\frac{E_{4}(\tau)^{3}-E_{6}(\tau)^{2}}{1728}=q-24q^{2}+252q^{3}-\cdots,

and the modular function

j(\tau):=\frac{E_{4}(\tau)^{3}}{\Delta(\tau)}=q^{-1}+744+196884q+\cdots

to define a “divisor polynomial” for
f(\tau)
.
Namely, there is a polynomial
\widetilde{F}(f;x)
(for example, see Lemma 2.34 of
[
15
]
) for which

(1.2)

\widetilde{F}(f;j(\tau))=\frac{f(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau)^{m(k)}\widetilde{E}_{k}(\tau)},

where

m(k):=\begin{cases}\lfloor k/12\rfloor\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{if}}\ k\not\equiv 2\pmod{12},\\
\lfloor k/12\rfloor-1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{12}.\end{cases}

Indeed, as the
\widetilde{E}_{k}(\tau)
capture the trivial zeros at
\tau\in\{i,\omega\},
this expression is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
. In other words, it is a weakly holomorphic modular function (i.e. poles are supported the cusp infinity). As the
j
-function defines a bijection between the fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\mathbb{C}
, it follows that
\widetilde{F}(f;j(\tau))
is a polynomial in
j(\tau)
.
After noting that
j(i)=1728
and
j(\omega)=0,

and then letting

h_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}1\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 0\pmod{12},\\
x^{2}(x-1728)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 2\pmod{12},\\
x\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 4\pmod{12},\\
x-1728\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 6\pmod{12},\\
x^{2}\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 8\pmod{12},\\
x(x-1728)\ \ \ \ \ &{\text{\rm if}}\ k\equiv 10\pmod{12},\end{cases}

we define the
divisor polynomial
of
f(\tau)
by

(1.3)

F(f;x):=h_{k}(x)\cdot\widetilde{F}(f;x).

As we have
j:\mathcal{A}\mapsto[0,1728]
(see (
1.1

the Rankin-Swinnerton-Dyer theorem asserts that the zeros of
F(E_{2k};x)
are in the interval
[0,1728].

Their paper has been generalized. R. A. Rankin, F. K. C. Rankin’s father, proved

To describe their result, we recall an important sequence of modular functions.
Let

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

These results rely on the delicate interplay between the action of the Hecke operators on
R(\tau)
, the weight
-10
harmonic Maass form, and the weight 12 cusp form
\Delta(\tau)
. To this end, we begin by recalling the action of the Hecke operators on these modular forms. For convenience, we let
\tau:=u+iv
throughout.
The action of the
m
th weight
k
Hecke operator on weight
k
modular forms, which preserves modularity, is given by

(2.1)

(f\ |\ T_{k}(m))(\tau)=m^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=m\\
a,d>0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{k}}\sum_{b\bmod d}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{d}\right).

This definition applies to harmonic Maass forms and classical meromorphic modular forms. Moreover, the Hecke action is easily described as combinatorial operators on Fourier expansions. In the case of harmonic Maass forms, extra care is required when the functions are not meromorphic.

To make this precise, we recall that if

f(\tau)
is a harmonic Maass form of even weight
k
, then it has a Fourier expansion of the form (for example, see Lemma 4.2 of
[
2
]
)

(2.2)

f(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{f}^{+}(n)q^{n}+\sum_{n<0}c_{f}^{-}(n)\Gamma(1-k,-4\pi nv)q^{n},

where

\Gamma(s,z):=\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{s}\frac{dt}{t}

is the incomplete gamma function. The
q
-series

f^{+}(\tau):=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{f}^{+}(n)q^{n}

is the
holomorphic part
of
f(\tau)
, while its
nonholomorphic part
is

f^{-}(\tau):=\sum_{n<0}c_{f}^{-}(n)\Gamma(1-k,-4\pi nv)q^{n}.

In the case of
R(\tau)
, we have
f^{+}(\tau)=M_{\Delta}(\tau)
and
f^{-}(\tau)=N_{\Delta}(\tau)
. Furthermore, for meromorphic modular forms
f(\tau)
, we have that
f(\tau)=f^{+}(\tau)
.

The following proposition describes the action of the Hecke operators on even integer weight meromorphic modular forms and harmonic Maass forms on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
f(\tau)
is an even integer weight
k
modular form on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

If
m\geq 1
is an integer, then the following are true.

(1) If
f(\tau)
is a meromorphic modular form, then we have

(f\ |\ T_{k}(m))(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n)\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}c^{+}_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)q^{n}.

(2) If
f(\tau)
is a harmonic Maass form, then we have

\begin{split}(f\ |\ T_{k}(m))(\tau)&=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n)\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}c_{f}^{+}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)q^{n}\\
&+\sum_{n<0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n)\\
d>0\end{subarray}}d^{k-1}c_{f}^{-}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)\Gamma(1-k,-4\pi n(d^{2}/m)v)q^{n}.\end{split}

1.2

whose action on the Fourier expansion of a harmonic Maass form of weight
k

[

(2.3)

\xi_{k}(f(\tau))=\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau))=-(4\pi)^{1-k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{c_{f}^{-}(-n)}n^{1-k}q^{n}.

Furthermore,

is a holomorphic cusp form weight
2-k

Lemma 2.2

If

m^{1-k}\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau)\ |\ T_{k}(m))=\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau))\ |\ T_{2-k}(m).

Proof.

We first write

\xi_{k}(f(\tau))=\xi_{k}(f^{+}(\tau))+\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau)).

Since

\displaystyle m^{1-k}\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau)\ |\ T_{k}(m))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=m\\

\displaystyle m^{1-k}\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau)\ |\ T_{k}(m))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=m\\

\displaystyle=m^{1-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=m\\

\displaystyle=\xi_{k}(f^{-}(\tau))\ |\ T_{2-k}(m).

m^{11}M_{\Delta}(\tau)\ |\ T_{-10}(m)=a_{\Delta}(-1)q^{-m}+a_{\Delta}(0)\sigma_{11}(m)+O(q).

We then multiply by
E_{4}(\tau)E_{6}(\tau)=E_{10}(\tau)
, making use of the Fourier expansion

E_{10}(\tau)=1-264\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{9}(n)q^{n}

to obtain

(2.5)

\begin{split}E_{4}(\tau)E_{6}(\tau)\cdot(m^{11}M_{\Delta}(\tau)\ |\ T_{-10}(m))&=a_{\Delta}(-1)\left(q^{-m}-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sigma_{9}(l)q^{-m+l}\right)\\
&\ \ \ \ \ +a_{\Delta}(0)\sigma_{11}(m)+O(q).\end{split}

Likewise, we compute

E_{4}(\tau)E_{6}(\tau)\cdot\left(-\tau(m)M_{\Delta}(\tau)\right)=-\tau(m)(a_{\Delta}(-1)q^{-1}-264a_{\Delta}(-1)+a_{\Delta}(0))+O(q),

which together with (

\begin{split}11!\cdot F_{m}(j(\tau))&=a_{\Delta}(-1)\left(q^{-m}-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sigma_{9}(l)q^{-m+l}\right)\\
&\ \ \ \ \ +a_{\Delta}(0)\sigma_{11}(m)-\tau(m)(a_{\Delta}(-1)q^{-1}-264a_{\Delta}(-1)+a_{\Delta}(0))+O(q).\end{split}

We finally recall that
\{j_{0}(\tau),j_{1}(\tau),\dots\}
is a polynomial basis for all modular functions on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Thus, thanks to (
1.4
) and (
1.5

\begin{split}11!\cdot F_{m}(j(\tau))&=a_{\Delta}(-1)\left(\psi_{m}(j(\tau))-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sigma_{9}(l)\psi_{m-l}(j(\tau))\right)\\
&\ \ \ \ \ +a_{\Delta}(0)\sigma_{11}(m)-\tau(m)(a_{\Delta}(-1)\psi_{1}(j(\tau))-264a_{\Delta}(-1)+a_{\Delta}(0)).\end{split}

Substituting the values of
a_{\Delta}(-1)
and
a_{\Delta}(0)
from (
1.9
) gives the formula in Theorem
1.2
(2).
∎

Lemma 2.5

Isolating

Next, we bound the terms

Lemma 2.6
.

If
m\geq 1
and
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}]

\left|G_{-m}(\theta)\right|\leq 2092m^{-4}e^{2\pi m\sin\theta}+1.27\cdot 10^{10}m^{6}.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\left|G_{-m}(\theta)\right|\leq\left|\phi_{-m}(e^{i\theta})\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\

Substituting the definition of
\phi_{-m}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{10}\left|\phi_{-m}\left(\frac{ae^{i\theta}+b}{ce^{i\theta}+d}\right)\right|=(4\pi m\sin\theta)^{5}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}M_{5,\frac{11}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi m\sin\theta}{\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}}\right).

Since
M_{5,\frac{11}{2}}
is monotonically increasing on the real line, the identity
\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}=c^{2}+2cd\cos\theta+d^{2}
and the fact that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 2,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}M_{5,\frac{11}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi m\sin\theta}{\left|ce^{i\theta}+d\right|^{2}}\right)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\geq 2,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\

\frac{4\pi m\sin\theta}{c^{2}+cd+d^{2}}\leq 4\pi\sin\theta\leq 4\pi,

satisfy

, there are at most

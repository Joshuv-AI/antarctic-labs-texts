On the Polytope Model and Near End node Isomorphisms of Type A A Kirillov–Reshetikhin Crystals
Dipnit Biswas, Irfan Habib

Abstract
. We prove an inductive formula to construct a path from the highest weight element to any given vertex in the crystal graph of the polytope realization of the Kirillov–-Reshetikhin crystal K ​ R i , m KR^{i,m} of type A A . For i ≤ 2 i\leq 2 or i ≥ n − 1 i\geq n-1 , we provide explicit formulas of the same by only using the lowering crystal operators and in those cases, using these paths, we determine the explicit image of any polytope under the affine crystal isomorphisms between the polytope and the tableau realizations of the Kirillov–-Reshetikhin crystals.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. Let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
be the associated untwisted affine Lie algebra and
U_{q}^{\prime}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
be the quantum affine algebra without the degree derivation. Finite-dimensional simple modules for
U_{q}^{\prime}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
have been classified in
[
4
]
in terms of their Drinfeld polynomials. The class of the prime simple objects in the category
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
of the finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}^{\prime}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
is still unknown. Kirillov-Reshetikhin (KR in short) modules
KR(m\omega_{i})
are prime simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
, indexed by a node
i
of the finite Dynkin diagram and a positive integer
m
. In
[
3
]
Chari and Pressley proved that for
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, then the Kirillov-Reshetikhin modules exhaust all prime simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
. KR modules have attracted significant attention due to their rich structure and interesting properties such as
T
-systems, fermionic character formulas; see
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
and the references therein for more details.

One of the ways to understand the modules in the category
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
is to study them via their crystal bases, if they exist. In
[
8
]
it was conjectured that the KR modules admit crystal bases in the sense of
[
12
]
and the conjecture was proved in
[
11
]
for
A_{n}^{(1)}
and in
[
16
]
for non-exceptional types. When a crystal base for
KR(m\omega_{i})
exists, we call the associated crystal the KR crystal
KR^{i,m}
. There have been researches where these crystals have been realized using explicit combinatorial models. If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
, then
KR^{i,m}
can be realized as all the semistandard Young tableau of rectangular shape
(m^{i})
and the crystal operators are defined as in
[
13
,
17
]
. The explicit combinatorial models for all non-exceptional types are given in
[
7
]
.

In
[
5
]
, when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n},
the authors constructed a polytope for each dominant integral weight
\lambda
and showed that the basis elements are parametrized by the integral points of the polytope. Kus in
[
14
]
defined an affine crystal structure on the polytope when
\lambda=m\omega_{i}
and proved that the resulting crystal, denoted by him as
B^{i,m}
, is isomorphic to the affine KR crystal
KR^{i,m}
. Later, in
[
15
]
, the same author provided a simplified formula for the action of the affine node. Similar polytopes are known for the other types as well e.g. see
[
6
]
for type
C
and
[
1
]
for type
B
.

This article is a step towards understanding the explicit bijection between the two combinatorial models for KR crystals, namely the tableau model in
[
13
,
17
]
and the polytope model given in
[
14
]
. In the polytope model, we provide an inductive formula to obtain an arbitrary polytope from the highest weight polytope, which is non-canonical in the sense that it involves the raising crystal operators. To describe the formula, for a polytope
A\in B^{i,m}
, let
r^{A}
be the row index of the first non-zero row in
A
and
t^{A}
be the least column index of the first non-zero entry in row
r^{A}
in
A.
Now let
A\in B^{i,m}
be arbitrary. Define the polytopes
A^{(k)}=(a^{(k)}_{s,t})\in B^{i,m}
for
r^{A}\leq k\leq n
by setting all the rows of
A
with row indices
<k
to zero (see section
2.5
for more details on the convention of the row-column indexing and interpreting the formula below). Note that
A^{(r^{A})}=A.
Define for
r^{A}\leq k\leq n
, the operator
\mathcal{K}^{A^{(k)}}
by

\mathcal{K}^{A^{(k)}}:=\left(\prod_{j=i+1}^{k}f_{k+i+1-j}^{\varphi_{k+i+1-j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=t^{A^{(k)}}+1}^{i}e_{i+t^{A^{(k)}}+1-j}^{h(k)}\right)\left(f_{t}^{g(k)}\prod_{j=t^{A^{(k)}}+1}^{i}f_{j}^{\varphi_{j}}\right),

where

h(k)=\epsilon_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+t^{A^{(k)}}+1-j\end{subarray}}-a^{(k)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+t^{A^{(k)}}+1-j\end{subarray},\,k},\ \ g(k)=a^{(k)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}t^{A^{(k)}}\end{subarray},\,k}.

Theorem
.

With all the notations as above, the operator

\mathcal{K}^{A^{(r^{A})}}\mathcal{K}^{A^{(r^{A}+1)}}\cdots\mathcal{K}^{A^{(n)}}

maps the highest weight polytope to an arbitrary polytope
A
in
B^{i,m}.
∎

In the last section, we provide an explicit formula to reach to any polytope from the highest weight polytope by only using the lowering crystal operators when
i\leq 2
and
i\geq n-1.
In those cases, we also provide the explicit image of the affine crystal isomorphism between the polytope and the tableau realizations.

The paper is organized as follows:
In section
2
, we recall the necessary definitions of crystals, tableau and polytope realization of Kirillov-Reshetikhin crystals and introduce some statistics associated to a polytope. In section
3
, we prove the inductive formula to obtain any polytope in the crystal graph of Kirillov–-Reshetikhin crystals from the highest weight polytope. In section
4
, we do the same when
i\leq 2
and
i\geq n-1
and provide the explicit image of the affine crystal isomorphism of a polytope between the polytope and the tableau realizations.

This research was greatly facilitated by experiments carried out in the computational algebra systems SageMath
[
19
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.

We adopt the conventions that an empty product equals
1
and an empty sum equals
0
. We denote by
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the sets of complex numbers, real numbers, integers, positive integers and non-negative integers, respectively. Given
m\in\mathbb{N},
we denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
by
[m].
Moreover, if
f
denotes a condition, we define the indicator function
\delta_{f}
by
\delta_{f}=1
if
f
holds and
\delta_{f}=0
otherwise. For instance,
\delta_{\,j\geq 2}=1
if and only if
j\geq 2
, and
\delta_{\,j\geq 2}=0
otherwise.

2.2.
Lie algebras of type
A_{n}

Throughout this article once and forever we fix
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
be a finite index set and
A=(a_{k,\ell})_{i,j\in I}
be the Cartan matrix of type
A_{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra associated to the matrix
A.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of traceless
(n+1)\times(n+1)
complex matrices, that is,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}
the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of diagonal matrices. Let
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}
and
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\alpha_{2}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\vee}\}
denote the sets of simple roots and simple coroots of
\mathfrak{g}
, respectively. Let
\theta
be the highest root and let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n}
be the fundamental weights of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
P
the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now set
\widehat{I}:=I\cup\{0\}
and let
\widehat{A}
be the corresponding untwisted affine Cartan matrix of type
A_{n}^{(1)}
. The Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
associated with
\widehat{A}
is the untwisted affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}}(\mathbb{C})
. Its sets of simple roots and simple coroots are
\widehat{\Pi}=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}
and
\widehat{\Pi^{\vee}}:=\{\alpha_{0}^{\vee},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\vee}\}
respectively, where
\alpha_{0}=-\theta+\delta,
the root
\delta
being the null root. We denote by
\widehat{P}
the weight lattice of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}.

2.3.
Abstract crystals

A
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}

crystal
is a non-empty set
\mathcal{B}
together with maps

\mathrm{wt}:\mathcal{B}\to\widehat{P},\ \ e_{\ell},f_{\ell}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\cup\{0\},\ \ \epsilon_{\ell},\varphi_{\ell}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\},\ \ \ell\in\widehat{I}

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

If
x,y\in\mathcal{B}
, then
e_{\ell}(x)=y
if and only if
f_{\ell}(y)=x
and in this case we have

\mathrm{wt}(y)=\mathrm{wt}(x)+\alpha_{\ell},\ \ \epsilon_{\ell}(y)=\epsilon_{\ell}(x)-1,\ \ \varphi_{\ell}(y)=\varphi_{\ell}(x)+1.

(2)

\varphi_{\ell}(x)-\epsilon_{\ell}(x)=\mathrm{wt}(x)(\alpha_{\ell}^{\vee})
for
x\in\mathcal{B}
where it is understood that if
\varphi_{\ell}(x)=-\infty
then
\epsilon_{\ell}(x)=-\infty
and in that case
e_{\ell}(x)=f_{\ell}(x)=0.

The operators
f_{\ell}
(resp.
e_{\ell}
),
\ell\in\widehat{I}
, are called the
lowering
(resp.
raising
) operators. For brevity, we shall sometimes call only
\mathcal{B}
as a crystal when the weight map and crystal operators are clear from the context. Throughout this article, we shall consider
seminormal crystals
only, i.e. crystals where the functions
\epsilon_{\ell},\varphi_{\ell}
are determined by:

\epsilon_{\ell}(x):=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:e_{\ell}^{k}(x)\neq 0\},\ \ \varphi_{\ell}(x):=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:f_{\ell}^{k}(x)\neq 0\},

with the convention that
\epsilon_{\ell}(x)=-\infty
(resp.
\varphi_{\ell}(x)=-\infty
) if
e_{\ell}(x)=0
(resp.
f_{\ell}(x)=0
) for
x\in\mathcal{B}
. The crystal graph of
\mathcal{B}
is a directed simple graph with vertex set
\mathcal{B}
and an edge
x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell}}{{\to}}y
whenever
f_{\ell}(x)=y.
The dual crystal
\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
of
\mathcal{B}
is defined by a bijection
x\mapsto x^{\vee}
between
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
such that

\mathrm{wt}(x^{\vee})=-\mathrm{wt}(x),\ \ e_{\ell}(x^{\vee})=f_{\ell}(x)^{\vee},\ \ f_{\ell}(x^{\vee})=e_{\ell}(x).

Note that the crystal graph of
\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
is obtained by reversing the direction of all the arrows in the crystal graph of
\mathcal{B}.

2.4.
KR crystals as tableaux

The tableaux model for the Kirillov-Reshetikhin crystals (KR crystals in short) is standard and can be found in
[
13
]
. Let
(i,m)\in I\times\mathbb{N}
. The KR crystal associated to the pair
(i,m)
is denoted by
\mathrm{SSYT}(m\omega_{i})
and is described as follows:

•

The underlying set
\mathcal{B}
is
\mathrm{SSYT}(m\omega_{i})
, consisting of all semistandard Young tableaux (SSYT in short) of rectangular shape
(m^{i})
filled with entries from
[n+1]
.

•

For
j\in I
, the crystal operators
e_{j},f_{j}
are defined via the signature rule:

Given
T\in\mathcal{B}
, let
w
be the word obtained by reading
T
from left to right along rows, starting at the bottom row and moving upward. Thus
T
may be regarded as a word over the alphabet
[n+1]
. Now fix
j\in I
. In
w
, successively pair each
j+1
with the nearest unpaired
j
to its right, provided no letters between them are equal to
j
or
j+1
. After all possible pairings, any unpaired
j
’s lie to the left of all unpaired
j+1
’s. Then:

–

If no unpaired
j
(resp.
j+1
) remains, set
f_{j}(w)=0
(resp.
e_{j}(w)=0
).

–

Otherwise,
f_{j}
changes the rightmost unpaired
j
to
j+1
, and
e_{j}
changes the leftmost unpaired
j+1
to
j
.

For example,

f_{1}(21122152731221)=21222152731221,\ \ e_{1}(21122152731221)=21112152731221.

•

The action of the crystal operators for the affine nodes are given by (
[
17
]
):

(2.1)

e_{0}=\mathrm{pr}^{-1}\circ e_{1}\circ\mathrm{pr},\ \ \ \ f_{0}=\mathrm{pr}^{-1}\circ f_{1}\circ\mathrm{pr}.

where
\mathrm{pr}
is the Schützenberger’s promotion operator. The image of the promotion (resp. inverse promotion) operator applied to a tableau
T
is computed as follows:

(1)

Remove all entries equal to
n+1
(resp.
1
) from
T
.

(2)

Apply jeu-de-taquin to slides the empty boxes to a corner.

(3)

Fill the empty boxes with
0
(resp.
n+2
).

(4)

Increase (resp. decrease) all remaining entries by
1
.

For more details about the jeu-de-taquin slides, the reader may refer to
[
18
, Section A1.2]
.

We denote by
T^{(0)}\in\mathrm{SSYT}(m\omega_{i})
(when
i
and
m
are clear from the context) the highest weight tableau i.e. the unique element of
\mathrm{SSYT}(m\omega_{i})
satisfying
e_{i}(T^{(0)})=0
for all
i\in[n].
Then all the entries of
T^{(0)}
in the
j
-th row are
j
for
j\in[i].

2.5.
KR crystals as polytopes

The polytope models for the KR crystals were introduced in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. This model was motivated by the polytope realization of the FFL basis defined in
[
5
]
. Let
i\in I.
Let
\widetilde{B}^{i}_{n}
be the set of all patterns of size with non-negative integer coefficients with the following indexing of rows and columns:

(2.2)

A=\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr a_{1,i}&a_{2,i}&\cdots&a_{i-1,i}&a_{i,i}\\
\hline\cr a_{1,i+1}&a_{2,i+1}&\cdots&a_{i-1,i+1}&a_{i,i+1}\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr a_{1,n}&a_{2,n}&\cdots&a_{i-1,n}&a_{i,n}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

A
Dyck path
in
\widetilde{B}^{i}_{n}
is a sequence
(\beta(1),\beta(2),\dots,\beta(n))
such that
\beta(1)=(1,i),\beta(n)=(i,n)
and if
\beta(r)=(a,b),
then
\beta(r+1)\in\{(a,b+1),(a+1,b)\}.
A subsequence of a Dyck path is called a
sub Dyck path
. For
m\in\mathbb{N},
define
B^{i,m}_{n}
to be the subset of
\tilde{B}^{i}_{n}
given by

(2.3)

B^{i,m}_{n}:=\left\{A\in\widetilde{B}^{i,m}_{n}:\sum_{r=1}^{n}p_{\beta(r)}\leq m\ \ \text{ for all Dyck paths }\beta\text{ in }\widetilde{B}^{i,m}_{n}\right\}.

We shall often suppress
n
from
B^{i,m}_{n}
and write
B^{i,m}
instead when
n
is clear from the context. The set
B^{i,m}
is indeed a polytope in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{i(n-i+1)}
(see
[
14
]
).
Let
A\in B^{i,m}
be given by (
2.2
). Define the weight function
\mathrm{wt}:B^{i,m}\to P
as

\mathrm{wt}(A)=m\omega_{i}-\sum_{1\leq p\leq i,i\leq q\leq n}a_{p,q}\alpha_{p,q}.

It is often convenient to set
a_{k,\ell}=0
if
k\notin[i]
or
\ell\notin\{i,i+1,\dots,n\}.
We now define the crystal operators following
[
14
]
. For
\ell\in I
, set:

p^{\ell}_{+}(A)=\min\left\{1\leq p\leq i|\sum^{p}_{j=1}a_{j,\ell-1}+\sum^{i}_{j=p}a_{j,\ell}=\max_{1\leq q\leq i}\{\sum^{q}_{j=1}a_{j,\ell-1}+\sum^{i}_{j=q}a_{j,\ell}\}\right\}

q^{\ell}_{+}(A)=\max\{1\leq p\leq i|\sum^{p}_{j=1}a_{j,\ell-1}+\sum^{i}_{j=p}a_{j,\ell}=\max_{1\leq q\leq i}\{\sum^{q}_{j=1}a_{j,\ell-1}+\sum^{i}_{j=q}a_{j,\ell}\}\}

p^{\ell}_{-}(A)=\max\{i\leq p\leq n|\sum^{p}_{j=i}a_{\ell,j}+\sum^{n}_{j=p}a_{\ell+1,j}=\max_{i\leq q\leq n}\{\sum^{q}_{j=i}a_{\ell,j}+\sum^{n}_{j=q}a_{\ell+1,j}\}\}

q^{\ell}_{-}(A)=\min\{i\leq p\leq n|\sum^{p}_{j=i}a_{\ell,j}+\sum^{n}_{j=p}a_{\ell+1,j}=\max_{i\leq q\leq n}\{\sum^{q}_{j=i}a_{\ell,j}+\sum^{n}_{j=q}a_{\ell+1,j}\}\}.

For
\ell\in I,
the functions
\varphi_{\ell},\epsilon_{\ell}:B^{i,m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are defined by

\varphi_{l}(A)=\begin{cases}m-\sum^{i-1}_{j=1}a_{j,i}-\sum^{n}_{j=i}a_{i,j},&\text{if $l=i$}\\
\sum^{p^{l}_{+}(A)}_{j=1}a_{j,l-1}-\sum^{p^{l}_{+}(A)-1}_{j=1}a_{j,l},&\text{if $l>i$}\\
\sum^{n}_{j=p^{l}_{-}(A)}a_{l+1,j}-\sum^{n}_{j=p^{l}_{-}(A)+1}a_{l,j},&\text{if $l<i$}\end{cases}

and

\epsilon_{l}(A)=\begin{cases}a_{i,i},&\text{if $l=i$}\\
\sum^{i}_{j=q^{l}_{+}(A)}a_{j,l}-\sum^{i}_{j=q^{l}_{+}(A)+1}a_{j,l-1},&\text{if $l>i$}\\
\sum^{q^{l}_{-}(A)}_{j=i}a_{l,j}-\sum^{q^{l}_{-}(A)-1}_{j=i}a_{l+1,j},&\text{if $l<i$,}\end{cases}

Now we can define the crystal operators. The images
{f}_{\ell}(A)
and
{e}_{\ell}(A)
are defined to be

{f}_{l}A=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{ replace }a_{i,i}\text{ by }a_{i,i}+1,&\text{if }l=i,\\
\text{replace }a_{p^{l}_{+}(A),l-1}\text{ by }a_{p^{l}_{+}(A),l-1}-1\text{ and }a_{p^{l}_{+}(A),l}\text{ by }a_{p^{l}_{+}(A),l}+1,&\text{if }l>i,\\

2.6.
Statistics on polytopes

In this subsection, we introduce some statistics associated with polytopes. Let
A=(a_{k,\ell})\in B^{i,m}
be a polytope and fix
1\leq s<i\leq r\leq n.
We define the
step path

p_{s,r}
as the sequence of positions:

p_{s,r}=\{(s,i),(s,i+1),\dots,(s,r),(s+1,r),(s+1,r+1),\dots,(s+1,n)\}.

We then set

S^{A}(s,r):=\sum_{x\in p_{s,r}}a_{x}\ \ \text{ and }M^{A}(s):=\max\{S^{A}(s,r):i\leq r\leq n\}.

Now we define a sequence
R^{A}(s)=\{r^{A}(s,0),r^{A}(s,1),\dots,r^{A}(s,\ell^{A}(s))\}
as follows: Set
r^{A}(s,0)=i-1
and define inductively

(2.6)

r^{A}(s,k+1):=\max\{r>r^{A}(s,k):S^{A}(s,r)=\max\{S^{A}(s,p):r^{A}(s,k)<p\leq n\}\}

with
\ell^{A}(s)
such that
r^{A}(s,\ell^{A}(s))=n
.

Finally, we define some non-negative integers
x^{A}(s,r)
. Set
x^{A}(s,r)=0
if
r\notin R^{A}(s)\backslash\{i-1\}
and for
1\leq k<\ell^{A}(s)
the integers
x^{A}(s,r)
satisfy:

(2.7)

S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,k+1))+\sum_{p=i}^{r^{A}(s,k)}x^{A}(s,p)=M^{A}(s),

and we set
x^{A}(s,n)=a_{s+1,n}.

For example, let
m
be large and take
(i,n)=(3,6).
Let
A\in B^{i,m}
be the polytope

A=\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr 2&0&2\\
\hline\cr 0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 1&2&1\\
\hline\cr 1&0&5\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Then we have

S^{A}(1,4)=5,\ \ S^{A}(2,5)=9,\ \ S^{A}(2,3)=10.

Moreover,

R^{A}(1)=\{2,5,6\},\ \ \ R^{A}(2)=\{2,3,5,6\}

and

x^{A}(2,3)=x^{A}(2,5)=x^{A}(2,6)=x^{A}(1,5)=1,

x^{A}(2,4)=x^{A}(1,3)=x^{A}(1,4)=x^{A}(1,6)=0.

Remark 1
.

By definition, we have
x^{A}(s,p)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for
i\leq p\leq n.
Moreover, we have
x^{A}(s,j)>0
if and only if
j\in\{r^{A}(s,p):1\leq p<\ell^{A}(s)+\delta_{(a_{s+1,n}>0)}\}
. In particular,
r^{A}(s,1)=i
if
x^{A}(s,i)>0.

We begin with an easy lemma which will be needed later.

Lemma
.

Let
1\leq k<\ell^{A}(s).
For any
0\leq x<x^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,k))
and
r^{A}(s,k)<r\leq n
, we have

S^{A}(s,r)+\sum_{p=i}^{r^{A}(s,k-1)}x^{A}(s,p)+x<M^{A}(s).

Proof.

Since by definition we have
S^{A}(s,r)\leq S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,k+1))
for all
r^{A}(s,k)<r\leq n
, the result follows from Eq.(
2.7
).
∎

The following proposition will be used throughout this section.

Proposition
.

Let
A\in B^{i,m}
be a polytope and let
1\leq s<i,\ 1\leq r\leq\ell^{A}(s).
Then we have

\sum_{p=r^{A}(s,r)}^{n}x^{A}(s,p)=S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,r))-S^{A}(s,n)+a_{s+1,n}.

Proof.

Eq.(
2.7
) can be encoded in a matrix equation
RX=B
where

R=(r_{k,\ell})
is a square matrix of order
\ell^{A}(s)-1
,
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell^{A}(s)-1})
and
B=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{\ell^{A}(s)-1})
are column vectors so that

r_{k,\ell}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }k\geq\ell,\\
0&\text{ elsewhere }\end{cases},\ \ \ x_{k}=x^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,k)),\ \ \ b_{k}=M^{A}(s)-S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,k+1)).

Solving the system by inverting
R
we obtain:

(2.8)

x^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,r))=S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,r))-S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,r+1)),\ \ 1\leq r\leq\ell^{A}(s)-1.

Now the result follows along with the fact that
x^{A}(s,\ell^{A}(s))=a_{s+1,n}.

∎

Corollary
.

We have
\sum_{p=1}^{n}x^{A}(s,p)=\varphi_{s}(A).

Proof.

Proof follows from proposition
Proposition
and the definition of
\varphi_{i}
in section
2.5
since

S^{A}(s,r^{A}(s,1))-S^{A}(s,n)+a_{s+1,n}=\varphi_{s}(A).

∎

Remark 2
.

Let
s<i.
Using lemma
Lemma
and corollary
Corollary
, we have that the polytope
B:=f_{s}^{\varphi_{s}}(A)=(b_{k,\ell})
is given by

b_{k,\ell}=a_{k,\ell}+\delta_{(k=s)}x^{A}(s,\ell)-\delta_{(k=s+1)}x^{A}(s,\ell).

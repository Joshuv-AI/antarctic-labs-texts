Prospective comparison of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT and salivary gland scintigraphy for the diagnosis of primary Sjögren’s syndrome
Authors: Ke Cheng, Tingting Xu, Zhuoyuan Li, Guangfu Liu, Rui Sun, Qingxue Shu, Yue Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrimary Sjögren’s syndrome (pSS) is characterized by progressive fibro-inflammatory glandular injury. While conventional salivary gland scintigraphy (SGS) provides macroscopic functional assessment, it cannot visualize underlying active fibrotic remodeling. This prospective study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of dual-time-point (10 and 40 min post-injection) [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT—a novel radiotracer targeting activated fibroblasts—compared to SGS in patients with suspected pSS.ResultsAmong 21 participants (15 with confirmed pSS, 6 non-SS controls with other autoimmune diseases), early-phase (10-minute) PET/CT achieved optimal diagnostic efficacy. Visually, early-phase PET/CT demonstrated substantially higher specificity (66.7% vs. 16.7%), positive predictive value (87.5% vs. 73.7%), and overall accuracy (85.7% vs. 71.4%) compared to SGS, while maintaining comparable high sensitivity (93.3%). Semiquantitatively, the SUVmax of the submandibular and sublingual glands exhibited significant diagnostic utility (AUC > 0.5, P < 0.05), achieving 100% specificity and significantly outperforming volumetric parameters, which were compromised by a “background dilution effect.” Clinically, early-phase global FAPI burden (TLF) correlated negatively with the systemic erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR). Regionally, early submandibular FTV correlated positively with the SGS excretion fraction (r = 0.594, P = 0.019), whereas the parotid glands exhibited no such correlation, corroborating the asynchronous structural progression of pSS. Furthermore, whole-body PET/CT successfully detected incidental extra-salivary fibro-inflammatory comorbidities (e.g., Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, osteoarthritis).Conclusions[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT demonstrates promising diagnostic utility and high specificity for pSS by selectively visualizing active fibroblast activation. It elegantly complements the macroscopic functional evaluation of SGS while providing profound in vivo insights into asynchronous disease progression. Crucially, its whole-body imaging capability delivers unparalleled clinical value for mapping systemic, multi-organ comorbidities. These findings underscore its potential as an advanced adjunctive diagnostic tool, warranting rigorous validation in large-scale, multicenter prospective cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13550-026-01438-4.

Introduction

Primary Sjögren’s syndrome (pSS) is a chronic systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by focal lymphocytic infiltration and the progressive destruction of exocrine glands, predominantly the salivary and lacrimal glands. Epidemiologically, pSS demonstrates a pronounced female predominance, with a female-to-male ratio ranging from 9:1 to 20:1 [1]. Clinically, the disease manifests as xerostomia, keratoconjunctivitis sicca, and various systemic involvements that substantially impair patients’ quality of life [2]. The pathological continuum progresses from early acinar epithelial necrosis to late-stage fibro-inflammatory remodeling and irreversible glandular atrophy [3]. Consequently, early and accurate diagnosis is crucial for initiating timely immunomodulatory therapy and preventing permanent anatomical damage [4]. Nevertheless, clinical evaluation remains challenging due to the high heterogeneity of disease presentation and the inherent limitations of conventional diagnostic imaging modalities [5].

Among conventional modalities, salivary gland ultrasonography (SGUS) has emerged as a widely adopted, first-line non-invasive tool for evaluating macroscopic glandular structural alterations [6]. Given its robust diagnostic utility, standardized SGUS scoring systems (e.g., the OMERACT criteria) are currently undergoing rigorous validation for incorporation into future international classification guidelines [7]. However, although advantageous for detecting gross morphological abnormalities—such as hypoechoic areas and parenchymal heterogeneity—SGUS remains inherently operator-dependent. More importantly, it is fundamentally incapable of visualizing early molecular events or ongoing fibroblast activation, which dictate disease progression.

While SGUS assesses structural changes, salivary gland scintigraphy (SGS) remains the principal functional imaging modality for pSS, providing a semiquantitative evaluation of glandular excretory capacity [8]. Widely integrated into clinical practice [9], SGS is valued for its non-invasive nature, favorable safety profile, and high sensitivity, justifying its inclusion in the 2002 international classification criteria [10]. Nevertheless, akin to SGUS, SGS is hampered by poor spatial resolution and fails to directly capture the underlying fibro-inflammatory activity driving glandular dysfunction [11].

Consequently, both conventional structural and functional imaging modalities fail to directly monitor fibrotic activity—the core pathological driver of pSS. To overcome this critical limitation, molecular imaging with targeted radiotracers offers superior diagnostic insight by bridging the crucial gap between anatomical damage and functional impairment.

In this context, fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (FAPI) have emerged as a novel class of radiotracers targeting activated fibroblasts, which are key orchestrators of chronic inflammation and tissue remodeling [12]. Emerging evidence demonstrates that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT can effectively visualize fibroblast-driven pathologies across various oncologic [13, 14] and non-oncologic conditions [15–18]. Notably, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 exhibits minimal physiological uptake in the salivary glands [19], providing an optimal target-to-background ratio for detecting aberrant fibroblast activation and subsequent fibro-inflammatory remodeling. However, its diagnostic potential in autoimmune salivary gland diseases remains largely underexplored.

To address this knowledge gap, this prospective exploratory study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic utility of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT in patients with suspected pSS, comparing its performance directly with conventional salivary gland scintigraphy. We hypothesized that by directly visualizing in vivo fibroblast activation, FAPI PET/CT would yield superior diagnostic accuracy and provide complementary insights into salivary gland dysfunction.

Materials and methods

Participants

This single-center, prospective exploratory study was conducted between January 2020 and December 2023. Consecutive patients presenting with clinically suspected pSS were prospectively enrolled. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (Approval No.: AHSWMU-2020-035) and conducted in strict adherence to the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to any study-related procedures.

The primary inclusion criteria were as follows: [1] a clinical presentation highly suspicious for pSS (e.g., persistent xerostomia or xerophthalmia for ≥ 3 months, Schirmer’s test ≤ 5 mm/5 min, or unstimulated salivary flow rate ≤ 0.1 mL/min), corroborated by positive anti-SSA/SSB autoantibodies or a labial gland biopsy demonstrating focal lymphocytic sialadenitis (focus score ≥ 1/4 mm²); and [2] the successful completion of both [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT and SGS within one week of enrollment [20].

The primary exclusion criteria were as follows: [1] concomitant conditions known to cause salivary gland dysfunction, including prior head and neck radiotherapy, hepatitis C or HIV infection, sarcoidosis, amyloidosis, or IgG4-related disease; [2] a confirmed diagnosis of secondary SS (i.e., coexisting with another established systemic autoimmune disease); [3] use of medications altering salivary secretion within 3 months prior to enrollment; and [4] pregnancy, lactation, or severe hepatic or renal impairment [20].

Imaging protocols

[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT Imaging. No specific patient preparation was required prior to scanning. Following the intravenous administration of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 (1.85 MBq/kg), dual-time-point imaging was performed using a uMI780 PET/CT scanner (United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China). After a localizing scout scan, a low-dose CT scan (120 kV, adaptive current, 3-mm slice thickness, 512 × 512 matrix, 60-cm field of view [FOV]) was acquired for attenuation correction and anatomical mapping. Subsequently, PET imaging was conducted in two phases: an early-phase localized scan (skull vertex to neck base; 1 bed position, 5 min/bed) at 10 min post-injection, and a delayed-phase whole-body scan (skull vertex to feet; 9–10 bed positions, 40% overlap, 3 min/bed) at 40 min post-injection. Finally, PET images were reconstructed using an ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm (2 iterations, 20 subsets).

99mTcO₄- Salivary Gland Scintigraphy. Following the intravenous administration of 99mTcO₄⁻ (185 MBq), dynamic imaging was performed using a dual-head SPECT/CT system (Infinia Hawkeye 4, GE Healthcare) equipped with a low-energy high-resolution (LEHR) collimator. Anterior planar acquisition (128 × 128 matrix, 140-keV photopeak, 20% energy window) was positioned to encompass both the salivary and thyroid glands. Immediately post-injection, a 1-minute perfusion phase (30 frames, 2 s/frame) was acquired, directly followed by a 30-minute functional clearance phase (45 frames, 40 s/frame). To stimulate salivary excretion, a sialogogue (vitamin C) was administered 5 min prior to scan completion. Images were processed using an Xeleris workstation (GE Healthcare).

Image analysis

Visual Analysis. All images were independently evaluated by three experienced nuclear medicine physicians blinded to the patients’ clinical data and group allocations. Interobserver discrepancies were resolved by consensus. Normal [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in the major salivary glands (parotid, submandibular, and sublingual) was defined as homogeneous, symmetric, and low-level, comparable to the background signal of adjacent soft tissues. Conversely, abnormal uptake was defined by either [1] symmetrically intense bilateral uptake or [2] asymmetric uptake, demonstrating markedly altered (elevated or reduced) activity relative to the contralateral gland. For SGS, glandular functional impairment was visually graded (grades 1–3) according to Kim et al. [21]: grade 1 denotes normal uptake and excretion; grade 2 indicates mild-to-moderate dysfunction (reduced uptake and/or delayed excretion); and grade 3 represents severe dysfunction (complete absence of glandular radioactivity).

Quantitative Analysis. To quantify [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake, dual-time-point PET images (10 and 40 min) were analyzed. Volumes of interest (VOIs) encompassing the bilateral major salivary glands were delineated, and the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) was extracted for each. Beyond peak voxel metrics, volumetric parameters were calculated to assess the global glandular FAPI burden. FAPI-avid tissue volume (FTV) was segmented using a background-based threshold, defined as 1.5 times the mean SUV (SUVmean) of the bilateral sternocleidomastoid muscles. Total lesion FAPI (TLF) was subsequently derived as the product of the segmented SUVmean and FTV. To evaluate tracer kinetics between the early and delayed phases, the retention index (RI) was calculated as follows: RI = (Value(40 min) - Value(10 min)) / Value(10 min) × 100%. For subsequent correlation analyses with clinical functional indicators and systemic inflammatory markers, bilateral PET and SGS parameters were averaged to yield patient-level, gland-specific data.

For salivary gland scintigraphy, semiquantitative evaluation utilized regions of interest (ROIs) delineated over the bilateral parotid and submandibular glands, with the temporal regions serving as background references. Software-generated time–activity curves (TACs) were used to extract the maximum pre-stimulation value, the minimum post-stimulation (vitamin C) value, and the background activity. Subsequently, the uptake ratio (UR) and excretion fraction (EF) were calculated: UR = (maximum – background uptake) / background uptake; EF = (maximum – minimum) / (maximum – background uptake) × 100% [22]. Impaired glandular uptake was defined by established thresholds (parotid UR < 2.28; submandibular UR < 1.60) [21]. Final SGS abnormality was confirmed if either visual or semiquantitative criteria were met.

Diagnostic reference standard

Final clinical diagnoses and cohort allocations were established according to the 2016 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for pSS [20]. Detailed ACR/EULAR scoring information for all individual participants is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Participants were stratified into a pSS group (total score ≥ 4) and a non-SS control group (total score < 4). Notably, patients meeting the criteria for secondary SS (score ≥ 4 coexisting with other systemic autoimmune diseases, e.g., SLE or RA) were strictly excluded. All final clinical adjudications were performed by experienced rheumatologists completely blinded to the PET/CT and SGS imaging data.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp.). Continuous data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). To evaluate diagnostic performance, the sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) of visual assessments were calculated alongside their exact 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and corresponding areas under the curve (AUCs) were generated to assess the diagnostic efficacy of PET/CT quantitative parameters (SUVmax, FTV, and TLF). Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was utilized to explore relationships between PET metrics (including the dynamic RI) and clinical functional or inflammatory indicators (SGS excretion fraction, ESR, and IgG). Furthermore, McNemar’s test and Cohen’s kappa (κ) coefficient were applied to determine the intra-modality agreement (visual vs. semiquantitative PET/CT) and inter-modality concordance (PET/CT vs. SGS). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant characteristics

Among the 27 patients screened for suspected pSS, 21 (77.8%) were included in the final analysis; six were excluded due to incomplete PET/CT or SGS data. The final cohort had a mean age of 55.9 ± 10.8 years and was predominantly female (20 females, 1 male). Based on the diagnostic reference standard, 15 patients (71.4%) were confirmed with pSS. The remaining six participants constituted the non-SS control group, all presenting with other systemic autoimmune diseases (two with systemic lupus erythematosus, two with rheumatoid arthritis, and two with mixed connective tissue disease). Visual analysis

Visual evaluation of the early-phase (10-minute) [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT revealed positive glandular uptake in 16 of 21 participants (76.2%). The study cohort ultimately comprised 14 true-positive, 4 true-negative, 2 false-positive, and 1 false-negative results. For diagnosing pSS, PET/CT demonstrated a sensitivity of 93.3% (14/15; 95% CI: 68.1%–99.8%), a specificity of 66.7% (4/6; 95% CI: 22.3%–95.7%), a PPV of 87.5% (14/16; 95% CI: 61.7%–98.4%), an NPV of 80.0% (4/5; 95% CI: 28.4%–99.5%), and an overall accuracy of 85.7% (18/21; 95% CI: 63.7%–97.0%). Furthermore, PET/CT exhibited a 76.2% (16/21) inter-modality concordance with SGS, with 86.7% (13/15) of confirmed pSS patients testing positive on both modalities.

Evaluation of the delayed-phase (40-minute) [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT revealed positive glandular uptake in 13 of 21 participants (61.9%). Overall, the imaging yielded 11 true-positive, 4 true-negative, 2 false-positive, and 4 false-negative results. For diagnosing pSS, delayed-phase PET/CT demonstrated a sensitivity of 73.3% (11/15; 95% CI: 44.9%–92.2%), a specificity of 66.7% (4/6; 95% CI: 22.3%–95.7%), a PPV of 84.6% (11/13; 95% CI: 54.6%–98.1%), an NPV of 50.0% (4/8; 95% CI: 15.7%–84.3%), and an overall accuracy of 71.4% (15/21; 95% CI: 47.8%–88.7%). Furthermore, it exhibited a 61.9% (13/21) inter-modality concordance with SGS, with 66.7% (10/15) of confirmed pSS patients testing positive on both modalities.

Visual evaluation of SGS revealed normal glandular function (grade 1) in two participants (9.5%), mild-to-moderate dysfunction (grade 2) in eight (38.1%), and severe dysfunction (grade 3) in 11 (52.4%). Consequently, the imaging yielded 14 true-positive, 1 true-negative, 5 false-positive, and 1 false-negative results. For diagnosing pSS, SGS demonstrated a sensitivity of 93.3% (14/15; 95% CI: 68.1%–99.8%), a specificity of 16.7% (1/6; 95% CI: 0.4%–64.1%), a PPV of 73.7% (14/19; 95% CI: 48.8%–90.9%), an NPV of 50.0% (1/2; 95% CI: 1.3%–98.7%), and an overall accuracy of 71.4% (15/21; 95% CI: 47.8%–88.7%).

Furthermore, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT incidentally detected extra-salivary uptake in the thyroid gland (n = 4; all confirmed Hashimoto’s thyroiditis) and osteoarticular structures (n = 3; consistent with osteoarthritis). Concurrently, gross morphological alterations—predominantly parotid or submandibular gland atrophy—were observed in eight participants (38.1%) (Fig. 1).

Semiquantitative analysis

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses revealed that the SUVmax of the bilateral submandibular and sublingual glands at both 10 and 40 min exhibited significant diagnostic utility for pSS (AUC > 0.5, P < 0.05) (Tables 2 and 3). For these specific glands, specificity reached 100% (6/6), while sensitivity ranged from 60.0% (9/15) to 66.7% (10/15). Notably, at the 40-minute delayed phase, the left sublingual gland demonstrated higher sensitivity (93.3%, 14/15) but compromised specificity (66.7%, 4/6) relative to the 10-minute early phase. Conversely, volumetric global metabolic parameters, including TLF and FTV, failed to reliably differentiate pSS from non-SS patients (e.g., delayed-phase global TLF: AUC = 0.611, P = 0.436; delayed-phase global FTV: AUC = 0.511, P = 0.938).

Semiquantitative analysis of SGS revealed impaired glandular function in 15 of 21 participants (71.4%). For diagnosing pSS, semiquantitative SGS demonstrated a sensitivity of 80.0% (12/15; 95% CI: 51.9%–95.7%), a specificity of 50.0% (3/6; 95% CI: 11.8%–88.2%), a PPV of 80.0% (12/15; 95% CI: 51.9%–95.7%), an NPV of 50.0% (3/6; 95% CI: 11.8%–88.2%), and an overall accuracy of 71.4% (15/21; 95% CI: 47.8%–88.7%).

Intra-modality agreement of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT

As detailed in Supplementary Table S2, McNemar’s test revealed no significant differences (P > 0.05) between visual and semiquantitative PET/CT assessments for the majority of salivary glands, with the exception of the right parotid gland at 10 min and the bilateral sublingual glands at 40 min. Furthermore, Cohen’s kappa (κ) analysis demonstrated substantial intra-modality concordance between the two analytical approaches, yielding κ values ranging from 0.622 to 0.904 (P < 0.05).

Inter-modality comparison of semiquantitative analyses

Inter-modality agreement

McNemar’s test revealed no significant differences between the semiquantitative parameters of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT and SGS for the parotid and submandibular glands (P > 0.05). However, significant inter-modality concordance was observed exclusively in the left parotid gland at the 10-minute early phase (κ = 0.417, P = 0.038), whereas agreement for the remaining glands failed to achieve statistical significance (Tables 4 and 5).

Diagnostic performance comparison

As detailed in Supplementary Table S3, semiquantitative PET/CT parameters generally yielded higher areas under the curve (AUCs) for both the parotid and submandibular glands across the 10- and 40-minute phases compared to SGS, with the sole exception of the right parotid gland at 40 min. Furthermore, AUCs derived from PET/CT parameters based on predefined cutoffs were consistently superior to those obtained with SGS; however, these inter-modality differences did not achieve statistical significance (P > 0.05).

Correlation with clinical function and systemic activity

Within the confirmed pSS cohort (n = 15), correlation analyses were conducted between [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT parameters and clinical indicators. At the regional level, early-phase submandibular FTV demonstrated a significant positive correlation with the corresponding SGS excretion fraction (r = 0.594; 95% CI: 0.117–0.848; P = 0.019) (Fig. 2A). A comparable correlation was maintained during the 40-minute delayed phase (r = 0.591; 95% CI: 0.112–0.846; P = 0.020) (Supplementary Table S4). Additionally, early-phase submandibular TLF exhibited a strong trend toward statistical significance (r = 0.510, P = 0.052). In stark contrast, no volumetric parameters correlated significantly with the excretion fraction in the parotid glands (all P > 0.700) (Fig. 2B).

Discussion

[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 has emerged as a state-of-the-art fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-targeted radiotracer, demonstrating broad utility across oncologic, inflammatory, and fibrotic imaging [14, 18]. Crucially, its minimal physiological accumulation in the salivary glands yields an optimal target-to-background ratio for delineating local pathology [19]. In this prospective exploratory study, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT exhibited encouraging diagnostic performance for pSS, providing profound structural and molecular insights that effectively complement conventional salivary gland scintigraphy.

Our visual and semiquantitative analyses highlighted that early-phase (10-minute) imaging achieved the optimal diagnostic balance, reflecting the tracer’s rapid specific binding to activated fibroblasts and fast background clearance. Moreover, visual interpretation demonstrated substantial agreement with semiquantitative metrics, supporting its reliability for routine clinical practice. Crucially, peak voxel metrics (SUVmax) significantly outperformed volume-based global parameters (TLF and FTV) in differentiating pSS from non-SS patients. Because early-to-moderate pSS is pathologically characterized by heterogeneous, focal fibro-inflammatory lesions [2], calculating whole-gland metabolic volumes inadvertently incorporates unaffected parenchyma. This introduces a “background dilution effect” that severely attenuates the true pathological signal. Therefore, SUVmax serves as a more robust biomarker for capturing severe focal areas of intense fibroblast activation.

In comparison to conventional SGS, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT exhibited a distinct trend toward superior specificity. Although visual evaluation of SGS yielded high sensitivity, its specificity was notably compromised (16.7%). This limitation is largely attributable to the composition of our small non-SS control cohort, which comprised patients with other systemic autoimmune diseases (e.g., SLE, RA, and MCTD). These overlapping conditions—frequently compounded by age-related changes or concurrent xerogenic medication use—readily induce secondary salivary gland dysfunction, thereby driving the high false-positive rate observed on SGS [23]. In stark contrast, FAPI selectively targets fibroblast activation, a core pathogenic driver intrinsically more specific to pSS. While the limited size of the control cohort (n = 6) necessitates cautious interpretation of these metrics, our findings elegantly highlight the complementary clinical utility of the two modalities: SGS serves as an effective screening tool for generalized excretory decline, whereas FAPI PET/CT provides a precise molecular map of active fibro-inflammatory remodeling.

Furthermore, the distinct advantage of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT is particularly striking when juxtaposed with [18F]FDG. While [18F]FDG primarily reflects acute inflammation and lymphocytic infiltration, its diagnostic utility in the salivary glands is fundamentally compromised by high and variable physiological uptake throughout the head and neck region [24]. Conversely, by selectively targeting activated fibroblasts against a minimal physiologic background, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 yields superior target-to-background ratios. This allows for the precise visualization of irreversible fibrotic remodeling, rather than merely capturing transient inflammatory bursts [25].

Beyond diagnostic performance, we investigated the pathophysiological interplay between PET parameters and clinical functional indicators. Notably, the dynamic retention index (RI) failed to correlate with the SGS excretion fraction. This dissociation is pathophysiologically sound: dynamic FAPI retention dictates the molecular binding kinetics of activated fibroblasts, a process fundamentally distinct from the macroscopic excretory failure quantified by SGS. However, static volumetric parameters revealed profound correlations across a distinct spatial hierarchy. At the regional level, early-phase submandibular FTV correlated significantly with the corresponding excretion fraction, with TLF demonstrating a robust trend. While seemingly counterintuitive, this positive correlation elegantly mirrors the natural morphologic evolution of pSS. Advanced-stage functional exhaustion is consistently accompanied by profound glandular atrophy; consequently, end-stage submandibular glands exhibit both severely impaired excretory capacity and a markedly contracted FAPI-avid absolute volume. The absence of a parallel correlation within the parotid glands further corroborates the asynchronous structural progression of pSS, wherein the submandibular glands are classically affected earlier in the disease course [2, 6, 8].

At the whole-patient level, the negative correlation between early-phase global TLF and systemic ESR—coupled with the lack of correlation with IgG—highlights a crucial clinical paradigm. This discordance suggests that chronic, localized structural damage within the target glands does not necessarily parallel acute systemic inflammatory or immunological surges. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT provides unique, quantifiable insights into fibro-inflammatory remodeling, extending far beyond the scope of routine serological assessments.

For typical patients with suspected pSS, established, cost-effective diagnostic modalities (e.g., serology and SGUS) are generally sufficient for primary diagnosis [20]. Consequently, the true clinical utility of whole-body [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT extends far beyond isolated salivary gland evaluation. As evidenced in our cohort, the radiotracer successfully identified incidental extra-salivary uptake associated with osteoarthritis and Hashimoto’s thyroiditis (Figs. 1 and 3) [26–28]. Because pSS is intrinsically a systemic autoimmune disease frequently complicated by extra-glandular fibro-inflammatory comorbidities [11], the unparalleled advantage of whole-body FAPI PET/CT lies in its capacity to noninvasively map this multi-organ fibro-inflammatory burden in a single examination. This holistic capability firmly justifies its deployment in complex clinical scenarios for comprehensive disease staging and comorbidity screening.

This study has several notable limitations. First, the small, single-center cohort (n = 21) with a pronounced female predominance (20 females, 1 male) inherently limits the statistical power. Although this distribution accurately reflects the natural epidemiology of pSS, our derived optimal SUVmax cutoffs may be overfitted and should be extrapolated to male patients with caution. Furthermore, this restricted sample size—specifically the limited number of confirmed pSS cases (n = 15)—precluded meaningful subgroup stratification based on distinct disease stages or serological profiles. Second, because our predefined protocol was specifically designed to evaluate FAPI PET/CT against functional SGS, SGUS was not routinely performed. Consequently, a direct head-to-head comparison with standardized quantitative SGUS scoring systems could not be conducted. Finally, in alignment with modern non-invasive diagnostic algorithms, only one patient underwent a labial gland biopsy, precluding direct histopathological validation. However, it is crucial to note that standard labial biopsies primarily evaluate lymphocytic infiltration in the minor salivary glands, which is anatomically and pathophysiologically incongruent with the major gland fibro-inflammatory burden targeted by FAPI. Therefore, future large-scale, multicenter studies incorporating targeted major gland biopsies and comprehensive subgroup analyses are warranted to definitively validate these imaging findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT demonstrates promising diagnostic utility and high specificity for pSS by targeting fibroblast activation and associated glandular remodeling. By visualizing early structural and molecular alterations, this novel radiotracer elegantly complements the macroscopic functional evaluation provided by conventional SGS. Furthermore, it yields profound in vivo insights into the asynchronous disease progression and structural atrophy characterizing the affected major salivary glands. Crucially, its whole-body imaging capability delivers unparalleled clinical value for the comprehensive mapping of systemic, multi-organ fibro-inflammatory comorbidities. Ultimately, these findings underscore its immense potential as an advanced, adjunctive diagnostic tool in complex pSS scenarios, warranting rigorous validation in large-scale, multicenter prospective cohorts.

Bubbles clustered inside for almost critical problems
Mohamed Ben Ayed, Khalil El Mehdi a : Department of Mathematics, College of Science, Qassim University, Buraydah 51542, Saudi Arabiab : Faculté des Sciences et Techniques, Université de Nouakchott, Nouakchott 2373, Mauritania.

Abstract




Abstract:
We investigate the existence of blowing-up solutions of the following almost critical problem

-\Delta u+V(x)u=u^{p-\varepsilon},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 4
,
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter,
p+1=(2n)/(n-2)
is the critical Soblolev exponent and the potential
V
is a smooth positive function.
We find solutions which exhibit bubbles clustered inside as
\varepsilon
goes to zero. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first existence result for interior non-simple blowing-up positive solutions to Dirichlet problems in general domains.
Our results are proven through delicate asymptotic
estimates of the gradient of the associated Euler-Lagrange functional.

Key Words:
Partial Differential Equations, Variational analysis, Nonlinear analysis, Critical Sobolev exponent.

2020

1
Introduction and Main Results

In this paper, we study the following almost critical problem

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon}):\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta u+Vu=u^{p-\varepsilon}\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad u>0\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad u=0\,\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 4
,
p+1=(2n)/(n-2)
is the critical Sobolev exponent for the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
, the potential
V
is a
C^{3}
positive function on
\overline{\Omega}
and
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter.

The problem in the form of
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
arises in various physical models, such as quantum transport and non-relativistic Newtonian gravity, as detailed in
[
2
,
7
,
13
]
and their associated references. It is also connected to the Yamabe problem in differential geometry, as discussed for example in
[
11
]
and the references therein.

In the critical case, where
\varepsilon=0
, it is well-established that the existence of solutions to problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
is influenced by the geometry of the domain, the characteristics of the potential
V
, and the dimension
n
. For instance, when
V
is constant and the domain is star-shaped, there are no solutions to the given problem. Due to the vast amount of research on this subject, we will only mention the seminal works by Brezis-Nirenberg
[
8
]
and Bahri-Coron
[
5
]
.

In the subcritical case where
\varepsilon>0
, proving the existence of a solution to the problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
is relatively simple. This can be shown by observing that the infimum

\inf\,\{\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V\,u^{2}\right):\quad u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\,\mbox{ and }\,\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p+1-\varepsilon}=1\,\}

is achieved, thanks to the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p+1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)
.

In
[
1
]
, solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
were constructed that concentrate, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, at interior blow-up points, forming isolated bubbles. By
bubble
here, we refer to the functions defined by

\delta_{a,\lambda}(x):=c_{0}\frac{{\lambda}^{(n-2)/2}}{\left(1+\lambda^{2}|x-a|^{2}\right)^{(n-2)/2}},\quad\mbox{with}\quad c_{0}:=\left[n(n-2)\right]^{\frac{n-2}{4}},\quad a\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\lambda>0

which are the only solutions to the equation
[
9
]

-\Delta u=u^{p},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

In this paper, we focus on the construction of interior bubbling solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
with clustered bubbles at critical points of
V
. These solutions reveal a new phenomenon for positive solutions, namely the existence of non simple blow-up points in the interior for the subcritical problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions in general domains. This phenomenon has been observed for changing-sign solutions on certain symmetric domains
[
14
]
and for positive solutions on the ball
[
15
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first existence result for interior non-simple blowing-up positive solutions to Dirichlet problems in general domains.

In order to formulate our results, we need to introduce some notation. Let
b
be a non-degenerate critical point of
V
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the following function

\mathcal{F}_{b,N}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N}):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}D^{2}V(b)(z_{i},z_{i})-\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq N}\frac{1}{|z_{j}-z_{i}|^{n-2}}.

(2)

The aim of our first result is to construct interior bubbling solutions with clustered bubbles at a critical point of
V
, Namely, we have:

Theorem 1.1

Let
n\geq 4
,
N\geq 2
and
b\in\Omega
be a non-degenerate critical point of
V
. Assume that the function
\mathcal{F}_{b,N}
has a non-degenerate critical point
(\overline{z}_{1},\cdots,\overline{z}_{N})
.
Then, there exists a small positive real
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
has a solution
u_{\varepsilon,b}
with the following property

u_{\varepsilon,b}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i,\varepsilon}\delta_{{a_{i,\varepsilon}},\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}}+v_{\varepsilon},

with

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}\|<c\sqrt{\varepsilon},

(3)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{i,\varepsilon}-1|\leq\varepsilon\ln^{2}\varepsilon\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq N

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\leq\frac{\ln^{\sigma_{n}}\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}^{2}}\leq c\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq N

(5)

\displaystyle|a_{i,\varepsilon}-b+\eta(\varepsilon)\sigma\overline{z}_{i}|\leq\eta(\varepsilon)\eta_{0}\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq N,

(6)

where
\eta_{0}
is a small positive constant,
\sigma_{4}=1
,
\sigma_{n}=0
for
n\geq 5
,
c
is a positive constant,
\eta(\varepsilon)
and
\sigma
are defined in (
54
).

In addition, if for each
N
,
\mathcal{F}_{b,N}
has a non-degenerate critical point, then, problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
has an arbitrary number of non-constant distinct solutions provided that
\varepsilon
is small.

Remark 1.2

Note that, for
N=1
, the existence of solution blowing up at a critical point of
V
with only one bubble has already proved in
[
1
]
.

Our second result addresses the case of multiple interior blow-up points with clustered bubbles. More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 1.3

Let
n\geq 4
,
b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}\in\Omega
be non-degenerate critical points of
V
and

N_{1},\cdots,N_{k}
be non-zero natural integers. For each
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
such that
N_{i}\geq 2
, assume that the function
\mathcal{F}_{b_{i},N_{i}}
has a non-degenerate critical point
(\overline{z}^{i}_{1},\cdots,\overline{z}^{i}_{N_{i}})
. Then, there exists a small positive real
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
has a solution
u_{\varepsilon,b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}}
satisfying

u_{\varepsilon,b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\alpha_{1,i,\varepsilon}\delta_{{a_{1,i,\varepsilon}},\lambda_{1,i,\varepsilon}}+\cdots+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\alpha_{k,i,\varepsilon}\delta_{{a_{k,i,\varepsilon}},\lambda_{k,i,\varepsilon}}+v_{\varepsilon},

with
\|v_{\varepsilon}\|\leq c\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and for each
l\leq k
the coefficients
\alpha_{l,i,\varepsilon}
, the speeds
\lambda_{l,i,\varepsilon}
and the points
a_{l,i,\varepsilon}
satisfy the properties (
4
), (
5
), and (
6
), respectively.

The proof of our results relies on refined asymptotic estimates of the gradient of the associated Euler-Lagrange functional in the neighborhood of bubbles. The goal is to determine the equilibrium conditions satisfied by the concentration parameters. These balancing conditions are derived by testing the equation with vector fields that represent the dominant terms of the gradient relative to the concentration parameters. Analyzing these conditions provides all the necessary information to establish our results.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the parameterization of the neighborhood of bubbles, provide a precise estimate of the infinite-dimensional part, and carry out a delicate asymptotic expansion of the gradient of the associated Euler-Lagrange functional. Section
3
is dedicated to proving our results. Finally, in Section
4
, we present several estimates that are referenced throughout the paper.

2
Analytical Framework

Problem
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
has a variational structure and its Euler-Lagrange functional is defined on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by

I_{V,\varepsilon}\left(u\right):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}Vu^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1-\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p+1-\varepsilon}\quad(\mbox{with }p:=\frac{n+2}{n-2}).

(7)

In the sequel, we will use the following scalar product and its corresponding norm defined by

\left\langle v,w\right\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+\int_{\Omega}Vvw\quad;\quad\|w\|^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}Vw^{2}.

(8)

Notice that, since
V
is a
C^{0}
-positive function on
\overline{\Omega}
, we deduce that this norm is equivalent to the two norms
||.||_{0}
and
||.||_{1}
of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
respectively.

We start by giving some estimates concerning the approximate solutions for
(\mathcal{P}_{V,\varepsilon})
. For
a\in\Omega
and
\lambda>0
, let
\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}
the projection defined by

\begin{cases}-\Delta\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}+V\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}=\delta_{a,\lambda}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}=0\,\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and

\theta_{a,\lambda}:=\delta_{a,\lambda}-\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}.

These functions are introduced in
[
1
]
and they satisfy, for
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}\leq\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad;\quad\left|\lambda\frac{\partial\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}}{\partial\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{n+2}{2}\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad;\quad\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\partial\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}}{\partial a_{j}}\right|\leq\frac{n+2}{2}\pi\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(9)

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{a,\lambda}\leq\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad;\quad\left|\lambda\frac{\partial\theta_{a,\lambda}}{\partial\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{n-2}{2}\theta_{a,\lambda}\quad;\quad\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\partial\theta_{a,\lambda}}{\partial a_{j}}\right|\leq\frac{n-2}{2}\theta_{a,\lambda}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(10)

\displaystyle\theta_{a,\lambda}\leq cR_{a,\lambda}^{1}\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad;\quad\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\partial\theta_{a,\lambda}}{\partial a_{j}}\right|\leq cR_{a,\lambda}^{2}\delta_{a,\lambda}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0}:=\{x\in\Omega:d(x,\partial\Omega)\geq d_{0}\},

(11)

\displaystyle\mbox{ with }R_{a,\lambda}^{1}(x):={\ln^{\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{-2}}+|x-a|^{2}|\ln|x-a||^{\sigma_{n}}\quad;\quad R_{a,\lambda}^{2}(x):=\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-1}|x-a|,

(12)

where
d_{0}
is any fixed small positive constant,
\sigma_{4}:=1
,
\sigma_{n}:=0
for
n\geq 5
and
a_{j}
is the
j^{th}
-component of
a
.

For the proof of these facts, the interested reader is referred to
[
1
]
.

Now, we introduce the parameterization of the neighborhood of bubbles. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu>0
be a small real. We define the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(N,\mu):=\{(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in(0,\infty)^{N}\times\Omega^{N}\times(\mu^{-1},\infty)^{N}

\displaystyle:\,\,|\alpha_{i}-1|<\mu;\,\,d(a_{i},\partial\Omega)>2d_{0};

(13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\ln\lambda_{i}<\mu;\,\,\forall\,\,i\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\varepsilon_{ij}<\mu\,\,\forall\,\,i\neq j\}

where
\varepsilon_{ij}
is defined by

\varepsilon_{ij}:=\Big(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}+\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{(2-n)/2}.

(14)

Observe that, since
\Omega
is bounded, for each
b\in\Omega
and
\lambda>0
satisfying
\varepsilon\ln\lambda
is small, it follows that

\delta_{b,\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}=c_{0}^{-\varepsilon}\lambda^{-\varepsilon(n-2)/2}\Big(1+\frac{n-2}{2}\varepsilon\ln(1+\lambda^{2}|x-b|^{2})\Big)+O\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\ln^{2}(1+\lambda^{2}|x-b|^{2})\Big)=1+o(1).

(15)

For each
(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}(N,\mu)
and
v\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
, we associate a function

\displaystyle u:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})+v:=\underline{u}+v,\quad\mbox{ where }

(16)

\displaystyle E_{a,\lambda}:=\mbox{span}\Big\{\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}},\frac{\partial(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})}{\partial\lambda_{i}},\frac{\partial(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})}{\partial a_{i,j}},\,\,i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},\,\,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\Big\}.

(17)

We notice that the orthogonality is taken with respect to the scalar product defined in (
8
).

In the sequel, we need to study the functional
I_{V,\varepsilon}
and to find some positive critical points
u
having the form (
16
). We start by studying the
v
-part of
u
.

2.1
Estimate of the infinite-dimensional part

Let
(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}(N,\mu)
,
v\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
and
u
be defined in (
16
). In this section, we are going to study the
v
-part of
u
. To this aim, we need to expand
I_{V,\varepsilon}
with respect to
v
.

Observe that, for
b_{1}
,
b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma>2
, it holds

\left|\left|b_{1}+b_{2}\right|^{\gamma}-\left|b_{1}\right|^{\gamma}-{\gamma}\left|b_{1}\right|^{{\gamma}-2}b_{1}b_{2}-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}(\gamma-1)\left|b_{1}\right|^{\gamma-2}b_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\begin{cases}c\left|b_{1}\right|^{\gamma-3}\left|b_{2}\right|^{3}+c|b_{2}|^{\gamma}&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma>3,\\
c\left|b_{2}\right|^{\gamma}&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma\leq 3.\end{cases}

(18)

Thus, using the fact that
\left\langle v,\underline{u}\right\rangle=0
, we get:

\displaystyle I_{V,\varepsilon}\left(u\right)=I_{V,\varepsilon}(\underline{u})-f_{\varepsilon}(v)+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\varepsilon}(v)+o\left({||v||}^{2}\right)\qquad\mbox{ where }

(19)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(v):=\int_{\Omega}(\underline{u})^{p-\varepsilon}v\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad Q_{\varepsilon}(v):=\left||v|\right|^{2}-(p-\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}({\underline{u}})^{p-\varepsilon-1}v^{2}.

(20)

Notice that
Q_{\varepsilon}
is a positive definite quadratic form (see Proposition
3.1
of
[
1
]
) and the linear form
f_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

Lemma 2.1

Let
(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}(N,\mu)
. Then, for each
v\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left|f_{\varepsilon}(v)\right|\leq c\left\|v\right\|\Big(\varepsilon+\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}T_{2}(\lambda_{i})+\sum_{i\neq j}T_{3}(\varepsilon_{ij})\Big),\qquad\mbox{
where }

\displaystyle T_{2}(\lambda):=\big(\lambda^{-2}\,\,\,\mbox{ if }\;\,n\geq 5\quad;\quad\lambda^{-2}\ln\lambda\,\,\,\mbox{ if }\;\,n=4\quad;\quad\lambda^{-1}\,\,\,\mbox{ if }\;\,n=3\big),

\displaystyle T_{3}(t):=(t^{(n+2)/(2(n-2))}(\ln t^{-1})^{(n+2)/2n}\text{ if }\;n\geq 6\quad,\quad t\text{ if }\;n\leq 5).

Proof.
First, observe that, for
\beta_{i}>0
and
\gamma>1
, it holds:

\left|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}\right)^{\gamma}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{i}^{\gamma}\right|\leq\begin{cases}c\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\right)^{{\gamma}/{2}}&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma\leq 2,\\
c\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_{i}^{\gamma-1}\beta_{j}&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma>2.\end{cases}

(21)

Thus, we obtain

f_{\varepsilon}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}^{p-\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)^{p-\varepsilon}v+\sum_{i\neq j}\begin{cases}O\left(\int_{\Omega}\left[(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}})\right]^{\frac{p-\varepsilon}{2}}\left|v\right|\right)&\text{ if }\;n\geq 6,\\
O\left(\int_{\Omega}(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})^{p-\varepsilon-1}(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}})\left|v\right|\right)&\text{ if }\;n\leq 5.\end{cases}

(22)

Observe that, using (
9
) and (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)\left(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)\right]^{\frac{p-\varepsilon}{2}}|v|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}|v|

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\right]^{(n+2)/2n}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|v\right|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{(n-2)/(2n)}.

(23)

Note that, Estimate (E2) of
[
3
]
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)^{n/(n-2)}\leq c\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\ln(\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})

(24)

and using the embedding theorem of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)
, we derive that

\int_{\Omega}\left[(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}})\right]^{\frac{p-\varepsilon}{2}}|v|\leq c\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}\left(\ln\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\|v\|.

(25)

In the same way, for
n\leq 5
(which implies
p-1:=\frac{4}{n-2}>1
), using Lemma 2.2 of
[
10
]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}})^{p-\varepsilon-1}(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}})|v|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{p-1}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}|v|\leq c\|v\|\left(\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{(p-1)\frac{2n}{n+2}}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\leq c\|v\|\varepsilon_{ij}.

(26)

It remains to estimate the first integral in (
22
). Observe that, for
0<b_{2}<b_{1}
and
\gamma>1
, it holds:

\left(b_{1}-b_{2}\right)^{\gamma}=b_{1}^{\gamma}+O\left(b_{1}^{\gamma-1}b_{2}\right)

(27)

thus, we get

\int_{\Omega}\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)^{p-\varepsilon}v=\int_{\Omega}\left(\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}-\theta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)^{p-\varepsilon}v=\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{p-\varepsilon}v+O\left(\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}-\varepsilon}\theta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}|v|\right).

The last integral can be deduced following the proof of Lemma 6.5 of
[
1
]
by using (
15
) and we get

\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{\frac{4}{n-2}-\varepsilon}\theta_{a,\lambda}|v|\leq c\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{4/(n-2)}\theta_{a,\lambda}|v|\leq c\|v\|T_{2}(\lambda).

(28)

For the other one, using (
15
), and the fact that
v\perp\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}
, we get:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{p-\varepsilon}v=

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-\varepsilon}\lambda_{i}^{-\varepsilon\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{p}v+O\left(\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{p}\ln(1+\lambda^{2}_{i}|x-a_{i}|^{2})|v|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\varepsilon\|v\|\left(\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\ln^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(1+\lambda_{i}^{2}|x-a_{i}|^{2})\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\right)=O\left(\varepsilon\|v\|\right).

(29)

Thus, (
28
) and (
29
) imply that

\int_{\Omega}\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)^{p-\varepsilon}v=O\left(\varepsilon\|v\|+\|v\|T_{2}(\lambda_{i})\right).

(30)

Putting (
30
), (
26
) and (
25
) in (
22
), the result follows.

Combining (
19
), Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
Q_{\varepsilon}
is a positive definite quadratic form, we deduce that

Proposition 2.2

Let
(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}(N,\mu)
and
\underline{u}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}
. Then for
\varepsilon
small, there exists a unique
\overline{v}\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\langle\nabla I_{V,\varepsilon}(\underline{u}+\overline{v}),h\rangle=0\quad\forall h\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}.

(31)

Moreover,
\overline{v}
satisfies

\left\|{\overline{v}}\right\|\leq cR_{v}(a,\lambda)\quad\mbox{ with }\quad R_{v}(a,\lambda):=\varepsilon+\sum_{i\neq j}T_{3}(\varepsilon_{ij})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}T_{2}(\lambda_{i})

where
T_{2}
and

are defined in Lemma
2.1
.

2.2
Expansion of the gradient of the associated functional

In this section, we will provide asymptotic expansions of the gradient of functional
I_{V,\varepsilon}
. We begin with the expansion with respect to the gluing parameter
\alpha_{i}^{\prime}s
, specifically proving:

Proposition 2.3

Let
(\alpha,a,\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}(N,\mu)
,
v\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
and
u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}+v
. Then, for
\varepsilon
small and
i\in\left\{1,\cdots,N\right\}
, we have:

\left<\nabla I_{V,\varepsilon}(u),\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right>=\alpha_{i}S_{n}\left(1-\alpha_{i}^{p-1-\varepsilon}\lambda_{i}^{-\varepsilon(n-2)/2}\right)+O(R_{\alpha_{i}})

where

S_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\delta_{0,1}^{2n/(n-2)},\quad\quad R_{\alpha_{i}}:=\varepsilon+T_{2}(\lambda_{i})+\left\|v\right\|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\varepsilon_{ij}

and
T_{2}
is defined in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.
Observe that, since
v\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
, it holds:

\displaystyle\left<\nabla I_{V,\varepsilon},\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right>=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\left<\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}},\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right>-\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p-1-\varepsilon}u\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}.

(32)

First, for
j\neq i
, we have:

\displaystyle\left<\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}},\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)\nabla\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)+\int_{\Omega}V\left(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\Delta+V\right)\left(\pi\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}\right)\left(\pi\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\leq c\varepsilon_{ij}.

(33)

by using Estimate (E1) of
[
3
]
.
Second, for
j=i

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the existence of solutions to a nonlinear elliptic problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions, involving a slightly subcritical exponent for Sobolev embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
. Through a careful asymptotic analysis of the gradient of the associated Euler-Lagrange functional near the ”bubbles,” we successfully constructed solutions that exhibit bubble formation clustered at interior points. The methodology adopted is tailored to variational problems. While our work demonstrates the existence of solutions with clustered bubbles for the given problem, several promising avenues for further research and open questions remain:

(i)

Location of the Concentration Points:

This paper focuses on the construction of interior bubbling solutions with clustered bubbles. A natural extension of this work would be to investigate the existence of solutions that concentrate at non-isolated interior points that approach the boundary in the limit.

(ii)

Impact of the Nature of Critical Points:

The solutions constructed in this paper are based on the assumption that the chosen critical point
b
of the potential
V
is non-degenerate, and that the corresponding function
\mathcal{F}_{b,N}
has a non-degenerate critical point, where
\mathcal{F}_{b,N}
is defined by (
2
) with
N
denotes the number of bubbles clustered at point b. A natural question arises: what occurs if
b
is degenerate, or if
\mathcal{F}_{b,N}
lacks non-degenerate critical points?

(iii)

Impact of Subcritical Exponent:
The present work addresses a slightly subcritical exponent for Sobolev embedding. Future investigations could extend the analysis to exponents that are slightly supercritical, that is, when
\varepsilon<0
but close to zero.

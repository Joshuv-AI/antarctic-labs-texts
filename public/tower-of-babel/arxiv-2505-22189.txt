Abstract
For integers k , ℓ ≥ 3 k,\ell\geq 3 , let ex ⁡ ( n , C → k , C → ℓ ) \ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}) denote the maximum number of directed cycles of length k k in any oriented graph on n n vertices which does not contain a directed cycle of length ℓ \ell . We establish the order of magnitude of ex ⁡ ( n , C → k , C → ℓ ) \ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}) for every k k and ℓ \ell and determine its value up to a lower error term when k ∤ ℓ k\nmid\ell and ℓ \ell is large enough. Additionally, we calculate the value of ex ⁡ ( n , C → k , C → ℓ ) \ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}) for some other specific pairs ( k , ℓ ) (k,\ell) showing that a diverse class of extremal constructions can appear for small values of ℓ \ell .



1
Introduction

One of the fundamental questions in extremal graph theory, commonly referred to as the Turán problem, is: ‘how many edges can an
n
-vertex graph have if it does not contain graph
F
as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph?’. The asymptotic value of this number is known for many classes of graphs, e.g. cliques by Turán’s theorem
[
25
]
or non-bipartite graphs
[
9
]
, but remains unknown for many bipartite graphs, e.g. even cycles
[
23
]
.

Since Turán’s theorem was proved, many variants of the original problem were investigated. One straightforward variation is the following
generalized Turán problem

[
2
]
: determining the maximum number of copies of a given graph
H
in an
n
-vertex graph which does not contain a forbidden graph
F
. We denote this quantity as
\text{ex}(n,H,F)
.
Particular attention was paid to the case when both
H
and
F
are cycles – starting with the Erdős pentagon conjecture
[
11
]
on the value of
\text{ex}(n,C_{5},C_{3})
, through its solutions
[
17
,
18
]
, to general results on the order of magnitude
[
14
]
or exact values
[
5
,
13
,
15
]
for other cycle-lengths.

In this work we consider an analogous question in the setting of oriented graphs (directed graphs in which every pair of vertices is connected by at most one arc). More specifically, we focus on
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
– the maximum number of directed cycles of length
k
in any
n
-vertex oriented graph which does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to a directed cycle of length
\ell
.

In Section
2
we prove an exact order of magnitude of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
for every
k
and
\ell
, by showing that if
k\nmid\ell
, then
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})=\Theta(n^{k})
, while if
k\mid\ell
, then
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})=\Theta(n^{k-1})
(throughout this paper, when using asymptotic notation, we treat
k
and
\ell
of each subproblem as constants). We refer to the case
k\nmid\ell
as the
dense setting
, and the case
k\mid\ell
as the
sparse setting
.

In the dense setting, a natural candidate for the extremal construction is a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
– it has
\Theta(n^{k})
copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
and does not contain cycles of length not divisible by
k
, in particular
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
.
The leading constant can be further improved by reducing the number of blobs to the smallest divisor of
k
greater than
2
which does not divide
\ell
(call this number
d
). We consider here only divisors greater than
2
to enable the existence of a blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptd}
.
In Section
3.1
we prove that a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptd}
is indeed the best construction when
\ell
is large enough (roughly at least
2k^{2}
), but in the case
d\geq 5
under an additional assumption that
k
is odd or
\ell
is even.

This additional assumption is needed, because if
k
is even and
\ell
is odd, then we can consider an oriented graph that somewhat imitates an “oriented blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt2}
” – a random orientation of a complete balanced bipartite graph. This graph contains many copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
when
2\mid k
and no
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
when
2\nmid\ell
. However, it has less copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
than a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptd}
if
d\leq 4
, that is why this construction is optimal for large enough
\ell
if
d\geq 5
,
2\mid k
and
2\nmid\ell
, as shown in Section
3.2
.

Then, in Section
3.3
, we determine the value of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
for some particular values of
k
and
\ell
, showing that when
\ell
is comparable to
k
, then diverse extremal constructions can appear, including iterated blow-ups (for
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3})
), blow-ups of cycles with transitive tournaments in blobs (for
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3})
), or blow-ups of graphs other than cycles (for
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7})
).

In Section
4
we consider the sparse setting and determine the value of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6})
up to a lower order error term.

Finally, in Section
5
, we consider an analogous question in the setting of directed graphs.
From the proven theorems for oriented graphs we derive an optimal bound, up to a lower order error term, for the maximum number of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in a directed graph not containing
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
for large enough
\ell
not divisible by
k
.

1.1
Preliminaries

An
oriented graph
is a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a set of
vertices
and
E\subset V\times V
is a set of ordered pairs called
arcs
with the property that for every
u,v\in V
either
uv\notin E
or
vu\notin E
. In particular,
vv\notin E
for any
v\in V
. From now on, unless specified otherwise, we will refer to oriented graphs simply as graphs. For
v\in V
, by
N^{+}(v)=\{u\in V:vu\in E\}
we denote the
out-neighborhood
of
v
and by
N^{-}(v)=\{u\in V:uv\in E\}
the
in-neighborhood
of
v
.

The
directed path

\overrightarrow{P\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
is a graph on vertex set
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
with
k-1
arcs
v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3},\ldots,v_{k-1}v_{k}
. We say that such a path
starts
in
v_{1}
,
ends
in
v_{k}
, while
v_{2},\ldots,v_{k-1}
are its
internal vertices
. The
length
of a directed path is the number of its arcs.
We denote by
N^{+}_{i}(v)
the set of end vertices of directed paths of length
i
starting in
v
.
N^{-}_{i}(v)
is defined analogously.
The
directed cycle

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
is a graph on vertex set
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
with
k
arcs
v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3},\ldots,v_{k}v_{1}
.
The graph
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}}}
is a graph on vertex set
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
and
k
arcs
v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3},\ldots,v_{k-1}v_{k},v_{1}v_{k}
. Note that in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}}}
one arc is oriented differently than in
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
. In particular, the graph
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}}}
is the transitive tournament on
3
vertices, and is therefore sometimes referenced as a
transitive triangle

\overrightarrow{T_{3}}
.
The graph
\overrightarrow{K}_{n,n}
is an oriented graph with vertex set
A\cup B
, where
|A|=|B|=n
, containing all arcs
ab
for any
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

A
blow-up
of a graph
G
on
k
vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
is any graph
G^{\prime}
on a vertex set partitioned into disjoint independent sets
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
, in which there is an arc
uv
for
u\in V_{i}
and
v\in V_{j}
if and only if there is an arc
v_{i}v_{j}
in
G
. The sets
V_{i}
are called
blobs
. A blow-up is
balanced
if
||V_{i}|-|V_{j}||\leq 1
for any
i,j
. Note that for every integer
n\geq k
there exists a balanced blow-up of
G
on
n
vertices.

By a
copy
of a graph
H
in
G
we mean a subgraph of
G
isomorphic to
H
. Observe that a set of
|V(H)|
vertices in
G
may contain more than one copy of
H
.
As mentioned in the Introduction, by
\ex(n,H,F)
we denote the maximum number of copies of
H
among
n
-vertex graphs not containing a copy of
F
.
Similarly, by
\ex(n,H,\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{k}\})
we denote the maximum number of copies of
H
among
n
-vertex graphs which contain no copies of
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
.

An oriented graph
H
is a
homomorphic image
of an oriented graph
F
if there exists a surjective map
\varphi:V(F)\rightarrow V(H)
such that
\varphi(u^{\prime})\varphi(v^{\prime})\in E(H)
for every arc
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(F)
and for every arc
uv\in E(H)
there exists an arc
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(F)
satisfying
\varphi(u^{\prime})\varphi(v^{\prime})=uv
.
Note that if
H
is a homomorphic image of
F
, then a sufficiently large blow-up of
H
contains
F
as a subgraph.

The following observation is a direct consequence of the directed version of Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma
[
1
]
and the blow-up lemma
[
20
]
.

Observation 1
.

For any oriented graphs
F
and
G
, if
G
does not contain
F
as a subgraph, then by removing
o(n^{2})
arcs one can guarantee that
G
does not contain any homomorphic image of
F
.

Oftentimes, when calculating the number of copies of
H
in an
n
-vertex graph
G
, we count the number of homomorphisms from
H
to
G
rather than subgraphs of
G
isomorphic to
H
, because they are much simpler to calculate. This makes no difference when we are interested in a value up to
o(n^{|V(H)|})
term, since the fraction of homomorphisms that are not injective is of order
o(n^{|V(H)|})
.

In the proofs, we often use a corollary from the following theorem of Brauer
[
6
]
generalizing Sylvester’s solution
[
24
]
of the well-known Frobenius Coin Problem.

Theorem 2

(Brauer
[
6
]
)
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
be positive integers that satisfy
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})=1
. Denote
d_{i}=\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})
. Then any integer

\ell>a_{2}\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}+a_{3}\frac{d_{2}}{d_{3}}+\ldots+a_{k}\frac{d_{k-1}}{d_{k}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}

can be represented as
x_{1}a_{1}+x_{2}a_{2}+\ldots+x_{k}a_{k}
for non-negative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.

Corollary 3
.

For positive integers
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
denote
d_{i}=\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})
. Then any integer
\ell
divisible by
d_{k}
and satisfying

\ell>a_{2}\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}+a_{3}\frac{d_{2}}{d_{3}}+\ldots+a_{k}\frac{d_{k-1}}{d_{k}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}

can be represented as
x_{1}a_{1}+x_{2}a_{2}+\ldots+x_{k}a_{k}
for non-negative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.
In particular, when
k=2
, any integer
\ell
divisible by
d_{2}=\gcd(a_{1},a_{2})
and satisfying
\frac{\ell}{d_{2}}\geq\left(\frac{a_{1}}{d_{2}}-1\right)\left(\frac{a_{2}}{d_{2}}-1\right)
can be expressed as
\ell=a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}
for some non-negative integers
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
2
for integers
\frac{a_{1}}{d_{k}},\ldots,\frac{a_{k}}{d_{k}}
obtaining that all integers greater than

\frac{a_{2}}{d_{k}}\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}+\frac{a_{3}}{d_{k}}\frac{d_{2}}{d_{3}}+\ldots+\frac{a_{k}}{d_{k}}\frac{d_{k-1}}{d_{k}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{d_{k}}

can be represented as
x_{1}\frac{a_{1}}{d_{k}}+x_{2}\frac{a_{2}}{d_{k}}+\ldots+x_{k}\frac{a_{k}}{d_{k}}
for non-negative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.
Multiplying by
d_{k}
we obtain the conclusion of the corollary.
∎

In everything that follows, when using the asymptotic notation
o
,
\mathcal{O}
and
\Theta
we treat
k
and
\ell
as constants.

Order of magnitude

In this section we show the following theorem determining the order of magnitude of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
for every
k
and
\ell
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k,\ell\geq 3
be different integers. If
k\nmid\ell
, then
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})=\Theta(n^{k})
, while if
k\mid\ell
, then
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})=\Theta(n^{k-1})
.

Proof.

Clearly, the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in an
n
-vertex graph is upper bounded by
n^{k}
. Since a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
(see Figure
1(a)
) does not contain any cycles of length not divisible by
k
and contains asymptotically
\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}
copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
, we immediately obtain the first part of the theorem.

For the second part, note that a blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
with one blob of size
1
and remaining blobs balanced (see Figure
1(b)
) contains no cycles of length different than
k
and has asymptotically
\left(\frac{n-1}{k-1}\right)^{k-1}
copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
. This gives the lower bound for the second part of the theorem.

3
The dense setting

When determining
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
in the dense setting, our usual framework is to take any extremal graph
G
and remove
o(n^{2})
arcs using Observation
1
to delete from
G
all homomorphic images of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
, that is, closed directed walks of length
\ell
. Note that this way we remove only
o(n^{k})
copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
. Finally, we delete from
G
all arcs and vertices that do not occur in any
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
. We refer to a graph obtained in such a process as a
(k,\ell)
-cleared
graph.

Definition 5
.

For any integers
k,\ell\geq 3
such that
k\nmid\ell
, an oriented graph
G
is
(k,\ell)
-
cleared
if it does not contain any homomorphic image of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
and every arc and vertex of
G
occurs in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
.

The advantage of
(k,\ell)
-cleared graphs is that they often do not contain specific small subgraphs, which greatly simplifies the proofs.

Observation 6
.

For any integer
k
, neither a
(k,k+1)
-cleared graph nor a
(k,2k-1)
-cleared graph contains a transitive triangle.

Proof.

Let us assume that a
(k,\ell)
-cleared graph contains a transitive triangle. Since each of its arcs occurs in
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
, there are directed paths of length
k-1
between the endpoints of the arcs. Together with the arcs of the transitive triangle, they form a homomorphic image of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk+1}
and a homomorphic image of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt2k-1}
, forbidden if
\ell=k+1
or
\ell=2k-1
. This is visualized for
k=4
in Figure
3
.

3.1
The cycle blow-up case

As mentioned in the Introduction, in this subsection we prove the following theorem, providing assumptions for
k
and
\ell
under which the value of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})
is attained (up to a lower order error term) in a blow-up of a directed cycle.

Theorem 8
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be integers satisfying
k\geq 3
,
\ell\geq 2(k-1)^{2}
, and
k\nmid\ell
. Denote by
d
the smallest integer greater than
2
that divides
k
but does not divide
\ell
. If
2\nmid k
,
2\mid\ell
or
d\leq 4
, then
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})=\frac{n}{k}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{k-1}+o(n^{k})
.

The lower bound is achieved by a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptd}
.
Before we proceed to the proof of the upper bound, we show the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a graph satisfying the following property: for any copy
C
of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
, each vertex of
G
has at most
\frac{2k}{d}
neighbors in
C
. Then the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
is at most
\frac{n}{k}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{k-1}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on a method developed by Král’, Norin, and Volec
[
21
]
. We say that a sequence
(v_{i})_{i=0}^{k-1}
of
k
vertices is
good
if
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{k-1}
is a directed cycle in
G
. Note that there are
k
different good sequences corresponding to a single
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
.

For a fixed good sequence
D=(v_{i})_{i=0}^{k-1}
, we define the following sets:

\displaystyle A_{0}(D)

\displaystyle=V(G),

\displaystyle A_{i}(D)

\displaystyle=N^{+}(v_{i-1})\textrm{ for }1\leq i\leq k-2,

\displaystyle A_{k-1}(D)

\displaystyle=N^{+}(v_{k-2})\cap N^{-}(v_{0}).

Define the
weight

w(D)
of a good sequence
D
as

\displaystyle w(D)=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left|A_{i}(D)\right|^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\left|A_{i}(D)\right|^{-1}.

This quantity has the following probabilistic interpretation. Suppose we want to sample
k
vertices
w_{0},\ldots,w_{k-1}
as follows. We start with choosing
w_{0}
uniformly at random from the set of all vertices of
G
. Next, we pick uniformly at random some out-neighbor of
w_{0}
to be
w_{1}
. In general,
w_{j}
is a random vertex from the set
A_{j}((w_{i})_{i=0}^{j-1})
. (Note that the definition of
A_{j}(D)
depends only on the first
j
elements of a sequence
D
.) Then,
w(D)
is just the probability that the sequence
(w_{i})_{i=0}^{k-1}
obtained in this random process is equal to
D
.

In particular, the sum of the weights of all good sequences is at most one, since it is the sum of probabilities of pairwise disjoint events.

We similarly define sets
B_{i}(D)
when vertices of a good sequence
D=(v_{i})_{i=0}^{k-1}
are sampled in the reversed order
v_{0},v_{k-1},\ldots,v_{1}
:

\displaystyle B_{0}(D)

\displaystyle=V(G),

\displaystyle B_{1}(D)

\displaystyle=N^{-}(v_{0}),

\displaystyle B_{i}(D)

\displaystyle=N^{-}(v_{k-i+1})\textrm{ for }2\leq i\leq k-2,

\displaystyle B_{k-1}(D)

\displaystyle=N^{-}(v_{2})\cap N^{+}(v_{0}).

Similarly, we define the corresponding weight

\displaystyle\overline{w}(D)=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left|B_{i}(D)\right|^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\left|B_{i}(D)\right|^{-1}.

For a copy
\mathcal{D}
of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
on vertices
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k-1}
, we define corresponding good sequences
D_{j}=(v_{j},v_{j+1},\ldots,v_{j+k-1})
for
j=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, where the indices are taken modulo
k
.

Denoting
n_{i,j}=\left|A_{i}(D_{j})\right|
and
\overline{n}_{i,j}=\left|B_{i}(D_{j})\right|
we have the following inequalities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(w(D_{j})+\overline{w}(D_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}w(D_{j})\overline{w}(D_{j})\right)^{1/2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}n_{i,j}\overline{n}_{i,j}\right)^{-1/2k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2k(k-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(n_{i,j}+\overline{n}_{i,j}\right)\right)^{-(k-1)}.

Claim 10
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(n_{i,j}+\overline{n}_{i,j}\right)\leq 2k(k-1)\frac{n}{d}.

Proof.

For any vertex
u\in V(G)
, let
n_{i,j}(u)=1
if
u\in A_{i}(D_{j})
and
n_{i,j}(u)=0
otherwise. Similarly, let
\overline{n}_{i,j}(u)=1
if
u\in B_{i}(D_{j})
and
\overline{n}_{i,j}(u)=0
otherwise. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(n_{i,j}+\overline{n}_{i,j}\right)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(n_{i,j}(u)+\overline{n}_{i,j}(u)\right).

For any fixed
1\leq i<k-1
, the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(n_{i,j}(u)+\overline{n}_{i,j}(u))
is equal to the number of neighbors of
u
in
\mathcal{D}
, hence is at most
\frac{2k}{d}
. Also,
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}n_{k-1,j}(u)
is not greater than the number of in-neighbors of
u
in
\mathcal{D}
, and
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\overline{n}_{k-1,j}(u)
is not greater than the number of out-neighbors of
u
in
\mathcal{D}
, hence

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(n_{k-1,j}(u)+\overline{n}_{k-1,j}(u))\leq\frac{2k}{d}

as well. By summing over all choices of

we get the desired inequality.
∎

Using Claim
10
above we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(w(D_{j})+\overline{w}(D_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{-(k-1)}

Recall that
\sum_{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}w(D_{j})\leq 1
and
\sum_{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\overline{w}(D_{j})\leq 1
, where the first summation is over all copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
. Denoting by
M
the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
, by summing over all copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{-(k-1)}M.

Hence

\displaystyle M\leq\frac{n}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{k-1}

as wanted.
∎

The random bipartite case

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

•

M_{ij}=0

m+1\leq i,j\leq n

•

For

or
M_{ji}=1

The number of homomorphic images of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}

\frac{1}{k}\text{tr}(M^{k})=\frac{1}{k}(\lambda_{1}^{k}+\ldots+\lambda_{n}^{k})

\text{Re}\,\lambda_{1}\geq\text{Re}\,\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\text{Re}\,\lambda_{n}
. Note the following properties:

•

If
\lambda\in\sigma(M)

\bar{\lambda}\in\sigma(M)

is a real matrix and so its characteristic polynomial has real coefficients.

•

If
\lambda\in\sigma(M)
, then
-\lambda\in\sigma(M)
, because when
\overrightarrow{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is an eigenvector for the eigenvalue
\lambda

\overrightarrow{v^{\prime}}=(-v_{1},\ldots,-v_{m},v_{m+1},\ldots,v_{n})
is an eigenvector for

\text{Re}\,\lambda_{n+1-i}=-\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}
.

•

By the non-negative variant of the Perron-Frobenius theorem (see e.g.
[
22
]
, Theorem 4.2),
\lambda_{1}
is real, non-negative and for every

|\lambda_{i}|\leq\lambda_{1}
.

We start with proving a bound on the sum of the positive real parts of the eigenvalues of

. In the proof we will make use of the following proposition, which is a special case of Theorem 2 from
[
12
]
.

Proposition 19

(Ky Fan
[
12
]
)
.

If
\rho_{1}\geq\rho_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\rho_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\frac{M+M^{T}}{2}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\rho_{i}
.

Claim 20
.

\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m(n-m)}

Proof.

The matrix
A=\frac{M+M^{T}}{2}
is a block matrix, where
A_{ij}=0
when
1\leq i,j\leq m
or
m<i,j\leq n
and
A_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}
otherwise. In other words, the matrix
2A
is the adjacency matrix of
K_{m,n-m}
. Therefore, the eigenvalues of
A
are

(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m(n-m)},0,0,\ldots,0,-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m(n-m)}\right)

and Proposition
19
gives
\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\rho_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m(n-m)}
.
∎

We are now ready to bound
\text{tr}(M^{k})
. Denoting
\rho=\lambda_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}^{k}=2\left(\rho^{k}+\sum_{i=2}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}^{k}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}2\left(\rho^{k}+\sum_{i=2}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}k\rho^{k-1}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{\leq}}2\left(\rho+\sum_{i=2}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\right)^{k}=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\right)^{k}\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m(n-m)}\right)^{k}\leq 2\left(\frac{n}{4}\right)^{k},

where the respective inequalities follow from the following properties:

(
a
)

Because
\text{tr}(M^{k})
is real.

(
b
)

As noted earlier,
\text{Re}\,\lambda_{n+1-i}=-\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}
, so
\text{Re}\,\lambda_{n+1-i}^{k}=\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}^{k}
since
k
is even.

(
c
)

It follows from the fact that for any complex number
z
with
\text{Re}\,z\geq 0
and
k\equiv 2\pmod{4}
we have
\text{Re}\,z^{k}\leq k|z|^{k-1}\text{Re}\,z
. The proof can be found in
[
16
]
, but we include it for completeness. Let
\alpha=\text{arg}\,(z)
. By symmetry, we can assume that
\alpha\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Let
\beta=\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha
. We first show that
-\cos k\beta\leq k\sin\beta
. Indeed, if
0\leq\beta\leq\frac{\pi}{2k}
, then
-\cos k\beta\leq 0\leq k\sin\beta
, while if
\beta>\frac{\pi}{2k}
, then
\sin\beta>\frac{1}{k}
and
-\cos k\beta\leq 1<k\sin\beta
. Having this, we have

\text{Re}\,z^{k}=|z|^{k}\cos k\alpha=-|z|^{k}\cos k\beta\leq k|z|^{k}\sin\beta=k|z|^{k}\cos\alpha=k|z|^{k-1}\text{Re}\,z.

(
d

Binomial expansion of the right-hand side yields the left-hand side and possible additional terms, which are non-negative since
\text{Re}\,\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for

The proven inequality shows that the number of homomorphic images of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}

. If

(a)

w\in A_{1}(D_{i}),A_{1}(D_{i+2})

Theorem 29
.

\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7})=\frac{27}{16}\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}+o(n^{5})
.

Proof.

The lower bound is achieved by the following construction shown in Figure
8
– take an
unbalanced
blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
, where arbitrary two of the blobs have size
\frac{3}{10}n
each and the two other blobs have size
\frac{2}{10}n
each. Add arcs inside the two larger blobs to create the balanced complete bipartite graphs with all arcs directed in the same direction. This forms a graph with
6
blobs distributed in
4
groups. Each
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
passes through all the groups and
5
of the
6
blobs.

Figure 8
:
An extremal graph for
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7})

To prove the upper bound, consider an extremal
(5,\,7)
-cleared graph
G
. We say that two vertices
u,v
of
G
are
equivalent
if
N^{+}(u)=N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(u)=N^{-}(v)
. Group vertices of
G
into equivalence classes (blobs) and let

be the graph on such blobs (these will correspond to the blobs in our construction) with an arc between the blobs if there is an arc between all vertices in the respective blobs.

Clearly, all the vertices in one blob are contained in the same number of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}

x
and
y
there is no
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G^{\prime}
containing both
x
and

and say that the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G
containing a vertex in

is at least as large as the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
containing a vertex in
y
. Then by moving all the vertices from
y
to

we do not decrease the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G
. Therefore, we may assume that for any two blobs, there is a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G^{\prime}

are connected by a directed path of length
2
in some direction.

Since
G
is a
(5,\,7)
-cleared graph, it cannot contain
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
, as otherwise it contains a homomorphic image of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7}
. Thus, also
G^{\prime}
does not contain
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
.

Claim 30
.

If two blobs have a common out-neighbor or a common in-neighbor, then they are adjacent in
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Consider a blob
x
with two out-neighbors
y
and
z
in
G^{\prime}
. If
y
and
z
are not adjacent, then there must be a path of length
2
between
y
and
z
, without loss of generality, there is a path
ywz
. But then
x,\,y,\,w,\,z
form a forbidden
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
giving a contradiction. Analogous reasoning can be done for two in-neighbors.
∎

This allows us to prove a structural description of
G^{\prime}
.

Claim 31
.

The graph
G^{\prime}
forms a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
, possibly with some extra arcs.

Proof.

Let
C=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
be a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G^{\prime}
. Due to the absence of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
, all other arcs among the vertices of

are of form
x_{i}x_{i+2}
(indices taken modulo
5
). There can be at most
3
such arcs, as otherwise a
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
is formed. It is straightforward to verify that such a graph contains no
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
other than
C
. Since
G
contains far more copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
than a blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, there is a blob of
G^{\prime}
not contained in
C
. Moreover, there is a blob adjacent to a vertex in
C
, because any two blobs appear in a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
.
We will show that
G^{\prime}
contains a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
.

Assume that there is an arc
x_{1}y
(the opposite case
yx_{1}
is analogous), where
y
is a blob not contained in
C
. Then
x_{2},y\in N^{+}(x_{1})
, so from Claim
30
, there is an arc between
x_{2}
and
y
.
If this arc is
yx_{2}
, then
x_{1}yx_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
forms a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
.
Otherwise, this arc is
x_{2}y
and we can use Claim
30
once again to deduce that there is an arc between
x_{3}
and
y
. Since the arc
x_{3}y
forms a forbidden
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}}}
, this must be the arc
yx_{3}
. In this case,

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}

The above reasoning can be also applied to a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
in

the same way as to a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
. Therefore, if there is another blob not contained in the proven
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
to
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7}
, giving a contradiction.
Thus,

consists of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
possibly with some extra arcs.
∎

Let
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}v_{6}
be a
6

. It cannot contain any arc of the form

(indices are taken modulo 6), because then we have the forbidden

v_{i+2}v_{i+4}

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}

Theorem 32
.

\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt8})=\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}+o(n^{5})

\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt13})=\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}+o(n^{5})

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
. By Observation
7

\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt8})\leq\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}+o(n^{5})
.
The proof follows similar lines as the proof of Theorem

.

Let
G
be an extremal
(5,8)
-cleared graph and
G^{\prime}
be the graph on equivalence classes (blobs) of
G
. First, recall that
G
being
(5,8)
-cleared implies that every arc of

(and consequently

) is contained in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
.
Moreover, similarly as in the proof of Theorem
29
, we may assume that for any two blobs, there is a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in
G^{\prime}
containing both of them. This implies that any two non-adjacent blobs in
G^{\prime}
are connected by a directed path of length
3
in some direction.
Since
G
is a
(5,\,8)
-cleared graph, it cannot contain
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}}}
, as otherwise it contains a homomorphic image of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt8}
. Assume now that two non-adjacent blobs
u
and
v
share a common in-neighbor or out-neighbor

. Then, a directed path of length
3
between
u
and

creates a forbidden homomorphic image of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}}}
. Therefore, two non-adjacent blobs cannot share any in-neighbors or out-neighbors.

Using this fact, we will argue that for any

, a blob connected to
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti}
extends it to
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti+1}
. If
x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{i}
is a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti}
and there is an arc
x_{1}y
in
G^{\prime}
, then we have an arc
yx_{2}
creating
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti+1}
or an arc
x_{2}y
. In the latter case, we have an arc
yx_{3}
creating
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti+1}
or an arc
x_{3}y
. This finally implies that there is an arc
yx_{4}
creating
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pti+1}
or an arc
x_{4}y
forming a forbidden
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nrightarrow}}{{{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}}}
in

.
Therefore,

is a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptm}

,
6
or
7

If
m=5
, this
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}

(5,8)
-cleared graphs do not contain
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
and
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
. Optimizing the sizes of the blobs shows that a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}

\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, implying the desired bound.

In the case
m=6
, let
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}v_{6}
be the
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
in
G^{\prime}
. Due to the absence of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
and
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
, the only possible additional arcs are of the type
v_{i}v_{i+2}
(indices are taken modulo
6
). Moreover, for any two such arcs
v_{i}v_{i+2}
and
v_{j}v_{j+2}
we have

, as otherwise we have a forbidden
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
. In particular, there are at most two such arcs. Without loss of generality, these are
v_{1}v_{3}
and
v_{2}v_{4}
. But then there is no
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
containing

is
(5,8)
-cleared.

For
m=7
, let
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}v_{6}v_{7}
be the
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt7}

. We do not have arcs of the form

v_{1}v_{4}
such that
v_{2}v_{6}
and
v_{3}v_{7}
are not in
G^{\prime}
. The obtained cycle

must be induced.
The arc
v_{1}v_{2}
needs to be contained in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, but this
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
must pass either through an arc of the form
v_{i}v_{i+3}
(which is impossible since
v_{2}v_{6}
,
v_{3}v_{7}
are not in

and other such arcs would be inside

) or through two arcs of the form

(also impossible, because one of them would have to be an arc inside

).

We are left with the case where the additional arcs are only of the form
v_{i}v_{i+2}
. Let
t
denote the number of these arcs. We consider separate cases depending on the value of
t
, showing in each of them an arc that is not contained in any
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, which is a contradiction.

•

Case
t\leq 2
: Let
v_{i}v_{i+2}
be one of those arcs. It is easy to verify that the arc
v_{i}v_{i+1}
is not contained in any
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
.

•

Case
t=3
: We say that two arcs
v_{i}v_{i+2}
,
v_{j}v_{j+2}

intersect
if
i=j\pm 1
. Note that if there is no pair of intersecting arcs, then
G^{\prime}
contains forbidden
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
. Thus, there is an intersecting pair
v_{i}v_{i+2}
,
v_{i+1}v_{i+3}
. But then the arc
v_{i+1}v_{i+2}
is not contained in
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
.

•

Case
t\geq 4
. In this case, there exist two arcs sharing a vertex:
v_{i-2}v_{i}
and
v_{i}v_{i+2}
. This creates a cycle
v_{i-3}v_{i-2}v_{i}v_{i+2}v_{i+3}
that must be induced. The only possible remaining arcs are
v_{i-3}v_{i-1}
,
v_{i-1}v_{i+1}
, and
v_{i+1}v_{i+3}
. For the arc
v_{i-2}v_{i-1}
to be contained in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, both
v_{i-1}v_{i+1}
and
v_{i+1}v_{i+3}
must be in
G^{\prime}
. Similarly, for
v_{i+1}v_{i+2}
to be contained in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, both
v_{i-3}v_{i-1}
and
v_{i-1}v_{i+1}
must be in
G^{\prime}
. But then
v_{i-3}v_{i-1}v_{i+1}v_{i+3}
is a forbidden
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
.

The contradiction obtained in each case concludes the proof.
∎

In this subsection, we considered all possible forbidden cycles in the dense case, except when the forbidden cycle is
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
. As we argue below, in this case, a different extremal example occurs. We leave finding its exact structure as an open problem.

Question 33
.

What is the value of
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4})
?

Interestingly enough, this case remains open and without a believable conjecture. An upper bound
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4})\leq 0.0567\binom{n}{5}
may be obtained using Flagmatic. In the following, we provide a (rather complicated) construction that gives a lower bound
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4})\geq 0.0517\binom{n}{5}
. Note that among all previously considered constructions that do not contain
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
, the best bound is achieved in an iterated blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
, but it is only around
0.0385\binom{n}{5}
.

A far better construction is obtained by splitting vertices equally into
7
blobs,
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{7}
and adding all arcs of the form
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+1}
and
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+3}
(with indices taken modulo
7
). Note that each of the diagonal arcs
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+3}
forms a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
with four
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+1}
arcs. This construction gives a lower bound of around
0.0499\binom{n}{5}
. Surprisingly, one can improve this even further.

We tweak the construction described above by flipping direction of some of the
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+1}
arcs (while leaving orientation of the
V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i+3}
arcs the same).
For arcs between consecutive blobs, we determine orientation as follows. We identify vertices in each blob with points distributed uniformly in the segment
[0,1]
and consider a non-decreasing function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
defined as
f(x)=\min\{1,x+c\}
for some fixed constant
c>\frac{1}{3}
. Now, for two blobs
V_{i}
and
V_{i+1}
, we put arc
xy
, for
x\in V_{i}
,
y\in V_{i+1}
, whenever
f(x)\geq y
and arc
yx
if
f(x)<y
. In particular, the out-neighborhood of
x
in
V_{i+1}
are the vertices of
V_{i+1}
in the subset
[0,f(x)]
and the in-neighborhood of
x
in
V_{i+1}
are the vertices in the subset
(f(x),1]
.

Observe that the graph induced on
3
consecutive blobs
V_{i-1}\cup V_{i}\cup V_{i+1}
does not contain
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
.
Assuming otherwise, since this graph is bipartite, let
xyx^{\prime}y^{\prime}
be a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
with
y,y^{\prime}\in V_{i}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in V_{i-1}\cup V_{i+1}
. Assume
y>y^{\prime}
. Notice that
N^{+}(y^{\prime})\cap V_{i-1}\subset N^{+}(y)\cap V_{i-1}
. This is because if
f^{-1}
exists for
y^{\prime}
, then
f^{-1}(y^{\prime})\leq f^{-1}(y)
. Additionally
N^{+}(y^{\prime})\cap V_{i+1}\subset N^{+}(y)\cap V_{i+1}
, simply because
f(y^{\prime})\leq f(y)
. But then
N^{+}(y^{\prime})\subset N^{+}(y)
and there is no
x
, such that
y^{\prime}\rightarrow x\rightarrow y
in
V_{i-1}\cup V_{i}\cup V_{i+1}
, hence no
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
.

Therefore, if there is a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
in our graph, it has to contain at least one diagonal arc. It is straightforward to verify that such a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
would have to contain exactly one such arc, denote it by
v_{i}v_{i+3}
. This leads to a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4}
on
4
consecutive blobs,
v_{i+3}v_{i+2}v_{i+1}v_{i}
. Recall that
v_{j+1}>f(v_{j})
for
j\in\{i,i+1,i+2\}
. For our choice of
f(x)=\min\{1,x+c\}
with
c>\frac{1}{3}
, it means that
v_{i+1}>\frac{1}{3}
,
v_{i+2}>\frac{2}{3}
and
v_{i+3}>1
, which is impossible.

Note that the construction has many
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
as there are relatively many paths on
3
edges in two consecutive blobs. Taking two diagonal arcs merges any such path into
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
. The choice of
c
allows to retain most of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in the previous construction and add new ones using diagonal edges. While there is technically a third way to obtain
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
in this construction, using diagonal arcs three times and special reverse arcs in consecutive blobs twice, optimal choice of
c
does not admit such
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5}
.
The stated bound
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt5},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt4})\geq 0.0517\binom{n}{5}
is achieved by considering function
f(x)=\min\{1,x+c\}
for
c\approx 0.67757
.

4
The sparse case

Lastly, we present one example for the sparse case.

Theorem 34
.

\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6})=\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+o(n^{2})
.

Proof.

The lower bound is achieved by the following construction, shown in Figure
10
. Isolate one vertex
v
and split remaining vertices into two parts
A
and
B
of roughly the same size. Add all arcs from
v
to
A
, from
A
to
B
, and from
B
to
v
.

Figure 10
:
The extremal graph for
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6})
.

Let
G
be an extremal oriented graph on
n
vertices. We may assume that each arc of
G
is contained in at least one copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
. We say that an arc of
G
is
thin
if it is contained in exactly one
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
, otherwise we say that it is
thick
.

The crucial observation is that each copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
in
G
contains at least one thin arc. Indeed, if there is a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
consisting of only thick arcs
vw
,
wu
, and
uv
, then we can find three more vertices
v^{\prime},w^{\prime},u^{\prime}
in
G
such that
vw^{\prime}u
,
uv^{\prime}w
, and
wu^{\prime}v
are directed triangles, which results in a forbidden
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}

vw^{\prime}uv^{\prime}wu^{\prime}
.

Since each copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
contains at least one thin arc and each such arc cannot belong to more copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
, we conclude that
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6})
is upper bounded by the number of thin arcs in
G
.

Let
vw
be a thin arc. Observe that the following configurations are not subgraphs of
G
:

v
w
blowup of a
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}

v
w
blowup of a
\overrightarrow{T_{3}}

First configuration is not allowed just by the definition of a thin arc. In the second configuration, for each upper arc one can find a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3}
containing it, to form a copy of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt6}
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
consisting of all thin arcs of
G
.
Applying the regularity lemma to
G^{\prime}
, we conclude that its reduced graph does not contain any triangles, so by removing
o(n^{2})
arcs we can delete all triangles in
G^{\prime}
. But then, Mantel’s theorem applied to the undirected underlying graph of
G^{\prime}
implies that
G^{\prime}
have at most
\frac{1}{4}n^{2}
arcs.
Therefore,
G
has
\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+o(n^{2})
thin arcs as desired.
∎

We believe that if a cycle of length
3t
is forbidden, then a similar construction with
t-1
vertices in one blob is extremal up to a lower order error term.

Conjecture 35
.

For any integer
t\geq 2
,
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt3t})=\frac{t-1}{4}n^{2}+o(n^{2})
.

5
Directed graphs

An analogous problem can be also considered in the setting of directed graphs, that is, when we additionally allow vertices to be connected by
digons
– pairs of vertices
u
and
v
connected simultaneously by arcs
uv
and
vu
.

In this setting, the maximum number of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in an
n
-vertex directed graph not containing
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
is also of order
\Theta(n^{k})
if
k\nmid\ell
and
\Theta(n^{k-1})
if
k\mid\ell
, because the proof of Theorem
4
does not use the assumption that the extremal directed graph does not contain digons.

Additionally, digons allow to consider a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt2}
, that is a complete balanced bipartite digraph, which is an optimal extremal constructions when
k
is even and
\ell
is odd. This leads to a more natural definition of
d
than in Theorem
8
and the following analog of Theorems
8
and
11
for directed graphs.

Theorem 36
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be integers satisfying
k\geq 3
,
\ell\geq 2(k-1)^{2}
, and
k\nmid\ell
.
Denote by
d
the smallest positive integer that divides
k
but does not divide
\ell
. Then the maximum number of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in an
n
-vertex directed graph not containing
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell}
is equal to
\frac{n}{k}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{k-1}+o(n^{k})
.

Proof.

The lower bound is achieved by a balanced blow-up of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptd}
. To prove the upper bound, consider an extremal directed graph
G
and, as before, apply the directed version of Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma to remove all directed walks of length
\ell
by losing
o(n^{k})
copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
. We may also assume that each arc is contained in
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
.

If
\ell
is even, then a digon forms a directed walk of length
\ell
. Therefore,
G
is an oriented graph and Theorem
8
implies the wanted bound.

Consider now the case that both
\ell
and
k
are odd, and assume that
G
contains a digon. Since every vertex is contained in some
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
, we obtain intersecting
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
and a closed directed walk of length
2
, which easily creates a closed directed walk of length
\ell
.
Knowing that
G
is an oriented graph, we obtain the wanted bound by Theorem
8
as before.

We are left with the case of odd
\ell
and even
k
, which means that
d=2
. Observe that
G
does not contain
\overrightarrow{T_{3}}
, because otherwise we have intersecting
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
and a closed directed walk of length
k+1
, which leads to a closed directed walk of length
\ell
by Corollary
3
as
\ell\geq(k-1)k
. This implies that
e(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
by the following inductive reasoning. If
G
contains a digon on vertices
u
and
v
, then, to avoid
\overrightarrow{T_{3}}
, for any vertex
w\in V(G\setminus\{u,v\})
there are at most
2
arcs between
w
and
\{u,v\}
, hence
e(G)\leq 2+2(n-2)+e(G\setminus\{u,v\})\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
. While if
G
contains no digons, then
e(G)\leq\binom{n}{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.
Knowing this, we can count the number of copies of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
in
G
as follows. Start with an arbitrary arc
v_{1}v_{2}
. Then, choose an arc
v_{3}v_{4}
such that
v_{3}\in N^{+}(v_{2})
. Observe that
v_{2}v_{4}
is not an arc, as otherwise it forms a
\overrightarrow{T_{3}}
. Therefore, the number of possible candidates for
v_{3}v_{4}
is
|N^{+}(v_{2})|\cdot|V(G)\setminus N^{+}(v_{2})|\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}
. This reasoning can be continued for
v_{5}v_{6},\ldots,v_{k-1}v_{k}
. Since each
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
is counted
k
times in the process, the number of
\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk}
is at most
\frac{1}{k}e(G)\cdot\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right)^{(k-2)/2}\leq\frac{n}{k}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{k-1}
as wanted.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

Other orientations of cycles.

In this article, we considered only the case where a directed cycle is forbidden, but one can note that the proven theorems imply similar results when a different orientation of a cycle is forbidden.
For any cycle
C
we define its
cycle-type
as an absolute value between the number of arcs directed clockwise and the number of arcs directed counterclockwise.
If a cycle
C
has cycle-type
\ell\geq 3
, then
\ell
is equal to the largest length of a directed cycle, to which
C
has a homomorphism.
Therefore, Observation
1
implies that
\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},C)\leq\ex(n,\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0ptk},\overrightarrow{C\hskip 4.0pt}_{\hskip-5.0pt\ell})+o(n^{k})
, and so the proven results also give upper bounds when
C
is forbidden.
In particular, since the extremal constructions in Theorems
8
,
11
,
21
,
25
,
27
and
32
do not contain any cycles of cycle-type equal to
\ell
, those theorems give sharp bounds (up to a lower order error term) for forbidding
C
.

Semi-inducibility of alternating cycles.

Recently, Basit, Granet, Horsley, Kündgen and Staden asked (
[
4
, Problem 9.1]
) for the maximum possible number of color-alternating cycles of length
k
in a 2-edge-colored complete graph on
n
vertices, if
2\mid k
and
4\nmid k
. As noted by Chen and Noel, who solved the case
k=6
in
[
8
]
, this problem is asymptotically equivalent to determining the maximum possible number of directed cycles of length
k
in an oriented bipartite graph with
n
vertices in each part.
To see this, given a red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, create an oriented bipartite graph with bipartition
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
and
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
, in which we direct
u_{i}
to
v_{j}
if the edge
ij
is red and
v_{j}
to
u_{i}
if
ij
is blue. Now, each color-alternating cycle corresponds to two directed cycles in the oriented bipartite graph (exchanging all
v_{i}
and
u_{i}
in a directed cycle gives the same alternating cycle), and each such pair of directed cycles corresponds to an alternating closed walk. Since the number of closed walks is asymptotically the same as the number of cycles, the two problems are asymptotically equivalent. Therefore, Lemma
13
implies that the answer to Basit et al. problem is equal to
\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{k}+o(n^{k})
.

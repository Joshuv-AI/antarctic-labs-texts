Uncertainty relations between quantum Fisher information and entanglement monotones
Shaowei Du ‡, Shuheng Liu ‡, Matteo Fadel, Giuseppe Vitagliano, Qiongyi He

Abstract
Entanglement is widely regarded as an essential resource for a number of tasks and can in some cases be quantified by figures of merit related to those tasks. In quantum metrology, this is showcased by the connections between the quantum Fisher information (QFI), providing a bound to the precision, and multipartite entanglement quantifiers such as the entanglement depth. However, a connection between the QFI and entanglement monotones , i.e., functions that do not increase under Local Operations and Classical Communications, has so far remained elusive. In this work, we fill this gap by introducing a family of uncertainty relations that bound bipartite entanglement monotones from below via elements of a quantum Fisher information matrix. To further emphasize the significance of our results, we connect these relations to the achievable precision in multiparameter estimation. Considering a system split into two parts with arbitrary dimension, we also show that, while two-dimensional entanglement is sufficient to estimate a single parameter with maximal precision, genuine high-dimensional entanglement is required for multiparameter estimation. We conclude by illustrating how our method extends naturally to a multipartite splitting.

Next, let us introduce the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM)

Results.—
Let us now try to characterize entanglement monotones via the QFIM. The key idea is to exploit its convexity, together with the known characterization of pure-state covariance matrices to bound convex-roof entanglement monotones. From
Eq.

1
, we can see that the values of these entanglement monotones are obtained from an optimal decomposition into pure states
\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}^{(o)}\outerproduct{\psi_k^{(o)}}{\psi_k^{(o)}}
. One can then use the convexity of QFIM
\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{g})\preceq\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{k}}(\boldsymbol{g})=4\sum_{k}p_{k}\Gamma_{\psi_{k}}(\boldsymbol{g})
and try to characterize the boundary covariance matrix
\Gamma_{\psi_{k}}(\boldsymbol{g})
for
\ket{\psi_k}
with a given value of some entanglement monotone
E(\psi_{k})
. This can be done due to the fact that the covariance matrix of a pure state can be conveniently expressed in an operator basis coming from the Schmidt bases
[
74
,
75
]
.

One can take a scalar combination of the QFIM elements and derive the corresponding bound coming from
\Gamma_{\psi_{k}}

T^{(t)}(\varrho)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathcal{F}_{a}\right)+t^{2}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathcal{F}_{b}\right)+2t\cdot\mathrm{tr}\left|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}\right|,

(6)

where
\mathrm{tr}\left|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}\right|:=\|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}\|
is the trace norm, which is also the norm that we use throughout the work.
Eq.

6

arises from the trace norm of the blocks of
\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{g})

t\geq 0

For pure states, this quantity is a linear combination of entanglement monotones, and thus its convex roof

(7)

Here, the subtracted term makes it vanish for separable states. For the special cases

(8)

The function

Consider a bipartite state
\varrho

Connections to QCRB.—
Finally, let us observe some implications that our results have on the relationship between entanglement monotones and phase-estimations. In fact, exploiting the usual quantum Cramér-Rao bound (QCRB), i.e., the fact that the precision of estimation is lower-bounded by the inverse of the QFI, it is possible to lower-bound the QFIs from the sensitivity of the corresponding estimation task obtained, e.g., via error-propagation formula as a signal-to-noise ratio of a measured observable. More precisely, the QFI of an observable

can be estimated by referring to the unitary parameter encoding

and, for example, considering the estimation via the measurement of a
\theta

and
\theta\simeq 0
as
(\delta\theta)^{-2}=\frac{|\langle{[O,H]}\rangle_{\varrho}|^{2}}{(\Delta O)_{\varrho}^{2}}\leq F_{\varrho}(H)

(\delta\theta)^{-2}=\frac{|\langle{[O,H]}\rangle_{\varrho}|^{2}}{(\Delta O)_{\varrho}^{2}}
is obtained via the usual error-propagation formula
[
7

13

. Thus our method, in particular inequalities like (

[

,

.

At the same time, the QCRB, together with our results, i.e., criteria like
Eq.

13

\sum_{i=2}^{d^{2}}(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}\geq\frac{(d^{2}-1)^{2}}{\sum_{i=2}^{d^{2}}F_{\varrho}(G_{i})},

(15)

we thus obtain that, for every entanglement monotone considered, greater entanglement lowers the total mean square error in multiparameter estimation tasks. See SM Sec.
VI
for a proof of this inequality.

Conclusions.—
In conclusion, we introduce a family of uncertainty relations based on QFIM and LOCC entanglement monotones, which generally show how the precision limit of multiparameter estimation increases as entanglement increases. We also propose a general approach for extending our observations to multipartite systems, enabling the detection of the entanglement-entropy vector. Our work raises several interesting questions that deserve further investigation, including, for example, the relation between the QFIM and the covariance matrix criteria and to other entropic entanglement measures that do not rely on convex-roof constructions. Another interesting problem would be to systematically find inequalities of the form (
13

17
), especially for the multipartite case, eventually optimized for specific situations like numerical investigation of many-body models or implementation in experiments
[
90
]
. It is worth pointing out that in the particular case of thermal equilibrium states, our criteria can be also evaluated from linear response functions, due to the relation between the QFI and dynamical susceptibilities
[

]
.

to finish our construction. For the concurrence, calling
\mathcal{N}(\psi)\leq\sqrt{\frac{d(d-1)}{2}}C(\psi)
and
C(\psi)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2(d-1)}{d}}
, we derive the bound to pure-state concurrence

C(\psi)\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}(T(\psi)-8(d-1))}{4(d+2)\sqrt{2(d-1)}}

(S20)

and further

C(\varrho)\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}(T(\varrho)-8(d-1))}{4(d+2)\sqrt{2(d-1)}}:=\mathcal{B}_{C}(\varrho).

(S21)

Now we use this bound to find a lower bound for the entanglement of formation, using the following argument.

Given a lower bound to the concurrence
\mathcal{B}_{C}(\psi)
valid for pure states and a convex, monotonically increasing function
V(x)
such that

E_{F}(\psi)\geq V(C(\psi))
, we can get a lower bound to the entanglement of formation as

E_{F}(\varrho)=\sum_{k}p_{k}E_{F}\left(\psi_{k}\right)\geqslant\sum_{k}p_{k}V\left(C\left(\psi_{k}\right)\right)\geqslant V\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}C\left(\psi_{k}\right)\right)\geqslant V\left(\mathcal{B}_{c}\right).

(S22)

In particular, we use the optimal such function, which was found in
[
67
,
78
]
and is given by

V(\lambda):=\operatorname{co}\left(R_{L}^{(d)}(\lambda)\right)=\begin{cases}H_{2}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}}{2}\right),&\lambda\in[0,1],\\
\frac{\log_{2}(k+1)-\log_{2}(k)}{\sqrt{\frac{2k}{k+1}}-\sqrt{\frac{2k-1}{k}}}\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\frac{2(k-1)}{k}}\right)+\log_{2}(k),&\lambda\in\left[1,\sqrt{\frac{2(d-1)}{d}}\right]\end{cases}

(S23)

where
k=\lfloor 2/(2-\lambda^{2})\rfloor
.
H_{2}(x)=-x\log_{2}x-(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)
is the standard binary entropy function and
\operatorname{co}(f(x))
is the convex hull of
f(x)
.

III
Proof of no-go theorem of Schmidt number detection by single QFI

The quantum Fisher information of an observable
M
on a pure state
\ket{\psi}
is given by

F_{\psi}(M)=4(\Delta M)_{\psi}^{2}.

(S30)

For a mixed state
\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\outerproduct{\psi_k}{\psi_k}
, the QFI is convex so that we obtain

F_{\varrho}(M)\leq 4\sum_{k}p_{k}(\Delta M)_{\psi_{k}}^{2}.

(S31)

Thus, in order to bound the QFI of an observable
M
from above, it is sufficient to find an upper bound on the
variance
of
M
valid for all pure states.

Let us now consider
M=A\otimes\mathbb{1}+\mathbb{1}\otimes B
with
A,B
being arbitrary local observables.
For a pure product state
\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi_a}\ket{\psi_b}
, we can make use of the additivity property and get:

(\Delta M)^{2}_{\psi}=(\Delta A)^{2}_{\psi_{a}}+(\Delta B)^{2}_{\psi_{b}},

(S32)

which is in turn easily observed to be upper-bounded by

(\Delta A)^{2}_{\psi_{a}}+(\Delta B)^{2}_{\psi_{b}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}(A)-\mu_{\min}(A)\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}(B)-\mu_{\min}(B)\right)^{2},

(S33)

where we denoted by
\mu_{\max}(\cdot)
and
\mu_{\min}(\cdot)
the largest and smallest eigenvalues of an operator respectively. This is because the variance of an observable on a quantum state is upper-bounded by

(\Delta A)^{2}_{\psi_{a}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{\max}(A)-\mu_{\min}(A)\right)^{2},

(S34)

with the state saturating this relation given by

\ket{\psi_a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{\mu_{\max}(A)}+\ket{\mu_{\min}(A)}\right).

(S35)

This way, we find that the QFI of a collective observable
M
is upper-bounded by

F_{\varrho}(M)\leq\left(\mu_{\max}(A)-\mu_{\min}(A)\right)^{2}+\left(\mu_{\max}(B)-\mu_{\min}(B)\right)^{2},

(S36)

for all
separable
states
\varrho
, i.e., for all states of Schmidt-number
r=1
. A state that saturates the upper bound is given by

\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{\mu_{\max}(A)}+\ket{\mu_{\min}(A)}\right)\otimes\left(\ket{\mu_{\max}(B)}+\ket{\mu_{\min}(B)}\right),

(S37)

where
\ket{\mu_{\max}(\cdot)}
and
\ket{\mu_{\min}(\cdot)}
denote eigenvectors corresponding to the maximal and minimal eigenvalues of the observables respectively. Note that if there are multiple such vectors, one can take any linear combination of them as
\ket{\mu_{\max}(\cdot)}
and
\ket{\mu_{\min}(\cdot)}
.

On the other hand, the overall upper-bound on
F_{\varrho}(M)
, valid
for all quantum states
, can also be found easily with a similar reasoning and we get

F_{\varrho}(M)\leq\left(\mu_{\max}(M)-\mu_{\min}(M)\right)^{2},

(S38)

which is in general strictly larger than the right-hand side of
Eq.

S36
. Note that
\mu_{\max}(M)=\mu_{\max}(A)+\mu_{\max}(B)
and the same equality holds for its minimum. Therefore, the following inequality holds
for all quantum states

F_{\varrho}(M)\leq\left(\mu_{\max}(A)+\mu_{\max}(B)-\mu_{\min}(A)-\mu_{\min}(B)\right)^{2},

(S39)

and is saturated by a state of the form

\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{\mu_{\max}(A)}\ket{\mu_{\max}(B)}+\ket{\mu_{\min}(A)}\ket{\mu_{\min}(B)}\right),

(S40)

where again
\ket{\mu_{\max}(\cdot)}
and
\ket{\mu_{\min}(\cdot)}
are any of the eigenvectors corresponding to the maximal and minimal eigenvalues respectively. Any two vectors
\ket{\mu_{\min}(\cdot)}
and
\ket{\mu_{\max}(\cdot)}
are always orthogonal, since they correspond to different eigenvalues, and thus we can see that the state in
Eq.

S40
has Schmidt rank equal to two. Hence, the bound in
Eq.

S39
is valid for all states with Schmidt number
r\geq 2
.

Now consider an illustrative example from Ref.
[
92
]
: a 4-qubit pure state with high-dimensional bipartite entanglement and vanishing single collective-spin QFI
[
26
,
27
]

\ket{\Phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\Big(\ket{0011}+\ket{1100}+\omega(\ket{1010}+\ket{0101})+\omega^{2}(\ket{1001}+\ket{0110})\Big),

(S41)

where
\omega:=e^{\frac{2\pi{\rm i}}{3}}
. Let
J_{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\sigma_{k}^{(\ell)}

(k=x,y,z)
be the total spin operators. Then
F_{\Phi}(J_{k})=4(\Delta J_{k})^{2}_{\Phi}=0
for
k=x,y,z
. This can be verified by expanding
\ket{\Phi}
as a coherent superposition of three pairwise singlet products,

|\Phi\rangle=\frac{\rm i}{\sqrt{2}}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{12}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{34}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{13}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{24}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{14}\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle_{23}

(S42)

where
\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)
, each of which is annihilated by every
J_{k}
. Consequently, any single-
J_{k}
-based criterion, including the
k
-producible upper bounds of
[
26
,
27
]
, returns a trivial result (
F_{Q}=0
) and cannot detect any entanglement in
\ket{\Phi}
. If we consider a more general form of a collective operator, namely
\tilde{J}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\sigma_{\hat{n}_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}
, entanglement can be detected via a single QFI. Although the solution of this problem requires numerical analysis
[
26
]
, we showcase this with an illustrative example by choosing
\tilde{J}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}^{(1)}+\sigma_{x}^{(2)}-\sigma_{x}^{(3)}-\sigma_{x}^{(4)})
. The QFI of
\tilde{J}
is
\frac{16}{3}
so that entanglement depth of
2
is certified. However, any such more general single QFI criterion still falls in the class included in our no-go theorem described above. Thus, the dimensionality of entanglement of the state cannot be certified with any such
\tilde{J}
.

Nevertheless,
\ket{\Phi}
is genuinely 4-partite entangled (entangled across every bipartition, e.g. composed of
1
vs
3
particles) and exhibits Schmidt rank
4
across all bipartitions of
2
vs
2
particles. For instance, across the

partition, the Schmidt coefficients are

, and the same holds for the

and the

bipartitions. This genuinely high-dimensional bipartite entanglement contrasts with a
4
-qubit GHZ state, which has entanglement depth
4

2

Let us also calculate the entanglement of formation of this state across the
2

2

, we obtain

E_{F}(\ket{\Phi})=-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\log_{2}\lambda_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{2}6\ \approx\ 1.7925

Treating
\ket{\Phi}

1

Criteria based on the QFIs of collective spin operators

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\sum_{l,m=1}^{r}\sqrt{\lambda_{l}\lambda_{m}}\bra{u_l, v_l}M_{k}\ket{u_m, v_m}\right)^{2}\right].

As a paradigmatic example, let us consider the spin operators in the spin-

(S45)

\displaystyle j_{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{l>k}\delta_{l,k+1}\sqrt{j(j+1)-m_{k}(m_{k}+1)}(\ket{k}\bra{l}-\ket{l}\bra{k}),

\displaystyle j_{z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}m_{k}\ket{k}\bra{k},

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{SN}(\psi_{r})=r}\sum_{k=x,y,z}(\Delta J_{k}^{\pm})_{\psi_{r}}^{2}

V
Certification of multipartite entanglement structure using QFIM

In the multipartite case, one has to look at the vector of entanglement entropies across all bipartitions,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}(\varrho)
, which is also further ordered non-increasingly
[
82
]
. For an
n
-particle system, the number of bipartitions is
\mathcal{P}=2^{n-1}-1
, which is the number of components of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}(\varrho)
, and each such component is difficult to calculate in general, even with full knowledge of the density matrix. Current methods for detecting multipartite high-dimensional entanglement include fidelity witnesses
[
83
,
84
,
85
,
86
,
87
]
, linear entropy vector
[
82
]
, correlation tensor norm
[
88
]
, and also covariances
[
77
]
, building upon the covariance matrix criterion developed in
[
75
]
. This latter approach can be also adapted to exploit the method presented here, in particular
Eq.

11b
, to find a relationship between QFIM and multipartite high-dimensional entanglement.

Concretely, in a multipartite setting the so-called entanglement entropy vector is defined element-wise as
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\downarrow}(\varrho):=\inf_{\mathcal{D}(\varrho)}\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\downarrow}\left(\psi_{k}\right)
, labeled with an index scanning all bipartitions
j\in\{1,2\dots,\mathcal{P}\}

[
82
]
, where
\mathcal{D}(\varrho)
is a pure state decomposition
\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\ket{\psi_k}\bra{\psi_k}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\downarrow}\left(\psi_{k}\right)
denotes the
j
-th largest entanglement entropy of
\ket{\psi_k}
across all bipartitions. Thus, for an optimal decomposition
\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\ket{\phi_k}\bra{\phi_k}
, the relation
\mathcal{E}^{\downarrow}_{j}(\phi_{k})\leq\mathcal{E}^{\downarrow}_{j}(\varrho)
holds for all
j
.

We showcase how zero-entropy (i.e., the Schmidt number) can be used to extend
Eq.

11b
from the bipartite to the multipartite case. Using this approach, we obtain a bound on the entanglement-dimensionality vector
\mathcal{SN}^{\downarrow}_{j}(\varrho)=\inf_{\mathcal{D}(\varrho)}\max_{\ket{\psi_k}\in\mathcal{D}(\varrho)}\mathcal{SN}^{\downarrow}_{j}(\psi_{k})
, where the notation is as defined above, and show that this bound can be computed via a linear program:

Observation 4
.

For an
n
-partite state
\varrho
, consider a candidate entanglement-dimensionality vector
\boldsymbol{v}=(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{\mathcal{P}})
.
\boldsymbol{v}
is feasible for
\varrho
if there exist a pure-state decomposition
\{p_{k},\left|\psi_{k}\right\rangle\}
and integer vectors
\boldsymbol{r}_{k}=((r_{1})_{k},(r_{2})_{k},\dots,(r_{\mathcal{P}})_{k})
, together with permutation matrices
M_{k}
, such that
\boldsymbol{r}_{k}\leq M_{k}\boldsymbol{v}
element-wise for all
k
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{1}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{1})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{1})_{k}}\right),\\
h_{2}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{2})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{2})_{k}}\right),\\
\vdots\\
h_{\mathcal{P}}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{\mathcal{P}})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{\mathcal{P}})_{k}}\right),\end{array}\right.

(S52)

where the functions
h_{j}(\varrho)
are defined in
Eq.

S58
for the bipartition
(j|\bar{j})
. Finally, taking the element-wise minimum over all feasible vectors, the resulting vector
\boldsymbol{v}^{\min}=(v_{1}^{\min},\dots,v_{\mathcal{P}}^{\min})
is the detected entanglement-dimensionality vector.

Proof.—
Consider a quantum state
\varrho
composed of
n
particles of dimension
d
. To certify its entanglement-dimensionality vector, we consider the QFIM for each bipartition
(j|\bar{j})
, labelled by a integer index

j\in\{1,2\dots,\mathcal{P}\}
:

\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{F}_{j}&\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}^{(j|\bar{j})}\\
(\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}^{(j|\bar{j})})^{T}&\mathcal{F}_{\bar{j}}\end{array}\right),

(S53)

where
\boldsymbol{g}_{j}
is a basis pair for the partition
(j|\bar{j})
. One example of such basis is obtained by the tensor product of the single-particle
su(d)
bases for all particles in each party.

Now, let us recall that a state
\varrho
that has a given entanglement-dimensionality vector
\boldsymbol{v}
can be decomposed by definition as

\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}\ket{\psi_k}\bra{\psi_k},

(S54)

where each pure state
\ket{\psi_k}
has a Schmidt rank vector
\boldsymbol{r}_{k}
that is such that
\boldsymbol{r}_{k}\leq M\boldsymbol{v}
for some permutation matrix
M
.

Due to the convexity of QFIM, we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})\leqslant\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{k}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=4\sum_{k}p_{k}\Gamma_{\psi_{k}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j}),

(S55)

and by multiplying both sides by the vector
\boldsymbol{t}_{\mu}=\left(A\boldsymbol{u}_{\mu},B\boldsymbol{v}_{\mu}\right)^{T}
and its transpose, we arrive at an inequality similar to
Eq.

S7
, namely

\displaystyle A^{2}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{j})+B^{2}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{\bar{j}})+2AB\ \mathrm{tr}|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}^{(j|\bar{j})}|\leqslant

\displaystyle 4A^{2}\sum_{k}p_{k}\left(d-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right)+4B^{2}\sum_{k}p_{k}\left(d-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right)+8AB\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{j})_{k}-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right),

(S56)

where we also used the bounds in
Eqs.

S24a
and
S24c
.
After optimizing
A
and
B
over real values, we obtain

\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}^{(j|\bar{j})}|-\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{j})_{k}-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right)\leqslant\sqrt{\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}\left(d-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{j})\right)\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}\left(d-\frac{1}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{\bar{j}})\right)}.

(S57)

Then, using the condition
\sqrt{(x-t)(y-t)}\leqslant\sqrt{xy}-t
, we arrive at

h_{j}(\varrho):=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}|\mathcal{X}_{\varrho}^{(j|\bar{j})}|-\sqrt{\left(d-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{j})\right)\left(d-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{\bar{j}})\right)}\leqslant\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{j})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{j})_{k}}\right).

(S58)

Once again, the constraint to the right-hand side is that the vector of Schmidt ranks across all bipartitions of
\ket{\psi_k}
must satisfy
\boldsymbol{r}_{k}\leq M\boldsymbol{v}
for some permutation matrix
M
. Thus, we have to check simultaneously the system of inequalities

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{1}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{1})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{1})_{k}}\right),\\
h_{2}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{2})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{2})_{k}}\right),\\
\vdots\\
h_{\mathcal{P}}(\varrho)&\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\left((r_{\mathcal{P}})_{k}-\frac{2}{(r_{\mathcal{P}})_{k}}\right),\end{array}\right..

(S59)

We denote by
\{\boldsymbol{v}\}_{\text{feas}}
the set of all non-increasingly ordered vectors
\boldsymbol{v}
such that the inequalities in
Eq.

S59
are all satisfied. By definition of
\mathcal{SN}^{\downarrow}(\varrho)
, we obtain a rigorous lower bound to the entanglement-dimensionality vector by taking the element-wise minimum over all feasible vectors,

v_{j}^{\min}:=\min_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\{\boldsymbol{v}\}_{\text{feas}}}v_{j}.

(S60)

This vector
\boldsymbol{v}^{\min}
satisfies
\mathcal{SN}^{\downarrow}_{j}(\varrho)\geq v_{j}^{\min}
for all
j
.
∎

To facilitate comprehension, we explain below the derivation of the full entanglement-dimensionality vector for the state in
Eq.

S61
, which we report here as well for clarity:

\ket{\phi}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+\ket{1})\otimes(\ket{00}+\ket{11})\otimes(\ket{0000}+\ket{1111}).

(S61)

We recall that its entanglement-dimensionality vector is given by

\boldsymbol{v}=(\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=1},\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=2},\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=3})\quad\text{with}\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=1}=(2_{6},1_{1}),\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=2}=(4_{8},2_{12},1_{1}),\quad\boldsymbol{v}_{|j|=3}=(4_{20},2_{14},1_{1}),

(S62)

where the subscript indicates the number of times each element is repeated.

We evaluate
Eq.

S59
by inserting on the right-hand side the values
r-\frac{2}{r}=-1
for
r=1
,
r-\frac{2}{r}=1
for
r=2
and
r-\frac{2}{r}=3.5
for
r=4
and calculating the quantities
h_{j}
depending on the partition sizes, which results in

\boldsymbol{h}_{|j|=1}=(1_{6},-1_{1}),\quad\boldsymbol{h}_{|j|=2}=(3.5_{8},1_{12},-1_{1}),\quad\boldsymbol{h}_{|j|=3}=(3.5_{20},1_{14},-1_{1}),

(S63)

meaning that the entanglement-dimensionality vector is precisely that in
Eq.

S62
.

For better clarity, we also summarize in the table below how to reconstruct its entanglement structure.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\text{Partition size}&\text{The number of elements equal to }1\text{ in }\boldsymbol{v}&\text{Bipartition }(j|\bar{j})\\

\hline\cr|j|=2&\geqslant 1&14567|23\\

Thus, for pure states, our method can allow to extract the full decomposition of the state, i.e., both the minimal number of parties and the size of each party. For mixed states instead, it is in general not possible to deduce the optimal pure state decomposition that clearly shows its exact entanglement structure, but only the least dimensionalities needed to describe them. In that case, our method can thus be used to detect complementary information about the entanglement structure as compared to the depth and the number of partitions.

VI
Connection between the QFIM bound and quantum metrology

First, it is useful to introduce the following statement that follows from the multiparameter quantum Cramér-Rao bound (QCRB) :

Lemma 1
.

Given a set of parameters
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
, encoded in a quantum state
\varrho
via a unitary evolution generated by a set of
\{\tilde{H}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
, the following “uncertainty relation” holds:

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}F_{\varrho}(\tilde{H}_{i})\right)\geq K^{2},

(S64)

where the
(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}
are the variances of the estimators and
K
is the number of parameters.

Proof.
Consider a single shot measurement, the QCRB reads

\Sigma\succeq\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}^{-1},

(S65)

where
\Sigma
is the covariance matrix of the estimators of the parameters and
\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}=\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{\tilde{H}})
is the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM) associated to the estimation and we have assumed the non-trivial circumstance that the QFIM is of full rank so that the encoded parameters are independent. Taking the trace of this equation, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)\geq\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}^{-1})=\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}^{-1}\boldsymbol{n}_{i},

(S66)

where the vectors
\boldsymbol{n}_{i}
form an orthonormal basis. Next, using that
\boldsymbol{n}^{T}M^{-1}\boldsymbol{n}\geq(\boldsymbol{n}^{T}M\boldsymbol{n})^{-1}
holds for any strictly positive definite matrix
M
and unit vector
\boldsymbol{n}
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}\geq\sum_{i}(\boldsymbol{n}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}\boldsymbol{n}_{i})^{-1}.

(S67)

Finally, by using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
(\sum_{i}(\boldsymbol{n}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}\boldsymbol{n}_{i})^{-1})(\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}\boldsymbol{n}_{i})\geq K^{2}
, we arrive at

\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\delta\theta_{i})^{2}\geq\frac{K^{2}}{\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{\varrho}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}}=\frac{K^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}F_{\varrho}(\tilde{H}_{i})},

(S68)

where in the last equation we used the fact that the diagonal elements of the QFIM are given by
F_{\varrho}(\tilde{H}_{i})
.
∎

Using
Lemma

1
, we can now relate our criterion in
Eq.

13
(
Corollary

1
) to a specific metrological task, in which
K=(d^{2}-1)
parameters are encoded in a bipartite quantum state
\varrho
by the unitary
U(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp\left(-{\rm i}\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\theta_{k}G_{k}\right)
, where
G_{k}=g^{(a)}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{1}+\mathbb{1}\otimes g^{(b)}_{k}
are bipartite collective
su(d)
generators. The task is to estimate the parameters in the proximity of zero, i.e.,
\theta_{i}\simeq 0
, so that we have
\tilde{H}_{i}=G_{i}
. In this situation, the following bound holds for the variances of the estimators

\sum_{i=2}^{d^{2}}\delta^{2}\theta_{i}\geq\frac{(d^{2}-1)^{2}r}{8(r^{2}+dr-2)},

(S69)

and for all probe states that have a Schmidt number bounded from above by
r
, i.e.,
\mathcal{SN}(\varrho)\leq r
.

As an example, for
d=2
with normalized Pauli matrices as
g_{k}
, entanglement is certified whenever
\sum_{k=x,y,z}\left(\delta\theta_{k}\right)^{2}<9/8
.

Note that analogous reasoning could be made considering a smaller set of Hamiltonians. We can also obtain bounds for combinations of sensitivities through the QCRB and conditions of the form (
17
).

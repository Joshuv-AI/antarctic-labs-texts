-Fractional -dimensional generalized KP model: nonlinear dynamical behaviors, analytical wave structures, bifurcation, and sensitivity analysis
Authors: Ulviye Demirbilek, Ali Danladi, Arzu Akbulut, Salah Boulaaras, Karim K Ahmed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study introduces an integrable generalization of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili model in arbitrary spatial dimensions. The Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation serves as a fundamental framework in describing a wide range of physical phenomena, including hydrodynamic wave disturbances, plasma dynamics, and nonlinear optical systems. In this work, the classical Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation is modified to incorporate -fractional derivatives, extending its applicability to more complex dynamical scenarios. By employing the extended tanh function method in conjunction with the Riccati differential equation, an analytical of exact solutions such as dark, singular, and periodic wave forms are derived. These solutions give valuable mathematical insight into wave propagation and offer significant physical relevance for practical applications in physics and engineering. Two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) plots of the obtained wave equations are drawn under certain values of the parameters and at different cases of . In order to investigate further the system’s dynamical properties, the governing equations were transformed by means of the Galilean transformation. That way, a detailed investigation of the nonlinear structure was possible. A phase space analysis is carried out using planar dynamical system techniques in order to find bifurcations, chaotic behavior, and sensitivity of the system to initial conditions. An instability analysis due to the interaction of nonlinear and dispersive effects in the proposed model is carried out. The stability criterion for waves with regard to small perturbations was determined. Our results are contrary to the findings available in the literature, and this is the first attempt that has been made to study the Hamiltonian structure, bifurcation analysis, sensitivity analysis, and modulation instability for the model considered.Keywords: Modified extended tanh approach, Beta-derivative, Bifurcation analysis, Quasi periodic behavior, Modulation instability, Differential EquationsSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

PDEs form a significant class of equations in mathematics, physics, engineering, and many other scientific fields. These equations tell how the physical quantities change with respect to time and space. In contrast with ordinary differential equations, where one has functions of a single variable, the rigorous classification of PDEs requires more than one variable. In fact, partial differential equations allow much more detailed and accurate depictions of dynamic systems. They thus provide a basic mathematical framework for the modeling of various natural systems and complex situations1–3. PDEs are generally used to model different types of phenomena in various disciplines. With technological development, all systems naturally become more complex. The differential equations, however, remain a fundamental and powerful method for representing real processes. Practical solutions for these increasingly complicated systems require the introduction of innovative and novel mathematical methods4.

In recent years, numerous studies have made significant contributions to the investigation of soliton behaviors in various nonlinear dynamical models. Ozışık et al.5 considered the Biswas–Arshed equation and applied the combined improved Kudryashov–sub-equation of the new extended auxiliary equation method (CIKAEM26) along with its modified version (mCIKAEM26) to investigate this model. In6, Younas et al. applied the Arnous and the modified generalized exponential function techniques to the linear quadratic model. Rahman et al.7 obtained solutions for the Westervelt equation representing the soliton structures and their dynamics by employing two different dual analytical approaches. Çınar et al.8 extracted solitons for the different types of generalized Sasa–Satsuma models using the new Kudryashov technique, whereas multidimensional models such as the Konopelchenko–Dubrovsky and shallow water-like equations are considered by Dey et al.9 to study wave profile and formation of solitons. Nevertheless, analysis by Yiasir et al.10 and Islam et al.11 extended these studies to some other nonlinear models, namely, Boiti-Leon-Manna-Pempinelli and M-fractional Kuralay-II equations, which demonstrated diverse soliton dynamics along with the bifurcation characteristics. Further contributions by Islam and coworkers presented detailed bifurcation analyses and soliton solutions for a range of nonlinear systems including Murnaghan’s rod, resonance interaction, and optical soliton equations. With this, one can identify the effectiveness of the analytical and semi-analytical methods in order to catch rich nonlinear wave phenomena12,13.

Fractional differential equations (FDEs) have attracted significant interest in the last few decades due to their potential to model complex systems more accurately than their classical integer-order counterparts. Unlike most traditional models, that assume well-defined integer-order derivatives, FDEs allow for non-integer (fractional) derivatives. In this respect, the framework of FDEs is more flexible to describe memory effects and hereditary properties in various physical, biological, and engineering systems14–17. They represent an important tool in control theory, signal processing, viscoelasticity, and anomalous diffusion, where the classical model fails in describing long-term dependencies and intricate dynamics. FDEs have an important place in promoting scientific research, technological research, offering more profound insights into real-world phenomena18.

The work in19 investigated quasi-periodic behaviours in the context of the double-chain DNA model, therefore enhancing our comprehension of biological nonlinearities. Furthermore,20 analyzed the traveling wave solutions and qualitative dynamics of the - D Camassa–Holm–Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation, exhibiting novel aspects of higher-dimensional nonlinear wave motion. Finally, a thorough bifurcation and chaos analysis for the dusty plasma system with generalized distributions was done in21, providing an example of the interaction between mathematical modeling and plasma physics. From an overall perspective, these studies demonstrate a continued effort to improve the theoretical and computational understanding of nonlinear evolution equations and the many applications pertaining to these.

Fractional-order derivatives have been one of the subjects of mathematics from its origin, and they are still of great importance in attracting the interests of many researchers today. It is well known that systems with a complicated structure cannot be effectively handled using ordinary classical derivatives. This restriction needs more flexible forms of differentiation techniques. In this perspective, fractional calculus generalizes the notion of differentiation to arbitrary orders. Such a differential operator enables better modeling of systems’ dynamic properties. There were several kinds of fractional-order derivatives available in the literature among which the Riemann-Liouville derivative22, the Caputo derivative23, the Grünwald-Letnikov derivative24, the Jumarie derivative25,the conformable derivative26, the M-truncated derivative27, the derivative28, the q-derivative29, and so on. In our study, we will be interested in the derivative because it satisfies the chain rule30.

After the significant role of fractional-order equations in modeling phenomena, obtaining their solutions has gained importance. There are different procedures for obtaining the solutions of fractional-order differential equations. Several of them are the generalized Laplace transformation procedure31, the Adomian decomposition procedure32, the Homotopy perturbation procedure33, the shifted Grünwald approximation procedure34, the wavelet procedure35, the modified trial equation procedure36, the modified auxiliary equation procedure37, the functional variable procedure38, the improved modified extended tanh-function procedure39,40, the Nucci’s reduction procedure41, the collocation procedure42, the Fourier expansion procedure43, the Jacobi elliptic equation procedure44, the generalized exponential rational function method45, the direct transformation procedure46, the singular manifold procedure47 and so on.

Kadomtsev and Petviashvili introduced the KP equation in 197048, it serves as a two-dimensional extension of the well-known Korteweg–de Vries equation. It describes various nonlinear phenomena in fields such as fluid mechanics, plasma dynamics, Bose-Einstein condensates, and optical systems. Due to its significant impact in both physical and mathematical contexts, the KP equation has attracted attention in literature, contributing to comprehensive investigations on its integrability characteristics and exact solutions through a variety of analytical methodologies, such as: Tian and Gao applied the generalized hyperbolic-function algorithm to the Spherical Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation in49, Zhang et al. obtained lump solutions in50, Wazwaz and Xu applied the simplified Hirota’s method in51, Gou et al. discovered rogue waves via the binary Darboux transformation in52, Ma et al. obtained the bilinear form of the equation in53, Zhao and He obtained a new type of multiple-lump solution in54, Rao et al. discovered resonant collisions of lumps with periodic solitons in55. Xu and Wazwaz obtained N- soliton solutions via binary Bell polynomial method in56.

It can be said that the quasi-periodic phenomenon corresponds to the periodic motion that is regularly interrupted in some manner or other in the dynamic process. The cause of the occurrence of the quasi-periodic phenomenon in a dynamic process could be considered to be the result of the interaction or effect of two distinct frequencies that are independent in nature but do not have matching periodic times. In other cases, when the ratio of the frequency is Irrational, the corresponding dynamic system will produce complex oscillations58,65.

Applications

Nonlinear Water Waves: The -fractional generalized KP model describes shallow water waves and surface interactions in coastal and ocean engineering. Small disturbances can cause wave breaking or create multiple soliton structures.

Plasma Dynamics: In plasma physics, bifurcation and chaotic transitions explain how the stability of ion-acoustic or Langmuir waves changes. These changes can affect energy transport and confinement in fusion devices.

Nonlinear Optics: In optical fibers and photonic crystals, the model shows modulation instability and the formation of bright and dark soliton pulses under various dispersion and nonlinearity conditions.

Biomedical Signal Propagation: Fractional models allow for the study of the spread of electrical signals in nerve fibers or cardiac tissues, such as the proposed -fractional KP equation. Bifurcation may be indicative of irregular rhythms or signal distortion.

Advantages and limitations of the technique

In this study, the method of modified extended tanh is utilized since it is quite effective in the determination of analytical solutions of the -fractional (n+1)-dimensional generalized KP model. Unlike other methods of analysis, which rely on auxiliary equations or predetermined forms of transformation, the method allows for the construction of various wave profiles, including bright, dark, and periodic solitons, without extra restrictions. Besides, the - fractional derivative appearing in the equation is included in an effective way that analyzes memory-dependent and nonlocal behaviors. The method has also provided strong and accurate results for equations with a balance number greater than two. Hence, this demonstrates a capability for handling higher-order nonlinear systems. One of the disadvantages of the method under scrutiny is that it yields only hyperbolic, periodic, and rational types of solutions. As such, the method is unsuitable for obtaining other forms of solutions.

Conclusions

In this study, the modified extended tanh expansion method, incorporating the Riccati differential equation, is employed to derive diverse solitons for the -fractional -D gKP equation. Trigonometric, hyperbolic and rational solutions are obtained via the modified extended tanh expansion method. Three dimensional and two dimensional figures are plotted for some results. Dark soliton, periodic singular soliton, combined dark and combined periodic solitons are obtained.

Hamiltonian system is constructed for the given model and bifurcation, chaotic and sensivity analysis are performed. The chaotic and bifurcation behaviors of the system were analyzed, as well as its sensitivity to initial conditions and modulation instability. These studies are considered a basis for explaining the dynamic structure of a physical model. In particular, the bifurcation analysis gives insight into how the system responds to changes in parameters. Furthermore, chaotic dynamics and sensitivity to initial conditions will provide conclusions about the unpredictability and long-term behavior of the system. Modulation instability is an important tool that illustrates the stability properties of physical models, since it has been used to show how small initial perturbations can be amplified and grow into wave breakups or drastic changes in the solution.

The results efficiently help explain the physical significance of the model under consideration and other related nonlinear physical models. The numerical method used is a very powerful mathematical tool with a wide range of potential applications for the investigation of the dynamic behavior of such nonlinear systems.

The present work intends to provide an integrated framework that connects the analytical, numerical, and dynamical analyses for the -fractional generalized KP equation. The method aims at representing a unified approach toward the investigation of nonlinear wave behaviors in higher dimensions using a modified extended tanh expansion method along with the analysis of bifurcation, chaotic structure, and modulation instability. The obtained soliton structures highlight the delicate interplay between nonlinearity and dispersion under the fractional order influence. These results extend the theoretical framework underlying the fractional KP-type models and will make a major impact on the modeling of various nonlinear physical processes, such as plasma oscillations, fluid dynamics, and optics.

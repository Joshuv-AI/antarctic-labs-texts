Variation Monte Carlo Study on the bilayer t − J ∥ − J ⟂ t-J_{\parallel}-J_{\perp} model for La 3 Ni 2 O 7
Zeyu Chen, Yu-Bo Liu, Fan Yang

Abstract
The discovery of high-temperature superconductivity (HTSC) in La 3 Ni 2 O 7 has aroused significant interest in exploring the pairing mechanism. Previous studies have proposed an effective d x 2 − y 2 {}_{x^{2}-y^{2}} -orbital bilayer t − J ∥ − J ⟂ t-J_{\parallel}-J_{\perp} model, in which the electrons of the d x 2 − y 2 {}_{x^{2}-y^{2}} orbital are charge carriers, which are subject to the intralayer antiferromagnetic (AFM) superexchange J ∥ J_{\parallel} and the large interlayer AFM superexchange J ⟂ ≈ 2 ​ J ∥ J_{\perp}\approx 2J_{\parallel} , with the latter transferred from the nearly half-filled and hence localized d z 2 d_{z^{2}} orbital through the strong Hund’s rule coupling. Here we study this model by the variational Monte Carlo (VMC) simulation and find a dominant interlayer s-wave pairing, in which the SC order parameters have a drastic improvement compared with those of the mean field (MF) type of theories. In real materials, the Hund’s coupling is finite, leading to reduced J ⟂ J_{\perp} , dictating that the MF-type theories have difficulty explaining the HTSC. However, our VMC calculations find that even for effective J ⟂ J_{\perp} as weak as J ⟂ = J ∥ J_{\perp}=J_{\parallel} , the interlayer pairing is still considerably large and can be compared with the T c T_{c} observed in experiments, which is very weak in MF-type theories. This result indicates the important role of the Gutzwiller projection in improving the T c T_{c} , which is ignored in the MF-type theories. In addition, our results show that suppressed interlayer hopping can promote interlayer pairing, which is consistent with the fact that the interlayer hopping of the d x 2 − y 2 {}_{x^{2}-y^{2}} orbital in La 3 Ni 2 O 7 is very weak. Our research offers a new perspective for understanding the pairing mechanism of bilayer nickelates and provides a reference for recent ultra-cold atom experiments in mixed-dimensional systems.

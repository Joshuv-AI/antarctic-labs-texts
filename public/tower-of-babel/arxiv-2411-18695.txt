Generalized snake posets, order polytopes, and lattice-point enumeration
Eon Lee, Andrés R. Vindas-Meléndez, Zhi Wang

Abstract
. Building from the work of von Bell et al. (2022), we study the Ehrhart theory of order polytopes arising from a special class of distributive lattices, known as generalized snake posets. We present arithmetic properties satisfied by the Ehrhart polynomials of order polytopes of generalized snake posets along with a computation of their Gorenstein index. Then we give a combinatorial description of the chain polynomial of generalized snake posets as a direction to obtain the h ∗ h^{*} -polynomial of their associated order polytopes. Additionally, we present explicit formulae for the h ∗ h^{*} -polynomial of the order polytopes of the two extremal examples of generalized snake posets, namely the ladder and regular snake poset. We then provide a recursive formula for the h ∗ h^{*} -polynomial of any generalized snake posets and show that the h ∗ h^{*} -vectors are entry-wise bounded by the h ∗ h^{*} -vectors of the two extremal cases.

This paper investigates the lattice-point enumeration and related properties of the order polytopes of a class of distributive lattices, known as generalized snake posets.
Order polytopes were popularized by Richard Stanley through his seminal paper

Ever since Stanley’s paper, order polytopes have actively drawn mathematician’s attention, where some aspects studied include: geometric and algebraic properties

One of Stanley’s fundamental observations is that the arrangement given by all hyperplanes of the form

Generalized snake posets are posets that are constructed recursively by adding a square face at the bottom and gluing it to an edge of the previous square.
These special posets make an appearance in

A geometric approach to studying these special posets can be considered through their order polytopes, which was the focus of

There the authors studied the circuits, flips, regular triangulations, and volumes of the order polytopes of generalized snake posets.

Our contributions in this paper add to the literature of order polytopes of generalized snake posets, where we take particular focus on their Ehrhart theory.
This work can be considered as initiating the study of order polytopes of distributive lattices in the context of Ehrhart theory.
The main object of interest is a distributive lattice with an upper bound

In the current work, we completely formulate the case for

This, together with the fact that the

2.2.
Order polytopes

Let
P
be a poset on the set of elements
[d]:=\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
We abuse notation and write
P
to denote the elements of
P
.
The
order polytope
of
P
, popularized by Stanley
[
22
]
, is defined as

\mathcal{O}(P)=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}:x_{i}\leq x_{j}\text{ for }i<_{P}j\right\}.

An
upper order ideal of
P
, also called a
filter
, is a set
A\subseteq P
such that if
i\in A
and
i<_{P}j
, then
j\in A
.
Let
J(P)
denote the poset of upper order ideals of
P
, ordered by reverse inclusion.
We use
\langle p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\rangle
to denote the upper order ideal generated by elements
p_{1},...,p_{k}\in P
.
Let
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
denote the standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For an upper order ideal
A\in J(P)
, define the
characteristic vector

\mathbf{v}_{A}:=\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
The vertices of
\mathcal{O}(P)
are given by

V(\mathcal{O}(P))=\left\{\mathbf{v}_{A}:A\in J(P)\right\}.

Define a hyperplane
\mathcal{H}_{i,j}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{i}=x_{j}\}
for
1\leq i<j\leq d
.
The set of all such hyperplanes induces a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{O}(P)
known as the
canonical triangulation
, which has the following three important properties:

(1)

\mathcal{T}
is unimodular,

(2)

the maximal simplices are in bijection with the linear extensions of
P
, so the normalized volume of the order polytope is

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}(P))=\#\text{ of linear extensions of }P,\text{ and}

(3)

the simplex corresponding to a linear extension
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
of
P
is

\sigma_{a_{1},...,a_{d}}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{d}:x_{a_{1}}\leq x_{a_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq x_{a_{d}}\right\},

with vertex set

\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{e}_{a_{d}},\mathbf{e}_{a_{d-1}}+\mathbf{e}_{a_{d}},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{a_{1}}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{a_{d}}=\mathbf{1}\}.

2.3.
Ehrhart theory

The
Ehrhart function
of a polytope (i.e., the convex hull of finitely many points)
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is the lattice-point enumerator
L_{P}(t):=|tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|
, where
tP=\{t\mathbf{x}:\ \mathbf{x}\in P\}
.
Ehrhart theory, developed by Eugène Ehrhart in the 1960s (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
) can be regarded as a discrete version of integration: the growth of the Ehrhart function provides information about the volume and surface area of
P
.
Ehrhart theory is connected to the combinatorics of simplicial complexes, as well as number theory and discrete analysis; for a comprehensive overview, see
[
2
]
.

When
P
is a lattice polytope (i.e., its vertices have integer coordinates), the Ehrhart function is a polynomial in
t
, with degree equal to the dimension of
P
, leading coefficient equal to its normalized volume, second-leading coefficient equal to half the normalized surface area, and constant coefficient 1.
The other coefficients of
L_{P}(t)
are more mysterious, and in general can be negative.
One of the main problems in Ehrhart theory is to characterize the coefficients that a polynomial must have in order to be the Ehrhart polynomial of a lattice polytope.
The Ehrhart polynomial of a lattice polytope
P
of dimension
n
can always be written in the form
L_{P}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}h_{i}^{*}\binom{t+n-i}{n}
; the sequence
(h_{0}^{*},\dots,h_{n}^{*})
is called the
h^{*}
-vector
.
One can also encode
L_{P}(t)
in a generating series, called the
Ehrhart series

\operatorname{Ehr}(P;z):=\sum_{t\geq 0}L_{P}(t)\,z^{t}=\frac{h^{*}(P;z)}{(1-z)^{n+1}}\,.

The polynomial
h^{*}(P;z)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}h^{*}_{i}z^{i}
is known as the
h^{*}
-polynomial
of
P
and is of degree less than
n+1
and has nonnegative integer coefficients.
Ehrhart and
h^{*}
-polynomials are important invariants of lattice polytopes, as they encode much of a polytope’s geometry, arithmetic, and combinatorics.
An important and useful result in Ehrhart theory is known as
Ehrhart–Macdonald reciprocity

[
8
,
16
]
, which states that for any convex lattice polytope
P
of dimension
n
:

L_{P}(-t)=(-1)^{n}L_{P^{\circ}}(t).

This provides a relationship between a polytope and its interior and also gives an interpretation for negative values of
t
.
Evaluating an Ehrhart polynomial at
-t
gives the lattice-point count for the
t^{\text{th}}
dilate of its interior (up to a sign).

For simplicity, denote the
h^{*}
-polynomial of
\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}))
as
h^{*}(\mathbf{w};z)
, i.e.,

h^{*}(\mathbf{w};z):=h^{*}(\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}));z).

Similarly, the Ehrhart polynomial will be denoted as
L(\mathbf{w};t)
.

2.4.
Order polytopes of generalized snake posets

As mentioned earlier, the volume of an order polytope
\mathcal{O}(P)
is determined by the number of linear extensions of the poset
P
.
The authors in
[

]
study the volume of

by considering the recursive structure of the poset of upper order ideals of

We recall some of their results here.

Theorem 2.6

(Theorem 3.6 and Corollaries 3.7 & 3.8,
[
3
]
)
.

For
n\geq 0
, let
\mathbf{w}=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
be a generalized snake word.
If

is given recursively by

v_{n}=\mathrm{Cat}(n-k+1)v_{k}+\left(\mathrm{Cat}(n-k+2)-2\cdot\mathrm{Cat}(n-k+1)\right)v_{k-1}

with
v_{-1}=1
and
v_{0}=2
, where
\mathrm{Cat}(m)=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}
is the
m

.
In particular,

•

for
S_{n}=P(\varepsilon LRLR\cdots)
or
S_{n}=P(\varepsilon RLRL\cdots)
,
the normalized volume of

The next result states that the normalized volume of an order polytope

of a generalized snake poset is bounded above and below by the volume of the order polytope of the ladder poset and the snake poset, respectively.
Note that the dimension of
\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}))
is

Theorem 2.7

(Theorem 3.10,

For any generalized snake word

\mathrm{vol}\mathcal{O}(S_{n})\leq\mathrm{vol}\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}))\leq\mathrm{vol}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{L}_{n}).

Recall that a lattice polytope is

if there exists a positive integer
k
such that
(k-1)P^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=
,
|kP^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=1
, and
|tP^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=|(t-k)P^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|
for all integers

.
This is equivalent to the polytope having a symmetric

-polynomial
[

]
.
Furthermore, the order polytope of a poset

is Gorenstein if and only if

is graded (i.e., all maximal

Observe that

is graded, thus we have that

is Gorenstein and has a symmetric

Furthermore when a polytope is Gorenstein the following holds:

|kP^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=1\text{ and }|tP^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=|(t-k)P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|,

for all integers

.
By also applying Ehrhart reciprocity and the above facts, we expect the root distribution of

to be symmetric, which motivates Theorem

Remark 2.8
.

To determine the Ehrhart polynomial (or

one focuses on the poset structure rather than the natural number labeling of the elements in the poset.
Hence, note that the Ehrhart polynomial of

corresponds with the Ehrhart polynomial of

Theorem 2.9
.

For any generalized snake word

of length

(1)

(t+1)(t+2)\cdots(t+n+3)\quad|\quad L(\mathbf{w};t)

Proof.

We will show that when

Assume that there is an integer point

.
Recall from
[

]

and then embedded to

Notice that a point

Since

Since

.

Thus,

\mathbf{w}=w_{0}w_{1}\dots w_{n}

\langle a,b\rangle

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\langle a,b\rangle}(\mathbf{w})

Denote the set of maximal chains of
\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathbf{w})

J(P(\mathbf{w}))
that are less than or equal to (greater than equal to) the element labeled with
\langle a\rangle

Then, for

\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathbf{w})=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\langle 2k+1,2k+2\rangle}(\mathbf{w})\cup\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\langle 2k,2k-1\rangle}(\mathbf{w})-\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\langle 2k+1,2k+2\rangle}(\mathbf{w})\cap\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\langle 2k,2k-1\rangle}(\mathbf{w})\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathbf{w})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}|\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\langle 2k+1,2k+2\rangle\downarrow}(\mathbf{w})||\mathcal{M}_{i-j}^{\langle 2k+1,2k+2\rangle\uparrow}(\mathbf{w})|+\sum_{j=0}^{i}|\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\langle 2k,2k-1\rangle\downarrow}(\mathbf{w})||\mathcal{M}_{i-j}^{\langle 2k,2k-1\rangle\uparrow}(\mathbf{w})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}|\mathcal{M}_{j}(\mathbf{w}[k:n])||\mathcal{M}_{i-j}(\mathbf{w}[0:k-1])|+\sum_{j=0}^{i}|\mathcal{M}_{j}(\mathbf{w}[k+1:n])||\mathcal{M}_{i-j}(\mathbf{w}[0:k])|

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{i}|\mathcal{M}_{j}(\mathbf{w}[k+1:n])||\mathcal{M}_{i-j}(\mathbf{w}[0:k])|-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{M}_{j}(\mathbf{w}[k+1:n])||\mathcal{M}_{i-j-1}(\mathbf{w}[0:k])|,

where the first equality follows from the construction of red L turns.
Combining Lemma
4.2

[z^{i}]h^{*}(\mathbf{w})=|\mathcal{D}_{i}(\mathbf{w})|=|\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathbf{w})|

4.3.

Consider the following
h^{*}
-polynomials:

h^{*}(\epsilon RRR,z)=z^{4}+10z^{3}+20z^{2}+10z+1,

h^{*}(\epsilon RRL,z)=z^{4}+8z^{3}+15z^{2}+8z+1,\text{ and }

h^{*}(\epsilon RLR,z)=z^{4}+7z^{3}+13z^{2}+7z+1.

Observe the coefficient-wise inequality:

[z^{k}]h^{*}(\epsilon RLR,z)\leq[z^{k}]h^{*}(\epsilon RRL,z)\leq[z^{k}]h^{*}(\epsilon RRR,z).

Our goal in this section is to generalize this inequality using the

Our result is strengthening of Theorem

Definition 4.17

5.
Future directions

To conclude, we present some conjectures for further investigation based on our computations.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be a generalized snake word of length
n+1
,

(1)

All roots of
h^{*}(\mathbf{w});z)
are real and negative.

(2)

All roots of
L(\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}));t)
are contained in the disk
|z-\frac{n+4}{2}|\leq\frac{n+2}{2},z\in\mathbb{C}
with axis of symmetry
x=\frac{-n-4}{2}
.

We have verified
5.1
for snake words of length up to
9
.

We also observed
Ehrhart positivity
empirically.

Conjecture 5.2
.

The order polytope
\mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w}))
is Ehrhart positive, i.e.,
L(\mathbf{w};t)
has nonnegative coefficients.

We have also observed a similar coefficient-wise inequality for the Ehrhart polynomial under the swap operator, parallel to Theorem
4.19
.

Conjecture 5.3
.

For
k>0
, the following coefficient-wise inequality holds:

[t^{k}]L(\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{w});t)\leq[t^{k}]L(\mathbf{w},t).

We also propose to generalize the operator
\varphi_{i}
to other distributive lattices, and to analyze the behavior of the
h^{*}
-vectors or the Ehrhart polynomials of other order polytopes under the generalized
\varphi_{i}
; the aim is to ascertain whether analogous monotonicity properties persist in those cases.

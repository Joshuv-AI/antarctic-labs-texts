Effective Bertini theorems and zeros of p p -adic forms of degree 7
Lea Beneish, Christopher Keyes

Abstract
. We establish an effective Bertini-type theorem for hypersurfaces X f : f = 0 X_{f}\colon f=0 defined over a finite field k k for which f f has no linear factors over the algebraic closure k ¯ \overline{k} . Given a line L L defined over k k and a nonreduced k ¯ \overline{k} -point x x on X f ∩ L X_{f}\cap L , we give an upper bound on the number of planes P P containing L L for which X f ∩ P X_{f}\cap P contains a line through x x . Underlying this result is a factorization algorithm for bivariate polynomials originally due to Kaltofen, which we present with slightly relaxed hypotheses. Our primary application is to Artin’s conjecture on p p -adic forms of degree 7: if K / ℚ p K/\mathbb{Q}_{p} is a finite extension with residue field isomorphic to 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} and F ⁡ ( x 0 , … , x n ) ∈ K ⁡ [ x 0 , … , x 49 ] F(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{49}] is a homogeneous form of degree 7, then there exists a K K -solution to F = 0 F=0 whenever q > 679 q>{679} . This improves on a result of Wooley.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we prove an effective Bertini-type theorem with a focus on ruling out certain factorizations in planar slices of a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
q
sufficiently large, this ensures
existence of a plane with the desired properties. We give an application of this to a conjecture of Artin concerning zeros of degree seven forms over
p
-adic fields.

1.1.
On Bertini theorems

The classical Bertini theorem states that given a smooth projective variety
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
over a field
k
, a generic hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has the property that
H\cap X
is smooth. In fact, there are several versions of this type of theorem where the adjective “smooth” is replaced with some other property of
X
, and a sufficiently general hyperplane intersected with
X
is guaranteed to have the same property. If
k
is infinite, the genericity of such a hyperplane is sufficient to guarantee the existence of one such hyperplane, but when
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
this need not be the case. One way to handle this is to replace the hyperplane
H
by a hypersurface of higher degree: Poonen showed for
X
a smooth quasiprojective subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, a positive density of hypersurfaces have smooth intersections with
X

[
Poo04
]
.

In another direction, Kaltofen
[
Kal95
]
gives an
effective
Bertini theorem replacing smoothness with geometric irreducibility for planar slices: given a geometrically irreducible hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
, he gives an upper bound on number of planes
P\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
for which
X\cap P
is not geometrically irreducible in terms of the degree of
X
. Over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this is essentially an effective version of the Hilbert Irreducibility Theorem. Cafure and Matera
[
CM06
]
modified this approach further to bound how often such a planar slice contains irreducible components of small degree. We give a modest improvement on this result below.

Theorem A

(See Theorem
2.1
and
[
CM06
, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]
)
.

Suppose
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is an absolutely irreducible (not necessarily homogeneous) polynomial of total degree
d
. Define a family of bivariate polynomials

f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}=f(X+v_{1},w_{2}X+z_{2}Y+v_{2},\ldots,w_{n}X+z_{n}Y+v_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,Y]

for

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}.

(i)

There exist at most

\frac{1}{8}\left(3d^{4}-2d^{3}+13d^{2}+2d\right)q^{3n-3}

tuples
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}
such that
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
is not absolutely irreducible.

(ii)

Fix a positive integer
D<d
. Then there exist at most

\frac{d}{8}\left(-D^{4}+4D^{3}d-6D^{3}+12D^{2}d-11D^{2}+8Dd-6D+16d\right)q^{3n-3}

tuples
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}
for which
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
has an irreducible factor of degree at most
D
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

We modify Theorem
A
further to address the situation where
f
may be reducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, but possesses no linear factors, subject to the hypothesis that for some
(\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n-1}
we have
f_{\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0},\mathbf{z}}(X,0)
has a simple root at
\alpha_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. More precisely, we construct a polynomial
\Psi(Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})
which, if nonzero, implies that
f_{\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0},\mathbf{z}}
has no linear factor vanishing at
(\alpha_{0},0)
; for the full statement, see Theorem
2.6
. In particular, we obtain the following corollary.

Theorem B

(See Corollary
2.7
)
.

Suppose
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a (not necessarily homogeneous) polynomial of total degree
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, possessing no linear factors over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Moreover, suppose there exist
(\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n-1}
such that
f_{\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0},\mathbf{z}}(X,0)\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[X]
is degree
d
with a simple root
\alpha_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

Then there exist at most
(3d-3)q^{n-2}
tuples
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n-1}
such that
f_{\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0},\mathbf{z}}
has no linear factor over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
containing
(\alpha_{0},0)
.

Our main application is to Artin’s conjecture for
p
-adic forms of degree 7.

1.2.
On Artin’s conjecture

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the field of
p
-adic numbers and let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Artin conjectured that
K
is
C_{2}
; that is, for all positive integers
d
and all
n\geq d^{2}
, every homogeneous polynomial
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d
has a nontrivial zero in
K
. This conjecture was proven false by Terjanian, who found a counterexample with
d=4
and
n=17
over
K=\mathbb{Q}_{2}

[
Ter66
]
; infinitely many counterexamples have since been shown to exist
[
LM83
]
.

However, weaker versions of the conjecture are known to hold. For a fixed degree
d
, we say that
K
is
C_{2}(d)
if for all
n\geq d^{2}
, every degree
d
form
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
has a nontrivial zero in
K
. All
p
-adic fields
K
are
C_{2}(2)
and
C_{2}(3)
, due to Hasse and Lewis, respectively
[
Lew52
]
. Using techniques from logic, Ax and Kochen showed that
p
-adic fields are
asymptotically

C_{2}(d)
, in the sense that for a fixed degree
d
,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
C_{2}(d)
for all but finitely many primes
p

[
AK65
]
. Moreover, a finite extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
C_{2}(d)
whenever
p\gg_{d,[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}0
. In fact, all of the known counterexamples to Artin’s conjecture involve composite degrees
d
, divisible by
q-1
, leaving open the following conjecture.

Conjecture

(Artin’s conjecture for prime degree
p
-adic forms)
.

Let
d>3
be prime and
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension. Then for all
n\geq d^{2}
, every degree
d
form
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots x_{n}]
has a zero in
K
, i.e.
K
is
C_{2}(d)
.

In degree
d=7
, we improve the best known effective lower bound on
q
for which Artin’s conjecture holds.

Theorem C
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a
p
-adic field with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
q>{679}
, then for all
n\geq 49
, every degree 7 form
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
has a zero in
K
, i.e.
K
is
C_{2}(7)
.

To establish Theorem
C
, we follow a
p
-adic minimization approach due to Birch and Lewis
[
BL59
]
and later refined by Laxton and Lewis
[
LL65
]
to give effective bounds on
q
for which Artin’s conjecture holds in degrees
d=5,7,11
. They proposed to reduce to the case of so-called
reduced
forms
F
, then use an effective version of the Lang–Weil bound for the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points on a variety to produce a nonsingular zero of
\overline{F}
, the image of
F
under the residue map, when
q
is sufficiently large. This then lifts to a
K
-zero of
F
by Hensel’s lemma.

Refinements of this general strategy have shown that
K
is
C_{2}(5)
whenever
q>{5}
due to
[
LY96
,
HB10
,
Dum17
]
and to forthcoming work of the authors, and that
K
is
C_{2}(7)
(resp.
C_{2}(11)
) whenever
q>{883}
(resp.
q>{8053}
) due to
[
Kna01
,
Woo08
]
. This strategy breaks down for prime degrees
d>11
, since
d
can be written as the sum of composite numbers, which allows for the hypersurface given by
\overline{F}=0
to have only totally nonreduced nonlinear components.

In degree 5, Leep and Yeomans
[
LY96
]
refined the
p
-adic minimization techniques of Birch and Lewis by exploiting the structure of
singular
plane quintic curves. They argue that if
\overline{F}
has no nonsingular zeros (which would then be liftable to
K
by Hensel’s lemma), then there exists a plane on which
\overline{F}
restricts to a quintic curve with at least 3 singularities. The presence of these singularities reduces the
q
threshold for which explicit point counting methods guarantee a liftable point.

In contrast, in degree 7 (resp. 11), Wooley’s bound
[
Woo08
, Theorem 1]
is obtained using the effective Bertini theorems
[
CM06
, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]
to find plane curves of degree 7 (resp. 11) with a geometrically integral component, then applying the Hasse–Weil bounds. In particular, Wooley does not exploit any singularities in the relevant degree 7 (resp. 11) curves, citing the absence of rigidity on the anatomy of a polynomial imposed by the relatively low degree of a quintic form.

Inspired by the work of Leep and Yeomans in the quintic case, we further exploit the structure of reduced degree 7 forms either to find nonsingular zeros of
\overline{F}
, or to show that (a dehomogenization of)
\overline{F}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
B
. Upon applying Theorem
B
, we produce a plane curve with at least two singular
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points and a geometrically irreducible component; these extra singularities lower the
q
threshold for which we can find nonsingular points on the curve, and hence zeros of
F
in
K
.

1.3.
Notation and organization

We reserve the following to have consistent meaning throughout:

•

p
denotes a prime;

•

q
denotes a power of
p
;

•

K
denotes
p
-adic field with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

•

\overline{k}
denotes the algebraic closure of a field
k
;

•

F
denotes a homogeneous form over
K
.

We will often write
f=\overline{F}
for the image of
F
under the residue map, to avoid confusion with the algebraic closure.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we describe a factorization algorithm for bivariate forms, which we use to prove Theorem

. We give a proof of Theorem
A
in Section
2.3
. In Section

and in Section
4
we recall the definition of reduced forms and collect auxiliary results about them. Finally, we prove Theorem
C
in Section
5
.

2.
Effective Bertini theorems

Let
k
be an arbitrary field and suppose
f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
over
k
, irreducible over
\overline{k}
. Recall that generically, the intersection of the projective hypersurface
X_{f}\colon f=0
remains geometrically irreducible when intersected with a hyperplane; this is Bertini’s theorem for irreducibility. However, when
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this does not suffice to guarantee the existence of even one such hyperplane.

By an
effective
Bertini theorem, we refer to a result which bounds how often the non-generic behavior occurs. When
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this can then be used give a lower bound on
q
for which the generic behavior is guaranteed to occur at least once. We will be particularly interested in intersecting
X_{f}
with a
plane
, rather than a hyperplane, and find it convenient to work in affine coordinates.

Dehomogenizing by setting
x_{0}=1
, consider the family of bivariate polynomials given by

f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}=f(1,X+v_{1},w_{2}X+z_{2}Y+v_{2},\ldots,v_{1},w_{n}X+z_{n}Y+v_{n})\in k[X,Y]

for

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in k^{3n-2}.

We think of
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
as cutting out an affine plane curve, obtained by slicing the
x_{0}\neq 0
affine patch of
X_{f}
by the plane determined by
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})
. If the original
f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
is absolutely irreducible, then generically so is
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
.

This was made effective by Kaltofen, using an algorithm for testing irreducibility of a bivariate polynomial to deduce the existence of a polynomial condition on
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})
whose vanishing is implied by reducibility of
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}

[
Kal95
]
. Cafure and Matera modified this approach to detect how often
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
has factors of small degree
[
CM06
]
. We recall Theorem
A
, a mild strengthening of
[
CM06
, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]
. For completeness, we describe the necessary modifications to the proofs of
[
CM06
, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]
in §
2.3
.

Theorem 2.1

(See
[
CM06
, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]
)
.

Suppose
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is an absolutely irreducible (not necessarily homogeneous) polynomial of total degree
d
.

(i)

There exist at most

\frac{1}{8}\left(3d^{4}-2d^{3}+13d^{2}+2d\right)

tuples
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}
such that
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
is not absolutely irreducible.

(ii)

Fix a positive integer
D<d
. Then there exist at most

\frac{d}{8}\left(-D^{4}+4D^{3}d-6D^{3}+12D^{2}d-11D^{2}+8Dd-6D+16d\right)q^{3n-3}

tuples
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}
for which
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
has an irreducible factor of degree at most
D
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
d=7
and assume
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is irreducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. By Theorem
2.1
(i), when
q>{896}
we have that there must exist at least one
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}
such that
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}(X,Y)
is absolutely irreducible. Suppose instead that we only require that
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}(X,Y)
does not have a
linear
factor over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Then applying Theorem
2.1
(ii) with
D=1
, such a
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})
exists whenever
q>{224}
.

The latter condition corresponds to the plane curve cut out by
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
having no linear irreducible components over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Since
d=7
, this forces at least one of the irreducible components to also have multiplicity 1 and be defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This was a key insight used by Wooley
[
Woo08
]
to show that
p
-adic fields are
C_{2}(7)
when
q>{883}
. (resp.
C_{2}(11)
when
q>{8053}
).

We will need a variant of Theorem
2.1
describing how often
X_{f}
has
linear
factors when sliced by planes through a specified line
L
, under some hypotheses on
X_{f}\cap L
. Before stating and proving this variant in Corollary
2.7
, we briefly describe a relaxation of Kaltofen’s factorization algorithm for
f(X,Y)
over
\overline{k}
, which gives partial information when
f(X,0)
is not squarefree.

2.1.
A factorization algorithm

In this subsection, suppose
f(X,Y)\in k[X,Y]
is monic in
X
and has total degree
d
(not necessarily homogeneous). Fix a positive integer
D<d
. The factorization algorithms found in
[
Kal95
]
and
[
CM06
]
underlying Theorem
2.1
request that
f(X,0)
is squarefree. With slight modifications however, they still yield useful information when
f(X,0)
is
not squarefull
, i.e. when considered over
\overline{k}
,
f(X,0)
has at least one simple root. We recall the setup for the algorithm; see also
[
Kal95
, §1]
,
[
CM06
, §3.1]
.

Let
\alpha_{0}\in\overline{k}
be a root of
f(X,0)
such that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(\alpha_{0},0)\neq 0
. Viewing
f
as a univariate polynomial in
X
over
k(\alpha_{0})[[Y]]
, since the partial derivative at
\alpha_{0}
is a unit, we can lift
\alpha_{0}
to
\widetilde{\alpha}\in k(\alpha_{0})[[Y]]
satisfying
f(\widetilde{\alpha},Y)=0
. Set
\ell_{\max}=dD
, our desired order of approximation. Truncating, we produce
\alpha\in k(\alpha_{0})[Y]
of
Y
-degree at most
\ell_{\max}
satisfying

\displaystyle f(\alpha,Y)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell_{\max}+1}}.

This may be accomplished explicitly via Newton’s method, as in
[
Kal95
,
CM06
]
. For
\mu=1,\ldots,D
we write
\alpha^{\mu}
as

\alpha^{\mu}\equiv\sum_{r=0}^{\ell_{\max}}a_{\mu,r}Y^{r}\pmod{Y^{\ell_{\max}+1}},

where the
a_{\mu,r}\in k(\alpha_{0})
.

We now look for a polynomial
g\in\overline{k}[X,Y]
of degree
m\leq D
for which
g\mid f
and
g(\alpha_{0},0)=0
. Here we will use
\ell=dm
as our order of approximation. For each such
m
we examine whether

(2.1)

\alpha^{m}+\sum_{\mu=0}^{m-1}h_{\mu}(Y)\alpha^{\mu}\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell+1}}

has a solution for
h_{\mu}(Y)\in\overline{k}[Y]
with
\deg h_{\mu}\leq m-\mu
. Writing

h_{\mu}(Y)=\sum_{\eta=0}^{m-\eta}u_{\mu,\eta}Y^{\eta}

for
u_{\mu,\eta}\in\overline{k}
, we have that (
2.1
) holds if and only if the linear system

(2.2)

a_{m,r}+\sum_{\mu=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\eta=0}^{m-\mu}a_{\mu,r-\eta}u_{\mu,\eta}=0\text{ for }0\leq r\leq\ell

has a solution in
\overline{k}
. Note that here we take the convention that
a_{\mu,r-\eta}=0
when
\eta>r
.

Example 2.3

(
D=1
)
.

As an explicit example, we describe (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) in the special case of
D=1
. Here we take
m=1
, so we are looking to solve

\alpha+h_{0}(Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{d+1}},

for
h_{0}(Y)=u_{0,1}Y+u_{0,0}\in k(\alpha_{0})[Y]
. We have

\alpha+h_{0}(Y)=a_{1,0}+u_{0,0}+(a_{1,1}+u_{0,1})Y+\sum_{r=2}^{d}a_{1,r}Y^{r},

so a solution to (
2.1
) must have

\displaystyle a_{1,0}+u_{0,0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{1,1}Y+u_{0,1}Y

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{1,2}Y^{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{1,d}Y^{d}

\displaystyle=0.

Rearranging (
2.2
) into a matrix equation over
\overline{k}
, this looks like

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0,0}\\
u_{0,1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-a_{1,0}\\
-a_{1,1}\\
-a_{1,2}\\
\vdots\\
-a_{1,d}\end{pmatrix}.

This system is insoluble for
d>1
, unless
a_{1,r}=0
for
2\leq r\leq d
, i.e.
\alpha\equiv a_{1,0}+a_{1,1}Y\pmod{Y^{d+1}}
all along.

When
D=1
, a solution to (
2.2
) is equivalent to
f
possessing a linear factor vanishing on
(\alpha_{0},0)
. This is essentially a modified version of
[
CM06
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
f(X,Y)\in k[X,Y]
is a (not necessarily homogeneous) polynomial of total degree
d
such that
f(X,0)
is degree
d
in
k[X]
with a simple root
\alpha_{0}\in\overline{k}
.

Then
f
factors over
\overline{k}
as
f=g(X,Y)h(X,Y)
for
\deg g=1
satisfying
g(\alpha_{0},0)=0
if and only if the linear system (
2.2
) has a solution in
k(\alpha_{0})
for
m=1
.

Proof.

If (
2.2
) has a solution in
k(\alpha_{0})
for
m=1
, then we have
\ell=d
and there exists
g(X,Y)\in k(\alpha_{0})[X,Y]
, linear in
X
and
Y
, such that
g(\alpha,Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell+1}}
. Consider
R(Y)=\mathrm{Res}_{X}(f,g)\in k(\alpha_{0})[Y]
, which is a polynomial in
Y
of degree at most
d
. Since
f(\alpha,Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell+1}}
as well, we have
R(Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell+1}}
, This forces
R=0
, so
f
and
g
have a nontrivial common factor in
k(\alpha_{0})[X,Y]
. But
g(X,Y)
is already linear, so we have
g\mid f
and
g(\alpha_{0},0)=0
.

Suppose
f(X,Y)=g(X,Y)h(X,Y)
over
\overline{k}
for
\deg g=1
satisfying
g(\alpha_{0},0)=0
. Since
\alpha_{0}
is a simple root of
f(X,0)
, we must in fact have
g
defined over
k(\alpha_{0})
; otherwise, the conjugates of
g
in
\overline{k}/k(\alpha_{0})
would also vanish at
(\alpha_{0},0)
, producing a multiple root of
f(X,0)
at
\alpha_{0}
. With
g,h
defined over
k(\alpha_{0})
, we also have
h(\alpha_{0},0)\neq 0
, so
h(\alpha,Y)\not\equiv 0\pmod{Y}
. Since
f(\alpha,Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell_{\max}+1}}
, we have
g(\alpha,Y)\equiv 0\pmod{Y^{\ell_{\max}+1}}
. The coefficients of
g
thus produce a solution to (
2.2
) in
k(\alpha_{0})
for
m=1
.
∎

If
1<D\leq d-1
, we can still apply the strategy above to study the factorization of
f
. Given a solution to (
2.2
), we similarly produce
g
of degree at most
D
which has a common factor with
f

Proposition 2.5

Suppose

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose

Proof.

We follow the same lines as

[

we observe the system (

Proof of Theorem

We conclude this section by giving a proof of Theorem

[

, §3]
and highlight the minor modification to the necessary order of approximation which leads to the modest improvement over
[

, Corollaries 3.2, 3.4]

[

]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Suppose that
f(X,Y)\in k[X,Y]
is a monic polynomial of
total degree

d
over an arbitrary field
k
, such that
f(X,0)
is squarefree. Cafure and Matera
[
CM06
, §3.1]
give an algorithm for testing whether such

has a factor of degree at most
D<d
, which can be improved slightly in this case by setting
\ell_{\max}=dD
(instead of
2dD
). The correctness of this modified algorithm is verified by the same argument as in the proof of
[
CM06
, Lemma 3.1]
, similarly to Proposition
2.4
above. Following their notation, we have since the total degrees of
f
and
g
are
d
and
m\leq D
, respectively, the resultant
\mathrm{Res}_{X}(f,g)\in\overline{k}[Y]
has degree at most
dm
in
Y
.

Returning to our absolutely irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, following
[
CM06
, §3.2]
and
[
Kal95
, Lemmas 4, 5]
, there exists a polynomial
\Upsilon(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})
of total degree at most

such that

\chi(X,Y,Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})=f(X+v_{1},w_{2}X+Z_{2}Y+v_{2},\ldots,w_{n}X+Z_{n}Y+v_{n})

is irreducible in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[X,Y,Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n}]
with
\chi(X,0,Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[X]
squarefree, whenever

\Upsilon(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\neq 0.

The upshot is that (after possibly dividing by the leading coefficient in
X
) we can think of
\chi\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})[X,Y]
and apply the aforementioned factorization algorithm. As in
[
CM06
, Theorem 3.3]
, this produces a polynomial
\Psi_{D}^{(i)}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n}]
such that for each root
\alpha_{i,0}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
of
\chi(X,0,Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})
, if
\Psi_{D}^{(i)}(z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0
, then
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}(X,Y)=\chi(X,Y,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
has no factors of degree at most
D
vanishing on
(\alpha_{i,0},0)
. Taking
\Psi_{D}=\prod\Psi_{D}^{(i)}
, we have that
f_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}}
has no factors of degree at most
D
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
whenever

\Xi_{D}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\Upsilon(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\Psi_{D}(z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0.

To estimate the degree of
\Psi_{D}
, and hence
\Xi_{D}
, the arguments of
[
CM06
, Theorem 3.3]
(see also
[
Kal95
, Theorem 5]
) remain unchanged, save for the updated value of
\ell_{\max}=dD
. We have

\displaystyle\deg_{Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n}}\Psi_{D}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{(D+1)(D+2)/2-1}\ell_{\max}-j

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(-D^{4}+4D^{3}d-6D^{3}+12D^{2}d-11D^{2}+8Dd-6D\right)

Multiplying this bound by

yields an upper bound for
\deg_{Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n}}\Psi_{D}
, and adding

to account for

gives

\deg\Xi_{D}\leq\frac{d}{8}\left(-D^{4}+4D^{3}d-6D^{3}+12D^{2}d-11D^{2}+8Dd-6D+16d\right).

Combining this with the well known fact (see e.g.
[
CM06
, Lemma 2.1]
)

\#\{(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3n-2}:\Xi_{D}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})=0\}\leq(\deg\Xi_{D})q^{3n-3}

gives the stated result (ii).

The result (i) follows from recognizing that we need only that

to ensure that

is absolutely irreducible, since at least one factor of

must vanish on
(\alpha_{1,0},0)
. The corresponding degree bound yields the stated result.
∎

Throughout this section, we take

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose

Proof.

Suppose

We proceed by induction on

∎

then

has at least four distinct linear factors cutting out the codimension 1 linear subvarieties
U,\langle U_{1}^{(1)},v\rangle,\langle U_{2}^{(1)},v\rangle,\langle U_{3}^{(1)},v\rangle\subset\langle U,v\rangle
. One of these components must have multiplicity 1, so by the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.5
we have
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
.
∎

We conclude this section by examining the situation of a plane curve of degree 7 over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which has at least three non-colinear
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points and no linear components defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Unfortunately, we cannot guarantee the existence of a smooth
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point, even when
q
is sufficiently large. We can, however, find either a smooth
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point or a line spanned by (singular)
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points which elsewhere meets the curve at a nonsingular geometric point.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
q>{591}
and suppose
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
is a nonzero ternary degree 7 form with no linear factors defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose further that there exist
u,v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle u,v,w\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then either
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
or there exist
u_{1},u_{2}\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
and
X_{f}\cap\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle
contains a geometric point of multiplicity 1.

Proof.

If any of
u,v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}
, then we are done, so we assume they are all singular. Since
f
has no linear factors defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the possible factorization types of
f
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are
(7),(5,2),(4,3),(3,2,2),(3,2^{2})
.

Suppose one of these factors of multiplicity 1 remains irreducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Let
C^{\prime}\subset X_{f}
denote the corresponding irreducible component curve of degree
d^{\prime}
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and absolutely irreducible. If
d^{\prime}=7
then the geometric genus of the normalization of
X_{f}
is at most 12, owing to the presence of the singular points
u,v,w
. Lemma
3.3
gives that
q>{591}
guarantees
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
. If
d^{\prime}<7
then the hypothesis
q>{591}
is sufficient to show
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
by Lemma
3.3
.

Assume now that all of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-factors of
f
with multiplicity 1 are reducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. In the case of factorization type (7), the only possibility is that
f
factors into 7 linear forms conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{7}}
. Such
f
can vanish on at most one
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point, contradicting our hypothesis that
u,v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Similarly, in the case of
(5,2)
we have
f
can vanish on at most two
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points, again contradicting the hypothesis.

In the remaining factorization types we see that
f
has a cubic factor which is geometrically reducible, necessarily factoring as a product of three linear forms conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. Let
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
be the corresponding lines in the plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

Suppose
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
meet at a common point
x\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Since the points
u,v,w
are not collinear, we must have that
x
does not lie on at least one of the lines
\langle u,v\rangle,\langle u,w\rangle,\langle v,w\rangle
. Without loss of generality, assume
x\notin\langle u,v\rangle
. Thus
f|_{\langle u,v\rangle}
vanishes to order 2 on
u
and
v
and order 1 on the three distinct
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
-points
\langle u,v\rangle\cap L_{i}
, so we may take
u_{1}=u
and
u_{2}=v
.

If
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
do not meet at a common point, then they contain no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points. Again considering the line
\langle u,v\rangle
, we have that at most two of of the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
-points
\langle u,v\rangle\cap L_{i}
can coincide. Thus
f|_{\langle u,v\rangle}
vanishes to order 2 on
u
and
v
and order 1 on at least one of the
\langle u,v\rangle\cap L_{i}
, so we may take
u_{1}=u
and
u_{2}=v
.
∎

4.
Reduced forms and consequences

We recall the setting of Artin’s conjecture for degree
d
forms.
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a
p
-adic field with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, uniformizer
\pi
, normalized discrete valuation
v
, and residue field
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(\pi)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We have a homogeneous form
F\in K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d
for
n\geq d^{2}
. After rescaling, we may assume
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
\overline{F}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We will use the lowercase
f=\overline{F}
to denote the reduction to the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
X_{f}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
for the special fiber of
X_{F}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

By Hensel’s lemma, if
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
, then
X_{F}(K)\neq\emptyset
. Therefore our goal will be to show that
X_{f}
has a smooth
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point when
q
is sufficiently large. A key strategy in achieving this was introduced by Birch and Lewis
[
BL59
]
in the degree
d=5
case, then later refined by Laxton and Lewis
[
LL65
]
to apply in degrees
d=7,11
. They define a notion of
reduced forms
which we recall now before establishing the relevant consequences. Both
[
LL65
]
and
[
LY96
, §4]
are useful references.

4.1.
Reduced forms

With
F
as above, let

I(F)=\mathrm{Res}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{0}},\ldots,\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{n}}\right)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

By the general theory of (multivariate) resultants,
I(F)
is homogeneous of degree
(d-1)^{n}
in the coefficients of the partial derivatives of
F
. We also have
[
LL65
, Lemma 5]

I(aF(T\mathbf{x}))=a^{(n+1)(d-1)^{n}}(\det T)^{d(d-1)^{n}}I(F)

for
a\in K^{\times}
and
T\in\GL_{n+1}(K)
acting on the variables
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
by linear change of coordinates.

Definition 4.1

(equivalent forms)
.

Two degree
d
forms
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
are
equivalent
if there exists
a\in K^{\times}
and
T\in\GL_{n+1}(K)
such that
F^{\prime}=aF(T\mathbf{x})
.

Definition 4.2

(reduced form)
.

We say a degree
d
form
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is
reduced
if
I(F)\neq 0
and

v(I(F))\leq v(I(aF(T\mathbf{x})))
for all
a\in K^{\times}
and
T\in\GL_{n+1}(K)
such that
aF(T\mathbf{x})
has coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. That is,
I(F)
has minimal valuation among equivalent forms with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
LL65
,
LY96
]
)
.

We have the following facts about reduced forms.

(i)

If for all reduced degree
d
forms
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
we have
X_{F}(K)\neq\emptyset
then
X_{F}(K)\neq\emptyset
for all degree
d
forms
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, i.e.
K
is
C_{2}(d)
.

(ii)

If
n\geq d^{2}
and
F
is a reduced degree
d
form, then its reduction
f=\overline{F}
has no linear factors defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

(iii)

Let
q\geq{d}
. If
n\geq d^{2}
,
F
is a reduced degree
d
form, and
f
vanishes on an
s
-dimensional linear subvariety
U\simeq\mathbb{P}^{s}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

\#X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\geq\frac{q^{s+2}-1}{q-1}.

Proof.

It is established in
[
LL65
, Corollary]
that to show every
F
has a
K
-solution, it suffices to show this only for forms with
I(F)\neq 0
. After possibly replacing
F
by an equivalent form, we may assume
I(F)
has minimal valuation, i.e.
F
is reduced. This does not change solubility, so it suffices to check
X_{F}(K)\neq 0
for reduced forms, giving (i).

(ii) is
[
LL65
, Lemma 9]
, while (iii) is
[
LY96
, Corollary 4.4]
. In both cases, the idea is to use the fact that
F
is a reduced form to bound the
order
of
f
(or its factors), i.e. to bound the minimal number of variables appearing nontrivially in
f
, up to change of coordinates. To prove (ii), one understands that linear factors have small order which is at odds with these lower bounds; for (iii) an order estimate is combined with Warning’s estimate for
\#X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(see e.g.
[
LY96
, Lemma 3.1]
).
∎

4.2.
Consequences

Drawing inspiration from the work of Leep and Yeomans in the quintic case
[
LY96
]
, we seek to exploit what we know about reduced forms to produce a nonsingular point of
X_{f}
which can be lifted to
X_{F}(K)
.

Our immediate goal, accomplished below in Proposition
4.5
, is show that either
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
or we can produce two points
u,v\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
f
possesses a nonsingular
geometric
point on the line between them. Later, in the proof of Theorem
C
, we will use this in conjunction with Theorem
2.6
to produce a planar slice of
X_{f}
with at least two (possibly singular)
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points. The presence of these two
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points is key to improving on
[
Woo08
, Theorem 1]
.

As in
[
LY96
]
, we will make use of a linear subvariety
U\subset X_{f}
of maximal dimension. In the following lemma, we show that not only can we find points
v,w\in(X_{f}-U)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, but we can ensure the line
\langle v,w\rangle
is skew to
U
. This is accomplished by strengthening an argument presented in
[
LY96
, Proof of Proposition 5.4]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
q>{7}
and suppose
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a reduced degree 7 form for
n\geq 49
. Suppose
U\subset X_{f}
is a linear subvariety defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of maximal dimension. Then either
X_{f}(F_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
or there exist
v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\langle v,w\rangle\not\subset X_{f}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle v,w\rangle\cap U=\emptyset.

Proof.

We have
\#U(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\frac{q^{s+1}-1}{q-1}<X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by Proposition
4.3
(iii), so there exists
v\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})-U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Moreover, we have
\langle U,v\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
by maximality of
U
, and since
q>{7}
an application of Lemma
3.1
reveals that
f
does not vanish on all
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points of
\langle U,v\rangle
. Since
\#\langle U,v\rangle(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\frac{q^{s+2}-1}{q-1}
, Proposition
4.3
(iii) again ensures there exists a point
w\in(X_{f}-\langle U,v\rangle)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

We now have
\langle v,w\rangle\cap U=\emptyset
. If
\langle v,w\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
then we are done. Suppose instead that for all
v,w\in(X_{f}-U)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle v,w\rangle\cap U=\emptyset
, we have
\langle v,w\rangle\subset X_{f}
. Let
W\subset X_{f}
be a linear subvariety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of maximal dimension such that
W\cap U\subset W
has codimension at least 2. Such
W
exists because earlier we found a line
\langle v,w\rangle\subset X_{f}
skew to
U
.
2
2

2

By convention, for
W
a linear variety of dimension
s
, the codimension of
\emptyset\subset W
is
s+1
.

We claim that
W
contains
(X_{f}-U)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Suppose to the contrary that
x\in(X_{f}-U-W)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Since
U\cap W\subset W
has codimension at least 2, we have that
\langle U,x\rangle\not\supset\langle W,x\rangle
.

Let
y\in\langle W,x\rangle(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. If
y\notin\langle U,x\rangle\cap\langle W,x\rangle
, then
\langle x,y\rangle\cap U=\emptyset
. Since
y
is contained on a line between
x
and a point of
W
, by assumption we have
\langle x,y\rangle\subset X_{f}
, and in particular,
y\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Thus
f
vanishes on all of
\langle W,x\rangle(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, save possibly for a a subspace of codimension at least 1 arising from
\langle U,x\rangle\cap\langle W,x\rangle
. But then on
\langle W,x\rangle
, we have that there exists a nonzero linear form
\ell
such that
\ell f|_{\langle W,x\rangle}
vanishes on all points; by Lemma
3.1
,
\ell f|_{\langle W,x\rangle}=0
. Since
\ell\neq 0
, we have
f|_{\langle W,x\rangle}=0
, i.e.
\langle W,x\rangle\subset X_{f}
.

Note that the dimension of
U\cap\langle W,x\rangle
is at most one more than that of
U\cap W
, so its codimension is still at most 2 in
\langle W,x\rangle
. However, this violates the maximality of
W
, yielding a contradiction. We conclude that
(X_{f}-U)(\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset W(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, as claimed.

Now we have
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=U(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cup W(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. However, counting
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points we see

\#X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq\#U(\mathbb{F}_{q})+\#W(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq 2\frac{q^{s+1}-1}{q-1}<X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

by Proposition
4.3
(iii), yielding another contradiction. Thus there exist
v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})-U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
satisfying the desired hypotheses.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
q>{591}
and suppose
F\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a reduced degree 7 form for
n\geq 49
. Then either
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
or there exist
u_{1},u_{2}\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
and
X_{f}\cap\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle
contains a geometric point of multiplicity 1.

Proof.

Let
U\subset X_{f}
be a linear subvariety defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of maximal dimension, denoted by
s
. We have
s\geq 0
by Proposition
4.3
(iii). We treat the
s=0
,
s=1
, and
s\geq 2
cases separately.

Case
s=0
. If
s=0
, then by Proposition
4.3
(iii) we have
\#X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\geq q+1>3
, so there exist at least four
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points on
X_{f}
. If any four of them are colinear, then at least one has multiplicity 1, so we have
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
. Thus there exist three points
u,v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle u,v,w\rangle\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Applying Lemma
3.7
to
f|_{\langle u,v,w\rangle}
, we have the desired result.

Case
s=1
. Let
U\subset X_{f}
be a line defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By Lemma
4.4
there exist
v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that the line
\langle v,w\rangle
is skew to
U
and not contained in
X_{f}
. If for any point
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we have that
f
vanishes on no lines in the plane
\langle u,v,w\rangle
, then we may apply Lemma
3.7
, as in the
s=0
case.

Assume to the contrary that for all
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we have that
f
vanishes on some line in
\langle u,v,w\rangle
. By Lemma
3.6
, either
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
or there are at most two points
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle u,v\rangle\subset X_{f}
, since
\langle U,v\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
in this case as
s=1
. Similarly, there are at most two
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for which
\langle u,w\rangle\subset X_{f}
, and if there is a third point
x\in(\langle v,w\rangle\cap X_{f})(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, at most two
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for which
\langle u,x\rangle\subset X_{f}
.

Thus for all but at most 6 points
u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we have that
f
does not vanish on any lines between
u
and a point on
(X_{f}\cap\langle v,w\rangle)(\mathbb{F}_{q})\supset\{v,w\}
. Instead,
f
must vanish on a line containing either
v
,
w
, or the potential third point
x
on
(X_{f}\cap\langle v,w\rangle)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. When
q>{17}
, this guarantees the existence of at least five points
u_{1},\ldots,u_{5}\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
f
vanishes on a line
V_{i}\subset\langle u_{i},v,w\rangle
through a common point
v
,
w
, or
x
. Without loss of generality, assume
v\in V_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,5
. Note that
V_{i}\cap U=\emptyset
; if not we find that
w\in\langle U,v\rangle
, contradicting our choice of
\langle v,w\rangle
skew to
U
.

Suppose for
1\leq i<j<k\leq 5
we have that the lines
V_{i}
,
V_{j}
,
V_{k}
are coplanar. Recalling that
V_{i}\cap U=\emptyset
, we have that the plane
\langle V_{i},V_{j},V_{k}\rangle
meets
U
at a point not contained in the union of
V_{i}
,
V_{j}
, and
V_{k}
. Examining Lemma
3.5
, we have that
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
.

Assume now that for no
(i,j,k)
triples are
V_{i},V_{j},V_{k}
coplanar, and set
u_{ij}=U\cap\langle V_{i},V_{j}\rangle
for all
i<j
. The points
u_{12},u_{13},u_{23}
are distinct, since otherwise
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
are coplanar, so
f
must not vanish on the line between one of them and
v
, by Lemma
3.6
. Without loss of generality, assume
\langle u_{12},v\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
.

Consider the plane spanned by
V_{1},V_{2}
. Here
f|_{\langle V_{1},V_{2}\rangle}
vanishes on the two lines
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, as well as a point
u_{12}
. By Lemma
3.5
, either we have
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
, or
f|_{\langle V_{1},V_{2}\rangle}
vanishes on a line defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
containing
u_{12}
. In the latter case, if the line is not defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then in fact there exist two or three conjugate lines of multiplicity 1, intersecting at

. This allows us to choose distinct points
u_{i}\in V_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{q})-\{v\}
for
i=1,2
such that
u_{12}\notin\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle
. Since
\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle
meets the aforementioned conjugate lines in distinct

Finally, suppose that
u_{12}
is contained in a line
L\subset X_{f}\cap\langle V_{1},V_{2}\rangle
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We have
L\cap V_{i}=\emptyset
for

, since no three

, each

\langle L,U\rangle

; moreover, these three points are not colinear, since the

\langle L,U\rangle
,

vanishes on two lines and three non-colinear points, so by Lemma
3.5
we must have
X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\mathrm{sm}}\neq\emptyset
, and we are done.

Case
s\geq 2
. As in the previous case, by Lemma
4.4
, there exist
v,w\in X_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\langle v,w\rangle\not\subset X_{f}
and is skew to
U
. First, we argue there exists a chain of linear subvarieties defined over

,

u=U^{(s)}\subset U^{(s-1)}\subset\ldots\subset U^{(2)}\subset U^{(1)}\subset U^{(0)}=U,

and

\langle U^{(j)},x\rangle\not\subset X_{f}\text{ for all }x\in(X_{f}\cap\langle v,w\rangle)(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

. To find

Suppose now that in

, the maximal dimension of such is

, and we may replace

by

and repeat the argument of this case. If

, then we have reduced to the case of

above, so we are done.

Suppose to the contrary that for

satisfying

(which implies (

) for

), we have that

of dimension

. Each

meets the line

at exactly one of the at most three

-points of

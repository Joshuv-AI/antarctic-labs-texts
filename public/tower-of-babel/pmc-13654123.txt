Three-dimensional rendering of optical coherence tomography for a vitreous cyst: moving beyond cross-sectional interpretation
Authors: Yunhan Tao, Yiteng Zhang, Hongtao Liu
Published: 2026

Vitreous cysts are uncommon intraocular findings that are often incidentally detected during routine ophthalmic examinations (1). While volumetric optical coherence tomography (OCT) acquisition and three-dimensional (3D) processing have been explored (2,3), routine clinical interpretation remains largely limited to isolated two-dimensional (2D) cross-sectional images (1,4,5). However, these modalities provide limited spatial information regarding the 3D configuration of the lesion and its relationship with surrounding vitreous structures. Building upon this framework, we present an image-based observation showing how 3D volumetric rendering can enhance diagnostic performance and better delineate the lesion’s relationships with surrounding structures.

A 33-year-old female presented with persistent monocular floaters in her right eye for four years. She had no history of ocular trauma, intraocular inflammation, or systemic parasitic infections. Ophthalmic imaging identified a freely mobile, translucent lesion within the mid-vitreous cavity. To further characterize the lesion, high-density volumetric scanning (6 mm × 6 mm field of view, 9 mm depth, comprising 512 horizontal B-scans with 512 A-scans each) was performed using a 1,050-nm swept-source OCT platform (VG200D; SVision Imaging, Henan, China).

As shown in Figure 1, conventional 2D OCT B-scans merely displayed an annular structure with a hyper-reflective wall, a hypo-reflective core, and posterior optical shadowing due to beam attenuation. Although this B-scan slice confirmed the presence of a cystic structure, the internal solid components, precise spatial morphology (e.g., tubular, discoid, or spherical), and its relationship to adjacent vitreous structures could not be adequately delineated.

Conventional 2D OCT B-scan demonstrating a cystic structure within the vitreous cavity, providing limited information regarding its overall spatial configuration. 2D, two-dimensional; OCT, optical coherence tomography.

To address these limitations and provide actionable diagnostic information, the volumetric dataset was reconstructed using the device’s integrated ray-casting rendering software to generate an interactive, multi-angle 3D view. The volumetric rendering (Figure 2) definitively revealed a smooth, perfectly spherical geometry. Importantly, this 3D visualization intuitively confirmed the cyst’s complete spatial separation from the retinal surface and definitively excluded internal solid components (such as a parasitic scolex). Given the benign morphological confirmation, the patient was managed conservatively. In this context, the application of 3D OCT provides imaging information beyond that obtained from conventional 2D scans. While vitreous cysts are generally benign entities, accurate morphological characterization may assist in differential diagnosis, particularly when distinguishing cystic lesions from other vitreous opacities or parasitic entities. Moreover, in common conditions such as epiretinal membranes or vitreomacular traction, 3D volumetric views help surgeons precisely map focal attachment points before procedures, minimizing iatrogenic risks (6,7).

In conclusion, 3D OCT imaging offers complementary information beyond conventional B-scan OCT. Moving beyond isolated cross-sectional interpretation maximizes the diagnostic utility of modern OCT platforms, serving as a powerful, non-invasive optical biopsy for accurately characterizing vitreous abnormalities.

PILOCARPINE, C11H16N2O2, an alkaloid found, together with
isopilocarpine and other related compounds, in the leaves of
jaborandi (Pilocarpus pennatifolius). It was first isolated by E.
Hardy in 1875 (Ber., 8, p. 1594), and is a crystalline, very hygroscopic
solid It is a strong poison. It has the properties of a
monacid base and contains the methylamino group, ·NCH3.
When heated with hydrochloric acid it gives isopilocarpine
Isopilocarpine was isolated in 1900 by H. A. D. Jowett (Journ.
Chem. Soc. 77, p. 473), and is a colourless oil which boils at 261° C.
(10 mm.). It is a monacid base which is readily soluble
in solutions of the caustic alkalis. Jowett is of the opinion
that pilocarpine and isopilocarpine are stereo-isomers of the structure:—

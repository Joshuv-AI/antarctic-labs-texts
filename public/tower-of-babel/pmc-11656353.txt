Predicting the Rate of Penetration while Horizontal Drilling through Unconventional Reservoirs Using Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Hassan Almomen, Ahmed Abdulhamid Mahmoud, Salaheldin Elkatatny, Abdulazeez Abdulraheem
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEstimating the rate of penetration (ROP) is one of most critical tasks for evaluating the efficiency and profitability of drilling operation, which will aim in decision-making related to well planning, time estimation, cost estimation, bit selection, operational troubles, and logistics in drilling operation. The rise in unconventional resource development underscores the need for accurate ROP prediction to optimize drilling operations in these valuable reserves. ROP prediction and optimization in unconventional hydrocarbon reservoirs are challenging due to the formations’ heterogeneity, high strength, and brittleness. These reservoirs often involve complex well designs, high pressures, and high temperatures, making it difficult to maintain optimal drilling conditions. This study presents the optimization and validation of the artificial neural network (ANN) model to predict the ROP during horizontal drilling through unconventional hydrocarbon reservoirs. The ANN model was trained using 34,869 data points from five wells (Well-1 to Well-5) and achieved a high correlation coefficient of 0.96 and an average absolute percentage error (AAPE) of 4.68%. An empirical correlation was developed based on the weights and biases of the optimized ANN model. The empirical correlation performance was rigorously tested with 23,246 data points, representing 40% of the data from the same wells, yielding an AAPE of 4.75% and a correlation coefficient of 0.96. Validation of the developed equation on data from Well-6 further confirmed the model’s robustness, maintaining a correlation coefficient of 0.91 and an AAPE of 5.75%. These results demonstrate the ANN model's and empirical equation's accuracy and reliability in predicting the ROP, highlighting their potential to optimize drilling operations in unconventional hydrocarbon reservoirs.

Introduction

The rate of penetration (ROP) is a parameter defining the average drilled distance into the formation measured in feet per time in hour, and it is commonly reported in feet per hour (ft/h) or rarely in meter per hour (m/h).1−3 The ROP reflects the speed of the drilling operation for a certain well, section, formation, or for a specified interval. In fact, the average ROP is one of the most critical parameters for evaluating the efficiency and profitability of drilling operation.4,5 The prediction of the ROP is also highly important in well planning, time estimation, cost estimation, bit selection, operational troubles, and logistics for drilling operation.6−8

Therefore, ROP optimization is always a concern in the well drilling operation; although a high ROP seems to increase the drilling rate, a high ROP may lead to bad hole cleaning conditions, which will result in reducing the ROP later and prevent further penetration into the formation.9−11

Many factors affect the rate of penetration including the formation properties and drilling parameters.12 Formation properties are uncontrolled factors that usually cannot be adjusted such as compressive strength, pressure gradient, formation fluids, lithology, and rock properties.13 On the other hand, drilling parameters are controlled parameters that directly affect the ROP such as weight on bit (WOB), drill string rotation (DSR), torque, drilling fluid inflow rate or pump rate, standpipe pressure (SPP), and differential pressure.7 In addition, some other factors affect the ROP including mud weight, hydraulics, and rheology, in addition to the well deviation, bit size, bit type, deformation, and string vibration.14,15 The interaction of all these parameters makes ROP prediction and optimization a very complex task.

Recent Advances in Predicting the Rate of Penetration in Conventional Hydrocarbon Formations through Artificial Intelligence Applications

Despite the complexity of ROP prediction, many authors were able to develop empirical correlations or machine learning models for prediction of the ROP while drilling through conventional hydrocarbon reservoirs vertically or horizontally, this including drilling oil-rich formations as well as gas bearing formations, regardless if these formations are carbonate or sandstone formations and if the well is onshore or offshore.16−24

Artificial intelligence (AI) has been used worldwide in many real-life problems and provides different solutions. In the oil industry, AI has been proven to be used in estimating bit wear, mud rheology, lithology identification, rock mechanics, petrophysical properties, reservoir pressure, and evaluation of unconventional resources.25−32

Elkatatny21 developed an empirical equation for predicting the ROP in real time using different AI techniques including artificial neural networks (ANN), adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference systems, and support vector machines (SVM). The model incorporated drilling parameters, PDC bit design, mud density, and gamma ray data. The ANN demonstrated the highest accuracy in predicting the ROP for complex formations, providing a reliable tool for improving drilling efficiency without requiring specialized software.

In another study, Deng et al.33 introduced a method for evaluating the accuracy of ROP prediction using a logistic regression model. By analyzing drilling and logging data from an oilfield in Xinjiang through MATLAB, the study trained an ROP classification model before drilling. Results showed that logistic regression outperformed support vector regression, achieving an R2 range of 0.80–0.86, making it suitable for drilling needs. The study highlighted the significant impact of lithology on prediction accuracy and suggested modifying the real-time ROP model with measured drilling parameters to reduce costs and optimize the drilling efficiency.

In 2022, Gamal et al.34 applied machine learning to predict drillability rates in complex lithologies using an ensemble technique. The model integrated three learners, the functional network (FN), radial basis function (RBF), and SVM, with the SVM as the meta learner. Utilizing data from two wells with complex geology, including anhydrite, carbonates, and abrasive sandstones, the ensemble model outperformed the base learners. It achieved a correlation coefficient above 0.95 compared to 0.91 for base learners. The results demonstrated that the ensemble model significantly improved the ROP prediction accuracy, addressing the challenges of drilling through complex formations.

Al-AbdulJabbar et al.35 developed an empirical correlation to estimate the ROP while drilling into sandstone formations, using the ANN model trained on mechanical drilling parameters. The model was trained on 630 data points from five wells and tested on 270 points from the same wells, achieving an average absolute percentage error (AAPE) of 7.5%. Validation on three additional wells yielded AAPEs of 9.0, 10.7, and 8.9%. The results showed that the new equation outperformed existing empirical models in the ROP prediction accuracy.

Recently, Zou and Hu36 developed a model to improve the accuracy of ROP prediction using a particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm to optimize parameters in support vector regression (SVR). The model utilized four drilling parameters, including DSR, WOB, flow rate, and depth, as inputs. Comparative experiments showed that the Gaussian kernel function enhanced model generalization. By applying PSO to optimize the SVR parameters, the proposed model outperformed traditional SVR and neural network models in prediction accuracy, demonstrating its effectiveness for drilling operations.

Challenges of ROP Prediction in Unconventional Reservoirs

Unconventional reservoirs refer to crude oil, natural gas, and condensate resources that form in low permeable rock such as shale, tight sandstone, and tight carbonate formations. These formations are called unconventional resources since they cannot be explored, developed, produced, and sustained using conventional methods.37−39 The unconventional resources are concentrated in many places in the world mainly in North America, Latin America, East of Europe, Middle East, and North Africa.40 An estimation of 227 trillion cubic meters is available in the world as sources of different forms of unconventional hydrocarbons.41 Due to the low permeability in unconventional resources, many wells were designed to be drilled horizontally to maximize the contact with the target formation and increase production.42,43

Since exploration and drilling through these unconventional recourses are highly costly, it is important to optimize the process of drilling through these resources, and one of the factors to help on this optimizing process is the control of the ROP.44−46 Despite the huge number of models developed for ROP estimation in conventional reservoirs, there is no model or empirical equation developed for ROP prediction in unconventional reservoirs.

Predicting and optimizing the ROP in unconventional hydrocarbon reservoirs present significant challenges due to the complex and heterogeneous nature of these formations. These reservoirs are characterized by varying rock properties, such as strength, brittleness, and porosity, which make it difficult to apply a one-size-fits-all approach for ROP prediction. Drilling through these formations often involves hard and abrasive rocks that accelerate bit wear and require frequent adjustments to drilling parameters like WOB and DSR.47,48

Additionally, high-pressure and high-temperature conditions, common in deeper unconventional wells, complicate the optimization process. Drilling fluid properties must also be carefully managed to avoid formation damage and ensure proper cutting transport. Wellbore instability, real-time data limitations, and nonstandardized models for these reservoirs further hinder the ability to maintain efficient drilling. Achieving an optimal ROP requires advanced real-time monitoring, customized bit designs, and automation to adapt drilling parameters in real time.

Artificial Neural Networks

Artificial neural networks (ANN) are the most common powerful tool in AI and have many applications in the petroleum industry, and the ANN model works in the same way as the human brain as it uses the biological neurons for learning aspects, to learn and generalize from input data or signal received; usually, it is used to solve complex problems. The general ANN model consists of a minimum of three layers: the input layer, hidden layer, and output layer.7

The performance of an ANN depends on many design parameters including the number of inputs, ratio of training to testing data points, number of layers, number of neurons, and type of the training and transfer function. The ANN works by processing the data to the output layer over input and hidden layers where the weights are updated based on the error between the experimental or actual measured data and the predicted data.

To obtain the best combination of all ANN design parameters, sensitivity analysis is needed to evaluate the accuracy of all combinations of these design parameters and to choose the best combination based on the highest accuracy and less error. The optimized ANN model is usually characterized by specific weights and biases; these are usually used to extract an empirical correlation relating the inputs to the output.16,19,49,50

This paper is aimed at developing for the first time an empirical correlation for predicting the ROP while horizontally drilling though an unconventional reservoir based on the drilling parameters of the input parameters of the WOB, DSR, torque, hookload, SPP, and mud weight (MW), and this model will be built as a function of the optimized weight and biases of the ANN model.

Methodologies

Data Collection and Preprocessing

In this study, real-time drilling data were collected from six wells while horizontally drilling through unconventional hydrocarbon reservoir (Figure 1), all collected data were real-time recorded drilling parameters, and these included the WOB, DSR, torque, hookload, SPP, MW, and the ROP. The use of real-time recorded data is important to ensure real-time prediction and optimization of the ROP.

Distance between the six wells considered to collect the data of this work.

The data were collected in all six wells from the same formation and identical lithology of the unconventional reservoir, all six wells are located in an area of 25 km diameter, and the first five wells are close to each other compared to Well-6, which is a little bit far. These wells were drilled using the same type of drillbit and utilizing the same BHA type; the same oil-based drilling fluid was also used in all wells. The length of the wellbore from which the data were collected in every well was around 2000 ft, where 4–5 data sets were recorded every foot.

The data gathered from the six wells originally contain a total of 59,726 data points of the different input parameters of the WOB, DSR, torque, hookload, SPP, and MW and their corresponding ROP. An initial data cleaning was performed by removing all null, abnormal, and negative values; hence, the number of data points was reduced to 58,231 after this initial cleaning.

Further cleaning was performed by removing data recorded during nondrilling time and the outliers. At this stage, all data collected during connections or tripping were removed; in addition, all data outside a range of ±3.0 standard deviation were considered as outliers and extracted from the training data set. This again reduced the number of data points to only 58,115.

Learning the Artificial Neural Network Model

After data preprocessing, 58,115 data points were considered valid for the model-learning process; data of five wells (Well-1 to Well-5) were used to train the ANN model and test the developed empirical correlation, while the data from the last well, Well-6, were considered for validating the empirical correlation. The goal was to develop an ANN model capable of accurately predicting the ROP based on the available drilling parameters. During this learning phase, careful optimization of both the training data percentage and the ANN model’s design parameters was conducted. This optimization involved fine-tuning key design parameters critical to the model’s effectiveness and efficiency, including the selection of an appropriate learning function, the optimal number of learning layers, the ideal number of neurons per layer, and the most suitable transfer function for transferring information within the network layers.

Optimizing these parameters is essential to ensure that the ANN model can capture complex relationships within the data and make accurate ROP predictions. By meticulously adjusting these parameters, the model can effectively learn from the training data and generalize its predictions to unseen data points. This optimization process enhances the model’s predictive performance and robustness, resulting in more reliable ROP estimations in the target wells.

The optimization of the training data percentage, ranging from 40 to 95% with a step size of 5%, was executed using a for loop in MATLAB. This iterative process aimed to determine the most suitable proportion of data for training the model. Subsequently, the performance of various training functions was evaluated to identify the most effective approach for training the ANN model. This assessment included testing the Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation function, resilient backpropagation function, and Bayesian regularization backpropagation function,51,52 each known for their unique characteristics and suitability in different scenarios.

Additionally, the effectiveness of different transfer functions in predicting the ROP was investigated. This analysis involved testing the tangential sigmoidal function, logarithmic sigmoidal function, and linear function,53,54 each offering distinct mathematical properties that can impact the model’s predictive capabilities. The influence of the number of layers in the ANN model architecture was also explored, ranging from single-layer configurations to models with two or three layers. Within each layer, varying neuron counts, from 4 to 30 neurons per layer, were examined to assess their impact on the model’s performance.55

The objective of this sensitivity analysis and optimization process was to refine the predictive accuracy and resilience of the ANN model, thereby furnishing reliable estimations of the ROP within the designated wells. By systematically probing the ANN design parameters, this part of the study was aimed to bolster the model’s capability to generate reliable predictions, thus enhancing its applicability and reliability in real-world scenarios.

The outcomes of the learning phase indicated that employing 60% of the available data, 34,869 data points to train the ANN model, coupled with a single learning layer comprising eight neurons, yielded optimal results for predicting the ROP. The choice of the Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation function for training the model, along with the tangential sigmoidal transferring function, further enhanced predictive accuracy. These configurations were determined through rigorous testing and an evaluation of various combinations of the ANN design parameters, and the selected parameters were found to show the minimum error and the highest correlation coefficient between the actual and estimated ROP.

Testing and Validation of the Rate of Penetration Empirical Correlation

The testing and validation process of the optimized ANN model, and consequently the equations derived from it, is vital for assessing generalization performance, preventing overfitting, tuning hyperparameters, comparing models, detecting data issues, and building trust in the model’s performance.56,57 This process is crucial to ensure that the model performs well on unseen data and effectively addresses its intended problem.

The developed correlation in eq 15 was then tested using the remaining data sets from the first five wells (Well-1 to Well-5), and it was validated on new unseen data from Well-6. The accuracy of eq 15 was evaluated by considering the AAPE and the correlation coefficient between the actual and predicted pore pressure.

The correlation coefficient is defined as a statistical measurement of the strength of relationship between two variables or parameters. The correlation coefficient ranged between [−1,1] where the values toward −1 are negatively correlated and values toward 1 indicated that variables are positively correlated. In addition, values toward 0 indicate no direct relationship between the variables.

The average absolute percentage error (AAPE) or mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) is a value calculated to find the average of errors for all predicted values in the data set and indicate the prediction and forecasting accuracy of a certain model. AAPE is one of the simple methods to make interpretation and evaluation of large data sets. In fact, the APPE can be calculated simply using eq 16.

The percentage calculated indicates how much the forecasted values deviate from actual values. This is a crucial indicator for any study that requires forecasting, and usually, each study or project done sets a standard criterion for setting the maximum accepted average absolute percentage error. If the AAPE exceeded the maximum acceptable values, then the study model or project strategy was revised to reduce the error.

Results and Discussion

Learning the Artificial Neural Network Model

The ANN model was trained using 60% of the data collected from the first five wells (Well-1 to Well-5), comprising a total of 34,869 data points. This extensive data set provided a robust foundation for the ANN to learn the intricate patterns necessary for accurate ROP prediction. Figure 2 illustrates the predicted versus actual ROP values for the training data. The scatter plot shows that all data points lie very close to the 45° line, indicating that the predicted ROP values closely match the actual values across the training data set. This visual confirmation of high accuracy underscores the model’s reliability in ROP prediction for the five wells used in this training process.

The optimum ANN model demonstrated impressive predictive capabilities, achieving a correlation coefficient of 0.96 for the training data, as shown in Figure 2. This high correlation indicates a strong linear relationship between the predicted and actual ROP values, suggesting that the model captures the underlying dynamics effectively.

Moreover, Figure 3 also shows that the model exhibited an AAPE of 4.68%. This relatively low AAPE reflects the model’s precision in predicting the ROP, with predictions deviating minimally from the actual values on average. Such accuracy is crucial in drilling operations, where precise ROP predictions can significantly impact decision-making and operational efficiency.

Actual vs predicted ROP using ANN for training data of the five first wells, Well-1 to Well-5, with a total of 34,869 data points.

The proximity of the data points to the 45° line along with the high correlation coefficient and low AAPE not only validates the model’s performance but also builds confidence in its applicability to real-world scenarios. The successful training on this diverse data set from multiple wells highlights the model’s potential to generalize and perform well on new, unseen data, thereby offering a valuable tool for optimizing drilling operations and enhancing overall efficiency.

Testing the Rate of Penetration Empirical Correlation

In addition to the training phase, the ROP empirical correlation of eq 15 underwent rigorous testing using 23,246 data points, representing 40% of the data collected from the first five wells (Well-1 to Well-5). The scatter plot of the predicted versus actual ROP for the test data, as shown in Figure 4, validates the performance of the developed ROP correlation. Similar to the training data, the points in the test data scatter plot align closely with the 45° line. This visual alignment demonstrates that the developed correlation predictions are consistently accurate across the testing data sets.

Actual ROP vs predicted ROP using ANN for testing data of the five first wells, Well-1 to Well-5, with a total of 23,246 data points.

Equation 15 during the testing phase was impressive, achieving an AAPE of 4.75%, as indicated in Figure 4. While slightly higher than the AAPE observed during the ANN model training (4.68%), this low error margin still indicates a high level of precision in the developed empirical correlation predictions. It also confirmed the robustness and ability of this correlation to maintain accuracy when applied to new data, a crucial factor for practical applications in drilling operations.

Furthermore, eq 15 achieved a correlation coefficient of 0.96 during the testing. This strong correlation underscores the empirical equation (eq 15) capability to accurately predict the ROP based on the input features. It suggests that the predicted and actual ROP values have a strong linear relationship, indicating reliability and effectiveness of the developed correlation for ROP prediction.

Validation of the Rate of Penetration Empirical Correlation

To further evaluate the developed ROP empirical correlation of eq 15 generalization capabilities, it was validated using data collected from Well-6, with data that were not considered during training and testing stages. This validation phase utilized a comprehensive data set, ensuring that the developed empirical correlation performance could be assessed in a new context, distinct from the wells used in the training and testing phases.

The scatter plot of the predicted versus actual ROP for the Well-6 validation data shown in Figure 5, akin to the plots for the training and testing phases, shows that the points align closely with the 45° line. This visual confirmation highlights the consistent accuracy of eq 15, reinforcing its capability to generalize well to new data.

Actual vs predicated ROP using ANN for validation data of Well-6.

Equation 15 on the Well-6 data was commendable, achieving an AAPE of 5.75%, as shown in Figure 4. This AAPE, a little bit higher than that observed during the testing phase (4.75%), indicates that the developed ROP correlation remains robust when applied to entirely new data. Such a consistency in error margins across different data sets underscores the empirical correlation reliability and accuracy in practical applications.

Additionally, the empirical correlation of eq 15 attained a correlation coefficient of 0.91 with the Well-6 data. This strong correlation signifies a high degree of linear relationship between the predicted and actual ROP values, confirming eq 15 effectiveness in capturing the underlying dynamics of the ROP even in new drilling environments. A correlation coefficient of 0.91 reflects the robustness and ability of the developed equation to maintain predictive accuracy beyond the original data set.

The successful validation with Well-6 data demonstrates the ANN model’s and eq 15's strong generalization capabilities. The consistent performance across training, testing, and validation phases also suggests that the ANN model and eq 15 effectively learn the patterns necessary for accurate ROP prediction, regardless of the specific well data that they encounter. These reliability and precision make the developed empirical equation a valuable asset for real-world drilling operations, contributing to enhanced decision-making and operational efficiency.

By improving the accuracy of ROP prediction, the developed an equation (i.e., eq 15) that has the potential to significantly reduce the drilling time and costs, thereby enhancing the overall well economics, particularly while horizontally drilling the complex formations in the unconventional reservoirs, which requires the use of complicated drilling strings with advanced and costly components. Such advancements could lead to more efficient resource extraction, reduced nonproductive time, and optimized bit and tool selection.

Conclusions

In this work, an empirical equation was developed based on the retrieved weights and biases of the optimized ANN model for ROP prediction in real time while horizontally drilling through an unconventional reservoir. The optimized ANN model was trained on a substantial data set from five wells (Well-1 to Well-5), which resulted in high predictive accuracy, as evidenced by a correlation coefficient of 0.96 and an AAPE of 4.68%.

The developed empirical correlation was tested on data collected from the same training wells. The results confirmed the robustness of this equation, achieving a correlation coefficient of 0.96 and an AAPE of 4.75%. Further validation of the developed ROP equation using data from a new well (i.e., Well-6) demonstrated the ability to generalize this equation well to new, unseen data, maintaining consistent performance metrics with a correlation coefficient of 0.91 and an AAPE of 5.75%.

The close alignment between the predicted ROP and actual measurements across all phases of evaluation underscores the ANN model and the developed equation reliability and effectiveness. These findings indicate that the ANN model and the developed equation are well-suited for real-world applications, offering a powerful tool to enhance decision-making and operational efficiency in horizontal drilling operations within unconventional hydrocarbon reservoirs.

For future work, it is recommended to validate the developed equation on data from another field to ensure more generalization of this equation; in addition, it is recommended to develop an equation for predicting the ROP ahead of the drill bit while drilling; this will help in taking an advanced decision for more optimization of the drilling process.

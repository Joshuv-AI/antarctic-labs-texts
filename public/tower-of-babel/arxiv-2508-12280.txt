Atom-surface interaction induced by quenched monopolar charge disorder
Bing-Sui Lu

Abstract
We study the modification to the energy level shifts of an atom induced by the quenched monopolar charge disorder inside the bulk of neighboring dielectric slabs as well as their surfaces. By assuming that the charge disorder follows Gaussian statistics with a zero mean, we find that the disorder generally results in a downward shift of the energy levels, which corresponds to an attractive force that can compete with and overcome the nonresonant Casimir-Polder force for sufficiently large atom-surface separations z 0 z_{0} . For an atom near a single semi-infinite slab with bulk (surface) charge disorder, the shift decays as z 0 − 1 z_{0}^{-1} ( z 0 − 2 z_{0}^{-2} ). For both surface and bulk disorder, the shift is proportional to the variance of the charge disorder density. In addition, we investigate the behavior of the charge disorder-induced energy level shift for an atom confined to a vacuum gap between two coplanar and semi-infinite slabs of the same dielectric material, finding that the position of net zero disorder-induced force occurs closer to the surface of the slab with the smaller charge disorder variance.

The model

II.1
Quenched charge disorder

For an atom positioned at a height
z_{0}
above the top surface of a semi-infinite planar dielectric slab (see Fig.
1
), we set the position of the top surface of the slab at
z=0
. Furthermore, we assume there is a homogeneously distributed monopolar charge disorder, which can occur inside the bulk of the slab as well as the surface.
We consider the case where the charge disorder is
quenched

[
37
]
, which means that the disorder does not relax on experimental timescales. Quenched disorder is quite prevalent and not limited to electrostatic ones, but occur in many other physical domains as well, such as DNA sequences
[
38
,
39
]
and liquid crystalline polymer networks
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
We also assume that each impurity charge has an equal probability of being positive or negative, so that the mean charge disorder is zero, thus ensuring the slab is overall charge neutral. The statistics of the charge disorder is described by

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho({\mathbf{r}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(1)

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}),

(2)

where the overline
\overline{\ldots}
denotes an average over all realizations of the quenched disorder, and
\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})
is a correlation function which is maximum when
{\mathbf{r}}={\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}
and decays to zero as
|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|\to\infty
.

In what follows, we assume that the charge disorder is spatially uncorrelated, so we can express the correlation function in terms of a Dirac delta-function.
This assumption corresponds to looking at atom-surface separations much greater than the correlation length of the monopolar charge disorder. In order to estimate the range of separations over which such an assumption is valid, we assume that the material is polycrystalline, with the surface potentials randomly varying across the grains. As similar impurity charges tend to be adsorbed onto the same grain, the grain size sets the correlation length of the charge disorder. If we consider an average grain size of 10 nm, the assumption of spatial decorrelation will be valid for separations much greater than 10 nm, which is the range that we consider in the present paper.

Writing
\delta\rho_{S}
and
\delta\rho_{B}
for the surface and bulk charge disorder respectively, the charge disorder statistics is

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}})}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle 0,\,\,\overline{\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}})}=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})\delta(z-0-)\delta(z^{\prime}-0-),

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}).

(3)

Here,
\sigma_{S}^{2}
denotes the mean-square charge disorder per unit area (or the variance of the surface charge disorder density), and
\sigma_{B}^{2}
denotes the variance of the bulk charge disorder per unit volume. The symbol “
0-
” indicates that the surface charge disorder resides just below
z=0
.

II.2
Charge disorder-induced energy shift

The atom is polarized by the local field which is a superposition of two types of fluctuating fields: a field generated by the fluctuating dipoles inside the dielectric slab, as well as a field generated by the quenched and randomly distributed impurity charges.
As we mentioned earlier, we assume that the atom is small, so that we can neglect the quadrupolar interaction, and describe the atom-slab interaction for a
given
realization of the charge disorder by the dipole interaction Hamiltonian:

H_{I}=\hat{\mu}_{a}(\mathcal{E}_{0a}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})+\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})),

(4)

where
a=x,y,z
labels the Cartesian axis directions, and
{\mathbf{r}}_{0}
is the position vector of the atom in three-dimensional space. We take the origin to be the point on the slab surface directly beneath the atom, so that

{\mathbf{r}}_{0}=z_{0}\,{\bf e}_{z},

(5)

where
{\bf e}_{z}
is the unit vector directed in the positive z-direction.
The symbol
\delta\hat{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})
denotes the (quantum) electromagnetic field fluctuation at the atom’s position, arising from the quantum fluctuations of dipoles inside the dielectric slab, and
{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{0}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})
denotes the electric field generated at the atom’s position by the monopolar impurity charges.
For the case where the atom is near a single slab, and the impurity charges are on the slab’s surface, the disorder-induced field at
{\mathbf{r}}_{0}
is given by

{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{0}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})=\frac{2}{\varepsilon(0)+1}\int d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}\,\frac{\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}})\,\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}})}{|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}_{0}|^{2}},

(6)

where
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}})
is the unit vector directed from a given local charge disorder at position
{\mathbf{r}}
to the atom, and the surface charge disorder density is defined by

\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}})\equiv\sum_{i}q_{i}\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{i\perp})\delta(z),

(7)

\varepsilon(0)
is the static dielectric permittivity of the slab,
q_{i}
is the
i
th monopolar impurity charge,
{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}
is a two-dimensional position vector in the plane of the surface, and
{\mathbf{r}}_{i\perp}
is the two-dimensional position vector of the
i
th impurity charge in the plane of the surface.
The vanishing of the mean charge disorder thus implies the vanishing of the disorder-averaged electric field,
viz
.,

\overline{{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{0}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})}=0,

(8)

and a similar result holds for the case where the field is induced by impurity charges in the bulk.

The dipole interaction in Eq. (
4
) leads to a shift in the energy levels of the atom. As the dipole interaction is weak, we can treat the interaction Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4
) as a perturbation. The first-order perturbation correction to the energy of the
n
th level is given by

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N}P(N)\langle N|\langle n|\hat{\mu}_{a}(\mathcal{E}_{0a}+\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{a})|n\rangle|N\rangle

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0a}\langle n|\hat{\mu}_{a}|n\rangle+\sum_{N}P(N)\langle N|\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{a}|N\rangle\langle n|\hat{\mu}_{a}|n\rangle.

In the equation above,
\{|n\rangle\}
is the set of eigenstates of the unperturbed atom,
\{|N\rangle\}
is the set of eigenstates of the unperturbed radiation bath, and
P(N)
is the Boltzmann probability of finding the radiation bath to be in the eigenstate
|N\rangle
.
The first term on the right-hand side of the above equation vanishes under disorder averaging (cf. Eq. (
8
)), and the second term vanishes under thermal and quantum averaging. Thus, we consider the second-order perturbation correction, which is given by

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{(2)}\!\!=\!\!-\mathcal{P}\Bigg(\sum_{m,M,N}P(N)\frac{\langle N|\langle n|\hat{\mu}_{a}(\mathcal{E}_{0a}+\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{a})|m\rangle|M\rangle}{E_{M}-E_{N}+E_{m}-E_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\langle M|\langle m|\hat{\mu}_{b}(\mathcal{E}_{0b}+\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{b})|n\rangle|N\rangle\Bigg).

(10)

After averaging over the charge disorder, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\delta E_{n}^{(2)}}

(11)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{m}\frac{\overline{\mathcal{E}_{0a}\mathcal{E}_{0b}}\,\mu_{a}^{nm}\mu_{b}^{mn}}{\omega_{mn}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\mathcal{P}\Bigg(\sum_{m,M,N}P(N)\frac{\langle N|\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{a}|M\rangle\langle M|\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{b}|N\rangle\mu_{a}^{nm}\mu_{b}^{mn}}{\omega_{M}-\omega_{N}+\omega_{mn}}\Bigg).

Here, we have used the notation
\omega_{M}\equiv E_{M}/\hbar
,
\omega_{mn}\equiv(E_{m}-E_{n})/\hbar
(where

\hbar=1.0546\times 10^{-27}\,{\rm erg.s}
is the reduced Planck constant in cgs units) and
\mu_{a}^{nm}\equiv\langle n|\hat{\mu}_{a}|m\rangle
.
In the expression above, there are no cross-terms involving products of
\mathcal{E}_{0a}
and
\delta\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
, as
\mathcal{E}_{0a}
vanishes under disorder averaging.
The first term on the right-hand side (RHS) represents the additional energy level shift induced by the presence of the charge disorder, whilst the second term leads to the Casimir-Polder shift for a charge disorder-free surface
[
2
,
18
,
19
,
4
,
20
]
.
Using the formula for the polarizability of the
n
th atomic state
[
2
]
,

\alpha_{ab}^{n}(\omega)=\frac{2}{\hbar}\sum_{m}\frac{\omega_{mn}\mu_{a}^{mn}\mu_{b}^{nm}}{\omega_{mn}^{2}-(\omega+i\eta)^{2}},

(12)

we can write the first term on the RHS of Eq. (
11
) as

\delta E_{n}^{{\rm Stark}}=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{ab}^{n}(0)\overline{\mathcal{E}_{0a}\mathcal{E}_{0b}}.

(13)

This correction describes a quadratic Stark effect produced by the charge disorder-induced electric field. Although we have derived the expression for the case of surface charge disorder, it applies equally well for the case of bulk charge disorder.
The force generated by the charge disorder is then

f=-\frac{\partial\delta E_{n}^{{\rm Stark}}}{\partial z_{0}}.

(14)

II.3
Nonresonant Casimir-Polder shift

As we will subsequently compare the charge disorder-induced interaction correction with the nonresonant Casimir-Polder energy shift, let us recall the formula for the nonresonant Casimir-Polder shift of a metastable or ground-state atom near a surface at zero temperature, which includes retardation effects
[
2
]
:

\delta E^{CP}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})=-\frac{\hbar}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\xi\,G_{ij}^{R}({\mathbf{r}}_{0},{\mathbf{r}}_{0};i\xi)\alpha_{ij}(i\xi).

(15)

We consider an atom such as metastable helium
{\rm{He^{\ast}}}
, whose dynamic polarizability can be described by the following one-oscillator model,

\alpha_{ij}(i\xi)=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{1+(\xi/\omega_{0})^{2}}\delta_{ij},

(16)

with the static polarizability
\alpha_{0}=315.63\,{\rm a.u.}=4.678\times 10^{-23}\,{\rm cm^{3}}
and
\omega_{0}=1.18\,{\rm eV}=1.794\times 10^{15}\,{\rm rad/s}

[
44
]
. The symbol
G_{ij}^{R}
denotes the reflection Green tensor. For an isotropic atom, we require only the xx, yy and zz components of the Green tensor, which are given by
[
43
]

\displaystyle G_{xx}^{R}({\mathbf{r}}_{0},{\mathbf{r}}_{0};i\xi)=G_{yy}^{R}({\mathbf{r}}_{0},{\mathbf{r}}_{0};i\xi)

(17)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2c^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dQ\,\frac{Q\,e^{-2\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}z_{0}}}{\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}-\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}{\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}-\Big(1+Q^{2}\frac{c^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\varepsilon(i\xi)\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}-\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}{\varepsilon(i\xi)\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}\bigg)

and

\displaystyle G_{zz}^{R}({\mathbf{r}}_{0},{\mathbf{r}}_{0};i\xi)

(18)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dQ\,\frac{Q^{3}e^{-2\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}z_{0}}}{\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\varepsilon(i\xi)\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}-\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}{\varepsilon(i\xi)\sqrt{(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon(i\xi)(\xi/c)^{2}+Q^{2}}}.

For a slab made of vitreous
{\rm SiO_{2}}
, we can use the Ninham-Parsegian representation for the dielectric function
[
44
]
:

\varepsilon(i\xi)=1+\frac{C_{\rm{UV}}\,\omega_{\rm{UV}}^{2}}{\xi^{2}+\omega_{\rm{UV}}^{2}}+\frac{C_{\rm{IR}}\,\omega_{\rm{IR}}^{2}}{\xi^{2}+\omega_{\rm{IR}}^{2}},

(19)

where
C_{\rm{UV}}=1.098
,
C_{\rm{IR}}=1.703
,
\omega_{\rm{UV}}=2.033\times 10^{16}\,{\rm rad/s}
, and
\omega_{\rm{IR}}=1.88\times 10^{14}\,{\rm rad/s}
.

III
Disorder-induced energy shift

III.1
A single slab with surface charge disorder

In this section and the following one, we study the case of an atom near a single slab.
We first consider the case where the charge disorder only resides on the surface.
Using Eq. (
6
), we find that the surface charge disorder-induced contribution to the energy correction described by Eq. (
13
) is given by

\delta E_{n}^{(S)}=-\frac{2\,\alpha_{ab}^{n}(0)}{(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}}\!\int\!d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}\!\int\!d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\,\frac{\overline{\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{S}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}\,n_{a}({\mathbf{r}})n_{b}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}{|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}_{0}|^{2}|{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{r}}_{0}|^{2}}.

Using Equation (

\delta E_{n}^{(S)}=-\frac{2\,\alpha_{ab}^{n}(0)}{(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}}\!\int\!d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}\frac{\sigma_{S}^{2}n_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})n_{b}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})}{(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{2}}.

(21)

In polar coordinates,
\int\!d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\int_{0}^{\infty}dr_{\perp}\,r_{\perp}
.
To make progress, we need an expression for
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
in terms of
r_{\perp}
,
z_{0}
and
\varphi
(which is the azimuthal angle in the xy-plane). In spherical coordinates,
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
is given by

\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})=-\sin\theta\cos\varphi\,{\bf e}_{x}-\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,{\bf e}_{y}+\cos\theta\,{\bf e}_{z},

(22)

where the angles
\theta
and
\varphi
are as shown in Fig.
1
, and
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
is the unit vector directed from a given local charge disorder at
{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}
on the surface to the atom.
Since

\cos\theta=z_{0}/(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{1/2}

and

\sin\theta=r_{\perp}/(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{1/2}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{x}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\,{\bf e}_{x}+n_{y}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\,{\bf e}_{y}+n_{z}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\,{\bf e}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{\perp}\cos\varphi}{(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{1/2}}\,{\bf e}_{x}-\frac{r_{\perp}\sin\varphi}{(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{1/2}}\,{\bf e}_{y}+\frac{z_{0}}{(r_{\perp}^{2}+z_{0}^{2})^{1/2}}\,{\bf e}_{z}.

After doing the angular and radial integrations, we obtain

\delta E_{n}^{(S)}=-\frac{\pi\sigma_{S}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))}{2(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}z_{0}^{2}}.

(24)

The minus sign indicates that the presence of the charge disorder leads to a negative shift of the atomic energy levels. The corresponding force exerted on the atomic state is thus attractive. As
\delta E_{n}^{(S)}
depends on the variance of the charge disorder density, a larger charge disorder density variance will result in a stronger attractive force. Finally,
\delta E_{n}^{(S)}
decays as
1/z_{0}^{2}
, which is a slower decay compared to the nonresonant Casimir-Polder shift.
For an isotropic atomic state, Eq. (
24
) becomes

\delta E_{0}^{(S)}=-\frac{2\pi\alpha_{0}\sigma_{S}^{2}}{(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}z_{0}^{2}}.

(25)

For a vitreous
{\rm{SiO_{2}}}
slab surface, the static dielectric permittivity is given by
\varepsilon(0)=3.801
.

III.2
A single slab with bulk charge disorder

Next, we derive the energy correction induced by bulk charge disorder. If we denote the
i
th impurity charge in the dielectric bulk by the symbol
\delta Q_{i}
, and let
{\mathbf{r}}_{i}
denote its position, the bulk charge disorder density is given by

\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i}\delta Q_{i}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}_{i}).

(26)

By defining
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}})=({\mathbf{r}}_{0}-{\mathbf{r}})/|{\mathbf{r}}_{0}-{\mathbf{r}}|
and
r_{\perp}^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
, the bulk charge disorder-induced field is given by

{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{B}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})=\int\!d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}\,\frac{2\,\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}})}{\varepsilon(0)+1}\frac{\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}})}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-z)^{2}}.

(27)

Using the above equation, we find that the bulk charge disorder-induced contribution to the energy correction described by Eq. (
13
) is given by

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{(B)}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{2\,\alpha_{ab}^{n}(0)}{(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}}\!\int\!d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}\!\int\!d^{3}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\,\frac{\overline{\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{B}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-z)^{2}}

(28)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{n_{a}({\mathbf{r}})n_{b}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}{(r_{\perp}^{\prime 2}+(z_{0}-z^{\prime})^{2}}.

After doing the disorder average using Eq. (
3
), and performing the volume integrations, we obtain

\delta E_{n}^{(B)}=-\frac{\pi\sigma_{B}^{2}\big(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\,\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0)\big)}{2(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}z_{0}}.

(29)

For an isotropic atomic state, Eq. (
29
) becomes

\delta E_{0}^{(B)}=-\frac{2\pi\alpha_{0}\sigma_{B}^{2}}{(\varepsilon(0)+1)^{2}z_{0}}.

(30)

We consider the case where both slabs contain quenched bulk charge disorder, so the atom would experience a superposition of local electric fields generated by the totality of the bulk charge disorder.
To find the electric field generated by a given local charge disorder, let us first consider the electric potential generated at an arbitrary point
{\mathbf{r}}
by a charge impurity
\delta q

{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\,{\bf e}_{z}

\displaystyle\varphi({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},z)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi_{1}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dk\,J_{0}(kr_{\perp})C_{k}e^{-kz}\quad\mbox{($z\geq d$)},\\

J_{0}
denotes a Bessel function of the first kind. The second term in the third line of the right-hand side is a rewrite of the Coulomb interaction,
(\delta q/\varepsilon_{2})/(r_{\perp}^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2})^{1/2}=(\delta q/\varepsilon_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,J_{0}(kr_{\perp})\exp(-k|z-z^{\prime}|)
.
The electric potential has to satisfy the boundary conditions
\varphi_{M}=\varphi_{2}
and
\partial\varphi_{M}/\partial z=\varepsilon_{2}\partial\varphi_{2}/\partial z
at
z=0
, and
\varphi_{M}=\varphi_{1}
and
\partial\varphi_{M}/\partial z=\varepsilon_{1}\partial\varphi_{1}/\partial z
at
z=d
. These boundary conditions give rise to four equations,
viz.

\displaystyle A_{k}+B_{k}-D_{k}=(\delta q/\varepsilon_{2})e^{-k|z^{\prime}|},

\displaystyle A_{k}-B_{k}-\varepsilon_{2}D_{k}=-\delta q\,e^{-k|z^{\prime}|},

\displaystyle A_{k}e^{kd}+B_{k}e^{-kd}-C_{k}e^{-kd}=0,

\displaystyle A_{k}e^{kd}-B_{k}e^{-kd}+\varepsilon_{1}C_{k}e^{-kd}=0.

(38)

We solve the equations for
A_{k}
,
B_{k}
,
C_{k}
and
D_{k}
:

\displaystyle A_{k}=-\frac{2\delta q\,e^{-k(|z^{\prime}|+2d)}}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\frac{\Delta_{1}}{1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd}},

\displaystyle B_{k}=\frac{2\delta q\,e^{-k|z^{\prime}|}}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\frac{1}{1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd}},

\displaystyle C_{k}=\frac{4\delta q\,e^{-k|z^{\prime}|}}{(\varepsilon_{1}+1)(\varepsilon_{2}+1)}\frac{1}{1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd}},

\displaystyle D_{k}=\frac{\delta q\,e^{-k|z^{\prime}|}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}e^{-2kd}}{1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd}},

(39)

where the dielectric discontinuity factors are given by

\Delta_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}-1}{\varepsilon_{1}+1},\quad\Delta_{2}=\frac{\varepsilon_{2}-1}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}.

(40)

The denominator factor
(1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd})^{-1}
accounts for the contribution of the (infinitely many) image charges.
To see this, let us expand the denominator factor in powers of
\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}e^{-2kd}
:

\displaystyle A_{k}e^{kz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\delta q}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{1}^{J+1}\Delta_{2}^{J}e^{k(z+z^{\prime}-2(J+1)d)},

\displaystyle B_{k}e^{-kz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\delta q}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J}e^{-k(z-z^{\prime}+2Jd)}.

(41)

We can thus express
\varphi_{M}
in Equation (
31
) as

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},z)

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\delta q}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(-\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J+1}\Delta_{2}^{J}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z+z^{\prime}-2(J+1)d)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z-z^{\prime}+2Jd)^{2}}}\Bigg).

From the above expression, it follows that the potential at a position
{\mathbf{r}}
inside the gap generated by an impurity charge

located at

in the lower slab is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}({\mathbf{r}})=\frac{2\delta q_{i}}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\Bigg(-\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J+1}\Delta_{2}^{J}}{\sqrt{(x-x_{i})^{2}+(y-y_{i})^{2}+(z+z_{i}-2(J+1)d)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J}}{\sqrt{(x-x_{i})^{2}+(y-y_{i})^{2}+(z-z_{i}+2Jd)^{2}}}\Bigg).

(43)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

i.e.
,

, and the variance of the local charge disorder per unit volume is
\sigma_{B1}^{2}
in slab 1 and

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}=\sigma_{B2}^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}).

{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}
, we obtain (see Appendix

for details)

\delta E_{n}^{(BB)}=\delta E_{n,1}^{(BB)}+\delta E_{n,2}^{(BB)},

\displaystyle\delta E_{n,1}^{(BB)}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi\sigma_{B1}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))}{2(\varepsilon_{1}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{d}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,1-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{2d-z_{0}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2,2-\frac{z_{0}}{d};3-\frac{z_{0}}{d};\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{d}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,1+\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}^{3}}{2d+z_{0}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2,2+\frac{z_{0}}{d};3+\frac{z_{0}}{d};\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi\sigma_{B1}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)-2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))\Delta_{2}}{(\varepsilon_{1}+1)^{2}(1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2})d}

and

\displaystyle\delta E_{n,2}^{(BB)}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi\sigma_{B2}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))}{2(\varepsilon_{2}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{d}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{d+z_{0}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2,1+\frac{z_{0}}{d};2+\frac{z_{0}}{d};\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{2}}{d}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,2-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{3}\Delta_{2}}{3d-z_{0}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(2,3-\frac{z_{0}}{d};4-\frac{z_{0}}{d};\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\right)\Bigg)

Here,
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function and
\Phi(z,s,a)
is the Lerch transcendent, defined by
\Phi(z,s,a)\equiv\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}z^{J}/(a+J)^{s}

[
46
]
. From the definition of the Lerch transcendent, we see that
\Phi(z,s,a)
diverges as
a\to 0
. This implies that as
z_{0}/d\to 0
, the prefactor
\frac{1}{d}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)
in the equation above diverges and becomes dominant over the other terms.
As we show in App.
D
, as

, we recover the result for an atom near a single charge-disordered slab (Eq. (
29
)).

III.4
Two coplanar slabs with surface charge disorder

The boundary value problem for surface charge disorder for two coplanar slabs is analogous to the one considered previously, with the modifications that the impurity charges
\delta q_{i}
are located just below the surface of slab 2, and the impurity charges
\delta Q_{i}
are located just above the surface of slab 1.
The charge disorder densities
\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}})
and
\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}})
for slabs 1 and 2 are now given by

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i}Q_{i}\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{i\perp})\delta(z-d+),

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i}q_{i}\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{i\perp})\delta(z-0-).

(57)

Correspondingly, the fields
{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})
and
{\bm{\mathcal{F}}}_{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})
at the atom’s position
{\mathbf{r}}_{0}=z_{0}\,{\bf e}_{z}
generated respectively by the images of surface impurity charges on slab 2 and the images of surface impurity charges on slab 1 are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})=\frac{2}{\varepsilon_{2}+1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\!\!\!\!dz\int\!d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}})

(58a)

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J}\,\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}+2Jd)^{2}}-\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J+1}\Delta_{2}^{J}\,\mathbf{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-2(J+1)d)^{2}}\Bigg),

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathcal{F}}}_{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})=\frac{2}{\varepsilon_{1}+1}\int_{d}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dz\int\!d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}})

(58b)

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J}\,\mathbf{N}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-(2J+1)d)^{2}}-\frac{\Delta_{1}^{J}\Delta_{2}^{J+1}\,\mathbf{M}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})}{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}+(2J+1)d)^{2}}\Bigg).

Here, the direction vectors
\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
,
\mathbf{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
,
\mathbf{N}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
and
\mathbf{M}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-x\,{\bf e}_{x}-y\,{\bf e}_{y}+(z_{0}+2Jd)\,{\bf e}_{z}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}+2Jd)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-x\,{\bf e}_{x}-y\,{\bf e}_{y}+(z_{0}-2(J+1)d)\,{\bf e}_{z}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-2(J+1)d)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-x\,{\bf e}_{x}-y\,{\bf e}_{y}+(z_{0}-(2J+1)d)\,{\bf e}_{z}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}-(2J+1)d)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-x\,{\bf e}_{x}-y\,{\bf e}_{y}+(z_{0}+(2J+1)d)\,{\bf e}_{z}}{\sqrt{r_{\perp}^{2}+(z_{0}+(2J+1)d)^{2}}}.

(59)

As before, we assume the surface charge disorder is Gaussian distributed with zero mean,
i.e.
,
\overline{\delta\rho_{1}}=\overline{\delta\rho_{2}}=0
, and the variance of the local surface charge disorder per unit area is
\sigma_{S1}^{2}
in slab 1 and
\sigma_{S2}^{2}
in slab 2, with the disorder being spatially uncorrelated:

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}=\sigma_{S1}^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})\delta(z-d+)\delta(z^{\prime}-d+),

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}=\sigma_{S2}^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}-{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})\delta(z-0-)\delta(z^{\prime}-0-),

\displaystyle\overline{\delta\rho_{1}({\mathbf{r}})\delta\rho_{2}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}=0.

(60)

The surface charge disorder-averaged contribution to the energy correction described by Eq. (
13
) is given by

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{(SS)}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})

(61)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{ab}^{n}(0)\overline{(\mathcal{E}_{Sa}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})+\mathcal{F}_{Sa}({\mathbf{r}}_{0}))(\mathcal{E}_{Sb}({\mathbf{r}}_{0})+\mathcal{F}_{Sb}({\mathbf{r}}_{0}))}.

After performing the disorder average and the integration over
{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}
, we obtain (see Appendix
C
for details)

\displaystyle\delta E_{n}^{(SS)}=-\frac{\pi\sigma_{S1}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))}{2(\varepsilon_{1}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,1-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)+\frac{z_{0}}{d^{3}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},2,1-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}}{d^{2}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,1+\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)-\frac{\Delta_{2}^{2}z_{0}}{d^{3}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},2,1+\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi\sigma_{S2}^{2}(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)+2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))}{2(\varepsilon_{2}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)+\frac{d-z_{0}}{d^{3}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},2,\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{2}}{d^{2}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},1,2-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{2}(z_{0}-d)}{d^{3}}\Phi\left(\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2},2,2-\frac{z_{0}}{d}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle-\pi(\alpha_{xx}^{n}(0)+\alpha_{yy}^{n}(0)-2\alpha_{zz}^{n}(0))\ln(1-\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{\sigma_{S1}^{2}}{\Delta_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}+1)^{2}}+\frac{\sigma_{S2}^{2}}{\Delta_{2}(\varepsilon_{2}+1)^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{d^{2}}.

(62)

In App.

, we show that the above result leads to Eq. (
24
) as

, which is the surface charge disorder-induced energy level shift in an atom near a single slab.

V
Summary and Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the atom-surface interaction behavior induced by surface and bulk charge disorders present in the dielectric material, for the case where the atom-surface separation is larger than the charge disorder correlation length. In particular, we have studied the disorder-induced interaction behavior of an atom next to a single planar semi-infinite dielectric slab as well as the disorder-induced energy level shifts for an atom confined between two coplanar and semi-infinite slabs. As an example, we take for the atom a metastable
{{\rm He^{\ast}}}
atom, and we assume the slab material is vitreous
{{\rm SiO_{2}}}
.
For the case where only a single slab is present, we have found that the surface (bulk) charge disorder leads to a downward energy shift which decays as
z_{0}^{-2}
(
z_{0}^{-1}
) (where
z_{0}
is the perpendicular distance of the atom from the surface), with a corresponding force that is attractive and decays as
z_{0}^{-3}
(
z_{0}^{-2}
). We also found that the disorder-induced force will dominate the nonresonant Casimir-Polder force at sufficiently large distances, and the distance at which this occurs is larger (smaller) for smaller (larger) values of the charge disorder variance per unit area. For the particular case where the bulk charge disorder variance per unit volume is
1.168\times 10^{-5}\,{\rm esu^{2}/cm^{3}}
and the surface charge disorder variance per unit area is
\sigma_{S}^{2}=3.146\times 10^{-10}\,{\rm esu^{2}/cm^{2}}
, such a crossover occurs at a separation of the order of
10^{-4}
cm.

Lastly, we have studied the behavior of the charge disorder-induced energy shift for an atom confined in the vacuum gap between two coplanar and semi-infinite slabs made of the same dielectric material. For the case of surface disorder as well as bulk disorder, we found that if the charge disorder variance is equal on both slabs, the atom experiences a net zero disorder-induced force when it is positioned right in the middle of the gap, whereas if the charge disorder variance on one of the slabs is smaller than the other one, the position of the net zero charge disorder-induced force (which is an unstable equilibrium position) shifts towards the slab with the smaller charge disorder variance.

Although we did not discuss in this paper, it is possible to extend our investigations to the case of charge disorder with a nonzero correlation length, which would be relevant for atom-surface separations of the order of a few nanometers.
One can also use the same formalism to study the charge disorder-induced energy level shifts in an excited and anisotropically polarized atom near a dielectric slab.
One can also use a similar approach to study the case of a Rydberg atom between two dielectric slabs with quenched monopolar charge disorder. For this case, one would need to account for multipolar contributions higher than the dipolar one.

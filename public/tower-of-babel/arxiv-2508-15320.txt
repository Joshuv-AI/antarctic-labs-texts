Reduced basis solvers for unfitted methods on parameterized domains
Nicholas Mueller †, Santiago Badia †, Yiran Zhao ¶

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a unified framework for reduced basis approximations of parametrized partial differential equations defined on parameter-dependent domains. Our approach combines unfitted finite element methods with both classical and tensor-based reduced basis techniques – particularly the tensor-train reduced basis method – to enable efficient and accurate model reduction on general geometries. To address the challenge of reconciling geometric variability with fixed-dimensional snapshot representations, we adopt a deformation-based strategy that maps a reference configuration to each parameterized domain. Furthermore, we introduce a localization procedure to construct dictionaries of reduced subspaces and hyper-reduction approximations, which are obtained via matrix discrete empirical interpolation in our work. We extend the proposed framework to saddle-point problems by adapting the supremizer enrichment strategy to unfitted methods and deformed configurations, demonstrating that the supremizer operator can be defined on the reference configuration without loss of stability. Numerical experiments on two- and three-dimensional problems – including Poisson, linear elasticity, incompressible Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations – demonstrate the flexibility, accuracy and efficiency of the proposed methodology.



1.
Introduction

Projection-based
rom
are advanced numerical techniques designed to approximate parametric
hf
(
hf
) models, which typically involve finely resolved spatio-temporal discretizations of
pde
. These methods aim to capture the
hf

hf
equations onto this subspace is performed. This stage often includes the hyper-reduction of nonaffinely parameterized
hf

The
rb
(
rb
) method is one of the most prominent projection-based

, with a broad range of applications, including unsteady
[
57
,
39
]
, nonlinear
[
53
,
18
]
, and saddle-point problems
[
7
,
42
]
. It constructs the projection subspace using a dictionary of high-fidelity solutions – commonly referred to as snapshots. For reducible problems
[
53
]
, i.e., those whose parameter-to-solution maps exhibit low Kolmogorov

[
59
]
, the reduced space can be of much smaller dimension than the
hf
space, while retaining high accuracy. Subspace extraction techniques include direct compression methods
[
32
,
33
]
and greedy algorithms
[
52
,
51
]
. Direct approaches benefit from a priori estimates, such as those derived from the Schmidt-Eckart-Young Theorem
[

,
37

, which underpins the widely used
tpod
(
tpod
) method
[
60

. Greedy procedures, in contrast, iteratively build the reduced basis using a posteriori error estimators, though often at a higher computational cost. More recently, tensor-based approaches such as the
ttrb
(
ttrb
) method
[
36
,
35
,
40

have been proposed, in which snapshots are treated as multidimensional arrays and compressed along Cartesian axes using

(

) or

(
ttcross
)
[

,
24
,
45
,
10
,
17
,
20

A multi-purpose
rom

ttrb
on arbitrarily parameterized domains can be effectively built upon unfitted (or embedded-boundary)
fe
(
fe
) methods
[
26
,
13
,
22
,
9

. These techniques allow for the discretization of

Despite the advantages offered by unfitted
fe

rom

The first major difficulty lies in reconciling geometric variability with the requirement that all snapshots reside in a fixed-dimensional vector space. Two primary strategies have been developed to address this issue. The first, known as the extension-based approach, involves computing each snapshot on its parameterized domain and subsequently extending it onto the background mesh. This method is widely adopted in embedded-domain frameworks due to its conceptual simplicity and compatibility with structured discretizations
[
44
,
6
,
29
,
62
,
31
,
30
,
28
]
. However, it often requires complex remeshing and may introduce artifacts or degrade accuracy due to the extension process itself
[
28
]
.

A more accurate alternative – though geometrically less flexible
[
16
]
– is the deformation-based approach. In this setting, a fixed reference configuration is employed, and each parameterized domain is mapped onto it through a smooth deformation map. The weak form of the
pde
is then integrated over the reference mesh via pull-back operations. This avoids remeshing and artificial extensions altogether, preserving snapshot fidelity. However, it requires solving additional high-fidelity systems to compute the deformation maps. Common choices include harmonic
[
5
]
, biharmonic
[
27
]
, and elastic deformations
[
56
,
55
,
58
]
, many of which stem from
ale
(
ale
) formulations in fluid-structure interaction
[
61
,
8
]
. In this work, we adopt the deformation-based approach as our default strategy; for
ttrb
approximations, we supplement it with an extension-based procedure to ensure that snapshot values are defined at every index of the background Cartesian grid.

A second major challenge arises from the limited reducibility of problems posed on parameterized domains. To mitigate this, we employ a localization strategy that enhances the compressibility of the reduced model. The use of local reduced subspaces has recently gained attention as an effective means to achieve meaningful dimensionality reduction
[
1
,
48
,
47
]
. Among the few works incorporating this idea within unfitted discretization frameworks,
[
15
]
constructs local subspaces for both projection and hyper-reduction. Their approach relies on traditional
tpod
-based bases and local hyper-reduction via
rbf
(
rbf
) interpolation
[
50
]
. By contrast, we explore both
tpod
and
ttrb
subspaces in conjunction with
mdeim
(
mdeim
)-based hyper-reduction. While the latter is more computationally expensive, we find it substantially more accurate than
rbf
-based methods, making it better suited for the high-fidelity requirements of our applications. Another key distinction from
[
15
]
lies in our reliance on deformation-based rather than extension-based snapshot representations, which, in our experience, results in improved compressibility and overall reducibility of the problem.

In summary, the main contributions of the work are the following.

•

We develop an interface that combines an unfitted
fe
method with deformation mappings from a fixed reference configuration to physical domains, enabling the generation of
hf

rb
methods – namely
ttrb
– not only on general geometries, but also on parameterized ones. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first such attempt in the literature.

•

We implement a localized strategy to construct dictionaries of reduced subspaces and corresponding hyper-reduction approximations for the residuals and Jacobians of parameterized problems. While this idea was previously explored in
[
15
]
, our framework employs
mdeim
-based hyper-reduction, which we find to be significantly more accurate than their
rbf
-based approach.

•

We extend the applicability of unfitted
rom

[
7
]
for ensuring the well-posedness of reduced saddle-point systems can be adapted to our problems by computing the supremizer operator on the reference configuration, instead of on the deformed one. This significantly improves the feasibility and efficiency of the enrichment procedure.

2.
Full order model

In this section, we present the
hf
framework used for solving parameterized
pde
. We first define the notion of mesh deformation maps for efficient handling of shape parameters. We then present the aggregated cell method
[
4
]
, which serves as the unfitted scheme employed in this work. Next, we discuss the extension of nodal values from the physical domain to the background grid. Finally, we define the induced norms on the previously introduced
fe
spaces.

2.1.
Mesh deformation techniques

We introduce a
reference
spatial domain
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d
denotes the spatial dimension. The reference boundary
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
is partitioned into two portion with prescribed non-zero
\widetilde{\Gamma}
and zero displacement
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma}
, resp. On
\widetilde{\Omega}
(resp.
\widetilde{\Gamma}
), we define a conforming, quasi-uniform mesh denoted by
\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}
(resp.
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h}
), with mesh size
h
. A boundary displacement
\underline{\psi}_{\Gamma}:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h}\to\Gamma_{h}
is prescribed on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. The goal of mesh deformation techniques is to construct a deformation map
\underline{\psi}:\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}\to\Omega_{h}
as a diffeomorphism that extends
\underline{\psi}_{\Gamma}
to the bulk. Once
\underline{\psi}
is computed, the deformed domain is defined as

\Omega=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bm{x}=\widetilde{\bm{x}}+\underline{\psi}(\widetilde{\bm{x}}),\ \widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}\}.

In this work, we employ elastic deformations, where
\underline{\psi}
solves the linear elasticity problem:

(1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\psi}))&=\underline{0}&&\text{in }\widetilde{\Omega},\\
\underline{\psi}&=\underline{\psi}_{\Gamma}&&\text{on }\widetilde{\Gamma},\\
\underline{\psi}&=\underline{0}&&\text{on }\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Gamma},\end{aligned}\right.

with stress tensor

(2)

\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\psi})=2\gamma\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}(\underline{\psi})+\lambda\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\underline{\psi})\underline{\underline{I}}_{d},

where
\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}
is the symmetric gradient,
\underline{\underline{I}}_{d}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix, and
\lambda
,
\gamma
are the Lamé coefficients, expressed in terms of the Young modulus
E
and the Poisson ratio
\nu
as:

(3)

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{E\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)},\qquad\gamma=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}.

To obtain the deformed mesh, we discretize (
1
) using the
fe
method and solve for the discrete displacement
\underline{\psi}_{h}
. The deformed mesh is then given by

\Omega_{h}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bm{x}=\widetilde{\bm{x}}+\underline{\psi}_{h}(\widetilde{\bm{x}}),\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}\}.

An example of a deformed mesh is shown in Fig.
1
.

Mesh deformation maps are widely used in fluid-structure interaction problems based on
ale
formulations
[
61
,
8
]
. They are also valuable in problems involving shape parameters, where one aims to define families of parameterized domains. For instance, consider a
pde
defined on the unit square with a circular hole whose center moves along the diagonal and whose radius varies. The domain is parameterized as:

(4)

\Omega(\bm{\mu})\doteq[0,1]^{2}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{(\mu_{1},\mu_{1})}(\mu_{2}),\quad\bm{\mu}\doteq(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

where
\mathcal{B}_{\bm{c}}(R)
denotes a ball of radius
R
centered at
\bm{c}
. Solving a
pde
on
\Omega(\bm{\mu})
for multiple values of
\bm{\mu}
can be costly in standard
fe
codes due to repeated remeshing and basis construction. This difficulty is alleviated by mesh deformation techniques
[
54
]
, which proceed as follows:

•

Choose a reference parameter
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
corresponding to the reference domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
– ideally one that minimizes mesh skewness (e.g., a hole centered in the square).

•

Solve (
1
) with boundary displacement

(5)

\underline{\psi}_{\Gamma}(\bm{\mu}):(x,y)\mapsto\left(\mu_{1}-x+\mu_{2}(x-\widetilde{\mu}_{1})/\widetilde{\mu}_{2},\ \mu_{1}-y+\mu_{2}(y-\widetilde{\mu}_{1})/\widetilde{\mu}_{2}\right)

applied on
\widetilde{\Gamma}\doteq\partial\mathcal{B}_{(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\widetilde{\mu}_{1})}(\widetilde{\mu}_{2})
, yielding a deformation map
\underline{\psi}(\bm{\mu})
.

•

Recast the original
pde
– defined on
\Omega(\bm{\mu})
– onto the reference configuration
\widetilde{\Omega}
.

\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}
\Omega_{h}(\bm{\mu})

.

To achieve the last step, gradients, integral measures, and boundary normals on the deformed configuration must be pulled back to the reference configuration. Denoting the Jacobian of the deformation map by
\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu})\doteq\boldsymbol{\nabla}\underline{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the following formulas hold:

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{\nabla}&\doteq\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}},\quad&&\int_{\Omega(\bm{\mu})}d\Omega(\bm{\mu})&&\doteq\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\mathrm{det}(\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu}))d\widetilde{\Omega},\\
\underline{n}(\bm{\mu})&\doteq\frac{\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\underline{n}}}{\|\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\underline{n}}\|_{2}},\quad&&\int_{\Gamma(\bm{\mu})}d\Gamma(\bm{\mu})&&\doteq\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\|\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\underline{n}}\|_{2}\mathrm{det}(\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu}))d\widetilde{\Gamma},\end{aligned}

where:

•

\bm{\nabla}
(resp.
\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}}
) is the gradient operator on the deformed (resp. reference) domain;

•

d\Omega(\bm{\mu})
(resp.
d\widetilde{\Omega}
) is the volume measure on the deformed (resp. reference) domain;

•

d\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
(resp.
d\widetilde{\Gamma}
) is the surface measure on the deformed (resp. reference) boundary;

•

\underline{n}(\bm{\mu})
(resp.
\widetilde{\underline{n}}
) is the outward unit normal vector on the deformed (resp. reference) boundary.

Finally, all physical quantities (e.g., boundary conditions, coefficients) must be expressed in the reference configuration via composition with the inverse mapping
\underline{\psi}(\bm{\mu})^{-1}
. The relations in (
6
) enable the definition of weak forms and finite element spaces on the reference configuration, and can thus be reused for all parameters. Throughout this work, unless otherwise stated, all expressions involving integration over deformed configurations are understood via the transformations in (
6
).

2.2.
Aggregated cell method

We consider a space of shape parameters
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
that determines the deformed domain
\Omega(\bm{\mu})
. We also define a non-parametric background domain
\widehat{\Omega}
, which serves as a bounding box for

, with the additional requirement that it be quadrangular. The cells of the partition can be classified as follows:

•

Cells that are fully contained within
\widetilde{\Omega}

internal
.

•

Cells that are fully contained within
\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}

external

The remaining are

In this section, we assume that our problem is stated in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and we use grad-conforming
fe
spaces with full
\mathcal{C}^{0}
continuity, whose basis functions can be constructed using Lagrangian bases (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). With the classification of cells proposed above, we can now define the
fe
spaces used in the aggregated cell method. First, we define a standard background
fe
space over the background grid

(7)

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\doteq\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)\ :\ v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p}(K)\ \forall\ K\in\widehat{\Omega}_{h}\},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{p}(K)
the space of polynomials of degree at most
p
in each coordinate. Similarly, we can define the interior and active
fe
spaces
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{IN}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{ACT}}
over the union of interior cells and the union of active cells, resp. We consider the same order
p\geq 1
for all these spaces, and we denote the number of
dof
for background, internal and active spaces by
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
,
\mathcal{N}_{h}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\texttt{ACT}}_{h}
, resp.

Clearly, the internal and the background spaces are not suitable choices for the
fe
approximation over
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Unfitted methods that simply employ
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{ACT}}
as approximation space are affected by severe ill-conditioning due to the presence of small cut cells
[
19
]
. There are two main ways to address this issue: either by using stabilization techniques, e.g., in the form of weakly consistent ghost penalty stabilization
[
12
]
, or by using a more robust
fe
space, e.g., in the form of an aggregated
fe
space
[
4
]
. In this work, we focus on the latter approach, though the
rom
we propose can be applied to other unfitted methods as well.

The aggregated
fe
method presented in
[
4
]
addresses this issue by assigning a set of judiciously constructed linear constraints to the aggregated
dof
associated with cut cells (see Fig.
2b
). The
fe
interpolation on the constrained
dof
is uniquely determined by the internal values and the imposed constraints. We denote this space – also called the aggregated
fe
space – by
\mathcal{V}_{h}
. By construction, the following inclusions hold:

\mathcal{V}^{\texttt{IN}}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\texttt{ACT}}_{h}.

In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{h}
is entirely characterized by the internal space, and an extension operator to the cut cells, whose expression is determined by the aforementioned constraints. We denote this so-called discrete extension operator by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, and define the aggregated space as

(8)

\mathcal{V}_{h}\doteq\mathcal{E}_{h}\circ\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\texttt{IN}},

The dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{h}
is therefore
\mathcal{N}_{h}
– that is, the number of internal
dof
. The formulation of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
along with further details can be found in
[
4
]
.

2.3.
Full order model

Let us consider the following parameterized
pde
on
\Omega(\bm{\mu})
: find
u(\bm{\mu})\in H^{1}(\Omega(\bm{\mu}))
such that

(9)

\mathcal{A}(u(\bm{\mu}))=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega(\bm{\mu}),\quad u(\bm{\mu})=u_{D}(\bm{\mu})\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu}),\quad\bm{\nabla}u(\bm{\mu})\cdot\underline{n}(\bm{\mu})=u_{N}(\bm{\mu})\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu}).

Without loss of generality, we assume that the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu})
coincides with the deformed boundary
\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
, whereas the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})\equiv\partial\Omega(\bm{\mu})\setminus\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
. Their respective data satisfy
u_{D}(\bm{\mu})\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu}))
and
u_{N}(\bm{\mu})\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu}))
.
Moreover, we assume for simplicity that the differential operator
\mathcal{A}\in H^{-1}(\Omega(\bm{\mu}))
is linear. Then, the weak formulation associated with (
9
) on the aggregated
fe
space (
8
) reads as: find
u_{h}(\bm{\mu})\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
such that

(10)

\widebar{a}(u_{h}(\bm{\mu}),v_{h})=\widebar{l}(v_{h})\quad\forall\ v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}.

The forms in (
10
) are defined as:

\begin{split}\widebar{a}(u_{h},v_{h})&=a(u_{h},v_{h})+\int_{\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu})}\tau_{h}u_{h}v_{h}-(\bm{\nabla}u_{h}\cdot\underline{n}(\bm{\mu}))v_{h}-(\bm{\nabla}v_{h}\cdot\underline{n}(\bm{\mu}))u_{h},\\
\widebar{l}(v_{h})&=l(v_{h})+\int_{\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu})}\tau_{h}u_{D}(\bm{\mu})v_{h}-(\bm{\nabla}u_{D}(\bm{\mu})\cdot\underline{n}(\bm{\mu}))v_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})}u_{N}(\bm{\mu})v_{h}.\end{split}

As is customary in unfitted
fe
applications, the Dirichlet condition is enforced weakly via the (symmetric) Nitsche penalty method. The quantity
\tau_{h}>0
denotes the (global) Nitsche penalty constant, and the forms
a
and
l
arise from testing the differential operator
\mathcal{A}
against the test function
v_{h}
. The analysis in
[
4
]
shows that
\tau_{h}
is independent of the minimum cut cell volume, a major difference with respect to standard unfitted
fe
methods. In particular,
\tau_{h}=\eta h^{-1}
for a sufficiently large (mesh-independent) positive constant
\eta

[
2
]
, similarly to standard body-fitted
fe
schemes. Although
\tau_{h}
should be defined with respect to the deformed mesh size, in practice it can be obtained from the penalty constant in the reference configuration, scaled by a factor accounting for the deformation map. This approach is reasonable here, as we do not operate in regimes involving large deformations. The scaling can be incorporated into an adjusted value of
\eta
that ensures coercivity for any
\bm{\mu}
. Henceforth, we make no distinction between penalty constants in reference or in deformed configurations. Performing numerical integration on (
10
) yields the so-called
fom
equation

(11)

\bm{A}(\bm{\mu})\bm{u}(\bm{\mu})=\bm{l}(\bm{\mu}),

where
\bm{A}(\bm{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}\times\mathcal{N}_{h}}
, and
\bm{u}(\bm{\mu}),\ \bm{l}(\bm{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}}
.

2.4.
Nodal value extension

As detailed in Section
3
, the
ttrb
method compresses the
fom
dimensions along each Cartesian direction. This requires constructing solution snapshots that can be assembled into a single
d
-dimensional tensor, which in turn demands solution values to be defined over the entire background domain. To achieve this, we apply a harmonic extension of the high-fidelity solution from the physical domain to the background mesh. This approach, commonly adopted in reduced-order modeling for unfitted discretizations
[
15
,
6
,
28
]
, enables the construction of global basis vectors on a structured grid. We emphasize that nodal extension is used exclusively for implementing the
ttrb
method, as the mesh deformation framework already suffices to support classical
rb
techniques. In this work, we consider a discrete harmonic extension
\mathcal{H}_{h}:\mathcal{V}_{h}\to\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
that takes the solution in the active mesh and returns an extended function on the whole background mesh
\widehat{u}_{h}(\bm{\mu})\doteq\mathcal{H}_{h}\circ u_{h}(\bm{\mu})
. In particular, the extension of the solution is defined as

(12)

\widehat{u}_{h}(\bm{\mu})\doteq u_{h}(\bm{\mu})\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega(\bm{\mu})}+u^{\texttt{OUT}}_{h}(\bm{\mu})\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega(\bm{\mu})}\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}),

where
u^{\texttt{OUT}}_{h}(\bm{\mu})\in\mathcal{V}^{\texttt{OUT}}_{h}
is computed as

(13)

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega(\bm{\mu})}\bm{\nabla}\left(u_{h}(\bm{\mu})+u_{h}^{\texttt{OUT}}(\bm{\mu})\right)\bm{\nabla}v_{h}^{\texttt{OUT}}=0\quad\forall\ v_{h}^{\texttt{OUT}}\in\mathcal{V}^{\texttt{OUT}}_{h},\quad u_{h}^{\texttt{OUT}}(\bm{\mu})=u_{h}(\bm{\mu})\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega(\bm{\mu}).

Here,
\mathcal{V}^{\texttt{OUT}}_{h}
denotes the restriction of the background
fe
space to external cells in the background mesh. In algebraic terms, solving (
11
)-(
13
) yields an
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
-dimensional vector of nodal values
\widehat{\bm{u}}(\bm{\mu})
, which can be used to implement the
ttrb
routines, given its
\bm{\mu}
-independent size.

2.5.
Induced norms

We conclude this section by defining the norms induced on the previously introduced
fe
spaces. Since these are finite-dimensional spaces, we can equivalently describe them via their associated (symmetric, positive definite) norm matrices.

On the aggregated space, we define the matrix
\bm{X}(\bm{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}\times\mathcal{N}_{h}}
associated with the bilinear form:

(14)

\int_{\Omega(\bm{\mu})}\bm{\nabla}u_{h}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v_{h}d\Omega(\bm{\mu})+\int_{\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu})}\tau_{h}u_{h}v_{h}d\Gamma(\bm{\mu}).

For convenience, throughout this work we often replace this parameter-dependent norm with a parameter-independent one
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}\times\mathcal{N}_{h}}
, associated with the bilinear form:

(15)

\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\bm{\nabla}\widetilde{u}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\widetilde{v}_{h}d\widetilde{\Omega}+\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{D}}\tau_{h}\widetilde{u}_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}d\widetilde{\Gamma},\quad\widetilde{u}_{h}\doteq u_{h}\circ\underline{\psi}^{-1},\ \widetilde{v}_{h}\doteq v_{h}\circ\underline{\psi}^{-1}.

This substitution is warranted by the fact that we always assume these induced norms to be equivalent, which holds under suitable hypotheses on the deformation map. In particular, let
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu}),\ldots,\lambda_{d}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})
denote the sorted eigenvalues of
\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu})
evaluated at
\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
. Moreover, let
C_{1}(\bm{\mu})
and
C_{d}(\bm{\mu})
be positive constants such that

(16)

0<C_{1}(\bm{\mu})\leq\inf\limits_{\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})\leq\sup\limits_{\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\lambda_{d}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})\leq C_{d}(\bm{\mu})<\infty.

By virtue of (
16
), for any
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, the norm induced by
\bm{X}(\bm{\mu})
can be bounded by that induced by
\bm{X}
as follows:

(17)

\begin{split}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{X}(\bm{\mu})}&=\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}}\widetilde{v}_{h}\cdot\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}}\widetilde{v}_{h}\mathrm{det}(\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu}))d\widetilde{\Omega}+\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{D}}\tau_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}\|\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\widetilde{\underline{n}}\|_{2}d\widetilde{\Gamma}\\
&\leq\sup\limits_{\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}|\underline{\underline{J}}^{-1}(\bm{\mu})\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})\mathrm{det}(\underline{\underline{J}}(\bm{\mu}))|\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}}\widetilde{v}_{h}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\nabla}}\widetilde{v}_{h}+\sup\limits_{\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}|\underline{\underline{J}}^{-T}(\bm{\mu})|\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{D}}\tau_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}d\widetilde{\Gamma}\\
&\lesssim\sup\limits_{\widetilde{\bm{x}}\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\max\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})\cdots\lambda_{d}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})}{\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})^{2}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu})}\right)\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{X}}\leq\frac{\max\left(C_{d}(\bm{\mu})^{d-1},1\right)}{C_{1}(\bm{\mu})}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{X}},\end{split}

where
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}}
denotes the vector of nodal values associated with
v_{h}
(and
\widetilde{v}_{h}
). Conversely:

(18)

\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{X}}\lesssim\frac{C_{d}(\bm{\mu})}{\min\left(1,C_{1}(\bm{\mu})^{d-1}\right)}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{X}(\bm{\mu})}.

In other words, replacing
\bm{X}(\bm{\mu})
with
\bm{X}
introduces an error that is controlled by the ratio
C_{d}(\bm{\mu})/C_{1}(\bm{\mu})
. As we do not operate in regimes involving large deformations, we expect this error to be reasonable.

On
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
, we can directly consider a norm that is independent of
\bm{\mu}
, since the background domain
\widehat{\Omega}
is, by definition, parameter-independent. Given that harmonic extension is used to define functions outside the physical domain, we adopt the matrix
\widehat{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{h}\times\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{h}}
, representing the bilinear form:

(19)

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\bm{\nabla}u_{h}\cdot\bm{\nabla}v_{h}d\widehat{\Omega}.

3.2.
Reduced order equations

Once

is computed, we can project the

The core idea is to partition the set of offline parameters into clusters and construct local reduced subspaces and hyper-reduction quantities within each cluster. This serves as a form of piecewise linearization of the parameter-to-solution map, which is typically highly nonlinear in the presence of parameterized geometries. In essence, the localization strategy aims to reduce the Kolmogorov
N

(1)

For any
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{D}
belonging to the
i

(31)

\|\bm{u}(\bm{\mu})-\bm{u}(\bm{\alpha}_{i})\|_{\bm{X}}\propto\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}_{i}\right),\quad\|\bm{A}(\bm{\mu})-\bm{A}(\bm{\alpha}_{i})\|_{F}\propto\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}_{i}\right),\quad\|\bm{l}(\bm{\mu})-\bm{l}(\bm{\alpha}_{i})\|_{2}\propto\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}_{i}\right),

where
\mathrm{\mathrm{dist}}:\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathrm{dist}(\bm{\mu}_{i},\bm{\mu}_{j})\mapsto\max\limits_{k,l}\{\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{l}\|_{2},\ \bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{l}\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu}_{i})\cap\Gamma(\bm{\mu}_{j})\},

and quantifies a geometric distance between the deformed boundaries associated with two parameters.

(2)

For any
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{D}
belonging to the
i
th subspace cluster, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(32)

\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}_{i}\right)\leq C\|\bm{\mu}-\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}.

If both assumptions (
31

32
) hold, then the localization strategy is justified: the intra-cluster variation in solutions and operators is controlled by the parameter distance. While these assumptions are reasonable in many cases, they do not hold universally. Consequently, the localization procedure remains outside the certified framework of classical
rb

[
15
]
, the development of rigorous a posteriori error estimators for Algs.
3
-
4

6

lhs
and
rhs
, or pursue alternative clustering algorithms to
Kmeans

[
34
]
. However, we do not pursue these options in the present work.

4.1.
Tensor-train decomposition

tt

tt

\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{N}_{h_{d}}}

(33)

\{\bm{\Phi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d},\quad\bm{\Phi}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{i-1}\times\mathcal{N}_{h_{i}}\times r_{i}},

with
r_{0}=r_{d}=1
. Sequentially contracting the

cores yields an approximation of
\bm{U}
:

(34)

\bm{U}\approx\bm{U}_{r}=\sum\limits_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{1}}\sum\limits_{i_{2}=1}^{r_{2}}\cdots\sum\limits_{i_{d-1}=1}^{r_{d-1}}\bm{\Phi}_{1}[1,:,i_{1}]\otimes\bm{\Phi}_{2}[i_{1},:,i_{2}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\bm{\Phi}_{d}[i_{d-1},:,1].

Here,
\otimes

\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{a}\times N_{b}\times N_{c}},\qquad\bm{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{c}\times N_{d}\times N_{e}}

defined as

(35)

\mathbb{R}^{N_{a}\times N_{b}\times N_{d}\times N_{e}}\ni\bm{T}\doteq\bm{R}\cdot\bm{S},\qquad\bm{T}\left[\alpha_{a},\alpha_{b},\alpha_{d},\alpha_{e}\right]=\sum\limits_{\alpha_{c}}\bm{R}\left[\alpha_{a},\alpha_{b},\alpha_{c}\right]\bm{S}\left[\alpha_{c},\alpha_{d},\alpha_{e}\right].

35

(36)

\bm{U}\approx\bm{U}_{r}=\bm{\Phi}_{1}\cdot\bm{\Phi}_{2}\cdots\bm{\Phi}_{d},

The rank of the decomposition (
33

\bm{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
. Each

is referred to as a reduced rank, and is chosen according to the energy selection criterion (
21

5.1.
Full order model

We begin this subsection by stating the weak formulation of the incompressible, steady Stokes equations: find
(\underline{u}_{h}(\bm{\mu}),p_{h}(\bm{\mu}))\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\times\mathcal{Q}_{h}

(42)

\begin{split}a(\underline{u}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})&=\int_{\Omega(\bm{\mu})}\bm{\nabla}\underline{u}_{h}:\bm{\nabla}\underline{v}_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{D}(\bm{\mu})}\tau_{h}\underline{u}_{h}\cdot\underline{v}_{h}-(\bm{\nabla}\underline{u}_{h}\underline{n}(\bm{\mu}))\cdot\underline{v}_{h}-(\bm{\nabla}\underline{v}_{h}\underline{n}(\bm{\mu}))\cdot\underline{u}_{h},\\

We consider an inf-sup stable velocity and pressure combination of
fe

and

[
3
]

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}(\bm{\mu})&-\bm{B}(\bm{\mu})^{T}\\

\bm{p}(\bm{\mu})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{l}(\bm{\mu})\\
\bm{k}(\bm{\mu})\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\bm{A}(\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{p}\times\mathcal{N}_{h}^{u}},\quad\bm{u}(\bm{\mu}),\bm{l}(\bm{\mu})

Theorem 1

Let

5.3.

The supremizer enrichment proposed in

\forall\ \bm{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{p}},\ \bm{s}=\bm{X}^{-1/2}\bm{B_{\Omega}}^{T}\bm{q}=\arg\sup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{u}}}\frac{\bm{v}^{T}\bm{B_{\Omega}}\bm{q}}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{X}}\|\bm{q}\|_{\bm{Y}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{u}}.

Here,
\bm{B_{\Omega}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{p}\times\mathcal{N}_{h}^{u}}
is the matrix associated with
\widetilde{b}_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
, i.e. the bulk contribution of the coupling operator on the reference configuration. Adding
\bm{s}
to the velocity basis, followed by an orthogonalization step

\bm{\Phi}^{u}\leftarrow\texttt{Gram-Schmidt}\left([\bm{\Phi}^{u},\bm{s}],\bm{X}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_{h}^{u}\times(n^{u}+1)}

is what constitutes the supremizer enrichment process. Since pressure modes in the reduced model are linear combinations of the columns of
\bm{\Phi}^{p}
, we may simply solve (
59
) for each pressure basis vector and perform the enrichment. As explained in detail in
[
7
]
, this is enough to guarantee the surjectivity of
\bm{B}_{n}
– the reduced coupling operator on the reference configuration – following the enrichment. We summarize the enrichment procedure in Alg.
6
.

Under the assumptions of Thm.
2
, it follows that
\bm{B}_{n}(\bm{\mu})
is surjective (i.e., the reduced problem (
57
) is inf-sup stable), as established in the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\bm{\Phi}^{u}
be the enriched velocity basis, computed according to Alg.
6
. If (
51
) holds, the Galerkin projection of (
41
) onto the reduced space
\mathrm{span}\{\bm{\Phi}^{u}\}\times\mathrm{span}\{\bm{\Phi}^{p}\}
satisfies the inf-sup stability condition (
52
) restricted to these spaces.

Proof.

The proof relies on Thm.
1
to prove stability in the reference configuration of the
rom
. The key step is the possiblity to choose
\underline{\widetilde{s}}_{h}\in\mathrm{span}\{\bm{\Phi}^{u}\}
that satisfies (
49
), for any
\widetilde{q}_{h}\in\mathrm{span}\{\bm{\Phi}^{p}\}
. Here,
\underline{\widetilde{s}}_{h}
is none other than the supremizer corresponding to
\widetilde{q}_{h}
. As a result, the supremizer enrichment procedure guarantees the inf-sup stability of the reduced problem in the reference configuration. Finally, we can
transport
the stability to the deformed configuration using Thm.
2
.
∎

Lastly, we note that assumption (
51
) can readily be checked in practice. In particular, the constant can be tracked for every deformed mesh to assess the validity of the assumption. In principle, one could also extend the localization procedure in Algs.
3
-
4
to include the definition of (local) reference
fe

7.
Conclusions and future work

In this work, we present a unified framework for reduced basis approximations of
pde
defined on parameterized domains. The pipeline of the method includes: (i) representing shape parameterizations via deformation mappings from a chosen reference configuration; (ii) unfitted
fe
discretizations – which rely on background Cartesian grids, along with an analytical representation of the cut boundaries – of weak formulations on the reference configuration; (iii) subdividing offline parameters into several clusters; and (iv) for each cluster, building (local)
rb
subspaces and
mdeim
approximations of the problem’s
lhs
and
rhs
. We discuss the use of both
tpod
-based reduced subspaces and
tt
-based representations of these subspaces. We also present an efficient supremizer enrichment procedure for stabilizing reduced saddle-point problems on parameterized geometries. In particular, it is possible to enforce the inf-sup stability of the problem by working on the reference configuration, thus avoiding a costly parameter-dependent procedure.

We assess the proposed methodology on several numerical benchmarks, including the Poisson equation, a linear elasticity problem, an incompressible Stokes equation and an incompressible Navier-Stokes equation. We show that the
rb
approximations yield high accuracy, with convergence rates closely tracking the decay of the chosen reduction tolerance. The quality of the approximations stems first from the use of local subspaces, which allows for local linearization of the manifolds of
hf
quantities – solutions,
lhs
, and
rhs
– around the centroids in the parameter space; and second from the use of the deformation map, which also has a regularizing effect on these
hf
manifolds. In terms of computational gains, the methods achieve moderately high speedups relative to
hf
simulations, particularly in wall time. For problems of medium-to-low size – such as those solved in this manuscript – the cost of the online hyper-reduction step prevents truly ground-breaking gains. This limitation arises from the large number of
fe
cells selected during the offline phase of
mdeim
, which requires assembling large portions of
fe
matrices and vectors to approximate the
lhs
and
rhs
. However, the gains are expected to increase dramatically with the size of the
hf
problem.

Despite these positive results, we envision two main avenues for future work. Firstly, a posteriori error estimators should be developed to certify the proposed methods, as such certification is currently lacking. Lastly, alternative strategies to the
mdeim
-based interpolation could be explored, since this step accounts for most of the online cost.

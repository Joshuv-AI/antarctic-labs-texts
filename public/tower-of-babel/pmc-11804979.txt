Prevalence of Impacted Canines among Dental Patients in the Industrial Workers of Hyderabad – A Radiographic Study
Authors: Salla Chetan Kumar, Saritha Madham, Visshishta Jaggannagari, Kavuda Nagarjuna Prasad, Ajjarapu Sai Sowjanya, Firdous Sultana
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Impacted teeth have a low prevalence but exhibit a very uneven distribution. Among dental visits, impaction is one of the most common issues encountered. The causes of impaction can can vary from person to person, including factors such as a lack of calcium, retained deciduous tooth, or the presence of supernumerary teeth that interfere with the normal eruption pattern. The presence of impacted teeth can hinder the progress of orthodontic treatment. Therefore, understanding the prevalence of impacted teeth is crucial for effective treatment planning. This study aims to examine the prevalence of impacted teeth among dental patients in the southwest zone of Hyderabad.Materials and Methods:Panaromic radiographs taken in 2019 served as the basis for this crosssectional investigation. After reviewing 2,819 radiographs, data on patients with impacted teeth were collected. We analyzed the age and gender distribution of 40 individuals with impacted canine teeth to determine prevalence. Data were tabulated and analyzed using Excel. The sectors identified according to Ericson and Kurol were used to categorize the data. Frequency and percentage were used as statistical measures.Results:The study found that the most common type of impacted teeth was in males aged 20 and above (45%), followed by females at (40%). Among those aged less than 20, the prevalence was 12.5% in females and 2.5% in males. The data suggest that the majority of dental patients with impacted teeth were male and over the age of 20.Conclusion:The study concludes that impacted teeth are more prevalent in males over the age of 20 among dental patients in the southwest zone of Hyderabad.

INTRODUCTION

A tooth is considered impacted if it has not broken through the gums and is either partially or fully retained, determined by clinical and radiographic examinations.[1] A tooth is classified as impacted when its eruption into the mouth is blocked by another tooth, the gums, or bone above it.[23] If a tooth does not erupt into its proper position by the expected time and is obstructed by another tooth, bone, or soft tissue, further eruption is unlikely.[4] Failure of normal eruption of the permanent teeth is a common dental abnormality, regardless of gender or age.

Tooth eruption is considered a complex, genetically determined process. As a result of the tooth germ’s expected eruptive period and path, the opposing tooth can reach a predefined occlusal level issues.[5] related to impacted teeth can include problems with tooth movement, aesthetics, and functionality. When a tooth erupts into the mouth, it does so in harmony with the forward and lateral growth of the jaw bones, compensating for any size differences between them.[6] Due to the complexity of the eruption process, various complications arise, such as delayed tooth development and failure to erupt.[7] It is important to address these complications promptly to prevent further potential issues.

Various systemic and local factors can lead to tooth impaction.[89] Some local causes include insufficient space, retained deciduous teeth, infection, trauma, jaw abnormalities, cyst tumors, and the presence of supernumerary teeth.[10] Systemic factors may include rare conditions like cleidocranial dysostosis or cleft palate, as well as prenatal factors such as hereditary influences[10] and postnatal causes like anemia, congenital syphilis, malnutrition, or endocrine dysfunction.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and study setting

The participants in this were individuals who recently visited a dentist in Hyderabad’s southwest zone. Researchers conducted this retrospective cross-sectional analysis by reviewing dental hospital radiographs from January 2019 to December 2019.

Study population and sampling

The patient data cover a wide age range, from fifteen to forty-five years. After reviewing 2,819 patient radiographs from the college radiographic records, forty patients with impacted canines were included in the study.

Data

A review of 2,819 radiographs allowed for the collection of data on patients with impacted teeth. The parameters evaluated included the number of impacted canines, the number of individuals with impacted canines, and the age distribution of these individuals. The sector classifications proposed by Ericson and Kurol were used to categorize the impacted canines. All collected data were tabulated using Microsoft Excel.

Statistical analysis

We used statistical methods to tabulate and analyze the recorded data to obtain our results. Categorical variables were presented as percentages and frequencies in bar charts.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

In this study, the panoramic radiographs of forty individuals diagnosed with impacted canine teeth were examined. The objective was to document the frequency of impacted teeth among 2,819 radiographs and identify patients requiring orthodontic treatment. Data were collected by reviewing the dental hospital’s records.

According to the sector classification by Ericson and Kurol, impacted canine were more common in females (52.5%) than in males (47.5%) [Figure 1]. Specifically, in Sector 1, impacted canines were found in 15% of females and 10% of males; in Sector 2-17.5% of females and 12.5% of males; and in Sector 3, 20% of females and 25% of males.

CONCLUSION

Extrapolating from the data collected in this study, impacted canines are more common in male dental patients aged 20 and older. Therefore, early diagnosis crucial to prevent further complications in orthodontic treatment.

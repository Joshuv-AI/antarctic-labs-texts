Lattice Translation Modulated Symmetries and TFTs
Ching-Yu Yao

Abstract




Modulated symmetries are internal symmetries that are not invariant under spacetime symmetry actions. We propose a general way to describe the lattice translation modulated symmetries in 1+1D, including the non-invertible ones, via the tensor network language. We demonstrate that the modulations can be described by some autoequivalences of the categories. Although the topological behaviors are broken because of the presence of modulations, we can still construct the modulated version of the symmetry TFT bulks by inserting a series of domain walls described by invertible bimodule categories. This structure not only recovers some known results on invertible modulated symmetries but also provides a general framework to tackle modulated symmetries in a more general setting.

In many-body quantum systems, symmetries play important roles in understanding physical phenomena and classifying phases of matter. The Landau paradigm distinguishes different phases by patterns of spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB); on the other hand, the symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases are quantum phases that do not undergo SSB, but can only be adiabatically deformed to each other if the symmetry is broken

The above framework is based on the assumption that the symmetry operators are topological. 3.4
Foliated BF Theory

In this subsection, we will demonstrate that the UV limit of our construction for abelian cases meets the construction of foliated BF theory. We will use the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
dipole symmetry discussed in the previous subsection as an example.

Since the internal symmetry group is
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, we first write down a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}
BF theory

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}}=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(b^{Q}\wedge da^{Q}+b^{D}\wedge da^{D}\right).

(3.37)

Due to the presence of domain walls, we should add an additional term given by the modulation (
3.22
)

a^{Q}_{\mu}(t,x+1,y)-a^{Q}_{\mu}(t,x,y)\mapsto a^{Q}_{\mu}(t,x+1,y)-\left(a^{Q}_{\mu}(t,x,y)+a^{D}_{\mu}(t,x,y)\right),

(3.38)

and the continuum limit is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{x}a^{Q}_{\mu}&\mapsto\partial_{x}a^{Q}_{\mu}-a^{D}_{\mu},\\
\implies da^{Q}&\mapsto da^{Q}+a^{D}\wedge dx.\end{split}

(3.39)

Thus, the Lagrangian of the modulated bulk of the dipole symmetry is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dip}}&=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(b^{Q}\wedge\left(da^{Q}+a^{D}\wedge dx\right)+b^{D}\wedge da^{D}\right)\\
&=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(b^{Q}\wedge da^{Q}+b^{D}\wedge da^{D}+b^{Q}\wedge a^{D}\wedge dx\right).\end{split}

(3.40)

This Lagrangian is exactly the foliated BF theory for dipole symmetry. Similar to the construction of foliated BF theory shown in
[
33
]
, but we only consider the dipole symmetry along x-axis. The construction is as follows.

Write the symmetry actions
Q
and
D
as

\displaystyle Q=\int_{V}*j^{Q},

(3.41)

\displaystyle D=\int_{V}*J^{D},

(3.42)

where
V
is an equal-time codimension-1 submanifold. Since
j^{Q}
and
J^{D}
are conserved currents, we have

\displaystyle d*j^{Q}=0,

(3.43)

\displaystyle d*J^{D}=0.

(3.44)

According to (
3.24
), we have

\displaystyle Q(x+1,y)=Q(x,y),

(3.45)

\displaystyle D(x+1,y)=D(x,y)+Q(x,y).

(3.46)

The continuum limit is

\displaystyle\partial_{i}Q=0,

(3.47)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}D=\delta_{x,i}Q.

(3.48)

Hence, we can write

*J^{D}=*j^{D}+x*j^{Q},

(3.49)

where
j^{D}
is defined to be local.

Couple the currents
j^{Q},j^{D}
to the background 1-form gauge fields
a^{Q},a^{D}

S=\int_{M}\left(a^{Q}\wedge*j^{Q}+a^{D}\wedge*j^{D}\right).

(3.50)

According to (
3.43
) and

d*j^{D}=-dx\wedge*j^{Q},

(3.51)

the gauge symmetry is then

\displaystyle a^{Q}

\displaystyle\mapsto a^{Q}+d\lambda^{Q}-\lambda^{D}dx,

(3.52)

\displaystyle a^{D}

\displaystyle\mapsto a^{D}+d\lambda^{D}.

(3.53)

Thus, the gauge invariant 2-forms are

\displaystyle f^{Q}:=da^{Q}+a^{D}\wedge dx,

(3.54)

\displaystyle f^{D}:=da^{D},

(3.55)

and the foliated BF theory is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}&=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(b^{Q}\wedge f^{Q}+b^{D}\wedge f^{D}\right)\\
&=\frac{N}{2\pi}\left(b^{Q}\wedge da^{Q}+b^{D}\wedge da^{D}+b^{Q}\wedge a^{D}\wedge dx\right).\end{split}

(3.56)

Therefore, our construction recovers the foliated BF theory.

4
Summary and Outlooks

We have proposed a natural way to describe the algebraic structure of the lattice translation modulated symmetries in 1+1D, and classified the gapped phases with modulated symmetries. The modulations are described by some autoequivalences, and this structure not only recovers some known results on invertible modulated symmetries, it also provides a general framework on describing non-invertible modulated symmetries. The construction of the modulated version of the 2+1D SymTFT bulk naturally arises from the data on the boundary, which is inserting a series of domain walls described by the corresponding invertible bimodule category.

There are several future research directions that we would like to study. The most straightforward one is to study more specific models, especially the ones with non-trivial mixed anomalies or non-invertible modulated symmetries. In addition, in this work we focus on the lattice translation modulation in 1+1D, it is natural to generalize the discussion for different types of spacetime modulation, modulated symmetries in higher dimension, and higher-form modulated symmetries.

Fracton orders are related to subsystem symmetries, which are modulated symmetries whose internal symmetry size grows with the system size. If we aim to study subsystem symmetries in a similar strategy, a generalized version of modulated infinite symmetries might be needed.

Lastly, although we can do numerical simulations using the modulated MPO and modulated MPS that we propose, some of the traditional techniques will be invalid. For example, the density matrix renormalization group (DMRG)
[
53
]
has been a powerful tool to study the critical behaviors of quantum lattice models, but it actually destroys the modulated structures. How to simulate systems with modulated symmetries efficiently is also an interesting problem for future study.

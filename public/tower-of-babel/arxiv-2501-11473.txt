Bounds on the Privacy Amplification of Arbitrary Channels via the Contraction of f α f_{\alpha} -Divergence Thanks: This work was supported by the Swedish Research Council (VR) under grants 2023-04787 and 2024-06615.
Leonhard Grosse 1 , Sara Saeidian 1 2 , Tobias J. Oechtering 1 , and Mikael Skoglund 1

Abstract
We examine the privacy amplification of channels that do not necessarily satisfy any LDP guarantee by analyzing their contraction behavior in terms of f α f_{\alpha} -divergence, an f f -divergence related to Rényi-divergence via a monotonic transformation. We present bounds on contraction for restricted sets of prior distributions via f f -divergence inequalities and present an improved Pinsker’s inequality for f α f_{\alpha} -divergence based on the joint range technique by Harremoës and Vajda [17] . The presented bound is tight whenever the value of the total variation distance is larger than 1 / α \nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}} . By applying these inequalities in a cross-channel setting, we arrive at strong data processing inequalities for f α f_{\alpha} -divergence that can be adapted to use-case specific restrictions of input distributions and channel. The application of these results to privacy amplification shows that even very sparse channels can lead to significant privacy amplification when used as a post-processing step after local differentially private mechanisms.



I
Introduction

An important property of
local differential privacy
(LDP) is its invariance to post-processing. Informally, the post-processing property states that whatever further processing is applied to the output of a system satisfying LDP will not weaken the overall system’s privacy guarantee. Mathematically, this property can be seen as a form of
data processing inequality
(DPI). As an extension of this observation, it is natural to ask about the
privacy amplification
of a post-processing operation. Basic results state that if a post-processing operation satisfying
\varepsilon_{2}
-LDP is applied to a system satisfying
\varepsilon_{1}
-LDP, the composition of both systems will satisfy
at least

\min\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\}
-LDP. However, it is generally not know when
arbitrary
post-processing operations, that is, operations that themselves do
not
satisfy any LDP guarantee, may improve the overall systems LDP guarantee. We take steps to answer this question in this paper. In the context of data processing inequalities, this relates to the investigation of
strong
data processing inequalities (SDPIs), or the
contraction
of a channel with respect to a suitable measure of divergence (see
[
25
, Chapter 33]
). For pure LDP, the divergence measure of interest is the Rényi-divergence
[
27
,
32
]
of order
\infty
, since

\max_{y}\log\frac{P_{X|W=w}(y)}{P_{X|W=w^{\prime}}(y)}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}D_{\alpha}(P_{X|W=w}||P_{X|W=w^{\prime}}).

(1)

Gilani et al. [16]
suggest the notion of
\alpha
-Rényi-LDP as a relaxation of pure LDP, which bounds the maximum Rényi-divergence between the induced output distributions for some arbitrary order
\alpha
. With this framework at hand, studying the privacy amplification of a channel becomes equivalent to studying SDPIs for Rényi-divergences. While SDPIs have been extensively studied for
f
-divergences
, results on Rényi-divergences are so far less explored. Although Rényi-divergence is not an
f
-divergence, it can be expressed as a monotonic transformation of a corresponding
f_{\alpha}
-divergence

[
29
]
, a family of
f
-divergences parameterized by
\alpha
. This connection allows us to translate results on
f
-divergences to Rényi-divergences—results that are often easier to obtain.

I-A

Contributions

In this work, we derive several technical tools for studying the privacy amplification of arbitrary discrete channels—those not necessarily satisfying any LDP guarantee—by analyzing how they affect the Rényi-LDP of composed systems. Our key contributions are:

•

We derive a general condition under which channels fail to contract Rényi-divergence, establishing structural limits on the contraction of a channel in Section
III
.

•

We develop new tools for bounding the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence in terms of total variation distance in Section
IV
. Specifically, we provide:

–

an improved and often tight version of Pinsker’s inequality for
f_{\alpha}
-divergence (for
\alpha>1
);

–

a simplification of the recently presented proof of Binette’s inequality
[
9
]
in
[
18
]
based on
E_{\gamma}
-divergence.

•

Using these tools, we analyze the contraction of
f_{\alpha}
-divergence and show how input distribution constraints can yield meaningful bounds in Section
V
.

•

Finally, in Section
VI
, we apply these bounds to the Rényi-LDP amplification of arbitrary post-processing channels. Our results show that many channels—despite not satisfying
any
(finite) LDP guarantee—can result in significant privacy amplification if applied to the output of a mechanism satisfying LDP. We illustrate these findings by applying the proposed bounds to randomized response mechanisms post-processed by channels with sparse channel matrices (i.e., matrices with many zeros).

II
Preliminaries

We use uppercase letters to denote random variables, lowercase letters to denote their realizations and caligraphic letters to denote sets. Specifically, we consider the two random variables
X
and
Y
, where
X
represents input data into a channel
P_{Y|X}
(also referred to as
mechanism
if it satisfies an RLDP guarnatee in the following) that induces a random variable
Y
at its output. Let
P_{XY}
denote the joint distribution of
X
and
Y
. Then we use
P_{XY}=P_{Y|X}\times P_{X}
to imply that
P_{XY}(x,y)=P_{Y|X=x}(y)P_{X}(x)
and
P_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}
to denote the marginalization
P_{Y}(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{Y|X=x}(y)P_{X}(x)
. We write
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
for the probability simplex on the set
\mathcal{X}
. For
N\in\mathbb N
, we define
[N]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,N\}
as the set of positive integers up to
N
. Finally,
\log(\cdot)
denotes the natural logarithm.

II-A

Rényi-divergence,
f_{\alpha}
-divergene and
E_{\gamma}
-divergence

For any convex function
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R
such that
f(1)=0
, define the
f
-divergence
between two probability measures
P\ll Q

[
11
]
as

D_{f}(P||Q)=\mathbb E_{Q}\bigg[f\bigg(\frac{P}{Q}\bigg)\bigg].

(2)

The
Rényi-divergence

[
27
]

D_{\alpha}(P||Q)
between
P
and
Q
is a monotone transform of the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence

[
25
]
:

Definition 1

(
f_{\alpha}
-divergence)
.

For any
\alpha>0
, the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence
is defined as the
f
-divergence
D_{f_{\alpha}}(P||Q)
, where

f_{\alpha}(x)=\begin{cases}1-x^{\alpha},\text{ if }\alpha<1,\\
x\log x,\text{ if }\alpha=1,\\
x^{\alpha}-1,\text{ if }\alpha>1.\end{cases}

(3)

The
Rényi-divergence of order
\alpha
is then obtained by

D_{\alpha}(P||Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\biggl\{D_{f_{\alpha}}(P||Q)+1\biggr\}.

(4)

We will use the
Hockeystick-divergence

E_{\gamma}
(from here on simply called
E_{\gamma}
-divergence
) as a tool for obtaining bounds on the Rényi-divergence, as similarly done in
[
24
, Section 3.2]
.

Definition 2

(
E_{\gamma}
-divergence, see, e.g.,
[
29
]
)
.

Given some parameter
\gamma>0
, the
E_{\gamma}
-divergence
between two discrete distributions
P
and
Q
is defined as

E_{\gamma}(P||Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|P(x)-\gamma Q(x)|-\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|.

(5)

For twice differentiable
f
, the relation shown in
[
10
]
,

D_{f}(P||Q)=\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\gamma}(P||Q)f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)d\gamma,

(6)

can then be used to obtain any such
f
-divergence.
For
\gamma=1
, the
E_{\gamma}
-divergence is equal to the total variation distance between the two distributions, i.e.,
E_{1}(P||Q)=\text{TV}(P||Q)
.

II-B

Strong Data Processing Inequalities

All
f
-divergences (including the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence)—and hence also the Rényi-divergence—satisfy the
data processing inequality
(DPI). The DPI states that for any two probability measures
P_{X},Q_{X}
on
\mathcal{X}
, and some transition kernel
P_{Y|X}
,

D_{f}(P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}||P_{Y|X}\circ Q_{X})\leq D_{f}(P_{X}||Q_{X}).

(7)

We define the contraction coefficient
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})
of the
\alpha
-Rényi-divergence given a channel
P_{Y|X}
as the maximum factor between the two sides of the above inequality, that is,

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha}(P_{X},P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})

(8)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P}:D_{\alpha}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\neq 0}\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}||P_{Y|X}\circ Q_{X})}{D_{\alpha}(P_{X}||Q_{X})},

(9)

\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})\coloneqq\sup_{P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}}\eta_{\alpha}(P_{X},P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}).

(10)

Here,
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes a subset of the probability simplex on the alphabet
\mathcal{X}
. In this framework, the DPI states that
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})\leq 1
. We are then interested in the conditions for which
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})<1
, that is, cases in which the inequality in (
7
) is
strict
. In this case, we say a
strong data processing inequality
(SDPI) holds. Similarly to the above, we define
\eta_{\text{TV}}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})
as the contraction coefficient of the total variation distance given that input distributions are picked from the set
\mathcal{P}
.
In the most general case, an SDPI considers
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
. However, we will see below that without any restriction,
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
in many cases. We will therefore derive bounds for cases in which
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
.

III
Conditions for
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1

The following result provides a condition on the channel
P_{Y|X}
for which there is no contraction, that is, for which no strong data processing inequality holds, in the case
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proposition 3
.

Consider a discrete channel
P_{Y|X}
. Whenever

\exists x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}:\textnormal{supp}\big(P_{Y|X=x}\big)\cap\textnormal{supp}\big(P_{Y|X=x^{\prime}}\big)=\emptyset,

(11)

we have
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
.

Proof:

We provide a constructive proof in Appendix
B
. Here, we show Proposition
3
by a simple chain of arguments following from previous results. Let
(X,Y)\sim P_{Y|X}\times P_{X}
be arbitrary but fixed. Define the
maximal correlation
as

\rho_{m}(X;Y)\coloneqq\sup_{f,g}\mathbb E_{(X,Y)\sim P_{XY}}\Big[f(X)g(Y)\Big],

(12)

where the supremum is over all
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb R
and
g:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb R
such that

\mathbb E\big[f(X)\big]=\mathbb E\big[g(Y)\big]=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb E\big[f^{2}(X)\big]=\mathbb E\big[g^{2}(Y)\big]=1.

(13)

The contraction coefficient of the
\chi^{2}
-divergence is closely related to
\rho_{m}
, and it is shown in, e.g.,
[
25
, Thm. 33.6(c)]
that

\eta_{\chi^{2}}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=\sup_{P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}}\rho_{m}^{2}(X;Y)\eqqcolon s(P_{Y|X}).

(14)

It was shown by
Ahlswede and Gács [1]
that
s(P_{Y|X})<1
if and only if for every pair
x,x^{\prime}
, it holds that
\textnormal{supp}(P_{Y|X=x})\cap\textnormal{supp}(P_{Y|X=x^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
. Together with the data processing inequality for
f
-divergences, this implies that whenever (
11
) holds, we have
\eta_{\chi^{2}}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
. Next, it was shown in
[
19
, Corollary 2]
that

\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})\geq\eta_{\chi^{2}}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}).

(15)

By the data processing inequality for Rényi-divergences, we also have
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})\leq 1
. Hence, if (
11
) holds, we have
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
.
∎

III-A

Connection to the Confusion Graph of
P_{Y|X}

Proposition
3
states that a channel does not contract the Rényi-divergence whenever there exist two input symbols that cause conditional distributions with disjoint support at the channel output. This condition can equivalently be expressed as requiring a channel’s
confusion graph
to be incomplete
[
12
]
: Define the confusion graph
G=(V,W)
by
V=\mathcal{X}
and

\displaystyle W=\{(x_{i},x_{j})\in\mathcal{X}^{2}\mid\exists\,y\in\mathcal{Y}:x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{(y)}\wedge x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}^{(y)}\},

(16)

where
\mathcal{X}^{(y)}
for each
y
denotes the positions at which the channel matrix contains non-zero entries, that is,

\mathcal{X}^{(y)}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}:P_{Y|X=x}(y)>0\}.

(17)

Heuristically, an edge
(x_{i},x_{j})
between input symbols in the confusion graph indicates that there exists an output symbol
y\in\mathcal{Y}
that can be caused by both
x_{i}
or
x_{j}
at the channel input. That is, a missing edge (compared to the complete version) indicates the existence of a pair of symbols that can be sent over the channel without being confused with each other. The confusion graph—along with its graph-theoretic dual—is extensively used in zero-error information theory, see e.g.,
[
31
,
21
]
for a detailed account of results and methods.

Note that a fully connected confusion graph is not necessarily equivalent to a strictly positive channel matrix. Proposition
3
in a loose sense states that for
sufficiently sparse

P_{Y|X}
, Rényi-divergences do not contract in general. The following examples illustrates this condition on the channel matrices.

Example 4

(Block-diagonal matrices)
.

Any block-diagonal matrix
\text{diag}(B_{1},\dots,B_{m})
with each
B_{i}\in\mathbb R^{k_{i}\times k_{i}},\,k_{i}\in\mathbb N
, satisfies
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
.

Example 5

(
k
-singular channels)
.

Inspired by
[
2
]
, we say a
N\times N
channel
P_{Y|X}
is
k
-singular
if

P_{Y|X=x}(y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{k},\text{ if }x\in\mathcal{X}^{(y)}\\
0,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(18)

By Example
4
, any
k
-singular channel with
k\leq N/2
satisfies
\eta_{\alpha}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})=1
. For illustration, consider the channels

P=\begin{bmatrix}\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&0\\
\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}\\
\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}\\
0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}\end{bmatrix},\hskip 10.00002ptQ=\begin{bmatrix}\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&0&0\\
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\\
0&0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}.\end{bmatrix}

(19)

Here,
P
is
3
-singular and
Q
is
2
-singular. It is easy to see that the assertion in Proposition
3
applies to
Q
, but not to
P
.

IV
Pinsker-type Inequalities for
f_{\alpha}
-divergence

The result in Section
III
shows that in many cases, the contraction coefficient can not be smaller than one whenever
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
. This motivates us to investigate the behavior of
\eta_{\alpha}
in cases where
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
. As we will see in Section
VI
, this can be useful in cases in which partial knowledge about the input distributions to a channel is available.

In this section, we present a generalization of the reverse Pinker’s inequality and Pinsker’s inequality for Rényi-divergences. These inequalities will then be used to derive “SDPI-style” bounds in Section
V
. In most of what follows, we will work with the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence as a substitute for Rényi-divergence. Due to the monotonic transformation in (
4
), conversion between the two can be done easily at any point.

The following theorem bounds the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence as a function of the total variation between two distributions. It was first shown by
Binette [9]
for general
f
-divergences. Here, we present a proof that utilizes the integral representation of
f
-divergences via the
E_{\gamma}
-divergence in (
6
). We remark that the same technique was recently used to proof Binette’s inequality in a quantum context by
Hirche and Tomamichel [18, Proposition 5.2]
. However, the proof presented below slightly simplifies arguments, and we include it here as we believe it to be instructive.

Theorem 6

(Reverse Pinkser’s Inequality for
f_{\alpha}
-divergence, special case of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\alpha>1
. For any two distributions
P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P}
, define

\Gamma_{\max}(X)\coloneqq\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P},\,x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{P_{X}(x)}{Q_{X}(x)},

(20)

\Gamma_{\min}(X)\coloneqq\inf_{P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P},\,x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{P_{X}(x)}{Q_{X}(x)}.

(21)

Then, the maximum
f_{\alpha}
-divergence between the two distributions is upper bounded by

D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\leq\textnormal{TV}(P_{X}||Q_{X})R_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{\max}(X),\Gamma_{\min}(X)),

(22)

where for
u\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
and
v\in[0,1]
,

R_{\alpha}(u,v)=\frac{u^{\alpha}-1}{u-1}-\frac{1-v^{\alpha}}{1-v}.

(23)

Proof:

Fix
Q_{X}
and
P_{X}
and let
\Delta\coloneqq\text{TV}(P_{X}||Q_{X})
. The proof depends on the fact that the only interval on which we may have
E_{\gamma}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\neq 0
is
(\Gamma_{\min}(X),\Gamma_{\max}(X))
. To see this, observe that

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}(P_{X}||Q_{X})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\biggl|P_{X}(x)-\gamma Q_{X}(x)\biggr|-\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|,

(24)

and whenever
P_{X}(x)-\gamma Q_{X}(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
or
P_{X}(x)-\gamma Q_{X}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}(P_{X}||Q_{X})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\biggl|P_{X}(x)-\gamma Q_{X}(x)\biggr|-\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\biggl(\pm P_{X}(x)\mp\gamma Q_{X}(x)\biggr)-\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|-\frac{1}{2}|1-\gamma|=0.

(27)

This happens exactly when

\gamma\leq\min_{x}\frac{P_{X}(x)}{Q_{X}(x)}\eqqcolon\Gamma_{\min}(X,P_{X},Q_{X}),

(28)

or,

\gamma\geq\max_{x}\frac{P_{X}(x)}{Q_{X}(x)}\eqqcolon\Gamma_{\max}(X,P_{X},Q_{X}).

(29)

Hence,
E_{\gamma}
must be zero everywhere but on the interval between these two quantities, that is, the only values of
\gamma
that allow a non-zero value of
E_{\gamma}
are

\gamma\in(\Gamma_{\min}(X,P_{X},Q_{X}),\Gamma_{\max}(X,P_{X},Q_{X}))\eqqcolon I_{\geq 0}.

(30)

From this, we get

E_{\gamma}(P_{X}||Q_{X})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\gamma\notin I_{\geq 0},\\[5.0pt]
E_{\gamma}(P_{X}||Q_{X}),&\text{ if }\gamma\in I_{\geq 0},\end{cases}

(31)

What remains is to bound
E_{\gamma}
on the interval
I_{\geq 0}
, which yields an upper bound on
D_{f_{\alpha}}
by (
6
). To do this, note that
\gamma\mapsto E_{\gamma}(P||Q)
is convex and increasing on
[0,1]
and convex and decreasing on
(1,\infty)

[
24
]
. Hence, the maximum value is obtained at
\gamma=1
, where we have
E_{1}(Q_{X}||P_{X})=\Delta
. By convexity, we can then upper bound
E_{\gamma}
by the piecewise linear function

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}

\displaystyle(P_{X}||Q_{X})\leq g(\gamma)

(32)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\gamma\notin I_{\geq 0},\\[6.99997pt]
\Delta\frac{\gamma-\Gamma_{\min}(X,P_{X},Q_{X})}{1-\Gamma_{\min}(X,P_{X},Q_{X})},&\text{if }\gamma\in I_{\geq 0}\cap(0,1],\\[6.99997pt]
\Delta\frac{-\gamma+\Gamma_{\max}(X,P_{X},Q_{X})}{\Gamma_{\max}(X,P_{X},Q_{X})-1},&\text{if }\gamma\in I_{\geq 0}\cap(1,\infty).\end{cases}

(33)

The theorem then follows from maximizing
g(\gamma)
over
P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P}
, which together with (
6
) yields

D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\leq\alpha(\alpha-1)\int_{\Gamma_{\min}(X)}^{\Gamma_{\max}(X)}g(\gamma)\gamma^{\alpha-2}d\gamma,

(34)

and the desired statement follows by solving this integral.
∎Note that this proof technique can also be used to obtain the more general result in
[
9
]
for any
f
-divergence for which
f
is twice differentiable by replacing
\gamma^{\alpha-2}
with the general
f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)
in (
34
). Further, if more information about the distributions is known, more specific bounds on
f(\gamma)
can potentially be used to obtain more specific inequalities in these cases.

.

Next, we present a generalized Pinsker’s inequality for
f_{\alpha}
-divergence of order
\alpha>1

Theorem 8

(Pinsker’s inequality for
f_{\alpha}
-divergences)
.

For any
\alpha>1
and any two mutually absolutely continuous probability measures
P_{X}\ll\gg Q_{X}
, we have

D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\geq g_{\alpha}\biggl(\text{TV}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\biggr),

(38)

where

g_{\alpha}(t)=\begin{cases}e^{2(\alpha-1)t^{2}}-1,&\text{ if }t<\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}\text{ and }\alpha<2,\\
(4t^{2}+1)^{\alpha-1}-1,&\text{ if }t<\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}\text{ and }\alpha\geq 2,\\
(1-t)^{1-\alpha}-1,&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(39)

Proof:

The proof follows from bounding the
joint range

[
17
]
of the divergences and is presented in Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 9
.

For any
\alpha>1
, the bound in Theorem
8
is attained by the pair of distributions
P_{X}=[0,1]
,
Q_{X}=[t,1-t]
in the case

. Hence, it is tight for this range of values. It is also tight for all

given that

. In this case, it reduces to the joint-range-based bound between

-divergence and total variation distance, as e.g. presented in

\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)=0.

(40)

In the range

that satisfies

. Due to the convexity of
\psi_{\alpha}
in

, a (tight) lower bound is then given by

.
However, it remains unclear if

can be expressed in closed form. Due to this complication, the result above utilizes more rudimentary bounds in this case. Further, we invoke standard Pinsker’s inequality for relative entropy to derive the first case of (

[
15

in implicit form. We rely on the more rudimentary standard Pinsker bound here for simplicity.

V
Strong Data Processing with Pinsker

The above results can now be used to reason about the data processing properties of channels by considering the presented inequalities “across” a given channel. That is, we can modify the bound presented in Theorem
6
to yield an upper bound on
D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{Y}||Q_{Y})
in terms of
\text{TV}(P_{X}||Q_{X})
. To this end, consider

\Gamma_{\max}(Y)=\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P},\,y\in\mathcal{Y}}\frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{Y|X=x}(y)Q_{X}(x)}{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{Y|X=x}(y)P_{X}(x)},

(42)

\Gamma_{\min}(Y)=\inf_{P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P},\,y\in\mathcal{Y}}\frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{Y|X=x}(y)Q_{X}(x)}{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P_{Y|X=x}(y)P_{X}(x)},

(43)

as the “cross-channel” modification of the crucial extremal quantities in Theorem
6
. We get the following result.

Corollary 11
.

For a discrete channel
P_{Y|X}
, distributions
P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
,
P_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}
,
Q_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ Q_{X}
and some
\alpha>1
, we have

\displaystyle D_{f_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle(P_{Y}||Q_{Y})

(44)

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{TV}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})R_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{\max}(Y),\Gamma_{\min}(Y))

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\times g_{\alpha}^{-1}\Big(D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{X}||Q_{X})\Big).

(46)

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
6
with
P_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}
and
Q_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ Q_{X}
, the inequality in Theorem
8
, the inverse given in Remark
10
and since by definition,
\text{TV}(P_{Y}||Q_{Y})\leq\eta_{TV}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P})\text{TV}(P_{X}||Q_{X})
if
P_{X},Q_{X}\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

This result stresses the relation between
\Gamma_{\max}(Y)
and
\Gamma_{\min}(Y)
as formulated in (
42
) and (
43
) and the contraction behavior of a channel in terms of Rényi-divergence: If
\Gamma_{\max}(Y)-\Gamma_{\min}(Y)
is small, the channel significantly contracts the
f_{\alpha}
-divergence. If the difference is large, contraction will be small.
What remains is to bound
\Gamma_{\max}(Y)
and
\Gamma_{\min}(Y)
so that an operational bound on this contraction is obtained. Such bounds should be use-case specific, as the structure of the uncertainty set
\mathcal{P}
, as well as specifics about the channel are crucial factors. One such bound can be obtained in the application of the above results to privacy amplification, which we will present in the following section.

Privacy Amplification Bounds for RLDP

In this section, we show how the above technical tools can be used to obtain improved post-processing inequalities for Rényi local differential privacy (RLDP)
[
23

16

W-X-Y

-RLDP,

1

\displaystyle\text{RLDP}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle(P_{X|W})

(48)

(50)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\eta_{TV}(P_{Y|X},\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})R_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{\max}(Y),\Gamma_{\min}(Y))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\times g_{\alpha}^{-1}\Big(D_{f_{\alpha}}(P_{X|W=w}||P_{X|W=w^{\prime}})\Big).

Example

First, the results motivate us to further investigate

13

Specifically, if some form of LDP processing is done to the data, even a learning system without a formal LDP guarantee on its own might be able to significantly increase the privacy guarantee with respect to the data used to train it.

Second, the results are promising for addressing the common issue of large values of

-LDP guarantees in practical systems

14

Future work in this direction should focus on more advanced tools and methods for bounding the values

\Gamma_{\min}
for various (practical) systems. Obtaining such bounds paves the way for easy plug-and-play solutions for improving privacy guarantees of weak LDP systems at a minimal loss of utility.

Proof of Theorem
8

From
[
29
]
and the fact that
D_{f_{\alpha}}
is a monotone transform of the Rényi-divergence of the same order, we know that

\inf_{P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{X}:\,TV(P||Q)=t}D_{f_{\alpha}}(P||Q)=\min_{p,q:\,|p-q|=t}d_{f_{\alpha}}(p||q),

(63)

where
d_{f_{\alpha}}(p||q)
denotes the binary
f_{\alpha}
-divergence

d_{f_{\alpha}}(p||q)\coloneqq\frac{p^{\alpha}}{q^{\alpha-1}}+\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha}}{(1-q)^{\alpha-1}}-1.

(64)

Define

\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)\coloneqq\frac{p^{\alpha}}{(p+t)^{\alpha-1}}+\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha}}{(1-p-t)^{\alpha-1}}-1.

(65)

From the definition of the total variation, we get
\min_{p,q:\,|p-q|=t}d_{f_{\alpha}}(p||q)=\min_{p\in[0,1-t]}\{\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)\}
. Taking derivatives with respect to
p
yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)=(\alpha t+p)\biggl[\frac{p^{\alpha-1}}{(p+t)^{\alpha}}+\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha-1}}{(1-p-t)^{\alpha}}\biggr]-\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha-1}}{(1-p-t)^{\alpha}}

(66)

and for any
\alpha>1
and all
t<1,\,p\leq 1-t
,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)=(\alpha-1)\alpha t^{2}\biggl[\frac{p^{\alpha-2}}{(p+t)^{\alpha+1}}+\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha-2}}{(1-p-t)^{\alpha+1}}\biggr]\geq 0,

(67)

from which we can conclude that
\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)
is convex in
p
.

First, consider the case
t\geq\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}
. We can bound the first derivative by

\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)\geq(\alpha t+p-1)\biggl(\frac{(1-p)^{\alpha-1}}{(1-p-t)^{\alpha}}\biggr)\geq 0,

(68)

with equality for
t=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}
if
p=0
. Otherwise strict inequality holds. Together with the convexity of
\psi_{\alpha}
in
p
, this implies that the maximum value of
\psi_{\alpha}
for all
t\geq\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}
will be achieved at
p=0
. At this point, we have
\psi_{\alpha}(0,t)=(1-t)^{1-\alpha}
.

Secondly, for
t<\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}
, we first consider
\alpha\geq 2
. Let
P,Q
be binary probability distributions with
P(0)=p
and
Q(0)=p+t
Then, noticing that
x\mapsto x^{\alpha-1}
is convex for
\alpha\geq 2
, we apply Jensen’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(p,t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb E_{X\sim P}\biggl[\biggl(\frac{P(X)}{Q(X)}\biggr)^{\alpha-1}-1\biggr]

\displaystyle\geq\biggl(\mathbb E_{X\sim P}\biggl[\frac{P(X)}{Q(X)}\biggr]\biggr)^{\alpha-1}-1

\displaystyle=\biggl(1+\frac{t^{2}}{(p+t)(1-p-t)}\biggr)^{\alpha-1}-1

\displaystyle\geq(1+4t^{2})^{\alpha-1}-1.

Finally, we bound the remaining case with the standard Pinsker’s inequality: Since the Rényi-divergence is increasing in
\alpha

[
32
]
, so is
D_{f_{\alpha}}
. Hence the standard Pinsker’s inequlity for the Kullback-Leibler divergence
\text{KL}(P||Q)
(Rényi-divergence of order

\text{KL}(P||Q)=D_{1}(P||Q)\geq 2\text{TV}(P||Q)^{2}

D_{f_{\alpha}}(P||Q)\geq\exp\big(2(\alpha-1)\text{TV}(P||Q)^{2}\big)-1.

(69)

Putting the derived bounds together to form

Impact of adopting digital FinTech techniques on banks’ cyber and systemic risks
Authors: Hussein Ahmed Alshari, Ebrahim M Almogahed, Mazen M Al-mekhlafi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid advancement of financial technology (FinTech) mandates a thorough investigation into its adverse effects, focusing on cyber risks and systemic vulnerabilities. This study aims to examine the impact of digital FinTech techniques, specifically artificial intelligence, big data, cloud computing, and blockchain technology, on cyber and systemic risks in Yemeni banks. Data for the study was collected through a questionnaire distributed to 332 respondents at the managerial level. The model’s constructs were validated using structural equation modeling with Partial Least Squares (PLS). The findings indicate that implementing digital FinTech techniques in banks significantly influences cyber risks. Among these techniques, blockchain technology has the most substantial impact on cyber risks, followed by big data technology. However, only blockchain technology affects systemic risks. This research is vital for professionals, individuals interested in FinTech development, and decision-makers who aim to manage the cyber and systemic risks associated with FinTech innovations.Keywords: FinTech, Cyber risks, Systemic risks, Artificial Intelligent, Big data, Cloud computing, BlockchainSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

FinTech is one of the more recent buzzwords that combines financial services with contemporary digital breakthroughs, including artificial intelligence, big data, cloud computing, the Internet of Things, and blockchain. The global investment in FinTech in 2016 was $24.7 billion1, and in 2018 is $111.8 billion2, a rise of $87.2 billion and a rate growth of 352%. The investments in FinTech in 2021 are $210 billion3, a rise of $98.2 billion in 2018, at a rate increase of 46.76%, indicating an acceleration in FinTech investment. Many clients still have faith in financial organizations like banks, which were among the original providers of financial services. FinTech adoption in banks has several benefits, including higher earnings, financial inclusion, and competitive advantage4.

Banks provide financial services by digital means through the adoption of FinTech techniques by banks, but the banks face risks. Therefore, it is crucial to understand how adopting digital techniques has affected these risks. The objectives of this study are to analyze the overall impact of adopting digital FinTech techniques in banks, including artificial intelligence (AI), big data, cloud computing, and blockchain, on cyber and systemic risks. Additionally, the study aims to evaluate the specific effects of each individual FinTech technique on these risks.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has many applications across various industries, including the financial and banking sectors. In banking, AI is a substitute for human-provided services, utilizing technological electronic services such as AI-driven applications in automated teller machines (ATMs)5. Integrating AI brings about increased dependability and enhanced credit assessment for banks. Moreover, AI aids banks in acquiring customer data, particularly for individuals without an established credit history, by analyzing data from sources like social media and web browser histories. This comprehensive understanding of customers enables banks to assess their creditworthiness more effectively5. One of the prominent advantages of AI in the financial sector lies in its ability to reduce and combat cyber-attacks and financial crimes. AI systems are crucial in identifying and preventing activities like money laundering while offering predictive capabilities for future conditions. Additionally, AI facilitates financial services to customers through user-friendly electronic channels, ensuring fast and efficient transactions. This integration of AI not only improves operational efficiency but also reduces costs while maintaining a high level of reliability6. However, AI integrating technologies in banking increases the complexity and connectivity of systems, which can amplify vulnerabilities to cyber-attacks. AI in financial systems often involves deploying machine learning algorithms, neural networks, and other sophisticated technologies that rely on extensive data collection and real-time processing. This increased reliance on interconnected digital systems can create new entry points for cyber-attacks and heighten financial institutions’ overall cyber risk profile7.

Big data has recently emerged as a significant digital application and program driven by the vast amount of daily data that requires analysis to extract its benefits. “Big data involves the examination of extensive, unstructured datasets to identify patterns, market trends, and customer behaviours and preferences, enabling businesses to make rapid and informed predictions”5,6. Big data’s appeal lies in its speed, scale, and diversity characteristics. The sheer size of the data and the need to store and preserve it are crucial factors5. Equally important are the speed of accessing data and the ability to retrieve it promptly, as these are vital considerations in big data. The diversity of data types offers customers various options, such as different methods of accessing and paying for the data. This diverse range of data provides multiple details and fields that require analysis8. These three considerations form the critical aspects of big data. Furthermore, data analysis is essential to extract valuable insights and enable future predictions5. Businesses have recognized the value of big data, leading to increased investments in this field. 62.5% of companies invested in big data, compared to 31.5% in 2013. Financial services firms, in particular, have embraced big data, with 69.6% confirming its meaningful contribution to their business success6. However, the extensive data collection and analysis also introduce potential cyber risks due to increased data exposure and complexity9. Systemic risk refers to the potential for a disturbance in a financial institution to spread throughout the entire financial system. Big data analytics can enhance the detection of systemic threats by identifying correlations and anomalies across different institutions and markets. However, the interconnectedness and dependencies created by big data systems can also exacerbate systemic risks. A cyber-attack or technical failure in a big data infrastructure can potentially disrupt multiple interconnected institutions simultaneously, amplifying the systemic impact10.

Cloud computing offers on-demand services for applications, storage, and processing power, encompassing various aspects such as software development platforms, servers, networks, artificial intelligence, standard applications, and more. These services are accessed via the Internet11. Users can enjoy several advantages through cloud computing, including cost efficiency, scalability, flexibility, integration capabilities, speed, auditing and compliance features, and business continuity planning. From a financial technology perspective, cloud computing brings significant benefits to users. It facilitates cost reduction and enhances efficiency across various FinTech applications and systems. Moreover, it plays a crucial role in expanding and diversifying consumer access to financial services, enabling real-time responsiveness to their evolving needs and preferences5. However, it is essential to note that security concerns pose a drawback to cloud computing5. Cloud environments are prime targets for cyber-attacks due to their vast amounts of sensitive data12. Their complexity and interconnectedness make them susceptible to advanced persistent threats (APTs), posing a continuous threat to security. Additionally, insider threats remain a critical concern since employees with access to cloud systems can unintentionally or maliciously compromise data security13.

Blockchain technology is a recent innovation in business and cryptocurrencies known for its exceptional security features8. The blockchain system consists of two types of records: the transaction log, which stores the operations performed at a specific time within a block, and the block register, where each block handles the encryption process for transaction operations5. The data from each block is compressed by creating a chain, making it detectable if any tampering occurs, thereby ensuring the security of the data6. Additionally, blockchain technology embraces decentralization, as each computer hosting the blockchain program contains a copy of the blockchain. So this enables the continuous operation of transactions without relying on a central server. Furthermore, the immutability of previous transactions and user privacy protection is maintained, as no individual can replace or modify any past transaction5. Utilizing blockchain technology in banking services has proven more effective than traditional banking systems. It offers enhanced security and accuracy in the encryption process, making it challenging for hackers and cybercriminals to interfere with the chains or decipher the codes. Thus, blockchain technology ensures a higher level of security compared to conventional systems. Moreover, blockchain can address inherent issues within traditional banking systems5,6. Although blockchain technology is highly secure, it is not completely impervious to cyber threats. Vulnerabilities in smart contract code and the potential attacks pose significant risks. Moreover, if public and private keys used for transaction authentication are lost or stolen, it can lead to security breaches14.

This study’s problem is to examine whether banks adopting digital FinTech techniques face systemic and cyber risks. This situation has prompted researcher to investigate the nature and impact of these risks associated with adopting FinTech techniques in the banking sector. The research problem summarized in the research questions: How do adopting digital FinTech techniques influence cyber and systemic risks in Yemeni banks? Which digital FinTech technique has the most significant impact on cyber and systemic risks in Yemeni banks? The results of this study will answer this.

This study is highly significant for experts and stakeholders in the financial technology sector. It provides critical insights for decision-makers on the impact of adopting digital FinTech techniques on systemic and cyber risks. Moreover, the study identifies which specific digital techniques most significantly affect these risks, offering a precise understanding of the relative impact of each technique.

This study is unique in that it thoroughly examines the cyber and systemic risks encountered by banks in Yemen adopting FinTech techniques. It emphasizes the importance of implementing preventive measures, distinguishing it from prior research. The findings offer valuable insights for banks, and other relevant authorities, aiding them in informed decision-making. Moreover, this study is a crucial reference for future research on adopting financial technology and its associated risks. Overall, it significantly contributes to financial technology innovation, underscoring the necessity of proactive risk management.

Review of literature

FinTech and its risks

Previous research suggests FinTech approaches are examples of contemporary digital technologies, including blockchain, big data, cloud computing, the Internet of things, and artificial intelligence5,15,16. These methods, which offer their services in an automated electronic manner, are regarded as one of the most essential innovations in FinTech. The Financial Innovations Report published by Kuwait in 2019 explained the extent to which banks’ use of FinTech, including blockchain, big data, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence, might be measured17. Using their initiative, banks have started offering the FinTech services mentioned in FinTech payment, P2P lending, crowdfunding, robo-advisors, wealth-tech, and insure-tech.

In 2016, Prescott and Larousse documented that cybersecurity and data confidentiality risks are strongly correlated and pose potential threats to the use and adoption of FinTech techniques18,19. The study20 states that FinTech is “particularly vulnerable to cyber-attacks due to its reliance on technology. The financial losses, reputational damage, and economic impact of such cyber-attacks have skyrocketed in recent years21. Recent studies have shown that over 50% of financial institutions worldwide face cybersecurity threats daily22. That is why cyber risks are a significant concern for FinTech companies and financial institutions, as traditional banks that provide services to financial technology have become more vulnerable to cyber-attacks in recent years23,24. So this calls for taking additional measures to ensure cyber security, especially when adopting digital financial technology technologies and those operating in the internet space, where cyber security rules must be implemented efficiently to repel advanced cyber-attacks25.

Cyber risks increase by associating several financial institutions with unified systems and weak communication, growing systemic risks26. Once a link encounters problems due to a technical error or malicious attack, it will cause the entire system to fail27. Systemic risks also occur when financial institutions adopt unified digital technologies, thus providing more digital solutions to more access points that hackers can target. In this case, targeting any financial institution affects the system, increasing systemic risks28. Systemic risks will heavily impact the financial system and the real economy29. The studies30,31 confirmed that FinTech increases systemic risk. While the study32 argued that FinTech companies do not contribute to systemic risks. Therefore, the research gap identified in this study is the need to determine the extent to which the adoption of digital FinTech technologies impacts systemic and cyber risks in the banking sector. Specifically, this study seeks to evaluate both the overall influence of these technologies and the individual effects of artificial intelligence, big data, cloud computing, and blockchain on these risks, whether the impact is negative or positive. This independent study significantly contributes to the literature on FinTech by exploring the systemic and cyber risks associated with the adoption of digital technologies in banks. Model development and hypotheses justification

Artificial intelligence

Studies33–35 show that digital FinTech technologies generally reduce risks and credit risks specifically. However, only a few studies have addressed the extent of the impact of banks’ adoption of FinTech technologies on cyber risks. Some studies36 have indicated that progress in artificial intelligence technologies increases cyber risks and threats due to the wireless connection of virtual networks and devices, increasing the complexity of personal, institutional, or national security. Studies7,37 indicate that advancing AI technologies significantly amplifies cyber threats. Integrating machine learning algorithms and neural networks in financial systems necessitates extensive data collection and real-time processing. This dependence on interconnected digital infrastructures creates additional entry points for cyber-attacks and elevates financial institutions’ overall cyber risk profile. Conversely, AI technologies in financial services have shown significant potential to mitigate cyber risks by enhancing threat detection, prediction, and response capabilities. For instance,38 emphasizes the efficacy of machine learning methods in intrusion detection within financial systems. Similarly,39 highlights the efficiency of AI-driven malware analysis. Review deep learning methods for cybersecurity, underscoring their significant contribution to improving threat detection and response capabilities40. Therefore, the study hypothesized the following:

H1

Banks’ adoption of Artificial Intelligence has a statistically significant impact on cyber risks.

AI technologies, particularly in trading algorithms, can contribute to market volatility. High-frequency trading driven by AI can lead to sudden and significant market movements, increasing systemic risk. The reliance on AI systems can introduce operational risks. System failures or incorrect algorithmic decisions can disrupt banking operations, leading to systemic disruptions41. AI can create new forms of interconnectedness within the financial system, which can lead to cascading failures. For example, an AI-driven decision in one financial institution can have far-reaching impacts on other institutions due to their interconnected nature42. Therefore, the study hypothesized the following:

H2

Banks’ adoption of Artificial Intelligence statistically significantly impacts systemic risks.

Big data

Studies43,44 show that adopting big data technologies and their analysis helps manage the risks that financial institutions may face. The adoption of big data technologies in the financial sector presents significant opportunities as well as challenges. Big data can improve risk management by offering insights into market trends, customer behavior, and operational efficiencies. Studies by45,46 demonstrate how big data analytics can enhance risk management practices in financial institutions, providing tools to predict and mitigate various risks. However, big data collection and analysis can also introduce potential cyber risks due to increased data exposure and complexity; these technologies can also heighten cyber vulnerabilities. As discussed by47, the increased volume of data handled and the need for real-time processing and analysis can create more entry points for cyber-attacks. Therefore, I want to know the impact of the adoption of big data on cyber risks positively or negatively, this study hypothesized the following:

H3

Banks’ adoption of big data has a statistically significant impact on cyber risks.

Big data technologies can affect systemic risks in the financial sector by enhancing the detection of threats and introducing new vulnerabilities. Research10 suggests that while big data can help predict systemic risk events, it can also exacerbate systemic risks due to interconnectedness and dependencies, this highlights the importance of managing potential disruptions, such as cyber-attacks or technical failures in big data infrastructure, which could have a widespread impact on interconnected institutions simultaneously. The study hypothesized the following:

H4

Banks’ adoption of big data has a statistically significant impact on systemic risks.

Cloud computing

Studies48,49 indicated that cloud computing has negatively affected the security of data and information, which is one of the drawbacks of this technology. While some studies50,51 indicate that cloud computing may help maintain the confidentiality and security of information. Previous studies show that security became more crucial with the development of cloud computing due to cyber risks grew52. More stringent security measures are needed as more full data nodes with less software diversity are produced due to cloud computing53. Endogenous dangers to such nodes come from unauthorized access to systems by users or employees, compromises of company or employee information, or across them. External threats include breaches from interacting with other third-party systems or using fraudulently acquired privileged account credentials to access data and perform transactions. Both threats constitute several concentric layers of security risk54. So, this study hypothesized the following:

H5

Banks’ adoption of cloud computing has a statistically significant impact on cyber risks.

Numerous studies show that cloud computing in finance brings benefits like improved efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and systemic risks. Centralizing data and services in the cloud can create vulnerabilities, posing potential widespread implications if compromised55. For instance, disruptions to major cloud providers could simultaneously impact multiple financial institutions, leading to systemic crises56. The Financial Stability Board57 has highlighted the systemic risks associated with cloud computing in the financial sector, noting that the concentration of cloud service providers amplifies these operational disruptions, underscoring the need to understand and mitigate the systemic risks in the banking industry.

H6

Banks’ adoption of cloud computing has a statistically significant impact on systemic risks.

Blockchain technology

Blockchain technology provides robust security features that can significantly mitigate cyber risks. The decentralized and immutable nature of blockchain ensures that data recorded on the blockchain is resistant to tampering and unauthorized access. Recent studies have highlighted the security benefits of blockchain in financial services, including transparency58, reduced operations time59, reduced costs, speed of work60,61, and contribution to preserving data and information62. Also, Liu et al.14 explain that blockchain’s cryptographic techniques ensure data integrity and security, making it challenging for cyber attackers to alter transaction records.

However, blockchain is not entirely immune to cyber threats. Vulnerabilities in smart contracts, where most of the network’s mining power is controlled by a single entity, pose significant risks. Some studies5,63,64 discuss these vulnerabilities and the importance of robust security measures to mitigate such risks. Therefore, the study hypothesized the following:

H7

Banks’ adoption of blockchain has a statistically significant impact on cyber risks.

Blockchain technology can contribute to systemic stability in the financial sector by providing a transparent and tamper-proof ledger, enhancing trust among financial institutions, and reducing the risk of fraud. For instance, Li et al.65 highlight that blockchain’s transparency and immutability can lead to more secure and efficient interbank transactions and settlements. However, the adoption of blockchain can also introduce systemic risks. The interconnectedness of blockchain networks and the potential for operational failures can have cascading effects on the financial system. Chen et al.66 discuss the systemic risks associated with blockchain, including the centralization of control by major mining pools and the potential for network disruptions. These risks necessitate careful consideration and robust regulatory frameworks to ensure systemic stability. Therefore, the study hypothesized the following:

H8

Banks’ adoption of blockchain has a statistically significant impact on systemic risks.

The model’s Goodness of Fit (GoF) was assessed using criteria developed by77, which classify the GoF as Not fit, Small, Medium, or Large. Discussions

Artificial Intelligence → Cyber Risks (H1). This result for the artificial intelligence on impact of cyber risks (β = 0.204, p = 0.022) supported, indicating a significant positive influence between adopting FinTech techniques on banks like artificial intelligence and cyber risks. That means the increase in the adoption of artificial intelligence will increase cyber risks. This result argues with other research that indicated that artificial intelligence helps to reduce cyber security risks78. The study’s findings align with research79 highlighting AI’s dual nature in cybersecurity. AI is beneficial but also introduces new vulnerabilities. For example, AI systems can be targeted by cyber-attacks that exploit machine learning algorithms, leading to compromised security. Additionally, the research80 indicates that in financial technology (FinTech), AI enhances operational efficiency and opens new avenues for cyber risks, as these systems handle large volumes of sensitive data, making them attractive targets for cybercriminals. Therefore, financial institutions must balance the benefits of AI with the need for enhanced cybersecurity protocols to mitigate these emerging risks. This result’ contributes to the on-going debate by highlighting the importance of developing comprehensive cybersecurity strategies that address the unique challenges posed by AI adoption in the banking sector. Future research should continue exploring these dynamics, focusing on leveraging AI’s benefits while minimizing its security vulnerabilities.

Artificial Intelligence → Systemic Risks (H2). The findings concerning artificial intelligence’s impact on systemic risks are nonsupport (p, 0.05). The hypothesis (H2) is rejected, which shows no significant impact of adopting artificial intelligence in banks on systemic risks. This result tells us that the adoption of artificial intelligence techniques in banks does not have a beneficial effect on systemic risks; this means that banking systemic risks are not affected by the adoption of artificial intelligence technologies by banks, which may lead to the possibility of risks affecting other banks and even the entire banking system in the event of the failure of one or more of the banks, which leads to the cessation of the work of the bank or the banking sector. This result aligns with recent studies, such as81, which found that AI enhances operational efficiencies and does not inherently reduce systemic risk factors. The ethical and systemic implications of AI in finance are complex and often leave core systemic vulnerabilities unaddressed82. Additionally83, emphasized that systemic risks are deeply embedded in the financial system’s structure, influenced by regulatory environments and market dynamics, which AI alone cannot comprehensively address. While AI has numerous advantages, its adoption does not appear to significantly impact systemic risks, highlighting the need for complementary strategies to address these vulnerabilities.

Big Data → Cyber Risks (H3). The result of big data on effects of cyber risks (β = 0.091, p = 0.014). The hypothesis (H3) result is supported, indicating significant positive path coefficients signifying the influence between big data and cyber risks on the banks. The results also showed that big data ranked second in impact among digital FinTech technologies on cyber risks, which indicates that adopting big data technologies, except blockchain technologies, may positively impact cyber risks more than other FinTech technologies. In other words, the more extensive data technologies are adopted, the more cyber risks increase significantly than the impact of different digital FinTech technologies, except the blockchain. This result supports findings from84, who noted that the vast amounts of data generated and processed by big data technologies create more opportunities for cyber threats and attacks. Similarly85, emphasized that the complexity and volume of big data pose significant challenges for cybersecurity, as they can overwhelm existing security measures and create vulnerabilities. Moreover47, highlighted that integrating big data technologies in financial institutions often leads to increased data breaches and cyber-attacks due to the extensive data flow and storage. Therefore, while big data technologies offer valuable insights and efficiencies, their adoption necessitates robust cybersecurity measures to mitigate the associated risks.

Although blockchain technology has brought many outstanding advantages, including maintaining data security and controlling to not changing it, it has vulnerabilities that lead to cyber risks. Blockchain → Systemic Risks (H6). The hypothesis is supported to explore the influence of blockchain techniques on systemic risks. The results of the hypothesis (H6) blockchain → systemic risks (β = 0.350, p = 0.002). Blockchain has a significant positive effect on systemic risks. That means adopting blockchain techniques in banks increases the systemic risks which may affect one banking system and extend to another. The enhanced interconnectivity and complexity introduced by blockchain can create vulnerabilities, as a failure or attack on one part of the network can propagate through the system. Empirical evidence supports this, with studies like90 showing blockchain networks’ susceptibility to manipulation and attacks with systemic consequences. Blockchain’s distributed nature can create a false sense of security, leading to overlooked vulnerabilities. Moreover, the immutability of blockchain can pose a risk in financial contexts where reversibility is often necessary for error correction and fraud prevention. Additionally, blockchain technology’s reliance on consensus mechanisms, such as proof-of-work or proof-of-stake, can introduce systemic risks if these mechanisms are compromised. Cloud Computing → Systemic Risks (H8). The hypothesis is rejected to propose the influence of adopting cloud computing on systemic risks in banks, (H8) cloud computing → systemic risks (β = 0.037, p = 0.463). This result means that adopting cloud computing technologies in banks does not positively affect systemic risks; this indicates that adopting cloud computing technology in a bank does not affect the systems of other banks in terms of risks. While cloud computing might pose direct cybersecurity challenges to individual institutions, these do not necessarily translate into systemic risks that could destabilize the entire financial system. This finding aligns with the perspective that systemic risks are more likely triggered by interconnected financial dependencies and large-scale operational failures rather than isolated technological adoptions. Literature supports this finding. Cloud computing enhances operational resilience and disaster recovery, mitigating potential systemic disruptions suggested by99. Buyya100 highlight that the distributed nature of cloud infrastructure compartmentalizes risks, preventing localized issues from becoming systemic crises. Additionally, a Financial Stability Board (FSB, 2019) report emphasizes that cloud-related operational risks are primarily institution-specific rather than systemic, underlining the importance of robust regulatory frameworks and risk management57.

Conclusions

Conclusions

The study successfully achieved its objective of revealing the impact of adopting digital FinTech technologies on cyber and systemic risks in banks. The analysis demonstrated that all the technologies examined, artificial intelligence, big data, cloud computing and blockchain, contribute to increased cyber risks. Among these, blockchain technology emerged as the most significant contributor to cyber risks, followed by big data, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence. This suggests that these technologies offer numerous advantages but also introduce vulnerabilities that banks must address to enhance their cybersecurity measures.

In terms of systemic risks, the study found that blockchain adoption has a significant positive impact, indicating that it can heighten systemic risks within the banking system. This result contrasts with the findings for artificial intelligence, big data, and cloud computing, which did not significantly influence systemic risks. The unique nature of blockchain, with its decentralized and immutable ledger, may introduce complexities that affect the broader financial system, highlighting the need for comprehensive risk management frameworks when integrating blockchain into banking operations.

The study underscores the dual-edged nature of digital FinTech technologies in the banking sector. While they offer potential improvements in efficiency and service delivery, they also pose significant risks that need to be managed. Banks must balance adopting these technologies with robust cybersecurity and systemic risk mitigation strategies to ensure stability and security in their operations. The study provides a foundational reference for future research regarding digital FinTech techniques with cyber and systemic risks and practical insights for policymakers and banking professionals to navigate the evolving landscape of digital finance.

Limitations and Future research

This research met its aims, although it has some the following limitations:

While the study employs a predictive-explanatory framework, it is governed by a cross-sectional design, with data collected between August and December 2023. This approach provides a valuable snapshot of the relationships between variables but limits the ability to draw definitive causal interpretations or observe temporal shifts compared to longitudinal research. Consequently, the findings represent predictive associations rather than absolute cause-and-effect.

This study was carried out on the effect of adopting digital FinTech techniques on cyber and systemic risks. Future studies can implement its impact on other risks, such as operational and outsourcing risks. This investigation used a survey to collect and analyse data. Future studies can use the data from reports issued by financial institutions and compare the results with those of this study. This study was conducted in Yemen, one of the least developed countries with economic and political problems. Future research may include this study in developed nations with stable financial and economic conditions. The current study was conducted in the banking sector. Future studies can examine the adoption of digital techniques such as blockchain, big data, the Internet of Things, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence over risks in other sectors.

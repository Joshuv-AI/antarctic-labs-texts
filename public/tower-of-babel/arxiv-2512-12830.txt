Ribbonlength bounds for pretzel links and knots with ≤ 9 \leq 9 crossings
Elizabeth Denne

Abstract

Before we give a formal definition of a folded ribbon knot, we remind readers that a
polygonal knot diagram
is the image of a piecewise linear immersion of
K:S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
with consistent crossing information. We use
K
to denote both the map and its image in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We denote the finite number of vertices in the polygonal diagram by
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
and the edges connecting consecutive vertices by
e_{1}=[v_{1},v_{2}],\dots,e_{n}=[v_{n},v_{1}]
. If the knot is oriented, we assume the labeling of the vertices follows the orientation.

When we consider folded ribbon knots (defined formally below), we see that near a fold line there is a choice of which piece of ribbon lies over the other. We say there is an
overfold
at vertex
v_{i}
if the ribbon corresponding to edge
e_{i}
lies over the ribbon of edge
e_{i-1}
. Similarly, an
underfold
if the ribbon corresponding to edge
e_{i}
lies under the ribbon corresponding to edge
e_{i-1}
. In the center image in Figure
1
, there is an overfold at vertex
p
and an underfold at vertex
q
. This choice of over or underfold at each fold line of
K_{w}
is called the
folding information
. We construct a folded ribbon link from a polygonal link diagram as follows (see also
[
6
,
9
]

Definition 1
.

Given an oriented polygonal knot diagram
K
, we define the
fold angle

\theta_{i}
at vertex

(where
0\leq\theta_{i}\leq\pi
) to be the angle between edges
e_{i-1}
and
e_{i}
. The
oriented folded ribbon knot of width
w

is constructed as follows.

(1)

At each vertex we place a fold line that acts like a mirror segment. That is at each vertex
v_{i}
, if the fold angle
\theta_{i}<\pi
, place a fold line of length
w/\cos(\frac{\theta_{i}}{2})
centered at

perpendicular to the angle bisector of
\theta_{i}
. If
\theta_{i}=\pi
at vertex

, there is no fold line.

(2)

Add in the ribbon boundary lines parallel to each edge
e_{i}
by joining the ends of the fold lines at
v_{i}
. By construction, each boundary line is parallel to and at distance
w/2
from
K
.

(3)

The ribbon has no singularities (is immersed) except at the fold lines, which are assumed to be disjoint.

(4)

K_{w}
has consistent crossing information, which agrees with the crossing information given by the knot diagram
K
and with the folding information at each vertex.

(5)

The ribbon inherits the orientation from
K

A
folded ribbon link
L_{w}
is defined similarly, as each component is a folded ribbon knot.

We observe that for a given polygonal knot diagram, if the width is too large, then the fold lines of consecutive vertices may touch. In
[
8
]
, we prove that every polygonal link diagram has a corresponding folded ribbon link for a small enough width.

A natural question is to try to work out the least amount of ribbon needed to tie any folded ribbon link. This is the folded ribbonlength problem. To model this mathematically, we need a scale-invariant quantity, called the folded ribbonlength.

Definition 2
.

The
folded ribbonlength

{\operatorname{Rib}}(L_{w})
of a folded ribbon link
L_{w}
is the quotient of the length of the link diagram
L
to the width
w
of the ribbon, that is
{\operatorname{Rib}}(L_{w})=\frac{{\operatorname{Len}}(L)}{w}
. The
(infimal) folded ribbonlength
of a link type
[L]
is defined to be

{\operatorname{Rib}}([L])=\inf_{L_{w}\in[L]}{\operatorname{Rib}}(L_{w}).

To simplify computations, we often assume that our folded ribbon link has width
w=1
. The folded ribbonlength is then just the length of the link. Observe that we just consider the link type when finding the infimal folded ribbonlength for a link. We do not consider the topological type of the ribbon (Möbius band or annulus), nor do we consider the framing of the ribbon around the knot.

When computing folded ribbonlength it is helpful to use the local geometry and trigonometry of folds and crossings — complete details can be found in
[
8
]
. As an example of this, we observe that locally, a fold in a piece of ribbon consists of two overlapping triangles, while a crossing consists of two overlapping parallelograms. The ribbonlength of any square ribbon segment is
1

\theta=\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}
, we get two overlapping isosceles right triangles, each with a ribbonlength of
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. This
\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}
-fold has a total ribbonlength of
1
.

Half-twists and pretzel links

One of the defining characteristics of pretzel links is that they are made from strands of half-twists that are joined together in a specific way. A
strand of
k
half-twists
is constructed by taking two parallel straight arcs and rotating the bottom ends of the arcs by
\pi
radians. If
k
is positive, then ends of the arcs are twisted in a counterclockwise direction and if
k
is negative, the ends of the arcs are twisted in a clockwise direction. There is a choice here, and other texts may use the opposite convention. In knot diagrams, a strand of
k
half-twists is sometimes replaced by a box with the integer
k
. This is shown on the left in Figure
2
, while on the right we see three strands of differently oriented half-twists joined to give a

pretzel knot.

Remark 8
.

We review some well-known facts about pretzel links (see for instance
[
5
,
17
]
).

(1)

A pretzel link
P(p,q,r)
is a knot if and only if at most one of
p
,
q
and
r
are even.

(2)

A pretzel link
P(p,q,r)
is alternating if and only if
p,q
and
r
have the same sign.

(3)

The mirror image
P^{m}(p,q,r)
of a pretzel link is equivalent to
P(-p,-q,-r)
.

(4)

A pretzel link

is equivalent to a pretzel link with any permutation of
p
,
q
and
r
. For example,
P(p,q,r)\cong P(p,r,q)\cong P(q,r,p)
.

(5)

The crossing number of a pretzel link can be hard to find in general. We have the following specific cases:

(a)

If
p,q,r
have the same sign, then
P(p,q,r)
has a reduced alternating diagram, so
{\operatorname{Cr}}(P(p,q,r))=|p|+|q|+|r|
.

(b)

The crossing number of a pretzel link

where
p,q,r\geq 2
is given by
{\operatorname{Cr}}(P(-p,q,r))=p+q+r
(see
[
23
]
).

In particular, we introduce the notation needed to describe folded ribbon half-twists, as well as several technical results used in the construction of a pretzel link.

Remark 21
.

Suppose that integers

and

have the same parity and that
|p|<|r|
. Suppose we have constructed both

. Observe that we can add an even number (

) of accordion folds to the ends

and

so that the

and

Generalized Bohr inequalities for K-quasiconformal harmonic mappings and their applications
Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. The classical Bohr theorem and its subsequent generalizations have become active areas of research, with investigations conducted in numerous function spaces. Let { ψ n ​ ( r ) } n = 0 ∞ \{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty} be a sequence of non-negative continuous functions defined on [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) such that the series ∑ n = 0 ∞ ψ n ​ ( r ) \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r) converges locally uniformly on the interval [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) . The main objective of this paper is to establish several sharp versions of generalized Bohr inequalities for the class of K K -quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mappings on the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } {\mathbb{D}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . To achieve these, we employ the sequence of functions { ψ n ​ ( r ) } n = 0 ∞ \{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty} in the majorant series rather than the conventional dependence on the basis sequence { r n } n = 0 ∞ \{r^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty} . As applications, we derive a number of previously published results as well as a number of sharply improved and refined Bohr inequalities for harmonic mappings in 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} . Moreover, we obtain a convolution counterpart of the Bohr theorem for harmonic mapping within the context of the Gaussian hypergeometric function.

Introduction and Preliminaries

In a noteworthy discovery, H. Bohr
[
15
]
demonstrated that if
f
is a bounded analytic function on the open unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
with the supremum norm

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|
and the Taylor series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

It is observed that, if
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z_{1})|=1
for some point
z_{1}\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f(z)
reduces to a unimodular constant function by the maximum
modulus principle. In this context, the quantity
1/3
is known as the Bohr radius and is regarded as an optimal value that cannot be improved further. Meanwhile, the inequality
(
1.1
) is known as the Bohr inequality.
The inequality (
1.1
) was originally proved by Bohr
[
15
]
for
r\leq 1/6
. Subsequently, the improved value
1/3
was obtained independently by Weiner, Riesz, and Schur (see
[
21
]
).
The function
f(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
demonstrates that
1/3
is optimal for
a\to 1^{-}
.
For further insight, see the survey articles by Abu-Muhanna
et al.

[
1
]
, Garcia
et al.
(see
[
23
, Chapter 8]
), and the highly informative monograph by Defant
et al.

[
20
]
on the Bohr phenomenon in the context of modern analysis of more general settings.

The concept of the Bohr phenomenon can be generalized to the class
{\mathcal{G}}
, which consists of analytic functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
.
These functions map from the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
into a given domain
U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
such that
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq U
.
The class
{\mathcal{G}}
is said to satisfy the Bohr phenomenon if there exists a largest number
r_{U}\in(0,1)
such that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for
|z|=r\leq r_{U}
.
In this context, the quantity
r_{U}
is referred to as the Bohr radius for the class
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Boas and Khavinson
[
14
]
contributed to the revival of interest in this topic by further developing the concept of the Bohr radius in the context of several complex variables.
Furthermore, the authors have identified the multidimensional Bohr radius as a significant contribution to this field of research.
Moreover, Dixon
[
21
]
employed the classical Bohr theorem to develop a Banach algebra that is not categorized as an operator algebra but nevertheless satisfies the
non-unital von Neumann inequality. Subsequently, several researchers have followed up and investigated this phenomenon in a more general abstract setting across various contexts
(see
[

,

10
]
).
In particular, following the publication of the articles
[

and
[

, researchers have explored a number of approaches and novel problems pertaining to Bohr’s inequality in the plane (see
[

]

1.1.
Bohr-Rogosinski inequality and known important theorems

In addition to the notion of the Bohr radius, there is another concept known as the Rogosinski radius
[
47
]
for bounded analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, which is defined as follows: Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
S_{N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
denotes the partial
sum of
f
. Then,
\left|S_{N}(z)\right|<1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
for
N\geq 1
. The number
1/2
is optimal.
Motivated by the Rogosinski radius, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
30
]
have considered the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
, which is defined by

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}.

Clearly,
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
.
Moreover, the classical Bohr sum (majorant series) associates the Bohr-Rogosinski sum with setting the value of
N
to 1 and substituting
|f(z)|
with
|f(0)|
.
In their article, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
30
]
have defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that the inequality
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
holds for the disk
|z|\leq r_{0}
.

In 2017, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
30
]
obtained the following results about the Bohr-Rogosinski radius for bounded analytic functions in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Theorem A
.

[
30
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq R_{N}
, where
R_{N}\in(0,1)
is the positive root of the equation
\psi_{N}(r)=0
,
\psi_{N}(r)=2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq R_{N}^{\prime}
, where
R_{N}^{\prime}\in(0,1)
is the positive root of the equation
(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}^{\prime}
is the best possible.

In light of the work of Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
30
]
, Liu
et al.

[
39

have investigated several results on Bohr-type inequality. Here is one of them.

Theorem B
.

[

Let

be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f^{\prime}(z)||z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq(\sqrt{17}-3)/4.

The radius
(\sqrt{17}-3)/4
is the best possible.

In addition, a number of authors have investigated other extensions of this kind (see
[

36
,
28
]
). Before proceeding with the discussion, and in order to contextualize the recent results, it is essential to introduce the requisite notations. Let
h
be an analytic function
in
{\mathbb{D}}
and
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<r\}
for
0<r<1
. Let
S_{r}(h)
denotes the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

If

, then it is well known that
S_{r}(h)/\pi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}
and if

is univalent, then

is the area of the image
h(\mathbb{D}_{r})
.

In 2018, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
31
]
obtained the following improved versions of Bohr’s inequality for the bounded analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem C
.

[
31
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
,
|f(z)|\leq 1
and

denotes the area of the image of the subdisk

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

The numbers

,

cannot be improved. Moreover,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{2},

and the numbers
1/2
,
9/8
cannot be improved.

Subsequently, Ismagilov

.

[

1.2.
Basic notations

In order to present our results in an organized and coherent manner, it is necessary to introduce some basic notations at this preliminary stage of the discussion.

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function of
z=x+iy
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f
satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
in
\Omega
, then
f
is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
. In other words,
u
and
v
are real harmonic in
\Omega
. Note that every harmonic mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and

g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known respectively as the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
, and
\overline{g(z)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
g(z)
. This representation is
unique up to an additive constant (see
[
22
]
). The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
35
]
shows that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in

\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
is non-zero in
\Omega
. A harmonic mapping
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving
in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
or equivalently if
h^{\prime}\not=0
in
\Omega

and the dilatation
\omega_{f}:=\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
of
f
has the property that
|\omega_{f}|<1
in
\Omega
(see
[
35
]
).

If a locally univalent and sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
satisfies the condition
|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)|\leq k<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is said to be

K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
27
,
40
]
). Clearly,
k\to 1
corresponds to the limiting case
K\to\infty
.

The generalization of the Bohr radius is an important study in understanding the Bohr phenomenon for various classes of functions. The idea involves replacing the basis sequence
\{r^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
by a more generalized sequence in the majorant series.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all sequences
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of non-negative continuous functions in
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)

converges locally uniformly on the interval
[0,1)
. For an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{r^{2n}}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r^{2n+1}}{1-r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+|a_{0}|}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right)

for
\psi_{k}(r)=r^{k}
, where
\Psi_{t}(r)=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)
.

1.3.
A generalization of the classical Bohr theorem

In a pioneering contribution to the literature, Kayumov
et al.

[
29
]
employed a different approach in their investigation of Bohr-type inequalities. Instead of relying on
the traditional use of the basis sequence
\{r^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, they introduced a new sequence of functions
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. By doing so, they were able to
establish a generalization of the classical Bohr theorem.

Theorem F
.

[
29
]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
and
p\in(0,2]
. If
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)<\psi_{0}(r)\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,R),

where
R
is minimal positive root of the equation
(2/p)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)=\psi_{0}(r)
,
then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)\leq\psi_{0}(r)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq R.

In the case when
(2/p)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)>\psi_{0}(r)
in some interval
(R,R+\varepsilon)
, then the number
R
cannot be improved. If the functions
\psi_{k}(r)

(k\geq 0)
are smooth functions, then the last condition is equivalent to the inequality
(2/p)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}^{\prime}(R)>\psi_{0}^{\prime}(R)
.

Subsequently, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
45
]
carried out further research in this general form and established the following refined generalization of Bohr’s theorem.

Theorem G
.

[
45
]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
and
p\in(0,2]
. If
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)<\psi_{0}(r)\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,R),

where
R
is minimal positive root of the equation
2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)=p\psi_{0}(r)
,
then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+|a_{0}|}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right)\leq\psi_{0}(r)\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq R,

where
\Psi_{t}(r)=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)
.
In the case when
2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)>p\psi_{0}(r)
in some interval
(R,R+\varepsilon)
, then the number
R
cannot be improved.

In light of the aforementioned findings, several questions naturally arise with regard to this study.

Question 1.1
.

Instead of the conventional dependence on the basis sequence
\{r^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, is it possible to establish general results by using the sequence

\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
in the majorant series for harmonic mappings in the settings of Theorems B-E?

Question 1.2
.

Can we establish the harmonic extension of the generalized Bohr inequality in the context of Theorem F?

Question 1.3
.

Can we establish the harmonic extension of the generalized Bohr inequality in refined form in the context of Theorem G?

Question 1.4
.

Can we establish several sharp versions of generalized Bohr inequalities for harmonic mappings in the context of Theorems F and G?

The purpose of this paper is primarily to answer Questions
1.1
-
1.4
in the affirmative.

The organization of the remaining part of this paper is: In Section
2
, we present a number of lemmas and a proof of one of these lemmas, which are essential for the proof of our main theorems. In Section
3
, we establish a sharp result that generalizes the Bohr inequality in an improved form for harmonic mappings. As a consequence, we establish a few
corollaries, remarks, and several sharply improved Bohr inequalities in the context of harmonic mappings. Furthermore, we establish a convolution counterpart of the Bohr theorem for
harmonic mapping within the context of the Gaussian hypergeometric function. In Section 4, we obtain a number of sharp results that generalize the Bohr inequality in a refined form
for harmonic mappings. As a consequence, we establish a few
corollaries, remarks, and several sharply refined Bohr inequalities in the context of harmonic mappings.

2.3

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2n}|\psi_{2n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2n+1}|\psi_{2n+1}(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|a_{k}|^{2}-\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{1+|a_{0}|}\right)\psi_{2n}(r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2n+1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{1+|a_{0}|}\psi_{2n}(r)

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|a_{k}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2n+1}(r)\right).

It is evident that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|a_{k}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2n+1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{1}|^{2}\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)+|a_{2}|^{2}\sum_{k=5}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)+|a_{3}|^{2}\sum_{k=7}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(r)\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{1+|a_{0}|}\psi_{2n}(r)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\Psi_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+|a_{0}|}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Improved generalized Bohr inequality and its applications

Let us consider a polynomial
G(x)
, which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle G(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}x^{t},\quad\text{where}\quad N\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad c_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq t\leq N.

(3.1)

In the following, we establish a sharp result that generalizes the Bohr inequality in an improved form for harmonic mappings.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. If
p\in(0,2]
and
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
is a decreasing sequence and satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(r):=\frac{2K}{K+1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}-\frac{p}{2}\psi_{0}(r)\leq 0

(3.2)

for
r\leq R_{N,K}
,
then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+G\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}(\psi_{n}(r))^{2}\right)\leq\psi_{0}(r)\|h(z)\|_{\infty}

for
r\leq R_{N,K}
,
where
G(x)
is a polynomial defined in (
3.1
) and
R_{N,K}\in(0,1)
is minimal positive root of the equation
\Phi_{1}(r)=0
.
In the case when
\Phi_{1}(r)>0
in some interval
(R_{N,K},R_{N,K}+\varepsilon)

(\varepsilon>0)
, then the number
R_{N,K}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume that
\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. In view of lemma
2.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}

for
n\geq 1
. As
f
is locally univalent and
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, Schwarz’s Lemma gives that the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}

is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|\omega(z)|=|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)|\leq k
in
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
,
k\in[0,1)
. Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
.
In view of Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n}(r)\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n}(r)\leq k^{2}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r).

(3.3)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the inequality (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{1/2}\leq k(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\leq\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}.

Let
F(x)=(1-x^{p})/(1-x^{2})
, where
x\in[0,1)
and
p\in(0,2]
. It is evident that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{xF_{1}(x)}{(1-x^{2})^{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad F_{1}(x)=2-(2-p)x^{p}-px^{p-2}.

Therefore,
F_{1}^{\prime}(x)=(2-p)px^{p-3}(1-x^{2})\geq 0
for
x\in[0,1)
and
p\in(0,2]
, which shows that
F_{1}(x)
is a monotonically increasing function of
x
. Thus, we have
F_{1}(x)\leq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}F_{1}(x)=0
and it’s shows that
F(x)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
x
. Hence, we have
F(x)\geq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}F(x)=p/2
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+G\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}(\psi_{n}(r))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+(1+k)\left(1-a^{2}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left((1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\frac{1-a^{p}}{1-a^{2}}\right)\psi_{0}(r)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left((1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{p}{2}\psi_{0}(r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)F_{1}(a,r),

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
and

\displaystyle F_{1}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}-\frac{p}{2}\psi_{0}(r).

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}F_{1}(a,r)=-2a\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}(2t-1)\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t-2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}\leq 0.

Therefore,
F_{1}(a,r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of

and it follows that

\displaystyle F_{1}(a,r)\leq F_{1}(0,r)=\frac{2K}{K-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}-\frac{p}{2}\psi_{0}(r)=\Phi_{1}(r)\leq 0

for
r\leq R_{N,K}
, under the assumption that (
3.2
) holds.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{1}(z)=h_{1}(z)+\overline{g_{1}(z)}
in

where

,

Thus,

\displaystyle|A_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|k\lambda A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+G\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|A_{n}|^{2}(\psi_{n}(r))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a^{n-1}\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na^{2(n-1)}\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}-(1-a^{p})\psi_{0}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)F_{2}(a,r)+\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na^{2(n-1)}\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle-\left(1-a^{2}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{N}c_{t}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na^{2(n-1)}\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{t},

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
and

\displaystyle F_{2}(a,r)=(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na^{2(n-1)}\left(\psi_{n}(r)\right)^{2}\right)^{k}-\frac{1-a^{p}}{1-a^{2}}\psi_{0}(r).

Letting

, we have

\displaystyle|A_{0}|^{p}\psi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|k\lambda A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+G\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|A_{n}|^{2}(\psi_{n}(r))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\Psi_{1}(r)+O\left(\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}\right).

Since
\Phi_{1}(r)>0
for
(R_{N,K},R_{N,K}+\varepsilon)
, thus the radius cannot be improved. This completes the proof.

3.2.
Convolution counterpart of Bohr theorem for harmonic mappings

To prove our convolution result, we need the following definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

[
48
]
Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
given by
\psi_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
\psi_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
. The convolution (or, Hadamard product) is defined by

\displaystyle\left(\psi_{1}*\psi_{2}\right)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is evident that
\psi_{1}*\psi_{2}=\psi_{2}*\psi_{1}
. In 2002, Goodloe
[
24
]
considered the Hadamard product of a harmonic function with an analytic function as follows:

\displaystyle f\tilde{*}\phi=h*\phi+\overline{g*\phi},

where
f=h+\overline{g}
is a harmonic mapping in
{\mathbb{D}}
, and
\phi
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.

The Gaussian hypergeometric function is a highly significant special function due to its extensive connections with other classes of special functions as specific or limiting cases and its numerous identities and expressions in terms of series and integrals. It is a solution of Euler’s hypergeometric differential equation

\displaystyle z(1-z)y^{\prime\prime}+\left(c-(a+b+1)z\right)y^{\prime}-aby=0,

which has three regular singular points at
0
,
1
and
\infty
.

Definition 3.2
.

For parameters
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
c\not=-m

(m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})
and
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the Gaussian hypergeometric function is defined by

\displaystyle F(a,b,c,z):={}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n},\quad\text{where}\quad\gamma_{n}=\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}(1)_{n}},

which converges for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
and converges on the circle
|z|=1
if
\text{Re}(c-a-b)>0
. Here
(a)_{n}
is the Pochhammer symbol, which is defined by:

\displaystyle(a)_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\text{if}\quad n=0\\
a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+n-1),&\text{if}\quad n>0.\end{array}\right.

It is evident that the Gaussian hypergeometric function
F(a,b;c;z)
is analytic within the domain
{\mathbb{D}}
, and it can also be analytically continued outside the unit circle.
The function reduces to a polynomial when either
a
or
b
is a non-positive integer,
i.e.,

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}(-m,b;c;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^{n}\binom{m}{n}\frac{(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}z^{n}.

Some of the simplest special cases of the hypergeometric functions are

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}(a,1;1;z)=(1-z)^{-a},{}_{2}F_{1}(1,1;2;z)=-\log(1-z)/z.

For further details, we refer to
[
46
]
and the references cited therein.

We consider the convolution operator of a harmonic function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
with an analytic function
F(a,b,c,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left(f\tilde{*}F\right)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}b_{n}z^{n}}.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following sharp Bohr radius for harmonic mappings within the context of the Gaussian hypergeometric function.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in

\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
and
p\in(0,2]
. Let
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be such that
(a+n)(b+n)r-(c+n)(1+n)\leq 0
for
n\geq 0
and
r\in[0,1]
. Then,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{0},

where
r_{0}\in(0,1)
is the minimal positive root of the equation
F(a,b,c,r)-1=p(K+1)/(4K)
. The number
r_{0}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{n}(r)=\gamma_{n}r^{n}
for
n\geq 0
. It is evident that
\gamma_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle F(a,b,c,r)-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}r^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}.

Note that
(a)_{n+1}=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+n)=(a+n)(a)_{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Clearly,
\gamma_{n}=(a)_{n}(b)_{n}/(c)_{n}(1)_{n}>0
for
n\geq 1
, since
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+1}=\frac{(a+n)(b+n)}{(c+n)(1+n)}\gamma_{n}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\psi_{n+1}(r)-\psi_{n}(r)=\gamma_{n+1}r^{n+1}-\gamma_{n}r^{n}=\gamma_{n}r^{n}\left(\frac{(a+n)(b+n)}{(c+n)(1+n)}r-1\right)\leq 0

for
n\geq 0
, by the assumption. Thus,
\{\psi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{F}}
is a decreasing sequence. In view of Corollary
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{n}||a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{n}||b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|h\|_{\infty}

holds for
r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is the minimal positive root of the equation
F(a,b,c,r)-1=p(K+1)/(4K)
.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that

a\in[0,1)
and
g_{4}(z)=\lambda k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
, where
|\lambda|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)

Also

\displaystyle|h_{4}(-r)|\psi_{0}(r)+|h_{4}^{\prime}(-r)|\psi_{1}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\lambda kA_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+|A_{0}|}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+r}{1+ar}\right)\psi_{0}(r)+\left(\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1+ar)^{2}}\right)\psi_{1}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)(1+k)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a^{n-1}\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle+k\left(1-a^{2}\right)\psi_{1}(r)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a^{2n-2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+a}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a)F_{5}(a,r)}{(1+ar)^{2}}+\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a^{2n-2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+a}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right),

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
and

\displaystyle F_{5}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+a\right)\psi_{1}(r)+(1+ar)^{2}\left(1+a\right)\left((1+k)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(r)+k\psi_{1}(r)\right)

\displaystyle-(1-r)(1+ar)\psi_{0}(r).

Letting

, we have

\displaystyle|h_{4}(-r)|\psi_{0}(r)+|h_{4}^{\prime}(-r)|\psi_{1}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|B_{n}|\psi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{2n}(r)}{1+|A_{0}|}+\Psi_{2n+1}(r)\right)\hskip 284.52756pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1+ar)^{2}}\Phi_{5}(r)+O\left(\left(1-a\right)^{2}\right).

Since
\Phi_{5}(r)>0
for
(R_{K},R_{K}+\varepsilon)
, thus the radius cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the relationship between Sobolev extension domains and homogeneous Sobolev extension domains. Precisely, we obtain the following results. • Let 1 ≤ q ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty . Then a bounded ( L 1 , p , L 1 , q ) (L^{1,p},L^{1,q}) -extension domain is also a ( W 1 , p , W 1 , q ) (W^{1,p},W^{1,q}) -extension domain. • Let 1 ≤ q ≤ p < q ⋆ ≤ ∞ 1\leq q\leq p<q^{\star}\leq\infty or n < q ≤ p ≤ ∞ n<q\leq p\leq\infty . Then a bounded domain is a ( W 1 , p , W 1 , q ) (W^{1,p},W^{1,q}) -extension domain if and only if it is an ( L 1 , p , L 1 , q ) (L^{1,p},L^{1,q}) -extension domain. • For 1 ≤ q < n 1\leq q<n and q ⋆ < p ≤ ∞ q^{\star}<p\leq\infty , there exists a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n {\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}} which is a ( W 1 , p , W 1 , q ) (W^{1,p},W^{1,q}) -extension domain but not an ( L 1 , p , L 1 , q ) (L^{1,p},L^{1,q}) -extension domain for 1 ≤ q < p ≤ n 1\leq q<p\leq n .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}({\Omega})
be a (semi-)normed function space defined on a domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\mathcal{Y}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
be a (semi-)normed function space defined on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. We say that
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is an
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
-
extension domain
, if there exists a bounded extension operator

E:\mathcal{X}({\Omega})\to\mathcal{Y}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

that is,
E(u)|_{\Omega}\equiv u
and there exists
C>0
such that

\|E(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}(\Omega)}.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Recall that the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
consists of all
L^{p}
-integrable functions whose first order distributional derivatives belong to
L^{p}(\Omega).
The homogeneous Sobolev space
L^{1,p}({\Omega})
is the semi-normed space of all locally
L^{1}
-integrable functions whose first order distributional derivatives belong to
L^{p}({\Omega})
.
Clearly,
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is always a subclass of
L^{1,p}({\Omega})
, but the other implication is not always true.
However, functions in
L^{1,p}(\Omega)
actually always belong to
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
, see
[
10
]
. For sufficiently nice
\Omega
these two function spaces coincide as sets. If
\Omega
is an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain with
1\leq q\leq p
, then functions
u
in
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
with
|\nabla u|\in L^{p}(\Omega)
can be extended to
W^{1,q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with gradient control.

The aim of this paper is to investigate the interconnection between the above two types of Sobolev extension domains.
In PDEs (e.g., see
[
11
]
) one typically relies on gradient estimates and hence the homogeneous version is perhaps more natural. On the other hand,
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the Sobolev space that one most frequently encounters in text books. Bounded Lipschitz domains are
(W^{1,p},W^{1,p})
-extension domains for
1\leq p\leq\infty
by results of Calderón
[
2
]
and Stein
[
16
]
. It follows from the respective proofs that they are also
(L^{1,p},L^{1,p})
-extension domains. Years later, Jones
[
7
]
proved that the more general
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domains are also
(W^{1,p},W^{1,p})
-extension domains. By analyzing the proof in
[
7
]
one notices that bounded
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domains are also
(L^{1,p},L^{1,p})
-extension domains.

The simultaneous extension properties above are no coincidences: by results of Herron and Koskela
[
5
]
, a bounded domain is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,p})
-extension domain precisely when it is an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,p})
-extension domain (when
1<p<\infty
).

In this paper, we deal with the case
1\leq q<p\leq\infty
. Extension domains can be rather irregular in this case. For example, an exterior monomial cusp or spire is an extension domain for suitable choices of
q
and
p
. For this see
[
12
]
by Maz’ya and Poborchi. Furthermore, there exist extension domains
\Omega
with
|\partial\Omega|>0
contrary to the case
q=p
, see
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
. Nevertheless, one could expect that the two classes of extension domains coincide.

Our first result shows that extendability does not always guarantee gradient control.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\frac{n}{2}
. There exists a bounded domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
which is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain for every
q^{\star}<p\leq\infty
, but is not an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain for
1\leq q<p\leq n
. Here
q^{\star}=\frac{qn}{n-q}
.

Our second result shows that the case
p<q^{\star}
is analogous to the case
q=p
. Towards the statement, we extend the definition of
q^{\star}
by setting
q^{\star}=\infty
when
q\geq n
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p<q^{\star}\leq\infty
. Then a bounded domain is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain if and only if it is an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain.

Theorem
1.2
does not allow for
p=\infty
, but the claim actually does also hold for
n<q\leq p\leq\infty
, see Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
4.1
. Actually,
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extensions always guarantee
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extensions, see Proposition
3.1
below. This verifies the expectation that gradient control should be harder to establish than norm control.

Theorem
1.2
is proven via appropriate modifications to the arguments in
[
4
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we present all the essential details. The crucial consequence of the assumption
p<q^{\star}
is that it allows us to employ the Rellich-Kondrachov compact embedding theorem to ensure that
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
embeds compactly into
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

2.
Preliminaries

We refer to generic positive constants by
C
. These constants may change even in a single string of estimates. The dependence of the constant on parameters
\alpha,\beta,\cdots
is expressed by the notation
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\cdots)
if needed.
First, let us give the definition of Sobolev spaces and homogeneous Sobolev spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\Omega})
, we say that
u
belongs to the homogeneous Sobolev space
L^{1,p}({\Omega})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
if its distributional gradient
\nabla u
belongs to
L^{p}({\Omega};{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. The homogeneous Sobolev space
L^{1,p}({\Omega})
is equipped with the semi-norm

\|u\|_{L^{1,p}({\Omega})}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&\ {\rm for}\ 1\leq p<\infty,\\
{\rm esssup}\left|\nabla u\right|,&\ {\rm for}\ p=\infty.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
u
is also
L^{p}
-integrable, then we say that
u
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
. The Sobolev space
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&\ {\rm for}\ 1\leq p<\infty,\\
{\rm esssup}\left(\left|u\right|+\left|\nabla u\right|\right),&\ {\rm for}\ p=\infty.\end{cases}

Poincaré inequality
:
Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded domain. The inequality

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-u_{\Omega}|^{p}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is referred to as a global
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality. This inequality does not hold for all domains
\Omega
, but many domains satisfy it. Here
u_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(y)dy
.

The following theorem is taken from
[
15
, Theorem 12]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
1\leq p<\infty
and
|\Omega|<\infty
. If the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is compact, then
\Omega
satisfies a global
(p,p)
- Poincaré inequality.

We continue with a simple fact whose proof we include for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain that supports a global
(p,p)
- Poincaré inequality. If
A\subset\Omega
is of positive measure, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.2)

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)-u_{A}|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

holds for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)-u_{A}|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)-u_{\Omega}|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{\Omega}-u_{A}|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}|u_{A}-u_{\Omega}|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{|A|}\left(\int_{A}|u(z)-u_{\Omega}|dz\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{|A|}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)-u_{\Omega}|dz\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{|\Omega|}{|A|}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)-u_{\Omega}|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\frac{|\Omega|}{|A|}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

Let us recall the definition of
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domains.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
and
\delta>0
. We say that a domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domain, if for all
x,y\in{\Omega}
with
|x-y|<\delta
, there is a rectifiable curve
\gamma\subset{\Omega}
joining
x,y
with

(2.3)

{\rm length}(\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}|x-y|

and

(2.4)

d(z,\partial{\Omega})\geq\frac{\epsilon|x-z||y-z|}{|x-y|}\ {\rm for\ all}\ z\ {\rm on}\ \gamma.

By a classical mollification argument, for an arbitrary domain
{\Omega}
, we always have that
C^{\infty}({\Omega})\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is dense in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
for every
1\leq p<\infty
. See
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
. However,
C^{\infty}(\overline{{\Omega}})\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
need not be dense in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, unless the domain
{\Omega}
is sufficiently nice.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain. We say that it satisfies the segment condition if for every
x\in\partial{\Omega}
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
and a nonzero vector
y_{x}
such that if
z\in\overline{{\Omega}}\cap U_{x}
, then
z+ty_{x}\in{\Omega}
for
0<t<1
.

The following lemma tells us that the Sobolev functions defined on a domain with segment condition can be approximated by functions in
C^{\infty}(\overline{{\Omega}})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain which satisfies the segment condition. Then for every
1\leq p<\infty
, the subspace
C^{\infty}(\overline{{\Omega}})\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is dense in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will construct a domain
\Omega
which is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain for all
1\leq q<q^{\star}<p<\infty
but not a
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain for any
1<q^{\star}\leq p\leq n
. We will employ a Whitney-type extension operator to prove that
\Omega
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain. During the development of the Sobolev extension theory, the so-called Whitney-type extension operators have been playing an essential role. They go back to a series of works
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
by Whitney on extending functions defined on a subset to the whole space with smoothness preserved. Several variants of Whitney-type extension operators have been used in different kinds of extension problems, for example see articles
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
20
]
. To begin, every open set
U\varsubsetneq{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
admits a Whitney decomposition, for example, see
[
16
, Chapter 6]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every open set
U\varsubsetneq{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, there exists a collection
\mathcal{W}(U)=\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of countably many closed dyadic cubes whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis such that

•

U=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{j}
and
\mathring{Q_{k}}\cap\mathring{Q_{j}}=\emptyset
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\neq k
;

•

l(Q_{j})\leq{\rm dist}(Q_{j},\partial U)\leq 4\sqrt{n}l(Q_{j})
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

\frac{1}{4}l(Q_{k})\leq l(Q_{j})\leq 4l(Q_{k})
whenever
k,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
Q_{k}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset
.

A collection
\mathcal{W}(U)
satisfying the properties of Lemma
2.3
is called a Whitney decomposition of
U
and the cubes
Q\in\mathcal{W}(U)
are called Whitney cubes.

For a given bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
we define
\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{W}(\overline{{\Omega}}^{c}).
In order to define the value of the extension on a Whitney cube
Q
in
W(\overline{\Omega}^{c}),
we need a suitable Whitney cube
\tilde{Q}
in
\mathcal{W}(\Omega),
so that we can transfer the average value of the function from
\tilde{Q}
to
Q.
After associating the value of a function from a Whitney cube inside the domain to the Whitney cube outside the domain, we need to glue the values together to obtain smoothness. In this step, the essential tool is a partition of unity. For a Whitney decomposition
\mathcal{W}(\overline{{\Omega}}^{c})=\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a corresponding class of smooth functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with the following properties:

•

for every
x\in\overline{{\Omega}}^{c}
, we have

(2.5)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\psi_{j}(x)=1;

•

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2.6)

0\leq\psi_{j}(x)\leq 1

for every
x\in\overline{{\Omega}}^{c}
;

•

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
{\rm supp}(\psi_{j})\subset\frac{17}{16}Q_{j}
and

(2.7)

|\nabla\psi_{j}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{l(Q_{j})}

for a constant
C>0
.

The collection
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a partition of unity associated to the Whitney decomposition
\mathcal{W}(\overline{{\Omega}}^{c})
.

3.
Gradient control implies norm control

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
. Then a bounded
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain is also a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain.

In this section, we prove Proposition
3.1
. The proof is similar to the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 4.4]
. For convenience of the readers, we still present the details here. In the proof, we will use the fact that for a ball
B
,
L^{1,p}(B)=W^{1,p}(B).
See
[
10
, Section 1.1.2]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain for
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and let
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a ball with
{\Omega}\subset\subset B
. Since
{\Omega}
is an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain, for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})\subset L^{1,p}({\Omega})
, we find
E(u)\in L^{1,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
E(u)\big|_{\Omega}\equiv u
and

(3.1)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}({\Omega})}

where
C>0.
By
[
10
, Section 1.1.2]
, we have
E(u)\big|_{B}\in W^{1,q}(B)
. Suppose first that
q<\infty
. Using Lemma
2.1
and (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}|E(u)(z)-u_{\Omega}|^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B}|\nabla E(u)(z)|^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality together with (
3.2
) give

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\lvert E(u)(z)\rvert^{q}+\lvert\nabla E(u)(z)\rvert^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\lvert E(u)(z)-u_{\Omega}+u_{\Omega}\rvert^{q}+\lvert\nabla E(u)(z)\rvert^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u(z)\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\left(\frac{|B|}{|{\Omega}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\lvert u(z)\rvert^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}\lvert u(z)\rvert^{p}dz+\lvert\nabla u(z)\rvert^{p}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Define an extension operator
\widetilde{E}
on
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
by setting

\tilde{E}(u):=E(u)\big|_{B}.

Inequality (
3.3
) tells us that
\tilde{E}
is a bounded extension operator from
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
to
W^{1,q}(B)
. Finally, the result that
{\Omega}
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain comes from the fact that each ball
B
is a
(W^{1,q},W^{1,q})
-extension domain.

The Case
q=\infty
follows similarly from the estimate

(3.4)

\|Eu-u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Also when
q<\infty
and
p=\infty
, we repeat the same arguments by replacing (
3.2
) with

(3.5)

\left(\int_{B}|E(u)(z)-u_{\Omega}|^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Proposition
3.1
, for
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, a bounded
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain.

Towards a partial converse, let
B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a large enough ball such that
{\Omega}\subset\subset B
. Since
{\Omega}
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain, for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, there exists a function
E(u)\in W^{1,q}(B)
such that
E(u)\big|_{{\Omega}}\equiv u
and

(4.1)

\|E(u)\|_{W^{1,q}(B)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})},

where
C>0.
Let
\{u_{j}\}
be a bounded sequence in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. By (
4.1
),
\{E(u_{j})\}
is also a bounded sequence in
W^{1,q}(B)
. Since
1\leq q\leq p<q^{\star}
, we can use the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem to conclude that there is a subsequence which converges in
L^{p}(B)
to some
v\in L^{p}(B)
. Especially, this subsequence converges to
v|_{\Omega}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Hence
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then, by using Theorem (
2.1
), we conclude that, for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, we have

(4.2)

\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-u_{\Omega}|^{p}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx

where
C>0
is independent of
u
.

Fix
u\in L^{1,p}({\Omega}).
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define a function
u_{n}
on
{\Omega}
by setting

u_{n}(x):=\min\left\{n,\max\left\{-n,u(x)\right\}\right\}.

Since
|\Omega|<\infty
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, by using an exhaustion argument we get that for some
m_{0}>0
it holds that

|\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|\leq m_{0}\}|\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}.

Set

A:=\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|\leq m_{0}\}.

For
m,n\geq m_{0},

u_{m}-u_{n}=0
on
A
and
u_{m}-u_{n}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).
If
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),
then by using Lemma
2.1
, we get that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v(x)-v_{A}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v(x)|^{p}dx.

By using inequality (
4.3
) for
v=u_{m}-u_{n}
, we conclude that

\int_{\Omega}|u_{m}(x)-u_{n}(x)|^{p}dx\leq C\left(\underset{\{x\in\Omega:|u_{m}(x)|\geq m_{0}\}}{\int}|\nabla u_{m}(x)|^{p}dx+\underset{\{x\in\Omega:|u_{m}(x)|\geq m_{0}\}}{\int}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{p}dx\right).

This yields

\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C\left(\underset{\{x\in\Omega:|u_{m}(x)|\geq m_{0}\}}{\int}|\nabla u_{m}(x)|^{p}dx+\underset{\{x\in\Omega:|u_{m}(x)|\geq m_{0}\}}{\int}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{p}dx\right).

Hence,
\{u_{m}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and by definition
\{u_{m}\}
converges point-wise to
u
in
\Omega
, which implies that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

So, for
u\in L^{1,p}(\Omega),
we also have
(u-u_{\Omega})\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).
We define a function on
B
by setting

T(u):=E(u-u_{\Omega})+u_{\Omega}.

Then
T(u)\in W^{1,q}(B)
with
T(u)\big|_{\Omega}\equiv u
. By using inequality (
4.2
) and the fact
{\Omega}
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain, we have

(4.4)

\|\nabla T(u)\|_{L^{q}(B)}=\|\nabla E(u-u_{\Omega})\|_{L^{q}(B)}\leq\|u-u_{{\Omega}}\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}({\Omega})}.

Inequality (
4.4
) and the fact that
B
is an
(L^{1,q},L^{1,q})
-extension domain show that
{\Omega}
is an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain.
∎

The formulation of Theorem
1.2
does not allow for the case
p=\infty
. Towards this case we first establish a preliminary result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
(W^{1,\infty},W^{1,q})
-extension domain with
q>n
. Then there exists a constant
C
so that

(4.5)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
x,y\in\Omega
and every continuous
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Fix a continuous
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
. We may assume that
u(x_{0})=1
and
u(y_{0})=0
. By truncation, we may further assume that
0\leq u(x)\leq 1
on
\Omega
. Then
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball such that
\overline{\Omega}\subset B
. Since
\Omega
is a bounded
(W^{1,\infty},W^{1,q})
-extension domain with
q>n
, there exists a function
E(u)\in W^{1,q}(B)
such that
E(u)|_{\Omega}\equiv u
and

(4.6)

\|E(u)\|_{W^{1,q}(B)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}

where
C>0
.

By Morrey’s inequality and (
4.6
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle|u(x_{0})-u(y_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{q}(B)},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{1-\frac{n}{q}}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}).

By using (
4.7
) and our normalization, we get that

(4.8)

1\leq C|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{1-\frac{n}{q}}+C|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Define
\delta>0
by the equation

(4.9)

1-C\delta^{1-\frac{n}{q}}=\frac{1}{2}.

By using (
4.8
), we get that

(4.10)

1\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

whenever
|x_{0}-y_{0}|<\delta
.

Hence (
4.10
) gives

(4.11)

1=|u(x_{0})-u(y_{0})|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

under the assumption that
|x_{0}-y_{0}|<\delta
.

Fix
j
such that
\sqrt{n}2^{-j}\leq\delta
and consider a dyadic decomposition
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into closed cubes of edge length
2^{-j}
. Since
{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded, there are only finitely many cubes, say
M\in\mathbb{N}
in
\mathcal{D}
which intersect
{\Omega}
. Let
x,y\in{\Omega}
be arbitrary and
\gamma\subset{\Omega}
be a locally rectifiable curve joining
x
and
y
. There exists a finite class of dyadic cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{D}
which intersect
\gamma
such that
x\in Q_{1}
and
y\in Q_{N}
with
N\leq M
. Set
x_{0}:=x
and
x_{N}:=y
. Choose
x_{1}
to be the last point on along the curve
\gamma
which is contained in the union of the cubes
Q_{j}
containing
x_{0}
. We continue with
x_{1}
taking the role of
x_{0}
. By repeating this argument (at most
\hat{N}
times), we obtain
\{x_{0}=x,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{\hat{N}}\},\hat{N}\leq N
with
x_{j}\in\Omega
and so that

(4.12)

|x_{j}-x_{j+1}|\leq\sqrt{n}2^{-j}\leq\delta.

Hence (
4.11
) together with triangle inequality gives

(4.13)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
x,y\in\Omega
.
∎

From Lemma
4.1
and the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following conclusion.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
q>n
. Then a bounded
(W^{1,\infty},W^{1,q})
-extension domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is also an
(L^{1,\infty},L^{1,q})
-extension domain.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
(W^{1,\infty},W^{1,q})
-extension domain with
q>n
. Then by Lemma
4.1
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(4.14)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
x,y\in\Omega
and every continuous
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Hence for
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, by considering a continuous representative of
u
, we conclude that

(4.15)

\|u-u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

where
C>0
is independent of
u
.

Since every
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies (
4.15
), we conclude that
\Omega
is a
(L^{1,\infty},L^{1,q})
-extension domain by again defining
Tu:=E(u-u_{\Omega})+u_{\Omega}
as at the end of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

∎

for

Let

. Set

is the projection of

into
(n-1)

. Let

and

whose precise value will be chosen later. We define two trapezoidal cones
\mathscr{C}_{k}^{+}
and
\mathscr{C}_{k}^{-}
by setting

\mathscr{C}_{k}^{+}:=\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:0\leq|\check{x}-z_{k}|\leq\left(1-2^{(k+1)}l_{k}\right)x_{n}+2^{-k-1},-2^{-k-1}\leq x_{n}\leq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{k}^{-}:=\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:0\leq|\check{x}-z_{k}|\leq\left(l_{k}2^{(k+1)}-1\right)x_{n}+2l_{k}-2^{-k-1},-2^{-k}\leq x_{n}\leq-2^{-k-1}\right\},

respectively. We further define a double cone
\mathscr{C}_{k}

\mathscr{C}_{k}:=\mathscr{C}_{k}^{+}\cup\mathscr{C}_{k}^{-}.

Here
\mathscr{C}_{k}
can be viewed as an hourglass.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We define an
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperplane
P_{k}
by setting

P_{k}:=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:x_{n}=-2^{-k-1}\right\},

and a cylinder
C_{k}
(which contains
\mathscr{C}_{k}
) by setting

C_{k}:=\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:0\leq|\check{x}-z_{k}|\leq 2^{-k-1},-2^{-k}\leq x_{n}\leq 0\right\}.

We define the domain
{\Omega}
to be the interior of the set

F:=\mathcal{Q}_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{C}_{k}

and the domain
{\Omega}_{1}
to be the interior of the set

F_{1}:=\mathcal{Q}_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{k}.

That is
{\Omega}=\mathring{F}
and
{\Omega}_{1}=\mathring{F}_{1}
. The following picture roughly explains the construction of the domain

.

5.2.
A Whitney-type extension operator

Let
\mathcal{W}(F^{c})
be a Whitney decomposition of the complement of
F
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\mathcal{W}^{1}_{k}
to be a subclass of
\mathcal{W}(F^{c})
by setting

\mathcal{W}^{1}_{k}:=\left\{Q\in\mathcal{W}(F^{c}):Q\cap C_{k}\neq\emptyset\right\},

and define
\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
to be a further subclass of
\mathcal{W}(F^{c})
by setting

\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}:=\left\{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}(F^{c}):\ {\rm there\ exists}\ Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1},\text{such that}\,\,\tilde{Q}\cap Q\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Obviously,
\mathcal{W}^{1}_{k}
is a subclass of
\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
. For arbitrary
k\neq j
, since the distance between
C_{k}
and

\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}\cap\mathcal{W}^{2}_{j}=\emptyset,

and for each

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(Q,{\Omega})={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(Q,\mathscr{C}_{k}).

Let
\mathcal{W}({\Omega})
be a Whitney decomposition of the domain

. By the geometry of the domain

, for each cube
Q\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
, we can choose a cube

such that

, the centers of

and

are on the same side of the hyperplane

,

(5.1)

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(Q,Q^{\star})\leq C_{1}l(Q),

(5.2)

. The Whitney cube

is called a reflected cube of the Whitney cube

\{\psi_{Q}\}

.

Definition 5.1
.

We define a Whitney-type extension operator

on

by setting

E_{W}(u)(x):=\begin{cases}u(x),\ \ &\ {\rm if}\ x\in{\Omega},\\

for all

be a collection of Whitney cubes such that a face of

is contained in a face of

, or vise versa,

. We then say that

to

.

. The nice geometry of the domain

gives the following observations.

•

and

. If, for

and a chain
\{Q_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{m}
which connects

and
Q_{2}^{\star}
with length at most

.

•

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}

and the centers of
Q_{1}^{\star}
and

are contained on the same side of

. Then there exists an integer

which connects
Q_{1}^{\star}
and

.

\mathcal{W}_{Q}

\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
which contains all the Whitney cubes

such that

Define

\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}:=\{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}:\exists\hskip 4.2679pt\text{a chain}\hskip 2.84526pt\{Q_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{ connecting }\hskip 2.84526ptQ^{\star}\hskip 4.2679pt\text{to}\hskip 4.2679pt\tilde{Q^{\star}}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{with length at most $M$}\},

\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}

Let

(5.5)

(5.6)

\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{1}_{k}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}^{1}_{Q}}\chi_{\tilde{\Gamma}(Q^{\star},\tilde{Q}^{\star})}(x)\leq C.

]

.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Now, we are ready to give a proof for Theorem
1.1
. We divide it into two parts.

Theorem 5.1
.

The domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
defined in Section
5.1
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain for all
1\leq q<q^{\star}<p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

It follows from the construction that
\Omega
is quasiconvex and hence
(W^{1,\infty},W^{1,\infty})
-extension domain
[
4
]
. It is clear that
{\Omega}_{1}
is an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domain for some
\epsilon,\delta>0
. By
[
7
]
, we have a bounded linear extension operator from
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
to
W^{1,q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for every
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Hence, it suffices to construct a bounded extension operator from
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
to
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
when
q^{\star}<p<\infty
. It is not difficult to check that
{\Omega}
satisfies the segment condition defined in Definition
2.3
. By Lemma
2.2
,
C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is dense in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
. To begin, we define a bounded linear extension operator
\widetilde{E_{W}}
from
C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
to
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
and later extend it to
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
. Given
u\in C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, we define a function
\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)
on
{\Omega}_{1}
by setting

(5.7)

\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x):=\begin{cases}u(x),\ \ &\ {\rm if}\ x\in F\cap{\Omega}_{1},\\
\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}u_{Q^{\star}}\cdot\psi_{Q}(x),\ \ &\ {\rm if}\ x\in{\Omega}_{1}\setminus F,\end{cases}

where

u_{Q^{\star}}:={\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 5.17493pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q^{\star}}u(y)dy=\frac{1}{|Q^{\star}|}\int_{Q^{\star}}u(y)dy

is the integral average of the function
u
over the reflected cube
Q^{\star}
and
\{\psi_{Q}\}
is the partition of unity associated to the Whitney decomposition of
\mathcal{W}(F^{c})
.

Since
\psi_{Q}
are smooth and, for every compact subset of
{\Omega}_{1}\setminus F
, only finitely many
\psi_{Q}
are non-zero on it,
\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)
is smooth on
\Omega_{1}\setminus F
. Hence, it suffices to show that the Sobolev norm of
\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)
is uniformly bounded from above by the Sobolev norm of
u
. To begin, we have

(5.8)

\int_{{\Omega}_{1}}|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{q}dx+\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx.

Since the volume of
{\Omega}
is finite, the Hölder inequality implies

(5.9)

\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Now

0\leq\psi_{Q}\leq 1\ {\rm for\ every}\ Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}

and for every
x\in C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}
, there are only finitely many
Q\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
such that

\psi_{Q}(x)\neq 0.

Since

C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}\subset\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}^{2}}Q

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(5.10)

\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}\int_{Q}|u_{Q^{\star}}|^{q}dx.

Combining (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}\int_{Q}|u_{Q^{\star}}|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}\int_{Q}\left|{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q^{\star}}u(y)dy\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}\int_{Q}{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q^{\star}}|u(y)|^{q}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}\int_{Q^{\star}}|u(y)|^{q}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{{\Omega}}|u(y)|^{q}dy\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Using (
5.10
) and (

), we deduce that

(5.12)

\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By using inequalities (
5.8
) and (
5.12
), we get that

(5.13)

\left(\int_{{\Omega}_{1}}|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Similarly, we have

(5.14)

\int_{{\Omega}_{1}}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx+\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
x\in C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}
, there are only finitely many
Q\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}
such that

\psi_{Q}(x)\neq 0.

Hence, for
x\in\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}
, we have

\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)=\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{k}}u_{Q^{\star}}\cdot\nabla\psi_{Q}(x).

Since

\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}\subset\bigcup_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}Q,

we have

(5.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\underset{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}^{1}_{k}}{\bigcup}Q}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\int_{Q}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx.

For
Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
, we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}}u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\left(\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}}\left(u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right)\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right)\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}}\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}\cdot\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}}\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}\cdot\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}}\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}\cdot\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx,

with a positive constant
C
independent of
u
and
Q
. By using (
5.2
) and the properties of our partition of unity, there exists a constant
C>1
such that for every
x\in Q
and each
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}
, we have

(5.17)

\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{l(Q)}.

Given
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}
, there exists a chain
\{Q_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of length at most
M
which connects
Q^{\star}
and
\tilde{Q}^{\star}
and satisfies the properties in Lemma
5.1
. The triangle inequality implies

(5.18)

\displaystyle\left|u_{Q_{1}^{\star}}-u_{Q_{m}^{\star}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left|u_{Q_{i}^{\star}}-u_{Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(\left|u_{Q_{i}^{\star}}-u_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\right|+\left|u_{Q_{i+1}^{\star}}-u_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\right|\right).

Since there exists a constant
C>1
such that for every
1\leq i\leq m-1
, we have

\max\left\{|Q_{i}^{\star}|,|Q_{i+1}^{\star}|\right\}\leq\left|Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}\right|\leq C\min\left\{|Q_{i}^{\star}|,|Q_{i+1}^{\star}|\right\},

the triangle inequality and
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
give that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\left|u_{Q_{i}^{\star}}-u_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\left|u(x)-u_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cl^{q}(Q_{i}^{\star}){\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q_{i}^{\star}\cup Q_{i+1}^{\star}}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx.

By Lemma
5.1
, there exists a constant
C>1
such that for all
Q_{i}^{\star},Q_{j}^{\star}\in\big\{Q_{k}^{\star}\big\}_{k=1}^{m}
, we have

(5.20)

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{l(Q_{i}^{\star})}{l(Q_{j}^{\star})}\leq C.

Since
n\leq M
, by combining inequalities (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), we obtain

(5.21)

\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}\leq C\frac{l^{q}(Q)}{|Q|}\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(Q^{\star},\tilde{Q}^{\star})}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx.

Hence, for
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}
, we have

(5.22)

\int_{Q}|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)|^{q}\cdot\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx\leq C\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(Q^{\star},\tilde{Q}^{\star})}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx.

If
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}
, then

Q^{\star}
and
\tilde{Q}^{\star}
are at different sides of the hyperplane
P_{k}
. Then the triangle inequality, (
5.17
) and the Hölder inequality imply that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}-u_{Q^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}\left|u_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle+C\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\psi_{\tilde{Q}}(x)\right|^{q}|u_{Q^{\star}}|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cl^{n-q}(\tilde{Q}^{\star}){\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}\left|u(x)\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle+Cl^{n-q}(Q^{\star}){\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 2.87498pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{Q^{\star}}|u(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cl^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(\tilde{Q}^{\star})\left(\int_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle+Cl^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(Q^{\star})\left(\int_{Q^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By combining (
5.15
), (
5.16
), (
5.22
) and (
5.23
), we conclude that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}\left|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}}\int_{\tilde{\Gamma}(Q^{\star},\tilde{Q}^{\star})}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx,

\displaystyle+C\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}}l^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(Q^{\star})\left(\int_{Q^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}}l^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(\tilde{Q}^{\star})\left(\int_{\tilde{Q}^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

For each
Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
, the cardinality of
\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}
is uniformly bounded by a constant
C
. By combining (
5.6
) and the Hölder inequality, we obtain

(5.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{1}}\int_{\tilde{\Gamma}(Q^{\star},\tilde{Q}^{\star})}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Given
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(5.26)

\mathcal{W}_{j}({\Omega}):=\left\{Q\in\mathcal{W}({\Omega}):2^{-j-1}\leq l(Q)<2^{-j}\right\}

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{j}(F^{c}):=\left\{Q\in\mathcal{W}(F^{c}):Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{W}_{j}({\Omega})\right\}.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\leq j
. Let
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
be such that
Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{W}_{j}({\Omega})
and
\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}
is non-empty. Then, for such a
Q
we have

l(Q)>cl_{k}

where
0<c<1
is independent of
j,k
. By the geometry of
{\Omega}
, there exists
C>1
such that for each
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
with non-empty
\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}
, we have

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(Q,P_{k})<Cl(Q)\ {\rm and}\ {\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(Q,{\Omega})<Cl(Q).

Hence, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and each
k\leq j
, the cardinalities of the sets
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{j}(F^{c})\cap\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{j}({\Omega})
are uniformly bounded from above by a constant
C
. Hence, the cardinality of the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{j}(F^{c})
is bounded from above by
Cj
. For each
Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}
, the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}
is uniformly bounded from above. Since
1\leq q<n
and
nq/(n-q)<p<\infty
, by (
5.23
), we have

(5.27)

\sum_{Q\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{1}}\sum_{\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}^{2}}l^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(Q^{\star})\left(\int_{Q^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\\
\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q^{\star}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{j}({\Omega})}l^{n-q-\frac{nq}{p}}(Q^{\star})\left(\int_{Q^{\star}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\\
\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j2^{-j\left(n-q-\frac{nq}{p}\right)}\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\\
\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By combining (
5.24
), (
5.25
) and (
5.27
), we obtain

(5.28)

\int_{\bigcup_{k}C_{k}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{k}}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}+|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and inequalities (
5.14
) and (
5.28
), we conclude that

(5.29)

\int_{{\Omega}_{1}}|\nabla\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)(x)|^{q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{{\Omega}}|u(x)|^{p}+|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By combining (
5.13
) and (
5.29
), we obtain that the extension operator constructed in (
5.7
) is a bounded linear extension operator from
C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
to
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
, which means that for every
u\in C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, we have
\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)\in W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
with

(5.30)

\|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u)\|_{W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})},

where
C>0
is independent of
u
. Since
C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
is dense in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, there exists a Cauchy sequence
\{u_{m}\}\subset C^{\infty}(F)\cap W^{1,p}({\Omega})
which converges to
u
in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
. Furthermore, there exists a subsequence of
\{u_{m}\}
which converges to
u
almost everywhere in
{\Omega}
. To simplify the notation, we still refer to this subsequence by
\{u_{m}\}
. By (
5.30
),
\{\widetilde{E_{W}}(u_{m})\}
is a Cauchy sequence in the Sobolev space
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
and hence converges to some function
v\in W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
. Furthermore, there exists a subsequence of
\{\widetilde{E_{W}}(u_{m})\}
which converges to
v
almost everywhere in
{\Omega}_{1}
. On the other hand, by the definition of
E(u_{m})
and
E(u)
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{E_{W}}(u_{m})(x)=E_{W}(u)(x)

for almost every
x\in{\Omega}_{1}
. Hence
v(x)=E_{W}(u)(x)
almost everywhere. This implies that
E_{W}(u)\in W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
with

(5.31)

\displaystyle\|E_{W}(u)\|_{W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|\widetilde{E_{W}}(u_{m})\|_{W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lim_{m\to\infty}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})},

where
C>0
is independent of
u
. Hence,
E_{W}
defined in (
5.4
) is a bounded linear extension operator from
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
to
W^{1,q}({\Omega}_{1})
. The fact that
{\Omega}_{1}
is a
(W^{1,q},W^{1,q})
-extension domain implies
{\Omega}
is a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain for all
1\leq q<q^{\star}<p<\infty
.
∎

In the next theorem, we will show that
\Omega
is not an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
1\leq q<p\leq n
. Then
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, defined in Section
5.1
, is not an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain.

Proof.

To prove that
{\Omega}
is not an
(L^{1,p},L^{1,q})
-extension domain, it suffices to show that, given
M<\infty
, there is
u\in L^{1,p}({\Omega})
whose every extension satisfies

(5.32)

\|E(u)\|_{L^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq M\|u\|_{L^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We define

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{-2^{-k-1}}{1-l_{k}2^{(k+1)}},

\displaystyle B^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-(z_{k},a_{k})|\leq 2l_{k}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-(z_{k},a_{k})|\leq 2l_{k}+2^{-2k}\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:0\leq|\check{x}-z_{k}|\leq\left(1-l_{k}2^{(k+1)}\right)x_{n}+2^{-k-1},a_{k}\leq x_{n}\leq 0\right\},

where
0<l_{k}<2^{-3k-1}
is sufficiently small and will be fixed later.
We define a function
\tilde{u}_{k}
on
\mathscr{C}_{k}
by setting

(5.33)

\tilde{u}_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}1,\ \ &{\rm if}\ \ x\in{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}\cap\Omega\setminus(\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}),\\
1-\frac{\ln\left(\frac{2l_{k}+2^{-2k}}{\|x\|}\right)}{\ln\left(\frac{2l_{k}+2^{-2k}}{2l_{k}}\right)},\ \ &{\rm if}\ \ x\in(\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+})\cap\Omega\setminus(B^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}),\\
0,\ \ &{}\ \text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

And we define
u_{k}
on
\Omega
by setting

(5.34)

u_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{k}(x),\ \ &{\rm if}\ \ x\in\mathscr{C}_{k}\cap\Omega,\\
1,\ \ &{}\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}

According to
p
equals to
n
or not, we divide the following argument into two cases.

Case
1(p=n)
:
In this case, we have

(5.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}(x)|^{n}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{((\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+})\setminus B_{k}^{n})\cap\Omega}\frac{1}{\left[\ln\left(\frac{l_{k}+2^{-2k-1}}{l_{k}}\right)\right]^{n}}\frac{1}{\|x\|^{n}}dx,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{2^{-2k-1}}{l_{k}}\right)\right]^{n-1}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n)\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{2^{-2k-1}}{l_{k}}\right)\right]^{1-n}.

Hence
u_{k}\in L^{1,n}(\Omega).

Let
B_{k}
be ball with radius
\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{-k}
and which is centered at
x_{k}=(z_{k},-2^{-k-1}).
Suppose that
E(u_{k})\in L^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an extension of
u_{k}
with
q<n
. The Sobolev-Poincaré inequality gives

(5.36)

\displaystyle\|E(u_{k})\|_{L^{1,q}(B_{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla E(u_{k})(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(5.37)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(n)\left(\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{B_{k}}|^{q^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}.

Let
A_{1}=((B^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+})\cup\mathscr{C}_{k}^{-})\cap\Omega
and
A_{2}=\mathscr{C}_{k}^{+}\cap\Omega\setminus(\tilde{B}^{n}_{{k}}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-{(E(u_{k}))}_{B_{k}}|^{q^{*}}dx

\displaystyle+C\int_{B_{k}}|(E(u_{k}))_{B_{k}}-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}dx.

Furthermore

\displaystyle|(E(u_{k}))_{B_{k}}-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|A_{1}|}\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{B_{k}}|^{q^{*}}dx.

Hence, we have

(5.38)

\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}dx\leq C\left(1+\frac{|B_{k}|}{|A_{1}|}\right)\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-{(E(u_{k}))}_{B_{k}}|^{q^{*}}dx.

By combining (
5.36
) and (
5.38
), we conclude that

(5.39)

\displaystyle\|E(u_{k})\|_{L^{1,q}(B_{k})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{k}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{A_{2}}|E(u_{k})(x)-(E(u_{k}))_{A_{1}}|^{q^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C|A_{2}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(n)2^{\frac{-(k+2)n}{q^{*}}}.

By using (
5.35
) and (
5.39
) and choosing

(5.40)

l_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2^{2k+1}\left(\exp{(2^{(\frac{(k+2)n}{q^{\star}}+k)\frac{n}{n-1}})}\right)},

we obtain

(5.41)

\frac{\|E(u_{k})\|_{L^{1,q}(B_{k})}}{\|u_{k}\|_{L^{1,n}(\Omega)}}\geq C(n)\left(\frac{2^{\frac{-(k+2)n}{q^{*}}}}{\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{2^{-2k}}{l_{k}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1-n}{n}}}\right)\geq C(n)2^{k}.

In conclusion,

\|Eu_{k}\|_{L^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq M\|u_{k}\|_{L^{1,p}(\Omega)},

when
k
is sufficiently large.

Case
2(p<n)
:
We use Hölder’s inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u_{k}(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{C}_{k}\cap\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{(\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+})\cap\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}(x)|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{n}}|({\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}\cap\Omega})|^{\frac{n-p}{n}}.

Hence, by using (
5.35
), we have

(5.42)

\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u_{k}(x)|^{p}dx\leq C(n)\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{2^{-2k-1}}{l_{k}}\right)\right]^{\frac{p}{n}-1}|({\tilde{B}^{n}_{k}\cap\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{k}^{+}\cap\Omega})|^{1-\frac{p}{n}}.

Similarly to the case above, Let
E(u_{k})
be an extension of
u_{k}
.
Then by using (
5.39
) and (
5.42
) and choosing

(5.43)

l_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2^{2k}\left(\exp{(2^{(\frac{(k+2)n}{q^{\star}}+k)\frac{np}{n-p})}})\right)},

we obtain that for
1\leq q<p<n
, we have

(5.44)

\frac{\|E(u_{k})\|_{L^{1,q}(B_{k})}}{\|u_{k}\|_{L^{1,p}(\Omega)}}\geq C(n)2^{k}.

Hence, again
\|Eu_{k}\|_{L^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq M\|u_{k}\|_{L^{1,p}(\Omega)}
when
k
is sufficiently large.
∎

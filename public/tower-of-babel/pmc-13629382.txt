Composition-Dependent Pseudocapacitive Behavior of MoO3–V2O5 Composite Thin Films in an Aprotic Electrolyte
Authors: Nimbure Revansiddappa, Ramachandra Gokul, Boodu Anuragh, Muthu Selvakumar, MG Mahesha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Transition-metal oxide electrodes offer high theoretical capacitance for electrochemical energy storage. Yet, their practical application is fundamentally constrained by chemical instability in aqueous electrolytes, where high-valence metal centers undergo rapid hydrolytic dissolution. Here, we prepared MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films across different compositions. Structural characterization reveals a composition-dependent evolution from orthorhombic phases in the pure oxides to the coexistence of both the metal oxides in intermediate compositions. We employ an aprotic organic electrolyte (LiBF4 in propylene carbonate), which suppresses hydrolytic degradation pathways and enables stable operation over an expanded electrochemical window. Under these conditions, V2O5-rich thin-film electrodes exhibit pronounced pseudocapacitive behavior, delivering a specific capacitance of 25 F g–1 and capacitance retention of 89.7% at a current density of 1 A g–1 after 10,000 cycles along with a columbic efficiency of 98.4% in a symmetric device configuration. This work demonstrates a synergistic interaction between MoO3 and V2O5 phases, leading to enhanced charge storage behavior compared to the individual oxides.

Introduction

The rapid expansion of renewable energy technologies such as solar and wind has increased the need for efficient energy storage systems. However, the intermittent nature of these resources necessitates efficient energy storage systems capable of storing excess energy and delivering it on demand. Electrochemical energy storage technologies including lithium-ion batteries, , sodium-ion batteries, , fuel cells, and supercapacitors have emerged as key solutions owing to their high energy conversion efficiency, scalability, and compatibility with renewable energy systems. Among these energy storage solutions electrochemical supercapacitors (SCs) emerged as essential devices due to their high power density, fast charge–discharge capability, and long cycle stability, which makes them better than conventional batteries in applications that require instantaneous energy delivery. Unlike batteries, which depend upon slow bulk ion diffusion, SCs store charge through a combination of electric double layer formation and fast reversible surface redox reactions, enabling exceptional rate performance and durability. Figure illustrates the three principal charge storage mechanisms in supercapacitors, namely, electric double layer capacitance (EDLC) arising from electrostatic ion accumulation, pseudo capacitance originating from fast surface or near surface redox reactions, and hybrid supercapacitors that integrate both the processes. As global energy consumption rises and sustainable power becomes increasingly essential, developing advanced electrode materials for high-performance SCs remains a critical scientific and technological priority. Among the various electrode material classes explored for SCs include carbon-based materials, conducting polymers, ,, and transition metal oxides (TMOs). , The performance of the device is intimately governed by the choice of electrode and its electrochemical properties. TMOs represent a diverse and fascinating class of pseudocapacitive materials with versatile electronic structures arising from partially filled d-orbitals. The metal oxygen bonding in these compounds can vary from nearly ionic to highly covalent, enabling multiple oxidation states and reversible redox reactions that are essential for charge storage. Among TMOs, RuO2 is thought to be an optimal pseudocapacitive electrode material owing to its high theoretical capacitance and rapid faradaic redox reactions; however, the high cost and toxicity to the environment seriously hinder its practical application in SCs. This has driven the research community towards earth-abundant, cost-effective TMOs such as MnO2, NiO, Co3O4, MoO3, and V2O5 as viable alternatives. Among these, molybdenum trioxide (MoO3) and vanadium pentoxide (V2O5) have attracted considerable attention due to their layered structures, multiple oxidation states, and relatively low cost. MoO3 has been explored as pseudocapacitive electrode material for improvement in energy density owing to its unique layered structure composed of MoO6 octahedra with van der Waals interactions, which helps in insertion and extraction of ions. Molybdenum possesses multiple accessible oxidation states ranging from +2 to +6, which facilitates complex, multi electron faradaic charge transfer mechanisms. MoO3 exists in three primary polymorphs: orthorhombic (α), monoclinic (β), and hexagonal (h). The most thermodynamically stable and commonly utilized form is α-MoO3. Its structure supports reversible Mo6+/Mo4+ redox transitions, enabling efficient pseudocapacitive charge storage. As a result, MoO3 shows high theoretical specific capacity exceeding 1117 mAh g–1. However, translating this immense theoretical potential into practical performance is hindered by inherent material limitations. Pristine MoO3 suffers from sluggish faradaic redox kinetics, low electrical conductivity, and lack of structural integrity during cycling at high rates. V2O5 is another pseudocapacitive TMO which offers multivalency, different oxidation states from V2+ to V5+, which promotes high-capacity ion intercalation and rich pseudocapacitive behavior. The well-defined layered crystal structure accommodates a variety cations including H+, L+, Na+, and K+ through intercalation-type pseudocapacitance making it attractive for asymmetric and hybrid SCs application. , V2O5 is limited by low power density and minimal cyclic stability because of low ionic diffusion rate.

While pristine V2O5 and MoO3 have been extensively researched, MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films remain largely unexplored for energy-storage applications. The existing literature has shown that MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films exhibit better performance for gas sensing, such as NO2 detection. Despite these promising attributes, most studies of MoO3 and V2O5 are limited to powder or nanostructured systems in aqueous electrolytes. Each of these oxides individually offers complementary electrochemical merits such as MoO3 contributing a unique layered structure with favorable Mo redox transitions, while V2O5 provides a wide accessible potential window through V5+/V4+/V3+ redox activity. A composite of these two materials is therefore expected to yield synergistic enhancements in charge storage through the simultaneous utilization of both Mo and V redox couples, potentially broadening the operational potential window and improving the overall pseudocapacitive performance. The present work investigates MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films as electrode materials for supercapacitors.

Our preliminary research revealed that MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films were catastrophically unstable in aqueous environments. Upon exposure to a standard aqueous electrolyte (1 M Na2SO4 or 1 M KOH), we observed an immediate and macroscopic dissolution of the active material, making standard electrochemical characterization impossible. This rapid degradation is attributed to the intrinsic amphoteric nature of high-valence Mo6+ and V5+ centers, transforming the solid oxide network into highly soluble anions of molybdate (MoO4 2–) and vanadate (VO3 –) that easily diffuse into the surrounding medium. Consequently, to overcome this fundamental hydrolytic instability and access the intrinsic pseudocapacitive properties of the prepared thin-film electrodes, we transitioned to an anhydrous organic electrolyte system (1 M LiBF4 in propylene carbonate), which not only preserves structural integrity but also expands the operating voltage window, thereby enhancing energy density. In the present study, the MoO3–V2O5 thin films were deposited by spray pyrolysis, and their electrochemical properties were systematically evaluated in LiBF4/propylene carbonate organic electrolyte, with the aim of establishing the fundamental electrochemical behavior of this composite system and demonstrating its viability as a next-generation thin-film supercapacitor electrode.

Experimental Methods

Preparation of Thin Films

MoO3–V2O5 composite thin films were synthesized using the spray pyrolysis technique, employing computer interfaced HOLMARC spray pyrolysis equipment (Model No. HO-TH-04). High purity precursors ammonium heptamolybdate ((NH4)6 Mo7 O24·4H2 O, AHM, ≥ 99% purity, Merck) and ammonium metavanadate (NH4 VO3, AMV, ≥ 99% purity, Merck) were dissolved in deionized water to prepare 0.01 M AHM and 0.07 M AMV solutions. Although the molarities are different, both solutions give an identical metal atom concentration of 0.07 M (AHM contains 7 Mo atoms per molecule; AMV contains 1 V atom per molecule), which provides an equal metal atom equivalence approach for precise compositional tailoring.

The resulting precursor solution was atomized using compressed air as the carrier gas with a pressure of 1 bar and delivered at a constant flow rate of 1 mL min–1. The nozzle to substrate distance was fixed at 17 cm to ensure uniform droplet distribution and controlled film growth. The precursor solution was sprayed onto cleaned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates maintained at 425 °C; upon reaching the heated substrate, the aerosol droplets underwent rapid solvent evaporation followed by thermal decomposition of the precursor salt, leading to the formation of oxide films. The decomposition results in nucleation of MoO3 and V2O5 phases followed by their growth into a continuous thin film. Films with different Mo:V ratios were grown, and these samples were named as shown in Table . All the deposition parameters were kept constant for different compositions. Material Characterizations

Structural, morphological, electrical, and electrochemical properties of all the prepared thin film samples were comprehensively analyzed using diverse characterization techniques. High resolution X-ray diffraction (HRXRD) measurements were conducted using a Rigaku Smart Lab diffractometer equipped with a 9 kW rotating anode Cu–Kα source (λ = 1.5406 Å) and operated at a step rate of 2° min–1. Surface morphology and cross-sectional thickness were examined using a Thermo Scientific apreo 2S FESEM. Raman measurements were performed using a LabRAM Raman spectrometer equipped with a 532 nm excitation laser. Electrical measurements were performed using a linear four-probe configuration connected to a Keithley 2450 source meter, operated at a constant current of 1 mA with a 20 V compliance limit to determine sheet resistance and resistivity. All the electrochemical characterizations including cyclic voltammetry, galvanostatic charge–discharge, and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy were carried out using an OrigaFlex-OGF05A potentiostat, enabling detailed evaluation of the pseudocapacitive response and charge storage performance of the thin film electrodes.

Raman Analysis

Raman spectroscopy was also employed to characterize the structural evolution of MoO3–V2O5 thin films across all the compositions. The Raman spectrum shown in Figure displayed prominent characteristic peaks at 117 cm–1, 129 cm–1, 158 cm–1, 197 cm–1, 218 cm–1, 243 cm–1, 285 cm–1, 292 cm–1, 337 cm–1, 378 cm–1, 471 cm–1, 667 cm–1, 820 cm–1, and 996 cm–1, which corresponds to orthorhombic α-MoO3 for the M100 sample. For V100 sample, the high-frequency bands at 998 cm–1 and 704 cm–1 are assigned to asymmetric and symmetric stretching of V(a)-O(3)-V(b) bridges. The vibrations of V(a)-O(2a) and V(b)-O(2b) bonds are found at 530 and 484 cm–1; also, ladder-like distortion modes are found at 305 and 284 cm–1. The detailed assignments for V2O5 are available in the literature. − For the intermediate compositions (M80 to M20), the Raman spectra show a significant evolution characterized by peak broadening, reduced intensity, and partial merging of vibrational modes. Unlike the sharp and well-resolved Raman bands observed for M100 and V100, the intermediate films show broadening of the Raman modes. This is due to the increased lattice disorder that arises from the coexistence of MoO3 and V2O5 within a shared oxygen network. The incorporation of Mo and V species with different ionic radii, coordination environments, and bonding preferences leads to the lattice distortion. Similarly, the mid-frequency region around 300-500 cm–1 shows merged bands associated with O–Mo–O bending, O–V–O bending, and mixed metal–oxygen-metal vibrations. The observed peak broadening and merging of Raman bands in the intermediate compositions are attributed to lattice distortion and mixed Mo–O–V bonding environments, consistent with earlier reports on mixed TMOs. ,

Electrochemical Analysis

Initial electrochemical characterization of all the prepared thin film electrodes was carried out in aqueous electrolytes (1M KOH and 1M Na2SO4) to assess their intrinsic electrochemical stability. However, both the electrolytes were found to be unstable with MoO3–V2O5 thin films. In alkaline 1M KOH, rapid degradation of film was observed due to hydroxyl ions, where OH– attacks oxide heterostructure, which leads to the formation soluble molybdate (MoO4 –2) and vanadate (VO3 –) ions shown in (9) and (10). 9 V2O5(s)+2OH−(aq)→2VO3−(aq)+H2O(l) 10 MoO3(s)+2OH−(aq)→MoO42−(aq)+H2O(l)

In neutral 1M Na2SO4 electrolyte, although the hydroxide concentration is negligible, film instability was still observed. This behavior is due to the water reactivity that disrupts the V–O–V and Mo–O–Mo metal oxide frameworks, which leads to the gradual structural degradation of the active material. These limitations made us take a transition to an aprotic organic electrolyte system. The 1 M LiBF4 in propylene carbonate was selected as electrolyte, which eliminates water- and hydroxide-driven degradation and also provides an expanded electrochemical stability window. Figure illustrates the schematic representation of symmetric two-electrode configurations. This electrolyte selection also provides an important distinction from many previously reported MoO3- and V2O5-based electrodes evaluated in aqueous electrolytes. − The present study instead emphasizes the systematic influence of MoO3–V2O5 composition on the structural and electrochemical behavior of binder-free thin-film electrodes under a consistent non-aqueous electrolyte environment. Consequently, electrochemical characterization for all the samples was done using cyclic voltammetry (CV), electrostatic impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) in 1 M LiBF4/propylene carbonate using a symmetric two-electrode configuration, ensuring both thermodynamic stability of the thin films and reliable evaluation of their intrinsic charge storage behavior.

Cyclic Voltammetry (CV)

The CV curves of all the samples, recorded in the potential window of 0 to 1.5 V at a scan rate of 100 mV s–1, exhibit nearly quasi rectangular and symmetric profiles, which is characteristic of pseudocapacitive charge storage behavior. Such shapes indicate a rapid and reversible redox process with minimal diffusion limitations, suggesting that the thin films provide accessible electroactive sites and efficient ion transport within the probed voltage range. Figure (a) illustrates the CV curves of all the MoO3–V2O5 thin films across different compositions. Among all compositions, it was observed that M20 sample shows the largest integrated area under the CV curve, followed by M40 and V100, which reflects its superior ability to store and deliver charge during cycling. This evolution of the CV response with composition is consistent with previous reports on mixed transition metal oxide systems, where optimized combinations of two oxides often lead to improved electrochemical activity owing to synergistic effects. The areal capacitance and specific capacitance were calculated using relations (11) and (12), where ΔV is the potential window, m is the mass of the active electrode material (g), v is the scan rate, and ∫ IdV represents the area under the CV curve. The active mass loading of each MoO3–V2O5 thin film electrode used in the symmetric two-electrode device configuration was approximately 0.35 mg. As shown in Figure (b), the calculated specific and areal capacitances vary systematically across the composition series, with the M20 sample exhibiting the highest capacitance which delivers an areal capacitance of 3.57 mF cm–2 and specific capacitance of 10.22 F g–1 at a scan rate of 100 mV s–1, far exceeding all other compositions. The coexistence of these two oxides MoO3 and V2O5 in M20 sample results in improved ion accessibility and faster charge transfer kinetics, leading to enhanced electrochemical response. In contrast it was observed that the compositions with higher Mo or V content show reduced current response. In pure V2O5 (V100 sample) strong redox activity was observed with an areal capacitance of 1.58 mF cm–2 and specific capacitance of 4.518 F g–1 at a scan rate of 100 mV s–1, but it performs lower than M20 sample. Charge Storage Mechanism

The charge storage mechanism of the MoO3–V2O5 composite thin film electrodes was analyzed to understand the nature of electrochemical processes and its performance in LiBF4/propylene carbonate electrolyte. In general, electrochemical energy storage in transition metal oxide based electrodes arises from a combination of faradaic processes involving reversible redox reactions and non-faradaic processes associated with electric double layer formation at the electrode/electrolyte interface. In this present system both contributions coexist due to the layered structure and multivalent redox activity of MoO3 and V2O5. In MoO3–V2O5 composite the pseudocapacitive behavior is primarily governed by the redox transitions of Mo and V ions represented by (13) and (14); also, because of electrolyte insertion and extraction of Li+ ions, it happens as shown in (15). The b-value obtained was 0.73, which indicates a hybrid charge storage mechanism where both surface controlled capacitive processes and diffusion limited intercalation leads to overall electrochemical response. The relative contributions of capacitive and diffusion controlled processes strongly depend on the scan rate. At lower scan rates, Li+ ions have sufficient time to diffuse, which leads to a higher diffusion controlled contribution. At higher scan rates, ion diffusion becomes limited and the charge storage is dominated by surface controlled processes. Based on the combined analyses MoO3–V2O5 thin films exhibit a hybrid charge storage mechanism. The synergetic interaction between MoO3 and V2O5 enhances electronic conductivity and provides multiple redox active sites and facilitates rapid ion transport. The EIS data were fitted using appropriate equivalent circuits, where Rs is the series/solution resistance, Rct is the charge-transfer resistance, CPEdl is the constant phase element for the electric double layer, and CPEdiff is the constant phase element representing diffusion-controlled ion transport. The presence of CPEdiff in the V2O5-rich compositions (V100-M40) is consistent with the well-known layered structure of V2O5, whose van der Waals interlayer galleries readily accommodate bulk cation intercalation, giving rise to a diffusion-controlled (Warburg type) low-frequency response. The electrochemical stability of the optimized M20 MoO3–V2O5 thin film electrodes was done using repeated GCD cycling over 10,000 cycles at a current density of 1 A g–1. Cycling stability is important as it tells about the structural stability of the electrode material and the reversibility of the charge storage processes. The M20 electrode shows capacitance retention of 89.7% after 10,000 cycles indicating only minimal loss of the initial capacitance over long cycling. In addition the electrode shows a high coulombic efficiency of 98.4% indicating highly reversible charge–discharge processes as shown in Figure (a). The GCD curves before cycling and after 10,000 cycles have nearly overlapping shapes with a slight increase in the discharge time after prolonged cycling as shown in Figure (b). This increase supports the activation process, indicating improved ion transport pathways within the electrode. A similar trend was observed in CV curves recorded before and after cycling. Figure (c) shows an increase in the enclosed area, which tells about the improved electrode-electrolyte interaction and enhanced accessibility of active sites due to structural rearrangement during cycling. The Nyquist plots obtained from EIS measurments before and after cycling (Figure (d)) show negligible change in series resistance (Rs) indicating stable electrolyte conductivity. A slight variation in charge transfer resistance (Rct) is observed, which can be associated with interfacial modification and activation of the electrode surface. The preservation of the linear low-frequency region confirms that Li+ ion diffusion kinetics remain effective even after extended cycling. Overall the combined results show that M20 MoO3–V2O5 thin film electrode exhibits excellent electrochemical stability.

Conclusion

In summary, MoO3–V2O5 thin films with precisely controlled Mo:V ratios were successfully synthesized using a spray pyrolysis approach, enabling a systematic investigation of composition property relationships. Structural and spectroscopic analyses confirmed the coexistence of MoO3 and V2O5 phases at intermediate compositions, with the formation of Mo–O–V linkages inducing lattice distortion and modifying the local electronic environment. Electrochemical studies revealed that aqueous electrolytes lead to rapid degradation of the films due to hydroxyl-driven reactions, whereas the use of an aprotic organic electrolyte (1 M LiBF4 in propylene carbonate) ensured electrochemical stability and enabled symmetric two-electrode supercapacitor operation over an extended potential window. Among the investigated compositions, the film having Mo to V ratio as 20:80 exhibited the highest specific capacitance. The enhanced charge storage relative to pristine V2O5 is attributed to the synergistic interplay between MoO3 and V2O5 phases, wherein complementary redox activity and improved charge transport collectively contribute to higher capacitance. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy further indicated reduced charge transfer resistance and more ideal capacitive behavior in V2O5 rich compositions. Also, it showed excellent long term stability retaining 89.7% of its initial capacitance after 10,000 GCD cycles along with coulombic efficiency of 98.4%. The minimal change in GCD and CV profiles after prolonged cycling confirms the preservation of the electrode’s electrochemical behavior and structural integrity. Future efforts will focus on increasing active mass loading, optimizing film thickness, and extending this compositional strategy to asymmetric device configurations. Overall, this work highlights the critical role of synergistic phase coexistence and appropriate electrolyte selection in enhancing the electrochemical performance of transition-metal oxide-based supercapacitors.

Lusztig sheaves and integrable highest weight modules in the symmetrizable case
Yixin Lan, Yumeng Wu, Jie Xiao

Abstract
. This paper continues the work of [ 7 ] and [ 8 ] . For a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix C C and the corresponding quantum group 𝐔 \mathbf{U} , we consider an associated quiver Q Q equipped with an admissible automorphism a a . We construct a category 𝒬 / 𝒩 ~ \widetilde{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{N}} obtained from localizations of Lusztig sheaves for the corresponding framed and 2 2 -framed quivers with automorphism. The Grothendieck groups of these categories realize the integrable highest weight module L ⁡ ( λ ) L(\lambda) and the tensor product L ⁡ ( λ 1 ) ⊗ L ⁡ ( λ 2 ) L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2}) of integrable highest weight 𝐔 \mathbf{U} -modules. After quotienting by traceless objects, Lusztig sheaves yield the signed canonical bases of L ⁡ ( λ ) L(\lambda) and L ⁡ ( λ 1 ) ⊗ L ⁡ ( λ 2 ) L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2}) . As applications, we recover symmetrizable crystal structures on Nakajima quiver varieties, Nakajima tensor product varieties, and Lusztig nilpotent varieties of preprojective algebras.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Symmetrizable Cartan data and quantum groups

Let
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}=DB
be a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix, where
D=\mathbf{diag}(\frac{1}{s_{i}})_{i\in I^{\prime}}
is diagonal and
B=(b_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}
is symmetric. This matrix determines a bilinear form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle:{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]\times{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
by
\langle i,j\rangle=c_{ij}
. The corresponding quantum group
\mathbf{U}
is the
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-algebra generated by
E_{i},F_{i},K_{\nu}
for
i\in I^{\prime},\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]
, subject to the following relations:

•

(a)
K_{0}=1
, and
K_{\nu}K_{\nu^{\prime}}=K_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}
for any
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]
;

•

(b)
K_{\nu}E_{i}=v^{\langle\nu,i\rangle}E_{i}K_{\nu}
for
i\in I^{\prime},\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]
;

•

(c)
K_{\nu}F_{i}=v^{-\langle\nu,i\rangle}F_{i}K_{\nu}
for
i\in I^{\prime},\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]
;

•

(d)
E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{\tilde{K}_{i}-\tilde{K}_{-i}}{v_{i}-v_{i}^{-1}},

•

(e)
\sum\limits_{p+q=1-c_{ij}}(-1)^{p}E_{i}^{(p)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(q)}=0
;

•

(f)
\sum\limits_{p+q=1-c_{ij}}(-1)^{p}F_{i}^{(p)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(q)}=0
.

Here we use the notation
v_{i}:=v^{s_{i}}
. For
\nu=\sum\limits_{i\in I^{\prime}}\nu_{i}i\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]
, we denote
\tilde{K}_{\nu}:=\prod\limits_{i\in I^{\prime}}K_{s_{i}\nu_{i}i}
, and
E_{i}^{(n)}:=E_{i}^{n}/[n]^{!}_{i}
,
F_{i}^{(n)}:=F_{i}^{n}/[n]^{!}_{i}
are the divided powers of
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
, where
[n]_{i}:=\frac{v_{i}^{n}-v_{i}^{-n}}{v_{i}-v_{i}^{-1}}
,
[n]^{!}_{i}:=\Pi_{s=1}^{n}[s]_{i}
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbf{U}
-module with a weight-space decomposition
M=\oplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]}M^{\lambda}
such that, for any
m\in M^{\lambda}
, one has
K_{\nu}m=v^{\langle\nu,\lambda\rangle}m
. We say that
M
is integrable if, for every
m\in M
, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
E_{i}^{(n)}m=0
and
F_{i}^{(n)}m=0
for all
n>n_{0}
. We refer to
[
11
,
17
]
for details.

For a dominant weight
\lambda
(that is,
\langle i,\lambda\rangle\in{\mathbb{N}}
for all
i\in I^{\prime}
), the irreducible integrable highest weight module
L(\lambda)
is defined by

L(\lambda):=\mathbf{U}/\left(\sum\limits_{i\in I^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}E_{i}+\sum\limits_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I^{\prime}]}\mathbf{U}(K_{\nu}-v^{\langle\nu,\lambda\rangle})+\sum\limits_{i\in I^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}F_{i}^{\langle i,\lambda\rangle+1}\right).

The
\mathbf{U}
-module structure on
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
is induced by the comultiplication on
\mathbf{U}
.

1.2.
Lusztig’s sheaves for quivers with automorphisms

In
[
20
, Chapters 11, 12, and 14]
, Lusztig considers a finite quiver
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
with an admissible automorphism
a
attached to a given Cartan matrix.

A finite quiver
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
consists of finite sets
I
and
H
, together with a subset
\Omega\subset H
, where
I
is the set of vertices,
H
is the set of all oriented arrows
s(h)\xrightarrow{h}t(h)
, and
H
is the disjoint union of
\Omega
and
\bar{\Omega}
. Here
\bar{\quad}:H\rightarrow H
sends an arrow to the same edge with the opposite orientation. Such a subset
\Omega
is called an orientation of
Q
.

For a finite quiver
(I,H,\Omega)
without loops, an admissible automorphism
a
consists of two permutations
a:I\rightarrow I
and
a:H\rightarrow H
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)
a(s(h))=s(a(h))
and
a(t(h))=t(a(h))
for any
h\in H
;

(b)
s(h)
and
t(h)\in I
belong to different
a
-orbits for any
h\in H
.

Given a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}=DB
, there exists, in general non-uniquely, a finite quiver
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
with an admissible automorphism
a
such that the set of
a
-orbits in
I
is in bijection with
I^{\prime}
, the order of the
a
-orbit corresponding to
i\in I^{\prime}
is
s_{i}
, and the number of arrows between the
a
-orbits corresponding to
i,j\in I^{\prime}
is
c_{i,j}s_{i}=c_{j,i}s_{j}
. For such a quiver with the automorphism
a
, Lusztig considers a full subcategory
\mathcal{Q}=\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{V}}
of semisimple perverse sheaves on the moduli space
\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
of quiver representations. Via the periodic functor
a^{\ast}
, Lusztig has constructed a certain graded additive category
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
from
\mathcal{Q}
, consisting of pairs
(B,\phi)
of semisimple complexes
B
and morphisms
\phi:a^{\ast}B\rightarrow B
. He has also defined the induction and restriction functors for the category
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
. (See
[
20
, Chapter 9 and 12]
or Section 2.1.) The (generalized) Grothendieck group of
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
has a bialgebra structure, which is canonically isomorphic to the positive (or negative) part
\mathbf{U}^{\pm}
of
\mathbf{U}
. Moreover, certain objects of
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}
provide an integral basis of
\mathbf{U}^{\pm}
, which is called the signed basis by Lusztig.

When the generalized Cartan matrix
C
is symmetric, or equivalently, the admissible automorphism
a
is trivial, the simple perverse sheaves in
\mathcal{Q}
form a basis of
\mathbf{U}^{\pm}
, which is called the canonical basis and shares many remarkable properties.

Lusztig also defines induction and restriction functors as follows. For a fixed subspace
\mathbf{W}\subset\mathbf{V}
,
\mbox{\rm dim}\mathbf{W}=\mbox{\rm dim}\mathbf{V}_{2}
,
\mathbf{V}\cong\mathbf{V}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{V}_{2}
,
P
is the stabilizer of
\mathbf{W}
in
G_{\mathbf{V}}
,
U
is the unipotent radical of
P
and
F=\{x\in\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}}|x(\mathbf{W})\subset\mathbf{W}\}
.

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}_{1}}\times\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}\xleftarrow{p_{1}}G_{\mathbf{V}}\times_{U}F\xrightarrow{p_{2}}G_{\mathbf{V}}\times_{P}F\xrightarrow{p_{3}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}},

where
p_{1}(g,x)=(x|_{\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{W}},x|_{\mathbf{W}})
,
p_{2}
is the quotient map, and
p_{3}(g,x)=g(x)
.

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}_{1}}\times\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}_{2}}\xleftarrow{\kappa}F\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V}},

where
\iota
is the embedding and
\kappa(x)=(x|_{\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{W}},x|_{\mathbf{W}})
.

Lusztig has defined
\mathbf{Ind}^{\mathbf{V}}_{\mathbf{V}_{1},\mathbf{V}_{2}}=(p_{3})_{!}(p_{2})_{\flat}(p_{1})^{*}[d_{1}-d_{2}]
, where the relative dimension of
p_{i}
is
d_{i}
,
(p_{2})_{\flat}
is the inverse functor of
p_{2}^{*}
and
\mathbf{Res}_{\mathbf{V}_{1},\mathbf{V}_{2}}^{\mathbf{V}}=(\kappa)_{!}(\iota)^{*}[d_{1}-d_{2}-2\mbox{\rm dim}G_{\mathbf{V}}/P]
.

1.3.
Localizations of Lusztig’s sheaves and other categorical realizations

When the generalized Cartan matrix
C
is symmetric, inspired by
[
31
,
32
,
16
]
, the authors of
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
have considered certain localizations of Lusztig’s category
\mathcal{Q}
to realize the irreducible integrable highest weight module
L(\lambda)
and tensor product
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
.

For a dominant weight
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, they have considered the moduli space
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet},\Omega}
of a framed quiver and certain thick subcategory
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}
of the
G_{\mathbf{V}}
-equivariant derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet},\Omega})
of constructible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
-sheaves. They have defined functors
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i},i\in I
for the Verdier quotient
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet},\Omega})/\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}
and showed that Lusztig’s sheaves
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}}
for the framed quiver are preserved by these functors
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
(up to isomorphisms in localizations). (See
[
7
]
or Section 2.2.) The Grothendieck group of
\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}
becomes a
\mathbf{U}
-module with the action of functors
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, and is isomorphic to
L(\lambda)
canonically. Moreover, the nonzero simple perverse sheaves in
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}
form the canonical basis of
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
.

1.4.
Perverse sheaf realizations of integrable highest weight modules and their tensor products

Since the work of Zheng
[
31
,
32
]
, there has been a sequence of developments in the categorification of integrable highest weight modules. For example, the authors of
[
5
]
construct geometric
\mathfrak{g}
-actions on coherent sheaves over quiver varieties, while the authors of
[
4
]
consider geometric
\mathfrak{g}
-actions on quantized quiver varieties. Webster also uses both D-modules
[
30
]
and diagrammatic approaches
[
29
]
to construct similar categorical actions. Kashiwara and Kang, in
[
12
]
, have constructed highest weight modules via KLR algebras, including the symmetrizable cases.

The present paper provides a geometric construction of highest weight modules and their tensor products in the symmetrizable case, within the framework of Lusztig
[
20
, Chapter 12]
. This work continues the program initiated by Fang, Lan, and Xiao
[
8
,
7
]
.

From the perspective of sheaf complexes, it is natural to ask how to generalize the construction of
[
7
]
to the symmetrizable case. More precisely, given a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
C
, our goal is to realize the irreducible highest weight module
L(\lambda)
of
\mathbf{U}
associated with
C
via sheaves on moduli spaces of a quiver with automorphism.

In this article, we apply Lusztig’s method of periodic functors to the localizations for
N
-framed quivers introduced in
[
8
]
, and obtain a category
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{N}}
. Its Grothendieck group is a
\mathbf{U}
-module, which is canonically isomorphic to
L(\lambda)
of the symmetrizable quantum group associated to
C
and, in the
2
-framed case, to
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
. See Theorem
4.6
and
4.23
for details.

Moreover, under the canonical isomorphism, the objects
(B,\phi)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{N}}
naturally give the signed basis of
L(\lambda)
, and this basis is almost orthogonal with respect to a contravariant geometric pairing. See Proposition
4.19
for details. The signed basis of
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
obtained in the same way coincides with Lusztig’s signed basis for tensor products of highest weight modules.

As an application, following
[
8
]
we give a new proof of the Yang–Baxter relation for the operators
\mathbf{R}_{ij}
arising from the functor
\Delta^{\prime}\circ(\Delta^{\prime,\vee})^{-1}
. We also deduce a symmetrizable crystal structure on Nakajima’s quiver varieties in
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
, Nakajima’s tensor product varieties in
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
21
]
and Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties
[
18
]
of preprojective algebras. See Proposition
5.1
, Theorems
5.4
and
5.7
and their corollaries for details.

1.5.
Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we recall the notation and results from
[
7
]
. In Section 3, we define our category
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{N}}
and its functors, and prove the commutation relations of these functors. The proofs of these commutation relations differ substantially from those in the symmetric case. In Section 4, we determine the module structure of the Grothendieck group of

2.1.
Lusztig’s sheaves

In this subsection, we recall the category of Lusztig sheaves for quivers. Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field with
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=p>0
. Given a quiver
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
and an
I
-graded
\mathbf{k}
-space
\mathbf{V}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{V}_{i}
of dimension vector
\nu
, the affine variety
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
is defined by

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}=\bigoplus\limits_{h\in\Omega}\mbox{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{V}_{s(h)},\mathbf{V}_{t(h)}).

The algebraic group
G_{\mathbf{V}}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}GL(\mathbf{V}_{i})
acts naturally on
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
by change of basis. We denote the
G_{\mathbf{V}}
-equivariant derived category of constructible
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
-sheaves on
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
by
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega})
. We denote by
[n]
the shift functor by
n
and by
\mathbf{D}
the Verdier duality functor.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of finite sequences
\boldsymbol{\nu}=(\nu^{1},\nu^{2},\cdots,\nu^{s})
of dimension vectors such that each
\nu^{l}=a_{l}i_{l}
for some
a_{l}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geqslant 1}
and
i_{l}\in I
. If
\sum\limits_{1\leqslant l\leqslant s}\nu^{l}=\nu
, we say
\boldsymbol{\nu}
is a flag type of
\nu
or
\mathbf{V}
.

For a flag type
\boldsymbol{\nu}
of
\mathbf{V}
, the flag variety
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega}
is the smooth variety which consists of pairs
(x,f)
, where
x\in\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
and
f=(\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}^{s}\subseteq\mathbf{V}^{s-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbf{V}^{0}=0)
is a filtration of the
I
-graded space such that
x(\mathbf{V}^{l})\subseteq\mathbf{V}^{l}
and the dimension vector of
\mathbf{V}^{l-1}/\mathbf{V}^{l}=\nu^{l}
for any
l
. There is a proper map
\pi_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega}:\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega};(x,f)\mapsto x.
Hence by the decomposition theorem in
[
3
]
, the complex
L_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}=(\pi_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega})_{!}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mbox{\rm dim}\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega}]
is a semisimple complex on
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}
, where
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
is the constant sheaf on
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\Omega}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
be the set consisting of those simple perverse sheaves
L
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega})
such that
L
is a direct summand (up to shifts) of

for some flag type
\boldsymbol{\nu}
of
\mathbf{V}
. Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{V}}
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega})
, which consists of
finite direct sums of shifted simple perverse sheaves in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V}}
. Following
[
28
]
, we call

the category of Lusztig sheaves for

.

In order to realize the tensor product of
N
irreducible integrable highest weight
\mathbf{U}
-modules, we introduce the
N
-framed quiver. The
N
-framed quiver
\tilde{Q}^{(N)}
of

is defined by
\tilde{Q}^{(N)}=(I^{(N)},\tilde{H}^{(N)},\tilde{\Omega}^{(N)})
. Here

such that each
I^{k}
is a copy of
I
, and we denote the
k

by

, and

is the involution defined by taking the opposite orientation. When

-framed quiver

is exactly the framed quiver considered in
[
7
]
and
[
32
]
.

Given graded spaces
\mathbf{W}^{1},\mathbf{W}^{2},\cdots,\mathbf{W}^{N}
, with each
\mathbf{W}^{k}
supported on

, the moduli space of the
N
-framed quiver is defined by

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet},\Omega}:=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I,1\leqslant k\leqslant N}\mbox{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{V}_{i},\mathbf{W}^{k}_{i^{k}})\oplus\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\Omega}.

The algebraic group
G_{\mathbf{V}}
acts canonically on

and we consider the

-equivariant derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{G_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet},\Omega})

Assume that

and

for each
l
and
k
. We take
\boldsymbol{e}^{k}=(d^{k}_{i_{1}}i^{k}_{1},d^{k}_{i_{2}}i^{k}_{2},\cdots,d^{k}_{i_{t}}i^{k}_{t})

\mathbf{W}^{k}

, the flag type

for some

. In particular, the sheaf

.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the category

Definition 3.1
.

Let

{\mathcal{C}}

as follows:

\bullet

.

\bullet
For any

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}}((A,\varphi),(A^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}))=\{f\in\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(A,A^{\prime})|f\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}(a^{*}(f))\}.

\bullet
The direct sum of

is defined naturally by

.

Definition 3.2
.

An object

and an integer

Definition 3.3
.

We say

and
B
in

Lemma 3.4

to be the vector space

\bigoplus_{h\in\Omega}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{s(h)},\mathbf{V}_{t(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{h\in\bar{\Omega}}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{s(h)},\mathbf{V}_{t(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{i}),

with
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}_{v}:=\prod_{k\in I}\text{GL}(\mathbf{V}_{k})
acting on it naturally. The moment map is defined by

\mu:\mathbf{M}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k\in I}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{k},\mathbf{V}_{k}).

For
B\in\bigoplus_{h\in\Omega}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{s(h)},\mathbf{V}_{t(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{h\in\bar{\Omega}}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{s(h)},\mathbf{V}_{t(h)})
,
j\in\bigoplus_{k\in I}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{V}_{k},\mathbf{W}_{k})
, and
i\in\bigoplus_{k\in I}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{W}_{k},\mathbf{V}_{k})
, the moment map can be denoted by:

\mu(B,i,j):=\sum_{h\in\Omega\cup\bar{\Omega}}\varepsilon(h)B_{h}B_{\bar{h}}+ij,

where
\varepsilon(h)=1
if
h\in\Omega
and
\varepsilon(h)=-1
if
h\in\bar{\Omega}
.

The affine variety
\mu^{-1}(0)
gives rise to the affine GIT quotient
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}={\mathcal{M}}_{0}(v,w):=\textbf{Spec}A(\mu^{-1}(0))^{\mathbf{G}}
. Fix the character
\chi
of
\mathbf{G}
given by
\chi(g)=\prod_{k\in I}\det(g_{k})^{-1}.
The quiver variety is defined by
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}(v,w):=\textbf{Proj}\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}A(\mu^{-1}(0))^{\mathbf{G},\chi^{n}}
.

Remark 5.3
.

When
\bf W=0
, the variety
\mu^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{V}}
is the moduli space of the preprojective algebra. Lusztig’s nilpotent variety
\Lambda_{\mathbf{V}}
is the subset of
\mu^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{V}}
consisting of nilpotent representations of the preprojective algebra. (See
[
17
, Section 12]
.)

Lift the action of
\mathbf{G}
to
\mu^{-1}(0)\times{\mathbb{C}}
by
g(B,i,j,z)=(gBg^{-1},gi,jg^{-1},\chi(g)^{-1}z)
, then
\mu^{-1}(0)^{s}:=\{(B,i,j)\in\mu^{-1}(0)\mid\overline{\mathbf{G}(B,i,j,z)}\cap(\mu^{-1}(0)\times\{0\})=\emptyset,z\neq 0\}
. According to Nakajima,
\mathbf{G}
acts freely on
\mu^{-1}(0)^{s}
, and the GIT quotient
\mu^{-1}(0)^{s}/\mathbf{G}
coincides with the geometric points of
{\mathcal{M}}
. There is a natural projective morphism
\pi:{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, with
\pi^{-1}(0)
being a Lagrangian subvariety denoted by
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
, which is homotopy equivalent to
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Nakajima’s quiver variety admits a stratification

{\mathcal{M}}(v,w)=\bigcup_{r\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{M}}_{k,r}(v,w),k\in I,

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{k,r}(v,w):=\{(B,i,j)\in{\mathcal{M}}(v,w)\mid\text{codim}(\text{im}\oplus_{h\in H,t(h)=k}B_{h}+\text{im}i_{k})=r\}.

There is a natural smooth map,
p:{\mathcal{M}}_{k,r}(v,w)\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{k,0}(v-rk,w)
whose fiber is a connected Grassmannian. This gives a bijection between the irreducible components of
\mathfrak{L}_{k,r}(v,w)
and
\mathfrak{L}_{k,0}(v-rk,w)
, where
\mathfrak{L}_{k,r}(v,w)=\mathfrak{L}\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{k,r}(v,w)
. For an irreducible component
X\in\mathfrak{L}_{k,r}(v,w)
, the closure
\bar{X}
of
X
is an irreducible component of
\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
and we denote
t_{k}(\bar{X})=r
. Hence
p
induces a bijection by
X\mapsto\overline{p(X\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{k,r}(v,w))}
and we denote this bijection by

\rho_{k,r}:\{X\in\text{Irr}(\mathfrak{L}(v,w))\mid t_{k}(X)=r\}\rightarrow\{X\in\text{Irr}(\mathfrak{L}(v-rk,w))\mid t_{k}(X)=0\}.

The admissible automorphism
a
acts naturally on
\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
and induces a permutation, which is still denoted by
a
, on the set of its irreducible components. Let
\text{Irr}^{a}\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
be the set of
a
-fixed irreducible components. For any irreducible
X\in\text{Irr}^{a}\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
, if
t_{k}(X)=r
, then
t_{k^{\prime}}=r
for any
k^{\prime}\in\underline{k}
. Then the map
\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)=\prod\limits_{k\in\underline{k}}\rho_{k,r}(X)
is well-defined. Since each
\rho_{k,r}
is bijective, it follows that
\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)
is also fixed by
a
. Hence
\rho_{\underline{k},r}
is a bijection between the corresponding sets of
a
-fixed irreducible components.

These maps
\rho_{\underline{k},r}
induce a crystal operator on the set
\bigcup\limits_{v}\text{Irr}^{a}\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
in the following way:

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{\underline{k}}(X)=\begin{cases}\rho^{-1}_{\underline{k},r-1}\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)&\text{if }t_{k}(X)=r>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}\end{split}

and

\tilde{f}_{\underline{k}}(X)=\rho^{-1}_{\underline{k},r+1}\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X),\text{if }t_{k}(X)=r.

(For the detailed definition of the crystal operators and crystal structures, see
[
13
]
. )

For
X\in\text{Irr}^{a}\mathfrak{L}(v,w)
, we also set
\epsilon_{\underline{k}}(X)=t_{k}(X)
and
\varphi_{\underline{k}}(X)=t_{k}(X)+\langle h_{\underline{k}},wt(X)\rangle
, then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4
.

Given a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}
, let
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
be an associated quiver with an admissible automorphism
a
. We identify
\underline{I}
with
I^{\prime}
, then the crystal structure of
(\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(\mathfrak{L}(v,w))^{a},\tilde{f}_{\underline{k}},\tilde{e}_{\underline{k}},\epsilon_{\underline{k}},\varphi_{\underline{k}},\underline{k}\in I^{\prime})
is isomorphic to the crystal structure
B(\lambda)
of the irreducible highest weight module
L(\lambda)
of the quantum group associated to
C
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of
[(L,\phi)]
, where
(L,\phi)\cong\mathbf{D}((L,\phi))
. We say
(L,\phi)
is equivalent to
(L^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
if
[(L,\phi)]=\pm[(L^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})]
, and denote the set of equivalence classes of
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. A crystal structure on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
can be described as follows:

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{\underline{i}}([(L,\phi)])=\begin{cases}\pi^{-1}_{\underline{i},r-1}\pi_{\underline{i},r}([(L,\phi)])&\text{if }t_{i}(L)=r>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}\end{split}

and

\tilde{f}_{\underline{i}}([(L,\phi)])=\pi^{-1}_{\underline{i},r+1}\pi_{\underline{i},r}([(L,\phi)]),\text{if }t_{i}(L)=r.

Applying
[
20
, Theorem 18.3.8]
to the signed basis of
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{K}(\nu,\omega)}\cong{{}_{\mathcal{A}}L(\lambda)}
, it follows that there is an isomorphism of crystals
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cong B(\lambda)
. Hence it suffices to show
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(\mathfrak{L}(v,w))^{a}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
as crystals.

Denote
\tilde{e}_{\underline{i}}^{max}([(L,\phi)])=\tilde{e}_{\underline{i}}^{r}([(L,\phi)])=[\pi_{\underline{i},r}((L,\phi))]
for those pairs with
t_{i}(L)=r
. Similarly, we can define
\tilde{e}_{\underline{i}}^{max}(X)=\rho_{\underline{i},r}(X)
for an irreducible component
X
with
t_{i}(X)=r
. By
[
7
, Theorem 4.20]
, there is a bijection
\Phi
from the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{W}}
of nonzero simple perverse sheaves in
\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}}/{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbf{V}}
to the set
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))
of irreducible components such that
\pi_{i,r}(L)=K
if and only if
\rho_{i,r}(\Phi(L))=\Phi(K).

If we forget the isomorphisms
\phi
, then
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is in bijection with the set of
a^{*}-
invariant nonzero simple perverse sheaves in
\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}}/{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbf{V}}
and

\pi_{\underline{i},r}(L)=\prod\limits_{i\in\underline{i}}\pi_{i,r}(L)
for any simple perverse sheaf
L
with
a^{\ast}L\cong L
. In particular,
\Phi
restricts to a bijection
\Phi^{a}
between the
a
-fixed points of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{W}}
and those of
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))
, such that
\pi_{\underline{i},r}(L)=K
if and only if
\rho_{\underline{i},r}(\Phi^{a}(L))=\Phi^{a}(K).
Since the
a
-fixed points of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{W}}
are naturally in bijection with
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and the
a
-fixed points of
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))
are
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))^{a}
, we obtain a bijection
\Phi^{a}:\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rightarrow\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))^{a}
which intertwines
\pi_{\underline{i},r}
and
\rho_{\underline{i},r}
. Since the crystal operators
\tilde{e}_{\underline{i}}
and
\tilde{f}_{\underline{i}}
are uniquely determined by
\pi_{\underline{i},r}
and
\rho_{\underline{i},r}
, it follows that
\Phi^{a}:\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rightarrow\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(L(v,w))^{a}
is an isomorphism of crystals in the sense of Kashiwara
[
13
]
.

∎

Choose the dimension vector of
\mathbf{W}
sufficiently large so that
\lambda=\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}\omega_{i^{\prime}}\beta_{i^{\prime}}
satisfies
\omega_{i^{\prime}}>\nu_{i}
for each
i
in the
a
-orbit corresponding to
i^{\prime}
and so that
B(\lambda)_{\nu}
is in bijection with
B(\infty)_{\nu}
for a fixed weight
\nu
. Then we obtain the following corollary, which is another main result of
[
27
]
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Given a symmetrizable Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}
, let
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
be an associated quiver with an admissible automorphism
a
, let
\bigcup\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\text{Irr}(\Lambda_{\bf V})^{a}
be the set of
a
-fixed irreducible components of Lusztig’s nilpotent variety
\coprod\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\Lambda_{\bf V}
of the preprojective algebra of
Q
. Then
\bigcup\limits_{\mathbf{V}}\text{Irr}(\Lambda_{\bf V})^{a}
carries a natural crystal structure and is canonically isomorphic to
B(\infty)
of the quantum group associated to
C
.

Proof.

Recall that by
[
22
, Lemma 5.8]
, the set of irreducible components of
L(v,w)
is in bijection with a subset of irreducible components of
\Lambda_{\bf V}
via the projection
(B,i,0)\mapsto B
. Observe that if
\mathbf{W}
is large enough, the map
(B,i,0)\mapsto B
induces a bijection from
\text{Irr}(L(v,w))
to
\text{Irr}(\Lambda_{\bf V})
, which is compatible with the crystal operator. Restricting this bijection to the
a
-fixed irreducible components completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

When the automorphism
a
is trivial, i.e, the Cartan matrix is symmetric, our results also agree with the main result of
[
14
]
and
[
26
]
.

5.3.
Symmetrizable crystal structure of tensor products

With the notation of Section 5.2, fix a decomposition
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^{1}\oplus\mathbf{W}^{2}
such that their dimension vectors
|\mathbf{W}^{1}|=\omega^{1}
and
|\mathbf{W}^{2}|=\omega^{2}
, then
\omega=\omega^{1}+\omega^{2}
. Recall that the group
\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{W}}
acts on
\mathbf{M}
by conjugation. This induces a
\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{W}}
-action on
\mathcal{M}
. Then the one-parameter subgroup

\displaystyle\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{W}^{1}}\times\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{W}^{2}}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto({\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbf{W}^{1}},t\,{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbf{W}^{2}})

acts on
\mathcal{M}
. Nakajima’s (Lagrangian) tensor product variety is defined to be

\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)=\{[x,y,z]\in\mathcal{M}(\nu,\omega)\mid\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lambda(t).[x,y,z]\in\bigsqcup_{\nu^{\prime}+\nu^{\prime\prime}=\nu}\mathfrak{L}(\nu^{\prime},\omega^{1})\times\mathfrak{L}(\nu^{\prime\prime},\omega^{2})\}.

Similarly, the morphism
p
also induces a bijection

\rho_{k,r}:\{X\in\text{Irr}(\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)\mid t_{k}(X)=r\}\rightarrow\{X\in\text{Irr}(\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu-rk,\omega))\mid t_{k}(X)=0\}.

The admissible automorphism
a
acts naturally on
\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)
and induces a permutation, which is still denoted by
a
, on the set of its irreducible components. Let
\text{Irr}^{a}\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)
be the set of
a
-fixed irreducible components. For any irreducible
X\in\text{Irr}^{a}\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)
, if
t_{k}(X)=r
, then
t_{k^{\prime}}=r
for any
k^{\prime}\in\underline{k}
. Then the map
\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)=\prod\limits_{k\in\underline{k}}\rho_{k,r}(X)
is well-defined. Since each
\rho_{k,r}
is bijective, it follows that
\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)
is also fixed by
a
. Hence
\rho_{\underline{k},r}
is a bijection between the corresponding sets of
a
-fixed irreducible components.

These maps
\rho_{\underline{k},r}
induce crystal operators on the set
\bigcup\limits_{v}\text{Irr}^{a}\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega)
in the following way:

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{\underline{k}}(X)=\begin{cases}\rho^{-1}_{\underline{k},r-1}\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X)&\text{if }t_{k}(X)=r>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}\end{split}

\tilde{f}_{\underline{k}}(X)=\rho^{-1}_{\underline{k},r+1}\rho_{\underline{k},r}(X),\text{if }t_{k}(X)=r;

\epsilon_{\underline{k}}(X)=t_{k}(X);

and

\varphi_{\underline{k}}(X)=t_{k}(X)+\langle h_{\underline{k}},wt(X)\rangle.

Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.7
.

Given a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I^{\prime}}
, let
Q=(I,H,\Omega)
be an associated quiver with an admissible automorphism
a
. Identifying
\underline{I}
with
I^{\prime}
, the crystal structure of
(\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}I^{a}}\text{Irr}(\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega))^{a},\tilde{f}_{\underline{k}},\tilde{e}_{\underline{k}},\epsilon_{\underline{k}},\varphi_{\underline{k}},\underline{k}\in I^{\prime})
is isomorphic to the crystal structure
B(\lambda_{1})\otimes B(\lambda_{2})
of the tensor product
L(\lambda_{1})\otimes L(\lambda_{2})
. Here
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the dominant weights of the quantum group associated to
C
determined by
\mathbf{W}^{1}
and
\mathbf{W}^{2}
respectively.

Proof.

We have the following facts.

(1) In
[
10
]
, there is a bijection
\Phi=\Phi_{Q^{(2)}}
between the set
\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{N}}\text{Irr}(\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega))
and the set of nonzero simple perverse sheaves in
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}
such that
\Phi
intertwines the bijections
\rho_{i,r}
and
\pi_{i,r}
.

(2) The bijections
\pi_{i,r}
induce operators

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{i}(L)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-1}_{i,r-1}\pi_{i,r}(L)&\text{if }t_{i}(L)=r>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}\end{split}

and

\tilde{f}_{i}(L)=\pi^{-1}_{i,r+1}\pi_{i,r}(L),\text{if }t_{i}(L)=r.

By
[
9
, Lemma 5.4]
, the operators defined above can be identified with Kashiwara’s crystal operators on the tensor product crystals for the symmetric quantum group associated to
Q
via the canonical isomorphism
\Delta
. (That is, we forget the automorphism
a
.)

Restricting (1) and (2) to the
a
-fixed subsets of
\text{Irr}(\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}(\nu,\omega))
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}}\backslash(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{V}}\cap\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{V},\mathbf{W}^{\bullet}})
, the proof follows by the same argument as in Theorem
5.4
. ∎

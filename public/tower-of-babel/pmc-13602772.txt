Integrative plasma proteomic network analysis identifies physical activity-associated protein modules potentially mediating type 2 diabetes risk
Authors: Zhenqian Wang, Chen-Yang Su, Jiawen Lu, Masashi Hasebe, Alejandro Mejia-Garcia, Hsuan Megan Tsao, Jingyi Hu, Tianyuan Lu, Sirui Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundModerate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes (T2D). Identifying protein modules that mediate this association may help dissect these biological underpinnings, but they remain incompletely characterized.MethodsWe studied 28,842 diabetes-free UK Biobank participants with baseline plasma proteomics. Guideline-adherent MVPA was defined as ≥ 600 metabolic equivalent of task-minutes per week. We first evaluated associations of MVPA with incident T2D and 2910 plasma proteins, followed by mediation analyses of individual proteins. We then identified protein co-expression modules using multiscale embedded gene co-expression network analysis (MEGENA), tested module-level associations with MVPA, and mediation of incident T2D. Protein modules were further evaluated using module-level Mendelian randomization, whole-genome-sequencing genome-wide association studies, human pancreatic islet single-cell RNA sequencing, leave-one-out sensitivity analyses, and pathway enrichment analyses.ResultsOver a median 13.60 years (IQR: [12.82, 14.35]), 1225 participants developed incident T2D. Guideline-adherent MVPA was associated with lower T2D risk and with 650 circulating proteins, of which 451 were Bonferroni-significant mediators. MEGENA analysis identified 171 robust modules, with 67 mediating the MVPA-T2D association. Twelve terminal modules, representing the finest-resolution functions, were selected for further analysis based on their significant mediated effects and enrichment in potential individual mediators. Genetic and functional analyses highlighted two modules: CKB-IGFBP2-centric module was positively associated with MVPA (β [95% CI] 0.196 [0.168, 0.224]) and genetically associated with lower T2D risk (OR [95% CI] 0.57 [0.43, 0.77]). Conversely, RIDA-PCBD1-centric module was inversely associated with MVPA (− 0.134 [− 0.162, − 0.108]) and genetically associated with higher T2D risk (1.54 [1.13, 2.09]). Compared to individual protein analyses, these modules captured unique pathways, supporting the CKB-IGFBP2-centered and RIDA-PCBD1-centered modules as antigen-presentation-enriched and complement/apoptic-cell-clearance-enriched modules, respectively. Finally, the constituent genes of both modules were enriched in T2D-related gene changes in pancreatic β-cells, with the antigen-presentation-enriched module showing additional enrichment across α-, δ-, and ductal cells.ConclusionsMVPA may reduce T2D risk through two coordinated plasma protein networks rather than only through individual circulating proteins. Evaluating coordinated protein modules may provide a system-level biological framework for understanding how exercise protects against T2D.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-026-03363-1.

Background

Type 2 diabetes (T2D) is a major and growing global health burden, projected to affect more than 780 million adults by 2045 and to contribute substantially to cardiovascular morbidity, premature mortality, and healthcare costs [1]. Regular moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) is a well-established behaviour for T2D prevention. Current World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines recommend that adults engage in least 150 min of moderate-intensity or 75 min of vigorous-intensity activity per week, corresponding to approximately 600 metabolic equivalent of task (MET)-minutes per week [2]. Large prospective cohorts and Mendelian randomization (MR) studies have linked guideline-adherent MVPA to a substantially lower risk of incident T2D, including risk reductions that persist after accounting for adiposity and other lifestyle factors [3, 4]. Despite this robust epidemiological evidence, the circulating molecular pathways through which physical activity reduces diabetes risk remain incompletely characterized. Clarifying these pathways may help inform future T2D prevention strategies, including approaches for individuals who have difficulty achieving recommended activity levels [5].

Circulating proteins may provide measurable links between physical activity and metabolic pathways relevant to T2D. Some exercise-responsive signaling proteins, often referred to as exerkines, have been implicated in communication among skeletal muscle, liver, adipose tissue, vasculature, the brain, and pancreatic islets [6]. High-throughput affinity proteomics now enables population-scale assessment of thousands of plasma proteins in deeply phenotyped resources, such as the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project (UKB-PPP) [7]. In the UK Biobank, MVPA has been linked to a plasma proteomic signature associated with lower risk of cancer and cardiometabolic multimorbidity, implicating individual proteins such as LEP, MSTN, and LPL [8]. Separately, plasma proteomic signatures have been shown to be associated with adiposity, cardiorespiratory fitness, and incident T2D [9]. These findings suggest that circulating proteins may capture biological pathways linking physical activity with metabolic disease risk. However, these investigations have mainly evaluated proteins as isolated biomarkers. Such single-protein mediation analyses in high-dimensional proteomics face a substantial multiple-testing burden and usually limit pathway analyses to proteins passing significance thresholds. Consequently, they may overlook coordinated protein networks in which individual proteins have modest associations but collectively reflect biologically relevant pathways [10].

Circulating proteins with related functions tend to vary together in co-expression networks. Such serum protein networks have been shown to link genetic variation to disease [11], and multiscale proteomic modules have been used to identify biological processes and candidate pathways in complex diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease and heart failure [12, 13]. Habitual aerobic activity has likewise been associated with coordinated proteomic patterns [14]. Analysing these modules, rather than individual proteins alone, may therefore uncover coordinated processes in which constituent proteins each have modest associations but jointly reflect a shared pathway. Integrating a module-based approach with mediation analysis may help identify protein modules that potentially mediate the association between physical activity and T2D, thereby capture coordinated pathways that are less apparent at the level of individual proteins. However, the specific plasma protein modules that may mediate the association between MVPA and incident T2D remain unclear. Integrating these modules with cell-type-specific expression data from disease-relevant tissues may further localize circulating proteomic signals to biological contexts relevant to T2D [15, 16]. This combined approach can clarify how MVPA-associated plasma protein modules may influence disease-relevant cell types and pathways.

To address this knowledge gap, we sought to identify plasma protein co-expression modules that may mediate the association between MVPA and incident T2D (workflow detailed in Fig. 1). We first evaluated individual proteins as potential mediators and then integrated co-expression network analysis with mediation analysis to identify candidate mediating modules. To provide complementary genetic evidence, we used MR and whole-genome sequencing (WGS)-based genome-wide association studies (GWAS) to characterize prioritized modules. Finally, we assessed whether these modules were enriched in T2D-relevant pancreatic islet cell types using a human single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset.

Methods

Study cohort and population

The UK Biobank is a large population-based prospective cohort, which recruited over 500,000 individuals aged 40 to 69 in 22 assessment centres in England, Scotland, and Wales [17]. Participant data include demographics, various questionnaire information, electronic health record linkage, whole-genome sequencing, and proteomics. For this study, we initially included 51,840 participants with baseline proteomic data, removing individuals with > 50% of missing values across all measured proteins and those from the COVID-19 repeat-imaging subset. Prevalent diabetes at baseline was defined by matching any of the following criteria: (i) HbA1c ≥ 48 mmol/mol; (ii) physician-diagnosed diabetes; (iii) self-reported insulin use; or (iv) International Classification of Diseases, Ninth or Tenth Revision (ICD-9: 250 / ICD-10: E10-E14) codes. After sequentially excluding participants with baseline prevalent diabetes (N = 2140) and those with missing data on MVPA and key covariates (N = 20,858), we finally obtained a prospective cohort of 28,842 individuals (Supplementary Fig. 1). All UK Biobank participants provided written informed consent. The UK Biobank received ethical approval from the North West Multi-centre Research Ethics Committee (REC reference 11/NW/0382) and this study was conducted under approved application number 73,958.

Assessment of MVPA and ascertainment of T2D

Baseline physical activity was evaluated using a touchscreen survey adapted from the validated International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ). Participants reported the weekly frequency and daily duration of walking, moderate-intensity, and vigorous-intensity activities. To quantify energy expenditure, these self-reported durations were multiplied by standard MET coefficients and summed to calculate total weekly MET-minutes [18]. To assess adherence to clinical recommendations, MVPA was analyzed as a binary exposure based on the WHO guideline threshold [19]. Participants who accumulated ≥ 600 MET-minutes per week were classified as having guideline-adherent MVPA, whereas those accumulating < 600 MET-minutes per week were classified as having insufficient MVPA.

Participants with T2D were identified using the UK Biobank first-occurrence health-outcome fields (https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/ukb/docs/first_occurrences_outcomes.pdf). Specifically, the date of first appearance of T2D was identified according to ICD-10 code E11 and ICD-9 codes 250.00 and 250.10 using hospital admission electronic health records, death records and self-report data. Incident T2D cases were defined by comparing the date of first appearance and the date of enrolment. Follow-up time was calculated as the period from baseline to the time of T2D diagnosis, death, loss to follow-up or the censoring date of October 31st, 2022, whichever came first.

Proteomic measurements and imputation

The UKB-PPP consortium conducted large-scale plasma proteomic research, measuring 2923 unique proteins across 54,219 individuals [7]. Protein abundances were quantified as Normalized Protein eXpression (NPX) values, and missing data were imputed using the chained random forests algorithm [20]. This imputation process was restricted to quality-controlled individuals with complete age and sex data, and to proteins with missingness of < 20%. To account for panel-specific variations, the imputation algorithm was applied independently to each of the 8 Olink biomarker panels. Following imputation, a completed data comprising 51,840 participants with 2910 proteins was obtained. Detailed information on proteomic measurements and imputation pipeline was described in the Supplementary Notes.

Whole-genome sequencing and quality control

WGS datasets of UK Biobank were used and pre-processed to subsequent GWASs [21]. Briefly, WGS were sequenced to an average coverage of 32.5× using Illumina Novaseq6000 sequencing machines. Rigorous and multi-step quality control (QC) protocols were implemented prior to downstream analysis. At the sample level, we systematically excluded participants exhibiting excessive genetic missingness, sex-chromosome aneuploidies, abnormal heterozygosity rates, and a mismatch between their reported clinical sex and their genetic sex. At the variant level, SNPs with call rate < 98%, minor allele frequency (MAF) < 0.01, and deviations from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (P < 1 × 10−15) were filtered out.

Assessment of covariates

Baseline covariates were systematically collected via touchscreen surveys and physical assessments. Sociodemographic adjustments included age, sex, ethnicity (White, Mixed, Asian, Black, or Other), educational attainment (degree/professional qualification versus none), and the Townsend Deprivation Index (TDI). Lifestyle factors consisted of smoking and drinking status (never, previous, current), unhealthy sleep duration (≤ 6 or ≥ 8 h), and a 10-component diet quality score [22]. Histories of cancer were defined using self-report data and physician diagnoses. All analytical models were further adjusted for technical and genetic confounders by including the protein measurement batch and the top ten genetic principal components (PCs).

Single-cell RNA sequencing data in human pancreatic islets

To explore our findings within the islet microenvironment, we utilized a comprehensive human pancreatic islet scRNA-seq dataset [23]. The original cohort profiled 245,878 individual cells across 48 donors. For the current analysis, we restricted the dataset to include only individuals with T2D (N = 17) and normoglycemic controls (N = 17), explicitly excluding all individuals with prediabetic status. Details on sequencing and QC of this data were in Supplementary Notes. Following data normalization and dimensionality reduction, after-QC cells were partitioned into 14 distinct, annotated islet cell types [23]. We directly adopted these validated cell cluster definitions for all subsequent cell-type-specific evaluations.

Mediation analyses of individual plasma proteins

To elucidate the potential mechanistic pathways linking MVPA to T2D risk, we executed a multi-step mediation analysis. First, Cox proportional hazards (PH) regression was employed to investigate the association between WHO guideline-adherent MVPA and the risk of incident T2D. Then, protein values underwent inverse-rank normalization before the following analyses. Multivariable linear regression models were utilized to assess the associations between MVPA and levels of 2910 individual plasma proteins. All models were adjusted for age, sex, ethnicity, educational attainment, TDI, smoking and drinking status, unhealthy sleep duration, diet quality score, history of cancer, protein measurement batch, and the top ten genetic PCs. For each identified MVPA-associated protein, we subsequently estimated its total natural indirect effect (TNIE) and the proportion of the MVPA effect on T2D that was mediated that protein. These mediation models were conducted using the regmedint R package [24]. This framework integrated linear regression models of MVPA with proteins and Cox models of proteins with incident T2D, adjusting for the identical covariate set. Enrichment analyses across KEGG, GO Biological Process (GO: BP) and Molecular Function (GO: MF), WikiPathways, and the Reactome were performed on the potential mediating proteins using g: Profiler [25] (data version updated on April 20, 2026, corresponding to Ensemble release 114; https://biit.cs.ut.ee/gprofiler/page/news), with all 2910 proteins as the background set.

Protein co-expression network construction and mediation analyses

Prior to network construction, the NPX values of all 2910 plasma proteins were normalized using a Yeo-Johnson transformation [11, 26]. A weighted and undirected protein co-expression network was subsequently constructed using pairwise Pearson correlation coefficients to define edge weights between protein nodes [11]. Protein modules were identified using Multi-scale Embedded Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (MEGENA) [15, 27]. MEGENA refines the raw correlation matrix by constructing a planar filtered network, which removes noisy or structurally redundant edges while preserving the fundamental topological hierarchy of the network. After network filtering, MEGENA applies multi-scale clustering to identify hierarchical clusters of highly connected proteins. To ensure structural robustness, empirical permutation tests inherent to the MEGENA pipeline were executed, and only modules achieving statistical significance (P < 0.05) were retained as true protein modules. Candidate key drivers of protein modules were defined as statistically significant module hub proteins identified by MEGENA, using a hub significance threshold of P < 0.05. Details on MEGENA were described in Supplementary Notes.

For each protein module, its eigenprotein was defined as the most representative abundance value for the module, equivalent to the module’s first PC (PC1), which explains the covariance of all proteins within that module [12]. Multivariable linear regressions, adjusting for the identical covariate set utilized in the single-protein analyses, were performed to estimate the effect of WHO guideline-adherent MVPA on each module eigenprotein. For the statistically robust modules, identical mediation frameworks were conducted to estimate the TNIE and mediated proportion in the pathway from sufficient MVPA to incident T2D. Since the eigenprotein summarizes the shared variation across a module’s constituent proteins, module-level indirect effects represent the joint mediating contribution of these co-expression proteins rather than the effect of any single protein. Because dimensionality reduction via principal component analysis (PCA) inherently results in partial information loss within a module, we executed the secondary validation by applying a hypergeometric test to determine if the Bonferroni-significant mediated modules were statistically enriched for the individually mediated proteins identified in our previous analyses. To minimize redundancy within MEGENA’s hierarchical parent-child structure, downstream analyses were restricted to terminal ‘leaf’ modules, which represent the finest-resolution co-expression proteins retained after multiscale clustering.

GWAS of prioritized terminal protein modules

To uncover the underlying genetic architecture of our identified protein modules, we conducted WGS-based GWAS specifically on the functionally prioritized leaf modules. WGS data were merged with phenotypic data, finally including 28,788 participants. The eigenprotein of each prioritized leaf module (i.e., PC1) was first subjected to an inverse-rank normal transformation. Following standardized normalization, genome-wide association testing was executed using REGENIE [28]. All genetic association models were comprehensively adjusted for age, sex, and the top 20 genetic PCs. Details on REGENIE analyses were in Supplementary Notes.

Mendelian randomization

To determine whether genetic evidence supports our observational mediation findings, we implemented MR. Corresponding genetic association data for terminal protein modules were sourced from aforementioned WGS-based GWASs. Summary statistics of T2D unadjusted for BMI and without UK Biobank samples were obtained from a meta-analysis from Mahajan et al. (55,005 cases and 400,308 controls) [29].

Valid instrumental variables (IVs) were selected in accordance with the three key assumptions of MR: strong association with the exposure, unconfounded instrument-outcome association, and the impact of IVs on the outcome exclusively through the target exposure [30]. Candidate IVs were required to be common (MAF > 0.01) and genome-wide significant (P < 5 × 10−8). To ensure instrument independence, variants underwent linkage disequilibrium (LD) clumping (r2 <0.001 within 10 Mb) utilizing the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European reference panel [31]. During dataset harmonization, missing outcome variants were substituted with highly correlated proxy SNPs (LD r2 >0.8), and ambiguous palindromic alleles with an MAF > 0.42 were discarded to prevent strand-alignment errors.

To assess the effects of the terminal protein modules on T2D, a fixed-effects model was applied by default; however, if significant heterogeneity was detected via Cochran’s Q test, a multiplicative random-effects model was subsequently utilized. MR-Egger, weighted median, and MR Robust Adjusted Profile Score (MR-RAPS) methods were used as sensitivity methods.

Sensitivity and exploratory analyses for terminal protein modules

Firstly, to elucidate the biological functions of the terminal modules, we performed enrichment analyses encompassing the KEGG, GO: BP, GO: MF, WikiPathways, and the Reactome databases using the aforementioned g: Profiler data [25].

To evaluate the structural robustness of the selected modules, we performed iterative leave-one-out sensitivity analyses. This ensured that the observed mediation effects were driven by the coordinated network rather than a single dominant protein. For each module, we systematically excluded one protein at a time, recalculated the eigenprotein, and repeated the mediation analysis to estimate the TNIE. A module was considered robust if the direction and statistical significance of the TNIE remained consistent across all iterations.

To determine whether these terminal modules exhibited cell-type-specific enrichment associated with T2D pathology, we utilized a human pancreatic islet scRNA-seq cohort. This analysis assumed that a candidate mediating module in the circulation might leave a transcriptional footprint in the islet, the tissue in which T2D pathology arises. We first performed pseudo-bulk differential gene expression analysis by aggregating raw single-cell read counts by cell type and donor. Differential expression was modeled using DESeq2, adjusting for donor age, sex, and sequencing batch [32]. To maintain robust dispersion estimates and statistical power, cell clusters present in fewer than 3 independent donors were excluded. Finally, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) using the resulting cell-type-specific fold-change vectors to quantify the enrichment of our prioritized protein modules across distinct islet cell populations.

To investigate underlying genetic architecture of prioritized protein modules, we performed Multi-marker Analysis of GenoMic Annotation (MAGMA) [33]. Utilizing the module-specific GWAS summary statistics generated previously, MAGMA was employed to map genetic association signals to the specific genes. Bonferroni correction was used to account for multiple testing. The MAGMA-identified significant gene sets were used to a final tier of biological validation through KEGG, GO: BP, GO: MF, Reactome, and WikiPathways enrichment analyses, providing genetic information of the mechanistic pathways regulating these finest-resolution protein modules [25].

Bonferroni correction was applied to the primary analyses, including the associations of MVPA with individual proteins and module eigenproteins, as well as their corresponding mediation analyses, to minimize false-positive findings. For downstream exploratory analyses, we controlled the false discovery rate (FDR) to maximize statistical power while evaluating broader patterns across correlated tests, including pathway and GSEA enrichment and module-level MR across the terminal modules. All analyses were two-sided and conducted using missRanger (v2.6.1), regmedint (v1.0.2), Seurat (v5.4.0), DESeq2 (v1.50.2), gprofiler2 (v0.2.4), msigdbr (v26.1.0), and TwoSampleMR (v0.6.29) packages in R software (v4.2.2).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among 28,842 initially diabetes-free UK Biobank participants with baseline plasma proteomics, 1225 incident T2D cases were ascertained over a median follow-up of 13.60 years [IQR: 12.82, 14.35], corresponding to an incidence rate of 3.24 [95% CI 3.06, 3.43] per 1000 person-years (Supplementary Table 1). Compared with non-events, individuals who developed T2D were older, more often male, more deprived, and exhibited a more adverse cardiometabolic profile at baseline, including higher body mass index, higher HbA1c, higher systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and more prevalent hypertension and prior heart attack (all P < 0.001; Supplementary Table 1). Adherence to the WHO guideline of ≥ 600 MET-min/week of MVPA was less common in incident T2D cases (76.7%) than in non-events (82.8%; P < 0.001).

Individual plasma proteins mediating the MVPA-T2D association

Multivariable Cox regression showed that meeting the WHO guidelines for MVPA was associated with a lower risk of incident T2D (HR [95% CI] 0.76 [0.66, 0.87]; P = 5.84 × 10−5). Multivariable linear regression of guideline-adherent MVPA on each of 2910 plasma proteins identified 650 MVPA-associated proteins at a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (P < 0.05/2910; Fig. 2a; Supplementary Table 2). Of the proteins associated with MVPA, 102 showed positive associations, whereas 548 showed inverse associations. The strongest inversely MVPA-associated proteins included LEP (β [95% CI] − 0.306 [− 0.330, − 0.281]; P = 6.64 × 10−133), FABP4 (− 0.241 [− 0.268, − 0.215]; P = 2.56 × 10−69), and IL1RN (− 0.227 [− 0.256, − 0.198]; P = 5.12 × 10−53), consistent with prior evidence [34, 35]. Conversely, the strongest positively MVPA-associated proteins were ITGAV (0.283 [0.254, 0.312]; P = 3.58 × 10−83), CA14 (0.252 [0.224, 0.281]; P = 2.94 × 10−68), and ITGAM (0.244 [0.214, 0.273]; P = 4.18 × 10−60).

We next examined whether circulating proteins mediate the relationship between MVPA and T2D. After Bonferroni-correction, mediation analyses showed 451 of the 650 MVPA-associated proteins as putative mediators of incident T2D (P < 7.69 × 10−5; Fig. 2b; Supplementary Table 3). Among these, 6 proteins exhibited positive indirect effects, while 445 proteins showed inverse indirect effects (Fig. 2b). The largest protective indirect effects were observed for several adiposity- and inflammation-related proteins, including LEP (TNIE [95% CI] − 0.260 [− 0.290, − 0.230]), IL1RN (− 0.174 [− 0.200, − 0.148]), and FABP4 (− 0.166 [− 0.190, − 0.142]), with LEP protein accounting for the largest mediated proportion overall (Supplementary Table 3).

Pathway enrichment analyses showed the most enriched pathway was KEGG cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway (FDR-corrected P = 2.00 × 10−6; Fig. 2c; Supplementary Table 4). Additional top enriched pathways for REACTOME, GO: BP, GO: MF, and Wikipathways are interleukin-10 signaling, leukocyte migration, cytokine binding, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, respectively (Fig. 2c; Supplementary Table 4).

Co-expression protein modules as potential coordinated mediators

Because circulating proteins act within co-regulated networks rather than as isolated markers, we next determined whether groups of co-expressed proteins that may jointly mediate the MVPA-T2D association. MEGENA approach was used in a plasma protein co-expression network across 2910 proteins to identify hierarchically organized protein modules [27]. After empirical permutation testing, 171 statistically significant modules were retained for subsequent analyses (P < 0.05; Supplementary Table 5). The proteins comprising each module are listed in Supplementary Table 5.

Multivariable linear regression identified 78 modules whose eigenproteins were associated with guideline-adherent MVPA (Bonferroni P < 0.05/171; Fig. 3a; Supplementary Table 6). The strongest positively MVPA-associated modules were M202 (β [95% CI] 0.294 [0.266, 0.322], P = 8.43 × 10−92) and M42 (0.196 [0.168, 0.224], P = 7.18 × 10−43), whereas the strongest inverse module was M199 (− 0.248 [− 0.276, − 0.220], P = 1.2 × 10−65). Additionally, M120 eigenprotein showed inverse associations with MVPA (− 0.134 [− 0.163, − 0.105], P = 1.09 × 10−19).

Using each module’s eigenprotein (i.e., PC1), 67 of the 78 MVPA-associated modules were Bonferroni-significant mediators of incident T2D (Supplementary Table 7). The two largest inverse indirect effects were observed for modules M199 (TNIE [95% CI] − 0.202 [− 0.230, − 0.174], P = 1.7 × 10−46) and M42 (− 0.165 [− 0.192, − 0.138]; P = 2.03 × 10−33). Additionally, the estimated indirect effect for M120 was − 0.087 [− 0.107, − 0.066] (Supplementary Table 7). Module indirect effects were estimated separately for each module without summing them across modules. As a result, these estimates reflect the coordinated mediating contribution of each module’s constituent proteins, rather than representing additive components of the total effect of MVPA on T2D. As shown in Supplementary Table 7, the M42 eigenprotein was associated with lower T2D risk, whereas the M199 and M120 eigenproteins were associated with higher T2D risk. Using hypergeometric tests, 25 of these 67 modules were statistically significant after Bonferroni correction and were highlighted within the MEGENA hierarchy (Fig. 3b and c; Supplementary Table 8). To focus on the finest-resolution co-expression modules, subsequent downstream analyses were restricted to the 12 terminal modules that were both significant in PC1-based mediation and enriched for individually mediating proteins (Fig. 3b).

Mendelian randomization triangulates module-level effects on T2D

To further examine whether genetically predicted protein modules highlighted in the mediation analysis may associated with T2D, we performed MR using module-specific genetic instruments derived from our WGS-based GWAS of module eigenproteins in 28,788 UK Biobank participants. After harmonization, we retained 213 instruments for the 12 terminal leaf modules included in downstream MR analyses (range 7–26 instruments per module; Supplementary Table 9). After FDR correction, only modules M42 and M120 remained significant, demonstrating both directional and statistical concordance with the observational results (Fig. 4a). Specifically, a 1-SD increase in the eigenprotein of M42 was associated with a lower T2D risk (OR [95% CI] 0.57 [0.43, 0.77]; FDR-corrected P = 0.003), whereas M120 was associated with a higher T2D risk (1.54 [1.13, 2.09]; FDR-corrected P = 0.036). Sensitivity analyses using weighted-median and MR-RAPS methods supported the concordant directions for M42 and M120, although heterogeneity across instruments was observed (Supplementary Table 10). Genetically predicted MVPA was associated with the eigenproteins of both modules (β [95% CI] 0.249 [0.062, 0.437], P-value = 0.009 for M42; − 0.258 [− 0.444, − 0.073], P-value = 0.006 for M120). Two-step MR further indicated that both modules showed protective indirect effects in the MVPA-T2D association (Supplementary Notes). Given this complementary observational and genetic evidence, modules M42 and M120 were selected for downstream functional characterization.

Internal architecture and leave-one-out analyses of modules M42 and M120

The M42 module comprised 17 proteins, with CKB and IGFBP2 acting as its central hubs (Fig. 4b). Similarly, the 13-protein M120 module was driven by two key hubs, RIDA and PCBD1 (Fig. 4c). Therefore, we named M42 the CKB-IGFBP2-centric module, and M120 the RIDA-PCBD1-centric module. In the leave-one-out analysis, individual member proteins were removed one at a time from each module, the direction, magnitude, and statistical significance of the TNIE remained substantially unchanged after the removal of individual proteins (Supplementary Table 11). For example, removal of the M42 hub protein IGFBP2 yielded a TNIE of − 0.166 [− 0.193, − 0.139], with a P-value of 2.9 × 10−34, closely matching the full-module estimate. These analyses support that M42 and M120 capture coordinated module-level protein variation rather than single-protein effects. In a sensitivity analysis restricted to the randomly selected UKB-PPP baseline subset (N = 25,947), the indirect effects of both modules on incident T2D remained consistent (TNIE [95% CI] − 0.140 [− 0.163, − 0.117] for M42; − 0.087 [− 0.104, − 0.070] for M120; Supplementary Table 12).

Pancreatic islet cell-type specific GSEA analyses of M42 and M120

Pathway enrichment analyses showed that proteins within the M120 module were enriched for amino-acid biosynthetic and metabolic processes (GO: BP, both FDR P = 1.7 × 10−2; Supplementary Table 13). To further examine these modules within T2D pathology, we performed pseudo-bulk GSEA analyses on the constituent genes of the CKB-IGFBP2-centric (M42) and RIDA-PCBD1-centric (M120) modules across 11 islet cell types using a human pancreatic islet single-cell RNA-seq dataset (Fig. 4d; Supplementary Table 14). Within pancreatic β-cells, both modules showed significant inverse enrichment in T2D compared to healthy controls, indicating that these member genes were primarily downregulated in T2D β-cells (M120: NES = − 1.601, FDR-corrected P = 0.040; M42: NES = − 1.569, FDR-corrected P = 0.040). CKB-IGFBP2-centric module also showed significant inverse enrichment in pancreatic α-cells (NES = − 1.611; FDR-corrected P = 0.035), ductal cells (NES = − 1.627; FDR-corrected P = 0.031), and δ-cells (NES = − 1.615; FDR-corrected P = 0.040).

Genetic architecture of CKB-IGFBP2-centric and RIDA-PCBD1-centric modules

We next characterized the genetic architecture of the two terminal modules using WGS-based GWAS of module PC1s in 28,788 UK Biobank participants. Both modules showed multiple genome-wide significant loci (Fig. 5a and b). For RIDA-PCBD1-centric module, the strongest signal mapped to PNPLA3, a well-established fatty liver susceptibility locus involved in hepatic lipid handling (Fig. 5a) [36]. For CKB-IGFBP2-centric module, the strongest association signal mapped near CHDH, with additional signals near immune and metabolic loci including HLA-DRA, IL17RB, KLB, CTSB, RET, and APOC1 (Fig. 5b). The Q-Q plot showed that the observed P largely followed the expected null distribution, with tail deviations indicating an enrichment of strong signals. This pattern was more pronounced for M42 than for M120, though genomic inflation (λ = 1.06 for M42 and λ = 1.03 for M120) remained modest for both modules (Fig. 5c).

Using GWAS summary statistics, MAGMA identified 439 and 30 genes associated with the eigenproteins of the CKB-IGFBP2- and RIDA-PCBD1-centric modules, respectively (Supplementary Table 15). Gene-level MAGMA enrichment identified distinct pathways for the two modules (Supplementary Table 16). CKB-IGFBP2-centric module was enriched primarily for antigen-presentation and adaptive-immune pathways, including peptide antigen binding, MHC class II-related processes, allograft rejection, and interferon-γ signaling (Fig. 5d). In contrast, RIDA-PCBD1-centric module was enriched for innate-inflammatory and clearance-related pathways, including apoptotic cell clearance, interleukin-1 production, and complement activation (Fig. 5d). Based on these enrichment patterns, we also named the CKB-IGFBP2-centric module the antigen-presentation-enriched module and the RIDA-PCBD1-centric module the complement/apoptotic-cell-clearance-enriched module.

Discussion

This study provides an integrated evaluation of plasma protein co-expression modules linking guideline-adherent MVPA with lower T2D risk. In 28,842 diabetes-free UK Biobank participants, MVPA was associated with lower incident T2D risk and with 650 circulating proteins, 451 of which were statistically significant mediators. By integrating co-expression network analysis, mediation analysis, and enrichment testing, we identified 25 potential mediating protein modules for further evaluation. Among these, the CKB-IGFBP2-centric module (M42) and RIDA-PCBD1-centric (M120) module showed concordant support from MR. Pancreatic islet single-cell analyses linked both modules to β-cell transcriptional gene changes, with CKB-IGFBP2-centric module showing additional enrichment across α-, δ-, and ductal cells. WGS-based GWAS and MAGMA analyses connected M42 to adaptive immunity and antigen presentation and M120 to complement and apoptotic cells clearance. Collectively, these findings provide convergent evidence that coordinated plasma protein modules are involved in pathways linking MVPA with lower T2D risk, and support module-level proteomics as a complementary approach to individual circulating biomarkers.

Most prior plasma proteomic studies of physical activity and cardiometabolic disease have focused on individual proteins [8, 37, 38]. In the present study, 451 proteins showed evidence of potential mediation, with LEP, IL1RN, and FABP4 showing the largest protective indirect effects. LEP and IL1RN have been implicated in exercise-related metabolic and inflammatory pathways relevant to T2D [8, 39]. Although direct evidence for a mediating role of FABP4 is limited, previous studies have reported that lower FABP4 levels are associated with greater physical activity [40] and lower T2D risk [41]. A minority of MVPA-associated proteins showed indirect effects in the opposite direction of the overall protective association. While observational data cannot determine the underlying causes, several explanations are plausible. They may represent competitive mediation, where certain exercise-associated muscle, or immune proteins increase with MVPA yet correlate with higher T2D risk. Alternatively, they may arise from chance, residual confounding, or reverse causation. Because these effects were modest, did not offset the overall protective effect of MVPA on T2D, and involved modules not carried forward into genetic or functional analyses, they do not alter our primary conclusions. Further replication and mechanistic studies will be needed before interpreting these counter-directional associations as true biological pathways. These potential mediating proteins were mainly enriched in pathways related to inflammation, leukocyte migration, lipid homeostasis and hepatic transport, and insulin-link growth factor (IGF) regulation and tissue stress. These pathways are biologically plausible in the context of T2D prevention: immune cell trafficking and anti-inflammatory signaling may contribute to lower systemic inflammation [42, 43], hepatic transport pathways may reflect improved lipid handling [44], and IGF-related pathways may be linked to insulin sensitivity and tissue remodeling [45].

Our findings extend prior individual-protein analyses by showing that the proteomic link between MVPA and lower T2D risk is also captured by coordinated protein modules. Higher eigenprotein values of the CKB-IGFBP2-centric module (M42) was associated with guideline-adherent MVPA and lower T2D risk. Conversely, lower eigenprotein values of the RIDA-PCBD1-centric module (M120) was associated with guideline-adherent MVPA and lower T2D risk. The protective profile of M42 is consistent with the known biology of its core proteins; IGFBP2 has been linked to insulin sensitivity [46] and lower T2D risk [47], whereas CKB is involved in cellular energy homeostasis [48]. M120, centered on RIDA and PCBD1, may reflect a distinct metabolic and inflammatory pathway, although the specific roles of these proteins in exercise-related T2D prevention require further investigation [49].

The biological profiles of the CKB-IGFBP2-centric and RIDA-PCBD1-centric modules suggest they capture distinct immune-metabolic pathways underlying the association between MVPA and T2D risk. The CKB-IGFBP2-centric module was enriched for antigen processing and presentation, MHC class II activity, and interferon-γ signaling, with its most significant genetic signal mapped near CHDH. These findings are consistent with evidence implicating antigen presentation and interferon-γ signaling in insulin resistance and T2D pathophysiology [50, 51]. In contrast, RIDA-PCBD1-centric module showed a strong GWAS signal near PNPLA3 and was enriched for innate-inflammatory and clearance-related pathways, including apoptotic cell clearance, interleukin-1 production, and complement cascade of LECTIN. Regular physical activity enhances apoptotic cell clearance to prevent the accumulation of pro-inflammatory cellular debris [52], while simultaneously attenuating the overactivation of the innate LECTIN complement cascade [53]. Thus, the RIDA-PCBD1-centric module may capture an innate immune and clearance-related proteomic pattern associated with T2D risk. Notably, antigen-presentation signals for CKB-IGFBP2-centric module and complement-related pathways for RIDA-PCBD1-centric module were not captured in the enrichment analysis of individually mediating proteins. Accordingly, we refer to these two modules as the antigen-presentation-enriched module and the complement/apoptotic-cell-clearance-enriched module, respectively, to provide more biologically interpretable labels. These findings highlight the value of module-level proteomics for identifying coordinated immune-metabolic processes that may be missed when proteins are examined in isolation. Further experimental work will be needed to define the precise mechanistic roles of these modules.

The single-cell analyses provided additional context for interpreting the protein modules in relation to pancreatic islet biology. Both modules were enriched among genes downregulated in T2D β-cells, whereas the antigen-presentation-enriched CKB-IGFBP2-centric module showed broader enrichment that also included α-, δ-, and ductal cells. These findings suggest that MVPA-associated plasma protein modules may be related to transcriptional changes in disease-relevant islet cell types. The broader enrichment of the CKB-IGFBP2-centric module across multiple islet cell types is consistent with the possibility that exercise-related proteomic pathways reflect coordinated changes in the pancreatic microenvironment, including paracrine interactions relevant to β-cell function [54, 55]. The complement/apoptotic-cell-clearance-enriched RIDA-PCBD1-centric module showed a more complex pattern. Higher plasma eigenprotein values were associated with increased T2D risk in both observational and MR analyses, whereas the module was enriched among genes downregulated in T2D β-cells. Given its enrichment for innate inflammatory and clearance-related pathways, this contrast may reflect different biological layers: a circulating signal related to metabolic inflammation and insulin resistance, and reduced β-cell expression of stress-response or clearance-related genes under diabetic conditions [56, 57]. This interpretation is consistent with recent single-cell studies showing substantial cell-type-specific transcriptional alterations, including β-cell defects, in human diabetic islets [58]. Thus, the single-cell results should be viewed as cellular context rather than direct evidence of tissue origin or local causal mechanisms.

Strengths and limitations

These findings highlight the value of examining coordinated protein modules, rather than only isolated biomarkers, to capture systemic proteomic patterns associated with MVPA. The added value of the module-based mediation framework is illustrated in three ways by the two prioritized modules. First, the modules uncovered coordinated pathways that were not identified when proteins were tested individually, suggesting that grouping proteins into modules may strengthen a shared biological signal rather than simply lowering the multiple-testing burden. Second, leave-one-out analyses showed that their mediating effects persisted after removal of any single constituent protein, suggesting that the modules capture distributed signal rather than the effect of one dominant protein. Third, both modules were corroborated by convergent genetic and functional evidence, with module-level MR linking them to T2D risk and their constituent genes enriched among T2D-associated transcriptional changes in pancreatic islet cells. Together, these features suggest that module-level proteomics may provide system-level biological insight beyond what individual circulating proteins reveal. This multi-layered design strengthens the biological plausibility of the prioritized modules and places MVPA-associated plasma protein modules in cell types relevant to T2D pathogenesis. Beyond their explanatory value, such immune-metabolic modules may help prioritize coordinated pathways, rather than single proteins, as candidates for mechanistic study of how exercise protects against T2D.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. First, MVPA was assessed using self-reported questionnaire data, which may be affected by measurement error and exposure misclassification. Observational mediation analyses also cannot fully exclude residual confounding or reverse causation, even with extensive covariate adjustment [59, 60]. The MR analyses provide complementary genetic evidence for the prioritized modules but should not be interpreted as definitive evidence, because they rely on core IV assumptions and may be affected by instrument heterogeneity. Second, module PC1s summarize the dominant shared variation within each module but may not capture all biologically relevant within-module structure. Consequently, module-level MR based on PC1 reflects this shared component and may miss associations with T2D that are carried by other within-module dimensions. The hypergeometric enrichment and leave-one-out analyses partly address this concern, but future work could evaluate alternative module summaries and nonlinear relationships. Third, the study population was derived from UK Biobank participants with available proteomic data, therefore, the generalizability of our findings to all age groups, other ancestry groups, and populations with different cardiometabolic risk profiles remains uncertain. Finally, plasma protein levels may reflect secretion, clearance, tissue injury, or systemic inflammation and do not directly identify the tissue of origin. Although the islet single-cell analyses provide important cellular insight, circulating proteins and single-cell transcriptomic signatures measure different biological layers.

Conclusions

In summary, this study identifies coordinated plasma protein modules that may mediate the association between guideline-adherent MVPA and lower incident T2D risk. By integrating proteomics, network analysis, mediation analysis, genetic evidence, and human pancreatic islet single-cell data, the study highlights coordinated protein modules as biologically informative signatures of the relationship between physical activity and T2D risk. These findings support module-level proteomics as a complementary approach to individual-protein analyses and nominate two protein modules for further validation in independent cohorts and intervention studies.

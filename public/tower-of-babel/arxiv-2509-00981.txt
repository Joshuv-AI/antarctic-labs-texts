Enhanced Mean Field Game for Interactive Decision-Making with Varied Stylish Multi-Vehicles
Liancheng Zheng, Zhen Tian, Yangfan He, Shuo Liu, Huilin Chen, Fujiang Yuan, Yanhong Peng

Abstract
This paper presents a MFG–based decision-making framework for autonomous driving in heterogeneous traffic. To model diverse human behaviors, we propose a quantitative driving style representation that maps abstract traits to parameters such as speed, safety factors, and reaction time. These are embedded into the MFG via a spatial influence field model. To ensure safe operation in dense traffic, we introduce a safety-critical lane-changing algorithm using dynamic safety margins, time-to-collision analysis, and multi-layered constraints. Real-world NGSIM data is used for style calibration and empirical validation. Experimental results show zero collisions across six style combinations, two 15-vehicle scenarios, and NGSIM-based trials, outperforming conventional game-theoretic baselines. Our approach offers scalable, interpretable, and behavior-aware planning for real-world autonomous driving applications.

In recent years, MFG theory has emerged as a promising framework for modeling interactive driving. Unlike traditional game-theoretic approaches, MFG approximates the aggregate behavior of a large population of vehicles using a mean field term, thereby avoiding the need to model every pairwise interaction explicitly and mitigating the scalability issues inherent in conventional game formulations. However, existing MFG-based methods for autonomous driving often suffer from several limitations: they lack simulations across diverse traffic scenarios, fail to compare against other game-theoretic baselines, and remain disconnected from control-level optimization. This integration addresses the conservatism of Stackelberg games and the computational burden of Nash games, providing a more flexible and scalable solution for interactive driving. Nevertheless, most prior MFG models overlook the heterogeneity of driving styles among surrounding vehicles, limiting their realism and applicability in real-world traffic. Furthermore, they fail to ensure safe interactions in densely populated environments with varied behavioral agents—an essential capability for real-world deployment.

To overcome these limitations, we propose a comprehensive MFG-based framework that explicitly incorporates driving style heterogeneity into large-scale vehicular interaction modeling. Departing from conventional pairwise interaction schemes, our method employs mean field approximations to represent the collective influence of surrounding vehicles, each parameterized by empirically grounded behavioral characteristics. To ensure realism, we integrate trajectory data from the Next Generation Simulation (NGSIM) dataset for both calibration and validation of our driving style models. Additionally, we develop a safety-critical lane-changing algorithm tailored for dense multi-vehicle scenarios, combining dynamic safety margins, time-to-collision (TTC) analysis, and multi-layered constraints to ensure robust maneuvering amid varied and unpredictable surrounding behaviors. This enables our framework to achieve reliable, safe interaction in high-density, heterogeneous traffic environments, bridging theoretical modeling with real-world applicability.
The principal contributions of this work encompass:

•

We propose a quantitative driving style model integrated with MFG that maps abstract behaviors to parameters including desired speed, safety factors, interaction weights, lane-change aggression, and reaction time. These are integrated into the MFG framework via an influence field approach, where each style produces distinct spatial patterns defined by longitudinal/lateral radii and intensity coefficients. This enhanced MFG model enables AVs to make style-aware, adaptive decisions in mixed-traffic environments.

•

A safety-critical lane-changing algorithm is developed for dense multi-vehicle scenarios, incorporating dynamic safety distances, TTC analysis, and multi-layered constraints. It enables robust planning even when surrounded by laerge number of vehicles with varied and unpredictable behaviors.

•

We conduct comprehensive validation across three paradigms: (1) six front-rear driving style combinations (e.g., super-aggressive vs. conservative) with 100% collision-free lane changes; (2) dense 15-vehicle surrounding scenarios, where our ego vehicle maintained safe margins and completed all maneuvers; and (3) real-world NGSIM-based tests, showing smooth integration of empirical behavior into the MFG framework. Across multiple trials, our method achieved zero collisions.

II-A

Stability Analysis Under Perturbations

Lemma II.1

(Robustness to Style Parameter Perturbations)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*},\{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N})
be the equilibrium for style parameters
\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, and let
(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})
be the equilibrium for perturbed parameters
\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

W_{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\text{robust}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}\|

(1)

for some constant
C_{\text{robust}}>0
depending on the system parameters.

This robustness result ensures that the MFG equilibrium is stable under small perturbations in driving style parameters, which is crucial for practical implementation.

Heterogeneous Interaction Kernel Design

The mean field interaction is characterized by the style-dependent coupling function:

\boldsymbol{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})=\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})

The heterogeneous interaction kernel
\mathbf{K}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{6}
is decomposed as:

\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\|\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j}\|,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})\cdot\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

The spatial influence kernel incorporates anisotropic interaction ranges:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(r,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})=\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{i}-s_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{(d_{i}-d_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma_{d}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\text{interact},i}\cdot\omega_{\text{interact},j}\cdot\sigma_{s,\text{base}}^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{\text{aggr},i}\cdot\alpha_{\text{aggr},j}}\cdot\sigma_{d,\text{base}}^{2}

The geometric interaction matrix
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 6}
accounts for relative positioning and motion:

\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{G}_{\text{long}}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}\\
\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&\mathbf{G}_{\text{lat}}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\end{bmatrix}

(26)

where the longitudinal and lateral interaction submatrices are:

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\text{long}}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_{ij})&-\sin(\psi_{ij})&0\\
\sin(\psi_{ij})&\cos(\psi_{ij})&0\\
0&0&\text{sign}(s_{j}-s_{i})\end{bmatrix}

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\text{lat}}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\text{sign}(d_{j}-d_{i})\\
0&\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})&-\sin(\Delta\phi_{ij})\\
0&\sin(\Delta\phi_{ij})&\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})\end{bmatrix}

where
\psi_{ij}=\arctan 2(d_{j}-d_{i},s_{j}-s_{i})
and
\Delta\phi_{ij}=\phi_{j}-\phi_{i}
.

The style compatibility matrix
\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 6}
captures behavioral compatibility:

\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})=\exp\left(-\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\theta}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{I}_{6}+\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

(28)

where
\mathbf{W}=\text{diag}(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{7})
is a weighting matrix for style parameters, and
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
captures cross-style interactions:

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{v}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&\lambda_{va}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{a}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&0&0&0\\
\lambda_{av}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&\lambda_{aa}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\lambda_{d}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&\lambda_{da}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})\\
0&0&0&0&\lambda_{vd}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0\\
0&0&0&\lambda_{ad}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&0&\lambda_{add}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})\end{bmatrix}

(29)

II-E

Safety-Critical Constraint Manifolds and Risk Assessment

The safety constraint set for the challenging surrounding scenario is formulated as the intersection of multiple constraint manifolds:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}^{t}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\text{coll},i}^{t}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\text{boundary},i}^{t}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\text{kinematic},i}^{t}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\text{comfort},i}^{t}\right)

(30)

II-E
1
Multi-Scale Collision Avoidance Manifold

The collision avoidance constraint manifold for vehicle
i
incorporates probabilistic safety guarantees:

\mathcal{M}_{\text{coll},i}^{t}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}):\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t}}[\mathcal{E}_{\text{coll},i}^{t}]\leq\epsilon_{\text{coll}}\right\}

(31)

where the collision event is defined by the multi-scale unsafe region:

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\text{coll},i}^{t}=\bigl\{(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}:\;&\exists\,\tau\in[t,\,t+T_{\text{pred}}],\\
d_{\text{min}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\tau},\mathbf{x}^{\tau})\leq d_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\tau},\mathbf{x}^{\tau},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigr\}.\end{split}

(32)

The dynamic safety distance incorporates relative motion, style compatibility, and temporal prediction:

\displaystyle d_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{x}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=d_{\text{base}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Xi}(\Delta v_{ij},\Delta\phi_{ij},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(t,T_{\text{pred}})

(33)

\displaystyle\cdot\boldsymbol{\Omega}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{surrounding}})

where the safety amplification factors are:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Xi}(\Delta v,\Delta\phi,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{5}\left(1+\beta_{k}\frac{f_{k}(\Delta v,\Delta\phi,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})}{g_{k}}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=|\Delta v|,\quad g_{1}=v_{\text{ref}},\quad\beta_{1}=\omega_{\text{interact},i}

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=|\Delta\phi|,\quad g_{2}=\pi,\quad\beta_{2}=\alpha_{\text{aggr},i}

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|,\quad g_{3}=\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\max},\quad\beta_{3}=1

\displaystyle f_{4}

\displaystyle=|a_{s,i}-a_{s,j}|,\quad g_{4}=a_{\max},\quad\beta_{4}=\kappa_{\text{safe},i}

\displaystyle f_{5}

\displaystyle=|v_{d,i}-v_{d,j}|,\quad g_{5}=v_{d,\max},\quad\beta_{5}=\alpha_{\text{aggr},i}

The temporal prediction factor accounts for uncertainty growth:

\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}(t,T_{\text{pred}})=1+\gamma_{\text{pred}}\left(\frac{t}{T_{\text{pred}}}\right)^{2}+\delta_{\text{pred}}\exp\left(-\frac{(T_{\text{pred}}-t)^{2}}{\sigma_{\text{pred}}^{2}}\right)

(35)

The surrounding scenario factor captures the challenging multi-vehicle environment:

\boldsymbol{\Omega}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{surrounding}})=1+\zeta_{\text{density}}\rho_{\text{local}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t})+\zeta_{\text{aggr}}\bar{\alpha}_{\text{local}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t})

(36)

where
\rho_{\text{local}}(\mathbf{x})
is the local density and
\bar{\alpha}_{\text{local}}(\mathbf{x})
is the average local aggressiveness.

II-E
2
Hierarchical Risk Assessment Functions

The instantaneous collision risk incorporates multi-modal uncertainty:

\displaystyle R_{\text{inst}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})

(37)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{W}_{\text{discrete}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{X}_{j}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

The continuous risk kernel captures probabilistic collision potential:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{d(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{x})^{\eta}}{d_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\eta}}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1+\lambda_{v}\frac{|\Delta v|}{\max(v_{i},v_{j})+\epsilon_{v}}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1+\lambda_{\phi}\frac{|\Delta\phi|}{\pi}\right)

where
\eta>2
provides sharp risk gradients near collision boundaries.

The discrete vehicle interaction weight accounts for direct vehicle-to-vehicle risks:

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}_{\text{discrete}}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{X}_{j}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})&=\frac{\exp\!\bigl(-d(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\mathbf{X}_{j}^{t})/\sigma_{\text{discrete}}\bigr)}{1+\tau_{\text{react},i}\,\tau_{\text{react},j}}\\
&\quad\cdot\Gamma(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j}).\end{split}

(39)

where
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})
is the behavioral compatibility factor:

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})=\begin{cases}2.0,&\text{if both aggressive}\\
1.5,&\text{if mixed aggressive/conservative}\\
1.0,&\text{if both conservative}\\
0.8,&\text{if ego vehicle}\end{cases}

(40)

The temporal risk function over prediction horizon
T
incorporates uncertainty propagation:

\displaystyle R_{T}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\frac{1}{1+\tau^{\nu}}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\varrho\left(d(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i}^{t+\tau},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{t+\tau}),d_{\text{safe}}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{t+\tau}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle\cdot\Psi_{\text{uncertainty}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})

where
\nu\geq 1
controls temporal discounting, and the uncertainty propagation factor is:

\begin{split}\Psi_{\text{uncertainty}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})&=\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\tau\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\|_{F}}{\sigma_{\text{base}}}\Bigr)\\
&\quad\cdot\Bigl(1+\kappa_{\text{hetero}}\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|^{2}\Bigr).\end{split}

(42)

The risk function
\varrho(d,d_{\text{safe}})
employs a smooth approximation to the collision indicator:

\varrho(d,d_{\text{safe}})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }d\leq d_{\text{safe}}/2\\[4.0pt]
\left(1-\frac{2d-d_{\text{safe}}}{d_{\text{safe}}}\right)^{\xi},&\text{if }d_{\text{safe}}/2<d\leq d_{\text{safe}}\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{if }d>d_{\text{safe}}\end{cases}

(43)

where
\xi\geq 2
provides smooth risk transitions.

II-F

Optimal Control Problem Formulation and HJB System

Each vehicle
i
with driving style
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}
solves the following mean field optimal control problem over the finite horizon
[0,T]
:

\displaystyle V_{i}(\mathbf{x},t,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathbf{u}_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}L_{i}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{s},\mathbf{u}_{i}^{s},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{s},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\mathrm{d}s

(44)

\displaystyle+\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{T},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{T},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\bigg|\mathbf{X}_{i}^{t}=\mathbf{x}\bigg]

subject to the stochastic dynamics (
7
) and safety constraints (
30
).

II-F
1
Style-Dependent Cost Function Design

The instantaneous cost function incorporates heterogeneous preferences and safety requirements:

\displaystyle L_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\ell_{\text{track}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})+\ell_{\text{safety}}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

(45)

\displaystyle+\ell_{\text{comfort}}(\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})+\ell_{\text{fuel}}(\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle+\ell_{\text{lane}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

The tracking cost reflects style-specific target preferences:

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{track}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\|_{\mathbf{Q}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\|_{\mathbf{R}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})}^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle+\|\dot{\mathbf{u}}-\dot{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\|_{\mathbf{S}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})}^{2}

where the style-dependent weighting matrices are:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(q_{s,i},q_{v_{s},i},q_{a_{s},i},q_{d,i},q_{v_{d},i},q_{a_{d},i})

(47)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(r_{a,i},r_{d,i},r_{\delta,i})

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\text{diag}(s_{a,i},s_{d,i})

The reference trajectories adapt to driving styles:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}s_{\text{ref},i}(t)\\
v_{\text{des},i}\\
0\\
d_{\text{target},i}(t)\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}

(48)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
\delta_{\text{plan},i}(t)\end{bmatrix}

The mean field safety cost integrates both continuous and discrete interaction risks:

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{safety}}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\text{interact},i}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})

(49)

\displaystyle+\kappa_{\text{safe},i}\varphi_{\text{boundary}}(d_{\text{c2b}}(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle+\alpha_{\text{aggr},i}\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{W}_{\text{discrete}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{X}_{j},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j})

where the boundary penalty function is:

\varphi_{\text{boundary}}(d)=\begin{cases}+\infty,&\text{if }d\leq 0\\[4.0pt]
\beta_{\text{boundary}}\left(\frac{d_{\min}}{d}\right)^{2},&\text{if }0<d\leq d_{\text{safe}}\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{if }d>d_{\text{safe}}\end{cases}

(50)

The comfort cost reflects style-specific tolerance levels:

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{comfort}}(\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=w_{\text{jerk},i}\|\dot{\mathbf{u}}\|^{2}+w_{\text{lateral},i}|u_{d,i}|^{2}

(51)

\displaystyle+w_{\text{aggr},i}\mathbb{I}_{\{\|\mathbf{u}\|>u_{\text{comfort},i}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\}}

\displaystyle+w_{\text{centripetal},i}\left|\frac{(v_{s})^{2}}{R_{\text{curvature}}}-a_{\text{cent},\text{des},i}\right|^{2}

The lane-changing cost captures mandatory scenario requirements:

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{lane}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=w_{\text{mandatory},i}\mathbb{I}_{\{|d-d_{\text{target}}|>\epsilon_{\text{lane}}\}}

(52)

\displaystyle+w_{\text{transition},i}|\delta_{i}|^{2}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\text{safety}}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle+w_{\text{smoothness},i}|\dot{d}|^{2}

where the safety margin for lane transitions is:

\mathcal{M}_{\text{safety}}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})=\exp\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})}{d_{\text{safe}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\boldsymbol{\theta})}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)

(53)

II-F
2
Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman System

The value function
V_{i}(\mathbf{x},t,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})
satisfies the coupled HJB system:

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial V_{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}}\bigg\{L_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})+\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}V_{i},\boldsymbol{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\rangle

(54)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}[\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}V_{i}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})]

\displaystyle+\langle\frac{\delta V_{i}}{\delta\boldsymbol{\mu}},\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\{\mathbf{u}_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{N}]\rangle\bigg\}

with terminal condition:

V_{i}(\mathbf{x},T,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{T},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})=\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{T},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})

(55)

The optimal control policy is characterized by the first-order condition:

\begin{split}\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}(\mathbf{x},t,\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})=\arg\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}}\Bigl\{&L_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\\
&{}+\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}V_{i},\,\boldsymbol{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\rangle\Bigr\}.\end{split}

(56)

II-G

Stochastic Path Planning via Enhanced Candidate Generation

For the challenging surrounding scenario requiring mandatory lane changes, we develop a stochastic path planning framework that generates and evaluates candidate trajectories under the MFG equilibrium.

II-G
1
Candidate Path Space Definition

The admissible path space for mandatory lane-changing scenarios is defined as:

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{\text{candidate}}=\{\boldsymbol{\gamma}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{S}\mid\;&\boldsymbol{\gamma}(0)=\mathbf{x}_{0},\\
&|\boldsymbol{\gamma}(T)-\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}}|\leq\epsilon_{\text{target}},\\
&\text{$\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ satisfies lane-change constraints},\\
&\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(t)\|\leq v_{\max},\\
&\|\ddot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(t)\|\leq a_{\max}\,\}.\end{split}

(57)

Each candidate path
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{candidate}}
is parameterized using a hierarchical representation:

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t)=\boldsymbol{\Gamma}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{global}},\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{local}},\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{temporal}},t)

(58)

where the parameter vectors capture different aspects of the trajectory:

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{global}}

\displaystyle=[s_{\text{start}},s_{\text{end}},d_{\text{init}},d_{\text{target}},v_{\text{target}}]^{T}

(59)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{local}}

\displaystyle=[s_{\text{lc,start}},s_{\text{lc,end}},\phi_{\text{transition}},\kappa_{\text{smooth}}]^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\text{temporal}}

\displaystyle=[t_{\text{start}},t_{\text{end}},\tau_{\text{transition}},\sigma_{\text{timing}}]^{T}

II-G
2
Enhanced Lane-Changing Trajectory Generation

For mandatory lane-changing maneuvers, the lateral trajectory follows an enhanced parameterization:

d_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}d_{\text{init}},&\text{(i)}\\[4.0pt]
d_{\text{init}}+(d_{\text{target}}-d_{\text{init}})\,\Upsilon_{k}\!\left(\dfrac{s-s_{\text{start},k}}{s_{\text{end},k}-s_{\text{start},k}}\right),&\text{(ii)}\\[4.0pt]
d_{\text{target}},&\text{(iii)}\end{cases}

(60)

where
(i)
s\leq s_{\text{start},k}
; (ii)
s_{\text{start},k}<s<s_{\text{end},k}
; (iii)
s\geq s_{\text{end},k}
.

The transition function
\Upsilon_{k}(\xi)
incorporates style-dependent smoothness:

\Upsilon_{k}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\cos(\pi\xi^{\beta}_{k})\right)+\gamma_{k}\xi^{2}(1-\xi)^{2}\sin(2\pi n_{k}\xi)

(61)

where
\beta_{k}=f(\alpha_{\text{aggr}})
controls transition sharpness and
\gamma_{k},n_{k}
add style-specific variations.

The longitudinal velocity profile adapts to the lane-changing maneuver:

v_{s,k}(s)=v_{\text{des}}\cdot\left(1-\epsilon_{\text{slowdown}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\text{mid},k})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\text{slowdown}}^{2}}\right)\right)\cdot\Psi_{\text{style}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

(62)

where
\Psi_{\text{style}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
reflects driving style preferences:

\Psi_{\text{style}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\begin{cases}1.2,&\text{if super-aggressive}\\
1.1,&\text{if aggressive/competitive}\\
0.8,&\text{if conservative}\\
1.0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(63)

II-G
3
Multi-Objective Path Evaluation Under MFG Equilibrium

The path evaluation function integrates MFG-based field costs, safety assessments, and style compatibility:

\displaystyle J_{k}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{ego}})

\displaystyle=w_{1}J_{\text{field}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t})+w_{2}J_{\text{safety}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t})

(64)

\displaystyle+w_{3}J_{\text{dynamics}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k})+w_{4}J_{\text{style}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{ego}})

\displaystyle+w_{5}J_{\text{mandatory}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k})

The MFG field interaction cost captures continuous field effects:

\displaystyle J_{\text{field}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t),\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{ego}},\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta})\mathrm{d}t

(65)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\mathbf{V}^{t}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t))\mathrm{d}t

The enhanced collision risk cost employs multiple risk scales:

\displaystyle J_{\text{safety}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{d(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t),\mathbf{X}_{j}^{t})^{4}}{d_{\text{safe}}^{4}}\right)\right.

(66)

\displaystyle\left.+\zeta_{\text{ttc}}\mathbb{I}_{\{\text{TTC}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t),\mathbf{X}_{j}^{t})<T_{\text{critical}}\}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{R}_{\text{boundary}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t))\mathrm{d}t

where TTC (Time-to-Collision) is computed as:

\text{TTC}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})=\begin{cases}\frac{d(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})}{|\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}|},&\text{if }\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}<0\\[4.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(67)

where
\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}}=\mathbf{v}_{i}-\mathbf{v}_{j}
and
\hat{\mathbf{n}}
is the unit vector from
\mathbf{x}_{i}
to
\mathbf{x}_{j}
.

The dynamics cost ensures kinematic feasibility:

\displaystyle J_{\text{dynamics}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl[\rho_{v}\bigl(\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}(t)\|-v_{\max}\bigr)_{+}^{2}

(68)

\displaystyle+\rho_{a}\bigl(\|\ddot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}(t)\|-a_{\max}\bigr)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle+\rho_{j}\bigl(\|\dddot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}(t)\|-j_{\max}\bigr)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle+\rho_{\kappa}\bigl(\kappa(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{k}(t))-\kappa_{\max}\bigr)_{+}^{2}\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t

where
(\cdot)_{+}=\max(0,\cdot)
and
\kappa(\boldsymbol{\gamma})
is the path curvature.

II-H

Mean Field Equilibrium Analysis and Convergence Guarantees

II-H
1
Fixed Point Characterization

A mean field equilibrium
(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*},\{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N})
for the heterogeneous system satisfies the coupled fixed point conditions:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}[\{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}](\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*})

(69)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{i}[\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}](\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}),\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}

where
\mathcal{T}
is the measure propagation operator defined by the Fokker-Planck evolution (
17
), and
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is the optimal control operator derived from the HJB system (
54
).

II-H
2
Existence and Uniqueness Theorems

Theorem II.4

(Existence of Heterogeneous MFG Equilibrium)
.

Consider the heterogeneous MFG system defined by (
7
)-(
54
). Under the following conditions:

1.

Lipschitz Continuity
: The drift
\boldsymbol{f}_{i}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})
and cost
L_{i}(\cdot,\cdot,\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})
are uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})
with respect to the Wasserstein metric on
\boldsymbol{\mu}
.

2.

Interaction Monotonicity
: The interaction coupling satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})-\boldsymbol{h}_{i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}\rangle_{W_{2}}

(70)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{\text{mono}}\,W_{2}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2})

for some
\alpha_{\text{mono}}>0
, where
W_{2}
denotes the 2-Wasserstein distance.

3.

Coercivity
: The cost function satisfies

L_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})\geq c_{1}\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}-c_{2}(1+\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2})

(71)

for constants
c_{1}>0,c_{2}\geq 0
.

4.

Style Boundedness
: The driving style parameter space
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is compact and
\inf_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\omega_{\text{interact}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})>0
.

Then there exists at least one mean field equilibrium
(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*},\{\mathbf{u}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N})
in the space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta})\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{U}_{i})
.

Proof Sketch.

The proof employs a combination of Schauder’s fixed point theorem and variational analysis. We construct a compact, convex subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{S}\times\boldsymbol{\Theta})\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{U}_{i})
and show that the combined operator
\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\mathbf{u}_{i}\})=(\mathcal{T}[\{\mathbf{u}_{i}\}](\boldsymbol{\mu}),\{\mathcal{G}_{i}[\boldsymbol{\mu}](\mathbf{u}_{i})\})
maps
\mathcal{K}
into itself. The Lipschitz and coercivity conditions ensure continuity and compactness, while the interaction monotonicity provides the necessary contraction properties in the Wasserstein metric.
∎

.

Under the conditions of Theorem

with the additional strong monotonicity condition:

where

IV
Conclusion

This work proposed a heterogeneous MFG based planning framework for
interactive autonomous driving. By formulating multi-agent dynamics with style-aware
parametrization, heterogeneous interactions were compactly represented through a mean
field approximation, where the aggregated intensity field provides a unified potential
for behavior prediction and risk assessment. The forward–backward MFG solution yields
an
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibrium policy, which is subsequently integrated into a
safety-critical path planner that balances field consistency, safety margins, dynamic
feasibility, and style adherence. Numerical evaluations and visualizations of the mean
field intensity confirm the interpretability of the proposed modelling, while the
integration with path planning demonstrates its capability to achieve adaptive, robust,
and safe decision-making under complex traffic interactions.

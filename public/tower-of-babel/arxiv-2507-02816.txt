Helicons in tilted-Weyl semimetals Preprint: APS/123-QED
Shiv Kumar Ram, Amit Gupta

Abstract
Helicons are transverse electromagnetic modes in three-dimensional (3D) electron systems in the presence of a static magnetic field. These modes have been proposed in isotropic or single Weyl semimetals(WSMs) (Francesco M.D. Pellegrino et al, Phys. Rev. B 92 , 201407(R) (2015)). In this work, we introduce a tilt term to investigate helicon modes in gapless WSMs within a semiclassical Boltzmann approach and show that the helicon modes exist in tilted WSMs.



I
Introduction

WSMs are topologically nontrivial conductors in which the valence and conduction bands touch at isolated points (the so-called Weyl nodes) in the Brillouin zone, locally forming Dirac cones
Wan et al. 2011
;
Burkov and Balents 2011
;
Xu et al. 2011
;
Huang et al. 2015
;
Lv et al. 2015
;
Xu et al. 2015
. In general, Weyl nodes in solids do not behave exactly in the same manner as their high-energy Lorentz invariant analogs because they are tilted and anisotropic. Tilting leads to the Weyl cones being ”over-tilted,” causing them to intersect with both conduction and valence bands, creating electron and hole pockets at the Fermi level. This impacts the nature of the Weyl fermions and their contribution to transport properties
Carbotte 2016
. The tilt term influences the chiral anomaly, a phenomenon where chiral charge (related to the handedness of Weyl fermions) is not conserved in the presence of parallel electric and magnetic fields. This leads to observable effects like negative longitudinal magnetoresistance
Johansson et al. 2019
. The tilt modifies the optical conductivity, affecting the interband and intraband transitions. For example, the linear dependence of conductivity on photon energy, characteristic of Dirac and Weyl semimetals, is altered in the presence of tilt
Carbotte 2016
. Tilting can also affect the anomalous Hall effect, which arises from the Berry curvature of the electronic bands
Ishizuka and Nagaosa 2019
. The tilt can be tuned by various means, such as structural distortions, spin textures, or external fields, allowing for the manipulation of material properties
Könye et al. 2022
.

Weyl semimetals introduce an axion term
\theta(\bf E\cdot\bf B
) in Maxwell’s equations due to their topological magnetoelectric effect, altering helicon dispersion
Pellegrino et al. 2015

\omega\sim\frac{k^{2}B}{\mu_{0}n_{e}e}+k

(1)

The second term originates due to the topological
\theta
term. Helicons are low-frequency, circularly polarized transverse electromagnetic waves propagating in a three dimesional(3D) conducting medium under a static magnetic field
(
\bf B
)
Kittel and McEuen 2018
;
Jackson 2021
;
Konstantinov and Perel 1960
. In conventional metals/plasmas, they follow a quadratic dispersion relation
Platzman and Wolff 1973

\omega=\frac{k^{2}B}{\mu_{0}n_{e}e}

(2)

where
n_{e}
is electron density and k is wave vector. This work has been studied for isotropic or single WSMs with the inclusion of orbital magnetic moment(OMM)
Pellegrino et al. 2015
. The OMM can be thought as self-rotation of the Bloch wave packet, and modify the energy of the Bloch electron under the external magnetic field
Sundaram and Niu 1999
. Helicons in Weyl semimetals are a rich testbed for topological electrodynamics, blending condensed matter physics with electromagnetic wave theory. Their unique properties—governed by chiral anomaly, Berry curvature, and axion fields—enable novel device concepts while posing intriguing theoretical challenges. Helicon modes in Weyl semimetals are modified due to pseudofields (e.g., strain-induced gauge fields), which refer to pseudohelicons
Gorbar et al. 2017
.

In this paper, we investigate the effect of a finite tilt in WSMs to study helicon modes for the type-I case. Our main findings are optical conductivity tensor are non-trivially modified by a finte tilt and henceforth the dispersion of helicon modes is altered. Our results are valid for type-I WSMs.

The organization of the paper is as follows: we start with semiclassical Boltzmann theory with the inclusion of magnetic orbital moment to evaluate the optical conductivity tensor in Sec. II. We evaluate the dispersion relation in Sec. III and finish with a discussion and conclusion in Sec. IV.

IV
Conclusion

In summary, we have studied helicon modes in type-I WSMs from semiclassical Boltzmann transport theory, with the inclusion of the orbital angular momentum. We have calculated the analytical expressions for the longitudinal part of the conductivity tensor as a function of the tilt parameter. The transverse parts of conductivity as a function of the tilt term have been calculated numerically. The dispersion modes exist in tilted WSMs. The degeneracy of the three gapped collective modes at k=0 is lifted by the presence of the axion term in the electromagnetic response of WSMs, but is unaffected by a tilt parameter for
t<1
. It is very interesting to study the helicon modes for type-II WSMs(
t>1
) case.

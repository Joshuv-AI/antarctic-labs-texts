Polarized Neutral and Charged Current Semi-Inclusive Deep-Inelastic Scattering at NNLO in QCD
Leonardo Bonino, Thomas Gehrmann, Markus Löchner, Kay Schönwald, and Giovanni Stagnitto

Abstract
The semi-inclusive production of identified hadrons in deeply inelastic lepton scattering on polarized nucleons allows to probe the spin structure of the nucleon target in a more detailed level than through fully inclusive processes. We compute the NNLO QCD corrections to longitudinally polarized semi-inclusive deep inelastic processes mediated by electroweak neutral and charged currents. We present the first calculation of polarized electroweak structure functions in the Larin scheme up to NNLO. Additionally, we reformulate the finite scheme transformation to the MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} scheme in a quark flavour basis and identify issues in its assignment to partonic channels in previous calculations. Using our results we perform a detailed phenomenological study of polarized cross sections and of single and double spin asymmetries. We observe large electroweak effects, which need to be included in precision studies of polarized observables.

2.1

At leading order in the electroweak theory a single gauge boson is exchanged.
The leptonic and hadronic parts

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\sigma^{h}}{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z}=\frac{2\pi y\alpha^{2}}{Q^{4}}\sum_{k}\eta_{k}L_{k}^{\mu\nu}W_{\mu\nu}^{h,k},

2.4
Parton-model expressions

Factorization in SIDIS separates the cross section into three pieces:

(i)

Long-range initial-state interactions, with a parton

(ii)

Long-range final-state interactions, with a parton

\mu_{A}

(iii)

The short-distance cross section
\hat{\sigma}

\hat{\sigma}
introduces a third scale, the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}

This factorization is expressed at the level of the structure functions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}^{h,k}(x,z,Q^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{x}}{\hat{x}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{z}}{\hat{z}}\,f_{p}^{N}\bigg(\frac{x}{\hat{x}},\mu_{F}^{2}\bigg)\,D^{h}_{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{z}{\hat{z}},\mu_{A}^{2}\bigg)\,\mathcal{C}^{i,k}_{p^{\prime}p}(\hat{x},\hat{z},Q^{2},\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{A}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j}^{h,k}(x,z,Q^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{x}}{\hat{x}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{z}}{\hat{z}}\,\Delta f_{p}^{N}\bigg(\frac{x}{\hat{x}},\mu_{F}^{2}\bigg)\,D^{h}_{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{z}{\hat{z}},\mu_{A}^{2}\bigg)\,\Delta\mathcal{C}^{j,k}_{p^{\prime}p}(\hat{x},\hat{z},Q^{2},\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{A}^{2})\,

q,{q^{\prime}}
.
Here,
\hat{v}
denotes combinations of vector couplings,
\hat{a}
denotes combinations of axial vector couplings, and
\hat{i}
denotes interferences between vector and axial vector couplings.
Note that
\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{\gamma Z}
is not symmetric in the quark indices:
\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{\gamma Z}\neq\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{\gamma Z}
.

3.2.1
Antisymmetric Polarized Coefficient Functions

In this section we describe the neutral current (NC) polarized coefficient functions for the
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
structure function up to NNLO.
We follow the notation of the unpolarized symmetric NC coefficient functions in
Bonino:2025qta
.

At LO only the
q\to q
channel contributes,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(0)}_{qq}=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(0)}_{qq}\,,

(24)

with
\Delta C^{1,(0)}_{qq}=\delta(1-\hat{z})\delta(1-\hat{x})
.
The
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
structure function at LO is therefore given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{1}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{1}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\Big[e_{q}^{2},\penalty\ 2\,e_{q}\,g_{V}^{q},\penalty\ (g_{V}^{q})^{2}+(g_{A}^{q})^{2}\Big]\big(\Delta f_{q}^{N}\,D_{q}^{h}+\Delta f_{\bar{q}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{q}}^{h}\big)\,.

(25)

The polarized SIDIS coefficient functions at NLO were first computed in
deFlorian:2012wk
.
At NLO the
q\to g
and
g\to q
channels open,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(1)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(1)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(1)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{qg}\,.

(26)

The contributions entering the right hand side of (
19
) are given in terms of the electroweak couplings from (
23
),
multiplying building blocks of different diagrammatic origin:

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}+\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,{\mathrm{Fcon}},2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}\left(a^{k}_{{q^{\prime}}}\right)\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}+\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{v}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}+\hat{a}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}+\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=-\hat{v}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}+\hat{a}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{gg}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,k,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}a_{{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{NoW}}\,.

(27)

Note that
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{NoW}}
in (
27
) is inherently symmetric and vanishes when contracted with the antisymmetric projector onto
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
.
As no contribution arises from the vector-axial vector interferences, the entire channel
\Delta C^{i,k,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{NoW}}
in (
19
) is zero.

The missing coefficient functions with anti-quarks in the initial state are obtained by charge conjugation,

\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}=\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,k,(j)}_{\bar{p}^{\prime}\bar{p}}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j\in\{0,1,2\}\,.

(28)

The contributions with both electroweak couplings connecting to the same quark line coincide for vector-vector and axial-vector-axial-vector coupling as is expected from naïvely anticommuting
\gamma_{5}
.

If the axial vector vertices appear on different fermion lines, such an equality is no longer expected and also not realised, cf.
\Delta C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}^{1,(2)}\neq\Delta C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}^{1,(2)}
.
The building blocks
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}
,
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}
,
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}
, and
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}
from the
q\to{q^{\prime}}
channel reemerge in the
q\to{\bar{q}^{\prime}}
channel, which is related by charge conjugation applied to the secondary quark line.

We note that unlike in inclusive DIS, the pure-singlet contributions are not restricted to a single channel.
The appearance of pure-singlet splitting functions in the mass factorization of
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}
associates the corresponding coefficient functions to the pure-singlet of SIDIS.
The contribution
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}
, which also constitutes a part of the pure-singlet, is finite and does not require mass factorization.

3.2.2
Symmetric Polarized Coefficient Functions

In this section we present the first calculation of the NNLO NC coefficient functions for the structure functions
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{h}
and
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{h}
.
These structure functions describe the interference contributions between vector and axial vector coupling.
In
\mathrm{NC}
SIDIS they contribute for
k\in\{\gamma Z,ZZ\}
.

At LO only the
q\to q
channel contributes,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(0)}_{qq}=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(0)}_{qq}\,,

(29)

with
\Delta C^{T,(0)}_{qq}=-\frac{1}{2}\delta(1-\hat{z})\delta(1-\hat{x})
and
\Delta C^{L,(0)}_{qq}=0
.
The polarized symmetric LO structure functions are given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{G}_{T}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{T}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{T}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{q}\Big[0,\penalty\ 2\,e_{q}\,g_{A}^{q},\penalty\ 2g_{V}^{q}g_{A}^{q}\Big]\big(\Delta f_{\bar{q}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{q}}^{h}-\Delta f_{q}^{N}\,D_{q}^{h}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{G}_{L}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{L}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{L}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=0\,.

(30)

At NLO the
qg
and
gq
channels open and the longitudinal components do not vanish any more,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qg}\,.

(31)

The
\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{p^{\prime}p}
coefficient functions above were computed up to NLO in
deFlorian:2012wk
.
Note that
\Delta\mathcal{C}^{L,k,(1)}_{qg}=0
.

The missing coefficient functions with anti-quarks in the initial state are obtained by charge conjugation through

\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(j)}_{\bar{p}^{\prime}\bar{p}}=-\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j\in\{0,1,2\}\,.

(32)

At NNLO, the contributions appearing in (
19
) in terms of elementary building blocks read as follow:

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a_{q^{\prime}}^{k}\right)\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}+\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}+\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}-\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}-\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{A}\mathrm{NoW}}\,.

(33)

The purely gluonic channels
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k}_{gg}
vanish to all orders with a line reversal argument like in
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
.
The contribution
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}
vanishes likewise.

The contributions
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}
,
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}
,
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}
, and
\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}
appear in two channels due to a charge conjugation symmetry on the secondary quark line (
{q^{\prime}}\to{\bar{q}^{\prime}}
).

3.3
Charged Current SIDIS

We proceed with describing the production of one identified hadron in polarized charged current SIDIS, i.e. the processes

\displaystyle\ell^{-}N\to\nu_{\ell}hX\,,

\displaystyle\ell^{+}N\to\bar{\nu}_{\ell}hX\,,

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}N\to\ell^{-}hX\,

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}_{\ell}N\to\ell^{+}hX\,.

(34)

In the following we present the coefficient functions in polarized charged current SIDIS at NNLO in QCD corresponding to the symmetric and antisymmetric structure functions.

3.3.1
Antisymmetric Polarized Coefficient Functions

In this section we describe the polarized CC coefficient functions contributing to the
\mathcal{G}_{1}
structure function, up to NNLO.
In
\mathrm{CC}
SIDIS the allowed quark transitions are restricted by the charge of the current entering the hadronic line.
In the following we explicitly give the contributions for
W^{-}
exchange.
The contributions for
W^{+}
exchange can be inferred.

Following the notation of
Bonino:2025qta
,
we denote the set of up-type quarks with
\mathfrak{u}=\{u,c,(t)\}
and the set of down-type quarks with
\mathfrak{d}=\{d,s,(b)\}
.
At LO we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(0)}_{\beta\alpha}=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(0)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(0)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(0)}_{qq}\,,

(35)

where
\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{d}
.
The factors of
2
arise from summing the vector and axial vector contributions.
The coefficients
V_{\alpha\beta}
denote the entries of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{ud}&V_{us}&V_{ub}\\
V_{cd}&V_{cs}&V_{cb}\\
V_{td}&V_{ts}&V_{tb}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(36)

and describe the size of the flavour mixing.

At LO the antisymmetric polarized SIDIS
\mathrm{CC}
structure functions are therefore given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h,W^{-}},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{1}^{h,W^{+}}\Big]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\phantom{|}\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}\phantom{|}}\sum_{\phantom{|}\beta\in\mathfrak{d}\phantom{|}}|V_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\Big[\Delta f_{\alpha}^{N}\,D_{\beta}^{h}+\Delta f_{\bar{\beta}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{\alpha}}^{h},\penalty\ \Delta f_{\beta}^{N}\,D_{\alpha}^{h}+\Delta f_{\bar{\alpha}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{\beta}}^{h}\Big]\,.

(37)

At NLO there exist the quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{g\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{gq}\,,

(38)

the anti-quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{g\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{gq}\,,

(39)

and the gluon-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{\beta g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(1)}_{qg}\,.

(40)

At NNLO the quark-initiated contributions are

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\alpha\alpha,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}\alpha,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{g\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gq}\,,

(41)

where
q\in\mathfrak{u}\cup\mathfrak{d}
denotes a quark of any active flavour; the anti-quark-initiated contributions are given by

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{q}\bar{q},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\bar{\beta},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\bar{\beta},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{g\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gq}\,,

(42)

and the gluon-initiated contributions read

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\beta g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,W^{-},(2)}_{gg}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{gg}\,.

(43)

In order to obtain a coefficient function from the channel decomposition, all the above contributions which admit the initial and final state particle need to be summed.

The coefficient functions
\Delta\mathcal{C}^{1,W^{+},(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}
describing the exchange of a
W^{+}
boson are obtained for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{d}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{u}
.

3.3.2
Symmetric Polarized Coefficient Functions

The symmetric polarized CC structure functions at LO are

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{T,W^{-},(0)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{T,(0)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{T,W^{-},(0)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{T,(0)}_{qq}\,,

(44)

where
\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{d}
.
The factors of
2
arise from our choice of normalization of
\Delta C^{T,(0)}_{qq}=-\frac{1}{2}\delta(1-\hat{x})\delta(1-\hat{z})
, like for NC.
The polarized longitudinal coefficient functions at LO vanish.

At NLO the quark-initiated coefficient functions are given by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{g\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

(45)

the anti-quark-initiated coefficient functions by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-\Delta C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{g\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

(46)

and the gluon-initiated coefficient functions by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{T,W^{-},(1)}_{\beta g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{T,(1)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{C}^{T,W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}g}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}\Delta C^{T,(1)}_{qg}\,.

(47)

As in the NC case the gluon-initiated longitudinal coefficient functions
\Delta C^{L,W^{\pm},(1)}_{qg}
and
\Delta C^{L,W^{\pm},(1)}_{\bar{q}g}
are zero.

Therefore the leading order
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{h}
,
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{h}
structure functions are given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{G}_{T}^{h,W^{-}},\penalty\ \mathcal{G}_{T}^{h,W^{+}}\Big]

Like in the unpolarized case

to calculate the NNLO corrections to neutral- and charged-current SIDIS.

To obtain the structure functions
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
,
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{h}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{h}
from (

) we apply the same projectors as for
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
,
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h}

With respect to the unpolarized case the projection onto the polarization difference for a quark or gluon introduces an additional antisymmetric structure.

4.1
Treatment of
\gamma_{5}

Since the treatment of
\gamma_{5}
in
d\neq 4
dimensions is not unique, we have to specify the scheme we are using for our calculation.
We choose to work in the Larin scheme
Larin:1993tq
, which, in our case, is defined by replacing

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\to\frac{i}{3!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma},

(52)

and subsequently contracting pairs of Levi-Civita tensors into
d
-dimensional scalar products via the identity

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\operatorname{det}\begin{pmatrix}g_{\mu\alpha}&g_{\mu\beta}&g_{\mu\gamma}&g_{\mu\delta}\\
g_{\nu\alpha}&g_{\nu\beta}&g_{\nu\gamma}&g_{\nu\delta}\\
g_{\rho\alpha}&g_{\rho\beta}&g_{\rho\gamma}&g_{\rho\delta}\\
g_{\sigma\alpha}&g_{\sigma\beta}&g_{\sigma\gamma}&g_{\sigma\delta}\end{pmatrix}.

(53)

However, with the standard Larin scheme replacement the known NLO results for the
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
,
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{T}
and
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{L}
structure functions
deFlorian:1994wp
;
Forte:2001ph

obtained in the ’t Hooft-Veltman-Breitenlohner-Maison (HVBM) scheme
tHooft:1972tcz
;
Breitenlohner:1977hr
are not necessarily reproduced.
The discrepancies can be traced back to two problems which have to be addressed:

1.

How to project correctly onto the polarization of an external quark.

2.

How to deal with contractions of four Levi-Civita tensors.

Projector of polarized external quarks

A quark-induced Wilson coefficient in polarized DIS can be expressed as

\Delta C_{q}(x,Q^{2})=\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi(p,n)\,\Gamma(p,n)]\,,

(54)

where
\Pi
denotes the polarized quark projector,
\Gamma
is the sum of amputated Feynman diagrams contributing to the Wilson coefficient
\Delta C_{q}
, and
n
is the light-like reference vector
n=q+xp
, which satisfies
n^{2}=0
,
n\cdot q=Q^{2}/2
and
n\cdot p=Q^{2}/2x
.
In
d=4
dimensions the general tensor structure of
\Gamma
can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma(p,n)\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}A\,\not{p}\gamma_{5}+B\,\not{n}\gamma_{5}\,.

(55)

The projectors onto these two structures are given in four dimensions by

\displaystyle\Pi_{A}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle-\frac{x}{2Q^{2}}\not{n}\gamma_{5}\,,

\displaystyle\Pi_{B}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle-\frac{x}{2Q^{2}}\not{p}\gamma_{5}\,,

(56)

where
\Pi_{B}^{(4)}
is the projector onto the relevant coefficient
B
.

We observe that choosing to apply the usual Larin prescription for the polarized quark projector
Zijlstra:1993sh
,
which amounts to the replacement

\displaystyle\Pi_{B}^{(4)}\to

\displaystyle-\frac{ix}{12Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\gamma_{\mu_{3}}

(57)

does not give rise to the appropriate non-singlet splitting functions
Vogelsang:1996im
;
Mertig:1995ny
appearing in the coefficient functions belonging to
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{T}
and
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{L}
starting from NNLO.
Likewise, the finite parts of the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
NLO coefficient functions
deFlorian:1994wp
;
Forte:2001ph
, which were computed in the HVBM
\gamma_{5}
scheme
are not correctly reproduced with the usual (process independent) scheme transformation
Matiounine:1998re
.
The non-singlet coefficient functions, however, have to coincide with their unpolarized counter parts belonging to
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{h}
by naïve anticommutation of
\gamma_{5}
to the vertex.
In the following we show why the naïve Larin prescription for
\not{p}\gamma_{5}
fails
and extend the prescription to correctly deal with the projection of the external polarization in this scheme.
The same problem was encountered before in the calculation of polarized operator matrix elements
Klein:2009ig

and solved in that context
Schonwald:2019gmn
;
Behring:2019tus
.

We notice that in the Larin scheme the analytic continuation of the tensor structures
\not{p}\gamma_{5}
and
\not{n}\gamma_{5}
to
d
dimensions in the presence of an additional external vector is not unique.
For off-shell quarks we can define four independent tensors which reduce to the two 4-dimensional structures
\not{p}\gamma_{5}
and
\not{n}\gamma_{5}
,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{6}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\gamma_{\mu_{3}}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}\not{p}\gamma_{5}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{6}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}n_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\gamma_{\mu_{3}}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}\not{n}\gamma_{5}\,,

(59)

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{x}{Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{3}}n_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\not{p}\,

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}\not{p}\gamma_{5}\,,

(60)

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle=i\frac{x}{Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{3}}n_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\not{n}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}\not{n}\gamma_{5}\,.

(61)

In a
d
-dimensional Larin scheme the Dirac structure therefore decomposes into

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\penalty\

\displaystyle\penalty\ A_{1}T_{1}+A_{2}T_{2}+A_{3}T_{3}+A_{4}T_{4}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}

\displaystyle\left(A_{1}+A_{3}\right)\not{p}\gamma_{5}+\left(A_{2}+A_{4}\right)\not{n}\gamma_{5}\,.

(62)

For on-shell quarks in
4
dimensions the projector
\not{p}\gamma_{5}
yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi_{B}^{(4)}\,\Gamma]\xlongrightarrow{d\to 4}A_{2}+A_{4}\,,

(63)

which is proportional to the coefficient of the tensor structure
\not{n}\gamma_{5}
.
However, in the
d
dimensional Larin scheme all tensor structures which reduce to
\not{n}\gamma_{5}
in the
4
-dimensional limit have to be taken into account simultaneously.
This implies that we need to construct a projector
\Pi_{2}
in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions fulfilling

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi_{B}\,\Gamma]=f(\epsilon)\,(A_{2}+A_{4})\,,

(64)

with
f(0)=1
.
Without loss of generality we set
f(\epsilon)=1
.
The corresponding projector then reads

\displaystyle\Pi_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2(1-\epsilon)(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{x^{2}}{Q^{4}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{3}}n_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\not{p}\,.

(65)

Conversely, the naïve prescription of (
57
) introduces a non-trivial
\epsilon
dependence between
A_{2}
and
A_{4}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi_{B}^{(4)}\,\Gamma]\to

\displaystyle-\frac{ix}{12Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{4}}\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\gamma_{\mu_{3}}\Gamma]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)(1-2\epsilon)\left[\frac{3-2\epsilon}{3}A_{2}+A_{4}\right]\,,

(66)

explaining why the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
results for the coefficient functions of
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{T}
and
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{L}
are not reproduced with the standard renormalization constants.

In four dimensions the projector onto the spin difference of the external quark state is given by
\not{p}\gamma_{5}
,
which in the Larin scheme translates into

\displaystyle\not{p}\gamma_{5}\to\frac{-i}{(1-\epsilon)(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{x}{Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{3}}q_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\not{p}\,.

(67)

Note that we replaced
n_{\mu_{4}}
with
q_{\mu_{4}}
, which is equivalent due to the antisymmetry of the Levi-Civita tensor.

Interestingly, the projection onto the structure function
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
is unaffected by the change
of projector, since the naïve prescription given by
\Pi_{B}^{(4)}
and
\Pi_{B}
yields the same result:

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\rho}q_{\sigma}\right)\left(\frac{i}{6}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\gamma_{\mu_{3}}\right)=\left(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\rho}q_{\sigma}\right)\left(-i\frac{x}{Q^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}p_{\mu_{3}}q_{\mu_{4}}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\gamma_{\mu_{2}}\not{p}\right)\,,

(68)

which can be seen by explicitly contracting the two Levi-Civita tensors on both sides according to (
53
).
This shows that any observable related to the
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
structure function is unaffected by this modification of the quark projector.

Contraction of four Levi-Civita tensors

*

When calculating the coefficient functions for neutral- and charged-current interactions of the structure function

\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
, we encounter the contraction of up to four Levi-Civita tensors.
Starting from the contraction of three Levi-Civita tensors, i.e.

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

the contraction in the Larin scheme is ambiguous,
since there are two ways of associating the Levi-Civita tensors for pairwise contraction
Belusca-Maito:2023wah
,

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma})\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=(24-100\varepsilon+140\varepsilon^{2}-80\varepsilon^{3}+16\varepsilon^{4})\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta})

\displaystyle=24\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\,,

which yield different results for
\epsilon\neq 0
.
Hence there is an ambiguity in
d\neq 4
depending on the order of contracting the Levi-Civita tensors.

In the case of SIDIS the Levi-Civita tensors either stem from couplings to axial vector currents or the projection onto external polarizations.
To obtain consistent results, a unique contraction order for the Levi-Civita tensors must be established.
We therefore apply the following prescription for
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
, which can be applied at any order in perturbation theory:

1.

Replace axial vector vertices
\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}
according to (
52
) and the polarized quark projector according to (
65
).

2.

Contract Levi-Civita tensors from the projectors onto
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
and the polarized parton state through (
53
).

3.

Contract Levi-Civita tensors from the axial vector couplings (if present) through (
53
).

The divergent and finite axial vector coupling renormalizations
Larin:1993tq
and the finite renormalization of the polarized quark current
Matiounine:1998re
are then carried out in the usual manner.

:

C_{qq,A,\mathrm{NoW}}^{\{1,T\},(2)}
.

Figure 3
:

With this prescription for the contraction of pairs of Levi-Civita tensors, the axial-vector coefficient functions and the vector coefficient functions coincide in the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme whenever both electroweak couplings attach to the same quark line.
As a result, current conservation is restored in these contributions, as required by the chiral symmetry of massless QCD.
For contributions with axial vector couplings attaching to different quark lines in SIDIS, we distinguish two cases:

•

Either (or both) axial vector couplings couple to a closed quark line, giving rise to the sum of axial vector couplings
\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}g^{{q^{\prime}}}_{A}
, which vanishes for an even number of flavours.
If an odd number of massless flavours is considered e.g. within a Variable Flavour Number Scheme, the anomaly contribution
\Delta C_{qq,A,\mathrm{NoW}}^{\{1,T\},(2)}
arising in the 2-loop VV contribution, cf. Fig.
2
, is the only non-finite contribution.
While the contribution is UV divergent, it is also IR finite, and thus only requires the divergent and finite 2-loop pure-singlet UV renormalization of the axial vector vertex.
Therefore, no additional finite renormalization can be introduced from the polarized external current at NNLO.

•

The axial vector couplings couple to two different quark lines, which both contain an identified quark as shown in Fig.
3
.
The corresponding coefficient functions
\Delta C^{\{i\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}
and
\Delta C^{\{i\},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}
are UV and IR finite after phase space integration and do not have to be renormalized.
This contribution may equivalently be calculated in
4
dimensions and is therefore scheme independent.

Hence we are confident that we have applied a proper
\gamma_{5}
prescription for the SIDIS coefficient functions.

4.2
Technical details on the calculation

Three classes of diagrams contribute at NNLO: the real-real (RR) contributions, describing the real radiation of two additional partons; the real-virtual (RV) contributions, describing the radiation of a single parton with an additional loop; and the virtual-virtual contributions with no extra radiation, but two additional loops, all with respect to the leading order.

With these prescriptions the calculation of the tree-level RR matrix elements becomes straightforward.
In order to reduce the loop integrals arising in the RV and VV contributions to a small set of master integrals, we use
REDUZE

vonManteuffel:2012np
to generate integration-by-parts identities
Chetyrkin:1981qh
;
Laporta:2000dsw
.
The loop integrals in the RV matrix elements reduce to the 1-loop bubble and box integral
Gehrmann:1999as
.
In the VV we recover the four master integrals in
Gehrmann:2005pd
.

The coefficient functions are obtained by integrating the contributions over their corresponding phase spaces.
For the RV, the phase space integration is fully constrained by
\hat{x}
and
\hat{z}
.
As a consequence it is sufficient to expand the unintegrated RV contributions in distributions in
(1-\hat{x})
and
(1-\hat{z})
.
These expansions are described in detail in
Gehrmann:2022cih
, and
must be performed in the appropriate kinematical regions requiring analytical continuations of
the one-loop bubble and box master integrals
Gehrmann:2022cih
;
Haug:2022hkr
.
To integrate the RR phase space, we map the phase space integrals onto 14 integral families through reverse unitarity
Anastasiou:2002yz
.
These families contain a set of 8 independent propagators, 3 constraints from on-shellness, and the SIDIS phase space constraint introducing the fragmentation variable
z
.
They reduce to a set of 21 master integrals
Bonino:2024adk
;
Ahmed:2024owh
.

The resulting bare coefficient functions are initially expressed in terms of harmonic polylogarithms (HPLs)
Remiddi:1999ew
using the Mathematica packages
HPL

Maitre:2005uu
and
PolyLogTools

Duhr:2019tlz
, and subsequently converted into logarithms and polylogarithms.

The ultraviolet poles are removed by renormalizing the QCD coupling constant in the usual manner, and applying the divergent renormalization on the axial vector current
Larin:1993tq
.
Both renormalizations are multiplicative and independent of the kinematics, and yield the UV-finite coefficient functions in the Larin scheme.
Subsequently we apply mass factorization to cancel the IR poles associated with PDFs and FFs and obtain coefficient functions in the polarized and axial Larin scheme.
To convert our results from the Larin scheme into
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
, two steps have to be carried out.

•

The axial coupling is renormalized with the finite (multiplicative) renormalization
Larin:1993tq
, restoring current conservation at the axial vector vertex.

•

The finite scheme transformation has to be applied to the polarized splitting functions
Matiounine:1998re
;
Moch:2014sna
.
Details about this scheme transformation are provided in Appendix
A.2
.

4.3
Checks on the Calculation

In the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme
coefficient functions for which the axial vector couplings couple to the initial state quark line or,
in absence of an axial vector coupling, if at least one vector coupling couples to the initial state quark line,
are related in the polarized and unpolarized case
Matiounine:1998re
by chirality conservation of massless fermions,
which can be enforced by the application of an anticommuting
\gamma_{5}
.
After performing the finite scheme transformation as described in
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
and in detail in
Goyal:2024emo
, we recover the known results for photon exchange.
In the following we shall refer to the polarized results obtained in this way as “naïve”.

Comparing these naïve polarized results to our unpolarized electroweak results presented in
Bonino:2025qta

we observe discrepancies in
the SIDIS coefficient functions
\Delta C^{i,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{qq}
and
\Delta C^{i,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{\bar{q}q}
for
i=1,T
.
For
\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq}
the difference reads

\displaystyle C^{3,(2)}_{qq}-\Delta C^{1,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{qq}=\frac{C_{F}}{N_{C}}\delta(1-z)\Bigg\{(1+x)\bigg[

\displaystyle 4\ln(x)\ln(1+x)-\ln^{2}(x)

\displaystyle+4\operatorname{Li}_{2}(-x)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-3\ln(x)\bigg]-7(1-x)\Bigg\}\,.

(69)

The terms missing in
\Delta C^{1,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{qq}
appear as a surplus in
\Delta C^{1,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{\bar{q}q}
,

\displaystyle C^{3,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}-\Delta C^{1,(2),\text{na\"{i}ve}}_{\bar{q}q}=-\Big(C^{3,(2)}_{qq}-\Delta C^{1,(2)}_{qq}\Big)\,,

(70)

so that the sum of both contributions stays the same.
For
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{T}
we observe the same discrepancy.
However, the relation between the polarized and unpolarized results should hold at a diagrammatic level, and hence for the individual channels in SIDIS.

A closer inspection of the polarized scheme transformation offers insight.
The non-singlet polarized scheme transformation by definition corresponds to a finite renormalisation of the polarized non-singlet splitting functions.
At NNLO the non-singlet polarized splitting functions
\Delta P_{qq}^{\mathrm{NS},(1)}
and
\Delta P_{\bar{q}q}^{\mathrm{NS},(1)}
however do not enter the same coefficient function in SIDIS, and hence may not be renormalized together.
This distinction will be relevant for any polarized process with sensitivity to a second parton, as is the case in SIDIS.
Applying the finite scheme transformation expressed in a basis of non-singlet and pure-singlet contributions naïvely will therefore produce incorrect results in SIDIS and should be abandoned in favour of a scheme transformation in the flavour basis.
The details of this flavour basis decomposition and its application are discussed in Appendix
A.2
.
The finite scheme transformation in the flavour basis resolves the discrepancy of (
69
) and shifts the terms in the difference from
\Delta C_{\bar{q}q}^{\{1,T\},(2)}
into
\Delta C_{qq}^{\{1,T\},(2)}
.
The longitudinal polarized coefficient functions at NNLO are unaffected due to the vanishing structure function at leading order.
In this way we obtain the expected results in all polarized non-singlet channels.

Our results are expressed with the full scale dependence on
(\mu_{A},\mu_{F},\mu_{R})
.
They are distributed inside an ancillary file with the
arXiv
submission.
A description of content and notation of the ancillary file is given in Appendix
B
.

To validate our results we have carried out the following checks:
Our results fulfill the scale dependence predicted by the renormalization group equations in
(\mu_{F},\mu_{A},\mu_{R})
.
We have cross-checked our results for
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}
against our previous results for photon exchange
Bonino:2024wgg
, which were later confirmed by another group
Goyal:2024tmo
;
Goyal:2024emo
.
We find full agreement for the coefficient functions with vector couplings upon undoing the polarized scheme transformation.
Furthermore we find agreement also for the axial vector coefficient functions with the axial vector couplings attaching to a common quark line.
An equality for axial vector coefficient functions, where couplings attach to different quark lines, is neither required by theory nor realized.

Finally, we have verified that the polarized non-singlet coefficient functions in the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme presented in this paper agree with the results of our calculation of the unpolarized EW coefficient functions
Bonino:2025qta

5.1
Neutral Current

In order to increase the sensitivity to electroweak effects we study cross section asymmetries, which isolate even and odd contributions in the lepton polarization in the cross section difference (
2.3
).
The
\lambda_{\ell}
-odd component

\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}_{\mathrm{odd}}=\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}(\lambda_{\ell}=+1)-\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)\,

(74)

contains
\gamma\gamma
,
\gamma Z
and
ZZ
contributions. The
\lambda_{\ell}
-even component instead

\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}_{\mathrm{even}}=\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}(\lambda_{\ell}=+1)+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)\equiv 2\,\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}(\lambda_{\ell}=0)\,

(75)

contains only
\gamma Z
and
ZZ
exchanges.
Note that due to the antisymmetric polarized quark projector, the role of
\lambda_{\ell}
-odd and
\lambda_{\ell}
-even contributions in the cross section difference is swapped with respect to unpolarized SIDIS.
While only the
\lambda_{\ell}
-even NC cross section receives contributions in unpolarized photon-induced SIDIS
Bonino:2025qta
, polarized photon-induced SIDIS contributes exclusively to the
\lambda_{\ell}
-odd NC cross section difference.

We also provide predictions for the structure functions decomposition of the cross section difference (
74
)

\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h}_{\mathrm{odd}}=\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\gamma\gamma,1}_{\mathrm{odd}}+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,(\gamma Z+ZZ),1}_{\mathrm{odd}}+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,(\gamma Z+ZZ),T}_{\mathrm{odd}}+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,(\gamma Z+ZZ),L}_{\mathrm{odd}}\,,

(76)

\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,(\gamma Z+ZZ),i}_{\mathrm{odd}}=\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\gamma Z,i}_{\mathrm{odd}}+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,ZZ,i}_{\mathrm{odd}}\,,\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i=1,T,L

in order to further isolate electroweak effects.

As already introduced in
Bonino:2025qta
it is interesting to study asymmetries.
We present predictions for the
polarized neutral current asymmetry

-even components in (

\eta_{\gamma Z}
and
\eta_{ZZ}
with respect to the photon exchange contribution in
\lambda_{\ell}
-odd.

5

,
x
or

\lambda_{\ell}
-even component of the CC cross section difference is defined as

\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{\mathrm{even}}=\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=+1)+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)=\begin{cases}+\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{\mathrm{odd}}&\text{for }e^{+}\,,\\
-\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{\mathrm{odd}}&\text{for }e^{-}\,.\end{cases}

(84)

By projecting on the
\lambda_{\ell}
-even component, the dominant photon exchange from the
denominator of (
83
) is removed, thereby enhancing the electroweak contributions.

A^{h,L}_{\mathrm{CC}/\mathrm{NC}}
is well suited for experimental measurements due to the cancellation of systematic uncertainties in both numerator and denominator, analogously to what has been discussed for the unpolarized
A^{h}_{\mathrm{CC}/\mathrm{NC}}
asymmetry
Bonino:2025qta
.
Theory uncertainties in
A^{h,L}_{\mathrm{CC}/\mathrm{NC}}
are obtained with a 31-point uncorrelated scale variation.

We also study the electron-initiated
double-longitudinal spin asymmetry
for charged current exchange

A^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{LL}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{\mathrm{odd}}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}_{\mathrm{even}}}\,.

(85)

Both numerator and denominator are obtained by setting
\lambda_{\ell}=-1
in (
2.3
)
and (
2.2
) respectively.
The CC double-longitudinal spin asymmetry is useful to understand the relative size of CC events in polarized with respect to unpolarized collisions.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived the NNLO QCD corrections to semi-inclusive hadron production cross sections in
deeply inelastic charged lepton scattering on longitudinally polarized nucleons. We extended previous NNLO results
for polarized SIDIS for photon exchange
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
;
Goyal:2024emo
by accounting for
electroweak gauge boson exchanges in neutral and charged current processes.
In rederiving these earlier
results
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
;
Goyal:2024emo
, we identified a previous misassignment of finite
renormalization contributions between flavour non-singlet and singlet channels, which we now overcome by consistently
working on the basis of individual flavour channels for initial and final state partons, as documented in detail
in Appendix
A.2
.

We observe in our computation of the coefficient functions of the
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{T}
and
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{L}
structure functions that applying the polarized quark projector in a naïve Larin prescription yields incorrect results.
We resolve this issue by using a projector for polarized quarks, (
65
), which was previously
introduced in the context of polarized OMEs in
Schonwald:2019gmn
;
Behring:2019tus
to eliminate evanescent
structures in the quark polarization.

Based on the newly derived NNLO corrections, we perform a detailed phenomenological study of longitudinally polarized
SIDIS neutral and charged current processes for the kinematics of the EIC, differentially in

Q^{2}
,
z
and
x
.
Several new polarized structure functions become accessible through the chiral couplings of the weak gauge bosons.
Starting from the cross section differences between the two nucleon polarizations, we investigate the role
of contributions that are even or odd in the incoming lepton helicity (allowing the construction of single or double spin
asymmetries). In the NC case, we observe that electroweak tree-level corrections
to the photon-exchange process in polarized SIDIS are typically one order of
magnitude larger than in the unpolarized case
Bonino:2025qta
.
Even at relatively low values of
Q^{2}\sim 25\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
we observe percent-level corrections, which rapidly grow to around
10\,\%
at
200\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
, cf. Fig.
4
.
This feature can be largely understood from
the chiral coupling nature of the electroweak gauge bosons, whose contribution becomes enhanced by taking
nucleon polarization differences.
In contrast to the unpolarized case an estimate of electroweak effects obtained by rescaling the electroweak leading order by photon K-factors gives only gives percent-level agreement with the full calculation.

For all NC and CC SIDIS processes, we investigate the flavour-decomposition of the underlying partonic processes.
We observe a dominance of flavour combinations relating to the valence content of the nucleon target and the observed
hadron. This dominance is more pronounced than in unpolarized SIDIS
Bonino:2025qta
, and it persists at
small
x
, resulting from the suppression of gluon and sea quark polarized PDFs compared to their unpolarized
counterparts.

By projecting onto even and odd contributions in the lepton helicity, we construct single and double spin asymmetries.
In many cases these turn out to be sizeable at EIC kinematics and offer opportunities to probe the underlying polarized structure functions.

Appendix A
Mass Factorization and Finite Polarized Scheme Transformation

Apart from the divergent and multiplicative renormalization of the axial vector vertices, another finite renormalization is required to restore the conservation of the external polarized current, transforming it into the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme.
In Appendix
A.1
we review the formalism in the non-singlet/singlet basis, and reformulate it in Appendix
A.2
in the flavour basis.

A.1
Finite Polarized Scheme Transformation in the Non-Singlet/Singlet Basis

The finite polarized scheme transformation has been worked out within operator matrix element calculus
Matiounine:1998re
and has been successfully applied to the calculation of the polarized splitting functions
Moch:2014sna
;
Blumlein:2021enk
;
Blumlein:2021ryt

and the
inclusive
Wilson coefficients to the
g_{1}
structure function
Blumlein:2022gpp
up to three loops in the non-singlet case.

Based on scheme invariance of the physical observable
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}
the scheme transformation is
schematically realised in form of a matrix equation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}^{h}

\displaystyle=D^{h}\otimes\left[\Delta C^{1,\overline{\textrm{MS}}}\otimes\Delta f^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}\right]

\displaystyle=D^{h}\otimes\left[(\Delta C^{1,\mathrm{Larin}}\otimes Z^{-1})\otimes(Z\otimes\Delta f^{\mathrm{Larin}})\right]

\displaystyle\equiv D^{h}\otimes\left[\Delta C^{1,\mathrm{Larin}}\otimes\Delta f^{\mathrm{Larin}}\right]\,.

(86)

For the following discussion we omit the convolution with the fragmentation function, which is carried out in a different kinematic variable (
z
instead of
x
) and remains unaffected by the initial state scheme transformation.

The finite polarized scheme transformation is formulated in the basis of non-singlet (NS) and singlet (S) splitting and coefficient functions
Matiounine:1998re
;
Moch:2014sna
. The non-singlet scheme transformation is given by

\displaystyle\Delta C^{1,\overline{\textrm{MS}}}_{\text{NS}}\otimes\Delta f_{q}^{\mathrm{NS}}=(\Delta C^{1,\mathrm{Larin}}_{\mathrm{NS}}\otimes z^{-1}_{qq,\mathrm{NS}})\otimes(z_{qq,\mathrm{NS}}\otimes\Delta f_{q}^{\mathrm{NS},\mathrm{Larin}})\,.

(87)

For the singlet the scheme transformation is given by the matrix equation

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Delta C^{1,\overline{\textrm{MS}}}_{q,\mathrm{S}}&\Delta C_{g}^{1,\overline{\textrm{MS}}}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\Delta\Sigma^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}\\
\Delta f_{g}^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta C^{1,\mathrm{Larin}}_{q,\mathrm{S}}&\Delta C_{g}^{1,\mathrm{Larin}}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}z_{qq,\mathrm{S}}&z_{qg,\mathrm{S}}\\
z_{gq,\mathrm{S}}&z_{gg,\mathrm{S}}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}z_{qq,\mathrm{S}}&z_{qg,\mathrm{S}}\\
z_{gq,\mathrm{S}}&z_{gg,\mathrm{S}}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\Delta\Sigma^{\mathrm{Larin}}\\
\Delta f_{g}^{\mathrm{Larin}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(88)

with the polarized singlet distribution
\Delta\Sigma=\sum_{q}\left(\Delta f_{q}+\Delta f_{\bar{q}}\right)
.

The scheme transformation coefficients admit a series expansion in terms of the strong coupling constant,

\displaystyle z_{ij,\mathrm{(N)S}}=\delta(1-x)\,\delta_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{k}z_{ij,\mathrm{(N)S}}^{(k)}

(89)

The expressions for the series coefficients of
z_{qq,\mathrm{NS}}
and
z_{qq,\mathrm{PS}}=z_{qq,\mathrm{S}}-z_{qq,\mathrm{NS}}
up to NNLO are given in
Matiounine:1998re
;
Moch:2014sna
.
In the conventional transformation to the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme the following relations are imposed:

z_{qg}=z_{gq}=0
, and
z_{gg}=\delta(1-x)
.

A.2
Finite Polarized Scheme Transformation in the Flavour Basis

Unlike in inclusive DIS a final state particle is identified in SIDIS.
As the finite scheme transformations renormalize the polarized splitting functions, they inherit the particle-identity- or flavour-changing nature of the splittings.
Explicit formulae for the mass factorization and the finite renormalization of the individual channels in polarized SIDIS
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
are presented in
Goyal:2024emo
.

Expressing the finite scheme transformation in a flavour basis, we are able to uniquely associate the contributions to the SIDIS channels.
In doing so we find differences in the implementation of the finite renormalization.
The differences originate from misallocating the finite renormalization counter terms with the appropriate SIDIS channels in
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
;
Goyal:2024emo
.

Working in the basis of partonic channels the finite renormalization can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta C_{p^{\prime}p}^{i,k}

\displaystyle=\Delta C_{p^{\prime}m}^{i,k,\mathrm{Larin}}\otimes\left(Z^{-1}\right)_{mp}\,,

(90)

where
p,m,p^{\prime}\in\left\{q,\bar{q},{q^{\prime}},{\bar{q}^{\prime}},g\right\}
with
{q^{\prime}}\neq q
and a summation over
m
is implied.
The coefficient functions with the superscript
\mathrm{Larin}
are the UV-renormalized and mass factorized
coefficients obtained using Larin’s prescription for
\gamma_{5}
, while the ones without additional superscript
are the ones in the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
-scheme.
The matrix
Z
is given by

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}Z_{qq}&Z_{q\bar{q}}&Z_{q{q^{\prime}}}&Z_{q{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}&Z_{qg}\\
Z_{\bar{q}q}&Z_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}&Z_{\bar{q}{q^{\prime}}}&Z_{\bar{q}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}&Z_{\bar{q}g}\\
Z_{{q^{\prime}}q}&Z_{{q^{\prime}}\bar{q}}&Z_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}&Z_{{q^{\prime}}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}&Z_{{q^{\prime}}g}\\
Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q}&Z_{{q^{\prime}}\bar{q}}&Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}&Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}&Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}g}\\
Z_{gq}&Z_{g\bar{q}}&Z_{g{q^{\prime}}}&Z_{g{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}&Z_{gg}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(91)

Due to the symmetries of QCD
\left(Z\right)_{ij}=\left(Z\right)_{ji}
is imposed for

i,j\in\left\{q,\bar{q},{q^{\prime}},{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\right\}
and

\displaystyle Z_{qq}

\displaystyle=Z_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=Z_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}\,,

(92)

\displaystyle Z_{q{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{q{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\bar{q}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\bar{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,,

(93)

\displaystyle Z_{qg}

\displaystyle=Z_{\bar{q}g}

\displaystyle=Z_{{q^{\prime}}g}

\displaystyle=Z_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}g}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle Z_{gq}

\displaystyle=Z_{g\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=Z_{g{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{g{\bar{q}^{\prime}}}\,.

(95)

The non-singlet flavour decomposition

In order to find the relation between the independent terms in the flavour basis and the renormalization terms in the non-singlet/singlet basis, it is sufficient to find the equivalent relation for the polarized splitting functions.
The polarized splitting functions can be decomposed into

\displaystyle\Delta P_{q_{i}q_{k}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ik}\Delta P_{qq}^{v}+\Delta P_{qq}^{s}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta P_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ik}\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{v}+\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{s}\,.

(96)

It is instructive to look into the decomposition of the flavour asymmetry

\displaystyle\Delta f_{q_{i}q_{k}}^{\mathrm{NS},\pm}=(\Delta f_{q_{i}}\pm\Delta f_{\bar{q}_{i}})-(\Delta f_{q_{k}}\pm\Delta f_{\bar{q}_{k}})\,.

(97)

The flavour asymmetry by the above definitions evolves as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta f_{q_{i}q_{k}}^{\mathrm{NS},\pm}}{\mathrm{d}\log\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\biggl[\biggl(\Delta P_{q_{i}q_{a}}\pm\Delta P_{\bar{q}_{i}q_{a}}\biggr)\otimes\Delta f_{q_{a}}+\biggl(\Delta P_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{a}}\pm\Delta P_{\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{a}}\biggr)\otimes\Delta f_{\bar{q}_{a}}\biggr]-\left(i\leftrightarrow k\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta P_{qq}^{v}\pm\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{v}\right)\otimes\Delta f_{q_{i}q_{k}}^{\mathrm{NS},^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{\pm}\otimes\Delta f_{q_{i}q_{k}}^{\mathrm{NS},^{\pm}}\,.

(98)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta P_{qq}^{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}+\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{-}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}-\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{-}\right]\,.

(99)

We now separate the even and odd Mellin moments of the polarized splitting functions.
We observe that the structure function
\mathcal{G}^{h}_{1}\propto(\Delta q+\Delta\bar{q})
only yields odd moments in Mellin-
N
space. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(-1)^{N}}{2}\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+(-1)^{N}}{2}\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{-}\,,

(100)

using that
[1\pm(-1)^{N}]/2
projects onto the even/odd moments.

We can therefore define

\displaystyle\Delta P_{qq}^{\mathrm{NS}}(N)=\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}(N)+\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{-}(N)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{N-1}\biggl(\Delta P_{qq}^{v}-(-1)^{N}\Delta P_{\bar{q}q}^{v}\biggr)\,.

(101)

In this way odd moments (
(-1)^{N}\to-1
) give the combination
\Delta P_{qq}^{v}+\Delta P_{\bar{q}q}^{v}
and
even moments (
(-1)^{N}\to 1
) give the combination
\Delta P_{qq}^{v}-\Delta P_{\bar{q}q}^{v}
.
This is in contrast to the unpolarized case, where even moments describe the
combination
P_{qq}^{v}+P_{\bar{q}q}^{v}
and for which we therefore have to define

\displaystyle P_{qq}^{\text{NS}}(N)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{N-1}\biggl(P_{qq}^{v}+(-1)^{N}P_{\bar{q}q}^{v}\biggr)\,.

(102)

The same discussion applies to the additional finite renormalization, for which we find

\displaystyle z_{qq}^{\text{NS}}(N)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,x^{N-1}\biggl(z_{qq}^{v}-(-1)^{N}z_{\bar{q}q}^{v}\biggr)\,.

(103)

The pure-singlet flavour decomposition

For the singlet distribution the evolution is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta\Sigma}{\mathrm{d}\log\mu^{2}}=\left[\Delta P_{\mathrm{NS}}^{+}+n_{f}\left(\Delta P_{qq}^{s}+\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{s}\right)\right]\otimes\Delta\Sigma+2n_{f}\,\Delta P_{qg}\otimes\Delta f_{g}\,.

(104)

The pure-singlet piece of the evolution is
\Delta P_{qq}^{\mathrm{PS}}=n_{f}\left(\Delta P_{qq}^{s}+\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{s}\right)
.
Since the pure-singlet diagrams are insensitive to the fermion flow of the external quark, we find

\displaystyle\Delta P_{qq}^{s}=\Delta P_{q\bar{q}}^{s}=\frac{1}{2n_{f}}\,\Delta P_{qq}^{\mathrm{PS}}\,.

(105)

As a consequence the finite pure-singlet scheme transformation in the flavour basis is given by

\displaystyle z_{qq}^{s}=z_{q\bar{q}}^{s}=\frac{1}{2n_{f}}z_{qq}^{\mathrm{PS}}\,.

(106)

Flavour decomposition for the finite scheme transformation

The independent terms of the finite scheme transformation were calculated in
Matiounine:1998re

up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
and read

\displaystyle Z_{qq}

\displaystyle=\delta(1-x)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}z_{qq}^{v,(0)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left(z_{qq}^{v,(1)}+z_{qq}^{s,(1)}\right)\,,

(107)

\displaystyle Z_{\bar{q}q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left(z_{\bar{q}q}^{v,(1)}+z_{qq}^{s,(1)}\right)\,,

(108)

\displaystyle Z_{q^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}z_{qq}^{s,(1)}\,,

(109)

\displaystyle Z_{qg}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(110)

\displaystyle Z_{gq}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(111)

\displaystyle Z_{gg}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(112)

with

\displaystyle z_{qq}^{v,(0)}

\displaystyle=z_{qq}^{(0)}=-4C_{\mathrm{F}}(1-x)\,,

(113)

\displaystyle z_{qq}^{v,(1)}

\displaystyle=C_{\mathrm{F}}T_{\mathrm{R}}n_{f}(1-x)\biggl[\frac{20}{9}+\frac{4}{3}\ln(x)\biggr]-C_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\biggl[4(1-x)+\bigl(2(2+x)

\displaystyle-4(1-x)\ln(1-x)\bigr)\ln(x)\biggr]-C_{\mathrm{F}}C_{\mathrm{A}}\biggl[\frac{148}{9}(1-x)-\frac{1}{3}\pi^{2}(1-x)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{3}(10-x)\ln(x)+(1-x)\ln(x)^{2}\biggr]\,,

(114)

\displaystyle z_{\bar{q}q}^{v,(1)}

\displaystyle=-C_{\mathrm{F}}\left(C_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}\right)\biggl[-14(1-x)+\frac{2}{3}\pi^{2}(1+x)-2(1+x)\ln(x)^{2}

\displaystyle-(1+x)\left(6-8\ln(1+x)\right)\ln(x)+8(1+x)\text{Li}_{2}(-x)\biggr]\,,

(115)

\displaystyle z_{qq}^{s,(1)}

\displaystyle=z_{\bar{q}q}^{s,(1)}=\frac{1}{2n_{f}}z_{qq}^{\text{PS},(1)}=C_{\mathrm{F}}T_{\mathrm{R}}\biggl(2(1-x)+(3-x)\ln(x)+\frac{1}{2}(2+x)\ln^{2}(x)\biggr)\,,

(116)

according to our flavour decomposition.
Here
\operatorname{Tr}[T^{a}T^{b}]=T_{\mathrm{R}}\delta_{ab}
for the colour group generators and
C_{\mathrm{A}}=N_{c}
and
C_{\mathrm{F}}=T_{\mathrm{R}}(N_{c}^{2}-1)/N_{c}
are the colour coefficients.
Note that we assume
{q^{\prime}}\neq q
in this discussion.

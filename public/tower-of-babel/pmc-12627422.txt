Optimizing the angle of arrival positioning error of a UAV via an improved nutcracker optimization algorithm
Authors: Yinglei Li, Qingping Hu, Shiyan Sun, Wenjian Ying, Xiaojia Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the issues of insufficient accuracy due to nonlinear error accumulation in traditional drone angle-of-arrival (AOA) positioning and the tendency of existing optimization algorithms to get stuck in local optima, this paper proposes a positioning error optimization method that integrates phased correction with an improved Starling optimization algorithm. By constructing a multi-source error propagation model, we analyze the error propagation characteristics of UAV position, attitude, and pod attitude. A phased optimization framework based on observation sequences is designed to suppress the nonlinear accumulation of errors. Subsequently, by integrating the improved Starling optimization algorithm with cubic chaotic mapping and a spiral search strategy, optimal allocation and compensation of error sources are achieved. Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate that the improved algorithm achieves a 73.29% reduction in positioning error distance compared to AOA positioning accuracy and a 58.12% improvement over the original Starling optimization algorithm. It significantly outperforms other comparison algorithms, proving this method effectively corrects nonlinear perturbations in electro-optical systems and provides a higher-precision solution for passive positioning of UAVs.Keywords: Target passive localization, Error analysis, Airborne optoelectronic pod, Angle of arrivalSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become important tools in modern reconnaissance because of their high covertness, low risk, small size and ability to carry various sensors according to mission requirements1. Moreover, monocular vision sensors have the advantages of high precision and low cost2; therefore, the combination of monocular vision and UAVs for target localization has been the object of extensive research by scholars3. Traditional UAV target localization methods are mainly divided into active and passive localization methods. Active localization uses the measured distance and angle information between the target point and the optoelectronic platform to localize the target4; however, a UAV also reveals its own position when5,6 electromagnetic waves are used for radiation. Passive positioning relies solely on the passive measurement of electromagnetic signals radiated by the target to achieve positioning7, which compensates for the shortcomings of active positioning; however, the positioning accuracy is highly correlated with the noise of the sensor8. With the rapid development of UAVs, optimizing the passive positioning of UAVs has become a new way to improve positioning accuracy.

Depending on different positioning principles, passive positioning can be classified into angle of arrival (AOA)9, time difference of arrival (TDOA)10, frequency difference of arrival (FDOA)11, received signal strength (RSS)12 and a variety of hybrid localization systems; the directional cosine intersection positioning method was the earliest researched and most mature passive localization system. Its positioning principle is simple and requires minimal computation; however, owing to the limitations of sensor accuracy and other factors, there is a problem of insufficient positioning accuracy, which needs to be optimized from several aspects13. Torrieri14 first introduced the Cramer–Rao lower bound (CRLB) into the accuracy evaluation system of passive localization and derived the CRLB formulation applied to purely azimuthal target localization and tracking. Oshman and Davidson15 established the relationship between the estimation error covariance matrix and the CRLB, the Fisher information matrix triple, for the first time. Ye et al.16 proposed a method for estimating angles based on signal fitting and direct data acquisition from transmitters to address the challenge of GPS positioning indoors.and with extensive research, scholars have found that the main difficulty of AOA localization lies in the nonlinear correlation between the measurement data and the target position17. Shao et al.18 developed an efficient closed-form self-localization algorithm based on the AOA via an auxiliary variable-based approach. Wang et al.19 proposed an algorithmic study of a position-biased maximum likelihood estimator (LPML) for orientation-only target localization to improve the accuracy of AOA localization in the 2D plane, and Wen et al.20 presented a novel AOA localization framework for unmanned aircraft that is applicable to 3D scenes. Zhao et al.21 proposed an ultraviolet hybrid positioning method by integrating RSSI positioning with AOA positioning algorithms, enabling collaborative positioning via ultraviolet communication among drone swarms. Du et al.22 proposed a passive positioning algorithm based on drone aerial imagery and angle of arrival (AOA) for target passive positioning, ensuring stable target tracking. XU et al.23 achieved high-precision target localization by employing passive positioning of unknown targets using two unmanned aerial vehicles and corresponding AOA measurement methods. The above AOA localization algorithms do not consider the sensor’s own localization errors, so how to effectively handle and distribute these errors is highly important for improving the accuracy of UAV AOA localization. If the standard deviation of the error source is taken as a variable for optimization, the error allocation problem can be transformed into a parameter optimization problem and solved via an optimization algorithm. Metaheuristic algorithms are based on principles found in nature and simulate the biological evolution process to find the best solution24. These algorithms do not rely on specific mathematical formulas, and they gradually approach the best solution through iterative searching and optimization, which is advantageous in dealing with nonlinear problems25. In recent years, several metaheuristic algorithms have been proposed, including particle swarm algorithm (PSO)26, the sparrow algorithm (SSA)27, gray wolf optimization (GWO)28 and so on. The nutcracker optimization algorithm (NOA) is a new intelligent optimization algorithm proposed by Abdel-Basset et al.29 in 2023. The algorithm simulates two different behaviors exhibited by a nutcracker in different periods, demonstrates excellent performance and powerful global searchability, and is widely used in path planning and other problems30, however, when dealing with some problems, the algorithm can exhibit uneven population initialization and easy fall into local optimization. To address the above shortcomings, an improved nutcracker optimization algorithm is proposed. By introducing cubic chaotic mapping and a spiral search strategy, this new approach can effectively prevent the algorithm from falling into a local optimal state, enhance the exploration ability of the algorithm and accelerate its convergence speed. Simulation and flight experiments demonstrate that the improved nutcracker optimization algorithm is more effective.

The main innovations of this paper are as follows:

By linearizing errors through Taylor series expansion and integrating the Jacobian matrix, a multi-source error propagation model is constructed to achieve linearization and quantitative analysis of positioning errors.

Targeted optimizations address limitations of the original Starling algorithm by introducing a cubic chaotic mapping to generate the initial population, resolving uneven population distribution issues. A spiral search strategy is incorporated to expand the optimization scope, overcoming local optima bottlenecks and enhancing coverage of the search space.

A phased error correction method based on observation sequences was designed. Unlike traditional single-step least-squares solutions that cause error accumulation, this approach suppresses the nonlinear accumulation effect of errors.

The improved AOA localization method is applied to UAV target localization, and better localization results are achieved.

The subsequent sections of this paper are outlined as follows: Section I establishes a suitable coordinate system and linearizes the error in the positioning process; Section II introduces the principle of the AOA positioning method and outlines the structure of the Nutcracker optimization algorithm and proposes an improved Nutcracker optimization algorithm; Section III conducts Monte Carlo simulation experiments to test and analyze the improved algorithm; Section IV uses the improved algorithm to conduct an application analysis of the UAV’s actual flight experiments via the improved algorithm for application and analysis; and Section V concludes the paper.

Error transfer theory

Definition of a coordinate system

The following coordinate systems need to be used in the UAV targeting process: the geodetic coordinate system, world coordinate system, geographic coordinate system, airframe coordinate system, camera coordinate system, image physical coordinate system, and pixel coordinate system.

Geodetic coordinate system

The WGS84 coordinate system was used, with the origin at the geometric center of the Earth’s ellipsoid, coinciding with the Earth’s center of mass; the latitude B, longitude L, and altitude H were used to describe the three-dimensional position of the target point.

World coordinate system

The coordinate origin is located in the center of the Earth’s ellipsoid; the Z-axis points to the Earth’s north pole and coincides with the axis of rotation, the X-axis points to the intersection of the prime meridian and the equator, the Y-axis is located in the equatorial plane, and the X-axis and Y-axis form a right-handed right-angled coordinate system, as shown in Fig. 1.

World coordinate system.

Geographical coordinate system

The coordinate system uses the northeast Earth coordinate system, with the center of mass of the UAV as the origin of the coordinate system, the X-axis pointing north of the Earth, the Y-axis pointing east of the Earth, and the Z-axis pointing downward perpendicular to the Earth’s surface, forming a right-handed coordinate system.

Airframe coordinate system

The origin coincides with the origin of the geographic coordinate system, the X-axis points directly in front of the carrier, the Y-axis points to the right wing, and the Z-axis is perpendicular to its other two axes and points downward. When the UAV does not experience an attitude change, this coordinate system coincides with the spatial right-angled coordinate system, as shown in Fig. 2.

Body coordinate system.

Camera coordinate system

The origin of the camera coordinate system is taken as the optical center of the camera, the Z-axis is coincident with the optical axis of the camera, and the camera direction is taken as the positive direction. When the camera has no attitude angle change, the X-axis points in the direction of the right wing of the UAV, and the Y-axis points to the right underneath the body of the UAV.

Image physics coordinate system

The origin of the physical coordinate system of the image is located at the intersection of the optical axis and the image plane, and the X- and Y-axes are parallel to the two perpendicular sides of the image plane, as shown in Fig. 3.

Image physical coordinate system and pixel coordinate system.

Pixel coordinate system

The coordinate origin is located at the top-left vertex of the image plane, and the X- and Y-axes are parallel to the X- and Y-axes, respectively, of the image physical coordinate system, as shown in Fig. 3.

Improved AOA localization method

When the classic least squares method is used to realize the target geometrical localization solution, the failure to establish an effective error compensation mechanism results in the nonlinear accumulation of errors in the coordinate conversion process, which ultimately leads to a significant deviation between the theoretical solution coordinates of the target point and the actual spatial coordinates.

To address this problem, this paper designs a phased positioning correction method that is based on observation sequences. During the observation process in which the UAV continuously collects n frames of image data, the complete trajectory is divided into a sub-sequence consisting of neighboring observation nodes. For each sub-sequence, the least squares localization solution is first performed by the geometric relationship between adjacent observation nodes, and then the error propagation model based on the covariance matrix is introduced. The error compensation is performed by the improved star raven optimization algorithm to effectively inhibit the cumulative effect of the error generated in the process of coordinate conversion. Finally, the error-corrected localization results are arithmetically averaged to obtain the coordinate estimate of the target point P in the world coordinate system. This staged processing mechanism significantly improves the localization accuracy in complex observation environments by reducing the error of a single solution.

Due to the close proximity of target points, the Earth is treated as a sphere and the spherical cosine theorem is adopted as an evaluation metric to fully account for the influence of Earth’s curvature on positioning results. A smaller difference between these two distances indicates a closer straight-line distance between the target point and the computational point. Improved nutcracker optimization algorithm

The original Star Crow algorithm uses random numbers to generate initial population positions, which can easily lead to uneven distribution of the population within the search space, causing the algorithm to get stuck in local optima. Therefore, this paper introduces the Cubic Chaos Map. Compared to traditionally uniformly distributed random numbers, the cubic chaotic mapping exhibits deterministic traversal properties. It uniformly covers all possible regions within the search space via chaotic trajectories, offering the advantages of rapid optimization speed and high precision.

The original Starling algorithm relies on the random stride length of Levy walks and a linear decay factor for position updates during storage strategy and cache search phases. This leads to excessively small stride lengths in the algorithm’s later stages, causing it to search near local optima and become trapped. Inspired by the whale optimization algorithm, a spiral search strategy is introduced to update the starling positions in both the storage strategy and caching search phases. This expands the search scope and prevents premature convergence to local optima.

By integrating a cubic chaotic map with the spiral search strategy, the Star-Crow Optimization Algorithm is enhanced. Building upon the uniform initial population provided by the cubic chaotic map, the spiral search guides the population toward convergence to the globally optimal error allocation scheme through its nonlinear spiral trajectory.

The core computational logic of the improved algorithm focuses on numerical operations at the coefficient level without introducing additional loop iteration structures. According to the analysis criteria for algorithmic time complexity, the number of loop iterations is a key factor influencing time complexity. Therefore, the time complexity of the improved algorithm remains consistent with the original algorithm and does not increase. It should be noted that to enhance optimization performance, the improved algorithm incorporates exponential operations and trigonometric functions. The inclusion of such operations slightly increases the computational load per iteration, resulting in a minor rise in computational complexity compared to the original algorithm. However, from an engineering cost-benefit perspective, the improved algorithm achieves a significant improvement in positioning accuracy at the expense of a minimal increase in computational complexity. The gain in accuracy far outweighs the cost associated with the increased computational complexity.

Conclusions

In this paper, an error compensation method based on staged positioning optimization and an improved nutcracker algorithm is proposed for the error accumulation problem in the traditional UAV AOA positioning method. The error transfer process is analyzed by constructing a coordinate system, the error transfer model is derived, the effects of the UAV attitude angle, pod angle and GPS position error on the final positioning result are quantified by the Jacobi matrix, the error transfer model is used to analyze the propagation of error in each link during the positioning process, and a staged correction method is designed to suppress the nonlinear accumulation of error. Cubic chaotic mapping is used to increase population diversity, the spiral search strategy is introduced to strengthen the global exploration and local development ability, the nutcracker optimization algorithm is improved to achieve the optimal distribution of errors, and the Monte Carlo simulation shows that the improved algorithm significantly reduces the localization deviation due to random errors and improves the overall accuracy of the target localization of the UAV. Actual flight experiments also verify the reliability of the simulation results, confirming that the method proposed in this paper can effectively improve the accuracy of UAV target localization in practical applications. In the future, we will explore a more accurate error distribution mechanism, extend our work to analyze the mechanism of error source interaction, construct a time-varying error transfer model that includes environmental parameters, and further improve the reliability of localization in complex scenarios.

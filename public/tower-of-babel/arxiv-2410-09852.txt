Strong Purity and Phantom Morphisms
R. Hafezi, J. Asadollahi, S. Sadeghi and Y. Zhang

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative ring and S ⊆ R S\subseteq R be a multiplicative subset. We introduce and study the concept of S S -purity based on the notion of S S -strongly flat modules. The class of S S -pure injective modules will be studied. We demonstrate that this class is enveloping and explore its closedness under extension. The concept of purity is closely connected to the existence of phantom maps. So we will delve into the study of the S S -phantom morphisms. We will establish that the S S -phantom ideal is a precovering ideal and examine the situations where it becomes a covering ideal. Finally, in the last section, we will investigate an ideal version of the ‘Optimistic Conjecture’, raised by Positselski and Slávik.

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative ring,
S\subseteq R
be a multiplicative subset and
R_{S}
be the localization of
R
at
S
. An
R
-module
G
is called
S
-strongly flat if
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(G,C)=0
, for every
S
-weakly cotorsion module
C
, i.e. for every
R
-module
C
with the property that
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{S},C)=0
. Let
{S\mbox{-}\rm{SF}}
, resp.
{S\mbox{-}\rm{WC}}
, denote the class of
S
-strongly flat, resp.
S
-weakly cotorsion,
R
-modules.

These classes of modules are introduced in
[
Tr1
, Section 2]
, when
R
is a commutative integral domain and
R_{S}
is the quotient field of
R
. In
[
FS
]
, they considered an arbitrary commutative ring but
S
is the multiplicative set of all regular elements. In
[
P
]
, the general case where
S
may contain zero-divisors was considered. For a more recent account on these two classes see
[
PS1
]
and
[
BP
]
. In the non-commutative case this class is studied in
[
FN
]
, when
R
is a right Ore domain with classical right quotient ring
Q
. See also the survey
[
Sa
]
.

It is known that the pair
({S\mbox{-}\rm{SF}},{S\mbox{-}\rm{WC}})
forms a complete cotorsion pair. This, in particular, implies that
{S\mbox{-}\rm{SF}}
is a precovering class. So a natural question is when it is a covering class. This question is raised in
[
Tr2
]
. In
[
BS
]
it is shown that when
R
is an integral domain and
R_{S}
is the quotient field of
R
, then
{S\mbox{-}\rm{SF}}
is a covering class if and only if
R
is almost perfect, that is, all proper quotients of
R
are perfect. Recall that a ring is perfect if all its flat modules are projective. This result is generalized in
[
FS
]
to an arbitrary commutative ring
R
with zero-divisors when
R_{S}
is the total ring of quotients. A wide generalization for the case when
S
may contain zero-divisors is studied in
[
BP
]
. It is shown that all
R
-modules have
S
-strongly flat covers if and only if
R
is an
S
-almost perfect ring, i,e, the localization
R_{S}
is a perfect ring and, for every
s\in S
, the quotient ring
R/sR
is perfect as well. This, in turn, implies that all flat
R
-modules are
S
-strongly flat.

On the other hand, ideal approximation theory is developed in
[
FGHT
]
. It provides a wide generalization of the classical approximation theory. In this new theory, modules are replaced by morphisms and additive subcategories are replaced by ideals. The phantom ideal is one of the most important examples of ideals. The concept of purity is closely related to the occurrence of phantom maps. A morphism
\varphi:U\rightarrow Z
of
R
-modules is called a phantom morphism if the pullback along
\varphi
of any short exact sequence with the right term
Z
gives rise to a pure exact sequence. This notion was first studied in homotopy theory
[
Mc
]
, then in the setting of triangulated categories
[
N
]
, and in the stable category of
k[G]
modules in
[
Gn
]
. It was also studied in the general setting of modules over an associative ring with identity in
[
H1
]
. The phantom ideal of the category of
R
-modules, denoted by
{\rm{Ph}}
, consists of all phantom morphisms. In
[
H1
, Theorem 7]
, it is shown that
{\rm{Ph}}
is a covering ideal.

This paper, aims to develop an ideal approximation theory by introducing the concept of
S
-strong flatness. We are working within a general setting where
R
is an arbitrary commutative ring and the multiplicative set
S
may contain zero-divisors. By definition every
S
-strongly flat module
G
is flat, and thus a short exact sequence
\delta
ending at an
S
-strongly flat module is pure exact. However, it has additional properties:
\delta_{S}
, the localization of
\delta
at
S
, and
\delta/s\delta
for every
s\in S
are split exact sequences as sequences of
R_{S}
-modules and
R/sR
-modules, respectively. This is because
G_{S}
and
G/sG
are projectives as
R_{S}
and
R/sR
modules, respectively (see
[
BP
, Lemma 3.1]
). Building on these properties, we define the notion of
S
-pure exact sequences. A short exact sequence
\delta
is called
S
-pure exact if it is pure exact and if the sequences
\delta_{S}=\delta\otimes_{R}R_{S}
and
\delta/s\delta=\delta\otimes_{R}R/sR
, for every element
s\in S
, are split exact. We show that the collection of all
S
-pure exact sequences is a sub-bifunctor of the bifunctor
{\rm{Ext}}
. Using this, we then define the notion of
S
-pure injective modules as injective modules with respect to the collection of all
S
-pure exact sequences. For a module, we provide an explicit
S
-pure injective preenvelope and, using standard arguments, we show that the class of all
S
-pure injective modules is an enveloping class.

We define
S
-phantom morphisms as those morphisms
\varphi:U\rightarrow Z
such that the pullback along
\varphi
of any short exact sequence yields an
S
-pure exact sequence. The collection of all
S
-phantom morphisms form an ideal, denoted by
{S\mbox{-}{\rm{Ph}}}
. Based on the existence of
S
-pure injective envelopes, we show that
{S\mbox{-}{\rm{Ph}}}
is a precovering ideal and discuss the situations where it is a covering ideal.

Note that pure-exactness and pure-injectivity for modules over a ring have been introduced by Cohn
[
Coh
]
. These notions have been studied in different settings, see for instance
[
Kra
]
and
[
Bel
]
.

Although the structure of strongly flat modules has been studied for over two decades, their structure is still mysterious. In
[
PS1
]
the authors posed a conjecture, called Optimistic Conjecture, hereafter denoted as
(\mathbb{OC})
, which states that in case the projective dimension of
R_{S}
as an
R
-module, does not exceed
1
, a flat
R
-module
F
is
S
-strongly flat if the localization module
S^{-1}F=F_{S}
is a projective
R_{S}
-module and, for every
s\in S
, the quotient module

is a projective
R/sR
-module. The authors also introduced the concept of right
1
-obtainability of an
R
-module from a given class of
R
-modules, called a ‘seed class’, and provided some positive results towards the conjecture in Theorems 1.3, 1.4, and 1.5 of
[

]
. They demonstrated that the conjecture holds if the multiplicative subset
S
of

is either countable, consists of nonzero-divisors in

, or is bounded, meaning there exists an element

such that

for

and

implies that

. Additionally, in
[

, Proposition 7.13]

is an

-h-nil ring, meaning for every element

, the ring

is semilocal of Krull dimension

. Towards the end of the paper, an ideal version of this conjecture is stated and proven. We remark that domains

with a ring of quotients having projective dimension at most one are extensively studied in
[

]
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section

is dedicated to preliminary results, where we gather known facts about strongly flat modules in the first subsection. For a class

of

-modules, in
[

]
the authors studied

-periodic modules, i.e. modules

that fit into a short exact sequence

where

, see also

]
and

]
. A

-periodic module

. In Subsection

-periodic modules are trivial, provided

holds for the pair
(R,S)
(Convention
2.4
). In Subsection

, the basics of ideal approximation theory are reviewed. In particular, we prove some results emphasizing that phantom morphisms could be considered as the morphism counterpart of flat modules.

In Section

we introduce and study the notion of
S
-purity (Definition

). Using the concept of

-pure exactness, the notion of

-pure injective modules will be defined. We investigate the properties of the class of

-pure injective modules. In particular, it is shown that it is a covering class (Proposition

). We also consider the situation where this class is closed under extension, see Subsection

. It will be shown that if

Section

-phantom morphisms (Definition

is

is perfect, and for every

, the quotient ring

.

The final section of the paper discusses an ideal version of the Optimistic Conjecture. It demonstrates that if

-phantom morphism, then

is a projective morphism, and for every

,

-almost flat, then

-phantom if

Let

2.1.
Strongly flat modules

An
R
-module

is called

-weakly cotorsion if
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{S},C)=0
. Let

denote the class of

-weakly cotorsion

{}^{\perp}{S\mbox{-}\rm{WC}}=\{M\in{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R\ |\ {\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C)=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ C\in{S\mbox{-}\rm{WC}}\}.

Facts 2.2
.

Here we list some known facts on these two classes of modules. See, for instance,

(i)

Let

(ii)

2.2.
Periodic strongly flat modules

Let

be an arbitrary class of modules. An

-module

is called

. Using the terminology of

[

-periodic module is trivial or equivalently every pure

-periodic module is trivial. Moreover, in

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

be a commutative ring and

be a multiplicative subset such that
(\mathbb{OC})
holds for
(R,S)
. Let

be an

-module fitting into a pure exact sequence

such that

is

-strongly flat. Then

is

-strongly flat.

Proof.

Since

is flat so is

. Therefore,

and

, for every

, are flat. On the other hand, by
[

, Lemma 3.1]

G_{S}
and

, are projective. Hence, by considering two exact sequences

0\rightarrow M_{S}\rightarrow G_{S}\rightarrow M_{S}\rightarrow 0,

and using

, Theorem 2.5]
, we get that

and

, for every

-strongly flat.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Phantom morphisms are morphism versions of flat modules. For instance, one can see that

is perfect if and only if every trivial phantom morphism is projective.
Recall that a morphism

of

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(f,B)=0

-module

be a flat

3.
S
-purity

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and
S\subseteq R
be a multiplicative subset that may contain some zero divisors, as in our setup.

Definition 3.1
.

A short exact sequence
\eta:0\rightarrow N\rightarrow M\rightarrow L\rightarrow 0
of
R
-modules is called
S
-pure exact if it is pure exact, the induced sequence
\eta\otimes_{R}R_{S}
is split exact and for every
s\in S
, the induced sequence
\eta\otimes_{R}R/sR
is split exact. In this case,
N
is called an
S
-pure submodule of
M
. We let
\mathbb{S}
denote the collection of all
S
-pure exact sequences.

Example 3.2
.

Every short exact sequence
\eta:0\rightarrow N\rightarrow M\rightarrow R_{S}\rightarrow 0
with right term
R_{S}
is
S
-pure exact. Indeed, since
R_{S}
is a flat
R
-module,
\eta
is pure exact. Moreover, since
R_{S}
is a projective
R_{S}
-module,
\eta_{S}:0\rightarrow M_{S}\rightarrow N_{S}\rightarrow R_{S}\rightarrow 0
splits. For the validity of the third condition note that
R_{S}\otimes_{R}R/sR=0
, for every
s\in S
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The collection
\mathbb{S}
comprise an additive subfunctor
{\rm{Ext}}_{\mathbb{S}}
of
{\rm{Ext}}
.

Proof.

It is plain that
\mathbb{S}
contains split short exact sequences and is closed under direct sums. We verify that
\mathbb{S}
is closed under pullbacks and pushouts.
Let
\eta:0\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0
be in
\mathbb{S}
and
\eta^{\prime}:0\rightarrow Y\rightarrow U\rightarrow A\rightarrow 0
be the pullback of
\eta
along morphism
f:A\rightarrow X
, as depicted in the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\prime}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Since the class of pure exact sequences is closed under pullbacks,
\eta^{\prime}
is pure exact. Now apply the functors
-\otimes_{R}R_{S}
and
-\otimes_{R}R/sR
to the above pullback diagram. The induced short exact sequences
\eta_{S}=\eta\otimes_{R}R_{S}
and
\eta_{R/sR}=\eta\otimes_{R}R/sR
are split, because

. Hence

. A similar argument, concludes that

is closed under pushout.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

An
R
-module

is
S
-pure injective if it is injective with respect to the class
\mathbb{S}
. The collection of all
S
-pure injective
R
-modules will be denoted by
{S\mbox{-}{\rm{PInj}}}
.

By definition, it is clear that every pure injective module is
S
-pure injective. Moreover, the adjoint duality of Hom and tensor implies that
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{S},X)
and
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R/sR,X)
, for any
R
-module
X
, are
S
-pure injective.

Remark 3.5
.

Every
S
-pure injective
R
-module

is an
S
-weakly cotorsion
R
-module. Indeed, let
\eta:0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow R_{S}\rightarrow 0
be an element in
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{S},M)
. Since
\eta\in\mathbb{S}
and
M
is
S
-pure injective, then
\eta
splits. Hence
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{S},M)=0
and

is
S
-weakly cotorsion.

Lemma 3.6
.

A pure exact sequence
0\rightarrow N\rightarrow M\rightarrow L\rightarrow 0
is

-pure if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied.

(i)

For every
R_{S}
-module
X
, the induced sequence

is exact.

(ii)

For every
s\in S
and every

-module

, the induced sequence

is exact.

Proof.

Let

be an
S
-pure exact sequence.
Since

\eta_{S}:0\rightarrow N_{S}\rightarrow M_{S}\rightarrow L_{S}\rightarrow 0

is split, for every
R_{S}
-module

, the sequence

0\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R_{S}}(L_{S},X)\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R_{S}}(M_{S},X)\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R_{S}}(N_{S},X)\rightarrow 0

is exact. Now by adjunction, we get the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(L,X)\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(M,X)\rightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(N,X)\rightarrow 0.

The validity of the Statement

Now let

and

. We have to show that

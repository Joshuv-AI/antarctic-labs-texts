Multi-scale analysis of nutrient and environmental dynamics in Hongfeng Lake Southwest China
Authors: Lizhi Wang, Fanli Meng, Hongli Song, Juan An, Yun Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional linear correlation analysis may not fully capture the true relationship between these variables. Therefore, multi-scale running correlation analysis, such as time-dependent intrinsic correlation (TDIC) and continuous wavelet transform based on Hilbert–Huang transform (HHT), provides valuable insights into local correlations and the evolving relationship between nutrients and environmental factors over time. In this study, we investigated seven environmental factors and four water quality nutrient indicators in deep lakes on the Yungui Plateau in southwestern China. The results revealed that there may be strong correlations between environmental factors and nutrient levels during certain periods, while opposite trends may emerge at other times. These variations in correlation could be attributed to uncertain physical processes, spatial heterogeneity, or the impact of different climatic factors on local hydrological processes. Wavelet analysis indicated that changes in environmental factors lag behind those in nutrient levels, particularly on a cycle of about 12 months. This suggests that changes in environmental factors align with natural patterns after the water body has been polluted. These conclusions underscore the complexity and dynamic nature of the relationship between environmental factors and nutrient levels in water bodies, highlighting the importance of employing advanced analysis techniques to capture this complexity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75812-4.Keywords: Eutrophication, Hilbert–Huang transform, Time-dependent intrinsic correlationSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Limnology

Introduction

Eutrophication is one of the most serious water environmental challenges confronting China today, and it is a major factor influencing lake water quality1. Nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) in water bodies are significant nutrients in lake ecosystems, and their forms and amounts not only determine lake primary productivity and ecosystem stability but are also important influencing elements in lake eutrophication2. N and P are acknowledged as the primary limiting factors for lake eutrophication and are critical nutrient constituents in the current lake ecology3. Environmental factors influence N and P release and absorption in sediments, hence influencing N and P levels in water bodies4.

Environmental factors such as pH, dissolved oxygen(DO), temperature, and redox potential play a significant role in the N and P cycling process5. The levels of nutrients, such as N and P, in aquatic environments are influenced by various environmental parameters including temperature, DO, pH, disturbance, and light availability6. Therefore, understanding these relationships is crucial for the management and mitigation of eutrophication in aquatic ecosystems.

Due to the interplay between these environmental factors and their combined impact on lake ecosystems, examining each factor separately is difficult. Therefore, using helpful analytical methods to simplify or separate these complex environmental elements is vital. For example, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) can reduce a dataset with several variables to a few principal components that capture the primary variance within the original dataset. Using this method, we may reduce the large dataset of environmental elements to a few essential parameters, making it easier to identify and explain their influence on lake ecosystems.

The variation of nutrients and environmental factors is a complex natural process, influenced by multiple factors, often exhibiting non-linear, non-stationary sequences, accompanied by a certain degree of randomness and periodicity7. Especially in recent years, the combined effects of global climate warming and human activities have not only accelerated eutrophication processes at regional scales but also exacerbated regional disparities and uncertainties in water body pollution8. In response to the multi-time scale and multi-frequency characteristics exhibited by nutrient and environmental factor time series, the analysis methods have evolved from the time domain to the time-frequency domain9. Fourier transform has been widely used since 1965 due to its time-frequency analysis characteristics. However, limited by the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, the Fourier transform cannot describe frequency components at arbitrary moments and may mask sequence features at certain scales10. Subsequently, wavelet analysis, developed based on the Fourier transform, has been increasingly used to study the multi-scale change patterns and evolutionary trends of time series. Although wavelet analysis uses an adjustable time-frequency window to resolve the contradiction between time and frequency resolution and possesses good time-frequency localization characteristics and multi-resolution capabilities, it is essentially an adjustable window Fourier transform and still suffers from the limitations of the Fourier transform11.

The Hilbert–Huang transform (HHT) is used to further strengthen the local features of the data by solving the instantaneous frequency, and accurately establishing the correspondence between frequency and time, thereby removing the limitations imposed by the uncertainty principle12. Therefore, this method has been widely applied in current multi-scale time series research. For example, Luo, et al.13 used HHT to identify the multi-time scale characteristics of runoff and sediment yield and through describing the correlation of two variables at different scales, elucidated their inherent sediment yield mechanisms. In addition, in recent years, some studies have also attempted to introduce improved HHT into water quality analysis to enhance the identification accuracy of periodic characteristics. For instance, Xiao et al.14 utilized time-frequency analysis techniques to study the relationship between climate oscillation and phytoplankton dynamics in five North Temperate Lakes. The study also found that the two types of El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), El Nino, and La Nina, have different effects on chlorophyll concentrations.

However, most current research focuses on the practical application of HHT, lacking comparisons with traditional periodic identification methods, and reliable methods for analyzing the evolutionary characteristics of nutrients at regional scales.

Traditional linear correlation analyses might not fully capture the authentic relationships between variables. Consequently, the utilization of Time-dependent intrinsic correlation (TDIC) and the continuous wavelet transform based on the HHT has been implemented to provide valuable insights into local correlations and the dynamic relationship between nutrients and environmental factors over time. The complementary ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN), recognized as an enhancement of the empirical mode decomposition (EMD), has been designed to augment the accuracy and reliability of the decomposition process through the integration of controlled noise. This method has been demonstrated to exhibit greater adaptability and precision in comparison to standard wavelet decomposition and spectral analysis techniques. By employing these methodologies, a more precise identification and analysis of the periodic characteristics of nutrients and environmental factors in aquatic systems can be attained.

In addition, many processes in lake ecosystem, such as the cycle and transformation of nutrients, are dynamic and nonlinear. These methods can better capture the dynamic changes of these processes. For example, CEEMDAN can reveal the periodic characteristics of nutrient concentration changes over time, which is crucial for understanding the interactions between nutrients and the environment in lake ecosystems.

Hongfeng Lake is the most important source of drinking water for Guiyang and its neighboring areas15. Pollutants from the surrounding area have contributed to substantial eutrophication and an increase in the emergence of algal blooms in the water body over the last two decades16. According to the current study, the N and P nutrients in Hongfeng Lake are mostly the result of contamination from outside sources17. The discharge of high-N, high-P wastewater has resulted in significant nutrient accumulation in both the water and the sediment. Various water pollution management and ecological restoration techniques have been adopted in the Hongfeng Lake basin in recent years, gradually limiting external contamination18. However, research on the relationship between internal environmental parameters and N and P dynamics inside the lake has been rather restricted.

The Hongfeng Lake’s environmental factors and N and P were evaluated using time series data from 2020 to 2023 in this study. The following are the goals of this paper: (1) By conducting dimensionality reduction analysis on environmental factors and nutrient data, the interaction between environmental factors and nutrient factors in Hongfeng Lake was studied using time-frequency analysis tools. (2) Identify the patterns of periodicity and synchronization between environmental variables and nutrients at various time intervals, as well as how these patterns respond to external environmental changes.

Materials and methods

Study area

Hongfeng Lake is a deep valley-type lake on the karst plateau, located in Qingzhen City, Pingba County, and Xixiu District of Guizhou Province (106°19′—106°28′E, 26°26′—26°35′N). It was built in 1960 with a drainage area of 1,596 km2. The dam is 52.5 m high, has a maximum water depth of 45 m, and an average water depth of 10.51 m. The Hongfeng Lake basin belongs to the subtropical monsoon humid climate zone, with an average annual temperature of 14.4 °C, an average temperature of 4.1 °C in January, and an average temperature of 22.7 °C in July. The average annual precipitation ranges from 1,174.7 to 1,386.1 mm, with precipitation mainly concentrated in the summer months of June to August19.

Sample collection and measurement

Seven monitoring points were set up across Hongfeng Lake from north to south (Fig. 1). Sampling was conducted at monthly intervals for a continuous period of four years (January to December).

Region of the study and sampling sites. The map was produced using ArcMap version 10.2 (https://www.esri.com, retrieved on March 1, 2021).

The results of each sample’s analysis were performed in triplicate, and the mean was shown. The Goulden and Brooksbank20 methods were used to calculate the N and P concentration. For the Total phosphorus (TP) and total nitrogen (TN) analyses, a continuous flow analyzer (Flowsys III, Systea Company, Italy) was then used to determine the concentration. The same method was used to determine ammonia nitrogen (NH + 4-N) and soluble active phosphate (PO3- 4-P), except the water sample was first filtered through a 0.45 μm cellulose acetate membrane and not autoclaved. All materials used for these analyses were purchased from Shanghai N&D Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

To measure environmental factors, two liters of water were collected. A COD III (Hach, USA) was used to measure chemical oxygen demand (CODCr) and chemical oxygen demand utilizing manganese (CODMn). A PHSJ-4 A (Lei-ci, Shanghai, China) and a YSI 5750 (USA) were used to assess temperature, pH, and DO concentration before sampling at each site. The transparency of water bodies was measured using a Secchi disk.

Statistics analysis

Hilbert–Huang transform method

The basic idea for decomposing complex raw data into a finite mix of intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) and a residual term is EMD. The monthly time series of nutrients and environmental characteristics for the Hongfeng Lake were examined using the CEEMDAN-HT approach. The HHT technique is comprised of two processes: the EMD, which divides the original time series into several unique time series known as IMFs, and the HT, which creates the Hilbert spectrum and marginal spectrum21.

After CEEMDAN decomposition, a collection of IMFs at various scales may be derived. These functions have high adaptive properties and may represent the key elements of the signal at multiple time scales and frequencies22. CEEMDAN is more adaptable and accurate than standard approaches such as wavelet decomposition and spectral decomposition.

The HT serves as the core component of the HHT, to obtain the instantaneous frequency that fully reflects the time-frequency-energy distribution of n-dimensional vector data, ensuring that each decomposed IMF possesses a clear physical meaning21. Discussions

Periodicity and synchronization of the time series of EPC and NPC in the Hongfeng Lake

After dimensionality is reduced, the examination of nutrients and environmental factors shows comparable patterns of variation and fluctuations, suggesting a complex interaction between these variables. A cubic curve equation was employed to elucidate the functional relationship between the EPC and the NPC. This methodology enabled the modeling of the interaction between these components in a non-linear fashion, thereby capturing the subtleties of their relationship. Furthermore, linear regression was utilized to establish a fitting equation between the IMF1-4 that were derived from the CEEMDAN decomposition applied to both EPC and NPC. The application of the HHT in this context has facilitated a robust analysis of the time-varying interactions between environmental factors and nutrients, thereby enhancing the understanding of their dynamic relationship.

Given the nuanced relationship between EPC and NPC, time-frequency analysis was further employed to explore their periodicity and synchronization. The analysis has demonstrated that a synchronized response of EPC and NPC to strong periodic bands is evident, indicative of a coherent interaction between environmental factors and nutrients. This finding is in alignment with previous studies that have identified significant relationships between these variables, but it extends the understanding by highlighting specific periodic behaviors and synchronization patterns23. The results have underscored the importance of considering time-varying dynamics in the assessment of eutrophication and the formulation of environmental management strategies.

The analysis of the dimensionality-reduced environmental factor and nutrients indicates that they exhibit similar patterns of variation and fluctuations. Given the intricate relationship between EPC and NPC, we used time-frequency analysis to analyze their periodicity and synchronization. The results showed that EPC and NPC responded in sync to strong periodic bands at the same time. While prior studies found significant relationships between nutrients and environmental variables, our findings revealed specific periodicity and synchronization for each of them26.

The Hilbert–Huang spectrum analysis indicated similarities in EPC and NPC alterations. All time series indicated persistent and high-energy swings in the low-frequency domain, which followed a similar pattern (Fig. 5). The varied amplitudes in the Hilbert–Huang spectra of IMF2 indicate that the EPC alone cannot adequately explain the nutritional changes in Hongfeng Lake. This suggests that nutrient changes are a complex process influenced not just by environmental variables but also by human activity, industrial and agricultural pollution, and other factors27.

The HHT model can effectively be used for the quantitative assessment of the relationship between environmental factors and nutrients using time series data. However, these models are only based on the interaction between environmental factors and nutrients, without considering changes in population, land use, and environmental management, which could significantly impact water quality28. When conditions change such that the time series of environmental factors and nutrients no longer exhibit generalized stationarity, a new transformation model based on a new dataset may be necessary.

The Hongfeng Lake exhibited particularly high fluctuations (2–4 cycle/month)in the high-frequency region of EPC and NPC(Fig. 5). This is related to the impact of environmental factor fluctuations (such as global warming, extreme weather, heavy rainfall, etc.) and human activities29. For example, with increasing temperatures, the most intense warming (a temperature rise of about 5 to 7 °C) mainly occurs in the summer and autumn seasons in mid-latitudes. This may imply that extreme heat events may have a strong impact on mid-latitude regions (such as China, Southern Europe, and the United States) during specific seasons30,31. For example, the increase in temperature favors the growth of cyanobacteria in water. In addition to directly affecting the growth rate of cyanobacteria, it further influences the cycling of substances such as N and P in the water, affecting the changes in nutrients in the water32,33.

This means that rising temperatures often accelerate the eutrophication process in water bodies by changing water temperature (even when external nutrient sources have stabilized). At the same time, higher temperatures promote algal reproduction and increase nutrient consumption34. Therefore, the impact of warming on P or N can be balanced between increased algal consumption and accelerated P or N cycling. Rising temperatures promote the internal release of P, which plays a dominant role in algal consumption35. As a result, the N and P concentration in reservoir water increases periodically with rising temperatures36. It is important to note that, while elevated temperatures are often associated with accelerating the eutrophication process through changes in water temperature, the observed impact on eutrophication cannot solely be attributed to temperature alone, given that the utilized EPC encompasses multiple environmental variables. Rather, it is plausible that the influence stems from a complex interplay among diverse factors, including increased algal reproduction, accelerated nutrient cycling, internal nutrient release, rainfall patterns, and human activities, all implicitly considered within the EPC analysis37.

Rainfall dilutes nutrient concentrations in lakes, while heavy rainfall increases soil erosion and nutrient transport, leading to increased non-point source pollution38,39. When precipitation increases, water volume and N and P losses due to soil erosion also increase accordingly40. The N and P content in Lake Hongfeng will also increase, especially in summer41. Additionally, due to human activities such as planting, fertilization, and unforeseen pollution events from industrial sources, the overall pollution of water bodies will also increase42,43.

The study’s findings, based on time series analysis, show a substantial relationship between the NPC and the EPC. This link demonstrates the close relationship between nutrient dynamics and environmental circumstances.

Due to the non-stationarity and transient nature of these time series, the HHT technique provides excellent insights into the connection between environmental impacts and nutrient concentration changes44. Our research findings reveal statistically significant relationships between nutrients and environmental conditions, which occur only transiently during certain periods. This finding is consistent with previous studies on Hongfeng Lake, which reported fluctuations in certain periodic patterns45,46. The observed periodicity and synchronicity of nutrients and environmental parameters will provide a solid theoretical basis for the application of complex time-frequency methods in predicting and warning about eutrophication in lakes. These methods have already been successfully applied in lakes and rivers worldwide. This study contributes to a deeper understanding of periodicity and synchronization in time series, as well as the interactions between environmental factors and nutrient levels. It reveals the complex dynamic relationship between environmental factors and nutrient levels, including periodic changes and correlations at specific time intervals and scales.

While utilizing time-frequency analytic techniques to explore the unpredictability and intermittent behavior of nutrients in river ecosystems has numerous benefits, it also has certain limitations. Such investigations rely on the availability of high-quality, durable, and large datasets. The integrity of the analysis is potentially affected in cases when data are absent, sparse, or tainted with noise.

Given the complexity and dynamics of environmental systems, it is imperative that our models continuously evolve and improve with the accumulation of new data. As such, in future research endeavors, ongoing attention will be devoted to monitoring the environmental changes in lakes such as Hongfeng and its counterparts, with a focus on gathering a more comprehensive and diverse range of data. This endeavor aims to establish models that are both more accurate and robust, tailored to accommodate the evolving nature of these systems.

The correlation of nutrients and environmental factors

Numerous environmental factors influence the release of N and P from lake sediment, with key factors including temperature, pH, DO, oxidation-reduction potential, biological activity, and disturbances. Therefore, studying the relationship between environmental conditions and nutrient levels in water is crucial47. However, due to a lack of studies, our understanding of this connection has been limited. Although modeling methods have provided in-depth insights into the impact of environmental changes on nutrient levels in shallow lakes, there is still a lack of experimental research48.

This study analyzed the relationship between nutrients and environmental factors based on time series. We calculated the correlation between the NPC and EPC to quantify the relationship between nutrients and environmental factors. However, the correlation only indicates a linear relationship between the two time series, which is not sufficient to detect the true relationship between two nonlinear and non-stationary time series. Therefore, it is necessary to consider the probability of similar reversals between the two sequences at different time scales. This means that although these two sequences may have a positive correlation at one time scale, they may have a negative correlation at another time scale49. It should also be emphasized that cross-correlation analysis considers the entire time range.

It is crucial to identify periods and periodic time scales where such anomalous negative or positive correlations exist. Information about these local relationships and correlation strengths can be obtained using running correlation analysis with a sliding window. One such method is the TDIC analysis based on HHT, which allows multi-scale running correlation analysis and provides insights into local correlations. Therefore, considering the comparable periodicities of nutrients and major components, a TDIC analysis was conducted on them50.

Pearson correlation analysis shows a significant positive correlation between EPC and NPC. However, in the TDIC analysis, EPC and NPC showed a positive correlation in different time windows before 2022 and a negative correlation after 2023. The positive correlation between EPC and NPC indicates an increasing trend in nutrient levels with worsening water pollution. The negative correlation between EPC and NPC suggests that the changes in nutrient levels are influenced by other external factors, such as water diversion, flood discharge, and heavy rainfall51. The negative correlation between EPC and NPC is more pronounced in the IMF1 component, with no significant expression in the remaining components. Therefore, it can be considered that the IMF components mainly reflect external factor information.

The TDIC analysis indicates that the relationship between nutrients and environmental factors evolves and exhibits regular changes, such as correlations. Analysis of the interaction between nutrients and environmental factors within a multi-scale framework suggests that strong correlations between the two can occur at specific time intervals and scales, while opposite behavior can occur at other time intervals and ranges. Although the current study does not provide a clear explanation for these shifts in correlation, they may be attributed to uncertain physical processes, spatial heterogeneity, or the influence of different climate forcings on local hydrological processes52.

From Fig. 6, it is evident that the EPC signal lags behind the NPC, and similar behavior is observed in each IMF component, particularly at around a 12-month cycle, indicating that environmental factor changes lag behind nutrient levels. Long and Qian45 studied Hongfeng Lake and demonstrated that lake water TP and TN exhibit a significant lag in response to sudden events such as rainfall and human activities. This result can be understood as the interference of rainfall and human activities needing some time to affect N and P in the water. That is, the impact of environmental changes on biological communities has a certain time delay53. For example, rainfall events may increase surface runoff over a period of time, thereby flushing more nutrients into the lake, but this impact may take some time to be reflected in the lake’s nutrient levels. In addition, human activities such as agricultural fertilization may not immediately reflect in the lake’s nutrient status, but there is a certain lag time54.

Environmental factors are not static but are subject to change as a direct consequence of water contamination. These changes not only confirm the profound impact of water pollution on the intrinsic mechanisms of ecosystems but also emphasize the necessity of considering the complex interactions between these environmental factors and nutrients when devising pollution mitigation measures. Specifically, through advanced time-frequency analysis techniques, it has been observed that changes in environmental factors exhibit a certain degree of lag following the occurrence of water pollution events, particularly within cycles of approximately 12 months. This lag may be associated with the accumulation of pollutants in the aquatic system and the subsequent ecological response. This finding is of significant importance for understanding and predicting the trends of lake eutrophication and provides a scientific basis for the development of effective environmental management strategies.

Conclusions

The relationship between nutrients and environmental factors is dynamic and exhibits regular changes, including correlations, at specific time intervals and scales. This suggests that strong correlations between the two can occur at certain periods, while opposite behavior can occur at other time intervals and ranges. These shifts in correlation may be attributed to uncertain physical processes, spatial heterogeneity, or the influence of different climate forcings on local hydrological processes.

Wavelet analysis and cross-wavelet analysis provide insights into the phase relationship and time series lag between environmental factors and nutrient levels. The results indicate that environmental factor changes lag behind nutrient levels, particularly at around a 12-month cycle. This suggests that environmental factors change under the influence of water pollution after the water body has been polluted, aligning with natural patterns.

The study emphasizes the need to consider the probability of similar reversals between two sequences at different time scales. It also highlights the importance of identifying periods and periodic time scales where anomalous negative or positive correlations exist. This information can be obtained using running correlation analysis with a sliding window, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of the relationship between nutrients and environmental factors.

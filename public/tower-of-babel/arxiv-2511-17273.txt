Investigation of the Spectator Effect on Light Nuclei Production in Nucleus-Nucleus Collisions at High Baryon Density Region Journal: Physics Letters B
Li’Ang Zhang, Hongcan Li, Junyi Han, Yaping Wang, Junlin Wu, Guannan Xie, Gao-Chan Yong

Abstract
The light nuclei yields and yield ratios, as sensitive probes of the QCD phase transition, have been precisely measured at various collision energies. However, due to limited detector acceptance, the full p T p_{\rm T} integral yield often requires extrapolation of the measured transverse momentum spectra to the unmeasured low- p T p_{\rm T} region via functional fitting. Simulations with the AMPT-HC transport model and an after-burner coalescence approach indicate a significant low- p T p_{\rm T} enhancement in peripheral collisions or forward rapidities, primarily originating from spectator nucleons. Consequently, conventional extrapolations tend to underestimate the true light nuclei yields.



1
Introduction

One of the major goals of heavy-ion collision experiments is to study the Quantum ChromoDynamics (QCD) phase diagram
[
17
,
18
]
, which is often represented in terms of temperature vs. baryonic chemical potential.
Studies based on lattice QCD calculations
[
10
]
and various theoretical models
[
5
,
23
,
13
]
indicate that the phase transition from Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP) to hadronic matter is a smooth crossover at very small baryon chemical potential (
\mu_{\rm B}
), while at larger
\mu_{\rm B}
, it is likely to be a first-order phase transition.
In low-energy collisions where the net-baryon density is large, a first-order phase transition between the QGP and hadronic matter is expected.
The production of light nuclei is an important observable in heavy-ion collision experiments, especially the double yield ratio (N
p

\times
N
t
/ N
2
d
), which is considered a probe sensitive to the QCD phase transition
[
20
]
.
The production mechanism of light nuclei in heavy-ion collisions is commonly described by the thermal model and the coalescence model
[
22
]
.
According to the thermal model, the yield of light nuclei is predicted to increase gradually as the collision energy decreases from the RHIC top energy to the fixed-target energy region, which can be well constrained by precise experimental measurements in the high baryon density region
[
9
]
.
Recently, the RHIC/STAR collaboration published measurements of the yield and correlation of light nuclei at
\sqrt{s_{\rm{NN}}}
= 3 GeV, which provide valuable insights into the production dynamics of light nuclei and the understanding of the QCD phase structure at high baryon denisty
[
21
,
2
]
.

The heavy-ion collisions are typically described by a two-component model, consisting of wounded nucleons (participants) and non-wounded nucleons (spectators).
In the high energy approximation, such as LHC and RHIC top energies, the initial transverse momentum of nucleon (
\sim
0.25 GeV at center of the atomic nucleus) from the motion of nucleon within nucleus and the potential field between nucleons is so small compared with beam momentum of nucleons that it can be neglected.
Therefore, the participants and spectators can be identified based on relative geometric position of nucleon-nucleon pairs from projectile and target nuclei.
This method, known as the Glauber model
[
16
]
, is commonly used to estimate the number of participating nucleons N
Part
and the number of nucleon-nucleon collisions N
Coll
in heavy-ion collisions. It is widely used in transport models to describe initial collision conditions, such as HIJING
[
12
]
, UrQMD
[
6
]
, AMPT
[
15
]
, etc.
However, when the collision energy goes down to a few GeV, the motion of nucleons within nucleus and the field interactions between nucleons become significant, which can not be neglected in simulations for the heavy-ion collisions in the high baryon density region.
In addition, the partonic interaction will be weakened or even disappear, therefore, the evolution of high-density nuclear matter produced through heavy-ion collision may be dominated by hadronic interactions.
Recently, a new version of the extended a multi-phase transport model (AMPT-HC) has been applied to study hadron cascade with hadronic mean-field potentials for heavy-ion collisions in the high baryon density region
[
24
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the spectator effect in light nuclei production in Au+Au collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm{NN}}}
= 3 GeV by the AMPT-HC model with an after-burner coalescence mechanism and compare the results with the STAR measurements.
Our studies show that there are significant light nuclei produced from the spectator coalescing at low
p_{\rm T}
region in peripheral Au + Au collisions, and the spectator effect becomes more important from middle to backward rapidity region.
Moreover, the integral light nuclei yields are obviously underestimated due to the extrapolation in the unmeasured detector acceptance range.

2
Brief introduction of the model

2.1
Transport model

The AMPT model is widely used to study heavy-ion collisions at high energies, such as LHC and RHIC top energies.
It includes simulation of partonic and hadronic phase which consist of four main components: parton production from nucleons by HIJING, partonic interaction and transport by ZPC
[
25
]
, hadronization by parton fragmentation or coalescence process, and hadronic interaction by ART
[
14
]
.

The AMPT-HC model, a pure hadron cascade version, is designed to describe hadronic interaction in nucleus-nucleus collisions at center-of-mass energy of a few GeV.
In this model, parton production, interaction and hadronization processes are switched off.
The Woods-Saxon nucleon density distribution Eq.(
1
) is used to initialize the position of each nucleon within the nuclei.

\displaystyle\rho(r)=\frac{\rho_{0}}{1+e^{-\frac{r-R}{d}}}

(1)

Where
R
is the half-density radius and
d
is the diffuseness parameter, while
\rho_{0}
denotes the normal nuclear density in Eq.(
1
).
And local Thomas-Fermi approximation Eq.(
2
) is used to initialize the momentum.

\displaystyle p_{\rm F}(\rho)=0.197\times\left(\frac{3\pi^{2}\rho}{2}\right)^{1/3}

(2)

Thomas-Fermi approximation depends on nuclear matter density, the coefficient 0.197 is the unit conversion between
\rm fm^{-1}
and GeV.
For example, at the center of an Au atomic nucleus, the density is about
\rho\sim 0.168~\rm fm^{-3}
, corresponding to a Fermi momentum of about
p_{\rm F}\sim 0.267
GeV.
Then these nucleons undergo transport simulations by Hamiltonian canonical equations Eq.(
3
) in the fields that they generate, such as electromagnetic field, Kaon potential field
[
8
]
, baryon mean-field
[
11
]
and others.

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\vec{p}}

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\vec{r}}

(3)

The baryon mean-field Eq.(
4
) depend on nuclear matter density,

\displaystyle U(\rho)=\alpha\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}+\beta\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\alpha=\left(-29.81-46.9\frac{k_{0}+44.73}{k_{0}-166.32}\right)~\rm MeV

\displaystyle\beta=23.45\frac{k_{0}+255.78}{k_{0}-166.32}~\rm MeV

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{k_{0}+44.73}{211.05}

(4)

k_{0}
is called incompressibility which used to control stiffness of the baryon mean-field (
k_{0}
is set to 380 MeV in this work).
These particles, including mesons (
\pi
,
\rho
,
\omega
,
\eta
,
\rm{K}
,
\rm{K}^{*}
,
\phi
), baryons (
\rm{N}
,
\Delta
,
\rm{N}^{*}(1440)
,
\rm{N}^{*}(1535)
,
\Lambda
,
\Sigma
,
\Xi
,
\Omega
) and their anti-particles, can be produced or absorbed through inelastic collision channels during the transport process.
In addition, the elastic collisions also occur between these particles, and the differential elastic scattering cross-section is described as below Eq.(
5
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dt}=a\times e^{bt},~~t=-2p^{2}(1-\rm{cos}~\theta),

(5)

where
p
and
\theta
represent momentum and scattering angle in center-of-momentum frame respectively.
a
and
b
depend on the collision energy and are taken from Ref.
[
7
]
.

Because the Fermi motion of nucleons and the field interactions between nucleons are taken into account, the method based on geometric positions of initial nucleons to distinguish between participants and spectators is no longer applicable.
By tracking initial and final state of each nucleon-nucleon collision in whole evolution of nucleus-nucleus collision, the nucleons that have undergone inelastic process will be recorded as participants, which is consistent with Glauber model and HIJING model
[
16
,
12
]
, while the nucleons that have undergone elastic process or those that do not participate in collisions at all will be recorded as spectators.

2.2
Coalescence model

Coalescence is commonly regarded as one of the primary mechanisms for the formation of light nuclei in heavy-ion collisions, and this process is generally performed after kinetic freeze-out.
When the transport evolution is completed, due to the interaction between nucleons, adjacent nucleons in the phase space can form light nuclei by coalescence mechanisms.
Therefore, in the coalescence model, the relative position
{\rm\Delta}r
and relative momentum
{\rm\Delta}p
of a nucleon-nucleon pair are usually used to determine whether coalescence occurs or not
[
22
,
3
,
4
]
.
The deuteron was formed using a two-body coalescence model, while the triton was formed by employing two successive two-body coalescence processes.
Considering that nucleons exhibit strong repulsive interactions at short distances (
\lesssim
1 fm), here we have chosen an elliptic window in the phase space to determine whether to form a nucleus,

\displaystyle(\Delta r-r_{low})^{2}/r_{high}^{2}+(\Delta p)^{2}/p_{high}^{2}<1,~\Delta r>r_{low}

(6)

Where the
r_{low}
is the lower limit of
\Delta r
. The
r_{high}
and
p_{high}
are the long axis and short axis of the ellipse, respectively. They also denote the largest threshold that allows coalescence to occur.

In order to determine the values of
r_{low}
,
r_{high}
and
p_{high}
that have the best descriptions for the light nuclei yield, we carry out the scan of these three parameters for each light nuclei species. The results of model calculations are obtained using the coalescence parameters for deuteron (triton)
r_{high}
= 5 (2) fm ,
p_{high}
= 0.3 (0.7) GeV/
c
and the
r_{low}
= 0.7 (0.7) fm, which are qualitatively consistent with the STAR data in each centrality bin.

3
Results and discussion

In this work, we use the AMPT-HC model with an after-burner coalescence model to simulate protons, deuterons and tritons production in Au + Au collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}
= 3 GeV.

Therefore, in peripheral collisions or backward rapidity regions, the fraction of spectator component will be larger, which causes the shape of the

4
Conclusions

In summary, the AMPT-HC model with an after-burner coalescence successfully reproduces the main trends of light nuclei production at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}
= 3 GeV.
The spectator component contributes significantly to the low-
p_{\rm T}
region, particularly in peripheral collisions and at forward rapidities, leading to a potential underestimation of yields in experimental extrapolations.
Incorporating the spectator effect is therefore essential for precise yield determination in the high-baryon-density regime.

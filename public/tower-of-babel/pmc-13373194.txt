Travel recommendation via diffusion-guided multiplex graph contrastive learning
Authors: Chaoyue Yu, Chaobo Zhang, Long Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Travel recommendation (TR) demonstrates immense commercial potential in global economic development. However, previous research has relied solely on single-form interaction attributes between users and travel products, overlooking the impact of different interaction attributes on the final travel product and the distinctions among these attributes. Additionally, travel data inherently suffers from data sparsity. To bridge this gap, this paper proposes a novel diffusion-guided multiplex graph contrastive learning (CL) for TR framework, namely DMGCLTR. It leverages an enhanced Diffusion Model (DM) to capture global interaction patterns across diverse attributes between users and travel products. Through a designed attribute-chain representation learner and perceptual encoder, it enables granular discovery of how different interaction attributes influence and depend upon the final selected travel product. Furthermore, we adopt a contrastive learning approach to jointly optimize these tasks and enhance its symmetry. Extensive experiments across three real-world datasets demonstrate DMGCLTR’s superior performance. On average, it outperforms the best baseline by 5.20% in HR@5 and 7.01% in MRR across all datasets. This enhanced TR method offers a novel perspective for innovation and integrated development in the smart tourism industry.

Introduction

As the tourism industry continues to grow globally, a pressing challenge remains: how to effectively recommend suitable travel destinations to users in numerous online travel agencies (OTAs) around the world, thus maximizing the quality of travel platforms and enriching the travel experience1,2.

Travel recommendation (TR) has ascended as a pivotal research frontier, garnering substantial academic interest in recent years. In contrast to conventional domains such as catering or cinema, TR is characterized by pronounced data sparsity and a high prevalence of cold-start users3,4, which pose formidable obstacles to effective recommendation. To address these issues, various methodologies have been proposed. For instance, Tao et al.5 leveraged spatio-temporal dynamics inherent in travel products, while Liu et al.6 utilized multi-behavioral data to predict potential destinations. Additionally, Chen et al.7 employed keyword augmentation techniques to capture nuanced traveler preferences. Nevertheless, existing TR models frequently overlook the intricate influence of diverse interaction attributes (e.g., user IP address, departure, destination, and pricing) on the terminal selection of travel products. Related work

Diffusion model

Diffusion models (DMs) have gained widespread adoption across various fields such as computer vision and natural language processing due to their exceptional generative capabilities and representational learning power. For instance, DiffuRec8 leverages DMs for sequence recommendation, capitalizing on their unique strengths in representation generation. Recdiff9 utilizes DMs for social recommendation. Diffmm10 attempts to apply DM to multimodal recommendation. DimeRec11 proposes a DM-based generative paradigm for sequential recommendation enhancement. Xuan et al.12 applied DM to cross-domain recommendation. DiffSBR13 designed a cluster-aware DM model for conversational recommendation. DiFashion14 pre-trained DM on images for apparel recommendation. TCM-KGDR15 employs deep learning and reinforcement learning techniques to recommend herbal prescriptions, providing decision support for clinical practitioners of traditional Chinese medicine. Inspired by these studies, we pioneer the application of an enhanced DM to travel recommendation to capture the importance of different attributes between users and travel products, along with their collaborative signals.

Travel recommendations and recommendation systems

With the widespread attention on artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies in the recommendation domain, many researchers have attempted to apply recommendation systems to downstream tasks in tourism recommendation, aiming to advance the development of smart tourism. MV-GAN16 designed a multi-view graph attention network for tourism recommendation. GHGCL17 proposed a heterogeneous graph contrastive learning network based on meta-path views for learning representations of users and tourism products. You et al.18 extracted and analyzed spatio-temporal trajectory data generated by tourism users in daily life to recommend Points of Interest (POIs) for their travel. Gan et al.19 introduced an innovative POI network that captures user preferences by creating distinct hyperspherical interest models for each user. STR-VGAE20 proposed a conversation-based travel recommendation model designed to perform both travel package recommendation and variational graph autoencoding tasks. TMS-Net21 leverages self-attention mechanisms to fuse location information for personalized travel recommendations.

Nevertheless, the aforementioned methodologies predominantly investigate the relationship between isolated interaction attributes and travel products. They tend to overlook the heterogeneous interplay among diverse interaction attributes associated with a user’s ultimate selection, as well as the varying degrees of nuanced influence that distinct attributes exert on the final decision-making process.

The overview of DMGCLTR

The DMGCLTR framework is illustrated in Fig. 2, comprising three main modules: (1) In the DGPT module, we pre-train two modalities–the diverse interaction attributes between users and travel products, and the keywords users search–using DM, aiming to learn travel product representation embeddings while mitigating the sparsity of travel data. (2) In the ACAR module, we design attribute chain-based representation learning and attribute chain-based contrastive learning. Specifically, this module leverages the aggregation of embeddings for multiple interaction attributes and attribute chain representation learning to capture the fine-grained influence of different attributes on the final travel product. (3) The DM-based and attribute chain-based contrastive learning designed in the CLET module enables learning the correlations and dependencies between attributes from both global and local perspectives, thereby further enhancing the impact of interaction attributes on the final travel product. This effectively addresses the two limitations identified. In summary, our rationale for the design of each module is as follows. For DGPT. This module is designed to tackle Challenge 3: Data Sparsity. Conditional generation evaluation block

To capture global information across the fusion of different multidimensional attributes interacting with travel product representations and to maximize modeling of users’ personalized search queries, we selected the Transformer22 with self-attention mechanisms as the generator for our conditional generation evaluation block. This choice enables selective attention to different parts of the input data, allowing the model to capture complex dependencies and relationships across various modalities. Specifically, we aggregate the predicted travel product representations with the user-input search keyword representations using the Transformer. Concurrently, we incorporate a diffusion step to jointly model the representation vectors with the aforementioned process. Following these operations, our conditional generation evaluation process can progressively refine the estimation of the pair.

Data augmentation for multi-task contrastive learning

In TR tasks, different users exhibit distinct interaction preferences toward selected travel products. Within multi-modal heterogeneous graphs, varying interaction attributes generate distinct collaborative patterns between users and their ultimately chosen travel products. Therefore, effectively modeling the dependencies and correlations among different attribute relationships within collaborative patterns is crucial.

Prior to this, we utilized the DM model to globally consider the relationships between different attributes of users and travel products, as well as the impact of users’ search keywords on these relationships. Equally important, however, is examining the user-travel product interactions embedded within different attribute interaction patterns. Therefore, we first established a contrastive learning module based on distinct interaction attributes to differentiate their relative importance. Specifically, we defined interactions involving the same user across different attributes as positive samples, while treating interactions across different users as negative samples. For user-travel product interactions, establishing a contrastive learning module helps capture attribute importance (e.g., destination attributes being more significant than IP address attributes for final travel product selection). Consequently, we introduce an attribute chain-based contrastive learning augmentation framework to integrate explicit weight functions from multiple contrastive losses across different interaction attributes between users and travel products.

To ensure a fair and rigorous comparison, all methodologies were evaluated under identical hardware configurations. For the baseline models, we utilized the official source code provided by the respective authors and strictly adhered to their recommended parameter configurations. Each experiment was executed ten times, and the reported results represent the mean performance across these runs.

RQ2: Ablation studies

To address RQ2, we conducted a comprehensive ablation study to isolate and evaluate the individual contributions of the key modules within the DMGCLTR framework. Specifically, we designed the following five variants:w/o DPM: Removes the diffusion model from the DGPT module, replacing it with a standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE).w/o DEM: Removes the conditional evaluation block, substituting it with a basic Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).w/o ACR: Eliminates the attribute chain-aware representation learning module.w/o ACL: Discards the attribute chain-enhanced CL mechanism.w/o ACE: Removes the attribute chain-based encoder (ensuring terminology consistency with “attribute chain”).The results of the ablation study are illustrated in Fig. 3. Discussion and implications

In this work, extensive experimental evaluations conducted in Section 4 demonstrate that DMGCLTR effectively addresses three fundamental challenges in TR through the synergistic joint optimization of an enhanced DM, an attribute chain-aware representation learner, and attribute chain-enhanced contrastive learning. These challenges are: 1) the semantic relations and modality differences among diverse interaction attributes; 2) the asymmetric mutual influence among these attributes; and 3) the inherent and severe data sparsity endemic to travel data. Furthermore, we analyzed diverse TR instances to qualitatively interpret and validate the model’s decision-making process. Our findings demonstrate that the DMGCLTR framework not only achieves superior recommendation performance but also provides unique, actionable insights for OTAs and the broader smart tourism industry.

From a theoretical perspective, this work advances the travel recommendation literature along three distinct dimensions. First, it pioneers the application of enhanced diffusion models to multi-attribute travel recommendation, demonstrating that the forward-reverse denoising paradigm can effectively reconstruct latent collaborative signals from extremely sparse interaction matrices–a capability beyond the reach of conventional graph-based methods. Second, it introduces the concept of the attribute chain as a structural prior, formalizing the asymmetric, sequential dependencies inherent in traveler decision-making (e.g., departure constrains destination, which in turn influences price expectations). This departs fundamentally from traditional multi-behavior GNNs that treat interaction types as symmetric and parallel. Third, it establishes a tripartite synergistic optimization framework, proving that the joint learning of global diffusion signals, local chain-based dependencies, and contrastive multi-view alignment yields emergent representational power that no isolated module can achieve. Collectively, these theoretical contributions provide a new lens for modeling complex, multi-faceted user preferences in data-sparse recommendation scenarios.

From a practical standpoint, our comprehensive empirical evaluation–spanning three established travel and recommendation datasets plus an independent large-scale benchmark–demonstrates consistent improvements of 3.84% in Recall@5 and 6.05% in NDCG@5 over the strongest baseline. The inclusion of additional evaluation metrics further corroborates the robustness of our approach across diverse performance dimensions. Ablation studies rigorously validate that the synergistic integration of DGPT, ACAR, and CLET is essential to the observed gains; removing any single module results in statistically significant performance degradation. Notably, the model’s strong performance on the IntTravel dataset, sourced from a fundamentally different platform and geographic distribution, confirms its cross-dataset generalizability and practical deployability. By explicitly modeling the connections and directional dependencies among interaction attributes–an aspect largely overlooked by prior TR approaches constrained by conventional recommendation system paradigms–DMGCLTR offers a principled and effective solution for enhancing user experience and operational efficiency in real-world OTA platforms. This methodology holds substantial promise for driving innovation and integrated development within the smart tourism industry.

Conclusions

In this study, we propose a novel DMGCLTR approach that, on one hand, utilizes an improved DM model to train on diverse interaction attributes between users and their ultimately selected travel products. On the other hand, it employs a designed attribute chain representation learner and an attribute chain-enhanced contrastive learning method to model semantic collaboration signals between users and travel products at both global and granular dimensions. This constitutes one of the most significant innovations of our model. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superior performance of DMGCLTR. Furthermore, we provide interpretable explanations for instances across two categories of TR to validate model performance and enhance transparency.

Limitations and future work

In this section, we will explore the limitations of current DMGCLTR approaches and highlight potential directions for future research, aiming to foster integrated development in the TR domain through innovation.Geographic awareness issue. In TR tasks, the absence of historical trajectory information poses a significant challenge, as geographic context plays a crucial role in both immediate and long-term planning. Each user possesses unique characteristics that necessitate tailored travel plans. Current research largely overlooks this critical factor, underscoring the necessity for further consideration in future work. We plan to adopt graph attention networks combined with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to model users’ historical behavior sequences and employ clustering strategies for training.User personalization attributes. In TR tasks, user personalization attributes are an extremely important factor. For example, individuals of different ages may exhibit varying preferences for travel products. Younger individuals often seek adventure, potentially traveling to sports destinations like Queenstown, New Zealand, while older adults typically prefer comfortable European cultural tours. Factors such as user personal attributes (age, gender, occupation, etc.) have not yet been considered. We plan to utilize prompting and resampling techniques through DM integration for embedded learning to capture the relationship between users’ multi-attributes and their ultimately selected travel products.

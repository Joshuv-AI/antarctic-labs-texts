Twice Fourier transformable measures and diffraction theory
Hans G. Feichtinger, Christoph Richard, Christoph Schumacher, Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. Mathematical diffraction theory has been developed since about 1995. Hof’s initial approach relied on tempered distributions in euclidean space. Nowadays often the Fourier theory by Argabright and Gil de Lamadrid is used, which applies to appropriate measures on locally compact abelian groups. We review diffraction theory using Wiener amalgams as test function spaces. For translation bounded measures, this unifies and simplifies the former two approaches. We treat weighted versions of Meyer’s model sets as examples.



1.
Introduction

Mathematical diffraction theory has emerged from the discovery of quasicrystals, in order to describe their unusual diffraction properties. From a mathematical point of view, it is concerned with Fourier analysis of translation bounded measures, which serve as an idealisation of a piece of matter.
Hof’s influential approach
[
30
]
used tempered distributions in euclidean space. An earlier approach by de Bruijn relied on Gelfand-Shilov test functions, a certain subclass of Schwartz functions
[
8
,
37
]
.

But yet another framework is prominently being used, namely the Fourier theory of measures by Argabright and Gil de Lamadrid, called AG theory in the sequel. It extends the Fourier theory of positive definite measures based on Bochner’s theorem
[
6
]
and has been developed in the 1970ies
[
1
]
. The AG theory proved useful for diffraction analysis for three reasons. Firstly, any translation bounded measure indeed admits an autocorrelation measure and a diffraction measure as its Fourier transform
[
3
]
, while the corresponding problem for tempered distributions is open
[
70
]
. Secondly, the AG theory had already incorporated almost periodicity
[
2
,
28
]
. As almost periodicity had been recognised central for diffraction analysis
[
37
]
, the AG theory could readily be used to study the nature of the diffraction based on the autocorrelation measure
[
4
,
50
,
69
]
. Moreover, it recently appeared that important classes of pure point diffractive translation bounded measures are characterised by various types of almost periodicity
[
43
,
38
,
41
]
. Thirdly, the AG theory applies to measures on locally compact abelian groups beyond euclidean space, in a simpler way than Schwartz–Bruhat tempered distributions. Such generality was indeed needed, as models for quasicrystals had emerged that were constructed from non-euclidean internal spaces
[
4
]
. These can be analysed using the Poisson summation formula of the underlying non-euclidean lattice
[
59
]
.

However using this framework seemed subtle, as the Fourier transform is not a bijection on the space of AG transformable measures. This holds as the Fourier transform of a measure is required to be a measure in the AG theory, which constrains the transform to be translation bounded
[
1
, Thm. 2.5]
. Whereas the question of twice AG transformability was posed as an open problem
[
1
, p. 50]
, a simple answer was given already in 1979: A measure is twice AG transformable if and only if it is AG transformable and translation bounded
[
14
,
15
]
. This explains why AG transformable translation bounded measures constitute a natural space for diffraction analysis.

As the above characterisation remained neglected in diffraction analysis until recently
[
58
]
, it is the aim of this article to provide sufficient background. The above characterisation has been obtained within the framework of Wiener amalgam spaces
[
19
,
18
]
. Two important ingredients, which we will review below, are the identification of translation bounded measures as the bounded linear functionals on the Wiener algebra
[
45
]
, and Feichtinger’s mild distributions
[
17
,
33
,
23
]
. Mild distributions can be defined on locally compact abelian groups, where they constitute a well tractable substantial subclass of Schwartz–Bruhat tempered distributions.

As any AG transformable measure is a mild distribution, the AG theory is subsumed by mild distribution theory.
We will thus review the AG theory using mild distributions, thereby providing detailed proofs of statements in
[
14
,
15
]
. In particular, we will give a characterisation of AG transformability
that is considerably simpler than the original definition, and we will prove the above characterisation of twice AG transformability.

We will also rephrase diffraction theory of translation bounded measures using mild distributions. While this streamlines arguments in comparison to the traditional approaches
[
30
,
3
]
, this also indicates extensions beyond translation bounded measures. We will finally review weighted versions of Meyer’s model sets using mild distributions. This will rely on a Poisson summation formula for test functions including Feichtinger’s algebra, which extends
[
47
,
48
]
beyond euclidean space. We thus obtain diffraction results that improve on previous work, as summarised in
[
59
]
.

Altogether, we demonstrate that mild distributions provide a flexible instrument for diffraction analysis, which subsumes and simplifies the traditional approaches on translation bounded measures.

Our article is organized as follows. We start with a detailed discussion of bounded uniform partitions of unity in Sections
2
and
3
. We then carefully review the Wiener algebra in Section
4
and Feichtinger’s algebra in Section
5
, using bounded uniform partitions of unity. The space dual to Feichtinger’s algebra, the mild distributions, is discussed in Section
6
. Section
7
reviews translation bounded measures and mild measures. The central Section
8
reviews AG transformability using mild distributions.
Section
9
reviews diffraction theory from the mild distribution viewpoint, taking into account recent developments
[
43
]
on pure point diffraction. Section
10
applies mild distribution theory to weighted model sets. The main result of that section has been announced elsewhere
[
57
]
.

In order to motivate the notion of bounded uniform partition of unity, consider a tiling of

It would not be possible to describe the Wiener algebra using arbitrary partitions of unity. For example, properties (a) and (f) in Theorem
4.3
would then be violated. For a bounded uniform partition of unity
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
, we need to describe where the functions
\phi_{i}
are located. We use indexed families of points in
G
for that purpose. Let us stress here that we do not use point sets, as we want to allow for finitely many repetitions of points. Indeed, finitely many repetitions occur naturally when refining a given bounded uniform partition of unity, see Remark
2.9
. For an indexed point family
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
, we call the set
\supp(X)=\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
the
supporting set of
X
.
We then have
\supp(X)=\supp(\delta_{X})
for the support of the point measure

\delta_{X}=\sum_{i\in I}\delta_{x_{i}}{\@index{\@indexphrase{Dirac~comb}\@indexphrase{weighted~Dirac~comb}}}
. Using the multiplicity function
r_{X}:G\to\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
, defined by
r_{X}(x)=\card\{i\in I:x_{i}=x\}
, we can write
\delta_{X}=\sum_{x\in\supp(\delta_{X})}r_{X}(x)\delta_{x}
. Thus
\delta_{X}
is a weighted Dirac comb.

In order to describe properties of indexed point families, we extend some standard notions for sets

2.2.
definition and elementary properties

We now give the definition of bounded uniform partition of unity.

Definition 2.5

(BUPU)
.

A family
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
of functions in
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
is called a bounded uniform partition of unity (or simply BUPU) of norm
M
and size
U
with overlap constant
B
, if there exists an indexed point family
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, a relatively compact zero neighborhood
U\subseteq G
and finite constants
M
and
B
such that

(a)

\supp(\phi_{i})\subseteq x_{i}+U
for all
i\in I
,

(b)

\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B
for all
i\in I
,

(c)

\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(x)=1
for all
x\in G
,

(d)

\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq M
for all
i\in I
.

Remark 2.6
.

BUPUs often satisfy
\phi_{i}\geq 0
, but non-negativity is not required in the definition of a BUPU.
Condition (b) implies that the sum in (c) has, for fixed
x\in G
, at most
B
nonzero terms.
We show in Corollary
2.8
that (b) is equivalent to translation boundedness of the weighted Dirac comb
\delta_{X}
. In fact (b) is replaced in
[
19
]
by translation boundedness of
\delta_{X}
. Note that
\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}
provides a (not necessarily optimal) overlap constant by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
be a point family in
G
, and let
U
be any relatively compact zero neighborhood in
G
. We then have

\|\delta_{X}\|_{-U}\leq\sup_{i\in I}\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\,.

Proof.

Note first that
(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing
if and only if there exist
u,v\in U
such that
x_{i}+u=x_{j}+v
, or equivalently if and only if
x_{j}\in x_{i}+(U-U)
. Therefore we have for all
i\in I
that

\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}=\delta_{X}(x_{i}+(U-U))\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\ ,

and the upper inequality follows. For the lower inequality note
\delta_{X}(x-U)=\card(I_{x})
for

I_{x}=\{i\in I:x_{i}\in x-U\}
.
As
x_{i},x_{j}\in I_{x}
implies
x\in(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)
, we get for any
x_{i}
from
X
that

\begin{split}\delta_{X}(x-U)&=\card(I_{x})\leq\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\\
&\leq\sup_{i\in I}\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\end{split}

uniformly in
x\in G
, and the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
be a point family in
G
. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

There exists some relatively compact zero neighborhood
U\subseteq G
and some finite
B
such that
\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B
for all
i\in I
.

(b)

For any relatively compact zero neighborhood
V\subseteq G
there exists some finite
B_{V}
such that
\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+V)\cap(x_{j}+V)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B_{V}
for all
i\in I
.

(c)

\delta_{X}
is translation bounded.

Moreover, in this case we can choose
B_{V}=\|\delta_{X}\|_{V-V}
.
∎

In the definition of a BUPU, it is natural to assume that
X
allows for repetitions of points. Indeed, refining a BUPU by another BUPU naturally leads to finitely many repetitions of center points from

.

Remark 2.9

(Refining a BUPU)
.

Let
\Phi,\Psi
be two BUPUs of size
U
and
V
with point families
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
and
Y=(y_{j})_{j\in J}
. The refinement
\Theta=\Phi|\Psi
of
\Phi
by
\Psi
is the following BUPU of size
U
with index set
K=\{(i,j)\in I\times J:(x_{i}+U)\cap(y_{j}+V)\neq\varnothing\}
. We take
Z=(z_{k})_{k\in K}
where
z_{(i,j)}=x_{i}
for
(i,j)\in K
. Note that the number of repetitions of any point in
Z
is uniformly bounded due to the finite overlap property. Indeed we have

\begin{split}\card\{j\in J:(i,j)\in K\}&=\card\{j\in J:(x_{i}+U)\cap(y_{j}+V)\neq\varnothing\}\\
&=\delta_{Y}(x_{i}+U-V)\leq\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}<\infty\ ,\end{split}

compare Eqn. (
2.1
).
We set
\theta_{(i,j)}=\phi_{i}\cdot\psi_{j}
for
(i,j)\in K
. Then
\Theta=(\theta_{k})_{k\in K}
is a BUPU of size
U
. Property (c) in Definition
2.5
holds trivially, and we have
\|\theta_{k}\|\leq\|\phi_{i}\|\cdot\|\psi_{j}\|\leq M_{\Phi}\cdot M_{\Psi}
as the norm is multiplicative. Moreover
\supp(\theta_{(i,j)})\subseteq x_{i}+U
for all
(i,j)\in K
, and we may set
B_{\Theta}=B_{\Phi}\cdot\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}
. Indeed for
z_{\ell}\in K

\begin{split}\card&\{k\in K:(z_{\ell}+U)\cap(z_{k}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\\
&=\card\{(i,j)\in I\times J:(x_{\ell}+U)\cap(x_{i}+U)\neq\varnothing\wedge(i,j)\in K\}\\
&\leq\card\{i\in I:(x_{\ell}+U)\cap(x_{i}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\cdot\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}\leq B_{\Phi}\cdot\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}\end{split}

For
\|f\|_{\Phi}=\sum_{i\in I}\|f\phi_{i}\|
and
\|f\|_{\Psi}=\sum_{j\in J}\|f\psi_{j}\|
we then have by multiplicativity of the norm that

\begin{split}\max\{\|f\|_{\Phi},&\|f\|_{\Psi}\}\leq\|f\|_{\Phi|\Psi}=\sum_{(i,j)\in K}\|\phi_{i}\psi_{j}f\|\\
&\leq\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}\cdot M_{\Psi}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}\|\phi_{i}f\|=\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}\cdot M_{\Psi}\cdot\|f\|_{\Phi}\ ,\end{split}

(2.2)

an estimate that will be useful a few times later on.

We now discuss BUPUs in a product of two LCA groups. As usual, for
\phi\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
and
\psi\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
we denote by
\phi\otimes\psi\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G\times H)
their tensor product
(x,y)\mapsto(\phi\otimes\psi)(x,y)=\phi(x)\cdot\psi(y)
. The tensor product will be discussed in more detail in Section

Lemma 2.10

(Product BUPU)
.

Consider two LCA groups
G
and
H
. Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a BUPU of size
U
and norm
M
with point family
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
. Let
\Psi=(\psi_{j})_{j\in J}
be a BUPU of size
V
and norm
N
with point family
Y=(y_{j})_{j\in J}
in
H
. Define
\Phi\otimes\Psi=\{\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j}:(i,j)\in I\times J\}
. Then
\Phi\otimes\Psi
is a BUPU of size
U\times V
and norm
M\cdot N
with point family
X\times Y=((x_{i},y_{j}))_{(i,j)\in I\times J}
in
G\times H
.

Note that
\delta_{X\times Y}
is translation bounded as

\|\delta_{X\times Y}\|_{K\times L}=\|\delta_{X}\|_{K}\cdot\|\delta_{Y}\|_{L}
.
Hence condition (b) in the BUPU Definition
2.5
is satisfied due to Corollary
2.8
. Moreover
U\times V
is a compact zero neighborhood in
G\times H
.
Clearly
\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j}\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G\times H)
and
\supp(\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j})\subseteq(x_{i},y_{j})+(U\times V)
for all
(i,j)\in I\times J
. We also have

\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}(\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j})((x,y))=\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\phi_{i}(x)\cdot\psi_{j}(y)=\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(x)\cdot\sum_{j\in J}\psi_{j}(y)=1\ .

As
\|\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\psi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq M\cdot N
for all
(i,j)\in I\times J
, the claim follows.

3.
Constructing BUPUs

In this section, we review constructions of BUPUs of arbitrary small size from
[
16
,
21
,
26
,
22
]
.
We first describe a smoothing procedure that allows to construct a BUPU from a family of functions that are not necessarily continuous but satisfy the other properties of a BUPU. In particular, this applies to the motivating tiling example of Section
2.1
for the case of a single prototile.
We then review two constructions of BUPUs with prescribed locations of their function centers. One uses Delone sets, the other uses arbitrarily small lattices, whose existence is granted by the structure theorem of LCA groups.

3.1.
BUPUs via smoothing

Given a BUPU, we explain how to obtain a BUPU of functions with stronger analytic properties by a certain smoothing procedure.

Lemma 3.1

(Smoothing)
.

Fix any precompact open zero neighborhood
U
in
G
and take an open zero neighborhood
V
in
G
such that
V+V\subseteq U
.
Let
n
be a finite bound on the number of translates of
-V
that cover
U-U
. Let
\psi\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
be such that
\esupp(\psi)\subseteq V
and
m_{G}(\psi)=1
.
Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of functions in
L^{\infty}(G)
.
Let
M,B
be finite constants. Let
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
be a point family in
G
with the following properties.

(a)

\esupp(\phi_{i})\subseteq x_{i}+V
for all
i\in I
,

(b)

\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+V)\cap(x_{j}+V)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B/n
for all
i\in I
.

(c)

\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(x)=1
for almost all
x\in G
,

(d)

\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq M/\|\psi\|_{1}
for all
i\in I
,

Then
\Psi=(\phi_{i}*\psi)_{i\in I}
is a BUPU of norm
M
and size
U
with point family
X
and overlap constant
B
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
\Phi
is a BUPU if and only if
\phi_{i}
is continuous for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

We check conditions (a) to (d) in Definition
2.5
. First note that
\phi_{i}\in L^{\infty}(G)
and
\psi\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
imply
\phi_{i}*\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
, as the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is translation invariant and translation continuous, see e.g.
[
11
, Lem. 1.4.2]
or
[
56
, Prop. 3.5.6]
.
Moreover we have
\|\phi_{i}*\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\psi\|_{1}\leq M
, which proves (d).
Condition (a) is obvious as
\supp(\phi_{i}*\psi)\subseteq x_{i}+V+V\subseteq x_{i}+U
, and we also get
\phi_{i}*\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.

Let us prove (b) next. Take a finite set
F\subseteq G
such that
U-U\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in F}(x-V)
, which is possible as
V
is open and as
U
is precompact. Letting
n=\card(F)
, we then clearly have
\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\leq n\|\delta_{X}\|_{-V}
, which is seen from standard estimates.
Thus Lemma
2.7
yields

\begin{split}\sup_{i\in I}&\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\\
&\leq n\|\delta_{X}\|_{-V}\leq n\sup_{i\in I}\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+V)\cap(x_{j}+V)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B\ .\end{split}

Thus (b) is satisfied.

In order to prove (c), let
x\in G
be arbitrary and define
I_{x}=\{i\in I:(x-V)\cap\esupp(\phi_{i})\neq\varnothing\}
. Now
i\in I_{x}
implies
(x-V)\cap(x_{i}+V)\neq\varnothing
as
\esupp(\phi_{i})\subseteq x_{i}+V
. We thus have
x_{i}\in x+V+V
, which implies
\card(I_{x})\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{V+V}<\infty
. Thus

\begin{split}1&=\int_{G}\psi(x-t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{x-V}\psi(x-t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{x-V}\psi(x-t)\left(\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{x-V}\psi(x-t)\left(\sum_{i\in I_{x}}\phi_{i}(t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i\in I_{x}}\int_{x-V}\psi(x-t)\phi_{i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\sum_{i\in I}\int_{x-V}\psi(x-t)\phi_{i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i\in I}\psi*\phi_{i}(x)\ .\end{split}

∎

Remark 3.3
.

We will later work with BUPUs in a suitable Banach algebra
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{A}})
of functions over
G
, see Definition
5.1
below. This generalises the case
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{A}})=({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
from Definition
2.5
. The above smoothing technique can then be applied as follows.
Assume that
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
of size
U
, and assume that
\psi\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
is a function having essential support inside a zero neighbourhood
V
, being normalized such that
m_{G}(\psi)=1
. Then
\Psi=(\phi_{i}*\psi)_{i\in I}
is a BUPU in
{\bm{A}}
of size
U+V
as long as
\phi_{i}*\psi\in{\bm{A}}
and as there exists a finite constant
C
such that
\|\phi_{i}*\psi\|_{A}\leq C\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}
for all
i\in I
. Consider for example
{\bm{A}}=\mathcal{F}{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{G})
the Fourier algebra of
G
with norm
\|f\|_{\bm{A}}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{1}
. If the above
\psi
additionally satisfies
\psi\in{\bm{A}}
, we then indeed have

\|\phi_{i}*\psi\|_{{\bm{A}}}=\|\widehat{\phi}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\psi}\|_{1}\leq\|\widehat{\phi}_{i}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{1}\leq\|\phi_{i}\|_{1}\cdot\|\psi\|_{\bm{A}}\leq m_{G}(U)\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\psi\|_{\bm{A}}\ .

3.2.
BUPUs centered at Delone sets

Here we give two different constructions of BUPU’s based on the existence of Delone sets in
G
. Our first construction follows
[
16
, Thm. 2]
, see also
[
22
, Thm. 1]
. In the tempered distribution setting, similar constructions appear in Theorem 1.4.6 and Lemma 1.4.9 of
[
31
]
.
The following well-known result (see
[
16
, Lem. 1]
and references therein) states that there exist
U
-relatively dense Delone sets, for any compact zero neighbourhood
U
in
G
. The proof uses that if
\Gamma
is a maximal
U
-uniformly discrete subset of
G
, then
\Gamma+(U-U)=G
. Existence of such a maximal set is ensured by Zorn’s lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U
be any zero neighbourhood in
G
and let
V
be an open zero neighborhood in
G
such that
V-V\subseteq U
. Then the following hold.

(a)

There exists a set
\Lambda
in
G
with the following properties.

–

\Lambda
is
V
-uniformly discrete;

–

\Gamma
is not
V
-uniformly discrete for any
\Gamma\supsetneq\Lambda
.

(b)

Any set
\Lambda
in
G
having the properties in (a) satisfies
\Lambda+U=G
.

Proof.

“(a)": Consider the collection
\mathcal{P}
of all
V
-uniformly discrete subsets of
G
, together with set inclusion as partial order.
Consider any chain
\mathcal{C}=\{\Lambda_{i}:i\in I\}
in
\mathcal{P}
and define the upper bound
\Lambda=\bigcup_{i\in I}\Lambda_{i}\subseteq G
for
\mathcal{C}
. We show that
\Lambda
is
V
-uniformly discrete, which implies
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}
. Let
x,y\in\Lambda
be such that
x\neq y
. Then there exist some
i,j\in I
such that
x\in\Lambda_{i}
and
y\in\Lambda_{j}
. Let
k=\max\{i,j\}
. Then
x,y\in\Lambda_{k}
as
\mathcal{C}
is a chain. As
\Lambda_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
, we get
(x+V)\cap(y+V)=\varnothing
, which shows
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}
. Now Zorn’s Lemma yields that
\mathcal{P}
has some maximal element.

“(b)":
Assume that
\Lambda\subseteq G
has the properties in (a). Clearly
x\in\Lambda
satisfies
x\in\Lambda+U
as
0\in U
. Consider now arbitrary
x\in G\setminus\Lambda
.
Then
\Lambda\cup\{x\}
is not
V
-uniformly discrete. Since
\Lambda
is
V
-uniformly discrete, there exists some
y\in\Lambda
such that
(x+V)\cap(y+V)\neq\varnothing
.
Let
u,v\in V
be such that
x+u=y+v
. Then

x=y+v-u\in y+V-V\subseteq y+U\subseteq\Lambda+U\ ,

which completes the proof.
∎

Using a maximally uniformly discrete Delone set, we can construct a BUPU of given size
U
as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
U\subseteq G
be a precompact zero neighbourhood and let
V\subseteq G
be an open zero neighborhood such that
V-V\subseteq U
. Let
\Lambda\subseteq G
be a Delone set such that
\Lambda+V=G
.
Fix
\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
supported within
U
such that
\psi\geq 1_{V}
, and define

f=\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
, where
(\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi)(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\psi(x-\lambda)
. Then
f\geq 1
, and the functions
(\phi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, given by
\phi_{\lambda}(x)=\psi(x-\lambda)/f(x)
constitute a BUPU of positive functions in
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
having norm
1
and size
U
with point family
\Lambda
.

Existence of a Delone set
\Lambda
such that
\Lambda+V=G
is granted by Lemma
3.4
.
As
\Lambda
is Delone, its Dirac comb
\delta_{\Lambda}
is translation bounded. Hence condition (d) in the definition of BUPU is satisfied with
B=\|\delta_{\Lambda}\|_{U-U}
by Remark
2.6
.
We indeed have
f\geq 1
as
f=\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi\geq\delta_{\Lambda}*1_{V}\geq 1_{\Lambda+V}=1_{G}
. Also
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
as
\delta_{\Lambda}
is translation bounded, compare Section
7.2
or
[
50
, Lem. 4.9.19]
. Indeed standard estimates show

|(\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi)(x)-(\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi)(y)|\leq 2\cdot\|T_{x-y}\psi-\psi\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\delta_{\Lambda}\|_{U}\ ,

from which the claim follows by uniform continuity of
\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.
Hence
\phi_{\lambda}\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
and
\phi_{\lambda}\geq 0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. For any fixed
x\in G
we have

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{f(x)}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\psi(x-\lambda)=\frac{1}{f(x)}(\delta_{\Lambda}*\psi)(x)=1\ ,

where the above sums are all finite due to translation boundedness of
\delta_{\Lambda}
.
This proves condition (a) in the definition of BUPU. Moreover we clearly have
0\leq\phi_{\lambda}\leq 1
, which implies
\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Finally we observe
\supp(\phi_{\lambda})=\lambda+\supp(\psi)\subseteq\lambda+U
, which completes the proof.

The second construction of a BUPU is extracted from
[
20
, Thm. 4.2]
. In that reference a construction of so-called bounded adapted partitions of unity (BAPUs) is given for discrete point sets that are relatively dense but may fail to be uniformly discrete. In comparison to the constrution in Proposition
3.5
, the second construction is better adapted to the underlying function algebra structure, as it needs no division. This construction has also been used in the tempered distribution setting, see e.g.
[
31
, Lem. 1.4.9]
or
[
63
, Thm. 6.20]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Fix any zero neighborhood
U
in
G
and choose some open zero neighborhood
V
such that
\overline{V}+\overline{V}+\overline{V}\subseteq U
. Take a well-ordered Delone set
\Lambda
which is
V
-uniformly discrete and
K
-relatively dense, where
K=\overline{V}+\overline{V}
.
Take
\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
supported within
K+\overline{V}\subseteq U
, satisfying
0\leq\psi\leq 1
, such that
\psi|_{K}=1
. Define
\psi_{\lambda}(x)=\psi(x-\lambda)
and
\phi_{\lambda}=\psi_{\lambda}\prod_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}(1-\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}})
.
Then
(\phi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a BUPU of nonnegative functions in
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
of size
U
.

Existence of a Delone set
\Lambda
with the claimed properties follows from Lemma
3.4
.
We can assume without loss of generality that the set
\Lambda
is well-ordered.
Note that, for any
x\in G
, the number of non-trivial factors in the product
\phi_{\lambda}=\psi_{\lambda}\prod_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}(1-\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}})
is uniformly bounded as
\Lambda
is uniformly discrete. Hence
\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq C
uniformly in
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Moreover, for any
x\in G
, the number of
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
\phi_{\lambda}(x)\neq 0
is uniformly bounded as
\Lambda
is uniformly discrete. Moreover
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\phi_{\lambda}=1
by construction. Indeed, for any
x\in G
take
F\subseteq\Lambda
of maximal cardinality such that
x\in\bigcap_{\lambda\in F}(\lambda+K)
. Then
F
is finite as
\Lambda
is uniformly discrete. Denote its smallest element by
\lambda_{0}
. We then have
\phi_{\lambda_{0}}(x)=1
and
\phi_{\lambda}(x)=0
for all
\lambda\in F\setminus\{\lambda_{0}\}
. Moreover
\phi_{\lambda}(x)=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus F
. Altogether
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(x)=1
.

Remark 3.7
.

The above two BUPU constructions work in suitable Banach algebras
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{A}})
beyond
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, compare Remark
3.3
. As an example, we consider the Fourier algebra
{\bm{A}}={\bm{A}}(G)=\mathcal{F}{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{G})
of
G
with norm
\|f\|_{\bm{A}}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{1}
. From the first construction, a BUPU in
{\bm{A}}
is obtained by smoothing as explained in Remark
3.3
. A more general statement is
[
16
, Thm. 2]
. For the second construction we note that there exists
\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\cap{\bm{A}}(G)
with the desired properties by
[
33
, Lem. 4.3(iv)]
. As
{\bm{A}}(G)
is a Banach algebra with respect to pointwise multiplication, the construction proceeds as in the case of
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
.

3.3.
BUPUs via the structure theorem

Arbitrarily fine BUPUs on an LCA group
G
can also be constructed using the structure theorem
[
56
, Thm. 4.2.29]
, which states that every compactly generated LCA group
G
is isomorphic to a product
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{K}
for some
d,m\geq 0
. We split the construction as follows. The first step uses Example
2.1
.

Lemma 3.8
.

G=\mathbb{R}
admits a BUPU
\Phi
of positive functions with norm
M=1
and size
U=[-r,r]
for any
r>0
in
\mathbb{R}
, with overlapping constant
B=5
and lattice
L=r\mathbb{Z}
as point family.
Moreover
\Phi
consists of translates of a single function. ∎

The second step concerns discrete groups. The following result is trivial.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G=D
be a discrete LCA group. Let
\phi_{a}=1_{\{a\}}
for
a\in D
. Then
\Phi=\{\phi_{a}\}_{a\in D}
is a BUPU of positive functions with norm
M=1
and size
U=\{0\}
, with overlapping constant
B=1
and lattice
D
as point family. Moreover, all functions in
\Phi
are translates of one another.
∎

The third result concerns compact groups. The proof is analogous to that of Proposition
3.5
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
G=\mathbb{K}
be a compact LCA group and let
U
be any open zero neighborhood in
\mathbb{K}
. Then there exists a BUPU
\Phi
of positive functions with norm
M=1
, size
U
, and with some finite set
F
as point family.

Proof.

Pick an open zero neighborhood
V
such that
\overline{V}\subseteq U
, and pick some function
\varphi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathbb{K})
such that
\varphi\geq 1_{\overline{V}}
and
\supp(\varphi)\subseteq U
. Take a finite set
F
such that
F+V=\mathbb{K}
.
Then the function

f(x)=\sum_{j\in F}\varphi(x-j)

satisfies
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathbb{K})
and
f\geq 1
. Now, defining

\phi_{j}(x)=\varphi(x-j)/f(x)

yields a BUPU
\Phi=(\phi_{j})_{j\in F}
with the claimed properties.
∎

Combining the previous results, we infer that any compactly generated LCA groups admits a BUPU of the following type.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated LCA group. Then for any precompact open zero neighborhood
U
in
G
there exists a BUPU
\Phi
of size
U
of positive functions with norm
M=1
with the following properties: The point family is of the form
L+F
for a lattice
L
and a finite set
F
in
G
, and there exists a finite set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
such that
\Phi
consists of translates of functions from
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

By the structure theorem of compactly generated LCA groups
[
56
, Thm. 4.2.29]
, we can identify
G
by
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{K}
for some
d,m\geq 0
and some compact group
\mathbb{K}
without loss of generality.
Pick
r>0
and some zero neighborhood
V
in
\mathbb{K}
such that
[-r,r]^{d}\times\{0\}\times V\subseteq U
. By Lemma
3.9
and by repeated application of Lemma
3.8
and Lemma
2.10
, there exists a BUPU in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
of positive functions with norm
M=1
and size
[-r,r]^{d}\times\{0\}
, having a lattice
L^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
as point family. Morover the functions of the BUPU are translates of one another. Applying Lemma
3.10
and Lemma
2.10
again, we get a BUPU
\Phi
of positive functions with norm
M=1
and size
U
, where the point family is the product of the lattice
L^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and some finite set
F^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{K}
. Hence the point family is of the form
L+F
, where
L=L^{\prime}\times\{0\}
and

F=\{0\}\times\{0\}\times F^{\prime}
.
Finally, each element in the BUPU
\Phi
is a product of the form
\varphi\otimes\psi
where
\varphi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m})
is in a family of translates of a single function, and
\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\mathbb{K})
belongs to a family of translates of a finite set of functions. Therefore, all functions in the BUPU are translates of a finite set of functions.
∎

For a general LCA group we use the following lemma, whose proof is obvious.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be an open subgroup in some LCA group
G
. Let
U\subseteq G^{\prime}
be any open zero neighborhood. Let
R\subseteq G
be a system of
representatives for
G/G^{\prime}
, i.e, any coset
x+G^{\prime}
contains exactly one element in
R
. Let
\Psi=(\psi_{i})_{i\in I}
be any BUPU in
G^{\prime}
of size
U
and norm
M
with point family
Y=(y_{i})_{i\in I}
. Then

\Phi=(T_{r}\psi_{i})_{(i,r)\in I\times R}

is a BUPU in
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
of size
U
and norm
M
with point family
X=(y_{i}+r)_{(i,r)\in I\times R}
. ∎

We can now state the promised result about BUPUs constructed via the structure theorem.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
U\subseteq G
be any precompact zero neighborhood in some LCA group
G
.
Then there exists a BUPU
\Phi
of positive functions with norm
M=1
and size
U
, such that the point family is a Delone set, and such that any function in
\Phi
is a translate of some function from some finite set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.

It is obvious from the construction in Lemma
3.12
that if
\Psi
consists of positive functions, then so does
\Phi
. Moreover, the functions in
\Phi
are translates of functions in
\Psi
. Therefore, if the functions in
\Psi
are translates of a finite set of functions, so are the functions in
\Phi
. Now, given any open precompact zero neighborhood
U
in
G
, the group
G^{\prime}
generated by
\overline{U}
is compactly generated and open in
G
. Therefore Proposition
3.11
and Lemma
3.12
yield the claim of the theorem.

4.
The Wiener algebra

We review the Wiener algebra on an LCA group
G
, following the approach from
[
19
]
based on BUPUs. The Wiener algebra dual coincides with the space of translation bounded measures, as shown in Section
7.2
. Feichtinger’s algebra will be constructed in Section
5
by an analogous approach. In the following, we will sometimes suppress the underlying group.

4.1.
Definition and elementary properties

Wiener introduced a certain algebra
M_{1}
of continuous integrable functions on the line, see
[
72
, p. 73]
and
[
29
, (39.33)]
for a discussion. This was extended to locally compact groups
G
in
[
56
]
and in
[
45
]
, where the function algebra was then denoted by
M_{1}(G)
. A characterisation of the Wiener algebra using Segal algebra theory was then given
[
13
]
and generalised in
[
16
]
. The identification as amalgam space
{{\bm{W}}({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}},{{\bm{\ell}}^{1}})}(G)
appears in
[
19
]
. Here we introduce the Wiener algebra
{\bm{W}}(G)
using BUPUs, see also
[
22
]
for a recent perspective. The identification
{\bm{W}}(G)={{\bm{W}}({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}},{{\bm{\ell}}^{1}})}(G)=M_{1}(G)
then follows from
[
19
, Thm. 2]
. For concreteness, we derive some of its properties directly from our definition of
{\bm{W}}(G)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be any BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. Then the Wiener algebra is given by

{\@index{\@indexphrase{$\|\,\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}$}}}{\bm{W}}(G)=\{f\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G):\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}=\sum_{i\in I}\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}<\infty\}\ .

In the following we often write
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}
for simplicity of notation.

Lemma 4.2
.

\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
defines a shift continuous norm on
{\bm{W}}(G)
, which satisfies
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
.

It is clear that
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
defines a norm on
{\bm{W}}(G)
. Consider arbitrary
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
. Then
\|f\|_{\infty}=\|\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|f\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\Phi}
, which shows
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
. For shift continuity consider arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and partition
I=I_{1}\cup I_{0}
such that
I_{1}
is finite and
\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. Take compact
K\subseteq G
such that
\supp(\phi_{i})\subseteq K
for all
i\in I_{1}
. Take a an open zero neighborhood
V
such that
\|(T_{x}f-f)1_{K}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon/\card(I_{1})
for all
x\in V
. We then have for all
x\in V
that

\begin{split}\|T_{x}f-f\|_{\Phi}&=\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\|(T_{x}f-f)\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\|(T_{x}f-f)\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\|(T_{x}f-f)\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\|f\cdot(T_{-x}\phi_{i})\|_{\infty}+\sum_{i\in I_{0}}\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq M\cdot\varepsilon+\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}M\cdot\varepsilon+\varepsilon\ ,\end{split}

where we note for the second term that the estimates in Eqn. (
2.2
) continue to hold for subsets of
I
.
Now adjusting
\varepsilon
finishes the argument.

In fact there exists an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{W}}}
on
{\bm{W}}(G)
that is shift continuous and shift invariant, see Lemma
4.7
below. The following properties of the Wiener algebra are not difficult to verify.

Theorem 4.3
.

(a)

Different choices of
\Phi
define equivalent norms on
{\bm{W}}(G)
. In particular, the definition of
{\bm{W}}(G)
is independent of the choice of
\Phi
.

(b)

f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
implies
\overline{f},|f|,f^{\dagger},T_{t}f,\chi f\in{\bm{W}}(G)

for arbitrary
t\in G
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.

(c)

({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is a Banach algebra with respect to both pointwise multiplication and convolution.

(d)

{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
, equipped with the inductive limit topology, is continuously embedded and dense in
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
.

(e)

({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is continuously embedded and dense in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Moreover, it is a pointwise multiplication ideal in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

(f)

({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is continuously embedded and dense in
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
. Moreover, it is a convolution ideal in
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
.

Remark 4.4
.

Part (a) and (b) of the above theorem imply absolute summability of
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
on any uniformly discrete point set. In fact there are equivalent norms on more general so-called BAPUs
[
25
,
20
]
. This leads to absolute summability beyond uniformly discrete point sets.

“(a)” By Eqn. (
2.2
), any two BUPUs
\Phi,\Psi
of size
U,V
with point families
X,Y
, respectively, satisfy

\|f\|_{\Psi}\leq\|\delta_{Y}\|_{U-V}\cdot M_{\Psi}\cdot\|f\|_{\Phi}\ ,\qquad\|f\|_{\Phi}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{V-U}\cdot M_{\Phi}\cdot\|f\|_{\Psi}\ .

“(b)” Note
\|\bar{f}\|_{\Phi}=\||f|\|_{\Phi}=\|\chi f\|_{\Phi}
. We also have
\|f\|_{\Phi}=\|f^{\dagger}\|_{\Phi^{\dagger}}=\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\Phi^{\dagger}}
, where
\Phi^{\dagger}=(\phi^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
is the BUPU of size
-U
with point family
(-x_{i})_{i\in I}
. Note that
\|T_{x}f\|_{\Phi}=\|f\|_{T_{-x}\Phi}
, where
T_{-x}\Phi=(T_{-x}\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
is the BUPU of size
U
with point family
(x_{i}-x)_{i\in I}
.

“(c)” Here we show completeness, the algebra properties follow from (e) and (f). Let
(f_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence in
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
. Then
(f_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
as
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
. Denote its limit by
f\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. Note that for fixed
i\in I
the sequence
(\|f_{n}\phi_{i}\|_{\infty})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\|f\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}
, and
(\|f\phi_{i}\|_{\infty})_{i\in I}\in{{\bm{\ell}}^{1}}
by completeness of
{{\bm{\ell}}^{1}}
. Thus
\|f\|_{\Phi}<\infty
.

“(d)” We use the identity as embedding map. Denseness is an immediate consequence of
f=\sum_{i\in I}f\cdot\phi_{i}
as
f\cdot\phi_{i}\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
for any
i\in I
. For continuity, assume
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
such that
\supp(f)\subseteq K
for some compact set
K\subseteq G
. Then the claim follows from
\|f\|_{\Phi}\leq M\delta_{X}(K-U)\|f\|_{\infty}
. To show the latter inequality, note
\|f\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq M\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}
. Moreover we have

\card\{i\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap K\neq\varnothing\}=\card\{i\in I:x_{i}\in K-U\}=\delta_{X}(K-U)\ .

This yields
\|f\|_{\Phi}=\sum_{i\in I}\|f\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta_{X}(K-U)\cdot M\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}
.

“(e)” We use the identity as embedding map. Note first that
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is continuously embedded in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
as
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
.
Denseness follows from denseness of
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. The ideal property holds as
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a Banach algebra with respect to pointwise multiplication, which leads to
\|f\cdot g\|_{\Phi}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\|g\|_{\Phi}
for
f\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
and
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
.

“(f)” We use the canonical quotient map
{\bm{W}}(G)\to{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
as embedding map. It is indeed injective as
{\bm{W}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{C}}(G)
. Take a BUPU
\Phi
of size
U
. Then we have for any
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
that

\|g\|_{1}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\|g\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{1}\leq m_{G}(U)\cdot\sum_{i\in I}\|g\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}=m_{G}(U)\cdot\|g\|_{\Phi}

This shows that
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is continuously embedded into
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
. Denseness follows from denseness of
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
in
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
. To show that
f*g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
for
f\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
and
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
, note first
g\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
, such that the convolution is well defined. Next note

\begin{split}\|T_{y}g\|_{\Phi}&=\|g\|_{T_{-y}\Phi}\leq\|g\|_{\Phi|T_{-y}\Phi}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}M\|g\|_{\Phi}\ ,\end{split}

where we used the estimate in Eqn. (
2.2
). We thus obtain

\begin{split}\|f*g\|_{\Phi}&=\sum_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in G}\left|\int f(y)g(x-y)\phi_{i}(x)\,{\rm d}y\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{i\in I}\int|f(y)|\cdot\|(T_{y}g)\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\,{\rm d}y=\int|f(y)|\cdot\|T_{y}g\|_{\Phi}\,{\rm d}y\\
&\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}M\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{\Phi}\ ,\end{split}

where we used monotone convergence for the second equality.

4.2.
Product property of
{\bm{W}}(G)

The following product property of the Wiener algebra will later be important.
We first recall the notion of projective tensor product of function spaces, see e.g.
[
64
, Ch. 2]
or
[
65
]
for background. Consider two Banach spaces
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{A}}})
and
({\bm{B}},\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{B}}})
of functions on LCA groups
G
and
H
, respectively. Then the
tensor product

g\otimes h
of
g\in{\bm{A}}
and
h\in{\bm{B}}
is the function
g\otimes h:G\times H\to\mathbb{C}
defined via
(f\otimes g)(x,y)=f(x)\cdot g(y)
.
Let
{\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}}
denote the vector space of functions on
G\times H
spanned by tensor products of functions from
{\bm{A}}
and
{\bm{B}}
, respectively. We have

{\@index{\@indexphrase{${\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}}$}}}{\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\otimes g_{j}:n\geq 1,f_{j}\in{\bm{A}},g_{j}\in{\bm{B}}\right\}\ .

By
[
64
, Prop. 2.1]
, there exists a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
{\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}}
such that

\|g\otimes h\|=\|g\|_{{\bm{A}}}\cdot\|h\|_{{\bm{B}}}
holds for all
g\in{\bm{A}}
and
h\in{\bm{B}}
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{A}}})
and
({\bm{B}},\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{B}}})
be two Banach spaces of functions on LCA groups
G
and
H
, respectively. The projective tensor product
{\bm{A}}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,{\bm{B}}
is defined as the abstract completion of
({\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}},\|\cdot\|)
.

In all examples below where we refer to the projective tensor product
{\bm{A}}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,{\bm{B}}
, the space
({\bm{A}}\otimes{\bm{B}},\|\cdot\|)
embeds inside some Banach space
({\bm{C}},\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{C}}})
. We will then always view
{\bm{A}}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,{\bm{B}}
as a subspace of
{\bm{C}}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G,H
be LCA groups. Then
{\bm{W}}(G)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,{\bm{W}}(H)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G\times H)
.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
,
and let
\Psi=(\psi_{j})_{j\in J}
be a BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
.
Consider the product BUPU
\Phi\otimes\Psi=(\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j})_{(i,j)\in I\times J}
in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G\times H)
, compare Lemma
2.10
.
We then have for
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
and
h\in{\bm{W}}(H)
that

\begin{split}\|g\otimes&h\|_{{\bm{W}}(G\times H),\Phi\otimes\Psi}=\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\|(\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{j})\cdot(g\otimes h)\|_{\infty}\\
&=\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\|(\phi_{i}\cdot g)\otimes(\psi_{j}\cdot h)\|_{\infty}=\sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\|\phi_{i}\cdot g\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\psi_{j}\cdot h\|_{\infty}\\
&=\|g\|_{{\bm{W}}(G),\Phi}\cdot\|h\|_{{\bm{W}}(H),\Psi}<\infty\ .\end{split}

The above argument shows that also
f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n}\otimes h_{n}\in{\bm{W}}(G\times H)
as long as
g_{n}\in{\bm{W}}(G)
,
h_{n}\in{\bm{W}}(H)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with a finite sum

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n}\|_{{\bm{W}}(G),\Phi}\|h_{n}\|_{{\bm{W}}(H),\Psi}<\infty\ .

We thus have
{\bm{W}}(G)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,{\bm{W}}(H)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G\times H)
.

4.3.
Segal algebra characterisation of
{\bm{W}}(G)

Recall that a
Segal algebra
is a Banach space
({\bm{B}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{B}})
that is contained in
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
as a dense subspace and is homogeneous, i.e.,
\|T_{x}f\|_{\bm{B}}=\|f\|_{\bm{B}}
and
\lim_{x\to 0}\|T_{x}f-f\|_{\bm{B}}=0
for all
f\in{\bm{B}}
, see
[
56
, Sec. 6.2]
and references therein for background. Segal algebras are convolution ideals in
({{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{1})
satisfying
\|g*f\|_{\bm{B}}\leq\|g\|_{1}\cdot\|f\|_{\bm{B}}
for
g\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
and
f\in{\bm{B}}
by
[
56
, Prop. 6.2.4]
. Any Segal algebra contains the integrable functions of finite bandwidth

{\@index{\@indexphrase{${\bm{LK}}(G)$}}}{\bm{LK}}(G)=\{f\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G):\widehat{f}\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})\}

as a dense subspace
[
56
, Prop. 6.2.19]
, and
{\bm{LK}}(G)
equals the intersection of all Segal algebras
[
55
, p. 26]
.
We will always consider
{\bm{LK}}(G)
as a space of continuous functions. Extending results from
[
56
]
, it is shown in
[
45
, Thm. 5.1]
that
M_{1}(G)={\bm{W}}(G)
is a Segal algebra. As shown in
[
13
, Thm. 3]
, the Wiener algebra satisfies a certain minimality property: Among all Segal algebras with the additional property of being a pointwise
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
-module it is the smallest one, i.e., it is continuously embedded into any such Segal algebra. This is extended in
[
16
, Thm. 5]
to
{\bm{W}}({\bm{B}},{{\bm{\ell}}^{1}})
beyond
{\bm{B}}={{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}
.

For our presentation to be self-contained, we argue that
{\bm{W}}(G)
is a Segal algebra by constructing a homogeneous norm
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
from
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be any BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
and define
\|f\|_{\bm{W}}=\sup_{x\in G}\|T_{x}f\|_{\Phi}
for
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
. Then
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
is an equivalent homogeneous norm on
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
.

Using Eqn. (
2.2
), we infer for
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
that

\|f\|_{\bm{W}}=\sup_{x\in G}\|T_{x}f\|_{\Phi}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\cdot M\cdot\|f\|_{\Phi}\ .

This shows that
\|f\|_{\bm{W}}
is finite. As
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
satisfies the norm axioms and
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
, this also shows that
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
are equivalent norms. The norm
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
is translation invariant by definition, and translation continuity is inherited from
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
as

\|T_{x}f-f\|_{\bm{W}}=\sup_{y\in G}\|T_{y}T_{x}f-T_{y}f\|_{\Phi}\leq\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}\cdot M\cdot\|T_{x}f-f\|_{\Phi}\ .

4.4.
The Wiener algebra dual

Consider the dual
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
of the normed space
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
. Then
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
is a Banach space when equipped with the operator norm
\mu\mapsto{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi}
, given by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi}=\sup\left\{|\mu(f)|:f\in{\bm{W}}(G),\|f\|_{\Phi}\leq 1\right\}\ .

Note that
\left|\mu(f)\right|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi}\cdot\|f\|_{\Phi}
for all
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
, and that different BUPUs lead to equivalent norms on
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
. It is well known
[
45
, Thm. 6.1]
that
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
can be identified with the space of complex Radon measures that are translation bounded. This will be discussed in Section
7
, see Theorem
7.1
below. For later reference, we note the following result on convolution.

Lemma 4.8
.

For
\mu\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
and
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
define
\mu*f:G\to\mathbb{C}
by
(\mu*f)(x)=\mu(T_{x}f^{\dagger})
for
x\in G
. Then
\mu*f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
.
Moreover, for each BUPU
\Phi
there exists a constant
C_{\Phi}
such that

\|\mu*f\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\Phi}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}

for all
\mu\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
and all
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
.

Consider a BUPU
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
of size
U
and norm
M
. Boundedness follows from the estimate

|(\mu*f)(x)|=|\mu(T_{x}f^{\dagger})|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Phi}\cdot\|T_{x}f^{\dagger}\|_{\Phi}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|\delta_{X}\|_{U-U}M\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\ ,

where we used Eqn. (
2.2
).
Continuity follows from shift continuity of
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
, see Lemma
4.2
.

5.
Feichtinger’s algebra

In this section, we discuss Feichtinger’s algebra
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
.
This function algebra has been introduced in
[
17
]
and has recently been reviewed in
[
33
]
. We will introduce
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
using BUPUs in
{\bm{A}}(G)
, in analogy to our treatment of the Wiener algebra.

5.1.
BUPUs for Banach algebras

We recall the general definition of bounded uniform partition of unity from
[
19
]
. Here boundedness is understood with respect to a suitable Banach space of continuous functions
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{A}})
over
G
, which is an algebra with respect to pointwise multiplication.

Definition 5.1

(BUPU in
{\bm{A}}
)
.

Let
({\bm{A}},\|\cdot\|_{\bm{A}})
be a suitable Banach algebra of functions over
G
.
A family
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
in
{\bm{A}}
is called a bounded uniform partition of unity (BUPU) of norm
M
and size
U
with overlap constant
B
, if there exists an indexed family of points
X=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
G
, a relatively compact zero neighborhood
U\subseteq G
and finite constants
M
and
B
such that

(a)

\supp(\phi_{i})\subseteq x_{i}+U
for all
i\in I
,

(b)

\card\{j\in I:(x_{i}+U)\cap(x_{j}+U)\neq\varnothing\}\leq B
for all
i\in I
,

(c)

\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(x)=1
for all
x\in G
,

(d)

\|\phi_{i}\|_{\bm{A}}\leq M
for all
i\in I
.

∎

Instead of working with the general definition of suitable Banach algebra, which we will not give here, we restrict to two examples in this article. For the Wiener algebra, we have considered
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
in the previous section, where
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
denotes the space of continuous functions on
G
vanishing at infinity, and where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm. For Feichtinger’s algebra
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, we will consider
({\bm{A}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)})
where
{\bm{A}}(G)=\mathcal{F}{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
is the Fourier algebra of
G
with norm
\|f\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{G})}
. In that context, an important function space appears to be

{\@index{\@indexphrase{${\bm{KL}}(G)$}}}{\bm{KL}}(G)=\{f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G):\widehat{f}\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{G})\}={\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\cap{\bm{A}}(G)\ .

We recall the following well-known result, compare
[
33
, Lem. 4.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The Fourier algebra
({\bm{A}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)})
is continuously embedded and dense in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. In fact
{\bm{K}}_{2}(G)=\text{span}\{f*\widetilde{f}:f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\}\subseteq{\bm{A}}(G)\cap{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
is dense in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

We have
{\bm{A}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
by the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma and
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)}
for
f\in{\bm{A}}(G)
. Thus the identity as embedding map is clearly continuous. An estimate using Cauchy’s inequality shows
{\bm{K}}_{2}(G)\subseteq{\bm{A}}(G)
.
We argue that
{\bm{K}}_{2}(G)
is dense in
({\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.
Then the lemma is proved, as
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
is dense in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Let
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
be arbitrary.
Take a Dirac net
(v_{i})_{i}
in
G
, i.e.,
v_{i}\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
is nonnegative,
\|v_{i}\|_{1}=1
and the support of
v_{i}
is contained in some zero neighborhood which shrinks to
0
with increasing
i
, compare
[
11
, Lem. 1.6.5]
. Then
(f*v_{i})_{i}
converges uniformly to
f
, compare
[
11
, Lem. 3.4.5]
. Moreover
f*v_{i}\in{\bm{K}}_{2}(G)
by polarisation
[
50
, page 244]
.

5.2.
Feichtinger’s algebra

As any BUPU in
{\bm{A}}(G)
is a BUPU in
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
due to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)}
, it is natural to consider the following subspace of
{\bm{W}}(G)
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be any BUPU in
{\bm{A}}(G)
. Then Feichtinger’s algebra is given by

{\@index{\@indexphrase{$\|\,\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}$}}}{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)=\{f\in{\bm{A}}(G):\|f\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}=\sum_{i\in I}\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{{\bm{A}}(G)}<\infty\}\ .

The following result is proved as in the previous section. In order to show shift continuity, one uses that the topology on
\widehat{G}
is the topology of convergence on compact sets.

Lemma 5.4
.

\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}
defines a shift continuous norm on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, which satisfies
\|\cdot\|_{A}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}
. Moreover
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}
is invariant by multiplication with continuous characters, i.e., we have
\|\chi\cdot f\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\phi}=\|f\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\phi}
for all
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
and all
\chi\in\widehat{G}
. ∎

The following result describes important relations to
{\bm{W}}(G)
and
{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

({{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi})
is continuously embedded and dense in both
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi})
and
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

We have argued above that
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. The identity map provides a continuous embedding as
\|\cdot\|_{W}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|_{A}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}
. Denseness in
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{W})
follows from denseness of
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
in
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}})
and denseness of
{\bm{K}}_{2}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
in
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Indeed, consider arbitrary
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
and let

5.4.
Fourier properties of
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)

Fourier analysis on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
shares many properties with that on
{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(G)
. This was studied in
[
17
]
using the minimality property of
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. Here we give direct arguments.

Theorem 5.10
.

[
17
, Thm. 7]

If
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, then
\widehat{f}\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
. As a consequence, the Fourier transform
\widehat{}:{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\to{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
is a bijection.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a BUPU in
A(G)
of size
U
and consider arbitrary
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. We then have
f=\sum_{i\in I}f\phi_{i}
pointwise and in
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. In particular, the sum is convergent in
{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
and thus also in
{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(G)
, by boundedndess of
f
. This implies
\widehat{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\widehat{f}*\widehat{\phi}_{i}
pointwise, by continuity of
\widehat{f}
and by continuity of the Fourier transform on
{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(G)
. We show that the latter sum is convergent in
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
, which proves the claim. Let
\psi\in{\bm{KL}}(G)
such that
\psi=1
on
U
and define
\psi_{i}=T_{x_{i}}\psi
. As
\psi_{i}\cdot\phi_{i}=\phi_{i}
, we conclude

\|\widehat{f}*\widehat{\phi}_{i}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}=\|\widehat{f}*\widehat{\phi}_{i}*\widehat{\psi}_{i}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}\leq\|\widehat{\psi}_{i}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}\cdot\|\widehat{f}*\widehat{\phi}_{i}\|_{1}=\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}\cdot\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{A(G)}

where, for convenience, we used a norm on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
that is character invariant. As
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, this shows

\sum_{i\in I}\|\widehat{f}*\widehat{\phi}_{i}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}\leq\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}\|f\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{A(G)}<\infty\ .

This shows
\mathcal{F}{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
. As
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)\cap{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, the bijectivity statement follows from the Fourier inversion theorem
[
56
, Thm. 4.4.5]
.

As an application, we will give short proof of the following well-known result. This result may alternatively be regarded as a consequence of the Segal algebra property of the Wiener algebra, compare Section
4.3
.

Corollary 5.11
.

If
f\in{\bm{KL}}(G)
then
\widehat{f}\in{\bm{W}}(\widehat{G})
.

As
{\bm{KL}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
by Theorem
5.6
(d), we have
{\bm{LK}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
by Theorem
5.10
. Noting
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})\subseteq{\bm{W}}(\widehat{G})
, the claim follows.

For the question of a norm such that the Fourier transform is isometric, fix arbitrary
g\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\backslash\{0\}
. We cite the following two results.

Theorem 5.12
.

[
33
, Prop. 8.1]
Let
f\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
. Then
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
if and only if

{\@index{\@indexphrase{$\|\,\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}$}}}\|f\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}=\int_{G}\int_{\widehat{G}}\left|\int_{G}\overline{\chi(s)}f(s)\overline{g(s-t)}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\,\mathrm{d}\chi\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\ .

Moreover,
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}
is a norm on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
that is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}}
. ∎

The above norm is central in time-frequency analysis, the function

\mathcal{V}_{g}(f)(t,\chi)=\int_{G}f(s)\overline{\chi(s)}\overline{g(s-t)}\,\mathrm{d}s

is called the short-time Fourier transform of
f
. The norm
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}
makes the Fourier transform an isometric bijection in the following sense.

Theorem 5.13
.

[
33
, Prop. 4.10, Prop. 8.1]

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}
on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
is equivalent to any norm
\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},\Phi}
. Moreover for
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\backslash\{0\}
we have

\|f\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g}=\|g\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),f}=\|\overline{f}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\overline{g}}=\|\widetilde{f}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\widetilde{g}}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G}),\widehat{g}}\ .

In particular, the Fourier transform is an isometric isomorphism

\widehat{\,}:({{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),g})\to({{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G}),\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G}),\widehat{g}})\ .

∎

Remark 5.14

Consider

Consider the dual space

Definition 6.1

[

6.1.
Positive definite mild distributions

We now discuss positive definite mild distributions and their Fourier transform.
Let us call a continuous function
f\in{\bm{C}}(G)
positive definite if there exists a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\widehat{G}
such that
f(x)=\int_{G}\chi(x){\rm d}\mu(\chi)
. This is justified by Bochner’s theorem on positive definite functions
[
62
, Ch. 1.4]
. Then
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
is positive definite if and only if
\widehat{f}\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
is non-negative. This holds as the measure
\mu=\widehat{f}{\d{m}_{\widehat{G}}}
is uniquely determined by
f
.
We call a mild distribution
\sigma\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(G)
positive definite if
\sigma(f)\geq 0
for all positive definite
f\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
. We first argue that positive definiteness is already implied by
\sigma(f)\geq 0
for all positive definite
f\in{\bm{KL}}(G)
.
This will be used in the following section to argue that, for a mild distribution that is a measure, the notion of positive definite measure coincides with the notion of positive definite mild distribution. We start with the following result on denseness of
{\bm{LK}}(G)
in
{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
, which slightly improves
[
56
, Prop. 5.4.1]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
f\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(G)
and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then there exists some
g\in{\bm{LK}}(G)
such that

\|f-g\|_{1}<\varepsilon
. If furthermore
f
is non-negative, then
g
can be chosen non-negative.

We follow
[
56
, Prop. 5.4.1]
and define
h(x)=\sqrt{|f(x)|}
. It is immediate that there exists some
h_{1}
such that

f=h\cdot h_{1}
. Moreover, by construction we have
|h|^{2}=|h_{1}|^{2}=|f|
. Therefore
h,h_{1}\in{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(G)
and hence
\widehat{h},\widehat{h_{1}}\in{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(\widehat{G})
. Since
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
is dense in
{{\bm{L}}^{2}}(\widehat{G})
, we can find two functions
\varphi,\psi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
such that

\|\widehat{h_{1}}-\varphi\|_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|h\|_{2}+2}\ ,\qquad\|\widehat{h}-\psi\|_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|\varphi\|_{2}+2}\ .

Then
g=\widecheck{\varphi}\cdot\widecheck{\psi}=\widecheck{\varphi*\psi}\in{\bm{LK}}(G)
and

\begin{split}\|f&-g\|_{1}=\|h\cdot h_{1}-\widecheck{\varphi}\cdot\widecheck{\psi}\|_{1}=\|h\cdot h_{1}-h\cdot\widecheck{\varphi}\|_{1}+\|h\cdot\widecheck{\varphi}-\widecheck{\varphi}\cdot\widecheck{\psi}\|_{1}\\
&\leq\|h\|_{2}\cdot\|h_{1}-\widecheck{\varphi}\|_{2}+\|h-\widecheck{\psi}\|_{2}\cdot\|\widecheck{\varphi}\|_{2}=\|h\|_{2}\cdot\|\widehat{h_{1}}-\varphi\|_{2}+\|\widehat{h}-\psi\|_{2}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{2}\\
&\leq\|h\|_{2}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|h\|_{2}+2}+\|\varphi\|_{2}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|\varphi\|_{2}+2}\leq\varepsilon\ .\end{split}

Consider finally the case that
f=h\cdot h_{1}
is non-negative. Then we can choose
h=h_{1}\geq 0
. Pick some

7.
Measures

In this section we review the basics of Radon measures and translation bounded measures, discuss the connection between translation boundedness and the dual Wiener algebra, and discuss mild measures.

7.1.
Radon measures

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on a locally compact (Hausdorff) abelian group
G
is called a Radon measure if it is locally finite and inner regular, i.e., if
\mu(K)<\infty
for any compact
K\subseteq G
and if
\mu(A)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subseteq A\text{ compact}\}
for any Borel set
A\subseteq G
. More generally, a complex Borel measure
\mu
on
G
is called a complex Radon measure if its four components are Radon measures (that is, the positive and negative parts of its real and imaginary part). When we speak of a measure in this article, we will always mean a complex Radon measure.
Let
\mathcal{M}(G)
denote the space of measures on
G
. According to the Riesz representation theorem, a linear map
\mu:{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)
if and only if for every compact
K\subseteq G
there exists a constant
C_{K}
such that
|\mu(f)|\leq C_{K}\|f\|_{\infty}
for every
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
such that
\supp(f)\subseteq K
. In fact
\mathcal{M}(G)
is the topological dual of
({\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G),\tau_{ind})
, where the inductive limit topology
\tau_{ind}
is defined as follows.
Consider
{\bm{C}}_{K}(G)=\{f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G):\supp(f)\subseteq K\}
for compact
K\subseteq G
, and equip
{\bm{C}}_{K}(G)
with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,K}
.
Then the inductive limit topology
\tau_{ind}
is generated by the seminorms on
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
whose restrictions to
({\bm{C}}_{K}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty,K})
are continuous, see e.g.
[
65
, Ch. II.6]
.
Similarly, the topological dual of
({{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
coincides with the space
M(G)
of finite Radon measures. To any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)
we can assign
|\mu|\in\mathcal{M}(G)
by
|\mu|(f)=\sup\{|\mu(f)|:g\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G),|g|\leq f\}
for
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
. This measure is the total variation measure associated to
\mu
.

7.2.
Translation bounded measures

Recall that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)

\|\mu\|_{K}=\sup_{x\in G}|\mu|(x+K)

K\subseteq G

[
1
, p. 5]

The condition
\|\mu\|_{K}<\infty
for any compact

is equivalent to
\mu*f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
for any
f\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
, see
[
1
, Thm. 1.1]
and
[
28
, Ch. 13]
, or
[
50
, Prop. 4.9.21]

2

2

In fact

Theorem 7.1

For any

Fix some BUPU

We will first show that

Conversely, consider any

7.3.
Mild measures

In order to study the relation between measures and mild distributions as introduced in Section
5

Definition 7.6
.

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)
is called mild if there exists a mild distribution
\sigma\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(G)
such that
\mu(f)=\sigma(f)
for all

G

\mathcal{M}_{m}(G)
, and we denote by
\iota:\mathcal{M}_{m}(G)\to{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(G)

Remark 7.7
.

By denseness of
{\bm{KL}}(G)={\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\cap{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)

and
({{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)})

\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)
and the corresponding mild distribution

Any translation bounded measure is mild. In fact the following result states that

8.1.
Fourier transformable measures

By Bochner’s theorem

In order to extend the latter theory, Argabright and Gil de Lamadrid asked when a measure admits a Fourier transform that is a measure
[

Definition 8.1

[

, Prop. 3.9]
A measure

is called AG transformable if there exists a measure

and is called the Fourier transform of

. The space of AG transformable measures on

Some properties of AG transformable measures are discussed in

We thus have the following simpler characterisation of AG transformability.

Lemma 8.2

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(G)

9.
Diffraction theory revisited

Here we review how to associate to a translation bounded measure its diffraction measure. For ease of presentation, we restrict to second countable LCA groups. In that case, we can average translation bounded measures on van Hove sequences. An extension to arbitrary LCA groups using van Hove nets
[
52
]
is straightforward. Whereas this setting is well studied
[
30
,
3
,
AO1
,
60
,
43
]
, our arguments treat translation bounded measures systematically as elements of the Wiener algebra dual. This streamlines the previous approaches and also indicates extensions to the mild distribution setting and to the tempered distribution setting
[
70
]
.

9.1.
Wiener diagram for a finite sample

The physicist’s recipe to describe kinematic diffraction of a piece of matter starts with a finite measure
\omega\in\mathcal{M}(G)
, which might be a Dirac comb of the finitely many positions of the atoms or an absolutely continuous measure describing the mass distribution. Its Fourier–Stieltjes transform
\hbox{\hskip 4.16667pt\hskip-3.11226pt\hbox{$\omega$}\hskip-3.11226pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 4.16667pt}\in B(G)
is a continuous function, whose squared modulus is the intensity of the diffraction at the given position in reciprocal space
\widehat{G}
. For sufficiently large finite subsets of a
lattice

L
, i.e., of a Delone subgroup of
G
, its diffraction intensity function peaks at the
dual lattice

L^{\circ}
, the annihilator of
L
in
\widehat{G}
.
The above approach is summarised in the lower left part of a so-called Wiener diagram.

\begin{CD}\omega @>{*}>{}>\omega*\widetilde{\omega}\\
@V{\widehat{}\,}V{}V@V{}V{\,\widehat{}}V\\
\hbox{\hskip 4.16667pt\hskip-3.11226pt\hbox{$\omega$}\hskip-3.11226pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 4.16667pt}@>{|\cdot|^{2}}>{}>\hbox{\hskip 9.50114pt\hskip-9.50114pt\hbox{$\omega*\widetilde{\omega}$}\hskip-9.50114pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{18.22235pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 9.50114pt}\end{CD}

The Wiener diagram displays that there is an alternative way to compute the diffraction via the upper right part of the Wiener diagram, by the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation of
\omega
, i.e., of the convolution of
\omega
with its twisted version
\widetilde{\omega}
, where
\widetilde{\omega}(f)=\overline{\omega(\widetilde{f})}
and
\widetilde{f}(x)=\overline{f(-x)}
. When working with a mass distribution, the associated autocorrelation function is sometimes called the Patterson function in the physics literature.

Mathematical diffraction analysis often uses an infinite idealisation of the given finite sample. The diffraction of the infinite idealisation then approximates the diffraction of the finite sample, by continuity of the Fourier transform. However when dealing with unbounded measures in the above Wiener diagram, the operations of convolving, transforming and squaring may no longer be well defined. Thus the above approach needs an appropriate modification.

9.2.
Autocorrelation measure

The infinite idealisation is usually described by a translation bounded measure
\omega\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
, see e.g.
[
30
,
3
,
AO1
,
59
]
. In particular, any uniformly discrete point set gives rise to a translation bounded measure via its associated Dirac comb. In euclidean space, one is typically interested in infinite point sets where the number of points in a ball grows like the volume of the ball. The diffraction measure associated to
\omega
is then defined through properly normalised finite sample approximations.

Take a van Hove sequence
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and consider the finite sample measures
\omega_{n}=\omega|_{A_{n}}
.
These give rise to positive definite finite autocorrelation measures

\gamma_{n}=\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\omega_{n}*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\ .

Normalising by
m_{G}(A_{n})
ensures that the sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
({\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G),{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\bm{W}})
. Then the Banach-Alaoglu theorem ensures that a subsequence of
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the weak*-topology. We will arrive at this result in Proposition
9.3
and first give an estimate on the growth of a van Hove sequence in the
W
-norm.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a van Hove sequence in
G
and consider the space
({\bm{W}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}})
. Let
\varphi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
be such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and let
K=\supp(\varphi)
. Then there exists a finite constant
C
such that

\frac{\|1_{A_{n}-K}*\varphi\|_{{\bm{W}}}}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\leq C

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Abbreviate
K=\supp(\varphi)
. Take a BUPU
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
centered at a Delone set
\Lambda\subseteq G
and abbreviate
I_{n}=\{i\in I:\supp(\phi_{i})\cap(A_{n}-K+K)\neq\varnothing\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We then have

\begin{split}\|1_{A_{n}}*\varphi\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}&=\sum_{i\in I_{n}}\|(1_{A_{n}}*\varphi)\cdot\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i\in I_{n}}\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq M\cdot\card(\Lambda\cap(A_{n}-K+K))\ .\end{split}

Since
(A_{n})
is a van Hove sequence, and
\Lambda
is uniformly discrete, we have

\limsup_{n}\frac{\card(\Lambda\cap(A_{n}-K+K))}{m_{G}(A_{n})}<\infty\,.

The claim is now obvious.

Boundedness of the sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is expressed in the following result.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any van Hove sequence in
G
and consider any
\omega\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
. Then there exists some weak*-compact set
K\subseteq{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
, such that
\gamma_{n}\in K
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and such that the weak*-topology is metrisable on
K
.

Our arguments use the auxiliary measures
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
given by

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\omega*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\ .

Note
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
as the convolution of a compactly supported measure and a translation bounded measure is translation bounded.
Fix a BUPU
\Phi
in
{\bm{W}}(G)
on some Delone set
\Lambda\subseteq G
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}
and associated translation invariant norm
\|\cdot\|_{\bm{W}}
.
Let
\varphi\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
m_{G}(\varphi)=1
. Let
K=\supp(\varphi)
and define
f_{n}=1_{A_{n}-K}*\varphi\in{\bm{W}}(G)
. Then
f_{n}\geq 1_{A_{n}}
by construction. For arbitrary
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
we can now estimate

\begin{split}|\omega&*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}(f)|=\left|\omega(\omega_{n}*f)\right|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|\omega_{n}*f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\\
&\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|f\|_{\bm{W}}\cdot|\omega_{n}|(G)\leq C\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left||\omega|\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot|\omega|(f_{n})\\
&\leq C\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left||\omega|\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}^{2}\cdot\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot\|f_{n}\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\leq C^{\prime}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left||\omega|\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}^{2}\cdot\|f\|_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\cdot m_{G}(A_{n})\ ,\end{split}

where we used Lemma
7.3
for the second inequality, Lemma
4.7
for the third inequality, and Lemma
9.1
for the last inequality. As
f\in{\bm{W}}(G)
was arbitrary, we thus have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\omega*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\leq C^{\prime}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left||\omega|\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}^{2}\ .

Thus
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\in K
for the set
K\subseteq{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
defined by

K=\left\{\sigma\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G):{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\sigma\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}\leq C^{\prime}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left||\omega|\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}},\Phi}^{2}\right\}\ .

Note that
K
is weak*-compact by Alaouglu’s theorem. Moreover the weak*-topology is metrisable on
K
as
{\bm{W}}(G)
is separable (recalling that
G
is assumed to be second countable). In fact also
\gamma_{n}\in K
, which follows from the estimate on
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
through

\begin{split}|\omega_{n}*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}(f)|&=\left|\omega_{n}(\omega_{n}*f)\right|\leq|\omega_{n}|(|\omega_{n}*f|)\leq|\omega|(|\omega_{n}*f|)\ .\end{split}

We can summarize our arguments as follows.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any van Hove sequence in
G
. Consider arbitrary
\omega\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
and define
\omega_{n}=\omega|_{A_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists some
\gamma\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
and a subsequence
(n_{k})
such that

\gamma=\lim_{k}\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n_{k}})}\omega_{n_{k}}*\widetilde{\omega_{n_{k}}}

in the weak*-topology of
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
.
∎

In the following, we thus assume that
\omega\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
admits an autocorrelation
\gamma_{\omega}\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
along some van Hove sequence
\mathcal{A}
, i.e., we have the weak*-limit

\gamma_{\omega}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\omega_{n}*\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\ .

We write
\gamma_{\omega}=\omega\circledast_{\mathcal{A}}\widetilde{\omega}
and call
\gamma_{\omega}
the Eberlein convolution of
\omega
along
\mathcal{A}
, compare
[
43
]
.

9.3.
Diffraction measure

By construction, any autocorrelation
\gamma_{\omega}
is a positive definite translation bounded measure.
Thus its Fourier transform exists as a mild distribution and is positive. By Proposition
7.10
we have
\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(\widehat{G})
, i.e., the Fourier transform is a translation bounded measure on
\widehat{G}
. The measure

\gamma_{\omega}

⋀

is called the diffraction measure of
\omega
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
.

Note that the Fourier transform on the cone of positive definite translation bounded measures is vaguely continuous by
[
6
, Thm. 4.16]
. Moreover, recalling
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(G)
, we note that the Fourier transform on
({{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(G),{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}})
is weak*-continuous by Theorem
6.5
. We thus have
\widehat{\gamma_{n}}(h)\to\widehat{\gamma_{\omega}}(h)
for every
h\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
and every
h\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(\widehat{G})
.
In this sense, the diffraction measure approximates the diffraction of a large finite sample
\omega_{n}
.

The diffraction measure can be decomposed into a discrete part and a continuous part. We write
\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}=(\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt})_{d}+(\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt})_{c}
. Then
\omega\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
is called pure point diffractive along
\mathcal{A}
if
\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}=(\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt})_{d}
.

9.4.
Weak topologies on translation bounded measures

Translation bounded measures, i.e., elements of
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)={\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
, may be evaluated on various test function spaces
{\bm{T}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
such as
{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
,
{\bm{KL}}(G)
,
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
or
{\bm{W}}(G)
. One might also consider the Schwartz–Bruhat space
{\bm{\mathcal{S}}}(G)
of test functions for tempered distributions or, in euclidean space, the space
{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}(G)={\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}^{\infty}(G)
of test functions for distributions.
Let us consider the associated topologies
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})
of pointwise convergence on
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
with respect to
{\bm{T}}(G)
. For example, the topology
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{W}})
is the weak*-topology on
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
, whereas the topology
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}})
is the trace topology of the weak*-topology on
\mathcal{M}(G)=({\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G))^{\prime}
, also called the vague topology. We have the following result.

Proposition 9.4
.

For
{\bm{T}}(G)\in\{{\bm{KL}}(G),{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(G),{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G),{\bm{W}}(G)\}
or
{\bm{T}}(G)\in\{{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}(G),{\bm{\mathcal{S}}}(G)\}
consider the topology
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})
of pointwise convergence on
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
with respect to
{\bm{T}}(G)
. Let
B_{r}=\{\mu\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G):{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}}(G)}\leq r\}
be any closed
r
-ball in
{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
. Then all the above topologies
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})
have the same trace on
B_{r}\subseteq{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)
.

This result shows that the traditional definition of an autocorrelation agrees with the above approach. Indeed, the topology on translation bounded measures from
[
30
,
3
]
coincides with the above topology by Proposition
7.4
, and weak*-convergence on balls is equivalent to vague convergence on balls by Proposition
9.4
. Adopting the traditional viewpoint, we have an alternative argument for
[
3
, Thm. 2]
. We also observe that any vague autocorrelation limit is even weak*-convergent. Moreover, it suffices to check weak*-convergence or vague convergence on test functions from
{\bm{KL}}(G)
or from
{\bm{\mathcal{S}}}(G)
or, in euclidean space, from
{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}(G)
.

The proof of Proposition
9.4
relies on Alaoglu’s theorem, in conjunction with a well-known fact from topology, compare
[
44
, Lem. 2.23]
.

Lemma 9.5
.

[
46
, Ch. I, 3A]
Assume that
(X,\tau_{1})
is Hausdorff and that
(X,\tau_{2})
is compact. Then
\tau_{1}\subseteq\tau_{2}
implies
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
. ∎

Let
B_{r}
be any closed
r
-ball in
({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}}})
. Note that we have
{\bm{T}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
for
{\bm{T}}\in\{{\bm{KL}},{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}},{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}},{\bm{\mathcal{D}}},{\bm{\mathcal{S}}}\}
, which implies
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})|_{B_{r}}\subseteq\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{W}})|_{B_{r}}
for all such
{\bm{T}}
.
Also,
(B_{r},\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})|_{B_{r}})
is Hausdorff for all such
{\bm{T}}
. Using standard arguments for measures, this follows from the property that for every compact
K\subseteq G
there exists some
f\in{\bm{T}}(G)
such that
1_{K}\leq f
, for all such
{\bm{T}}
.
As the topological space
(B_{r},\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{W}})|_{B_{r}})
is compact by Alaoglu’s theorem, we obtain
\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{T}})|_{B_{r}}=\sigma({\bm{W}}^{\prime},{\bm{W}})|_{B_{r}}
for all such
{\bm{T}}
by Lemma
9.5
.

9.5.
Wiener diagram for Besicovitch almost periodic measures

We ask whether there exists a modified Wiener diagram for the infinite idealisation
\omega\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
.
As in Section
9.2
above, consider normalised Fourier transforms of
\omega_{n}
along a van Hove sequence
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n})
. For any
\chi\in\widehat{G}
, we might then ask for existence of the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m_{G}(A_{n})}\widehat{\omega_{n}}(\chi)=a^{\mathcal{A}}_{\chi}(\omega)\ ,

i.e., we might ask for existence of the Fourier–Bohr coefficients of
\omega
along
\mathcal{A}
. For the following discussion, we restrict to measures that are pure point diffractive along
\mathcal{A}
. We thus restrict to measures that are mean almost periodic along
\mathcal{A}
, see
[
28
,
50
,
43
,
41
]
for background of various notions of almost periodicity with respect to pure point diffractivity.

For concreteness, consider the class
\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
of twice AG transformable measures. Such measures are indeed pure point diffractive, as AG transformability implies weak almost periodicity
[
50
, Thm. 4.10.4]
, which implies pure point diffractivity
[
44
, Thm. 4.4]
. For
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
, both its autocorrelation measure and its Fourier–Bohr coefficients are independent of the choice of the van Hove sequence
\mathcal{A}
. The relation
a^{\mathcal{A}}_{\chi}(\omega)=\widehat{\omega}(\{\chi\})
then suggests a Wiener diagram where left down arrow corresponds to taking the discrete part of the AG transform
\widehat{\omega}
, and where
|\cdot|^{2}
means taking the absolute square of the Fourier–Bohr coefficients. For
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
we can thus write

\begin{CD}\omega @>{\circledast}>{}>\gamma_{\omega}\\
@V{(\widehat{\hskip 3.01389pt})_{d}\,}V{}V@V{}V{\,\widehat{}}V\\
(\widehat{\omega})_{d}@>{|\cdot|^{2}}>{}>\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}\end{CD}

If
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
is even strongly almost periodic, then
(\widehat{\omega})_{d}=\widehat{\omega}
, and both down arrows in the above Wiener diagram correspond to the AG transform.
A simple explicit example is
\omega=\delta_{L}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
the Dirac comb of a lattice
L
. Its autocorrelation is
\gamma_{\omega}=\dens(L)\cdot\delta_{L}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
, and we have
\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}=\dens(L)^{2}\cdot\delta_{L^{\circ}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
. In fact
\widehat{\omega}=\dens(L)\cdot\delta_{L^{\circ}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
, which shows that
\omega
is strongly almost periodic. A simple example of a weakly almost periodic measure that is not strongly almost periodic is
\omega=\delta_{L}+\delta_{0}
. More generally, one might take
\omega=\mu_{s}+\mu_{0}
with
\mu_{s}
a strongly almost periodic measure and
\mu_{0}
a finite measure. We will give further examples of strongly almost periodic twice transformable measures below, see the discussion of weighted model sets in Section
10.2
.

More generally, consider the class
\mathcal{B}\hskip-1.0pt{\mathsf{ap}}_{\mathcal{A}}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
of translation bounded measures that are Besicovitch almost periodic along
\mathcal{A}
in the sense of
[
43
, Def. 3.30]
. Examples are Dirac combs of model sets of maximal density along
\mathcal{A}
, compare
[
43
, Sec. 3.5]
. In that case the autocorrelation and the Fourier–Bohr coefficients of
\omega
along
\mathcal{A}
indeed exist. In fact, for
\omega\in\mathcal{B}\hskip-1.0pt{\mathsf{ap}}_{\mathcal{A}}(G)
the Wiener diagram

\begin{CD}\omega @>{\circledast_{\mathcal{A}}}>{}>\gamma_{\omega}\\
@V{FB_{\mathcal{A}}\,}V{}V@V{}V{\,\widehat{}}V\\
(a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\omega))_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}@>{|\cdot|^{2}}>{}>\hbox{\hskip 5.35753pt\hskip-5.35753pt\hbox{$\gamma_{\omega}$}\hskip-5.35753pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{15.30566pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 5.35753pt}\end{CD}

commutes, where
FB_{\mathcal{A}}
means taking Fourier–Bohr coefficients along
\mathcal{A}
, and where
|\cdot|^{2}
means taking the absolute square of the Fourier–Bohr coefficients
[
43
, Thm. 3.36]
. For the subclass of so-called Weyl almost periodic measures, the above limits are independent of
\mathcal{A}
by
[
43
, Thm. 4.16]
. Examples are given by weakly almost periodic measures
[
43
, Cor. 4.26]
and hence in particular by the doubly transformable measures considered above. Note that for Besicovitch almost periodic measures,
\widehat{\omega}\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})
exists as a mild distribution, but might not be a measure.
Also note that the Fourier–Bohr series
(a_{\chi}^{\mathcal{A}}(\omega))_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}
might not define a measure or a mild distribution. Moreover if it did, the corresponding mild distribution need not coincide with the mild distribution
\widehat{\omega}
.

Also note that, for a translation bounded measure
\omega\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
that is pure point diffractive along
\mathcal{A}
, commutativity of the above Wiener diagram characterises Besicovitch almost periodicity along
\mathcal{A}
by
[
43
, Thm. 3.36]
. The above Wiener diagram fails to be commutative for the pure point diffractive Dirac comb
\omega=\delta_{\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
on the van Hove sequence where
A_{n}=[-n,n]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For another simple class of pure point diffractive counterexamples see
[
43
, Prop. A.2]
.

10.
Pure point diffraction and Poisson summation revisited

In the following, we specialise to weighted model sets. We will obtain extended versions of results from
[
59
]
, using straightforward proofs based on mild distributions. Throughout this section, we consider LCA groups
G,H
such that
\mathcal{L}\subseteq G\times H
for some lattice
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., a discrete co-compact subgroup of
G\times H
.

10.1.
Weighted model sets that are AG transformable

Weighted model sets in
G
are supported on projected lattice points. For a weight function
h:H\to\mathbb{C}
consider the formal sum

\omega_{h}=\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}}h(y)\cdot\delta_{x}\ .

We are interested in weight functions that result in translation bounded measures
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
. This is the case for the continuous weight functions from the Wiener algebra.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
G,H
be two LCA groups and let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice in
G\times H
. Then, there exists a finite constant
C=C(\mathcal{L})
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\omega_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}}}\leq C\cdot\|h\|_{{\bm{W}}}

for all
h\in{\bm{W}}(H)
. In particular, for all
h\in{\bm{W}}(H)
we have
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
.

Note that
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\delta_{\ell}
satisfies
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G\times H)
as
\mathcal{L}
is a Delone set in
G\times H
.
Fix BUPUs
\Phi
on
G
and
\Psi
on
H
. Fix
h\in{\bm{W}}(H)
and consider arbitrary
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
. We then have
g\otimes h\in{\bm{W}}(G\times H)
by Lemma
4.6
.
Using the product BUPU
\Phi\otimes\Psi
on
G\times H
, compare Lemma
2.10
, we get that

\begin{split}|\omega_{h}(g)|&=|\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(g\otimes h)|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\delta_{\mathcal{L}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}}(G\times H),\Phi\otimes\Psi}\cdot\|g\otimes h\|_{{\bm{W}}(G\times H),\Phi\otimes\Psi}\\
&\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\delta_{\mathcal{L}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\bm{W}}(G\times H),\Phi\otimes\Psi}\cdot\|g\|_{{\bm{W}}(G),\Phi}\cdot\|h\|_{{\bm{W}}(H),\Psi}\ ,\end{split}

compare Lemma
4.6
.
As
g\in{\bm{W}}(G)
was arbitrary, this shows
\omega_{h}\in{\bm{W}}^{\prime}(G)=\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
.

We are especially interested in translation bounded measures
\omega_{h}
that allow for Fourier analysis based on the Poisson summation formula for the underlying lattice
\mathcal{L}
. This idea has been followed for AG transformable measures in
[
59
]
, which also contains a review of the diffraction theory literature, and for tempered distributions in euclidean space in
[
AO1
]
. Here we note that the Poisson summation formula for a discrete co-compact subgroup of
G
does hold not only on
{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
, but also on the larger Segal algebra
{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{\bm{W}}(G)
given by

{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)=\{h\in{\bm{W}}(G):\widehat{h}\in{\bm{W}}(\widehat{G})\}

with norm
\|f\|_{{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)}=\|f\|_{{\bm{W}}(G)}+\|\widehat{f}\|_{{\bm{W}}(\widehat{G})}
, see
[
10
]
,
[
17
, Rem. 15]
,
[
33
, p. 1617]
. This yields the following result, which refines
[
49
, Eqn. (7.15)]
,
[
AO1
, Lem. 9.3]
,
[
59
, Thm. 4.10]
,
[
47
, Thm. 4.2]
and
[
48
, Thm. 2.10]
.

Theorem 10.2

(PSF for weighted model sets)
.

Consider LCA groups
G,H
, and let
\mathcal{L}
be a discrete co-compact subgroup in
G\times H
. Then, for all
g\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
and all
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
we have

\omega_{h}(g)=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{\widecheck{h}}(\widecheck{g})\,.

(10.1)

In particular, for all
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
we have
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
and
\widehat{\omega_{h}}=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{\widecheck{h}}
. Hence both
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
and
\widehat{\omega_{h}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
are strongly almost periodic measures for
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
.

We infer from Lemma
10.1
that the formal sums
\omega_{h}
and
\omega_{\widecheck{h}}
are translation bounded measures for all
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
. Let now
g\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
be arbitrary. Then
g\otimes h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\otimes{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)\subseteq{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G\times H)
by Lemma
4.6
. Since the Poisson summation formula for
\mathcal{L}
holds for test functions in
{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G\times H)
, see
[
33
, p. 1617]
, and since
\widecheck{g\otimes h}=\widecheck{g}\otimes\widecheck{h}
, we have

\omega_{h}(g)=\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(g\otimes h)=\dens(\mathcal{L})\cdot\delta_{\mathcal{L}^{0}}(\widecheck{g}\otimes\widecheck{h})=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{\widecheck{h}}(\widecheck{g})\,,

with all sums being absolutely convergent by Theorem
7.1
. This proves Eqn. (
10.1
). Fix now
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
. Noting
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
,
\omega_{\widecheck{h}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(\widehat{G})
and validity of Eqn. (
10.1
) for all
g\in{{\bm{S}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)\subseteq{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(G)
, the transformability claim follows from Theorem
8.8
(c). Strong almost periodicity follows from
[
50
, Cor. 4.10.13]
.

Using the Poisson summation formula, we obtain the following extended version of the density formula, by a proof exactly as in
[
59
, Prop. 4.13]
. The density formula for Riemann integrable weight functions then follows from a standard approximation argument, see e.g.
[
59
, Thm. 4.14]
.

Proposition 10.3

(Density formula for weighted model sets)
.

Assume that
G
is
\sigma
-compact and that
\pi^{H}(\mathcal{L})
is dense in
H
. Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any van Hove sequence in
G
. We then have for
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{h}(t+A_{n})}{m_{G}(A_{n})}=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{\mathcal{L}})\cdot\int_{H}h\,{\rm d}m_{H}\ ,

where the convergence is uniform in
t\in G
. ∎

With
c=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{\mathcal{L}})
we can argue

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{h}(t+A_{n})}{m_{G}(A_{n})}=\widehat{\omega_{h}}(\{e\})=c\cdot\omega_{\widecheck{h}}(\{e\})=c\cdot\widecheck{h}(e)=c\cdot\int_{H}h\,{\rm d}m_{H}\

uniformly in
t\in G
. Here the first equation holds by Proposition
8.12
, and the second one is the Poisson summation formula Theorem
10.2
.

10.2.
Diffraction of AG transformable weighted model sets

Versions of the following result have been proved for Riemann integrable weight functions
[
59
, Prop. 5.1]
and for continuous weight functions that are called admissible in
[
40
, Thm. 3.2]
. Here we treat continuous weight functions from
{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
. Instead of adapting the direct proof of
[
59
, Prop. 5.1]
that uses the density formula, we prefer a more general argument from
[
44
]
that is based on almost periodicity.

Proposition 10.4
.

Assume that
G
is
\sigma
-compact and that
\pi^{H}(\mathcal{L})
is dense in
H
. Assume that
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
. Then the weighted model set
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
has a unique autocorrelation measure
\gamma=\omega_{h}\circledast\widetilde{\omega_{h}}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
which is given by
\gamma=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{h*\widetilde{h}}
.

Let
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
. As
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is a strongly almost periodic measure by Theorem
10.2
, it has a unique autocorrelation
\gamma=\omega_{h}\circledast\widetilde{\omega_{h}}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
, see
[
44
, Thm. 7.6]
. Its Fourier transform
\widehat{\gamma}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(\widehat{G})
is a pure point measure, which can be evaluated at
\chi\in\widehat{G}
via
\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=|a_{\chi}(\omega_{h})|^{2}
, where
a_{\chi}(\omega_{h})
is the Fourier–Bohr coefficient of
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
at
\chi
, compare
[
44
, Thm. 7.6]
,
[
50
, Thm. 4.10.14]
and Eqn. (
8.1
). We thus have

\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=\left|\widehat{\omega_{h}}(\{\chi\})\right|^{2}=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})^{2}\cdot\left|\omega_{\widecheck{h}}(\{\chi\})\right|^{2}

for all
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.
In particular, we get
\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=0
if
\chi\notin\pi^{\widehat{G}}(\mathcal{L}^{\circ})
. If
(\chi,\eta)\in\mathcal{L}^{\circ}
, then we get
\widehat{\gamma}(\{\chi\})=\dens(\mathcal{L})^{2}\cdot\left|\widecheck{h}(\eta)\right|^{2}
. Here we used that
\mathcal{L}^{\circ}
projects injectively to
\widehat{G}
, which follows from the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
projects densely to
H
by Pontryagin duality.
Noting
h*\widetilde{h}\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
, we get by Theorem 6.2 that
\dens(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{h*\widetilde{h}}
is AG transformable and has the same Fourier transform as
\gamma
. Hence
\gamma=\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{h*\widetilde{h}}
.

10.3.
Discussion of results

For any weight function
h\in{{\bm{W}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)
, the weighted model set comb
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
is a strongly almost periodic measure. In that case, the Wiener diagram from Section
9.5
has the following explicit form:

\begin{CD}\omega_{h}@>{\circledast}>{}>\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{h*\widetilde{h}}\\
@V{\widehat{}\,}V{}V@V{}V{\,\widehat{}}V\\
\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega_{\widecheck{h}}@>{|\cdot|^{2}}>{}>\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{L})^{2}\cdot\omega_{|\widecheck{h}|^{2}}\end{CD}

All objects in the above diagram can be interpreted as translation bounded measures, compare Theorem
10.2
. Also note that, on the level of the weight functions, the above Wiener diagram reflects the Wiener diagram for finite measures from Section
9.1
.

Remark 10.5
.

The above result extends
[
59
, Rem. 5.6]
. In addition, it applies to weight functions from the Schwartz–Bruhat class and thus includes the Gelfand-Shilov functions, which were used in
[
8
]
for Fourier analysis of model sets in euclidean cut-and-project schemes.

For appropriate weight functions, weighted model sets
\omega_{h}
provide additional examples of Besicovitch almost periodic measures. Let us first consider examples that are strongly almost periodic translation bounded measures (and hence Besicovitch almost periodic, compare Section
9.5
). Such examples arise from continuous weight functions and already appear implicitly in de Bruijn’s work, see
[
8
,
9
]
and the recent discussion in
[
38
]
. If
h\in{\bm{C}}_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
, then
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is strongly almost periodic
[
40
]
. In that case we have
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{dT}(G)
if and only if
\widecheck{h}\in{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{H})
, see
[
59
, Thm. 5.3]
. In particular, we get examples that are not AG transformable.
More generally, so-called admissble
h\in{{\bm{C}}_{\negthinspace 0}}(H)\cap{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(H)
result in
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
being strongly almost periodic, see
[
40
, Thm. 3.1]
and
[
40
, Prop. 6.7]
.

If the weight function fails to be continuous, then weighted model sets will usually fail to be strongly almost periodic. Consider for example weak model sets of so-called maximal density, see
[
43
, Sec. 3.5]
. In that case
h=1_{W}
is the characteristic function of the model set window
W
, which is assumed to be compact. To be specific, assume that
W
has nonempty interior and is Riemann measurable. Then
\omega_{h}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is almost automorphic
[
35
, Thm. 1]
. Moreover typically
\widecheck{1_{W}}\notin{{\bm{L}}^{1}}(\widehat{H})
, such that AG transformability might be violated.

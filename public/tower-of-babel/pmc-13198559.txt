Evaluation of Injection Site Pain and Adherence in Patients Transitioning from a High to Low Volume Adalimumab Formulation (AVT02, Simlandi®) Across Multiple Indications (EASE PAIN)
Authors: Dara K. Shahrokh, Ronald B. Vender, Charles W. Lynde, Jaggi Rao, Waqqas Afif, A. Hillary Steinhart, Neeraj Narula, Evelyn Sutton, Carter Thorne, Louis Bessette
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionInjection site pain (ISP) is a frequent concern with subcutaneous adalimumab therapy and may negatively affect treatment adherence and patient satisfaction. AVT02 is a high-concentration, low-volume (40 mg/0.4 mL), citrate-free biosimilar of reference product (RP) adalimumab (40 mg/0.8 mL; citrate-containing). The EASE PAIN study evaluated the real-world impact of switching from adalimumab RP or another biosimilar to AVT02 on ISP and patient-reported outcomes over 180 days.MethodsThe EASE PAIN study (NCT05913817) was a national, observational, prospective phase IV study with a 6-month follow-up. The study enrolled Canadian patients with gastrointestinal conditions (Crohn’s disease [CD], ulcerative colitis [UC]), rheumatological conditions (rheumatoid arthritis [RA], ankylosing spondylitis [AS], psoriatic arthritis [PsA]) or dermatological conditions (hidradenitis suppurativa [HS], psoriasis [PsO]). Participants were eligible if their treating physician had already decided to switch them from a high-volume RP or alternative adalimumab biosimilar to low-volume AVT02. The study assessed ISP measured via the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), adherence via the compliance rate, patient satisfaction and perception of change in pain via the Likert scale, injection site reactions (ISR) via a checklist (bleeding, burning, erythema, itching, soreness, swelling), quality of life based on EQ-5D-5L, and disease activity via the patient and physician global assessment scores for participants up to day 180 after switching. Healthcare utilization was measured as per the frequency, type, and volume of medical services accessed.ResultsThe intention-to-treat (ITT) population comprised 324 participants. Following the first administration of AVT02, mean ISP VAS scores improved by − 19.9 ± 26.1 mm compared with baseline. Adherence rate was 93.4% overall. More than 74.4% of patients reported being mostly or completely satisfied with treatment, and 76.9% of the participants perceived AVT02 as less painful as measured by the 5-Likert scale. Moreover, a lower number of patients experienced ISRs after their first dose of AVT02 (36 patients; 12.4%) compared with their last dose of high-volume adalimumab (124 patients; 42.3%). Quality-of-life scores remained stable throughout follow-up. No clinically meaningful changes were observed in disease activity or healthcare utilization.ConclusionsIn this real-world Canadian study, switching from adalimumab RP or alternative biosimilar to AVT02 was associated with reductions in ISP and reactions while maintaining high adherence, patient satisfaction, quality of life, and disease control across multiple indications.Trial RegistrationThis trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05913817.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40744-026-00846-1.

Introduction

Immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs) are a diverse group of conditions in which a dysregulated immune system leads to chronic inflammation in tissues including those found in the gastrointestinal system, joints, and skin [1]. In 2019, there were approximately 67.6 million new cases globally of six key IMIDs (rheumatoid arthritis (RA), asthma, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), psoriasis, multiple sclerosis, and atopic dermatitis), with an age-standardized incidence rate of approximately 908.7 per 100,000. In Western society, the overall prevalence of IMIDs is estimated to be between 5% and 7% [2, 3]. Advances in biologic therapies have transformed the management of IMIDs by enabling targeted modulation of inflammatory pathways and sustained disease control across multiple indications with fewer side effects [4].

Subcutaneous administration is the most common route for biologic therapies in dermatology, rheumatology, and gastroenterology due to its convenience and suitability for long-term self-administration. However, injection site pain (ISP) and injection site reactions (ISRs) remain common concerns and may negatively influence patient satisfaction, adherence, and long-term treatment persistence. In addition to needle-related factors, formulation characteristics such as injection volume, buffer composition, and excipients have been shown to contribute to injection-related discomfort [5].

Adalimumab is a human immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) monoclonal antibody that targets tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) in its soluble and transmembrane forms and, thereby, neutralizes TNFα’s pro-inflammatory activity, which has been implicated in many inflammatory diseases [6, 7]. Reference product (RP) adalimumab (Humira®) was first approved in 2002 for patients with RA. In Canada, the first approval was granted in 2004 for the treatment of moderate to severe RA and has since been approved for the treatment of 12 indications [7]. Multiple biosimilars of Humira® have received Health Canada approval and have been available in Canada since 2022, contributing to increased market competition, reduced treatment costs, and improved patient access. A study by Bergman et al. showed that patients receiving a citrate-free adalimumab formulation had significantly greater adherence, fewer discontinuations, and longer time on treatment compared with those receiving a citrate-containing adalimumab formulation [8]. Moreover, in a randomized crossover study in patients with RA, it was demonstrated that in addition to the removal of citrate from the adalimumab formulation, the lower volume associated with the high adalimumab concentration formulation (40 mg/0.4 mL) is associated with less ISP compared with the citrate-containing high-volume formulation of adalimumab (40 mg/0.8 mL) [9].

AVT02 (Simlandi®, JAMP Pharma Corporation) is a biosimilar to RP adalimumab that was approved in Canada in 2022 and developed by Alvotech for treatment of RA, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, psoriatic arthritis (PsA), ankylosing spondylitis (AS), adult CD, adult UC, adult and adolescent HS, PsO, and adult and pediatric uveitis. Compared to RP adalimumab, which is available as a low-concentration, high-volume (40 mg/0.8 mL) citrate-containing formulation in Canada, AVT02 is a low-volume (40 mg/0.4 mL), citrate-free biosimilar of adalimumab with a lower number of non-medicinal excipients. Given the increasing use of biosimilars and the importance of patient experience in chronic therapy, real-world data reporting key factors in successful adoption of biosimilar products are an increasingly valuable part of the landscape in the management of IMIDs. The phase IV EASE PAIN study was conducted to assess ISP, tolerability, adherence, treatment satisfaction, quality of life, and disease activity among Canadian patients transitioning from reference or alternative adalimumab formulations to AVT02 across gastroenterological, rheumatological, and dermatological indications. The transitions were primarily driven by provincial biosimilar policies rather than clinical inefficacy or intolerance.

Methods

Study Design

EASE-PAIN trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05913817) was an observational, multicenter prospective study conducted in 13 gastroenterological, 26 rheumatological, and 21 dermatological centers across Canada. The study took place during the period when several Canadian provinces implemented mandatory non-medical switching policies from originator biologics to biosimilars. The study was carried out in compliance with the ethical standards of the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments, and Canadian regulations on prospective non-interventional studies. All participants were informed about the study details, and their voluntary consent for their participation and use of data was obtained before enrollment.

The study protocol (Protocol v2.0) was approved on March 3, 2023, by the independent Ethics Committee of Advarra (372 Hollandview Trail, Suite 300, Aurora, ON, L4G 0A5, Canada (Reference number Pro00069327)).

Study Population

Participants aged 18 years or older at the time of consent and who had been diagnosed with CD, UC, RA, AS, PsA, HS, or PsO at least 6 months before enrollment were eligible for the study. As per the criteria of the study, participants had to have been treated for at least 6 months prior to study screening with RP adalimumab or with another high-volume biosimilar and participants had to be about to transition to AVT02 on the basis of the decision of the treating physician.

Participants were eligible for the study if the treating physician wished to transition a participant from RP adalimumab to AVT02, if the treating physician wished to transition a participant from an alternative adalimumab biosimilar to AVT02, or if the participant had already transitioned from either RP adalimumab or an alternative biosimilar to AVT02 in the 3 months prior to the study enrollment.

The participants continued using the same type of injector when switching from their previous adalimumab treatment to AVT02 (i.e., prefilled syringe to prefilled syringe switch or autoinjector to autoinjector switch). Participants were further required to have access to commercial AVT02.

Excluded participants were those who were previously treated with AVT02 or another low-volume adalimumab biosimilar for more than 3 months, participants who had, in the opinion of the investigator, any condition that may impair their ability to participate in the study, who had a current or history of any condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, may pose an increased risk to the patient in whom treatment with adalimumab may be contraindicated. Also excluded were patients with known hypersensitivity to AVT02 or its excipients, or who were unable to secure reimbursement for AVT02.

The study had a planned dose-depletion period wherein patients were asked to use any outstanding doses of their existing high-volume adalimumab, required for baseline assessments, before switching to AVT02 treatment. No assessment was carried out during this period.

Study Treatment and Assessments

The maximum possible duration of study per participant was 300 days, which included up to 90 days of screening, followed by 180 ± 30 days of treatment and active observation. On the screening visit, baseline characteristics and full medical history including prior treatments, and relevant comorbidities were collected. Information related to injector type for adalimumab treatment and current adalimumab injection frequency were also recorded. Effectiveness of adalimumab therapy prior to switching to AVT02 was evaluated by the Physician Global Assessment (PGA), which is a 100-mm visual analogue scale (VAS) with lower scores (nearing 0) indicating lower disease and vice versa and available disease-specific assessments performed by the physician. The patient perception of treatment effectiveness was assessed by Patient Global Assessment of Disease Activity (PtGA), which is a 100-mm VAS with lower scores (nearing 0) indicating lower disease and vice versa. In addition to PtGA, available disease-specific patient-reported outcome measures were also used for evaluation.

After switching to AVT02 treatment, physician assessments were performed based on the routine care plan until day 180. Patient-reported outcome measures were assessed monthly until day 180 where patients completed self-administered questionnaires from home, online, or on paper. In addition, safety assessments for adverse event reporting and monitoring were done throughout the study.

The disease-specific assessments performed by the physician and the disease-specific patient-reported outcome measures considered for the study are reported in Supplementary Table S1.

The day of last dose, high-volume adalimumab was considered as baseline for evaluation of ISP using VAS (0–100) and ISR using a checklist that included bleeding, burning, erythema and/or itching, soreness, and swelling, both at 15 min after injection. During this evaluation, adalimumab injection frequency prior to switching to AVT02 was also recorded and was used as reference for initiation of AVT02 treatment. Throughout the study, the ISP and ISR assessments were done through the patient diary provided and collected during the patient visits to the clinic.

Participants switched from their previous adalimumab regimen to the AVT02 regimen based on their baseline injection frequency. On the first day of AVT02 treatment, participants assessed ISP, ISR values as above, and patient perception of change in ISP using a five-point Likert scale 15 min after AVT02 administration. ISP and ISR values 15 min after each dose administration were assessed up to day 180. For participants who were transitioned to AVT02 treatment within the previous 3 months before screening (n = 49; 15.1% of the cohort), the assessment values for ISP, ISR, and perceived change in ISP after first dose of AVT02 treatment were extracted from the patient chart, if available. If not available in the patient chart, participants were asked to recall these assessments, which is subject to recall bias.

In addition, monthly assessments from screening were performed for quality of life (QoL), the overall patient satisfaction, and healthcare resource utilization. Assessment of QoL was done using EQ-5D-5L, a questionnaire that assesses health-related QoL covering five dimensions (i.e., mobility, ability to self-care, ability to undertake usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression). Permission to use the EQ‑5D‑5L instrument was obtained from the EuroQol Research Foundation. Each dimension is assessed using five levels of severity (i.e., no problems, slight problems, moderate problems, severe problems, and extreme problems). Overall patient satisfaction was evaluated by a seven-point Likert scale. Healthcare resource utilization related to the patient indication, including medical events and treatment-emergent adverse events experienced by the participant in the last month as assessed through a questionnaire.

Patients could be discontinued from AVT02 treatment upon the advice of their treating physician. If discontinuation occurred prior to the end of the study (day 180), the discontinuation date and reason(s) for discontinuation were recorded, along with the appropriate patient assessment data collection.

Study Objectives and Endpoints

The primary objective of the study was to assess the impact on ISP levels in patients when switching from low-concentration RP adalimumab or another low-concentration biosimilar to AVT02 for the management of gastroenterological (CD or UC), rheumatologic diseases (RA, AS, or PsA) or dermatological conditions (HS or PsO), Accordingly, the primary endpoint of the study was the change from baseline in ISP (based on VAS 0–100 pain score) after the first dose of low-volume adalimumab AVT02.

Secondary objectives included evaluation of changes from baseline in ISP and ISR over 180 days, adherence rates assessed by reported missed doses, patient-perceived change in ISP following the first AVT02 dose, overall treatment satisfaction as measured by the 7-point Likert scale, QoL as reflected in EQ-5D-5L index scores [10], disease activity based on physician and patient global assessments [11, 12], healthcare utilization (frequency, type, and volume of medical services accessed), and safety outcomes. Safety endpoints included the incidence of ISRs, treatment discontinuations due to adverse events, treatment-emergent adverse events, and adverse events of special interest (including infections, malignancies, thromboembolic events, demyelinating disorders, and hypersensitivity reactions).

Statistical Analyses

Sample size requirements were based on the desired precision of the primary endpoint estimate, assessed using the width of the 95% confidence interval (CI). Assuming a mean change from baseline in ISP on the VAS (1–100) scale of − 25.0 with a standard deviation of 30.0 after switching to AVT02 treatment, a sample size of 600 patients was estimated to yield a 95% CI width of 0.24%, equivalent to 9.6% precision around the point estimate. The primary analysis population was the intent-to-treat (ITT) population comprising all patients who were enrolled in the study and had at least one dose of AVT02 (N = 324). Safety analysis was conducted on all patients that received at least one dose of AVT02. There was no per protocol analyses.

Given the observational nature of the study, all available observations were included in the analyses. Descriptive statistics were generated for all endpoints, and no imputation was performed for missing data. For evaluations through 180 days, data were grouped into time windows based on the number of days elapsed since baseline. Changes from baseline to each visit/time window were assessed for statistical significance using paired t tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for continuous variables, as appropriate, and McNemar’s chi-square test for categorical variables. No adjustments were made for multiplicity. All P values reported are nominal and should be interpreted descriptively, with emphasis on consistency rather than individual P values.

Results

Demographic Characterization of the Study Sample

A total of 354 patients were screened and 328 enrolled, of whom 324 comprised the safety and ITT populations (Fig. 1a). From the ITT population, 50 patients had gastroenterological indications (CD, UC), 166 patients had rheumatological indications (RA, AS, PsA), and 108 patients had dermatological indications (HS, PsO), Overall, 285 patients (88.0%) completed follow-up through day 180, with 36 (11.1%) discontinuations primarily due to loss to follow-up, adverse events, or patient withdrawal (Fig. 1b). The mean (SD) age was 57.9 (14.7) years, and 55.9% of participants were female (Table 1). Mean (SD) age was lowest in the HS cohort at 42.4 years and highest in the RA and PsO cohorts at 65.6 and 62.6 years (Table S2). Gastroenterological, rheumatological, and dermatological indications accounted for 15.4%, 51.2%, and 33.3%, of patients, respectively. Baseline disease activity was predominantly mild (79.0%), with a mean disease duration of 18.4 years (Table 1). Physician Disease Assessments

There were no clinically meaningful changes noted in physician-reported outcomes as measured by the physician global assessment (PGA) score. The physician’s judgement of disease severity did not change as a result of the switch from alternative low concentration adalimumab to AVT02 (Table S7).

Patient-Reported Disease Assessments

Patient-reported outcomes remained generally stable over time. No statistically significant changes from baseline (last dose of high-volume adalimumab) were observed in patient-reported global assessment (PtGA) scores through day 180 after switching to AVT02 (Table S8).

Healthcare Utilization

Generally, no changes in healthcare utilization patterns were reported by patients following their switch from high-volume adalimumab to AVT02. A lower proportion of patients reported visits to any doctors or any healthcare professionals (1 month look back window) after switching to AVT02 through 180 days of treatment (10.8–19.0% vs 30.6% with high-volume adalimumab; Table S9).

Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events (TEAEs)

A total of 324 patients were included in a safety analysis set. Overall, 155 (47.8%) patients experienced 713 TEAEs after switching to AVT02 during the study. Of these, 35 TEAEs experienced by 32 (9.9%) patients were considered of special interest. The majority of TEAEs were mild with 142 (43.8%) patients experiencing 649 events, while 34 (10.5%) patients experienced 57 moderate TEAEs and 2 (0.6%) patients experienced 6 severe TEAEs. Overall, the majority of TEAEs recovered or resolved (628 events in 134 patients). For most TEAEs, the study drug was continued without dose modification (595 TEAEs in 134 [41.4%] patients). Most frequently reported TEAEs by system organ class (SOC) were general disorders and administration site conditions (586 events in 120 patients) and infections and infestations (32 events in 29 patients). Injection-site hemorrhage, ISP, and injection site pruritus were the most reported preferred term (PT), with frequencies of 18.5%, 15.7%, and 10.5%, respectively (Table S10). As the EASE-PAIN study was designed as a prospective observational study evaluating outcomes after switching to AVT02, detailed adverse event data from the prior adalimumab therapy were not systematically collected.

Discussion

Biosimilars have recently entered the treatment landscape for IMIDs and are increasingly used in clinical practice. In Canada, biosimilar uptake has been driven primarily by provincially implemented mandatory non-medical switching policies. In this trial, we looked at the impact on ISP when switching from high-volume RP adalimumab or another biosimilar to low-volume AVT02.

In placebo-controlled clinical trials, subcutaneously administered adalimumab has been associated with injection-site reactions, including erythema and/or pruritus, hemorrhage, pain, and swelling [9]. The incidence of such reactions in the adalimumab group was 20.3% versus 13.8% in the placebo group. The pain and the ISR often impedes patient adherence to treatment and causes psychological stress which could eventually lead to reduced efficacy [5].

Previous studies have established that high-concentration, low-volume, and citrate-free formulations of adalimumab cause less ISP and ISRs [9, 13]. As a clarification point, certain studies describe adalimumab formulations based on both volume and concentration, referring to the high-volume formulation as 40 mg/0.8 mL and the low-volume formulation as 40 mg/0.4 mL [9]. One of the factors causing ISP and ISR is the use of citrate buffer in the formulation. The role of acid-sensing ion channels (ASICs) specifically of ASIC1 subtype as the key receptor causing injection site nociception has been reported [14]. The changes in ISP and ISRs with low-volume and citrate-free formulation have been reported from the studies and support the direct development of biosimilars with low-volume and citrate-free formulations to aid better patient acceptability [13].

Accordingly, the EASE-PAIN study demonstrated a change in ISP VAS score of − 19.9 ± 26.13 mm (mean ± SD) between the last dose of high-volume formulation and first dose of AVT02. It has been reported that the minimum clinically significant difference in VAS pain score on a 100-mm scale is 9–10 mm, regardless of gender, age, or cause of pain [15, 16]. Although the mean reduction in the ISP VAS score in the EASE PAIN cohort exceeds commonly cited minimum clinically important differences, baseline ISP was in the mild range (mean 25.5 mm), and therefore the magnitude of clinical impact should be interpreted conservatively. A statistically significant change in ISP VAS score was observed across all indications. Interestingly, the largest reduction in ISP was in patients with HS (− 31.2 ± 27.72) as measured by the VAS score. Several factors may explain the larger reduction observed in patients with HS. The HS cohort was younger on average and more likely to receive weekly dosing; and the disease itself is characterized by painful nodules and sinus tracts in typical injection areas, which may heighten sensitivity to injection-related discomfort. These explanations are hypothesis generating and require confirmation in disease-specific studies. In addition, given the relatively modest HS subgroup size (n = 39), these findings should be interpreted cautiously. The reported reduction of ISP VAS scores was observed at all evaluation timepoints through 180 days follow-up and the reduction observed after initial switch persisted throughout the study. In addition, 146 (51.0%) described their first AVT02 dose as ‘a lot less painful’, 74 (25.9%) as ‘less painful’. These findings are consistent with those reported in the literature [9, 10], with the EASE PAIN study demonstrating ISR and ISP differences across a broad range of IMIDs, suggesting that the benefits of citrate-free, low-volume adalimumab formulations are not disease-specific but rather formulation-driven. It should be noted that the enrolled cohort included multiple IMIDs with differing pain profiles and treatment patterns, which may complicate interpretation of pooled ISP outcomes, and that systemic pain related to underlying disease activity could influence ISP reporting.

Significantly fewer patients experienced any ISR after switching to AVT02 (36 patients; 12.4%) vs previous adalimumab treatment (124 patients; 42.3%; P < 0.001), a 70.7% drop overall. Interestingly, there was also a diminution of 88.9% in burning sensation, 86.4% in soreness, and 67.1% in swelling. Patients transitioning from a high-volume adalimumab to AVT02 reported a mean overall treatment compliance of 93.4% with 87.7% of patients attaining > 80% compliance. Our findings are consistent with those of Bergman et al., who reported improved adherence and persistence with citrate-free adalimumab formulations compared with citrate-containing formulations [8]. While the designs and primary endpoints between the two studies differ, both studies support the potential for citrate-free, optimized formulations to improve patient experience and adherence. The persistence of patient compliance to treatment after switching to biosimilar therapy may be correlated with the patient perception of change in ISP [9]. Reductions in ISP and ISRs, together with patients’ perceived improvement in ISP, were consistently observed. In parallel, patient satisfaction with AVT02 remained high, with more than 74.4% of patients reporting being “mostly satisfied” or “completely satisfied” over 180 days of treatment. Consistently positive patient compliance and patient satisfaction with treatment are the correlating factors that could lead to lower active disease states [17, 18]. The authors acknowledge that the open-label observational design of the study may introduce expectation bias, particularly for subjective outcomes such as ISP and patient satisfaction. However, this study design was intentionally chosen to reflect real-world clinical practice during the period of mandated biosimilar switching in Canada. In addition, the study incorporated several measures that mitigate this concern: objective ISR assessments using standardized checklists, consistent measurement of ISP using a validated VAS instrument, and repeated assessments over a 180-day follow-up period demonstrating persistence of the observed improvements.

With respect to the safety of transitioning to AVT02, most patients had been receiving adalimumab for a prolonged period prior to enrollment (minimum 6 months), suggesting that the population represented individuals with established treatment tolerance. This context may partially explain the relatively low proportion of severe TEAEs observed after switching.

Prior to switching to AVT02, the vast majority of participants (304/324; 93.8%) were treated with the reference product adalimumab (Humira®), which in Canada is predominantly available as a citrate-containing, high-volume formulation (40 mg/0.8 mL). Only 20 patients (6.2%) were treated with another high-volume adalimumab biosimilar before transitioning to AVT02. Among these biosimilars, 11 patients were receiving a citrate-free formulation and 9 a citrate-containing formulation (Table 2). Therefore, the proportion of patients previously treated with citrate-free formulations was relatively small within the study population. Consequently, the observed reduction in ISP is unlikely to be driven solely by the removal of citrate, but more broadly reflects the combined formulation characteristics of AVT02, including the reduced injection volume and absence of citrate.

The EASE-PAIN study demonstrated that switching to AVT02 maintained the persistence of efficacy as shown in disease-specific assessments, disease-specific patient-reported outcomes, and frequency of adverse events reported. While switching to a new product and new formulation can be a cause for uncertainty in patients, participants in this study maintained a high QoL and satisfaction score after adopting AVT02.

Limitations and Strengths

The limitations of the study were inherent to its phase IV, observational, open-label design. The open-label, uncontrolled design conducted during a mandated switch is vulnerable to expectation bias and placebo effects. Treatment switches were initiated at the discretion of the treating physician rather than through randomization, introducing the potential for selection bias and limiting causal inference. There was also a recall bias for patients who switched up to 3 months before the enrollment (n = 49, 15.1% of study population), and these retrospective assessments may have influenced the magnitude of perceived differences in ISP and ISRs in this subgroup. The lack of a comparator arm limits the ability to directly attribute the observed improvements to AVT02 and to distinguish formulation-related effects from contextual or switch-related influences. The open-label nature of the study, combined with reliance on patient-reported outcomes such as VAS pain scores, ISRs, Likert scales, QoL and satisfaction measures, introduces the possibility of reporting bias, which may influence the magnitude of perceived benefit. Sponsor involvement in study conduct may also represent a potential source of operational or interpretative bias, despite standardized data collection procedures. The smaller‑than‑planned sample size reduces statistical precision and should be considered when interpreting the estimates and some subgroup analyses, although the observed confidence intervals remain reasonably narrow. The results reported for the UC disease subgroup should also be interpreted with discretion as there were only 12 participants with UC in the study. The cohort included multiple IMIDs with differing pain profiles and that systemic pain related to disease activity could confound ISP reporting. Most patients in this study had mild disease activity at baseline and were clinically stable on originator/biosimilar adalimumab; hence, patients with unstable or highly active disease were underrepresented, limiting inference about switching in more severe populations. The analyses were based on observed cases without imputation, and attrition and incomplete diary completion may bias estimates at later time points if patients with missing data differed systematically from completers (e.g., 195 patients were available for the ISP VAS assessment at day 180). The study would have benefited from more transitions from alternative adalimumab biosimilars (high-volume formulations with or without citrate) to AVT02 to tease out the effects of citrate vs volume on the differences observed in ISP and ISRs. The limited number of patients switching from citrate-free biosimilars restricted the ability to fully disentangle the relative contribution of citrate removal versus injection volume. Nevertheless, the study provides valuable real-world evidence across multiple indications in a Canadian clinical setting, offering clinically relevant insights into patient-reported outcomes, adherence, and treatment experience following a switch to a high-concentration, low-volume, citrate-free adalimumab formulation. These findings are most applicable to similar healthcare settings and biosimilar policies, and extrapolation to other jurisdictions/formulations should be undertaken with caution. The lack of systematic TEAE data for prior treatment precludes direct safety comparisons between high-volume adalimumab and AVT02.

Conclusions

In this real-world Canadian study, switching from reference or other high-volume, low-concentration adalimumab formulations to the low-volume, high-concentration, citrate-free biosimilar AVT02 was associated with reductions in ISP and ISRs. These improvements were observed across multiple disease indications and were accompanied by high treatment adherence, preserved QoL, stable disease control, and no increase in healthcare utilization. The EASE PAIN study was designed as an observational, open-label, uncontrolled study and therefore carries the potential for reporting bias. The findings support the use of AVT02 as a well-tolerated biosimilar option and underscore the importance of formulation characteristics in optimizing patient experience during non-medical switching.

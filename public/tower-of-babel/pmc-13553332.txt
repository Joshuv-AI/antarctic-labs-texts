Predictive Value of the Triglyceride-Glucose-Frailty Index for Depression and Cognitive Function in Perimenopausal Women: Insights from CHARLS
Authors: Hengyu Chi, Qunqi Hu, Jingqi Liang, Jingjing Zhang, Siying Qu, Hanzhi Wang, Yurong Kang, Zhouqi Liu, Yuran Chen, Yongliang Jiang, Xiaofen He, Jianqiao Fang, Yinhang Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposePerimenopause represents a critical neuroendocrine transition stage in the lives of women, characterized by metabolic disorders and physiological vulnerability, which also commonly manifests as depression and cognitive impairment. This study explores associations between the triglyceride-glucose-frailty index (TyGFI) and depression/cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women.MethodsData were obtained from the 2015 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) and 2,596 perimenopausal women were included. Logistic regression models were used to evaluate the association between TyGFI and depression/cognitive impairment. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) curves were plotted to visualize the dose–response relationship. Subgroup analyses and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to assess interaction effects and evaluate the discriminatory ability of TyGFI. The results were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI).ResultsPerimenopausal women with higher TyGFI levels exhibited a greater tendency towards depression and cognitive impairment, older age, higher blood pressure, and poorer self-reported health. In the fully adjusted model, each one-point increase in TyGFI was associated with elevated risk of depression (OR = 3.70, 95% CI: 3.18–4.32) and cognitive impairment (OR = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.34–1.71). RCS curves confirmed non-linear, positive associations and clear dose–response trends. Subgroup analysis revealed that the associations remained largely robust across most subgroups. ROC analysis demonstrated moderate-to-good discriminatory ability, with area under the curve (AUC) of 0.786 for depression and 0.634 for cognitive impairment.ConclusionThe TyGFI was concurrently correlated with depression and cognitive impairment in Chinese perimenopausal women, which may serve as a potential indicator for clinical risk screening.Keywords: perimenopause, triglyceride-glucose index, frailty index, depression, cognitive impairment, CHARLS

Introduction

Perimenopause is a special life transition period that middle-aged women experience around the menopause, encompassing the menopause transition period and the first year after the final menstrual period.1 During the perimenopause, with hormonal fluctuations and other endocrine system changes, some women may experience a range of vasomotor and neurological symptoms, such as hot flash, mood disruption, and cognitive decline.2,3 These symptoms have caused great distress to the perimenopausal women worldwide. Due to the transition state of central nervous system in the perimenopause, depression and cognitive decline show a high susceptibility at this stage.4 Previous studies indicated that approximately 27.8% of perimenopausal women suffer from depression, while 16.7% suffer from cognitive decline, with a great negative impact on female life.5,6 Perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment also may develop into severe affective disorders and neurodegenerative diseases in postmenopausal stage.7,8 As a window of vulnerability for neurological diseases in women, the perimenopause requires close attention from doctors to evaluate health status. However, owing to the particularity of perimenopause, perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment are challenging to identify at an early stage by common indicators.

The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index is a comprehensive biomarker composed of fasting triglyceride and fasting blood glucose, which has been recognized as a new surrogate marker for insulin resistance.9 With its routine original parameters and simple calculation method, TyG index is extensively employed across clinical studies.10 During the perimenopause, fluctuations in estrogen levels and metabolic states during perimenopause can elevate women’s risk of developing insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome.11 Additionally, decreased insulin sensitivity may promote alterations in neural structure and function, resulting in neuronal injury and adverse effects on cerebral energy metabolism.12,13 Recent evidences suggest that lipid-derived atherogenic indices may be relevant not only for cardiometabolic risk stratification but also for psychological outcomes, particularly among middle-aged women undergoing menopause-related transitions.14,15 More research confirmed strong correlations of TyG with mental illness, cerebrovascular disease, and neurodegenerative diseases.16,17 Thus, TyG is considered as a potential diagnostic biomarker for depression and cognitive impairment.

Frailty represents a clinical state featuring diminished physiological reserves and elevated risk of adverse health outcomes, which is typically associated with advancing age.18 For perimenopausal and menopausal women, with lower estrogen levels and physical function degradation, frailty has also become one of the main characteristic symptoms in the midlife transition period.19,20 Frailty index (FI) is used to quantify frailty conditions as a continuous score. It incorporates at least 30 health variables across multiple dimensions and is constructed using a standardized ten-step procedure to ensure the consistency of the index.21 Previous studies have confirmed that the FI exhibited potential predictive capabilities for various diseases, including cardiovascular disease, depression, and cognitive impairment.22,23 Moreover, heightened FI is potentially correlated to depression and cognitive impairment risk.24

Although both TyG and FI have been established as independent associations of depression and cognitive impairment, previous studies have rarely explored their combined effects on neurological diseases. Limited research focused on whether TyG and FI are strongly correlated with depression and cognitive impairment during the unique neurological transition state of perimenopause. Thus, based on the characteristics of endocrine disorders and frailty state in perimenopausal women, this study integrated TyG and FI to derive a composite indicator—the TyG-Frailty Index (TyGFI), which can comprehensively reflect the burden of metabolic disorders and clinical vulnerability. The publicly available data from wave 2015 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) were used to explore the potential association between TyGFI and depression/cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women. This study aimed to provide a potential risk indicator for perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment and raise public awareness of protecting perimenopausal women’s health.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

This study extracted data from CHARLS, a nationally representative survey of middle-aged and older Chinese adults launched in 2011. It tracked health status and economic adaptation of Chinese every two years. Ethical approval for CHARLS was granted by the Peking University Institutional Review Board (IRB00001052-11015), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

CHARLS Wave 3 (2015) data were chosen for analysis due to the inclusion of blood-based bioassays, which enabled us to examine the cross-sectional association of TyGFI with depression and cognitive impairment among perimenopausal women. Among 21,095 participants in the 2015 CHARLS, 5,127 were perimenopausal women aged 45–59 years. After excluding the missing variable data, 2,596 eligible perimenopausal women were included. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) missing data on triglyceride or glucose (n=1,738), (2) missing data on variables required for constructing the FI index (n=37), (3) missing data on the 10-item Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CESD-10) score (n=42), (4) missing data on cognitive tests (n=610), and (5) missing data on covariates (n=104). Final eligible subjects were categorized into four TyGFI-derived quartiles (Figure 1).

Main Variables

TyGFI Measurement

In the CHARLS cohort, participants were required to fast overnight, and venous blood samples were collected by professional enumerators. All blood samples were processed in a local laboratory and transported to Beijing Central Laboratory for analysis. Fasting triglyceride and fasting glucose levels were analyzed using standardized procedures. The calculation of TyG index used the following formula: ln [fasting triglycerides (mg/dL) × fasting glucose (mg/dL)/2].25 The validity and predictive value of TyG had been widely recognized in metabolic research. FI is a comprehensive indicator assessing age-related health decline, proven to effectively predict multiple diseases. FI was constructed by standardized calculation with 30 items covering all major health domains, including chronic diseases, disabilities, and activities of daily living and instrumental activities of daily living.21 Each item was dichotomously scored (0/1), with 0 indicating no defects and 1 indicating defects existed. FI was calculated as the sum of all items divided by 30, with higher scores indicating a higher level of frailty. Spearman correlation analysis was employed to examine the relationships between the TyG and FI, with details provided in the Supplementary Figure S1. We used a multiplicative model to construct a composite indicator combining TyG and FI: TyGFI = TyG × FI.26 This model reflects potential interactive effects between metabolic and functional impairments in the study analysis.

Depression Assessment

Depressive status of participants was evaluated by the CESD-10, which assesses the frequency of depression-related experiences over the past week. The CESD-10 is widely used as a brief screening instrument for detecting clinically depressed individuals, comprising ten items scored 0–3 points each, with a total score ranging from 0 to 30 points.27 In addition, since questions 5 and 8 are related with positive emotions, these two questions are reverse-scored. Higher scores indicated higher risks of depressive disorders. Individuals with CESD-10 scores ≥10 were defined as having clinical depression symptoms, and those with CESD-10 scores <10 were defined as non-depressed.28

Cognitive Impairment Assessment

Three composite tests were examined for cognition scoring: a word-recall test, Telephone Interview of Cognitive Status (TICS-10), and figure drawing test. Episodic memory was evaluated by the word-recall trial, requiring to memorize ten words, perform immediate recall, then complete delayed recall after four minutes. Final episodic memory score was the average of immediate and delayed recall scores. Ten mental status questions were recorded in the TICS, including subtracting 7 consecutively from 100 (up to five times), the specific date, day of the week, the season, and asking participants to redraw two overlapping images to calculate mental intactness indicators. The overall cognitive score was summed up the episodic memory and the mental intactness indicators, ranging from 0 to 21.29 Cognitive impairment was defined as a score <11, and people with scores ≥11 were defined as normal cognitive function.30

Covariates

Based on previous literature relating to depression and cognitive function, this study incorporated and analyzed 11 covariates from CHARLS. The collected covariates included age, education level, marital status, retirement status, smoking, drinking, social activity, co-residence with children, self-reported health, systolic blood pressure (SBP), and diastolic blood pressure (DBP). The covariates were defined as follows: (1) Demographic characteristics: age (continuous variable), education level (high school or above, primary school or below), marital status (married and partnered, unmarried and others), and retirement status (yes or no); (2) Lifestyle characteristics: smoking (current smoking was defined as yes, currently not smoking was defined as no), drinking (having alcohol consumption in last 12 months was defined as yes, not having alcohol consumption in last 12 months was defined as no), social activity (participating in social activities in the past month was defined as yes, not participating in social activities in the past month was defined as no), co-residence with children (yes or no); (3) Clinical characteristics: self-reported health (bad, fair, good), SBP and DBP were defined as the average of the second and third blood pressure measurements (continuous variable).

Statistical Analysis

Data analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1). TyGFI was stratified into four quartiles from low to high. The characteristics of the 2015 CHARLS samples were described stratified by TyGFI quartiles (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4). Missing variable data were removed without any interpolation being applied. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, analyzed using chi-square tests. Results were considered statistically significant at P<0.05.

Multivariable logistic regression analyses were used to evaluate odds ratio (OR), 95% confidence interval (CI), and p-values for the relationship between TyGFI index and the outcomes (depression and cognitive impairment). Analyses included both continuous and categorical TyGFI (quartiles). The model was adjusted three times to correct for potential confounding variables: A crude model (univariate analysis); Model 1, adjusted for age, education, marital status, retirement; Model 2, further adjusted for smoking status, drinking status, social activity, co-residence with children; Model 3, further adjustment for self-reported health, SBP, DBP. Post-hoc power analysis was used to confirm that the sample (n = 2596) had sufficient statistical power to detect the association (Supplementary Table S1).

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) curves were plotted to visualize the relationship between TyGFI and depression/cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women, selecting 3 knots to make a balance between model flexibility and avoiding overfitting. Subgroup analyses were conducted for variables including education, marital status, retirement, smoking status, drinking status, and co-residence with child. Results were presented in the form of forest plots, and interaction analysis was performed to identify the effect modifications. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves evaluated the discriminatory ability of TyGFI for distinguishing perimenopausal women with and without depression/cognitive impairment by assessing the area under the curve (AUC), best threshold, sensitivity, and specificity. Additionally, two sensitivity analyses were performed as follows: (1) To reduce impacts of remaining potential covariates, we re-ran the models with additional covariates adjustment for body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC), and diabetes. (2) Using “MICE” package of the R software, multiple imputations by chained equations method addressed all missing covariates data.

Results

Basic Characteristics of Population

This study included 2596 Chinese perimenopausal women (mean age: 51.9 ± 4.2 years). Participants were stratified by TyGFI quartiles (Q1–Q4) to analyze the characteristics of samples, with each group comprising 649 individuals (Table 1). The specific characteristics showed as follows: primary school or below education (1617, 62.3%), unmarried and others status (151, 5.8%); retired women (658, 25.3%); smoking (101, 3.9%); drinking (416, 16.0%); participating in social activities (1455, 56.0%), co-residence with children (1584, 61.0%). Additionally, certain variables exhibited considerable differences in different TyGFI quartiles groups. With TyGFI quartiles increased, the mean age also progressively increased and the sample exhibited worse health status: age increased from 50.8 ± 4.1 in Q1 to 53.1 ± 3.9 years in Q4, SBP increased from 118.1 ± 15.8 in Q1 to 123.6 ± 18.6 in Q4, DBP increased from 72.9 ± 10.5 in Q1 to 75.4 ± 11.6 in Q4, self-report of bad health increased from 16 (2.5%) in Q1 to 305 (47.0%) in Q4, depression increased from 40 (6.2%) in Q1 to 429 (66.1%) in Q4, cognitive impairment increased from 97 (14.9%) in Q1 to 236 (36.4%) in Q4. Therefore, perimenopausal women in higher TyGFI quartiles exhibited a greater tendency towards depression/cognitive impairment.

Association Between TyGFI and Perimenopausal Depression

Logistic regression models with gradually adjusted covariates were performed to analyze correlations between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression (Table 2). The crude model showed a significantly elevated risk of perimenopausal depression for each one-point increase in TyGFI, with an OR of 3.62 (95% CI: 3.20–4.11, P<0.001). Adjusting for all demographic, lifestyle, and clinical covariates, the OR in model 3 was 3.70 (95% CI: 3.18–4.32, P<0.001). Furthermore, we observed similar graded correlations between TyGFI quartiles and perimenopausal depression across all models: Relative to Q1, ORs for perimenopausal depression elevated stepwise across higher quartiles (Q2-Q4 groups) (P<0.001). In Model 3, with Q1 as the reference group, the OR for perimenopausal depression was 5.74 (95% CI: 3.90–8.61, P<0.001) in Q2, 13.39 (95% CI: 9.05–20.24, P<0.001) in Q3, 33.94 (95% CI: 22.38–52.61, P<0.001) in the Q4 group. The above results demonstrated higher TyGFI was strongly associated with elevated prevalence of perimenopausal depression.

Association Between TyGFI and Perimenopausal Cognitive Impairment

As a continuous variable, each one-point increase in TyGFI elevated cognitive impairment risk across four models (P<0.001) (Table 3). And the OR for perimenopausal cognitive impairment was 1.56 (95% CI: 1.43–1.72, P<0.001) in crude model, 1.44 (95% CI: 1.31–1.60, P<0.001) in model 1, 1.43 (95% CI: 1.30–1.60, P<0.001) in model 2, 1.51 (95% CI: 1.34–1.71, P<0.001) in model 3. In addition, when analyzing the quartiles of TyGFI, regression analysis results revealed a distinct dose–response relationship. In Model 3 which adjusted for all covariates, the OR for cognitive impairment was 1.92 (95% CI: 1.38–2.68, P<0.001) in Q2, 3.07 (95% CI: 2.20–4.32, P<0.001) in Q3, 3.41 (95% CI: 2.39–4.90, P<0.001) in the Q4 group. Significant trend relationships were observed across TyGFI quartiles in all models (P<0.001). Above results indicated TyGFI was highly correlated with perimenopausal cognitive impairment.

RCS Analysis

The RCS curve was used to visualize and analyze correlations between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment. As TyGFI levels increased, the OR for depression exhibited an upward trend, and a sharp increase in OR was observed at high TyGFI levels (Figure 2A). Non-linear relationships were identified between two variables across the crude model and three adjusted models (p-overall<0.001, p-nonlinear<0.001). RCS curves also illustrated that the OR for cognitive impairment increased as TyGFI levels rose, which further confirmed the results of previous TyGFI quartiles analysis (Figure 2B). And the relationships were nonlinear across four models (p-overall<0.001, p-nonlinear<0.01). Overall, results revealed significant non-linear associations between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment. The associations remained stable under different model adjustments.

RCS curves for depression and cognitive impairment risk according to TyGFI. (A) Non-linear relationship between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression; (B) Non-linear relationship between TyGFI and perimenopausal cognitive impairment.

Subgroup Analysis

We further conducted subgroup analyses of perimenopausal women based on educational level, marital status, retirement status, smoking, drinking, and co-residence with children. Subgroup analysis revealed that increased TyGFI showed heightened correlations with perimenopausal depression among individuals who were higher educated, unmarried, non-retired, smokers, non-drinkers and residing with children (Figure 3A). An interaction effect between TyGFI and education level was detected (P for interaction <0.05). The risk of perimenopausal cognitive impairment is higher among individuals who were higher educated, unmarried, non-retired, drinkers, and residing with children. There was no substantial difference in the risk of perimenopausal cognitive impairment between smokers and non-smokers (Figure 3B). And no statistically significant interactions were observed across different subgroups (P for interaction >0.05).

Logistic regression analysis of TyGFI among subgroups. (A) A forest plot showing the results of multivariable logistic regression analyses for perimenopausal depression; (B) A forest plot showing the results of multivariable logistic regression analyses for perimenopausal cognitive impairment.

ROC Curves of TyGFI for Perimenopausal Depression and Cognitive Impairment

ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of TyGFI in perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment (Figure 4). For perimenopausal depression, AUC value was 0.786 (95% CI: 0.769–0.804), with a best threshold of 0.849 yielding sensitivity of 0.832 and specificity of 0.598. TyGFI exhibited superior discriminatory ability for perimenopausal depression. In contrast, TyGFI index showed a reduced discriminatory ability for perimenopausal cognitive impairment, as evidenced by a lower AUC value of 0.634 (95% CI: 0.610–0.657). At the best threshold of 0.671, sensitivity was 0.800 and specificity was 0.411. Overall, the TyGFI exhibited discriminatory ability in distinguishing perimenopausal women with and without depression/cognitive impairment.

ROC Curves for TyGFI in distinguishing the risk of depression/cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women.

Sensitivity Analyses

Two sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness. In the fully adjusted model with additional covariates, the effect size of perimenopausal depression (OR from 3.70 to 3.68) and cognitive impairment (OR from 1.51 to 1.50) was reduced (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). Moreover, after multiple imputations of missing data, the effect size of perimenopausal depression (OR from 3.70 to 3.73) slightly increased while the effect size of cognitive impairment (OR from 1.51 to 1.49) slightly decreased (Supplementary Tables S4 and S5). Both analyses showed statistical significance and dose–response relationships. In summary, sensitivity analysis verified the robustness of correlations between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment.

Discussion

This study explored corrections between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment by analyzing nationally representative cohort data from China. Elevated TyGFI levels were positively correlated with heightened perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment risk, especially for depression. The relationship exhibited a distinct non-linear, dose–response pattern and remained robust in two sensitivity analyses. Perimenopausal women with highest quartile of TyGFI demonstrated the highest risk of depression and cognitive impairment. While the exceptionally high OR for depression in Q4 compared to Q1 should be interpreted cautiously, it may be due to sparse data bias resulting from the relatively small event count in the reference group (Q1), statistically overestimating the relative odds. Subgroup analyses and ROC curves identified the discriminatory ability of TyGFI for both perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment. These findings revealed combined effects of metabolic disorders and increased physiological vulnerability may have adverse effects on nervous system function of perimenopausal women, which supported potential utility of TyGFI in comprehensive prevention and management for perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment.

Extensive research has validated the independent roles of TyG and FI in depression and cognitive impairment. As a robust marker of metabolic disorders, elevated TyG can lead to heightened risks of depression, anxiety, suicide, especially for people under 60 years old and with higher education.31,32 Moreover, TyG index exhibited significant association with cognitive impairment and dementia.16,33 Elevated FI levels were also correlated with heightened depression/cognitive impairment risk in previous studies.34,35 However, most research focuses on the cumulative effects of TyG and FI on cardiovascular diseases.36 Few studies explored the synergistic interaction of TyG and FI on depression and cognitive impairment. Current research also lacks investigation into the influence of these two factors on neurological disorders during perimenopause. Our study demonstrated the potential association between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment for the first time and confirmed TyGFI may serve as a potential indicator for perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment risk.

Although the specific mechanism underlying TyGFI’s impact on perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment remains unclear, we speculate that there are multiple potential patholgical mechanisms explaining the higher risk of perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment. The severity of insulin resistance could be indirectly reflected by TyG levels, and perimenopause increases the risk of insulin resistance with fluctuating female hormone levels. Insulin resistance aggravating perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment may be achieved by regulating neurotransmitters, chronic inflammation, and hippocampal damage. Brain insulin resistance can lead to abnormal spontaneous firing of dopaminergic neuron and serotonergic neuron in the brain reward circuit, causing abnormal release of dopamine and 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT).37,38 Meanwhile, with a decrease in brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) levels, impaired hippocampal neurogenesis contributes to depression and cognitive impairment.39,40 Insulin resistance is also associated with systemic chronic inflammation and hyperactivity of the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, manifested by high expression of tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), which can exacerbate central nervous system damage and establish a vicious cycle.41,42 In addition, the vascular endothelial function is impaired by insulin resistance, reducing vasodilation capacity and causing diminished cerebral blood flow.43 Similarly, frailty can lead to chronic inflammation, decreased BDNF levels, HPA axis dysfunction and increased oxidative stress, which may be the reasons why elevated FI levels increased perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment risk. Long-term frailty is accompanied by high expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1beta (IL-1β) and TNF-α. These cytokines can increase permeability of blood-brain barrier, activate microglia and astrocytes, and further release more pro-inflammatory cytokines, exacerbating neuroinflammation.44,45 Decreased BDNF levels and HPA axis dysfunction also affect neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity.46 Moreover, mitochondrial dysfunction and increased oxidative stress may be the significant factors promoting the cognitive impairment.47 The above potential mechanisms clarify the synergistic effect of TyG and FI and provide evidence support for the reliability of constructing the new composite indicator—TyGFI for evaluating perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment by multiplicative interaction method.

TyGFI provided a clinically convenient, feasible integration of metabolic dysfunction and frailty for distinguishing risk of depression and cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women. The integrative nature of TyGFI enables a comprehensive assessment of patient health. As a multidimensional risk biomarker, TyGFI exhibited a conceptual advantage in capturing two distinct dimensions simultaneously. Moreover, most of the data for constructing TyGFI can be obtained from basic blood tests and medical history collection, indicating the clinical feasibility of TyGFI. Thus, TyGFI has the potential to achieve more sensitive distinctions of depression/cognitive impairment risk in perimenopausal individuals.

However, despite the use of analytical methods, this study still has some limitations. First, this study is a cross-sectional study, which could not determine the specific causal correlations between TyGFI and perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment, and the effects of longitudinal changes and cumulative exposure need to be further explored. Second, the definition of perimenopausal status in this study was based solely on age due to the missing data of menstrual cycle and surgical menopause. During the perimenopause, estrogen levels exhibit marked fluctuation.2 This age-based approach was unable to account for the complex, nonlinear influence of these hormonal changes on depression and cognitive health. Third, this study did not conduct a formal comparison of alternative model specifications of TyGFI construction or discrimination assessment of different indicators. Added values of the composite indicator require further validation. Fourth, the high OR for depression in Q4 compared to Q1 may be overestimated due to sparse data bias resulting from the relatively small event count in Q1.

Overall, this study confirms the relevance and potential of TyGFI as a new composite indicator that can reflect metabolic characteristic and physiological function status in perimenopausal women for depression/cognitive impairment, providing theoretical support for the future clinical application.

Conclusions

Our research identified significant correlations between TyGFI and depression/cognitive impairment in perimenopausal women. With the heightened TyGFI level, perimenopausal depression/cognitive impairment risk increased, especially in depression. Strong correlations and non-linear relationships were verified using the RCS curve and subgroup analysis. TyGFI also possessed a certain discriminatory ability for perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment. Our findings indicate TyGFI is concurrently associated with both perimenopausal depression and cognitive impairment, which may serve as a potential risk indicator and provide a foundation for future longitudinal studies. It has important clinical significance for protecting the emotional and cognitive health of women during perimenopause.

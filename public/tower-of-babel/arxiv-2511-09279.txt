Production of Light Dark Particles from Nonlinear Compton Scattering Between Intense Laser and Muon or Proton Beam
Tong Li, Kai Ma, Man Yuan

Abstract
The laser of an intense electromagnetic field promotes the studies of strong-field particle physics in high-intensity frontier. Particle accelerator facilities in the world produce high-quality muon and proton beams. In this work, we propose the nonlinear Compton scattering to light dark particles through the collision between intense laser pulse and muon or proton beam. We take light dark photon and axion-like particle as illustrative dark particles. The cross sections of relevant nonlinear Compton scattering to dark photon or axion-like particle are calculated. We also analyze the background processes with missing neutrinos. The prospective sensitivity shows that the laser-induced process provides a complementary and competitive search of new invisible particles lighter than about 1 MeV.



I
Introduction

In 1951, J. Schwinger showed that at external field strengths of
E\simeq 1.32\times 10^{18}~{\rm V/m}
, the virtual electron-positron pair
fluctuations in quantum electrodynamics (QED) vacuum are converted
into real electron-positron pairs
Schwinger 1951
. This critical field strength corresponds to an extreme intensity limit of laser, that is
I=2.1\times 10^{29}~{\rm W/cm^{2}}
. The laser of an intense electromagnetic field strength has a lot of applications in such as atomic physics, nuclear physics and particle physics (see a recent review
Fedotov et al. 2023
and references therein).
One is able to study processes that normally do not occur in vacuum but can be induced under strong fields.
Therefore, intense laser pulses allow for the investigation of rich phenomena at the high-intensity frontier of physics.

The experiments performed at SLAC observed two strong-field processes in the interaction of
an ultra-relativistic electron beam with a terawatt laser pulse in 1990s, i.e., the nonlinear Compton scattering and the nonlinear Breit-Wheeler pair production in high intense laser field
Burke et al. 1997
;
Bamber et al. 1999
. The observation of these two processes promotes the studies of non-perturbative QED and nonlinear effects in QED (see a recent review in Ref.
Hartin 2018
). The nonlinear effects from the higher-order terms in the electron beam scattering become significant when

\displaystyle\eta_{e}\equiv{e\varepsilon_{0}\over\omega m_{e}}\gtrsim 1\;,

(1)

where
e
is the electric charge unit,
\varepsilon_{0}
is the electromagnetic field strength,
m_{e}
is the electron mass and
\omega
is the laser beam energy. Interestingly, these laser-induced scattering processes can provide a potentially useful strategy for the search of new physics beyond the Standard Model (SM)
Fuchs et al. 2024
;
Dillon and King 2018
;
King 2018
;
Bai et al. 2022
;
Dillon and King 2019
;
King et al. 2019
;
Beyer et al. 2022
;
Huang et al. 2022
;
Ma and Li 2025a
;
Ma and Li 2025b
;
Ma and Li 2025c
.

Nevertheless, with the help of higher power lasers, the collision of laser pulse and muon or proton beam leads to distinct phenomena in electroweak physics when the above dimensionless effective coupling is greater than
m_{\mu}/m_{e}\simeq 207
. For instance, the vacuum polarization correction to elastic muon scattering was explored in an intense field
Ritus 1972
. Muon decay can be significantly altered in a strong electromagnetic field
Becker et al. 1983
;
Ritus 1985
;
Narozhny and Fedotov 2008
;
Dicus et al. 2009
;
Farzinnia et al. 2009
;
King and Liu 2025
. The laser-muon interaction was also proposed to probe exotic physics beyond the SM
Tizchang et al. 2017
;
Tizchang et al. 2019
.

Nowadays, high-quality muon and proton beams are being developed to enable studies in high-precision muon physics and high-energy elementary particle physics. Particle accelerator facilities in the world provide the beam sources, for example, Paul Scherrer Institute (PSI) in Switzerland, Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (FNAL) and Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL) in the United States, Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex (J-PARC), China Spallation Neutron Source (CSNS) and so on. For these operations, protons are accelerated
in the linac and booster synchrotron and directed to a target. The secondary beam pions then decay into final muons. For example, in FNAL accelerator complex, protons are accelerated to 8 GeV in booster and
\mu^{+}
beam is selected in momentum as 3.1 GeV so as to measure muon anomalous magnetic moment
Stratakis et al. 2019
;
Aguillard et al. 2023
. Moreover, the proton and muon beams are designed to be accelerated to TeV energy scale for the direct search of new physics beyond the SM at hadron colliders (e.g., Tevatron at FNAL and LHC at CERN) or the proposed muon colliders
Al Ali et al. 2022
;
Accettura et al. 2025
;
Hamada et al. 2022
. It is intriguing to consider the access of new physics at low-energies by combining increasingly intense lasers and energetic muon or proton beams.

In this work, we investigate the collision of intense laser pulse and a high-energy muon (
\mu^{+}
) or proton (
\mathcal{P}
) beam. The laser-induced nonlinear Compton scattering can produce a new massive dark particle beyond the SM
Dillon and King 2018
;
King 2018
;
Dillon and King 2019
;
Ma and Li 2025a
. We consider two illustrative invisible particles, i.e., dark photon (DP) and axion-like particle (ALP). In the nonlinear Compton scattering, a muon or proton beam absorbs multiple photons from the laser field and radiates a single dark particle

\displaystyle\mu^{+}/\mathcal{P}+{\rm laser}\to\mu^{+}/\mathcal{P}+{\rm DP~or~ALP}\;.

(2)

DP couples to SM fermions through the kinetic mixing with visible photon. The fermionic interactions with the derivatively
coupled ALP are at dimension 5. They are expected to be long-lived and escape the detector if their masses are less than
2m_{e}\approx 1
MeV. We calculate the nonlinear Compton scattering cross sections of single fermion together with invisible DP and ALP. The charged and neutral weak currents in the SM can also result in single fermion with missing neutrinos in final states. We analyze the relevant SM background processes mediated by
W^{\pm}
or
Z
bosons in details.
Compared to other collider or beam dump experiments, the sizable cross section of this process for the light dark particle creation results in an increased observation sensitivity to the kinetic mixing of DP and ALP derivative coupling to muon or proton.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II

III
, we consider the laser-induced Compton scattering to dark particles. The cross sections of the relevant Compton scattering to DP or ALP are calculated. We also analyze the SM background processes with missing neutrinos. We show the sensitivity reach for dark particle parameters in Sec.
IV
. Our conclusions are drawn in Sec.
V
.

II
Dark particle hypotheses and laser-induced scattering

II.1
Dark particle hypotheses: dark photon and axion-like particle

The dark photon (or called hidden photon)
Holdom 1986a
;
Holdom 1986b
is a spin-one particle gauged by an Abelian group
U(1)_{\rm D}
in dark sector (see a recent review Ref.
Fabbrichesi et al. 2020
and references therein). A gauge kinetic
mixing between the field strength tensors of the SM electromagnetic gauge
U(1)_{\rm EM}
group and the
U(1)_{\rm D}
group is introduced.
The relevant DP Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DP}\supset-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}{F}_{D}^{\mu\nu}{F}_{D\mu\nu}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}F^{\mu\nu}{F}_{D\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{D}^{2}{A_{D}}^{\mu}A_{D\mu}\;,

(3)

where
F^{\mu\nu}
(
F^{\mu\nu}_{D}
) is the SM (dark) electromagnetic field strength, and
A_{D}
is the dark gauge boson with mass
m_{\gamma_{D}}
. By assuming that the SM particles are uncharged under the dark gauge group, the kinetic mixing
\epsilon
can be obtained by integrating out heavy particles charged under both gauge groups at one-loop level. The two gauge fields can then be rotated to get rid of the mixing. As a result, the SM matter current gains a shift as
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\epsilon A_{D\mu}
. Then, the physical DP
\gamma_{D}
has a coupling to the electromagnetic current of the SM fermions

\displaystyle-e\epsilon J_{\rm EM}^{\mu}A_{D\mu}=-eQ\epsilon\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi A_{D\mu}\;.

(4)

The massive DP particle can be searched in terrestrial experiments in the presence of this kinetic mixing.

The axion-like particle is a CP-odd pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson from the spontaneous breaking of a global
U(1)
symmetry. The QCD axion is a well-considered example of such CP-odd particle
Peccei and Quinn 1977a
;
Peccei and Quinn 1977b
;
Weinberg 1978
;
Wilczek 1978
(see a recent review Ref.
Di Luzio et al. 2020
).
The mass of generic ALP (
m_{a}
) and the symmetry breaking scale (or called decay constant
f_{a}
) associated with an ALP can be completely free parameters in many other theoretical models
Dimopoulos 1979
;
Tye 1981
;
Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Dine et al. 1981
;
Holdom and Peskin 1982
;
Kaplan 1985
;
Srednicki 1985
;
Flynn and Randall 1987
;
Kamionkowski and March-Russell 1992
;
Berezhiani et al. 2001
;
Hsu and Sannino 2005
;
Hook 2015
;
Alonso-Álvarez et al. 2019
;
Hook et al. 2020
. The range of ALP mass spans from sub-micro-eV
Kim 1979
;
Shifman et al. 1980
;
Dine et al. 1981
;
Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Turner 1990
to TeV scale and even beyond
Rubakov 1997
;
Fukuda et al. 2015
;
Gherghetta et al. 2016
;
Dimopoulos et al. 2016
;
Chiang et al. 2016
;
Gaillard et al. 2018
;
Gherghetta and Nguyen 2020
. Thus, the search for the ALPs relies on rather different strategies and facilities in experiments. The probe of ALP also depends on the interactions between ALP and the SM fields. Here, we are interested in the ALP-fermion coupling defined via the Lagrangian term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm ALP}\supset c_{af}{\partial_{\mu}a\over 2f_{a}}\overline{f}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}f\;,

(5)

where
c_{af}
is a dimensionless constant and
f=\mu,\mathcal{P}
here. One usually defines another dimensionless ALP-fermion coupling
g_{af}=c_{af}m_{f}/f_{a}
.

II.2
Basics of laser-induced nonlinear scattering

In the presence of an electromagnetic potential, the wave function of a relativistic fermion of mass
m
subjects to the following Dirac equation

(i\not{\partial}-Qe\not{A}-m)\psi(x)=0\;,

(6)

where
e
is the unit of electric charge and
Q
is the charge operator (e.g.
Q\psi=+\psi
for proton).
With circular polarization, the vector potential
A^{\mu}
takes the following form

A^{\mu}(\phi)=a_{1}^{\mu}\cos(\phi)+a_{2}^{\mu}\sin(\phi)\,,\quad\phi=k\cdot x,

(7)

where
k
is the four-momentum of laser photon and

\phi
is the phase of the laser field. The polarization vectors are given by

\displaystyle a_{1}^{\mu}=|\vec{a}\,|(0,1,0,0)\,,~~~a_{2}^{\mu}=|\vec{a}\,|(0,0,1,0)\;.

(8)

They satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle a_{1}\cdot a_{2}=0\;,~~~a_{1}^{2}=a_{2}^{2}=-|\vec{a}\,|^{2}\equiv-a^{2}\;,

(9)

where
a=\varepsilon_{0}/\omega
with
\varepsilon_{0}
being the electric and magnetic field strength.
Note that here we choose the laser field to be circularly polarized and monochromatic
King 2018
.
In fact, the spatial dependence of the vector potential is described by a function
f(x)
for the pulse shape in a realistic experiment
Dillon and King 2018
. The pulse shape for
f(x)
is usually assumed to be Gaussian with respect to the phase
\phi
. The spatial dependence of the vector potential would enter the following calculation of the
S
matrix element through the Fourier transform of the pulse shape
\tilde{f}
.
Next we instead work in the monochromatic limit.

The wave functions of the incoming proton
\mathcal{P}
and anti-muon
\mu^{+}
with
Q=+1
charge are given by the Volkov state
Wolkow 1935

normalized to the volume
V
as follows

\displaystyle\psi_{p,s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[1+\frac{Qe\not{k}\not{A}}{2\left(k\cdot p\right)}\right]\frac{u\left(p,s\right)}{\sqrt{2q^{0}V}}e^{iF(q,s)}\;,

(10)

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}^{(+)}_{p,s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{v}\left(p,s\right)}{\sqrt{2q^{0}V}}\left[1-\frac{Qe\not{k}\not{A}}{2\left(k\cdot p\right)}\right]e^{iF(q,s)}\;,

(11)

where the label “
(+)
” refers to the wave function of anti-particle,
u(p,s)
and
v(p,s)
are the usual Dirac spinors for the free particle and anti-particle, respectively, and
p
denotes the initial momentum before entering the electromagnetic background. In the external plane-wave field, they receive an “effective momentum”

q^{\mu}=p^{\mu}+\frac{Q^{2}e^{2}a^{2}}{2k\cdot p}k^{\mu}

(12)

which satisfies the following dispersion relation

q^{2}=m^{2}_{f}+Q^{2}e^{2}a^{2}=m_{f}^{\ast 2}\;,~~~f=\mu,\mathcal{P}\,.

(13)

The phase function
F_{1}(q,s)
is given by

F(q,s)=-q\cdot x-\frac{Qe\left(a_{1}\cdot p\right)}{\left(k\cdot p\right)}\sin\phi+\frac{Qe\left(a_{2}\cdot p\right)}{\left(k\cdot p\right)}\cos\phi\;.

(14)

Similarly, the Volkov state of the final proton or
\mu^{+}
in the laser field can be obtained with the substitution
p\to p^{\prime}
and
q\to q^{\prime}
. The Volkov wave function is the exact solution of the Dirac equation describing the “dressed” fermion which continuously
interacts with the traveling cloud of laser photons. It automatically includes the nonlinear effects of the laser field in the series of
ea/m_{f}
.

III
Laser-induced nonlinear Compton scattering to dark particles

III.1
Nonlinear Compton scattering to dark particles

In this section, we start from the calculation of laser-induced Compton scattering to new massive particles in intense laser field. As illustrative examples, we consider dark photon
\gamma_{D}
and ALP
a
as dark particles in the following Compton scattering processes

\displaystyle\mu^{+}/\mathcal{P}(p)+n\omega(k)\to\mu^{+}/\mathcal{P}(p^{\prime})+\gamma_{D}/a(k^{\prime})\;,

(15)

where
n
denotes the number of optical photons with energy
\omega
.

The lowest-order scattering
S
matrix element for the laser-proton production of DP
\gamma_{D}
reads

\displaystyle S_{fi}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\epsilon{1\over\sqrt{2k^{\prime 0}V}}\int d^{4}xe^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}\;\overline{\psi}_{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(x)\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\psi_{p,s}(x)\;,

(16)

where
\varepsilon^{\mu}_{D}
is the polarization vector of the outgoing physical DP.
The scattering amplitude is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\epsilon{1\over\sqrt{2k^{\prime 0}V}}e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}\overline{\psi}_{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(x)\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\psi_{p,s}(x)

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\epsilon{e^{i(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q)\cdot x}e^{-i\Phi}\over\sqrt{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}}\overline{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[1+\frac{e\not{A}\not{k}}{2k\cdot p^{\prime}}\right]\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\left[1+\frac{e\not{k}\not{A}}{2k\cdot p}\right]u(p,s)\;,

where the phase factor
\Phi
is given by

\Phi=-ea_{1}\cdot y\sin\phi+ea_{2}\cdot y\cos\phi

(18)

with
y
being defined as

y^{\mu}=\frac{p^{\prime\mu}}{k\cdot p^{\prime}}-\frac{p^{\mu}}{k\cdot p}\;.

(19)

After substituting the electromagnetic field given in Eq. (
7
), the amplitude can be factorized as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\epsilon{e^{i(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q)\cdot x}\over\sqrt{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}C_{i}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}\;.

(20)

The reduced amplitudes
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}
become

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{u_{2}}\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}u_{1}+\overline{u_{2}}{e^{2}a^{2}\over 2k\cdot pk\cdot p^{\prime}}k\cdot\varepsilon_{D}\cancel{k}u_{1}\;,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\overline{u_{2}}\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\frac{e\not{k}\not{a}_{1}}{2k\cdot p}u_{1}+\overline{u_{2}}\frac{e\not{a}_{1}\not{k}}{2k\cdot p^{\prime}}\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}u_{1}\;,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\overline{u_{2}}\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\frac{e\not{k}\not{a}_{2}}{2k\cdot p}u_{1}+\overline{u_{2}}\frac{e\not{a}_{2}\not{k}}{2k\cdot p^{\prime}}\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}u_{1}\;,

(23)

where
u_{1}\equiv u(p,s)
and
\overline{u_{2}}\equiv\overline{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})
.
The corresponding coefficients
C_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\Phi}\;,

(24)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\phi\,e^{-i\Phi}\;,

(25)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\phi\,e^{-i\Phi}\;,

(26)

where the phase
\Phi
takes the form

\Phi=z\sin(\phi-\phi_{0})

(27)

with

z=e\sqrt{(a_{1}\cdot y)^{2}+(a_{2}\cdot y)^{2}}\,,\quad\cos\phi_{0}=-\frac{ea_{1}\cdot y}{z}\,,\quad\sin\phi_{0}=-\frac{ea_{2}\cdot y}{z}\,.

(28)

Since the function
e^{-i\Phi}
is a periodic function, it can be performed by Fourier series expansion as

e^{-i\Phi}=e^{-iz\sin\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)}\;,

(29)

where the expansion coefficients are given as

c_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\varphi\,e^{-\mathrm{i}z\sin\varphi}e^{\mathrm{i}n\varphi}=J_{n}(z)\;.

(30)

This is the integral representation of the Bessel function
J_{n}(z)
. Thus, we have

C_{0}=e^{-i\Phi}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}B_{n}(z)e^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi}\;,

(31)

where
B_{n}(z)=J_{n}(z)\mathrm{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,1}-}in\phi_{0}}
. This is a well-known relation known as Jacobi-Anger expansion.
By using the following relations

\displaystyle\cos\phi=\frac{1}{2}\Big(e^{i\phi}+e^{-i\phi}\Big)\,,~~~\sin\phi=\frac{1}{2i}\Big(e^{i\phi}-e^{-i\phi}\Big)\,,

(32)

we obtain

\displaystyle C_{1}=\cos\phi\,e^{-i\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}B_{n}(z)\Big[e^{-i(n-1)\phi}+e^{-i(n+1)\phi}\Big]\;,

(33)

\displaystyle C_{2}=\sin\phi\,e^{-i\varPhi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}B_{n}(z)\Big[e^{-i(n-1)\phi}-e^{-i(n+1)\phi}\Big]\;.

(34)

Given the redefinition of integers
n^{\prime}=n+1
and
n^{\prime\prime}=n-1
, together with the completeness of the
n^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime\prime}
, they become

\displaystyle C_{1}=\cos\phi\,e^{-i\varPhi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big[B_{n+1}(z)+B_{n-1}(z)\Big]e^{-in\phi}\;,

(35)

\displaystyle C_{2}=\sin\phi\,e^{-i\varPhi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big[B_{n+1}(z)-B_{n\-1}(z)\Big]e^{-in\phi}\;.

(36)

Then, the amplitude can be simplified as

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}=ie\epsilon\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q-nk)\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}\;,

(37)

where

\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}=e^{in\phi}C_{i}^{n}\;,~~~\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}e^{-in\phi}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}C_{i}^{n}=C_{i}\;.

(38)

After integrating out the coordinate
x
, we have

S_{fi}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}=ie\epsilon\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q-nk)}{\sqrt{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}\;,

(39)

and its square is given by

\big|S_{fi}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}\big|^{2}=e^{2}\epsilon^{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q-nk)VT}{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{2}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}\big(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{n}\big)^{\dagger}\overline{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}{\dagger}}}\;.

(40)

The “overline” in above equation denotes the summation and average over the spin degree of freedom of relevant particles. For the Compton scattering, after averaging the proton spin, the squared amplitude is then

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{2}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}\big(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{n}\big)^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}}_{i}\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}\gamma_{D}{\dagger}}_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(m_{\chi}^{2}+2m_{\mathcal{P}}^{2})J_{n}^{2}(z)

(41)

\displaystyle+{e^{2}a^{2}(1+u^{2})\over u}[J_{n-1}^{2}(z)+J_{n+1}^{2}(z)-2J_{n}^{2}(z)]\;,

where
m_{\chi}
denotes the universal mass of dark particle here and below. Here, we have

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{k\cdot p\over k\cdot p^{\prime}}=\frac{\sqrt{s_{n}}}{Q^{\prime}-|\vec{q}^{\prime}|\cos\theta}\;,

\displaystyle s_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{P}}^{*2}+2n\omega(E_{\mathrm{Lab}}+\cos\theta_{\rm Lab}\ p_{\mathrm{Lab}})\;,

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s_{n}+m_{\mathcal{P}}^{*2}-m_{\chi}^{2}}{2\sqrt{s_{n}}}\;,~~|\vec{q}^{\prime}|={\lambda^{1/2}(s_{n},m_{\mathcal{P}}^{*2},m_{\chi}^{2})\over 2\sqrt{s_{n}}}\;,

(42)

where
\lambda(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-2xy-2xz-2yz
,
\sqrt{s_{n}}
is the center-of-mass (c.m.) energy,
\theta
is the scattering polar angle in the c.m. frame,
E_{\rm Lab}
is the energy of proton beam and
\theta_{\rm Lab}
is the initial scattering angle in the laboratory frame.

The amplitude of Compton scattering to ALP can be expressed in terms of its ALP-proton coupling

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{P}a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{a\mathcal{P}}}{2f_{a}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2{k^{\prime 0}}V}}e^{i{k^{\prime}}\cdot x}\overline{\psi}_{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(x)\cancel{k}^{\prime}\gamma_{5}\psi_{p,s}(x)

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{a\mathcal{P}}}{2f_{a}}\frac{e^{i(k^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-q)\cdot x}e^{-i\Phi}}{\sqrt{2^{3}V^{3}q^{0}q^{\prime 0}k^{\prime 0}}}\overline{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[1+\frac{eA\cancel{k}}{2k\cdot p^{\prime}}\right]\cancel{k}^{\prime}\gamma_{5}\left[1+\frac{e\cancel{k}A}{2k\cdot p}\right]u(p,s)\;.

The spin averaged and squared matrix element for this process is

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{2}\widetilde{C}_{i}^{n}\big(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{n}\big)^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\mathcal{P}a}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\mathcal{P}a{\dagger}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4m_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}m_{\chi}^{2}J_{n}^{2}(z)

(44)

\displaystyle+{e^{2}a^{2}2m_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}(1-u)^{2}\over u}[J_{n-1}^{2}(z)+J_{n+1}^{2}(z)-2J_{n}^{2}(z)]\;.

For laser-muon scattering to DP and ALP, the
S
matrix elements are respectively given by

\displaystyle S_{fi}^{\mu^{+}\gamma_{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ie\epsilon{1\over\sqrt{2k^{\prime 0}V}}\int d^{4}xe^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}\;\overline{\psi}_{p,s}^{(+)}(x)\cancel{\varepsilon}_{D}\psi_{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{(+)}(x)\;,

(45)

and

\displaystyle S_{fi}^{\mu^{+}a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{a\mu}}{2f_{a}}{1\over\sqrt{2k^{\prime 0}V}}\int d^{4}xe^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}\;\overline{\psi}_{p,s}^{(+)}(x)\cancel{k}^{\prime}\gamma_{5}\psi_{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{(+)}(x)\;.

(46)

We find that their squared amplitudes are the same as their respective results in laser-proton scattering but with the mass substitution
m_{\mathcal{P}}\to m_{\mu}
.
The cross section of Compton scattering is given by
Greiner and Reinhardt 1992

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|S_{fi}|^{2}\over VT}{1\over 2(1/V)}{1\over\rho_{\omega}}V\int\frac{d^{3}q^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}V\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}

V
Conclusion

The laser of an intense electromagnetic field promotes the studies of strong-field particle physics in high-intensity frontier. Nowadays, particle accelerator facilities in the world provide the high-quality muon and proton beams. They motivate the access of new physics beyond the SM by combining intense lasers and energetic muon or proton beams.

In this work, we propose the nonlinear Compton scattering to dark particles through the collision between laser pulse and muon or proton beam. We take dark photon and axion-like particle as illustrative dark particles and assume the existence of their couplings to muon and proton.
The cross sections of nonlinear Compton scattering to light dark photon or axion-like particle are calculated in strong-field QED. We also analyze the background processes with missing neutrinos. We find that

•

The absorption of multiple laser photons results in a series of edges for the cross section beyond the exact
n=1
result. For proton (muon) beam, when
\eta_{e}
exceeds
10^{3}
(
10^{2}
) and gets larger, more nonlinear effects contribute to the total cross section.

•

The limit of DP kinetic mixing can reach
\epsilon\sim 1.6\times 10^{-7}
(
1.4\times 10^{-6}
) for muon (proton) beam and is independent of beam energy.

•

The muon beam collision can probe ALP-muon coupling
g_{a\mu}
as low as
5\times 10^{-2}
(
2\times 10^{-4}
) [
2\times 10^{-5}
] for
E_{\rm Lab}=3.1~{\rm GeV}
(1 TeV) [10 TeV]. The sensitivity of proton beam collision to ALP-proton coupling
g_{a\mathcal{P}}
is three orders of magnitude weaker.

•

The laser-induced Compton scattering provides a complementary and competitive probe of dark particles with mass less than 1 MeV, compared with other experiments. Increasing beam energy or intensity parameter
\eta_{e}

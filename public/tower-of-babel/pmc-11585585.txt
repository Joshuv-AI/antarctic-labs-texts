Experimental solubility of omeprazole in pure and ethanol-modified subcritical water
Authors: Meysam Salehian, Ali Haghighi Asl, Maryam Khajenoori
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe current research aimed to study and measure the solubility of omeprazole (used to treat and reduce stomach acid) for the first time in pure subcritical and ethanol-modified water. These tests have been performed in the 25–130 °C temperature range and constant pressure of 20 bar with the use of 0–10% ethanol weight as the cosolvent. The solubility of omeprazole in subcritical water ranged from 0.0697 × 10–4 to 5.843 × 10–4 mol fraction at temperatures from 25 to 130 °C, respectively. The highest solubility was obtained at 130 °C with a 10% ethanol weight as the cosolvent, which is indicative of a significant trend of improvement in omeprazole solubility with the increase in temperature and the use of ethanol cosolvent in various weight percentages. The solubility experimental data obtained were fitted using the semi-experimental and modified Apelblat linear model, and the findings revealed an excellent correlation between the experimental data and the data received from the linear and modified Apelblat model. Also, at the 25–130 °C temperature range, no degradation, phase transition, or other changes in the structure and physical state of the drug were observed.Keywords: Solubility, Omeprazole, Subcritical water, EthanolSubject terms: Engineering, Green chemistry

Introduction

Omeprazole (C17H19N3O3S, Molecular weight 345.42 g·mol−1, Melting temperature 156 °C1) is primarily used to treat conditions related to excess stomach acid, but it has several other interesting applications2,3 such as: (1): Helicobacter pylori eradication that often used with antibiotics to treat infections caused by Helicobacter pylori, which can lead to ulcers, (2) Protection against NSAID-Induced ulcers that helps protect the stomach lining in patients on long-term NSAID therapy, (3) Potential anti-cancer properties that some studies suggest it might reduce the risk of esophageal cancer in patients with Barrett’s esophagus, (4) Treatment of Zollinger-Ellison syndrome that manages this rare condition by significantly reducing acid production, (5) Veterinary medicine that used to treat ulcers and gastrointestinal issues in animals, especially horses.

Solubility plays a vital role in the recognition of phase balance or liquid–solid equilibrium in liquid–liquid equilibrium or crystallization during extraction processes4. The dissolution rate and solubility of drugs in an aqueous environment can considerably affect their bioavailability after oral administration5. Poor aqueous solubility of the drugs reduces their bioavailability after oral administration6. The Subcritical Water (SBW) is a natural, green, environment-friendly, non-toxic, and fully available solvent7. SBW is usually known as liquid water which is heated to any temperature below its critical point at a pressure sufficient to preserve the liquid phase. It is widely used as an effective factor for hydrophilic and hydrophobic compounds because the water polarity can be changed by modifying the temperature in the appropriate flux8. Water polarity and solubility are highly dependent on the temperature. Water at ambient temperature has a high dielectric constant due to its strong hydrogen bond structure9. In subcritical conditions, with increasing temperature (at constant pressure), hydrogen bonds are weakened and as a result, the dielectric constant of water decreases and the water acquires a behavior similar to the behavior of organic solvents, which can dissolve a wide range of polar and insoluble substance and drugs10,11. The polarity of water decreases as the temperature of water exceeds the boiling point12,13. The decrease in water molecules’ hydrogen bond power and water polarity generally increases the Hydrophobic Organic Compound (HOC) solubility in water14,15. Also, many measurements on the aqueous solubility of various compounds in subcritical water reported that pressure had a minimal effect on the aqueous solubility in subcritical water, particularly when the range of pressure considered is relatively small. Typically subcritical water extractions and other subcritical water processes are conducted at pressures between 20 and 100 bar, where the solubility differences due to pressure changes are negligible16,17. Usually, solubility in the SBW and other SBW processes occurs in pressures ranging from 20 to 100 bar, where the solubility difference due to the change in pressure is tiny, and it leads to higher water solubility for nonpolar organic compounds18,19.

Hu et al. measured the omeprazole solubility in various solvents such as ethanol, methanol, 1-Butanol, ethyl acetate, acetone, acetonitrile, and tetrahydrofuran (ethyl acetate + tetrahydrofuran) in the temperature range of 333.15–278.15 K20. The empirical results indicated that omeprazole solubility in Tetrahydrofuran is higher than in other selected pure solvents. Also, omeprazole solubility in mixed and pure solvents is increased with the temperature rise at constant pressures. However, the trend of this increase varies in different conditions. Figueras et al. investigated the omeprazole solubility and equilibrium with derivatives of methyl beta cyclodextrins21. The results showed that omeprazole solubility significantly increases with each Cyclodextrin as cosolvent. The phase solubility studies and graphs of constant change indicated that the omeprazole of a complex consists of both Cyclodextrins. The molecular dynamics showed that Methyl-β-cyclodextrin (MβCD), compared to β-cyclodextrin (βCD), shows a higher apparent stability constant (KS), and it is better and deeper infused in MβCD. MβCD has proved to be an efficient amplifier for omeprazole solubility. Therefore, it has the properties required for pharmaceutical formulations in this drug. Al-Namry et al. investigated a linear method (RP-HPLC) by UV to determine the solubility of omeprazole in HC1 0.1N in 0.01 molar phosphate buffer with a pH of 7.421. The results indicated that the linear method was in the range of 1–50 μg/ml of omeprazole (a specified amount of drug), and the pharmaceutical side substances did not affect the specification of its concentration. This method was very effective against small changes in flow rate, composition, and pH of the mobile phase. The omeprazole in the form of Solid-SNEDDS dissolved faster than the commercial drug form and unprocessed dissolution. Also, this method had the necessary criteria to be performed, and it was conducted successfully in the present study22.

The present study aimed to offer some helpful data for the industrial production of omeprazole as well as an investigation of the thermodynamic attributes of its solubility. This knowledge allows for the exploration of the separation processes such as extractive crystallization and operations safety23. In the present study, for the first time, the omeprazole solubility in subcritical water was experimentally investigated. It was aimed at the measurement and determination of the solubility of omeprazole as a poorly water-soluble drug, using the subcritical water technique in the temperature range of 25–130 °C (Based on drug stability) and constant pressure of 20 bar, with a 0–10% ethanol weight as a cosolvent. Also, the thermal stability of omeprazole in subcritical water was analyzed using the TG and DSC analytical methods. The FTIR and XRD analyses were performed on the raw and processed samples of the drug to ensure the structural and crystal stability as well as the physical state of omeprazole. Therefore, the main objective of the current study was to obtain the omeprazole solubility in water to use these drugs in many stages, including reducing the size of particles and producing nanomedicine from these materials. On the other hand, subcritical water has been chosen as the solvent, as a green and environment-friendly liquid.

Devices and solubility measurement procedure

A schematic diagram of the laboratory equipment to measure the solubility using the omeprazole static method has been presented in Fig. 1. The laboratory equipment contained the filters, nitrogen tank, magnetic stirrer, equilibrium cell, collection vessel, electric heater, temperature and pressure indicator, water bath, control panel, and valves. The solubility was measured in a solubility cell with an internal volume of 100 mL. In addition, all the equipment components including the solubility cell tubes and joints were made of Grade 316 stainless steel, to achieve a high resistance against pressure and temperature in operational conditions.

The solubility measurement device and procedures.

At the beginning of the experiment, a specified amount of omeprazole and 50 mL of water were placed in the solubility cell together with a magnet. The loaded omeprazole amount in the cell was more than the amount used during the solubility operation (after the solubility procedure, the presence of pharmaceutical powder was checked). To reduce solute decomposition and oxidation, water was deoxygenated by ultrasonic waves for 40 min before being loaded into the tube. Then, it was deoxygenated by bubbling a mild nitrogen gas stream. In the next step, the system was pressurized with nitrogen under a pressure of 20 bar by opening Valve No.1. An electrical heater (DB-050772, OMEGA) was used to sustain the cell’s temperature in the specified values, and the temperatures were set by a controller to maintain the cell’s temperature at ± 0.5 K. As the electrical band heater heated up, the pressure in the system increased. At the onset of heating, Valve No.2 was occasionally opened to relieve excess pressure generated from thermal expansion. After setting the pressure and temperature, the mixing was done by a magnet stirrer. The cell’s ingredients were mixed to obtain better equilibrium conditions. As a result, the mass transfer between the soluble solid and the solvent was done more rapidly. The static time was 5 min (as set in previous experiments) until it was not possible to further increase the measured solubility in each stage. As a result, the static time of the solubility was approved. At the static time was passed, the magnetic stirrer was turned off, and Valve No. 3 was fully opened, the solution could be transitioned to the collection vessel by the 20-bar nitrogen gas pressure in the solubility cell, so that the solution is transitioned into the collection vessel. The solution was passed through a filter with a 1 µm pore size, which was placed in the solubility cell to keep the undissolved omeprazole inside the equilibrium cell. The undissolved solids were observed in the solution after each solubility test. Also, the nitrogen was used to keep the equipment pressure at a constant value (20 bar) as well as to maintain the solution in a liquid form and prevent the solvent from premature evaporation inside the cell during the sampling and experiment. When the solution is entirely drained from the solubility cell, Valve No.1 is closed and the electrical heater is turned off. In the next step, and with opening the of Valve No.4, the system pressure is decreased and the complex vessel is cooled with a water bath. The drug and solvent were prepared in a sampling container and placed at a temperature of 55 °C in an oven to be dried.

Results and discussion

TG analysis

The TG analysis was done on the raw and processed samples of omeprazole at a temperature of 130 °C to investigate the thermal behavior, (Fig. 2).

TG thermal analysis for raw and processed forms.

The TG analysis shows that in the raw omeprazole, the drug has 99% stability up to the temperature of 60 °C, while the drug began to significantly degrade at 153 °C. For the processed sample also, the drug is almost stable up to a temperature of 150 °C, and the degradation begins at temperatures above 150 °C.

DSC analysis

As seen in Fig. 3, the results obtained from the DSC experiments indicate that raw and processed omeprazole forms melt at 157.9 °C and 153.5 °C, respectively. Also, the peaks observed at 174.4 °C and 171.9 °C pertain to the thermal degradation of raw and processed forms of omeprazole.

DSC analysis for raw and processed forms.

FTIR analysis

To investigate and prove the non-degradation of omeprazole in the laboratory conditions, and to measure the solubility, the FTIR analysis was done on the raw and processed samples of omeprazole at the temperature of 130 °C. The FTIR spectrum is shown in Fig. 4. Using spectrometry and the KBr disc, it was proved that no significant changes and degradation were observed in omeprazole. In other words, since no changes were observed in the location and intensity of the peaks, the chemical structure of omeprazole is stable during the process without any hydrothermal decomposition and thermal degradation. As a result, to ensure that the drug is not degraded, all the experiments were done at a maximum temperature of 130 °C.

FTIR analysis of omeprazole in the raw and processed forms.

Dynamic methods are more erroneous than static methods for a drug with very poor solubility because, in the dynamic method, a very low solvent flow rate is used. In this state, there may be errors due to two causes: thermal degradation because of the increase in sampling time, and the error in measurement of drug amount (because of its low amount). Omeprazole is a non-polar and hydrophilic compound that dissolves in water very poorly. It is essential to ensure reaching a state of equilibrium before sampling. Therefore, the static method was used to measure omeprazole solubility in subcritical water. So that the solubility data error recording in a non-equilibrium position is minimized11,24–30.

XRD analysis

In the XRD spectrum of omeprazole shown in Fig. 5, the samples were examined with steps of 0.05 in ambient temperatures ranging from 10 to 70 °C, processed at 130 °C. Also, this spectrum is observed with six relatively sharp peaks at the values of 10.6, 12.8, 14.3, 19.9, 27.7, and 0.32°, which indicate slight changes in the crystal structure of the drug. Comparing the two patterns of XRD for the two states, it is revealed that the peak zone and intensity are lower in the processed form. It proves that with the solution of omeprazole, its degree of crystallinity is decreased. As the intensity of the crystal decreases and it approaches the amorphous state, the structure of the material becomes unstable, and as a result, the melting speed of the material increases.

The XRD spectrum of omeprazole in subcritical water in the raw and processed forms.

Omeprazole solubility

In the present study, the effects of temperature and ethanol percentage as modifiers on the omeprazole solubility in the SBW were investigated. The effects of pressure on the organic compounds’ solubility in SBW are tiny. Usually, the extraction of SBW and its related procedures are performed at pressures ranging from 20 to 100 bar, where the differences in solubility due to pressure changes are tiny19. All the experiments were conducted at a constant pressure of 20 bar to maintain the liquid state of water prevent evaporation and boiling of water at high temperatures and maintain subcritical conditions. Based on Fig. 6, the omeprazole solubility in pure and modified ethanol SBW enhances exponentially with the temperature rise. According to the results11,26–29, a significant increase in solubility can be observed in all experimental measurements with the increase in temperature30,31. This behavior can be attributed to the temperature’s direct effect on the dielectric constant, such that the solubility of SBW increases at higher temperatures. It is due to the decrease in water’s dielectric constant. The thermodynamic attributes of water depend on the stability and structure of hydrogen bonds. The dielectric constant value also depends on the stability of the hydrogen bond12. The hydrogen bonds are strong in lower temperatures, and the dielectric constant is high. At high temperatures, with the increase in the molecule’s thermal stirring, the hydrogen bond strength of water decreases, and as a result, the dielectric constant value reduces14.

Omeprazole solubility rate per temperature and ethanol percentage.

The decrease in the water dielectric constant leads to the reduction in its polarity, and as a result, the non-polar compound solubility in water15. In the present study, because the solubility is measured before decomposition temperature, there were no solubility reductions induced by thermal degradation. The ethanol addition increased the omeprazole solubility in SBW by 1.5 times, compared to the pure SBW (without ethanol). The effect of solvent on omeprazole solubility can be explained through the difference between the dielectric constants of water and ethanol. As mentioned before, omeprazole molecules are non-polar and compared to ethanol-modified water, are poorly dissolved in pure water. The increase in ethanol percentage decreases the SBW dielectric constant. Therefore, the omeprazole solubility is improved. Omeprazole is non-polar, and compared to ethanol-modified water, it was poorly dissolved in pure water. Other studies reported similar results for the effects of modifiers on other non-polar organic compounds16,18.

The ΔGsol values of the solution often decrease with the increase in temperature and do not change significantly with temperature. Solution enthalpy (ΔHsol) has a positive value in all temperatures investigated, i.e., the omeprazole dissolution is endothermic. Positive values of ΔHsol for omeprazole solution can be attributed to the breaking of stronger bonds and the formation of weaker bonds, which leads to energy consumption. Also, the positive values of ΔHsol may be because the interplay between omeprazole molecules and SBW molecules is stronger compared to the interactions between omeprazole-omeprazole and SBW-SBW molecules36. In the endothermic process, increasing the temperature increases the solubility of solutes in the solution. The positive values of entropy in the solubility of omeprazole indicate that the dissolution process is entropy-based. This is probably explained by the disruption of the arrangement of water molecules by the water molecules during the dissolution process and thus decreased the overall degree of an order37.

Conclusion

The solubility of omeprazole in SBW as a green, environment-friendly, and very cost-effective medium, was studied in the present study for the first time. The solubility of omeprazole was obtained to be 0.0697 × 10–4 to 5.843 × 10–4 (making the solubility value 20 times higher) per molar fraction, under constant pressure of 20 bar in the temperature range of 25 to 130 °C, with the use of 0–10% ethanol as the co-solvent. The thermal stability of omeprazole in the temperature range of 25–130 o C was approved by the TG and DSC thermal analyses. Also, the stability of omeprazole’s chemical and crystal structure in the mentioned temperature range was confirmed by the FTIR and XRD analyses. Using the two models namely the linear semi-experimental and modified Apelblat models per temperature, it was revealed that the present model is highly correlated with the experimental values for investigation of the omeprazole solubility.

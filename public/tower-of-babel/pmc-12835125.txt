An application of FBGs sensors to measuring the strains of industrial boiler hanger rods
Authors: Magdalena Palacz, Bolesław Bąk, Łukasz Felkowski, Marek Krawczuk
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background of the research presented is the undoubted fact that industrial boiler maintenance is essential for efficient operation and preventing failures. Traditional stress measurement methods for hanger rods (with hydraulic pressure pump) can be unreliable due to operator subjectivity. Therefore, the objective is to introduce a more accurate method for measuring the stress state of hanger rods in industrial boilers using fibre Bragg grating (FBG) sensors. The study compares traditional hydraulic pressure pump measurements with FBG sensors, which allow continuous strain measurement without shutting down the boiler. The new approach follows from the fact, that FBG sensors have been used in structural health monitoring but not in this specific application. The experimental strain measurements have been conducted on an industrial boiler hanger rod in an operational facility. The FBG sensors provided more reliable and continuous measurements compared to the hydraulic pressure pump method. After the analysis of the results obtained it has been clear that the use of FBG sensors offers a promising new approach for continuous strain measurement in boiler hanger rods, contributing to the development of diagnostic methods for estimating stress levels in industrial boilers, what is a promising conclusion.

Introduction

For the last few decades, researchers in structural health monitoring have made significant attempts to address the challenges of monitoring structures in harsh environments, particularly when it comes to strain measurements. For example, the development of robust sensor technology has resulted in the elaboration of sensors that can withstand extreme conditions such as high temperatures, corrosive environments, and mechanical stress. Recent developments in smart and networked sensors have significantly impacted structural health monitoring systems. These sensors can collect and transmit data in real time, enabling researchers to monitor structures in harsh environments online more effectively1. In addition, novel results for understanding how temperature variations, humidity, and vibrations impact deformation measurements are crucial for accurate monitoring and maintenance of structures in harsh environments. Ongoing research and advances in sensor technology continue to improve the monitoring capabilities and reliability of structural health monitoring systems under challenging conditions. Therefore, several measurement solutions have also been available to determine the uniaxial force value of the hook. Systems are widely known on the market, and some of them are strictly related to the pipeline support rod process2, but certain ones can be successfully applied to the boiler hanger rod system.

The importance of proper maintenance

The boiler hanger rod suspension system is designed to maintain a balanced force distribution according to the boiler load distribution design. Overload of the individual hanger rod can lead to unexpected areas of stress concentration in a boiler pressure part3. One of the diagnostic methods commonly used so far in industry to test the stress state of the hanger rod is based on the mechanical rule using the design of the hydraulic jack impact boiler suspension system and has been described by4. However, a practical application of a hydraulic jack system requires time-consuming preparation, including heavy hydraulic unit goods transportation to the site location.

The steps of the hydraulic pressure setup procedure must be repeated one by one on each hanger rod to calculate the individual load force of the hanger rod. Those operations do not allow for the collection of input data for continuous boiler operation, but only for a limited time when the measurement system is performed. Hanger rod load force data of the bearing rod gives a picture of the force distribution in a boiler hanger. In that sense, the force adjustment steps must be followed for each individual hanger rod, taking into account the nearby hanger rods. The specific force value is applied for each by the set pressure of the hydraulic system in the hook rod, and then the hook rod is locked with counter nuts under the required conditions. The impact of the force adjustment is observed when one hanger rod is taken into account at a time. The periodic return to the previously adjusted hanger rod is required to check the impact of the already adjusted force on the hanger rod. This method is simple and easy to perform, but the results need to be evaluated, and the impact of the physical condition needs to be taken into account.

Techniques used for measurement of force in hanger rods

Another type of measurement method used so far for tension analysis is the application of conventional electrical strain gauge measurements5. However, this method is also known for its measurement uncertainty that has been widely expressed in detail in research publications6–8. Additionally, the high number of hanger rods in the boiler suspension system might cause difficulties in implementing proper wire connections, as well as reduce the possibility of further correct connection systems due to the complex configuration of the measurement set. Some researchers have also tried to overcome the problems of electrical strain gauges by using measurement systems based on acceleration sensors that measure changes in the vibration frequency of the stressed hanger rod, which have been presented as a stress monitoring system by8.

However, the main disadvantage of the measurement techniques described above is that the boiler usually has to be taken out of operation to carry out such tests. The approaches described above do not provide continuous feedback. On the other hand, there are methods that allow for continuous measurement of the rod’s condition. Among others, a measurement method using FBG sensors is such an approach.

FBG for strain/stress monitoring

FBG sensors have emerged as a prominent technology for strain measurement in demanding environments. These sensors consist of a short segment of optical fibre that features periodic variations in its refractive index, known as the Bragg grating. This grating acts as a wavelength-specific reflector, reflecting light of a specific wavelength, termed the Bragg wavelength. The Bragg wavelength is directly determined by the period of the refractive-index variations within the fibre.

Any alteration in the fibre’s physical parameters, such as strain or temperature, induces a shift in the Bragg wavelength. This shift is precisely measurable and can be correlated with the corresponding physical parameter. For longitudinal strain measurement, the FBG sensor is securely attached to the structure or material under investigation. When the structure undergoes longitudinal deformation, the FBG sensor experiences a change in length, resulting in a corresponding shift in the Bragg wavelength. Monitoring this wavelength shift allows an accurate determination of the longitudinal strain. The applicability of FBG sensors for longitudinal strain measurement is extensive and is widely used in the monitoring of structural health of civil infrastructure, including bridges, buildings, and pipelines.

Measurement uncertainty considerations

Measurement uncertainty and the definition of true value are delineated by several standards, including the ISO Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) and the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) as described in ISO / IEC Guide 9819,20. The VIM3 (measurement standard) is referenced for its dual approaches to measurement21. These guides play a pivotal role in the advancement of expressing measurement uncertainties22–26. Therefore, also a low-incertitude FBG interrogator is essential for the measuring system, equipped with traceable wavelength reference components to ensure the accurate detection of received wavelength values. FBG interrogators, which incorporate reference components aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), facilitate internal calibration of the interrogator with each wavelength measurement. Although periodic calibration of the FBG interrogator is not mandatory due to the built-in reference components, additional calibration can improve accuracy27. The stability and repeatability of the interrogator are critical factors that affect the performance of the measuring system, and the resolution of the FBG sensor [\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}m/m] is directly contingent upon the interrogator’s resolution in wavelength measurement [pm]. The performance and accuracy of the FBG sensor and interrogator system have been widely acknowledged27,28. Consequently, the overall accuracy of the components of the strain measurement system must be considered in a comprehensive way.

Purpose and scope of the article

The manuscript under consideration details the initial quantification of strain within a hanger rod located in a power boiler, with the objective of evaluating the fatigue levels of the corresponding expansion joint. This evaluation is predetermined by the dynamic inner force fluctuations of a designated hanger rod in the boiler’s operational phase. Quantification has been performed using FBG sensors, thus marking their novel application in this particular context. The use of these sensors is optimal due to their dual function of strain measurement and ambient temperature compensation. A distinctive characteristic of this measurement system is its capacity for continuous and non-invasive monitoring of the subject structure.

The following sections of this document will provide a detailed structural analysis, including an assessment of its industrial relevance, the identification of critical components, and a discussion of the limitations of conventional methodologies. The subsequent section will describe the experimental framework in detail and provide a comprehensive explanation of the methodology and the underlying assumptions. The results of the analysis will be presented, including the measurement timeline and a selection of exemplary data representations. Finally, the manuscript will conclude with a synthesis of the findings and recommendations for future analytical efforts.

It is important to note that an unequal distribution of the boiler’s mass can induce disproportionate stress and deformation within the pressure components. This, in turn, can result in the development of stress fractures. The design of the Circulating Fluidized Bed (CFB) boiler requires that the upper hanger rods bear the weight of the boiler chamber and its constituent elements, thereby accommodating the anticipated thermal expansion during the transition from a stable, ambient state to an operational, elevated temperature state, as well as during operational load fluctuations within the Maximum Continuous Rating (MCR).These hanger rods are attached directly to the boiler and structural grid at position 6. The distribution of force across the individual hanger rods is affected by variations in the boiler’s operational load, as well as by alterations in mass and thermal expansion, resulting in shifts in the centre of gravity. Disproportionate force distribution can lead to material fractures and damage, potentially resulting in unanticipated steam release and necessitating an emergency cessation of operations to facilitate the cooling and subsequent repair of the boiler. Recovery efforts that are required to rectify such issues can have a detrimental effect on the operational reliability of the boiler and result in significant financial expenditure, both in terms of the costs of the repairs and the interruption of steam generation. However, these issues can be mitigated by real-time monitoring of the force distribution across the hanger rods, ensuring adherence to the prescribed design parameters throughout the boiler’s active and maintenance phases.

A method for assessing the hanger rods load force

Non-invasive hydraulic system

This part of the article discusses a non-invasive hydraulic system to determine the axial forces that are applied to the hanger rods. It is simple and commonly used but requires the transport of heavy equipment and sometimes gives results based only on the operator’s experience, which can be misleading. The proposed system uses a hydraulic pump, hoses and a hydraulic jack to measure the load on each hanger rod, as shown in Fig. 3. An additional support system equipped with a counternut (components 9 and 10) is required to enable this measurement. The hanger rod (component 11) is attached to the lower part of the CFB’s convection cage and transfers its weight to the underlying structural grid (component 5).

The hydraulic pump (component 3), controlled by the regulating unit (component 1), generates the hydraulic pressure that is transmitted through the hoses (component 4) to the hydraulic jacks (component 6). These jacks are placed on the structural grid (component 5) under the additional mechanical support of the hanger rod (component 9). The support is secured to the hanger rod (component 11) by a threaded lock nut (component 10). To initiate measurement, the hex nut (component 8) supporting the hanger rod in the convection cage is loosened. Subsequent incremental increases in hydraulic pressure raise the hydraulic jack plunger (component 7) until it contacts the underside of the additional mechanical support. This process continues until the hanger rod nut (component 8) separates from the structural grid (component 5), causing the support and attached hanger rod to rise. This occurs when the upward hydraulic force exerted by the piston exceeds the downward load on the hanger rod from the convection cage. The hydraulic pressure applied is recorded by the pressure gauge (component 2). This data, combined with the dimensions of the hydraulic jack plunger, is used as input to calculate the axial force on the boiler hanger rod during the test. Accurate determination of the moment of release of the hanger rod nut from the structural grid is essential for correct recording of hydraulic pressure and subsequent calculation of the actual load on the hanger rod.Fig. 4Support deflection details for hanger rod lift up (1- CFB boiler hanger rod, 2- structural grid, 3- hanger rod hex nut, 4- additional support, 5- counter nut, 6- hydraulic jack, 7- hydraulic jack plunger), prepared using Inkscape v.1.3.2.

FBG sensors characteristics.

For the measurements reported in this study, a single-mode FBG sensor capable of measuring both strain and temperature has been selected. This sensor operates within the interrogator’s spectral range without overlap with other FBG systems, accurately translating wavelength shifts into the corresponding physical parameters. According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the FBG exhibited negligible strain-induced birefringence and was readily adaptable to the target structure. Furthermore, its low optical reflectance and compliance with the fibre bending guidelines ensured minimal signal attenuation. The sensor has been attached to the specimen using the method illustrated in Fig. 6. The passive nature of FBG technology, coupled with its inherent self-referencing, long-term stability, and compatibility with most interrogators, made it suitable for the harsh environment while delivering a wavelength measurement resolution of 0.5 [pm], translating to a strain sensor resolution of ±0.5 [\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}m/m].

The interrogator’s stability and reproducibility, critical for accurate measurements, were determined to be 1 pm, aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Technical Note 1297 definitions of repeatability and reproducibility. Instrument calibration against a NIST-traceable gas cell ensured measurement accuracy. For further technical details, see the HBM FiberSensing technical notes32.

Force calculation considerations

A comprehensive analysis of the uncertainty of the FBG measurement system is essential, given the complexity of the system. The system consists of an FBG strain sensor, a temperature FBG sensor, and an interrogator, each element potentially contributing to the overall uncertainty. FBGs are typically used as strain sensors through direct application to deforming materials, and FBGs also function as temperature sensors when strain is isolated from the grating region33. The unique properties of FBGs enable the creation of transducers in which measurements are transferred to the deforming FBG. The operational principle of FBGs is based on the reflection of a narrow spectral band centred at the Bragg wavelength (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _0$$\end{document}) when broadband light is incident on the grating. The remaining light propagates through the fibre. Experimental results

The measurement procedure performed within this study can be outlined as follows. A reference force of known magnitude has been applied to the analysed hanger rod using hydraulic actuators, and the resulting reaction of the rod has been measured using FBG sensors. The deformation and stress values in the rod have been then determined from the recorded data taking into account the FBG sensor parameters as k-factor, initial wavelength, CTE, TCS, T and T0 - rasulting in application of Eq.(9). Such approach constitutes an indirect method of determining the load state in an analysed hanger rod.

Conclusions and discussion

In conclusion, the presented research shows that hydraulic systems are widely used in industrial applications due to their versatility and ability to generate significant forces. Although the technology is generally sufficiently precise for many engineering tasks, its inherent limitations limit its use in real-time, continuous monitoring applications. However, hydraulic systems remain a viable solution for industrial processes that are temporally constrained. The performance of these systems can be optimised through the implementation of rigorous calibration and maintenance protocols. The integration of reference force transmitters during hydraulic jack calibration offers a potential pathway to enhance overall system accuracy. Moreover, the hydraulic unit method presents several limitations. Primarily, it precludes the establishment of repeatable measurement conditions. Furthermore, the method necessitates the utilisation of substantial equipment, including a hydraulic pump and a lifting jack, to generate the requisite lifting forces. Furthermore, the restriction of measurements to static boiler states (either cold or hot) prevents continuous monitoring during operation. The testing process is time-consuming, involving multiple repeated measurements for each hanger rod, and this is another significant drawback. In addition, the inherent hysteresis in the hydraulic pump control unit, in conjunction with the necessity for regular system calibration, results in a reduction in both accuracy and efficiency.

In contrast, FBG strain sensors offer several key advantages. Their response to applied strain is highly linear, ensuring accurate data acquisition. Additionally, FBG sensors demonstrate exceptional repeatability, enabling reliable and consistent measurements. The inherent long-term stability of the technology ensures the integrity of the data over extended periods. The utilisation of FBG sensors facilitates both short-term and long-term continuous monitoring applications, thereby providing real-time insights into the behaviour of structures. The multiplexing of multiple sensors onto a single optical fibre further simplifies system expansion and enhances measurement capabilities.

Consequently, the utilisation of FBG sensors for the surveillance of the stress state of industrial boiler hanger rods emerges as a promising technique. Nevertheless, further experimental work is necessary to develop an effective algorithm for the processing of measurement signals and to establish a method for determining the pre-stress value in the rods under investigation. This approach holds great promise, but it is essential to meticulously plan the subsequent experiments on the actual object, taking into account the operational characteristics of the facilities, which require special attention to the continuity of the technological equipment.

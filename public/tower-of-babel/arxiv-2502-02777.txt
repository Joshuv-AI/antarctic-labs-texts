The Kolmogorov complexity of a bitstring

The

A special case is the

The

Kolmogorov complexity satisfies an additivity property: the information in a pair

Remarkably, such a result does not hold for online complexity: the sum of odd and even complexities can be much larger than the complexity of a string.

Definitions

For background in Kolmogorov complexity, see
[

,

]
.
Typically, space-bounds in Kolmogorov complexity are defined and used up to arbitrary constant factors,
see for example
[

,

,

]
.
In
[
5
]
, a definition is given with additive precision
O(1)
. We use it here as well.

We consider Turing machines that contain the following.

•

For each input, there is a separate one-directional read-only tape with end markers. (Both for the program and each auxiliary string in the definition of complexity.)

•

A one-directional write-only output tape.

•

Two binary stacks, with
push
,
pop
and
isempty
requests.

Remarks.

–

A computation uses
space
s
if the sum of the sizes of the 2 stacks is always at most
s
.

–

One may think of the 2 stacks as a standard work tape, where 1 stack represents the part left of the computation head and other stack the right part.
Note that to make this analogy work, one needs to think about a binary work tape with some “end marker” that can be moved.
This allows for cleaner statements and proofs of space-bounded additivity (with
s^{\prime}=s+O(|xy|)
without extra
O(\log s)
term).

–

In proofs, one may use a more general type of machine, which is equipped with several standard binary work tapes that have end markers.
Consider a machine with
k
such tapes. Also, consider a computation on which
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
bits of these tapes are used, and the total stack size is at most
s
.
This computation is simulated on a machine of the above type using space
s+\sum_{i\leq k}t_{i}+2\lceil\log t_{i}\rceil
.

Definition 1
.

Let
x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{k}
be strings.
The
Kolmogorov complexity of tasks
x_{1}{\leftarrow}y_{1}
; …;
x_{k}{\leftarrow}y_{k}
with space bound
s
relative to machine
V
is

\C^{s}_{V}(x_{1}{\leftarrow}y_{1};\ldots;x_{k}{\leftarrow}y_{k})=\min\big\{|p|:\forall j\mathop{\leq}k\big[V(p,y_{j})=x_{j}\textnormal{ using space }s\big]\big\}.

Proposition 1
.

There exists a machine
U
with the following property. For each machine
V
there exists a constant
c
such that for all
s,k
and strings
x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{k}
,

\C_{U}^{s+c}(x_{1}{\leftarrow}y_{1};\ldots;x_{k}{\leftarrow}y_{k})\leq\C_{V}^{s}(x_{1}{\leftarrow}y_{1};\ldots;x_{k}{\leftarrow}y_{k})+c.

The proof follows the one of
[
5
, proposition 1]
:
we follow the construction of a universal machine in which a self-delimiting description of the simulated machine

is prepended to its program.
However, this description is stored on an auxiliary tape. The constant
c
is the extra space needed for storing this description on the auxiliary tape.

We fix such a machine
U
and drop subscripts
U
in all complexities.

Definition 2
.

Let
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{*}
. The

complexity is
\C^{s}(x|y)=\C^{s}(x{\leftarrow}y)
.
If
y

.
We use the prefix code

and encode a tuple of strings

The conditional complexity with tuples is defined using this encoding, for example

The

\displaystyle\C_{\textnormal{ev}}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\min\big\{|p|:\forall j\leq|x|/2\big[U(p,x_{1}x_{3}\cdots x_{2j-1})=x_{2j}\textnormal{ using space }s\big]\big\}

\displaystyle=\C^{s}(x_{2}{\leftarrow}x_{1};\,x_{4}{\leftarrow}x_{1}x_{3};\,\ldots).

The

For unbounded complexity, thus

Remark.
The

variant of the Kolmogorov complexity of

some string or infinite sequence that has

since the odd and even complexity do not require to indicate where

However, the decision and plain complexities differ by at most
O(\log\C(|x|))

and this is less than the precision

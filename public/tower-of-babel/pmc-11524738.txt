Baveno VII for HCC: Are we there yet?
Authors: Ashraf Ullah, Umar I J Choudhary, Naeem A Khan, Syed B Shah
Published: 2024

To the editor,

We read with great interest the manuscript by Zhang et al,1 who applied the Baveno VII criteria to predict HCC risk in chronic hepatitis B (CHB)–related recompensated cirrhosis. The findings highlighted the important role of antiviral therapy in mitigating HCC incidence in this high-risk population. Although the authors provided detailed baseline and follow-up data up to an average duration of 44.5 months, we have some specific questions about the robustness of the evidence presented in this paper.

In their analysis, patients in the recompensation group had a lower rate of HCC compared to the decompensated cohort. It is important to determine if the recompensated and the decompensated groups had similar disease states prior to the initiation of antiviral therapy. For example, what proportion of the patients in each group had acute HBV reactivation leading to decompensation versus progressive deterioration of liver function over time. This information is crucial for patients with acute hepatitis flare leading to temporary decompensation are expected to have favorable long-term prognosis similar to the compensated cohort. Could the higher baseline ALT levels and HBeAg-positive status in the recompensated group reflect episodes of severe but transient decompensation from hepatitis flare? The patients in the decompensated group might have had advanced cirrhosis for a longer duration, as suggested by their older age and a higher proportion of HBeAg-negative CHB. Patients with HBeAg-negative CHB are known to have higher levels of HBV DNA integration into their genome, which is associated with an increased risk of HCC development.2,3

In this study, the patients designated in the decompensated group had clinical features, namely, older age, HBeAg-negative status, and longer disease duration, which are associated with higher HCC risk. These clinical characteristics need to be taken into consideration before one can confidently apply the Baveno VII criteria in assessing HCC risks in CHB.

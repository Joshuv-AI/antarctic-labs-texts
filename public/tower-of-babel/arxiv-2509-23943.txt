On random bipartite graphs evolving by degrees
Neeladri Maitra

Abstract
In this paper, we study a bipartite analogue of the ‘random graphs evolving by degrees’ process. We are given a bipartitioned set of vertices V V into two disjoint parts ℒ \mathcal{L} and ℛ \mathcal{R} and possibly unequal positive constants α \alpha and β \beta . The graph evolves starting from B 0 B_{0} , the empty graph (with only isolated vertices). Given B t B_{t} , a non-adjacent vertex pair u ∈ ℒ , v ∈ ℛ u\in\mathcal{L},v\in\mathcal{R} is sampled with probability proportional to ( d u ​ ( t ) + α ) ​ ( d v ​ ( t ) + β ) (d_{u}(t)+\alpha)(d_{v}(t)+\beta) , and the edge { u , v } \{u,v\} is included to B t B_{t} to form B t + 1 B_{t+1} , where d u ​ ( t ) d_{u}(t) is the degree of u u in B t B_{t} . For this model, we establish the threshold for the appearance of a giant component, the connectivity threshold for the associated multigraph variant, and provide a superlinear lower bound on the connectivity threshold for the simple graph case. For the proof of the giant component result, our methods involve setting up an exact coupling of the multigraph case with a bipartite configuration model and using existing results on the giant of bipartite configuration models. This is an adaptation of the technique of Janson and Warnke [ 8 ] where they treat the unipartite case similarly. For the connectivity results, we first set up a formula for the exact probability of the occurrence of certain connectivity events in the multigraph process, which is interesting in its own right. To then derive the connectedness threshold, we analyze a particular case of it à la Pittel [ 10 ] . For the superlinear connectivity lower bound in the simple graph case, we establish and use a tail bound on the number of isolated vertices in the multigraph process, together with a change of measure statement to go from the multi to the simple graph process.

Introduction

This paper is about a random graph process, dynamically evolving over time. Such models are motivated by different perspectives. For example, we observe most real-world networks grow over time, imagine social media networks of friendships, or research article networks, where new articles come into the literature, and one citing the other represent connections between them. Random graph models designed to mimic growing real-world networks include the Barabási-Albert preferential attachment model
[
2
]
and related variants; we point the reader to
[
1
,
6
]
for detailed discussions on the topic. However, sometimes it is also worth looking at variants of existing random graph evolutions, which shed light on the formation of certain structures in them. Consider the work
[
3
]
for example, where the authors study a variant of the classical Erdős-Rényi random graph process, with an attempt to
delay
the formation of a giant component. We also refer the reader to the works
[

,

,

,
10
,
8
]
where variants of the Erdős-Rényi process are studied.

In a by-now celebrated paper
[
10
]
, Pittel initiates the study of an
\alpha
-variant of the Erdős-Rényi random graph evolution process,
\alpha>0
a constant. In this process, one starts with an empty graph (with only isolated vertices)
G_{0}
on
n
vertices, and given the graph
G_{t}
, a currently non-adjacent vertex pair
\{u,v\}
is sampled with a probability proportional to
(d_{u}(t)+\alpha)(d_{v}(t)+\alpha)
, where
\alpha>0
is a fixed real, and the edge
\{u,v\}
is added to

to obtain
G_{t+1}
from it. Let us informally call this the
\alpha
-dynamics
. Observe that this dynamics can be interpreted as a generalization of the classical Erdős-Rényi (E-R) dynamics, where the sampling is instead done uniformly, in that, letting
\alpha\to\infty
at least intuitively recovers the latter. Indeed, as Pittel
[
10
]
proves, many known features of the E-R dynamics are recovered from the analogous
\alpha

\alpha\to\infty
.

There are certain qualitative features of the E-R dynamics that persist when considering bipartite, or in general multipartite variants. For example, in the bipartite case with equal sized partitions, say of size
n

t=n
edges (e.g., by
[
5

), which is of the same order as of the giant component emergence threshold for the original unipartite E-R graph, around
n/2
edges
[
4
]
. Note that even though the thresholds are both linear, they differ by a constant factor
1/2
, a consequence of the bipartite structure imposed. Not just the emergence of the giant component, even the connectivity threshold for both these processes appear at the same scale (see
[
5
, Chapter 4]

(n\ln n)/2
edges for the unipartite case, while around
n\ln n
edges for the bipartite case; the factor
1/2
difference also persisting here.

For the unipartite case, it is believed that when the number of edges included so far satisfies
t=\Theta(n)
, the final graph of the
\alpha
-dynamics, in many ways, belong in the same universality class as the final graph of the E-R dynamics. Pittel
[
10
]
proves for example, a giant component emerges in the former at around
t=f(\alpha)n
many edges, with

as
\alpha\to\infty
, recovering E-R behavior, and in fact the critical window for the appearance of a giant component has width of order
n^{4/3}
about
f(\alpha)n
just like the E-R dynamics, see
[
10
,
8
]
. In contrast, when the number of edges is superlinear, the effect of
\alpha
should be strong enough to cause a change in behavior from the E-R case. The first instance of a behavior exhibiting this was proved by Pittel, who showed in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
that the connectivity threshold for the multigraph version of the
\alpha
-dynamics occur at about
n^{1+\alpha^{-1}}
many edges, much larger than
(n\ln n)/2
for the E-R dynamics. He conjectures that this should also be the connectivity threshold for the simple graph
\alpha
-dynamics case, something that still stands widely open today. Towards this, we take a small first step by showing that the connectivity threshold for the simple graph case is
strictly superlinear
, in that there is an explicit
\delta>0
, such that the simple graph is disconnected with probability tending to
1
after the inclusion of at most
n^{1+\delta}
edges, see Theorem
1.5
below and the discussion in Section
4.1

Model definition, and some motivating questions.

In this paper, we are motivated by the similarity in behavior between the uni and the bipartite case for the E-R dynamics, as discussed above. We study a natural bipartite analogue of the
\alpha
-dynamics. We are given a set of vertices
V
and a partition of it into two disjoint parts
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
. One starts with the empty graph
B_{0}
(with only isolated vertices), and given
B_{t}
, one constructs
B_{t+1}
from it by randomly sampling a pair
\{u,v\}
of currently non-adjacent vertices with
u\in\mathcal{L}
,
v\in\mathcal{R}
, and including the edge
\{u,v\}
in
B_{t}
. This pair is sampled with a probability proportional to

(d_{u}(t)+\alpha)(d_{v}(t)+\beta),

where
\alpha,\beta
are fixed positive reals (possibly unequal). Let us call this the
(\alpha,\beta)
-dynamics. We have been driven by an attempt to answer the following questions.

•

Does the bipartite
(\alpha,\beta)
-dynamics for
\alpha=\beta
behave similarly to the bipartite E-R dynamics, in that it exhibits some similarity with the unipartite analogue?

•

What sort of difference does taking
\alpha\neq\beta
make?

•

Can we prove a result for the
simple
graph
(\alpha,\beta)
-dynamics that shows that when the number of edges is superlinear, already the model is quite different from the E-R case with the same number of edges? As remarked before, for the multigraph unipartite version,
[
10
, Theorem 2]
establishes this.

•

Are there constant factor gaps between the thresholds in the
(\alpha,\beta)
-dynamics and its unipartite analogue, like in the E-R case?

We answer some of these questions partially in this paper. To formally state our theorems, we need to set up some notations, which let us get into next.

Theorem 1.5

(Simple graph connectedness lower bound)
.

Consider the bipartite simple graph process
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,\beta):=\left(\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha)}\wedge\frac{1}{2(1+\beta)}\right)\wedge\left(\left(\frac{1}{4+\alpha}\wedge\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\vee\left(\frac{1}{4+\beta}\wedge\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\right).

(1.11)

Strategy and proof of emergence of giant

This proof is an adaptation of techniques developed by Janson and Warnke
[
8
]
for the unipartite setting.

Approximating with the multigraph process.

The first step is to approximate the simple graph process
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with the multigraph process
(B^{*}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
t=t_{n}\leq C(L_{n}+R_{n})
for some constant
C>0
. Further assume
\alpha,\beta>0
. Consider a deterministic sequence of bipartite multigraphs
(H_{i})_{0\leq i\leq t}
on the vertex set
V=\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{R}
, with the property that
H_{i+1}
is obtained from
H_{i}
by including a single edge. Then there exists a constant
K=K(C,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(B_{i}=H_{i}\;\;\forall\;\;1\leq i\leq t\right)\leq K\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(B^{*}_{i}=H_{i}\;\;\forall\;\;1\leq i\leq t\right)+o(1).

Thus, when the number of edges is at most linear, rare events for the multigraph process are also rare events for the simple graph process. This result is useful, if we have some control over the multigraph process. Indeed, as we will prove, it is a bipartite configuration model.

Bipartite configuration models.

Consider a bipartition
V=\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{R}
of a vertex set
V
. Assume that we are given non-negative integer sequences
\boldsymbol{D}^{(\mathcal{L})}=(d_{u})_{u\in\mathcal{L}}
and
\boldsymbol{D}^{(\mathcal{R})}=(d_{v})_{v\in\mathcal{R}}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathcal{L}}d_{u}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}}d_{v}.

(2.1)

We form a bipartite multigraph with
\boldsymbol{D}=(\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{L}},\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{R}})
as its bi-degree sequence as follows. Associate to each vertex
u\in V
a set of
d_{u}
many half-edges
H_{u}:=\{l^{(u)}_{1},\dots,l^{(u)}_{d_{u}}\}
. Take a uniformly random pairing
\mathcal{P}
of the
left
half-edges
\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{L}}H_{u}
with the
right
half-edges
\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{R}}H_{v}
. Given the pairing, to construct a bipartite multigraph on
V
, let
u
and
v
share
n_{\mathcal{P}}(u,v)
many edges between them where
n_{\mathcal{P}}(u,v)
is the number of elements of the form
(l^{(u)}_{i},r^{(v)}_{j})
in the pairing
\mathcal{P}
. We call the obtained bipartite multigraph the
bipartite configuration model
corresponding to vertex partitions
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
, and bi-degree sequence
\boldsymbol{D}=(\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{L}},\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{R}})
, and denote it as
\mathrm{BCM}(\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{L}},\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{R}})
. The key result for the multigraph process
(B^{*}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the following.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
t\geq 0
, and any bi-degree sequence
(\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{L}},\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{R}})
, the bipartite multigraph

conditioned on having the bi-degree sequence
(\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{L}},\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{R}})

.

With the last result at hand, to conclude Theorem

Proof of connectivity properties

In this section we give the proofs of Theorems

and

Lemma 3.1

(Number of isolated vertices)
.

Fix
\square\in\{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}\}
. Let
t=t_{n}
satisfy
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}}{(L_{n}+R_{n})^{1+\rho(\square)^{-1}}}=x\in[0,\infty]

the number of isolated vertices from

in the graph

•

If
x=0

\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{I}(\square,t)\geq 1\right)\to 1
.

•

If
x\in(0,\infty)
, as

,
\mathcal{I}(\square,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Poi}\left(\left(\frac{\rho(\square)}{x}\right)^{\rho(\square)}(\zeta(\square))^{-1-\rho(\square)}\right).

•

If
x=\infty
,
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{I}(\square,t)\geq 1\right)=0
.

Proof.

We work with the independent continuous-time processes
(D_{u}(r))_{r\geq 0}
for
u\in\square
. Note that denoting by
\mathcal{B}_{u}(s)
the event that no birth has taken place in the process
(D_{u}(r))_{r\geq 0}
up to time
s\geq 0
, we need to conclude about the random variable
\sum_{u\in\square}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{\square,t})}
. Using Lemma
2.8
and the monotonicity of the processes

,
whp

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\square}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{n}^{\square,+}(t,s))}\leq\sum_{u\in\chi(\eta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{\square,t})}\leq\sum_{u\in\square}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{n}^{\square,-}(t,s))},

(3.1)

where let
s=(L_{n}+R_{n})^{3/4+\rho(\square)^{-1}}
. As the first birth in any of the processes
(D_{u}(r))_{r\geq 0}
takes place after an
\mathrm{Exp}(\rho(\square))
amount of time,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\square}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{n}^{\square,\pm}(t,s))}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(|\square|,p_{\pm}\right),\;\;\text{with}\;\;p_{\pm}=\exp\left(-\rho(\square)\log\left(1+\frac{t\pm s}{|\square|\rho(\square)}\right)\right).

(3.2)

Using (
3.1
), it is thus enough to prove
\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{u\in\square}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{u}(\tau_{n}^{\square,-}(t,s))}\geq 1\right)\to 1
to conclude the first assertion of the lemma. Since
\rho(\square)>0
and
t\ll|\square|^{1+\rho(\square)^{-1}}
, let us write

where
s_{n}=o(1)
. Using (
3.2
), note that

, and

, so that a standard second-moment method finishes the proof.

The proof of the second and the third assertions are similar. For the second one, we simply note that the upper and lower bounding variables in (

) converge in distribution to the required Poisson variable, thus so does the middle one. For the third one, a mean computation shows that

, and the proof is finished using (
3.1
) and Markov’s inequality.
∎

guarantees that there are isolated vertices with high probability, so that

is disconnected. Hence we need to prove that when

,

is connected with high probability. Towards this, we need the following structural result. Pittel

makes a similar observation in the unipartite case.

Proposition 3.8

(Multigraph structure beyond connectedness threshold)
.

Assume
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{t}{\tau_{c,n}}\in(0,\infty]
. Then
whp
,

has the following structure. There is one big connected component
\mathscr{C}_{t}
, and if this component does not cover all the vertices, then the rest of the components are simply isolated vertices.

To prove this proposition, we need a few technical results.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
M
be a non-negative integer and
c,N
are non-negative reals. Then
\prod_{i=0}^{M}\left(1-\frac{c}{N+i}\right)\leq\left(\frac{N}{N+M}\right)^{c}
, and
\prod_{i=0}^{M}\left(1+\frac{c}{N+i}\right)\leq\left(\frac{M+N}{N}\right)^{c}
.

Proof.

Using the bounds
1-x\leq e^{-x}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{M}\frac{1}{N+i}\leq\int_{0}^{M}\frac{dy}{N+y}
,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{M}\left(1-\frac{c}{N+i}\right)\leq\exp{\left(-c\int_{0}^{M}\frac{dy}{N+y}\right)}=\left(\frac{N}{N+M}\right)^{c}.

The argument for the second assertion is identical, now using
1+x\leq e^{x}
.
∎

Next, we state a couple of results assuming which we first finish the proof of Proposition
3.8
and Theorem
1.2
. For a sequence

. We denote this by

Let

be a sequence of

Let us not provide the proof of this result, a proof can be worked out essentially following the argument of

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq L_{n}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq R_{n},\atop j\geq k}\mathcal{S}_{1}(k,j),\;\;\text{say.}

\displaystyle=\frac{(R_{n}-j)(t-j)}{(j+1)^{2}}\frac{(k\alpha+j)}{((L_{n}-k)\alpha+t-j-1)}\frac{(k\beta+j)}{((R_{n}-j)\beta+t-j-1)}\prod_{p=0}^{t-j-2}\left(\frac{(R_{n}-j-1)\beta+p}{(R_{n}-j)\beta+p}\right).

so that the sum

4
Discussion

4.1
Connectivity threshold lower bound for the unipartite case

For the unipartite case as was considered in
[
10
]
and
[
8
]
, an argument similar to the proof of Theorem
1.5
gives that the corresponding simple graph process is disconnected
whp
after the inclusion of
n^{1+\delta}
edges for any
\delta\in(0,1/(4+\alpha))
, here
n
is the total number of vertices, and recall edges are included with probability proportional to
(d_{u}(t)+\alpha)(d_{v}(t)+\alpha)
. Thus, the simple graph connectivity threshold is at least
n^{1+1/(4+\alpha)}
many edges. As mentioned in the introduction, this result is a small first step towards Pittel’s conjecture that the connectivity threshold for the simple graph is
n^{1+\alpha^{-1}}
.

4.2
Multipartite case

The natural multipartite case, where the vertex set
V
is multipartitioned as
V=V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{k}
, and edges between non-adjacent vertices appear with probability proportional to
(d_{u}(t)+\alpha_{i})(d_{v}(t)+\alpha_{j})
for
u\in V_{i}
and
v\in V_{j}
, turns out to be more difficult. The analog of Proposition
2.2
can be proved, where in this case one has to consider a multipartite configuration model (MCM). This is a generalization of the BCM. Here for each vertex its degree into each of the partitions is provided. Then one constructs BCM’s between each partition pair, and the union of all of these edges form the MCM. For a brief discussion of this model, see van der Hofstad
[
6
, Chapter 9]
. Proving an analog of Theorem
1.1
following the recipe of this paper (which in turn is inspired by the route-map of the paper
[
8
]
) requires – firstly, establishing the giant component threshold for the multipartite configuration model when the degree sequences between partition pairs are given; secondly, and more importantly, for a given partition pair, to understand the behavior of these degrees between the said pair. The latter seems the harder step as these degrees are not negative binomials anymore (unless all the
\alpha_{i}
are equal), however, the former is an interesting question in its own right. We leave these explorations for future work.

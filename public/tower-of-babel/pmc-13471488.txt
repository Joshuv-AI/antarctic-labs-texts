Gender Differences in Primary Care Utilization and Determinants of General Practitioner Visits Among Saudi Females: A Cross-Sectional Analysis Using World Health Survey in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia 2019
Authors: Ali M. Alzahrani, Holly C. Felix, Abdulrhman A. Alzhrani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryObjective:To document the number of visits Saudi adults have with general practitioners (GP) or family medicine doctors (FD), determine if there is an association between GP/FD visits and sex, and then identify factors associated with GP/FD visits among Saudi females.Methods:A cross-sectional analysis was conducted using data from the 2019 World Health Survey in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSAWHS 2019), a nationally representative household survey. The outcome variable was number of visits to GP/FD. Survey-weighted negative binomial regression was applied.Results:From April to July 2019, 8,912 individuals participated in the KSAWHS 2019 individual questionnaire, of whom 7,677 were Saudi nationals aged 15 years and older, had completed responses and were included in this study. Females had more GP visits than males (incidence rate ratio [IRR] = 1.26; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.06–1.49). Among females, higher use associated with having access to a private car (IRR 3.87, 95% CI 3.20–4.68, p < 0.001), living in rural areas (IRR 1.30, 95% CI 1.08–1.57, p = 0.005), being currently married (IRR 1.58, 95% CI 1.17–2.13, p = 0.003), and being separated/divorced/widowed (IRR 2.11, 95% CI 1.44–3.07, p < 0.001).Conclusion:These nationally representative findings provide a baseline for monitoring future trends and evaluating health-system reforms.

Introduction

Family medicine was first recognized as a specialty in the United States in 1969, with the practice starting in Saudi Arabia (KSA) in the early 1980s [1]. General practitioners (GP) or family medicine doctors (FD) “manage common and long-term illnesses in children and adults, focusing on overall health and well-being” [2]. The GP/FD are on the front lines of the healthcare system, serving as gatekeepers to access other aspects of the healthcare system [3]. By addressing chronic health conditions early, they can help minimize the overuse of secondary and tertiary hospitals, which see many patients with health conditions that could be addressed in primary care. For example, one study found that 82.3% patients with minor health issues visiting an emergency department in one of the three major hospitals in the Qassim province of KSA bypassed primary care for emergency department care [4].

Family medicine doctors in KSA reported preference for managing high risk conditions, addressing chronic conditions and operating preventive health clinics [5]. The Saudi populace has indicated understanding of family medicine, that GP/FD can treat most chronic health conditions and can refer patients to specialty care to treat health conditions they are unable to address; however, use of GP/FD is low [6]. This is unfortunate as chronic health conditions are prevalent among the general Saudi population. Studies show rates of diabetes at 23.1%, hypertension at 9.2% and obesity at 23.1% in the country [7,8,9]. These and other chronic conditions present significant burdens to the Saudi society and healthcare system, with high rates of attributable morbidity and mortality, and high healthcare expenditures [10].

In 2016, KSA launched a national healthcare reform effort through Vision 2030 that aims to build trust in the Saudi healthcare system and address the growing burden of chronic disease among the Saudi population. One aspect of the reform effort targeted the primary care system, considered the “cornerstone of the health system” [11]. A variety of providers and services are available in primary care clinics in KSA however, the majority of GP/FD work in primary care clinics. Previous research has shown that about half of Saudi adults in Riyadh visited primary care clinics, with females using primary care services more than males [12]. No study could be found that specifically examined GP/FD visits among Saudi adults overall or by sex. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to document the number of visits Saudi adults have with GP/FD overall, determine if there is an association between GP/FD visits and sex, and then identify factors associated with GP/FD visits among Saudi females.

Methods

A cross-sectional analysis was conducted using data from the 2019 World Health Survey in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSAWHS 2019), a nationally representative household survey. The survey was designed to generate comprehensive and up-to-date estimates of health indicators and health-related behaviors among individuals aged 15 years and older throughout KSA. Data collected included sociodemographic factors, health status, presence of chronic conditions, and patterns of access to and use of healthcare services. The Saudi Ministry of Health obtained ethical approval for the survey, and all participants provided informed consent after being informed of the voluntary nature of their participation.

The KSAWHS 2019 included both household and individual-level questionnaires. The survey employed a stratified, three-stage sampling design to achieve national representativeness. In the first stage, the General Authority for Statistics defined geographic units referred to as enumeration areas (EAs), which served as primary sampling units (PSUs). In the second stage, eight households were selected from each PSU using systematic sampling. In the third stage, one household member aged ≥15 years was randomly selected to complete the individual questionnaire. The household questionnaire was administered to the head of household or the member most knowledgeable about household characteristics.

In this study, only data from the individual questionnaires were available through the Ministry of Health (IRB approval No. 24-14 E; dated 31/01/2024). The total number of KSAWHS 2019 respondents was 8,912, of whom 7,677 had complete responses and were Saudi nationals aged 15 years and older. Non-Saudi respondents, those under 15 years of age and those with incomplete responses were excluded.

The primary outcome was the number of visits to GP/FD assessed using a single item: “In the last 12 months, how many times in total have you seen a general practitioner/family doctor (in the governmental or the private sector) or been visited by a general practitioner/family doctor, about your own health?” Responses were recorded as count data (non-negative integers).

The independent variables were the participants’ sociodemographic characteristics and health needs and availability of healthcare resources by region. The sociodemographic characteristics included age (continuous), sex (male, female), marital status (never married, married, separated/divorced/widowed), education (≤ primary school, intermediate/high school, ≥ college), residence (rural, urban), working status (working, not working), transportation (whether have access to a private car). Health needs were defined by presence of self-reported chronic conditions including diabetes, hypertension, depression, arthritis, stroke, kidney diseases, obstructive lung disease, asthma and dyslipidemia. Responses to the chronic conditions were binary as 1 for yes and 0 for no. Finally, the number of GP per region measured the availability of regional healthcare resources.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analyses were conducted using frequencies and percentages for categorical variables and mean and standard deviation for continuous variables to describe the characteristics of the study population. Survey weighted negative binomial regression was then applied to examine first whether there was an association between sex and frequency or rate of GP visits while controlling for sociodemographic factors and health conditions (Table 2) and then identifying determinants of GP visits among Saudi females (Table 3). Incidence Rate Ratios (IRR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated and reported. Statistical significance was established at p ≤ 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata/BE version 18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Survey-specific analytical procedures were employed to account for the complex sampling design and sampling weights.

Results

From April to July 2019, 8,912 individuals participated in the KSAWHS 2019 individual questionnaire, of whom 7,677 were Saudi nationals aged 15 years and older, had completed responses and were included in this study. Table 1 presents unweighted descriptive statistics for the study sample. Females represented 49.1% (n = 3,770) of the sample and males 50.9% (n = 3,907). The average age was 35.5 years (standard deviation = 13.54), and participants reported an average of 1.44 GP/FD visits in 2019. More than half of participants (52%) were not working, and 82.7% resided in urban areas. Approximately 45.2% had completed intermediate or high school, and 43.5% held a college degree or higher. Most participants were married (70.1%), while 22.5% were never married, and 7.4% were separated, divorced, or widowed. Less than half (46.7%) reported access to a private car. Regarding health conditions, 8.4% reported diabetes, 7.9% reported hypertension, 6.4% reported arthritis, and smaller percentages reported depression (1%), asthma (4.1%), kidney disease (0.3%), and stroke (0.2%). Table 3 also shows that several health conditions were significantly associated with GP/FD visits among females. The analysis revealed that females with diabetes (IRR 1.68; 95% CI 1.31–2.16, p < 0.001), hypertension (IRR 1.46; 95% CI 1.10–1.95, p = 0.010), dyslipidemia (IRR 1.40; 95% CI 1.06–1.83, p = 0.017), kidney disease (IRR 2.72; 95% CI 1.12–6.58, p = 0.027), or arthritis (IRR 1.53; 95% CI 1.12–2.09, p = 0.007) had significantly higher rates of GP/FD visits than females without these conditions. Discussion

Results indicate that in 2019 Saudi adults across the country make 1.44 visits to GP/FD per year. Previous research in KSA has also shown low rates of primary care service utilization, with one study showing just two primary care visits per year while others have showed low rates of regular medical checkups [10,13]. Saudi adults experience high rates of chronic disease as previously noted, and experience multimorbidity – having two or more chronic conditions – at rates ranging from 23.3% to 37.2% [14]. More frequent visits with GP/FD can help Saudi adults manage chronic disease, help prevent unnecessary emergency care for when conditions are unmanaged, and exacerbate.

The current study also found that Saudi females visit GP/FD 26% more often than males. Previous research indicates that females in KSA utilize more healthcare services than males making our result unsurprising [12]. This may be due to higher rates of multimorbidity among Middle Eastern females compared to males [14]. We also found that having certain chronic conditions – diabetes, hypertension dyslipidemia, kidney disease, and arthritis – were associated with GP/FD visits among Saudi females. Standards in medical care for certain chronic conditions, such as diabetes, requires regular doctor visits for proper disease management [15]. Females with these conditions may then seek more GP/FD visits than females without these conditions as was found in our study. Interestingly, we found that having a stroke was inversely related to GP/FD visits. It is unclear why this is the case, but it is possible that females with stroke history are referred to a specialist, such as a vascular neurologist, and thus see GP/FD less than those without a stroke history.

We found that residing in a rural area was significantly associated with more GP/FD visits among Saudi females. This is a surprising finding as rural areas generally have fewer healthcare services than urban areas. However, in KSA, a survey of the primary healthcare system found that there are more primary health clinics in rural areas than urban areas [16]. In contrast to our finding, one study found no difference in the rate of seeing a doctor or a specialist based on living in a rural versus an urban area of the Riyadh Province of KSA [17].

Marital status was identified in the present study as being related to Saudi females having more GP/FD visits. Specifically, we found higher rates of GP/FD visits among females who were married or who were separated/divorced/widowed compared to those who have never been married. Previous research has shown that being married is associated with using healthcare services [18]. Being married brings greater levels of support to the individual married partners and it is that support that is thought to facilitate service utilization. It is unclear as to why we found higher service utilization among Saudi females who were separated/divorced/widowed as those females are likely to have less support given the absence of a spouse.

Research indicates that access to transportation is an important facilitator for healthcare utilization. Specifically, one study on healthcare service utilization among Saudi females found difficulty in reaching healthcare was a barrier [19]. Thus, it is not surprising that we found a significant relationship between having a car and GP/FD visits.

Study limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, the data came from a cross-sectional design, which limits assessment of causality. Second, the analysis was restricted to the data available from the KSAWHS 2019 individual questionnaire, which didn't capture all characteristics associated with access to and utilization of primary care, such as beliefs and perceptions toward primary care [20]. Therefore, some important factors influencing the use of primary healthcare might be missed in this study. Despite these limitations, the study findings were based on a large sample size, remain nationally representative and provide accurate data due to the complex sampling design and weighting used in data collection. In addition, the findings can be an essential benchmark to monitor future trends in utilization of primary care and evaluate the impact of ongoing healthcare system reforms in the country.

In conclusion, this study analyzed nationally representative data from KSAWHS 2019 to examine the frequency of GP/FD utilization and their associated determinants. The analysis identified a significant association between sex and GP/FD visits. Furthermore, the analysis identified sociodemographic factors and health conditions associated with GP/FD visits among Saudi females. The results provide a reference point for primary care utilization, especially among Saudi females. Future research should examine changes in utilization over time and assess how transformation initiatives influence utilization of primary care services.

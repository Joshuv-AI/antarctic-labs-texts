Optimizing oil productivity by immiscible CO2 and N2 injection comparison of the Asmari formation in southeastern Iraq
Authors: Ahmed N. Al-Dujaili, Maaly S. Asad, Mustafa M. Hashim, Najah Saeed, Nurbol Tileuberdi, Zahraa Ayad Mahdi, Mohammed Thamer Jaafar, Sundus Hameed Mousa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Gas injection is a fundamental process for hydrocarbon recovery, whereby gas is injected into the reservoir to maintain pressure and increase production of oil or gas. This study was conducted to investigate the optimization of gas injection, differentiating between the behavior of CO2 and N2, as a potential alternative to water in southern Iraqi fields (the Abu-Ghirab field, particularly) to overcome the water supply shortage as a result of climate change. Techlog, Petrel, and CMG software were utilized to examine the CO2 and N2 behaviour. The increased percentages (CO2 and N2 injection processes) in the oil production rate are 139.45 and 66.6%, respectively. The wells located near the fault section show the best water cut (approximately 18%) among all the wells. The water cut percentages are 520 and 380 for CO2 and N2 injection, respectively, pointing out that the water cut produced by the N2 injection process is higher than that of CO2 by percentage of 36.8. An acute increase in the gas-oil ratio by N2 injection (more than 40 times on average) could result. The wells far away from the fault region have the best performance of gas-oil ratio among all the wells. For the entire field, the oil production rate for the CO2 injection process shows a significant increase in both ultimate and maximum production compared to N2. This could be due to the specifications of crude oil. The sensitivity analysis of the results from CO2 injection for the Asmari formation in Abu-Ghirab for production/injection percentage and water cut shows that the water cut percentage will be acceptable during the first 9 years of the process, with concerns of economic issues due to increasing gas-oil ratio (GOR).

Introduction

The long-term development strategy for any formation in a specific oilfield aims to maintain sustainable oil production for the maximum duration while considering the uncertainties1. The development plan has to be sufficiently flexible to optimize recovery based on the assessment of new field data and ongoing studies2,3. The field life passes through three eras that represent the oil recovery stages4, including primary (formation pressure is sufficient to extract oil to the surface), secondary (injection of water or gas), and enhanced oil recovery (to extract oil trapped in the pores)5.

CO2 injection began in the late 1950s and has garnered increasing attention over the years, particularly as concerns about greenhouse gas emissions, especially CO2, have grown6–8. The USA, with its high levels of CO2 emissions from fossil fuel combustion, has been a significant adopter of CO2 injection. CO2 is captured from various industrial sources and stored in deep geological formations like saline aquifers, depleted oil and gas fields, and coal beds in a process known as Carbon Capture Storage (CCS), aiming to reduce atmospheric CO2 emissions9,10. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA) in 2023, investments in oil and gas extraction were $239 billion. Saturation of oil with CO2 using a higher amount causes mass transfer between phases. Subsequently, leading to condensation of CO2 into the oil phase and vaporization of light and medium components of oil into the gas phase11. Phase diagrams are used to describe the different zones formed during the injection-vaporization process in the reservoir cross-section12 (Fig. 1). The relation between the vapor phase and pressure differs depending on the difference between the CO2 concentration and the critical point13. The volume of the vapor phase is inversely proportional to pressure when the concentration of CO2 is lower than the critical point. Moreover, the vapor phase volume is directly proportional to pressure when the CO2 concentration is above the critical point14.Fig. 1Various oil displacement methods by gas injection12.

Various oil displacement methods by gas injection12.

The affordability, availability, and anti-corrosivity of Nitrogen are more favourable than those of CO2 and natural gas15,16. N2 stops swelling in clay formations due to its ineffectual characteristics, obviating permeability deterioration, and keeps the rocks stable11. Various methods, including pure nitrogen, nitrogen foam, and nitrogen-water alternative injections that are tailored to different reservoir types for reservoir development17–19. The lower pressure demand to attain the mixture between the injected fluid and crude oil is known as the minimum miscibility pressure (MMP). Experiments, correlations, and the equation of state are the essential procedures to determine MMP20. Temperature and the oil and gas composition are the characteristics that affect MMP. Most correlations consider reservoir temperature, concentration of intermediate components, and the molecular weight of the heavy components21.

The unfavourable water outputs, recognized in terms of water cut, in the hydrocarbon extraction process, continue to be one of the hardest factors to manage and overcome22. Many technologies have been studied over the last few years for the injection process in oil reservoirs23, among which injecting hydrocarbon gas, CO2, and N2 are the most promising for secondary and EOR recovery24. Gas has a much higher injectivity compared to water and supplies reservoirs with additional energy25. Gas also tends to affect swelling, oil viscosity, and reduced interfacial tension in miscible injection, which often causes more mobilized oil26.

The primary objective of this study is to develop and comparatively evaluate the optimization of gas injection. The novelty of this study is that it represents the first study applied in the Iraqi fields, differentiating between the behaviour of CO2 and N2, as a potential alternative to water in southern Iraqi fields (the Abu-Ghirab field, particularly), to overcome the water supply shortage as a result of climate change.

Geological settings

The Abu-Ghirab oilfield is located in the Missan governorate, southeast Iraq, near the Iraqi-Iranian border27. It has an axial length of 30 km and a width of 5 km, with coordinates from 3,575,000 to 360,000 Northing lines and 71,000 to 73,500 Easting lines. This field has two domes (northern and southern) with a saddle zone28,29, as shown in Fig. 2.Fig. 2Left- Location of the Abu-Ghirab oilfield30, Right- Contour map of the field27.

Left- Location of the Abu-Ghirab oilfield30, Right- Contour map of the field27.

The Asmari is considered a heterogeneous, fractured distribution, and implementation of water control formation at a later stage of field development. It is recommended to adopt a cased hole perforation method for vertical and directional wells31. The reservoir consists of Cretaceous and Tertiary carbonate reservoirs within the transitional zone between the Zagros Mountains and the Arabian Plate32.

Four sub-units of the Asmari formation in this field can be distinguished33: (A) Jeribe- Euphrates, which is composed mainly of dolomite with some limestone and anhydrite34. (B) Upper Kirkuk is limestone, dolomite, and some sandstone. (C) Buzurgan Member, containing sandstone with dolomite, limestone, and shale in the upper. (D) The Middle-Lower Kirkuk is generally composed of limestone, dolomite, and sandstone35 (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Geological column of Asmari Formation in the Abu-Ghirab oilfield36.

Methods

Geologic model by Petrel™

The Asmari Formation in the Abu-Ghirab field consists of three main zones (A, B, and C). In this model, the zones were subdivided into eight zones (three for A, four for B, and one for C). The geologic model was simulated to create the porosity, permeability, and water saturation models37.

Geologic model by CMG™

The geologic model was transformed into the CMG TM to apply two scenarios: the first for Carbon dioxide and the second for Nitrogen. The injection rate for each well was 10 × 106 ft3/day with total injection rate equal to 80 × 106 ft3/day for the period between 2021 and 2036. The reservoir simulation setup was completed in Petrel™. Relative permeability curves, capillary pressure models, boundary conditions, and other properties are shown in Fig. 5. The eight injection wells were arranged as in Fig. 6. The data were simulated using IMEX (Implicit–Explicit Reservoir Simulator) in the SMG™, since PVT/EOS model was used for miscible gas injection, near-critical reservoirs, or compositionally sensitive cases, a black-oil approach yields overly simplistic and potentially inaccurate results, therefore there is no need for PVT/EOS model.Fig. 6The wells under study and injection wells in the CMG model.

The wells under study and injection wells in the CMG model.

The physical properties comparison between CO2 and N2 injection can be explained as follows: The high solubility of CO2 in oil causes significant swelling and tends to extreme viscosity reduction, while the much lower solubility of N2 in oil results in minimal oil swelling and a low viscosity reduction. For CO2 injection, higher density and lower interfacial tension lead to an unfavourable mobility ratio, viscous fingering, and poor sweep in the Asmari heterogeneous reservoir. On the other hand, lower density and higher interfacial tension for N2 create a more favourable mobility ratio, which can suppress viscous fingering and improve macroscopic sweep.

CO2 achieves miscibility at relatively low Minimum Miscibility Pressures (MMP), while N2 has a very high MMP, making miscibility unattainable under most reservoir conditions; displacement is typically immiscible. Experiences an early breakthrough for CO2 due to low IFT, and allows it to channel rapidly through large pores. Delayed breakthrough occurs in N2 due to high IFT, trapping gas in small pores. A diversity between CO2 and N2 in sensitivity to reservoir heterogeneity, when the CO2 is considered, is more sensitive to the heterogeneous reservoir than N2. As a result, CO₂ behaves as a strong solvent for mobilizing and displacing oil at the pore scale; on the other hand, N₂ is more efficient as an immiscible flooding (a pressure-maintaining agent).

Economic analysis

The Economic Evaluation characterizes the best growth option by expertise, data, and propositions with modern software to estimate profitability38. Economic indices are divided based on the time value. Discounted indicators, including Net Present Value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), and profit investment ratio, consider the time value of money, whereas non-discounted indicators (Breakeven Analysis, Sensitivity Analysis, and Exposure Point) do not39. In addition, the reduction in monetary value over time should be considered when evaluating returns.

Net present value

The economic analysis of the results indicates that CO2 injection was more profitable than N2, in terms of the price of supplied gas. For this reason, if the CO2 injection period extends by an additional eight years than the planned (with the current prices of CO2 and crude oil), the net profits will drop.

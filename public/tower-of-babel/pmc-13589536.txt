Mechanistic Insights and Therapeutic Potential of Plant-Derived Exosome-Like Nanovesicles in Skin Tissue Regeneration
Authors: Xiaomeng Ma, Jiayu Shi, Ruifang Li, Wenjie Hu, Kun Li, Le Zhao, Min Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe integrity of the skin barrier is paramount for human physiological homeostasis. Recently, plant-derived exosome-like nanovesicles (PENs) lipid bilayer nanostructures inherently enriched with diverse natural bioactive cargos have emerged as transformative nanotherapeutic platforms. Compared to conventional mammalian and microbial-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs), PENs offer distinct clinical translation advantages. These include scalable and sustainable production, exceptional biosafety, and remarkably low immunogenicity, providing a highly biocompatible alternative for regenerative medicine. This review systematically integrates the multifactorial roles of PENs across the full continuum of skin wound healing. By mediating dynamic cross-kingdom signal transduction, PENs orchestrate a seamless regenerative cascade. Initially, they efficiently mitigate inflammation by tuning macrophage polarization, suppressing inflammasome activation, and alleviating oxidative stress to restore a permissive tissue microenvironment. Furthermore, PENs drive robust angiogenesis by upregulating essential proangiogenic markers and inhibiting local ferroptosis. Concurrently, they promote peripheral neural repair via PI3K/ERK pathway activation, facilitating vital neurovascular crosstalk that ultimately stabilizes nascent microvascular networks. Beyond early wound closure, PENs crucially support the high quality structural reconstruction and functional homeostasis of skin appendages specifically hair follicles, sebaceous glands, and sweat glands through modulating key cascades such as the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Additionally, we summarize standard isolation and characterization technologies, highlighting PENs’ unique functional attributes. By exploring advanced bioengineering prospects, this review establishes a comprehensive mechanistic framework for developing PENs as safe and precise nanomedicines for complete skin functional reconstruction.Keywords: plant-derived exosome-like nanovesicles, tissue regeneration, cutaneous inflammation, wound healing, angiogenesis, neurovascular regeneration, clinical translation

Introduction

Intercellular communication mechanisms are crucial for maintaining normal physiological processes of life, and exosome-like nanovesicles (ENs) play a pivotal role in these communication mechanisms. Plant-derived exosome-like nanovesicles (PENs) are lipid bilayer membrane vesicles secreted by plant cells into the external environment. They play significant roles in regulating growth and development, cell differentiation, immune responses, the occurrence and progression of cancer, as well as virus transmission.1 PENs exhibit structural characteristics similar to mammalian exosomes and represent a safer alternative with reduced risks, owing to their lower immunogenicity, high biocompatibility, and negligible risk of pathogen transmission or tumorigenic contamination commonly associated with animal derived counterparts.2 They not only exert positive effects on various physiological processes but also carry an abundance of bioactive molecules, such as polyphenols, flavonoids, polysaccharides, and terpenoids.3 These molecules are abundant in organisms and play significant roles. Plant and fruit extracts have long been studied for their anticancer properties. Fruit derived ENs are scalable and biocompatible, offering a novel strategy for cancer therapy.4 This characteristic enables them to meet the growing demand for delivering natural bioactive compounds through nutrition or as therapeutic delivery systems. Moreover, PENs have demonstrated efficacy in modulating inflammation and oxidative processes, as well as promoting skin wound healing.5,6

The skin is one of the largest and most vital organs of the human body, composed of three structural layers: the outermost epidermis, the middle dermis, and the deepest subcutaneous tissue.7 As the primary protective barrier against the external environment, the skin performs multiple physiological functions in maintaining overall health, such as barrier protection, sensory perception, thermoregulation, immune defense, and metabolic activities.8 Skin damage can be caused by physical, chemical, biological, or pathological factors (such as burns, cuts, infections, or chronic diseases), leading to compromised barrier function, moisture loss, increased risk of infection, and reduced immune function. Severe damage may also trigger systemic inflammatory responses, posing life threatening risks.

In recent years, the biological and functional properties of ENs have begun to be extensively studied, with the beneficial effects of PENs on human health gradually gaining attention.9 This article provides a comprehensive review of the current research status of PENs and their impacts on human tissue physiological functions, skin inflammation, wound healing, and related areas. The aim is to explore the roles and mechanisms of PENs in the skin and to establish a theoretical foundation for better safeguarding human health.

Traditional therapeutic approaches for skin wounds, including surgical debridement, skin grafting, and the application of synthetic dressings or growth factor formulations, have considerably improved acute wound management.10 However, these strategies often face significant limitations, such as poor bioavailability of bioactive agents, insufficient penetration into deep tissue layers, suboptimal control over the inflammatory microenvironment, and unsatisfactory outcomes in chronic or large area wounds due to impaired angiogenesis and re-epithelialization.11 Consequently, there is an urgent need for innovative therapeutic platforms that can simultaneously modulate inflammation, promote tissue regeneration, and restore skin appendages. PENs offer distinct advantages that directly address the aforementioned limitations of conventional wound therapies. On one hand, their lipid bilayer membrane structure enables efficient encapsulation and protection of bioactive molecules, significantly improving bioavailability and allowing sustained release at wound sites;12 on the other hand, PENs possess intrinsic bioactive cargo that exerts anti-inflammatory effects, for instance, downregulating NF-κB, IL-6, and TNF-α while promoting M2 macrophage polarization and facilitate angiogenesis and re-epithelialization through PI3K-Akt and TGF-β signaling pathways.13,14 These multifunctional properties position PENs as a next generation therapeutic platform that actively participates in tissue repair.

The translational potential of PENs has gained considerable momentum in recent years. Plant sources offer abundant raw materials and scalability, overcoming yield constraints of mammalian derived exosomes,12 while advances in isolation technologies have improved batch-to-batch reproducibility.13 Moreover, integration of PENs with functional biomaterials (eg, hydrogels and microneedles) enhances skin penetration and targeted delivery.15 Notably, recent preclinical studies have demonstrated efficacy in diabetic wound healing, with lemon derived nanovesicles promoting macrophage reprogramming and angiogenesis.16,17 Although clinical translation remains in early stages, the expanding evidence supports the feasibility of PENs for skin repair.12

Despite these promising prospects, the rapid growth of this field has exposed several critical bottlenecks that must be resolved prior to widespread clinical application. Primary challenges include the lack of standardized isolation protocols affecting reproducibility, incomplete understanding of tissue specific targeting mechanisms, and insufficient long term biosafety data.18 To overcome the scalability and standardization hurdles, advanced downstream processing technologies, such as tangential flow filtration (TFF) and microfluidic based isolation, are increasingly being adopted to replace conventional ultracentrifugation, thereby securing high yield, current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) compliant manufacturing.19 Furthermore, to circumvent off-target effects and enhance deep-tissue penetration, cutting-edge bioengineering strategies are emerging. By modifying the lipid surface of PENs with specific targeting ligands or co-assembling them into stimuli-responsive smart hydrogels, researchers can achieve spatiotemporally controlled release perfectly synchronized with the dynamic phases of wound healing.20 From a regulatory standpoint, establishing comprehensive multi-omics databases (eg, lipidomics and transcriptomics) and rigorous Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) criteria will be pivotal in translating PENs from benchtop discoveries into approved nanomedicines.21 Importantly, while numerous reviews have summarized the general bioactivities of PENs, a comprehensive analysis integrating their regulatory roles across the entire cascade of skin repair is still lacking. For instance, a recent comprehensive review by Lou et al thoroughly discussed the immunomodulatory functions of PENs in skin health.22 In contrast, the present review adopts a distinct and complementary tissue regeneration-axis perspective, systematically dissecting how PENs orchestrate macrophage polarization during the inflammatory phase, promote fibroblast keratinocyte crosstalk in the proliferative phase, enhance vascular remodeling, and stimulate nerve and appendage regeneration (eg, hair follicles and sebaceous glands). This regenerative centric framework bridges the mechanistic actions of PENs with the practical requirements of functional skin restoration, while also critically assessing translational challenges in scale up production, quality control, and regulatory pathways. This article ultimately aims to provide a holistic and forward looking resource for researchers and clinicians, establishing a theoretical foundation for leveraging PENs based nanotherapeutics to safeguard human dermatological health.

The Concept of PENs

PENs are nanoscale vesicles with a lipid bilayer structure actively secreted or released by plant cells into the extracellular environment. Their diameter typically ranges from 30 to 1000 nanometers, and they resemble animal derived exosomes in morphology, size, and certain biosynthetic mechanisms, thus often referred to as “exosome-like” nanovesicles.23 PENs possess unique structures and compositions, enabling them to participate in various biological functions such as intercellular communication, material transport, immune regulation, antioxidant activity, and tissue repair.24 Based on their origin and function, they can be further categorized into various types such as exosomes, apoptotic bodies, micro vesicles, and apoptotic body derived vesicles, all of which hold significant biological functions and potential application value. Three major pathways for PENs secretion have been identified (Figure 1). ① MVBs pathway: multivesicular bodies (MVBs) fuse with the cell membrane, releasing intraluminal vesicles (ILVs) into the extracellular space, thereby forming PENs.25 ②EXPO pathway: Exocyst positive organelles (EXPOs) are double membraned structures that fuse with the plasma membrane to release vesicles. This is a plant specific unconventional pathway.26 ③Vacuolar pathway: the central vacuole fuses with the plasma membrane, releasing hydrolases and defense related proteins, and may also contribute to PENs formation.27 Among the three pathways for PENs secretion, the MVBs pathway is considered dominant.25,28 In the MVBs pathway, early endosomes mature into MVBs, encapsulating RNA, DNA, lipids, and proteins into ILVs, which are released as PENs upon fusion with the plasma membrane.3,29 This process is highly similar to mammalian derived exosomes (MDEs).30–33 The EXPO pathway releases single membrane vesicles via fusion of double membraned structures with the plasma membrane.26 The vacuolar pathway mainly functions in plant defense, releasing hydrolytic enzymes and defense proteins during pathogen attack.34

Diverse secretory pathways mediating the extracellular release of PENs.

These ENs feature a phospholipid bilayer structure and contain a variety of bioactive molecules, including lipids, proteins, RNA, and other metabolites.35 Therefore, these ENs are recognized as important mediators of intercellular communication among plant cells and also serve as cross-kingdom communication carriers. They participate in key physiological processes such as signal transduction, nutrient transport, and plant immune responses, enabling information exchange between plants and pathogens.36,37 PENs play a critical role in signal transduction. When plants are subjected to biotic or abiotic stress, PENs can transport signaling molecules to neighboring cells, thereby triggering a series of defense responses. This mechanism is referred to as the “signalosome”, which effectively regulates plant immune responses and enhances resistance against pathogens. By transferring information and substances, PENs promote intercellular interactions and coordination, supporting plant growth, development, and adaptation to environmental stress. Notably, PENs are not limited to communication within plant cells; they also participate in interactions between plants and microorganisms. By carrying specific small RNAs, PENs enable cross-kingdom communication between plants and pathogens. Such cross-boundary signaling allows plants to better recognize and respond to pathogen invasion, thereby activating their own defense mechanisms.38 PENs also play a significant role in nutrient transport and substance translocation. They can serve as carriers for transporting essential nutrients and signaling molecules required by plants, ensuring nutritional balance and material exchange between cells. This mechanism is crucial for plant growth and development, especially under environmental stress, where PENs assist plants in adjusting their metabolic processes to adapt to varying growth conditions.24,39,40 In the plant immune system, they rapidly respond to pathogen attacks by mediating intercellular signal transmission. When pathogens invade plants, the plants release vesicles to convey alarm signals, stimulating neighboring cells to enhance their defensive capabilities. Furthermore, these ENs can carry specific molecules for combating pathogens, thereby directly participating in the plant’s defense responses.41

PENs may play an important role in regulating the gut microbiome in recent studies. By modulating the composition of intestinal microorganisms, plants can indirectly influence their own growth and health. This indicates that PENs not only function within plants but may also impact health and disease states through interactions with the gut microbiota of animals, including humans.42

Isolation and Characterization of PENs

Isolation and Purification

Commonly employed isolation techniques primarily encompass the following: ultracentrifugation (UC), polymer based precipitation, sucrose density gradient ultracentrifugation, immunoaffinity capture, ultrafiltration, nanotechnology-based methods, size exclusion chromatography (SEC), and commercial kits (Figure 2).

Isolation of PENs: Overview of Major Methods.

Ultracentrifugation (UC) remains the most prevalent technique for isolating PENs and is widely regarded as the “gold standard”.43 The fundamental principle involves the application of centrifugal force to segregate PENs from biological fluids. Specifically, ENs of similar diameters are isolated through alternating cycles of low speed and high speed centrifugation. While this method is characterized by high efficiency and relative technical simplicity, its primary drawbacks include prolonged processing times and the potential for mechanical damage or aggregation of the vesicles.43

Polymer based precipitation leverages solubility differentials to isolate ENs. By employing hydrophilic polymers, such as polyethylene glycol (PEG), a hydrophobic microenvironment is induced around the ENs, thereby reducing their solubility and causing them to precipitate. The resulting precipitate is subsequently collected via low speed centrifugation. This technique has gained increasing prominence in recent years,44,45 offering advantages such as operational simplicity, cost effectiveness, broad sample compatibility, minimal ENs damage, and high recovery yields. However, it may result in lower purity due to the risk of residual polymer contaminants, which necessitates additional purification steps and may interfere with downstream analyses; furthermore, the processing duration is relatively extensive.46

Sucrose Density Gradient Ultracentrifugation isolates ENs based on density gradients. During ultracentrifugation, vesicles and biomolecules of varying densities settle into distinct layers within a sucrose gradient, enabling high purity isolation.47 The strengths of this approach lie in its superior purity and effective removal of impurities, such as proteins and lipoproteins, while avoiding exogenous chemical contamination by relying solely on physical density differences. Conversely, the method is time consuming (often exceeding 4 hours), requires substantial initial sample volumes (rendering it unsuitable for micro scale samples), and is highly dependent on expensive ultracentrifugation equipment. Additionally, recovery rates may be compromised as a portion of ENs can be lost during the process.45

Immunoaffinity capture based technique utilizes the specific binding between antibodies and antigens to isolate ENs. This technique targets specific surface protein markers (eg, TET8, CD63) using antibody functionalized magnetic beads or solid-phase substrates to selectively capture target vesicles, followed by purification through low-speed centrifugation or magnetic separation.48 Its advantages include high specificity, allowing for the precise isolation of ENs subpopulations expressing specific markers (such as PENs) and significantly reducing contamination from non-target proteins to yield high-purity analytes.45 The process is rapid and can be integrated with density gradient centrifugation to further enhance purity. Its limitations, however, include the potential omission of ENs lacking specific markers, resulting in a loss of heterogeneity information and lower overall yield. Furthermore, it necessitates prepurification steps, involves high antibody costs, and carries a risk of non-specific binding, making it less suitable for large-scale sample processing. Its application is also restricted by the requirement for predefined surface markers.49

Nanotechnology is emerging as a cutting-edge paradigm for the isolation of ENs, utilizing precise physical and chemical modalities to overcome the long standing bottlenecks in purity and efficiency associated with conventional isolation techniques. By leveraging microfluidic chip technology for the sophisticated manipulation of micro/nanofluidics integrating precision architectures such as nanopore filtration and Deterministic Lateral Displacement (DLD) arrays, high resolution fractionation of exosomes from complex biological matrices has been achieved.50 Furthermore, the implementation of noninvasive manipulation via acoustic, magnetic, and electric force fields at the nanoscale, coupled with advanced materials such as functionalized magnetic beads, ZnO nanoarrays, and click chemistry, has significantly enhanced the sensitivity of specific capture. These advancements facilitate a highly integrated workflow transitioning from rapid isolation to in situ molecular characterization, establishing a robust foundation for the application of exosomes in clinical Point-of-Care Testing (POCT).50

Size exclusion chromatography (SEC), also known as gel filtration chromatography, is an efficient separation technique based on particle size and hydrodynamic volume, particularly suitable for isolating PENs of varying sizes.43,51 The principle involves utilizing a porous gel matrix or resin filtration, where larger particles elute rapidly with the mobile phase, while smaller PENs traverse a longer path through the internal pores of the solid phase matrix, achieving precise molecular separation.52,53 This method has been successfully applied to extract PENs from cabbage; its core advantage lies in maintaining the structural integrity and biological activity of the vesicles under mild conditions, yielding high purity samples for downstream analysis.54,55 Although traditional SEC faces limitations such as sample dilution, potential loss, and the challenge of excluding impurities of similar size, the development of specialized commercial columns like the Izon qEV® size exclusion chromatography columns has enabled standardized and rapid processing (approximately 15 min), significantly enhancing the concentration and purity of the isolates.56–58 Consequently, SEC, especially when integrated with other techniques to balance yield and purity, is widely recognized as a preferred strategy for the separation of PENs.54

The isolation and extraction technologies for PENs are diverse, with each methodology possessing distinct advantages and inherent limitations (Table 1). Ultracentrifugation remains the most widely utilized isolation technique, frequently regarded as the “gold standard” in the field. In contrast, polymer based precipitation (eg, PEG-based) and immunoaffinity chromatography offer enhanced flexibility and superior specificity for target subpopulations. As technological frontiers continue to advance, the emergence of novel isolation methodologies is anticipated to further augment the efficiency and purity of vesicle separation, thereby providing robust support for the mechanistic investigation and clinical application of PENs.

Characterization Techniques for PENs

The physical characterization of PENs primarily focuses on three dimensions: size distribution, zeta potential, and morphological features. Regarding particle size and surface charge analysis, Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA) and Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS) are the most frequently employed methodologies. While DLS offers advantages in rapid detection and high sensitivity, its resolution remains relatively limited. In contrast, NTA, as a high throughput single particle tracking technology, exhibits superior performance in resolution and concentration quantification, effectively overcoming the limitations of DLS in distinguishing subtle variations in particle size.67,68 Research indicates that the average diameter of PENs typically ranges from 30 to 400 nm (Figure 3A), with a characteristically negative zeta potential (frequently between −6 mV and −30 mV). This negative surface charge distribution not only confers excellent colloidal stability but also exerts a decisive influence on the capacity of PENs to traverse biological barriers and their efficiency in cellular uptake.69–72

In terms of morphological evaluation, Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) remains the “gold standard” for acquiring nanometer scale resolution images, providing a direct visualization of the characteristic spherical or cup shaped structures (Figure 3B). However, TEM is prone to generating artificial artifacts during sample fixation and dehydration processes.69 To observe PENs in their natural state, Cryo-Electron Microscopy (Cryo-EM) enables direct imaging under near native physiological conditions, thereby offering a more authentic reflection of the lipid bilayer architecture.74,75 Furthermore, Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) is commonly utilized to observe vesicle morphology in a dehydrated state, while Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) provides high resolution surface topography and further elucidates nanomechanical properties, such as stiffness and adhesion.76,77

Beyond physical attributes, and in accordance with the Minimal Information for Studies of EVs (MISEVs) guidelines, the characterization of PENs must be integrated with biochemical analyses. This involves validating vesicle integrity through the detection of marker proteins (eg, HSP70, GAPDH), elucidating biological functions via lipidomic analysis of membrane components (eg, phosphatidylserine, phosphatidyl choline), and identifying bioactive constituents (eg, miRNAs) through RNA sequencing.77,78 Additionally, the ratio of particle count to protein concentration (eg, approximately 1.3 × 103 P/mg) has been established as a critical metric for assessing sample purity.74 In empirical research, a multidimensional analytical approach should be adopted to ensure a comprehensive and accurate evaluation of the physicochemical properties, biological composition, and functional potential of PENs. Orchestrating Skin Tissue Regeneration: Mechanistic Paradigms of PENs-Based Nanotherapeutics

Wound healing is a highly coordinated, spatiotemporal biological process comprising four core phases, hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling, among which the precise regulation of cellular behaviors during the proliferative phase critically determines the quality of tissue repair (Figure 4). However, during impaired wound repair, dysregulated immune responses frequently result in the persistence of inflammation, trapping the tissue in a hostile microenvironment. This pathological niche is characterized by the excessive accumulation of pro-inflammatory cells, the marked upregulation of inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), as well as the sustained elevation of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) activity and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels. The concomitant overactivation of MMPs and accumulation of ROS create a destructive hydrolytic and oxidative microenvironment that severely suppresses the proliferation and migration of multiple skin cell types, including fibroblasts, ultimately impairing tissue repair and contributing to the development of chronic, non-healing wounds.99 Therefore, downregulating proinflammatory cell differentiation and inflammatory mediator secretion, reducing excessive ROS production, suppressing oxidative stress, and restoring immune homeostasis represent critical therapeutic strategies for overcoming chronic inflammatory skin wounds and facilitating the transition to the proliferative phase.100

Wound healing process.101

To address these pathophysiological barriers, PENs naturally occurring nanoscale vesicles enriched in bioactive cargos such as lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids have recently been demonstrated to play pivotal regulatory roles in bridging the transition from inflammation to active tissue repair. Accumulating evidence indicates that ENs derived from diverse plant sources can effectively reprogram the hostile wound microenvironment, thereby selectively promoting the proliferation and migration of skin resident cells and other key cell populations involved in tissue repair. By acting through multiple signaling pathways and molecular mechanisms, these multifaceted nanoplatforms offer novel therapeutic avenues for accelerating high quality wound healing and comprehensive skin regeneration.

PENs as Immunomodulatory Nanoplatforms: Restoring Systemic and Local Tissue Homeostasis

PENs have emerged as promising immunomodulatory nanoplatforms capable of maintaining gastrointestinal (GI) homeostasis and regulating broad spectrum inflammatory responses.102 Inflammation is a critical component of innate immunity; however, persistent or dysregulated inflammatory signaling contributes to the development of acute and chronic inflammatory disorders as well as multiple chronic diseases.30 Increasing evidence indicates that PENs exert potent anti-inflammatory effects primarily through the dynamic modulation of host immune cells, cross-kingdom regulation of microbiota, and the targeted inhibition of pathological intracellular signaling cascades. After internalization into recipient cells, PENs initiate multiple signaling cascades that resolve excessive inflammation and promote tissue homeostasis.102

Macrophage Reprogramming and Cytokine Network Modulation

In various inflammatory disorders, macrophages often lose their tolerogenic phenotype. PENs efficiently reverse this by driving anti-inflammatory macrophage polarization and reshaping the local cytokine milieu. For instance, grapefruit derived ENs selectively target intestinal macrophages, enhancing the expression of heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and interleukin-10 (IL-10) while suppressing pro-inflammatory mediators (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α), thereby alleviating dextran sulfate sodium (DSS) induced colitis in mice without detectable toxicity uptake.86,102,103

Beyond the gastrointestinal tract, garlic derived ENs (GaENs) also bind to ligands on HepG2 cells and inhibit inflammatory responses.104 Importantly, GaENs regulate the glycolytic enzyme PFKFB3 to suppress macrophage mediated inflammation in high fat diet models by reducing TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 expression.105 Tea derived ENs further corroborate this immunoregulatory capacity by attenuating TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-12 while elevating IL-10 in macrophages.106 Notably, exploiting their natural nanocarrier properties, grapefruit ENs have been engineered to co-deliver the chemotherapeutic drug methotrexate to inflammatory sites, synergistically amplifying the suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and improving therapeutic outcomes in colitis models.42,103,106

Cross-Kingdom Microbiota Regulation and Mucosal Immunity

A unique translational advantage of PENs lies in their high stability under acidic conditions, allowing them to remain intact following oral administration, enter intestinal epithelial cells via endocytosis, and deploy bioactive cargos for mucosal therapy.107 Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs) are characterized by recurrent intestinal inflammation associated with immune dysregulation and microbial imbalance.108–110 In this context, PENs miRNAs mediate inter kingdom communication between gut microbiota and host immune systems, thereby maintaining immune microbiota equilibrium.

Ginger and grapefruit derived ENs miRNAs directly target and modulate the gut probiotic Lactobacillus rhamnosus, promoting bacterial proliferation and enhancing antimicrobial immunity.102,111 Specifically, miRNAs encapsulated in ginger ENs (GENs) target L. rhamnosus genes, increasing indole-3-carboxaldehyde production and subsequently inducing IL-22 via the Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) pathway, ultimately alleviating colitis.112 GENs also downregulate cyclin D1 and inflammatory factors in intestinal epithelial (Caco-2) cells, reducing acute colitis and preventing chronic inflammation.107 Furthermore, GENs exhibit direct antimicrobial properties by dose dependently inhibiting pathogens like Porphyromonas gingivalis.113 Similar immunomodulatory properties have been reported for vesicles derived from broccoli and grapes.102,111 Broccoli derived nanovesicles regulate dendritic cells (DCs) by activating AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and elevating IL-10 levels,102 while grape derived ENs protect against colitis through the activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway.114 Tea leaf derived ENs also attenuate oxidative stress and maintain microbiota homeostasis to treat IBD.42,106

Targeted Inhibition of Inflammasomes and Intracellular Stress Sensors

At the molecular level, PENs exert profound systemic anti-inflammatory effects by directly inhibiting critical intracellular stress sensors. PENs effectively regulate inflammation through the inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation.24 Shiitake mushroom and leek derived ENs suppress NLRP3 formation in macrophages and reduce IL-6 expression, protecting against drug induced acute liver injury.24,115 GENs additionally protect the liver by activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway via TLR4/TRIF signaling.116

Crucially, owing to their nanoscale dimensions and unique lipid composition, certain PENs can traverse physiological barriers such as the blood-brain barrier (BBB) to mitigate neuroinflammation. GaENs are efficiently taken up by brain microglia and ameliorate brain inflammation through AHR and c-GAS/STING signaling pathways.117 Oat derived ENs also cross the BBB and reduce alcohol induced neuroinflammation.118 Collectively, these findings demonstrate that PENs function as highly versatile nanotherapeutics, orchestrating systemic immune resolution through immune modulation, microbiota regulation, cytokine suppression, inflammasome inhibition, and precise signaling pathway regulation across multiple tissues.

Targeted Cellular Reprogramming: PENs Mediated Proliferation and Migration in Wound Healing

During the proliferative phase of tissue repair, PENs function as highly biocompatible nanocarriers, delivering bioactive cargos that directly reprogram the cellular behaviors of fibroblasts, keratinocytes, and endothelial cells. This targeted modulation orchestrates the rapid spatiotemporal reconstruction of damaged cutaneous tissues and restores the critical barrier function of the skin.

Coordinated Reprogramming of Dermal Fibroblasts and Epidermal Keratinocytes

Fibroblasts are the central effector cells in the dermal layer; their proliferative capacity directly dictates extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and wound contraction. Mechanistically, PENs accelerate fibroblast proliferation primarily through two interconnected pathways: cell cycle acceleration and TGF-β/SMAD signaling activation. Multiple PENs including those derived from Lithospermum erythrorhizon callus (LENs),119 Physalis (PDNCs),120 Panax ginseng (GNENs)121 and Aloe saponaria (AS-ENs)122 have been shown to upregulate key cyclin dependent kinases (eg, Cyclin D1, Cyclin E). This upregulation drives the G1-to-S and G2-to-M phase transitions, significantly shortening the cell division cycle. Concurrently, these nanovesicles stimulate the TGF-β/SMAD cascade and ERK1/2 phosphorylation (as seen with Rosa damascena stem cell ENs),123,124 upregulating COL1A1 transcription and repressing matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-1/3), thereby establishing a synergistic “proliferation matrix synthesis” microenvironment.

Furthermore, mitigating excessive ROS is critical for preventing fibroblast senescence in chronic wounds. PENs from balloon flower roots,125 coriander,20 and tomatoes126 exert potent intracellular ROS scavenging effects, frequently by activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway. This not only protects DNA and lipids from oxidative damage but also promotes a pro-regenerative macrophage polarization (M1 to M2 phenotype), fostering a stable niche for cellular expansion.

Epidermal Keratinocyte Regulation: Promoting Re-Epithelialization

Keratinocyte migration and proliferation are decisive for epidermal barrier reconstruction. PENs precisely regulate these processes without inducing the pathological hyperproliferation associated with disorders like psoriasis. For instance, ginseng derived ENs modulate the ERK and AKT/mTOR pathways to safely enhance HaCaT cell viability.127 Additionally, ROS induced keratinocyte damage can be robustly mitigated by the intrinsic antioxidant cargos of Aloe vera peel and grapefruit derived nanovesicles, which drive cell cycle entry and ECM synthesis.24,43,128

To facilitate directed cellular migration (re-epithelialization), certain PENs carry adhesion molecules that interact with cell membrane integrins. Tomato derived ENs, for example, trigger focal adhesion kinase (FAK) phosphorylation and downstream Rho family GTPases (Rac1, Cdc42).6,129 This initiates cytoskeletal reorganization such as actin polymerization and lamellipodia formation accelerating targeted keratinocyte migration toward the wound bed.

Endothelial Cell Activation and Neovascularization

Coupled with ECM synthesis, neovascularization is essential for supplying oxygen and nutrients to healing tissues. PENs from wheat5 and grapefruit6 have demonstrated concentration dependent enhancement of human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) tube formation. Furthermore, Morinda officinalis derived ENs can activate the MAPK/YAP1 signaling pathway, simultaneously stimulating endothelial proliferation and tubular networking.13

Highlighting the potential of advanced nanomedicine platforms, broccoli derived ENs have been engineered into integrated therapeutic systems (eg, SK@E-Au@Exos). By combining the innate spatiotemporal immunomodulatory properties of PENs (suppressing NF-κB while upregulating TGF-β/IL-10) with active antibacterial agents, such bioengineered platforms significantly boost endothelial and basal epidermal cell proliferation, ultimately enabling scar-free healing in challenging MRSA infected wounds.130

Translational Perspectives: Advantages and Challenges

As next generation nanomedicines, PENs offer profound advantages for wound therapy. Their native lipid bilayer structure preserves cargo stability against enzymatic degradation, while their high biocompatibility, negligible immunogenicity, and lack of in vivo toxicity bypass the safety concerns of synthetic liposomes or mammalian cell derived exosomes.131 Moreover, their widespread availability ensures cost effective scalability.

However, translating PENs from benchtop discovery to clinical wound therapy faces critical hurdles. Current efficacy data is predominantly derived from rodent models, necessitating rigorous human clinical trials.132 Technologically, standardizing isolation protocols to completely separate PENs from plant impurities remains challenging. Future research must prioritize scaling up yields under current cGMP guidelines, optimizing targeted delivery vehicles (eg, smart hydrogels), and establishing comprehensive pharmacokinetic profiles.131,133

Nanotherapeutic Regulation of Angiogenesis: Multi Pathway Orchestration by PENs

The reconstruction of a functional microvascular network is a prerequisite for successful skin regeneration, whereas impaired angiogenesis remains the pathological hallmark of chronic diabetic ulcers. Consequently, PENs have emerged as highly biocompatible nanoplatforms that orchestrate vascular repair through integrated metabolic, immune, and cellular signaling cascades.

Pro-Angiogenic Mechanisms: Metabolic Remodeling and Microenvironmental Crosstalk

PENs drive endothelial cell (EC) behavior and tube formation through distinct nanotherapeutic mechanisms. A primary pathway involves the metabolic reprogramming of ECs via horizontal miRNA transfer. For instance, ginseng derived nanovesicles (GExos) deliver ptc-miR396f to upregulate critical glycolytic enzymes (PFKM, PGK1, ENO1), thereby amplifying ATP production to fuel cell migration and sprouting.134,135 Beyond direct endothelial targeting, PENs actively remodel the inflammatory and oxidative microenvironment to indirectly promote neovascularization. Vesicles from lemon and cactus drive macrophage polarization toward a pro-healing M2 phenotype, triggering robust VEGF secretion for endothelial activation.17,136

Concurrently, specific PENs (eg, from coriander and tomato) activate the Nrf2/Keap1 antioxidant axis to scavenge ROS, shielding nascent capillary networks from oxidative damage and preserving functional CD31 expression.20,137 These microenvironmental regulations are further amplified by the direct activation of classical prosurvival cascades including PI3K/Akt and ERK/mTOR pathways yielding multi-fold increases in capillary branching.5,138,139

Bi Directional Modulation: Context Dependent Angiogenic Regulation

Interestingly, PENs function as smart nanomedicines capable of context dependent angiogenic modulation. In pathological microenvironments such as tumor associated skin lesions, specific PENs exhibit potent anti-angiogenic properties. GExos can silence the c-MYC oncogene via ptc-miR396f to reduce VEGF secretion,76 while Brucea javanica derived vesicles suppress the PI3K/Akt/mTOR axis to inhibit aberrant vessel formation.140 This unique bidirectional regulatory capacity highlights the precision medicine potential of PENs in complex cutaneous lesions.

Comparative Assessment and Translational Advantages of PENs in Angiogenesis

In the context of angiogenesis, PENs, animal derived EVs, and microbial EVs exhibit distinct biological characteristics and translational advantages. Animal derived EVs (eg, MSC exos and deer antler blood derived EVs) directly upregulate VEGF/TGF-β and promote endothelial proliferation, benefiting from well characterized mechanistic pathways.141–143 Recently, cutting edge therapies have successfully integrated these mammalian EVs with smart biomaterials such as trace element dictated modules or Mn-doped ZIF-8 hydrogels to orchestrate profound angiogenesis and antibacterial responses in diabetic ulcers.144,145 However, their broader translation remains severely constrained by complex cell culture dependencies, high production costs, batch variability, and potential immunogenic risks.141 Similarly, while microbial EVs (eg, bacterial OMVs) induce angiogenesis via robust early stage immune recruitment, their highly immunogenic profile necessitates strict regulation to prevent endotoxin driven hyper inflammation.146

In contrast, PENs provide a biosafety favorable alternative characterized by scalable, cost effective production, low immunogenicity, and an abundance of intrinsic bioactive phytocompounds (eg, flavonoids, polyphenols, plant miRNAs).134,147 They serve as highly accessible nanoplatforms that seamlessly couple anti-inflammatory regulation with safe vascular reconstruction. Despite this immense therapeutic potential, critical translational bottlenecks remain. The inherent compositional heterogeneity of PENs necessitates the urgent development of standardized production protocols, multi-omics-guided quality control, and comprehensive long term biosafety evaluations prior to their widespread clinical application.

Neurovascular Synergy: Mechanisms and Nanotherapeutic Delivery of PENs in Skin Innervation

The integumentary system is densely innervated, and cutaneous reinnervation actively orchestrates keratinocyte proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune recruitment during tissue repair.148–150 When this neuroregenerative cascade is compromised as is prevalent in diabetic chronic wounds healing is substantially delayed.151 Consequently, PENs have emerged as potent nanotherapeutic mediators of neurovascular crosstalk, offering a highly accessible “dual-targeting” approach that simultaneously addresses nerve and vessel deficits with remarkably low toxicity.76

Mechanistic Paradigms of PENs Mediated Neural Repair and Crosstalk

PENs intrinsically modulate neural differentiation and neurovascular crosstalk through diverse, multi-target molecular cascades. A defining feature of certain PENs is their capacity for horizontal miRNA transfer, which effectively reprograms endogenous signaling networks. For instance, ginseng derived GExos deliver specific miRNAs into bone marrow derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), triggering PI3K/Akt pathway activation to drive the expression of neural progenitor and mature neuron markers homeostasis.148,149,152 Similarly, the Lycium barbarum ISL@PE formulation exerts robust neuroprotection by simultaneously modulating the PI3K/Akt axis and Bax/Bcl2 anti-apoptotic pathways, thereby reducing neuronal apoptosis.153,154 Beyond direct neurogenesis, PENs actively enhance paracrine crosstalk between neural and vascular compartments. Kelp derived CA@Exos activate Camk2a mediated Erk1/2 and cGMP-PKG pathways, significantly upregulating the coordinated neurotrophic and angiogenic factors (VEGF, CD31, BDNF, NGF) required for integrated neurovascular repair.149

Microenvironmental Remodeling: Anti-Ferroptotic and Antioxidant Cascades

PENs exhibit profound antioxidant and anti-ferroptotic capacities, which are crucial for neurovascular niche regeneration under high-oxidative-stress conditions. Kelp derived CA@Exos effectively scavenge ROS and inhibit advanced glycation end products (AGEs), creating a permissive microenvironment for Schwann cell survival.149

Remarkably, Salvia miltiorrhiza derived EVs (SMEVs) counteract macrophage ferroptosis following peripheral nerve injury by upregulating protective factors (GPX4, Fth1) and suppressing lipid peroxidation.155 This anti-ferroptotic mechanism simultaneously restores VEGFA expression, establishing a direct, innovative molecular link between macrophage survival, neurogenesis, and angiogenesis at the injury site.156

Advanced Delivery Systems for Cutaneous Neurovascular Translation

To overcome cutaneous physiological barriers and achieve sustained neurovascular restoration, PENs are increasingly integrated with advanced bio-instructive materials. For example, the incorporation of kelp derived CA@Exos into conductive microneedles (CA@Exos-MNs) leverages mild electrical stimulation to enhance vesicle cellular uptake, synergistically promoting M2 macrophage polarization and deep tissue neurovascular crosstalk.149

Likewise, photocrosslinked hydrogels and 3D printed bionic scaffolds loaded with PENs (eg, G-Exos and ISL@PE) significantly prolong vesicle retention, sustain chemokine mediated stem cell recruitment, and accelerate both in situ axonal repair and comprehensive wound closure.148,153,157 However, precisely matching scaffold degradation rates to vesicle release kinetics remains a crucial parameter for optimizing long term functional recovery.158

Comparative Translational Potential and Future Perspectives

In the realm of neurovascular regeneration, animal derived EVs (particularly from MSCs and Schwann cells) provide a crucial reference standard, featuring well characterized repair mechanisms such as miR-22-3p-mediated AKT/mTOR activation and PI3K/Akt-dependent axonal growth alongside established clinical translation pathways.159,160 However, their broader application is fundamentally constrained by complex cell culture dependencies, high production costs, and potential immunogenic risks.161 Concurrently, while microbial derived EVs (eg, bacterial EVs) exhibit robust pro-angiogenic and immunomodulatory properties,162,163 their application in neurovascular regeneration is severely restricted by the inherent presence of pathogen associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), which risk inducing endotoxin driven hyper inflammation in delicate neural tissues.164

In contrast, PENs emerge as a biosafety favorable nanotherapeutic platform characterized by scalable, cost effective extraction from renewable sources and a complete absence of zoonotic or endotoxin related risks.161 Crucially, specific PENs harbor intrinsic phytocompound bioactivities such as caffeic acid mediated anti-glycation, ferroptosis regulating lipid cargos, and unique plant miRNAs (eg, pab-miR482c) that are entirely absent in mammalian or microbial vesicles.149,155,165 By systematically modulating PI3K/Akt, NF-κB, and Erk1/2 pathways, PENs seamlessly couple axonal growth with microvascular stabilization. This “dual-targeting” neurovascular synergy makes PENs exceptionally advantageous for treating chronic wounds characterized by persistent neurodegeneration and vascular dysfunction,155,166 especially when integrated with smart nanobiomaterials for sustained delivery.167

Despite this immense therapeutic potential, critical translational bottlenecks remain. The inherent compositional heterogeneity of PENs necessitates the development of standardized, multi-omics-guided quality control and purification protocols.168–170 Furthermore, comprehensive pharmacokinetic profiles, long term biosafety evaluations, and large animal clinical validations are currently absent.171,172 Future research should prioritize engineered combinational strategies that integrate structurally standardized PENs with intelligent biomaterials, ultimately accelerating their safe and precise clinical translation in cutaneous neurovascular reconstruction.

Targeting Skin Appendage Homeostasis: The Apex of Regenerative Nanomedicine

In the preceding sections, the multifaceted nanotherapeutic effects of PENs in cutaneous repair have been systematically elucidated spanning immune polarization, oxidative stress attenuation, and neurovascular cellular reprogramming. Collectively, these spatiotemporal events prime a microenvironment conducive to tissue repair. However, genuine skin regeneration extends beyond mere wound closure and inflammation resolution. The ultimate apex of cutaneous regenerative nanomedicine is complete, scar free healing, a milestone that strictly depends on the structural and functional neogenesis of skin appendages (primarily hair follicles, sebaceous glands, and sweat glands).

These appendages are far more than accessory structures; they play indispensable roles in maintaining epidermal barrier integrity, thermoregulation, and cutaneous microecological balance via lipid and sweat secretion. Crucially, hair follicles serve as vital adult stem cell reservoirs that drive sustained re-epithelialization and high-quality tissue remodeling. Therefore, transitioning from a purely systemic or microenvironmental perspective to an organ and structure oriented paradigm is essential. Understanding how PENs leverage the previously described neurovascular crosstalk and immunomodulatory networks to orchestrate appendage homeostasis will provide deeper mechanistic insights, ultimately unlocking their full translational potential for functional skin regeneration.

Direct Modulation of Hair Follicle Cycling: A Paradigm Shift in Epigenetic Therapy

Hair follicles (HFs) are currently the most thoroughly studied target among skin appendages in PEN research. PENs directly promote the transition of HFs from the telogen to the anagen phase and protect them from immune mediated damage through multiple mechanisms, introducing a novel “cross-kingdom epigenetic modulation” paradigm.

Polygoni Multiflori Radix-derived ENs (PMENs) deliver plant miRNAs (aof-miR168a, osa-miR164a) that cross-kingdom suppress the human AR. This downregulates DKK1 and stabilizes β-catenin, thereby reversing androgen-induced HFs miniaturization.173 This suggests that PENs can act as natural, programmable gene-silencing vectors, offering a highly specific alternative to conventional small-molecule alopecia drugs. Similarly, Iris germanica rhizome derived nanovesicles activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling via AKT, ERK, and GSK3β phosphorylation while restoring the inductive capacity of dermal papilla cells by mitigating mitochondrial ROS.174

Building upon the angiogenic and inflammatory paradigms discussed earlier, garlic derived nanovesicles exert a synergistic dual effect: they upregulate Wnt-1, β-catenin, and growth factors (VEGF, PDGF, TGF-β) to drive anagen entry and perifollicular angiogenesis (aligning with Nanotherapeutic Regulation of Angiogenesis: Multi Pathway Orchestration by PENs), while simultaneously suppressing NF-κB and MAPK pathways to resolve perifollicular inflammation (aligning with PENs as Immunomodulatory Nanoplatforms: Restoring Systemic and Local Tissue Homeostasis).175,176 Additionally, ENs derived from germinated Cannabis sativa seeds promote HFSC differentiation and protect follicles from immunological damage by downregulating BMP4, suppressing the AR-SP1 axis, and modulating IL-15R and STAT1 pathways.177,178 Collectively, these actions orchestrate an “immune privileged” niche vital for HFs survival.

Microenvironmental Niche Reprogramming: The “Seed and Soil” Paradigm

From a regenerative perspective, appendage stem cells act as the “seed”, while the dermal macroenvironment serves as the “soil”. PENs effectively act as “soil conditioners”, facilitating post injury HFs neogenesis indirectly by optimizing the local injury microenvironment rather than exclusively targeting follicular progenitors. For instance, cactus derived PENs promote follicle regeneration by scavenging ROS, polarizing macrophages toward the pro-healing M2 phenotype, and stimulating angiogenesis.136 Similarly, PENs derived from Allium fistulosum179 and Portulaca oleracea180 resolve inflammation and enhance vascularization in acute and diabetic wounds.

At the molecular level, PENs shift the wound environment from a chronic inflammatory state to a regenerative one by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant axis and suppressing NF-κB and STAT3 signaling, which downregulates key pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-17, IL-23, and TNF-α).181,182 Clinically, RSCEs deliver Let-7 family miRNAs to suppress inflammation and promote collagen synthesis, restoring the atrophic dermal architecture flanking HFs.124 Furthermore, a hydrogel loaded with curcumin containing nanovesicles restores skin barrier function and mitigates oxidative stress in atopic dermatitis,183 while tomato derived vesicles facilitate the coordinated migration of keratinocytes and fibroblasts essential for follicle neogenesis.126 Dandelion derived nanovesicles neutralize Staphylococcus aureus exotoxins, preserving follicle integrity in infected wounds.184

Crucially, because sweat gland functional recovery strictly depends on low oxidative and low inflammatory niches, the robust Nrf2/ARE activating properties of PENs185,186 theoretically offer potent indirect protection against inflammation induced glandular atrophy, circumventing the hostile microenvironment that typically drives glandular apoptosis.

Pilosebaceous Tropism and Trans Annexal Nanotherapeutic Delivery

The pilosebaceous unit is a lipid rich microenvironment where PENs exhibit unique biophysical and translational advantages. While direct studies of PENs in sebaceous glands remain scarce, preliminary evidence suggests PENs can modulate key lipid metabolism pathways, such as PPARγ and SREBP1c, to limit sebum overproduction and alleviate acne associated perifollicular inflammation.13

From a nanotherapeutics delivery perspective, PENs demonstrate a remarkable biomimetic tropism for follicular sebaceous pathways. The lipid bilayer of PENs exhibits a “like dissolves like” interaction with sebum, allowing them to act as a physical shunt bypassing the highly impermeable stratum corneum.187 Notably, cucumber derived nanovesicles can disrupt stratum corneum lipid packaging to significantly enhance transdermal drug delivery via the follicular sebaceous pathway.188 This intrinsic trans annexal permeability positions PENs not merely as active regenerative agents, but as ideal next generation nanocarriers for targeted drug co-loading in deep skin glandular disorders, overcoming a major anatomical hurdle in dermatology.189

Translational Paradigms, Comparative Advantages, and Future Bioengineering

PENs offer multiple advantages in dermatological applications. Compared with animal derived EVs (costly and ethically restricted) and microbial derived EVs (immunogenic and endotoxin prone), PENs are characterized by low immunogenicity, high biosafety, and a rich cargo of bioactive compounds (polyphenols, flavonoids, miRNAs) that exert multi-target effects. Comparison Among PENs, Animal-Derived EVs and Microbial-Derived EVs

However, their functional efficacy varies significantly by plant source. High cost stem cell derived PENs (eg, RSCEs) excel in anti-inflammation and dermis remodeling,124 whereas traditional medicinal herbs (Polygoni Multiflori,173 Iris germanica,174 Garlic176) directly target HFs regeneration through conserved developmental pathways. In contrast, agricultural and food derived PENs (Allium fistulosum,179 Portulaca oleracea,180 tomato,126 dandelion184) offer low cost, scalable platforms for wound closure and toxin neutralization, though they suffer from lower concentrations of active payloads and high batch-to-batch variation.

The primary limitations for the clinical translation of PENs lie in their profound heterogeneity across different species and anatomical origins, which severely complicates standardization and scalable cGMP manufacturing. Additionally, direct evidence demonstrating their therapeutic efficacy on sebaceous and sweat glands is still lacking. To overcome these shortcomings, future research should focus on establishing robust characterization frameworks and quality control standards (standardizing size, zeta potential, surface markers, and miRNA profiles). Echoing the integrated SK@E-Au@Exos platform discussed in Targeted Cellular Reprogramming: PENs Mediated Proliferation and Migration in Wound Healing, future strategies must pivot towards bioengineering PENs via surface ligand modification or synergistic drug co-loading in smart hydrogels to improve targeting resolution. Finally, employing single vesicle sequencing and spatial transcriptomics to map the precise targeting of PENs within the follicle sebaceous sweat gland axis, alongside longitudinal investigations into their long term biosafety, will be imperative for unlocking their full clinical potential.

Conclusion

PENs have emerged as promising natural nanocarriers in wound healing and regenerative medicine. They exert pleiotropic regulatory effects such as suppressing inflammatory pathways (eg, NF-κB), promoting fibroblast and epithelial cell proliferation, and enhancing angiogenesis driven by their intrinsic bioactive cargos, including miRNAs, proteins, and polyphenols. Within the landscape of nanoscale delivery, mammalian derived EVs exhibit excellent biocompatibility and tissue specific targeting, whereas microbial derived extracellular vesicles offer high amenability to genetic engineering. However, their broad clinical translation is often hindered by complex ethical considerations, prohibitive mammalian cell culture costs, and stringent requirements for endotoxin clearance. In this context, PENs offer unique and complementary advantages.197 They effectively preclude the risk of zoonotic pathogen transmission, possess an inherently immuno-silent profile, and maintain robust structural stability even in harsh acidic microenvironments. Consequently, PENs represent a safe, economically viable, and highly scalable alternative for sustained skin regeneration therapies.

To successfully transition PENs into clinical practice, their in vivo pharmacokinetic (ADME) profiles require comprehensive elucidation. Current evidence suggests that the cellular uptake of PENs is primarily mediated by non-endocytic membrane fusion and macrophage-dependent phagocytosis, while their systemic biodistribution is highly dependent on both the specific plant source and the route of administration.86,198 Following internalization, PENs predominantly enter the endosomal pathway, where efficient endo-lysosomal escape within this acidic microenvironment is a prerequisite for releasing bioactive cargos into the host cytoplasm.199 Despite these insights, precisely defining their terminal metabolic and excretory pathways (eg, hepatic versus renal clearance) remains a critical knowledge gap. Future studies must move beyond static end point observations by integrating advanced isotopic or near infrared fluorescent tracking technologies. Establishing physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models will be essential to quantitatively map their dynamic ADME processes within complex physiological environments, ultimately guiding precise clinical dosing regimens.

A major barrier hindering the widespread clinical application of PENs is the lack of standardized isolation technologies and unified quality control protocols. Current isolation techniques are often constrained by low yields, batch-to-batch inconsistency, and the co-purification of plant impurities.21 Transitioning to large-scale, cGMP compliant production requires overcoming the inherent biological variability of plant sources, which fluctuate based on climate, harvest timing, and genetic background. Establishing robust physicochemical profiling, defining specific molecular biomarkers, and ensuring product stability during industrial scale up are imperative milestones for achieving reproducible and standardized manufacturing.21 Despite these manufacturing challenges, PENs hold profound translational potential as cost effective, natural nanoplatforms for precision tissue repair. However, navigating the complex regulatory landscape requires highly proactive strategies. Developers must clarify the specific regulatory classification of PENs early in the development pipeline determining whether they fall under medical devices, botanical drugs, biological products, or combination products based on their degree of manipulation and intended therapeutic claims.200 To streamline the approval process, it is highly recommended to implement Quality by Design (QbD) principles and maintain GMP compliant documentation starting from the preclinical stages. Transparent reporting of botanical sourcing, reproducible validation of biological activity, and early pre-submission engagement with regulatory agencies (such as the FDA or EMA) will pave a definitive pathway from bench to bedside.21

In conclusion, PENs represent a highly promising and transformative nanoplatform. With continuing advances in nanotechnology, multi-omics, and biomaterials engineering, future research must focus on deciphering the precise molecular mechanisms underlying their cross-kingdom regulation. By continuously optimizing isolation efficiencies and employing surface engineering strategies to enhance targeted delivery, PENs are poised to evolve into safe, highly efficient, and clinically viable therapeutic nanocarriers for regenerative medicine.

on a conjecture about the sum-freedom of the binary multiplicative inverse function
Xiang-dong Hou, Shujun Zhao

Abstract
. A recent conjecture by C. Carlet on the sum-freedom of the binary multiplicative inverse function can be stated as follows: For each pair of positive integers ( n , k ) (n,k) with 3 ≤ k ≤ n − 3 3\leq k\leq n-3 , there is a k k -dimensional 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} -subspace E E of 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} such that ∑ 0 ≠ ∈ E 1 / u = 0 \sum_{0\neq\in E}1/u=0 . We confirm this conjecture when n n is not a prime.

1.
Introduction

While studying certain mathematical questions with potential applications in cryptography, C. Carlet introduced the notion of sum-free functions
[
3
,
4
]
. A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is said to be
k
th order sum-free
if
\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\neq 0
for every
k
-dimensional affine subspace
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. (Throughout this paper, unless specified otherwise, subspaces and affine subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
are
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-subspaces and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-affine subspaces.) The
2
nd order sum-free functions are precisely
almost perfect nonlinear
(APN) functions, which were introduced in
[
11
]
and have since been extensively studied for their immunity to differential cryptanalysis in block ciphers; see
[
2
]
and the references therein. The multiplicative inverse function
f_{\text{\rm inv}}:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, defined by
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(x)=1/x
for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{*}
and
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(0)=0
, is known to be APN for odd
n

[
12
]
, and this function has been used in the
S
-boxes of the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES)
[
6
]
. Carlet
[
3
]
systematically studied the values of
k
for which
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free, and he left open the following question which is referred to as Carlet’s Conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For
3\leq k\leq n-3
,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free.

We know that
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
1
st order sum-free (trivially), and it is
2
nd order sum-free if and only if
n
is odd
[
12
]
. Moreover, for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free if and only if it is
(n-k)
th order sum-free
[
3
]
. Therefore, a positive solution to Conjecture
1.1
would provide a complete determination of all values of
1\leq k\leq n-1
for which
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free.

It is known that if
A
is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
not containing
0
, then
\sum_{x\in A}1/x\neq 0

[
4
, Theorem 1]
. Therefore, Conjecture
1.1
is equivalent to the claim that for each pair
(n,k)
with
3\leq k\leq n-3
, there is a
k
-dimensional subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
such that

(1.1)

\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u}=0.

We call such a subspace
E
a
zero-sum subspace
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. A subspace
E
with
\sum_{0\neq u\in E}1/u\neq 0
is called a
non zero-sum subspace
. There are mainly two approaches to zero-sum subspaces. The first one is algebraic and combinatorial: one uses various secondary constructions to produce zero-sum subspaces with desired dimensions. The other approach is algebro-geometric: one uses the Land-Weil bound on the number of zeros of absolutely irreducible polynomials over finite fields to prove the existence of zero-sum subspaces. Using these methods, Conjecture
1.1
has been confirmed under various conditions; see Section 2. However, the conjecture itself remains open. The main result of the present paper is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Conjecture
1.1
is true when
n
is not a prime.

In Section 2, we review some previous results that are essential for the present paper. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Section 3; the proof relies on several previous results and a critical refinement of one of them. Theorem
1.2
compares to an early weaker version in
[
7
, Theorem 3.6]
which states that Conjecture
1.1
is true if the minimal prime divisor
l
of
n
satisfies
(l-1)(l+2)\leq(n+1)/2
.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

It is well known (see for example
[
10
, Lemma 3.51]
) that

(3.1)

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\prod_{0\neq(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}(a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}).

For all
0\leq i\leq k
, we have
\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})\mid\Delta_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
since
(a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})\mid\Delta_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
for all
0\neq(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and treat them as being fixed. For
k>0
define

(3.2)

F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=F_{k+l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=\frac{\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})}.

(Note that
F_{k,l}
depends not only on
l
, but also on
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
.) Here are a few straightforward facts about
\Delta
,
\Delta_{1}
and
F_{k,l}
:

•

v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
if and only if
\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})

\neq 0
.

•

v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
form a basis of a non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
if and only if
\Delta_{1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\neq 0
.

•

\deg\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=2^{k+l}-2^{l}
.

•

If
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
(
l>0
) is a basis of a non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, then

\displaystyle\deg\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=2^{k+l+1}-2^{l+1}\quad\text{(see Lemma~\ref{L1} below)},

\displaystyle\deg F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=2^{k+l}-2^{l},

\displaystyle\deg_{X_{j}}\!F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=2^{k+l-1},\quad 1\leq j\leq k.

•

For each
1\leq j\leq k
,
\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
is a
2
-polynomial in
X_{j}
whose coefficient of
X_{j}
is nonzero, i.e.,

\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}X_{j}^{2^{i}},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{j-1},X_{j+1},\dots,X_{k}]
and
a_{0}\neq 0
. Consequently,
\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
is separable in
X_{j}
, and hence
\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},

u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
is square-free in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
.

•

It follows from (
3.2
) that

(3.3)

\gcd(F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}),\,\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l}))=1

since
\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
is square-free.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
(
l>0
) is a basis of a non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Then

\deg\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=2^{k+l+1}-2^{l+1}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=\,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{2^{l+1}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2^{l+1}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{2^{k+l}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2^{k+l}}\end{matrix}\right|\Delta_{1}(u_{,}\dots,u_{l})

\displaystyle+\text{terms of lower degree},

where
\Delta_{1}(u_{,}\dots,u_{l})\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, where
l\leq n-2
. Then there exists
u_{l+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
such that
u_{1},\dots,u_{l+1}
is a basis of an
(l+1)
-dimensional non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that there exists
u_{l+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
such that
\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l+1})\neq 0
. Consider
\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l},X)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}[X]
. We have

\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l},X)=X\Delta(u_{1}^{2^{2}},\dots,u_{l}^{2^{2}})+\text{terms of higher degree},

where
\Delta(u_{1}^{2^{2}},\dots,u_{l}^{2^{2}})=\Delta(u_{1},\dots,u_{l})^{4}\neq 0
. Hence
\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l},X)\neq 0
. Since
\deg\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l},X)\leq 2^{l+1}<2^{n}
, there exists
u_{l+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
such that
\Delta_{1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{l+1})\neq 0
.
∎

It was proved in
[
5

that
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=F_{k,0}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible (i.e., irreducible over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) when
k\geq 3
. We extend this result to the following form as the first step towards the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
k\geq 2
,
l>0
, and
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
is a basis of non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Then
F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible.

Proof.

By Equations (4.3) – (4.6) in
[
5
]
, we have

(3.4)

F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=C_{k+l-1}X_{1}^{2^{k+l-1}}+C_{k+l-2}X_{1}^{2^{k+l-2}}+\cdots+C_{0}X_{1}+C_{-1},

where

(3.5)

\displaystyle C_{k+l-1}=F_{k-1,l}(X_{2},\dots,X_{k}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle C_{i}=F_{k-1,l}(X_{2},\dots,X_{k})\frac{\Delta_{i}(X_{2},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})}{\Delta(X_{2},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})},\quad 0\leq i\leq k+l-2,

(3.7)

\displaystyle C_{-1}=\Delta(X_{2},\dots,X_{k},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})^{2}.

Note that
\deg C_{k+l-1}=\deg F_{k-1,l}(X_{2},\dots,X_{k})=2^{k-1+l}-2^{l}>0
since
k\geq 2
. By (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and (
3.7
),

(3.8)

\gcd(C_{k+l-1},C_{-1})=1.

Thus
F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
as a polynomial in
X_{1}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{2},\dots,X_{k}]
is primitive. Let
f\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{1}[X_{2},\dots,X_{k}]
be any irreducible factor of
C_{k+l-1}
. Then
f\mid C_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq k+l-2
(by (
3.6
)),
f\nmid C_{-1}
(by (
3.8
)), and
f^{2}\nmid C_{k+l-1}
(since
C_{k+l-1}=F_{k-1,l}(X_{2},\dots,X_{k})
is square-free). By Eisenstein’s criterion,
F_{k,l}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is irreducible in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
.
∎

For
f(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
, where
\mathbb{F}
is any field, define

V_{\mathbb{F}^{k}}(f)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{F}^{k}:f(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=0\}.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
l>0
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be a basis of a non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. If

(3.9)

n\geq 4l+7,

then
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
can be extended to a basis of an
(l+2)
-dimensional zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
q=2^{n}
. By Theorem
2.4
, it suffices to show that there exists
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
such that
F_{2,l}(x_{1},x_{2})=F_{2+l}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})=0
but
\Delta(x_{1},x_{2},u_{1},\dots,u_{l})\neq 0
, that is,

(3.10)

\bigl|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(F_{2,l})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(\Delta(X_{1},X_{2},u_{1},\dots,u_{l}))\bigr|>0.

By the Hasse-Weil bound (as stated in
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
), we have

\displaystyle\bigl|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(F_{2,l})\bigr|\,

\displaystyle\geq q+1-(2^{2+l}-2^{l}-1)(2^{2+l}-2^{l}-2)q^{1/2}-2^{1+l}

\displaystyle=q-(3\cdot 2^{l}-1)(3\cdot 2^{l}-2)q^{1/2}+1-2^{1+l}

\displaystyle>q-(9\cdot 2^{2l}-9\cdot 2^{l}+2)q^{1/2}-q^{1/2}

\displaystyle=q-9\cdot 2^{2l}q^{1/2}+(9\cdot 2^{l}-3)q^{1/2}

\displaystyle>q-9\cdot 2^{2l}q^{1/2}+9\cdot 2^{2l}.

(In the first line of the above estimation,
2^{1+l}
is an upper bound for the number of points at infinity on the curve
F_{2,l}(X_{1},X_{2})=0
.) On the other hand, by Bezout’s theorem
[
8
, §5.3]
,

\bigl|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(F_{2,l})\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(\Delta(X_{1},X_{2},u_{1},\dots,u_{l}))\bigr|\leq(2^{2+l}-2^{l})^{2}=9\cdot 2^{2l}.

Therefore,

\bigl|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(F_{2,l})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}(\Delta(X_{1},X_{2},u_{1},\dots,u_{l}))\bigr|>q-9\cdot 2^{2l}q^{1/2}=q^{1/2}(q^{1/2}-9\cdot 2^{2l}).

From (
3.9
), we have

n\geq 4l+7>4l+2\log_{2}9\;(\approx 4l+6.34).

Thus

q^{1/2}=2^{n/2}>9\cdot 2^{2l},

which proves (
3.10
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
l
be the minimal prime divisor of

. By Theorem
2.2
, we may assume

, whence
n\geq 49
. In this case,

n-4l-6\geq n-4\sqrt{n}-6>0,

whence
n\geq 4l+7
.

For each
3\leq r\leq l+2
, write
r=2+l^{\prime}
, where
1\leq l^{\prime}\leq l
. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{l^{\prime}-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and exetnd
u_{1},\dots,u_{l^{\prime}-1}
to a basis

of an

-dimensional non zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
(by Lemma

). By Theorem

,
u_{1},\dots,u_{l^{\prime}}
can be extended to a basis of an
r
-dimensional zero-sum subspace
F
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Note that

. Thus by Theorem

,

\Bigl\{tl+r:0\leq t\leq\frac{n}{l}-4\Bigr\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{n}.

Since

\Bigl(\frac{n}{l}-4\Bigr)l+r=n-4l+r\geq n-4\sqrt{n}+3\geq\frac{1}{2}(n-1),

(here we used the fact that

By Theorem

∎

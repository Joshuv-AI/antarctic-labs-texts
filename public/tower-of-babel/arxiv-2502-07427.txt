1.
Introduction

A
word

w
in
k
variables is an element of the free group on
k
generators.
For every group
G
, we can interpret a word as a map
w\colon G^{k}\rightarrow G
defined by substitution of group elements for variables.
Verbal problems in groups study the relationship between the set of
w
-values
in
G
, defined as

w\{G\}=\{w(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\mid g_{i}\in G\},

and the
verbal subgroup
of
w
in
G
, defined as
w(G)=\langle w\{G\}\rangle
. Consistent with the terminology introduced by P. Hall, we say that
w
is
concise
in a class of groups
\mathfrak{C}
if, for all
G
in
\mathfrak{C}
, the verbal subgroup
w(G)
is finite whenever
w\{G\}
is finite.

In the context of profinite groups, this notion has been recently extended (
cf.

[
7
,
8
]
): we say that
w
is
strongly concise
in a class
\mathfrak{C}
of profinite groups if, for any
G
in
\mathfrak{C}
, the subgroup
w(G)
is finite whenever
|w\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
. The study of conciseness and strong conciseness has drawn significant attention in recent years (
cf.

[
3
,
5
,
9
,
12
,
16
,
20
]
).

Even though P. Hall conjectured that every word is concise in the class of all groups, this was proved false by Ivanov in
[
10
]
, by building an example of a word that is not concise in a certain non-residually finite group.
However, it remains unknown whether all words are concise – or even strongly concise – in the class consisting of all profinite groups.

Several classes of groups are known to have the property that every word is concise on them.
For instance, every word is concise in the class of linear groups (Merzjalkov
[
15
]
), or in the class of residually finite groups all of their quotients are residually finite (Turner-Smith
[
17
]
).
In particular, it follows from the latter that every word is concise in the classes of virtually abelian-by-nilpotent or virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups.

Among the aforementioned classes, strong conciseness
has been so far only proved for virtually nilpotent profinite groups, meaning that every word is strongly concise in this class of groups (
cf.

[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
).
In this article we establish the same property for profinite analogues of two previously mentioned classes: profinite linear groups (profinite groups with an embedding into matrix groups), and pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions of either linear or abelian-by-polycyclic groups. Here and henceforth,
\mathcal{C}
refers to a
formation
of finite groups – a class of finite groups closed under taking quotients and subdirect products of its members. This study is based on the relationship between conciseness, strong conciseness, and the notion of equationally Noetherian groups, a key concept in algebraic geometry over groups; we refer to Section
2
for the definition. We remark that the connection between equational Noetherianity and residual finiteness has already been studied in
[
18
]
.

The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and let
w
be a word such that
|w\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Suppose that
G
has an equationally Noetherian subgroup that is dense in
G
with respect to the profinite topology. Then
w\{G\}
is finite.

As a result, since linear groups are well-known to be equationally Noetherian (
cf.

[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
), we extend Merzjalkov’s Theorem as follows:

Corollary 1.2
.

Every word is strongly concise in the class of profinite linear groups.

Furthermore, Theorem
1.1
also applies to pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions of residually
\mathcal{C}
linear groups, which might not be linear themselves. For instance, it is still unknown whether non-abelian free pro-
p
groups are linear over any field (
cf.

[
14
, Conjecture 3.8]
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Every word is strongly concise in the class of groups consisting of pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions of residually
\mathcal{C}
linear groups.

Finitely generated abelian-by-polycyclic groups are residually finite (
cf.

[
13
, §7.2]
) and equationally Noetherian (
cf.

[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
). Thus Theorem
1.1
implies that every word is strongly concise in any profinite group containing a dense subgroup of this kind. In the following theorem, we extend this result to (non-necessarily finitely generated) virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups, although it remains unknown whether they are equationally Noetherian.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a profinite group, and suppose that
G
has a dense virtually abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup.
Then every word is strongly concise in
G
.

As a consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 1.5
.

Every word is strongly concise in the class of:

(i)

pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions of virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups,

(ii)

profinite virtually abelian-by-(finitely generated nilpotent) groups.

In the previous result,
finitely generated
is understood as topologically finitely generated. The following remains open:

Question 1.6
.

Is every word strongly concise in the class of virtually abelian-by-polyprocyclic groups?

The question falls outside the techniques of this paper, as a polyprocyclic group may not contain a dense polycyclic subgroup. For instance, for an odd prime
p
, the pro-
p
group

G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, topologically generated by
x
and
y
, and with the action given by
x^{y}=x^{2}
, does not contain any dense metacyclic subgroup. However, every word is strongly concise in the class of virtually polyprocyclic pro-
p
groups, which includes the group in the previous example (
cf.

[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
).

Finally, abelian-by-nilpotent groups may not be nor equationally Noetherian (
cf.

[
1
, Propositions 4.1 & 5]
) nor abelian-by-polycyclic, and, therefore, the following remains open as well:

Question 1.7
.

Is every word strongly concise in the class of profinite virtually abelian-by-nilpotent groups?

2.
Preliminaries

Given a group
G
and the free group on
k
variables
F_{k}=F(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
, every element
w
of the free-product
G*F_{k}
can be represented using the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
and, if necessary, additional constants
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}
from
G
. That is,

w=\hat{w}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

for a suitable group word
\hat{w}
in
l+k
variables.
By analogy with word maps,
w
defines in
G
a so-called
generalised word map

w\colon G^{k}\to G,\quad(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\mapsto w(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})=\hat{w}(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},g_{1},\dots,g_{k}).

The
verbal topology
on
G^{k}
is the topology whose sub-basis for the closed sets consists of the
solution sets

V_{G}(S)=\left\{(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\in G^{k}\mid w(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})=1\text{ for all }w\in S\right\},

where
S\subseteq G*F_{k}
. We say that
G
is
equationally Noetherian
if, for all
k
, any descending chain of verbally closed subsets in
G^{k}
stabilizes (
cf.

[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
for alternative characterizations).
A straightforward consequence, which will play a central role in the article, is that the connected component of the identity in
G
with respect to the verbal topology, also known as the
irreducible component
of
G
, is a normal irreducible subgroup of finite index (
cf.

[
19
, Lemma 5.2]
).

Before delving further, we should establish some terminology. Given a set
X
contained in two distinct groups
G^{k}
and
H^{k}
, one may consider the verbal topology on both
G^{k}
and
H^{k}
.
To avoid confusion, we will denote them respectively as the
G
-verbal and
H
-verbal topologies. Furthermore, in a profinite group
G
, these verbal topologies will be frequently used simultaneously with the original profinite topology of
G
.
Therefore, topological concepts will be clarified with a descriptor indicating the topology being used: profinite-continuous, verbally connected,
H
-verbally closed, etc.

Finally, given a subset
X\subseteq G
, its
profinite closure
in
G
will be denoted by
\pcl(X)
, while the
verbal closure in
G
will be denoted by
\vvcl(X)
, or
\Gvcl(X)
when we want to specify the group.

We will require the following lemma regarding the verbal closure of finite Cartesian products:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}\subseteq G
.
Then

\vvcl(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{k})=\vvcl(L_{1})\times\cdots\times\vvcl(L_{k}).

Proof.

By an inductive argument, it suffices to prove the result for
k=2
.
It is clear that
\vvcl(L_{1}\times L_{2})\subseteq\vvcl(L_{1})\times\vvcl(L_{2})
.
In order to prove the other inclusion, write

\vvcl(L_{1}\times L_{2})=\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigg(\bigcup_{j\in J_{i}}V_{G}\left(w_{ij}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\bigg)

for some set
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, finite sets
J_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and generalised words
w_{ij}(x_{1},x_{2})\in G*\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
.
For each
g\in L_{2}
, we have
w_{ij}(x_{1},g)\in G*\langle x_{1}\rangle

and that

\vvcl(L_{1})\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigg(\bigcup_{j\in J_{i}}V_{G}(w_{ij}(x_{1},g))\bigg).

Thus,
\vvcl(L_{1})\times\{g\}\subseteq\vvcl(L_{1}\times L_{2}),
and since
g
was arbitrary,

\vvcl(L_{1})\times L_{2}\subseteq\vvcl(L_{1}\times L_{2}).

Following the same line of reasoning with
g\in\vvcl(L_{1})
, we prove that
\{g\}\times\vvcl(L_{2})\subseteq\vvcl(L_{1}\times L_{2})
, and we therefore obtain the desired equality.
∎

Proof of the main result

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
and deduce Corollary
1.3
.
We start stating the following result, which is often key when working on strong conciseness.
Recall that a profinite space is a topological space that is Hausdorff, compact and totally disconnected.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
F\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous map between non-empty profinite spaces, and suppose that
|F(X)|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Then, there exists a non-empty profinite-open subset
U\subseteq X
such that
F|_{U}
is constant.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and
w
a word such that
|w\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Let

. If
N
is
H
-verbally irreducible, then
\pcl(N)
is marginal for
w
in
\pcl(H)
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the number of variables of the word
w
.
We fix a tuple
x
in
H^{k}
, and we define
w_{\textbf{x}}
to be the generalised word map

\displaystyle w_{\textbf{x}}\colon G^{k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G

g

\displaystyle\longmapsto w(\textbf{x}\cdot\textbf{g})w(\textbf{x})^{-1},

denotes the component-wise product of tuples. Observe that

is profinite-continuous and that
|w_{\textbf{x}}\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=\pcl(N)
be the profinite closure of

in

. Since

is normal in

, the subgroup
\mathcal{N}
is also normalised by

, and thus

. Hence, in view of Proposition

, there exists a tuple

(in particular

, that is,

Since

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a Hausdorff topological group and let
H\leq G
be a sequentially dense subgroup of

.
If
w\{H\}
is finite for a word

, then
w(G)=w(H)
.

Proof.

For each tuple
(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})

there exist sequences

converges to

Then, by the continuity of word maps, it follows that

.

4.
Virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups

In this last section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a virtually abelian-by-polycyclic group. Then there exists an abelian normal subgroup
A
of
G
such that
G/A
is virtually polycyclic.

Proof.

Let
H
be a finite-index normal abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup of
G
, and let
B\trianglelefteq H
be an abelian subgroup such that
H/B
is polycyclic.
Write
n=|G:H|
and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
be representative elements for each right coset of
H
in
G
. Being
B
normal in
H
, the subgroup
A=\cap_{i=1}^{n}B^{g_{i}}
is abelian and normal in
G
.
We will show that
G/A
is virtually polycyclic.

For that purpose define recursively
A_{0}=B
and
A_{i}=A_{i-1}\cap B^{g_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
. Note that each
A_{i}
is a normal subgroup of
H
and that
A_{n}=A
. It thus suffices to show that each
H/A_{i}
is polycyclic.
We already know it for
A_{0}=B
, so assume by induction that

is polycyclic for

. Thus, the subgroup
B^{g_{i}}A_{i-1}/A_{i-1}
of
H/A_{i-1}
is polycyclic too, and hence
B^{g_{i}}/A_{i}
is polycyclic.
Since the homomorphic image

of

is also polycyclic, then
H/A_{i}
is itself polycyclic.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
G
be a virtually abelian-by-polycyclic group, and let

).
Let

be the
G
-verbally connected component of the identity in

. Then
A_{0}
has finite index in
A

.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Z}[G/A]
is right Noetherian (

[

4.2

A

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
H
be a dense virtually abelian-by-polycyclic subgroup of

. Suppose that

Since every word is concise in the class of virtually abelian-by-polycyclic groups, by Lemma
3.3
, it is enough to prove that
w\{G\}
is finite.

Let
A
be an abelian normal subgroup of
H

H/A
is virtually polycyclic (

Lemma
4.1
).
By Corollary
4.3
, we may assume
A
is
H
-verbally connected, so applying Proposition
3.2
, we obtain that
\pcl(A)
is marginal for
w
in
G
.

Let

be a finite subset of

, and write

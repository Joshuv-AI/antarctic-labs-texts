A class of forward-backward diffusion equations for multiplicative noise removal
Yihui Tong, Wenjie Liu, Zhichang Guo, Wenjuan Yao

Abstract
. This paper investigates a class of degenerate forward-backward diffusion equations with a nonlinear source term, proposed as a model for removing multiplicative noise in images. Based on Rothe’s method, the relaxation theorem, and Schauder’s fixed-point theorem, we establish the existence of Young measure solutions for the corresponding initial boundary problem. The continuous dependence result relies on the independence property satisfied by the Young measure solution. Numerical experiments illustrate the denoising effectiveness of our model compared to other denoising models.

1.
Introduction

Multiplicative noise, often appearing in synthetic aperture radar, ultrasound, and laser images, significantly degrades image edges and details
[
1
,
39
]
. Over the past three decades, various methods based on partial differential equations (PDEs) have been developed for image denoising. Given an original image
u
, we assume it has been corrupted by multiplicative noise
\eta
. Our aim is to recover
u
from
f
that satisfies
f=u\cdot\eta
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be a bounded domain with appropriately smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
0<T<\infty
. We consider the following nonlinear degenerate diffusion equation of forward-backward type:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\operatorname{div}\left(\alpha(x)\left(\frac{1}{1+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}+\delta\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\right)\nabla u\right)-\lambda\frac{u-f}{u^{2}},

in
Q_{T}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla u,\vec{n}\rangle=0,

in
S_{T}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x),

in
\Omega
,

(1.3)

where
Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
,
S_{T}=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, with parameters
1<p\leq 2
,
\delta>0
, and
\lambda>0
. The positive coefficient
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
denotes the gray intensity of the noisy image
f
. The forward-backward diffusion nature of equation (
1.1
) makes it a suitable model for simultaneously enhancing image edges and reducing noise. However, this characteristic often precludes the existence of classical solutions, leading to the analysis of problems (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in the sense of Young measure solutions.

Since the work of
[
16
]
, numerous variational models
[
27
,
13
,
22
]
have been developed to address multiplicative noise. The standard approach to implementing these models involves deriving their corresponding evolution equations and discretizing them to evaluate their performance on noisy images. In general, a variational model yields the following evolution equation:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\text{div}\left(a(\left|\nabla u\right|,u)\nabla u\right)-\rho h(f,u),\quad\text{in }Q_{T},

where
a(\left|\nabla u\right|,u)
and
h(f,u)
are given by the corresponding variational model. However, in practice, the denoising problem can be resolved directly using the diffusion equation rather than the evolution equation derived from the variational model; that is, the problem reduces to designing the proper diffusion coefficient
a(\left|\nabla u\right|,u)
and the source term
h(f,u)
to guide the image denoising process
[
36
]
. Based on the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation of multiplicative Gamma noise, the source term
h(f,u)=\frac{u-f}{u^{2}}
was derived in
[
26
]
, providing an effective approach for multiplicative noise removal. For instance, see
[
30
,
40
]
. Additionally, the image gray-level indicator
\alpha
is designed to be much smaller at low gray levels than at high gray levels
[
28
]
, aligning with the characteristic that higher multiplicative noise intensity causes greater damage to the image. This design serves as an effective tool for simultaneously preserving details and removing noise. For further information, we refer to
[
32
,
38
]
. Inspired by the work
of
[
37
]
, various diffusion coefficients of the form
a(\left|\nabla u\right|,u)
have been proposed for image denoising applications
[
23
,
31
,
39
]
. To preserve the image restoration capability of the Perona-Malik (PM) equation
[
37
]
, Guidotti et al.
[
35
]
considered a class of forward-backward diffusion equations to remove additive noise

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\text{div}\left(q(\nabla u)\right)=:\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{1+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}}+\delta\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right),\quad\text{in }Q_{T},

(1.4)

with
q=\nabla\varphi
satisfying the structure conditions:

\max\{\gamma_{1}\left|\xi\right|^{p}-1,0\}\leq\varphi(\xi)\leq\gamma_{2}\left|\xi\right|^{p}+1,\quad\left|q(\xi)\right|\leq\gamma_{2}\left|\xi\right|^{p-1},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.5)

for some constants
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
and parameters
1<p\leq 2
,
\delta>0
. Equation (
1.4
) can be interpreted either as a spatial regularization of the PM equation or as a nonlinear scale-space model for image edge enhancement, see
[
33
,
36
,
41
]
. Even after regularization, the equation remains of forward-backward type, at least for
\delta<\frac{1}{8}
. Equation (
1.4
) effectively mitigates the staircasing effect resulting from the discretization of the PM equation, leading to a milder micro-structured ramping characterized by alternating gradients of finite size
[
34
]
. To the best of our knowledge, theoretical investigations into the forward-backward reaction-diffusion equation for addressing multiplicative noise in image processing remain scarce.

For problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), there are several papers concerning the existence and uniqueness of
degenerate forward-backward diffusion equations without a source term, namely
\lambda=0
. In this case, equation (
1.1
) can be generalized to the following form:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\operatorname{div}\left(\varPhi\left(\nabla u\right)\right),\quad\text{in }Q_{T},

(1.6)

where the heat flux
\varPhi=\nabla\varPsi
with some potential
\varPsi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\varPhi
and
\varPsi
satisfy
the same structure conditions (
1.5
) as
q
and
\varphi
, respectively.

When
\varPsi
is not convex, violating the monotonicity condition
(\varPhi(x)-\varPhi(y))\cdot(x-y)\geq 0
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equation (
1.6
) constitutes a degenerate, forward-backward parabolic equation that generally admits no classical strong or distributional solutions. Equations like (
1.6
) arise in modeling phenomena such as melting or freezing during superheating or supercooling, as well as in phase transitions where unstable and metastable states are permitted
[
5
,
43
]
. In the one-dimensional case, the study of equation (
1.6
) is primarily motivated by the Clausius-Duhem inequality. Based on the main constitutive assumption that
\varPhi
is piecewise affine, Höllig
[
2
]
established the existence of infinitely many weak solutions for (
1.6
) under the homogeneous Neumann boundary
condition. The assumption on
\varPhi
was relaxed by enhancing the regularity of the initial values in
[
4
]
. However, Lair
[
3
]
showed that there exists at most one smooth solution. For the
first or second initial boundary value problem of (
1.6
), Slemrod
[
8
]
studied the asymptotic behavior of measure-valued solutions.

Young measure representation was employed for forward-backward diffusion equations in
[
10
]
, where Kinderlehrer and Pedregal explored the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary problem (
1.6
) under the structure condition (
1.5
). When
p=2
, they defined a Young measure solution
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
with
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
as follows:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)=\text{div}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varPhi(\xi)d\nu_{x,t}(\xi),\quad\text{in}~H^{-1}(Q_{T}),

\nabla u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi d\nu_{x,t}(\xi),\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T},

and (
1.3
) holds in the sense of trace, where
(\nu_{x,t})_{(x,t)\in Q_{T}}
denotes a
H^{1}(Q_{T})
-gradient Young measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Valuable discussions on Young measures are available in
[
6
,
9
,
12
,
19
]
. Kinderlehrer et al.
[
10
]
established the existence of Young measure solutions by employing Rothe’s method and variational method, incorporating the relaxation theorem. Within this framework, Demoulini
[
17
]
further explored the qualitative properties of Young measure solutions obtained in
[
10
]
and found that the uniqueness result depends on the following independence property:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varPhi(\xi)\cdot\xi d\nu_{x,t}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varPhi(\xi)d\nu_{x,t}(\xi)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi d\nu_{x,t}(\xi),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{T},

namely that the heat flux
\varPhi
and the gradient
\nabla u
of the solution be independent with respect to the Young measure
\nu
. The results of Demoulini were subsequently extended from the Dirichlet case to the periodic and Neumann settings in
[
34
]
.
When
1\leq p<2
, Yin and Wang
[
20
]
studied the first initial-boundary value problem of (
1.6
). In the case
1<p<2
, they employed the approach similar to that in
[
17
]
to prove the existence of Young measure solutions. As for the case
p=1
, the equation (
1.6
) is singular, and Young measure solutions should be considered in
L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
. The uniqueness of the Young measure solution was also analyzed in
[
20
]
.

To our knowledge, only a few papers address forward-backward diffusion equations with a source term. For more details, interested readers may refer to
[
21
]
. In
this paper, we study the existence and continuous dependence of Young measure solutions for problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
). There are three main difficulties to overcome. The first difficulty arises from the presence of the source term, which prevents the direct application of the relaxation theorem to obtain approximate solutions for the Young measure solution of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
). As a result, the existence of Young measure solutions cannot be established using the method from
[
34
]
. To address this, we employ Schauder’s fixed-point theorem in the proof to establish existence. The second one stems from the nonconvexity of the potential
\varphi
, which implies the minimization of a nonconvex variational principle. To overcome this, we consider the relaxation of the nonconvex functional. The last one is the complexity introduced by the nonlinear dependence of the heat flow
q
on the gradient of the solution
\nabla u
. This is managed by replacing the nonlinearity
q(\nabla u)
with the expected value
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}q(\xi)d\nu(\xi)
, where
q
is evaluated against a Young measure
\nu
. Our main result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\inf\{f(x)\}>0
, then there exists a unique Young measure solution
u
to the problem
(
1.1
)
–
(
1.3
)
. Furthermore, the solution
u
is also continuously dependent on the initial data in the
L^{2}
norm.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we establish an auxiliary problem related to (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and recall some preliminaries on Young measure. Sect.
3
is devoted to showing the existence of Young measure solutions to the auxiliary problem. In Sect.
4
, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Sect.
5
, we show some numerical results
to demonstrate the ability of denoising by our model.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some useful definitions and results related to Young measure.
We denote by
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the closure of the set of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support. The dual of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be identified with the space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of signed Radon measures with finite mass via the pairing

\left\langle\mu,f\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\mu,\quad f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),~\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set with finite measure, denoted by
\text{meas}(D)
. A map
\nu:D\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called weakly
∗
measurable if

\left\langle\nu,f\right\rangle:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R},x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\nu_{x}

is measurable for each
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\nu_{x}=\nu(x)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

​
(Fundamental theorem on Young measure)
​
[
6

Let
z_{j}:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

(

)
be a sequence of measurable functions. Then there exists a
subsequence
\{z_{j_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a weakly
∗

\nu:D\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(i)

\nu(x)\geq 0

\left\|\nu(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\nu_{x}\leq 1

;

(ii)

\varphi(z_{j_{k}})
converges weakly

\left\langle\nu,\varphi\right\rangle

\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(iii)

Furthermore, one has
\left\|\nu(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1

\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\emph{meas}\left\{x\in D:\left|z_{j_{k}}(x)\right|\geq L\right\}=0.

(2.2)

(2.6)

Our purpose is to construct a Young measure solution to the problem (

Considering this, we first introduce the solution space

1.3
), defined as follows:

W(0,T)=\left\{w\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right),\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}\in L^{2}(Q_{T})\right\},

which is a Banach space equipped with the graph norm
[
14
]
. In the following, we assume that
\alpha(x)\geq\alpha_{1}>0
for
x\in\Omega
, and the initial value
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfies

0<l:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}f(x)~\text{and}~d:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}f(x)<\infty.

(2.7)

Take
w\in W(0,T)\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
such that

l\leq w(x,t)\leq d,\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

(2.8)

Let us denote the following auxiliary problem by (
\mathcal{P}_{w}
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\operatorname{div}\left(\alpha(x)q(\nabla u)\right)+\frac{f-u}{w^{2}},

(x,t)\in Q_{T}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla u,\vec{n}\rangle=0,

(x,t)\in S_{T}
,

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x)

x\in\Omega
.

Next, we define the Young measure solution to problem (
\mathcal{P}_{w}
).

Definition 2.11
.

We say
u_{w}\in W(0,T)\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
is the Young measure solution of problem (
\mathcal{P}_{w}
), if there exists a
W^{1,p}(Q_{T})
-gradient Young measure
\nu^{w}=(\nu^{w}_{x,t})_{(x,t)\in Q_{T}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\iint_{Q_{T}}\left(\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{w},q\rangle\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{\partial u_{w}}{\partial t}\eta+\frac{u_{w}-f}{w^{2}}\eta\right)dxdt=0,

(2.9)

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q_{T}})
, and

\nabla u_{w}(x,t)=\langle\nu^{w}_{x,t},\mathrm{id}\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T},

(2.10)

\langle\nu^{w}_{x,t},q\cdot\mathrm{id}\rangle=\langle\nu^{w}_{x,t},q\rangle\cdot\langle\nu^{w}_{x,t},\mathrm{id}\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T},

(2.11)

\operatorname{supp}\nu^{w}_{x,t}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(\xi)=\varphi^{**}(\xi)\right\},\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T},

(2.12)

and

u_{w}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

(2.13)

in the sense of trace.

3.
Existence of Young measure solutions to
(\mathcal{P}_{w})

In this section, we will study the existence of Young measure solutions to problem
(\mathcal{P}_{w})
, inspired by the methods of Demoulini
[
17
]
, Yin and Wang
[
20
]
, and Guidotti
[
34
]
.

Let
m
be a positive integer. For
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we define

E_{m}(v;u^{j-1}_{m})=\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi(\nabla v)+\frac{m}{2T}(v-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}+\frac{(v-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\right)dx,

where
u_{m}^{0}=f
,
u_{m}^{j-1}\in W^{1,p}_{l,d}(\Omega)
,
w_{m}^{j}(x)=w(x,jT/m),~\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega
,
1\leq j\leq m
. Since
\varphi
is not necessarily convex, and following relaxation theory,
\varphi
is replaced by its convexification
\varphi^{**}
. The energy functional
E_{m}
has the same infimum as

E_{m}^{**}(v;u^{j-1}_{m})=\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla v)+\frac{m}{2T}(v-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}+\frac{(v-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\right)dx.

The two functionals are both lower bounded owing to (
1.5
) and (
2.2
). It is readily seen that

E_{m}^{**}(T_{l}^{d}(v);f)\leq E_{m}^{**}(v;f),\quad v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

and that

E_{m}(T_{l}^{d}(v);f)\leq E_{m}(v;f),\quad v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

where the truncation operation
T_{l}^{d}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined by

T_{l}^{d}(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &d,\quad&z>d,\\
&z,\quad&l\leq z\leq d,\\
&l,\quad&z<l.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

It follows that minimizers and minimizing sequences can all be assumed to lie in
W^{1,p}_{l,d}(\Omega)
without loss of generality.
First, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
u_{m}^{j}\in W_{l,d}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
u_{m}^{j}
is a minimum of
E_{m}^{**}(v;u^{j-1}_{m})
and

\left\|u_{m}^{j}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq M_{1},

(3.1)

moreover,

\frac{m}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq M_{2},

(3.2)

where
M_{i}~(i=1,2)
are positive constants independent of
m
and
j
.

Proof.

By relaxation theorem
[
25
]
, we get that

\inf\left\{E_{m}(v;u_{m}^{j-1}):v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}=\inf\left\{E_{m}^{**}(v;u_{m}^{j-1}):v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}.

(3.3)

Let
\{u_{m}^{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,p}_{l,d}(\Omega)
be a minimizing sequence of
E_{m}(\cdot;u_{m}^{j-1})
satisfying

E_{m}(u_{m}^{j,k};u_{m}^{j-1})<\inf\left\{E_{m}(v;u_{m}^{j-1}):v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}+\frac{1}{mk},~k\geq 1.

(3.4)

Since
\varphi^{**}\leq\varphi
, (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) yield

E_{m}^{**}(u_{m}^{j,k};u_{m}^{j-1})<\inf\left\{E_{m}^{**}(v;u_{m}^{j-1}):v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}+\frac{1}{mk},~k\geq 1,

(3.5)

which, together with the Young’s inequality, implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})+\frac{m}{2T}(u_{m}^{j,k}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{mk}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})+\frac{(u_{m}^{j-1}-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}-\frac{(u_{m}^{j,k}-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})+\frac{u_{m}^{j-1}+u_{m}^{j,k}-2f}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}(u_{m}^{j-1}-u_{m}^{j,k})\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{T}{m}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j-1}+u_{m}^{j,k}-2f)^{2}}{4(w_{m}^{j})^{4}}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{m}{4T}\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}^{j,k}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx,\quad k\geq 1.

Using this formula, (
2.2
), (
2.8
) and the Poincaré inquality, one gets that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})+\frac{m}{4T}(u_{m}^{j,k}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{T}{m}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j-1}+u_{m}^{j,k}-2f)^{2}}{4(w_{m}^{j})^{4}}dx+\frac{1}{mk}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{T}{m}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}+(u_{m}^{j,k})^{2}+2f^{2}}{2l^{4}}dx+\frac{1}{mk}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{C_{1}}{m}\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx+\frac{C_{1}}{m}+\frac{1}{mk}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{C_{2}}{m}\int_{\Omega}d^{2-p}\left|u_{m}^{j-1}\right|^{p}dx+\frac{C_{2}}{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{C_{3}}{m}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{m}^{j-1}\right|^{p}dx+\frac{C_{3}}{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{C_{0}}{m},\quad 1\leq j\leq m,\ k\geq 1,

(3.6)

where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
and
C_{0}
depend only on
\Omega
,
T
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\gamma_{1}
,
p
,
l
and
d
.

Take
u_{m}^{0}=f
. Then (
3.6
) with
j=1
shows that
\{u_{m}^{1,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By the growth condition (
2.2
) and the Rellich theorem
[
24
]
, there exists a function
u_{m}^{1}\in W^{1,p}_{l,d}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
a convergent subsequence of
\{u_{m}^{1,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, denoted by itself, such that as
k\rightarrow\infty

u_{m}^{1,k}\rightarrow u_{m}^{1}~\text{strongly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),\quad\nabla u_{m}^{1,k}\rightharpoonup\nabla u_{m}^{1}~\text{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega).

By virtue of Theorem 2.4 in
[
45
]
, we get that
E_{m}^{**}(\cdot;f)
is sequentially weakly lower semi-continuous in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Hence
u_{m}^{1}
is a minimum of
E_{m}^{**}(\cdot;f)
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{1})dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{1,k})dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi(\nabla u_{m}^{1,k})dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{1})+\frac{m}{4T}(u_{m}^{1}-f)^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla f)dx+\frac{C_{0}}{m}.

Repeating the above process in turn, we obtain
u_{m}^{j}\in W^{1,p}_{l,d}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
j=1,2,\cdots,m
, which satisfies

u_{m}^{j,k}\rightarrow u_{m}^{j}~\text{strongly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),\quad\nabla u_{m}^{j,k}\rightharpoonup\nabla u_{m}^{j}~\text{weakly in}~L^{p}(\Omega),

(3.7)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and

E_{m}^{**}(u_{m}^{j};u_{m}^{j-1})=\inf\left\{E_{m}^{**}(v;u_{m}^{j-1}):v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}.

(3.8)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})dx,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})+\frac{m}{4T}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\frac{C_{0}}{m}.

(3.10)

Direct calculation shows that

\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)^{m}\leq e^{C_{0}},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)^{i}\leq\frac{m}{C_{0}}e^{C_{0}}.

Then, one gets from (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) that

\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})dx\leq e^{C_{0}}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla f)dx+e^{C_{0}},~k\geq 1,

(3.11)

\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})dx\leq e^{C_{0}}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla f)dx+e^{C_{0}},~1\leq j\leq m.

(3.12)

Summing up (
3.10
) from
1
to
m
leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})dx+\frac{m}{4T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{C_{0}}{m}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+C_{0}.

Combining the above inequality with (
3.12
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{m}{4T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j-1})dx+\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla f)dx+C_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C_{0}e^{C_{0}}+1)\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla f)dx+C_{0}e^{C_{0}}+C_{0}.

(3.13)

This completes the proof.
∎

Recalling Lemma 1.2 in
[
18
]
and (
3.8
), together with the
W^{1,p}
-weak sequential lower semi-continuity of
E_{m}^{**}(\cdot;u_{m}^{j-1})
, it can be deduced that

\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})\rightharpoonup\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})~\text{weakly in}~L^{1}(\Omega),~\text{as}~k\rightarrow\infty.

For
j=1,2,\cdots,m
, denote
\nu^{m,j}=(\nu^{m,j}_{x})_{x\in\Omega}
the Young measure generated by
\{\nabla u_{m}^{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. By Lemma
2.7
with (
2.2
),
\nu^{m,j}
is a
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-gradient Young measure and the sequence
\{\varphi(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is also weakly convergent in
L^{1}(\Omega)
; the representation formula (
2.1
) describes the weak
L^{1}
limits and by (
3.9
) we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})dx=\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m,j},\varphi^{**}\rangle dx=\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m,j},\varphi\rangle dx,

which together with
\varphi^{**}\leq\varphi
, implies

\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j}(x))=\langle\nu_{x}^{m,j},\varphi^{**}\rangle=\langle\nu_{x}^{m,j},\varphi\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega,

and therefore

\operatorname{supp}\nu_{x}^{m,j}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi^{**}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)\right\},\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega.

(3.14)

Noticing that
\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(\xi)=\varphi^{**}(\xi)\right\}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:q(\xi)=q^{**}(\xi)\right\}
, we see that

\langle\nu_{x}^{m,j},q\rangle=\langle\nu_{x}^{m,j},q^{**}\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega.

(3.15)

In addition, it follows from
\mathrm{id}\in\mathcal{E}_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and (
3.7
) that

\nabla u_{m}^{j}(x)=\langle\nu_{x}^{m,j},\mathrm{id}\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega.

(3.16)

Let
\chi_{m}^{j}
the characteristic function of
[(j-1)T/m,jT/m)
and denote

\lambda_{m}^{j}(t)=\left(\frac{m}{T}t-(j-1)\right)\chi_{m}^{j}(t),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Define

\displaystyle u_{m}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{m}^{j}(t)\left\{u_{m}^{j-1}(x)+\lambda_{m}^{j}(t)(u_{m}^{j}(x)-u_{m}^{j-1}(x))\right\},

\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q_{T},

\displaystyle v_{m}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{m}^{j}(t)u_{m}^{j}(x),

\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q_{T},

\displaystyle w_{m}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{m}^{j}(t)w_{m}^{j}(x),

\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q_{T},

\displaystyle v_{m}^{k}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{m}^{j}(t)u_{m}^{j,k}(x),~k=1,2,\cdots,

\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q_{T},

and

\nu^{m}=(\nu_{x,t}^{m})_{(x,t)\in Q_{T}},~\nu_{x,t}^{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{m}^{j}(t)\nu_{x}^{m,j},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ (x,t)\in Q_{T}.

From the definitions of
u_{m}^{j}
,
u_{m}^{j,k}
and
\nu^{m,j}
, we get that
u_{m}
,
v_{m}
,
v_{m}^{k}\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{l,d}^{1,p}(\Omega))
with
u_{m}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega
and the
W^{1,p}(Q_{T})
-gradient Young measure
\nu^{m}\in L^{1}(Q_{T};(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\prime})
is generated by
\{\nabla v_{m}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Moreover, (
3.14
) and (
3.16
) yield

\nabla v_{m}(x,t)=\langle\nu_{x,t}^{m},\mathrm{id}\rangle,\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T},

(3.17)

\operatorname{supp}\nu_{x,t}^{m}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi^{**}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)\right\},\quad\text{a.e.}~(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

(3.18)

To establish the existence of Young measure solutions to the problem (
\mathcal{P}_{w}
), we require uniform estimates for the approximate solutions
u_{m}
,
v_{m}
and the Young measures
\nu^{m}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The approximate solution
u_{m}
,
v_{m}
, and the Young measure
\nu^{m}
defined above satisfy

\iint_{Q_{T}}\left(\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m},q\rangle\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial t}\eta+\frac{v_{m}-f}{(w_{m})^{2}}\eta\right)dxdt=0,

(3.19)

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q_{T}})
. Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
M
that depends only on
\Omega
,
T
,
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
and
\gamma_{2}
, but not on
m
, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|v_{m}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq M,\quad l\leq v_{m}(x,t)\leq d,

(3.20)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u_{m}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq M,\quad l\leq u_{m}(x,t)\leq d,

(3.21)

\left\|\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq M,\quad\left\|u_{m}-v_{m}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq\frac{M}{m},

(3.22)

\left\|\nu^{m}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T};(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\prime})}\leq M,\quad\left\|\langle\nu^{m},q\rangle\right\|_{L^{p/(p-1)}(Q_{T})}\leq M.

(3.23)

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,
-1<\epsilon<1
. Then there
exists
C>0
such that

\varphi^{**}(\xi+\epsilon\nabla\eta)\leq C(1+\left|\xi\right|^{p}),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Again by applying Theorem 2.4 in
[
45
]
and the representation formula (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle E_{m}^{**}(u_{m}^{j};u^{j-1}_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\left\langle\nu^{m,j},\varphi^{**}\right\rangle+\frac{m}{2T}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}+\frac{(u_{m}^{j}-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\nabla\eta)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{2T}(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\varphi^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j,k}+\epsilon\nabla\eta)dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{2T}(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\left\langle\nu^{m,j},\varphi^{**}(\cdot+\epsilon\nabla\eta)\right\rangle dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{2T}(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-u_{m}^{j-1})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{m}^{j}+\epsilon\eta-f)^{2}}{2(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}dx,

which implies the equilibrium equation

\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m,j},q^{**}\rangle\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{m}{T}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})\eta+\frac{u_{m}^{j}-f}{(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\eta\right)dx=0,

(3.24)

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. Setting the Gâteaux derivative of
E_{m}^{**}(\cdot;u_{m}^{j-1})
to zero at the minimum
u_{m}^{j}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)q^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{m}{T}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})\eta+\frac{u_{m}^{j}-f}{(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\eta\right)dx=0,

(3.25)

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. Then, (
3.15
), (
3.24
) and (

) show that

\langle\nu^{m,j}_{x},q\rangle=\langle\nu^{m,j}_{x},q^{**}\rangle=q^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j}(x)),\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\Omega,

(3.26)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m,j},q\rangle\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{m}{T}(u_{m}^{j}-u_{m}^{j-1})\eta+\frac{u_{m}^{j}-f}{(w_{m}^{j})^{2}}\eta\right)dx=0,

(3.27)

for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. By Lemma
3.1
, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\langle\nu^{m,j},q\rangle\right\|_{L^{p/(p-1)}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|q^{**}(\nabla u_{m}^{j})\right\|_{L^{p/(p-1)}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{\mathrm{meas}(\Omega),1\}+2\gamma_{2}(M_{1}+1).

According to (
3.27
), we deduce

\iint_{Q_{T}}\left(\alpha(x)\langle\nu^{m},q\rangle\cdot\nabla\eta+\frac{\partial u_{m}}{\partial t}\eta+\frac{v_{m}-f}{w_{m}^{2}}\eta\right)dxdt=0,\quad\eta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q_{T}}).

From the direct calculation, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|\langle\nu^{m},q\rangle\right\|_{L^{p/(p-1)}(Q_{T})}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j\leq m}\left\|\langle\nu^{m,j},q\rangle\right\|_{L^{p/(p-1)}(\Omega)}T^{(p-1)/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\max\{\mathrm{meas}(\Omega),1\}(T+1)+2\gamma_{2}(M_{1}+1)(T+1).

Note that

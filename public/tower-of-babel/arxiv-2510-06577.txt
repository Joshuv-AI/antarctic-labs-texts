Introduction

Over the past few decades, the Yamabe problem for the
k
-curvature of the Schouten tensor—often called the
k
-Yamabe problem—has been extensively studied. Let
(N^{n},g)
be a compact connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n\geq 3
, and denote by
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
and
R_{g}
its Ricci tensor and scalar curvature, respectively. The prescribed
k
-curvature problem seeks to establish the existence of a conformal metric
\tilde{g}\in[g]
on
N
satisfying

\sigma_{k}^{1/k}(\lambda(A_{\tilde{g}}))=f\,,

(1.1)

where
1\leq k\leq n
is an integer,
f
is the prescribed smooth function on the manifold,
\lambda(A_{\tilde{g}})=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of

A_{\tilde{g}}=\frac{1}{n-2}\big(\mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}R_{\tilde{g}}\tilde{g}\big)

with respect to the metric
\tilde{g}
, and
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function and defined by

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}\,.

The particular case where
f
is a positive constant has been extensively studied. When
k=1
, equation (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known classical Yamabe problem concerning scalar curvature. Originally proposed by Yamabe in the 1960s, this problem saw a landmark resolution through the successive contributions of Yamabe
[
41
]
, Trudinger
[
37
]
, Aubin
[
1
]
, and Schoen
[
31
]
. For
k\geq 2
, the equation represents a generalization of the Yamabe problem involving fully nonlinear elliptic equations defined by the
k
-Hessian operators. This line of inquiry was initiated by Viaclovsky
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
and has since attracted considerable attention.

Under the assumption that
\lambda(A_{g})\in\Gamma_{k}
, equation (
1.1
) has been solved in a variety of settings. Specific cases include:
(k,n)=(2,4)
by Chang, Gursky and Yang
[
6
,
7
]
; locally conformally flat manifolds by Li and Li
[
28
]
(see also Guan and Wang
[
20
]
);
k>\frac{n}{2}
by Gursky and
Viaclovsky
[
23
]
and
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
by Li and Nguyen
[
30
]
(both of which hold for any positive function
f
); for
k=2
and
n>8
by Ge and Wang
[
24
]
. Furthermore, equation (
1.1
) was solved via a variational approach by Brendle and Viaclovsky
[
4
]
for the case
k=\frac{n}{2}
, and by Sheng–Trudinger–Wang
[
32
]
for
2\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
. This approach applies to the case
k=2
and is equivalent to
M
being locally conformally flat when
k\geq 3
as established in
[
3
]
.

Within the setting of a negatively curved Riemannian manifold
(N,g)
, Gursky and Viaclovsky
[
22
]
introduced the modified Schouten tensor for any parameter
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is given by

A^{t}_{g}=\frac{1}{n-2}\bigg(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{t}{2(n-1)}R_{g}g\bigg)\,.

Note that when
t=1
, the tensor
A^{1}_{g}
recovers the classical Schouten tensor. In the same work, Gursky and Viaclovsky
[
22
]
showed that for
t<1
and
\lambda(-A_{g}^{t})\in\Gamma_{k}
, there exists a unique conformal metric
\tilde{g}\in[g]
such that

\sigma_{k}^{1/k}(\lambda(-A_{\tilde{g}}))=f\,,

holds for any smooth positive function
f
on
N
. An alternative treatment of this problem using parabolic methods was subsequently provided by Li and Sheng
[
27
]
. More recently, Chen-Guo-He
[
8
]
and Chen-He
[
9
]
have extended this line of work to more general Krylov-type equations. It is worth emphasizing that the restriction to
t<1
is likely optimal: as noted in
[
39
]
, ellipticity may fail for
t>1
, and the crucial
C^{2}
estimates are unavailable for
t=1
.

In this paper, we investigate a different generalization of the Yamabe problem, based on the
p
-fold sum of the modified Schouten tensor. For a fixed integer
1\leq p\leq n
, we consider a
p
-fold sum operator
\mathcal{M}_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{M}_{p}(\lambda)=\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n}(\lambda_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}})\,.

(1.2)

This operator is symmetric in the eigenvalues. In contrast to the elementary symmetric functions
\sigma_{k}
,
expressing
\mathcal{M}_{p}
in terms of elementary symmetric polynomials is nontrivial; further details can be found in
[
10
]
.

In
[
25
]
, Harvey and Lawson introduced the following
p
-convex cone

\mathcal{P}_{p}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}>0,\,1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n\}\,,

which is clearly connected. A straightforward computation shows that

\mathcal{M}_{p}
is an elliptic operator on
\mathcal{P}_{p}
. Correspondingly, given a fixed metric
g
, we define the subset of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors by

P_{p}=\big\{V_{g}\in\mathrm{S}^{(0,2)}(TM):\lambda(V_{g})\in\mathcal{P}_{p}\big\}\,,

where
\lambda(V_{g})
denotes the vector of eigenvalues of
V_{g}
with respect to the metric
g
. When no confusion arises, we also write
\mathcal{M}_{p}(V_{g})=\mathcal{M}_{p}(\lambda(V_{g}))
for every
V_{g}\in P_{p}
.

This article is devoted to establishing an existence and uniqueness theorem for the prescribed
p
-curvature problem. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t<1
, and let
f
be a smooth positive function on
N
. Then there exists a unique conformal metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
satisfying
-A_{\tilde{g}}^{t}\in P_{p}
and

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{1/\binom{n}{p}}(-A^{t}_{\tilde{g}})=f\,.

(1.3)

To contextualize a key special case, we introduce the following terminology.

Definition 1.2
.

A compact Riemannian manifold
(N,g)
is said to have
p
-negative Ricci curvature
if
-\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\in P_{p}
.

As an immediate consequence for
t=0
and constant
f
, we obtain the following geometric result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
(N,g)
has
p
-negative Ricci curvature. Then there exists a unique metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
with
p
-negative Ricci curvature satisfying

\mathcal{M}_{p}(\mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde{g}})=constant.

This result generalizes the classical theorem of Gursky and Viaclovsky. More precisely, in the specific case
p=1
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}
coincides with the determinant, Theorem
1.3
then specializes to the result in
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
22
]

Suppose that
(N,g)
has negative Ricci curvature. Then there exists a unique metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
with negative Ricci curvature satisfying

\det(\mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde{g}})=constant.

We begin by reformulating the prescribed
p
-curvature problem (
1.3
) as a fully nonlinear elliptic partial differential equation. Under the conformal change
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
, the tensor
A_{\tilde{g}}^{t}
transforms as

A^{t}_{\tilde{g}}=A^{t}_{g}-\nabla^{2}u-\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)g+du\otimes du-\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g\,.

Therefore, equation (
1.3
) can be rewritten in terms of the background metric
g
as

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{1/\binom{n}{p}}\Big(\nabla^{2}u+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)g+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g-du\otimes du-A^{t}_{g}\Big)=fe^{2u}\,.

(1.4)

Consequently, solving the prescribed
p
-curvature problem (
1.3
) reduces to deriving a priori estimates for solutions
u
of (
1.4
).

Equation (
1.4
) is a variant of the
p
-Monge-Ampère equation

\mathcal{M}_{p}(\nabla^{2}u)=f(x,u)\,,

where
1\leq p\leq n-1
, which has been extensively explored in a vast body of literature. The case
p=1
, corresponding to the standard Monge-Ampère equation, was extended to Riemannian manifolds by Guan and Li
[
19
]
, generalizing the classical Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For further developments, see
[
2
,
18
,
26
,
36
]
and references therein. The case
p=n-1
is of particular importance in complex geometry, as it is intricately tied to the Gauduchon conjecture and the form-type Calabi-Yau equation; we refer to
[
15
,
16
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
for details. In contrast, the intermediate cases
2\leq p\leq n-2
present greater complexity, and the operator
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is less understood. For further progress in this direction, see
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
42
]
and the references therein.

The organization of this paper is as follows. We begin by analyzing the ellipticity of equation (
1.4
) in Section 2, a result that will be essential later. The core argument in this part relies on an intricate application of the maximum principle, first to obtain a
C^{0}
estimate for the solution, then a gradient bound, and finally the full
C^{2}
estimate by constructing appropriate auxiliary functions and controlling the relevant tensor terms. With these uniform estimates established, we proceed in Section 6 to prove the main existence theorem. This is achieved via the continuity method. The a priori estimates are essential for the closedness part, while the ellipticity result is used in the openness argument via the invertibility of the linearized operator.

2
Ellipticity

Let us define an endomorphism

\overline{\nabla}^{2}u:=\nabla^{2}u+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)g+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g-du\otimes du-A^{t}_{g}\,.

Then we can reformulate (
1.4
) as the following simpler form

M_{p}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big):=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{1/\binom{n}{p}}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)=fe^{2u}\,.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t\leq 1
, then for any solution
u
of (
2.1
), we have
\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\in P_{p}
.

Proof.

At a minimum point
x_{0}\in N
of
u
, we have
\nabla u(x_{0})=0
and
\nabla^{2}u(x_{0})\geq 0
. Using the hypothesis
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
and the expression for
\overline{\nabla}^{2}u
, we obtain

\overline{\nabla}^{2}u(x_{0})=\nabla^{2}u(x_{0})+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u(x_{0}))g-A_{g}^{t}(x_{0})\geq-A_{g}^{t}(x_{0})\,.

This implies
\overline{\nabla}^{2}u(x_{0})\in P_{p}
as we have assumed
-A_{g}^{t}(x_{0})\in P_{p}
. By the connectedness of
P_{p}
, the result
\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\in P_{p}
follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t\leq 1
, then the equation (
2.1
) is elliptic.

Proof.

Given a smooth function
v
, define a one-parameter family
u_{s}=u+\epsilon v
. Let

F[u_{s},\nabla u_{s},\nabla^{2}u_{s}]=M_{p}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u_{s}\big)-fe^{2u_{s}}\,.

For any matrix
U=\{U_{ij}\}\in P_{p}
, the linearized operator is represented by the positive matrix

\big\{M_{p}^{ij}(U)\big\}=\bigg\{\frac{\partial M_{p}}{\partial U_{ij}}\bigg\}\,.

A direct computation yields

\frac{d}{ds}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u_{s}\big)\mid_{s=0}=\nabla^{2}v+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta v)g+W(dv)\,,

with
W(dv)=(2-t)\langle du,dv\rangle g-2du\otimes dv
being linear in
dv
. It follows that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}M_{p}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u_{s}\big)\mid_{s=0}&=M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)\Big(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta v)g_{ij}+W_{ij}(dv)\Big)\\
&=\Big(M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)+\frac{1-t}{n-2}M_{p}^{kl}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)g_{kl}g^{ij}\Big)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v+M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)W_{ij}(dv)\,.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.2
, we know that the matrix

\big\{\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)\big\}=\Big\{M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)+\frac{1-t}{n-2}M_{p}^{kl}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)g_{kl}g^{ij}\Big\}

(2.2)

is positive definite. Then we conclude that the operator

\begin{split}L(v):&=\frac{d}{ds}F[u_{s},\nabla u_{s},\nabla^{2}u_{s}]\mid_{s=0}=\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v+M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\big)W_{ij}(dv)-fe^{2u}v\end{split}

is indeed elliptic.
∎

3
Zeroth order estimate

In this section, we prove the following
C^{0}
estimate:

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t\leq 1
. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}
depending only on
f
,
A_{g}^{t}
,
n
, and
p
, such that for any solution
u
of (
1.4
), we have

\sup_{N}|u|\leq C_{0}\,.

Proof.

We will prove this result using the standard maximum principle. Since
N
is compact, assume that
u
attain its minimum at a point
x_{min}\in N
. At this point, we have
\nabla u(x_{min})=0
and
\nabla^{2}u(x_{min})
is positive semi-definite. Consequently

\nabla^{2}u+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)g\geq 0

at
x_{min}
, which implies

M_{p}(-A^{t}_{g}(x_{min}))\leq f(x_{min})e^{2u(x_{min})}\,.

It follows that

u(x_{min})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\bigg(\frac{M_{p}(-A^{t}_{g}(x_{min}))}{f(x_{min})}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{N}\log\bigg(\frac{M_{p}(-A^{t}_{g})}{f}\bigg)\,.

Similarly, if
u
attain its maximum at a point
x_{max}\in N
, then

u(x_{max})\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\bigg(\frac{M_{p}(-A^{t}_{g}(x_{max}))}{f(x_{max})}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{2}\max_{N}\log\bigg(\frac{M_{p}(-A^{t}_{g})}{f}\bigg)\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
First order estimate

In this section, we shall concentrate on the following global gradient estimate on (
1.4
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t\leq 1
. Let
u\in C^{3}(N)
be a solution to (
1.4
). Then we have

\sup_{N}|\nabla u|\leq C_{1}\,,

where
C_{1}
is a constant depends only on
f
,
A_{g}^{t}
,
n
,
p
and
C_{0}=\|u\|_{C^{0}}
.

Proof.

To proceed, we introduce an auxiliary function following a strategy of Gursky and Viaclovsky
[
22
]
, which itself builds upon earlier work by Li

[
29
]
. Define a
C^{2}
smooth auxiliary function

G=\log\beta+\phi(u)\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{ with }\beta=1+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

on
N
, where
\phi
is a monotone increasing function given by

\phi(s)=-\log(e^{2+2C_{0}}-e^{2s})\,,\hskip 10.00002pts\in[-C_{0},C_{0}].

We may assume that the maximum of
G
over
N
is attained at some
x_{0}\in N
. Near this maximum point, we work in a smooth orthonormal local frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
in the vicinity of the point
x_{0}
such that the following statements hold:

•

the Christoffel symbols vanishes at
x_{0}
, i.e.
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=0
;

•

the metric matrix
\big\{g_{ij}\big\}
is diagonal at
x_{0}
;

•

at
x_{0}
, the identity

\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\bigg(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}(g^{kl})+2\nabla_{l}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})\bigg)u_{k}u_{l}=2\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}R_{iljk}u_{k}u_{l}

(4.1)

holds, where
R_{iljk}
denotes the Riemann curvature tensor components of the metric
g
. We refer to
[
40
]
for a detailed proof.

For notational convenience, we denote

\widetilde{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{p}^{kk}\,.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}=M_{p}^{ij}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}\delta_{ij}.

(4.2)

Furthermore, we set

L=\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}e_{i}e_{j}=M^{ij}_{p}e_{i}e_{j}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}\Delta.

The condition that
G
is maximized at
x_{0}
yields

0=\nabla_{i}G=\frac{1}{\beta}u_{k}u_{ki}+\phi^{\prime}u_{i}\,,\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\,,

(4.3)

hence the Hessian matrix satisfies

\begin{split}\nabla_{ij}G=&\frac{1}{\beta}\big(u_{k}u_{kij}+u_{ki}u_{kj}+\frac{1}{2}\,(g^{kl})_{ij}u_{k}u_{l}\big)-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}(u_{k}u_{ki})(u_{l}u_{lj})+\phi^{\prime\prime}u_{i}u_{j}+\phi^{\prime}u_{ij}\\
=&\frac{1}{\beta}\big(u_{k}u_{kij}+u_{ki}u_{kj}+\frac{1}{2}\,(g^{kl})_{ij}u_{k}u_{l}\big)+(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2})u_{i}u_{j}+\phi^{\prime}u_{ij}\,,\end{split}

which is negative semi-definite. This yields the inequality

\begin{split}0\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{l}u_{lij}+\frac{1}{2\beta}L(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}+(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2})\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+\phi^{\prime}L(u).\end{split}

(4.4)

We first estimate the last term in (
4.4
). In the local coordinates above, we have

(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)_{ij}=u_{ij}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)\delta_{ij}+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\delta_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j}-A_{ij}\,,

where
A^{t}_{ij}=(A^{t}_{g})_{ij}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}L(u)=&M^{ij}_{p}u_{ij}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}\Delta u\\
=&M^{ij}_{p}\big((\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)_{ij}-\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)\delta_{ij}-\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\delta_{ij}+u_{i}u_{j}+A^{t}_{ij}\big)+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}\Delta u\\
=&fe^{2u}-\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{i}u_{j}+A^{t}_{ij}\big).\end{split}

(4.5)

Next, we estimate the first term in (
4.4
). Differentiating the equation (
2.1
) and using
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=0
at
x_{0}
, we deduce from (
4.3
) that

\begin{split}(f_{l}+2fu_{l})e^{2u}&=M_{p}^{ij}\big[(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)_{ij}\big]_{l}\\
&=M^{ij}_{p}\big[u_{ij}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)g_{ij}+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j}-A^{t}_{ij}\big]_{l}\\
&=M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{ijl}-u_{k}(\Gamma_{ij}^{m})_{l}\big)-M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{il}u_{j}+u_{i}u_{jl}+\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}\big)\\
&\quad+\Big(\frac{1-t}{n-2}(u_{kkl}-u_{i}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l})-(2-t)\beta\phi^{\prime}u_{l}\Big)\widetilde{M}\,.\end{split}

Note that (
4.2
) implies

\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{ijl}=M_{p}^{ij}u_{ijl}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}u_{kkl}.

It follows that

\begin{split}(f_{l}+2fu_{l})e^{2u}=&\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{ijl}-\Big((2-t)\beta\phi^{\prime}u_{l}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\Big)\widetilde{M}\\
&\qquad-M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{il}u_{j}+u_{i}u_{jl}+\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+u_{k}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{l}\big)\,.\end{split}

Multiplying by
u_{l}
and using (
4.3
) again, we obtain

\begin{split}\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{l}u_{lij}=(\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle&+2|\nabla u|^{2})e^{2u}+(2-t)\beta\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}u_{l}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\widetilde{M}\\
&+M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{il}u_{j}u_{l}+u_{i}u_{l}u_{jl}+u_{l}\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+u_{k}u_{l}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{l}\big)\\
=(\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle&+2|\nabla u|^{2})e^{2u}+(2-t)\beta\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}u_{l}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\widetilde{M}\\
&+M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{l}\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+u_{k}u_{l}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{l}\big)-2\beta\phi^{\prime}M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}\,.\end{split}

(4.6)

Substituting (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) into (
4.4
), we infer that

\begin{split}0\geq&\frac{1}{\beta}(\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2|\nabla u|^{2})e^{2u}+\phi^{\prime}fe^{2u}+\frac{2-t}{2}\phi^{\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}u_{l}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\widetilde{M}\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta}M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{l}\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+u_{k}u_{l}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{l}\big)+(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime})M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+\frac{1}{2\beta}M^{ij}_{p}e_{i}e_{j}(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\\
&+\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}\Delta(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\widetilde{M}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2})|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+\phi^{\prime}M^{ij}_{p}A^{t}_{ij}.\end{split}

(4.7)

By (
4.1
), we obtain

\frac{1}{\beta}M^{ij}_{p}u_{k}u_{l}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{l}+\frac{1}{2\beta}M^{ij}_{p}e_{i}e_{j}(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}=\frac{1}{\beta}M^{ij}_{p}R_{iljk}u_{k}u_{l}

and

\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}u_{l}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\widetilde{M}+\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}\Delta(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\widetilde{M}=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}R_{mlmk}u_{k}u_{l}\widetilde{M}\,.

Plugging the these equalities into (
4.7
), we deduce that

\begin{split}0\geq&\frac{1}{\beta}(\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2|\nabla u|^{2}+\beta\phi^{\prime}f)e^{2u}+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}R_{mlmk}u_{k}u_{l}\widetilde{M}\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta}M^{ij}_{p}u_{l}\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime})M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+\frac{1}{\beta}M^{ij}_{p}R_{iljk}u_{k}u_{l}\\
&+\Big(\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2})+\frac{2-t}{2}\phi^{\prime}\Big)|\nabla u|^{2}\widetilde{M}+\phi^{\prime}M^{ij}_{p}A^{t}_{ij}.\end{split}

(4.8)

From the definition of
\phi
, one may verify that

\begin{split}\phi^{\prime}(s)&=\frac{2e^{2s}}{e^{2+2C_{0}}-e^{2s}}=\frac{2}{e^{2+2C_{0}-2s}-1}\,,\\
\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)&=\frac{4e^{2+2C_{0}+2s}}{(e^{2+2C_{0}}-e^{2s})^{2}}\,,\end{split}

which implies

\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi^{\prime 2}-\phi^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\phi^{\prime}>\epsilon_{0}:=\frac{2}{e^{2+4C_{0}}-1}\,.

It follows from (
4.8
) that

\begin{split}0\geq&\epsilon_{0}M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+M_{p}^{ij}U_{ij}+\frac{1}{\beta}\big(\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle+2|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon_{0}\beta f\big)e^{2u}\\
\geq&\epsilon_{0}M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+M_{p}^{ij}U_{ij}-C^{\prime}\end{split}

(4.9)

for some uniform constant
C^{\prime}>0
, where
U=\{U_{ij}\}
is a symmetric matrix defined by

\begin{split}U_{ij}=&\Big(\frac{2-2t}{n-2}+\frac{2-t}{2}\Big)\epsilon_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-t}{n-2}R_{mlmk}u_{k}u_{l}\delta_{ij}\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta}u_{l}\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+\frac{1}{\beta}R_{iljk}u_{k}u_{l}+\epsilon_{0}A^{t}_{ij}.\end{split}

Let
\lambda_{1}(U)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(U)
be the eigenvalues of
U
. We divide the proof into two cases:

(i)

If
\lambda_{n}(U)\geq 1
, then (
4.9
) implies

C^{\prime}\geq\epsilon_{0}M^{ij}_{p}u_{i}u_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{p}^{ii}\,,

(4.10)

so that

M_{p}^{ii}\leq C^{\prime}\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for all }i=1,\cdots,n\,.

Making use of the Appendix Lemma
A.1
below, we have

M_{p}^{ii}\geq C(n,p)C^{\prime 1-n}\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for all }i=1,\cdots,n\,.

Substituting this into (
4.10
) yields the desired bound for
|\nabla u|(x_{0})
, thus completing the proof.

(ii)

Otherwise, we have
\lambda_{n}(U)\leq 1
. There exists an unit eigenvector
e_{n}\in T_{x_{0}}N
of
U
such that

1\geq\lambda_{n}(U)=\langle Ue_{n},e_{n}\rangle\geq\Big(\frac{2-2t}{n-2}+\frac{2-t}{2}\Big)\epsilon_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{C^{\prime}}{\beta}|\nabla u|-C^{\prime\prime}\,,

where
C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime\prime}
are two uniform positive constants. This again yields the gradient estimate.

Therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

5
Second order estimate

In this section, we will prove the following global second order estimate:

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
for some
t<1
. Let
u\in C^{4}(N)
be a solution to (
1.4
). Then we have

\sup_{N}|\nabla^{2}u|\leq C_{2}\,,

where
C_{2}
is a constant depends on
p,

C_{0}
,
C_{1}
, and
f
.

Proof.

For each
x\in N
, let
S(T_{x}N)
denote the unit tangent bundle of
N
at
x
. For a large constant
B
to be specified later, we define the quantity

G_{0}=\max_{x\in N}\max_{\xi\in S(T_{x}N)}\bigg\{\nabla^{2}u(\xi,\xi)+\frac{B}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\bigg\}\,.

Our goal is to estimate
G_{0}
. Suppose
G_{0}
is attained at a point
x_{0}\in N
and a unit vector field
\xi_{0}\in S(T_{x_{0}}N)
such that

G_{0}=\nabla^{2}u(\xi_{0},\xi_{0})+\frac{B}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(x_{0})\,.

In a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, we may choose a smooth orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
satisfying:

(i)

e_{1}(x_{0})=\xi_{0}
;

(ii)

\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}=0
at
x_{0}
, i.e.
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(x_{0})=0
for all
i,j
and
k
;

(iii)

the symmetric matrix
\{\nabla^{2}u(e_{i},e_{j})\}
is diagonal at
x_{0}
.

Now consider the auxiliary function

G=\nabla^{2}u(e_{1},e_{1})+\frac{B}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=u_{11}-\Gamma_{11}^{l}u_{l}+\frac{B}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,.

By the definition of
G_{0}
, the function
G
attains its maximum
G_{0}
at
x_{0}
.
The maximum principle at
x_{0}
then implies that

\begin{split}0=\nabla_{i}G&=u_{11i}-\Gamma_{11}^{l}u_{li}-(e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{l})u_{l}+\frac{B}{2}(e_{i}g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}+Bg^{kl}(e_{i}u_{k})u_{l}\\
&=u_{11i}-(e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{l})u_{l}+Bu_{ik}u_{k}\,,\end{split}

(5.1)

and

\begin{split}0\geq\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}u_{11ij}&-2\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}(e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{j})u_{jj}-\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}(e_{j}e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{l})u_{l}+\frac{B}{2}\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}e_{j}e_{i}(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\\
&+B\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}u_{ijl}u_{l}+B\overline{M}_{p}^{ii}u_{ii}^{2}\,,\end{split}

(5.2)

where
{\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}}
is defined as in (
4.2
).

We first proceed to compute the last two terms in (
5.2
). Differentiating the equation (
1.4
) in the direction
e_{l}
and using the fact that
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=0
at
x_{0}
, we deduce from (
4.3
) that

\begin{split}(f_{l}&+2fu_{l})e^{2u}=M_{ij}^{p}\big[(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)_{ij}\big]_{l}\\
&=M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{ijl}-u_{k}(\Gamma_{ij}^{m})_{l}\big)-M^{ij}_{p}\big(u_{il}u_{j}+u_{i}u_{jl}+\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}\big)\\
&\quad+\Big(\frac{1-t}{n-2}(u_{kkl}-u_{i}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l})+(2-t)u_{k}u_{kl}\Big)\widetilde{M}\\
&=\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{ijl}-M^{ij}_{p}\big(2u_{il}u_{j}+\nabla_{l}A^{t}_{ij}+u_{k}(\Gamma_{ij}^{m})_{l}\big)+\big((2-t)u_{k}u_{kl}-\frac{1-t}{n-2}u_{i}(\Gamma_{kk}^{i})_{l}\big)\widetilde{M}\,.\end{split}

(5.3)

Let
\overline{u_{ij}}=(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u)_{ij}
for simplicity. Then

\overline{u_{ij}}=u_{ij}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u)\delta_{ij}+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\delta_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j}-A^{t}_{ij}\,.

Since
\overline{\nabla}^{2}u\in P_{p}
and by gradient estimates, we have

0<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{u}_{ii}\leq\big(1+\frac{n(1-t)}{n-2}\big)\big(\Delta u-nC^{\prime}\big)

for some uniform positive constant
C^{\prime}
. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{ii}-C^{\prime})>0\,.

Note that
u_{11}
is the largest eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}u
, so for all
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,

|u_{ii}|=|u_{ii}-C^{\prime}+C^{\prime}|\leq(n-1)(u_{11}-C^{\prime})+C^{\prime}=(n-1)u_{11}+nC^{\prime}\,.

It follows from (
5.3
) and the gradient estimates that

B\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}u_{ijl}u_{l}\geq-BC^{\prime}(1+\widetilde{M}+u_{11}\widetilde{M})\,.

(5.4)

For the last term in (
5.2
), we obtain

\overline{M}_{p}^{ii}u_{ii}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(M_{p}^{ii}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}\Big)u_{ii}^{2}\geq\frac{1-t}{n-2}\widetilde{M}u_{11}^{2}

(5.5)

For the second to forth terms in (
5.2
), we have

-2\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}(e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{j})u_{jj}-\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}(e_{j}e_{i}\Gamma_{11}^{l})u_{l}+\frac{B}{2}\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}e_{j}e_{i}(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\geq-C^{\prime}(1+u_{11}\widetilde{M}+B\widetilde{M})\,.

(5.6)

Substituting (
5.3
), (
5.5
), and (
5.6
) into (
5.2
), we deduce that

\begin{split}BC^{\prime}(1+\widetilde{M}+u_{11}\widetilde{M})\geq&\quad\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}u_{11ij}+\frac{B(1-t)}{n-2}\widetilde{M}u_{11}^{2}\,.\end{split}

(5.7)

Differentiating the equation (
1.4
) in the
e_{1}
-direction twice, we obtain

M^{ij,kl}e_{1}(\overline{u_{ij}})e_{1}(\overline{u_{kl}})+M_{p}^{ij}e_{1}e_{1}(\overline{u_{ij}})=(f_{11}+4f_{1}u_{1}+4fu_{1}^{2}+2fu_{11})e^{2u}\,.

(5.8)

Since
M_{p}
is concave on
P_{p}
by Corollary 1.15 of
[
10
]
, the first term on the left-hand side must be non-positive. Therefore,

M_{p}^{ij}e_{1}e_{1}(\overline{u_{ij}})\geq-C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\,.

(5.9)

A direct computation at
x_{0}
gives

\begin{split}e_{1}e_{1}(\overline{u_{ij}})=&-e_{1}e_{1}(A^{t}_{ij})+u_{ij11}+2u_{k1}e_{1}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})+u_{k}e_{1}e_{1}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})\\
&+\frac{1-t}{n-2}\big(u_{kk11}g_{ij}+u_{kk}e_{1}e_{1}(g_{ij})-2u_{l1}e_{1}(\Gamma_{kk}^{l})+u_{r}e_{1}e_{1}(\Gamma_{kk}^{l})\big)\\
&-(u_{i11}u_{j}+u_{i}u_{j11}+2u_{i1}u_{j1})+\frac{2-t}{2}e_{1}e_{1}(g^{kl})u_{k}u_{l}\delta_{ij}\\
&+(2-t)\big(u_{k11}u_{k}+u_{11}^{2}\delta_{ij}+|\nabla u|^{2}e_{1}e_{1}(g_{ij})\big)\,.\end{split}

Substituting this into (
5.9
) and using (
5.1
) to replace terms involving
u_{11i}
, we obtain

-C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\leq\overline{M}_{p}^{ij}u_{11ij}+C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\widetilde{M}+(2-t)\widetilde{M}u_{11}^{2}\,.

(5.10)

Combining (
5.7
) and (
5.10
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{B(1-t)}{n-2}-(2-t)\Big)\widetilde{M}u_{11}^{2}\leq&BC^{\prime}(1+\widetilde{M}+u_{11}\widetilde{M})+C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})+C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\widetilde{M}\\
=&(1+B)C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\widetilde{M}+C^{\prime}(1+u_{11}+B)\,.\end{split}

Since we have assumed
t<1
, we may now choose
B
to be sufficiently large such that

\frac{B(1-t)}{n-2}-(2-t)=2.

Then it follows that

\big(2u_{11}^{2}-(1+B)C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\big)\widetilde{M}\leq C^{\prime}(1+u_{11}+B)\,.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
u_{11}
is sufficiently large so that

u_{11}^{2}-(1+B)C^{\prime}(1+u_{11})\geq 0\,,

since otherwise the desired bound already holds. Therefore,

C^{\prime}(1+u_{11}+B)\geq\widetilde{M}u_{11}^{2}\geq pu_{11}^{2},

where we have used the fact that
\widetilde{M}\geq p
(see Lemma 1.17 in
[
10
]
for a proof). This yields the desired upper bound for
u_{11}(x_{0})
, and hence an upper bound for
G_{0}
. The proof is then complete.
∎

6
Proof of Theorem
1.1

With the
C^{2}
estimates from Proposition
5.1
established, the proof of Theorem
1.1
follows by a standard continuity argument, as implemented in
[
22
]
.

Fix a parameter
s\in[0,1]
and consider the family of equations

M_{p}\Big(\nabla^{2}u_{s}+\frac{1-t}{n-2}(\Delta u_{s})g+\frac{2-t}{2}|\nabla u_{s}|^{2}g-du_{s}\otimes du_{s}-\tilde{A}^{t}_{g}\Big)=f_{s}e^{2u_{s}}\,.

(6.1)

Here,
f_{s}=sf+(1-s)
and
\tilde{A}^{t}_{g}=sA^{t}_{g}-(1-s)C_{n,p}g
, where
C_{n,p}
is a positive constant chosen so that

C_{n,p}M_{p}(I_{n})=1\,.

Define the set

S=\{s\in[0,1]\,:\,\eqref{family}\textrm{ admits a solution }u_{s}\in C^{2,\alpha}(N)\}\,.

To apply the continuity method, we verify that
S
is non-empty open and closed.

Note that when
s=0
, the constant function
u_{0}\equiv 0
is a solution to (
6.1
), so
0\in S
and
S\neq\emptyset
. By Lemma
2.2
, the linearization
L:C^{2,\alpha}(N)\to C^{\alpha}(N)
with

\begin{split}L(v)=\overline{M}^{ij}_{p}(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u_{s})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v+M_{p}^{ij}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{2}u_{s}\big)W_{ij}(dv)-fe^{2u_{s}}v\end{split}

is an elliptic operator. The ellipticity of
L
ensures its invertibility, which implies that
S
is open.

We now establish that
S
is closed by invoking the a priori estimates from Proposition
5.1
. Let
\{s_{i}\}\subset S
be a sequence converging to some
s_{\infty}\in[0,1]
.
Under the hypothesis that
-A_{g}^{t}\in P_{p}
, we also have
-\tilde{A}^{t}_{g}\in P_{p}
. Therefore, the
C^{2}
estimates of
u_{s}
–applicable upon replacing
f
by
f_{s}
–persist in this setting. To upgrade these to
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates, we can now invoke the Evans-Krylov theorem
[
14
,
21
]
, yielding

\|u_{s_{i}}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(N)}\leq C\,,

where
C
is a constant independent of
i
. Differentiating the equation (
6.1
) now gives a uniformly elliptic PDE with uniformly Hölder coefficients. Applying the Schauder estimates then gives

\|u_{s_{i}}\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(N)}\leq C.

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, a subsequence of
\{u_{s_{i}}\}
converges in
C^{4,\alpha}(N)
to a limit function
u_{\infty}
, which solves the equation (
6.1
) at
s_{\infty}
. Hence,
s_{\infty}\in S
, and
S
is closed. Since
S
is nonempty, open and closed, it follows that
S=[0,1]
.

This establishes the existence of a
C^{4,\alpha}
solution to equation (
1.4
). By repeatedly differentiating the equation and applying Schauder theory, we conclude that the solution is in fact smooth. Uniqueness follows from the maximum principle. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Appendix A
Determinantal majorization inequality

The following inequality is undoubtedly known, one can find the proof in
[
42
]
. We shall provide a proof here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a positive number
C(n,p)
dependent only on
n
and
p
such that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}M_{p}^{ii}(\lambda)\geq C(n,p)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{ for all }\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{p}\,.

(A.1)

Proof.

For every
i=1,\cdots,n
we introduce a set
\Lambda_{i}
as follows:

\Lambda_{i}:=\big\{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}):1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n,\,i\in\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\}\big\}\,.

Clearly, for each
i
, the set
\Lambda_{i}
contains
\binom{n-1}{p-1}
distinct elements. Additionally, observe that
n\binom{n-1}{p-1}=p\binom{n}{p}
. Hence, for an arbitrary
p
-tuple element
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{p})
, it exclusively belongs to those sets
\Lambda_{j_{1}},\cdots,\Lambda_{j_{p}}
and to no others. To simplify the notation, for every
i=1,\cdots,n
, we define
\Lambda_{i}
to be spanned by

\Lambda_{i}=\bigg\{\Lambda^{1}_{i}\,,\cdots,\Lambda^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}_{i}\bigg\}

with

\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}l\textrm{ is one of }\\
\textrm{ components in }\Lambda^{k}_{i}\end{subarray}}{\sum}\lambda_{l}>0,\hskip 20.00003ptk=1,\cdots,\binom{n-1}{p-1}\,.

It follows that

\begin{split}M_{p}^{ii}(\lambda)&=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{p}}M_{p}(\lambda)\underset{i\in\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\}}{\sum}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{p}}M_{p}(\lambda)\underset{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{p})\in\Lambda_{i}}{\sum}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{p}}M_{p}(\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}}\,.\end{split}

In what follows,
C(n,p)
will denote a constant depending only on
n
and
p
, whose value may change from line to line. We therefore obtain

\prod_{i=1}^{n}M_{p}^{ii}(\lambda)\geq C(n,p)\big(M_{p}(\lambda)\big)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}}\,.

Hence, we only need to demonstrate the following inequality:

\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}}\bigg)^{1/n}\geq C(n,p)\big(M_{p}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}\,.

(A.2)

Using the mean value inequality

x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\geq\binom{n-1}{p-1}\big(x_{1}\cdots x_{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}}

and the identity relation
n\binom{n-1}{p-1}=p\binom{n}{p}
, we immediately have

\begin{split}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}}\bigg)^{1/n}&\geq C(n,p)\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{\binom{n-1}{p-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\Lambda^{k}_{i}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n\binom{n-1}{p-1}}}\\
&=C(n,p)\big(\mathcal{M}_{p}(\lambda)^{-p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p\binom{n}{p}}}\\
&=C(n,p)\big(M_{p}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of (
A.2
), thereby establishing Lemma
A.1
.
∎

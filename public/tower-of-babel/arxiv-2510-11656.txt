Field Theoretic Approach to Interacting Two Body Tunneling
YE Guo

Abstract
Two body tunneling problems are hard to treat analytically due to the incompatibility between tunneling and perturbation theory. The lack of classical solutions of the Euclidean Lagrangian of continuous systems further thwarts semi-classical expansions. To develop an analytic theory which provides insight on interacting two-particle tunneling, we use new results to derive the Bethe-Salpeter equation of a tunneling field theory with Yukawa coupling. We show that in the 1+1D case a closed solution in the instantaneous positive-energy (Salpeter) regime is permitted. We then compute the scattering amplitude by perturbing on interparticle interaction and recover the Lippmann–Schwinger equation to confirm physical consistency and relevancy.



I
Introduction

Experimental advancements in the recent decade, especially in the realm of cold atom physics, have given us unprecedented ability to manipulate and measure quantum tunneling processes, even for investigating under barrier phenomena
Ramos et al. 2020
. By contrast, theoretical treatment of many particle tunneling remains challenging due to the non-perturbative nature of tunneling. Outside of single body cases, where we may solve exactly; discrete models, where it is easier to employ conventional many body formalisms
Fölling et al. 2007
; and the large
N
regime, where statistical effects dominate
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
, few analytical approaches have probed the interplay between interaction and tunneling beyond mean field approximations. The continuous two particle tunneling problem, with a Hamiltonian of the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{h}(1)+\hat{h}(2)+\alpha(W(\hat{x}_{1})+W(\hat{x}_{2}))+\beta U(\hat{x}_{1}-\hat{x}_{2})

(1)

with
\hat{h}(1),\hat{h}(2)
being the kinetic terms for particles 1 and 2,
W
being the static potential,
U
being the non-local interaction, and
\alpha,\beta
being the respective interaction strength, is a typical problem of this kind. It has broken translational symmetry on top of inseparability between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. These features typically preventing exact solutions, including classical solutions in imaginary time.

Despite these difficulties, theoretical understanding of two-particle tunneling remains desirable due to several reasons. It is abundant in nature
Pfützner et al. 2012
, vastly applied in technology such as the tunnel diode, Josephson junction, and scanning tunneling microscope
Esaki 1958
;
Josephson 1962
;
Binnig et al. 1982
, and provides a simplified model for nuclear problems such as two-proton decay, where the third body is treated as a background field
Goldansky 1960
;
Goldansky 1960
;
Pfützner et al. 2012
.

A field theoretic approach to tunneling has recently been formulated by
Zielinski et al. 2024a
which naturally encompasses many particle effects with the creation and annihilation of mesons, although the mesons themselves do not interact with the tunneling barrier. Using the same field Lagrangian, we go beyond a single particle
S
-matrix by using the resummed tunneling free propagator, obtained in the previous paper
Zielinski et al. 2024b
, to construct two particle ladder diagrams. Although we derive the resummed four point correlation function recursively in the form of the Bethe-Salpeter equation, which we show to be equivalent to the class of problems of (1) in the NR limit, obtaining a closed solution that is non-perturbative with respect to both the barrier and interparticle interaction remains mathematically complex and warrants a second study. Following the methodology of
Zielinski et al. 2024a
, we perturb the interparticle interaction and evaluate the tunneling amplitudes in the
\left|\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right|\xrightarrow{}0
limit to derive explicit tuneling amplitudes.

Whilst (5) was shown to be consistent with relativistic quantum mechanics

V
Conclusion

We have constructed a field theoretic, interactive two particle tunneling model using building blocks form a one particle tunneling theory, and shown that under approximations it permits closed solutions which may be readily extracted. We may now also confirm that such a model is consistent with known theoretical
Tajima et al. 2021
, numerical
Brugger et al. 2025
, and experimental
Fölling et al. 2007
results by analyzing the leading order effect of interaction in a fully relativistic setting. On the other hand, we have also confirmed that our model is the relativistic generalization of the canonical and hard to solve class of Hamiltonians of form (1). These confirmations now justify and provide checks for the next steps towards explicitly obtaining non-perturbative solution via. (41).

Without going into the non-perturbative regime for interparticle interaction, we have already been able to recover physical properties by the tunneling amplitude computed in section IV.A. Intriguing structures such as the ”peninsulas”, and the non zero tunneling amplitude for truly zero momentum in and out states remains to be examined in more detail, whilst tuning the parameters should reveal even more diverse structures. Whether they are physical or mathematical artifacts is also of question, and if they may be observed experimentally, the current model may serve as a good testing ground for understanding new quantum phenomena.

More fundamentally, our results demonstrate that a fully relativistic two-body tunneling framework can reproduce nontrivial correlated effects without invoking phenomenological potentials, thereby bridging few-body quantum mechanics and field-theoretic formulations. Unlike conventional non-relativistic tunneling models that rely on effective potentials or separable ansätze, the present construction arises directly from the underlying propagator structure of a relativistic field theory. This ensures that particle correlations, retardation effects, and virtual excitations are all treated on an equal footing, rather than being imposed by hand. In this sense, the model serves as a minimal yet self-consistent realization of interacting tunneling in which both the single-particle and two-particle amplitudes emerge from the same dynamical kernel.

Two-particle Green’s function with two planar
\delta
-barriers

Consider two nonrelativistic particles of masses
m_{1},m_{2}
in
3

H\;=\;H_{0}+W,\qquad H_{0}=\frac{\bm{p}_{1}^{2}}{2m_{1}}+\frac{\bm{p}_{2}^{2}}{2m_{2}},\qquad W=\lambda_{1}\,\delta(z_{1})+\lambda_{2}\,\delta(z_{2}).

We work at fixed total energy
E
, with the resolvents

G^{(\pm)}(E)\equiv(E-H\pm i0)^{-1},\quad G_{0}^{(\pm)}(E)\equiv(E-H_{0}\pm i0)^{-1}.

Use the mixed momentum basis

\big|\bm{k}_{1\parallel},p_{1z};\,\bm{k}_{2\parallel},p_{2z}\big\rangle
,
where
\bm{k}_{i\parallel}=(k_{ix},k_{iy})
are in-plane momenta and
p_{iz}
is the longitudinal one.
The free matrix element is

\displaystyle\langle\cdots|G_{0}^{(+)}(E)|\cdots\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}(\bm{k}_{1\parallel}-\bm{k}^{\prime}_{1\parallel})(2\pi)\delta(p_{1z}-p^{\prime}_{1z})(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}(\bm{k}_{2\parallel}-\bm{k}^{\prime}_{2\parallel})(2\pi)\delta(p_{2z}-p^{\prime}_{2z})}{E-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}+i0},

(83)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{i}}\!\left(k_{i\parallel}^{2}+p_{iz}^{2}\right)\quad(i=1,2).

(84)

Since
W
is a sum of two operators that act on different particles and are each rank-one in the longitudinal coordinate,
the Dyson equation resums independently on each leg. For matrix elements, this produces

\displaystyle\langle 1,2|G^{(+)}(E)|1^{\prime},2^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 1,2|G_{0}^{(+)}|1^{\prime},2^{\prime}\rangle+\langle 1,2|G_{0}^{(+)}\lambda_{1}\delta(z_{1})G^{(+)}|1^{\prime},2^{\prime}\rangle+\langle 1,2|G_{0}^{(+)}\lambda_{2}\delta(z_{2})G^{(+)}|1^{\prime},2^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\langle 1,2|G_{0}^{(+)}\lambda_{1}\delta(z_{1})G_{0}^{(+)}\lambda_{2}\delta(z_{2})G^{(+)}|1^{\prime},2^{\prime}\rangle+\cdots.

(85)

In the mixed representation this becomes a geometric series in the local (at the plane) free Green’s functions for each particle.
To make this explicit and obtain a diagonal kernel in the unprimed momenta, integrate over all primed momenta:

\mathcal{G}^{(+)}(E;\bm{k}_{1\parallel},p_{1z};\bm{k}_{2\parallel},p_{2z}):=\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{2}k^{\prime}_{1\parallel}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{dp^{\prime}_{1z}}{2\pi}\frac{d^{2}k^{\prime}_{2\parallel}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{dp^{\prime}_{2z}}{2\pi}\,\langle\bm{k}_{1\parallel},p_{1z};\bm{k}_{2\parallel},p_{2z}|G^{(+)}|\bm{k}^{\prime}_{1\parallel},p^{\prime}_{1z};\bm{k}^{\prime}_{2\parallel},p^{\prime}_{2z}\rangle.

\delta

p^{\prime}_{iz}

\displaystyle I_{1}^{(\pm)}(E;\bm{k}_{1\parallel}\,|\,\bm{k}_{2\parallel},p_{2z})

(87)

Write the longitudinal on-shell wavenumbers

k_{1z}:=\sqrt{\frac{2m_{1}}{\hbar^{2}}\big(E-\varepsilon_{2}\big)-k_{1\parallel}^{2}+i0},\qquad k_{2z}:=\sqrt{\frac{2m_{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\big(E-\varepsilon_{1}\big)-k_{2\parallel}^{2}+i0}.

Chromosome level genome assembly of giant freshwater prawn (Macrobrachium rosenbergii)
Authors: Shiyan Liu, Meihui Li, Chong Han, Shuisheng Li, Jin Zhang, Cheng Peng, Yong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The giant freshwater prawn (Macrobrachium rosenbergii) has many advantages in aquaculture, such as fast growth rate, short breeding cycle and good nutrition, which makes it a freshwater shrimp with high economic value. Herein, high-quality chromosome-level genome of both female and male prawns were obtained by combining Illumina paired-end sequencing, PacBio single molecule sequencing technique and High-through chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C) technologies. In ZZ male prawn, a final contig assembly of 3118.58 Mb with a N50 length of 956,237 bp was obtained. In WW female prawn, a final contig assembly of 3333.31 Mb with a N50 length of 1,143,555 bp was obtained. The assembled genome sequences from prawns were anchored to 59 chromosomes. Moreover, the sex chromosomes including W chromosome and Z chromosome were generated in prawn with the length of 36.23 Mb and 27.33 Mb, respectively. The sequence similarity of Z chromosome and W chromosome reached to 74.90%. The high-quality genome resource will be useful for further molecular breeding and functional genomic research of giant freshwater prawns.

Background & Summary

The giant freshwater prawn, Macrobrachium rosenbergii, is an ecologically and economically important native species of Southeast Asia, which mainly distributes in tropical and subtropical areas of the Indian ocean and the Pacific1. China is a crucial producing country of giant freshwater prawns, and has become the largest output country of giant freshwater prawns in the world2. The giant freshwater prawn is a ZW sex-determining type species3. Through removing androgenic glands or transplanting androgenic gland cells, researchers achieved sexual reversal and obtained WW neo-male and ZW neo-female populations of giant freshwater prawns4,5. In addition, giant freshwater prawn has a unique life history. The larvae need to live in salt water with a salinity of 12 to 14‰ to finish molting and metamorphosis, and then enter fresh water and reach sexual maturity after three months of growth. Moreover, male individuals of giant freshwater prawn are naturally aggressive and have large differences in body size, while females are gentle and more uniform in body size6–8. Thus, breeding monosex population can be benefit to aquaculture management. Besides, monosex shrimp culture is considered as an effective biotechnological alternative to raise yield9, which has been demonstrated in Penaeus monodon and Litopenaeus vannamei10,11. Also in pervious study, monosex culture was also performed in giant freshwater prawns and showed better performance than mixed culture in many important aquaculture parameters12. Therefore, to better breed monosex culture, it is necessary to get knowledge of the sex determination mechanism of giant freshwater prawns.

It is well known that crustacean genomes are very large and highly complex. For crustaceans, high-quality genome assembly are only available for the pacific white shrimp L. vannamei, the Antarctic krill Euphausia superba and the American lobster Homarus americanus13–16. Nearly no sex chromosome of crustaceans has been published, which limited the understanding of sex determination mechanism of crustaceans. In this study, high-quality chromosome-level genome assembly of ZZ male and WW female of giant freshwater prawns were first generated by a combination of Illumina paired-end sequencing, PacBio long-read sequencing and Hi-C technology (Fig. 1). Methods

Ethics statement

All experiments were performed according to the guidelines and approval of the respective Animal Research and Ethics Committees of Sun Yat-Sen University, Guangzhou, China, under permit No. SYSU-SK221027.

Sample collection and preparation

Samples from one-year-old giant freshwater prawns (body weight: 50 ± 5 g) were collected from Guangdong Marine and aquatic high-tech Park in Guangzhou, Guangdong, China. Method of producing WW female prawns was as previously described5. Healthy ZZ male prawns and WW female prawns which share the same parents were randomly selected and anesthetized using MS222 (60 mg/L) for 10 min to minimize suffering. Then, muscle tissues of three ZZ male prawns and three WW female prawns were used to construct six genomic DNA sequencing libraries, respectively.

Illumina DNA sequencing

To construct Illumina short-read sequencing libraries, genomic DNA were broken into small fragments of 350 bp and connected with universal connector. These libraries were quality-controlled by qPCR methods and Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA). Then, these libraries were sequenced by Illumina Novaseq6000 (Illumina, USA) using 150 base pair-end reads according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After removing adaptor sequences and low-quality reads, the clean data from Illumina sequencing were generated. As a result, a total of 310.38 Gb of clean short reads were generated, in which the coverage rate of ZZ male prawn genome was 49.88×, the value of Q20 was 95.75%, the value of Q30 was 90.15%, and the coverage rate of WW female prawn genome was 46.46×, the value of Q20 was 95.94%, the value of Q30 was 90.54% (Table 1). PacBio sequencing and assembly

To construct PacBio sequencing libraries, genomic DNAs were interrupted into a large fragment by 26 G Needle, and fragments over 10 kb were selected by BluePippin. After terminal repair and A-tail addition, the DNA libraries were prepared by connecting splices at both ends of the fragment respectively. The DNA sequencing libraries were released after the fragment combination with primer and polymerase. The DNA sequencing libraries were calculated by PacBio Caculater17, and then sequencing primers and sequencing enzymes were combined to the SMRT template according to the proportion. Finally, the on-board sequencing was conducted on the PacBio Sequel (Pacific Biosciences of California, USA). After quality control filtering, a total of 499.20 Gb of PacBio sequences data were obtained, in which the coverage rate of ZZ male prawn genome was 69.55×, and the coverage rate of WW female prawn genome was 86.96× (Table 1). Clean reads from PacBio DNA sequencing were used to construct a 23-mer frequency distribution map of ZZ male prawn and WW female prawn by jellyfish (version 2.2.10), and the Kmer distribution map was drawn to evaluate genome size and chromosome ploidy18 (Fig. 1). As a result, the ploidy of giant freshwater prawn was diploid (Fig. 2).Fig. 223-mer frequency distribution of giant freshwater prawn.

23-mer frequency distribution of giant freshwater prawn.

For genome assembly, the sequencing reads of PacBio was firstly assembled to contigs using Canu (version 2.2) with default parameters19. Then, reads from PacBio sequencing were compared to the contig sequence using minimap2 (version 2.20). Reads that were completely compared to the inside of the contig sequence and to both ends of the contig sequence and whose coverage was greater than 60% were considered to be from the same contig. Finally, Canu with default parameters was used to reassemble the mapped reads into contigs, repeating the above steps several times to ensure the accuracy of the data. As a result, a final contig assembly of 3118.58 Mb with a N50 length of 956,237 bp was obtained in ZZ male prawn, and a final contig assembly of 3333.31 Mb with a N50 length of 1,143,555 bp was obtained in WW female prawn (Table 2). BUSCO (version 5.2.2)20 was used to assess the integrity of genome assembly with the reference database of arthropoda_odb10. Hi-C analysis and chromosome assembly

The Hi-C technique was applied for chromosome construction for the giant freshwater prawn21,22. The construction of Hi-C library was conducted as following steps: firstly, the muscle tissues of ZZ male prawns and WW female prawns were fixed in formaldehyde to maintain the 3D structure of DNA in cells. Then, the products were digested by restriction endonuclease Hind III to obtain fragments with sticky ends. Thirdly, the DNA of giant freshwater prawns were purified by QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen) and then fragmented into 350 bp. Finally, the Hi-C libraries were quantified and sequenced using an Illumina NovaSeq6000 (PE150). From the Hi-C libraries, 118.68 Gb sequence data with 37.27 × depth of ZZ male prawn and 78.53 Gb sequence data with 24.38 × depth of WW female prawn were generated (Table 1). The genome was assembled using juicer (version 1.5)23 and 3D-DNA24 in combination with PacBio sequencing and Hi-C sequencing data to obtain a chromosomal level genome. The Hi-C sequencing data was compared with the chromosome genome by HiC-Pro (version 2.11.3)25 to obtain the interaction matrix, and then the Hi-C interaction map of the genome was drawn by Hicplotter (version 0.6.6)26 using the interaction matrix. As a result, the contigs from giant freshwater prawns were successfully clustered into 59 groups, which were further ordered and oriented into chromosomes. The number of chromosomes we generated were consistent with the number in giant freshwater prawn (2n = 118) (Table 4)27. Moreover, the sex chromosomes including Z chromosome and W chromosome were also generated with the length of 27.33 Mb and 36.23 Mb, respectively (Table 4). Repeat analysis

The method of homology-based analysis was used to identify the known repetitive elements in the whole genome of giant freshwater prawn by RepeatMasker (version 4.1.5)30. In this study, RepeatMasker analysis used Ecdysozoa classification in Dfam (version 3.7)31 for TEs detection. Various TE subfamilies were detected in the whole genome, accounting for 10.67% in ZZ male prawn genome and 10.67% in WW female prawn genome (Supplement Table 1). Especially, the length and distribution of TEs on W and Z chromosomes were counted and compared (Fig. 4b,c). The distribution of TEs in W and Z chromosomes were as follows: short interspersed nuclear elements (SINE), 0.26% and 0.29%; long interspersed nuclear elements (LINE), 2.31% and 2.45%; long terminal repeat (LTR), 1.04% and 0.98%; DNA transposons, 2.66% and 2.54%; and unknown elements, 2.66% and 2.68%. The length of the Z and W chromosome repeat sequences to be ~2.4 and 3.2 Mb, and they both account for 8.93% of the W or Z chromosomes. Then, the TE insertion times in genomes of female prawn and male prawn were calculated by LTR_retriever (version 2.9.8)32 with default parameters. The results indicated very recent mass insertion events of TEs into the W chromosome, which was dramatically different from those of Z chromosome TEs.

Gene prediction and annotation

Protein-coding genes prediction were conducted through a combination of de novo and transcriptome-based methods. The RNA-seq data of the giant freshwater prawn were obtained from NCBI (accession number: PRJNA818307). Firstly, to optimize the accuracy of gene annotation, the clean reads from RNA-seq were mapped to the giant freshwater prawn genome using Hisat2 (version 2.2.1)33, and reconstructed using Stringtie (version 1.3.5)34. Secondly, two de novo gene prediction programs, namely, Augustus (version 3.5.0)35 and GlimmerHMM (version 3.0.4)36 were used to predict coding regions in the repeat-masked genome. Finally, all these results were merged by MAKER2 (version 3.01.03)37, leading to a total of 24,761 protein-coding genes in ZZ male prawn and 24,773 in WW female prawn.

Functional annotation of protein-coding genes was performed using BLASTP (E-value: 1e-10) (version: 2.15.0+)38 against five integrated databases: Gene Ontology (GO, http://www.geneontology.org/page/go-database), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG, http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html), KOG (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/COG), TrEMBL, NR (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Technical Validation

Tissues were stored in liquid nitrogen until use. High-quality genomic DNA was isolated by TIANamp Marine Animals DNA Kit (Tiangen, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Firstly, the quality of DNA was detected using NanoDrop2000 spectrophotometer and 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Then, the concentration of DNA was quantified by Qubit Fluorometer (version 3.0; Invitrogen)50 for the construction of sequencing libraries.

Illumina high-throughput sequencing signals were converted into raw reads after base calling by CASAVA software. The original disembarkation sequence obtained by sequencing contain sequencing connector sequences and sequences with low quality. In order to ensure the quality of the information analysis data, we filtered the original sequences to obtain high quality clean reads, and all subsequent analyses were conducted based on clean reads. The judgment principle of sequences with low quality was as follow: when the N content of any sequence read exceeded 10% of the read base number, the paired reads were deleted; when the number of low-quality (Q < = 5) bases in any sequenced read exceeded 50% of the number of read bases, the paired reads were also deleted.

The constructed library was sequenced by Illumina Novaseq6000 (PE150) with small data volume to confirm whether the Hi-C library was successfully constructed. hiCUP51 was used to compare and analyze the Hi-C sequencing data with the reference genomes separately assembled from three generations of female samples. The efficient calculation formula of Hi-C data was Unique Di-Tags/Total Reads52.

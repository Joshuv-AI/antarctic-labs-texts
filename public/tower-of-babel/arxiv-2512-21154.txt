Limits of equi-affine equi-distant loci of planar convex domains with two non-parallel asymptotes
Nikita Kalinin, Mikhail Shkolnikov

Abstract
In this note we discuss the novel approach to define equi-affine invariants starting from tropical geometry, where one averages over the space of tropical structures of fixed co-volume. Applied to the tropical distance series, this gives a family of equi-affine invariant functions associated with convex domains which are expected to satisfy a number of remarkable properties. The present note contains a conjecture about the limit structure of corresponding level sets in the compact case, as well as the proof of an analogue of this conjecture for unbounded domains with two non-parallel asymptotes. In addition, we carry out an explicit computation for the arithmetic mean value at center of the unit disk.

Introduction

An equi-affine plane is modeled by
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the symmetry group generated by all translations and
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acting in the usual way. This means that among classical geometric quantities only the area makes sense. In particular, there is no invariant notion of distance between a pair of points on such a plane.

In this note, we introduce a family of functions assigning a positive number to a point inside a convex domain, which may be interpreted as a distance from the point to the boundary of the domain. The definition of such an “equi-affine distance function” stems from a suggestion of Conan Leung, who proposed to average the tropical distance function over the space of all tropical structures of fixed co-area of the ambient plane. The expectation is that this way we may recover the classical Monge-Ampére equation and the affine normal flow. See a popular exposition on how the optimal transport problem leads to the Monge-Ampére equation
[
2
]
and a polyhedral approximation for its solution by Pogorelov
[
14
,
13
,
7
,
3
,
8
]
.

Remarkably, in contrast with the tropical distance function, whose non-zero levels are polygonal, equi-affine distance function seems to have smooth level sets, which we may call “equi-distant loci” of a domain, even if we start with a polygon (Figure
2
shows a plot on a square). Our purpose is to initiate the study of these level sets. A particular conjecture that we put forward is that for a fixed compact convex domain, these loci, after an appropriate rescaling, converge to an ellipse. In fact, as a few numerical simulations performed by Ernesto Lupercio suggest, these level sets have monotonically increasing Mahler area (which is a scale free affine invariant roughly measuring the “roundness” of a domain) approaching that of the global maximum in the space of convex domains, i.e. that of ellipses. Thus, one can expect that developing the theory in higher dimensions may give a tool to tackle Mahler conjecture, which is a fundamental unsolved problem in affine and convex geometries.

At present, we do not know how to treat the maximal limit of equi-affine equi-distant loci in the compact case. However, for the analogous question, but in the non-compact case of a domain with two non-parallel asymptotes, it is relatively straightforward to show that the equi-affine equi-distant loci after a rescaling converge to branches of hyperbolas, which is the main proven result of this note, a first step toward a more general theory.

The prominence of ellipses and hyperbolae also relates to affine isoperimetric principles. In affine geometry
[
1
,
11
]
, ellipses often extremize or simplify affine-invariant functionals. For example, the affine isoperimetric inequality states that among all closed convex curves of a given affine length, the ellipse encloses the maximal area. Similarly, the affine curvature flows tend to “round out” convex shapes into ellipses. In fact, affine curve shortening flow and affine normal flow provide a dynamic illustration: Sapiro and Tannenbaum
[
9
]
showed that evolving a convex plane curve under motion by its affine normal causes the curve to become asymptotically ellipsoidal.

Definition, conjecture, theorem

An (integrable) tropical structure on the plane is a two-dimensional lattice
\Lambda
in the tangent space of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
at some point, which is extended to all other points via translations. The co-area of tropical structure
\Lambda
is the area of the quotient torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\penalty\Lambda
. The standard tropical structure
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
has co-area one.

The group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on the space of tropical structures of co-area one. The stabilizer of the standard tropical structure is
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
thus the space of all co-area one tropical structures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is identified with the quotient
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which has a finite volume with respect to a natural measure
\mu
(this volume is actually
\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
).

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with tropical structure
\Lambda.
We call it
\Lambda
-admissible if every line it contains (there might be none) goes in the direction of some vector in
\Lambda.
Note that admissibility of
\Omega
is equivalent to asking that among numbers
c_{\lambda}^{\Omega}=-\inf_{p\in\Omega}\lambda\cdot p
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
there exist a non-infinite one.

For a
\Lambda
-admissible
\Omega,
define the tropical distance function as a tropical series

\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega}(p)=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda\backslash\{0\}}(c_{\lambda}^{\Omega}+\lambda\cdot p),\hskip 15.0pt\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,+\infty).

A lot can be said about such tropical series, see for instance
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
. One important geometric property following from elementary number theory is that
(\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega})^{-1}(0)=\partial\Omega.
It is an important conceptual challenge to figure out if the tropical distance can be defined for a non-convex
\Omega,
see
[
10
]
.

A convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called strongly admissible if it is admissible for every tropical structure on the plane, i.e. if it doesn’t contain any lines.

Definition
.

For a real number
h>0
we define the
h
-equi-affine distance function
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
an
h
-Hölder average of the tropical distance series over the space of tropical structures of co-area one, i.e. as

\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}(p)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\Big(\int_{[\Lambda]\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}(\mathcal{F}^{\Omega}_{\Lambda}(p))^{h}d\mu[\Lambda]\Big)^{h^{-1}}

We will prove a bit later that
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}
is finite at every point. If we assume this, then it is clear from the definition that this notion is equi-affine invariant and homogeneous of degree one, i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{A(\Omega)}(A(p))=\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}(p)
and
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{r\Omega}(rp)=r\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}(p)
for any
A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\,p\in\Omega,
and
r>0.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the space of simple continuous curves (embedded circles or lines, unbounded in the latter case) on the plane considered up to homothety and translations, with its elements denoted by
[C]
.

We put forward the following conjecture:

Conjecture
.

Let
\Omega
be a compact convex domain with non-empty interior. For
h>0
denote by
\mathbb{m}_{h}^{\Omega}
the maximal value of
\mathcal{A}^{\Omega}_{h}.
Then there exists an ellipse
E^{h}_{\Omega}
such that when
t
approaches
\mathbb{m}_{h}^{\Omega}
from the left, the classes of level sets
[(\mathcal{A}^{\Omega}_{h})^{-1}(t)]
converge to
[E^{\Omega}_{h}]\in\mathcal{C}.

An interesting supplementary problem is to understand how fast the level sets degenerate, i.e. what is an asymptotic for the area of
(\mathcal{A}^{\Omega}_{\Lambda})^{-1}[t,\mathbb{m}_{h}^{\Omega}]
as
t
approaches the maximal value
\mathbb{m}_{h}^{\Omega}
(Figure
2
suggests that it is quadratic). It is also likely that the class of an ellipse
[E^{\Omega}_{h}]
wouldn’t depend on
h.

In the next section, we prove the following analogue of the conjecture above.

Theorem
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex planar domain with two non-parallel asymptotes. Denote by
H^{\Omega}
a branch of a hyperbola with the same asymptotes. Then, for any
h>0
the classes of level sets
[(\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega})^{-1}(t)]\in\mathcal{C}
converge to
[H^{\Omega}]
as
t\rightarrow+\infty.

The proof uses continuity on
\Omega
and reduction to the case of a quadrant, for which the level sets are hyperbolas due to the “hyperbolic rotation” symmetry.

(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\times\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})g,\ a_{t})\longmapsto\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})(ga_{t}).

Moreover, under the standard identification of

Hence, for averages along geodesic-flow trajectories, it suffices to analyze the right

4
Example: center of the disk

In this section we would like to carry out a concrete and explicit computation for a non-trivial value of some of the functions under consideration in the compact case. Take the standard unit disk
\bigcirc\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the underlying convex domain, we compute below
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\bigcirc}
at the center of the disk (other
h
are fully analogous).

Again, we may think of
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\bigcirc}(p)
as the average of
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}^{\bigcirc}(p)
over all tropical structures
\Lambda,
or as an average of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}^{A\bigcirc}(Ap)
over classes
[A]\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
i.e. fixing the standard tropical structure and varying within the orbit of the domain. The stabilizer of the disk
\bigcirc
under
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is precisely the rotation group
\operatorname{SO}(2),
thus the orbit is identified with the upper half-space
\mathbb{H}.

Under this identification, we think of points of
\mathbb{H}
as of ellipses of area
\pi
and with the origin as the center of symmetry. Such an ellipse is given by
Q=1,
where
Q
is the unique quadratic form
Q(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
satisfying
a,c>0
and
4ac-b^{2}=4.
Explicitly, the complex point in
\mathbb{H}
is represented by the positive imaginary part root
x
of the equation
Q(x,1)=0,
which is
-\frac{b}{2a}+\frac{i}{a}.

To compute the tropical distance function (for the standard tropical structure) at an interior point of this ellipse, we need to figure out which of the tropical monomials minimizes the infinite tropical sum, i.e. the infimum in the definition. In general and by itself, this is a hard linear programming problem, however in the specific case of the center of the ellipse
Q=1,
the solution is easy to formulate, and we do below something slightly more subtle.

For each compact convex domain
\Omega
we may look at the set of tropical monomials contributing to the maximum of the tropical distance series, which is the local Newton polygon, together with its subdivision – this information encodes the singularity type of the tropical caustic of
\Omega.
Domains with the same caustic singularity type define a stratum in the space of all convex domains. The miracle is that the restriction of this stratification to the space
\mathbb{H}
of area
\pi
ellipses coincides with the most standard stratification given by the translation of the interior
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\Re z|<\frac{1}{2},|z|>1\}
of the fundamental domain of the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-action, as well as its edges and the boundary.

More specifically, ellipses corresponding to points
z\in U
are exactly those where the monomials contributing to the maximum have gradients
(\pm 1,0)
and
(0,\pm 1),
and the tropical caustic has the vertical weight two edge (which passes through the origin). Thus, the value of the tropical distance series is just the
(1,0)
coefficient of the tropical series. In general, for the ellipse
Q=1
of area
\pi
the
\lambda
-coefficient in its tropical distance series is given by
\sqrt{Q^{*}(\lambda)},
where
Q^{*}
denotes the dual quadratic form for which the
xy
coefficient is replaced by its opposite. Therefore, in the above notation the value of the tropical distance series with standard tropical structure is
\sqrt{a},
i.e. root the of the
x^{2}
coefficient.

With all the above, we compute the desired value as

Discussion

As classical geometry teaches us, it is customary, convenient, and easy to apply the results of a more general affine geometry, with a larger group of symmetries, to a more specific geometry, such as Euclidean, with a smaller group of symmetries
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes\operatorname{SO}(2)
. What happens in the present note is going backwards – from tropical, with “rotation” group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, to equi-affine plane having larger non-translational part
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
of its symmetry group.

In addition, this is the second instance of an interaction between the tropical and the affine – the other one is the
s=\frac{2}{3}
pre-zeta
s\mapsto\int_{p\in\Omega}(\mathcal{F}^{\Omega}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(p))^{s-2}dp
residue formula for the affine arc-length, a subject of a work in progress that will be released soon (hopefully). In fact, it is conceivable that the equi-affine distance function would allow for a more transparent proof of that result.

There is yet another possible relation with other streams of our work – namely, we expect that the uniformly infinitely perturbed state in the tropical sandpile model, whose existence, apart from the (almost) trivial case of an interval as a domain, is currently established only numerically for some domains, and equi-affine invariance is not yet fully verified even at this level, should be a superposition of an equi-affine distance function, which would indeed be a clean explanation for its invariance and the emergence of a larger symmetry group at a bigger scale.

One stark difference between the affine and the tropical distance functions is that going to a level set of the tropical one defines a flow on the space of convex domains, the tropical wave front propagation, satisfying the most fundamental Huygens’ principle. It is unclear at the moment if the analogous property holds for equi-affine level sets at least for some averaging protocol, thus we refrain for the moment from using the terminology “affine wave front”. Clarifying this is a crucial direction for future research.

We observe smoothness of non-zero level sets, and wouldn’t be surprised if they are analytic (and thus likely remember the boundary in contrast with the blow-down information loss in the tropical wave front). Of course, by symmetry, we know that in the case of ellipses and hyperbolas, their level sets are again ellipses or hyperbolas. It is peculiar, that the same argument doesn’t work for parabolas, although, Konstantin Delchev has recently proposed using the continuity of the construction to establish this fact, i.e. that equi-affine equi-distant loci of conics stay conics.

The phenomenon that maximal limits of equi-distant sets become conics, that is partially established above in the unbounded case, has an important implication in the compact case: the maxima of the equi-affine distant functions on compact domains would be attained in single points (this is another discrepancy with the tropical case, where the maximum is often attained along a segment). One may expect that these points are centers of mass, which might be deducible from the global topology of the space of convex domains modulo the respective group of symmetries.

It is important to note that the existence of
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\Omega}
relies on two fundamental properties. The first is that the space of tropical structures of fixed co-area has finite volume, which formally allows the averaging (for instance, the space of Euclidean structures with fixed area form –
\mathbb{H}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SO}(2)
– doesn’t have this property). Second, the considered quantity must behave well with respect to the variation of tropical structure, i.e. be continuous or at least measurable, which wouldn’t hold for such basic invariants as point-to-point distances or angle’s tropical cotangent, see
[
6
]
. Even so, the integral still may diverge – the Lemma of the previous section states that it doesn’t happen for positive
h
.

Ramaswami Type translation formulae for the polylogarithm functions
Pawan Singh Mehta and Biswajyoti Saha

Abstract
. In 1934, Ramaswami proved a number of curious translation formulae satisfied by the Riemann zeta function. Such translation formulae, in turn give the meromorphic extension of the Riemann zeta function. In 1954, Apostol extended those identities to establish a family of such similar translation formulae. In this article, we establish many such Ramaswami and Apostol type translation formulae for the Dirichlet series defining the polylogarithm functions. This extended set up has many interesting applications, for example, it allows us to also find some (seemingly new) recurrence relations between the Bernoulli numbers, and use them to deduce some congruence properties of the tangent numbers.

1.
Introduction

For a complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
, the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
is defined by

\zeta(s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{s}}.

It defines a holomorphic function in the complex half plane
\Re(s)>1
and can be
continued to a meromorphic function in
{\mathbb{C}}
having a simple pole at
s=1
with residue 1.
V. Ramaswami
[
8
]
obtained some interesting translation formulae involving
the Riemann zeta function, which can be used recursively to establish the analytic
continuation of
\zeta(s)
.
For each
n\geq 0
, let
P_{n}(s)
be defined by

P_{0}(s)=1,P_{n}(s)=\frac{s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)}{n!}.

Then for complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
, he
[
8
]
proved the following identities:

(1)

\zeta(s)\left(1-2^{1-s}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{P_{n}(s)\zeta(s+n)}{2^{s+n}},

(2)

\zeta(s)\left(1-3^{1-s}\right)=1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{P_{2n}(s)\zeta(s+2n)}{3^{s+2n}},

(3)

\zeta(s)\left(1-2^{1-s}-3^{1-s}-6^{1-s}\right)=1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{P_{2n}(s)\zeta(s+2n)}{6^{s+2n}}.

He then used the last two formulae to show non-vanishing of
\zeta(s)
in the region
1/2\leq\Re(s)\leq 1
and
|s|\leq 8
.
These similar looking formulae however does not seem to have a common natural extension.
T. Apostol
[
1
]
proved two different formulae, one extending (
1
), and another
extending (
2
) and (
3
).
For any given integer
k\geq 2
and a complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
, he proved:

(4)

\zeta(s)\left(1-k^{1-s}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{P_{n}(s)\zeta(s+n)}{k^{s+n}}\frac{B_{n+1}(k)-B_{n+1}}{n+1},

where for
n,k\geq 0
,
B_{n}(k)
are the values of Bernoulli polynomial
B_{n}(x)
defined by the generating function

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

and
B_{n}:=B_{n}(0)
is the
n
th Bernoulli number.
Note that when
k=2
, (
4
) is same as (
1
) (using (
10
) below).
Moreover, for all integers
k>2
, Apostol
[
1
]
proved that

(6)

\displaystyle\zeta(s)\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)d^{-s}=\phi(-s,k)+2\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{P_{2n}(s)\zeta(s+2n)}{k^{s+2n}}\phi(2n,k),

where
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function and
\phi(s,k):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq\frac{k}{2}\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}h^{s}
.
Taking
k=3,6
in (
6
), one gets back (
2
), (
3
), respectively.

In this article, we consider the polylogarithm function
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
, where
z
is a root of unity,
defined for complex numbers
s
with
\Re(s)>1
as a Dirichlet series by

(7)

\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{s}}.

The above series converges uniformly in every compact subset of the complex half plane

\Re(s)>1
. Therefore, it defines a holomorphic function in the complex half plane
\Re(s)>1
. If
z=1
then

\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)=\zeta(s)
. Hence it has a simple pole at
s=1
with residue 1 and for other values of
z
,

\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
can be extended to an entire function.
The aim of this article is to obtain Ramaswami and Apostol type formulae involving
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
.
Another translation formula satisfied by
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
, which can also be used to get the analytic continuation
of
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
, can be found in
[
10
]
.

We also exhibit some applications of such identities.
For example, we obtain some (seemingly new) recurrence formulae for the Bernoulli numbers.
We show that (see (
45
) below) for every integer
n\geq 1
,
the even indexed Bernoulli number
B_{2n}
satisfies the recurrence relation

(1-2^{2n})(1-3^{2n-1})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}=\frac{2^{2n-1}-1}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}\ 3^{2i-1}(1-2^{2i})(2^{2n-2i}-1).

This has in turn interesting applications regarding the tangent numbers, defined for
n\geq 1
by

T_{n}:=(-1)^{n}2^{2n}(1-2^{2n})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}.

Note that the
n
-th tangent number
T_{n}
is the coefficient of
x^{2n-1}/(2n-1)!
in the Taylor expansion
of the tangent function around
x=0
, i.e.,

\tan x=\sum_{n\geq 1}T_{n}\frac{x^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}.

Properties of the Tangent numbers are of classical interest (see
[
5
]
).
It is well-known that
T_{n}
’s are integers
[
5
]
(also see
[
2
, Remark 1.18]
).
From the work of
[
4
]
, it also follows that they are all even integers for
n\geq 2
(also see
[
11
]
).

In this article, as an application of our formulae, we prove that for
n\geq 2
, the last digit of
T_{n}
is either
2
or
6
,
depending on
n
is even or odd. We also prove that the Tangent numbers are never divisible by
3
and give
explicit congruence relations modulo
3
.

We also get some series expressions involving the odd zeta values, for example,

(8)

-\frac{3}{16}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{m}{3^{2m+1}}\zeta(2m+1)(2^{2m-1}-1)(2^{-2m}-1).

This brings us to the notion of
\zeta
-series representation
for a real number
(see
[
3
]
for an analogous concept). We say that a real number
x
has a

\zeta
-series representation
if there exists a sequence of rational numbers
(p_{n}/q_{n})_{n\geq 2}

such that
x
can be written as a convergent infinite series of the form

\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\zeta(n).

Using (
8
), we thus get that every rational number has a
\zeta
-series representation. Moreover, we get
that every rational number has a
\zeta
-series representation, which only includes the odd zeta values. This can also
be concluded from
[
8
, eq. (2)]
.

2.
Translation Formulae for
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)

In this section, we present Ramaswami and Apostol type translation formulae for
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
where
z
is a primitive
q
-th root of unity
for
q\geq 1
. Define
\delta_{q}
to be the integral part of
1/q
. We prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re(s)>1
and
z
be a primitive
q
-th root of unity with
q\geq 1
. Let
k\geq 2

be an integer of the form
bq+1
for some positive integer
b
. Then we have,

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)(1-k^{\delta_{q}-s})=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{-h}h^{m}.

For
z=1
, (
9
) reduces to (
4
), using the formula (see
[
2
, p. 58]
)

(10)

\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}h^{m}=\frac{B_{m+1}(k)-B_{m+1}}{m+1},

which is true for any positive integers
k,m\geq 1
.
Hence (
9
) can be deemed as an extension of Apostol’s translation formula (
4
).
We also establish a closed formula for the sum
\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}
, similar to (
10
),
when
z\neq 1
is a primitive root of unity.
As it turns out, this sum is related to the values of the generalised Euler polynomials defined in
[
7
, Appendix A]
.
Using this formula, we obtain another form of the translation formula (
9
) for
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
,
when
z\neq 1
is a root of unity (see (
12
) below).

We first recall the definition of the generalised Euler polynomials. For a given integer
q\geq 2
, the
n
-th generalised
Euler polynomial
E_{q,n}(x)
is defined by the following generating series:

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{qe^{xt}}{1+e^{t}+\cdots+e^{(q-1)t}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}E_{q,n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Corollary 1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer and other notations be as in Theorem
1
. Then we have,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)(1-k^{-s})=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{z^{j}-1}{z^{j}(z-1)}\left(E_{q,m}(j)-E_{q,m}(k+j-1)\right).

Remark 1
.

With the notations as in Theorem
1
, we have the following alternating version of the translation formula (
9
):

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)(1-k^{\delta_{q}-s})=\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}.

The identities (
9
) and (
13
) lead to the following two identities, by addition and subtraction:

(14)

\displaystyle 2\ \mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)(1-k^{\delta_{q}-s})=\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\left(z^{h}(-1)^{m}+z^{-h}\right)h^{m},

(15)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\left(z^{h}(-1)^{m}-z^{-h}\right)h^{m}.

Now if we substitute
z=1
and
k=2
in (
14
) and (
15
),
we recover
[
8
, eq. (1) and (2)]
. Ramaswami used these identities to give
\zeta
-series representations of

1
,
\log 2
, Euler’s constant
\gamma
, and
\zeta(3)/\zeta(2)
(see
[
8
, §3, §5]
).
We further derive the following formulae, involving the Möbius function
\mu(n)
.

Theorem 2
.

With the notations as in Theorem
1
, we have,

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s}}\mathrm{Li}_{z^{d}}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{m}P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}z^{h}h^{m},

and

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s}}\mathrm{Li}_{z^{d}}(s)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}z^{-h}h^{m}.

Subtracting (
17
) from (
16
), we get the following translation formula:

(18)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(z^{h}(-1)^{m}-z^{-h}\right)h^{m}.

Note that if we substitute
z=1
and
k=3
in (
17
), we get

\zeta(s)(1-3^{1-s})=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\zeta(s+m)}{3^{s+m}}(1+2^{m}),

which can be found in
[
6
, (2.16)]
.
Following the steps in Theorem
2
, we also prove the following theorem, which gives an extension
of Apostol’s identity (
6
) for
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
k>2
be an integer and the other notations be as in Theorem
1
. Then we have,

(19)

\sum_{d|k}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{s}}\mathrm{Li}_{z^{d}}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k/2\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}z^{h}h^{-s}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k/2\\
(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(z^{h}(-1)^{m}+z^{-h}\right)h^{m}.

Note that if we take
z=1
in (
19
), we get back (
6
), which in turn includes
(
2
) and (
3
).

3.
Proof of Translation Formulae for
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)

Inspired by
[
1
]
, we begin with the Lerch zeta function

\phi(s,a,z)
. For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re(s)>1
and a real number
a>0
,
\phi(s,a,z)
is defined by the following absolute convergent series,

(20)

\phi(s,a,z):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{(n+a)^{s}}.

It is easy to see that
z\ \phi(s,1,z)=\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
.
Note that it is easy to see that
the Lerch zeta function
\phi(s,a,z)
satisfies the following indentities,

(21)

\displaystyle\phi(s,a,z)-z\phi(s,a+1,z)=a^{-s},

and

(22)

\displaystyle\phi(s,ka,z)=k^{-s}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z^{i}\phi(s,i/k+a,z^{k}),

where
k\geq 1
is an integer. The identity (
22
) can be seen as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{(n+ka)^{s}}=k^{-s}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{(n/k+a)^{s}}=k^{-s}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{i}\frac{z^{kn}}{(n+i/k+a)^{s}}=k^{-s}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z^{i}\phi(s,i/k+a,z^{k}).

Now for a fixed complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
and an integer
m\geq 0
, the
m
-th derivative of the Lerch zeta function

\phi(s,a+1,z)
with respect to
a
is,

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\frac{\partial^{m}\mkern 0.0mu\phi(s,a+1,z)}{\partial\mkern 0.0mua^{m}}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{m}\mkern 0.0mu\phi(s,a+1,z)}{\partial\mkern 0.0mua^{m}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial^{m}\mkern 0.0mu\phi(s,a+1,z)}{\partial\mkern 0.0mua^{m}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial^{m}\mkern 0.0mu\phi(s,a+1,z)}{\partial\mkern 0.0mua^{m}}}}

\displaystyle=(-s)(-s-1)\cdots(-s-m+1){\phi(s+m,a+1,z)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}m!P_{m}(s){\phi(s+m,a+1,z)}.

Therefore, the Taylor series of
{\phi(s,a+1,z)}
at
a=0
is given by the series,

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{m\geq 0}(-a)^{m}P_{m}(s){\phi(s+m,1,z)}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{m\geq 0}(-a)^{m}P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m).\end{split}

We prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

For fixed complex numbers
s,z
with
\Re(s)>1
and
|z|=1
, the Taylor series (
23
) has the radius of convergence
1
.
Moreover, the Taylor series (
23
) converges uniformly to the Lerch zeta function
\phi(s,a+1,z)
for
a\in(-1,1)
.

Proof.

Note that for a fixed complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
,
\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)=z
.
Also,

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\frac{P_{m+1}(s)}{P_{m}(s)}\right\rvert=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\lvert\frac{s+m}{m+1}\right\rvert=1.

Therefore, the radius of convergence of the series (
23
) is 1 and the series converges uniformly for
a\in(-1,1)
.
Next we want to show that for every
a\in(-1,1)
, the Taylor series in (
23
) converges to the function
\phi(s,a+1,z)
.
To do this, for each
n\geq 0
, we consider the remainder term
R_{n}(a)
, defined by

R_{n}(a)=\phi(s,a+1,z)-\frac{1}{z}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(-a)^{m}P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m).

So it is enough to show that for each
a
with
|a|<1
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{n}(a)=0
. Now by a
version of Taylor’s theorem (see
[
9
, Corollary 31.6]
), for each
a\in(-1,1)
, there exists a real number
x
in between 0 and
a
such that

(24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{n}(a)&=(-1)^{n}n!P_{n}(s){\phi(s+n,x+1,z)}\frac{(a-x)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}a\\
&=(-1)^{n}nP_{n}(s){\phi(s+n,x+1,z)}(a-x)^{n-1}a.\end{split}

Note that,

|\phi(s+n,x+1,z)|\leq\frac{1}{(x+1)^{\Re(s)+n}}+\frac{1}{(2+x)^{n}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{(m+x+1)^{\Re(s)}}.

Since
\Re(s)>1
and
x
is in between 0 and
a
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(2+x)^{n}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{(m+x+1)^{\Re(s)}}=0.

If
0<x<a<1
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(x+1)^{\Re(s)+n}}=0
. Since the series (
23
) converges uniformly
for
a\in(-1,1)
, so does its formal derivative.
Therefore,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}nP_{n}(s)a^{n}=0
and hence
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{n}(a)=0
, as
(a-x)^{n-1}a\leq a^{n}
.

Next let
-1<a<x<0.
Note that it is enough to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(-1)^{n}nP_{n}(s)\frac{(a-x)^{n-1}a}{(x+1)^{\Re(s)+n}}=0.

For this, first note that
\lvert\frac{a-x}{x+1}\rvert\leq|a|
for
-1<a<x<0
. Therefore,

\left|nP_{n}(s)\frac{(a-x)^{n-1}}{(x+1)^{n-1}}\frac{a}{(x+1)^{\Re(s)+1}}\right|\leq\frac{n|P_{n}(s)||a|^{n}}{(x+1)^{\Re(s)+1}}.

Hence, by a similar argument as in the previous case, we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{n}(a)=0
when
-1<a<0

This completes the proof of Lemma
1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Note that from Lemma
1
we have,

(25)

z\phi(s,a+1,z)=\sum_{m\geq 0}(-a)^{m}P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m).

Taking
a=-\frac{h}{k}
in the above equation and summing for
0\leq h\leq k-1
after multiplying by
z^{-h}
on
both the sides of the above equation, we have,

(26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}z\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}z^{-h}\phi\left(s,(k-h)/k,z\right)&=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{m}}\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}z^{-h}h^{m}\\
&=\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}z^{-h}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{-h}h^{m}.\end{split}

Since
k=bq+1
, we have
\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}z^{-h}=k^{\delta_{q}}
. Hence the right-hand side of the above equation is

k^{\delta_{q}}\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{-h}h^{m}.

To calculate the left-hand side of (
26
), we first substitute
a=1/k
in (
22
). Since
k=bq+1

for some positive integer
b
then (
22
) becomes

k^{s}\phi(s,1,z)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z^{i}\phi(s,(i+1)/k,z)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z^{k-i-1}\phi(s,(k-i)/k,z)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}z^{-i}\phi(s,(k-i)/k,z).

Since
z\phi(s,1,z)=\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
, using the above equations, we can write (
26
) as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)(1-k^{\delta_{q}-s})=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s+m)}{k^{s+m}}\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{-h}h^{m}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1
.

To prove Corollary
1
, we derive a closed formula for
\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}
, where
z

is a primitive
q
-th root of unity. The proof is then completed by the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer and
z
be a primitive
q
-th root of unity. Then for every positive integers
k,m
, we have

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{z(z^{j}-1)}{z-1}\left(E_{q,m}(j)-z^{k-1}E_{q,m}(k+j-1)\right).

Proof.

Note that the polynomial
E_{q,n}(x)
satisfies the formula,

(28)

E_{q,n}(x)+E_{q,n}(x+1)+\cdots+E_{q,n}(x+q-1)=qx^{n},\text{\ \ \ for all $n\geq 0$ and $x\in{\mathbb{R}}$.}

This follows from the fact that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(E_{q,n}(x)+E_{q,n}(x+1)+\cdots+E_{q,n}(x+q-1)\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=qe^{xt}.

By comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
on both the sides of the above equation, we get the identity (
28
).
Therefore we have,

\displaystyle q\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}E_{q,m}(h+i)=\sum_{j=1}^{k+q-2}E_{q,m}(j)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h+i=j,\\
1\leq h\leq k-1,\\
0\leq i\leq q-1\end{subarray}}z^{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}E_{q,m}(j)\sum_{h=1}^{j}z^{h}+\sum_{j=q}^{k-1}E_{q,m}(j)\sum_{h=j-q+1}^{j}z^{h}+\sum_{j=k}^{k+q-2}E_{q,m}(j)\sum_{h=j-q+1}^{k-1}z^{h}.

Since
1+z+\cdots+z^{q-1}=0
, the second sum on the right-hand side of the above equation is zero. Hence we can write,

\displaystyle q\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}z^{h}h^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}E_{q,m}(j)z\frac{z^{j}-1}{z-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}E_{q,m}(k+j)z^{k-q}\sum_{h=j+1}^{q-1}z^{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}E_{q,m}(j)z\frac{z^{j}-1}{z-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}E_{q,m}(k+j)z^{k+j+1}\frac{z^{q-j-1}-1}{z-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}E_{q,m}(j)z\frac{z^{j}-1}{z-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}z^{k}\frac{1-z^{j}}{z-1}E_{q,m}(k+j-1).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2
.
∎

4.
Some applications

For complex number
s
with
\Re(s)>1
, the Dirichlet series
\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n^{s}}

is related to
\zeta(s)
as

\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)=(2^{1-s}-1)\zeta(s).

Therefore,
\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(0)=B_{1}
and for every
n\geq 1
,

(37)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(-n)=(1-2^{n+1})\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}.

The above relation between
\zeta(s)
and
\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)
in fact allows us to derive many more
translation formulae satisfied by the Riemann zeta function.
Note that all the translation formulae in §
2
also hold for meromorphic functions
\mathrm{Li}_{z}(s)
in the whole complex plane.
We will use the translation formulae (
14
) and (
15
) to find alternate expressions of the special values of
\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)
at negative
integers. Then we will apply (
37
) to get certain recurrence relations satisfied by the Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
and integer
k\geq 2
, define

T(s,k):=\sum_{1\leq h\leq k-1}(-1)^{h}h^{s}.

For
z=-1
(i.e.
q=2
and
k>2
odd), (
14
) and (
15
) give us

(38)

\displaystyle 2\ \mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)(1-k^{-s})=T(-s,k)+2\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{2m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s+2m)}{k^{{s+2m}}}T(2m,k),

(39)

\displaystyle T(-s,k)=2\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{2m-1}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s+2m-1)}{k^{{s+2m-1}}}T(2m-1,k).

Remark 3
.

Note that
T(n,k)\in{\mathbb{Q}}
for any integer
n
. If we take
k=3
and
s=2
in (
39
) and use the fact that
\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)=(2^{1-s}-1)\zeta(s)
,
we get (
8
).

Note that for any integers
m,n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle P_{m}(-n)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{m}\binom{n}{m}&\text{ for }n\geq m,\\
0&\text{ for }n<m.\end{array}\right.

Now for
n\geq 1
, substituting
s=-2n
in (
39
) and
s=-2n+1
in (
38
), we get

(40)

\displaystyle T(2n,k)+2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{2n}{2m-1}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(-2n+2m-1)}{k^{-2n+2m-1}}T(2m-1,k)=0,

and

(41)

\begin{split}&2\ \mathrm{Li}_{-1}(1-2n)(1-k^{2n-1})\\
&=T(2n-1,k)+2\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2m}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(1-2n+2m)}{k^{{1-2n+2m}}}T(2m,k),\end{split}

respectively. These lead us to the following formulae, on using (
37
).

Theorem 4
.

For odd integer
k\geq 3
, the even indexed Bernoulli numbers satisfy the following relations:

(42)

\displaystyle T(2n,k)=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{2n}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}(2^{2i}-1)k^{2i-1}T(2n-2i+1,k),

and

(43)

\begin{split}&(1-2^{2n})(1-k^{2n-1})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\\
&=\frac{T({2n-1},k)}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}k^{2i-1}(1-2^{2i})T(2n-2i,k),\end{split}

for all
n\geq 1
.

As
T(s,3)=2^{s}-1
, taking
k=3
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

For all
n\geq 1
, the even indexed Bernoulli numbers satisfy the following recurrence relations:

(44)

\displaystyle B_{2n}(2^{2n}-1)3^{2n-1}=\frac{2^{2n}-1}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}(1-2^{2i})3^{2i-1}(2^{2n-2i+1}-1),

(45)

\displaystyle(1-2^{2n})(1-3^{2n-1})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}=\frac{2^{2n-1}-1}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}\ 3^{2i-1}(1-2^{2i})(2^{2n-2i}-1).

In fact, by taking different values of
k
, we can derive many such recurrence relations satisfied by
the even indexed Bernoulli numbers. The above recurrence relation (
45
) has an interesting application for the
tangent numbers. Recall that, for integer
n\geq 1
,
T_{n}
denotes the
n
-th tangent number defined by

T_{n}=(-1)^{n}2^{2n}(1-2^{2n})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}.

Note that
T_{1}=1
. Moreover, this is the only odd tangent number. This can be seen from the following corollary,
where we, more importantly, give an explicit congruence relation modulo
3
.

Corollary 3
.

For all integers
n\geq 2
, the
n
-th tangent number
T_{n}
is an even integer and not divisible by
3
. Moreover, for any
n\geq 1
,

T_{n}\bmod{3}\equiv\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd},\\
2&\text{ if }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Put
t_{n}=(-1)^{n}T_{n}
. By multiplying both the sides of (
45
) by
2^{2n}
, we get

(46)

\displaystyle(1-3^{2n-1})t_{n}=2^{2n-1}(2^{2n-1}-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}3^{2i-1}2^{2n-2i}(2^{2n-2i}-1)t_{i}.

As
t_{i}
’s are integers, note that for
n\geq 2
, each term in the right-hand side of the above equation is divisible by
4
.
However, as
1-3^{2n-1}\equiv 2\bmod{4}
, we get 2 must divide
t_{n}
.

Note that
3
divides all the terms of the right-hand side of (
46
), except the first term. Also,

2^{2n-1}(2^{2n-1}-1)\equiv 2\bmod{3},\quad 1-3^{2n-1}\equiv 1\bmod{3}.

Hence
t_{n}\equiv 2\bmod{3}
, i.e.,
T_{n}\equiv(-1)^{n}2\bmod{3}
. This completes the proof of Corollary
3
.
∎

For
n\geq 2
, the tangent numbers
T_{n}
’s are not only even, but they also have a special feature modulo
10
.
This can be proved as a corollary of Theorem
3
.

Corollary 4
.

For
n\geq 2
, the last digit of tangent number
T_{n}
is either
2
or
6
, depending on
n
is even or odd. More precisely,
for any
n\geq 1
,

T_{n}\bmod{5}\equiv\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd},\\
2&\text{ if }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Taking
z=-1
and
k=5
in (
19
), we get the translation formula,

\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s)(1-5^{-s})=(2^{-s}-1)+2\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{P_{2m}(s)\mathrm{Li}_{-1}(s+2m)}{5^{s+2m}}(2^{2m}-1).

Now if we substitute
s=-2n+1

for
n\geq 1
and use (
37
), we get the following identity:

(1-5^{2n-1})(1-2^{2n})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}=(2^{2n-1}-1)+2\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2m}{5^{2n-2m-1}}(1-2^{2n-2m})\frac{B_{2n-2m}}{2n-2m}(2^{2m}-1).

As
\binom{2n-1}{2m}=\binom{2n-1}{2n-2m-1}
, replacing
n-m
by
i
, we get the following recurrence relation:

\displaystyle(1-5^{2n-1})(1-2^{2n})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}=(2^{2n-1}-1)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}\frac{B_{2i}}{2i}{5^{2i-1}}(1-2^{2i})(2^{2n-2i}-1).

Multiply both the sides of the above equation by
2^{2n}
and put
t_{n}=(-1)^{n}T_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, we get the following recurrence for
t_{n}
:

(47)

\displaystyle(1-5^{2n-1})t_{n}=2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{2n-1}{2i-1}{5^{2i-1}}2^{2n-2i}(2^{2n-2i}-1)t_{i}.

We are now ready to prove Corollary
4
.

Proof of Corollary
4
.

For integer
n\geq 1
, using (
47
) we have
t_{n}\equiv 2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)\bmod{5}
. It is easy to compute that

2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)\bmod{5}\equiv\begin{cases}4&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd},\\
2&\text{ if }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

T_{n}\equiv(-1)^{n}2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)\bmod{5}\equiv\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd},\\
2&\text{ if }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Now we know from Corollary
3
that for
n\geq 2
,
T_{n}\equiv 0\bmod{2}
. Hence by the Chinese remainder theorem
we get that

T_{n}\bmod{10}\equiv\begin{cases}6&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd},\\
2&\text{ if }n\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

This completes the proof.
∎

On Column sufficiency and Extended Horizontal Linear Complementarity Problem
Punit Kumar Yadav

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the concept of the column-sufficient W W -property for a set of matrices and prove the convexity of the solution set for the Extended Horizontal Linear Complementarity Problem. Additionally, we present an uniqueness result and establish several results related to the column-sufficient W W -property.

W

M

Introduction

A real
n\times n
matrix
C
is a column-sufficient matrix if

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~x*Cx\leq 0\implies x*Cx=0,

where
{}^{\prime}*^{\prime}
denotes the pointwise product. Initially, Cottle et al.
[
2
]
introduced the concept of a column-sufficient matrix, and proved that the class of column-sufficient matrices ensures the convexity of the solution set of linear complementarity problem (LCP). The LCP(
C,q
) is to find a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},~y=Cx+q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\text{,~and}~x*y=0,

where
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The LCP has wide-ranging applications in optimization, economics, and game theory, see
[
2
,
4
,
10
,
13
]
. Over the past three decades, many generalizations of the LCP have been studied in the literature (see
[
1
,
6
,
10
]
). Many important properties have been explored in relation to the class of column-sufficient matrices, see
[
2
,
4
]
. For example, the
P
matrix property (
[x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~x*Cx\leq 0\implies x=0]
) is equivalent to the column-sufficient matrix property under the nondegeneracy condition (
[x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~x*Cx=0\implies x=0]
). For the class of
Z
-matrices, the column-sufficient matrix property is also equivalent to the column-sufficient matrix property on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, see in
[
4
]
.

Mangasarian and Pang
[
10
]
has expanded the concept of the column sufficient property to the X-column-sufficiency property
([x_{0}*x_{1}\leq 0,~C_{0}x_{0}-C_{1}x_{1}=0\implies(x_{0},x_{1})=(0,0)])
within the context of horizontal linear complementarity problem (HLCP). Furthermore, Gowda
[
3
]
introduced and characterized column-sufficiency and
P
properties specific to vertical, horizontal, and mixed LCPs. He also proves that the X-column-sufficiency property is equivalent the convexity of solution set for HLCP. For a pair of real
n\times n
matrices
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
, along with a vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the HLCP seeks a pair of vectors
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfy the conditions:

\displaystyle C_{0}x-C_{1}y=q~~\text{and}~~x\wedge y=0.

The HLCP has gained significant research attention due to its extensive applications across various domains (see references
[
6
,
11
,
10
]
). The HLCP is generalized, in context to the set of matrices, as the Extended horizontal linear complementarity problem, see
[
3
,
12
,
13
]
. For an ordered set of matrices
{\bf C}:=\{C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, a vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and an ordered set of positive vectors
{\bf d}:=\{d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{k-1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Extended Horizontal Linear Complementarity Problem, denoted EHLCP(
{\bf C},{\bf d},q
), aims to find vectors
x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfy the conditions:

\displaystyle C_{0}{{x}_{0}}=

\displaystyle q+\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}{x}_{i},

\displaystyle~~{x}_{0}\wedge{x}_{1}=0~~\text{and}~~(d_{j}-

\displaystyle{\displaystyle x}_{j})\wedge{x}_{j+1}=0~\forall j\in[k-1].

When
k=1
, EHLCP reduces to the HLCP. Furthermore, if we set
C_{0}=I,k=1
, the EHLCP becomes to the standard LCP. A comprehensive analysis of the EHLCP can be found in the article by Sznajder and Gowda
[
13
]
. Recently, Yadav and Palpandi
[
8
,
9
]
have extended the concepts of
R_{0}
, SSM, and nondegenerate matrix properties in the context of the EHLCP to study the solution set of the EHLCP.

Motivated by the column-sufficient property in context with LCP and HLCP, we pose the question: Can we define column-sufficient complementarity conditions for sets of matrices and demonstrate the convexity of the solution set for the EHLCP? To address this, we introduce the notion of the column sufficient-
W
property for sets of matrices. Our contributions include the following:

(i)

We define the column sufficient-
W
property and prove the convexity of the solution set for EHLCP.

(ii)

We prove that the EHLCP has a unique solution for
q>0
due to the column sufficient-
W
property.

(ii)

We present relationship between the column
W
-property and the column sufficient-
W
property. Also, we prove a result related to
Z
-matrices in context of column sufficient-
W
property.

The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 presents essential definitions and necessary results for subsequent discussions. In next section, we introduce the column sufficient-
W
property and establish our main theorem. The final section examines some properties related to the the column
W
-property and the column sufficient-
W
property.

3
Column Sufficient-W Property

In this section, we define the
column sufficient-
W
property as a generalization of the column-sufficient matrix property. We then show that the column sufficient-
W
property ensures the convexity of the solution set of the EHLCP.

Definition 5
.

We say
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}
has column sufficient-W(cS-
W
) property if

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{r}x_{i}*x_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k,\\
C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},x_{0}*x_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k]\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0~\forall 0\leq i\leq k-1.

For
i=1
, we observe that the cS-
W
property becomes the X-column-sufficiency property for a pair of square matrices as described in
[
10
]
. Additionally, for
i=1
and
C_{0}=I
, it reduces to the column-sufficient matrix property.

Next we prove a relation between the cS-
W
property and X-column-sufficiency property.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\bf C}
has the
cS
-
W
property. Then,
the pair
(C_{i},C_{i+1})
has the X-column-sufficiency property for all
0\leq i\leq k-1.

Proof.

We consider here two cases.

Case-I
: For
i=0,
let
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

C_{0}x_{0}-C_{1}x_{1}=0\text{ and }x_{0}*x_{1}\leq 0.

Set
{\bf{x}}:=(x_{0},x_{1},0,...,0)\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
. Since
x_{i}=0
for
i\in\{2,3,...,k\}
, we have
x_{i}*x_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k
and

C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~\text{and }x_{0}*x_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k].

As
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property,
x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq{k-1}.
This implies
x_{0}*x_{1}=0.
Thus,
(C_{0},C_{1})
has the X-column-sufficiency property.

Case-II
: For
i\in[k-1]
, let
x_{i},x_{i+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

C_{i}x_{i}-C_{i+1}x_{i+1}=0\text{ and }x_{i}*x_{i+1}\leq 0.

Consider
{\bf v}:=(v_{0},...,v_{i},v_{i+1},...,v_{k})=(0,0,...,0,x_{i},-x_{i+1},0,...,0)\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
. Since
x_{i}*x_{i+1}\leq 0
,
(x_{i})_{r}
and
(-x_{i+1})_{r}
will have same sign whenever
(x_{i})_{r}*(x_{i+1})_{r}\neq 0
. Thus,
v_{s}*v_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq s<j\leq k
and
v_{0}*v_{s}\leq 0~\forall s\in[k]
. This gives
v_{s}*v_{s+1}=0=x_{s}*x_{s+1}
for all
0\leq s\leq{k-1}
. Hence,
(C_{i},C_{i+1})
has the X-column-sufficiency property for all
i\in[k-1]
.
∎

{\bf d}\in\Lambda^{(k-1)}_{n,++}
,
\text{\rm EHLCP}({\bf C},{\bf d},q)
has a unique solution.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
and
{\bf d}=(d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{k-1})\in\Lambda^{(k-1)}_{n,++}
. As
C_{0}
is a
M
matrix and
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
, we have
C_{0}^{-1}q>0
. Let
{\bf{x}}=(C_{0}^{-1}q,0,...,0).
By observation
{\bf{x}}=(C_{0}^{-1}q,0,...,0)\in\text{SOL}({\bf C},{\bf d},q).
We clam that
\text{SOL}({\bf C},{\bf d},q)=\{(C_{0}^{-1}q,0,...,0)\}.

Suppose
{\bf y}=(y_{0},y_{1},...,y_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
is an another solution to EHLCP(
{\bf C},{\bf d},q
). Then,

\displaystyle C_{0}y_{0}=q+\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}y_{i},~y_{0}\wedge y_{1}=0,~(d_{j}-y_{j})\wedge y_{j+1}=0~\forall j\in[k-1].

(12)

From the Lemma
2.2
, we have

C_{0}y_{0}=q+\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}y_{i}~\text{and}~y_{0}\wedge y_{j}=0~\forall~j\in[k].

(13)

We let
{\bf{z}}:={\bf{y}}-{\bf{x}}
, then
{\bf{z}}=(y_{0}-C_{0}^{-1}q,y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{k})
. As
y_{j}\geq 0~\forall j\in[k]
,
z_{i}*z_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k
. Also by a direct implication, from (
13
), we get

C_{0}(y_{0}-C_{0}^{-1}q)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}y_{i}

(14)

and

y_{j}\geq 0,~~(y_{0}-C_{0}^{-1}q)*y_{j}=y_{0}*y_{j}-C_{0}^{-1}q*y_{j}=-C_{0}^{-1}q*y_{j}\leq 0~\forall j\in[k].

As
\bf C
has the cS-
W
property, we have
z_{0}*z_{1}=0,
this implies

(y_{0}-C_{0}^{-1}q)*y_{1}=0\implies-C_{0}^{-1}q*y_{1}=0

As
C_{0}^{-1}q>0,y_{1}=0.
From complementarity property of solution
{\bf{y}}
, we have
(d_{j}-y_{j})\wedge y_{j+1}=0~\forall j\in[k-1].
As
y_{1}=0,(d_{1}-0)\wedge y_{2}=0\implies y_{2}=0.
Similarly,
y_{j}=0~\forall j\in[k].

As
C_{0}
is invertible, then from (
14
),
(y_{0}-C_{0}^{-1}q)=0.
This gives
{\bf z}={\bf 0}.
Hence
{\bf y}={\bf{x}}.
We have our conclusion.
∎

4
Some relation between Column
W
and cS-
W
properties

In LCP
[
4
]
, we have a significant implication that follows:

{\rm P~matrix\iff Column\text{-}Sufficient~matrix~+~Nondegenerate~matrix}.

It is interesting to observe a similar type of result for a set of matrices in the context of the EHLCP. To proceed further, we first prove a column representative result related to the column ND-
W
property.

For a nondegenerate matrix, there is a nonzero principal minor result, which states that a matrix is nondegenerate if and only if all the principal minors of the given matrix are nonzero, see
[
4
]
. Also, for a real matrix
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, all the principal minors are simply the determinants of the column representative matrices of
(I,C)
. Motivated by this, we prove a similar result for the column ND-
W
property.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}.
Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

{\bf C}
has the column ND-
W
property.

(ii)

The determinant of every column representative matrix of
{\bf C}
is nonzero.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii):
Let
{\bf C}
has the column ND-
W
property. Assume the contrary. Suppose there exists a column representative matrix
M
of
{\bf C}
such that
\text{det}(M)=0
. According to Remark
1
, there exists a set of
n^{th}
order diagonal matrices
I_{0},I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
with entries of
0
or
1
only, satisfying
I_{i}I_{j}=0
for all
0\leq i<j\leq k
and
\text{diag}(I_{0}+I_{1}+\cdots+I_{k})\neq 0
which satisfies

M=C_{0}I_{0}+C_{1}I_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}I_{k}.

Since
\text{det}(M)=0
, there exists a nonzero vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(C_{0}I_{0}+C_{1}I_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}I_{k})y=0.

Define
x_{0}=-I_{0}y
and
x_{i}=I_{i}y
for all
i\in[k]
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~x_{i}*x_{j}=0~\forall 0\leq i<j\leq k.\end{array}

By the column ND-
W
property of
{\bf C},~{\bf{x}}=(-I_{0}y,I_{1}y,...,I_{k}y)={\bf 0}.
As
\mathop{\rm diag}(I_{0}+I_{1}+\cdots+I_{k})\neq 0
and
I_{i}I_{j}=0~\forall 0\leq i<j\leq k
, we get that
y=0.
Thus, we get a contradiction.

(ii)
\implies
(i): Suppose there exists a nonzero vector
{\bf{x}}=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r}C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~x_{i}*x_{j}=0~\forall 0\leq i<j\leq k.\end{array}

(15)

Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a vector whose
j^{th}
component is given as

v_{j}=\begin{cases}-(x_{0})_{j}&\text{ if }~(x_{0})_{j}\neq 0\\
(x_{i})_{j}&~\text{ if }(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}\neq 0~\text{for some}~i\in[k]\\
0&\text{ if }~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}=0~\text{for all}~i\in[k]\end{cases},

and let
\{E_{i}\}^{k}_{i=1}
be the set of
n^{th}
order diagonal matrices, defined by

(E_{0})_{jj}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }(x_{0})_{j}\neq 0\\
0&\text{ if }~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}\neq 0~\text{for some}~i\in[k]\\
1&\text{ if }~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}=0~\text{for all}~i\in[k]\end{cases},

and for
i\in[k],

(E_{i})_{jj}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }(x_{i})_{j}=0\\
1&\text{ if }(x_{i})_{j}\neq 0\end{cases}.

By an easy verification,
v\neq 0
,
x_{0}=-E_{0}v
and
x_{i}=E_{i}v~\forall i\in[k].
Also, from Remark
1
,
C_{0}E_{0}+C_{1}E_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}E_{k}
is a column representative matrix of
{\bf C}
. Thus, from (
15
), we have

(C_{0}E_{0}+C_{1}E_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+C_{k}E_{k})v=0.

This implies that det
(C_{0}E_{0}+C_{1}E_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+C_{k}E_{k})=0.
This gives us a contradiction. Hence
{\bf C}
has the column ND-
W
property. ∎

Now, we present our main result ot this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

{\bf C}
has the column
W
-property.

(ii)

{\bf C}
have the cS-
W
property and column ND-
W
property.

(iii)

{\bf C}
have the column
W_{0}
-property and column ND-
W
property.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii): Let
{\bf C}
has the column
W
-property. Then, from Definition
3
and Theorem
4.1
,
{\bf C}
also has the column ND-
W
property. To show
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property, we proceed with a contrary argument. Suppose there exists
{\bf{x}}=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
and
s\in\{0,1,2,...,k-1\}
satisfies

C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~x_{i}*x_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k,~x_{0}*x_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k]\text{ and }x_{s}*x_{s+1}\neq 0.

(16)

We now construct a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose
j^{\rm{th}}
component is given by

y_{j}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}>0\\
1&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}<0\\
1&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}>0~\text{for some}~i\in[k]\\
-1&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}<0~\text{for some}~i\in[k]\\
0&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}=0~\text{for all}~i\in[k]\end{cases}.

Since
x_{s}*x_{s+1}\neq 0
,
{{y}}
is a nonzero vector. Next, we construct a set of nonnegative diagonal matrices
D_{0},D_{1},...,D_{k}
which are defined by

(D_{0})_{jj}=\begin{cases}|(x_{0})_{j}|&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}\neq 0\\
0&\text{if}(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}\neq 0~\text{for some}~i\in[k]\\
1&\text{if}~(x_{0})_{j}=0~\text{and}~(x_{i})_{j}=0~\text{for all}~i\in[k],\end{cases}

and for
i\in[k]
,

(D_{i})_{jj}=|(x_{i})_{j}|.

By an easy verification we can see that
\text{diag}(D_{0}+D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k})>0
with

x_{0}=-D_{0}y~\text{and}~x_{i}=D_{i}y~\forall i\in[k].

(17)

Substituting (
17
) in
C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i}
, we get

C_{0}(-D_{0}y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}D_{i}(y)\Rightarrow\big(C_{0}D_{0}+C_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}D_{k}\big)y=0.

This implies that det
(C_{0}D_{0}+C_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}D_{k}\big)=0
. Since
x\neq 0
,
{\bf C}
does not have the column
W
-property from Theorem
3
. Thus, we get a contradiction. Therefore,
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property.

(ii)
\implies
(iii): Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}
. It is enough to prove
{\bf C}
has the column
W_{0}
-property. Let us assume a set of positive diagonal matrices
\{D_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}
of order
n
and a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

({C_{0}D_{0}+C_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}D_{k}})x=0.

(18)

Let
x_{0}=-D_{0}x\text{ and }x_{i}=D_{i}x~\forall 1\leq i\leq k,
Then, substituting these into (
18
), we get

C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i}.

Also,

x_{i}*x_{j}=D_{i}x*(D_{j}x)=D_{i}D_{j}x^{2}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k,

and

x_{0}*x_{i}=-D_{0}x*D_{i}x=-D_{0}D_{i}x^{2}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k].

As
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property, from the above,
x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0~\forall 0\leq i\leq k-1
. This implies that
D_{i}x*D_{i+1}x=0.
Since
D_{i}
is a positive diagonal matrix for
0\leq i\leq k
,
x=0.
Therefore,
{\bf C}
has the column
W_{0}
-property.

(iii)
\implies
(i): Suppose
{\bf C}
have the column
W_{0}
and column ND-
W
properties. From Definition
3
, the column
W_{0}
-property ensures that the determinants of all the column representative matrices are nonnegative (nonpositive). Additionally, from Theorem
4.1
, the column ND-
W
property implies that the determinants of all the column representative matrices are nonzero. Therefore, the determinants of all the column representative matrices are positive (negative). Hence,
{\bf C}
has the column
W
-property.
∎

In LCP
[
4
]
, for a real matrix, we have the following implication:

P\subseteq\text{Column~Sufficient}\subseteq P_{0}.

(19)

From Theorem
4.2
, we have a direct result as an extension of implication (
19
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}
. Consider the following statements:

(i)

{\bf C}
has the column
W
-property

(ii)

{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property.

(iii)

{\bf C}
has the column
W_{0}
-property

Then,
\rm(i)\implies\rm(ii)\implies\rm(iii).

In above theorem, implication (ii)
\implies
(i) will not hold. To show this we present the following example.

Example 4.4
.

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},C_{2})\in\Lambda^{(3)}_{2\times 2}
where

C_{0}=C_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix},~C_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}.

First we show that
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property. Let
{\bf{x}}=(x,y,z)\in\Lambda^{(3)}_{2}
such that

C_{0}x=C_{1}y+C_{2}z,~x*y\leq 0,x*z\leq 0\text{ and }y*z\geq 0,

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2}),y=(y_{1},y_{2})
and
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

From this, we have
x_{i}y_{i}\leq 0,x_{i}z_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in\{1,2\}
and

\displaystyle x_{1}=y_{2}+z_{1},~~x_{2}=-y_{1}+z_{2},

(20)

Now multiplying both
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
with
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
respectively, we get

\displaystyle x_{1}y_{1}=y_{1}y_{2}+z_{1}y_{1}\leq 0,~~x_{2}y_{2}=-y_{1}y_{2}+z_{2}y_{2}\leq 0

(21)

As
x_{i}y_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in\{1,2\}
, we have
x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}\leq 0
. This implies

y_{1}y_{2}+z_{1}y_{1}-y_{1}y_{2}+z_{2}y_{2}\leq 0\implies y_{1}z_{1}+y_{2}z_{2}\leq 0.

Since
y*z\geq 0
,
y_{1}z_{1}
and
y_{2}z_{2}
will be non negative. Therefore,

y_{1}z_{1}+y_{2}z_{2}=0\implies y_{i}z_{i}=0~\forall i\in\{1,2\}.

This gives
x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}=0\implies x_{1}y_{1}=x_{2}y_{2}=0
. Thus
x_{i}y_{i}=y_{i}z_{i}=0
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
This ensures that
{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property.

Now we take a column representative matrix of
{\bf C}
which is

R=[{(C_{0})}_{.1},{(C_{2})}_{.2}]=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\\
\end{bmatrix}.

By observation, det
(R)
=0. Hence
{\bf C}
does not have the column
W
-property. Hence the cS-
W
property does not imply column
W
-property.

Remark 2
.

Similar to previous example, the column
W_{0}
-property does not imply the cS-
W
property. By taking
k=3
and
C_{0}=I,C_{1}=C_{2}=0
, we can see that converse of implication (ii)
\implies
(iii) in Theorem 4.2 is not true.

For a
Z
-matrix [A matrix with off-diagonal entries are nonpositive], the column-sufficient matrix property is equivalent to the column-sufficient matrix property on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, see
[
4
]
. For a set of
Z
-matrices, we prove a similar result for the cS-
W
property. First, we define the cone cS-
W
property. Then, we establish an equivalence between the cS-
W
property and the cone cS-
W
property for
Z
-matrices.

Definition 6
.

We say
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n\times n}
has cone column sufficient-W (cone cS-
W
) property if

\big[C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~x_{i}\geq 0~\text{and}~x_{0}*x_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k]~\big]\Rightarrow x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0~\forall 0\leq i\leq k-1.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},C_{1},...,C_{k})\in\Lambda_{n\times n}^{(k+1)}
such that
C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}
be a
Z
-matrix for all
i\in[k]
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

{\bf C}
has the cS-
W
property.

(ii)

{\bf C}
has the cone cS-
W
property.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii): It follows from Definitions
5
and
6
.

(ii)
\implies
(i): Assume
{\bf C}
has the cone cS-
W
property. Let
{\bf{x}}=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{k})\in\Lambda^{(k+1)}_{n}
such that

x_{i}*x_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k~\text{ and }C_{0}x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}x_{i},~x_{0}*x_{i}\leq 0~\forall i\in[k].

(22)

As
C_{0}
is invertible, from
22
, we have

x_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}x_{i}.

(23)

Consider
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

y_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}|x_{i}|.

(24)

For some coordinate
r\in[n]

\displaystyle(y_{0}*{(|x_{j}|)})_{r}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}|x_{i}|)_{r}{(|x_{j}|)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(C_{0}^{-1}C_{i})_{rr}(|x_{i}|)_{r}{(|x_{j}|)}_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{r\neq s,s=1}^{n}(C_{0}^{-1}C_{i})_{rs}(|x_{i}|)_{s}{(|x_{j}|)}_{r}.

As
C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}
is a
Z
matrix, then the off diagonal entries of
C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}
will be nonpositive. Since
x_{i}*x_{j}\geq 0~\forall 1\leq i,j\leq k
, we have

\begin{aligned} (y_{0}*{(|x_{j}|)})_{r}\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{k}(C_{0}^{-1}C_{i})_{rr}{(x_{i})}_{r}{(x_{j})}_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{r\neq s,s=1}^{n}(C_{0}^{-1}C_{i})_{rs}{(x_{i})}_{s}{(x_{j})}_{r}\\
=&(\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{0}^{-1}C_{i}x_{i})_{r}{(x_{j})}_{r}\\
\end{aligned}.

From (
23
), we get that

y_{0}*{(|x_{j}|)}\leq x_{0}*x_{j}\leq 0.

As
{\bf C}
has the cone cS-
W
property, then for
{\bf{y}}=(y_{0},y_{1},...,y_{k})=(y_{0},|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,,,|x_{k}|),
we have

y_{j}*y_{j+1}=0,0\leq j\leq k-1.

For
j=0,

y_{0}*|x_{1}|=0
. Then from (
24
),

0=y_{0}*|x_{1}|\leq x_{0}*x_{1}\leq 0\implies x_{0}*x_{1}=0.

For
j\in[k-1],

|x_{i}|*|x_{i+1}|=0\implies x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0.

Therefore
x_{i}*x_{i+1}=0,0\leq i\leq k-1.
Hence
C
has the cS-
W
-property.
∎

Let

.

5
Conclusion

In this article, we introduce the column sufficient-
W
property and prove a convexity result for the solution set of the EHLCP. We also establish a uniqueness result for

in the presence of the cS-
W

W
(
W_{0}
) property and the cS-
W
property with the help of the column ND-
W
property. Lastly, we present a result for cS-
W
property on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

Expression analysis of hsp70 and hsp90 chaperone-encoding genes in Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) in response to osmotic stress
Authors: Abdul Rahem Faqih, Seto Sugianto Prabowo Rahardjo, Ellana Sanoesi, Septi Anitasari, Diana Aisyah, Cinca Hayyu Putri Satiti, Asad Aulia Iqomuddin, Rahayu Salsabila, Wahyu Surya Prasdiawan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Braja 001 Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) is a locally developed hybrid strain intended for brackish-water aquaculture in Indonesia. This study evaluated the molecular stress response using hsp70 and hsp90 genes, together with endocrine, hematological, immune, and respiratory indicators, to determine the adaptive capacity of Braja 001 tilapia under different salinity levels. Juveniles were reared for 28 days at four salinity treatments (0, 7, 14, and 21 ppt) in a completely randomized design with three replicates. The expression of hsp70 and hsp90 was quantified by qRT-PCR, while serum cortisol, hematocrit, leukocyte counts, and operculum beat frequency were measured as physiological stress markers. Increasing salinity significantly elevated cortisol concentration, hematocrit, leukocyte counts, and operculum beat frequency, while hsp70 and hsp90 expression showed increasing trends without significant differences. At 0 ppt, all parameters remained at basal levels, reflecting stable freshwater conditions. At 7 ppt, a slight increase in hsp70 expression with minimal physiological changes suggested mild stress that could be effectively compensated. At 14 ppt, higher cortisol levels accompanied by increased hsp70 and hsp90 expression indicated a stronger physiological response. In contrast, exposure to 21 ppt resulted in consistently elevated stress indicators, suggesting increased physiological demands. Based on the integrated responses, salinity levels of 7–14 ppt may still be tolerated under the present conditions, whereas higher salinity (21 ppt) is associated with increased physiological stress and should be applied with caution in aquaculture systems.

Introduction

Braja 001 Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) is a locally developed hybrid strain derived from crosses between the Kekar and Jatimbulan strains and was specifically designed to support tilapia aquaculture in Indonesian brackish-water environments. The Kekar strain is recognized for its rapid growth performance, whereas the Jatimbulan strain exhibits superior environmental adaptability and tolerance to suboptimal culture conditions. The hybridization of these two strains was intended to combine favorable growth characteristics with enhanced stress tolerance, thereby producing a genetically improved strain suitable for culture under variable environmental conditions. Molecular identification based on the mitochondrial COI gene revealed that Braja 001 exhibits 100% genetic similarity with O. niloticus and clusters closely with its parental strains as well as global populations, thereby confirming its genetic integrity and suitability as a domesticated strain for culture in variable-salinity waters.1 In addition to genetic confirmation, the development of Braja 001 is conceptually supported by heterosis (hybrid vigor), whereby hybrid offspring may exhibit enhanced physiological performance, metabolic efficiency, and environmental resilience compared with their parental strains, particularly under fluctuating environmental conditions.

Salinity is a critical environmental factor influencing osmoregulation, growth, and survival of Nile tilapia. In Indonesian brackish-water aquaculture systems, pond salinity commonly ranges from approximately 5 to 30 ppt depending on location, season, and water exchange dynamics, with many traditional and semi intensive ponds fluctuating within 10–25 ppt due to tidal intrusion and evaporation.2 Although Nile tilapia is recognized as a euryhaline species, optimal performance is generally achieved at moderate salinity levels (approximately 5–15 ppt), whereas prolonged exposure to elevated salinity can induce osmotic stress, disrupt ionic balance, and compromise physiological efficiency (.3 Numerous underutilized aquaculture ponds in Indonesia offer high potential for the cultivation of Nile tilapia in saline environments, thereby providing a strategic opportunity to expand sustainable brackishwater aquaculture systems.2 Recent studies have demonstrated that salinity stress triggers coordinated physiological and molecular responses in tilapia, including endocrine activation, respiratory adjustment, oxidative stress regulation, and transcriptional modulation of stress-related genes3, 4, 5

Prolonged osmotic stress forces fish to allocate substantial metabolic energy toward maintaining ionic and osmotic balance, thereby reducing the energy available for growth and immune defense.3 Salinity is a major abiotic stressor that disrupts osmotic and ionic balance in freshwater fish, forcing a physiological shift from passive freshwater regulation to an energetically expensive hypo-osmoregulatory strategy. Consequently, understanding the physiological and molecular mechanisms underlying salinity tolerance is essential for optimizing management strategies and ensuring the long-term sustainability of brackish-water tilapia culture.6 Such physiological adjustments involve endocrine activation, altered hematological regulation, and changes in respiratory performance, collectively reflecting the adaptive capacity of fish to environmental stressors.

The identification of molecular indicators associated with stress resilience has become an important approach in modern aquaculture. Genes involved in cellular protection and protein quality control are increasingly used as biomarkers to assess the adaptive capacity of cultured stocks under challenging environmental conditions. Such biomarkers provide an early warning system that can detect sub-lethal stress before overt physiological or production losses become evident (Jeyachandran.et al., 2023). At the molecular level, the capacity of tilapia to cope with salinity stress is closely associated with the activation of heat shock proteins, particularly hsp70 and hsp90. These proteins function as molecular chaperones that stabilize unfolded or damaged proteins, prevent aggregation, and maintain cellular homeostasis under adverse environmental conditions. Elevated expression of hsp70 and hsp90 has been widely recognized as a sensitive biomarker of osmotic stress and adaptive capacity in teleost fishes.7 Nevertheless, despite being specifically developed for brackish-water aquaculture, the transcriptional responses of hsp70 and hsp90 in Braja 001 Nile tilapia under different salinity regimes remain poorly documented. Therefore, the present study aimed to characterize the expression dynamics of hsp70 and hsp90 genes in Braja 001 Nile tilapia exposed to varying salinity levels in order to support the sustainable development of brackish-water tilapia farming systems in Indonesia. To address this knowledge gap, the present study was designed based on the following hypotheses. Salinity exposure is expected to induce measurable changes in the expression levels of hsp 70 and hsp 90 genes in Braja 001 Nile tilapia, reflecting molecular responses to osmotic stress. Furthermore, variations in hsp70 and hsp90 expression are anticipated to be associated with physiological stress indicators, such as cortisol levels and hematological parameters, indicating coordinated molecular–physiological responses. In addition, intermediate salinity levels (7–14 ppt) are presumed to be associated with relatively stable molecular and physiological responses compared to more extreme salinity conditions, suggesting a potential adaptive range for this strain.

Materials and methods

Time and location

This study was conducted from August to November 2025 at the Freshwater Aquaculture Laboratory, Sumberpasir, the Hydrobiology Laboratory of the Faculty of Fisheries and Marine Science, the Central Biomedical Laboratory of the Faculty of Medicine, and the Integrated Research Laboratory, Universitas Brawijaya, Indonesia

Experimental design

A completely randomized design (CRD) was applied with four salinity treatments (0, 7, 14, and 21 ppt), each in triplicate. Each experimental unit consisted of a 40-L aquarium stocked with 10 Braja 001 Nile tilapia juveniles (9–12 cm total length). Prior to the experiment, fish were acclimated for 5 days, and only healthy individuals showing active swimming behavior and normal feeding responses were selected. Fish were reared using a recirculating aquaculture system and fed commercial pellets twice daily at 3% of body weight. The experiment was conducted for 28 days, with sampling performed

Parameters

Expression of hsp 70 and hsp90 Genes

Gene expression of hsp70 and hsp90 was analyzed by qRT-PCR. Total RNA was extracted from 25 mg liver tissue using a Geneaid kit and reverse-transcribed into cDNA after genomic DNA removal. RNA quantity and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA) (A260/A280 ratio of 1.8–2.0), and RNA integrity was confirmed with RIN values >7 prior to downstream analysis. The synthesized cDNA was diluted to a working concentration of approximately 50 ng/μL for qPCR analysis. Quantitative PCR was performed using SensiFAST™ SYBR® No-ROX in a 20 μL reaction volume consisting of 10 μL master mix, 0.8 μL of each primer (10 μM), 2 μL cDNA template, and nuclease-free water. The amplification was carried out under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, annealing at 60 °C for 20 s, and extension at 72 °C for 20 s. A melt curve analysis (65–95 °C) was performed to verify amplification specificity. The β-actin gene was used as the reference (housekeeping) gene for normalization.8 The primer sequences for hsp90 were forward 5’-TTTGCTGAACTGGGGTGAGGA-3′ and reverse 5’-AGAGACCAGGGTCTTGCCAT-3′, producing a 340 bp amplicon (Chakrabarty et al., 2025). The primers for Hsp70 were forward 5’-CTCCACCCGAATCCCCAAAA-3′ and reverse 5’-TCGATACCCAGGGACAGAGG-3′, producing a 195 bp amplicon (accession no. XM_01935557.1). The β-actin primers were forward 5’-TGGCAATGAGAGGTTCCG-3′ and reverse 5’-TGCTGTTGTAGGTGGTTTCG-3′, producing a 136 bp amplicon (9; accession no. XM_00345549.2). Primer sequences for hsp70, hsp90, and β-actin were designed based on El-Leithy et al.,9 and Chakrabarty et al., (2025) Primer efficiency for all genes was evaluated using standard curves generated from serial dilutions of cDNA, with amplification efficiencies ranging between 90% and 110% and correlation coefficients (R2) >0.99.. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method.10ΔΔCt=Cttarget−Ctβ−actinsample−Cttarget−Ctβ−actincontrolR=2−ΔΔCt.

. Where ΔCt represents the difference between target gene Ct (hsp70 or hsp90) and reference gene Ct (β-actin), while ΔΔCt represents the difference between treated and control ΔCt values. Relative expression values were normalized to the freshwater control (0 ppt).

Cortisol hormone

Serum cortisol concentration was determined using an ELISA kit. A total of 40 μL fish serum was mixed with 10 μL antibody, followed by the addition B of 50 μL streptavidin-HRP and incubation at 37 °C for 60 min. After washing five times, substrate solutions A and B (each 50 μL) were added and incubated for 30 min. The reaction was stopped using 50 μL stop solution, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a Diatek DR-200 BCE microplate reader.11

Hematocrit

Hematocrit values were measured using microhematocrit capillary tubes centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 5 min. The percentage of packed cell volume was calculated using:

Hematocrit%=ab×100where a is the length of packed erythrocytes and b is the total blood column length.12

Leukocyte count

Total leukocytes were determined using a hemocytometer. Blood was diluted with Turk's solution (1:20), homogenized, and counted under 400× magnification. Leukocytes were counted in four large squares and calculated as: Leukocytes=n×500cellsmm−3 where n is the number of leukocytes counted.12

Operculum beat frequency

Operculum movements were counted for 1 min using a hand counter with three replicates for each treatment to evaluate respiratory stress.13

Survival rate (SR)

Survival rate (SR) was determined at the end of the experimental period to evaluate fish survival under different salinity treatments. The number of surviving fish in each experimental unit was recorded and expressed as a percentage of the initial number of fish stocked. Survival rate was calculated using the following formula:SR%=NtN0×100where Ntis the number of fish that survived at the end of the experiment, and N0is the initial number of fish stocked at the beginning of the experiment. Observations of mortality were conducted daily, and dead fish were removed immediately to maintain water quality.

Specific growth rate (SGR)

Specific growth rate (SGR) was calculated to evaluate the growth performance of Nile tilapia under different salinity treatments. Fish were weighed at the beginning and at the end of the experimental period. The SGR was calculated using the following formula:SGR%day−1=lnWt−lnW0t×100where Wtis the final mean body weight (g), W0is the initial mean body weight (g), and tis the duration of the experiment (days). All fish were weighed collectively per experimental unit to minimize handling stress, and measurements were conducted using a digital balance with appropriate precision.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) at a significance level of P < 0.05. Data normality and homogeneity of variance were tested using the Shapiro–Wilk test and Levene's test, respectively. When significant differences were detected, Tukey's HSD post hoc test were performed using SPSS version 26.0

Ethical approval

This study was conducted in accordance with ethical standards for animal experimentation and was granted ethical clearance by the Research and Innovation Ethics Committee of Universitas Brawijaya (Approval No. 267/EC/KEPK/09/2025).

At 21 ppt, leukocyte levels reached their highest values and did not differ significantly from those at 14 ppt, indicating a sustained physiological response under higher salinity conditions. Elevated salinity may be associated with physiological disturbances, including changes in osmotic balance and stress responses.. Sustained leukocytosis under such conditions is energetically costly and may reduce immune efficiency over time, ultimately compromising growth and overall physiological performance. Hsp70 not only functions as a molecular chaperone to maintain protein homeostasis during stress, but is also involved in interactions with the immune system, including cytokine activation and modulation of both innate and adaptive immune responses. This indicates that hsp70 can influence leukocytes and other immune factors as part of a comprehensive response to environmental stress.28

Overall, the leukocyte profile is generally consistent with the molecular (hsp70 and hsp90) and hematological findings of this study, suggesting that salinity levels between 7 and 14 ppt may still be tolerated of Braja 001 Nile tilapia. Results operculum beat frequency

The significant increase in operculum beat frequency with rising salinity indicates a physiological response to salinity exposure. Operculum beat frequency increased progressively from 93 ± 4.96 beats min−1 at 0 ppt (notation a) to 96 ± 2.87 beats min−1 at 7 ppt (notation a), followed by a significant increase at 14 ppt (145 ± 14.94 beats min−1; notation b) and reaching the highest value at 21 ppt (154 ± 14.02 beats min−1; notation c) (p < 0.05).

In freshwater conditions (0 ppt), Nile tilapia maintain ion and water balance with relatively low energetic cost, resulting in stable respiratory activity. The minimal increase observed at 7 ppt suggests that fish were able to maintain relatively stable physiological conditions under low salinity exposure. At higher salinity levels, particularly 14 ppt, increased salinity is known to affect osmotic balance and may elevate metabolic demand associated with osmoregulation. Consequently, oxygen demand increases sharply, necessitating a substantial rise in ventilation rate, as reflected by the significant increase in operculum beat frequency at this salinity.

The further elevation at 21 ppt, suggests a continued physiological response to higher salinity levels. This increase in ventilation rate may reflect higher energetic demands required to maintain physiological balance under elevated salinity.22

The observed respiratory patterns may be associatedto molecular and physiological stress indicators in this study. Elevated operculum beat frequency at higher salinity corresponds with increased cortisol levels, increasing trends of hsp70 and hsp90, and concurrent changes in hematological parameters. Collectively, these responses suggest that higher salinity levels impose greater physiological and metabolic demands on the fish . These findings indicate that although fish can tolerate salinity up to 21 ppt, increasing salinity may lead to sublethal stress, as reflected by elevated respiratory activity and associated physiological responses.

Survival rate (SR)

Survival rate (SR) of Braja 001 Nile tilapia remained relatively high across all salinity treatments (Fig. 7), with 100% survival at 0 and 7 ppt, followed by slight decreases at 14 ppt (93.33 ± 5.77%) and 21 ppt (90 ± 10%). Although survival tended to decline with increasing salinity, the reduction was not drastic, indicating that fish were able to tolerate salinity up to 21 ppt without significant mortality. This pattern contrasts with the significant increase in cortisol levels (p < 0.05), which indicates that fish experienced physiological stress as salinity increased. In addition, hsp70 and hsp90 expression showed increasing trends, although not statistically significant (p > 0.05). Together, these results suggest that the fish were undergoing stress at the molecular and endocrine levels, even though this stress was not severe enough to cause mortality.

The relatively stable survival rate despite elevated cortisol and increased hsp expression indicates that Braja 001 Nile tilapia can maintain short-term survival under salinity stress through physiological and molecular adjustments. However, the decline in SR at higher salinity levels may reflect cumulative sublethal stress effects, which are better captured by sensitive biomarkers such as cortisol and Hsp expression rather than survival alone.29

Specific growth rate (SGR)

Specific growth rate (SGR) of Braja 001 Nile tilapia showed a decreasing trend with increasing salinity (Fig. 8), with the highest values observed at 7 ppt (1.49 ± 0.07% day−1) and 0 ppt (1.45 ± 0.05% day−1), followed by lower values at 14 ppt (1.25 ± 0.14% day−1) and 21 ppt (1.08 ± 0.05% day−1). This decline in growth performance at higher salinity levels suggests that increasing salinity may impose physiological constraints that reduce energy allocation for growth. In contrast to survival rate, which remained relatively high, SGR appeared to be more sensitive to salinity changes.

The reduction in SGR at elevated salinity is consistent with the observed increase in cortisol levels (p < 0.05), indicating enhanced metabolic demand associated with stress adaptation. Elevated cortisol is known to redirect energy toward osmoregulation and maintenance processes, thereby limiting the energy available for somatic growth. In addition, although hsp70 and hsp90 expression did not differ significantly (p > 0.05), their increasing trends suggest activation of molecular stress responses. The combined increase in cortisol and Hsp expression may reflect higher energetic costs required to maintain cellular homeostasis under salinity stress, which can contribute to reduced growth performance. Overall, these findings indicate that while Braja 001 Nile tilapia can survive under higher salinity conditions, growth performance is negatively affected, suggesting that SGR is a more sensitive indicator of sublethal stress compared to survival rate.30

Conclusion

Increasing salinity was associated with elevated cortisol levels, as well as increases in hsp70 and hsp90 expression (non-significant), hematocrit values, leukocyte counts, and operculum beat frequency in Braja 001 Nile tilapia, indicating a progressive physiological response to osmotic stress from the molecular to the organismal level. Based on the integrated molecular, endocrine, hematological, immune, and respiratory responses, salinity levels up to 14 ppt may still be tolerated under the present experimental conditions, with 7 ppt representing a mild stress condition and 14 ppt indicating a higher but manageable physiological response. In contrast, exposure to 21 ppt resulted in consistently elevated stress indicators, suggesting increased physiological demands that may affect growth performance and overall condition; therefore, higher salinity levels should be applied with caution in aquaculture practices, and further studies are required to evaluate their long-term effects.

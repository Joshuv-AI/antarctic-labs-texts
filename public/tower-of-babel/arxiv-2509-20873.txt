Introduction

Let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even and
N
squarefree. Let
f
denote a Hecke newform of weight
k
with respect to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\;N\mid c\right\}
. It is known that
f
admits a Fourier expansion of the form

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\lambda_{f}(n)q^{n},\qquad q=e^{2\pi iz}.

Here,

\lambda_{f}(1)=1
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{T_{n}(f(z))}{n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}=\lambda_{f}(n)f(z),

where
T_{n}
denotes the
n
-th Hecke operator acting on the space
S_{k}(N)
of cusp forms of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

The standard
L
-function
L(s,f)
corresponding to
f
is defined as

L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

which converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.
L(s,f)
has an Euler product representation of the form

L(s,f)=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,

where
\chi_{0}(p)=1
if
p\nmid N
and
\chi_{0}(p)=0
if
p\mid N
. It is well-known that
L(s,f)
can be analytically continued to an entire function on the complex plane, and
L(s,f)\neq 0
when
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
. This leads to the study of zero-free regions of
L(s,f)
in the vertical strip
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1
.

In the case of the Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet
L
-functions, a delineation of explicit zero-free regions combined with explicit zero density estimates leads to vital applications such as effective error terms in the prime number theorem, its analogue for primes in arithmetic progressions and the average orders of several arithmetic functions. The last two decades have witnessed a surge of activity in this direction, most of which aimed at sharpening the results of the 60-year old paper of Rosser and Schoenfeld
[
RS62
]
. Among the many recent contributions to these efforts, we mention
[
For02
]
,
[
Kad05
]
,
[
Kad12
]
,
[
Tru14
]
,
[
Ram16
]
,
[
Pla16
]
,
[
Kad18
]
,
[
KNW19
]
,
[
BKL+21
]
,
[
PT21
]
,
[
BMOR21
]
,
[
Kha24
]
,
[
Bel24
]
, and
[
DGL+25
]
.

In general, explicit determination of zero-free regions of
L
-functions associated with an arithmetic or algebraic structure helps us better understand the nature and properties of this structure. For example, the zero-free regions for
L(s,f)
and the symmetric power
L
-functions
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
for
m\geq 2
(see Section
2.2
for the definition) yield effective estimates for the sums

\sum_{p\leq x}\lambda_{f}(p^{m})
.
Analogous to the fundamental ideas in the proof of the prime number theorem, wider zero-free regions for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
lead to sharper estimates for these sums.
These estimates further lead to effective error terms in the Sato-Tate distribution law for the Hecke eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{f}(p)\}_{p\to\infty}\subset[-2,2]
of a fixed non-CM Hecke newform
f
(see, for example,
[
Mur85
]
,
[
RT17
]
,
[
Tho21
]
and
[
HIJTS22
]
).

The first zero-free region result for modular forms beyond the classical context of Dirichlet
L
-functions and their extensions to number fields is due to Moreno
[
Mor72
]
who established a zero-free region
for the
L
-function associated with the Ramanujan delta function
\Delta(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)q^{n}\in S_{12}(1)
. This zero-free region took the form

(1)

\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1-\frac{1}{c\log(\lvert\operatorname{Im}(s)\rvert+2)},

for some constant
c>0
. Using (
1
), Moreno derived the estimate

\sum_{p\leq x}\tau(p)p^{-\frac{11}{2}}\log p=\operatorname{O}\left(xe^{-A(\log x)^{1/2}}\right),

where
A
is a suitably chosen positive constant, thereby improving Rankin’s estimates in
[
Ran39
]
and
[
Ran40
]
).

A generalization of Moreno’s zero-free region to all newforms of level
N
and weight
k\geq 1
is given in
[
IK04
, Theorem 5.39]
as follows: there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that
L(s,f)
does not have any zeros in the region

\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1-\frac{1}{c\log(N(\lvert\operatorname{Im}(s)\rvert+k+3))},

except possibly one simple real zero, called the exceptional zero, in which case
f
is self-dual. We know now that this exceptional zero can be provably eliminated thanks to the work of Hoffstein and Ramakrishnan
[
HR95
]
on the non-existence of Siegel zeros for
L
-functions of cusp forms on
\mathrm{GL}(2)
.

For a newform
f
of squarefree level
N
and weight
k
, Thorner
[
Tho21
]
derived an unconditional estimate for
\sum_{p\leq x}\lambda_{f}(p^{m})\log p
. An essential ingredient in Thorner’s work is the formulation of zero-free regions for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
. Thorner shows that there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
does not have any zeros in the region

\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1-\frac{1}{c\log(\mathfrak{q}(\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)(3+\lvert\operatorname{Im}(s)\rvert))},

where
\mathfrak{q}(\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
denotes the analytic conductor of
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
.

The “state-of-the-art” result in this direction, which also provides explicit constants in the determination of zero-free regions is the following:

Theorem

(
[
HIJTS22
]
)
.

Let
N
be a squarefree positive integer, and let
k
be an even positive integer. For a newform
f
of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, let
\beta_{m}+it
be a zero of
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
such that
\beta_{m}>1/2
. Then,

\beta_{m}\leq 1-\frac{1}{C_{m}\log(1.214(m+7)N(k-1)\sqrt{1+t^{2}})},

where

C_{m}=\frac{(m+7)^{2}}{2(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}.

In particular, if
m=1
, let
\beta+it
be a zero of
L(s,f)
such that
\beta>1/2
. Then,

\beta\leq 1-\frac{1}{C\log(9.712N(k-1)\sqrt{1+t^{2}})},

where

(2)

C=\frac{64}{2(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}<445.994.

The general analytic framework applied to obtain zero-free regions for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
issues from the property that for each
m\geq 1
,
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
can be analytically continued to the entire complex plane and has a functional equation. This follows from the fact that
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
is the
L
-function of a cuspidal automorphic representation on
\textrm{GL}_{m+1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, proved by Newton and Thorne
[
NT21a
,
NT21b
]
.

We note here that
[
HIJTS22
]
provides explicit constants in the zero-free region of
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
for any positive integer
N
. Let
q(\operatorname{Sym}^{n}f)
denote the arithmetic conductor of
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{n}f)
, and let
Q
be a positive constant such that
q(\operatorname{Sym}^{n}f)\leq Q^{n+1}
for all
1\leq n\leq 2m+1
. Then,
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)\neq 0
in the region

\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1-\frac{2(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}{(m+7)^{2}\log\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{e}}(k-1)Q(m+7)\sqrt{1+\lvert\operatorname{Im}(s)\rvert^{2}}\right)}.

If
N
is squarefree, then
q(\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=N^{m}
for each
m\geq 1
. However, such closed form expressions are not available for non-squarefree values of
N
(see
[
HIJTS22
, Remark 2.1]
).

The goal of the current article is to further improve the constant
C
given in (
2
) in the zero-free region for
L(s,f)
. We prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N
be a squarefree positive integer, and let
k
be an even positive integer. For a newform
f
of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, let
\beta+it
be a zero of
L(s,f)
such that
\beta>1/2
.
Then,

\beta\leq\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{16.7053\log(kN\lvert t\rvert)}&\text{ if }\lvert t\rvert\geq 1\\
1-\frac{1}{16.9309\log(kN)}&\text{ if }\frac{0.30992}{\log(kN)}<\lvert t\rvert<1\\
1-\frac{1}{16.7053\log(kN)}&\text{ if }\lvert t\rvert\leq\frac{0.30992}{\log(kN)}.\end{cases}

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we apply a classical technique due to Stečkin
[
Ste70
]
as employed by McCurley
[
McC84
]
in order to obtain explicit zero-free regions for Dirichlet
L
-functions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first instance in which this technique, in combination with other methods from explicit number theory, has been applied to the setting of
\mathrm{GL}(2)
automorphic
L
-functions. However, it is worth mentioning here that the methods used in
[
McC84
]
have been further refined, and its results have been improved by Kadiri in
[
Kad18
]
in the setting of Dirichlet
L
-functions.

2.
Preparation for the Proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
Isolating the zeros of
L
-functions

One of the earliest arguments to derive zero-free regions for the Riemann zeta function goes back to the classical proof of the fact that
\zeta(s)\neq 0
if
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1
. This proof depends on the trigonometric inequality

(3)

3+4\cos\theta+\cos 2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^{2}\geq 0,\qquad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

For
s=\sigma+it,\,\sigma>1
, we have

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\left(\cos(t\log n)-i\sin(t\log n)\right),

where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function.

By (
3
), we have

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Re}\left(-3\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma)-4\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+it)-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\left(3+4\cos(t\log n)+\cos(2t\log n)\right)\geq 0.\end{split}

The above trigonometric inequality motivates several techniques to identify a non-trivial zero of
\zeta(s)
and obtain wider zero-free regions in the critical strip. The classical template, due to Hadamard and de la Vallee Poussin, uses the Hadamard factorization for

\xi(s)=(s-1)\zeta(s)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)\pi^{-s/2}
to obtain (see, for example,
[
MV07
, Section 10.2]
)

(4)

\operatorname{Re}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)=-\frac{\log\pi}{2}+\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)-\sum_{\rho}\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}\right),

where
\sum_{\rho}
runs over all the zeros of
\zeta(s)
in the region
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1
. Observing that

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}\right)>0,

we isolate a single zero
\rho=\beta+it_{0}
with
\lvert t_{0}\rvert\geq 2
in the above sum, and derive the inequality,

\operatorname{Re}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)<-\frac{\log\pi}{2}+\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)-\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-(\beta+it_{0})}\right).

Choosing
s=\sigma+it_{0}
and applying standard bounds to the term
\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)
, we obtain

-\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+it_{0})\right)<A(\log\lvert t_{0}\rvert)-\frac{1}{\sigma-\beta},

for a positive constant
A
.
Inserting the above inequality into

0\leq\operatorname{Re}\left(-3\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma)-4\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+it_{0})-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+2it_{0})\right),

we get

\frac{4}{\sigma-\beta}<\frac{3}{\sigma-1}+B\log\lvert t_{0}\rvert,\qquad\text{for }\sigma>1,\,\lvert t_{0}\rvert\,\geq 2.

Choosing

\sigma=1+\frac{1}{(B+1)\log\lvert t_{0}\rvert},

one can show that there exists a positive constant
c
such that if
\zeta(\beta+it_{0})=0
and
\lvert t_{0}\rvert\geq 2
, then

\beta\leq 1-\frac{1}{c\log\lvert t_{0}\rvert}.

Using a clever modification of the above argument, Stečkin
[
Ste70
]
obtains a precise value for
c
if
\lvert t_{0}\rvert
is sufficiently large. Stečkin’s argument is a fundamental ingredient in this article. We explain it briefly and refer the reader to Section
3
for further details.
Consider a trigonometric polynomial

P_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\cos k\theta

such that
a_{k}\geq 0
for each
0\leq k\leq n
,
a_{0}<a_{1}
and
P_{n}(\theta)\geq 0
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

We set
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and choose a real number
1<\sigma<\phi
. For a given
\sigma
, we set
\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+4\sigma^{2}}
. We denote
s=\sigma+it
and
s_{1}=\sigma_{1}+it
. Further, we denote

G(z)=\operatorname{Re}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(z)\right),\qquad\operatorname{Re}(z)>1.

Since
P_{n}(\theta)\geq 0
, we have

(5)

\begin{split}&S(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\left(G(\sigma+ikt)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}G(\sigma_{1}+ikt)\right)\\
&=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}n^{\sigma_{1}}}\right)P_{n}(t\log n)\geq 0.\end{split}

On the other hand, by an application of (
4
), Stečkin observes that if
\beta+it_{0}
is a zero of the zeta function such that
1/2<\beta<1
and
t_{0}\geq T\geq 12
, then, for
k\geq 1
,

(6)

G(\sigma+ikt_{0})-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}G(\sigma_{1}+ikt_{0})\leq\frac{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}}{2}\log kt_{0}-\frac{1}{\sigma-\beta}+A_{3}(T),

where

A_{3}(T)=\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1-\frac{3}{2}\log 2-\log\pi\right)+\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{4}{T}\right)\frac{1}{T}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{2}{T}\right)\frac{1}{T}.

We note here that to obtain the above inequality, one requires explicit bounds for terms of the form

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s}{2}+1\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s_{1}}{2}+1\right)\right).

Combining (
5
) and (
6
) with a bound for
G(\sigma)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}G(\sigma_{1})
, we isolate
\beta
as follows:

\frac{a_{1}}{\sigma-\beta}<\frac{a_{0}}{\sigma-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\left(\frac{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}}{2}\right)\log\,t_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\left(\frac{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}}{2}\right)\log\,k+A_{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}.

The choice

P_{4}(\theta)=8(0.9126+\cos\theta)^{2}(0.2766+\cos\theta)^{2}

yields

\beta\leq 1-\frac{1}{9.65\log t_{0}}.

In other words,
\zeta(s)\neq 0
in the region

\sigma>1-\frac{1}{9.65\log\lvert t\rvert},\qquad\lvert t\rvert\,\geq 12.

The explicit constant in the zero-free region for
\zeta(s)
obtained by Stečkin’s argument is significantly sharper than those obtained before. McCurley
[
McC84
]
adapted the above argument to derive explicit zero-free regions for Dirichlet
L
-functions, extending Stečkin’s argument to small values of
\lvert t\rvert
as well. For a positive integer
q
, let
\mathcal{L}_{q}(s)
be the product of the
\phi(q)
Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
associated with the characters
\chi
modulo
q
. Then,
\mathcal{L}_{q}(s)
has at most a single zero in the region

(7)

\sigma>1-\frac{1}{9.65\log M},\qquad M=\max\{q,q\lvert t\rvert,10\}.

This exceptional zero is a simple real zero of a Dirichlet
L
-function associated with a real non-principal character modulo
q
. We note here that the result of McCurley has been significantly improved by Kadiri in
[
Kad18
]
where the explicit constant
9.65
in (
7
) has been replaced by
5.60
(building on the ideas pioneered in
[
Kad05
]
).

In this article, we adapt the ideas of Stečkin and McCurley for the modular
L
-function
L(s,f)
associated with a Hecke newform
f
of even weight
k
and squarefree level
N
. This argument will require us to consider the symmetric power
L
-functions
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
for
0\leq m\leq 4
. In the next section, we review fundamental analytic properties of these
L
-functions, such as the analogue of the classical Hadamard factorization (
4
) for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
, which will be required for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
Symmetric power
L
-functions: definitions, functional equation and explicit formulae

2.2.1.
Definitions

Let
N
be a positive squarefree integer and let
k
be a positive even integer. Consider a Hecke newform
f
of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. We mention here that
f
is necessarily non-CM because it has squarefree level and trivial nebentypus (see the proof of the
Corollary of Theorem A in
[
DK00
]
).

In this section, we start by reviewing fundamental analytic properties of the symmetric power
L
-functions
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
associated with the newform
f
. We refer the reader to
[
IK04
, Sections 5.3 and 5.11]
,
[
LW06
, Section 1]
, and
[
HIJTS22
, Section 2]
for further details.

Following the notation in the introduction,
f
has a Fourier expansion of the form

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\lambda_{f}(n)q^{n},

and the standard
L
-function corresponding to
f
is defined by the Dirichlet series

L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Moreover, for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have the Euler product representation

\displaystyle L(f,s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\lambda_{f}(p)p^{-s}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\nmid N}\left(1-\lambda_{f}(p)p^{-s}+p^{-2s}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{2}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},

where
\lambda_{f}(p)=\alpha_{1}(p)+\alpha_{2}(p)
and more generally

\lambda_{f}(p^{r})=\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)^{r+1}-\alpha_{2}(p)^{r+1}}{\alpha_{1}(p)-\alpha_{2}(p)},

for all
r\geq 1
and all primes
p
. Moreover,
\alpha_{1}(p)\alpha_{2}(p)=1
if
p\nmid N
and
\alpha_{1}(p)\alpha_{2}(p)=0
if
p\mid N
. The celebrated work of Deligne (see
[
Del74
]
and
[
Del80
]
) asserts that
\lvert\alpha_{1}(p)\rvert=\lvert\alpha_{2}(p)\rvert=1
if
p\nmid N
. If
p\mid N
, we shall assume without loss of generality that
\alpha_{2}(p)=0
. By the Atkin-Lehner-Li theory (see
[
Li75
, Theoem 3]
), we infer that

\alpha_{1}(p)=\epsilon_{f}(p)p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
if
p\mathrel{\|}N
. Here
\epsilon_{f}(p)=\pm 1
, and
-\epsilon_{f}(p)
is the eigenvalue of
f
under the action of the Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{p}
.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, the symmetric power
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
is given by the Euler product

(8)

L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=\prod_{p}\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)^{m-j}\alpha_{2}(p)^{j}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},

and the Dirichlet series representation

L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

Note that
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{0}f)=\zeta(s)
and
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{1}f)=L(s,f)
.

2.2.2.
The functional equation and explicit formulae

We set

(9)

\gamma(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=\begin{cases}\prod_{l=0}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)(k-1)\right)&\text{ if }m=2n+1\\
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+\delta_{2\nmid n}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s+l(k-1))&\text{ if }m=2n,\end{cases}

where
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
,
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
, and
\delta_{2\nmid n}=1
if 2 does not divide
n
, and 0 otherwise.
The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=N^{ms/2}\gamma(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)

is entire, and it satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=\epsilon_{\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f}\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f),

where

\epsilon_{\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f}=\pm 1
.
We denote

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f}(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,

and as per (
8
),

\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f}(n)=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{1}(p)^{(m-j)l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{jl}&\text{ if }n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Using the Hadamard factorization of the completed
L
-function
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
, we have the following analogue of (
4
) for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)
(see
[
IK04
, Theorem 5.6]
):

(10)

\operatorname{Re}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f\right)\right)=\frac{m}{2}\log N+\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f\right)\right)-\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho:\\
L(\rho,\operatorname{Sym}^{m}f)=0\\
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(\rho)\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}\right).

2.2.3.
Logarithmic derivatives of certain products of
L
-functions

The argument to obtain zero-free regions for
L(s,f)
requires us to consider a quartic trigonometric polynomial (see Section
4
) of the form:

\displaystyle P_{4}(\theta;n)=8(a+\Lambda_{f}(n)\cos\theta)^{2}(b+\Lambda_{f}(n)\cos\theta)^{2},

for appropriate constants
a
and
b
. The following lemma provides a relationship between the terms
\Lambda_{f}(n)^{m}
for
0\leq m\leq 4
(which appear as we expand
P_{4}(\theta;n)
) and the Dirichlet coefficients of the logarithmic derivatives of the functions

\displaystyle L_{(0)}(s,f)

\displaystyle=\zeta(s),

\displaystyle L_{(1)}(s,f)

\displaystyle=L(s,f),

(11)

\displaystyle L_{(2)}(s,f)

\displaystyle=L(s,\chi_{0})L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{2}f),

\displaystyle L_{(3)}(s,f)

\displaystyle=L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{3}f)L(s,f)^{2}\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle L_{(4)}(s,f)

\displaystyle=L(s,\chi_{0})^{2}L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{2}f)^{3}L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{4}f)\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)^{2}}{p^{s}}\right)^{3}.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
0\leq m\leq 4
, we have

-\frac{L^{\prime}_{(m)}}{L_{(m)}}(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\Lambda_{f,m}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,

where

\Lambda_{f,m}(n)=\Lambda_{f}(n)^{m}.

Proof.

The lemma is immediate for
m=0
and
m=1
.
Observe that

\Lambda_{f}(n)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}&\text{ if }n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here, we recall that
\alpha_{1}(p)\alpha_{2}(p)=1
for
(p,N)=1
, and
\alpha_{2}(p)=0
for
p\mid N
. Also, note that

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi_{0}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

For
m=2
, we have

\begin{split}\Lambda_{f,2}(n)&=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}+2\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l}&\text{ if }n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1,\,(p,N)=1\\
\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}&\text{ if }n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1,\,(p,N)>1.\\
\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

For
m=3
, we can write

(12)

L_{(3)}\left(s,f\right)=L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{3}f)\prod_{p\nmid N}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-2}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{2}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-2}.

For
n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1
and
(p,N)=1
, we use (
2.2.3
) to deduce that

\begin{split}&\Lambda_{f,3}(n)=\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{3}f}(n)+2\Lambda_{f}(n)\\
&=\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{3}}(p^{l})+2\Lambda_{f}(p^{l})\\
&=\alpha_{1}(p)^{3l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{3l}+2\left(\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}\right)\\
&=\alpha_{1}(p)^{3l}+3\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}+3\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{3l}\\
&=(\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{l})^{3}\\
&=\Lambda_{f}(n)^{3}.\\
\end{split}

On the other hand, for
n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1
and
p\mid N
, we use (
12
). Thus, we have,

\Lambda_{f,3}(n)=\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{3}f}(n)=\alpha_{1}(p)^{3l}\\
=\Lambda_{f}(n)^{3}.

Here, the equality
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{3}f}(n)=\alpha_{1}(p)^{3l}
follows from the fact that
\alpha_{2}(p)=0
.
Thus,

\Lambda_{f,3}(n)=\Lambda_{f}(n)^{3}

for all
n
.
For
m=4
, we have

(13)

L_{(4)}\left(s,f\right)=L(s,\chi_{0})^{2}L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{4}f)\prod_{p\nmid N}\left[\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)^{2}}{p^{s}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}(p)\alpha_{2}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{2}(p)^{2}}{p^{s}}\right)\right]^{-3}.

Using (
2.2.3
) and (
13
), we have, for
n=p^{l},\,l\geq 1
,

\begin{split}&\Lambda_{f,4}(p^{l})=2\chi_{0}(p^{l})+\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{4}f}(p^{l})+\begin{cases}3\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{2}f}(p^{l})&\text{ if }(p,N)=1\\
0&\text{ if }(p,N)>1.\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

If
(p,N)=1
, then

\begin{split}&\Lambda_{f,4}(p^{l})=2+3(\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l})\\
&+\alpha_{1}(p)^{4l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{3l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l}+\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}\alpha_{2}(p)^{3l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{4l}\\
&=6+4(\alpha_{1}(p)^{2l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{2l})+\alpha_{1}(p)^{4l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{4l}\\
&=(\alpha_{1}(p)^{l}+\alpha_{2}(p)^{l})^{4}\end{split}

If
(p,N)>1
, then

\Lambda_{f,4}(p^{l})=\Lambda_{\operatorname{Sym}^{4}f}(p^{l})=\alpha_{1}(p)^{4l},

since
\alpha_{2}(p)=0.

Thus,

\Lambda_{f,4}(n)=\Lambda_{f}(n)^{4}.

∎

We fix some notation that will be used throughout the article. We set

. For a given

For

The following lemmas are at the heart of Stečkin’s trick and will be used extensively in this work.

Lemma 3.1

Let

If

(b)

If
\frac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}(z)<1
and
\operatorname{Im}(z)=t

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-1+\overline{z}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}F(s_{1},z)\geq 0.

Proof.

This is

.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}(z)\leq 1
and
\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert<1
.
Then

\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\sigma-1+\overline{z}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}F(\sigma_{1},z)\geq-0.6.

Proof.

By observing that
F(\sigma_{1},z)<\sqrt{5}
,
it suffices to verify that

(14)

-\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-1+\overline{z}}\right)\leq-0.4.

To get this bound, we remark that

-\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-1+\overline{z}}\right)=-\frac{\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}<-\frac{\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+1}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)<\phi
and the function

\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}

is concave down in
(\frac{1}{2},\phi)
, we have the lower bound
\frac{\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+1}\geq 0.4
, and (
14
) follows.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}(z)<1
and
\operatorname{Im}(s)=3\operatorname{Im}(z)
.
Then

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-1+\overline{z}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}F(s_{1},z)\geq-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{5}}-\frac{2}{5}\geq-1.12361.

Proof.

Observe that

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s-1+\overline{z}}\right)=\frac{\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}>0.

We write

\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}F(s_{1},z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\sigma_{1}-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma_{1}-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}.

Notice that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}-\operatorname{Re}(z)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{1}{\phi-1}=\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{5}},

where the last inequality follows since
\sigma_{1}\geq\phi
and
\operatorname{Re}(z)<1
.
We also have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{\sigma_{1}-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}{(\sigma_{1}-1+\operatorname{Re}(z))^{2}+4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}-1+\operatorname{Re}(z)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{1}{\phi-1/2}=\frac{2}{5}.

Combining all these bounds yields the desired result. ∎

4.
A Positivity Argument via a Quartic Trigonometric Polynomial

For
n=0,1,2,3,4
, we define the auxiliary functions
j_{f,n}(\sigma,t)
as Stečkin’s differences:

j_{f,n}(\sigma,t)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{L_{(n)}^{\prime}}{L_{(n)}}(\sigma_{1}+it,f)-\frac{L_{(n)}^{\prime}}{L_{(n)}}(\sigma+it,f)\right).

A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem

&+\frac{(a_{2}+4)(\sigma-1)}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+4t^{2}}+\frac{2(\sigma-1)}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+16t^{2}}-\frac{2a_{3}(\sigma-\beta_{0})}{(\sigma-\beta_{0})^{2}+4t^{2}}{-93.3945}.\end{split}

We write
\sigma=1+\frac{r_{2}}{\log kN}
for some
r_{2}>0
chosen such that
1<\sigma<1.15
. Using this notation and observing that

\frac{(a_{2}+4)(\sigma-1)}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+4t^{2}}-\frac{2a_{3}(\sigma-\beta_{0})}{(\sigma-\beta_{0})^{2}+4t^{2}}
is decreasing in
t
, we get

(59)

\begin{split}\frac{a_{1}+3a_{3}}{r_{2}+c}&<\frac{8a^{2}b^{2}+a_{2}+2}{r_{2}}+\kappa\left(32+a_{1}+2a_{2}+13a_{3}\right)+\frac{(a_{2}+4)r_{2}}{r_{2}^{2}+4\gamma^{2}}+\frac{2r_{2}}{r_{2}^{2}+16\gamma^{2}}-\frac{2a_{3}(r_{2}+c)}{(r_{2}+c)^{2}+4\gamma^{2}}.\end{split}

For
\delta>0
chosen so that

(60)

\frac{(a_{2}+4)r_{2}}{r_{2}^{2}+4\gamma^{2}}+\frac{2r_{2}}{r_{2}^{2}+16\gamma^{2}}-\frac{2a_{3}(r_{2}+c)}{(r_{2}+c)^{2}+4\gamma^{2}}-\delta<0,

we see that (
59
) yields

\begin{split}\frac{A}{r_{2}+c}&<\frac{B}{r_{2}}+C\kappa+\delta,\end{split}

where, as in Section
6
, we set
A=a_{1}+3a_{3}
,
B=8a^{2}b^{2}+a_{2}+2
and
C=32+a_{1}+2a_{2}+13a_{3}
. It follows that

c>\frac{(A-B)r_{2}-C\kappa r_{2}^{2}-\delta r_{2}^{2}}{B+C\kappa r_{2}+\delta r_{2}}.

The function

f_{\delta}(r_{2})=\frac{(A-B)r_{2}-C\kappa r_{2}^{2}-\delta r_{2}^{2}}{B+C\kappa r_{2}+\delta r_{2}}

attains its maximum value at

r_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{AB}-B}{C\kappa+\delta},

giving us the inequality

c>\frac{A+B-2\sqrt{AB}}{C\kappa+\delta}.

Substituting
r_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{AB}-B}{C\kappa+\delta}
and
c=\frac{A+B-2\sqrt{AB}}{C\kappa+\delta}
in (
60
) gives

(61)

\frac{(a_{2}+4)(\sqrt{AB}-B)(C\kappa+\delta)}{\left(\sqrt{AB}-B\right)^{2}+4\gamma^{2}(C\kappa+\delta)^{2}}+\frac{2(\sqrt{AB}-B)(C\kappa+\delta)}{\left(\sqrt{AB}-B\right)^{2}+16\gamma^{2}(C\kappa+\delta)^{2}}-\frac{2a_{3}(A-\sqrt{AB})(C\kappa+\delta)}{(A-\sqrt{AB})^{2}+4\gamma^{2}(C\kappa+\delta)^{2}}-\delta<0.

Using
\gamma=0.30992
, one can verify that (
61
) is satisfied if we take
\delta=1.62622
. With this choice of
\delta
, we get
c>\frac{1}{16.9309}
.

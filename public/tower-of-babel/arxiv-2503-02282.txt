1.
Introduction

In [32], Spivey discovered by using combinatorial method a recurrence relation for the Bell numbers

(1)

\phi_{n+m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}\binom{n}{k}j^{n-k}\phi_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,21,32]),

which generalizes both of the following well-known expressions for those numbers (see (
6
), (
7
)):

\phi_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\brace j},\quad\phi_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\phi_{k}.

Degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers have been explored by using various methods, including quantum mechanics, combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus,
p
-adic analysis, probability theory, differential equations, operator theory and analytic number theory.

The aim of this paper is to derive Spivey’s type recurrence relations for the degenerate Bell polynomials, the degenerate Dowling polynomials and the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials by using the boson annihilation and creation operators satisfying the commutation relation
aa^{+}-a^{+}a=1
.

In more detail, the outline of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we derive a recurrence relation for degenerate Bell polynomials

(2)

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}(x)

from the normal ordering of ‘degenerate
n
-th power’ of the number operator
\hat{n}=a^{\dagger}a

\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},

in terms of boson annihilation and creation operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
satisfying the commutation relation with
aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a=1
(see (
9
), (
11
)).
Note that taking
x=1
and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
2
) yield the Spivey’s recurrence relation for the Bell numbers in (
1
).

In Section 3, we deduce recurrence relations for the degenerate Dowling polynomials
D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)
(see Theorem 3.1) and the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials
D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,x)
(see Theorem 3.2), respectively from the normal orderings of degenerate
n
-th powers of the operators
ma^{\dagger}a+1
(see (
34
)) and
ma^{\dagger}a+r
(see (
35
)). For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(3)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),

where

(4)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-27]).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(5)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,13-27,30]).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}={n\brace k}\displaystyle
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(6)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-33]).

The Bell polynomials are given by

(7)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(e^{t}-1)},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,10,12,26,31]).

When
x=1
,
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1),\ (n\geq 0)
, are called the Bell numbers.
The degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(8)

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n,k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16-19]).

From (
8
), we note that (see (
3
))

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14-18,23,24]).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\phi_{n}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

When
x=1,\ \phi_{n,\lambda}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the degenerate Bell numbers.

We recall that the boson annihilation and creation operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
satisfy the commutation relation given by

(9)

[a,a^{\dagger}]=aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a=1,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5,11-13,16-18,23-25,27,29]).

In the corresponding Fock space, we note that

(10)

a^{\dagger}|k\rangle=\sqrt{k+1}|k+1\rangle,\quad a|k\rangle=\sqrt{k}|k-1\rangle,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11-13,16-18,23-25,27,29]).

By (
10
), we get
a^{\dagger}a|k\rangle=k|k\rangle
. The coherent states
|z\rangle
, where
z
is complex number, satisfy
a|z\rangle=z|z\rangle,\ \langle z|z\rangle=1
and
\langle z|a=\langle z|\overline{z}
. We recall that the harmonic oscillator has Hamiltonian
\hat{n}=a^{\dagger}a
(neglecting the zero point energy) and the usual eigenstates
|n\rangle
(for
n\in\mathbb{N}
) satisfying
\hat{n}|n\rangle=n|n\rangle
and
\langle m|n\rangle=\delta_{m,n}
, where
\delta_{m,n}
is the Kronecker’s symbol (see [16]). It is known that the standard basis commutation relation
aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a=1
can be considered formally, in a suitable space of functions, by letting
a=\frac{d}{dx}
and
a^{\dagger}=x
.

We note that (see (
6
), (
16
))

\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)^{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k\brace l}\big(a^{\dagger})^{l}a^{l},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11-13,16-18,23-25,27]).

The normal ordering of degenerate
n
-th power of the number operator
\hat{n}=a^{\dagger}a
is given in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
by (see (
4
), (
5
), (
16
))

(11)

\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},

and

\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{n}a^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [16]),

where
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16,17,19,22]).

2.
Recurrence relation for degenerate Bell polynomials via Boson operator

In this section, we derive a Spivey’s type recurrence relation for the degenerate Bell polynomials via boson operator.

From (
9
), we note that

(12)

\displaystyle\big[a,\hat{n}\big]

\displaystyle=a\hat{n}-\hat{n}a=a\big(a^{\dagger}a)-(a^{\dagger}a)a

\displaystyle=\big(aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a\big)a=a

and

(13)

\displaystyle\big[\hat{n},a^{+}\big]

\displaystyle=\hat{n}a^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}\hat{n}=\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)a^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)

\displaystyle=a^{\dagger}\big(aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a\big)=a^{\dagger}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
9
), we get

(14)

\displaystyle\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big]

\displaystyle=a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}-\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a

\displaystyle=\big(a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}-\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}a\big)a^{\dagger}+\big(a^{\dagger})^{k-1}\big(aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a\big)

\displaystyle=\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\big]a^{\dagger}+\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\big[a,a^{\dagger}\big]

\displaystyle=\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\big]a^{\dagger}+\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}=\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-2}\big]\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{2}+2\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\big[a,a^{\dagger}\big]\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}+(k-1)\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}=k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}.

In a similar manner, we can show that

(15)

\big[a^{k},a^{\dagger}\big]=ka^{k-1},

which in turn implies that

(16)

(a^{\dagger}a)_{k}=(a^{\dagger})^{k}a^{k}.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the coherent states
|z\rangle
satisfy
a|z\rangle=z|z\rangle
. Note that
a|0\rangle=0|0\rangle=0
.

Thus, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big]\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=a\big(a^{\dagger})^{k}-\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a\big|0\big\rangle=a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big|0\big\rangle-\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=a\big(a^{+}\big)^{k}\big|0\big\rangle,\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

From (
14
) and (
17
), we note that

(18)

a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big|0\big\rangle=\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big]\big|0\big\rangle=k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\big|0\big\rangle.

Therefore, by (
12
), (
13
), (
14
), (
15
) and (
18
), we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big]=k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1},\quad\big[a^{k},a^{\dagger}\big]=ka^{k-1},

\displaystyle a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big|0\big\rangle=\big[a,\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big]\big|0\big\rangle=k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\big|0\big\rangle.

In addition,

\big[a,\hat{n}\big]=a\quad\mathrm{and}\quad[\hat{n},a^{\dagger}]=a^{\dagger}.

From (
11
), we have

(19)

\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

Now, we observe from (
18
) that

(20)

\displaystyle a^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l!}a^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l}\big|0\big\rangle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l-1)!}a^{k-1}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l-1}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l!}a^{k-1}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l}\big|0\big\rangle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l-1)!}a^{k-2}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l-1}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l!}a^{k-2}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l}\big|0\big\rangle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l-1)!}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l-1}\big|0\big\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l!}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{l}\big|0\big\rangle=e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

By (
5
), (
8
), (
19
) and (
20
), we get

(21)

\displaystyle\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\phi_{n,\lambda}(a^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

Therefore, by (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

a^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle,\quad\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\phi_{n,\lambda}(a^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}|0\big\rangle.

From (
14
), we note that

(22)

a\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a+k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

By (
22
), we get

(23)

\displaystyle\hat{n}\big(a^{\dagger})^{k}

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)(a^{\dagger})^{k}=a^{\dagger}\big(a(a^{\dagger})^{k}\big)=a^{\dagger}\Big(\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a+k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k+1}a+k\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}a+k\big).

Thus, by (
23
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}=\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\times\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda\big)\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-2)\lambda\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda+j\big)

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-2)\lambda\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\big(a^{+}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-2)\lambda+j\big)\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda+j\big)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda+j\big)_{n,\lambda},

where
n,j
are nonnegative integers.

From (
23
) and (
24
), the next theorem follows.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
k
be a nonnegative integer. For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{n}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}=\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}a+k\big),

\displaystyle\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda+k\big)_{n,\lambda},

\displaystyle m\big(a^{\dagger}a)\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}=m\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(aa^{\dagger}+j)=\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+mj\big).

From (
11
) and Theorem 2.3, we note that

(25)

\displaystyle\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{m+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}a-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j}.

Therefore, by (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

(26)

\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{m+n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j}.

We recall the following identity:

(27)

(x+y)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(x)_{n-k,\lambda}(y)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
26
) and (
27
), we note that

(28)

\displaystyle\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{m+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}\big(j-m\lambda\big)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{k,\lambda}a^{j}.

Thus, by Theorem 2.2 and (
28
), we get

(29)

\displaystyle\phi_{n+m,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{n+m,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}a\big)_{k,\lambda}a^{j}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(aa^{\dagger}\big)_{k,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

Therefore, by (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big),

and hence

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}\phi_{k,\lambda}(x).

3.
Recurrence relation for degenerate Dowling polynomials via boson operator

In this section, we derive Spivey’s type recurrence relations for the degenerate Dowling and
r
-Dowling polynomials via boson operators.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the degenerate Whitney numbers of the second kind are defined by

(30)

(mx+1)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17]).

Note that

W_{1,\lambda}(n,k)={n+1\brace k+1}_{\lambda}+\lambda n{n\brace k+1}_{\lambda},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

From (
30
), we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)=W_{m}(n,k),

where
W_{m}(n,k)
are the Whitney numbers of the second kind given by

(mx+1)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16,17,28]).

We consider the degenerate Dowling polynomials defined by

(31)

D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

D_{m}(n,x)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)x^{k}

are the Dowling polynomials.

For
r\geq 0
, the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind are given by

(32)

(mx+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

In view of (
31
), the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials are defined by

(33)

D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16,17]).

From (
16
), (
30
) and (
32
), we note that

(34)

\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},

and

(35)

\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

By (
34
), we get

(36)

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{l+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{l,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j},

where
l,n
are nonnegative integers.

By (
36
) and Theorem 2.3, we get

(37)

\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{l+n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1+mj-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j},

where
l,n
are nonnegative integers.

By Theorem 2.2, (
31
) and (
34
), we get

(38)

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{l,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\rangle=D_{m,\lambda}(l,ma^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\rangle,

where
l
is a nonnegative integer.

For
l,n\geq 0
, by Theorem 2.2, (
27
), (
37
) and (
38
), we get

(39)

\displaystyle D_{m,\lambda}(l+n,ma^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{l+n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1+mj-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1+mj-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}(a^{\dagger})^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{k,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}(a^{\dagger})^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}(k,ma^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

Therefore, by (
39
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n,l\geq 0
, we have

D_{m,\lambda}(n+l,ma^{\dagger})=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)m^{j}(a^{\dagger})^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}(k,ma^{\dagger}),

and hence

D_{m,\lambda}(n+l,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)x^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}(k,x).

From Theorem 2.2, (
33
) and (
35
), we note that

(40)

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}\big(n,ma^{\dagger}\big)e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

By Theorem 2.3, (
27
) and (
35
), we get

(41)

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{l+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{l,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)m^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(a^{\dagger})^{j}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r+mj-l\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}a^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{k,\lambda}a^{j}.

By Theorem 2.2, (
40
) and (
41
), we have

\displaystyle D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l+n,ma^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle=\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{l+n,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{k,\lambda}a^{j}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{k,\lambda}e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)\binom{n}{k}m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(k,ma^{\dagger})e^{a^{\dagger}}\big|0\big\rangle.

This shows the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
l,n\geq 0
, we have

D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l+n,ma^{\dagger})=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)m^{j}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(k,ma^{\dagger}),

and hence

(42)

D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l+n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)x^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(k,x).

From (
42
) and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we have

D_{m}^{(r)}(l+n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m}^{(r)}(l,j)x^{j}(mj)^{n-k}D_{m}^{(r)}(k,x),

where
D_{m}^{(r)}(k,x)
are the
r
-Dowling polynomials given by

D_{m}^{(r)}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}^{(r)}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

4.
Conclusion

In recent years, various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers have been explored with renewed interests. Studying degenerate versions was initiated by Carlitz in his work on degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials (see [7]).
In this paper, we showed Spivey’s type recurrence relations for the degenerate Bell polynomials, the degenerate Dowling polynomials and the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials, respectively by using normal orderings of degenerate
n
-th powers of
a^{\dagger}a
,
ma^{\dagger}a+1
, and
ma^{\dagger}a+r
.

Indeed, we derived the following recurrence relations for the degenerate Dowling polynomials
D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)
and the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials
D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,x)
:

\displaystyle D_{m,\lambda}(n+l,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}(l,j)x^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}(k,x),

\displaystyle D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l+n,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,j)x^{j}(mj-l\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(k,x),

respectively from

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+1\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},

\displaystyle\big(ma^{\dagger}a+r\big)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}\big(a^{\dagger}\big)^{k}a^{k},

where
W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)
and
W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
are the degenerate Whitney numbers of the second kind and the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind.

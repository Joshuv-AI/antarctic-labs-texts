Cocycle superrigidity for median spaces of finite rank
Biao Ma, Lamine Messaci

Abstract
. We investigate cocycle superrigidity in the setting of finite rank median spaces. By exploiting a dynamical approach to superrigidity, we establish, for a median space X X of finite rank, the superrigidity of I ​ s ​ o ​ m ​ ( X ) Isom(X) -valued cocycles for a product of locally compact second countable groups. In the case of action by irreducible lattices in such product groups, this approach yields a new and self-contained proof of the superrigidity of homomorphisms.

Introduction

1.1.
Overview

After Margulis’ celebrated superrigidity theorems on lattices of higher rank followed up with Zimmer’s superrigidity for cocycles (
[

In this paper, we consider the superrigidity of cocycles for a product of locally compact second countable groups, and the target group is the isometry group of a complete separable irreducible median space of finite rank. The argument when applied to the case of action of irreducible lattices in a product of locally compact second countable groups on such median spaces gives rise to a new proof of known superrigidity in this context.

Median spaces (algebras) first appeared in the mid of the 20th in the context of distributive lattices and Boolean algebras. They raised again in the beginning of the 21th in the area of geometric group theory as median spaces give a common framework for the study of real trees and CAT(0) cube complexes, as well as the geometric characterization of Kazhdan’s property (T) and Haagerup property. Particular examples of median spaces of finite rank include, for instance,

1.2.
Statement of the results

Let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a product of second countable, locally compact, non compact groups, and let
\Omega
be a standard probability space on which each
G_{i}
acts ergodically by measure-preserving action. The reader is referred to Section
3
for cocycles.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.8
)
.

Let
X
be a complete separable irreducible median space of finite rank and
\alpha:G\times\Omega\rightarrow Isom(X)
a non-elementary cocycle.
Then, there exists an
Isom(X)
-invariant median subspace
Y\subseteq X
such that the cocycle
\alpha
is cohomologous to a group homomorphism
h:G\rightarrow Isom(Y)
that factors through the projection onto one factor
G_{i}
.

We note that the superrigidity of cocycles is new even when the target group is the automorphism group of a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex.

We have two remarks. Firstly, the
Isom(X)
-invariant median subspace
Y
arising in Theorem
1.1
, that we refer to as the
median core
, has the property that the restriction of any isometry of
X
on
Y
is uniquely determined by its restriction on its boundary. In general, a homomorphism from
G
to
Isom(Y)
cannot be lifted to
Isom(X)
(see Example 4.7
[
16
]
). Secondly, The median core
Y
is in general not convex, hence its existence does not contradict the Roller minimality assumption.

Using the same approach in the particular context of the superrigidity of homomorphism of lattices, this gives rise to a new argument that does not rely on the non vanishing of a bounded cohomology or induced cohomology class. It recovers Fioravanti’s superrigidity result for actions on median spaces of finite rank (
[
16
]
) and extends it to the class of non square-integrable lattices.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq G_{1}\times G_{2}
be an irreducible lattice in a product of locally compact second countable groups. If
\Gamma
acts Roller minimally and Roller non-elementarily on a complete separable irreducible median space of finite rank, there exists an
Isom(X)
-invariant median subspace
Y
on which the
\Gamma
-action extends to
G
and factors through one of the
G_{i}
’s.

The main step in the proof of Theorem
5.11
is the proof of the existence and uniqueness of a boundary map from the
G
-boundary to the regular boundary
\partial_{r}X
of the median space
X
, that is the part of the Roller boundary that encodes the tree-like part of the median space.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group acting Roller non elementarilly and Roller minimally on an irreducible finite rank median space. Then there exists a unique
G
-equivariant measurable map
\phi:B\rightarrow\partial_{r}X
.

For results on boundary maps in the median setting, see
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
5
]
.
The regular boundary
\partial_{r}X
can be defined as the limit set of a particular set of contracting isometries. These are isometries whose axes of translation skewer through two strongly separated halfspaces, where two halfspaces are said to be strongly separated if there is no halfspace that is transverse to both. The existence of such elements is ensured under the mild assumptions that the median space is irreducible and the isometry group acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally (see Definitions
2.24
).

To construct the boundary map, we adapt to the median case the approach given in
[
12
]
which relies on the halfspaces to associate a “center of mass” to each measure. In their approach, to prove the measurability of some key maps, they relied on the fact that the space of halfspaces of the cube complex is countable. To remedy the lack of discreteness of the median space, we prove the existence of a countable dense subset of halfspaces that is invariant under the whole group of isometries.

Theorem 1.4
.

[Theorem
2.36
]
Let
X
be a complete separable irreducible median space of finite rank. If
Isom(X)
acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally, there exists an
Isom(X)
invariant countable set of halfspaces that separate any pair of points of the median core.

The theorem above is very practical for dealing with the measurability question regarding maps and subsets in the Roller compactification.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
follows almost the same lines, the paper is organized to first prove Theorem
1.2
and then Theorem
1.1
by pointing out minor changes. It has the advantage that no background on cocycles is needed to understand the main ideas. We now present an outline of the proof.

Superrigidity of homomorphisms (Section
5
).

Let
\Gamma\leq G_{1}\times G_{2}
be an irreducible lattice in a product of locally compact groups and let
X
be a complete, irreducible, separable median space of finite rank. Assume that
\Gamma
acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally on
X
. For a fixed
G
-boundary
B
, the first step is to construct a
G
-equivariant map
\phi:B\rightarrow\overline{X}
(Theorem
5.2
). To do so, one first starts from the
G
-equivariant map
\Phi:B\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
, where
\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
is the space of probability measures over the Roller compactification
\overline{X}
. Such
G
-equivariant map is ensured by the amenability of the action
G\curvearrowright B
. For each probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
one associates a center of mass by considering the intersection of all closed halfspaces having measure bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
(see Section
4
). Using the ergodicity of the diagonal
G
-action on
B\times B
, one deduces that for almost all
b\in B
, the center of mass of the corresponding measure is a singleton (Proposition
5.1
). Thus, the map
\Phi
descends to a measurable map
\phi:B\rightarrow\overline{X}
.

The second step consists of showing that the map
\phi
has an image essentially in the regular boundary (Theorem
5.4
). To do so, one uses again the double ergodicty of the action
G\curvearrowright B\times B
to deduce that for almost all pair
b_{1},b_{2}
their images under
\phi
are separated by an arbitrarily large set of strongly separated hyperplanes. The regular boundary has the property that for any triple of pairwise distinct points, their median point lies in
X
.

Having these data in hand and borrowing an argument from Bader-Furman
[
3
]
, one shows that any
G
-equivariant map from
B\times B
into
\partial_{r}X
depends on one variable and is mapped through
\phi
(Theorem
5.6
). Restricting to the situation where the group is an irreducible lattice in a product of locally compact second countable groups, one has
B=B_{1}\times B_{2}
where
B_{i}
is a
G_{i}
-boundary. In this case, the product
B\times B
contains a non “trivial” automorphism that commute with the action (the generalized Weyl group introduced in
[
2
]
is bigger than
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
). Combining this with the proven fact that any map from
B\times B
to
\partial_{r}X
depends on one variable, this imposes the boundary map
\phi
to depend only on one factor
B_{i}
(Theorem
5.9
). Hence, one obtains a
G_{i}
-action on
\phi(B)\subseteq\partial_{r}X
extending the
\Gamma
-action. The last step is to show that the
G_{i}
-action extends continuously to the median closure of
\phi(b)
inside
X
, using the median ternary operation and the density of the projection of
\Gamma
in
G_{i}
(Theorem
5.11
).

Superrigidity of cocycles (Section
6
).

Let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a product of second countable locally compact groups, and let
\Omega
be a standard probability space on which each
G_{i}
acts ergodically with measure-preserving transformations. Let
X
be a complete separable irreducible median space of finite rank and let
\alpha:G\times\Omega\rightarrow Isom(X)
be a non elementary cocycle (see Section
3
). The cocycle defines a natural
G
-action on the space of class of measurable sections from
\Omega
to the median core
Y
. This space has a natural structure of a median algebra, where the median operation is defined pointwise. The goal, inspired by
[
4
]
, is to prove the existence of a median subalgebra which, when endowed with the
L^{1}
-metric, is a median space isometric to
Y
(the median closure of the regular boundary) and which is
G
-invariant with one of the factors
G_{i}
acting trivially (say
G_{2}
).

This median subspace consists of class of
\alpha_{G_{2}}=\alpha|_{G_{2}}
-equivariant sections from
\Omega
to
Y
. To prove the existence of such
\alpha_{G_{2}}
-equivariant sections, we start with an
\alpha
-equivariant section
\Phi:\Omega\times B\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
. Adapting the same arguments used in the superrigidity of homomorphisms to the case of cocycle, we show that the map
\Phi
descends to an
\alpha
-equivariant map
\phi:\Omega\times B\rightarrow\partial_{r}X
that factors through the projection into
\Omega\times B_{1}
(Proposition
6.1
, Proposition
6.7
). Almost all fixed
b_{1}\in B_{1}
gives rise to an
\alpha_{G_{2}}
-equivariant section from
\Omega
into
\partial_{r}X
. After composing such sections with the ternary operation, one obtains infinitely many
\alpha_{G_{2}}
-equivariant sections from
\Omega
to
Y
, denoted by
\mathcal{Y}
. The final step is then to show that such a
subspace is isometric to
Y
(Theorem
6.8
), using the ergodicity of the
G_{i}
-actions on
\Omega
.

2.
Geometry of median spaces and compactifications

2.1.
Median spaces and median algebras

Definition 2.1

(Median spaces)
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is a (metric) median space if, for any
x,y,z\in X
, there exists a unique point
m=m(x,y,z)\in X
, called the median point of
x,y,z\in X
, such that

[x,y]\cap[x,z]\cap[y,z]=\{m\}

where, for any two points
a,b
in
X
, the interval
[a,b]
between
a
and
b
is defined to be

[a,b]:=\{c\in X\ |\ d(a,b)=d(a,c)+d(c,b)\}.

We provides a collection of examples of median spaces (cf.
[
11
]
for more examples).

Example 2.2
.

•

A real tree is a median space. Moreover, it is the only case where the intervals coincide with the geodesics.

•

The 1-skeleton of a CAT(0) cube complex endowed with a combinatorial metric is a median graph. By
[
13
]
, any median simplicial graph arises as the 1- skeleton of a CAT(0) cube complex.

•

The space of
L^{1}
-integrable functions over a measurable space
(A,\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a median space
[
11
]
.

•

By
[
6
]
, any asymptotic cone of a coarse median group admits a natural median structure. Hence, the asymptotic cones of hyperbolic groups, mapping class groups are median spaces.

As for CAT(0) cube complexes, a key feature of median geometry is the concept of halfspaces. Recall that a subset
A\subseteq X
in a median space
X
is called
convex
if
[a,b]\subseteq A
, whenever
a,b\in A
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a median space. A subset
\mathfrak{h}\in X
is a
halfspace
if both
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{c}
are convex.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}(X)
the subset of halfspaces of
X
. Note that the subset
\mathcal{H}(X)
is not empty. In fact, the following Separation Theorem implies that there are enough halfspaces in
X
, in the sense that for any two disjoint convex subsets in a median space, there is a halfspace to separate them.

Theorem 2.4

(Separation Theorem,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
X
be a median space, then for any two disjoint convex subsets
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq X
, there exists a halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
such that
C_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
and
C_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{c}
.

In general, median spaces are not uniquely geodesic spaces. However, convex subsets are rigid enough to impose some nice property on the geometry of the space. In particular, they verify the Helly property.

Theorem 2.5

(Helly’s property,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
X
be a median space and
C_{1},...,C_{n}
be pairwise-intersecting convex subsets in
X
. Then the intersection
\displaystyle{\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}}C_{i}
is not empty.

The separation theorem and Helly’s property constitute the cornerstone of the median geometry. They are the key ingredient in the construction of a natural compactification (bordification) of the median spaces through halfspaces (see, for instance,
[
28
]
and
[
17
]
). For a subset
A\subset X
, we use both
\mathring{A}
and
int(A)
to denote the interior of A.

Definition 2.6

(Half-space interval)
.

Let
X
be a median space.

•

For any subsets
A,B\subseteq X
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ B\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\ \&\ A\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{c}\}

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ B\subseteq int(\mathfrak{h})\ \&\ A\subseteq int(\mathfrak{h}^{c})\}

For singletons we simply write
\mathcal{H}(x,y)=\mathcal{H}(\{x\},\{y\})
and
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(\{x\},\{y\})
. If
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(A,B)
we say that
\mathfrak{h}

separates

B
from
A
.

•

A halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq X
is said to be
thick
if
int(\mathfrak{h})\neq\emptyset
and
int(\mathfrak{h}^{c})\neq\emptyset
.

•

Two disjoint halfspaces
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
are
strongly separated
if there are no halfspaces transverse to them.

The set of halfspaces is canonically endowed with a structure of measured space
(\mathcal{H}(X),\mathcal{B},\nu)
where
\mathcal{B}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{\mathcal{H}(x,y)\}_{x,y\in X}
and the measure
\nu
verifies a Crofton’s like formula
\nu(\mathcal{H}(x,y))=d(x,y)
(cf.
[
11
]
and
[
17
]
).

Any median space
X
comes with a ternary operation
m_{X}:X^{3}\rightarrow X
that associates to each triple of points their median point. When two variables of
m_{X}
are fixed, it corresponds exactly to the nearest point projection onto intervals as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
X
be a complete median space, and
C\subseteq X
a closed convex subset.

•

The nearest point projection onto
C
, denoted by
\pi_{C}
is a well-defined
1
-Lipschitz map from
X
to
C
, and satisfies
\pi_{C}(x)\in[x,c]
for any
x\in X
and
c\in C
.

•

If
C=[a,b]
, the nearest point projection
\pi_{C}
is exactly the map

m(a,b,*):x\mapsto m(a,b,x).

Moreover, intervals in median spaces are closed and convex.

Note that the interval between
a,b\in X
can also be defined by the mean of the median operation as the subset of fixed points under the map
m(a,b,*)
.

Later in the paper, we will consider a compactification of the median space called the Roller compactification. The latter does not have a canonical median metric, but does come with a very useful canonical structure of median algebra that is defined as follows:

Definition 2.8

(Median algebras)
.

A median algebra
(M,m)
is a set
M
endowed with a ternary operation
m:M\times M\times M\to M
satisfying:

\displaystyle m(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x;

\displaystyle m(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(y,x,z)=m(x,z,y);

\displaystyle m(m(x,y,z),t,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(x,m(y,t,w),m(z,t,w)).

Note that any metric median space has a natural structure of a median algebra given by the ternary operation (see
[
31
]
for the equivalence of the axioms). We remark that the separation theorem and Helly’s property come from the structure of median algebra.

The rank of a median algebra reflects the “dimension” of the median algebra. Recall that a halfspace
\mathfrak{h}
is
transverse
to a convex subset
C
if both
\mathfrak{h}\cap C
and
\mathfrak{h}^{c}\cap C
are nonempty. We say that two halfspaces
\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2}\subseteq M
are
transverse
if the following intersections are nonempty:

\mathfrak{h}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{2},\ \mathfrak{h}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*},\ \mathfrak{h}_{1}^{*}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{2},\ \mathfrak{h}_{1}^{*}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}.

A family of pairwise transverse halfspaces gives rise to an embedded
0
-skeleton of an
n
-cube through a median morphim, i.e. a morphism that preserves the median operation. Hence, the transversality of halfspaces gives a natural way to characterize dimension in median algebras.

Definition 2.9

(Rank)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that a median algebra
M
is of
rank

n
if
n
is the maximal number of pairwise transverse halfspaces. If such
n\in\mathbb{N}
exists, we call
M
a
median algebra of finite rank
.

The reader is referred to
[
6
]
for other equivalent definitions of the rank of median algebras. The rank of a median space is defined as the rank of the associated median algebra. Median spaces of finite rank include, for instance, real trees and the 0-skeleton of finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube complexes endowed with the combinatorial metric.

We now collect some facts about median algebras that will be used in the sequel. For median algebras, even if one does not necessarily have a notion of distance, one has an analogue of the nearest-point projection.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
M
be a median algebra. A
retract

C\subseteq M
is a convex subset endowed with a retraction
\pi_{C}:M\rightarrow C
such that for any
x\in M
and any
c\in C
,

\pi_{C}(x)\in[x,c].

Proposition 2.11

(Lemma 2.13
[
11
]
and Lemma 2.6
[
17
]
)
.

If
X
is a median space or a compact topological median algebra, then a convex subset is a retract if and only if it is closed.

The
convex hull
of a subset
A\subseteq X
, denoted by
Conv(A)
, is the smallest convex subset containing
A
. The convex hull between two convex subsets
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq X
, denoted by
Conv(C_{1},C_{2})
, is the convex hull of their union.

Proposition 2.12

(Proposition 2.12
[
28
]
)
.

If
M
is a median algebra and
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq M
are two retract subsets. Then the convex hull
Conv(C_{1},C_{2})
is also a retract of
M
.

As a consequence of the previous two propositions, the convex hull between two closed convex subsets is also closed.

Lemma 2.13
.

If
X
is a median algebra and let
C\subseteq X
be a retract subset. Then for any convex subset
C^{\prime}\subseteq X
such that
C\cap C^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, we have
\pi_{C}(C^{\prime})\subseteq C\cap C^{\prime}
.

In particular, we have
\pi_{C}(C^{\prime})=C\cap C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
c^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
and
c\in C\cap C^{\prime}
. Since
C^{\prime}
is convex, we have
\pi_{C}(c^{\prime})\in[c,c^{\prime}]\subseteq C^{\prime}
which implies that
\pi_{C}(c^{\prime})\in C\cap C^{\prime}
.
∎

The following proposition is needed to ensure the measurability of some subsets and maps that we will encounter in the next subsections.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
X
be a complete separable median space of finite rank and let
C\subseteq X
be a proper convex subset. Then the subset of minimal closed convex subsets containing
C
is a non empty countable subset.

Let us prove the above proposition in a larger setting.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
X
be a complete separable median space and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(X)
be a familly of open halfspaces such that for any
\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, the halfspaces
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
are either disjoint or transverse. If there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains no more than
n
pairwise transverse halfspaces, then
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is countable.

Proof.

Since
X
is separable, fix a dense countable subset
Y\subseteq X
and proceed by induction on the number of maximal pairwise transverse halfspaces in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. If all halfspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are disjoint, i.e., n = 1, then
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is countable as we have a natural surjection from Y to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

Let us assume that the proposition is true for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and prove that it is true for
n+1
. We assume that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains no
n+1
halfspaces which are pairwise transverse. Assuming Zorn’s lemma, consider a maximal subset
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
consisting of pairwise disjoint halfspaces. Note that the subset
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
is countable by the same argument as in the first paragraph of the proof. By the maximality of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
, any
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
, is transverse to some
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
. Hence we have

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t},

where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t}
is the subset of halfspaces in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
that are transverse to
\mathfrak{h}
. Hence, it is enough to show that each
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t}
is countable. The familly
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t}
contains no
n
halfspaces that are pairwise transverse. By the induction hypothesis, we conclude that each
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t}
is countable, which completes the proof.

∎

2.2.
Roller compactification

There is a natural compactification for median spaces called the Roller compactification introduced by Fioravanti in
[
17
]
. Let
X
be a complete median space of finite rank. We have the following injective embedding of median algebra

\displaystyle i:X

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(X):=\prod_{a,b\in X}[a,b].

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(m(x,a,b))_{a,b\in X}

Intervals in finite rank median spaces are compact, hence the median algebra
\mathcal{I}
endowed with the product topology is a compact median algebra. The
Roller compactification

\overline{X}
of
X
is defined to be the closure of
i(X)
in
\mathcal{I}(X)
. The boundary
\partial X=\overline{X}-i(X)
of
i(X)
in
\mathcal{I}(X)
is called the
Roller boundary
of
X
.

Theorem 2.16

(
[
17
]
)
.

The space
\overline{X}
is a compact topological median algebra.

The median operation on
\overline{X}
is denoted by
\tilde{m}
. This compactification comes naturally with a monomorphisms of poc sets
i_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{H}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(\overline{X})
which associates to each halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
the following halfspace in the Roller compactification:

\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}:=i_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathfrak{h})=\{x\in\overline{X}\ |\ \forall\ a\in\mathfrak{h},\forall\ b\in\mathfrak{h}^{c},\ \tilde{m}(x,a,b)\in\mathfrak{h}\}

Remark that the intersection of
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
with an interval
[\tilde{a},\tilde{b}]
where
a,b\in X
is exactly the image of
i(\mathfrak{h}\cap[a,b])
as we have
[\tilde{a},\tilde{b}]=i([a,b])
(see Corollary 4.4 (1)
[
17
]
).

Proposition 2.17
.

For any halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
and
a,b\in X
such that
a\in\mathfrak{h}
and
b\in\mathfrak{h}^{c}
, we have
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\tilde{\pi}_{[a,b]}^{-1}(\mathfrak{h}\cap[a,b])

\tilde{\pi}_{[a,b]}
is the retraction on
\overline{X}
to

In particular, any halfspace of
\overline{X}
that is transverse to

is of the form
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
for some
\mathfrak{h}\in X
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the fact that the inverse image of a halfspace under an median morphism is also a halfspace.

The following lemma states that any pair of distinct points in the Roller compactification are separated by the lift of a halfspace of

Lemma 2.18

For any
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\overline{X}

\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\overline{X}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})

We write

i(x_{I})=\tilde{\pi}_{I}(\tilde{x})

(we abuse the notation by identifying

\tilde{\pi}_{I}(\tilde{x})
and
\tilde{\pi}_{I}(\tilde{y})

under

gives rise to a halfspace

Proposition 2.20

The topology induced on

Before proving the above proposition, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.21

Let

Proof.

Let us fix a point

\mathcal{H}_{x}^{t}:=\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ \mathfrak{h}\ \text{minimal and closed with}\ d(x,\mathfrak{h})\leq t\}

Proof of Proposition

The result comes from Lemma

Let

We recall the definition of the regular boundary of an irreducible median space, which is the part of the boundary of
\overline{X}
that encodes the tree-like behavior of the median space.

Definition 2.23
.

Let
X
be a complete irreducible median space of finite rank. A point
x\in\partial X
is said to be
regular
if there exists a sequence of halfspaces
(\mathfrak{h}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have

•

x\in\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
,

•

\mathfrak{h}_{i+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{i}
,

•

\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{i+1}
are strongly separated (see Definition
2.6
), with
d(\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{c},\mathfrak{h}_{i+1})>r
for some
r>0
that does not depends on the index
i
.

We call the sets of regular points the
regular boundary
and denote it by
\partial_{r}X
.

Under mild regularities assumptions on the action of the isometry group of the median space, the median space contains uncountably many regular points. The regularity assumption ensures the existence of many contracting elements in the isometry group, and the limit set of such contracting isometries is constituted of regular points. The regularity of an action is essentially characterized by the following definitions.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
X
be a complete median space of finite rank that is not a single point. We say that an isometric action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is

•

Roller minimal
if there is no proper
\Gamma
-invariant closed convex subset in the Roller compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
.

•

Roller non elementary
if there is no finite orbit in the Roller compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
.

Remark 2.25
.

Let
X
be a complete irreducible median space of finite rank. If the action
Isom(X)\curvearrowright X
is Roller minimal and Roller non elementary, then
\partial_{r}X
is not empty (see Remark
2.28
), and in fact, it is not difficult to see that it is uncountable.

One particularity of the regular boundary is that the median point of any triple of distinct points in it lies inside the median space, as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
X
be a complete finite rank median space and
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\partial_{r}X
two distinct regular points in the regular boundary. Then for any
\tilde{z}\in\overline{X}\backslash\{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\}
, we have
m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in X
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{h}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\mathfrak{t}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be descending sequences of open halfspaces of
X
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{i+1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{c}
(respectively
\mathfrak{t}_{i=1}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{i}^{c}
) are strongly separated, with
\displaystyle{\tilde{x}=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}}
and
\displaystyle{\tilde{y}=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\mathfrak{t}}_{i}}
. Fix
\tilde{z}\in\overline{X}\backslash\{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\}
and assume without loss of generality that
z\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{c}\cap\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{c}
. Consider then sequences of points
(\tilde{x}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}}
and
(\tilde{y}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}}
such that
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}_{i}\backslash\mathfrak{h}_{i+1}
and
y_{i}\in\mathfrak{t}_{i}\backslash\mathfrak{t}_{i+1}
. Remark that
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{c}}(x_{i})=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{c}}(x_{j})
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{c}}(y_{i})=\pi_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{c}}(y_{j})
, for any
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. Hence, we get

m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{y}_{i})=m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{j},\tilde{y}_{j})=m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{y}_{1})\in X.

On the other hand, by the continuity of the median operation, we conclude that

m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\lim_{i\rightarrow+\infty}m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{y}_{i})=m(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{y}_{1})\in X.

∎

The idea of generating rank 1 elements from elements that “skewer” through two strongly separated halfspaces goes back to Caprace and Sageev
[
9
]
, where they proved rank rigidity theorem for CAT(0) cube complexes. In
[
15
]
, Fioravanti extended the machinery to the case of median spaces.

Proposition 2.27

(Flipping Lemma (Corollary 5.4
[
15
]
))
.

Let
X
be a complete irreducible median space of finite rank such that
Isom(X)
acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally. Then for any thick halfspace
\mathfrak{h}
, there exists
g\in Isom(X)
such that
g.\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{c}
. We say that
g

flips
the halfspace
\mathfrak{h}
.

Note that here we slightly change the definition of flipping to allow the case
g.\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{c}
, as in the irreducible case one can show that, under the Roller minimality condition, any halfspace is flippable in the original sense (i.e. there exists
g\in Isom(X)
such that
d(g.\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})>0
).

Remark 2.28
.

Let
X
be an irreducible complete median space of finite rank such that
Isom(X)
acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally.

If
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
are two strongly separated halfspaces and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Isom(X)
are two elements that flip
\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{c}
, respectively. Then the isometry
g_{1}\circ g_{2}
is a rank 1 element which its “translation axe” skewers through
\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{c}
and
g.\mathfrak{h}_{1}
. Its contracting and repulsive points are respectively given by

\displaystyle\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}g^{i}.\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}g^{-i}.\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{c}_{1},

and are regular points.

The following is a useful characterization of the regular points in
\overline{X}
.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
\tilde{x}\in\partial X
,
x_{0}\in X
,
r>0
and assume that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\mathfrak{h}_{1},..,\mathfrak{h}_{n}
such that
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{c}
,
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
where
\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{i+1}
are strongly separated with
d(\mathfrak{h}_{i}^{c},\mathfrak{h}_{i+1})>r
for any
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

Then,
\tilde{x}
is a regular point.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
be the set of sequences of halfspaces satisfying the conditions stated in the lemma. We endow
\mathfrak{H}
with the order given by the inclusion. Note that
\mathfrak{H}
is an inductive set. By the definition of the regular point, it suffices to show that there exists a sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
that is infinite. Hence, to prove the lemma it is enough to prove that maximal elements in
\mathfrak{H}
are infinite, which is equivalent to show that any finite element in
\mathfrak{H}
can be extended. Let us consider a sequence
(\mathfrak{h}_{1},..,\mathfrak{h}_{n})
and note that
d(x_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{n})\geq(n-1)r
. According to the hypothesis of the lemma, there exists a sequence
(\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{\prime},...,\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{H}
where
N\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
(N-2)r>d(x_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{n})
, that is,
d(x_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{N-1}^{\prime})>d(x_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{n})
. As both
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{N-1}^{\prime}
contain
\tilde{x}
,
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{N-1}^{\prime}
are either transverse or
\mathfrak{h}_{N-1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. If
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{N-1}^{\prime}
are transverse, then we have
\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{n}
as
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime c}_{N-1}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{N}
are strongly separated. In either case, we have
\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. Hence,
(\mathfrak{h}_{1},..,\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{\prime})
extends the sequence
(\mathfrak{h}_{1},..,\mathfrak{h}_{n})
in
\mathfrak{H}
which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.30
.

For any pair of distinct points
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\overline{X}
, there exists a pair of points
x,y\in X
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\overline{X}}(x,y)\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{\overline{X}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\subseteq i_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}(X))

Note that for any

, we have
\mathcal{H}_{\overline{X}}(x,y)=i(\mathcal{H}(x,y))
(Proposition

\tilde{x}
and

being distinct, there exists an interval

where

, such that

and

Remark that we have the following inclusion

Let us consider a closed convex neighborhood

2.3.
Countable
Isom(X)
-invariant median subspace and fundamental family

The aim of this section is to construct a countable
Isom(X)
-invariant median subspace and a countable dense family of halfspaces. These sets are useful in showing measurability in the sequel. The density of the set of halfspaces is in the following sense.

Definition 2.31
.

Let
X
be a median space of finite rank. A
fundamental family of halfspaces

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(X)
is a countable family of halfspaces such that

•

For any
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\overline{X}
, there exists
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\overline{X}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
.

•

For any thick halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
, there exists
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.

We proceed as follows: from a dense countable subset of
X
, we construct a dense countable family of halfspaces. Using the latter family, we prove that the intersection of the median hull of the regular boundary with
X
is countable.

Proposition 2.32
.

Any complete separable median space
X
of finite rank contains a fundamental family of halfspaces.

Proof.

Fix a dense countable subset
Y\subseteq X
. For each
x\in Y
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
we set the following subset of halfspaces

\mathcal{H}_{x,n}:=\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ Conv(B(x,\frac{1}{n}))\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\ \text{with}\ \mathfrak{h}\ \text{closed and minimal}\}

The subsets
\mathcal{H}_{x,n}
are countable by the separability of
X
(Proposition
2.15
). We set

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in Y\\
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{x,n}

and claim that the subset
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a fundamental family.

Let us first show that any interval that is distinct from a point is separated by a halfspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

We fix
\tilde{x}\neq\tilde{y}\in\overline{X}
. Then there exists an interval
I=[a,b]
with
a,b\in X
, such that
x:=\pi_{I}(\tilde{x})
is distinct from
y:=\pi_{I}(\tilde{y})
. Let us consider a point
y^{\prime}\in Y
which is close enough to
y
(if
y
is isolated then
y\in Y
, in which case we choose
y^{\prime}
to be
y
) and consider
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
such that
C:=\overline{Conv(B(y^{\prime},\frac{1}{n}))}
contains
y
and does not contain
x
. Then there exists a halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{y^{\prime},n}
which separates
C
from
x
, that is
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(x,y)
. This implies
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\overline{X}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
and finishes the first claim of the proposition.

Let us prove the second claim. As the halfspace
\mathfrak{h}
is thick, there exists
x\in Y\cap\mathfrak{h}^{c}
. Any halfspace that separates
x
from
\pi_{\overline{\mathfrak{h}}}(x)
separates
x
from
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
. Hence, any halfspace in
\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\overline{\mathfrak{h}}}(x),x)
contains
\mathfrak{h}
. By the first claim, the family
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
intersects any interval, particularly
[\pi_{\overline{\mathfrak{h}}}(x),x]
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.35

•

G-boundaries and cocycles

3.1.
Cocycles in the isometry group of median spaces

We now introduce necessary backgrounds on cocycles, focusing on cocycles which take values on the isometry group of median spaces. The main goal is to give a geometric description of the non elementarity condition on the cocycle by the mean of the non elementarity of the induced action on such median space. One can skip this subsection and take Definition
3.1
as the definition of the non elementarity. The reader is referred to
[
3
]
,
[
19
]
for related materials.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group and let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a standard probability space endowed with a measurable measure-preserving ergodic
G
-action. When
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, we say that the action is irreducible if both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
act ergodically on
\Omega
.

Let
X
be a complete median space of finite rank and let

\alpha:G\times\Omega\rightarrow Isom(X)

be a
measurable cocycle
, that is,
\alpha
is a measurable map such that for almost all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
\omega\in\Omega
we have
\alpha(g_{1}.g_{2},\omega)=\alpha(g_{1},g_{2}.\omega).\alpha(g_{2},\omega)
. Two such cocycles
\alpha
and
\beta
are said to be
cohomologous or equivalent
if there is a measurable map
f:\Omega\to Isom(X)
such that
\forall g\in G,\alpha(g,\omega)=f(g.\omega)^{-1}\beta(g,\omega)f(\omega)
for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
.

We say that a section
f:\Omega\rightarrow X
is
\alpha
-equivariant
if for almost all
g\in G,\omega\in\Omega
we have

f(g.\omega)=\alpha(g,\omega).f(\omega).

In general, Roller minimality and Roller non elementarity are independent conditions. However, if the median space is not lineal, then Roller minimality implies Roller non elementarity (Proposition 6.4
[
24
]
). A median space is
lineal
if its Roller compactification is an interval. Note that if a median space is lineal, then any action is Roller elementary. In a non lineal median space the assumption of the flipping lemma with regard to an action is equivalent to the assumption on the action being Roller non elementary and Roller minimal. This motivates the following unified definition of Roller minimality and Roller non elementarity for cocycles (see Section 3
[
19
]
and Appendix 7.b
[
4
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a complete non lineal median space of finite rank.
We say that a cocycle
\alpha:G\times\Omega\rightarrow Isom(X)
is
non elementary
if for any halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
and any subset
A\subseteq\Omega
of positive measure, there exists
g\in G
and
\omega\in A\cap g^{-1}.A
such that
\alpha(g,\omega).\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{c}
.

Let us remark in what follows that in the case where the cocycle arises as the returning cocycle with regard to an action of a lattice, then the non elementarity coincides with the Roller non elementarity and Roller minimality of the action.

Let
\Gamma\leq G
be a lattice and let us fix a right fundamental domain
\mathfrak{D}\subseteq G
for
\Gamma
. Endow the fundamental domain with the restriction of the Haar measure of
G
normalized such that the volume of
\mathfrak{D}
is one and consider the ergodic
G
-action on it given through the identification between
\mathfrak{D}
and
G/\Gamma
. The returning cocycle
\alpha_{\mathfrak{D}}:G\times\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow\Gamma
associates to each
g\in G
and
\omega\in\mathfrak{D}
the unique
\alpha_{\mathfrak{D}}(g,\omega)\in\Gamma
such that
g.\omega.(\alpha(g,\omega))^{-1}\in\mathfrak{D}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be an irreducible non lineal median space of finite rank and let
\pi:\Gamma\rightarrow Isom(X)
be a representation of
\Gamma
.

The cocycle
\pi\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{D}}
is non elementary if and only if the
\Gamma
-action is Roller minimal.

Proof.

Let us set
\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{D}}:=\pi\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{D}}
and remark that if
\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{D}}
is non elementary, then any thick halfspace is flippable by some element of
\pi(\Gamma)
.

Let us assume now that the
\Gamma
-action is Roller minimal and consider a thick halfspace
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)
with a subset
A\subseteq\mathfrak{D}
of positive measure. There exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma.\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{c}
. As
G.A=G
, there exists
g\in G
such that
g.A\cap A.\gamma^{-1}\neq\emptyset
. Hence, for any
\omega\in A\cap g^{-1}.A.\gamma^{-1}
, we have
\alpha_{\mathfrak{D}}(g,\omega)=\gamma
which completes the proof.
∎

4.
The barycenter map

The aim of the section is to show that one can associate measurably a “center of mass” to each probability measure on the Roller compactification
\overline{X}
of a median space
X
. It is a essential ingredient in the construction of the boundary map, see Section
5
. The way to characterize the “center of mass” is through halfspaces. Each halfspace having measure bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
contains the center of mass. The center of mass is then defined to be the intersection of all closed halfspaces having a measure bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, the center of mass is a closed convex subset of
\overline{X}
. More concretely, let
X
be a complete separable median space of finite rank on which
Isom(X)
acts Roller non elementarily and Roller minimally. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
the space of probability measures on
\overline{X}
endowed with the Borel structure with regard to the weak
∗
convergence. Using the same notation as in
[
12
]
, we associate to each probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
the following subsets of halfspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{+}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ \mu(\mathfrak{h})>\frac{1}{2}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{e}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}(X)\ |\ \mu(\mathfrak{h})=\frac{1}{2}\}

and define the center of mass to be

C_{\mu}:=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{+}\\
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\ \text{closed}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}

where
\tilde{h}
is the canonical halfspace of
\overline{X}
associated to
\mathfrak{h}
(see subsection
2.2
). Note that, by Helly’s property and by the compactness of
\overline{X}
, the center of mass

is a non empty closed convex subset.

The subset of measures that will be of interest are those that have a single point as a center of mass. We denote this subset of measures by

\mathcal{P}_{1}(\overline{X}):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})\ |\ C_{\mu}\ \text{is a singleton}\ \}

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\overline{X})
the complement of
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\overline{X})
in
\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
, that is, the subset of measures having a non singleton barycenter.

In the next sections, we will show that the boundary map will take image in fact inside
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\overline{X})
. To do so, we need the following criterion to determine when
C_{\mu}
is a singleton for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X})
. The closed convex subset

is a singleton if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{e}
contains no halfspace-intervals, that is, there is no pair
x\neq y
in
X
such that
\mathcal{H}(x,y)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{+}
.

Let us separately prove the first direction of Proposition
4.1
in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in C_{\mu}
, we have
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{e}
.

In particular, Lemma
2.30
shows that there exists
x,y\in X
such that
\mathcal{H}(x,y)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{e}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.30
, any halfspace in
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
is of the form
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
where
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)

. Let

be such halfspace. By Corollary 2.23
[
17
]
, at least one of
\mathfrak{h}
and

is closed. Hence, by the construction of

, there is no closed halfspace in

and

, which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition

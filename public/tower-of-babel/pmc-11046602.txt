Hormone therapy in the management of stage four (asymptomatic metastatic) breast cancer in women
Authors: Suvam Banerjee
Published: 2023

Background

Breast cancer is the world's second leading cause of cancer deaths in women (it is far less common in men) and hence screening for early diagnosis and treatment is important to prevent breast cancer deaths. Here we compare hormone therapy with chemotherapy for managing stage four breast cancer (asymptomatic metastasis).1

Methods

We conducted a prospective, randomised, controlled open study label. Ethical approval was obtained along with written informed consent from all participants. The study was conducted from January 2017 till December 2020.

The inclusion criteria specified women aged >40 years with TNM stage 4 biopsy-proven breast cancer and literacy of standard 4 or higher. Exclusion criteria were other associated malignancies and psychiatric illnesses.

Eligible patients were randomised into the hormone therapy group (oral tamoxifen 20 mg daily orally) and the chemotherapy group, which received the CMF regimen (cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, fluorouracil).2 Follow-up was carried out every 3 months, including regular physical examinations and medical tests before and after intervention.

Parameters included blood tests and tumour markers in addition to annual mammography with a standard point scale.3 Quality of life was measured on day 1 and at the end of December 2020 using the abbreviated EORTC QLQBR23 questionnaire.

Results

54 women were assessed for eligibility and 40 entered randomisation. Of the 12 who were excluded, seven did not meet the eligibility criteria and five were not willing to participate. 20 women were assigned to hormone therapy, of whom two were lost to follow-up and 18 were analysed. 20 women were assigned to hormone therapy, of whom one was lost to follow-up and 19 were analysed. The results favoured towards hormone therapy.

Conclusion

Hormone treatment was found to be more effective in asymptomatic metastic breast cancer compared to chemotherapy (as found in previous studies4). However, further studies are recommended for validation.

The strength of this study was that it provided a randomised head-to-head comparison. The limitations were its sample size and its lack of blinding.

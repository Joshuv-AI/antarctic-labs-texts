Asymptotic behavior of penalty dynamics for constrained variational inequalities
Juan Peypouquet, Siqi Qu, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
We propose a comprehensive framework for solving constrained variational inequalities via various classes of evolution equations displaying multi-scale aspects. In an infinite-dimensional Hilbertian framework, the class of dynamical systems we propose combine Tikhonov regularization and exterior penalization terms in order to induce strong convergence of trajectories to least norm solutions in the constrained domain. Our construction thus unifies the literature on regularization methods and penalty-based dynamical systems. An extension to a full splitting formulation of the constrained domain is also provided, with associated weak convergence results involving the Attouch-Czarnecki condition.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and corresponding norm
\norm{\cdot}
.

Problem 1
.

We are given

•

\mathsf{A}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
a maximally monotone operator;

•

\mathsf{D}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is monotone and
\frac{1}{\eta}
-Lipschitz;

•

\mathcal{C}\triangleq\zer(\mathsf{B})\neq\varnothing
a nonempty closed convex set which can be represented as the zeros of a cocoercive operator
\mathsf{B}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
.

Our aim is to find
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

0\in\Phi(x)\triangleq\mathsf{A}(x)+\mathsf{D}(x)+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x).

(P)

This is a three-operator formulation of a general class of variational problems, in which a constrained equilibrium of the sum of two maximally monotone operators
\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}
is requested over a closed convex domain
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
. In this paper we approach this problem via a combination of an exterior penalization method and Tikhonov regularization, essentially leading to a hierarchical equilibrium formulation. A key assumption in our approach is thus that the feasible set
\mathcal{C}
admits a simple representation in terms of the set of zeros of another single-valued monotone operator
\mathsf{B}
. This general formulation admits many applications in optimal control and optimization, in particular those problems featuring a hierarchical structure. We briefly describe some examples below, and provide a more detailed exposition on applications in Section
5
.

Example 1

(Simple Bilevel Optimization)
.

A simple bilevel optimization problem is formulated as

\displaystyle\min f(x)

s.t.:

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{C}=\argmin\{\Psi(y):y\in\mathcal{H}\}

where
\Psi
is a convex and Fréchet differentiable function, with
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, i.e.
\Psi\in{\mathbf{C}}^{1,1}_{L}(\mathcal{H})
. By Fermat’s optimality condition

x\in\mathcal{C}\Leftrightarrow 0=\nabla\Psi(x).

Hence,
\mathcal{C}=\zer(\nabla\Psi)
defines the set of solutions to the lower-level optimization problem over which a minimizer of the function
f
is searched for.
These are simplest hierarchical optimization problems, in which the decision variables in the lower and an upper level variational problems are completely decoupled. This class of problems has been studied extensively. See, for instance
Xu and Kim (2003)
;
Ogura and Yamada (2002)
;
Boţ and Nguyen (2019)
in an infinite-dimensional setting, or
Sabach and Shtern (2017)
in finite dimension. For a recent dynamical systems perspective with acceleration, we refer to
Boţ et al. (2025)

Example 2

(Constrained Variational Inequalities)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a set for which we know a convex function
\Psi\in{\mathbf{C}}^{1,1}_{L_{\Psi}}(\mathcal{H})
having the properties that
\Psi\geq 0
and
\mathcal{C}=\Psi^{-1}(0)
. Then,
\mathsf{B}=\nabla\Psi
is a maximally monotone operator whose zero set is
\mathcal{C}
. Given a mapping
\mathsf{D}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and a proper convex and lower semi-continuous function
h:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, we search for a solution of the variational inequality of the second kind

\text{Find $\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}$ such that }\langle\mathsf{D}(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle+h(x)-h(\bar{x})\geq 0\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{C}.

(1)

This is equivalent to the inclusion problem

0\in\mathsf{D}(\bar{x})+\partial h(\bar{x})+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x),

and arises frequently in optimal control problems
Barbu (1984)
;
De los Reyes (2011)
;
De los Reyes and Meyer (2016)
.

Our approach

To tackle problem class (
P
), we follow a recent trend in variational analysis by approaching the general resolution of problem (
P
) via dynamical systems derived from operator splitting methods featuring multiscale aspects. Our schemes can be considered as hybrid versions of penalty-based methods, inspired by
Attouch and Czarnecki (2010)
;
Attouch et al. (2011)
;
Banert and
Boţ (2015)
;
Noun and Peypouquet (2013)
;
Boţ and Csetnek (2016)
;
Boţ and Csetnek (2014)
, and Tikhonov regularization of dynamical systems
Attouch and
Cominetti (1996)
;
Cominetti et al. (2008)
;
Boţ et al. (2020)
.
Specifically, if
\mathsf{D}
is cocoercive, we propose a forward-backward dynamical system of the form

\dot{x}(t)+x(t)=\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}\left(x(t)-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x)\right),

(FB)

where
V_{t}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a time-dependent vector field, defined as

V_{t}(x)\triangleq\mathsf{D}(x)+\varepsilon(t)x+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}(x).

The evolution equation (
FB
) generalizes most of the first-order dynamical systems that have been studied so far in the literature. Indeed, if
\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}=\Id_{\mathcal{H}}
(arising if
\mathsf{A}=\{0\})
, then (
FB
) simplifies to

\dot{x}(t)+\lambda(t)(\mathsf{D}(x(t))+\varepsilon(t)x(t)+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}(x(t)))=0.

In the full potential case, i.e. when
\mathsf{D}=\nabla f
and
\mathsf{B}=\nabla\Psi
, for convex potentials
f,\Psi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
having a Lipschitz continuous gradient, with
\mathcal{C}=\Psi^{-1}(0)=\argmin(\Psi)=\zer(\nabla\Psi)
, we obtain an exterior penalization of the gradient method, as studied in
Peypouquet (2012)
, augmented with Tikhonov regularization, for solving set-constrained convex minimization problems of the form

\min f(x)\quad\text{s.t. }x\in\mathcal{C}.

Setting
\mathsf{B}=0
(no penalty), the dynamics reduce to a gradient system with Tikhonov regularization, a class of dynamical systems which has received enormous attention in the literature (references
Attouch (1996)
;
Alvarez and Cabot (2006)
;
Cominetti et al. (2008)
;
Attouch and Czarnecki (2010)
;
Baillon and Cominetti (2001)
;
Cabot (2005)
give a partial overview on this literature). In the context of full splitting and maximal monotone operators instead of potentials, particular instances of (
FB
) have been studied in
Boţ and Csetnek (2017)
without explicit constraints and Tikhonov regularization, in
Boţ and Csetnek (2016)
for the case with a penalty term but no Tikhonov regularization, and in
Boţ et al. (2020)
in the context of Tikhonov regularization and without penalty terms.

For large-scale equilibrium and minmax problems, the cocoercivity of the single-valued operator
\mathsf{D}
is typically an incompatible hypothesis. For such problems, we study an extragradient-based dynamical system in the spirit of Tseng
Tseng (2000)
, featuring multi-scale aspects. This dynamical system is defined in terms of the projection-differential dynamical system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}p(t)&=&\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}\left(x(t)-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x(t))\right)\\
\dot{x}(t)&=&p(t)-x(t)+\lambda(t)[V_{t}(x(t))-V_{t}(p(t))].\end{array}\right.

(FBF)

The asymptotic properties of this evolution equation with Tikhonov regularization has been studied in
Boţ et al. (2020)
, while the penalty case has been investigated in
Boţ and Csetnek (2014)
, although in discrete time. While we do not treat this problem directly here, it should be mentioned that our approach can be used to solve constrained min-max problems in infinite dimensions
Dai et al. (2024)
;
Ding et al. (2025)
. A thorough analysis of this important problem class in our multi-scale dynamical system framework is left for future research.

Contributions and related literature

Our dynamical system combines exterior-penalty methods with Tikhonov regularization. With this hybrid construction, we generalize the pure penalization-based dynamical systems studied in
Attouch et al. (2011)
;
Peypouquet (2012)
, who concentrate on the case where
\mathsf{B}
is the gradient of a convex function
\Psi:\mathcal{H}\to[0,\infty]
, satisfying
\mathcal{C}=\Psi^{-1}(0)=\argmin_{\mathcal{H}}(\Psi)
, and
Boţ and Csetnek (2014)
who extended this to the monotone inclusion setting. Adding the Tikhonov term to the dynamical system allows us to enforce strong convergence to the least-norm solution. We borrow ideas from
Boţ et al. (2020)
to analyze the effects of the Tikhonov term, and extend this technique to deal with constrained variational inequalities. To the best of our knowledge this is a new result in the literature since the analysis of penalty dynamics,
in tandem
with Tikhonov regularization, has not been done so far. In particular, this combined approach gains relevance in inverse problems and PDE constrained optimization; See
Guth et al. (2024)
for a recent approach in this direction.

Our work is closely related to the hierarchical minimimization formulation of Cabot
Cabot (2005)
. He proposed an inexact proximal point method for the hierarchical minimization of a finitely many convex potentials. Specifically, in the case of three convex functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
, the method of
Cabot (2005)
is designed for solving the nested optimization problems
S_{0}=\argmin_{\mathcal{H}}f_{0},S_{1}=\argmin_{S_{0}}f_{1}
and
S_{2}=\argmin_{S_{1}}f_{2}
. Under time rescaling and constraint qualification, the exact version of the proximal point method of
Cabot (2005)
thus becomes

-\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\in\beta_{n}\partial f_{0}+\partial f_{1}+\varepsilon_{n}\partial f_{2}.

In our work, we are interested in continuous-time systems and general monotone operators. Both directions lie out of the scope of
Cabot (2005)
.

In parallel to this work, a series of papers studied penalty methods and Tikhonov regularization from the lens of dynamical systems (separately). In particular,
Bot and Nguyen (2024)
establishes an interesting connection between the Tikhonov regularization and the Halpern schemes and thereby shows the acceleration potential of the Tikhonov regularization. Inertial dynamics with Tikhonov regularization have been studied in
Attouch et al. (2018)
;
Boţ et al. (2024)
, among many others.
Penalty dynamics have been extended to second order in time systems in
Boţ and Csetnek (2017)
;
Boţ et al. (2018)
.

Organization of the paper

After briefly recalling some known facts from convex analysis and monotone operator theory, Section
2
presents a detailed analysis of the
central paths
, which are curves parameterized by regularization variables, representing solutions to auxiliary monotone inclusion problems. In particular, we show that central paths are absolutely continuous, differentiable - an important ingredient in our proof building on Lyapunov analysis - and approximate the least-norm solution of (
P
). Section
3
is concerned with two dynamical systems intended to approximate solutions of (
P
). The nature of these systems, of either forward-backward or forward-backward-forward type, depends on whether the operator
\mathsf{D}
is cocoercive. For each system, and under suitable assumptions on the regularization parameters, we show that every trajectory convergence strongly to the least norm solution of (
P
). This is achieved by establishing a tracking property of the dynamics with respect to the central paths. Section
4
extends our analysis of the (
FB
) dynamical systems to a domain decomposition problem, modeled via multiple penalty functions. Section
5
describes some scenarios in optimal control and non-linear analysis to which our method naturally applies. We also present implementations on a class of image deblurring problems, to illustrate the computational efficacy of the method.

Preliminaries

We follow the standard notation from convex and non-smooth analysis (see e.g.
Bauschke and Combettes (2016)
;
Peypouquet (2015)
). Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and associated norm
\norm{\cdot}
. The symbols
\rightharpoonup
and
\to
denote weak and strong convergence, respectively. For an extended-real valued function
f:\mathcal{H}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}\triangleq\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
, we denote by
\dom(f)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}|f(x)<\infty\}
its effective domain and say that
f
is proper if
\dom(f)\neq\varnothing
and
f(x)\neq-\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. If
f
is convex, we let
\partial f(x)\triangleq\{u\in\mathcal{H}|f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle y-x,v\rangle\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\}
the subdifferential of
f
at
x\in\dom(f)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be a nonempty set. The indicator function
\delta_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{H}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
, is the function satisfying
\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=0
if
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
+\infty
otherwise. The subdifferential of the indicator function is the normal cone

\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}|\langle\xi,y-x\rangle\leq 0\;\forall y\in\mathcal{C}\}&\text{if }x\in\mathcal{C},\\
\varnothing&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

For a set-valued operator
\mathsf{M}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
we denote by
\gr(\mathsf{M})\triangleq\{(x,u)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}|u\in\mathsf{M}(x)\}
its graph,
\dom(\mathsf{M})\triangleq\{x\in\mathcal{H}|\mathsf{M}(x)\neq\varnothing\}
its domain, and by
\mathsf{M}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
its inverse, defined by

(u,x)\in\gr(\mathsf{M}^{-1})\Leftrightarrow(x,u)\in\gr(\mathsf{M}).
We let
\zer(\mathsf{M})=\{x\in\mathcal{H}|0\in\mathsf{M}(x)\}
denote the set of zeros of
\mathsf{M}
. A operator
\mathsf{M}
is
monotone
if
\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq 0
for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\gr(\mathsf{M})
. A monotone operator
\mathsf{M}
is maximally monotone if there exists no proper monotone extension of the graph of
\mathsf{M}
on
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
. A single-valued operator
\mathsf{M}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is
cocoercive
(inverse strongly monotone) if there exists
\mu>0
such that

\langle\mathsf{M}(x)-\mathsf{M}(y),x-y\rangle\geq\mu\norm{\opM(x)-\opM(y)}^{2}.

A cocoercive operator is Lipschitz, but the converse is not true in general.

Fact 1
.

(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2016
, Proposition 23.39)

If
\mathsf{M}
is maximally monotone, then
\zer(\mathsf{M})
is convex and closed.

Fact 2
.

If
\mathsf{M}
is maximally monotone, then

p\in\zer(\mathsf{M})\Leftrightarrow\langle u-p,w\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall(u,w)\in\gr(\mathsf{M}).

The resolvent of
\mathsf{M}
,
\res_{\mathsf{M}}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by
\res_{\mathsf{M}}=(\Id+\mathsf{M})^{-1}
. If
\mathsf{M}
is maximally monotone, then
\res_{\mathsf{M}}
is single-valued and maximally monotone. In particular, the resolvent of the normal cone mapping
\mathsf{M}=\NC_{C}
of a closed convex set
C\subset\mathcal{H}
is the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{C}
. The support function of a closed set
C\subset\mathcal{H}
is
\sigma_{C}(u)=\sup_{y\in C}\langle y,u\rangle
. Clearly,
p\in\NC_{C}(u)
if, and only, if
\sigma_{C}(p)=\langle p,u\rangle
. According to
(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2016
, Proposition 23.31)
, we have the relation

\norm{\res_{\lambda M}(x)-\res_{\alpha M}(x)}_{\lambda M}(x)-\res_{\alpha M}(x)\leq\absolutevalue{\lambda-\alpha}\cdot\norm{\opM_{\lambda}(x)},

(2)

where
\mathsf{M}_{\lambda}\triangleq\frac{1}{\lambda}(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\res_{\lambda\mathsf{M}})
denotes the
Yosida approximation
, with parameter
\lambda
, of the maximally monotone operator
\mathsf{M}
.
The Fitzpatrick function associated to a monotone operator
\mathsf{M}
is the convex and lower semi-continuous function defined as

\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}(x,u)=\sup_{(y,v)\in\gr(\mathsf{M})}\{\langle x,y\rangle+\langle y,u\rangle-\langle y,v\rangle\}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle 2\langle x-y,z-y\rangle=\norm{x-y}^{2}-\norm{x-z}^{2}+\norm{z-y}^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle\norm{\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y}^{2}+\alpha(1-\alpha)\norm{x-y}^{2}=\alpha\norm{x}^{2}+(1-\alpha)\norm{y}^{2}

(4)

To assess the global asymptotic stability of the dynamical systems we consider, we recall the following central result (see e.g.
Abbas et al. (2014)
, Lemma 5.1):

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally absolutely continuous and bounded below and that there exists
G\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
such that

\frac{\dif}{\dif t}F(t)\leq G(t)\qquad\text{a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Then
\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)
exists in
\mathbb{R}
.

2.1
Perturbed solutions and the central funnel

We follow a
double penalization
approach to solve the constrained variational inequality (
P
). We work under following standing assumption:

Assumption 1
.

The main set of standing hypothesis in this paper is the following:

(i)

\mathsf{A}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}
is maximally monotone and
\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})\neq\varnothing
.

(ii)

\mathsf{B}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is
\mu
-cocoercive (for some
\mu>0)
, and coercive:

\lim_{\norm{x}\to\infty}\frac{\langle x,\mathsf{B}(x)\rangle}{\norm{x}}=\infty

(iii)

\mathsf{D}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is maximally monotone and
\frac{1}{\eta}
-Lipschitz.

Since
\mathsf{A}
is maximally monotone and
\mathcal{C}
is a nonempty convex and closed set,
\mathsf{A}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}
is maximally monotone if additional regularity conditions are satisfied. A comprehensive list of conditions guaranteeing the maximal monotonicity of the sum of two maximally monotone operators can be found in
Borwein and
Vanderwerff (2010)
;
Bauschke and Combettes (2016)
. In particular, if
\dom(\mathsf{D})=\mathcal{H}
and
\dom(\mathsf{B})=\mathcal{H}
(which we assume throughout this manuscript), Corollary 25.5 of
Bauschke and Combettes (2016)
implies that the operators
\Phi=\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathsf{F}}_{n}=\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\beta_{n}\mathsf{B}
are maximally monotone.

To ensure strong convergence, we follow the classical iterative Tikhonov regularization approach. However, besides strong convergence, we have to respect the constraints imposed on the variational inequality problems. To enforce these constraints, we augment our operator by a time-varying penalty term which regulates over time the importance we attach to constraint violation of the generated trajectory. The combination of these two dynamic effects leads to study a family of
auxiliary problems
, formulated as:

Problem 2
.

Given
(\varepsilon,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{>0}
find
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

0\in\Phi_{\varepsilon,\beta}(x)\triangleq(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\varepsilon\Id_{\mathcal{H}}+\beta\mathsf{B})(x).

(5)

The logic behind this model is as follows: Suppose
0\in\Phi_{\varepsilon,\beta}(\hat{x}_{\varepsilon,\beta})
. On the one hand, the presence of the Tikhonov term
\varepsilon\Id_{\mathcal{H}}
will attract the vector
\hat{x}_{\varepsilon,\beta}
towards the origin and, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\hat{x}_{\varepsilon,\beta}
will approach the set of solutions of (
P
), selecting its least-norm element. On the other hand, the penalization term
\beta\mathsf{B}
will force
\mathsf{B}(\hat{x}_{\varepsilon,\beta})\to 0
, as
\beta\to\infty
. As a result, the limit of
\hat{x}_{\varepsilon,\beta}
will be a zero of
B
, thus an element of
\mathcal{C}
.

In the remainder of this section, we show that solutions of (
5
) do approximate the least-norm solution of (
P
), as
(\varepsilon,\beta)\to(0,\infty)
, and establish some important structural properties of this approximation. For
(\varepsilon,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
, we define the mapping
V_{\varepsilon,\beta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

V_{\varepsilon,\beta}(x)\triangleq\mathsf{D}(x)+\varepsilon x+\beta\mathsf{B}(x)

(6)

for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Assumption
1
immediately implies the following regularity properties of the parameterized family
(V_{\varepsilon,\beta})_{(\varepsilon,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}}.

Lemma 3
.

For each
(\varepsilon,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
, the mapping
x\mapsto V_{\varepsilon,\beta}(x)
is Lipschitz continuous with modulus
L_{\varepsilon,\beta}\triangleq\frac{1}{\eta}+\varepsilon+\frac{\beta}{\mu}
, and
\varepsilon
-strongly monotone. Furthermore,
\dom(V_{\varepsilon,\beta})=\mathcal{H}
.

Hence, the auxiliary problems (
5
) constitute a family of strongly monotone inclusions. Since
\Phi_{\varepsilon,\beta}
is strongly monotone, the set
\zer(\Phi_{\varepsilon,\beta})
is a singleton whose unique element we denote by
\bar{x}(\varepsilon,\beta)
. The function
(\varepsilon,\beta)\mapsto\bar{x}(\varepsilon,\beta)
maps the positive quadrant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a region in
\mathcal{H}
, which we call the
central funnel
for the reasons we will explain in the following.

2.2
Central paths approximate the least-norm zero of
\Phi

Given absolutely continuous functions
\varepsilon,\beta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon(t)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta(t)=\infty
, the curve
t\mapsto\bar{x}(\varepsilon(t),\beta(t))
is called the
central path
for the approximation of Problem (
P
) given by (
5
). Similarly, given positive sequences
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
\beta_{n}\to\infty
, the sequence
\big(\bar{x}(\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
(discrete) central path
. As shown below, every central path converges strongly to the least-norm zero of
\Phi
, which motivates our choice of the word funnel.

Proposition 4
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0,\beta_{n}\to+\infty
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}\beta_{n}=\infty
. Then
\bar{x}(\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n})\to\Pi_{\zer(\Phi)}(0)
as
n\to\infty
.

We prove this proposition via a sequence of Lemmas.

Lemma 5
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0,\beta_{n}\to+\infty
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}\beta_{n}=\infty
. Denote by
(\bar{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the discrete central path obtained by
\bar{x}_{n}=\bar{x}(\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n})
. Any weak limit point of
(\bar{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n})\to(0,\infty)
as
n\to\infty
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\bar{x}_{n}\equiv\bar{x}(\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n})
, the unique root of the set-valued mapping
\Phi_{n}\equiv\Phi_{\varepsilon_{n},\beta_{n}}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
. To simplify the notation, we define the set-valued operator
{\mathsf{F}}_{n}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
by

{\mathsf{F}}_{n}\triangleq\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\beta_{n}\mathsf{B}.

Hence, for each
n\geq 1
, we have the inclusion

\displaystyle 0\in

\displaystyle{\mathsf{F}}_{n}(\bar{x}_{n})+\varepsilon_{n}\bar{x}_{n}\Leftrightarrow(1-\varepsilon_{n})\bar{x}_{n}\in(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}+{\mathsf{F}}_{n})(\bar{x}_{n})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\bar{x}_{n}=\res_{{\mathsf{F}}_{n}}((1-\varepsilon_{n})\bar{x}_{n})

At the same time, for every
z\in\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
, it holds that
z\in\mathcal{C}
, and therefore
\beta_{n}\mathsf{B}(z)=0
for all
n\geq 1
. This implies that there exists
p\in\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(z)
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

z-p\in(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}+{\mathsf{F}}_{n})(z)\Leftrightarrow z=\res_{{\mathsf{F}}_{n}}(z-p).

Since
{\mathsf{F}}_{n}
is maximally monotone,
\res_{{\mathsf{F}}_{n}}
is firmly non-expansive. Hence,

\displaystyle\norm{\bar{x}_{n}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\res_{{\mathsf{F}}_{n}}((1-\varepsilon_{n}\bar{x}_{n}))-\res_{{\mathsf{F}}_{n}}(z-p),(1-\varepsilon_{n})\bar{x}_{n}-(z-p)\rangle

3
Penalty-regulated dynamical systems for constrained variational inequalities

Recall that a function

(where
b>0
) is said to be absolutely continuous if there exists an integrable function
g:[0,b]\to\mathcal{H}
such that

f(t)=f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\dif s\quad\forall t\in[0,b].

Definition 1
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a vector field depending on time and space, and let
(0,x_{0})\in[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}
be given. We say
x:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
is a strong global solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}(t)=f(t,x(t))\\
x(0)=x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\end{array}\right.

(D)

(i)

x:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
is absolutely continuous on each interval
[0,b],0<b<\infty
;

(ii)

\dot{x}(t)=f(t,x(t))
for almost every
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Existence and strong uniqueness of non-autonomous systems can be proven by means of the classical Cauchy-Lipschitz Theorem (see e.g.
(
Sontag, 2013
, Theorem 54)
). To use this, we need to ensure the following properties enjoyed by the vector field
f(t,x)
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a given function satisfying:

(f1)

f(\cdot,x):[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
is measurable for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
;

(f2)

f(t,\cdot):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is continuous for each
t\geq 0
;

(f3)

there exists a function
\ell(\bullet)\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R})
such that

\norm{f(t,x)-f(t,y)}\leq\ell(t)\norm{x-y}\qquad\forall t\in[0,b],\forall b\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H};

(16)

(f4)

for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
there exists a function
\Delta(\bullet)\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R})
such that

\norm{f(t,x)}\leq\Delta(t)\qquad\forall t\in[0,b],\forall b\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(17)

Then, the dynamical system (
D
) admits a unique strong solution

,

.

Under this additional assumption on the data, we investigate the asymptotic properties of the following evolution equation

Remark 1

The dynamical system (

) by including the time-scale parameter

Let

3.1.1
Existence and uniqueness of strong solutions

In this section we verify that the data defining the evolution equation (
18
) satisfy all conditions stated in Theorem
10
. Whence, existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for (
18
) follow.

Proposition 11
.

Consider the dynamical system (
18
), where the parameter function
\lambda:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is continuous, and Assumption
3
being in place. Then, for every
t\geq 0
and every
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\displaystyle\norm{f(t,x)-f(t,y)}\leq\gamma(t)(2+\lambda(t)L(t))\norm{x-y},\text{ and }

(19)

\displaystyle(\forall x\in\mathcal{H})(\forall b>0),\quad f(\,\cdot\,,x)\in L^{1}([0,b],\mathcal{H}).

(20)

Proof.

Properties
(f1),(f2)
are clearly satisfied. To simplify the verification of the remaining properties, we set
\res_{t}\equiv\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}
and
R_{t}(x)\triangleq x-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x)
, so that
T_{t}\triangleq\res_{t}\circ R_{t}
. The evolution equation (
18
) admits then the simpler representation
f(t,x)=T_{t}(x)-x
. Since the resolvent
\res_{t}
is firmly non-expansive, it follows that

\displaystyle\norm{f(t,x)-f(t,y)}

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)\norm{T_t(x)-x-T_t(y)+y}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\big(\norm{T_t(x)-T_t(y)}+\norm{x-y}\big)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)\big(\norm{J_{t}\circ R_{t}(x)-J_{t}\circ R_{t}(y)}+\norm{x-y}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\big(\norm{R_{t}(x)- R_{t}(y)}+\norm{x-y}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\big(\norm{x-\lambda(t)V_t(x)-y+\lambda(t)V_t(y)}+\norm{x-y}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)(2+\lambda(t)L(t))\norm{x-y}.

where
L(t)=\frac{1}{\eta}+\varepsilon(t)+\frac{\beta(t)}{\mu}.

As
\lambda,\varepsilon,\beta:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are continuous on each interval
[0,b]
, where
0<b<\infty
, we get

L_{f}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\quad L_{f}(t)=\gamma(t)(2+\lambda(t)L(t)),

which is clearly a locally integrable functions. This verifies condition
(f3)
.
It remains to establish condition
(f4)
. From the continuity of
\lambda,\varepsilon,\beta
, there exist
\lambda_{\min},\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}
, such that

0<\lambda_{\min}<\lambda(t),\quad 0<\varepsilon_{\min}<\varepsilon(t)\text{ and }0<\beta_{\min}<\beta(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,b].

Hence, we have for all
t\in[0,b]
, using the triangle inequality, nonexpansiveness of
\res_{t}
and eq. (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{f(t,x)}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\norm{T_t(x)-x}\leq\gamma(t)\big(\norm{T_{t}(x)}+\norm{x}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\left(\norm{x}+\norm{\res_{\lambda(t)\opA}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\eps_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))}_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))\right)

\displaystyle+\gamma(t)\norm{\res_{\lambda(t)\opA}(x-\lambda(t)V_{\eps(t),\beta(t)}(x))-\res_{\lambda(t)\opA}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\eps_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))}_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}(x-\lambda(t)V_{\varepsilon(t),\beta(t)}(x))-\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\norm{x}+\gamma(t)\norm{\res_{\lambda(t)\opA}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\eps_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))}_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))

\displaystyle+\gamma(t)\|x-\lambda(t)V_{\varepsilon(t),\beta(t)}(x)-x+\lambda_{\min}V_{\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(t)\norm{x}+\gamma(t)\norm{\res_{\lambda_{\min}\opA}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\eps_{\min},\beta_{\min}(x)})}_{\lambda_{\min}\mathsf{A}}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\varepsilon_{\min},\beta_{\min}(x)})

\displaystyle+\gamma(t)(\lambda(t)-\lambda_{\min})\norm{\opA_{\lambda_{\min}}(x-\lambda_{\min}V_{\eps_{\min},\beta_{\min}}(x))}

\displaystyle+\gamma(t)(\lambda(t)-\lambda_{\min})\norm{\opD(x)}+\gamma(t)(\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)-\lambda_{\min}\varepsilon_{\min})\norm{x}

\displaystyle+\gamma(t)(\lambda(t)\beta(t)-\lambda_{\min}\beta_{\min})\norm{\opB(x)}.

Property
(f4)
follows by integrating.
∎

3.1.2
Strong convergence of the trajectories to the least-norm solution of (
P
)

Our asymptotic analysis of the FB-dynamical system (
18
) relies on Lyapunov techniques, building on the following technical result.

Lemma 12
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
3
be in place. Furthermore, we impose the conditions

\displaystyle\lambda(t)<\frac{\eta}{1+\eta\varepsilon(t)}\;\text{ and }\lambda(t)<\frac{\mu}{\mu\varepsilon(t)+\beta(t)}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(21)

Then, we have

2\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle\leq\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)(\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)-2)\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}.

Proof.

Recall, that (
18
) can be written as
\dot{x}(t)=\gamma(t)(T_{t}(x)-x)
, with
T_{t}(x)=x\Leftrightarrow x\in\zer(\Phi_{t})
. With this remark in mind, and using the definition of the dynamics, we observe that

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle=\norm{\dot{x}(t)+x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{\dot{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{\gamma(t)(T_{t}(x(t))-\bar{x}(t))+(1-\gamma(t))(x(t)-\bar{x}(t))}^{2}-\norm{\dot{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)\norm{T_{t}(x(t))-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}+(1-\gamma(t))\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}-\gamma(t)(1-\gamma(t))\norm{T_{t}(x(t))-x(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;-\norm{\dot{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

where the last equality uses (
4
). On the other hand,

\displaystyle\norm{(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(x)-(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(y)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{(1-\lambda(t)\eps(t))(x-y)-\lambda(t)\left(\opD(x)-\opD(y)+\beta(t)(\opB(x)-\opB(y))\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))^{2}\norm{x-y}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\lambda(t)(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))\langle x-y,\mathsf{D}(x)-\mathsf{D}(y)-\beta(t)(\mathsf{B}(x)-\mathsf{B}(y))\rangle

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}(t)\norm{\opD(x)-\opD(y)+\beta(t)(\opB(x)-\opB(y)}^{2}

Since
\mathsf{D}
and
\mathsf{B}
are cocoercive, we have
\langle x-y,\mathsf{D}(x)-\mathsf{D}(y)\rangle\geq\eta\norm{\opD(x)-\opD(y)}^{2}
and
\langle x-y,\mathsf{B}(x)-\mathsf{B}(y)\rangle\geq\mu\norm{\opB(x)-\opB(y)}^{2}.
Moreover

\norm{\opD(x)-\opD(y)+\beta(t)(\opB(x)-\opB(y))}^{2}\leq 2\norm{\opD(x)-\opD(y)}^{2}+2\beta(t)^{2}\norm{\opB(x)-\opB(y)}^{2},

so that we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(x)-(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(y)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))^{2}\norm{x-y}^{2}+2\lambda(t)(\lambda(t)-\eta(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)))\norm{\opD(x)-\opD(y)}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\beta(t)(\lambda(t)\beta(t)-\mu(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)))\norm{\opB(x)-\opB(y)}^{2}.

Thanks to (
21
), we remain with

\norm{(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(x)-(\Id_{\scrH}-\lambda(t)V_{t})(y)}^{2}\leq(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))^{2}\norm{x-y}^{2}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Therefore, using the non-expansiveness of the resolvent, we can continue the previous estimate to obtain

\displaystyle 2\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \gamma(t)\norm{T_{t}(x(t))-T_{t}(\bar{x}(t))}^{2}+(1-\gamma(t))\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\gamma(t)(1-\gamma(t))\norm{T_{t}(x(t))-x(t)}^{2}-\norm{\dot{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)(\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)-2)\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}.

∎

For the reader’s convenience, we summarize the assumptions on the parameter sequences we have used so far:

Assumption 4
.

The function
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is continuous, with

\lambda(t)<\frac{1}{1/\eta+\varepsilon(t)+\beta(t)/\mu}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Theorem 13
.

Let
t\mapsto x(t)
be the strong solution of (
FB
). Let be Assumptions
1
-
4
in place. Moreover, consider the following scaling relations:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon^{2}(t)}=0,

(22)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\dot{\beta}(t)}{\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon^{2}(t)}=0,\text{ and }

(23)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)(2-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))\dif t=\infty.

(24)

Then,
x(t)\to\Pi_{\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})}(0)
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Set
\theta(t)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}
. We then have

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)-\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle+\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}\cdot\frac{\norm{\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t)}}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t).

Setting
\delta(t)\triangleq-\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)(\frac{\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)}{2}-1)
and
\Delta(t,0)\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}\delta(s)\dif s
, Lemma
12
gives

\dot{\theta}(t)\leq-2\delta(t)\theta(t)+\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}\cdot\frac{\norm{\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t)}}{\dif t}\bar{x}(t).

Following Lemma
9
, we can bound the second addendum to get

\dot{\theta}(t)\leq-2\delta(t)\theta(t)+\sqrt{2\theta(t)}\left(\frac{\dot{\beta}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}\norm{\opB(\bar{x}(t))}+\frac{\absolutevalue{\dot{\eps}(t)}}{\varepsilon(t)}\norm{\bar{x}(t)}\right).

By putting
\varphi(t)\triangleq\sqrt{2\theta(t)}
, we thus finally arrive at the inequality

\dot{\varphi}(t)\leq-\delta(t)\varphi(t)+w(t),

(25)

where (recall that
\dot{\varepsilon}(t)\leq 0
)

w(t)\triangleq\frac{\dot{\beta}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}\norm{\opB(\bar{x}(t))}-\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}\norm{\bar{x}(t)}.

(26)

Introducing the integration factor
\exp(-\Delta(t,0))
, we thus obtain

\varphi(t)\leq\varphi(0)\exp(-\Delta(t,0))+\exp(-\Delta(t,t_0))\int_{0}^{t}w(s)\exp(\Delta(s,t_0))\dif s.

If the integral on the right-hand side is bounded, we are done. Else, apply l’Hôspital’s rule, the definition of
\delta(t)
, and conditions (
22
) and (
23
), to get

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\exp(-\Delta(t,0))\int_{0}^{t}w(s)

\displaystyle\exp(\Delta(s,0))\dif s=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{w(t)\exp(\Delta(t,0))}{\delta(t)\exp(\Delta(t,0))}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\delta(t)\varepsilon(t)}\left(\frac{\dot{\beta}(t)}{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}\norm{\opB(\bar{x}(t))}-\norm{\bar{x}(t)}\right)=0.

This shows that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}=0
. Since

x(t)-\Pi_{\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\norm{x(t)-\Pi_{\zer(\opA+\opD+\NC_{\scrC})}(0)}_{\mathcal{C}})}(0)\leq\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}+\bar{x}(t)-\Pi_{\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\norm{\bar{x}(t)-\Pi_{\zer(\opA+\opD+\NC_{\scrC})}(0)}_{\mathcal{C}})}(0),

the strong convergence claim follows from Corollary
7
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Since
\varepsilon(t)\to 0
and
\beta(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, the hypothesis (
21
) implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)=0
, as well as
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)\beta(t)\leq\mu
. We also see that
\lambda(t)<\eta
for all
t\geq 0
. Hence, the rate of the decay of the step size must be on par with the rate of divergence of the penalty parameter.

Remark 3
.

The assumptions formulated in Theorem
13
can be satisfied by the following set of functions:
\gamma(t)=\cos(1/t),\lambda(t)=\frac{\lambda}{\beta(t)+\lambda\varepsilon(t)}
, where
\lambda<c\min\{\mu,\eta\}
for some
c\in(0,1)
(recommended to be close to 1), as well as
\varepsilon(t)=(t+b)^{-r}
and
\beta(t)=(t+b)^{s}
for
b\geq 1
and
0<r<s
. Then,
\delta(t)=\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}(t)}{\beta^{2}(t)}\big)=\mathcal{O}\big((t+b)^{-2(r+s)}\big)
, and consequently we need to impose the restriction
s+r<\frac{1}{2}
to ensure that
\delta\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Additionally, we compute
\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon^{2}(t)}=\mathcal{O}\big((t+b)^{r+s-1}\big)
. This yields

. Finally, we have
\frac{\dot{\beta}(t)}{\gamma(t)\lambda(t)\varepsilon^{2}(t)}=\mathcal{O}\big((t+b)^{2(r+s)-1}\big)
, and to make this a bounded sequence, we have the restriction
r+s<\frac{1}{2}
. These conditions together span a region of feasible parameters
(r,s)
which is nonempty.

3.2
Penalty regulated forward-backward-forward dynamics

A critical assumption underlying the forward-backward dynamical system (
FB
) is the cocoercivity (inverse strong monotonicity) of the single-valued operators
\mathsf{D}
and
\mathsf{B}
. Cocoercivity is guaranteed to hold when the monotone inclusion problem (
P
) models optimality conditions for constrained convex optimization problems. However, it generically fails in structured monotone splitting problems arising from primal-dual optimality conditions derived from the Fechel-Rockafellar theorem. Section
5
describes a very general class of splittings illustrating this claim. Motivated by this observation, this section derives a new dynamical system formulation exhibiting multiscale aspects, respecting Tikhonov regularization and penalization. Specifically, the class of dynamical systems we are investigating in this section builds on
Boţ et al. (2020)
, and extends it to the constrained setting.

We consider the following first-order dynamical system

\displaystyle p(t)

\displaystyle=\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}(x(t)-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x(t)),

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=p(t)-x(t)+\lambda(t)[V_{t}(x(t))-V_{t}(p(t))]

To obtain a simpler expression of this dynamics, we define the reflection
R_{t}(x)\triangleq x-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x)
, and the vector field
f(t,x):[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
given by

f(t,x)\triangleq\left(R_{t}\circ\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}}\circ R_{t}\right)(x)-R_{t}(x)

(27)

The first-order dynamical system (
FBF
) is then exactly of the form (
D
). To prove existence and uniqueness of strong global solutions, we can use the same arguments as in Section 5.1 of
Boţ et al. (2020)
, based on the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem for absolutely continuous trajectories. We therefore omit these straightforward derivations.

Remark 4
.

A discrete-time formulation of the FBF dynamical system can be given along the same lines as in Remark
1
. Adopting the notation used there, let
\pi_{\delta}=\{0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n^{\delta}}=T\}
be a partition of
[0,T]
; let
h^{\delta}_{k}\triangleq t_{k+1}^{\delta}-t_{k}^{\delta}
, and let
\delta
be the mesh size given by
\delta\triangleq\max\{h_{k}^{\delta}:0\leq k\leq n^{\delta}-1\}
. We define a pair process
(P^{\delta}_{k},X^{\delta}_{k})_{k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n^{\delta}-1\}}
recursively by

\displaystyle P^{\delta}_{k}

\displaystyle=\res_{\lambda^{\delta}_{k}\mathsf{A}}(X^{\delta}_{k}-\lambda^{\delta}_{k}V_{k}(X^{\delta}_{k})),

\displaystyle X^{\delta}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=P^{\delta}_{k}+\lambda^{\delta}_{k}(V_{k}(X^{\delta}_{k})-V_{k}(P^{\delta}_{k})).

This is just a dynamic version of Tseng’s modified extragradient method, augmented with penalty terms (as studied in
Boţ and Csetnek (2014)
), together with a Tikhonov term (a new ingredient).

3.2.1
Strong convergence of the trajectories to the least-norm solution

This section contains the necessary Lyapunov analysis for understanding the long-run behavior of trajectories issued by (
FBF
). We begin with some technical lemmata.

Lemma 14
.

For almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-\norm{x(t)-p(t)}^{2}+\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}-(1+2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle

Proof.

From the definition of the process
t\mapsto p(t)
, we deduce

(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}+\lambda(t)\mathsf{A})p(t)\ni x(t)-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x(t)),

it follows,

\Phi_{t}(p(t))=\mathsf{A}(p(t))+V(t,p(t))\ni\frac{x(t)-p(t)}{\lambda(t)}-V_{t}(x(t))+V_{t}(p(t))=-\frac{\dot{x}(t)}{\lambda(t)}.

Recall that
\Phi_{t}\equiv\Phi_{\varepsilon(t),\beta(t)}
is
\varepsilon(t)
-strongly monotone, so that
\{\bar{x}(t)\}=\zer(\Phi_{t})
. Consequently,

\Big\langle-\frac{\dot{x}(t)}{\lambda(t)}-0,p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle\geq\varepsilon(t)\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}.

(28)

Using these properties, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle x(t)-p(t),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle

(29)

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\norm{x(t)-p(t)}^{2}+\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}-\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle,

which completes the proof.
∎

Let
t\mapsto x(t)

For almost all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle=\|\bar{x}(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}-\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),V_{t}(x(t))-V_{t}(p(t))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\|\bar{x}(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-\|\bar{x}(t)-x(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle

then,

\displaystyle 2\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),V_{t}(x(t))-V_{t}(p(t))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\Big\langle x(t)-p(t),V_{t}(x(t))-V_{t}(p(t))\Big\rangle

the proof is completed.
∎

\displaystyle=\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-\dot{\varepsilon}(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\bar{x}(\varepsilon(t),\beta(t))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle-\dot{\beta}(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\bar{x}(\varepsilon(t),\beta(t))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-(1-\lambda(t)L(t))\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}-\varepsilon(t)\lambda(t)\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\dot{\varepsilon}(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\bar{x}(\varepsilon(t),\beta(t))\Big\rangle-\dot{\beta}(t)\Big\langle x(t)-\bar{x}(t),\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\bar{x}(\varepsilon(t),\beta(t))\Big\rangle

Since

\varepsilon(t)

\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq\langle x(t)-p(t)+\lambda(t)[V_{t}(p(t))-V_{t}(x(t))],p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle,

\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)}+1+\frac{1}{\eta\varepsilon(t)}+\frac{\beta(t)}{\mu\varepsilon(t)}\right)\|x(t)-p(t)\|.

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|

For almost all

-\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{a^{2}(t)}\|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}.

(32)

Defining

(33)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\exp(-\Delta(t))\int_{0}^{t}\exp(\Delta(s))\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(s)}{\varepsilon(s)}w(s)\differential s=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\exp(\Delta(t))\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}w(t)}{\delta(t)\exp(\Delta(t))}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}w(t)}{\delta(t)}

4
Multiscale forward-backward dynamics

In this section we extend the forward-backward penalty dynamics to the challenging case in which the feasible set
\mathcal{C}
can be represented as the set of joint minimizers of two penalty terms. This structural assumption can be motivated by decomposition methods in optimization and optimal control, in which different structural properties of the feasible set can be incorporated via different penalty functions. To make this concrete, we refine the general problem formulation (
P
) by assuming that the constrained domain
\mathcal{C}
is of the form

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2},

with
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
closed convex and nonempty sets. This geometry could represent conic domains intersected with an affine subspace, leading to a very generic class of variational problems. To be consistent with our penalty formulation, we impose the following structural assumption on the geometry.

Assumption 5
.

The constrained domain
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
admits the representation

\mathcal{C}=\argmin\Psi_{1}\cap\argmin\Psi_{2},

(34)

with convex potentials
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty)
satisfying

1.

\Psi_{1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L_{\Psi_{1}}
-smooth;

2.

\Psi_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex with subdifferential
\partial\Psi_{2}
;

3.

\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is coercive;

4.

The operator
\mathsf{A}+\partial\Psi_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone.

To align the notation with the previously studied penalty dynamics, we set
\mathsf{B}_{1}\triangleq\nabla\Psi_{1}
and
\mathsf{B}_{2}\triangleq\partial\Psi_{2}
. Note that
\mathsf{B}_{1}
is
\frac{1}{L_{\Psi_{1}}}
-co-coercive and monotone, and
\mathsf{B}_{2}
is maximally monotone. Since our penalization framework only uses information on the gradient and subgradients of the penalty potentials, we can assume without loss of generality that
\argmin\Psi_{i}=\Psi^{-1}_{i}(0)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. If this is not originally the case, we can always re-shift the graph of the function so that the problem formulation remains the same. To solve the constrained variational inequality (
P
) within this more structured setup, we propose a forward-backward based dynamical system via a full splitting of the resulting problem. Given positive functions
\lambda(t),\beta(t),\varepsilon(t)
, we assume that the time-varying operator

\mathsf{A}_{t}(x)\triangleq\mathsf{A}(x)+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}_{2}(x)

(35)

has an easy-to-compute resolvent mapping
\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}_{t}}=(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}+\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}_{t})^{-1}
. This evaluation condition is the main reason why we assume that
\mathsf{A}+\partial\Psi_{2}
is maximally monotone. We further assume that the resolvent is everywhere single-valued and nonexpansive. Proceeding then in the spirit of the dynamical system (
FB
), we design a vector field which exploits a full splitting of the operators involved by moving all single-valued operators into the backward step and all set-valued information into the forward step. Hence, we arrive at the following first-order dynamical system

\dot{x}(t)+x(t)=\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}_{t}}(x(t)-\lambda(t)V_{t}(x(t)))

(SFBP)

where

V_{t}(x)\triangleq\mathsf{D}(x)+\varepsilon(t)x+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}_{1}(x).

(36)

With the introduction of the time-varying operators
\mathsf{A}_{t}
and
V_{t}
, we achieve a full splitting of the penalized auxiliary problems of the form (
5
). This is done on purpose to reduce computational costs in the implementation of the dynamics. To the best of our knowledge, the first full splitting dynamics of this kind has been studied in
Czarnecki
et al. (2016)
in the potential case and without Tikhonov regularization. We extend their analysis to the monotone operator case and add Tikhonov regularization on top of the operators to induce strong convergence. Moreover, the dynamical system (
SFBP
) contains the penalty-regulated forward-backward dynamical system (
FB
) by setting
\mathsf{B}_{2}=0
.

We shall prove the convergence of the trajectories associated with (
SFBP
) under the Attouch-Czarnecki condition
Attouch and Czarnecki (2010)
;
Attouch et al. (2018)
:

Assumption 6

(Attouch-Czarnecki condition)
.

(\forall\xi\in\range(\NC_{\mathcal{C}})):\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left[(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})^{\ast}(\frac{\xi}{\beta(t)})-\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}(\frac{\xi}{\beta(t)})\right]\dif t<\infty.

(37)

This condition involves the Fenchel conjugate of the convex function
\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}
, defined as

(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})^{\ast}(y)\triangleq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\{\langle y,x\rangle-(\Psi_{1}(x)+\Psi_{2}(x))\}

The integrability criterion (
37
) has been exploited heavily in the analysis of penalty regulated dynamical systems. Early references include
Attouch et al. (2011)
;
Boţ and Csetnek (2014)
;
Boţ and Csetnek (2016)
;
Peypouquet (2012)
, among many others. It is implied by an Hölderian growth condition of the combined penalty function
\Psi=\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}
. A proper convex and lower semi-continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
\argmin(f)\neq\varnothing
is said to satisfy a Hölderian growth condition with exponent
\rho\in(1,2]
if for some
\tau>0
we have

\frac{\tau}{\rho}\dist(x,\argmin f)^{\rho}\leq f(x)-\min f\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Assuming that
\Psi
satisfies a Hölderian growth condition around
\argmin\Psi=\mathsf{C}
, we have

0\leq\Psi^{\ast}(z)-\sigma_{\mathsf{C}}(z)\leq\tau^{1-\rho^{*}}\frac{1}{\rho^{*}}\norm{z}^{\rho^{*}}\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\rho^{*}
ist he dual conjugate exponent given by
\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{*}}=1
. Therefore, the Hölderian growth condition implies (
37
) whenever
\beta(t)
is a function satisfying the integrability condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{1-\rho^{*}}\norm{p/\beta(r)}^{\rho^{*}}\dif t=\norm{p}^{\rho^{*}}\tau^{1-\rho^{*}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(t)\beta(t)^{1-\rho^{*}}\dif t<\infty.

In the important case where
\Psi(z)=\frac{1}{2}\dist_{\mathcal{C}}(z)^{2}
, then
\Psi^{\ast}(z)-\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{z}^{2}
, and thus the integrability condition (
37
) simplifies to
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(t)}{\beta(t)}\dif t<\infty
.

General weak convergence analysis

The asymptotic behavior of the multi-penalty system turned out to be more complex. However, we can establish the weak ergodic convergence of the trajectories, following the arguments in
Attouch and Czarnecki (2010)
;
Attouch et al. (2011)
. The proof of the following result can be found in Appendix
7
.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\varepsilon,\lambda:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be absolutely continuous functions in
L^{2}(0,\infty)\setminus L^{1}(0,\infty)
, and such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\lambda(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}=\infty
. Suppose, moreover, that
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\lambda(t)\beta(t)>0
and, for every
\xi\in\range(\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left[(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})^{\ast}(\frac{\xi}{\beta(t)})-\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}(\frac{\xi}{\beta(t)})\right]\dif t<\infty.

Then,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\norm{\opB_{1}(x(t))}=\lim_{t\to\infty}(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})(x(t)+\dot{x}(t))=0.

Define the ergodic trajectory

\bar{x}(T)\triangleq\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(s)x(s)\dif s}{\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(s)\dif s}\quad(T>0).

Then,
\bar{x}(t)
converges weakly, as
t\to\infty
, to a point in
\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
.

Remark 6
.

Assume that the function
\Psi\triangleq\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}
are boundedly inf-compact, which means that every set of the form

\{x\in\mathcal{H}|\;\norm{x}\leq R\wedge(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})(x)\leq M\},

with
R\geq 0
and
M\in\mathbb{R}
, is relatively compact. Under the assumptions of Theorem
17
, since
\lim_{t\to\infty}(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})(x(t))=0
, the convergence of
\{x(t)\}
to
\zer(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
must be strong.

5
Applications

In this section we describe some prototypical applications of our splitting framework.

5.1
Sparse optimal control of linear systems

Given
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and matrix-valued functions
A:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},B:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
as well as a vector-valued function
c:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the control system

\dot{y}(t)=Ay(t)+B(t)u(t)+c(t)\quad y(0)=y_{0}.

(38)

The process
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is an open-loop control. We assume that
A,B
and
c
are bounded and sufficiently regular, so that the
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and the system has a absolutely continuous solution, denoted by
Y^{u,y_{0}}_{\bullet}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We are interested in solving the optimal control problem

\min\{\frac{1}{2}\norm{Y^{u,y_{0}}_{\bullet}-\bar{y}(\bullet)}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\alpha_{1}\norm{u}^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;R^{m})}+\alpha_{2}\norm{u}_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\}

(39)

where the minimum is taken over the set of admissible controls

\mathcal{U}:=\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})|u(\bullet)\text{ is measurable and }\norm{u(t)}_{\infty}\leq 1~~\text{a.e. }t\in[0,T]\}

Let
P:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denote the resolvent of the matrix equation
\dot{X}=AX
,
X(0)=\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
satisfying
P(t)=\exp(tA)
. Then

Y^{y_{0},u}_{t}=P(t)y_{0}+P(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(s)^{-1}[B(s)u(s)+c(s)]\dif s

satisfies

\frac{\dif}{\dif t}Y^{y_{0},u}_{t}=AY^{y_{0},u}_{t},\quad Y^{y_{0},u}_{0}=y_{0}.

This in turn is equivalent to

S(u,y)+z_{0}=0,

where

\displaystyle S(u,y)(t)\triangleq-y(t)+P(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(s)^{-1}B(s)u(s)\dif s,\text{ and }

\displaystyle z_{0}(t)\triangleq P(t)y_{0}+P(t)\int_{0}^{t}P(s)^{-1}c(s)\dif s

Set
\mathcal{H}\triangleq L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
S
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and consequently the function
\Psi_{1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\Psi_{1}(u,y)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\norm{S(u,y)+z_{0}}^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})}

is convex and continuously differentiable. Next, define
\Psi_{2}(u,y)=\delta_{\mathcal{U}}(u)
to obtain a convex, proper and lower semi-continuous function. Moreover,
(u,y)\in\mathcal{H}
solves the control system if and only if
(u,y)\in\argmin(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})
. With this notation,

\displaystyle J_{1}(u,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\norm{y-\bar{y}}^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\alpha_{2}\norm{u}^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})},

\displaystyle J_{2}(u,y)

\displaystyle\triangleq\alpha_{1}\norm{u}_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}

so that the optimal control problem becomes

\min\{J_{1}(u,y)+J_{2}(u,y):(u,y)\in\argmin(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})\}

With
\mathsf{D}\triangleq\nabla J_{1}
and
\mathsf{A}\triangleq\partial J_{2}
, so that
\mathcal{C}=\argmin(\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2})
, we arrive at a constrained variational inequality problem of the form (
P
).

5.2
Monotone inclusions involving compositions with linear continuous operators

We next show how our method can be applied to solve monotone inclusion problems involving compositions of operators, as proposed by
Combettes and
Pesquet (2012)
;
Boţ and Hendrich (2014)

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
be real Hilbert spaces. We introduce operators
\mathsf{A}_{1}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathsf{A}_{2}:\mathcal{G}\to 2^{\mathcal{G}}
which we assume to be maximally monotone. Additionally, we let
L:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
represent a linear continuous operator. Lastly, we consider
\mathsf{D}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
monotone and and
\frac{1}{\eta}
-Lipschitz continuous operator with
\eta>0
, and a monotone operator
\mathsf{B}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
a monotone and
\frac{1}{\mu}
-Lipschitz continuous operator with
\mu>0
satisfying
\mathcal{C}=\zer(\mathsf{B})\neq\varnothing
. The monotone inclusion problem to solve is

0\in\mathsf{A}_{1}(x)+L^{\ast}\circ\mathsf{A}_{2}\circ Lx+\mathsf{D}(x)+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x).

(40)

This splitting gains relevance in the generic convex optimization model

\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\{f(x)+h(x)+g(Lx)\}

where
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}),g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{G})
,
K:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is a bounded linear operator and
h\in{\mathbf{C}}^{1,1}_{L_{h}}(\mathcal{H};\mathbb{R})
. Via the classical Fenchel-Rockafellar duality, we can transform this problem into the constrained saddle point problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\max_{y}\{f(x)+h(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{\ast}(y)\}

which amounts to solving the monotone inclusion problem consisting in finding a pair
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial f(x^{\ast})+\nabla h(x^{\ast})+L^{\ast}y+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{\ast})

\displaystyle 0\in Lx^{\ast}-\partial g^{\ast}(y^{\ast})

Since
\partial g^{\ast}=(\partial g)^{-1}
, we can combine these two inclusions to a single one reading as

0\in\partial f(x^{\ast})+\nabla h(x^{\ast})+L^{\ast}\partial g(Lx^{\ast})+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{\ast}).

We thus arrive at an instantiation of problem (
40
), by identifying
\mathsf{A}_{1}=\partial f,\mathsf{A}_{2}=\partial g,\mathsf{D}=\nabla h
.

We use the product space approach in order to show that the general problem (
40
) can be formulated as the monotone inclusion problem (
P
). To this end, we consider the product space
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}
endowed with the inner product

\langle(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}}=\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle y,y^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}

and corresponding norm. We define the operators

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(x,y)\triangleq\mathsf{A}_{1}(x)\times\mathsf{A}_{2}^{-1}(y),\;\tilde{\mathsf{D}}(x,y)\triangleq\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{D}(x)+L^{\ast}y\\
-Lx\end{pmatrix},\;\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(x,y)\triangleq\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{B}(x)\\
0\end{pmatrix},

and for
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\triangleq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{G}=\zer(\tilde{\mathsf{B}})
,

\NC_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(x,v)=\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\times\{0\}.

One can easily show that if
(x,v)\in\zer(\tilde{\mathsf{A}}+\tilde{\mathsf{D}}+\NC_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}})
, then
x\in\zer(\mathsf{A}_{1}+L^{\ast}\mathsf{A}_{2}L+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
. Conversely, when
x\in\zer(\mathsf{A}_{1}+L^{\ast}\mathsf{A}_{2}L+\mathsf{D}+\NC_{\mathcal{C}})
, then there exists
v\in\mathsf{A}_{2}(Lx)
such that
(x,v)\in\zer(\tilde{\mathsf{A}}+\tilde{\mathsf{D}}+\NC_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}})
. Thus, determining the zeros of operator
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}+\tilde{\mathsf{D}}+\NC_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}
will provide a solution for the monotone inclusion problem (
40
).

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}
is maximally monotone
(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2016
, Proposition 20.23)
,
\tilde{\mathsf{D}}
is monotone and
\tilde{\eta}
-Lipschitz continuous, where
\tilde{\eta}=\sqrt{2(1/\eta^{2}+\norm{K}^{2})}

\tilde{B}
is monotone and

-Lipschitz continuous. We can thus directly use our dynamical system to determine zeros of

. We write the trajectory in terms of pairs
t\mapsto(p(t),q(t))
and
t\mapsto(x(t),y(t))
given by

\displaystyle p(t)=\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}_{1}}(x(t)-\lambda(t)(\mathsf{D}(x(t))+\varepsilon(t)x(t)+L^{\ast}y(t)+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}(x(t)))

\displaystyle q(t)=\res_{\lambda(t)\mathsf{A}_{2}^{-1}}(y(t)+\lambda(t)Lx(t)-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)y(t))

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))(p(t)-x(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda(t)[\mathsf{D}(x(t))-\mathsf{D}(p(t))+\beta(t)(\mathsf{B}(x(t))-\mathsf{B}(p(t)))+L^{\ast}(y(t)-q(t))]

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)=(1-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))(q(t)-y(t))+\lambda(t)L(p(t)-x(t)).

Remark 7
.

Let us underline the fact that, even in the situation when
B
is cocoercive and, hence,
\tilde{B}
is cocoercive, the forward-backward penalty scheme studied in
Boţ and Csetnek (2016)
cannot be applied in this context, because the operator
\tilde{D}
is definitely not cocoercive. This is due to the presence of the skew operator
(x,y)\mapsto(K^{\ast}y,Kx)
in its definition. This fact provides a good motivation for formulating, along the forward-backward penalty scheme, a forward-backward-forward penalty scheme for the monotone inclusion problem investigated in this paper.

5.2.1
Application to linear inverse problems

Building on the primal-dual splitting approach of
Boţ and Hendrich (2014)
;
Boţ and Csetnek (2016)
, we consider a linear inverse problem with forward operator
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
which is the problem of finding
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that solves the linear system

K\theta=b

Typically, this linear system is ill-posed, and therefore a regularization framework is adopted. A popular formulation is to consider flattened gradient via an isotropic total variation regularization, which reads as the simple bilevel optimization problem

\min_{\theta\in[0,1]^{n}}\textsf{TV}(\theta)\quad\text{s.t.: }\theta\in S:=\argmin_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\frac{1}{2}\norm{K\theta'-b}^{2}\}

(41)

where the mapping
\textsf{TV}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\displaystyle\textsf{TV}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\sqrt{(\theta_{i+1,j}-\theta_{i,j})^{2}+(\theta_{i,j+1}-\theta_{i,j})^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\absolutevalue{\theta_{i+1,N}-\theta_{i,N}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\absolutevalue{\theta_{M,j+1}-\theta_{M,j}}

and
\theta_{i,j}
denotes the normalized value of the pixel located in the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Define the linear operator
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{Y}
by
\theta\mapsto(L_{1}\theta,L_{2}\theta)\in\mathcal{Y}
defined coordinate-wise by

\displaystyle L_{1}\theta_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{i+1,j}-\theta_{i,j}&\text{ if }i<M,\\
0&\text{else.}\end{array}\right.,\quad L_{2}\theta_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{i,j+1}-\theta_{i,j}&\text{ if }i<N,\\
0&\text{else.}\end{array}\right.

L
represents a discretization of the gradient using Neumann boundary conditions. We note that
\norm{L}^{2}\leq 8
.

For
(y,z),(u,v)\in\mathcal{Y}
, we introduce the inner product

\langle(y,z),(u,v)\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(y_{i,j}u_{i,j}+z_{i,j}v_{i,j}),

with the corresponding norm
\norm{(y,z)}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\sqrt{\langle(y,z),(y,z)\rangle}
. It then follows
\mathsf{TV}(\theta)=\norm{L\theta}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. The dual norm to
\norm{\cdot}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is defined as

\norm{(u,v)}_{\mathcal{Y},\ast}:=\sup_{\norm{(u,v)}_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq 1}\langle(y,z),(u,v)\rangle.

Accordingly, we define the dual space
\mathcal{Y}^{\ast}
, as the Euclidean space
\mathcal{Y}
endowed with the norm
\norm{\cdot}_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Define the function
f(\theta)=\delta_{[0,1]^{n}}(\theta)
and
g(u,v)=\norm{(u,v)}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. It follows that (
41
) is representable as

\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{H}}\{f(\theta)+g(L\theta)\}\quad\text{s.t.: }\theta\in S:=\argmin_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}}\{\frac{1}{2}\norm{K \theta'-b}^{2}\}.

The Fenchel-Rockafellar dual approach gives us the saddle point bilevel problem

\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{H}}\max_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Y}^{\ast}}\{f(\theta)+\langle L\theta,(u,v)\rangle-g^{\ast}(u,v)\}\quad\text{s.t.: }\theta\in S:=\argmin_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}}\{\frac{1}{2}\norm{K \theta'-b}^{2}\}.

(42)

where

g^{\ast}(u,v)=\delta_{M}(u,v),\;M=\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Y}|\;\norm{(u,v)}_{\mathcal{Y},\ast}\leq 1\}.

This yields the optimality conditions

\displaystyle 0\in\partial f(\bar{\theta})+L^{\ast}(\bar{u},\bar{v})+\NC_{S}(\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle 0\in\partial g^{\ast}(\bar{u},\bar{v})-L\bar{\theta}

Define
\mathcal{C}=S\times\mathcal{Y}
, so that
\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta,u,v)=\NC_{S}(\theta)\times\{\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{Y}}\}
, to obtain the monotone inclusion

0\in\mathsf{A}(\bar{\theta},\bar{u},\bar{v})+\mathsf{D}(\bar{\theta},\bar{u},\bar{v})+\NC_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta,u,v).

where
\mathsf{D}
is the skew symmetric linear operator
\mathsf{D}(\theta,u,v)=[L^{\ast}(u,v),-L\theta]
. To solve this problem with our penalty regularized dynamical system, we relax the variational problem to arrive the the unconstrained min-max optimization formulation

\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{H}}\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\{f(\theta)+\langle L\theta,(u,v)\rangle-g^{\ast}(u,v)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\norm{\theta}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\norm{(u,v)}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}+\beta\Psi(\theta,u,v)\},

(43)

where
\Psi(\theta,u,v)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\norm{K\theta-b}^{2}
. Define the monotone and co-coercive operator

\mathsf{B}(\theta,u,v)\triangleq\nabla\Psi(\theta,u,v)=[K^{\ast}(K\theta-b);\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{Y}}]\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{Y},

so that
\mathcal{C}=\zer(B)
. We thus can approach the solution of our linear inverse problem with the outer penalization scheme using the monotone operator

\Phi_{\varepsilon,\beta}(\theta,u,v)=\mathsf{A}(\theta,u,v)+\mathsf{D}(\theta,u,v)+\varepsilon[\theta,u,v]+\beta\mathsf{B}(\theta,u,v).

For the implementation of the algorithm, we use the formulas

\res_{\lambda\partial f}=\Pi_{[0,1]^{n}},\;\res_{\lambda\partial g^{\ast}}=\Pi_{M},

where
\Pi_{S}:\mathcal{Y}\to M
is defined componentwise as
Boţ and Hendrich (2014)

(u_{i,j},v_{i,j})\mapsto\frac{(p_{i,j},q_{i,j})}{\max\{1,\sqrt{p^{2}_{i,j}+q^{2}_{i,j}}\}}\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq M,1\leq j\leq N.

Writing out the iterations of the FBF penalty system, we construct two absolutely continuous functions
p(t)=[\tilde{\theta}(t),\tilde{u}(t),\tilde{v}(t)]
and
x(t)=[\theta(t),u(t),v(t)]
solving the following system of ODEs

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}(t)=\Pi_{[0,1]^{n}}[\theta(t)-\lambda(t)L^{\ast}(u(t),v(t))-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\theta(t)-\lambda(t)\beta(t)K^{\ast}(K\theta(t)-b)],

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{u}(t)\\
\tilde{v}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\Pi_{M}\left[\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}+\lambda(t)\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}\theta(t)\\
L_{2}\theta(t)\end{pmatrix}-\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}\right],

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)+\theta(t)=\tilde{\theta}(t)+\lambda(t)\biggl[L^{*}(u(t)-\tilde{u}(t),v(t)-\tilde{v}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon(t)(\theta(t)-\tilde{\theta}(t))+\beta(t)K^{\ast}K(\theta(t)-\tilde{\theta}(t))\biggr],

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\dot{u}(t)\\
\dot{v}(t)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{u}(t)\\
\tilde{v}(t)\end{pmatrix}-\lambda(t)\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}(\theta(t)-\tilde{\theta}(t))\\
L_{2}(\theta(t)-\tilde{\theta}(t))\end{pmatrix}+\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\begin{pmatrix}u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\\
v(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\end{pmatrix}.

6
Conclusion

The asymptotic analysis of dynamical systems derived from operator splitting problems has been a very productive line of research over the past 20 years. In this paper we develop a family of dynamical systems featuring Tikhonov regularization and penalty effects. Tikhonov regularization induces strong convergence towards the minimum norm solution, whereas the penalty term steers the system to satisfy constraints subjected to the variational problem we aim to solve. We prove the asymptotic convergence in three paradigmatic settings: (i) Operator splitting with coocercive data, (ii) Operator splitting with monotone and Lipschitz data, and (iii) Operator splitting with multiple penalty terms. Future directions of research include the extension to stochastic operator equations, such as
Rodrigo et al. (2024)
. Other potentially interesting directions would be the inclusion of inertia or momentum effects into the dynamical system in order investigate the potential for acceleration. We leave these interesting directions for future research.

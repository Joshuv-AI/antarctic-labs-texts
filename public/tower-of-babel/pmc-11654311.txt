Impact of different antiretroviral therapy regimens on bone mineral density in people living with HIV: a retrospective and longitudinal study in China
Authors: Xuelei Liang, Hanxi Zhang, Mingnan Guo, Hongxin Zhao, Di Yang, Rui Sun, Xiaojie Lao, Yali Xu, Fengting Yu, Fujie Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHIV infection and antiretroviral therapy (ART) are associated with bone loss of people living with HIV (PLWH), but limited studies exist on the impacts of ART regimens on bone mineral density (BMD) in China. This study evaluated BMD changes with three common ART regimens: tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) + lamivudine (3TC) + efavirenz (EFV), tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)-containing, and dolutegravir (DTG)-containing (non-TDF/non-TAF) therapies.MethodsIn this retrospective study, the prevalence of low BMD was analyzed in PLWH who underwent dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) before ART initiation. BMD changes were assessed in participants who had follow-up DXA scans after ≥ 1 year of ART with TDF + 3TC + EFV, TAF-containing, or DTG-containing regimens. We used multivariate logistic regression to evaluate the impact of different ART regimens on > 3% BMD reduction, adjusting for demographic and clinical variables that were significant in univariate analysis (P < 0.10).Results22.99% (630/2740) of PLWH before ART initiation had low BMD. Among 571 individuals followed up for over 1 year, BMD at the femoral neck (FN) and total hip (TH) decreased significantly in the TDF + 3TC + EFV [FN: -0.03(-0.07, 0.00) g/cm2, TH: -0.02(-0.05, 0.00) g/cm2, P < 0.001 for both] and TAF-containing regimens [FN: -0.02(-0.05, 0.01) g/cm2, TH: -0.02(-0.04, 0.01) g/cm2, P < 0.001 for both]. Lumbar spine (LS) BMD decreased significantly only with TDF + 3TC + EFV [-0.02(-0.05, 0.01) g/cm2, P < 0.001]. TDF + 3TC + EFV caused greater BMD loss at the FN and LS than the TAF-containing regimen[FN: -3.66% (-8.05%, 0.34%) vs. -2.38% (-5.44%, 1.12%), P = 0.044; LS: -2.11% (-4.50%, 0.62%) vs.-0.06% (-2.05%, 2.57%), P < 0.001]. Compared to TDF + 3TC + EFV, DTG-containing regimens showed smaller BMD reductions across all sites [FN: -1.49% (-4.65%, 3.83%), TH: 0.00% (-3.98%, 3.18%), LS: 0.59% (-2.73%, 3.09%), P = 0.004, 0.008 and 0.004, respectively]. TAF-containing and DTG-containing regimens showed no significant differences in BMD changes. Multivariable logistic regression showed that TDF + 3TC + EFV, compared to DTG-containing regimens, had higher odds of > 3% FN and LS BMD reduction (FN: OR 2.91, 95% CI: 1.33 to 6.37, P = 0.009; LS: OR 2.93, 95% CI: 1.17 to 7.32, P = 0.022), while TAF-containing regimens were not independently linked to > 3% BMD loss (P > 0.05).ConclusionsTAF-containing and DTG-containing regimens caused less bone loss than TDF + 3TC + EFV, offering safer options for preserving bone health in Chinese PLWH.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-024-10299-y.

Introduction

Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) remains a significant global public health problem, with an estimated 39.9 million people living with HIV worldwide by the end of 2023 [1]. As people living with HIV (PLWH) enjoy longer lifespans due to advances in treatment, they are increasingly at risk of developing complications such as osteoporosis and diabetes [2]. These complications contribute to rising healthcare costs [3]. Low bone mineral density (BMD) occurs in 22–66% of PLWH, while 1–26.8% experience osteoporosis [4]. A considerable proportion of mortality among PLWH is associated with fractures [5].

Numerous risk factors contribute to low BMD in PLWH, which can be categorized as either traditional or HIV-related. Traditional risk factors include age, gender, race, and low body mass index (BMI), among others. HIV-related factors encompass the infection itself, chronic inflammation, and the use of ART regimens [6].

Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) is the most widely used component of the nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) backbone in the China National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Program (NFATP) [7]. Studies have found that TDF contributes to decreased BMD [8, 9]. TDF and tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) are both prodrugs of tenofovir. Compared to TDF, TAF has higher antiviral activity and lower systemic exposure [6]. Studies conducted primarily in Western countries suggest that TAF has a less pronounced impact on BMD compared to TDF [6, 10, 11]. However, due to the inherent genetic diversity, the impacts of TAF on bone mass in Chinese PLWH may differ.

Dolutegravir (DTG) is a second-generation integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) [12]. Owing to its good efficacy and safety, INSTIs have become the first-choice recommendation in both international and Chinese AIDS treatment guidelines and are now widely used in clinical practice [13]. Despite this, no studies have compared the impact of TAF-containing and DTG-containing (non-TDF/non-TAF) regimens on BMD in Chinese PLWH based on real-world data.

In light of this, this study aimed to evaluate changes in BMD over 1 year after the initiation of different ART regimens in Chinese ART-naïve PLWH, providing evidence to guide treatment decisions for these populations.

Methods

Study design and population

A retrospective study was conducted on adult outpatients receiving medical care for HIV at the Infectious Diseases Department of Beijing Ditan Hospital, a large tertiary care hospital in Beijing, between October 2017 and June 2024.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) individuals with a confirmed diagnosis of HIV (by Western blot), (2) age ≥ 18 years, and (3) individuals who had a dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scan before ART initiation and had at least one follow-up DXA scan 1 year or more after ART initiation. The exclusion criteria were: (1) individuals who had been diagnosed with tumors, opportunistic infections, advanced liver disease, chronic kidney disease, chronic endocrine conditions, malabsorption syndrome, bone diseases, chronic hepatitis B or chronic hepatitis C; (2) individuals who had no clinical data on liver function tests [aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ] or renal function test (estimated glomerular filtration rate, eGFR) at baseline; (3) the initiation of a regimen containing protease inhibitor or TDF/TAF + DTG; and (4) prior treatments that may affect the bone health, such as systemic glucocorticoids or anti-osteoporotic medications.

Variables

For each included PLWH, data were extracted from medical records, including age, gender, date of birth, clinical history, AST, ALT, eGFR, body mass index (BMI), HIV diagnosis date, route of HIV transmission, viral load, CD4+ T-cell count, time since HIV diagnosis, and ART treatment history. BMI was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height square (m2).

BMDs of the left hip [femur neck (FN) and total hip (TH)]and lumbar spine (L1–L4, LS) were evaluated using a DXA scanner (HOLOGIC Discovery A Bone, Boston, MA, USA). The same DXA scanning method was used throughout the study to ensure accuracy. For patients who had undergone multiple DXA scans, relevant characteristics were recorded based on the follow-up closest to the scan used in this study.

Definitions

In this study, the initiation of a regimen containing a protease inhibitor was excluded, and the DTG - containing regimen did not include TDF or TAF.

BMD classification was determined according to WHO criteria [7, 14]. For postmenopausal women and men aged 50 years or older, the diagnosis was based on the T-score: a T-score ≥ -1.0 indicated normal, a T-score between − 1.0 and − 2.5 indicated osteopenia, and a T-score ≤ -2.5 indicated osteoporosis. Given the retrospective nature of this study, the specific menopausal status of the female PLWH was unavailable. As the average menopausal age of Chinese women is 45 to 50 years old [7], women aged 50 years and older were considered postmenopausal. For patients younger than 50 years, diagnosis was based on the Z-score: a Z-score > − 2.0 was considered within the expected range for age, and a Z-score ≤ − 2.0 indicated below the expected range for age. As a supplement, premenopausal women and men under the age of 50 years with BMD Z-score ≤ − 2 and a history of fragility fracture were also diagnosed with osteoporosis according to the European AIDS Clinical Society guideline (version 10.1) [15].

In our study, PLWH were classified as having low BMD if they had a T-score < -1.0 or a Z-score ≤ − 2.0, depending on the score that was most appropriate for the individual.

A 3% reduction in BMD was considered the minimum significant change needed to account for variability between repeated DXA scans [11].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean (standard deviation, SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as frequency (percentage).

For comparisons of normally distributed continuous variables between baseline and follow-up, or between two groups, a paired t-test or independent samples t-test was used, respectively. For abnormal distribution continuous variables, comparison between baseline and follow-up were conducted using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and between two groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Multivariate logistic regression was used to assess the association between a > 3% decrease in BMD and different ART regimens, adjusting for variables of demographic and clinical characteristics (P < 0.10 in univariate analysis).

Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05, with all p-values two-sided. The SPSS 26.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, United States) was used for statistical analysis.

Prevalence of low BMD

According to WHO criteria, 22.99% (630/2740) of PLWH in the ART-naïve group were diagnosed with low BMD, including 1.75% (48/2740) with osteoporosis and 21.24% (582/2740) with osteopenia (Fig. 2A). The prevalence of low BMD at the LS was 20.29% (556/2740), while at the FN and TH, it was 9.01% (247/2740) and 10.4% (285/2740), respectively (Fig. 2B).

Among the 571 PLWH included, the prevalence of low BMD before ART initiation was 21.54% (123/571), of which the prevalence of osteoporosis was 1.40% (8/571) and the prevalence of osteopenia was 20.14% (115/571) (Fig. 2C). The prevalence of low BMD at the LS was 16.11% (92/571), while at the FN and TH, it was 8.58% (49/571) and 5.78% (33/571), respectively (Fig. 2D).

There were no statistically significant difference in the prevalence of low BMD at the FN, TH, LS and any of the three sites between the 2740 PLWH in the ART-naïve group and the 571 PLWH included before ART initiation (P>0.05).

Changes in BMD from baseline to follow‑up by three ART regimens

The mean absolute BMD at the FN, TH and LS decreased significantly from baseline to follow-up after initiating the TDF + 3TC + EFV [FN: -0.03 (-0.07, 0.00) g/cm2, TH: -0.02 (-0.05, 0.00) g/cm2, LS: -0.02 (-0.05, 0.01) g/cm2, P < 0.001 for all comparisons].

For the TAF-containing regimen, significant reductions were observed in FN and TH [FN: -0.02 (-0.05, 0.01) g/cm2, TH: 0.02 (-0.04, 0.01) g/cm2, P < 0.001 for both], while BMD at the LS remained stable [0.00 (-0.02, 0.03) g/cm2, P = 0.894].

No statistically significant differences were found in the mean absolute BMD at the FN, TH or LS after initiation of the DTG-containing regimens [FN: -0.01 (0.05) g/cm2, TH: 0.00 (0.05) g/cm2, LS: 0.00 (0.04) g/cm2, P = 0.501, 0.907 and 0.822, respectively] (Table 3).

No significant differences were found in the percent change in BMD at FN, TH and LS between the TAF-containing group and the DTG-containing group (P = 0.105, 0.065 and 0.887, respectively) (Fig. 3).

The impact of ART regimens on decreased > 3% BMD

In the multivariable logistic regression model (Table 4) , compared with the DTG-containing regimen, the TDF + 3TC + EFV regimen was associated with higher odds of a > 3% reduction in FN and LS BMD (FN: odds ratio (OR) 2.91, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.33 to 6.37, P = 0.009; LS: OR 2.93, 95% CI: 1.17 to 7.32, P = 0.022) adjusting for demographic and clinical variables that were significant in univariate analysis (Supplementary Table 1). However, it was not independently associated with a > 3% reduction in TH BMD (OR 1.48, 95% CI: 0.70 to 3.13, P = 0.312). Similarly, the TAF-containing regimen was not independently associated with a > 3% reduction in FN, TH, or LS BMD (FN: OR 1.98, 95% CI: 0.86 to 4.58, P = 0.116; FN: OR 1.39, 95% CI: 0.62 to 3.12, P = 0.427 ; LS: OR 1.00, 95% CI: 0.37 to 2.74, P = 0.999).

Discussion

We observed that the prevalence of low BMD in ART-naïve Chinese PLWH, predominantly young men (median age: 31 years), was 22.99%, higher than the 15.25% reported for healthy males aged 20.0-39.9 years in China [16]. Our findings align with previous studies, where the prevalence of low BMD in untreated young PLWH ranged from 10 to 24.5% [14, 17, 18]. PLWH were at a higher risk of vertebral fractures compared to healthy individuals. Consistent with previous studies [18, 19], we found that the LS in PLWH was more prone to bone loss than other sites.

Our study is, to the best our knowledge, the first longitudinal assessment of BMD changes in naïve PLWH after the initiation of different ART regimens (TDF + 3TC + EFV, TAF-containing and DTG-containing) in a real-life setting. TDF and TAF are commonly used to treat HIV and hepatitis B, as well as for HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP). However, these medications have been associated with varying degrees of BMD loss [20, 21]. As expected, the absolute BMD at the FN, TH and LS decreased significantly after the initiation of TDF + 3TC + EFV, consistent with previous findings [22, 23]. We also observed a significant decline in FN and TH BMD in PLWH on TAF-containing therapy for more than 1 year, consistent with other studies showing that TDF-containing regimen was associated with more significant BMD reductions compared to TAF-containing regimen [24–26]. Our results showed a greater decline in FN and LS BMD in the TDF + 3TC + EFV group than in the TAF-containing group, although the change in TH BMD was not significantly different between the two groups. This could be attributed to the fact that TAF may normalize bone turnover, stabilizing the bone loss typically seen with TDF. Bone turnover changes occurred mainly in trabecular bone [27, 28]. The initiation of ART resulted in approximately 6% bone loss during the first two years of treatment. Later, different ART regimens led to different outcomes in BMD, which may stabilize, increase, or further decrease [29]. Given the median duration of TAF treatment in our study was 54 weeks, the lack of difference in TH BMD changes between the groups can be explained.

Notably, our key finding was that the mean absolute BMD did not change significantly from baseline after the initiation of DTG-containing regimen, aligning with the result by Paolo Bonfanti et al. [29], who also reported no statistically significant change in FN or LS BMD in ART-naïve PLWH. Our study found that PLWH in the TDF + 3TC + EFV group had a greater percentage decline in FN, TH, and LS BMD than those in the DTG-containing group after more than 1 year of ART. Logistic regression analysis also revealed an independent association between TDF + 3TC + EFV use and a > 3% decrease in FN and LS BMD. This finding was consistent with prior studies. In a retrospective study from Italy [30], 20 virologically suppressed PLWH, 19 with a history of taking TDF, had improved BMD at both the LS and FN 12 months after switching to the DTG/3TC regimen. Similarly, Grace A McComsey et al. [31] found significant improvements in TH and LS BMD in PLWH switching to DTG with rilpivirine versus continuing TDF-containing regimen.

A randomized, double-blind, multicenter, phase 3 trial on ART- naïve HIV patients by Joel Gallant et al. [32] found that both the bictegravir/TAF/emtricitabine and DTG/ abacavir/3TC groups showed small decreases in hip and LS BMD after 48 weeks, with no significant difference between the groups. In our study, similarly, no significant differences were observed in BMD changes between the TAF-containing and DTG-containing groups. Logistic analysis also showed no independent association between the TAF-containing regimen and a > 3% decrease in BMD. Although DTG has been shown to reduce BMD by delaying the osteogenic differentiation of human mesenchymal stem cells, reducing the activity of osteoblasts and inhibiting their conversion into osteocytes [33], the small longitudinal changes in FN, TH, and LS BMD observed in our study may be due to the smaller sample size in the DTG group.

While age, gender and BMI were not significantly correlated with > 3% BMD loss in our study, we cannot entirely rule out their potential as confounders. Previous studies have reported that older age, female sex and lower BMI are associated with BMD loss [34–36]. The younger male population in our study, along with differences in race and ART regimens, may account for this discrepancy, warranting further investigation in future studies.

We acknowledge some limitations in our study. First, being a single-center study based primarily on young men may limit the generalizability of our findings. However, our results provide valuable longitudinal BMD data from Asia, where relevant literature is scarce. Second, the relatively small sample size of the DTG-containing group limited subgroup analyses. Nevertheless, given the lack of longitudinal studies assessing BMD in PLWH treated with these three different regimens, our findings are an important contribution. Third, the one-year duration of our study was a significant limitation in assessing long-term effects, such as bone loss. However, TAF and DTG regimens have been introduced for PLWH for a short time in China, and both doctors and PLWH have historically placed less emphasis on monitoring bone health during ART period. Consequently, many PLWH did not undergo bone mass assessments throughout their ART. As a result, we were unable to evaluate the long-term impact of the three ART regimens on BMD in this study. Future research will address long-term effects on bone loss. Additionally, as this was a retrospective study, we were unable to systematically collect important information such as data on bone turnover markers, smoking and alcohol status, women’s menopausal status, and personal or parental fracture risk. Furthermore, this study only explored the influence of TDF + 3TC + EFV, TAF-containing, and DTG-containing regimens on patients’ BMD. However, we chose these three regimens based on the China national guidelines for HIV/AIDS treatment and compelling reasons from the perspective of bone health. Lastly, although DXA is the current standard for BMD measurement, it has some limitations. For instance, it cannot provide three-dimensional images or information about bone microstructure, and its accuracy may be affected by degenerative diseases and tissue artifacts [37].

Conclusions

This study is the first longitudinal assessment among Chinese PLWH to evaluate the impact of three different ART regimens, TDF + 3TC + EFV, TAF-containing, and DTG-containing, on BMD. Overall, in ART-naïve PLWH, TAF-containing and DTG-containing regimens demonstrated a similar impact on BMD, with both showing less bone loss compared to TDF + 3TC + EFV. Our findings highlight the importance of closely monitoring BMD in PLWH receiving TDF + 3TC + EFV, including HBV patients treated with TDF-containing regimens, and may inform future guidelines on bone health management and medication selection in this vulnerable population. Further studies with larger sample sizes, prospective designs, multi-center participation, and diverse methods of bone assessment are needed to investigate the mechanisms of ART-related bone loss.

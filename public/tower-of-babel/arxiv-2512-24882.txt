Description of Baryon Mass Spectrum by Open Strings and Diquarks Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25
Yuki Fujimoto

Abstract
We analyze the mass spectra of hadrons and demonstrate that the physical spectra of mesons and baryons are well described by the exponential spectrum of open strings. The open string spectrum, derived from string theory, is characterized by a unique Hagedorn temperature T H T_{\rm H} and free from any other parameters. Notably, our fitting to the physical spectra yields consistent values for both mesons and baryons, T H ≃ 0.34 ​ GeV T_{\rm H}\simeq 0.34\,\text{GeV} , which contrasts with previous phenomenological analyses that suggested different values. This obtained value aligns well with typical string tension derived from lattice-QCD calculations and the Regge slope. In the baryonic sector, our results indicate that diquarks play a crucial role in describing the mass spectrum, implying that baryons can be understood as a quark-diquark system, as anticipated by Regge phenomenology. These findings have significant implications for our understanding of quark deconfinement, especially in the possibly existing regime at high temperature and small baryon chemical potential within the QCD phase diagram.



I
Introduction

It is widely believed that deconfinement from hadronic matter to quark-gluon plasma (QGP) occurs at a temperature
T
close to the chiral crossover temperature, around
T_{\mathrm{c}}\simeq 0.16\,\text{GeV}

Aoki et al. 2006
;
Bazavov et al. 2019
;
Borsanyi et al. 2020
(see, e.g., Ref.
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
for review).
However, recent lattice-QCD studies suggest different view on the deconfinement.
For example, the meson state persists even above
T_{\mathrm{c}}
for both heavy and light flavors
Rothkopf 2020
;
Petreczky et al. 2021
;
Lowdon and Philipsen 2022
;
Aarts et al. 2024
;
Bala et al. 2024
, and in connection with that, the real part of the heavy quark-antiquark potential at finite
T
remains unscreened at least up to
2T_{\mathrm{c}}

Bazavov et al. 2024
;
Bala et al. 2025
(see also Refs.
Asakawa and Hatsuda 2004
;
Rothkopf et al. 2012
;
Burnier and Rothkopf 2013
;
Burnier et al. 2015
;
Bala and Datta 2020
;
Bala et al. 2022
;
Spriggs et al. 2023
).
Further, there is evidence for the second transition around
2T_{\mathrm{c}}
up to where the center-vortex percolation persists
Mickley et al. 2025
.

These observations have led to the speculation that there may be a novel region in the QCD phase diagram between hadronic matter and QGP
Rohrhofer et al. 2019
;
Rohrhofer et al. 2020
;
Glozman et al. 2022
;
Glozman 2023
;
Cohen and Glozman 2024
;
Fujimoto et al. 2025
;
Hanada et al. 2025
.
This region, dubbed spaghetti of quarks with glueballs (SQGB), shows distinct deconfinement properties from QGP and incorporates the color flux tubes and glueballs as relevant degrees of freedom.
The multiplicity of glueballs is scarce in the SQGB, so flux tubes dominate and it becomes stringy fluid.
In the SQGB, quarks should be understood as being connected by these open color strings.
These quarks appear to be thermally liberated because it is energetically more favorable to form a new open string with a nearby meson than to stretch the original string.
This aligns with the behavior of (correlators of) the Polyakov loop
Bazavov and Weber 2021
.

That the SQGB embodies open strings is supported by the fact that QCD thermodynamics are well reproduced by ideal gas of open strings
Fujimoto et al. 2025
.
In the usual treatment, the thermodynamic properties of the low-
T
QCD matter can largely be accounted by non-interacting hadron resonance gas model.
Thus, this in turn suggests that the hadron mass spectrum can well be described by the open string spectrum, and it was confirmed in Ref.
Marczenko et al. 2025
that this is indeed the case.
The open string density of states can be derived from string theory and predicts an exponentially growing mass spectrum; the exponent is characterized by the Hagedorn limiting temperature
T_{\mathrm{H}}
.
The string spectrum looks similar to the Hagedorn spectrum introduced in the context of statistical bootstrap model
Hagedorn 1965
;
Frautschi 1971
, but theory is fundamentally different from the bootstrap model.
In string theory,
T_{\mathrm{H}}
is related to the string tension
\sigma
or the Regge slope
\alpha^{\prime}=1/(2\pi\sigma)
, and therefore to deconfinement.
In fact
T_{\mathrm{H}}
can be interpreted as the temperature at which partons deconfine
Cabibbo and Parisi 1975
(see also Ref.
Fujimoto et al. 2021
for application).

In Ref.
Fujimoto et al. 2025
, it was found that the value of
T_{\mathrm{H}}
that best reproduces the QCD thermodynamics is
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.3\,\text{GeV}
.
This is close to the deconfinement temperature
T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 0.285\,\text{GeV}
in pure SU(3) Yang-Mills theory
Borsanyi et al. 2022
;
Giusti et al. 2025
;
Svetitsky 1986
and almost twice the chiral pseudocritical temperature
T_{\mathrm{c}}
.
Incidentally, this description using the string spectrum was inspired by the closed string description of glueball gases in pure Yang-Mills theory, in which the author also found
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.3\,\text{GeV}

Meyer 2009
.
The use of string-based spectrum in the mesonic sector led to the increase in
T_{\mathrm{H}}
.
The typical value obtained by using the Hagedorn spectrum from the statistical bootstrap is around
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.15\,\text{GeV}
(See, e.g., Refs.
Broniowski and Florkowski 2000
;
Broniowski et al. 2004
;
Majumder and Muller 2010
;
Lo et al. 2015
;
Man Lo et al. 2016
;
Rafelski 2016
).
The notable difference is that while the spectrum in the statistical bootstrap model suffers from the ambiguity in the coefficient, the string theory determines the coefficient without ambiguity, leading to more robust prediction.
We note, however, that such string-based description used in Refs.
Fujimoto et al. 2025
;
Marczenko et al. 2025
was limited to mesons.

In this work, we extend the string-based description to baryon spectrum.
In doing so, we regard a baryon as a system of a quark-diquark pair.
A diquark is strong correlation between quarks inside hadron.
This is not gauge-invariant quantity so is not well-defined object appearing in the asymptotic state, nevertheless, it is supported by many successful phenomenology by assuming a diquark as a constituent of hadrons (see, e.g., Refs.
Jaffe 2005
;
Shifman 2024
for review).
Among such successful applications of diquarks, the baryonic states lie on the straight-line Regge trajectories if one assumes diquark correlation inside baryons
Wilczek 2004
;
Selem and Wilczek 2006
.
This can be interpreted as arising from a rotating string connecting a quark-diquark configuration.
Also, this picture is in accordance with the classical string models that the Y-shaped baryon junction is unstable against the collapse to the asymmetric quark-diquark junction
Sharov 2000a
;
Sharov 2000b
;
’t Hooft 2004
.
One may also consider a possibility of the closed
\Delta
-shaped baryon with quarks on each vertex, but this does not work because the closed string density of states is much suppressed compared to the open string
Fujimoto et al. 2025
.

II
Mass spectra of hadrons and open strings

For hadron mass spectra, we use the 2020 edition of the Particle Data Group (PDG) tables
Zyla et al. 2020
, which we adopted from the latest version of Thermal-FIST
Vovchenko and Stoecker 2019
.
The table only includes established mesons and baryons with three- and four-star ratings in the PDG.
All the states are approximately treated as stable point-like particles.
The density of states for the discrete hadron spectrum is given by

\displaystyle\rho(m)=\sum_{i}d_{i}\delta(m-m_{i})\,,

(1)

where the sum runs over all stable hadrons and resonances with mass
m_{i}
and degeneracy factor
d_{i}
.
It is customary to consider the cumulative mass spectrum
Broniowski and Florkowski 2000
:

\displaystyle N(m)=\int_{0}^{m}dm^{\prime}\,\rho(m^{\prime})\,,

(2)

For the density of states for the discrete spectrum, the cumulative spectrum is given by a step function

\displaystyle N(m)=\sum_{i}d_{i}\theta(m-m_{i})\,.

(3)

As shown in Figs.
1
and
2
, the spectrum shows exponential growth.
Such growth is predicted in Hagedorn’s statistical bootstrap model and usually fitted with the following equation
Hagedorn 1965
:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{H}}(m)=\frac{c}{(m^{2}+m_{0}^{2})^{a/2}}e^{m/T_{\mathrm{H}}}\,.

(4)

This equation has three free parameters
a
,
c
, and
m_{0}
.
Typically, for
2.5\leq a\leq 4
, the fitted value of
T_{\mathrm{H}}
ranges between
[0.14,0.16]\,\text{GeV}
(see Chap. 22 in Ref.
Rafelski 2016
).

String theory is also known to naturally predict exponentially increasing mass spectrum from the partition of an integer number appearing in quantization of strings.
The asymptotic open bosonic string density of states spectrum in
D
spacetime dimensions is given by
Green et al. 1988
;
Fujimoto et al. 2025

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{str}}(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b\pi/6)^{-b/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}T_{\mathrm{H}}}\left(\frac{m}{T_{\mathrm{H}}}\right)^{-(b+1)/2}e^{m/T_{\mathrm{H}}}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\overset{D=4}{=}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{6T_{\mathrm{H}}}\left(\frac{m}{T_{\mathrm{H}}}\right)^{-3/2}e^{m/T_{\mathrm{H}}}\,,

(6)

where
b=D-2
is the number of polarizations.
This is the open string counterpart of the closed-string formula used in Ref.
Meyer 2009
.
The string formula is free from the ambiguity in the coefficient as in Eq. (
4
).
The only free parameter is the Hagedorn temperature, which is related to the tension of the confining string
\sigma
through the following relation:

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{H}}=\sqrt{\frac{3\sigma}{b\pi}}\overset{D=4}{=}\sqrt{\frac{3\sigma}{2\pi}}\,.

(7)

Notably, if one uses the oft-used value
\sigma\simeq 1\,\text{GeV/fm}
in this formula, one gets
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.31\,\text{GeV}
.
Also, the typical Regge slope
\alpha^{\prime}\simeq 1\,\text{GeV}^{-2}
leads to a similar value
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.28\,\text{GeV}
.

Note the difference between the previous result obtained in Ref.
Huang and Weinberg 1970
within dual resonance model
Veneziano 1974
and ours.
In Ref.
Huang and Weinberg 1970
, the authors obtained a
-5/2
power of
m
in the coefficient, whereas it is
-3/2
in our result;
this exponent
-5/2
by coincidence matched with the exponent in the Hagedorn’s original work
Hagedorn 1965
, corresponding to taking
a=5/2
in Eq. (
4
).
This is because they substituted
D
instead of
b
in Eqs. (
5
,
7
).

III
String description of meson spectrum

Here, we review the open string description of the meson mass spectrum.
We describe a meson as an open color flux tube with a constituent quark and a constituent antiquark attached at its each end.
Such description is already known to work well
Fujimoto et al. 2025
;
Marczenko et al. 2025

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=\pi,K,\eta}d_{i}\theta(m-m_{i})

IV
String description of baryon spectrum

Here, we explore the possibility of describing baryonic mass spectrum below 2 GeV by an open string, given the successful description of mesonic mass spectrum by an open string.
As we already noted in the introduction, this is motivated by the fact that the baryonic spectrum are found to lie on a linear Regge trajectory with a quark-diquark configuration
Wilczek 2004
;
Selem and Wilczek 2006
.

Since each quark is a color triplet, a diquark can form an antisymmetric color
\bm{\bar{3}}_{c}
, or a symmetric color
\bm{6}_{c}
representation.
Normally, a color
\bm{\bar{3}}_{c}
diquark is solely considered because this channel is attractive by one gluon exchange, and the color
\bm{6}_{c}
is unfavored configuration due to large electrostatic field energy.
Thus, in terms of the color representation, the QCD string does not distinguish between an antiquark and the color
\bm{\bar{3}}_{c}
diquark.
This justifies the open string description of baryons as a quark-diquark system.

The Fermi statistics require the total diquark wave function to be antisymmetric.
Since color
\bm{\bar{3}}_{c}
is antisymmetric representation, spin and flavor must be symmetrized in a diquark.
One can either antisymmetrize or symmetrize both indices.
It turns out that spin antisymmetric configuration is favored over the symmetric one.
This is due to the spin-dependent chromomagnetic interaction, derived from the nonrelativistic expansion of the one-gluon exchange amplitude.
This spin antisymmetric diquark is spin singlet and flavor antisymmetric (for non-strange case, this is isosinglet
I=0
) configuration.
We denote such favored “good” diquark configuration as
[qq]
.
One can also consider spin triplet and flavor symmetric (isotriplet
I=1
for light quarks), and we write this “bad” diquark configuration as
(qq)
.

As in the case of mesons, we consider the cumulative mass spectrum for baryons:

\displaystyle N(m)=\int_{0}^{m}dm^{\prime}\,d_{B}(m^{\prime})\rho_{\mathrm{str}}(m^{\prime})\,,

where the string mass spectrum is the same as in Eq. (
6

V
Summary and outlook

We presented a semi-quantitative analysis of baryon mass spectrum using the mass spectrum derived from string theory.
Our description involves minimal modeling in the way that only the quark-diquark composition of baryons and their threshold are modeled to reproduce all the isospin and strangeness states in the physical spectrum.
Threshold mass is fixed to the observed values, and the only free parameter is the Hagedorn temperature
T_{\mathrm{H}}
.
By fitting
T_{\mathrm{H}}
in the string-based spectra to the physical mass spectra, we found the common values
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.34\,\text{GeV}
for both mesonic and baryonic case.
The value is consistent with zero-temperature lattice-QCD calculation, and also with an observation that the baryons and mesons have the same Regge slope in the Chew-Frautchi plot.
Our result further adds supporting argument toward the diquark correlation inside baryons.

With this handy formula of baryonic states at hand, one can now extend our string-model-based study of the QCD phase diagram to the finite chemical potential region.
As a first step, applications to QCD thermodynamics can be considered.
One can also test this behavior by extending to the higher-order cumulants coupled to the chemical potentials.

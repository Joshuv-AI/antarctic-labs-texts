Extensions of homogeneous distributions on deformations to the normal cone
Moudrik CHAMOUX

Abstract




Abstract

On a deformation to the normal cone
\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)
we show that given a distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\setminus V\times\mathbb{R})
if
u
is homogeneous of order
a
for the zoom action, then it admits an
a
-homogeneous extension
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V))
. We describe all such extensions and discuss briefly about how it translates to the work of Van Erp and Yuncken in
[
EY17
]
. The technique used come from the results on the extension of weakly homogeneous distributions provided by Yves Meyer in
[
MS97
]
.

Introduction

In their two papers
[
EY17a
]
,
[
EY17
]
Erik Van Erp and Robert Yuncken, inspired by the work of Claire Debord and Georges Skandalis in
[
DS13
]
, managed to give a purely geometric and algebraic characterization of pseudodifferential operators on a filtered manifold. The starting idea can be traced back to the work of Connes presented in
[
Con94
]
where he states an index theorem using the tangent groupoid

\mathbb{T}M=M\times M\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\sqcup TM\times\{0\}\rightrightarrows M\times\mathbb{R}.

Very roughly the idea is as follows : given a manifold
M
and an elliptic differential operator
D
the index theorem relates the analytical index of
D
with the topological index of
D
(which only depends on
\sigma(D)
the principal symbol of
D
and topological invariants of
M
). The analytical index of
D
can be obtained through the study of the
K
-theory of the
C^{*}
-algebra associated to the pair groupoid
M\times M
whereas the topological index can be retrieved through the study of the
K
-theory of the
C^{*}
-algebra associated to the tangent bundle
TM
where the cosymbol of
D
”lives”. The idea of Connes was to construct from
D
an object
\mathbb{D}
living on
\mathbb{T}M
such that :

1.

\mathbb{D}_{1}=D
,

2.

\mathbb{D}_{0}
is the cosymbol of
D
,

3.

\mathbb{D}=(\mathbb{D}_{t})_{t}
is a continuous family of Fredholm operators.

Then one can use homotopy results to obtain the index theorem by the study of the K-theory associated to the
C^{*}
-algebra associated to
\mathbb{T}M
.

In
[
EY17
]
the authors constructed an explicit realization of a tangent groupoid
\mathbb{T}_{H}M
with respect to a filtration
H
on a manifold
M
in
[
EY17
]
. Then in
[
EY17a
]
they develop the pseudodifferential calculus suggested by
[
DS13
]
. Moreover Erik Van Erp and Robert Yuncken manage to characterize explicitly, differential operators and pseudodifferential operators on
M
compatible with the filtration
H
. So in particular they retrieve (pseudo)-differential calculus in a coordinate free way.

Theorem 0.1

(Characterization of differential operators on
M

[
EY17a
]
)
.

A semiregular kernel
P\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(M\times M)
is the Schwartz kernel of a differential operator of order
a
if and only if
P=\mathbb{P}|_{t=1}
for some
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(\mathbb{T}M)
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times},\ \alpha_{\lambda*}\mathbb{P}=\lambda^{a}\mathbb{P}.

Theorem 0.2

(Characterization of pseudodifferential operators on
M

[
EY17a
]
)
.

A semiregular kernel
P\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(M\times M)
is the Schwartz kernel of a properly supported classical pseudodifferential operator of order
a
if and only if
P=\mathbb{P}|_{t=1}
for some
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(\mathbb{T}M)
such that

\forall,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times},\ \alpha_{\lambda*}\mathbb{P}-\lambda^{a}\mathbb{P}\text{ is a smooth properly supported density of the r-fibers.}

Here
\alpha
denotes the ”zoom action” defined in
[
DS13
]

\alpha:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}_{H}M
. But one should notice that the zoom action was defined in the general context of the Deformation to the Normal Cone of a submanifold
V
in a manifold
M
:
\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)
. Also note that the adiabatic groupoid of
[
DS13
]
and so the tangent groupoid
\mathbb{T}M
are instances of Deformations to the Normal Cone. Also note that the filtered tangent groupoid
\mathbb{T}_{H}M
can be obtained with iterated Deformations to the Normal Cone thanks to
[
Moh20
]
. If Van Erp and Yuncken ruled out the case of r-fibred (quasi)homogeneous distributions on
\mathbb{T}_{H}M
, in this context one might ask the naive question : what do the homogeneous distributions for the zoom action look like on
\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)
? This is what this work was meant to investigate. The initial problem of this work was the following. Given a manifold
M
and an embedded submanifold
V
, take the Deformation to the Normal Cone
\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)
.

Given an
a
-homogeneous distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\setminus V\times\mathbb{R}\right)
, is there an
a
-homogeneous extension
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\right)
, and is it unique ?

1
General results on (weakly) homogeneous distributions

This section presents general results used in the following sections. Given a manifold
M
, we will denote by
C^{-\infty}(M)
the dual of
C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
.

Definition 1.1

(Homogeneous functions, distributions)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}M
be a smooth group action and let
X
be an open subset of
M
stable under this action. Let
f\in\mathbb{C}^{X}
be a function,
f
is called ”positively homogeneous of degree
a\in\mathbb{C}
” or simply ”homogeneous of degree
a\in\mathbb{C}
” if:

\forall s>0,\ \forall x\in X,\ f(\alpha_{s}(x))=s^{a}f(x).

(1)

A distribution
u\in C^{-\infty}(X)
is called ”positively homogeneous of degree
a\in\mathbb{C}
” or simply ”homogeneous of degree
a\in\mathbb{C}
” if:

\forall s>0,\ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X),\ \langle\alpha_{s}^{*}u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\rangle:=\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\alpha_{s^{-1}}^{*}\phi\rangle=s^{a}\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\rangle.

(2)

Let us denote by
H^{a}_{\alpha}(X)
the set of such distributions. When there is no ambiguity we will denote
H^{a}_{\alpha}:=H^{a}_{\alpha}(M)
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\alpha
be the dilation action
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\curvearrowright\mathbb{R}
given by
\forall s>0,\ \forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha_{s}(t)=st
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
the distribution
\partial_{t}^{k}\delta_{0}
is
-k
-homogeneous.

Remark 1.2.1
.

Observe in the above definition that given
a\neq b
, the set
H^{a}_{\alpha}(X)
is a vector space and
H^{a}_{\alpha}(X)\cap H^{b}_{\alpha}(X)=\{0\}
.

Definition 1.3

(
[
Hör15
]
section 2.3)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
then

1.

if
\mathfrak{Re}(a)>0
we denote

t_{+}^{a-1}=[\varphi\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}t^{a-1}\varphi(t)dt]\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

2.

If
\mathfrak{Re}(a)\in]-\infty,0]\setminus\rrbracket-\infty,0\rrbracket
we denote

t_{+}^{a-1}=[\varphi\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}t^{a-1+k}(-1)^{k}\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(t)}{(a-1)(a-2)\ldots(a-1+k-1)}dt]\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R})

where
k=\lfloor-a\rfloor+1
.

3.

If
\mathfrak{Re}(a)\in\rrbracket-\infty,0\rrbracket
we denote

t_{+}^{a-1}=[\varphi\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\ln(t)(-1)^{k}\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(t)}{(a-1)(a-2)\ldots(a-1+k-1)}dt]\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R})

where
k=-a+1
.

We will also use the distribution
t_{-}^{a-1}:=[\varphi\mapsto\langle t_{+}^{a-1}\,,\mathopen{}\varphi(-t)\rangle]\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

Proposition 1.4

(
[
Hör15
]
section 2.3)
.

Given
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
t_{+}^{a-1}
coincides with the function
t^{a-1}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
and
\operatorname{supp}(t_{+}^{a-1})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover if
a\notin\rrbracket-\infty,0\rrbracket
, the distribution
t_{+}^{a-1}
is
a
-homogeneous for the dilation action
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\curvearrowright\mathbb{R}
. If
a\in\rrbracket-\infty,0\rrbracket
one has:

\forall s>0,\ \langle t_{+}^{a-1}\,,\mathopen{}\varphi(t)\rangle=s^{-a}\langle t_{+}^{a-1}\,,\mathopen{}\varphi(st)\rangle-\ln(s)\frac{\varphi^{(-a)}(0)}{(-a)!}.

Proposition 1.5

(Homogeneous distributions for the dilation action)
.

Take any manifold
M
and the action
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\overset{\alpha}{\curvearrowright}M\times\mathbb{R}
given by:

\alpha=s\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}&\to&M\times\mathbb{R}\\
(p,t)&\mapsto&(p,st)\end{bmatrix}.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
and let
u\in C^{-\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})
, assume
u
to be
a
-homogeneous for the above action.
Then there is
v\in C^{-\infty}(M)
such that

u|_{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}=v\otimes t_{+}^{a-1}|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}.

Proof.

Since the distribution spaces are nuclear we only need to evaluate
u
against tensor products
\phi\otimes\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)\otimes C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})
to characterize it.
Choose
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(s)s^{-1}ds=1
then

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\otimes\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho(s)\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\otimes\psi\rangle s^{-1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{a-1}\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}\rho(s)\phi(p)\psi(st)\rangle ds

\displaystyle\text{ By homogeneity of }u.

\displaystyle=\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{-a-1}\rho(s)\phi(p)\psi(st)ds\rangle.

The last equality is given by the continuity of
u
and by the approximation of the parameter integral with a Riemann sum converges in
C^{\infty}_{c}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})
. Finally a change of variable
s\to st^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\otimes\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{-a-1}t^{a}\rho(st^{-1})\phi(p)\psi(s)ds\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}s^{-a}t^{a}\rho(st^{-1})\phi(p)\rangle\psi(s)s^{-1}ds

and since
u
is
a
-homogeneous

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\phi\otimes\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{a}\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}t^{a}\rho(t^{-1})\phi(p)\rangle\psi(s)s^{-1}ds.

Now notice that
v:=[\phi\mapsto\langle u_{p,t}\,,\mathopen{}t^{a}\rho(t^{-1})\phi(p)\rangle]\in C^{-\infty}(M)
and the proof is done.
∎

In
[
MS97
]
, a notion of weak homogeneity is presented for distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This notion can be seamlessly extended to vector bundles.

Let
V
be a manifold, of dimension
m^{\prime}
, let
W
be a vector bundle over
V
of rank
n
. Assume that
\pi:W\to V
is equipped with an Euclidean metric
g
. Consider the dilation action
\alpha:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\curvearrowright W
:

\forall s>0,\ \alpha_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}W&\to&W\\
(p,w)&\mapsto&(p,sw)\end{bmatrix}.

We will denote
W\setminus\{0\}:=W\setminus V\times\{0\}
, this open set is stable by the above action.

Definition 1.6

(Weakly homogeneous distributions around the base)
.

Given
a\in\mathbb{R}
we will denote
O^{a}_{\alpha}
the subset of
C^{-\infty}(W)
of distributions
u
such that:

\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W),\ \exists C(\varphi)>0,\ \forall s\in]0,1],\ |\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\alpha_{s^{-1}}^{*}\varphi\rangle|\leq s^{a}C(\varphi).

And we will denote by
\overset{\circ}{O^{a}_{\alpha}}
the set of distributions on
W\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the above estimate.

Definition 1.7

(Weakly homogeneous distributions)
.

Given
a\in\mathbb{R}
we will denote
E^{a}_{\alpha}
the subset of
C^{-\infty}(W)
of distributions
u
such that:

\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W),\ \exists C(\varphi)>0,\ \forall s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},\ |\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\alpha_{s^{-1}}^{*}\varphi\rangle|\leq s^{a}C(\varphi).

And we will denote by
\overset{\circ}{E^{a}_{\alpha}}
the set of distributions on
W\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the above estimate.

We might omit the
α
subscript in the above spaces when there is no ambiguity on the action.

Proposition 1.8

(Some properties)
.

1.

First notice that for
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
E^{a}_{\alpha}\subset O^{a}_{\alpha}
and
\overset{\circ}{E^{a}_{\alpha}}\subset\overset{\circ}{O^{a}_{\alpha}}
.

2.

Let

3.

4.

One can easily prove that given

5.

Given

.

Lemma 1.15

(Schwartz 1966,
[

Let

u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\iff\left(\exists\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\ \exists k\in\mathbb{N},\ \exists f\in C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ u=\partial^{\alpha}((1+|x|^{2})^{k}f)\right).

This lemma can be adapted to vector bundles to obtain the following.

Lemma 1.16

(Tempered distribution a a vector bundle)
.

Let
u\in S^{\prime}(W)
, then

\exists A\in\operatorname{HomDiff}_{\pi}(W),\ \exists k\in\mathbb{N},\ \exists\mu\in E_{\alpha}^{n}\cap C^{-\infty}_{\operatorname{bc}}(W),\ u=A((1+|X|^{2})^{k}\mu).

Proof.

This proof will be conducted in local charts. Denote by
W\to V
the vector bundle
\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}}

\exists\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{m^{\prime}+n},\ \exists k\in\mathbb{N},\ \exists f\in C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n}),\ u=\partial^{\beta}((1+|x|^{2})^{k}f).

Now let us write

u=\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}((1+|x|^{2})^{k}f).

Since

, we have that

for some

. Now denote

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}\cdot\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}((1+|X|^{2})^{k}\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+|X|^{2}}\right)^{k}f)

\displaystyle=\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}((1+|X|^{2})^{k}\mathcal{X}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}(\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+|X|^{2}}\right)^{k}f)).

A simple integration by part shows that the distribution

\alpha

Remark 1.16.1
.

The previous result, thanks to
Proposition

1.8
, tells us that
u\in S^{\prime}(W)
can be written a sum of weakly homogeneous distribution of integer order, namely

u=A((1+|X|^{2})^{k}\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\begin{pmatrix}k\\
j\end{pmatrix}A(|X|^{2j}\mu).

One can modify this result to obtain a decomposition in a sum of weakly homogeneous distributions of non-integer order. Recall the notations in the above proof above, in local charts we had

\mu=\mathcal{X}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}(\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+|X|^{2}}\right)^{k}f)dx_{\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n}}

with
f\in C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n})
. Take
0<\varepsilon<1
.
Assume

to be a positive function, then define

f_{\varepsilon}:=\min(|X|^{\varepsilon},f),\ f_{-\varepsilon}:=\max(f-|X|^{\varepsilon},0).

One easily sees that
f=f_{\varepsilon}+f_{-\varepsilon}
. Moreover one can prove that
f_{\varepsilon}dx\in E_{\alpha}^{n+\varepsilon}
and conversely
f_{-\varepsilon}dx\in E_{\alpha}^{n-\varepsilon}
. If
f
is complex-valued on can apply this process to the positive and negative parts of the real and imaginary part of
f
and still find such complex-valued
f_{\varepsilon}
and
f_{-\varepsilon}
in
C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n})
. Then one can write

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{X}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}(\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+|X|^{2}}\right)^{k}f_{\varepsilon})dx_{\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n}}

and

\mu_{-\varepsilon}=\mathcal{X}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{\beta_{m^{\prime}}}(\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+|X|^{2}}\right)^{k}f_{-\varepsilon})dx_{\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+n}}

and obtain
\mu=\mu_{\varepsilon}+\mu_{-\varepsilon}
. The

1.8
tells us that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in E_{\alpha}^{n+\varepsilon}\cap C^{-\infty}_{\operatorname{bc}}(W)
and
\mu_{-\varepsilon}\in E_{\alpha}^{n-\varepsilon}\cap C^{-\infty}_{\operatorname{bc}}(W)
. Hence

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}((1+|X|^{2})^{k}(\mu_{\varepsilon}+\mu_{-\varepsilon}))

can be written as a sum of non-integer degree weakly homogeneous functions.

Definition 2.1

(Exponential Charts (

From the previous map we can define a bijection

\mathcal{E}xp:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{E}xp(\mathcal{U})\subset\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\\

(Zoom action

(4)

(Zoom action on

Notice that both the domain and the image of the exponential charts are stable under the respective zoom actions. Moreover one can verify that this zoom action is just

Remark 2.4.1
.

Note that

Thus in the following we will assume without loss of generality that the exponential charts to be global, that is

Lemma 2.5

(Derivations of homogeneous functions)
.

Let

be a smooth function on
\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let

. If

-homogeneous then:

-homogeneous,

For any

the function

-homogeneous.

Proof.

Because derivations are local notions we can work as in a local trivialization of

, let

(7)

Remark 3.0.1

(Notations)

Recall the weak-homogeneity spaces of

Theorem 3.1

(Locally integrable homogeneous distributions)

Let

admits an extension
\dot{T}\in H^{a}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap O_{\beta}^{b}\cap O_{\gamma}^{b-\mathfrak{Re}(a)}

Proof.

Let
T\in H^{a}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\setminus V)\times\mathbb{R}^{*})\cap\overset{\circ}{O_{\beta}^{b}}

1.10
around the base

of the vector bundle
\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}

. Moreover, thanks to

7
, this extension satisfies
\overset{\circ}{T}\in\overset{\circ}{O}^{b-\mathfrak{Re}(a)}_{\gamma}
. Thus
\overset{\circ}{T}
admits a canonical extension
\dot{T}\in C^{-\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap O_{\gamma}^{b-\mathfrak{Re}(a)}
.
Now take a cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
around
0
. One can easily see that
\dot{T}
can be written as a simple limit of

-homogeneous distributions, namely:

\dot{T}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi(n|tX|)\cdot T.

is

-homogeneous.
∎

is the sphere bundle associated to

Then for

\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}^{-1}\beta_{s}\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\\

which means that
\beta
reads as the canonical radial dilation in polar coordinates. By abuse of notation we will also denote by
\beta_{s}
the previous map. We will denote accordingly by
\gamma
and
\alpha
the corresponding actions in this setting. We can extend easily
\beta
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
by the obvious formula.

Recall that we have a submersion extending
✫
and collapsing the sphere bundle

to

given by:

\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 56\relax}}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\\
(r,\omega,t)&\mapsto&(r\omega,t)\end{bmatrix}.

Now our object of study for the following theorem will be
a
-homogeneous distributions for the
\alpha
action on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
and the following theorem will be arguably our most important one. First we need a convenient lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(Characterization of homogeneous distributions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
u\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
. Then
u
is
a
-homogeneous iff

\displaystyle\exists w\in C^{-\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}),\

\displaystyle\forall(f,g)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})\times C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}s^{a-1}f(s)\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{s^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle ds.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
be an
a
-homogeneous distribution. Take
(f,g)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})\times C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
.
Then

\forall s>0,\ \langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle=s^{a}\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\alpha_{s}^{*}(f\otimes g)\rangle.

Now choose
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(s)s^{-1}ds=1
. Now by integrating the last equation we obtain:

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(s)s^{a-1}\langle u\,,\mathopen{}\alpha_{s}^{*}(f\otimes g)\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(s)s^{a-1}\langle u_{r,\omega,t}\,,\mathopen{}f(sr)g(\omega,s^{-1}t)\rangle ds.

Now we can exchange between
u
and the integral for the same reasons as in the proof of
Proposition

1.5
and get

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{r,\omega,t}\,,\mathopen{}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(s)s^{a-1}f(sr)g(\omega,s^{-1}t)ds\rangle.

A change of variable yields

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{r,\omega,t}\,,\mathopen{}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}\rho(r^{-1}s)s^{a-1}r^{-a}f(s)g(\omega,s^{-1}rt)ds\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}s^{-1}f(s)\langle u_{r,\omega,t}\,,\mathopen{}\rho(r^{-1}s)s^{a}r^{-a}g(\omega,s^{-1}rt)\rangle ds.

Finally the
a
-homogeneity of
u
gives

\displaystyle\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}s^{a-1}f(s)\langle u_{r,\omega,t}\,,\mathopen{}\rho(r^{-1})r^{-a}g(\omega,s^{-1}rt)\rangle ds

which concludes the proof. The reciprocal statement is easily checked to be true.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Canonical extensions in polar coordinates and weak homogeneity)
.

Take
u\in H^{a}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
and assume that
u\in E_{\gamma}^{c}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. If
c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)\notin-\mathbb{N}
then
u
admits a canonical
a
-homogeneous extension
\widetilde{u}\in H^{a}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{c+a}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
be an
a
-homogeneous distribution. Thanks to
Lemma

3.2
we can write:

\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}s^{a-1}f(s)\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{s^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle ds.

(8)

A naive way to extend
u
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
would be for the previous formula to make sense for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Now recall that by assumption

\exists C>0,\ \forall s>0,\ |s^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{s^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle|\leq Cs^{c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)}

Then if
c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)>0
by assumption the integrand of
Equation

8
is Riemann-integrable. Then

\tilde{u}=\begin{bmatrix}C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})&\to&\mathbb{C}\\
f(r)\otimes g(\omega,t)&\mapsto&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}s^{a-1}f(s)\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{s^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle ds\end{bmatrix}

defines a continuous map and extends
u
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Now assume
c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)\in]-\infty,0]\setminus\rrbracket-\infty,0\rrbracket
. Denote
k=-\lfloor c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)\rfloor
. In this setting, if we have no guaranty of the integrability of the integrand as in
Equation

8
. However the estimate

\exists C>0,\ \forall s>0,\ |s^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{s^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle|\leq Cs^{c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)}

(9)

still holds.
Now observe that for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})
, by integration by parts,
Equation

8
can be written:

\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}f^{\prime}(s)\int_{+\infty}^{s}t^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{t^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle dtds.

Note that the integral
\int_{+\infty}^{s}\tau^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{\tau^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle d\tau
makes sense thanks to the estimate
Equation

9
. More generally we can integrate (from infinity) up to
k
times this estimate and obtain:

\begin{split}&\forall j\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket,\ \exists C_{j}>0,\ \forall s>0,\\
&\quad\quad\quad\int_{+\infty}^{s}\ldots\int_{+\infty}^{t_{3}}\int_{+\infty}^{t_{2}}t_{1}^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{t_{1}^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle dt_{1}dt_{2}\ldots dt_{j}\leq C_{j}s^{c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)+j}.\end{split}

(10)

We obtain then that for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})
,

\langle u\,,\mathopen{}f\otimes g\rangle=(-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}}f^{(k)}(s)\int_{+\infty}^{s}\ldots\int_{+\infty}^{t_{3}}\int_{+\infty}^{t_{2}}t_{1}^{a-1}\langle w\,,\mathopen{}\gamma_{t_{1}^{-1}}^{*}g\rangle dt_{1}dt_{2}\ldots dt_{k}ds.

But now the above formula still makes sense if
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
since the integrand is Riemann integrable, thanks to
Equation

10
(take
j=k=-\lfloor c+\mathfrak{Re}(a)\rfloor
). This formula easily defines an extension
\dot{u}\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
of
u
. Since every integration is performed from infinity one can easily check that this extension is
a
-homogeneous.
∎

Now notice that this theorem gives us a canonical extension theorem on
(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\setminus V)\times\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed we have the polar coordinates isomorphism inducing:

\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}_{*}=C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{\sim}C^{-\infty}((\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\setminus V)\times\mathbb{R})

and the collapsing extension inducing

\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 56\relax}}}_{*}=C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{}C^{-\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}).

This, given
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that

, gives us a map:

\begin{matrix}H^{a}_{\alpha}((\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\setminus V)\times\mathbb{R})\cap E^{c}_{\gamma}&\hookrightarrow&H^{a}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E^{c}_{\gamma}\\
u&\mapsto&\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 56\relax}}}_{*}\left(\widetilde{\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}^{*}u}\right)\end{matrix}

where
\widetilde{\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}^{*}u}
is the extension of
\text{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\char 59\relax}}}^{*}u
provided by
Theorem

3.3
.

Remark 3.3.1

(Optimality)
.

We cannot loosen the condition on

: it would not work if

. Assume
\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\{0\}
, take the function

If we identify
u
with the distribution

, and with this we have

, it admits a canonical extension in

that is
\frac{-1}{2}
-homogeneous. We will also denote this extension by

admits an

-homogeneous extension

Now our main result.

Theorem 3.4

(Extension theorem for tempered distributions)
.

Let

and let

. Recall the decomposition of

3.2
and assume that

w=u|_{\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}}\in S^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{S}_{V}^{M})

admits an

-homogeneous extension
\widetilde{u}\in S^{\prime}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\to V)
.

Proof.

Just take
w
and write it as a finite sum
w=\sum_{i}w_{i}
of weakly-homogeneous distributions thanks to
Lemma

1.16
. Now choose your favorite
\varepsilon\in[0,1[
such that every weakly-homogeneous summand
w_{i}
of the decomposition provided by
Remark

1.16.1
respects the hypothesis of
Theorem

3.3
. Apply the theorem to each summand
u_{i}=\int_{s>0}s^{a-1}\gamma^{*}_{s}w_{i}ds
, and the sum of the extensions provides us with and
a
-homogeneous extension
\sum_{i}\widetilde{u_{i}}=\widetilde{u}\in C^{-\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
of

. This extension is
S^{\prime}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M})
by

1.16.2
. Since
\widetilde{T}
is
a
-homogeneous one easily show that it implies that
\widetilde{T}\in S^{\prime}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}\to V)
.
∎

Remark 3.4.1
.

The extension provided by this theorem is not canonical, it relies on the decomposition provided by
Lemma

1.16
which is not canonical.

Finally we can now answer our original question: given an
a
-homogeneous distribution
u\in C^{-\infty}(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\setminus V\times\mathbb{R})
does

have an
a
-homogeneous extension on
\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)
? Yes. Is our extension method canonical in some sense? No.

Take
a\in\mathbb{C}
, take
u\in C^{-\infty}(\operatorname{DNC}(M,V)\setminus(V\times\mathbb{R}))
. Recall the setting of
Section

2
. One can easily construct a bump function
\chi:\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\operatorname{supp}(\chi)\subset U
where

is the domain of the
\operatorname{Exp}
map. Notice that
\chi
has proper support for the projection
\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\to V
. The smooth function
\operatorname{Exp}_{*}\chi
can be extended by
0
as a smooth function on
M
. Then the function
h
given by

gives us a smooth bump function around

. This function is trivially
0
-homogeneous for the

-homogeneous distribution on

Take

write it in polar coordinates as:

the weak-homogeneity for the

.

The case of

Finally we can characterize the discrepancy in the uniqueness of the extension. For the following proposition we can assume with a partition of unity argument that
V
is compact. Given
a\in\mathbb{C}
, denote by
H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})
the set of
a

\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
with support in

.

Proposition 3.5

(Characterization of homogeneous distribution supported on
V\times\mathbb{R}
)
.

We have a decomposition of the above set by:

H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})=\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{N}}H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{-b}.

Moreover we have an explicit description of the summand given by four cases.

1.

If
a+b\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then:

\displaystyle H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{-b}=

\displaystyle\{P_{-}\nu_{-}\otimes t_{-}^{-a-b-1}+P_{+}\nu_{+}\otimes t_{+}^{-a-b-1}\ |

\displaystyle\quad P_{-},P_{+}\in\operatorname{HomDiffop}^{b}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}),\ \nu_{-},\nu_{+}\in C^{-\infty}(V)\}.

2.

If
a+b\in-\mathbb{N}^{*}
, then
H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{-b}
is given by:

\displaystyle\{P_{-}\nu_{-}\otimes t_{-}^{-a-b-1}+P_{0}\nu_{0}\otimes\partial_{t}^{-a-b}\delta_{0}+P_{+}\nu_{+}\otimes t_{+}^{-a-b-1}\ |

\displaystyle\quad P_{-},P_{0},P_{+}\in\operatorname{HomDiffop}^{b}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}),\ \nu_{-},\nu_{0},\nu_{+}\in C^{-\infty}(V)\}.

3.

If
a+b=0
, then:

\displaystyle H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{-b}=

\displaystyle\{-P\nu\otimes t_{-}^{-1}+P_{0}\nu_{0}\otimes\delta_{0}+P\nu\otimes t_{+}^{-1}\ |

\displaystyle\quad P,P_{0}\in\operatorname{HomDiffop}^{b}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}),\ \nu,\nu_{0}\in C^{-\infty}(V)\}.

4.

If
a+b\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, then:

\displaystyle H^{a}_{\alpha,V\times\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R})\cap E_{\beta}^{-b}=

\displaystyle\{-P\nu\otimes t_{-}^{-a-b-1}+P\nu\otimes t_{+}^{-a-b-1}\ |

\displaystyle\quad P\in\operatorname{HomDiffop}^{b}(\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}),\ \nu\in C^{-\infty}(V)\}.

Proof.

A distribution on
\mathcal{N}_{V}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}
supported in
V\times\mathbb{R}
is a sum of order
b\in\mathbb{N}
normal derivatives of distributions on
V\times\mathbb{R}
. Thus the direct sum. Now given

Global higher integrability for systems with p p -growth structure in noncylindrical domains
Kristian Moring, Leah Schätzler, Christoph Scheven

Abstract
. We consider the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem to systems with p p -growth structure with 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , whose prototype is ∂ t u − div ⁡ ( | D ​ u | p − 2 ​ D ​ u ) = div ⁡ ( | F | p − 2 ​ F ) , \partial_{t}u-\Div\big(|Du|^{p-2}Du\big)=\Div\left(|F|^{p-2}F\right), in a bounded noncylindrical domain E ⊂ ℝ n + 1 E\subset\mathds{R}^{n+1} . For p > 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) n + 2 p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2} and domains E E that satisfy suitable regularity assumptions and do not grow or shrink too fast, we prove global higher integrability of D ​ u Du . The result is already new in the case p = 2 p=2 .

1.
Introduction

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
T>0
, we suppose that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T)
is a bounded relatively open domain, and we consider the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem to partial differential equations (PDEs) of parabolic
p
-Laplace type

\partial_{t}u-\Div\big(|Du|^{p-2}Du\big)=\Div\left(|F|^{p-2}F\right)\qquad\text{in }E,

where
1<p<\infty
,
u\colon E\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some
N\geq 1
, and
F\in L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})
.
In particular, in the case
N>1
, we are dealing with systems of PDEs.

More generally, denoting the initial and boundary values by
g_{o}
and
g
, respectively, we are concerned with systems with
p
-growth structure of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u-\Div\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)=\Div\left(|F|^{p-2}F\right)&\text{ in }E,\\
u=g&\text{ on }\bigcup_{t\in[0,T)}\partial E^{t}\times\{t\},\\
u=g_{o}&\text{ on }E^{0}\times\{0\}.\end{array}\right.

For the precise assumptions on
E
,
\mathbf{A}
,
g_{o}
, and
g
, we refer to Section
2.1
.
Moreover, the time slice of
E
at a fixed time
t\in[0,T)
is given by

E^{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x,t)\in E\},

such that

E=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T)}E^{t}\times\{t\}.

Viewing
E^{t}
as a spatial set that changes over time instead of focusing on
E
as a
noncylindrical domain
in space-time, the terms
time-varying
or
time-changing domains
have also been used in the literature.
For the existence theory for PDEs of parabolic
p
-Laplace type, and some generalizations in such domains, we refer to
[
3
,
5
,
17
,
23
,
27
,
26
]
.

The motivation for considering PDEs in noncylindrical domains is twofold.
On the one hand, they appear naturally in some physical and biological applications, e.g. the flow of fluids through a container whose walls can be moved, or pattern formation in a growing organism.
For an overview of applications of different PDEs involving a noncylindrical setting, we refer to
[
6
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
28
,
29
]
.
On the other hand, from a purely mathematical point of view, the noncylindrical formulation truly takes the evolutionary nature of the parabolic system (
1
) into account, whereas the cylindrical case
E=\Omega\times[0,T)
constitutes a simpler special case.

In the present paper, we establish the global higher integrability of the gradient of weak solutions
u
to (
1
).
This means that while a priori we only know that
|Du|
is integrable to power
p
, under suitable conditions on
E
,
g_{o}
,
g
, and
F
we prove that
|Du|
is actually integrable to a higher exponent than
p
.
To the best of our knowledge, our result is already new for the case
p=2
in the noncylindrical situation.

Before we state the precise result, we briefly discuss the history of the problem.
For elliptic PDEs of
p
-Laplace type in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, local higher integrability is due to Meyers & Elcrat
[
20
]
, while global higher integrability was established by Kilpeläinen & Koskela
[
11
]
.
In particular, the authors of the latter article assume that the complement of
\Omega
satisfies a
uniform
p
-fatness condition
(see Definition
2.1
below), and show that this condition is essentially optimal.
A key component in their proof is the self-improving property of the uniform
p
-fatness condition that was first established by Lewis
[
18
]
in the more general context of Riesz potentials (see Lemma
2.7
below).

For PDEs of parabolic
p
-Laplace type with quadratic growth
p=2
, Giaquinta & Struwe
[
7
]
prove local higher integrability.
However, their technique does not carry over to the case of more general
p
.
The breakthrough was achieved almost 20 years later by Kinnunen & Lewis
[
13
,
14
]
, who were able to deal with the whole range
\max\big\{1,\frac{2n}{n+2}\big\}<p<\infty
.
Note that the critical exponent
\frac{2n}{n+2}
appears naturally in different areas of regularity theory for the parabolic
p
-Laplace equation.
Their key idea was to use a suitable
intrinsic geometry
to balance the different scalings in the evolution and diffusion terms, i.e., to consider cylinders of the form
Q_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(x_{o},t_{o}):=B_{\varrho}(x_{o})\times(t_{o}-\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2},t_{o}+\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2})
, whose length depends on the integral average of
|Du|^{p}
over the same cylinder via

\lambda^{p}\approx\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-\!-$}}\kern-8.47218pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-5.86113pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-4.56949pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-3.81949pt}\!\iint_{Q_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(x_{o},t_{o})}|Du|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

The main tool in the proof of higher integrability is a
reverse Hölder inequality
for solutions in such intrinsic cylinders, meaning that the integral average of
|Du|^{p}
over an intrinsic cylinder can be estimated from above in terms of the integral average of
|Du|^{q}
with a smaller exponent
q<p
.
Subsequently, Parviainen
[
24
,
25
]
established global higher integrability in the cylindrical setting
\Omega\times[0,T)
under the condition that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is uniformly
p
-fat.
Since this condition is analogous to the elliptic case, it is optimal.

In the noncylindrical setting, we are able to deal with the same level of generality concerning the spatial regularity of
E
.
In addition, we give sufficient assumptions on the speed at which
E
may grow or shrink in time.
It is an interesting question for future research to determine the optimal condition on
E
with respect to the time variable that allows for global higher integrability.
To state our main result, we need the following notation.
For
z_{o}=(x_{o},t_{o})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
R>0
, we denote standard parabolic cylinders by

Q_{R}:=Q_{R}(z_{o})=B_{R}(x_{o})\times\big(t_{o}-R^{2},t_{o}+R^{2}\big).

Further, throughout the paper, let

(1.4)

G:=|Dg|^{p}+|\partial_{t}g|^{\hat{p}^{\prime}}+|F|^{p},

where
\hat{p}^{\prime}
is defined in (
2.8
).
Moreover, letting
\beta
given by (
2.5
) be the exponent in the two-sided condition (
2.4
), the
scaling deficit
is given by

(1.5)

d=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{p}{2}&\text{for }p\geq 2,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{p(2\beta-1)}{\beta(p(n+2)-2n)-2}&\text{for }\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}<p<2.\end{array}\right.

Then, our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}<p<\infty
, suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}
is uniformly
p
-fat in the sense of Definition

2.1
with a fatness constant
\alpha>0
for every
t\in(0,T)
, and assume that
E
satisfies the conditions (
2.1
) with
\ell
given by (
2.1
), and (
2.4
) with some
\beta
in the range given by (
2.5
).
Furthermore, let the operator
\mathbf{A}
be given according to
(
2.1
), and assume that for some
\sigma>0
, the
lateral and initial boundary values
g\in\mathfrak{G}^{\sigma}
and

g_{o}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\sigma}_{o}

(
see (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) for the definition of these spaces
)
satisfy (
2.12
), and the source term
F\in L^{p+\sigma}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})
.
Finally, let
u
be a weak solution to (
1
) according to Definition
2.3
.
Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{o}=\varepsilon_{o}(n,p,C_{o},C_{1},M,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

Du\in L^{p+\varepsilon_{1}}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn}),

where
\varepsilon_{1}=\min\{\varepsilon_{o},\sigma\}
. Furthermore, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{o}]
and any cylinder
Q_{2R}(z_{o})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
z_{o}\in\overline{E}
, the bound

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-\!-$}}\kern-8.47218pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-5.86113pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-4.56949pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-3.81949pt}\!\iint_{Q_{R}}|Du|^{p+\varepsilon}\chi_{E}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-\!-$}}\kern-8.47218pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-5.86113pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-4.56949pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-3.81949pt}\!\iint_{Q_{4R}}(|Du|^{p}+G)\chi_{E}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1+\frac{\varepsilon d}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+c\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-\!-$}}\kern-8.47218pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-5.86113pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-4.56949pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-\!-$}}\kern-3.81949pt}\!\iint_{Q_{2R}}G^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\chi_{E}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{2R}}|Dg_{o}|^{{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\chi_{E^{0}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}+\varepsilon}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}}

holds true for some
c=c(n,p,C_{o},C_{1},M,\alpha,\beta)>0
, where
G
is defined according to (
1.4
) and
d
is given by (
1.5
).

While we follow the same overall strategy as in the cylindrical setting, two key steps in the proof of Theorem
1.1
rely on new ideas.
First of all, the integration by parts formula in Proposition
2.14
, that was first developed in
[
3
]
and then generalized in
[
26
]
, is a crucial component in the proof of the energy estimate near the lateral boundary of
E
in Lemma
3.1
.
Note that it is applicable, since any weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.3
possesses a distributional time derivative, see Remark
2.4
.
In turn, the integration by parts formula is based on the one-sided growth condition (
2.1
) with
\ell
given by (
2.1
).
Note that it would be interesting to determine whether the lower bound
p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}
appearing in this context is optimal.

The next new idea concerns the precise distinction between lateral, initial and interior cylinders, and the proof of reverse Hölder inequalitites for lateral cylinders in the noncylindrical setting, see Lemma
4.4
.
In particular, in this context we need to ensure that all time slices of an intrinsic cylinder
Q_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(x_{o},t_{o})
are close to the lateral boundary
\bigcup_{t\in[0,T)}\partial E^{t}\times\{t\}
if this is the case for one of the time slices of the cylinder.
In this context, the two-sided growth condition (
2.4
) with
\beta
in the range given by (
2.5
) comes into play.
Again, while the lower bound
p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}
is vital in our approach, it would be interesting to determine whether it is optimal.

The article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we give the precise setting, clarify notation, and collect auxiliary results.
Next, in Section
3
we establish an energy estimate, which is used in Section
4
to derive a reverse Hölder inequality close to the lateral boundary.
Moreover, we recall the corresponding results for cylinders away from the lateral boundary from the literature.
We conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
by standard arguments in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Setting

In this section, we give the precise setting.
First of all, we assume that the complement of the time slices of
E
is uniformly
p
-fat in the following sense.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is uniformly
p
-fat if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\operatorname{cap}_{p}\big(A\cap\overline{B}_{\varrho}(x),B_{2\varrho}(x)\big)\geq\alpha\operatorname{cap}_{p}\big(\overline{B}_{\varrho}(x),B_{2\varrho}(x)\big)

for every
x\in A
and
\varrho>0
.

Moreover, we need to control the speed at which
E
is allowed to grow and shrink over time.
To this end, we denote the complementary excess by

\mathbf{e}^{c}(E^{s},E^{t}):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}}\dist(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{s}),\quad\text{ for }s,t\in[0,T).

We ensure that
E
does not grow too fast by assuming that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{c}\big(E^{t},E^{s}\big)\leq|\ell(t)-\ell(s)|\quad\text{for $0\leq s\leq t<T$,}

where the function
\ell\colon(-1,T+1)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
satisfies

\displaystyle\ell\in W^{1,r}(-1,T+1)\mbox{\quad for\quad}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r=\frac{p}{p-1},&\text{if }p\geq 2,\\[6.45831pt]
r=\frac{p(n+2)-2n}{p(n+2)-2(n+1)},&\text{if }\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}<p<2.\end{array}\right.

In addition to (
2.1
), we impose the two-sided condition

(2.4)

\mathbf{e}^{c}(E^{s},E^{t})\leq M|t-s|^{\beta}\quad\text{ for all }s,t\in[0,T)

for some
M>0
(without loss of generality, we assume that
M\geq 1
throughout the paper), and
\beta
satisfying

(2.5)

\beta\in\left(\max\left\{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{2}{p(n+2)-2n}\right\},1\right].

Note that the interval in (
2.5
) is nonempty when
p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

At this point, we would like to discuss why we assume both the one-sided condition (
2.1
) and the two-sided condition (
2.4
) on
E
instead of imposing only one condition on the speed at which
E
can grow and shrink, respectively.
On the one hand, we cannot omit (
2.1
) with
\ell
given by (
2.1
), since the Hölder type condition (
2.4
) does not imply the existence of the required time derivative
\ell^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, (
2.1
) with
\ell
given by (
2.1
) implies a one-sided condition of the type (
2.4
) with
0\leq t\leq s<T
,
M=\|\ell^{\prime}\|_{L^{r}(-1,T+1)}
, and
\beta=\frac{2}{p(n+2)-2n}>\tfrac{1}{2}
in the case
\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}<p<2
, and
\beta=\tfrac{1}{p}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
in the case
p\geq 2
. However, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we need that
\beta>\tfrac{1}{2}
in all cases, see e.g. Lemma
4.1
. Moreover, in the case
\tfrac{2}{p(n+2)-2n}<p<2
, we assume the strict inequality
\beta>\tfrac{2}{p(n+2)-2n}
in order to guarantee that the scaling deficit
d
, defined in (
1.5
), is positive and well defined in order to exploit a suitable stopping time argument in the beginning of Section
5
.

Next, we suppose that in (
1
),
\mathbf{A}\colon E\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nn}\to\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
is a Carathéodory
function satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,\xi)\cdot\xi\geq C_{o}|\xi|^{p},\\[5.0pt]
|\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,\xi)|\leq C_{1}|\xi|^{p-1}\end{array}\right.

for a.e.
(x,t)\in E
and all
(u,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
with constants
0<C_{o}\leq C_{1}
.

We will consider exponents

(2.8)

\hat{p}:=\max\{2,p\},\quad\hat{p}^{\prime}:=\min\left\{2,\tfrac{p}{p-1}\right\},\quad\hat{p}_{*}:=\max\left\{1,\tfrac{n\hat{p}}{n+2}\right\}.

Moreover, we define the parabolic function spaces

V^{p}(E):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N}):Du\in L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})\right\}

and

(2.9)

V^{p}_{2}(E):=V^{p}(E)\cap L^{2}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and consider the subspaces

\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E):=\Big\{u\in V^{p}(E):u(t)\in W^{1,p}_{0}(E^{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})\mbox{ for a.e.\penalty\ $t\in[0,T)$}\Big\}

and

\mathcal{V}^{p,0}_{2}(E):=\Big\{u\in V^{p}_{2}(E):u(t)\in W^{1,p}_{0}(E^{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})\mbox{ for a.e.\penalty\ $t\in[0,T)$}\Big\}.

We equip these spaces with the norms

\|v\|_{V^{p}(E)}:=\|Dv\|_{L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and

\|v\|_{V^{p}_{2}(E)}:=\|Dv\|_{L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})}+\|v\|_{L^{\hat{p}}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})},

respectively.
As usual,
(\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E))^{\prime}
and
(\mathcal{V}^{p,0}_{2}(E))^{\prime}
equipped with the respective operator norms denote the dual spaces of
\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E)
and
\mathcal{V}^{p,0}_{2}(E)
.
For the lateral and initial boundary data, we define the spaces

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}^{\sigma}=\big\{g\in L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N}):

\displaystyle\;Dg\in L^{p+\sigma}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn}),g\chi_{E}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N}))

\displaystyle\text{ and }\partial_{t}g\in L^{\hat{p}^{\prime}(1+\frac{\sigma}{p})}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})\big\},

and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{o}^{\sigma}=W^{1,\hat{p}_{*}+\sigma}(E^{0},\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(E^{0},\mathbb{R}^{N}),

respectively, where
\sigma\geq 0
.
We consider lateral data
g\in\mathfrak{G}^{\sigma}
and initial data

g_{o}\in\mathfrak{G}_{o}^{\sigma}
that are compatible in the sense

(2.12)

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{0}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g-g_{o}|^{2}\chi_{E}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{h\to 0}0.

Now, we are ready to define weak solutions to (
1
).

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that the vector field
\mathbf{A}\colon E\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nn}\to\mathbb{R}^{Nn}
satisfies (
2.1
) and
F\in L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{Nn})
. We call a map
u\in V^{p}_{2}(E)
, with
u\chi_{E}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, a weak solution to (
1
) with lateral boundary values
g\in\mathfrak{G}^{0}
and initial boundary values
g_{o}\in\mathfrak{G}^{0}_{o}
if

(u-g)(t)\in W^{1,p}_{0}(E^{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\mbox{ for a.e.\penalty\ $t\in(0,T)$},

(2.13)

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{0}^{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u-g_{o}|^{2}\chi_{E}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{h\to 0}0,

and

(2.14)

\iint_{E}u\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi-\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)\cdot D\varphi\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{E}|F|^{p-2}F\cdot D\varphi\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 2.4
.

We note that under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
,
every weak solution
u
to (
1
) in the sense of
Definition
2.3
satisfies
\partial_{t}u\in(\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E))^{\prime}
.
To verify this claim, we consider a weak solution
u
in the above sense and a test function

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Equation (
2.14
), assumption (
2.1
) and
Hölder’s inequality imply

\displaystyle|\langle\partial_{t}u,\varphi\rangle|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\iint_{E}u\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg|=\bigg|\iint_{E}\big(\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)+|F|^{p-2}F\big)\cdot D\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{E}\big(C_{1}|Du|^{p-1}+|F|^{p-1}\big)|D\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\big(C_{1}\|Du\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p-1}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(E)}^{p-1}\big)\|\varphi\|_{V^{p}(E)}.

Consequently, the distributional time derivative

\partial_{t}u\colon C^{\infty}_{0}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous with respect to
the
V^{p}(E)
-norm. Moreover, since
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}

is uniformly
p
-fat in the sense of Definition
2.1

for every
t\in(0,T)
and (
2.4
) holds
true, Lemma
2.12
implies
that
C^{\infty}_{0}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in

\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E)
with respect to the
V^{p}(E)
-norm. Therefore, the time derivative can be
extended in a unique way to a bounded linear operator

\partial_{t}u\colon\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E)\to\mathbb{R}
. In this sense, we have

\partial_{t}u\in(\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E))^{\prime}
.

2.2.
Further notation

Later on, we will use the following notation.
For a point
z_{o}=(x_{o},t_{o})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
we consider cylinders in space-time of the form

Q_{r,s}(z_{o}):=B_{r}(x_{o})\times\Lambda_{s}(t_{o}),

where
B_{r}(x_{o})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the ball with center
x_{o}
and radius
r>0
, and

\Lambda_{s}(t_{o}):=(t_{o}-s,t_{o}+s);

further, for
\lambda>0
intrinsic cylinders are defined by

Q_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(z_{o}):=B_{\varrho}(x_{o})\times\Lambda_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o}),

where

\Lambda_{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o}):=(t_{o}-\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2},t_{o}+\lambda^{2-p}\varrho^{2}).

For the sake of simplicity, we will omit
z_{o}
,
x_{o}
and
t_{o}
in our notation when it is clear from the context.

Next, for sets
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and functions
u
and
v
defined in
A
and
U
, respectively, we write

\gamma

3.
Energy

In this section, we establish energy estimates, also called
Caccioppoli estimates for cylinders near the lateral boundary of

E
. The interior case and the case of cylinders close to the initial
boundary that do not touch the lateral boundary
are analogous to the case of cylindrical domains, see
[
1
,
4
]
.

The following energy estimate is well-known in cylindrical domains, see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
.
In the noncylindrical situation, a key ingredient of the proof is the integration by parts formula in Lemma
2.14
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}
and assume that
u
is a weak solution according to Definition
2.3
with lateral and initial boundary values
g\in\mathfrak{G}^{0}
and

g_{o}\in\mathfrak{G}_{o}^{0}
that satisfy (
2.12
). Suppose that
E
satisfies (
2.1
) with
\ell
given by (
2.1
), and (
2.4
), and that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}
is uniformly
p
-fat for every
t\in[0,T)
. Let
Q_{R,S}(z_{o})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
0<r<R\leq 1
and
0<s<S\leq 1
. Then, there exists
c=c(C_{o},C_{1},p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\Lambda_{s}(t_{o})\cap(0,T)}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t}}|u(t)-g|^{2}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{Q_{r,s}(z_{o})\cap E}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{Q_{R,S}(z_{o})\cap E}\frac{|u-g|^{p}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{|u-g|^{2}}{S-s}+G\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

holds true.

Proof..

For
t_{1}\in\Lambda_{s}(t_{o})\cap(0,T)
we define

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{t-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon},&\text{for }t\in(\varepsilon,2\varepsilon],\\[5.0pt]
1,&\text{for }t\in(2\varepsilon,t_{1}-2\varepsilon],\\[5.0pt]
\frac{t_{1}-\varepsilon-t}{\varepsilon},&\text{for }t\in(t_{1}-2\varepsilon,t_{1}-\varepsilon],\\[5.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\tilde{\eta}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be given by
\tilde{\eta}(\sigma)=\min\{\max\{\sigma,0\}^{\max\{2/p,1\}},1\}
and set
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
,

\eta(x):=\tilde{\eta}\left(1-\frac{\dist(x,B_{r}(x_{o}))}{R-r}\right).

Then, we have that
\eta^{p},\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}\in W^{1,\infty}(B_{R}(x_{o});[0,1])
with
\eta\equiv 0
outside of
B_{R}(x_{o})
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{r}(x_{o})
, and
|D\eta|\leq\frac{c}{R-r}
.
Further, let
\zeta\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Lambda_{S}(t_{o}),[0,1]\right)
be defined by

\displaystyle\zeta(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{t-t_{o}+S}{S-s},&\text{for }t\in(t_{o}-S,t_{o}-s),\\[5.0pt]
1,&\text{for }t\geq t_{o}-s.\end{array}\right.

Now, note that
\partial_{t}u\in(\mathcal{V}^{p,0}(E))^{\prime}
by Remark
2.4
.
Rewriting (
2.14
) as

(3.1)

\langle\partial_{t}u,\varphi\rangle+\iint_{E}\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)\cdot D\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=-\iint_{E}|F|^{p-2}F\cdot D\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

and using that
C^{\infty}_{0}(E,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
\mathcal{V}^{p,0}_{2}(E)
by Lemma
2.12
, and that
u-g\in\mathcal{V}^{p,0}_{2}(E)
by Definition
2.3
, we may choose

\varphi(x,t):=\eta^{p}(x)\zeta(t)\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)(u(x,t)-g(x,t))

as a testing function in (
3.1
).
For the parabolic part, i.e. the first term on the left-hand side of (
3.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}u,\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}(u-g)\big\rangle=\big\langle\partial_{t}(u-g),\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}(u-g)\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{t}g,\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}(u-g)\big\rangle=:\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II},

where the definition of
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
is clear from the context.
By Proposition
2.14
applied to

\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}(u-g)
, assumption (
2.12
), and the initial condition (
2.13
), we find that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}|u-g|^{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\zeta+\zeta^{\prime}\psi_{\varepsilon})\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}\cap E^{t_{1}}}\eta^{p}|u-g|^{2}\zeta\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta^{\prime}|u-g|^{2}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

On the one hand, in the case
p>2
we use Young’s inequality and the fact that
R-r\leq 1
to get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}g\cdot(u-g)\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq-\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}|\partial_{t}g|^{p^{\prime}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t-c(p)\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u-g|^{p}}{(R-r)^{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

On the other hand, if
\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}\leq p<2
, Young’s inequality and the fact that
S-s\leq 1
yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\geq-\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}|\partial_{t}g|^{2}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t-c\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\eta^{p}\zeta\psi_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u-g|^{2}}{S-s}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

For the divergence part, i.e. the second term on the left-hand side of (
3.1
), by the structural conditions (
2.1
) and Young’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\iint_{E}

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)\cdot D[\eta^{p}(u-g)]\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p}\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)\cdot D(u-g)\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+p\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p-1}(u-g)\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)\cdot D\eta\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq C_{o}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t-C_{1}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p}|Du|^{p-1}|Dg|\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-C_{1}p\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p-1}|u-g||Du|^{p-1}|D\eta|\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{C_{o}}{2}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t-c(C_{o},C_{1},p)\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}|Dg|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-c(C_{o},C_{1},p)\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\frac{|u-g|^{p}}{(R-r)^{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

For the term on the right-hand side of (
3.1
), Young’s inequality gives us that

\displaystyle-\iint_{E}

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta|F|^{p-2}F\cdot D[\eta^{p}(u-g)]\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{C_{o}}{4}\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta\eta^{p}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+c(C_{o},p)\iint_{Q_{R,S}\cap E}\frac{|u-g|^{p}}{(R-r)^{p}}+|Dg|^{p}+|F|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

Inserting the preceding estimates into (
3.1
) and passing to the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we infer

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t_{1}}}

\displaystyle|u-g|^{2}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{(B_{r}(x_{o})\times(t_{o}-s,t_{1}))\cap E}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{Q_{R,S}(z_{o})\cap E}\frac{|u-g|^{p}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{|u-g|^{2}}{S-s}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c\iint_{Q_{R,S}(z_{o})\cap E}|F|^{p}+|Dg|^{p}+|\partial_{t}g|^{\hat{p}^{\prime}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

Noting that both terms on the left-hand side of the preceding inequality are non-negative, taking the supremum over
t_{1}\in\Lambda_{s}
in the first term, and passing to the limit
t_{1}\uparrow t_{o}+s
in the second term, we conclude the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.

Our goal in this section is to derive reverse Hölder inequalities.
To this end, for
z_{o}\in E
,
\lambda>0
,
\beta\in\big(\tfrac{1}{2},1\big]
, and

(4.1)

0<\varrho\leq\bigg(\frac{\min\left\{1,\lambda^{(p-2)\beta}\right\}}{2^{7\beta-3}M}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2\beta-1}},

we will distinguish between the following cases.
On the one hand, if

(4.2)

B_{8\varrho}(x_{o})\setminus E^{t}\neq\varnothing\text{ for some }t\in\Lambda_{8\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o})\cap(0,T),

the cylinder is near the lateral boundary; we refer to Section

B_{8\varrho}(x_{o})\setminus E^{t}=\varnothing\text{ for all }t\in\Lambda_{8\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o})\cap(0,T)\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\in\Lambda_{2\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o});

we are dealing with cylinders near the initial boundary; see Section

.
In Section

we consider the remaining case of interior cylinders, i.e., cylinders such that

B_{8\varrho}(x_{o})\times\Lambda_{2\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o})\subset E.

4.1.
Near the lateral boundary

In this section, we prove a reverse Hölder inequality for cylinders near the lateral boundary of

.
We start with the following lemma that shows that if

for all times

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
E
satisfies (
2.4
), that
\lambda>0
, and that the radius

satisfies (
4.1
) for some

B_{16\varrho}(x_{o})\setminus E^{t}\neq\varnothing\quad\text{ for every }t\in\Lambda_{8\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o})\cap(0,T).

Let
s\in\Lambda_{8\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(t_{o})\cap(0,T)
denote the time in (

) at which
B_{8\varrho}(x_{o})\setminus E^{s}\neq\varnothing
.

, and further

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we prove the following boundary Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}
be uniformly
p
-fat with fatness constant
\alpha>0
, and let
u(t)\in g(t)+W^{1,p}_{0}(E^{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Moreover, assume that for
x_{o}\in E^{t}
and

we have that
B_{\frac{r}{3}}(x_{o})\setminus E^{t}\neq\varnothing
.
Then, there exists an exponent
\gamma=\gamma(n,p,\alpha)
such that for every
\gamma\leq\vartheta\leq p

\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t}}\bigg|\frac{u-g}{r}\bigg|^{\vartheta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t}}|D(u-g)|^{\vartheta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x

holds true with a constant

.

Proof..

By Theorem
2.8
and Lemma
2.7
, there exists
\gamma=\gamma(n,p,\alpha)
such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E^{t}
is uniformly
\vartheta
-fat for any
\gamma\leq\vartheta\leq p
with the fatness constant
\alpha_{\vartheta}=a_{\vartheta}(n,p,\vartheta,\alpha)
.
Extending
u-g\in W^{1,\vartheta}_{0}(E^{t},\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
B_{r}(x_{o})\setminus E^{t}
by zero, and choosing a
\vartheta
-quasicontinuous representative, by Lemma
2.9
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t}}

\displaystyle|u-g|^{\vartheta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n,\vartheta)r^{n}}{\operatorname{cap}_{\vartheta}\big(N_{B_{r/2}(x_{o})}(u-g),B_{r}(x_{o})\big)}\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})\cap E^{t}}|D(u-g)|^{\vartheta}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

N_{B_{r/2}(x_{o})}(u-g):=\big\{x\in\overline{B_{r/2}(x_{o})}:u(x)-g(x)=0\big\}.

Note that according to
[

, Theorem 5.26]
, the representative
of

can be chosen such that
u-g=0

by
\lambda
to simplify notation, we deduce

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{E}(R_{1},\lambda)}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\mathbf{E}(R_{2},\lambda)}\lambda^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t+c\iint_{\mathbf{G}(R_{2},\lambda)}G\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c(R_{2}-R_{1})^{n+2-n\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\mathbf{E}(R_{2},\lambda)}\lambda^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t+c\iint_{\mathbf{G}(R_{2},\lambda)}G\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c|Q_{R_{2}}|\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}}

for any
\lambda\geq\eta B\lambda_{o}
, where
c=c(n,p,C_{o},C_{1},\alpha,\beta,M)
.
We denote

|Du|_{k}=\min\{|Du|,k\},

and

\mathbf{E}_{k}(r,\lambda)=\left\{z\in Q_{r}\cap E:|Du|_{k}(z)>\lambda\right\}.

Note that
\mathbf{E}_{k}(r,\lambda)=\varnothing
if
k\leq\lambda
, and
\mathbf{E}_{k}(r,\lambda)=\mathbf{E}(r,\lambda)
if
k>\lambda
.
Therefore, in the truncated level sets, the last inequality above implies

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda)}|Du|_{k}^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{2},\lambda)}\lambda^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t+c\iint_{\mathbf{G}(R_{2},\lambda)}G\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c|Q_{R_{2}}|\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}}.

We multiply the preceding inequality by
\lambda^{\varepsilon-1}
and integrate over
(\lambda_{1},\infty)
, where
\lambda_{1}:=\eta B\lambda_{o}
.
Since
\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)\subset\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})
for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, and by Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\varepsilon-1}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}}{\hat{p}_{*}}-1}\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\varepsilon-1}\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}-\hat{p}_{*}}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R_{2}}|}\int_{\mathbf{G}_{o}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}+\varepsilon}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}}.

Analogously, by Fubini’s theorem we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\varepsilon-1}\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda)}|Du|_{k}^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda_{1})}\left(|Du|_{k}^{p+\varepsilon-q}|Du|^{q}-\lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}|Du|_{k}^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\right)\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t,

that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-q+\varepsilon-1}\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{2},\lambda)}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{p-q+\varepsilon}\iint_{\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}|Du|_{k}^{p-q+\varepsilon}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t,

and that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\varepsilon-1}\iint_{\mathbf{G}(R_{2},\lambda)}G\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\iint_{\mathbf{G}(R_{2},\lambda_{1})}G^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, by definition of
\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda_{1})
we conclude that

\displaystyle\iint_{(Q_{R_{1}}\cap E)\setminus\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda_{1})}

\displaystyle|Du|_{k}^{p+\varepsilon-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}\int_{(Q_{R_{1}}\cap E)\setminus\mathbf{E}_{k}(R_{1},\lambda_{1})}|Du|_{k}^{p-q}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t.

By combining all the estimates we have that

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R_{1}}\cap E}|Du|_{k}^{p-q+\varepsilon}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{*}\varepsilon}{p-q}\iint_{Q_{R_{2}}\cap E}|Du|_{k}^{p-q+\varepsilon}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}G^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c|Q_{2R}|\left(\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{2R}\cap E^{0}}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}+\varepsilon}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}},

with a constant
c_{\ast}=c_{\ast}(n,p,C_{o},C_{1},M,\alpha,\beta)\geq 1
.
Then, we choose

\varepsilon_{o}=\frac{p-q}{2c_{*}}<1

and consider
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{o}
.
Since
\lambda_{1}^{\varepsilon}\leq B\lambda_{o}^{\varepsilon}
, recalling the definition of
B
we obtain that

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R_{1}}\cap E}|Du|_{k}^{p-q+\varepsilon}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{R_{2}}\cap E}|Du|_{k}^{p-q+\varepsilon}|Du|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c\left(\frac{R}{R_{2}-R_{1}}\right)^{(n+2)\frac{d}{p}}\lambda_{o}^{\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}G^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c|Q_{2R}|\left(\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{2R}\cap E^{0}}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}+\varepsilon}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}}.

By using the iteration lemma, i.e., Lemma
2.11
, and
passing to the limit
k\to\infty
by means of Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R}\cap E}|Du|^{p+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\lambda_{o}^{\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}|Du|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t+c\iint_{Q_{2R}\cap E}G^{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+c|Q_{2R}|\left(\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{2R}\cap E^{0}}|Dg_{o}|^{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\hat{p}+\varepsilon}{\hat{p}_{*}+\varepsilon}}.

Recalling the definition of
\lambda_{o}
and dividing by
|Q_{R}|
, this concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

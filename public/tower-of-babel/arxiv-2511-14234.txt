Large deviations for number of irreducible divisors of the Dirichlet series distribution
Michael Cranston, Mariia Khodiakova

Abstract
In this paper we produce precise large deviation estimates through the lens of mod-Poisson convergence. We apply a general result to various examples from number theory, Dedekind domains and polynomials over finite fields when an element is selected using a distribution based on a Dirichlet series.



Keywords:
Dirichlet distribution, Large deviations, zeta functions, mod-Poisson.

AMS Subject Classification:

11A41, 11R42, 11R59, 60F10

1
Introduction

In this paper we use Dirichlet series to sample elements from commutative semigroups
R
that are endowed with an addition operation
+
and multiplication operator
\cdot
, which is distributive over
+
, with a multiplicative identity element
1
, and without zero divisors. We assume a statement analogous to the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic holds, i.e. there is some set of prime elements and unique factorization, holds in
R
. In addition, we will need some notion of size of the elements that is a positive integer valued, multiplicative function on
R.
This role will be played by the function
b
which appears in definition of the Dirichlet series at (
1.1
) below.
For instance,
R
could be the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
or a unique factorization domain (factorial ring) (
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}[x]
) or a Dedekind domain. We shall further assume that the set of multiplicative invertible elements
E
is finite;

R
can have an additive identity element
0
, but this is not required.
That is why, further, if there is no
0
in
R
we will mean
R
writing
R\setminus\{0\}
.

On account of our assumption about the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, there is a well defined greatest common divisor of two elements, which is the set of common divisors that are divisible by all other common divisors.
Using that we can introduce a notion analogous to that of a multiplicative function on the positive integers.

Definition 1.1
.

A non-negative real-valued function
a
is multiplicative on
R\setminus\{0\}
if
a(nm)=a(n)a(m)
for any
n,m\in R\setminus\{0\}
such that
\gcd(n,m)=E

and
a(e)=1
for any
e\in E
.

The function
a(n)
is completely multiplicative on
R\setminus\{0\}
if
a(nm)=a(n)a(m)
for any
n,m\in R\setminus\{0\}
and
a(e)=1
for any
e\in E
.

Suppose we are given a commutative semigroup
R
as above and let
a
and
b
be multiplicative functions on
R\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
a(n)\geq 0
and
b(n)\geq 1
for all
n\in R\setminus\{0\}
.
Then define a Dirichlet series by

\displaystyle A(z)=\sum_{n\in R\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a(n)}{b(n)^{z}},

(1.1)

that is an analytic function in
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z>\kappa
for some real
\kappa
.

In the article
[
CM1
]
, the authors considered the Riemann zeta distribution of a positive integer-valued random variable.
Similar to that work, we will consider the distribution of the
R
-valued non-zero random variable
X_{s}
,
s>\kappa
, based on the Dirichlet series and defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(X_{s}=n)=\frac{a(n)}{A(s)\,b(n)^{s}},\quad n\in R\setminus\{0\}.

(1.2)

We will call this the Dirichlet series distribution. In our applications, the quantity
b(n)
can be thought of as a measure of the size of the element
n.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of irreducible elements in
R
.
Now we obtain some basic properties of this distribution.
For any irreducible element
p\in\mathcal{P}
and natural number
\alpha
we get the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{P}\left(p^{\alpha}|X_{s}\right)=&\sum_{k\in R\setminus\{0\}}\mathbf{P}\left(X_{s}=p^{\alpha}k\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{A(s)}\sum_{k\in R\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a\left(p^{\alpha}k\right)}{b(p^{\alpha}k)^{s}}\\
=&\frac{1}{A(s)}\sum_{k:\gcd(k,p)=E}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a\left(p^{\alpha+m}k\right)}{b(p^{\alpha+m}k)^{s}}\\
=&\frac{1}{A(s)}\sum_{k:\gcd(k,p)=E}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a\left(p^{\alpha+m}\right)a\left(k\right)}{b(p^{\alpha+m})^{s}b(k)^{s}}\\
=&\frac{1}{A(s)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a\left(p^{\alpha+m}\right)}{b(p^{\alpha+m})^{s}}\sum_{k:\gcd(k,p)=E}\frac{a\left(k\right)}{b(k)^{s}}\\
=&\frac{A_{p^{\alpha}}(s)}{1+A_{p}(s)},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle A_{p^{\alpha}}(s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a\left(p^{\alpha+m}\right)}{b(p^{\alpha+m})^{s}}=\sum_{m=\alpha}^{\infty}\frac{a\left(p^{m}\right)}{b(p^{m})^{s}},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.3)

A similar computation shows that for any distinct irreducible elements
p,q\in\mathcal{P}
and natural numbers
\alpha
,
\beta,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(p^{\alpha}|X_{s},q^{\beta}|X_{s}\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(p^{\alpha}|X_{s}\right)\mathbf{P}\left(q^{\beta}|X_{s}\right).

We consider two classic functions of number theory.
The first one is the number of distinct irreducible divisors

\displaystyle\omega(n)=\#\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p|n\}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}I_{p|n},

and the second one is the number of irreducible divisors counted with multiplicities

\displaystyle\Omega(n)=\#\{p\in\mathcal{P},\alpha\in\mathbb{N}:p^{\alpha}|n\}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}I_{p^{\alpha}|n}.

In the papers
[
CP
]
,
[
CM1
]
and
[
CM2
]
, a central limit theorem was proven for
\omega(X_{s})
and
\Omega(X_{s}).
When
R
is the set of positive integers,
a(n)\equiv 1
and
b(n)=n
and
s\searrow 1
the function
A
at (
1.1
) is the Riemann zeta function. In contrast, we also consider
\omega(X_{s})
,
\Omega(X_{s})
as
s\searrow\kappa
, but compare their distribution with the Poisson distribution instead of the normal distribution and formulate some limit theorems using large deviation theory obtained using mod-Poisson convergence.

The work is organized as follows.
In section
2
we consider the necessary information that will be used in the paper.
In section
3
we formulate and prove the main results about the number of irreducible divisors of
X_{s}
, counted without multiplicities.
In section
4
we obtain similar results for the number of irreducible divisors of
X_{s}
, counted with multiplicities.
In section
5
we consider examples of various integer-valued Dirichlet series distributions.
In section
6
we consider non-numeric applications for ideals in Dedekind domains and random polynomials with coefficients in a finite field.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\{\xi_{n}:n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of real-valued random variables, and denote by

\phi_{n}(z)=\mathbf{E}e^{z\xi_{n}}
their moment generating functions. We suppose these exist for all
n
in a fixed strip

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}=\{z:c<\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z<d\},\quad c<0<d.

We also assume that
\phi
is a non-constant infinitely divisible distribution with moment generating function

\displaystyle e^{\eta(z)}\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{zx}\phi(dx)

that is well defined on
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
, and that
\psi(z)
is an analytic function that does not vanish on the real part of
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the random sequence
\{\xi_{n}:n\geq 1\}
converges mod-
\phi
as
n\to\infty
on
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}

with parameters
t_{n}
and limiting residue function
\psi
if

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(z)\equiv e^{-t_{n}\eta(z)}\phi_{n}(z)\to\psi(z),\quad n\to\infty,

locally uniformly over
z\in\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
,
where
t_{n}
is some sequence tending to
+\infty
.

In addition, we say that the sequence of random variables

\{\xi_{n}:n\geq 1\}
converges mod-
\phi
at speed

O\left(t_{n}^{-v}\right)
if for any compact subset
K
of
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
there is a positive constant
C_{K}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{n}(z)-\psi(z)\right|<C_{K}t_{n}^{-v}

for any
z\in K.

We can use the similar definition with the
o(\cdot)

notation. In this paper we will be dealing not with a sequence of random variables, but rather with a family of random variables parametrized by real values
s
greater than some value
\kappa.
Then the limit in the definition of mod-Poisson convergence will be as
s
goes down to
\kappa
rather than as
n
goes to
\infty,

all else being the same.
In the general case, this notation was used in the papers

[
FMN1
]
and
[
FMN2
]
.
When
\phi
is the standard Gaussian,
in that case
\eta(z)=z^{2}/2,
or standard Poisson distribution, where

\eta(z)=e^{z}-1,
we talk about mod-Gaussian or mod-Poisson convergence, respectively.
One can read more about these convergences in the articles

[
JKN
]
or
[
KN1
]
, respectively.

The notion of mod-
\phi
convergence is related to the
Legendre-Fenchel transform. The Legendre-Fenchel transform of a function
\eta
is defined by

\displaystyle F(x)=\sup_{h\in\mathbb{R}}(hx-\eta(h)).

(2.1)

This is an involution on convex lower semi-continuous functions.
For instance, if we consider mod-Poisson convergence then
\eta(h)=e^{h}-1
and the
\sup
is attained at
h=\ln x,
so that

\displaystyle\quad F(x)=\begin{cases}x\ln x-x+1,\quad x>0,\\
+\infty,\quad x\leq 0.\end{cases}

Also, there is a connection between mod-
\phi
convergence and the Cramer transform.
The Cramer transform of a random variable
\xi
with a parameter

h\in[H^{-},H^{+}]
for some
H^{-}\leq 0\leq H^{+}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\xi^{(h)}\in dx\right)=R(h)^{-1}e^{hx}\mathbf{P}(\xi\in dx),\quad R(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{hx}\mathbf{P}(\xi\in dx)=\mathbf{E}e^{h\xi}<\infty.

The distribution of the random variable

\xi^{(h)}
is called the conjugate distribution with a parameter
h
with respect to the distribution of
\xi
.
We can obtain that for any parameter
h\in[H^{-},H^{+}]
the following is true:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\xi^{(h)}=\left(\ln{R(h)}\right)^{\prime},\quad\mathbf{D}\xi^{(h)}=\left(\ln{R(h)}\right)^{\prime\prime}.

Note that if
\psi\equiv 1
then the function
\ln R(h)
corresponds to
\eta(h)
and we can consider Legendre-Fenchel transform of the function
\ln R(h)
.

We will need the following statements for lattice random variables from the paper
[
FMN2
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\xi
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued random variable whose generating function
\phi_{\xi}(z)=\mathbf{E}e^{z\xi}

converges absolutely in the strip
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}.
Then

\displaystyle\forall h\in(c,d)\quad\mathbf{P}(\xi=k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-k(h+iu)}\phi_{\xi}(h+iu)du,

\displaystyle\forall h\in(0,d)\quad\mathbf{P}(\xi\geq k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{-k(h+iu)}}{1-e^{-(h+iu)}}\phi_{\xi}(h+iu)du.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose a sequence of random variables
\{\xi_{n}:n\geq 1\}
converges mod-
\phi
with parameters
t_{n}
and residue function
\psi
at speed
o(1)
.
Let
F
be given by (
2.1
) and
h
be defined by the implicit equation

\eta^{\prime}(h)=x
.

1.

The following expansion holds:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\xi_{n}=t_{n}x)=\frac{e^{-t_{n}F(x)}}{\sqrt{2\pi t_{n}\eta^{\prime\prime}(h)}}\psi(h)(1+o(1)),\quad n\to\infty,

where
o(1)
is uniformly small over
x\in(\eta^{\prime}(c),\eta^{\prime}(d))
,

t_{n}x\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\xi_{n}\geq t_{n}x)=\frac{e^{-t_{n}F(x)}}{\sqrt{2\pi t_{n}\eta^{\prime\prime}(h)}}\frac{1}{1-e^{-h}}\psi(h)(1+o(1)),\quad n\to\infty,

where
o(1)
is uniformly small over
x\in(\eta^{\prime}(0),\eta^{\prime}(d))
,

t_{n}x\in\mathbb{N}
.

3.

By applying the result to
-\xi_{n}
, one gets

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\xi_{n}\leq t_{n}x)=\frac{e^{-t_{n}F(x)}}{\sqrt{2\pi t_{n}\eta^{\prime\prime}(h)}}\frac{1}{1-e^{-|h|}}\psi(h)(1+o(1)),\quad n\to\infty,

where
o(1)
is uniformly small over
x\in(\eta^{\prime}(c),\eta^{\prime}(0))
,

t_{n}x\in\mathbb{N}
.

3
Number of distinct irreducible divisors of
X_{s}

Now we consider the random variables
\omega(X_{s})
where
X_{s}
,

s>\kappa
, has the Dirichlet series distribution at (
1.2
).
The expectation and variance of
\omega(X_{s})
are given by the following:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\omega(X_{s})=\mathbf{E}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}I_{p|X_{s}}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{A_{p}(s)}{1+A_{p}(s)}=:\mathbb{P}(s),

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\omega(X_{s})=\mathbf{E}\omega^{2}(X_{s})-\left(\mathbf{E}\omega(X_{s})\right)^{2}=\mathbb{P}(s)-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{A_{p}(s)}{1+A_{p}(s)}\right)^{2}.

The quantity
A_{p}(s)/(1+A_{p}(s))
comes up frequently so we shall simplify notation by defining

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}(s)=\frac{A_{p}(s)}{1+A_{p}(s)}.

We will make the following assumptions:

{\bf{A1}}

There exists a positive number
\kappa
such that
A(s)
converges for any
s>\kappa
.

{\bf{A2}}

A(s)\nearrow+\infty
as
s\searrow\kappa
.

{\bf{A3}}

There exists an enumeration of irreducible elements
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{i},i\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\displaystyle b(p_{i})\to\infty,\quad i\to\infty,

\displaystyle A_{p_{i}}(\kappa)\to 0,\quad i\to\infty.

(3.1)

From now on, our enumerations of
\mathcal{P}
we will satisfy this condition.

{\bf{A4}}

For any natural numbers
n\geq 2
,
l\geq 1
and real
s\geq\kappa,

\displaystyle\sum_{i>l}\alpha^{n}_{p_{i}}(s)\leq\frac{C(n,l)}{b(p_{l})^{\,n(s-(\kappa-1))}}

(3.2)

for some positive function
C(n,l)
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{C(n,l)}{b(p_{l})^{\,n(s-(\kappa-1))}}=0,

(3.3)

and for all
l
and for some
c>0,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}C(n,l)^{1/n}\leq 1/c.

(3.4)

{\bf{A5}}

For any
p\in\mathcal{P},
the derivative
A^{\prime}_{p}(s)
exists for any
s\geq\kappa,
and

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}A^{\prime}_{p}(s)A_{p}(s)<\infty.

A quantity that plays a large role in this paper is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)\equiv\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\alpha^{n}_{p}(s),\quad n\geq 1,

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{1}(s)\equiv\mathbb{P}(s).

Observe that due to
{\bf{A4}}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\omega(X_{s})=\mathbb{P}(s)-\mathbb{P}_{2}(s)=\mathbb{P}(s)+O(1),\quad s\searrow\kappa.

The next Lemma is the analog of Euler’s famous equation:

\displaystyle\ln\zeta(s)=\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+O(1),\quad s\searrow 1,

where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume conditions
{\bf{A1-A4}}
hold. Then

\mathbb{P}(s)
converges for any
s>\kappa
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(s)\asymp\ln A(s),\quad s\searrow\kappa.

Proof.

Since
a
and
b
are multiplicative functions,

\displaystyle A(s)=

\displaystyle\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(p^{\alpha})}{b(p^{\alpha})^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1+A_{p}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln\left(1+A_{p}(s)\right)\right).

Due to condition
{\bf{A3}}
, (
3.1
),
\exists\,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A_{p_{i}}(s)\leq A_{p_{i}}(\kappa)<1
for all
i>N.

Then we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(s)=&\exp\left(\sum_{i\leq N}\ln\left(1+A_{p_{i}}(s)\right)\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i>N}\ln\left(1+A_{p_{i}}(s)\right)\right)\\
=&\exp\left(\sum_{i\leq N}\ln\left(1+A_{p_{i}}(s)\right)\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i>N}\left(A_{p_{i}}(s)-\frac{A^{2}_{p_{i}}(s)}{2}+o\left(A^{2}_{p_{i}}(s)\right)\right)\right)\\
=&\exp\left(\sum_{i\leq N}\left(\ln\left(1+A_{p_{i}}(s)\right)-A_{p_{i}}(s)\right)-\sum_{i>N}\frac{A^{2}_{p_{i}}(s)}{2}+o(1)\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{p_{i}}(s)\right),\end{split}

(3.6)

where according to
{\bf{A4}}
the first factor of the last equality in (
3.6
) is bounded as
s\searrow\kappa.

This implies that

\ln A(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{p_{i}}(s)+O(1).

But

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{p_{i}}(s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(1+A_{p_{i}}(s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{2}_{p_{i}}(s))}{1+A_{p_{i}}(s)}

and by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{2}_{p_{i}}(s))}{1+A_{p_{i}}(s)}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{p}(1+A_{p}(\kappa))\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{2}_{p_{i}}(s)=O(1).

Thus,
\mathbb{P}(s)
converges for any
s>\kappa
and

\displaystyle\ln A(s)\asymp\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}A_{p}(s)\right)\asymp\mathbb{P}(s),\quad s\searrow\kappa.

Lemma
3.1
is proved.
∎

We now consider the mod-
\phi
convergence for
\omega(X_{s})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume conditions
{\bf{A1-A5}}
are satisfied and let the random variables
X_{s},s>\kappa
, have the Dirichlet series distribution given at (
1.2
). Then
\omega(X_{s})
converges mod-Poisson as
s\searrow\kappa
on
\mathbb{C}
with parameters
t_{s}=\mathbb{P}(s)
and residue function

\displaystyle\psi(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p}(\kappa)(e^{z}-1)\right)-\alpha_{p}(\kappa)(e^{z}-1)\right)\right).

(3.7)

The mod-Poisson convergence occurs at speed

o(1)
,
s\searrow\kappa
.

Proof.

Due to independence of the events
\{p|X_{s}\}
and
\{q|X_{s}\}
, where
p
,
q
are different irreducible elements, we get the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{s}(z)=&\mathbf{E}e^{z\omega(X_{s})}\\
=&\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1+\alpha_{p}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)\\
=&\exp\left(\mathbb{P}(s)(e^{z}-1)-\mathbb{P}(s)(e^{z}-1)+\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)\right)\\
=:&e^{\mathbb{P}(s)(e^{z}-1)}\psi_{s}(z),\end{split}

where
\eta(z)=e^{z}-1
corresponds to the standard Poisson distribution.
For mod-Poisson convergence with parameters
t_{s}\equiv\mathbb{P}(s)
and residue function
\psi(z)\equiv\psi_{\kappa}(z)
we need to prove that the function
\psi(z)
is well defined on some strip
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
and does not vanish on the real
part of
\mathcal{S}_{(c,d)}
.
Let
e^{\vartheta_{s}(z)}\equiv\psi_{s}(z)
,
s\geq\kappa
.
Since the point
z
is fixed, due to condition
{\bf{A3}}
there exists
an
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|1-e^{z}|<c\,b(p_{l})
,
where
c
is from condition (
3.4
).
So in this domain we get

\displaystyle\psi_{s}(z)=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{i\leq l}\left(\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)-\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{i>l}\left(\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(1-e^{z})-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)^{n}\frac{(1-e^{z})^{n}}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{i\leq l}\left(\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)-\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i>l}\alpha_{p_{i}}(s)^{n}\right)\frac{(1-e^{z})^{n}}{n}\right).

Using condition
{\bf{A4}},
part (
3.2
),
for any
l\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
and

s\geq\kappa,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i>l}\alpha^{n}_{p_{i}}(s)\right)\frac{(1-e^{z})^{n}}{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{C(n,l)}{n}\left(\frac{|1-e^{z}|}{b(p_{l})^{s-(\kappa-1)}}\right)^{n},

where the power series on the right side of the inequality converges for
z
in a strip containing the origin due to the root test using (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
It means that
\vartheta_{s}(z)
and consequently
\psi_{s}(z)
are well defined and holomorphic functions in the domain

\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{s}
, where

\mathbb{S}_{s}\equiv\left\{-\ln A_{p}(s)+i(\pi+2\pi k),\,p\in\mathcal{P},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
,
s\geq\kappa
.

But since
\phi_{s}(z)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1+\alpha_{p}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)
we see that
\psi_{s}(z)=0
for any complex number
z\in\mathbb{S}_{s},\,\,s\geq\kappa
.
That is why
\psi(z)
is well defined on
\mathbb{C}
due to its continuity and does not vanish on the real
part of
\mathbb{C}
.
Let us now consider the speed of mod-Poisson convergence.
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}

we have

\displaystyle|\psi_{s}(z)-\psi(z)|=|\psi(z)(f(s,z)-1)|=|\psi(z)f_{s}^{\prime}(\kappa,z)|(s-\kappa)+o(s-\kappa),\quad s\searrow\kappa,

where
f(\kappa,z)=1
, and

\displaystyle f(s,z)=\exp\left(-(\mathbb{P}(s)-\mathbb{P}(\kappa))(e^{z}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p}(s)(e^{z}-1)\right)-\ln\left(1+\alpha_{p}(\kappa)(e^{z}-1)\right)\right)\right).

The derivative of
f(s,z)
with respect to
s
is equal to

\displaystyle f_{s}(s,z)=f(s,z)(e^{z}-1)^{2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{A^{\prime}_{p}(s)A_{p}(s)}{(1+A_{p}(s))^{2}(1+A_{p}(s)e^{z})}.

Hence, if
s=\kappa
then

\displaystyle f_{s}(\kappa,z)=(e^{z}-1)\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{A^{\prime}_{p}(\kappa)A_{p}(\kappa)}{(1+A_{p}(\kappa))^{2}(1+A_{p}(\kappa)e^{z})},

where the series is well defined for any

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{\kappa}
according to condition
{\bf{A5}}
and the Weierstrass M-test.
Thus, we can define

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose conditions
{\bf{A0-A4}}
and
\widetilde{{\bf{A5}}}
are satisfied and let

be a Poisson random variable with parameter

t_{s}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{N_{s}-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{n+1}(\kappa)\right)

holds.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}=\frac{[t_{s}+k\sqrt{t_{s}}]}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}-\sqrt{t_{s}}\right).

Then due to Fourier transform and Lemma
2.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iu\left(\frac{[t_{s}+k\sqrt{t_{s}}]}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}-\sqrt{t_{s}}\right)}\widetilde{\phi}_{s}(iu)du,

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\phi}_{s}(z)\equiv&\mathbf{E}e^{z\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}}\\
=&e^{-z\sqrt{t_{s}}}\mathbf{E}e^{\frac{z}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\Omega(X_{s})}\\
=&e^{-z\sqrt{t_{s}}}e^{t_{s}\left(e^{z/\sqrt{t_{s}}}-1\right)}\psi_{s}\left(z/\sqrt{t_{s}}\right)\end{split}

and the function
\psi_{s}(z)
,
s\geq\kappa
, is defined in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
3.3
using mod-Poisson convergence of
\Omega(X_{s})
,
s\searrow\kappa
, with parameters
t_{s}
and residue function
\psi(z)
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{N_{s}-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+o(1)}{4\pi t_{s}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{n+1}(s)\right)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{-iut}}{1-e^{-iu/\sqrt{t_{s}}}}u^{2}e^{-u^{2}/2}du

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+o(1)}{4\pi\sqrt{t_{s}}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{n+1}(s)\right)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iut}e^{-u^{2}/2}u\,du

because there are the following equalities:

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{s}(z)=\psi_{s}(z)\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)(e^{nz}-e^{z})\right),

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}_{s}(z)=\psi_{s}(z)\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)(e^{nz}-e^{z})\right)^{2}+\psi_{s}(z)\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)(ne^{nz}-e^{z})\right).

Hence, when
s
is large enough

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{N_{s}-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{t_{s}}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{n+1}(s)\right)\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iut}e^{-u^{2}/2}u\,du\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{n+1}(\kappa)\right),

where the series on the right hand side of the last inequality converges due to conditions
{\bf{A3}}
and
{\bf{A4}}
.

Similarly, we can consider the case when

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}\geq t\right)=\sum_{k=t+1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\Omega(X_{s})-t_{s}}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}=\frac{[t_{s}+k\sqrt{t_{s}}]}{\sqrt{t_{s}}}-\sqrt{t_{s}}\right)

and obtain the analogous result.

Theorem
4.3
is proved.
∎

The Riemann zeta function

Let

In the article

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(n|X_{s},m|X_{s})=\mathbf{P}(n|X_{s})\mathbf{P}(m|X_{s}),\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\quad\gcd(n,m)=1.

In the papers

]

6.1
Random polynomials

In this subsection we demonstrate an application of mod-Poisson convergence tools in the context of randomly selected monic polynomials over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is prime or a power of a prime.
We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
the polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and define a norm on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
by

\displaystyle|F|=q^{\deg F},\quad F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x].

Notice that
|FG|=|F|\cdot|G|
for any non-zero
F,G\in F_{q}[x]
.

Denote by
\mathbb{P}_{q}[x]
and
\mathbb{M}_{q}[x]
the monic irreducible polynomials and monic polynomials, respectively, in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Denote by
\mathbb{P}_{q,k}[x]
and
\mathbb{M}_{q,k}[x]
the monic irreducible polynomials and monic polynomials of degree

, respectively.
We note that as in
[

]
,

\displaystyle\#\mathbb{M}_{q,k}[x]=q^{k},

\displaystyle\#\mathbb{P}_{q,k}[x]=\frac{q^{k}}{k}+O\left(\frac{q^{k/2}}{k}\right),\quad k\to\infty.

The analog of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta
is then

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(z)=\sum_{F\in\mathbb{M}_{q}[x]}\frac{1}{|F|^{z}}=\prod_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{q}[x]}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|^{z}}\right)^{-1}.

Let us consider
R=\mathbb{M}_{q}[x]
, where
E=\{1\}
,
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{P}_{q}[x]
.
Let
a(F)=1
and
b(F)=|F|
for any

These functions are completely multiplicative and
b(F)\geq 1
.
Hence,
A(z)=\zeta_{q}(z)
.
From the first expression for
\zeta_{q}
we derive for
s>1
the following Dirichlet series:

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{F\in\mathbb{M}_{q,k}[x]}\frac{1}{|F|^{s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{F\in\mathbb{M}_{q,k}[x]}q^{-ks}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k}}{q^{ks}}=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}.

(6.1)

We remark that this makes it clear that
\zeta_{q}(z)
is analytic function in
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z>1
(so
\kappa=1
) with no zeroes on
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z=1
. It is meromorphic with a simple pole at
z=1
with a residue of
1/\ln q
.

We now describe how to sample a monic polynomial from
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
using
\zeta_{q}(z)
and examine some simple properties of the sampled random polynomial.
Fix
s>1
and denote the randomly selected polynomial by
F_{s}
. It will have distribution given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(F_{s}=F)=\frac{1}{\zeta_{q}(s)|F|^{s}},\quad F\in\mathbb{M}_{q}[x].

We will call this the
q
-zeta distribution. The random polynomial
F_{s}
has a factorization into a product of irreducible monic polynomials, and writing

to denote that the monic polynomial
F
appears in the factorization of

.
In this section, we will establish large deviation and Berry-Esseen estimates for
\omega(F_{s}),
the number of distinct irreducible factors and
\Omega(F_{s}),
the total number of irreducible factors of
F_{s}.
The
q
-zeta distribution has the following associated quantities:

\displaystyle A_{P^{\alpha}}(s)=\frac{1}{|P|^{\alpha s}(1-1/|P|^{s})},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{q}[x],\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(F|F_{s})=\frac{1}{|F|^{s}},\quad F\in\mathbb{M}_{q}[x],

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(F|F_{s},G|F_{s})=\frac{1}{|F|^{s}}\frac{1}{|G|^{s}}=\mathbf{P}(F|F_{s})\mathbf{P}(G|F_{s}),\quad F,G\in\mathbb{M}_{q}[x].

.

The random variables
\omega(F_{s})
,

on
\mathbb{C}
with parameters

and residue function

at speed

.

3.2
,
3.3
and

hold for
\omega(F_{s})
under the

-zeta distribution.

Proof.

We show the conditions

are satisfied in this case. The first three conditions

are obvious, where for condition

we choose an enumeration of

that satisfies

for any

.
Let us check condition
{\bf{A6}}

) we get
\zeta_{q}(z)
has a simple pole at
z=1
with residue

This implies that condition

We now consider condition

Notice that in the present case

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{P:|P|>q^{l}}\frac{1}{|P|^{ns}}=&\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{q,k}[x]}\frac{1}{q^{kns}}\\

) we can take

3.1
and remark
3.1
to finish the proof.

.

6.2
Dedekind Domains

In this subsection we demonstrate how mod-Poisson convergence can be applied to sampling random ideals in Dedekind domains.
We start with a number field
K
, i.e. an extension field of
\mathbb{Q}
of finite degree.
The ring of integers of
K
, denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, is the ring of all algebraic integers in
K
.
An algebraic integer is the root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients.
In any
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, there will be unique (up to order) factorization of ideals into a product of prime ideals.

There is a well-defined norm on ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
This is denoted by
N
and is defined as

\displaystyle N(I)=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:I]=|\mathcal{O}_{K}/I|.

A key property of the norm is that it is multiplicative: for any ideals
I
,
J
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,

\displaystyle N(I\,J)=N(I)N(J).

Define
\mathcal{I}_{K}=\{I:I\text{ is an ideal in }\mathcal{O}_{K}\}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{K}\subset\mathcal{I}_{K}
be the set of prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Further, we will use
Landau’s prime number Theorem (PNT).

Theorem 6.3
.

(Landau)
If for
x>0
,
\rho(x)=\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}|N(\mathfrak{p})\leq x\}
then

\displaystyle\rho(x)\sim\frac{x}{\ln x},\quad x\to\infty.

Let us consider
R=\mathcal{I}_{K}
, where
E=\{\mathcal{O}_{K}\}
,
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{K}
is the set of prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Let
a(I)=1
and
b(I)=N(I)
for any
I\in\mathcal{I}_{K}\setminus\{0\}
, these functions are completely multiplicative and
b(I)\geq 1
.
Hence, we get the following series

\displaystyle A(z)=\zeta_{K}(z)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{K}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{N(I)^{z}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

This is called the Dedekind zeta function and is analytic in
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z>1
(so
\kappa=1
).
As in the case of the Riemann zeta function, the Dedekind zeta function has an extension to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}.

This extension has a simple pole at
z=1
with residue
c_{K},
which is the class number (see
[
M
]
or
[
L
]
).
The actual value of
c_{K}
will not play a role in our development.

We can now define an
\mathcal{I}_{K}
-valued random variable,

I_{s}
for
s>1,
that has distribution under some fixed probability measure
\mathbf{P}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(I_{s}=I)=\frac{1}{\zeta_{K}(s)N(I)^{s}},\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{K}\setminus\{0\}.

We call this the Dedekind zeta distribution.
The random ideal
I_{s}
has a factorization into a product of prime ideals, and writing
I|I_{s}
to denote that the ideal
I
appears in the factorization of
I_{s}
. We will establish large deviation and Berry-Esseen estimates for
\omega(I_{s}),
the number of distinct factors, and
\Omega(I_{s}),
the total number of factors factors of
I_{s}.

This distribution has the following associated quantities:

\displaystyle A_{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}(s)=\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{\alpha s}(1-1/N(\mathfrak{p})^{s})},\quad\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(I|I_{s})=\frac{1}{N(I)^{s}},\quad I\in\mathcal{I}_{K}\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}|I_{s},\mathfrak{q}^{\beta}|I_{s})=\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{\alpha s}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{q})^{\beta s}}=\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}|I_{s})\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q}^{\beta}|I_{s}),\quad\mathfrak{p}\not=\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}.

Theorem 6.4
.

The random variables
\omega(I_{s}),\,\,s>1
, converge mod-Poisson as
s\searrow 1
on
\mathbb{C}
with parameters

t_{s}=\mathbb{P}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}

and residue function

\displaystyle\psi(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}\left(\ln\left(1+\frac{e^{z}-1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)-\frac{e^{z}-1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)\right)

at speed
o\left(\left(\mathbb{P}(s)\right)^{-\vartheta}\right)
for any
\vartheta>0
.

Moreover, Theorems
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
hold for
\omega(I_{s})
under the Dedekind zeta distribution.

Proof.

We show the conditions
{\bf{A1-A6}}
are satisfied. The first three conditions
{\bf{A1-A3}}
are obvious, where for condition
{\bf{A3}}
we choose an enumeration of
\mathcal{P}_{K}=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i},i\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
N(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\leq N(\mathfrak{p}_{i+1})
for all
i\geq 1
.
Condition
{\bf{A6}}
is fulfilled with

[
M
]
(chapter 7) there exists the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 1}\frac{\zeta_{K}(s)}{\zeta(s)}=c_{K},

Let us check condition
{\bf{A4}}

Notice that
\mathbb{P}_{n}(s)=\mathbb{P}(ns)

Put

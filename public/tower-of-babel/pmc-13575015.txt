Irreversibility analysis in stagnation-point flow of Casson bioconvective hybrid nanofluid under chemical reaction and stratification effects
Authors: Mujeeb Ur Rahman, Ghada A Khouqeer, Fazal Haq, Naglaa AbdelAll, Jihad Younis, Mohammed Sallah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEntropy generation (EG) in hybrid nanofluid flows (HNFs) plays a crucial role in determining the efficiency and performance of thermal systems. It serves as a measure of irreversibility within these complex fluid flows, which directly impacts energy transfer and thermal conductivity. By analyzing EG, we can assess how effectively HNFs minimize energy losses, ultimately leading to improved overall efficiency in thermal management systems. The present study focuses on the analysis of heat transfer and EG in stratified bioconvective and magnetohydrodynamic Casson HNF flow near the stagnation point. The flow in HNF is generated due to stretched sheet. The surface of the sheet is considered porous. Radiation, heat source, and Joule heating effects are considered in the expression for thermal energy. While reporting the concentration equation impact of a chemical reaction is accounted. The bioconvection phenomenon within HNF is considered. The HNF is formed by inserting copper and silver nanoparticles in engine oil-based Casson liquid. The EG for the flow considered is modeled by using the second law of thermodynamics. The flow representing model equations is transformed into a non-dimensional system through transformations. To solve the dimensionless reduced system, we have implemented the NDSolve code in Mathematica. Behavior of HNF versus diverse parameters is graphically analyzed. Engineering quantities are scrutinized numerically. The results reveal that the HNF velocity profile decreases with increasing magnetic and porosity parameters, whereas the HNF temperature rises with higher magnetic parameter and Eckert number but decreases with increasing Prandtl number. The concentration field is reduced under stronger chemical reaction effects. Moreover, EG significantly increases with the Brinkman number and diffusion parameters, indicating enhanced irreversibility. The findings provide important insights into optimizing thermal efficiency and minimizing energy losses in advanced engineering and bio-thermal systems utilizing hybrid nanofluids.Keywords: Entropy generation, Chemical reaction, Casson hybrid nanofluid, Bioconvection, Heat source

Introduction

Hybrid nanofluid is an advanced class of nanofluid formed by dispersing two or more different types of nanoparticles (such as metals, metal oxides, or carbon-based materials) within a conventional base fluid like water, ethylene glycol, or oil. This combination is designed to synergistically enhance thermal and physical properties beyond what is achievable with single-particle nanofluids. The presence of multiple nanoparticles allows for tailored thermophysical characteristics, such as optimized viscosity and enhanced convective heat transfer performance. Consequently, HNFs have gained significant attention in recent years as a promising solution for improving the efficiency of heat transfer systems in both engineering and technological applications. HNFs exhibit improved thermal conductivity, heat transfer rate, and stability, making them highly attractive for applications in energy systems, cooling technologies, biomedical devices, and industrial processes. When gyrotactic microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, protozoa, algae) are added to a nanoliquid or hybrid nanoliquid, the bioconvection process happens. In the bio-convection process, the self-propelled gyrotactic microorganisms, which are denser than the base fluid, move upward against gravity regularly. Due to upward swimming of microorganisms density stratification occurs in the nanoliquid, and the concentration of microorganisms at the top layer increases. Consequently, these microorganisms fall down and again swim upward, and this process continues. By adding microorganisms in a base fluid, the heat and mass transfer abilities of HNF increase; furthermore, the instability of the base fluid increases. Nanoliquids with gyrotactic microorganisms have vast applications in micro reactors, biomedicine, microbial enhanced oil recovery, biosensors, and biotechnology [25–29]. Saleem et al. Significant progress in the investigation of bioconvection in fluid flows has been comprehensively reported in the literature (see Refs. [37–41]).

Entropy generation plays a critical role in evaluating the thermodynamic performance and irreversibility of HNF flows, mainly in advanced heat transfer systems. In nanofluids, EG arises due to multiple mechanisms, including heat transfer across finite temperature gradients, fluid friction, Joule heating, and surface porosity. The presence of nanoparticles enhances thermal conductivity, which can reduce thermal irreversibility; however, it may simultaneously increase viscous dissipation, leading to competing effects on total entropy production. Analyzing EG enables the identification of optimal operating conditions that minimize energy losses and maximize system efficiency. The EG is particularly important in engineering applications such as microchannel heat sinks, combustion engines, pollution control, air conditioners, refrigerators, renewable energy devices, and power plants [42–46]. Ajaykumar et al. [47] analyzed optimization of entropy in stratified radiative Casson-Williamson nanoliquid flow by exponentially stretching curved sheet with the methodology of response surface and sensitivity analysis. Jamel et al. [48] reported Maxwell nanofluid flow over a permeable stretchable sheet having Joule heating along with Darcy-Forchheimer relation and Fourier heat flux model. Wang et al. [49] numerically studied radiative nanofluid flow over a surface with irreversibility. Maria et al. [50] scrutinized water-based chemically reactive HNF flow over a porous stretchable sheet with slip and Joule heating impacts. Alharbi et al. [51] disclosed heat transfer and EG in a MHD convective nanofluid flow by curved stretching surface with cross diffusion and heat source influences. Rabia et al. [52] numerically explored irreversibility analysis in radiative nanoliquid flow by a wedge with aggregation effect. Khan et al. [53] reported EG in a couple stress magneto stagnation point water-based bio-nanofluid flow by permeable stretching sheet having gyrotactic microorganisms. Kayalvizhi and Vijaya [54] disclosed stagnation point radiative EMHD nanoliquid flow over a permeable stretchable sheet with influences of melting heat transfer and viscous dissipation. Naseem and Shazad [55] discovered heat transfer in a thin film of water-based hybrid MHD nanoliquid flow over an unsteady sheet with dissipation and Joule heating effects. Aslam et al. [56] scrutinized the EG production in thermo-HNF by an absorbent pile-cap enclosure containing an equally heated pair of circular cylinders under the influence of uniform magnetic flux.

This study uniquely integrates bioconvection due to motile microorganisms, entropy generation analysis based on the second law of thermodynamics, and stratification effects within a Casson HNF framework near a stagnation point. While these aspects have been explored individually or in limited combinations, their combined influence on flow behavior, thermal transport, and irreversibility characteristics remains largely unexplored in the existing literature. Moreover, the inclusion of entropy generation alongside bioconvection provides a deeper thermodynamic perspective, which is rarely reported for such systems. Physical quantities

In bioconvective HNF flows, key physical quantities such as skin friction, Nusselt number, Sherwood number, and motile density number are essential for describing transport phenomena at the surface boundary. The skin friction coefficient represents the wall shear stress and characterizes the viscous resistance exerted by the fluid on the stretching surface. The Nusselt number quantifies heat transfer and indicates the effectiveness of thermal energy transport within the system. The Sherwood number describes the rate of mass transfer, reflecting the influence of species diffusion and chemical reactions on concentration distribution. The motile density number represents the distribution of microorganisms responsible for bioconvection and provides insight into their transport and accumulation within the fluid. Collectively, these parameters offer a comprehensive understanding of momentum, heat, mass, and bioconvective transport behaviors in the flow system. Conclusions

This study presents a comprehensive analysis of entropy generation in an engine oil–based bioconvective, stratified stagnation-point MHD flow of a Casson hybrid nanofluid containing silver and copper nanoparticles over a porous stretching sheet. The model incorporates key physical effects, including thermal radiation, Joule heating, chemical reaction, and bioconvection, enabling a detailed thermofluid and thermodynamic characterization of the system. The present study offers significant implications for advanced thermal and transport engineering systems involving engine oil–based Casson HNF. The results demonstrate that magnetic field, porosity, and Casson effects can effectively regulate flow resistance, while thermal radiation, heat generation, and nanoparticle enhancement improve heat transfer performance. The inclusion of bioconvection and entropy generation analysis provides deeper insight into microorganism transport and thermodynamic irreversibility, enabling identification of conditions that minimize energy losses. These findings are highly relevant to applications in engine cooling and lubrication systems, electronic cooling devices, microchannel heat sinks, and bio-thermal technologies, where efficient thermal management and energy optimization are essential.

Limitations of the present ptudy

The present investigation is based on several simplifying assumptions that facilitate the mathematical modeling and numerical solution. The flow is assumed to be steady, laminar, and two-dimensional, while the hybrid nanofluid is considered homogeneous, with a uniform dispersion of Ag–Cu nanoparticles throughout the base fluid. The effects of nanoparticle agglomeration, sedimentation, and particle shape are neglected. In addition, the thermophysical properties of the hybrid nanofluid are assumed to remain constant, except where explicitly incorporated into the adopted correlations. The analysis is restricted to the Rosseland diffusion approximation for thermal radiation and does not account for non-equilibrium thermal effects. Furthermore, the study is entirely numerical, and the results have not been validated through experimental measurements. These assumptions may limit the direct applicability of the.

Future research directions

The future research directions may include extending the analysis to ternary or tetra-hybrid nanofluids, incorporating more complex non-Newtonian models, and considering unsteady or three-dimensional flow configurations. Further enhancements can be achieved by including slip boundary conditions, variable thermophysical properties, and more advanced bioconvection models. Experimental validation and optimization of entropy generation for practical thermal systems, as well as exploration of complex geometries, are also promising avenues for future work.

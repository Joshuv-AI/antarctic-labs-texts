Linear independence of periods for the symmetric square L L -functions
Tianyu Ni, Hui Xue

Abstract
. For S k S_{k} , the space of cusp forms of weight k k for the full modular group, we first introduce periods on S k S_{k} associated to symmetric square L L -functions. We then prove that for a fixed natural number n n , if k k is sufficiently large relative to n n , then any n n such periods are linearly independent. With some extra assumption, we also prove that for k ≥ e 12 k\geq e^{12} , we can always pick up to log ⁡ k 4 \frac{\log k}{4} arbitrary linearly independent periods. Résumé. Pour S k S_{k} , l’espace des formes parabolique de poids k k pour le groupe modulaire complet, nous d’abord introduire des périodes sur S k S_{k} associées à des L L -fonctions carrées symétriques. Nous prouvons alors que pour un nombre naturel fixé n n , si k k est suffisamment grand par rapport à n n , alors n n de telles périodes sont linéaires indépendant. Avec quelques hypothèses supplémentaires, nous prouvons également que pour k ≥ e 12 k\geq e^{12} , nous pouvons toujours choisir jusqu’à log ⁡ k 4 \frac{\log k}{4} périodes arbitraires linéairement indépendantes.

1.
Introduction

In a paper from 1979, Deligne
[
3
]
proposed a conjecture on the algebraicity of values of motivic
L
-functions at critical points in terms of motivic periods. A special family of examples are the symmetric power
L
-functions associated to modular forms. For each even integer
k\geq 4
, let
M_{k}
be the space of modular forms of weight
k
for the full modular group
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and let
S_{k}
be its subspace of cusp forms. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)e^{2\pi inz}\in S_{k}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform such that
a(1)=1
. For each prime
p
, we denote by
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
the roots of the polynomial

X^{2}-a(p)X+p^{k-1}.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. The
m^{{\rm th}}
symmetric power
L
-function of
f
for
\re(s)
sufficiently large is defined as

(1.1)

\displaystyle L_{m,f}(s)=\prod_{p}\prod_{0\leq i\leq m}(1-\alpha_{p}^{m-i}\beta_{p}^{i}\cdot p^{-s})^{-1},

and these functions are conjectured
[
16
]
to have an analytic continuation to the whole complex plane and to satisfy the following functional equation for the completed
L
-function:

(1.2)

\displaystyle L^{\ast}_{m,f}(s)

\displaystyle:=\gamma_{m}(s)\cdot L_{m,f}(s)=\pm L^{\ast}_{m,f}((k-1)m+1-s),

where
\gamma_{m}
is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(s):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(2\pi)^{-rs}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\Gamma(s-j(k-1))&m=2r-1,\\
\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}-\lfloor\frac{r(k-1)}{2}\rfloor\right)\gamma_{2r-1}(s)&m=2r.\end{cases}

See also
[
23
, p.177-178]
for details.

In particular, if
m=1
, then (
1.2
) is known as the functional equation of the standard
L
-function of the Hecke eigenform
f
in
S_{k}
. The case
m=2
was proved by Shimura
[
18
]
, where the functional equation (
1.2
) is

(1.4)

\displaystyle L^{\ast}_{2,f}(s)=2^{-s}\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-k+2}{2}\right)L_{2,f}(s)=L^{\ast}_{2,f}(2k-1-s).

Recently, Newton and Thorne
[
12
,
13
]
showed that the analytic continuation holds true for all eigenforms
f
.
Deligne’s conjecture for
L^{\ast}_{m,f}(s)
at critical points have been considered by various authors. For instance, the case
m=1
was proved by Shimura
[
19
,
20
]
and Manin
[
10
]
(independently), and the case
m=2
was proved by Zagier
[
23
]
and Sturm
[
21
]
.

We can view the values of
L^{\ast}_{m,f}(s)
at the critical points as linear functionals on
f\in S_{k}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be the set of normalized Hecke eigenforms in
S_{k}
. For each
f=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}c_{g}g\in S_{k}
and a critical point
n+1
for
L^{\ast}_{m,f}(s)
, we define

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{m}}(f):=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}c_{g}L^{\ast}_{m,g}(n+1),

which makes
\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{m}}
an element in the dual space
S_{k}^{\ast}:=\Hom(S_{k},\mathbb{C})
.
These linear functionals are expected to carry interesting information. For example, if
m=1
, then

\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{1}}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(it)t^{n}dt=\frac{n!}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}L(f,n+1)

is the
n^{{\rm th}}
period of
f
, see
[
6
]
. These periods are subject to many linear dependence relations, called the Eichler-Shimura relations
[
10
]
; more precisely, for
0\leq t\leq k-2
, we have

(ES1)

\displaystyle\quad\mathfrak{r}_{t}(f)+(-1)^{t}\mathfrak{r}_{k-2-t}(f)=0,

(ES2)

\displaystyle\quad(-1)^{t}\mathfrak{r}_{t}(f)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq t\\
m\equiv 0\mod 2\end{subarray}}\binom{t}{m}\mathfrak{r}_{k-2-t+m}(f)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq k-2-t\\
m\equiv t\mod 2\end{subarray}}\binom{k-2-t}{m}\mathfrak{r}_{m}(f)=0,

(ES3)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq t\\
m\equiv 1\mod 2\end{subarray}}\binom{t}{m}\mathfrak{r}_{k-2-t+m}(f)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq k-2-t\\
m\not\equiv t\mod 2\end{subarray}}\binom{k-2-t}{m}\mathfrak{r}_{m}(f)=0,

where
\mathfrak{r}_{t}(f)=i^{t+1}\mathfrak{r}_{t,\vee^{1}}(f)
. But not much seems to be known about the linear relations among
\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{m}}
for
m\geq 2
since the corresponding motives are mysterious.

Another interesting aspect of these functionals or periods is the linear independence of a subset of
\{\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{m}}\}_{n}
. For example, even in the case of
m=1
, it is not simple to know whether a certain set of periods is linearly independent or not by only looking at the period relations (
ES1
), (
ES2
) and (
ES3
).
Recently, when
m=1
, Lei et al.
[
9
,
8
]
have provided some evidence for the linear independence of odd periods and even periods for modular forms, respectively. Furthermore, the linear independence between one odd and one even periods has been addressed in
[
22
]
.
In this paper, when
m=2
, we shall extend the ideas of
[
9
,
8
]
to provide some evidence for the linear independence of a subset of the periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{n,\vee^{2}}\}_{n}
. First, note that the critical points for the symmetric square
L
-function
L^{\ast}_{2,f}(s)
are odd integers in the set
\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\}
and even integers in the set
\{k,k+1,\cdots,2k-2\}
; see Hida
[
5
, p. 96]
. Thus, via the definition (
1.5
) and the functional equation (
1.4
), we will focus on the “critical” periods
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}
for integers
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{k-2}{2}
. Also, due to Lemma
2.2
, we will need to confine ourselves to periods
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}
for
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
.

We now state our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. For sufficiently large
k
, if
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
are integers, then the set of periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

In Proposition
5.2
, we give an explicit lower bound of
k
in Theorem
1.1
.

(2)

We will also prove the following complete result for
n=2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
be integers. If
\dim S_{k}\geq 2
, then
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{1},\vee^{2}}
and
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{2},\vee^{2}}
are linearly independent.

If we require that the periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are “far away” from the “central” period
\mathfrak{r}_{\frac{k-4}{2},\vee^{2}}
, for example, if
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-2}{4}
, then we can pick up to
\frac{\log k}{4}
linearly independent periods. More precisely, we proved the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer.
For
k\geq e^{12}
, if
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-2}{4}
are integers and
n\leq\frac{\log k}{4}
, then the set of periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent.

We sketch the main idea of the proofs. A natural idea is to consider the “dual” of the periods, which are certain modular forms. Thanks to the work of Cohen
[
2
]
and Zagier
[
23
]
, we can find the integral representation of
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}
and reduce the linear independence among the periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to the linear independence among modular forms
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
defined in (
2.1
). Next, following the idea of
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
, we consider the matrix formed by the Fourier coefficients of
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, which reduces the linear independence among
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to the nonsingularity of the matrix.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we state the integral representation of the periods and give the equivalence between the linear independence among periods and the linear independence among the corresponding modular forms. In Section
3
, we first provide some preparatory lemmas that will be used to estimate the Fourier coefficients of
c_{k,r}
. Then we give the asymptotics of the Fourier coefficient of
c_{k,r}
. In Section
4
, we define the matrix that is formed by the Fourier coefficients of
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Moreover, we give a criterion for the nonsingularity of this matrix. In Section
5
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
by showing the corresponding matrices are nonsingular. In the last section, we discuss a potential way to improve Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
and propose a related conjecture.

2.
Integral representation of the periods

Following Cohen
[
2
]
and Zagier
[
23
]
, we recall the integral representation of the periods
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}
(
1.5
). Let
k\geq 4
be an even integer and
r
be an odd integer such that
3\leq r\leq k-1
. Define

(2.1)

\displaystyle c_{k,r}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z},t^{2}\leq 4m}^{\infty}P_{k,r}(t,m)H(r,4m-t^{2})\right)e^{2\pi imz},

where
P_{k,r}(t,m)
is the polynomial defined by
[
23
, p.114]

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{k,r}(t,m)={\rm coefficient~of~}x^{k-r-1}~{\rm in~}\frac{1}{(1-tx+mx^{2})^{r}}.

Note that
P_{k,r}(t,m)=P_{k,r}(-t,m)
. The generalized Hurwitz class number
H(r,N)
is defined as follows (see
[
2
, p.272-273]
). Let
r
and
N
be non-negative integers with
r\geq 1
. For
N\geq 1
, define

(2.3)

\displaystyle h(r,N)=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{[r/2]}(r-1)!N^{r-1/2}}{2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}L(r,\chi_{(-1)^{r}N})&(-1)^{r}N\equiv 0,1\pmod{4},\\
0&N\equiv 2,3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

where we write
\chi_{D}
for the quadratic character given by the Kronecker symbol
\chi_{D}(d)=(\frac{D}{d})
. For
N\geq 0
, we define

(2.4)

\displaystyle H(r,N)=\begin{cases}\sum_{d^{2}\mid N}h(r,N/d^{2})&(-1)^{r}N\equiv 0,1\pmod{4},N>0,\\
\zeta(1-2r)&N=0,\\
0&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

If we set
(-1)^{r}N=Df^{2}
with
D
a fundamental discriminant (we allow
D=1
as a fundamental discriminant), then

(2.5)

\displaystyle H(r,N)

\displaystyle=L(1-r,\chi_{D})\sum_{d\mid f}\mu(d)\chi_{D}(d)d^{r-1}\sigma_{2r-1}(f/d)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{(r-1)/2}(r-1)!}{2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}N^{r-1/2}L(r,\chi_{D})\sum_{d\mid f}\mu(d)\chi_{D}(d)d^{r-1}\sigma_{2r-1}(f/d),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function and
\sigma_{2r-1}(n)=\sum_{d>0,d\mid n}d^{2r-1}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 6.2]
,
[
23
, p.114]
)

Let
k\geq 4
be an even integer and
r
be an odd integer such that
3\leq r\leq k-1
. Then the function
c_{k,r}
in (
2.1
) is a modular form of weight
k
for the full modular group. If
r<k-1
, it is a cusp form.

The following lemma gives an integral representation of the periods. It is the reason why we need to exclude the period
\mathfrak{r}_{k-2,\vee^{2}}
(or equivalently
\mathfrak{r}_{k-1,\vee^{2}}
); see the remark before Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
23
, Theorem 2]
)

Let
r,k
be integers with
3\leq r\leq k-1
,
r
odd,
k
even. The Petersson inner product of the modular form
c_{k,r}
with a Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{k}
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\langle f,c_{k,r}\rangle=-\frac{(r+k-2)!(k-2)!}{(k-r-1)!}\frac{1}{4^{r+k-2}\pi^{2r+k-1}}L_{2,f}(r+k-1).

Now, we prove the equivalence between the linear independence among the periods and the linear independence among the corresponding modular forms.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
be integers. The set of periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
S_{k}
is linearly independent if and only if the set of modular forms
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in
S_{k}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Letting
r=k-1-2\ell_{i}
in (
2.7
), together with (
1.4
), we get the Petersson inner product

(2.8)

\displaystyle\langle f,c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=A_{k,\ell_{i}}\cdot L^{\ast}_{2,f}(2\ell_{i}+1)

(2.9)

\displaystyle=A_{k,\ell_{i}}\cdot\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}(f),

where
f\in S_{k}
is a Hecke eigenform, and
A_{k,\ell_{i}}
is some nonzero constant depending only on
k
and
\ell_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
denote the set of normalized Hecke eigenforms in
S_{k}
. Note that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}=0~{\rm in}~S_{k}\quad{\rm if~and~only~if}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}\langle f_{j},c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\rangle=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}.

First, assume that
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent. We claim that
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent. Suppose that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}=0\in S_{k}^{\ast}.

It follows that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}(f_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}A_{k,\ell_{i}}^{-1}\langle f_{j},c_{k-1-2\ell_{i}}\rangle=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{k},

which implies that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{b_{i}A_{k,\ell_{i}}^{-1}}c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}=0\quad{\rm by}\quad\eqref{eq:suminnerproduct}.

Since
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent, we get
\overline{b_{i}A_{k,\ell_{i}}^{-1}}=0
, and
b_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Conversely, suppose that
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent. We show that
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent by a similar argument. Assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}=0\in S_{k}.

It follows that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}\langle f_{j},c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\rangle=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}\quad{\rm if~and~only~if}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}A_{k,\ell_{i}}\cdot\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}(f_{j})=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}.

Since
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent, we get
\overline{a_{i}}A_{k,\ell_{i}}=0
, and
a_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
∎

3.
Asymptotics of Fourier coefficients

First, we prove some preparatory lemmas that will be used to estimate the Fourier coefficients of
c_{k,r}
. The key is that for a perfect square
m
, the asymptotics of the
m^{{\rm th}}
Fourier coefficient of
c_{k,r}
is clear. We would like to mention that this observation is inspired by Serre’s work
[
17
, Proposition 3]
, which describes the asymptotics of the trace of the Hecke operator
T_{n}
on
S_{k}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For integers
m\geq 1
and
t
with
t^{2}<4m
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle|P_{k,r}(t,m)|\leq\binom{k-2}{r-1}\cdot\frac{2^{r}m^{(k-1)/2}}{(4m-t^{2})^{r/2}}.

Proof.

Following Zagier’s appendix to
[
7
]
, we recall that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-tx+mx^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\overline{\rho}^{n+1}}{\rho-\overline{\rho}}x^{n},

where
\rho+\overline{\rho}=t
and
\rho\cdot\overline{\rho}=m
. We write
a_{j}:=\frac{\rho^{j+1}-\overline{\rho}^{j+1}}{\rho-\overline{\rho}}
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle|a_{j}|\leq\frac{2|\rho|^{j+1}}{|\rho-\overline{\rho}|}\leq\frac{2m^{(j+1)/2}}{(4m-t^{2})^{1/2}}.

Since
P_{k,r}(t,m)
is the coefficient of
x^{k-r-1}
in
(1-tx+mx^{2})^{-r}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle|P_{k,r}(t,m)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},...,i_{r}\geq 0\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}=k-r-1\end{subarray}}a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{r}}\right|

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},...,i_{r}\geq 0\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}=k-r-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{2m^{(i_{j}+1)/2}}{(4m-t^{2})^{1/2}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\binom{k-2}{r-1}\cdot\frac{2^{r}m^{(k-1)/2}}{(4m-t^{2})^{r/2}}.

Here we use the fact that a non-negative integer
n
can be written as sum of
d
many non-negative integers in
\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}
ways.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
m
is a perfect square and
1\leq r\leq k-3
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle P_{k,r}(2m^{1/2},m)=\binom{k+r-2}{2r-1}\cdot m^{(k-r-1)/2}.

Proof.

Recall that
P_{k,r}(2m^{1/2},m)
is the coefficient of
x^{k-r-1}
in

\frac{1}{(1-2m^{1/2}x+mx^{2})^{r}}=\frac{1}{(1-m^{1/2}x)^{2r}}.

It is straightforward to see that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-m^{1/2}x)^{2r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{-(2r-1)/2}}{(2r-1)!}\frac{d^{2r-1}}{dx^{2r-1}}\left(\frac{1}{1-m^{1/2}x}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{-(2r-1)/2}}{(2r-1)!}\frac{d^{2r-1}}{dx^{2r-1}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(m^{1/2}x)^{n}\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{-(2r-1)/2}}{(2r-1)!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m^{(2r-1+n)/2}(2r-1+n)\cdots(n+1)x^{n},

and letting
n=k-r-1
gives the result.
∎

We also need the following estimates of the generalized Hurwitz class number
H(r,N)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an odd integer and
N
be a positive integer. Then

(3.11)

\displaystyle|H(r,N)|\leq\frac{(r-1)!\zeta(2r-1)\zeta(r)}{2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}\cdot N^{2r-1/2}\log N.

Proof.

We write
-N=Df^{2}
with
D
a fundamental discriminant. First, we give an upper bound of
L(r,\chi_{D})
. By Abel’s partial summation formula (for example, see
[
11
, Theorem 6.8]
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_{D}(n)n^{-r}=x^{-r}\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_{D}(n)+r\int_{1}^{x}\left(\sum_{n\leq t}\chi_{D}(t)\right)\frac{dt}{t^{r+1}}.

Note that
\chi_{D}
is a primitive Dirichlet character with conductor
-D
since
D
is a fundamental discriminant. By Pólya’s inequality
[
1
, Theorem 8.21]
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_{D}(n)\right|\leq\sqrt{-D}\log(-D).

Letting
x
go to infinity, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle|L(r,\chi_{D})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(r\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{r+1}}\right)\cdot\sqrt{-D}\log(-D)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{-D}\log(-D).

On the other hand, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{d\mid f}\mu(d)\chi_{D}(d)d^{r-1}\sigma_{2r-1}(f/d)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d\mid f}d^{r-1}\zeta(2r-1)(f/d)^{2r-1}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=f^{2r-1}\zeta(2r-1)\sum_{d\mid f}d^{-r}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq f^{2r-1}\zeta(2r-1)\zeta(r),

where we used
\sigma_{\alpha}(n)\leq\zeta(\alpha)n^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>1
in the first inequality, see
[
11
, p.245, Exercise 9]
. It follows that

(3.19)

\displaystyle|H(r,N)|

4.
Criterion for the linear independence

Following the idea of
[
9
]
and
[

]
, we define matrices that are used to study the linear independence of
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, see (
2.1
) and Proposition
2.3
. Let
k\geq 4
be an even integer and
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
be integers. We pick out the Fourier coefficients
a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(1),a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(4),a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(4^{2}),...,a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(4^{n-1})
of each
f_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(1\leq i\leq n)
(
3.26
) and place them in a
(n,n)
-matrix:

(4.1)

\displaystyle A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:

\displaystyle=\left[a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(4^{j-1})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\left[4^{\ell_{i}(j-1)}(1+\delta_{k,4^{j-1},k-1-2\ell_{i}})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

To show that
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent, it suffices to show that
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
is non-singular.
We write

\varepsilon_{k,i,j}:=\delta_{k,4^{j-1},k-1-2\ell_{i}}.

Such a choice of
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
allows us to relate it to the well-known Vondermonde matrix. More precisely, we ignore the error terms
\varepsilon_{k,i,j}
of
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
and define

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&4^{\ell_{1}}&(4^{\ell_{1}})^{2}&\cdots&(4^{\ell_{1}})^{n-1}\\
1&4^{\ell_{2}}&(4^{\ell_{2}})^{2}&\cdots&(4^{\ell_{2}})^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&4^{\ell_{n-1}}&(4^{\ell_{n-1}})^{2}&\cdots&(4^{\ell_{n-1}})^{n-1}\\
1&4^{\ell_{n}}&(4^{\ell_{n}})^{2}&\cdots&(4^{\ell_{n}})^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, similar to
[
9
, (2.8)]
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\det\mathscr{A}_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(4^{\ell_{j}}-4^{\ell_{i}})

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}4^{\ell_{j}}(1-4^{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}})

(4.6)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{3}{4}\cdot 4^{\ell_{j}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}4^{\ell_{j}(j-1)}.

Remark 4.1
.

In fact, if
m
is a perfect square, then the asymptotics of
a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}
is clear (see Proposition
3.5
), and the
(n,n)
-matrix

(4.8)

\displaystyle A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:

\displaystyle=\left[a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(m^{j-1})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\left[m^{\ell_{i}(j-1)}(1+\delta_{k,m^{j-1},k-1-2\ell_{i}})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

is related to the Vandermonde matrix

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&m^{\ell_{1}}&(m^{\ell_{1}})^{2}&\cdots&(m^{\ell_{1}})^{n-1}\\
1&m^{\ell_{2}}&(m^{\ell_{2}})^{2}&\cdots&(m^{\ell_{2}})^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&m^{\ell_{n-1}}&(m^{\ell_{n-1}})^{2}&\cdots&(m^{\ell_{n-1}})^{n-1}\\
1&m^{\ell_{n}}&(m^{\ell_{n}})^{2}&\cdots&(m^{\ell_{n}})^{n-1}\end{bmatrix},

which has determinant

(4.11)

\displaystyle\det\mathscr{A}_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(m^{\ell_{j}}-m^{\ell_{i}})

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}m^{\ell_{j}}(1-m^{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}})

(4.13)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{m-1}{m}\cdot m^{\ell_{j}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}m^{\ell_{j}(j-1)}.

Any such choice of matrix
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
will suffice to study the linear independence of
\{c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. However, the lower bounds of
k
in Propositions
5.2
and
5.4
will not be significantly influenced by the choice of
m
, see Proposition
4.2
. So we choose

to ease the notation.

The rest of this section is devoted to giving a criterion for the nonsingularity of
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
.
We have the following observation, see also
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
9
, Corollary 2.5]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S_{n}
denote the permutation group on the set
\{1,...,n\}
and
\tau\in S_{n}
. Then

(4.15)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}4^{\ell_{i}(\tau(i)-1)}

attains maximum when
\tau
is the identity permutation in

Proof.

It suffices to show that for integers
\ell_{2}>\ell_{1}\geq 0
and

we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle 4^{\ell_{2}(n_{2}-1)}\cdot 4^{\ell_{1}(n_{1}-1)}>4^{\ell_{2}(n_{1}-1)}\cdot 4^{\ell_{1}(n_{2}-1)},

which is clear from

(4.17)

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(n_{2}-1)+\ell_{1}(n_{1}-1)-\ell_{2}(n_{1}-1)-\ell_{1}(n_{2}-1)=(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})(n_{2}-n_{1})>0.

∎

Combining the inequality (

For any

[

\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{1},\vee^{2}}
and
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{2},\vee^{2}}
are linearly independent. Denote by
c_{k,r}(m)
the
m^{\rm th}
Fourier coefficient of
c_{k,r}
. We verified the nonsingularity of the matrix

(5.19)

\displaystyle C_{k;\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}:=\begin{bmatrix}c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{1}}(1)&c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{1}}(4)\\
c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{2}}(1)&c_{k,k-1-2\ell_{2}}(4)\end{bmatrix}

for
12\leq k\leq 9882,k\neq 14
, and
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
by
SageMath
, see
[
14
]
for the codes. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

We last prove Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
k\geq e^{12}
. If
0\leq\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-2}{4}
are integers and
n\leq\frac{\log k}{4}
, then

(5.20)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|\leq k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}-\frac{2(\log(5(\log k)^{2})-\log 4)}{k\log k}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}.

Proof.

First, we find a uniform bound for
\varepsilon_{k,i,j}(1\leq i,j\leq n)
.
Let
m=4^{j-1}
and
r=k-1-2\ell_{i}
. By Proposition
3.5
, we have

(5.21)

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{k,i,j}|

\displaystyle\leq 5\left(\frac{32\pi}{k-1}\right)^{r}m^{2r}\log 4m

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq 5\left(\frac{32\pi}{k-1}\right)^{r}m^{2r}2(4m)^{1/2}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\leq 20\left(\frac{32\pi}{k-1}\right)^{r}m^{2r}\cdot m^{r/6}

(5.24)

\displaystyle=20\left(\frac{32\pi\cdot 4^{13(j-1)/6}}{k-1}\right)^{k-1-2\ell_{i}}.

Since
j\leq\frac{\log k}{4}
, we have

(5.25)

\displaystyle\frac{32\pi\cdot 4^{13(j-1)/6}}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6}\cdot 4^{13j/6}}{k-1}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6}\cdot 4^{13\log k/24}}{k-1}

(5.27)

\displaystyle=k^{-\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}+\frac{13\log 4}{24}}.

Note that in particular,

(5.28)

\displaystyle k^{-\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}+\frac{13\log 4}{24}}\leq k^{-\frac{\log(e^{12}-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log e^{12}}+\frac{13\log 4}{24}}\leq k^{-0.115}<1.

As
k-1-2\ell_{i}\geq\frac{k}{2}
, (
5.24
) and (
5.27
) imply that

(5.29)

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{k,i,j}|

\displaystyle\leq 20k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}

We now give the bound of
\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|
. To simplify the proof, we will use a different bound of
f
defined in (
4.19
). By a similar argument as (
5.5
), if
|x_{i}|\leq M
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
nM\leq 1
then

(5.30)

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},...,x_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{1}M+\binom{n}{2}M^{2}+\cdots+\binom{n}{n}M^{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(nM)^{i}\leq n^{2}M.

Note that (
5.29
) and (
5.28
) imply that

(5.31)

\displaystyle|\varepsilon_{k,i,j}|\leq 20\left(\frac{1}{k^{0.115}}\right)^{k/2}\leq\frac{4}{\log k}\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Hence by (
5.30
) and (
5.29
),

(5.32)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\cdot 20k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}

(5.33)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5(\log k)^{2}}{4}\cdot k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}

(5.34)

\displaystyle=k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}-\frac{2(\log(5(\log k)^{2})-\log 4)}{k\log k}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}},

as desired.
∎

Theorem
1.3
follows from the next result.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
k\geq e^{12}
. If
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-2}{4}
and
n\leq\frac{\log k}{4}
, then
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
is nonsingular.

Proof.

First, we give a lower bound of the right hand side of (
4.20
). Recall the non-asymptotic Stirling’s approximation
[
15
]
, which states that for all
n\geq 1

(5.35)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}<n!<\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}}.

In particular, we have

(5.36)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}<\frac{n!e^{n}}{n^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq e.

Using (
5.36
) and the fact that
(e/x)^{x}
decreases for
x>0
, we get

(5.37)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\cdot(n!)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\frac{1}{e\sqrt{2\pi n}}\left(\frac{e}{n}\right)^{n}

(5.38)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{\log k}{4}\left(\frac{\log k}{4}-1\right)/2}\frac{2}{e\sqrt{2\pi\log k}}\left(\frac{4e}{\log k}\right)^{\frac{\log k}{4}}

(5.39)

\displaystyle=k^{\frac{-\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{\log k}{4}-1\right)/2}\frac{2}{e\sqrt{2\pi\log k}}\left(\frac{4e}{\log k}\right)^{\frac{\log k}{4}}.

It is not difficult to see that for all
k\geq e^{12}
, we have

(5.40)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{e\sqrt{2\pi\log k}}\geq\frac{1}{k^{\log(4/3)}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\left(\frac{4e}{\log k}\right)^{\frac{\log k}{4}}\geq k^{\frac{-\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot\frac{\log k}{4}},

which implies that

(5.41)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\cdot(n!)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq k^{\frac{-\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot\left[\left(\frac{\log k}{4}-1\right)/2+4+\frac{\log k}{4}\right]}

(5.42)

\displaystyle=k^{\frac{\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot(-\frac{3\log k}{8}-\frac{7}{2})}

(5.43)

\displaystyle\geq k^{\frac{\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}.

On the other hand,

(5.44)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|\leq k^{\left[\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}-\frac{2(\log(5(\log k)^{2})-\log 4)}{k\log k}\right]\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}.

Note that for
k\geq e^{12}
, we have

(5.45)

\displaystyle\frac{\log(k-1)-\log(32\pi\cdot 4^{-13/6})}{\log k}-\frac{13\log 4}{24}-\frac{2(\log(5(\log k)^{2})-\log 4)}{k\log k}\geq 0.115>\frac{\log(4/3)}{4},

which implies that

(5.46)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|\leq k^{0.115\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}<k^{\frac{\log(4/3)}{4}\cdot\frac{-k}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n(n-1)/2}\cdot(n!)^{-1}

Therefore,
A_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
is nonsingular by Proposition
4.2
.
∎

6.
Discussions

Since the lower bound for
k
in Proposition
5.2
is gigantic, a natural question is how much we can improve it.
As mentioned in
[
9
, Remark 2.9]
, there is another natural choice of matrix to investigate the linear independence of the periods. Recall that
a_{k,r}(m)
denotes the
m^{{\rm th}}
Fourier coefficient of
f_{k,r}
defined by (
3.26
). We define

(6.1)

\displaystyle B_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:

\displaystyle=\left[a_{k,k-1-2\ell_{i}}(j^{2})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\left[j^{2\ell_{i}}(1+\eta_{k,i,j})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n},

where
\eta_{k,i,j}
goes to zero as
k
tends to infinity.
Then the “limit” matrix becomes

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{k;\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&2^{2\ell_{1}}&3^{2\ell_{1}}&\cdots&n^{2\ell_{1}}\\
1&2^{2\ell_{2}}&3^{2\ell_{2}}&\cdots&n^{2\ell_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&2^{2\ell_{n-1}}&3^{2\ell_{n-1}}&\cdots&n^{2\ell_{n-1}}\\
1&2^{2\ell_{n}}&3^{2\ell_{n}}&\cdots&n^{2\ell_{n}}\end{bmatrix},

which is a generalized Vandermonde matrix
[
4
, Vol.2, p.99]
. If one can get a good result about the lower bound of the determinant of (
6.3
), then the upper bound for
\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\varepsilon_{k,1,\tau(1)},...,\varepsilon_{k,n,\tau(n)})|
in (
4.20
) becomes bigger, which will lead to a smaller lower bound for
k
in Proposition
5.2
. It is reasonable to propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Let
0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{k-4}{2}
. If
n\leq\dim S_{k}
, then the set of periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell_{i},\vee^{2}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent.

Note that Theorem
1.2
provides evidence for Conjecture
6.1
when
n=2
. The case
n=1
is also true since we verified the nonsingularity of the matrix
C_{k,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
defined in (
5.19
) for
12\leq k\leq 9882,k\neq 14
.
There is another question related to the set of periods
\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}\}_{\ell=0}^{(k-4)/2}
, that is, can this set span the whole space of
S_{k}^{\ast}
? We believe that the answer is affirmative, which also follows from Conjecture
6.1
, but we are only able to show the following corollary of Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Suppose
k\geq e^{12}
is even. Then

(6.4)

\displaystyle\dim\Span\left(\{\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}\}_{\ell=0}^{(k-4)/2}\right)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\log k}{4}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

This is a direct corollary of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Another natural question about the periods
\mathfrak{r}_{2\ell,\vee^{2}}
is whether we can find Eichler-Shimura
type linear relations (
ES2
) and (
ES3
) among them. We hope to take it up in a future work.

Abstract
We studied formation of charge density wave between valleys in a system with double-well-like dispersive valence band which captures some of the features of rhombohedral graphene trilayer. In a regime with 2 Fermi surfaces: electron- (with radius p i p_{i} ) and hole-like ( p o p_{o} ) – an instability in particle-hole channel appears at q = q c + δ ​ q q=q_{c}+\delta q , where q c = p o − p i q_{c}=p_{o}-p_{i} . In a weak coupling regime ( x / ϵ F ≪ 1 x/\epsilon_{F}\ll 1 ) presence of an additional energy scale ∝ m ​ q c ​ δ ​ q \propto mq_{c}\delta q gives rise to several regimes with distinct spectrum and transport properties: in a regime with small order parameter x ⪅ m ⁡ ⟨ p F ⟩ ​ δ ​ q x\lessapprox m\langle p_{F}\rangle\delta q Fermi arcs show up and change conductance qualitatively. At larger values of the order parameter Fermi arcs are gapped out. Regimes are also distinguished by different effective exponents ζ \zeta in conductance correction σ ∝ τ D ζ \sigma\propto\tau_{D}^{\zeta} where τ D \tau_{D} is scattering time off disorder and 1 ≤ ζ ≤ 2 1\leq\zeta\leq 2 .



I
Introduction

When electrical current fixed by the external conditions, dissipated heat goes as
\propto j^{2}/\sigma
, so that in order to minimize external influence
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
, most of the things at low temperatures conduct well. It is then reasonable to expect finding unusual physics in areas where highly resistive phases are present.

Insulators (
\sigma=0
) usually appear because of very severe restrictions, such as statistics
Mott 1968
, particle conservation
Mermin 1968
or charge of particles effectively being zero
Frenkel 1931
.

Besides true insulators, highly resistive phases can be of interest (known as failed insulators
Cao et al. 2020
). We may expect resistance per quasiparticle being high in systems where two interacting subsystems present: one with charged carriers, and the other without such. One of the examples of such phase is high-temperature superconductors, where both spinons and chargons are present
Lee et al. 2006
.

Another system of this kind would consist of excitons (compound particles with zero charge) and holes/electrons.

When a system consists of several layers with energy hybridisation
\gamma
doped away from the charge neutrality, it is reasonable to expect that at some displacement field
D>\gamma
, but not
D\gg\gamma
a hole-like Fermi surface will be localized in the top-most layer, and the electron-like Fermi surface will be largely in a bottom-most layer, given the field direction is positive. ABC-stacked (or rhomboedral) graphene has three layers and in sufficiently strong electrical field (of order 100 mV/nm) perpendicular to the plane seem to satisfy a condition with two types of carriers present. In this regime, two Fermi surfaces
Zhang et al. 2010
, electron- and hole-like for each spin and valley are present.

There, a number of correlated phases were observed
Zhou et al. 2021a
.
A resistive phase, called partially isospin polarized (PIP) is mostly consistent with an intervalley coherence
Zhou et al. 2021a
;
Chatterjee et al. 2021
. Similarly to twisted graphene bilayer, adjacent phase is superconducting
Zhou et al. 2021b
and appears in a regime with two Fermi surfaces. Besides the
C_{6}
-symmetric intervalley coherent phase
Chatterjee et al. 2021
, a phase with additionally broken rotational symmetry
Huang et al. 2022
was suggested to explain the experiment. However, it is known that Stoner transition in a system with quadratically dispersive bands and screened interactions in 2D does not change shape of the bands and therefore does not change the resistance according to Drude formula. Then there are several alternatives:

1.

Drude formula is inaccurate due to large quantum corrections;

2.

long-range nature of interaction is important;

3.

a phase realized in nature is not intervalley coherent.

Mean-free path in the sample
l_{D}\approx 1\mu m

Zhou et al. 2021b
, which makes the first alternative less plausible, since quantum correction decays as a power of
l_{D}p_{F}
. And, while the band curvature induced by the momentum-dependence of interaction could be important, it is known that in many cases most of the relevant physics can be modeled with a properly chosen value of the contact interaction in the limit of small density
Lifshitz and Pitaevskii 2013
or small screening length. As a consequence, in this, as well as in the previous paper
Igor V. Blinov 2024
we have explored the last path.

Namely, we suggest as a candidate a phase with valley and spatial symmetries broken simultaneously: e.g. an intervalley coherence established at a vector
Q=K-K^{\prime}+q
, where
q\approx p_{o}-p_{i}\ll Q
, which would correspond to formation of an incommensurate large-period crystal lattice in the second order of the order parameter or, another words, to an incommensurate charge density wave. In the language of the band theory, the intervalley potential flattens bands, and as a result, the effective mass changes. This phase explains qualitatively suppression of conductivity at low temperatures. In this case, as long as the state is metallic, correction should go as
\delta\sigma\propto\tau_{D}
, where
\tau_{D}
is the initial scattering time.

However, because of presence of two Fermi surfaces in the system: inner, electron-like, with a smaller Fermi momentum
p_{i}
and outer, hole-like, with a large Fermi momentum
p_{o}
, local gaps may open and, consequently, system may enter non-metallic regime of conductance. As a result, correction to the conductance contradicts Drude formula. Conductance correction depends on
q
and has, as a function of it, few regimes, with a leading term going as some power of
\tau_{D}
(the scattering time due to disorder), and
m_{e}
is the effective mass of electrons:

\delta\sigma\propto\left(\frac{x_{q}}{m_{e}}\right)^{\alpha}(m_{e}\tau_{D})^{\zeta},

(1)

where effective
\zeta
is from
1
to
2
(in the experimentally relevant regime it has a value around
3/2

To shed some light on these regimes, in this paper we start from a simple fermionic model (Sec
III

Zhou et al. 2022
;
Dong and Levitov 2021
;
Bernevig and Kwan 2025

x_{q}
and, extending perturbative in

To conclude, we comment on the difference with related theories

In what follows, I denote a SO(3) order parameter as

.

Displacement field breaks
z\to-z
symmetry and opens a gap. When the gap is sufficiently large in comparison to other energy scales present in the model (kinetic, coupling between the layers, interaction), the low energy description may involve a single band. Sufficiently strong displacement field
D\|\hat{z}
distributes electrons between the layers. At very small momentum valence band of the trilayer is mainly located within the conduction band of the B-sublattice of the bottom layer, while at larger momentum it restores the original form, which is delocalized in the sublattice space. As a result, Fermi surface has a property of having positive mass for small momentum and negative mass for large momentum. Hence a regime with 2 Fermi surfaces (annular Fermi surface) can be established, with bands being electron-like for inner circle, and hole-like for outer.

Because of presence of 2 different Fermi surfaces within each electron flavor, the order parameter
M_{q}
at sufficiently large momentum will have two components qualitatively different from each other:
M_{q}=M_{m,q}+M_{ex,q}
, where
M_{m,q}
is a metallic component and corresponds to coherence established between alike Fermi surface (inner-inner, outer-outer). We call it metallic for it does not lead to a gap opening.
M_{ex,q}
is an excitonic component that establishes coherence between different Fermi surfaces (inner-outer, outer-inner). Having in mind presence of both component may help to understand both the critical behavior and electromagnetic response. We will see later that the excitonic component is related to Fermi arcs.

VII
Conclusion

A phase with partial intervalley exciton condensate is similar to Larkin-Ovchinnikov (LO) phase of superconductivity. It also appears because of the presence of two Fermi surfaces (electron- and hole-like) with different radius (
p_{i}
and
p_{o}
) of a band model, and leads to oscillations of density with period
\approx(p_{o}-p_{i})^{-1}
much larger than the lattice constant.

We showed that unlike the LO-phase in 3D, minimum of energy at least within the weak-coupling theory, corresponds to a phase with
C_{6}
symmetric order parameter and not to
C_{2}
. In contrast to LO-phase, reciprocal lattice vectors of such intervalley crystal are not exactly equal to
p_{o}-p_{i}
, but with a small correction
\delta q
because of the anharmonicity of bands. This fact modifies both order parameter behavior and transport properties qualitatively.

As such, coefficients
U_{4}
, corresponding to scattering of 2 intervalley excitons, diverge as
\Omega^{-1/2}
and not
\Omega^{-1}
, which leads to a larger value of the order parameter
x_{q}
. Interestingly, critical temperature of the phase
\propto x_{q}
has two regimes: when
T_{c}\gg T_{1}
, the behavior is of MacMillan type
T_{c}\propto T_{L}e^{-\frac{m_{e}}{\theta_{c}\lambda}}
, while at weakly interacting regime it has a square root dependence on interaction strength
T_{c,L}\propto T_{L}(-V\Pi_{0}-1)^{1/2}
.

Order has two components, metallic (coming from the contribution between alike Fermi surfaces) and insulating (coming from the contribution between the different Fermi surfaces).

Because for small
x_{q}
the phase has Fermi arcs, Fourier transform of quantum oscillations at very low frequencies should have a maximum, which is consistent to the experiment. However, the latter is also consistent with partially isospin polarized
Huang et al. 2022
order.
What seems to be inconsistent, is that resistance by a factor of
1.5-2
larger than the resistance of the paramagnetic phase. It is, however, straightforward to explain with partial condensation of excitons, since some of electron-hole pairs condense and do not contribute to the transport on the mean-field level.

In the regime where emission of an exciton is permitted by energy conservation
\epsilon_{o}(p+q)-\epsilon_{i}(p)=\epsilon_{x}(q)
, charge carriers acquire new mechanism to relax energy with a characteristic time scale
\propto\tau_{X}
. Then ignoring other changes induced by the presence of the order parameter, by Matthiessen’s rule, the correction to the conductivity

\delta\sigma\propto\frac{\tau_{D}}{\tau_{D}/\tau_{X}+1}-\tau_{D}\propto-\tau_{D}^{2}/\tau_{X}

(38)

for
\tau_{X}\gg\tau_{D}
. This is exactly what we obtained in small
x_{q}
limit. Remnants of this
\tau_{D}^{2}

In this region, change of the resistance, dependent on relative strength of the order parameter
x_{q}
, inverse scattering time
\tau^{-1}_{D}
, and characteristic transversal kinetic energy of excitons
m_{e}qp_{i}\theta_{c,i}^{2}/m_{e}qp_{o}\theta_{c,o}^{2}

2
to
1

\delta\sigma/\sigma_{0}\propto-x_{q}^{2}/n_{o}
is disorder independent. We described this regime before
Igor V. Blinov 2024
.

Another feature of the phase with
C_{6}
symmetric order parameter is that in the second order of the order parameter density should have a correction of the form
\delta\rho(r)\propto x_{q}^{2}\int e^{i(K-K^{\prime}-q)r}e^{-i(K-K^{\prime}+q)r}\rho(q)\rho^{*}(-q)
which generates large-period oscillations with periods
10-100nm
visible in STM microscopy for sufficiently large systems or Bragg spectroscopy. Third, because electron- and hole-like Fermi surfaces for large values of the displacement field
D>\gamma_{1}
, where
\gamma_{1}

A
and
B
sublattices of adjacent layers, enhancement of tunneling conductivity in
z

As for quantum correction to the conductance, they decay with increase of the scattering length, and thus are not important in clean
p_{f}l_{s}\gg 1
limit. We also note that since excitons have a weak coupling to electrical field

and their response is going to be proportional to their density at momentum other than
q=q_{c}+\delta q

\theta_{c,i}
such that
\cos(\theta_{c,i})=(p_{o}^{2}-p_{i}^{2}-(q_{c}+\delta q)^{2})/(2p_{i}(q_{c}+\delta q))
:

-\frac{\delta_{i}}{m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{i}q}<-\sin(\theta_{c,i})(\theta-\theta_{c,i})<+\frac{\delta_{i}}{m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{i}q}.

(45)

which gives:

\theta_{c,i}^{2}=\frac{\delta_{i}}{m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{i}q}.

(46)

On the other hand, using the definition above for the central angle
\theta_{c,i}
:

1-\frac{\theta_{c,i}^{2}}{2}=\left(1-\frac{\delta q}{q_{c}}+\left(\frac{\delta q}{q_{c}}\right)^{2}\right)\left(1-\delta q\frac{2q_{c}+\delta q}{2p_{i}q_{c}}\right)=1-\frac{\delta qp_{o}}{q_{c}p_{i}}+\frac{\delta q^{2}}{q_{c}^{2}}\left(\frac{p_{o}+p_{i}}{2p_{i}}\right),

(47)

meaning that

\frac{\delta_{i}}{2m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{i}q}=\frac{\delta qp_{o}}{q_{c}p_{i}}-\frac{\delta q^{2}}{q_{c}^{2}}\left(\frac{p_{o}+p_{i}}{2^{2}p_{i}}\right).

(48)

Similarly, we get for the outer Fermi surface:

\theta_{c,o}^{2}=\frac{\delta_{o}}{m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{o}q}.

(49)

and from an analogous definition of the outer Fermi surface we can obtain:

\frac{\delta_{o}}{2m_{e}}\frac{1}{p_{o}q}=\frac{\delta qp_{i}}{q_{c}p_{o}}-\frac{\delta q^{2}}{q_{c}^{2}}\left(\frac{p_{o}+p_{i}}{2^{2}p_{o}}\right).

(50)

Note that from the requirement
\delta q_{o}=\delta q_{i}
follows

\delta_{i}/p_{o}^{2}\approx\delta_{o}/p_{i}^{2}\equiv\delta^{\prime}.

(51)

Introducing their arithmetic average
\delta
we get
\delta=(\delta_{i}+\delta_{o})/2=\delta^{\prime}(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})/2=\delta^{\prime}|m/\lambda|
. As a result:

\theta_{c,i/o}^{2}=\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{m_{e}}\frac{p_{o/i}^{2}}{q_{c}p_{i/o}}=\frac{2\delta qp_{o/i}}{q_{c}p_{i/o}}-\frac{\delta q^{2}\langle p\rangle}{q_{c}^{2}p_{i/o}},

(52)

so that
\delta q\propto\langle p\rangle\delta^{\prime}/m_{e}
. Then we choose
\delta
such that it minimizes the mistake of the quadratic approximation:

\Delta I=\sum_{p}\frac{n(\xi(p+q))-n(\xi(p))}{\xi(p+q)-\xi(p)+i\delta}-\sum_{p,a,a^{\prime}}\frac{n(\xi_{a}(p+q))-n(\xi_{a^{\prime}}(p))}{\xi_{a}(p+q)-\xi_{a^{\prime}}(p)+i\delta},

(53)

where
\xi(p)=\xi_{a}(p)+\lambda(p^{2}-p_{a}^{2})^{2}
. Since the energy range
\delta
,
\epsilon(p+q)-\epsilon(p)\approx\delta
, then mistake from the range should scale, by the order of magnitude, as
-(\delta-\delta_{1/2})/m_{e}\delta
(
\delta_{1/2}
is the division of momentum range exactly in the middle between
p_{i}
and
p_{o}
) and the mistake from the approximation comes in the second order expansion in
\lambda
, hence should be around
\propto C\int n(m_{e}(p^{2}-p_{a}^{2}))\lambda(p^{2}-p_{a}^{2})^{2}/m_{e}^{2}(p^{2}-p_{a}^{2})^{2}\propto C\lambda\log(\delta)/m_{e}
, where we ignored the mistake coming from the restriction over the angle range, since it should take care of double-counting. Then the best value should be given by, approximately,

-\frac{\delta_{1/2}}{m_{e}\delta}+\frac{\lambda}{m_{e}}C=0,

(54)

where
C
is of order 1.
It should be then that
\delta\approx(m_{e}/\lambda)\delta_{1/2}
. With
\delta_{1/2}\approx(p_{o}^{2}-p_{i}^{2})/2=m_{e}/\lambda
, so that
\delta\approx(m_{e}/\lambda)^{2}
.

Appendix B
Response

The generic expression for response is

\Pi(q,i\Omega)=\sum_{n}\int d^{2}pG(p,i\omega_{n})G(p+q,i(\omega_{n}+\Omega)),

(55)

or, after summation over Matsubara frequencies, is:

\Pi(q,i\Omega)=\int d^{2}p\frac{n(\xi_{p+q})-n(\xi_{p})}{i\Omega+\xi_{p+q}-\xi_{p}}.

(56)

B.1
Homo-part

Homo-part has the form normal for metallic response at finite
q
:

\Pi(q,i\Omega)=\int d\theta dpp\frac{n(\xi_{p+q})-n(\xi_{p})}{i\Omega+m_{e}(2pq\cos(\theta)+q^{2})}.

(57)

Zero temperature value

For small frequencies, response is peaked at
\xi_{p+q}\approx\xi_{p}
, which for homo-part at temperatures far below Fermi is equivalent to
\xi_{p}\approx 0
and hence corrections due to constrain of the integration range over angle discussed in section
A.1
should be
O(\Omega)
. We then extend integration range to
-\pi
and
\pi
and represent the response on the inner Fermi surface in the form:

\Pi(q,i\Omega)=-\int\frac{d\theta dppn(\xi_{p})}{-i\Omega+m_{e}(2pq\cos(\theta)+q^{2})}-\int\frac{d\theta dppn(\xi_{p})}{i\Omega+m_{e}(2pq\cos(\theta)+q^{2})}=-2\text{Re}\left(\int\frac{d\theta dppn(\xi_{p})}{i\Omega+m_{e}q(2p\cos(\theta)+q)}\right)

(58)

Which gets us

\Pi_{ii}(q<2p_{i},i\Omega)=-\frac{\pi}{m_{e}q}\left(\left(\frac{i2^{2}\Omega}{m_{e}}-\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}q}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}+i\left(-\frac{i2^{2}\Omega}{m_{e}}+\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}q}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)\\
+\frac{\pi}{m_{e}q}\left(\left(q^{2}+\frac{i2^{2}\Omega}{m_{e}}-\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}q}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}-i\left((2p_{i})^{2}-q^{2}-\frac{i2^{2}\Omega}{m_{e}}+\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}2}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)

(59)

and

\Pi_{ii}(q>2p_{i},i\Omega)=-\frac{\pi}{m_{e}q}\left(\left(q^{2}-(2p_{i})^{2}+\frac{2^{2}i\Omega}{m_{e}}-\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}q}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(q^{2}+\frac{i2^{2}\Omega}{m_{e}}-\left(\frac{2\Omega}{m_{e}q}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).

(60)

And identical expressions for the outer-part, except for
p_{i}\to p_{o}
. For finite temperatures, we get quadratic dependence on temperature with a characteristic scale of
\epsilon_{F,i}
.

B.2
Hetero-part

Zero temperature value

The hetero-response can be written in the form

\Pi(q,i\Omega)=-\int d^{2}p\frac{n(\xi_{p,i})}{-i\Omega-\xi_{p,i}+\xi_{p+q,o}}+\int d^{2}p\frac{n(\xi_{p,o})}{-i\Omega-\xi_{p+q,i}+\xi_{p,o}}\\
+\int d^{2}p\frac{n(\xi_{p,o})}{i\Omega+\xi_{p,o}-\xi_{p+q,i}}-\int d^{2}p\frac{n(\xi_{p,i})}{i\Omega+\xi_{p+q,o}-\xi_{p,i}}\\
\equiv-2\text{Re}(I_{i}(q,i\Omega))+2\text{Re}(I_{o}(q,i\Omega))

(61)

where the first term responsible for electrons on the inner Fermi-surface, while the second – on the outer Fermi-surface. The integral over the inner part is

I_{i}(q,\Omega)=\int\frac{d^{2}p}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n(\xi_{p,i})}{i\Omega+\xi_{p+q,o}-\xi_{p,i}}=\frac{1}{m_{e}}\int\frac{d^{2}p}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n(\xi_{p,i})}{\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}-((p+q)^{2}-p_{o}^{2})-(p^{2}-p_{i}^{2})}

(62)

The integral over p can be taken either through a substitution
\delta p=p^{2}-p_{i}^{2}
and linearization in
\delta p
, or directly as an integral over
p
. In zero temperature limit,

1.

Exact integral over
p

I_{i}(q,\Omega)=\frac{1}{m_{e}}\int_{0}^{p_{i}}\frac{d\theta dp}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{p}{\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}-2p^{2}-2pq\cos(\theta)+p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}}

(63)

which has roots at
p_{i,1/2}=-\frac{q}{2}\cos(\theta)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(q\cos(\theta))^{2}+2(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}+i\Omega/m_{e})}
.
Note here that since the angle is close to
0
, we can expand in the vicinity of
\theta=0
:

p_{i,1/2}=-\frac{q}{2}(1-\theta^{2}/2)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(q(1-\theta^{2}/2))^{2}+2(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}+i\Omega/m_{e})}\\
=-\frac{q}{2}(1-\theta^{2}/2)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{q^{2}(1-\theta^{2})+2(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}+i\Omega/m_{e})}\\
=-\frac{q}{2}(1-\theta^{2}/2)\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})-q^{2}}\\
\pm\frac{i\Omega/2m_{e}}{(2p_{o}^{2}+2p_{i}^{2}-q^{2})^{3/2}}\mp\frac{q^{2}\theta^{2}}{2^{2}\sqrt{2p_{o}^{2}+2p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}}}=\kappa_{i,1/2}+q\theta^{2}r_{i,1/2}\pm i\alpha\Omega,

(64)

which defines coefficient
r_{i,1/2}
as well as
\alpha
:

r_{i,1/2}=\frac{p_{i/o}}{2(p_{o}+p_{i})}

(65)

The angle at which the real part of
p_{i}-p_{i,1/2}
vanishes we call the central angle

\theta_{c,i}^{2}\equiv\frac{p_{i}-\kappa_{i,1}}{qr_{i,1}}\approx\left(\frac{2\delta qp_{o}}{p_{i}q_{c}}+\frac{\delta q^{2}(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})}{q_{c}p_{i}(p_{o}+p_{i})^{2}}\right),

(66)

The integral now can be represented in the form:

I_{i}(q,\Omega)=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\int_{0}^{p_{i}}\frac{d\theta dp}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{p}{(p-p_{i,1})(p-p_{i,2})}=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\int_{0}^{p_{i}}\frac{d\theta dp}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{p_{i,1}}{p-p_{i,1}}-\frac{p_{i,2}}{p-p_{i,2}}\right)\frac{1}{p_{i,1}-p_{i,2}}

(67)

Integration over
p
gives:

I_{i}(q,\Omega)\approx\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}}\int_{0}^{\theta_{c,i}}\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{p_{i,1}-p_{i,2}}\Bigg(p_{i,1}\log\left(\frac{(p_{i}-\bar{p}_{i,1})^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}{\bar{p}_{i,1}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}\right)-p_{i,2}\log\left(\frac{(p_{i}-\bar{p}_{i,2})^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}{\bar{p}_{i,2}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}\right)+i\pi p_{i,1}\Bigg),

(68)

where
p_{i,1/2}=\text{Re}(\bar{p}_{i,1/2})
. Introducing a critical angle:

\theta_{c,i/o}^{2}\equiv\frac{p_{i}-\kappa_{i,1}}{qr_{i,1}}\approx\left(\frac{2\delta qp_{o/i}}{p_{i/o}q_{c}}\pm\frac{\delta q^{2}(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})}{q_{c}p_{o/i}(p_{o}+p_{i})^{2}}\right)

(69)

or, for small
\delta q
,

\theta_{c,i/o}^{2}=\frac{2\delta qp_{o/i}}{p_{i/o}q_{c}}.

(70)

we can rewrite the response in the form:

I_{i}(q,\Omega)=\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}}\int_{0}^{\theta_{c,i}}\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{p_{i,1}-p_{i,2}}\Bigg(p_{i,1}\log\left(\frac{(\theta_{c,i}^{2}-\theta^{2})^{2}+(\alpha/qr_{i})^{2}\Omega^{2}}{(\kappa_{i}/qr_{i}+\theta^{2})^{2}+(\alpha/qr_{i})^{2}\Omega^{2}}\right)-p_{i,2}\log\left(\frac{(p_{i}-p_{i,2})^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}{p_{i,2}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\Omega^{2}}\right)+i\pi p_{i,1}\Bigg).

(71)

which reaches its maximal value for
\theta_{c,i}=\theta_{f}
and after integration, is equal to

I_{i}(q=q_{c}+\delta q,\Omega=0)\approx\frac{\theta_{c,i}}{2^{2}m_{e}}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}}\log\left(\frac{\epsilon_{c}}{\epsilon_{F,i}e^{2}}\right),

(72)

where
\epsilon_{c}=(\theta_{c}q_{c})^{2}m_{e}
and
\epsilon_{F}=m_{e}(p_{i}+p_{o})^{2}/2^{2}
, where
\epsilon_{c}
plays the role of infra-red cutoff for the relative degree of freedom of an exciton, has a form similar to the density-density response of the 1D atom chain at
q
and is related to the Peierls distortion
Peierls 1996
.

When frequency kept finite,

I_{het,i}=\frac{\kappa_{1}\theta_{f}}{\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}}\left(\log((\theta_{f}^{2}-\theta_{c}^{2})^{2}+(\alpha\Omega)^{2})-2^{2}\right)\\
-\frac{\kappa_{1}\theta_{c}^{\prime}}{\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}}\left(\log((\theta_{f}-\theta_{c})^{2}+(\alpha\Omega)^{2})-\log(\theta_{c}^{2}+(\alpha\Omega)^{2})-\log((\theta_{c}+\theta_{f})^{2}+(\alpha\Omega)^{2})+\log(\theta_{c}^{2}+(\alpha\Omega)^{2})\right).

(73)

Finite temperature correction:
inner FS contribution

Inner part of the correction due to the finiteness of the temperature can be written as

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=\frac{1}{m_{e}}\int^{p_{i}}_{0}dppd\theta\frac{n(m_{e}(p^{2}-p_{i}^{2}))-1}{2p^{2}+q^{2}+2pq\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}}\\
+\frac{1}{m_{e}}\int_{p_{i}}^{p_{\Lambda}}dppd\theta\frac{n(m_{e}(p^{2}-p_{i}^{2}))}{2p^{2}+q^{2}+2pq\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}},

(74)

where
\Delta=m_{e}(p_{o}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})
. Changing integration variable to
y=\beta m_{e}(p^{2}-p_{i}^{2})
, I get for the correction:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{0}_{-\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)-1}{\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}+2p_{i}^{2}+q^{2}+2(\frac{y}{\beta m_{e}}+p_{i}^{2})^{1/2}q\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}}\\
+\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta m_{e}p_{\Lambda}^{2}}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}+2p_{i}^{2}+q^{2}+2(\frac{y}{\beta m_{e}}+p_{i}^{2})^{1/2}q\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}},

(75)

where
\bar{n}(y)=(1+e^{y})^{-1}
which can be rewritten as

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}_{0}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{-\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}+2p_{i}^{2}+q^{2}+2(-\frac{y}{\beta m_{e}}+p_{i}^{2})^{1/2}q\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}}\\
+\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta m_{e}p_{\Lambda}^{2}}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}+2p_{i}^{2}+q^{2}+2(\frac{y}{\beta m_{e}}+p_{i}^{2})^{1/2}q\cos(\theta)-\frac{\Delta}{m_{e}}+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}},

(76)

At a fixed
y
, the denominator, as a function of
\theta
, has two regimes: when the integral has a root at some
\theta
, and the regime of analyticity. Let us define
\theta_{i}
to be an angle where the denominator vanishes at
y=0
:

\cos(\theta_{i})=\frac{p_{o}^{2}-p_{i}^{2}-q^{2}}{2p_{i}q}.

(77)

Expanding in the vicinity of
\theta=\theta_{c,i}
, I can rewrite the integrals in the form:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}_{0}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{-\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))-2(1-\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})(\theta-\theta_{i})+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}}\\
+\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta m_{e}p_{\Lambda}^{2}}dyd\theta\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))-2(1+\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})(\theta-\theta_{i})+\frac{i\Omega}{m_{e}}},

(78)

which after integration gives

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{\Lambda}_{0}\frac{dy\bar{n}(y)}{2(1-\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\Bigg(\\
\log\left(\left(\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)-\log\left(\left(\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))-2(1-\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})\theta_{i}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)\Bigg)\\
-\frac{1}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dy\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{2(1+\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\Bigg(\\
\log\left(\left(\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)-\log\left(\left(\frac{2y}{\beta m_{e}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))+2(1+\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}})p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})\theta_{i}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)\Bigg)\\
+\frac{\pi i}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\int\frac{dyn(y)}{1-\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}}-\frac{\pi i}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\int\frac{dyn(y)}{1+\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}},

(79)

The imaginary part
\propto T^{2}/T_{F,i}^{2}
here will not contribute to the action, and therefore we omit it in what follows. Clearly, there are 2 characteristic temperatures present here. One corresponds to the Fermi scale:
T_{F,i}=m_{e}p_{i}^{2}
, while the other corresponds to mixing of electron-hole excitations:

T_{L,i}=\frac{m_{e}p_{i}q\theta_{c,i}^{2}}{1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i})+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\sin(\theta_{c,i})\theta_{c,i}}\approx\frac{m_{e}p_{o}\delta q}{1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}}.

(80)

a)

For low temperatures,
T\ll T_{L,i}
, we neglect terms
O((T/T_{F,i})^{2})
,

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=-\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{\Lambda}_{0}\frac{dy\bar{n}(y)}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\log\left(\left(-\frac{y}{\beta p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i})+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\theta_{c,i}^{2})+1\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dy\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\log\left(\left(\frac{y}{\beta p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i})+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\theta_{c,i}^{2})+1\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\\
+\frac{\pi i}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\int\frac{dyn(y)}{1-\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}}-\frac{\pi i}{2m_{e}^{2}\beta p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\int\frac{dyn(y)}{1+\frac{y}{2\beta m_{e}p_{i}^{2}}},

(81)

and expand the logarithms in powers of
yT/T_{L,i}
:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=\frac{1}{12m_{e}^{2}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\left(\frac{T^{2}}{T_{L,i}}\right)\approx\frac{\pi^{2}\theta_{i}}{12m_{e}(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}})}\left(\frac{T^{2}}{T_{L,i}^{2}}\right).

(82)

b)

At higher temperatures,
T_{L,i}<T\ll T_{F,i}
the integrand is peaked at a value of
y
where the argument of the logarithm vanishes, which is

\displaystyle y_{1,<}=\frac{\beta p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}{1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i})+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\theta_{c,i}^{2}}=\frac{T_{L,i}}{T},

(83)

\displaystyle y_{2,<}=-\frac{\beta p_{i}q\theta_{i}^{2}}{1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i})+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\theta_{c,i}^{2}}=-\frac{T_{L,i}}{T}.

(84)

for the corresponding terms. We then write the integral in the form:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=-\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int^{\Lambda}_{0}\frac{dy\bar{n}(y)}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\Bigg(\log\left(\left(y-\frac{T_{L,i}}{T}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega T_{\kappa}}{2Tm_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)\Bigg)\\
+\frac{1}{2^{2}m_{e}^{2}\beta}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dy\frac{\bar{n}(y)}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\Bigg(\log\left(\left(y+\frac{T_{L,i}}{T}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega T_{\kappa}}{2Tm_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)\Bigg),

(85)

where I additionally introduced
T_{\kappa}=m_{e}/(1+\frac{q}{2p_{i}}\cos(\theta_{i}))
. We rescale the integration variable
y\to yT_{L,i}/T
and extend the range to
\infty
, after the division of the integration range we get:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)=-\frac{T_{L,i}}{2^{2}m_{e}^{2}}\int^{\Lambda}_{0}\frac{dy\bar{n}(y)}{p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{i})}\Bigg(\log\left(\left(y-1\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega T_{\kappa}}{2T_{1}m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)-\log\left(\left(y+1\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Omega T_{\kappa}}{2T_{1}m_{e}}\right)^{2}\right)\Bigg)\\
=\frac{T_{L,i}}{m_{e}^{2}p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{c})}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{1+e^{yT_{L,i}/T}}+\frac{T_{L,i}}{m_{e}^{2}p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{c})}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y(1+e^{yT_{L,i}/T})}

(86)

which after division of the latter integral into 2 over ranges
(1,T/T_{L,i})
and
(T/T_{L,i},\infty)
:

\Delta I_{i}(q,\Omega,T)\approx\frac{T_{L,i}}{2m_{e}^{2}p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{c,i})}\left(1+\Gamma_{0}(1)\right)+\frac{T_{L,i}}{2m_{e}^{2}p_{i}q\sin(\theta_{c,i})}\left(\log(T/T_{L,i})-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{T_{L,i}}{T}\right)\right).

(87)

clearly, the linear behavior here may only arise as an approximation to
\log(T/T_{L,i})
: for example while doing an expansion close to 1.

Finite temperature correction:
outer FS contribution

The expression for the outer contribution is

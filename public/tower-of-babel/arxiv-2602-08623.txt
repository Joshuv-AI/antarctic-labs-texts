On soft contributions to the B − → γ ∗ B^{-}\to\gamma^{*} form factors
Aoife Bharucha, Danny van Dyk, Eduardo Velásquez

Abstract
The photoleptonic decay B − → γ ​ ℓ − ​ ν ¯ B^{-}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu} is the simplest low-energy process that probes the substructure of the B B meson, making it an excellent candidate to determine the parameters of B B -meson light-cone distribution amplitudes from experimental data. More recently, the decay B − → γ ∗ ( → ℓ ′ ⁣ − ℓ ′ ⁣ + ) ℓ − ν ¯ B^{-}\to\gamma^{*}(\to\ell^{\prime-}\ell^{\prime+})\ell^{-}\bar{\nu} has received attention as an alternative probe of these parameters. Both decays are described through a common set of hadronic form factors, which if computed within the framework of QCD factorization give rise to the sensitivity to the B B -meson light-cone distribution amplitudes. Nevertheless, in this case the form factors still receive so-called soft contributions that can only be estimated but not rigorously computed. In this work, we provide results for the QCD factorization expressions for the form factors, including terms at next-to-leading power in the b b quark mass and in the photon energy. We further use these results to estimate the soft contributions within a light-cone sum rule setup. Finally, using numerical results obtained from a benchmark model of the light-cone distribution amplitudes, we show that the soft contributions are under significantly better theoretical control at a mildly spacelike photon virtuality.

Beside lattice QCD simulations

QCD factorization (QCDF), also called collinear factorization

10
;
11
;
13

Another source are light-cone sum rules with an on-shell
B

30

31

24

Beyond the form factors, QCDF calculation of nonleptonic
B

8

A dominant source of uncertainty for calculations within the latter three frameworks
emerges from our incomplete knowledge of the
B
meson light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs).
A particularly important role in such calculations is played by the inverse moment
\lambda_{B}^{-1}
of the twist-2 LCDA

and its inverse moment. Further inverse-logarithmic moments arise in QCDF calculations at next-to-leading order in
\alpha_{s}

The light-cone distribution amplitude

of the heavy quark effective theory (HQET).
Neither the inverse moment, its logarithmic cousins, nor the functional form of the LCDA

The prevalent approach to extract information on these quantities, and therefore the substructure of the

-meson LCDAs.
The photoleptonic decay

For an energetic photon, its amplitude can be accessed within the framework of QCDF

28

18

The sensitivity to the LCDA parameters in analyses involving the photoleptonic decay has been recently studied in detail
33

The physical photoleptonic amplitude arises in the
q^{2}\to 0
limit of the
B^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}
amplitude,
where
q
denotes the photon momentum. The amplitude involving an off-shell photon is relevant to the description of
the decay
B^{-}\to\ell^{\prime+}\ell^{\prime-}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}

17

15

36

However, for timelike photon virtuality, the treatment of hadronic resonances in the photon spectrum are a challenge

In this work, we investigate the general case of an off-shell photon; the on-shell limit is discussed where relevant.

The description of both of these processes involves the hadronic two-point correlation function

T_{\text{H}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)=\int dx\ e^{iq\cdot x}\matrixelement{0}{T\{J_\text{em}^\mu(x) J_\mathrm{H}^\nu(0)\}}{B^-(q + k)}\,,

(2)

Moreover,

J_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(x)=\bar{q}(x)\mathcal{Q}\gamma^{\mu}q(x)+\sum_{\ell}Q_{\ell}\bar{\ell}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\ell(x)

represents the electromagnetic current. We abbreviate
q(x)=(u(x),d(x),s(x),c(x),b(x))^{\mathrm{T}}
and introduce the

Q_{\ell}=-1

\mathcal{Q}=\operatorname{diag}[2/3,-1/3,-1/3,2/3,-1/3]

Furthermore,

(4)

As the dominant contribution to

-meson LCDAs. Corrections to the dominant light-cone contribution

However, despite this light-cone dominance, soft configurations also contribute to the time-ordered product via field configurations with

The soft contributions are not accessible within the framework of QCDF. An estimate using a light-cone sum rule setup was first discussed

The current state of the art calculation of the

Afterwards, a new form factor basis was proposed that avoids kinematic singularities, which is a prerequisite for the application of dispersion relations

In this paper, we calculate the off-shell form factors in this complete and singularity-free form factor basis within the framework of QCDF, including higher-power corrections and higher-twist contributions up to twist 4.
We further revisit the derivation of the original light-cone sum rule setup for an estimate of the soft contributions and apply it to the off-shell case.

Factorization framework and beyond

To leading order in QED, the photoleptonic amplitude reads

\mathcal{M}(B^{-}(p)\to\ell^{-}(p_{\ell})\bar{\nu}_{\ell}(p_{\nu})\gamma^{*}(q,\varepsilon))=\frac{4G_{\textrm{F}}V_{ub}}{\sqrt{2}}\bra{\ell^-\bar{\nu}_\ell\gamma^*}J^{\nu}_{\mathrm{L}}(0)J_{\mathrm{H}\nu}(0)\ket{B^-}\,.

(5)

The prefactor contains the Fermi constant
G_{\textrm{F}}
and the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix element
V_{ub}
.
We abbreviate the charged currents as
J^{\nu}_{\mathrm{L}}(x)=\bar{\ell}(x)\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}(x)

and
J_{\mathrm{H}\nu}

4

It is convenient to split the matrix element into a hadronic tensor
T_{\text{H}}
and a final-state radiation (FSR)
tensor

\bra{\ell^-\bar{\nu}_\ell\gamma^*}J^{\nu}_{\mathrm{L}}(0)J_{\mathrm{H}\nu}(0)\ket{B^-}=eQ_{B^{-}}\varepsilon^{*}_{\mu}\left[T_{\text{H}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)+T_{\text{FSR}}^{\mu\nu}(p_{\ell},p_{\nu})\right]L_{\nu}\,.

(6)

Here
T_{\text{H}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)
is the hadronic tensor defined in
eq.

2
,

is the positron charge,
Q_{B^{-}}=-1
is the
B
-meson’s relative charge in units of

,

is the polarisation vector of the photon, and we abbreviate
L^{\nu}=\bra{\ell^-\bar\nu}J_{L}^{\nu}\ket{0}
.
The second term,
T_{\text{FSR}}
, can be computed perturbatively to order
\alpha_{e}^{1}
by taking into account the FSR
off the charged-lepton; we refer to
section

A.1
for its definition.
The splitting of the matrix element into the two terms

and
T_{\text{FSR}}
is somewhat arbitrary (albeit convenient) and certainly not gauge invariant;
hence, the two tensors do not independently fulfil the Ward identity. Following ref.
32
, we further split the hadronic tensor into
a homogeneous and non-homogeneous part,
T_{\text{H}}=T_{\text{hom}}+T_{\text{inhom}}
; see
section

A.2
for their definitions.
The former admits a Lorentz decomposition in terms of four scalar-valued functions:
the
B^{-}\to\gamma^{*}
form factors

through
F_{4}

.

2.1
Leading factorizable contributions

For an on-shell photon with large energy in the
B
-meson rest frame,
the time-ordered product in
eq.

2
is dominated by field configurations at lightlike distances,

i.e.

x^{2}=0

23
;
14
.
This light-cone dominance is most readily discussed using light-cone coordinates. To this end, one commonly
introduces two lightlike vectors
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
, with
n_{+}\cdot n_{-}=2
, and write

\displaystyle p_{b}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{b}}{2}\left(n_{+}+n_{-}\right)^{\mu}=m_{b}v^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle p_{s}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{2}n_{+}^{\mu}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n_{+}q)}{2}n_{-}^{\mu}+\frac{(n_{-}q)}{2}n_{+}^{\mu}+q_{\perp}^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle k^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{b}-(n_{+}q)}{2}n_{-}^{\mu}+\frac{m_{b}-(n_{-}q)}{2}n_{+}^{\mu}-q_{\perp}^{\mu}\,.

Here,
p_{b}
and
p_{s}
are the momenta of the
b
quark and the spectator anti-quark, respectively.
Light-cone dominance
x^{2}\lesssim 1/(m_{b}\Lambda_{\text{had}})
is achieved if
(n_{+}q)=\order{m_b}

and
(n_{-}q)=\order{\Lambda_\text{had}}
.

As a consequence of the light-cone dominance, a light-cone expansion of the relevant propagators becomes possible,
leading to a systematic expansion of the form factors in terms of
\Lambda_{\text{had}}/m_{b}
,

\Lambda_{\text{had}}/(n_{+}q)
, and
\alpha_{s}
. The leading power result for each form factor
factorizes into hard-collinear scattering kernels
T_{i}
and the leading two-body light-cone distribution
amplitude of the
B
meson
\phi_{+}(\omega;\mu)
:

F_{i}(q^{2},k^{2})=\frac{f_{B}}{n_{+}q}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\ T_{i}^{\text{tw2}}(\omega,n_{+}q,n_{-}q;\mu)\,\phi_{+}(\omega;\mu)+\order{\frac{\Lambda_\text{had}}{\lbrace n_+q, m_b\rbrace}}^{2}\,.

(10)

Here
\omega
is the usual leading light-cone component of the spectator quark momentum.
The above holds for two-body Fock states within the
B
meson; three-body contributions
have similar factorization formulas as discussed in ref.
6
.
The factorization formula
eq.

10
receives further contributions due to off-light-cone field configurations. Contributions arising from
x^{2}\simeq 1/(\Lambda_{\text{had}}m_{b})
are captured by a systematic expansion in
x^{2}
,
which involve light-cone distribution amplitudes of higher twist
6
.

We expand hard scattering kernels for the form factors in
eq.

10
to leading power in
\Lambda_{\text{had}}/m_{b}
.
Despite the fact that
\omega
is integrated on the interval
[0,\infty)
,
we assume a power counting of
\omega\sim\Lambda_{\text{had}}
.
This is motivated by a rapid decline of the leading-twist LCDA
\phi_{+}(\omega)

that always multiplies the leading-power two-particle hard scattering kernels.
In our notation, this yields

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\text{tw2}}

\displaystyle=C_{V}^{(A0)}K^{-1}J\frac{M_{B}Q_{u}}{\omega-n_{-}q}\,,

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\text{tw2}}

\displaystyle=2C_{V}^{(A0)}K^{-1}J\frac{M_{B}(M_{B}-n_{+}q)Q_{u}}{(n_{+}q)(\omega-n_{-}q)}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle T_{3}^{\text{tw2}}

\displaystyle=0+\order{\alpha_s \frac{\Lambda}{m_b}},

\displaystyle T_{4}^{\text{tw2}}

\displaystyle=C_{V}^{(A0)}K^{-1}J\frac{M_{B}Q_{u}}{\omega-n_{-}q}\,,

where
C_{V}^{(A0)}\left(n_{+}q;\mu\right)=U_{1}\left(n_{+}q;\mu_{h1},\mu\right)C_{V}^{(A0)}\left(n_{+}q;\mu_{h1}\right)

is the Wilson coefficient that arises when the QCD heavy-to-light current
J_{H}
, defined in
eq.

4
,
is matched to the corresponding soft collinear effective theory (SCET) current at the hard scale
\mu_{h1}
;
K^{-1}(\mu)=U_{2}(\mu_{h2},\mu)K^{-1}(\mu_{h2})

is the conversion factor between the scale dependent decay constant and the physical
B
meson decay constant to
one loop accuracy as
f_{B}=F_{B}(\mu)K(\mu)
;
J\left(n_{+}q,n_{-}q,\omega;\mu\right)
is the hard collinear matching function.
Here we introduce
U_{1}\left(n_{+}q;\mu_{h1},\mu\right)
, the evolution factor associated with the SCET current
and
U_{2}(\mu_{h2},\mu)
, arising from the matching of the
B
meson decay constants in QCD with HQET.
Explicit formulae for
C_{V}^{(A0)}\left(n_{+}q;\mu_{h1}\right)
and
J\left(n_{+}q,n_{-}q,\omega;\mu\right)
are given
in
15
while those for
K^{-1}(\mu_{h2})
,
U_{1}\left(n_{+}q;\mu_{h1},\mu\right)
and
U_{2}(\mu_{h2},\mu)

are given in
14
.
For brevity, we have dropped the arguments of these functions in
eq.

11
.

Our results provide the hard-scattering kernel for the complete and singularity-free basis of physical form factors for the first time.
We confirm the general structure at leading power and to leading order in
\alpha_{s}
discussed
in the literature
21
;
6
;
15
: the hard-scattering kernels
exhibit a universal
\omega
dependence.

2.2
Power corrections and higher-twist contributions

In this section we will present our results for the form factors at non-zero
q^{2}
and including next-to-leading power corrections.
We express these results as

F_{i}(v\cdot q,q^{2})=F_{i}\big|_{\text{LP}}+F_{i}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}+F_{i}\big|_{1/m_{b}}\,,

(12)

for
i=1,2,4
, separating out the leading-power (LP),
1/(v\cdot q)
and
1/m_{b}
suppressed contributions,
where
v=(n_{+}+n_{-})/2
is the velocity of the
B
meson:
p^{\mu}=M_{B}v^{\mu}
.
Here, we prefer to use
v\cdot q
in lieu of
k^{2}
due to its manifest role in the power expansion.
Note that we will not extract
higher-twist contributions to the form factor
F_{3}
, due to its nature as a power-suppressed form factor.
The LP terms are given in
eqs.

10
and
11
.
As mentioned previously, corrections to the factorization formula
eq.

10
arise
due to higher twist light-cone operators. The twist of an operator increases either when its mass dimension increases,
in which case the hard-scattering kernels
T_{i}
in
eq.

10
receive additional contributions, or when additional fields arise, in which case generalized kernels need to be defined.

Following the approach in ref.
15
, we introduce the light-cone expansion of the hadronic tensor
T_{H}

up to the twist-four level in heavy quark effective theory (HQET).
This leads to a different choice of kinematic variables,
i.e.

\displaystyle p_{b}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=m_{b}v^{\mu}+\tilde{k}^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle p_{s}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\omega v^{\mu}\,.

(13)

where
\tilde{k}^{\mu}
is a small “residual” momentum satisfying
\tilde{k}^{2}\ll m_{b}^{2}
. Expanding the light-quark propagator in a soft background gluon field beyond leading order
4
yields

\displaystyle T_{\text{H},\text{LP}+1/(v\cdot q)}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\frac{Q_{u}f_{B}M_{B}}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\ \frac{e^{iq\cdot x}}{x^{4}}v_{\rho}x_{\sigma}\,\left(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle\left[\Phi_{+}(v\cdot x)+x^{2}G_{+}^{\text{WW}}(v\cdot x)-\frac{x^{2}}{2(v\cdot x)^{2}}\Phi_{-}^{\text{t3}}(v\cdot x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\phantom{-}\,\,\phantom{\left(\right.}-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \big[\Psi_{4}-\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big]^{\text{t4}}\left(v\cdot x,u(v\cdot x)\right)\right]\,.

In the above, we use
\Phi_{+}(v\cdot x)
to denote the two-particle (2pt) leading-twist (tw2) LCDA.
Further LCDAs arise, including
\Phi_{-}^{\text{t3}}(v\cdot x)
the two-particle twist-three (tw3) LCDA,

G_{+}^{\text{WW}}(v\cdot x)
the two-particle twist-four (tw4) LCDA, and

\big[\Psi_{4}-\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big]^{\text{t4}}(v\cdot x,u(v\cdot x))
as one of the three-particle (3pt) twist-four LCDAs.
These LCDAs follow from the the definitions in ref.
20
, which we repeat for convenience in

section

A.4
.
In the notation used throughout this work, upper-case letters refer to these LCDAs as functions in position space,
while the same letter in lower case denotes its momentum space Fourier conjugate function.

We set out to derive the higher-twist contribution presented in
eq.

14
as dispersive integrals,
which is necessary to estimate their impact in the soft contributions. Before doing so, we note that the three-particle LCDA
\big[\Psi_{4}-\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big]^{\text{t4}}(z_{1},z_{2})
cannot be written
in terms of a generic profile function, contrary to the orthogonal linear combination
\big[\Psi_{4}+\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big](z_{1},z_{2})

6
.
Hence, we replace the former with the latter, using
eq.

52
. Further eliminating
G_{+}^{\text{WW}}(v\cdot x)
using
eq.

53
, the hadronic tensor takes the form

\displaystyle T_{\text{H},\text{LP}+1/(v\cdot q)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q_{u}f_{B}M_{B}}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\ \frac{e^{iq\cdot x}}{x^{4}}v_{\rho}x_{\sigma}\,\left(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\phantom{-}\,\,\left[\Phi_{+}(v\cdot x)-\frac{x^{2}}{2(v\cdot x)^{2}}\Phi_{-}^{\text{t3}}(v\cdot x)+\frac{x^{2}}{2(v\cdot x)}\Phi_{-}^{\text{WW}\prime}(v\cdot x)\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left.\phantom{-}\,\,\phantom{\left(\right.}-\frac{x^{2}}{4(v\cdot x)}\Phi_{+}^{\prime}(v\cdot x)+\frac{x^{2}}{4}\bar{\Psi}_{4}^{+}(v\cdot x)\right]\,.

(15)

In the above, we introduce an LCDA in position space that renders the above expression very compact,

\bar{\Psi}_{4}^{+}(z)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}du\ \Psi_{4}^{+}(z,uz)\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\Psi_{4}^{+}(z_{1},z_{2})\equiv\big[\Psi_{4}+\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big](z_{1},z_{2})+\big[\Psi_{4}+\tilde{\Psi}_{4}\big](z_{2},z_{1})\,.

(16)

This form also facilitates expressing the higher-twist contributions at order
1/(v\cdot q)
as dispersive integrals.
In order to transform to the momentum-space representation, we use integral identities which are collected in
section

A.5
.
Performing the Fourier transform, projecting onto our basis, defining
\hat{q}^{2}\equiv q^{2}/(2v\cdot q)
and expanding in
1/(v\cdot q)
,
we obtain expressions for each of the form factors at order
\text{LP}+1/(v\cdot q)
.
These expression involve only three of the LCDAs:
\phi_{+}
,
\phi_{-}^{\text{t3}}
and
\bar{\psi}_{4}^{+}
.

We organise our result for the
1/(v\cdot q)
corrections in terms of two-particle twist-2 and twist-3 and three-particle twist-4 contributions as

F_{i}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}=F_{i}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{2pt,tw2}}+F_{i}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{2pt,tw3}}+F_{i}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{3pt,tw4}}\,.

(17)

On carrying out the calculation, we find these three contributions to the form factors to be:

\displaystyle F_{1}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{2pt,tw2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}F_{2}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{2pt,tw2}}=F_{4}\big|_{1/(v\cdot q)}^{\text{2pt,tw2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{u}f_{B}M_{B}}{4(v\cdot q)^{2}}\left[3\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega-\hat{q}^{2}}\,\omega\,\phi_{+}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega-\hat{q}^{2}}\omega^{2}\phi^{\prime}_{+}(\omega)\right.

Despite the light-cone dominance, the time-ordered product giving rise to the hadronic matrix element in

also receives soft contributions due to field configurations with
x^{2}\simeq\Lambda_{\text{had}}^{2}
.
These soft contribution are not accessible within the framework of QCDF. However, an estimate using a light-cone sum rule setup is possible, which was first discussed for the case of an on-shell photon in ref.
21
; further refinements followed in
refs.
38
;
6
.
Here, we revisit the derivation of the original light-cone sum rule setup for the general off-shell case. In the process, we critically
assess the impact of working with a basis of form factors free of kinematic singularities, which is a pre-requisite for the application
of dispersive methods such as light-cone sum rules.

The soft-contributions arising from interactions at
x^{2}\simeq\Lambda_{\text{had}}^{2}
cannot be captured in the framework
of QCDF. They therefore inhibit our ability to use
B^{-}\to\{\gamma,\ell^{\prime-}\ell^{\prime+}\}\ell\bar{\nu}

data to extract information
34
;
33
on the light-cone distribution amplitudes, either from
experimental measurements
1

or from lattice QCD simulations
26
;
27
.
The biggest concern here is the creation of an intermediate on-shell
u\bar{u}
pair at timelike
q^{2}
,
which is resonantly enhanced as
q^{2}
approaches
m_{\rho}^{2}
or the mass of any other unflavoured hadronic resonance with
the same quantum numbers as the photon; see
fig.

1
for a sketch of how this contribution arises.
This resonant enhancement is not restricted to purely timelike
q^{2}
. Instead, one can expect a substantial “tail”
of the soft contribution also affecting
q^{2}<m_{\rho}^{2}
.
On the other hand, for the purpose of dealing with soft contributions, the emission of the photon from the
b
quark line is not a cause of concern.
This follows since a
b\bar{b}
pair can only go on-shell at
q^{2}\simeq 4m_{b}^{2}
, far beyond the kinematic boundaries of the
physical decay process.
Therefore, we only investigate soft contributions due to emission from the
u
quark line.
We follow the original derivation for estimating these soft contributions as discussed in ref.
21

and start by considering their dispersion relation at fixed
k^{2}
,

F_{i}(k^{2},q^{2})=\frac{f_{\rho}\mathcal{F}_{i}^{B\to\rho}(k^{2})}{m_{\rho}^{2}-q^{2}}+\frac{f_{\omega}\mathcal{F}_{i}^{B\to\omega}(k^{2})}{m_{\omega}^{2}-q^{2}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{{s_{0}}}^{\infty}ds\,\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}(k^{2},s)\right\}}{s-q^{2}}\,.

(29)

Here, the first two terms isolate the production of an intermediate on-shell
\rho
or
\omega
meson in a vector-meson dominance model,
whereas the last term corresponds to the production of all other intermediate on-shell multi-meson states above an effective threshold
s_{0}
.
The resonance terms are of particular interest, since they can be assumed to encode the brunt of the soft contributions.
The calligraphic
\mathcal{F}_{i}
symbolize a linear combination of the respective
B\to\rho
and
B\to\omega
form factors.
Note that this representation of the form factors requires the absence of spurious kinematic singularities.
The presence of such spurious singularities would lead to additional terms in the dispersion integral.
Hence, this representation should ideally be used only in singularity-free basis of form factors proposed in ref.
32
.
The broad nature of the
\rho
leads to the expectation of a non-negligible tail,
i.e.
substantial contributions for
k^{2}
values
significantly smaller than
M_{\rho}^{2}
, while the effects of the narrow
\omega
resonance
will be more localized. We therefore concentrate on the
\rho
contribution and drop the
\omega
-resonance term.

The QCDF results for the form factors can also be expressed in the form of a dispersive integral,

F_{i}(k^{2},q^{2})\big|_{\text{QCDF}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}^{\text{QCDF}}(k^{2},s)\right\}}{s-q^{2}}\,.

(30)

The dispersive representation of the results at leading power
eq.

10
and
our new results beyond leading power as provided in

eqs.

18
,
19
,
20
,
26
and
28

makes it possible to read off
their imaginary parts by identifying
s
with
(n_{+}q)\omega
.
For large values of
s
above the effective threshold, one can now assume quark-hadron duality to hold locally
21
,

\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}(k^{2},s)\right\}\simeq\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}^{\text{QCDF}}(k^{2},s)\right\}\quad\text{for }s>s_{0}\,,

(31)

i.e.
assume that the partonic and hadronic contributions coincide
22
.
Using this assumption, one equates
eq.

29
and
eq.

30
to extract the resonance terms (
i.e.
the expected dominant soft contributions)

\frac{f_{\rho}\mathcal{F}_{i}^{B\to\rho}(k^{2})}{m_{\rho}^{2}-q^{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}ds\,\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}^{\text{QCDF}}(k^{2},s)\right\}}{s-q^{2}}\,.

(32)

Note here that the characterisation of the resonance terms as being dominant does not follow from a strict power-counting argument.

To reduce the sensitivity to the approximation in
eq.

31

f_{\rho}\mathcal{F}_{i}^{B\to\rho}(k^{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}ds\,\operatorname{Im}\left\{F_{i}^{\text{QCDF}}(k^{2},s)\right\}e^{-\left(s-m_{\rho}^{2}\right)/M^{2}}\,.

(33)

29

\displaystyle F_{i}(k^{2},q^{2})=

4
Conclusion

The decays
B^{-}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}
and
B^{-}\to\ell^{\prime+}\ell^{\prime-}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}
provide the opportunity to
probe the substructure of the
B
meson by confronting measurements of the decay rates with theoretical predictions.
In this article, we have investigated the four physical form factors for
B^{-}\to\gamma^{*}
transitions within the framework of QCD factorization (QCDF),
taking care to carry out this investigation for a basis of form factors that is free of spurious kinematic singularities.
We provide, for the first time, results at non-zero
q^{2}
that include power corrections at orders
1/m_{b}
and
1/(v\cdot q)
, relative to the leading terms.
Our results agree with those for the form factor to an on-shell photon already available in the literature.
We have taken care to express our results in the form of a dispersion relation.

Besides the dominant hard-collinear contributions arising in QCDF, the form factors receive so-called soft contributions
that cannot be computed at present.
Following previous work, we provide an estimate of these contributions within a light-cone sum rule setup.
This setup makes use of a dispersion relation for the form factors, and the expressions within this framework reuse the dispersive integrals
and integration kernels from our QCDF results.
Importantly, our work generalises the existing estimates based on
B
-meson light-cone distribution amplitudes available in the literature,
which only account for two of the four form factors.

We provide benchmark numerical results for two photon virtualities, illustrating that the soft contributions represent a substantial part of the full result
for an on-shell photon. However, we find that shifting the photon virtuality mildly into the spacelike region, the soft contribution
reduces much more strongly than the total form factor result, to
\leq 8\%
of the total form factor.
We therefore conclude that determinations of the
B
-meson LCDA parameters
from external inputs, such as experimental or lattice QCD results, should ideally be carried out at spacelike photon virtualities;
a virtuality as small as
q^{2}\simeq-2\,\textrm{GeV}^{2}
suffices for this purpose.

The final state radiation (FSR) contribution is commonly included through the matrix element

(41)

where
J_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}
is the electromagnetic current as defined in
eq.

3
, and
J_{L}^{\nu}
is the leptonic current
introduced in
eq.

5
.
It is convenient to define instead an FSR tensor
32
:

T_{\text{FSR}}^{\mu\nu}\left(p_{\ell},p_{\nu},q\right)=f_{B}\left[g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2p_{\ell}^{\mu}p_{\ell}^{\nu}+p_{\ell}^{\mu}q^{\nu}+q^{\mu}p_{\ell}^{\nu}-\left(p_{\ell}\cdot q\right)g^{\mu\nu}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\left(p_{\ell}\right)_{\rho}q_{\sigma}}{\left(p_{\ell}+q\right)^{2}-m_{\ell}^{2}}\right]

q_{\mu}\left[T_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)+T_{\mathrm{FSR}}^{\mu\nu}\left(p_{\ell},p_{\nu},q\right)\right]L_{\nu}=0\,.

(44)

\displaystyle q_{\mu}T_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)

\displaystyle=-f_{B}(k+q)^{\nu}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle q_{\mu}T_{\mathrm{FSR}}^{\mu\nu}\left(p_{\ell},p_{\nu},q\right)

32

\displaystyle q_{\mu}T_{\mathrm{H},\text{hom.}}^{\mu\nu}(k,q)

(47)

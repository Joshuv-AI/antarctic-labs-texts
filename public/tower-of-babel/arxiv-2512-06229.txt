Abstract

In this paper, we show that, for every prime p p , R ⁡ ( F , ℤ p ) ≤ n + 9 ​ p − 12 R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+9p-12 for every forest F F in n ≥ 3 ​ p 2 − 12 ​ p + 11 n\geq 3p^{2}-12p+11 vertices with p | e ⁡ ( F ) p\mid e(F) .

Introduction and main result

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
G
be a graph with
k\mid e(G)
. The
zero-sum Ramsey number

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
is the smallest integer
N
such that, for every edge-coloring
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}E(K_{N})\to\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, there exists a copy of
G
in
K_{N}
satisfying
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\chi(e)=0
. We say that such a copy of
G
has zero-sum. It is simple to see that
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
exists if, and only if,
k\mid e(G)
.

Recall that the
k
-colored Ramsey number
R_{k}(G)
is the minimum
N
such that every
k
-coloring of the edges of
K_{N}
has a monochromatic copy of
G
. In particular,
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})\leq R_{k}(G)
. For a comprehensive review of zero-sum Ramsey problems, we recommend the survey of Caro
[
4
]
.

The exact value of
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
is known for on a few instances of
G
and
k
. On the other hand, some general upper bounds are known for some families of graphs. For complete graphs, the following is known (
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
):

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
be an integer and suppose
k\mid\binom{n}{2}
.

(i)

If
k
is an odd prime-power, and
n+k
is at least the Ramsey number
R(K_{2k-1},k)
, then
R(K_{n},Z_{k})\leq n+2k-2
. If, in addition,
k
is a prime that divides
n
then
R(K_{n},Z_{k})=n+2k-2
.

(ii)

If
n\geq R(K_{3k-1},k)
then
R(K_{n},Z_{k})\leq n+k(k+1)(k+2)\log_{2}k
.

A curious feature of the zero-sum Ramsey number, in contrast to its usual (monochromatic) counterpart, is that, for a fixed
k
,
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
is not a monotonic function of
G
. That is, it is not true, in general, that if
k\mid e(G),e(H)
and
H
is a subgraph of
G
, then
R(H,\mathbb{Z}_{k})\leq R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
. For instance,
R(C_{4},\mathbb{Z}_{2})=4<5=R(2K_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, where
C_{4}
is the cycle on
4
vertices, and
2K_{2}
is the matching on two edges. In particular, Theorem
1
does not give bounds for
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
for non-complete graphs.

Some other notable results on zero-sum Ramsey numbers are the complete determination of
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and the determination of
R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{3})
for forests, as the next two results show:

Theorem 2
.

(
[
4
]
)
For any graph
G
with
n
vertices and an even number of edges,

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\begin{cases}n+2&\text{if $G=K_{n}$ and $n=0,1\mod{4}$,}\\
n+1&\text{if $G=K_{p}\cup K_{q}$ and $\binom{p}{2}+\binom{q}{2}=0\mod{4}$, or $G$ is odd,}\\
n&\text{otherwise, }\end{cases}

Theorem 3
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let
F
be a forest on
n
vertices with
3\mid e(F)
and without isolated vertices. Then,

R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{3})=\begin{cases}n+2,&\text{if $F$ is $1\pmod{3}$ regular or a star;}\\
n+1,&\text{if $3\nmid d(v)$ for every $v\in V(F)$ or $F$ has exactly one}\\
&\text{vertex of degree $0\pmod{3}$ and all others are $1\pmod{3}$,}\\
&\text{and $F$ is not $1\pmod{3}$ regular or a star;}\\
n,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Following the line of research suggested by these results, in this paper we prove the following general upper bound on the zero-sum Ramsey number of forests in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

Theorem 4
.

Let
p
be a prime number. Then,

R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+9p-12

for every forest
F
in
n\geq 3p^{2}-12p+11
vertices with
p\mid e(F)
.

2
Definitions and notation

In this section, we give some definitions that will be used later in the proof.

For a leaf in a forest, we call its only neighbor its
parent
, and the leaf is a
child
of this vertex.

Definition 5

(Bushy forest)
.

A forest
F
is called
bushy
if it contains at least
2(p-1)
distinct leaves. Otherwise, it is called
non-bushy
. Notice that, for
p=2
, every nonempty forest is bushy.

Throughout the paper,
p
will represent a fixed prime number and
K
will denote the complete graph on which we are trying to embed our forest. Also, a
coloring
of a graph
G
always means a coloring of the edges of
G
whose colors are elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, i.e., a coloring is a function
{\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. From this point on, we will always assume the edges of
K
are colored by some coloring
\chi
.

Definition 6

(
b
-colorful vertex)
.

If
b
is a positive integer, a vertex
v\in K
will be called
b
-colorful
if there exists a color such that
v
is connected to at least
b
but at most
|K|-b-1
edges of that color.

Definition 7

(Vibrant coloring)
.

We call a coloring of a clique
\chi

vibrant
if it has
p-1
vertices that are
(3p-5)
-colorful. Otherwise it is called
non-vibrant
.

Definition 8

(Switcher
C_{4}
)
.

A colored
C_{4}
(cycle of size 4) in
K
will be called a
switcher
if its edges are, in some consecutive order,
\{a,b,c,d\}
and

\chi(a)+\chi(b)\neq\chi(c)+\chi(d).

Definition 9

(Switchable coloring)
.

A coloring
\chi
of
K
is called
switchable
if it contains
p-1
pairwise vertex-wise disjoints
C_{4}
that are switchers. Otherwise, call it
non-switchable
.

As usual, we will use the sum-set notation:

A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}\coloneq\{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\mid(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})\in A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\cdots\times A_{n}\}.

We will also make extensive use of the following generalization of the classical theorem of Cauchy-Davenport in additive number theory, which can easily be derived from the original result through induction:

Theorem 10

(Generalized Cauchy-Davenport)
.

If
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{n}
are nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then

|A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}|\geq\min(p,|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|+\cdots+|A_{n}|-n+1).

3
Proof of the main result

In this section, we provide the linear upper bound for
R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. We will split the proof between two major cases concerning the number of leafs in the graph, each divided into two subcases according to the structure of the coloring
\chi
. Also, from now on, we suppose
F
does not contain any vertices with degree
0
, since these clearly don’t interfere with the main result.

3.1
Bushy forests

In this subsection, we will deal with the case when
F
is bushy, meaning we can select some parents
v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{m}
and some of their leaves, each one with
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m}
selected leafs, respectively, in such way that we select a total of
p-1
leaves (meaning
a_{i}\geq 1
for all
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
and
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{m}=p-1
) and no
v_{i}
is among the selected leaves.

Remark 11
.

We asked for a bushy forest to have at least
2(p-1)
leafs instead of simply
p-1
in order to deal with the case when two leaves are both parent and children of each other (meaning a connected component that is just an edge). Thus, we would be able to select a set of
p-1
leaves that doesn’t contain their parents (which will prove useful in the future).

We now consider the following subcase:

3.1.1
The coloring
\chi
is vibrant

In this case, we choose
m
vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m}
among the
p-1
vertices of
K
that are
(3p-5)
-colorful. For each
u_{i}
, let
x_{i}
be the color that incides on it with at least
3p-5
and at most
|K|-3p+4
edges. Also, denote by
S_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}-\{x_{i}\}
, notice that
u_{i}
neighbors at least
3p-5
and at most
|K|-3p+4
edges with color in
S_{i}
. Now we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 12
.

There exists pairwise disjoints sets
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{m},Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}
of vertices of
K
that satisfy all of the following:

•

|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|=a_{i}
;

•

The edges connecting
u_{i}
to the vertices of
X_{i}
are all colored
x_{i}
and the edges connecting
u_{i}
to the vertices of
Y_{i}
are
not
colored
x_{i}
;

•

u_{i}\notin X_{j},Y_{k}
for all
1\leq i,j,k,\leq m
.

Proof.

We will choose each pair
(X_{i},Y_{i})
inductively:

1.

For
i=1
just take
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
as
a_{1}-
sized subsets of the neighbors of
u_{1}
different from
u_{2},\cdots,u_{m}
that are connected to it with color
x_{1}
and with some color in
S_{i}
, respectively. Notice that this is possible since both sets have at least
(3p-5)-(m-1)\geq(3p-5)-[(p-1)-1]=2p-3\geq a_{1}
vertices to choose from;

2.

Now, suppose
(X_{1},Y_{1}),\cdots,(X_{k-1},Y_{k-1})
have all been chosen for some
1\leq k\leq m
. We will show how to select
(X_{k},Y_{k})
.

Since the sets are pairwise disjoint, the union of all
X_{i}
with all
Y_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq k-1
has exactly:

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|X_{i}|+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|Y_{i}|=2(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k-1})

Thus, the number of vertices of
K
that connect with
u_{k}
with color
x_{k}
that aren’t in this union (this is necessary to meet the disjoint condition) nor in the set
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{k-1},u_{k+1},\cdots,u_{m}\}
(this is necessary to meet the third condition) is at least:

3p-5-2(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1})-(m-1)\geq 3p-5-2(p-1-a_{k})-(p-2)\geq a_{k}

Therefore, we can choose
X_{k}
to be an
a_{k}
-sized subset of this set. Analogously, we can select
Y_{k}
.

Through this induction, we select all the
2k
sets satisfying the conditions.
∎

Now, define
X\coloneq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
and
Y\coloneq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
be the union of all
X_{i}
and
Y_{j}
, respectively. Notice that
|X|=|Y|=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=p-1
.

Notice that
|F|+|X|=|F|+|Y|=n+(p-1)\leq|K|
, thus, we can embed
F
on
K
in such way that each of the
v_{i}
and
u_{i}
coincide and each of the
a_{i}
of
v_{i}
’s children we have selected lie on the set
X_{i}\cup Y_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,m
. Considering all such embeddings, our goal is to prove the following:

Proposition 13
.

One of these embeddings of
F
is zero-sum.

Proof.

For each
v_{i}=u_{i}
, let
A_{i}
be the set of all the possible values of the sum of the edges joining
u_{i}
and its
a_{i}
selected children when they vary in
X_{i}\cup Y_{i}
.

If
X_{i}=\{p_{i,1},p_{i,2},\cdots,p_{i,a_{i}}\}
and
Y_{i}=\{q_{i,1},q_{i,2},\cdots,q_{i,a_{i}}\}
such that the edges
p_{i,j}u_{i}
have color
x_{i}
for each
j
and
q_{i,j}u_{i}
have color in
S_{i}
for each
j
.

Now, consider the
a_{i}
pairs
\mathcal{P}_{i,j}=(p_{i,j},q_{i,j})
for
j=1,2,\cdots,a_{i}
. On one hand, by selecting one of
u_{i}
’s children as one of the elements in
\mathcal{P}_{i,j}
for each
j
, we obtain
2^{a_{j}}
choices of how to embed the children whose possible sums of the
a_{i}
edges connecting them to
u_{i}
lie on the set
\mathcal{P}_{i,1}+\mathcal{P}_{i,2}+\cdots+\mathcal{P}_{i,a_{i}}
, meaning:

\mathcal{P}_{i,1}+\mathcal{P}_{i,2}+\cdots+\mathcal{P}_{i,a_{i}}\subseteq A_{i}

On the other hand, since
|\mathcal{P}_{i,j}|=2
, we can apply the generalized version of the Cauchy-Davenport (Theorem
10
):

\displaystyle|A_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{P}_{i,1}+\mathcal{P}_{i,2}+\cdots+\mathcal{P}_{i,a_{i}}|

\displaystyle\geq\min(p,|\mathcal{P}_{i,1}|+|\mathcal{P}_{i,2}|+\cdots+|\mathcal{P}_{i,a_{i}}|-(a_{i}-1))

\displaystyle\geq\min(p,2+2+\cdots 2-(a_{i}-1))

\displaystyle\geq\min(p,a_{i}+1)=a_{i}+1.

Now, the set of all the possible values of the sum of the edges in the embeddings of
F
we described earlier is precisely the set

A\coloneq A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{m}.

Therefore, we again apply theorem
10
to bound its size by

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\geq\min(p,|A_{1}|+\cdots+|A_{m}|-(m-1))

\displaystyle\geq\min(p,(a_{1}+1)+\cdots+(a_{m}+1)-m+1)

\displaystyle=\min(p,a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m})

\displaystyle=\min(p,p)=p.

Proving
A
spans over all the residues modulo
p
, which implies that
0\in A
and so there must be a embedding of
T
that has sum
0
.
∎

Remark 14
.

This proof actually shows the better bound (and optimal, as shown in Section
4
)

R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+p-1,

whenever
F
is bushy and
\chi
is vibrant, with no constraints on the number of vertices of
F
whatsoever.

3.1.2
The case when
\chi
is non-vibrant

In this case
F
has at most
p-2
vertices that are
(3p-5)-colorful
. Therefore, there is an induced subgraph
K^{\prime}
of
K
with
|K|-(p-2)
vertices such that for all color
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
v\in K
they either share at most
3p-5
or at least
|K^{\prime}|-3p+4
edges. However, by pigeonhole principle, each
v
shares at least
\frac{|K^{\prime}|-1}{p}=\frac{|K|-(p-1)}{p}=\frac{n+9p-12-(p-1)}{p}\geq\frac{3p^{2}-12p+11+9p-12-(p-1)}{p}\geq 3p-4
with some color
c_{v}
. Thus,
c_{v}
must have at least
|K^{\prime}|-(3p-4)
edges connecting to
v
. Call the color
c_{v}

v^{\prime}s

dominant
color and let
\alpha\coloneq 3p-4
.

Now, for
r=0,1,\cdots,p-1
, let
G_{r}\subseteq K^{\prime}
be the set of the vertices with dominant color
r
. We will prove the following lemma:

Lemma 15
.

|G_{0}|^{2}+|G_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|G_{p-1}|^{2}\geq|K^{\prime}|^{2}-2\alpha|K^{\prime}|.

Proof.

The idea is to count the total number of edges of
K^{\prime}
that join some
G_{i}
and
G_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Let
E
be this set of edges.

One one hand, each
v\in G_{r}
for some
r=0,1,\cdots,p-1
neighbors at least
|K^{\prime}|-|G_{r}|-\alpha
edges colored
r
in
E
. Altogether, each component
G_{r}
will emit at least
|G_{r}|(|K^{\prime}|-|G_{r}|-\alpha)
edges in
E
. Thus

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|(|K^{\prime}|-|G_{r}|-\alpha)

\displaystyle=(|K^{\prime}|-\alpha)\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|-\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(|K^{\prime}|-\alpha)|K^{\prime}|-\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}

On the other hand, among all edges in
K^{\prime}
,
E
only doesn’t contain the ones that connect vertices among the components
G_{r}
, each with
\tbinom{|G_{r}|}{2}
internal edges. Therefore:

\displaystyle|E|=

\displaystyle\tbinom{|K^{\prime}|}{2}-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\tbinom{|G_{r}|}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|K^{\prime}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|K^{\prime}|}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|K^{\prime}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|K^{\prime}|}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|K^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|K^{\prime}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}

Combining both equations:

\displaystyle\frac{|K^{\prime}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}=|E|\geq(|K^{\prime}|-\alpha)|K^{\prime}|-\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}\geq|K^{\prime}|^{2}-2\alpha|K^{\prime}|

∎

Now let,
M\coloneq\max(|G_{r}|)
be the size of the largest
G_{r}
. We now derive the following claim:

Proposition 16
.

M\geq|K^{\prime}|-2\alpha=|K|-6p+8

Proof.

Since
M\geq|G_{r}|
for all
r\in 0,1,\cdots,p-1
we have that:

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}\leq\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}M|G_{r}|=M\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|=M|K^{\prime}|

However, because of
15
we conclude that:

M|K^{\prime}|\geq\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}|G_{r}|^{2}\geq|K^{\prime}|^{2}-2\alpha|K^{\prime}|\implies M\geq|K^{\prime}|-2\alpha=|K|-6p+8

∎

Now, we focus on
l
such that
|G_{l}|=M
. We finish this case with the following proposition:

Proposition 17
.

G_{l}
contains a monochromatic copy of
F
.

Proof.

We will rely on the fact that
|G_{l}|=M\geq|K|-6p+8\geq n+\alpha
.

We will obtain the desired monchromatic copy through the following greedy algorithm:

1.

Start by selecting one of the trees
T
of
F
;

2.

Pick one of
T
’s leaves and place it on one of
G_{l}
’s vertices;

3.

If we have already embedded a subtree
T_{0}
of
T
, pick a vertex of
T
that was not selected already
v
that is connected to some vertex
u
in
T_{0}
(note
u
must be unique for each
v
, else
T
would have a cycle);

4.

By pigeonhole principle, among the
|G_{l}|-(|F|-1)\geq\alpha+1
non-selected vertices of
G_{l}
at least one must connect to
u
(actually the vertex of
G_{l}

u
was embedded on) with color
l
. Thus, we can embed
v
;

5.

Go back to step 3 until all of
T
was embedded;

6.

Go back to step 1 until all of
F
was embedded.

∎

Hence, there is a monochromatic copy of
F
in
G_{l}
. Since
p\mid E(F)
this copy must also be zero-sum.

Remark 18
.

In this case, we actually used all of the size conditions we imposed about
|K|
and
n
.

3.2
Non-bushy forests

In this section we deal with the case when
F
is non-bushy. Since for
p=2
,
F
is always bushy, we will suppose from now on that
p\geq 3
. The main idea will be to focus on the vertices that have degree
2
. For that, we start off with the proposition:

Proposition 19
.

F
has at least
n-4p
vertices of degree
2
.

Proof.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}
and
n_{3}
be the number of vertices of
F
that have degree 1,2 and at least 3, respectively.

Notice
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n
and, since
F
is non-bushy,
n_{1}\leq 2p-3
. Using the handshake lemma,

\displaystyle 2e(F)=\sum_{v\in F}\deg v

\displaystyle\geq n_{1}+2n_{2}+3n_{3}

\displaystyle=n+n_{2}+2n_{3}

\displaystyle=n+n_{2}+2(n-n_{2}-n_{1})

\displaystyle\geq n+n_{2}+2(n-n_{2}-(2p-3))

\displaystyle=3n-n_{2}-4p+6

On the other hand, since
F
is a forest:

3n-n_{2}-4p+6\leq 2e(F)\leq 2(n-1),

which implies
n_{2}\geq n-4p
.

∎

3.2.1
The coloring
\chi
is switchable

In this subsection we discuss the case when
\chi
is switchable, meaning there are
p-1
pairwise vertex-wise disjoint switcher
C_{4}
s which we will call
D_{1},\cdots,D_{p-1}
.

Since
n-4p\geq 3(p-1)
, it is not hard to see we may select

vertices of
F
with degree 2
t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{p-1}
, such that no two of them are or share neighbors.

Now, for each
i=1,2,\cdots,p-1
, let
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
be
t_{i}
’s neighbors. The main idea will be two embed each triple
(a_{i},b_{i},t_{i})
in three of
D_{i}
’s vertices.

We may label each
D_{i}
’s vertices in consecutive order
d_{i,1},d_{i,2},d_{i,3}
and
d_{i,4}
in such way that
\chi(d_{i,4}d_{i,1})+\chi(d_{i,1}d_{i,2})\neq\chi(d_{i,2}d_{i,3})+\chi(d_{i,3}d_{i,4})
.

Now, call an embedding of
F
in

•

a_{i}=d_{i,2}
for
i=1,2\cdots,p-1
;

•

b_{i}=d_{i,4}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,p-1
;

•

t_{i}\in\{d_{i,1},d_{i,3}\}
for

.

Remark 20
.

Notice a good embedding exists since
|K|\geq n+(p-1)
, with the extra

Abstract
. We solve a long-standing open problem, formulated by Krasner in the 1950’s, in the context of Polish (i.e. separable complete) ultrametric spaces by providing a characterization of their isometry groups using suitable forms of generalized wreath products of full permutation groups. Since our solution is developed in the finer context of topological (Polish) groups, it also solves a problem of Gao and Kechris from 2003. Furthermore, we provide an exact correspondence between the isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces belonging to some natural subclasses and various kinds of generalized wreath products proposed in the literature by Hall, Holland, and Malicki.

An

Problem 1

Characterize the isometry groups of ultrametric spaces.

This is one of the oldest problems concerning ultrametric spaces, as it was proposed shortly after the concept of ultrametric was introduced by Krasner himself as an abstraction of the special properties of the

Although the problem has remained wide open for about 70 years, several partial results were obtained, each one involving some form of generalized wreath product of transitive permutation groups.
For example, Feĭnberg

Fei74

Fei78
]

This result was later extended by Nosova and Feĭnberg

We remark that Krasner’s problem and all the partial results mentioned so far are purely algebraic, as no attempt to define a group topology on generalized wreath products appears in the literature.

Problem

The isometry group

[
GK03

, Gao and Kechris proved that, indeed, every Polish group is of this form, up to topological group isomorphism; therefore, the class of isometry groups of Polish metric spaces and the class of Polish groups are the same.
This naturally started the search for similar characterizations for interesting subclasses of Polish metric spaces.
To mention a few examples:
compact Polish groups are the isometry groups of compact metric spaces
[
GK03
,
Mel08

closed subgroups of

are the isometry groups of zero-dimensional locally compact Polish metric spaces

locally compact Polish groups are the isometry groups of proper Polish metric spaces

Along the same lines, Gao and Kechris posed the following problem
[
GK03
, Problem 10.10]

Problem 2

(Gao and Kechris, 2003)
.

Characterize the isometry groups of Polish (or locally compact Polish) ultrametric spaces.

This time, the problem must be intended in the framework of topological groups.
Very little was known in this direction.
It is easy to see that if
X
is a Polish ultrametric space, then
\operatorname{Iso}(X)
is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of the infinite symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)

\operatorname{Iso}(X)

[

However, not all closed subgroups of
\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)
are isomorphic to the isometry group of a Polish ultrametric space: for example, if
\operatorname{Iso}(X)
is nontrivial, then it must contain a nontrivial involution
[
GK03

\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)
or to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

[
MS09
, Proposition 4.1]

The main goal of this paper is to solve Krasner’s Problem

Then, in Section

This already yields that all such classes give rise to the same collection of automorphism or isometry groups, up to (topological) group isomorphism.

In Section

Theorem 1.1
.

Up to (topological) isomorphism, the following classes of groups are the same:

(1)

isometry groups of arbitrary Polish ultrametric spaces,

(2)

isometry groups of locally compact Polish ultrametric spaces,

(3)

generalized wreath products over local domains
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S}}_{\delta\in\Delta}\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
,

(4)

projective wreath products
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
,

(5)

projective wreath products of the form
\operatorname{Wr}^{\operatorname{LF},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
,

where the underlying order
\Delta
of each wreath product is a countable
L

\mathbf{S}
is a countable collection of local domains satisfying Holland’s maximum condition.

Our methods are flexible enough to allow for restricted forms of the above characterization, drawing exact correspondences between certain natural subclasses of Polish ultrametric spaces (like homogeneous or homogeneous discrete spaces, Urysohn spaces, and exact spaces), and various natural variants (both old and new) of generalized wreath products.

Preliminaries

We assume some familiarity with classical descriptive set theory
[
Kec95
]
, and with the basics of category theory
[
ML98
]
.
In this section, we present only a few concepts and notations which are maybe less standard.

We let
\mathrm{Card}
denote the class of all cardinals. The first infinite cardinal
\aleph_{0}
coincides with the ordinal
\omega
, which is the order type of
(\mathbb{N},{\leq})
and is identified with it.
Given a linear order
(L,{\leq_{L}})
, its coinitiality
\operatorname{coi}(L)
is the smallest
\kappa\in\mathrm{Card}
such that there is a sequence
(\ell_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}
in
L
which is coinitial, i.e. such that for every
\ell\in L
there is
\alpha<\kappa
such that
\ell_{\alpha}\leq_{L}\ell
.
A subset
C\subseteq L
is convex if for every
\ell\leq_{L}\ell^{\prime}\leq_{L}\ell^{\prime\prime}
, if
\ell,\ell^{\prime\prime}\in C
then
\ell^{\prime}\in C
as well. Among the convex subsets of
L
we find the intervals, for which we use standard notations like
[\ell;+\infty)_{L}
,
(\ell;\ell^{\prime})_{L}
, and alike.
The reverse of a linear order
L
is denoted by
L^{*}
; in particular,
\omega^{*}
is the linear order
(\mathbb{N},{\geq})
.
The product of two linear orders
L
and
L^{\prime}
is denoted by
L\cdot L^{\prime}
, and consists of the cartesian product of
L
and
L^{\prime}
ordered antilexicographically. Similarly, products of partial orders are always endowed with the antilexicographic order.

Suppose that
(Q,{\leq_{Q}})
is a partial order, and
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}
is a family of nonempty sets. If
x\in\prod_{q\in Q}A_{q}
, for every
p\in Q
we let
x|_{p}=x\restriction\{q\in Q\,:\,q\geq_{Q}p\}\in\prod_{q\geq_{Q}p}A_{q}
be the restriction of
x
to the cone above
p
. If
A_{q}\in\mathrm{Card}
for every
q\in Q
, we also let

\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{q\in Q\,:\,x(q)\neq 0\}

be the support of
x
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{+}=\{r\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,r>0\}
.
A Polish metric space is a metric space
(X,d)
such that
d
is complete and induces a second-countable topology.
Its distance set is
D=\{d(x,x^{\prime})\,:\,x,x^{\prime}\in X\}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
A metric space
(X,d)
is uniformly discrete if there is
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
B_{d}(x,r)=\{x\}
for every
x\in X
, where
B_{d}(x,r)
is the sphere centered in
x
with radius
r
.
Equivalently,
(X,d)
is uniformly discrete if its distance set
D
is bounded away from
0
, that is,
\inf D>0
.
Throughout the paper, we will tacitly use the known special properties of ultrametric spaces, such as the fact that every point in a sphere is a center of it, or the fact that if two spheres intersect, then one of them is contained into the other one. Some of the constructions concerning ultrametric spaces that will be used in this work are inspired by those appearing in
[
MR17
,
CMMR18
]
, so the interested reader might consult those papers for more information on the matter.

In this paper, several groups that were introduced as purely algebraic objects, such as the generalized wreath products, will be turned into topological groups by introducing natural topologies on them. To distinguish the two setups, we use
\simeq
to denote algebraic isomorphism, while
G\cong H
means that
G
and
H
are isomorphic as topological groups;
when speaking of topological isomorphism, we usually drop the adjective from the terminology.
Moreover, unless otherwise stated (and with the notable exception of generalized wreath products), symmetric groups
\operatorname{Sym}(N)
over a nonempty set
N
will be endowed with the pointwise convergence topology with respect to the discrete topology on
N
, and their subgroups
H\leq\operatorname{Sym}(N)
will be equipped with the relative topology.

Finally, recall that a functor
\mathscr{F}
between categories
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
is faithful (respectively, full) if for every
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}
, the restriction of
\mathscr{F}
to the set of arrows between
A
and
A^{\prime}
is injective (respectively, surjective onto the set of arrows between
\mathscr{F}(A)
and
\mathscr{F}(A^{\prime})
). A (categorical) full embedding is a fully faithful functor which, moreover, is also injective on objects.

L
-trees

Let
(L,{\leq_{L}})
be a linear order.

Definition 3.1
.

An
L
-tree
is a triple
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
such that
(T,{\leq_{T}})
is a partial order,
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}\colon T\to L
is a surjective map, and the following conditions are satisfied, for every
t,t^{\prime}\in T
:

(1)

if
t<_{T}t^{\prime}
then
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)<_{L}\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t^{\prime})
;

(2)

for every
\ell\geq_{L}\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)
there exists a unique
t^{\prime\prime}\in T
, denoted by
t|_{\ell}
, such that
t^{\prime\prime}\geq_{T}t
and
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t^{\prime\prime})=\ell
(in particular
t|_{\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)}=t
);

(3)

there exists
\ell\geq_{L}\max\{\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t),\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t^{\prime})\}
such that
t|_{\ell}=t^{\prime}|_{\ell}
;

(4)

if
t,t^{\prime}\in T
are
\leq_{T}
-incomparable,
then the nonempty set

\{\ell\geq_{L}\max\{\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t),\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t^{\prime})\}\,:\,t|_{\ell}\neq t^{\prime}|_{\ell}\}

has a maximum in
L
, denoted by
\mathrm{split}(t,t^{\prime})
.

If condition
(4)
is dropped, then
\mathcal{T}
is called a
weak
L
-tree
.
The cardinality of a weak
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
|\mathcal{T}|
, is the cardinality of its domain
T
.

Notice that if the reverse of
L
is well-founded (or even just
\omega+1
does not embed into
L

then every weak
L
-tree is an
L
-tree.

Examples of
L
-trees are the usual descriptive set-theoretic trees
T\subseteq{}^{<\omega}A
for any set
A
, once they are turned upside down.
Indeed, it is enough to let
L=n^{*}
, where
n\leq\omega
is the height of the tree,
\leq_{T}
be the order of

inclusion (i.e.
t\leq_{T}s\iff t\supseteq s
, for
s,t\in T
), and
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}\colon T\to n^{*}
be the function assigning to each

Other examples of
L
-trees are set-theoretic trees of transfinite height and linear orders.

Remark 3.2
.

Our notion of
L
-tree can be regarded as a generalization of the

-trees introduced by Gao and Shao in
[
GS11

Indeed, if
R\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
has no maximum, then any

Nevertheless, letting

Given a weak

\operatorname{Lev}_{\ell}(\mathcal{T})=\{t\in T\,:\,\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)=\ell\},

Moreover, let

[\mathcal{T}]=\{b\in{}^{L}T\,:\,\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(b(\ell))=\ell\text{ for every }\ell\text{ and }b(\ell)\leq_{T}b(\ell^{\prime})\text{ for every }\ell\leq_{L}\ell^{\prime}\}

\mathcal{T}

.

An
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}
is
special
if its condensed tree
\Delta(\mathcal{T})
is still an
L
-tree.

If
L
is finite, or even just a reverse well-order, then every
L
-tree is special.
It can be further shown that every
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}
can be turned into a special
L^{\prime}
-tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
such that
|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{T}|
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})

by possibly adding suitable “intermediate levels” to
L
.
If
L
is a countable linear order, this can also be obtained by composing the functors
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{F}
from the next section.

4.
Some useful functors

In this section we consider several embeddings among various categories of
L
-trees and complete ultrametric spaces.

Given a linear order
L
, let
\mathbb{T}_{L}
be the category of all
L
-trees with their embeddings, and
\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
be the induced subcategory consisting of all pruned
L
-trees.
These categories are clearly determined by the isomorphism type of
L
.
Indeed, if
f\colon L\to L^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of linear orders, then each
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}
can be transformed into a corresponding
L^{\prime}
-tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
by letting
T=T^{\prime}
,
t\leq_{T^{\prime}}t^{\prime}\iff t\leq_{T}t^{\prime}
, and
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}=f\circ\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}
.
This transformation yields a categorical isomorphism between
\mathbb{T}_{L}
and
\mathbb{T}_{L^{\prime}}
, which moreover preserves all relevant properties such as being pruned, being special, and so on.
Notice also that if
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{L^{\prime}}
is obtained from
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{T}_{L}
using the transformation above, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.

For
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let
\mathbb{U}_{D}
be the category whose objects are the nonempty complete ultrametric spaces with distance set contained in
D
, and whose arrows are the distance-preserving functions (i.e. the isometric embeddings).
Let also
\mathbb{U}_{D}^{0}
be the induced subcategory of
\mathbb{U}_{D}
of discrete spaces.

4.1.
The functor
\mathscr{U}

Throughout this subsection, we assume

Condition 1
:

D\subseteq D^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
are such that
\inf D>0
and
\inf D^{\prime}=0
.

We define the functor
\mathscr{U}\colon\mathbb{U}_{D}\to\mathbb{U}_{D^{\prime}}
as follows.

Let
(r_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be a strictly decreasing coinitial sequence in
D^{\prime}
such that
r_{0}<\inf D
.
Let
\bar{U}
be a
strongly rigid
perfect compact Polish ultrametric space with distance set
\{r_{n}\,:\,n\in\omega\}
, where strongly rigid means that the only isometric embedding from
\bar{U}
into itself is the identity (and hence, in particular,
\operatorname{Iso}(\bar{U})
is trivial).
For example, such a
\bar{U}
can be obtained by equipping the Cantor space
{}^{\omega}2
with the compatible complete ultrametric
\bar{d}
defined as follows. Let
\{s_{n}\,:\,n\in\omega\}
be an enumeration without repetitions of
{}^{<\omega}2
such that if
\operatorname{lh}(s_{n})<\operatorname{lh}(s_{m})
then
n<m
.
Given distinct
y,y^{\prime}\in{}^{\omega}2
, let
s_{n}
be the longest common initial segment of
y
and
y^{\prime}
, and set
\bar{d}(y,y^{\prime})=r_{n}
.
The Polish ultrametric space
\bar{U}=({}^{\omega}2,\bar{d})
is perfect and compact because, topologically, it is the usual Cantor space.
Moreover, it is also strongly rigid.
Indeed, given distinct
y,y^{\prime}\in{}^{\omega}2
, let
s_{n}
be the longest common initial segment of
y
and
y^{\prime}
.
Then there is
y^{\prime\prime}\in{}^{\omega}2
such that
\bar{d}(y,y^{\prime\prime})=r_{n}
, while this fails for
y^{\prime}
, which means that no isometric embedding of
\bar{U}
into itself can map
y
to
y^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathscr{U}(U)\in\mathbb{U}_{D^{\prime}}
be the space obtained from
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
by replacing each of its points with a distinct copy of
\bar{U}
. Formally, if
d
is the ultrametric on
U
, then
\mathscr{U}(U)
is obtained by endowing
U\times\bar{U}
with the complete ultrametric
\hat{d}
defined by

\hat{d}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))=\begin{cases}d(x,x^{\prime})&\text{if }x\neq x^{\prime}\\
\bar{d}(y,y^{\prime})&\text{if }x=x^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Moreover, given an isometric embedding
\psi\colon U\to U^{\prime}
with
U,U^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
, define
\mathscr{U}(\psi)\colon\mathscr{U}(U)\to\mathscr{U}(U^{\prime})
by setting
\mathscr{U}(\psi)(x,y)=(\psi(x),y)
, for all
x\in U
and
y\in\bar{U}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The functor
\mathscr{U}\colon\mathbb{U}_{D}\to\mathbb{U}_{D^{\prime}}
is a categorical full embedding such that
\mathscr{U}(U)
is a perfect locally compact space with
\operatorname{weight}(\mathscr{U}(U))=\max\{|U|,\aleph_{0}\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that, by construction,
\mathscr{U}(U)\in\mathbb{U}_{D^{\prime}}
is a nonempty perfect locally compact complete ultrametric space with weight

|U|\cdot\operatorname{weight}(\bar{U})=\max\{|U|,\aleph_{0}\}.

Moreover,
\mathscr{U}
is clearly a faithful functor which is injective on objects, so we only need to show that it is full.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{U}(U)\to\mathscr{U}(U^{\prime})
be an arbitrary isometric embedding. Let
d
,
d^{\prime}
,
\hat{d}
, and
\hat{d}^{\prime}
be the ultrametrics on
U
,
U^{\prime}
,
\mathscr{U}(U)
, and
\mathscr{U}(U^{\prime})
, respectively.

Claim 4.1.1
.

Let
x\in U
and
y\in\bar{U}
. Then
\varphi(x,y)=(x^{\prime},y)
, for some
x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
.

Proof of the claim.

Let
x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}\in\bar{U}
be such that
\varphi(x,y)=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
. Since

B_{\hat{d}}((x,y),\inf D)=\{x\}\times\bar{U}
and
B_{\hat{d}^{\prime}}((x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),\inf D)=\{x^{\prime}\}\times\bar{U}
, it follows from the definition of
\hat{d}
and the strong rigidy of
\bar{U}
that the map sending
z\in\bar{U}
to the unique
z^{\prime}\in\bar{U}
such that
\varphi(x,z)=(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
is the identity. Therefore,
y^{\prime}=y
.
∎

Now fix any
\bar{y}\in\bar{U}
. Let
\psi\colon U\to U^{\prime}
be defined by setting
\psi(x)=x^{\prime}
for the unique
x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(x,\bar{y})=(x^{\prime},\bar{y})
.
Notice that the definition of
\psi
does not depend on the choice of
\bar{y}
.
It is easy to verify that
\psi
is an isometric embedding, and that
\mathscr{U}(\psi)=\varphi
.
∎

In particular, for any
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
the restriction of the fully faithful functor
\mathscr{U}
to
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
witnesses
\operatorname{Iso}(U)\simeq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{U}(U))
; we now check that this isomorphism is also
topological
when, as usual, both groups are equipped with the pointwise convergence topology.

Corollary 4.2
.

For every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
,

\operatorname{Iso}(U)\cong\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{U}(U)).

Proof.

Let
d
be the ultrametric on
U
and
\hat{d}
be the ultrametric on
\mathscr{U}(U)
.
A fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity of
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
is given by the sets of the form

V_{x,r}=\{\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)\,:\,d(\psi(x),x)<r\}
, for
x\in U
and
0<r<\inf D
. By choice of
r
, we indeed get that
V_{x,r}=\{\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)\,:\,\psi(x)=x\}
, and moreover
V_{x,r}=V_{x,r^{\prime}}
for all
r,r^{\prime}<\inf D
. On the other hand, a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{U}(U))
is given by the sets of the form

W_{x,y,r}=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{U}(U))\,:\,\hat{d}(\varphi(x,y),(x,y))<r\}
, for
x\in U
,
y\in\bar{U}
, and
0<r<\inf D
.
By choice of
r
, definition of
\hat{d}
, and Claim
4.1.1
, we get
W_{x,y,r}=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{U}(U))\,:\,\varphi(x,y)=(x,y)\}
, and moreover
W_{x,y,r}=W_{x,y^{\prime},r^{\prime}}
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\bar{U}
and
r,r^{\prime}<\inf(D)
. Finally, by definition of
\mathscr{U}(\psi)
we have that for every
x\in U
and
r<\inf D
,
\mathscr{U}(V_{x,r})=W_{x,y,r}
for some (equivalently, any)
y\in\bar{U}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
The functor
\mathscr{F}

Throughout this subsection, we assume

Condition 2
:

D\subseteq L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
are such that

•

either
D
has no maximum or there exists
\ell\in L
with
\ell>\max D
;

•

either
\inf D=0
or there exists
\ell\in L
with
\ell\leq\inf D
.

We define the functor
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathbb{U}_{D}\to\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
as follows.

Given
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
, let
d
be its ultrametric.
Let
\mathscr{F}(U)
be the triple
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
where:

•

T=\{(B,\ell)\,:\,\ell\in L\text{ and }B=B_{d}(x,\ell)\text{ for some }x\in U\}
,

•

(B,\ell)\leq_{T}(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\iff\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}\text{ and }B\subseteq B^{\prime}
, and

•

\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}\colon T\to L
is the map sending each
(B,\ell)
to
\ell
.

Remark 4.3
.

Notice that
\mathscr{F}(U)=\{(B_{d}(x,\ell),\ell)\,:\,x\in A_{U}\text{ and }\ell\in L\}
for any dense
A_{U}\subseteq U
.
This is useful to control the cardinality of
\mathscr{F}(U)
, as in Theorem
4.5
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
,
\mathscr{F}(U)
is a pruned
L
-tree.

Proof.

We have to check that conditions
(1)
–
(4)
of Definition
3.1
are satisfied by the triple
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
defined as above.
Condition
(1)
holds by definition. As for
(2)
, given
(B,\ell)
and
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
, it is enough to set
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime}}=(B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime}),\ell^{\prime})
for some (equivalently, any)
x\in B
.

Given now
(B,\ell),(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\in T
, let
x,x^{\prime}
be such that
B=B_{d}(x,\ell)
and
B^{\prime}=B_{d}(x^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume
\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}
.
If
d(x,x^{\prime})<\ell^{\prime}
, then
(B,\ell)\leq_{T}(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
.
If
\ell^{\prime}\leq d(x,x^{\prime})
, then by the first assumption of Condition
2
we can find
\ell^{\prime\prime}\in L
such that
\ell^{\prime\prime}>d(x,x^{\prime})
: setting
B^{\prime\prime}=B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime\prime})
we get that
x,x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime\prime}
, and hence both
(B^{\prime\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime})\geq_{T}(B,\ell)
and
(B^{\prime\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime})\geq_{T}(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
because
\ell^{\prime\prime}\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
.
This shows that
(3)
is satisfied as well.

Finally, assume that
(B,\ell)
and
(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
are
\leq_{T}
-incomparable, so that
B\cap B^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
Pick any
x\in B
and
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, and set
\bar{\ell}=d(x,x^{\prime})\in D\subseteq L
.
For every
\ell^{\prime\prime}\in L
, if
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\leq\ell^{\prime\prime}\leq\bar{\ell}
then
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}=(B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})\neq(B_{d}(x^{\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})=(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}
because
B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime\prime})\cap B_{d}(x^{\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime})=\emptyset
; on the other hand, if
\ell^{\prime\prime}>\bar{\ell}
, then
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}=(B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})=(B_{d}(x^{\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})=(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}
. Thus
(4)
is satisfied and
\bar{\ell}=\mathrm{split}((B,\ell),(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}))
.

Moreover, the
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}
is pruned because if
(B,\ell)\in T
, then for every
x\in B
the sequence
b\in{}^{L}T
defined by
b(\ell^{\prime})=(B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime}),\ell^{\prime})
for every
\ell^{\prime}\in L
is an element of
[\mathcal{T}]
such that
b(\ell)=(B,\ell)
, as desired.
∎

Let
U,U^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
with ultrametrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
, respectively, and let
\mathcal{T}=\mathscr{F}(U)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathscr{F}(U^{\prime})
. For every isometric embedding
\psi\colon U\to U^{\prime}
, let
\mathscr{F}(\psi)
be the map
f\colon T\to T^{\prime}
defined by
f(B,\ell)=(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\ell),\ell)
for some (equivalently, any)
x\in B
. It is easy to check that
f
is a well-defined embedding between the pruned
L
-trees
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

The functor
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathbb{U}_{D}\to\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
is a categorical full embedding such that
|\mathscr{F}(U)|\leq\operatorname{weight}(U)\cdot|L|
for every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathscr{F}(U)\in\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
.
Then
|\mathcal{T}|\leq\operatorname{weight}(U)\cdot|L|
because by Remark
4.3
the map
(x,\ell)\mapsto(B_{d}(x,\ell),\ell)
is a surjection from
A_{U}\times L
onto
T
, for any dense set
A_{U}\subseteq U
.

The functor
\mathscr{F}
is clearly injective on objects.
We now check that it is also faithful. Fix
U,U^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
with ultrametrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
, respectively, and let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\colon U\to U^{\prime}
be distinct isometric embeddings.
Since
\psi_{0}\neq\psi_{1}
, there is
x\in U
such that
\psi_{0}(x)\neq\psi_{1}(x)
: let
\ell=d^{\prime}(\psi_{0}(x),\psi_{1}(x))\in D\subseteq L
, and let
B=B_{d}(x,\ell)
. Then
\mathscr{F}(\psi_{0})(B,\ell)=(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi_{0}(x),\ell),\ell)
and
\mathscr{F}(\psi_{1})(B,\ell)=(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi_{1}(x),\ell),\ell)
, which implies that
\mathscr{F}(\psi_{0})(B,\ell)\neq\mathscr{F}(\psi_{1})(B,\ell)
because
B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi_{0}(x),\ell)\cap B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi_{1}(x),\ell)=\emptyset
by choice of
\ell
. This shows that
\mathscr{F}(\psi_{0})\neq\mathscr{F}(\psi_{1})
.

It remains to show that
\mathscr{F}
is full. Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathscr{F}(U)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathscr{F}(U^{\prime})
for some
U,U^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
, and let
f\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be an embedding. Let
d
and
d^{\prime}
be the ultrametrics of
U
and
U^{\prime}
, respectively.
For every
x\in U
, let
b_{x}\in[\mathcal{T}]
be defined by
b_{x}(\ell)=(B_{d}(x,\ell),\ell)
for every
\ell\in L
.
Consider the branch
b^{\prime}\in[\mathcal{T}^{\prime}]
defined by
b^{\prime}(\ell)=f(b_{x}(\ell))
, and let
B^{\prime}_{\ell}
be such that
f(b_{x}(\ell))=(B^{\prime}_{\ell},\ell)
.
The intersection
\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}
contains at most one point.
Indeed, suppose towards a contradiction that there are two distinct points
y,y^{\prime}\in\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}
, and let
\bar{\ell}=d^{\prime}(y,y^{\prime})\in D\subseteq L
: since
B^{\prime}_{\bar{\ell}}
is a ball with radius
\bar{\ell}
, it cannot contain both
y
and
y^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
We claim that
\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}
is also nonempty. We distinguish two cases. If
\inf D=0
we use the fact that the balls
B^{\prime}_{\ell}
are clopen and
d^{\prime}
is complete: in this case,
\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}
contains the limit of the
d^{\prime}
-Cauchy sequence given by the centers of the balls
B^{\prime}_{\ell_{n}}
, for any sequence
(\ell_{n})_{n\in\omega}
coinitial in
L
.
Suppose now that
\inf D>0
, in which case there is some
\ell\in L
with
\ell\leq\inf D
by the second assumption of Condition
2
: then
B^{\prime}_{\ell}=\{y\}
is a singleton, and necessarily
y\in\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}
because for each
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
the ball
B^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}
must be nonempty and contained in
B^{\prime}_{\ell}
.
We thus showed that in all cases
\bigcap_{\ell\in L}B^{\prime}_{\ell}=\{y\}
for some
y\in U^{\prime}
: set
\psi(x)=y
.

We claim that the map
\psi\colon U\to U^{\prime}
just described is an isometric embedding.
Let
x,x^{\prime}\in U
be distinct, and let
\bar{\ell}=d(x,x^{\prime})\in D\subseteq L
. Let
b_{x},b_{x^{\prime}}\in[\mathcal{T}]
be the branches determined by
x
and
x^{\prime}
as above.
Notice that
b_{x}(\ell)\neq b_{x^{\prime}}(\ell)
if and only if
\ell\leq\bar{\ell}
.
Since
f
is an embedding,
f(b_{x}(\ell))=(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\ell),\ell)
, and
f(b_{x^{\prime}}(\ell))=(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x^{\prime}),\ell),\ell)
, we have that

(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\ell),\ell)\neq(B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x^{\prime}),\ell),\ell)
if and only if
\ell\leq\bar{\ell}
.
Therefore,
B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\bar{\ell})\cap B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x^{\prime}),\bar{\ell})=\emptyset
, while
B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\ell)=B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x^{\prime}),\ell)
for every
\ell>\bar{\ell}
.
It follows that
d^{\prime}(\psi(x),\psi(x^{\prime}))<\ell
for every
\ell\in L
with
\ell>\bar{\ell}
, but
d^{\prime}(\psi(x),\psi(x^{\prime}))\geq\bar{\ell}
. But since
U^{\prime}\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
, the distance
d^{\prime}(\psi(x),\psi(x^{\prime}))
must belong to
D
, and hence to
L
: thus
d^{\prime}(\psi(x),\psi(x^{\prime}))=\bar{\ell}=d(x,x^{\prime})
, as desired.

Finally, we show that
\mathscr{F}(\psi)=f
. Fix any
(B,\ell)\in T
and pick
x\in B
, so that
(B,\ell)=b_{x}(\ell)
. By the way
\psi
is defined,
\psi(x)\in B^{\prime}
where
B^{\prime}
is such that
f(B,\ell)=(B^{\prime},\ell)
. But this implies that
B^{\prime}=B_{d^{\prime}}(\psi(x),\ell)
, and thus
\mathscr{F}(\psi)(B,\ell)=(B^{\prime},\ell)=f(B,\ell)
, as desired.
∎

Analogously to what happened with the functor
\mathscr{U}
from the previous subsection, also in this case for every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
the restriction of
\mathscr{F}
to
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
is a
topological
group isomorphism between
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathscr{F}(U))
.

Corollary 4.6
.

For every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
,

\operatorname{Iso}(U)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathscr{F}(U)).

Proof.

Let
d
be the ultrametric of
U
.
By choice of
L
, the sets of the form
V_{x,\ell}=\{\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)\,:\,d(\psi(x),x)<\ell\}
for
x\in U
and
\ell\in L
form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity of
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
.
On the other hand, since
\mathcal{T}=\mathscr{F}(U)
is given the discrete topology, the sets of the form
W_{B,\ell}=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\,:\,f(B,\ell)=(B,\ell)\}
for
(B,\ell)\in T
form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.
The result thus follows from the fact that for
B=B_{d}(x,\ell)
,

d(\psi(x),x)<\ell\iff\mathscr{F}(\psi)(B,\ell)=(B,\ell),

and hence
\mathscr{F}(V_{x,\ell})=W_{B_{d}(x,\ell),\ell}
for every
x\in U
and
\ell\in L
.
∎

We conclude this subsection by discussing to what extent one can control extra properties of
\mathscr{F}(U)
and
L
.

In general, the
L
-trees produced by
\mathscr{F}
are not necessarily special.
However, for every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
we can arrange the construction so that
\mathscr{F}(U)
is special for the chosen
U
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For every
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
there is an
L
satisfying Condition
2
such that

\mathscr{F}(U)
is special. Moreover:

•

if
\inf D>0
, then we can assume that
L
has a minimum,
\min L=\inf D
, and
|L|\leq\operatorname{weight}(U)^{2}+|D|+2
;

•

alternatively, if
D
is countable and
U
is separable we can assume that
L
has order type
\eta
, the order type of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
d
be the ultrametric of
U
and fix a dense set
A_{U}\subseteq U
of cardinality
\operatorname{weight}(U)
.
Let
L^{\prime}
be obtained from
D
by adding to it
\inf D
if the latter is greater than
0
, and
2\max D
if
D
has a maximum, so that
|L^{\prime}|\leq|D|+2
.
For each
x,x^{\prime}\in U
, let
L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
be the set of those
\ell\in L^{\prime}
such that there is no
\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)
for which
d(\psi(x),x^{\prime})<\ell
, and observe that

L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
is downward closed in
L^{\prime}
. Set

(4.1)

L=L^{\prime}\cup\{\sup L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}\,:\,x,x^{\prime}\in U\text{ and }L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset\}.

Then
L
has size
|L|\leq\operatorname{weight}(U)^{2}+|D|+2
.
Indeed, if
\sup L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}=\bar{\ell}
,
d(x,y)<\bar{\ell}
, and
d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})<\bar{\ell}
, then
L^{\prime}_{y,y^{\prime}}=L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
; so in (
4.1
) the points
x,x^{\prime}
can be taken ranging in a dense subset of
U
.
Also,
L
satisfies Condition
2
because
L\supseteq L^{\prime}
. Moreover, by construction
\inf L=\inf L^{\prime}=\inf D
, and
L
has minimum
\min L=\min L^{\prime}=\inf D
if
\inf D>0
.

We need to show that if
\mathscr{F}
is defined starting from such
L
, then
\mathcal{T}=\mathscr{F}(U)
is special, i.e.
\Delta(\mathcal{T})
is an
L
-tree.
The key point is that by the way the
full
functor
\mathscr{F}
is defined,
for all
\ell\in L
,
(B,\ell),(B^{\prime},\ell)\in T
,
x\in B
, and
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
we have

(4.2)

(B,\ell)\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)\iff d(\psi(x),x^{\prime})<\ell\text{ for some }\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U),

and hence if
\ell\in L^{\prime}
also

(4.3)

(B,\ell)\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)\iff\ell\notin L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}.

Pick two
\leq_{\Delta(T)}
-incomparable
[(B,\ell)],[(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})]\in\Delta(T)
and two points
x\in B
and
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
.
Notice that for every
\ell^{\prime\prime}\leq\min\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
, we have that
\mathrm{split}([(B,\ell)],[(B^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})])
exists if and only if
\mathrm{split}([(B_{d}(x,\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})],[(B_{d}(x^{\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime}),\ell^{\prime\prime})])
exists, and in that case they coincide.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, and that
\ell\in L^{\prime}
(since
L^{\prime}
is coinitial in
L
).
We need to show that there is a maximal
\bar{\ell}\geq\ell
such that
(B,\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}
, as in this case
\bar{\ell}=\mathrm{split}([(B,\ell)],[(B^{\prime},\ell)])
.
Since
(B,\ell)\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)
, we have
\ell\in L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
by (
4.3
), and in particular
L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\bar{\ell}=\sup L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}\in L
, so that
\bar{\ell}\geq\ell
.
Suppose first that
\ell^{*}\in L
is such that
\ell\leq\ell^{*}<\bar{\ell}
. Then there is
\ell^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
such that
\ell^{*}\leq\ell^{\prime\prime}
.
Therefore
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}
by (
4.3
), and thus
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}
.
Now consider
\bar{\ell}
. If
\bar{\ell}\in L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
, then
(B,\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}
by (
4.3
). If instead
\bar{\ell}\notin L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
, then there are
\ell^{*}\in L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
which are arbitrarily close to
\bar{\ell}
. Therefore, using (
4.2
) we get that
(B,\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}
would imply
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}
for some
\ell^{*}\in L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
, contradicting (
4.3
). Therefore in all cases
(B,\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}\not\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\bar{\ell}}
. Finally, assume that
\ell^{*}\in L
is such that
\bar{\ell}<\ell^{*}
: we claim that
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}
. If
\ell^{*}\in L^{\prime}
, then
\ell^{*}\notin L^{\prime}_{x,x^{\prime}}
by choice of
\bar{\ell}
, and therefore the claim follows again from (
4.3
). If instead
\ell^{*}\notin L^{\prime}
, then by definition of
L
it is the supremum of a set of elements of
L^{\prime}
. It follows that there is
\ell^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\prime}
such that
\bar{\ell}<\ell^{\prime\prime}<\ell^{*}
. As already shown, this implies that
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}
, and hence also
(B,\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}\sim(B^{\prime},\ell)|_{\ell^{*}}
.
This proves that
\bar{\ell}
is as required.

The first item in the additional part is already achieved by the above construction.
For the second item, instead, one can easily check that it is enough to start the construction with
L^{\prime}=D\cup\mathbb{Q}
: since under our assumptions the resulting

will be a countable dense linear order with neither a minimum nor a maximum, its order type will be
\eta
by Cantor’s theorem.
∎

As observed, the category
\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
is determined up to isomorphism by the isomorphism type of
L
, therefore we get:

Corollary 4.8
.

If
D
is countable, then for every separable
U\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
there is a categorical full embedding
\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}\colon\mathbb{U}_{D}\to\mathbb{T}^{\mathrm{pr}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(U)
is countable and special, and moreover
\operatorname{Iso}(U)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(U))
.

In particular, for every countable
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
there is a categorical full embedding from the category of Polish ultrametric spaces with distance set contained in
D
into the category of countable pruned
\mathbb{Q}
-trees, and for every Polish ultrametric space

fixed in advance we can further ensure that its associated
\mathbb{Q}
-tree is special.

4.3.
The functor
\mathscr{G}

Throughout this subsection, we assume

Condition 3
:

L
is a countable linear order such that either
L
has no minimum or else
L\setminus\{\min L\}
has no minimum, and
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
is such that
\inf D>0
and
2\cdot L
embeds into
D
.

Notice that since
\inf D>0
, then
\mathbb{U}_{D}=\mathbb{U}^{0}_{D}
, and
\mathbb{U}^{0}_{D}
actually consists of
uniformly
discrete spaces.
We define the functor
\mathscr{G}\colon\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}\to\mathbb{U}^{0}_{D}
as follows.

Fix an embedding of
2\cdot L
into
D
, and for every
\ell\in L
let
\ell^{-}
be the image of
(0,\ell)
and
\ell^{+}
be the image of
(1,\ell)
, so that
\ell^{-}<\ell^{+}
.
Given
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
, let
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})\in\mathbb{U}_{D}
be defined by equipping
T
with the distance function

d_{T}(t_{0},t_{1})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }t_{0}=t_{1}\\
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{i})^{-}&\text{if }t_{1-i}<_{T}t_{i}\text{ for some }i\in\{0,1\}\\
\mathrm{split}(t_{0},t_{1})^{+}&\text{if }t_{0}\text{ and }t_{1}\text{ are $\leq_{T}$-incomparable}.\end{cases}

Obviously
|\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})|=|\mathcal{T}|
because we did not change the underlying set.
It is straightforward to verify that
d_{T}
is an ultrametric, and that
d_{T}(t,t^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)^{-}
for all distinct
t,t^{\prime}\in T
, hence
B_{d_{T}}(t,\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)^{-})=\{t\}
for every
t\in T
. Moreover,
(T,d_{T})
is uniformly discrete, and thus complete, because
\inf D>0
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For every
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
and every
\varphi\colon T\to T^{\prime}
, the map
\varphi
is an embedding between the
L
-trees
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
if and only if it is an isometric embedding between the complete ultrametric spaces
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})
and
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

One direction is easy. If
\varphi
is an embedding of
L
-trees, then, in particular,
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t))=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)
, and
t_{0},t_{1}\in T
are
\leq_{T}
-incomparable if and only if
\varphi(t_{0}),\varphi(t_{1})
are
\leq_{T^{\prime}}
-incomparable too, in which case
\mathrm{split}(\varphi(t_{0}),\varphi(t_{1}))=\mathrm{split}(t_{0},t_{1})
. Thus
\varphi
is also an isometric embedding.

Vice versa, suppose that
d_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}),\varphi(t_{1}))=d_{T}(t_{0},t_{1})
for all
t_{0},t_{1}\in T
, so that, in particular,
\varphi
is injective.
We first show that
\varphi
preserves levels.
Pick any
t_{0}\in T
.
If
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})=\min L
, then it realizes all distances in
\{\ell^{-}\,:\,\ell\in L\setminus\{\min L\}\}
, that is:
for every
\ell\in L\setminus\{\min L\}
there is
t_{1}\in T
such that
d_{T}(t_{0},t_{1})=\ell^{-}
(take
t_{1}=t_{0}|_{\ell}
).
By Condition
3
, we have that
L\setminus\{\min L\}
does not have a minimum (since
L
does by case assumption), hence the only nodes in
T^{\prime}
realizing all distances in
\{\ell^{-}\,:\,\ell\in L\setminus\{\min L\}\}
are those in
\operatorname{Lev}_{\min L}(T^{\prime})
: it follows that
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}))=\min L=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})
.
Suppose now that
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})
is not the minimum of
L
. Using again that
L
satisfies the first part of Condition
3
, there are infinitely many
\ell\in L
below
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})
.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is pruned, this means that we can find
t_{1},t_{2}\in T

such that
t_{2}<_{T}t_{1}<_{T}t_{0}
. Since
d_{T}(t_{1},t_{0})=d_{T}(t_{2},t_{0})=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})^{-}>\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{1})^{-}=d_{T}(t_{1},t_{2})
,
the points

\varphi(t_{0})
,
\varphi(t_{1})
, and
\varphi(t_{2})
are pairwise
\leq_{T^{\prime}}
-comparable (otherwise two of them would be at distance
\ell^{+}
for some
\ell\in L
, which is impossible since
\varphi
is distance preserving), and
\varphi(t_{0})>_{T^{\prime}}\varphi(t_{1}),\varphi(t_{2})

because the distance of
\varphi(t_{0})
from both
\varphi(t_{1})
and
\varphi(t_{2})
is greater than
d_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{1}),\varphi(t_{2}))
.
Hence
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}))^{-}=d_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}),\varphi(t_{1}))=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})^{-}
, and therefore
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}))=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})
.

It remains to show that
t_{0}<_{T}t_{1}\iff\varphi(t_{0})<_{T^{\prime}}\varphi(t_{1})
, for all
t_{0},t_{1}\in T
. If
t_{0}<_{T}t_{1}
, then
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})<\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{1})
and
d_{T}(t_{0},t_{1})=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{1})^{-}
: it follows that
\varphi(t_{0})
and
\varphi(t_{1})
are
\leq_{T^{\prime}}
-comparable (because their distance is of the form
\ell^{-}
, for some
\ell\in L
), and indeed
\varphi(t_{0})<_{T^{\prime}}\varphi(t_{1})
because
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{0}))=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{0})<\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t_{1})=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T^{\prime}}(\varphi(t_{1}))
. The argument for the reverse implication is similar.
∎

In view of Lemma
4.9
, it is natural to let
\mathscr{G}
be the identity on arrows. This means that
\mathscr{G}
is a fully faithful functor, and since it is obviously injective on objects we get:

Theorem 4.10
.

The functor
\mathscr{G}\colon\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}\to\mathbb{U}^{0}_{D}
is a categorical full embedding such that
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})
is uniformly discrete and
|\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})|=|\mathcal{T}|
(hence also

).

In particular, for every countable linear order

satisfying the first part of Condition

-trees into the category of uniformly discrete Polish ultrametric spaces, which combined with the functor

from Section

yields also a categorical full embedding from the category of pruned
L
-trees into the category of perfect locally compact Polish ultrametric spaces.
This can be extended to an arbitrary countable linear order

because, by the construction in the proof of
(4)

\Rightarrow

(3)
of Theorem

, there is a categorical full embedding from
\mathbb{T}_{L}^{\mathrm{pr}}
to
\mathbb{T}_{L^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{pr}}
, where

Since the restriction of
\mathscr{G}
to

are the same set

For every

5.
Generalized wreath products

5.1.
The classical definition

Generalized wreath products were introduced by Hall
[
Hal62
]
(linear case) and Holland
[
Hol69
]
(general case) as powerful generalizations of the usual wreath product of two groups.
A further variant was introduced by Malicki
[
Mal14
]
to study isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces with certain special features, called
W
-spaces.
Here we present their definitions in a unified and quite general framework.
Although our applications to isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces involve wreath products of full permutation groups, up to Section
5.3
we follow the literature and consider wreath products
\operatorname{Wr}^{S}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
of arbitrary transitive permutation groups
H_{\delta}
.

Each variant of the generalized wreath product is determined by two ingredients:
4
4

4

In
[
Mal14
]
, the partial order
\Delta
is called the “underlying plenary family” of
\operatorname{Wr}^{S}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
, while
S
is called the “underlying set” of
\operatorname{Wr}^{S}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
.

(a)

A nonempty partially ordered set
\Delta
, together with a labeling function
N\colon\Delta\to\mathrm{Card}\colon\delta\mapsto N_{\delta}
such that
N_{\delta}\neq 0
for all
\delta\in\Delta
.
The order of
\Delta
will be denoted by
\leq_{\Delta}
, or by
\leq
when
\Delta
is clear from the context.
The labeled partial order
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
is called the
skeleton
of the wreath product.
We say that the skeleton is
countable
if
|\Delta|\leq\aleph_{0}
and
N_{\delta}\leq\omega
for all
\delta\in\Delta
. It is
linear
when
\Delta
is a linear order.

(b)

A set
S\subseteq\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}N_{\delta}
, called (
global
)
domain

of the wreath product, closed under pointwise perturbations, i.e. such that for every
x\in S
,
\delta\in\Delta
, and
i\in N_{\delta}
, there is some
x^{\delta}_{i}\in S
satisfying
x^{\delta}_{i}(\delta)=i
and
x^{\delta}_{i}(\gamma)=x(\gamma)
for all
\gamma>\delta
.

Despite the chosen notation, in general the element
x^{\delta}_{i}\in S
in condition
(b)
might not be unique, although in many cases there are natural choices for it.
For example, Holland
[
Hol69
]
and Malicki
[
Mal14
]
set
x^{\delta}_{i}(\gamma)=0
and
x^{\delta}_{i}(\gamma)=x(\gamma)
for every
\gamma\not\geq\delta
, respectively.
As we will see (Definition
5.1
), these variants are inessential, as the actual choice of
x^{\delta}_{i}
does not matter at all.

Given a skeleton
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
and a domain
S
, the associated generalized wreath product
\operatorname{Wr}^{S}_{\delta\in\Delta}
is an operator that takes as input a family
(H_{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}
, where each
H_{\delta}\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
is a transitive permutation group on
N_{\delta}
that will be used on the
\delta
-th coordinate, and produces a group of permutations of the domain
S
.
The following definition comes from
[
Hol69
, p. 160]
, except that we allow arbitrary domains instead of the specific one considered by Holland.
See below for details.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
be a skeleton,
S\subseteq\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}N_{\delta}
be a domain, and let
(H_{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}
be a family of transitive permutation groups over the corresponding sets
N_{\delta}
.
The
generalized wreath product

\operatorname{Wr}^{S}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}

is the group of all permutations
g\in\mathrm{Sym}(S)
satisfying the following two conditions,
5
5

5

The letter “H” in the enumeration refers to Holland, who first introduced and studied wreath products of infinite families of groups over arbitrary partial orders.

for all
x,y\in S
and
\delta\in\Delta
:

(H1)

x|_{\delta}=y|_{\delta}
if and only if
g(x)|_{\delta}=g(y)|_{\delta}
;

(H2)

the map
i\mapsto g(x^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)
is a permutation of
N_{\delta}
belonging to
H_{\delta}
.

Condition
(H1)
implies that
(H2)
is independent of the choice of the elements
x^{\delta}_{i}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Condition
(H1)
already yields that the map
i\mapsto g(x_{i}^{\delta})(\delta)
considered in condition
(H2)
is a permutation of
N_{\delta}
.
Therefore, if each
H_{\delta}
is the full permutation group
\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
, as it will be often the case in this paper, condition
(H2)
can be dropped.

In most cases, the domain
S
of the generalized wreath product is determined through a family
\mathscr{A}
of admissible supports.
More in detail,
let
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\Delta)
be a nonempty family of sets such that if
A\in\mathscr{A}
and
A^{\prime}
has finite symmetric difference from
A
, then
A^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
as well.
Then

S^{\mathscr{A}}=\left\{x\in\prod\nolimits_{\delta\in\Delta}N_{\delta}\,:\,\operatorname{supp}(x)\in\mathscr{A}\right\}

is a domain;
indeed, the condition on
\mathscr{A}
ensures that for every
x\in S^{\mathscr{A}}
,
\delta\in\Delta
, and
i\in N_{\delta}
, there is a canonical choice for
x^{\delta}_{i}
, namely, the one obtained by setting
x^{\delta}_{i}(\gamma)=x(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\neq\delta
(as in Malicki’s
[
Mal14
]
).
Thus we can form the wreath product
\operatorname{Wr}^{S^{\mathscr{A}}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
, that for the sake of simplicity will also be denoted by
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathscr{A}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
.

Hall’s generalized wreath product from
[
Hal62
]
corresponds to the case where
\Delta
is linearly ordered and
S=S^{\operatorname{Fin}}
, where
\operatorname{Fin}
is the collection of all finite subsets of
\Delta
.
Notice that for an arbitrary
\mathscr{A}
as above, if
\emptyset\in\mathscr{A}
then
\operatorname{Fin}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
, so that Hall’s choice for
\mathscr{A}
is somewhat minimal.

Holland
[
Hol69
]
considers instead the case of an arbitrary partial order
\Delta
with
N_{\delta}>1
for all
\delta\in\Delta
, and
S
the set of all
x\in\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}N_{\delta}
satisfying the
maximum condition
, that is,
S=S^{\operatorname{Max}}
for
\operatorname{Max}
the collection of all
A\subseteq\Delta
such that every nonempty subset of
A
has a maximal element or, equivalently, such that all strictly increasing chains in
A
are finite.

Finally, in
[
Mal14
]
Malicki introduced a further variant of Holland’s construction by restricting the attention to supports
A\in\operatorname{Max}
all of whose infinite descending chains have no lower bound in
\Delta
.
Since this variant was specifically introduced to study the isometry groups of
W
-spaces, we will denote by
\operatorname{Wsp}
the collection of all supports
A\subseteq\Delta
with such property, and say that
x\in\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}N_{\delta}
satisfies the
Malicki’s condition
if
\operatorname{supp}(x)\in\operatorname{Wsp}
.

Remark 5.3
.

In
[
Mal14
]
, condition
(H1)
is weakened by considering only the forward implication. This is justified by the fact that, by definition, all elements
x\in S^{\operatorname{Wsp}}
in the domain chosen by Malicki satisfy Holland’s maximum condition. Together with
(H2)
, this entails that the forward implication in condition
(H1)
can be automatically reversed.
Indeed, suppose that
x|_{\delta}\neq y|_{\delta}
, and consider the set
A=\{\gamma\geq\delta\,:\,x(\gamma)\neq y(\gamma)\}
. Then
A
has a maximal element
\bar{\gamma}
: if not, from any infinite strictly increasing sequence in
A
one could extract an infinite subsequence contained in either
\operatorname{supp}(x)
or
\operatorname{supp}(y)
. By choice of
\bar{\gamma}
,
y
is of the form
x^{\bar{\gamma}}_{i}
for
i=y(\bar{\gamma})\neq x(\bar{\gamma})
. Therefore by condition
(H2)
we have
g(y)(\bar{\gamma})\neq g(x)(\bar{\gamma})
. Since
\bar{\gamma}\geq\delta
, this means that
g(x)|_{\delta}\neq g(y)|_{\delta}
, as desired.
Although we preferred to follow Holland’s original formulation, in all definitions below we could instead follow Malicki, as the two approaches remain equivalent whenever all elements in the chosen domain satisfy the maximum condition, which will always be the case in this paper.

Yet another variant is obtained by considering the collection
\operatorname{LF}
of locally finite supports, that is:
A\in\operatorname{LF}
if and only if
A\cap\{\gamma\in\Delta\,:\,\gamma\geq\delta\}\in\operatorname{Fin}
for all
\delta\in\Delta
.
Clearly,
\operatorname{Fin}\subseteq\operatorname{LF}\subseteq\operatorname{Wsp}\subseteq\operatorname{Max}
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{Wsp}=\operatorname{LF}
whenever
\Delta
has no infinite antichain with a lower bound: this happens in particular when
\Delta
is an
L
-tree, which is the case we consider in our main results.

Notice that if
\Delta=\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
with
\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}
, so that necessarily
\mathscr{A}=\operatorname{Fin}=\mathscr{P}(\Delta)
, then
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathscr{A}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
is the usual wreath product
of the two groups
H_{\delta_{0}}
and
H_{\delta_{1}}
.
On the other hand, if
\Delta
is an antichain and
\mathscr{A}=\operatorname{LF}=\mathscr{P}(\Delta)
,
then
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathscr{A}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
is the direct product
\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
.

Since the groups
H_{\delta}
are transitive, whenever
\mathscr{A}

Definition 5.4

A domain

There is a natural way to turn each generalized wreath product

Topology

Unless otherwise stated, we endow

Notice that when

Suppose that the skeleton

5.2.
Generalized wreath products over local domains

Recall from (
5.1

\delta\in\Delta

S_{\delta}=\{x|_{\delta}\,:\,x\in S\}
.
By condition

(and hence

8
8

8

If furthermore all the groups

is, up to isomorphism, a closed subgroup of

Nothing in the description above depends on the fact that the sets

\langle\Delta,N\rangle
.
Given any

Definition 5.6
.

A

(a)

;

(b)

\{z^{\delta}_{i}\,:\,i\in N_{\delta}\}\subseteq\mathbf{S}_{\delta}

, where
z^{\delta}_{i}(\delta)=i
and
z^{\delta}_{i}(\gamma)=z(\gamma)
if
\gamma>\delta
.

Notice that the family of local domains

is countable exactly when the skeleton

\delta\in\Delta
.

Let

As discussed above, the classical generalized wreath products

More precisely, say that the family

5.3.
Projective wreath products

By replacing in Definition

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
be a skeleton.
A
system of projections
(over the skeleton
\langle\Delta,N\rangle

\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\mathbf{S}\subseteq\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta}\left(\prod_{\gamma\geq\delta}N_{\gamma}\right)

z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and
\beta\geq\gamma\geq\delta

(a)

\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)=\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z^{\prime})
if and only if
z|_{\gamma}=z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}
;

(b)

\pi_{\gamma\beta}\circ\pi_{\delta\gamma}=\pi_{\delta\beta}

\delta\in\Delta

\mathbf{S}_{\delta}

\mathbf{S}
.

H_{\delta}
are closed.

It is clear that the generalized wreath products
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
from Section
5.2
are precisely the projective wreath products in which the family of projections
\boldsymbol{\pi}
is trivial.
Conversely, we are now going to show that under the mild assumption
\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
every projective generalized wreath product can be realized as a generalized wreath product over some family of local domains,
up to isomorphism
.
We say that a system of projections
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
has
finite character
if for every
\delta\in\Delta
, the set
\{\gamma\in\Delta\,:\,\gamma\geq\delta\}
can be partitioned into finitely many convex sets
C_{0},\dotsc,C_{n}
such that for all
i\leq n
and all
\beta\geq\gamma
with
\beta,\gamma\in C_{i}
, the projection
\pi_{\gamma\beta}
is trivial (i.e.
\pi_{\gamma\beta}(z)=z|_{\beta}
for every
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\gamma}
).

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
be a skeleton, and let
(H_{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}
be a family of transitive permutation groups over the sets
N_{\delta}
. Let
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
be a system of projections such that
\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
.
Then there is family of local domains
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
such that

\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}\,\cong\,\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}.

If moreover
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
has finite character, then we can further require
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
.

Proof.

For every
\delta\in\Delta
and
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
, let
\rho(z)\in\prod_{\gamma\geq\delta}N_{\delta}
be defined by setting
\rho(z)(\gamma)=\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)(\gamma)
, for every
\gamma\geq\delta
.
The “rewriting map”
\rho\colon\mathbf{S}\to\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta}\big(\prod_{\gamma\geq\delta}N_{\delta}\big)
has the following properties.

Claim 5.12.1
.

For every
\gamma\geq\delta
,
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
, and
z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbf{S}_{\gamma}
,

(i)

z|_{\gamma}=z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}\iff\rho(z)|_{\gamma}=\rho(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma}
,

(ii)

\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)=z^{\prime\prime}\iff\rho(z)|_{\gamma}=\rho(z^{\prime\prime})
,

(iii)

\rho(z)(\delta)=z(\delta)
.

Proof of the claim.

(i)

Suppose first that
z|_{\gamma}=z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}
. Then for every
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma
we have
z|_{\gamma^{\prime}}=z^{\prime}|_{\gamma^{\prime}}
, and hence
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})
by condition
(a)
of Definition
5.10
.
Therefore
\rho(z)(\gamma^{\prime})=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)(\gamma^{\prime})=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})=\rho(z^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})
for every
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma
, that is,
\rho(z)|_{\gamma}=\rho(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma}
.

Now suppose that
z|_{\gamma}\neq z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}
. By the maximum condition, we can find a maximal
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma
such that
z|_{\gamma^{\prime}}\neq z^{\prime}|_{\gamma^{\prime}}
, so that
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)\neq\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})
by Definition
5.10
(a)
. Fix any
\gamma^{\prime\prime}>\gamma^{\prime}
. By maximality of
\gamma^{\prime}
,
z|_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}=z^{\prime}|_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}
, and hence
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(z)=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(z^{\prime})
.
On the other hand, by condition
(b)
of Definition
5.10
we have
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(z)=\pi_{\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z))
and
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(z^{\prime})=\pi_{\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime}))
, hence
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)|_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}
for every
\gamma^{\prime\prime}>\gamma^{\prime}
by Definition
5.10
(a)
again. Since
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)\neq\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})
, necessarily
\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z)(\gamma^{\prime})\neq\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})
, hence
\rho(z)(\gamma^{\prime})\neq\rho(z^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})
, and thus
\rho(z)|_{\gamma}\neq\rho(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma}
.

(ii)

Using the surjectivity of
\pi_{\delta\gamma}
, pick
z^{*}\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
such that
\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z^{*})=z^{\prime\prime}
.
Then by Definition
5.10
(a)
and
(i)
we have

\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)=z^{\prime\prime}\iff z|_{\gamma}=z^{*}|_{\gamma}\iff\rho(z)|_{\gamma}=\rho(z^{*})|_{\gamma},

hence it is enough to show that
\rho(z^{*})|_{\gamma}=\rho(z^{\prime\prime})
. But this follows from the fact that, by Definition
5.10
(b)
and the choice of
z^{*}
, for every
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma

\rho(z^{*})(\gamma^{\prime})=\pi_{\delta\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{*})(\gamma^{\prime})=\pi_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z^{*}))(\gamma^{\prime})=\pi_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(z^{\prime\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})=\rho(z^{\prime\prime})(\gamma^{\prime}).

(iii)

Recall that
\pi_{\delta\delta}(z)=z
. Thus,
\rho(z)(\delta)=\pi_{\delta\delta}(z)(\delta)=z(\delta)
.
∎

Set
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}=\rho(\mathbf{S}_{\delta})
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}
.
By Claim
5.12.1
(i)
,
\rho\restriction\mathbf{S}_{\delta}\colon\mathbf{S}_{\delta}\to\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}
is injective, and hence a bijection.
If moreover
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
has finite character, then
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
for every
\delta\in\Delta
.
Indeed, suppose towards a contradiction that there are
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and an infinite sequence
\delta<\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\dotsc
such that
\rho(z)(\delta_{j})\neq 0
for all
j\in\omega
. Let
C_{0},\dotsc,C_{n}
be a finite partition of
\{\gamma\in\Delta\,:\,\gamma\geq\delta\}
witnessing the finite character of
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
with respect to
\delta
. By convexity, we can assume that there is
\bar{\imath}\leq n
such that
\delta_{j}\in C_{\bar{\imath}}
for all
j\in\omega
. Let
z^{\prime}=\pi_{\delta\delta_{0}}(z)\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta_{0}}
. By choice of
C_{\bar{\imath}}
,

z^{\prime}(\delta_{j})=(z^{\prime}|_{\delta_{j}})(\delta_{j})=\pi_{\delta_{0}\delta j}(z^{\prime})(\delta_{j})=\pi_{\delta\delta_{j}}(z)(\delta_{j})=\rho(z)(\delta_{j})\neq 0

for all
j\in\omega
, contradicting
\mathbf{S}_{\delta_{0}}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
.

The surjectivity of
\pi_{\delta\gamma}
entails that for every
\gamma\geq\delta
we have
\mathbf{S}_{\gamma}=\{\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)\,:\,z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}\}
, hence by Claim
5.12.1
(ii)
we have that
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
satisfies condition
(a)
of Definition
5.6
, i.e.
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\gamma}=\{z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}\,:\,z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}\}
.
Condition
(b)
of the same definition easily follows from items
(i)
and
(iii)
in Claim
5.12.1
, together with the fact that
\mathbf{S}
satisfies the first item of Definition
5.10
. Thus
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
is a family of local domains.

Given
g\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbf{S})
we let
g^{\prime}=\rho\circ g\circ\rho^{-1}
. Then
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbf{S}^{\prime})
because
\rho\colon\mathbf{S}\to\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
is a bijection, and the map
g\mapsto g^{\prime}
is an isomorphism between
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbf{S})
and
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbf{S}^{\prime})
(where both groups are endowed with the pointwise convergence topology with respect to the discrete topology on
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
, respectively). It remains to show that its restriction to
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
is onto
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
.

By Claim
5.12.1
(ii)
, for every
\gamma\geq\delta
and
w\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}

(5.2)

\rho(w)|_{\gamma}=\rho(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(w)).

Also, if
w^{\prime}=\rho(w)
then by definition of
g^{\prime}

(5.3)

g^{\prime}(w^{\prime})=g^{\prime}(\rho(w))=\rho(g(w)).

As
\rho\restriction\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
is a bijection between
\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}
, it follows that
g(\mathbf{S}_{\delta})=\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
if and only if
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta})=\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\delta}
.
Moreover, by equations (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have that for every
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and
z^{\prime}=\rho(z)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z^{\prime}|_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(\rho(z)|_{\gamma})=g^{\prime}(\rho(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)))=\rho(g(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)))\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\rho(g(z))|_{\gamma}=\rho(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(g(z))).

Since
\rho
is injective, it follows that

g^{\prime}(z^{\prime}|_{\gamma})=g^{\prime}(z^{\prime})|_{\gamma}\iff\rho(g(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)))=\rho(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(g(z)))\iff g(\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z))=\pi_{\delta\gamma}(g(z)).

Therefore
g
satisfies
(P1)
if and only if
g^{\prime}
satisfies
(G1)
.

By items
(i)
and
(iii)
of Claim
5.12.1
we further have
\rho(z^{\delta}_{i})=\rho(z)^{\delta}_{i}
for every
i\in N_{\delta}
. Therefore, using also (
5.3
)

g^{\prime}(\rho(z)^{\delta}_{i})=g^{\prime}(\rho(z^{\delta}_{i}))=\rho(g(z^{\delta}_{i})),

hence using again Claim
5.12.1
(iii)
we get

g^{\prime}(\rho(z)^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)=\rho(g(z^{\delta}_{i}))(\delta)=g(z^{\delta}_{i})(\delta).

This means that the bijections
i\mapsto g(z^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)
and
i\mapsto g^{\prime}((z^{\prime})^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)
are identical, hence
g
satisfies
(P2)
if and only if
g^{\prime}
satisfies
(G2)
. This concludes our proof.
∎

An important instance where Theorem
5.12
applies consists of projective generalized wreath products of the form
\operatorname{Wr}^{\operatorname{LF},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
because
\operatorname{LF}\subseteq\operatorname{Max}
implies
\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{LF}}\subseteq\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Max}}
.

Remark 5.13
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
5.12
that
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
is locally homogeneous if and only if so is
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
.

5.4.
Some useful lemmas

The following lemma implies that condition
(P2)
in Definition
5.11
can be dropped if
H_{\delta}=\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
for all
\delta\in\Delta
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
\langle\Delta,N\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}\rangle
be as in Definition
5.11
. Then for every
\delta\in\Delta
,
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
, and
g\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbf{S})
satisfying condition
(P1)
, the map
i\mapsto g(z^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)
is a permutation of
N_{\delta}
.

Proof.

For any
i,j\in N_{\delta}
and
\gamma>\delta
, we have
z^{\delta}_{i}|_{\gamma}=z^{\delta}_{j}|_{\gamma}
, and therefore
\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z^{\delta}_{i})=\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z^{\delta}_{j})
by Definition
5.10
(a)
. By
(P1)
, it follows that
\pi_{\delta\gamma}(g(z^{\delta}_{i}))=\pi_{\delta\gamma}(g(z^{\delta}_{j}))
, hence
g(z^{\delta}_{i})|_{\gamma}=g(z^{\delta}_{j})|_{\gamma}
by Definition
5.10
(a)
again. On the other hand, by injectivity of
g
we have that if
i\neq j
then
g(z^{\delta}_{i})\neq g(z^{\delta}_{j})
, which combined with the discussion above yields
g(z^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)\neq g(z^{\delta}_{j})(\delta)
. This proves that the map
i\mapsto g(z^{\delta}_{i})(\delta)
is injective.

To prove surjectivity, given any
j\in N_{\delta}
let
z^{\prime}=g(z)^{\delta}_{j}
and, using surjectivity of
g
, set
z^{\prime\prime}=g^{-1}(z^{\prime})
: we need to show that
z^{\prime\prime}=z^{\delta}_{i}
for some
i\in N_{\delta}
. If this was not the case,
z^{\prime\prime}|_{\gamma}\neq z|_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma>\delta
.
Arguing as above, we then would get
z^{\prime}|_{\gamma}=g(z^{\prime\prime})|_{\gamma}\neq g(z)|_{\gamma}
, contradicting the choice of
z^{\prime}
.
∎

It follows that when each
H_{\delta}
is the full permutation group
\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
, we can conveniently reformulate
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
in terms of certain automorphisms of a partial order
P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
canonically associated to it.
More in detail, let
P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be obtained by endowing
\mathbf{S}
with the ordering
\preceq
defined as follows: if
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and
z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}_{\gamma}
, then
z\preceq z^{\prime}
if and only if
\delta\leq\gamma
and
\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)=z^{\prime}
.
In particular, if
\boldsymbol{\pi}
is trivial then
z\preceq z^{\prime}\iff z\supseteq z^{\prime}
for every
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}
.
We refer to
P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}=(\mathbf{S},{\preceq})
as the
canonical partial order
associated to
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
.
When
\mathbf{S}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}
are clear from the context, we might drop them from the notation and simply write
P
instead of
P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
, in which case we also conflate
P
with its domain and denote its order by
\leq_{P}
instead of
\preceq
.
We say that an automorphism
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
of
P=P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is
localized
if
f(\mathbf{S}_{\delta})=\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
for every
\delta\in\Delta
.
The subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
consisting of all localized automorphisms will be denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
.
Notice that since
P=P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is a countable partial order when
\mathbf{S}
is countable,
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
can be construed as a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)
, and
\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
becomes a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)
.
By Lemma
5.14
, we obviously get
\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)=\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}\operatorname{Sym}(N_{\delta})
.

To prove our main results, we need to connect generalized wreath products with automorphism groups of
L
-trees.
Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
be a countable pruned
L
-tree.
Recall that since
\mathcal{T}
can be viewed as a first-order structure with domain
\omega
, its automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
can be construed as a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Sym}(\omega)
, from which it inherits the usual product topology.
Consider the condensed tree
\Delta(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
.
We define a labeling
N_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\Delta(\mathcal{T})\to\omega\cup\{\omega\}\colon\delta\mapsto N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}
as follows: for
\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})
, fix any
t\in\delta
and set
N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}=|C_{t}|
,
where

(5.4)

C_{t}=\{t\}\cup\{t^{\prime}\sim t\,:\,\mathrm{split}(t,t^{\prime})=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)\}.

It is clear that the definition of
N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}
does not depend on the choice of
t\in\delta
.

In the next sections, we are going to show that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\cong\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})}\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta})
, under various assumptions on
\mathcal{T}
and for the appropriate choices of
\mathbf{S}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}
. To this aim, we will often
employ the following lemma, which provides a sufficient condition to obtain
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\cong\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
for
P
the canonical partial order associated to
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})}\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta})
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
be a countable pruned
L
-tree, and let
P=P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be the canonical partial order associated to the projective wreath product
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})}\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta})
.
Let

g\colon T\to P
be an order-isomorphism
such that
g(t)\in\mathbf{S}_{[t]}
for every
t\in T
.
Then the map
\varphi\mapsto f_{\varphi}
defined by setting
f_{\varphi}=g\circ\varphi\circ g^{-1}
witnesses
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\cong\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\cong\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})}\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.
It is clear that
f_{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Aut}(P)
.
Given any
z\in P
, let
\delta\in\Delta(\mathcal{T})
be such that
z\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
and
t=g^{-1}(z)
, so that
[t]=\delta
by choice of
g
.
Since
t\sim\varphi(t)
, we get that
[\varphi(t)]=[t]=\delta
, and hence
f_{\varphi}(z)\in\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
by choice of
g
again.
Since
z\in P
was arbitrary, this yields
f_{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
.
It easily follows that
\varphi\mapsto f_{\varphi}
is a topological group embedding of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
into
\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
: we claim that it is also onto.

Given
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
, let
\varphi_{f}=g^{-1}\circ f\circ g
, so that
f_{\varphi_{f}}=f
: we only need to show that
\varphi_{f}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
. Clearly
\varphi_{f}\colon T\to T
is bijective, and it satisfies
t\leq_{T}t^{\prime}\iff\varphi_{f}(t)\leq_{T}\varphi_{f}(t^{\prime})
for all
t,t^{\prime}\in T
because
g
is order-preserving.
Fix any
t\in T
.
By definition of
\varphi_{f}
, we have
g(\varphi_{f}(t))=f(g(t))
.
By
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{*}(P)
and choice of
g
, we must have
[t]=[\varphi_{f}(t)]
, so that
t\sim\varphi_{f}(t)
and, in particular,
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(\varphi_{f}(t))
.
This concludes the proof that
\varphi_{f}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.
∎

Conversely, the following lemmas provide a sufficient condition for turning a projective wreath product into the automorphism group of some
L
-tree.
A skeleton

is said to be

if there is a linear order

such that

.

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
P=P^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be the canonical partial order associated to a projective wreath product of the form

, for some countable skeleton

and some system of projections

.

such that

is constant on each

.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{\lambda}_{\Delta}\colon\Delta\to L

-tree, and let
\operatorname{\lambda}_{P}(z)=\operatorname{\lambda}_{\Delta}(\delta)

, so that by construction

whenever
z
and
z^{\prime}
belong to the same
\mathbf{S}_{\delta}
. Items
(1)
and
(2)
of Definition

are obviously satisfied, because they are satisfied by the

and

, the unique

is
\pi_{\delta\gamma}(z)
, for

.

To check that
(3)
is satisfied, pick any

, and let

be such that

and

.
Since
(\Delta,{\leq_{\Delta}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{\Delta})
is an
L
-tree, there is

and

.
Replacing
z
and

with

, respectively, we can assume without loss of generality that

. This means that

, and therefore

(a)
in the second item of Definition
5.10
.

Next we check that also item
(4)
of Definition
3.1
is satisfied. Consider two

-incomparable

and

.

Suppose first that
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
are
\leq_{\Delta}
-comparable, say
\delta\leq_{\Delta}\delta^{\prime}
for the sake of definiteness. Replacing

6.1.
Homogeneous spaces

Informally speaking, a mathematical structure is (one-point) homogeneous if for all points
x,y
in the structure, if the map sending
x
to
y
is a partial automorphism, then there is an automorphism of the whole structure which sends
x
to
y
.
In the metric context, this translates to the following:
a Polish ultrametric space

is
homogeneous
if
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
acts transitively on
U
, that is, for every
x,y\in U
there is
\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)
such that
\psi(x)=y
.
Moving to the realm of weak
L
-trees
\mathcal{T}
, instead, we have that
\mathcal{T}
is
homogeneous
if
\Delta(\mathcal{T})
is linear, i.e., for every
t,t^{\prime}\in T
with
\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t)=\operatorname{\lambda}_{T}(t^{\prime})
there is
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})
such that
f(t)=t^{\prime}
.
When
L
is countable, this is equivalent to requiring a homogeneity property of the body of
\mathcal{T}
, as shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that

(2)

Proof.

(1)

\Rightarrow

(2)

\Rightarrow

(1)
.
Obvious.

Another class of interest in the context of homogeneous spaces is given by Polish ultrametric Urysohn spaces.

In order to characterize their isometry groups

We provide two characterizations in terms of generalized wreath products: to our taste, the most appealing is obtained by considering only maximal countable linear skeletons, and then taking Hall’s generalized wreath products together with generalized wreath products induced by locally finite supports over such skeletons. Along the way, we also consider a related class of spaces. A metric space is

, where

, if all its points are at distance

from each other. A Polish ultrametric space

is

if for every

in its distance set,

contains a copy of the countably infinite

6.2.
Exact spaces

If a Polish ultrametric space
U
is not homogeneous, it makes sense to consider its homogeneous components.
These are the equivalence classes induced by the natural action of
\operatorname{Iso}(U)
on
U
: the
homogeneous component

[x]
of
x\in U
is the set
\{y\in U\,:\,\varphi(x)=y\text{ for some }\varphi\in\operatorname{Iso}(U)\}
.

Definition 6.9
.

Two homogeneous components
[x]
and
[y]
of a Polish ultrametric space have
exact distance
if their distance is realized by points, i.e., if the infimum in
d([x],[y])=\inf\{d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,:\,x^{\prime}\in[x],y^{\prime}\in[y]\}
is attained.
A Polish ultrametric space is
exact
if every two homogeneous components of the space have exact distance.

Not all Polish ultrametric spaces are exact.

As Lemma
6.11
shows, the counterpart of exactness for an
L
-tree
\mathcal{T}=(T,{\leq_{T}},\operatorname{\lambda}_{T})
is the following:

(
\star
)

For every proper upward closed chain
C\subseteq T
and every
t\in T
such that
[t]\leq_{\Delta(T)}[C]
there is
\bar{t}\in[t]
such that
\bar{t}\leq_{T}C
.

Remark 6.10
.

Property
(

⋆

)
‣
6.2
needs to be checked only when
C
has no minimum; indeed if
C
has a minimum
t^{\prime}
then, since
[t]\leq_{\Delta(T)}[t^{\prime}]
by hypothesis, there is
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(T)
such that
\bar{t}=f(t)\leq_{T}t^{\prime}\leq_{T}C
.

.

As a by-product of Theorem

, every collection of systems of projections between those employed in items

and

gives rise to the same class of Polish groups, and thus characterizes isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces.

7.
Additional results and open problems

The functors
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{G}
from Sections
4.2
and
4.3
, respectively, can be construed as Borel functions when restricted to the standard Borel spaces of (codes for) Polish ultrametric spaces in
\mathbb{U}_{D}
or countable pruned
L
-trees in
\mathbb{T}_{L}
.
Moreover, categorical full embeddings are also reductions between the corresponding isomorphism and embeddability relations; for example, if
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
are countable pruned
L
-trees, then
\mathcal{T}
embeds into
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
if and only if there is an isometric embedding of
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})
into
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
, and
\mathcal{T}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T})
and
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
are isometric.
Finally, categorical full embeddings preserve the automorphism groups of the objects (algebraically); for example,
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{T})\simeq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{T}))
.
It then follows that Theorems
4.5
and
4.10
can be used to reprove the main results of Sections 5.1 and 6.1 of
[
CMMR18
]
.
(Here we use that rooted combinatorial trees without terminal nodes can be identified with pruned
\omega^{*}
-trees in our sense, and that
2\cdot\omega^{*}\cong\omega^{*}
embeds into any ill-founded set of distances
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
.)

The same comments apply to the functor
\mathscr{U}
from Section
4.1
. This yields the following Borel-reducibility results, which escaped the analysis in
[
CMMR18
]
— we refer the reader to that paper for more details on the concepts and the background involved.

Theorem 7.1
.

(1)

The relation of isometry on the class of perfect locally compact Polish ultrametric spaces is Borel bi-reducible with graph isomorphism, and thus is
S_{\infty}
-complete.

(2)

The relation of isometric embeddability on the class of perfect locally compact Polish ultrametric spaces is invariantly universal, and hence complete for analytic quasi-orders.

The characterizations obtained in this paper might be helpful in tackling some natural open problems in the area. For example, Gao and Kechris showed that if the Polish ultrametric space
X
is Heine-Borel, then
\operatorname{Iso}(X)
is the closure of an increasing union of compact subgroups, and hence it is amenable (
[
GK03
, Theorem 8.9]
). This raises the question of which other isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces are amenable.
Similar questions might be asked for interesting group properties other than amenability, such as being oligomorphic, or having uncountable strong cofinality. Concerning the latter, for example, one might attempt to generalize Malicki’s characterization
[
Mal14
, Theorem 5.3]
, which crucially uses generalized wreath products, from
W
-spaces to arbitrary Polish ultrametric spaces.

In a somewhat different direction, a natural prolongation of the present work is to seek for characterizations of isometry groups of Polish ultrametric spaces belonging to some natural subclasses, such as compact spaces, proper spaces, Heine-Borel spaces, and so on.

On the group-theoretic side, it would be desirable to elaborate more on the various classes of generalized wreath products employed in our characterizations. For example, it is unclear whether the four classes identified by the columns of the table in Appendix
A
are distinct from each other; by Proposition
5.9
and Theorem
5.12
, any generalized wreath product used to distinguish the various situations need to have an infinite skeleton. One might also wonder whether generalized wreath products over families of local domains
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
and projective wreath products
\operatorname{Wr}^{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
enjoy natural universality properties analogous to those uncovered by Holland and Malicki for the groups of the form
\operatorname{Wr}^{\operatorname{Max}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
and
\operatorname{Wr}^{\operatorname{Wsp}}_{\delta\in\Delta}H_{\delta}
, respectively. We expect a positive answer for both problems, but even negative answers would have interesting consequences concerning the study of Polish ultrametric spaces.

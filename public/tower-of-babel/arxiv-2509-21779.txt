Abstract
Motivated by systematic observations of B c B_{c} decays at LHCb, we analyze B c + → J / ψ ⁡ ( A , T ) B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi(A,T) decays (where A A denotes axial-vector mesons a 1 ​ ( 1260 ) + a_{1}(1260)^{+} , b 1 ​ ( 1235 ) + b_{1}(1235)^{+} , and T T denotes tensor mesons a 2 ​ ( 1320 ) + a_{2}(1320)^{+} , K 2 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) + K_{2}^{*}(1430)^{+} ) within the perturbative QCD (pQCD) factorization framework. Nonperturbative dynamics are regulated by light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs), with endpoint divergences controlled via Sudakov suppression. Predictions yield branching ratios ranging from 10 − 3 10^{-3} to 10 − 2 10^{-2} for B c + → J / ψ ​ A B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi A and at the level of 10 − 6 10^{-6} for B c + → J / ψ ​ T B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi T , which seem to be in the reach of future experiments. The Theoretical Framework and Helicity Amplitudes

II.1
The Wave Functions

The effective Hamiltonian of the
B_{c}
non-leptonic decays into charmonium and axial-vector mesons or tensor mesons is given by
Buchalla:1996

\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}^{*}V_{uq}\left[C_{1}(\mu)O_{1}(\mu)+C_{2}(\mu)O_{2}(\mu)\right],

(1)

where
q\in\{s,d\}
denotes a light down-type quark.
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant, and
V_{cb}^{*}
,
V_{uq}
represent CKM matrix elements. The Wilson coefficients
C_{1,2}(\mu)
(encoding short-distance effects above scale
\mu
) and their corresponding four-quark operators
O_{1,2}(\mu)
are defined as:

\begin{split}&O_{1}(\mu)=\bar{b}_{\alpha}\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})c_{\beta}\otimes\bar{u}_{\beta}\gamma_{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})q_{\alpha},\\
&O_{2}(\mu)=\bar{b}_{\alpha}\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})c_{\alpha}\otimes\bar{u}_{\beta}\gamma_{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})q_{\beta}.\end{split}

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are color indices (summation over repeated indices implied). Since the Hamiltonian involves four distinct quark flavors, these decays are free from penguin operator contamination, hence direct CP violation is naturally absent.

The calculation is performed in the
B_{c}
meson rest frame. The momenta are defined in light-cone coordinates as follows:
{P_{1}}
for the
B_{c}
meson,
{P_{2}}
for the recoiling charmonium, and
{P_{3}}
for the emitted light meson.

\begin{split}&\emph{P}_{1}=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1,\mathbf{0}_{T}),\\
&\emph{P}_{2}=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1-r_{3}^{2},r_{2}^{2},\mathbf{0}_{T}\right),\\
&\emph{P}_{3}=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2}}\left(r_{3}^{2},1-r_{2}^{2},\mathbf{0}_{T}\right).\end{split}

(2)

The mass ratio
r_{2,3}
is defined as
m_{2,3}/M
, where
M
(
m_{2}
) denotes the mass of the
B_{c}
meson (
J/\psi
meson), while
m_{3}
represents the mass of the axial-vector (tensor) meson. The momentum of the valence quarks
k_{1,2,3}
is represented by the parameterization:

\begin{split}&k_{1}=x_{1}P_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{1T},\\
&k_{2}=x_{2}P_{2}+\mathbf{k}_{2T},\\
&k_{3}=x_{3}P_{3}+\mathbf{k}_{3T},\end{split}

(3)

where parameters
k_{iT}
and
x_{i}
are defined as the transverse momentum and longitudinal momentum fraction, respectively, of the quark/antiquark confined within the meson. When the final state includes a vector charmonium in conjunction with an axial-vector meson, the formalism employs the longitudinal polarization vector
\epsilon_{L}
and the transverse polarization vector
\epsilon_{T}
, which serve to describe the polarization states as follows:

\begin{split}&\epsilon_{2L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1-r_{3}^{2})}}r_{2}\left(1-r_{3}^{2},-r_{2}^{2},\mathbf{0}_{T}\right),\quad\epsilon_{2T}=\left(0,0,\mathbf{1}_{T}\right);\\
&\epsilon_{3L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1-r_{2}^{2})}}r_{3}\left(-r_{3}^{2},1-r_{2}^{2},\mathbf{0}_{T}\right),\quad\epsilon_{3T}=\left(0,0,\mathbf{1}_{T}\right).\end{split}

(4)

The polarization vectors satisfy the normalization conditions
\epsilon_{2L}^{2}=\epsilon_{2T}^{2}=-1
and the orthogonality relations
\epsilon_{2L}\cdot P_{2}=\epsilon_{3L}\cdot P_{3}=0
.

For the spin-2 polarization tensor
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\lambda)
satisfying the transverse condition
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}p_{2}^{\nu}=0

;
Cheng2011

\begin{split}&\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\pm 2)=\epsilon^{\mu}(\pm 1)\epsilon^{\nu}(\pm 1),\\
&\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\pm 1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\epsilon^{\mu}(\pm 1)\epsilon^{\nu}(0)+\epsilon^{\mu}(0)\epsilon^{\nu}(\pm 1)\right),\\
&\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\epsilon^{\mu}(+1)\epsilon^{\nu}(-1)+\epsilon^{\mu}(-1)\epsilon^{\nu}(+1)\right)+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\epsilon^{\mu}(0)\epsilon^{\nu}(0).\end{split}

(5)

where

IV
Summary

In this article, we systematically investigate the exclusive two-body decays of the
B_{c}
meson to an
S
-wave charmonium state (
J/\psi
) and either axial-vector mesons (
a_{1}(1260)
,
b_{1}(1235)
) or tensor mesons (
a_{2}(1230)
,
K_{2}^{*}(1430)
) within the perturbative QCD factorization framework, incorporating the nonperturbative distribution amplitudes of the final-state mesons to ensure infrared safety. Since flavor non-degeneracy in four-quark operators forbids penguin diagrams, weak phase differences vanish, eliminating CP violation in these decays. We calculate the branching ratios and polarization fractions. Our results indicate that: (1) The branching ratio for
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi A
is at the order
10^{-3}\sim 10^{-2}
, while for
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi T
it is at the order
10^{-6}
. This disparity arises from CKM factor enhancement in the former and suppression in the latter due to phase-space suppression from higher orbital angular momentum (
L=1
). (2) In
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi A
decays, the branching ratio of
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi b_{1}^{+}
exceeds that of
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi a_{1}^{+}
. This is attributed to the distinct quantum states:
b_{1}(1235)
(
J^{PC}=1^{+-}
,
{}^{1}P_{1}
) exhibits symmetric distribution amplitude
\phi_{b_{1}}(x)\propto\text{constant}
, enhancing hadronic matrix elements, whereas
a_{1}(1260)
(
J^{PC}=1^{++}
,
{}^{3}P_{1}
) has endpoint-suppressed wave function
\phi_{a_{1}}(x)\propto x(1-x)
. (3) Three polarization patterns consistently emerge across decay channels, reflecting universal longitudinal polarization dominance (
f_{0}\gg f_{\parallel}\sim f_{\perp}
). The longitudinal polarization fraction
f_{0}
for
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi T
exceeds that for
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi A
due to a systematic enhancement originating from the
J^{\mathrm{PC}}=2^{++}
quantum numbers of tensor mesons, where higher orbital angular momentum (
L=1
) amplifies endpoint contributions in light-cone distribution amplitudes. (4) For
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi A
decays, the longitudinal polarization fraction of
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi b_{1}^{+}
is larger than that of
B_{c}^{+}\to J/\psi a_{1}^{+}
. This difference stems from their distinct G-parities (
G_{a_{1}}=-1
,
G_{b_{1}}=+1
), which govern asymptotic behaviors of distribution amplitudes:
\phi_{a_{1}}(x)
is endpoint-suppressed, while
\phi_{b_{1}}(x)
is flat, increasing transverse component dominance. The predictions are subject to experimental scrutiny in upcoming collider measurements.

h_{2}(\beta,b_{1},b_{2})&=\begin{cases}\theta(b_{1}-b_{2})I_{0}(\sqrt{\beta}b_{2})K_{0}(\sqrt{\beta}b_{1})+(b_{1}\leftrightarrow b_{2}),&\beta\textgreater 0,\\
\theta(b_{1}-b_{2})J_{0}(\sqrt{-\beta}b_{2})K_{0}(i\sqrt{-\beta}b_{1})+(b_{1}\leftrightarrow b_{2}),&\beta\textless 0,\end{cases}\end{split}

(A.8)

With

K_{0}(ix)=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(-N_{0}(x)+iJ_{0}(x)\right)
. The expressions for

in the hard functions are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{e}&=\left[x_{1}+r_{J/\psi}^{2}\left(x_{2}-1\right)\right]\left[x_{1}+x_{2}-1+r_{A}^{2}\left(1-x_{2}\right)\right]M_{B}^{2},\\
\beta_{a}&=\left[r_{b}^{2}+\left(1+r_{J/\psi}^{2}\left(x_{2}-1\right)\right)\left(x_{2}-r_{A}^{2}\left(x_{2}-1\right)\right)\right]M_{B}^{2},\\

\beta_{d}&=\left[x_{1}+x_{2}-1-r_{A}^{2}\left(x_{2}-x_{3}\right)\right]\left[1-x_{1}-x_{3}-r_{J/\psi}^{2}\left(x_{2}-x_{3}\right)\right]M_{B}^{2}.\end{split}

(A.9)

The hard scales

\displaystyle\begin{split}t_{a}&=\max\left(\sqrt{\left|\alpha_{e}\right|},\sqrt{\left|\beta_{a}\right|},1/{b_{1}},1/{b_{2}}\right),&t_{b}&=\max\left(\sqrt{\left|\alpha_{e}\right|},\sqrt{\left|\beta_{b}\right|},1/{b_{1}},1/{b_{2}}\right),\\

(A.10)

&\quad+\frac{11}{3}\int_{1/b_{1}}^{t}\frac{d\mu}{\mu}\gamma_{q}(\mu)+2\int_{1/b_{3}}^{t}\frac{d\mu}{\mu}\gamma_{q}(\mu),\end{split}

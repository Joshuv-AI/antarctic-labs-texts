Central limit theorems for linear spectral statistics of inhomogeneous random graphs with graphon limits
Xiangyi Zhu, Yizhe Zhu

Abstract
. We establish central limit theorems (CLTs) for the linear spectral statistics of the adjacency matrix of inhomogeneous random graphs across all sparsity regimes, providing explicit covariance formulas under the assumption that the variance profile of the random graphs converges to a graphon limit. Two types of CLTs are derived for the (non-centered) adjacency matrix and the centered adjacency matrix, with different scaling factors when the sparsity parameter p p satisfies n ​ p = n Ω ⁡ ( 1 ) np=n^{\Omega(1)} , and with the same scaling factor when n ​ p = n o ⁡ ( 1 ) np=n^{o(1)} . In both cases, the limiting covariance is expressed in terms of homomorphism densities from certain types of finite graphs to a graphon. These results highlight a phase transition in the centering effect for global eigenvalue fluctuations. For the non-centered adjacency matrix, we also identify new phase transitions for the CLTs in the sparse regime when n 1 / m ≪ n ​ p ≪ n 1 / ( m − 1 ) n^{1/m}\ll np\ll n^{1/(m-1)} for m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . Furthermore, weaker conditions for the graphon convergence of the variance profile are sufficient as p p decreases from being constant to n ​ p → c ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) np\to c\in(0,\infty) . These findings reveal a novel connection between graphon limits and linear spectral statistics in random matrix theory.

1.3.3.
Three types of graphon convergence

All results for the CLTs in this paper assume the variance profile matrix
S_{n}
of the inhomogeneous random graph converges to limit under various types of graphon convergence. The limiting covariance of the CLT is expressed in terms of homomorphism densities from finite graphs to a graphon.

A notable feature of our results is that the required topology for graphon convergence becomes weaker as the sparsity parameter
p
decreases. This occurs because the finite graphs contributing to the limiting variance term become simpler in sparser regimes.

•

Dense regime (
p\in(0,1)
): A stronger notion of convergence, called
\delta_{1}
-convergence (see Assumption
3.2
), is needed. This is due to the influence of multigraph homomorphism densities on the variance term.

•

Sparse regime (
np\to\infty,p\to 0
): Multigraph homomorphism densities no longer contribute to the limiting variance, and convergence of tree homomorphism suffices for the CLT of
\overline{A}_{n}
. While for
A_{n}
,
\delta_{\Box}
convergence in required when
np=n^{\Omega(1)}
(Assumption
3.3
).

•

For the CLT of
\overline{A}_{n}
when
np\to c
, and the CLT of
A_{n}
when
np=n^{o(1)}
, the only nonzero covariance in the limit arises from homomorphism densities of trees to a graphon. Consequently, only a weaker assumption on finite tree homomorphism densities is required (Assumption
3.4
), as shown in
[
68
]
.

Furthermore, by adapting the proof in
[
68
, Theorem 5.1]
, all results in Section
3
are also valid for
W
-random graphs where the inhomogeneous random graph is generated such that each edge

is connected independently with probability

, with
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
i.i.d. samples uniformly distributed on
[0,1]

2.
Graphon theory

In this section, we introduce fundamental definitions from graphon theory. For more details, see
[
53
]
. A
graphon
is a symmetric, integrable function
W:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. Here symmetric means
W(x,y)=W(y,x)
for all
x,y\in[0,1]
.

Every weighted graph
G
has an associated graphon
W^{G}
constructed as follows: First, divide the interval
[0,1]
into subintervals
I_{1},\dots,I_{|V(G)|}
, each of length
\frac{1}{|V(G)|}
. Then give the edge weight
\beta_{ij}
on
I_{i}\times I_{j}
, for all
i,j\in V(G)
. In this way, every finite weighted graph gives rise to a graphon.

The most important metric on the space of graphons is the
cut metric
. The space of all graphons taking values in
[0,1]
, endowed with the cut metric, forms a compact metric space.

Definition 2.1

(cut norm)
.

For a graphon
W:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, the
cut norm
is defined as

\displaystyle\|W\|_{\Box}:=\sup_{S,T\subseteq[0,1]}\left|\int_{S\times T}W(x,y)dxdy\right|,

where
S
,
T
range over all measurable subsets of
[0,1]
. For two graphons
W,W^{\prime}:[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

d_{\Box}(W,W^{\prime}):=\|W-W^{\prime}\|_{\Box}
.
The
cut metric

\delta_{\Box}
is then given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\Box}(W,W^{\prime}):=\inf_{\sigma}d_{\Box}(W^{\sigma},W^{\prime}),

where
\sigma
ranges over all measure-preserving bijections
[0,1]\to[0,1]
and

W^{\sigma}(x,y):=W(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))
.

The cut metric provides a notion of convergence for graph sequences, with the limiting object being the graphon.

Similarly, the
\delta_{1}
-distance is defined as

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(W,W^{\prime}):=\inf_{\sigma}\|W^{\sigma}-W^{\prime}\|_{1}=\inf_{\sigma}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|W^{\sigma}(x,y)-W^{\prime}(x,y)|dxdy.

By definition, for any two graphons
W,W^{\prime}
,
\delta_{\Box}(W,W^{\prime})\leq\delta_{1}(W,W^{\prime})
.

Another way of defining the convergence of graphs is to consider graph homomorphisms.

Definition 2.2

(homomorphism density)
.

For any graphon
W
and multigraph
F=(V,E)
(without loops), define the
homomorphism density
from
F
to
W
as

\displaystyle t(F,W):=\int_{[0,1]^{|V|}}\prod_{ij\in E}W(x_{i},x_{j})\prod_{i\in V}dx_{i}.

It is natural to think two graphons
W
and
W^{\prime}
are similar if they have similar homomorphism densities from any finite graph
G
. This leads to the following definition of left convergence.

Definition 2.3

(left convergence)
.

Let
W_{n}
be a sequence of graphons. We say
W_{n}
is
convergent from the left
if
t(F,W_{n})
converges for any finite simple graph
F
(without loops or multi-edges).

The importance of homomorphism densities lies in their ability to characterize convergence under the cut metric.
Let
\mathcal{W}_{0}
be the set of all graphons such that
0\leq W\leq 1
.
The following is a characterization of convergence in the space
\mathcal{W}_{0}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Theorem 11.5 in
[
53
]
)
.

Let
\{W_{n}\}
be a sequence of graphons in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and let
W\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
. Then
t(F,W_{n})\to t(F,W)
for all finite simple graphs if and only if
\delta_{\Box}(W_{n},W)\to 0
.

The following lemma shows that the homomorphism density function is continuous under the cut metric only when
F
is a simple graph.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma C.2,
[
44
]
)
.

The mapping
W\mapsto t(F,W)
is continuous on
(\mathcal{W}_{0},\delta_{\square})
if and only if F is a simple graph.

To guarantee convergence of homomorphism density for loopless multigraphs, we need a stronger notation of convergence under the
\delta_{1}
metric. Lemma
2.6
was stated without a proof in
[
44
]
. We provide its proof in Appendix
A
for completeness.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma C.4,
[
44
]
)
.

The mapping
W\mapsto t(F,W)
is continuous on
(\mathcal{W}_{0},\delta_{1})
for every loopless multigraph.

The following lemma for the convergence of the graphon sequence
W_{n}^{\prime}=W_{n}(1-pW_{n})
under
\delta_{1}
metric is also needed in our proof. We provide its proof in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
p\in[0,1]
.
Let
W_{n}^{\prime}=W_{n}(1-pW_{n}),W^{\prime}=W(1-pW)
.
Suppose
\delta_{1}(W_{n},W)\to 0
, then
\delta_{1}(W_{n}^{\prime},W^{\prime})\to 0
.

3.
Main results

In this paper, we study the spectrum of Hermitian random matrices under the following assumptions:

Assumption 3.1

(Inhomogeneous random graphs)
.

Assume
A_{n}
is the adjacency matrix of a random graph satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The entries

are independent (up to symmetry) with

, for all

(2)

a_{ij}\sim\text{Ber}(ps_{ij})

is the sparsity parameter and

is the variance profile matrix.

(3)

The entries in

satisfy
0\leq s_{ij}\leq C
for an absolute constant

and
\sup_{ij}(ps_{ij})\leq 1
.

The sparsity parameter

•

Dense regime
:
p
is a constant independent of

•

Sparse regime
:
p\to 0
and
np\to\infty
.

•

Bounded expected degree regime
:
p\to 0
and
np\to c\in(0,\infty)
.

We associate a graphon

to the matrix
S_{n}
. Treat

as the adjacency matrix of a weighted complete graph such that the weight of each edge is

. The graphon

is defined as the corresponding graphon representation of

.
The centered adjacency matrix

is defined as

\overline{A}_{n}=A_{n}-\mathbb{E}A_{n}
.

When the variance profile matrix

has a graphon limit, we show that for monomial test functions

, the linear spectral statistics of

and

For different sparsity regimes and depending on whether

Assumption 3.2

(

-convergence)

Assume

Assumption 3.3

(

-convergence)
.

Assume

.

Assumption 3.4

(Convergence of tree homomorphism densities)
.

Assume for any finite tree

such that

.

Remark 3.5

(Hierarchy of graphon convergence assumptions)

Note that Assumption

is the strongest assumption, which ensures convergence of homomorphism densities of finite loopless multigraphs (Lemma

). Assumption

4.1.
Proof of Theorem

The proof consists of two steps. In this proof, we will also introduce several definitions that will be repeatedly used in other theorems.
Let
S_{n}^{\prime}=(s^{\prime}_{ij})
be another variance profile matrix related to
S_{n}
, given by

s^{\prime}_{ij}=s_{ij}(1-ps_{ij})
.
We associate a graphon
W^{\prime}_{n}
to the matrix
S^{\prime}_{n}
.

Definition 4.1

(
k
-tuplet)
.

We define a
k
-tuplet as
I_{k}=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k})
, where each
i_{j}
is an element of the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. We define
\overline{a}_{I_{k}}=\overline{a}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\overline{a}_{i_{2}i_{3}}\dots\overline{a}_{i_{k}i_{1}}
.

Step 1: Computing the limiting covariance.

We start with an asymptotic analysis of the covariance of
X_{n,k},k\geq 2
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n,k},X_{n,h})

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{(np)^{(k+h)/2}}\sum_{I_{k},J_{h}}\mathrm{Cov}(\overline{a}_{I_{k}},\overline{a}_{J_{h}})

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{(np)^{(k+h)/2}}\sum_{I_{k},J_{h}}\mathbb{E}[\overline{a}_{I_{k}}\overline{a}_{J_{h}}]-\mathbb{E}[\overline{a}_{I_{k}}]\mathbb{E}[\overline{a}_{J_{h}}],

(17)

where the sum is over all
k
-tuplet
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
and
h
-tuplet
j_{1},\dots,j_{h}
.

Definition 4.2

(Union graph)
.

To each pair of
\overline{a}_{I_{k}},\overline{a}_{J_{h}}
, we associate a graph
G_{I_{k}}
to
a_{I_{k}}
, constructed over the set of distinct indices appearing in
I_{k}
and edges formed by the unordered pairs
\{i_{j},i_{j+1}\}
. Then
a_{I_{k}}
is a closed walk on
G_{I_{k}}
through all vertices. Similarly, We construct
G_{J_{h}}
associated to
a_{J_{h}}
. We denote the union graph of
G_{I_{k}}
and
G_{J_{h}}
by
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
.

Then to find the asymptotic value of (
16
), it suffices to estimate the contribution from all possible union graphs
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
.
For a given
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
, since
p
is a constant and with Assumption
3
, when
v<(k+h)/2
, we have the contribution from all
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
with a given vertex number
v
and edge number
e
is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{p}{(np)^{(k+h)/2}}\cdot n^{v}\left(Cp\right)^{e}=O(n^{v-(k+h)/2})=o(1).

(18)

Therefore, it remains to consider all union graphs with

\displaystyle v-(k+h)/2\geq 0.

(19)

Since each entry in
A_{n}
is independent, to have a nonzero
\mathrm{Cov}(\overline{a}_{I_{k}},\overline{a}_{J_{h}})
,
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
must be connected. Also, by Assumption
3.1
that
a_{ii}=0,i\in[n]
,
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
cannot contain any loop. Let
e_{I_{k}},e_{J_{h}}
be the number of distinct edges in
G_{I_{k}},G_{j_{h}}
, respectively.
These restrictions lead to

\displaystyle(k+h)-1\geq e_{I_{k}}+e_{J_{h}}-1\geq e.

(20)

In addition, since each entry is centered, to have a nontrivial
\mathrm{Cov}(\overline{a}_{I_{k}},\overline{a}_{J_{h}})
in the limit, each edge in
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
must be visited at least twice in the walk defined by
I_{k}
and
J_{h}
. These conditions lead to

\displaystyle v-1\leq e\leq(k+h)/2.

We first consider when
k+h
is odd. Since

and
v
are integers, (
19
) and (
21
) together can be further restricted to

\displaystyle(k+h)/2-\frac{1}{2}\leq v-1\leq e\leq(k+h)/2-\frac{1}{2}.

(22)

This implies the only case we have is
v-1=e=(k+h)/2-1/2
and
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
is a tree. In the closed walk defined by
I_{k}\cup J_{h}
, each edge is walked twice, except one is walked three times. This scenario is not feasible since an odd number of walking times is not possible in a closed walk on a rooted tree. Then, when
k+h
is odd, the limiting covariance is 0.

Thus, we only need to consider when
k+h
is even. With (

) and (

), the union graphs

that contribute to the limiting variance must satisfy

\displaystyle e\leq v\leq e+1.

Therefore

or

. We then consider 2 cases below.

Case 1:

.

In this case,
G_{I_{k}\cup J_{h}}
is a tree formed by the union of two smaller tres,
G_{I_{k}}
and
G_{J_{h}}
with overlapping edges, which implies
e_{I_{k}}+e_{J_{h}}-1\geq e
. At the same time, each
\overline{a}_{I_{k}},\overline{a}_{J_{h}}
represents a closed walk on a tree that requires every edge to be visited at least twice, which leads to

are even. Together with (

\displaystyle(k+h)/2-1\geq e_{I_{k}}+e_{J_{h}}-1\geq e\geq(k+h)/2-1.

(24)

Then it implies
k,h
are even and
e=v-1=(k+h)/2-1
. Thus, the union is a planar tree formed by two rooted planar trees with an overlapping edge, which belongs to
\mathcal{T}^{k,h}_{1}
defined in Definition
3.6
.

Case 2:

.

In this case,

is a graph with one cycle. From (

), we have

. From (

. Hence,

, which implies each edge in the union graph is visited exactly twice by

, and it holds only when

since a cycle has length at least

. In this case, both
G_{I_{k}}
and
G_{J_{h}}
are a rooted planar trees attached with one cycle, where edges on the tree are visited twice and the edge on the cycle is visited once.
Therefore, the union graph belongs to
\mathcal{TC}^{k,h}
in Definition
3.7
.

Having discussed all possible choices of
v,e
for the union graphs, we are ready to compute the limiting covariance when
k+h
is even.

(i)
k,h\geq 3

To compute the size of

Centralizers in Hecke Algebras of Any Coxeter Group
Haiyu Chen

Abstract
. We study the centralizer of a parabolic subalgebra in the Hecke algebra associated with an arbitrary (possibly infinite) Coxeter group. While the center and cocenter have been extensively studied in the finite and affine cases, much less is known in the indefinite setting. We describe a basis for the centralizer, generalizing known results about the center. Our approach combines algebraic techniques with geometric tools from the Davis complex, a CAT( 0 0 )-space associated to the Coxeter group. As part of the construction, we classify finite partial conjugacy classes in infinite Coxeter groups and define a variant of the class polynomial adapted to the centralizer.

1.2.
Problem Setup and Main Results

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{J}
be as above. The structure of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
is investigated in three main steps, each centered around a key result:

Step 1: Classification of Finite
W_{J}
-Conjugacy Classes

We first determine exactly which elements
w\in W
have finite
W_{J}
-conjugacy classes. This is formalized in the following theorem, which may be of independent interest.

Theorem A

(Classification of Finite
W_{J}
-Conjugacy Classes)
.

Suppose
(W,S)
is an infinite Coxeter group and
J
is irreducible. Then
\mathcal{O}_{J}(w)
is finite only in the following cases:

(i)

J
is spherical, i.e.,
W_{J}
is finite;

(ii)

w\in W_{J^{\perp}}
;

(iii)

J
is affine, and
w=w_{1}w_{2}
,
w_{1}\in W_{J^{\perp}}
,
w_{2}\in W_{J}
, and
w_{2}
is a translation in
W_{J}
.

Here,
J^{\perp}=\{s\in S\setminus J\mid sj=js\text{ for all }j\in J\}
.

Step 2: Maximal Length Elements and Geometric Finiteness

Two important concepts for analyzing Coxeter group conjugacy classes are cyclic shift and strong conjugation.

Roughly speaking, a cyclic shift on
w\in W
means moving a generator from the start of a reduced expression of
w
to the end or vice versa. We denotes
w\xrightarrow{s}w^{\prime}
if
w^{\prime}=sws
and
\ell(w^{\prime})\leq\ell(w)
,
s\in S
, and denote
w\rightarrow w^{\prime}
for a sequence of such arrows.

Strong conjugation means moving the first few generators of a reduced expression of
w
to the end while preserving the length, or vice versa. We denote strong conjugation by
w\sim w^{\prime}
when
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
and there exists
x\in W
such that
w^{\prime}=xwx^{-1}
and
\ell(xw)=\ell(x)+\ell(w)
or
\ell(wx^{-1})=\ell(x)+\ell(w)
.

A fundamental theorem for Coxeter groups states that every element can be cyclically shifted to minimal length in its conjugacy class, and any two minimal length elements are strongly conjugate
[
14
]
. In this work, we develop the maximal version of this result:

Theorem B

(Maximal Length Representatives)
.

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system. Let
\mathcal{O}_{J}
be a
W_{J}
-conjugacy class of
W
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\mathcal{O}_{J}
is finite, then for each
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}
, there exists a finite chain
u_{k}\xrightarrow{s_{k}}\cdots\xrightarrow{s_{1}}u
, where
u_{k}\in\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
and all
s_{j}\in J
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{O}_{J}
is infinite, then for each
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}
, there exists an infinite chain of distinct elements
\cdots\xrightarrow{s_{2}}u_{1}\xrightarrow{s_{1}}u
, where all
s_{j}\in J
.

(iii)

For any
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
, we have
u\backsim_{J}u^{\prime}
. (definition in
5.2
)

One important step in dealing with infinite conjugacy classes is using metric geometry. We study the set

U^{+}_{J}(w):=\{w^{\prime}\in W\mid w^{\prime}\to_{J}w\}

and show that it is finite if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{J}(w)
is finite. This is achieved by applying tools from geometric group theory via the Davis complex.

The Coxeter complex
\Sigma
, constructed from cosets
\{wW_{T}\}
and simplicial inclusions, encodes much of the algebraic structure of
W
. However, it is not locally finite when
W
is infinite, preventing properly discontinuous group actions. Davis
[
4
]
introduced a metric realization of
\Sigma
into a complete CAT(0) space, called the
Davis complex

X
. This space is piecewise Euclidean, contractible, and admits a proper, cocompact action of
W
by isometries. Convexity properties of displacement functions on
X
—a consequence of the CAT(0) geometry—are essential to our proof that
U^{+}_{J}(w)
and
\mathcal{O}_{J}(w)
have matching finiteness behavior.

Step 3: Construction of Centralizer Basis

If
\mathcal{O}_{J}(w)
is infinite, then the entire conjugacy class contributes nothing to the centralizer. Otherwise, we inductively define
f^{\max}_{w,\mathcal{O}_{J}}\in R
, a variant of the class polynomials of Geck-Pfeiffer
[
7
]
, and construct a basis element for each finite
W_{J}
-conjugacy class.

Theorem C

(Basis of the Centralizer)
.

The centralizer
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
is a free
R
-module. An
R
-basis is given by

\left\{z_{\mathcal{O}_{J}}=\sum_{w\in W}b_{w}^{-1}f^{\max}_{w,\mathcal{O}_{J}}T_{w^{-1}}\right\}_{\mathcal{O}_{J}},

where the index
\mathcal{O}_{J}
ranges over all
finite

W_{J}
-conjugacy classes in
W
. Each sum is finite.

Conjecture on the Cocenter

Motivated by our work, we propose the following conjecture, consistent with known results in the finite and affine settings
[
7
,
11
]
, and a partial result is known in
5.9
:

Conjecture
.

The cocenter
\mathcal{H}/[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}]
has a basis indexed by the set of conjugacy classes
\cl(W)
.

Coxeter complex

Any two

For any two chambers

2.3.
Class Polynomials

Class polynomials arise from studying the cocenter
\mathcal{H}/[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}]
. The theory of conjugacy classes of finite Coxeter groups can be found in
[
8
, §8]
. We will summarize some important results for a finite Coxeter group
(W,S)
here.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
w\sim w^{\prime}
, then
T_{w}\equiv T_{w^{\prime}}\mod[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}]
.

Proof.

Write
w^{\prime}=xwx{{}^{-1}}
with
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
and
x\in W_{J}
. We have either
\ell(xw)=\ell(w)+\ell(x)
or
\ell(wx{{}^{-1}})=\ell(w)+\ell(x)
. As we assume
\{b_{s}\}_{s\in S}
are invertible,
T_{x}
is invertible. In the first case,
T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{x}T_{w}T_{x}{{}^{-1}}
. Hence
T_{w}-T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{w}-T_{x}T_{w}T_{x}{{}^{-1}}=(T_{w}T_{x}-T_{x}T_{w})T_{x}{{}^{-1}}\in[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}]
. The second case is similar.
∎

Let
\cl(W)
be the set of all conjugacy classes in
W
. For each finite conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}
, we choose once and for all
w_{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{O}^{\min}
a representative of minimal length.

By
2.3
, every element can be cyclically reduced to minimal length.
When
w\xrightarrow{s}w^{\prime}
, we have

T_{w}\equiv\begin{cases}T_{w^{\prime}}&\text{if }\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime}),\\
b_{s}T_{w^{\prime}}+a_{s}T_{sw}&\text{if }\ell(w)>\ell(w^{\prime})\end{cases}\mod[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}].

Indeed for the second case, write
w^{\prime}=sws
, we compute

T_{s}T_{w}=T_{s}(b_{s}T_{sws}+a_{s}T_{sw})=b_{s}T_{ws}+a_{s}T_{w}=T_{w}T_{s}.

Hence, by
2.3
, we may inductively, express
T_{w}
into linear combination of
T_{w_{\mathcal{O}}}
. The coefficients
f_{w,\mathcal{O}}\in R
are called the
class polynomials
. The class polynomials
f_{w,\mathcal{O}}
satisfy the following properties. If
w
is a minimal length element, then

Theorem 3.5

Suppose

is an infinite Coxeter group and

(ii)

(iii)

4.
Infinitude of cyclic shift classes

In this section, we will show that the cyclic shift class is infinite precisely when the conjugacy class is infinite, generalizing results in
[

, Section 4]
.

Let
X=X(W,S)
be a Davis complex associated with the Coxeter group
(W,S)
with metric
d
. Let
J\subseteq S
. Let
X_{J}
be the subcomplex of

with the
1

W_{J}
and only Coxeter cells

spherical. Note that as the metric is piecewisely defined on each

, and they glue into a metric, denoted as

into a Davis complex, which is again a CAT(

)-space. Note that although

is different from
\left.\kern-1.2ptd\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X_{J}}
in general, if for

on

The first two are displacement functions on Davis complexes. It is a result of CAT(

)-space that displacement functions are convex, see for example
[
2
, II, 6.2(3)]
. In particular, both

are convex on

. In particular, the statement that two

-adjacent chambers

.

We call a chamber

as

-local maximum

-gallery is flat, i.e., for every

in

for all

.

First, we quote a result on the relations of

and

.

.

[

We also need to quote a lemma for the Davis complex.

5.
The centralizers
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{J})

5.1.
The generic Hecke algebra

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter group with the length function
\ell
. Let
R
be a commutative ring. Let
\mathcal{H}(W,S,(a_{s},b_{s})_{s\in S})
be a
generic Hecke algebra
as in the introduction.

We may use another equivalent definition in practice, namely

T_{s}T_{w}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{s}T_{w}+b_{s}T_{sw}&\text{if }\ell(sw)<\ell(w)\\
&T_{sw}&\text{if }\ell(sw)>\ell(w)\end{aligned}\right.

As centralizers of products are products of centralizers, we may assume
J
is irreducible.

Assumption
.

In the sequel, we assume
b_{s}
are all invertible in
R
and
J
is irreducible.

As a consequence,
\{T_{w}\}_{w\in W}
are all invertible.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(W,S,(a_{s},b_{s})_{s\in S})
and
\mathcal{H}_{J}=\mathcal{H}(W_{J},J,(a_{s},b_{s})_{s\in J})
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}
.
The centralizer (or
partial center
)
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
is a generalization of the center.

Proposition 5.1
.

Write
x=\sum_{w\in W}x_{w}T_{w}
,
x_{w}\in R
. Then
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{J})
if and only if

(i)

x_{w}=x_{w^{\prime}}
whenever
w\approx_{J}w^{\prime}
;

(ii)

x_{w^{\prime}}=b_{s}x_{w}-a_{s}x_{sw}
whenever
w\xrightarrow{s}w^{\prime}
and
\ell(w)>\ell(w^{\prime})
for
s\in J
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathcal{H}
and
w\in W
, we use
(z)_{w}
to denote the coefficient of the basis element
T_{w}
in
z
, for example,
(x)_{w}=x_{w}
.

We first show the only if part.

(i)

It suffices to show
x_{w}=x_{w^{\prime}}
for
w\xrightarrow{s}w^{\prime}
with
\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(w)
. We may assume
w^{\prime}\neq w
.

By the exchange property of Coxeter groups, we cannot have both
\ell(sw)<\ell(w)
and
\ell(ws)<\ell(w)
. Indeed,
\ell(sw)<\ell(w)
implies there must exist a reduced expression
w
starting with
s
, say
w=s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{k}
with
s=s_{0}
. But then
\ell(ws)<\ell(w)
implies
ws=s_{0}s_{1}\cdots\hat{s_{j}}\cdots s_{k}
with
\hat{}
denoting omission. If
j\neq 0
then
\ell(sws)<\ell(w)
. If
j=0
, then
ws=sw
and that further implies
w^{\prime}=sws=ssw=w
.

Hence either
\ell(sw)>\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
or
\ell(ws)>\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
.

In the first case,
(T_{s}x)_{sw}=x_{w}+a_{s}x_{sw}
and
(xT_{s})_{sw}=x_{sws}+a_{s}x_{sw}
. Hence
x_{w^{\prime}}=x_{w}
. The second case is similar.

(ii)

In this case, we have
\ell(sws)<\ell(ws)=\ell(sw)<\ell(w)
and
sw\approx_{J}ws
. Then
(T_{s}x)_{sw}=b_{s}x_{w}
and
(xT_{s})_{sw}=a_{s}x_{sw}+x_{sws}
. Hence
x_{w^{\prime}}=b_{s}x_{w}-a_{s}x_{sw}
.

Now we show the if part, i.e., for
s\in J
, we want to show
T_{s}x=xT_{s}
when
x_{w}
,
w\in W
satisfy both conditions. For each
w\in W
,
s\in J
, there are two cases. If
|\ell(sws)-\ell(w)|=2
or (
\ell(sws)=\ell(w)
and
|\ell(ws)-\ell(sw)=2|
), we are in the situation of
(ii)
. By the proof in
(i)
above, the case
\ell(w)=\ell(sws)>\ell(ws)=\ell(sw)
is trivial. The only case left is
\ell(w)=\ell(sws)<\ell(ws)=\ell(sw)
. But then this implies
sws\approx_{J}w
, and
sw\approx_{J}ws
. We are in the situation of
(i)
. Hence
T_{s}x=xT_{s}
for any
s\in J
.
∎

Remark
.

The proof does not assume
\{b_{s}\}_{s\in S}
are invertible.

Remark
.

Note that in (ii), we also have
x_{sw}=x_{ws}
by (i).

Remark
.

It seems likely

We aim to define a maximal version of the class polynomial to record the coefficients of all

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system and

-conjugacy class of

\mathcal{O}^{\min}_{\delta,J}

\mathcal{O}_{\delta,J}

(i)

For each
w\in\mathcal{O}_{\delta,J}
, there exists
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{\delta,J}^{\min}
such that

w\rightarrow_{\delta,J}w^{\prime}
.

(ii)

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\min}_{\delta,J}
, then
w\sim_{\delta,J}w^{\prime}
.

Define a new Coxeter group
(\hat{W},S\sqcup\{s_{J,\infty}\})
with
s_{J,\infty}j=js_{J,\infty}
for all

and
s_{J,\infty}s
having infinite order for

. Consider the embedding
\hat{}:W\to\hat{W}
,

. Then

in

is equivalent to

, and

We can also have the maximal length element version of the above corollary. Before doing this, we need a variant of the relation
\sim
. (Notice the difference between
\sim
and
\backsim

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W
be such that
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
. We shall write
w\overset{x}{\backsim}w^{\prime}
if
w^{\prime}=xwx{{}^{-1}}
for some
x\in W_{J}
and if
\ell(xw)=\ell(w)-\ell(x)
or
\ell(wx{{}^{-1}})=\ell(w)-\ell(x)
.
Again, we also write
w\backsim_{J}w^{\prime}
if there exist sequences of elements
x_{i}\in W_{J},w_{i}\in W
(
1\leq i\leq n
) such that

w\overset{x_{1}}{\backsim}w_{1}\overset{x_{2}}{\backsim}\dots\overset{x_{n}}{\backsim}w_{n}=w^{\prime}.

Now we want to prove the maximal length theorem. We need two lemmas. We will use the following setting.

Assume
J\subseteq S
to be spherical, and
w^{\prime}\in W
. Then the double closet
W_{J}w^{\prime}W_{J}
contains a unique maximal element, denoted as
w
. Then any element
u\in W_{J}w^{\prime}W_{J}
is of the form
u=w_{1}ww_{2}
,
w_{1},w_{2}\in W_{J}
with
\ell(u)=\ell(w)-\ell(w_{1})-\ell(w_{2})
. For a finite conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}_{J}
, denote the set of maximal length elements as
\mathcal{O}_{J}^{\max}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u=w_{1}ww_{2}\in W_{J}wW_{J}
, and
v=ww_{2}w_{1}\in wW_{J}
. Denote the conjugacy class of
u,v
by
\mathcal{O}_{J}
. Then
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}^{\max}
if and only if
v\in\mathcal{O}_{J}^{\max}
and
\ell(w_{2}w_{1})=\ell(w_{2})+\ell(w_{1})
.

In fact, if we write
w_{1}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{k}
, then
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}^{\max}
if and only if

s_{t+1}\cdots s_{k-1}s_{k}ww_{2}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{J}^{\max}
and is reduced for all
t
.

Proof.

As
\ell(v)=\ell(w)-\ell(w_{2}w_{1})
, and
\ell(w_{2}w_{1})\leq\ell(w_{2})+\ell(w_{1})
. Hence
\ell(u)\leq\ell(v)
with equality if and only if
\ell(w_{2}w_{1})=\ell(w_{2})+\ell(w_{1})
. The same proof works for the in fact part.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K:=\{s\in J\ |\ wsw{{}^{-1}}\in W_{J}\}
. Then

(i)

K=\{s\in J\ |\ wsw{{}^{-1}}\in J\}
;

(ii)

W_{K}=\{x\in W_{J}\ |\ wxw{{}^{-1}}\in W_{J}\}

Hence,
\delta:=\Ad(w):(W_{K},K)\to(W_{\delta(K)},\delta(K))
is an isomorphism of Coxeter systems.

Proof.

(i)

Let
v=wsw{{}^{-1}}\in W_{J}
. Then
\ell(vw)=\ell(ws)=\ell(w)-1
. Then
\ell(v)=1
.

(ii)

Follows from
(i)
as simple reflections in
W_{K}
is necessarily contained in
K
.

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system and
J\subseteq S
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{J}
be a
W_{J}
-conjugacy class of
W
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\mathcal{O}_{J}
is finite, then for each
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}
, there exist a finite chain
u_{k}\xrightarrow{s_{k}}u_{k-1}\xrightarrow{\dots}u_{1}\xrightarrow{s_{1}}u
, where
u_{k}\in\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
and all
s_{j}\in J
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{O}_{J}
is infinite, then for each
u\in\mathcal{O}_{J}
, there exist an infinite chain of distinct elements
\xrightarrow{\dots}u_{2}\xrightarrow{s_{2}}u_{1}\xrightarrow{s_{1}}u
, where all
s_{j}\in J
.

(iii)

For any
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
, we have
u\backsim_{J}u^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For infinite
\mathcal{O}_{J}
,
4.4
implies
(ii)
.

According to the classification
3.5
, either
J\subseteq S
is spherical or affine.
Let
J
be spherical and
u\in W
. Write
w
for the unique longest element in
W_{J}uW_{J}
. Then
u=u_{1}wu_{2}
, for
u_{1},u_{2}\in W_{J}
. We have
wu_{2}u_{1}\rightarrow_{J}u
as
\ell(wu_{2}u_{1})=\ell(w)-\ell(u_{2}u_{1})\geq\ell(w)-\ell(u_{2})-\ell(u_{1})=\ell(u)
. We may thus assume
u=wy\in wW_{J}
. By
5.4
, we may assume
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
are of the form
u^{\prime}=wy^{\prime}\in wW_{J}\cap\mathcal{O}^{\max}_{J}
. Now for
x\in W_{J}
,

wy=xwy^{\prime}x{{}^{-1}}\iff y=(w{{}^{-1}}xw)y^{\prime}w(w{{}^{-1}}xw){{}^{-1}}w{{}^{-1}}=zy^{\prime}(\Ad(w)\cdot z){{}^{-1}},

where
z:=w{{}^{-1}}xw
. By
5.5
,
z\in W_{K}
and if we define
W_{K}
-action on
W_{J}
by
z\cdot_{\Ad(w)}y=zy(\Ad(w)\cdot z){{}^{-1}}
, called
\Ad(w)
-twisted conjugation, then we have
wy
is
W_{J}
-conjugate to
wy^{\prime}
if and only if
y
is
\Ad(w)
-twisted
W_{K}
-conjugate to
y^{\prime}
. Thus,
wy^{\prime}\rightarrow_{J}wy\iff y\rightarrow_{K,\Ad(w)}y^{\prime}
. Similarly
wy^{\prime}\backsim_{J}wy\iff y\sim_{K,\Ad(w)}y^{\prime}
by
5.4
.

Let
J\subseteq S
be affine. By the proof of
3.5
, every conjugate of a translation element is of the same length. The claim holds trivially.
∎

Now we define a variation of the class polynomial

in

Lemma 5.7

If
w\backsim_{J}w^{\prime}

T_{w}\equiv T_{w^{\prime}}\mod[\mathcal{H}_{J},\mathcal{H}]

Proof.

Write
w^{\prime}=xwx{{}^{-1}}

\ell(xw)=\ell(w)-\ell(x)

T_{w}=T_{x{{}^{-1}}}T_{xw}

T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{w^{\prime}x}T_{x{{}^{-1}}}

T_{w}-T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{x{{}^{-1}}}T_{xw}-T_{xw}T_{x{{}^{-1}}}\in[\mathcal{H}_{J},\mathcal{H}]

∎

According to the inductive definition of

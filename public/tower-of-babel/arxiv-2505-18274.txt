Abstract
. This work concerns notions of multi-algebra independence introduced by Liu and how they can be studied in the context of bi-free probability. In particular, we show how the free-free-Boolean independence for triples of algebras can be embedded into and therefore studied from a lens of bi-free probability. It is also shown how its cumulants can be constructed from the bi-free cumulants.

46L53, 46L54

1.
Introduction

The advent of free probability in the 1980’s, owing its existence to Voiculescu, brought with it a fertile ground for many rich inquiries into the depths of non-commutative probability. One such question that naturally arose was ‘
What other kinds of non-commutative probability are there and how are they related to each other?
’ It is an extended branch of this question that this work concerns.

A hallmark of probability is in its notion of independence. In non-commutative probability theory, independence shows how joint moments in independent algebras is built from the individual algebras. Speicher showed in
[
6
]
that there are only five types of this independence. Classical independence, free independence, and Boolean independence are the three symmetric types, while there are two antisymmetric independences called monotone and anti-monotone independence. Each of these can be seen via an action of operators on a corresponding product space. For example, the classical independence arises from an action on a tensor product space, while the free independence arises from an action on a free product space.

Voiculescu originally considered only one-sided actions on the free product in his development of free probability, but later he noticed that he could simultaneously consider both left and right sided actions, giving birth to bi-free independence for pairs of algebras in
[
7
]
. He noticed that both classical independence and free independence could be studied through bi-free probability, and conjectured that Boolean independence could also be modeled therein. This conjecture was answered affirmatively by Skoufranis in
[
5
]
. Skoufranis also showed that the monotone independence for a pair of algebras could be modeled in a similar fashion.

The success of bi-free probability opened up questions of what other types of independences exist for tuples of algebras. In
[
2
]
, Liu defined several such notions, with emphasis on a free-Boolean independence for pairs of algebras, and a free-free-Boolean independence for triples of algebras in
[
3
]
. Voiculescu’s question then extends to this setting. Can these multi-algebra independences be studied through bi-free probability as well? This work answers this question in the affirmative for the free-free-Boolean (and consequently for the free-Boolean) independence with amalgamation over an algebra
B
. We extend the ideas of Skoufranis in
[
5
]
to see how.

Apart from the introduction, this paper is split into five sections. Section 2 introduces the basic structures of non-commutative probability with amalgamation. Abstractly, these take the form of
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability spaces which act on
B
-
B
-bimodules with specified
B
-projections. Free product modules are recalled to make sense out of bi-free independence. We define the Boolean projections on the free product, which form a key part of the definition of Liu’s free-free-Boolean independence.

Section 3 discusses a very useful calculus arising from the incidence algebra on the so-called bi-non-crossing partitions. The LR diagram calculus from
[
1
]
is called upon to handle vector components of actions, and combined with our Boolean projections. These combinatorial tools are a staple of many non-commutative probability techniques.

Section 4 serves as a very brief introduction to free-free-Boolean independence with amalgamation. Originally defined in the non-amalgamated setting in
[
3
]
, we extend the definition to the amalgamated case and mention a couple of quick facts.

In section 5 we tackle the main result of this paper, which shows that free-free-Boolean independent families of triples of algebras can be embedded into, and therefore studied through, bi-free probability. To do so, a bi-free family with a few extra moment conditions are leveraged to house free-free-Boolean independent families. An explicit representation is discussed, and we demonstrate that the bi-free cumulants can be used to define free-free-Boolean cumulants.

Section 6 concludes this work with a brief comment on Liu’s other independences.

2.1.
Non-commutative Probability Spaces and Bimodules

Throughout this work, we denote by
B
, a unital algebra over
{\mathbb{C}}
. In
B
-valued probability theory, we concern ourselves with algebras of random variables and
B
-valued distributions. More formally, we can define a
B
-valued probability space as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A
B
-valued probability space is a pair
({\mathcal{A}},E)
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is an algebra over
B
and
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{A}}\to B
is a linear map such that

E(b_{1}ab_{2})=b_{1}E(a)b_{2}

for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
.

As the notation might suggest, we often call elements of
{\mathcal{A}}

non-commutative random variables
and
E
an
expectation
onto the algebra
B
.

We can consider elements of
{\mathcal{A}}
as operators, most simply, if we think of these elements as acting on
{\mathcal{A}}
by left (or right) multiplication. This turns
{\mathcal{A}}
into a
B
-
B
-bimodule, and embeds
{\mathcal{A}}
into the space of linear operators on
{\mathcal{A}}
. The expectation returns to us in the form of a projection onto
B
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
B
-
B
-bimodule with a specified
B
-projection is a triple
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
where
{\mathcal{X}}
is a direct sum of
B
-
B
-bimodules
{\mathcal{X}}=B\oplus\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}
and
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{X}}\to B
is the projection (with kernel
\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}

p(b\oplus\eta)=b.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
denote the algebra of
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear maps on
{\mathcal{X}}
. We have an
expectation
,
E_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})\to B
defined by

E_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})}(a)=p(a(1_{B}\oplus 0))=pa1_{B}.

One can check that starting with a
B
-
B
-bimodule with specified projection
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
, we get for free a
B
-valued probability space
({\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}),E_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})})
. As alluded to above, the same is true in reverse; starting with a
B
-valued probability space
({\mathcal{A}},E)
, we can create a
B
-
B
-bimodule with specified projection,
({\mathcal{A}},\ker(E),E)
. This correspondence between
B
-valued probability and
B
-
B
-bimodules lies at the heart of many of our investigations that follow.

Now, in order to talk about pairs of faces and bi-free probability, we need to be able to discuss left and right acting operators. These behave slightly differently when we consider the algebra
B
over which we are amalgamating. For example, if
b\in B
, let
L_{b}
and
R_{b}
denote the action on a
B
-
B
-bimodule
{\mathcal{X}}
by left and right multiplication by

respectively, we can consider the left algebra of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
to be

{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})\ |\ TR_{b}=R_{b}T\}

and the right algebra of

to be

{\mathcal{L}}_{r}({\mathcal{X}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)\ |\ TL_{b}=L_{b}T\}.

In other words, left-acting operators should commute with right multiplication by
B
, and right acting operators should commute with left-multiplication by
B
. Motivated by this, we look at the following abstraction, that of a
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability space.

Definition 2.3
.

A
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability space
is a triple
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital algebra,
\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\otimes B^{\text{op}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital homomorphism such that
\varepsilon|_{B\otimes 1_{B}}
and
\varepsilon|_{1_{B}\otimes B}
are injective, and
E_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{A}}\to B
is a linear map such that

E_{\mathcal{A}}(\varepsilon(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})T)=b_{1}E_{\mathcal{A}}(T)b_{2}

for all
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
and
T\in{\mathcal{A}}
, and

E_{\mathcal{A}}(T\varepsilon(b\otimes 1_{B}))=E_{\mathcal{A}}(T\varepsilon(1_{B}\otimes b))

for all
b\in B
and
T\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

We define the
left
and
right algebras
of
{\mathcal{A}}
respectively by

{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in{\mathcal{A}}\ |\ T\varepsilon(1_{B}\otimes b)=\varepsilon(1_{B}\otimes b)T\text{ for all }b\in B\}

and

{\mathcal{A}}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{T\in{\mathcal{A}}\ |\ T\varepsilon(b\otimes 1_{B})=\varepsilon(b\otimes 1_{B})T\text{ for all }b\in B\}.

Often, we use
L_{b}
to denote
\varepsilon(b\otimes 1_{B})
and
R_{b}
to denote
\varepsilon(1_{B}\otimes b)
for simplicity.

Previously we saw a connection between
B
-valued probability spaces and
B
-
B
-bimodules with specified projections. This connection can be refined in the case of
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability spaces, with a bit more attention to detail.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 3.2.4)
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
be a
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability space. Then there is a
B
-
B
-bimodule with a specified
B
-projection
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
and a unital homomorphism
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
such that
\theta(L_{b_{1}}R_{b_{2}})=L_{b_{1}}R_{b_{2}}
,

\theta({\mathcal{A}}_{\ell})\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}}),\quad\theta({\mathcal{A}}_{r})\subseteq L_{r}({\mathcal{X}}),\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})}(\theta(T))=E_{\mathcal{A}}(T)

for all
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
and
T\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Because the details are necessary for later use, we mention the basic construction that verifies the above result.

Given
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
, let

{\mathcal{X}}=B\oplus\left(\ker(E_{\mathcal{A}})/\text{span}\{TL_{b}-TR_{b}|T\in{\mathcal{A}},b\in B\}\right).

From here, a unital homomorphism
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
is defined with the required properties by way of

\theta(T)(b)=E_{\mathcal{A}}(TL_{b})\oplus q(TL_{b}-L_{E_{\mathcal{A}}(TL_{b})})

and

\theta(T)(q(A))=E_{\mathcal{A}}(TA)\oplus q(TA-L_{E_{\mathcal{A}}(TA)})

for each
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
E_{\mathcal{A}}(A)=0
,
T\in{\mathcal{A}}
, and
b\in B
. Here
q
is the quotient map

q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ker E_{\mathcal{A}}\to\ker(E_{\mathcal{A}})/\text{span}\{TL_{b}-TR_{b}|T\in{\mathcal{A}},b\in B\}.

The
B
-
B
-bimodule structure on
{\mathcal{X}}
is then defined by

b\cdot\xi=\theta(L_{b})(\xi)\quad\text{ and }\quad\xi\cdot b=\theta(R_{b})(\xi)

for all
b\in B
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{X}}
.

Remark and Notation 2.5
.

With notations as above, we can form a new
B
-
B
-bimodule with specified
B
-projection by taking a direct sum. In particular, let

{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{X}}\oplus{\mathcal{X}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{Y}}}=\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}\oplus{\mathcal{X}}.

So that
p_{\mathcal{Y}}(\xi_{1}\oplus\xi_{2})=p_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi_{1})\oplus 0
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}
.

2.2.
Free Product Modules and Bi-Free Independence

With our basic structures in hand, we discuss the main reason why we call this
probability
by highlighting the notion of
independence
between algebras or random variables that we care about. Independence between algebras arises naturally from their actions on a corresponding product space. Let’s consider the free product.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
be a family of
B
-
B
-bimodules with specified
B
-projections. The
reduced free product of
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
with amalgamation over
B
is defined to be the
B
-
B
-bimodule with specified
B
-projection
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
, where
\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}
is the
B
-
B
-bimodule defined by

\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\bigoplus_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}\neq\cdots\neq k_{n}}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{1}}\otimes_{B}\cdots\otimes_{B}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{n}}.

For each
k\in K
, we collect the submodule generated by all those tensor words which do not start with elements of
\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k}
, and denote it by

{\mathcal{X}}_{\ell}(k)=B\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq k_{2}\neq\cdots\neq k_{n}\\
k_{1}\neq k\end{subarray}}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{1}}\otimes_{B}\cdots\otimes_{B}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{n}},

and let
V_{k}
be the natural isomorphism of
B
-
B
-bimodules

V_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}_{k}\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{X}}_{\ell}(k).

Similarly, we can denote the submodule generated by all those tensor words which do not end with the elements of
\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k}
. We denote this by

{\mathcal{X}}_{r}(k)=B\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\neq k_{2}\neq\cdots\neq k_{n}\\
k_{n}\neq k\end{subarray}}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{1}}\otimes_{B}\cdots\otimes_{B}\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k_{n}}.

Let also
W_{k}
be the natural isomorphism of
B
-
B
-bimodules

W_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}_{r}(k)\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{X}}_{k}

For each

, denote by
\lambda_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
the unital homomorphism called the left regular representation of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
on
{\mathcal{X}}

\lambda_{k}(a)=V_{k}^{-1}(a\otimes\text{Id})V_{k},

and
\rho_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
the unital homormorphism called the right regular representation of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
on
{\mathcal{X}}

\rho_{k}(a)=W_{k}^{-1}(\text{Id}\otimes a)W_{k}.

Then, bi-free independence for pairs of
B
-faces can be defined as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
be a
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability space. A
pair of
B
-faces of
{\mathcal{A}}
is a pair
(C,D)
of unital subalgebras of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that

\varepsilon(B\otimes 1_{B})\subseteq C\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad\varepsilon(1_{B}\otimes B)\subseteq D\subseteq A_{r}.

A family
\{(C_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\in K}
of pairs of
B
-faces of

is said to be
bi-free over

if there exist
B

B
-bimodules with specified
B
-projections
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
and unital homomorphisms
\ell_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{k}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
,
r_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}D_{k}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{r}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
such that the joint distribution of
\{(C_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\in K}
with respect to

is equal to the joint distribution of the family

\{((\lambda_{k}\circ\ell_{k})(C_{k}),(\rho_{k}\circ r_{k})(D_{k}))\}_{k\in K}

({\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}),E_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})})

{\mathcal{X}}
is the reduced free product of the
{\mathcal{X}}_{k}

\lambda_{k}
and
\rho_{k}

2.3.
Boolean Projections on the Free Product

One can define Boolean independence similarly through an action on a free-product space. But to do so, we must project onto a special submodule.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
be a family of
B
-
B
-bimodules with specified
B
-projections, and let
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
be their reduced free product. For each
k\in K
we define the
k^{\text{th}}
Boolean projection

P_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
, to be the natural projection onto
B\oplus\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k}
.

We collect a couple of easy facts regarding these Boolean projections.

Lemma 2.9
.

With notations as above, fix
k\in K
. If
T\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
then

\lambda_{k}(T)P_{k}=P_{k}\lambda_{k}(T)\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho_{k}(T)P_{k}=P_{k}\rho_{k}(T),

and

\lambda_{k}(T)P_{k}=\rho_{k}(T)P_{k}.

Proof.

The first claim follows easily from the definitions. For the second claim, note that for any
\eta\in{\mathcal{X}}
, we have
P_{k}\eta\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}
so that

\lambda_{k}(T)P_{k}\eta=TP_{k}\eta=\rho_{k}(T)P_{k}\eta.\qed

3.
LR diagrams and bi-free cumulants

Instead of the abstract approach of looking for products of bimodules to see independence, we can instead look at patterns in moment calculations (that is, expectations of words) between independent algebras. This gets captured neatly in the combinatorial framework of bi-non-crossing partitions which act as a medium through which to define the bi-free cumulants. We will use these to define cumulants for the free-free-Boolean independence later.

3.1.
Bi-non-crossing partitions

We begin with the combinatorial objects that encode our moment-cumulant calculus. Let
{\mathcal{P}}(n)
denote the set of partitions of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
{\mathcal{P}}(n)
is a lattice with the refinement order: say
\pi
is
finer
than
\sigma
and write
\pi\leq\sigma
if each block (set) of
\pi
is contained in a block (set) of
\sigma
. For
\pi\in{\mathcal{P}}(n)
denote by
\sim_{\pi}
the equivalence relation on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
corresponding to
\pi
. That is,
i\sim_{\pi}j
if
i
and
j
are in the same block of
\pi
. A partition
\pi
is said to be
non-crossing
if whenever
a_{1}<b_{1}<a_{2}<b_{2}
with
a_{1}\sim_{\pi}a_{2}
and
b_{1}\sim_{\pi}b_{2}
, then
a_{1}\sim_{\pi}b_{1}
(that is, all of
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
must be in the same block of
\pi
). The set of non-crossing partitions, often denoted by

Since we deal with left and right acting operators, we should pay attention to this. To do so, let
{\raise 1.50696pt\hbox{$\chi$}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{\ell,r\}

\chi
which reorders the elements

by taking into account if they should be left or right operators. In particular, order the lefts in the usual way:

{\raise 1.50696pt\hbox{$\chi$}}^{-1}(\{\ell\})=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\},

and for the rights, we order them in reverse, as right acting operator multiplication gets reversed

{\raise 1.50696pt\hbox{$\chi$}}^{-1}(\{r\})=\{i_{p+1}>\cdots>i_{n}\}.

Now define our permutation
s_{\chi}
by
s_{\chi}(j)=i_{j}
. It is convenient to put a new ordering,
\prec_{\chi}
on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
by

if and only if
s_{\chi}^{-1}(a)<s_{\chi}^{-1}(b)

s_{\chi}
.

Then

is an order-isomorphism from
(\{1,\ldots,n\},<)
to
(\{1,\ldots,n\},\prec_{\chi})
. So we can use this to transform the non-crossing partitions into the so-called

Definition 3.1
.

A partition

3.2.
Bi-Multiplicative Functions

Our interest in these partitions comes from their natural presence in computing moments between bi-freely independent pairs of algebras. To see how, first we recall the definition of the operator-valued bi-multiplicative functions from

]

Definition 3.3

Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}

E_{\pi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})\in B

\pi

•

•

3.4.
An LR-diagram calculus

Dealing with freeness comes with a certain chaos in performing moment calculations with longer strings of operators. The number of

Definition 3.9
.

Let

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}

-

. For each

with

.

Given the notations as above, a simple but useful remark is that we have

E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n}))\in\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{\epsilon(i_{1,1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{\epsilon(i_{m,1})}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 3.12

(
[
1
]
, Lemma 7.1.3)
.

With notation as in Definition

]

In fact, the proof of the above result can be extended more generally, as it details a recursive way to add on to the diagram-indexed vectors,

, with more operators. We start with some notation to help us keep track.

Definition 3.13
.

Let

. Let

, and

.

It is immediate from the definition above that if

for applying new (left and right) operators to diagram-indexed vectors. With this notation in hand, we extend Theorem

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
i\leq n
and other notations be as above. Then for any

and constants

\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{k-1}(T_{i-1})\left(\sum_{D\in S}c_{D,S}E_{D}(\mu_{k}(T_{i}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n}))\right)=\sum_{D\in S^{\chi}}c_{D,S}^{\prime}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n}))

Proof.

The proof is the same as for

1

, as the process inductively added on to existing diagrams. The only part that differs now is that we may end up with different constants scaling the vectors

∎

Notation 3.16
.

We want to combine this result with our Boolean projections. To this end, for each

, we define the set

LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi,\epsilon,k)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{D\in LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi,\epsilon)\mid\text{if a spine of $D$ reaches the top gap then it has colour $k$}\}.

, this is precisely the collection of

.

It is worth noting that since lateral refinements may only cut spines between ribs (not at the top gap), it follows that

if

has either no spines reaching the top gap, or exactly one spine reaching the top gap of colour

.

We denote the complement of

in

simply by

. This is the collection of diagrams with at least one spine reaching the top gap with a different colour than

Combining Theorem
3.12
with Remark

yields the following.

Lemma 3.17
.

With notations as in Definition
3.10
, let
k\in K
and let

.
Then we have

P_{k}\sum_{D\in S}c_{D,S}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n}))=\sum_{D\in LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi,\epsilon,k)\cap S}c_{D,S}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})),

We can combine our observations to get the following technical lemma which serves as an extension of the LR-diagram calculus, but now including the Boolean projections.

Lemma 3.18
.

With notations as above, for
m\geq 0
, let
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n
and define

\mu_{j}^{\prime}(T_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\mu_{j}(T_{j})&\text{ if }j\notin\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\\
P_{\epsilon(j)}\mu_{j}(T_{j})&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Then there is some
S\subseteq LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi,\epsilon)
and constants

such that

\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}^{\prime}(T_{n})1_{B}\\
+\sum_{D\in S}c_{D,S}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})),

where
S\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}S_{i_{j}}^{\chi}
, and

S_{i_{j}}=LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi|_{\{i_{j},\ldots,n\}},\epsilon|_{\{i_{j},\ldots,n\}},i_{j})^{c}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

, letting

be otherwise arbitrary. When
m=0
, we notice that
\mu_{j}^{\prime}(T_{j})=\mu_{j}(T_{j})
for all

and so we can take
S=\emptyset
.

Now suppose for
m\geq 0
the claim holds, fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq m+1
and let
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m+1}\leq n
. For convenience, let
\eta_{a}=\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots P_{\epsilon(i_{1})}\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}
and
\eta_{b}=\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots(1-P_{\epsilon(i_{1})})\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}
. Then we have

\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=\eta_{a}+\eta_{b}

We take each piece individually. Let
\tilde{\chi}=\chi|_{\{i_{1},\ldots,n\}}
and
\tilde{\epsilon}=\epsilon|_{\{i_{1},\ldots,n\}}
. By hypothesis, there is some
S_{0}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=2}^{m+1}S_{i_{j}}^{\tilde{\chi}}
where

\displaystyle\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=

\displaystyle\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\mu_{i_{1}+1}^{\prime}(T_{i_{1}+1})\cdots\mu_{n}^{\prime}(T_{n})1_{B}

\displaystyle\ \ +\sum_{D\in S_{0}}c_{D,S_{0}}E_{D}(\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})).

Now by definition,
\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots P_{\epsilon(i_{1})}\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}^{\prime}(T_{i_{1}})
, so that

\eta_{a}=\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots P_{\epsilon(i_{1})}\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}^{\prime}(T_{n})1_{B}+\eta_{a}^{\prime}

3.15

\eta_{a}^{\prime}=\sum_{D\in(LR^{\text{lat}}(\tilde{\chi},\tilde{\epsilon},i_{1})\cap S_{0})^{\chi}}c_{D,S_{0}}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})).

S_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(LR^{\text{lat}}(\tilde{\chi},\tilde{\epsilon},i_{1})\cap S_{0})^{\chi}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{k+1}S_{i_{j}}.

\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=\sum_{D\in LR^{\text{lat}}(\chi,\epsilon)}c_{D}E_{D}(\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})),

so by Lemma

(1-P_{\epsilon(i_{1})})\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}=\sum_{D\in LR^{\text{lat}}(\tilde{\chi},\tilde{\epsilon},i_{1})^{c}}c_{D}E_{D}(\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})).

\displaystyle\eta_{b}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots(1-P_{\epsilon(i_{1})})\mu_{i_{1}}(T_{i_{1}})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in S_{b}}c_{D,S_{b}}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})).

Notice immediately from its definition that

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}(T_{n})1_{B}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}^{\prime}(T_{n})1_{B}+\eta_{a}^{\prime}+\eta_{b}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}^{\prime}(T_{1})\cdots\mu_{n}^{\prime}(T_{n})1_{B}+\sum_{D\in S}c_{D,S}E_{D}(\mu_{1}(T_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{n}(T_{n})),

4.
Free-Free-Boolean Independence with Amalgamation

In
[
3
]

independence for triples of algebras. In this section we generalize this definition to the amalgamated setting, and then in the following section we will show how this independence can arise in the context of bi-free probability, in both a representational sense, and combinatorially with the bi-free cumulants.

Definition 4.1
.

A triple of
B
-faces in a
B
-
B
-noncommutative probability space
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
is a triple
(A,C,D)
where

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\{(A_{k},C_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\in K}
be a family of triples of
B
-faces in
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
. The
joint distribution
of
\Gamma
is the
B
-valued functional
\mu_{\Gamma}=E_{\mathcal{A}}|_{\text{alg}(\Gamma)}
.

Remark 4.3
.

If we know how the distribution behaves on words of operators from
\Gamma
, then we know the total distribution by linearity. To this end, we will exclusively discuss the expectations of words, called
moments
. An arbitrary word of operators from
\Gamma
will require an arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a function
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{\ell,r,b\}
and a colouring
\epsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{1,\ldots,n\}\to K
, as well as elements
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
such that

Z_{i}\in\begin{cases}A_{\epsilon(i)}\text{ if }\chi(i)=\ell,\\
C_{\epsilon(i)}\text{ if }\chi(i)=r,\\
D_{\epsilon(i)}\text{ if }\chi(i)=b\end{cases}.

Then
\mu_{\Gamma}
is completely determined by the moments

\mu_{\Gamma}(Z_{1}\cdots Z_{n})=E_{\mathcal{A}}(Z_{1}\cdots Z_{n}).

Definition 4.4
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},E_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
be a
B
-
B
-noncommutative probability space. Given a family
\Gamma=\{(A_{k},C_{k},D_{k})\}_{k\in K}

-
B
-bimodules with specified

\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
and unital homomorphisms
\ell_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{k}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
and

, and (not necessarily unital) homomorphisms
m_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}D_{k}\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}_{k})
. Let
({\mathcal{X}},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}},p)
be the reduced free product of the family
\{({\mathcal{X}}_{k},\accentset{\circ}{{\mathcal{X}}}_{k},p_{k})\}_{k\in K}
. Let
\lambda_{k}

Remark 4.6

Remark 4.7
.

A natural property of independence of a family of algebras or tuples of algebras should be that the overall joint distribution should depend only on the individual (joint) distributions within the family. This is true of bi-freeness, but it is also readily seen to be true of free-free-Boolean independence. Indeed, one can observe that by rearranging the equation in Lemma

As a simple consequence of this, one can choose any space on which to represent our free-free-boolean independent family without fear of losing our independence.

6.
A brief final word on other multi-algebra independences

By considering the case when
B={\mathbb{C}}
, we see that the work above condenses down to a result about the free-free-Boolean independence (without the amalgmation over
B
) as defined in
[
3
]
. Indeed, for example, if
B={\mathbb{C}}
, then
B
-
B
-non-commutative probability spaces become simply non-commutative probability spaces. While Liu’s definition of the free-free-Boolean independence in
[
3
]
may appear different, we note that by taking a suitable free product of the algebras that appear in that definition, we can match that with the definition provided here. Thus the non-amalgamated setting yields corresponding results for free.

In
[
2
]
, a notion of free-Boolean independence was discussed, and studied in the amalgamated setting in
[
4
]
. For the amalgamated free-Boolean independence, we can note simply from the work above that if
\{(A^{\ell}_{k},A_{k}^{r},A_{k}^{b})\}_{k\in K}
is free-free-Boolean independent over
B
, then the family
\{(A_{k}^{\ell},A_{k}^{b})\}_{k\in K}
is free-Boolean independent over
B
. In the non-amalgamated setting, when
B={\mathbb{C}}
, the family
\{(A_{k}^{\ell},A_{k}^{b})\}_{k\in K}
would be remain free-Boolean independent. By restricting our attention to the family
\{(A_{k}^{\ell},A_{k}^{b})\}_{k\in K}
in the above work, we get corresponding results for free-Boolean independence (amalgamated over
B
or not) for free. So in general, free-Boolean independence can be studied in bi-free probability as well.

Liu also suggests a more general description of similar multi-algebra independences in
[
2
]
. In particular, looking at the definition of free-free-Boolean independence (Definition
4.4
), the family

\{(\lambda_{k}(\ell_{k}(A_{k}^{\ell})),\rho_{k}(r_{k}(A_{k}^{r})),P_{k}(\lambda_{k}(m_{k}(A_{k}^{b})))P_{k})\}_{k\in K}

has implicit identity projections flanking the left and right acting algebras. Alongside the Boolean projections and identity projections, there are also
monotone projections
(see
[
2
]
). By mixing and matching these projections, we can define other types of multi-algebra independence. For example, if we replace the Boolean projections with the monotone projections, we get a notion of free-free-monotone independence.

In
[
5
]
Skoufranis also constructed a model for a pair of monotone independent algebras. It seems likely that extending this model with similar constructions performed in this work would allow one to study free-free-monotone (and hence free-monotone) independence for pairs of tuples of algebras through the lens of bi-free probability as well.

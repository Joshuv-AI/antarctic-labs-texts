Universal geometric non-embedding of random regular graphs
Dylan J. Altschuler and Konstantin Tikhomirov

Abstract
. Let Δ ≥ 3 \Delta\geq 3 be fixed, n ≥ n Δ n\geq n_{\Delta} be a large integer. It is a classical result that Δ \Delta –regular expanders on n n vertices are not embeddable as geometric (distance) graphs into Euclidean space of dimension less than c ​ log ⁡ n c\log n , for some universal constant c c . We show that for typical Δ \Delta -regular graphs, this obstruction is universal with respect to the choice of norm. More precisely, for a uniform random Δ \Delta -regular graph G G on n n vertices, it holds with high probability: there is no normed space of dimension less than c ​ log ⁡ n c\log n which admits a geometric graph isomorphic to G G . The proof is based on a seeded multiscale ε \varepsilon –net argument.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a normed space with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and let
V
be a collection of
n

distinct points in
X
. A graph
\Gamma=(V,E)

with the edge set

E:=\big\{\{v,w\}\subset V:\;\|v-w\|_{X}\leq 1\big\}

is said to be a
geometric
(or a
distance
) graph in
X
1
1

1

We note that there are varying definitions of distance graphs in the literature. The definition we adopt here is natural in the context of high-dimensional convex geometry.
. If, for a normed space
X
and a given graph
G
, there does not exist a distance graph isomorphic to
G
, we say
G
is
geometrically non-embeddable
into
X
.
Geometric graphs arise naturally in data science as
similarity networks in a feature space
where distinction between data points
is quantified by a norm. An aspect of particular interest is the construction of geometric graph
embeddings:

Problem
.

Given an
n
–vertex graph
G
and a normed space
X
,
construct a distance graph in
X
isomorphic to
G
, or show that
G
is geometrically non-embeddable into
X
.

This problem has been actively studied due to algorithmic applications, especially in the Euclidean setting. The minimum dimensions
d
for which a graph
G
admits a geometric embeddings into
\ell_{2}^{d}
and
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
, respectively, are sometimes called the sphericity and cubicity of
G
. We refer to
[
12
, Section 2]
and
[
2
, Section 3]

for a comprehensive discussion of related results.
For Euclidean spaces of constant dimension greater than one,
even the corresponding decision problem
is known to be NP hard
[
4
,
9
]
.
However, the problem has been efficiently solved in some special cases.
In particular, it was shown in
[
7
]

(see also
[
18
]
for an earlier result as well as

[
19
]
for a generalization)
that any
n
–vertex graph of maximal degree
\Delta
admits
a geometric embedding into a Euclidean space
of dimension
C\Delta^{2}\log n
, where
C>0

is a universal constant,
and, moreover, that embedding can be constructed algorithmically.

In the other direction, it was shown in
[
18
]

that any
n
–vertex
graph
G
does not admit
geometric embeddings into Euclidean spaces
of dimension smaller than
\frac{\log\alpha(G)}{\log(2r(G)+1)}
,
where
\alpha(G)
is the independence number of
G
, and

r(G)
is its radius. In particular, this implies that
with high probability, a random
\Delta
–regular (
\Delta\geq 3
)
n
–vertex
graph is not embeddable into Euclidean spaces of dimension smaller than

\frac{c_{\Delta}\log n}{\log\log n}
(see also
[
17
]
).

More recent developments within the theory of metric embeddings
provide a functional-analytic viewpoint for proving geometric non-embedding of expander graphs.
Given a
\Delta
–regular graph
G

on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, a parameter
p\in[1,\infty)
,
and a normed space
X
,
the
non-linear Poincaré constant

\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})

is defined as the smallest positive number such that
for every choice of
n
points
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}

in
X
,

(1)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}^{p}\leq\frac{\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})}{\Delta n}\sum_{(i,j):\,i\sim j}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}^{p},

where we write
i\sim j
for adjacent vertices
i,j
of
G
.

Note that whenever
G
admits a geometric embedding
\Gamma
in
X
,
the parameter
\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})
provides an upper bound
on the expected distance (in
X
) between two randomly
chosen vertices of
\Gamma
. On the other hand,
a simple volumetric argument yields a
lower
bound
for the average distance of the form
\Omega((n/\Delta)^{1/\dim X})

(see Section
2
for details).
A combination of these two observations produces a natural

necessary
condition for the existence of a geometric embedding for arbitrary
\Delta
–regular graph
G
:

(2)

\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})=\Omega((n/\Delta)^{1/\dim X}),

where we use a standard asymptotic notation
\Omega(\cdot)
.

We recall that an
n
–vertex
\Delta
–regular graph
G

is a
spectral expander
with spectral gap at least
\beta
,
if
1-\frac{1}{\Delta}\,\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\beta
,
where
\lambda_{2}(G)
is the second eigenvalue
of the adjacency matrix of
G
.
It is known that Euclidean spaces
satisfy the Poincaré inequality with a constant
depending only on the spectral gap of the graph and not on the space dimension
[
6
]
.
Together with (
2
), this implies:

Proposition 1.1

(Corollary of the Poincaré
inequality in Euclidean spaces)
.

For every
\beta>0

there is
c>0
depending only on
\beta

with the following property.
Let
\Delta\geq 3
, and for a large
n

with
\Delta n
even,
let
G_{n}
be a
\Delta
–regular graph
on
n
vertices with spectral gap at least
\beta
.
Then for every
d\leq c\log n

there is no geometric embedding of
G_{n}
into
a
d
–dimensional Euclidean space.

In view of
[
8
]
,
for every constant
\Delta\geq 3

a uniform random

n
–vertex
\Delta
–regular graph has a spectral
gap bounded below by a universal constant with probability
1-o(1)
.
Therefore, the above proposition implies that
with high probability a uniform random

\Delta
–regular graph on
[n]
does not admit
geometric embeddings into Euclidean spaces of dimension
smaller than a constant multiple of
\log n
.

The statement of Proposition
1.1

generalizes to arbitrary normed spaces
that enjoy “dimension–independent” Poincaré constants.
In particular, the result holds for normed spaces with a

1
–unconditional basis and non-trivial cotype
[
13
,
15
,
1
]
.
However, it is known that for certain spaces,
such as
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
,
the parameter
\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})
grows
with the space dimension
[
6
]
.
The dimension–optimal general upper bound on
\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})
,
independent of the geometry of the normed space
X
,
was obtained in
[
14
]

(see also the survey
[
6
]
):

\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})=O\Big(\frac{\log(\dim X+1)}{1-\Delta^{-1}\lambda_{2}(G)}\Big).

Combined with (
2
),
this gives the following result:

Proposition 1.2

(A corollary of
[
14
]
and (
2
))
.

For every
\beta>0

there is
c>0
depending on
\beta

with the following property.
Let
\Delta\geq 3
, and let
n
be a large integer with

\Delta n
even.
Let
G_{n}
be a
\Delta
–regular graph
on
n
vertices with spectral gap at least
\beta
,
and let
X
be a normed space
of dimension
d\leq\frac{c\log n}{\log\log\log n}
.
Then
G
does not
admit a geometric embedding into
X
.

We refer to Section
2
of this note for a discussion of the above statements.
We emphasize that these statements are essentially known, as they can be obtained by simple combinations
of existing results.

The main purpose of the present article
is to prove an analog of
Proposition
1.1
for
arbitrary
normed spaces,
i.e remove the extra
\log\log\log n
from the statement
of Proposition
1.2
.
Whereas Proposition
1.2
deals
with arbitrary deterministic expanders, we prove
the result for
random
regular graphs:

Theorem 1.1

(Main result)
.

Fix
\Delta\geq 3
.
For every large
n

with
\Delta n
even,
let
G_{n}
be a uniform random
\Delta
–regular graph
on
n
vertices. Then with probability
1-o(1)
,

G_{n}
has the following property:
for every
d\leq c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}\log n
and for every
d
–dimensional
normed space
X
,
there is no geometrical embedding of
G_{n}
into
X
.
Here,
c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}>0
is a universal constant.

Without any attempt to optimize, the value
.001
suffices for
c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}
.

Remark 1.1
.

While our proof naturally extends to the regime

\Delta=n^{\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)}
, we prefer to focus on the constant
degree in this note. When
\Delta\to\infty
,
the optimal threshold for embeddability is a function of
both
d
and
\Delta
, and finding the
function even in the Euclidean setting appears to be an open problem as of this writing.

Remark 1.2
.

There are related notions of embeddings considered in the literature, such as monotone embeddings
[
2
,
5
]
. It is plausible the methods of this article may be of use in studying non-embeddability of typical regular graphs in these settings.

2.
Non-linear Poincaré inequalities, and geometric embeddings

In this section, we apply
the non-linear Poincaré inequalities
mentioned in the introduction, to prove Propositions
1.1

and
1.2
.
Note that applications
of the Poincaré inequalities to
bi-Lipschitz

embeddings of graphs into normed spaces
is a well established research direction (see, in particular,

[
6
, Section 6]
).
The geometric embeddings we consider in this note
are conceptually quite different.
At the same time, we would like to emphasize that
the statements proved in this section are simple
corollaries of known facts, and are not the main results of this note.

In order to make use of the Poincaré inequalities,
we utilize the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let parameters
n,\Delta
, and
d
satisfy

n\geq 2^{d+1}(\Delta+1)
.
Further, let
\Gamma
be a
\Delta
–regular

n
–vertex geometric graph in a normed space
X

of dimension
d
, and denote the vertices by
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
. Then

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}>\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{n}{2(\Delta+1)}\Big)^{1/d}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality,
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
.
Define
B:=\frac{1}{2}\big\{x\in X:\;\|x\|_{X}\leq 1/2\big\}
,
and observe that, in view of
\Delta
–regularity
and the definition of a geometric graph, for every
i\leq n

we have

\big|\big\{j\neq i:\;(x_{j}+B)\cap(x_{i}+B)\neq\emptyset\big\}\big|\leq\Delta.

Fix any
t\geq 1
and any index
i\leq n
.
By the above inequality,
the sum of indicator functions

\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{x_{j}+B}

is at most
\Delta+1
everywhere on
x_{i}+(t+1)\,B
.
Hence,

|\{j\leq n:\;x_{j}\in x_{i}+tB\}|\leq\frac{(\Delta+1){\rm Vol}_{d}((t+1)B)}{{\rm Vol}_{d}(B)}\leq(\Delta+1)(2t)^{d}.

Thus, taking
t:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{n}{2(\Delta+1)}\big)^{1/d}\geq 1
,
we get

\|x_{j}-x_{i}\|_{X}>\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{n}{2(\Delta+1)}\Big)^{1/d}

for at least
n/2
indices
j\leq n
.
The result follows.
∎

As an immediate corollary of the above lemma,
we obtain the following quantitative version of (
2
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a
\Delta
–regular graph on
n

vertices, and
X
be a
d
–dimensional normed space,
where the parameters
n,\Delta
, and
d
satisfy

n\geq 2^{d+1}(\Delta+1)
.
Assume further that
\gamma(G,\|\cdot\|_{X})\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{n}{2(\Delta+1)}\big)^{1/d}
.
Then
G
does not admit a geometric embedding into
X
.

It was shown in
[
14
]

(see also survey
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
)
that for any
\Delta
–regular
n
–vertex
expander graph
G_{n}
and any
d
–dimensional
normed space
X
, the Poincaré constant

\gamma(G_{n},\|\cdot\|_{X})
in (
1
)
is bounded above by
c\log d
, where

c>0
may only depend on the spectral gap of
G_{n}
.
If
\Delta\geq 3
and
if
G_{n}
is a uniform random
\Delta
–regular
graph on
n
vertices then
G_{n}
is an expander with
high probability
[
8
]
,
and hence
\gamma(G_{n},\|\cdot\|_{X})=O(\log d)
.
Combined with Proposition
2.1
,
this implies
G_{n}
is not embeddable into
X
w.h.p
whenever

\log d\leq c\Big(\frac{n}{2(\Delta+1)}\Big)^{1/d},

where
c>0
may only depend on
\Delta
.
Solving the last inequality for
d

leads to Proposition
1.2

from the introduction.

We would like to emphasize that
the above approach via Proposition
2.1

cannot be used to verify the statement
of Theorem
1.1
.
Perhaps the most natural example is the
space
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
. While this is briefly discussed in

[
6
, Section 6]
, let us briefly sketch
the basic idea, originating from
[
10
]
, for completeness.
Let
G_{n}
be the uniform random
\Delta
–regular
graph on
n
, and let
d\leq\log n
.
Pick
d
vertices
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{d}

of
G_{n}
uniformly at random
and independently of the edge set of
G_{n}
,
and define a mapping from
[n]
to
\ell_{\infty}^{d}

as

i\in[n]\longrightarrow({\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(i,i_{k}))_{k=1}^{d},

where
{\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(\cdot)
is the
usual graph distance on
G_{n}
.
It is immediate from the definition that for any pair of adjacent
vertices
i\sim j
in
G_{n}
we have

\big\|({\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(i,i_{k}))_{k=1}^{d}-({\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(j,i_{k}))_{k=1}^{d}\big\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

On the other hand, it can be shown that
for two independently uniformly chosen vertices
i,j
, with a constant
probability

\big\|({\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(i,i_{k}))_{k=1}^{d}-({\rm dist}_{G_{n}}(j,i_{k}))_{k=1}^{d}\big\|_{\infty}=\Omega(\log d).

Therefore,
\gamma(G_{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})=\Omega(\log d)
,
and hence the above method using Proposition
2.1
fails to produce the optimal
(logarithmic in
n
)
bound for non-embeddability of
G_{n}
into an arbitrary normed space.

3.
Construction of the discretization scheme

The goal of this section is to produce, for a given finite-dimensional normed space
X
, a discretization
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:X^{n}\to\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a discrete subset of the Cartesian product
X^{n}
having both relatively small cardinality and “sufficient density.” The construction presented below can be viewed as a version of the classical net argument with advanced features provided through multiscaling and seeding.

For the remainder of this article,
\epsilon
,
c_{0}
,
c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}
,
C_{\mathrm{BM}}
and
C_{\mathrm{diam}}
are some universal constants. In particular, these constants do not depend on
X
,
\Delta
, or
n
. Without making effort to optimize, the following choices suffice:

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle:=.1

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{diam}}

\displaystyle:=8

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle:=.01

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{BM}}

\displaystyle:=10

\displaystyle c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}

\displaystyle:=\min\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2\log(320)},\,\frac{\epsilon\log(3/c_{0})}{10},\,\frac{C_{\mathrm{BM}}}{2}\right)\geq.001\,.

We will think of a normed space
X
as the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. The unit ball in
X
will be denoted by
B_{X}
. More generally, a ball of radius
r
centered at a point
y\in X
will be denoted by
B_{X}(y,r)
.
Given a point
y\in X
and a non-empty closed subset
A
of
X
,
the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
–projection of
y
onto
A
is any point in
A
with the minimal
\|\cdot\|_{X}
–distance to
y
.
Whenever projections of points onto subsets are not uniquely defined, a representative will be chosen arbitrarily. Finally, “
n
sufficiently large” below means
n\geq n_{0}
for some function
n_{0}:=n_{0}(\epsilon,C_{\mathrm{diam}},c_{0},c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}},\Delta)
. We emphasize that
n_{0}
does not depend on the underlying normed space
X
. However, as
n_{0}

may
depend on
\Delta
(which is, for our purposes, a fixed constant), we may assume
n
is arbitrarily large compared to
\Delta
.

Definition 3.1

(
n
–tuples)
.

In what follows,
\mathbf{x}
will always denote an
n
-tuple of points
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
. Occasionally, we will view
\mathbf{x}
as a multiset (i.e disregard the ordering).

Definition 3.2

(Sparse
n
–tuples)
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{X}
be any norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Say that a tuple of points
\mathbf{x}
is
(1/2,\,\Delta)
-sparse
if, for all
i\in[n]
,
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 1/2
for at most
\Delta
values of
j\in[n]\setminus\left\{i\right\}
.

As will be made precise shortly, we limit our search for embeddings of graphs into
n
-tuples
\mathbf{x}
that are sparse and have small diameter. The diameter restriction is in view of the following classical fact:

Lemma 3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

There exists a universal constant
C_{\mathrm{diam}}>0
such that for any
\Delta\geq 3
, with high probability as
n
diverges, an
n
-vertex
\Delta
-regular random graph has diameter at most
C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log(n)/2
.

Definition 3.3

(Domain)
.

Assume that
X
is a normed space.
Let domain
\Xi=\Xi(X)
be given by

(3)

\Xi=\Xi(X):=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in(B_{X}(0;\,C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n))^{n}\,:\,\mathbf{x}\text{ is $(1/2,\,\Delta)$-sparse in $X$}\right\}\,.

Note that
\Xi
implicitly depends on parameters
n,\Delta
.

The discretization of
X
will be based on a multiscale net-argument with
seeding.
Recall that an
r
-net
on a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
X=(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{X})
is a collection of points
(y_{i})_{i}
so that for all
x\in A
, there exists
y_{i}
with
\|x-y_{i}\|_{X}\leq r
. We will always assume that the points
y_{i}
are contained in
A
for convenience. We introduce the following notation:

Definition 3.4

(Simple nets)
.

Given
r>0
and
y\in X
, define
\mathcal{N}(y,r;c_{0}r)
as an (arbitrary)
c_{0}r
-net in
B_{X}(y;r)
of minimum cardinality. Further, let
\mathcal{N}_{0}
denote an arbitrary
1
-net in
B_{X}(0;C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)
of minimum cardinality.

Definition 3.5

(Dyadic sequence)
.

For every integer
\ell
define
r_{\ell}:=2^{\ell}
.

Definition 3.6

(Multiscale net)
.

Let
X
be a normed space. We define the
multiscale net

\mathcal{N}_{M}
by:

\mathcal{N}_{M}:=\mathcal{N}_{0}\cup\left(\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{N}_{0}}\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil}\mathcal{N}(y,\,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})\right)\,.

In what follows, we will use points from
\mathcal{N}_{M}
to approximate locations
of vertices in a geometric graph embedding into
X
.

Definition 3.7

(Scale of separation)
.

Given
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
,
define the
local scale of separation for
x_{i}
by:

\ell_{i}:=\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x},X)=\min\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell}\right\}\right|\geq n^{2\epsilon}\right\}\,,\quad 1\leq i\leq n\,.

Informally, for every
i
, the local scale of separation for
x_{i}
is the logarithm of the radius of a ball in
X
centered at
x_{i}
containing about
n^{2\epsilon}
points
x_{j}
. We further write
\ell(\mathbf{x})=\ell(\mathbf{x},X)
for the tuple
(\ell_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that whenever
\mathbf{x}
is
(1/2,\,\Delta)
-sparse and
n
is sufficiently large, we necessarily have
\ell_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\leq n
.
Moreover, whenever
\mathbf{x}\in(B_{X}(0,r))^{n}
for some
r>0
, we must have
\ell_{i}\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2r)\rceil
for all
i
.
In particular, for every
\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\Xi

we have
\ell_{i}\in\{0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\}

for all
i\leq n
.

The following simple property says that,
assuming the appropriate choice of the constants involved,
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
a vast majority of pairs
of components
x_{i},x_{j}
are at large distance from each other.

Lemma 3.2

(Lower-bound on scales of separation)
.

For all
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
and all
i\in[n]
,

\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 36\right\}\right|<n^{2\epsilon}\,.

In particular,
r_{\ell_{i}}\geq 36
for all
i\in[n]
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is easy to verify from the proof below that the constant
36
in the lower bound for
r_{\ell_{i}}
can be replaced with arbitrary fixed number, by adjusting
c_{d}
accordingly.

Proof.

As
\Delta
is fixed, we may assume
n
is large enough that
\Delta+1\leq n^{\epsilon/2}
. Additionally,
d\leq c_{d}\log n
by assumption.
Assume for the sake of contradiction there is a
(1/2,\,\Delta)
-sparse tuple
\mathbf{x}
in
(B_{X}(0;\,C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n))^{n}
and an index
i\in[n]
such that

\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 36\right\}\right|\geq n^{2\epsilon}\,.

Note that any covering of
B_{X}(x_{i},36)
using translate of
B_{X}(0,\,1/4)
must have cardinality at least
n^{\epsilon}/(\Delta+1)
. (By the pigeonhole principle, any smaller covering would have a ball with more than
\Delta+1
points from
\mathbf{x}
, violating the
(1/2,\,\Delta)
-sparse assumption on
\mathbf{x}
). By the standard duality between packing and covering, this implies
B_{X}(x_{i},37)
contains as a subset at least
n^{\epsilon}/(\Delta+1)
disjoint copies of
B_{X}(0,1/8)
. By volumetric considerations, we obtain:

\frac{n^{\epsilon}}{\Delta+1}\,8^{-d}\leq 37^{d}\,.

However, by our choices of parameters,
320^{d}\leq e^{\epsilon\log(n)/2}\leq n^{\epsilon}/(\Delta+1)
, providing the desired contradiction. Finally, note that by construction of
\ell_{i}
, we have
\ell_{i}\geq\log_{2}(36)
, so that
r_{\ell_{i}}\geq 36
.
∎

In the next proposition we define
seeds
. Seeds are
small-cardinality subsets of
\mathbf{x}
that play a key role in the discretization scheme:
\mathbf{x}
will be embedded into simple (local) nets centered at the seeds.

Proposition 3.1

(Existence of good seeds)
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large (independent of
X
). For any
n
–tuple
\mathbf{x}
, and for each integer
\ell\geq 0
, there exists a multiset
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\subset\mathbf{x}
, with
|S_{\ell}|=\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor
and the following property. For any component
x_{i}
of
\mathbf{x}
with
\ell_{i}=\ell
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
with

\|y-x_{i}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell_{i}}\,.

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{x}
and
\ell
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
be the collection of
\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor
points selected independently uniformly at random from
\mathbf{x}
(viewed as a multiset). Let
z\sim\mathrm{unif}(\mathbf{x})
. For all
x_{i}\in\mathbf{x}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{y\in S_{\ell}}\left\{\|y-x_{i}\|_{X}>r_{\ell}\right\}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\|z-x_{i}\|_{X}>r_{\ell}\right]^{\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor}\leq\left(1-\frac{n^{2\epsilon}}{n}\right)^{\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor}

\displaystyle=e^{-\Theta(n^{\epsilon})}\,.

Taking a union bound over the
n
possible values of
i
, it easily follows that with high probability (in particular, positive probability), our random selection of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
has the desired property. Thus, by the probabilistic method, there must deterministically be some satisfactory realization of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
.
∎

Definition 3.8

(Discretization)
.

We define a
discretization scheme
in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
as a mapping of every tuple
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)
into
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{N}_{M}^{n}
constructed as follows. For each
\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\}
, construct
S_{\ell}
, as defined in
Proposition

3.1
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}
denote a multiset which is the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
onto
\mathcal{N}_{0}
, and let
\hat{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{\ell}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}\subset\mathcal{N}_{0}
. Then, for each
i\in[n]
:

(1)

Let
\hat{s}_{i}
be the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
x_{i}
onto
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell_{i}}
.

(2)

Let
\hat{x}_{i}
be the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
x_{i}
onto
\mathcal{N}(\hat{s}_{i},r_{\ell_{i}};c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}})\subset\mathcal{N}_{M}
.

Define
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{n})\in\mathcal{N}_{M}^{n}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that, by the definition of
S_{\ell_{i}}
, the distance
from
x_{i}
to
S_{\ell_{i}}
is at most
r_{\ell_{i}}
.
Since
\mathcal{N}_{0}
is a
1
–net, the distance from
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell_{i}}

to
x_{i}
is at most
r_{\ell_{i}}+1
. Thus,

\|x_{i}-\hat{s}_{i}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell_{i}}+1
,
i\leq n
.
In turn, this implies
\|x_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}\|_{X}\leq c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}}+1
for all
i\leq n
.

Note that the above construction
depends on the underlying normed space
X
, and the parameters
n
and
\Delta
. We collect some properties of this discretization.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large (independently of
X
).

(1)

(Cardinality estimate)

\left|\left\{(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}),\,\ell(\mathbf{x}))\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi\right\}\right|\leq\exp\left\{n\log n\right\}\,.

(2)

(Preservation of small distances)
Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})
. For all
(i,j)\in[n]^{2}
with
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 2
,

\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{6}\,r_{\ell_{i}}\,.

(3)

(Preservation of local scales) Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})
. For all
i\in[n]
,

\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{3}\,r_{\ell_{i}}\right\}\right|\leq n^{2\epsilon}\,.

Proof.

We prove the items in order.

Proof of claim 1
. By definition,
|\mathcal{N}_{0}|
is the covering number of
B_{X}(0,C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)
using translates of
B_{X}(0,1)
.
By elementary volumetric considerations, along with the standard duality between covering and packing,

|\mathcal{N}_{0}|\leq\left(3\,C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n\right)^{d}\leq e^{\log(n)^{2}}\,.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{N}(y,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})
, we similarly have:

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\ell\geq 0\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{N}(y,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})|\leq\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\,.

Recall further that for every

\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\}
,

\hat{S}_{\ell}
is a submultiset of
\mathcal{N}_{0}
of size
\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor
.
Fix for a moment
any multisets
S_{\ell}^{*}\subset\mathcal{N}_{0}
of size
\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor
,
0\leq\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil
,
arbitrary numbers
\ell_{i}^{*}\in\{0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\}
,
and arbitrary elements
s_{i}^{*}\in\hat{S}_{\ell_{i}}^{*}
,
i\leq n
, and consider the set

\big\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi,\,\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*},\,\hat{s}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=s_{i}^{*},\;i\leq n;\;\;\hat{S}_{\ell}=S_{\ell}^{*},\,\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\big\}.

By definition, for every
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
with
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*}

and
\hat{s}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=s_{i}^{*}
, the
i
–th component of
\hat{\mathbf{x}}

is the projection of
x_{i}
onto the net
\mathcal{N}(s_{i}^{*},r_{\ell_{i}^{*}};c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}^{*}})
of size at most
\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}
. Thus, the cardinality of the above set is at most

\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}.

Further, given
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi
with
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*}
,
i\leq n
,
and
\hat{S}_{\ell}=S_{\ell}^{*}
,
\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil
,
there are at most
\big(n^{1-\epsilon}\big)^{n}

admissible values for the tuple
\big(\hat{s}_{i}(\mathbf{x})\big)_{i=1}^{n}
, and hence

\big|\big\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi,\,\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*},\,\;\hat{S}_{\ell}=S_{\ell}^{*},\,\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\big\}\big|\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\epsilon}\big)^{n}.

The upper estimate on the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}_{0}
allows for estimating
the number of potential choices for sets
\hat{S}_{\ell}
,
leading to the bound

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi,\,\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*}\big\}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\epsilon}\big)^{n}\cdot|\mathcal{N}_{0}|^{(1+\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil)\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\epsilon}\big)^{n}\cdot\big|e^{\log(n)^{2}}\big|^{(1+\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil)\lfloor n^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor}.

Finally, there are at most
\big(1+\lceil\log_{2}(2C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log n)\rceil\big)^{n}
possible realizations of
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})
,
i\leq n
.
Combining the bounds and using the assumptions on
d
, we get the claim.

Proof of claim 2.
By triangle inequality and the construction of
\ell_{j}
:

\left|\left\{i^{\prime}\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{j}-x_{i^{\prime}}\|_{X}\leq\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}+r_{\ell_{i}}\right\}\right|\geq n^{2\epsilon}\,.

As we have assumed
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 2
, it holds by definition of
\ell_{j}
that
r_{\ell_{j}}\leq 2(r_{\ell_{i}}+2)
.
Then, by construction of the discretization
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
(see Remark
3.3
),

\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}+\|x_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}\|_{X}+\|x_{j}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq 4+c_{0}(r_{\ell_{i}}+r_{\ell_{j}})\leq 4+3c_{0}\,r_{\ell_{i}}+4c_{0}\,.

For all
i
, we have that
4\leq r_{\ell_{i}}/9
by
Lemma

3.2
. In addition,
c_{0}=.01
by assumption. Thus, the right-hand side of the above inequality is at most
r_{\ell_{i}}/6
, as desired.

Proof of claim 3.

We prove the claim by contradiction. Assume that
there is
i\in[n]
such that

\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{3}\,r_{\ell_{i}}\right\}\right|>n^{2\epsilon}\,.

Recall that, by the definition of
r_{\ell_{i}}
,

\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell_{i}}/2\right\}\right|<n^{2\epsilon}\,.

Therefore, there exists
x_{j}
with both

\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|>r_{\ell_{i}}/2
and
\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|\leq r_{\ell_{i}}/3
.
By triangle inequality, one of the following must hold:

\|\hat{x}_{i}-x_{i}\|\geq r_{\ell_{i}}/12\,,\quad\text{or}\quad\|\hat{x}_{j}-x_{j}\|\geq r_{\ell_{i}}/12\,.

By construction of
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
(see Remark
3.3
), we have that
\|\hat{x}_{i}-x_{i}\|\leq c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}}+1
and
\|\hat{x}_{j}-x_{j}\|\leq c_{0}r_{\ell_{j}}+1
. Since
c_{0}=.01
and in view of Lemma
3.2
, the first of the two options above is impossible. Then the second option must hold, which implies

(4)

c_{0}r_{\ell_{j}}+1\geq r_{\ell_{i}}/12.

On the other hand, the definition of
\ell_{j}

implies that
\ell_{j}\leq\lceil\log_{2}(\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}+r_{\ell_{i}})\rceil\leq\lceil\log_{2}(3r_{\ell_{i}}/2)\rceil
, i.e
r_{\ell_{j}}\leq 3r_{\ell_{i}}
.
Combining the last assertion with (
4
), we get

3c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}}+1\geq r_{\ell_{i}}/12,

which again contradicts Lemma
3.2
. This proves the claim.
∎

4.
Proof of Main Result

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
proceeds in two steps. First, we show that for any particular
X
, the proportion of
d
-regular graphs which do not admit robust obstacles to embedding (i.e. are “close” to being embeddable) is at most
\exp\{-cn\log n\}
for some
c>0
. Second, using known metric entropy estimates on the Banach-Mazur compactum, we show that we can discretize the space of all possible
X
with a net having cardinality
\exp\left\{\mathrm{o}(n\log n)\right\}
that is fine-grained enough to preserve “robust obstacles” to embeddability.

4.1.
Preliminaries

We first introduce some relevant notation. As before, let
\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}
denote the collection of simple undirected
\Delta
–regular graphs on vertices
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{d}
denote the set of all
d
-dimensional normed spaces, equipped with the so-called (multiplicative) Banach-Mazur distance. Recall that the (multiplicative) Banach-Mazur distance
2
2

2

It is
\log d_{\mathrm{BM}}
which actually forms a metric; under this metric, it is a simple fact that
\mathcal{B}_{d}
is compact.
is given by:

d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,X^{\prime}):=\inf\left\{\|u\|\|u^{-1}\|\right\}\,,

where the infimum is over isomorphisms from
X
to
X^{\prime}
and
\|u\|
is the operator norm. Given a normed space

and an

\mathbf{x}
in

{\rm geom}_{X}(\mathbf{x})
for the geometric graph in

generated by
\mathbf{x}
, i.e the graph with vertex set
\mathbf{x}
, where any two vertices are adjacent if and only if the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
–distance between them is at most one. Finally, we write
{\rm geom}_{X}(\mathbf{x})\cong G
for a graph

on
[n]

if the mapping
i\to x_{i}
,

G
and
{\rm geom}_{X}(\mathbf{x})
.

The following proposition is the main technical result of the paper; the proof is deferred.

Proposition 4.1

(Robust non-embedding)
.

Let

be a
d

n
be sufficiently large.
Let

denote the set of

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}}\left[~\bigcup_{(X^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x})\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,\,2)\,\times\,\Xi(X)}~\left\{{\rm geom}_{X^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})\cong G\right\}\right]\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}\,n\log n\right\}\,.

Next, we record a known metric entropy estimate on the Banach-Mazur compactum.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
16
]
)

There is some positive universal constant

and a
2

Opening up the proof of the lemma in

suffices. Let us check that the above results readily imply the main theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Recalling

|\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)|\leq\exp\left\{n^{c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}C_{\text{BM}}}\right\}\leq e^{\sqrt{n}}\,.

Let

{\mathcal{D}}:=\left\{\mathrm{diam}(G)\leq C_{\mathrm{diam}}\log(n)/2\right\}\,.

Applying

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}}\left[\bigcup_{d\leq c_{\text{\tiny\ref{thm:main}}}\log n}~\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,{\rm geom}_{X}(\mathbf{x})\cong G\right\}\right]

given in

3

4.2.
Robust non-embedding

We use the following classical enumeration result.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let

|\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}|=\left(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)\right)\,e^{1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}}\frac{(\Delta n)!}{(\Delta n/2)!\,2^{\Delta n/2}(\Delta!)^{n}}\,.

In particular, the following consequence will be utilized later:

Corollary 4.1

Let
\Delta n/2
be an integer, and let

[n]

\Delta n/2

. If

\mathbb{P}\left[G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}\right]=e^{-\mathrm{o}\left(n\Delta\log n\right)}\,.

As

4.1
roughly asserts that the change-of-measure between the uniform

-regular model and the more tractable

Proof of
Corollary

4.1
.

We prove the result instead for

drawn from the “

” Erdös-Rényi model,

By definition of the Erdös-Rényi model, we have:

\mathbb{P}\left[G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}\right]=|\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}|\left(\frac{\Delta}{n}\right)^{\Delta n/2}\left(1-\frac{\Delta}{n}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}(n-1-\Delta)}=|\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}|\,\exp\left\{-\frac{\Delta}{2}\,n\log n-\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta n\log\Delta\right)\right\}\,.

Next, applying Stirling’s formula to the conclusion of

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}|

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\pm\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta n\log\Delta\right)\right\}\left(\frac{\Delta n}{e}\right)^{\Delta n/2}=\exp\left\{\pm\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta n\log\Delta\right)\right\}\,\exp\left\{\frac{\Delta}{2}\,n\log n\right\}\,.

Combining the previous two equations yields the result:

\mathbb{P}\left[G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}\right]=\exp\left\{-\mathcal{O}\left(n\Delta\log\Delta\right)\right\}=\exp\left\{-\mathrm{o}\left(n\Delta\log n\right)\right\}\,.

Proof of

4.1

For now on, we fix any normed space

Observe that
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)

and

. Thus, the first assertion of

3.3
yields:

(5)

\left|\left\{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\right\}\right|\leq\left|\left\{(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\ell(\mathbf{x}))\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\right\}\right|\leq\exp\left\{n\log n\right\}\,.

Fix for a moment any
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)
.
We claim that if the edge-set

of a graph

{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
, then for any

Indeed, assume for contradiction there exists a pair

with

, and
{\rm geom}_{X^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})\cong G

\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 2

\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{6}r_{\ell_{i}}\,.

This implies that
(i,j)\not\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)

.

In view of the proven claim, letting

denote the edge-set of an

G

, we obtain:

\mathbb{P}\left[\exists\,(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\in B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)\times\Xi(X)\,:\,{\rm geom}_{X^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})\cong G\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\,:\,E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\,.

In the remainder of this proof, let
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}
respectively denote the uniform measure on
\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}
and on the set of graphs on
[n]
with
\Delta n/2
edges.
We compute:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\,:\,E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\,:\,E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\,\big|\,G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\,:\,E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]}{\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}\right]}\,.

In the last line, the probability in the denominator is lower-bounded via
Corollary

4.1
. For the numerator, recalling (
5
), a union bound yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)\,:\,E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{n\log n}\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)}\,\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\,.

We claim that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)
,

(6)

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{(1-\epsilon)}{2}\,n\,\Delta\log(n)+\mathcal{O}\left(n\Delta\right)\right\}\,.

Indeed, note that
|E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)|
is distributed as a hypergeometric random variable with
n(n-1)/2
total population,
|{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)|
population marked as “success,” and
n\Delta/2
trials. Then:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]=\frac{\binom{\binom{n}{2}-|{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)|}{n\Delta/2}}{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{n\Delta/2}},

where, again by a slight abuse of notation, we think of
|{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)|
as the number of unordered pairs
\{i,j\}
,
i\neq j
, with
(i,j)
or
(j,i)
in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
.
For any
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(X)
, we have by the third assertion of
Lemma

3.3
that
|\binom{n}{2}-|{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)||\leq n^{1+2\epsilon}
. Applying the elementary inequality
(m/k)^{k}\leq\binom{m}{k}\leq(me/k)^{k}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G(n,\Delta n/2)}\left[E(G)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{en^{1+\epsilon}}{n(n-1)/2}\right)^{n\Delta/2}=\exp\left\{-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\,n\,\Delta\log n+\mathcal{O}\left(n\Delta\right)\right\}\,.

This establishes (
6
). Combining (
6
) with the computations preceding it yields the desired result:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}}\left[\exists\,(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\in B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)\times\Xi(X)\,:\,{\rm geom}_{X^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})\cong G\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{n\log n}\,e^{-\frac{(1-\epsilon)}{2}\,n\,\Delta\log(n)+\mathrm{o}\left(n\log n\right)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{1}{4}\,n\log n}\,.

∎

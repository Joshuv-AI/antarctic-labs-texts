Guillermo Matera 1,2

Abstract
. We investigate the asymptotic behavior of entropy polymatroids associated with algebraic matroids over finite fields. Given an algebraic matroid 𝖬 := ( ℰ , r ) {\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r) and the irreducible variety V V associated with 𝖬 {\sf M} , we consider the polymatroid h 𝔽 h_{\mathbb{F}} induced by the entropies of the projections of V ⁡ ( 𝔽 ) V(\mathbb{F}) , where 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is a finite extension of 𝔾 \mathbb{G} . Revisiting a construction of Matú s ˇ \check{\mathrm{s}} , we show that the polymatroid h 𝔽 h_{\mathbb{F}} converges to the rank function r r of 𝖬 {\sf M} as q := | 𝔽 | q:=|\mathbb{F}| tends to infinity. Our main contribution is to make this convergence quantitative: we derive explicit uniform error bounds for the deviation | h 𝔽 − r | |h_{\mathbb{F}}-r| , expressed in terms of the degree of V V , the ground set size | ℰ | |\mathcal{E}| , the rank r ⁡ ( ℰ ) r(\mathcal{E}) , and q q . The proofs combine tools of algebraic geometry (effective Lang-Weil estimates and intrinsic degree bounds for annihilating polynomials of circuits) with information-theoretic arguments (submodularity of entropy and conditional entropy estimates). These results provide the first effective and uniform approximation bounds for algebraic matroids by entropy polymatroids, clarifying the quantitative link between algebraic independence (captured by matroid rank) and information-theoretic independence (captured by entropy).

1.
Introduction

Matroid theory provides a unifying framework for combinatorial
structures arising in graph theory, geometry, and optimization.
Among the various classes of matroids,
algebraic matroids

occupy a distinguished position. They arise naturally from sets of
algebraically dependent elements in a field extension and serve as
a bridge between combinatorial independence and algebraic
geometry. Classical references such as
[
12
]
(see also

[
13
]
) emphasize their central role both in combinatorics and in
applications to algebraic geometry.

An important line of research initiated by
Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}

[
10
,
11
]
(see also

[
4
]
) concerns the relationship between algebraic
matroids and
entropic matroids
. In particular,
Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}
showed that every algebraic matroid is

almost entropic
, meaning that its rank function can be
approximated by entropic polymatroids. This result establishes a
deep connection between information theory and algebraic
combinatorics. While the original proof is qualitative in nature, it
leaves open the problem of obtaining explicit quantitative bounds—
a question that is the focus of the present work.

A quantitative refinement of Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}
’s theorem is
important not only from a conceptual perspective, but also for
potential applications. Explicit bounds on the rate at which
entropic polymatroids approximate the rank function of an algebraic
matroid are relevant, for instance, in the design of
information-theoretic protocols such as polynomial secret sharing
schemes
[
16
,
2
]
, where sequences of entropic
inequalities allow one to construct secret sharing schemes with
information ratio tending to one (see
[
3
]
for details).
At the same time, quantitative approximations play a key role in
network coding
[
17
]
, where entropic polymatroids provide
outer bounds for feasible rate regions.

The purpose of this paper is to present a refined and quantitative
version of Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}
’s theorem. As shown
in
[
11
]
, it suffices to consider the case of algebraic
matroids over a finite field. Our main
contribution is an explicit approximation scheme showing that
algebraic matroids over a finite field are almost entropic, with the error
bounded in terms of geometric invariants of the associated algebraic
variety. More precisely, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
be an algebraic matroid defined
over a finite field
\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is its ground set and
r
is the
corresponding rank function. Let
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
be
the variety associated with
{\sf M}
, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a
finite field extension of
\mathbb{G}
with
|\mathbb{F}|\geq(5\deg(V)^{7/3}+1)
.
For an arbitrary
subset
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, with
n:=|\mathcal{E}|
,

m:=r(\mathcal{E})
,
\delta:=\deg(V)
and
q:=|\mathbb{F}|
, we have

|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})|\leq\frac{nm\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{n\ln\delta}{\ln q}-\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}.

This yields a quantitative refinement of Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}
’s theorem,
with effective constants associated to the underlying variety
V
.

Beyond the proof of this quantitative version, we also undertake a
systematic study of
theoretical aspects
of the varieties
associated with algebraic matroids. In this part of the paper we
analyze some of their algebraic, geometric, and arithmetic
properties, and explain how these features influence the entropic
behavior of the corresponding matroids. In particular, we highlight
the role of dimension theory, degrees, and rational points in
controlling the quality of entropic approximations. As an example,
we obtain the following result.

Proposition 1.2
.

With hypotheses and notations as in Theorem
1.1
,
let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a circuit and let

j\in\mathcal{C}
. Then there exists a nonzero absolutely
irreducible polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]\setminus\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}]
of
degree at most
\delta
such that

f_{\mathcal{C}}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{C}})=0.

The existence of an annihilating polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}
for
the set
\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\}
is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 11]
).
Furthermore, an
extrinsic
upper bound on the
degree of this polynomial in terms of the degrees of the polynomials

\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\}
can be obtained applying
[
1
, Corollary
6]
. Our contribution relies on the
intrinsic
bound
of the proposition in terms of the degree of the variety
V
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review the necessary background on information
theory, matroid theory, and algebraic geometry.
Section
3
discusses
theoretical aspects of algebraic, geometric, and arithmetic nature
of the variety underlying an algebraic matroid. Finally,
Section
4
contains the precise statement and proof
of our main theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall basic notions on information theory,
matroid theory, and algebraic geometry that will be used throughout
the paper.

2.1.
Entropy and polymatroids

Given a discrete random variable
S
taking values in a finite set

\mathcal{S}
with probability mass function

p(s):=\mathbb{P}(S=s)
, the
Shannon entropy
of
S
is defined by

H(S):=-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}p(s)\ln p(s),

where
\ln
represents the logarithm in base
e
, and where terms
with
p(s)=0
are omitted. For two random variables
S
and
T
with
joint probability distribution
p(s,t)
, one defines the
joint
entropy

H(S,T):=-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}p(s,t)\log p(s,t),

and the
conditional entropy
of
S
given
T
as

H(S\mid T):=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}p(t)H(S\mid T=t),

where

H(S\mid T=t):=-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}p(s\mid t)\,\ln p(s\mid t).

Equivalently,

H(S\mid T):=H(S,T)-H(T).

These notions extend to random vectors
S:=(S_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{E}}

indexed by a finite set
\mathcal{E}
, where for
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
one considers the subvector

S_{\mathcal{A}}:=(S_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
. Associated to such a
vector is the set function
h:2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

h(\mathcal{A}):=H(S_{\mathcal{A}})
. A function
h:2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called a
polymatroid

if it is:

•

normalized (
h(\emptyset)=0
),

•

monotone (
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}
implies

h(\mathcal{A})\leq h(\mathcal{B})
), and

•

submodular
(
h(\mathcal{A})+h(\mathcal{B})\geq h(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B})+h(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})

for all
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
).

When
h
arises as above from
the entropies of a random vector
S
, it is said to be

entropic
. More precisely,
h
is called the
entropic polymatroid
induced
by the
random vector
S
.

2.2.
Matroids and algebraic matroids

We next recall basic notions of matroid theory; see
[
12
]

for background.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a finite set. A
matroid
on
\mathcal{E}

is a pair
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
where
r:2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is a function, called the
rank function
,
satisfying the following axioms for all subsets
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{E}
:

(R1)

0\leq r(\mathcal{A})\leq|\mathcal{A}|
,

(R2)

If
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{E}
, then
r(\mathcal{A})\leq r(\mathcal{B})
,

(R3)

For all
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{E}
,

r(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B})+r(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})\leq r(\mathcal{A})+r(\mathcal{B}).

These axioms imply that
r
is a normalized, monotone,
and submodular function with integer values.

A subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
is said to be

independent
if
r(\mathcal{I})=|\mathcal{I}|
, and a

base
is an inclusion-maximal independent set. A

circuit
is a minimally dependent subset, i.e., a dependent
set all of whose proper subsets are independent. If
\mathcal{I}
is
a base and
j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}
, then the

fundamental circuit of
j
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
is
the unique circuit contained in
\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\}
.

Using the notion of entropic polymatroid introduced above,
we call a matroid
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)

almost entropic
if its rank function
r
lies in the closure
of the cone of entropic polymatroids, i.e., if there exists a
sequence of entropic polymatroids
(h^{(m)})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

such that for every subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}h^{(m)}(\mathcal{A})=r(\mathcal{A}).

A matroid
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
is said to be
algebraic
over a field
\mathbb{G}
if there exists a family of elements

(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{E}}
in a purely transcendental extension

\mathbb{H}
of
\mathbb{G}
such that for every
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
the rank is given by

r(\mathcal{A})=\deg_{\operatorname{tr}/\mathbb{G}}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{A}),

where
\deg_{\operatorname{tr}/\mathbb{G}}
denotes
the transcendence degree of
\mathbb{G}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{A})
over
\mathbb{G}
.

2.3.
Algebraic geometry

We use standard notions and notations of
algebraic geometry, as found, for example, in
[
7
]
,

[
9
]
, and
[
15
]
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a perfect field and
\overline{\mathbb{G}}
its algebraic
closure. We denote by
\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
the
n
–dimensional
affine space
\overline{\mathbb{G}}{}^{n}
. This space is endowed with its
Zariski topologies over
\mathbb{G}
, for which a closed set is the zero
locus of a set of polynomials of
\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.
An
affine variety of
\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
defined over

\mathbb{G}

(or an affine
\mathbb{G}
–variety) is the set of common zeros in

\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
of polynomials
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. We will often write
V(F_{1},\ldots,F_{m})
or
\{F_{1}=0,\ldots,F_{m}=0\}
to
denote this affine
\mathbb{G}
–variety.

A
\mathbb{G}
–variety
V
is said to be
\mathbb{G}
–
irreducible
if it
cannot be expressed as a finite union of proper
\mathbb{G}
–subvarieties
of
V
. Furthermore,
V
is
absolutely irreducible
if it is

\overline{\mathbb{G}}
–irreducible as a
\overline{\mathbb{G}}
–variety. Any

\mathbb{G}
–variety
V
admits a unique (up to reordering) irredundant
decomposition
V=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{C}_{s}
, where
each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is
\mathbb{G}
–irreducible (absolutely irreducible
and defined over
\mathbb{G}
). These are called the
irreducible

\mathbb{G}
–
components
(
absolutely irreducible components
) of

V
.

For a
\mathbb{G}
–variety
V
of
\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
, we denote by

I(V)
its
defining ideal
, namely, the set of all polynomials
in
\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
that vanish on
V
. The
coordinate
ring

\mathbb{G}[V]
of
V
is defined as
\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/I(V)
. When

V
is irreducible, then
\mathbb{G}[V]
is a domain, and its field of
fractions
\mathbb{G}(V)
is called the
field of rational functions
of

V
. The
dimension

\dim V
of
V
is the maximal length
r

of chains
V_{0}\varsubsetneq V_{1}\varsubsetneq\cdots\varsubsetneq V_{r}
of nonempty irreducible
\mathbb{G}
–varieties contained in
V
. We
say that
V
has
pure dimension

r
, or it is
equidimensional of dimension
r
, if all its irreducible

\mathbb{G}
–components are of dimension
r
.

The
degree

\deg V
of an equidimensional
\mathbb{G}
–variety
V
is
the maximum number of points in the intersection
V\cap L
, where

L
is a linear space of codimension
\dim V
such that
V\cap L
is
a finite set. More generally, following
[
8
]
(see also

[
6
]
), if
V=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{C}_{s}

is the decomposition of
V
into irreducible
\mathbb{G}
–components, we
define the degree of
V
as

\deg V:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg\mathcal{C}_{i}.

We will frequently use the following
Bézout inequality
(see

[
8
]
,
[
6
]
): if
V
and
W
are

\mathbb{G}
–varieties of
\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
, then

(2.1)

\deg(V\cap W)\leq\deg V\cdot\deg W.

Let
V
and
W
be irreducible affine
\mathbb{G}
–varieties of the same
dimension, and let
f:V\to W
be a regular map for which

\overline{f(V)}=W
, where
\overline{f(V)}
is the closure of

f(V)
with respect to the Zariski topology of
W
. Such a map is
called
dominant
. Then
f
induces a ring extension

\mathbb{G}[W]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}[V]
by composition with
f
.

2.4.
Rational points

Let
\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}
be the finite field of
q
elements and let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}
be the
algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}
. Let
\mathbb{A}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq})
be the
n
–dimensional

\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}
–vector space
\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}^{n}
. For an affine variety

V\subset\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}}^{n}
, we denote by
V(\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq})
the set of

\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}
–rational points of
V
, namely
V(\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq}):=V\cap\mathbb{A}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq})
.
If
V
is an affine variety of dimension
m
and degree
\delta
,
then (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
)

(2.2)

|V(\mathbb{F}_{\hskip-1.99168ptq})|\leq\delta q^{m}.

3.
On the algebraic and geometric structure of algebraic matroids

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a finite field and let
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
be
an algebraic matroid over
\mathbb{G}
, that is, there is a family of elements

(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{E}}
in a purely transcendental field
extension
\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{s})
of
\mathbb{G}
such that the
rank function
r:=2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfies

r(\mathcal{I}):=\deg_{\operatorname{tr}/\mathbb{G}}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}),

where
\deg_{\operatorname{tr}/\mathbb{G}}
denotes the transcendence degree
of the field extension
\mathbb{G}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I})
over
\mathbb{G}
.
We denote
n:=|\mathcal{E}|
and let
d
be the maximum of the degrees of
the polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
. We shall assume that

m:=r(\mathcal{E})<n
.

In this section we establish a number of facts concerning algebraic,
geometric, and arithmetic aspects of the algebraic matroid

{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
.

Let
I_{\mathbb{G}}

be the ideal of
\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
consisting of all polynomials

f
such that
f(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=0
.

Remark 3.1
.

I_{\mathbb{G}}
is a prime ideal of
\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
the
polynomial map defined by
\Phi(\boldsymbol{x}):=(f_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}),\ldots,f_{n}(\boldsymbol{x}))
. Then
f
belongs to
I_{\mathbb{G}}
is and only if

f
vanishes on the image
\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
of
\Phi
.
Equivalently,
f
vanishes on the Zariki closure of

\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
. As
\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
is irreducible, the
Zariski closure of
\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
is also irreducible, and
therefore its ideal
I_{\mathbb{G}}
is prime.
∎

It follows that
V:=V(I_{\mathbb{G}})\subset\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
is an
affine absolutely irreducible variety defined over
\mathbb{G}
. The
dimension of
V
equals the transcendence degree of its function
field, which in this case is the field of fractions of

\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/I_{\mathbb{G}}\cong\mathbb{G}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]
. As a consequence,
we obtain the following remark.

Remark 3.2
.

The dimension of
V
equals
\deg_{\operatorname{tr}/\mathbb{G}}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{E})=r(\mathcal{E})
.

Although we shall express our estimates in terms of geometric
invariants associated to the variety
V
, such as its dimension and
degree, we first provide a simple upper bound on the degree of

V
in terms of the degrees of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
m:=r(\mathcal{E})
denotes the dimension of
V
, then
\deg V\leq d^{m}
.

Proof.

As stated before, if
\Phi:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
is the map defined
above, then
V\subset\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
is the Zariski closure of

\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
. Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
be hyperplanes of

\mathbb{A}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}^{n}
such that
|V\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{m}|=\deg V
. Since
\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
is a dense subset of
V
containing a
Zariski-open dense subset, we may assume without loss of generality
that
V\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{m}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{S}:=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{m}
. Then

\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})=\Phi^{-1}(H_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\Phi^{-1}(H_{m}).

Observe that
\Phi^{-1}(H_{i})=\{g_{i}=0\}
, where
g_{i}
is a linear
combination of the polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
Therefore, by the Bézout inequality (
2.1
) it follows
that

\deg\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})\leq\deg(\Phi^{-1}(H_{1}))\cdots\deg(\Phi^{-1}(H_{m}))\leq\deg g_{1}\cdots\deg g_{m}\leq d^{m}.

Let
\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})=\cup_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the
decomposition of
\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})
into irreducible
components. Since
\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S}))=\mathcal{S}
and
each irreducible component
\mathcal{C}_{i}
of

\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})
is mapped by
\Phi
to a point of

\mathcal{S}
, we have

\deg V=|\mathcal{S}|\leq N\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\deg\mathcal{C}_{i}=\deg\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{S})\leq d^{m}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.∎

For any finite field extension
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{G}
, let
V(\mathbb{F}):=V\cap\mathbb{F}^{n}
denote the set of
\mathbb{F}
-rational points of
V
.
As a consequence of
[
5
, Theorem 5.4]
, we have the
following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\delta:=\deg V
. If
|\mathbb{F}|\geq 2\delta^{4}
, then the set
V(\mathbb{F})
is nonempty.

In the following, we will need a lower bound on the number
|V(\mathbb{F})|
of

\mathbb{F}
-rational points of
V(\mathbb{F})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
q:=|\mathbb{F}|>(5\delta^{7/3}+1)^{2}
. Then

|V(\mathbb{F})|\geq q^{m}-5\delta^{7/3}q^{m-1/2}>q^{m-1/2}.

Proof.

According to
[
5
, Theorem 7.1]
,
for the absolutely irreducible variety
V
defined over
\mathbb{F}
we have
the following estimate:

||V(\mathbb{F})|-q^{m}|\leq\delta^{2}q^{m-1/2}+5\delta^{13/3}q^{m-1}.

Taking into account that
q:=|\mathbb{F}|\geq 2(\deg V)^{4}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|V(\mathbb{F})|

\displaystyle\geq q^{m}-\delta^{2}q^{m-1/2}-5\delta^{13/3}q^{m-1}

\displaystyle\geq q^{m}-\big(\delta^{2}+\mbox{$\frac{5}{\sqrt{2}}$}\delta^{7/3}\big)q^{m-1/2}\geq q^{m}-5\delta^{7/3}q^{m-1/2}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

We conclude this section with a discussion of circuits in

\mathcal{E}
. To any circuit
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{E}
we can
naturally associate an annihilating polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}
of
the set
\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 11]
).
An
extrinsic
upper bound on the
degree of this polynomial in terms of the degrees of the polynomials

\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\}
can be obtained from
[
1
, Corollary
6]
. Nevertheless, we are interested in
intrinsic
bounds
in terms of invariants associated with the variety
V
. In this direction,
we have the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a circuit and let

j\in\mathcal{C}
. Then there exists a nonzero absolutely
irreducible polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]\setminus\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}]
of
degree at most
\delta
such that

f_{\mathcal{C}}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{C}})=0.

Proof.

By definition, the polynomials
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{C}}
are
algebraically dependent. Therefore, there exists a nonzero
polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\overline{\mathbb{G}}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]
such that
f_{\mathcal{C}}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{C}})=0
. Without loss of generality we may take
f_{\mathcal{C}}
in

\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]
, and irreducible in
\overline{\mathbb{G}}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]
. As
\mathcal{C}
is a circuit, the set

\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}\}
is algebraically
independent. It follows that
f_{\mathcal{C}}
cannot be a polynomial
in
\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}]
.

To prove the degree bound for
f_{\mathcal{C}}
, note that since the
set
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}
is independent, we may extend it to
a base
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
of
{\sf M}
. We have

|\mathcal{I}|=m=r(\mathcal{E})=\dim V
, and

\{X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\}
is a transcendence basis of the field
extension
\mathbb{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}(V)
. In particular, we have the field
extension
\mathbb{G}(X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}(V)
. This
implies that the morphism
\pi_{\mathcal{I}}:V\to\mathbb{A}^{m}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}

defined by
\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{x}):=(x_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I})
is
dominant (see, e.g.,
[
9
, §3.3, Exercise 6]
). Applying

[
14
, Proposition 1]
, we conclude that there exists a
nonzero polynomial

m_{j,\mathcal{I}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\}]\setminus\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}]
of minimal degree with
\deg m_{j,\mathcal{I}}\leq\delta
such that

m_{j,\mathcal{I}}(X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\})=0\quad\textrm{in }\mathbb{G}[V].

More precisely, the ideal
I_{\mathbb{G}}\cap\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\}]

is principal and
m_{j,\mathcal{I}}
is a generator of this ideal.
As
f_{\mathcal{C}}
also belongs to

I_{\mathbb{G}}\cap\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\}]
, we conclude that

m_{j,\mathcal{I}}
divides
f_{\mathcal{C}}
in

\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{j\}]
. As

f_{\mathcal{C}}
also belongs to
\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\cup\{j\}]
and it
is irreducible, we conclude that
m_{j,\mathcal{I}}
also belongs to

\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}\cup\{j\}]
and differs from
f_{\mathcal{C}}

by a nonzero constant in
\mathbb{G}
. This proves the degree bound.
∎

Example 3.7

(Gap between extrinsic bounds and
the intrinsic bound of Proposition
3.6
)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a perfect field, let
n\geq 2
and consider the polynomial morphism

\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}},\qquad\boldsymbol{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\longmapsto\big(x_{1},\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})\big),

where
\sigma_{j}
denotes the
j
th elementary symmetric polynomial
in
n
variables.

Let
V:=\overline{\varphi(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}})}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}

denote the Zariski closure of the image of
\varphi
.
Consider the algebraic matroid
{\sf M}
on the ground set
\mathcal{E}:=\{1,\dots,n+1\}

defined by the algebraic dependencies among the coordinate functions of

\varphi
.

The associated variety of
{\sf M}
is
V
, which
is the hypersurface defined by the classical relation

x_{1}^{n}-\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})x_{1}^{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=0,

of degree
\deg(V)=n
. On the other hand,
the extrinsic bound of
[
1
, Corollary 6]
applied to the parametrization

\varphi
gives
\deg(V)\leq n^{n}
, since the map
\varphi
is defined by
n+1
polynomials
of degree at most
n
, and the hypersurface
V
has dimension
n
.
From the perspective of our entropy bounds, this example is particularly
illustrative: the actual degree of the annnihilating polynomial of the only circuit
of the matroid, namely
\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
, is
n
, while the extrinsic (optimal in general) bound
of
[
1
, Corollary 6]
grows exponentially in
n
.

This example shows that our entropy estimates, expressed intrinsically in terms
of
\deg(V)
, can be considerably sharper than those derived from extrinsic degree bounds.

In summary, this section established the algebraic, geometric, and
arithmetic framework underlying algebraic matroids. We derived explicit
degree bounds (Lemma
3.3
) for
the associated variety
V
and obtained
estimates concerning its number of rational points over a finite field
(Lemmas
3.4
and

3.5
). Moreover, we analyzed circuits and
proved that their algebraic dependencies admit intrinsic degree bounds
in terms of the degree of
V
(Proposition
3.6
).
These results provide the structural foundation for the entropic
approximation of algebraic matroids developed in the next section.

4.
Quantitative entropic approximation of algebraic matroids

The connection between algebraic matroids and entropic polymatroids
is of particular importance in information theory. Entropic
polymatroids arise naturally as entropy functions of collections of
random variables, and thus capture fundamental constraints in
communication, coding, and secret sharing. Showing that algebraic
matroids are almost entropic establishes that combinatorial
independence relations of algebraic origin can be asymptotically
realized by information-theoretic models. This perspective provides
a bridge between algebraic geometry and information theory, and
motivates the study of quantitative refinements, where one seeks
explicit error bounds in the approximation of rank functions by
entropic polymatroids.

Let
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
be an algebraic matroid over a
finite field
\mathbb{G}
as in Section
3
, i.e., there exist elements
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{E}}
in
a purely transcendental field extension
\mathbb{H}
of
\mathbb{G}
such
that the rank function
r
is given by

r(\mathcal{I}):=\deg_{\text{tr}/\mathbb{G}}(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I})

for all subsets
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
.

A construction due to
[
11
]
(see also
[
3
]
) shows
that algebraic matroids are almost entropic. More precisely, the
author exhibits a family of entropic polymatroids whose entropies
approximate the rank function
r
of
{\sf M}
. However,
several open questions remain concerning this proof, which we will
address in the following.

We begin with a description of the construction of
[
11
]
.

4.1.
An entropic polymatroid

Let
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
be the variety associated with
the algebraic matroid
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
as in Section

3
. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite
field extension of
\mathbb{G}
such that the set of
\mathbb{F}
-rational points

V(\mathbb{F})
of
V
is nonempty (for example, satisfying the hypothesis
of Lemma
3.4
). Define a random
variable
S:=(S_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{E}}\in\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{E}}
taking
values in
V(\mathbb{F})
, where each
\boldsymbol{z}\in V(\mathbb{F})
is chosen with
equal probability, that is,

\mathbb{P}(S=\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{1}{|V(\mathbb{F})|},\quad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{z}\in V(\mathbb{F}).

For each
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
, let
S_{\mathcal{I}}:=(S_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
denote the projection of
S
onto the
coordinates indexed by
\mathcal{I}
. This defines a probability
distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
on
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{I}}
. Define a
function
h_{\mathbb{F}}:2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

(4.1)

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I}):=\frac{H(S_{\mathcal{I}})}{\ln|\mathbb{F}|},

where
H(S_{\mathcal{I}})
denotes the Shannon entropy of the random
variable
S_{\mathcal{I}}
, and
\ln
denotes the logarithm to the
base
e
. The function
h_{\mathbb{F}}
is normalized, monotone, and
submodular; hence it is a polymatroid. Since it is defined via
entropies of actual random variables,
h_{\mathbb{F}}
is an entropic
polymatroid.

It is shown in
[
11
]
(see also
[
3
]
) that, as

|\mathbb{F}|\to\infty
, the function
h_{\mathbb{F}}
converges pointwise to
r
,
in the sense that for all
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
,

(4.2)

\left|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{A})-r(\mathcal{A})\right|\leq\frac{\mathfrak{n}}{\ln|\mathbb{F}|},

for some constant
\mathfrak{n}
independent of
q:=|\mathbb{F}|
. In
particular, the matroid
{\sf M}
is almost entropic: its rank
function is the pointwise limit of entropic polymatroids.

Nevertheless, several questions remain open in this
proof. First, the size of the field
\mathbb{F}
such that the set
V(\mathbb{F})

is nonempty is not known, and this size defines later the first field
where the sequence of polymatroids is defined.

Moreover, an explicit constant
\mathfrak{n}
in (
4.2
) would provide a more precise understanding of the
approximation of the rank function of the polymatroids
h_{\mathbb{F}}
, and
any improvement of this bound would directly sharpen the rate of convergence of
the polymatroids to the original matroid.

Our purpose is to address these issues, providing explicit
expressions for the constants underlying this construction in terms
of geometric invariants associated to the variety
V
, which can be
effectively computed from the polynomials
(f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{E})
.

4.2.
Explicit bounds on the convergence rate

From now on, let
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
be the variety
associated to the matroid
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
under consideration,
and let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field extending
\mathbb{G}
and satisfying the hypothesis of
Lemma
3.5
. Denote by
\delta:=\deg(V)
. We shall prove that the
polymatroids defined in (
4.1
) converge
to the algebraic matroid
{\sf M}
under consideration when the
cardinality
q:=|\mathbb{F}|
tends to infinity. Furthermore, we
provide explicit estimates for the convergence rate.

We start with the analysis of independent sets

\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
.

4.2.1.
Independent sets

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an arbitrary subset of
\mathcal{E}
. For
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(V(\mathbb{F}))\subset\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{I}}
, denote
by

\pi^{-1}_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}):=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in V(\mathbb{F}):\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\}

its
\mathbb{F}
-fiber under

\pi_{\mathcal{I}}
. The probability that

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{I}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}
is

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{I}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})=\frac{|\pi^{-1}_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}.

As a consequence, the entropy of the marginal
probability
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
can be written as

(4.3)

H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}})=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}.

Following the approach of
[
11
]
, we first consider the case of bases,
and then extend the estimates to arbitrary independent sets.
Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a base of
{\sf M}
,
i.e., a maximal independent subset of
\mathcal{E}
. Let
j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}:=\gamma(j,\mathcal{I})
denote the fundamental circuit of
j
with
respect to
\mathcal{I}
. Let

f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]\setminus\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}]

be the absolutely irreducible polynomial whose existence is guaranteed
by Proposition
3.6
.
In order to estimate the entropy expression in (
4.3
),
we split the sum distinguishing those
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}

for which there exists
j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}
such that the specialization
f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}},X_{j})
vanishes identically.

Define

W_{j}:=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in V:f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(\boldsymbol{x}),X_{j})\textrm{ is the zero polynomial}\},\quad W:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}}W_{j}.

Let
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(W(\mathbb{F}))
and let

j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}
be such that

f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}},X_{j})
is identically zero.
Write
f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}
as a polynomial in

\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}][X_{j}]
:

f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}}m_{h}X_{j}^{h},

where each
m_{h}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}]
is nonzero. Let
h_{j}
be
the largest element of
\mathcal{H}_{j}
. Then
W_{j}\subset\{m_{h_{j}}=0\}\cap V
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is an independent set, we
have
I_{\mathbb{G}}\cap\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}]=\{0\}
. Thus
m_{h_{j}}
is
neither a zero divisor nor zero in the quotient ring

\mathbb{G}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/I_{\mathbb{G}}
, and therefore
V\cap\{m_{h_{j}}=0\}
is a
subvariety of pure codimension 1 of
V
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chapter V,
Corollary 3.2]
). It follows that

|W_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(\mathbb{F})|\leq|V\cap\{m_{h_{j}}=0\}\cap\mathbb{F}^{n}|\leq\deg(V\cap\{m_{h_{j}}=0\})q^{\dim V-1}\leq\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1},

where the second inequality follows from (
2.2
),
and the third from the Bézout inequality (
2.1
).
Hence,

|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|\leq\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1}.

From this we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(W(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(W(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|W(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1}}.

On the other hand, for
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}((V\setminus W)(\mathbb{F}))
and any

j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{I}
, the polynomial

f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}},X_{j})
is nonzero and has a
finite number of roots in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
. Thus the fiber

\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})
is finite. Moreover,

\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})=V(\mathbb{F})\cap\{X_{i}=y_{i}\ (i\in\mathcal{I})\}\subset V\cap\{X_{i}=y_{i}\ (i\in\mathcal{I})\}.

By definition of degree,

|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|\leq\big|V\cap\{X_{i}=y_{i}\ (i\in\mathcal{I})\}\big|\leq\delta.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}((V\setminus W)(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y_{\mathcal{I}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{I}}((V\setminus W)(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{I}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{|(V\setminus W)(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|W(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1}}+\frac{|(V\setminus W)(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\ln(|V(\mathbb{F})|)-\ln(\delta^{2})-\frac{|W(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln(q^{m-1})

\displaystyle=m\ln q+\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})-\ln(\delta^{2})-\frac{n\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}\ln(q^{m-1}),

where the lower bound for
\ln(|V(\mathbb{F})|)
comes from Lemma

3.5
:

\ln(|V(\mathbb{F})|)\geq\ln(q^{m}-5\delta^{7/3}q^{m-1/2})=m\ln q+\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}}).

Since
m:=r(\mathcal{E})=|\mathcal{I}|=r(\mathcal{I})
, we obtain

0\geq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})-r(\mathcal{I})=\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}})}{\ln|\mathbb{F}|}-|\mathcal{I}|\geq\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}-\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{\ln q}-\frac{nm\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}.

Summarizing, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
be an algebraic matroid defined over
a finite field
\mathbb{G}
, let
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
be the
associated variety, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{G}

as in Lemma
3.5
. For a base

\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{E}
, with
n:=|\mathcal{E}|
,

m:=r(\mathcal{E})
,
\delta:=\deg(V)
and
q:=|\mathbb{F}|
, we have

|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})-r(\mathcal{I})|\leq-\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}+\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{\ln q}+\frac{nm\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}.

As in
[
11
]
, once the case of bases has been established,
the extension to arbitrary independent sets follows by exploiting
the submodularity of entropy.
In our setting, this allows us to transfer the explicit bounds obtained
for bases to any independent set, thereby refining Matú
\check{\mathrm{s}}
’ original
approximation scheme with effective constants depending only on
the degree and dimension of the associated variety
V
.

Let
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{E}
be independent but not a base, and let

\mathcal{I}
be a base containing
\mathcal{J}
.
By the submodularity of entropy,

(4.4)

H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}})\leq H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})+H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}}).

As
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}}
has support

\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}}
, it follows that

H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}})\leq\ln|\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}}|=|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|\ln|\mathbb{F}|
. Therefore, dividing by
\ln|\mathbb{F}|
and
subtracting
|\mathcal{I}|
in (
4.4
),
and using that
r(\mathcal{J})=|\mathcal{J}|
,
we obtain

\displaystyle 0\geq\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})}{\ln\mathbb{F}}-r(\mathcal{J})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}})}{\ln\mathbb{F}}-|\mathcal{J}|-|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|=h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})-r(\mathcal{I}).

From Theorem
4.1
we deduce the following
corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any independent set
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{E}
, with hypotheses
and notations as in
Theorem
4.1
, the following estimate holds:

|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})-r(\mathcal{J})|\leq-\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}+\frac{\ln(\delta^{2})}{\ln q}+\frac{nm\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}.

4.2.2.
Dependent sets

In the previous subsection we obtained upper bounds for the
deviation
|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})-r(\mathcal{I})|
when
\mathcal{I}
is
an independent set. We now turn to the case of dependent sets.
Following the approach of
[
11
]
, we first focus on the
fundamental case of circuits: we establish a sharp bound for the
conditional entropy
H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})
when

\{j\}\cup\mathcal{J}
forms a circuit. This estimate is then used to
control the excess
h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})-r(\mathcal{C})
for any circuit

\mathcal{C}
, and subsequently extended to arbitrary dependent sets.
Our contribution lies in making this strategy quantitative, by
deriving a uniform explicit bound for the deviation

|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})|
valid for every subset

\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{E}
, expressed in terms of
\delta
,
n
,
and
q
.

Consider a circuit
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{E}
.
According to Proposition
3.6
, there exists a
nonzero absolutely irreducible polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]
of degree at most
\delta
such that

f_{\mathcal{C}}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{C}})=0.

For
j\in\mathcal{C}
,
f_{\mathcal{C}}
may be viewed as a polynomial
in the indeterminate
X_{j}
of degree
d_{j,\mathcal{C}}\leq\delta
,
with coefficients in
\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}]
,
where
\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}
.
In this setting we have

H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}})=H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}},\mathbb{P}_{\{j\}})=H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})+H(\mathbb{P}_{\{j\}}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}}).

We denote
\mathbb{P}_{j}:=\mathbb{P}_{\{j\}}
. By definition,

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}}(S_{\mathcal{J}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\mathbb{P}_{j}(S_{j}=y_{j}|S_{\mathcal{J}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})\ln\mathbb{P}_{j}(S_{j}=y_{j}|S_{\mathcal{J}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{\mathcal{C}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})\ln\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{\mathcal{C}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})}{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}}(S_{\mathcal{J}}=\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}))}

Observe that

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{\mathcal{C}}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})=\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|},\quad{\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}}(S_{\mathcal{J}}=\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}))}=\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}))|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{C}}))|}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}.

To bound
H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})
, we analyze the
inner sum for each
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}
, distinguishing whether the
specialization
f_{\mathcal{C}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},X_{j})
vanishes
identically or not. Denote by
W_{j,\mathcal{C}}
the set of
\boldsymbol{x}\in V
for which
f_{\mathcal{C}}(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{x}),X_{j})
is
the zero polynomial.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(V(\mathbb{F}))
, with

d_{j,\mathcal{C}}:=\deg f_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}
, we have

\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\ln q\textrm{\hskip 10.22217ptfor }\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})),\\[3.00003pt]
\ln d_{j,\mathcal{C}}\textrm{\hskip 10.22217ptotherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

If
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F}))
,
then the polynomial
f_{\mathcal{C}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},X_{j})
vanishes
identically and thus the fiber
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})
decomposes as the disjoint union

\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})=\bigcup_{y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}}\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j}),

where all the sets in the right-hand side have the same cardinality.
As a consequence,

|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|=\sum_{y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}}|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|=q\,|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|,

for any
y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}
, which readily implies

\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}=\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln q=\ln q.

On the other hand, if
\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\notin\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F}))
, then
f_{\mathcal{C}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},X_{j})
has at most
d_{j,\mathcal{C}}
roots in
\mathbb{F}
.
Hence the fiber
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})

decomposes as the disjoint union

\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})=\bigcup_{y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}:f_{\mathcal{C}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})=0}\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j}).

If
A_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}}:=\{y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}:f_{\mathcal{C}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})=0\}
, then

|A_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}}|\leq d_{j,\mathcal{C}}
, and it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}

\displaystyle\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}=\sum_{y_{j}\in A_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}.

This sum is maximized when all the fibers
\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})
have the same cardinality, in which case is upper
bounded by the logarithm of the number of
summands. As this number is at most
d_{j,\mathcal{C}}
, we easily
conclude that

\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}\leq\ln d_{j,\mathcal{C}},

as claimed.
∎

Now we proceed to obtain an upper bound for
H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
q:=|\mathbb{F}|>(5\delta^{7/3}+1)^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
circuit of
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
. Let
j\in\mathcal{C}
and

\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}
. Then

H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}\ln q+\ln\delta.

Proof.

From (
4.5
) we have

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\sum_{y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(V(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}\ln\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|\pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}},y_{j})|}.

Since

\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(V(\mathbb{F}))=\pi_{\mathcal{J}}\big(W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})\big)\,\cup\,\pi_{\mathcal{J}}\big((V\setminus W_{j,\mathcal{C}})(\mathbb{F})\big)
, we can
decompose the sum into two parts. Applying Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln q+\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}}\in\pi_{\mathcal{J}}((V\setminus W_{j,\mathcal{C}})(\mathbb{F}))}\frac{|\pi_{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathcal{J}})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln d_{j,\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln q+\ln d_{j,\mathcal{C}}

Next we bound
|W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})|
. Recall that

W_{j,\mathcal{C}}
is the set of
\boldsymbol{x}\in V
for which

f_{\mathcal{C}}(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\boldsymbol{x}),X_{j})=0
. The polynomial

f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{C}]
is absolutely
irreducible and lies in
\mathbb{G}[(X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}][X_{j}]\setminus\mathbb{G}[X_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}]
.
Writing

f_{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}m_{h}X_{j}^{h},

with
m_{h}\in\mathbb{G}[(X_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{J}}]
nonzero for all
h
, we
note that the algebraic independence of
\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{J}\}

implies
m_{h}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{J}})\not=0
. Hence
m_{h}\notin I_{\mathbb{G}}
, and since
V
is absolutely irreducible,
V\cap\{m_{h}=0\}

has pure codimension one in
V
or is empty. Therefore

W_{j,\mathcal{C}}\subset V\cap\{m_{h}=0\}.

Applying (
2.2
), together with
(
2.1
) and Proposition
3.6
, we
obtain

\displaystyle|W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})|\leq|(V\cap\{m_{h}=0\})(\mathbb{F})|

\displaystyle\leq\deg(V\cap\{m_{h}=0\})q^{\dim V-1}

\displaystyle\leq\deg(V)\deg(m_{h})\,q^{\dim V-1}\leq\delta^{2}\,q^{m-1}.

Finally, by Lemma
3.5
,

|V(\mathbb{F})|\geq q^{m}-5\delta^{7/3}q^{m-1/2}.

The assumption on
q
implies that the right-hand side is greater
than
q^{m-1/2}
(see Lemma
3.5
). We
thus conclude

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|W_{j,\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{F})|}{|V(\mathbb{F})|}\ln q+\ln d_{j,\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q-5\delta^{7/3}q^{1/2}}\ln q+\ln\delta\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}\ln q+\ln\delta,

as claimed.
∎

Now we are able to obtain an upper bound on the difference

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})-r(\mathcal{C})
.

Corollary 4.5
.

With the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, and with
h_{\mathbb{F}}:2^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as in
(
4.1
), for any circuit
\mathcal{C}
we
have

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})-r(\mathcal{C})\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{C}\setminus\{j\}
for a given

j\in\mathcal{C}
. By definition,

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{A}):=\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}})}{\ln|\mathbb{F}|}
for
any
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
. For a circuit
\mathcal{C}
and

\mathcal{J}
as above, we have

H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}})-H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})=H(\mathbb{P}_{j}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}\ln q+\ln\delta,

by Proposition
4.4
. Dividing by
\ln q
yields

(4.6)

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}.

Finally,

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})-\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})=\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{J}})}{\ln q}\leq\frac{\ln|\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{J}}|}{\ln q}=|\mathcal{J}|=r(\mathcal{C}),

which proves the claim.
∎

Next we obtain a corresponding bound for an arbitrary dependent set.

Lemma 4.6
.

With the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, for any dependent set
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{E}
we
have

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})\leq|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a dependent subset of
\mathcal{E}
, and let

\mathcal{J}
be a maximal independent set contained in

\mathcal{K}
. For
k\in\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{J}
, let

\gamma(k,\mathcal{J})
be the unique circuit contained in
k\cup\mathcal{J}
. By the submodularity of the entropy,

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\gamma(k,\mathcal{J}))-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\gamma(k,\mathcal{J})\setminus\{k\})\geq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J}\cup\{k\})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J}).

Setting
\{k_{1},\dots,k_{t}\}:=\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{J}

with
t\geq 1
, and considering the chain

\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{J}\cup\{k_{1}\}\subset\mathcal{J}\cup\{k_{1},k_{2}\}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{J}\cup\{k_{1},\dots,k_{t}\}=\mathcal{K},

by the submodularity of entropy we deduce

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\big(h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K}\setminus\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1}\})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K}\setminus\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}\})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\big(h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J}\cup\{k_{i}\})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\big(h_{\mathbb{F}}(\gamma(k_{i},\mathcal{J}))-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\gamma(k_{i},\mathcal{J})\setminus\{k_{i}\})\big).

Applying (
4.6
) to each term yields

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})\leq t\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)\leq|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg).

Thus

(4.7)

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})\leq|\mathcal{J}|=r(\mathcal{K}).

This implies the lemma.
∎

Now we derive a corresponding lower bound for

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})
. Observe that

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\ln|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\ln q}
. By Lemma
3.5
,

|V(\mathbb{F})|\geq q^{m}-5\delta^{7/3}q^{m-1/2}=q^{m}\bigg(1-\frac{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{1/2}}\bigg),

so that

h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\ln|V(\mathbb{F})|}{\ln q}\geq m+\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}.

Applying (
4.7
) with
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{I}
a maximal independent subset, for any

\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I}
, we have
h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})+h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J})
, and
therefore

\displaystyle m+\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{I})+|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})+|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|+|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg),

since
\frac{H(\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}})}{\ln q}\leq\frac{\ln|\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}}|}{\ln q}=|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|
. Hence

(4.8)

m-|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|+\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}-|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J}).

Finally, let
\mathcal{K}
be any dependent set, let
\mathcal{J}

be a maximal independent subset of
\mathcal{K}
, and let

\mathcal{I}
be a maximal independent subset of
\mathcal{E}

containing
\mathcal{J}
. Since
r(\mathcal{E})-|\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{J}|=r(\mathcal{K})
, from (
4.8
) we deduce

r(\mathcal{K})+\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}-|\mathcal{E}|\bigg(\frac{\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{\ln\delta}{\ln q}\bigg)\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{J})\leq h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K}),

which gives the desired lower bound.

Combining Lemma
4.6
with the
lower bound derived from Lemma
3.5
,
we obtain the following quantitative refinement of Matú
\check{\textrm{s}}
’s
approximation scheme.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
be an algebraic matroid defined over
a finite field
\mathbb{G}
, let
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
be the variety
associated to
{\sf M}
, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{G}

as in Lemma
3.5
. For an arbitrary subset

\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{E}
, with
n:=|\mathcal{E}|
,

\delta:=\deg(V)
and
q:=|\mathbb{F}|
, we have

|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})|\leq\frac{n\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}+\frac{n\ln\delta}{\ln q}-\frac{\ln(1-{5\delta^{7/3}}{q^{-1/2}})}{\ln q}.

This theorem shows that the qualitative scheme of

[
11
]
—first controlling circuits and then extending to
arbitrary dependent sets–—admits an explicit quantitative version.
Our contribution lies in providing effective constants depending
only on
n
,
\delta
, and
q
, thereby turning the almost entropic
approximation of algebraic matroids into a concrete, computable
bound.

5.
Conclusions

We established explicit, uniform bounds comparing the entropy
polymatroid
h_{\mathbb{F}}
of the evaluation distribution on
V(\mathbb{F})
with
the rank function
r
of the algebraic matroid
{\sf M}:=(\mathcal{E},r)
under consideration, where

V\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\overline{\mathbb{G}}}
is the associated variety and
\mathbb{F}
is
a finite extension of the finite field
\mathbb{G}
of definition of
{\sf M}
. In particular, for every
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{E}
,

\bigl|h_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{K})-r(\mathcal{K})\bigr|\;\leq\;\frac{nm\delta^{2}}{q^{1/2}}\;+\;\frac{nm\ln\delta^{2}}{\ln q}\;-\;\frac{\ln\!\bigl(1-5\delta^{7/3}q^{-1/2}\bigr)}{\ln q}.

Hence, as
q:=|\mathbb{F}|\to\infty
, the discrepancy tends to
0
with rate

\mathcal{O}(1/\ln q)
, with constants depending only on

n:=|\mathcal{E}|
and
\delta:=\deg(V)
. The argument proceeds by
first controlling independent sets, then circuits via a conditional
entropy bound, and finally extending to arbitrary dependent sets by
submodularity.

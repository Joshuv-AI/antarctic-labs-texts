Stochastic Control for Fine-tuning Diffusion Models: Optimality, Regularity, and Convergence
Yinbin Han, Meisam Razaviyayn, Renyuan Xu

Abstract
Diffusion models have emerged as powerful tools for generative modeling, demonstrating exceptional capability in capturing target data distributions from large datasets. However, fine-tuning these massive models for specific downstream tasks, constraints, and human preferences remains a critical challenge. While recent advances have leveraged reinforcement learning algorithms to tackle this problem, much of the progress has been empirical, with limited theoretical understanding. To bridge this gap, we propose a stochastic control framework for fine-tuning diffusion models. Building on denoising diffusion probabilistic models as the pre-trained reference dynamics, our approach integrates linear dynamics control with Kullback–Leibler regularization. We establish the well-posedness and regularity of the stochastic control problem and develop a policy iteration algorithm (PI-FT) for numerical solution. We show that PI-FT achieves global convergence at a linear rate. Unlike existing work that assumes regularities throughout training, we prove that the control and value sequences generated by the algorithm maintain the regularity. Additionally, we explore extensions of our framework to parametric settings and continuous-time formulations and demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed PI-FT algorithm through numerical experiments. Our code is available at https://github.com/yinbinhan/fine-tuning-of-diffusion-models .

Our work and contributions.

We introduce a discrete-time stochastic control formulation with linear dynamics and Kullback–Leibler (KL) regularization for fine-tuning diffusion models. Specifically, we establish a novel connection to denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)
[
32
]
by treating the fine-tuned score as a
control to be learned
. In particular, the soft constraint or human preference is modeled by a reward signal evaluated on the generated data output and the KL regularization term penalizes the deviation of the control from the pre-trained score function. We utilize the discrete-time formulation since fine-tuning is
inherently
a
discrete-time
problem, as it relies on a pre-trained model which is typically implemented in discrete time, with DDPM being the most commonly-used example. By appropriately selecting the regularization parameter, we demonstrate the well-posedness of the control problem and analyze the regularity of the optimal value function. A key insight arises from the concavity of the nested one-step optimization problem in the Bellman equation, a direct result of KL regularization under a properly chosen regularization coefficient. Leveraging this property, we develop a policy iteration algorithm (PI-FT) with guaranteed convergence to the globally optimal solution at a linear rate. A central challenge involves preserving the regularity of the sequence of value functions and output controls generated during algorithm iterations. This is achieved through a novel coupled induction argument and precise estimations of the regularization parameters. Finally, we discuss the algorithm design in the parametric setting and the connection to continuous-time stochastic control problems, and demonstrate the practical
efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed PI-FT method.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work on fine-tuning of diffusion models that presents a
convergence guarantee
.
While reinforcement learning (RL) and control-based fine-tuning frameworks have been explored in prior literature, analyzing algorithm convergence remains challenging due to the continuous nature of the state-action space.
Our approach addresses this challenge by leveraging linear dynamics and the (one-step) concavity introduced by the KL regularization term, which inherently captures the essence of fine-tuning problems. Specifically, we establish the universal regularity of both the optimal value function and the sequence of value functions generated during iterative update. Moreover, the foundational techniques underlying our results extend naturally to parametric algorithm design and continuous-time formulations, offering insights beyond the specific context of diffusion model fine-tuning.

Continuous-time control/RL for fine-tuning.

Inspired by the continuous-time nature of diffusion processes, recent work has explored the application of continuous-time control/RL for fine-tuning. For instance,
Berner et al. [4]
established a connection between fine-tuning and stochastic control by analyzing the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation for the log density.
Domingo-Enrich et al. [15]

Compared with the literature that technically remain at the level of exploring HJB equations, we establish convergence rate for the proposed algorithm in discrete time. Despite the continuous-time nature of diffusion processes, the discrete-time setting is more suitable because fine-tuning builds upon a pre-trained model, which is naturally implemented and provided in discrete time.

Problem set-up

Remark 2.2

(Control as score function)

Our formulation is rooted in both diffusion models and control theory. Comparing (

Remark 2.3

(Rationale for the objective function and the known reward assumption)

The objective function in the control formulation(
2

3

In practice, fine-tuning is typically performed on a dataset containing reward ratings for each data sample, which is much smaller than the pre-trained dataset. Using this fine-tuning dataset, one can estimate the expected reward function. The assumption of known reward is not overly restrictive, as online learning to acquire new rewards is generally uncommon.

V_{t}^{*}
satisfies the Bellman equation:

\displaystyle V^{*}_{t}(y)=\sup_{u_{t}}\ \mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left\|u_{t}(y)-s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right].

(9)

In the next section, we will discuss the well-posedness of the optimal control problem (
2
)–(
3
) and the regularity of the optimal value function
V_{t}^{*}
.

2.2
Regularity and well-posedness

In this section, we establish the regularity of the optimal value function and the optimal control policy. To start, we assume the following assumptions on the reward and pre-trained score functions.

Assumption 2.6

(Smoothness of the reward)
.

Assume
r(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[R(y)]
is
L_{0}^{r}
-Lipschitz and
L_{1}^{r}
-gradient Lipschitz in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|r(y_{1})-r(y_{2})\right|\leq L_{0}^{r}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

(10)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla r(y_{1})-\nabla r(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq L_{1}^{r}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.

(11)

Assumption 2.7

(Smoothness of the pre-trained score function)
.

Assume
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
is
L_{0}^{s}
-Lipschitz and
L_{1}^{s}
-gradient Lipschitz in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq L_{0,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

(12)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq L_{1,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.

(13)

While Assumptions
2.6
and
2.7
guarantee the smoothness of both the expected reward
r
and the pre-trained score
\left\{s_{t}^{\rm pre}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, no convexity assumptions are imposed, and thus the control problem (
2
)–(
3
) is in general
non-concave
with respect to
\left\{u_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
.

The next theorem establishes the existence and uniqueness of the optimal control, as well as the regularities of the solution to problem (
2
)–(
3
). For ease of exposition, we define a series of constants recursively. Let
0<\lambda_{t}<1
for each
t<T
. Set
L_{0,T}^{V^{*}}=L_{0}^{r}
and
L_{1,T}^{V^{*}}=L_{1}^{r}
. For
t<T
, define

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{V^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}},

(14)

\displaystyle L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{s}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}},

(15)

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left(L_{0,t}^{s}+\frac{1-\lambda_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left(L_{1,t}^{s}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right](1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\right).

(17)

Here,
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Gamma((d+1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}<\infty
is a constant with
\Gamma(\cdot)
being the Gamma function. Note that the above constants only depend on the system parameters
\alpha_{t}
,
L_{0}^{r}
,
L_{1}^{r}
,
L_{0}^{s}
and
L_{1}^{s}
as well as the hyper-parameter
\lambda_{t}
. In the next theorem, we show that the above constants are indeed the Lipschitz coefficients for
\left\{V_{t}^{*}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
and
\left\{u_{t}^{*}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
.

Theorem 2.8

(Regularity and well-posedness)
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.6
and
2.7
hold and let the constants be defined according to (
14
)–(
17
). Choose
\beta_{t}
such that
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\geq\lambda_{t}>0
. Then,

(i)

The optimal value function
V_{t}^{*}(y)
defined in (
8
) is
L_{0,t}^{V^{*}}
-Lipschitz, differentiable and
L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}
-gradient Lipschitz in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

There is a unique optimal control
u_{t}^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of problem (
2
)–(
3
) satisfying

\displaystyle u_{t}^{*}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

(18)

Moreover, the optimal value function
V_{t}^{*}
is the unique
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solution to the Bellman equation (
9
).

(iii)

The optimal control
u_{t}^{*}(y)
is
L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}
-Lipschitz, differentiable, and
L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}
-gradient Lipschitz in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

When the regularization coefficient
\beta_{t}
is sufficiently large, Theorem
2.8
states that the optimal value function
V_{t}^{*}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz and gradient Lipschitz. The choice of
\beta_{t}
also ensures the right hand side of (
9
) is strongly concave in
u_{t}(\cdot)
, guaranteeing the existence and uniqueness of the optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
. Furthermore, Theorem
2.8
shows the optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
is also Lipschitz and gradient Lipschitz. The regularities of
V_{t}^{*}
and
u_{t}^{*}
will serve as the foundation of the algorithm design and convergence analysis in the subsequent sections.

We outline a brief proof sketch to highlight the ideas before providing the detailed proof, which is based on backward induction. Assuming
V_{t+1}^{*}
is Lipschitz and gradient Lipschitz, the first-order optimality condition implies that (
18
) holds, given the optimization problem is unconstrained. Direct calculation leads to the Lipchitz condition of
u_{t}^{*}
and the smoothing effect of the Gaussian random variable is utilized to obtain differentiability. In addition, the Lipschitz property of
\nabla u_{t}^{*}
is a consequence of the integration by parts formula in (
26
); hence (
iii
) holds. Finally, the regularities of
V_{t}^{*}
follow from the Lipschitz conditions of
u_{t}^{*}
and
\nabla u_{t}^{*}
, which completes the induction.

Proof.

We prove Theorem
2.8
by backward induction. At time
t=T
, Assumption
2.6
implies
V_{T}^{*}(y)=R(y)
is
L_{0,T}^{V^{*}}
-Lipschitz and
L_{1,T}^{V^{*}}
-gradient Lipschitz with
L_{0,T}^{V^{*}}=L_{0}^{r}
and
L_{1,T}^{V^{*}}=L_{1}^{r}
.

Step 1: Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{*}
.
Assume that
V^{*}_{t+1}
is
L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}
-Lipschitz and
L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}
-gradient Lipschitz in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The choice of
\beta_{t}
implies that the mapping

\displaystyle u\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left\|u-s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

(19)

is
\gamma_{t}
-strongly concave with
\gamma_{t}=\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\left(\frac{\beta_{t}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\right)>0
. Hence, there is a unique optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
satisfying

\displaystyle u_{t}^{*}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right],

(20)

which is obtained by setting the gradient of mapping (
19
) as zero for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here, we apply the Lipschitz condition of
V_{t+1}^{*}
and the dominated convergence theorem to interchange the operators.

We next prove the Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{*}
in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that for any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Eq. (
20
) implies

\displaystyle u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{1}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{2}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)\right].

Utilizing the Lipschitz condition of
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
and
\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{1}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{2}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)\Big\|_{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq L_{0,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{1}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y_{2}+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{0,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\right).

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L^{V^{*}}_{1,t+1}\right)\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(L_{0,t}^{s}+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}L^{V^{*}}_{1,t+1}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\right)\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.

Since
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L^{V^{*}}_{1,t+1}\geq\lambda_{t}>0
, we established the Lipschitz condition of the optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left(L_{0,t}^{s}+\frac{1-\lambda_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\right)\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}=L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.,

where we recall
L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}
defined in (
16
).

Step 2: Differentiability of
u_{t}^{*}
.
We now argue that
u_{t}^{*}(y)
is differentiable for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary non-zero vector and let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be fixed. Since
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
is differentiable, we have

\displaystyle s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y+h)-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)h+o(\left\|h\right\|_{2}).

(21)

Moreover, by the inductive hypothesis,
\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}(y)
is Lipschitz and thus
\nabla^{2}V_{t+1}^{*}(y)
exists for almost all
y
and is bounded.
[
22
, Theorem 2.27]
implies the mapping
z\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right]
is differentiable everywhere and its derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{2}V^{*}_{t+1}(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right].

(22)

Let
z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)
and
k=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(h+(1-\alpha_{t})(u_{t}^{*}(y+h)-u_{t}^{*}(y))
. Eq. (
22
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}(z+k+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{2}V_{t+1}^{*}(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right]k+o(\left\|k\right\|_{2}).

(23)

Since
u_{t}^{*}
is Lipschitz, we have
k=\mathcal{O}(\left\|h\right\|_{2})
as
\left\|h\right\|_{2}\to 0
. Combining (
21
) and (
23
), we obtain

\displaystyle u_{t}^{*}(y+h)-u_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y+h)-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left((y+h)+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y+h)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)h+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(h+(1-\alpha_{t})(u_{t}^{*}(y+h)-u_{t}^{*}(y))\right)+o(\left\|h\right\|_{2}),

where
\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{2}V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]
.
Re-arranging the terms leads to

\displaystyle u_{t}^{*}(y+h)-u_{t}^{*}(y)=\left(I_{d}-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right)^{-1}\left(\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right)h+o(\left\|h\right\|_{2}),

which proves that
u_{t}^{*}(y)
is differentiable for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and its derivative is given by

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)=\left(I_{d}-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right)^{-1}\left(\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right).

(24)

Step 3: Lipschitz condition of
\nabla u_{t}^{*}
.
Next, we show that
\nabla u_{t}^{*}
is Lipschitz. Taking the derivative of both sides of (
20
),

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

(25)

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the integration by parts formula implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{2}{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\frac{W_{t}\top}{\sigma_{t}}\right].

(26)

Substituting
z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)
and applying the chain rule, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]=\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y)\mathcal{U}_{t}(y),

where
\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\frac{W_{t}^{\top}}{\sigma_{t}}\right]
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}(y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)
. It follows from (
25
) that

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y)\mathcal{U}_{t}(y).

(27)

For any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Eq. (
27
) implies that

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\big(\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{1})\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{1})-\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{2})\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{2})\big).

(28)

Note that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}=\left\|\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}},

(29)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right),

(30)

where (
29
) holds by applying the identity (
26
), and (
30
) is a consequence of the Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{*}
.
Moreover, for any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Lipschitz conditions of
\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}
and
u_{t}^{*}
imply that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{1})-\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\sigma_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{{V}^{*}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.

(31)

Furthermore, the definition of
\mathcal{U}_{t}
implies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{1})-\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}.

(32)

Combining (
28
)–(
32
) together, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\left\|\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{1})-\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{1})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\left\|\mathcal{W}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{1})-\mathcal{U}_{t}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\bigg(L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\sigma_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}\bigg).

Since
\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\leq 1-\lambda_{t}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left(L_{1,t}^{s}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right](1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\right)\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

where we recall
L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}
defined in (
17
).

Step 4: Lipschitz conditions of
V_{t}^{*}
and
\nabla V_{t}^{*}
.
Finally, we turn to prove the Lipschitz and gradient Lipschitz conditions of
V_{t}^{*}
. Plugging
u_{t}^{*}
into the Bellman equation (
9
), we have

\displaystyle V_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y)-s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(33)

Since
V_{t+1}^{*}
and
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
are differentiable with Lipschitz gradients and
u_{t}^{*}
is differentiable, we know that
V_{t}^{*}
is differentiable and

\displaystyle\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[V^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left\|u_{t}^{*}(y)-s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)^{\top}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}(\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)-\nabla s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y))^{\top}(u_{t}^{*}-s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)).

Define
\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)\right)^{\top}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}(\nabla u_{t}^{*}(y)-\nabla s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y))^{\top}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right)

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\right)^{\top}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y),

where (
20
) is applied to obtain the first equality. Next, we use (
34
) to establish the Lipschitz conditions of
V_{t}^{*}
and
\nabla V_{t}^{*}
. The Lipschitz condition of
\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}
implies
\left\|\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Based on (
34
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}=L_{0,t}^{V^{*}},

which proves the Lipschitz condition of
V_{t}^{*}
. Next, we prove the gradient Lipschitz condition of
V_{t}^{*}
. For ease of exposition, we denote
\mathcal{S}_{t}(y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\right)
. We now use (
34
) to show the Lipschitz condition of
\nabla V_{t}^{*}
. We first note that
\nabla\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)=\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\mathcal{U}_{t}(y)
is well-defined at every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and thus

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}.

With the Lipschitz condition of
\mathcal{G}_{t+1}
in hand, for any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{1})^{\top}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{1})-\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{2})^{\top}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{1})^{\top}\left(\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{1})-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{1})-\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{2})\right)^{\top}\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{1})\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{1})-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{1})-\mathcal{S}_{t}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{s}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

where the last equation uses the Lipschitz conditions of
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
,
\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}
,
V_{t+1}^{*}
and
\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

where we recall
L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}
defined in (
15
). In other words,
L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}
is indeed the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla V_{t}^{*}
. This completes the proof.

∎

3
Algorithm development and convergence analysis

In this section, we propose an iterative algorithm for fine-tuning and provide non-asymptotic analysis of its convergence. Assuming that the expected reward function
r(\cdot)
is known, we develop an iterative algorithm to approximate the optimal policy in high-dimensional settings, suitable for practical implementation. In practice,
r(\cdot)
is approximated using a small sample set with human feedback, which then serves as the basis for fine-tuning. The unknown
r(\cdot)
case is left as a topic for future investigation.

3.1
Proposed algorithm

Recall that the optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
satisfies (
18
). Motivated by this observation, we propose the following iterative algorithm for fine-tuning and computing the optimal control
u_{t}^{*}
.

Algorithm 1

Policy Iteration for Fine-Tuning (PI-FT)

1:

Input:
Expected reward function
r(\cdot)
, pre-trained model
\left\{s_{t}^{\rm pre}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
, and number of iterations
m_{t}
at each timestamp
t

(0\leq t\leq T-1)
.

2:

Set
V_{T}^{(m_{T})}(y)=r(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

3:

for

t=T-1,\dots,0

do

4:

Set
u_{t}^{(0)}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)
.

5:

for

m=1,\dots,m_{t}-1

do

6:

Update the control using

\displaystyle u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}_{t+1}^{(m_{t+1})}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

(35)

7:

end

for

8:

Compute the value function
V_{t}^{(m_{t})}
using

\displaystyle V_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[{V}^{(m_{t+1})}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\|u_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\|^{2}\right].

(36)

9:

end

for

10:

return

\left\{u_{t}^{(m_{t})}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
and
\left\{V_{t}^{(m_{t})}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}

Algorithm
1
consists of two nested loops. The inner loop updates the control at each iteration
m
and the outer loop computes the value function at time
t
. Given the value function
V_{t+1}^{(m_{t+1})}
at time step
t+1
, we update
u_{t}^{(m)}
using (
35
) and then evaluate the associated value function using (
36
). Note that (
35
) can be viewed as an approximation of the following update rule:

\displaystyle u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right],

(37)

and (

) can be seen as an approximation of the Bellman equation (
9
), where we replace the unknown
V_{t+1}^{*}
with
V_{t+1}^{(m_{t+1})}
.
Direct calculation shows that the fixed-point update rule (
37
) guarantees the convergence of
\left\{u_{t}^{(m)}\right\}_{m\geq 0}
to
u_{t}^{*}
; see (
67
). However, in practice we cannot access
V_{t+1}^{*}
. Hence, Eq. (

) as long as we can control the error
\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}
for all

. With
u_{t}^{(m_{t})}
and
V_{t+1}^{(m_{t+1})}

36

V_{t}^{*}
.

3.2
Convergence analysis

In this section, we analyze the convergence of Algorithm
1
. For a fixed
\lambda_{t}\in(0,1)
, define a series of constants recursively as follows. Let
L_{0,T}^{\bar{V}}=L_{0}^{r}
and
L_{1,T}^{\bar{V}}=L_{1}^{r}
. For each
t<T
, set

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{\bar{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})L_{0,t+1}^{\bar{V}}+\frac{L_{0,t+1}^{\bar{V}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)(1-\lambda_{t}),

(38)

\displaystyle\text{and}\;\;L_{1,t}^{\bar{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}})L_{1,t+1}^{\bar{V}}+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{s}L_{0,t+1}^{\bar{V}}

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{m\geq 1}\left\{\bigg(\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}+(m+2)\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}}\bigg)L_{0,t+1}^{\bar{V}}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}\right\}.

(39)

Here, the constant
L_{1,t}^{\bar{V}}
is well-defined as the maximum must be achieved for some
m<\infty
due to the exponential decay of
(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}
.
Similar to the constants defined in (
14
)–(
17
), the above constants only depend on the system parameters
\alpha_{t}
,
L_{0}^{r}
,
L_{1}^{r}
,
L_{0}^{s}
and
L_{1}^{s}
as well as the hyper-parameter
\lambda_{t}
. Later, we will show that the constants defined in (
38
) and (
39
) indeed serve as the Lipschitz coefficients of
\left\{V_{t}^{(m)}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
universal over all
m\geq 0
.
The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.6
and
2.7
hold.
For each
t<T
, choose
\beta_{t}
such that
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\bar{V}}\geq\lambda_{t}>0
. Let
\left\{u_{t}^{(m_{t})}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
and
\left\{V_{t}^{(m_{t})}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
be the output of Algorithm
1
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m_{t}}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}},

(40)

where

\displaystyle E_{t}\coloneqq\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\left(\prod_{\ell=t}^{k-1}C_{1,\ell}\right)C_{2,k}(1-\lambda_{k})^{m_{k}+1},

(41)

and

\displaystyle C_{1,t}=\frac{1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s}}{\lambda_{t}\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}},\;\;\text{and}\;\;C_{2,t}=\frac{\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{0,t+1}^{\bar{V}}+\frac{\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s}\right)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}.

To obtain a simplified convergence rate from Theorem
3.1
, we set
\lambda_{t}\equiv\lambda\coloneqq\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}\lambda_{t}
and
m_{t}\equiv M
for all
t
. In this case, we have
E_{t}=\mathcal{O}((1-\lambda)^{M+1})
and consequently
\left\|u_{t}^{(M)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}=\mathcal{O}((1-\lambda)^{M})
. In other words, the control sequence
\left\{u_{t}^{(m)}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
converges to the optimal control at a linear rate. Note that the parameter
\lambda
determines the convergence rate. In particular, setting a larger

\lambda
results in faster convergence. However, when

\lambda
is large, Theorem
3.1
requires that the regularization parameter

\beta_{t}
must also be chosen to be sufficiently large. Recall that
\beta_{t}
controls the distance between the fine-tuned model and the pre-trained model, while a bigger
\lambda
may result in an optimal control far away from
s_{t}^{\rm pre}
. Therefore, choosing an appropriate
\lambda
(or equivalently
\beta_{t}
) is crucial to trade-off computational efficiency and closeness to the pre-trained model.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need a few intermediate results for one-step update rule. We begin with a lemma that characterizes the regularity of control and value functions after one update. Given a function
\widehat{V}_{t+1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the following update rule:

\displaystyle u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right],

(42)

with initialization
u_{t}^{(0)}(y)=s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)
. Furthermore, let
V_{t}^{(m)}
be the value function induced by
u^{(m)}_{t}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle V_{t}^{(m)}(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\|^{2}\right].

(43)

Later, we will choose
\widehat{V}_{t+1}=V_{t+1}^{(m_{t+1})}
to analyze the convergence of Algorithm
1
. With (
42
) and (
43
), we state the lemma as follows.

Lemma 3.2

(One-step regularity and universal upper bound)
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.6
and
2.7
hold.
Let
\widehat{V}_{t+1}
be a function that is
L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}
-Lipschitz and

-gradient Lipschitz. Consider
\left\{u_{t}^{(m)}\right\}_{m=0}^{m_{t}}
and
\left\{V_{t}^{(m)}\right\}_{m=0}^{m_{t}}
defined in (
42
) and (
43
). For
t<T
, choose
\beta_{t}
such that
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\geq\lambda_{t}>0
. Then it holds for every

that

(i)

u_{t}^{(m)}
is
L_{0,t}^{u^{(m)}}

-gradient Lipschitz, with coefficients
L_{0,t}^{u^{(m)}}

(ii)

\displaystyle L_{1,t}^{V^{(m)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s})(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}})L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{s}L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}+(m+2)\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}}\bigg)L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}.

provides regularity properties of the sequence of controls and value functions generated by (

are bounded by those of the optimal control

, Eqns. (

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{V^{(m)}}=L_{0,t}^{V^{*}}+\mathcal{O}((1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1})\;\;\text{and}\;\;L_{1,t}^{V^{(m)}}=L_{1,t}^{V^{*}}+\mathcal{O}((m+2)(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}).

The residual terms diminish to zero as

and thus providing the convergence of Lipschitz constants.
Consequently, selecting
\widehat{V}_{t+1}
as

We outline a brief proof sketch to highlight the ideas before providing the detailed proof. The Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{(m)}
is derived through direct calculation, while the gradient Lipschitz condition leverages the integration by parts formula (

V_{t}^{(m)}
requires a careful analysis of the gradient expression, along with a tightly controlled upper bound; see (

Proof.

Step 1: Lipschitz conditions of
u_{t}^{(m)}
and
\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}
.

We begin with the Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{(m)}
. For any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the update rule (
42
) implies

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y_{1})-u_{t}^{(m)}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}\right).

Since
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\geq\lambda_{t}
, unrolling the recursion leads to

\displaystyle L_{0,t}^{u^{(m+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{0,t}^{s}+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}_{t}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}L_{0,t}^{u^{(m)}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left(L_{0,t}^{s}+\frac{1-\lambda_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\right)=L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}.

Here, we also use the fact that
L_{0,t}^{u^{(0)}}=L_{0,t}^{s}
. Note that the condition
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\geq\lambda_{t}
is important as it decouples the upper bound, in the sense that the last line does not depend on
L^{\widehat{V}}_{1,t+1}
directly.

Next, since
u_{t}^{(0)}=s_{t}^{\rm pre}
is differentiable, a simple inductive argument shows that
\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}
is well-defined. Furthermore, we show that
\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}
is Lipschitz. Differentiate both sides of the update rule (
42
),

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

(47)

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the integration by parts formula implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{2}\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(z+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\frac{W_{t}\top}{\sigma_{t}}\right].

(48)

Substituting
z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)
and applying the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\frac{W_{t}\top}{\sigma_{t}}\right]\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)\right).

(49)

Define

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\frac{W_{t}\top}{\sigma_{t}}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right).

It follows from (
49
) that
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)=\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y)\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y)
. Consequently, Eq. (
47
) becomes

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)

\displaystyle=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y)\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y).

(50)

Since
\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}
and
u_{t}^{(m)}
are both Lipschitz, we
know that
\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}
is Lipschitz as long as
\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}
is Lipschitz. Specifically, for any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{1})-\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y_{2})+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{1})\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y_{1})-\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{2})\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y_{2})\right).

Note that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}},

(51)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right),

(52)

where the (
51
) holds by applying the identity (
48
), and (
52
) is a consequence of the Lipschitz condition of
u_{t}^{(m)}
.
Moreover, for any
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Lipschitz conditions of
\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}
and
u_{t}^{(m)}
imply that

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{1})-\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\sigma_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{(m)}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}\right)

(53)

and the Lipschitz condition of
\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{u^{(m)}}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}.

Combine (

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{1})-\nabla u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y_{2})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1,t}^{s}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\bigg(L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{u^{(m)}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\sigma_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\bigg)\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2},

Since

\displaystyle L_{1,t}^{u^{(m+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1,t}^{s}+\bigg(L_{1,t}^{u^{(m)}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{(1-\alpha_{t})\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}}\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\bigg)(1-\lambda_{t}).

Equivalently, we have

Step 2: Lipschitz conditions of
V_{t}^{(m)}
and
\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}\Big\|\left(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{1})\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y_{1})\right)^{\top}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y_{1})-\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m-1)}(y_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}^{{(m)}}(y_{2})\mathcal{U}^{(m)}_{t}(y_{2})\right)^{\top}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y_{2})-\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m-1)}(y_{2})\Big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}\bigg(\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{1,t}^{u^{*}}+(m+2)\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{t}\right\|_{2}\right]}{\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}}\bigg)L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m}\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{2}

Our next result characterizes the error of the control sequence obtained from the one-step update rule (

).

Lemma 3.3

(One-step error analysis on controls)
.

Assume the same assumptions as in Lemma
3.2
.
Moreover, suppose there is a constant
E_{t+1}
such that for all
y
that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}-\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}\right)(y)\right\|_{2}\leq E_{t+1}.

Let
u_{t}^{(m)}
and
V_{t}^{(m)}
be defined as in (
42
) and (
43
), respectively. Then it holds that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{n}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}}.

Proof.

Define the Bellman optimality operator
\mathcal{T}_{t}
at time
t
as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}_{t}u)(y)\coloneqq s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

Moreover, define the approximate Bellman operator
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}
as

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}u)(y)\coloneqq s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

The update rule (
42
) implies
u_{t}^{(m+1)}=\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)}
. Together with the optimality condition
u_{t}^{*}=\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{*}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{*})(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)\right\|_{2}+\left\|(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{*})(y)\right\|_{2}.

(66)

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{t}
is a contraction operator. Indeed for any
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{T}_{t}u)(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}v)(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Bigg\|\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})v(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\Bigg\|_{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\left\|u(y)-v(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{t})\left\|u(y)-v(y)\right\|_{2},

(67)

where we apply the Lipschitz condition of
\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}
and the choice of
\beta_{t}
. Consequently, the second term in (
66
) is bounded with

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{*})(y)\right\|_{2}\leq(1-\lambda_{t})\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}.

(68)

Next, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

and recall that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right].

For the first term in (
66
), note that

\displaystyle\left\|(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)-(\mathcal{T}_{t}u_{t}^{(m)})(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}-\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}\right)(y^{\prime})\right\|_{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}E_{t+1},

(69)

where
y^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)})(y)\right),\sigma_{t}^{2}I_{d}\right)
.

With (
68
) and (
69
), we can further bound (
66
) with

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m+1)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}E_{t+1}+(1-\lambda_{t})\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}.

(70)

Unrolling the recursion (
70
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{t})^{m}\left\|u_{t}^{(0)}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}E_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{(1-\alpha_{t})\beta_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}},

where we apply the condition that
1-\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\geq\lambda_{t}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

The last lemma in this section is on the error analysis of
\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}
.

Lemma 3.4

(One-step error analysis on gradient of the value function)
.

Assume the same assumptions as in Lemma
3.3
. Then it holds that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{1,t}E_{t+1}+C_{2,t}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1},

(71)

where

\displaystyle C_{1,t}\coloneqq\frac{1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s}}{\lambda_{t}\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}},\;\;\text{and}\;\;C_{2,t}\coloneqq\frac{\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}\right)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}+\frac{\left(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s}\right)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}.

Proof.

Recall the definition that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]
. It follows from (
34
) and (
55
) that

\displaystyle\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(I_{d}+(1-\alpha_{t})\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\right)^{\top}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(\nabla u_{t}^{(m)}(y)-\nabla s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)\right)^{\top}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m-1)}(y)\Big).

(72)

Observe that for any
m\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2},

(73)

where we recall
\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla{V}^{*}_{t+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)}(y)\right)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)\right]
.
For the first term on the RHS of (
73
), we notice the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(\nabla\widehat{V}_{t+1}-\nabla V^{*}_{t+1}\right)(y^{\prime})\right\|_{2}\right]\leq E_{t+1},

(74)

where
y^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u_{t}^{(m)})(y)\right),\sigma_{t}^{2}I_{d}\right)
. For the second term in (
73
), the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}
implies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{G}^{(m)}_{t+1}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}-u_{t}^{*}\right\|_{2}.

(75)

Consequently, Eq. (
73
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{t+1}^{(m)}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{t+1}(y)\right\|_{2}\leq E_{t+1}+L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}-u_{t}^{*}\right\|.

(76)

With (
51
), (
52
), (
59
) and (
76
), we further bound (
72
) with

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,t}^{\prime}\left(E_{t+1}+L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left\|u_{t}^{(m)}-u_{t}^{*}\right\|_{2}\right)+C_{2,t}^{\prime}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1},

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle C_{1,t}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{s}),\;\;\text{and}\;\;C_{2,t}^{\prime}=\frac{1+(1-\alpha_{t})L_{0,t}^{u^{*}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}L_{0,t+1}^{\widehat{V}}.

Finally, we apply Lemma
3.3
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m)}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,t}^{\prime}\left(E_{t+1}+L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}}\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\right)+C_{2,t}^{\prime}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,t}^{\prime}\left(E_{t+1}+(1-\lambda_{t})\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}E_{t+1}\right)\right)+C_{2,t}^{\prime}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1,t}E_{t+1}+C_{2,t}(1-\lambda_{t})^{m+1},

where

\displaystyle C_{1,t}=\lambda_{t}^{-1}C_{1,t}^{\prime},\;\;\text{and}\;\;C_{2,t}=C_{2,t}^{\prime}+C_{1,t}^{\prime}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}.

Therefore, we finish the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
3.1
with the results in Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

It is straightforward to check that
L_{0,t}^{\bar{V}}\geq L_{0,t}^{V^{*}}
for all
t\leq T
. Thus, the choice of
\beta_{t}
guarantees that Theorem
2.8
holds. To prove the theorem, we begin with the error
\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{m_{t}}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}
. Let
t<T
be fixed. To apply Lemma
3.4
, we choose
\widehat{V}_{t^{\prime}}=V_{t^{\prime}}^{(m_{t^{\prime}})}
for all
t^{\prime}>t
. Unrolling recursion (
71
) and use the fact that
V_{T}^{(m_{T})}(y)=R(y)=V_{T}^{*}(y)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla V_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-\nabla V_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=t}^{T}C_{1,k}\left\|\nabla V_{T}^{(m_{T})}(y)-V_{T}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}+\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\left(\prod_{\ell=t}^{k-1}C_{1,\ell}\right)C_{2,k}(1-\lambda_{k})^{m_{k}+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\left(\prod_{\ell=t}^{k-1}C_{1,\ell}\right)C_{2,k}(1-\lambda_{k})^{m_{k}+1}.

Next, we apply Lemma
3.3
to have

\displaystyle\left\|u_{t}^{(m_{t})}(y)-u_{t}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m_{t}}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}\left\|\nabla V_{t+1}^{m_{t+1}}(y)-\nabla V_{t+1}^{*}(y)\right\|_{2}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-\lambda_{t})^{m_{t}}L_{0,t+1}^{V^{*}}+\lambda_{t}^{-1}\sum_{k=t+1}^{T-1}\left(\prod_{\ell=t+1}^{k-1}C_{1,\ell}\right)C_{2,k}(1-\lambda_{k})^{m_{k}+1}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\lambda_{t})}{(1-\alpha_{t})L_{1,t+1}^{V^{*}}},

which finishes the proof.

∎

4
Discussions

Here, we provide some discussions on two future directions that we want to further explore.

4.1
Parameterized formulation

In this section, we adopt a linear parameterization to enable practical and efficient update of Algorithm
1
:

\displaystyle u_{t}(y)=K_{t}\phi(y),\;\;t=0,\dots,T-1,

where
\phi(y)=(\phi_{1}(y),\dots,\phi_{p}(y))^{\top}
is the given basis function and
{\textbf{K}}=\left\{K_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
are (unknown) parameters to learn. Despite its simplicity, this parameterization is flexible, expressive, and tractable, and is widely used in control and reinforcement learning
[
1
,
36
]
. With an appropriate choice of basis, it can capture a broad class of score approximators, including random features
[
48
]
, kernel methods
[
56
]
, and overparameterized neural networks in the NTK regime
[
35
]
.

We provide a roadmap to show how a linear convergence rate can be achieved, with details similar to earlier sections omitted. We make the following realizability assumption.

Assumption 4.1

(Realizability)
.

Assume that at each timestamp
t
, the optimal control satisfies
u_{t}^{*}(y)=K_{t}^{*}\phi(y)
for some matrix
K_{t}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
.

Define the expected value function associated with the policy
K
as

\displaystyle J({\textbf{K}})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Y_{0}\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[V_{0}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{0})\right],

where
\mathcal{D}
is the distribution of the initial state and we can take
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
, which is the distribution of
Y_{0}^{\rm pre}
. Here the value function is defined as

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\textbf{K}}(y)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.R(Y_{T})-\sum_{\ell=t}^{T-1}\beta_{\ell}\frac{(1-\alpha_{\ell})^{2}}{2\alpha_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}\left\|u_{\ell}(Y_{\ell})-s_{\ell}^{\rm pre}(Y_{\ell})\right\|_{2}^{2}\,\,\right|\,\,Y_{t}=y\right],

with terminal condition

\displaystyle V_{T}^{\textbf{K}}(y)=r(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[R(y)\right].

Similarly, define the Q-function:

\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}}(y,u)=\mathbb{E}\left[V_{t+1}^{\textbf{K}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(y+(1-\alpha_{t})u)+\sigma_{t}W_{t}\right)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left\|u-s_{t}(y)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right],

where the expecation is taken over
W_{t}
.
We remark that the policy
K
in the superscript refers to
\left\{K_{t^{\prime}}\right\}_{t^{\prime}>t}
at each
t
.
Recall the choice of
\beta_{t}
implies the map
u\mapsto Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}^{*}}(y,u)
is
\gamma_{t}
-strongly concave uniformly over all
y
; see Theorem
2.8
. If the feature mapping
\phi
is well-behaved, the map
K_{t}\mapsto Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}^{*}}(y,K_{t}\phi(y))
is also strongly concave.

Next, we calculate the policy gradient with respect to
\mathbf{K}
. Note that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}})\coloneqq\frac{\partial J({\textbf{K}})}{\partial K_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial K_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[-\sum_{\ell=0}^{t-1}\beta_{\ell}\frac{(1-\alpha_{\ell})^{2}}{2\alpha_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}\left\|K_{\ell}\phi(Y_{\ell})-s_{\ell}^{\rm pre}(Y_{\ell})\right\|_{2}^{2}+Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{t},K_{t}\phi(Y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial K_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{t},K_{t}\phi(Y_{t}))\right],

(77)

where the expectation is taken over the initial state
Y_{0}\sim\mathcal{D}
and noise
\left\{W_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
. We next show that the optimal policy
\textbf{K}^{*}
is the unique stationary point of
J(\textbf{K})
. Let
\overline{\textbf{K}}
be a stationary point, i.e.,
\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}})\Big|_{\textbf{K}=\overline{\textbf{K}}}=0
for all
t
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial K_{T-1}}\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{T-1}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{T-1},K_{T-1}\phi(Y_{T-1}))\right]\Big|_{\textbf{K}=\overline{\textbf{K}}}=0.

The strong concavity in
K_{T-1}
implies that
K_{T-1}^{*}=\overline{K}_{T-1}
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial K_{T-2}}\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{T-2}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{T-2},K_{T-2}\phi(Y_{T-2}))\right]\Big|_{\textbf{K}=\overline{\textbf{K}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial K_{T-2}}\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{T-2}^{\textbf{K}^{*}}(Y_{T-2},K_{T-2}\phi(Y_{T-2}))\right]\Big|_{\textbf{K}=\overline{\textbf{K}}}=0.

By strong concavity again, we have
K_{T-2}^{*}=\overline{K}_{T-2}
. Repeating the argument to conclude
{\textbf{K}}^{*}=\overline{\textbf{K}}
is the unique stationary point of the objective function
J(\textbf{K})
.
The above analysis replies on the two facts:

(i)

At
T-1
, the map
K_{T-1}\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{T-1}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{T-1},K_{T-1}\phi(Y_{T-1}))\right]
has a unique stationary point.

(ii)

For any
t<T-1
, the map
K_{t}\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}^{*}}(Y_{t},K_{t}\phi(Y_{t}))\right]
has a unique stationary point.

We remark that the above two conditions (i)–(ii)
relax

[
7
, Assumption 2.A]
, which assumes that
K_{t}\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t}^{\textbf{K}}(Y_{t},K_{t}\phi(Y_{t}))\right]
has no sub-optimal stationary points under
any

K
. While
[
7
]
primarily focuses on global convergence guarantees for infinite-horizon problems, it also introduces a condition (
cf.
Condition 4) for finite-horizon MDPs that aligns in spirit with our analysis.

Notably,
Bhandari and Russo [7]

did not
analyze the convergence rate of policy gradient methods for finite-horizon problems.
Since
{\textbf{K}}^{*}
is the unique stationary point, the policy gradient method converges to the globally optimal solution as long as the standard smoothness condition is satisfied. Moreover, we conjecture a
linear convergence rate
due to the presence of strong concavity. Consider the policy gradient update rule:

\displaystyle K_{t}^{(m+1)}=K_{t}^{(m)}+\eta\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}^{(m)}),\;\;0\leq t\leq T-1,m\geq 0.

For each
t<T
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|K_{t}^{(m+1)}-K_{t}^{*}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{t}^{(m)}+\eta\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}^{(m)})-K_{t}^{*}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{t}^{(m)}+\eta\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}_{\leq t}^{(m)},{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{*})-K_{t}^{*}\right\|_{F}+\eta\left\|\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}_{\leq t}^{(m)},{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{*})-\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}^{(m)})\right\|_{F},

where
{\textbf{K}}_{\leq t}\coloneqq\left\{K_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=0}^{t}
and
{\textbf{K}}_{>t}\coloneqq\left\{K_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell>t}
.
Eq. (
77
) implies
\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}})=\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}_{\geq t})
is independent of
\left\{K_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell<t}
. The (one-step) strong concavity and smoothness leads to

\displaystyle\left\|K_{t}^{(m)}+\eta\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}_{\leq t}^{(m)},{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{*})-K_{t}^{*}\right\|_{F}\leq c\left\|K_{t}^{(m)}-K_{t}^{*}\right\|_{F},

for some constant
c<1
.
If
\partial_{t}J(\textbf{K})
is further
L_{1,t}^{J}
-Lipschitz in
{\textbf{K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}_{\leq t}^{(m)},{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{*})-\partial_{t}J({\textbf{K}}^{(m)})\right\|_{F}\leq L_{1,t}^{J}\left\|{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{(m)}-{\textbf{K}}_{>t}^{*}\right\|_{F},

and consequently we prove the linear convergence rate of the policy gradient method.

4.2
Continuous-time limit

While we find the discrete-time formulation more natural for the fine-tuning problem, the continuous-time limit is inherently intriguing from a mathematical perspective. The potential benefit of continuous-time formulation includes robustness and stability in training
[
75
]
.

In the continuous time limit, we have

\displaystyle V(t,y)=\sup_{U:=\left\{U_{s}\right\}_{t\leq s\leq T}\in\mathcal{U}}\;\;\mathbb{E}\left[R(Y_{T})-\int_{t}^{T}\beta_{s}\frac{(1-\alpha_{s})^{2}}{2\alpha_{s}\sigma_{s}^{2}}\|s_{s}^{\rm pre}(Y_{s})-U_{s}\|_{2}^{2}{\rm d}s\,\,\Bigg|\,\,Y_{t}=y\right]

subject to

\displaystyle{\rm d}Y_{t}=\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}Y_{t}+\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}U_{t}\right){\rm d}t+\sigma_{t}{\rm d}W_{t},

where
\mathcal{U}
contains all Markovian policies that adapts to
\{\mathcal{F}^{W}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
The HJB follows:

\displaystyle 0=\partial_{t}V(t,y)+\sup_{u}\left\{\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}y+\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}u\right)\partial_{y}V(t,y)-\beta_{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}}\|s_{t}^{\rm pre}(y)-u\|_{2}^{2}\right\}+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{2}\partial_{yy}V(t,y),

with terminal condition
V(T,y)=r(y)=\mathbb{E}[R(y)]
. Hence the optimal control follows:

\displaystyle u^{*}_{t}(y)=s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha_{t})}\partial_{y}V(t,y),

(78)

and the HJB can be rewritten as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V(t,y)+\frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}y\,\,\partial_{y}V(t,y)+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\partial_{y}V(t,y)+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{2\beta_{t}}(\partial_{y}V(t,y))^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{2}\partial_{yy}V(t,y)

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V(t,y)+\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}y+\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}s^{\rm pre}_{t}(y)\right)\,\,\partial_{y}V(t,y)+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{2\beta_{t}}(\partial_{y}V(t,y))^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{2}}{2}\partial_{yy}V(t,y)

with terminal condition
V(T,y)=r(y)=\mathbb{E}[R(y)]
.

Note that both the optimal control (
78
) and the HJB (
79
) share the same form as the discrete-time system, respectively, (
18
) and (
33
).

There are a few interesting aspects to study for the continuous-time system (
78
)–(
79
):

Regularity of the value function:
Under conditions
2.6
–
2.7
, we believe the regulatory of the optimal value function, in terms of
V\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, can be achieved by adapting the analysis in
[
57
, Proposition 5.3]
and
[
72
, Theorem 4.3.1]
via forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) representation. However, the regularity of the value function and iterated control policies
along training
remains less clear and non-trivial in the continuous-time regime
[
77
, Assumption 2]
. Furthermore, the integration-by-parts result (
48
) aligns with the stochastic representation of the gradient in the continuous-time analysis, as detailed in
[
41
, Eqns 2.12–2.15]
and
[
71
, Chapter 2]
. We believe this approach will be instrumental in establishing regularity results throughout the training process.

Algorithm design and convergence for continuous-time systems:

The algorithm design and convergence analysis is challenging in continuous-time systems. In the literature,
Zhou and Lu [77]
studied the convergence of policy gradient flow and then
[
79
]
extended the analysis to actor-critic algorithms. The recent work
[
45
]
proposed an efficient algorithm to compute the value function of a continuous-time system given discrete trajectory data. However, all these work only focused on the algorithm design in the functional space while the analysis in parametric settings is overlooked. We believe our framework would provide insights of global convergence guarantees for parametric algorithms in both continuous- and discrete-time problems.

via numerical experiments, focusing on the following questions:

•

In practice, how fast does the PI-FT algorithm converge to the optimal solution?

•

How does the choice of
\beta
affect the convergence rate and the quality of the fine-tuned models?

\beta

6
Conclusion

We introduce a stochastic control framework for fine-tuning diffusion models, integrating linear dynamics with KL regularization. Our approach establishes the well-posedness and regularity of the control problem and proposes a policy iteration algorithm (PI-FT) that guarantees global linear convergence. Unlike prior work that assumes regularity throughout training, we prove that PI-FT inherently maintains these properties. Additionally, we extend our framework to parametric settings, broadening its applicability. This work advances the theoretical understanding of fine-tuning diffusion models and provides a foundation for developing more effective fine-tuning algorithms. Our algorithmic design and theoretical findings are also supported by thorough numerical experiments.

Proactive graded follow-up strategy for reducing recurrence in acute pancreatitis (PROGRESS): protocol for a multicentre, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Yingyu Pan, Yuelun Zhang, Zihan Yang, Yonghao Chen, Yiyang Min, Xiaoxuan Huang, Shengyu Zhang, Jianing Li, Dong Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRecurrence of acute pancreatitis (AP) is common, leading to cumulative pancreatic injury and reduced quality of life. While aetiology-based treatments and lifestyle interventions are effective, long-term adherence is often poor due to insufficient health awareness and a lack of continuous reminders. We designed the PROGRESS (PROactive GRadEd follow-up Strategy for reducing recurrence in acute pancreatitiS) trial to evaluate whether a physician-initiated, proactive and graded follow-up strategy can reduce AP recurrence.Methods and analysisThe PROGRESS trial is a multicentre, open-label, randomised controlled trial. A total of 194 patients with AP will be randomised 1:1 to either a proactive follow-up group or a standard follow-up group. In the proactive follow-up group, patients are stratified into high-risk or low-risk categories based on reported risk factors. Follow-up is conducted via WeChat with varying frequencies: every 3 months for the high-risk group and every 6 months for the low-risk group. The standard follow-up group receives routine discharge education and a 1-month outpatient visit. The primary endpoint is the number of AP recurrence episodes within 24 months post-discharge. Secondary endpoints include AP recurrence rate, progression to chronic pancreatitis, quality of life (Short Form 12 Health Survey), anxiety/depression scores (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7/Patient Health Questionnaire-9), readmission rates and mortality. Analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (No. I-25PJ2556, I-26PJ0895). Ethics approval of each participating centre is required before initiation of patient enrolment. The results of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500113459 (Chinese Clinical Trial Registry).Keywords: Pancreatic disease, Follow-Up Studies, Clinical Protocols

Introduction

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common and severe gastrointestinal disease, and its recurrence poses a significant clinical challenge. Epidemiological data indicate that approximately 21% of patients with a primary episode of AP will experience at least one recurrence.1 Each episode of recurrent AP (RAP) contributes to cumulative pancreatic injury, significantly increasing the risk of progression to chronic pancreatitis (CP).2 Even in the absence of progression to CP, RAP significantly impairs quality of life.3

Evidence suggests that aetiology-based treatment can effectively reduce the risk of recurrence.4 Specifically, abstinence for alcoholic AP, cholecystectomy for biliary AP and hypertriglyceridaemia (HTG) management for HTG-associated AP (HTG-AP) are critical preventive measures. Furthermore, basic lifestyle interventions, including restricting dietary fat and simple carbohydrates, smoking cessation, alcohol abstinence, weight management and appropriate pancreatic enzyme replacement, have been associated with reduced RAP rates.5,7

The aforementioned preventive measures rely on patients’ long-term self-management. However, this is often difficult to achieve in real-world settings. Insufficient adherence results from a complex interplay of factors. On one hand, some patients lack health awareness or relevant knowledge; on the other hand, influenced by long-standing habits, patients require continuous reminders to maintain motivation for behavioural change.8 9 Effective patient follow-up and health education are pivotal to addressing these challenges. Currently, these are primarily based on proactive outpatient visits by the patient, while routine education is often limited to one-way information delivery at the time of discharge.

We hypothesise that a physician-initiated proactive follow-up strategy can reduce the frequency of AP recurrence and improve long-term prognosis by reinforcing healthy behaviours. The hypothesis will be tested in a multicentre, randomised controlled trial comparing the efficacy of a standard follow-up strategy versus a proactive follow-up strategy. Considering some known risk factors for RAP, we will implement a graded follow-up strategy within the proactive arm, applying a higher frequency of follow-up for patients identified as at high risk.

Randomisation and blinding methods

Stratified block randomisation will be used. An independent methodologist, not involved in recruitment or assessment, will manage the process using R software. Stratification will be based on aetiology (biliary/HTG/other) and severity (mild AP/moderately severe AP (MSAP)/severe AP (SAP)), forming nine strata. Within each stratum, random block sizes (varying between 4 and 6) will be used to generate a 1:1 allocation sequence.

Allocation concealment will be achieved using sealed, opaque, tamper-proof envelopes. Each envelope will be marked with the stratum and sequence number. On patient enrolment, the investigator will request the next envelope for the specific stratum from the methodologist. The envelope will be opened in the presence of the patient.

Due to the nature of the intervention (follow-up strategy), blinding of investigators and participants is not feasible. Outcome assessment is also not blinded. However, the endpoints are objective events defined by diagnostic criteria or can be objectively measured through scoring scales, laboratory reports and medical records, minimising assessment bias.

Intervention

Eligible patients will be screened by each centre according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. After being fully informed and providing written informed consent (online supplemental materials) prior to discharge, participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention group (proactive follow-up) or the control group (standard follow-up). Patients will be followed according to their assigned model for 24 months post-discharge to assess outcome measures.

Risk stratification: patients will be stratified into a high-risk group and a low-risk group based on the presence of the following risk factors47 11,14: MSAP or SAP, history of RAP, smoking, alcohol consumption, idiopathic aetiology, biliary aetiology without cholecystectomy or HTG-AP with comorbidities (diabetes, age<40 years or pre-discharge triglycerides (TGs)>5.65 mmol/L). Patients with any of the above risk factors will be classified into the high-risk group.

Standard follow-up group

Per routine clinical practice, patients will receive health education and lifestyle guidance at discharge (online supplemental materials) and will be advised to return for an outpatient clinic visit at 1 month. Physicians will not intervene in subsequent follow-up plans. Patients will be encouraged to retain all post-discharge medical records to minimise information bias. Telephone follow-up will be conducted at 12 and 24 months to ascertain outcome status. If a potential recurrence event is reported, patients will be asked to provide supporting medical records and the date of recurrence.

Endpoints

Primary endpoint

Number of AP recurrence episodes within 24 months post-discharge

RAP is defined as a new episode meeting the AP diagnostic criteria (recorded in the hospital inpatient system or outpatient records) occurring at an interval of ≥3 months from the discharge date of the previous episode, with complete resolution of pancreatitis-related signs and symptoms during the interval. If the recurrence was treated at an external hospital, the patient must provide the specific date of onset (to verify the ≥3-month interval) and medical records/test results to confirm the diagnosis. If the patient describes typical symptoms of recurrence but cannot provide medical records, the event will be recorded but excluded from the primary analysis, which relies on medically confirmed diagnoses; these will be included in a sensitivity analysis.

Trial committees

The trial is an investigator-initiated, multicentre research project led by Peking Union Medical College Hospital. To ensure high-quality execution and data collection, a trial management committee (TMC) has been established, consisting of the principal investigators and members from each participating site. The TMC is responsible for the day-to-day running, coordination and management of the trial. An independent data and safety monitoring committee (IDSMC) has been formed to oversee the safety of participants and the overall quality of the study data. This committee is entirely independent and has no competing interests with the research or its sponsors. The IDSMC will regularly review protocol adherence, trial withdrawal rates and safety monitoring data to provide formal recommendations regarding the continuation, modification or termination of the study. A trial steering committee, comprising the lead investigators and methodology experts, is responsible for the overall scientific oversight of the trial and for considering the recommendations provided by the IDSMC to ensure the study’s integrity.

Sample size calculation and enrolment

The sample size was calculated based on the primary endpoint, the number of AP recurrence episodes, assuming the data follows a Poisson distribution. Based on published data, the mean number of recurrent episodes for HTG and biliary AP is estimated at 1.010 and 0.136, respectively.16 17 In the leading centre (Peking Union Medical College Hospital cohort), the ratio of biliary to HTG aetiology is approximately 2:3. Therefore, the weighted expected mean number of recurrent episodes in the control group is estimated at 0.660. For the intervention group, we anticipated a 50% reduction in the recurrence rate for HTG-AP (mean 0.505) due to proactive follow-up and health education, while conservatively assuming no effect on biliary AP (mean 0.136). Consequently, the expected mean number of recurrent episodes for the intervention group was estimated at 0.357. Using a two-sided α=0.05 and power (1−β)=0.80, the calculated sample size is N1=N2=87. Accounting for a 10% loss to follow-up, we intend to enrol 194 patients (97 per group).

The trial will adopt a competitive enrolment strategy, whereby participating centres recruit eligible individuals based on their institutional capacity, without fixed site-specific quotas. The total recruitment period is projected to span 30–36 months.

Data collection and management

A web-based electrical database (Digital Health China Technologies, Beijing, China) will be used for the data collection and storage. All data will be input by nominated investigators (one or two per site), who will undergo standardised data entry training conducted by the leading centre (Peking Union Medical College Hospital). To ensure the accuracy, completeness and legibility of the data, an independent administrator from the leading centre will perform weekly quality audits via the electronic data capture system and provide timely feedback to the investigators. To safeguard patient privacy, all data stored in the database will be de-identified. The complete dataset will be securely locked prior to the commencement of statistical analysis.

To ensure the reliability of self-reported data via WeChat, investigators will systematically cross-check participants’ responses against internal electronic medical records at each study centre to verify consistency. For participants who undergo follow-up examinations at external hospitals, submission of scanned or photographed medical records and diagnostic reports will be required. Furthermore, at the follow-up endpoint, a random sample of 10% of the participants in the proactive follow-up group will be selected for formal data auditing via either an in-person outpatient visit or a structured telephone verification to assess the accuracy of their longitudinal questionnaire responses.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses will be performed according to the intention-to-treat principle, encompassing all randomised subjects regardless of their adherence to the follow-up protocol. For the primary outcome, the count of recurrence episodes will be compared between groups using a Poisson regression model with sandwich robust SE estimation. The results will be presented as incidence rate ratios with 95% CIs. The dispersion of the model will be assessed by examining the ratio of the residual sum of squares to the degrees of freedom; if overdispersion is detected, a negative binomial regression model will be employed.

To ensure the robustness of the primary findings, three sensitivity analyses will be conducted. First, a worst-case analysis will be performed where missing outcome data in the intervention group are imputed as the ‘maximum observed recurrences+1’ and in the control group as 0 events. Second, a covariate-adjusted analysis will be carried out using the same regression model, controlling for baseline characteristics that show an Absolute Standardised Difference>0.1. Third, the analysis will be repeated using a modified definition of recurrence, reducing the required symptom-free interval between episodes from 3 months to 1 month.

For secondary endpoints, categorical variables will be analysed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, reporting risk ratios and 95% CIs. Continuous variables will be compared using independent sample t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests, presenting mean or median differences with 95% CIs. A pre-specified subgroup analysis based on aetiology (biliary/HTG/other) will be conducted, including tests for interaction effects. Exploratory endpoints will be analysed only within specific patient subgroups using complete case analysis. If the proportion of missing values for a certain endpoint in the corresponding subgroup exceeds 20% or the sample size is less than 30, the exploratory analysis for that endpoint will not be conducted. As these are exploratory, no adjustments for multiple comparisons will be made, and p values will be considered descriptive.

Adverse events

Given the nature of the intervention (follow-up and education), no intervention-related adverse events are anticipated. Adverse events related to the disease course will be recorded as outcomes.

Discussion

The PROGRESS trial aims to optimise the post-discharge management of AP from a passive, patient-initiated approach to a proactive, physician-directed strategy. Through scheduled WeChat-based questionnaires and reminders, supplemented by manual verification when necessary, the intervention is designed to enhance follow-up adherence, strengthen disease awareness and promote behavioural modification, thereby reducing preventable recurrence risk and improving patient-reported outcomes.

WeChat serves as the primary communication platform for follow-up, leveraging its widespread penetration in China and demonstrated feasibility in enhancing adherence and improving outcomes across multiple disease-management studies.18,20 Should the present trial demonstrate that proactive, risk-stratified follow-up meaningfully reduces overall recurrence or the risk of first recurrence, it would establish a pragmatic workflow for long-term post-discharge care. Specifically, risk stratification at discharge, followed by remote, low-cost, scalable education and monitoring, plus targeted manual confirmation, could enable more intensive outreach to patients at high risk and potentially reduce recurrence-related hospitalisations, medical expenditures and quality-of-life loss. Subsequent research could evaluate cost-effectiveness, assess the equivalence of different communication modalities (eg, text messaging, stand-alone applications, telephone or in-person contact) and explore integrated programmes that combine structured follow-up with long-term behavioural support (eg, alcohol-cessation initiatives, smoking cessation or sustained lipid-lowering adherence management).

This design also introduces a bias that operates against the hypothesised benefit. Participants in the intervention arm will receive scheduled contact and health education; they may develop greater awareness of AP symptoms and a lower threshold for seeking medical attention than controls. Theoretically, this differential exposure could inflate recurrence detection in the intervention arm by increasing recognition of mild symptoms and triggering earlier presentation, whereas the control arm may under-report due to limited surveillance. This bias acts in the opposite direction of the expected therapeutic effect; thus, if the intervention arm still demonstrates a materially lower recurrence rate, the observed benefit would be further strengthened. To mitigate this concern, the control arm will undergo structured telephone contact at 12 and 24 months solely to ascertain vital status and AP recurrence, without any educational component or behavioural guidance, thereby minimising inadvertent intervention.

Whittaker modules for U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
Xiangqian Guo, Xuewen Liu and Limeng Xia

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Whittaker modules for the quantum enveloping algebra U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}) with respect to a fixed Whittaker function. We construct the universal Whittaker module, find all its Whittaker vectors and investigate the submodules generated by subsets of Whittaker vectors and corresponding quotient modules. We also find Whittaker vectors and determine the irreducibility of these quotient modules and show that they exhaust all irreducible Whittaker modules. Finally, we can determine all maximal submodules of the universal Whittaker module. The Whittaker model of U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}) are quite different from that of U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) and finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras, since the center of our algebra is not a polynomial algebra.



Keywords: Whittaker module, Whittaker function, quantum group.

MSC 2010: 17B37, 20G42.

1.
Introduction

Whittaker modules were first introduced and studied by B. Kostant [Ko] for finite-dimensional
complex semisimple Lie algebras. These modules are related to the Whittaker equations
(see
[
WW
]
, P.337) that arise in the study of the corresponding representations of the associated Lie groups.
Whittaker modules for finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebras are also related
to parabolic induction and BGG category
\mathcal{O}
(see for example,
[
Ba
,
KM
,
M1
,
M2
,
MS1
,
MS2
]
).
In Block’s classification of all simple
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules in [Bl],
all simple modules fall into two classes: weight modules (including highest and lowest weight modules) and modules on which the action of the Cartan subalgebra is torsion-free (including Whittaker modules).

Recently, many authors studied the Whittaker modules for other algebras, for example,
quantum groups (
[
On1
,
On2
,
Se
]
), generalized Weyl algebras (
[
BO
]
), affine Lie algebras (
[
Ch
,
ALZ
]
), Virasoro algebra (
[
OW1
,
LGZ
]
), and some other algebras (see for example,
[
OW2
,
BM
,
GL
]
and references therein.)

In particular, Whittaker modules for the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
were fully investigated in
[
On1
]
, where Whittaker
modules with respect to a fixed Whittaker function are in one-to-one correspondence with the ideals of the center of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. This is an analogue of the classical results in the Lie algebra setting due to Kostant
[
Ko
]
.

In present paper, we will consider the Whittaker model for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
.
The main difference is that there does not exist non-singular Whittaker function for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
duo to the quantum Serre relations ((see Section 3)). So we consider the Whittaker modules with respect to a singular Whittaker function. In this case, the Whittaker module theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
turns out to be much different, since the algebra itself is more complicated as well as the center of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
is no longer a polynomial algebra, which cause much trouble for our discussion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some basic notation and terminology for the
quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
. Then we start with the construction of universal Whittaker module
M(\eta)

for some Whittaker function
\eta
and make some preparations for later use in Section 3.

In Section 4, we define certain submodules generated by subsets of Whittaker vectors in
M(\eta)
and prove some technique results for them, which lead to two consequences. The first is that we can determine the Whittaker vectors in
M(\eta)
and its quotient modules corresponding to the previous submodules (Theorem
4.4
).
The second one allows us to divide submodules and quotient modules of
M(\eta)
into two classes:
critical and non-critical ones (see definitions above Theorem
4.4
).
In particular, we construct quotient modules
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
with two parameters
\kappa,c\in\mathbb{C},\kappa\neq 0
,
which we show is irreducible if and only if
(\kappa,c)
is non-critical, see Theorem
4.7
.

The last section is devoted to the critical case, where we completely determine the submodule structure of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
, which admits a unique composite series of length at most
2
(Theorem
5.6
).
In particular, the homomorphisms between two modules
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
are computed (Corollary
5.4
) and
the irreducible quotient modules are determined.
Finally, we can give an explicit description of all maximal submodules of the universal Whittaker module
M(\eta)
and obtain a characterization of irreducible Whittaker modules in terms of quotient modules of
M(\eta)
.

2.
The algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\mathbb{C}
the
sets of integers, positive integers, nonnegative integers and
complex numbers respectively. All vector spaces and algebras are
over
\mathbb{C}
, although most of the arguments work for any fields with characteristic
0
.
We always fix a nonzero
q\in\mathbb{C}
which is not a root of unity.

First recall the Jimbo presentation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
(see
[
Ja
]
). The
algebra is an associative algebra generated by
E_{1},E_{2},F_{1},F_{2},K_{1},K_{2},K_{1}^{-1},K_{2}^{-1}

subject to the following relations.
For
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, the commutator relations:

(2.1)

\displaystyle[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle K_{i}^{\pm 1}E_{j}=q^{\pm a_{ij}}E_{j}K_{i}^{\pm 1},

(2.3)

\displaystyle K_{i}^{\pm 1}F_{j}=q^{\mp a_{ij}}F_{j}K_{i}^{\pm 1};

and the quantum Serre relations:

(2.4)

\displaystyle E_{i}^{2}E_{j}-(q+q^{-1})E_{i}E_{j}E_{i}+E_{j}E_{i}^{2},\quad|i-j|=1,

(2.5)

\displaystyle F_{i}^{2}F_{j}-(q+q^{-1})F_{i}F_{j}F_{i}+F_{j}F_{i}^{2},\quad|i-j|=1,

where
a_{11}=a_{22}=2
and
a_{12}=a_{21}=-1
.

The algebra
U=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
admits a triangular decomposition

U=U^{-}\otimes U^{0}\otimes U^{+},

where
U^{+}
is the subalgebra generated by
E_{1},E_{2}
,
U^{-}
is
the subalgebra generated by
F_{1},F_{2}
and
U^{0}=\mathbb{C}[K_{1}^{\pm 1},K_{2}^{\pm 1}]
.
For convenience, we denote
E_{3}=E_{1}E_{2}-q^{-1}E_{2}E_{1}

and
F_{3}=F_{1}F_{2}-qF_{2}F_{1}
. Then
U^{+}
admits a PBW basis

\{E_{3}^{r_{3}}E_{2}^{r_{2}}E_{1}^{r_{1}},r_{1},r_{2},r_{12}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\},

and
U^{-}
admits a PBW basis

\{F_{3}^{r_{3}}F_{2}^{r_{2}}F_{1}^{r_{1}},r_{1},r_{2},r_{12}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}.

For
E_{3},F_{3}
, we have the following easily-verified commutator relations:

(2.6)

\displaystyle K_{i}E_{3}=qE_{3}K_{i},\ K_{i}F_{3}=q^{-1}F_{3}K_{i};\quad i=1,2

(2.7)

\displaystyle F_{1}F_{3}=q^{-1}F_{3}F_{1},\ F_{2}F_{3}=qF_{3}F_{2},\ [E_{1},F_{3}]=F_{2}K_{1}^{-1},\ [E_{2},F_{3}]=-K_{2}F_{1};

(2.8)

\displaystyle E_{1}E_{3}=qE_{3}E_{1},\ E_{2}E_{3}=q^{-1}E_{3}E_{2},\ [F_{1},E_{3}]=E_{2}K_{1}^{-1},\ [F_{2},E_{3}]=-K_{2}E_{1}.

3.
Whittaker modules for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})

For convenience we always denote
U=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
throughout the paper.

Fix any nonzero algebra homomorphism
\eta:U^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, which we call a

Whittaker function
. In particular,
\eta(E_{3})=(1-q^{-1})\eta(E_{1})\eta(E_{2})

and hence
\eta
is determined by its values on
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The
Whittaker function
\eta
is called
non-singular
if

\eta(E_{1})
and
\eta(E_{2})
are both nonzero and is called

singular
otherwise. From the quantum Serre relations
(
2.4
), we see easily that
\eta(E_{1})\eta(E_{2})=0
.
Consequently, there do not exist non-singular Whittaker functions
and we will consider the singular case.
In the rest of this
paper, we will always suppose that
\eta(E_{1})=\alpha\neq 0
and

\eta(E_{2})=0
without loss of generality due to the symmetry
between
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
.

Now take any
U
-module
W
. A vector
w\in W
is called a

Whittaker vector of type
\eta
if
xw=\eta(x)v
for all

x\in U^{+}
and
W
is called a
Whittaker module of type

\eta
or
(\alpha,0)
if it is generated by a nonzero Whittaker vector of the same
type. In what follows, all Whittaker modules and Whittaker vectors
are assumed of type
\eta
if not stated otherwise.

We first define the universal Whittaker module. Define a

1
-dimensional
U^{+}
-module
\mathbb{C}v_{\eta}
given by
xv_{\eta}=\eta(x)v_{\eta}
for all
elements
x\in U^{+}
. Then we form the induced
U
-module

M(\eta)=U\otimes_{U^{+}}\mathbb{C}v_{\eta},

which is called the
universal Whittaker module of type

\eta
. Obviously, any Whittaker module is isomorphic
to a homomorphic image of the universal Whittaker module.

Let
U^{(1)}
be the subalgebra of
U
generated by
E_{1},K_{1},F_{1}
.
Denote
K=K_{1}K_{2}^{2}
. It is clear that
K
commutes with
E_{1},F_{1}

and
K_{1}
. Recall the Casimir element of
U^{(1)}

C_{1}=F_{1}E_{1}+\frac{qK_{1}+q^{-1}K_{1}^{-1}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}.

Since
[C_{1},K]=0
, we have the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
and
[U^{(1)},\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]]=0
.

Note that
E_{1}w=\alpha w
and
E_{2}w=0
for any
w\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta}
,
so
w
is a Whittaker vector.
In next section, we will show that
\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta}
is just the set of all Whittaker vectors
(see Theorem
4.4
).

By the PBW Theorem,
M(\eta)
has a basis

\{F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}K_{1}^{p}F_{1}^{r}v_{\eta}\ |\ l,p\in\mathbb{Z},j,k,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}.

Noticing
E_{1}v_{\eta}=\alpha v_{\eta}
and the definition of

C_{1}
and
K
, the module
M(\eta)
has another basis

\{F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}K^{p}C_{1}^{r}v_{\eta}\ |\ l,p\in\mathbb{Z},j,k,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

Denote
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
and define a total order on
\Gamma

as follows: for any
\gamma=(j,k,l)
,
\gamma^{\prime}=(j^{\prime},k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\in\Gamma
, define
\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}
if and only if
j+k>j^{\prime}+k^{\prime}
, or
j+k=j^{\prime}+k^{\prime}

and
k>k^{\prime}
, or
j+k=j^{\prime}+k^{\prime},k=k^{\prime}
and
l\succ l^{\prime}
, where
l\succeq l^{\prime}
means
|l|>|l^{\prime}|
or
l=-l^{\prime}\geq 0
.
We have the induced filtration on
M=M(\eta)
given by:

M_{j,k,l}=\sum_{(j^{\prime},k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\leq(j,k,l)}F_{2}^{j^{\prime}}F_{3}^{k^{\prime}}K_{2}^{l^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta}

and

M^{\prime}_{j,k,l}=\sum_{(j^{\prime},k^{\prime},l^{\prime})<(j,k,l)}F_{2}^{j^{\prime}}F_{3}^{k^{\prime}}K_{2}^{l^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta}.

For any element
w\in M_{j,k,l}\setminus M^{\prime}_{j,k,l}
we denote
\deg(w)=(j,k,l)
, called the
degree
of
w
.
We also denote

M_{n}=\sum_{j+k=n}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta},\ \text{and}\ M^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{n^{\prime}<n}M_{n^{\prime}},\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

As usual, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote

[k]=\frac{q^{k}-q^{-k}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad[K_{i};k]=\frac{q^{k}K_{i}-q^{-k}K_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},\ i=1,2.

The following identities and relations will be used from time t time.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
j,k,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(1)

F_{1}F_{2}^{j}=[j]F_{2}^{j-1}F_{3}+q^{j}F_{2}^{j}F_{1};

(2)

[E_{1},F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}]=[k]F_{2}^{j+1}F_{3}^{k-1}K_{1}^{-1}
;

(3)

[E_{2},F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}]=[j]F_{2}^{j-1}F_{3}^{k}[K_{2};1-j-k]-q^{1-k}[k]F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k-1}K_{2}F_{1}
.

(4)

(E_{1}-q^{l}\alpha)M_{j,k,l}\subseteq M^{\prime}_{j,k,l}
and
E_{2}M_{j,k,l}\subseteq M^{\prime}_{j,k,l}
, where
M^{\prime}_{0,0,0}=0
.

(5)

E_{1}M_{n}\subseteq M_{n}
and
E_{2}M_{n}\subseteq M_{n-1}
, where
M_{-1}=0
.

Proof.

All assertions can be checked straightforward.
∎

For any ideal
I
of the commutative algebra
\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
, we denote
by
W(\eta,I)
the
U
-submodule of
M(\eta)
generated by
Iv_{\eta}
, that is,
W(I,\eta)=UIv_{\eta}
. Set
V(\eta,I)=M(\eta)/W(\eta,I)
. We will see later that
W(\eta,I),I\unlhd\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
, are precisely the submodules
generated by subsets of Whittaker vectors of type
(\alpha,0)
in
M(\eta)
(see Remark
4.5
).

The following obvious results will be used frequently in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any ideal
I\unlhd\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
, we have

W(\eta,I)=\bigoplus_{k,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Iv_{\eta}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
and
w=\sum_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Q_{j,k,l}v_{\eta}
, where
Q_{j,k,l}\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
. Then
w\in W(\eta,I)
if and only if
Q_{j,k,l}\in I
for any
(j,k,l)\in\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
N
is a subspace of
M(\eta)
stable under the action of
E_{1}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}^{i}Q_{i}v_{\eta}\in N
for some
Q_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
implies
Q_{i}v_{\eta}\in N
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Suppose
\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}^{i}Q_{i}v_{\eta}\in N
, then applying
E_{1}^{j}
on it, we get

E_{1}^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{k}K_{2}^{i}Q_{i}v_{\eta}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}q^{ij}K_{2}^{i}E_{1}^{j}Q_{i}v_{\eta}=\alpha^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{k}q^{ij}K_{2}^{i}Q_{i}v_{\eta}\in N,\ \forall\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Using the property of the Vandermonde’s determinant, we deduce

K_{2}^{i}Q_{i}v_{\eta}\in N
.
∎

4.
Whittaker vectors and non-critical irreducible Whittaker modules

Let notation be as before. Since irreducible Whittaker modules are just irreducible quotient modules
of the universal Whittaker module
M(\eta)
, we need to determine maximal submodules of
M(\eta)
.
We start with the study of structure of submodules
W(\eta,I)
, which is generated by
Iv_{\eta}
for an ideal
I\unlhd\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Suppose

(4.1)

u=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{0}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{2}^{n_{0}-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Q_{k,l}v_{\eta}\in M_{n_{0}},

where only finitely many
Q_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
are nonzero.
Suppose
u\notin W(\eta,I)
in addition, then
(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})u\in W(\eta,I)
for some
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if

(4.2)

u\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{0}-k}q^{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}j+k\\
k\end{array}\right]F_{2}^{n_{0}-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l_{0}+2j}\big(\alpha(1-q^{2})q^{l_{0}+j}K\big)^{k}Q_{l_{0}+2j+2k}v_{\eta}\hskip-5.0pt\mod W(\eta,I),

for some
0\leq k_{0}\leq n_{0}
, where
Q_{l_{0}+2i}=Q_{0,l_{0}+2i}
for
0\leq i\leq k_{0}
with
Q_{l_{0}+2k_{0}}\notin I
, the
q
-binomial coefficients
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}j+k\\
k\end{array}\right]=\frac{[j+k]!}{[j]![k]!}
,
[k]!=[k][k-1]\cdots[1]
and
[0]!=1
for
k,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

If
u
is of the form in (
4.2
), we can check straightforward that
(E-\alpha q^{l_{0}})u\in W(\eta,I)
.
Now suppose on the contrary
(E-\alpha q^{l_{0}})\in W(\eta,I)
for some
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we can compute fro any
\ (j,k,l)\in\Gamma
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
that

(4.3)

\begin{split}E_{1}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Qv_{\eta}=&[E_{1},F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}]K_{2}^{l}Qv_{\eta}+F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}E_{1}K_{2}^{l}Qv_{\eta}\\
=&[k]F_{2}^{j+1}F_{3}^{k-1}K_{2}^{l+2}K^{-1}Qv_{\eta}+\alpha q^{l}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Qv_{\eta}.\\
\end{split}

Let
k_{0}
be the maximal
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
Q_{k,l}\notin I
for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Denote
S=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ Q_{k_{0},l}\notin I\}
which is finite. Without loss of generality, we assume
Q_{k_{0},l}=0
for
l\notin S
. There exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that

S=\{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{s}\}\ \text{with}\ l_{1}\succ l_{2}\succ\dots\succ l_{s}.

Then we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}&(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})u\\
\equiv&\sum_{l\in S}[k_{0}]F_{2}^{n_{0}-k_{0}+1}F_{3}^{k_{0}-1}K_{2}^{l+2}K^{-1}Q_{k_{0},l}v_{\eta}+\alpha\sum_{l\in S}(q^{l}-q^{l_{0}})F_{2}^{n_{0}-k_{0}}F_{3}^{k_{0}}K_{2}^{l}Q_{k_{0},l}v_{\eta}\\
\equiv&\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{s}(q^{l_{i}}-q^{l_{0}})F_{2}^{j_{0}}F_{3}^{k_{0}}K_{2}^{l_{i}}Q_{k_{0},l_{i}}v_{\eta}\mod M^{\prime}_{n_{0}-k_{0},k_{0},0}.\end{split}

Then
(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})u\in W(\eta,I)
implies
s=1
and
l_{1}=l_{0}
.

Now we have
Q_{k_{0},l}=0
for
l\neq l_{0}
.
Applying
E_{1}
to
u
, by (
4.3
) we deduce

\begin{split}&E_{1}u-\alpha q^{l_{0}}u\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}[k]F_{2}^{n_{0}-k+1}F_{3}^{k-1}K_{2}^{l+2}K^{-1}Q_{k,l}v_{\eta}+\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(q^{l}-q^{l_{0}})F_{2}^{n_{0}-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}Q_{k,l}v_{\eta}\\
=&\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{2}^{n_{0}-k+1}F_{3}^{k-1}\big([k]K_{2}^{l+2}K^{-1}Q_{k,l}+\alpha(q^{l}-q^{l_{0}})K_{2}^{l}Q_{k-1,l}\big)v_{\eta}.\end{split}

By hypothesis on
u
, we see that

(4.5)

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}[k]K_{2}^{l+2}K^{-1}Q_{k,l}-\alpha\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(q^{l_{0}}-q^{l})K_{2}^{l}Q_{k-1,l}\in\mathbb{C}[K_{2}]I,\ \quad\forall\ k=1,2,\dots,k_{0}.

Noticing that
Q_{k_{0},l}=\delta_{l,l_{0}}Q_{k_{0},l_{0}}
, the above formula for
k=k_{0}
implies
Q_{k_{0}-1,l}=0
for all
l\neq l_{0},l_{0}+2
.
Inductively, we can deduce that
Q_{k_{0}-i,l}=0
for all
l\neq l_{0},l_{0}+2,\dots,l_{0}+2i
.
Then the equation (
4.5
) becomes

\sum_{j=0}^{i}[k_{0}-i]K_{2}^{l_{0}+2j+2}K^{-1}Q_{k_{0}-i,l_{0}+2j}-\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{i+1}q^{l_{0}}(1-q^{2j})K_{2}^{l_{0}+2j}Q_{k_{0}-i-1,l_{0}+2j}\in\mathbb{C}[K_{2}]I,

or, more precisely,

Q_{k_{0}-i,l_{0}+2j}-\frac{\alpha q^{l_{0}}(1-q^{2j+2})}{[k_{0}-i]}KQ_{k_{0}-i-1,l_{0}+2j+2}\in I,

for all
i=0,1,\dots,k_{0}-1
and
j=0,1,\dots,i
.
Replacing each
Q_{k-i,l_{0}+2j}
with
Q_{k-i,l_{0}+2j}
plus a suitable elements in
I
, we may assume that

Q_{k_{0}-i,l_{0}+2j}=\frac{\alpha q^{l_{0}+j}(1-q^{2})[j+1]}{[k_{0}-i]}KQ_{k_{0}-i-1,l_{0}+2j+2},

for all
j=0,1,\dots,i
and
i=0,1,\dots,k_{0}-1
.

Now setting
Q_{l_{0}+2i}=Q_{0,l_{0}+2i}
, we can deduce that

(4.6)

Q_{k,l_{0}+2j}=q^{\frac{1}{2}k(2j+k-1)}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}j+k\\

n_{+}=\max\{n\ |\ h_{n}(\kappa,c)=0\}\ \text{and}\ n_{-}=\min\{n\ |\ h_{n}(\kappa,c)=0\}.

We have seen
\kappa=\pm q^{n_{+}+n_{-}-3}
if
n_{+}\neq n_{-}
.
Denote
u_{\pm}=u(n_{\pm},0,1)
, where
1\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
is the constant polynomial. Recall that
u(n_{\epsilon},l,1)=q^{2n_{\epsilon}l}K_{2}^{l}u_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon\in\{+,-\}
.

For any
w\in M(\eta)
, we still use
\bar{w}
to denote its image in
V(\eta;\kappa,c)=M(\eta)/W(\eta;\kappa,c)
. Note that
J(\kappa,c)v_{\eta}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta}\subseteq M_{0,0,0}
, so there is a natural filtration on
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
inherited from that on
M(\eta)
(see Section 3) and we can define the degree of an element in
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
. Namely,
\deg(\bar{w})=\deg(w)
for any
w\in M(\eta)
.
In particular,

\deg(\bar{u}(n_{\epsilon},l,1))=(n_{\epsilon},n_{\epsilon},l),\ \forall\ \epsilon\in\{+,-\}.

Fix
\epsilon\in\{+,-\}
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\bar{u}_{\epsilon}
is a Whittaker vector in
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
of type
(\alpha,0)
and hence generates a proper submodule of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
.
We denote this submodule by
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
and
its preimage in
M(\eta)
by
{\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
.
More precisely,

{\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)=Uu_{\epsilon}+W(\eta;\kappa,c)

and

{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)=U\bar{u}_{\epsilon}={\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)/W(\eta;\kappa,c)\subseteq V(\eta;\kappa,c).

Note that the least degree of nonzero elements in
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is
(n_{\epsilon},n_{\epsilon},0)=\deg(\bar{u}_{\epsilon})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}u(n,l,Q)=

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}(q-q^{-1})^{-2}\Big(u(n,l,q^{-l}(q^{n-3}K^{-1}+q^{3-n}K)Q)

\displaystyle-u(n,l+2,q^{-n-l-1}K^{-1}Q)-u(n,l-2,q^{n-l+1}KQ)

\displaystyle-q^{(2n-1)l}K_{2}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}h_{n}(K,C_{1})Qv_{\eta}\Big).

Proof.

Recall that
u(n,l,Q)=q^{2nl}K_{2}^{l}u(n,0,Q)
and
F_{1}K_{2}=q^{-1}K_{2}F_{1}
.
We need only to prove the lemma for
l=0
.

Applying
F_{1}
to
u(n,0,Q)
(see (
4.7
)), we get

\displaystyle F_{1}u(n,0,Q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}a_{kj}(n)F_{1}F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}a_{kj}(n)(q^{n-k}F_{2}^{n-k}F_{1}+[n-k]F_{2}^{n-k-1}F_{3})F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}F_{1}K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}[n-k]a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k-1}F_{3}^{k+1}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\alpha^{-1}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}\Big(C_{1}-\frac{qKK_{2}^{-2}+q^{-1}K^{-1}K_{2}^{2}}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}}\Big)K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k+1}[n-k+1]a_{k-1,j}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k+1}Qv_{\eta},

where we used the identity
F_{1}F_{2}^{m}-q^{m}F_{2}^{m}F_{1}=[m]F_{2}^{m-1}F_{3}
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Substituting the formula
C_{1}=(q-q^{-1})^{-2}\big(q^{3-2n}K+q^{2n-3}K^{-1}-h_{n}(K,C_{1})\big)
into the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(q-q^{-1})^{2}F_{1}u(n,0,Q)+u(n,2,q^{-n-1}K^{-1}Q)+u(n,-2,q^{n+1}KQ)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}(q-q^{-1})^{2}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}C_{1}K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}(q^{n-2k-2j+1}-q^{n+1-2k})a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j-2}K^{1-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}(q^{n-2k-2j-1}-q^{-n-1+2k})a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j+2}K^{-2j-k-1}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k+1}\alpha(q-q^{-1})^{2}[n-k+1]a_{k-1,j}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k+1}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}h_{n}(K,C_{1})K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}(q^{3-2n}K+q^{2n-3}K^{-1})K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=-1}^{n-k-1}(q^{n-2k-2j-1}-q^{n+1-2k})a_{k,j+1}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-1-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k+1}(q^{n-2k-2j+1}-q^{-n-1+2k})a_{k,j-1}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k+1}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k+1}\alpha(q-q^{-1})^{2}[n-k+1]a_{k-1,j}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k+1}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=-1}^{n-k+1}F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}f_{kj}(K,C_{1})K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}h_{n}(K,C_{1})K^{-2j-k}Qv_{\eta}

where
a_{kj}(n)=0
whenever
k<0,j<0
or
k+j>n
and

\displaystyle f_{kj}(K,C_{1})=

\displaystyle a_{kj}(n)q^{n-2k-2j}(q^{3-2n}K+q^{2n-3}K^{-1})-(q^{n-2k-2j-1}-q^{n+1-2k})a_{k,j+1}(n)K^{-1}

\displaystyle-(q^{n-2k-2j+1}-q^{-n-1+2k})a_{k,j-1}(n)K+\alpha(q-q^{-1})^{2}[n-k+1]a_{k-1,j}(n)K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{kj}(n)\Big(q^{3n-2k-2j-3}K^{-1}-q^{2n-k-j-3}(q-q^{-1})[n-k-j]K^{-1}

\displaystyle q^{3-n-2k-2j}K+q^{3-n-k-j}(q-q^{-1})\frac{[n-k+1][k]+[n-2k-j+1][j]}{[n-k-j+1]}K\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{kj}(n)(q^{n-3}K^{-1}+q^{3-n}K).

Combining the above two formulas and noticing
f_{kj}(K,C_{1})=0
for
j=0,n-k+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(q-q^{-1})^{2}F_{1}u(n,0,Q)+u(n,2,q^{-n-1}K^{-1}Q)+u(n,-2,q^{n+1}KQ)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n,0,(q^{n-3}K^{-1}+q^{3-n}K)Q)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}h_{n}(K,C_{1})Qv_{\eta},

which is just the desired result for
l=0
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}[C_{1},K]
, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}u(n,l,Q)=

\displaystyle(q-q^{-1})^{-2}\Big(u(n,l,(q^{n-3}K^{-1}+q^{3-n}K)Q)

\displaystyle-q^{2nl}K_{2}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}q^{n-2k-2j}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k}h_{n}(K,C_{1})Qv_{\eta}\Big).

Proof.

Follows from the definition of
C_{1}
, Lemma
5.1
and the fact

\hskip 14.22636ptE_{1}u(n,l,Q)=\alpha q^{l}u(n,l,Q),\ \ K_{1}u(n,l,Q)=K_{2}^{-2}Ku(n,l,Q)=q^{n}u(n,l-2,KQ).\hskip 14.22636pt\qed

For convenience, we denote

\kappa_{\epsilon}=q^{-3n_{\epsilon}}\kappa,\quad c_{\epsilon}=\frac{q^{n_{\epsilon}-3}\kappa^{-1}+q^{3-n_{\epsilon}}\kappa}{(q-q^{-1})^{2}},\ \forall\ \epsilon\in\{+,-\}.

Corollary 5.3
.

In the module
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
, we have
K\bar{u}_{\epsilon}=\kappa_{\epsilon}\bar{u}_{\epsilon},C_{1}\bar{u}_{\epsilon}=c_{\epsilon}\bar{u}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon=+,-
.

Proof.

The first equation follows from

Ku(n,l,Q)=q^{-3n}u(n,l,KQ)
and the second one follows from
the fact
h_{n_{\epsilon}}(\kappa,c)=0
and Corollary
5.2
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

For any
(\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\kappa^{\prime}\neq 0
, we have another quotient module
V(\eta;\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})
.

(a)

\Hom_{U}(V(\eta;\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime}),V(\eta;\kappa,c))=0
if
(\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})\notin\{(\kappa,c),(\kappa_{+},c_{+}),(\kappa_{-},c_{-})\}
.

(b)

\End_{U}(V(\eta;\kappa,c))=\mathbb{C}\id
and
\Hom_{U}(V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon}),V(\eta;\kappa,c))=\mathbb{C}\phi_{\epsilon}
, where
\id
is the identity map,
\phi_{\epsilon}
is the
U
-module homomorphism defined by
\phi_{\epsilon}(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\eta})=\bar{u}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon=+,-
and
\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\eta}
is the image of
v_{\eta}
in
V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
.

(c)

In particular,
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
is indecomposable.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
, the set of Whittaker vectors of type
(\alpha,0)
in
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
is
spanned by
\bar{v}_{\eta}
and
\bar{u}_{\pm}=\bar{u}(n_{\pm},0,1)
, which are eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues

(\kappa,c),(\kappa_{\pm},c_{\pm})
respectively, with respect to the action of
(K,C_{1})
,
by Corollary
5.3

On the other hand,
\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\eta}\in V(\eta;\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is an eigenvector of eigenvalue
(\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})
with respect to the action of
(K,C_{1})
. Any
\phi\in\Hom_{U}(V(\eta;\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime}),V(\eta;\kappa,c))
must take
\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\eta}
to a multiple of one of
\bar{v}_{\eta},\bar{u}_{\pm}
.
The results follow since
V(\eta;\kappa^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is generated by
\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\eta}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

The module
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
has a basis
\{F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}\bar{u}_{\epsilon}\ |\ j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for

. Moreover, we have the
U
-module isomorphism:

{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)\cong V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon}),\ \forall\ \epsilon\in\{+,-\}.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\{F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}\bar{v}_{\eta}\ |\ j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a basis of

. On the other hand,
we have
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)=\spn\{F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}\bar{u}_{\epsilon}\ |\ j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, which is a linearly-independent subset since elements therein have distinct degrees.
The first result follows.

The
U
-module homomorphism
\phi_{\epsilon}
in Corollary
5.4
(b) is an isomorphism
from
V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
to
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
,
since it sends basis to basis, proving the second result.
∎

By Corollary

\bigoplus_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}u(n_{\epsilon},0,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\bigoplus_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}J(\eta;\kappa,c)v_{\eta}\Big)\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}u(n_{\epsilon},0,1)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\bigoplus_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}J(\eta;\kappa,c)v_{\eta}\Big)\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{(j,k,l)\in\Gamma}\mathbb{C}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}u(n_{\epsilon},l,1)\Big).

Set

.
Since

Theorem 5.6
.

Let notation be as before.

(a)

{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is a maximal submodule of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
.

(b)

{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is a minimal submodule of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
.

(c)

If
n_{+}=n_{-}
, then
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is the unique nonzero proper submodule of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
. In particular,
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
admits a unique composition series

0\subset{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)={\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)\subset V(\eta;\kappa,c).

(d)

If
n_{-}<n_{+}
, then
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)\subset{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
are the only two nonzero proper submodules of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
. In particular,
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
admits a unique composition series

0\subset{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)\subset{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)\subset V(\eta;\kappa,c).

Proof.

(a) Fix an
\epsilon\in\{+,-\}
. Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M(\eta)
that properly contains
{\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}={\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
.
Take any
w\in N\setminus{\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}
with minimal degree, then
by (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we can write

w=\sum_{(j,k,l)\in\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}}a_{j,k,l}F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}v_{\eta},

where only finitely many
a_{j,k,l}\in\mathbb{C}
are nonzero.

Set
\deg(w)=(j_{0},k_{0},l_{0})\in\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}
and
n_{0}=j_{0}+k_{0}
. If
n_{0}=0
, then
w=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{0,0,l}K_{2}^{l}v_{\eta}
, forcing
v_{\eta}\in N
by Lemma
3.4
and hence
N=M(\eta)
, contradiction.
So we have
n_{0}\geq 1
.

By Lemma
3.1
(4), we have
\deg((E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})w)<\deg w
and
\deg(E_{2}w)<\deg w
, hence
(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})w,E_{2}w\in{\widehat{W}}_{\epsilon}
.

On the other hand, both
(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})w
and
E_{2}w
are linear combinations of elements in
F_{2}^{j}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{l}\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]v_{\eta},0\leq k<n_{\epsilon}
by Lemma
3.1
, c.f. (
4.4
) and (
4.9
).
By (
5.1
), we must have
(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})w,E_{2}w\in W(\eta;\kappa,c)
.

Write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{p}w_{n_{i}}
with
0\neq w_{n_{i}}\in M_{n_{i}}
for distinct
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
By Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.1
(4), we also have

(E_{1}-\alpha q^{l_{0}})w_{n_{i}},E_{2}w_{n_{i}}\in W(\eta;\kappa,c)
, forcing each
w_{n_{i}}
to be a multiple of one
of the elements
u(n_{\pm},l_{0},1)\in M_{n_{\pm}}
or
K_{2}^{l_{0}}v_{\eta}\in M_{0}
. Hence
n_{i}\in\{0,n_{+},n_{-}\}
. Note that
n_{i}<n_{\epsilon}
for
i=0,1,\dots,p
.

Now take
\epsilon=-
, then we have
p=0
and
n_{0}=0
, which indicate that
w
is a nonzero multiple of
v_{\eta}
, contradiction. So
{\widehat{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is a maximal submodule of
M(\eta)
and hence
{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is a maximal submodule of
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
. This proves (a).

(b) By Lemma
5.5
, we have
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)\cong V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
as a
U
-module,
where
\kappa_{\epsilon}=q^{-3n_{\epsilon}}\kappa
,
c_{\epsilon}=(q-q^{-1})^{-2}(q^{n_{\epsilon}-3}\kappa^{-1}+q^{3-n_{\epsilon}}\kappa)
.
If
V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
is not irreducible, then
(\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
is critical.
Say,
h_{n^{\prime}}(\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})=0
for some
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, then
V(\eta;\kappa_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
has a Whittaker vector of
degree
(n^{\prime},n^{\prime},0)
and hence
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
has a Whittaker vector of
degree
(n^{\prime}+n_{\epsilon},n^{\prime}+n_{\epsilon},0)
, according to the isomorphism
\phi_{\epsilon}
in Corollary
5.5
and
5.4
). Since the Whittaker vector in
{\widetilde{W}}_{\epsilon}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is also a Whittaker vector in
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
, we must have
n_{\epsilon}=n_{-}
and
n^{\prime}+n_{\epsilon}=n_{+}
.
If
\epsilon=+
, this is impossible and hence
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)
is irreducible, proving (b).

(c) follows from a combination of (a), (b) and the fact that
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
is indecomposable by Corollary
5.4
.

(d) Continuing the argument in (b) and taking
\epsilon=-
.
Recall that
n_{-}<n_{+}
implies
\kappa=\pm q^{n_{+}+n_{-}-3}
and

\kappa_{-}=\pm q^{n_{+}-2n_{-}-3},\quad c_{-}=\pm(q-q^{-1})^{-2}(q^{-n_{+}}+q^{n_{+}}).

It is easy to check that
n^{\prime}=n_{+}-n_{-}
is the only integer such that
h_{n^{\prime}}(\kappa_{-},c_{-})=0
.
Then
{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)\cong V(\eta;\kappa_{-},c_{-})
has a unique nonzero proper submodule by (c).

If
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)\not\subseteq{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
, then
the minimality of
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)
and maximality of
{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)=0
imply

{\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)\cap{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)=0
and

V(\eta;\kappa,v)={\widetilde{W}}_{+}(\eta;\kappa,c)\oplus{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c),

contradicting the fact that
V(\eta;\kappa,c)
is indecomposable by Corollary
5.4
.
So
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}
is the only nonzero proper submodule of
{\widetilde{W}}_{-}
.

Now let
\bar{N}\subseteq V(\eta;\kappa,c)
be an arbitrary nonzero proper submodule other than
{\widetilde{W}}_{\pm}
.
If
\bar{N}\cap{\widetilde{W}}_{-}=0
, then
V(\eta;\kappa,c)=\bar{N}\oplus{\widetilde{W}}_{-}
, contradiction.
So
\bar{N}\cap{\widetilde{W}}_{-}={\widetilde{W}}_{+}
and hence
{\widetilde{W}}_{+}
is a proper submodule of
\bar{N}
.
By the argument in (a) for
\epsilon=+
, we have
\bar{w}=aK_{2}^{l_{0}}\bar{v}_{\eta}+\bar{u}(n_{-},l_{0},1)\in\bar{N}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
K_{2}^{l_{0}}\bar{v}_{\eta}
and
\bar{u}(n_{-},l_{0},1)
are eigenvectors of different eigenvalues with respect to the action of
K
and
C_{1}
, we see
\bar{u}(n_{-},l_{0},1)\in\bar{N}
, forcing
\bar{u}_{-}\in\bar{N}
and
{\widetilde{W}}_{-}\subseteq\bar{N}
, contradiction. (d) is proved.
∎

By the proof of the above theorem, we can determine the Whittaker vectors of the quotient modules of

Remark 5.8
.

Let notation be as in Theorem
5.6
. Suppose
n_{-}<n_{+}
.
If we denote

g_{n}=g_{n}(F_{2},F_{3},K_{2},K)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}a_{kj}(n)F_{2}^{n-k}F_{3}^{k}K_{2}^{2j}K^{-2j-k},\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N},

then
u(n,0,Q)=g_{n}Qv_{\eta}
for any
Q\in\mathbb{C}[K,C_{1}]
. In particular,
u_{\pm}=g_{n_{\pm}}v_{\eta}
.

By the proof of Theorem
5.6
,
V(\eta;\kappa_{-},c_{-})
has only one Whittaker vector of type
(\alpha,0)
up to a scalar,
which is just the image of
u(n_{+}-n_{-},0,1)=g_{n_{+}-n_{-}}v_{\eta}\in M(\eta)
in
V(\eta;\kappa_{-},c_{-})
and corresponds to

g_{n_{+}-n_{-}}\bar{u}_{-}
under the isomorphism
V(\eta;\kappa_{-},c_{-})\cong{\widetilde{W}}_{-}
(see Corollary
5.5
). Then we have
g_{n_{+}-n_{-}}\bar{u}_{-}=a\bar{u}_{+}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
.
Comparing the coefficients, we get
a=1
, which is equivalent to

g_{n_{+}-n_{-}}g_{n_{-}}\bar{v}_{\eta}=g_{n_{+}}\bar{v}_{\eta}
, or,

g_{n_{+}-n_{-}}g_{n_{-}}=g_{n_{+}}
. This motivates us to deduce the following simpler formula of
u(n,l,Q)
.

Corollary 5.9
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N},l\in\mathbb{Z},Q\in\mathbb{C}[K^{\pm 1},C_{1}]
, we have
u(n,l,Q)=q^{2nl}K_{2}^{l}g^{n}Qv_{\eta}
, where

g=g_{1}(F_{2},F_{3},K_{2},K)=F_{2}(1-q^{-2}K_{2}^{2}K^{-2})+\alpha(1-q^{-2})F_{3}K^{-1}.

Proof.

By the definition of
u(n,l,Q)
, we need only to prove
g_{n}=g_{1}^{n}
,
or equivalently,
g_{n+1}=g_{n}g_{1}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This can be done straightforward by induction.
∎

Finally, we conclude this paper with a characterization of simple Whittaker modules over
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
.
Let us first recall the center of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
from
[
LXZ
]
. Set

\tilde{F}_{3}=F_{2}F_{1}-qF_{1}F_{2}\ \text{and}\ \tilde{E}_{3}=E_{2}E_{1}-q^{-1}E_{1}E_{2}.

Then the center of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
is generated by
X_{1}X_{2},X_{1}^{3}K,X_{2}^{3}K^{-1}
, where

X_{1}=q^{-3}K^{-1}+qK_{1}+q^{-1}K_{1}^{-1}+(q-q^{-1})^{2}\big(F_{1}E_{1}+q^{-2}F_{2}E_{2}K_{1}^{-1}K_{2}^{-1}-q^{-1}F_{3}E_{3}K_{2}^{-1}\big),

X_{2}=q^{3}K+qK_{1}+q^{-1}K_{1}^{-1}+(q-q^{-1})^{2}\big(F_{1}E_{1}+q^{2}F_{2}E_{2}K_{1}K_{2}-q\tilde{F}_{3}\tilde{E}_{3}K_{2}\big).

Theorem 5.10
.

Any irreducible Whittaker module of type
\eta
is isomorphic to one of:

(a)

V(\eta;\kappa,c)=M(\eta)/W(\eta;\kappa,c)
with
(\kappa,c)
non-critical;

(b)

V(\eta;\kappa,c)/V(\eta;\kappa_{-},c_{-})\cong V(\eta;\kappa,c)/{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)\cong M(\eta)/{\widehat{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
with
(\kappa,c)
critical.

Proof.

Let
V
be an irreducible Whittaker
U
-module of type
\eta
.
Then
X_{1}X_{2},X_{1}^{3}K,X_{2}^{3}K^{-1}
act as scalars on
V
, say,

x,a^{3},b^{3}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
x^{3}=a^{3}b^{3}
, so we may assume
x=ab
by adjusting the value of
a
with a cubic root multiple.

Let
w
be a nonzero type-
\eta
Whittaker vector of
V
and noticing
E_{2}w=0
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle X_{1}X_{2}w=((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{-3}K^{-1})((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{3}K)w=abw,

\displaystyle X_{1}^{3}Kw=K((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{-3}K^{-1})^{3}w=a^{3}w,

\displaystyle X_{2}^{3}K^{-1}w=K^{-1}((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{3}K)^{3}w=b^{3}w,

Combining these expressions, we can deduce

(q^{18}K^{6}+(3ab-3-q^{-3}b^{3})q^{12}K^{4}+(q^{3}a^{3}+3-3ab)q^{6}K^{2}-1)w=0.

Then there exist some nonzero
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
and polynomial
f_{1}(K)\in\mathbb{C}[K]
such that
f_{1}(K)w\neq 0
and
(K-\kappa)f_{1}(K)w=0
.

On the other hand, by (
5.3
) we also have

\kappa((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{-3}\kappa^{-1})^{3}f_{1}(K)w=K((q-q^{-1})^{2}C_{1}+q^{-3}K^{-1})^{3}f_{1}(K)w=a^{3}f_{1}(K)w.

Similarly, we can find
c\in\mathbb{C}
and polynomial
f_{2}(C_{1})\in\mathbb{C}[C_{1}]
such that
f_{2}(C_{1})f_{1}(K)w\neq 0
and
(C_{1}-c)f_{2}(C_{1})f_{1}(K)w=0
.

Since
w^{\prime}=f_{2}(C_{1})f_{1}(K)w
is still a nonzero Whittaker vector of type
\eta
, there exists
a surjective
U
-module homomorphism
\phi:M(\eta)\rightarrow V
such that
\phi(v_{\eta})=w^{\prime}
.
It is clear that
W(\eta;\kappa,c)\subseteq\ker\phi
.
So
\phi
induce a nonzero
U
-module homomorphism
\bar{\phi}:V(\eta;\kappa,c)\rightarrow V
.
As a result,
V\cong V(\eta;\kappa,c)
if
(\kappa,c)
is non-critical and

V\cong V(\eta;\kappa,c)/{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
if
(\kappa,c)
is critical by Theorem
4.7
and Theorem

5.6
respectively.
∎

Theorem 5.11
.

Any maximal submodule of
M(\eta)
is one of:

(a)

W(\eta;\kappa,c)
with
(\kappa,c)
non-critical;

(b)

{\widehat{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
with
(\kappa,c)
critical.

Proof.

Let
N
be a maximal submodule of
M(\eta)
and
I=I(N)
.
Obviously,
v_{\eta}\notin N
and
\bar{v}_{\eta}\in M(\eta)/N
, the image of
v_{\eta}\in M(\eta)
,
is a nonzero Whittaker vector of type
\eta
.

By Theorem
5.10
, there exists a
U
-module isomorphism
\phi:M(\eta)/N\rightarrow V
,
where
V=V(\eta;\kappa,c)
for some non-critical pair
(\kappa,c)

or
V=V(\eta;\kappa,c)/{\widetilde{W}}_{-}(\eta;\kappa,c)
for some critical pair
(\kappa,c)
.

Note that
\phi(\bar{v}_{\eta})
is a Whittaker vector of type
(\alpha,0)
in
V
,
and hence a scalar multiple of the image of
v_{\eta}
in
V
by Corollary
5.7
.
Hence
J(\kappa,c)\phi(\bar{v}_{\eta})=0
and also
J(\kappa,c)\bar{v}_{\eta}=0
.
We have
W(\eta;\kappa,c)\subseteq N
and the results follow from Theorem
4.7

and Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

Note that the above results are independent of
\eta
, that is, maximal submodules of a universal Whittaker module and irreducible Whittaker modules with respect to any Whittaker function can be described in the same way.

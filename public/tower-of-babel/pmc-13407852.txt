Multi-scenario simulation of spatiotemporal changes of land use and ecosystem services in the plateau mountain city
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhang, Kouqiang Zhang, Shu’na Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe ecosystem of plateau mountain cities is highly vulnerable to damage and possesses limited ecological resilience. Influenced by human activities, they face prominent issues such as ecosystem degradation. Scenario-based analysis of land use and ecosystem services (ESs) is therefore essential for ensuring ecological sustainability in such regions, taking Kunming, a typical plateau mountain city, as the study area. We employed an integrated modeling approach that combined Markov-FLUS and InVEST to examine water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality under different land use scenarios from 2030 to 2050. Trade-offs/synergies among ESs were examined through Spearman correlation analysis. Results demonstrate that: (1) Under the natural development scenario (NDS), construction land will expand substantially. Under the cropland protection scenario (CPS), construction land will expand slightly, and cropland will increase. Under the ecological protection scenario (EPS), forest land and grassland will increase, while construction land will remain almost unchanged. (2) Under NDS and CPS, water yield will increase, mainly in the metropolitan center of Kunming and its adjacent areas. However, soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality will decrease in these areas. Under EPS, water yield will decrease, while soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality will increase. (3) There will be a trade-off enhancement of ESs in Kunming under NDS, while synergies will be enhanced under CPS and EPS. This study provides a scientific foundation for the adjustment of land use structure, the optimization of ecosystems, and sustainable urban development in cities with similar plateau mountain characteristics.Keywords: Ecosystem services, Land use simulation, Trade-offs/synergies, Markov-FLUS model, InVEST model, Plateau mountain citySubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences

Introduction

Ecosystem services (ESs) are the benefits that humans directly or indirectly obtain from ecosystems. They provide the environmental conditions necessary for human survival as well as offer the essential goods for human life-production1,2. As a core component of the United Nations Millennium Ecosystem Assessment3, ESs provide a fundamental foundation for societal development and are closely related to human well-being4. Nevertheless, land use changes brought about by human activity have a substantial impact on urban ecosystems, especially in plateau mountain cities. Characterized by complex and diverse geological environments, these cities exhibit pronounced natural resource constraints and heightened ecological sensitivity5. Their land use changes frequently trigger issues such as soil erosion, biodiversity loss, and environmental pollution6–8. Coupled with rapid urbanization that continuously disturbs and damages the ecological environment, the carrying capacity of ecosystems has gradually weakened, significantly exacerbating challenges in land use management and environmental protection, posing threats to urban ecological security9,10. Therefore, given limited land resources, it is essential to thoroughly explore future changes in land use and ESs in plateau mountain cities for urban land use planning, ecosystem restoration, and environmental protection.

Research indicates that by integrating ESs assessment models and land use simulation models, future ESs can be predicted and evaluated. ESs assessment models such as MIMES11, SoLVES12, CITYgreen13, and InVSET14 have been proposed. Among them, the InVEST model can handle large datasets, accurately quantifiable results, and clear spatial expression, making the model a reliable choice for assessing ESs15,16. In terms of land use simulation, the process of land use change in a region is simulated via the use of land use simulation models. Currently, quantity prediction and spatial prediction are the two main types of models. The Markov model is representative of quantity prediction models17,18. Cellular automata19, CLUE-S20, FLUS21, and PLUS22 are examples of spatial prediction models. In contrast, the FLUS model builds upon the cellular automata model by integrating Artificial Neural Network (ANN) algorithms and a roulette wheel selection mechanism. This integration enables the model to effectively handle intricate local land use interactions and competition, achieving more realistic simulations of the geographical distribution of land use23,24. Moreover, it demonstrates high accuracy in simulating urban land use25. Although improving spatial prediction capabilities, the FLUS mode has certain drawbacks when it comes to predicting land amount26. Coupling multiple models presents distinct advantages, which can solve the constraints of individual model application and yield more rigorous and reliable research results. To accomplish the twin benefits of quantity prediction and spatial distribution prediction, as well as to enable flexible modeling of land use changes across multi-scenarios, we combined the Markov and FLUS models for land use simulation.

Land use modeling and ESs prediction have been maturely applied in the urban field and extensive research has been carried out in recent years. Lyu et al. (2019)27 employed scenario analysis to demonstrate that land use patterns under the ecological conservation policy scenario could mitigate the negative impacts of urbanization on carbon storage. Gomes et al. (2021)28 proposed that land use/land cover (LULC) changes under different scenarios and their effects on habitat quality were key indicators of ESs supply. Mallick et al. (2022)29 accurately predicted carbon storage in Abha City, Saudi Arabia, for 2040 by integrating ANN-CA with the InVEST model. Li et al. (2022)30 simulated habitat quality under various land use scenarios in Tianjin, China, for 2030 using PLUS and InVEST models, revealing that the average habitat quality followed the order: ecological protection scenario > natural development scenario > economic construction scenario. Parvar et al. (2024)31 utilized urban remote sensing data to predict LULC changes and mapped urban carbon storage under five scenarios in 2050. Cuellar and Perez (2023)32 employed Markov chain and FLUS-CA models to simulate and analyze land cover changes in Bogotá, Colombia, providing a foundation for ESs research. Hua et al. (2024)33 combined the FLUS and InVEST models to predict land use and ESs under different scenarios for Xiamen, China, in 2030, while analyzing trade-offs/synergies among ESs. In summary, existing city-scale studies have predominantly focused on individual ESs such as carbon storage and habitat quality, whereas comprehensive studies on the prediction of multiple ESs and their interrelationships are still limited, especially for the plateau mountain city. Furthermore, most existing literature focuses on studying ESs under single-year, multiple-scenario land use, with relatively few studies conducting multi-year, multi-scenario simulations. Research spanning multiple years and scenarios can better reflect the future trends of ESs. Therefore, investigating land use and ESs in the plateau mountain city under multi-year, multi-scenario frameworks is critically important.

Kunming, a typical plateau mountain city in southwest China, serves as a vital gateway connecting China to South Asia, Southeast Asia, and even the Middle East, Southern Europe, and Africa. This city not only has a unique location advantage but also holds a crucial role in the national development strategy. As one of the few low-latitude plateau cities in the world, Kunming features complex geological structures, dramatic topographic variations, and pronounced vertical climatic differences. These challenging environmental conditions inherently render its ecosystem notably vulnerable. Against this backdrop, rapid urbanization has further exacerbated the human-land conflicts in the region, significantly impacting local ecosystem structure and function. This has led to various ecological issues, including soil erosion, declining carbon sequestration capacity, and habitat degradation, which collectively pose serious threats to the city’s sustainable development. Consequently, ecosystem maintenance and ecological environment protection will be crucial strategic tasks for Kunming’s future development. To reveal the law of ecological evolution of plateau mountain cities and formulate scientific development strategies, investigating how land use and ESs would evolve in Kunming under different scenarios is crucial.

The study’s specific aims are as follows: (1) Through the integrated modeling method, utilizing the Markov-FLUS model to predict land use changes in the plateau mountain city of Kunming under three distinct development scenarios from 2030 to 2050; (2) employing the InVEST model to evaluate spatiotemporal variations in four vital ESs: water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality in Kunming under three scenarios, analyzing their spatiotemporal heterogeneity; (3) applying Spearman correlation analysis to examine the trade-offs/synergies among these ESs and their temporal dynamics across various scenarios, revealing interaction patterns of ESs to the plateau environment. These studies can provide key methodology and a theoretical foundation for the optimization of land use structure and ecosystem management in plateau mountain cities and help the cities to develop in a high-quality and sustainable way.

Materials and methods

Study area

Kunming (10–10340 E, 23–2633 N) is situated in the central part of Yunnan Province, China. It administers seven municipal districts (Chenggong, Wuhua, Panlong, Guandu, Xishan, Jinning, and Dongchuan), one county-level city (Anning), and six counties (Songming, Yiliang, Fumin, Shilin, Luquan, and Xundian), as illustrated in Fig. 1. The terrain of Kunming slopes gradually from north to south, with lower elevations in the east and west and a raised central area. The city is characterized by a predominantly plateau-mountain landscape, where hills and mountains make up around 85% of the total area, while valleys and basins account for the remaining 15%. Kunming encompasses three major river systems: the Yangtze, Pearl, and Red Rivers. It is bordered by Dian Lake to the south and the Santai, Gongwang, and Liangwang Mountains to the north. The coexistence of towering mountains, vast rivers, and interspersed basins creates significant topographical variations, making Kunming a typical plateau mountain city. With a multi-year average temperature of around 15C and a yearly precipitation of roughly 1,000 mm, Kunming has a subtropical plateau monsoon climate. Its low-latitude plateau location contributes to a “perpetual spring-like” climate, earning it the reputation as the “Spring City”. However, Kunming’s land use structure has changed dramatically due to fast urbanization and socioeconomic growth. The construction land area expanded by 1.5 times between 2000 and 202034. This unchecked growth has caused ecological development to lag behind economic progress, and if such land use patterns continue in the upcoming decades, the urban ecological environment will suffer greatly.

Data sources

The following categories of data were utilized (Table1): (1) Land use data, with 2010 serving as the base year and 2020 as the validation year; (2) Topographic data; (3) Meteorological data; (4) Soil data; (5) Socioeconomic data; (6) Traffic data. Natural environments, socioeconomic conditions, and transportation accessibility are all strongly correlated with changes in land use. We selected the following drivers of land use change based on Kunming’s regional context and data availability: Natural factors (DEM, slope, aspect, temperature, and precipitation), Socioeconomic factors (GDP and population), Transportation accessibility factors (distance from national highways, provincial roads, county roads, railways, highways and rivers through Euclidean distance tools), Policy constraints (nature reserves and basic farmland protection zones). To ensure spatial consistency, all data were preprocessed in ArcGIS, with a unified Krasovsky_1940_Albers projection coordinate system.

Research framework

Based on land use data and the factors driving land use change, utilizing the Markov-FLUS model to project the spatial distribution of future land use in Kunming: first employing the Markov model to forecast quantitative demands of land use types under the natural development scenario, cropland protection scenario, and ecological protection scenario from 2030 to 2050, then applying the FLUS model to simulate the geospatial location of land use types. Following the prediction, four key ESs indicators were evaluated in Kunming from 2030 to 2050, combining the generated land use spatial distribution maps with other datasets to assess ESs using the InVEST model. Methods

Land use simulation

Scenarios design

Scenarios design

The Kunming Territorial Spatial Master Plan (2021–2035) (https://www.km.gov.cn/) (hereinafter referred to as the Plan) served as the guide for this research’s land use scenario design. The Plan emphasizes the establishment of a territorial space development and protection pattern based on food security, ecological conservation, and high-quality development. Consequently, the present study devised three scenarios to simulate future land use, namely Natural Development Scenario (NDS), Cropland Protection Scenario (CPS), and Ecological Protection Scenario (EPS), to reveal the differences in land use changes in Kunming based on different development objectives. Considering the historical land use patterns, future development goals, and social benefit requirements of the study area, specific scenarios were designed using Markov models and drawing on existing research in the field regarding the modification of Markov transition probability matrices. The baseline Markov transition probability matrix used for scenario setting is provided in Table S1 of the Supplementary Materials.

(1) NDS. This scenario simulates unrestricted urban growth patterns, allowing land use changes to evolve freely according to historical trends observed in Kunming between 2010-2020. The future quantity demand of each land category is forecasted using the Markov model within the coupled framework. This serves as the quantity demand parameter in the FLUS model, forming the foundation for other scenario settings.

(2) CPS. Cropland is fundamental to safeguard national food security. The Plan emphasizes implementing Kunming’s cropland protection tasks with food security as the primary consideration. Kunming, as the capital city, undertakes the fundamental function of ensuring regional food security. Therefore, this scenario builds upon the NDS by designating permanent basic farmland protection zones as restricted conversion areas. Additionally, drawing on relevant research35,36, the Markov transition probability matrix is adjusted to reduce the probability of cropland converting to other land categories while increasing the probability of other land converting to cropland (Table S2). There is a 60% decrease in conversion probability from cropland to construction land. Meanwhile, 30% decreased transition rates from cropland to non-construction land types. Conversely, a 50% rise in the transition probability from the two land types of unused land and construction land to cropland, respectively.

(3) EPS. Within the context of the current territorial spatial planning, ecological conservation and restoration have become crucial components. The government’s objectives align with enhancing eco-environmental stability and securing Kunming’s role as an ecological security barrier in the upper Yangtze River basin. Building upon the NDS, this scenario emphasizes the protection of ecological land and restricts the expansion of urban land. Strict measures are implemented to limit the overall increase in built-up areas, control the conversion of ecological land to other land types, and curb the disorderly expansion of construction land. With reference to relevant studies35,37, the Markov transition probability matrix was modified (Table S3). There is a 50% decrease in the transition probability from the two land types of forest land and grassland to construction land, and a 40% decrease in the transition probability to other land types. 30% rose the probability of transforming cropland, construction land, and unused land to forest land and grassland. Furthermore, there is a 30% decrease in conversion rates from cropland and water area to construction land.

ESs evaluation

The InVEST model was utilized to assess ESs in Kunming, which calculates the biophysical quantities of ESs while reflecting their geographical distribution characteristics using GIS tools, and is a comprehensive tool for evaluating ESs47. For simulating and predicting future ESs and their changes in Kunming, we comprehensively considered both the structural characteristics of Kunming’s ecosystems and the local government’s management priorities, selecting four essential ES: water yield, soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality. As a low-latitude plateau city, Kunming experiences distinct wet and dry seasons characterized by concentrated precipitation and high evaporation rates, making the study of water yield particularly indicative of water resource regulation capacity. The plateau’s mountainous areas feature thin soil layers where soil erosion can readily lead to irreversible rocky desertification, underscoring the critical importance of soil conservation for maintaining soil ecological security. As the core area of the Yunnan carbon sink pilot, the carbon storage function of high-altitude forests in Kunming plays a pivotal role in achieving regional carbon neutrality. Kunming’s unique vertical climate zones foster rich species, but the mountainous terrain exacerbates habitat fragmentation, the research on habitat quality holds significant value for preserving regional biodiversity. The calculation principles and formulas are provided in Equations S(1)-S(15) in the Supplementary Material. The relevant input parameters for the InVEST model, based on the actual conditions of Kunming and related references, are listed in Tables S6-S10.

Trade-offs/synergies of ESs

The mutually reinforcing and beneficial phenomena between ESs are referred to as synergies, while the mutually constraining and opposing phenomena between ESs are termed trade-offs48. To elucidate the relationships among multiple ESs, this study employed Spearman correlation to analyze the trade-offs/synergies among four key ESs under different development scenarios in Kunming across future time periods. The direction and strength of these interactions were determined using correlation coefficients, with a range of [−1, 1]. A range of [−1, 0] indicates a negative correlation, while [0, 1] signifies a positive correlation. Positive correlations between ESs represent synergies, with higher values representing stronger synergies, while negative correlations denote trade-offs, with lower values representing stronger trade-offs49.

To address the inconsistency in units among different ESs, the results of each ES were normalized in this study. Sampling was then conducted in ArcGIS 10.8. Based on the spatial extent of the study area, different sampling scales were compared, including 1 km, 2 km, and 3 km grids. Although the 1 km scale can capture local variability in greater detail, the large number of sampling points (21000) makes the results more susceptible to local outliers. In contrast, the 3 km scale results in too few sampling points (2332), which may be insufficient to adequately represent the spatial variability of ESs and may reduce the reliability of spatial statistical analyses. The 2 km scale ensures a relatively even distribution of sampling points (5250) within Kunming and effectively captures the spatial heterogeneity of different ESs. Therefore, a 2 km sampling scale was adopted in this study, generating 5250 sampling points to extract data for each ES. Subsequently, R version 4.3.2 was used to analyze the direction and strength of pairwise interaction relationships among ESs.

Simulation accuracy validation

To validate the accuracy of the FLUS model, this study provided the accuracy rates for each land type (Table S4) and the producer’s accuracy and user’s accuracy (Table S5) and employed overall accuracy and the Kappa coefficient as metrics to evaluate the goodness-of-fit of land use simulation. The simulation accuracy increases with the proximity of the Kappa coefficient and overall accuracy to 1, and the simulation effect and consistency of the model are superior when the Kappa coefficient exceeds 0.8050. Utilizing the selected driving factors and incorporating 2010 land use data to simulate Kunming’s land use distribution in 2020, then juxtaposing the simulation result in 2020 with Kunming’s real land use in 2020. Our simulation achieved an overall accuracy of 0.916 and a Kappa coefficient of 0.875, both surpassing the 0.80 threshold. The above validation demonstrates that the FLUS model maintains a high degree of simulation accuracy for Kunming while accurately reflecting the dynamics of actual land use change.

Results

Land use changes under different scenarios

Under NDS, construction land will undergo rampant expansion. Between 2030 and 2050, the construction land area will increase by 454.56 km2, 853.37 km2, and 1205.75 km2 compared to the 2020 land use status. Meanwhile, it rose from 1193.29 km2 in 2020 to 2399.04 km2 by 2050 (a 101.04% increase). Conversely, cropland, forest land, and grassland will continue to decline, decreasing by 465.97 km2, 383.70 km2, and 426.08 km2, respectively. Under CPS, cropland will reverse its decreasing trend observed in NDS and instead exhibit a gradual increase. Compared with 2020, the cropland area will expand by 78.50 km2, 159.22 km2, and 236.05 km2 during 2030–2050. By 2050, the cropland area will have grown by 5.94% to its high of 4213.06 km2. The growth rate of construction land area (31.24%) will be lower than that under NDS. Under EPS, forest land and grassland will show a modest expansion trend compared with 2020, increasing by 142.08 km2 (1.50%) and 73.36 km2 (1.26%), respectively, by 2050. Although the magnitude of increase is limited, forest land and grassland will no longer experience continuous decline as seen in NDS. Construction land will essentially remain unchanged based on the implementation of the ecological protection strategy. In these three scenarios, the water area and unused land will not vary much (Fig. 3).

Area of land use in Kunming under three scenarios.

The spatial distribution of land use in Kunming under the three scenarios from 2030 to 2050 (Fig. 4) will largely maintain the 2020 spatial pattern (Figure S1). Forest land and grassland are widely distributed in Kunming. Water area is primarily concentrated in Dianchi Lake. Construction land is mainly distributed around Dianchi Lake. Cropland is distributed around the construction land, and the unused land is less and dotted. Under different scenarios, the NDS will see construction land expanding outward from Dianchi Lake, encroaching significantly on forest land, grassland, and cropland. In the CPS, driven by cropland conservation policies, cropland will no longer be encroached upon, with newly added cropland appearing in scattered patches, while the spread of urban areas will be constrained. Construction land will almost completely stop expanding under EPS, and there will be a successful conservation of forest and grassland from further encroachment.

Overall, different development objectives exert significant influence on land use evolution. The NDS reveals the persistent displacement of ecological land under unrestrained expansion. The CPS effectively safeguards agricultural land but fails to fundamentally curb the expansion of construction land. The EPS demonstrates that achieving “zero growth” in construction land and a “stabilization and recovery” of forest and grassland areas. This demonstrates that ecological conservation is a critical prerequisite for achieving sustainable land use and ecosystem stability.

This distribution shows a robust geographical pattern of carbon storage across Kunming. Under both NDS and CPS, Carbon storage in the major urban area of Kunming and the surrounding regions will have a declining trend, with the NDS exhibiting more extensive areas of reduction than the CPS. Under EPS, a small part of the northern region of Kunming will see an increase in carbon storage, while other regions will still experience decreases. Notably, the reduction of carbon storage under EPS will be substantially less severe relative to both NDS and CPS (Fig. 7).

The spatial distribution of habitat quality in Kunming mirrors similar of carbon storage, exhibiting a distinct “high in the north, low in the south” geographical distribution. Areas dominated by forest and grassland demonstrate higher habitat quality, while other land types show lower values. Under NDS, habitat quality will undergo significant degradation in Kunming’s main urban area and surrounding regions. Habitat quality will also decline under CPS, though with a reduced spatial extent of degradation compared to NDS. In contrast, the EPS will maintain stable habitat quality with no observable degradation, and will even exhibit slight improvements in northeastern and southern regions (Fig. 8).

In summary, there is a significant heterogeneity in the geographical distribution patterns of different ESs as well as in spatial changes of ESs in the three scenarios. These changes display differentiated responses to land use change. Water yield is highly sensitive to human activities, and its increase reflects the process of urban expansion. Soil conservation remain relatively stable due to its strong dependence on topographic conditions. The shared “higher in the north and lower in the south” pattern of carbon storage and habitat quality jointly indicates that the core ecological security barrier of Kunming is located in its northern and central regions.

Discussion

Responses of ESs and their trade-offs/synergies to land use change

Changes in land use correspond to changes in ESs51. In Kunming, from 2020 to 2050, land use changes are particularly pronounced and directly impact ESs under different scenarios. Water yield will show an increasing trend under both NDS and CPS, with the primary growth areas concentrated around Kunming’s main urban zone, where construction land continues to expand. Construction land features extensive impervious surfaces and low evapotranspiration52,53, which makes its water yield capacity relatively strong54. Therefore, when construction land increases, water yield also increases. This is primarily because the impervious surface of construction land not only reduces evapotranspiration but also accelerates the convergence of surface runoff, forming a positive feedback mechanism of “expansion-increased runoff-rapid increase in water yield”. Under EPS, water yield will be expected to decline as forest land and grassland areas expand, while construction land remains basically unchanged. Compared to the impervious surfaces of construction land, forests can absorb more water55. Furthermore, Kunming’s unique subtropical plateau monsoon climate provides favorable conditions for vegetation growth and enhances its water absorption capacity. Thus, as forest land area increases, water yield in Kunming decreases. Soil conservation exhibits distinct spatial variation characteristics but relatively minor changes. This is primarily because soil conservation is more strongly influenced by slope gradient56,57. Kunming’s topography, which slopes gradually from north to south, also contributes to a corresponding decrease in soil conservation from north to south. Although Kunming exhibits significant elevation differences characteristic of plateau mountainous terrain, land use changes primarily occur around the urban area, where the terrain is relatively gentle. This gentle topography results in soil conservation being less affected by land-type variations. Accordingly, compared to the other three ESs, soil conservation exhibits a relatively muted response to land use change. Its dynamics are more dependent on stable factors such as topography, making it less sensitive to short-term policy-driven scenarios and unlikely to show pronounced fluctuations. Nevertheless, this does not imply that variations in soil conservation can be disregarded. Although the relative rate of change remains modest (0.06%–0.14%), the absolute magnitude is considerable. Under NDS, soil conservation increases by approximately 1.0 million tons, while under EPS it rises by about 2.49 million tons. This means that millions of tons of soil are effectively retained, thereby preventing associated nutrient depletion and river siltation, and delivering tangible ecological benefits for water bodies such as Dianchi Lake. Carbon storage and habitat quality under NDS and CPS exhibit degradation trends around Kunming’s main urban areas, primarily due to land use changes. The amount of land used for urban building has expanded greatly in these regions, encroaching on forest land and grassland, with their areas continuing to decrease while the amount of land used for construction land is increasing. Forest land has a high carbon storage content58, and high vegetation coverage in areas where forest land and grassland are widely distributed contributes to biological survival59. The carbon storage capacity of natural vegetation and its ability to mitigate habitat fragmentation are amplified by terrain in the context of the highland mountainous topography. The process of land use conversion, such as the encroachment of construction land on ecological land, has been shown to result in a significant intensification of carbon loss and habitat fragmentation60. Consequently, diminishment of forest land and grassland directly causes a decline in carbon storage as well as habitat quality. Furthermore, the encroachment of construction land in NDS is more serious compared to CPS, and more forest and grassland are being occupied. Therefore, the degradation of carbon storage and habitat quality in NDS is more serious than in the CPS. Under EPS, mainly due to the stringent regulations on urban construction development and ecological land conservation measures, carbon storage and habitat quality have not been significantly degraded.

Considering the complexity and interconnectivity of ESs in plateau mountain cities, this study further investigated the interrelationship among ESs in Kunming as well as their changes under different scenarios. It advances the research on ESs’ trade‑offs/synergies from static description to dynamic mechanism, giving urban ecosystem management strategies a scientific foundation. A certain relationship exists between the change of ESs’ trade-off/synergy and land use change, as documented in the existing literature61–63. Across the three scenarios, there is a trade-off between water yield and carbon storage. High water yield areas in Kunming are mostly distributed in construction land concentrated zones, where inadequate vegetation coverage constrains carbon sequestration capacity. Consequently, water yield and carbon storage show a significant trade-off as a whole. Similarly, intensive urban development led to simplified vegetation structures in construction land areas, which restricts the growth and development of animals and plants, and makes water yield and habitat quality show a significant trade-off. In contrast, carbon storage and habitat quality exhibit a strong synergy. Areas with high carbon storage typically correspond to the forest land concentration area, with dense vegetation coverage, which provides necessary habitat for the survival of animals, and also constitutes a high biodiversity level. Consequently, carbon storage demonstrated a robust synergistic relationship with habitat quality. The findings that construction land represents a functional zone with high water yield but low carbon storage/habitat quality, while forest land constitutes a functional zone with high carbon storage and high habitat quality, further confirm that trade-offs/synergies in ESs are driven by the structural characteristics of land use composition. Furthermore, we found that under different scenario settings, the trade-offs/synergies among ESs change as land use evolve. The expansion of construction land is a key driver pushing the deterioration of ESs relationships toward trade-offs, whereas the increase in forest land is a major driver promoting the enhancement of synergistic relationships among ESs. Under NDS, population growth and economic demand increase, and the intensity of human activities becomes larger, which will cause Kunming’s land use structure to become unbalanced, with a notable rise in the area used for development, destroying the ecosystem. As a result, the provisioning and requirements of ESs are out of balance, and the trade-offs among ESs deteriorate. Compared to NDS, the synergies among ESs are enhanced to varying degrees under CPS and EPS. Under CPS, with the implementation of relevant measures, the incursion of construction land on cropland is alleviated, so safeguarding cropland to a degree. Under EPS, there is a substantial increase in the areas of grassland, forest land, and water area, while the expansion of construction land is halted. Both CPS and EPS demonstrate varying degrees of optimization in the ecological configuration of land use patterns. The enhancement of the synergies has improved ecosystem stability and functional coordination, fostering a gradually improving ecosystem condition.

Implications for land planning and ecosystem management

Different development scenarios play distinct critical roles in maintaining ESs. Adherence to the NDS in Kunming would stimulate urban economic growth. However, it would inevitably result in the extension of construction land, accelerated urbanization, and intensified population agglomeration. This would create ecological antagonism linking urban development to the natural environment, ultimately driving the disruption of both ecosystem integrity and the ecological environment. Conversely, land use patterns and ESs under CPS and EPS would maintain a better level of development, especially in EPS.

Based on this study, the following proposals are proposed to promote rational land utilization, balanced ecosystem development, and sustainable urban growth in Kunming: (1) Regulating urban development. Given the ecological vulnerability of Kunming’s plateau mountainous areas, it is recommended to strictly control the unregulated growth of construction land, with a focus on protecting the Dianchi Lake Basin and core forest areas. A red line for the growth of construction land around Dianchi Lake should be delineated to restrict large-scale development activities, avoiding the negative impacts on ESs caused by excessive construction land expansion (101.04% increase) under NDS, which would exacerbate ecosystem degradation. Furthermore, implementing a compact development model in Kunming’s main urban areas and surrounding regions is advised. Particularly in built-up areas such as Wuhua and Panlong districts, urban land redevelopment should be encouraged, while disorderly sprawl toward the southern (Jinning) and western (Anning) shores of Dianchi Lake should be avoided. By enhancing land use efficiency and reducing ecological land encroachment, a balance between urban development and ecological protection can be achieved. (2) Strengthening ecological protection and restoration. Under EPS, the area of forest land and grassland increases (1.50% and 1.26%, respectively), and the overall ESs improved compared to NDS, demonstrating the positive effects of conservation measures. Kunming needs to intensify the safeguarding and rehabilitation of ecological land, especially in the northern regions (Luquan, Dongchuan, Xundian), through afforestation, grassland restoration, and other ecological restoration measures to enhance carbon sequestration, protect habitats of rare species, and prevent soil erosion. Additionally, the spatial connection between key ecological nodes is optimized, and the fragmented ecological land is connected as much as possible to enhance the connectivity of the ecosystem. Under CPS, cropland area increased (+5.94%), while soil conservation decreased by 0.12%, reflecting the need to remain vigilant about the loss of soil and water conservation functions while ensuring food security. Particularly in the southern concentrated cropland areas (Jinning, Shilin), contour farming and protective tillage on terraces should be promoted to reduce soil erosion caused by cropland expansion. (3) Optimizing synergistic management of ESs. Under the EPS, trade-offs between water yield and carbon storage/habitat quality are alleviated, and there is a trend of synergistic optimization. Prioritization in ESs management should be clarified, avoiding the pursuit of increased water yield at the expense of carbon sequestration and habitat quality. Solutions that achieve multiple benefits–specifically, water retention, carbon sequestration, and biodiversity conservation–should be prioritized. For instance, adopting the development paradigm of EPS, protecting and restoring forest and grassland ecosystems. Additionally, ecological transition zones (composite zones of trees, shrubs, and grasses) should be established between built-up areas and forest land to alleviate the strong trade-off between water yield and carbon storage. These measures aim to reduce trade-offs as well as enhance synergies in ESs, thereby achieving sustainable development of plateau mountain cities. These recommendations provide scientific reference for planning and management in plateau mountain cities facing similar development challenges. However, the proposed policies and plans are indicative rather than prescriptive.

Strengths and limitations

This study demonstrates several key strengths: (1) The coupling of Markov and FLUS models for multi-scenario land use simulation in plateau mountain city fully integrates the strengths of both models: the Markov model provides reliable quantitative constraints for land use quantity changes, while the FLUS model achieves refined spatial simulation of land use distribution. This combines their respective advantages in quantitative prediction and spatial allocation, achieving dual benefits in land use prediction. This coupling also supports flexible modeling of land use changes under multiple scenarios, thereby enhancing the rationality and applicability of the simulation results, representing an innovation in the application of the method. (2) Rather than limiting the analysis to ESs under single-year multi-scenario projections, we simulated ESs under multi-year, multi-scenario land use patterns, providing more comprehensive insights into long-term trends in ESs and their interactive patterns. (3) Compared with existing FLUS-InVEST coupling studies, this study is the first to apply the FLUS-InVEST framework to a typical plateau mountain city. Taking Kunming, a representative plateau mountain city, as the research area. We reveal the responses of ESs to land use changes in urban areas characterized by special topography and fragile ecological environments in different future development scenarios. This improves the existing land use and ESs linkage theory. In general, it offers valuable scientific support for phased land management, ecosystem conservation, and urban planning in similar cities.

However, some limitations should be acknowledged. Due to data constraints, some key parameters (such as biophysical parameters for certain ESs) of the InVEST model in scenario simulation relied on empirical values in historical research or literature, which may not fully capture the dynamic nature of these parameters under evolving future development strategies. To address this issue, a sensitivity analysis of the key parameters was conducted, in which the relative rate of change in the model outputs of ESs was calculated, and the sensitivity index was derived accordingly. The sensitivity index for the simulated ESs outcomes under different scenarios from 2030 to 2050 was all less than 1 (Table S11), indicating that the model outputs are not extremely sensitive to parameter perturbations. Therefore, the results of this study are robust. Future research could adjust model input parameters according to projected regional development trends, mainly by integrating long-term ground monitoring, remote sensing inversion, and process models to develop a dynamic parameter library suitable for regional future scenarios. This would better align ES simulations with scenario assumptions. Additionally, scenario setting has certain limitations. Scenario simulations were developed based on references from existing literature and were representative, but the assumptions underlying scenario simulations inevitably introduce uncertainties, which constitute key issues to be addressed in future research. In subsequent studies, we plan to further refine scenario simulations. First, more complex environmental drivers will be incorporated. Ecosystems in plateau mountain cities are vulnerable to complex environmental conditions, with climate change representing a critical environmental risk factor. Future scenario designs could incorporate Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6) climate scenario datasets, such as temperature, precipitation, and evapotranspiration. Second, socioeconomic factors will be included. Socioeconomic conditions exert a strong influence on urban development, and future studies could integrate data on population, economic development, and urbanization rates under different Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs). By constructing a composite scenario framework that integrates climatic and socioeconomic factors, it will be possible to quantify the impacts of climate change and human activities on land use and ESs, thereby enabling a more comprehensive and integrated analysis of future land use patterns and ESs dynamics.

Conclusion

This study employed an integrated model (Markov-FLUS and InVEST) to explore ESs under multiple land use scenarios from 2030 to 2050 in Kunming, a representative plateau mountain city, with Spearman correlation further analyzing ESs’ trade-offs/synergies.

Key conclusions are drawn: (1) Land use changes under three scenarios exhibit significant differences during 2030–2050 in Kunming. Compared to 2020, the NDS shows continuous expansion of construction land (will increase by 1205.75 km2), while the CPS can effectively restrict urban sprawl and preserve cropland (will increase by 236.05 km2). Notably, the EPS maintains relatively stable construction land while significantly increasing forest land and grassland (by 142.08 km2 and 73.36 km2, respectively), demonstrating its effectiveness in controlling urban expansion and conserving ecological lands. (2) ESs in Kunming exhibit different changes under three scenarios during 2030–2050. Compared with 2020, water yield will increase under both NDS and CPS, mainly in Kunming’s main urban area and surrounding regions, while soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality will decrease significantly in these areas. Under EPS, water yield will show a decreasing trend, while soil conservation, carbon storage, and habitat quality will increase. (3) From 2030 to 2050, water yield will continue to show a weak trade-off with soil conservation in Kunming, but stronger trade-offs with both carbon storage and habitat quality. Soil conservation will continue to show synergies with both carbon storage and habitat quality. Carbon storage and habitat quality exhibit a strong synergy. Under NDS, ESs in Kunming generally show a trend of strengthened trade-offs, whereas under CPS and EPS, synergies will be enhanced.

This study shows the response of four key ES and their trade-offs/synergies to land use change under different scenarios (NDS, CPS, and EPS) in the plateau mountain city of Kunming. The findings provide management implications for plateau mountain cities facing similar development challenges and offer a basis for decisions related to the balance between development and conservation. Nevertheless, our research is required to deepen the analysis by coupling future regional climate change with socioeconomic factors so that scenario simulations more closely reflect the actual situation. To address the uncertainties associated with current scenario settings and provide a theoretical foundation and technical support for the future sustainable development of plateau mountain cities.

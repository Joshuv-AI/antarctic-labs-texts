relOBI: A Reliable Low-latency Interconnect for Tightly-Coupled On-chip Communication
Michael Rogenmoser, Angelo Garofalo, Luca Benini

Abstract
On-chip communication is a critical element of modern systems-on-chip (SoCs) , allowing processor cores to interact with memory and peripherals. Interconnects require special care in radiation-heavy environments, as any soft error within the SoC interconnect is likely to cause a functional failure of the whole SoC . This work proposes relOBI , an extension to Open Bus Interface (OBI) combining triple modular redundancy (TMR) for critical handshake signals with error correction codes (ECC) protection on other signals for complete reliability. Implementing and testing a fully reliable crossbar shows improved reliability to injected faults from a vulnerability of 34.85 % 34.85\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} to 0 % 0\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} compared to a reference design, with an area increase of 2.6 × 2.6\text{\,}\times and 1.4 × 1.4\text{\,}\times timing impact. The area overhead is 1.8 × 1.8\text{\,}\times lower than that reported in the literature for fine-grained triplication and voting.



I
Introduction

As more satellites are deployed, especially in constellations for earth observation or communication
[
1
]
, many more advanced
systems-on-chip (SoCs)
are being used in the hostile space environment. Radiation-induced
single-event upsets (SEUs)
and
single-event transients (SETs)
pose significant threats to system reliability, particularly in crucial devices, components, and applications where high availability and fault tolerance are paramount. Smaller, more modern technology nodes are more likely to be affected by
SETs
, as increasing clock frequency and shrinking transistors make it more likely that transient faults are latched by sequential elements instead of being masked thanks to temporal masking
[
2
]
.

Recent testing of a partially protected microcontroller design under radiation and in simulation revealed that faults in high activity areas, such as the system interconnect, severely impact overall device reliability
[
3
]
. While processor cores and memory banks are usually protected
[
3
]
, protecting the interconnect is also critical, but interconnect protection approaches are less widely explored and understood.

Previous research has explored methods for protecting large
networks-on-chip (NoCs)

[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, where the main options proposed are error correction encoding, error checking with retransmission, and ensuring reliability at the transistor level.
However, these methods and their implementation mostly rely on offline test modes, which disrupt normal operation. Furthermore, these
NoC
-centric approaches have not been adapted to smaller, tightly coupled low-latency interconnects.
Focusing on low-latency interconnects for microcontrollers,
[
7
]
enhances reliability with
fine-grained triplication and voting for a
4.86.9\text{\,}\times
area increase and
1.4\text{\,}\times
critical path timing increase. A lighter protection is applied to the
Advanced Peripheral Bus 5 (APB5)
peripheral bus by triplicating only the control and protecting data and address with
error correction codes (ECC)
, reducing the number of signals by
42\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
w.r.t. full triplication.
The
Open Bus Interface (OBI)

[
8
]
and
Advanced eXtensible Interface 5 (AXI5)

[
9
]
standards define integrity signalling as an optional extension, which adds parity to various signals for error detection, but the specifications do not specify a correction or recovery mechanism. Retransmission may correct some error types, but handshake errors are not recoverable, meaning a system reset would be required. Relying on the underlying transistors and cells to be tolerant can address this issue, but is limited to technologies where cell implementation has been radiation hardened.

To address these limitations, we present
relOBI
, a novel augmentation of the on-chip interface
OBI

[
8
]
that provides complete soft error tolerance for combinatorial and sequential elements within system interconnects and allows for continuous operation at a much lower cost than fine-grained triplication. Specifically, this work includes the following contributions:

•

Definition of an extension to the interface allowing for rapid encoding and decoding to handle any single error on the interface wires while maintaining flexibility.

•

Design and implementation of open-source interconnect IP blocks, including a crossbar architecture, that ensure complete system reliability and correct routing.

•

Evaluation of the performance and area implications of the proposed interconnects, complemented by soft error tolerance analysis and assessment of the proposed interface extension and interconnect IP blocks through fault simulation, demonstrating the approach’s effectiveness.

To ensure correct handling of
OBI

transactions, all handshaking signals are triplicated and voted, as shown in
Figure

1
.
This ensures that both the manager and subordinate keep a consistent view of the interconnect to avoid the creation or deletion of a transaction due to a soft error.
Furthermore, all other signals, such as address and data, are protected with
ECC
, grouped as shown in
Figure

1
to balance overheads in signalling and en-/decoding. This protection scheme ensures continuous operation at no additional latency and enables in-flight correction and atomic transactions, unlike other schemes such as parity and retransmission. The encoding and decoding blocks enable compatibility with existing
OBI

interfaces.

Internal interconnect blocks are hardened by ensuring that all logic-modifying signals are triplicated, as shown for the demultiplexer in
Figure

2
. Each of the three handshakes is controlled individually, where triplicated voters are inserted internally to align states between the redundant handshake signals to avoid error accumulation. Where applicable, the
ECC
-protected packets are passed through to avoid triplication overhead. However, all decoding, modification, and selection of these signals is triplicated and voted to ensure correctness.

III
Results

A
6\times 8
crossbar is used for testing, a module where 6
OBI

or
relOBI
managers individually connect to demultiplexers, which are fully connected to 8
OBI

or
relOBI
multiplexers for 8 subordinates. To ensure the tested crossbars are free from interface timing effects, each interface is pipelined inside the tested module before the multiplexer/demultiplexer connection. The
relOBI
crossbar does not include the encoders and decoders within the tested module, as these components are assumed to be separately protected by extending and reusing protection methods applied to the manager and subordinate components, e.g., by triplicating and voting the encoding.

Each bus is configured with 32-bit address and data widths and optional features (e.g., atomic support), resulting in a total bus width of 137 bits. Converting to
relOBI
results in a 177-bit bus width, an increase of
29\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
in the number of signals.

Synopsys VC_Z01X 2025.06
was used to evaluate the design’s fault tolerance characteristics. A randomized testbench simulated 1000 transactions from each manager, with the subordinates simply responding. Transient faults were injected into the crossbar internals, injecting
FLOP
(sequential blocks) and
PORT
(module ports) faults in RTL, as well as
PRIM
(Verilog primitives) faults in the synthesized netlist. The results in
Table

I
highlight whether the base
OBI

interface behaved correctly, with the
relOBI
design indicating any detected corrections or uncorrectable internal faults. While the baseline
OBI

shows
11.5234.85\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of injected faults cause errors, the
relOBI
design corrects all injected faults, with
21.9238.53\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
indicating an internal error was corrected.

To evaluate the physical characteristics, the design was synthesized in a commercial
7\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
node in typical conditions with
Synopsys Design Compiler NXT 2024.09
in topographical mode, ensuring triplicated partitions are kept and their boundary is not optimized to avoid removing reliability structures.
Implemented at
500\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
, the
relOBI
crossbar requires
123\text{\,}\mathrm{k}
compared to the
47\text{\,}\mathrm{k}
required for the
OBI

crossbar, an increase of
2.6\text{\,}\times
, which is
1.82.6\text{\,}\times
smaller than fine-grained triplication
[
7
]
. The interconnect area in a system utilizing an
OBI
crossbar occupies less than
2\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the
SoC
area
[
3
]
, showing that the area overhead is quite small at the system level. With the internal reliability, the critical timing through the interconnect increases from
0.27\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}
to
0.46\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}
, due to added voters and
ECC
decoding to ensure correct routing. Modifying the design to abort transactions on an
ECC
error and recovering in the next cycle reduces the achievable clock period to
0.38\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}
, similar to a fine-grained triplication
[
7
]
.

While timing overhead is not negligible, we want to point out that the critical path is short in the absolute sense, allowing a frequency above
2\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
. Hence, when integrated into a system, the critical path will likely be dominated by the manager and subordinate components, such as processor cores and memory banks connected through the crossbar.

IV
Conclusion

The proposed
relOBI
interface and protected interconnection blocks are shown to be fully
SEU
-tolerant, reducing the vulnerability from up to
34.85\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
to zero at a much lower area impact and similar timing overhead compared to fine-grained triplication.
In the context of a reliable microcontroller design
[
3
]
, where cores and memories are protected,
relOBI
would significantly increase system-level
SEU
coverage, at a negligible area and timing overhead.

Venous thromboembolism and ovarian cancer risk: a Mendelian randomized study
Authors: Xiaolin Liu, Shan Wang, Hongwei Lv, Enli Chen, Jing Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionA potential link between venous thromboembolism and the risk of ovarian cancer has been identified in clinical practice. However, it is unclear whether there is a causal relationship between the two. In this study, we applied a univariate two-sample Mendelian randomization method to explain the possible link between venous thromboembolism and ovarian cancer pathogenesis at the genetic level, and pointed out that lipid metabolism and ovarian cancer pathogenesis have innovative basic experimental directions.ObjectiveThis study explored the causal effect between a history of venous thromboembolism and the risk of ovarian cancer.MethodsGenome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) data of venous thromboembolism patients (n = 9176) of the same ethnicity were selected as study exposures, and GWAS data of ovarian cancer patients (n = 1218) of the same ethnicity were selected as study exposures. In this study, univariate two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis (UVMR) was performed separately using inverse variance weighted (IVW), MR-Egger regression, and weighted median (WM) to assess causal effects. In this study, Cochran’s Q test, MR-Egger regression intercept term, MR-PRESSO, and leave-one-out method were used for sensitivity analysis to assess the stability and reliability of the results.ResultsThe GWAS data screened in this study were all European ethnicity data. In this study, we found that genetically predicted history of venous thromboembolism was associated with an upward trend in ovarian cancer incidence, and the results of Weighted median, Simple mode, Weighted mode, and MR Egger showed a similar trend (OR = 1.0006, 95% CI: 1.00007–1.0013, p < 0.05). There was no heterogeneity of results (p = 0.18) and no horizontal pleiotropy (p = 0.77). The instrumental variables selected for venous thromboembolism in this study were all strong instrumental variables (F = 669.7). The results of the sensitivity analysis remained consistent.ConclusionThe results of this study indicate that patients with a history of venous thromboembolism are at increased risk of developing ovarian cancer and point to possible associations between lipid metabolism genes, such as CYP4V2, and the development of ovarian cancer, which provide interesting directions for further basic research.Keywords: Venous thromboembolism, Ovarian cancer, Mendelian randomization

Introduction

Ovarian Cancer (OC) is the 8th Most Common and 5th Deadliest Cancer in Women [1]. More than 300,000 women suffer from OC each year, and about 152,000 women die from the disease [2]. The prognostic implications of the high recurrence rate of OC in the clinical setting remain grim [3, 4]. The prognostic implications of the high recurrence rate of OC remain severe. Due to the lack of typical signs and symptoms in the early stages of OC and the aggressive tendency to progress rapidly, more than 70% of patients are diagnosed with advanced OC [5]. Therefore, it is of particular importance to accurately stratify and screen patients at high risk for OC [6]. The Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a common vascular disease and the third most common cardiovascular disease in Western countries [6] VTE results from a state of hypercoagulability of the blood due to an imbalance between coagulation and anticoagulation mechanisms and is caused by a complex interaction between acquired and genetic factors [7–9]. VTE is caused by a complex interaction between acquired and genetic factors. Studies have shown that hemostatic components and tumor biology are intricately linked, and that cancer and VTE share biological processes that promote interaction [10–13]. In addition, up to 15% of patients with cancer also have symptomatic VTE, and up to 20% of patients with VTE have active cancer [14], > 10% of patients with VTE are diagnosed with an occult malignancy within one year [15].

OC is the most common solid tumors associated with VTE, and VTE has adverse effects on patient prognosis [16]. Specifically, thromboembolic events may reflect the aggressiveness of the tumor, so VTE can be used as a predictive model parameter for clinical outcomes in patients with OC [17–19]. Considering this relationship between unprovoked VTE and OC, some clinicians have noted that screening patients with a first acute unprovoked VTE episode for potentially occult malignancies can reduce the public health burden on society [20–22].

In this paper, we used UVMR to explore the association between VTE history and the risk of OC. Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis is a new method for exploring causal relationships between diseases that relies on Mendelian laws of inheritance. Parental alleles are randomly assigned to offspring during inheritance, and different genotypes of offspring determine different intermediate phenotypes [23]. The relationship between gene exposure and disease outcome is not confounded by confounding factors such as postnatal environment and is not plagued by reverse causation, thus genes can serve as effective IVs. The current two-sample MR methodology benefits from the large number of high-quality studies carried out by GWAS [24]. They have been widely used to analyze associations between diseases [25–27]. The two-sample MR method has been widely used to analyze the association between disease, providing a powerful tool to explore the causality of diseases. With this approach, researchers can gain a more comprehensive understanding of the potential associations between VTE history and OC, thus providing more information and guidance for future clinical practice and treatment.

In recent years, GWAS studies on VTE have gradually revealed the predictive value of VTE-associated SNPs for tumorigenesis and development. To better help identify people at high risk of OC, this paper will investigate the association between VTE history and the risk of OC development by a two-sample Mendelian randomization method.

Materials and methods

Source and study design

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) were searched to identify the exposure variable as VTE and the outcome variable as OC. Pooled genome-wide association study (GWAS) data were obtained for the exposure variable and the outcome variable, respectively, where GWAS data for VTE were obtained from the finn database, which enrolled 9176 VTE cases and 209,616 controls, respectively, and GWAS data for OC were obtained from the UK Biobank database, which included 1218 OC cases and 198,523 controls, respectively, as shown in Table 1. The causal effect of VTE history on the risk of OC was assessed using the designed TSMR model, as shown in Fig. 1.

Selection of instrumental variables

The selection of instrumental variables must satisfy three main assumptions: (1) the selected SNPs have a certain strength of association with exposure; (2) the SNPs are not associated with any confounders involved in the exposure-outcome pathway; and (3) the SNPs affect the study outcome only through their effect on exposure, i.e., the SNPs do not have a significant level of pleiotropy [28]. We used a two-sample MR analysis framework in which GWAS summary statistics from different exposure and outcome studies were used to estimate causal effects and to increase the statistical efficacy and precision of MR analyses.

Genetically variable SNP loci with genome-wide significance (p < 5 × 10–8) for venous thromboembolism were selected for pooling in this study. A threshold of 0.1 for the linkage disequilibrium parameter (r2) and a genetic distance of 1000 kb were set to select SNPs to ensure their independence and to exclude the influence of linkage disequilibrium (LD) on the results [29–32]. The remaining SNPs related to phenotypes were retrieved through the human genotype–phenotype association database, along with SNPs satisfying hypotheses (1), (2), and (3).Information on the outcome CRC was extracted through GWAS, and SNPs satisfying hypotheses (1), (2), and (3) were obtained from the outcome in relation to the outcome. Merge the exposure and outcome dataset, this merged dataset contains the above instrumental variables simultaneously with the relationship of the outcome and exposure, and remove the palindromic SNPs. the last remaining SNP is the final instrumental variable referring to the exposure.

TSMR programme

In this study, five methods, including Mendelian randomized Egger regression, median weighted method, IVW random effects model, IVW radial method, and IVW fixed effects model were used to carry out the TSMR, and the ORs and 95% CIs of the above five methods were visualized.

Sensitivity analyses

Cochran’s Q test for SNPs matching the 3 hypotheses using the Racial Mendelian randomization R package was used to assess heterogeneity among individual genetic variants. If the p < 0.05 of Cochran’s Q test, the results were heterogeneous, representing that the relationship between exposure and outcome was affected by different age, different sex, etc., then the final results of Mendelian randomization referred to the IVW random-effects model as the gold standard; otherwise, the IVW fixed-effects model was used as the gold standard, and the heterogeneity was visualized by a forest plot. Results of the test. The Mendelian randomization polytropy test Egger-intercept method and MR-PRESSO test were also used to check whether there was a violation of the Mendelian randomization assumptions due to horizontal polytropy. For the Egger-intercept method of horizontal pleiotropy, in which the cutoff value estimates whether the genetic variance significantly affects the outcome through pathways other than exposure, p < 0.05 means that there is horizontal pleiotropy, i.e., the selected instrumental variable significantly affects the outcome through pathways other than exposure, and then Hypotheses 2 and 3 are violated. p > 0.05 means that exposure does not significantly affect the outcome variables through pathways other than exposure. The leave-one-out test was used as a sensitivity analysis to infer whether any of the final SNPs were outliers. The stability of the results was also checked by looking for asymmetry in the funnel plot. The MR-PRESSO method was then used to identify outliers and assess the impact of outliers on the results.

Evaluation of instrumental variables

Using the formula: R2 = 2 × (1-MAF)(MAF) × (β/SD)2. where MAF is the minor allele frequency of exposure, β is the allele effect value of exposure, and SD is the standard deviation. The R2 involved in the calculation of the F value is the sum of the R2s of all SNPs, indicating that the final instrumental variable refers to the percent of exposure. Using the formula: F = R2 (N-K-1)/(1-R2)K, where N is the total number of samples exposed, K is the number of SNPs, and R2 is the same as above, F > 10 is a strong instrumental variable and F < 10 is a weak instrumental variable. All the above methodology and visualization graphs were obtained in part using R version 4.3.1.

Discussion

Based on publicly available large-sample GWAS data, this study was the first to explore the causal association between VTE and OC by using Mendelian randomization analysis. Instrumental variables satisfying the three hypotheses of MR were screened with stringent criteria, and multiple sensitivity tests demonstrated that the analysis results were reliable. With stable results (OR = 1.0006, 95% CI: 1.00007–1.0013, p < 0.05), this study demonstrated a positive causal association of VTE history for the risk of OC development. This is important for the early and precise stratification of high-risk groups of OC patients.

In terms of pathogenesis, thrombin and tissue factor and their promoters play an important role in the development of malignant tumors. Previous basic studies have shown that coagulation proteins are involved in tumor growth, neoangiogenesis, immune evasion, invasion, and metastatic dissemination, which contribute to tumor development [17, 33]. Tissue factors constitutively expressed in OC cells act as initiators of thrombin formation [34–37] which can influence tumor development through signaling or thrombin generation [11, 38]. Newcomer et al., have found that tissue factors serve as a diagnostic key in the early detection of ultrasound microvesicles in ovarian cancer [39]. Unlu and Belting et al. proved that the expression of tissue factor promoting factor was related to tumor neovascularization [40, 41].

In addition, the SNPs screened in this study were associated with CYP4V2, a gene related to retinal lipid metabolism, and PROCR, a gene encoding the protein C receptor, respectively. Ducros et al. investigated the role of the PROCR-encoded protein that mediates the process of anticoagulation in OC cells [42], observed that increased levels of protein C receptor in human soluble vascular endothelial cells were associated with increased levels of the OC-specific tumor marker CA-125. Koizume et al. study further revealed that ovarian clear cell carcinoma cells respond to lipid metabolism and in turn develop through fibronectin junctions [43]. Based on this, we are able to further understand the relationship between a history of VTE and the risk of OC development in terms of the molecular mechanisms of the coagulation system and malignancy, as well as from a genetic perspective.

Limitations section

There are several limitations to this study. First, all analyses were performed using only European subjects, making it difficult to assess whether these results can be extrapolated to other populations, and more studies are needed in the future to validate the applicability of these results to other populations and other ethnicities. Second, the statistical power to detect the degree of association of exposure factors was limited due to database selection, and the above analyses should be repeated when larger GWAS databases become available. Third, previous clinical studies have shown that a history of VTE adversely affects the development of OC in patients with OC, but the predictive effect of different OC staging on clinical outcomes remains controversial [17, 44, 45]. The present study did not investigate the impact of VTE history on the development of OC. The present study did not investigate the relationship between history of VTE and the risk of developing ovarian malignancies of different subtypes and stages, and subsequent refinement of the Meta-analysis of Mendelian randomization and subgrouping by region and different subtypes and stages of ovarian malignancies could address this limitation. Fourth, a systematic review by Carrier, et al. noted a six- to sevenfold increase in the reported prevalence of new cancers over a 12-month period in patients with a history of unprovoked VTE compared with the general population [46]. This suggests that a history of VTE is likely to be a risk factor for the development of certain types of malignancies. Therefore, studies on the association between a history of VTE and the risk of developing genitourinary tumors could be conducted. Fifth, as the study method was a two-sample Mendelian randomization approach, the enumerated genes could not be exhaustive, and it has been reported that studies such as ZFPM2 and CNTN6, which could play a key role in the association between VTE and tumorigenesis through the structure of zinc finger proteins encoding negative regulators of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-protein kinase B (PI3K-AkT) pathway, or possible triggering of neuroadhesion molecules in the NOTCH pathway development, play a key role in the relationship between VTE and tumor development [47–51]. Therefore, two-sample Mendelian randomization derived from multiple exposure-endpoints with Meta-analysis based on SNP Mendelian randomization is necessary.

In conclusion

This investigation substantiates, to a certain degree, the association between a history of VTE and the incidence of OC, thus offering a foundational premise for the restructuring of coagulation surveillance protocols among VTE patients and precise screening procedures for ovarian malignancies. Through a two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis, this study revealed a positive causal link between VTE and OC. The scrutiny of genes conducted in this research and the identification of their mutation status hold significance for the precise categorization of individuals at elevated risk of OC. Moreover, from a fundamental research perspective, this inquiry unveils a distinct correlation between VTE and OC pathogenesis. It underscores the potential association between lipid metabolism genes, exemplified by CYP4V2, and OC development, thereby indicating an intriguing avenue for future basic research endeavors.

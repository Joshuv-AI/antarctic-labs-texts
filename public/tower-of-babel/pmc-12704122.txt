Efficacy, safety, and patient-centered outcomes of needle-free injectors versus insulin pens in diabetes management: a comprehensive meta-analysis
Authors: Jing Wang, Xinhao Chen, Zhili Ge, Yingran Xia, Zixiang Tang, Zehao Sun, Lijing Jiao, Jing Zhou, Lihui Zhang, Zhansheng Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo evaluate the comparative effectiveness, safety, and patient acceptance of needle-free injectors and insulin pens through a meta-analysis. A thorough literature search was performed across multiple databases, including CNKI, the Weipu Database, the Wanfang Database, PubMed, Embase, and The Cochrane Library for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on the use of needle-free injectors and insulin pens in diabetes treatment, with a time frame from database inception until June 2024. Five researchers carefully screened the literature in accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria, assessed study quality with validated tools and conducted the meta-analysis using RevMan 5.4. This study included 22 publications encompassing 22 studies, involving a total of 2,580 patients, with 1,290 in the needle-free group and 1,290 in the insulin pen group. The findings suggest that needle-free injectors were superior to insulin pens in improving treatment efficacy, local adverse reactions, and increasing patient acceptability, with statistically significant differences. However, no significant variations were observed in continuous glucose monitoring. Needle-free injectors can improve certain aspects of glycemic control, such as reducing HbA1c, FPG, and 2hPPG; improve local adverse reactions and increase patient acceptability. However, improvements in glycemic variability metrics were not statistically significant, indicating that further research is needed to fully assess their long-term safety and therapeutic effectiveness. In terms of systemic safety, the risk of hypoglycemia in the needle-free group was not significantly different from that in the needle group (P = 0.14). Additionally, our publication bias analysis revealed potential biases in the outcomes related to hypoglycemia and insulin dosage.KEYWORDS: Needle-free injection, diabetes, insulin pen, continuous glucose monitoring, meta-analysis

Introduction

Globally, the number of people with diabetes continues to rise, making effective blood sugar control and improving treatment adherence urgent challenges. According to the International Diabetes Federation (IDF), in 2023, there were 537 million people with diabetes worldwide (Kumar et al. 2024), with a prevalence rate of 6.1%. China accounted for one-quarter of the global total, with approximately 141 million people living with diabetes. By 2050, more than 1.31 billion people worldwide are projected to have diabetes (GBD 2021 Diabetes Collaborators 2023).

Diabetes is a chronic disease characterized by high blood sugar levels caused by insufficient insulin production or impaired insulin utilization (Harreiter and Roden 2019). This leads to ineffective glucose use and storage in the body.

Insulin therapy is crucial for diabetes management, especially for patients with long-term diabetes. Conventional insulin is injected into subcutaneous tissue using needles and is the most common method. However, traditional syringes can cause pain and discomfort, reducing treatment adherence. Frequent insulin users also face infection risks. Moreover, there is uncertainty in insulin delivery speed and absorption uniformity.

Given the drawbacks of traditional insulin injection, the medical community urgently needs a more advanced and effective method. Needle-free injectors, using high-pressure jet technology to deliver medication into subcutaneous tissue or muscle without a needle, offer a promising solution (Rho et al. 2024). Modern needle-free injectors are evolving to ensure precise drug injection, efficient absorption, and minimal discomfort. They have broad applications in healthcare and drug delivery, including diabetes treatment, vaccination (Sonoda 2023), dermatological therapy (Bekkers et al. 2023), and cosmetic injections.

Compared to traditional needle-based injectors, numerous clinical trials have confirmed that needle-free injectors offer advantages such as lower infection risk, fewer injection site restrictions, and higher treatment adherence. This study further analyzes and summarizes these advantages. However, needle-free injectors still face technical challenges, such as precise drug control, and the control of drug absorption efficiency, injection speed, depth, and stability (Schoppink and Fernandez Rivas 2022). This requires ongoing technical improvement and development, for which this study collects and analyzes data while comparing it with traditional injectors. Additionally, there is controversy surrounding needle-free injectors regarding their applicable population, cost, immunogenicity, and association with complications, with limited research in these areas. Therefore, this study systematically retrieves and analyzes relevant randomized controlled trials to comprehensively and objectively evaluate the differences in efficacy, safety, and patient acceptance between needle-free injectors and insulin pens in diabetes management, providing a scientific basis for clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Literature search strategy

We searched Chinese databases, including the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data, VIP Chinese Science and Technology Journal Database, and China Biology Medicine (CBM), as well as English databases such as PubMed, Embase, and The Cochrane Library. The search covered Chinese and English literature from their inception to June 2024. The Chinese search terms were ‘insulin,’ ‘diabetes,’ ‘needle-free injection technology,’ and ‘needle-free injector,’ while the English terms included ‘RCTs,’ ‘diabetes,’ ‘insulin,’ ‘needle-free injection,’ and ‘needle-free injector.’ The search strategy for the Chinese databases was ‘(diabetes) AND (needle-free injector) OR (diabetes) AND (needle-free injection technology).’ The aim was to identify recent RCTs published between June 2016 and June 2024 that met the inclusion criteria.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for studies: (1) Study type: randomized controlled trials (RCTs). (2) Intervention: insulin injection (any type), in which needle-free injectors are compared with insulin pens. (3) Participants: patients who were diagnosed with diabetes (any type). (4) Baseline comparability: no statistically significant differences between groups in patient characteristics before intervention. (5) Outcome measures: Studies must report at least one outcome or efficacy indicator relevant for data extraction.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria for studies: (1) Duplicated publications. (2) Case reports, reviews, or commentaries. (3) Non-RCTs. (4) Studies with confounding factors unrelated to the trial, preventing valid comparisons. (5) Studies with invalid outcome measures. (6) Studies with incomplete or unavailable data.

Data extraction

Two researchers independently screened and extracted data from eligible studies, cross-verifying the results. Disagreements were resolved with a third-party adjudicator. A data extraction form was designed based on the protocol to collect study information, including basic parameters, methodological quality, and outcome data (e.g. first author, publication date, sample size, age, insulin type, and intervention details).

Efficacy outcomes included glycated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)‌, fasting blood glucose (FBG), two-hour postprandial glucose (2 h PPG), postbreakfast 2 h BG, and continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) metrics: absolute means of daily differences (MODD), standard deviation blood glucose (SDBG), mean amplitude of glycemic excursions (MAGE), and time in range (TIR). Safety outcomes included pain, erythema, bleeding, and drug residue. Acceptability outcomes focused on patient satisfaction (number and scores). For studies published in multiple phases, the final assessment data were used as outcome indicators.

Quality assessment methods

Quality assessment was conducted using the Cochrane Handbook. Studies were evaluated for risk of bias in random sequence generation, allocation concealment, performance bias, detection bias, attrition bias, reporting bias, and other biases. Study quality was graded as follows: Grade A (low risk of bias), Grade B (moderate risk of bias), or Grade C (high risk of bias), based on the extent to which the studies met the assessment criteria.

Statistical methods

The data were analyzed using Rev Man 5.4 software. Heterogeneity was assessed using I². If heterogeneity was not significant (P > 0.05 and I² < 50%), a fixed-effects model was applied; if heterogeneity was significant (P < 0.05 and I² ≥ 50%), a random-effects model was used. Continuous data are expressed as the mean ± SD and were analyzed using t-tests. Categorical data were described using risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Publication bias and sensitivity analyses for outcome measures were conducted using Stata 11 software.

All the experiments were conducted in mainland China. Figure 1 and Table 1 show the study selection flowchart and basic information of the included studies. Meta-analysis results

Treatment effect indicators

HbA1c

A total of 8 studies reported comparisons of HbA1c levels between the needle-free group and the needle group. Subgroup analysis was carried out according to different types of diabetes. There was considerable statistical heterogeneity among the results (I² = 87%), and a random effects model (REM) was utilized for the statistical analysis. The findings of this study indicate that the needle-free group demonstrated a superior effect in improving HbA1c levels in diabetic patients compared to the needle group, with significant differences observed between the two groups [mean difference (MD) = −0.80, 95% confidence interval (CI) (−1.18, −0.42), P < 0.01]. Subgroup analysis showed that in patients with type 1 diabetes (T1DM), the effect of reducing the FPG level in the needle-free group was greater than that in the needle-free group, and the difference was not statistically significant [MD = −0.53, 95% CI (−1.06, 0.00), P = 0.05]. In patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), the effect of reducing the FPG level in the needle-free group was greater than that in the needle-only group, and the difference was statistically significant [MD = −0.87, 95% CI (−1.31, −0.42), P < 0.01]. There was no significant heterogeneity among the subgroups (I2 = 0%), as illustrated in Figure 4.

Publish bias analysis

Publication bias analysis was conducted for the outcome measures. The funnel plots are shown in Figures 23 and 24. The results showed that the Egger's test for FPG, 2hPPG, HbA1c, MOOD, SDBG, MAGE, TIR, pain score, pain, induration, drug residue, redness, and incidence of hemorrhagic ecchymosis yielded p-values greater than 0.05, indicating no publication bias for these measures. The Egger's test for insulin dosage yielded a p-value of 0.005, indicating bias. After excluding Linong Ji 2020, the Egger's test yielded a p-value of 0.6, indicating no bias, which may be due to heterogeneity bias caused by this literature. The Egger's test for the incidence of hypoglycemia yielded a p-value of 0.002, indicating bias. After trimming and filling, no bias was detected. After excluding Linong Ji 2020, the Egger's test yielded a p-value of 0.242, indicating no bias, which may be due to heterogeneity bias caused by this literature. After the exclusion of the article, the conclusions of the studies that included the main indicators of the article, such as HbA1c, FPG, and insulin dose, etc., did not change significantly, and we still obtained consistent results, which showed that the bias of the study had a relatively limited impact on the overall results. Therefore, despite this bias, we decided to retain it in the analysis after comprehensive consideration. The research results after excluding these studies can be viewed in the Appendix.

Discussion

This study reviewed the relevant domestic and international literature to analyze the therapeutic effects, safety, and acceptability of needle-free injectors compared to insulin pens. The results revealed that needle-free injectors were significantly more effective than insulin pens in reducing HbA1c, FPG, 2hPPG, and insulin dosage (P < 0.01). This superiority may be attributed to several factors. First, needle-free injectors have shown superiority in terms of pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics. The needle free injectors have faster absorption, higher early insulin exposure, better early hypoglycemic effect, and similar overall efficacy compared with the traditional insulin pen. The insulin absorption mode of needle-free injection syringe administration is closer to the endogenous early insulin secretion mode, which may better simulate the postprandial physiological insulin response (Hu 2016). The second factor is drug distribution and absorption. Needle-free injectors use high-pressure jet technology to deliver insulin more widely in subcutaneous tissue, resulting in faster and more uniform absorption. In contrast, insulin pens create local high-concentration areas at the injection site, which may lead to uneven absorption and thus affect glycemic control. The third factor is patient compliance. Needle-free injectors reduce injection pain and anxiety, leading to better treatment adherence. Good compliance ensures that patients administer insulin regularly and at the correct doses, which is crucial for long-term stable glycemic control. In contrast, the pain and discomfort associated with insulin pens may cause some patients to skip or delay injections, thereby affecting glycemic control.

In the subgroup analyses stratified by insulin type, insulin aspart failed to achieve statistically significant reductions in fasting plasma glucose (FPG) or 2-h post-prandial glucose (2h-PG), whereas insulin aspart 30 and insulin glargine demonstrated significant advantages. Three reasons may account for this heterogeneity. First, aspart is a rapid-acting analogue whose primary pharmacological target is post-prandial glucose excursions, leaving limited room for improvement in fasting glycaemia. Second, the aspart subgroup comprised only three studies with a total of 118 participants, yielding low statistical power and a high risk of false-negative findings. Third, different insulins exhibit inherent variations in onset, peak concentration and duration of action; the altered dispersion-absorption kinetics produced by high-pressure jet injection may exert disproportionate effects across formulations, amplifying or attenuating the glucose-lowering benefit of the needle-free device. Despite these subgroup discrepancies, the overall pooled estimate still favoured needle-free injectors over conventional insulin pens (P < 0.01), indicating a consistent directional benefit. Large-scale, head-to-head randomized controlled trials dedicated to individual insulin categories are warranted to confirm the preliminary signals observed in this meta-analysis.

While our meta-analysis demonstrated statistically significant reductions in HbA1c (−0.80%) and FPG (−0.77 mmol/L) with needle-free injectors compared to insulin pens, it is essential to consider the clinical significance of these findings. A reduction of 0.80% in HbA1c is clinically meaningful, as it aligns with the general guideline that a decrease of 0.5% or more in HbA1c is considered clinically significant for diabetes management (Inzucchi 2015). This level of reduction can translate to a substantial decrease in the risk of long-term complications, such as a 35% reduction in the risk of microvascular complications for each 1% decrease in HbA1c (UK Prospective Diabetes Study (UKPDS) 1998). However, we acknowledge that for some patients, especially those with already well-controlled diabetes, such improvements may not significantly alter their long-term complication risks. Similarly, the reduction in FPG (−0.77 mmol/L) is statistically significant, but its clinical impact may vary depending on the baseline FPG levels of patients. For patients with high baseline FPG levels, this reduction could be clinically meaningful, contributing to better overall glycemic control. However, for patients with lower baseline FPG levels, the clinical benefit may be less pronounced.

It is also important to consider the cost and availability of needle-free injectors. While their higher cost and limited availability in some regions may limit their widespread adoption, needle-free injectors offer several advantages, including reduced pain, fewer local adverse reactions, and potentially better patient acceptance. Studies also suggest that needle-free injectors correct significant hyperglycemia more rapidly in overweight or obese patients with insulin-requiring diabetes (de Wit et al. 2015). In addition, needle-free injectors can reduce the risk of complications in some patients, thereby reducing their long-term costs. Healthcare providers and patients should weigh these factors carefully when deciding on the most appropriate treatment modality.

In terms of safety, the needle-free group had significantly lower risks of pain, pain scores, drug residue, induration, skin erythema, and ecchymosis than did the needle group (P < 0.01). However, the risk of hypoglycemia in the needle-free group was not significantly different from that in the needle group (P = 0.14). Needle-free injection can reduce local adverse reactions, possibly because of its non-invasive nature, causing less trauma to patients. The effect on systemic safety (e.g. hypoglycemia, long-term metabolic outcomes) needs to be confirmed by further research.

In terms of acceptability, the needle-free group had higher satisfaction, ITSQ scores, and 10-point scale ratings than the needle group, with statistically significant differences (P ≤ 0.05). These results indicate that needle-free injection is more acceptable to patients, possibly because of its lower incidence of adverse reactions, reduction in needle fear and anxiety, and ability to enhance therapeutic effects while reducing the insulin dosage. This highlights the significant role of needle-free injection in improving the quality of life and prognosis of diabetic patients.

In terms of blood glucose fluctuations, the needle-free group had better MODD, SDBG, and MAGE levels than did the needle group. However, improvements in glycemic variability metrics, such as MODD, SDBG, MAGE, and TIR, were not statistically significant. These findings suggest that while needle-free injectors offer notable benefits in terms of specific therapeutic outcomes, their overall impact on glycemic variability remains inconclusive. Further research is needed to fully elucidate the long-term safety and therapeutic effectiveness of needle-free injectors compared to insulin pens. Despite the significant advantages of needle-free injectors in reducing HbA1c and FPG, no statistically significant improvements were observed in glycemic variability metrics (MODD, SDBG, MAGE, TIR). This may be attributed to several factors. First, glycemic variability is influenced by multiple factors, including patients' diet, exercise habits, and medication adherence, which may not have been adequately controlled in our study. Second, the limited availability of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data and the relatively small sample size may have been insufficient to detect significant differences. Additionally, while needle-free injectors offer benefits in terms of drug absorption and local reactions, their impact on glycemic variability may only become apparent with longer-term observation. Some scholars have reported that, compared to traditional insulin pens, needle-free insulin injection can improve fasting glucose variability (FGV) in T2DM patients (Sun 2022). When injecting rapid-acting insulin, needle-free injectors allow faster insulin absorption, resulting in a significant but moderate reduction in postprandial hyperglycemia in type 1 and 2 diabetes patients. This improved early postprandial glucose control may be particularly beneficial for patients struggling to limit postprandial glucose fluctuations (Engwerda et al. 2013). Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) is an important indicator for future monitoring and comparison efforts, and more high-quality randomized controlled trials are needed. The 2024 ADA standards also highlight that CGM helps patients understand their glucose fluctuation patterns and make corresponding treatment adjustments, with its use associated with improved glucose control (American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee 2024).

While our study demonstrated strong statistical significance for pain reduction, drug residue, induration, and local injection site reactions, it is crucial to recognize that these outcomes, although important for patient comfort, are secondary to the primary clinical outcomes of glycemic control and safety. The significant improvements in patient comfort with needle-free injectors should not overshadow the need for robust evidence of their impact on glycemic control and long-term safety. While the reductions in HbA1c and FPG levels observed with needle-free injectors are clinically meaningful, the lack of significant differences in glycemic variability metrics and the inconclusive systemic safety data suggest that further research is essential to fully establish the clinical value of needle-free injectors in diabetes management. Over-emphasizing the improvements in local injection site reactions and patient comfort may lead to an overstatement of their overall clinical value. Therefore, healthcare providers and patients should carefully weigh these factors when considering the adoption of needle-free injectors, ensuring that the primary goals of effective glycemic control and safety are not compromised.

Currently, insulin therapy is one of the most critical treatments for diabetes. For type 1 diabetes, lifelong insulin therapy is essential. In type 2 diabetes, insulin may be required when oral antidiabetic medications fail to adequately control blood glucose (Cloete 2022). Insulin pens are associated with adverse effects such as pain, lipoatrophy, needle phobia, and psychological insulin resistance. Needle-free injection, a newer technology, offers advantages over insulin pens, including broader medication distribution, faster diffusion, rapid and uniform absorption, and elimination of injection-related pain and anxiety (Ravi et al. 2015). These benefits facilitate healthcare efficiency by reducing clinician workload and patient‒clinician conflicts while optimizing resource utilization. Studies indicate that needle-free injectors may be particularly beneficial for individuals with higher body weights, as they mitigate the impact of BMI, waist circumference, and the waist‒to-hip ratio on insulin absorption (de Galan et al. 2013). Guo et al. found that the injection site does not affect insulin absorption via needle-free injectors, allowing diabetic patients to rotate injection sites across different body regions (Pan 2022). Needle-free injectors can release a sufficient fluid volume for blood glucose measurement. Using trough-shaped nozzles or nozzles with larger exit areas enhances fluid release. This technology may enable the integration of blood sampling, glucose measurement, and insulin delivery into a single device (Xu et al. 2023).

Differences between study groups were attributed primarily to variations in administration methods. However, the pharmacological agent itself remains the dominant influencing factor across interventions. In the included studies, drug type and dosing regimen influenced outcomes, potentially explaining some non-significant differences. Additionally, publication bias analysis revealed high heterogeneity in certain studies. We evaluated the quality of these articles. This study is a well-designed and well-executed randomized controlled trial that provides direct comparative evidence of the efficacy and safety of needle-free insulin injection and traditional insulin pen in Chinese patients with type 2 diabetes. The main outcome is objective indicators, with complete data and reliable results, accounting for a certain proportion in our overall study, so it was retained due to its high methodological quality ratings. This study has several limitations. First, all included studies were conducted in mainland China, which may introduce ethnic and publication biases. This may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups and regions. Second, despite the use of a comprehensive search strategy, eligible studies may have been missed. Third, no statistically significant improvements were observed in glycemic variability metrics (MODD, SDBG, MAGE, TIR). This may be due to the limited sample size, short intervention duration, and insufficient CGM data in the included studies. Future research should address these limitations by conducting multicenter, large-sample, long-term randomized controlled trials to assess the impact of needle-free injectors on glycemic variability more comprehensively. We acknowledge that key outcomes – MODD, SDBG, TIR, patient satisfaction (ITSQ/Decile system), and drug residue –were each reported by only 2–3 studies. This limited evidence base inherently reduces statistical power and generalizability, potentially increasing the likelihood of false-positive findings. Nevertheless, these indicators address clinically relevant dimensions of diabetes management. Their inclusion allows for a more comprehensive assessment of the potential benefits of needle-free injectors. While the observed trends are not definitive, they offer valuable directions for future research. We strongly recommend that subsequent trials employ larger sample sizes and extended follow-up periods to verify these preliminary signals. Fourth, some randomized controlled trials had shorter intervention durations, necessitating extended observation periods for more robust conclusions. Finally, many of the key outcomes (HbA1c, FPG, 2hPPG, insulin dosage, MODD, SDBG, pain scores) presented very high heterogeneity. Significant differences in baseline patient characteristics (such as type of diabetes, disease duration, BMI, etc.) across studies may have influenced the comparison of treatment effects. Moreover, variations in insulin types and dosages used in different studies may have further exacerbated heterogeneity. We also noted that differences in measurement methods and time points could have impacted the results. Future studies should consider these factors to reduce heterogeneity and enhance the reliability of pooled analyses.

Needle-free injection is a highly valuable technology with significant application potential. For instance, needle-free injection-based mRNA-LNP vaccination serves as an effective alternative to traditional methods, with the potential to enhance immune responses and reduce pain (Mao 2023). Additionally, needle-free jet injectors are effective and safe for treating proliferative and atrophic acne scars (Ridyard et al. 2016). However, further research on needle-free injection is still needed, such as multicenter studies involving patients of different races, ages, and disease severities, as well as in-depth research on the application effects of needle-free injectors with various insulin types and treatment regimens.

Conclusion

Our study indicates that needle-free injectors demonstrate significant advantages over insulin pens in certain therapeutic outcomes, such as reducing HbA1c, FPG, 2hPPG, and insulin dosage, as well as improving local adverse reactions and patient acceptance. However, the improvements in glycemic variability metrics, including MODD, SDBG, MAGE, and TIR, were not statistically significant. These findings suggest that while needle-free injectors offer notable benefits in specific aspects of diabetes management, their overall impact on glycemic variability remains inconclusive.

Regarding systemic safety, the risk of hypoglycemia in the needle-free group was not statistically significantly different from that in the needle group. Additionally, our publication bias analysis revealed potential biases in the outcomes related to hypoglycemia and insulin dosage. This underscores the need for further research to fully elucidate the long-term safety and therapeutic effectiveness of needle-free injectors compared to insulin pens, particularly in terms of systemic safety indicators such as hypoglycemia and long-term metabolic outcomes.

In summary, while needle-free injectors show promise in enhancing local safety and patient acceptance, their systemic safety and long-term therapeutic effectiveness require further investigation through additional high-quality randomized controlled trials and longer-term observational studies.

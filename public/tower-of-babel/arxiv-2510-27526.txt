Contour Integral for the Partition Function of 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 Topologically Twisted on ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} and Physical Fluxes
Lorenzo Ruggeri

Abstract
We compute the contour integral for the partition function of an 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) topologically twisted theory on ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} , dimensionally reducing from an 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 theory on S 5 S^{5} . Earlier works presented the partition function as a sum over three equivariant fluxes, one for each toric divisor of ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} . Our result depends only on a single physical flux, assigned to the non-trivial two-cycle of the manifold. The reduced summation over fluxes is compensated by a contour of integration, arising from a different solution of the BPS equations, which captures more poles in each topological sector. As our observable involves a position-dependent Yang–Mills coupling, we compute new equivariant invariants of ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} , which reduce to Donaldson invariants in the non-equivariant limit. Stability conditions of gauge bundles over ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} appear intrinsically via the dimensional reduction.

Future Directions.

This work is just the first stepping stone of a program which aims at computing exactly partition function of generic
\mathcal{N}=2
theories and to study its physical and mathematical properties. Given the (relative) simplicity of our procedure, achieving the following goals now seems within reach.

•

Higher rank gauge theories: the methodology described in this paper can be extended to
SU(N)
theories. This applies to both the stability condition restricting the sum over fluxes and Zamolodchikov’s rewriting of the Nekrasov partition function

Hosseini:2018uzp

•

Quasi-toric four-manifolds: exploiting the results in

;
Ruggeri:2025kmk

. In this case, the sum is over two independent physical fluxes.

•

Orbifolds: recently, SQFTs on spaces with orbifold singularities have drawn a lot of attention in connection to supergravity solutions with singularities in their near-horizon geometry

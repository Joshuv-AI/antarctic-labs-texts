A new class of magic positive Ehrhart polynomials of reflexive polytopes
Masato Konoike

Abstract
. The magic positivity of Ehrhart polynomials is a useful tool for proving the real-rootedness of the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomials. In this paper, we provide a new class of reflexive polytopes whose Ehrhart polynomials are magic positive. First, we prove that the Ehrhart polynomials of Stasheff polytopes are magic positive. Second, we provide a partial proof of the magic positivity of the Ehrhart polynomials of the dual polytopes of the symmetric edge polytopes of cycles.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 52B12, Secondary: 05A15, 52B20

1.
Introduction

A
lattice polytope
is the convex hull of finitely many elements in a lattice contained in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, typically
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. A lattice polytope
P
is called
reflexive
if

P^{\ast}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for any }x\in P\}

is also a lattice polytope, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

By a theorem of Ehrhart
[
3
]
,
|nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
is given by a polynomial
E_{P}(n)
of degree
\dim P
in
n
for all integers
n>0
. The polynomial
E_{P}(n)
is called the
Ehrhart polynomial
of
P
. The
h^{\ast}
-polynomial

h^{\ast}_{P}(t)=h_{0}^{\ast}+h_{1}^{\ast}t+\cdots+h_{d}t^{d}
of a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
encodes the Ehrhart polynomial in a particular basis consisting of binomial coefficients:

E_{P}(n)=h_{0}^{\ast}{n+d\choose d}+h_{1}^{\ast}{n+d-1\choose d}+\cdots+h_{d}^{\ast}{n\choose d}.

A fundamental theorem by Stanley
[
14
]
states that the coefficients of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial are always nonnegative integers. It was proved by Hibi
[
8
]
that a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
is reflexive if and only if its
h^{\ast}
-polynomial is palindromic and has degree
d
, that is,
h^{\ast}_{P}(t)=t^{d}h^{\ast}_{P}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)
.

Consider the polynomial
f(n)
expressed in a different basis:

f(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}n^{i}(1+n)^{d-i}.

If
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d}\geq 0
, then
f(n)
is said to be
magic positive
. The term ‘magic positive‘ was introduced by Ferroni and Higashitani
[
4
]
. A common area of study is whether
h^{\ast}
-polynomials are real-rooted. See Section 2 for the real-rootedness of polynomials. The motivation for studying the magic positivity comes from the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 4.19]
)
.

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
. Consider the Ehrhart polynomial written in a different basis:

E_{P}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}n^{i}(1+n)^{d-i}.

If
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d}\geq 0
, then we have simultaneously that
E_{P}(n)
has non-negative coefficients and
h^{*}_{P}(n)
is real-rooted.

And, the following question naturally arises.

Question 1.2
.

When is the Ehrhart polynomial of lattice polytopes magic positive?

It is known that the Ehrhart polynomials of zonotopes
[
1
]
and Pitman-Stanley polytopes
[
6
]
are magic positive.

The CL-ness of cross polytopes, dual of the Stasheff polytopes, root polytopes of type A, and root polytopes of type C has been studied in
[
10
]
. Here, a reflexive polytope
P
is said to be a
CL-polytope
if all of the complex roots of the Ehrhart polynomial
E_{P}(n)
lie on the line
\mathrm{Re}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}
, where
\mathrm{Re}(z)
denotes the real part of
z
. Since the dual of these three polytopes, except for the dual of the Stasheff polytopes are zonotopes
[
2
]
, their Ehrhart polynomials are magic positive. However, since Stasheff polytopes are not zonotopes, it is not immediately clear whether their Ehrhart polynomials are magic positive. The answer to this question is presented in the following first main theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

The Ehrhart polynomials of Stasheff polytopes are magic positive.

We also study the magic positivity of the Ehrhart polynomials of the dual of symmetric edge polytopes. The result of this study is presented in the following second main theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

We transform
E_{P_{C_{d+1}}^{\ast}}(n)
into the form
E_{P_{C_{d+1}}^{\ast}}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}n^{j}(1+n)^{d-j}
. Then, the coefficients
a_{i}
and
a_{d-i}
for
i=0,1,2
are positive. Here,
P_{C_{d+1}}
represents symmetric edge polytopes of cycles.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we prepare the materials for the proofs of the main results.
In Section
3
, we provide a proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In Section
4
, we investigate the magic positivity of the Ehrhart polynomials of the dual of symmetric edge polytopes and provide a proof of Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several materials used in this paper.

2.1.
Polynomial real-rooted

A polynomial
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}t^{i}
of degree
d
with real coefficients is said to be
real-rooted
, if all its roots are real. If all the coefficients of a real-rooted polynomial are nonnegative, or equivalently, if all its roots are nonpositive, then
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for all
i

[
15
]
. A sequence
a_{i}
of coefficients satisfying these inequalities is called
log-concave
. An immediate consequence is that the nonnegative, log-concave sequence is
unimodal
, meaning
a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}\geq\cdots\geq a_{d}
for some
k
.

2.2.
Stasheff polytope

Let
\mathrm{St}_{d}
be the convex hull of
\{\pm\mathbf{e}_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}\cup\{\mathbf{e}_{i}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq d\}
.
Then
\mathrm{St}_{d}
is a reflexive polytope of dimension
d
.
This polytope is the dual polytope of the so-called
Stasheff polytope
(
associahedron
).
For more detailed information, see, e.g.,
[
7
]
.

2.3.
Symmetric edge polytope

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
[d]
and the edge set
E(G)
. The
symmetric edge polytope

P_{G}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the convex hull of the set

\{\pm(e_{v}-e_{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid vw\in E(G)\}

Here, the vectors
e_{v}
are elements that form a lattice basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It known that
[
13
]

P_{G}
is reflexive. For more context on symmetric edge polytopes, see e.g.
[
9
,
12
]
.

2.4.
Magic positive

We use the following proposition to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
1
, Proposition 4.11]
)
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope and let

E_{P}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}n^{i}(1+n)^{d-i}

be its Ehrhart polynomial. Then
P
is reflexive if and only if
a_{j}=a_{d-j}
.

We explain why the dual of cross polytopes, and root polytopes of type A and type C have Ehrhart polynomials that are magic positive.

Example 2.2

(Dual of the Cross Polytope)
.

Let
\mathrm{Cr}_{d}
be the convex hull of
\{\pm\mathbf{e}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}
.
Then
\mathrm{Cr}_{d}
is a reflexive polytope of dimension
d
, called the
cross polytope
.
Since dual polytopes of
\mathrm{Cr}_{d}
and
[-1,1]^{d}
are equal, the Ehrhart polynomial of dual polytopes of
\mathrm{Cr}_{d}
can be computed as follows:

E_{\mathrm{Cr}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)=(2n+1)^{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{d}{i}(n+1)^{d-i}n^{i}.

Therefore
E_{\mathrm{Cr}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)
is magic positive.

Example 2.3

(Dual of the Root Polytope of Type A)
.

Let
{\bf A}_{d}
be the convex hull
\{\pm\mathbf{e}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}\cup\{\pm(\mathbf{e}_{i}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{j})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq d\}
.
Then
{\bf A}_{d}
is a reflexive polytope of dimension
d
, called the
root polytope of type A
.
The Ehrhart polynomial of dual polytope of
{\bf A}_{d}
is calculated in
[
10
, Lemma 5.3]
as follows:

E_{{\bf A}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{d+1}{k}n^{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(n+1)^{d-i}n^{i}.

Therefore
E_{{\bf A}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)
is magic positive.

Example 2.4

(Dual of the Root Polytope of Type C)
.

Let
{\bf C}_{d}
be the convex hull of
\{\pm\mathbf{e}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}\cup\{\pm(\mathbf{e}_{i}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{j-1})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq d\}\cup\{\pm(2\mathbf{e}_{i}+\cdots+2\mathbf{e}_{d-1}+\mathbf{e}_{d})\mid 1\leq i\leq d-1\}
.
Then
{\bf C}_{d}
is a reflexive polytope of dimension
d
, called the
root polytope of type C
.
The Ehrhart polynomial of dual polytope of
{\bf C}_{d}
is calculated in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
as follows:

E_{{\bf C}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)=(n+1)^{d}+n^{d}.

From this indication, it is clear that
E_{{\bf C}_{d}^{\ast}}(n)
is magic positive.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.
For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we use the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
d\geq 2,E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)
satisfies the following recurrence:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)=(2n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-2}}(n).

(3.1)

Proof.

First, we define some sets. By the definition of the Stasheff polytope,

\mathrm{St}_{d}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid-1\leq x_{i}\leq 1,x_{j}+\cdots+x_{k}\leq 1(1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j<k\leq d)\},

\mathrm{St}_{d-1}\times[-1,1]=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid-1\leq x_{i}\leq 1,x_{j}+\cdots+x_{k}\leq 1(1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j<k\leq d-1)\}.

Let

\mathcal{A}=(\mathrm{St}_{d-1}\times[-1,1])\backslash\mathrm{St}_{d}.

For integers
m
with
-n\leq m\leq n
, let

A_{m}=\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d-2}(n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\mid x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d-2}=m\})\right)\backslash\bigcup_{i=m+1}^{n}A_{i},

\Delta_{m}=\{(x_{d-1},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid x_{d-1}\leq n-\max\{m,0\},x_{d}\leq n,x_{d-1}+x_{d}>n-\max\{m,0\}\}.

The following equation can be easily verified:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}\times[-1,1]}(n)=(2n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n).

(3.2)

Since
\mathrm{St}_{d}\subset\mathrm{St}_{d-1}\times[-1,1]
, by (3.2)

\displaystyle\left|n\mathcal{A}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right|=(2n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)-E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n).

(3.3)

Since
\Delta_{m}
and
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid x\geq 0,y\geq 0,x+y<n\}
are unimodularly equivalent, we have the following equation:

\displaystyle|\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}|=\frac{1}{2}n(n+1).

(3.4)

The first step
: We prove the following equality:

\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}A_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}=n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}.

(3.5)

Since
\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}A_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}\subset n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
is clear, we will prove
\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}A_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}\supset n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
. For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
, we define the value
p
as follows:

p=\max{\{x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d-2}\mid 1\leq i\leq d-2\}}.

Then, since
-n\leq p\leq n
holds, and
x\in A_{p}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
follows from the definition of
A_{m}
. Therefore, we get (3.5).

From (3.4) and (3.5), we have the following equation:

\displaystyle\left|\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}(A_{m}\times\Delta_{m})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right|=\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-2}}(n).

(3.6)

The second step
: Next, we prove the following equality:

\displaystyle n\mathcal{A}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}(A_{m}\times\Delta_{m})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(3.7)

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in n\mathcal{A}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then,
x_{d}\leq n
holds, and since
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
, that is,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}A_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
holds by (3.5), there exists an integer
-n\leq k\leq n
such that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in A_{k}
.

Now, suppose
x_{d-1}>n-\max\{k,0\}
. From the first step, since there exists
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq d-2
such that
x_{i^{\prime}}+\cdots+x_{d-2}=\max\{k,0\}
, we have
x_{i^{\prime}}+\cdots+x_{d-1}>n
, implying
x\notin n(\mathrm{St}_{d-1}\times[-1,1])\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which contradicts
x\in n\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, we conclude that
x_{d-1}\leq n-\max\{k,0\}
.

Next, suppose
x_{d-1}+x_{d}\leq n-\max\{k,0\}
. From
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in n\mathrm{St}_{d-2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d-2}
, we know that
x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d-2}\leq\max\{k,0\}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-2
. Consequently,
x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d}\leq n
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, implying
x\in n\mathrm{St}_{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which contradicts
x\in n\mathrm{St}_{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Thus, we deduce
x_{d-1}+x_{d}>n-\max\{k,0\}
.
Therefore, we have
x\in\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}(A_{m}\times\Delta_{m})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Now, let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}(A_{m}\times\Delta_{m})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then there exists
-n\leq k\leq n
such that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in A_{k}\times\Delta_{k}
. Since
x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d-2}\leq\max\{k,0\}
holds for all
1\leq i\leq d-2
by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\in A_{k}
, we have
x_{i}+\cdots+x_{d-1}\leq n
.
Additionally, since there exists
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq d-2
such that
x_{i^{\prime}}+\cdots+x_{d-2}=\max\{k,0\}
and
x_{d-1}+x_{d}>n-\max\{k,0\}
by
(x_{d-1},x_{d})\in\Delta_{k}
, we conclude
x_{i^{\prime}}+\cdots+x_{d}>n
. Therefore,
x\in n\mathcal{A}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

The third step
: From (3.3) and (3.7), we have the following equation:

\left|\bigsqcup_{-n\leq m\leq n}(A_{m}\times\Delta_{m})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right|=(2n+1)E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)-E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n).

From this equation and (3.6), we get (3.1).
∎

By using Theorem 3.1, we can prove Theorem 1.3 without explicitly determining
E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Transform
(3.1)
into the following form
:

E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)=\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)+(n+1)\left(E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-2}}(n)\right).

By the recurrence relation, we observe that if both
E_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-2}}(n)
and
\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)
are magic positive, so are
E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{d-1}}(n)
and
E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)
. In other words, it suffices to show that
E_{\mathrm{St}_{1}}(n)
and
E_{\mathrm{St}_{1}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{0}}(n)
are magic positive.
In fact, we have
E_{\mathrm{St}_{0}}(n)=1,E_{\mathrm{St}_{1}}(n)=2n+1=(n+1)+n
and
E_{\mathrm{St}_{1}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}nE_{\mathrm{St}_{0}}(n)=(n+1)+\frac{1}{2}n
, as required. Therefore,
E_{\mathrm{St}_{d}}(n)
is magic positive.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

From
[
16
, Theorem 2.2]
, the coefficients of the Ehrhart polynomial of a zonotope are all integers. On the other hand, since the coefficients of the Ehrhart polynomial of a Stasheff polytope are rational numbers, the Stasheff polytope is not zonotope.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we provide the background that led us to consider Theorem
1.4
, followed by a proof of Theorem
1.4
. We begin by examining the cases of tree
T_{d}
and complete graph
K_{d}

Proposition 4.1
.

The Ehrhart polynomials of the dual of the symmetric edge polytopes of trees and complete graphs are magic positive.

Proof.

From
[
12
, Proposition 4.6]
,
P_{T_{d+1}}
and
\mathrm{Cr}_{d}

P_{K_{d+1}}
and
{\bf A}_{d}
are unimodularly equivalent. Therefore, by Example 3.2, the Ehrhart polynomials of
P_{K_{d+1}}^{\ast}
are magic positive.
∎

G

P_{K_{d}}^{\ast}\subset P_{G}^{\ast}\subset P_{T_{d}}^{\ast}.

Question 4.3
.

Is the Ehrhart polynomial of dual polytopes of symmetric edge polytopes of cycle magic positive?

We computed the Ehrhart polynomial of the dual of the symmetric edge polytopes of cycles and partially resolved its magic positivity.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
C_{d}
denote a cycle of length
d\geq 2
. Then we have

\displaystyle E_{P_{C_{d+1}}^{\ast}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{(d+1-2i)n+(d-i)}{d}\binom{d+1}{i}.

(4.1)

Proof.

By the definition of symmetric edge polytope,
P_{C_{d+1}}
is the convex hull of
\{\pm(e_{i}-e_{i+1})\mid 1\leq i\leq d-1\}\cup\{\pm(e_{d}-e_{1})\}
. Since
P_{C_{d+1}}
and the convex hull of
\{\pm e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d-1\}\cup\{\pm(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{d})\}
are unimodular equivalent, we obtain the following equality:

P_{C_{d+1}}^{\ast}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid-1\leq x_{i}\leq 1,-1\leq x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\leq 1(1\leq i\leq d)\}.

Using
[
5
, Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.5]
, we obtain

E_{P_{C_{d+1}}^{\ast}}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}\sum_{v=0}^{d}\binom{(d+1-v)n+(d-i)}{d}\rho_{\mathbf{c},i}(v)

where
\mathbf{c}=(2,\ldots,2)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}
and

\rho_{\mathbf{c},i}(v):=\#\left\{I\in\binom{[d+1]}{i}:\sum_{j\in I}c_{j}=v\right\}=\begin{cases}\binom{d+1}{i}&\text{if $v=2i$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have (4.1).
∎

By using Proposition 4.4, we can prove Theorem 1.4.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since the symmetric edge polytope is reflexive, from Proposition 2.4, it suffices to show that
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}
are positive. For
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, let

B_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1-i&2-i&\cdots&d-1-i&d-i\\
d-i&d-1-i&\cdots&2-i&1-i\end{pmatrix}

be the
2\times d
-matrix and for
I\subset[d]=\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
, let

B_{i}^{I}=(-1)^{i}\prod_{j\in I}b_{i2j}\prod_{j\in[d]\backslash I}b_{i1j}\binom{d+1}{i},C_{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}B_{i}^{I}

where
b_{ijk}
denotes the
(j,k)
-entry of the matrix
B_{i}
.
Since

\binom{(d+1-2i)n+(d-i)}{d}=\frac{1}{d!}\prod_{j=1}^{d}((j-i)n+(d+1-j-i)(n+1)),

we have
a_{i}=\sum_{I\in\binom{[d]}{i}}C_{I}
. From this equation, it suffices to show that
C_{I}\geq 0
for all
I\in\binom{[d]}{i}
. Note that
B_{0}^{I}>0
, and for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor\}
, if
i\notin I
or
d+1-i\in I
, then
B_{i}^{I}=0
.

Obviously
a_{0}=1
, so we prove that
a_{1}
is positive. For
1\leq i\leq d
, let
I=\{i\}
.

If
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor<i\leq d
, then
B_{j}^{\{i\}}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
. Therefore
C_{I}=B_{0}^{I}>0
.

If
1\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, then

\displaystyle C_{I}

\displaystyle=B_{0}^{I}+B_{i}^{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1-i}{i}-\frac{1}{d!}\binom{d+1}{i}(i-1)!(d-i)!(d+1-2i)

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1-i}{i}-(d+1)\left(\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{d+1-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1}{d+1-i}-1=\frac{i}{d+1-i}>0.

Therefore
a_{1}
is positive.

Next we prove that
a_{2}
is positive in three cases. For
1\leq i<j\leq d
, let
I=\{i,j\}
.

The first case
: If
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor\leq i<j\leq d
for all
1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
. Therefore
C_{I}=B_{0}^{I}>0

The second case
: If
1\leq i<\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor<j\leq d
, then
C_{I}=\frac{d+1-j}{j}B_{0}^{\{i\}}+\frac{d+1-j-i}{j-i}B_{i}^{\{i\}}
. Since

\frac{d+1-j}{j}-\frac{d+1-j-i}{j-i}=\frac{i(2j-d-1)}{j(j-i)}\geq 0,

B_{0}^{\{i\}}+B_{i}^{\{i\}}>0
and
B_{0}^{I}>0
, we obtain
C_{I}>0

The third case
: If
1\leq i<j\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, then

\displaystyle C_{I}

\displaystyle=B_{0}^{I}+B_{i}^{I}+B_{j}^{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d+1-i)(d+1-j)}{ij}-\frac{d+1}{(d+1-i)i}\frac{d+1-i-j}{j-i}(d+1-2i)

\displaystyle-\frac{d+1}{(d+1-j)j}\frac{d+1-i-j}{i-j}(d+1-2j)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d+1-i)(d+1-j)}{ij}-\frac{(d+1)(d+1-i-j)}{j-i}\left(\frac{d+1-2i}{(d+1-i)i}+\frac{d+1-2j}{(d+1-j)j}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(d+1)(d+1-i-j)}{ij}-(d+1)(d+1-i-j)\left(\frac{1}{ij}+\frac{1}{(d+1-i)(d+1-j)}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(d+1)(d+1-i-j)}{(d+1-i)(d+1-j)}=\frac{ij}{(d+1-i)(d+1-j)}.

Since
ij\geq 2
and
i,j<d+1
,
C_{I}>0
. Therefore
a_{2}
is positive.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

This method can’t be used for coefficient of
(n+1)^{d-3}n^{3}

d=6,I=\{1,2,4\}

C_{I}=B_{0}^{I}+B_{1}^{I}+B_{2}^{I}=(6\times 5\times 3)^{2}-7\times(5\times 4\times 2)^{2}+21\times(4\times 3)^{2}=-76

.

By computational experiments,
E_{C_{d+1}^{\ast}}
is magic positive for all

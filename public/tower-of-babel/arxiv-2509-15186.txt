The Integral Chow Rings of the Moduli Stacks of Hyperelliptic Prym Pairs III
Alessio Cela, Alberto Landi

Abstract
This paper is the third and final part of a series devoted to the description of the integral Chow rings of the moduli stacks of hyperelliptic Prym pairs. For a fixed genus g g , there are two natural stacks, ℛ ​ ℋ g \mathcal{RH}_{g} and ℛ ​ ℋ ~ g \widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g} , parametrizing hyperelliptic Prym pairs, with the former being the μ 2 \mu_{2} -rigidification of the latter. Both decompose as the disjoint union of ⌊ ( g + 1 ) / 2 ⌋ \lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor components, denoted ℛ ​ ℋ g n \mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n} and ℛ ​ ℋ ~ g n \widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}^{n} for n = 1 , … , ⌊ ( g + 1 ) / 2 ⌋ n=1,\ldots,\lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor . In this paper we present quotient stack descriptions of the components ℛ ​ ℋ g n \mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n} for even g g and compute their integral Chow rings, thereby completing the computation for all irreducible components of ℛ ​ ℋ g \mathcal{RH}_{g} . In addition, we give quotient stack presentations for all irreducible components of ℛ ​ ℋ ~ g \widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g} and determine when the rigidification map ℛ ​ ℋ ~ g n → ℛ ​ ℋ g n \widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}^{n}\to\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n} is a root gerbe. We then use this to compute the Chow rings of ℛ ​ ℋ ~ g n \widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}^{n} for all g g and n n , with the sole exception of the case where g g is odd and n = ( g + 1 ) / 2 n=(g+1)/2 . Finally, in the appendix, we discuss G G -gerbes induced by an homomorphism of abelian groups H → G H\to G and an H H -gerbe.

1.1
Main (formal) definitions

We begin by briefly recalling the definition of Prym curves and their associated moduli stacks.
We refer to
[
CIL25
, Section 2.1]
and
[
CL25a
, Section 1.1]
for more details.

A
Prym curve
of genus
g
over a scheme
S
is a triple
(C/S,\eta,\beta)
where

•

C\to S
is a smooth proper curve of genus
g
with geometrically connected fibers;

•

\eta\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
is a line bundle that restricts to a nontrivial element on each geometric fiber of
C\to S
;

•

\beta:\eta^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{C}
is an isomorphism of line bundles.

The moduli stack
\mathcal{R}_{g}
is obtained as the stackification of the prestack whose objects over
S
are genus
g
Prym curves
(C\to S,\eta,\beta)
.
Morphisms will play a central role in this paper: a morphism
(C\to S,\eta,\beta)
to
(C^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
consists of a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

(2)

such that there exists an isomorphism
\tau:\varphi^{*}\eta^{\prime}\to\eta
for which the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}(\eta^{\prime\otimes 2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\otimes 2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{\otimes 2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}(\beta^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

(3)

commutes.

The isomorphism
\tau
is not part of the data in
\mathcal{R}_{g}
; keeping track of it instead yields a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe over
\mathcal{R}_{g}
, denoted
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
in
[
CIL25
]
.

The stacks of
hyperelliptic Prym pairs
, denoted
\mathcal{RH}_{g}
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}
, are defined as the fiber product of the hyperelliptic
locus
\mathcal{H}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
with
\mathcal{R}_{g}
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
, respectively, along the natural forgetful morphisms to

\mathcal{M}_{g}
. The stack
\mathcal{RH}_{g}
(and similarly
\widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}
) decomposes into open and closed substacks

\mathcal{R}\mathcal{H}_{g}=\bigsqcup_{1\leq n\leq\lfloor\tfrac{g+1}{2}\rfloor}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{H}_{g}^{n},

(4)

which can be viewed as the stack-theoretic analogue of the set-theoretic partition for a genus
g
curve
C
:

\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[2]\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{C}\}=\bigsqcup_{1\leq n\leq\lfloor\tfrac{g+1}{2}\rfloor}B_{n},

where
B_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[2]\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{C}\}
denotes the subset of line bundles expressible as
(n\cdot g^{1}_{2})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-e)
, with
e
an effective divisor of degree
2n
consisting of
2n
distinct Weierstrass points. Here
g^{1}_{2}
denotes the unique
g^{1}_{2}
on
C
.

1.2
Statement of the main results

1.2.1
The Chow ring of
\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}
for
g
even

Let
g\geq 2
be an even genus. Our first result is a presentation for the stacks
\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}
for all
n=1,\ldots,g/2
. To state them we require some notation.

Notation 1.1
.

We denote by
\chi
the standard
\mathbb{G}_{m}
representation and by
V
the standard
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
representation. Furthermore, we let
t=c_{1}(\chi)
be the first Chern class of
\chi
in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
c_{i}=c_{i}(V)
be the Chern classes of
V
in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{GL}_{2})

For each
j\geq 1
, we denote by
W_{j}
the
(2j+1)
-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
whose underlying vector space is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
2j
in two variables. The action is given by
[B]\cdot f(X,Y)=\mathrm{det}(B)^{j}\,(f\circ B^{-1})(X,Y)
for
[B]\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
and

2j

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose

is even. Then for every

\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}\cong\Bigg[\frac{(\chi^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{det}(V)^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2n}(V^{\vee}))\times(\chi\otimes\mathrm{det}(V)^{g-n}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2g+2-2n}(V^{\vee}))\smallsetminus\Delta}{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\Bigg]

where
\Delta
is the locus of pairs of polynomials
(f,g)
such that
fg
is singular.

The proof is presented in §
2
. In particular, we can express
\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}
is a

-torsor

\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n}\to\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2n}(V^{\vee}))\times\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2g+2-2n}(V^{\vee}))\smallsetminus\underline{\Delta}}{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\bigg]

where
\underline{\Delta}
denotes again the locus of pairs of polynomials
(f,g)
(up to scalar) with

Together with
[

, Proposition 3.5]

to that of
\mathrm{CH}^{*}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2n}(V^{\vee}))\times\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2g+2-2n}(V^{\vee}))\smallsetminus\underline{\Delta})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
a,b\geq 1
, and if the characteristic of the base field is 0 or greater than
\max(2a,2b)

\mathrm{CH}^{*}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}(({\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2a}(V^{\vee}))\times\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2b}(V^{\vee}))\setminus\underline{\Delta}})=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{2a},\xi_{2b},c_{1},c_{2}]}{J}

Fix

Our main geometric input is the next proposition, whose statement requires some preliminary facts and notation.

Recall that

For

\mathcal{P}\to S

\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-action) double cover
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

For
a,b\geq 1
, define
\mathcal{D}_{a,b}
as the open substack of

\mathcal{D}_{a}\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{b}

parametrizing triples
(\mathcal{P}\to S,D_{a},D_{b})
, where
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
are disjoint Cartier divisors on
\mathcal{P}
, finite étale of degrees
a
and
b
, respectively, over
S
. There is a canonical map
\mathcal{D}_{a,b}\to\mathcal{D}_{a+b}

Proposition 1.5
.

For every
g\geq 2
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,(g+1)/2\}
, there is an isomorphism of stacks:

•

if
n<(g+1)/2
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}^{n}\;\cong\;(\mathcal{H}_{n-1}\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{g-n})\setminus\Delta,

•

if
g
is odd and

, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{RH}}_{g}^{n}\;\cong\;\left[\frac{(\mathcal{H}_{\frac{g-1}{2}}\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{\frac{g-1}{2}})\setminus\Delta}{\mu_{2}}\right],

\mu_{2}
exchanges the two factors
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{g-1}{2}}
.

Here the fiber product is taken with respect to the natural maps to
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, and
\Delta
denotes the preimage of the locus of singular polynomials under the composition

\mathcal{H}_{n-1}\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{g-n}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{D}_{2n,\,2g+2-2n}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{D}_{2g+2}\;\hookrightarrow\;[\mathbb{P}(W_{g+1})/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}].

The proof is given in §

(16)

,

G
-2-equivariant. Then, one concludes by noticing that a

∎

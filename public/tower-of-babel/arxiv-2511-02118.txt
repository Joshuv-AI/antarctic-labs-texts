Euler–Heisenberg action for fermions coupled to gauge and axial vectors: Hessian diagonalization, sector classification, and applications
Lucas Pereira de Souza

Abstract
Abstract We derive the closed-form one-loop Euler–Heisenberg effective actions for Dirac fermions coupled simultaneously to classical electromagnetic vector and massive pseudo-vector backgrounds within a controlled quasi-static approximation. Through complete diagonalization of the functional Hessian, we systematically delineate the parameter space into distinct sectors characterized by stability properties and spectral structure. We identify subspaces that encompass and extend results from previous studies into a broader class, admitting propagating axial fields as physically viable regimes; strikingly, we note sectors presenting chirality-asymmetric instability. This addresses long-standing questions regarding the well-defined nature, diagonalizability, and stability of these models. From the effective actions, we derive novel nonperturbative pair-production rates for simultaneously propagating electromagnetic and axial vector backgrounds; remarkably, we find pronounced vacuum stabilization compared to previous results. Furthermore, we demonstrate that this framework allows for a unified derivation of the chiral anomaly structures in the general case and show that the electromagnetic coupling induces instanton-like configurations for the axial field, even when it is not a fundamental gauge field. As a proof-of-concept, we analyze a cosmological toy model of baryogenesis driven by an axial vector, providing numerical estimates that support the viability of this hypothesis. Additionally, we outline qualitative predictions for Weyl/Dirac semi-metals and briefly discuss potential applications in related phenomena, such as the Strong-CP problem.

II
The Electroaxial Theory

The strategy adopted throughout closely follows the approach established by Maroto
Maroto 1999
, which is itself an extension of Schwinger’s seminal treatment of the pure QED case
Schwinger 1951
—a procedure systematically reviewed in Refs.
Dobado et al. 1997
;
Dunne 2005
. We assume a familiarity with functional methods at the level of standard quantum field theory texts
Schwartz 2013
;
Buchbinder and Shapiro 2021
while providing sufficient detail to make the calculations self-contained and establish notation conventions consistent with the broader literature on background-field effective actions.

We analyze a model-dependent theory of Dirac fermions
\psi
coupled concurrently to an electromagnetic vector potential
A_{\mu}
and a (possibly massive) pseudo-vector field
S_{\mu}
. The classical action in Minkowski spacetime is expressed as

\mathrm{S}\left[A,S,\,\overline{\!{\psi}},\psi\right]=\mathrm{S}_{0}\left[A,S\right]+\mathrm{S}_{\text{Dirac}}

(1)

Here,
\mathrm{S}_{0}\left[A,S\right]
pertains only to the vector fields, incorporating the standard Maxwell action

\mathrm{S}_{\text{Maxwell}}=-\frac{1}{4}\int d^{4}xF_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}

(2)

along with the free axial-vector action
Shapiro 2002
;
*ShapiroBelyaevTorsion

\mathrm{S}_{\text{Proca}}=\int d^{4}x\left[-\frac{1}{4}S_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}M_{S}^{2}S_{\mu}S^{\mu}\right]

(3)

In these expressions,
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
and
S_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}S_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}S_{\mu}
are the field-strength tensors corresponding to the vector and pseudo-vector sectors, respectively, while
M_{S}
denotes the mass of the pseudo-vector field. Based on the model of choice, classical interaction terms (e.g.,
A_{\mu}S^{\mu}
) can also be included in the analysis.

The Dirac action for the fermions is defined as

\mathrm{S}_{\text{Dirac}}=\int d^{4}x\,\,\overline{\!{\psi}}(i\not{D}-m)\psi

(4)

where the covariant derivative is expressed as
\not{D}=\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}-i\eta S_{\mu}\gamma^{5})
. Here,
e
and
\eta
are the respective coupling constants to the vector and axial-vector backgrounds, while
\gamma^{5}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
satisfies
\{\gamma^{5},\gamma^{\mu}\}=0
. The presence of
\gamma^{5}
distinguishes this theory qualitatively from standard QED: left- and right-handed fermion components, obtainable through the application of the chiral projectors
\mathrm{P}_{L/R}=(1\mp\gamma^{5})/2
, couple to different “effective vectors”

V_{\mu}^{(R/L)}=eA_{\mu}\pm\eta S_{\mu}

(5)

rather than to a common gauge field. This allows for the convenient notation
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-iV^{(R)}_{\mu}\mathrm{P}_{R}-iV^{(L)}_{\mu}\mathrm{P}_{L}
for the covariant derivative.

The effective action can then be constructed by integrating out the fermions within the generating functional:

Z\left[A,S\right]=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}\,\overline{\!{\psi}}\mathcal{D}\psi e^{i\mathrm{S}\left[A,S,\,\overline{\!{\psi}},\psi\right]}}{\int\mathcal{D}\,\overline{\!{\psi}}\mathcal{D}\psi e^{i\mathrm{S}\left[A=S=0\right]}}=e^{iW\left[A,S\right]}

(6)

This yields the effective action

W\left[A,S\right]=\mathrm{S}_{0}\left[A,S\right]-i\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left(\frac{i\not{D}-m}{i\not{\partial}-m}\right)}

(7)

The one-loop quantum corrections are encapsulated in the last term, which forms the focal point of our analysis.

For simplicity, we assume that the background fields are smooth and confined to a compact spatial domain, with appropriate boundary conditions, within which the quasi-static approximation holds
Omanović 1997
. This assumption is standard for EH-type effective actions
Schwinger 1951
;
Dunne 2005
as it enables the application of translation-operator manipulations described in Sec.
IV
while effectively capturing the physics of slowly varying fields. Generalizations to time-dependent backgrounds extend beyond one-loop exactness and typically require resummation techniques
Vassilevich 2003
;
*HeatKernel2
;
Barvinsky and Vilkovisky 1985
;
*resummedeffectiveaction
;
*sebastianresummedheatkerneleffective
or worldline methods
Gies et al. 2005
. Under this quasi-static approximation, the extended Dirac operator exhibits elliptic properties and possesses a discrete spectrum, thereby ensuring it is trace-class
Vassilevich 2003
;
*HeatKernel2
. Consequently, the traces of
\not{D}
and its transpose are equivalent, such that

\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left(\frac{i\not{D}-m}{i\not{\partial}-m}\right)}=\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left(\frac{i\not{D}^{\mathrm{T}}-m}{i\not{\partial}^{\mathrm{T}}-m}\right)}

(8)

The transpose of the Dirac operator can be derived using the charge conjugation matrix defined as
C=i\gamma^{2}\gamma^{0}
. This matrix exhibits the properties:

\displaystyle C\gamma^{\mu}C^{-1}=-\left(\gamma^{\mu}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle C\gamma^{5}C^{-1}=\left(\gamma^{5}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}=\gamma^{5}

(9)

These properties yield:

\not{D}^{\mathrm{T}}=-C\left(\not{\partial}-i\not{V}^{(R)}\mathrm{P}_{L}-i\not{V}^{(L)}\mathrm{P}_{R}\right)C^{-1}=-C\not{D}^{\ast}C^{-1}

(10)

where
\not{D}^{\ast}=\not{\partial}-ie\not{A}+i\eta\not{S}\gamma^{5}
. Consequently, we can express the quantum correction as:

\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left(\frac{i\not{D}-m}{i\not{\partial}-m}\right)}=\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left[\frac{1}{\left(i\not{\partial}^{\mathrm{T}}-m\right)}CC^{-1}\left(i\not{D}^{\mathrm{T}}-m\right)\right]}=\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left[\frac{\left(-i\not{D}^{\ast}-m\right)}{\left(-i\not{\partial}-m\right)}\right]}

(11)

Adding the term on the left-hand side, we find that this correction can be represented as:

i\overline{\Gamma}\left[A,S\right]=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln{\left[\frac{-\not{D}^{\ast}\not{D}-im\left(\not{D}-\not{D}^{\ast}\right)+m^{2}}{\not{\partial}^{2}+m^{2}}\right]}

(12)

In this equation, we denote the quantum corrections as
\overline{\Gamma}[A,S]
. Direct substitution gives:

\not{D}^{\ast}\not{D}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right\}-i\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]\right)D_{\mu}D_{\nu}=D^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(eF_{\mu\nu}+\eta S_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\right)\sigma^{\mu\nu}

(13)

Thus, we arrive at:

\overline{\Gamma}[A,S]=-\frac{i}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\left[\frac{\left(i\partial+eA+\eta S\gamma^{5}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(eF_{\mu\nu}+\eta S_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\bigg)\sigma^{\mu\nu}+2\eta m\not{S}\gamma^{5}-m^{2}}{\left(-\partial^{2}-m^{2}\right)}\right]

(14)

Here, we have utilized Eq. (
8
) and substituted Eq. (
13
), with both numerator and denominator multiplied by
-1
for convenience.

Next, we transition to an operator formalism, employing hat notation for operators temporarily. Let
\hat{x}^{\mu}
and
\hat{p}_{\mu}
be the position and momentum operators, respectively, which satisfy the commutation relations
[\hat{x}^{\mu},\hat{p}_{\nu}]=-i\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}
, each possessing a complete eigenbasis
\hat{x}^{\mu}\left|x\right>=x^{\mu}\left|x\right>
and
\hat{p}_{\mu}\left|p\right>=p_{\mu}\left|p\right>
satisfying

\left<p\vphantom{x}\right|\left.x\vphantom{p}\right>=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}e^{ip\cdot x}

(15)

Therefore, we have:

\left<x\right|\hat{p}_{\mu}\left|\Psi\right>=i\partial_{\mu}\left<x\vphantom{\Psi}\right|\left.\Psi\vphantom{x}\right>

(16)

for any state
\left|\Psi\right>
. We denote

i\hat{D}_{\mu}\left<x\vphantom{\Psi}\right|\left.\Psi\vphantom{x}\right>\to\left|\Psi\right>=\left<x\right|\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\mu}+\eta\hat{S}_{\mu}\gamma^{5}\right)\left|\Psi\right>

(17)

where
\hat{\Pi}_{\mu}=\hat{p}_{\mu}+e\hat{A}_{\mu}
, and we will drop the hat notation for the remainder of this paper. By employing the identities

\displaystyle\gamma^{5}\sigma^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}=-\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\nu}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}

(18)

we simplify Eq. (
14

\overline{\Gamma}[A,S]=-\frac{i}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\left[\frac{\left(\Pi_{\mu}+\eta S_{\mu}\gamma^{5}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(V_{\mu\nu}+\frac{2\eta m}{3}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}S^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}\bigg)\sigma^{\mu\nu}-m^{2}}{p^{2}-m^{2}}\right]

(19)

where we define
V_{\mu\nu}=eF_{\mu\nu}-i\eta\tilde{S}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{S}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}S^{\alpha\beta}
.

Now, we proceed to employ Fock–Schwinger’s proper-time representation

;
Fock 1937
for every
\tau>0
, provided that analytic continuation exists:

\overline{\Gamma}\left[A,S\right]=\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}e^{-i\tau m^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[e^{i\tau\mathcal{H}}-e^{i\tau p^{2}}\right]

(20)

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the quadratic Hessian functional:

\mathcal{H}=\left(\Pi_{\mu}+\eta S_{\mu}\gamma^{5}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(V_{\mu\nu}+\frac{2\eta m}{3}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}S^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}\bigg)\sigma^{\mu\nu}

(21)

This representation brings forth a long and well discussed

;
Schwartz 2013
;
*QFTQGdoprof
analogy between these exponentials in Eq. (
20

e^{i\tau\mathcal{H}}\left|x\right>=\left|x;\tau\right>
. In this context,
\tau
acts as a form of “proper time,” while the remaining terms function akin to a “Hamiltonian” that adheres to its own Schrödinger-like functional equations, expressed as follows:

-i\partial_{\tau}\left<y;0\vphantom{x;\tau}\right|\left.x;\tau\vphantom{y;0}\right>=\left<y;0\right|\mathcal{H}\left|x;\tau\right>

(22)

Here,
\partial_{\tau}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
\tau

Moreover, the correlation between the last term in Eq. (
20
) and the coincidence limit of the free particle’s propagator facilitates the application of standard integration techniques within momentum representation

. This yields:

\mathrm{Tr}\ e^{i\tau p^{2}}=\int d^{4}x\left<x\right|e^{i\tau p^{2}}\left|x\right>\mathrm{tr}\mathbf{1}=\lim_{y\to x}\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}x\ d^{4}p\left<y;0\vphantom{p}\right|\left.p\vphantom{y;0}\right>\left<p\vphantom{x;\tau}\right|\left.x;\tau\vphantom{p}\right>=\int d^{4}x\ \frac{i}{(2\pi\tau)^{2}}

In this equation,
\mathrm{tr}\mathbf{1}=4
represents the trace in Dirac space. In contrast, the first term in Eq. (

III
Hessian Diagonalization and Sector Classification

In this section, we present the diagonalization procedure and the taxonomy of sectors. We begin by decomposing the first term of the Hessian as follows:

(\Pi+\eta S\gamma^{5})^{2}=\left(\Pi^{2}+\eta^{2}S^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}+2\eta\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}\gamma^{5}

(24)

Here,
S^{2}=S_{\mu}S^{\mu}
, and it is noteworthy that any operator product will henceforth be interpreted as anti-commutators; for instance,

2\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}\equiv\left\{\Pi_{\mu},S^{\mu}\right\}=\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}+S^{\mu}\Pi_{\mu}=i\partial_{\mu}\left(S^{\mu}\right)+2eA_{\mu}S^{\mu}

We can recast (
21

\mathcal{H}=(\Pi^{2}+\eta^{2}S^{2})\mathbf{1}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{R}}

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{R}}=2\eta\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}\gamma^{5}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(V_{\mu\nu}+\frac{2\eta m}{3}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}S^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}\bigg)\sigma^{\mu\nu}

(26)

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=\left(\pm\right)\frac{\sqrt{6}}{6}\left[\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{-}}\pm i\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{+}\left(\pm\right)\frac{24i\mathcal{B}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{-}}}}\right]

\pm
represent independent signs, with

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.95}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}V_{\mu\nu}=e^{2}\mathcal{G}_{A}+\eta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{S}+\frac{ie\eta}{2}S^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$}}

The parameter space can be systematically categorized into various types based on the limiting regions where the system displays distinct properties:

Extended-Quantum-Electrodynamics-type (EQED):

In this regime, the conditions
\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}=\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}=0
and
S^{2}\simeq 0
hold, leading to the simplification of the Hessian to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1}{2}V_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}
. This yields expressions that are analogous to those found in QED, where the quantity
V^{\mu\nu}
serves as an extended version of the Faraday tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
. The eigenvalues associated with this system can be expressed as follows:

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=(\pm)\sqrt{2\mathcal{V}\pm 2i\mathcal{W}}

(30)

Mixed-Transverse-type (MT):

In the MT case, the fields are constrained by the condition
\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}=0
, indicating that they are transverse and orthogonal, specifically
A_{\mu}S^{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}(S^{\mu})=0
. Under these constraints, the eigenvalues take the following form:

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=\left(\pm\right)\sqrt{2\mathcal{V}-4m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}\pm 2\sqrt{4m^{2}\eta^{2}\left(\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}\right)^{2}-\mathcal{W}^{2}}}

(31)

The system remains well-behaved as long as the condition
\mathcal{V}+\Re\sqrt{4m^{2}\eta^{2}(\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu})^{2}-\mathcal{W}^{2}}\geq 2m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{W}=0
, Eq. (
31

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=\left(\pm\right)2\sqrt{\eta m\left(-\eta mS^{2}\pm\left|\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}\right|\right)}

(32)

When the pseudo-vector is light-like (

\mathcal{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\Pi^{2}\mathbf{1}+2\sqrt{\eta m\left|\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}\right|}\,\sigma_{z}&0\\
0&\Pi^{2}\mathbf{1}+2i\sqrt{\eta m\left|\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}\right|}\,\sigma_{z}\end{bmatrix}

(33)

where
\sigma_{z}=\mathrm{diag}\{1,-1\}
represent the Pauli matrix in the
z

(\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu})^{2}=0
and the generalized fields are (anti-)self-dual, i.e.,
\mathcal{V}=\pm i\mathcal{W}

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=(\pm)\sqrt{2\left(1\pm 1\right)\mathcal{V}-4m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}}

(34)

which means the Hessian can show a similar structure to Eq. (
33

\mathcal{V}\geq m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}

33

Non-anomalous-type (NonAnom):

In this region, where
\mathcal{W}=0

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\pm}=\left(\pm\right)\sqrt{2\mathcal{V}+4\eta^{2}\left[\left(\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}\right)^{2}-m^{2}S^{2}\right]\pm 4\eta\sqrt{2\left(\Pi_{\mu}S^{\mu}\right)^{2}\mathcal{V}+m^{2}\left(\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\nu}\right)^{2}}}

This regime corresponds to disconnected vector and pseudo-vector sectors, which propagate in configurations for which any local CP violation arising from one field is counterbalanced by an opposite CP violation from the other field. This dynamic results in an (almost) preserved CP symmetry; for instance, a circularly polarized light ray is associated with an “axial-light ray” from

IV
The Exact One-Loop Effective Action

In this section, we derive closed-form expressions for effective actions of models situated within the previously classified consistent subspaces by employing zeta-function regularization. This process involves taking the trace in the eigenbasis of the Hessian and subsequently applying minimal subtraction (MS). We will initiate our analysis by categorizing the previously determined Hessians into two distinct classes and systematically identifying their eigenbasis on a case-by-case basis.

First class:

Since both fields are massless, we can utilize Fock-Schwinger’s gauge for each field, such that
V^{(R/L)}_{\mu}=-(eF_{\mu\nu}\pm\eta S_{\mu\nu})x^{\nu}/2\pm\bar{S}_{\mu}
. If
F_{\mu\nu}
and
S_{\mu\nu}
form a regular pencil
*[See
;
e.g.
;
][forfurtherdetails.]gantmacher
, it is possible to simultaneously transform them into Jordan’s form through an appropriate choice of coordinates. By applying boosts and rotations, we can choose a reference frame where each vector potential can be parameterized as

V^{(H)}_{\mu}=\left(-E^{(H)}_{V}x,0,0,-B^{(H)}_{V}z,0\right)

(44)

where
(H)=(R)
or
(L)
indicates the chirality sector. Here, the quantities
E^{(H)}_{V}
and
B^{(H)}_{V}
represent the electric and magnetic field analogs extracted from
V^{(H)}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}V^{(H)}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V^{(H)}_{\mu}

29

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{F}^{(H)}_{V}=\frac{1}{4}V^{(H)}_{\mu\nu}V_{(H)}^{\mu\nu}=e^{2}\mathcal{F}_{A}+\eta^{2}\mathcal{F}_{S}\pm\frac{e\eta}{2}S^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\\
\mathcal{G}^{(H)}_{V}=-\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{V}^{(H)}_{\mu\nu}V_{(H)}^{\mu\nu}=e^{2}\mathcal{G}_{A}+\eta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{S}\pm\frac{e\eta}{2}\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}\end{gathered}

(45)

and therefore

\displaystyle\left(B^{(H)}_{V}\right)^{2}-\left(E^{(H)}_{V}\right)^{2}=2\mathcal{F}^{(H)}_{V}

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(H)}_{V}\mathbf{B}^{(R/L)}_{V}=\mathcal{G}_{V}^{(H)}

(46)

Furthermore, since
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{W}=0
, whereby we find

when
\mathcal{F}^{(H)}_{V}=\mathcal{G}^{(H)}_{V}=0

B^{(H)}_{V}=iE^{(H)}_{V}=\sqrt{\mathcal{F}^{(H)}_{V}}

42

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}=\left[p_{t}-E^{(H)}_{V}x\right]^{2}-p^{2}_{x}-\left[p_{y}-B^{(H)}_{V}z\right]^{2}-p_{z}^{2}+X_{(H)}

(47)

where

and
X_{(L)}=2i\sqrt{\eta m|\widetilde{V}^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu}|}\,\sigma_{z}=\mathcal{X}_{-}\sigma_{z}

n

\mathcal{H}

, we can express the trace as
\mathrm{Tr}e^{-i\tau\mathcal{H}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{R}}e^{-i\tau\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{R}}}+\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{L}}e^{-i\tau\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{L}}}
and employ momentum operators as

\left[p_{t}-E^{(H)}_{V}x\right]^{2}=e^{\frac{ip_{t}p_{x}}{E^{(H)}_{V}}}\left[E^{(H)}_{V}x\right]^{2}e^{-\frac{ip_{t}p_{x}}{E^{(H)}_{V}}}

(48)

allowing us to reduce Eq. (
42
) into quantum harmonic oscillators, such that

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{H}}=-\left[p^{2}_{x}-\left(E^{(H)}_{V}\right)^{2}x^{2}\right]-\left[p^{2}_{z}+\left(B^{(H)}_{V}\right)^{2}z^{2}\right]+X_{(H)}

(49)

Second class:

For more general theories, the Hessian can be expressed as

\mathcal{H}=\Pi^{2}+\eta^{2}S^{2}+X

(50)

where
X=\mathrm{diag}\{\mathcal{X}_{+}\sigma_{z},\mathcal{X}_{-}\sigma_{z}\}
and
X_{\pm}=|\lambda^{\left(\pm\right)}_{\pm}|
. The next step involves decomposing
S_{\mu}
into its transverse and longitudinal modes, followed by adopting an approximate Fock-Schwinger gauge for its transverse component within the domain defined by the quasi-static assumptions, specifically
S_{\mu}=S_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}+\bar{S}_{\mu}
, satisfying
S^{2}=(S_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu})^{2}+\bar{S}^{2}\approx\bar{S}^{2}
. We can extract
\bar{S}^{2}
from the Hessian, leading us to define the effective mass as

M^{2}=m^{2}-\eta^{2}\bar{S}^{2}

\mathcal{H}=\left(p_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}eF^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\right)^{2}+\eta^{2}\left(S^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}\right)^{2}+X

e^{-i\frac{p_{t}p_{x}}{a}}\left[\left(p_{t}-ax\right)^{2}+b^{2}t^{2}\right]e^{i\frac{p_{t}p_{x}}{a}}=a^{2}x^{2}+b^{2}\left(t+\frac{p_{x}}{a}\right)^{2}

46

\displaystyle B^{2}_{A/S}-E^{2}_{A/S}=2\mathcal{F}_{A/S}

\displaystyle\text{ and }

where we define

VI.2
Baryogenesis and Cosmology

Particle creation, closely associated with the chiral anomaly
Navarro-Salas 2021
, also plays a pivotal role in providing CP violations; thus, such theories align with Sakharov’s conditions for baryogenesis. To delve into the feasibility of this scenario, we propose a toy model as a proof-of-concept.

Consider the presence of an axial vector (for instance, the pseudo-trace of torsion) with mass
M_{S}\gg m
in the primordial universe. Assuming thermal equilibrium, once the temperature of the universe falls below
T\sim M_{S}
, the excitation of its new modes begins to decrease as
\propto e^{-M_{S}/T}
. This change allows us to model it as oscillating classically, with an energy density given by
\mathcal{E}\approx M_{S}+3T/2
.

At this juncture, the energy stored in this pseudo-vector is sufficiently substantial to initiate a particle creation process. This process will continue until the strength of its electric field analogue drops below a critical value or until it “freezes.” Subsequently, the remaining energy will undergo dilution until the present day. The asymmetry in particle numbers for each chirality at the conclusion of this process translates into a baryon asymmetry that remains conserved until the values recently measured
Aghanim et al. 2020
.

Let us assume an appropriate coarse-graining process that preserves the Markovian approximation
Álvarez-Domínguez et al. 2024
, allowing the particle creation rates to remain approximately constant at each step. From Eq. (
89
), it follows that
n_{5,0}=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\rho[A,S]dt=N_{5}/V_{\text{comoving}}
represents the total number of particles generated per co-moving volume at zero temperature, where
t_{i}
and
t_{f}
denote the initial and final instants of the process. At this time, the universe was dominated by radiation
Kolb and Turner 1994
;
*ParticleDataGroup
, leading to

t=\frac{0.301M_{\text{Planck}}}{\sqrt{g_{\ast}}T^{2}}\implies dt\approx-\frac{0.602M_{\text{Planck}}}{\sqrt{g_{\ast}}T^{3}}dT

(98)

where
M_{\text{Planck}}=1.22\times 10^{19}\,GeV
and
g_{\ast}\approx 106.75
is taken as a constant. Assuming the entire process occurs prior to the electroweak symmetry breaking or even before neutrinos acquire mass (alternatively, we could consider calculating the temperature correction for massless fermions as a first approximation, given that their masses are small compared to
M_{S}
), we follow the prescription outlined by
Kim et al. 2009
. This involves assuming maximum efficiency in particle creation—thus using QED’s rate as an approximation—yielding

n_{5}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int n_{5,0}\tanh{\left(\frac{\omega}{2T}\right)}d^{3}\mathbf{k}=-\frac{-0.301\zeta\left(3\right)\eta^{2}E^{2}_{S}M_{\text{Planck}}}{\left(4\pi\right)^{4}\sqrt{g_{\ast}}}\Delta T

(99)

where
\Delta T=T_{f}-T_{i}
represents the temperature difference between the onset and conclusion of the process.

We can then leverage the fact that
n_{5}/s=\text{constant}
to extrapolate the measured baryon-to-photon ratio
\eta_{B}=n_{5}/n_{\gamma}\approx 6.12\times 10^{-10}

s\approx 7.04n_{\gamma}
today, but was
s\approx(2\pi^{2}/45)g_{\ast}T_{f}^{3}
during the radiation-dominated epoch. This allows us to find

S_{\mu}

\eta\sqrt{\left<S^{2}\right>}=\frac{2\left(2\pi\right)^{3}T_{f}}{M_{S}\sqrt{45M_{\text{Planck}}}}\left(\frac{T_{i}}{T_{f}}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4g_{\ast}^{3}}{\zeta^{2}\left(3\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(1.6974\times 10^{-5}\right)\,GeV^{\frac{3}{2}}

(100)

\eta\left<{S}\right>_{\text{rms}}\approx 1.54\frac{\alpha_{f}^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{f}}}10^{-11}\,GeV

to

VII
Discussion and Outlook

In this work, we have presented a complete one-loop analysis of the electroaxial theory—Dirac fermions coupled to both electromagnetic and massive axial-vector backgrounds—within a controlled quasi-static approximation. By performing a full diagonalization of the functional Hessian, we classified the parameter space into physically distinct sectors, derived exact closed-form expressions for the EH effective action in the viable regimes, and extracted novel nonperturbative predictions for vacuum stability, pair production, and chiral anomaly structure. Our results unify and extend prior treatments revealing a robust vacuum-stabilization mechanism.

Nonetheless, several limitations inherent to our approach warrant acknowledgment. First, the current analysis lacks a thorough examination of the physical motivations underlying the necessity for a system to remain confined to one of the identified subspaces, as well as the reasons for its adherence to the stability criteria within that region of phase space. These constraints are likely application-dependent; however, for fundamental theories that incorporate axial vectors, there may not be an inherent justification for such restrictions. This potential absence of a priori reasoning could pose significant challenges for the applicability of the theoretical framework presented. Nevertheless, the framework offered here can be applied whenever a system visits the regions that we identify.

Second, our analysis assumes constant field invariants, which restricts applicability to homogeneous, slowly varying backgrounds. While standard for exact one-loop effective actions, this quasi-static approximation precludes direct modeling of rapidly time-dependent phenomena—such as ultrafast laser pulses or early-universe phase transitions—where non-adiabatic effects dominate. In such dynamical settings, one would expect contributions from topological terms like the Nieh–Yan invariant, which vanish identically in our constant-background limit but may become significant in curved or time-varying geometries.

Third, while we have verified consistency with perturbative expansions up to third order and matched known QED and Maroto limits, a full Functional Renormalization Group
Wetterich 1993
analysis of the Electroaxial theoretic model at used (including higher-loop corrections and wave-function renormalization) remains open. This is particularly relevant for massive axial fields motivated by torsion
Reuter and Schollmeyer 2016
, where consistency with quantum gravity or UV-completion scenarios (e.g., string theory) may impose additional constraints on couplings and mass scales.

Finally, our treatment is confined to flat spacetime. Gravitational effects, which are crucial for cosmological applications like baryogenesis or primordial magnetogenesis, are thus incorporated only indirectly via the axial field’s origin (e.g., as torsion’s pseudo-trace). A fully covariant generalization, incorporating curved backgrounds
Avramidi 1995
;
*HeatKernelQG2
and spin-connection couplings, would be essential to assess backreaction, gravitational particle production
Dobado and Maroto 1999
, and the interplay between spacetime curvature and axial-induced anomalies
Dobado and Maroto 1998
.

Despite these limitations, our framework opens several promising avenues for future research in Condensed Matter Physics, Extensions of the Standard Model, Gravitation, and Cosmology. The exact results derived here not only resolve long-standing questions about stability and consistency but also furnish a toolkit for probing quantum vacuum structure from tabletop Weyl materials to the primordial universe. Therefore, we expect experimental verification of interesting new phenomena related to this work within the next years.

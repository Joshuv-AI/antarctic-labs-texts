NHBA antibodies elicited by 4CMenB vaccination are key for serum bactericidal activity against Neisseria gonorrhoeae
Authors: Yih-Ling Tzeng, Soma Sannigrahi, David S Stephens
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe 4CMenB (BexseroR) vaccine contains detergent-extracted outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) from a Neisseria meningitidis (Nm) group B strain NZ98/254 and three recombinant Nm protein antigens: Neisseria adhesin A (NadA), Factor H binding protein (FHbp, as the C-terminal protein in the GNA2091-FHbp fusion), and Neisserial Heparin Binding Antigen (NHBA, as the N-terminal protein in the NHBA-GNA1030 fusion). Previous work has shown that 4CMenB generates serum antibodies to Nm and Neisseria gonorrhoeae (Ng) OMV proteins and lipooligosaccharide (LOS). Mounting evidence indicates 4CMenB can partially protect against mucosal infections with Ng. The immunologic basis for Ng cross protection remains to be fully elucidated. Ten paired human sera obtained pre- and post-immunization with 4CMenB (1 month after a third vaccine dose) were used in ELISAs and in Western blots to determine IgG and IgA serum responses to OMVs from Nm strain NZ98/254 (OMVNm) and two Ng strains, 1291 and CNG20 (OMVNg), and gonococcal recombinant NHBA (rNHBANg) proteins. Post 4CMenB sera, but not pre-sera, showed strong IgG and variable IgA responses to the OMVNm but lower (2–11-fold difference in signal intensity) recognition of OMVNg. All post (not pre) 4CMenB sera showed strong IgG, but variable IgA, recognition of rNHBANg by ELISAs and Western blots. Three post 4CMenB sera at 10% (v/v) concentration had serum bactericidal activity (SBA) against Ng strains 1291 and CNG20 (~30–40% killing), not seen in paired pre-sera. These data confirmed 4CMenB-induced cross-reactive functional antibody responses to Ng. In competitive SBA assays, in which sera were pre-incubated with rNHBA, minimal SBA against Nm strain NZ98/254 was titrated away. However, most of the SBA against Ng strains 1291 and CNG20 required NHBA-specific antibodies, and the Δnhba mutants were resistant to killing by post 4CMenB sera. Removing NHBA-specific and LOS-specific OMV antibodies simultaneously decreased SBA significantly more than the sum of removing individual antibodies alone, suggesting synergy between anti-NHBA and anti-OMV antibodies. Anti- NHBANm antibodies induced by 4CMenB vaccination cross react with NHBANg and substantially contribute to the bactericidal response toward Ng induced by the vaccine.Subject terms: Protein vaccines, Bacterial infection

Introduction

The sexually transmitted infection gonorrhea, caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae (Ng), and its complications remain a key global health concern. The World Health Organization estimated 82.4 million new gonorrhea cases among adolescents and adults worldwide in 20201. In the United States, an estimated ~1.5 M new gonococcal infections occur each year2, and gonorrhea is the second most commonly reported bacterial communicable disease3. Further, the rise of multi-drug resistant gonorrhea1 is complicating treatment strategies4. In the US in 2022, Gonococcal Isolate Surveillance Project estimated about half of all gonococcal infections to be resistant or have elevated minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) to at least one antibiotic3,5,6. A vaccine with even partial effectiveness against gonorrhea would provide a substantial public health benefit.

The lack of defined immune protection correlates and unclear mechanisms of protective immunity against infections have hampered gonococcal vaccine development. However, implementation of Neisseria meningitidis (Nm) outer membrane vesicles (OMV)-based vaccines in Norway, Cuba, and Canada7–9 coincided with declines in the incidence of gonorrhea, in contrast with other sexually transmitted infections. A 2017 retrospective case control study that assessed vaccine effectiveness against gonorrhea among young adults who received the meningococcal MeNZB OMV vaccine found an estimated vaccine effectiveness against gonorrhea of 31% after adjustment10. Additional retrospective studies had reported the effectiveness of a broadly licensed Nm group B meningococcal vaccine (4CMenB/BexseroR) against gonorrhea. Data from New York City and Philadelphia showed that individuals who received two doses of 4CMenB had 40% effectiveness in preventing gonorrhea as compared to subjects without vaccination11. Another US study in Southern California found a 46% lower rate of gonococcal infection, but not chlamydia infection, among 4CMenB recipients compared with matched counterparts who had received meningococcal MenACWY conjugated polysaccharide vaccine12. A study from South Australia also found that two doses of 4CMenB vaccination provided effectiveness of 32.7% against gonorrhea compared to chlamydia infections13. The modest protection against gonococcal infections by 4CMenB vaccination suggests that cross reactivities of shared antigens between Nm and Ng elicit antibody responses.

The 4CMenB vaccine contains three major and two minor recombinant protein antigens together with detergent-extracted OMVs from the Nm group B strain NZ98/254, the basis for the MenBNZ OMV vaccine previously developed in response to a group B meningococcal epidemic in New Zealand14. A major immunogenic outer membrane protein (OMP) in the NZ98/254 OMVs is PorA, subtype P1.7-2, 4. The three major recombinant protein antigens are Factor H binding protein (FHbp, GNA1870), Neisserial adhesin A (NadA, GNA1994), and Neisserial heparin binding antigen (NHBA, GNA2132)15. To further increase immunogenicity, two minor antigens are present as fusion proteins to NHBA and FHbp, NHBA as the N-terminal protein in the NHBA-GNA1030 fusion and FHbp as the C-terminal protein in the GNA2091-FHbp fusion, respectively. While both minor antigens are predicted to be OMP16, GNA1030 is a periplasmic Neisseria ubiquinone binding protein (NUbp) that binds to ubiquinone-8, a cofactor mainly involved in the electron transport chain17. GNA2091 is shown to localize at the periplasmic side of the outer membrane and functions in the assembly of OMPs18. In contrast to Nm, NadA and PorA are absent in the gonococcus. Further, the gonococcal FHbp homolog, termed Ghfp, lacks a signal peptide and is therefore not surface exposed19. Thus, NHBA is the only major recombinant protein antigen of 4CMenB found on the gonococcal surface. NHBA together with other minor OMPs and lipooligosaccharide (LOS)20 in OMVs are the potential meningococcal antigens in 4CMenB that cross-react with the gonococcus and may perhaps protect against gonococcal infections. In this study, we provide further understanding of the contributions of NHBA-directed antibodies elicited by the 4CMenB vaccine and show that NHBA-specific antibodies are key to the bactericidal activity against Ng.

Results

IgG and IgA responses induced in post 4CMenB vaccinated sera to gonococcal antigens including NHBA

Previous work has shown that the OMV (Nm strain NZ98/254) component of 4CMenB generates serum antibodies that cross-react with proteins and LOS in Ng OMV20–22. To assess the cross reactivities of the 4CMenB vaccine-induced antibodies to gonococcal antigens, OMVs were isolated from the spent culture supernatants of Nm vaccine strain NZ98/254, the widely used laboratory gonococcal strain 1291 and a recent gonococcal urethral clinical isolate CNG20. The gonococcal strains express the two most common NHBA variants that account for >60% of the Ng genome records (n = 16,315) in the PubMLST database. In addition, recombinant NHBANg (rNHBANg) proteins expressed by 1291 (NHBA_542) and CNG20 (NHBA_475) were cloned and purified from E. coli as mature proteins without the signal peptide. These two most common gonococcal NHBANg proteins are highly similar (99.3% identity), with only three residue variations (A390V, G414S, and S434G) at the C-terminal domain. In preliminary analyses of IgG and IgA recognition of the two gonococcal OMVs and two rNHBANg proteins, no strain-dependent differences in antibody responses of 4CMenB sera between two gonococcal OMVs and between two rNHBANg antigens were observed, thus the OMVCNG20 and rNHBA1291 were selected as the representative of OMVNg and rNHBANg, respectively. The OMVs from the vaccine strain NZ98/254 was used as a reference (OMVNm) for comparisons to the gonococcal antigens. We subsequently probed the IgG and IgA responses to OMVNm, OMVNg, and rNHBANg by ELISA with 10 matched sets of pre- and post-4CMenB sera. The post-sera were obtained 1 month after the third dose of 4CMenB23. All ten post-4CMenB sera showed increases in IgG responses to OMVNm (1.7–13.6-fold) when compared to the corresponding pre-sera (Fig. 1A); the post serum #10 had the lowest IgG response (1.7-fold). Two pre-sera (#19 and #20) showed higher baseline IgG levels than the other pre-sera, implying potential preexisting antibodies. The IgG levels detected by OMVNg paralleled those for OMVNm with 1.6–10.0-fold increases post 4CMenB immunization; however, the recognition of OMVNg was lower compared to OMVNm, both in baseline and post vaccination sera (1.9–11.3-fold, Fig. 1A Table). As NHBA is the only recombinant protein of the 4CMenB vaccine that is surface expressed in Ng, we assessed the antibodies specifically targeting NHBA using purified rNHBANg as the capturing antigen in ELISAs. All post 4CMenB sera showed very strong IgG reactivity to the rNHBANg protein and the IgG of post 4CMenB sera compared to the pre-sera increased 6.0-160.6-fold, which was not surprising considering there is 50 μg recombinant NHBA-GNA1030 fusion protein in a 0.5-mL dose of 4CMenB vaccine. The markedly higher IgG signals captured by rNHBANg relative to those captured by OMVNg (2.0–14.7-fold, Fig. 1A Table) indicated that 4CMenB vaccination induced a strong anti-NHBANm response that cross-react with NHBANg. However, the amount of NHBANg present in the gonococcal OMVs based on the lower signal intensity appeared limited. These signal differences were not caused by different variants present in the OMVNg and rNHBANg coating antigens as IgG recognitions of NHBA variants expressed by 1291 and CNG20 are near identical (Supplementary Fig. 1). In addition, the limited amount of NHBA in gonococcal OMVs preparations is supported by Western blots (Fig. 2, lane 3). Weak or no signal was seen at the position of NHBANg in OMVNg samples despite there were 5-fold higher protein loadings of OMVNg preparations relative to the OMVNm samples, which showed NHBANm signals.

We also evaluated the serum IgA responses by ELISA and observed considerable individual variability toward OMV antigens among ten paired sera (Fig. 1B). Increases of post vs pre sera were 1.6–12.5 fold and 0.9–8.9 fold for OMVNm and OMVNg, respectively (Fig. 1B Table). The IgA responses were, as expected, higher against OMVNm than OMVNg (1.7–8.1-fold, Fig. 1B Table). Relative to the IgA responses to OMVNg, the IgA reactivity to rNHBANg was higher for most post-sera (0.7–20.8-fold, Fig. 1B Table), which is in contrast to the consistently high IgG reactivity to rNHBANg. Immunization-induced IgA responses to rNHBANg were robust in over half of the post vaccination sera (#2, 7, 10, 17, 19, and 20), each with greater than a 10-fold increase in post vaccination recognition (10.9–64.8-fold), but were lower for the other sera.

To define the OMV proteins that were reactive with 4CMenB antibodies and confirm the specific recognition of gonococcal NHBA, we performed Western blots using both pre- and post- 4CMenB sera to probe IgG (Fig. 2) and IgA (Fig. 3) responses. As the signal of OMVNg were lower than that of OMVNm in ELISA, a 5-fold higher amount of OMVNg relative to OMVNm was loaded to obtain similar signal intensities between two OMV samples and enable the detection of weak reactive bands in OMVNg without over-exposure of signals from OMVNm. Pre-existing antibodies were detected in most of the pre-sera (Top panels). These antibodies recognized proteins usually <75 kD in OMVNm (lane 2) and/or OMVNg (lane 3), but no signals were detected for rNHBANg in pre-sera (lane 4). IgG signals for OMVNm were detected in pre-sera of #7, #10, and #20 and there were IgA signals in pre-serum #17. For OMVNg, pre-sera of #7, #17, and #20 showed IgG bands, and IgA bands were in pre sera of #2, #9, #17, and #21. For the post sera (Bottom panels), recognition of multiple Nm and Ng OMPs and rNHBANg was observed. The dominant IgG responses appeared to target the three recombinant antigens of the 4CMenB vaccine (GNA2091, FHbp, and NHBA) in OMVNm (lane 2). For OMVNg, the signals of GNA2091, which is localized at the periplasmic side of the outer membrane18 and in the vaccine as the C-terminal protein of the FHbp-GNA2091 fusion, were the highest for all post sera. As expected, there was no signal for FHbp in the OMVNg samples (lane 3). The position of the rNHBANg in Western blots was lower than that of meningococcal NHBA, reflecting a 60-amino acid (aa) deletion in gonococcal NHBA described below. The NHBA_2 variant in the 4CMenB vaccine has ~85% protein sequence similarity to gonococcal NHBA_542 and NHBA_475 variants and the two NHBANg proteins are highly similar (99.3% identity) (Fig. 5A). IgG antibodies in post sera clearly reacted with gonococcal rNHBANg (lane 4), however, no, or very weak reactivity was detected at the corresponding NHBA position in the OMVNg samples (lane 3 vs. lane 4), even though there was a 5-fold higher protein loadings of OMVNg relative to the OMVNm samples with detectable NHBANm bands. This result was consistent with the ELISA data and again suggested that NHBA expression on the gonococcal outer membrane was limited.

In contrast to the consistent IgG recognition of rNHBANg by all ten post 4CMenB sera, variable recognition of rNHBANg by IgA antibodies was observed (Fig. 3), correlating with the ELISA data shown in Fig. 1B. For example, strong rNHBANg signals were seen with post sera #7, #17 and #20, whereas post sera #6 and #15 yielded faint signals. In addition, the IgA signals to GNA2091 and FHbp (in OMVNm) were also highly variable in comparison to the IgG responses. No correlation of IgG and IgA signal intensities was seen between GNA2091 and FHbp in OMVNm. For example, the IgG signal intensity of FHbp in OMVNm was greater than that of GNA2091 for post serum #19, but the reverse was true for the IgA responses of post sera #19.

Western blots of nhba mutants confirmed the presence of NHBA antibodies

To confirm the reactive band near the 75-kD molecular weight (MW) marker was indeed NHBA, an nhba::aphA3 deletion-insertion mutation was created in two meningococcal strains, NZ98/254 and CNM3, and two gonococcal strains, 1291 and CNG20. CNM3 is a representative isolate of the N. meningitidis urethritis clade adapted to urogenital tract. Gonococcal strains 1291 and CNG20 express the two most common gonococcal NHBA protein variants (NHBA_542, 31.8%, and NHBA_475, 28.7%). OMVs prepared from WT and the nhba mutants were probed with post-sera #19 by Western blot (Fig. 4). In Nm, three dominant signals were observed, corresponding to the recombinant antigens present in the vaccine, GNA2091, FHbp and NHBA. As anticipated, the FHbp signals were absent in both gonococcal strains, indicating that the gonococcal homolog, Gfhp, is not present in OMV. The reactive proteins near the 75-kD MW marker in all WT strains were absent in all nhba mutants (Fig. 4), indicating the signal was NHBA. The NHBA bands of both gonococcal strains and CNM3 migrated faster than NHBA of the vaccine strain NZ98/254 due to the N-terminal deletion of ~60 residues (see Fig. 5A).

Analysis of meningococcal and gonococcal NHBA proteins

A search of the PubMLST database (performed 3/5/2024) identified 16,305 Ng records having NHBA (NEIS2109). Among these, 46 NHBA peptide variants (426-429 residues in length) were found in 87.7% of these isolates, while the rest were without an assigned variant number. Fourteen NHBA variants represented 97.7% of Ng isolates, of which nine variants have >2% prevalence and together accounted for 81.1%. NHBA_542 (strain 1291, 31.8%) and NHBA_475 (strain CNG20, 28.7%) are present in > 60% gonococci (Fig. 5B). There were only 1-5 residue differences among the top 10 NHBANg variants. The NHBA_2 in the 4CMenB vaccine strain NZ98/254 was not found in Ng, although there was~80% protein similarity between NHBA_2 and the two major gonococcal NHBAs. Of note, NHBA_20 of the meningococcal urethritis clade (NmUC) strain CNM324 had ~90% similarity to the gonococcal homologs (Fig. 5). NHBA_20 in NmUC and NHBA_17 found in Nm strain 2996, which was examined in previous NHBA related vaccine studies25, shared the deletion of ~60 residues in the gonococcal NHBA variants. Processing of NHBA by bacterial (NalP) and human proteases is centered around the highly conserved Arg-rich motif. Curiously, a second deletion near the arginine rich motif of the gonococcal NHBA variants, but not in Nm, would appear to eliminate the NalP cleavage site (Fig. 5A).

Serum bactericidal activities of post 4CMenB sera against N. gonorrhoeae

Complement-fixing antibodies directed against three recombinant proteins and OMV antigens (both OMPs and LOS) in the vaccine mediate 4CMenB-induced serum bactericidal activity (SBA). SBA has been established as a surrogate for protective immunity against Nm26. While the role of SBA in protection against gonococcal infections is not clear, SBA continues to be a useful assay for evaluating functional antibody-mediated vaccine responses. Thus, we investigated the activity of 4CMenB-induced bactericidal antibodies against two gonococcal strains, expressing the most commonly found NHBA variants, and compared to the Nm vaccine strain NZ98/254. Initially, we tested three individual paired pre- and post-4CMenB sera against Nm strain NZ98/254 (Supplementary Fig. 2). As expected, the post sera showed stronger SBA relative to the pre-sera from the same individuals, indicating vaccine-induced systematic antibody response. Post serum #10 displayed lowest SBA among these three post sera against NZ98/254—22% survival at 10% sera. This correlated with the lowest IgG response to OMVNm as detected by ELISA (Fig. 1A). At the highest concentration tested (10%), <10% survival was observed for post sera #7 and #17 against the vaccine strain NZ98/254. However, the same 10% post sera #7 showed less activity against gonococcal strain 1291 than post sera #17 (~87% vs. 66% survival, respectively). Therefore, additional post sera were examined against Ng strain 1291 at 10% (v/v) concentration. Low bactericidal activities were also observed for post-sera #6, #9, and #15 (84-87% survival). Three post sera, #2, #17, and #19, with higher SBA (~65% survival) are shown in Fig. 6. All three post sera exhibited higher SBA than the corresponding paired pre-sera with 2-5-fold rise in bactericidal activity post 4CMenB immunization. The lower bactericidal activity toward Ng compared to Nm correlated with the overall lower antibody recognition of gonococcal OMV antigens detected by ELISA (Fig. 1).

Serum bactericidal activity of post 4CMenB sera targeting NHBA

The weaker SBA against Ng relative to Nm was not surprising considering that major antigens in the 4CMenB vaccine were either not present (NadA and PorA) or not expressed on the surface of the gonococcus (the gonococcal homolog of FHbp, Ghfp)19. The remaining major recombinant antigen in 4CMenB is NHBA. We evaluated the importance of 4CMenB-induced NHBA-specific antibodies by competitive SBA experiments with post sera #2, #17 and #19, which showed appreciable bactericidal activities against Ng (Fig. 6). Post sera were pre-incubated with increasing amounts of rNHBA proteins to compete out NHBA-specific antibodies and % killing in the absence of rNHBA was set as 100% to evaluate the effects of titrating out NHBA-specific antibodies. Initially, post serum #17 at 10% (v/v) was tested against the Nm vaccine strain NZ98/254 and there was near complete killing with no significant titration with up to 100 ng rNHBA (black line, Supplementary Fig. 3). Thus, for Nm strain NZ98/254 the serum concentration was lowered to 5% to enhance the sensitivity of rNHBA titration, whereas 10% sera was kept for Ng strains. Both the meningococcal NHBA_2 and gonococcal NHBA_542 proteins were first assayed and all titrations were saturated at 50 ng rNHBA under the experimental conditions (Supplementary Fig. 3). There was no difference in the titration of meningococcal NHBA_2 or gonococcal NHBA_542 against Ng strain 1291 or Nm strain NZ98/254 (Supplementary Fig. 3), implying common epitope(s) shared between NHBA variants sequestered NHBA-specific antibodies. Subsequently, all three post-sera, #2, #17, and #19, were examined by competitive SBA with Nm strain NZ98/254 and Ng strain 1291 using the corresponding rNHBA variant expressed by the strain (Fig. 7A). For the Nm strain NZ98/254, only ~10-20% of bactericidal activities of post-sera #2 and #17 were titrated away, while there was no reduction seen with post-sera #19 up to 50 ng rNHBA. Thus, the removal of anti-NHBA antibodies had limited effect on killing of Nm strain NZ98/254 because bactericidal antibodies targeted other antigens, such as FHbp, were present in the vaccine strain, likely at a higher level. However, while post-sera, #2, #17, and #19, only yielded ~30–40% killing against Ng strain 1291 at 10% serum concentration (Fig. 6), ~80% of this killing was titrated away by the presence of rNHBA (Fig. 7A). To independently support this observation, we tested one additional gonococcal strain CNG20 with competitive SBA assays. As shown in Fig. 7B, the titration profiles of all three post-sera against CNG20 were similar to each other and analogous to those for Ng strain 1291. Thus, NHBA-specific antibodies contributed to a majority of bactericidal activities against gonococci.

Removing the antigen targeted by the key bactericidal antibodies was expected to impact strain susceptibility to SBA. Thus, Δnhba mutants in both the Ng 1291 and Nm NZ98/254 backgrounds were evaluated for the contribution of NHBA-specific antibodies to overall SBA. No significant difference in serum sensitivity was observed between WT and the Δnhba mutant of the NZ98/254 strain (Fig. 8A, red lines), whereas greater than 90% of the Ng 1291Δnhba mutant survived in the presence of 10% post-serum #17 as compared to 70% survival of the WT 1291. This effect was lost at lower serum concentrations. These data were consistent with the competitive SBA assays (Fig. 7), in which rNHBA titrated out most bactericidal activity against gonococci. The enhanced serum resistance of the nhba mutant was further confirmed with two other post-sera, #2 and #19, also tested at 10% (Fig. 8B). Finally, we have also compared another Ng strain CNG20 and the CNG20Δnhba mutant with 10% post-4CMenB serum #2. The CNG20Δnhba mutant indeed survived better relative to the parental WT CNG20 (75.3 ± 5.1% and 53.6 ± 6.3% survival for mutant and WT, respectively, p < 0.01). Thus, the enhanced serum resistance to post-4CMenB sera upon an Δnhba mutation was independently reproduced in two gonococcal strains.

Evidence of synergy in bactericidal activities between anti-NHBA and anti-LOS antibodies against N. gonorrhoeae

We recently found that removing anti-LOS antibodies in post 4CMenB sera significantly reduced the SBA against Ng strain 129120. As shown in Fig. 7, titration with rNHBA also removed a significant portion of SBA against strains 1291 and CNG20. These two sets of data suggested that anti-NHBA and anti-LOS antibodies may act cooperatively in the SBA assay27. To address, competitive SBA assays [again using 10% (v/v) post 4CBMenB sera] were conducted with strain 1291 in the absence or with pre-incubation of the two antigens individually or in combination. Lower amounts of competing antigens (e.g., 1 ng rNHBANg alone, 1 ng purified L3 LOS alone, or 1 ng rNHBANg + 1 ng L3 LOS) were used to assess synergy. The percentage killing of the control reaction with 10% post-sera #2 was set as 100% to define the titration effect of pre-incubation with exogenous antigens. As shown in Fig. 9, pre-incubation of sera with 1 ng of either rNHBANg alone or L3 LOS alone reduced ~30% of the bactericidal activity of the serum. However, absorption with both antigens titrated away ~90% of the SBA.

Evidence of synergy of anti-NHBA and anti-LOS antibodies in post 4CMenB sera in serum bactericidal activity (SBA) against N. gonorrhoeae.

The competitive SBA assays were conducted with strain 1291 in the presence of 10% IgG & IgM depleted human sera (complement source) and 10% post-sera #2 (heat-inactivated) with a 30-min pre-incubation of the following antigens: (1) PBMI control, (2) 1 ng rNHBA1291 alone, (3) 1 ng purified L3 LOS alone, and (4) 1 ng rNHBA1291 + 1 ng L3 LOS. The reaction with complement only was used for normalization to calculate % killing under each condition. The % killing of the PBMI control (10% post sera only; 38.6 ± 0.2) was set as 100% to determine the titration effect of pre-incubation with exogenous antigens. Data are the mean and standard deviations with individual data points of biologically independent experiments shown as black dots. One-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple comparison test for pairwise comparisons across each of the variables was applied for statistical analysis (****, p < 0.0001).

Discussion

The outer membrane, containing OMPs and LOS, is key to human immunological recognition of Nm and Ng. Considerable work has led to successful OMV vaccines for Nm, including 4CMenB (BexseroR). Mounting evidence from multiple retrospective and ecological studies in several countries suggests that meningococcal OMV vaccines have effectiveness at preventing mucosal gonococcal infections and their complications7,8,10–13. Multiple studies have defined differences and similarities between the Nm and Ng outer membranes16,21. While the precise mechanism(s) of cross-protection conferred by meningococcal OMV vaccines against Ng is not clear, OMPs sharing high degrees of sequence similarity with corresponding homologs in Ng16,21 likely contribute to cross-protection. Structural epitopes of Nm LOS in OMVs are cross-reactive with Ng LOS and similarly may contribute to the protection against gonococcal infections20. Leduc et al. 22 showed that antibodies from mice and human vaccinated with 4CMenB differentially recognized several Ng surface proteins, including NHBA, PilQ, BamA, MtrE, PorB, and Opa. For example, immune sera from both mice and humans recognized Ng NHBA and PilQ, but MtrE was only recognized by mouse serum22.

NHBANm in the 4CMenB vaccine induced bactericidal activities against heterologous Nm and Ng strains, suggesting the presence of multiple shared epitopes. NHBA is divided into two domains by a highly conserved Arg-rich motif that is involved in binding to heparin15,28. The N-terminal region has considerable sequence variation and is predicted as an intrinsically unfolded structure, while the highly conserved C-terminal domain folds into an anti-parallel β-barrel29,30. The conserved C-terminal β-barrel potentially supports the cross-reactivity across Nm and Ng30. However, previous studies have indicated that cleavage and release of the C-terminal domain by a human lactoferrin treatment does not influence susceptibility to bacterial killing15, thus many of the bactericidal epitopes are presumably located in the N-terminal region of NHBA.

NHBA influences serum resistance and the contributions of anti-NHBA antibodies to SBA against Nm using human complement varies depending on antibody specificity in each individual sera and the test strain. Against a Nm strain matched for both NHBA and FHbp, as in the case of strain NZ98/254, nearly all the SBA in post 4CMenB sera was dependent on anti-FHbp antibodies31. In contrast, against three Nm strains matched only for NHBA, depletion of NHBA antibodies decreased SBA titers by 43–79%. Our data show that while the titration of anti-NHBA antibodies from post 4CMenB sera diminished a small fraction of the SBA against strain NZ98/254, competing away anti-NHBA antibody with rNHBA reduced a significant portion of the SBA against gonococcal strains 1291 and CNG20 (although SBA is considerably lower toward Ng). Considering that N. gonorrhoeae lacks other key 4CMenB antigens, the data that anti-NHBA antibodies can contribute to SBAs against Ng is analogous to the scenario of Nm strains matched only for NHBA. While antibodies targeting recombinant antigen GNA2091, which is an OMP facing periplasm, are abundant in post 4CMenB sera (Fig. 2), these antibodies do not appear to have a noticeable contribution to SBA against Ng since complete depletion of anti-NHBA antibodies removed the majority of SBA targeting Ng.

A recent study has reported that OpcA and PorB are important bactericidal antigens of the 4CMenB OMVs that provide broad protection against diverse Nm strains32. SBA can be mediated not only by antibodies directed against a single antigen but also by the cooperativity and synergy of antibodies recognizing varied epitopes within a single antigen or multiple antigens present in the outer membrane, which may or may not be bactericidal on their own. In the development of 4CMenB, antibodies against OMV components alone or in combination with antibodies targeting the major vaccine antigens act cooperatively in inducing meningococcal lysis and expanding Nm strain coverage (reviewed in ref. 26). For example, Vu et al.32 showed cooperative serum bactericidal activity between human antibodies to meningococcal FHbp and NHBA and that non-bactericidal antibodies against sparsely exposed individual antigens can cooperate and elicit SBA. Further, Natali et al.33 also showed mAbs to NHBA and FHbp can act synergistically against Nm to activate complement even when the single antibody is not bactericidal. Synergy is likely due to a higher avidity and stable binding of two or more C1q globular heads initiating the downstream complement cascade resulting in bacteriolysis and is affected by antigen density26.

Our ELISA data using rNHBA as capturing antigens demonstrated that there are abundant anti-NHBA antibodies in post 4CMenB sera due to the NHBA-GNA1030 fusion protein formulated in the vaccine. However, the amount of NHBA present in Ng OMVs was limited and does not capture all anti-NHBA antibodies in ELISA. NHBA has been shown to be a minor component in Ng OMVs, representing less than 0.02% of total detergent-extracted OMV proteins33. While its surface level during infection is not known, NHBA expression is temperature regulated. NHBA is maximally expressed at ~30 °C34, the temperature on the surface of the human anterior nares, and expression is increased at 34 °C, the temperature in the posterior nasopharynx and tonsillar region35. However, the temperature at the urinary tract and vagina is reported to be 36.9 °C or above36, thus, NHBA probably is not temperature-enhanced in the urogenital tract. Thus, the potentially limited NHBANg on gonococcal cell surface alone is likely insufficient to support a high portion of SBA against Ng but as shown by the competition SBA experiments and the post-4CMenB serum resistant phenotype of the Δnhba mutants, NHBA antibodies substantially contribute to the vaccine-induced bactericidal response toward Ng. Anti-NHBA antibodies together with antibodies targeting other OMPs or LOS epitopes conserved between Ng and Nm could cooperatively augment the overall bactericidal activity against Ng. Finally, anti-NHBA antibodies could mediate bacterial killing via both SBA and opsonophagocytic (OP) activity25,37 or by anti-adhesion effects38. Anti-NHBA antibodies that are not bactericidal in SBA still confer passive protection from bacteremia in an infant rat model39.

The density of a target antigen on the bacterial surface is fundamental for C3 deposition40. The nature and density of the antigen-bound antibodies is the most important requirement for efficient complement killing and influence synergistic complement activation by different antigen combinations27,41. Previously, we found LOS-specific antibodies in 4CMenB sera contribute to the SBA against Ng20. The density of LOS epitopes—~50% of the outer leaflet of the outer membrane- would fulfill this role and anti-LOS and anti-NHBA antibodies appear to function in concert to carry out much of the gonococcal serum bactericidal activity. The synergy between NHBA and LOS antibodies was demonstrated by competitive SBA assays, in which combining both antigens together competed away more SBA than the sum of individual antigens (Fig. 9). The cooperativity between anti-NHBA and anti-LOS antibodies is consistent with our earlier data showing that titrating out anti-LOS antibodies with purified LOS matched for the immunotype of the target strain also significantly reduced SBA against Ng20.

Of note, near complete resistance of the Ng 1291Δnhba and CNG20Δnhba mutants was seen with post-4CMenB immune sera. Heparin-mediated recruitment of complement regulatory proteins, such as factor H, C4-binding protein, C1 inhibitor, etc., via binding to NHBA15,38 has been proposed to confer serum resistance in Nm. Serruto et al.15 found no difference in sensitivity to normal human serum (NHS) between a meningococcal WT and the nhba mutant in the absence of heparin, but the addition of heparin significantly enhanced the survival of WT Nm but not the mutant. Semchenko et al.38 reported a gonococcal 1291 nhba insertional mutant had ~60% decreased survival relative to the WT in the absence of heparin. Interestingly, survival of this mutant was increased by adding heparin, thus heparin rescue was independent of NHBANg. We have not observed high levels of killing of the 1291Δnhba mutant by commercial pooled NHS or pooled pre-sera of the vaccinees, but the different antibody populations likely contribute to the distinct phenotypes of gonococcal Δnhba mutants seen with pre-immune/NHS vs. post-4CMenB immune sera. One aspect of the human immune response to 4CMenB and Ng that has not been evaluated is the role of blocking antibodies. The meningococcal class 4 OMP has ~94% homology with gonococcal reduction modifiable protein (Rmp or protein III). Antibodies directed against gonococcal Rmp block complement-mediated killing of Ng42,43. Additional studies are needed to explain the gonococcal nhba mutant phenotypes and 4CMenB immune sera.

In summary, 4CMenB immunization elicits different IgG and IgA responses to Nm and Ng OMVs and NHBA among vaccinated individuals. The generated antibodies primarily target the three abundant recombinant proteins formulated in the vaccine but also other OMV components. In particular, anti-NHBANm antibodies in post 4CMenB sera cross react robustly with NHBANg and recognize the two major gonococcal NHBA variants found in > 60% of the gonococcal population. Importantly, NHBANg can be a target of bactericidal antibodies. Anti-NHBA antibodies, while usually responsible for a fraction of serum bactericidal activity against Nm, catalyzed the major serum bactericidal activity toward Ng strains 1291 and CNG20. As anticipated, serum bactericidal activity of post 4CMenB vaccine sera against the gonococcal strains was lower than against the Nm vaccine strain. In these studies, we focused on a 1/10 serum dilution- (10% concentration) and 10% complement for most experiments anticipating this level of bactericidal activity if present could contribute to Ng protection at mucosal surfaces. The traditional measurements of “protective” serum titer/dilutions (defined as >50% killing in serum titers of 1/4 or greater) used as a correlate of protection against systemic meningococcal disease26 may not be very meaningful for Ng as a mucosal pathogen where antibody and complement are limited. Bactericidal IgG antibodies to homologous OMPs and shared LOS epitopes on Ng OMVs20 also appear to contribute to the effectiveness of meningococcal OMV vaccines against gonococcal infections. The limited NHBA target on the cell surface suggests anti-NHBA antibodies act synergistically with antibodies targeting anti-LOS antibodies and potentially other OMV antigens. Characterization of critical inter-antigenic antibody partner(s) creating synergistic interactions to boost biological activity may help to increase the effectiveness of OMV vaccines against gonococcal infections.

Methods

Human sera

Ten anonymized-residual, paired (pre- and post- vaccine) human sera used in this study were obtained from a vaccine clinical trial of 4CMenB in laboratory workers in the Public Health Laboratory, Manchester, United Kingdom23; www.clinicaltrials.gov identifier: NCT00962624. Enrollment was open to staff aged 18–65 years. 4CMenB was administered at 0, 2, and 6 months in the non-dominant arm and ACWY-CRM concomitantly at 0 months in the dominant arm. Samples used in this study were obtained at 0 months (pre-sera) and 1 month after the third dose (post-sera).

Bacterial isolates and growth conditions

Bacterial strains used in this study are listed in Supplementary Table 1. CNM3 is a representative isolate of the N. meningitidis urethritis clade adapted to urogenital tract and causing urethritis. Gonococcal stains 1291 and CNG20 express the two most common NHBA protein variants and are from patients with symptomatic gonococcal urethritis. Strain 1291 is a well-characterized Ng laboratory strain44, while CNG20 is a relatively recent clinical isolate45. Neisseria were cultured on GC base agar containing 0.4% glucose and 0.68 mM Fe(NO3)3 at 37 °C and 5% CO2 or in GC broth with the same supplements and 0.043% NaHCO3 as the CO2 source at 37 °C.

Mutation in nhba

The Δnhba::aphA3 construct were generated by overlapping PCR. A 5’ 757-bp fragment (5n) of nhba was obtained by PCR using primers nhba-5F2 and nhba-5RA3 (Supplementary Table 1), and the 3’ 466-bp sequence (3a) was amplified with nhba-3FA3 and nhba-3R using genomic DNAs of strains to be mutated. The ahpA3 cassette fragment (A3) was obtained using aphA3-SmF and aphA3-SmR. Mixtures of 5n and A3 fragments were used as the template for the 1st round of overlapping PCR with primers nhba-5F2 and A3-SmR. The resulting ~1.5 kb fragment was then mixed with 3a and the mixtures used for the second PCR with primers nhba-5F2 and nhba-3R to obtain the final ~2-kb construct, in which a 505-bp nhba coding sequence had been deleted. The PCR products were used to transform Nm strains NZ98/254 and CNM3 as well as Ng strains 1291 and CNG20. Transformants were selected on BHI agar plates with 80 μg/mL kanamycin. The colonies were screen by PCR with a 5’ primer nhba-5F1, which is upstream of the cloned fragment, and a 3’ primer, nhba-3R. The PCR products were 2 kb and 2.3 kb for WT and mutant, respectively.

Outer membrane vesicle (OMV) preparation

Secreted OMVs of N. meningitidis and N. gonorrhoeae are isolated from overnight supplemented GC broth cultures. Cultures were treated with 0.05% sodium azide at 60 °C for 30 min to kill bacteria and then bacteria were removed by centrifugation at 5000 × g for 15 min. The supernatant was cleared by PES filter (0.45 μm), concentrated with Centricon Plus 70 filtration units with a 100 kDa cut-off (Thermo Scientific) at 3500 × g and then OMV collected by ultracentrifugation (100,000 × g, 80 min, 4 °C). The resulting pellets containing OMVs were washed three times with PBS and then solubilized in PBS containing 0.2% SDS. The protein concentration was measured using a BCA protein assay.

Recombinant NHBA protein

The coding sequences of gonococcal strains 1291 (variant 542) and CNG20 (variant 475) without the signal peptide were codon optimized, synthesized and cloned into pET28b(+) using NcoI and XhoI restriction sites, resulting in a (His)10 tagged protein followed by a TEV protease cleavage site at the N-terminus (GenScript). The constructs were transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3). Expression and purification of recombinant proteins were performed by the Bioexpression and Fermentation Facility at the University of Georgia. Protein expression was induced by 1 mM IPTG at 25 °C and soluble proteins were purified with Nickel-NTA chromatography. After cleavage of the His-tag by TEV protease, the mature NHBA proteins were again chromatographed to remove the tag and protease.

ELISA

Maxisorp 96-well plates were coated with 200 ng of OMVs or 10 ng of NHBA proteins diluted with PBS, Incubate overnight at 4 °C. After one wash of the coated plates with TBST wash buffer (1x TBS + 0.05% Tween 20), the plates were blocked with ChonBlock buffer (Chondrex, Inc) for 1 hr at RT. Tested sera were diluted in ChonBlock buffer and aliquots of 50 μL per well were added and the plates were incubated at 37 °C for 1 hr. After washing 3 times, the secondary goat anti Human IgG-HRP antibody at 1:20,000 dilution or anti-human IgA-HRP at 1:15,000 dilution was added and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Aliquots of KPL TMB peroxidase substrate (75 μL) were added after another 3 washes and the plate incubated at RT. The readings of OD450 were recorded after color development stopped by addition of 50 μL of sulfuric acid stop solution.

Western blot

The meningococcal OMVNZ98/254 (2 μg), gonococcal OMVCNG20 (10 μg), and purified recombinant NHBA1291 (500 ng) were probed in Western blots with the human sera obtained pre- and post-immunization with 4CMenB. Samples were resolved by 4-20% TGX gradient gel (Bio-Rad) and then transferred to nitrocellulose membrane for 2 h at 100 V by tank transfer method (Tris-Glycine buffer with 20% methanol). The membrane was blocked in a solution of 5% non-fat dry milk (NFDM) and 0.1% tween20 in 1x TBS for 1 hr at room temperature (RT) and then probed with primary post 4CMenB serum (1:5,000) in 2.5% NFDM-0.1% Tween-1x TBS overnight at 4 °C. After 3 5-min washes with 1x TBST + 0.1% Tween 20, the membrane was incubated with anti-human IgG-HRP or anti-human IgA-HRP at 1:15,000 dilution in 2.5% NFDM-0.1% Tween-1xTBS for 1 h at RT, followed by 3 additional 10-min washes. The signals were developed with West Pico Dura (Pierce) and recorded using an I Bright 20 scanner.

Serum bactericidal assays with human complement

SBA protocol was as described46. Commercial IgG- and IgM-depleted normal human serum pool (Pel-Freez, Rogers, AR) is used as the complement source at 10% and assayed with test 4CMenB sera, in which the complement has been heat-inactivated at 56 °C for 30 min. Strains grown in supplemented GC broth to mid-log Phase of OD550 at ~0.4–0.7 were adjusted to A550 to 0.12 for NZ98/254 and 0.15 for 1291 with clear RPMI Media and further diluted 1:2500 in RPMI. Alternatively, colonies from an overnight chocolate agar plate were re-streaked onto a fresh chocolate agar plate and growth after a 3-h incubation was collected and resuspended in RPMI. A 20-μL aliquot of bacterial suspension was added into a mixture (60 μL) of pooled complement source (8 μL) and diluted test sera in 96-well plate to start the assay. After 1-hr incubation at 37 °C, 40-μL aliquots were diluted with 160 μL of RPMI and 25 μL of the dilutions were spotted onto GC agar plates. Viable counts were obtained after overnight incubation. For each reaction, 3-7 replicates determined the mean value of viable cfu count. When needed, rNHBA protein and/or purified L3 LOS preparation20 was added into the 60-μL reaction mixture and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min prior to the addition of bacteria. The wells with 10% Pel-Freez sera only, which has no bactericidal activity up to 40%, was set as 100% survival for normalization.

Serum survival assay

Resistance of N. gonorrhoeae to serum-mediated killing was tested by incubating ∼104 cfu in RPMI with 10% of commercial gender-pooled normal human serum (BIOIVT) or pooled pre-immune sera at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Colonies from overnight chocolate blood agar plates were re-streaked onto fresh chocolate agar plates and growth after 3 h were collected and adjusted in RPMI media to OD550 of 0.15 followed by a further 5000-fold dilution. Aliquots of diluted cells were added into 96-well plates containing appropriately diluted serum in RPMI for a final volume of 100 μL to initiate the incubation and the inoculum at T = 0 were determined. After 60-min incubation at 37 °C, the viable cfu counts were determined as described above. The percentage of survival was calculated by normalizing to the cfu counts of heat-inactivated sera after 1 h incubation.

Statistical analysis

Two-tailed unequal variance Welch t tests to address unequal sample sizes were performed to compare serum bactericidal activities between pre- and post-sera and between WT and mutant47. The One-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple comparison test that makes pairwise comparisons across each of the treatments was applied to the synergistic interaction data in Fig. 9. Line graphs were analyzed with two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s HSD test. The p values less than 0.05 (*), 0.01 (**), 0.001 (***), and 0.0001 (****) were considered statistically significant.

Plain language summary of the KRYSTAL-12 study results: adagrasib for non−small cell lung cancer with KRASG12C mutations
Authors: Fabrice Barlesi, Wenxiu Yao, Michaël Duruisseaux, Ludovic Doucet, Aitor Azkárate Martínez, Vanesa Gregorc, Oscar Juan-Vidal, Shun Lu, Charlotte De Bondt, Filippo de Marinis, Helena Linardou, Young-Chul Kim, Robert Jotte, Enriqueta Felip, Giuseppe Lo Russo, Martin Reck, Mary F MichenzIe, Wenjing Yang, Julie N Meade, Beata Korytowsky, Tony S K Mok, for the KRYSTAL-12 Investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Plain Language Summary What is this summary about? In this article, we summarize results from the phase 3 KRYSTAL-12 clinical study that were published online in The Lancet July 2025. This study included patients who had non−small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that had spread beyond their lungs (locally advanced or metastatic) with KRASG12C mutations. The KRASG12C mutation affects the KRAS gene that normally helps control cell growth. This mutation causes cells to grow and divide uncontrollably.The goal of this study was to find out if adagrasib, a medicine taken by mouth that is designed to block the effects of the KRASG12C mutation, helped patients with KRASG12C-mutated NSCLC previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy, which are recommended as the first-line of treatment for patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC. What happened in the study? Adults who had previously been treated for advanced NSCLC that had spread outside the lungs and whose tumors had KRASG12C mutations were included in this study. During the study, 301 people were randomly assigned to receive adagrasib and 152 were randomly assigned to receive docetaxel, a type of chemotherapy they had not received before and is the standard of care for patients with advanced NSCLC previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy.The researchers assessed whether people who received adagrasib lived longer without the cancer becoming worse (compared with people who received docetaxel), and whether taking adagrasib was safe. What are the key takeaways? Researchers found that people taking adagrasib lived longer before their cancer got worse compared with people taking docetaxel. On average, half of the people taking adagrasib lived 5.5 months without their cancer worsening, while those taking docetaxel lived 3.8 months without their cancer getting worse. These benefits of adagrasib were seen across people with different characteristics. More people taking adagrasib also showed decreases in the size of their tumors, including tumors in the brain, than those taking docetaxel.People taking adagrasib and docetaxel had similar rates of side effects, although the types of side effects were different. Most side effects with adagrasib were considered mild to moderate, but approximately half of the people needed dose changes or delays because of side effects. What were the main conclusions reported by the researchers? Results from this study support the benefits of using adagrasib over docetaxel in people with advanced or metastatic NSCLC with KRASG12C mutations, after platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy.Clinical trial number: NCT04685135KEYWORDS: Non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), KRASG12C mutations, Adagrasib, KRYSTAL-12 trial, Targeted therapy

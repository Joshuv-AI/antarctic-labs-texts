Presence of Aspartate Aminotransferase in Peri-Implant Crevicular Fluid with and without Mucositis
Authors: Priyanka Devadiya, Yashika Goyal, Divya Divya, Jyoti Wasti, Dipooja Patil, Anamika Sinha, Mahesh Ghadage
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) is an enzyme commonly found in various body fluids and tissues, including peri-implant crevicular fluid (PICF). Its presence and activity in PICF have been implicated in inflammatory conditions such as mucositis around dental implants.Materials and Methods:This study aimed to compare the levels of AST in PICF collected from sites with mucositis (group A) and healthy sites (group B). PICF samples were collected using standardized paper strips and analyzed using enzymatic assays. The AST levels were quantified using a spectrophotometric method, and the data were statistically analyzed using Student’s t-test.Results:The mean AST level in group A (mucositis) was found to be 12.3 ± 2.1 U/mL, whereas in group B (healthy sites), it was significantly lower at 5.7 ± 1.3 U/mL (P < 0.05). These findings indicate a significant elevation of AST in PICF associated with mucositis compared to healthy peri-implant sites.Conclusion:Elevated levels of AST in PICF suggest its potential as a biomarker for detecting inflammatory conditions such as mucositis around dental implants. Monitoring AST levels in PICF could aid in the early detection and management of peri-implant diseases, contributing to improved clinical outcomes and implant longevity.

INTRODUCTION

Peri-implant diseases, encompassing peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis, are prevalent inflammatory conditions affecting soft and hard tissues surrounding dental implants.[1] Aspartate aminotransferase (AST), an enzyme involved in amino acid metabolism, has been identified in various biological fluids and tissues, including peri-implant crevicular fluid (PICF).[2] The presence and activity of AST in PICF have been implicated in inflammatory responses and tissue damage, making it a potential biomarker for assessing peri-implant health status.[3]

Peri-implant mucositis, characterized by reversible inflammation of the soft tissues surrounding implants without bone loss, is considered a precursor to peri-implantitis.[4] Early detection of mucositis is crucial for implementing timely interventions to prevent progression to peri-implantitis, which can lead to irreversible bone loss and implant failure.[5] Biomarkers such as AST in PICF hold promise for aiding in the early diagnosis and monitoring of peri-implant mucositis, thereby enhancing clinical management strategies and improving the long-term outcomes of dental implant therapy.

This study aims to investigate the presence and quantitative levels of AST in PICF collected from sites exhibiting mucositis compared to healthy peri-implant sites. Understanding the role of AST in peri-implant diseases could potentially contribute to the development of diagnostic tools and therapeutic approaches aimed at preserving peri-implant tissue health.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study employed a cross-sectional design to compare the levels of AST in PICF collected from two distinct groups: sites with peri-implant mucositis (group A) and healthy peri-implant sites (group B).

Sample selection

Group A (mucositis): PICF samples were collected from sites diagnosed with peri-implant mucositis based on clinical and radiographic criteria.[1] Inclusion criteria included visible signs of inflammation (redness, swelling) and bleeding on probing (BoP) without radiographic evidence of bone loss.Group B (healthy sites): PICF samples were collected from peri-implant sites with no clinical signs of inflammation or BoP and the absence of radiographic bone loss.[1]

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study indicate that AST levels in PICF are significantly elevated in sites with peri-implant mucositis compared to healthy peri-implant sites. This supports the hypothesis that AST could serve as a potential biomarker for detecting inflammatory conditions around dental implants.[1]

Peri-implant mucositis is characterized by reversible inflammation of the soft tissues surrounding implants, often manifesting as BoP and increased probing depths without radiographic evidence of bone loss.[2] Early detection and intervention at this stage are critical to preventing progression to peri-implantitis, a more severe condition associated with irreversible bone loss.[3]

The observed correlation between AST levels and clinical parameters such as probing depth and BoP in sites with mucositis suggests that AST may reflect the inflammatory activity and tissue damage associated with peri-implant diseases.[4] This aligns with previous research indicating that enzymes, including AST, released during tissue breakdown may accumulate in PICF during inflammation.[5]

The clinical implications of our findings are significant. Monitoring AST levels in PICF could potentially facilitate early diagnosis and monitoring of peri-implant mucositis, enabling timely intervention and improved management strategies to preserve peri-implant tissue health. This could ultimately contribute to enhancing the long-term success and survival rates of dental implants in clinical practice.[6]

Despite these promising findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. The cross-sectional design of this study limits our ability to establish causality between elevated AST levels and peri-implant mucositis. Longitudinal studies are needed to assess the predictive value of AST as a biomarker for disease progression. Additionally, the study sample size was relatively small, and future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate our findings across diverse patient populations and implant types.[7]

CONCLUSION

This study provides preliminary evidence suggesting that AST in PICF may serve as a useful biomarker for detecting and monitoring peri-implant mucositis. Further research is needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms and clinical utility of AST in peri-implant diseases, paving the way for its integration into routine clinical practice as a diagnostic tool.

On Higher Du Bois Singularities and K K -Regularity
Wanchun Shen

Abstract
. We study the relationship between higher Du Bois singularities and K K -regularity, a notion that measures the 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariance of the algebraic K K -groups. Building on this relationship, we establish a strengthened form of Vorst’s conjecture for local complete intersections in characteristic zero. Our work also provides tools to construct new examples that illustrate various phenomena in the study of K K -regularity. The main inputs for our results are vanishing theorems for the Du Bois complexes.

1.
Introduction

Understanding the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance of
K
-groups is a central problem in algebraic
K
-theory. A natural way to quantify the failure of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance is by studying the cokernels of the maps

\varphi_{m,r}:K_{m}(X)\to K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{r})

for all integers
m
and
r\geq 0
. A scheme
X
is said to be
K_{m}
-regular
if the maps
\varphi_{m,r}
are isomorphisms for all
r\geq 0
. It is a theorem of Quillen that regular schemes are
K_{m}
-regular for all
m
. In general, the extent to which
K_{m}(X)
fails to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant provides a measure of the singularities of
X
, and this is captured in the notion of
K
-regularity.

This paper is devoted to applications of Hodge-theoretic methods to the study of
K
-regularity. As an illustration of the kind of results obtained by these methods, we highlight a few consequences, the first one being a numerical characterization of
K_{m}
-regularity in terms of the
minimal exponent

\tilde{\alpha}_{X}
introduced by Saito
[
Sa2
]
. See e.g.
[
P
]
*Section 3.7 for a nice introduction to this numerical invariant that refines the log canonical threshold of
X
:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a complex hypersurface of dimension
d
with isolated singularities. Then

X\text{ is $K_{m}$-regular}\iff\tilde{\alpha}_{X}\geq\lceil\frac{m+d}{2}\rceil.

The following example illustrates the use of Theorem
A
to detect
K
-regularity:

Example
.

Consider the hypersurface
X=(x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{7}=0)\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\mathbb{C}}
. By
[
Sa4
]
*4.1.5, the minimal exponent of
X
is given by
\tilde{\alpha}_{X}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{7}=\frac{41}{42}
. Thus, Theorem
A
says that

X
is
K_{m}
-regular

\displaystyle\iff\frac{41}{42}\geq\lceil\frac{m+2}{2}\rceil

\displaystyle\iff m\leq-2

Thus
X
is
K_{-2}
-regular, but not
K_{m}
-regular for
m\geq-1
. This recovers
[
W1
]
*Example 6.1.

Moreover, we have the following regularity criterion:

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be an affine local complete intersection. If
X
is
K_{2}
-regular, then it is regular.

Interest in regularity criteria of this kind goes back to the work of Vorst
[
Vor
]
, who conjectured that for an affine, finite type
F
-scheme of dimension
d
, regularity and
K_{d+1}
-regularity are equivalent. This conjecture was confirmed in
[
CHW
]
(as usual, we assume the base field has characteristic zero; over perfect fields of characteristic
p
, Vorst’s conjecture was confirmed in
[
KST
]
). In
[
HW
]
, two extensions of Vorst’s conjecture were established. First, it was shown that
K_{2}
-regular affine varieties are normal. Second, it was shown that for local complete intersections of minimal embedding codimension
r
(see Definition
5.3
), regularity is implied by
K_{m}
-regularity for
m>\frac{d-r}{2}+1
. Our theorem may be viewed as a further strengthening of Vorst’s conjecture along these lines.

The proof of Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, along with other applications discussed later in the paper, combines fundamental results of Cortiñas, Haesemeyer, Schlichting, Walker and Weibel
[
CHSW
,
CHW
,
CHWW1
]
—now standard in the study of
K
-regularity—with a new ingredient: the theory of
higher Du Bois singularities
.

Recently, developments in Hodge theory have led to considerable interest in the study of higher analogs of the classical notion of Du Bois singularities for complex algebraic varieties. Following
[
JKSY
,
MOPW
,
MP2
]
, a local complete intersection
X
is said to have
m
-Du Bois singularities
if the natural maps

\Omega_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p},

from the sheaf of algebraic differential forms to the Du Bois complexes (which are natural objects in the bounded derived category
D^{b}_{\rm coh}(X)
of coherent sheaves on
X
, arising from Deligne’s mixed Hodge theory) are isomorphisms for all
p\leq m
.

For varieties that are not local complete intersections, there is currently no known example satisfying
\Omega_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{1}\simeq\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{1}
. For this reason, one works with a less restrictive notion for general complex algebraic varieties. Following
[
SVV2
]
, we say
X
has
pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities
if

\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p}=0\text{ for all $i>0$ and $p\leq m$},

where we write
\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathcal{F}
for the
i
-th cohomology sheaf of an object
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{\rm coh}(X)
. Several other conditions are imposed in
loc. cit.
for the definition of
m
-Du Bois singularities in general. See Section
2.3
for the natural extensions of these notions over more general base fields.

The following theorem relates higher Du Bois singularities to
K
-regularity. A related result can be found in
[
HW
]
, where it is shown that
K_{m}
-regularity implies the maps
\Omega_{X}^{p}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
are isomorphisms for
p\leq m-1
.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
d
, and denote
s=\dim X_{\sing}
. Then

(1)

If
X
has Du Bois singularities, then it is
K_{-d+1}
-regular;

(2)

If
X
is pre-
m
-Du Bois, then it is
K_{-t}
-regular for
t\geq\max\{s+1,d-2m-2+s\}
; if
X
is in addition affine, then the bound can be improved to
t\geq\max\{1,d-2m-2\}
.

(3)

If
X
is affine, and either of the following holds:

•

X
is a local complete intersection;

•

\depth\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\geq d-p
for all
p\geq 0
.

If
X
is
K_{-d+2m+1+s}
-regular, then it is pre-
m
-Du Bois.

This theorem, combined with results from the theory of higher Du Bois singularities, most notably the characterization via the minimal exponent
[

,

]
and the codimension bound on the singular locus (Theorem

), serves as the main technical input in the proofs of Theorems

and

-regular varieties, both in the local complete intersection setting and beyond; see Example

.

We conclude with two further applications of Hodge-theoretic methods to
K
-regularity. First, following
[
CHWW1
,
CHWW2
]
, we use results on Du Bois invariants (Proposition

) to obtain new examples in which Bass’ question about whether
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance of the algebraic

\mathbb{A}^{2}
-invariance has either a positive or a negative answer; see Section
6
.

Second, we use the degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence to characterize
K
-regularity for projective varieties:

Theorem D
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety. Then
X
is
K_{m}
-regular if and only if the natural maps

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\mathbb{L}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p})

are isomorphisms for all

. Here, we denote by
\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,-)
the hypercohomology functor on

, and by

the

This leads to examples showing that local

Preliminaries

2.1.
The cdh-differentials and the Du Bois complexes

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme over a field
F
. The
cdh differentials
of
X
are defined as the sheafification of the Kähler differentials
\Omega_{X/F}^{p}
in the cdh-topology introduced by Suslin and Voevodsky
[
SV
]
. More precisely,

\Omega_{cdh,X/F}^{p}:={\bf R}a_{*}a^{*}\Omega_{X/F}^{p},

where
a:X_{cdh}\to X_{zar}
is the natural change-of-topology morphism from the cdh site to the Zariski site.

On the other hand, for a complex algebraic variety (i.e. when
F=\mathbb{C}
), Du Bois
[
DB
]
introduced the notion of
Du Bois complexes
(or
filtered de Rham complexes
) following ideas of Deligne. The same construction applies to define the Du Bois complexes of any quasi-excellent Noetherian scheme
X
over a field
F
of characteristic zero. Indeed, by Hironaka’s theorems, the assumptions on
X
guarantee the existence of a hyperresolution
\epsilon_{\bullet}:X_{\bullet}\to X
. We then define the
p-th Du Bois complex
of
X
over
F
as

\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}:={\bf R}\epsilon_{\bullet*}\Omega^{p}_{X_{\bullet}/F}.

We refer the readers to
[
GNPP
]
*Chapter V and
[
PS
]
*Chapter 7.3 for the construction of hyperresolutions, and for the definition of derived pushforward from a hyperresolution. Being natural objects arising from Deligne’s mixed Hodge theory, the Du Bois complexes are mostly studied for complex algebraic varieties. In this case, the Du Bois complexes
\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p}
in our notations are usually denoted simply as
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
in the literature.

The relationship between the Du Bois complexes and the cdh-differentials is observed in
[
CHWW2
]
*Lemma 2.1:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a finite type
F
-scheme, and let
k\subset F
be a subfield. Then for
p\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism

\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}\simeq\Omega_{cdh,X/k}^{p}.

Proof.

When
k=F
, this is exactly
[
CHWW2
]
*Lemma 2.1. For
k\subset F
, the result follows by the same proof, using
[
CHW
]
*Corollary 2.5 and
[
SV
]
*Lemma 12.1.
∎

For an
F
-scheme
X
, we denote by
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p}
the (derived) cdh-sheafification of the following complex of quasi-coherent sheaves:

\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega_{X/F}^{1}\to\cdots\to\Omega_{X/F}^{p}

supported in cohomological degrees
0,1,\cdots,p
. Explicitly,
\Omega_{cdh,X/F}^{\leq p}:={\bf R}a_{*}a^{*}\Omega_{X/F}^{\leq p}
, where
a:X_{cdh}\to X_{zar}
is the natural change-of-topology morphism from the cdh site to the Zariski site.

When
X
is of finite type over
F
, this represents an object in the derived category of differential complexes on
X
. When
F=\mathbb{C}
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that for each
p\geq 0
,

\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\leq p}=\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\bullet}/F^{p+1}

agrees with the quotient of the filtered de Rham complex by the Hodge filtration. It is immediate from the definition that we have the following exact triangle for each
p\geq 0
:

(1)

\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}[-p]\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p-1}\xrightarrow{+1}.

In the proof of
[
CHSW
]
*Theorem 6.1 and
[
CHW
]
*Proposition 2.6, the following useful properties of the objects
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}
and
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p}
are established:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a separated, finite type
F
-scheme, and let
k\subset F
be a subfield. Then for each
p\geq 0
,

(1)

\dim\supp\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}\leq\dim X-q-1
if
q>0
; in particular,
\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}=0
for all
p
and all
i\geq d
;

(2)

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p})=0
for all
q>\dim X
;

(3)

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{\leq p})=0
for all
q>\dim X+p
.

Proof.

(1) is stated in the proof of
[
CHSW
]
*Theorem 6.1 when
p=0
(in which case
\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{0}=\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{0}
). The proof for
p\geq 1
is identical when
k=F
, and the case for general

follows from Lemma

(1). (2) follows from (1) and the spectral sequence

E_{1}^{ij}=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{H}^{j}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}).

Finally, (3) follows from (2) by induction using the exact triangle (

∎

2.2.
Derived de Rham complex and the cotangent complex

Following Bhatt
[
Bh
]
*Construction 4.1, for an
F
-algebra
A
, we define the
(Hodge-completed) derived de Rham complex of
A
over
F
as

\widehat{\rm dR}_{A/F}:=\lim_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{op}}L\Omega_{A/F}^{\leq p},

where
L\Omega_{A/F}^{\leq p}:=\mathrm{Tot}(\Omega_{P/F}^{\leq p}\otimes_{P}A)
is the single complex associated to the double complex
\Omega_{P}^{\leq p}\otimes_{P}A
, where
P\to A
is a polynomial
F
-algebra resolution of
A
. It can be shown that the objects
L\Omega_{A/F}^{\leq p}
and
\widehat{\rm dR}_{A/F}
are independent of the choice of the polynomial resolution
P\to A
(up to homotopy), and that the construction globalizes to give definitions of
L\Omega_{X/F}^{\leq p}
and
\widehat{\rm dR}_{X/F}
for any
F
-scheme
X
.

By construction, there is a natural
derived filtration

\Fil_{H}
on
\widehat{\rm dR}_{X/F}
, with respect to which the graded pieces are given by

\gr^{p}_{H}\widehat{\rm dR}_{X/F}=\mathbb{L}^{p}_{X/F}[-p]

where
\mathbb{L}^{p}_{X/F}
is the
p
-th derived wedge product of the cotangent complex, introduced by Illusie
[
I
]
.

As explained in
[
Bh
]
*section 5, for a finite type
F
-scheme
X
, there is a map

\varphi:\widehat{\rm dR}_{X/F}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\bullet}

preserving the derived filtration on
\widehat{\rm dR}_{X/F}
and the Hodge filtration on
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\bullet}
. Taking associated graded pieces with respect to the two filtrations, we obtain a natural map

\mathbb{L}_{X/F}^{p}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}

for each
p
. It is a natural question when these maps are isomorphisms. For local complete intersections over
F=\mathbb{C}
, this question is studied in the theory of higher Du Bois singularities. When
X
is not a local complete intersection, this map is rarely an isomorphism unless

; see Remark
2.5
.

Background in higher Du Bois singularities

Remark 2.5

For a variety

, for some (any) field extension
F^{\prime}
of
F
.

Remark 2.7
.

Criteria for various classes of varieties (that are typically not local complete intersections) to have pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities have been worked out. See
[
GNPP
]
*V Theorem 4.6 for toric varieties,
[
CHWW3
,
PSh
]
for cones over projective varieties, and
[
ORS
]
for secant varieties.

For local complete intersections, one can characterize
m
-Du Bois singularities using the minimal exponent
[
JKSY
,
MOPW
,
CDMO
,
CDM
]
, which is an invariant that refines the log canonical threshold.

On the other hand, necessary and sufficient criteria for an arbitrary variety to have pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities are not known at this moment.

Following
[
SVV2
]
, we say
X
has
m
-Du Bois singularities
if it is seminormal, pre-
m
-Du Bois, the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}
are reflexive for all
p\leq m
, and that
\codim X_{\sing}\geq 2m+1
. As explained in
[
SVV2
]
*Section 5, this new definition agrees with the well-studied notion in the case of local complete intersections, and includes more examples in general. Varieties with
m
-Du Bois singularities in this sense provide examples that are
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular; see Example
4.2
.

2.4.
Background in
K
-regularity

Let
X
be a scheme. We say
X
is
K_{m}
-regular
if the natural maps

K_{m}(X)\to K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{r})

between algebraic
K
-groups are isomorphisms for all
r\geq 0
. It is a fact that if
X
is regular, then it is
K_{m}
-regular for all
m
. Also,
K_{m}
-regularity implies
K_{m-1}
-regularity for all
m
; see
[
Vor
]
and
[
DW
]
*Corollary 4.4.

In a sequence of foundational papers
[
CHSW
,
CHWW1
,
CHW
]
in this area, it was discovered that
K_{m}
-regularity of
X
is controlled by the Kähler differentials, the cdh-differentials and the Hochschild homology of
X
. Following
loc. cit.
, we denote by

NK_{m}(X)=\coker(K_{m}(X)\to K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}))

the
NK
-groups of Bass. For
r\geq 2
, the groups
N^{r}K(X)
are inductively defined by

N^{r}K_{m}(X):=N(N^{r-1}K_{m})(X):=\coker(N^{r-1}K_{m}(X)\to N^{r-1}K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}))

By
[
CHWW1
]
*Theorem 0.1, we have

\displaystyle N^{r}K_{m}(X)=0

\displaystyle\iff NK_{m}(X)=NK_{m-1}(X)=\cdots=NK_{m-r+1}(X)=0

\displaystyle\iff K_{m}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{r})\text{ is an isomorphism}.

This reduces the study of
K
-regularity to that of the
NK
-groups. Another theorem in
[
CHWW1
]
expresses
NK
, or more precisely,
NK^{(i)}
, the
i
-th eigenspace of the
\lambda
-operation on
NK
, in terms of the Kähler differentials, the cdh differentials and the Hochschild homology of
X
. We rephrase the statement with the notation of Du Bois complexes, using Lemma
2.1
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
CHWW1
]
*Theorem 0.4
Let
X=\Spec R
be an affine scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

NK_{n}^{(i)}(X)\cong TK_{n}^{(i)}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}t\mathbb{Q}[t]

where
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
in the definition of
NK
, and

TK_{n}^{(i)}(X)\simeq\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}^{i-n-1}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1}\quad&n\leq i-2\\
\coker(\Omega_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i})\quad&n=i-1\\
\ker(\Omega_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i})\quad&n=i\\
HH^{(i-1)}_{n-1}(X)\quad&n\geq i+1\end{cases}

Remark 2.9
.

Since
HH_{n}^{(i)}(X)\simeq\mathcal{H}_{n-i}\mathbb{L}^{i}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{i-n}\mathbb{L}^{i}
, this theorem can be rephrased in the following way: Let
C^{i}
be the cone of the natural map
\mathbb{L}^{i}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}\to\underline{\Omega}^{i}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}
. Then the

TK_{n}^{(i)}(X)
’s are determined by the cohomologies of
C^{i}
:

TK^{(i+1)}_{i-j}(X)\simeq\mathcal{H}^{j}C^{i}.

As a consequence,
X
is
K_{m}
-regular if and only if

\mathcal{H}^{j}C^{i}=0\quad\text{ for all }j-i\geq-m.

Thus, in view of Lemma
2.4
, the notion of
K
-regularity is naturally related to higher singularities, at least for local complete intersections.

To study the
K
-regularity for varieties that are not necessarily affine, it is convenient to work with a local version of Bass’
NK
-groups, denoted
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{m}
, and defined as the sheafification of

U\mapsto NK_{m}(\Gamma(U))

on the étale site of
X
in
[
vK
]
. As shown in
loc. cit.
, we have

H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{m})=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{m}),

where
H^{i}
denotes the cohomology taken in the Zariski topology. When
X
is affine,

H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{m})=\begin{cases}NK_{m}(X)\quad&\text{if $i=0$},\\
0\quad&\text{if $i>0$}.\end{cases}

We have a local-to-global spectral sequence relating local and global
NK
-groups

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X))\implies NK_{q-p}(X).

In view of these results, Theorem
2.8
can be stated in this local setting as follows:

Theorem 2.10

(
[
CHWW1
]
)
.

Let
X
be any scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{m}(X)\simeq\bigoplus_{j-i=-m}\mathcal{H}^{j}C^{i}\otimes t\mathbb{Q}[t],

where
C^{i}=\Cone(\mathbb{L}^{i}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i})
. In particular,
X
is locally
K_{m}
-regular, i.e.
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X)=0
for all
q\leq m
, if and only if

\mathcal{H}^{j}C^{i}=0\text{ for }j-i\geq-m.

2.5.
Base change results

We saw that the theory of higher Du Bois singularities concerns the study of the Du Bois complex
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}
for a variety over
F
, whereas the
K
-regularity of
X
is related to
\mathbb{L}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p}
and
\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p}
. Thus, to relate the two theories we will need base change results for the Du Bois complex and the cotangent complex over various fields, which we collect below.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
X
be a variety over
F
.

(1)

(
[
KS
]
*4.3a,
[
CHW
]
*Lemma 4.1, 4.2)
If
k\subset F
is a subfield, then we have spectral sequences

E_{1}^{ij}=\Omega^{i}_{F/k}\otimes_{F}\mathcal{H}^{i+j}\mathbb{L}_{X/F}^{p-i}\implies\mathcal{H}^{i+j}\mathbb{L}_{X/k}^{p}

E_{1}^{ij}=\Omega^{i}_{F/k}\otimes_{F}\mathcal{H}^{i+j}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p-i}\implies\mathcal{H}^{i+j}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}

E_{1}^{ij}=\Omega^{i}_{F/k}\otimes\mathbb{H}^{j}(X,L\Omega_{X/F}^{\leq p-i})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X,L\Omega_{X/k}^{\leq p})

E_{1}^{ij}=\Omega^{i}_{F/k}\otimes\mathbb{H}^{j}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p-i})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{\leq p})

(2)

If
F\subset F^{\prime}
is a field extension, then we have isomorphisms

\mathbb{L}_{X\times_{F}F^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}^{p}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{X/F}^{p}\otimes_{F}F^{\prime}.

\underline{\Omega}_{X\times_{F}F^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}^{p}\simeq\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}\otimes_{F}F^{\prime}.

Proof.

(1) The first spectral sequence follows from
[
KS
]
*4.3a, using that
HH_{n}^{(i)}(X)\simeq\mathcal{H}_{n-i}\mathbb{L}^{i}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{i-n}\mathbb{L}^{i}
. The second one is exactly
[
CHW
]
*Lemma 4.2, by the comparison between cdh differentials and the Du Bois complexes (see Lemma
2.1
). The last two spectral sequences are proved along the same lines as in
[
KS
]
*4.3a and
[
CHW
]
*Lemma 4.2, using instead the filtration
\mathcal{G}
on
\Omega_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{\leq p}
given by

\mathcal{G}^{i}/\mathcal{G}^{i+1}\simeq\Omega_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\leq p-i}[-i]

when
X
is smooth.

(2)
For Kähler differentials, we have isomorphism

\Omega_{X\times_{k}k^{\prime}/k^{\prime}}^{p}\simeq\Omega_{X/k}^{p}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}.

This gives the two desired isomorphisms, taking left derived functor for the first, and cdh sheafification for the second.
∎

3.
Vanishing theorems for the Du Bois complexes

In this section, we collect vanishing theorems for the Du Bois complexes, with focus on the effect of the change of base fields on the statements. These will be applied later to study
K
-regularity.

The first general result is due to Steenbrink when
F=\mathbb{C}
. The general case follows from Lemma
2.11
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
St2
]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety over
F
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}=0

for
p+q>\dim X
.

In view of Lemma
2.11
(1), Steenbrink’s vanishing theorem need not hold for Du Bois complexes over a subfield
k\subset F
. Instead, we have the following weaker statement:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a variety over
F
, and
k\subset F
a subfield. If
X
has pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities, then

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}=0

for all
q\geq\dim X-m-1
and
q>0
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the result when
p\geq m+1
. By
[
PSV
]
*Proposition C and Steenbrink vanishing (and reduction to the case
F=\mathbb{C}
as usual), we have that
\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}=0
for
q\geq\dim X-p\geq\dim X-m-1
. It then follows from the spectral sequence in Lemma
2.11
(1) that
\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X/k}=0
for
q\geq\dim X-m-1
.
∎

Complementary to Steenbrink’s theorem, we have vanishing results for
\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}
when
p+q
is small:

Theorem 3.3

(
[
MP2
]
)
.

Let
X
be a local complete intersection over
F
, and
k\subset F
a subfield. Denote by
X_{\sing}
the singular locus of
X
. Then for all
p\geq 0
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}=0

for
1\leq q<\codim X_{\sing}-p-1
.

Proof.

When
F=\mathbb{C}
, this is
[
MP2
]
*Corollary 13.9. The result for
F
follows from Lemma
2.11
(2), and the statement for a subfield
k\subset F
follows by analyzing the spectral sequence in Lemma
2.11
(1).
∎

Beyond local complete intersections, we have:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
PSV
,
K
]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety over
F
, and
k\subset F
be a subfield. Then

(1)

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{0}=\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{0}=0
for
1\leq q<\depth\mathcal{O}_{X}-\dim X_{\sing}-1
.

(2)

If
X
is pre-
(p-1)
-Du Bois with
\dim X_{\sing}=s
, then

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}=0

for
1\leq q<\depth\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}-s-1
. In particular, if
\depth\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}\geq\dim X-p
for all
p\geq 0
, then

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{p}=0

for
1\leq q<\dim X-p-s-1
, and any subfield
k\subset F
.

Proof.

Part (1) follows from
[
PSV
]
*Corollary B, using Lemma
2.11
(2) and the fact
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{0}=\underline{\Omega}_{X/k}^{0}
. Part (2) follows from
[
PSV
]
*Theorem A,
[
K
]
*Theorem 1.1 and Lemma
2.11
.
∎

4.
Higher Du Bois singularities and
K
-regularity

We start by establishing a general relationship between higher Du Bois singularities and
K
-regularity. Later, we will apply vanishing theorems for the Du Bois complexes to improve these results under additional hypothesis.

Proposition 4.1
.

(
\implies
Theorem
C
(1))
Let
X
be a variety of dimension
d
.

(1)

If
X
has
m
-Du Bois singularities, then it is
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular;

(2)

Suppose
X
is either affine, or has isolated singularities. If
X
is
K_{m}
-regular, then it is pre-
(m+1)
-Du Bois.

Proof.

(1) Since
X
has
m
-Du Bois singularities in the sense of
[
SVV2
]
, the following conditions are satisfied:

•

X
is seminormal;

•

X
has pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities;

•

\codim X_{\sing}\geq m+2
if
m\geq 1
.

To show
X
is
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular, by the local-to-global spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X))\implies NK_{q-p}(X),

it suffices to show

H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X))=0\text{ for all $q-p\leq-d+m+1$}.

By Theorem
2.10
, this is equivalent to showing

H^{p}(X,\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t})=0\text{ for $s-t=-q$ and $q-p\leq-d+m+1$},

where
C^{t}
is the cone of the natural map
\mathbb{L}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{t}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{t}
.

We verify this through a case-by-case analysis:

•

If
s>0
and
t\leq m
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}=0
because
X
is pre-
m
-Du Bois;

•

If
s>0
and
t\geq m+1
, then
p\geq q+d-m-1=t-s+d-m-1\geq d-s
. By Proposition
2.2
,

\dim\supp\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}=\dim\supp\mathcal{H}^{s}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{t}\leq d-s-1,

Thus, we have
H^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t})=0
.

•

If
s\leq 0
, then
q\geq s+q=t\geq 0
, and
p\geq q+d-m-1\geq d-m-1
. By assumption, the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}
are supported on
X_{\sing}
, which has dimension
\leq d-m-2
when
m\geq 1
, so we have
H^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t})=0
.

For
m=0
, some further caseworks are needed:

–

If
t=0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}=0
;

–

If
t\geq 1
, then
p\geq q+d-1=d-1-s+t\geq d
, so
H^{p}(\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t})=0
, because
\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}
is supported on
X_{\sing}
, which has dimension
\leq d-1
.

This completes the proof that
X
is
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular.

(2) Suppose
X
is affine and
K_{m}
-regular. By Theorem
2.8
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{s}C^{t}=0\text{ for $s-t\geq-m$}.

In particular, taking
s>0
, this implies

\mathcal{H}^{s}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p}=0

for all
p\leq m+1
and
s>0
, i.e. that
X
is pre-
(m+1)
-Du Bois.

Now, suppose
X
has isolated singularities. Then, the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X))\implies NK_{q-p}(X)

degenerates, giving that the local
NK
-groups
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X)=0
for
q\leq m
. It then follows from the affine case that
X
is pre-
(m+1)
-Du Bois.
∎

Proposition
4.1
(1) allows us to construct many examples of
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular varieties:

Example 4.2
.

We indicate some ways to construct varieties with
m
-Du Bois singularities:

(1)

A codimension
r
local complete intersection
X
has
m
-Du Bois singularities if and only if its minimal exponent
\tilde{\alpha}_{X}\geq m+r
; see
[
Sa2
,
JKSY
,
MOPW
,
CDM
]
. Playing around with the minimal exponents, one can construct varieties with
m
-Du Bois singularities;

(2)

For toric varieties and cones over smooth projective varieties, one can find criteria for them to have
m
-Du Bois singularities in
[
SVV2
]
*Section 6-7.

(3)

Criteria for secant varieties to have
m
-Du Bois singularities are worked out in
[
ORS
]
.

By Proposition
4.1
(1), these provide examples of
K_{-d+m+1}
-regular varieties.

Next, applying vanishing theorems in Section
3
, we obtain the following strengthening of Proposition
4.1
in the local setting:

Proposition 4.3

(
\implies
Theorem
C
(2))
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional variety over
F
with pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities. Then

(1)

X
is locally
K_{-t}
-regular if
t\geq\max\{1,d-2m-2\}
;

(2)

If
X
is seminormal, then it is locally
K_{0}
-regular if
m\geq\frac{d-2}{2}
;

(3)

If
X
is seminormal, and the natural map
\Omega_{X/F}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{1}
is surjective, then it is locally
K_{1}
-regular if
m\geq\frac{d-1}{2}
;

(4)

Let
s=\dim X_{\sing}
. Then
X
is
K_{-t}
-regular if
t\geq\max\{s+1,d-2m-2+s\}
; if
X
is in addition seminormal, then the bound can be improved to
t\geq\max\{s,d-2m-2+s\}
.

(5)

If
X
is either affine, or has isolated singularities, then we can remove the adjective “locally” in (1), (2) and (3).

Proof.

(1) By Theorem
2.10
, we need to show

\mathcal{H}^{q}C^{p}=0\text{ for }q-p\geq\max\{1,d-2m-2\},

where
C^{p}=\Cone(\mathbb{L}^{p}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}\to\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X/\mathbb{Q}})
. Since
q\geq q-p\geq 1
, this is equivalent to

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p}=0\text{ for }q-p\geq d-2m-2.

Since
X
has pre-
m
-Du Bois singularities, this holds when
p\leq m
. When
p\geq m+1
, we have
q\geq d-2m-2+p\geq d-m-1
, in which case the vanishing follows from Proposition
3.2
.

(2) By (1), it suffices to show
\mathcal{H}^{0}C^{0}\simeq\coker(\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{0})=0
. This is true by
[
Sa3
]
*Corollary 0.3, since
X
is assumed to be seminormal.

(3) By (1) and (2), since
\mathcal{H}^{-1}C^{0}=0
, it suffices to show
\mathcal{H}^{0}C^{1}\simeq\coker(\Omega_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{1})=0
. This follows by assumption and Lemma
2.11
(1).

(4) Consider the local-to-global spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q})\implies NK_{q-p}(X).

Since
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}
is supported on
X_{\sing}
and
\dim X_{\sing}=s
, we have

H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q})=0\qquad\text{for }p>s.

On the other hand, if
p-q\geq d-2m-2+s
, then for every
0\leq p\leq s
we have

q=(q-p)+p\leq-(d-2m-2+s)+s=-d+2m+2.

Note also that
q=p-t\leq p-s-1<0
. Hence
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}=0
by part (1). It follows that
NK_{-t}(X)=0
for
t\geq 1
and
t\geq d-2m-2+s
. That is,
X
is
K_{-t}
-regular. When
X
is in addition seminormal, the assertion follows by invoking (2) in place of (1).

(5) If
X
is either affine, or has isolated singularities, the local-to-global spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(X,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{K}_{q}(X))\implies NK_{q-p}(X)

degenerates, so local
K_{-d+2m+2}
-regularity implies
K_{-d+2m+2}
-regularity.
∎

As an application of this Proposition, we give some examples that are
K_{0}
-regular:

Example 4.4
.

We list some varieties with pre-
k
-Du Bois singularities for all
k
. These have “mild” singularities from the perspective of Hodge theory. By Proposition
4.3
, such an

is locally

(1)

K_{n}
-regular for all

;

(2)

K_{0}
-regular if

is seminormal.

Examples include: toric varieties
[
GNPP
]
, finite or more generally geometric quotients (
[

]
), cones over varieties satisfying Bott vanishing
[

]
,
[
SVV2
]
*Corollary 7.6. Since all these varieties are normal, they are locally

-regular.

Next, we investigate the converse to the implication in Proposition
4.3
:

Proposition 4.5

(
\implies
Theorem
C
(3))
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional affine variety with
\dim X_{\sing}=s
. Suppose either of the following holds:

(1)

X
is a local complete intersection;

(2)

\depth\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\geq d-p
for all

.

If
X
is

-Du Bois.

Since

is

\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{j}=0\text{ for $i\geq d-2m-1-s+j$ and $i>0$}.

Applying Theorem

in case (1), and Theorem

in case (2) (with

\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{j}=0\text{ for $1\leq i\leq d-s-2-j$}.

, the two inequalities of

cover all indices

. It follows that

is pre-

-Du Bois.
∎

Combining the two Propositions above, we obtain:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let

In

]
*Theorem 5.9, characterizations for

-regularity that are more geometric in nature are obtained. For example, given a resolution of singularities

with exceptional divisor

that

-regular if and only if

H^{1}(E,\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{n})=0\text{ for all }n\geq 2,

where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal of

defining

One can directly see that this condition characterizes Du Bois singularities for normal surfaces, as follows: since
X
is seminormal, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
; moreover, by dimension reasons
\mathcal{H}^{2}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=0
. It follows that
X
has Du Bois singularities if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=0
.

By Steenbrink’s triangle for the Du Bois complexes,

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\to\mathcal{R}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-E)\to\mathcal{O}_{x}\xrightarrow{+1},

where
x
is the isolated singularity of
X
. We thus see that

\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-E),

which by the Formal Function Theorem is

R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-E)=\varprojlim_{n}H^{1}(E_{n},\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-E)|_{E_{n}})=\varprojlim_{n}H^{1}(E,I/I^{n}).

Since
H^{2}(E,{-})=0
, from the short exact sequence

0\to I^{n}/I^{n+1}\to I/I^{n+1}\to I/I^{n}\to 0

we see that for all
n\geq 1
,

H^{1}(E,I/I^{n})=0\implies H^{1}(E,I/I^{n+1})=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle X\text{ is Du Bois}

\displaystyle\iff R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-E)=\varprojlim_{n}H^{1}(E,I/I^{n})=0

\displaystyle\iff H^{1}(E,I/I^{n})=0\text{ for sufficiently large $n$}

\displaystyle\iff H^{1}(E,I/I^{n})=0\text{ for all $n$}.

I am grateful to Sung Gi Park for telling me about this argument.

5.
Application to regularity criteria

In this section, we prove the following regularity criterion. Recall that
K_{m}
-regularity implies
K_{m-1}
-regularity for all
m

[

,

]
, hence it immediately implies Theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

(
\implies
Theorem
B
)
Let
X
be an affine local complete intersection, with singular locus

. Then

(1)

If
\codim X_{\sing}
is odd, and

is
K_{2}
-regular, then it is regular;

(2)

If
\codim X_{\sing}
is even, and
X
is
K_{1}
-regular, then it is regular.

The proof is similar to that of Corollary
4.11
, and relies on three main ingredients:

(1)

A regularity criterion due to Mustaţă-Popa (Theorem
2.3
). The relevance of this result in relation to extensions of Vorst’s conjecture is observed in
[
HW
]
;

(2)

Results due to Graf and Miller-Vassiliadou about the torsion and cotorsion of the Kähler differentials of local complete intersections (Theorem
4.10
);

(3)

The relationship between
K_{m}
-regularity and pre-
k
-Du Bois singularities (Proposition
4.5
), which in turn relies on a vanishing theorem of Mustaţă-Popa (Theorem

).

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
F
be the field of definition of
X
. By Theorem
2.3
, regularity of
X
is implied by having
m
-Du Bois singularities for
m>\frac{d-1}{2}
.

(1) Suppose
\codim X_{\sing}=2m+1
is odd. To show
X
is smooth, it suffices to show
X
has
(m+1)
-Du Bois singularities, i.e. the following two conditions hold:

•

X
has pre-
(m+1)
-Du Bois singularities. By Proposition
4.5
, this is implied by
K_{-d+2(m+1)+1+s}=K_{2}
-regularity;

•

The maps

are isomorphisms for

. By Theorem

, this is the case when

. Thus we have the desired isomorphism when

, i.e. when

.

When

, we need to show
\Omega_{X/F}^{2}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{2}
is an isomorphism. By Theorem

,

-regularity implies that this map is surjective with

replaced by
\mathbb{Q}
, and Lemma

(2) implies the surjectivity over

; on the other hand Theorem

Finally, it is impossible that

, as

-regularity implies

.

-Du Bois singularities, hence smooth.

(2) Suppose

is even. To show

is smooth, it suffices to show

has

-Du Bois singularities, i.e. the following two conditions hold:

•

, this is implied by

•

The maps

, this is the case when

When

∎

6.
Bass’ question and the Du Bois invariants

Let
X
be a variety over
F
. The Du Bois invariants, defined as

b^{p,q}=\dim_{F}\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}

for
q>0
, are introduced by Steenbrink
[
St1
]
when
X
has isolated singularities. It follows from Proposition
2.2
that
b^{p,q}=0
for all
q\geq d:=\dim X
. We arrange the invariants
b^{p,q}
for
0\leq p\leq d
and
1\leq q\leq d-1
into a
(d-1)\times(d+1)
table, and refer to it as the
Du Bois table
.

For surfaces over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have a
1\times 3
table of the form:

b^{0,1}

b^{1,1}

b^{2,1}

In fact, it is well known that for surfaces, the only interesting invariants are
b^{0,1}
and
b^{1,1}
:

Lemma 6.1

(
[
CHWW2
]
*Proposition 3.2)
.

Let
X
be a surface over
F
. For
q>0
and
p\geq 0
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}=0

unless
(p,q)=(0,1)
or
(1,1)
.

Proof.

By Steenbrink’s vanishing theorem (see Theorem
3.1
), we have
\mathcal{H}^{q}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}=0
when
p+q>2
. The vanishing when
(p,q)=(0,2)
follows from Proposition
2.2
(1).
∎

Applications of the Du Bois invariants to
K
-theory started in
[
CHWW2
]
, where the authors used an example of surface with
b^{0,1}=1
and
b^{1,1}=0
to give negative answers to Bass’ question
[
B
]
, which asks whether

K_{m}(X)\simeq K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})\implies K_{m}(X)\simeq K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{2}).

Indeed, for surfaces, the main theorem of
[
CHWW1
]
implies that Bass’ question for the negative
K
-groups has an affirmative answer if and only if
b^{0,1}
and
b^{1,1}
are both zero, or both nonzero. In view of the potential applications of these invariants to
K
-theory, it is desirable to have a better understanding of them. In this section, we give a complete classification of these invariants for surfaces.

First, along the lines of
[
PSV
]
*Proposition C, we show that
b^{0,1}
and
b^{1,1}
are constrained by the inequality
b^{0,1}\geq b^{1,1}
:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X
be a surface over
F
. Then the map

d:\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{0}\to\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{1}

is surjective. In particular, we have
b^{0,1}\geq b^{1,1}
when
X
has isolated singularities.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.11
(2), it suffices to prove the result when
F=\mathbb{C}
. In this case, we have by the Poincaré Lemma that

\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\bullet})^{\an}

as sheaves in the analytic topology on
X(\mathbb{C})
.

By construction of the Du Bois complex (in the analytic category, see
[
DB
]
), we have a spectral sequence

E_{1}^{pq}=\mathcal{H}^{q}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{p})^{\an}\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{p+q}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\bullet})^{\an}

converging to
(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{\bullet})^{\an}\simeq\mathbb{C}[0]
supported in cohomological degree zero. Analyzing the maps in the spectral sequence shows that there is a surjection

d^{\an}:\mathcal{H}^{1}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{0})^{\an}\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{1})^{\an}.

Using Serre’s GAGA theorem, we then conclude that the map
d
is surjective.
∎

In fact, the invariants
b^{0,1}
and
b^{1,1}
can take on arbitrary values:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X
be a normal surface over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(1)

If
X
is an affine cone over a projective curve, or a quasi-homogeneous hypersurface, then
b^{0,1}=b^{1,1}
; in these cases, Bass’ question for
K_{m}
has positive answer when
m\leq 0
.

(2)

Let
m\geq n\geq 0
be any pair of nonnegative integers. The hypersurface
(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3m}+xyz^{m+n}=0)
has Du Bois invariants

b^{0,1}=m\quad\text{ and }\quad b^{1,1}=n.

Proof.

(1) When
X
is an affine cone over a projective curve
C
associated to a line bundle

on

, explicit calculation as in
[

]
and
[

For quasi-homogeneous hypersurfaces, we use van Straten-Steenbrink’s computation of the geometric genus
p_{g}\leq b^{0,1}
and the
alpha invariant

\alpha:=b^{0,1}-b^{1,1}
: by
[

]
*Theorem 3.1, Corollary 6.3, for a surface
X=(f=0)\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}=Y
with isolated singularities at
0
, we have

p_{g}=\dim Q^{f}/V_{>0}

and

p_{g}+\alpha=\dim Q^{f}/\overline{K}\cap V_{>0},

where
Q^{f}=\Omega_{Y,0}^{3}/df\wedge\Omega_{Y,0}^{2}
and
\overline{K}
is the kernel of the multiplication by
f
on
Q^{f}
. If
f
is quasi-homogeneous, multiplication by
f
is zero on
Q^{f}
, so
\overline{K}=Q^{f}
, it follows that
\alpha=b^{0,1}-b^{1,1}=0
, as claimed.

(2) For semi-quasi-homogeneous hypersurfaces of weight
(w_{x},w_{y},w_{z})
with isolated singularities, the
V
-filtration on
Q^{f}
is computed in
[
Sa1
]
. One has that
V_{>l}Q^{f}
is generated by monomials

satisfying

w_{x}(a+1)+w_{y}(b+1)+w_{z}(c+1)-1>l.

In particular, for
f=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3m}+xyz^{m+n}
, we have

V_{>0}=(x,y,z^{m})

and a Gröbner basis computation shows that

\overline{K}=(x,y,z^{2m-n})

This gives that

p_{g}=\dim Q^{f}/V_{>0}=m\geq b^{0,1}

and

p_{g}+\alpha=\dim Q^{f}/\overline{K}\cap V_{>0}=2m-n.

Next, using the deformation argument in
[
CHWW2
]
(see Theorem 2.14 and the proof of Proposition 4.3 in

), we see that

is independent of

. When

, we have
\alpha=m
, and since
0\leq b^{1,1}=b^{0,1}-\alpha=b^{0,1}-m\leq 0
, we have
b^{0,1}=m
and
b^{1,1}=0
. It follows that for
n\geq 1
, we have

b^{0,1}=m\quad\text{ and }\quad b^{1,1}=n,

as required.
∎

Moving to higher dimensions, we show that for homogeneous hypersurfaces with isolated singularities, Bass’ question has positive answer for the non-positive

For

(2)

For

Proof.

The result follows from

*Theorem D, Proposition 1, Remark 1, and also the paragraphs before the proof of Theorem D, when

When

Our main result is the following characterization of

Theorem 7.1

Remark 7.2

Let

In particular, if

By

*Theorem 5.5, there is a homotopy fibration sequence

. Following

for the cohomology taken in cdh topology, and by

the cohomology taken in Zariski topology. By definition, a scheme

-regular if and only if
\pi_{m}\tilde{C_{j}}\mathcal{K}(X)=\ker(K_{m}(X)\to K_{m}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}))=0

Taking cohomology, we obtain a long exact sequence

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\cdots\to&H^{d-1}(\tilde{C}_{j}{\bf HC}(X))\to H^{d-1}_{cdh}(\tilde{C}_{j}{\bf HC}(X))\to\pi_{-d+1}\tilde{C}_{j}\mathcal{K}(X)\\

\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p}(X,\tilde{C}_{j}L\Omega_{X/F}^{\leq p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p}_{cdh}(X,\tilde{C}_{j}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p})
are surjective for all
p\geq 0
.

By definition of
\tilde{C}_{j}
, it suffices to show

(1)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p}(X,L\Omega_{X/F}^{\leq p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{\leq p})
are isomorphisms for all
p\geq 0
.

(2)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j},L\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j},\underline{\Omega}_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})
are isomorphisms for all
p,j\geq 0
.

(3)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j},L\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j},\underline{\Omega}_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})
are surjective for all
p,j\geq 0
.

Part (1) follows by assumptions. For (2) and (3), analyzing the spectral sequences

E_{1}^{ij}=\mathbb{H}^{j}(p^{*}L\Omega^{i}_{X/F}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-i})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(L\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})

E_{1}^{ij}=\mathbb{H}^{j}(p^{*}\underline{\Omega}^{i}_{X/F}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-i})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(\underline{\Omega}_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/F}^{\leq p})

we see that it suffices to show

(1)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p-k}(p^{*}L\Omega_{X/F}^{k}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k})\to\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+2p-k}(p^{*}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{k}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k})
are isomorphisms for all
p\geq 0
and
k\leq p
.

(2)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p-k}(p^{*}L\Omega_{X/F}^{k}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k})\to\mathbb{H}^{-m+2p-k}(p^{*}\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{k}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k})
are surjective for all
p\geq 0
and
k\leq p
.

By induction using long exact sequences associated to

\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{p-k}[-p+k]\to\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k}\to\Omega_{X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}/X}^{\leq p-k-1}\xrightarrow{+1},

it suffices to show

(1)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+p}(L\Omega_{X/F}^{k})\to\mathbb{H}^{-m+1+p}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{k})
are isomorphisms for all
p\geq 0
and
k\leq p
.

(2)

\mathbb{H}^{-m+p}(L\Omega_{X/F}^{k})\to\mathbb{H}^{-m+p}(\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{k})
are surjective for all
p\geq 0
and
k\leq p
.

We have (1) by assumption, and (2) follows by Proposition
7.3
.

Example 7.5

We construct a 3-fold

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
for
\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X/\mathbb{C}}

Let
C
be a genus 2 curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
S=C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Denote by
\pi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
the projection map. The line bundle

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\omega_{S}\otimes(\pi^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1))^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\omega_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}}\otimes(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1))^{\vee}

is relatively ample over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, so we can consider the relative affine cone

Y={\bf Spec}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\pi_{*}L^{m}

over
S
defined by
L
. One checks that the arguments in
[
CHWW3
]
computing the Du Bois complexes of cones carry over to the relative case, giving that

\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{0}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}R^{1}\pi_{*}L^{m}.

is a sheaf supported on
Y_{\sing}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We have
R^{1}\pi_{*}L\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
by Grothendieck duality, and
R^{1}\pi_{*}L^{m}=0
by Kodaira vanishing. It follows that

\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{0}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1).

Similarly, one checks that

\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1).

X
be a projectivization of
Y
that is smooth away from the singular points of
Y
. Then, we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{1}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1).

X
is not pre-
0

, this is equivalent to the following maps being isomorphisms:

\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{3}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})

\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{2}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})

\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathbb{L}_{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}^{3}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{1})

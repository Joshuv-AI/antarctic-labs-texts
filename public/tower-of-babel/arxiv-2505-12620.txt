BusterX: MLLM-Powered AI-Generated Video Forgery Detection and Explanation
Haiquan Wen, Yiwei He, Zhenglin Huang, Tianxiao Li, Zihan Yu, Xingru Huang, Baoyuan Wu, Xiangtai Li, Guangliang Cheng

Abstract
Advances in AI generative models facilitate super-realistic video synthesis, amplifying misinformation risks via social media and eroding trust in digital content. Several research works have explored new deepfake detection methods on AI-generated images to alleviate these risks. However, with the fast development of video generation models, such as Sora and WanX, there is currently a lack of large-scale, high-quality AI-generated video datasets for forgery detection. In addition, existing detection approaches predominantly treat the task as binary classification, lacking explainability in model decision-making and failing to provide actionable insights or guidance for the public. To address these challenges, we propose GenBuster-200K , a large-scale AI-generated video dataset featuring 200K high-resolution video clips, diverse latest generative techniques, and real-world scenes. We further introduce BusterX , a novel AI-generated video detection and explanation framework leveraging multimodal large language model (MLLM) and reinforcement learning for authenticity determination and explainable rationale. To our knowledge, GenBuster-200K is the first large-scale, high-quality AI-generated video dataset that incorporates the latest generative techniques for real-world scenarios. BusterX is the first framework to integrate MLLM with reinforcement learning for explainable AI-generated video detection. Extensive comparisons with state-of-the-art methods and ablation studies validate the effectiveness and generalizability of BusterX. The code, models, and datasets will be released.

3.2
Benchmark Construction

Data Sources:
Our dataset, GenBuster-200K, consists of two parts: real videos from real-world scenarios and synthetic videos that simulate real-world conditions. The details are as follows:

Real Videos.
More than 100K real videos sourced from OpenVid-1M
[
36
]
, covering a diverse range of real-world scenarios. These videos were carefully filtered to ensure high quality and eliminate any duplicate clips.

Synthetic Videos Generation.
More than 100K videos were generated with high realism and diversity. We developed Multi-dimensional Controlled Prompt Pipeline (MCPP) and experimented with several state-of-the-art generative models, including open-source models, such as HunyuanVideo
[
26
]
and
LTX-Video
[
19
]
, and commercial models accessible via APIs, such as Sora
[
39
]
and Jimeng
[
1
]
. For each model, we generated video frames at various resolutions and with different hyper-parameters to ensure diversity.

Figure 2
:
Video generation and collection pipeline.

Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline:
Figure
2
illustrates our synthetic video generation pipeline. The main process, contained within MCPP, comprises three stages:

Stage-1: Diverse Seed Generation.
This stage involves collecting keywords to encompass a wide variety of scenarios, with particular attention to human-related contexts. We systematically account for diversity across gender, age, ethnicity, and other key attributes to maintain balanced representation. Beyond human-centric terms, we also incorporate keywords from diverse domains, such as animals, sceneries, and other non-human contexts, to broaden the semantic scope. This methodical approach ensures our initial semantic space is both inclusive and comprehensive, spanning a wide range of subjects and perspectives.

Stage-2: Description Generation.
Building on the prior output from stage-1, the forthcoming synthetic video descriptions will be generated by an LLM using a predefined prompt. Specifically, the process includes:

1)
Prompt Constraints Guidance: The system prompt introduces physical and aesthetic constraints. Example dialog templates are based on content understanding of common real videos using VLM, with manual adjustments to cover multiple scenarios. These constraints guide the LLM to generate descriptions that are both realistic and contextually appropriate.

2)
Multi-model Overfitting Resistance: To combat model-specific bias, we randomly sample from a diverse pool of over 20 open-source LLMs, spanning varied architectures and parameter scales (e.g., DeepSeek-V3
[
15
]
, LLaMA
[
55
]
, etc.). Additionally, we employ high-temperature sampling to maximize output diversity during generation.

Stage-3: Post-filtering.
After generating the prompt, we employ a multi-step post-filtering mechanism to ensure the quality and reliability of the generated prompts. Specifically, the post-filtering process includes the following key steps:

1)
Length Control: We first check the length of each generated prompt to ensure it falls within a predefined range. Prompts that are too short may lack sufficient context, while those that are excessively long can introduce unnecessary complexity or redundancy.

2)
Special Character Check: We then scan for any special characters or formatting issues that may have been inadvertently introduced during the generation process.

3)
Readability Scoring: The LLM Judge assigns a readability score based on linguistic coherence, grammatical correctness, and overall clarity. Prompts with scores below a predefined threshold are discarded to ensure that only high-quality prompts are retained.
By integrating these steps, we can ensure that the generated prompts are of high quality.

Post-processing.
We perform unified post-processing on both the generated video frames and the selected real videos. This process standardizes the resolution to 1024×1024, the duration to 5 seconds, and the frame rate to 24 FPS. This ensures consistency across videos generated by different models, which may have varying original resolutions and frame rates.

Additionally, we employ HEVC encoding using x265 with default parameters to encode the videos in the yuv420p10le format. This achieves a better balance between file size and video quality, allowing for higher video quality at the same bitrate. The unified post-processing eliminates potential biases from underlying encoding preferences (e.g., commonly used x264), which is crucial for ensuring the generalizability of forgery detection models across diverse video sources.

In this section, we introduce BusterX, a novel framework for detecting AI-generated videos and providing detailed explanations. We train a reasoning MLLM with Chain of Thought (CoT), which enables the model to perform step-by-step reasoning, significantly enhancing the interpretability of the detection results. Unlike traditional deepfake detection techniques that depend on reference videos, BusterX operates without such references, substantially improving its practical applications.

4.1
Multi-Stage Training

As shown in Figure
3
, BusterX’s training involves two stages: Cold Start and RL Training. We use Qwen2.5-VL-7B
[
7
]
as the base model, which enhances spatial understanding by using raw coordinates and improves temporal modeling via dynamic FPS training and absolute time encoding—features well aligned with our task. Prompt details are provided in the appendix.

Cold Start.
We adopt Prompt-Tuning to collect 10k short CoT data samples from the base model. We filter and format the data, ensuring that the positive and negative samples are balanced at a ratio of 1:1. Then, we use the collected data to perform a rapid Cold Start using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). This initial training phase helps the model quickly learn the answer format.

RL Training.
After the Cold Start phase, we proceed with Reinforcement Learning (RL) training to further refine the model’s ability to produce detailed and high-quality explanations.

In our implementation, we employ LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)
[
21
]
to boost efficiency. By integrating low-rank trainable matrices into the model’s layers, LoRA enables efficient SFT while retaining the original pre-trained weights. This strategy significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, and in our experiments, only about 0.66% of the total parameters need to be trained.

4.2
Reward Functions

We adopt DAPO
[
64
]
as our reinforcement learning strategy, as its core techniques align well with our goal of generating reasonable and interpretable outputs. DAPO incorporates several key methods—including clip-higher, dynamic sampling, token-level policy gradient loss, and overlong reward shaping—all designed to enhance learning efficiency and output quality. Specifically, DAPO samples multiple responses for each question, computes relative advantages using predefined reward functions, and updates the policy to maximize rewards. We design the following reward functions:

Format
: If the model adheres to the specified format (i.e., <think>…</think>and <answer>…</answer>), it receives a reward of
r_{\text{fmt}}=0
; otherwise, it receives a penalty of
r_{\text{fmt}}=-1
.

Soft Overlong
: When the response length exceeds the maximum value, we define a penalty interval.

\small r_{\text{overlong}}=\begin{cases}0,&L_{\text{gen}}\leq L_{\text{max}}-L_{\text{cache}}\\
\frac{(L_{\text{max}}-L_{\text{cache}})-L_{\text{gen}}}{L_{\text{cache}}},&L_{\text{max}}-L_{\text{cache}}<L_{\text{gen}}\leq L_{\text{max}}\\
-1,&L_{\text{max}}<L_{\text{gen}}\end{cases}

(1)

Accuracy-Based Length
: If the model classifies correctly and follows the format, it receives a reward of
\frac{L_{\text{gen}}}{L_{\text{max}}}
; otherwise,
r_{\text{len}}=0
.

The total reward is computed as
R=r_{\text{fmt}}+r_{\text{overlong}}+r_{\text{len}}
.

Figure 4
:
CoT Length comparsion across different reward functions.

As shown in Figure
4
, without the length reward mechanism, the model fails to generate sufficiently long CoT responses. In our task, long CoT is crucial for fostering deeper video reasoning capabilities. However, naively encouraging longer outputs risks producing excessively verbose answers, which both squander computational resources and destabilize training. To mitigate this, we propose a soft overlength penalty. By strategically balancing these opposing objectives, we maintain responses within an optimal length range. This approach ensures the generation of thorough explanations while preserving training stability, ultimately yielding more interpretable results.

5.1
Experimental Details and Evaluations

Implementation Details.
We resized video frames to a resolution of 256x256 and uniformly sampled 16 frames at a rate of 4 FPS for video-level detection. For both models, we employed LoRA with a rank of 16 and alpha of 32, and trained using a learning rate of
1\times 10^{-5}
, which was empirically determined to provide a good balance between convergence speed and model performance. We used bfloat16 for training to optimize computational efficiency. The baseline models used for comparison were trained with default settings from their original papers. In addition to fine-tuning, we also evaluated the zero-shot capabilities of several MLLMs to assess their performance.

Conclusion and Limitations

In this paper, we present GenBuster-200K, a large-scale dataset comprising 100K real videos and 100K AI-generated videos. Building on GenBuster-200K, we propose BusterX, a novel MLLM framework to detect and explain AI-generated videos. As the first framework capable of providing detailed explanations, BusterX sets a new standard by enhancing the interpretability of AI-generated video detection.
Despite promising progress, we recognize certain limitations and aim to address them through future optimization.

1) Data modality.
Our dataset currently includes video only, while AI-generated content spans multiple modalities (e.g., audio, subtitles). Incorporating these is a key future goal.

2) Dataset Scale.
Real-world complexity demands a larger dataset. Expanding video coverage is essential to improve detection robustness and generalizability.

Appendix C
CoT Length

In our experiments, we extensively explored the optimal settings for CoT to strike a balance between training stability and the model’s ability to perform frame-level reasoning. We discovered that overly long CoT responses tend to destabilize training, leading to erratic performance and increased computational overhead. Conversely, excessively short CoT responses fail to capture the necessary reasoning steps required for frame-level inference, resulting in superficial or inaccurate explanations.

To address this challenge, we conducted numerous experiments to identify the ideal CoT length. We found that setting the maximum length
L_{\text{max}}
to 2048 tokens and the cache length
L_{\text{cache}}
to 1448 tokens yielded the best results. This configuration aims to maintain an expected CoT length of around 600 tokens, which is sufficient for detailed reasoning while avoiding excessive verbosity.

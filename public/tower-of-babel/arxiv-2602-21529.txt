TM-RugPull: A Temporally Sound, Multimodal Dataset for Early Detection of Rug Pulls Across the Tokenized Ecosystem
Fatemeh Shoaei, Mohammad Pishdar, Mozafar Bag-Mohammadi, Mojtaba Karami

Abstract
Rug-pull attacks pose a systemic threat across the blockchain ecosystem, yet research into early detection is hindered by the lack of scientific-grade datasets. Existing resources often suffer from temporal data leakage, narrow modality, and ambiguous labeling—particularly outside DeFi contexts. To address these limitations, we present TM-RugPull , a rigorously curated, leakage-resistant dataset of 1,028 token projects spanning DeFi, meme coins, NFTs, and celebrity-themed tokens. RugPull enforces strict temporal hygiene by extracting all features—on-chain behavior, smart contract metadata, and OSINT signals—strictly from the first half of each project’s lifespan. Labels are grounded in forensic reports and longevity criteria, verified through multi-expert consensus. This dataset enables causally valid, multimodal analysis of rug-pull dynamics and establishes a new benchmark for reproducible fraud detection research.



Keywords
Rug pull detection, DeFi fraud, leakage-resistant dataset, multimodal blockchain analytics, temporally aligned OSINT, on-chain behavioral features, smart contract scams

1
Introduction

Rug-pull attacks have emerged as a systemic threat across the blockchain ecosystem, causing billions of dollars in investor losses. Existing datasets for studying these scams suffer from critical flaws: temporal data leakage, narrow modality (e.g., on-chain only), and ambiguous labeling. Moreover, most focus exclusively on DeFi, ignoring the broader landscape of tokenized assets where rug pulls frequently originate—from meme coins to fake celebrity tokens. To address these gaps, we introduce
TM-RugPull
, a rigorously curated, leakage-resistant dataset of 1,028 token projects spanning Ethereum, BSC, Polygon, Arbitrum, and Fantom from 2016 to 2025. Unlike prior resources, RugPull explicitly excludes post-attack signals by enforcing a strict temporal boundary—
Project Midpoint
—and extracting all features solely from the first half of each project’s active lifespan. The dataset integrates three modalities: (i)
on-chain behavioral metrics
(e.g., token concentration ratio, holder variance, quarterly price dynamics), (ii)
smart contract metadata
(blockchain type, consensus mechanism, contract status), and (iii)
open-source intelligence (OSINT)
(Google Search hits, Twitter/X activity), all temporally aligned and capped at the midpoint. Our dataset encompasses a diverse set of token categories—including DeFi protocols, meme coins, NFT-based games, and celebrity-themed tokens—reflecting the heterogeneous nature of rug-pull threats beyond narrow DeFi contexts. To provide an intuitive overview of TM-RugPull , we include three complementary visualizations in Section
6
: category distribution, on-chain feature comparison, and temporal alignment of OSINT signals. By combining temporal hygiene, multimodal coverage, and cross-domain representation, TM-RugPull establishes a new benchmark for reproducible, causally sound research in blockchain fraud detection.

Background

A
rug pull
is a specific type of exit scam in tokenized ecosystems
pishdar2024major
, formally defined in this work as a project that satisfies all three of the following operational conditions: (i) an
abrupt and near-total withdrawal of liquidity
from decentralized exchanges, (ii) a
cessation of meaningful on-chain activity
(e.g., transactions, trades) within a short post-withdrawal window, and (iii) a
collapse in token price or trading volume to a non-determinable or negligible state
, rendering the asset effectively worthless. Crucially, not all projects exhibiting suspicious traits—such as high token concentration or short lifespans—are classified as rug pulls. Transient phenomena like temporary liquidity shortages, failed token launches, or legitimate project sunsets do not constitute rug pulls unless they meet the above tripartite criterion. Effective detection of rug pulls requires two complementary data modalities:

•

On-chain behavioral signals

2
: Tamper-proof records of token distribution, transaction patterns, and liquidity dynamics.

•

Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT)

6
: Off-chain indicators such as social media activity and search interest that often precede on-chain anomalies.

However, any analysis that incorporates post-collapse information suffers from
temporal data leakage
, which invalidates causal inference and inflates performance unrealistically. Thus, strict temporal hygiene—extracting features only from pre-attack periods—is essential for operational early-warning systems.

Dataset Construction

4.1
Source and Scope

TM-RugPull is constructed to capture the heterogeneous landscape of rug-pull threats across blockchain ecosystems. The dataset encompasses projects deployed on multiple public blockchains and spans diverse application domains, including decentralized finance protocols, meme tokens, NFT and gaming projects, and impersonation-based schemes.

4.2
Labeling Protocol

Project labels are assigned through a conservative and evidence-driven verification process. A project is labeled as a rug pull **if and only if it satisfies all three of the following operational conditions for at least 72 consecutive hours**:

1.

Zero liquidity
: All liquidity pools associated with the token are drained to negligible levels.

2.

Zero transactional activity
: No meaningful on-chain transactions (e.g., trades, transfers) occur.

3.

Undefined price/volume
: Token price and trading volume become untraceable or non-determinable on major decentralized exchanges.

This tripartite criterion—adapted from blockchain forensics standards—ensures that only projects exhibiting genuine "token death" are classified as scams. Transient phenomena (e.g., temporary liquidity shortages, failed launches, or legitimate sunsets) that do not meet all three conditions are **not** labeled as rug pulls, even if they appear suspicious. Projects labeled as legitimate demonstrate sustained on-chain activity and the absence of scam-related reports over an extended observation period (minimum one year). Ambiguous or borderline cases are excluded to preserve label reliability and analytical clarity.

4.3
Temporal Boundary

A core design principle of TM-RugPull is strict temporal integrity. All features are extracted prior to a predefined temporal anchor referred to as the
project midpoint
, representing the halfway point between project initiation and termination or last observed activity. This constraint ensures that no post-collapse or post-exposure information is included, thereby preventing temporal data leakage and enforcing a realistic early-warning setting.

4.4
Manual Curation and Quality Control

TM-RugPull is the product of extensive
manual curation and expert-led verification
. All 1,028 token projects were individually inspected by a team of three blockchain security analysts with domain expertise in DeFi, NFTs, and token forensics. Projects were sourced exclusively from
real, deployed smart contracts
on public blockchains (Ethereum, BSC, Polygon, Arbitrum, Fantom).
No synthetic, artificially generated, or simulated data
is included in the dataset. During the curation process, any project with incomplete on-chain records, inaccessible contracts, inconsistent metadata, or ambiguous behavioral patterns was
excluded from the final dataset
. This quality-control step removed over 200 borderline candidates, ensuring that only high-fidelity, analytically sound instances remain. Labels were assigned through a conservative, evidence-based protocol:

•

Scam
: Only if corroborated by at least two independent sources (e.g., CertiK, Rekt.news, De.Fi)
and
satisfying the formal rug-pull definition above.

•

Legitimate
: Required >1 year of sustained on-chain activity and absence of scam reports.

To minimize subjectivity, all ambiguous cases were reviewed by at least two analysts, and final labeling required consensus. This meticulous, human-in-the-loop approach guarantees label precision, domain representativeness, and resistance to hindsight bias.

4.5
Data Collection Pipeline

The dataset construction follows a multi-stage workflow that integrates heterogeneous data sources under a unified temporal framework. On-chain behavioral data are collected from blockchain platforms, off-chain signals are obtained from open-source intelligence sources, and label evidence is gathered from security reporting services. All data streams are temporally aligned at the project midpoint and subjected to verification before inclusion in the final dataset.

Illustrative Benchmarking

While earlier sections demonstrate that a substantial fraction of rug-pull incidents in the benchmark
originate from non-DeFi categories such as meme tokens, NFT-based projects, and impersonation-driven
schemes, the implications of this diversity extend beyond descriptive statistics. In operational
environments, detection systems are exposed to heterogeneous token types rather than narrowly defined
DeFi protocols, motivating an empirical illustration of the benefits of cross-domain coverage.

To isolate the effect of dataset composition, we construct a DeFi-only subset by filtering the benchmark
to include exclusively decentralized finance projects, thereby emulating the scope of prior DeFi-centric
datasets. All remaining components—feature definitions, temporal constraints, labeling protocol, and
preprocessing steps—are kept identical to ensure a controlled comparison. This design ensures that any
observed differences arise from dataset diversity rather than modeling or optimization artifacts.

Both the full benchmark and the DeFi-only subset are evaluated using identical lightweight tabular
classification pipelines, including logistic regression and random forest models. These classifiers
are intentionally selected to minimize architectural bias and emphasize dataset-level effects instead
of model complexity or hyperparameter tuning.

Figure 5:
Distribution of predicted scam probabilities under identical classification pipelines.
The full benchmark exhibits clearer separation between scam and legitimate projects compared to the
DeFi-only subset, indicating improved probability calibration and class separability induced by
cross-domain and multimodal data coverage.

Figure
5
illustrates the resulting probability distributions. When trained on the
full benchmark, predicted probabilities for scam and legitimate projects occupy largely disjoint
regions, with limited overlap near the decision boundary. This behavior reflects a more informative
and structured feature space that enables the classifiers to assign confident and well-calibrated
risk estimates.

In contrast, models trained on the DeFi-only subset produce substantially overlapping probability
distributions, with a high concentration of samples in intermediate probability ranges. This pattern
indicates increased classification uncertainty and weaker separability, even under identical learning
conditions.

Importantly, this comparison does not claim superiority of any particular model. Instead, it
demonstrates that the inclusion of heterogeneous token categories and temporally aligned OSINT signals
acts as a form of natural regularization. Exposure to diverse manipulation patterns reduces overfitting
to DeFi-specific artifacts and yields more stable probability estimates—an essential property for
early-warning and real-world risk-scoring systems.

Design Rationale and Integrity Guarantees

TM-RugPull is explicitly designed to support causally valid and operationally meaningful analysis. The core innovation lies in the enforcement of
temporal hygiene
: all features are extracted strictly before the project midpoint, a timestamp defined as the halfway point between project inception and termination. This policy ensures that no post-collapse information—such as price crashes, social media backlash, or liquidity drain—is included, thereby eliminating temporal data leakage and aligning the dataset with real-world early-warning constraints. Figure
6
validates that the midpoint is not biased toward early or late phases. The distribution of midpoint ratios (midpoint time / total lifespan) is centered near 0.5, confirming that our temporal boundary is applied consistently across projects with varying lifespans.

The integration of on-chain metrics with OSINT signals enables the capture of both structural manipulation patterns (e.g., token concentration) and coordinated promotional behavior (e.g., Twitter spikes). Additionally, domain diversity—from DeFi to meme tokens—mitigates overfitting to a single class of scams and improves robustness across heterogeneous project types. Together, these design choices provide strong guarantees against data leakage, hindsight bias, and domain-specific artifacts, making TM-RugPull suitable for training and evaluating models intended for real-world deployment.

Conclusion

TM-RugPull establishes a dataset-centric foundation for early-stage rug-pull analysis by combining multimodal observability with strict temporal integrity. Unlike prior resources, it is built exclusively from real deployed smart contracts—with no synthetic, artificially generated, or simulated data—and undergoes rigorous manual curation by a team of three blockchain experts. Projects exhibiting ambiguous behavior or low-quality data were explicitly excluded, ensuring only high-fidelity, analytically sound instances remain. Crucially, labeling adheres to a precise operational definition of rug pulls, and all scam cases were verified through

cross-referenced forensic reports (CertiK, Rekt.news, De.Fi) and multi-expert consensus. This human-in-the-loop approach guarantees label precision and eliminates false positives. Statistical analysis confirms that TM-RugPull captures meaningful discriminative signals: scam tokens exhibit significantly higher token concentration (
p
< 0.001, Mann-Whitney U test) and earlier OSINT spikes compared to legitimate projects. These findings translate into practical benefits: The deployment of TM-RugPull causes a significant decrease in the rate of false positives; it is due to the fact that it creates a clearer distinction between scam and legitimate projects. By aligning on-chain behavior, off-chain intelligence, and conservative labeling under a leakage-resistant framework, the dataset enables realistic, generalizable, and causally valid fraud detection research. We expect TM-RugPull to serve as a benchmark for future work in trustworthy blockchain analytics, early-warning systems, and multimodal anomaly detection in asynchronous, data-scarce environments.

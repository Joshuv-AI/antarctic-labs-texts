On the Maximum Size of Codes Under the Damerau-Levenshtein Metric
Zuo Ye, Gennian Ge

Abstract
The Damerau–Levenshtein distance between two sequences is the minimum number of operations (deletions, insertions, substitutions, and adjacent transpositions) required to convert one sequence into another. Notwithstanding a long history of this metric, research on error-correcting codes under this distance has remained limited. Recently, motivated by applications in DNA-based storage systems, Gabrys et al and Wang et al reinvigorated interest in this metric. In their works, some codes correcting both deletions and adjacent transpositions were constructed. However, theoretical upper bounds on code sizes under this metric have not yet been established. This paper seeks to establish upper bounds for code sizes in the Damerau–Levenshtein metric. Our results show that the code correcting one deletion and asymmetric adjacent transpositions proposed by Wang et al achieves optimal redundancy up to an additive constant.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{x}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}

\Sigma

. The Levenshtein distance (or edit distance) between two sequences is the minimum number of insertions, deletions and substitutions needed to transform one sequence into another. The Damerau–Levenshtein distance additionally allows transpositions.

[

, Tenengolts in 1984 constructed a non-binary code correcting one insertion/deletion
[
4

. Primarily driven by applications in DNA-based storage systems
[

,

, racetrack memories
[

,

[

. This type of errors (known as peak-shifts) also occur in magnetic recording systems

Although there are a lot of works focusing on codes under the Levenshtein metric or codes combating only transpositions, there are limited results on the interaction between insertions/deletions/substitutions and transpositions. As far as we know, prior to

Prompted by applications in DNA-based storage systems, Gabrys

Regarding the binary alphabet

The aforementioned two works predominantly concentrated on constructions of codes. Upper bounds on cardinality of respective codes remain unknown, except the asymptotic upper bound on the maximum size of binary codes correcting
t
deletions of symbol

s and

transpositions given in

At last, we upper bound the size of codes correcting deletions and asymmetric transpositions. Our results show that the redundancy of a code is at least

General framework for deriving upper bounds

In

,
VI
and
VII
, we will apply the framework in
[

]
to derive upper bounds on the maximum size of specific codes. In this subsection, we briefly describe this framework.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)
be a hypergraph, where
\mathcal{V}=\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}
is the set of vertices and
\mathcal{E}=\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right\}
is the set of hyperedges. Here, each
E_{i}
is a non-empty subset of
\mathcal{V}
. Define an
n\times m
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
as
a_{i,j}=1
if
v_{i}\in E_{j}
and
a_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
. If

for any distinct

, we call
\mathcal{M}
a matching in
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\nu\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be the number of hyperedges in the largest matching. It holds that

\nu\left(\mathcal{H}\right)=\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}z_{j}\leq 1,\forall 1\leq i\leq n,\text{ where }z_{j}\in\{0,1\},\forall 1\leq j\leq m\right\}.

(1)

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a channel, which introduces certain errors to sequences passing through it. For
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x})
be the set of all
possible outputs when
\mathbf{x}
passes through
\mathscr{C}
. For example, if
\mathscr{C}
introduces
s
deletions and at most
t
transpositions, then
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{B}_{s,t}(\mathbf{x})
. Suppose that
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\Sigma_{q}^{m}
. We can define a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{C}}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)
, where
\mathcal{V}=\Sigma_{q}^{m}
and
\mathcal{E}=\left\{\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}\right\}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sigma_{q}^{n}
be a code which can correct errors introduced by channel
\mathscr{C}
. Then it is necessary that
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x})\cap\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}\left(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right)
for any distinct
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. In other words, the set
\left\{\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}\right\}
is a matching in the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{\mathscr{C}}
.

Next, we define a matrix
A=\left(a_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}\right)
, whose rows are indexed by
\Sigma_{q}^{m}
and columns are indexed by
\Sigma_{q}^{n}
. For
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}
, the entry

is given by

0,\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By (
1
), we have

\left|\mathcal{C}\right|\leq\max\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}}u_{\mathbf{x}}:\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}}a_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}\cdot u_{\mathbf{x}}\leq 1,\forall\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m},\text{ where }u_{\mathbf{x}}\in\{0,1\},\forall\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}\right\}.

(2)

Then it follows from (
2
) and
[
37
, Section II]
that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}}w_{\mathbf{y}}:\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}}a_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}\cdot w_{\mathbf{y}}\geq 1,\forall\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n},\text{ where }w_{\mathbf{y}}\geq 0,\forall\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}}w_{\mathbf{y}}:\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathbf{x})}w_{\mathbf{y}}\geq 1,\forall\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n},\text{ where }w_{\mathbf{y}}\geq 0,\forall\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{m}\right\}.

(3)

It is easy to see that, in this framework, the critical step is to find a suitable

for each
\mathbf{y}
.

III-A

Intersection of two single-transposition balls

In this subsection, it is always assumed that

Claim III.1

Let

\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathbf{y})

Let

and write

\begin{array}[]{l}r_{1}^{\prime}=\left|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\prime}\right|=\left|\left\{1<i<r:l_{i}=1,a_{i-1}=a_{i+1}\right\}\right|,\\

r_{2}^{in}=\left|\left\{1\leq i\leq r-2:l_{i+1}=2,a_{i}=a_{i+2}\right\}\right|.\end{array}

The following theorem establishes that the size of

is completely determined by the aforementioned parameters.

Theorem III.2
.

For a sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
, let
r
,
r_{1}^{\prime}
,
r_{1}^{\prime\prime}
,
r_{1}^{side}
,
r_{\geq 2}
,
r_{1}^{pair}
and
r_{2}^{in}
be as above. Then we have

\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|=r^{2}-4r_{1}^{\prime}-r_{1}^{\prime\prime}-r_{1}^{side}-r_{1}^{pair}-r_{2}^{in}.

(9)

This implies that

\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq\begin{cases}\max\{r(r-1),1\},\mbox{ if }r_{1}^{\prime}=0,\\
r(r-2),\mbox{ if }r_{1}^{\prime}=1,\\
r(r-5)+9,\mbox{ if }r_{1}^{\prime}\geq 2.\end{cases}

In particular, we have
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq r(r-5)+9
whenever
r\geq 3
.

Proof:

By
Corollary

III.1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\mathcal{T}_{\leq 1}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\right)\right|=r\cdot r_{\geq 2}+(r-2)r_{1}^{\prime}+(r-1)\left(r_{1}^{side}+r_{1}^{\prime\prime}\right).

(10)

By
Theorem

III.1
and
Lemma

III.4
, we have

\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq r}\left|\mathcal{T}_{\leq 1}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(i_{1})}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}_{\leq 1}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(i_{2})}\right)\right|=2r_{1}^{\prime}+2r_{1}^{pair}+r_{2}^{in}.

(11)

By
Theorem

III.1
and
Lemma

III.5
, we have

\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}\leq r}\left|\bigcap_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{T}_{\leq 1}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(i_{j})}\right)\right|=r_{1}^{pair}.

(12)

Now
Equation

9
follows from
Equations

7
,
10
,
11
and
12
,
Lemma

III.2
and the fact that
r=r_{\geq 2}+r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime\prime}+r_{1}^{side}
.

By definition, we have
r_{1}^{pair}\leq\max\left\{r_{1}^{\prime}-1,0\right\}
. When
r_{1}^{\prime}=0
, it follows from (
9
) that
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|-r(r-1)=r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}\geq 0
. Also, note that
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq 1
for any
\mathbf{x}
.
Next, suppose that

. Then we have
r\geq 3
. Therefore, we have

\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|-r(r-2)=r_{1}^{\prime\prime}+r_{1}^{side}+2r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}-2\geq r_{1}^{side}+r_{\geq 2}+r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}-2\geq r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}\geq 0

At last, suppose

\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})\right|-r(r-5)-1\geq 4r_{1}^{side}+4r_{1}^{\prime\prime}+5r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}\geq 4r_{1}^{side}+4r_{\geq 2}+r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}\geq 4r_{1}^{side}+8-4r_{1}^{side}+r_{\geq 2}-r_{2}^{in}\geq 8

We now investigate the size of

. Therefore, instead of giving an exact formula for
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,t}(\mathbf{x})\right|
, we aim to bound the size of
1
-deletion-
t
-transposition balls.

Recall that in the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathbf{x})
, the
t
adjacent transpositions may occur sequentially. To get a lower bound on
\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathbf{x})\right|
, it is convenient to consider the case where all
t
transpositions occur simultaneously. Let
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})
be the set of all sequences obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by
exactly

t

simultaneous
transpositions. In other words, the
t
transposed pairs do not overlap with each other. It is clear that
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathbf{x})
.

For each
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})
, there exist
k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}
satisfying
k_{i+1}-k_{i}>2
, such that
\mathbf{y}
is obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by transposing
x_{k_{i}}
and
x_{k_{i+1}}
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. For this reason, we also write
\mathbf{y}=T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}}(\mathbf{x})
. The next lemma will be helpful in the proof of

III.7
and

.

Lemma III.6
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
. Suppose that integers
1\leq k_{1},\ldots,k_{t},l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}<n
satisfy
k_{i+1}-k_{i},l_{i+1}-l_{i}>2
for all
i
and
x_{k_{i}}\neq x_{k_{i+1}}
,
x_{l_{i}}\neq x_{l_{i+1}}
for all
i
. If
\left(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}\right)\neq\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}\right)
, then
T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}}(\mathbf{x})\neq T_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}}(\mathbf{x})
.

Proof:

Let
\mathbf{y}=T_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{z}=T_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}}(\mathbf{x})
. Both
\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{z}
are obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by altering exactly
2t

positions. Note that each adjacent transposition alters exactly two positions. Therefore, if
\left(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}\right)\neq\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}\right)
, there must be some
i
, such that at least one of
x_{k_{i}}
and
x_{k_{i+1}}
is not affected by the
t
simultaneous transpositions at positions
l_{1},\ldots,l_{t}
. This implies that
y_{k_{i}}\neq z_{k_{i}}
or
y_{k_{i+1}}\neq z_{k_{i+1}}
. Now the proof is completed.
∎

By convention, let
\binom{m}{n}
denote the binomial coefficients, where
m,n
are integers and
0\leq n\leq m
. In addition, we set
\binom{m}{0}=1
for any integer
m
, and
\binom{m}{n}=0
for other values of
m
and
n
.
Regarding the size of
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})
, we have the following lemma, which is implicit in the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.1]
. Since
[

]

Lemma III.7
.

[
32
]

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
be a sequence with
r
runs, where
r\geq 2t+1
. Then it holds that

\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor}{i}\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor-2i-1}{t-i}.

(13)

In particular, we have
\left|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq\binom{\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}{t}\geq\left(\frac{r-1}{2t}\right)^{t}

Proof:

Recall that each sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
can be written as
\mathbf{x}=a_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{l_{r}}
, where
a_{1}^{l_{1}},\ldots,a_{r}^{l_{r}}
are all distinct runs in
\mathbf{x}
. If
i
is odd (or even), we call
a_{i}^{l_{i}}
an odd-numbered (or even-numbered) run. Denote
I_{o}=\left\{i:1\leq i\leq r,i\text{ is odd}\right\}
. Clearly, we have
\left|I_{o}\right|\geq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
.

Note that each of the
t

We can choose
t

0\leq u\leq\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor
, choose

. For each

, transpose

and
a_{i_{k}+1}
. In other words, we transpose the right-most symbol in the

-th run with the left-most symbol in the
(i_{k}+1)
-th run.
Next, choose

. For each

a_{j_{k}}
and
a_{j_{k}-1}

-th run.

It is clear from the choice of

that the

Lemma

, different choices of
\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{u},j_{1},\ldots,j_{t-u}\right)
result in different sequences in
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})
. Then the proof of (

\left|\left\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{u}\right\}\cup\left\{i_{1}+2,\ldots,i_{u}+2\right\}\cup\{1\}\right|\leq 2u+1
. The second lower bound on

for any

.
∎

Theorem III.3
.

Let
t\geq 1
be an integer and
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
be a sequence with

Proof:

. Therefore, we have
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,t}(\mathbf{x})\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\mathcal{T}_{\leq t}\left(\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\right)\right|\leq r^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}(r+2i)
. This proves the upper bound.

whenever

. Then it follows that
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1,t}(\mathbf{x})\right|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}\right|
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{(i)}=\cdots a_{i}^{l_{i}-1}a_{i+1}^{l_{i+1}}\cdots a_{j-1}^{l_{j-1}}a_{j}a_{j}^{l_{j}-1}\cdots,

(14)

(15)

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper, we upper bound the size of codes under the Damerau-Levenshtein metric. Our results show that when the total number
t
of all errors is a constant, then the redundancy of a code is at least
t\log n-O(1)
. This proves that the redundancy of the code correcting one deletion and asymmetric transpositions constructed in
[
36
]
is optimal up to a constant.

Regarding transpositions and deletions of arbitrary symbols, existing works
[
33
,
36
]
focused exclusively on codes correcting one deletion and multiple transpositions. The problem to construct codes capable of correcting multiple deletions and multiple transpositions with redundancy close to
t\log n-O(1)
is still open. In addition, both works focused on binary codes. It is also interesting to construct codes over non-binary alphabets. At last, constructing codes capable of correcting all four types of errors is also an interesting problem. We leave these three problems for future research.

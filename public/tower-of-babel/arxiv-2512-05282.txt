Entropic selection for optimal transport on the line with distance cost
Armand Ley

Abstract

Key words and phrases.

optimal transport, entropic regularization, decomposition theorem, stochastic orders.

Abstract:

We study the small-regularisation limit of the entropic optimal transport problem on the line with distance cost. While convergence of entropic minimizers is well understood in the discrete setting and in the case where the cost is continuous and there is a unique optimal transport plan, the question of existence and characterization outside these settings remains largely open. We propose a natural candidate for the limiting object and establish its convergence under mutual singularity of the marginals. For arbitrary marginals, we moreover prove that every limit point of entropic minimizers obeys a structural condition known as weak multiplicativity. The construction of our candidate relies on a decomposition theorem for optimal transport plan that we believe is of independent interest. This article complements the previous work of Di Marino and Louet
[
10
]
.

Introduction

1.1
On the Monge-Kantorovitch problem and its entropic regularization

In recent decades, optimal transport theory has attracted considerable attention, due to its many connections to probability, analysis, geometry and other areas of mathematics (see, e.g.,
[
31
,
30
,
26
,
2
,
25
]
). In this paper, we investigate one of the most elementary optimal transport problems, namely the
L^{1}
Monge-Kantorovich problem on the real line. Given two probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
, this problem consists of minimizing the global transport cost function

J:\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y-x|\ \mathrm{d}\pi(x,y),

over the set
\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)
of measures that satisfy the marginal constraints
\pi(A\times\mathbb{R})=\mu(A)
and
\pi(\mathbb{R}\times B)=\nu(B)
for every pair
(A,B)
of Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. If
|y-x|
is replaced by
|y-x|^{p}
for some
p>1
in the definition of the cost function
J
, then the set of minimizers of
J
, known as optimal transport plans, reduces to a singleton which consists of the well-known (co)monotone transport. This transport plan, also known as quantile transport plan, is defined as
(G_{\mu},G_{\nu})_{\#}\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}]0,1[}\right)
, where
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}]0,1[}
stands for the restriction of the Lebesgue measure to
]0,1[
, while
G_{\mu}
and
G_{\nu}
denote the quantile functions of
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively. For
p=1
uniqueness of an optimal transport plan does not hold, and describing the set
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
of optimal transport plans becomes a delicate problem which will address later through a decomposition result. The lack of uniqueness of optimal transport plans for the distance cost raises the broader question of selection under perturbation. Entropic regularisation, the most prominent perturbation of the Monge–Kantorovich problem, provides a natural framework in which to address this issue. Given a regularization parameter
\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0
, the entropic optimal transport problem consists in minimizing the regularized cost function

J_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y-x|\ \mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)+\mathbb{\varepsilon}\textnormal{Ent}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu),

where the relative entropy
\textnormal{Ent}(\cdot|\mu\otimes\nu)
is defined as follows:

\textnormal{Ent}(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\nu}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\nu}\ \mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\nu&\text{if }\pi\ll\mu\otimes\nu\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise }\end{cases}.

When
\mathbb{\varepsilon}=0
, we recover the usual optimal transport problem. Heuristically, since
\textnormal{Ent}(\cdot|\mu\otimes\nu)
can be interpreted as a measure of divergence from
\mu\otimes\nu
, the entropic term in
J_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
encourages the minimizer
\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:=\textnormal{argmin}J_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
to balance two competing effects: minimizing the transport cost
and
being as close as possible to the product measure. This regularization, which applies to more general cost functions, was popularized by Cuturi
[
9
]
and has since become a highly active area of research. Its main appeal is likely due to the fact that it transforms the optimal transport problem —which solutions are hard to compute in high dimensions— into a computationally tractable problem. Indeed, adding this regularization term allows the use of robust and efficient algorithms, such as the celebrated Sinkhorn algorithm
[
27
]
. We refer to
[
24
]
and the numerous references therein for a comprehensive overview of the computational aspects related to optimal transport and to
[
20
]
for a general introduction to entropic optimal transport theory.

Since entropic optimal transport is viewed as an approximation of classical optimal transport, understanding its behaviour as the regularization parameter
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
tends to
0^{+}
is of critical importance. Beyond its computational applications, several aspects of this convergence have been studied, including the convergence of minimizers, convergence of solutions to the associated dual problem, stability with respect to the data (marginals and cost function), and convergence rate estimates. In this paper, we are interested in the question of the convergence of the minimizers of
J_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
. We refer to the works
[
8
,
17
]
in the context of the squared Euclidean distance, where
\Gamma
-convergence methods are used to prove the convergence of the entropic minimizers to the unique optimal transport plan. This result has been extended in
[
5
]
, where the authors show that for continuous cost functions, every cluster point of entropic minimizers is an optimal transport plan: in particular, if there exists a unique optimal transport plan, we obtain the convergence toward this transport plan. In the case of measures with finite support, it is known
[
24
, Proposition 4.1]
that entropic minimizers converge to
\textnormal{argmin}_{\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)}\textnormal{Ent}(\cdot|\mu\otimes\nu).
Outside these cases, proving the convergence and finding a characterization of the potential limit remains an open question and an active area of research. This selection problem can be reformulated as follows: is an optimal transport plan selected by entropic regularization, and if so, how can it be characterized? The most elementary setting in which this selection problem arises is the distance cost on the real line:
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
is generally not a singleton, and optimal transport plans are not even absolutely continuous with respect to the product measure.

1.2
A decomposition result for optimal transport plans

By a decomposition result for
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
, we mean a representation of any optimal transport plan as a sum of simpler components that can be analysed independently. More specifically, we construct a set
\mathcal{D}=\left\{(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})\penalty\ ;\penalty\ i\in\mathcal{I}\right\}
of pairs of measures such that, for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, the set
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})
admits a tractable description and, for every
\pi\in\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
, there exists a unique family
(\pi_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\in\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{O}(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})
such that
\pi=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\pi_{i}
. This motivates the following notation.

Notation 1.1

(Minkowski sum and direct sum of set of measures)
.

Given a countable family
(E_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
of subsets of
\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we denote by
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}E_{i}
the image of the map
\phi:(\pi_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\in\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}E_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\pi_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).
If
\phi
is injective, we write
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}E_{i}
instead of
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}E_{i}
.

With this notation, our decomposition theorem becomes
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{O}(\mu_{i},\nu_{i}).
Strictly speaking, the decomposition is formulated in the more general setting of cyclical monotone transport plans (see Theorem
2.37
). In the case of measures without atoms and with compact support, it coincides with a previous result by Di Marino and Louet
[
10
, Proposition
3.1
]
. To motivate our decomposition theorem and clarify the exposition of the decomposition procedure, we briefly present a standard decomposition result for the set of transport plans satisfying the martingale constraint.

Decomposition theorem for the set of martingale transport plans

A classical theorem (see Strassen
[
28
]
) states that the set
\textnormal{Mart}(\mu,\nu)
of martingale transport plans, i.e. the transport plans
\pi
that admit a disintegration of the form
\pi(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)=\mu(\mathrm{d}x)k_{x}(\mathrm{d}y)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}yk_{x}(\mathrm{d}y)=x
, is non-empty if and only if the measures
\mu
and
\nu
are in the convex order, meaning that the potential functions
u_{\mu}:t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x-t|\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)
and
u_{\nu}:t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x-t|\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)
satisfy
u_{\mu}\leq u_{\nu}
.
2
2

2

The measure
\mu
is smaller than
\nu
in the convex order if
\int f\ \mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int f\ \mathrm{d}\nu
for all convex function
f
. This characterization trough the ordering of potential functions holds only for measures on
\mathbb{R}
. In the following, we refer to a statement as a
Strassen-type
result if it characterizes a stochastic order via the existence of a transport plan satisfying structural constraints.
Assuming
u_{\mu}\leq u_{\nu}
, let
(I_{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
be the family of connected components of
\{u_{\mu}<u_{\nu}\}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ u_{\mu}(x)<u_{\nu}(x)\right\}
, and define the components of
\mu
by setting
\mu^{=}=\mu_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\{u_{\mu}=u_{\nu}\}}
and
\mu_{k}=\mu_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}I_{k}}

[
4
, Theorem A.4]
proved that there exists a unique family
((\nu_{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}},\nu^{=})
such that
\nu=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\nu_{k}+\nu^{=}
,
u_{\mu^{=}}\leq u_{\nu^{=}}
and
u_{\mu_{k}}\leq u_{\nu_{k}}
for each
k\in\mathcal{K}
. Moreover, the inequality
u_{\mu_{k}}<u_{\nu_{k}}
is satisfied in the interior of the support of
\mu
and
\nu
, and they established that
\textnormal{Mart}(\mu,\nu)
admits the following decomposition:

\textnormal{Mart}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\bigoplus_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\textnormal{Mart}(\mu_{k},\nu_{k})\right)\oplus\textnormal{Mart}(\mu^{=},\nu^{=}).

The proof of this result mainly relies on the fact that the set

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ \forall\pi\in\textnormal{Mart}(\mu,\nu),\pi(\left(]-\infty,x[\times[x,+\infty[\right)\uplus\left(]x,+\infty[\times]-\infty,x]\right))=0\right\}

coincides precisely with the set where the potential functions are equal. In higher dimensions, although the situation is notably more intricate, similar results have recently been established
[
13
,
19
,
22
]
.

1.3
Back to the selection problem

Convergence of minimizers for the
L^{1}
entropic regularization on the real line

Our first contribution to the study of the selection problem for the distance cost on the line is to apply our decomposition result for
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
and a Strassen-type theorem due to Kellerer
[
15
]
to build a natural candidate
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
to be the limit of the entropic minimizers
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
. This optimal transport plan
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
is characterized by the fact that it is as close from the product measure as possible in the following sense: each positive (respectively negative) component of
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
is strongly multiplicative, meaning that it coincides with the restriction of a product measure on
\mathrm{F}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ x\leq y\right\}
(respectively on
\tilde{\mathrm{F}}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ x\geq y\right\}
). We conjecture that
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
converges to
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
when
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}
. In
[
10
, Theorem
4.1
]
, the authors proved that
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
converges to an optimal transport whose components are strongly multiplicative, under the assumption that
\mu
and
\nu
are compactly supported, have finite entropy with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
, and there exists
\pi\in\mathcal{O}(\mu-\mu^{=},\nu-\nu^{=})
with finite entropy with respect to
\mu\otimes\nu
(see Sub-subsection
3.2.2
).
7
7

7

Along with some additional technical assumption, see Theorem
3.9
for the precise statement.
Our main convergence result is stated as follows.

Theorem B
.

Consider a pair
(\mu,\nu)
of probability measures satisfying one of the two following hypothesis:

1.

\mu
and
\nu
both have finite support;

2.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
(respectively
k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
),
(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
(resp.
(\mu_{k}^{-},\nu_{k}^{-})
) forms a pair of mutually singular measures.

Then
\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\xrightarrow[\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}]{}\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu).

This covers the case where one measure is atomic and the other has no atoms. In the general case the conjecture is still open, but we prove the following result.

Theorem C
.

Let
(\mu,\nu)
be a pair of probability measures. If
\pi^{*}
is a cluster point of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
, then every restriction of
\pi^{*}
to a product set contained in
\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ x\leq y\right\}
or
\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ x\geq y\right\}
coincides with a product measure.

This property, known as weak multiplicativity was introduced by Kellerer
[
15
]
, and its “strict version” has proven useful in studying the Markov property of real-valued point processes
[
16
]
.

Decomposition and regularized problem in higher dimension

In the Monge–Kantorovich problem on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with cost given by the Euclidean norm, a classical decomposition strategy due to Sudakov
[
29
]
has proven instrumental to establish the existence of an optimal transport map. The core idea of this decomposition is to disintegrate the measures
\mu
and
\nu
into families
(\mu_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
and
(\nu_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
of measures concentrated on one-dimensional “transport rays”. This allows one to consider, on each transport ray
\sigma
, a solution
\pi_{\sigma}
to the one-dimensional transport from
\mu_{\sigma}
to
\nu_{\sigma}
. The delicate step consists in gluing the
\pi_{\sigma}
together into an optimal transport plan from
\mu
to
\nu
. Assuming
\mu\ll\mathcal{L}^{n}
, if each
\pi_{\sigma}
is chosen as the monotone transport between
\mu_{\sigma}
and
\nu_{\sigma}
, this gluing procedure is well-defined and yields an optimal transport
map
. For further details on this decomposition, we refer the reader to the lecture notes by Ambrosio
[
1
]
as well as to the textbooks
[
26
, Sections 3.1.3 and 3.1.4 ]
and
[
18
, Chapter 18]
(which contains an extensive bibliographical note on the historical development of this decomposition).
The idea of decomposing
\mu
and
\nu
along maximal transport rays has proven itself essential in addressing the selection problem arising in the study of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
entropic optimal transport for the Euclidean distance cost
c(x,y)=\left\|y-x\right\|
with
d\geq 2
. According to a recent preprint of Aryan and Ghosal
[
3
]
, if
X
and
Y
are compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
d(X,Y)=\inf\left(\left\{\left\|y-x\right\|\penalty\ ;\penalty\ x\in X,y\in Y\right\}\right)>0
, and
\mu
and
\nu
are probability measures on
X
and
Y
with smooth densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then the family
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
converges to an optimal transport plan
\pi^{\text{opt}}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a decomposition into maximal transport rays
\sigma
, with respect to which the optimal coupling
\pi^{\text{opt}}
admits the following variational characterization. For all
\sigma

\pi^{\text{opt}}

\inf_{\pi_{\sigma}\in\textnormal{Marg}(\mu_{\sigma},\nu_{\sigma})}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(c_{\sigma}|y-x|-\frac{d-1}{2}\log(2\pi|y-x|)\right)\mathrm{d}\pi_{\sigma}(x,y)+\textnormal{Ent}(\pi_{\sigma}|\mu_{\sigma}\otimes\nu_{\sigma}).

(2)

1.4
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we focus on the decomposition theorem for optimal transport plans. We begin by presenting the result of Di Marino and Louet (Proposition
2.1
) in the case of atomless marginals. This serves both to build intuition for the reader and to introduce the tools that will be used throughout the paper. After recalling the notions of cyclically monotone transport plans (Definition
2.3
) and barrier points (Definition
2.8
), we provide a concise alternative proof of their result. The argument relies on the fact that the points where
F_{\mu}^{+}=F_{\nu}^{+}
are barrier points (Proposition
2.9
), together with a characterization of optimal transport plans for stochastically ordered marginals (Proposition
2.11
). Building on these ingredients, we obtain our decomposition theorem for atomless marginals (Proposition
2.14
). We then extend this result to arbitrary marginals, including those with atoms. The first step is a technical lemma showing that the relative position of the cumulative distribution functions constrains the regions where optimal transport plans may concentrate (Proposition
2.15
). We next introduce the components of the real line (Definition
2.18
), of the marginals (Definition
2.22
), and of an optimal transport plan (Definition
2.26
). We subsequently prove that the components of an optimal transport plan have the appropriate marginals (Proposition
2.29
). After introducing the reinforced stochastic order (Definition
2.34
), we show that any two distinct marginal components are ordered w.r.t. this order (Proposition
2.36
). These results culminate in our decomposition theorem for
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
(Theorem
2.37
). Finally, we discuss several applications of the decomposition. After recalling the Strassen-type theorem for
\leq_{\mathrm{F}}
, we define candidate
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
(Definition
2.43
). We then provide a characterization of barrier points in terms of cumulative distribution functions (Proposition
2.44
), and conclude by proving that a transport plan is optimal if and only if it is supported on a family of transport paths that do not “freely” cross (Proposition
2.48
).

In Section
3
, we study the behaviour of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
as
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}
. We first use an adaptation of a result of Di Marino and Louet (Lemma
3.5
), together with the fact that the relative entropy of an optimal transport plan equals the sum of the entropies of its components (Proposition
3.23
), to establish that
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
minimizes the relative entropy among all optimal transport plans (Theorem
3.6
). This immediately implies that, whenever there exists an optimal transport plan with finite entropy, the family
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
converges to
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu).
We then introduce the notion of (large) weak multiplicativity (Definition
3.10
) and prove that this property is stable under weak convergence of transport plans (Proposition
3.14
). As a consequence, every cluster point of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
is weakly multiplicative (Theorem
3.16
). Finally, after introducing strict versions of the reinforced stochastic order, of weak multiplicativity, and of strong multiplicativity, we prove that if the non-equal pairs of components of
(\mu,\nu)
are mutually singular, then
\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
converges to
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)
as
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}
. This result in particular covers the case of semi-discrete pairs of measures.

The decomposition result.

2.1
The structure result of Di Marino–Louet

Before presenting our decomposition, we examine the result by Simone Di Marino and Jean Louet regarding the structure of optimal transport plans, when
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
is a pair of atomless marginals
[
10
, Proposition
3.1
]
. Although their result is not originally presented as a decomposition of the space
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
in direct sums of subspaces, we shall see that the proof of this result leads to our decomposition result (for atomless measures). When
\mu
and
\nu
are atomless, the functions
F_{\mu}^{+}
and
F_{\nu}^{+}
are continuous; therefore, the sets
\{F_{\mu}^{+}>F_{\nu}^{+}\}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ F_{\mu}^{+}(x)>F_{\nu}^{+}(x)\right\}
and
\{F_{\mu}^{+}<F_{\nu}^{+}\}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ F_{\mu}^{+}(x)<F_{\nu}^{+}(x)\right\}
are open and consists of a countable family of open, connected components. Let
(]a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}[)_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}
and
(]a_{k}^{-},b_{k}^{-}[)_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}
denote the connected components of
\{F_{\mu}^{+}>F_{\nu}^{+}\}
and
\{F_{\mu}^{+}<F_{\nu}^{+}\}
, respectively. Using our notation, the result by Di Marino and Louet can be stated as follows.

Proposition 2.1

(Structure result by Di Marino–Louet)
.

Assume that
\mu
and
\nu
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
are atomless and have compact support. For all
\pi\in\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
, there exists a set
S_{\pi}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\pi(S_{\pi})=1
and:

1.

[S_{\pi}\cap(\{F_{\mu}^{+}=F_{\nu}^{+}\}\times\mathbb{R})]\subset\mathrm{D}
;

2.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
,
[S_{\pi}\cap(]a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}[\times\mathbb{R})]\subset\mathrm{F}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times]a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}[)
;

3.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
,
[S_{\pi}\cap(]a_{k}^{-},b_{k}^{-}[\times\mathbb{R})]\subset\tilde{\mathrm{F}}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times]a_{k}^{-},b_{k}^{-}[)
.

Interpreting a transport plan
\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)
as a mass displacement from the distribution
\mu
to the distribution
\nu
, a point
(x,y)
represents a transport path and
\pi(x,y)
the amount of mass displaced from
x
to
y
. Observe that
\mathrm{F}
represents forward transports,
\tilde{\mathrm{F}}
represents backward transports, and
\mathrm{D}
corresponds to fixed mass. From this perspective, Point
1
of Proposition
2.1
states that the mass on the set
\{F_{\mu}^{+}=F_{\nu}^{+}\}
remains fixed under any optimal transport for the distance cost. Point
2
(resp. Point
3
) of Proposition
2.1
states that for an optimal displacement, the mass of
\mu(dx)
on a connected component of
\{F_{\mu}^{+}>F_{\nu}^{+}\}
(resp.
\{F_{\mu}^{+}<F_{\nu}^{+}\}
) remains within the same component and moves forward (resp. backward). Interpreting a transport as a measure on the plan
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Proposition
2.1
can be viewed as an assertion about the concentration region of elements of
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu).
Specifically, every element of
\mathcal{O}(\mu,\nu)
is concentrated on the following disjoint union of triangles and diagonal parts:

\biguplus_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}(\mathrm{F}\cap]a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}[^{2})\biguplus\biguplus_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}(\tilde{\mathrm{F}}\cap]a_{k}^{-},b_{k}^{-}[^{2})\biguplus\left(\mathrm{D}\cap\{F_{\mu}^{+}=F_{\nu}^{+}\}^{2}\right).

We propose a brief alternative proof of Proposition
2.1
, which will provide the opportunity to introduce notions used throughout the article. Our proof relies on cyclical monotonicity, and does not use the construction and manipulation of an explicit Kantorovich potential that appears in the proof of Proposition
3.1
in
[

. Cyclical monotonicity is a standard tool in optimal transport theory: for further details, we refer to the monograph
[
31

Definition 2.3
.

A set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is said to be cyclically monotone if, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
((x_{i},y_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\in\Gamma^{n}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i}-x_{i}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i+1}-x_{i}|
(with the convention
y_{n+1}=y_{1}
). A measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is said to be cyclically monotone if it is concentrated on a cyclically monotone set.

Notation 2.4
.

For any pair
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, define
\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
as the set of cyclically monotone measures in
\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

Remark 2.5
.

For any pair
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{2}
,
\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is non-empty. Indeed, denoting by
G_{\gamma_{1}}
and
G_{\gamma_{2}}
the quantile functions of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, respectively, the reader may verify that the monotone transport plan
(G_{\gamma_{1}},G_{\gamma_{2}})_{\#}\left(\mathds{1}_{[0,1]}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{1}\right)
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

The following result is classical; see
[
31
, Theorem
5.10
]
for a proof with more general cost functions.

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
and
W_{1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})<+\infty
. Then
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

The following remark states that a sub-measure of a cyclically monotone transport plan remains cyclically monotone for its own marginals. We next derive a corresponding result for optimal transport plans. For any measurable space
(E,\mathcal{T})
and any pair
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(E)^{2}
, we write
\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
if, for all
A\in\mathcal{T}
,
\gamma_{1}(A)\leq\gamma_{2}(A)
.

Remark 2.7

(Cyclicity and optimality of sub-measures)
.

1.

Consider
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
,
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).
If
\pi^{*}\leq\pi
, then
\pi^{*}\in\mathfrak{C}({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*}).
Indeed, if
\Gamma
is a cyclically monotone set on which
\pi
is concentrated, then
\pi^{*}
is also concentrated on
\Gamma.

2.

Consider
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
such that
W_{1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})<+\infty
,
\pi\in\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
, and
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
\pi^{*}\leq\pi,
then
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{O}({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*}).
Indeed, since

W_{1}\left({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*}\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y-x|\ \mathrm{d}\pi^{*}(x,y)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y-x|\ \mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)=W_{1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})<+\infty,

by Theorem
2.6
,
\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\mathfrak{C}({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*})=\mathcal{O}({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*})
. Thus,
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{O}({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi^{*},{p_{2}}_{\#}\pi^{*})
directly follows from the previous point.

Definition 2.8

(Barrier points)

For all

if every

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
W_{1}(\mu,\nu)<+\infty.
The inclusion
\{F_{\mu}^{+}=F_{\nu}^{+}\}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mu,\nu)

This proposition is an important step toward the proof of Proposition

1/4

Definition 2.10

(Stochastic Order)
.

We say that
\gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
is smaller than
\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
in the stochastic order if, for all bounded increasing function

\gamma_{1}\leq_{\textnormal{st}}\gamma_{2}
.

It is well known that
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\textnormal{st}}\gamma_{2}
if and only if both
\gamma_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\gamma_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}\geq F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}.
We now recall the Strassen-type theorem for the order
\leq_{\textnormal{st}}
, along with the characterization of the set of transport plans for measures in the stochastic order. We refer the reader to

\leq\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j=i}^{\sigma(i)-1}|y_{j+1}-x_{j}|\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i+1}-x_{i}|.

Thus,

is cyclically monotone.∎

\pi([t,+\infty[\times]-\infty,t])\pi([t,x]\times]-\infty,t\wedge y])=\pi([t,x]\times]-\infty,t])\pi([t,+\infty[\times]-\infty,t\wedge y]).

From Proposition
3.12
, it follows that
\pi
is weakly multiplicative.
∎

3.3.2
Weak multiplicativity of cluster points of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}

The following lemma provides us with information on the global form of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
-cyclically invariant transport plans. Its proof is relatively straightforward and can be found in
[
20
, Lemma
2.7
]
by taking
R(dx,dy)=\exp(-|y-x|/\mathbb{\varepsilon})\mu(dx)\nu(dy).

Lemma 3.15
.

Consider
\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)
and
\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0.
The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\pi
is
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
-cyclically monotone;

2.

There exists two measurable maps
\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mu\otimes\nu}(x,y)=\exp\left(\frac{\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)+\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(y)-|y-x|}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)\hskip 28.45274pt(\mu\otimes\nu)(dx,dy)-a.s.\ .

Theorem 3.16
.

Consider
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{2}
. If
\pi^{*}
is a cluster point of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
, then
\pi^{*}
is a weakly multiplicative measure. From Proposition
3.7
, it follows that cluster points of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
are weakly multiplicative elements of
\mathfrak{C}(\mu,\nu)
.

Proof.

Consider
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0
. By Lemma
3.15
, there exists
\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable such that

\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(dx,dy)=\exp\left(\frac{\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)+\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(y)-|y-x|}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)\mu(dx)\nu(dy).

Thus, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathds{1}_{]-\infty,t]\times[t,+\infty[}\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}=(f_{1}^{t}\mu)\otimes(f_{2}^{t}\nu)

and

\mathds{1}_{[t,+\infty[\times]-\infty,t]}\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}=(f_{3}^{t}\mu)\otimes(f_{4}^{t}\nu),

where the maps
f_{1}^{t},f_{2}^{t},f_{3}^{t},f_{4}^{t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by

\displaystyle f_{1}^{t}(x)=\mathds{1}_{]-\infty,t]}(x)\exp\left(\frac{\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)+x}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle f_{2}^{t}(y)=\mathds{1}_{[t,+\infty[}(y)\exp\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(y)-y}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle f_{3}^{t}(x)=\mathds{1}_{[t,+\infty[}(x)\exp\left(\frac{\phi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)-x}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle f_{4}^{t}(y)=\mathds{1}_{]-\infty,t]}(y)\exp\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(y)+y}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right).

From Lemma
3.12
, it follows that
\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
is weakly multiplicative. By Proposition
3.14
, every cluster point of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
is weakly multiplicative.
∎

3.4
Convergence of entropic minimizer when the marginal components are mutually singular

To handle convergence in presence of mutually singular measures, we introduce the notions of strict reinforced stochastic order, strict strong multiplicativity, and strict weak multiplicativity. The definition of this notions and their relations are analogues, for the strict half-planes
\mathrm{G}={(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>x}
and
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}={(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y<x}
, to (large) reinforced stochastic order (Definition
2.34
), (large) strong multiplicativity (Definition
2.41
), and (large) weak multiplicativity (Definition
3.10
).

Definition 3.17

(Strict strong multiplicativity)
.

A measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is called strictly strongly multiplicative if there exists
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\pi=\left(\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}\right)_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{G}}.

We now introduce the order
\leq_{\mathrm{G}}
, that will be related to strict strong multiplicativity by its associated Strassen theorem in the following.

Definition 3.18

(Strict reinforced stochastic order)
.

Consider
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
and define

T_{*}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)>0\text{ and }F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t)<\gamma_{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

We say that
\gamma_{1}
is smaller than
\gamma_{2}
in the reinforced strict stochastic order if
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}\geq F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}
and
T_{*}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subset\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}\}
. In this case, we write
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\gamma_{2}
.
13
13

13

This notation is motivated by the associated Strassen-type theorem.

Similarly to Theorem
2.42
for the order
\leq_{\mathrm{F}}
, the following theorem from Kellerer
[
15
, Theorem
2.4
]
provides a Strassen-type result for
\leq_{\mathrm{G}}
.

Theorem 3.19

(Strassen-type theorem for
\leq_{\mathrm{G}}
)
.

Consider
({\gamma_{1}},{\gamma_{2}})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}.
The relation
{\gamma_{1}}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}{\gamma_{2}}
is satisfied if and only if
\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
contains a stricly strongly multiplicative measure,
i.e.
, there exists
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\left(\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}\right)_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{G}}\in\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

(25)

In this case,
\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
contains exactly one strictly strongly multiplicative measure.

We denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
the unique strongly multiplicative measure in
\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
when
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\gamma_{2}
. Similarly as for the definition of
\mathcal{C}
(Definition
3.10
), let
\mathcal{D}
denote the class

\left\{B_{1}\times B_{2}\penalty\ ;\penalty\ (B_{1},B_{2})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})^{2},B_{1}\times B_{2}\subset\mathrm{G}\text{ or }B_{1}\times B_{2}\subset\tilde{\mathrm{G}}\right\}

of product sets included in
\mathrm{G}
or
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}.

Definition 3.20

(Strict weak multiplicativity)
.

A measure
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is said to be strictly weakly multiplicative if, for all
A\in\mathcal{D}
,
\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}A}
is a product measure.

The following result states that for measures
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
in the strict reinforced stochastic order,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is the only weakly multiplicative measure in
\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).
There is no similar result for the analogue large properties: when
\gamma_{1}\leq_{F}\gamma_{2}
, in general,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is not the only weakly multiplicative element of
\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{F}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
. For the proof, we refer to
[
15
, Proposition
4.3
]

Lemma 3.21
.

Consider
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
such that
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\gamma_{2}.
If
\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\pi
is weakly multiplicative, then
\pi=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

Proposition 3.22
.

Assume
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
have no atoms in common and
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{F}}\gamma_{2}
. Then,
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\gamma_{2}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, as
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
have no atom in common,
\gamma_{1}(t)=0
or
\gamma_{2}(t)=0.
Therefore,
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t)\in\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t),F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}(t)\}
, and the inequality
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t)\leq\min\big(F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t),F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}(t)\big)
is an equality. As
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\textnormal{st}}\gamma_{2}
, we get
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t)=\min\left(F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t),F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}(t)-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}(t)\right)\geq 0.
Furthermore, the sets
T^{*}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
(Definition
3.18
),
T_{+}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
T_{-}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
(Definition
2.34
) satisfy:
T_{*}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subset T_{+}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\cap\ T_{-}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subset\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}\}\cap\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}\}.
As
\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}\}\cap\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}\}=\{\min\left(F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+},F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}\right)>0\}
, and, as we just saw,
F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}=\min\left(F_{\gamma_{1}}^{+}-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+},F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}-F_{\gamma_{2}}^{-}\right)
, we get
T_{*}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subset\{F_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}>F_{\gamma_{2}}^{+}(t)\}.
Therefore,
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\gamma_{2}.
Now, as
\gamma_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{F}}\gamma_{2}
, by Theorem
2.42
, there exists two
\sigma
-finite measures
\nu_{1}\ll\gamma_{1}
and
\nu_{2}\ll\gamma_{2}
such that
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu,\nu)=(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{F}}
. As
\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{1})\cap\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{2})\subset\textrm{Atom}(\gamma_{1})\cap\textrm{Atom}(\gamma_{2})=\emptyset
and
\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{1})
is countable, we have
\nu_{2}(\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{1}))=0
. Since
x\mapsto\nu_{1}(\{x\})
is null outside
\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{1})
, we get

(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})(\mathrm{D})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathds{1}_{x=y}\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{1}(x)\right)\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{2}(y)=\int_{\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{1})^{c}}\nu_{1}(y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{2}(y)=0.

Thus,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu,\nu)=(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{G}}+(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}=(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{G}}
belongs to
\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and is the restriction of a product measure to
\mathrm{G}
. This establishes
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

∎

Before stating that
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)=\lim_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}}\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
when the components of non-equal pairs of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}
are singular, we prove that two measures
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{2}
forms a pair of singular measures if and only if there exists no transport plan from
\gamma_{1}
to
\gamma_{2}
that fixes mass. To prove this equivalence, we shall use that two measures are singular if and only if their common part is null. Let us recall the definition of common part of two measure. For every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
x\wedge y
denote the minimum between
x
and
y
. Now, if we define
h_{1}=\frac{d\gamma_{1}}{d(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}
and
h_{2}=\frac{d\gamma_{2}}{d(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}
, the common part
\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}
between
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
is defined by
\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}=(h_{1}\wedge h_{2})\cdot(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})
.

Proposition 3.23
.

For all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{2}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}=0
;

2.

(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is a pair of singular measures;

3.

For all
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
,
\pi(\mathrm{D})=0.

Proof.

The equivalence between the two first points is classical and omitted. We just prove the implications
(2)\implies(3)
and
(3)\implies(1)
, beginning with
(2)\implies(3)
. Consider
E
such that
\gamma_{1}(E)=\gamma_{2}(E^{c})=0
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).
As
{p_{1}}_{\#}\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}\leq\gamma_{1}
,
{p_{1}}_{\#}\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}={p_{2}}_{\#}\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}\leq\gamma_{2}
and
\mathrm{D}=[\mathrm{D}\cap(E\times\mathbb{R})]\uplus[\mathrm{D}\cap(E^{c}\cap\mathbb{R})]
, we have
0\leq\pi(\mathrm{D})=\left({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}\right)(E)+\left({p_{1}}_{\#}\pi_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}\mathrm{D}}\right)(E^{c})\leq\gamma_{1}(E)+\gamma_{2}(E^{c})=0+0=0
. We now prove
(3)\implies(1).
To derive a contradiction, assume
\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}\neq 0
. Then, consider
\pi_{0}\in\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2})
, and define
\pi=\pi_{0}+(\textnormal{id},\textnormal{id})_{\#}\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}.
By construction
\pi\in\textnormal{Marg}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and, from Point
2
of Lemma
2.32
,
\pi
is cyclically monotone. Hence,
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
, and
\pi(\mathrm{D})\geq[(\textnormal{id},\textnormal{id})_{\#}\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}](\mathrm{D})=(\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2})(\mathbb{R})>0
. This is a contradiction, hence
3\implies 1.

∎

Recall the notation for
\{(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}\cup\{(\mu_{k}^{-},\nu_{k}^{-})\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}\cup\{(\mu^{=},\mu^{=})\}
introduced in Definition
2.22
. We now show that under the hypothesis,

\begin{cases}\forall k\in\mathcal{K}^{+},\mu_{k}^{+}\wedge\nu_{k}^{+}=0\\
\forall k\in\mathcal{K}^{-},\mu_{k}^{-}\wedge\nu_{k}^{-}=0\end{cases},

(26)

we have
\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu)=\lim_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}}\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
. Note that Condition (
26
) implies the following condition,

\begin{cases}\forall k\in\mathcal{K}^{+},\textrm{Atom}(\mu_{k}^{+})\cap\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{k}^{+})=0\\
\forall k\in\mathcal{K}^{-},\textrm{Atom}(\mu_{k}^{-})\cap\textrm{Atom}(\nu_{k}^{-})=0\end{cases},

(27)

which will allow us to apply Proposition
3.22
.
The assumption
\mu\wedge\nu=0
is sufficient condition for Assumption (
27
), but is not necessary. For instance,
\mu=\delta_{1}+\delta_{3}+\delta_{4}
and
\nu=\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}+\delta_{5}
satisfy Assumption (
27
), but we do not have
\mu\wedge\nu=0.

Theorem 3.24
.

Consider
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}^{2}
. If Assumption (
26
) is satisfied, then

\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\xrightarrow[\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}]{}\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

Consider a cluster point
\pi
of
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}.
According to Theorem
3.16
,
\pi
is weakly multiplicative and belongs to
\mathfrak{C}(\mu,\nu)
. We now define
\mathcal{F}=((\pi_{k}^{+})_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}},(\pi_{k}^{-})_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}},\pi^{=})
using the notation in Definition
2.26
and
\mathfrak{P}
as in Theorem
2.37
. From this theorem, it follows that
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{P}
and

\pi=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}\pi_{k}^{+}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}\pi_{k}^{-}+(\textnormal{id},\textnormal{id})_{\#}\mu^{=}.

Fix
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
. As Equation (
27
) is satisfied and
\mu_{k}^{+}\leq_{\mathrm{F}}\nu_{k}^{+}
, from Proposition
3.22
, it follows that
\mu_{k}^{+}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\nu_{k}^{+}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
. Moreover, as Equation (
26
) is satisfied and
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
, by Proposition
3.23
,
\pi_{k}^{+}(\mathrm{D})=0
. As
\mathfrak{C}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})=\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
,
\pi_{k}^{+}\in\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
. Now, consider
B_{1}\times B_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
. As
\pi
is weakly multiplicative, there exists
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
such that
{\pi}_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}B_{1}\times B_{2}}=(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})
. Thus,
{\pi_{k}^{+}}_{\hskip 2.04877pt\mathbin{\vrule height=4.82224pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.39182pt\vrule height=0.39182pt,depth=0.0pt,width=3.91806pt}B_{1}\times B_{2}}=(\mathds{1}_{[a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}[}\cdot\nu_{1})\otimes(\mathds{1}_{]a_{k}^{+},b_{k}^{+}]}\cdot\nu_{2}).
Therefore
\pi_{k}^{+}
is weakly multiplicative. As
\mu_{k}^{+}\leq_{\mathrm{G}}\nu_{k}^{+}
and
\pi_{k}^{+}\in\textnormal{Marg}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})
,
\pi_{k}^{+}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+}).
Similarly, for all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
,
\pi_{k}^{-}=i_{\#}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\nu_{k}^{-},\mu_{k}^{-}).
Hence
\pi=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\mu_{k}^{+},\nu_{k}^{+})+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}i_{\#}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{F}}(\nu_{k}^{-},\mu_{k}^{-})+(\textnormal{id},\textnormal{id})_{\#}\mu^{=}=\mathcal{K}(\mu,\nu).
Therefore
(\pi_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})_{\mathbb{\varepsilon}>0}
admits a unique cluster point
\pi
. As
\textnormal{Marg}(\mu,\nu)
is compact, this establishes our result.

Measurement, spatiotemporal evolution, and obstacle factor diagnosis of new quality productive forces in China’s leading TCM manufacturing enterprises
Authors: Li Zhiguang, Yang Yu, Shao Jingyu, Chen Yushan, Xie Ruijin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBased on unbalanced panel data from 75 listed traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) enterprises during the period 2015–2023, this study constructs an evaluation index system to assess the development of new quality productive forces among TCM leading enterprises.MethodsThe entropy method is employed to measure the development level of new quality productive forces of these China’s leading TCM manufacturing enterprises. Furthermore, Kernel density estimation and the Dagum Gini coefficient are applied to analyze the spatiotemporal evolution and regional disparities. In addition, the study utilizes an obstacle factor identification model to pinpoint the key constraints affecting the development of new quality productive forces in TCM leading enterprises.ResultsThe results reveal an overall upward trend in the development level of new quality productive forces. Regionally, the performance follows the pattern: Central China > Eastern China > Western China, indicating some regional disparities, though the overall gap remains relatively stable. The obstacle analysis indicates that, at the criterion level, the ranking of obstacle degrees is: means of labor > laborers > objects of labor. Technological drive, digital application, and environmental ratings emerge as the main influencing factors in the development of new quality productive forces in China’s TCM leading enterprises.ConclusionNew quality productive forces show an upward development trend, with regional disparities persisting but the overall gap remaining stable. The main obstacles lie in means of labor, laborers, and objects of labor in sequence. Technological drive, digital application, and environmental ratings are crucial influencing factors for TCM leading enterprises.Keywords: New quality productive forces, Traditional Chinese medicine, TCM manufacturing industry, Chain-leading enterprises, Productivity, Spatiotemporal evolution, Technological progress, TCM Modernization

Introduction

In September 2023, during an inspection tour in Heilongjiang, General Secretary Xi Jinping emphasized the need to “integrate scientific and technological innovation resources, lead the development of strategic emerging and future industries, and accelerate the formation of new quality productive forces”. This important statement not only provides strategic direction for national development but also offers theoretical guidance for the transformation and upgrading of various industries. On January 31, 2024, at the 11th collective study session of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee, Xi once again stressed the importance of enhancing scientific and technological innovation, especially original and disruptive innovations, accelerating the realization of high-level self-reliance and strength in science and technology, and tackling critical core technologies to foster the emergence of transformative innovation outcomes and cultivate new drivers of new quality productive forces (NQPF). The 2025 national government work report further clarifies the need to develop NQPF based on local conditions, foster and expand emerging and future industries, and upgrade traditional industries. Furthermore, at the Fourth Plenary Session of the 20th CPC Central Committee held from October 20 to 23, 2025, the Party once again proposed “building a modern industrial system and consolidating the foundation of the real economy,” while stressing the importance of “accelerating high-level scientific and technological self-reliance and self-strengthening to lead the development of new quality productive forces.” Evidently, as a key driver of industrial transformation and upgrading, NQPF have increasingly become a new development pathway for enhancing enterprise competitiveness in the international arena.

Studies on the development of NQPF have focused on factor-driven mechanisms and corporate governance. From a factor-driven perspective, scholars investigate how emerging digital inputs, such as data elements, empower firms’ NQPF [1]. At the corporate governance level, advancements in ESG [2], artificial intelligence [3], and digital governance [4] are recognized as pivotal drivers of NQPF development. Despite the widespread recognition of NQPF as a novel impetus for economic and social development, there remains a dearth of systematic exposition and research on the definition, content, and constituent elements of NQPF within the specific context of TCM manufacturing enterprises. This research gap impedes a deeper understanding of new drivers in the TCM industry, particularly concerning the concrete operations and guidance for identifying and leveraging key disruptive technological breakthroughs.

As one of China’s key sectors with independent intellectual property rights in China, the traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) industry not only embodies a rich cultural heritage but also holds immense potential for independent innovation [5–7].

TCM has demonstrated unique advantages in disease prevention and treatment, and its clinical value has been widely recognized within the healthcare field [8]. However, the TCM industry currently faces dual challenges in the process of inheritance and innovation. On one hand, the application of chemical drug standards to evaluate the modernization of TCM tends to obscure its distinctive value [9]; on the other hand, the industry’s insufficient integration of modern technological achievements limits its global competitiveness [10, 11]. In this context, how to promote the modernization and industrialization of TCM manufacturing enterprises under the guidance of NQPF has become a pressing issue. This study focuses on leading TCM enterprises in China, constructing a comprehensive and objective evaluation index system of NQPF. Using the entropy method, Kernel density estimation, the Dagum Gini coefficient, and an obstacle factor identification model, it measures and analyzes the spatiotemporal evolution and regional disparities of NQPF, and identifies key constraints to their development.

This study makes several key theoretical contributions. First, it introduces the concept of NQPF into the research on Chinese medicine manufacturing enterprises, constructing a tailored evaluation index system specifically for leading TCM enterprises. This enriches the applicability of the NQPF theory within the context of traditional industry modernization and expands the literature on its application at the industry level in the Chinese medicine sector. Second, by employing Kernel density estimation and the Dagum Gini coefficient, the study reveals the evolutionary trends and spatial distribution characteristics of NQPF across regions, thereby deepening the theoretical linkage between regional development disparities and industrial upgrading. Finally, through the use of an obstacle factor identification model, the research systematically identifies key bottlenecks constraining the development of NQPF in TCM enterprises. It proposes a hierarchical analytical framework based on the dimensions of labor force, labor materials and labor objects, offering a novel theoretical tool and perspective for studying mechanisms to enhance NQPF.

Measurement of new quality productive forces in leading TCM enterprises

Theoretical logic of new quality productive forces in leading TCM enterprises

NQPF are an inevitable outcome of the intertwined development of the times and technological innovation. They consist of high-quality laborers, new-medium labor materials, and new-quality labor objects. This form of productive forces represents a dynamic and gradual leap in the quality of productivity, driven by high-level R&D and management teams, new production tools, and technological systems [12]. This productivity form not only breaks through the traditional limitations relying mainly on natural resources, labor, and capital inputs, but also achieves a leapfrog improvement through the guiding role of technological innovation. The NQPF of TCM enterprises arise from technological innovation, optimization of production factors, and industrial transformation and upgrading. They not only embody the wisdom of TCM but also integrate the power of modern technology, achieving innovative development characterized by modernization, industrialization, and internationalization.

Leading TCM enterprises are undergoing a transition from traditional production models toward new quality-oriented production modes. Leading enterprises refer to core companies that play a dominant role within modern industrial and supply chain organizations. These enterprises possess strong capabilities in technology integration, resource allocation, and market control, enabling them to effectively drive coordinated development among upstream and downstream enterprises in the industrial chain [13, 14]. Specifically, in the field of TCM, publicly listed TCM enterprises, relying on their resource integration capabilities, technological barriers, brand advantages, and strong policy support, occupy a leading position in the TCM industry chain.

The NQPF of leading enterprises within the TCM industrial chain signify cutting-edge production capacities forged through technological innovation, optimization of production factors, and industrial upgrading. These forces encapsulate not only the time-honored wisdom of TCM but also the immense power of modern science and technology, propelling TCM towards innovative development marked by modernization, industrialization, and globalization. In the digital intelligence era, the seamless integration of artificial intelligence technologies into TCM industry applications presents opportunities to tackle major challenges confronting the sector and accelerate its modernization trajectory. At present, leading enterprises in the TCM industrial chain are also transitioning from traditional production paradigms to new quality-driven production models.

Laborers are the most dynamic component of productivity, with their quality and capabilities directly shaping the development of NQPF in the TCM manufacturing sector [17]. In this dimension, we distinguish between decision-making personnel and general employees. Board chairpersons, as primary innovation drivers, play a crucial role in technological advancement, talent development, and policy alignment. Managerial staff skilled in applying advanced production resources serve as the operational backbone and significantly support NQPF formation. Therefore, the quality of decision-makers is reflected in the human capital of board chairs and senior executives, measured by their educational attainment. Meanwhile, the quality of general employees, particularly R&D personnel, forms the foundation of innovation, as their expertise, creativity, and experience directly influence the pace and quality of new drug development. Consequently, indicators such as the proportion of R&D staff and employees with postgraduate degrees are important.

As the material basis of productivity [18], labor objects also play a vital role in TCM manufacturing. We evaluate two aspects: ecological environment and manufacturing expenses. Within the NQPF framework, enterprises are expected not only to pursue economic gains but also to emphasize environmental protection, corporate social responsibility, and governance. Environmental performance is measured by the China Chengxin Green Finance ESG environmental rating [19], while manufacturing expenses reflect how efficiently raw materials and energy are used. Improving production efficiency reduces costs and thus contributes to NQPF advancement.

The means of labor, as the technical instruments of productivity [20], are also key to developing NQPF. With the growing adoption of digital technologies and artificial intelligence, firms increasingly implement intelligent, automated, and refined production systems. This integration enhances productivity and lowers costs, providing strong support for high-quality development in the sector. Therefore, this study includes indicators such as technological innovation level, digitalization level, and fixed asset investment to capture enterprises’ efforts and achievements in this area.

Data sources of the new quality productive forces indicator for leading TCM enterprises

As of the end of 2024, China had a total of 4912 TCM production enterprises. To thoroughly analyze the leading forces in the TCM industry, reveal their development trends, and highlight their roles, this study has constructed a scientific, rigorous, and highly operational screening system.

In terms of scope, we strictly focus on pharmaceutical manufacturing under the broader manufacturing category, further refining it to the TCM manufacturing sector. This approach precisely targets the core industrial segment of the TCM industry. Regarding firm size, we have set clear, quantifiable screening criteria. First, we require that an enterprise have no fewer than 100 employees. This ensures that enterprises have sufficient human resources to support basic production and operational needs. Second, we stipulate that the annual operating revenue of an enterprise should reach 400 million yuan or above. This indicator directly reflects an enterprise’s competitive strength and operational scale in the market, highlighting its position and influence in the industry.

Additionally, to ensure the selected enterprises have mature development experience and standardized operational models, we require that they have been listed before 2015. Listing reflects recognition in the capital market and indicates that the enterprise has reached high standards in corporate governance, information disclosure, and financial compliance. This demonstrates stronger risk resistance and sustainable development potential.

During the screening process, we utilized authoritative data resources, such as enterprise classification codes from the National Bureau of Statistics and listed companies’ annual reports. We followed a rigorous process, carrying out three key steps in sequence: industry identification, size screening, and listing qualification verification. After strict screening at each level, we identified 75 TCM enterprises that met all the above conditions and are leading enterprises. These enterprises play a significant role as leaders and exemplars in the TCM industry.

Samples with missing or abnormal values in relevant variables were excluded, and linear interpolation was applied to fill in missing data for certain companies in specific years. The related data mainly come from the CSMAR and CNRDS databases as well as corporate annual reports. Specifically, human capital, proportion of R&D personnel, proportion of employees with graduate degrees, and financial data such as manufacturing expenses are sourced from the CSMAR database and corporate annual reports; environmental scores are obtained from the Zhongchengxin Green Finance ESG Rating Center; patent applications are drawn from the CNRDS database; and digitalization levels are derived from the CSMAR database. Among these, corporate annual reports are accessed via the Tonghuashun official website.

Overall development level and trends

Based on panel data from 75 publicly listed TCM manufacturing enterprises in China from 2015 to 2023, this study measures the development level of NQPF in leading TCM enterprises using the entropy method. The enterprises are categorized by region into central, eastern, and western regions for analysis. Specifically, between 2015 and 2017, the average level declined slightly from 0.3043 to 0.2956. This decline may be attributed to industry restructuring, intensified market competition, and shifts in policy environments during that period. According to data from the National Bureau of Statistics and the China Pharmaceutical Industry Information Center, the number of large-scale pharmaceutical manufacturing enterprises reached 7392 in 2015. However, the industry was highly fragmented, with the top 100 enterprises accounting for only 68.9% of total main business revenue, presenting characteristics of fragmentation and disorder. The government encouraged mergers and acquisitions, advocating for vertically integrated enterprise groups. Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) faced consolidation or elimination pressures, affecting production efficiency. During 2015–2017, approximately 80% of TCM enterprises were SMEs, with products focused on popular areas such as cardiovascular diseases and colds. The lack of product differentiation led to homogeneous competition. Furthermore, the policy on generic drug consistency evaluation was launched in 2015, prompting listed pharmaceutical companies to compete for market share.

Since 2018, the overall average began to rebound and has continued to grow steadily in the following years. Particularly from 2021 to 2023, the growth rates were 1.371%, 0.998%, and 1.849%, respectively, indicating an accelerating upward trend. This improvement is largely due to the continuous efforts of leading TCM enterprises in technological innovation, optimization of production factors, and industrial upgrading. In recent years, the State Council and relevant ministries have issued several policy documents to support the development of the TCM industry, such as the Policies for Accelerating the Development of Characteristic TCM, the 14th Five-Year Plan for TCM Development, and the Implementation Plan for the Major Project on TCM Revitalization and Development.

Regional development level and trends

There are evident regional disparities in the development level of NQPF among leading TCM enterprises. The eastern region, with its strong economic foundation, robust technological innovation capacity, and rapid pace of industrial transformation and upgrading, generally exhibits a higher level of NQPF development. Enterprises in this region demonstrate outstanding performance in intelligence-driven and innovation-oriented development, resulting in scores that not only exceed the national average but also grow at a relatively fast pace. In contrast, the central region shows a steady upward trend. Although its average NQPF scores lagged behind those of the eastern region from 2015 to 2021, it has caught up and even surpassed the eastern region since 2022, continuing its upward momentum. This may be associated with the joint issuance of the Good Agricultural Practice (GAP) for Chinese Medicinal Materials Production by four ministries and commissions, including China’s National Medical Products Administration, in 2022. Following the introduction of this policy, central provinces responded swiftly. For instance, Henan took the lead in issuing implementation guidelines, encouraging enterprises to establish their own GAP bases, with eight bases across the province achieving certification. Anhui actively promoted the Top Ten Anhui Medicinal Herbs industry, pioneering the fresh-cutting of Chinese medicinal materials at their production sites and establishing an information-based traceability system. Jiangxi, leveraging its chain leader system, drove the development of the entire TCM industry chain, with its TCM exports surging from 19th place nationwide in 2021 to 9th place in 2022.

Furthermore, the Guiding Opinions on Promoting the Orderly Transfer of the Manufacturing Industry (2022) issued by ten departments under the State Council explicitly stated that the eastern region should strengthen innovation in key core technologies and enhance its capacity as an innovation hub, while the central region should actively undertake the layout and transfer of emerging industries to build modern manufacturing bases. This has fostered a collaborative pattern of R&D in the east and production in the central region. At this juncture, TCM manufacturing enterprises in the central region not only possess resource and industrial foundational advantages but also embrace digital transformation at a lower cost. In contrast, enterprises in the eastern region are constrained by their existing models, and those in the western region, despite having abundant resources, respond sluggishly. This has gradually positioned the central region as a leader in driving high-quality development of the national TCM industry.

Even in Table 2, it is evident that the western region, meanwhile, lags behind in NQPF development among TCM leading enterprises, though it has shown gradual improvement in recent years. The relatively weak economic base, remote geographic location, and insufficient innovation capacity have constrained its productivity progress. However, with policy support such as the Action Plan for Poverty Alleviation through the TCM Raw Materials Industry and its unique resource and climatic advantages, several TCM manufacturing enterprises have taken root and gradually grown into industry leaders.

Characteristics of intra-regional and inter-regional differences

Figures 1(b) and 1(c) visually illustrate the characteristics of intra-regional and inter-regional differences across the three major regions. In contrast, the western region exhibited an upward trend in the Gini coefficient from 2015 to 2023, especially notable between 2019 and 2021. Although there was a slight decline in 2022 and 2023, overall disparities remained significant. Generally, the eastern and western regions displayed larger intra-regional disparities, while the central region showed smaller and more stable differences, reflecting uneven development levels of NQPF among leading TCM enterprises across regions. The larger internal differences in the eastern region may be attributed to its developed economy, whereas the expanding disparities in the western region may result from uneven development. The central region appears relatively balanced with minimal disparities. According to Fig. 1(c), the inter-regional Gini coefficients among the eastern, central, and western regions fluctuate over time, indicating that differences in NQPF development levels among these regions are not pronounced.

Contribution rate analysis

Figure 1(d) shows that transvariation intensity contributes the most to the overall spatial differentiation, followed by intra-regional differentiation. During the sample period, the contribution of intra-regional differentiation to the overall spatial variation remained relatively stable, while inter-regional differentiation contributed the least. These results suggest that transvariation intensity is the primary source of spatial differentiation in the NQPF of leading TCM enterprises, indicating that the transvariation intensity and intersection of regions have a significant impact on regional disparities in NQPF.

Kernel density estimation analysis of the development level of NQPF in leading TCM enterprises

Kernel density estimation is a non-parametric method in probability theory and statistics used to estimate the probability density function of a random variable. It is well suited to describe the distribution dynamics and evolutionary patterns of NQPF. Therefore, this study employs Gaussian kernel density estimation to analyze the distribution, shape, and development trends of NQPF levels among China’s leading TCM enterprises. Figure 2 presents the spatiotemporal dynamic evolution of NQPF development levels from 2015 to 2023.

In terms of distribution location, the kernel density curves for the nation as a whole and the eastern, central, and western regions all show a general rightward shift over time, indicating a continuous improvement in NQPF levels among leading TCM enterprises. Regarding distribution shape, the main peak heights for the nation and eastern region exhibit slight fluctuations while gradually widening, suggesting that development disparities among leading TCM enterprises in these areas have increased, reflecting a growing regional imbalance. The central region shows a steady rise in the main peak height year by year, but the width does not significantly narrow, indicating a lack of clear convergence in absolute differences of NQPF levels. In the western region, the main peak demonstrates an overall upward trend and a gradually narrowing width, signifying a reduction in absolute disparities among leading TCM enterprises.

Regarding distribution tail behavior, the central region displays both left and right tails, indicating the coexistence of enterprises with extremely high and extremely low development levels. The nation and eastern and western regions exhibit right tails, indicating the presence of high-performing enterprises. Concerning polarization, no significant polarization phenomena are observed nationally or in the eastern and western regions. However, the central region presents a pattern of one main peak with multiple side peaks, reflecting a multi-polar differentiation in NQPF development among its leading enterprises, with clusters of both low and high levels coexisting, and clear gradient characteristics in enterprise NQPF development.

Identification analysis of obstacle factors affecting the development level of NQPF in leading TCM enterprises

Analysis of obstacle factors at the criterion level

The obstacle factors model can identify the resistances and barriers hindering the development of NQPF in China’s leading TCM enterprises. This study uses the obstacle degree model to pinpoint the key obstructive factors. As shown in Table 4, during the study period, the ranking of obstacle degrees at the criterion level remains stable and unchanged, in the order of labor materials, laborers, and labor objects. It is evident that the labor materials dimension constitutes the greatest barrier to the development of NQPF in leading TCM enterprises, with an average obstacle degree as high as 57.72%, consistently exceeding 57% across the years, demonstrating a relatively stable characteristic. Specifically, this indicates that although leading TCM enterprises have achieved significant progress in recent years, there are still shortcomings and imbalances in terms of means of labor. These include slow progress in new drug research and development and process innovation, low levels of digitalization, and weak accumulation of intangible assets [22].

Additionally, the laborers dimension also exerts a notable constraint on the development of NQPF, with an average obstacle degree of 31.10%. It is worth noting, however, that the obstacle degree in the labor objects dimension shows a slight upward trend over the years, signaling the need for enhanced management and improvements in environmental protection and cost control in the future.

The relatively high obstacle scores for technology drive and digital application highlight shortcomings in technological advancement and digital transformation within leading TCM enterprises. This may be attributed to the lack of unified technical standards across the industry, with significant regional and enterprise-level variations. Such fragmentation poses difficulties for the widespread adoption and promotion of new technologies, thereby slowing the rapid development of NQPF.

To ensure that the research analysis is not affected by endogeneity issues such as omitted variable bias, measurement error, or bidirectional causality, this study employs the two-stage least squares (2SLS) method with instrumental variables (IV) for robustness testing. Specifically, the study uses the regional averages of technology drive, digital application, fixed asset ratio, environmental rating, and manufacturing cost ratio for leading TCM enterprises across China’s seven major regions as instrumental variables.

These instruments satisfy both the relevance and exogeneity conditions. On the one hand, enterprises within the same region tend to exhibit similar levels of development, including similarities in technological advancement, which supports the relevance of the instruments. On the other hand, due to geographical segmentation, the development characteristics of neighboring regions exert limited influence on local enterprises, thus meeting the exogeneity requirement. In a practical context, individual behaviors may be influenced by past actions due to inertia, implying that NQPF might exhibit path dependence. To avoid the subjectivity associated with the selection of instrumental variables in the Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) method, we employed the System Generalized Method of Moments (System GMM) estimation for the model, with the results presented in Model (2) of Table 9. Upon examination, the p-value of the AR(2) test is 0.193, which is greater than 0.1, indicating that there is no second-order serial correlation in the differenced error terms. The p-value of the Hansen test is 1.00, also greater than 0.1, which means we fail to reject the null hypothesis, suggesting that there is no over-identification problem, and thus the conditions for using the System GMM are satisfied.

From the regression results, it can be observed that the coefficient of the first-order lag term (0.763) is significantly positive, indicating that the development quality in the previous period indeed has an impact on the development of NQPF in the current period. The signs and significance levels of the coefficients for the obstacle factors (technology-driven factors, digital applications, proportion of fixed assets, environmental ratings, and proportion of manufacturing expenses) are generally consistent with those in the original benchmark regression. This consistency suggests that the conclusions are robust and not subject to interference from dynamic effects and endogeneity.

Conclusions and implications

Conclusions

Based on unbalanced panel data from 75 listed TCM enterprises between 2015 and 2023, this study constructs a comprehensive evaluation index system to assess the development of NQPF among leading TCM enterprises. Using the entropy method, Kernel density estimation, Dagum Gini coefficient decomposition, and obstacle degree modeling, the study systematically explores the spatiotemporal evolution, regional disparities, and key constraints affecting new quality productivity. The main findings are as follows:

(1) The overall development level of NQPF among TCM leading enterprises has steadily increased. This growth is driven by the widespread advancement of digital transformation, technological innovation, and intelligent upgrades, which have significantly improved production efficiency, process optimization, and management innovation. (2) There are observable and fluctuating regional differences in the development of NQPF, although the overall level of disparity has remained relatively stable. Decomposition of the Dagum Gini coefficient shows that the main source of disparity comes from hypervariability density, followed by intra-regional differences, with inter-regional differences contributing the least. (3) Due to differing levels of digitalization, greening, technological capabilities, managerial innovation, and policy support across regions, the kernel density estimations vary significantly. Nonetheless, the national and regional trends uniformly show a steady annual improvement in NQPF levels. (4) In terms of constraint severity, the ranking of the three main factors from highest to lowest is: means of labor > laborers > objects of labor. Key obstacles include inadequate digital technological drive, lagging digital applications, environmental performance, and the structure of capital investments and manufacturing costs. These collectively hinder the further development of NQPF.

Practical implications

Building on the above findings, this paper proposes a series of targeted policy recommendations to promote the sustained and high-quality development of NQPF in the TCM manufacturing industry.

At the level of laborers, it is essential to enhance the quality of human resources and strengthen the support of innovative talents. On the one hand, the government should provide subsidies and tax incentives for TCM manufacturing enterprises to attract highly educated talents, encouraging enterprises to increase the proportion of R&D personnel and highly skilled employees. On the other hand, enterprises need to strengthen the cultivation of interdisciplinary talents to meet the demands of intelligent manufacturing and digital transformation. Meanwhile, enterprises can also establish incentive mechanisms such as equity incentives and innovation awards to stimulate the innovative vitality of laborers and drive them towards intelligence and efficiency.

At the level of labor objects, the optimization of resource utilization efficiency and green and sustainable development in TCM manufacturing enterprises should be emphasized. Enterprises should strengthen cooperation and exchanges in green manufacturing technologies, such as clean energy substitution and waste recycling systems, to reduce resource consumption and pollution emissions during the production process. The government can also provide subsidies for technological transformation to support enterprises in introducing energy-efficient equipment and reducing raw material and energy costs. On this basis, enterprises can further reduce the proportion of manufacturing expenses and optimize resource utilization efficiency through lean production, process optimization, and supply chain management, thereby promoting the development of their NQPF.

At the level of labor means, TCM manufacturing enterprises should keep pace with the times and strengthen the application and promotion of digital technologies. The government can increase support for R&D investment in the TCM manufacturing industry by establishing special funds to support enterprises in conducting technological innovation and equipment transformation, promoting the intelligent and automated upgrading of TCM production processes. At the same time, enterprises should be encouraged to strengthen cooperation with universities and research institutions to jointly conduct R&D on key common technologies and cutting-edge leading technologies, enhancing the independent innovation capabilities of the TCM manufacturing industry.

To address the main obstacles hindering the development of NQPF in China’s leading TCM enterprises, a four-in-one policy coordination system should be constructed to break through bottlenecks: First, a national collaborative innovation platform for TCM manufacturing technologies should be established. This platform should integrate resources across government, industry, academia, research institutions, and end users to address shared technological challenges, such as automation in processing and real-time quality monitoring. To encourage technological adoption, a “use-first, pay-later” mechanism can be introduced, along with enterprise-led, government-supported challenge-driven projects to foster institutional support for breakthroughs.

Second, digital empowerment should be prioritized by enhancing full value-chain connectivity through integrated internet platforms. Policymakers should consider providing capital expenditure subsidies for the construction of digital workshops and implementing specialized training initiatives, such as a digital craftsman talent development program. In addition, a differentiated regulatory framework based on environmental performance should be introduced, creating a three-pronged support system encompassing data, equipment, and human capital.

Third, a green transformation strategy should be advanced by establishing a carbon footprint labeling system for TCM products and a circular waste recycling mechanism. This should be complemented by incentive schemes for clean energy substitution and eco-tax refund policies. Embedding environmental, social, and governance (ESG) principles within industrial upgrading processes will be vital for long-term sustainability.

Fourth, cost optimization should be achieved by accelerating the modernization of the means of labor. This includes developing key technologies such as real-time process monitoring and intelligent production scheduling. Simultaneously, digital skill training for the workforce and the implementation of lean management practices will be essential to reduce manufacturing costs and enhance overall competitiveness.

The role of metabolites in the causal effect of immune cell phenotypes on immunotherapy toxicity risk
Authors: Meng Tang, Fei Mu, Zhen Yan, Rui Gong, Chen Cui, KeXin Sun, JinYi Zhao, JingWen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) therapy have significantly improved survival in cancer patients. However, immune-related adverse events (irAEs) have emerged as a major challenge limiting its therapeutic efficacy. Recent studies have demonstrated that certain immune cell phenotypes and metabolites may be associated with the risk of irAEs. Nevertheless, the precise biological mechanisms for this association remain poorly understood.MethodsUsing genetic variants from large-scale genome-wide association studies (GWAS), we employed two-sample mendelian randomization (MR) to systematically investigate causal relationships among 731 immune cell phenotypes, 1400 metabolites, and the risk of both all-grade and high-grade irAEs. A two-step MR method was employed to further analyze the mediating effects of key immune cell phenotypes and metabolites in irAEs. The inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method served as the primary approach to assess causal relationships, while Cochran’s Q test, MR-Egger intercept test, MR-PRESSO, and leave-one-out analysis were employed to evaluate heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy.ResultsOur results found that metabolites mediate several causal pathways linking immune cell phenotypes to high-grade irAEs. Specifically, etiocholanolone glucuronide levels partially mediated the causal effect of CCR2 on monocyte in high-grade irAEs, with a mediation proportion of 0.0730 (95% CI 0.0032, 0.1428; P = 0.0403). Thymol sulfate levels partially mediated the causal effect of CD39 on monocyte in high-grade irAEs, with a mediation effect estimate of − 0.0321 (95% CI − 0.0621, − 0.0021; P = 0.0358). Our analysis identified putative associations for all-grade and high-grade irAEs with 10 and 11 immune cell phenotypes and causal relationships with 30 and 36 metabolites, respectively. Sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of these associations.ConclusionThis study suggests causal relationships between immune cell phenotypes, metabolites and irAEs. Two metabolites that may act as potential mediating factors between immune cells and high-grade irAEs were identified. Of course, further investigation is warranted to validate these results.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04319-9.Keywords: Immunotherapy toxicity, Immune cell phenotypes, Metabolites, Mendelian randomization, Mediation effect

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are a class of anticancer immunotherapeutic agents that function by blocking the activity of immune checkpoints using antibodies, such as programmed cell death protein 1/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-1/PD-L1) inhibitors and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) inhibitors. This blockade restores and enhances the ability of effector T lymphocytes to recognize and kill tumor cells, thereby augmenting systemic antitumor immune responses [1]. However, T cells activated by ICIs may inadvertently attack normal tissues while targeting tumor cells, leading to an increase in autoantibodies, cytokines, and other immune-related factors, which can induce autoimmune inflammation and result in a series of immune-related adverse events (irAEs). These events often manifest as systemic reactions, including dermatological, pulmonary, and endocrine toxicity, etc [2]. Studies have shown that the incidence of all-grade irAEs can reach 54%–76% [3], while the incidence of high-grade irAEs with ipilimumab plus nivolumab is approximately 60% [4]. Importantly, ICI can also lead to permanent organ damage [5], increased cardiovascular complications [6], impaired fertility [7], and even death [8]. It is now understood that the pathophysiological mechanism underlying irAEs is the overactivation and subsequent dysregulation of the immune system. This knowledge is crucial for effectively reducing the incidence of high-grade irAEs and providing support for safe and effective irAEs treatment.

It is now understood that the pathophysiological mechanism underlying irAEs is the overactivation and subsequent dysregulation of the immune system. Recent studies have revealed that multiple immune cell types are associated with the occurrence of irAEs. The abundance of activated CD4 memory T cells and T-cell receptor diversity has been correlated with the development of severe irAEs in melanoma patients [9]. A subset of macrophages expressing secreted phosphoprotein 1 has been associated with the development of irAEs in patients with lung squamous cell carcinoma [10]. It is widely thought that eosinophils are associated with irAEs, and the underlying mechanism involves their ability to modulate the tumor microenvironment, induce chemokines and trigger inflammation when expressed at high levels [11]. These studies suggest a complex association between immune cells and irAEs. However, due to the profound complexity inherent to the human immune system, the precise role of immune cells in irAEs is not fully defined in the current literature, necessitating further research to establish definitive causality.

Host genetic background is also a key regulatory factor in irAEs. Recent studies have demonstrated that various gene polymorphisms can significantly influence susceptibility to irAEs. For instance, the IL7 gene rs16906115 polymorphism is markedly associated with an increased risk of irAEs in melanoma patients receiving ICI therapy [12]; Conversely, a particular subtype in the T-cell receptor beta (TRBV) haplotype group appears to confer protection against grade ≥ 3 severe irAEs, suggesting that TRBV polymorphisms can serve as predictive markers [13]. Furthermore, genetic variations in immune-related genes such as MAPK1, PTPRC, ADAD1, and IL6 have been found to independently predict the risk of PD 1/PD L1 inhibitor-related irAEs [14], with potential for clinical translation. Collectively, these findings suggest that changes at the genetic and polymorphism levels may play a critical role in the pathogenesis of irAEs.

As downstream products with an amplification effect, metabolites provide a sensitive and direct measure of disease-related physiological changes, making them excellent candidates for predictive biomarkers. Recent studies have shown that specific metabolites are linked to the development of irAEs. In this respect, research by Juan Chen et al. [15] indicated that metabolites such as acylcarnitines and steroids can be used to predict the occurrence of irAEs in lung cancer patients treated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Jiayi Gao et al. [16] identified low ALDOB and lactate/pyruvate ratios and high ST6GAL1 levels as predictors of irAEs in elderly patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer. Therefore, systematic studies are necessary to identify more metabolites as targets for predicting irAEs.

Current evidence suggests that immune cells and metabolites are related to the occurrence of irAEs, not as independent factors, but through complex interactions. In vivo, immune and metabolic processes are co-regulated in an interdependent manner, working in concert to maintain the homeostatic balance of the internal environment [17]. Cellular metabolism affects the state of immune cells, which may lead to diseases such as inflammation and cancer [18]. Immune signaling can also affect metabolism by regulating the expression of specific genes [19]. The specific mechanism of interaction between immune cells and metabolites in irAEs remains unknown.

Traditional statistical methods can assess causal relationships between metabolites, immune cell phenotypes, and irAEs. However, their findings may be biased due to the limitations of sample size, reverse causality, and various confounding factors. Mendelian randomization (MR) is a statistical method that uses single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as instrumental variables to infer causal relationships between exposure and outcome [20]. Owing to the randomization of SNPs at conception, MR can mimic randomized controlled trials, avoid reverse causal bias, and reduce confounding factors in routine epidemiological and observational studies. Over the past few years, MR has been widely used to infer the causal relationship between exposure and disease. For instance, Wei Li et al. used MR to analyze more than 30 types of immune cells with osteonecrosis, offering new insights into its immunology [21]. Yi Liu et al. used MR to explore the importance of immune cell HLA DR on CD33dim HLA DR+ CD11b+ cells in the development of heart failure, a process potentially mediated by octadecanedioic acid levels [22]. These studies overlap in their assertion that MR offers a powerful tool for elucidating the causal role of immune cell phenotypes and metabolites in disease onset and progression, and is instrumental in advancing research on related disease mechanisms and the development of targeted clinical intervention strategies.

In this study, we systematically leveraged the advantages of MR to investigate the causal associations among immune cell phenotypes, metabolites, and irAEs. We identified specific immune cell phenotypes and metabolites significantly associated with irAE risk and elucidated their potential modes of action within the irAE pathological process. Furthermore, mediation analysis was conducted to reveal, from a genetic perspective, how these factors mediate the onset and progression of irAEs. Importantly, this study provides a new biomarker for predicting the risk of irAEs, contributes a direct theoretical basis for the development of intervention strategies based on metabolic regulation or immune cell reprogramming. Ultimately, these findings facilitate an in-depth exploration of irAE mechanisms and advance the path toward clinical translation.

Method

Study design

This study strictly adhered to three core assumptions of MR [23]: Assumption 1, the relevance assumption; Assumption 2, the independence assumption; and Assumption 3, the exclusion restriction assumption, as depicted in Fig. 1A. A two-sample and two-step MR approach was employed to investigate the causal relationships among exposures, mediators, and irAEs. First, a two-sample analysis was conducted to examine the total effect of the exposure on the outcome. Second, a two-step method was used to quantify the indirect effect mediated through the hypothesized mediator, as depicted in Fig. 1B. The detailed analytical workflow is presented in Fig. 1C.

Data sources

The large-scale genome-wide association studies (GWAS) summary data for irAEs were obtained from a recent study on European populations with the latest genetic data [24]. This study included 1751 patients of European ancestry who received ICIs for the treatment of various types of cancer (n = 12) between 2013 and 2020. Approximately 90% of the patients received PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, whereas the remaining 10% received combined treatment with CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. the all-grade irAE cohort (n = 339 cases; n = 1412 controls) and the high-grade irAE cohort (n = 259 cases; n = 1375 controls).

The GWAS data for immune cells were derived from a study on the most recent genetic data of European populations [25]. A total of 3757 patients of European ancestry were included. The study analyzed 731 immune cell phenotypes, characterized by absolute cell counts (n = 118), relative counts (n = 192), median fluorescence intensities of surface antigens (n = 389), and morphological parameters (n = 32), involving B cell, cDC, monocytes, TBNK cell, Treg, myeloid cell, and mature stages of T cell.

The GWAS data for metabolites were obtained from a recent study on European populations with the latest genetic data [26]. A total of 8299 individuals of European descent were included. The dataset comprised 1091 plasma metabolites and 309 metabolite ratios, totaling 1400 entries. Among the 1091 plasma metabolites, 850 had known identities across eight super pathways, lipid, amino acid, xenobiotics, nucleotide, cofactors and vitamins, carbohydrate, peptide, and energy. The remaining 241 were categorized as unknown or “partially” characterized molecules.

Detailed information on the data sources is provided in the supplementary material (Supplementary Tables S1–3).

Selection of instrumental variables

To ensure the reliability and robustness of the MR analysis results, instrumental variables (IVs) were required to meet the following criteria: (1) IVs must be correlated with the exposure; (2) IVs should not be influenced by factors other than the exposure that could affect the outcome; (3) IVs should not be correlated with the outcome independently of the exposure.

To ensure a strong correlation between IVs and exposure, we set the significance threshold at 1 × 10− 5. Besides, to ensure the independence of the SNPs, linkage disequilibrium clumping was applied with the following parameters: clump_kb = 10,000 and clump_r² = 0.001. To further ensure a strong correlation between IVs and exposure, F-statistic was calculated, with an F-value greater than 10 indicating that the results are not substantially affected by weak instrument bias [27]. To ensure consistency, the effect alleles and their corresponding effect sizes between the exposure and outcome datasets were aligned, with any SNPs featuring strand-ambiguous or mismatched alleles removed from the analysis.

MR analysis

Two-sample MR

A two-sample MR analysis was conducted to examine the causal relationships between immune cell phenotypes, metabolites, and irAEs. Five methods were used to assess causality: Inverse-variance weighted (IVW), MR Egger, weighted median, weighted mode, and simple mode. Given its superior statistical power [28], we used the IVW random effects model which was designated the primary analysis, while complementary methods were used to validate the findings. A causal relationship was established only when the IVW analysis yielded p < 0.05, and all five analytical methods produced directionally concordant effect estimates, as follows, all odds ratios (ORs) > 1 or all ORs < 1. Besides, the total causal effect of the exposure on the outcome, denoted as βtotal, was estimated. In addition, the False Discovery Rate (FDR) was controlled by applying Benjamini-Hochberg procedures. A statistically significant genetic causality between the outcome and exposure was inferred when the FDR-corrected p-value was less than 0.05 [29].

Mediation analysis

A two-step MR analysis was used for mediation analysis to verify whether metabolites (immune cell phenotypes) could act as mediators between immune cell phenotypes (metabolites) and irAEs. Step 1: Using immune cells as the exposure and mediator metabolites as the outcome, the causal effect (β1) of the immune cells on the metabolites was calculated. Step 2: Using mediator metabolites as the exposure and irAEs as the outcome, the causal effect (β2) of the metabolites on irAEs was calculated. The same two-step analytical procedure was applied to evaluate the alternative model, wherein immune cell phenotypes were tested as mediators in the causal pathway from metabolites to irAEs.

Sensitivity analysis

The steiger test was used to exclude data with reverse causal effects. Cochran’s Q test was applied to assess the heterogeneity, with a P-value > 0.05 indicating no heterogeneity. MR Egger intercept was used to detect and adjust for horizontal pleiotropy, with a P-value ＞0.05 indicating no horizontal pleiotropy. Meanwhile, MR-PRESSO was employed to identify outliers of results . Leave-one-out analysis was performed to evaluate whether single SNPs affected the MR results. All analyses were performed in R software (version 4.3.3), with the TwoSampleMR package (version 0.6.14).

Results

Causal relationships between immune cell phenotypes and irAEs

MR analyses were first conducted to examine the relationships between 731 immune cell phenotypes and both all-grade irAEs and high-grade irAEs. The IVW results indicated that 10 and 11 immune cells phenotypes were significantly associated with all-grade irAEs and high-grade irAEs respectively (Fig. 2A, B). In this respect, CD20 on IgD+ CD38- (OR = 1.8118, 95% CI 1.1141–2.9465), CD28+ CD45RA+ CD8dim AC(OR = 1.8207, 95% CI 1.0105–3.2806), CD33-HLA DR-AC(OR = 1.2503, 95% CI 1.0023–1.5598), and EM CD4+ % T cell (OR = 2.0935, 95% CI 1.1686–3.7504) were associated with an increased risk of all-grade irAEs, whereas the remaining 6 immune cell phenotypes were associated with a reduced risk of all-grade irAEs (Fig. 2C, Table S4).

Causal relationships between metabolites and irAEs

We next conducted MR analyses to investigate the relationships between 1400 metabolites and all-grade irAEs and high-grade irAEs. The IVW method identified metabolites associated with all-grade irAEs (n = 30) and high-grade irAEs (n = 36) (Fig. 3A, B). Mediation of the causal effect of immune cell phenotypes on irAEs by metabolites

We next analyzed whether 1400 metabolites could serve as mediators in the causal pathways linking 731 immune cell phenotypes to both all-grade irAEs and high-grade irAEs. Despite identifying several metabolites implicated in modulating the relationship between immune cells and irAEs, only two causal mediation pathways demonstrated significant mediation effects (Table S8). Specifically, thymol sulfate levels partially mediated the causal association between CD39 on monocytes and high-grade irAEs, with a mediating effect of − 0.0321 (95% CI − 0.0621, − 0.0021; P = 0.0358) (Table 1; Fig. 4). Besides, etiocholanolone glucuronide levels partially mediated the causal association between CCR2 on monocytes and high-grade irAEs, with a mediating effect of 0.0730 (95% CI 0.0032, 0.1428; P = 0.0403) (Table 1; Fig. 4).

Discussion

Leveraging large-scale GWAS data, this study presents the comprehensive genetic investigation into the potential causal effects and mediating mechanisms of 731 immune cell phenotypes and 1400 metabolites on irAEs from a genetic perspective. These results indicate that some metabolites act as mediators that can either increase or decrease the risk of high-grade irAEs associated with specific immune cells. Specifically, etiocholanolone glucuronide levels increased the protective effect of CCR2 on monocytes against high-grade irAEs. Higher levels of thymol sulfate mediated a protective effect, reducing the risk of high-grade irAEs conveyed by CD39 on monocytes. Overall, these findings provide a crucial foundation for the subsequent development of biomarkers for irAEs and the identification of therapeutic targets. Our analysis revealed 10 and 11 immune cell phenotypes causally associated with all-grade and high-grade irAEs, respectively, and identified 30 and 36 metabolites with putative causal relationships to these toxicities. These extensive data on causal associations further enrich our understanding of the complex pathogenic mechanisms of irAEs, providing an actionable pathway for translation into clinical practice.

It is now understood that immune cells undergo significant metabolic reprogramming during activation, differentiation, and execution of functions, and metabolites, as intermediate or end products of cellular metabolism, can directly act as signaling molecules to regulate immune function [30, 31]. Previous research [32] has consistently demonstrated that specific bacteria and their beneficial metabolites can reduce intestinal inflammation and enhance the repair and integrity of the intestinal barrier by modulating immune cells, thereby potentially alleviating irAEs. A study revealed that thymol sulfate levels could negatively regulate the function of CD39 on monocytes, thereby reducing the risk of irAEs. These findings suggest that thymol sulfate levels may play a key role in regulating the dynamic balance of CD39 on monocytes, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. Thymol sulfate [33] is a metabolic product of thymol, which possesses strong anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, protecting cells from damage caused by free radicals and chronic inflammation. Research indicates [34] that thymol can alleviate inflammation by inhibiting the separation of IκB proteins and NF-κB dimers and the activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase signaling pathway; Besides, thymol can interact with dendritic cells, lymphocytes, Treg cells, macrophages, and neutrophils to modulate immune balance. CD39 [35] is a nuclease expressed on the cell surface that hydrolyzes extracellular ATP to convert it to AMP, while AMP is hydrolyzed by CD73 to adenosine; adenosine acts as a potent immunomodulator preventing antitumor responses by binding to A2A receptors on lymphocytes. In the tumor immune microenvironment [36], high expression of CD39 activates pathways related to the inflammatory response, such as the α-interferon response and inflammatory responses, which are conducive to tumor growth; high CD39 expression has been associated with the occurrence of bladder cancer. In another mouse experiment [37], CD39 was found to be upregulated in colitis, and an inhibitor of CD39 was shown to reverse the vasoconstriction response caused by ATP. In autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus, the expression of CD39, a key molecule in the adenosine metabolic pathway, is decreased, resulting in an excessive immune response and tissue and organ damage [38]. Although there is currently no direct evidence showing how thymol sulfate levels regulate CD39 on monocytes to mitigate the occurrence of high-grade irAEs, the aforementioned studies provide theoretical support for further in-depth exploration of the mechanisms linking metabolism-immunity-disease. Etiocholanolone glucuronide is one of the primary metabolites of testosterone, a class of testosterone metabolites that also includes dihydrotestosterone glucuronide, testosterone glucuronide, and dihydrotestosterone glucuronide. Indeed, the liver is the key site for etiocholanolone glucuronide formation, after which etiocholanolone glucuronide is released into the blood [39]. Previous research has suggested that etiocholanolone glucuronide is associated with the risk of breast cancer [40]. However, the associations between etiocholanolone glucuronide and irAEs remain largely unexplored. Importantly, our study findings provide new insights into the relationship between etiocholanolone glucuronide and irAEs. CCR2 is a G protein-coupled receptor primarily involved in the chemotaxis and migration of immune cells, and it is expressed on macrophages, monocytes, and dendritic cells. A study has shown that cytotoxic CD8+ T cells can interact with liver CCR2+ monocytes, leading to immune-mediated liver damage [41]. Moreover, the CCR2/CCL2 axis in the tumor microenvironment drives and promotes local immune suppression and metastasis development. However, studies have shown that monocytes/macrophages expressing CCR2 within the tumor microenvironment, which polarize monocytes into metastasis-promoting M2 macrophages, may exert potent immunosuppressive effects [41, 42]. Based on the current evidence, this study provides a theoretical basis for enhancing etiocholanolone glucuronide levels in protecting monocytes against high-grade irAEs through the CCR2 pathway, although further validation remains necessary.

Although the pathogenesis of irAEs is not fully understood, existing studies have demonstrated that a variety of immune cells, including effector T cells, Treg, B cells, and dendritic cells, are involved in the pathogenesis of irAEs [43]. Our study revealed that EM CD4+ T cell are associated with an increased risk of irAEs. EM CD4+ %T cell refers to the percentage of effector memory CD4+ T cell in the CD4+ T-cell population. It is well-established that memory CD4+ T cells have unique functional attributes in the secondary immune response, enabling them to recruit the innate immune system at an early stage and enhance the immune response at a later stage through direct effector function [44]. A study conducted on patients with advanced melanoma undergoing treatment with ICIs (such as dual blockade of PD-1/CTLA-4) revealed that patients who were CMV seropositive experienced expansion of effector memory CD4+ T cells before treatment, which increased the risk of hepatitis [45]. B cells are important white blood cells in the immune system that are primarily responsible for humoral immune responses.They can express CD25 early in their maturation. It has been reported that CD25+ B lymphocytes are highly specialized professional antigen-presenting B cells, whose CD25 expression is strictly regulated. Unlike CD25+ CD4+ T cells, these cells function as enhancers of immune responses [46]. Tregs are a subset of T cells with immune regulatory functions that are primarily responsible for maintaining the balance of the immune system and preventing excessive immune responses that could lead to autoimmune diseases and inflammatory damage [47]. Moreover, previous studies have demonstrated that Treg cells release immunosuppressive cytokines, including TGFβ and IL10, to promote tissue repair following autoimmune responses in vivo [48]. However, it has also been demonstrated that Treg cells in patients with irAEs undergo significant transcriptome reprogramming, involving inflammatory, apoptoticand metabolic pathways. Specifically, in patients with irAEs, genes such as CXCL2, CXCL16, CXCL10, and CCR1, which are associated with inflammation and leukocyte migration, are upregulated in Treg cells. Downregulated genes such as CCR3 and SERPINE2 are associated with Treg cells function and metabolic processes [49]. These findings suggest that Treg cells increase the risk of irAEs.

Metabolites are small molecular compounds produced during cellular metabolism and are direct products of the intracellular and extracellular environment; thus, they are more reflective of the physiological state of disease. In our study, we found that metabolites of the polyamine class, including spermidine levels, the spermidine-to-pyruvate ratio, the spermidine-to-histidine ratio, and the spermidine-to-phosphate ratio, have a protective effect against irAEs. Spermidine is a polyamine widely present in living organisms and has attracted significant attention due to its biological roles in regulating DNA synthesis, promoting cell proliferation, and modulating ion channel function [50]. Studies have shown that spermidine is an inhibitor of immune responses capable of downregulating neutrophil motility and exerting immunosuppressive effects on T cells [51]. These findings provide compelling evidence that spermidine and related compounds can reduce the risk of irAEs. In addition, acylcarnitines are a class of carnitine-containing acyl chains that are involved in fatty acid metabolism, transporting organic acids and fatty acids from the cytoplasm to the mitochondria for breakdown and energy production. A study revealed that plasma levels of multiple acylcarnitines, such as decenoylcarnitine (AcCa (10:1) 2), decenoylcarnitine (AcCa (10:1) 3), and decenoylcarnitine (AcCa (10:1)) were significantly elevated in patients with irAEs [15]. This phenomenon may be related to their involvement in T cell proliferation and B cell activation, which in turn promotes the immune response. In our study, decanoylcarnitine (C10) levels were associated with the risk of developing high-grade irAEs, which is consistent with the above conclusions. Notably, several metabolites, including serotonin and gamma-glutamyltyrosine were associated with the occurrence of irAEs in our study. Although no direct study has shown a direct correlation between these metabolites and irAEs, this finding provides new directions and potential biomarkers for future research.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. (1) Although MR studies can provide valuable insights into the occurrence and progression of irAEs, the estimates are derived from aggregated data. Accordingly, this approach cannot elucidate mechanisms specific to individual types of irAEs. (2) Given that our analysis is based exclusively on European genomic databases, the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic populations remains uncertain. (3) We selected the IV using a significance threshold of p < 1 × 10− 5. The correlations between these IVs and the exposure were not extremely strong, so the interpretation of the results requires caution. (4) The sample size of GWAS data for irAE patients is small, which may limit the statistical efficacy of the analysis. (5) While MR minimizes certain forms of bias, it is not a substitute for clinical trials. Therefore, our results necessitate further validation through prospective clinical and functional studies.

Conclusion

This study systematically assessed the potential causal relationships between immune cell phenotypes, metabolites, and irAEs. The results indicated that multiple immune cell phenotypes and metabolites have significant causal effects on both all-grade and high-grade irAEs. Notably, the levels of the metabolites thymol sulfate and etiocholanolone glucuronide partially mediate the causal impact of the immune cell phenotype CD39 on monocyte and CCR2 on monocyte in high-grade irAEs, respectively. These findings not only provide new research directions for the in-depth exploration of the biological mechanisms of irAEs but also offer novel ideas for the development of early interventions and therapeutic strategies targeting irAEs.

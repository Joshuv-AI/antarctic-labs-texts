On two systems of Burgers type arising in nonlinear wave interactions
Diego Alonso-Orán, Rafael Granero-Belinchón

Abstract
. In this note we study the well-posedness of two systems of Burgers type arising in nonlinear wave interactions. The first model describes the interaction of a Burger’s bore with the classical Korteweg-de Vries equation while the second exemplify the interaction of weak sound waves and entropy waves with small amplitudes. For the former, we show the local existence and uniqueness of solutions in Sobolev spaces and Wiener-type spaces. For the latter, we provide an elementary proof of finite time singularity.



1.
Introduction

Wave interactions play a crucial role in the dynamics of various fluid systems. More precisely, In the field of hydrodynamics, waves are generated by a range of disturbances in a fluid medium, including wind stress on ocean surfaces, pressure fluctuations, and gravitational influences. These waves can manifest as surface gravity waves, internal waves within stratified fluids, capillary waves, and solitons, each defined by unique restoring forces and patterns of wave behavior. Investigating the interactions between these waves is essential for gaining insights into the transfer of energy, momentum, and mass across varying spatial and temporal scales. A key element of wave interactions in hydrodynamics is nonlinearity. While linear theory can effectively describe basic wave propagation, it often falls short in addressing the complexities of actual wave interactions. Nonlinear phenomena become particularly significant when wave amplitudes are large or when waves travel through highly variable environments. In the present work we are interested in studying two different systems describing the interactions of waves.

\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]

is defined as

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{T})=\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}),\partial_{x}^{s}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})\}

where

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Without loss of generality we assume that
\gamma=\nu=1
(the proof can be easily adapted to different, positive values of
\nu
and
\gamma
). The proof follows from the combination of appropriate a priori energy estimates and the use of a suitable approximation procedure using the periodic heat kernel,
[
10
]
. Thus, we first focus in deriving a priori energy estimates and later comment briefly on the approximation procedure to construct the solution.

A priori energy estimates

Integrating system (
1.6
) in space and using the periodicity we find that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x,t)\ \diff x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x)\ \diff x,\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}v(x,t)\ \diff x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{0}(x)\ \diff x,

thus, the mean property is conserved in time. It is then sufficient to focus on the higher-order estimates
(u,v)\in\dot{H}^{3}\times\dot{H}^{4}
. The evolution of the
\dot{H}^{3}
norm is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|\partial_{x}^{4}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{2}(u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}^{4}u\ \diff x-6\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{3}(v^{2}\partial_{x}v)\partial_{x}^{3}u\ \diff x-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{3}(v\partial_{x}^{3}v)\partial_{x}^{3}u\ \diff x

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=-\|\partial_{x}^{4}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Using integration by parts and Hölder inequality we obtain that

(2.1)

|I_{1}|\leq C\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad|I_{2}|\leq C\|\partial_{x}v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}

Similarly, we compute the evolution of the
\dot{H}^{4}
norm of
v

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=6\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{4}(v\partial_{x}v)\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{4}v\partial_{x}^{7}v\ \diff x+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{5}(uv)\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x.

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

Once again, the Burgers type term can be bounded by

(2.2)

|J_{1}|\leq C\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and
J_{2}
vanishes using the periodicity condition since

J_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}\left(\partial_{x}^{5}v\right)^{2}\diff x=0.

The remaining terms
I_{3}
and
J_{3}
must be treated more carefully. We first decompose
J_{3}
as

J_{3}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{6}K_{j},

where

K_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{5}uv\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x,\quad K_{2}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\partial_{x}^{5}v\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x,\quad K_{3}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{4}u\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x,

and

K_{4}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{3}u\partial_{x}^{2}v\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x,\quad K_{5}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{2}u\partial_{x}^{3}v\partial_{x}^{4}v\ \diff x,\quad K_{6}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}u(\partial_{x}^{4}v)^{2}\ \diff x.

We first notice that using Hölder inequality and Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert f_{x}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}},

to estimate
K_{4}
and
K_{5}
we infer that

(2.3)

|K_{2}+K_{4}+K_{5}+K_{6}|\leq C\left(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{x}^{2}v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right).

On the other hand, recalling the term
I_{3}
and integrating twice by parts we find that

I_{3}=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{5}u(\partial_{x}v\partial_{x}^{3}v+v\partial_{x}^{4}v)\diff x,

and hence combining
I_{3}+K_{1}
we infer that

I_{3}+K_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{4}u\partial_{x}^{4}v\partial_{x}v\diff x+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{x}^{4}u\partial_{x}^{2}v\partial_{x}^{3}v\diff x.

Therefore, we have that

|I_{3}+K_{1}|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{4}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Notice that the previous structure in
I_{3}+K_{1}
can be obtained as long as
\gamma>0
. Similarly,

|K_{3}|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{4}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

In particular, using
\varepsilon
-Young’s inequality, we can find

(2.4)

|I_{3}+K_{1}+K_{3}|\leq\varepsilon\|\partial_{x}^{4}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right),

for
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary. Therefore, collecting estimates (
2.1
)-(
2.4
), using the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, for
\epsilon>0
and Young’s inequality we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|u\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{4}}^{2})+(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\partial_{x}^{4}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{4}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\|u\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{4}}^{2})^{2}.

Therefore, denoting by
\mathcal{E}(t)=\|u(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}
we find that

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}(0)-Ct},\quad 0<t<T_{\max},

which ensures a local uniform time of existence for the solution

(u,v)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T_{\max});H^{3}\times H^{4}\right).

The approximation procedure

The local existence of solution will follow now from a standard application of Picard’s theorem to a sequence of approximate problems via the periodic heat kernel,
[
10
]
. Indeed, these sequence of regularized problems have a unique solution
(u^{\epsilon},v^{\epsilon})=C^{1}([0,T_{\epsilon}];H^{3}\times H^{4})
. By repeating the same energy estimates, we can take
T_{\max}=T(\mathcal{E}_{0})
independent of
\epsilon
and passing to the limit we conclude the existence of at least one solution in

(u,v)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T_{\max});H^{3}\times H^{4}\right),

that we skip for the sake of brevity.

Uniqueness

At this level of regularity, the uniqueness of such local strong solution can be easily obtained from a standard contradiction argument. Indeed, assume there exists two soutions
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})
corresponding to the same initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
. Denoting by
(\overline{u},\overline{v})=(u_{1}-u{2},v_{1}-v_{2})
we find that

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{u}+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}\left(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\partial_{x}\left(v_{1}^{3}-v_{2}^{3}\right)-\gamma\left(v_{1}\partial_{x}^{3}v_{1}-v_{2}\partial_{x}^{3}v_{2}\right)+\nu\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u},

(2.5b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{v}-3\partial_{x}\left(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{v}+\partial_{x}(u_{1}v_{1})-\partial_{x}(u_{2}v_{2}).

Then, multiplying by
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
, integrating by parts and repeating the previous estimates we have that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\nu\left\lVert\partial_{x}\overline{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\beta(t)\left(\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

with

\beta(t)=\left(\|\partial_{x}v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Invoking Grönwalls inequality,

\left(\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq 2\left(\|\overline{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\overline{v}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\mbox{exp}\big(\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\ \diff s \big),

and hence by means of the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, the uniqueness follows.

Endpoint continuity in time

To obtain the endpoint continuity, we regularize the initial data using the periodic heat kernel at time
t=\epsilon
for certain
\epsilon>0
fixed. The corresponding solution
(u^{\epsilon},v^{\epsilon})
exists in

(u^{\epsilon},v^{\epsilon})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{4}\times H^{5})\cap C([0,T],H^{3}\times H^{4}),

up to certain positive time that depends on the
C^{1}\times C^{2}
semi-norms of the solutions. Thus, they are uniformly bounded in terms of the
H^{3}\times H^{4}
size of the initial data. As a consequence, both solutions
(u,v)
and
(u^{\epsilon},v^{\epsilon})
are defined in the same lifespan. We consider now

\sup_{t}(\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|v(t)-v^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{4}}).

Performing the same kind of energy estimates as before (and the same as in the uniqueness step), we find that

\sup_{t}(\|u(t)-u^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|v(t)-v^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{4}})\leq C\epsilon,

from where the uniform convergence is obtained. The continuity follows.

Proof of Theorem

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, let us focus on deriving the a priori energy estimates. Recall that the space
\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}^{s}

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}^{s}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}^{s}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}^{s}}.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\mu(t)|k|}|\widehat{u}(k,t)|=\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k|\mu^{\prime}(t)e^{\mu(t)|k|}|\widehat{u}(k,t)|+\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\mu(t)|k|}\mathfrak{R}\bigg(\widehat{\partial_{t}u}(k,t)\frac{\overline{\widehat{u}(k,t)}}{|\widehat{u}(k,t)|}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\prime}(t)\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k|e^{\mu(t)|k|}|\widehat{u}(k,t)|+\left\lVert u_{t}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}

Therefore, taking
\mu(t)=\mu_{0}-Ct
for a certain
C
to be chosen and using equation (
1.6a
) we find that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}

\displaystyle\leq-C\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k|e^{\mu(t)|k|}|\widehat{u}(k,t)|+\left\lVert-3u\partial_{x}u-6v^{2}\partial_{x}v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}.

Using the Banach Algebra property we observe that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}

\displaystyle\leq-C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}+3\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}+6\left\lVert v^{2}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}.

Repeating the same computations for the evolution of
v
, one can show that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}

\displaystyle\leq-C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}+6\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}+\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{\dot{A}}^{1}_{\mu(t)}}

Collecting both estimates and taking

C=2(3\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu_{0}}}+6\|v_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu_{0}}}^{2}+6\|v_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu_{0}}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu_{0}}}+\|v_{0}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu_{0}}})

we find that

\frac{d}{dt}(\|u\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mu(t)}})\leq 0.

for certain uniform time
T=T(u_{0},v_{0},\mu_{0})
. After this, the existence of solution follows from a standard mollifier
approach together with Picard’s theorem as shown in Theorem
1.1
. Similarly, the uniqueness follows from a standard contradiction argument. This concludes the result.

3.
Proof of the Theorem
1.6

We argue by contradiction. Let us assume that the solution of (
1.5
) remains smooth globally in time for initial data
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
satisfying the hypothesis stated in Theorem
1.6
. First notice that the odd symmetry is preserved for smooth solutions of the evolution equation (
1.5
). Define

U^{(\ell)}(t)=\partial_{x}^{\ell}u(x,t)\bigg|_{x=0},

and

V^{(\ell)}(t)=\partial_{x}^{\ell}v(x,t)\bigg|_{x=0}.

Then, we compute that the solution of (
1.5
) satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}U^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-(U^{(1)})^{2}+V^{(1)},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-(V^{(1)})^{2}-U^{(1)}.

To show the finite time blow up of this system we define the functions

F(t)=U^{(1)}-V^{(1)},

G(t)=U^{(1)}+V^{(1)}.

Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F

\displaystyle=-FG+G,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}G

\displaystyle=-G^{2}+2((F+G)/2)((G-F)/2)-F.

As a consequence, we find that if
F(0)=1
, then
F(t)=1
and the equation for
G
becomes

\frac{d}{dt}G=-G^{2}+(1+G)(G-1)/2-1=-G^{2}/2-3/2.

From here we find that if
G(0)<-1
the solution blows up in finite time and this is a contradiction with our initial hypotheses of global solvability.

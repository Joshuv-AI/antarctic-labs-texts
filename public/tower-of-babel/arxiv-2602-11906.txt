Largeness notions and polytime translation for ∀ Σ 3 0 \forall\Sigma^{0}_{3} -consequences of 𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}

Abstract
Le Houérou, Patey and Yokoyama defined a parameterized version of α \alpha -largeness to prove that 𝖶𝖪𝖫 0 + 𝖱𝖳 2 2 \mathsf{WKL}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2} is a ∀ Σ 3 0 \forall\Sigma^{0}_{3} -conservative extension of 𝖱𝖢𝖠 0 + 𝖡 ​ Σ 2 0 \mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2} , where ∀ Σ 3 0 \forall\Sigma^{0}_{3} is the universal set-closure of the class of Σ 3 0 \Sigma^{0}_{3} -formulas. We introduce a variant of this notion of largeness and obtain polynomial bounds, using a tree partition theorem based on Milliken’s tree theorem. If a computably axiomatized theory

1.1
Notation

Most of the proofs are over a weak subsystem of second-order arithmetic. We therefore distinguish the formal set of integers
\mathbb{N}
from the theory from the standard set of integers
\omega
from the meta-theory. For instance, if
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
is a non-standard model of second-order arithmetic,
\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{M}}=M
and
\omega^{\mathcal{M}}=\{0^{\mathcal{M}},1^{\mathcal{M}},\dots\}\subseteq M
. Similarly, we distinguish the formal ordinals of the theory and the ordinals from the meta-theory. We therefore write for instance
\bbomega^{\bbomega}
for the formal counter-part of
\omega^{\omega}
. Indeed, since
\bbomega^{\bbomega}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bbomega^{n}
, if
\omega\subsetneq M
, then
\bbomega
and a fortiori
\bbomega^{\bbomega}
are not well-founded from the viewpoint of the meta-theory.

We often identify an integer
k
with its ordinal
\{0,\dots,k-1\}
.
Given a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
[X]^{n}
for the collection of all subsets of
X
of size
n
.
The set
[X]^{n}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of all increasing ordered
n
-tuples over
X
. Therefore, for simplicity of notation, given a coloring
f:[X]^{n}\to k
, we write
f(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})
for
f(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}\})
assuming that
x_{0}<\dots<x_{n-1}
.

2
Notions of largeness

Partial conservation theorems are closely related to ordinal computation for appropriate notions of largeness.
In this section, we survey three notions of largeness related to our polytime translation.

2.1
Largeness and sparsity

Definition 2.1
.

A family
\mathcal{L}\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{<\mathbb{N}}
is a largeness notion if any infinite set has a finite subset in
\mathcal{L}

and
\mathcal{L}
is closed under supersets. A finite set

is
\mathcal{L}
-large
if
F\in\mathcal{L}
.

We are particularly interested in the closure of notions of largeness under combinatorial statements coming from Ramsey theory.
Given a function
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
and a finite set
G=\{x_{0}<\dots<x_{\ell-1}\}
, let
f_{G}:[\ell]^{n}\to k
be defined by
f_{G}(i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1})=f(x_{i_{0}},\dots,x_{i_{n-1}})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given

, an
\mathsf{RT}
-like statement

-formula of the form

(\forall f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k)[\Phi(f,\mathbb{N})\to(\exists Y)(Y\mbox{is infinite}\wedge\Psi(f,Y))]

and

(\forall G\subseteq_{\mathtt{fin}}Y)\Theta(f_{G})

\Delta^{0}_{0}

Ramsey’s theorem for
n
-tuples and

-colors is the most obvious example of an
\mathsf{RT}
-like statement:
Given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k

is
f
-homogeneous
if

is constant on
[H]^{n}
.

Statement

(Ramsey’s theorem)
.

Given
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,

-homogeneous set”.

In this case,

\Psi_{\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}}(f,Y)
means “
Y
is
f
-homogeneous”. Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and its consequences is a central subject of study in reverse mathematics
[
40
,
6
,
33

[

,
36
,
28
]

Our main focus in this article will be to study of largeness closure under applications of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}

\mathsf{RT}
-like statements: Given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2

is
f
-transitive
if for every
i<2

is transitive.

Statement

(Erdős-Moser theorem)
.

\mathsf{EM}
is the statement “For every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, there is an infinite

-transitive set”.

Statement

(Ascending Descending Sequence)
.

\mathsf{ADS}
is the statement “For every transitive coloring

-homogeneous set”.

The Erdős-Moser theorem is originally a statement from graph theory
[
12
]
formulated in terms of tournaments, that is, complete oriented graphs. Infinite graphs are one-to-one correspondence with colorings

Both statements have been extensively studied in computability theory and reverse mathematics
[
5
,
32
,
31
,
16
]

In particular, the first-order part of

is well-understood, as

is a

-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}

and by applying the techniques of

is polynomially simulated by
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}

.

Let

Sparsity.

Every notion of largeness induces a dual notion of sparsity.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{<\mathbb{N}}
be a largeness notion.
A set

is
\mathcal{L}
-sparse

(x,y]_{\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{L}

By closure of largeness notions under supersets, any subset of an

-sparse.
Any increasing function

Then, an
\mathcal{L}_{g}

We will be particularly interested in

[

, one can assume that the sets are

-sparse for any fixed primitive recursive function

Although sparsity can be translated into some largeness assumption, it is convenient to separate the largeness and sparsity hypothesis in the ordinal computation of largeness, as sparsity has a better behavior with respect to composition of

-like statements and avoids overheads redundancy.

3
Combinatorics of largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)

In this section, we develop the framework of
\alpha
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
. It admits many similarities with the development of Le Houérou, Levy Patey and Yokoyama
[
28
]
, with better upper bounds. However, the proofs and notation a significantly more complicated due to the structural differences of the two notions of largeness.

We start with a technical lemma which formalizes the intuition that
\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize d\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}}k)
admits a tree structure, with some
\theta
-apartness conditions at each node. More precisely, every set in
\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize d\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}}k)
can be represented as a
k
-regular tree of depth

whose leaves are pairwise disjoint
\mathcal{L}
-large sets, with some
\theta
-apartness property at every node of the tree.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a notion of largeness. A set
X
is in
\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize d\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}}k)
iff
there is a map
g:k^{\leq d}\to\mathcal{L}\cap[X]^{<\mathbb{N}}
such that

(a)

for every
\sigma\in k^{<d}
,
g(\sigma)=\bigcup_{i<k}g(\sigma\cdot i)

(b)

for every
\ell\leq d
and
\sigma,\tau\in k^{\ell}
with
\sigma<_{lex}\tau
, then
g(\sigma)<g(\tau)
and
g(\sigma)
and

are
\theta
-apart

Proof.

By induction on
d
.

For

, let
X
be in
\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize 0\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},k)=\mathcal{L}
, and let

defined by

,

vacuously satisfy
(a)
and
(b)
. Reciprocally, given a function

satisfying
(a)
and

,

belongs to
\mathcal{L}\cap[X]^{<\mathbb{N}}
, hence

is in
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize 0\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},k)
.

Assume the property to be true for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
X
be in
\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize d+1\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},k)
, there exists pairwise
\theta
-apart subsets
X_{0}<\dots<X_{k-1}\subseteq X
such that
X_{i}\in\mathcal{L}\cdot(k,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize d\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},k)
for every

. By the inductive hypothesis applied to every

, there exists maps
g_{i}:k^{\leq d}\to\mathcal{L}\cap[X_{i}]^{<\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
(a)
and
(b)
for every

. Let

defined by

and

for every

and

. It is clear that

satisfies
(a)
and
(b)
.

Reciprocally, consider such a function

satisfying

and
(b)
. For

and
(b)

and are pairwise

3.1
Construction and deconstruction

We now prove the standard construction and deconstruction propositions for this new notion of largeness. They intuitively state sufficient conditions for a large union of
\bbomega^{b}
-large sets to be
\bbomega^{a+b}
-large, and conversely, the amount of largeness necessary to obtain an
\bbomega^{a}
-large collection of
\bbomega^{b}
-large sets.

There are multiple possible interpretations of “large collection” of sets.
In the weakest sense,
\{\max X_{i}:i<k\}
is large, and in the strongest sense, any set
H\in\prod_{i<k}[\min X_{i},\max X_{i}]
is large. We shall prove the construction proposition (
Proposition

3.2
) with the weakest hypothesis, and the deconstruction proposition (
Proposition

3.3
) with the strongest conclusion.
Note that in the case of the notion of largeness of Ketonen and Solovay, both interpretations are equivalent, by regularity: indeed, if
E=\{x_{0}<\dots<x_{k-1}\}
is
\alpha
-large, and
F=\{y_{0}<\dots<y_{k-1}\}
is such that
y_{i}\leq x_{i}
for every
i<k
, then
F
is also
\alpha
-large.

Proposition 3.2

(Construction,
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
)
.

Let

. Let

-apart
\bbomega^{a}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)

\bbomega^{\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor+2b}

\bbomega^{a+b}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Proof.

By induction on

. The case

Assume the property to be true for some

. And let

{}^{*}(\theta)

-large
{}^{*}(\theta)

\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
. We now prove a partial reversal, which will be useful in

to translate existing theorems over largeness
(\theta)

By

\bbomega^{n}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize k\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)
-large
(\theta)

\min X
-regular tree of depth

\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)

\theta
-apartness conditions.
Similarly, by the definition of largeness
(\theta)
, any
\bbomega^{n+k}
-large
(\theta)
set can be seen as a tree of
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
sets, with a level of branching of the parent nodes depending on the children. This “dependently branching” tree is much more branching than
\min X
. The following lemma reflects this explanation:

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
n\geq 0
and
k>0
.
If

is
\bbomega^{n+k}
-large
(\theta)
, then

is

\bbomega^{n}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize k\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)
-large
(\theta)
.

Proof.

By induction on
k
.
Case
k=1
. Then
X\setminus\{\min X\}
is
\bbomega^{n}\cdot\min X
-large
(\theta)
by definition.
Suppose
k>1
. Then
X\setminus\{\min X\}
is
\bbomega^{n+k-1}\cdot\min X
-large
(\theta)
.
Let
X_{0}<\dots<X_{\min X-1}\subseteq X\setminus\{\min X\}
be pairwise
\theta
-apart
\bbomega^{n+k-1}
-large
(\theta)
sets.
By induction hypothesis, for every
i<\min X
, letting
Y_{i}=X_{i}\setminus\{\min X_{i}\}
,
Y_{i}
is
\bbomega^{n}\cdot(\min X_{i},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize k-1\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X_{i})
-large
(\theta)
, hence is
\bbomega^{n}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize k-1\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)
-large
(\theta)
. Moreover, the
Y_{i}
’s are pairwise
\theta
-apart, so
X\setminus\{\min X\}
is
\bbomega^{n}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize k\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)
-large
(\theta)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove a partial reversal to the fact that every
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
set is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

If

is
\bbomega^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
-large

, then it is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Proof.

By induction on
n
.
Case
n=0
. If
X
is
\bbomega^{0}
-large
(\theta)
, then
X\neq\emptyset
so
X
is
\bbomega^{0}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.
Case
n>0
. If

is
\bbomega^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
-large
(\theta)
, then it is
\bbomega^{\frac{(n-1)n}{2}+n}
-large
(\theta)
. By
Lemma

,
X\setminus\{\min X\}
is
\bbomega^{\frac{(n-1)n}{2}}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize n\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)
-large
(\theta)
. By induction hypothesis,

is
\bbomega^{n-1}\cdot(\min X,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize n\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},\min X)

-large

∎

3.3
Cut

Recall that a
cut
is an initial segment of
\mathbb{N}
closed under successor.
A cut
I
is
additive
if for every
n\in I
,
n+n\in I
.

Let
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}
(resp.
\mathsf{J}^{*}_{\theta}
) be the set of all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that every infinite set contains a finite subset that is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
(resp.
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
). The authors showed in
[
28
]
that
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}
is an additive cut, provably in
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
. In this section, we study the cut
\mathsf{J}^{*}_{\theta}
, and show that it satisfies the same closure properties as
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
, that is, it is an additive cut, and no more closure properties are provable, in that there is a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)\models\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
and some
a\in M\setminus\omega
such that
\mathsf{J}^{*}_{\theta}(\mathcal{M})=\sup(a\cdot n:n\in\omega)
.

Lemma 3.7

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a largeness notion. For every infinite set
X
,
there is an infinite sequence
E_{0}<E_{1}<\dots
of pairwise
\theta
-apart
\mathcal{L}
-large subsets of
X
.

Proof.

Consider the greedy algorithm enumerating such a sequence.
Once
E_{0}<\dots<E_{k-1}
have been listed, search for some
E_{k}>E_{k-1}
that is
\theta
-apart from
E_{0},\dots,E_{k-1}
and
\mathcal{L}
-large. If for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
E_{0},\dots,E_{k-1}
have been outputted by this procedure, since
T
and
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
hold, there exists a bound
b_{k}
such that every subset of
[b_{k},+\infty)
is
\theta
-apart from
E_{0}\cup\dots\cup E_{k-1}
.
Since
X\cap[b_{k},+\infty)
is an infinite set and
\mathcal{L}
is a largeness notion, there exists some
\mathcal{L}
-large subset
E_{k}\subseteq X\cap[b_{k},+\infty)
, hence the greedy algorithm will eventually list some
E_{k}
.
∎

Proposition 3.8

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
)
.

\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
is an additive cut.

Proof.

Any
\bbomega^{n+1}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
set contains many
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subsets, so by
\Delta^{0}_{0}
-induction which holds over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
,
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
is an initial segment of
\mathbb{N}
.

By
[
28
]
,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
proves that
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}
is a cut, so
\omega\subseteq\mathsf{J}_{\theta}
.
By
Proposition

3.6
, every
\bbomega^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
-large
(\theta)
set is
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
. It follows that
\omega\subseteq\mathsf{J}^{*}_{\theta}
.

We now prove that if
n\in\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
, then
n+\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor\in\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
.
Suppose that
n\in\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
. Let
X
be an infinite set.
By
Lemma

3.7
, there is a countable collection
E_{0}<E_{1}<\dots
of pairwise
\theta
-apart
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subsets of
X
.
The set
Y=\{\max E_{s}:s\in\mathbb{N}\}
is infinite, so there is an
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subset

.
By
Proposition

3.2
with

and

,

is an
\bbomega^{n+\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor}
-large

.

Next, we prove that if

, then

.
Let
n\in\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
. If

, so since
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}

Since
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
is an initial segment of

∎

The remainder of this section is mostly focused on proving that
\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}
satisfies no better closure property than additivity over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+T
.
A cut
I\subseteq M
is said to be

if for every
M
-finite set

such that

,

is bounded in

.
The following proposition shows that semi-regular cuts are exactly those coding a model of

:

Proposition 3.9

(See Scott
[
39
]
and Kirby and Paris
[
21
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Let

be a cut and let

. Then

is semi-regular if and only if
(I,\operatorname{Cod}(M/I))\models\mathsf{WKL}_{0}
.

In what follows, let
\mathsf{PA}^{-}
denote the axioms of a discrete ordered commutative semiring (see
[
17

Proposition 3.10

Let

\mathsf{J}_{\theta}^{*}(\mathcal{M})=\sup\{a\cdot k:k\in\omega\}

By

3.8

Lemma 3.12

Let

3.4
Sparsity

In Ketonen and Solovay’s notion of largeness, a set

From a purely logical viewpoint, one could have fixed a provably total, increasing function

\bbomega
-
g
-large. Over the remainder of this article, we shall use multiple times the same idea to artificially increase the number of branches of the induced tree in the definition of an

-large
{}^{*}(\theta)

, the provably total computable functions are the primitive recursive ones.

3.6
Partition theorem for regular trees

The purpose of this section is to prove a Ramsey-type theorem for colorings of pairs of leaves of a regular tree (
Proposition

3.26
), which will be used in the inductive step of the computations of
\mathsf{EM}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
and
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
. For this, we will use a finite version of Milliken’s tree theorem with primitive recursive bounds
[
34
,
11
]
.

Milliken’s tree theorem is formulated in terms of
strong subforests
, which is a notion of subforest perserving some structural properties of the original forest. More precisely, nodes at the same level of a strong subforest have to come from the same level in the original forest, and the branching degree must be preserved. In this section, we shall exclusively work with strong suforests of
(2^{<\mathbb{N}},\dots,2^{<\mathbb{N}})
, and define the notion of strong subforest only in this specialized case.

Definition 3.20
.

A finite sequence
\vec{T}=(T_{0},\dots,T_{d-1})
of finite subsets of
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
is a
strong
d
-forest
if there is some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, a
level function

h:\{0,\dots,\ell\}\to\mathbb{N}
and a finite sequence of functions
(f_{0},\dots,f_{d-1})
with
f_{i}:2^{\leq\ell}\to T_{i}
such that for every
i<d

(1)

for every
\sigma\in 2^{\leq\ell}
,
|f_{i}(\sigma)|=h(|\sigma|)
;

(2)

for every
\sigma\in 2^{<\ell}
and
i<2
,
f_{i}(\sigma\cdot i)\succeq f_{i}(\sigma)\cdot i
.

We call
\ell
the
height
of
\vec{T}
, and write
\mathrm{Leaves}(T_{i})=\{f_{i}(\sigma):\sigma\in 2^{\ell}\}
for the set of
leaves
of
T_{i}
.

Remark 3.21
.

Note that contrary to computability theory in which a tree is classically required to be closed under prefix, the notion of tree in Milliken’s tree theorem has to be understood as the structure
(S,\preceq)
for a subset
S\subseteq 2^{<\mathbb{N}}
.

Given a strong
d
-forest
\vec{T}=(T_{0},\dots,T_{d-1})

\mathrm{Str}_{\ell}(\vec{T})
for the set of all strong
d
-forests

of depth

such that for every

The following theorem is the restriction of Dodos et al.
[
11

to strong forests. We write

for the

[
13
]
of primitive recursive functions.

Theorem 3.22

(Finite Milliken’s tree theorem (see
[
11
]
))

For every integer

and every triple

of positive integers with
\ell\geq k

with the following property. If

Let
n,x\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a primitive recursive bound
B=B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(n,x)
such that, for
(T,\preceq)\cong(B^{\leq 2n-1},\preceq)
, and for every coloring
f:[\mathrm{Leaves}(T)]^{2}\to 2
, there exists a subtree
S\subseteq T
such that
(S,\preceq)\cong(x^{\leq n},\preceq)
,
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)\subseteq\mathrm{Leaves}(T)
and
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
is
f
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of integers
M_{0},\dots,M_{2n-2}
defined inductively as follows:

\begin{cases}M_{i}=B_{\ref{lem:singleton-coloring-one-step}}(2^{M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1}},B_{\ref{lem:pairs-coloring-one-step}}(2^{M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1}},x),2^{2n-1-i})&\textit{ for }i<2n-2\\
M_{2n-2}=B_{\ref{lem:pairs-coloring-one-step}}(2^{M_{0}+\dots+M_{2n-3}},x)&\end{cases}

Note that, in the case
i=0
, the sum
M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1}
is equal to
0
, hence
M_{0}=B_{\ref{lem:singleton-coloring-one-step}}(1,B_{\ref{lem:pairs-coloring-one-step}}(1,x),2^{2n-1})
.
Then, let
B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}=2^{M_{2n-2}}
and let
T
be isomorphic to
B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}^{\leq 2n-1}
. Since
M_{i}\leq M_{2n-2}
for every
i\leq 2n-2
, we can then trim
T
and consider a subtree
T^{\prime}
of
T
such that the nodes of
T^{\prime}
at level
i<2n-2
are
2^{M_{i}}
branching. We can then view
T^{\prime}
as a
2
-branching tree of depth
M_{0}+\dots+M_{2n-2}
by replacing every
2^{M_{i}}
-branching by
M_{i}
consecutive
2
-branching.

Let
P_{0}=\{\epsilon\}
, that is, the singleton root of
T^{\prime}
and let
d_{0}=1=\operatorname{card}P_{0}
. For
1\leq k\leq 2n-1
, let
P_{k}
be the nodes of
T^{\prime}
at level
M_{0}+\dots+M_{k-1}
and let
d_{k}=2^{M_{0}+\dots+M_{k-1}}=\operatorname{card}P_{k}
. For
0\leq k\leq 2n-2
, let
\vec{T}^{k}=(T^{k}_{0},\dots,T^{k}_{d_{k}-1})
be the family of subtrees of
T^{\prime}
beneath the elements of
P_{k}
that corresponds to the levels between
M_{0}+\dots+M_{k-1}
and
M_{0}+\dots+M_{k}
of
T^{\prime}
. Notice that
P_{k+1}=\mathrm{Leaves}(T_{0}^{k})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(T_{d_{k}-1}^{k})
for every
k\leq 2n-2
, and that
P_{2n-1}=\mathrm{Leaves}(T^{\prime})
.

We can then define two families of colorings
f^{k}:[P_{k}]^{2}\to 2
and
g^{k}:P_{k}\to 2^{2n-1-k}
, as well as a family of forest
\vec{S}^{k}=(S_{0}^{k},\dots,S_{d_{k}-1}^{k})\in\mathrm{Str}_{x}^{l}(\vec{T}^{k})
, for
k\leq 2n-2
inductively as follows:

We have
M_{2n-2}=B_{\ref{lem:pairs-coloring-one-step}}(d_{2n-2},x)
, hence, by
Lemma

3.25
, there exists some
\vec{S}^{2n-2}=(S_{0}^{2n-2},\dots,S^{2n-2}_{d_{2n-2}-1})\in\mathrm{Str}_{x}^{l}(\vec{T}^{2n-2})
such that the value of
f
on
[\mathrm{Leaves}(S_{0}^{2n-2})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{2n-2}_{d_{2n-2}-1})]^{2}
only depends on the indexes of the
S^{2n-2}_{i}
where the leaves belong.

For
\sigma,\tau\in P_{2n-2}
, let
f^{2n-2}(\sigma,\tau)=f(\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
for any
\sigma^{\prime}\succ\sigma
and any
\tau^{\prime}\succ\tau
in
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{2n-2}_{0})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{2n-2}_{d_{2n-2}-1})
. For
\sigma\in P_{2n-2}
, let
g^{2n-2}(\sigma)=f(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})
for any
\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}\succ\sigma
in
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{2n-2}_{0})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{2n-2}_{d_{2n-2}-1})
. Note that both
f^{2n-2}
and
g^{2n-2}
are well-defined by choice of
\vec{S}^{2n-2}
.

Suppose that
f^{k+1}:[P_{k+1}]^{2}\to 2
and
g^{k+1}:P_{k+1}\to 2^{2n-1-k}
are defined for some
1\leq k<2n-2
. Since

M_{k}=B_{\ref{lem:singleton-coloring-one-step}}(d_{k},B_{\ref{lem:pairs-coloring-one-step}}(d_{k},x),2^{2n-1-k})
,
we can apply successively
Lemma

3.24
and
Lemma

3.25
and obtain some
\vec{S}^{k}=(S_{0}^{k},\dots,S^{k}_{d_{k}-1})\in\mathrm{Str}_{x}^{l}(\vec{T}^{k})
such that
g^{k+1}
is constant on each
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{i})
and such that the value of
f^{k+1}
on
[\mathrm{Leaves}(S_{0}^{k})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{d_{k}-1})]^{2}
only depends on the indexes of the
S^{k}_{i}
where the leaves belong.

For
\sigma,\tau\in P_{k}
, let
f^{k}(\sigma,\tau)=f^{k+1}(\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
for any
\sigma^{\prime}\succ\sigma
and any
\tau^{\prime}\succ\tau
in
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{0})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{d_{k}-1})\subseteq P_{k+1}
. For
\sigma\in P_{k}
, let
g^{k}(\sigma)=(f^{k+1}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}),g^{k+1}(\sigma^{\prime}))
for any
\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}\succ\sigma
in
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{0})\cup\dots\cup\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{k}_{d_{k}-1})\subseteq P_{k+1}
. Here again, this is well-defined by choice of
\vec{S}^{k}
.

Finally, consider the subtree
S^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime}
equal to
S^{0}_{0}
on levels
0
to
M_{0}
, then equal to the
S^{1}_{i}
beneath the leaves of
S^{0}_{0}
on levels
M_{0}
to
M_{0}+M_{1}
and so on.

Claim
:
for every
k\leq 2n-2
, every
\rho\in P_{k}\cap S^{\prime}
with
g^{k}(\rho)=(c_{k},\dots,c_{2n-2})
, and every pair
\sigma,\tau\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{\prime})
with
\sigma,\tau\succeq\rho
and with lowest common ancestor at level in the interval
[M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1},M_{0}+\dots+M_{i})
, we have
f(\sigma,\tau)=c_{i}
.
Proceed by induction on
k
, if
k=2n-2
, then let
\rho\in P_{2n-2}\cap S^{\prime}
and write
g^{2n-2}(\rho)=c_{2n-2}
. By construction,
g^{2n-2}(\rho)=f(\sigma,\tau)=c_{2n-2}
for every
\sigma,\tau\succ\rho
in
\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{\prime})
. Let
k<2n-2
and assume the property to be true for
k+1
, let
\rho\in P_{k}\cap S^{\prime}
and write
g^{k}(\rho)=(c_{k},\dots,c_{2n-2})
. Let
\sigma,\tau\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S^{\prime})
with
\sigma,\tau\succeq\rho
and with lowest common ancestor at level in the interval
[M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1},M_{0}+\dots+M_{i})
for some
i\leq 2n-2
, since
\sigma,\tau\succeq\rho
, we have necessarily
i\geq k
. If
i>k
, then let
\rho^{\prime}\in P_{k+1}\cap S^{\prime}
be the common prefix of
\sigma
and
\rho
in
P_{k+1}
, by definition of
g^{k}
,
g^{k+1}(\rho^{\prime})=(c_{k+1},\dots,c_{2n-2})
and by the induction hypothesis,
f(\sigma,\rho)=c_{i}
. If
i=k
, then let
\sigma_{k+1},\dots,\sigma_{2n-2}
and
\tau_{k+1},\dots,\tau_{2n-2}
be the prefixes of
\sigma
and
\tau
belonging to
P_{k+1},\dots,P_{2n-2}
respectively, then
c_{k}=f^{k+1}(\sigma_{k+1},\tau_{k+1})=f^{k+2}(\sigma_{k+2},\tau_{k+2})=\dots=f^{2n-2}(\sigma_{2n-2},\tau_{2n-2})=f(\sigma,\tau)
. This completes the proof of the claim.

Thus,
P_{0}
is a singleton (the root of
T^{\prime}
), and the value taken by
g^{0}
on its element is a
(2n-1)
-tuple
(c_{0},\dots,c_{2n-2})\in 2^{2n-1}
, such that, for
\sigma,\tau
two leaves of
S^{\prime}
with lowest common ancestor at a level in the interval
[M_{0}+\dots+M_{i-1},M_{0}+\dots+M_{i})
,
f(\sigma,\tau)=c_{i}
.

S^{\prime}
is a binary tree of depth
x\times(2n-1)
, and by grouping
x
successive
2
-branching into one,
S^{\prime}
can be seen as a
2^{x}
-branching subtree of
T
of depth
2n-1
. As
2^{x}\geq x
, we can trim
S^{\prime}
and consider it to be
x
-branching. By the finite pigeonhole principle, there exists some
c<2
such that
c_{i}=c
for
n
values of
i<2n-1
, hence, by only keeping the nodes of
S^{\prime}
at those levels (and the final level so that
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)\subseteq\mathrm{Leaves}(T)
), there exists some subtree
S\subseteq S^{\prime}
, isomorphic to
(x^{\leq n},\preceq)
, such that
f
is constant on the leaves of
S
.
∎

3.7
Grouping principle

Patey and Yokoyama
[
36
]
defined a notion of “grouping” as follows: given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to k
, and two notions of largeness
\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1}
, a collection of sets
F_{0}<\dots<F_{s-1}
is an
(\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1})
-grouping for
f
if (1) each
F_{i}
is
\mathcal{L}_{0}
-large, (2)
\{\max F_{i}:i<s\}
is
\mathcal{L}_{1}
-large, and (3) for every
i<j<s
and
x_{0},x_{1}\in F_{i}
and
y_{0},y_{1}\in F_{j}
,
f(x_{0},y_{0})=f(x_{1},y_{1})
. In other words, the behavior of
f
over different
F
’s depends only on the indices.
This notion was very useful to give an inductive construction of
\mathsf{EM}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness. Explicit bounds were later computed by Kołodziejczyk and Yokoyama
[
26
]
.

The notion of grouping does not directly translate to largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
because of the induced tree structure at every level, but the following lemma can be considered as its counterpart, and plays a similar role in the construction of an
\mathsf{EM}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
set.

Lemma 3.27
.

Let
X\subseteq_{fin}\mathbb{N}
be
\bbomega^{n+2}\cdot(d,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize n\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},d)
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and exp-sparse for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{d^{n}}\leq\min X
and let
f:[X]^{2}\to 2
be a coloring.

Then, there exists an
\bbomega^{n}\cdot(d,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize n\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},d)
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subset
Y
of
X
, such that, for
(Y_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in d^{n}}
the decomposition of
Y
into
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
blocs, there exists a coloring
g:[d^{n}]^{2}\to 2
such that
f(x,y)=g(\sigma,\tau)
for every
x\in Y_{\sigma}
and
y\in Y_{\tau}
for some
\sigma,\tau\in d^{n}
with
\sigma\neq\tau
.

Proof.

Let
(X_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in d^{n}}
be a decomposition of
X
into
\bbomega^{n+2}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
blocs. Consider the sequence
(f_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in d^{n}}
of colorings and the sequence
(Y_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in d^{n}}
of sets defined inductively as follows:

Let
\sigma\in d^{n}
and assume that
Y_{\tau}
have been defined for every
\tau>_{lex}\sigma
, and consider the two colorings
f_{\sigma}^{0}:X_{\sigma}\to 2^{\sum_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}|X_{\tau}|}
and
f_{\sigma}^{1}:X_{\sigma}\to 2^{|\{\tau\in d^{n}:\tau>_{lex}\sigma\}|}
defined respectively by

f_{\sigma}^{0}(x)=(f(y,x))_{y\in\bigcup_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}X_{\tau}}\mbox{ and }f_{\sigma}^{1}(x)=(f(x,y))_{y\in\{\min Y_{\tau}:\tau>_{lex}\sigma\}}

for every
x\in X_{\sigma}
. Finally, let

f_{\sigma}:X_{\sigma}\to 2^{\sum_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}|X_{\tau}|+|\{\tau\in d^{n}:\tau>_{lex}\sigma\}|}

be defined by
f_{\sigma}(x)=(f^{0}_{\sigma}(x),f^{1}_{\sigma}(x))
.
As
\min X\geq 2^{d^{n}}
and as the
X_{\tau}
are disjoint, we get that
\sum_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}|X_{\tau}|+|\{\tau:\tau\in d^{n}\wedge\tau>_{lex}\sigma\}<|[0,\min X)\cup\bigcup_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}X_{\tau}|
, hence

2^{\sum_{\tau<_{lex}\sigma}|X_{\tau}|+|\{\tau\in d^{n}:\tau>_{lex}\sigma\}|}\leq\min X_{\sigma}

by exp-sparsity of
X
.
Then, by
Corollary

3.19
there exists some
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and
f_{\sigma}
-homogeneous subset
Y_{\sigma}\subseteq X_{\sigma}
.
By definition of
(Y_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in d^{n}}
, for
\sigma<_{lex}\tau
and
x,x^{\prime}\in Y_{\sigma}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y_{\tau}
,
f(x,y)=f(x,\min Y_{\tau})=f(x^{\prime},\min Y_{\tau})=f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
.
∎

3.8
Erdős-Moser theorem

Thanks to the partition theorem on trees (
Proposition

3.26
) and the grouping-like
Lemma

3.27
,
we can now prove a central theorem in this article, namely, a polynomial bound for
\mathsf{EM}
-
\bbomega^{k}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
. In
Section

3.9
, it will be combined with the existing known polynomial bound for
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{k}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
and obtain a bound for
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
-
\bbomega^{k}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Theorem 3.28
.

There is a primitive recursive function
g_{\ref{thm:em-largeness-star-theta-bound}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that if
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is
\bbomega^{6k}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and
g_{\ref{thm:em-largeness-star-theta-bound}}
-sparse, then it is
\mathsf{EM}
-
\bbomega^{k}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
g_{\ref{thm:em-largeness-star-theta-bound}}
be the map
n\mapsto B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(n,n)
.
We proceed by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
is immediate.

Assume the property to be true for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
X
be an
\bbomega^{6(k+1)}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
set and
f:[X]^{2}\to 2
be a coloring. There exist two
\bbomega^{6k+5}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subsets
X_{0}<X_{1}
of
X
. Let
x_{0}=\max X_{0}
. By
Corollary

3.19
, there exists some
\bbomega^{6k+3}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subset
Y_{1}\subseteq X_{1}
such that
f(x_{0},y)=f(x_{0},y^{\prime})
for every
y,y^{\prime}\in Y_{1}
.

Note that
x_{0}>6k
since
x_{0}=\max X_{0}
which is
\bbomega^{6k+5}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
. By
g_{\ref{thm:em-largeness-star-theta-bound}}
-sparsity of
X
,
B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k)<g_{\ref{thm:em-largeness-star-theta-bound}}(x_{0})<\min Y_{1}
. Thus
Y_{1}
is

\bbomega^{6k+2}\cdot(B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize 6k\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k))\text{-large}{}^{*}(\theta)

and by
Lemma

3.27
, there exists an
\bbomega^{6k}\cdot(B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize 6k\mbox{ times}}}{{\dots}},B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k))
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subset
Z_{1}
of
Y_{1}
such that, for
(Y_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k)^{6k}}
its decomposition into
\bbomega^{6k}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
blocks, the value of
f(x,y)
for
x\in Y_{\sigma}
and
y\in Y_{\tau}
only depends on the indexes
\sigma,\tau
when
\sigma\neq\tau
.

By
Lemma

3.1
, we can view
Z_{1}
as a
B_{\ref{prop:partition-theorem-coloring-products}}(x_{0},k)
-branching tree
T
of depth
6k
with leaves labelled with the
Y_{\sigma}
. The coloring
f
induces a coloring
\hat{f}
on the pairs of leaves of that tree, and, by
Proposition

3.26
, as
6k\geq 2k
there exists a subtree
S\subseteq T
such that
(S,\preceq)\cong(x_{0}^{\leq k},\preceq)
,
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)\subseteq\mathrm{Leaves}(T)
, and such that
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
is homogeneous for
\hat{f}
.

We can then apply the inductive hypothesis on each
Y_{\sigma}
, for
\sigma\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
and obtains
\bbomega^{k}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
subsets
Z_{\sigma}\subseteq Y_{\sigma}
that are
f
-transitive. Let
H=\{x_{0}\}\cup\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)}Z_{\sigma}
, by construction
H
is
\bbomega^{k+1}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
as every
Z_{\sigma}
is
\bbomega^{k}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and as
S
is a subtree of
T
.

We claim that the set
Z
is
f
-transitive. Indeed, pick by contradiction some
x<y<z\in Z
such that
f(x,y)=f(y,z)
and
f(x,y)\neq f(x,z)
. We cannot have
x=x_{0}
, as
f(x_{0},y)=f(x_{0},z)
for every
y,z\in Y_{1}
. We cannot have
x,y,x\in Z_{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
by our application of the inductive hypothesis. We cannot have
x,y\in Z_{\sigma_{0}}
and
z\in Z_{\sigma_{1}}
for some
\sigma_{0}<_{lex}\sigma_{1}\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
by our application of
Lemma

3.27
, and similarly we cannot have
x\in Z_{\sigma_{0}}
and
y,z\in Z_{\sigma_{1}}
for some
\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1}\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
. Finally, we cannot have
x\in Z_{\sigma_{0}}
,
y\in Z_{\sigma_{1}}
and
z\in Z_{\sigma_{2}}
for some
\sigma_{0}<_{lex}\sigma_{1}<_{lex}\sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
, as by our application
Lemma

3.27
we have
f(x,y)=\hat{f}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
,
f(y,z)=\hat{f}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
and
f(x,z)=\hat{f}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{2})
and by
Proposition

3.26
,
\hat{f}
is homogeneous on
\mathrm{Leaves}(S)
. Hence, every case is impossible and
Z
is
f
-transitive. This completes our claim and the proof of
Theorem

3.28
.
∎

3.9
Ramsey’s theorem for pairs

Le Houérou, Levy Patey and Yokoyama
[
28
, Proposition 5.4]
proved that every
\bbomega^{4n+4}
-large
(\theta)
set is
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
, using the theorem of Ketonen and Solovay
[
20
]
. By replacing its use with
Theorem

2.7
, this yields the following bound for
\mathsf{ADS}
:

Proposition 3.29

(Le Houérou, Levy Patey and Yokoyama
[
28
]
revisited,
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
. If
F\subseteq_{\mathtt{fin}}\mathbb{N}
is
\bbomega^{4n}\cdot 2
-large
(\theta)
and
(x\mapsto 2x^{x^{2}})
-sparse, then it is
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
(\theta)
.

Thanks to the translation between largeness
(\theta)
and largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
, one obtains a polynomial bound for
\mathsf{ADS}

in terms of largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
:

Corollary 3.30
.

Let
n\geq 1
. If
F\subseteq_{\mathtt{fin}}\mathbb{N}
is
\bbomega^{2n^{2}+2n}\cdot 2
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and
(x\mapsto 2x^{x^{2}})
-sparse, then it is
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Since
F
is
\bbomega^{4\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\cdot 2
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
, it is also
\bbomega^{4\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\cdot 2
-large
(\theta)
,
so by
Proposition

3.29
, it is
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
-large
(\theta)
. By
Proposition

3.6
,
F
is
\mathsf{ADS}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.
∎

We are now ready to give a polynomial bound to
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-largeness
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Theorem 3.31
.

There is a primitive recursive function
g_{\ref{thm:rt22-largeness-star-theta-bound}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every finite set
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, if
X
is
\bbomega^{12n^{2}+12n+6}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
and
g_{\ref{thm:rt22-largeness-star-theta-bound}}
-sparse, then
X
is
\mathsf{RT}_{2}^{2}
-
\bbomega^{n}
-large
{}^{*}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Let

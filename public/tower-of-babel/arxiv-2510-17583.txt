Abstract

4
Conclusion

As a flagship of the reactor antineutrino oscillation experiments,
JUNO is going to measure the behaviors of

\overline{\nu}_{e}\to\overline{\nu}_{e}
oscillations to an
unprecedented degree of accuracy. It will therefore provide us
with a great opportunity to direct and precisely determine the three
elements in the first row of the PMNS matrix
U
, especially when its
upcoming data are combined with the existing Daya Bay data.
That is why we are well motivated to discuss the precision tests
of leptonic first-row unitarity and first-row correlation in the
canonical seesaw framework. Two highlights are in order.

•

We find that a global analysis of all the currently
available neutrino oscillation data has given us an impressive
lower bound on
\left|U_{e1}\right|^{2}+\left|U_{e2}\right|^{2}+\left|U_{e3}\right|^{2}
as shown in Eq. (
1
). Let us
await that the direct JUNO and Daya Bay precision measurements will
test the first-row normalization condition of
U
to a much better
degree of accuracy.

•

We find that the first-row correlation in Eq. (
3
)
is in good agreement with the present neutrino oscillation data,
as shown in Eq. (
3
) and Fig.
1
. The
RGE-induced quantum corrections to
\theta_{12}
and
\theta_{13}

are essentially insignificant in the SM framework. We are waiting
for a direct and more accurate test of Eq. (
3
), together with
its non-unitary generalization in Eq. (
2
), with the
help of the JUNO and Daya Bay precision measurements.

Last but not least, we emphasize that the TM1 lepton flavor mixing pattern
deserves a complete experimental test in the coming years, simply because
it belongs to the shortest list of viable forms of the PMNS matrix
U
that
can naturally originate from some simple flavor symmetry models.

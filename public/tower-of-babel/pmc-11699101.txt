Propolis does not significantly modulate immune function in an older population: A preliminary study
Authors: Lingling Pu, Yonghui Wang, Wei Pang, Jingyu Wei, Jianquan Wu, Changjiang Guo, Weina Gao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe human trial aimed to study the influence of propolis on immune function in the elderly.MethodsNon-institutionalized community-dwelling individuals aged 60–80 years were recruited from Tianjin, China. Participants were assigned randomly to either the Chinese propolis group (600 mg/day, n = 32) or the control group (n = 30). Serum antibodies and biochemical indices were measured using commercial kits; cytokines were detected using ELISA assay, and the functions of lymphocytes and neutrophils were assayed using the MTT and Staphylococcal methods, respectively. A dietary survey was conducted based on 24-h recall.ResultsThe study lasted for 12 weeks. Immunoglobulin G (IgG) levels notably increased at week 6 although these levels returned to baseline by week 12 in the Chinese propolis group. No significant difference was detected in serum antibodies, cytokines, or lymphocyte and neutrophil functions between the two groups at any time point.ConclusionsChinese propolis could stimulate IgG secretion within a certain period, but it was restored to baseline levels over time. Propolis does not significantly affect other immune markers in the elderly.

Introduction

Immune system function decreases with age [1]. With aging, the immune system exhibits less effective innate and adaptive immune responses [[2], [3], [4]], leading to increased susceptibility to immunity related diseases [5]. Natural products such as conjugated linoleic acids and Chlorella vulgaris, have various health-promoting effects, including reducing inflammation and oxidative stress [6,7]. Melatonin and propolis supplementation reportedly alleviated infectious status and improved oxidative stress and inflammation markers in patients with primary sepsis and pneumosepsis [8,9].

Propolis is a product collected by honeybees from the buds of leaves, branches, flowers, and pollen, and so on [10]. Propolis possesses several beneficial properties, including immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects [11,12] that are mainly attributed to its high contents of flavonoids and phenolic acids and their derivatives [12]. Evidences showed that propolis could inhibit the inflammation in joints of rheumatoid arthritis patients [13]. Although propolis administration to aged mice for 4 weeks enhances phagocytosis of peritoneal macrophages and increases serum immunoglobulin G levels [14], there are few studies on propolis and immunity in the elderly. It is still unclear whether propolis supplementation improves immune function in older adults, and the reports of the effects of propolis on innate and adaptive immunity are inconsistent [12]. The trial was to explore how propolis affected immune functions in the elderly population.

Materials and methods

Sample size calculation and flowchart of the human trial

Sample size was calculated by a formula recorded in the textbook of ⟪Health Statistics (2019). The formula is: N=[(zα/2+zβ)sδ]2(Q1−1+Q2−1). Let the standard deviations of the two groups be equal, α = 0.05, β = 0.10, s = δ, the sample size is calculated by the formula. It is known that s = δ = 2, α = 0.05, z0.05/2 = 1.96, β = 0.10, z0.1 = 1.28, Q1 = Q2 = 0.5, and N = 41.99. Each group needs 21 people at least.

The elderly were recruited from two communities in Tianjin, China. Inclusion and exclusion criteria were used for the participant enrollment. Subjects were divided randomly into control and Chinese propolis groups. The Chinese propolis group received Chinese propolis capsules daily for 12 weeks. Biomedical parameters were measured at baseline. Immune indicators were detected at baseline and at 6 and 12 weeks (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Flowchart of the human trial.Fig. 1

Flowchart of the human trial.

Dietary survey

A 24-h recall method was employed to conduct the dietary survey. The dietary survey was conducted before the third venous blood sample collection. Energy and nutrient intake were calculated based on reported food intake and carefully aligned with Chinese Food Composition tables.

Measurement of blood biochemical and immune parameters

Fasting blood samples were collected from the antecubital vein at baseline and at weeks 6 and 12. Serum total protein, albumin, glucose, blood lipids and antibodies (IgA, IgG, and IgM) were assayed using commercial kits (BioSino Biotechnology and Science, Inc., Beijing, China).

Serum interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) were measured by using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Bioswamp Life Science Lab, Wuhan, China). The standard protocol of the manufacturer was followed.

Concanavalin A-induced peripheral lymphocyte transformation test

Briefly, 2 mL fresh anticoagulant blood was mixed with 2 mL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Diluted fresh blood was carefully added to 4 mL lymphocyte separation solution. The solution was centrifuged at 300–400 g for 20 min. The white cell ring between the upper and lower solutions was carefully removed and washed twice with PBS by centrifugation at 1500g for 10 min. The harvested cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS). An MTT assay was used to evaluate the concanavalin A (ConA)-induced peripheral blood lymphocyte transformation ratio [15]. Briefly, cells from the same subject were divided into two duplicate. One duplicate was cultured by complete culture medium, the other by complete culture medium containing 10 μg/mL ConA (St Louis, Missouri). The cells were cultured for 20h. Subsequently, 20 μl of MTT was added to all wells. Four hours later, the plate was centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 10min, and the supernatant was removed. Afterward, 200 μl of microliter of DMSO was added to each well and mixed well. The absorbance value at 570 nm was determined. The ratio of the test well to the control well was calculated.

Neutrophil phagocytosis ability

Briefly, 60 μL whole blood was mixed with 20 μL saline containing 25 U/mL heparin. The mixture was held in a water bath at 37 °C for 1 min. The phagocytic ability of neutrophils was evaluated using the Staphylococcal method [16,17] with slight modifications. Fifty neutrophils were counted under a microscope, and the percentage of cells engulfing Staphylococcal cells (phagocytosis rate) was calculated.

Statistical analysis

SPSS software (version 10.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used to perform Statistical analyses. Data were showed as mean ± SD and checked for normality by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Difference between two groups was analyzed by Student's t-test. Data for fiber, cholesterol, ascorbic acid, vitamin A, retinol, and cytokines were transformed into square roots because they were not normally distributed. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

General characteristics of the participants

A total of 62 older adults were recruited from two communities in Tianjin, China. Inclusion criteria: (1) community residents living in Tianjin for over 5 years; (2) healthy people aged 60–80 years; and (3) individuals with no history of allergy, immune system diseases, tumors, or serious systemic diseases, and no addiction to alcohol and tobacco.

Exclusion criteria: (1) not aged 60–80 years; (2) any allergies; (3) use of functional foods or health products; (4) heavy drinking (alcohol intake >60 g/d for men and >40 g/d for women) or smoking (smoking index ≥400); (5) Hepatic diseases, serious tumor, and any other acute or chronic systemic diseases or disorders; (6) unable or unwilling to undergo questionnaire survey or sample collection; (7) current participation in other experiments or unsuitability for the present study as judged by the researchers. Serious disease conditions were diagnosed. The aims and procedures of the study were told to potential participants. Participant who was enrolled signed an informed consent.

Sixty-two healthy older adults were enrolled with 58 of these participants completing the intervention. In the intervention group, two subjects withdrew from the study owing to allergies to Chinese propolis before the second blood collection, and another two subjects withdrew for personal reasons immediately before the end of the experiment. One participant in the control group did not participate in the second blood collection for personal reasons.

Chinese propolis (600 mg/day) was purchased from Feng Language Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China) and administered daily for 12 weeks. A dose of 600 mg may have an impact on the human immune system [[18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24], [25]]. The older adults participating in this study were randomly divided into two distinct groups: the control group and the Chinese propolis group. The Chinese propolis group received 600 mg Chinese propolis capsules daily for 12 weeks. At baseline, serum levels of proteins, fasting glucose, and lipids of the participants were measured. Serum antibodies and cytokines were detected, and lymphocyte and neutrophil functions were evaluated at baseline and at 6 and 12 weeks.

Serum levels of immunoglobulins

Immunoglobulins are critical for adaptive immunity. To evaluate adaptive immunity in the elderly, 3 types of immunoglobulins were detected. Compared to baseline, the serum levels of IgG notably increased at week 6 in the Chinese propolis group (Fig. 2) (Baseline: 16.78 ± 4.28; week 6: 20.01 ± 4.32). However, IgG levels significantly decreased from week 6 to week 12 in the intervention group (Week 6: 20.01 ± 4.32; week 12: 16.53 ± 2.63); week 12 IgG levels were not different from those at baseline. Discussion

Serum IgG levels increased in older adults following the ingestion of Chinese propolis at week 6 but returned to their initial baseline by week 12. These results suggested that Chinese propolis has a transient stimulatory effect on IgG secretion over time. Other innate and adaptive immune indicators showed no changes over the intervention period.

The administration of Brazilian green propolis for 4 weeks increases serum IgG levels in aged mice [14]. Similar results were observed in laying hens and mice after treatment with propolis or its ethanolic extract [[26], [27], [28]]. The administration of flavonoids extracted from propolis affects IgG levels in Holstein calves over time [29]. Serum IgG levels are low in the first 3 weeks, reach a moderate level in week 4, and return to a low level in weak 8 [29]. Therefore, the effect of propolis on IgG secretion may fluctuate, stimulating IgG production for a certain period of time that then returns to normal levels with time. It may be that the body potentially adapts to the active components of propolis, leading to a reduction in the immune-stimulating effect. However, the duration of our study was 12 weeks, which was a relatively short time to observe the long-term effects of propolis on immune function, and a population study over a longer duration is required.

Propolis and its active ingredients (including phenolic compounds and flavonoids) can be used as adjuvants. Birds and mammals inoculated with propolis and its constituent adjuvant vaccines have significantly higher antibody titers than those inoculated with non-adjuvanted vaccines [[30], [31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. However, conflicting results have been reported. The use of Suid herpesvirus type 1 vaccine with propolis extract did not induce higher levels of antibodies in mice [17,18]. It appeared that propolis and its active ingredients as an adjuvant did not incite a heightened production of antibodies. Combined with the changes in antibody levels observed in this study, evidence suggests that propolis does not invariably enhance immune capacities in older people.

The main chemical constituents of propolis are flavonoids, flavones, tannins, phenols, flavanones, and resins [36]. Propolis-flavonoid liposomes (PFL) can significantly enhance antibody titers and serum concentrations of IgG in chickens [34], and a propolis flavone microemulsion (PFM) has been shown to increase serum IgG levels in mice [37,38]. These results indicate that propolis constituents can stimulate antibody production. Previously, we examined components of propolis from different sources. The total flavonoid content in the raw material of Feng-Language propolis capsules was 139.12 mg/g gallic acid equivalents [39]. Therefore, we speculated that flavonoids may be one of the active components in propolis that stimulates IgG secretion over time in older people.

Lymphocyte reactivity is important for adaptive immune function; in contrast, neutrophil phagocytic ability and serum cytokines represent innate immune responses. In our previous study, we did not find that Brazilian green propolis affected T cell proliferation or IL-1β, IFN-γ, or IL-4 levels in old mice [14]. Propolis does not stimulate cytokine production in old mice [40]. Propolis and its active components play different roles in various models. Propolis does not affect IL-4 production in mice over a 3-day treatment [41], but it lowers serum IL-1β levels in older people living at an altitude after a 24-month treatment [19]. Propolis modulated cytokine secretion in chronic diseases via its antioxidant and poly-phenolic compounds [42]. Meanwhile, propolis and its active ingredients induce IFN-γ and IL-4 release and increase T lymphocyte blastogenesis in mice after a 14-day treatment [[43], [44], [45]]. In other studies, PFL and PFM promote lymphocyte proliferation, and PFL enhances IFN-γ secretion in chickens [34,37,38]. Consequently, we believe that study subjects and treatment time are decisive factors in the different effects of propolis. Otherwise, propolis appears to exert selective effects on the immune system, and these effects may be dependent on the individual's baseline immune status, age, and other factors. The variability in response could also be due to differences in the composition of propolis, which is known to vary based on geographic origin and environmental conditions.

At baseline, serum parameters between the control and intervention groups had no differences, and white blood cell counts and proportions were all within normal range. However, calcium intake in the Chinese propolis group exhibited higher levels compared to the control group. The original data from the dietary survey indicated that the intake of calcium-rich foods such as milk, dairy products, legumes, and nuts in the Chinese propolis group were elevated in comparison to the control group, which could be the reason for the higher calcium intake observed in the intervention group. A previous study showed that dietary calcium from different sources did not have a significant effect on immune indices (IgA, IgG, and IgM) in weaning piglets [33,45]. However, a study showed that reduced dietary calcium led to T cells reduction in blood and cecal tonsils in laying hens, indicated that adaptive immunity was affected [46]. Therefore, we speculate that calcium intake in the Chinese propolis group may influence immune indices in participants.

Limitations

The study is beset with several limitations. Firstly, the lack of a placebo for the control group may have exerted a psychological influence on its participants. Secondly, only three types of cytokines and two types of immune cell functions were tested. Third is the small number of participants in the present study. A relatively small sample size would reduce the statistical efficacy or certainty to some extent. The sample size should be expanded in future studies to obtain more generalizable results.

Conclusion

Collectively, our results suggest that Chinese propolis stimulates IgG secretion over time. However, this impact weakens over time. Other innate and adaptive immune indicators showed no changes over the intervention period. Due to the relatively short duration and small sample size of the present study, human trials with a longer duration and a larger sample size are required.

Linear Cutaneous Tumors

Published: 2026

Case Presentation

We present four histopathologically confirmed benign and malignant neoplasms with a linear morphology that, at first glance, may appear similar, but closer inspection of the dermoscopic structures reveals their distinct diagnostic features: pigmented squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) in situ (Figure 1A), lichen planus–like keratosis/seborrheic keratosis (Figure 1B), melanoma (Figure 1C), and basal cell carcinoma (BCC) (Figure 1D).

Teaching Point

Cutaneous tumors typically manifest clinically as round-to-oval or irregularly shaped lesions; however, occasionally they appear in a linear configuration. BCC is the bestrecognized linear malignancy and is defined by a length-to-width ratio ≥3:1 [1]. The mechanisms underlying the linear morphology of cutaneous tumors remain unclear. One hypothesis is that these tumors extend along rhytids or Langer lines by following paths of least resistance, consistent with reports that more than 80% of linear BCCs align with Langer lines [2].

With over 3 million BCCs diagnosed each year in the U.S., linear BCC is still encountered despite its low proportion. It is possible that linear SCCs, melanomas and other lesions can also have a linear morphology, but due to their lower prevalence they are not encountered often in routine clinical practice or may be overlooked by clinicians and not reported. The aforementioned features may cause anchoring bias in clinicians resulting in a snap judgment diagnosis of BCC for any lesion manifesting a linear pattern. It is important to underscore that irrespective of the linear shape to a lesion, the clinical and dermoscopic clues to the correct diagnosis are usually present.

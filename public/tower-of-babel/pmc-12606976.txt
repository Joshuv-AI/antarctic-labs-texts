Educational program based on health belief model on knee osteoarthritis pain management in Iranian peri menopausal women: a randomized parallel controlled study
Authors: Masoomeh Dashtian, Sedigheh Sadat Tavafian, Kambiz Karimzade Shirazi, Parviz Yazdanpanah, Reza Maghbouli, Sarallah Shojaei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn light of the alarming rise in knee osteoarthritis (KOA) among the population in Iran, this study rigorously evaluated the efficacy of a model-based educational intervention designed to significantly alleviate knee pain and other debilitating symptoms in peri-menopausal women affected by this condition in Yasouj, Iran.MethodsThis randomized, parallel-controlled study was conducted on 100 peri-menopausal women with KOA who were randomly divided into two groups: intervention and control (50 women in each group). The model-based knee protective interventional program was applied just to the intervention group. However, the control group received just the medication that the physician prescribed. Data by demographic questionnaire, Rheumatoid arthritis self-efficacy (RASE), and Western Ontario and Mc. Master Universities Arthritis Index (WOMAC) was collected at baseline and 6-month follow-up and analyzed by SPSS 20. The primary outcome in this study was pain severity, which was measured by WOMAC.ResultsA total of 100 eligible women, including 50 women with a mean age of 44.8 ± 4.5 years in the intervention group and 50 women with a mean age of 47.2 ± 4.9 years in the control group, took part in this study. Comparing WOMAC subscales of intervention group versus control group at 6- month follow up, showed intervention group has obtained significant improvement in clinical symptom (13.10 ± 11.42 vs. 5.5 ± 10.6 ; P < 0.001), joint stiffness (8.99 ± 8.85 vs. 1.69 ± 8.96 ; P < 0.001), muscle strengths (3.00 ± 8.2 vs. 1.00 ± 0.01; P < 0.001 ), daily activities (12.50 ± 8.31 vs. 5.06 ± 4.04 ; P < 0.001) and pain severity (16.50 ± 7.37 vs. 2.56 ± 4.08; P < 0.001).ConclusionThis study could be a scientific basis for future studies in research centers that work to promote self-management behaviors among KOA patients and improve the health systems of their countries.Trial registrationThe study was registered in IRCT20220105053636N1 on 05/02/2022.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09248-w.Keywords: Knee osteoarthritis, Preventive behaviors, Health belief model, Pain, Peri menopausal women

Background

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic disease that is the main cause of pain and functional disability among the elderly and has major socioeconomic costs for individuals and society [1]. Individuals with KOA experience knee pain, swelling, stiffness, and reduced daily function [2]. Globally, it has been argued that knee Osteoarthritis (KOA) is a prevalent health problem in developed and developing countries [3, 4]. Many people, especially women in different countries, are suffering from (KOA), so more attention should be paid to females, especially people who are over 40 years old [5]. It is reported that in Iran, this problem appears at an earlier age in life, due to poor posture and an unhealthy lifestyle [6]. The Community Oriented Program for Control of Rheumatic Diseases (COPCORD) study in Iran revealed that about 20% of the Iranian population suffered from osteoarthritis in at least one joint, of which KOA was most prevalent. According to this study, KOA is more prevalent among female, older people, and rural people [7]. Regarding the risk factors of KOA, a national study in Iran has reported that the most important cause of KOA is due to poor posture of knee and not doing knee protective behavior like strengthen exercises [8]. Previous systematic review showed that in Iran, the mean age of menopause - because of sociodemographic characteristics and life style - was evaluated earlier than developed countries. This study claimed that lifestyle changes in Iranian women have interfered the younger menopause age and so earlier age of KOA [9]. Therefore, some affecting factors on menopause age should be paid more attention in Iranian women to reduce related health problems [9].

Regarding the treatment of KOA, even though no disease-modifying medication exists at the moment [10], there are several methods of treatment ranging from non-pharmacological treatment (weight management, exercise, biomechanical interventions and the use of walking canes) to pharmacological therapy (oral, topical and intraarticular) and even surgery as a last line when the first lines of management have failed [11]. However, various guidelines by Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI), American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS), and American College of Rheumatology (ACR) review the recommendations of treatment [12]. On the other hand, an existing systematic review focused on just physical activity to decrease the symptoms of KOA among people [13]. Moreover, other studies revealed that therapeutic interventions could reduce KOA symptoms for a short time [10]. However, as a condition that progresses with age, KOA requires modified and preventive behaviors for long-term pain and disability management [8]. In this regard, an existing study in Saudi Arabia highlighted that the high prevalence of KOA in this country supported the need for prevention programs through educational intervention that improved modifiable risk factors to decrease the rate and cost of the problem in the country [14]. Accordingly, this study recommended an educational program to address the needs of people to improve their risky behaviors [8]. Another evidence addressed KOA issues and managing its progression, lifestyle modification, which included efficient learning of OA-related information, self-management, and physical exercises, is one of the effective strategies universally recommended in KOA guidelines [15]. Education plays a vital role in improving people’s health through changing inappropriate behaviors and correcting lifestyle [16]. Applying behavior change techniques such as self-management in an educational program empowers patients to manage their disease symptoms and rehabilitate their knees. Previous study verified that modifying health behaviors is essential to controlling chronic disease, and so health care providers who practice proper behavioral change skills with the patients can empower them to participate in an educational program and keep healthy behaviors for the long term [17]. Although many studies verified that educational programs regarding strengthening exercise behaviors lead to muscle strength, bone density, improved function, and reduced knee pain due to osteoarthritis without the risks that pharmacological therapy or surgical intervention have for the patients [5, 14]. However, despite these benefits, the majority of women in Iran do not perform these knee protection behaviors. Studies discussed that one of the main reasons for not doing preventive behaviors is that the individuals did not perceive the benefits of the protective and rehabilitation behavior, and so they perceive many subjective barriers to doing them [18, 19]. According to the recommendations of these studies, model-based training programs are more effective in promoting protective rehabilitation behavior because these models are rooted in behavioral change patterns [18]. Therefore, to address the understanding of individuals and improve their perceptions to promote the behaviors, the Health Belief Model (HBM) has been considered as an effective framework for designing appropriate educational interventions for promoting protective behaviors among KOA people [20]. This model focuses on improving perceptions and beliefs of individuals to better understand the severity of KOA and its consequences, and the benefits of protective behaviors to prevent the disease progression. On the other hand, this model tries to promote the self-efficacy of individuals to do the behaviors [20]. To our knowledge, this study is the only randomized controlled trial assessing an HBM-based educational intervention for KOA in peri-menopausal Iranian women. This research hypothesized that improving the perception of women suffering from KOA to adopt protective behaviors could reduce KOA pain. Thus, this study aims to evaluate an educational intervention based on HBM in adopting the behaviors and reducing the knee symptoms in peri-menopausal women with KOA in Yasouj, Iran.

Methods Study design and population

The present study was a randomized parallel-controlled clinical trial research adopted from the declaration of Helsinki and received ethical approval from the Human This research aimed to design and evaluate an educational intervention based on the HBM in adopting protective behaviors for knee pain in peri menopausal women with KOA whose age ranged between 40 and 55 years and referred to Yasouj Orthopedic Clinic in Yasouj a city in South of Iran. In Iran and also in most countries, the majority of women aged 40–55 years are considered peri menopausal women. Thus, this age range is placed on eligibility.

In this study, the sample size was calculated based on the statistical parameter of pain severity as a primary outcome, which was reported in previous evidence [21]. In this valid Iranian study, the parameters for sample size calculation were as follows: β = 0.2; α = 0.05; ∂1 = 2; ∂2 = 2.36; u1 = 3.61 and u2 = 4.93. Accordingly, the sample size was 44 participants in each group. Considering the attrition rate of 10%, fifty participants (N = 50) in each group were calculated based on the following formula.

Participant selection

Through inclusion/exclusion criteria, the eligible women were randomly selected from referred clients to the orthopedic clinic in Yasouj city of Iran and then divided into two groups of intervention and control by simple random sampling. Inclusion criteria included items as being aged around menopause (40 to 55 years), suffering from a moderate degree of knee pain due to KOA for three months or more (chronic pain), suffering from knee stiffness while sitting and standing up, being able to read and write, and being fluent in the Farsi language. All this data was collected based on women’s self-reporting. However, the exclusion criteria were as history of knee joint surgery, knee joint replacement, knee joint trauma, knee joint infections, knee intra-articular injection in the past 6 months, taking oral corticosteroid within 6 weeks (present or past), any other disease that affects the function of the lower limb, skeletal muscle disabilities (which interfere in doing therapeutic exercise movements). To confirm the criteria, the referred women were firstly examined by a physical medicine specialist according to the inclusion/exclusion criteria, and if they were eligible for the study, they were divided into the groups randomly by an individual who was blind regarding the study aim and procedure. All the research aims and procedures of the study, as well as women’s rights regarding confidentiality, were explained to the potential participants, and if they were satisfied to be studied, they were provided with a consent form to be signed. All procedure was carried out in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Randomization

At the first stage, the referral orthopedic clinic affiliated with Yasouj University of Medical Sciences was informed about the research objectives and its procedures. Then the referred women to the clinic were examined by a physical medicine specialist and other orthopedics according to inclusion/exclusion criteria, and if they were eligible for the study, they were enrolled and numbered in a list by the clinic secretary, who was blind to the study. After being selected by a blinded independent person, they were randomly assigned to the intervention or control group. The physicians, the researchers, and the statistics specialists were blind to the study’s groups. The CONSORT flow chart shows the randomization. There was no stratification for any important baseline differences.

Intervention description

The educational intervention was delivered to the intervention group to manage KOA pain and symptoms. In this program, promoted protective behaviors were achieved by improving the beliefs of women regarding their self-efficacy and other HBM constructs. The designed educational program was held during 5 sessions due to constructs of HBM and 1 session for practice, all in the form of face-to-face training sessions, as well as providing reminder training on social networks, which could be provided for the women by health care professionals. Each session lasted for 90 to 120 min. The educational content was provided by rehabilitation specialists and was discussed in the context of HBM constructs. In the knowledge session, an attempt was made to promote the knowledge of the participants regarding knee pain and its causes, susceptible people, knee-protective behaviors, and knee-specific training about keeping the correct posture of the knee while sitting, standing, walking, sleeping, and performing therapeutic exercises. In the perceived susceptibility session, the possibility of peri menopausal women being involved with KOA and manifesting risks for them was discussed. Moreover, in the perceived severity session, the negative harmful effects caused by KOA for the women, their family, job, and social relationships were debated. The benefits of doing the preventive behaviors and existing barriers were discussed in the perceived benefits/barriers session. Improving the level of self-efficacy of the women about performing healthy knee behaviors was done by dividing complex behaviors into small behaviors. Furthermore, practical and believable steps were practiced in the self-efficacy session. Providing ergonomic postures and endurance exercise to strengthen muscles was trained with the participants to increase their muscle strength. Educational contents were presented to the participants of the intervention group in different ways, like group discussions, lectures, face-to-face training, question and answer sessions, educational videos, posters, pamphlets, podcasts, infographics, and animations. National social networks like Bale and Eita were used for interaction with participants. During this period, the control group did not receive any intervention. However, both groups received pharmacological treatments as conventional therapy, which mostly were Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs). This therapy is common in Iran because most KOA patients refer to a medical physician. After one month of training for the intervention group, the intervention group received a training reminder once a week for six months. Immediately after the end of this period, the tools were again given to the intervention and control groups to be completed. Since the maintenance period of behavior change is at least 6 months, the data of the 6-month follow-up were collected in order to check the effect of the educational program on the studied variables due to protective behavior (Fig. 1).

Outcomes

The primary outcome was pain severity, which was explored using WOMAC and the secondary outcomes were Rheumatoid arthritis self-efficacy, knee clinical symptoms, joint stiffness, daily and activity function. It is suggested to change these variables after the intervention.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analyzed using SPSS 24 software. Shapiro-Wilk was used to assess data normality due to the sample size. An Independent T-test was used to compare the means and mean difference of quantitative data between the intervention and control groups. Paired T-test was used to compare group differences before and after the intervention. Chi-square test was used to compare the frequency of qualitative data between the intervention and control groups (before and 6 months after the intervention). The U Mann–Whitney test was used for comparing the median difference of parametric data.

Discussion

The aim of this study was to evaluate the effectiveness of a designed HBM-based educational intervention program on adopting knee protective behaviors among peri-menopausal women suffering from knee osteoarthritis. The results of the present study showed that keeping the correct posture of the knee while sitting, standing, sleeping, and walking during daily activities, and also doing knee endurance exercise, which the professional health care providers practiced with the women, could reduce the knee pain score statistically among the studied women. However, these women, while they were visited by their physician, were more satisfied with their knee pain and reported reduced pain in their medical history. Furthermore, they reported better physical function and also reduced knee joint stiffness. In addition, the physician verified that these women had better clinical symptoms compared to their past physical exam. In line with these results, other studies strongly recommended doing protective behaviors, such as physical activity, which could strengthen muscle and bone density of knee joints and consequently reduce pain and symptoms of KOA [6, 14]. It has been argued that enhancing participation in knee-related educational programs and empowering patients to manage knee pain through performing protective practices related to KOA could prevent further damage to the knee joint and stop disease progression [25]. In the present study, the score of Rheumatoid Arthritis Self-Efficacy was improved significantly through statistical measures. In addition to this scale that showed the educational program based on HBM could improve the perception and beliefs of the women regarding benefits and barriers of knee protective behaviors, in clinical visits, these educated women stated that they perceived more self-efficacy to be able to do protective behaviors during most daily activities, while before intervention these women believed that they were unable to do these behaviors because they perceived more barriers which mostly were not real barriers but cultural and social barriers. Thus, this successful finding of the study is due to the educational intervention capacity, in which these subjective barriers were discussed with the women so that their wrong beliefs were improved and corrected through question-and-answer approaches. To support this justification, previous evidence verified that model-based training programs can be more effective in improving people’s high-risk behaviors and instilling healthy lifestyles among people who have wrong beliefs [18, 25].

In Iran, sitting on the ground in the seiza position and also squatting on the Iranian toilet are so common that they could be a subjective barrier for individuals to change these behaviors. In this educational program, the women were educated to be aware of these risky behaviors for their knees, and so they practiced healthy positions of their knees with a healthcare professional. Furthermore, they recommended sitting on the chair and using a sitting toilet as far as possible.

Additionally, in the educational program, it was made to promote knowledge and beliefs of the studied women regarding their susceptibility to severe complications of knee osteoarthritis, leading to disability if they did not comply with protective behaviors. Not only did these educations lead to promoted protective behavior and finally statistically significant scores of WOMAC subscales in studied women, but also these benefits in turn led to decreased knee clinical symptoms and knee muscle stiffness in clinical examination by a physician. Moreover, these women during their visit with their physician stated that reduced knee pain and improved physical function during daily activities were better than in the past 6 months. This finding is supported by other evidence, which reported that KOA is more prevalent among patients with lower-level education, and so verified that increasing their knowledge could be effective in knee pain prevention. These studies also recommended that these patients should participate in educational and rehabilitation programs [5, 14]. In the present study, the educational program, due to adopting the knee posture of women with knee osteoarthritis, could lead to better knee function compared to individuals who did not participate in the program. This finding is in agreement with the findings of an existing study that reported strengthening the quadriceps muscles could reduce pain in patients with KOA [26].

All these improvements that were obtained from the current study are due to the promoted perceived self-efficacy of these women who play an effective role in doing protective behaviors in order to manage their knee pain. The role of promoting self-efficacy to improve protective behavior is discussed in other evidence [18]. The educational program of this study could promote the skill of the studied women to keep the right postures of their knees and accomplish knee endurance exercises to strengthen the quadriceps muscles and reduce the stiffness of their knee joints, and ultimately reduce their knee pain so that they could do their housework more easily. This finding is in line with existing evidence that indicates empowerment of KOA patients to do self-care behaviors is more effective for controlling their KOA disease [25]. In addition, another study that applied HBM for removing unhealthy behavior among the participants revealed that the educational programs could promote healthy behaviors through improving the HBM constructs [27].

Although in this study, these benefits due to the educational program were observed in the intervention group, it should be considered that at the beginning of the research, the studied women in the intervention group were younger and more highly educated compared to the other group, which might overestimate the impact of the educational program. On the other hand, the population of this study, women living in Yasouj, differed from other Iranian populations due to many knee-related risky behaviors, which were mostly related to their social and cultural norms that might heighten the results.

Limitations

Despite the strong points of this study, there were some limitations, such as not addressing all confounding factors that could affect the preventive behaviors of pre-menopausal women, self-report data collection, assessing just female sex, potential bias from self-selection, or the single-center setting and short follow-up period. To remove these limitations, the larger sample size of both female and male participants from multiple central sites should be considered. Furthermore, self-selection bias affects the generalizability of findings, and a short follow-up period (6 months) limits conclusions about long-term behavior change. Stratification for important variables was not done in this study, which could be a limitation. Furthermore, significant baseline differences in age and education level between groups could confound outcomes and have a potential impact on the results.

Implications

Despite these limitations, this study has its own strong points because it revealed the findings that are consistent with existing valid studies [18, 30– ]. On the other hand, this study added one further evidence to existing evidence regarding the issue that HBM-based educational programs could improve wrong beliefs of the traditional women who are less educated and more influenced by the culture and social norms of their society, rather than scientific recommendations. To our knowledge, this is the only RCT evaluating an HBM-based educational intervention for KOA in peri menopausal Iranian women. It should be mentioned that during the study, no complications and adverse events were reported by the studied women.

Conclusions

Based on the authority and limitations, the findings of this study could be a scientific base for future studies in research centers that work for promoting self-management behaviors among KOA patients and improving the health systems of their countries. However, practical guidance such as training requirements for clinicians, cost considerations, and scalability should be researched in future studies. Furthermore, the economic and social implications of the program could be an area for further research in the future.

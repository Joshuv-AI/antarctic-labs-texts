Protocol to generate endothelial cell-specific knockout mouse models using Cas9/Cdh5-Cre mice coupled with sgRNA
Authors: Dong-Mei Wang, Chinnaswamy Tiruppathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe vascular endothelium is a critical regulator of vascular homeostasis and tissue fluid balance, and mouse models are essential for studying these processes in vivo. Here, we present a protocol to generate adult endothelial cell (EC)-specific gene knockout (KO) mouse models. We describe steps for Cas9-active and Cdh5-Cre-positive (Cas9/Cdh5-Cre) mouse line generation, single guide RNA (sgRNA) design for vector construction, plasmid DNA generation, and liposome preparation. We then detail procedures for liposome/plasmid complex injection, lung harvest, homogenization, protein quantification, and verification with western blotting.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Wang et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol involves two mouse strains: Cdh5 Cre B6.FVBTg(Cdh5-cre)7Mlia/J2 and Cre-dependent Cas9 knockin B6J.129(B6N)-Gt(ROSA)26Sortm1(CAG-cas9∗,-EGFP)Fezh/J.3 Both strains are available from the Jackson Laboratory.

The pGS-gRNA plasmid construct service is available from GenScript, under agreement with the Feng Zhang Laboratory at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard.Note: The pGS-gRNA from GenScript is a 2.7 kb size DNA plasmid vector. It contains a U6 promoter, a gRNA scaffold, and an ampicillin selection marker that allows for the insertion of a custom 20-nucleotide target sequence. The design of this plasmid vector is specifically for expressing sgRNA, a crucial component of the CRISPR/Cas9 system that directs the Cas9 enzyme to a target DNA sequence for gene editing.4 444,5,6

Innovation

The vascular endothelium, a continuous monolayer lining blood vessel lumen, acts as a dynamic and critical regulator of vascular homeostasis and tissue fluid balance. Mouse models are especially paramount in biomedical research due to their high fidelity to human physiology and genetics, as well as their efficient, cost-effective, and reproducible modeling capabilities. The protocol described here introduces endothelial cell (EC)-specific Cdh5-Cre into Cas9 knockin mouse to obtain both Cas9-active and Cdh5-Cre-positive (Cas9/Cdh5-Cre) mouse line by using two-round breeding schemes, and further generates adult EC-specific gene knockout mice, e.g., the ETS (Erythroblast Transformation Specific) transcription factor family member E74-like factor 2 EC-KO (Elf2ΔEC) by injecting a single gRNA (sgRNA) into adult mice of this mouse line. This protocol utilizes only commercially available resources and common laboratory supplies ensuring a successful replication. With this mouse line, users can rapidly generate any EC-specific gene knockout adult mouse model of interest. To the best of our knowledge, no comparable protocols have been published to date.

Materials and equipment

Alternatives: This protocol uses a Beckman Avanti J-20 XP centrifuge with JLA-16.250 and JA-12 rotors for expanded bacterial culture centrifugation and plasmid DNA extraction and purification. In the absence of a large-compacity and high-speed centrifuge, bacterial culture can be harvested in 50 mL aliquots, and plasmid DNA extraction and purification can be performed at a lower speed of 4,000 g for a longer spin time of 1 h by using a conventional centrifuge, e.g., Heraeus Megafuge 16R (Thermo Fisher). Alternatives: The Rotavapor system is a critical instrument for liposome preparation. This protocol uses the Rotavapor R-300 rotavapor system for liposome preparation. An older version of Rotavapor also works well, e.g., Buchi R-124 rotary vap system. Alternatives: This protocol uses Isoflurane for anesthesia. The alternative is Ketamine/Xylazine solution respectively at doses of 100 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg. Ketamine is classified into the category of Schedule III controlled substance under federal law in the United States that requires DEA registration to purchase, store, and administer. Xylazine is currently not controlled at the federal level but is classified as a controlled substance in some states of the United States with Schedule categories ranging from I to III. Xylazine is a Schedule III controlled substance in Illinois. Alternatives: This protocol uses the TISSUMIZER to homogenize lung tissues. Alternatives are using liquid nitrogen to grind lung tissues into a fine powder with a mortar and pestle or using dry ice to maintain a low temperature during the cutting of lung tissues into small pieces with a blade, followed by mechanical dissociation and homogenization via passage through a 21-gauge needle ten times. Alternatives: This protocol uses the Lowry Protein Assay to measure total protein concentrations in UV/Vis Spectrophotometer DU 530 (Beckman). Alternatives are the Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) Protein Assay (Thermo Scientific) and FlexStation 3 Microplate Reader (Molecular Devices) with clear, flat-bottom 96-well polystyrene microplates (Thermo Scientific). Expected outcomes

CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) and its associated protein Cas9, known as CRISPR/Cas9, are part of a bacterial adaptive immune response system and have been harnessed as a precise genomic editing tool since 2012.6 Due to its simplicity in design and use, high efficiency, low cost and versatility in cutting and modifying DNA,4,5,6 CRISPR/Cas9 has become the world’s leading gene-editing technology. To facilitate in vivo applications of Cas9, Platt et al. (2014)3 established a Cre-dependent Rosa26 Cas9 knockin mouse.

Our study employed only Cas9/Cdh5-Cre mice for experiments and the injections of gene-specific sgRNA and non-targeting control sgRNA respectively generated experimental EC specific knockout mice and control mice. We routinely observed remarkable loss of protein expression of our target genes in EC knockout mice compared to control mice by using the protocol described here. We expect the binding of at least one of our two selected sgRNAs to the target Elf2 gene and significant reduction in Elf2 expression. As shown in Figure 2, both Elf2 sgRNAs resulted in a significant reduction in Elf2 expression in lung tissues in comparison with control sgRNA, with Elf2 sgRNA1 demonstrating slightly higher editing efficiency (90%) compared to sgRNA2 (84%). Quantification and statistical analysis

The mean grey value (average brightness of pixels) for each Western blotting band and corresponding background blank was measured by using NIH ImageJ software. The calculation and normalization of those mean grey values were carried out with a background correction in Microsoft Excel. The statistical analysis of one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test was performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.4.1 for Windows, GraphPad Software, Boston, Massachusetts USA, www.graphpad.com. The p value < 0.05 is considered significant.

Limitations

Implementation of this protocol requires availability of both Cas9 knockin and Cdh5-Cre mouse strains. Establishing a homozygous line for both Cas9 active and Cdh5-Cre alleles requires a prolonged, multi-generational breeding scheme. Although our protocol offers EC-specific modeling and relatively rapid generation of stable knockout mice that holds significant potential for long-term phenotypic studies, particularly in modeling vascular homeostasis and disease progression, we cannot rule out the possibilities of toxicity of conventional Cre expression, off-target effects from the chosen sgRNAs, and mosaicism with incomplete gene editing in all target cells due to absence of distinct phenotypes and lack of longitudinal studies. Besides, female and male mice were included in both experimental groups and there were no sex-specific differences in gene editing outcomes. Further investigations with higher N numbers for both sexes are warranted to solidify this observation.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Variability in knockout efficiency between individual mice, e.g., CRISPR mosaicism (Step 3; very faint PCR band observed in the Cas9 mutant positive samples in Figure 1B) and limitation of ordinary genotyping PCR detection (Figures 1A and 1B).

Potential solution

Ensure the truly homozygous mutant identification from a founder with positive and negative controls in genotyping PCR amplifications. Use the correct homozygous mutant for mouse breeding, e.g., mice 3 and 4 in Figure 1. As shown in Figure 1 with one very faint WT PCR band and one unintended PCR band in mouse 2 DNA sample and one non-specific band in both mouse 1 DNA sample and the positive control C57BL/6 DNA sample, the potential solution to resolve these issues is the high-resolution genotyping e.g., Sanger sequencing or Next Generation Sequencing (NGS).

Problem 2

Trouble in maintenance of Cas9/Cdh5-Cre mouse lines (Step 4).

Potential solution

Avoid cross-contamination while cutting tails from different mice and identify Cas9 active and Cdh5-Cre positive mice correctly with positive and negative controls. Select real Cas9/Cdh5-Cre mice, e.g., mice 3 and 4 in Figure 1 to start breeding at the age of 6–8 weeks and keep detailed records to ensure high-producing breeders are prioritized.

Problem 3

Trouble in sgRNA design or failed sgRNA outcomes (Step 5b).

Potential solution

Choose sgRNAs based on multiple criteria including the shared consensus sequence in different gRNA design programs, targeting 40%–60% GC content, early exons with 3 or more mismatches with off-target sites, avoiding TTT stretches to prevent premature RNA polymerase III termination, low self-complementarity, no less than two sgRNAs per gene for testing as editing efficiency varies by sequence.

Problem 4

When picking up a single colony, a mixed genetic background occurred (Step 6j).

Potential solution

Use streak dilution for plating and choose from a sparsely growing field rather than the densely growing ones. The colony picked should be distinct and well separated from other colonies.

Problem 5

In preparing retro-orbital injection, there is precipitation observed while mixing plasmid DNA with liposome (Step 19).

Potential solution

Dissolve plasmid DNA in sterile ultrapure water and perform re-purification by using the Qiagen plasmid maxi kit. First add 10 times the Buffer QBT of the volume of the original sample solvent and then follow the steps from Step 7j to 7t. For the details to re-purify plasmid DNA, please refer to https://www.qiagen.com/resources/download.aspx?id=34362537-bfde-48e3-99c0-6d8374c484bd&lang=EN&ver=3. To use the re-purification protocol, the DNA must be free of SDS and other anionic detergents. If plasmid DNA is contaminated with RNA, it must be treated with RNase A first.

Problem 6

While performing retro-orbital injections of liposome-plasmid complex, mice died from the operation procedure (Steps 20 and 21).

Potential solution

It might result from anesthesia with prolonged exposure to Isoflurane or retro-orbital injections. During anesthesia, anesthetic depth should be continuously monitored by respiration and toe pinches. Once appropriate anesthesia is confirmed, mouse needs to be removed from the induction chamber for injection. Retro-orbital injections require specialized training: insert the insulin syringe attached with the 29G1/2 needle at a 30°C–45°C angle at the medial/nasal corner of the eye with the bevel facing down and push the needle down until gently touching the orbital bone, then withdraw slightly before injecting.

Problem 7

Inefficient liposome/plasmid complex delivery with spills (Step 21).

Potential solution

It might result from improper needle placement, e.g., failing to reach/damage to the venous sinus, too much injection volume, e.g., > 200 μL, rapid administration. For the correct retro-orbital injection, please refer to potential solution, Problem 6.

Problem 8

The protein marker is absent from the membrane after gel transfer (Step 44).

Potential solution

The observed issue might be improper transfer sandwich assembly or incorrect cassette orientation within the transfer unit, leading to reversed polarity. Ensure the membrane is placed on the anode side and the gel on the cathode side and the transfer cassette is set up in a correct orientation.

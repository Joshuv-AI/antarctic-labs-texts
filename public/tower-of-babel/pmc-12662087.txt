Reply
Authors: Achille Gaspardone, Gregory A Sgueglia, Carlo Gaspardone
Published: 2025

We sincerely thank Mares et al1 for their thoughtful appraisal of our work, which reflects their deep expertise in patent foramen ovale (PFO) closure. They have rightly underscored what we consider the central clinical message of our study: despite its excellent safety profile and the clear advantages of a deviceless strategy, the key finding is that the presence of a residual shunt does not appear to diminish the clinical benefit of suture-mediated closure.

Our interpretation, although likely not the only one, is that even a partial reduction of the interatrial communication is sufficient to confer clinical benefit by preventing the passage of larger emboli. In this sense, suture-mediated closure resembles the condition observed in roughly one-quarter of the general population, where a small PFO with modest right-to-left shunting does not lead to ischemic events. The technique aims to maximize septal overlap by placing the stitch as centrally as possible, yet in very large PFOs (as predicted by the LASSO [Length of the PFO, Aneurysm, Shunt Severity, Opening of the PFO] score) small lateral fissures may persist. Even in such cases, the overlapping septa make the passage of clinically relevant emboli highly improbable. At most, only microbubbles, comparable in size to blood cells, may still cross, but these microemboli are unlikely to have clinical significance.

As our colleagues correctly noted, other factors beyond residual shunt may contribute to ischemic recurrences, including atrial dysfunction induced by metal prostheses, which can promote supraventricular arrhythmias, such as atrial fibrillation.2 We have previously shown that suture-mediated closure preserves atrial function, whereas device-based closure is associated with its deterioration, a mechanism that may, in turn, favor atrial arrhythmogenesis.3 Notably, in our study, we had no immediate or long-term complications related to the closure technique.

Current evidence supports suture-mediated PFO closure as a safe technique with excellent long-term outcomes. Careful patient selection remains essential, and the LASSO score may serve as a practical tool in this regard. Its broader adoption and wider experience will ultimately show whether suture-mediated closure will further brighten our days.

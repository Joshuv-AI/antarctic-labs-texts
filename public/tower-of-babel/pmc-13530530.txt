Poster 225. Latarjet Offers Lower Two-Year Re-operation Rate with Similar Rate of Nerve Injury Compared to Anterior Free Bone Block Procedures
Authors: Natalie K. Kucirek, Marcus V. Trotter, Brian T. Feeley, Benjamin Ma, Alan L. Zhang, Drew A. Lansdown
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The objective of this study is to determine rates of re-operation and associated risk factors after complex shoulder stabilization surgery, with a focus on comparing the Latarjet procedure to anterior free bone block procedures. Rates of nerve injury after these procedures will be determined. Additionally, national utilization trends in complex shoulder stabilization surgery will be identified.Methods:A retrospective study was conducted using the PearlDiver Mariner M170Ortho Dataset. Patients undergoing the Latarjet procedure (CPT-23462), capsulorrhaphy with anterior free bone block (CPT-23460), and open Bankart procedure (CPT-23465) between 2015 and 2022 were identified. Patients were tracked for two years to assess rates of subsequent closed reduction, manipulation under anesthesia, arthroscopic and open shoulder surgery including stabilization and debridement procedures, irrigation and debridement, shoulder arthroplasty, nerve injury, and electromyography or nerve conduction studies. Risk factors for subsequent procedures were then identified using univariate and multiple logistic regression. The incidence of each procedure between 2010 and 2022 was queried to determine national trends.Results:A total of 4933 patients undergoing complex shoulder stabilization procedures were identified. At two years, 7.1% of patients underwent a subsequent procedure, with 3.4% of patients undergoing revision open or arthroscopic stabilization. Male sex was associated with lower rates of re-operation (OR 0.62, 95% CI [0.49.78]), while increasing Elixhauser comorbidity index (OR 1.13, 95% CI [1.09, 1.17] and tobacco use (OR 1.47, 95% CI [1.15, 1.87]) were associated with higher rates of re-operation. There was a higher rate of re-operation after anterior free bone block procedures (61/455, 13.4%) compared to Latarjet procedures (217/2524, 8.6%, p =0.002). The rates of nerve injury after Latarjet (32/2524, 1.3%) and anterior free bone block procedures (9/455, 2.0%) were not significantly different (p=0.27). Between 2010 and 2022, there was an increase in Latarjet procedures (r2 = 0.5, p= 0.004) and a decrease in open Bankart procedures (r2 = 0.89, p<0.001), while there was no change in anterior free bone block procedures (r2= -0.05, p =0.53).Conclusions:Anterior free bone block procedures had a higher re-operation rate at two years with no difference in incidence of nerve injury compared to the Latarjet procedure. Over twelve years, the incidence of Latarjet procedure increased and the incidence of open Bankart procedures decreased, while there was no change in the incidence of anterior free bone block procedures.

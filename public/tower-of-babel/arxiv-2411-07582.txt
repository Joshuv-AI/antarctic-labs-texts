The Talented Monoid of Higher-Rank Graphs with Applications to Kumjian-Pask Algebras
Roozbeh Hazrat, Promit Mukherjee, David Pask, Sujit Kumar Sardar

Abstract
. Given a row-finite higher-rank k k -graph Λ \Lambda , we define a commutative monoid T Λ T_{\Lambda} which is a higher-rank analogue of the talented monoid of a directed graph introduced in (J. Algebra 384 (2013), 242-266) and (J. Algebra 547 (2020), 430-455). The talented monoid T Λ T_{\Lambda} is canonically a ℤ k \mathbb{Z}^{k} -monoid with respect to the action of state shift. This monoid coincides with the positive cone of the graded Grothendieck group K 0 gr ​ ( KP 𝗄 ⁡ ( Λ ) ) K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)) of the Kumjian-Pask algebra KP 𝗄 ⁡ ( Λ ) \operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda) with coefficients in a field 𝗄 \mathsf{k} . The aim of the paper is to investigate this ℤ k \mathbb{Z}^{k} -monoid as a capable invariant for classification of Kumjian-Pask algebras. We establish the ℤ k \mathbb{Z}^{k} -monoid isomorphism T Λ ≅ T 𝒢 Λ = Typ ⁡ ( 𝒢 Λ × c ℤ k ) T_{\Lambda}\cong T_{\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}}=\operatorname{Typ}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}\times_{c}\mathbb{Z}^{k}) , the type monoid of the inverse semigroup of the compact open bisections of the skew-product groupoid 𝒢 Λ × c ℤ k \mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}\times_{c}\mathbb{Z}^{k} where c : 𝒢 Λ ⟶ ℤ k c:\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{k} is the canonical cocycle. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ample Hausdorff groupoid,
\Gamma
a discrete group and

c\colon\mathcal{G}\to\Gamma
a cocycle. Consider the skew-product groupoid
\mathcal{G}\times_{c}\Gamma
. The homogeneous compact open bisections of this groupoid form a Boolean inverse semigroup
[
3
,
12
]
. We call the type monoid of this inverse semigroup, the
talented monoid
of
\mathcal{G}
:

T_{\mathcal{G}}:=\operatorname{Typ}(\mathcal{G}\times_{c}\Gamma)

which is a commutative
\Gamma
-monoid generated by symbols
\operatorname{typ}(U\times\alpha)
, where
U
is a compact open subset of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
\alpha\in\Gamma
, with an action of
\Gamma
on
T_{\mathcal{G}}
defined
on generators by

{}^{\beta}\operatorname{typ}(U\times\alpha):=\operatorname{typ}(U\times\beta\alpha)

with
\beta\in\Gamma
and extended linearly to all elements.

This paper pursue the following program for the case of higher-rank graphs:

Program 1.1
.

Describe the class of
\Gamma
-graded ample groupoids
\mathcal{G}
such that the talented monoid
T_{\mathcal{G}}
, as a
\Gamma
-monoid, is a complete invariant for Steinberg and groupoid

-algebras.

When

is a directed graph and

is the associated graph groupoid, then

is the talented monoid of the graph
E

[
16
,
17
]
which coincides with the positive cone of the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
of Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
with coefficient in a field
\mathsf{k}
.
[
17
]
. Then Program
1.1
takes the form of the Graded Classification Conjecture
[
13
,
15
]
. Till date a lot of evidence has been found in the support of this conjecture (see
[
17
,
12
]
), also it has been proved to be true for some classes of directed graphs; for details see
[
13
]
.

The notion of higher-rank graphs (or,
k
-graphs) was first introduced in
[
20
]
to obtain a graph theoretic framework of the study performed by Robertson and Steger in the seminal papers
[
33
,
34
]
. Modelled on the path category of a directed graph, higher-rank graphs, their

-algebras (see
[
20
,
29
,
32
]
etc.) and their algebraic counterparts namely Kumjian-Pask algebras (see
[

,

,
11
]
) cover a wider class of examples including graph

-algebras
[
21
]
and Leavitt path algebras
[

,
2
]
respectively.

Given a row-finite
k
-graph with no sources, Kumjian and the third named author in
[
20
]
associated an ample Hausdorff groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
which is known as the
path groupoid
of
\Lambda
. This gives a groupoid model for the higher-rank graph

-algebra as it was shown in
[

that

. On the other hand it follows from the work of Clark and Pangalela

that the Steinberg algebra of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
is isomorphic to the Kumjian-Pask algebra
\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)
where

is a unital commutative ring. As a consequence of this, fundamental algebraic properties like simplicity and basic ideal simplicity of the Kumjian-Pask algebra are reflected by certain properties (minimality and effectiveness) of the path groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
. Thus to translate Program
1.1
in the language of higher-rank graphs, we should focus on the talented monoid
T_{\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}}
and observe how the algebraic properties of the higher-rank graph algebras are governed by
T_{\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}}
; this is what we have tried to accomplish in the present paper.

As the talented monoid of a directed graph is the graded version of the graph monoid (see
[
4
,
5
]
), we first define
k
-graph monoids
as higher-rank graph analogue of graph monoids. However the transition from directed graphs to
k
-graphs is not straightforward at all since in a general
k
-graph where
k\geq 2
, a vertex can receive paths from several different directions while in a graph a vertex can emit edges in only one direction. Keeping this fact in mind, we suitably define a monoid
M_{\Lambda}
for a row-finite
k
-graph
\Lambda
. One of the important properties of graph monoids which transits also to the talented monoid (via the natural isomorphism of talented monoid with the graph monoid of the covering graph) is the

. We observe that unlike graph monoid,
k
-graph monoid
M_{\Lambda}
of a
k
-graph
\Lambda
with sources may not have the confluence property; see Remark
3.3
. Also we exhibit a
k
-graph monoid

(see Example

) which can not be realized as the graph monoid

of any row-finite directed graph

. Looking at these obstacles which arise for

-graphs with sources, we decide to concentrate only on row-finite

-graphs which do not have any source and prove that the monoids of such

-graphs indeed admit a suitable confluence property (see Lemma

We define the

for a row-finite

-graph

as the commutative monoid generated by
\Lambda^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
subject to the relations:

v(n)=\displaystyle{\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{m}}}s(\alpha)(n+m)
for each

with
v\Lambda^{m}\neq\emptyset

There is a natural action of

on

given by state shift:
{}^{n}v(m):=v(m+n)
for all

and

a

-monoid which is isomorphic to the

-graph monoid

(see Theorem

) where

is the canonical skew-product of
\Lambda
by
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Interestingly enough, the monoid

has two more realizations. Firstly, in Proposition

, we show that

is the direct limit of a certain directed system considered by Ara et al.
[
6
]
in the study of the graded

-groups of Kumjian-Pask algebras. As a consequence,

becomes isomorphic with the graded

-monoid of the Kumjian-Pask algebra of

over a field

and its group completion becomes the graded Grothendieck group

. Secondly, in Theorem

is isomorphic (as
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid) to the talented monoid

of the path groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
. It then readily follows that

is a conical refinement monoid. Moreover, in view of this result, the isomorphism
T_{E}\cong T_{\mathcal{G}_{E}}
obtained in
[
12
]
in the level of directed graphs, is perfectly generalized in the setting of
k
-graphs.

In the attempt of gathering evidence that
T_{\Lambda}
is a possible complete invariant for most of the Kumjian-Pask algebras, we initiate in this paper, the study of connecting the monoid theoretic properties of
T_{\Lambda}
as well as the properties based on the natural
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-action with the geometry of the underlying
k
-graph
\Lambda
. In this direction, here we mainly characterize the two most important properties of

namely
aperiodicity
and

.

Aperiodicity, in the setting of
k
-graphs, is the suitable analogue of Condition
(L)
of directed graphs which states that every cycle in the directed graph has an exit. In the literature there are various formulations of aperiodicity depending on the complexity of the
k
-graph concerned e.g., see
[
20
,
32
,
29
,
24
]
. However these are all equivalent when considered for row-finite
k
-graphs with no sources. Aperiodicity is the main hypothesis in the Cuntz-Krieger uniqueness theorem for

-algebras (see
[
20
, Theorem 4.6]
) and Kumjian-Pask algebras (see
[
7
, Theorem 4.7]
). We prove in Proposition

\mathbb{Z}^{k}
acts freely on the monoid

then
\Lambda
is aperiodic in the sense of Lewin and Sims (see Definition

). However unlike directed graphs, the converse does not hold for general

-graphs; see Examples
5.3
,
6.3
. By establishing that

satisfies the confluence property, we show that if

is

then it is also a

. In this case the converse is proved to be true (see Theorem

-graph without sources
\Lambda
with atomic talented monoid, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for

of

, which is the

-graph theoretic generalization of Condition

used in the study of Leavitt path algebras and graph

-algebras.

Cofinality on the other hand, is the property of a

-graph which is a necessary and sufficient requirement for the graded ideal simplicity of the associated Kumjian-Pask algebra. Indeed, in
[
7
, Lemma 5.12]
, it was proved that if

are the trivial ones. By establishing a lattice isomorphism in Proposition

and the lattice of

-order ideals of

, here we show that the converse implication also holds. Also, this helps us to show that the simplicity of

as a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid, is equivalent to the cofinality of
\Lambda
(see Proposition

). Therefore our results show that the properties of Kumjian-Pask algebras of being graded ideal simple, graded basic ideal simple and ideal simple remain invariant under
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-isomorphism of the talented monoids.

In
[
9
]
the authors identifies the socle of the Kumjian-Pask algebra

by introducing

in the

-graph

. They obtained several equivalent conditions for semisimplicity of

. In Theorem

-graph

can be described completely in terms of the

-orbits of the aperiodic atoms of

. It provides us with a talented monoid realization of the socle of

. Based on this we characterize semisimple Kumjian-Pask algebras through the lens of

and end up adding some more conditions to the list given in

. Our result shows that for a row-finite

-graph

which do not have sources,

is semisimple if and only if

is atomic and

acts freely on

. As both these properties are

-isomorphism invariant, we conclude that for two row-finite

-graphs without sources
\Lambda,\Omega
whose talented monoids are
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-isomorphic,
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
is semisimple if and only if
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Omega)
is semisimple. Moreover, considering the matrix decomposition of these algebras we are able to show that in this case the Kumjian-Pask algebras

and

are isomorphic as

-algebras (see Theorem

2.1.
Higher-rank graphs

Throughout this paper for any positive integer

stands for the set of all

-tuples of non-negative integers. This can be realized simultaneously as an commutative monoid under coordinatewise addition with identity

-tuples

and

m_{i}\leq n_{i}
for all

which in turn makes it into a lattice with join and meet defined as the coordinatewise maximum and minimum respectively. For
i=1,2,...,k
we denote

to be the
k
-tuple with
1
at the
i^{\text{th}}
coordinate and
0

Definition 2.1
.

A
k

5

\Lambda=(\Lambda^{0},\Lambda,r,s,d)
where
\Lambda
is a countable small category with (countable) set of objects

(which can also be viewed as a subset of
\Lambda
by identifying each object with the identity morphism on it),

are the

and

maps, and

is a functor (called the
degree
functor) which satisfies the
unique factorization property
: for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
with
d(\lambda)=m+n
, there exist
unique

\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}
,
d(\mu_{1})=m
,
d(\mu_{2})=n
.

The factorization property is equivalent to saying that the canonical map

\displaystyle\{(\lambda,\mu)~|~d(\lambda)=m,d(\mu)=n,s(\lambda)=r(\mu)\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow d^{-1}(m+n)

\displaystyle(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\lambda\mu

is bijective. We often call the arrows or morphisms of the category
\Lambda
as

and the objects as

.

We fix some notations related with

, we write

. For objects

,

. Similarly the sets

,

are respectively defined as

,

and

, we write
UV:=\{\lambda\mu~|~\lambda\in U,\mu\in V

2.1.1.

We now briefly recall the notion of infinite paths in a

k
-graph morphism
x:\Omega_{k}\longrightarrow\Lambda
is called an
infinite path
and the set

\Lambda^{\infty}:=\{x:\Omega_{k}\longrightarrow\Lambda~|~x
is a
k
-graph morphism
\}

is called the
infinite path space
of
\Lambda
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we denote
x((m,m))
simply as
x(m)
. Thus
x(m)
is a vertex in
\Lambda
. We define the range of
x
as
r(x):=x(0)
. For
v\in\Lambda^{0}
,
v\Lambda^{\infty}:=\{x\in\Lambda^{\infty}~|~x(0)=v\}
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
x\in\Lambda^{\infty}
, the
p
-shifted infinite path
\sigma^{p}(x)
is defined as:

\sigma^{p}(x)((m,n)):=x((m+p,n+p))
for all
(m,n)\in\Omega_{k}
.

The notion of
aperiodicity
of a
k
-graph was first introduced in
[
20
]
as an analogue of Condition
(L)
used in the study of Cuntz-Krieger algebras of directed graphs
[
21
]
. Aperiodicity is the main hypothesis for the Cuntz-Krieger uniqueness theorems for higher-rank graph
C^{*}
-algebras and Kumjian-Pask algebras and consequently it is used to characterize the simplicity of these algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. An infinite path
x\in\Lambda^{\infty}
is said to be
periodic
if there exists
0\neq p\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that
\sigma^{n}(x)=\sigma^{n+p}(x)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
n+p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. It is called
aperiodic
if it has no nonzero period i.e.,
\sigma^{m}(x)=\sigma^{n}(x)
for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
implies
m=n
. The
k
-graph
\Lambda
is said to satisfy the aperiodicity condition
(A)
(or
\Lambda
is
aperiodic
) if for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
there is an aperiodic infinite path
x
with
r(x)=x(0)=v
.

The concept of aperiodicity can also be defined in terms of finite paths.

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
32
]
,
Robertson-Sims finite path formulation
)

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. Then
\Lambda
is called aperiodic if for each
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and each pair of distinct elements
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, there is a path
\lambda\in v\Lambda
such that
d(\lambda)\geq m\vee n
and

\lambda(m,m+d(\lambda)-(m\vee n))\neq\lambda(n,n+d(\lambda)-(m\vee n)).

In 2010, Lewin and Sims
[
24
]
gave another definition of aperiodicity using finite paths in the general setting of a finitely aligned
k
-graph.

Definition 2.5
.

(
[
24
]
,
Lewin-Sims finite path formulation
)

Let
\Lambda
be a finitely aligned
k
-graph. Then
\Lambda
is called aperiodic if for every pair of distinct paths
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
with
s(\alpha)=s(\beta)
, there exists
\tau\in s(\alpha)\Lambda
such that
\operatorname{MCE}(\alpha\tau,\beta\tau)=\emptyset
(or equivalently,
\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha\tau,\beta\tau)=\emptyset
).

In case of row-finite
k
-graphs with no sources, all the above aperiodicity conditions are equivalent. We find Lewin-Sims criterion more easier to work with in our context.

There is also a higher-rank generalization of the so called condition
(K)
of directed graphs, known as
strong aperiodicity

[
19
]
. For this we need the notion of hereditary and saturated subsets in a
k
-graph. A subset
H\subseteq\Lambda^{0}
is called
hereditary
if
r(\lambda)\in H
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
implies
s(\lambda)\in H
; on the other hand
H
is called
saturated
if for
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
,
s(v\Lambda^{n})\subseteq H
implies
v\in H
. The set of all hereditary saturated subsets of
\Lambda^{0}
is denoted as
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
and it forms a lattice under the partial order of inclusion.

For any
H\in\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
, the
quotient
k
-graph
of
\Lambda
by
H
(see
[
7
]
) is defined as a
k
-graph with underlying set

\Lambda/H:=\{\lambda\in H~|~s(\lambda)\notin H\}

and set of objects

(\Lambda/H)^{0}:=\Lambda^{0}\setminus H
.

The range, source and the degree maps are the restrictions of
r,s
and
d
to
\Lambda/H
. It is easy to see that
\Lambda/H
is also a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. The
k
-graph is called
strongly aperiodic
(see
[
19
]
) if
\Lambda/H
is aperiodic for every proper hereditary saturated subset
H
of
\Lambda^{0}
.

Another notion which plays significant role in characterizing simplicity of higher-rank graph
C^{*}
-algebras and Kumjian-Pask algebras is
cofinality
. A
k
-graph
\Lambda
is said to be
cofinal
(see
[
20
, Definition 4.7]
) if for every
x\in\Lambda^{\infty}
and every
v\in\Lambda^{0}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
v\Lambda x(n)\neq\emptyset
.

2.1.2.
Kumjian-Pask algebras

In 2013, Aranda Pino et al.
[
7
]
defined Kumjian-Pask algebras as a higher-rank analogue of Leavitt path algebras. It was first defined for row-finite
k
-graphs without sources and later generalized for locally convex
k
-graphs by Clark, Flynn and an Huef
[
10
]
and for finitely aligned
k
-graphs by Clark and Pangalela
[
11
]
.

Suppose
\Lambda
is a row-finite
k
-graph. Let
\Lambda^{\neq 0}:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda~|~d(\lambda)\neq 0\}=\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{0}
and set
G(\Lambda^{\neq 0}):=\{\lambda^{*}~|~\lambda\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}\}
. The symbols
\lambda^{*}
are called
ghost paths
and accordingly
G(\Lambda^{\neq 0})
is the set of ghost paths.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[
7
, Definition 3.1]
,
Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda
-family
)

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources and
R
a unital commutative ring. A
Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda
-family

(P,S)
in an
R
-algebra
A
consists of two maps
P:\Lambda^{0}\longrightarrow A
and
S:\Lambda^{\neq 0}\cup G(\Lambda^{\neq 0})\longrightarrow A
(where images are written as subscripts) such that the following hold:

(KP1)

P_{u}P_{v}=\delta_{u,v}P_{u}
for all
u,v\in\Lambda^{0}
;

(KP2)

S_{\lambda}S_{\mu}=S_{\lambda\mu}
,
S_{\mu^{*}}S_{\lambda^{*}}=S_{(\lambda\mu)^{*}}
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
with
s(\lambda)=r(\mu)
and

P_{r(\lambda)}S_{\lambda}=S_{\lambda}=S_{\lambda}P_{s(\lambda)}
,
P_{s(\lambda)}S_{\lambda^{*}}=S_{\lambda^{*}}=S_{\lambda^{*}}P_{r(\lambda)}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
;

(KP3)

S_{\lambda^{*}}S_{\mu}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}P_{s(\lambda)}
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
with
d(\lambda)=d(\mu)
;

(KP4)

P_{v}=\displaystyle{\sum_{\lambda\in v\Lambda^{n}}}S_{\lambda}S_{\lambda^{*}}
for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

Given a row-finite
k
-graph
\Lambda
with no sources and a unital commutative ring
R
, the authors in
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
constructed an
R
-algebra
\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)
generated by a Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda
-family
(p,s)
which satisfies the following universal property: whenever
(Q,T)
is any Kumjian-Pask
\Lambda
-family in any
R
-algebra
A
, then there is a unique
R
-algebra homomorphism
\pi_{Q,T}:

\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)\longrightarrow A
such that

\pi_{Q,T}(p_{v})=Q_{v}
,
\pi_{Q,T}(s_{\lambda})=T_{\lambda}
and
\pi_{Q,T}(s_{\mu^{*}})=T_{\mu^{*}}

for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
.

Moreover the algebra
\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)
is equipped with a natural
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-grading where the
n^{\text{th}}
homogeneous component is defined as

\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)_{n}=\operatorname{span}_{R}(\{s_{\lambda}s_{\mu^{*}}~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
with
s(\lambda)=s(\mu)
and
d(\lambda)-d(\mu)=n\})

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. The
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
R
-algebra
\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)
is known as the
Kumjian-Pask algebra
of the
k
-graph
\Lambda
.

2.2.
Commutative monoids

We recall some basic concepts related with commutative monoids.

Let
(M,+)
be a commutative monoid with identity
0
. One can define a natural pre-order
\leq
on
M
as follows:

x\leq y\Longleftrightarrow y=x+z
for some
z\in M
.

This relation is usually called the
algebraic pre-order
on
M
. One can easily see that
\leq
is compatible with the addition of
M
i.e., if
x\leq y
and
x^{\prime}\leq y^{\prime}
then
x+x^{\prime}\leq y+y^{\prime}
. A submonoid
N
of
M
is called an
order ideal
if it is a down set with respect to the algebraic pre-order i.e., if
x\leq y\in N
implies
x\in N
. The set of all order ideals of
M
is denoted as
\mathcal{L}^{o}(M)
and it forms a
lattice
under set inclusion where the meet is the usual intersection and the join of two order ideals
N_{1},N_{2}
is defined as

N_{1}\vee N_{2}:=\overline{N_{1}+N_{2}}=\{x\in M~|~x\leq y_{1}+y_{2}
for some
y_{1}\in N_{1}
and
y_{2}\in N_{2}\}
.

In connection with the algebraic pre-order
\leq
on
M
, we have the following list of definitions which can be found in
[
3
,
5
,
27
,
35
]
.

Definitions 2.7
.

(i)
An element
u\in M
is called a
unit
if
u\leq 0
.

(ii)

M
is called
conical
if
0
is the only unit in
M
(equivalently, if
M^{*}:=M\setminus\{0\}
is a semigroup).

(iii)

M
is called
antisymmetric
if the relation
\leq
is antisymmetric.

(iv)
A nonunit
a\in M
is called an
atom
if
a=b+c
implies either
b
is a unit or
c
is a unit in
M
.

(v)
The monoid
M
is called
atomic
if each nonunit in
M
can be written as a finite sum of atoms.

(vi)
A nonunit
x\in M
is said to have a
unique factorization into atoms
if
x
can be written as a sum of atoms and whenever

x=a_{1}+a_{2}+...+a_{m}=a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}+...+a_{n}^{\prime}

where
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{m}
,
a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},...,a_{n}^{\prime}
are atoms then
n=m
and there is a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
such that

a_{i}\leq a_{\pi(i)}^{\prime}
and
a_{\pi(i)}^{\prime}\leq a_{i}
for all
i=1,2,...,n
.

(vii)

M
is called
unique factorization monoid (UFM)
if
M
is atomic and every nonunit in
M
has a unique factorization into atoms.

(viii)
An element
a\in M
is called
minimal
if
b\leq a
for
b\in M
implies either
b=0
or
b=a
.

A commutative monoid
M
is called a
refinement monoid
if for all
a,b,c,d\in M
with
a+b=c+d
there exist
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}\in M
such that

a=z_{1}+z_{2}
,
b=z_{3}+z_{4}
and
c=z_{1}+z_{3}
,
d=z_{2}+z_{4}
.

M
is called a
cancellative monoid
if
x+y=x+z
for
x,y,z\in M
implies
y=z
.

Remark 2.8
.

In a conical monoid
M
,
0
is a minimal element and
\{0\}
is an order ideal. Moreover if
M
is cancellative then it is antisymmetric and in this case atoms and nonzero minimal elements do coincide.

Given a commutative monoid
M
and a submonoid
N
, one can define a relation
\equiv_{N}
on
M
by

x\equiv_{N}y\Longleftrightarrow x+a=y+b
for some
a,b\in N

It is straightforward to check that
\equiv_{N}
is a monoid congruence on
M
. We sometimes write the quotient monoid
M/\equiv_{N}
as
M/N
.

2.2.1.
\Gamma
-monoid

Given a group
\Gamma
and a commutative monoid
M
, we say that
M
is a
\Gamma
-monoid
if
\Gamma\curvearrowright M
(
\Gamma
acts on
M
) by monoid automorphisms. Following the standard notation used in
[
12
,
17
]
, we denote the action of
\alpha\in\Gamma
on
x\in M
by
{}^{\alpha}x
. Note that the
\Gamma
-action is compatible with the algebraic pre-ordering in the sense that
x\leq y
implies
{}^{\alpha}x~\leq~^{\alpha}y
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
.

If
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
\Gamma
-monoids then a
\Gamma
-monoid homomorphism

f:M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
is a monoid homomorphism which respects the
\Gamma
-action i.e.,
f(^{\alpha}x)=~^{\alpha}f(x)
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
and
x\in M
.

An order ideal
J
of a
\Gamma
-monoid
M
is said to be a
\Gamma
-order ideal
if
J
is closed under the
\Gamma
-action. Equivalently a
\Gamma
-order ideal
J
is a subset of
M
such that for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
and
x,y\in M
,
{}^{\alpha}x+~^{\beta}y\in J
if and only if
x,y\in J
. We denote the lattice of all
\Gamma
-order ideals of
M
by
\mathcal{L}(M)
. For a subset
A
of
M
,
\langle A\rangle
denotes the
\Gamma
-order ideal generated by
A
. A nonzero conical
\Gamma
-monoid
M
is called
simple
if
\{0\}
and
M
are the only
\Gamma
-order ideals of
M
. A nonzero element
a\in M
is called
periodic
if there exists
\alpha\neq 0\in\Gamma
such that
{}^{\alpha}a=a
, otherwise
a
is called
aperiodic
. We say that
\Gamma
acts
freely
on
M
if there is no periodic element in
M
.

A
\Gamma
-order ideal
P
of
M
is called
prime
if for any two
\Gamma
-order ideals
J_{1},J_{2}
of
M
,
J_{1}\cap J_{2}\subseteq P
implies either
J_{1}\subseteq P
or
J_{2}\subseteq P
.

A
\Gamma
-monoid
M
is called
finitely generated as a
\Gamma
-monoid
if there is a finite subset
A\subseteq M
such that for any
x\in M
there exist
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{n}\in\Gamma
and
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\in A
(not necessarily distinct) such that
x=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}~^{\alpha_{i}}a_{i}
. Clearly in this case
M=\langle A\rangle
.

2.2.2.
Talented monoid of a directed graph

As we seek to cast the analogue of talented monoid for higher-rank graphs, we include its definition in the setting of directed graphs.

Definition 2.9
.

(
[
17
,
12
]
) Let
E
be any row-finite directed graph. The
talented monoid
of
E
is denoted as
T_{E}
and is defined to be the commutative monoid generated by
\{v(i)~|~v\in E^{0},i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
subject to the relations:

v(i)=\displaystyle{\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}}r(e)(i+1)

for all vertices
v
which are not sink and for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The monoid
T_{E}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-monoid with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}
-action defined on generators as:

{}^{n}v(i):=v(i+n)
.

This monoid can be seen as a graded version of the graph monoid
M_{E}
. To witness the beautiful relations of the talented monoid with the geometry of the underlying directed graph as well as with the algebraic properties of the associated Leavitt path algebra, the readers may go through
[
17
,
12
]
.

2.3.
Groupoid and Type semigroup

If
\Lambda
is a
k
-graph then using the degree functor
d
together with the fact that
\mathbb{N}^{k}
is a conical monoid, one can verify that the only arrows of
\Lambda
which are invertible are the units i.e., no
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{0}
is invertible. In the world of small categories,
groupoids
sit completely at the other corner. A
groupoid
is a small category consisting of isomorphisms only. We assume that the readers are familiar with the basic notions of topological groupoids.

2.3.1.
The path groupoid of a
k
-graph

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph with no sources. In
[
20
]
, the authors defined a groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
to obtain a groupoid model for the higher-rank graph
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\Lambda)
. We briefly describe the construction.

Define

\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}:=\{(x,n-m,y)\in\Lambda^{\infty}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Lambda^{\infty}~|~n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
\sigma^{n}(x)=\sigma^{m}(y)\}
.

The unit space
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{(0)}:=\Lambda^{\infty}
; the range and source maps are defined as
r((x,n,y)):=x
and
s((x,n,y)):=y
. Two arrows
(x,n,y)
and
(z,m,w)
are composable if
y=z
and in that case

(x,n,y)(y,m,w):=(x,n+m,w)
.

The inverse of an arrow
(x,n,y)
is defined as
(x,n,y)^{-1}:=(y,-n,x)
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
is a groupoid which is known as the
(infinite) path groupoid
of
\Lambda
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
with
s(\lambda)=s(\mu)
define

Z(\lambda,\mu):=\{(\lambda z,d(\lambda)-d(\mu),\mu z)~|~z\in s(\lambda)\Lambda^{\infty}\}
.

Then
\mathcal{B}:=\{Z(\lambda,\mu)~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,s(\lambda)=s(\mu)\}
is a basis for a locally compact, Hausdorff topology on
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
. Moreover each
Z(\lambda,\mu)
is a compact open bisection which makes
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
an ample groupoid. If we identify
x\in\Lambda^{\infty}
with the arrow
(x,0,x)
then the sets
Z(\lambda):=Z(\lambda,\lambda)
form a basis of compact open sets for the subspace topology on
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{(0)}=\Lambda^{\infty}
.

If
\Lambda=E^{*}
(the path category of a row-finite directed graph
E
with no sink), then
\mathcal{G}_{E^{*}}\cong\mathcal{G}_{E}
, the boundary path groupoid of
E
. Both the groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{E}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
are prototypes of the so-called
Deaconu-Renault groupoids

[
14
]
.

2.3.2.
Type semigroup

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup with
0
. Two elements
x,y\in S
are said to be
orthogonal
if
x^{*}y=xy^{*}=0
. If
x,y
are orthogonal then we write
x\perp y
. The semigroup
S
is called a
Boolean inverse semigroup
if the semilattice of idempotents
E(S)
is a Boolean ring (equivalently a
generalized Boolean algebra
) such that any two orthogonal elements
x,y
have a
join
which is denoted by
x\oplus y
.

Definition 2.10
.

(
[
3
,
12
,
35
]
) Let
S
be a Boolean inverse semigroup. For each
x\in E(S)
we set a symbol
\operatorname{typ}(x)
. The
type semigroup
or
type monoid
of
S
is denoted as
\operatorname{Typ}(S)
and defined as the commutative monoid freely generated by the symbols
\operatorname{typ}(x)
, where
x\in E(S)
subject to the following relations:

(i)

\operatorname{typ}(0)=0
;

(ii)

\operatorname{typ}(x)=\operatorname{typ}(y)
whenever there exists
s\in S
such that
x=s^{*}s
and
y=ss^{*}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{typ}(x\oplus y)=\operatorname{typ}(x)+\operatorname{typ}(y)
whenever
x,y\in E(S)
are such that
x\perp y
and
x\oplus y\in E(S)
.

An interesting fact about type monoid is that it is always a conical refinement monoid (see
[
35
, Corollary 4.1.4]
). Let
\mathcal{G}
be a (not necessarily Hausdorff) ample groupoid. We denote
\mathcal{G}^{a}
to be the collection of all compact open bisections in
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\mathcal{G}^{a}
is an inverse semigroup with
0
(the empty bisection) where the operations are defined as:

AB:=\{\alpha\beta~|~(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\cap(A\times B)\}

A^{-1}:=\{\alpha^{-1}~|~\alpha\in A\}

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{G}^{a}
.

The natural partial order on this inverse semigroup becomes the usual set inclusion. The semilattice of idempotents
E(\mathcal{G}^{a})
coincides with the set of all compact open subsets of the unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and it is a Boolean ring where sum and product are respectively the symmetric difference and the intersection. Moreover if
A,B\in\mathcal{G}^{a}
are such that
A\perp B
(that means
A^{-1}B=AB^{-1}=\emptyset
) then
A\cap B=\emptyset
and in this case
A\oplus B:=A\sqcup B
is the join of
A,B
. This shows that
\mathcal{G}^{a}
is a Boolean inverse semigroup. One of the important examples of type semigroups is the
type semigroup of
\mathcal{G}
which is defined as
\operatorname{Typ}(\mathcal{G}):=\operatorname{Typ}(\mathcal{G}^{a})
.

Type semigroup of an ample groupoid is defined in some other ways also, for example see
[
8
, Definition 5.3]
and
[
30
, Definition 5.4]
. However all these definitions are equivalent. We briefly recall Bönicke and Li’s definition from
[
8
]
for its use in this article.

Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an ample groupoid. Define a relation
\sim
on

\left\{\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}}~(A_{i}\times\{i\})~|~n\in\mathbb{N},A_{i}\in E(\mathcal{G}^{a})\right\}

as follows: for two such elements
A=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}}~(A_{i}\times\{i\})
and
B=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}}~(B_{j}\times\{j\})
,
A\sim B
if there exist
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{1},W_{2},...,W_{l}\in\mathcal{G}^{a}
and
n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{l},m_{1},m_{2},...,m_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

A=\displaystyle{\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{l}}~s(W_{k})\times\{n_{k}\}
and
B=\displaystyle{\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{l}}~r(W_{k})\times\{m_{k}\}
.

It is an equivalence relation as shown in
[
8
, Lemma 5.2]
. The
type semigroup
of
\mathcal{G}
is defined as the set

In

2.3.3.

Let

Lemma 3.4

Let

and

\textstyle{\mathfrak{s}^{i}(v)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Clearly

(1)

\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{m}}\sum_{\beta\in s(\alpha)\Lambda^{n}}s(\beta)=\sum_{\beta\in v\Lambda^{n}}\sum_{\alpha\in s(\beta)\Lambda^{m}}s(\alpha)=\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{m+n}}s(\alpha).

We have

\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{m}}\sum_{\beta\in s(\alpha)\Lambda^{n}}s(\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{m}}\sum_{\beta\in s(\alpha)\Lambda^{n}}s(\alpha\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\beta\in A}s(\alpha\beta),

where

Now using (

\mathfrak{s}^{i}(v)=\displaystyle{\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{e_{i}}}}s(\alpha)\longrightarrow\sum_{\alpha\in v\Lambda^{e_{i}}}\sum_{\beta\in s(\alpha)\Lambda^{e_{j}}}s(\beta)=\sum_{\lambda\in v\Lambda^{e_{i}+e_{j}}}s(\lambda)

Lemma 3.5
.

(

) Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. Let

\alpha\sim\beta
if and only if there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\Lambda}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\alpha\longrightarrow\gamma
and
\beta\longrightarrow\gamma

Proof.

The sufficiency is clear from the definition of the relation
\sim
. So assume that
\alpha\sim\beta
. Then there exists a finite sequence
\alpha=\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}=\beta
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\Lambda}}\setminus\{0\}

\alpha_{t}\longrightarrow_{i}\alpha_{t+1}
or
\alpha_{t+1}\longrightarrow_{j}\alpha_{t}
for some

then
\alpha=\beta
then taking
\gamma=\alpha

\alpha=\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},...\alpha_{n-1}

\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\Lambda}}\setminus\{0\}

\alpha\longrightarrow\lambda
and
\alpha_{n-1}\longrightarrow\lambda

Case-1:

and taking

Case-2:
Suppose

for some

Subcase-1:

and
\langle X\rangle=\{0\}
if
X=\emptyset
. For
X(\neq\emptyset)\subseteq\Lambda^{0}
, it is not hard to show that

\langle X\rangle=\{x\in T_{\Lambda}~|~x\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}~^{n_{i}}v_{i}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N};~n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and

for all

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
J
be any
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal of
T_{\Lambda}
. Then
J=\langle H_{{}_{J}}\rangle
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\langle H_{{}_{J}}\rangle\subseteq J
is quite clear. For the other inclusion let
0\neq x\in J
. Suppose
x=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}v_{i}(n_{i})
is any representation of
x
in
T_{\Lambda}
. Then for each
i=1,2,...,t
we have
v_{i}(n_{i})\leq x
and so
v_{i}=~^{-n_{i}}v_{i}(n_{i})\in J
since
J
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal. This implies
v_{i}\in H_{{}_{J}}
for each

and hence
x=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}v_{i}(n_{i})\in\langle H_{{}_{J}}\rangle
.
∎

We obtain the following proposition which gives a necessary condition for cofinality of
\Lambda
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. If
\Lambda
is cofinal then
T_{\Lambda}
is a simple
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Lemma 5.12]
the only hereditary and saturated subsets of

are

and
\Lambda^{0}
itself. Suppose

The next lemma helps us to prove our desired lattice isomorphism as well as the converse of Proposition

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
H
be any hereditary and saturated subset of
\Lambda^{0}
. Let
J_{{}_{H}}:=\langle H\rangle
. Then
H=H_{J_{{}_{H}}}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
H\subseteq H_{J_{{}_{H}}}
is clear. Take
v\in H_{J_{{}_{H}}}
. Then
v\in\langle H\rangle
. Hence
v\leq\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}u_{i}(n_{i})
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{i}\in H
and
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
for all
i=1,2,..,t
. It implies
v+x=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}u_{i}(n_{i})
for some
x\in T_{\Lambda}
. Now we use the isomorphism
\phi:T_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow M_{\overline{\Lambda}}
to bypass this equality to
M_{\overline{\Lambda}}
. Let

. Then we have

(v,0)+y\sim\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}(u_{i},n_{i})
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\setminus\{0\}
.

By Lemma
3.5
, there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(v,0)+y\longrightarrow\gamma
and
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}(u_{i},n_{i})\longrightarrow\gamma
. Since
H
is hereditary so is
H\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
in
\overline{\Lambda}^{0}
. Therefore each vertex in the basic representation of
\gamma
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\overline{\Lambda}}}
is in
H\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Now by Lemma
3.1
, there are
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{\overline{\Lambda}}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(v,0)\longrightarrow\gamma_{1}
,

and
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}

(v,0)\longrightarrow_{j_{1}}\eta_{1}\longrightarrow_{j_{2}}\eta_{2}\longrightarrow_{j_{3}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\longrightarrow_{j_{m}}\eta_{m}=\gamma_{1}

Since
\eta_{m-1}\longrightarrow_{j_{m}}\gamma_{1}
, so
\eta_{m-1}=\eta_{m-1}^{\prime}+(w,l)
and
\gamma_{1}=\eta_{m-1}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{s}^{j_{m}}((w,l))

and

As

and

is saturated so

which in turn implies

and we are done.

By

and the lattice of

-order ideals of

and

Proposition 4.5
.

Define
\rho:\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(T_{\Lambda})
by

and
\eta:\mathcal{L}(T_{\Lambda})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
by

. Then

and

is lattice isomorphic with
\mathcal{L}(T_{\Lambda})
.

Let
R
be a unital commutative ring and
\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)
the Kumjian-Pask algebra of
\Lambda
over

(see Definition

). We denote
\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{gb}}(\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda))

As an immediate application of these lattice isomorphisms, we obtain the following proposition which is an analogue of
[
17
, Lemma 5.5]
in the level of higher-rank graphs.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. Let
J
be any
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal of
T_{\Lambda}
. Then
J
is prime if and only if for any
x,y\in T_{\Lambda}\setminus J
, there exist
z\in T_{\Lambda}\setminus J
and
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that
{}^{n}x
,
{}^{m}y\geq z
.

Proof.

The sufficiency part follows mutatis-mutandis the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 5.5]
. For the other way round suppose
J
is any prime
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal of
T_{\Lambda}
. We claim that
I(H_{{}_{J}})
is a graded prime ideal of
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
, where
\mathsf{k}
is any field. Assume that
I_{1},I_{2}
are graded ideals of
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
such that
I_{1}I_{2}\subseteq I(H_{{}_{J}})
. Using the lattice isomorphism between
\mathcal{L}(T_{\Lambda})
and
\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{gb}}(\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda))
, we can find
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideals
J_{1},J_{2}
of
T_{\Lambda}
such that
I_{i}=I(H_{J_{i}})
for
i=1,2
. Since any graded ideal of
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
is idempotent (see
[
7

), so
I_{1}I_{2}=I_{1}\cap I_{2}
. Then

I(H_{J_{1}\cap J_{2}})=I(H_{J_{1}})\cap I(H_{J_{2}})=I_{1}\cap I_{2}=I_{1}I_{2}\subseteq I(H_{{}_{J}})

which implies
J_{1}\cap J_{2}\subseteq J
. Hence
J_{1}\subseteq J
or
J_{2}\subseteq J
since

is a prime order ideal. So
I_{1}\subseteq I(H_{{}_{J}})
or
I_{2}\subseteq I(H_{{}_{J}})
and our claim is established. It follows that
\Lambda^{0}\setminus H_{{}_{J}}
is a maximal tail (see
[
19
, Definition 3.10]
) by
[
22
, Proposition 4.1]
. Let
x=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{p}}v_{i}(n_{i})
,
y=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{q}}w_{j}(m_{j})\in T_{\Lambda}\setminus J
. Since
J
is an order-ideal and

(by Theorem

), so there must exist indices

and

such that

. By the definition of maximal tail, there exists

such that

. Choose
\lambda\in v_{i}\Lambda u
and
\mu\in w_{j}\Lambda u
. Then in

,

and

. Finally letting

,

we have

,
{}^{m}y\geq u
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
\overline{v}
be the smallest hereditary and saturated subset containing
v
. An explicit description of
\overline{v}
can be found in
[
28
, Lemma 5.1]
. However for the sake of completion, we exhibit a way of constructing
\overline{v}
.

For any subset
X\subseteq\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we denote

X^{n}:=\{u\in\Lambda^{0}~|~s(u\Lambda^{n})\subseteq X\}

and set
\mathcal{S}(X):=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}}X^{n}
. Now for
v\in\Lambda^{0}
, let
X_{0}:=s(v\Lambda)
and
X_{i+1}:=\mathcal{S}(X_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

With notations as above,
\overline{v}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
for any
v\in\Lambda^{0}
.

Proof.

It is easy to observe that any hereditary saturated subset containing
v
indeed contains
\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
. So if we can show that
\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
is hereditary and saturated then we are done. First we show that
X_{i}
is hereditary for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and this will establish that their union is hereditary. For this we use induction. Clearly
X_{0}=s(v\Lambda)
is hereditary. Suppose
X_{i}
is hereditary for some
i\geq 0
and
r(\lambda)\in X_{i+1}
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then
r(\lambda)\in X_{i}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. Then
s(\alpha)\in X_{i}
for all
\alpha\in r(\lambda)\Lambda^{n}
. Let
m:=(n\vee d(\lambda))-d(\lambda)
. We claim that
s(\mu)\in X_{i}
for all
\mu\in s(\lambda)\Lambda^{m}
. Let
\mu\in s(\lambda)\Lambda^{m}
. Then
\lambda\mu\in r(\lambda)\Lambda^{n\vee d(\lambda)}
. By factorization property, there exists unique
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda\mu=\nu_{1}\nu_{2}
with
d(\nu_{1})=n
and
d(\nu_{2})=(n\vee d(\lambda))-n
. Then
\nu_{1}\in r(\lambda)\Lambda^{n}
and so
r(\nu_{2})=s(\nu_{1})\in X_{i}
which implies
s(\mu)=s(\nu_{2})\in X_{i}
since
X_{i}
is hereditary. This shows that
s(\lambda)\in X_{i}^{m}\subseteq X_{i+1}
. Therefore
X_{i+1}
is hereditary. Next we show that
\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
is saturated. Let
u\in\Lambda^{0}
be such that
s(u\Lambda^{m}))\subseteq\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. Since
\Lambda
is row-finite and
X_{i}\subseteq X_{i+1}
so there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
s(\alpha)\in X_{t+1}
for all
\alpha\in u\Lambda^{m}
(note that if
s(u\Lambda^{m})\subseteq X_{0}
then
u\in X_{0}^{m}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
and so we only consider the case where
s(u\Lambda^{m})\subseteq X_{j}
for some
j\geq 1
). Now for each
\alpha\in u\Lambda^{m}
, there is
n_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
s(\alpha)\in X_{t}^{n_{\alpha}}
. Set
n:=m+\displaystyle{\bigvee_{\alpha\in u\Lambda^{m}}}n_{\alpha}
. We will show that
u\in X_{t}^{n}
. So take any
\lambda\in u\Lambda^{n}
. Let
\mu:=\lambda(0,m)
. Since
\mu\in u\Lambda^{m}
we have
s(\mu)\in X_{t}^{n_{\mu}}
. Applying unique factorization again, we obtain unique
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda(m,n)=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
with
d(\gamma_{1})=n_{\mu}
and
d(\gamma_{2})=n-m-n_{\mu}
. Then
\gamma_{1}\in s(\mu)\Lambda^{n_{\mu}}
which implies
r(\gamma_{2})=s(\gamma_{1})\in X_{t}
and consequently
s(\gamma_{2})\in X_{t}
since
X_{t}
is hereditary. Now
s(\lambda)=s(\lambda(m,n))=s(\gamma_{2})\in X_{t}
. Hence we have
u\in X_{t}^{n}\subseteq X_{t+1}\subseteq\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
and so
\displaystyle{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}}X_{i}
is saturated.
∎

Now we are ready to characterize cofinality of
\Lambda
using the property of the monoid
T_{\Lambda}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources. Then
\Lambda
is cofinal if and only if
T_{\Lambda}
is a simple
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid.

Proof.

The necessity is already proved in Proposition
4.3
. For the sufficiency assume that
T_{\Lambda}
is a simple
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid. Let
x\in\Lambda^{\infty}
be any infinite path and
v\in\Lambda^{0}
. Consider the nonzero
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal
\langle v\rangle
. Then
\langle v\rangle=T_{\Lambda}
and so
\overline{v}=H_{\langle\overline{v}\rangle}=H_{\langle v\rangle}=\Lambda^{0}
. Therefore
x(0)\in\overline{v}
. So
x(0)\in X_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
by Lemma
4.7
. If
x(0)\in X_{0}=s(v\Lambda)
then clearly
v\Lambda x(0)\neq\emptyset
. Assume that
x(0)\in X_{j+1}
for some
j\geq 0
. Then
x(0)\in\mathcal{S}(X_{j})
. So there exists
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
x(0)\in X_{j}^{n_{1}}
. Hence
x(n_{1})=s(x(0,n_{1}))\in X_{j}=\mathcal{S}(X_{j-1})
. Thus there exists
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
x(n_{1})\in X_{j-1}^{n_{2}}
which implies
x(n_{1}+n_{2})=s(x(n_{1},n_{1}+n_{2}))\in X_{j-1}
. Since
x
is an infinite path we can continue this process and after
j
-steps we can find
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that

x(n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{j})=s(x(n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{j-1},n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{j}))\in X_{1}=\mathcal{S}(X_{0}).

Now there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
x(n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{j}+m)\in X_{0}
. Let
n=n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{j}+m
. Then we have
x(n)\in s(v\Lambda)
and consequently

.
∎

\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda)

.

The following statements are equivalent.

\Lambda

(ii)

is a simple
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid.

(iii)

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources and
\mathsf{k}
any field. Then the Kumjian-Pask algebra
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
is graded simple if and only if

As the structure of any
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-order ideal of

is now clear, we next focus on the quotient of

by any such ideal.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
. Define
\rho:T_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow T_{\Lambda/H}
on generators as follows:

and then extend linearly. Since

is hereditary and saturated one can check that

is a well-defined surjective homomorphism of

-monoids.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
H
be any hereditary saturated subset of
\Lambda^{0}
. Suppose
\equiv_{H}
is the congruence on
T_{\Lambda}
defined by
a\equiv_{H}b
if and only if
a+x=b+y
for some

T_{\Lambda}/\equiv_{H}~\cong T_{\Lambda/H}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the kernel congruence
Ker(\rho)=~\equiv_{H}
. If
a\equiv_{H}b
then it is easy to show that
\rho(a)=\rho(b)
and hence
(a,b)\in Ker(\rho)
. Let
(a,b)\in Ker(\rho)
. Let
a=a^{\prime}+h_{1}
and
b=b^{\prime}+h_{2}
be any two representations of
a,b\in T_{\Lambda}
such that the vertices appearing in
h_{1},h_{2}
are in
H
and the vertices in
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
are outside
H
. Then in
T_{\Lambda/H}
,
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}
. Passing this equality to

via the natural isomorphism, we can find

such that
a^{\prime}\longrightarrow c
and
b^{\prime}\longrightarrow c
(note that we are making some abuse of notation for simplicity). Clearly
\operatorname{supp}(a^{\prime})
,
\operatorname{supp}(b^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq(\Lambda^{0}\setminus H)\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Let us analyze

. It implies that there is a chain of arrows:

a^{\prime}\longrightarrow_{i_{1}}\alpha_{1}\longrightarrow_{i_{2}}\alpha_{2}\longrightarrow_{i_{3}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\longrightarrow_{i_{l}}\alpha_{l}=c
in
\mathbb{F}_{\overline{\Lambda/H}}
.

Then
a^{\prime}=a^{\prime\prime}+a_{0}
for some
a_{0}\in(\Lambda^{0}\setminus H)\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
\alpha_{1}=a^{\prime\prime}+\mathfrak{s}^{i_{1}}(a_{0})
where
\mathfrak{s}^{i_{1}}(a_{0})=\displaystyle{\sum_{\lambda\in a_{0}\overline{\Lambda/H}^{e_{i_{1}}}}}\overline{s}(\lambda)
. Therefore in
\mathbb{F}_{\overline{\Lambda}}
,
a^{\prime}\longrightarrow_{i_{1}}\alpha_{1}+h_{\alpha_{1}}
for some

with
\operatorname{supp}(h_{\alpha_{1}})\subseteq H\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Inductively we can show that
a^{\prime}\longrightarrow c+h_{a}
for some
h_{a}
with
\operatorname{supp}(h_{a})\subseteq H\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Similarly
b^{\prime}\longrightarrow c+h_{b}
in
\mathbb{F}_{\overline{\Lambda}}
for some
h_{b}
with
\operatorname{supp}(h_{b})\subseteq H\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Then
a^{\prime}+h_{b},b^{\prime}+h_{a}\longrightarrow c+h_{a}+h_{b}
and so
a^{\prime}+h_{b}=b^{\prime}+h_{a}
in
M_{\overline{\Lambda}}
. Now returning to
T_{\Lambda}
via the inverse of the natural isomorphism, we have
a^{\prime}+h_{b}=b^{\prime}+h_{a}
in
T_{\Lambda}
(here also we make abuse of notation). Hence
a+h_{2}+h_{b}=b+h_{1}+h_{a}
. Finally since
h_{a}+h_{1},h_{b}+h_{2}\in\langle H\rangle
, so

.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources and

any unital commutative ring. Suppose

acts freely on every quotient of

\mathcal{L}^{b}(\operatorname{KP}_{R}(\Lambda))

Let

. By the hypothesis

acts freely on

which, by Lemma

. Hence

is aperiodic by Proposition

together with Proposition

.

Proposition 5.2

Let

be a row-finite
k
-graph such that
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
acts freely on

. Then
\Lambda
is aperiodic.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive statement. Suppose

is a periodic pair in
\Lambda

m:=d(\alpha),n:=d(\beta)
. Then
\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)=\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha s(\alpha),\beta s(\beta))\neq\emptyset
. It implies

. Let
v=s(\alpha)=s(\beta)
,
m^{\prime}:=(m\vee n)-m
and
n^{\prime}:=(m\vee n)-n
. We claim that the three sets

and
\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)
are in bijective correspondences. Let

is a periodic pair, so
\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha\mu,\beta\mu)\neq\emptyset
. Thus there exists
\overline{\mu}\in\Lambda
such that

\overline{\mu}(0,m\vee n)=\alpha\mu
and
\overline{\mu}(0,d(\beta\mu))=\beta\mu

. So by factorization property, there exist unique

and

. But then
\overline{\mu}(0,n)=\beta^{\prime}
which shows that
\beta^{\prime}=\beta
and so
\alpha\mu=\beta\nu_{\mu}
. Hence

. Note that the choice of
\nu_{\mu}
does not depend on the choice of the minimal common extension
\overline{\mu}
of
\alpha\mu
and
\beta\mu
. Thus we have a well-defined map

\displaystyle\phi:v\Lambda^{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\longmapsto\phi(\mu):=(\mu,\nu_{\mu}).

Since
\Lambda
is cancellative, the map

is bijective. Using similar construction one can show that the map

\psi:\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)\longrightarrow v\Lambda^{n^{\prime}};(\gamma,\delta)\longmapsto\delta

is also bijective. Since

|\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)|<\infty
. Let
\Lambda^{\min}(\alpha,\beta)=\{(\mu_{1},\nu_{1}),(\mu_{2},\nu_{2}),...,(\mu_{r},\nu_{r})\}
. Now using the bijection

we have,

v=\displaystyle{\sum_{\mu\in v\Lambda^{m^{\prime}}}}s(\mu)(m^{\prime})=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{r}}s(\mu_{i})(m^{\prime})=~^{m^{\prime}}(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{r}}s(\mu_{i}))
.

Similarly using the bijection
\psi
and defining relations of

we can have
v=~^{n^{\prime}}(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{r}}s(\nu_{i}))
. Now since
s(\mu_{i})=s(\nu_{i})
, so
{}^{m^{\prime}}a=~^{n^{\prime}}a
where

. Thus
{}^{m^{\prime}-n^{\prime}}a=a
and
m^{\prime}-n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\setminus\{0\}

.
∎

v\sim_{\Lambda}w\Longleftrightarrow x(m)=y(n)

If

is semisimple then we can provide a necessary and sufficient criterion for

to be finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoid. First note that if

is finite then choosing a representative from each equivalence class we can form a finite generating set for
T_{\Lambda}
. Conversely suppose

is a finite generating set for
T_{\Lambda}
with

for all

. Since each element of

can be written as a sum of aperiodic atoms, we can assume from the beginning that all the
a_{i}
’s are in fact aperiodic atoms. Then for any line point

, there exists

and

such that

in

. This implies that there exists

and

and

,

and

.

Suppose
v\Lambda^{\infty}=\{x\}
and

. Then we have

x(m)=s(x(0,m))=s(\lambda)=w=s(\mu)=s(y(0,m-n))=y(m-n)

Hence
v\sim_{\Lambda}a_{\alpha}
and it proves that
|P_{l}(\Lambda)/\sim_{\Lambda}|\leq m
.

We now obtain the final result of this paper.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
\Lambda
and

be row-finite

-graphs without sources and

any field. Let

\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Omega)

\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)\cong

\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Omega)

5.13
. Now if

Remark 5.16
.

If the Kumjian-Pask algebras
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Lambda)
and
\operatorname{KP}_{\mathsf{k}}(\Omega)
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded algebras then their graded
\mathcal{V}
-monoids are isomorphic as
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoids. By Proposition
3.14

(i)
, it implies
T_{\Lambda}
and
T_{\Omega}
are isomophic as
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-monoids. In Theorem
5.15
, we show that for semisimple Kumjian-Pask algebras if the talented monoids are
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-isomorphic and finitely generated then the algebras are in fact isomorphic. However they may not be isomorphic as
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded algebras. The reason is that semsimple Kumjian-Pask algebras of row-finite
k
-graphs without sources are non-unital; moreover according to
[
9
, Remark 4.1]
these algebras can be realized as Leavitt path algebras of certain directed graphs. These directed graphs fall in the class considered by the first named author and Vaš in
[
18
]
. It was shown that
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module isomorphism between the graded Grothendieck groups is not enough to ensure graded isomorphism between the corresponding Leavitt path algebras; for more details see
[
18
, Example 5.2 and Corollary 5.6]
.

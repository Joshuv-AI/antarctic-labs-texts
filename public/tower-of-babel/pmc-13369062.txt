Comparative Cost-Utility and Clinical Efficacy of 5-HT3 Receptor Antagonists in Sufentanil-Based PCIA: A Propensity Score-Matched Analysis
Authors: Youmei Wu, Yingchao Ye, Han Gong, Fangfang Xia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative pain management using sufentanil-based patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) is often compromised by opioid-induced nausea and vomiting. Although 5-HT3 receptor antagonists are commonly added to PCIA regimens, the effectiveness of continuous of low-concentration infusion strategies remains uncertain from a pharmacokinetic perspective. Furthermore, China’s national volume-based procurement (VBP) policy has drastically altered the cost landscape, necessitating a re-evaluation of premium agents such as palonosetron. This study aimed to assess the incremental clinical outcomes and cost-effectiveness of palonosetron versus ondansetron in this specific post-VBP setting.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study with propensity score matching (PSM). Patients receiving sufentanil-based PCIA were grouped as sufentanil alone, sufentanil plus ondansetron, or sufentanil plus palonosetron. The primary outcomes were the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). A cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA) was performed using bootstrapping and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEAC).ResultsAfter matching, neither palonosetron nor ondansetron significantly reduced overall PONV incidence compared with sufentanil alone. However, subgroup analyses revealed that palonosetron significantly reduced severe nausea in elderly patients (P < 0.05). From an economic perspective, palonosetron demonstrated a high probability (87.0%) of being cost-saving in a probabilistic sensitivity analysis, whereas ondansetron was likely to be “dominated.”ConclusionsUnder low-concentration background infusion regimens, 5-HT3 antagonists may provide limited benefit in the general patient population, especially high-risk female patients. However, in the context of VBP-driven price reductions, palonosetron may represent a cost-saving option, with potential benefits in elderly patients, and may confer additional prophylactic advantages in male patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40122-026-00847-6.Keywords: Postoperative nausea and vomiting, Palonosetron, Ondansetron, Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia, Cost-effectiveness

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) remains a common complication after opioid-based analgesia, with economic implications.The cost-effectiveness of different 5-HT3 receptor antagonists in PCIA settings remains unclear.This study evaluated the clinical and economic outcomes of palonosetron versus ondansetron. What was learned from the study? Palonosetron showed comparable clinical efficacy to ondansetron but with lower overall costs and an 87% probability of economic dominance.Subgroup analysis suggested a potential benefit in elderly patients, while findings in male patients were marginal.These results support palonosetron as a cost-saving alternative, though clinical superiority was not established.

Introduction

Postoperative pain control is an important component of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) [1]. Sufentanil, distinguished by its potent analgesia and hemodynamic stability, is widely used in opioid-based patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) [2]. However, its side effects (mostly opioid-induced emesis) limit the duration of therapy in approximately 30–80% of patients due to drug toxicity [3]. Severe postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) can decrease the satisfaction with treatment and may cause serious medical complications such as wound dehiscence, lung aspiration, and metabolic disorder [4]. Such complications may lead to prolonged length of stay and increased cost of care, indicating that maintenance of analgesia without causing PONV is an important goal.

In recent years, 5-HT3 receptor antagonists are often used in PCIA regimen to mitigate these side effects [5]. While ondansetron is still widely used [6], palonosetron is another more recent antiemetic drug with the advantage of having a higher affinity for the receptors and a longer half-life (> 40 h) [7]. An often-overlooked factor is the mode of drug administration. For example, “one dose” of an anti-emetic may be diluted in 100 ml of analgesic fluid from one bottle, which results in a maintenance infusion rate of only 2 ml/h [8]. It is unclear how effective the “low concentration constant infusion” method will be at maintaining therapeutic drug concentrations. Given the short half-life of ondansetron (3–5 h) and its lower affinity, these dilution solutions may be insufficient to prevent nausea/vomiting associated with opioids [9]. Conversely, given that palonosetron exhibits approximately 100-fold higher receptor affinity than ondansetron, palonosetron may retain effective antiemetic action even under the current dosing regimens [10].

Economically, the introduction of the volume-based procurement (VBP) policy in China is transforming its drug market and resulting in deep discounts on palonosetron; overcoming conventional barriers to low prices [11]. Yet, despite this substantial legislative action, comprehensive pharmacoeconomic evaluations are lacking. Further studies are needed to establish whether palonosetron, under its updated pricing, is superior to conventional sufentanil therapy in terms of efficacy and cost-effectiveness. Moreover, literature lacks subgroup analyses of patients and, therefore, it is not possible to understand who will benefit from treatment.

To fill this gap in literature, this study employed a large-sample retrospective cohort design using propensity score matching (PSM) to minimize selection bias. We compared patients receiving sufentanil alone as a control with those receiving sufentanil combined with ondansetron or palonosetron. The main aim of this study was therefore to compare the incremental clinical effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of these interventions, especially considering VBP price changes and the way the “low concentration infusion” is administered. In addition, subgroup analysis was done for individualized treatment plans.

Methods

Study Design and Ethics

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (approval no. KY2025-R216, 11 July 2025). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Participants

Inclusion criteria: patients undergoing elective thoracic, abdominal, and orthopedic surgery with requirement of postoperative PCIA between July 2022 and June 2025 were selected. Exclusion criteria: (1) patients who needed emergency surgery and/or any other operation in the following 30 days, (2) those who spent greater than 1 day in the intensive care unit following surgery, and (3) patients with any missing clinical outcomes or essential cost information.

PCIA Regimens

A standardized protocol for general anesthesia induction and maintenance was administered to all subjects. PCIA pumps were set up using a standardized 100 ml mixture containing sufentanil (1.5–2.0 μg/kg). The PCIA device was programmed with the following parameters: the background infusion rate of 2 ml/h, the bolus dose of 2 ml, and the lockout interval of 10–15 min. Antiemetics were admixed into the 100 ml solution as follows:

Group S: received only sufentanil.

Group S: received only sufentanil.

Group O: administered a combination of sufentanil with 8 mg of ondansetron.

Group O: administered a combination of sufentanil with 8 mg of ondansetron.

Group P: treated with sufentanil paired with 0.25 mg of palonosetron.

Group P: treated with sufentanil paired with 0.25 mg of palonosetron.

Note: the antiemetics were added to the analgesic solution in their original formulation, with each single-dose vial used undiluted to provide continuous low-concentration delivery throughout the infusion.

Outcome Measures

The primary clinical endpoints focused on the incidence and severity of PONV during the first 24 postoperative hours. On the basis of the Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Nausea and Vomiting [12] and real-world clinical data extracted from electronic medical records, we assessed the severity of nausea and emesis using a 5-point ordinal scale: grade 0 (none), grade 1 (mild, no treatment), grade 2 (moderate, responsive to treatment), grade 3 (severe, refractory), and grade 4 (very severe/intractable). For the purposes of logistic regression and subgroup analysis, “severe nausea” was strictly defined as grade ≥ 3. Vomiting was analyzed by overall incidence and severity distribution.

Secondary outcomes encompassed numerical rating scale (NRS) at rest and during movement, other adverse events (e.g., dizziness, respiratory depression), and patient satisfaction scores. Economic metrics were retrieved directly from the hospital billing system. To ensure transparency in our cost-effectiveness analysis, the cost components were explicitly defined as follows: (1) the anesthetic drugs cost includes the cost of anesthetic drugs, preventive antiemetic drugs (palonosetron or ondansetron), and any emergency drugs; (2) the anesthetic consumables cost covers the expenses for disposable medical supplies, mainly including PCIA pumps and anesthetic monitoring and airway management equipment; (3) the anesthesia professional fee refers to the service fees for anesthesia implementation, intraoperative monitoring, and postoperative analgesia; and (4) the total costs represents all direct medical expenses incurred by the patient during the entire hospital stay, including the aforementioned anesthetic-related costs, surgical costs, hospital room accommodation, and routine clinical care costs. All costs are expressed in RMB (CNY).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.2). To minimize selection bias, group O and group P were independently matched 1:1 with group S using a nearest-neighbor propensity score matching (PSM) algorithm (caliper = 0.02) [13]. Covariates included age, gender, Body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA), and surgery type; balance was confirmed by an absolute standardized mean difference (SMD) < 0.1. Given this robust baseline balance, outcomes were assessed using direct comparisons within the PSM cohorts. For the matched cohorts, continuous variables (mean ± SD) were compared using the independent-samples t-test, reporting mean differences (MD) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Categorical variables were analyzed via Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests, reporting odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs. The cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted from a healthcare provider’s perspective because the study’s primary objective is to evaluate the impact of prophylactic antiemetic strategies on hospital resource utilization and budget impact in the context of the VBP policy. Uncertainty around the incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) was assessed via 1000 bootstrap iterations and visualized on a cost-effectiveness plane. Lacking an established willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold for acute postoperative complications, a cost-effectiveness acceptability curve (CEAC) evaluated hypothetical WTP values from 0 to 1000 CNY. Subgroup analyses stratified by sex, age group, and surgery type were also performed to identify beneficiary cohorts, where the level of significance was set as P < 0.05. Given the exploratory nature of the subgroup analyses, we applied the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure to correct for multiple testing. Adjusted P-values were calculated, and an FDR-adjusted P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

After exclusion of 239,386 participants on the basis of eligibility criteria, we identified the final sample for our study (Fig. 1). Patients were distributed into group S (n = 7862), group P (n = 3299), and group O (n = 1231). We observed significant variation across groups for baseline characteristics such as age or gender (P < 0.05). We performed a propensity score match (ratio of 1:1). Two similar groups were formed as follows: group I (S versus P, n = 3276 each) and group II (S versus O, n = 1229 in each arm). After matching, the balance was good, as the standardized mean difference of all variables was less than 0.1 (Table 1; Fig. 2).

Group P versus group S: bootstrap test results showed that the mean ΔE was +0.0058 while the mean ΔC was –1025.98 CNY. Although the confidence ranges covered the null value, most of the points fell within the fourth quadrant of the cost-effectiveness scatter plot (more effective and less costly), which led to palonosetron having a cost-effectiveness ratio of 87.0% probability to be the cost-saving treatment strategy.

Group O versus group S: the results from ondansetron were relatively less favorable; most points were located where greater costs correspond to lower benefits (“quadrant II”). There was an estimated 0.497 chance that it would be dominated, and only a 7.6% probability that it would be the most cost-effective option.

These findings were further confirmed by the cost effectiveness acceptability curve which showed that for any WTP threshold studied, there was always a greater than 80% chance that palonosetron would be considered as cost effective. While ondansetron remained a consistent low-cost-effectiveness possibility.

Subgroup Analysis

A stratified analysis of the data (Fig. 4; Supplementary Tables S1 and S2) revealed differences that were masked when evaluating the entire cohort. While the overall analysis showed no significant improvements in group P, subgroup evaluations indicated targeted clinical benefits. Specifically, compared with group S, group P demonstrated a significantly improved rate of severe nausea in elderly patients, a finding that remained statistically robust even after FDR correction for multiple comparisons (FDR-adjusted P = 0.035). Furthermore, reductions in severe nausea were observed in male patients; although initial unadjusted analyses suggested statistical significance (raw P < 0.05), these specific findings demonstrated marginal significance following FDR correction (FDR-adjusted P = 0.052), indicating a potential but exploratory benefit. In contrast to group P, the subgroup analyses for group O did not demonstrate any statistically significant treatment benefits in any of these groups.

Discussion

The present study provides the first comprehensive evaluation of antiemetic strategies administered within sufentanil-based PCIA under a value-based pricing policy and in the context of low-concentration continuous infusion. Our findings indicate that the relationship between clinical efficacy and cost is nonlinear, with diminishing marginal gains in efficacy as costs increase. Although neither agent demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in overall PONV incidence, palonosetron showed a high probability of cost-effectiveness (87%) in probabilistic analyses. Subgroup analyses further suggested that elderly patients derived a greater benefit, supporting the potential for more individualized prophylactic strategies.

In contrast to our results, previous RCTs have shown positive effects for the use of 5-HT3-antagonists [14, 15], which was observed with relatively minor benefit in our overall cohort size. These differences could be due to different PK profiles for our dosing regimen. Whereas the traditional approach uses rapid bolus injections (4 mg of ondansetron) to rapidly achieve effective drug concentrations [16], our study employed a continuous low-concentration infusion strategy (2 ml/h). Given ondansetron’s short half-life (3–5 h), this minimal continuous infusion likely fails to maintain adequate receptor occupancy during opioid-induced emetogenic peaks [17]. To compensate for ondansetron’s short half-life under this slow-infusion setting, anesthesiologists may consider pre-administering an intravenous loading dose prior to initiating PCIA, though this approach requires further clinical validation. For palonosetron, despite its high pKi value (10.4) and long half-life [7], the low concentrations used in this study may still be subtherapeutic in at-risk individuals—highlighting the potential utility of multimodal therapies, including corticosteroids treatment according to current guidelines [18].

From an economic perspective, our results challenge the conventional economic classification of palonosetron. Previous studies—mostly performed in Western healthcare systems or in China prior to VBP implementation—have typically characterized palonosetron as cost effective but not cost-saving, primarily owing to its high acquisition cost [19]. In contrast, our study suggests that palonosetron may represent a cost-saving strategy, which appear conceptually counterintuitive, given the absence of significant overall clinical superiority. However, within the context of probabilistic sensitivity analysis, this economic dominance is fundamentally driven by the VBP policy rather than substantial clinical gains. VBP has so drastically reduced the acquisition cost of palonosetron that it neutralizes the financial penalty of using a premium agent. Therefore, it functions as a highly affordable, clinically noninferior alternative. This mirrors trends in other therapeutic areas under VBP, where improved affordability facilitates the adoption of premium drugs as standard care [20]. Conversely, ondansetron displayed a high probability of being “dominated” (less effective and more costly), suggesting that the use of ineffective prophylactics represents an inefficient allocation of healthcare resources.

Additional subgroup analyses provided further insights into differential effectiveness among patients. Physiologically, female patients possess intrinsically lower emetic thresholds, potentially driven by hormonal factors. Thus, the continuous slow infusion of 0.25 mg palonosetron may be sub-therapeutic for this high-risk population [21]. In contrast, elderly patients appeared to derive greater benefit. The divergent effect may be attributable to the age-related changes, such as decreases in hepatic blood flow or a decline in cytochrome P450 enzyme activity, which can result in higher drug concentrations among elderly people [22]. Notably, our analysis revealed a statistically significant reduction in the odds of severe hypotension within the palonosetron group. However, it is crucial to interpret this finding with clinical caution. Given the exceedingly low absolute incidence of severe hypotension in both matched cohorts, the absolute risk reduction is minimal. While this phenomenon mechanistically aligns with the blockade of 5-HT3 receptors known to mediate the Bezold–Jarisch reflex [23], its overall clinical significance remains limited. Therefore, this specific reduction in hypotensive events should be considered as an exploratory, mechanistically relevant finding rather than a primary determinant for drug selection in routine practice [24].

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, owing to its retrospective design, certain components of the Apfel score—such as smoking status and prior history of PONV or motion sickness were inconsistently recorded and could not be included in the PSM model. Second, only a single dose of palonosetron (0.25 mg) was evaluated in this study, as higher concentrations were not feasible owing to sample size constraints. Future studies should investigate whether higher cumulative doses could overcome the pharmacokinetic barriers in high-risk female patients. Third, the primary endpoint was restricted to the first 24 postoperative hours. Under ERAS protocols, early mobilization often leads to the premature discontinuation of PCIA before 48 h, resulting in inconsistent longer-term infusion data.

Conclusions

Palonosetron appears to be a cost-effective, and potentially cost-saving, option within low-concentration continuous PCIA regimens under the VBP framework, despite the absence of a significant reduction in overall PONV incidence. Notably, it was associated with a reduction in severe nausea among elderly patients. In contrast, ondansetron did not demonstrate meaningful clinical or economic advantages under the evaluated dosing strategy, suggesting limited utility in this setting.

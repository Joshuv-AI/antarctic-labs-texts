Periostin as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Yao Yuan, Mengping Xu, Tian Qin, Heng Sun, Jianhua Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pathophysiology of intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD), a condition associated with significant morbidity, remains incompletely elucidated. Identification of key proteins that drive IDD progression is crucial for developing effective strategies to mitigate disease advancement. In this study, we discovered that periostin (POSTN) play an important role in IDD through the bioinformatic analysis, and confirmed its presentation in patient nucleus pulposus (NP) tissues and serum samples. Additionally, the serum POSTN level is positively correlated with IDD. To further investigate the functions of POSTN, the nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) were isolated and stimulated with 10 ng/mL tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) for 48 h as the in vitro disease model. And evaluated by qPCR, western blots, immunofluorescence (IF) staining, transmission electron microscopy and transcriptome sequencing. The data demonstrated that POSTN promotes IDD by inhibiting autophagy in NPCs. The results were validated in vivo through the caudal vertebra needle puncture model of Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats, with X-ray, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining, safranin O-fast green staining, and immunohistochemical staining. In summary, this study demonstrated that POSTN has potential to be a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for IDD treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-025-06463-y.Keywords: Intervertebral disc degeneration, Periostin, Nucleus pulposus cells, Autophagy

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is a major global clinical burden, yet its precise pathophysiological mechanisms remain incompletely elucidated [1, 2]. IDD is characterized by progressive structural and functional deterioration of the intervertebral disc (IVD) and adjacent tissues, primarily driven by age-related changes and pathological stimuli. Studies have demonstrated that IDD onset occurs shortly after skeletal maturity, with its prevalence increasing significantly with advancing age. By the age of 50, approximately 90% of individuals show evidence of IDD to some extent [3–6]. In recent years, shifting lifestyles and societal pressures have contributed to an earlier onset of IDD, with a gradually rising incidence. Notably, severe cases of IDD are emerged even in adolescents, posing IDD a significant public health challenge to the entire society [7–9].

IDD arises from multiple reasons, including infection, trauma, and genetic predisposition. However, its precise pathological mechanisms remain unclear [10]. At the cellular level, senescence is recognized as a pivotal driver of IDD, disrupting disc structure and function [11]. This process is mediated by diverse factors, such as inflammatory infiltration, oxidative stress, dysregulated autophagy, pathological mitophagy, mechanical overloading, exogenous stimuli, and epigenetic modifications [12]. Proteomic and transcriptomic studies suggest that IDD is caused by the coordinated action of genes and proteins at multiple levels, resulting in reduced normal IVD cell number, impaired biomechanical ability, and dysregulated extracellular matrix (ECM) metabolism (mainly proteoglycans and type Ⅱ collagen), ultimately leading to changes in IVD structure, narrowing of the intervertebral space, and decreased or even lost biological function [6, 13–16]. Given the complexity of multiple contributing factors, elucidating pathogenesis of IDD remains critical for improving early diagnosis and identifying novel therapeutic targets.

Periostin (POSTN), an 836-amino acid extracellular matrix (ECM) protein encoded by the gene on chromosome 13q, exerts pleiotropic effects on tissue remodeling and disease pathogenesis. POSTN was initially discovered in the periosteum for promoting aggregation and differentiation of osteoblasts as well as their precursor cells [17–19]. In subsequent studies, POSTN was gradually identified as a secreted protein which expressed in various human tissues, and playing different roles in signal transmission and molecule regulation. For instance, it was reported that the expression of POSTN mRNA increases in knee osteoarthritis (OA), indicating that POSTN is related to the occurrence of degenerative diseases [20, 21] conversely, in diabetic arterial calcification rat models, POSTN alleviates the disease progression through autophagy regulation, i.e. preventing the fusion of autophagosomes and lysosomes, and cutting off the autophagic flux of the calcification process [22], highlighting its context-dependent regulatory functions.

Despite its established roles in other pathologies, the involvement of POSTN in IDD remains poorly characterized. Among various pathophysiological factors, the degeneration and programmed cell death of nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) are particularly significant [23, 24]. Currently, several studies have demonstrated that autophagy play a crucial role in cell proliferation, differentiation, senescence, and death [23, 24, 25]. In IDD, autophagy has a “double-edged sword” effect: in the early stage of degeneration, the activation of autophagy slows down the degradation rate of the ECM and effectively reduce cell death, thus serving as a protective factor [26]. However, in the late stage, autophagy promotes the death of NPCs [27–30]. Therefore, whether POSTN can regulate IDD through autophagy is yet to be defined.

In this study, we identified POSTN as a key factor in promoting nucleus pulposus (NP) degeneration. Clinical investigations revealed a significant elevation of POSTN in human NP tissue samples as well as the corresponding serum with the aggravation of IDD. Notably, serum POSTN > 2033 pg/mL is a potential diagnostic threshold with advanced IDD (Pfirrmann grade ≥ 4). In vitro and in vivo experiments indicate that the increase of POSTN contribute to the degeneration of NPCs. Transcriptome sequencing proposed a correlation between POSTN and autophagy, and subsequent validation confirmed that the increase of POSTN inhibit autophagy in rat NPCs. Collectively, we propose a pathogenic mechanism that IDD induced POSTN increase in NPCs inhibits autophagy and exacerbate IDD progression. These data indicate that POSTN has potential to be a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for IDD treatment.

Methods and materials

Isolation and culture of rat NPCs

Rat NP tissue was obtained from the tails of 4-week-old Sprague-Dowley (SD) rats. The NP tissue was digested with collagenase II (0.25%, Proteintech) at 37 °C for 4 h, followed by adding Dulbecco Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM)/F12 medium (Gibco) with 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and Penicillin-Streptomycin antibiotics, and culturing in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator. Rat NPCs were subjected to subsequent experiments when cell confluency reached 80%. The cells were stimulated with 10 ng/mL TNF-α, siPostn, or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) for 48 h before harvest.

Western blotting

The total protein of NPCs was extracted with RIPA lysis buffer and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime) following the manufacture instruction. Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) assay (Thermo Fisher) was adopted to detect the concentrations of the proteins. The samples were then isolated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel (SDS-PAGE) electrophoresis. After blocking the polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA), the primary antibodies were incubated with corresponding membranes at 4 °C overnight: anti-POSTN (1:1000, Abcam), anti-P62 (1:2000, Abcam), anti-LC3B (1:2000, Abcam), anti-collagen II (1:2000, Abcam), anti-β-Actin (1:4000, Abcam). The secondary antibodies used were: anti-rabbit IgG (1:500, Proteintech) and anti-mouse IgG (1:500, Proteintech). Finally, a chemiluminescence detection reagent (Invitrogen) was used to detect the proteins on the membrane.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was gradually extracted from NPCs using the Thermo Fisher extraction kit following the manufacture instruction. Subsequently, complementary DNA (cDNA) was obtained by reverse transcription. The primers used for qPCR: Postn (forward, 5’-TGCAA AAAGA CACAC CTGCA AA-3’; Reverse, 5’-GGCCT TCTCT TGATC GCCTT-3’) (Sangon Biotech). The relative mRNA expression was calculated by 2−ΔΔCT method. All experiments were repeated three times.

Transfection of SiRNA

NPCs were transfected with siPostn or a scramble sequence by transfermate as a transfection reagent (1:1) when the cell confluency reached 50% to 70%. The knockdown efficiency validated by qPCR. The transfection medium was replaced by DMEM/F12 before the next intervention was performed.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The patient blood samples were collected, and the concentration of POSTN was measured using the ELISA kit (BosterBio) and anti-POSTN (BosterBio) antibody following the manufacturer instruction.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

Human and rat IVD sections were obtained by paraffin sectioning. After dewaxing, antigen retrieval was performed trypsin at 37 °C, followed by blocking with 5% BSA, and incubating with the primary and secondary antibodies subsequently: anti-POSTN (1:100, Abcam), anti-collagen II (1:200, Abcam), anti-rabbit IgG (1:500, Proteintech), and anti-mouse IgG (1:500, Proteintech). The color was then developed using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) solution (Gene-Tech) and nuclear staining was performed using hematoxylin (Solarbio). An upright microscope (Zeiss) was used to observe and photograph.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

NPCs were obtained and fixed with Solarbio kit for TEM and kept at 4 °C for 48 h. The samples were then dehydrated in gradient-based ethanol and impregnated with Poly/Bed 812 kit (Polysciences, Warrington, PA). Ultrathin section (70 nm) Sample sections were obtained using a microtome (Leica Ultracut) and then double stained with uranium acetate and lead citrate. Observations were made under the TEM (Leica) using an accelerated voltage of 80 kV.

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde before blocking by 100 µL 5% BSA (containing an appropriate amount of Triton X-100). then the primary antibody dilutions were added: POSTN (1:100), LC3B (1:100), P62 (1: 100). The samples were incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing, TRITC anti-mouse IgG (1:100) and TRITC anti-Rabbit IgG (1:100) were added and incubated at 37 °C for 1.5 h. After mounting, the photos were taken with an inverted fluorescence microscope (Leica).

Hematoxylin-eosin and Safranin O-fast green staining

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E; Solarbio) and safranin O-fast green (Solarbio) staining were implemented following the manufacturer instructions. Images were taken by an upright microscope (Leica).

Establishment and treatment of the IDD rat model

SD rats were anesthetized with sodium pentobarbital (45 mg/g). The model was prepared surgically under sterile conditions, and randomly selected rats were assigned into control, IDD, or IDD + lenti-shPostn groups (n = 6). The tail midline was cut longitudinally and the coccyx (Co6 / 7, Co8 / 9) discs were exposed. In the IDD group, a 21-gauge needle was placed parallel to the endplate and inserted into the disc at 3.0 mm for 30 s; in the IDD + lenti-shPostn group, the lenti-shPostn construct was injected (10 µL) into the disc after needling. The control group did not receive any treatment. muscle incision was sutured with 3 − 0 Moss silk suture and the skin edges with 4 − 0 Moss thread.

X-ray and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

At 4 and 8 weeks after surgery, the caudal vertebrae of the rats were examined by X-ray and MRI. After the completion of X-ray scanning, the vertebrae were analyzed using ImageJ software, and the degree of disc degeneration was inferred indirectly according to disc height index (DHI). All MRI images were determined by Pfirrmann criteria for the degeneration evaluation, and all scoring were completed by three professional physicians independently.

Bioinformatic analysis

High throughput human transcriptome data were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database in NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The inclusion criteria were: (1) both degenerated and control samples were included; (2) sequencing using NPCs or tissue samples; (3) not single-cell RNA sequencing. Finally, gene sets numbered GSE147383, GSE245147, and GSE34095 were included. The differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis was performed using limma package in R, and the threshold of P < 0.05 was set as statistically significant. The |logFC| was set accordingly based on the datasets to guarantee enough DEGs for the following analysis (GSE34095: 0.32; GSE245147: 2.0; GSE147383: 1.5). The diagrams were generated using ggplot, pheatmap. Venn diagram was generated in the jvenn website (https://jvenn.toulouse.inra.fr/app/index.html).

Statistical methods

SPSS 21.0 (IBM) was used to analyze and process the data. The measured data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used to delimit whether there were differences between two groups or multiple groups accordingly. P < 0.05 was set as statistically significant.

Results

POSTN is a key factor in IDD

To investigate the key factors in IDD, we first screened the GEO database. The data in GSE34095 was obtained from the degenerated and non-degenerated discs directly, so we analyzed this dataset in the first place. The heatmap demonstrates clear clustering of degenerated and non-degenerated samples, confirming accurate grouping (Fig. 1A). In the DEG analysis, POSTN came out as the most significantly changed gene in the degenerated discs, with the largest fold change among 134 up-regulated genes (Fig. 1B). To validate this result, GSE245147 and GSE147383 were analyzed subsequently. GSE245147 contains the transcriptome data from non-degenerated and degenerated NPCs, and the samples were well-clustered as well, with very similar expression patterns within groups (Fig. 1C). The DEG analysis by limma package discovered 615 up-regulated and 340 down-regulated genes, with POSTN among the up-regulated genes (Fig. 1D). In GSE147383, although the samples were not as distinguishable as the ones in the other two datasets, the remarkable differences between the two groups could be seen (Fig. 1E). There were 237 up-regulated and 391 down-regulated genes between non-generated and degenerated samples in GSE147383 (Fig. 1F). As expected, POSTN was consistently upregulated across all three datasets (Fig. 1G). Therefore, we hypothesized that POSTN plays a pivotal role in IDD development.

Interestingly, although there were 54 / 340 / 391 down-regulated genes in the three datasets respectively, no universal gene was found in these datasets (Fig. 1H), indicating the variation and complexity of the disc degeneration on the transcriptomic level.

POSTN correlates with disc degeneration severity and serves as a potential diagnostic biomarker

To further elucidate the relationship between POSTN and IDD, degenerated NP samples from 31 patients were collected. Analysis of baseline patient characteristics revealed statistically significant differences only in age (P < 0.05), while gender, body mass index (BMI), and surgical segments showed no significant variations (P > 0.05) (Table 1). MRI of the lumbar discs confirmed the disc degeneration status of these patients with different Pfirrmann grades (Fig. 2A). IHC staining detected a progressive increase of POSTN expression in the degenerated NP tissue along with advancing Pfirrmann grades, and the prominent expression of POSTN could be identified in samples exceeding grade 4 (Fig. 2B, C). ELISA quantification of circulating POSTN levels in patient serum samples (n = 26) was then performed, followed by Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis on advanced degeneration group (grades 4–7) and controls (grades 2–3). The results demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance (area under curve (AUC) = 0.95), identifying 2033 pg/mL as the optimal cutoff value (Jorden index = 0.77) for detecting grade ≥ 4 degeneration (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2D). These results established POSTN as a diagnostic marker for clinical diagnosis of advanced IDD.

POSTN mediates TNF-α-induced NP degeneration by impairing autophagy and ECM synthesis

To investigate whether POSTN promote NPC degeneration, we utilized the inflammation-induced NP degeneration model in vitro. After TNF-α stimulation (10 ng/mL, 48 h), the fluorescence intensity of POSTN was significantly increased, both in the cytoplasm and nucleus (Fig. 3A). Western blot showed up-regulated POSTN and down-regulated collagen Ⅱ and aggrecan in NPCs under TNF-α stimulation, indicating a decline in the ECM synthesis ability of the NPCs (Fig. 3B). To establish causality, the POSTN expression in NPCs was knocked down by small interfering RNAs (siPostn1-4), and the knockdown efficiency was >80% as detected by qPCR (P < 0.05, Fig. 3C). Postn knockdown eliminated TNF-α induced POSTN expression (Fig. 3D), and elevated collagen Ⅱ and aggrecan levels (Fig. 3E), indicating that increased expression of POSTN in NPCs under inflammatory stimulation promotes NPC degeneration. Transcriptomic sequencing on the TNF-α + siPostn group and the TNF-α group found that autophagy regulation, as well as one of the autophagy marker Bcl9l [28], was up-regulated when POSTN was inhibited (P < 0.05, Fig. 3F, G). These findings collectively demonstrate that inflammation-induced POSTN up-regulation drives NPC degeneration, at least partially through autophagy suppression.

Inflammation-induced POSTN suppresses autophagy in NPCs

As previous study revealed the correlation between POSTN and autophagy in NPCs, we validated the regulatory function of POSTN on autophagy in vitro. NPCs were stimulated with TNF-α for 48 h, then Postn was knocked down. IF staining demonstrated that the LC3B protein fluorescence level was increased in the TNF-α + siPostn group significantly, while the P62 fluorescence intensity was decreased (Fig. 4A). Western blot analysis further confirmed these findings (Fig. 4B). Unlike the TNF-α group, the LC3B I/II ratio was elevated in the group of TNF-α + siPostn, while P62 expression was relatively reduced, indicating enhanced autophagy levels in NPCs following POSTN inhibition. TEM was used to observe the subcellular structure of NPCs from the three groups. The data showed a significant increase in the number of autophagosomes in the TNF-α + siPostn group compared to the other two groups, with the TNF-α group exhibiting the least number of autophagosomes (P < 0.05, Fig. 4C, D), indicating that autophagy was re-activated in the TNF-α + siPostn group. These data indicate that the autophagy level is inhibited by the increase of POSTN in NPCs.

Inhibition of POSTN alleviates IDD in vivo

After confirming the functions of POSTN in vitro, the in vivo experiments were performed in the caudal vertebra needle puncture model. SD rats were assigned into three groups randomly: sham group, IDD group, and IDD + shPostn group. The animals were all recovered from the surgery, with no remarkable postoperative complications observed. H&E staining shows that the discs in the IDD + shPostn group exhibited a relatively well-maintained overall structure with a clear outline at 4 weeks, and a continued preservation of the IVD structure, with a partial recovery of the NP at 8 weeks (Fig. 5A). At both 4 weeks and 8 weeks post-surgery, the POSTN expression in the IDD group was the highest (Fig. 5B). At 8 weeks post-surgery, the POSTN level in the IDD + shPostn group was slightly higher than the sham group, but still significantly lower than the IDD group (Fig. 5B). IVD height was assessed using the disc height index (DHI). At 4 weeks post-surgery, DHI values in the group of IDD + shPostn were not significantly different from these in the IDD group (P > 0.05, Fig. 5C), but MRI showed a mild degeneration in the IDD + shPostn group compared with the IDD group (P < 0.05, Fig. 5D). However, at 8 weeks post-surgery, the DHI in the IDD + shPostn group was remarkably higher than the IDD group (P < 0.05, Fig. 5E), as well as the MRI and Pfirrmann grades (P < 0.05, Fig. 5F). Overall, these histological examinations revealed not only decelerated degeneration, but also structural restoration by the inhibition of POSTN expression. These findings consistently support targeting POSTN as a therapeutic spot for IDD.

Discussion

IDD remains inadequately understood regarding its etiology, diagnostic markers, and therapeutic targets [31]. Currently, the diagnosis of disc degeneration primarily depends on imaging techniques such as X-ray and MRI, which evaluate the severity of disc degeneration using parameters such as DHI and Pfirrmann grades [32, 33]. However, these methods depend heavily on the experience and expertise of the diagnosing physicians, leading to significant subjectivity and a non-negligible rate of misdiagnosis. Additionally, imaging examinations are associated with high costs and lengthy processing times.

This study identifies POSTN as a differentially expressed protein and potential biomarker for diagnosis of IDD, addressing a critical knowledge gap in the field. POSTN expression increases with the IDD severity, and a plasma POSTN concentration above 2033 pg/mL indicate a ≥ grade 4 disc degeneration, providing a novel therapeutic strategy for IDD diagnosis. In line with our study, Zhu et al. demonstrated that POSTN promotes disc degeneration by inhibiting the Wnt/β-catenin pathway and inducing apoptosis in NPCs [34]. Wu et al. proposed that the PIEZO1 channel could facilitate the transport of molecules to and from the cell membrane, initiating a self-expanding loop of POSTN and accelerating NPC aging [35], which was supported by our IHC results.

While the functions of POSTN in mechanical stress and exogenous factors have been examined by several studies, little attention has been paid to its relationship with inflammatory mediators in IVDs [10, 35–38]. Our study found that inflammatory stimulation increased POSTN expression, decreased collagen Ⅱ level, and exacerbated NP degeneration. Transcriptomic sequencing revealed that POSTN can inhibit autophagy in NPCs. TEM observations after POSTN knockdown showed enhanced autophagy activity. Consistently, Studies in cancer and alcoholic hepatitis have shown that POSTN regulates cellular functions via autophagy [33, 39–44]. Interestingly, in our study, mitochondria in the TNF-α group exhibited reduced folding, suggesting POSTN involvement in mitochondrial autophagy regulation, an area requiring further exploration [45, 46].

Since IVD is a fibrocartilage-like tissue, the difference between preservation of disc structure and fibrosis could be an interesting topic. Our in vivo experiments showed that POSTN inhibition promoted disc structure preservation, while degenerated discs had a more fibrotic structure, indicating the differences between disc regeneration and fibrosis. Similarly, The study of Zhang et al. indicated that POSTN can promote the fibrosis of local tissues and recruit related fibroblast factors [47]. IHC staining revealed higher POSTN expression in the IDD group, particularly at the annulus fibrosus-nucleus pulposus junction, consistent with POSTN’s role in promoting local tissue fibrosis [42, 47]. However, this hypothesis needs to be verified by future studies.

In summary, our study suggests that POSTN protein is a potential biomarker for disc degeneration diagnosis, and a potential therapeutic target for IDD treatment. Despite limitations, including a relatively small sample size and potential confounding variables, our findings offer valuable insights into the role of POSTN in IDD pathogenesis and underscore the need for larger-scale, longitudinal studies to validate these observations.

Long time dynamics of the Cauchy problem for the predator-prey model with cross-diffusion
Chunhua Jin, Yifu Wang

Abstract
This paper is concerned with a predator-prey model in N N -dimensional spaces ( N = 1 , 2 , 3 N=1,2,3 ), given by { ∂ u ∂ t = Δ u − χ ∇ ⋅ ( u ∇ v ) , ∂ v ∂ t = Δ v + ξ ∇ ⋅ ( v ∇ u ) , \displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\\ &\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u),\end{aligned}\right. which describes random movement of both predator and prey species, as well as the spatial dynamics involving predators pursuing prey and prey attempting to evade predators. It is shown that any global strong solutions of the corresponding Cauchy problem converge to zero in the sense of L p L^{p} -norm for any 1 < p ≤ ∞ 1<p\leq\infty , and also converge to the heat kernel with respect to L p L^{p} -norm for any 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty . In particular, the decay rate thereof is optimal in the sense that it is consistent with that of the heat equation in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} ( N = 2 , 3 N=2,3 ). Undoubtedly, the global existence of solutions appears to be among the most challenging topic in the analysis of this model. Indeed even in the one-dimensional setting, only global weak solutions in a bounded domain have been successfully constructed by far. Nevertheless, to provide a comprehensive understanding of the main results, we append the conclusion on the global existence and asymptotic behavior of strong solutions, although certain smallness conditions on the initial data are required.



Keywords
: Pursuit-evasion, cross diffusion, decay rate, heat kernel.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35K51, 35B45, 92C17.

Introduction

The interaction between different species generates a complex ecosystem, and the predator-prey relation
is one of the fundamental binary interactions, in which predators pursue preys. Since Lotka-Volterra-type prey-predator system was introduced,
various mathematical models have
been proposed to explore a diverse range of complex ecological phenomena, including Allee effects
[
38
]
, group defense
[
42
]
and so on, and especially predator-prey model invoking some mechanism of spatial interaction
is derived to describe complex space-time patterns which reflect nonlocal spatial interactions between the predators and preys
(see
[
36
,
34
,
28
]
for example). Therefore the investigation of the diffusive predator-prey systems becomes an important step
through which one can get a rather deeper understanding of the effects of the spatial dispersal of the predators and preys on
the entire ecosystem.
In the spatial predator-prey relationship,
besides the kinetic dynamics and the random movement of the predators and preys,
the predators and preys may also partially orient
their movement towards or away from higher concentrations of the other species, that is predators move toward the gradient direction of prey distribution (called “prey-taxis”), and/or preys move opposite to the gradient of predator distribution (called “predator-taxis”). To capture this pursuit-escape phenomenon,
Tsyganov et.al
[
39
]
proposed the cross-diffusion system of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+T_{1}(u,v),\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)+T_{2}(u,v),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the unknown functions
u(t,x)
and
v(t,x)
correspond to the predator and prey population densities respectively at position
x
and time
t>0
;

-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)
and
+\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)

stand for the predators are attracted by the preys with coefficient
\chi
and the preys escape from predators with coefficient
\xi
, respectively. The
functions
T_{1}(u,v)
and
T_{2}(u,v)
represent the inter-specific interaction of
u
and
v
.

As pointed in
[
40
]
, (
1.1
) can be regarded as a distant relative of the classic Shigesada-Kawasaki-Teramoto model (
[
37
]
)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta[(a_{1}+a_{2}u+a_{3}v)u]+T_{1}(u,v),\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta[(b_{1}+b_{2}u+b_{3}v)v]+T_{2}(u,v),\end{aligned}\right.

abbreviated as the SKT model. The SKT model and its simplified version have been widely studied in the past few decades, see for example
[
8
,
20
,
26
,
24
,
25
]
or the references therein. However up to now, it seems that the global well-posedness theory of solutions thereof has not been fully established yet.
Unlike the SKT model, the cross-diffusion in (
1.1
) originates from a specific mechanism which characters the attractive and repulsive taxis between the two populations simultaneously. As a remark feature, (
1.1
) possesses
a quite colorful wave-like solution, for example, quasi-soliton pattern is identified in
[
39
]
.
In recent years, the global well-posedness theory of
(
1.1
) has received a considerable attention from researchers.
However, the achievement on system (
1.1
) seems
at a rather rudimentary stage with rather limited results available for the references
[
10
,
40
,
41
,
45
,
46
]
. For instance, for the case
T_{1}=T_{2}=0
or
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
being Lotka-Volterra response terms, Fuest investigated the existence of global classical solutions of homogeneous Neumann boundary value problem of
(
1.1
) on a smooth bounded domain when the initial values are sufficiently close to the constant equilibrium points, as well as the asymptotic behavior thereof in
[
10
]
.
Nevertheless, due to the double tactic mechanism in (
1.1
), there is significant challenge in the rigorous analysis of (
1.1
) with large initial data. Indeed, to the best of our knowledge, the global solvability for the initial boundary value problem for (
1.1
) with large initial data is limited in one-dimensional spatial situation yet, see
[
40
,
41
,
45
]
for example.

In contrast to the sparse results on model (
1.1
) by far, extensive studies have been carried out on the simplified variants thereof.
As an important particular case of (
1.1
) in which the predator-taxis is neglected, the diffusive predator-prey system only with prey-taxis reads as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+T_{1}(u,v),\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+T_{2}(u,v).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Here the most type of ecological inter-specific interaction can be encapsulated by the choice of
T_{1}(u,v)=\gamma uF(v)-uh(u)
,
T_{2}(u,v)=-uF(v)+k(v)
,
F(v)
is the predation rate, the parameter
\gamma>0
denotes the conversion rate from prey to predator,
uh(u)
and
k(v)
are referred to the intra-specific interaction of the predators and preys, respectively.
Up to date there are a few results available to (
1.2
) in the bounded domain (see
[
6
,
13
,
16
,
47
,
43
,
50
]
for example and references cited therein). For instance, the global existence and stability of corresponding solutions to (
1.2
) with no-flux boundary conditions was established in a two-dimensional bounded domain under some specific condition on
F,h
and
k
(
[
16
]
), while the existence of global bounded solutions was obtained in higher dimensions for small
\chi>0
and in three-dimensional setting for suitably small initial value
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, respectively (see
[
22
,
50
]
). In particular, Winkler (
[
43
]
) showed the global existence of weak solutions over a bounded domain with space dimension
N\leq 5
and their eventual smoothness under some weak conditions which includes
k(v)=0
and
h(u)=0
.

On the other hand, prey-taxis model (
1.2
) can also be regarded as the extension of the classical Keller-Segel model (
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),&\quad x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v-v+u,&\quad x\in\Omega,t>0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where
\Omega
is either a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
or the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which
describes a chemotactic feature, the aggregation of some organisms (cellular slime molds) denoted by
u

sensitive to the gradient of a chemical substance denoted by
v
. The possibly most striking feature of this model is
the occurrence of a critical mass phenomenon in two dimensions, namely there exists a threshold value
M_{c}>0
with respect to
initial datum
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
such that all solutions exist globally in time for

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq M_{c}
, while finite time blow-up occurs if
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}>M_{c}
. Here

M_{c}
is either
\frac{8\pi}{\chi}
or
\frac{4\pi}{\chi}
according to
\Omega
is the whole space or the bounded domain

[
15
,
32
,
27
]
. Notice that the approach to dealing with the dynamical behavior of (
1.3
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 3)
is slightly different from that on the bounded domain. For instance, it is well known
that the functional

\mathcal{F}(u,v):=\int u\log u-\chi\int uv+\frac{\chi}{2}\int v^{2}+\frac{\chi}{2}\int|\nabla v|^{2}

satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(u,v)=-\mathcal{D}(u,v):=-\int|\triangle v-v+u|^{2}-\int\left|\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{u}}-\chi\sqrt{u}\nabla v\right|^{2}

(1.4)

along reasonably regular solution curves of (
1.3
), which then allows us for identifying the circumstances under which solutions exist globally and remain bounded. Going beyond that, (
1.4
) can also serve as
a fundamental ingredient in the detection of blow-up phenomena. Indeed, if there are some
C>0
and
\vartheta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u,v)\geq-C\cdot(\mathcal{D}^{\vartheta}(u,v)+1)

(1.5)

throughout some radially symmetric functions
(u,v)
, (
1.4
) is then capable of identifying large
sets of initial data which lead to finite-time blow-up in Neumann problems for (
1.3
) (
[
49
]
).
However, as point out in
[
48
]
, term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u\log u
in
\mathcal{F}(u,v)

may be no longer bounded from below, hence the energy argument above is not accessible for the Cauchy problem.

The investigation of long-time dynamic properties in biological models is crucial for comprehending the fundamental processes of population growth, death, and migration within biological populations. Additionally, it sheds light on how these processes impact the stability and structure of ecosystems, this research also serves as a valuable resource for ecological conservation and management practices.
Regarding the global solution of this kind of model on a bounded domain, the homogeneous Neumann boundary value problem has been thoroughly investigated as we stated above. It is well-known that the asymptotic behavior under zero Neumann
boundary condition is nearly the same as the corresponding ODE systems, that is the solution will tend to some constant steady
state eventually. In such cases, numerous studies have demonstrated, see for example
[
16
,
44
]
or the references therein. In addition, some necessary conditions for pattern formation have also been explored
[
18
,
23
]
. However, the long-time asymptotic behavior of the Cauchy problem for solutions in the whole space differs significantly from that observed on bounded domains.
As an illustrative example, considering the 2D Patlak-Keller-Segel system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\\
&\Delta v+u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

an important observation in this model is its scaling invariance property, meaning that if
(u(x,t),v(x,t))
is a solution, so is

(u_{\lambda}(x,t),v_{\lambda}(x,t))
, where

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{-2}u(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-2}t),\quad v_{\lambda}(x,t)=v(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-2}t).

This is a crucial property that significantly influences its dynamic behavior.
Biler et al.
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
29
,
30
]
have delved into the existence and characteristics of self-similar solutions in the form of
t^{-1}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)
when the initial mass
\alpha
is below the critical threshold in two dimensional space. It has been confirmed that such self-similar solutions also act as global attractors
[
1
,
3
]
, that is

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\|u(x,t)-t^{-1}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}=0

for
p\in[1,\infty]
if
\|u\|_{L^{1}}=\alpha
.
Furthermore, the optimal rate of convergence towards this self-similar profile has been presented
[
5
,
10
]
.
Similarly, for the following parabolic-parabolic Keller-Segel system,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+u,\end{aligned}\right.

it is shown that the solution will finally converge to the self-similar solution of this system for small mass in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
21
,
35
]
. Similar results have also been observed in the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations
[
11
]
, and some reaction-diffusion equations
[
9
,
12
,
19
]
.
While for the following Keller-Segel system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v-v+u,\end{aligned}\right.

it is evident that this equation does not exhibit scaling invariance, hence no self-similar solutions exist. Regarding the long-time asymptotic behavior of this model, Nagai
[
31
]
in 2001 investigated the asymptotic profile of bounded solutions
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and demonstrated that the solution converges to the Gauss kernel in terms of the
L^{\infty}
-norm. Later, in 2003, Nagai, Syukuinn et.al
[
33
]
extended this result to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
, demonstrating convergence in
L^{p}
-norm for
1<p\leq\infty
.
Recently, Carrillo et al.
[
7
]
studied a general aggregation-diffusion equation of the form

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(u\nabla W*u),

and proved that if the interaction potential is bounded and its first and second derivatives decay fast enough at infinity, then the linear diffusion eventually dominates its attractive or repulsive effects, and thereby solutions finally behave like the fundamental solution of the heat equation.

Motivated by the above works, this paper considers the long-time dynamical behavior of strong solutions to the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) in the higher dimensions, which reads as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)&&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

where
Q=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
N=1,2,3
, and the initial value
u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are nonnegative. Our focus is that in the whole space, how far the attractive and repulsive interaction of the species
u
and
v

affect their long dynamical behavior in the situation that their populations dynamics is neglected.

It is observed that the system (
1.6
) adheres to the law of conservation of mass, that is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)dx,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{0}(x)dx

(1.7)

for all
t>0
, whereas our findings reveals that the bounded strong solutions of this system converge to zero in the sense of
L^{p}
-norm with any
1<p\leq\infty
. Apart from that, due to the fact that the
L^{p}
-norm of the heat kernel
G(\cdot,t)
decreases to
0
as
t\to\infty
for any
p>1
, that is

\|G(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-\frac{p|x|^{2}}{4t}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}(4t)^{-\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp(-py^{2})dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\to 0,

the strong solutions hence converges to the heat kernel in
L^{p}
-space for any
p>1
.
Therefore a natural question is whether this solution also converges to the heat kernel in the sense of
L^{1}
-norm.
To this end, the more rigorous analysis is required. Indeed, we not only prove that
for any
p
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the solutions converge to the heat kernel in
L^{p}
-norm sense, but also provide accurate convergence rate estimates and the decay rate, which appears to be optimal in the sense that the rate achieved herein is consistent with that of the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N=2,3
) except for
N=1
.
In addition, as the complement to the above results, it is shown that the global existence of strong solutions to (
1.6
) in the space of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for some small initial data. Here, we define a strong solution as a pair
(u,v)
belonging to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, which not only satisfies the system of equations (
1.6
) in the sense of distributions, but also meets the equations almost everywhere.

In Section 2, we employ a scaling transformation and
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for
e^{t\Delta}
to derive the decay estimate of any strong solution
(u,v)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
of (
1.6
), and thereby obtain the following convergence results by means of a bootstrap argument.

Theorem 1.1

Let
N=2,3
and
(u,v)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
be the global strong solution of (
1.6
).
Then there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\\
&\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\end{aligned}

where
p\in(1,\infty]
.

Moreover, for any
p\in[1,\infty]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|u(\cdot,t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,\\
&\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|v(\cdot,t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{0}(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,\end{aligned}

where
G(x,t)
is the heat kernel, i.e.
G(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\right).

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{m\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|u(\cdot,t)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,

As a necessary complement to the above result, we make sure that the strong solution of (

2
Long-time dynamic behavior and decay rate estimation

In this section, we are devoted to investigating the long-time asymptotic behavior of strong solutions to problem (
1.6
).
To this end, we begin by recalling following results which come from
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
)
For function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\alpha}\|u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

where
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
,
\frac{j}{m}\leq\alpha\leq 1
,

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{N}+(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{N})\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{q}.

Next, we introduce the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on ball
B_{R}
centered at 0 with radius
R\geq 1
. The crucial aspect is to elucidate the relationship between the embedding constants and the ball radius.

Lemma 2.2

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in a bounded domain)
For function
u:B_{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
we have

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C_{1}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(B_{R})}^{\alpha}\|u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+C_{2}R^{\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{s}-j}\|u\|_{L^{s}(B_{R})}.

where
C_{1}>0,C_{2}>0
are independent of
R
,
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
,
\frac{j}{m}\leq\alpha\leq 1
,

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{N}+(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{N})\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{q},

and
s>0
is arbitrary.

Proof.
We use scaling techniques to prove the inequality. Firstly, it is well-known that there exist constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
such that the inequality

\|D^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C_{1}\|D^{m}f\|_{L^{r}(B_{1})}^{\alpha}\|f\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(B_{1})}+C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{s}(B_{1})}

(2.8)

holds for any
f\in W^{m,r}(B_{1})
.
Let

\tilde{u}(y)=u(Ry)=u(x)
for
y\in B_{1}
.
Then

\|D^{j}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=R^{j-\frac{N}{p}}\|D_{j}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})},\quad\|D^{m}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{r}(B_{1})}=R^{m-\frac{N}{r}}\|D_{j}u\|_{L^{r}(B_{R})},\quad\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}=R^{-\frac{N}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})},

which in conjunction with (
2.8
), results in

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C_{1}R^{(m-\frac{N}{r}+\frac{N}{q})\alpha-\frac{N}{q}+\frac{N}{p}-j}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(B_{R})}^{\alpha}\|u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+C_{2}R^{\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{s}-j}\|u\|_{L^{s}(B_{R})}.

From
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{N}+(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{N})\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{q}
, we have

(m-\frac{N}{r}+\frac{N}{q})\alpha-\frac{N}{q}+\frac{N}{p}-j=0,

and hence obtain that

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C_{1}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(B_{R})}^{\alpha}\|u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+C_{2}R^{\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{s}-j}\|u\|_{L^{s}(B_{R})}.

\Box

Lemma 2.3

Let
T\leq\infty
,
b>0
,
\beta>0
and
a(t)
be a nonnegative function locally integrable on
(0,T)
. If

u(t)
is nonnegative and locally integrable on
(0,T)

In order to analyze the long-time behavior of solutions, we employ a scaling transformation on the solution by letting

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{N}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\quad v_{\lambda}(x,t)=v(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

It is observed that if
(u,v)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)\quad\text{in}\ Q,\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v+\xi\lambda^{-N}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)\quad\text{in}\ Q,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{\lambda 0}(x)=\lambda^{N}u_{0}(\lambda x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{\lambda 0}(x)=v_{0}(\lambda x)\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

We reformulate problem (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{\lambda}(x,t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{\lambda 0}-\chi\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\cdot\left(e^{(t-s)\Delta}(u_{\lambda}\nabla v_{\lambda})\right)ds,\\
&v_{\lambda}(x,t)=e^{t\Delta}v_{\lambda 0}+\xi\lambda^{-N}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\cdot\left(e^{(t-s)\Delta}(v_{\lambda}\nabla u_{\lambda})\right)ds,\end{aligned}\right.

As the preparation of the following analysis, we recall the following

Lemma 2.4

\displaystyle\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{q}},

Lemma 2.13

Assume that
f\geq 0
, and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,

(2.55)

where
G(x,t)
is the heat kernel.

Proof.
Given that
f(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}f(y)dy<\varepsilon.

We also note that

|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|=|y\cdot\nabla G(x-\theta y,t)|=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}\left|\frac{x-\theta y}{2\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{|x-\theta y|^{2}}{4t}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}.

Then by a direct calculation, we see that

\displaystyle(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(x,t)\right|=(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(G(x-y,t)-G(x,t))f(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|f(y)dy+(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|f(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}f(y)dy+e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{R}{\sqrt{2t}}\int_{B_{R}}f(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{R}{\sqrt{2t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dy,

which leads to

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{N}{2}}\left\|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

(2.56)

Next, we show that
\left\|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\to 0
. For this purpose, we first prove the following
inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}G(x-sy,t)ds\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|y\nabla G(x-sy,t)\right|dsdx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}\left|\frac{x-sy}{2\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{|x-sy|^{2}}{4t}}\right|dxds

\displaystyle=(\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|xe^{-x^{2}}|dx

\displaystyle=(\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}|\partial B_{1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N}e^{-r^{2}}dr.

It follows from the latter that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(x,t)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|f(y)dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|G(x-y,t)-G(x,t)|dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}f(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}f(y)dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|G(x-y,t)|+|G(x,t)|)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{R}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dy+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}f(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{R}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dy+2\varepsilon.

As a result of this, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\|e^{t\Delta}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)dyG(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}=0.

(2.57)

Therefore according to (
2.56
) and (
2.57
), (
2.55
) can be identified by the fact that

3
Existence of global strong solutions

In Section 2, we have investigated the long-time dynamic behavior of the global strong solution
(u,v)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
of (
1.6
). Hence this section concentrates on the existence of such strong solutions to the Cauchy problem of (
1.6
). For this purpose, we consider the following approximation problem of (
1.6
)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u=-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&v_{t}-\Delta v=\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{\varepsilon 0}(x)\geq 0,v(x,0)=v_{\varepsilon 0}(x)\geq 0,&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.68)

where
Q=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
u_{\varepsilon 0},v_{\varepsilon 0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are the smooth approximations of
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, respectively.
Let

\mathcal{D}=L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}=\{u\in L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),u_{t}\in L^{2}(Q_{T}),xu\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\}.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{D}\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{D},\qquad\mathcal{F}(\tilde{u},\sigma)=u,

where
u
is the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+\varepsilon\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u=-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&v_{t}-\Delta v=\xi\nabla\cdot(v\nabla\tilde{u})&&\text{in}\ Q,\\
&u(x,0)=\sigma u_{\varepsilon 0}(x)\geq 0,v(x,0)=\sigma v_{\varepsilon 0}(x)\geq 0,&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.69)

According to Proposition
A.1
, Proposition
A.2
and Lemma
A.1
in Appendix, we see that for
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{D}
, the problem (
3.69
) admits a unique solution
u\in\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
is well-defined.
By Aubin-Lions Lemma and the compact results in
[
51
]
one also see that
\mathcal{F}
is a compact mapping.
Next, it is easy to see that

\mathcal{F}(\tilde{u},0)=0.

According to the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem,
to show the existence of strong solutions to the problem (
3.68
), it remains only to show that there exists constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma\in[0,1]
such that

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}}\leq C

for any
\mathcal{F}(u,\sigma)=u
.

Lemma 3.1

Assume that
\mathcal{F}(u,\sigma)=u
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)+2\varepsilon\|\Delta u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{*}(\chi^{2}+\xi^{2})\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right),

(3.70)

where constant
C^{*}>1
depends only on the spatial dimension
N
.

Proof.
Multiplying the first equation of (
3.69
) by
u
, integrating the results over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\nabla v\nabla udx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{\chi^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}dx.

Similarly testing the second equation of (
3.69
) by
v
and integrating the corresponding result over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\nabla v\nabla udx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

Combining the above two equalities, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}\right)dx+2\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\chi^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\xi^{2}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\chi^{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}\xi^{2}\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.71)

Furthermore, multiplying the first equation of (
3.69
) by
-\Delta u
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)\Delta udx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\chi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+u^{2}|\Delta v|^{2})dx.

(3.72)

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u)\Delta vdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta v|^{2}dx+\xi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+v^{2}|\Delta u|^{2})dx.

(3.73)

Combining (
3.72
) and (
3.73
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right)dx+2\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\Delta u|^{2}+|\Delta v|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\chi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+u^{2}|\Delta v|^{2})dx+2\xi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+v^{2}|\Delta u|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\chi^{2}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+2\xi^{2}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(\chi^{2}+\xi^{2})(\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{H^{1}}^{2})(\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}).

(3.74)

Moreover, applying
\nabla
to the equations in (
3.69
), and multiplying the resulting equations by
-\nabla\Delta u
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\Delta u|^{2}+|\Delta v|^{2})dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta^{2}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v))\nabla\Delta udx-\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(v\nabla u))\nabla\Delta vdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta v|^{2})dx+C\chi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+u^{2}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2})dx

\displaystyle+C\xi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+v^{2}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta v|^{2})dx

\displaystyle+C(\xi^{2}+\chi^{2})\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta v|^{2})dx+C(\xi^{2}+\chi^{2})\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right).

The latter inequality implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\Delta u|^{2}+|\Delta v|^{2})dx+2\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta^{2}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi^{2}+\chi^{2})\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right).

(3.75)

Now combining the inequalities (
3.71

3.74
) and (
3.75
), we then arrive at
the desired inequality (

Lemma 3.2

Assume that
\mathcal{F}(u,\sigma)=u

Lemma 3.3

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon|\Delta u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle 2C\chi^{2}\sup_{t>0}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varepsilon|\Delta u_{0}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{0}|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}.

Multiplying the second equation of (

&\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial B_{R_{n}}}=0,\left.\frac{\partial\Delta u}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial B_{R_{n}}}=0,\\
&u(x,0)=\sigma u_{n0}(x),\quad x\in B_{R_{n}},&&\end{aligned}\right.

(A.88)

where

By classical theory of linear parabolic equations, the problem (
A.88
) admits a unique classical solution

. Therefore, to show Proposition

, we only need to derive the following a priori estimate

with
C
independent of
n
.

Multiplying the equation of (
A.88
) by
u_{n}
, then integrating the resultant equation over
B_{R_{n}}
, and using Sobolev embedding
inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}u_{n}^{2}\Delta\tilde{v}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi}{2}\|\Delta\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+C\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx,

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|u_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}(\varepsilon|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{n}|^{2})dxdt\leq C,

(A.89)

where
C
is independent of
n
.

Testing (
A.88

-\Delta u_{n}
, and integrating the resultant equation over
B_{R_{n}}
, then the Sobolev embedding
inequality gives us

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\chi\int_{B_{R_{n}}}u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\nabla\Delta u_{n}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{\chi^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{\chi^{2}}{2\varepsilon}(C\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+C\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2})\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon}(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+1),

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|\nabla u_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}(\varepsilon|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}+|\Delta u_{n}|^{2})dxdt\leq C,

(A.90)

where
C
is independent of
n
.
Noticing that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n})\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\Delta\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\|\Delta\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\|\nabla\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}(\|\Delta\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+1),

recalling that
\tilde{v}_{n}\in L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})((0,T);H^{3}(B_{R_{n}}))
, and (
A.90
), we then can see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\cdot(u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n})|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(A.91)

Multiplying the equation in (
A.88
) by
\Delta^{2}u_{n}
, and using (
A.89
), (
A.90

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\Delta^{2}u_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{B_{R_{n}}}\nabla\cdot(u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n})\Delta^{2}u_{n}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\Delta^{2}u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{\chi^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\nabla\cdot(u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n})|^{2}dxdt.

From (
A.91
), we infer that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|\Delta u_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}(\varepsilon|\Delta^{2}u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta u_{n}|^{2})dxdt\leq C,

(A.92)

where
C
is independent of
n
.

Similarly, by multiplying (
A.88
) by
\partial_{t}u_{n}
and using (
A.91
), we also have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R_{n}}}|\partial_{t}u_{n}|^{2}dxdt\leq C,

is independent of

.

Recalling that
\tilde{v}_{n}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{2}(B_{R_{n}}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{3}(B_{R_{n}}))

A.89
), (
A.90

\displaystyle\|\nabla\nabla\cdot(u_{n}\nabla\tilde{v}_{n})\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}(\|u_{n}\|_{H^{3}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\|\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}+\|u_{n}\|_{H^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\|\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{H^{3}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2})

Lemma A.1

Assume that
v\in L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Let
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
be the corresponding strong solution of (
A.87
)
such that

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C,

where
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
. Furthermore, if
(1+|x|^{2})^{r/2}u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some positive constant
r\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}u^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\Delta u|^{2}dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt\leq C_{T},

where
C_{T}
is constant depending on
T
but independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.
For any
R>1
, let
\eta_{R}(x)\in C_{0}^{2}(B_{2R})
be a cut off function on
B_{2R}
such that
0\leq\eta_{R}(x)\leq 1
,
\eta_{R}(x)=1
for
x\in B_{R}
,
|\nabla^{k}\eta_{R}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{R^{k}}
, where
C
is a constant independent of
R
.
Let

\varphi(x)=|\eta_{R}(x)|^{q}(1+|x|^{2})^{r},

where
q
is a positive constant to be determined below,
r\in(0,1)
.
By a direct calculation, we obtain

\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\leq 8r^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{r-2}|x|^{2}\eta_{R}^{q}+2q^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}\eta_{R}^{q-2}|\nabla\eta_{R}|^{2}<8+C\eta_{R}^{q-2}\frac{(1+4R^{2})^{r}}{R^{2}},

(A.96)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta\varphi|}{\varphi}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\eta_{R}^{q-2}|\nabla\eta_{R}|^{2}+\eta_{R}^{q-4}|\nabla\eta_{R}|^{4}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}+\eta_{R}^{q-2}|\Delta\eta_{R}|^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\eta_{R}^{q-2}+\eta_{R}^{q-4}\frac{(1+4R^{2})^{r}}{R^{4}}\right).

(A.97)

|\nabla\varphi|\leq 2r\eta_{R}^{q}(1+|x|^{2})^{r-1}|x|+q\eta_{R}^{q-1}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\nabla\eta_{R}|\leq 2\varphi+q\eta_{R}^{q-1}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\nabla\eta|.

(A.98)

In what follows, we take
q=4
. Then

\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\leq C,\quad\frac{|\Delta\varphi|}{\varphi}\leq C.

(A.99)

Multiplying the first equation of (
A.87
) by
\varphi(x)u
, and integrating it over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and using (
A.98
), (
A.99
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)u^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}\nabla\varphi\nabla u\Delta udx-\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}u\Delta\varphi\Delta udx-\int_{B_{2R}}u\nabla\varphi\nabla udx+\chi\int_{B_{2R}}u\nabla v(\varphi\nabla u+u\nabla\varphi)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi|\Delta u|^{2}dx+4\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{|\Delta\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+2\chi\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+4\chi\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B_{2R}}\eta_{R}^{3}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\nabla\eta|u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\Delta u|^{2}dx+C\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+C\int_{B_{2R}}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+(\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+2\chi\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}})\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+4\int_{B_{2R}}\eta_{R}^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{r}|\nabla\eta|^{2}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx+(3\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+1)\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+C\int_{B_{2R}}|u|^{2}dx+4\frac{(1+|4R|^{2})^{r}}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{2R}}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx+(3\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+1)\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+C.

That is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)u^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq(6\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+2)\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx+C,

(A.100)

where
C
is independent of
R
and
\varepsilon
. Ignoring the last two terms on the left side of the above inequality, a direct calculation yields

\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}(6\chi^{2}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+2)ds\right\}\right\}\leq C\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}(6\chi^{2}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+2)ds\right\}.

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx\leq C,

(A.101)

where
C
is independent of
R
and
\varepsilon
.
Recalling (
A.100

A.101

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<t<T}\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi u^{2}dx\int_{0}^{T}(6\chi^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+2)dt+\int_{B_{2R}}\varphi(x)u_{0}^{2}dx+CT

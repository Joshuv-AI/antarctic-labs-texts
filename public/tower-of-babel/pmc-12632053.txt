Chinese leek-derived extracellular vesicles ameliorate sarcopenia by regulating mitochondrial biogenesis and autophagy via AMPK and maintaining myosin homeostasis
Authors: Weihui Qi, Libin Yang, Yanli Pan, Ke Lv, Lan Zhao, Yujun Zhang, Guoyin Kai, YueLi Sun, Hao Pan, Dong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSarcopenia, a prevalent age-related degenerative disorder, poses significant challenges in geriatric care. Chinese leek demonstrates therapeutic potential against sarcopenia progression, with emerging evidence suggesting its extracellular vesicles (EVs) may mediate these effects. Notably, plant-derived EVs have garnered increasing attention due to their low immunogenicity and capacity for cross-kingdom molecular delivery. This study investigates Chinese leek-derived EVs (CL-EVs) as novel regulators of muscle homeostasis through multi-omics approaches. CL-EVs were isolated via differential ultracentrifugation and characterized using nanoparticle tracking analysis, TEM, and proteomic profiling. Using a dexamethasone (DEX)-induced C2C12 myotube atrophy model, we demonstrated CL-EVs’ cellular internalization and dose-dependent restoration of myotube diameter. CL-EVs significantly alleviated DEX-induced mitochondrial impairment in C2C12 cells, evidenced by restored ATP production, reduced ROS levels, and stabilized mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP). Multi-omics analysis revealed CL-EVs activate the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α axis, confirmed by Western blotting. Proteomic analysis identified selenium-associated proteins in CL-EVs. Expanding on selenocompounds’ known anti-proteolytic effects through Akt modulation, we demonstrate CL-EVs attenuate myotube atrophy through dual mechanisms: inactivation of Akt/FoxO3a/Atrogin-1/MuRF1 proteolytic signaling and activation of mitochondrial biogenesis/mitophagy pathways, collectively improving muscle homeostasis. To investigate gut-muscle axis interactions, 16 S rDNA sequencing and untargeted metabolomic profiling were performed on fecal samples. CL-EVs treatment attenuated DEX-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis and correlated metabolic abnormalities in sarcopenic mice. This study establishes CL-EVs as novel regulators of muscle homeostasis through dual modulation of AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α activation and Akt/FoxO3a/Atrogin-1/MuRF1 inhibition. Introduction

Sarcopenia manifests as an age-associated progressive and systemic musculoskeletal disorder, with potential onset even during early adulthood [1]. Its hallmark features of diminished muscle mass/functionality constitute a primary contributor to physical frailty, locomotor impairment, disability, and compromised life quality, generating significant clinical and socioeconomic burdens [2]. While the pathobiology of sarcopenia remains multifactorial, core etiological drivers encompass dysregulated muscle protein turnover (anabolic-catabolic imbalance) and potential neurogenic components [3]. AWGS epidemiological data reveal a sarcopenia prevalence of 5.5–25.7% across Asian populations, demonstrating a male predominance (males: 5.5–21% vs. females: 4.1–16.3%) [4]. The escalating prevalence of sarcopenia underscores the imperative for developing evidence-based preventive and therapeutic interventions. While exercise therapy demonstrates clinical efficacy in sarcopenia management, its implementation faces limitations due to lack of universal applicability across patient populations [5, 6]. This underscores the critical imperative to develop novel therapeutic approaches targeting sarcopenia pathogenesis.

Chinese leek, a perennial herb in the Allium genus (Liliaceae), functions dually as a dietary vegetable and pharmacologically important herb, exhibiting rapid growth cycles and exceptional regenerative capacity. Historically, Chinese leek and their parts (roots/seeds) have been used medicinally for kidney-tonifying and stomach-warming effects, with earliest records in “Xia Xiao Zheng Zheng Yi” from Xia-Shang-Zhou dynasties. As a millennial traditional herb, “Compendium of Materia Medica” documents its acrid-dispersing and spleen-tonifying properties, while modern “Chinese Pharmacopoeia” records kidney-tonifying, stomach-warming, and qi-activating effects [7]. Studies demonstrate Chinese leek’ bioactivities including anti-inflammatory [8, 9], antioxidant [10], antibacterial [11], and anticancer effects [12]. Chinese leek contains selenium, an essential trace element for human physiology. Low serum selenium levels independently correlate with reduced muscle mass in elderly patients [13]. Selenium deficiency states are pathologically linked to malnutrition-associated myotonic disorders [14]. Selenium repletion facilitates biosynthesis of critical selenoproteins including GPX4 and glutathione, which orchestrate redox equilibrium in skeletal muscle systems [15, 16]. Chinese leek contains flavonoids like quercetin and kaempferol with potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacities. Studies show quercetin prevents muscle atrophy by targeting mitochondria in denervated mouse models [17].

Recent advances in plant biology have enabled the isolation of extracellular vesicle-like nanoparticles (EVNs) from fresh plant exudates across multiple species [18]. EVNs composed of lipids, proteins, nucleic acids and other components can deliver to recipient cells inducing cross-species regulation [19]. Plant-derived EVNs possess advantages including multicomponent composition, low immunogenicity, and high bioavailability [20]. Membrane-encapsulated biomolecules remain stable in harsh gastrointestinal environments, traversing biological barriers to reach target tissues for bioactivity [21]. For instance, oral administration of ginger-derived nanoparticles induces hepatic antioxidant genes (Hmox1, Nqo1, Gclc) expression, ameliorating alcohol-induced liver injury [22]. Similarly, grape-derived VLNs are absorbed by intestinal stem cells and promote proliferation via Wnt/β-catenin signaling [23]. Moreover, plant-derived natural products have been documented for sarcopenia treatment [24]. This rationale drives our hypothesis that Chinese leek-derived EV (CL-EVs) possess anti-sarcopenic potential, while their precise anti-aging mechanisms and molecular targets demand systematic investigation.

Herein, we developed novel plant-derived extracellular vesicles from Chinese leek (CL-EVs) that ameliorate sarcopenia by enhancing mitochondrial biogenesis, augmenting autophagy flux, and suppressing myofibrillar protein degradation. (Scheme 1) CL-EVs isolated via differential centrifugation demonstrated AMPK-mediated activation of mitochondrial biogenesis and autophagy-lysosomal pathways, effectively restoring mitochondrial homeostasis in DEX-induced muscle atrophy models. Concurrent activation of the Akt/FoxO3a axis suppressed ubiquitin-proteasome system activity, attenuating DEX-induced muscle wasting. Oral administration of CL-EVs restored gut microbial composition and modulated sarcopenia-associated metabolic profiles. Integrated in vitro/in vivo investigations combined with multi-omics analyses elucidated CL-EVs’ capacity to enhance muscle regeneration, positioning them as promising anti-sarcopenic therapeutic agents.

Materials and methods

CL-EVs preparation and characterization

We selected fresh local Chinese leek as raw material for leek vesicle preparation. Flat strap-shaped leek leaves were washed 3–4 times with deionized water, air-dried, then homogenized in a blender for 3 min. The juice was centrifuged at 1,000×g (10 min), 2,000×g (20 min), and 10,000×g (60 min) sequentially. Precipitates were discarded and supernatants collected. Subsequent ultracentrifugation at 15,000×g for 1.5 h yielded pellets resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Sequential filtration through 0.44 μm and 0.22 μm filters was performed (Fig. 1 A illustrates the workflow). CL-EVs underwent morphological characterization via transmission electron microscopy (TEM) with quantitative analysis of hydrodynamic diameter and surface charge (Hitachi High-Technologies Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The particle size distribution and zeta potential of CL-EVs were measured using a Zetasizer Nano ZS90 analyzer (Malvern Instruments Ltd., Malvern, Worcestershire, UK). LC/MS (Milford, MA, USA) and proteomics were employed to analyze the composition and identify bioactive components within CL-EVs.

Analysis of physical and biochemical stability of CL-EVs

The stability of CL-EVs under different storage conditions was systematically evaluated. Particle size was monitored weekly over a period of four weeks for two formulations: liquid CL-EVs stored at −20 °C and lyophilized powder stored at room temperature. Measurements were performed by Zetasizer Nano ZS90 analyzer (Malvern Instruments Ltd., Malvern, Worcestershire, UK). Concurrently, biochemical stability was assessed through protein and RNA integrity analyses. Protein composition was examined weekly by SDS-PAGE followed by Coomassie blue staining. RNA stability was evaluated through RNase digestion experiments followed by agarose gel electrophoresis to detect RNA degradation patterns.

Cellular uptake assay

CL-EVs were labeled with CM-Dil (red fluorescence). CM-Dil was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to prepare 1 mM stock solution. The stock solution was diluted 1:100 with PBS to obtain 10 µM working solution. Then, C2C12 cells were incubated with working solution at 37 °C for 5 min followed by 4 °C for 15 min. Cytoskeleton was stained with FITC-phalloidin (green fluorescence, Yesen, China), nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (blue fluorescence, Beyotime, China). These dyes enabled precise tracking of CL-EVs endocytosis. CL-EVs endocytosis was observed using point-scanning confocal microscopy.

Cell culture and treatments

C2C12 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modifed Eagle’s medium (DMEM; Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100IU/mL penicillin and 0.1 mg/mL streptomycin in an incubator at 37 °C in a 5% of CO2 atmosphere. C2C12 cells were inoculated with a diferentiation medium containing DMEM, 2% horse serum, 100IU/mL penicillin and 0.1 mg/mL streptomycin for 5 days to diferentiate into myotube.

Cell viability

Cell viability was detected using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Beyotime, China). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 5 × 10³/well density with 100 µL medium for 24 h. Following adhesion, cells received combinatorial treatments: CL-EVs (0, 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 150, 200 µg/mL) with or without 100 µM DEX for 24 h. The DEX concentration was selected based on previous studies [25]. 10 µL CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. All experimental conditions were performed in six biological replicates, with absorbance measurements (450 nm) recorded using a microplate reader.

Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining

To validate sarcopenic myotubes in the in vitro model, senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining was performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). After culture and differentiation in 6-well plates, DEX-induced myotube atrophy was established, followed by CL-EV treatment. Using SA-β-gal-stained untreated myotubes as controls, myotubes across groups were analyzed. The percentage of SA-β-gal-positive cells was quantified from three independent biological replicates (n = 3), with ≥ 20 myotubes evaluated per experimental group per replicate using light microscopy.

Measurement of diameter of C2C12 cells

We used MyHC staining to stain differentiated C2C12 myotubes, while nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and observed the morphological characteristics of C2C12 cells using an inverted fluorescence microscope. Myotube diameter was measured using Image J. C2C12 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and divided into CON group, DEX group, and three CL-EVs concentration groups (50, 100, 150 µg/ml), with 5 wells per group. After 5 days of differentiation, cells were incubated for 24 h in CON group, DEX group (100 µM DEX), and different CL-EVs concentration groups (100 µM DEX + 50, 100, 150 µg/ml CL-EVs). We measured approximately 50 myotubes/well (250 tubes/group), with each myotube measured three times to obtain the average value.

Measurement of ATP in C2C12 cells

The C2C12 cells were seeded in 6-well plates in a 2 mL medium. Then C2C12 cells were incubated with the intervention for 24 h in the CON group, DEX group (100 µM DEX) and different CL-EVs concentration groups (100 µM DEX + 50, 100, 150 µg/ml CL-EVs) after 5 days of diferentiation. The concentration of ATP in cells was measured by a commercial kit (Beyotime, China).

Measurement of ROS in C2C12 cells

Mitochondrial dysfunction pathologically manifests through ROS accumulation and MMP dissipation, establishing ROS quantification as a reliable surrogate marker for mitochondrial homeostasis evaluation [26]. Intracellular ROS levels were quantified in C2C12 myotubes using 2’,7’-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) fluorescent probe following standardized protocols. C2C12 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were incubated with DCFH-DA (10 µM), a fluorescent probe, at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark. Intracellular ROS levels were detected using inverted fluorescence microscope and flow cytometer.

Measurement of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm)

Using JC-1 mitochondrial membrane potential assay kit, we investigated DEX and CL-EVs effects on MMP. In brief, experimental myotubes were incubated with JC-1 staining solution for 20 min at 37 °C, washed with JC-1 staining buffer, and immersed in medium. Images of positively stained cells were taken using a fluorescence microscope. Normal mitochondria produced red fluorescence and depolarized, or inactive mitochondria produced green fluorescence. ΔΨm was calculated as the ratio of green to red.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Following treatment, C2C12 cells in all experimental groups were PBS-washed and collected via trypsinization. Initially fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde and 1% osmium tetroxide. Followed by post-fixation staining with 2% uranyl acetate. Subsequently dehydrated using graded ethanol and acetone solutions. Samples were resin-embedded and polymerized at 37 °C overnight. Ultrathin sections were subjected to dual staining with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Ultimately, TEM was employed for image acquisition.

pCMV-GFP-LC3B transfection and autophagy monitoring

C2C12 Cells were plated in fluorescent dishes and cultured to 80% confluence. Following the manufacturer’s protocol, cells were transfected with pCMV-GFP-LC3B plasmid (Beyotime, China) using transfection reagent. Following the aforementioned treatments, confocal microscopy was employed for cell observation and image acquisition. Under non-autophagy conditions, GFP-LC3 exhibits a diffuse distribution in the cytoplasm under fluorescence microscopy. During autophagy, GFP-LC3 aggregates on autophagosomal membranes, presenting as punctate or spot-like structures.

Co-localization of mitochondria and lysosomes

C2C12 cells were plated in fluorescent dishes. Following 5 days of differentiation induction, cells were allocated to five experimental groups: CON, DEX, and CL-EV (50, 100, 150 µg/ml). Cells were then incubated with Mito-Tracker Red (mitochondrial marker, Beyotime, China) at 37 °C in the dark for 30 min. Followed by incubation with Lyso-Tracker Green (lysosomal marker, Beyotime, China) at 37 °C in the dark for 60 min. Subsequently stained with Hoechst in the dark for 15 min. Representative images were acquired and analyzed using a confocal laser scanning microscope.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction(qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using the SteadyPure RNA Extraction Kit (AG, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized from the extracted RNA using the Evo M-MLV Reverse Transcription Kit (AG, China). qRT-PCR amplification was conducted using the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS Premix (AG, China) on a real-time PCR system. The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 1 min; 40 cycles of denaturation at 90 °C for 30 s and annealing/extension at 60 °C for 30 s; followed by a melt curve analysis at 95 °C for 15 s and 60 °C for 1 min. GAPDH was used as the internal reference gene. Each sample has 5 duplications, and the results were processed by ΔΔCt method. The sequences of primers corresponding to each gene are shown in Table S1.

Western blotting

Mouse C2C12 myotubes were lysed with cold RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China) to extract protein samples based on the manufacturer’s instructions. After lysis and removal of the centrifuged precipitate, total protein was added with 5 loading buffer (Beyotime, China) and thoroughly denatured by boiling for 10 min. According to their molecular weight, the proteins moved and were separated by SDS-PAGE at a suitable concentration (8–15% resolving gel) and transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane. After blocking, the membranes were incubated with the primary antibody and the corresponding secondary antibody (Beyotime, China). After immersion in an exposure solution, images of the blots were captured via enhanced chemiluminescence detection devices. β-Actin served as the internal control to correct the loading number of samples.

The inhibition of AMPK pathway

We further evaluated the phosphorylation level of AMPKα and the expression of the SIRT1/PGC-1α signaling pathway using Western blot in C2C12 cells treated with the AMPK inhibitor Compound C (10 µM). Additionally, qPCR was performed to examine the effects on key factors associated with muscle growth and atrophy. The experimental groups were designed as follows: CON group, DEX group (100 µM DEX), CL-EV group (100 µM DEX + 150 µg/ml CL-EVs), and Compound C group (10 µM Compound C + 100 µM dexamethasone + 150 µg/ml CL-EVs).

Animal

This study used C57BL/6 mice (6- to 8-week-old), and all mice were treated according to standard guidelines approved by Zhejiang University of Traditional Chinese Medicine’s animal ethics committee.

Murine models of sarcopenia and their treatment

We established a DEX-induced model. DEX is widely used to create mouse models of muscle atrophy, consistent with aging-induced muscle wasting characterized by type II fiber reduction. After 7-day acclimatization, C57/B6 mice were randomly divided into CON (normal + 200 µL PBS), DEX (atrophy + 200 µL PBS), and CL-EVs groups (atrophy + 25/50/75 mg/kg CL-EVs), 10 mice per group. The muscle atrophy model was established via intraperitoneal DEX injection following previous studies [27]. Muscle atrophy model was created by 9-day intraperitoneal DEX injection (25 mg/kg/day) in C57/B6 mice. Maintenance dose (5 mg/kg/day DEX, i.p.) was then administered to sustain atrophy. CL-EVs groups received oral administration of different CL-EVs concentrations with body weight monitoring. Therapeutic groups received 2-week CL-EVs pretreatment followed by concurrent administration during modeling. Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry quantified body composition parameters: bone mineral density (BMD), fat mass/body weight (FM/BW), and lean mass/body weight (LM/BW) ratios in whole-body and hindlimb regions.

Grip strength test

We used a grip strength meter to measure mouse grip strength. After zeroing the meter, mice were placed horizontally on the grip platform. The tail was slowly pulled backward until grip release occurred. Three consecutive trials per mouse were conducted with averaged values calculated.

Gait analysis

Gait analysis was performed using DigGait system (Mouse Specifics, USA) following previous studies [28]. Mice were placed on a moving treadmill at 18 cm/s speed. Camera recordings captured gait parameters including stride length, cycle time, shared support phase percentage, and coefficients of variation (CV) for stride length, width, and swing time.

Open field test

Mice from different groups were placed in a flat open field with clear boundaries. Before formal testing, mice were habituated to the arena through multiple 5–10 min sessions to reduce errors. Infrared devices recorded free movement trajectories and total distance during 5–10 min formal tests.

The cross-sectional area of muscle fibers in mice

Following standard procedures, tissues were collected from the tibialis anterior (TA), quadriceps femoris (Quad), and gastrocnemius (Gast) muscles of each experimental group for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. From representative cross-sectional muscle sections of each mouse, 100 muscle fibers per sample were randomly selected. The cross-sectional area (CSA) of these muscle fibers was quantified using ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism for Windows (Version 10.1.2, GraphPad Software) was used for data analysis and charting. In this study, multiple groups (≥ 3 groups) were compared and analyzed by One-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA) and the pairwise comparisons in multiple groups were analyzed using the Tukey multiple comparison test. P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Isolation, characterization and compositional analysis of CL-EVs

CL-EVs were isolated from fresh Chinese leek using differential centrifugation, followed by purification and comprehensive characterization (Fig. 1 A). TEM imaging demonstrated that CL-EVs possessed homogeneous spherical morphology with an average diameter of approximately 150 nm (Fig. 1B, C). Zeta potential analysis revealed a surface charge of −14 mV for CL-EVs (Fig. 1D). Western blot analysis showed positive signals for both TET8 and PEN1 in both freshly prepared CL-EVs solutions and a lyophilized powder (Fig. S8). Proteomic profiling identified CL-EVs as containing abundant functional proteins, notably oxidation-related regulators, ribosome-associated factors, peroxidase, thiopropanol oxidase, selenocompound metabolism, and alliinlyase (Fig. 1I). Rigorous quality control was implemented through evaluation of inter-sample quantitative correlations at both peptide and protein levels across all specimens (Fig. S1). Lyophilization was performed to assess CL-EVs stability, yielding dark green lyophilized powders (Fig. 1E). Stability assessment demonstrated comparable vesicle sizes between lyophilized powders (room temperature) and solution-stored (−20 °C) CL-EVs over 4 weeks, with transient minor differences observed at week 1 (Fig. 1 F). SDS-PAGE analysis at day 28 confirmed intact protein profiles in CL-EVs (Fig. 1G). RNase treatment caused comparable band attenuation in both lyophilized and solution groups, indicating equivalent RNA stability profiles across CL-EVs formulations (Fig. 1H). Typical TIC chromatograms were acquired under optimized conditions in both positive (POS) and negative (NEG) ionization modes. LC-MS characterization revealed 17 bioactive components in CL-EVs, including in NEG mode: Adenine, Quercetin, Vanillic acid, Diallyl disulfide,2,5-octanedione, p-Coumaric acid, trans-2,4-Decadienal, 15-hydroxylinoleic acid, 16 − 2,4-Di-tert-butylphenol. POS mode analysis identified Adenosine, Phenylalanine, Caffeic Acid, Cianidanol, Docosadienoic acid, 17-Quercetin, 9,11-Octadecadienoic acid, Allyl [2-propenyl] isothiocyanate. Quercetin was uniquely identified through dual-mode detection. The identified compounds were either definitively characterized or tentatively annotated. Comprehensive metabolite profiles of CL-EVs, including compound identities, retention times, ionization modes, adduct formations, molecular formulae, and m/z values, are systematically catalogued in Fig. S2.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that lyophilized CL-EVs maintain long-term structural integrity and compositional stability, exhibiting superior storage stability and batch-to-batch reproducibility. Integrated proteomic and LC-MS analyses further revealed that CL-EVs are enriched with bioactive constituents functionally linked to diverse metabolic pathways.

CL-EVs internalization rescued DEX -induced myotube atrophy through cytoskeletal remodeling

To establish the sarcopenia model, C2C12 myotubes were differentiated and subsequently challenged with 100 µM DEX for 24 h (Fig. 3 A). Cellular uptake was confirmed through confocal microscopy imaging of Dil-labeled CL-EVs internalized by C2C12 cells following 6-hour co-culture (Fig. 3B). We established the in vitro model by differentiating C2C12 into myotubes and stimulating with 100µM DEX for 24 h, then treated with CL-EVs concentrations (0–200 µg/ml) for 24 h. CCK-8 analysis revealed that CL-EVs pretreatment (0–200 µg/ml) exhibited no intrinsic cytotoxicity (Fig. 3C), yet dose-dependently mitigated DEX-induced cytotoxicity (Fig. 3D). Dose-response analysis identified 150 µg/ml as the optimal protective concentration, leading to selection of three dosage gradients (50, 100, and 150 µg/ml) for mechanistic investigations.

Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining, the gold standard for detecting cellular senescence, validated successful establishment of the DEX-induced senescence model (Fig. 3G) [29]. CL-EVs dose-dependently alleviated DEX-induced senescence phenotypes across tested concentrations (Fig. 3E). Myosin heavy chain (MyHC) immunostaining further revealed that CL-EVs treatment significantly preserved myotube diameter compared to DEX-treated controls (Fig. 3 F, H). Myogenin (MyoG), a key myogenic regulatory factor (MRF), orchestrates the initiation phase of myoblast fusion [30]. MyoD regulates myoblast proliferation and differentiation through the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α axis, critically governing myogenic lineage specification [31]. MuRF1, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, mediates proteasomal degradation of contractile proteins during muscle wasting [32]. Similarly, Atrogin-1 has been reported to inhibit Akt-dependent cardiac hypertrophy in mice through ubiquitin-dependent FoxO protein activation [33]. PCR analysis of these four key proteins showed expected trends (Fig. 3I): DEX group significantly suppressed MyOG/MyOD expression while increasing MuRF1/Atrogin-1, which were reversed by CL-EVs treatment. These results indicate CL-EVs could inhibit DEX-induced myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells.

AMPK signaling dysregulation contributed to age-related sarcopenia pathogenesis

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis followed standardized pipelines (Fig. 4 A) involving data integration, clustering, and cell type annotation, with batch effect correction implemented via Harmony algorithm. Rigorous quality control filtering (Fig. 4B) yielded 79,120 high-quality cells for subsequent analyses. Unsupervised clustering revealed 10 transcriptionally distinct cell clusters (Fig. 4D) annotated via established marker genes (Fig. 4C), encompassing myofibers, fibroblasts, basal cells, endothelial cells, and macrophages. Myofiber sub-clustering (6,362 cells, Fig. 4E) enabled age-stratified comparative analysis. Differential expression analysis (genes detectable in ≥ 25% of cells) revealed 5,514 DEGs between age groups. Enrichment analysis of significant DEGs using GO and KEGG databases showed autophagy, cellular senescence, and AMPK signaling pathways in KEGG, with mitochondrial organization and autophagy regulation in GO (Fig. 4F-G). These mechanistic insights implicate AMPK signaling as a pivotal regulator in sarcopenia pathogenesis, mediated through mitochondrial homeostasis and autophagic processes, thereby informing CL-EVs-based therapeutic strategies for mitigating age-related muscle decline.

CL-EV attenuates DEX-induced sarcopenia signatures in C2C12 Cells: transcriptomic analysis highlights role of AMPK pathway activation

To evaluate the effects of DEX and CL-EV on transcriptional alterations in C2C12 cells, we performed high-throughput RNA sequencing to systematically profile intracellular mRNA dynamics. Volcano plots demonstrated significant differential gene expression across groups. Specifically, 2,423 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (|log2FC| >1, P < 0.05) were identified between the DEX and control (Con) groups, comprising 1,217 upregulated and 1,206 downregulated genes. In contrast, 198 DEGs were detected between the CL-EV and DEX groups, with 103 upregulated and 95 downregulated genes (Fig. S3A). Notably, a Venn diagram revealed 48 overlapping DEGs between these comparisons (Fig. S3B). Furthermore, hierarchical clustering heatmaps confirmed distinct transcriptomic profiles across groups, with clustering patterns tightly aligned to experimental conditions and no detectable batch effects (Fig. S3C, D). To functionally annotate these DEGs, we conducted KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on upregulated and downregulated gene sets separately. As shown in Fig. S3E, statistically significant pathways (ranked by ascending p-value) are displayed. In the DEX vs. Con comparison, upregulated DEGs were predominantly enriched in ribosome biogenesis in eukaryotes, amino acid metabolism, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Conversely, downregulated DEGs were associated with HIF-1 signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, and mTOR signaling pathway. In the CL-EV vs. DEX comparison, upregulated genes were enriched in autophagy, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, and FoxO signaling pathway, whereas downregulated genes clustered within calcium signaling pathway and TNF signaling pathway. To further explore pathway-level perturbations, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was employed. Strikingly, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) was significantly upregulated in the DEX-induced model group (p < 0.01) but downregulated in the CL-EV treatment group (p < 0.01). Although the AMPK signaling pathway showed no statistically significant changes (P > 0.05), its downregulation in the model group and subsequent upregulation in the CL-EV group collectively suggest that AMPK pathway activation may contribute to CL-EV-mediated mitigation of sarcopenia-related phenotypes (Fig. S3F).

CL-EVs rescued mitochondrial dysfunction of C2C12 cells induced by DEX

Mitochondrial dysfunction is a characteristic of sarcopenic muscle cells, often accompanied by ROS accumulation and loss of mitochondrial membrane potential [34]. As the energy currency of life, ATP synthesis via mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation constitutes the cornerstone of cellular bioenergetics. Therefore, mitochondrial ATP levels reflect functional status, and mitochondrial dysfunction is a key factor in neurodegenerative diseases, metabolic disorders, and sarcopenia [35]. ATP assay showed significantly reduced mitochondrial ATP in DEX group, which was attenuated by CL-EVs treatment (Fig. 5B). Impaired electron transport chain flux increases ROS generation, while compromised antioxidant defenses exacerbate oxidative stress, damaging mitochondrial structure and function [26]. DCFH-DA assay revealed significant ROS accumulation in DEX group, which was mitigated by CL-EVs (Fig. 5E, F). Flow cytometric quantification (Fig. 5 C) confirmed elevated mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) and ROS-positive cells in DEX-treated myotubes, both normalized by CL-EVs intervention (Fig. 5D). We also measured MDA (oxidative marker) and SOD (antioxidant enzyme) levels (Fig. 5G, H) [36, 37]. CL-EVs treatment reduced MDA and increased SOD, alleviating oxidative damage.

CL-EVs augmented mitochondrial bioenergetics via AMPK activation and Akt targeting to ameliorate DEX-induced muscle atrophy

To elucidate the molecular basis of CL-EVs’ anti-atrophic activity, we focused on key regulatory pathways governing muscle homeostasis. AMPK is a central regulator of metabolic homeostasis, particularly controlling muscle fiber growth and size in skeletal myocytes [41]. Notably, LC-MS analysis revealed high quercetin content in CL-EVs. Previous studies show quercetin activates SIRT1/PGC1α pathway to enhance mitochondrial biogenesis. The AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α axis is a canonical pathway mitigating mitochondrial dysfunction in sarcopenia. Immunoblotting analysis of the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α axis demonstrated CL-EVs’ pathway-modulating effects: CL-EVs treatment rescued the DEX-induced suppression of p-AMPK/AMPK ratio, SIRT1 expression, and PGC-1α levels (Fig. 6 C). These findings indicate CL-EVs activate the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α axis to drive mitochondrial biogenesis, thereby restoring bioenergetic capacity in atrophic myotubes. To further validate whether the activation of the AMPK signaling pathway is essential for the protective effects of CL-EVs against muscle atrophy, we employed the AMPK-specific inhibitor Compound C for verification. As shown in Fig S9A, compared with the CL-EVs treatment group (DEX + CL-EVs), co-treatment with Compound C (10 µM) significantly inhibited the CL-EVs-induced phosphorylation of AMPK (p-AMPK/AMPK), and subsequently downregulated the protein expression levels of its downstream signaling molecules SIRT1 and PGC1α. Consistent with this, qRT-PCR results (Fig S9B) demonstrated that inhibition of AMPK activity also reversed the upregulatory effects of CL-EVs on the mRNA expression of muscle growth-related factors (MyoG, MyoD), and partially restored the expression of muscle atrophy-related factors (MuRF1, Atrogin-1). These results indicate that AMPK activation is indispensable for the protective effects of CL-EVs, thereby confirming the central role of the AMPK signaling pathway in mediating the alleviation of dexamethasone-induced myotube atrophy by CL-EVs.

Mitochondrial stress (ATP depletion, ROS accumulation) under dysfunction activates mitophagy. AMPK is a key initiator of autophagy in skeletal muscle [42]. Mitophagic flux was assessed via key regulators: Beclin1 (mitophagy initiator), LC3-II/I ratio (autophagosome formation), and p62 (selective autophagy substrate) (Fig. 6D). DEX impaired autophagic flux, evidenced by reduced LC3-II/I conversion, suppressed Beclin1, and accumulated p62. CL-EVs restored autophagic activity in a dose-dependent manner. LC3 (Fig S 4B, D) and P62 (Fig S 4 C, E) immunofluorescence corroborated these findings. TEM was further employed to directly assess autophagosomes in cellular samples across each group. The results showed that the DEX-treated group had lower autophagosome abundance, while CL-EV treatment significantly increased autophagosome abundance (Fig. 6 F). CL-EV-treated C2C12 cells demonstrated significantly increased punctate GFP-LC3 distribution (Fig. 6G, I), further confirming CL-EV-mediated autophagy induction. Concurrently, increased mitochondrial-lysosomal colocalization events following CL-EV treatment suggested enhanced mitophagy (Fig. 6H, J). Selenium-associated proteins in CL-EVs proteome, combined with elemental selenium in Chinese leek, suggest selenium-mediated mechanisms. Selenium supplementation targets Akt/FoxO3a/Atrogin-1 to inhibit proteolysis. CL-EVs activated Akt signaling (Fig. 6B). CL-EVs inhibited DEX-induced activation of Akt/FoxO3a/Atrogin-1 proteolytic pathway. These findings demonstrate CL-EVs alleviates muscle atrophy via AMPK-mediated mitophagy, mitochondrial biogenesis, and AKT-dependent proteolysis inhibition.

CL-EVs attenuated DEX-induced skeletal muscle atrophy and promotes skeletal muscle mass and function

Correspondingly, we validated CL-EVs’ therapeutic effects on sarcopenia in animal models, with experimental details shown in Fig. 7A. Sarcopenia modeling and intervention protocols are shown in Fig. 7B: 25 mg/kg/d DEX intraperitoneal injection for 9 days to establish model, then 5 mg/kg/d for maintenance. Multi-modal assessment included muscle mass quantification, functional tests, and kinematic analysis to determine therapeutic outcomes. Body weight was monitored throughout, with no significant intergroup differences during weeks 1–2. DEX group showed significant weight loss post-modeling, attenuated by CL-EVs at 25/50/75 mg/kg (Fig. 7 C). Grip strength analysis demonstrated dose-dependent restoration of muscle function, with 75 mg/kg significantly improved strength, though not to CON levels (Fig. 7D).

CL-EVs administration affected the gut microbiota composition and metabolism

The gut microbiota is widely recognized to influence not only intestinal health but also systemic organ functions. Accordingly, fecal samples from control (CON), DEX, and CL-EVs-treated groups were subjected to 16 S rDNA sequencing analysis. Gut microbiome composition was systematically characterized across groups, with taxonomic classification rigorously validated. α-Diversity indices (Chao1, Shannon, and Simpson) were employed to assess microbial community richness and diversity (Fig. 9E). Quantitative analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in α-diversity metrics between CL-EVs and DEX groups. Furthermore, PCoA utilizing Weighted Unifrac distance demonstrated distinct microbial community structures (Fig. 9 F). At the phylum level, Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes constituted the predominant taxa, where the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio exhibited significant depletion in DEX-treated mice but notable restoration following CL-EVs intervention (Fig. 9G). Additionally, at the genus level, it was found that Muribaculum was significantly reduced after CL-EV treatment (Fig. 9H). Relevant studies indicate that Muribaculum can promote the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines [47]. These findings suggest that CL-EVs treatment mitigates DEX-induced sarcopenia through modulation of gut microbial ecology.

To delineate the metabolic impact of CL-EVs, untargeted metabolomics was employed to characterize gut microbial metabolic alterations. Unsupervised principal component analysis (PCA) and supervised orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) revealed distinct metabolic clustering patterns across experimental groups (Fig. 9I−J). Metabolomics analysis revealed that DEX significantly altered metabolite profiles. Among 481 metabolites down-regulated in DEX vs. Control groups, CL-EV treatment selectively up-regulated 78 metabolites toward control levels, indicating reversal of DEX-induced suppression (Fig. 9 K). Conversely, among 172 metabolites up-regulated by DEX, CL-EVs significantly down-regulated 43 metabolites toward control levels, counteracting DEX-induced elevations (Fig. 9L). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DAMs (top 20 pathways ranked by ascending P-values, Fig. 9M) highlighted significant perturbations in histidine metabolism (most pronounced), bile secretion, primary bile acid biosynthesis, GABAergic synapse, porphyrin metabolism, and β-alanine metabolism. Hierarchical clustering of 38 pathway-associated metabolites (Fig. 9 N) demonstrated CL-EVs-mediated metabolic remodeling in gut microbiota. These metabolic reprogramming effects induced by CL-EVs in DEX-challenged mice suggest a potential interplay between CL-EVs-mediated microbiota modulation and metabolic homeostasis.

Discussion and conclusion

Sarcopenia is defined as a systemic, age-related skeletal muscle disorder characterized by accelerated loss of muscle mass and function. It increases the risk of adverse outcomes such as falls, fractures, disability, and even mortality [48]. The escalating global burden of sarcopenia underscores the urgent need for developing targeted therapeutics with improved efficacy and safety profiles. Identifying effective therapeutic targets is crucial for developing novel sarcopenia treatment strategies. Our study identifies Chinese leek-derived extracellular vesicles (CL-EVs) as novel biological agents that effectively counteract DEX-induced myopathy both in vitro and in vivo, positioning them as promising candidates for managing muscle wasting disorders.

Chinese leek contain various highly bioactive nutrients including flavonoid aglycones [49], sulfur-containing nutrients [50], fructose, alliaceous compounds, and amino acids [51]. Notably, Chinese leek are natural selenium sources - this micronutrient plays critical roles in selenoprotein synthesis and redox regulation. Selenium deficiency can lead to malnutrition-related muscle stiffness. Emerging evidence indicates selenium activates Akt signaling to suppress proteasomal degradation pathways, thereby preserving muscle mass. Furthermore, Chinese leek contain flavonoids like quercetin and kaempferol with potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacities. Previous research demonstrated quercetin prevents muscle atrophy by targeting mitochondria in denervated mouse models [17]. In this study, we isolated exosome-like nanovesicles (CL-EVs) from fresh Chinese leek leaves, characterized their properties, and confirmed structural and compositional stability under various conditions, validating the reproducibility of our preparation method. We also found CL-EVs shares similar components with Chinese leek. Building on previous plant-derived nanovesicle studies [52], we demonstrated CL-EVs can be internalized by C2C12 myoblasts without significant cytotoxicity. Dose-response analysis identified 50–150 µg/ml CL-EVs as the optimal therapeutic window, significantly rescuing DEX-compromised myoblast viability (vs. DEX group). Notably, CL-EVs predominantly accumulated perinuclearly, suggesting potential dual roles as both therapeutic agents for sarcopenia and drug delivery vehicles for broader applications.

DEX-induced sarcopenia models have been widely reported and are well-established [53–55]. We induced C2C12 myotubes with 100µM DEX and confirmed model establishment via β-galactosidase assay. Additionally, we analyzed morphological changes in C2C12 myotubes. CL-EVs treatment dose-dependently enhanced myosin heavy chain (MyHC) expression (vs. DEX) and increased myotube diameter, indicating structural restoration. Transcriptional dynamics of myogenic regulatory factors (MyoD, MyoG) and atrophy markers (Atrogin-1, MuRF1) were quantified to elucidate CL-EVs’ regulatory effects on muscle protein turnover [56]. DEX group showed suppressed MyoD/MyoG expression but elevated Atrogin-1/MuRF1 levels. Interestingly, CL-EVs reversed these changes dose-dependently, demonstrating restorative effects on atrophic myotubes. Muscle mass and functional improvements were also validated in mouse models after CL-EVs administration.

To explore CL-EVs’ anti-sarcopenia mechanisms, we analyzed public single-cell data from Human Muscle Ageing Cell Atlas (https://db.cngb.org/cdcp/hlma/) on Cell-omics Data Coordinate Platform. Single-cell analysis revealed enrichment in mitochondrial metabolism, autophagy, cellular senescence, and AMPK pathways, leading us to investigate AMPK and mitochondrial mechanisms.

Mitochondrial function plays a crucial role in maintaining muscle health and function. Beyond ATP generation, mitochondria orchestrate critical processes: cellular stress responses, metabolic flux regulation, redox homeostasis, and apoptosis signaling cascades. Mitochondrial dysfunction is closely associated with various diseases, particularly sarcopenia characterized by loss of muscle mass and function [57]. Mitochondrial dysregulation promotes ROS production, exacerbating oxidative stress during aging. Moreover, the delicate balance between mitochondrial fission and fusion is critical for maintaining normal mitochondrial dynamics [58]. CL-EVs administration significantly attenuated oxidative stress and preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, indicating functional restoration. In DEX-treated C2C12 myotubes, Drp1 mRNA was upregulated while Mfn1 expression was suppressed, leading to excessive fission and mitochondrial fragmentation. CL-EVs intervention reversed these alterations, demonstrating its potent effect on maintaining mitochondrial dynamic homeostasis.

Mitochondrial biogenesis emerges as a critical determinant in sarcopenia pathogenesis through its regulation of muscle energetics. In skeletal muscle, the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α signaling pathway is believed to alleviate mitochondrial dysfunction and improve sarcopenia [59]. By activating PGC1α in the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α pathway, SIRT1 enhances mitochondrial biogenesis in skeletal muscle, regulates mitochondrial metabolism, reduces oxidative damage, and may have therapeutic potential for preventing atrophy and treating sarcopenia. AMPK is a central regulator of metabolic homeostasis that controls myofiber growth in skeletal muscle cells. SIRT1 is deeply involved in skeletal muscle remodeling: directly deacetylating/activating PGC1α to regulate biogenesis [60], modulating FoxOs activity to control muscle mass/function loss [61], and influencing MyoD/myogenic factors to inhibit myogenesis [62]. Therefore, AMPK activation represents a potential therapeutic approach for preventing/rescuing muscle atrophy. Meanwhile, quercetin has been reported to upregulate mitochondrial biogenesis markers (PGC1α, cytochrome c, SIRT1) in skeletal muscle [63]. LC-MS quantification revealed substantial quercetin enrichment in CL-EVs preparations, aligning with parental Chinese leek phytochemistry. Thus, we focused on AMPK and its downstream protein expression in this study. Our results demonstrate CL-EVs activates the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α axis to enhance mitochondrial biogenesis, regulate energy metabolism, and reduce oxidative stress, thereby alleviating muscle atrophy.

Ampelopsin has been reported to activate autophagy by stimulating AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α signaling during muscle aging [64]. Autophagy clears abnormal protein aggregates and pathogens in skeletal muscle, maintaining myofiber homeostasis, while its inhibition leads to myocyte degeneration. Dysregulated autophagic flux is a key pathogenesis mechanism in sarcopenia [65], making autophagy balance crucial for muscle maintenance. The SIRT1-AMPK axis serves as a master regulator of autophagic initiation. Resveratrol activates autophagy via SIRT1/AMPK signaling, elevating Beclin-1/LC3 expression while suppressing apoptotic proteins [66]. AMPK activators like AICAR reportedly improve dystrophic phenotypes in diaphragmatic atrophy by activating mitophagy [67]. Furthermore, AMPK directly phosphorylates and activates ULK1 and downstream molecules including ATG9 and Beclin-1 [68]. Luo et al. found SIRT1 promotes cardioprotective autophagy via AMPK activation, directly deacetylating essential autophagy proteins including LC3 [69]. LC3 lipidation (phosphatidylethanolamine conjugation) serves as the definitive biochemical marker for autophagosome formation. In conclusion, our data suggest CL-EVs alleviates muscle atrophy by AMPK-mediated autophagy induction.

The decline in muscle mass and function observed in sarcopenia primarily results from myofiber quality loss, which is caused by proteolysis in atrophic myocytes. Atrophic signaling involves PI3K/Akt pathway suppression, enabling FoxO nuclear translocation via coordinated dephosphorylation and acetylation, thereby elevating MuRF1 and atrogin-1 expression to drive ubiquitin-proteasome-mediated proteolysis [70, 71]. Selenium deficiency accelerates protein degradation. Selenium crucially activates PI3K/Akt signaling, potentially via N-terminal cysteine residues critical for Akt phosphorylation [72, 73]. In this study, CL-EVs treatment reversed DEX-induced FoxO3a dephosphorylation and Atrogin-1 upregulation, effectively alleviating myotube atrophy, preventing mass loss, and stimulating Akt phosphorylation. These findings suggest CL-EVs ameliorates sarcopenia by enhancing Akt phosphorylation to inhibit FoxO3a-mediated proteolysis.

The gut-muscle axis is believed to be a micronutrient and metabolites derived from the gut microbiota can modulate muscle function and mass [74]. Recent studies reveal significant correlations between gut microbiota and sarcopenia, with microbiota playing crucial roles in maintaining skeletal muscle homeostasis. Gut microbiota reportedly influence muscle protein synthesis by modulating host inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, nutrient absorption, and hormone levels [75]. Aging impairs gut function and induces microbial composition alterations. Altered gut microbiota composition correlates with modified host metabolic phenotypes, significantly impacting age-related diseases. Our data show CL-EVs administration ameliorates DEX-induced sarcopenia by modulating gut microbiota composition. CL-EVs normalized the dysbiotic Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio while enriching SCFA-producer Roseburia and suppressing Parabacteroides. The Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes (F/B) ratio has emerged as a diagnostic biomarker for sarcopenic progression [76]. The F/B ratio is crucial for intestinal homeostasis and often decreased in inflammatory diseases. Studies report increased F/B ratios in metabolically healthy Asian midlife populations [77]. Furthermore, Muribaculum promotes pro-inflammatory cytokine production, whereas CL-EV treatment significantly reduces its abundance. Our results suggest CL-EVs’ microbial modulation partially contributes to DEX-induced sarcopenia improvement.

Among identified metabolites, histidine pathway metabolites (L-histidine, carnosine) decreased in DEX-induced sarcopenia but improved with CL-EVs. Carnosine, predominantly in skeletal muscle, is a β-alanine/histidine dipeptide. It regulates pH homeostasis, enhances calcium sensitivity, and provides antioxidant (ROS scavenging/metal chelation) effects. Carnosine decreases in sarcopenia [78, 79]. As a muscle pH buffer, carnosine delays systemic acidosis (detrimental to performance), allowing higher lactate accumulation during exercise. Sarcopenia reduces carnosine content, limiting pH buffering and accelerating acidosis. Histidine, an essential amino acid, is crucial for disease treatment, tissue protection, and muscle performance enhancement [80]. Therefore, elevating muscle carnosine/histidine may slow sarcopenia progression, with CL-EVs improving microbial metabolism to alleviate DEX-induced sarcopenia.

Despite these promising findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, due to the lack of universally established markers for plant-derived extracellular vesicles, TET8 and PEN1 were employed as putative indicators for identifying CL-EVs; their specificity and general applicability, however, require further validation. Second, although LC-MS and proteomic analyses were performed, the precise bioactive constituents within CL-EVs responsible for the anti-sarcopenia effects have not been fully elucidated. Further fractionation and functional studies are necessary to identify the active molecules. Additionally, while the dexamethasone-induced muscle atrophy model offers high controllability and reproducibility, it does not fully recapitulate the natural progression of age-related sarcopenia in humans. Validation in aged animal models would improve the physiological relevance of the findings. Finally, although both cellular uptake of CL-EVs and gut-mediated effects were demonstrated, the underlying mechanisms and relative contributions of these pathways remain incompletely understood. Further investigations involving microbiota transplantation would help clarify the role of the gut–muscle axis.

In conclusion, Our study found CL-EVs alleviates muscle atrophy and enhances muscle strength. We demonstrated CL-EVs activates AMPK/SIRT1/PGC1α pathway to enhance mitochondrial biogenesis and mitophagy. Additionally, CL-EVs activates Akt/FoxO3a/Atrogin-1 pathway to suppress proteolysis. Fecal 16 S rDNA sequencing and untargeted metabolomics revealed CL-EVs modulates gut microbiota and metabolites in DEX-induced sarcopenic mice, ameliorating disease progression. These findings position CL-EVs as a novel phyto-nanotherapeutic agent that coordinately targets mitochondrial quality control (AMPK/SIRT1 axis) and proteostasis (Akt/FoxO3a pathway), with translational potential for combating age-related muscle wasting.

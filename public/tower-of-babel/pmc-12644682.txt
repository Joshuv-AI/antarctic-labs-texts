Keyphrase extraction by the use of glove and ResNeXt optimized by enhanced human evolutionary optimization (EHEO) algorithm
Authors: Chao Pan, Yanshu Liu, Mohammad Sarabi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Keyphrase extraction (KPE) is an essential process in natural language processing, facilitating the document content summarization for diverse uses like search engine optimization and information retrieval. Nevertheless, manual extraction can be labor-intensive, and automated techniques often face challenges in understanding contextual relationships within the text. This research introduces an innovative method that employs the ResNeXt neural network architecture, optimized by an enhanced human evolutionary optimization algorithm, and integrated with GloVe-100 word embeddings. The model was evaluated utilizing the KP20k dataset, a commonly utilized resource that includes approximately 500,000 scientific papers labeled with keyphrases, the Inspec database that comprises 2,000 English abstracts, and the SemEval-2010 benchmark dataset that comprises 244 research papers. The proposed model was evaluated against other advanced approaches, such as Convolutional Neural Network, k-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine, BERT, Convolutional Neural Network-BERT, and Gated Recurrent Unit, and outperformed them with recall, precision, and F1-score values of 98.81%, 98.67%, and 98.74%, respectively. It could achieve 96.54%, 96.32%, and 96.43% in precision, recall, and F1-score on the Inspect dataset, respectively. Moreover, the proposed model indicated remarkable performance over the SemEval-2010 dataset by achieving 97.32% precision, 97.81% recall, and an F1-score of 97.56%. These findings highlighted the efficacy of integrating high-quality embeddings and optimization strategies with advanced neural architectures. The suggested model provided a strong and effective solution for automatic extraction, which can be potentially used in various fields.

Introduction

Keyphrases are groups of words that represent the primary topic addressed in a particular document. Generally, keyphrases are composed of one to five words that are found as they appear in the text, and they might be utilized to summarize the content. In general, extraction of keyphrase refers to automatically extracting and recognizing the most representative and pertinent terms or phrases from a document that comprehend its main themes or subjects1. These keyphrases act as brief summaries, allowing for a quick comprehension of the content and assisting with tasks such as search engine optimization, document classification, and information retrieval.

Compared to keywords, keyphrases typically consist of more than 2 words. Generally, the meaning conveyed by these phrases is more specific than the individual words. Additionally, with the ongoing advancement of the internet, this type of summarization has achieved sustained attention recently from both enterprise and academic sectors2,3.

Keyphrases are designed to fulfill different purposes. For instance, when they appear on the initial page of a journal article, their purpose is to provide a summary. They allow the reader to quickly assess if the article merits further reading. The other purpose is that when they have been included in the cumulative index of a journal, their purpose is related to indexing. They help the reader find a paper that meets a particular requirement. The last purpose is probably that once a search engine for has a field labeled keywords, the aim is to help the reader refine their search. A search for articles that align with a given query term within the field of keyword will result in a smaller, more relevant list of results compared to searching for the same term in the entire text of the articles.

Extracting keyphrases is a complex endeavor once done manually, since it necessitates a thorough comprehension of the content, context, and subtleties of the document to recognize the most pertinent phrases. Human annotators often face challenges with subjectivity, scalability, and consistency, particularly when confronted with specialized terminology or text4. Consequently, automatic techniques, especially those that utilize neural networks, have become the chosen method5. Neural networks, particularly architectures such as deep learning models and transformers, are adept at grasping semantic connections, patterns, and context within text, which makes them exceptionally suitable for extraction of keyphrase. These networks can efficiently handle large volumes of data reliably, diminishing the workload on humans while enhancing effectiveness and accuracy in recognizing important keyphrases6.

Deep learning has been used for many different purposes like air quality7,8, Pothole and plain road classification9, risk identification10, click fraud detection11, Cyberbullying Sexism Harassment12, and crop yield forecasting13. Neural network architectures, including CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks)14, BiLSTM (Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory) networks15, RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks)16, and encoder-based models17, are extensively used for extraction of keyphrase because they can identify sequential and local text patterns. Nonetheless, these networks encountered several issues, including challenges in managing long-terms dependencies, limited adaptability across different fields, and significant computational demands throughout training. For example, CNNs are proficient at capturing local attributes but have difficulty grasping the global context, whereas GRUs and LSTMs, while effective for sequential information, can be resource-intensive and susceptible to vanishing gradient issues in lengthy texts. Encoder-based networks utilizing attention mechanisms have demonstrated potential but still struggle with scalability and interpretability when dealing with large datasets. However, some optimization algorithms have been developed in order to overcome some of the problems that models might have18.

Despite advancements in keyphrase extraction based on neural networks, there are still some limitations: (1) Existing architectures lack support for long-range dependency or adaptation for context or domain; (2) these types of models remain computationally expensive and convergence methods are not efficient; and (3) Few have begun the process of linking word embedding with more advanced neural modelling. To overcome these barriers, the new framework has been presented that integrates ResNeXt’s hierarchical feature fusion with GloVe-100 word embeddings, which can be optimized through an Enhanced Human Evolutionary Optimization (EHEO) algorithm. This framework overcomes the drawback of vanishing gradients, achieves scalable performance in adaptive cross-domain generalization, and promotes efficient convergence, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods for diverse datasets.

Literature review

Empirical findings

Nikzad-Khasmakhi et al.19 established a multimodal approach for keyphrase extraction called Phraseformer, which utilized graph embedding and transformer approaches. In Phraseformer, the keywords were represented via a vector formed by combining structural learning and text representations. Phraseformer utilized advancements from recent studies like ExEm and BERT to maintain types of representations. Additionally, Phraseformer viewed the task of keyphrase extraction as a sequence labeling challenge that was addressed through a categorization task. The effectiveness of Phraseformer has been evaluated on three datasets, including SemEval 2017, SemEval2010, and Inspec. Also, the efficiency of various classifiers has been examined using the Phraseformer approach on the Inspec dataset. The experimental findings highlighted the efficacy of the Phraseformer approach across the three diverse datasets. Furthermore, the Random Forest achieved the highest F1-score compared to all other classifiers. This was because of integration of ExEm and BERT was more informative and provided a better representation of word semantics. As a result, Phraseformer surpassed single-modality approaches.

Yao et al.20 investigated the issue of KE (Keyphrase Extraction) in the context of talent recruitment. However, in contrast to conventional textual data, recruitment-related texts like job postings and resumes possessed features of conciseness and abbreviation. To address this, an interactive neural network was introduced called INKE that aimed to improve KE in the talent recruitment field. The study began by presenting a keyphrase indicator that captured explicit hint data for all the keyphrases. Next, a dynamically-initialized decoder was developed that generated keyphrases interactively. Additionally, a hierarchical reinforcement learning optimizer was introduced to improve the connection between keyphrase generation and hint data comprehension. Lastly, comprehensive examinations on real-life information clearly demonstrated the interpretability and effectiveness of INKE in comparison with other baseline models.

Joshi et al.21 introduced an alternative method for identifying keyphrases, defining them as phrases in a document that are significant for determining the document’s topic. In line with this, INSPECT was proposed that employed self-explaining approaches to identify influential keyphrases by evaluating how input phrases affected the task of classifying the document’s topic. The findings demonstrated that the approach eliminated the reliance on ad-hoc heuristics and set some benchmarks within unsupervised extraction of keyphrase across four datasets within two fields, including news articles and scientific publications.

Zhu et al.22 introduced a framework for Unsupervised Keyphrase Extraction. The task was framed as a completely generative procedure by the use of an encoder-decoder architecture, which integrated the forecast of missing and existing keyphrases within an end-to-end manner. The decoding process was accelerated by implementing a non-autoregressive decoder, allowing for extracting all keyphrases simultaneously in a rapid way. To improve the relevance of keyphrases, a neural topic module was explored that learned latent topic data without supervision, thereby enhancing the input document’s semantic comprehension. To enhance the coverage of keyphrases, an anchor-region approach was proposed for extracting current keyphrases, which initially identified the anchor points of these phrases and subsequently set the eventual keyphrase boundaries. The first anchor phrases were structured within an anchor-aware graph that was represented via the proposed topic-guided graph attention model that captured the document’s context at a global scale. The system demonstrated superior experimental findings compared to the current baseline models in six benchmark datasets of UKE, establishing itself as UKE model with high efficacy. Furthermore, detailed examinations revealed that the model successfully addressed the coverage and relevance issues in extraction of keyphrase, while also being more efficient throughout the decoding process.

Song et al.23 introduced a model for extraction of keyphrase known as MPNKE (Multiple Perspectives Neural Keyphrase Extraction), which learned representations and assessed the significance of phrases from various viewpoints, akin to a human’s method to the task. Comprehensive experimental findings across multiple benchmark datasets indicated that MPNKE surpassed the performance of other models in the majority of instances.

Villoth et al.24 explored the application of NLP and machine learning for analyzing source code, evaluating both modern and traditional error detection methods. Metaheuristic algorithms were employed to optimize the performance of machine learning, and a modified version of the well-established firefly algorithm was introduced in this study. A dual-layer framework was proposed, featuring a CNN that addressed complex feature environments, while the second layer utilized classifiers like XGBoost, categorical boosting (CatBoost), and adaptive boosting (AdaBoost) to enhance defect diagnosis. Additional simulations were performed using custom term frequency-inverse document frequency encoding to demonstrate the effectiveness of the suggested architecture. A total of seven experiments were conducted using publicly available datasets.

Mladenovic et al.25 addressed the complex challenge of insider threats to organizational security, including risks data breaches, like ransomware, and extortion. It involved six experiments using email, HTTP, and file content data. To tackle these threats, it employed emerging Natural Language Processing techniques with Machine Learning classifiers such as XGBoost and AdaBoost, focusing on the context and sentiment of malicious actions. A term frequency-inverse document frequency approach was introduced for enhanced detection. The study also highlighted the importance of hyperparameter selection on classifier performance and tested various optimizers, including a modified red fox optimization algorithm, in three simulated scenarios with promising results.

Motivation and contribution

The motivation for this research arises from the increasing demand for precise and effective keyphrase extraction (KPE) techniques, especially concerning the information retrieval and processing of large documents. Keyphrases are essential for summarization of documents, facilitating rapid comprehension of content, and enhancing optimization of search engine. Nevertheless, conventional approaches to keyphrase extraction frequently encounter difficulties, including ensuring scalability, addressing domain-specific language, and managing long-range dependencies. Manual extraction requires significant time and is often subjective, whereas current automated techniques, including those utilizing neural network, have limitations in their ability to grasp global and local contextual correlations within text. This research aims to overcome these issues by introducing a novel method that utilizes the ResNeXt neural network architecture, optimized through an enhanced human evolutionary optimization (EHEO) algorithm, along with GloVe-100 word embeddings. The objective is to improve the efficiency and accuracy of keyphrase extraction by effectively capturing intricate patterns and semantic relationships within text, while simultaneously lowering computational costs and enhancing generalization in various fields.

The main contribution of the present study is the creation of a strong keyphrase extraction approach that combines the GloVe-100 embeddings with ResNeXt architecture and an enhanced human evolutionary optimization algorithm. This integration enables the approach to effectively manage sentences of varying lengths, grasp distant word correlations, and enhance the entire efficiency of keyphrase extraction. While utilizing GloVe-100 embeddings, the human evolutionary optimization algorithm improves the model’s capability to converge fast and attain high efficacy.

Furthermore, the research presents an in-depth analysis of the suggested model compared to other methods, showcasing its enhanced performance regarding recall, precision, as well as F1-score. The findings emphasize the significance of integrating neural network structures with high-quality embeddings and optimization methods, representing a substantial advancement in the area of automatic extraction of keyphrase.

Methodology

The suggested approach

For extracting keyphrases, several steps have been followed. Initially, the document has been divided into sentences, after which those sentences were tokenized into words by the use of NLTK26. Next, a word embedding representation has been assigned that converts each word into a continuous vector format. Subsequently, the word embeddings was input into ResNeXt. The ResNeXt is linked to a fully connected hidden layer, which is also connected to a softmax output layer containing three neurons for each word. Dropout27 was implemented between the hidden layer and the ResNeXt layer, as well as between the output layer and the hidden layer, to mitigate overfitting. In the methods applied for keyphrase extraction, three neurons correspond to three potential output categories, including NO KP, BEGIN KP, and INSIDE KP. These classifications indicate tokens that are not part of keyphrases, the keyphrase’s initial token, and a keyphrase’s subsequent tokens, respectively. For instance, if the input sentence is “BiGRU can work well”, the keyphrase in that sentence is “BiGRU”, and the tokens’ classes will be BiGRU/BEGIN KP can/NO KP work/NO KP well/NO KP. GloVe embeddings of dimensionality 50, 100, and 200 have been evaluated. GloVe-100 was selected for final integration due to its optimal balance of accuracy and computational efficiency. Scientific documents (KP20k/Inspec) use exact technical terms. GloVe-100’s dimensionality can capture morphological richness (neurodegenerative vs. neural) and compound nouns, while GloVe-50 is not granular enough and GloVe-200 segments phrases too much. Dataset

The KP20k dataset is commonly utilized for extraction of keyphrase, especially within the field of scientific studies. It contains more than 500,000 scientific articles gathered from major publishers, and their keyphrases have been taken from the abstracts and titles of these documents. Each manuscript within the dataset has keyphrases that demonstrate the primary concepts or topics presented within the text. The dataset has been categorized into test, validation, and training sets, making it appropriate for supervised learning methods. KP20k is publicly available and has been utilized in numerous studies to assess keyphrase generation and extraction networks. The dataset and associated resources can be found on GitHub. This dataset is especially useful for scholars focusing on tasks such as knowledge discovery, information retrieval, and scientific document summarization. This dataset can be found at https://github.com/memray/OpenNMT-kpg-release.

The dataset utilized for the experiments comprises 2,000 English abstracts, along with their corresponding keywords and titles, taken from the Inspec database. These abstracts were published between 1998 and 2002 and originated from journal articles in the fields of Computers and Control, as well as Information Technology. The abstracts are linked to two sets of keywords labeled by a professional indexer, a collection of controlled terms, which are limited to those found in the Inspec thesaurus, and a group of uncontrolled terms that may include any relevant words. The abstracts were randomly divided into three subsets, including a training set containing 1,000 documents for model construction, a test set consisting of the final 500 abstracts, and a validation set of 500 documents for model assessment and selection of the best one to ensure unbiased results. This dataset can be found at https://github.com/INESCTEC/KeywordExtractor-Datasets.

The SemEval-2010 benchmark dataset28 consists of 244 research papers gathered from the ACM Digital Library, including workshop and conference articles. The included papers vary in length from 6 to 8 pages. Scientific articles were chosen from 4 distinct research domains as outlined in the ACM classification and were evenly divided into training, testing and validation of 146, 49, and 49, respectively. The gold standard keyphrases include both the author-assigned keyphrases sourced from the original PDF documents and reader-assigned keyphrases provided by student annotators. This dataset can be accessed at https://huggingface.co/datasets/midas/semeval2010.

Preprocessing

Text cleaning represents the initial phase in preprocessing, during which unrelated elements, such as special symbols, have been eliminated to prepare the text for analysis. Lowercasing is implemented for standardizing the text, facilitating easier processing. Punctuation marks have been omitted unless they form part of a keyphrase. Numbers may be retained, discarded, or converted to words, hinging on the dataset’s specific context. Tokenization involves splitting the text into phrases or individual words utilizing tools like NLTK. This step breaks down sentences into minor units, such as words, that are essential for recognizing multi-word keyphrases. Removal of Stopword involves the elimination of frequent words like “and”, “is”, and “the” that do not add significant meaning to keyphrases. Additionally, custom stopwords can be included to get rid of domain-specific words that are not relevant. This phase aids in concentrating on important terms and minimizing noise. Lemmatization and stemming are techniques used to standardize words by bringing them back to their root. Stemming reduces words to their core form (e.g., “running” becomes “run”), whereas lemmatization transforms words into their canonical form (e.g., “better” changes to “good”). This approach helps ensure that different forms of the identical word (such as “learning”, “learned”, and “learns”) are regarded as a single term, thereby enhancing consistency within extraction of keyphrase. Normalization aligns terms to a uniform format. For instance, abbreviations like “U.S.A” can be changed to “USA”, and synonyms like “AI” and “Artificial Intelligence” can be consolidated into one term. This process guarantees that various representations of the identical idea have been regarded consistently, improving the extracted keyphrases’ quality.

Word embedding

The input layer of the model consists of vector representations for each word found in the input document. A recent study29 demonstrated that these representations, known as word embeddings, provide a better understanding of word semantics compared to a “one hot” encoding word representation, especially once trained on extensive datasets. On the other hand, since the datasets for Automatic Keyphrase Extraction (AKE) are comparatively small, training word embeddings to accurately comprehend word semantics is challenging. Therefore, Stanford’s GloVe Embeddings have been utilized, which have been trained on 6 billion words sourced from web texts and Wikipedia30.

ResNeXt

Residual Neural Network (ResNet) has been recognized as a type of Deep Neural Network (DNN) designed to resolve the challenge of vanishing gradients that exists within CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks) using multiple layers. The disappearing gradient challenge occurs once gradients get excessively small while they move through numerous layers, which hinders efficient learning and optimal performance. The suggested network presents an innovative idea of residual links or skip links that enables data to skip particular layers in the network as it moves through.

The use of preceding layer activations within ResNet facilitates the effective learning of complex representations and enhances the flow of gradients. The key concept behind ResNet is the application of residual blocks, which feature multiple layers of convolution using skip connections. The current skip connections integrate the output of the block using its original input, which creates a shortcut path for data flow. The hyper-parameters in ResNet are reduced by the regular ResNeXt, which integrates cardinality with both the depth and width of the ResNet architecture. Description of the HEOA and its motivation

The motivation to enhance this algorithm arises from a few key factors. A significant reason behind this motivation is the extraordinary adaptability shown by human evolution, which facilitates the identification of better solutions within complicated environments.

The ability to manage complicated situations and find optimal solutions has resulted in the development of HEOA, an algorithm that navigates intricate optimization challenges. The idea of a Chaotic Universe represents a unique perspective on the origin and evolution of the universe. This present concept acts as an impetus for Chaos Mapping by utilizing Logistic mapping as a means of initialization. Chaos theory is integrated into the human evolutionary optimization algorithm, adding both dynamic and analytical aspect to the process of finding optimal solutions.

The phases of human evolution can be divided into two primary steps, including human exploration as well as human enhancement. Evaluation metrics

When assessing models of keyphrase extraction, it is crucial to employ strong metrics that can effectively measure their performance. The most frequently utilized metrics include F1-Score, Recall, and Precision. These metrics are based on False Negatives (FN), False Positives (FP), and True Positives (TP). Each metric presents a unique view of the model’s effectiveness, and they create a thorough evaluation framework.

Precision assesses the accuracy of the keyphrases identified via the model. Precision is determined by the ratio of TPs (correctly recognized keyphrases) to the total of TPs and FPs (wrongly recognized keyphrases). A high precision score signifies that the model effectively minimizes FPs, indicating that its predictions are reliable. The formula for precision is:23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Precision=\frac{True\:positives\:\left(TP\right)}{True\:positives\:\left(TP\right)+False\:positives\:\left(FP\right)}$$\end{document}

Recall evaluates how complete the keyphrases identified by the model are. Recall has been determined by the ratio of TPs to the total of TPs and FNs (keyphrases that the model failed to identify). A high recall suggests that the network effectively identifies the majority of pertinent keyphrases, even if it mistakenly comprises some irrelevant ones. The formula for recall is:24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Recall=\frac{True\:positives\:\left(TP\right)}{True\:positives\:\left(TP\right)+False\:negatives\:\left(FN\right)}$$\end{document}

The F1-Score represents the harmonic average of Recall and Precision. It offers a unified metric that balances recall and precision, making it especially helpful for assessing a model’s efficiency by taking into account FPs and FNs. The F1-Score is particularly useful in cases with imbalanced class distribution or once recall and precision carry equal significance. A high F1-Score reflects a network that excels in both recall and precision. The formula for calculating the F1-Score is:25\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:F1-score=2\times\:\frac{precision\times\:recall}{precision+recall}$$\end{document}

To summarize, precision emphasizes the accuracy of the forecasts, confirming that the keyphrases recognized via the network are correct. Recall emphasizes the range of the forecasts, ensuring that the network captures as many pertinent keyphrases as possible. The F1-Score balances these metrics, offering once indicator that represents the model’s entire effectiveness. By utilizing these metrics, a thorough insight into the performance of a keyphrase extraction model can be obtained and, well-informed decisions can be made regarding its appropriateness for various tasks.

Conclusion

Keyphrase extraction (KPE) is a vital process in Natural Language Processing (NLP) that focuses on recognizing the most significant and representative phrases with the purpose of summarizing its content. The keyphrases are crucial for a variety of uses, such as retrieval of information, search engine optimization, and summarization of document. Nevertheless, manual extraction of keyphrases can take much time and be subjective, while automatic techniques often struggle to accurately capture global and local contextual relationships within the text. To overcome these difficulties, the current study introduced an innovative method utilizing the ResNeXt architecture, optimized via enhanced human evolutionary optimization (EHEO) algorithm, and integrated with GloVe-100. The model has been assessed using the KP20k dataset, which is a well-known resource for extraction of keyphrase that includes over 500,000 scientific articles tagged with keyphrases, and Inspec database that comprises 2,000 English abstracts. The proposed model was benchmarked against several other models, such as CNN, KNN, SVM, BERT, CNN-BERT, and GRU. The findings revealed that the suggested ResNeXt + GloVe-100 + Algorithm model surpassed other models, reaching precision, recall, and F1-score values of 98.67%, 98.81%, and 98.74%, respectively. It could achieve 96.54%, 96.32%, and 96.43% on Inspect dataset, respectively. Moreover, the proposed model indicated remarkable performance over the SemEval-2010 dataset by achieving 97.32% precision, and 97.81% recall, and an F1-score of 97.56%. These outcomes underscored the efficacy of integrating progressive neural network architectures with optimization algorithm and high-quality embeddings for extraction of keyphrase, underscoring optimal embedding dimensionality and evolutionary optimization. The capability of the model to identify intricate patterns and semantic connections within text, while ensuring computational efficacy, made it an effective tool for tasks of automatic keyphrase extraction. The accomplishments of the suggested model contributed several significant implications. Firstly, it showcased the value of combining pre-trained GloVe with ResNeXt to enhance the efficiency of keyphrase extraction. Secondly, the application of a EHEO emphasized the significance of optimization approaches in improving model efficacy, especially in tasks that necessitate capturing contextual relationships and long-range dependencies. Ultimately, the model’s outstanding performance on the KP20k, Inspec, and SemEval-2010 datasets indicated that it could be efficiently utilized in other areas and datasets, providing a flexible solution for extraction of keyphrase across different contexts. This approach has some limitations as well. While our model performs exceptionally well on scientific datasets (KP20k, Inspec), and technical benchmarks (SemEval-2010), its performance diminishes when a domain with unique linguistic patterns- such as social media, legal documents, or conversational text-is encountered. This weakness stems from the homogeneity of training data and the bias of the academic keyphrase structure. For instance, this model may have difficulties with informal abbreviations and domain-specific jargon outside, or with the construct of non-Western languages. Moreover, while the EHEO algorithm exhibited good performance over most of the benchmark functions, its performance varied on a number of functions (e.g., F2, F5, and F8), as discussed. It accords with the No Free Lunch theorem that stresses no single algorithm ever being best. The algorithm also benefits from chaotic initialization (sine map) and adaptive parameters, which may cause unpredictable behavior in highly noisy or high-dimensional spaces. Although the suggested model has demonstrated good results, there are several potential directions for future exploration. Firstly, examining larger and more varied datasets could further confirm the model’s ability to generalize in diverse types and domains of text. Secondly, exploring the incorporation of other pre-trained embeddings, like GPT or BERT, might yield further insights into the impact of semantic representations on keyphrase extraction. Thirdly, enhancing the model to handle keyphrase extraction with multiple languages could expand its usefulness in international settings. Lastly, creating more interpretable versions of the model could aid users in understanding the keyphrase extraction procedure better, making it more user-friendly for those without technical expertise. In summary, this investigation provided a solid basis for further developments in the field of automatic keyphrase extraction.

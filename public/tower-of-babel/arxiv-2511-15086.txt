Abstract
. In this paper, we study three types of Birkhoff-James orthogonality in Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules, that is, the strong, quasi-strong, and original Birkhoff-James orthogonality. In general, the strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality is stronger than the quasi-strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality, and the quasi-strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality is stronger than the original Birkhoff-James orthogonality. Meanwhile, each reverse implication in this chain requires additional conditions. As the main results, we show that the strong and quasi-strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality are equivalent in a full Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -module if and only if the underlying C ∗ C^{*} -algebra is commutative, and that the equivalence of the quasi-strong and original Birkhoff-James orthogonality in a full Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -module implies the primeness of the underlying C ∗ C^{*} -algebra. Moreover, two examples, explaining the complexity of conditions for full Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules in which the quasi-strong and original Birkhoff-James orthogonality are equivalent, are given in the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra settings.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with three types of Birkhoff-James orthogonality in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules. The definition of the original Birkhoff-James orthogonality is the following.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normed space over
\mathbb{K}
, and let
x,y\in X
. Then,
x
is said to be
Birkhoff-James orthogonal
to
y
, denoted by
x\perp_{BJ}y
, if
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|
for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.

The relation
\perp_{BJ}
is recognized as a generalized orthogonality relation since it is equivalent to the usual inner product orthogonality
\perp
in inner product spaces. The first formulation of this orthogonality dates back to Birkhoff
[
12
]
, and its basics was completed by Day
[
16
]
and James
[
19
,
20
]
later, mainly in 1940s. The importance of the Birkhoff-James orthogonality comes from the strong connection with geometric structures of normed spaces. For example, we know characterizations of rotundity and smoothness of norms in terms of the Birkhoff-James orthogonality. Moreover, inner product spaces are characterized as normed spaces in which the relation
\perp_{BJ}
is symmetric. The readers interested in the Birkhoff-James orthogonality are referred to the surveys by Alonso-Martini-Wu
[
1
,
2
]
which give good expositions of the existing results on Birkhoff-James orthogonality in normed spaces.

In view of classification, the Birkhoff-James orthogonality gives enough information for identifying normed spaces linearly. To be precise, if a pair of normed spaces admits a linear Birkhoff-James isomorphism, that is, a linear bijection preserving the Birkhoff-James orthogonality in both directions, then they are isometrically isomorphic. This fact immediately follows from better results by Koldobsky
[
23
]
(for the real case) and Blanko-Turnšek
[
14
]
(for the general case). Based on this situation, recently, nonlinear classification of normed spaces using Birkhoff-James isomorphisms has been begun, for example, in
[
4
,
18
,
33
,
34
]
; see also
[
6
]
for understanding the topic. In particular, in the context of
C^{*}
-algebras, it was shown in
[
35
]
that commutative
C^{*}
-algebras can be completely classified in
C^{*}
-algebras by their Birkhoff-James orthogonality structure. Moreover, in
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
, finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras were classified in terms of the Birkhoff-James orthogonality, and Birkhoff-James isomorphisms between finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras were determined. As these examples indicate, the Birkhoff-James orthogonality is getting more important for analyzing
C^{*}
-algebras as Banach spaces.

The strong-version of Birkhoff-James orthogonality was introduced in Arambašić-Rajić
[
8
]
by using the module structure of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
. Then
x
is said to be
strongly Birkhoff-James orthogonal
to
y
, denoted by
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
, if
x\perp_{BJ}ya
for each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Considering an approximate unit for
\mathfrak{A}
, we can realize that
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
implies
x\perp_{BJ}y
. As expected, the relation
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
in a Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules captures algebraic information of the underlying
C^{*}
-algebra. For example, it was shown in
[
24
]
that every linear strong Birkhoff-James isomorphism, that is, every linear bijection preserving strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality in both directions, between von Neumann algebras is a scalar multiple of a
*
-isomorphism, follows by a unitary multiplication. In other words, von Neumann algebras are distinguished in the sense of
*
-isomorphisms by the combination of the linear and strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality structures. A result on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
similar to above, but for the mutual strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality, was obtained in
[
3
]
. There also exists a nonlinear classification theory for strong Birkhoff-James isomorphisms. In
[
5
]
, Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules over compact
C^{*}
-algebras were classified by using strong Birkhoff-James isomorphisms, and in
[
36
]
, commutative
C^{*}
-algebras were characterized and classified in terms of the strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality structure.

As a useful characterization of the Birkhoff-James orthogonality, we know the following result by James
[
20
]
: Let
X
be a normed space, and let
x,y\in X
. Then,
x\perp_{BJ}y
if and only if
\rho(x)=\|x\|
and
\rho(y)=0
for some
\rho\in X^{*}
with
\|\rho\|=1
. We also have the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module version of this result; see Arambašić-Rajić
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
and also
[
11
]
for its prototype. Namely, if
X
is replaced with a Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
, then
x\perp_{BJ}y
if and only if
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\rho(\langle x,y\rangle)=0
for some state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
. Moreover, it follows from
[
8
, Theorem 2.5]
that
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
if and only if
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\rho(|\langle x,y\rangle|^{2})=0
for some state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
, where the state
\rho
can be chosen pure as was indicated in the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 3.14]
. These results suggest a natural intermediate of the strong and original Birkhoff-James orthogonality.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module, and let
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
. Then,
x
is said to be
quasi-strongly Birkhoff-James orthogonal
to
y
, denoted by
x\perp_{BJ}^{q}y
, if
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\rho(\langle x,y\rangle)=0
for some pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
.

It follows that
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
implies
x\perp_{BJ}^{q}y
, and
x\perp_{BJ}^{q}y
implies
x\perp_{BJ}y
in any Hilbert
C^{*}
-module; for
|\rho(\langle x,y\rangle)|\leq\rho(|\langle x,y\rangle|^{2})^{1/2}
whenever
\rho
is a state. The relation
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
was first introduced in
[
17
]
for
C^{*}
-algebras to analyze locally parallel sets and their inclusion relation, and the preceding definition is a natural generalization of
[
17
, Definition 3.13]
. As interesting features of the quasi-strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality in a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, we know that it is equivalent to the strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality if
\mathfrak{A}
is commutative, and to the original Birkhoff-James orthogonality if
\mathfrak{A}
is
*
-isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
; see
[
17
, Remarks 3.16 and 3.17]
.

The existing results on
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are sometimes analogous but sometimes completely different. To avoid unintentional duplications of results in future and to develop those theories in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules properly, it is important to understand their relationship well. The present paper approaches this theme. In this direction, we only know that Hilbert spaces are the only full Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules in which
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent; see
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
.

The main results of the present paper are Theorems
3.1
and
3.5
. We show in Theorem
3.1
that
\perp\mathfrak{A}
is commutative; and in Theorem
3.5
that the underlying
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is prime if
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent in a full Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
. It is also shown that the converse to Theorem
3.5
is false in general. In fact, as is indicated in Theorem
4.1
, there are many primitive
C^{*}
-algebras in which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are not equivalent. Meanwhile, we have a non-simple
C^{*}
-algebra in which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent. This explains the complexity of conditions for full Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules in which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Let
X
be a vector space. A subset
C
of
X
is said to be
convex
if
(1-t)x+ty\in C
whenever
x,y\in C
and
t\in[0,1]
. A convex subset
D
of a convex set
C
is called a
face
of
C
if
x,y\in C
and
(1-t)x+ty\in D
for some
t\in(0,1)
imply
x,y\in D
. Let
x\in C
. Then,
x
is called an
extreme point
of
C
if
\{x\}
is a face of
C
. The set of all extreme points of
C
is denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits(C)
. If
X
is a normed space, then the
unit ball
of
X
means
\{x\in X:\|x\|\leq\}
. The symbol
X^{*}
stands for the (continuous) dual space of
X
. A standard textbook of Banach space theory is
[
29
]
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra is a complex Banach algebra
\mathfrak{A}
with a conjugate-linear involution operation
x\mapsto x^{*}
such that
\|x^{*}x\|=\|x\|^{2}
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
. The algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a typical example of a
C^{*}
-algebra. The algebra
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
of all compact operators on
\mathcal{H}
forms a closed two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
which is itself a
C^{*}
-algebra.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
x\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
x
is said to be
self-adjoint
if
x^{*}=x
, and
positive
, denoted by
x\geq 0
, if
x=y^{2}
for some self-adjoint element
y\in\mathfrak{A}
. We can define a natural partial ordering, so-called the L”owner order, for self-adjoint element
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
by declaring that
x\leq y
if
y-x\geq 0
. If
\mathfrak{A}
is unital with unit
1
, then
u\in\mathfrak{A}
is said to be
unital
if
u^{*}u=uu^{*}=1
. A spectrum
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(x)
of
x\in\mathfrak{A}
is defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(x)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\text{$x-\lambda 1$ is non-invertible in $\mathfrak{A}$}\}.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is non-untial, then
\widetilde{A}
denotes the unitization of
\mathfrak{A}
. The spectrum of an element of a non-unital
C^{*}
-algebra is considered in its unitization. Let
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
be a net in
\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is called an
approximate unit
for
\mathfrak{A}
if
\lim_{\lambda}\|x-xe_{\lambda}\|=0
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
. It is known that every
C^{*}
-algebra contains an increasing approximate unit consisting of positive elements of the unit ball.

An element
\rho\in\mathfrak{A}^{*}
is called a
state
of
\mathfrak{A}
if
\|\rho\|=1
and
\rho(a)\geq 0
whenever
a\geq 0
. The set of all states of
\mathfrak{A}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
. A
pure
state is an element of
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A}))
. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})=\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A}))
. Each state
\rho
of
\mathfrak{A}
induces, by the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal construction, a representation
(\mathcal{H}_{\rho},\pi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
is a Hilbert space,
\pi_{\rho}:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
is a
*
-homomorphism, and
\xi_{\rho}
is a unit vector such that
\overline{\pi_{\rho}(\mathfrak{A})\xi_{\rho}}=\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
and
\rho(x)=\langle\pi_{\rho}(x)\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
. If
\rho
is pure, then
\pi_{\rho}(\mathfrak{A})
acts irreducibly on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
, in which case, the representation
(\mathcal{H}_{\rho},\pi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho})
is said to be
irreducible
. The Kadison transitivity theorem is an important tool for analyzing irreducible representations. There are many celebrated textbooks for the theory of
C^{*}
-algebras; see, for example,
[
15
,
21
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then, an
inner product
\mathfrak{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
is a right
\mathfrak{A}
-module endowed with an
\mathfrak{A}
-valued inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathfrak{M}\times\mathfrak{M}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\langle x,\alpha y+\beta z\rangle=\alpha\langle x,y\rangle+\beta\langle x,z\rangle
for each
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{M}
and each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

\langle x,ya\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle a
for each
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
and each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

(3)

\langle x,y\rangle^{*}=\langle y,x\rangle
for each
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
.

(4)

\langle x,x\rangle\geq 0
for each
x\in\mathfrak{M}
, and
\langle x,x\rangle=0
implies
x=0
.

The norm of an element
x\in\mathfrak{M}
is defined by
\|x\|=\|\langle x,x\rangle\|^{1/2}
. If
\mathfrak{M}
is complete with respect to this norm, then
\mathfrak{M}
is called a
Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module
. A Hilbert
\mathbb{C}
-module corresponds to a Hilbert space. Since
\langle xa,y\rangle=\langle y,xa\rangle^{*}=a^{*}\langle x,y\rangle
and

0\leq\langle xa,xa\rangle=a^{*}\langle x,x\rangle a\leq\|x\|^{2}a^{*}a,

it follows that
\|xa\|=\|\langle xa,xa\rangle\|^{1/2}\leq\|x\|\|a\|
for each
x\in\mathfrak{M}
and each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
. A Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
is said to be
full
if the linear span of
\{\langle x,y\rangle:x,y\in\mathfrak{M}\}
is norm-dense in
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\mathfrak{M}
is a full
\mathfrak{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
and
a\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\{0\}
, then
xa\neq 0
for some
x\in\mathfrak{M}
. We note that
\mathfrak{A}
is itself a full Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module with the inner product defined by
\langle x,y\rangle=x^{*}y
for each
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
. For the basics of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules, the readers are referred to
[
28
, Chapter 1]
.

3.
Main results

First, we consider the equivalence of
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
and
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
in full Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules. The following theorem improves
[
9
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
\mathfrak{M}
be a full Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module. Then
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
and
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{M}
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}
is commutative.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is commutative. Let
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
be such that
x\perp_{BJ}^{q}y
, and let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
be such that
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\rho(\langle x,y\rangle)=0
. Since every pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
is multiplicative, it follows that
\rho(\langle x,ya\rangle)=\rho(\langle x,y\rangle a)=\rho(\langle x,y\rangle)\rho(a)=0
for each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
. Hence,
x\perp_{BJ}ya
for each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, that is,
x\perp_{BJ}^{s}y
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is non-commutative. We claim that there exists a pair
(x,\rho)\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
such that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\rho}\geq 2
and
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}=1
. To show this, let
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})
be the set of all multiplicative states of
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})=\emptyset
, then the claim is true. In the case
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})\neq\emptyset
, we have a
*
-homomorphism
a\mapsto(\rho(a))_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})}
from
\mathfrak{A}
into
\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A}))
. Since
\mathfrak{A}
is non-commutative, there exists a nonzero
a\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
\rho(a)=0
for each
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})
. By the fullness of
\mathfrak{M}
, we obtain
xa\neq 0
for some
x\in\mathfrak{M}
. It may be assumed that
\|xa\|=1
. Let
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
be such that
\tau(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=\|xa\|^{2}=1
. We note that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\geq 2
. Indeed, if
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\tau}=1
, then
\pi_{\tau}(a)=\tau(a)I\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})\cong\mathbb{C}
, which ensures
\tau\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})
. However, this is impossible since
\tau\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathfrak{A})
implies
\tau(a)=0
and
\tau(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=\tau(\langle xa,x\rangle)\tau(a)
. Therefore,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\rho}\geq 2
. This proves our claim.

Let
(x,\rho)\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
be a pair such that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\rho}\geq 2
and
\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}=1
. Since
\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle=\rho(\langle x,x\rangle)=1
, it follows that
\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
. We divide our argument into three cases:

(I)
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)=1
. In this case,
\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)=E_{\xi_{\rho}}
. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
such that
\xi_{\rho}\perp\xi
. By the Kadison transitivity theorem, there exist a self-adjoint element
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and a unitary element
u\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
such that
\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
,
\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi=\xi
, and
\pi_{\rho}(u)\xi_{\rho}=\xi
. Replacing
h
with
|\sin(2^{-1}\pi a)|
if necessary, we may assume that
a
is positive and
\|a\|=1
. It follows that
\|xa\|^{2}=\|\langle xa,xa\rangle\|=\|a\langle x,x\rangle a\|\leq 1
,

\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=\rho(a\langle x,x\rangle a)=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}\rangle=1

and

\displaystyle\rho(\langle xa,xau\rangle)=\rho(a\langle x,x\rangle au)

\displaystyle=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(a)\pi_{\rho}(u)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle E_{\xi_{\rho}}\xi,\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

(II)
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\geq 2
and
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle))\cap(0,1)=\emptyset
. In this case,
E=\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)
is a projection with
\xi_{\rho}\in E(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
E(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
such that
\xi_{\rho}\perp\xi
. The same pair
(a,u)
as (I) satisfies
\|xa\|^{2}=\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=1
and

\rho(\langle xa,xau\rangle)=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(a)\pi_{\rho}(u)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}\rangle=\langle\xi,\xi_{\rho}\rangle=0.

(III)
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\geq 2
and
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle))\cap(0,1)\neq\emptyset
. By functional calculus, we obtain a pair of positive norm-one elements
h,k\in\mathfrak{A}
commuting with
\langle x,x\rangle
such that
hk=0
,
\pi_{\rho}(h)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
and
\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle k)\neq 0
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
be such that
\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi\neq 0
. It may be assumed that
\|\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi\|=1
. We note that
\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi=\pi_{\rho}(k)\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\xi
and

\langle\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi\rangle=\langle\pi_{\rho}(h)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(k)\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\xi\rangle=0=\langle\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi\rangle

By the Kadison transitivity theorem, as in (I), we can find a positive element
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and a unitary element
u\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
such that
\|a\|=1
,
\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}
,
\pi_{\rho}(a)\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi=\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi
, and
\pi_{\rho}(u)\xi_{\rho}=\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi
. It follows that
\|xa\|\leq 1
,

\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=\rho(a\langle x,x\rangle a)=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}\rangle=1

and

\displaystyle\rho(\langle xa,xau\rangle)=\rho(a\langle x,x\rangle au)

\displaystyle=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(a)\pi_{\rho}(u)\xi_{\rho},\pi_{\rho}(a)\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi_{\rho}(\langle x,x\rangle)\pi_{\rho}(k)\xi,\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Thus, in either case, we have a norm-one element
x\in\mathfrak{M}
, a positive norm-one element
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and a unitary element
u\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
such that
xa\perp_{BJ}^{q}xau
. Meanwhile, we derive
xa\not\perp_{BJ}^{s}xau
by
0\leq a\leq 1
and

\|xa-(xau)u^{*}a\|=\|x(a-a^{2})\|\leq\|a-a^{2}\|=1/4<\|xa\|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we consider the equivalence of
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
in full Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules. In this direction, we have a very strong sufficient condition and an algebraic necessary conditon for the equivalence. The following proposition gives a former one.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module. If
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{A}
, then so is on
\mathfrak{M}
. In particular,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on every Hilbert
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
-module.

Proof.

Suppose that
a\perp_{BJ}^{q}b
if and only if
a\perp_{BJ}b
for each
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
. It follows that
\langle x,x\rangle\perp_{BJ}^{q}\langle x,y\rangle
if and only if
\langle x,x\rangle\perp_{BJ}\langle x,y\rangle
for each
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
. We also know that
x\perp_{BJ}y
is equivalent to
\langle x,x\rangle\perp_{BJ}\langle x,y\rangle
for each
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
. Thus,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{M}
. The last statement follows from
[
17
, Remark 3.17]
.
∎

Next, we consider a necessary condition. Recall that a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is said to be
prime
if
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}\neq\{0\}
for each pair of nontrivial closed two-sided ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathfrak{A}
. The following lemma is essentially a version of
[
15
, Lemma I.9.15]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
\mathcal{I}
be a closed two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{A}
, and let
\rho
be a pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\|\rho|\mathcal{I}\|=1
. Then,
\rho|\mathcal{I}
is a pure state of
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Since
\|\rho\|=\|\rho|\mathcal{I}\|=1
, by
[
13
, Proposition II.6.4.16]
,
\rho
is a unique extension of
\rho|\mathcal{I}
to
\mathfrak{A}
and
\rho(x)=\lim_{a}\rho(xe_{a})
for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
, where
(e_{a})_{a}
is an (increasing) approximate unit. Now, let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
be positive linear functionals on
\mathcal{I}
such that
\|\tau_{1}\|\leq 1
,
\|\tau_{2}\|\leq 1
and
(1-t)\tau_{1}+t\tau_{2}=\rho|\mathcal{I}
. In this case, again by
[
13
, Proposition II.6.4.16]
,
\tau_{j}
extends uniquely to a positive linear functional
\rho_{j}
on
\mathfrak{A}
satisfying
\|\rho_{j}\|=\|\tau_{j}\|
, in which case,
\rho_{j}(x)=\lim_{a}\tau_{j}(xe_{a})
. It follows from

\displaystyle((1-t)\rho_{1}+t\rho_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=(1-t)\lim_{a}\tau_{1}(xe_{a})+t\lim_{a}\tau_{2}(xe_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{a}((1-t)\tau_{1}+t\tau_{2})(xe_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{a}\rho(xe_{a})

\displaystyle=\rho(x)

for each
x\in\mathfrak{A}
that
(1-t)\rho_{1}+t\rho_{2}=\rho
. Since
\rho
is pure, we obtain
\rho=\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
and
\rho|\mathcal{I}=\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
. This shows that
\rho|\mathcal{I}
is a pure state of
\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be nontrivial closed two-sided ideals of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\}
, and let
\rho
be a pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\|\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})\|=1
. Then,
\rho|\mathcal{I}=0
or
\rho|\mathcal{J}=0
.

Proof.

First, we note that
\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}
is also a closed two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{A}
by
[
13
, Corollary II.5.1.3]
. From this, Lemma
3.3
ensures that
\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})
is a pure sate of
\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}
. Let
(e_{a})_{a}
and
(f_{b})_{b}
be increasing approximate units for
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
, respectively. Using
[
29
, Technique 2.1.32]
, we can find subnets
(e_{c})_{c}
of
(e_{a})_{a}
and
(f_{c})_{c}
of
(f_{b})_{b}
having the same index set. Since
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\}
and
(e_{c})_{c}
and
(f_{c})_{c}
are also increasing approximate units for
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
, it follows that
\|e_{c}+f_{c}\|\leq 1
for each
c
and

\lim_{c}(e_{c}+f_{c})(x+y)=\lim_{c}(e_{c}x+f_{c}y)=x+y

for each
x\in\mathcal{I}
and each
y\in\mathcal{J}
. Hence,
(e_{c}+f_{c})_{c}
is an increasing approximate unit for
\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}
. Combining this with the fact that
\rho|\mathcal{I}\geq 0
,
\rho|\mathcal{J}\geq 0
and
\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})\geq 0
, we obtain

1=\|\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})\|=\lim_{c}\rho(e_{c}+f_{c})=\lim_{c}\rho(e_{c})+\lim_{c}\rho(f_{c})=\|\rho|\mathcal{I}\|+\|\rho|\mathcal{J}\|

by
[
15
, Lemmas I.9.5 and I.9.6]
.

Now, let
\tau_{1}(x+y)=\rho(x)
and
\tau_{2}(x+y)=\rho(y)
for each
x+y\in\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}
. Then,
\tau_{j}
is well-defined by
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\}
and linear. If
x+y\in\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}
and
x+y\geq 0
, then
x^{*}=x
and
y^{*}=y
, which implies that
x^{3}=x(x+y)x\geq 0
and
y^{3}=y(x+y)y\geq 0
. Therefore,
x\geq 0
and
y\geq 0
by the spectral mapping theorem. This shows that
\tau_{1}(x+y)=\rho(x)\geq 0
and
\tau_{2}(x+y)=\rho(y)\geq 0
. Namely,
\tau_{1}\geq 0
and
\tau_{2}\geq 0
. Combining this with
[
15
, Lemmas I.9.5 and I.9.6]
, we obtain
\|\tau_{1}\|=\lim_{c}\tau_{1}(e_{c}+f_{c})=\|\rho|\mathcal{I}\|
and
\|\tau_{2}\|=\lim_{c}\tau_{2}(e_{c}+f_{c})=\|\rho|\mathcal{J}\|
. Finally, if
\|\tau_{1}\|\|\tau_{2}\|\neq 0
, then

\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}=\|\tau_{1}\|(\|\tau_{1}\|^{-1}\tau_{1})+\|\tau_{2}\|(\|\tau_{2}\|^{-1}\tau_{2}),

while
\|\tau_{1}\|^{-1}\tau_{1}\neq\|\tau_{2}\|^{-1}\tau_{2}
by
\lim_{c}(\|\tau_{1}\|^{-1}\tau_{1})(e_{c})=1\neq 0=\lim_{c}(\|\tau_{2}\|^{-1}\tau_{2})(e_{c})
. This contradicts the fact that
\rho|(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})
is pure. Thus,
\|\tau_{1}\|\|\tau_{2}\|=0
, that is,
\rho|\mathcal{I}=0
or
\rho|\mathcal{J}=0
.
∎

The following result gives an algebraic necessary condition for full Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules on which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\mathfrak{M}
be a full
\mathfrak{A}
-module. If
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{A}
, then
\mathfrak{A}
is prime.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}
is not prime. Then, there exists a pair of nontrivial closed two-sided ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\}
. Let
a\in\mathcal{I}
and
b\in\mathcal{J}
be nonzero. Since
\mathfrak{M}
is full, we have
xa\neq 0
and
yb\neq 0
for some
x,y\in\mathfrak{M}
. It may be assumed that
\|xa\|=\|yb\|=1
. We note that

\displaystyle\langle\alpha xa+\beta yb,\alpha xa+\beta yb\rangle

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{2}\langle xa,xa\rangle+\alpha\overline{\beta}\langle xa,yb\rangle+\overline{\alpha}\beta\langle yb,xa\rangle+|\beta|^{2}\langle yb,yb\rangle

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{2}\langle xa,xa\rangle+|\beta|^{2}\langle yb,yb\rangle

for each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
by
a^{*}\langle x,y\rangle b,b^{*}\langle y,x\rangle a\in\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\}
. Combining this with

\langle xa,xa\rangle\langle yb,yb\rangle=a^{*}\langle x,xa\rangle\langle yb,y\rangle b\in\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\{0\},

we obtain
\|\alpha xa+\beta yb\|=\max\{|\alpha|,|\beta|\}
for each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence, it follows from

\displaystyle\|(xa+yb)+\lambda(xa-yb)\|=\|(1+\lambda)xa+(1-\lambda)yb\|=\max\{|1+\lambda|,|1-\lambda|\}\geq 1

for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
that
xa+yb\perp_{BJ}xa-yb
. Meanwhile, if
\rho
is a pure state of
\mathfrak{A}
such that

1=\rho(\langle xa+yb,xa+yb\rangle)=\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)+\rho(\langle yb,yb\rangle),

then
\rho|\mathcal{I}=0
or
\rho|\mathcal{J}=0
by Lemma
3.4
. In the case
\rho|\mathcal{J}=0
, we obtain
\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=1
and

\displaystyle\rho(\langle xa+yb,xa-yb\rangle)=\rho(\langle xa,xa\rangle)=1;

while, in the caes
\rho|\mathcal{I}=0
, we get
\rho(\langle yb,yb\rangle)=1
and

\displaystyle\rho(\langle xa+yb,xa-yb\rangle)=-\rho(\langle yb,yb\rangle)=-1;

Therefore,
xa+yb\not\perp_{BJ}^{q}xa-yb
.
∎

Corollary 3.6

(Arambašić and Rajić, 2015)
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\mathfrak{M}
be a full Hilbert
\mathfrak{A}
-module. Then,
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{M}
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

If
\perp_{BJ}^{s}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{M}
, then Theorems
3.1
and
3.5
ensures that
\mathfrak{A}
is commutative and prime. Since every prime
C^{*}
-algebra has the trivial center, it follows that
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{C}
. The converse follows from Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4.
Remarks

We conclude this paper with some remarks on Theorem
3.5
. It will turn out that the converse to Theorem
3.5
is not true and conditions for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules on which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent is rather complicated even in the
C^{*}
-algebra settings.

Before starting, we recall about a dichotomy for pure states. Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathfrak{A}
. Then, the following hold:

(i)

If
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
is ultraweakly continuous on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, then
\rho
is a vector state.

(ii)

If
\rho
is singular on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, then
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\ker\rho
.

(iii)

If
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
, then
\rho
is a vector state or
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\ker\rho
.

We explain these clauses for the sake of completeness.

(i) We note that
\rho
extends uniquely to a pure state of
\mathcal{P}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}})
. Moreover, the ultraweak continuity of
\rho
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
ensures that it extends (uniquely) to an ultraweakly continuous state of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by
[
22
, Corollary 10.1.11]
. From this and
[
22
, Theorem 7.1.12]
, we have an at most countable orthogonal system of nonzero vectors
(\zeta_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that
\sum_{n\in N}\|\zeta_{n}\|^{2}=1
and
\rho=\sum_{n\in N}\omega_{\zeta_{n}}=\sum_{n\in N}\|\zeta_{n}\|^{2}\omega_{\|\zeta_{n}\|^{-1}\zeta_{n}}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Combining this with
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
, we conclude that
N
is a singleton. Namely,
\rho=\omega_{\zeta}
for some unit vector
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
.

(ii) We assume that
\rho
is singular on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
. By
[
22
, Corollary 10.1.16]
, whenever
B,C\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, the functional
\rho_{0}
defined by
\rho_{0}(A)=\rho(BAC)
for each
A\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
is singular or zero. Let
\xi
be a unit vector in
\mathcal{H}
. It follows from

\rho(E_{\xi}AE_{\xi})=\omega_{\xi}(A)\rho(E_{\xi})

for each
A\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
that
\rho(E_{\xi})\omega_{\xi}
is singular as well as ultraweakly continuous. Hence,
\rho(E_{\xi})=0
. Thus,
\rho
vanishes on every rank-one projection on
\mathcal{H}
, which ensures
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\ker\rho
.

(iii) By
[
22
, Theorem 10.1.15]
,
\rho
(which can be considered as a pure state of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
) is decomposed into the sum of ultraweakly continuous part
\rho_{u}
and singular part
\rho_{s}
, in which case,
\|\rho_{u}\|+\|\rho_{s}\|=1
. If
\rho\neq\rho_{u}
and
\rho\neq\rho_{s}
, then
\|\rho_{u}\|>0
and
\|\rho_{s}\|>0
, which implies that

\rho=\|\rho_{u}\|(\|\rho_{u}\|^{-1}\rho_{u})+\|\rho_{s}\|(\|\rho_{s}\|^{-1}\rho_{s}).

This contradicts
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
. Hence,
\rho=\rho_{u}
or
\rho=\rho_{s}
. The conclusion follows from (i) and (ii).

Now, we present two contrasting results explaining the complexity of conditions for conditions for
C^{*}
-algebras on which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, and let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathfrak{A}
if and only if
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the ‘only if’ part. Suppose to the contrary that
\mathfrak{A}\neq\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Then, there exists a self-adjoint element
A\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
\|A+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\|=1
. By
[
13
, Proposition II.5.1.5]
, it may be assumed that
\|A\|=1
. In this case, we also have
\|A^{2}\|=1=\|A^{2}+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\|
. Set
B=A^{2}\geq 0
for short. If
B
is not norm attaining, then we replace
B
with
(I-E)B(I-E)+E
for some rank-one projection
E
. We note that
0\leq(I-E)B(I-E)+E\leq I
and

(I-E)B(I-E)+E=B-BE-EB+EBE+E\in B+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathfrak{A}.

In what follows, we assume that
BE=E
. Now, let
\xi_{1}
be the unit vector such that
E=E_{\xi_{1}}
, and let
(\xi_{n})_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
. Set

C=B^{1/2}\left(I-\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n}E_{\xi_{n}}\right)B^{1/2}=B-\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n}B^{1/2}E_{\xi_{n}}B^{1/2}\in B+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})

and
D=C-2E\in\mathfrak{A}
. Since
0\leq C\leq B\leq I
and
1=\|B+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\|\leq\|C\|
, it follows that
\|C\|=1
.

We claim that
C\perp_{BJ}D
. Since
\|C+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\|=1
, there exists a state
\tau_{0}
of
\mathfrak{A}/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\tau_{0}(C+\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))=1
. Set
\tau=\tau_{0}\circ\pi
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is the quotient map. Then,
\tau\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A})
,
\tau(C)=1
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\subset\ker\tau
. It follows from
B\xi_{1}=B^{1/2}\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}
that

\left(\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{\xi_{1}}+\tau)\right)(C)=1

and

\left(\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{\xi_{1}}+\tau)\right)(D)=1-\left(\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{\xi_{1}}+\tau)\right)(2E)=0.

Hence,
C\perp_{BJ}D
.

Next, let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{A})
be such that
\rho(C)=1
. If
\rho
is singular, then
\rho(D)=\rho(C)=1
. If
\rho=\omega_{\xi}
for some
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|\xi\|=1
, then
\sum_{n}E_{\xi_{n}}=I
implies

\displaystyle 1=\langle C\xi,\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(I-\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n}E_{\xi_{n}}\right)B^{1/2}\xi,B^{1/2}\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle E_{\xi_{1}}B^{1/2}\xi,B^{1/2}\xi\rangle+\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\langle E_{\xi_{n}}B^{1/2}\xi,B^{1/2}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=|\langle B^{1/2}\xi,\xi_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)|\langle B^{1/2}\xi,\xi_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n}|\langle B^{1/2}\xi,\xi_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|B^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\|B^{1/2}\xi\|=1
,
\langle B^{1/2}\xi,\xi_{n}\rangle=0
whenever
n\geq 2
, and
B^{1/2}\xi=\lambda\xi_{1}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|=1
. In particular, we obtain
C\xi=\lambda B^{1/2}\xi_{1}=\lambda\xi_{1}
. Meanwhile, it follows from
1=\rho(C)\leq\rho(C^{2})^{1/2}=1
that
\rho((I-C)^{2})=\rho(I-2C+C^{2})=0
and

|\rho((I-C)T)|=|\rho(T^{*}(I-C))|\leq\rho(T^{*}T)^{1/2}\rho((I-C)^{2})^{1/2}=0

for each
T\in\mathfrak{A}
. Thus,
\rho(T)=\rho(CTC)=\omega_{\xi}(CTC)=\omega_{\xi_{1}}(T)
for each
T\in\mathfrak{A}
, which together with
E=E_{\xi_{1}}
shows that
\rho(D)=\rho(C-2E)=-1\neq 0
. Consequently,
C\not\perp_{BJ}^{q}D
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a nonseparable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Then,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{\sim}
.

Proof.

We infer that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{\sim})
contains a unique singular element
\rho_{\infty}
which is defined by
\rho_{\infty}(A+cI)=c
for each
A+cI\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{\sim}
. We have
\rho_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{\sim})
. Let
A,B\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
and
c,d\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
A+cI\perp_{BJ}B+dI
, and let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))
be such that
\rho(|A+cI|)=\|A+cI\|
and
\rho((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))=0
. We may assume that
\|A+cI\|=1
. By
[
22
, Theorem 10.1.15]
, we have a pair of ultraweakly continuous and singular functionals
(\rho_{u},\rho_{s})
satisfying
\|\rho_{u}\|+\|\rho_{s}\|=1
and
\rho=\rho_{u}+\rho_{s}
. Since
1=\rho(I)=\rho_{u}(I)+\rho_{s}(I)
, it follows that
\|\rho_{u}\|=\rho_{u}(I)
and
\|\rho_{s}\|=\rho_{s}(I)
, which implies that
\rho_{u}\geq 0
and
\rho_{s}\geq 0
. Moreover, the identity

\rho_{u}(|A+cI|)+\rho_{s}(|A+cI|)=\|A+cI\|=\|\rho_{u}\|\|A+cI\|+\|\rho_{s}\|\|A+cI\|

ensures
\rho_{u}(|A+cI|)=\|\rho_{u}\|\|A+cI\|
and
\rho_{s}(|A+cI|)=\|\rho_{s}\|\|A+cI\|
. We note that
\rho_{s}=\|\rho_{s}\|\rho_{\infty}
. Moreover, by
[
22
, Corollary 10.1.11]
,
\rho_{u}
extends (uniquely) to an ultraweakly continuous state of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. From this and
[
22
, Theorem 7.1.12]
, we have an at most countable orthogonal system of nonzero vectors
(\zeta_{n})_{n\in N}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that
\rho_{u}=\sum_{n\in N}\omega_{\zeta_{n}}=\sum_{n\in N}\|\zeta_{n}\|^{2}\omega_{\|\zeta_{n}\|^{-1}\zeta_{n}}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\sum_{n}\|\zeta_{n}\|^{2}=\|\rho_{u}\|
. In particular, by
\rho_{u}(|A+cI|)=\|\rho_{u}\|\|A+cI\|
, we obtain
|A+cI|\zeta_{n}=\zeta_{n}
for each
n\in N
, which means that
|A+cI|E=E
for the projection
E
from
\mathcal{H}
onto
[\{\zeta_{n}:n\in N\}]
.

Suppose that
|c|<1
. In this case,
\rho=\rho_{u}
by

\rho_{\infty}(|A+cI|^{2})=|c|^{2}<1=\|A+cI\|^{2}.

We claim that
E
is finite-rank (or equivalently,
N
is finite). To show this, suppose to the contrary that
E(\mathcal{H})
contains an orthonormal sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n}
. Since
(\xi_{n})_{n}
is weakly null, we have
\lim_{n}\|C\xi_{n}\|=0
for each
C\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
and

1=\|\xi_{n}\|=\||A+cI|^{2}\xi_{n}\|\leq\|(A^{*}A+cA^{*}+\overline{c}A)\xi_{n}\|+|c|^{2}\to|c|^{2}.

However, this contradicts
|c|<1
. Therefore,
E
must be finite-rank. Now, let
W_{E(\mathcal{H})}(C)=\{\langle C\xi,\xi\rangle:\xi\in E(\mathcal{H}),~\|\xi\|=1\}
for each
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Since
W_{E(\mathcal{H})}(C)=W_{E(\mathcal{H})}(ECE)
and
ECE
can be viewed as an operator on the finite-dimensional subspace
E(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
, it turns out that
W_{E(\mathcal{H})}(C)
is always closed. Now, recall that
\rho((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))=0
, which implies that
0\in W_{E(\mathcal{H})}((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))
by the Toeplitz-Hausdorff theorem. This proves
A+cI\perp_{BJ}^{q}B+dI
.

Suppose that
|c|\geq 1
. It follows from

1\leq|c|^{2}=\rho_{\infty}(|A+cI|^{2})\leq 1

that
\rho_{\infty}(|A+cI|)=|c|=1=\|A+cI\|
. If
\rho_{\infty}((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))=c\overline{d}=0
, then
A+cI\perp_{BJ}^{q}B+dI
. We assume that
c\overline{d}\neq 0
. In this case,
\rho((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))=0
implies
\rho_{u}((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))=-c\overline{d}\|\rho_{s}\|
. Meanwhile, if
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, then
C_{1}^{*},\ldots,C_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
has the separable range and so is
\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}R(C_{j}^{*})
by
(\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}R(C_{j}^{*}))(\mathcal{H})=[\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\overline{C_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{H})}]
. From this, we derive
\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}N(C_{j})=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}(I-R(C_{j}^{*}))=I-\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}R(C_{j}^{*})\neq 0
, where
R(C_{j}^{*})
and

are respectively the projections onto
\overline{C_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{H})}
and
\ker C_{j}
. Now, let

for short. Then, we get

ensures that
W_{E^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})}(B^{*}A+cB+\overline{d}A)

\displaystyle W_{E^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})}((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))

\displaystyle=W_{E^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})}(B^{*}A+cB+\overline{d}A+c\overline{d}I)

\displaystyle=\{\lambda+c\overline{d}:\lambda\in W_{E^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})}(B^{*}A+cB+\overline{d}A)\}

-c\overline{d}\|\rho_{s}\|\|\rho_{u}\|^{-1}=(\|\rho_{u}\|^{-1}\rho_{u})((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI))\in W_{E^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})}((B+dI)^{*}(A+cI)).

, the Toeplitz-Hausdorff theorem generates a unit vector

. Finally, we remark that

which ensures

.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Then,
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{\sim}
if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is nonseparable.

Recall that a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is said to be
elementary
if
\mathfrak{A}
is
*
-isomorphic to some
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
,
simple
if
\mathfrak{A}
contains no nontrivial closed two-sided ideal, and
primitive
if there exists a faithful irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{A}
. It is obivious that an elementary
C^{*}
-algebra is simple. Since the kernel of each representation is a closed two-sided ideal, a simple
C^{*}
-algebra is primitive. It is known that the primitivity of a
C^{*}
-algebra implies primeness; see, for example,
[
13
, II.5.4.6]
. By Corollary
4.3
, we have a non-simple
C^{*}
-algebra in which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent, and a primitive
C^{*}
-algebra in which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are not equivalent. This situation for
C^{*}
-algebras is summarized as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\text{\framebox{\hskip 61.25009pt$\perp_{BJ}^{q}$ and $\perp_{BJ}$ are equivalent\hskip 61.25009pt}}\\[10.00002pt]
\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\Uparrow&&\not\Downarrow&&\not\Uparrow&&\Downarrow\\[10.00002pt]
\text{\framebox{elementary}}&\Rightarrow&\text{\framebox{simple}}&\Rightarrow&\text{\framebox{primitive}}&\Rightarrow&\text{\framebox{prime}}\end{array}\end{array}

The following problems are still open.

.

\mathfrak{A}
be a simple
C^{*}
-algebra. Are
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
equivalent on
\mathfrak{A}
?

Problem 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra on which
\perp_{BJ}^{q}
and
\perp_{BJ}
are equivalent. Is
\mathfrak{A}
primitive?

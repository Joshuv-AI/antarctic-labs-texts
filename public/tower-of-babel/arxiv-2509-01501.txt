Effective termination of general type MMPs in dimension at most five
Jingjun Han, Jihao Liu, Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. We prove the effective termination of general type MMPs in dimension at most 5 5 , and give explicit bounds (in terms of topological invariants and volume of divisors) on the number of steps in the MMP when the dimension is at most 4 4 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14E30, 14J30, 14J35.

Throughout this paper, we work over
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers.

1.
Introduction

The termination of the minimal model program (MMP) is one of the major open problems in birational geometry. The termination is known in full generality in dimension three by
[
28
,
22
,
19
,
29
]
. Several special cases are also known in dimension four
[
18
,
10
,
1
,
3
,
31
,
4
,
14
,
7
,
27
]
, and in higher dimensions we have the termination of MMP with scaling (a special kind of MMP) in the general type or big boundary case
[
2
]
. Beyond these, the termination conjecture remains extremely difficult. In particular, it is an open question whether every fivefold general type MMP terminates.

Our first result in this paper is the effective termination of general type MMPs, or more generally, of pseudo-effective MMP with a big boundary, in dimension five.

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem
3.9
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair of dimension
5
. Assume that at least one of the following holds.

(1)

K_{X}+\Delta
is big.

(2)

K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective and
\Delta
is big.

Then there exists a positive integer
m
depending only on
(X,\Delta)
such that every
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates after at most
m
steps.

We also give some explicit bounds on the number of steps in the MMP in dimensions
3
and
4
. A sample result is the following. Here
h_{4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)
is the dimension of the subspace in the singular homology group

H_{4}(X,\mathbb{R})
generated by algebraic cycles.

Theorem 1.2

(see Theorems
4.15
)
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
(X,\Delta)
a projective log smooth klt pair of dimension
4
such that
N\Delta
has integer coefficients. Assume that

(1)

(X,\Delta)
has at most
N
strata, each stratum has Picard number at most
N
, and
h_{4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\leq N
, and

(2)

\Delta
is big,
\mathrm{vol}(\Delta)\geq\frac{1}{N}
, and
(\Delta\cdot H^{3})\leq N
for some very ample divisor
H
on
X
.

Then any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates after at most
M^{M}
steps, where
M:=(2N)^{9}!
.

By passing to log resolutions, Theorem
1.2
gives explicit termination results for klt MMP with a big boundary (see Lemma
2.6
).

We remark that even the termination of such fourfold MMPs was not previously known. We also prove similar explicit termination results for general type fourfold MMPs (Theorem
4.14
), terminal fourfold MMPs (Corollary
4.9
) and arbitrary threefold MMPs (Theorem
4.16
). For some past works on the explicit or effective termination problem for threefolds, see for example
[
8
,
26
,
6
,
20
]
.

Sketch of proof.
A key ingredient in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is some (explicit) uniform bounds on the Cartier index of Weil divisors in a general type MMP
[
16
, Theorem 3.2, Corollaries 3.18 and 3.20]
. By Birkar’s inductive approach
[
3
]
, if termination holds in dimension
n-1
, then it also holds for pairs of nonnegative Kodaira dimension in dimension
n
. We refine Birkar’s argument to show that if termination holds for MMPs with discrete discrepancies in dimension
n-1
, then we also have termination for MMPs with uniform index bound in dimension
n
. By the termination of terminal fourfold flips, it is not hard to see that MMP with discrete discrepancies always terminates in dimension
4
, and this gives the termination of general type MMPs in Theorem
1.1
. Once we have termination, the effective bound on the number of steps essentially comes from Kőnig’s lemma.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
divides into two main steps: we first find explicit bounds on the number of steps in terminal MMPs, and then use the index bound from
[
16
]
to reduce to the terminal case. By a variation of difficulty function type arguments for termination (see e.g.
[
28
,
18
,
22
,
19
,
29
,
10
,
1
]
), one may write down a sequence of invariants involving the Picard numbers of boundary divisors, the (weighted) difficulties of the pair, and certain algebraic Betti numbers, that is lexicographically decreasing in any terminal MMPs in dimensions
3
and
4
. To turn this into an explicit bound on the number of steps in the terminal MMP, we show in Section
4.2
that the above lexicographic sequence of invariants decreases in a bounded way, in the sense that each time an individual term drops by one, the terms thereafter increases by at most a uniformly fixed amount. To deal with the general klt case, we further introduce a weighted combination of the above invariants, where the weights ultimately depends on the index bound from
[
16
]
, and combine with the results in the terminal case to show that the weighted combination decreases by (at least) a fixed amount after each step of the MMPs we consider. This gives the explicit bound in Theorem
1.2
.

Outline of the article.
In Section
2
we summarize some preliminary results that will be used in the later part of this paper. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.2
and its variants.

2.1.
Notation and conventions

In this paper, varieties are assumed to be quasi-projective unless otherwise specified. We follow the standard terminology from
[
21
,
24
]
.

Given two
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
on a variety
X
, we write
\Delta^{\prime}\geq\Delta
if
\mathrm{mult}_{D}\Delta^{\prime}\geq\mathrm{mult}_{D}\Delta
for any prime divisor
D
on
X
.

A
sub-pair

(X,\Delta)
consists of a normal variety
X
together with an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
(a priori, we do not require that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier). It is called a
pair
if
\Delta\geq 0
. We denote by
\mathrm{Coeff}(\Delta)
the (finite) set of coefficients of
\Delta
(in particular, we require that
0\in\mathrm{Coeff}(\Delta)
). A
log (sub-)pair
is a (sub-)pair
(X,\Delta)
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

A sub-pair
(X,\Delta)
is called
log smooth
if
X
is smooth and
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)
is a simple normal crossing divisor. A
stratum
(resp.
open stratum
) of a log smooth pair
(X,\Delta=\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}B_{i})
, where
B_{i}
are the irreducible components of
\Delta
, is defined to be a connected component of
B_{J}:=\cap_{j\in J}B_{j}
(resp.
B^{\circ}_{J}:=\cap_{j\in J}B_{j}\setminus\cup_{j\not\in J}B_{j}
) for some
J\subseteq I
(by convention,
B_{\emptyset}=X
and
B^{\circ}_{\emptyset}=X\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)
).

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log sub-pair. A proper birational morphism
\pi\colon(Y,\Delta_{Y})\rightarrow(X,\Delta)
from a sub-pair is called
crepant
if
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
, in which case we also call
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
the
crepant pullback
of
(X,\Delta)
(via
\pi
).

An
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on a variety
X
is
big
if
D=A+E
for some ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
and some
E\geq 0
. Let
X\to Z
be a morphism. We say
D
is
big over
Z
if
D\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}D^{\prime}
for some big
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D^{\prime}
on
X
.

An
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on a projective variety
X
is
pseudo-effective
if its numerical class is a limit of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
D_{i}\geq 0
(
i=1,2,\dots
). Given a projective morphism
X\to Z
, we say
D
is
pseudo-effective over
Z
if its restriction to the fiber
X_{\eta}
is pseudo-effective, where
\eta\in Z
is the image of the generic point of
X
.

A
fibration
is a proper surjective morphism
f\colon X\to Z
of normal varieties such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
. If
X\to Z
is a fibration and
(X,\Delta)
is a pair, by abuse of notation we also call
(X,\Delta)\to Z
a fibration.

Let
X
be a variety. For any proper birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
and any prime divisor
E
on
Y
, we call
E
a
(prime) divisor over

X
and denote by
\mathrm{Center}_{X}(E):=\pi(E)
the
center
of
E
on
X
. We also denote by
c_{X}(E)
the generic point of
\mathrm{Center}_{X}(E)
and sometimes also call it the center of
E
if no confusion arises. We also call
E
as a
(prime) divisor over

x\in X
where
x
is the generic point of
\pi(E)
. We denote by
\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)\subseteq Y
the exceptional locus of
\pi
. The
discrepancy
of
E
with respect to a log sub-pair
(X,\Delta)
, denoted by
a(E,X,\Delta)
, is defined by

a(E,X,\Delta):=\mathrm{mult}_{E}(K_{Y}-\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)).

The notation of
terminal
,
klt
,
dlt
, and
log canonical
(sub-)pairs are defined as in
[
21
, Definitions 2.34 and 2.37]
. Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log canonical pair. A subvariety
W\subseteq X
is called a
log canonical center
if either
W=X
, or
W
is the center of some divisor
E
over
X
with
a(E,X,\Delta)=-1
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any log canonical sub-pair
(X,\Delta)
, we define

\mathrm{mld}(X,\Delta):=\inf\{a(E,X,\Delta)+1\mid E\text{ is a prime divisor over }X\}

as the
total minimal log discrepancy
(
total mld
) of
(X,\Delta)
.

2.2.
Minimal Model Program (MMP)

We refer to e.g.
[
21
, Paragraph 3.31]
for a description of the MMP. In this subsection, we recall some results on the MMP that will be used in this paper.

Definition 2.2
.

A birational map
\varphi\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
of log pairs is called an
MMP type contraction
if
\varphi^{-1}
has no exceptional divisor, and there exist proper birational morphisms
f\colon Y\to X
,
g\colon Y\to X^{\prime}
such that

f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)-g^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})\geq 0

and
g=\varphi\circ f
. In particular, we have
\varphi_{*}\Delta\geq\Delta^{\prime}
.

As the name suggests, if
\varphi\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is obtained by running a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP, then
\varphi\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is an MMP type contraction. The following index bound is proved in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair where
\Delta
is big. Then there exist a positive integer
r
depending only on
(X,\Delta)
such that for any MMP type contraction
\varphi\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
with
\Delta^{\prime}=\varphi_{*}\Delta
, the Cartier index of any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on
X^{\prime}
is at most
r
.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

We will frequently use the following observation, which is well known to the experts (
cf.

[
2
, Lemma 3.7.5 and Proof of Theorem 1.2]
), to reduce questions about general type MMPs to MMPs with a big boundary.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair and
X\rightarrow T
a morphism.

(1)

If
K_{X}+\Delta
is big over
T
, then there exists a klt pair
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
, such that
K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},T}\mu(K_{X}+\Delta)
for some
\mu>0
and
\Delta^{\prime}
is big.

(2)

If
\Delta
is big over
T
, then there exists a klt pair
(X,\Delta_{0})
such that
K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{R},T}K_{X}+\Delta_{0}
and
\Delta_{0}\geq A\geq 0
for some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
that is ample over
T
. ∎

Corollary 2.12

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair of dimension

. Let

be the smallest positive discrepancy of
(X,\Delta)
(i.e. for any prime divisor

over

). Then in any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP, the Cartier index of any

-Cartier Weil divisor is at most

\left(\left\lceil\frac{e_{+}(X,\Delta)+3}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil!\right)^{2^{1+e_{+}(X,\Delta)}}.

Proof.

Let
(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
be a sequence of steps of a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP. We first prove that
\mathrm{mld}(X,\Delta)\geq\varepsilon
. In fact, by passing to the terminalization, it suffices to prove this when
(X,\Delta)
is terminal. Write

where the

are irreducible components of
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)

\mathrm{mld}(X,\Delta)=1-\max_{i}\{b_{i}\}

Let

. By definition, every divisor

over

with

also satisfies

. As

is a

-MMP, we have

, hence any divisor

∎

4.
Explicit termination

In this section, we give explicit bounds (see Corollary

, Theorems

,

, and

) on the number of steps in various types of MMP in dimension at most

. This improves Theorem

, which only gives the existence of an inexplicit upper bound.

4.1.
Invariants

Before we state the result, we need to introduce a few invariants that we shall use to formulate the explicit bound and recall some of their basic properties.

Definition 4.1

(Picard number)
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety. Let
j\colon X\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
be a smooth projective compactification. We set
N^{1}(X):=\mathrm{Pic}(X)/j^{*}\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(\overline{X})
and define the
Picard number
of
X
as

\rho(X):=\dim N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}.

This is well-defined since
j^{*}\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(\overline{X})
is independent of the compactification. In general, for any normal variety
X
, we define
\rho(X):=\rho(U)
where
U
is the smooth locus of
X
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties. Then
\rho(X)=\rho(Z)+\ell
, where
\ell
is the number of
\pi
-exceptional divisors.

Proof.

If
X
is smooth, this follows from
[
1
, Lemma 1.6(1)]
. The general case can be reduced to this by considering a common resolution of
X
and
Z
.
∎

Definition 4.3

(Algebraic Betti number)
.

Let
X
be a variety and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)
the subspace in the singular homology group

H_{2m}(X,\mathbb{R})
generated by the the classes of
m
-dimensional complete algebraic subvarieties. We denote by
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)
the dimension of
H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a projective birational morphism of normal varieties such that
\dim\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)\leq m
. Then

(4.1)

h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)+\min\{1,\ell\}\leq h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)\leq h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)+\ell

where
\ell
is the number of
m
-dimensional complete irreducible components of
\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we abbreviate all singular homology groups
H_{*}(-,\mathbb{R})
as
H_{*}(-)
in this proof.

As
\pi
is projective, every complete subvariety of
Z
is the image of some complete subvariety of
X
of the same dimension. Thus the natural homomorphism
\pi_{*}\colon H_{2m}(X)\to H_{2m}(Z)
induces a surjection
H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)\twoheadrightarrow H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)
. In particular,
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)\leq h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)
. For any complete subvariety
V\subseteq X
of dimension
m
and any projective compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
, we have
[V]\neq 0\in H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(\overline{X})
, hence
[V]\neq 0\in H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)
as well. If
\pi
contracts at least one complete subvariety of dimension
m
(i.e.
\ell\geq 1
), then it follows that
\ker(\pi_{*})\neq 0
and we get
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)\leq h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)-1
. This proves the first inequality in (
4.1
).

Let
W=\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)
. By assumption,
\dim\pi(W)\leq m-1
, thus
H_{i}(\pi(W))=0
for any
i>2m-2
. By the long exact sequence of homology we deduce that
H_{2m}(Z)\cong H_{2m}(Z,\pi(W))
where
H_{2m}(Z,\pi(W))
is the relative homology. Since
X\setminus W\cong Z\setminus\pi(W)
, by excision we also have
H_{2m}(Z,\pi(W))\cong H_{2m}(X,W)
. Combining with the long exact sequence of homology we see that

H_{2m}(W)\to H_{2m}(X)\to H_{2m}(X,W)\cong H_{2m}(Z)

is exact. Note that
\dim H_{2m}(W)=\ell
by the Mayer–Vietoris sequence and the fact that for any variety
V
of dimension at most
m
, we have

\dim H_{2m}(V)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if $V$ is complete and }\dim V=m,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that
\ker(\pi_{*})\leq\ell

and hence
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(X)\leq h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2m}(Z)+\ell
. This proves the other inequality in (
4.1
).
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair of dimension
n
and let
\widetilde{B}
be the normalization of
B:=\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)
. We define

(4.2)

\rho(X,\Delta):=\rho(X)+\rho(\widetilde{B})+h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2n-4}(X).

Following
[

]
, we call an exceptional divisor

with

if its center

is a codimension one point in the smooth locus of

. We also define

(4.3)

d(X,\Delta):=\#\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}\mbox{Exceptional prime divisor $E$ over $X$ with}\\
\mbox{$a(E,X,\Delta)<1$ that is not an echo of $(X,\Delta)$}\end{tabular}\right\}.

Note that if
(X,\Delta)
is terminal, then

by
[
22
]
*Lemma (4.14.2.1) or
[
1
]
*Lemma 1.5.
Finally, we define

(4.4)

s(X,\Delta):=\min_{(Y,D)}\{\rho(Y,D)+d(Y,D)\}

where the minimum runs over all terminal pairs
(Y,D)
with a projective birational morphism
\pi\colon(Y,D)\to(X,\Delta)
such that
K_{Y}+D=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+E
for some
\pi
-exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

.

Since the last invariant

will play a major role in controlling the number of steps in the MMP, we give an explicit upper bound of

at least for log smooth pairs. We also bound the invariant

defined after (

).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(X,\Delta=\sum b_{i}B_{i})
be a log smooth pair of dimension
n
. Let
N
be a positive integer. Assume that
b_{i}\leq 1-\frac{1}{N}
for all
i
,
(X,\Delta)
has at most
N
strata, each stratum has Picard number at most
N
, and
h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\leq N
. Then

e_{+}(X,\Delta)<N^{n+1}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad s(X,\Delta)<3N^{2n+3}.

Proof.

The result is clear when
N=1
: in this case
\Delta=0
and
\rho(X)=h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)=1
by assumption, which gives
e_{+}(X,\Delta)=d(X,\Delta)=0
(as
(X,\Delta)
is already terminal) and
s(X,\Delta)=2
by definition. Thus we may assume that
N\geq 2
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
(Y,D)
of
(X,\Delta)
can be constructed by repeatedly blowing up the center of some prime exceptional divisor
E
over
X
with
a(E,X,\Delta)\leq 0
until all such divisors have been extracted. Note that the center of such a divisor
E
is necessarily a stratum at each step (this follows from e.g. the calculations in
[
21
, Corollary 2.31]
). For each stratum
W\subseteq B_{J}
, every divisor
E
with center
W
and
a(E,X,\Delta)\leq 0
can also be obtained by a weighted blowup with some weights
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
along
B_{j}
. Since

a(E,X,\Delta)=-1+\sum_{j\in J}(1-b_{j})m_{j}

for the exceptional divisor
E
of such a weighted blowup, and
b_{i}\leq 1-\frac{1}{N}
by assumption, we see that
m_{j}\leq N
and hence for each stratum
W
there are no more than
N^{n}
divisors
E
with center
W
and
a(E,X,\Delta)\leq 0
. As there at no more than
N
strata and one of them is
X
, we deduce that the
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on the terminalization has less than
N^{n+1}
components, and we only need to blowup less than
N^{n+1}
times to obtain the terminalization
Y
. In particular,
e_{+}(X,\Delta)<N^{n+1}
.

At the initial stage (i.e. on
X
), the intersection of any two strata has at most
N
connected components, and this number does not increase under the blowup process. This implies that after each stratum blowup, the Picard number of a stratum can increase at most
N
, hence after
\ell
blowups, the Picard number of any stratum is at most
(\ell+1)N
. It follows that every stratum of
(Y,D)
has Picard number at most
N^{n+2}
; in particular,
\rho(Y)\leq N^{n+2}
, and if
\widetilde{B}_{Y}
is the normalization of
B_{Y}:=\mathrm{Supp}(D)
, then

\rho(\widetilde{B}_{Y})\leq N^{n+2}(N^{n+1}-1)

as
D
has less than
N^{n+1}
components. On the other hand, by
[
32
, Theorem 7.31]
, if we blowup a stratum of Picard number
\rho
, then
h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}
increases by at most
\rho+1
. By assumption,
h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\leq N
, hence

h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(Y)\leq h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)+(N+1)+(2N+1)+\dots+((N^{n+1}-1)N+1)<N^{2n+3}.

Putting the above together we see that

\rho(Y,D)=\rho(Y)+\rho(\widetilde{B}_{Y})+h_{2n-4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(Y)<N^{n+2}+N^{n+2}(N^{N+1}-1)+N^{2n+3}=2N^{2n+3}.

Next observe that by
[
23
, Corollary 3.2(iii)]
, every divisor
E
over
Y
with
a(E,Y,D)=a(E,X,\Delta)<1
that is not an echo of
(Y,D)
is necessarily obtained by a weighted blowup along some stratum of
(X,\Delta)
, as the assumptions of
loc. cit.
is satisfied after blowing up some strata. For each fixed stratum
W\subseteq B_{J}
, if
m_{j}
(
j\in J
) are the weights along
B_{j}
, then

\sum_{j\in J}(1-b_{j})m_{j}=1+a(E,Y,D)<2.

As
b_{i}\leq 1-\frac{1}{N}
, we have
m_{j}<2N
, hence there are at most
(2N)^{n}
divisors
E
with
a(E,X,\Delta)<1
that are centered at any given stratum. Since there are no more than
N
strata, we see that
d(Y,D)\leq 2^{n}N^{n+1}
and (since
N\geq 2
)

s(X,\Delta)\leq\rho(Y,D)+d(Y,D)<2N^{2n+3}+2^{n}N^{n+1}<3N^{2n+3},

which concludes the proof.
∎

We start with some general setup. For a terminal pair

is defined in (

This suggests that if we define an “

Theorem 4.8
.

The following elementary lemma has been used in the proof above.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\,\,(i=0,\dots,m)
and
M>1
be such that
a_{i}\geq b_{i}
and
c_{i}-b_{i}\leq(M-1)\sum_{0\leq j<i}(a_{j}-b_{j})
for all
i
. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{m}M^{-i}c_{i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}M^{-i}a_{i}

with equality if and only if
a_{i}=b_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i
.

In the applications above, the sequence
a_{i}
(resp.
b_{i}
, resp.
c_{i}
) represents the sequence
(\rho_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{k+1},\rho_{k+1})
(resp.
(\bar{\rho}_{0},\bar{d}_{1},\dots,\bar{d}_{k+1},\bar{\rho}_{k+1})
, resp.
(\rho^{\prime}_{0},d^{\prime}_{1},\dots,d^{\prime}_{k+1},\rho^{\prime}_{k+1})
).

Proof.

We may assume that
b_{i}=0
by replacing
a_{i}
(resp.
c_{i}
) with
a_{i}-b_{i}
(resp.
c_{i}-b_{i}
). The assumption then becomes
a_{i}\geq 0
and
c_{i}\leq(M-1)\sum_{0\leq j<i}a_{j}
. It follows that
M^{-i}c_{i}\leq(1-\frac{1}{M})\sum_{0\leq j<i}M^{1-i}a_{j}
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}M^{-i}c_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{M}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{0\leq j<i}M^{1-i}a_{j}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{M}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=j}^{+\infty}M^{-i}a_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}M^{-j}a_{j}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m}M^{-j}a_{j}.

The second and the last equality hold if and only if
a_{i}=0
for all
i
. If this is the case, then the first equality holds if and only if
c_{i}=(M-1)\sum_{0\leq j<i}a_{j}=0
for all
i
.
∎

To prove Corollary

Lemma 4.11
.

We have
\rho(X,\Delta)+d(X,\Delta)\leq\delta_{M}(X,\Delta)

The first inequality is clear from the definition (

. If

, then as

for all

\displaystyle\delta_{M}(X,\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq M^{2k+2}\rho(X,\Delta)+(M^{2k+1}+M^{2k-1}+\dots+M)\cdot|S|\cdot d(X,\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq M^{2k+2}\rho(X,\Delta)+(M^{2k+1}+M^{2k-1}+\dots+M)\cdot(M-1)\cdot d(X,\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq M^{2k+2}(\rho(X,\Delta)+d(X,\Delta)),

which gives the second inequality.

.
∎

4.3.
MMP with discrete discrepancies

Our next goal is to give explicit bounds for klt MMP in dimension
3
, or in dimension
4
when one of
K_{X}+\Delta
or
\Delta
is big. A common feature in these cases is that the log discrepancies belong to some fixed finite set during the MMP (
cf.

[
16
, Section 3]
). To give a more uniform treatment, we first prove an explicit termination statement by fixing this set. See Definition
4.5
for the definition of
s(X,\Delta)
and
e(X,\Delta)
that appear in the statement below.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
S\subseteq[0,1]
be a finite set such that
0,1\in S
and for any
a,b\in S
with
a+b\leq 1
, we have
a+b\in S
.
Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair of dimension
n\in\{3,4\}
. Let

(4.16)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\Delta)=:(X_{0},\Delta_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{1},\Delta_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{i},\Delta_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{i}}

be an
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP such that for any divisor
E
over
X
and any
i
with
a(E,X_{i},\Delta_{i})<0
, we have
-a(E,X_{i},\Delta_{i})\in S
.
Then this MMP terminates after at most

M^{2(k+1)^{2}|S|}s(X,\Delta)

steps, where
k=e_{+}(X,\Delta)
and
M=\left(2+\left\lceil\frac{1}{\min S\setminus\{0\}}\right\rceil\cdot\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\max S\setminus\{1\}}\right\rceil\right)\cdot|S|
.

Before proving this we make an elementary observation.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a terminal pair and let
0\leq D\leq\Delta
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor such that
K_{X}+D
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. Then
\rho(X,D)\leq\rho(X,\Delta)
and
d(X,D)\leq d(X,\Delta)
.

Proof.

As
0\leq D\leq\Delta
, every component of
D
is also a component of
\Delta
, hence the first inequality is clear from the definition. Since
a(E,X,\Delta)\leq a(E,X,D)
holds for any prime divisor
E
over
X
, to prove the second inequality it suffices to show that if
E
is an exceptional divisor with
a(E,X,D)<1
and it is not an echo of
(X,D)
, then it is also not an echo of
(X,\Delta)
. Suppose that
E
is an echo for
(X,\Delta)
, then its center
\xi
is a codimension one smooth point of
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)
. Note that in this case
\xi\in\mathrm{Supp}(D)
, otherwise
a(E,X,D)=a(E,X)\geq 1
since
X
is terminal and hence smooth at
\xi
. It follows that
\xi
is also a codimension one smooth point of
\mathrm{Supp}(D)
and hence an echo for
(X,D)
, a contradiction. This gives the second inequality.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.12
.

Let
\pi\colon(Y,D)\to(X,\Delta)
be a projective birational morphism from a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal pair such that
K_{Y}+D=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+E
for some
\pi
-exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E\geq 0
. In other words,
\mathrm{mult}_{F}(D)\geq\min\{0,-a(F,X,\Delta)\}
for all prime divisors
F
on
Y
. We need to prove that the MMP (
4.16
) terminates after at most
M^{2(k+1)^{2}|S|}(\rho(Y,D)+d(Y,D))
steps. By Lemma
4.13
, we may decrease the coefficients of
D
and assume that

\mathrm{mult}_{F}(D)=\min\{0,-a(F,X,\Delta)\}

for all prime divisors
F
on
Y
. In particular, we may assume that
\mathrm{Coeff}(D)\subseteq S
.

By Lemma
2.8
, we can lift the given
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP to a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y,D)=:(Y_{0},D_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y_{1},D_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y_{i},D_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\Delta)=:(X_{0},\Delta_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{1},\Delta_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{i},\Delta_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}=\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{i}}

where each
\pi_{i}
(
i\geq 1
) is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
(X_{i},\Delta_{i})
and each
\psi_{i}
(
i\geq 0
) is composed of a sequence of
(K_{Y_{i}}+B_{i})
-MMP where
B_{i}=(\psi_{i}^{-1})_{*}D_{i+1}\leq D_{i}
. In particular,
\psi_{i}
does not contract any component of
B_{i}
. Let
\delta_{i}:=\delta_{M}(Y_{i},D_{i})
which is defined by (
4.8
). At each step we have two possibilities:

(1)

B_{i}=D_{i}
. In this case
\delta_{i+1}\leq\delta_{i}-1
by Theorem
4.8
.

(2)

B_{i}\neq D_{i}
. By Lemma
4.13
and Lemma
4.11
, we have
\delta_{M}(Y_{i},B_{i})\leq M^{2k+2}\delta_{M}(Y_{i},D_{i})
, hence Theorem
4.8
gives
\delta_{i+1}\leq\delta_{M}(Y_{i},B_{i})\leq M^{2k+2}\delta_{i}
(note that in this case it might be possible that
Y_{i+1}=Y_{i}
).

By construction
\mathrm{Supp}(D_{i})
has only
k
components, and
\mathrm{Coeff}(B_{i})\cup\mathrm{Coeff}(D_{i})\subseteq S
for all
i
, hence the second case can only happen at most
k\cdot|S|
times since we always have
0\leq B_{i}\leq D_{i}
. If we rescale the sequence
\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots
by setting
\tilde{\delta}_{i}:=M^{(2k+2)\ell_{i}}\delta_{i}
where
\ell_{i}=\#\{j\geq i\,|\,B_{j}\neq D_{j}\}
, then the above analysis gives
\tilde{\delta}_{i+1}\leq\tilde{\delta}_{i}
for all
i
and the equality holds at most
k\cdot|S|
times. From here we see that the MMP (
4.16
) must terminate after at most

\tilde{\delta}_{0}\leq M^{(2k+2)k\cdot|S|}\delta_{0}+k\cdot|S|\leq M^{2(k+1)^{2}|S|}(\rho(Y,D)+d(Y,D))

steps, where the last inequality is by Lemma
4.11
. This proves the desired result.
∎

We now put together the results obtained in this section and give some concrete applications. We shall only consider MMP that starts with a log smooth pair, since for any klt pair
(X,\Delta)
, we can always choose a log resolution
\pi\colon Y\to X
and a log smooth klt pair
(Y,D)
such that
K_{Y}+D\geq\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+E
for some
\pi
-exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E\geq 0
, and any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP can be lifted to a
(K_{Y}+D)
-MMP by Lemma
2.6
. Moreover, if
K_{X}+\Delta
(resp.
\Delta
) is big, then we can choose the log smooth model
(Y,D)
so that
K_{Y}+D
(resp.
D
) is big.

The first application is about fourfold general type MMPs.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective log smooth klt pair of dimension
4
such that
N\Delta
has integer coefficients. Assume that

(1)

(X,\Delta)
has at most
N
strata, each stratum has Picard number at most
N
, and
h_{4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\leq N
,

(2)

K_{X}+\Delta
is big,
\mathrm{vol}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq\frac{1}{N}
, and
((K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot H^{3})\leq N
for some very ample divisor
H
on
X
.

Then any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates after at most
M^{M}
steps, where
M:=(2N)^{9}!
.

Proof.

By
[
16
, Corollary 3.20]
, we know that in any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP, the Cartier index of any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor is at most
(2^{8}N^{9})!
. Since
N\Delta
has integer coefficients, we see that the assumptions of Lemma
4.12
are satisfied if we set

S:=[0,1]\cap\frac{1}{N\cdot(2^{8}N^{9})!}\mathbb{R}.

Let
N_{1}:=N\cdot(2^{8}N^{9})!
. Then
|S|=N_{1}+1
and
\min(S\setminus\{0\})=1-\max(S\setminus\{1\})=\frac{1}{N_{1}}
. By Lemma
4.6
, we also have
e(X,\Delta)\leq N^{5}-1
and
s(X,\Delta)\leq 3N^{11}
. Thus by Lemma
4.12
we see that the MMP terminates after at most

(2N_{1}+N_{1}^{3})^{2N^{10}(N_{1}+1)}\cdot 3N^{11}\leq M^{M}

steps.
∎

The same argument also works when the boundary
\Delta
is big.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective log smooth klt pair of dimension
4
such that
N\Delta
has integer coefficients. Assume that

(1)

(X,\Delta)
has at most
N
strata, each stratum has Picard number at most
N
, and
h_{4}^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)\leq N
,

(2)

\Delta
is big,
\mathrm{vol}(\Delta)\geq\frac{1}{N}
, and
(\Delta\cdot H^{3})\leq N
for some very ample divisor
H
on
X
.

Then any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates after at most
M^{M}
steps, where
M:=(2N)^{9}!
.

Proof.

This follows from the same proof of Theorem
4.14
, replacing
[
16
, Corollary 3.20]
by
[
16
, Corollary 3.18]
.
∎

Finally, we give an explicit bound on the length of threefold MMPs. It is not clear if such an explicit termination should be expected in dimension
\geq 4
, see
[
30
, Example 12]
for some discussions.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective log smooth klt pair of dimension
3
such that
N\Delta
has integer coefficients,
(X,\Delta)
has at most
N
strata, and each stratum has Picard number at most
N
. Then any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates after at most

3(N\cdot M!)^{8N^{8}(M!+1)}

steps, where
M:=((N^{5}+2N)!)^{2^{N^{4}}}
.

Proof.

Since
N\Delta
has integer coefficients, any positive discrepancy of
(X,\Delta)
is at least
\frac{1}{N}
. For smooth threefolds we also have
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2}(X)=\rho(X)
(indeed, the invariant
h^{\mathrm{alg}}_{2}(X)
does not play any role in proving the termination of
3
-fold MMP, but we don’t need this fact). By Lemma
4.6
, we see that
e(X,\Delta)\leq N^{4}-1
and
s(X,\Delta)\leq 3N^{9}
. Thus by Corollary
2.12
, the Cartier index of any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor is at most
M
. Then the assumptions of Lemma
4.12
are satisfied by
S:=[0,1]\cap\frac{1}{N\cdot M!}\mathbb{R}
, and it follows that the MMP terminates after at most

((2+(N\cdot M!)^{2})\cdot N\cdot M!)^{2N^{8}(M!+1)}\cdot 3N^{9}\leq 3(N\cdot M!)^{8N^{8}(M!+1)}

steps.
∎

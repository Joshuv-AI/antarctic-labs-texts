The dib-chromatic number of digraphs
Nahid Y. Javier-Nol, Christian Rubio-Montiel, Ingrid Torres-Ramos

Abstract
We study an extension to directed graphs of the parameter called the b b -chromatic number of a graph in terms of acyclic vertex colorings: the dib-chromatic number. We give general bounds for this parameter. We also show some results about tournaments and regular digraphs.



Keywords.
Complete coloring, acyclic coloring, b-chromatic number, Nordhaus-Gaddum relations, directed graph.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
05C15, 05C20.

Introduction

In this paper, we introduce the concept of the dib-chromatic number of a digraph, which is a generalization of the b-chromatic number of a graph. The term
b
-chromatic number of a digraph is reserved for the
b
-chromatic number of the underlying graph of a given digraph; see
[
11
]
.

To begin with, we recall concepts about graphs. A coloring of a graph
G
is
proper
if each color class induces an empty subgraph. The
chromatic number

\chi(G)
of
G
is the smallest natural number
k
such that
G
admits a proper coloring with
k
colors.

A
b
-coloring
of a graph
G
with
k
colors is a coloring of the vertices of
G
such that in each color class there exists a vertex having neighbors in all the other
k-1
color classes. Such a vertex will be called a
b
-vertex
. The
b
-chromatic number

b(G)
of a graph
G
is the largest
k
for which
G
has a proper
b
-coloring with
k
colors
[
9
]
.

This parameter belongs to a family of parameters that comes from complete colorings, namely, the chromatic number, the Grundy number, and the achromatic number (and their no-proper versions). A coloring of a graph
G
is
complete
if for every pair of colors
i
and
j
there exists an edge
uv
such that
u
is colored
i
and
v
is colored
j
. In fact, any proper coloring of
G
with
\chi(G)
colors is complete.

Another way to understand this parameter comes from algorithmic graph theory. Suppose a graph with a given proper coloring. We would like to perform an operation to reduce the number of colors as follows. Recolor its vertices, but not necessarily each one, with the same color. Clearly, such recoloring is not possible if each color class contains a
b
-vertex. Hence, the
b
-chromatic number of the graph serves as the worst case for the number of colors used by this coloring heuristic method.

Next, we define digraph, the dib-chromatic number of a digraph, and we recall some valuable results about the digrundy and diachromatic numbers.

In this paper, we consider simple, loopless, and finite digraphs. A
digraph

D
is a pair
(V,A)
where
V
is the set of vertices and
A
is the set of darts. A
dart
(or
arc
) is a pair
(u,v)
of vertices; for short, we write
uv
, such that
u\not=v
.

A
coloring
of a digraph
D
is
acyclic
if each color class induces a subdigraph without directed cycles. The
dichromatic number

dc(D)
of a digraph
D
is the smallest number
k
such that
D
admits an acyclic coloring with
k
colors
[
15
]
.

A coloring of a digraph
D
is
complete
if for every pair of different colors
(i,j)
there exists at least one dart
uv
such that
u
is colored
i
and
v
is colored
j

[
8
]
(see also
[
6
,
7
]
). Any acyclic coloring of
D
with

colors is also a complete coloring. The diachromatic number

of

is the largest number of colors for which there exists a complete and acyclic coloring of
D

[
1
]
.

Let

and

be two vertices of a digraph

such that

is a dart of

. We say that

is

u
, and
incident to

, while

is

is
incident from

. The out-degree

of a vertex

. The degree

. We also say that

is
incident to

is
incident from

A vertex

-vertex

is incident to a vertex colored with each color different from the color of

A

-vertex and a

-vertex.
We define the

-coloring with

For any digraph

-coloring of

is also a complete coloring, and then

(1)

If

(2)

Moreover, for an induced subdigraph

(3)

A dart

is symmetric if

and asymmetric if

. Two vertices

are adjacent if the dart

is symmetric. A digraph is symmetric (resp. asymmetric) if every arc of

is symmetric (resp. asymmetric). If

For example,
complete symmetric digraph

This paper is organized into three sections: The second section contains general bounds such as the Nordhaus-Gaddum relations. The third section deals with tournaments and fourth section with the dib-chromatic number in regular digraphs.

Bounds

A
clique
of a digraph
D
is an induced subdigraph of

which is a complete symmetric subdigraph. A

of

is a clique such that there is no clique with more vertices, and the

of

\omega(D)\leq dc(D)
.

Let

be a digraph of
n

of

[
1
,

]
.

It is not hard to see that a greedy acyclic coloring of a graph

using
k
colors is a coloring of

having the property that for every two colors

and
j
with
i<j
, every vertex colored

has a neighbor colored

. Note that for each vertex

in the color class

and each color class

, with
i<j
,
N^{+}(v)\cap V_{i}\not=\emptyset
and

then

, see also
[

]
.

Let
\varsigma
be a

-coloring of a digraph

using
k

be any

-vertex of color

is a

of
\varsigma

be any

-vertex colored

; we say that the set

of

. A

-coloring may have many positive and negative bases, and the intersection between positive and negative bases can be empty or not.

The following result is a generalization of a result in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
D
be a digraph of order
n
. Assume that the vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
of
D
are ordered such that
deg^{+}(u_{1})\geq deg^{+}(u_{2})\geq\dots\geq deg^{+}(u_{n})
. Let
t^{+}(D):=\max\{i\colon deg^{+}(u_{i})\geq i-1\}
be the maximum number
i
such that
D
contains at least
i
vertices of out-degree at least
i-1
. Now, re-label the vertices as
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
such that
deg^{-}(v_{1})\geq deg^{-}(v_{2})\geq\dots\geq deg^{-}(v_{n})
. Let
t^{-}(D):=\max\{i\colon deg^{-}(v_{i})\geq i-1\}
be the maximum number
i
such that
D
contains at least
i
vertices of in-degree at least
i-1
. Therefore,

dib(D)\leq t(D):=\min\{t^{+}(D),t^{-}(D)\}.

Proof.

The proof is by contradiction. Suppose
dib(D)\geq t(D)+1
then there exist at least
dib(D)
vertices of out-degree and at least
dib(D)
vertices of in-degree no smaller than

dib(D)-1\geq t(D).

Therefore,

deg^{+}(u_{1}),deg^{+}(u_{2}),\dots,deg^{+}(u_{t+1})\geq t(D)

and

deg^{-}(v_{1}),deg^{-}(v_{2}),\dots,deg^{-}(v_{t+1})\geq t(D),

are a contradiction to the definition of
t(D)
.
∎

The
complement

of a digraph

and where

In

]

for any digraph of order

The following result is a generalization of a result in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2
.

If
D
is a digraph of order
n
, then

dib(D)+dib(D^{c})\leq n+1.

Proof.

Consider
b
-colorings of
D
and
D^{c}
with
dib(D)
and
dib(D^{c})
colors, respectively. Observe that for any
b^{+}
-vertex

in

and then
deg^{+}_{D^{c}}(u)\leq n-dib(D)
. We denote the color class of
D
with the color
i
by
C_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,dib(D)\}
and

is a
b^{+}
-vertex in

.

Case 1. There exists a color
i
such that for every

in
C_{i}
,

is a
b^{+}
-vertex in

. In particular,
u_{i}
is a

-vertex in

and

, therefore

and then the result follows.

Case 2. For every

such that it is not a

-vertex in

∎

An

Corollary 3

For any digraph

dib(D)\leq n-\beta(D)+1.

Since

Theorem 4
.

For every digraph

dac(D)\leq\frac{n+\omega(D)}{2}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\omega(D)=k
and
dac(G)=l
. Then

. So, there exists a complete acyclic coloring of

with

The

of

is the largest possible order of an acyclic subdigraph of

Theorem 5
.

For every digraph

of order

\frac{n}{\mathcal{A}(D)}\leq dc(D)\leq n-\mathcal{A}(D)+1.

Proof.

Suppose that

with

a partition given by

Next, let

∎

Finally, we give the following bound.

Corollary 6
.

For every digraph

of order

,

dac(D)\leq\left\lceil n-\frac{\mathcal{A}(D)}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Since

and

then

∎

Corollary

The

A

Corollary 7
.

Let

dib(T)=\left\lceil{n}/{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let

is incident to the vertices

is incident from the vertices

and the result follows.

4
On regular digraphs

In this section, we state some results about regular digraphs. We recall that the number of darts in a shortest weak
uv
-path (not necessarily a directed path) is denoted by
d(u,v)
, and if
u
and
v
belong to different components of a week disconnected digraph, then
d(u,v)=\infty
.

The following two theorems are generalizations of results in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
D
be a digraph, with
\Delta(D)=\Delta
, containing sets of vertices
B^{+}=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\Delta+1}\}
and
B^{-}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\Delta+1}\}
with
deg^{+}(u_{i})=\Delta
and
deg^{-}(v_{i})=\Delta
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\Delta+1\}
such that
d(x,y)\geq 4
for any
x,y\in B^{+}\cup B^{-}
. Therefore,
dib(D)=\Delta+1
.

Proof.

First, we proceed to give a coloring. If
|B^{+}\cap B^{-}|=l
, assume that they are the first
l
elements of each set, i.e.,
u_{1}=v_{1},\ldots,u_{l}=v_{l}
for
0\leq l\leq\Delta+1
.

The color
i
is assigned to the vertices
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\Delta+1\}
. Then, for each dart
u_{i}z
, the vertex
z
is colored by a color of
\{1,\ldots,\Delta+1\}-\{i\}
such that the vertices incident from
u_{i}
have different colors. Next, for each dart
zv_{i}
the vertex
z
(consider only uncolored vertices) is colored with a color from
\{1,\ldots,\Delta+1\}-\{i\}
such that the vertices incident from
u_{i}
have different colors.

Finally, we color the remaining vertices of the digraph following a greedy acyclic coloring. Therefore, each color class is acyclic and the result follows.
∎

A digraph
D
is called

if
deg^{+}(v)=deg^{-}(v)=r
for every vertex

Theorem 15
.

Let
D
be an
r

and at least

vertices then
dib(D)=r+1
.

Proof.

We find
r+1

v_{1},\ldots,v_{r+1}
such that
d(v_{i},v_{j})\geq 4
for all
i\neq j
as follows.
Let
v_{1}
be an arbitrary vertex and delete it together with the first, second, and third neighborhoods, then the number of deleted vertices is at most
1+2r+2r(2r-1)+2r(2r-1)^{2}=1+2r-4r^{2}+8r^{3}\in o(r^{4})
. Now, we choose a vertex
v_{2}
(then
d(v_{2},v_{1})\geq 4
in
D
) and delete it together with the first, second, and third neighborhood, then the total number of vertices deleted is at most
1+2r-4r^{2}+8r^{3}
. So, we can repeat this process for vertices
v_{3},\ldots,v_{r}
. Since
r(1+2r-4r^{2}+8r^{3})<8r^{4}
, there remains at least a vertex
v_{r+1}
. Due to
d(v_{i},v_{j})\geq 4
for
i>j
and by Theorem
14
,
dib(D)=r+1
.

∎

Visual Abstract Thinking Empowers Multimodal Reasoning
Dairu Liu, Ziyue Wang, Minyuan Ruan, Fuwen Luo, Chi Chen, Peng Li, Yang Liu

Abstract
Images usually convey richer detail than text, but often include redundant information which potentially downgrades multimodal reasoning performance. When faced with lengthy or complex messages, humans tend to employ abstract thinking to convert them into simple and concise abstracts. Inspired by this cognitive strategy, we introduce V isual A bstract T hinking ( VAT ), a novel thinking paradigm that prompts Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with visual abstract instead of explicit verbal thoughts or elaborate guidance, permitting a more concentrated visual reasoning mechanism. Explicit thinking, such as Chain-of-thought (CoT) or tool-augmented approaches, increases the complexity of reasoning process via inserting verbose intermediate steps, external knowledge or visual information. In contrast, VAT reduces redundant visual information and encourages models to focus their reasoning on more essential visual elements. Experimental results show that VAT consistently empowers different models, and achieves an average gain of 17% over GPT-4o baseline by employing diverse types of visual abstracts, compared with 13% of CoT, demonstrating that VAT better enhances visual reasoning abilities for MLLMs regarding conceptual, structural and relational reasoning tasks. VAT is also compatible with CoT in knowledge-intensive multimodal reasoning tasks. These findings highlight the effectiveness of visual reasoning via abstract thinking and encourage further exploration of more diverse reasoning paradigms from the perspective of human cognition. 1 1 1 Code and data will be available: https://github.com/THUNLP-MT/VAT .

Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT)

Humans demonstrate
visual abstract thinking
by selectively focusing on essential visual elements while disregarding redundant details during visual reasoning. For instance, when counting the number of cars in a photograph, background information is typically ignored. Our objective is to demonstrate that this cognitive strategy can also enhance the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. To emulate
visual abstract thinking
in MLLMs, we transform input images into visual abstract that retain the primary conceptual, relational and structural visual information. The visual abstract can be represented as silhouettes, edge features and free-hand sketches. By converting raw images into visual abstract, such as sketches, it enables the extraction of core concepts from complex and cluttered scenes. These sketches are then provided as complementary visual inputs to guide the reasoning process of MLLMs.

Specifically, we denote the query for a multimodal visual reasoning task as
Q=[I\,;\,T]
, where
I\in\mathcal{I}
,
I
refers to input images,
\mathcal{I}
denotes regular image space, and
T
denotes input text. The model predicted answer is denoted as
A
. The reasoning processes adopted by used CoT-like and tool-augmented methods are described as follows:

\textbf{Chain-of-Thought:}\quad Q\rightarrow(T_{\text{chain}},A),

(1)

\textbf{Tool-Augmented Reasoning:}\quad(Q,V_{c}^{n})\rightarrow A,

(2)

\textbf{Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT):}\quad(Q,V_{a})\rightarrow A.

(3)

Here,
T_{\text{chain}}
denotes a CoT style intermediate reasoning trace expressed in natural language, which primarily belongs to the verbal reasoning space
\mathcal{L}
.
V_{c}
represents a concrete visual trace generated by external tools (e.g., Grounding DINO
liu2024grounding
, SAM
kirillov2023segment
), belonging to the concrete visual space
\mathcal{V}_{c}
. Both types of reasoning traces provide detailed content grounded in linguistic or visual perceptual observations.
In addition, our proposed VAT introduces a visual abstraction
V_{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{a}
, where
\mathcal{V}_{a}
denotes the space of geometric and semantic visual abstractions. Unlike explicit visual traces,
V_{a}
captures conceptual and spatial regularities, such as contours, layouts, and part relations, enabling the model to engage in
visual abstract thinking
.

VAT performs reasoning over a joint space
\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{V}_{a}
, integrating verbal reasoning with visual abstraction. The model internally generates reasoning steps in the language space
\mathcal{L}
, while simultaneously attending to aligned semantic and conceptual visual abstractions in
\mathcal{V}_{a}
, thereby enhancing its capability in visual reasoning tasks.

Visual Abstract Transformation.

To reflect the process of visual abstract thinking for multimodal reasoning tasks, VAT uses image-to-
visual abstract
transformer function
T:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{a}
to converts input images
I
into various forms of visual abstracts
V_{a}
, preserving essential visual concepts, geometric information, silhouettes, and relational features. This transformer function
T
can take diverse forms. In this paper, we employ both code-based conversion approaches, including binary maps and edge-detected outlines, and semantic-based deep learning methods, such as contour style sketch as examples of transformer functions. These abstracted visual representations
V_{a}
are incorporated into the multimodal input context to support reasoning with our
V
isual
A
bstract
T
hinking paradigm(
(Q,V_{a})\rightarrow A
). By transforming complex visual scenes into simplified yet semantically meaningful forms, MLLMs can focus on core concepts and object relationships in visual abstracts(
V_{a}
) for visual reasoning within the language-abstract joint space
\mathcal{L}\cup\mathcal{V}_{a}
.

How visual abstracts assist in reasoning?

The effectiveness of VAT is mainly attributed to its visually intangible form that filters redundant information and extract essential conceptual, contoured, and structural features. The ability to preserve such core features improves progressively from basic pixel-level conversions to more advanced geometric-semantic sketch transformations. As shown in Table
4

But due to less abstraction and information loss, without selectively capture core semantic and geomtric information, its improvement is not as dramatic as the sketch form in most cases. What tasks can benefit form abstractions?

While VAT consistently improves performance, the extent of performance gains from VAT varies across task types.
Figure
4
provides further insight into the impact of VAT across task categories. While the motivation of VAT is to simulate human cognitive process of abstraction, it is especially effective in tasks aligned with natural cognition, such as Rot-Diff in CoSpace, that challenges models with natural scenes from different viewpoints.
Notably, for object-centric task requiring models to focus on specific objects attributes or features, visual abstract thinking larger assists in filtering out irrelevant background clutters. Though achieving improvement in spatial reasoning and commonsense reasoning compared to the GPT-4o baseline, VAT presents relatively limited average advantages over CoT. We conclude that these ability is highly reasoning intensive that require explicit verbal processing. However, for these tasks, VAT can still assist models from the visual side to reduce redundancy. For example, the BLINK-spatial task is significantly improved by binary and PhotoSketch style visual abstracts that keeps the least information among the employed styles, which emphasizes the effectiveness reducing visual information via visual abstracts.
In conclusion, VAT benefits visual reasoning through visual abstracts that stress implicit structural and semantic cues, and is particularly helpful for coarse-grained tasks, while it sometimes provide obscure details via visual abstract, which may limit the performance for tasks requiring fine-grained perception, such as counting small objects in densely packed scenes (e.g., CoSpace-count).

5.4
Compatibility of VAT and CoT

While VAT facilitates visual abstract thinking, CoT elicits verbal explicit reasoning. We examine the compatibility of these two paradigm via additional experiments that combines VAT and CoT, and present detailed results in the Appendix
G
. Incorporating CoT in reasoning-intensive tasks, such as visual planning tasks of CoSpace and commonsense reasoning tasks of MME, can further enhance model performance by introducing an explicit verbal reasoning process to complemetory the visual reasoning of VAT. However, CoT also introduces additional complexity that may conflict with the simplicity and abstraction emphasized by VAT. Consequently, while combining CoT with VAT can lead to performance gains, these improvements are occasionally smaller than those achieved through VAT alone in tasks that are less dependent on explicit reasoning.

TAKEAWAY:
VAT can complement CoT in some knowledge-intensive visual reasoning tasks, to elicit both verbal concrete and visual abstract thinking from different perspective.

5.5
Discussion of Failure Cases

We observe that VAT improves the overall performance across different task categories though, it degrades the model performance on specific tasks regarding different models.
These failures can be categorized into two types: (1) VAT does not effectively reduce visual redundancy. This occurs when the original image is already visually simple or contains minimal noise, leaving limited room for simplification through sketch conversion, and thus offering negligible performance gain; (2) Conversely, VAT may remove critical visual details that are essential for accurate comprehension. In such cases, the MLLM may misidentify objects or miss important contextual information, resulting in wrong responses. Figure

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce the concept of
visual abstract thinking
, and propose a new multimodal reasoning paradigm,
V
isual
A
bstract
T
hinking (
VAT
). VAT utilizes visual abstracts, simplified and concise representations of input images, to convey essential visual elements out from a massy and redundant context. It outperforms investigated verbal and visual prompting methods, and achieves remarkably higher efficiency both in time and cost compared with tool-using methods. VAT is a visual complement for CoT, and can be used together to yield better performance for visual reasoning tasks that intensively require commonsense knowledge. We hope this study could encourage further exploration of more diverse reasoning paradigms from the perspective of human cognition.

Limitation and Future Work

This work evaluated
VAT
on multimodal models, but future research should explore its applicability to general language models, expanding its potential beyond multimodal systems.
Furthermore,
VAT
has not yet been tested in real-world environments, such as embodied scenarios, which present challenges not covered by existing benchmarks. Testing in dynamic, real-world contexts would help assess its practical applicability.

Analysis of Open-Source Model Performance

Compared with CoT, VAT consistently performs better for both models in BLINK tasks that intensively require visual perception. This find also holds for visual, such as those require to recognize directional features, and to count specific objects. However, we notice a significant performance gap bewteen CoT and VAT in rotation-based reasoning tasks of CoSpace.

This performance difference may be explained by the varying cognitive demands of different task types. Visual perception tasks benefit from the semantic simplification provided by VAT, which enables even smaller models to better understand geometric structures and spatial layouts. For example, in tasks such as
Spatial
,
Count
, and
Vis. Corr.
, VAT provides clear improvements over CoT by enhancing the ability of models to extract and align key visual signals.

In contrast, task such as
Odd-One-Out
place emphasis on verbal logical inference, where CoT tends to perform better. One possible explanation is that VAT introduces additional visual abstract images, significantly increasing the input context length. For smaller models like MiniCPM and Qwen2.5-VL, this expanded input may strain their limited attention and processing capacity. Moreover, these models are often pretrained or finetuned with a strong emphasis on CoT-style verbal reasoning, which may make it more difficult for them to adapt to the VAT paradigm that relies on processing and integrating abstract visual representations. As a result, VAT can be less effective in tasks that depend heavily on structured verbal reasoning in these smaller models.

Larger models such as GPT-4o and Gemini demonstrate emergent capabilities in
visual abstract thinking
. These models are able to process and integrate abstract visual representations more effectively, which supports coherent and structured multimodal reasoning.

Appendix E
Impact Statement

This work introduces Visual Abstract Thinking (VAT), a novel reasoning paradigm for MLLMs that leverages visual abstracts to encourage models to think from the visual side and to enhance model performance and efficiency. By replacing verbose intermediate steps with concise visual sketches that capture essential structural and semantic elements, VAT enables more focused and effective visual reasoning. It offers significant gains for large proprietary models in both accuracy and computational cost. VAT opens a different direction in cognitively inspired prompting strategies and broadens the design space for efficient multimodal reasoning.
Our work is less likely to pose significant risks of negative societal impact.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathsf{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a finite set, and let
X_{\mathsf{A}}^{(n)}
be the collection of all permutations
\sigma
of
\mathbb{N}_{n}=\{0,\dots,n-1\}
with the property that
\sigma(i)-i\in\mathsf{A}\pmod{n}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
. What can one say about the number
\bigl|X_{\mathsf{A}}^{(n)}\bigr|
of all such permutations as
\mathsf{A}
remains fixed, but
n\to\infty
? Both for the history and for variations of this problem we refer to
[
Baltic10
,
Diaconis99
,
Edwards15
,
Kaplansky44
,
Kaplansky46
,
Klove09
,
Lehmer70
]
, to mention a few references.

As
n\to\infty
, one is led to consider permutations
\sigma
of
\mathbb{Z}
which move each
s\in\mathbb{Z}
by some element of a given finite set
\mathsf{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
. This embeds questions about the sizes of the sets
X_{\mathsf{A}}^{(n)},\,n\geq 1
, in problems concerning the dynamics of the shift action of
\mathbb{Z}
on the shift-invariant set of all permutations
\sigma
of
\mathbb{Z}
with
\sigma(i)-i\in\mathsf{A}
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. If one replaces
\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, or more generally, by a countably infinite discrete amenable group
\Gamma
, the analogous problem becomes considerably more challenging: for every nonempty finite set
\mathsf{A}\subset\Gamma
(in symbols:
\mathsf{A}\Subset\Gamma
), the set of permutations
\sigma
of
\Gamma
satisfying that
s^{-1}\sigma(s)\in\mathsf{A}
for every
s\in\Gamma
, can be identified with a shift of finite type (
SFT
)
X_{\mathsf{A}}\subset\mathsf{A}^{\Gamma}
over
\Gamma
. For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the study of such restricted-permutation
SFT
s was initiated in the paper
[
SS16
]
and subsequently developed further in
[
Elimelech21
]
, where the author used a correspondence between certain
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-
SFT
s of this form and dimer coverings of specific locally finite planar graphs to compute the topological entropy of
X_{\mathsf{A}}
in some examples.

In the general setting of countable discrete groups
\Gamma
, these spaces
X_{\mathsf{A}},\,\mathsf{A}\Subset\Gamma
, form a distinguished class of
SFT
s over
\Gamma
with interesting and only partially understood dynamical properties. We should also mention connections between restricted permutations of general groups (and, more generally, of metric spaces),
restricted orbit equivalence
of group actions (in the sense of
[
BT98
,
KR
]
or
[
R
]
), and
wobbling groups
as discussed, for example, in
[
Juschenko13
,
Juschenko15
,
Juschenko22
]
. Note, however, that the restricted permutations considered here do not form a group (unlike the families of restricted permutations arising from orbit equivalence or in geometric group theory).

This paper is organized as follows: Section
2
is devoted to local entropy and local pressure for shift-actions of countable amenable groups
\Gamma
, including
SFT
s with finite alphabets over
\Gamma
(cf. e.g.
[
HM10
,
CM21
,
DFR
]
) and algebraic
\Gamma
-actions as defined in
[
DSAO
,
Deninger
,
LT
]
. In Section
3
we focus on local entropy and invariant probability measures for the specific
\Gamma
-
SFT
s arising from restricted permutations of countable amenable groups
\Gamma
. In Section
4
we look in more detail at the topological entropies
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})
of such restricted-permutation
SFT
s
X_{\mathsf{A}}
. These can be expressed as logarithmic growth rates of permanents of certain finite (0,1)-matrices associated with right Følner sequences in
\Gamma
(Proposition
3.6
and Remark
4.8
).

Since the computation of permanents of
(n\negthinspace\times\negthinspace n)
-matrices is NP-hard and conjecturally not possible in polynomial time (cf. e.g.,
[
Valiant79
]
), there is little hope for general numerical results about
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}}),\,\mathsf{A}\Subset\Gamma
. One attempt to overcome the difficulty of computing explicitly the permanent of a large scalar matrix
M
is to search for linear operations (like sign changes) on the entries of
M
, resulting in a matrix
M^{\prime}
whose determinant is equal to the permanent of
M
. This leads to the notion of the
permanent

\textup{per}(f)
for nonnegative elements
f
in the real group ring
\mathbb{R}\Gamma
whose support is the alphabet
\mathsf{A}
of the
SFT

X_{\mathsf{A}}
, and to the problem of comparing, for an arbitrary element
f\in\mathbb{R}\Gamma
, the
Fuglede-Kadison determinant

\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
with the permanent
\textup{per}(|f|)
of the absolute value
|f|
of
f
. As is to be expected from negative results about this problem (
[
Marcus-Minc61
,
Gibson71
]
), this approach can succeed in only few examples, investigated in Section
5
. However, from a conceptual point of view it is interesting that the permanent
\textup{per}(f)
of a nonnegative element
f\in\mathbb{R}\Gamma
can be viewed as
topological pressure
of the restricted-permutation
SFT

X_{\mathsf{A}}
associated with the function
\log f
on the alphabet
\mathsf{A}=\textup{supp}(f)
of
X_{\mathsf{A}}
.

In Section
6
we discuss finite approximations of the permanents
\textup{per}(f)
,
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
, for residually finite amenable groups. For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
,
\textup{per}(f)
is indeed equal to the upper limit of permanents corresponding to finite quotients of
\mathbb{Z}
(Corollary
6.3
), but in general
\textup{per}(f)
may be larger than the
\limsup
of the permanents of
f
induced on the finite quotients of
\Gamma
(Proposition
6.1
). When applying this to the function
f\equiv 1
on
\mathsf{A}
this translates into the familiar statement that the logarithmic growth rate of the number of periodic points of the
SFT

X_{\mathsf{A}}
is less than or qual to
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})
, but even for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
equality of these two quantities is an open problem.

In the final Section
7
we apply a few known results on permanents of matrices to obtain estimates like
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})\leq\frac{1}{|\mathsf{A}|}\log(|\mathsf{A}|!)
if
\Gamma
is finitely generated with polynomial growth and
\mathsf{A}\Subset\Gamma
(Theorem
7.8
), or
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})\geq\log\frac{|\mathsf{A}|}{e}
if
\Gamma
is infinite amenable and
\mathsf{A}\Subset\Gamma
(Corollary
7.5
).

2.
The local pressure formula

2.1.
The local pressure formula for shift actions

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group with identity element
\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}
, and let
\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
be the collection of all nonempty finite subsets of
\Gamma
.

If
\mathsf{B}
is a compact metrizable space we consider the
full shift

\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
, furnished with the product topology, and write
\lambda
and
\rho
for the left and right shift actions of
\Gamma
on
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
, defined by

(2.1)

(\lambda^{s}x)_{t}=x_{s^{-1}t},\qquad(\rho^{s}x)_{t}=x_{ts}

for every
s\in\Gamma
and
x=(x_{t})_{t\in\Gamma}\in\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
. For
\varnothing\neq F\subseteq\Gamma
,
s\in\Gamma
and
x\in\mathsf{B}^{F}
, we denote by
sx\colon sF\to\mathsf{B}
the function sending
st
to
x_{t}
for every
t\in F
. If
F^{\prime}
is a subset of
F
we denote by
x|_{F^{\prime}}=\pi_{F^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathsf{B}^{F^{\prime}}
the restriction of
x\in\mathsf{B}^{F}
to
F^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set consisting of pairs
(K,\mathscr{O})
, where
K\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
and
\mathscr{O}
is an open subset of
\mathsf{B}^{K}
. We treat
\mathcal{P}
as a set of
forbidden patterns
and write

(2.2)

X_{\mathcal{P}}=\{x\in\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}\colon sx|_{K}=\pi_{K}(\lambda^{s}x)\notin\mathscr{O}\;\textup{for every}\;s\in\Gamma\kern 5.0pt\textup{and all}\kern 5.0pt(K,\mathscr{O})\in\mathcal{P}\}

for the corresponding closed, left shift-invariant set of
admissible elements
in
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
. For given
F\subseteq\Gamma
we denote by

(2.3)

X_{\mathcal{P},F}=\{x\in\mathsf{B}^{F}\colon s^{-1}x|_{K}\notin\mathscr{O}\;\textup{for every}\;(K,\mathscr{O})\in\mathcal{P}\;\textup{and}\;s\in\Gamma\;\textup{with}\;sK\subseteq F\}

the set of
locally admissible
patterns on
F
.

Note that every closed, left shift-invariant subset
X\subseteq\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
can be written as
X_{\mathcal{P}}
for suitable
\mathcal{P}
: indeed, one may take
\mathcal{P}=\{(K,\mathsf{B}^{K}\smallsetminus\pi_{K}(X)):K\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)\}
.

Now assume that
\Gamma
is amenable. We use right Følner sets.

Let
\mathscr{U}
be a finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}
. For each
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
we denote by
\mathscr{U}^{F}
the finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}^{F}
consisting of
\prod_{t\in F}U_{t}
for all maps
t\mapsto U_{t}
from
F
to
\mathscr{U}
. For each
Y\subseteq\mathsf{B}^{F}
, we denote by
N(\mathscr{U}^{F},Y)
the minimal number of elements of
\mathscr{U}^{F}
needed to cover
Y
.

Denote by
\pi=\pi_{\{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\}}
the coordinate map
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}\to\mathsf{B}
sending each
x=(x_{s})\in\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
to
x_{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}}
. Then
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})
is a finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
. For each
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, denote by
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F}
the open cover
\bigvee_{s\in F}s\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})
of
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
. It is well known that the limit

(2.4)

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathcal{P}},\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}))\coloneqq\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log N(\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F},X_{\mathcal{P}})

exists in the sense that there is some
C\in[-\infty,+\infty)
such that, for any neighbourhood
U
of
C
in
[-\infty,+\infty)
, there are
K\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
and
\delta>0
such that
\frac{1}{|F|}\log N(\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F},X_{\mathcal{P}})\in U
for all
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
with
|FK\vartriangle F|<\delta|F|
(cf.
[
KL16
]
*page 220). For each
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, it is easy to check that
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F}=\pi_{F}^{-1}(\mathscr{U}^{F})
. Hence

N(\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F},X_{\mathcal{P}})=N(\pi_{F}^{-1}(\mathscr{U}^{F}),X_{\mathcal{P}})=N(\mathscr{U}^{F},\pi_{F}(X_{\mathcal{P}})).

Thus

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathcal{P}},\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}))=\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log N(\mathscr{U}^{F},\pi_{F}(X_{\mathcal{P}})).

It is also easy to check that for every finite open cover
\mathscr{V}
of
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
, one has
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{K}\succeq\mathscr{V}
for some finite open cover
\mathscr{U}
of
\mathsf{B}
and some
K\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
.
It follows that

(2.5)

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathcal{P}})=\sup_{\mathscr{U}}{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathcal{P}},\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}))

for
\mathscr{U}
ranging over finite open covers of
\mathsf{B}
.

We denote by
C(\mathsf{B})
the set of real-valued continuous functions on
\mathsf{B}
. Let
f\in C(\mathsf{B})
, and let
\mathscr{U}
be a finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}
. For each
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
,
Y\subseteq\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
and
Z\subseteq\mathsf{B}^{F}
,
we set

p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),Y)=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{V\in\mathscr{V}}\,\sup_{x\in V\cap Y}e^{\sum_{s\in F}f(x_{s})}\colon\mathscr{V}\text{ is a finite cover of }\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}\text{ refining }\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F}\Biggr\}

and

p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},Z)=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{V\in\mathscr{V}}\,\sup_{x\in V\cap Z}e^{\sum_{s\in F}f(x_{s})}\colon\mathscr{V}\text{ is a finite cover of }\mathsf{B}^{F}\text{ refining }\mathscr{U}^{F}\Biggr\}.

Clearly
p_{F}(0,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),Y)=N(\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),Y)
and
p_{F}(0,\mathscr{U},Z)=N(\mathscr{U}^{F},Z)
. For any finite cover
\mathscr{V}
of
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
refining
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{F}
, if we set
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}=\{\pi_{F}(V\cap Y)\colon V\in\mathscr{V}\}\cup\{U\smallsetminus\pi_{F}(Y)\colon U\in\mathscr{U}^{F}\}
, then
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
is a finite cover of
\mathsf{B}^{F}
refining
\mathscr{U}^{F}
, and

\sum_{V\in\mathscr{V}}\,\sup_{x\in V\cap Y}e^{\sum_{s\in F}f(x_{s})}=\sum_{V^{\prime}\in\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}\,\sup_{x\in(V^{\prime}\times\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma\smallsetminus F})\cap Y}e^{\sum_{s\in F}f(x_{s})}.

In the definition of
p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),Y)
we may thus require that
\mathscr{V}=\pi_{F}^{-1}(\mathscr{V}^{\prime})
for some finite cover
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{B}^{F}
refining
\mathscr{U}^{F}
. Therefore

p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),Y)=p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},\pi_{F}(Y)).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in C(\mathsf{B})
and let
\mathscr{U}
be a finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}
. As
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
becomes more and more right invariant, the limits
\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),X_{\mathcal{P}})
and
\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})
exist in the same sense as in (
2.4
).

Proof.

We prove the assertion about
p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})
. The proof for
p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),X_{\mathcal{P}})
is similar.

If
X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}}=\varnothing
for some
F_{1}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, then
X_{\mathcal{P},F}=\varnothing
for all sufficiently right invariant
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, so that
\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})=-\infty
. Thus we may assume that
X_{\mathcal{P},F}\neq\varnothing
for all
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
.

We consider first the case
f\geq 0
.

For any
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
and
s\in\Gamma
, clearly
sX_{\mathcal{P},F}=X_{\mathcal{P},sF}
and

p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})=p_{sF}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},sF}).

For any
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, note that if
\mathscr{V}_{j}
is a finite cover of
\mathsf{B}^{F_{j}}
refining
\mathscr{U}^{F_{j}}
for
j=1,2
, then the sets
(V_{1}\times\mathsf{B}^{F_{2}\smallsetminus F_{1}})\cap(\mathsf{B}^{F_{1}\smallsetminus F_{2}}\times V_{2})
for
V_{1}\in\mathscr{V}_{1}
and
V_{2}\in\mathscr{V}_{2}
form a finite cover of
\mathsf{B}^{F_{1}\cup F_{2}}
refining
\mathscr{U}^{F_{1}\cup F_{2}}
. Hence

\displaystyle p_{F_{1}\cup F_{2}}

\displaystyle(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{V_{1}\in\mathscr{V}_{1},V_{2}\in\mathscr{V}_{2}}\,\sup_{x\in(V_{1}\times\mathsf{B}^{F_{2}\smallsetminus F_{1}})\cap(\mathsf{B}^{F_{1}\smallsetminus F_{2}}\times V_{2})\cap X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}}}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}}f(x_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{V_{1}\in\mathscr{V}_{1},V_{2}\in\mathscr{V}_{2}}\,\sup_{x\in(V_{1}\times\mathsf{B}^{F_{2}\smallsetminus F_{1}})\cap(\mathsf{B}^{F_{1}\smallsetminus F_{2}}\times V_{2})\cap X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}}}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{1}}f(x_{s})}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{2}}f(x_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{V_{1}\in\mathscr{V}_{1},V_{2}\in\mathscr{V}_{2}}\Biggl(\sup_{x\in V_{1}\cap\pi_{F_{1}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{1}}f(x_{s})}\Biggr)\Biggl(\sup_{x\in V_{2}\cap\pi_{F_{2}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{2}}f(x_{s})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\sum_{V_{1}\in\mathscr{V}_{1}}\sup_{x\in V_{1}\cap\pi_{F_{1}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{1}}f(x_{s})}\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum_{V_{2}\in\mathscr{V}_{2}}\sup_{x\in V_{2}\cap\pi_{F_{2}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})}e^{\sum_{s\in F_{2}}f(x_{s})}\Biggr),

where in the second inequality we use that
f\geq 0
. It follows that

(2.6)

\displaystyle p_{F_{1}\cup F_{2}}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})\leq p_{F_{1}}(f,\mathscr{U},\pi_{F_{1}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}}))\cdot p_{F_{2}}(f,\mathscr{U},\pi_{F_{2}}(X_{\mathcal{P},F_{1}\cup F_{2}})).

Now by the Ornstein-Weiss lemma
[
KL16
]
*Theorem 4.38 the limit
\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})
exists.

For general
f\in C(\mathsf{B})
, we can find a constant
C
such that
f\geq C
. Then

\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F})=C+\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f-C,\mathscr{U},X_{\mathcal{P},F}).

∎

For any
f\in C(\mathsf{B})
and any finite open cover
\mathscr{U}
of
\mathsf{B}
, we set

P(X_{\mathcal{P}},f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}))=\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log p_{F}(f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}),X_{\mathcal{P}}).

For every finite open cover
\mathscr{V}
of
\mathsf{B}^{\Gamma}
, one has
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})_{K}\succeq\mathscr{V}
for some finite open cover
\mathscr{U}
of
\mathsf{B}
and some
K\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. Thus
the
topological pressure of the
\Gamma
-action
\lambda
on
X_{\mathcal{P}}
associated to
f
is

P(X_{\mathcal{P}},f)=\sup_{\mathscr{U}}P(X_{\mathcal{P}},f,\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U}))

for
\mathscr{U}
ranging over finite open covers of
\mathsf{B}
(cf.
[
MO
]
*Section 5.2).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in C(\mathsf{B})
and let
\mathscr{U}
be a finite open cover of
\mathsf{B}
.
Let
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
,

, and

. Then there

\mathsf{B}^{F}

If
X

. Thus we may assume that

.

, take an open subset

.

, any

Let

Lemma 3.4

Let

Y
is a closed and open subset of
X_{\mathsf{A}}^{\iota}
containing

g\neq sy_{s}
for all

and

Suppose that

Proposition 3.6

.

(1)

(2)

(3)

. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F^{\prime},F}}\,\prod_{s\in F^{\prime}}f_{x_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|}\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F^{\prime},F}}\,\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|}|\mathsf{A}_{1}|^{|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|}\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F}}\,\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}

\displaystyle=(\|f\|_{\infty}|\mathsf{A}_{1}|)^{|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|}\,\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F}(f).

Therefore

e^{|F^{\prime}|(L_{2}-\varepsilon)}\leq\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A}_{2},F^{\prime}}(f)\leq\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F^{\prime},F}}\,\prod_{s\in F^{\prime}}f_{x_{s}}\leq(\|f\|_{\infty}|\mathsf{A}_{1}|)^{|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|}\,\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F}(f).

It follows that

L_{1}=\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A}_{1},F}(f)\geq\lim_{F}\frac{|F^{\prime}|(L_{2}-\varepsilon)-|F^{\prime}\smallsetminus F|\log(\|f\|_{\infty}|\mathsf{A}_{1}|)}{|F|}=L_{2}-\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain that
L_{1}\geq L_{2}
, as claimed.
∎

Let

2|\mathsf{A}|\varepsilon^{\eta}<e^{D}

U

Let

. Then
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 2

(5) follows from (4) of Lemma

F

Proposition 4.10
.

For any
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
and
\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
with
\textup{supp}(f)\subseteq\mathsf{A}
, we have

\textup{per}(f)=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f)=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f).

Proof.

We may assume that
f\neq 0
.

Consider first the case
\mathsf{A}=\textup{supp}(f)
. We have the function
\log f\colon\mathsf{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
3.4

[
MO
]
*Theorem 5.2.7 we have
P(X_{\mathsf{A}},\log f)=P(X_{\mathsf{A}}^{\iota},\log f)
. Then from Remark
4.5
we have

\textup{per}(f)=P(X_{\mathsf{A}},\log f)=P(X_{\mathsf{A}}^{\iota},\log f)=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f).

According to Theorem
2.13
we have

\textup{per}(f)=P(X_{\mathsf{A}},\log f)=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f),

and

\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=P(X_{\mathsf{A}}^{\iota},\log f)=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f).

Now consider the general case
\textup{supp}(f)\subseteq\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. Take a decreasing sequence
(f^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathsf{A}}
with limit
f
. Then by Proposition
4.9
we have that
\textup{per}(f)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textup{per}(f^{(n)})
and
\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f^{(n)})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\textup{per}(f)

\displaystyle=\smash[t]{\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textup{per}(f^{(n)})=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f^{(n)})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f^{(n)})=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f).

The proof for
\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\inf_{F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f)
is similar.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

\textup{per}(f)=\textup{per}(f^{*})

we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}:\,\,\varphi^{(x)}(F)=F^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}:\,\varphi^{(x)}(F)=F^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt\prod_{t\in F^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\bar{x}_{t}}\leq\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},\,F^{\prime}}^{\iota}}\,\prod_{t\in F^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\bar{x}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1},\,F^{\prime}}(f^{*})\leq e^{|F^{\prime}|(C-2\varepsilon)}=e^{|F|(C-2\varepsilon)}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\iota}(\mathsf{A},F)}\kern 5.0pt\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}:\,\,\varphi^{(x)}(F)=F^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\iota}(\mathsf{A},F)}e^{|F|(C-2\varepsilon)}=|\Theta^{\iota}(\mathsf{A},F)|e^{|F|(C-2\varepsilon)}\leq e^{|F|C}.

It follows that
\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\lim_{F\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f)\leq C
. Letting

we obtain that
\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)\leq\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f^{*})
, as desired.

(2) By Proposition
4.10
it suffices to show that
\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(fg)\geq\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)+\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g)

. Put

and
\mathsf{B}=\textup{supp}(g)
. Then

.

Let

, and denote by

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in X_{\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B},F}^{\iota}}\,\prod_{s\in F}(fg)_{z_{s}}\geq\sum_{z\in X_{\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B},F}^{\iota}}\,\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}}\,\sum_{y\in\Xi_{x},\,xy=z}\,\prod_{s\in F}(f_{x_{s}}g_{y_{s}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}}\,\Biggl(\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}\Biggr)\,\sum_{w\in X_{\mathsf{B},F_{x}}^{\iota}}\,\prod_{t\in F_{x}}g_{w_{t}}=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}}\,\Biggl(\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}\Biggr)\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{B},F_{x}}(g)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}}\,\Biggl(\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}\Biggr)e^{|F_{x}|\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g)}=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}}\Biggl(\prod_{s\in F}f_{x_{s}}\Biggr)e^{|F|\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g)}

\displaystyle=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}_{\mathsf{A},F}(f)\,e^{|F|\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g)}\geq e^{|F|\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)}e^{|F|\,\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g)}=e^{|F|(\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)+\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(g))},

Remark 5.3
.

In general, for
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
the identity
\textup{per}(f)=\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
will fail. For example, if we put
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{R}\Gamma=\mathbb{R}[x^{\pm 1}]
as in Remark
4.12
, and if
f=(1+x)(1+x^{-1})=x^{-1}+2+x
, we have
\textup{per}(f)\geq\textup{per}(x^{-1}+1+x)={\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})>0
for
\mathsf{A}=\{-1,0,1\}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{Z})
, while
\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)=2\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}({1+x})=0
.

However, the problem of finding possible connections between
\textup{per}(f)
and
\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
is intriguing. Both these quantities arise naturally in dynamical contexts: if
f
is the indicator function of a set
\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, Remark
4.8
shows that
\textup{per}(f)
is the topological entropy of the restricted permutation space
X_{\mathsf{A}}\subseteq\mathsf{A}^{\Gamma}
; on the other hand, if
f\in\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
is arbitrary,
\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
is the topological entropy of the principal algebraic
\Gamma
-action
(X_{f},\lambda_{f})
arising from
f
(
[
LSW
,
Deninger
,
LT
]
). At least for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the explicit value of
\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
is given by the logarithmic Mahler measure of
f
(
[
LSW
]
), allowing straightforward numerical calculations and, in special cases, explicit arithmetical formulas for
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(\lambda_{f})=\textup{det}_{\textup{FK}}(f)
(cf. e.g.,
[
Boyd
]
). On the other hand, the complexity of computing the permanent of an
n\times n
(0,1)-matrix is
NP-hard
and conjecturally not possible in polynomial time (cf.
[
Valiant79
]
). This doesn’t give much hope for general results concerning explicit values of
\textup{per}(f),\,f\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
, or, indeed, of
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}}),\,\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

In view of the difficulty of computing the permanent of a large matrix
M
one may try to search for linear operations (like sign changes) on the entries of
M
, resulting in a matrix
M^{\prime}
whose determinant is equal to the permanent of
M
. Although this approach works only under severe restrictions (cf. e.g.,
[
Gibson71
,
Marcus-Minc61
]
), it is the motivation for the following lemma.

6.
Approximation by finite quotient groups

In this section we discuss briefly finite approximations of permanents
\textup{per}(f)
,
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
, for residually finite amenable groups.

Let
\Gamma
be amenable,
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
,
\mathsf{A}\coloneqq\textup{supp}(f)\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, and let
\Delta\subset\Gamma
be a finite-index normal subgroup such that
(\mathsf{A}\mathsf{A}^{-1})\cap\Delta=\{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\}
. We denote by
\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma/\Delta
the quotient map
s\mapsto s\Delta
as well as the induced homomorphism
\mathbb{C}\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(\Gamma/\Delta)
.

We define
X_{\mathsf{A}}\subseteq\mathsf{A}^{\Gamma}
by (
3.1
) – (
3.3
), set
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}=\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}(\mathsf{A})\subseteq\Gamma/\Delta
, and denote by
\tilde{\mathscr{S}}_{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}\subseteq\mathscr{S}(\Gamma/\Delta)
the set of all permutations
\tilde{\sigma}
of
\Gamma/\Delta
with

\tilde{x}_{\tilde{s}}=\tilde{s}^{-1}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{s})\in\tilde{\mathsf{A}}

for every
\tilde{s}\in\Gamma/\Delta
. We define
X_{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathsf{A}}^{\Gamma/\Delta}
exactly as in (
3.1
) – (
3.3
), with
\Gamma
and
\mathsf{A}
replaced by
\Gamma/\Delta
and
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}
, respectively. For every
y\in X_{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}
, the composition
y\circ\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}
lies in
X_{\mathsf{A}}
(actually it is an element of
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}^{\Gamma}
, but since the restriction of
\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}
to
\mathsf{A}
is injective by assumption we can abuse notation and treat it as an element of
X_{\mathsf{A}}\subseteq\mathsf{A}^{\Gamma}
which is invariant under
\lambda^{t}
for every
t\in\Delta
(cf. (
2.1
)). Conversely, every
x^{\prime}\in\textup{Fix}_{\Delta}(X_{\mathsf{A}})=\{x\in X_{\mathsf{A}}\colon\lambda^{t}x=x\kern 5.0pt\textup{for every}\kern 5.0ptt\in\Delta\}
arises in this manner. It follows that

|\textup{Fix}_{\Delta}(X_{\mathsf{A}})|=|X_{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}|

and

\textup{per}(\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}(f))=\frac{1}{|\Gamma/\Delta|}\log\Biggl(\sum_{y\in X_{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}}\,\prod_{\tilde{s}\in\Gamma/\Delta}\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta}(f)_{y_{\tilde{s}}}\Biggr).

Let
\Gamma
be amenable and residually finite, and let
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite-index normal subgroups such that
\bigcap_{m}\bigcup_{n\geq m}\Delta_{n}=\{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\}
. By
[
Weiss
]
there is a right Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Gamma
such that
\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}
maps
F_{n}
bijectively to
\Gamma/\Delta_{n}
for every

and
\mathsf{A}\subset F_{n}
for every

, say.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
. Then
\textup{per}(f)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\textup{per}(\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}(f))
.

Proof.

We assume that
f\neq 0
and set
\textsf{A}=\textup{supp}(f)\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
.

For every
n\geq 1
we put
\mathsf{A}^{(n)}=\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}(\mathsf{A})
and
f^{(n)}=\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}(f)
. For every
y\in X_{\mathsf{A}^{(n)}}\subset(\mathsf{A}^{(n)})^{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}
, the composition
y\circ\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}
lies in
(\mathsf{A}^{(n)})^{\Gamma}
. If
n\geq N
, we may view
y\circ\pi_{\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}
as an element of
\mathsf{A}^{\Gamma}
whose restriction to
F_{n}
lies in
X_{\mathsf{A},F_{n}}
. In the notation of (
4.1
) we obtain that

\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F_{n}}(f)=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathsf{A},F_{n}}}\prod_{s\in F_{n}}f_{x_{s}}\geq\sum_{y\in X_{\mathsf{A}^{(n)}}}\prod_{\tilde{t}\in\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}f^{(n)}_{y_{\tilde{t}}}.

According to Definition
4.4
,

\displaystyle\textup{per}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F_{n}}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Gamma/\Delta_{n}|}\log\Biggl(\sum_{y\in X_{\mathsf{A}^{(n)}}}\prod_{\tilde{t}\in\Gamma/\Delta_{n}}f^{(n)}_{y_{\tilde{t}}}\Biggr)

∎

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\textup{per}(f^{(n)}).

∎

For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
,
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathbb{Z}
, and
\Delta_{n}=n\mathbb{Z}
, the inequality
\textup{per}(f)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\textup{per}(\pi_{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}(f))
in Proposition
6.1
is actually an equality. This is a consequence of the following classical result, essentially taken from
[
LM
]
*Theorem 4.3.6. and Exercise 4.4.3.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a nonempty finite set,
Y\subseteq\mathsf{B}^{\mathbb{Z}}
a shift of finite type
(
as defined in Subsection
2.3
)
, and let
\lambda
be the
(
left
)
shift action (
2.1
) of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathsf{B}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For every
N\geq 1
we denote by
\textup{Fix}_{N}(Y)=\{y\in Y\colon\lambda^{N}y=y\}
the set of periodic points in

with period

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\,

\displaystyle\log\Biggl(\sum_{y\in\pi_{\{0,\dots,N-1\}}(Y)}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\phi(y_{k})\Biggr)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\,\log\Biggl(\sum_{y\in\textup{Fix}_{N}(Y)}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\phi(y_{k})\Biggr)

for every map

.

By applying Proposition

to the

and the function

where the limits are taken over increasing intervals

) or (

). As indicated in Remark

coincides with the topological pressure

, which gives us equality in Proposition

.

Let

7.
Bounds for permanents

Although the problem of calculating of
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}}),\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
, doesn’t seem tractable in general, some of the known results on permanents of matrices lead to useful estimates on the topological entropies of these spaces. For general reference on permanents we refer to
[
Minc78
]
and
[
VW01
]
*Chapters 11 and 12.

We recall that the
permanent
of a matrix
B=(b_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})
with
m\leq n
is defined as

\textup{per}(B)=\sum_{\sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{m}b_{i\sigma(i)},

where
\sigma
ranges over all injective maps
\{1,\dots,m\}\to\{1,\dots,n\}
. Note that the permanent does not change if we exchange two rows or two columns of
B
. Thus we can talk about
\textup{per}(B)
for any
B\in M_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
where
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are nonempty finite sets with
|F_{1}|\leq|F_{2}|
.

A matrix
B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is called
doubly stochastic
if all entries are nonnegative and all row-sums and column-sums are equal to
1
. We denote by
\Omega_{n}
the set of all
n\times n
doubly stochastic matrices.

We shall need the following result
[
VW01
]
*Theorem 12.1, known as
the van der Waerden conjecture
, proved independently by Falikman (without the uniqueness part)
[
Falikman
]
and Egoryčev
[
Egorycev
]

Theorem 7.1

(van der Waerden Conjecture)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
B\in\Omega_{n}
, one has

\textup{per}(B)\geq\frac{n!}{n^{n}},

and the equality holds exactly when all entries of
B
are
\frac{1}{n}
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. Let
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
and
\mathsf{A},F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. We denote by
B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f}
the matrix in
M_{F,F\mathsf{A}}(\mathbb{R})
with value
f_{s}
at
(t,ts)
for all
t\in F
and
s\in\mathsf{A}
. In the notation of (
4.1
) we have

\textup{per}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})=\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f).

Now assume that
\Gamma
is infinite and amenable.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
with
\|f\|_{1}=1
. Then

\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)\geq\log\frac{1}{e},

where
e=2.71828\dots
is the Euler number.

Proof.

We fix an
\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
containing
\textup{supp}(f)\cup\{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\}
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. We define a matrix
C_{F\mathsf{A},f}\in M_{F\mathsf{A}}(\mathbb{R})
as follows. For each
s\in\textup{supp}(f)
, we take a bijection
\sigma_{s}\colon F\mathsf{A}\rightarrow F\mathsf{A}
such that
\sigma_{s}(t)=ts
for all
t\in F
. For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}
, we set

(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\sum_{s\in\textup{supp}(f),\,\sigma_{s}(t_{1})=t_{2}}f_{s}.

Then

(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}=(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}

for all
t_{1}\in F
and
t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}
. That is,
B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f}
is a submatrix of
C_{F\mathsf{A},f}
.

For any
t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1}\in F\mathsf{A}}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\sum_{t_{1}\in F\mathsf{A}}\,\sum_{s\in\textup{supp}(f),\,\sigma_{s}(t_{1})=t_{2}}f_{s}=\sum_{s\in\textup{supp}(f)}f_{s}=1.

For any
t_{1}\in F\mathsf{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\sum_{t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}}\,\sum_{s\in\textup{supp}(f),\,\sigma_{s}(t_{1})=t_{2}}f_{s}=\sum_{s\in\textup{supp}(f)}f_{s}=1.

Thus
C_{F\mathsf{A},f}
is a doubly stochastic matrix. From Theorem
7.1
we have

\textup{per}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})\geq\frac{|F\mathsf{A}|!}{|F\mathsf{A}|^{|F\mathsf{A}|}}.

Denote by
\Phi
the set of injective maps
\phi\colon F\rightarrow F\mathsf{A}
, and by
\Psi
the set of bijections
\psi\colon F\mathsf{A}\rightarrow F\mathsf{A}
. For each
\phi\in\Phi
, denote by
\Psi_{\phi}
the set of
\psi\in\Psi
extending
\phi
, and by
\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}
the set of
\psi\in\Psi_{\phi}
satisfying
(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}>0
for all
t\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F
. For each
t\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F
, the
t
-row of
C_{F\mathsf{A},f}
has at most
|\textup{supp}(f)|
positive entries. Thus

|\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}|\leq|\textup{supp}(f)|^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}

for every
\phi\in\Phi
. Now

\displaystyle\textup{per}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\prod_{t\in F\mathsf{A}}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}=\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{\phi}}\prod_{t\in F\mathsf{A}}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}}\prod_{t\in F\mathsf{A}}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}}\left(\prod_{t\in F}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\phi(t)}\right)\left(\prod_{t\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F}(C_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}}\prod_{t\in F}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\phi(t)}=\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}|\Psi^{\prime}_{\phi}|\prod_{t\in F}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\phi(t)}

\displaystyle\leq|\textup{supp}(f)|^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}\prod_{t\in F}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=|\textup{supp}(f)|^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}\textup{per}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})=|\textup{supp}(f)|^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f).

Thus

\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f)\geq|\textup{supp}(f)|^{-|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}\frac{|F\mathsf{A}|!}{|F\mathsf{A}|^{|F\mathsf{A}|}}.

Since
\Gamma
is infinite,
|F|\to\infty
as
F
becomes more and more right invariant.
Therefore, using Stirling’s approximation formula, we get

\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\textup{per}_{\mathsf{A},F}^{\iota}(f)\geq\lim_{F}\frac{1}{|F|}\log\left(|\textup{supp}(f)|^{-|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|}\frac{|F\mathsf{A}|!}{|F\mathsf{A}|^{|F\mathsf{A}|}}\right)=\log\frac{1}{e}.

∎

Remark 7.3

Prior to the resolution of the van der Waerden conjecture by Falikman and Egoryčev, Friedland

Theorem 7.4
.

For every
f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Gamma
we have

\textup{per}(f)\geq\log\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{e}.

Proof.

This is obvious when
f=0
. Thus we may assume
f\neq 0
. We have

\textup{per}(f)=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}(f)=\textup{per}^{\mspace{1.5mu}\iota}\biggl(\frac{f}{\|f\|_{1}}\biggr)+\log\|f\|_{1}\geq\log\frac{1}{e}+\log\|f\|_{1}=\log\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{e},

where the first equality is from Proposition
4.10
, the second equality is from Proposition
4.9
.(3),
and the inequality is from Lemma
7.2
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. Then

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})\geq\log\frac{|\mathsf{A}|}{e}.

Furthermore, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})=0
.

(2)

|\mathsf{A}|=1
or
|\mathsf{A}|=2
with
\mathsf{A}=\{s,t\}
such that
st^{-1}
has infinite order.

Proof.

Let
f
be the characteristic function of
\mathsf{A}
. From Remark
4.8
we have

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})=\textup{per}(f)
.
Then the inequality follows from Theorem
7.4
.

If
|\mathsf{A}|\geq 3
, then
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})\geq\log\frac{|\mathsf{A}|}{e}>0
.

If
|\mathsf{A}|=1
, then clearly
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})=0
.

Now assume that
|\mathsf{A}|=2
. Say,
\mathsf{A}=\{s,t\}
. Set
\mathsf{A}^{\prime}=\mathsf{A}t^{-1}=\{\matheur{e}_{\Gamma},st^{-1}\}
.
Denote by
G
the subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by
st^{-1}
. By parts (1) and (6) of Proposition
4.9
we have that

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})=\textup{per}_{\Gamma}(f)=\textup{per}_{\Gamma}(ft^{-1})=\textup{per}_{G}(ft^{-1})

is equal to the topology entropy of the action
G\curvearrowright Y_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
, where
Y_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
is defined similar as
X_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}
replacing
\Gamma
by
G
.

If
st^{-1}
has infinite order, then
G
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, so that
{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})={\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(Y_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}})=0
.

If
st^{-1}
has finite order, then

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})={\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(Y_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{|G|}\log|Y_{\mathsf{A}^{\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{|G|}\log 2>0.

∎

Theorem 7.6

Let
B
be an
n\times n

(0, 1)

A finitely generated group
\Gamma
has
polynomial growth
if, given a finite generating subset
S
of
\Gamma
satisfying
\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\in S=S^{-1}

g
such that
|S^{n}|\leq g(n)
for all

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group of polynomial growth. Then there is a right Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Gamma
such that

(7.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|F_{n}K\smallsetminus F_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}\log(1+|F_{n}K\smallsetminus F_{n}|)=0

Theorem 7.8
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is finitely generated of polynomial growth and infinite. Let
\mathsf{A}\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. Then

{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})\leq\frac{1}{|\mathsf{A}|}\log(|\mathsf{A}|!).

Proof.

Replacing
\mathsf{A}
by
\mathsf{A}t^{-1}
for some
t\in\mathsf{A}
, we may assume that
\matheur{e}_{\Gamma}\in\mathsf{A}
. Let
f
be the characteristic function of
\mathsf{A}
.

Let
F\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma)
. We denote
\{t\in\Gamma\colon t\mathsf{A}^{-1}\subseteq F\}
by
\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)
. We define a matrix
D_{F\mathsf{A},f}\in M_{F\mathsf{A}}(\mathbb{R})
via

\displaystyle(D_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\begin{cases}(B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f})_{t_{1},t_{2}}&\quad\text{if }t_{1}\in F\\
1&\quad\text{if }t_{1}\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F\text{ and }t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)\\
0&\quad\text{if }t_{1}\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F\text{ and }t_{2}\in\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)\\
\end{cases}

for
t_{1},t_{2}\in F\mathsf{A}
.
Then
B_{F,F\mathsf{A},f}
is a submatrix of
D_{F\mathsf{A},f}
. Note that every injective map
\phi\colon F\rightarrow F\mathsf{A}
extends to a (not necessarily unique) bijections
\psi\colon F\mathsf{A}\rightarrow F\mathsf{A}
. If
\phi(F)\supseteq\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)
, then necessarily
(D_{F\mathsf{A},f})_{t,\psi(t)}=1
for all
t\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F
. It follows that

\textup{per}(D_{F\mathsf{A},f})\geq|X_{\mathsf{A},F}|,

where
X_{\mathsf{A},F}
is defined in (
3.6
).

Note that
D_{F\mathsf{A},f}
is a (0, 1)-matrix. For each
t\in F
, the sum of the
t
-row of
D_{F\mathsf{A},f}
is
|\mathsf{A}|
. For each
t\in F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F
, the sum of the
t
-row of
D_{F\mathsf{A},f}
is
|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|
. By Theorem
7.6
we have

\textup{per}(D_{F\mathsf{A},f})\leq(|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|!)^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|/|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|}(|\mathsf{A}|!)^{|F|/|\mathsf{A}|}.

Note that

\displaystyle|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|

\displaystyle\leq|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|+|F\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|\leq|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|+|\mathsf{A}|\cdot|F\mathsf{A}^{-1}\smallsetminus F|

\displaystyle\leq(|\mathsf{A}|+1)|F(\mathsf{\mathsf{A}}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F|.

Also note that
((n+1)!)^{1/(n+1)}\geq(n!)^{1/n}
for all nonnegative integers
n
.
Thus

\displaystyle|X_{\mathsf{A},F}|

\displaystyle\leq(|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|!)^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|/|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus\textup{Int}_{\mathsf{A}^{-1}}(F)|}(|\mathsf{A}|!)^{|F|/|\mathsf{A}|}

\displaystyle\leq(((|\mathsf{A}|+1)|F(\mathsf{\mathsf{A}}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F|)!)^{|F\mathsf{A}\smallsetminus F|/((|\mathsf{A}|+1)|F(\mathsf{\mathsf{A}}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F|)}(|\mathsf{A}|!)^{|F|/|\mathsf{A}|}

\displaystyle\leq(((|\mathsf{A}|+1)|F(\mathsf{\mathsf{A}}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F|)!)^{1/(|\mathsf{A}|+1)}(|\mathsf{A}|!)^{|F|/|\mathsf{A}|}.

Since
\Gamma
has polynomial growth, by Lemma
7.7
we can find a right Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
7.1
). By Proposition
3.6
we have

\displaystyle{\textup{h}}_{\textup{top}}(X_{\mathsf{A}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log|X_{\mathsf{A},F_{n}}|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log\Biggl((((|\mathsf{A}|+1)|F_{n}(\mathsf{A}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F_{n}|)!)^{1/(|\mathsf{A}|+1)}(|\mathsf{A}|!)^{|F_{n}|/|\mathsf{A}|}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|\cdot(|\mathsf{A}|+1)}\log((|F_{n}(\mathsf{A}\cup\mathsf{A}^{-1})\smallsetminus F_{n}|)!)+\frac{1}{|\mathsf{A}|}\log(|\mathsf{A}|!)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathsf{A}|}\log(|\mathsf{A}|!),

where in the second inequality we use that
|F_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
since
\Gamma
is infinite, and in the second equality we use Stirling’s approximation formula and (
7.1
).
∎

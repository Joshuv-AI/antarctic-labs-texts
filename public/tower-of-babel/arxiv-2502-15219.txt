Abstract
. In this article, we prove an ϵ \epsilon -regularity theorem for Perelman’s reduced volume. We show that on a Ricci flow, if Perelman’s reduced volume is close to 1 1 , then the curvature radius at the base point cannot be too small.

1.
Introduction

The
\epsilon
-regularity theorem is an important tool in geometric analysis, primarily used to study the regularity (smoothness) of the solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations and geometric variational problems. The idea of the
\epsilon
-regularity theorems is that if a certain geometric quantity is sufficiently small in a small region, then the solution in that region is regular in some sense (e.g., smooth). The
\epsilon
-regularity theorems are particularly useful in the study of minimal surfaces, harmonic maps, Yang-Mills fields, and related problems.

For geometric flows, many
\epsilon
-regularity theorems are derived through the utilization of monotonicity formulas. The first
\epsilon
-regularity theorem for the Ricci flow is Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem
[
Pe02
]
, one may see
[
TW15
]
[
Wa20
]
[
Bam20b
]
for recent developments in that respect. These pseudolocality theorems were obtained by using one of Perelman’s monotonicity formulas — the
\mathcal{W}
-functional.

Another relevant result concerning the
\mathcal{W}
-functional is Hein-Naber’s
\epsilon
-regularity theorem
[
HN14
]
: for each
C>0
there exists an
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,C)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\left(M^{n},g_{t}\right)_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Assume that there are a space-time piont
(x,t)\in M\times I
and a scale
r>0
with
[t-r^{2},t]\subset I
, satisfying

\displaystyle R_{g_{t-r^{2}}}\geq-\frac{C}{r^{2}},\quad\inf_{\tau\in(0,2r^{2})}\mu(g_{t-r^{2}},\tau)\geq-C,

and

\mathcal{W}_{x,t}(r^{2})\geq-\epsilon,

then we have

r_{\mathrm{Rm}}\left(x,t\right)^{2}\geq\epsilon r^{2}.

Here
\mathcal{W}_{x,t}(\tau)
is Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
-functional with its test function being the conjugate heat kernel based at
(x,t)
(also known as Perelman’s pointed entropy),
\mu
is Perelman’s
\mu
-functional, and
r_{\mathrm{Rm}}
is the curvature radius defined in Definition
2.1
.

Bamler
[
Bam20a
]
proved a stronger
\epsilon
-regularity theorem for the Nash entropy:
there is a constant
\epsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let
\left(M^{n},g_{t}\right)_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Let
(x,t)\in M\times I
be a space-time point and
r>0
a scale with
[t-r^{2},t]\subset I
. If

\mathcal{N}_{x,t}\left(r^{2}\right)\geq-\epsilon,

then

r_{\mathrm{Rm}}(x,t)\geq\epsilon r.

Monotonicity formulas of geometric flows are closely related to their rigidity properties. Here by rigidity we mean that if a geometric quantity is
equal
to the standard value, then the geometric object is
identical
to the standard model. For instance, if Perlman’s pointed entropy or the Nash entropy is ever equal to zero, then the Ricci flow is the (static) Euclidean space. In view of this fact,
\epsilon
-regularity theorems and rigidity theorems often appear in pairs, because one monotonicity formula would give rise to both. Indeed, these rigidity properties are essential to the proofs of the aforementioned

\epsilon
-regularity theorems.

Another important monotonicity formula discovered by Perelman is the reduced volume, which also has the rigidity property that if the reduced volume is ever equal to 1, then the Ricci flow is the trivial one on the Euclidean space. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect an
\epsilon
-regularity theorem for the reduced volume. The object of the present paper is to prove such a theorem. All definitions and notations in our results can be found in Section 2.1.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a dimensional constant
\epsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let

(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Let
(x,t)\in M\times I
be a space-time point and
r>0
a scale with
[t-2r^{2},t]\subset I
. If, furthermore

(1.1)

\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(r^{2})\geq 1-\epsilon,

then we have
r_{\operatorname{Rm}}(x,t)\geq r
; here
\mathcal{V}
and
r_{\Rm}
are defined in (
2.3
) and Definition
2.1
, respectively.

Remark 1.2
.

Yokota
[
Yo09
]
proved a gap theorem for the reduced volume: if an ancient Ricci flow with Ricci curvature bounded from below satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(\tau)\geq 1-\epsilon(n),

where
(x,t)
is a space-time point and
\epsilon(n)
is a dimensional constant, then the Ricci flow is a static Euclidean space. The second author
[
Z21
]
also proved a similar gap theorem for the asymptotic
\mathcal{W}
-entropy on ancient Ricci flows.
Theorem
1.1
should be viewed as a complement of
[
Yo09
]
.

Let us recall the ideas of the proofs of
[
HN14
]
and
[
Bam20a
]
; each proof breaks down to the following steps:

(1)

Assume the
\epsilon
-regularity theorem is false, so one can find a sequence of counterexamples — the Nash entropies go to zero, but the curvature radii are small.

(2)

Adjust the sequence by a point-picking process, so that the sequence still satisfies the conditions of step (1), and at the same time the geometry is locally uniformly bounded.

(3)

Apply the compactness theorem, such as
[
H95
]
, to take a smooth limit and obtain a contradiction.

In fact, many
\epsilon
-regularity theorems are proved in the above way. However, when one applies the above idea to Perelman’s reduced volume, one would encounter a difficulty in Step (2). The reason is that, unlike the Nash entropy, there is no estimate showing how Perelman’s reduced volume depends on the base point in the general case. Nevertheless, the success of Yokota’s gap theorem
[
Yo09
]
was due to the fact that, when considering the so-called asymptotic reduced volume

\overline{\mathcal{V}}(x,t)=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(\tau)

on an ancient Ricci flow with Ricci curvature bounded from below, and, in particular, the way it depends on the base point
(x,t)
, the fuzzy stuff introduced by local geometry will be eliminated by taking the limit in
\tau
, and hence one can obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{V}}(x,t)\leq\overline{\mathcal{V}}(y,s)\quad\text{whenever}\quad t>s.

With this estimate, the point-picking argument is clearly available. In fact, this is also the idea of the proof of
[
Z21
]
.

However, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will not perform a point-picking argument, but will overcome this difficulty with much more sophisticated techniques — Bamler’s
\mathbb{F}
-compactness and partial regularity theories. Namely, in Step (2), we will perform a simple normalization instead of point-picking, and we will take an
\mathbb{F}
-limit in Step (3), instead of smooth limit, to obtain the contradiction.

It is also straightforward to observe that, by replacing the reduced volume by Nash entropy and applying the similar “no-point-picking-argument” of the current article, Bamler’s
\epsilon
-regularity theorem
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.2]
can be improved: if
[t-2r^{2},t]\subset I
and

\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2})\geq-\epsilon(n),

then

r_{\Rm}(x,t)\geq r;

here (and in Theorem
1.1
as well)
r_{\Rm}
can even be the curvature radius defined as
[
Bam20a
, Definition 10.2]
.
Since the proof is a simple adaptation of our argument, we leave the details to the reader.

Finally, we give a corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there is a positive number
\delta=\delta(\epsilon,n)>0
with the following property. Let

(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Let
(x,t)\in M\times I
be a space-time point and
r>0
a scale with
[t-r^{2},t]\subset I
. If, furthermore,

(1.2)

\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(r^{2})\geq 1-\delta,

then, for all
r^{\prime}\in(0,\epsilon^{-1}r)
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t}}\big(B_{g_{t}}(x,r^{\prime})\big)\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{n}(r^{\prime})^{n},

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Definitions

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be a solution to the Ricci flow

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=-2\Ric_{g_{t}}.

Fixing a
t\in I
, Perelman’s
\mathcal{L}
-energy for a piecewise
C^{1}
curve
\gamma(\sigma):[0,\tau]\to M
, where
[t-\tau,\tau]\subset I
, is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\gamma)=\int^{\tau}_{0}\sqrt{\sigma}\big(R_{g_{t-\sigma}}(\gamma(\sigma))+|\gamma^{\prime}(\sigma)|_{g_{t-\sigma}}^{2}\big)d\sigma.

The critical points of the
\mathcal{L}
-energy are called minimal
\mathcal{L}
-geodesics. Similar to the standard theory of geodesics in Riemannian geometry, the minimizer with respect to fixed end points always exists and is almost always unique, should the Ricci flow in question have a Ricci curvature lower bound
[
Ye08
]
.

Thus,
\mathcal{L}
-geodesics can be used to define a type of space-time distance, namely,
Perelman’s reduced distance
. Let
(x,t)
,
(y,s)\in M\times I
be two space-time points in the Ricci flow, where
s<t
. Define

\displaystyle L_{x,t}(y,t-s):=\inf_{\gamma}\mathcal{L}(\gamma)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t-s}\sqrt{\tau}\left(R_{g_{t-\tau}}(\gamma(\tau))+|\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}_{g_{t-\tau}}\right)d\tau,

where the infimum is taken among all piecewise
C^{1}
-curves
\gamma:[0,t-s]\to M
with
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(t-s)=y
, then

\displaystyle\ell_{x,t}(y,t-s):=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t-s}}L_{x,t}(y,t-s)

is the
reduced distance from
(x,t)
to
(y,s)
, and a minimizer
\gamma
is called a minimizing
\mathcal{L}
-geodesic from
(x,t)
to
(y,s)
. Furthermore, if we fix
(x,t)
, then the space-time function

\displaystyle\ell_{x,t}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,):M\times(0,t-\inf I)\to\mathbb{R}

is called the
reduced distance based at
(x,t)
. We remark here that the second variable of
\ell_{x,t}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
is the backward time starting from
t
. However, sometimes it is also convenient to consider the forward time. So we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle\overline{\ell}_{x,t}(y,s):=\ell_{x,t}(y,t-s),\quad(y,s)\subset M\times\big(I\cap(-\infty,t)\big).

Perelman’s
reduced volume
is defined as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(\tau):=\int_{M}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{x,t}(\cdot,\tau)}\,dg_{t-\tau},

where
(x,t)
is the base point and
\tau\geq 0
is the backward time from
t
. The reduced volume is one of the two important monotonic quantities discovered by Perelman —
\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(\tau)
is always decreasing with respect to
\tau
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\left(M^{n},g_{t}\right)_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow. Given
(x,t)\in M\times I
, the curvature radius at
(x,t)
is defined as

r_{\mathrm{Rm}}(x,t):=\sup\left\{r>0\,\bigg|\,[t-r^{2},t]\subset I,\ \sup_{P_{r}(x,t)}|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq r^{-2}\right\},

where

P_{r}(x,t):=B_{g_{t}}(x,r)\times\left[t-r^{2},t\right].

2.2.
Perelman’s
\mathcal{L}
-geometry

Consider a Ricci flow
(M,g_{t})_{t\in I}
and fix
(x,t)\in M\times I
. We shall recall some basic properties of
\ell_{x,t}
. By the first variation of the
\mathcal{L}
-energy (
2.1
), the
\mathcal{L}
-geodesic equation is (see
[
Pe02
, (7.2)]
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla^{g_{t-\tau}}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{g_{t-\tau}}R_{g_{t-\tau}}(\gamma(\tau))+\frac{1}{2\tau}\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)+2\Ric_{g_{t-\tau}}(\gamma^{\prime}(\tau))=0.

Given any
v\in T_{x}M
, denote by
\gamma_{v}
the
\mathcal{L}
-geodesic satisfying
\lim\limits_{\sigma\to 0}\sqrt{\sigma}\gamma^{\prime}(\sigma)=v
. Then the
\mathcal{L}
-exponential map
\mathcal{L}\text{exp}^{\tau}_{x,t}:T_{x}M\to M
is defined as

\mathcal{L}\text{exp}^{\tau}_{x,t}(v)=\gamma_{v}(\tau).

Similar to the standard theory of geodesics, we define

\displaystyle\Omega^{TM}_{x,t}(\tau)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}M\ \Big|

\displaystyle\ \gamma_{v}|_{[0,\tau]}:[0,\tau]\to M\text{ is the unique minimizing }\mathcal{L}\text{-geodesic}

\displaystyle\text{\ from $(x,t)$ to $(\gamma_{v}(\tau),t-\tau)$};

\displaystyle\text{\ $x$ and $\gamma_{v}(\tau)$ are not conjugate along }\gamma_{v}.\Big\}.

Correspondingly,

\displaystyle\Omega_{x,t}(\tau)=\Big\{y\in M\ \Big|\

\displaystyle\ \text{There is a unique minimizing }\mathcal{L}\text{-geodesic }\gamma:[0,\tau]\to M

\displaystyle\text{\ from $(x,t)$ to $(y,t-\tau)$;}\text{ $x$ and $y$ are not conjugate along }\gamma.\Big\}.

It is well known that

\Omega_{x,t}(\tau)=\mathcal{L}\text{exp}_{x,t}^{\tau}\big(\Omega_{x,t}^{TM}(\tau)\big),

and the
\mathcal{L}
-cut-locus is
defined as

C_{x,t}(\tau)=M\backslash\Omega_{x,t}(\tau).

When the base point is understood, we also omit the subindices in the notations introduced above.

3.
local estimates for the reduced distance

Proposition 3.1

If

(2)

Remark.

Proof.

(1) First of all, the lower bound of

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{t_{0}-s}}\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t_{0}-s}\sqrt{\tau}\big(|\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}_{g_{t_{0}-\tau}}+R_{g_{t_{0}-\tau}}(\gamma(\tau))\big)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -C(r,T).

\displaystyle\gamma(\tau)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\zeta_{1}(\tau)&\tau\in[0,t_{0}-t]\\
\zeta_{2}(\tau)&\tau\in[t_{0}-t,t_{0}-s]\end{array}\right.,

where

\displaystyle\ C(r,L,T).

Then, similar to

Proof of the claim.

4.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
by using Bamler’s technique in
[
Bam20b
]
and the estimates in the previous section. Corollary
1.3
will follow in a similar fashion. We shall split the proof into several steps.

4.1.
Setting up the contradictory sequence

Arguing by contradiction, assume that Theorem
1.1
is false, then we can find a sequence of counterexamples, namely, a sequence of
n
-dimensional Ricci flows
\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in I_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, each with bounded curvature within compact time intervals, a sequence of points
(x_{i},t_{i})\in M^{i}\times I_{i}
, a sequence of scales
r_{i}>0
with
[t_{i}-2r_{i}^{2},t_{i}]\subset I_{i}
, and a sequence of positive numbers
\epsilon_{i}\searrow 0
, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{x_{i},t_{i}}(r_{i}^{2})\geq 1-\epsilon_{i},

\displaystyle r_{\Rm,i}(x_{i},t_{i})<r_{i}.

First of all, we shall perform a normalization for the sequence. Letting

\displaystyle\overline{r}_{i}=r_{\Rm,i}(x_{i},t_{i})<r_{i},

we consider the scaled flows instead:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\overline{r}^{2}_{i}}g^{i}_{t_{i}+\overline{r}_{i}^{2}t}.

These new flows will still be name
g^{i}_{t}
to avoid notational complexity. In this way, we have obtained a sequence of Ricci flows
\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{x_{i},0}(T_{i}/2)\geq 1-\epsilon_{i},

\displaystyle r_{\Rm}(x_{i},0)=1,

where we have defined

\displaystyle T_{i}:=\frac{2r_{i}^{2}}{\overline{r}_{i}^{2}}>2.

Indeed, the normalized sequence is noncollapsed.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x_{i},0}(1)\geq-Y(n)\quad\text{ for all }\quad i.

Proof.

By Perelman’s no local collapsing theorem
[
Pe02
, §7.3]
(see also
[
MT07
, Theorem 8.1]
), we have

\displaystyle\Vol_{g^{i}_{0}}\big(B_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i},1)\big)\geq c(n)\quad\text{ for all }\quad i.

On the other hand, by the standard maximum principle for scalars, we have

\displaystyle R_{g^{i}}\geq-\frac{n}{2}\quad\text{ on }\quad M^{i}\times[-1,0]

for all
i
. The lemma then follows from
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 8.1]
.
∎

Now, we can apply the theory of
[
Bam23
]
to extract a (not relabeled) subsequence from
\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]}\}
, such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle((M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]},(\nu^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]})\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447pti\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt]{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t<0}),

where
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t<0})
is a metric flow pair defined on
(-T_{\infty},0)
, where

, and

\displaystyle d\nu^{i}_{t}=K(x_{i},0\,|\,\cdot,t)\,dg^{i}_{t}=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{i}(\cdot,t)}\,dg^{i}_{t}

is the conjugate heat kernel based at
(x_{i},0)
.

The limit metric flow
\mathcal{X}
admits a decomposition
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{R}\cupdot\mathcal{S}
. We shall denote by

\displaystyle K_{t}:=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{X}_{t}\cap K,

\displaystyle K_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}:=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{X}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}\cap K,

the time-slice and time-slab of a set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
, respectively. We remark here that Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
can be applied to the limit flow
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t<0})
and to the convergence (
4.3
). In particular,

\displaystyle d\nu_{t}=(4\pi|t|)e^{-f}\,dg_{t}\quad\text{ on }\quad\mathcal{R},

where
g_{t}
is the Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{R_{t}}
, and

is a positive solution to the conjugate heat equation.

We define

\displaystyle\ell_{i}:=\overline{\ell}_{x_{i},0}.

Note that
\overline{\ell}_{x_{i},0}
is (the forward time version of) the reduced distance (

) based at
(x_{i},0)
. Our goal is to analyse the possible convergence of

Lastly, we recall the following useful estimate due to Bamler.

Since

\displaystyle\ \nu_{t^{\prime}}\big(B_{t^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{y}(t^{\prime}),\epsilon r)\big)

\displaystyle\ 2c>0,

f\equiv\ell
on
\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}
.

Proof.

Let us fix an arbitrary
\tau\in(0,1)
and let
K\subset\mathcal{R}_{-\tau}
be a compact set. Then by Lemma
4.4
and the local convergence, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{K}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}(e^{-f}-e^{-\ell})\,dg_{-\tau}=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\psi_{i}(K)}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}(e^{-f_{i}}-e^{-\ell_{i}})\,dg^{i}_{-\tau}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{M^{i}}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}(e^{-f_{i}}-e^{-\ell_{i}})\,dg^{i}_{-\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}(1-\mathcal{V}_{x_{i},0}(\tau))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0,

by the assumption (
4.1
). The lemma follows immediately.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

The following holds on
\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle-2\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}-R_{g_{t}}+\frac{f}{\tau}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}+R_{g_{t}}+\frac{f-n}{\tau}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

where
\tau=-t=|t|
.

Proof.

Since each
\ell_{x_{i},0}
satisfies (
2.5
) in the sense of distribution, and the functions
\ell_{i}\circ\psi_{i}=\overline{\ell}_{x_{i},0}\circ\psi_{i}
converge to
\ell
in the
C^{0,\alpha}_{\operatorname{loc}}
and weak
*W^{1,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}
senses on
\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}
, we have that
\ell
(hence
f
) satisfies the first equation of (
4.4
) in the sense of distribution on
\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}
. While
f
is smooth on
\mathcal{R}
, we obtain the first equation in the classical sense. The second equation is a combination of the first with the conjugate heat equation.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

By
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 2.18]
and
[
CMZ24
, Theorem 6.3]
, we need only to show that
\mathcal{N}(\tau)\equiv 0
for all
\tau\in(0,1)
.

Let us define

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f},

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\tau(2\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}+R_{g_{t}})+f-n\right)u,

where
\tau=-t=|t|
. Perelman’s computation
[
Pe02
, Proposition]
shows that

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g_{t}}+R_{g_{t}}\right)v=-2\tau\left|\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}{g_{t}}\right|^{2}u,

holds on the smooth part
\mathcal{R}_{(0,1)}
. Since
v\equiv 0
and
u>0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g=0\qquad\text{ on }\qquad\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}.

Combining the trace of the above equation with the second equation in (
4.4
), we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\tau\big(\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}\big)+f-\frac{n}{2}=0\qquad\text{ on }\qquad\mathcal{R}_{(-1,0)}.

Claim.

For any
T\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{t}}\tau(\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}\big)\,d\nu_{t}dt=\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{t}}\tau(\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}\big)\,(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}\,dg_{t}dt=0.

Proof of the claim.

Let us fix any
T\in(0,1/2)
and let
\delta>0
be an arbitrary small number. By Theorem
2.6
(a), we fix
j
large enough, so that
U_{j}^{\prime}:=U_{j}\cap\mathcal{R}_{[-2T,-T]}
(
U_{j}
is as in the statement of Theorem
2.7
) satisfies that

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{U^{\prime}_{j,t}}\,d\nu_{t}dt>T-\delta,\qquad\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{t}\setminus{U^{\prime}_{j,t}}}\,d\nu_{t}dt<\delta.

Furthermore, local smooth convergence implies that

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{M^{i}\setminus\psi_{i}({U^{\prime}_{j,t}})}\,d\nu^{i}_{t}dt=

\displaystyle\ T-\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{\psi_{i}({U^{\prime}_{j,t}})}\,d\nu^{i}_{t}dt<T-(T-2\delta)=2\delta

whenever
i\gg j
. Thus,

\displaystyle\ \left|\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{{U^{\prime}_{j,t}}}\tau(\Delta_{g_{t}}f-|\nabla f|^{2}_{g_{t}}\big)\,d\nu_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}\left|\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{\psi_{i}({U^{\prime}_{j,t}})}\tau(\Delta_{g^{i}_{t}}f_{i}-|\nabla f_{i}|^{2}_{g^{i}_{t}}\big)\,d\nu^{i}_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}\left|\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{M^{i}\setminus\psi_{i}({U^{\prime}_{j,t}})}\tau(\Delta_{g^{i}_{t}}f_{i}-|\nabla f_{i}|^{2}_{g^{i}_{t}}\big)\,d\nu^{i}_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \lim_{i\to\infty}2T\left(\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{M^{i}\setminus\psi_{i}({U^{\prime}_{j,t}}t)}\,d\nu^{i}_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\int_{-2T}^{-T}\int_{M^{i}}\left(\left|\Delta_{g^{i}_{t}}f_{i}\right|^{2}+|\nabla f_{i}|^{4}_{g^{i}_{t}}\right)\,d\nu^{i}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C(Y,T,n)\delta^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we have applied Bamler’s integral estimates (Proposition
4.2
). The claim follows from taking
j\to\infty
and
\delta\to 0
.
∎

Combining (
4.5
) and the claim above, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\mathcal{N}(\tau)\,d\tau=0

for all
T\in(0,1/2)
. Since
\mathcal{N}(\tau)\leq 0
for all
\tau>0
(
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 2.11]
), we have that
\mathcal{N}(\tau)\equiv 0
for all
\tau\in(0,1)
.

∎

4.4.
Completion of the proof

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By
§4.1—§4.3, a contradictory sequence satisfying (
4.1
) converges, after passing to a subsequence, to the Euclidean space in the local-smooth sense over the time interval
(-1,0)
. The definition of local smooth convergence shows that, for any
\delta>0
, the geometry in

\displaystyle B_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i},\delta^{-1})\times[-(1-\delta),-\delta]

must be very Euclidean-like in the smooth sense, whenever
i
is large enough. This fact, combined with Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem
[
Pe02
, Theorem 10.1]
and Bamler’s backward pseudolocality theorem
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 2.47]
, shows that the curvature on

\displaystyle B_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i},\delta^{-1})\times[-1,0]

also converges to
0
uniformly. This is a contradiction to (
4.1
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

We implement a similar argument of contradiction as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Assume for some
\epsilon>0
the Corollary fails, then we can find a sequence of counterexamples, which, after proper scaling and shifting of time, satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of Ricci flows, each with bounded curvature, and
T_{i}\geq 1
;

(2)

\mathcal{V}_{x_{i},0}(T_{i})\to 1
;

(3)

\operatorname{Vol}_{g^{i}_{0}}\big(B_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i},r_{i}^{\prime})\big)\leq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{n}(r_{i}^{\prime})^{n}
for some
r_{i}^{\prime}\in(0,\epsilon^{-1})
.

By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we have
\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\in[-1/2,0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges to the static Euclidean space in the Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton sense. This contradicts (3) above.
∎

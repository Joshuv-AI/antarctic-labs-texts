Defects and type D relativistic Toda lattice for some 5d gauge theories
Kimyeong Lee, and Norton Lee

Abstract
We perform folding on the ADHM construction of the instanton moduli space from S ​ U SU to S ​ O SO group. A Young diagram description for the S ​ O SO instanton is obtained after modifying the real and complex moment maps of the ADHM data. We study the Bethe gauge correspondence between type D relativistic Toda lattice and 5d 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 folded theory. In particular we prove that the regular monodromy defect in the folded gauge theory is the stationary wavefunction of the type D relativistic Toda lattice.

Bethe/Gauge correspondence
. It has been an active research area since the ground breaking work by Seiberg and Witten

When the gauge group of the 4d gauge theory is

N

[

The stationary state of the quantum integrable system – under Bethe/Gauge correspondence – are the vacuua of the effective 2d

This beautiful correspondence has a K-theoretic uplift: The R-matrix, associated with the Ding-Iohara-Miki algebra

\mathcal{N}=2

When the gauge group

qq

In this work, our goal is to study the Bethe/Gauge correspondence between the quantum type D relativistic Toda lattice (RTL for short) and 5d

On the integrable systems side, RTL of type BCD can be treated as type A with specific boundary condition

On the gauge theory side,
We fold the

The effective potential of 3d

The main results of this paper are:

•

Establishing the folding from type A ADHM construction to type D, resulting Young diagram descriptions of instanton configuration for

•

The Bethe ansatz equation of the type D RTL is recovered from the gauge theory through both the vacuum equation of the twisted superpotential and analyticity of

•

The identification of the vacuum expectation value of the monodromy defect
\psi

Relativistic Toda lattice

The integrability of Relativistic Toda lattice (RTL for short) is characterized by the
R
-matrix
R_{a_{i},a_{j}}:V_{a_{i}}\otimes V_{a_{j}}\to V_{a_{i}}\otimes V_{a_{j}}
, satisfying the Yang-Baxter equation

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})R_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x)R_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x^{\prime})=R_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x^{\prime})R_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime}).

(1)

The
2\times 2
Lax matrix is a special case of the
R
-matrix with the choice of

V_{a_{1}}=V_{a_{2}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}:=V_{\rm aux},\ V_{a_{3}}=\mathcal{H}_{n}

on each lattice site.

\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the Hilbert space of the
n
-th particle.
V_{\rm aux}
is called the
auxiliary space
.
On each of the lattice site we define a
2\times 2
Lax operator as a
GL_{2}
-valued function
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
:

\displaystyle L_{n}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\text{sh}(x-\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n})&-Re^{-\mathtt{q}_{n}}\\
Re^{\mathtt{q}_{n}}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\text{End}(\mathcal{H}_{n}\otimes V_{\text{aux}})

(2)

where
\hat{\mathtt{p}}_{n}=\hbar\partial_{\mathtt{q}_{n}}
and
\mathtt{q}_{n}
are the canonically conjugated momentum and coordinate of the
n
-th particle.
R
is the potential constant.
For later convenience, we define

\text{sh}(x)=2\sinh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

The R-matrix acting on the space
V_{\text{aux}}\otimes V_{\text{aux}}
by

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}\text{sh}(x-x^{\prime}+\hbar)&0&0&0\\
0&\text{sh}(x-x^{\prime})&\text{sh}\hbar&0\\
0&\text{sh}\hbar&\text{sh}(x-x^{\prime})&0\\
0&0&0&\text{sh}(x-x^{\prime}+\hbar)\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

The commutation relations between two elements in the Lax operator is governed by the Yang-Baxter RLL-relation (train track relation)

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})L_{a_{1}}(x)L_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})=L_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})L_{a_{1}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})

(4)

which can be verified true by direct computation.

The monodromy matrix
\mathbf{T}(x)
of type A RTL with periodic boundary condition is an ordered product of the Lax matrices across
N
particles

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(x)=L_{N}(x)L_{N-1}(x)\cdots L_{2}(x)L_{1}(x)\in\text{End}\left(\bigotimes_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}\otimes V_{\rm aux}\right).

(5)

It is obvious that the monodromy matrix
\mathbf{T}(x)
satisfies the same Yang-Baxter equation as the Lax operator:

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{a_{1}}(x)\mathbf{T}_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})=\mathbf{T}_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{a_{1}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime}).

(6)

The spectral curve of the integrable system is defined through introduction of spectral parameter
y
:

\displaystyle\text{q-det}(\mathbf{T}(x)-y)=y^{2}-{\rm Tr}\mathbf{T}(x)y+\text{q-det}\mathbf{T}(x)=0.

(7)

3.1
SU group

The ADHM construction for the
k
-instanton of
U(N)
gauge group is given by the supersymmetric quantum mechanics of a
U(k)
gauge theory with
N
hypermultiplet in the fundamental representation. The ADHM data are defined by maps between the two vector spaces
\mathbf{N}=\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\mathbf{K}=\mathbb{C}^{k}
:

\displaystyle B_{1,2}\in\text{End}(\mathbf{K}),\ I\in\text{Hom}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{K}),\ J\in\text{Hom}(\mathbf{K},\mathbf{N}).

(17)

along with the real and complex moment maps given by

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[B_{i},B_{i}^{\dagger}\right]+II^{\dagger}-J^{\dagger}J,

(18a)

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle=[B_{1},B_{2}]+IJ.

(18b)

The instanton moduli space are constructed by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{(SU)}_{N,k}=\left\{(B_{1},B_{2},I,J)|\ \mu_{\mathbb{R}}=\zeta\text{id}_{k},\ \mu_{\mathbb{C}}=0\right\}.

(19)

Here
\zeta
is the FI-parameter. The instanton partition function for
\mathcal{N}=2
is the integration over the moduli space of instanton

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{(SU)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{q}^{k}\mathcal{Z}_{k}^{(SU)},\ \mathcal{Z}_{k}^{(SU)}=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{N,k}^{(SU)}}1.

(20)

The partition function of the supersymmetric quantum mechanics enjoys a global symmetry

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=U(N)\times U(1)_{\varepsilon}^{2}

(21)

of the ADHM construction of
U(N)
gauge group subjected to
\Omega
-deformation, with its maximal torus
\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{H}}\subset\mathsf{H}
acts naturally on the instanton moduli space
\mathfrak{M}^{(SU)}_{N,k}
, allowing further equivariant localization. The
\Omega
-deformation acts on the ADHM data by

\displaystyle(q_{1},q_{2})\cdot(B_{1},B_{2},I,J)\to(q_{1}B_{1},q_{2}B_{2},I,q_{1}q_{2}J)

(22)

where
q_{i}=e^{{\varepsilon}_{i}}
,
i=1,2
are the exponentiated
\Omega
-deformation parameters.
As a result, the partition function is computed as rational function in the equivariant parameters
\xi\in\text{Lie}(\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{H}})
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Z}_{k}^{(SU)}&=\int_{\mathfrak{M}^{(SU)}_{N,k}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{H}}}1\end{split}

(23)

The integration can be evaluated by calculating residues at appropriate poles
[
32
,
33
]
. Let us show some details here for better demonstration. The fix points on the moduli space is determined by the condition that the
U(k)
and
U(N)
transformation of the maps can be compensated by
\Omega
-deformation

\displaystyle gB_{1}g^{-1}=q_{1}B_{1},\ gB_{2}g^{-1}=q_{2}B_{2},\ gIh^{-1}=I,\ hJg^{-1}=q_{1}q_{2}J

(24)

Here
g=e^{\hat{\phi}}
,
h=e^{\hat{a}}
.
\hat{\phi}\in\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(k))
,
\hat{a}\in\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(N))
. Denote

I=\bigoplus^{N}_{\alpha=1}I_{\alpha},\quad J=\bigoplus^{N}_{\alpha=1}J_{\alpha}.

The infinitesimal analogue of the symmetry transformation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[\hat{\phi},B_{1}]={\varepsilon}_{1}B_{1},\ [\hat{\phi},B_{2}]={\varepsilon}_{2}B_{2},\ [B_{1},B_{2}]+IJ=0\\
&\hat{\phi}I_{\alpha}-I_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}=0,\ a_{\alpha}J_{\alpha}-J_{\alpha}\hat{\phi}=({\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2})J_{\alpha}\end{split}

(25)

modulo the complex gauge transformation. In the matrix form
\hat{\phi}=\text{diag}(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k})
.

Lemma:

The instanton vector space
\mathbf{K}
satisfies the stability condition:

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}=\mathbb{C}[B_{1},B_{2}]I(\mathbf{N})

(26)

when the FI-parameter
\zeta
is positive.

Proof:

Assume there exists a
\mathbf{K}_{\perp}=\mathbf{K}-\mathbb{C}[B_{1},B_{2}]I(\mathbf{N})
. We can define a projection operator
P_{\perp}:\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{K}_{\perp}
such that
P_{\perp}=P_{\perp}^{2}=P_{\perp}^{\dagger}
, and
P_{\perp}B_{1,2}=B_{1,2}P_{\perp}
,
P_{\perp}I=0
. Consider the projection on the real moment map

\displaystyle\zeta\text{id}_{\mathbf{K}_{\perp}}=P_{\perp}\mu_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\perp}=[(P_{\perp}B_{1}P_{\perp}),(P_{\perp}B_{1}P_{\perp})^{\dagger}]+[(P_{\perp}B_{2}P_{\perp}),(P_{\perp}B_{2}P_{\perp})^{\dagger}]-P_{\perp}J^{\dagger}JP_{\perp}

(27)

Taking the trace over the full vector space
\mathbf{K}=\mathbb{C}^{k}
gives

\displaystyle 0\leq\zeta{\rm Tr}(\text{id}_{\mathbf{K}_{\perp}})=-{\rm Tr}((JP_{\perp})^{\dagger}(JP_{\perp}))\leq 0

(28)

This means
\mathbf{K}_{\perp}=0
.
q.e.d
.

Furthermore, since
\mathbf{K}
is independent of
J
one can simply set
J=0
. The complex moment map immediately implies
[B_{1},B_{2}]=0
. The fix points, which are the poles in (
23
), can be labeled by
B_{1}^{i-1}B_{2}^{j-1}I_{\alpha}
,
i,j=1,2,\dots
, spamming the vector space
\mathbf{K}
. One can easily show that

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}B_{1}^{i-1}B_{2}^{j-1}I_{\alpha}=(a_{\alpha}+(i-1){\varepsilon}_{1}+(j-1){\varepsilon}_{1})B_{1}^{i-1}B_{2}^{j-1}I_{\alpha}

(29)

These fix points are classified by a set of Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(N)})
. The partition function becomes a statistic ensemble over states labeled by
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

The pseudo-measure associated to the instanton state
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is defined through the
\mathbb{E}
-functor which converts the additive Chern character class into multiplicative class:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{a}\mathsf{n}_{a}e^{\mathsf{x}_{a}}\right]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{a}\mathsf{x}_{a}^{-\mathsf{n}_{a}}&(\text{rational/4d})\\
\displaystyle\prod_{a}(\text{sh}\mathsf{x}_{a})^{-\mathsf{n}_{a}}&(\text{trigonometric/5d})\\
\displaystyle\prod_{a}\theta(e^{-\mathsf{x}_{a}};p)&(\text{elliptic/6d})\end{cases}

(30)

with
\text{sh}(x)=e^{\frac{x}{2}}-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}
. In this paper we mostly only apply the trigonometric convention which corresponds to the five dimensional gauge theory.
The pseudo measure associated to the instanton configuration
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(N)})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{N}\mathbf{K}^{*}+q_{1}q_{2}\mathbf{N}^{*}\mathbf{K}-P_{1}P_{2}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^{*}-\mathbf{M}\mathbf{K}^{*}\right].

(31)

q_{i}=e^{{\varepsilon}_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, are the exponentiated

-deformation parameters with

. Here we are abusing the notation of the vector spaces and their Chern characters

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{N}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}e^{a_{\alpha}},\quad\mathbf{K}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_{(\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j})\in\lambda^{(\alpha)}}e^{a_{\alpha}+(\mathrm{i}-1){\varepsilon}_{1}+(\mathrm{j}-1){\varepsilon}_{2}},\ \mathbf{M}=\sum_{f=1}^{N_{f}}e^{m_{f}}.\end{split}

(32)

Given a virtual character

we denote by

its dual virtual character.

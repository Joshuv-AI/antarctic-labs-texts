Intelligent predictive neural network analysis on microbes in bioconvection flow of hybrid nanoliquid under diffusion thermo and thermo diffusion effects
Authors: Humaira Kanwal, Shouki A Ebad, Fuad Alsarari, Abed Saif Ahmed Alghawli, Dhouha Choukaier, Ruzimurod Abiyev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe model optimizes microfluidic and lab-on-a-chip systems by predicting gyrotactic microbe-driven mixing and transport in the presence of linked thermal and concentration gradients. It aids in the design and operation of bioreactors and wastewater treatment units by simulating microbe dispersal, nutrient transport, and nanoparticle-assisted mass transfer for better biodegradation. It aids in targeted medication administration and biomedical experiments by predicting microbe and nanoparticle behavior in thermally heterogeneous solutions. Simulating microbial mobility in thermo-stratified, nanoparticle-laden fluids helps to inform environmental monitoring and cleanup techniques. It improves heat-exchanger and cooling technologies by utilizing bioconvective enhancement and Marangoni surface-tension phenomena. It optimizes biofuel and fermentation processes by improving microbe aggregation and stability in hybrid nanofluids. It aids in the creation of biosensors and advanced materials processing, where thermo-diffusive transport and microbial motility interact to effect performance. Artificial neural network (ANN) modeling optimized with the Bayesian Regularization technique is used in this study. The method avoids over-fitting and improves prediction accuracy. The outcomes of thermo-diffusion and diffusion-thermo properties on bioconvection flow of a hybrid nanofluid around a disk with gyrotactic microbes are investigated using homotopy analysis modeling. Introduction

Artificial neural networks and regression-based techniques are now common machine learning (ML) technologies that may effectively replace traditional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation. Conventional CFD techniques can need a substantial amount of computational time and resources, especially for complex heat transfer and fluid flow models. However, when trained using high-fidelity datasets like as those from direct numerical simulation, ML models may accurately predict critical flow variables like temperature, pressure, and velocity at a far reduced processing cost. Additionally, ML-driven approaches enhance turbulence prediction by learning intricate nonlinear flow features that are challenging to capture with conventional turbulence models. By outcome important components that maximize efficiency and minimize energy wounded, ML augments fluid flow investigation in uses ranging from industrial cooling systems to aerodynamic optimization. The transfer of species propelled by a temperature gradient, when thermal and concentration effects are closely related, is referred to as thermo-diffusion. The Soret and Dufour impacts become important in flows with discernible density fluctuations, resulting in simultaneous mass and heat transfer. Non-uniform species concentration during chemical reactions, especially in binary fluid systems, is the cause of this coupling. Supervised Learning Algorithms (SLAs)

This study’s artificial neural network is based on a feed forward multilayer perceptron (FFNN) design with nine neurons in a single hidden layer. The hidden layer used a sigmoid activation function, whereas the output layer used a pure linear (purelin) activation function. The dataset consisted of 300 samples, with 80% used for network training and the remaining 20% used to test the model’s prediction ability. Before network training, all output and input data were normalized to the range ([0,1]) to ensure numerical stability, promote convergence, and prevent variables of bigger magnitudes from dominating the learning process. To solve difficult real-world problems, MLAs have been created in a variety of fields, engineering, including science and mathematics. By completing tasks or examples, the supervised learning algorithm (SLA) simulates how humans learn. By comparing the anticipated outputs with the known desired outputs, weight modifications are made for this work, which employs the SLA. To aid in this process, the neural network (NN) is provided with a training dataset, also known as a teaching signal. By back-propagating the error between the actual and anticipated outputs during training, the model can modify its internal weights to enhance performance and guarantee that the neural network learns the intended mapping. This section provides a concise overview of the outcomes for the suggested back-propagated Bayesian Regularization approach with neural network scheme (BBRT-NNS).

Based on the performance trends, the network successfully learns the physics of the problem and maintains a strong prediction ability outside of the trained data range. Figures 2b, 3b, 4b, and 5b display the variations in training performance, gradient, and mean squared error for four representative cases from epochs 231, 179, 1000, and 519. It is clear that the ANN has learned the nonlinear mapping between the input and output variables because the predicted curves closely resemble the real data. Further information on the numerical stability and convergence properties of the suggested ANN model may be found in the error histograms shown in Figures 2d, 3d, 4d, and 5d. The mainstream of the error values concentrates around zero, there are most likely few departures from the predictions, indicating that the network has a high degree of accuracy and precision. Finally, Figures 2e, 3e, 4e, and 5e display the comprehensive regression investigation for the testing and training datasets. A high degree of connection between the actual and expected results is demonstrated by a regression constant (R = 1) and the regression line's nearly perfect arrangement with the target data. As demonstrated by the combined results from Figures 2e, 3e, 4e, and 5e, the back-propagation Bayesian-Regularization technique, when combined with the well-trained ANN model, offers a computational framework for modeling complex heat transportation phenomena that is exceptionally precise, stable, and reliable. Regression analysis also enhances the interpretability of the model by ensuring higher fidelity and forecast constancy when compared to experimental and mathematical reference datasets. This approach provides a number of advantages for contemporary thermal-fluid analysis, allowing engineering systems to be optimized with greater computing efficacy and physical realism. Figures 8(a&b) show how the Peclet number Pe affects the concentration of microorganisms (Θ). It has been shown that the concentration of microorganisms reduces as Pe increases. Because Pe describes the relative impacts of advective and diffusive transport, a rise in Pe implies that advective transport is more effective than microbiological diffusion. As a result, microorganisms are carried away from the surface more quickly, resulting in a decrease in microbe concentration and a thinner microorganism boundary layer close to the surface. This trend suggests that enhanced advective transport predominates microbial diffusion and influences the geographic distribution of motile bacteria.

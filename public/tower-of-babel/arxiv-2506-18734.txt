Can boundary configuration be tuned to optimize directional quantum steering harvesting?
Xiao-Li Huang 1 , Xiao-Ying Jiang 1 , Yu-Xuan Wang 1 , Si-Yu Liu 1 , Zejun Wang 2 , Shu-Min Wu 1

Abstract
We investigate the harvesting of quantum steering and its asymmetry between two static detectors locally interacting with a vacuum massless scalar field near an infinite, perfectly reflecting boundary. The detectors are arranged either parallel or orthogonal to the boundary, with detector B B assumed to have an energy gap greater than or equal to that of detector A A . It is interesting to observe that, with increasing distance between the detectors and the boundary, the boundary tends to suppress quantum steering in one direction while enhancing it in the opposite direction. In the case of identical detectors, steering is symmetric when they are aligned parallel to the boundary. However, orthogonal alignment breaks this symmetry due to their unequal spatial proximity to the boundary. For non-identical detectors in the parallel configuration, the steering from A A to B B ( A → B A\rightarrow B ) generally surpasses that from B B to A A ( B → A B\rightarrow A ). In contrast, when the detectors are oriented orthogonally to the boundary, the relative strength of A → B A\rightarrow B and B → A B\rightarrow A steerability depends on the interplay between the boundary effects and the detectors’ energy gap difference. Across most of the parameter space, the orthogonal alignment tends to enhance B → A B\rightarrow A steering while suppressing A → B A\rightarrow B steering compared to the parallel setup. These findings suggest that boundary configurations should be flexibly adjusted according to the directional dependence of steering harvesting in order to optimize quantum information extraction.

V
Conclusion

We have conducted a comprehensive analysis of the quantum steering harvesting behavior of two UDW detectors interacting with a vacuum scalar field in flat spacetime with a perfectly reflecting boundary. The detectors are assumed to be non-identical, with the energy gap of detector
B
greater than or equal to that of detector
A
. We considered two spatial configurations of the detector pair: parallel and orthogonal to the boundary plane. Our study focused on how the presence of the boundary and the energy gap difference affect the steering in both directions harvested by the detectors. Notably, as the detector-boundary distance
\Delta z/\sigma
increases, the boundary suppresses quantum steering in one direction while simultaneously enhancing it in the other. This demonstrates that the boundary plays an asymmetric role in different directions of quantum steering.

For the parallel configuration, our results show that the amount of steering harvested decreases with increasing inter-detector separation
L/\sigma
. In the case of identical detectors, the bidirectional steering
S^{A\rightarrow B}
(steering from
A
to
B
) and
S^{B\rightarrow A}
(steering from
B
to
A
) are equal. However, in some parameter space, as the energy gap difference increases,
S^{A\rightarrow B}
is enhanced while
S^{B\rightarrow A}
is suppressed, indicating that the energy gap difference plays a dual role-enhancing steering in one direction while inhibiting it in the other. Non-identical detectors can harvest more steering from
A
to
B
than identical ones, but at the expense of harvesting less from
B
to
A
. Moreover, we identify an optimal energy gap difference that maximizes the harvested steering, which depends on both the detector-to-boundary distance
\Delta z/\sigma
and the inter-detector separation
L/\sigma
. When the detectors are placed near the boundary (i.e., small
\Delta z/\sigma
), the harvested steering approaches zero due to strong suppression by the boundary. As
\Delta z/\sigma
increases, the boundary’s influence gradually transitions from suppressive to facilitative, and in the asymptotic regime, the steerability converges to the value in flat spacetime without a boundary. These findings underscore the dual nature of boundary effects: while proximity to the boundary can strongly suppress steering, it can also enhance steering in certain parameter regimes.

In the orthogonal configuration, we observe qualitatively similar effects, but with important quantitative differences. For identical detectors, asymmetrical positioning with respect to the boundary leads to directional asymmetry in the harvested steerability. In general, detectors aligned orthogonally to the boundary tend to harvest more steering from
B
to
A
and less from
A
to
B
compared to the parallel case. This directional sensitivity suggests that the detector-boundary configuration should be carefully tailored to optimize the extraction of quantum information, depending on the desired steering direction.

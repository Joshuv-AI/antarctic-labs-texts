Existence, uniqueness and blow-up estimates for a reaction-diffusion equation with
p

Abstract




Abstract

Let
d\in\{3,4,5,\ldots\}
and
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be open bounded with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
Q=\Omega\times(0,\infty)
and
p\in C(\overline{Q})
be such that

2<p^{-}\leq p(\cdot)\leq p^{+}<2^{*}:=\frac{2d}{d-2},

where

p^{-}:=\essinf_{(x,t)\in Q}p(x,t)

and

p^{+}:=\esssup_{(x,t)\in Q}p(x,t).

Consider the reaction-diffusion parabolic problem

(P)\hskip 9.24994pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{u_{t}}{|x|^{2}}-\Delta u=k(t)\,|u|^{p(x,t)-2}u&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
T>0
and
0\neq u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We investigate the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to
(P)
.
The upper and lower bounds on the blow-up time of the weak solution are also considered.

Introduction

Differential equations with variable-exponent growth originates from the study of non-linear elasticity, rheological and electrorheological fluids
[
13
,
1
,
8
,
21
]
.
They also occur in image processing
[
18
]
.
Therefore, a systematic study of such equations is of practical interest and potentializes their further applications in real-life situations.
Recently one can observe an exciting movement in this direction, from which many results have been established.
The development up to date has reached a state that core ideas are well-documented.
We refer the readers to
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
and the references therein for a general mathematical framework on spaces with variable exponents.

Meanwhile for various types of reaction-diffusion partial differential equations, blow-up behaviors of the solutions are common.
This means that the solutions to these equations exist only in a finite time and their energy functionals blow up when the maximal existence time has been reached.
Known methods for the investigation include the first eigenvalue method by Kaplan in 1963, the potential well method by Levine and Payne in 1970, the comparison method and other methods involving integration.
A recent overview of the account can be found in the monograph
[
16
]
.
Also confer the surveys
[
11
]
and
[
19
]
for the blow-up properties of more general evolution problems.

In this paper, we are motivated by the work
[
12
]
on a class of nonlinear heat equation with nonlinearities of
p(x,t)
-type on the one hand and the works
[
23
]
and
[
14
]
on a reaction-diffusion equation with a special diffusion process on the other hand.
It is our aim to extend the models in
[
23
]
and
[
14
]
to the setting of variable exponents.

Specifically, let
d\in\{3,4,5,\ldots\}
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be open bounded with Lipschitz boundary.
Denote
Q:=\Omega\times(0,\infty)
.
Let
p:\overline{Q}\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
satisfy

\begin{cases}p\in C^{1}(\overline{Q}),\quad p_{t}\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle 2<p^{-}\leq p(\cdot)\leq p^{+}<\frac{2^{*}}{2}+1:=\frac{\frac{2d}{d-2}}{2}+1=\frac{2(d-1)}{d-2}.\end{cases}

(1)

Here

p^{-}:=\essinf_{(x,t)\in Q}p(x,t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad p^{+}:=\esssup_{(x,t)\in Q}p(x,t).

Hereafter we set

p_{0}(x):=p(x,0)\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega.

Next let
k
be a function with the following properties:

\begin{cases}k\in C^{1}[0,\infty),\quad k(0)>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad k^{\prime}(t)\geq 0\,\,\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
k_{\infty}:=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}k(t)<\infty.\end{cases}

(2)

In this paper, we consider the following reaction-diffusion parabolic problem:

(P)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{u_{t}}{|x|^{2}}-\Delta u=k(t)\,|u|^{p(x,t)-2}u,&(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
T>0
and
0\neq u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
When
p
is a constant, our model reduces to that of
[
15
]
and
[
23
]
.
Whereas, if
\frac{u_{t}}{|x|^{2}}
and
p(x,t)
are replaced by
u_{t}
and
p(x)
respectively and
k
is constantly 1,
(P)
restores the model investigated in
[
17
]
.
For other models along our line, one may consult
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[
22
]
,
[
24
]
,
[

]
and the references therein.

Our aim here is to provide upper and lower bounds on the blow-up time of a weak solution to
(P)
the precise definitions of which is given next.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p
be given by (
1
) and
0\neq u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
A function
u(x,t)
is called a weak solution to
(P)
if
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))

with
u(0)=u_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{t}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\,dt<\infty

and
u(x,t)
satisfies

\left(\frac{u_{t}}{|x|^{2}},\varphi\right)+(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)=k(t)\,(|u|^{p(x,t)-2}\,u,\varphi)

(3)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to
(P)
.
Then we say that
T^{*}\in(0,\infty]
is the maximal existence time of
u
if
T^{*}
is the largest possible time such that
u(t)
exists for all
t\in[0,T]
with
T\in(0,T^{*})
.
Furthermore,

•

if
T^{*}=\infty
then
u
is called a global solution to
(P)
,

•

if
T^{*}<\infty
then
u
is said to blow up at
T^{*}
if

\limsup_{t\to(T^{*})^{-}}\left\|\frac{u(t)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\infty.

(4)

Hereafter, for each
t\geq 0
,
\delta>0
and
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
define the following functionals:

•

Energy functional:

J_{\delta}(u,t)=\frac{\delta}{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x,t)}\,|u(x)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx.

•

Nehari functional:

I_{\delta}(u,t)=\delta\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx.

Next for each
t\geq 0
define the following quatities:

•

Nehari’s manifold:

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(t)=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:I_{\delta}(u,t)=0\right\}.

•

Potential well depth:

d_{\delta}(t)=\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\lambda>0}J_{\delta}(\lambda u,t)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(t)}J_{\delta}(u,t).

(5)

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(t)
is non-empty for each
t\geq 0
and
\delta>0
.
Furthermore, to justify the second equality in (
5
) we argue as follows.
One has

\displaystyle d_{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\lambda>0}J_{\delta}(\lambda u,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\lambda>0}\left[\frac{\delta\,\lambda^{2}}{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{p(x,t)}}{p(x,t)}\,|u|^{p(x,t)}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\left[\frac{\delta\,\lambda_{0}^{2}}{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{0}^{p(x,t)}}{p(x,t)}\,|u|^{p(x,t)}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}J_{\delta}(\lambda_{0}u,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda_{0}u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}J_{\delta}(\lambda_{0}u,t)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(t)}J_{\delta}(v,t)

for each
t>0
and
\delta>0
, where
\lambda_{0}>0
is such that

\lambda_{0}\,\delta\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{0}^{p(x,t)-1}\,|u|^{p(x,t)}\,dx=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I_{\delta}(\lambda_{0}u,t)=0.

Taking the time-dependent exponent
p(x,t)
into account, we define

E_{\delta}(u,t)=J_{\delta}(u,t)+k_{\infty}\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x,t)}\,dx

(6)

for each
t\geq 0
,
\delta>0
and
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

For short, when
\delta=1
we will drop the sub-indices in the notation.
For example, we will simply write
J
in place of
J_{1}
.

Keeping the potential well depth and the aforementioned functionals in mind, we are now able to define the stable set as follows for each
t\geq 0
and
\delta>0
:

•

Stable set:

\Sigma_{1,\delta}(t)=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):J_{\delta}(u,t)<d_{\delta,*}\mbox{ and }I_{\delta}(u,t)>0\right\},

where

d_{\delta,*}:=\inf_{t\in[0,\infty)}d_{\delta}(t).

Note that

\displaystyle d(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}(t)}J_{\delta}(v,t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(t)}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v(x,t)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k(t)}{p^{-}}\,\int_{\Omega}|v(x,t)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\delta\,\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{-}}\right)\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\geq 0,

whence
d_{\delta,*}\in[0,\infty)
.

Observe that
J_{\delta}
,
I_{\delta}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
,
d_{\delta}
,

and
\Sigma_{1,\delta}
all depend on time, which is due to the presence of

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we discuss appropriate function spaces for our setting and some preliminary estimates to be used in the proof of the main results.
We assume throughout that
Q\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
with
N\in\mathds{N}
is open bounded with Lipschitz boundary.

2.1.
Function spaces with variable exponents

For the sake of clarity, we provide the definitions for variable exponent Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces as well as the log-continuity condition in the sense of
[
7
, Definition 4.1.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
in the sense that
s:Q\longrightarrow[1,\infty]
is measurable.
The
variable exponent Lebesgue space

L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
is defined to consist of all measurable functions
u:Q\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
such that

\varrho_{L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)}(u):=\int_{Q}|u(z)|^{s(z)}\,dz<\infty.

We endow
L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

It is well-known that
L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
so-defined is a Banach space.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
.
The
variable exponent Sobolev space

W^{1,s(\cdot)}(Q)
is defined to consist of all
u\in L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
whose distributional derivative
\partial_{j}u\in L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

The space
W^{1,s(\cdot)}(Q)
is a Banach space under the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,s(\cdot)}(Q)}:=\|u\|_{L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)}.

The following smoothness condition on the exponent
s(\cdot)
is well-known in the literature (cf.
[
7
, Definition 4.1.1]
).

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
\alpha:Q\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
is
locally log-Holder continuous
if there exists a
c_{1}>0
such that

|\alpha(\xi)-\alpha(\eta)|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/|\xi-\eta|)}

for all
\xi,\eta\in Q
.

We say that
\alpha_{T}:Q\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
satisfies the
log-Holder decay condition
if there exist constants
\alpha_{\infty}\in\mathds{R}
and
c_{2}>0
such that

|\alpha(\xi)-\alpha_{\infty}|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log(e+|\xi|)}

for all
\xi\in Q
.

We say that
\alpha:Q\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
is
globally log-Holder continuous
if it is locally log-Holder continuous and satisfies the log-Holder decay condition.

The class
\mathcal{P}^{\log}(Q)
is defined to consist of all
s\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
such that
\frac{1}{s}
is globally log-Holder continuous.

In the sequel we will implicitly make use of the following convenient facts.
A thorough account can be found in
[
7
]
.

(i)

If
s\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
with
s^{+}<\infty
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
is dense in
L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
.

(ii)

If
s\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(Q)
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
is dense in
W^{1,s(\cdot)}(Q)
.

(iii)

Let
r,s\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
be such that
r\geq s
.
Define
w\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
by

\frac{1}{w(\cdot)}=\frac{1}{s(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{r(\cdot)}.

Then
L^{r(\cdot)}(Q)\hookrightarrow L^{s(\cdot)}(Q)
provided that
1\in L^{w(\cdot)}(Q)
.
The condition
1\in L^{w(\cdot)}(Q)
is automatic when
|Q|<\infty
due to
[
7
, Lemma 3.2.12]
.

(iv)

If
s\in C(\overline{Q})
and
r\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
are such that
r^{+}<\infty
and

\essinf_{\xi\in Q}\big(s^{*}(\xi)-r(\xi)\big)>0,

then

W^{1,s(\cdot)}(Q)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{r(\cdot)}(Q).

Here

s^{*}(\xi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d\,s(\xi)}{d-s(\xi)}&\mbox{if }s(\xi)<d,\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(Confer
[
10
]
and
[
7
, Theorem 8.4.6]
.)

In what follows, for each measurable function
f:Q\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
denote

[f\geq 1]:=\{\xi\in Q:f(\xi)\geq 1\}.

The set
[f>1]
is understood likewise.
We also need the following result on zero-trace functions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
s\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)
and
u\in W^{1,s(\cdot)}_{0}(Q)
.
Then
u
,
u\,\mathds{1}_{[u\geq 1]}
,
u\,\mathds{1}_{[u<1]}

.

Proof
.

It suffices to show that
u\in W^{1,s^{-}}_{0}(Q)

Clearly

.
Let

.
Then by identifying

by
[
4

.
The rest is similar.
∎

2.2.
Fundamental inequalities

Next we present two crucial inequalities for a later development.
Let us begin with the following Hardy inequality.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
d\geq 3

Then
\displaystyle\frac{u}{|x|}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

Proof
.

This follows at once from
[

The next result is the well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
r\in[2,\infty)
,

\alpha=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-1}\in(0,1).

(7)

As a special case of Lemma

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
2<q<2^{*}
.
Then there exists a constant
S_{q}=S_{q}(d,q)>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq S_{q}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

2.3.
Energy estimates

Let
p
,
k
satisfy (
1
) and (
2
) respectively.
Let
T>0
.
Recall that for each
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
t\in[0,T)
define the following:

•

Energy functional:

J(u,t)=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x,t)}\,|u(x)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx.

•

Nehari functional:

I(u,t)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx-k(t)\,\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx.

The roles of the energy and Nehari functionals are fundamental to our analysis.
The following identities hold for them.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to
(P)
on
[0,T)
.
Then the following identities hold.

(i)

For a.e.
t_{0}\in[0,T)
one has

\displaystyle J(u(t_{0}),t_{0})

Next we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\beta\in\left(0,\frac{p^{+}}{(p^{+}-1)\,C_{1}}\,M(0)\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma\in\left(\frac{L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)\,\beta},\infty\right).

(33)

The choices of
\beta
and
\sigma
are justified below by (
35
) and (
36
) respectively.
Define the nonnegative functional

G(h)=\int_{0}^{h}L(s)\,ds+(\tau-h)\,L(0)+\beta\,(h+\sigma)^{2},

where
h\in[0,\tau]
.
Then

G^{\prime}(h)=L(h)-L(0)+2\beta\,(h+\sigma)=2\int_{0}^{h}\left(\frac{u(s)}{|x|^{2}},u_{t}(s)\right)\,ds+2\beta\,(h+\sigma)

and

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(h)+2\beta

(34)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2p^{-}\,J(u(h),h)+\left(p^{-}-2\right)\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx+2\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2p^{-}\,\left[J(u_{0},0)-\int_{0}^{h}\left(\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+k^{\prime}(s)\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x,t)}\,|u(x,s)|^{p(x,t)}\,dx+k(s)\,\mathfrak{P}(s)\right)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle{}+\left(p^{-}-2\right)\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx+2\beta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2p^{-}\,\left[J(u_{0},0)-\int_{0}^{h}\left(\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{d}{ds}\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p(x,s)}\,dx\right)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle{}+\frac{p^{-}-2}{H_{d}}\,\left\|\frac{u(h)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2p^{-}\,\left[J(u_{0},0)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\right]+2p^{-}\,\left[\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds+\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p(x,t)}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle{}+\frac{p^{-}-2}{H_{d}}\,\left\|\frac{u(h)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\beta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2p^{-}\,\left[J(u_{0},0)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\right]+2p^{-}\,\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle{}+\frac{p^{-}-2}{H_{d}}\,\left\|\frac{u(h)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\beta

for each
h\in[0,\tau]
, where we used Lemma
2.8
in the third step.

In what follows it is convenient to denote

\displaystyle\theta(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\int_{0}^{h}L(s)\,ds+\beta\,(h+\sigma)^{2}\right)\,\left(\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds+\beta\right)

\displaystyle{}-\left(\int_{0}^{h}\left(\frac{u(s)}{|x|^{2}},u_{t}(s)\right)\,ds+\beta\,(h+\sigma)\right)^{2}\geq 0

for each
h\in[0,\tau]
, where we used Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to verify the last step.

In view of Lemma
2.9
, consider

\displaystyle G(h)\,G^{\prime\prime}(h)-\frac{p+1}{2}\,(G^{\prime}(h))^{2}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(h)\,G^{\prime\prime}(h)-2p^{+}\,\left[\int_{0}^{h}\left(\frac{u(s)}{|x|^{2}},u_{t}(s)\right)\,ds+\beta\,(h+\sigma)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(h)\,G^{\prime\prime}(h)+2p^{+}\,\left[\theta(h)-\big(G(h)-(\tau-h)\,L(0)\big)\,\left(\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds+\beta\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G(h)\,G^{\prime\prime}(h)-2p^{+}\,G(h)\,\left(\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds+\beta\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G(h)\,\left[G^{\prime\prime}(h)-2p^{+}\,\left(\int_{0}^{h}\left\|\frac{u_{t}(s)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds+\beta\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G(h)\,\Bigg[-2p^{+}\,\left(J(u_{0},0)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\right)+\frac{2(p^{-}-2)}{H_{d}}\,L(h)-2(p^{+}-1)\beta\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G(h)\,\Bigg[-2p^{+}\,\left(J(u_{0},0)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\right)+\frac{2(p^{-}-2)}{H_{d}}\,L(0)-2(p^{+}-1)\beta\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{+}\,G(h)\,\Bigg[-J(u_{0},0)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{k_{\infty}}{p_{0}(x)}\,dx+\frac{p^{-}-2}{H_{d}\,p^{+}}\,L(0)-\frac{(p^{+}-1)\beta}{p^{+}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0

for all
h\in[0,\tau]
, where we used (

) and (

) in the fifth and sixth steps respectively.

Next observe that

G(0)=\tau\,L(0)+\beta\,\sigma^{2}>0

and

G^{\prime}(0)=2\beta\sigma>0.

Consequently, Lemma
2.9
implies

\tau\leq\frac{2G(0)}{(p^{+}-2)\,G^{\prime}(0)}=\frac{2\,(\tau\,L(0)+\beta\,\sigma^{2})}{2(p^{+}-2)\,\beta\sigma}=\frac{L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)\,\beta\sigma}\,\tau+\frac{\sigma}{p^{+}-2}.

This in turn yields

\tau\,\left(1-\frac{L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)\,\beta\sigma}\right)\leq\frac{\sigma}{p^{+}-2}

or equivalently

\tau\leq\frac{\sigma}{p^{+}-2}\,\left(1-\frac{L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)\,\beta\,\sigma}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\beta\,\sigma^{2}}{(p^{+}-2)\beta\sigma-L(0)}.

(36)

Minimizing this last display over the range of

in (

) leads to

\tau\leq\frac{4\,L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)^{2}\,\beta}.

(37)

Then we minimize (

) over the the range of

in (

) to see that

\tau\leq\frac{4p^{+}\,C_{1}\,L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)^{2}\,p^{+}\,M(0)}.

Lastly, (

) holds for all
\tau\in(0,T^{*})
, from we deduce that

T^{*}\leq\frac{4p^{+}\,C_{1}\,L(0)}{(p^{+}-2)^{2}\,p^{+}\,M(0)}

as required.
∎

5.
Lower bound for blow-up time

In this section we provide a lower bound for the blow-up time, which is Theorem
1.8
.
Recall from Section
4
that we define

L(t)=\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|\frac{u(t)}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for each
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Recall from the hypothesis that
T^{*}
is the blow-up time of the solution
u
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)|^{p(x,h)}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)\,\mathds{1}_{[u(h)\geq 1]}|^{p(x,h)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)\,\mathds{1}_{[u(h)<1]}|^{p(x,h)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)\,\mathds{1}_{[u(h)\geq 1]}|^{p^{+}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)\mathds{1}_{[u(h)<1]}|^{p^{-}}\,dx

for all
h\in[0,T^{*})
, where

[u(h)\geq 1]:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x,h)\geq 1\right\}.

By assumption
2<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\displaystyle\frac{4}{d-4}
, which leads to

0<\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}<2\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}<2

where
\alpha^{\pm}
are given in Lemma
2.6
.
Now in view of Lemma
2.4
we have

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(h)

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u(h)}{|x|^{2}},u_{t}(h)\right)=-I(u(h),h)=k(h)\,\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)|^{p(x,h)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{\infty}\,\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)\,\mathds{1}_{[u(h)\geq 1]}|^{p^{+}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u(x,h)\mathds{1}_{[u(h)<1]}|^{p^{-}}\,dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{\infty}\,\left(N_{p^{+}}\,\|\nabla u(h)\|_{L^{2}([u(h)\geq 1])}^{\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}([u(h)\geq 1])}^{(1-\alpha^{+})\,p^{+}}+N_{p^{-}}\,\|\nabla u(h)\|_{L^{2}([u(h)<1])}^{\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}([u(h)<1])}^{(1-\alpha^{-})\,p^{-}}\right)

\displaystyle{}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[\nabla u(h)\geq 1]}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{[\nabla u(h)<1]}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,h)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle{}+\frac{2-\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{+}}\,\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}\right)^{-\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}/(2-\alpha\,p^{+})}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\gamma^{+}}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{2-\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{-}}\,\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}}\right)^{-\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}/(2-\alpha\,p^{-})}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\gamma^{-}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2-\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{+}}\,\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}\right)^{-\alpha\,p^{+}/(2-\alpha^{+}\,p^{+})}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\gamma^{+}}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{2-\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{-}}\,\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}}\right)^{-\alpha^{-}\,p^{-}/(2-\alpha^{-}\,p^{-})}\,\|u(h)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\gamma^{-}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2-\alpha\,p^{+}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{+}}\,\alpha\,p^{+}}\right)^{-\alpha\,p^{+}/(2-\alpha\,p^{+})}\,(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{4\gamma^{+}}\,L(h)^{\gamma^{+}}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{2-\alpha\,p^{-}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{-}}\,\alpha\,p^{-}}\right)^{-\alpha\,p^{-}/(2-\alpha\,p^{-})}\,(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{4\gamma^{-}}\,L(h)^{\gamma^{-}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{*}\,\left(L(h)^{\gamma^{+}}+L(h)^{\gamma^{-}}\right)

for all
h\in(0,T^{*})
, where

\displaystyle C^{*}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{2-\alpha\,p^{+}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{+}}\,\alpha\,p^{+}}\right)^{-\alpha\,p^{+}/(2-\alpha\,p^{+})}\,(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{4\gamma^{+}},\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\left.\frac{2-\alpha\,p^{-}}{2}\,\left(\frac{2}{k_{\infty}\,N_{p^{-}}\,\alpha\,p^{-}}\right)^{-\alpha\,p^{-}/(2-\alpha\,p^{-})}\,(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{4\gamma^{-}}\right\},

\gamma^{+}:=\frac{(1-\alpha^{+})\,p^{+}}{2}\,\left(1-\frac{\alpha^{+}\,p^{+}}{m^{-}}\right)^{-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma^{-}:=\frac{(1-\alpha^{-})\,p^{-}}{2}\,\left(1-\frac{\alpha^{-}\,p^{+}}{m^{-}}\right)^{-1}

and we applied Lemma
2.6
in the fourth step and Young’s inequality in the fifth step.
Equivalently one has

\frac{L^{\prime}(h)}{L(h)^{\gamma^{+}}+L(h)^{\gamma^{-}}}\leq C^{*},

from which we obtain

\int_{L(t_{0})}^{L(t)}\frac{ds}{s^{\gamma^{+}}+s^{\gamma^{-}}}\leq C^{*}\,(t-t_{0})

Lastly, using
\gamma^{\pm}>1
and
\lim_{t\to T^{*}}L(t)=\infty
, we send
t\longrightarrow T^{*}
in the above inequality to obtain

T^{*}\geq t_{0}+\frac{1}{C^{*}}\,\int_{L(t_{0})}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^{\gamma^{+}}+s^{\gamma^{-}}}

as required.

On the Interplay of Cube Learning and Dependency Schemes in QCDCL Proof Systems
Abhimanyu Choudhury, Meena Mahajan

Abstract
Quantified Conflict Driven Clause Leaning (QCDCL) is one of the main approaches to solving Quantified Boolean Formulas (QBF). Cube-learning is employed in this approach to ensure that true formulas can be verified. Dependency Schemes help to detect spurious dependencies that are implied by the variable ordering in the quantifier prefix of QBFs but are not essential for constructing (counter)models. This detection can provably shorten refutations in specific proof systems, and is expected to speed up runs of QBF solvers. The simplest underlying proof system [BeyersdorffBöhm-LMCS2023], formalises the reasoning in the QCDCL approach on false formulas, when neither cube-learning nor dependency schemes is used. The work of [BöhmPeitlBeyersdorff-AI2024] further incorporates cube-learning. The work of [ChoudhuryMahajan-JAR2024] incorporates a limited use of dependency schemes, but without cube-learning. In this work, proof systems underlying the reasoning of QCDCL solvers which use cube learning, and which use dependency schemes at all stages, are formalised. Sufficient conditions for soundness and completeness are presented, and it is shown that using the standard and reflexive resolution path dependency schemes ( 𝙳 𝚜𝚝𝚍 \mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}} and 𝙳 𝚛𝚛𝚜 \mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}} ) to relax the decision order provably shortens refutations. When the decisions are restricted to follow quantification order, but dependency schemes are used in propagation and learning, in conjunction with cube-learning, the resulting proof systems using the dependency schemes 𝙳 𝚜𝚝𝚍 \mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}} and 𝙳 𝚛𝚛𝚜 \mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}} are investigated in detail and their relative strengths are analysed.

Introduction

Despite the NP-hardness of the satisfiability problem, in the
last three decades SAT solvers have been phenomenally
successful in solving instances of humongous size, and have
become the go-to tool in many practical industrial
applications (see e.g.
[
32
,
24
]
).
This success has spurred ambitious programs to develop solvers
for computationally even more hard problems. In particular,
the PSPACE-complete problem of determining the truth of
Quantified Boolean Formulas QBFs has many more applications
(see e.g.
[
29
]
), and over the last
twenty years QBF solvers have rapidly approached the state of
industrial applicability.

The paradigm that revolutionized SAT solving is Conflict Driven Clause
Learning CDCL (
[
30
]
), and this is also one of
the principal approaches (but not the only one) in QBF solving. The
CDCL technique was lifted to QBFs in the form of QCDCL
(
[
33
]
, see also
[
19
]
; in
[
21
]
, the term QDPLL is used), and
implemented in state-of-the-art solvers DepQBF

[
22
,
23
]
and Qute
[
25
]

with further augmentations to enhance performance.

For both SAT and QBF, solving techniques are intricately connected
with proof systems. The runtime trace of a solver on a formula can be
thought of as a proof of the final outcome (sat/unsat,
true/false). Proof systems abstract out the reasoning employed in the
solvers, and allow representing these traces-as-proofs as formal
proofs. The CDCL paradigm in SAT solvers corresponds to resolution, a
very well-studied proof system. There are multiple
ways in which resolution can be lifted to QBFs, see

[
5
]
for an overview. As
shown in
[
4
]
, resolution proofs can be efficiently
extracted from traces of CDCL-based SAT solvers. For QBFs, QCDCL
traces yield proofs in the proof system long-distance Q-resolution

\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}

[
33
,
3
]
. However, the
converse direction, going from resolution proofs to CDCL runs,
famously shown for SAT in

[
27
,
1
]
, seemingly breaks
down for QBF and QCDCL as currently implemented; the reasoning
employed in basic QCDCL solvers was abstracted in

[
7
,
15
]
as the proof systems

\mathtt{QCDCL}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
, and shown to be exponentially weaker than
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
.

The proof system
\mathtt{QCDCL}
is a refutational proof system; it was
formulated in
[
7
]
to explain the reasoning of basic QCDCL-style algorithms on
false QBFs. The proof system

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
, defined in
[
15
]
, incorporates cube-learning as
well, and can thus certify both falsity and truth of
QBFs. Intriguingly, it was shown in
[
15
]
that even
for false QBFs, where cube-learning is not necessary for completeness,
it can still significantly shorten refutations.
Very recently, it was
shown in
[
8
]
that even when short
refutations are actually found, it may take an exponentially long time to
find them.
Many other variants (different policies for decision order, propagations, reductions) have been studied extensively in
[
14
]
.

One heuristic that has been used in some QCDCL solvers is the use of
dependency schemes. These schemes involve performing some basic
analysis of the formula structure and identifying spurious
dependencies amongst variables, dependencies that are implied by the
quantification order of variables but are not necessary for
constructing (counter)models; see
for instance
[
31
]
. Eliminating such dependencies would
transform a QBF to a Dependency QBF, DQBF, for which the computational
problem of deciding truth/falsity is even harder; it is NEXP-complete
(
[
2
,
12
]
). However, retaining the
formulas as a QBF, and using information about spurious
dependencies while propagating and learning, is still a
feasible approach, that has been implemented in the solver DepQBF

[
22
,
23
]
using the
standard dependency scheme

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
. In resolution-based QBF proof
systems, employing reduction rules based on the
reflexive
resolution path dependency-scheme

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, is known to exponentially
shorten refutations (
[
10
]
), and the
expectation is that a similar advantage also manifests in QCDCL
solvers.

This work makes progress towards formally understanding the
strengths/limitations of using the dependency-scheme heuristic. The first
steps in this direction were initiated in a recent work in

[
17
]
. It considered the simplest setting, in
which the
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof system uses the
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
decision policy
(deciding variables according to the quantification order), and does
not learn cubes. A
dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
is used in propagation by, and learning of,
clauses. Additionally, a dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}^{\prime}
may be used to
preprocess the formula, reducing all clauses according to
\mathtt{D}^{\prime}

before beginning the QCDCL trails. In this setting,
when
\mathtt{D}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\prime}
are “normal” schemes (as
defined in
[
26
]
), the resulting proof systems were shown to be
sound and refutationally complete.
In the same setting, the four systems arising from using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in preprocessing, in propagation/learning, in both, and in neither,
were shown to be incomparable with each other.
In the
underlying proof system
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
, using dependency information can
never lengthen proofs. The handicap in QCDCL arises because QCDCL
algorithms also need to search for the proof.

In this work, we consider more general settings. Our contributions
are as follows:

Formalising intensive use of dependency schemes in QCDCL:

We formalise the definitions of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
proof
systems that use dependency schemes more intensively: in the decision
policy, which determines which variables can be ”decided” at a particular stage, as well as in propagation and learning, with and without cube
learning. Using a dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}_{1}
in the decision policy
means that a variable can be decided if all variables on which it
depends, according to
\mathtt{D}_{1}
, are already assigned; this is the policy

{\mathtt{D}_{1}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
. Using a dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}_{2}
in propagation and
learning means that reductions enabled by
\mathtt{D}_{2}
are performed
whenever possible. For two dependency schemes
\mathtt{D}_{1}
and
\mathtt{D}_{2}

(which may be the same) we consider
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
proof systems that
use
\mathtt{D}_{1}
in the decision policy and
\mathtt{D}_{2}
in propagation through and
learning of clauses. We consider three scenarios with respect to cube-learning:
(1) cube-learning is switched off completely; (2) cube propagation and
learning is done without using any dependency schemes; or (3) cube
propagation and learning use the scheme
\mathtt{D}_{2}
but disallow
long-distance term-resolution. The reason for this difference between
clause and cube learning is that long-distance term resolution is not
(yet?) known to be sound if used in conjunction with dependency
schemes.
We show that for normal
\mathtt{D}_{1}
,
\mathtt{D}_{2}
, the resulting systems are
sound and refutationally complete;
\cref
thm:depord-sound-complete.

Provable advantage of
{\mathtt{D}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
:

We show that other parameters remaining the same, using either

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
as
\mathtt{D}_{1}
is strictly better than using
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
;
\cref
thm:depord-levord.

Using cube-learning, and dependency schemes only in propagation/learning:

When the decision policy is restricted to
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
, we generalise the results from

[
17
]
to settings with cube-learning switched on, and also to settings where
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
rather than
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is used. Specifically,
we show that

1.

Using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
in pre-processing is useless;
\cref
prop:DtrvDstd-nopre.

2.

Switching on cube learning provably adds power
even in the presence of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
;

\cref
thm:QCDCLcubeD-stronger.

3.

Adding
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in various non-trivial ways to

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
results in proof systems that are not only pairwise
incomparable,
\cref
thm:QCDCLcubeDrrs-incomparable, but are also incomparable with both

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
and
\mathtt{QCDCL}
;
\cref
thm:QCDCLcubeDrrs-QCDCLcube,thm:QCDCLcubeDrrs-DrrsQCDCL.

4.

Adding
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
to
\mathtt{QCDCL}
is orthogonal to switching on
cube learning;
\cref
thm:DstdCube-incomp. In certain cases, adding
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}

to both
\mathtt{QCDCL}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
offers a provable advantage.

5.

Although
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
strictly refines
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
, in
the context of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
, adding these schemes
gives rise to incomparable systems;
\cref
thm:DstdDrrs-incomp. Thus, the
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
policy can negate
potential benefits of the strict refinement.

We use several known bounds on formulas from earlier
works, and also show some new bounds for them. To obtain desired separations, we also introduce three carefully handcrafted new formulas.
For easy reference, the known and new results (about
previously defined and new formulas) are collated in

\cref
tab:for-sideways in
\cref
sec:QCDCLcuberesults. The known simulation order of the proof systems, incorporating prior known results as well as the new results proved here, are summarised in
\cref
fig:all-figures, also in
\cref
sec:QCDCLcuberesults.

Organisation of the paper:

In
\cref
sec:Prelim, we give some basic definitions and describe the background about known proof systems and dependency schemes. In
\cref
sec:QCDCLDep, we define the new proof systems, show soundness and completeness, and show that the decision policy
{\mathtt{D}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
is strictly more powerful than
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
. In
\cref

2.1
Basic Notation

We follow notation from

[
7
,
15
]
; see also
[
5
]
.
Selected relevant items are included here.

A literal
\ell
is a Boolean variable
x
or its negation
\bar{x}
, and
\mathtt{var}(\ell)
denotes the associated variable
x
. A clause is a disjunction of literals; a term or a cube is a conjunction of literals. For a clause or cube
C
,
\mathtt{var}(C)
denotes the set
\{\mathtt{var}(\ell)\mid\ell\in C\}
. A propositional formula
\varphi
is built from variables using conjunction, disjunction, and negation; it is in conjunctive normal form (CNF) if it is a conjunction of clauses. For a formula
\varphi
, a variable
x
in
\varphi
, and a Boolean value
a
,
\varphi|_{x=a}
refers to the formula obtained by substituting
x=a
everywhere in
\varphi
. For a set
S
of clauses and a literal
\ell
, we use shorthand
\ell\vee S
to denote the set of clauses
\{\ell\vee C\mid C\in S\}
. The
empty clause is denoted
\square
and is unsatisfiable; the empty cube is denoted
\top
and is always true. A clause (cube) is said to be tautological (contradictory) if for some variable
x
it contains both
x
and
\bar{x}
.

The resolution rule can be applied to clauses and to cubes.
The resolvent of clauses
A^{\prime}=A\vee x
and
B^{\prime}=B\vee\bar{x}
is the clause
A\vee B
denoted as
\mathtt{res}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},x)
or
\mathtt{res}(B^{\prime},A^{\prime},x)
. The resolvent of cubes
A^{\prime}=A\wedge x
and
B^{\prime}=B\wedge\bar{x}
is the cube
A\wedge B
, also denoted as
\mathtt{res}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},x)
or
\mathtt{res}(B^{\prime},A^{\prime},x)
.

A Quantified Boolean Formula (QBF) in prenex conjunction normal form (PCNF) is a prefix with a list of variables, each quantified either existentially
or universally, and a matrix, which is a set (conjunction) of clauses over these variables. That is, it has the form

\Phi=\mathcal{Q}\vec{x}\cdot\varphi=Q_{1}x_{1}Q_{2}x_{2}\ldots Q_{n}x_{n}~~\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

where
\varphi
is a propositional formula in CNF, and each
Q_{i}
is in
\{\exists,\forall\}
. We denote by
X_{\exists}
(
X_{\forall}
respectively) the set of all variables quantified existentially (resp. universally).

A QBF is true if for each existentially quantified variable
x_{i}
, there exists a (Skolem) function
s_{i}
, depending only on universally quantified variables
x_{j}
with
j<i
, such that
substituting these
s_{i}
in
\varphi
yields a tautology. Similarly, the formula is false if for each universally quantified variable
u_{i}
, there is a (Herbrand) function
h_{i}
, depending only on existentially quantified variables
x_{j}
with
j<i
, such that substituting
h_{i}
in
\varphi
yields an unsatisfiable formula.

2.2
Some QBF proof systems, and the Dependency Scheme heuristic

The propositional proof system Resolution certifies unsatisfiability of a propositional formula by adding clauses obtaining through resolution until the empty clause is added. This can be lifted to QBFs in many ways. One of the simplest ways is to use the resolution rule along with a universal reduction rule, that allows removing a universal literal
u
or
\bar{u}
from a clause if it is not ‘
blocked
; that is, the clause has no existential literals quantified after
u
in the prefix. This gives rise to the system
\mathtt{QU}
-
\mathtt{Res}
; its restriction where resolution is allowed only on existential pivots is the system
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
. The long-distance resolution rule generalises resolution by permitting seemingly useless universal tautological clauses under certain side-conditions, and gives rise to the system
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
that generalises
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
. Informally, in this system, a resolution on
x
is permitted even if the resolvent ends up having
u
and
\bar{u}
for some universal variable
u
, provided
u
is quantified to the right of
x
. The presence of
u
and
\bar{u}
together, often referred to as a
merged literal

u^{*}
, is to be interpreted not as a tautology but as a place-holder for a partial strategy for
u
depending on the value of the pivot
x
.

In direct analogy to
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
and
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
are the proof systems
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
and
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
for certifying truth. Here the resolution is performed on terms, or cubes, with universal pivots, and existential literals can be reduced from a term if not blocked by universal literals quantified after them. The goal is to derive the empty term. A key point of difference is at the start; since the QBF is in PCNF, there are no terms to begin with. The Axiom rule in these systems permits starting with any term that satisfies the matrix.

For formal definitions of these proof systems, see for instance, Figure 2 in
[
9
]
(for
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
,
\mathtt{QU}
-
\mathtt{Res}
,
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
), Figure 2, in
[
31
]
(for
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
). To help readability, we also include the definitions of the rules in the appendix.

The notion of blocking, used in the reduction rules, stems from the understanding that if variables
x,y
are quantified differently with
y
quantified after
x
, then the value of
y
in a (counter)model may non-trivially
depend
on
x
. If there is no literal blocking
x
, then the satisfaction of the clause (falsification of cube) should not rely on the unblocked universal (resp. existential)
x
. The dependency scheme heuristic refines this further. If a syntactic examination of the clause-variable structure can reveal that
y
does not really depend on
x
,
even though it is quantified later
, then
x
can be reduced even in the presence of
y
. This can drive the process towards the empty clause/term faster. Dependency schemes do precisely this. They identify pairs
(x,y)
where
x
and
y
are quantified differently and where
y
can be safely assumed to be independent of
x
. (Actually, they list pairs where
y
may depend on
x
; the other pairs can be assumed to be independent.)
The trivial dependency scheme associates with each QBF
\Phi
the dependency set
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)=\{(x,y)\mid\textrm{$y$ is quantifed after, and differently from, $x$}\}
. Other schemes can associate subsets of this set. The schemes relevant to this paper are the standard scheme
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
(Def 7 in
[
28
]
and Def 9 in
[
31
]
), and the reflexive resolution scheme
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
(Defs 3,4,6 in
[
31
]
); these definitions are reproduced below. For every
\Phi
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)\supseteq\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)\supseteq\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)
.

Definition 1

(Standard Dependency Scheme,
[
31
, Def 9]
)
.

For a PCNF QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}\vec{x}\cdot\varphi
, the pair
(x,y)
is in
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)
if and only if
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
and there exists a sequence of clauses
C_{1},\cdots,C_{n}\in\varphi
and a sequence of existential literals
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n-1}
such that:

•

x\in C_{1}
and
y\in C_{n}
, and

•

for each
i\in[n-1]
,
(x,\mathtt{var}(\ell_{i}))\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
,
\mathtt{var}(\ell_{i})\in\mathtt{var}(C_{i})
, and
\mathtt{var}(\ell_{i})\in\mathtt{var}(C_{i+1})
.

Definition 2

(Reflexive Resolution Path Dependency Scheme,
[
31
, Defs 3,4,6]
)
.

Fix any PCNF QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}\vec{x}\cdot\varphi
.

An ordered pair of literals
\ell_{1},\ell_{2k}
is connected (via a resolution path) if there is a sequence of clauses
C_{1},\cdots,C_{k}\in\varphi
and a sequence of existential literals
\ell_{2},\cdots,\ell_{2k-1}
such that:

•

\ell_{1}\in C_{1}
and
\ell_{2k}\in C_{k}
,

•

For each
i\in[k-1]
,
\ell_{2i}=\neg\ell_{2i+1}
.

•

For each
i\in[k-1]
,
(\mathtt{var}(\ell_{1}),\mathtt{var}(\ell_{2i}))\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
.

•

For each
i\in[k]
,
\mathtt{var}(\ell_{2i-1})\neq\mathtt{var}(\ell_{2i})
.

•

For each
i\in[k]
,
\ell_{2i-1},\ell_{2i}\in C_{i}
.

An ordered pair of variables
(x,y)
is a resolution-path dependency pair if
both
(x,y)
and
(\neg x,\neg y)
are connected, or if
both
(x,\neg y)
and
(\neg x,y)
are connected.

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)=\{(x,y)\mid(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}};\textrm{$(x,y)$ is
a resolution-path dependency pair}\}.

Roughly,
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)
if there is a sequence of clauses with the first containing
x
or
\bar{x}
, the last containing
y
or
\bar{y}
, and each pair of consecutive clauses containing an existential variable quantified to the right of
x
. For
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
scheme,
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)
if
(x,y)
and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
or
(x,\bar{y})
and
(\bar{x},y)
are connected by a sequence of clauses, each pair of consecutive clauses containing an existential variable in opposite polarities quantified to the right of
x
.

When a dependency scheme
D
is incorporated into any of the preceding proof systems, the reduction rule becomes more generally applicable, and the side-conditions concerning merged literals also become more permissive. This gives rise to the proof systems
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
,
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
,
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
,
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
. See, for instance, Figure 3 and Section 3.3 in
[
31
]
(for
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
and
\mathtt{D}
-reductions), and Figure 1 in
[
26
]
(for
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
). For a clause and a dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
, we denote by
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(C)
the clause obtained by removing all unblocked universal literals from
C
. Similarly, for a cube
C
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}_{\exists}(C)
denotes the cube obtained by removing all unblocked existential literals from
C
. We denote by
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(\Phi)
the QBF
\Psi
obtained by replacing each clause
C
in the matrix of
\Phi
with the clause
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(C)
. When
\mathtt{D}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
, we use the notation
\mathtt{red}(C)
and
\mathtt{red}(\Phi)
.

An important subclass of dependency schemes are the so-called
normal
dependency schemes, which have the property of being ”monotone” and ”simple”. See Def. 7 in
[
26
]
for the precise definition.
(Though we will not need the precise definitions, for completeness, we include the definition of normal dependency schemes in the appendix.)
The dependency schemes
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
are all normal. This class of schemes is of interest to us because it is known that for normal dependency schemes,
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-

2.3
The proof systems
\mathtt{QCDCL}
with and without cube learning

The proof system for
\mathtt{QCDCL}
, as defined in
[
7
]
, formalizes reasoning in QCDCL algorithms operating on false formulas without cube learning. These algorithms construct
trails
or partial assignments in a specific way –
decide
values of variables according to some policy,
propagate
values of existential variables that appear in clauses which become unit after restriction by the trail so far and by universal reduction (call such a clause the
antecedent
of the propagated literal) – trying to satisfy the matrix. If a conflict is reached, then the trail is inconsistent with any Skolem function.
Conflict analysis
is performed, and a new clause is
learnt
and added to the matrix. If the empty clause is learned, the formula is deemed false. The corresponding refutation in the proof system
\mathtt{QCDCL}
consists of the sequence of constructed trails, and for each trail the sequence of long-distance resolution steps performed in conflict analysis to learn a clause. The full definition can be found in
[
7
]
(Def. 3.5).

The above formulation of the
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system only considers trails
that end in a conflict. Trails ending in a satisfying assignment are ignored. This is enough to ensure refutational completeness – the ability to prove all false QBFs false. However, from satisfying assignments, solvers can and do learn cubes (or terms), and this is necessary to prove true QBFs true. In
[
15
]
it was shown that even while refuting false QBFs, allowing cube learning from satisfying assignments can be advantageous. This led to the definition of the proof system
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{cube}}
, and in
[
15
]
, it was shown to be strictly stronger than the standard
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system. The main idea in cube learning systems is to consider satisfying assignments also as conflicts, albeit of a different kind, and to learn cubes from these conflicts. (The algorithm learns that such a trail is inconsistent with any Herbrand function.) Learnt cubes are added disjunctively to the matrix, which thus at intermediate stages is not necessarily in CNF but is the disjunction of a CNF formula and some cubes. With the augmented CNF matrices, cubes that become unit after existential reduction can now propagate universal variables in a way that falsifies the cube. Also, with the augmented CNF matrices, a trail may end up satisfying a cube rather than the CNF; this too is now a conflict, and conflict analysis involving term-resolution can be performed to learn a new cube.
For formal details, see Section 3 in
[
15
]
.

Three factors affect the construction of a refutation or verification, and are relevant for our generalized definition in the following section:
Three factors affect the construction of the refutation.

1.

The decision policy: how to choose the next variable to branch
on. In standard
\mathtt{QCDCL}
as defined in
[
7
]
, decisions must
respect the quantifier prefix level order. (Variables
x,y
are at
the same level if they are quantified the same way, and no variable
with a different quantification appears between them in the prefix
order.) This policy is called
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
. The most unrestricted policy is
{\rm Appl.~Opt.}
; any variable can be decided at any point.

Other policies such as
{\rm Appl.~Opt.}
,
\mathtt{ASS\textrm{-}R\textrm{-}ORD}
,
\mathtt{UNI\textrm{-}ANY}
, are also
possible; see
[
7
,
16
]
.

2.

The unit propagation policy. Upon a partial assignment
\alpha

to some variables, when does a clause
C
propagate a literal?
In standard
\mathtt{QCDCL}
the Reduction policy (used by most current QCDCL solvers
[
22
,
25
]
) is used: a clause
C

propagates literal
\ell
if after restricting
C
by
\alpha
and
applying all possible universal reductions, only
\ell
remains.
Also, propagations are made as soon as possible; see the description of natural trails (Def 3.4 in
[
7
]
).

3.

The set of learnable clauses/cubes. These clauses/cubes explain the conflict at the end of a trail, and are derived using (possibly long-distance) clause/term resolution with propagated literals as pivots.

3
Adding dependency schemes to the
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof system

The work done in
[
17
]
was the first to formalise the addition of dependency schemes to the
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof system. It was done only for the setting where trails follow level-ordered decisions, and there is no cube learning. Here we relax both these restrictions; we allow dependency schemes to affect the decision policy of the trail, and we allow cube-learning.

Proposition 12

For any normal dependency scheme

A minor adaptation of the proof of Theorem 3.9 in

Theorem 13
.

For any normal dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
, the proof systems
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\rm Appl.~Opt.}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\rm Appl.~Opt.}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}$})

Proof.

To show that both these systems are sound, it is enough to show the following three statements: (1) The derivation of any learnt clause is a valid
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})

\mathtt{Res}
derivation.
(2) If

-
\mathtt{TermRes}

.

Lemma 14
.

For any normal dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
, the derivations of a clause learnt from a trail in the proof systems
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\rm Appl.~Opt.}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\rm Appl.~Opt.}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}$})
are valid
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
derivations.

Proof.

This proof essentially replicates the proofs of Lemma 3.7 and Proposition 3.8 from
[
7
]
.
We need to show that every clause learnt from a trail :

\mathcal{T}=(p_{(0,1)},\cdots,p_{(0,g_{0})};\mathbf{d_{1}},p_{(1,1)},\cdots p_{(1,g_{1})};\mathbf{d_{2}},\cdots\cdots\cdots;\mathbf{d_{r}},p_{(r,1)},\cdots p_{(r,g_{r})})

is a valid
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
derivation. Let
C_{i,j}
denote the clause learnt corresponding to propagated literal
p_{i,j}
.

Step 1:
No
C_{i,j}
contains an existential tautology.

Suppose there exists a variable
x
such that
x\neq\mathtt{var}(p_{i,j})
and
x\in C_{i,j+1}
and
\bar{x}\in\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j}))
. Let
A=\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})
, then since
x
is existential variable, and
\bar{x}\in A
, therefore
x
must be assigned in the trail prior to the propagation of
p_{i,j}
.

On the other hand, we have
x\in C_{i,j+1}
, which is the learnable clause which is derived with the aid of antecedent clauses of literals occurring right of
p_{i,j}
in the trail. In particular, we can find some
p_{k,m}
right of
p_{i,j}
in the trail with
x\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{k,m})
. But because
x
appears in the trail to the left of
p_{i,j}
, this gives a contradiction since
\mathtt{ante}(p_{k,m})
must not become true before propagating
p_{k,m}
.

Step 2:
Derivation of clauses with universal tautolgies is sound.

Proof goes via contradiction. Suppose there exists a universal tautology derived which is unsound. Without loss of generality let that variable be
u
and the propagated literal
over which this resolution happens be
p_{i,}
. Since the resolution is unsound
(u,\mathtt{var}(p_{i,j}))\in\mathtt{D}
and on of the following conditions must hold:

1.

u\in C_{i,j+1}
and
\bar{u}\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})

2.

u\vee\bar{u}\in C_{i,j+1}
and
\bar{u}\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})

3.

u\in C_{i,j+1}
and
u\vee\bar{u}\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})

4.

u\vee\bar{u}\in C_{i,j+1}
and
u\vee\bar{u}\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})

Consider the first case: Since
u\in C_{i,j+1}
there has to be a propagated literal
p_{k,m}
right of
p_{i,j}
in the trail such that
u\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{k,m})
. In order to become unit, the
u
in
\mathtt{ante}(p_{k,m})
needs to vanish. We distinguish two cases:

Case (i):
\bar{u}
was assigned before
p_{k,m}
was propagated. Then
\bar{u}
does not appear in the trail, then for
p_{i,j}
to be propagated
\bar{u}
must have been reduced in
\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})
which is possible only if
(u,\mathtt{var}(p_{i,j}))\not\in\mathtt{D}
giving rise to a contradiction.

Case (ii):
u\in\mathtt{ante}(p_{k,m})
is removed via reduction. For propagations
p_{i,j}
,
p_{k,m}
to both happen
u
,
\bar{u}
could not be assigned in the trail prior to propagating
p_{i,j}
, therefore for
p_{i,j}
to be propagated
\bar{u}
must have been reduced in
\mathtt{ante}(p_{i,j})
which is possible only if
(u,\mathtt{var}(p_{i,j}))\not\in\mathtt{D}
giving rise to a contradiction.

The same argument above works for all the remaining cases.
∎

Thus using dependency schemes in decision order gives sound and complete systems.

\mathtt{D}

3.2
Strength of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
based proof systems with
{\mathtt{D}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}

In
\mathtt{QCDCL}
based proof systems, incorporating dependency schemes into propagation and learning processes does not always yield benefits: as shown in
[

\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}

If the dependency shceme is allowed to influence the decision order (i.e., the system adopts the
{\mathtt{D}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}

\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}

\mathtt{D}_{1}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}

\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
systems.

Theorem 16
.

For dependency schemes
\mathtt{D}_{1}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}\}
and
\mathtt{D}_{2}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}\}
, and
for policy
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}_{2}$}\}
,
the proof system
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\mathtt{D}_{1}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})

p
-simulates
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
and is not simulated by it.

Proof.

The
p
-simulation follows from
\cref
obs:lodoao.

For
\mathtt{D}_{1}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
, an advantage over
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
was noted already in
[
21
]
.

For
\mathtt{D}_{1}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, to show that there is no reverse simulation, we consider the
\mathtt{TwoPHPandCT}
formulae defined in
[
17
]
, and described in
\cref
ex:illustration.
. These have an unsatisfiable matrix, so by
\cref
obs:unsatnocubeEqcube, it suffices to show lower and upper bounds for
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}=\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$}
.

For these formulas,

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}=\emptyset
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}=\{(u,x_{i}),(u,y_{i}):\textrm{~for all~}i\}\cup\{(v,z_{1}),(v,z_{2})\}
.

It is shown in
[

]
(Lemma 5) that these formulas require exponential size refutations in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}=\{(u,x_{i}),(u,y_{i}):\textrm{~for all~}i\}\cup\{(v,z_{1}),(v,z_{2})\}
.
With the
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
decision policy, the first decision must be on
u
, which causes no propagations, and subsequent decisions in

force refuting

(in either

or

), which is known to requires exponential size.

On the other hand, we have already seen in

ex:illustration that they have short (constant-sized) refutations in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{{\mathtt{D}_{1}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
if

For

We now restrict our attention to proof systems utilizing only the
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
decision policy. Even with
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
, dependency schemes can affect (enhance or impair) the performance of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
systems. These are the systems also considered in
[
7
,
15
,

]
.

4.1
Preprocessing as a tool

In
[
17
]
, another way of incorporating dependency schemes was also considered, through “preprocessing”. For a more complete comparison, we very briefly define such systems here as well.
Preprocessing a formula using a dependency scheme simply means applying all reductions enabled by it on the input formula, and then proceeding with whatever version of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
is of interest, on the reduced formula.

Definition 18
.

For a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}\cdot\phi
and a normal dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
,
a derivation of a clause
C
from
\Phi
in

\mathtt{D}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{ORD}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{ClausePol}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}

is a derivation of
C
from the QBF
\Psi=\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(\Phi)
in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{ORD}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{ClausePol}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
.

As shown in Theorem 4 of
[
17
]
, preprocessing using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
can significantly alter the system strength. In contrast, we observe below that preprocessing using schemes
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
has no effect, no matter what version of
\mathtt{QCDCL}
is the subsequent system.

Proposition 19
.

For every decision policy
\mathtt{ORD}
, dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}\}
and for
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}$}\}
, the proof systems

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{ORD}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
,

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{ORD}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
, and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{ORD}}(\mathtt{D},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}

are equivalent to each other.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be any given PCNF formula, and let
\Psi=\mathtt{red}(\Phi)
.
By definition,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)\subseteq\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
.
So all reductions permitted by
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
are also permitted by
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
. If a clause
C
of
\Phi
has variables
x,y
with
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
, then by definition of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
,
(x,y)
is also in
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)
. So
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
does not enable any new reductions on initial clauses.
Hence
\Psi=\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)=\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
, so the second and third proof systems are equivalent.

For any clause
C
,
\mathtt{red}(C)
can only remove universal
variables from
C
. By the way
\Psi
is defined, if
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}(\Psi)
, then it is also in
\mathtt{D}(\Phi)
. In the other direction, if
(x,y)\in\mathtt{D}(\Phi)
, and if both
x,y
appear in the matrix of
\Psi
, then
(x,y)
is also in
\mathtt{D}(\Psi)
because the prefix of
\Phi
and
\Psi
is the same, and the witnessing sequence (in the case of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
) has only existential literals which are not removed, so the same sequence is a witness in
\Psi
too.

To show that the first and second proof systems are the same, note that for
\mathtt{D}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}\}
, for any clause
C
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(C)=\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}(\mathtt{red}(C))
. Therefore if one of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
are used in propagation and learning, then all the propagations in the first trail of either refutation are also enabled in the other. (To be pedantic, a derivation in
\Phi
may have universal variables that have vanished from the matrix of
\Psi
but are still in the quantifier prefix of
\Psi
; these can have no effect on any propagation since they vanished through applications of
\mathtt{red}
.) Hence the first clause/cube learnt in any one system can also be learnt in the other. Continuing this argument on subsequent trails, the entire derivation can be replicated.
∎

4.2
Some New Formulae

We now introduce some new formulas which will be used to pinpoint the relative strengths of the proof systems.

The
\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$}_{n}
formulas.

The
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas, first defined in
[
6
]
,
show that the proof system
\mathtt{QCDCL}
with cube learning is stronger than
\mathtt{QCDCL}
without

[
15
]
. We wish to show that cube-learning offers a similar advantage for systems with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
.
The
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas cannot show this because
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{Equality}$})=\emptyset
; so using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in any way makes them easy to refute irrespective of cube-learning. To achieve the desired separation, we modify the
\mathtt{Equality}
formula by adding two clauses that make
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
identical. These new formulas, called
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
, maintain the separation without altering the hardness of
\mathtt{Equality}
.

Formula 1
.

The
\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$}_{n}
formula has the prefix

\exists x_{1}\cdots x_{n}~\forall u_{1}\cdots u_{n}~\exists t_{1}\cdots t_{n}
and the PCNF matrix

\begin{array}[]{lc}&\underbrace{(\bar{t}_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\bar{t}_{n})}_{T_{n}}~\wedge~\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\left[\underbrace{(x_{i}\vee u_{i}\vee t_{i})}_{A_{i}}\wedge\underbrace{(\bar{x}_{i}\vee\bar{u}_{i}\vee t_{i})}_{B_{i}}\right]~\wedge\\
&\underbrace{(\bar{u}_{1}\vee\cdots\bar{u}_{n}\vee\bar{t}_{1}\vee\cdots\bar{t}_{n})}_{UT_{n}}~\wedge~\underbrace{(\bar{u}_{1}\vee\cdots\bar{u}_{n}\vee t_{1}\vee\cdots t_{n})}_{UT^{\prime}_{n}}\end{array}

(Note: deleting the clauses
UT_{n}
and
UT^{\prime}_{n}
gives the
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas.)

Proposition 20
.

For
\Phi=\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$}
,
\mathtt{red}(\Phi)=\Phi
, and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$})=\{(u_{i},t_{j}):i,j\in\{1\cdots n\}\}
; that is, each
t_{j}
variable depends on each
u_{i}
variable. Since each occurrence of a
u
variable in the formula is blocked by some
t
variable, we have
\mathtt{red}(\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$})=\mbox{$\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}$}
.

The next claim is that for this family of formulae
\Phi
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\Phi)=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)
.
It suffices to show that
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}(\Phi)\subseteq\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\Phi)
.

We want to show that each
t_{j}
depending on each
u_{i}
is the case for
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
as well. We consider two cases.

Case 1:
i=j
. The clauses
A_{i}
and
UT_{n}
contain the resolution paths
(u_{i},t_{i})
and
(\bar{u}_{i},\bar{t}_{i})
respectively. Therefore
(u_{i},t_{i})\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
for all
i\in\{1\cdots n\}
.

Case 2:
i\neq j
. The sequence of clauses
A_{i},UT_{n}
contains the resolution path
(u_{i},t_{i}),(\bar{t}_{i},\bar{t}_{j})
, while the clause
UT^{\prime}_{n}
conatins the resolution path
(\bar{u}_{i},t_{j})
. Therefore
(u_{i},t_{j})\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
for all
i\neq j\in\{1\cdots n\}
. ∎

This makes the usage of the dependency schemes
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
completely useless. We now show that the formulas are easy to refute with cube-learning, but hard if cube-learning is switched off.
Both these results closely mirror the corresponding results for
\mathtt{Equality}
shown in
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
respectively.

Lemma 21
.

For
\mathtt{D}_{1},\mathtt{D}_{2}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}\}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}_{2}$}\}
, the
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
formulas have polynomial size refutations in
\mathtt{D}_{1}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}

To prove this, we first show that these formulas are easy to refute with cube-learning.

Proposition 22
.

The
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
formulas have polynomial size refutations in the proof system
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
.

Proof.

The polynomial size refutation for these formulas in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
is exactly the same as the refutation in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
for the
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas, as described in
[
15
]
. By constructing trails in exactly the same manner, we first learn
2n-2
cubes of the form
(x_{i}\wedge\bar{u}_{i})
and
(\bar{x}_{i}\wedge u_{i})
for
i=1...n-1
and then start clause learning by constructing trails ending in a conflict. The two new clauses
UT_{n}
and
UT^{\prime}_{n}
play no role whatsoever. For completeness, we reproduce the entire refutation below; a reader familiar with the construction from
[
15
]
can completely skip these details.

The proof goes in two stages. The first stage involves learning the cubes
x_{i}\wedge\bar{u}_{i}
and
\bar{x}_{i}\wedge u_{i}
for
i\in[n-1]
. The first trail is the following.

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathbf{x_{1};\cdots;x_{n};\bar{u}_{1};\cdots;\bar{u}_{n};\bar{t}_{1};t_{2};\cdots;t_{n}}

It assigns all variables without conflict and satisfies the matrix. The partial assignment
x_{1}\wedge\bar{u}_{1}\wedge\bar{t}_{1}\wedge t_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge t_{n}
contained in it is also a satisfying assignment, and reducing it with
\mathtt{red}_{\exists}
we can learn the cube
x_{1}\wedge\bar{u}_{1}
from this trail.

Analogously, creating a complementary trail
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}
where each decision is the complement of the decision in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, we can learn the cube
\bar{x}_{1}\wedge u_{1}
.

Suppose we have learn
2i
cubes in the same manner;
x_{j}\wedge\bar{u}_{j}
and
\bar{x}_{j}\wedge u_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,i
. For
i+1
, create the following trail.

\mathcal{T}_{i+1}=\mathbf{x_{1}},u_{1},t_{1};\cdots;\mathbf{x_{i}},u_{i},t_{j};\mathbf{x_{i+1};\cdots;x_{n};\bar{u}_{i+1};\cdots;\bar{u}_{n};\bar{t}_{i+1};t_{i+2};\cdots;t_{n}}

In this trail, for
j\leq i
,

\mathtt{ante}(u_{j})=x_{j}\wedge\bar{u}_{j}
and

\mathtt{ante}(t_{j})=\bar{x}_{j}\vee\bar{u}_{j}\vee t_{j}
.
As earlier, the trail satisfies all clauses without conflict. Extracting the partial assignment
x_{i+1}\wedge\bar{u}_{i+1}\wedge t_{1}\wedge\cdots t_{i}\wedge\bar{t}_{i+1}\wedge t_{i+2}\wedge\cdots\wedge t_{n}
which also satisfies the matrix, and reducing it, we can learn the cube
x_{i+1}\wedge u_{i+1}
. Analogously through a trail
\mathcal{T}_{i+1}^{\prime}

In
[
13
]
, the authors consider

-property, the size of a refutation in

The
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
formulas are easily seen to be

-formulas with the

-property. We show now that they have linear gauge, implying that they are exponentially hard.

Recall the definition of
XUT
formulas and the gauge measure:

Definition 24

(
XT
-property,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a PCNF QBF of the form
\exists X\forall U\exists T\cdot\phi
, where

are non-empty sets of variables. Then
\Phi
is an
XUT
-formula. We call a clause

an

•

X
-clause: if it is non-empty and contains only
X
variables,

•

T
-clause: if it is non-empty and contains only
T
variables,

•

XT
-clause: if it contains no
U
variable and at least one
X
and one
T
variable,

•

XUT
-clause: if it contains atleast one each of
X
,
U
, and
T
variables.

\Phi
is said to fulfill the

-property if
\phi
contains no
XT

T

T

Definition 25

(gauge,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be an
XUT
formula. The gauge of

is the size of the narrowest
X
-clause derivable using only reductions and resolutions over variables in

.

First observe that none of the axioms of
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
are

-clauses. Therefore to derive an

-clause, there has to be some

-resolutions. A first

-resolution must involve either

or

, since only these clauses have

n

T
-variables. Thus to eventually derive an

.

Formula 2

.

The variable
"w"

Proposition 27
.

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{PreRRSTrapdoor}$})=\{(u,b),(v,b),(p,q)\}

Proof.

We look at all universal variables individually.

First consider

q
, and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}

Next consider

Third consider
v
. Consider any path starting with
\bar{v}
and ending in
v
, and linked by existential variables (in opposing polarities) right of

\bar{s}

\bar{b}

v

((\bar{v},b),(\bar{b},v))
,

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}

Finally consider

r,s

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{PreRRSTrapdoor}$})=\{(u,b),(v,b),(p,q)\}

, propagating a
q
literal:

\mathcal{T}=\mathbf{a/\bar{a}},\bar{b};\mathbf{p/\bar{p}},\bar{q}/q
.

At this point in the trail, the formula matrix has reduced to

\begin{array}[]{lc}&\mbox{$\mathtt{PHP}$}_{n}^{n+1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{s_{n}})\\
\textrm{for~~}i\in[s_{n}]:&(\bar{y}_{i}\vee x_{i}\vee u)\ ,\ (y_{i}\vee\bar{x}_{i}\vee u)\\
\textrm{for~~}i\in[s_{n}]:&(y_{i}\vee v\vee t)\ ,\ (y_{i}\vee v\vee\bar{t})\ ,\ (\bar{y}_{i}\vee v\vee t)\ ,\ (\bar{y}_{i}\vee v\vee\bar{t})\\
&(\bar{v}\vee s)\\
\end{array}

This is effectively the matrix of the
\mathtt{Trapdoor}
formulas from
[
7
]
, with one extra clause
\bar{v}\vee s
. After this point, all decisions are made on
y
variables propagating a corresponding
x
variable. By the time all
y
variables are decided, a conflict in
\mathtt{PHP}
on the
x
variables is achieved. Thus, exactly like the
\mathtt{Trapdoor}
formulas, refuting
\mathtt{PreRRSTrapdoor}
boils down to refuting
\mathtt{PHP}
, which is known to require exponential size.
∎

The
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas.

The third new formula
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
we introduce shows the advantage of using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
in propagation and learning. The goal is to create clauses that can be learnt easily with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
, but are hard to learn without it. These learned clauses enable a quick refutation in
\mathtt{QCDCL}
with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
, but without them, one is stuck refuting something hard.

Formula 3
.

The
\mbox{$\mathtt{StdDepTrap}$}_{n}
formula has the prefix

\exists b~\forall w_{1}~\exists z_{1},\cdots,z_{s_{n}}~\forall w_{2}~\exists a~\exists d~\exists c~\forall u~\exists x~\exists y~\exists p~\exists e_{1}~\exists e_{2}
, and the matrix is as given below.

\begin{array}[]{lcc}&\bar{b}\vee\mbox{$\mathtt{PHP}$}_{n}^{n+1}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{s_{n}})&\\
&(y\vee p)\ ,\ (y\vee\bar{p}),&(w_{1}\vee e_{1})\ ,\ (w_{2}\vee e_{2})\\
&(b\vee y)\ ,\ (a\vee\bar{y})\ ,\ (\bar{a}\vee x)\ ,\ (\bar{c}\vee u\vee\bar{x}),&(d\vee c\vee\bar{y})\ ,\ (\bar{d}\vee c\vee\bar{y})\end{array}

The variables
w_{1},w_{2},u
are essentially “separators”, putting existential variables into different levels. The variables
e_{1},e_{2},x
are blockers, ensuring that the separators do not get reduced too early in the trail.

Proposition 30
.

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{StdDepTrap}$})=\{(w_{1},e_{1}),(w_{2},e_{2}),(u,x)\}
.

Proof.

We want to show that
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{StdDepTrap}$})=\{(w_{1},e_{1}),(w_{2},e_{2}),(u,x)\}
. For each universal variable (there are three,
w_{1},w_{2},u
), we consider its dependencies.

The variable
w_{1}
appears in exactly one clause, and that clause has
just one other variable
e_{1}
, This variable
e_{1}
is quantified to
the right ot
w_{1}
and appears in no other clause. So
e_{1}
, and no
other variable, depends on
w_{1}
in
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
. Similarly,
e_{2}
, and no
other variable, depends on
w_{2}
.

The variable
u
appears only in the clause
(\bar{c}\vee u\vee\bar{x})
. Since
c
is left of
u
in the quantifier prefix, it does not depend on
u
or provide a path for other variables to depend either. Since
x
is quantified after
u
, it does depend on
u
. However,
x
appears only in this clause and no other clause, so it cannot provide further paths either. Therefore it is the only variable depending on
u
.

Thus,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{StdDepTrap}$})=\{(w_{1},e_{1}),(w_{2},e_{2}),(u,x)\}
.
∎

When
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
is used in propagation, we show that these formulas have short refutations.

Lemma 31
.

For
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}$}\}
the
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas have polynomial size refutations in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})

Proof.

By
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube, it suffices to show polynomial size refutations in the system
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
.

For this purpose, consider the trail

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathbf{\bar{b}},y,a,x,\bar{c},d,\square
.

Since
(u,y)\not\in\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}(\mbox{$\mathtt{StdDepTrap}$})
, the learnable sequence for this trail is

L_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=\{c\vee\bar{y},u\vee\bar{x}\vee\bar{y},\bar{a}\vee\bar{y},\bar{y},b\}.

We choose to learn
\bar{y}
, and then proceed to the next trail

\mathcal{T}_{2}=\bar{y},p,\square
.
The learnable clauses for this trail are

L_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}=\{y\vee\bar{p},y,\square\}.

Thus the empty clause
\square
is learnt, completing the refutation.
∎

However, without
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
, the propagations force refuting the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses, which is hard.

Lemma 32
.

For
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}$}\}
the
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas require exponential size refutations in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})

Proof.

We first show that the the
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas require exponential size without cube learning i.e. in
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
and then extend the argument for the case of
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}$})

Every
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
trail must start with a decision on the variable
b
, unless and until a literal on
b
is learnt; thenceforth a trail must begin by propagating that literal.

Suppose that the
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
trail starts with a decision
b
. In such a case there are no propagations possible. Next a decision must be made on
w_{1}
, which may or may not propagate
e_{1}
. At this point, no other propagations are possible, and decisions must be made on all the
z
variables. Since the
z
variables are only involved in the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses, this leads to a conflict in the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses.

Suppose that the
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
trail starts with the decision
\bar{b}
. Then there is a unique forced trail leading to a conflict, namely,

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathbf{\bar{b}},y,a,x,\bar{c},d,\square.

The learnable sequence for this trail under regular reduction is

L_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=\{c\vee\bar{y},u\vee\bar{x}\vee\bar{y},\bar{a}\vee u\vee\bar{y},u\vee\bar{y},b\}.

It is easy to see that learning any clause other than
b
does not affect
the decisions in the trail at all. Thus, with trails of this type, in a few stages the clause
b
will be learnt inevitably.

To summarise, any
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
trail must start with a decision on
b
. If the decision is
b
, it leads to a conflict in the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses. If the trails avoid decision
b
and start with
\bar{b}
, we will eventually be forced to learn the unit clause
b
, leading to trails starting with propagating
b
, and again reaching a conflict in the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses. Therefore, any
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
refutation of
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
reduces to refuting
\mathtt{PHP}
, thus requiring exponential size.

In the case of
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}$})
, since the
\mathtt{PHP}
clauses are unsatisfiable,
\bar{b}
must be in any satisfying assignment of the matrix of
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
.
Therefore for cube learning to ever play a role, the
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
or
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}$})
trail must start with deciding
b
as
\bar{b}
. However, as discussed above, trails starting with
\bar{b}
are forced and rapidly hit a conflict; they cannot lead on to a satisfying assignment. Thus, even though the matrix of
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
is satisfiable (e.g. by the literals
\bar{b},y,a,x,c,u,w_{1},w_{2}
), such assignments can never be discovered through
\mathtt{QCDCL}
trails. Hence, the
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
hardness lifts as cube learning is useless, and the
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas continue to be hard for
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}$})
.
∎

As discussed cube-learning in
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof systems is the concept of allowing ”terms” to be learnt from satisfying trails, and intuitively more (optional)
learning power would mean a more powerful proof system. The first theorem validates this claim . It states that for any
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof system with dependency schemes,
adding cube-learning yields a more powerful system than the corresponding system without it.

Theorem 33
.

For
\mathtt{D}_{1},\mathtt{D}_{2}\in\{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}\}
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}_{2}$}\}
the proof system
\mathtt{D}_{1}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
is strictly stronger than
\mathtt{D}_{1}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}

Proof.

From
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube the systems with cube-learning are at least as strong as the corresponding systems without cube learning. The
\mathtt{DoubleLongEq}
formulas show that they are in fact strictly stronger; see the bounds in
\cref
lem:DoubleLongEq-easyQCDCLcube,lem:DoubleLongEq-hardQCDCL.
∎

For the next three theorems we focus specifically on
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
.
The first of these shows that irrespective of the manner in which
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}

is used in a
\mathtt{QCDCL}
proof system, the resulting system is provably incomparable in strength to the
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system that does not use dependency schemes. This was already known for the setting without cube-learning, Theorem 5 in
[
17
]
. We show here that cube learning makes no difference; the systems still remain incomparable. This highlights the fact that adding dependency schemes is not always beneficial.

Theorem 34
.

Any proof systems
\mathtt{P}_{1},\mathtt{P}_{2}
are incomparable, where

\mathtt{P}_{1}\in\left\{\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}\mid\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}$}\right\}\}\textrm{~and}

\mathtt{P}_{2}\in\left\{\mathtt{D}_{1}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}\mid\parbox{216.81pt}{$(\mathtt{D}_{1},\mathtt{D}_{2})\in\{(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}),(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}),(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}})\},\\
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}_{2}$}\}$}\right\}.

Proof.

There are eighteen incomparability claims expressed so thirty-six separations are required! Fortunately, just two formulas establish all the desired separations.

The bounds from
[
17
]
along with
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube and
\cref
obs:unsatnocubeEqcube imply that the
\mathtt{Dep\textrm{-}Trap}
formulas require exponential size refutations in every system in
\mathtt{P}_{2}
, but have polynomial size refutations in every system in
\mathtt{P}_{1}
.
The bounds from
[
7
]
and
[
17
]
along with
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube imply that the
\mathtt{TwinEq}
formulas require exponential size refutations in every system in
\mathtt{P}_{1}
, but have polynomial size refutations in every system in
\mathtt{P}_{2}
.
∎

The next theorem shows that adding
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in different ways to
\mathtt{QCDCL}
yields systems incomparable in strength. (Again, this was already known for the setting without cube-learning, Theorem 4 in
[
17
]
.) This highlights that adding dependency schemes in any one of preprocessing or propagation and learning is not inherently better than the other.

Theorem 35
.

For a fixed
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}$}\}
, the three systems

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
, and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
are pairwise incomparable.

Proof.

Refer to
\cref
tab:for-sideways and
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube and
\cref
obs:unsatnocubeEqcube.

It can be seen that
\mathtt{TwoPHPandCT}
formulas are easy to refute when
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is used in preprocessing i.e. in
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
, but hard otherwise i.e.
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
. On the other hand, the
\mathtt{PreDepTrap}
formulas are hard to refute if preprocessed by
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, but easy otherwise. Together, they witness that
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
is incomparable with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
.

Further, the
\mathtt{PropDep\textrm{-}Trap}
formulas are easy to refute if the propagations and clause learning do not use
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, but become hard if
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is used. This is independent of whether preprocessing is used and whether cube-learning is switched on. On the other hand, the (new)
\mathtt{PreRRSTrapdoor}
formulas show that using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in propagations can be advantageous. Together, these two formulas witness
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
are incomparable.
∎

Earlier, we have seen that adding cube-learning to a
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system with dependency always yields a stronger system,
\cref
thm:QCDCLcubeD-stronger. The following theorem shows that even adding cube-learning to a
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is incomparable to a
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system without cube-learning but using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
in a different way.

Theorem 36
.

For any
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}$}\}
, adding
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
to the proof system
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
in one way and to
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
in any different way yields incomparable proof systems. In particular,

1.

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
is incomparable with
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
.

2.

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
is incomparable with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
.

3.

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
incomparable with
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
.

Proof.

Refer to
\cref
tab:for-sideways and
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube and
\cref
obs:unsatnocubeEqcube.

The
\mathtt{TwoPHPandCT}
formulas are easy to refute if and only if preprocessed by
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, irrespective of whether or not cube-learning is used and whether or not
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is used in propagation and learning.
The situation is exactly reversed for the
\mathtt{PreDepTrap}
formulas, which are easy to refute if and only if not preprocessed by
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
. Together, these show eight of the twelve claimed incomparability relations, namely

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
,

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
,

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
,

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
.

The
\mathtt{PropDep\textrm{-}Trap}
formulas are easy to refute if and only if
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is not used for propagation and learning, irrespective of its use in preprocessing, and irrespective of whether or not cube-learning is used. On the other hand, the
\mathtt{PreRRSTrapdoor}
formulas are easy to refute if
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is used for preprocessing and in propagation and learning, but not if it is used only for preprocessing. Together, these show the remaining four claimed incomparability relations, namely

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
and

\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})$}
.

∎

Now we come to
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
. First, we show that, as for
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
, a
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system with
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
is incomparable in strength to a standard
\mathtt{QCDCL}
system without any dependency schemes.

Theorem 37
.

For
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}$}\}
, the proof systems
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
and
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
are incomparable.

Proof.

For
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}$}\}
, the
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas have polynomial size
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
refutations (the refutations in
[
15
]
are of this type), but require exponential size
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
refutations (the lower bound from
[
7
]
carries over, because
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
). On the other hand, the newly defined
\mathtt{StdDepTrap}
formulas have polynomial size
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$})
refutations but require exponential size
\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
refutations (
\cref
lem:StdDepTrap-easyQCDCLDstd,lem:StdDepTrap-hardQCDCL).
∎

Finally, we compare the different dependency schemes
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
.
The mere fact that
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is a refinement of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
(more general, eliminates more dependencies) does not make it better; for that matter,
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
is a refinement of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
, but using it can be a disadvantage for some formulas. Similarly, we prove below that neither of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
has a proof-theoretic advantage over the other, irrespective of the presence or absence of cube-learning.

Theorem 38
.

For any
(\mathtt{D}_{1},\mathtt{D}_{2})\in\{(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}),(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}),(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}},\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}})\}
,

and
\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$}\in\{\mbox{$\mathtt{No}$-$\mathtt{Cube}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{LD}$},\mbox{$\mathtt{Cube}$-$\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}$}\}
, the proof systems

\mathtt{P}_{1}\in

\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})
and
\mathtt{P}_{2}\in

\mathtt{D}_{1}+\mbox{$\mbox{$\mathtt{QCDCL}$}^{\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}}(\mathtt{D}_{2},\mbox{$\mathtt{CubePol}$})$}
are incomparable.

Proof.

Using refutations and lower bound arguments from
[
7
,
17
]
along with
\cref
obs:nocubeImplycube and
\cref
obs:unsatnocubeEqcube, the
\mathtt{Trapdoor}
and
\mathtt{Dep\textrm{-}Trap}
formulas bear witness; the former are easy in
\mathtt{P}_{2}
but hard in
\mathtt{P}_{1}
, whereas the situation is reversed for the latter.
∎

5
Conclusions

In the context of QBF proof systems, dependency schemes are
expected to aid the process of refutation. Indeed, in the
proof systems
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
and
\mathtt{QU}
-
\mathtt{Res}
, two of the earliest
resolution-based QBF proof systems to be studied
[
20
,
18
]
, it
is known that using
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
can shorten proofs exponentially
– the
\mathtt{Equality}
formulas require exponential refutation size
in these but have polynomial-sized proofs in
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}})
-
\mathtt{Res}
. It was
thus a surprise to see this advantage does not automatically
translate to QCDCL algorithms; we see that when restricted to
\mathtt{LEV\textrm{-}ORD}
decisions,
even in the presence of cube learning, usage of dependency schemes for propagation
and learning is not always advantageous. The hardness in these systems is primarily a consequence
of the level-ordered nature of decisions – it can be shown that all formulas with lower bounds
shown in
\cref
sec:QCDCLcuberesults are easy when the decision policy for QCDCL is
{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
or
{\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
.
In fact, proper lower bound techniques for a decision policy
{\mathtt{D}}\textrm{-}\mathtt{ORD}
are unknown, and to us, this is the big open
question arising from this work.

Even for the level-ordered policy, there are many unresolved questions.
The comparison between
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
already shows that using a more refined scheme is not necessarily an advantage. The relative strengths of
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
and
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
(which refines
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
) is not yet clear. How other dependency schemes would relate in this scenario is completely unexplored.

Since QCDCL-style reasoning is explained through long-distance clause/term resolution, it is worth highlighting three long-standing open questions about those systems. Firstly, does using dependency schemes confer any advantage with clausal long-distance; i.e. is the simulation of
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
by
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
strict for
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
or
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{rrs}}
? Secondly, is the use of dependency schemes in long-distance term resolution (the system
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
) sound? Thirdly, since
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
is the scheme actually used in DepQBF, separations specific to
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
would be quite interesting. Can we even show that
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}})
-
\mathtt{Res}
is strictly more powerful than
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
? This question has remained open since
\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{std}}
was first implemented in DepQBF over 15 years ago.

Appendix B
Rules in various QBF Proof systems

For an initial PCNF and a fixed dependency scheme
\mathtt{D}
, rules for various proofs systems are defined as follows.

For clauses:

•

\mathtt{Axiom}
(clause axiom rule)

A

where
A
is any clause in the matrix.

•

\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}
(clause reduction with
\mathtt{D}
):

A\vee\ell

A

where
\mathtt{var}(\ell)\in X_{\forall}
, and for each
x\in X_{\exists}\cap\mathtt{var}(A)
,
(\mathtt{var}(\ell),x)\not\in\mathtt{D}
.

•

\mathtt{Res}
(Resolution):

A\vee\ell

B\vee\neg\ell

A\vee B

where
\mathtt{var}(\ell)\in X_{\exists}
, and
A\vee B
is not tautological.

•

\mathtt{LD}\mbox{$\mathtt{Res}$}(\mathtt{D})
(Long-distance Resolution with
\mathtt{D}
):

A\vee\ell

B\vee\neg\ell

A\vee B

where
\mathtt{var}(\ell)\in X_{\exists}
; for each
x\in X_{\exists}
, either
x
or
\neg x
is not in
A\cup B
; for each

u\in X_{\forall}
, if
u\in A
and
\neg u\in B
, then
(u,\mathtt{var}(\ell))\not\in\mathtt{D}
.

(Note that tautological clauses can be generated. In much of the literature, the symbol
u^{*}
is used to denote that both
u
and
\neg u
are in a clause.)

The proof system
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
uses the rules
\mathtt{Axiom}
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}
, and
\mathtt{Res}
; the proof system
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
uses the rules
\mathtt{Axiom}
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}
, and
\mathtt{LD}\mbox{$\mathtt{Res}$}(\mathtt{D})
. The proof systems
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{Res}
and
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{Res}
are the special cases of
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
and
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{Res}
where
\mathtt{D}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
. A refutation of a false QBF is a derivation of the empty clause using the permitted rules.

For cubes/terms, the situation is essentially dual, exchanging the roles of
X_{\exists}
and
X_{\forall}
, to give the rules
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}_{\exists}
(term reduction with
\mathtt{D}
),
\mathtt{TermRes}
(Term Resolution), and
\mathtt{LDT}\mbox{$\mathtt{Res}$}(\mathtt{D})
(Long-distance Term Resolution with
\mathtt{D}
). Also, the
Axiom rule is modified to the term axiom rule:

A

where
A
is a non-contradictory cube whose literals satisfy the matrix.

The proof system
\mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
uses the rules
\mathtt{Axiom}
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}_{\exists}
, and
\mathtt{TermRes}
; the proof system
\mathtt{LDQ}(\mathtt{D})
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
uses the rules
\mathtt{Axiom}
,
\mathtt{red}\mbox{-}\mathtt{D}_{\exists}
, and
\mathtt{LDT}\mbox{$\mathtt{Res}$}(\mathtt{D})
. The proof systems
\mathtt{Q}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
and
\mathtt{LDQ}
-
\mathtt{TermRes}
are their respective special cases where
\mathtt{D}=\mathtt{D}^{\mathtt{trv}}
. A verification of a true QBF is a derivation of the empty term using the permitted rules.

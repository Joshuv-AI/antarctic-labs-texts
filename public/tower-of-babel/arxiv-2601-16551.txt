Analysis of M ​ 1 M1 capture in the α ​ ( d , γ ) 6 \alpha(d,\gamma)^{6} Li reaction
Ergash M. Tursunov, Daniel Baye

Abstract
An effective operator is exactly equivalent to the long-wavelength form of the M ​ 1 M1 operator in transition matrix elements. It allows us to analytically and numerically analyze the M ​ 1 M1 contribution to the α ​ ( d , γ ) 6 \alpha(d,\gamma)^{6} Li reaction. Isoscalar M ​ 1 M1 transitions from an initial S S wave are shown to be forbidden in radiative capture reactions when distortion is neglected in the initial state. A calculation in a three-body model with proton, neutron, and a structureless α \alpha interacting through effective forces leads to a negligible M ​ 1 M1 S S -factor at small energies. The dominant M ​ 1 M1 contribution comes from transitions from an initial S S wave to isospin 1 components of the 6 Li ground state. It is suggested that using this effective M ​ 1 M1 operator in other models should clarify the origin of large discrepancies between M ​ 1 M1 S S -factors appearing in the literature.

Introduction

The interest for the

A discrepancy exists between the observed and calculated
6

Most probably, this problem is not due to inaccuracies in the knowledge of the

Li reaction rate
but it has motivated a number of experiments and theoretical papers.
The LUNA measurements

;
TAA17

i.e.
, within the Gamow peak of astrophysical interest,
have provided strict constraints for testing the validity of theoretical radiative capture calculations
and restricted the probability of a nuclear physics solution of the lithium problem.
The origin of the discrepancy should rather be found in an astrophysical context

;
LMA13
;
FO22

The study of
\alpha(d,\gamma)^{6}
Li radiative capture remains nevertheless very interesting by itself
because it requires solving a number of delicate problems crucial for reactions involving
N=Z

The conclusions from its analysis will be essential for future accurate microscopic studies
of the important

\alpha,\gamma)^{16}

In reactions involving

Indeed, due to an approximate selection rule, the usually dominant

between isospin-zero states by an isospin selection rule.
At the long-wavelength approximation (LWA), the isoscalar part of the

and transitions only take place via its isovector part.
Matrix elements of isovector operators vanish between

However, except for the deuteron, realistic wave functions of

Moreover, isoscalar

As a result, the

to the strengths of the usually much weaker

M1

Each multipolarity appearing in the
\alpha(d,\gamma)^{6}
Li capture leads to theoretical difficulties.
For
E1

and its isovector part requiring isospin mixing add coherently.
The isovector partial matrix element requires taking account of small
T=1
components in the final
6
Li and initial scattering wave functions.
The
E2

6
Li ground state.
A correct treatment of

the perfect orthogonality between the initial and final wave functions

The relative importance of these electromagnetic components is still an open question
as existing models

;

;

;

;

In Ref.

Only

II.1
Microscopic
M1

At the LWA, the magnetic dipole operator for pointlike nucleons is given in translation-invariant form by
(see Eq. (50) of Ref.
Ba23
where
\hbar=1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{M1}=\frac{\mu_{\rm N}}{\hbar c}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\sum_{j=1}^{A}\left(g_{lj}L^{\prime}_{j\mu}+g_{sj}S_{j\mu}\right),

(1)

where
\mu_{\rm N}=e\hbar/2m_{p}
is the nuclear magneton involving the proton mass
m_{p}
.
The orbital angular momentum of nucleon
j
with respect to the center of mass of the
A
nucleons and its spin
are denoted as
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$L$}$}^{\prime}_{j}
and
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$S$}$}_{j}
, respectively.
In terms of the third component
t_{j3}
of the isospin
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$t$}$}_{j}
of nucleon
j
, the coefficients read

g_{lj}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}-t_{j3}
and
g_{sj}=g_{p}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}-t_{j3})+g_{n}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}+t_{j3})

as a function of the proton
g_{p}
and neutron
g_{n}
gyromagnetic factors.
The
M1
operator can be rewritten as a sum of isoscalar and isovector terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{M1}=\mu_{\rm N}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+(g_{p}+g_{n})S_{\mu}\right]-\sum_{j=1}^{A}t_{j3}\left[L^{\prime}_{j\mu}+(g_{p}-g_{n})S_{j\mu}\right]\right\},

(2)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{L}$}=\sum_{j=1}^{A}\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$L$}$}^{\prime}_{j}
is the total orbital momentum operator of the system
and
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$S$}$}=\sum_{j=1}^{A}\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$S$}$}_{j}
is its total spin.
The total angular momentum is
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$J$}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{L}$}+\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$S$}$}
.
The total isospin operator is
\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$T$}$}=\sum_{j=1}^{A}\mbox{$\mbox{\boldmath$t$}$}_{j}
.

Operator (
1
) is usually employed in radiative capture calculations
NWS01
;
So22
;
HHK22
.
Here we replace it by an operator which is exactly equivalent in those calculations
but allows a deeper analysis of the structure of
M1
transitions.
To this end, we take advantage of the facts that initial and final states of a transition are eigenstates of the total angular momentum of the system
and are therefore orthogonal in radiative capture reactions because of different angular momenta or parities, or otherwise of different energies.
Indeed, the matrix elements

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{f}^{JM\pi}|J_{\mu}|\Psi_{i}^{J^{\prime}M^{\prime}\pi^{\prime}}\rangle=0

(3)

of components
J_{\mu}
of the total angular momentum between the initial and final wave functions
\Psi_{i}^{J^{\prime}M^{\prime}\pi^{\prime}}
and
\Psi_{f}^{JM\pi}

always vanish because of this orthogonality.
Hence, the operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}^{M1}=\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{M1}-\mu_{\rm N}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\frac{1}{2}J_{\mu}

(4)

has the same radiative capture matrix elements as operator (
1
).
This operator contains isoscalar and isovector parts.
It has the explicit form (51) of Ref.
Ba23
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}^{M1}=\mu_{\rm N}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g_{p}+g_{n}-1)S_{\mu}-\sum_{j=1}^{A}t_{j3}\left[L^{\prime}_{j\mu}+(g_{p}-g_{n})S_{j\mu}\right]\right\}

(5)

with
g_{p}+g_{n}-1\approx 0.760
and
g_{p}-g_{n}\approx 9.412
.
Only the total spin operator of the nucleons appears in the isoscalar first term.
The isovector part is unchanged with respect to Eq. (
2
).

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{1M+}=\Psi_{i0}^{1M+}+\Psi_{i2}^{1M+}.

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{f}^{1M+}|\mathcal{M}_{z}^{M1}|\Psi_{i}^{1M+}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{f}^{1M+}|\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{z}^{M1}|\Psi_{i}^{1M+}\rangle

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm N}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g_{p}+g_{n}-1)\langle\Psi_{f}^{1M+;0}|S_{z}-M|\Psi_{i2}^{1M+}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\langle\Psi_{f}^{1M+;1}|\sum_{j=1}^{A}t_{j3}\left[L^{\prime}_{jz}+(g_{p}-g_{n})S_{jz}\right]|\Psi_{i}^{1M+}\rangle\right\}

IV
Discussion

In radiative capture reactions, operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}^{M1}
in Eq. (
5
) is equivalent to – but simpler than –
the magnetic dipole operator
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{M1}
for pointlike particles in Eq. (
1
).
It can also be useful to compute nuclear magnetic dipole moments by adding the matrix elements
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{z}^{M1}
and a term proportional to the
z
component of the total angular momentum as in Eq. (
4
).

The main information provided by the equivalent effective
M1
operator is that its isoscalar part is proportional to the total spin operator.
This means that the total spin is conserved in such magnetic transitions.
Isoscalar
M1
transitions are forbidden from initial
S
waves if distortion is neglected because of the orthogonality between initial and final states.
More generally, these transitions are forbidden if the initial
S
component is an eigenstate of the spin operator,

\displaystyle S_{z}\Psi_{i0}^{JM+}=M\Psi_{i0}^{JM+}.

(22)

An isoscalar transition from the
S
wave, which is expected to provide the largest contribution to the
M1

S
-factor, is not possible without components with total spin
S=0
or total orbital momentum
\mathcal{L}\geq 2
in this
L=0
initial partial wave.

A transition from an
S
wave is nevertheless also made possible by isospin 1 components in the wave functions.
Given the fact that the shapes of
D
wave
S
-factors at small energies are affected by centrifugal barriers,
this suggests that such partial
S
-factors decrease when the energy tends to zero.
Even with a weak
T=1
component, transitions from the initial
S
partial wave can dominate at the lowest energies.

These findings are illustrated by the
p+n+\alpha
three-body model.
Both isoscalar and isovector
M1
contributions from an initial
D
wave are negligible in the astrophysical energy domain.
Isovector
M1
transitions from an
S
wave are much more important at small energies because of their rather flat behavior but they are restricted by their dependence on small isospin 1 components in the final wave function.
The smallness of the
M1

S
-factor seems therefore quite normal and agrees with some previous calculations
NWS01
;
So22
.

Improvements of this three-body model might enhance the
M1

S
-factor.
A more elaborate initial scattering wave function than Eq. (
14
) should read

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{JM+}=\sum_{\gamma}[[Y_{l}(\Omega_{r})\otimes Y_{L}(\Omega_{R})]^{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\chi_{S}]^{JM}\psi^{J}_{i\gamma}(r,R),

(23)

where
\gamma=(lL)\mathcal{L}S
and
l+L
is even.
This flexible three-body wave function corresponds to relaxing the frozen deuteron in the initial wave by allowing
p+n
distortions.
Function
\psi^{J}_{i(00)01}(r,R)
has the same asymptotic behavior as in Eq. (
14
).
With Eq. (
23
), Eq. (
22
) can be violated and isospin 1 components which are not present in this model can appear in the initial state for
l=1
.
Such components will contribute to isovector transitions.

Transitions to the
6
Li(
1^{+}
) ground state from the
S
wave involve initial components
\psi^{1}_{i(l0)lS}(r,R)
with
l
even.
As
S_{f}=1
is the largest spin component in the final state
TDB07
, spin conservation suggests
that the main transitions will involve
S=1
.
The main part of the transition should occur from
\psi^{1}_{i(00)01}(r,R)
to
\psi^{1}_{f(00)01}(r,R)
.
But the overlap of these components is strongly constrained by the orthogonality condition

\displaystyle\sum_{lL\mathcal{L}S}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\,R^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2}\psi^{1}_{f(lL)\mathcal{L}S}(r,R)\psi^{1}_{i(lL)\mathcal{L}S}(r,R)=0.

(24)

Since terms with either
S=0
,
l>0
, or
L>0
in the sum are small, the
(00)01
term of this is overlap should also be small.
It is thus not obvious that the size of the
M1

S
-factor would be increased much with this generalization.

The
D
wave part might be enhanced by the introduction of a tensor force coupling the initial
S
and
D
waves
but this effect might be marginal on
S
-factors because of the
L=2
centrifugal barrier.

Microscopic cluster models allow extensions which include excitations of the
\alpha
particle.
Distortion of the
T=0
part and small
T=1
components in these excitations could modify the
M1

S
-factor
but the effect of such complicated modifications cannot be predicted.

In the
ab initio
calculation of Ref.
HHK22
, the
M1

S
-factor
is orders of magnitude larger than here and in Refs.
NWS01
;
So22
.
Its energy dependence is also very different.
These differences may come from a more realistic description of the
6
Li nucleus by the no-core shell-model part of the wave function in Ref.
HHK22
.
It would be interesting to perform a similar calculation using the effective microscopic
M1
operator presented in Eq. (
5
).
An analysis of the various contributions to the
M1

S
-factor would provide information about the origin of its large value in that approach
and suggest possible improvements for other models.

V
Conclusion

The
M1
contribution to cross sections of the
\alpha(d,\gamma)^{6}
Li radiative capture reaction
is subject to a controversy.
Here and in other models
NWS01
;
So22
, it is found negligible
while it is important at low energies in an
ab initio
calculation
HHK22
.

To analyze this problem, we make use of an effective operator
exactly equivalent to the LWA form of the
M1
operator
in radiative capture transition matrix elements
Ba23
.
This operator can also simplify the computation of magnetic dipole moments by inverting Eq. (
4
).
A general analysis of matrix elements of this operator for capture reactions shows
that isoscalar transitions from an initial
S
wave are not allowed
if a rather general condition is satisfied [see Eq. (
22
)].
These transitions are forbidden by the orthogonality between the initial and final states.
Isovector radiative capture from the
S
wave remains possible
but depends on the amount of isospin 1 mixing in the wave functions.

An evaluation in a three-body model involving proton, neutron and a structureless
\alpha
is performed.
This three-body model is interesting as it offers flexibility
leading to simpler physical interpretations than elaborate models.
Its initial
4
He + deuteron and final
6
Li(
1^{+}
) wave functions arise from different Hamiltonians and are properly orthogonalized.
The resulting
M1

S
-factor is very small as expected from the structure of the effective operator
and is thus negligible with respect to the
E2

S
-factor.
It is too weak to contribute to the capture process at the lowest energies where experiments are available.
This confirms results of the partly phenomenological six-body model of Ref.
NWS01
and the microscopic cluster model of Ref.
So22
.
Some aspects of the present three-body study which can limit its predictive power are discussed.

The large size difference of
M1
capture between the
ab initio
calculation of Ref.
HHK22
and other models remains unexplained.
It would be interesting to have detailed
ab initio
results using the effective
M1
operator presented in Eq. (
5
).
They would explain the physical origin of the large
M1

S
-factor in that approach.

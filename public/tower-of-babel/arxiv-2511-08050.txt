Abstract
We introduce new semi-algebraic proof systems for Quantified Boolean Formulas (QBF) analogous to the propositional systems Nullstellensatz, Sherali-Adams and Sum-of-Squares. We transfer to this setting techniques both from the QBF literature (strategy extraction) and from propositional proof complexity (size-degree relations and pseudo-expectation). We obtain a number of strong QBF lower bounds and separations between these systems, even when disregarding propositional hardness.

Introduction

Two key results in algebraic and semi-algebraic geometry are the Nullstellensatz and the Positivstellensatz.
The first can be seen as an algebraic identity certifying that a set of polynomial equations is unsatisfiable while the second as an algebraic identity certifying that a system of polynomial
inequalities
is unsatisfiable.
In other words, both the Nullstellensatz and the Positivstellensatz naturally give rise to proof systems and in recent years intense research was performed on the proof complexity of such systems.
In particular, the proof system
Sum-of-Squares
, a special case of Positivstellensatz, starting from
[
BBH+12
]
, has received a lot of attention for its connection with algorithms based on hierarchies of SDP relaxations (see for instance
[
Las01
,
OZ13
]
). For similar reasons, the even more restrictive
Sherali-Adams
proof system has been investigated, named after its connection with the Sherali-Adams hierarchy of linear programming since its original definition (see for instance
[
SA90
,
DM13
]
).

In this work, we devise a simple and natural way to extend the proof systems Nullstellensatz (
\mathsf{NS}
), Sum-of-Squares (
\mathsf{SOS}
) and Sherali-Adams (
\mathsf{SA}
) from the context of propositional existentially quantified variables to existentially and universally quantified variables. In other words, we show how to define proof systems for quantified Boolean formulas (QBF) inspired by the propositional proof systems above.

The study of propositional and QBF proof systems is motivated both by theoretical reasons and also by connections to SAT and QBF solving
[
BN21
,
BJLS21
,
Bey22
]
.
While for SAT-solvers conflict-driven clause learning (CDCL) is the ruling paradigm, which by the seminal work of
[
AFT11
,
PD11
]
is essentially equivalent to the propositional proof system
Resolution
(
Res
), there are several competing approaches in QBF, with CDCL-based
[
ZM02
]
and
expansion
-based solving
[
JM15
]
among the main paradigms.
To model the strength of QBF-solvers several (often incomparable) QBF proof systems have been introduced and analysed
[
ELW13
,
JM15
,
BB23
]
. Of most relevance to this work is QU-Resolution (
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
)
[
KKF95
,
VG12
]
.

\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
adds to propositional Resolution the
\forall
-reduction rule that allows to eliminate universal variables from clauses. In
[
BBCP20
]
it has been shown that this approach of augmenting a propositional proof system by a
\forall
-reduction rule taking care of universal quantifiers also works for other common inference-based proof systems such as various Frege systems
[
CR79
,
BBCP20
]
,
Cutting Planes

[
CCT87
,
BCMS18
]
—a proof system modelling geometric reasoning related to Chvátal-Gomory cuts— and
Polynomial Calculus

[
CEI96
,
BHKS24
]
, modelling algebraic reasoning related to Gröbner bases computations.

A common feature of all the propositional proof systems above is that they are
inference
-based, unlike the static systems
\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{SOS}
where a proof is just an algebraic identity of some specific form depending on the system at hand.
This has posed quite some problems to adapt such systems to the QBF setting, and it was not clear at all whether an approach similar to a
\forall
-reduction rule was even viable. Recently there has been a suggestion to define QBF analogues of
\mathsf{NS}
based on
\forall
-expansion
[
CCKS23
]
, but these differ considerably from our approach here and the
\forall
-reduction paradigm discussed above.

We show that an approach similar to a
\forall
-reduction rule
does
allow to define QBF versions of
\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{SOS}
, which we call
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
respectively. We argue that our definitions are quite natural: they add to the algebraic equations of
\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{SOS}
simple polynomials that strongly resemble
\forall
-reduction and meet the same technical condition on variable dependence.

2
Preliminaries

QBF preliminaries.

We consider Quantified Boolean Formulas (QBF) of the form
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, where
\varphi
is a CNF formula and
\mathcal{Q}
is the quantifier prefix. Both the variables of
\varphi
and the variables of
\mathcal{Q}
range over a set of Boolean variables
V
. Let
\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})
(resp.
\mathrm{vars}_{\exists}(\mathcal{Q})
) be the set of universally (resp. existentially) quantified variables in
\mathcal{Q}
.

The evaluation of a QBF formula
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
can be seen as a game (the
evaluation
game) between two players: the existential
\exists
-player and the universal
\forall
-player, where the
\exists
-player’s goal is to satisfy the formula
\varphi
and the
\forall
-player’s goal is to falsify it. The players take turns according to the order of the quantifiers in
\mathcal{Q}
. We call this game the evaluation game to distinguish it from a new game, the
score
game, which we introduce in
Section

3.1
.

A very well-studied QBF proof system is
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}

[
BWJ14
,
KKF95
,
VG12
]
, which can be seen as a natural extension of the propositional proof system Resolution
[
Bla37
,
Rob65
]
to the QBF setting.

The QBF proof system
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutes a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
inferring the empty clause
\bot
from the clauses in
\varphi
using the resolution rule
\frac{C\lor v\hskip 8.19447ptD\lor\lnot v}{C\lor D}
, but also using a
\forall
-reduction
rule
\frac{C\lor u}{C}
, where all the variables in
C
must be on the left of
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
.
The
size
of a
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutation
\pi
(
\size(\pi)
) is the number of applications of rules in
\pi
, while
Q-size
(
\qsize(\pi)
) is the number of applications of the
\forall
-reduction rule.

Algebraic proof systems.

Given a set of Boolean variables
V
, let
\overline{V}
be the set of new formal variables
\overline{v}
for
v\in V
. We consider polynomials with rational coefficients and variables in
V\cup\overline{V}
, i.e. polynomials in the ring
\mathbb{Q}[V\cup\overline{V}]
. Given a polynomial
p
and an assignment
\alpha
of its variables, we denote with
\left.{p}\right|_{\alpha}
the evaluation of
p
in
\alpha
.

In this work we encode clauses and CNF formulas into polynomials using the so-called
twin-variables encoding
.
A clause
C=\bigvee_{v\in P}v\lor\bigvee_{v\in N}\lnot v
is encoded as the set of polynomials

\enc(C)=\left\{\prod_{v\in P}\overline{v}\prod_{v\in N}v\right\}\cup\{v^{2}-v,\ v+\overline{v}-1:v\in P\cup N\}\ .

A CNF
\varphi=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}
is encoded as a set of polynomials
\enc(\varphi)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\enc(C_{j})
. The formula
\varphi
is satisfiable if and only if the set of polynomial equalities
\{p=0:p\in\enc(\varphi)\}
is satisfiable.

Fact 2.1
.

Given a polynomial
r\in\mathbb{Q}[V\cup\overline{V}]
, if
r
evaluates to
0
over every Boolean assignment satisfying
\varphi
(and setting
v+\overline{v}
to
1
), then
r
is in the ideal generated by
\enc(\varphi)
, i.e. there are polynomials
q_{p}
such that
r=\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p
.

A refutation of an unsatisfiable CNF
\varphi
in variables
V
in the system Nullstellensatz,
\mathsf{NS}
, (resp. Sherali-Adams,
\mathsf{SA}
, or Sum-of-Squares,
\mathsf{SOS}
) is an algebraic identity
\pi
of the form

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+q+1=0\ ,

(1)

where all the polynomials
q_{p}
,
q
are in
\mathbb{Q}[V\cup\overline{V}]
, and for
\mathsf{NS}

q
is identically
0
(resp. for
\mathsf{SA}

q
is a polynomial with non-negative coefficients, and for
\mathsf{SOS}

q=\sum_{s\in S}s^{2}
, that is
q
is a sum of squares). The
size
of the
\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi
,
\size(\pi)
, is the number of monomials (counted with repetition) in
q_{p}
and
q
. The
degree
of
\pi
,
\deg(\pi)
, is the maximum degree of any of the polynomials
q_{p}p
, and
q
.

Remark 2.2

(On variations of
\mathsf{NS}
,
\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{SOS}
)
.

The proof system
\mathsf{NS}
has been considered also for polynomials over arbitrary fields
[
BGIP01
]
. In this paper we focus only on polynomials with rational coefficients. The proof systems
\mathsf{NS}
,
\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{SOS}
have been also studied using a different encoding of CNF formulas: the encoding
\enc^{\prime}(C)
, which is the same as
\enc(C)
but with each
\bar{v}
variable substituted by
1-v
. The systems
\mathsf{NS}
,
\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{SOS}
under the
\enc^{\prime}
encoding are exponentially weaker than the corresponding system under the encoding
\enc

[
dRLNS21
]
. In this paper we focus only on polynomials using the encoding
\enc
. The proof system
\mathsf{SOS}
has also been studied recently on Boolean variables representing the Boolean values as
\pm 1
instead of
0/1

[
Sok20
]
, i.e. using instead of the polynomials
v^{2}-v
the polynomials
v^{2}-1
. In this paper we focus only on polynomials using
0/1
-valued variables.

Another well-studied algebraic propositional proof system is
Polynomial Calculus
(
\mathsf{PC}
)
[
CEI96
]
. A Polynomial Calculus (over
\mathbb{Q}
) refutation of the set of polynomials
\enc(\varphi)
, for an unsatisfiable CNF formula
\varphi
, is a sequence of polynomials showing that
1
can be derived from
\enc(\varphi)
using the inference rules
\frac{p\hskip 8.19447ptq}{p+q}
for polynomials
p,q
, and
\frac{p}{vp}
where
v
is a variable or
v\in\mathbb{Q}
. The
degree
and (monomial)
size
of the refutation are respectively the largest degree of a polynomial in it and the number of monomials in it (counted with multiplicity).

In
[
BBCP20
,
BHKS24
]
, the authors showed how to extend the proof system
\mathsf{PC}
to the QBF context. This resulted in QBF proof system
Q-Polynomial Calculus
(
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
).
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
refutes a QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
analogously to the system
\mathsf{PC}
, i.e. showing that the polynomial
1
can be derived from the polynomials in
\enc(\varphi)
using the inference rules
\frac{p\hskip 8.19447ptq}{p+q}
for polynomials
p,q
, and
\frac{p}{vp}
where
v
is a variable or
v\in\mathbb{Q}
, but also using a
\forall
-reduction
rule
\frac{p}{\left.{p}\right|_{u=b}}
, for
b\in\{0,1\}
where all the variables in
p
distinct from
u
must be left of
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
. The
\size
of a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
refutation is the number of monomials in the refutation (counted with repetition), while the
\qsize
is the number of monomials in the polynomials involved in the
\forall
-reduction steps (again counted with repetition). The reason to study
\qsize
and not just
\size
is to factor out the propositional hardness of the principles and focus on genuine QBF hardness (cf.
[
BHP20
,
Che17
]
).

3
Algebraic systems for QBFs

We introduce new QBF proof systems inspired by propositional
\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{SOS}
. We call them
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
. As their propositional counterparts they are static proof systems: a refutation of a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
over variables
V
in Q-Nullstellensatz,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
, (resp. Q-Sherali-Adams,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
, and Q-Sum-of-Squares,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
) is an algebraic identity
\pi
of the form

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0\ ,

(2)

where all the polynomials
q_{p}
,
q_{u}

q
are in
\mathbb{Q}[V\cup\overline{V}]
, the variables in
q_{u}
are all quantified before
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
(i.e. on the left of
u
), and for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}

q
is identically
0
(resp. for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}

q
is a polynomial with non-negative coefficients, and for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}

q=\sum_{s\in S}s^{2}
, that is
q
is a sum of squares). We call the expression in eq. (
2
) a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
-refutation
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
(resp.
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
-refutation/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
-refutation).

Definition 3.1

(size, degree,
\qsize
and
\qdeg
)
.

The
size
of a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi
(
\size(\pi)
) is the number of monomials (counted with repetition) in
q_{p}
,
q_{u}
and
q
. The
degree
of
\pi
(
\deg(\pi)
) is the maximum degree of any of the polynomials
q_{p}p
,
q_{u}(1-2u)
, and
q
.

The
Q-size
of
\pi

(
\qsize(\pi)
)
is defined analogously to the
\size
but accounts only for the monomials in the polynomials
q_{u}
. The existential Q-degree of
\pi

(
\qdeg(\pi)
)
is the maximum existential degree of any
q_{u}
, where the existential degree is the highest number of existentially quantified variables in any monomial.

The definitions of size and degree for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
are completely analogous to the definitions in the propositional setting, while Q-size and Q-degree factor out propositional hardness and therefore give measures more appropriate to study principles where the hardness stems from quantification.
The definition of Q-size also aligns with genuine QBF hardness measures defined in
[
BHP20
]
and analysed e.g. in
[
BBMP23
,
BHKS24
]
for QU-Resolution and
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
, where only universal reduction steps are counted. In a sense, the polynomial
q_{u}
in (
2
) can be understood as a universal reduction step on
u
. In particular, on QBFs without universal variables,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
are equivalent to their propositional counterparts
\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{SOS}
.

Any lower bound on Q-size immediately implies the same lower bound on size.
The reason to consider the
existential
Q-degree is a connection between Q-size and existential Q-degree similar to the inequality between size and width in resolution
[
BBMP23
]
(see
Section

3.3
).

As a first result we prove that
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
are sound QBF proof systems.

Theorem 3.2

(soundness)
.

If there exists a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
- or
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
- or
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
-refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, then
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
is false.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
is a true QBF, so the
\exists
-player has a winning strategy
\sigma
, but at the same time there is a refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
of the form as in eq. (
2
)
where all the variables in polynomials
q_{u}
are on the left of
u
and
q
is identically zero (
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
), or a polynomial with non-negative coefficients (
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
), or a sum of squares (
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
).

For every strategy
\tau
of the
\forall
-player, the game proceeds following the strategies
\sigma
and
\tau
and constructs a total Boolean assignment
\alpha_{\sigma,\tau}
satisfying the matrix
\varphi
. That is
\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p
evaluates to
0
under every assignment
\alpha_{\sigma,\tau}
. For a universal variable
u
, we write
\tau_{<u}
for the part of
\tau
on variables to the left of
u
and
\tau_{\geqslant u}
for the rest of
\tau
. Taking a uniform probability distribution over all universal strategies
\tau
, for every universal variable
u
it holds that:

\E_{\tau}\left[q_{u}(1-2u)\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star)}}{{=}}\E_{\tau_{<u}}\left[q_{u}\E_{\tau_{\geqslant u}}\left[1-2u\right]\right]=\E_{\tau_{<u}}\left[q_{u}\cdot 0\right]=0\ ,

where in the equality
(\star)
we used the fact that all the polynomials
q_{u}
only depend on variables on the left of
u
.
Hence, evaluating both sides of eq. (
2
) on
\alpha_{\sigma,\tau}
and taking
\E_{\tau}
, the LHS equals
\E_{\tau}[\left.{q}\right|_{\alpha_{\sigma,\tau}}+1]
, which is always at least
1
, while the RHS is
0
. Contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.3

(
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
over arbitrary fields)
.

The definition of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
from eq. (
2
) can be trivially adapted from polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
to arbitrary fields of characteristic different from
2
. In characteristic
2
it gives an unsound system since all the terms
(1-2u)
are identically 1. Indeed, in characteristic 2 every formula with at least one universal variable could be “refuted” by setting all
q_{p}=0
and a single
q_{u}=1
.

Remark 3.4

(unary vs binary coefficients)
.

Unlike in the propositional setting where the unary versions of
\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{SOS}
give rise to non-trivial (and interesting) proof systems
[
GHJ+24
]
, in the QBF setting imposing unary (i.e.
\pm 1
) coefficients in
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}/\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations seems to give rise to very weak systems. For instance, unary
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
cannot even efficiently refute a false QBF formula as simple as
\forall u_{1}\forall u_{2}\cdots\forall u_{n}.\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Any
unary

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\forall u_{1}\forall u_{2}\cdots\forall u_{n}.\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}
has an exponential number of monomials.

Proof.

Consider a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
-refutation of
\forall u_{1}\forall u_{2}\cdots\forall u_{n}.\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}
:

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0\ .

(3)

We show that, if the polynomials are written as sums of monomials with
\pm 1
coefficients then there must be an exponential number of them. The argument is very similar to the one used to prove the soundness of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
(
3.2
).

For every universal strategy
\tau
, the evaluation game following
\tau
gives a total Boolean assignment
\alpha_{\tau}
. The sum
\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p
under
\alpha_{\tau}
evaluates to
q_{0}(\alpha_{\tau})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{u_{i}}(\alpha_{\tau})
, which is
0
for every
\tau
assigning some
u_{i}=1
(and hence
\overline{u_{i}}=0
). Let
\tau^{*}
be the universal strategy assigning all variables to
0
.
For a universal strategy
\tau
and universal variable
u
, we write
\tau_{<u}
for the part of
\tau
on variables to the left of
u
and
\tau_{\geqslant u}
for the rest of
\tau
.
Taking the uniform probability distribution over all universal strategies
\tau
, for every universal variable
u
it holds that:

\E_{\tau}\left[q_{u}(1-2u)\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star)}}{{=}}\E_{\tau_{<u}}\left[q_{u}\E_{\tau_{\geqslant u}}\left[1-2u\right]\right]=\E_{\tau_{<u}}\left[q_{u}\cdot 0\right]=0\ ,

where in the equality
(\star)
we used the fact that all the polynomials
q_{u}
only depend on variables on the left of
u
.
Evaluating both sides of eq. (
3
) on
\alpha_{\tau}
and taking
\E_{\tau}
we obtain that

\E_{\tau}[\left.{q_{0}}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left.{\overline{u_{i}}}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau}}+\left.{q}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau}}+1]=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left.{q_{0}}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau^{*}}}+\E_{\tau}[\left.{q}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau}}]+1=0\ .

Since
q
is always non-negative,
\E_{\tau}[\left.{q}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau}}]\geqslant 0
and hence the absolute value of
\left.{q_{0}}\right|_{\alpha_{\tau^{*}}}
is at least
2^{n}
. Therefore the polynomial
q_{0}
when written as a sum of monomials with
\pm 1
coefficients must have an exponential number of them.
∎

3.1
Completeness via a score game

To show completeness of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
, we introduce a new
score
game. We call it
score
game to distinguish it from the
evaluation
game used for the QBF semantics (cf. Sec.
2
).

The
score
game, as the evaluation game, is played between a universal and an existential player on a QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, building a total Boolean assignment. As in the evaluation game, the players take turns according to the quantifier prefix
\mathcal{Q}
and the existential player can freely decide on the value of existential variables.
For the universal variables the score game differs from the usual evaluation game: the universal player gives a preference for the universal variable
u
in the form of a number
s_{u}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then, the existential player sets
u
to
b\in\{0,1\}
and the universal player scores
s_{u}(2b-1)
points. There are two variants of this game that differ in the winning condition:

variant 1

the universal player wins if
\varphi
is falsified or the total score is strictly positive;

variant 2

the universal player wins if
\varphi
is falsified or the total score equals 1.

Clearly every winning strategy of
variant 2
is also a winning strategy of
variant 1
.
The intuition behind the universal preferences is that the sign of
s_{u}
encodes the preferred assignment (if the preferred assignment is
u=0
then the universal player sets
s_{u}>0
, and
s_{u}<0
for
u=1
) and the absolute value encodes the magnitude of this preferred choice.
If the existential player follows the choice, the universal player loses
|s_{u}|
points; otherwise he gains the same amount.

Our interest in the score game is that the universal winning strategies can be transformed into
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations.

Proposition 3.6
.

A QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
is false if and only if the universal player has a winning strategy in the score game for
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
(in either variant).

Proof.

Let the QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
be false.
Then there exists a winning strategy
\tau=(\tau_{u})_{u\in\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}
for the universal player in the evaluation game.
In the score game, on the universal variable
u
the universal player plays depending on the total score
S
up to this point and
\tau
. The preferred choice of the universal player is
\tau_{u}
and he assigns to
u
score

\displaystyle s_{u}=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{if }S=1\ ,\\
(1-2\tau_{u})(1-S)\quad&\text{otherwise}\ .\end{cases}

If in each step of the game the universal player gets always his preference
\tau_{u}
, then the matrix
\varphi
is falsified (since
\tau
is a winning strategy in the evaluation game).
Otherwise, let
u^{*}
be the first variable where the universal player does not get his preference and
S^{*}
the total score before deciding the value for
u
. Since, by assumption the universal player does not get his preference but the value
1-\tau_{u^{*}}
instead, then, after setting
u^{*}
, the total score is

S^{*}+s_{u^{*}}(2(1-\tau_{u^{*}})-1)=1\ .

(4)

At this moment the universal player has essentially just won since he can set all following scores to
0
and the final total score of the game will be
1
.

For the other direction, if the universal player has a winning strategy in the score game on
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
then he wins also against the case when the existential player makes the scores negative in each moment of the game. In this case the resulting assignment must falsify the matrix
\varphi
since the universal player is using a winning strategy. As such, this strategy is also a winning strategy for the universal player in the usual evaluation game and the QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
is false.
∎

We require the universal strategy for each universal variable
u
to be expressed as a polynomial in all variables to the left of
u
in the quantifier prefix.
The
size
of a universal strategy in the score game is then the sum of the number of monomials in all the
s_{u}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\pi
be a shortest
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
(resp.
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
) refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
with respect to its Q-size. Then
\qsize(\pi)
equals the size of the shortest universal winning strategy in the score game in
variant 1
(resp.
variant 2
) on
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
U=\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})
, let
S
be the size of the shortest universal winning strategy in the score game on
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, and let
\pi
be the LHS of a shortest
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
(resp.
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
) refutation written as

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q=-1\ .

(5)

To show that
\qsize(\pi)\geqslant S
consider the universal strategy setting
s_{u}=q_{u}
. This is a winning strategy in the score game, i.e. for every total assignment
\alpha
the universal player wins the score game. Indeed, if
\alpha
falsifies
\varphi
then the universal player wins automatically (in both versions of the game). Assume then that
\alpha
satisfies
\varphi
, that is for every
p\in\enc(\varphi)
,
\left.{p}\right|_{\alpha}=0
.
Therefore,
\pi
evaluated at
\alpha
is the same as
\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(1-2u)+q
evaluated at
\alpha
. The expression
\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(1-2u)
evaluates under
\alpha
to
-1
if
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutation or to
\leqslant-1<0
if
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation.
Therefore the total score when playing the game given the total assignment
\alpha
is

\sum_{u\in U}s_{u}(2u-1)=\left.{\left(\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(2u-1)\right)}\right|_{\alpha}=-\left.{\left(\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(1-2u)\right)}\right|_{\alpha}

and this latter sum equals
1
if
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutation or it is
>0
if
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation. In other words the universal player in such cases wins using the scores.

To prove
\qsize(\pi)\leqslant S
, we consider the cases where
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
or
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation.

Case 1:
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutation.

Let
(s_{u})_{u\in U}
be a shortest universal winning strategy for the score game in
variant 2
on
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and let
q_{u}
be the polynomial computing
s_{u}
as a function of the variables left of
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
.
In particular, on all Boolean assignments
\alpha
satisfying the matrix
\varphi
, the universal player wins because the total score is
1
, i.e.
\left.{\left(\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(1-2u)\right)}\right|_{\alpha}=-1
and therefore
\sum_{u\in U}q_{u}(1-2u)+1
is in the ideal generated by the polynomials in
\enc(\varphi)
(this follows from
2.1
).
This gives a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
with a Q-size of at most
S
.

Case 2:
\pi
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation.

Let
(s_{u})_{u\in U}
be a shortest universal winning strategy for the score game in
variant 2
on
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and let
q_{u}
be the polynomial computing
s_{u}
as a function of the variables left of
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
.
For an assignment
\alpha
, let
\score(\alpha)=\sum_{u\in U}s_{u}\left.{(2u-1)}\right|_{\alpha}
. For every Boolean assignment
\alpha
satisfying the matrix
\varphi
, since
(s_{u})_{u}
is a winning strategy, we have
\score(\alpha)>0
. That is for
c=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\alpha\models\varphi}\score(\alpha)
we have

\sum_{u\in U}\frac{s_{u}}{c}(1-2u)=-\frac{\score(\alpha)}{c}<-1\ .

(6)

In this way, for every
\alpha
satisfying
\varphi
,
1-\frac{\score(\alpha)}{c}<0
. Let

q=-\sum_{\alpha\models\varphi}\left(1-\frac{\score(\alpha)}{c}\right)\chi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{v})\ ,

where
\chi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{v})
is the monomial which evaluates to
1
if the variables
\boldsymbol{v}
are set according to
\alpha
, and
0
on any other Boolean assignment. Modulo the polynomials
v^{2}-v
,
q
is a sum of squares, hence to conclude it is enough to notice that the polynomial

\sum_{u\in U}\frac{1}{c}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1

evaluates to
0
on every assignment satisfying
\enc(\varphi)
, hence it belongs to the ideal generated by the polynomials in
\enc(\varphi)
(this follows from
2.1
). This gives a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
having the same
\qsize
as the strategy
(s_{u})_{u}
.
∎

Corollary 3.8

(completeness)
.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
are complete.

Proof.

Proposition
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
immediately imply the completeness of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
. As
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutations are special cases of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations their completeness also follows.
∎

Alternatively, it is also possible to prove the completeness of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
directly (without using the score game), as follows.

Alternative proof of completeness of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
.

It is sufficient to show the completeness without twin variables. For this proof we use the encoding where
\enc(x)=1-x
,
\enc(\neg x)=x
,

\enc(C)=\prod_{\ell\in C}\enc(\ell)
.

For a partial assignment
\rho
, let
\textsf{Ind}(\rho)
be the “indicator” polynomial that evaluates to
1
on all assignments extending
\rho
, and to
0
on all
Boolean assignments that disagree with
\rho
. Formally, let

\textsf{Ind}(\rho)=\prod_{x\in\rho^{-1}(1)}x\prod_{x\in\rho^{-1}(0)}(1-x)\ .

Notice that if
\rho
falsifies
C
, then
\enc(C)
divides

\textsf{Ind}(\rho)
.
Given a winning strategy
S
for the universal player in the
evaluation game, we use it to prune the complete assignment tree, i.e. remove branches not consistent with
S
.

Furthermore, we terminate a path
as soon as a clause is falsified, and label the corresponding leaf with that falsified clause. (If there is more than one, pick any one
arbitrarily.)
This is a decision tree
T
which, given any
assignment
\alpha
to the existential variables, finds a clause
falsified by the assignment
\alpha\cup S(\alpha)
.
The tree is unary-binary, since nodes labelled by universal variables have only one child.
For a node
v
in the tree
T
, let
\alpha_{v}
denote the partial
assignment leading to
v
. For the root node
r
,

\alpha_{r}=\emptyset
, and
\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{r})=1
.

We show, by a bottom-up traversal, that for each node
v
, the
polynomial
\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})
can be expressed as a polynomial
combination of the initial clause encodings
\{\enc(C)\mid C\in F\}

and the universal polynomials
(1-2u)
, where the multipliers for

1-2u
involve only variables quantified left of
u
.

Case 1:

v
is a leaf, where clause
C
is falsified. In this case,

\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})
is a polynomial multiple of
\enc(C)
.

Case 2:

v
is a node with two children,
v_{0}
and

v_{1}
. Then, for some existential variable
x
,
\alpha_{v_{b}}

extends
\alpha_{v}
by
x=b
. We have inductively expressed

\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v_{b}})
for
b=0,1
. Now

\displaystyle\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})

\displaystyle=\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v_{0}})+\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v_{1}})\ .

\displaystyle\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v_{0}})+\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v_{1}})

\displaystyle=\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-x)+\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})x

\displaystyle=\textsf{Ind}(\alpha)v\ .

Case 3:

v
is a node with one child
w
. Then for some
universal variable
u
and some
b
,
\alpha_{w}
extends
\alpha_{v}

by
u=b
, and we have inductively expressed
\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{w})
.
Consider
b=0
, then

\displaystyle 2\,\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{w})-\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-2u)

\displaystyle=2\,\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-u)-\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-2u)

\displaystyle=\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})\ .

Since all variables in
\alpha_{v}
are quantified
left of
u
,
\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})
is a valid multiplier for
(1-2u)
.
Similarly if
b=1
, then

\displaystyle 2\,\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{w})+\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-2u)

\displaystyle=2\,\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})u+\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})(1-2u)

\displaystyle=\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{v})\ .

Proceeding in this way all the way to the root, we get that

\textsf{Ind}(\alpha_{r})=1
is expressible as a polynomial combination of the
clause encodings and the universal polynomials with valid multipliers.
∎

3.2
Upper bounds in
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
via the score game

Due to
3.7
, the score game can be used to obtain bounds on the Q-size of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations.
The advantage is being able to argue directly on countermodels (without reference to the syntactic representation of the matrix).
To that end, we use QBFs
Q\text{-}C_{n}
from
[
BBCP20
]
, which are defined via their countermodel computed by a family of circuits
C_{n}
.

Definition 3.9

(
Q\text{-}C_{n}

[
BBCP20
]
)
.

Let
n
be an integer and
C_{n}
be a circuit with inputs
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and a single output.
We define

Q\text{-}C_{n}=\exists x_{1}\cdots\exists x_{n}\forall u\exists t_{1}\cdots\exists t_{m}.\big(u\nleftrightarrow C_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\big)\ ,

where the additional variables
t_{i}
are used for a Tseitin-encoding of the circuit
C_{n}
into CNF (the
i^{\text{th}}
node in
C_{n}
is represented by variable
t_{i}
, and if, e.g., node
i
is an
\land
-gate between nodes
j
and
k
, then we have clauses encoding
t_{i}\leftrightarrow(t_{j}\land t_{k})
, and similarly for
\lor
and
\neg
gates).

For the
Q\text{-}C_{n}
formulas, the only countermodel sets
u
to
C_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n})
.
Here, we are specifically interested in choosing circuits
C_{n}
that compute
\textsf{Majority}_{n}
.
A circuit calculating
\textsf{Majority}_{n}
evaluates to true, if and only if at least half of the
n
input variables are set to true.
We show that
\textsf{Q-Majority}_{n}
has short
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations in the
\qsize
measure.

Proposition 3.10
.

\textsf{Q-Majority}_{n}
has
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations of linear
\qsize
.

Proof.

In the score game for
\textsf{Q-Majority}_{n}
, set
s_{u}=-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{n}+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
. The choice of the constant
\frac{1}{4}
is somewhat arbitrary, but should be between 0 and
\frac{1}{2}
.
As there is only a single universal variable, this defines a complete strategy for the universal player.
We show that
s_{u}
is a winning strategy.
For an arbitrary assignment
\alpha
,
\left.{s_{u}}\right|_{\alpha}<0
if and only if at least half of the existential
x_{i}
variables are set to 1, otherwise
\left.{s_{u}}\right|_{\alpha}>0
. As such, the total score of the score game on
\alpha
is

\left.{\big(-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{n}+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big)(2u-1)}\right|_{\alpha}
.
This is negative only if either
u=1
and at least half of the
x_{i}
equal 1 or
u=0
and less then half of the
x_{i}
equal 1,
i.e. if the matrix is satisfied, the score is positive.

This strategy has size
n+1
and degree
1
, hence, by
3.7
, there exists a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\textsf{Q-Majority}_{n}
with a
\qsize
linear in
n
.
∎

It is known that
\textsf{Q-Majority}_{n}
requires
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
refutations of exponential
\qsize

[
BHKS24
]
, therefore the previous result yields the exponential separation between
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}
and
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
in
Figure

1
.

3.3
From existential Q-degree to Q-size

In various proof systems, strong enough lower bounds on the degree/width of proofs immediately imply non-trivial lower bounds on proof size. This happens for instance in propositional proof systems such as Resolution
[
BSW01
]
, Polynomial Calculus
[
CEI96
]
, Sherali-Adams and Sum-of-Squares
[
AH19
]
; and in QBF proof systems as well, for instance in QU-Resolution
[
BBMP23
]
, and Q-PC
[
BHKS24
]
.
It turns out that a very similar statement holds in
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
between the existential Q-degree (
\qdeg(\cdot)
, see
3.1
) and Q-size (
\qsize(\cdot)
, see
3.1
).

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
Q.\varphi
be a false QBF with
n
variables that has a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\qsize

s
. Then it has a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\qdeg

O(\sqrt{n\log s})
.

The argument is similar to the proof of the analogous size-width inequality for Resolution from
[
BSW01
]
. The main difference is the proof of the lemma below showing how to combine a proof of
\qdeg

k-1
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi|_{x=1}
and a proof of
\qdeg

k
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi|_{x=0}
into a proof of
\qdeg

k
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
. This is done using the score game from the previous section.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
Q.\varphi
be a false QBF and
x\in\mathrm{vars}_{\exists}(\mathcal{Q})
. If there is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi_{1}
of
Q.\varphi|_{x=1}
with
\qdeg(\pi_{1})\leqslant k-1
, and a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi_{0}
of
Q.\varphi|_{x=0}
with
\qdeg(\pi_{0})\leqslant k
, then there is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
with
\qdeg(\pi)\leqslant k
.

Proof.

We consider the equivalent representation of the proofs as universal strategies in the score game.
Let
q_{1}
be the final score in
\pi_{1}
and
q_{0}
be the final score in
\pi_{0}
.
Let
c
be the maximum of
\lvert q_{0}\rvert+1
over all assignments, and
d
be the smallest
positive
value that
q_{1}
can take (or
1
if
q_{1}
does not take positive values).
For each
u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})
, let
q_{u}=x\cdot q_{u}^{1}+\frac{d}{c}q_{u}^{0}
, where
q_{u}^{0}
and
q_{u}^{1}
are the polynomials for
u
in
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
. Combining these
q_{u}
yields a strategy
\pi
with final score
q=x\cdot q_{1}+\frac{d}{c}q_{0}
and
\qdeg(\pi)\leqslant k
.

We still need to argue that
\pi
is a universal winning strategy, i.e. that on every assignment
\alpha
satisfying
\varphi
we have
q(\alpha)>0
.
If
\alpha
sets
x=0
, then it satisfies
\varphi|_{x=0}
, so
q_{0}(\alpha)>0
due to the correctness of
\pi_{0}
. But if
x=0
then
q=0\cdot q_{1}+\frac{d}{c}q_{0}>0
.
If
\alpha
sets
x=1
, then it satisfies
\varphi|_{x=1}
, so
q_{1}(\alpha)>0
due to the correctness of
\pi_{1}
. By the definitions of
c
and
d
, we have
c>-q_{0}(\alpha)
and
d\leqslant q_{1}(\alpha)
. This means that
\frac{d}{c}q_{0}>-d\geqslant-q_{1}(\alpha)
and
q=1\cdot q_{1}+\frac{d}{c}q_{0}>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
d,n,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\geqslant 0}
and
Q.\varphi
be a false QBF. Let
\pi
be a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
Q.\varphi
so that its
q_{u}
polynomials contain, in total, fewer than
(1-\frac{d}{2n})^{-b}
monomials of existential degree
>d
. Then there is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi^{\prime}
of
Q.\varphi
with
\qdeg(\pi^{\prime})\leqslant d+b
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is virtually identical to the proof of
[
BSW01
, Theorem 3.5]
.
Informally, the proof is by an inductive argument on
n
and
b
, considering the
high-degree
monomials to be the ones with
\qdeg
at least
d
. By a counting argument there will be a literal
x
appearing in at least a
\frac{d}{2n}
fraction of them. Restricting by
x=0
and
x=1
, we have that the first restriction eliminates at least
\frac{d}{2n}
of the high-degree monomials, while the second eliminates one variable. Then using the inductive hypothesis and
3.12
concludes the argument.

More formally, we fix
d
and prove the statement by induction over
n
and
b
.
In the base cases where
b=0
or
n\leqslant d
,
\pi^{\prime}=\pi
has the required
\qdeg
.
Otherwise, we call those monomials
high-degree
which have existential degree
>d
. If
\pi
has
k<(1-\frac{d}{2n})^{-b}
high-degree monomials, they contain in total
>kd
existential literals. Because there are only
2n
existential literals, one of these, say
x
, must occur in more than
\frac{kd}{2n}
high-degree monomials.

When restricting
\pi
to
x=0
, all these monomials vanish, leaving fewer than
k-\frac{kd}{2n}=k(1-\frac{d}{2n})<(1-\frac{d}{2n})^{-(b-1)}<(1-\frac{d}{2(n-1)})^{-(b-1)}
high-degree monomials.
Applying the induction hypothesis yields a proof
\pi_{0}
of
Q.\varphi|_{x=0}
with
\qdeg

\leqslant d+b-1
.
When restricting
\pi
to
x=1
, the number of high-degree monomials certainly cannot increase, so it is at most
k<(1-\frac{d}{2n})^{-b}<(1-\frac{d}{2(n-1)})^{-b}
. Applying the induction hypothesis yields a proof
\pi_{1}
of
Q.\varphi|_{x=1}
with
\qdeg

\leqslant d+b
.

Finally, applying Lemma
3.12
to
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
yields a proof
\pi^{\prime}
of
\qdeg

\leqslant d+b
.
∎

Given the two lemmas above it is immediate to prove
3.11
.

Proof of
3.11
.

Set
b=d=\sqrt{2n\log s}
and observe that
s<(1-\frac{d}{2n})^{-b}
.
The number of high-degree monomials in the refutation is smaller than its total number of monomials
s
, so we can apply Lemma
3.13
and get a refutation of
\qdeg(b+d)\in O(\sqrt{n\log s})
.
∎

4
Lower bounds via strategy extraction in the evaluation game

In QBF proof systems, strategy extraction is a welcome and ubiquitous feature.
Informally, given a refutation of a false QBF, strategy extraction allows to represent in some computational model a winning strategy of the universal player in the
evaluation
game.
Different QBF proof systems give rise to strategy extraction in different computational models.
For example, from
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutations we get
unified decision lists

[
BBMP23
]
and from Frege+
\forall
-reduction refutations we get
\mathsf{NC}_{1}
circuits
[
BBCP20
]
.

In this section, we show that
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
also admit strategy extraction, using
polynomial threshold functions
(PTF) as the computational model (
4.3
). We use this fact to prove a lower bound in
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
(
4.4
) and a p-simulation of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
by
Q-TC
0
-Frege
(
4.5
).

Definition 4.1

(polynomial threshold function)
.

A Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{\pm 1\}
is computed by a
polynomial threshold function (PTF)
, if there exists some
n
-variate polynomial
p\in\mathbb{Q}[\boldsymbol{x}]
such that
f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sign(p(\boldsymbol{x}))
.
The
size
of the PTF is the number of monomials in
p
and the
degree
of the PTF is the degree of
p
.

Remark 4.2

(On the PTF degree of parity)
.

It is well known that
f(\boldsymbol{x})=x_{1}\oplus x_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus x_{n}
cannot be computed by PTFs of degree less than
n
. We recall briefly the argument (see also for instance
[
MP87
]
). Let
f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sign(p(\boldsymbol{x}))
with
p
a
n
-variate polynomial of degree
d
. Since
f
is symmetric, there exists a symmetric
n
variate polynomial
p^{\prime}
such that
f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sign(p^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{x}))
and
\deg(p^{\prime})\leqslant\deg(p)=d
. Since
p^{\prime}
is symmetric, there exists a univariate polynomial
p^{\prime\prime}
such that
p^{\prime\prime}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})=p^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{x})
and
\deg(p^{\prime\prime})=\deg(p^{\prime})
. In other words,
f(\boldsymbol{x})=\sign(p^{\prime\prime}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}))
. To conclude it is enough to notice that on
0
,
1
,
\dots
,
n
the polynomial
p^{\prime\prime}
must be alternating signs and hence it must have at least
n
real roots; therefore
d\geqslant\deg(p^{\prime\prime})\geqslant n
.

Given a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
refutation

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0

of a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, we claim that for every universal variable
u
,
\sign(q_{u})
computes a strategy for
1-2u
.
1
1

1

We use the convention that
\sign(0)=+1
.

This can then be easily transformed into a strategy for
u
through a linear output transformation mapping
-1
to
1
and
1
to
0
.
Per definition, for every universal variable
u
,
\sign(q_{u})
is a PTF. The size of the extracted strategy is the sum of the sizes of the PTFs and, as such, the Q-size of the refutation.
Analogously, the degree of the extracted strategy, i.e. the maximum degree of the PTFs, equals the total Q-degree of the refutation.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0
be a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
/
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
proof of a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
. Then the universal strategy that maps each universal variable
u\in\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})
to

\frac{1}{2}(1-\sign(q_{u}))

is a countermodel (i.e. falsifies
\varphi
).

Proof.

The syntactic restriction of countermodels, i.e. that each variable only depends on variables left of it in the quantifier prefix
\mathcal{Q}
, holds by definition of
q_{u}
.

For every universal variable
u
played according to the strategy, we have
q_{u}(1-2u)=q_{u}\sign(q_{u})\geqslant 0
.
As such,

\sum_{u\in\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1\geqslant 1
.
Hence,
\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p\leqslant-1
, which is only possible if the matrix
\varphi
is not satisfied (otherwise
q_{p}p=0
for all
p\in\enc(\varphi)
).
∎

To exemplify the strategy extraction technique for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
, we use
4.2
and
4.3
to prove that the

formulas
[

]

\textsf{Parity}_{n}=\exists x_{1}\cdots\exists x_{n}\forall u\exists t_{1}\cdots\exists t_{n}.\ (t_{1}\leftrightarrow x_{1})\land(u\nleftrightarrow t_{n})\land\bigwedge_{i=2}^{n}(t_{i}\leftrightarrow t_{i-1}\oplus x_{i}).

are exponentially hard for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
.
Notice that the only winning strategy for the universal player is to set
u=x_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus x_{n}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Every
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\textsf{Parity}_{n}
requires Q-size
\exp(\Omega(n))
.

Proof.

Let

and apply strategy extraction (
4.3
) to get a PTF of degree

that computes

. By
4.2
, its degree is at least

, so

. Let

and apply Theorem

to obtain

and therefore

.
Theorem
3.11
can be applied here, because
\textsf{Parity}_{n}
only has a single universal variable and, as such, its existential Q-degree equals its total Q-degree.
∎

As a second consequence of

, strategy extraction can also be used to embed
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
into more powerful systems, in this case
Q-TC
0
-Frege

Q-TC

-Frege
is the
\mathsf{TC}_{0}\text{-}\mathsf{Frege}

Corollary 4.5
.

Q-TC
0
-Frege

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}

Proof.

We extract PTFs calculating a countermodel for the QBF from

refutations.
A PTF can be transformed into a depth-2 threshold circuit as follows.
Every monomial can be seen as an AND Gate, since we are only interested in Boolean assignments. In the second layer, we can use a single weighted linear threshold function using the AND gates as inputs.

It is known that the
\qsize
of
Q-TC
0
-Frege
refutations is characterized by the size of expressing the strategy in
\mathsf{TC}_{0}
circuits
[

]
.
As such, there exists a
Q-TC
0
-Frege

bounded by the size of the

of the

5
Lower bounds via Q-pseudo-expectation

In the propositional setting, the notion of pseudo-expectation is the standard tool to obtain degree lower bounds for
\mathsf{SOS}
, see for instance
[
FKP19
]
. Thanks to the size-degree relation for
\mathsf{SOS}

[
AH19
]
, degree lower bounds also give size lower bounds.

Inspired by the propositional notion of pseudo-expectation, we give a notion of pseudo-expectation for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
and use it to prove lower bounds on the
\qdeg
of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations. In particular, for a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
expression
\pi
of the form

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1\ ,

(7)

where all the variables in
q_{u}
are on the left of
u
in
\mathcal{Q}
and
q
is a sum of squares, we consider
witnesses
that
\pi\neq 0
, i.e. that
\pi

is not
a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
. In analogy to the propositional case, we call the witnesses we construct

.

Definition 5.1

(Q-pseudo-expectation in
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
)
.

Given
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
expression
\pi
as in eq. (

), a

for
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and
\pi

such that:

1.

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[1]=1
;

2.

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[q+\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p]\geqslant 0
;

3.

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\sum_{u\in\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)]\geqslant 0
.

In the propositional context, a single pseudo-expectation for
\mathsf{SOS}
typically targets a fixed degree
d
and has properties similar to 1.–3. above but for arbitrary
q_{p}
and sum-of-squares
q
such that the degree of
q_{p}p
and
q
are at most
d
. In this way, a single pseudo-expectation rules out the possibility of
any
small-degree
\mathsf{SOS}
refutation. There are exceptions to this general approach, for instance the pseudo-expectations used in
[

]
that are targeting all
\mathsf{SOS}
proofs over a certain set of monomials,
but
we are not aware of degree lower bounds in
\mathsf{SOS}
proved by constructing a family of pseudo-expectations each tailored to a specific set of polynomials (i.e. as in
5.1
but without the condition in item 3.). In the QBF context, this is what we do. To rule out small
\qdeg

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutations we use a family of pseudo-expectations, each targeting
one
possible candidate
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
proof, i.e. an expression as in eq. (
7
). We formalise this approach in
5.2
and exemplify it in
5.3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Given a QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
, if for every
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
expression
\pi
as in eq. (

) with
\qdeg(\pi)<d

\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
has

at least

.

Proof.

If there was a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation
\pi=0
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
with
\qdeg(\pi)<d
, then taking the pseudo-expectation
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
for
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
and
\pi
we get
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\pi]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[0]=0
. Notice that necessarily
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[0]=0
, by linearity and the identity
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[0+0]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[0]

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\pi]\geqslant 1
.
∎

We apply the Q-pseudo-expectation technique to show a

\left.{g^{\prime}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is
-2
. That is, to prove eq. (
11
), it suffices to show that for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
s such that
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|
is odd,
\left.{g^{\prime}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\geqslant 0
.
By construction
\left.{g^{\prime}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\left.{g}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma},\ \boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and since there are no
t_{i}
variables in
g
,
\left.{g}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma},\ \boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\left.{g}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma},\ \boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{t}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
.

Now, if
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|
is odd, in particular
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma}\neq\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. It is easy to check that the assignment
\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma},\ \boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{t}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}
sets to
0

. Since

\left.{g}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma},\ \boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{t}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\geqslant 0

\left.{g^{\prime}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\geqslant 0

∎

6
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
as weighted proofs

In propositional proof complexity it has been shown that
\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{NS}
are equivalent to the systems
weighted Resolution

w-Res
and
restricted
weighted Resolution
restricted-w-Res

[
BBL24
]
. Unsurprisingly this characterization also adapts to the QBF context. For simplicity we only do it for
w-Res
and show the characterization for
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
as
Q-weighted Resolution
.

Definition 6.1

(Q-weighted Resolution)
.

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
be a false QBF. A
Q-weighted Resolution
(
Q-w-Res
) refutation of
\Phi
is a sequence of configurations
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{k}
, where each
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is a multi-set of weighted clauses,
i.e.
pairs of the form
(D,w)
where
D
is a clause and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
. We require that

•

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\emptyset,

•

(\bot,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
for some
w>0,

•

for each
(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{k},w\geqslant 0,

•

\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by the use of one of the following substitution rules:

Universal Reduction

\dfrac{(C\lor u,2w)}{(C,w)}
, where all the variables in
C
are on the left of
u
in the quantifier prefix
\mathcal{Q}
and
u
is a universal variable,

Axiom

\dfrac{\phantom{(C,w)}}{(C,w)}
, where
C
is a clause in
\varphi
,
w\in\mathbb{Z}
,

Symmetric Cut

\dfrac{(C\lor x,w)\quad(C\lor\neg x,w)}{(C,w)}
with
w\in\mathbb{Z}
,

Idempotency

\dfrac{(C\lor x\lor x,w)}{(C\lor x,w)}
with
w\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
\size
of a
Q-w-Res
refutation is the number of lines in the proof, and the Q-size is the number of universal reduction steps.

We say that two configurations are equivalent, if the total weight of each clause is the same in both. For example,
\{(C,w),(C,v)\}
is equivalent to
\{(C,w+v)\}
.
Additionally, every configuration is implicitly assumed to contain every clause with weight 0.
These two properties combined allows one to use every clause
(C,w)
for some rule, as long as
(C,-w)
is also added to the next configuration.
(In
6.1
, we implicitly allow that

\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is equivalent to some configuration obtained from another configuration equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by the use of one of the substitution rules.)

Note that every rule of
Q-w-Res
can also be used in the reverse direction. The “reverse” rules are not required to be defined as such, because this is equivalent to using the “forward” rules with negative weights.
For example, the reverse of the
symmetric cut
rule is removing
(C,w)
from the configuration and adding
(C\lor x,w)
and
(C\lor\neg x,w)
. This is the same as adding
(C,-w)
and removing
(C\lor x,-w)
and
(C\lor\neg x,-w)
due to the equivalence definition.

Recall that clause
C=\bigvee_{v\in P}v\lor\bigvee_{v\in N}\lnot v
is encoded as the set of polynomials

\enc(C)=\left\{\prod_{v\in P}\overline{v}\prod_{v\in N}v\right\}\cup\{v^{2}-v,\ v+\overline{v}-1:v\in P\cup N\}\ .

Let
M(C)=\prod_{v\in P}\overline{v}\prod_{v\in N}v
be the monomial associated to
C
in
\enc(C)
.

It is easy to see that multi-sets of weighted clauses and polynomials are just two ways of describing the same objects.
The configuration
\{(C_{1},a_{1}),...,(C_{n},a_{n})\}
corresponds to the polynomial
\sum_{i=1}^{n}-a_{i}\mathsf{M}(C_{i})
and the polynomial

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\prod_{j\in N_{i}}\overline{x_{j}}\prod_{j\in P_{i}}x_{j}

corresponds to the configuration
\{(\bigvee_{j\in N_{i}}x_{j}\lor\bigvee_{j\in P_{i}}\neg x_{j},-a_{i}):i\in\{1,...,n\}\}
. This isomorphism preserves equivalence
[
BBL24
]
.

Lemma 6.2

(
[
BBL24
]
)
.

Let configurations of weighted clauses
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be given.
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are equivalent if and only if

\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}w\mathsf{M}(C)=\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{2}}w\mathsf{M}(C).

Proposition 6.3
.

Q-w-Res
is polynomially equivalent to
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
in both the
\size
and
\qsize
measure.

Proof.

This proof is completely analogous to the equivalence in the propositional case between
\mathsf{SA}
and
\mathsf{w}
-
Res

[
BBL24
]
. It suffices to show that the method can be extended for the respective reduction rules.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
p-simulates
Q-w-Res

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{t}
be a
Q-w-Res
refutation of some false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}
.
We construct algebraic expressions
S_{1},...,S_{t}
having the form of a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
derivation, with the property that
S_{i}=-\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{i}}w\cdot\mathsf{M}(C)
. That is, each
S_{i}
is constructed as a sum of (i) multiples of the polynomials in
\enc(C_{j})
for some
j
(recall, this encoding includes the Boolean and the twin variable axioms), and (ii) multiples of
(1-2u)
for some universal variable
u
, where the multiplier polynomials depend only on variables quantified left of
u
.

Let
S_{1}=0
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\emptyset
this fulfils our requirement.

Now suppose we have constructed an algebraic expression
S_{i}
that equals
-\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{i}}w\cdot\mathsf{M}(C)
. We want to construct
S_{i+1}
.
We differentiate between the rules used to obtain
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
. Note that only the first case is new to the QBF variant, all the other cases are completely analogous to the propositional case. We write them anyway for completeness.

•

If
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by a universal reduction rule
\frac{(C\lor u,2w)}{(C,w)}
, then add to
S_{i}
the terms

2w\mathsf{M}(C\lor u)-w\mathsf{M}(C)=-w\mathsf{M}(C)(1-2u).

That is
S_{i+1}=S_{i}-w\mathsf{M}(C)(1-2u)
.

•

If
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by an axiom rule
\frac{}{(C,w)}
, then add to
S_{i}
the term
-w\mathsf{M}(C)
.
That is
S_{i+1}=S_{i}-w\mathsf{M}(C)
.

•

If
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by an idempotency rule
\frac{(C\lor x\lor x)}{(C\lor x,w)}
, then add to
S_{i}
the terms

w\mathsf{M}(C\lor x\lor x)-w\mathsf{M}(C\lor x)=w\mathsf{M}(C)(x^{2}-x).

That is
S_{i+1}=S_{i}+w\mathsf{M}(C)(x^{2}-x)
.

•

If
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i}
by a symmetric cut rule
\frac{(C\lor x,w)\hskip 8.19447pt(C\lor\neg x,w)}{(C,w)}
, then add to
S_{i}
the terms

-w\mathsf{M}(C)+w\mathsf{M}(C\lor x)+w\mathsf{M}(C\lor\neg x)=w\mathsf{M}(C)(x+\overline{x}-1).

That is
S_{i+1}=S_{i}+w\mathsf{M}(C)(x+\overline{x}-1)
.

One can easily verify, that
S_{i+1}
constructed as above, fulfils

S_{i+1}=-\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{i}}w\mathsf{M}(C)

and in particular
S_{t}=-\sum_{(C,w)\in\mathcal{L}_{t}}w\mathsf{M}(C)
.
Since
\mathcal{L}_{t}
contains only an empty clause
(\bot,c)
for some
c>0
and clauses with positive weights,
S_{t}=-c-q
for some polynomial
q
having only positive coefficients.
Therefore
\frac{S_{t}}{c}+\frac{q}{c}+1=0
is a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
refutation, and its size is no more than the size of the
Q-w-Res
refutation.

The only times a monomial added to
S_{i}
contributes to
\qsize
is in the first case, if
\mathcal{L}_{i+1}
is obtained by the universal reduction rule.
As a result, the
\qsize
of the resulting
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
proof is exactly the number of reduction steps, i.e. the
\qsize
of the
Q-w-Res
proof.

Q-w-Res
p-simulates
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}

Let

\sum_{p\in\enc(\varphi)}q_{p}p+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0

(12)

be a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
refutation of a false QBF
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
. We split
\enc(\varphi)
into the encoding of the clauses of
\varphi

P,
the Boolean axioms
B
and the twin variable axioms
T
, such that (
12
) is equivalent to

\sum_{p\in P}q_{p}p+\sum_{b\in B}q_{b}b+\sum_{t\in T}q_{t}t+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)+q+1=0.

(13)

Without loss of generality,
q_{p}
can be non-positive scalars for all
p\in P
(cf. Lemma 5.4 in
[
BBL24
]
; the universal terms are untouched).
Therefore, we can rewrite (
13
) as

-1-q=\sum_{p\in P}-a_{p}p+\sum_{b\in B}q_{b}b+\sum_{t\in T}q_{t}t+\sum_{u\in\mathrm{vars}_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)

(14)

for scalars
a_{p}\geqslant 0
.

We construct a
Q-w-Res
proof
(\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{t})
of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
.
This is achieved by going through the right-hand side of (
14
) term by term and constructing sets of weighted clauses corresponding to these partial sums.
Additionally, each set of weighted clauses can be constructed by the previous set and a
Q-w-Res
rule.

We start with the empty sum and set
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\emptyset
. There is nothing to show.

Every clause
(C_{i},a_{i})
corresponds to the term
-a_{i}\mathsf{M}(C_{i})
.
Therefore, we can construct a sequence of configurations
\mathcal{L}_{2},...,\mathcal{L}_{k_{1}}
, with each

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\{(C_{1},a_{1}),...,(C_{i-1},a_{i-1})\}

corresponding to the
i-1
-th partial sum of
\sum_{p\in P}-a_{p}p
.
In this sequence, each configuration is obtained with the previous one and the axiom rule.

Continuing, for every binomial
am(x^{2}-x)
from the sum
\sum_{b\in B}q_{b}b
construct

\mathcal{L}_{k_{2}}=\mathcal{L}_{k_{2}-1}\cup\{(C\lor\neg x\lor\neg x,-a),(C\lor\neg x,a)\}

where
C
is the unique clause such that
\mathsf{M}(C)=m
. This is an application of the idempotency rule.
Analogously, every term
am(x_{i}+\overline{x}_{i}-1)
from the sum
\sum_{t\in T}q_{t}t
constructs

\mathcal{L}_{k_{3}}=\mathcal{L}_{k_{3}-1}\cup\{(C\lor\neg x,-a),(C\lor x,-a),(C,a)\},

with
C
again being the unique clause such that
\mathsf{M}(C)=m
. This is an application of the symmetric cut rule.
This construction is extended to the terms
am(1-2u)
from
\sum_{u\in\vars_{\forall}(\mathcal{Q})}q_{u}(1-2u)
.
Here

\mathcal{L}_{k_{4}}=\mathcal{L}_{k_{4}-1}\cup\{(C\lor\neg u,2a),(C,-a)\}.

This corresponds to the universal reduction rule. Due to these terms being obtained from a
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
refutation, the syntactic restrictions for the reduction rule, i.e.
C
only containing variables left of
u
and
u
being universal, are met.

Equation (
14
) shows that this resulting configuration is equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\{(\bot,1)\}\cup\{(C_{i},a_{i}):-q=\sum_{i}-a_{i}\mathsf{M}(C_{i})\}
for clauses
C_{i}
and non-negative scalars
a_{i}
.
This representation of

is possible due to the definition of
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
.
As such,
(\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{t})
is a
Q-w-Res
refutation of
\mathcal{Q}.\varphi
with rational weights, and a suitable multiple gives an integral version matching
6.1
.
For every monomial in
q_{u}
, exactly one universal reduction is added. As such, the Q-sizes match.
∎

7
Simulations

We now investigate how the algebraic QBF systems relate to each other and to other known QBF proof systems such as QU-Resolution and Q-PC and show the p-simulations of
Figure

1
.

Theorem 7.1
.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
p-simulates
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
w.r.t. the
\size
and
\qsize
measures.

Proof.

It is well known that (degree
2d
)
\mathsf{SOS}
p-simulates (degree
d
)
\mathsf{SA}
(see for instance
[
FKP19
, Lemma 3.63]
). The same argument works without change in the QBF setting: every variable
v
in a positive monomial can be substituted by
v^{2}
summing a suitable multiple of
v^{2}-v
. In this way, every positive monomial
\frac{a}{b}m
with
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
can be converted into
\frac{s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{3}^{2}+s_{4}^{2}}{b^{2}}m^{2}
where
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4}
are four integers that sum up to
ab
(they exists by Lagrange’s Four Squares Theorem). This converts
\frac{a}{b}m
into a sum of at most four squares with rational coefficients.
∎

In the argument above, notice that the only increment in degree is in the propositional part, hence, different from the propositional case,
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
with
\qdeg

d
p-simulates
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
with
\qdeg

d
. In the
\qsize
measure, the converse also holds.

Theorem 7.2
.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
p-simulates
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
w.r.t. the
\qsize
measure.

Proof.

Every polynomial
q
that is non-negative on the Boolean assignments can be written as a (possibly exponentially large) sum of the form
\sum_{\alpha}\left.{q}\right|_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{v})
, where
\chi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{v})
is a monomial that evaluates to
1
when the variables are set according to the Boolean assignment
\alpha
and on any other Boolean assignment it is
0
. In other words, every
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}
refutation can be written as a possibly exponentially larger
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
refutation. The exponential blow-up appears in the propositional part which is not accounted for in
\qsize
.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
p-simulates
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
w.r.t. both
\size
and
\qsize
.

Proof.

Due to
6.3
, to show that
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SA}
p-simulates
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
it is enough to show that
Q-w-Res
p-simulates
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
. This p-simulation is an adaptation of the proof that
\mathsf{w}
-
Res
p-simulates
Res

[
BBL24
]
.
This proof is done in two steps. First, we show that every resolution step in a
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutation can be transformed into a sequence of
weakening
and
symmetric cut
rules.
Second, we then add appropriate weights to the clauses to transform it into a
Q-w-Res
refutation.

Let

\frac{(C\lor D\lor x)\quad(C\lor E\lor\neg x)}{(C\lor D\lor E)}

be an arbitrary application of the resolution rule in a
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutation, where
C,D,E
are clauses with pairwise disjoint variables and none containing
x
or
\neg x
. Let
D
consist of literals
d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{k}
and
E
of literals
e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{\ell}
.

Using
\ell
consecutive

rules, we expand
(C\lor D\lor x)
to
(C\lor D\lor E\lor x)
and
(C\lor E\lor\neg x)
to
(C\lor D\lor E\lor\neg x)
.
These two clauses can then be used in a single
symmetric cut
to gain
(C\lor D\lor E)
.

For the second step, we need to add weights to the clauses. Since
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
proofs in general are not treelike, we need to chose the weights in a way that keeps all previous clauses in the configuration.

Let
\pi=C_{1},...,C_{t}
be an arbitrary
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
refutation of a false QBF, using symmetric cut instead of resolution.
We inductively construct a sequence of configurations
\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{t}
, such that these are a valid
Q-w-Res
proof and for every
i\leqslant t
there exist strictly positive weights
w_{1}^{i},...,w_{i}^{i}
with
\{(C_{j},w_{j}^{i}),j\in\{1,...,i\}\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{i}
. Additionally, all the weights in every
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are non-negative.

We differentiate between the rules used to obtain
C_{i}
.

•

C_{i}
is an axiom. Then
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{i-1}\cup\{(C_{i},1)\}
and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
using the axiom rule.

•

C_{i}
is obtained from
C_{j}
using the universal reduction rule, i.e.
C_{j}=C_{i}\lor u
for some universal literal
u
. Let
w_{j}
be the weight such that
(C_{j},w_{j})\in\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
. Then

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{i-1}\cup\{(C_{j},-\frac{w_{j}}{2})\}\cup\{(C_{i},\frac{w_{j}}{4})\}

and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
using the universal reduction rule.

•

C_{i}
is obtained from
C_{j}
using the weakening rule, i.e.
C_{i}=C_{j}\lor v
for some literal
v
. Let
w_{j}
be the weight such that
(C_{j},w_{j})\in\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
. Then

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{i-1}\cup\{(C_{j},-\frac{w_{j}}{2})\}\cup\{(C_{i},\frac{w_{j}}{2}),(C_{j}\lor\neg v,\frac{w_{j}}{2})\}

and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
using the symmetric rule.

•

C_{i}
is obtained from
C_{j}
and
C_{k}
using the symmetric cut rule, i.e.
C_{j}=C_{i}\lor v
and
C_{k}=C_{i}\lor\neg v
for some variable
v
. Let
w_{j}
and
w_{k}
be the weights such that
(C_{j},w_{j}),(C_{k},w_{k})\in\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
and define

. Then

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{i-1}\cup\{(C_{j},-\frac{w}{2}),(C_{k},-\frac{w}{2})\}\cup\{(C_{i},\frac{w}{2})\}

and
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
using the symmetric cut rule.

The non-negativity of all weights follow directly.
Since
\pi
is a
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}
proof,
C_{t}=\bot
. As such, there exists some weight
w_{t}>0
with
(\bot,w_{t})\in\mathcal{L}_{t}
.
Thus
\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{t}
is a valid
Q-w-Res
refutation with rational weights, and a suitable multiple gives an integral version matching
6.1
.

In total, for every resolution rule of
\pi
we add at most
n
lines to the
Q-w-Res
proof and for every reduction rule we add exactly one line (where
n
is the number of variables). The resulting proof has size at most
n|\pi|
, and its Q-size is
\qsize(\pi)
.
∎

.

Theorem 7.5
.

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{SOS}

\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{PC}

8
Conclusion

In this work we defined semi-algebraic proof systems for QBF and initiated their proof complexity investigation. While our results already reveal an interesting picture in terms of simulations and lower and upper bounds, a number of questions remain that appear to be of interest for further research.

In the propositional setting
Res
and
\mathsf{NS}
are incomparable proof systems. Are also
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}
and
\mathsf{QU}\text{-}\textsf{Res}

In
section

4

using polynomial threshold functions. Although this suffices for lower bounds, it appears interesting to determine the correct computational model

and
\mathsf{Q}\text{-}\mathsf{NS}

]
.

Finally, it would be interesting to determine the relationship of our new semi-algebraic QBF systems to the static expansion-based algebraic systems suggested in

]
, which might turn out to be incomparable in strength.

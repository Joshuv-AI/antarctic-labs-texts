Symmetry and Approximate Symmetry for Solutions of Mixed Local-Nonlocal Singular Equations
Sanjit Biswas

Abstract
In this article, we establish radial symmetry for positive weak solutions of a class of mixed local-nonlocal equations with possibly singular nonlinearity via the moving plane method. Furthermore, we provide a quantitative version of Gidas-Ni-Nirenberg type theorem for mixed local-nonlocal equations. To this regard, we establish a weak Harnack-type inequality and an analogue of the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci inequality in the mixed nonhomogeneous setting with a lower order term, which appear to be new. To the best of our knowledge, this paper initiates the study of the quantitative properties of solutions to mixed problems.



Keywords: Mixed local-nonlocal problems, singular nonlinearity, symmetry, approximate symmetry, moving plane method.

1
Introduction and main results

This article is devoted to the study of symmetry and approximate symmetry of weak solutions to a class of semilinear equations of the form:

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=f(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma\text{ and }u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
-type boundary and
u
is singular on the closed set
\Gamma\subset\Omega
. Here,
\Delta
denotes the usual Laplacian operator and the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is known as the fractional Laplacian and defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy,

where
s\in(0,1)
and P.V. stands for the principal value integral. Note that some definitions of the fractional Laplacian include a normalization constant, but for simplicity, we omit it here. The equations involving mixed local and nonlocal operators have numerous applications in stochastic processes, image processing, biology, etc.; for more details, we refer to
[
30
]
and the references therein. The study of mixed operators has been a central focus of many researchers; for instance, see
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
26
,
29
,
34
,
44
]
and the references therein. In this article, we plan to establish several quantitative and qualitative properties of weak solutions to equation (
1.1
). To this concern, we assume the following conditions on the continuous function
f:\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, which are as follows:

(
\mathscr{F}_{1}
)

For
0<t\leq s\leq b<\infty
and for every
\omega\Subset\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma
, there exists
\mathscr{K}_{1}=\mathscr{K}_{1}(b,\omega)>0
such that

f(x,s)-f(x,t)\leq\mathscr{K}_{1}(s-t)\text{ for all }x\in\omega.

(
\mathscr{F}_{2}
)

For every
I\Subset(0,\infty)
, and
\omega\Subset\Omega\setminus\Gamma
, there exists
\mathscr{K}_{2}=\mathscr{K}_{2}(I,\omega)>0
such that

|f(x,s)-f(x,t)|\leq\mathscr{K}_{2}|s-t|\text{ for all }s,t\in I,\;x\in\omega.

The following singular equation is one of the prototypes of equation (
1.1
) with
\Gamma=\phi
:

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\epsilon(-\Delta)^{s}u=\gamma u^{-\delta}+h(u)\text{ in }\Omega,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.2)

where
\delta>0,\epsilon>0,\gamma>0
and
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous. Here, the positivity of
\delta
leads to a blow-up of the nonlinearity near the origin, a phenomenon referred to as singularity. When
h
satisfies the subcritical or critical growth conditions, the existence of multiple solutions to equation (
1.2
) was addressed in
[
7
,
10
,
35
]
, while purely local and purely nonlocal cases were studied in
[
1
,
39
]
and
[
42
]
.

The symmetry and monotonicity of solutions to purely local equations have been widely studied in the literature. In the seminal work of Gidas, Ni, and Nirenberg
[
38
]
, it was shown that every classical solution
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
to the purely local equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u=h(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega

(1.3)

is radially symmetric and monotonically decreasing in the radial direction, provided that
\Omega
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
h:\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is of type
C^{1}
. For the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the symmetry and monotonicity of solutions to a class of semilinear equations were obtained in
[
13
,
18
]
. The Gidas-Ni-Nirenberg type results for quasilinear equations can be found in
[
24
,
25
]
, while purely local and mixed local-nonlocal cases were addressed by Chen et al.
[
19
]
and Valdinoci et al.
[
6
]
, respectively.

Very recently, a quantitative version of the Gidas-Ni-Nirenberg theorem was obtained by Cozzi et al. in
[
21
]
. More precisely, the authors quantitatively measure the deviation of classical solutions to the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u=k(x)g(u)\text{ in }\Omega,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.4)

from radial function, where
\Omega
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function, and the function
k:\overline{\Omega}\to(0,\infty)
belongs to
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Furthermore, in
[
20
]
, the authors established several quantitative estimates for classical solutions to equation (
1.4
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
using the moving plane technique, while the quasilinear analogues of these results were addressed in
[
37
]
. More qualitative results can be found in
[
22
,
28
]
.

The radial symmetry and monotonicity properties of solutions to equations involving singular nonlinearities of the form

\displaystyle-\Delta u=u^{-\delta}+h(u)\text{ in }\Omega,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.5)

have also been extensively studied, where
\delta>0
and
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function. The presence of the singular source term
u^{-\delta}
causes the nonlinearity to lose local Lipschitz continuity near zero, and as a result, classical symmetry results are no longer directly applicable to solutions of (
1.5
). For the existence and uniqueness, we refer the reader to
[
11
,
17
,
23
,
40
]
and the references therein. In
[
14
,
16
]
, Sciunzi et al. established the Gidas–Ni–Nirenberg type results for solutions of (
1.5
) using the moving plane technique. A similar result for the purely nonlocal equation was investigated by Giacomoni et al. in
[
3
]
.

In the mixed local–nonlocal setting, Garain and Anthal in
[
2
]
studied the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{-\delta}+h(u)\text{ in }\Omega,u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(1.6)

and showed the radial symmetry of weak solutions to the equation under the assumptions that
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is locally Lipschitz, non-decreasing and
h(u)>0
when
u>0
. Their approach relies on a decomposition technique combined with the moving plane method. In this article, we provide an alternative proof of their result that accommodates a broader class of singular nonlinearities. Our first theorem can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Symmetry)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth domain that is convex with respect to the
x_{1}
-direction and symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=0\}
. Assume that
\Gamma\subset\{x\in\Omega:x_{1}=0\}
is a closed set such that
\Gamma
is a point when
n=2
and
\mathrm{cap}_{2}(\Gamma)=0
when
n\geq 3
. Furthermore, we assume that
f:\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function which satisfies the hypotheses
(\mathscr{F}_{1}),(\mathscr{F}_{2})
, and

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},u)\leq f(y_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},u)\text{ for all }0<y_{1}<x_{1},

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},u)=f(-x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},u)\text{ for every }x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\in\Omega.

(1.8)

Suppose that
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)\cap C(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.4
. Then
u
is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=0\}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The function
f
is allowed to take negative values, and the map
s\to f(x,s)
is not assumed to be monotonically decreasing—assumptions that played a central role in
[
2
, Theorem 2.14]
. Our result therefore extends
[
2
, Theorem 2.14]
.

To establish this result, we mainly use the moving plane technique (see
[
6
]
). However, it is worth emphasizing that the solutions belong to the local Sobolev space, and hence the moving plane technique can not be applied directly. To overcome this, we adopt the methodologies introduced in
[
31
,
41
]
. Specifically, to start the moving plane procedure, we show that a suitably constructed auxiliary function belongs to the Sobolev space (see Lemma
3.1
), which requires several careful estimates. Finally, using the moving plane technique, we obtain our desired result.

This theorem leads us to the following important corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a ball and
f:\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that satisfies hypotheses
(\mathscr{F}_{1}),(\mathscr{F}_{2})
. Assume further that
f(x,u)=f(|x|,u)
for all
x\in\Omega
and
f
is radially non-increasing. Suppose that
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
is a weak solution to the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=f(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.9)

in the sense of Definition
2.4
. Then
u
is radially symmetric in
\Omega
.

The following corollary ensures the radial symmetry of solutions to variable exponent singular problems, which is itself a new result.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball and
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function. Suppose
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
is such that
0<u(x)\leq 1
and weakly satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=\frac{1}{u^{\delta(x)}}+g(u)\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.10)

where
\delta:\overline{\Omega}\to(0,\infty)
is radial and non-increasing in the radial direction. Then
u
is radially symmetric in
\Omega
.

Our second main theorem provides a quantitative version of Corollary
1.3
and it can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Approximate Symmetry)
Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unit ball. Suppose
f:\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function of the form
f(x,u)=k(x)g(u)
, where
k\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};(0,\infty))
and
g\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);[0,\infty))
. Assume that
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is a weak solution to (
1.9
) such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{0}}(1-|x|)\leq u(x)\leq C_{0}\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.11)

for some
C_{0}\geq 1.
Then there exist positive constants
C>0
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, depending only on
n,\;s,\;C_{0},\;\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]},

\|k\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}
such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\mathrm{def}(k)^{\gamma},\text{ for all }x,y\in\Omega\text{ such that }|x|=|y|.

Here
\mathrm{def}(k)=\|\nabla^{T}k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\partial^{+}_{r}k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\nabla^{T}=\nabla-\frac{x}{|x|}\partial_{r}
and
\partial_{r}=\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla
.

Remark 1.6
.

If the function
k
is radial and radially decreasing, then
\mathrm{def}(k)=0
. Consequently, this estimate is consistent with the conclusion of Corollary
1.3
.

In order to prove this theorem, we adopt the ideas developed in
[
20
,
21
,
37
]
. However, the absence of a weak Harnack inequality and an analogue of Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci-type estimate for the mixed local-nonlocal nonhomogeneous equations with lower order terms prevent us to apply their method directly. Inspired by
[
7
,
36
,
34
]
, we establish a weak Harnack-type inequality (Proposition
2.7
) for this setting, which is based on the Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory. An iteration technique is used to obtain an ABP-type inequality, which is reminiscent of
[
37
]
. Subsequently, using the moving plane technique, we conclude our result.

This article is organized in the following way. In Section 2, we present all the functional spaces and several useful results. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of several preliminary results for Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Sections 4 and 5, we provide a detailed proof of our main results.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Functional analytical setting

The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)
is defined by

H^{1}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\nabla u=\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}\Big)
. We say
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
. The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,2}(\Omega)
for
0<s<1
, is defined by

W^{s,2}(\Omega)=\Bigg\{{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}:\,}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)\Bigg\}

under the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We refer to
[
27
]
and the references therein for more details on fractional Sobolev spaces. Due to the mixed behavior of our equations, we consider the space

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}

under the norm

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following Lemma guaranties that the norm
\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
defined above is equivalent to the norm
\|u\|=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
12
, Lemma
2.1
]
)
For
0<s<1
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Omega)>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(2.1)

for every
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

For subsequent Sobolev embedding, refer, for instance, to
[
33
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The embedding operators

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\begin{cases}L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{ for }t\in[1,2^{*}],\text{ if }n>2,\\
L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{ for }t\in[1,\infty),\text{ if }n=2\end{cases}

are continuous. Moreover, they are compact except for
t=2^{*}
if
n>2
. Here
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
if
n>2
.

Definition 2.3
.

(Capacity measure)
For a compact set
K\subset\Omega
, the
2
-capacity of
K
is denoted by
\mathrm{cap_{\text{$2$}}(K)}
and defined as

\mathrm{cap\text{${}_{2}$}(K)}:=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx:\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\phi\geq\chi_{K}\right\},

where

\chi_{K}(x):=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }x\in K,\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Finally,
2
-capacity of any subset
B
of
\Omega
is defined by the standard way. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
32
]
. Now, we define the notion of solutions.

Definition 2.4
.

(Weak solution)
A function
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a weak solution to equation (
1.1
) if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\phi\;dx,

for every
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
.

For
0<s<1
, the tail space
\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{n+2s}}\;dy<\infty\}.

Moreover, the Tail of a function
u
with respect to the ball
B_{r}(x_{1})
is denoted by
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{1},r)
and defined as

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{1},r):=r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{B_{r}(x_{1})}}\frac{|u(y)|}{|x_{1}-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy}.

Definition 2.5
.

(Supersolution)
A function
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a supersolution (subsolution) to

-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+c(x)u=g\text{ in }\Omega

if the following inequality holds:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx+\int_{\Omega}cu\phi\geq(\text{ or }\leq)\int_{\Omega}g\phi\;dx,

for every
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.6
.

It is noteworthy that the above definitions are well-defined due to the fact
u\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.2
Weak Harnack estimate

This section is devoted to the establishment of a weak Harnack-type inequality, which is crucial for our argument. This result slightly extends
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
, whose proof relies heavily on the proof of that theorem. To avoid repetitions, we just highlight the main changes here. For the homogeneous case, this inequality has already been obtained in
[
7
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a weak supersolution to the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+c(x)u=g\text{ in }\Omega,

(2.2)

where
0\leq c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{q}(\Omega)
with
q>\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Then for every
0<r\leq 1
with
r<\frac{R}{2}
, there exist two constants
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,s,q,\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})>0
and
C=C(n,s,q,\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})
such that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{0})}|u|^{\epsilon}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{0})}u+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\mathrm{Tail}(u^{-};x_{0},R)+r^{2-n}R^{n(1-\frac{1}{q})}||g\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right).

(2.3)

Before proceeding to the proof of this proposition, we prove several auxiliary results analogous to
[
36
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
36
, Lemma 3.2]
, and
[
36
, Lemma 4.1]
. Since our arguments follow similar lines, we omit the full proofs and only highlight the effect of the lower-order term.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{L}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a supersolution of (
2.2
) such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Suppose that
0\leq\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for some
0<r\leq 1
with
r<R
, and
d>0\;,\delta>0
are two fixed real numbers. Then, for any
\eta>1
, there exists
C=C(\eta,\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\psi^{2}|\nabla v^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{2}\Big[\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}v^{-\alpha}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\;dx+\|\psi v^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{(v^{-\alpha}(x)+v^{-\alpha}(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dx\;dy

\displaystyle+\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}(\psi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}

\displaystyle+d^{-1}\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}(\psi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\Big)\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}v^{-\alpha}\psi^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle+d^{-1}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\Big(\delta\|\psi v^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+\delta^{\frac{-n}{q-n}}\|\psi v^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\Big)\Big],

(2.4)

where
v=u+d
and
\alpha=\eta-1>0
.

Proof.

Since
c\geq 0
,
v
is also a super-solution of equation (
2.2
). To prove the energy estimate, we incorporate
\phi=-\psi^{2}v^{-\eta}
in the weak formulation of (
2.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle I+J+L\leq K,

(2.5)

where

\displaystyle I=\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(-\psi^{2}v^{-\eta})\;dx,

\displaystyle J=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(v(x)-v(y))(\psi^{2}v^{-\eta}(y)-\psi^{2}v^{-\eta}(x))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx,

\displaystyle L=-\int_{\Omega}c(x)\psi^{2}v^{1-\eta}\;dx,

\displaystyle K=-\int_{\Omega}f\psi^{2}v^{-\eta}\;dx.

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{((u_{\lambda^{*}}-u)(x)-(u_{\lambda^{*}}-u)(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx

\displaystyle\geq-M(r,\eta)\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u_{\lambda^{*}}-u))v\;dx,

which implies that
\tilde{w}=u_{\lambda^{*}}-u
is a super-solution of the equation

-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u+Mu=0\text{ in }B_{r}(x_{0}).

Thus,
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
ensures
u=u_{\lambda^{*}}
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
. Since
\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}\setminus R_{\lambda^{*}}\Gamma
is connected, by the usual covering argument, one has
u=u_{\lambda^{*}}
in
\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}\setminus R_{\lambda^{*}}\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,

u<u_{\lambda^{*}}\text{ in }\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}\setminus R_{\lambda^{*}}\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}.

Assume
K\Subset\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}\setminus R_{\lambda^{*}}\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}
(to be determined later). There exists
\eta_{1}>0
such that

u_{\lambda^{*}}-u\geq 2\eta_{1}>0\text{ in }K.

By continuity, there exists
0<\lambda^{**}<\lambda^{*}
such that for every
\mu\in(\lambda^{**},\lambda^{*})
we have

u_{\mu}-u>\eta_{1}>0\text{ in }K.

Incorporating
\phi_{\lambda^{**}}=w_{\lambda^{**}}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}
in the weak formulation of (
1.1
) and along the lines of proof of (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\;dx+\int_{R_{\lambda}\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{-}|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\;dx+2I

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\lambda^{**}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}}}|w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\;dx+32\big(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}\;dx+\int_{R_{\lambda}\Omega_{\lambda}}|(u-u_{\lambda})^{-}|^{2}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle+\int_{R_{\lambda}\Omega_{\lambda}}|(u-u_{\lambda})^{-}|^{2}|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}\;dx\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda^{**}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}}}|w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\;dx+\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\Big),

(4.6)

where the constant
C
depends on
f,\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\lambda})}
and

I(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{((u-u_{\lambda^{**}})(x)-(u-u_{\lambda^{**}})(y))(\phi_{\lambda^{**}}(x)-\phi_{\lambda^{**}}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx.

Along the lines of proof of the fact
3.11
, we have
I(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\geq o(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
. This, together with Fatou’s lemma, leads us to conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{(\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}\Omega_{\lambda^{**}})\setminus(K\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}K)}|\nabla w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{(\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}\Omega_{\lambda^{**}})\setminus(K\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}K)}|w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\;dx.

Due to Remark
3.2
, Poincaré inequality leads to conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{(\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}\Omega_{\lambda^{**}})\setminus(K\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}K)}|\nabla w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\,dx\leq C|\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\setminus K|^{\frac{2}{n}}\int_{(\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}\Omega_{\lambda^{**}})\setminus(K\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}K)}|\nabla w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\;dx.

We choose
K\Subset\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}\setminus R_{\lambda^{*}}\Omega_{\lambda^{*}}
and
\lambda^{**}<\lambda^{*}
such that

C|\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\setminus K|^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Using this fact, the above inequality implies

\int_{(\Omega_{\lambda^{**}}\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}\Omega_{\lambda^{**}})\setminus(K\cup R_{\lambda^{**}}K)}|\nabla w_{\lambda^{**}}|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

Therefore,
w_{\mu}=0
in
\Omega_{\mu}\setminus K
and hence,
u\leq u_{\mu}
in
\Omega_{\mu}\setminus R_{\mu}\Gamma
for every
\mu\in(\lambda^{**},\lambda^{*})
. This contradicts the definition of
\lambda^{*}
. Hence,
\lambda^{*}=0
.

Step-III:
In this final step, we show

for all

. Combining these two facts, we get our desired result.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:
Suppose that
\Omega
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is a weak solution of equation (
2.16
). Due to
[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
, there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
u\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
. Furthermore, there exists
C_{1}>0
, depending only on
n,s,C_{0}
and
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,C_{0}])}
such that

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[Du]_{C^{0,\beta}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}.

(5.1)

It is enough to prove that for every unit vector
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the following hold

|u(x)-u(x_{\nu})|\leq\mathscr{C}\mathrm{def}(k)^{\gamma}\text{ for all }x\in\Omega\text{ with }x\cdot\nu>0,

for some constants
\mathscr{C}>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, depending only on
n,s,C_{0},\|k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]}
. Here
x_{\nu}=x-2(x\cdot\nu)\nu
. We may assume
\mathrm{def}(k)
is arbitrarily small; otherwise, the above claim is trivially satisfied. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\nu=(1,0,...,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and aim to show

|u(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})-u(-x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})|\leq\mathscr{C}\mathrm{def}(k)^{\gamma},\text{ for all }x\in\Omega\text{ with }x_{1}>0.

In this regard, we define

\Lambda:=\{\lambda>0:\|(u-u_{\mu})^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathrm{def}(k),\text{ for all }\mu\in(\lambda,1)\},

where
\mathcal{C}=\max\{\mathcal{K},\tilde{C}\}
. Here, the constants
\mathcal{K}
and
\tilde{C}
are obtained in Lemma
2.13
and in inequality
5.3
, respectively.

Step-I:
Here, we show that the set
\Lambda
is nonempty. To this end, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{\mu}+(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\mu}=k_{\mu}(x)g(u_{\mu})\text{ in }\Sigma_{\mu},

where
k_{\mu}(x)=k(x_{\mu})
. Thus,
w_{\mu}=u-u_{\mu}
satisfies the following equation in the weak sense:

\displaystyle-\Delta w_{\mu}+(-\Delta)^{s}w_{\mu}+c(x)w_{\mu}=(k-k_{\mu})g(u_{\mu})\text{ in }\Sigma_{\mu},

where

c(x):=\begin{cases}-k(x)\frac{g(u)-g(u_{\mu})}{u-u_{\mu}}&\text{ if }u\neq u_{\mu},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
k
is bounded and
g
is Lipschitz continuous in
[0,C_{0}]
, we have
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Choosing
\lambda_{0}>0
(close to 1) such that
|\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}|\leq\delta
, where
\delta
is obtained in Lemma
2.13
. Due to Lemma
2.13
, for every
\lambda_{0}<\mu<1
we deduce

\displaystyle\|(u-u_{\mu})^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})}\leq\mathcal{K}\|(k-k_{\mu})^{+}g(u_{\mu})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})}.

(5.2)

Since
g\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle(k-k_{\mu})^{+}(x)g(u_{\mu})

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]}\big(k(x)-k(x_{\mu}^{*})+k(x_{\mu}^{*})-k(x_{\mu})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]}\big(\|\nabla^{T}k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\frac{\partial^{+}k}{\partial r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big),

where
x_{\mu}^{*}:=\frac{|x_{\mu}|}{|x|}x
.
Therefore, (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|(u-u_{\mu})^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})}\leq\tilde{C}\mathrm{def}(k),

(5.3)

where
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(n,\|k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]})>0
.
Consequently,
[\lambda_{0},1)\subset\Lambda
, and hence
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.

Step-II:
We shall show that

\lambda^{*}:=\inf\Lambda\leq C\mathrm{def}(k)^{\gamma},\text{ for some }\gamma=\gamma(n,s,\|k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{C^{0,1}[0,C_{0}]})>0.

For this concern, we first prove that
\lambda_{*}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. We proceed by contraction. If possible, let
\frac{1}{4}<\lambda_{*}\leq\lambda_{1}<1
.
Due to the continuity of the solution, we have
u-u_{\lambda_{*}}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathrm{def}(k)
in
\Sigma_{\lambda^{*}}
. Therefore,
v_{\lambda_{*}}:=u_{\lambda_{*}}-u+\mathcal{C}\mathrm{def}(k)
is non-negative in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}}
, and satisfies the following equation in the weak sense:

\displaystyle-\Delta v+(-\Delta)v+c^{+}v\geq-(k_{\lambda_{*}}-k)^{+}g(u_{\lambda_{*}})-\mathcal{C}c^{-}(x)\mathrm{def}(k)\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}}.

Since
p_{0}=(1-2\eta,0,0,...,0)\in\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}
, due to Lemma
5.1
, for
0<\eta<\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{6}
we have

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B_{\frac{\eta}{2}(p_{0})}}v_{\lambda_{*}}^{\epsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}\eta^{-\alpha}\Big(\inf_{\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}}v_{\lambda_{*}}+\|(k_{\lambda_{*}}-k)^{+}g(u_{\lambda_{*}})+\mathcal{C}c^{-}(x)\mathrm{def}(k)\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-\alpha}\Big(\inf_{\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}}(u_{\lambda_{*}}-u)+\mathrm{def}(k)\Big),

(5.4)

where
C=C(n,s,\|g\|_{C^{0,1}([0,C_{0}])},\|k\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})>0
is a constant and
\epsilon>0
is obtained in Lemma
5.1
. Now, using (
5.1
) and (
1.11

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B_{\frac{\eta}{2}(p_{0})}}v_{\lambda_{*}}^{\epsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\geq\inf_{B_{\frac{\eta}{2}(p_{0})}}v_{\lambda_{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{B_{\frac{\eta}{2}(p_{0})}}(u_{\lambda_{*}}-u)

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in B_{\frac{\eta}{2}(p_{0})}}(u(x_{\lambda_{*}})-(u(x)-u(\tilde{x})))

\displaystyle\geq C_{3}(1-|x_{\lambda_{*}}|)-\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}|x-\tilde{x}|

\displaystyle\geq C_{3}(1-|x_{\lambda_{*}}|)-\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\eta

\displaystyle\geq C_{3}\min\{{1-|2\lambda_{*}-1+\frac{5\eta}{2}|,1-|2\lambda_{*}-1+\frac{3\eta}{2}|\}}-\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{2}-\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\eta,

(5.5)

where
C_{3}=C_{0}^{-1}
and
\tilde{x}
denotes the intersecting point of the line passing through the point
x
along the direction
(1,0,...,0)
and
\partial\Omega
. Combining (
5
) and (
5

\displaystyle\inf_{\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}}(u_{\lambda_{*}}-u)\geq\Big(\frac{(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{2}-\frac{5C_{1}}{2}\eta\Big)\frac{\eta^{\alpha}}{C}-\mathrm{def}(k).

Choose
\eta<\eta_{1}:=\frac{(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{10C_{1}}
,
\mathrm{def}(k)\leq\gamma_{1}:=\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{8C}
and hence

\displaystyle\inf_{\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}}(u_{\lambda_{*}}-u)\geq\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{8C}.

For
0<\xi<\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{32CC_{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle(u_{\lambda_{*}-\xi}-u)=(u_{\lambda_{*}-\xi}-u_{\lambda_{*}}+u_{\lambda_{*}}-u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{8C}-2\xi\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{8C}-2\xi C_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta^{\alpha}(1-\lambda_{1})C_{3}}{16C}\geq 0\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda^{*},\eta}.

(5.6)

Now, we denote
U_{\xi,\eta}:=\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}-\xi}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda_{*},\eta}\subset(\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}-\xi}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}+\eta})\cup\{x\in\Omega:1-\eta\leq|x|\leq 1\}
and it is immediate to show the existence of a positive constant

\displaystyle|U_{\xi,\eta}|\leq\tau\eta,

(5.7)

|U_{\xi,\eta}|\leq\delta

is obtained in Lemma
2.13

Applying Lemma

\displaystyle\|(u-u_{\lambda_{*}-\xi})^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{*}-\xi})}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathrm{def}(k),\text{ for small enough }\xi>0,

(5.8)

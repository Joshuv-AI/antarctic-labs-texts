Analysis and improving countermeasures of medical disputes from the perspective of legal changes
Authors: Zhang Linlin, Zhang Shimeng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Under the background of medical disputes growing in number, scale and intensity, tracing back legal changes in medical field as a breakthrough point, this paper took a legal perspective to illustrate changes in medical dispute settlements from legislative orientation to legal system improvement. In view of the fact that early legislation in medical field was biased towards identification and punishment of doctors’ responsibility, and later intensive legislation in balancing increasing "medical trouble" phenomenon with limited effects and difficulties to abide by the law, this paper proposed to improve doctor-patient dispute settlements system in China referencing from foreign law experience, to reduce investigation of doctors at the judicial level, and to establish a settlement mechanism on doctors' apology at the legislative level, so as to promote a healthy development of doctor-patient relationship.

In today's China, remarkable achievements have been made in various fields, and the rapid development and changes in politics, economy, culture, and technology are subtly influencing every citizen. Particularly, the legal changes in the medical field, which is deeply rooted in society and closely related to every citizen, have attracted high attention.

Due to their professional characteristic of saving lives and assisting the wounded, doctors are often referred to as "angels in white." However, in recent years, with the frequent occurrence and increasingly severity of medical dissension, the continuous tension in doctor-patient relationships has turned the once highly respected medical occupation into a high-risk one. The causes of medical disputes are numerous, including the difficulty and high cost of seeking medical treatment in the current society, issues with doctors' medical skills and attitudes, reduced trust and high expectations of patients towards doctors, as well as patients transferring their emotions to doctors due to the pain of losing loved ones, among various other multi-faceted and multi-level issues. Many scholars have conducted in-depth analyses of these problems from the perspectives of medicine, law, sociology, and other fields. In order to better address the multi-faceted issues existing in the resolution of medical disputes from different channels, this article starts with tracing back the legal improvement in medical disputes and proposes comprehensive countermeasures to address existing problems by analyzing the legislative orientation and legal path in the dispute resolution process.

The definition and legislative intent of medical accidents: the shift from emphasizing post-punishment to advocating for prevention

In the last century, influenced by factors such as the limited awareness of the rights of the public and overall low quality, there were a few of dispute arising from medical accidents. Therefore, the earlier legal regulations were all aimed at addressing medical accidents. The first specialized legal provision to address issues in the medical field in our country was the "Regulations on the Handling of Medical Accidents" promulgated by the State Council in 1987 (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulations"). The "Regulations" defined medical accidents as "in diagnosis and treatment nursing work, due to medical personnel's diagnostic and nursing negligence, directly resulting in patient death, disability, and organ damage leading to functional impairment. (http://m.law-lib.com/law/law_view.asp?id = 4383.) The handling of medical accidents in the "Regulations" mainly focuses on medical institutions and medical personnel with the civil, administrative, and criminal penalty. However, with the development and progress of society, the vague handling provisions in the "Regulations" and the emphasis only on post-punishment, neglecting the prevention aspect, became prominent drawbacks. Therefore, after 15 years of implementation, the "Regulations" had been abrogated.

In 2002, the State Council promulgated the "Regulations on the Handling of Medical Accidents" (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulations (2002)", replacing the aforementioned "Regulations." The "Regulations" redefined medical accidents as "accidents causing personal injury to patients due to medical institutions and their medical personnel violating laws, administrative regulations, departmental rules, and diagnostic and nursing norms, routines, and negligence in medical activities". (a) Comparing these two definitions, the "Regulation (2002)” not only expands the scope of subjects of accidents to include medical institutions, while the scope of medical accidents has been expanded, which there is a chapter on "prevention and disposal of medical accidents" added from the perspective of prevention. Handling methods have also been refined from a single post-incident punishment approach to the subdivision of responsibility types and handling procedures, especially the clarification of the resolution of civil liability disputes into three methods: self-resolution through negotiation between doctors and patients, third-party participation in resolution by applying for mediation to the health administrative department, and judicial resolution by directly filing civil lawsuits with the people's court, (b)which resolves the problems in the application of the "Regulations (1987)".

However, whether it is the"Regulations (1987)" or the "Regulations (2002)", from the perspective of legislative ideology, the definition of medical accidents and the corresponding accountability, there is a greater intention to protect the legitimate rights and interests of patients. With the enhancement of legal awareness and the advancement of medical system reform, disputes arising from medical accidents or disguised medical accidents are increasing. It is apparent that using the aforementioned methods for handling medical disputes is becoming increasingly inadequate, and the provisions on medical accidents in both regulations are unable to cover the various manifestations of medical disputes in reality. In view of this, the State Council issued the "Regulations on the Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes" in 2018 (referred to as the "New Regulations" below), which only defines medical disputes as "disputes arising from diagnosis and treatment activities between doctors and patients". However, the reduction of defining qualifiers also implies a further expansion of the scope of disputes.The current Special Law on Handling Medical Disputes is also the highest legislation in the field of medical law. The new "Regulations" further emphasize the importance of prevention and put forward the idea of "patient-centered" thinking, guiding the resolution of medical disputes through mediation channels. At the same time, it increases the avenues for both medical and patient parties to apply for mediation to resolve disputes,(c) demonstrating a diversified approach to dispute resolution.

The legal responsibilities and legal principles of both doctor and patient parties: from a focus on patient rights to a balanced trend towards medical and patient rights

Generally speaking, both doctor-and patient parties have a high degree of consensus on the goal of curing patient diseases. However, once the disease is not cured, disputes may arise. From the relevant provisions of the medical legislation mentioned above, it initially focused on the punishment of medical malpractices by doctors and had a single form, later adding private remedies such as consultation between both parties in the punishment methods, with the aim of effectively resolving disputes. However, from an overall perspective, due to the lack of forward-looking guidance that legislation should provide in line with social development, the resolution of medical disputes appears somewhat passive and less effective.

Early legislation orientation (1987–2002)- "small" patients, "small" responsibilities

In 1987, the "Regulation" stipulated three legal responsibilities for the medical side (including medical institutions and medical personnel), namely, compensation in the field of civil liability, a series of punishments such as demerits, demotion, and degradation in the field of administrative liability, and criminal liability for constituting crimes. This already shows the legislative orientation of strict control of medical accidents and serious handling. Subsequently, further enhancement of the medical side's responsibility in medical accidents in both criminal and civil fields. In the criminal domain, a specific offense of medical accidents was added in Article 335 of the Criminal Law in 1997; in the civil domain, a chapter specifically on "Compensation for Medical Accidents" was included in the Regulations (2002), enumerating eleven types of compensation items such as medical expenses, lost income, and corresponding compensation standards, which no longer falls under the vague "one-time economic compensation" in the previous practices. The responsibility of the patient is relatively simple, and the legislative approach of "Regulations (1987)" and the "Regulations (2002)" is basically the same.

The intention of early legislation to protect vulnerable patients is very clear, mainly due to the serious imbalance of medical knowledge between doctors and patients. Compared with doctors, patients are relatively vulnerable "small" patients, hence corresponding to "small" responsibility; while doctors as a member of powerful medical institutions, are considered to be knowledgeable in medicine and should benefit the public, seen as "big" doctors, hence corresponding to "big" responsibility. This inherent tendency in the design of the legal system itself has increased the responsibility of medical institutions and their medical staff.

Mid-term (2002–2010) "big" doctor role: subtle change in trend

Different from the relatively mild medical disputes in the early stage, in the 21st century, with the rapid development of society, especially the rapid progress of information technology, patients have gradually increased their access to medical knowledge through social interaction, information technology, and other means. The dependence of the general public on doctors to obtain medical knowledge has decreased, and at the same time, with the enhancement of patients' own medical knowledge and awareness of rights, t'he situation of patients questioning the legality of doctors' behavior and leading to disputes and even criminal activities is becoming increasingly common.

The doctor-patient relationship in our country is gradually deteriorating, with the number of medical disputes showing a year-on-year increasing trend, sometimes even growing at a rate of 100% in certain years (Zelin et al., 2017). Medical disputes are not only increasing in quantity but also in scale and intensity, particularly concerning are incidents of "medical disturbance" such as placing wreaths at medical institutions, verbally abusing doctors, openly damaging property, and causing public disturbances. Faced with this situation, medical institutions, in the interest of settling matters and building harmonious relationships, often find themselves forced to provide substantial compensation. Yet, the disputes do not cease, and the reluctance of medical institutions to confront the issues and the emphasis on harmonious resolutions has led to increasingly aggressive behavior from patients. The previous legislative approach, which heavily penalized medical institutions in favor of the patients, has become a trigger for the high-risk nature of the medical profession, causing medical institutions to shift from being powerful to vulnerable. It has become imperative to improve the legal governance of medical disputes.

Improved legal system in later stage (2010-): reversing the trend of "big" doctors and "big" responsibilities

With the escalating trend of "medical disturbance," in 2012, the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Public Security jointly issued the "Notice on Maintaining the Order of Medical Institutions," while in 2013, the National Health and Family Planning Commission, along with 11 other departments, issued the "Special Action Plan to Maintain Medical Order and Combat Medical-related Crimes.In 2014, the Supreme People's Court and four other departments jointly issued the "Opinions on Punishing Medical-related Illegal and Criminal Acts in Accordance with the Law to Maintain Normal Medical Order." From the frequent issuance of the above-mentioned "Notices," "Plans," and "Opinions," we can perceive that the seriousness of medical disputes has reached a point where it must be addressed. The content of the three documents is progressive and in-depth, ranging from the "Notice" which clearly lists seven types of medical disturbance behaviors that will be held criminally responsible, to the "Plan" which outlines the main measures and implementation steps for the seven behaviors, and finally to the "Opinion" which explicitly states "to increase the efforts to crack down on illegal and criminal activities such as violence against medical staff, injuring medical staff, and disrupting normal medical order." Each aspect reflects the firm intention of relevant institutions to protect the rights and interests of medical institutions, but these normative documents have relatively low hierarchical rank and weak effectiveness, resulting in limited punitive and deterrent power.

As medical disputes increasingly highlight issues led by the patient side, and as the strong medical side has become the victim in medical disputes, this also indicates a trend of transition from previous medical malpractice and violations by doctors to intentional crimes by patients. In 2015, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress formally criminalized "medical disturbance" in the form of legislation in the "Amendment to the Criminal Law (IX)," demonstrating the authority and deterrent power of the law, to a certain extent reducing the occurrence of medical disturbance incidents.

At the same time, in order to further combat medical-related crimes and encourage medical institutions to courageously refuse "medical disturbance" behaviors, in 2016, the National Health and Family Planning Commission and four other departments once again jointly issued the "Notice on Further Enhancing the Maintenance of Medical Order," clarifying that "the public security organs shall not mediate cases until the illegal acts of creating disturbances are stopped" and "medical institutions shall not settle disputes by paying compensation before the responsibilities for medical disputes are determined."

In addition to the legislative measures taken by the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress criminalizing "medical disturbance," there has been a concerted effort from multiple government departments to address medical-related crimes and preserve medical order. The joint issuance of the "Notice on Further Enhancing the Maintenance of Medical Order" emphasizes the role of public security organs in intervening only after illegal acts creating disturbances have ceased, as well as underlining that medical institutions should not settle disputes through compensation before responsibilities for medical disputes are determined. These measures signify a comprehensive approach aimed at discouraging disruptive behaviors and supporting healthcare facilities in upholding professional standards. (d) The "New Regulations" issued by the State Council in 2018 incorporated the behavior of both doctors and patients into the same provision, stating that "in the handling of medical disputes, if it causes personal, property, or other damages, they shall bear civil liability according to the law; if it constitutes a violation of public security management, public security organs shall impose public security management penalties according to the law; if it constitutes a crime, criminal responsibility shall be pursued according to the law." As a result, the responsibility of both parties has finally been placed on the same starting line.

The comprehensive measures taken have played a certain role in solving the phenomenon of "medical disturbance."

However, looking at the series of laws and normative documents currently promulgated in our country regarding medical disputes, neither the practical implementation effect nor the completeness of the current dispute resolution system has yet reached perfection.

Due to the complexity of medical disputes

The new "Regulations" in 2018 adopted the research results of the theoretical community, incorporating a variety of dispute resolution mechanisms, providing four solutions: "voluntary consultation of both parties, applying for people's mediation, applying for administrative mediation, and filing a lawsuit with the people's court." These four approaches include both litigation and non-litigation means, as well as private and public relief, which can be said to be relatively complete. However, the actual use of these approaches is not very optimistic: most people prefer to start with "medical disturbance," a minority choose to solve the problem through voluntary negotiation between doctors and patients, and most are unwilling to choose judicial approaches for resolution (Qiao, 2020).

A careful analysis reveals that the reason why most people choose to start with "medical disturbance" is that, firstly, life and health are priceless, coupled with the uneven development level in various regions of our country, making it not only difficult to form a unified compensation standard, but also resulting in significant differences in the "valuation" of life and health by different patients. The compensation obtained through legal procedures may not be as high as the compensation obtained through "disturbance." (Jun et al., 2018).

Visible, in response to the issues between doctors and patients, from the initial medical accidents to the current medical disputes and even "medical disturbances", although a series of regulations have been introduced and diverse resolution pathways provided, including criminalizing "medical disturbances", litigation is not the optimal nor the preferred way for the patients. The ultimate goal of medical litigation is not just a judgment on paper, but the peaceful resolution of medical disputes. The current situation also shows that mere civil compensation and criminal penalties as the ultimate disposal mechanism have not solved the fundamental problems.

Targeted solutions: foreign legal experience and the Chinese approach

In fact, regardless of the various changes in medical disputes, the constant entities are the doctors and patients. For doctors, this is a profession with inherent social responsibilities and is therefore respected. For patients, when their lives or health are in danger, they often become a sympathetic role. Therefore, the best path is to address the shortcomings of the existing dispute resolution system between doctors and patients to the greatest extent, without compromising the doctors' responsibility to save lives, and to achieve a balanced state of mind for patients.

Doctors' perspective: reduce criminal prosecution of doctors at the judicial level

Whether in the English common law system of England and the United States or the continental law system of Germany, the rate of doctors being sentenced for negligence in medical disputes is very low, and criminal liability for doctors' medical negligence is also handled very cautiously (Jun 2015). In Taiwan, in response to the phenomenon of the exodus of doctors from five major departments in the medical sector, such as paediatrics and the emergency department, a campaign on "rationalising medical criminal liability" was launched in the hope of lowering the criminal liability of medical personnel.

From the inherent nature of medical behavior, it is essentially a behavior that carries a certain risk to the life and health of patients. Coupled with the individual differences of patients, the limited medical skills of doctors, and the particularities of various diseases, certain problems are unavoidable.Generally speaking, when people realize that they may face punishment, especially criminal punishment, they often consciously try to protect themselves, and doctors are no exception. Criminal punishment can lead doctors to deny their faults in order to avoid liability. Therefore, the principle of leniency in the application of criminal law to doubtful cases can be applied in the judicial stage to minimize doctors' concerns and anxieties about practicing medicine.

From the perspective of patients, at the legislative level, establishing a resolution mechanism based on doctor's apology is necessary

Currently, in terms of compensation for harm to patients in medical disputes, China's legal remedies are abundant, but there is still a lack of remedies for the mental harm suffered by patients. As mentioned earlier, human life and health are priceless, and the harm caused cannot be solely compensated for with money." (Shuman, 2000) Apologizing and making amends" can soothe the spiritual harm suffered by the victim, enhance the wrongdoer's moral consciousness, and resolve conflicts, playing an irreplaceable role that other forms of liability cannot achieve (Zhenying, 2011).

It is precisely because "apologizing and making amends" functions in such a way as a form of civil tort liability that over 20 countries including North America and Australia have implemented "Apology Laws." The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of China and Taiwan have also made similar provisions in their relevant laws. Therefore, affirming the social value of healthcare, it is imperative to improve the existing medical dispute resolution mechanism with the comprehensive health of patients in mind, in addition to the existing material compensation. Legislatively, in medical tort liability, the form of doctors apologizing and making amends to patients is beneficial for comforting the spiritual harm suffered by patients, alleviating their anger, and also for doctors to face their own faults and establish the concept of putting patient health first, thereby fulfilling the legal relief function and the value of fairness and justice.

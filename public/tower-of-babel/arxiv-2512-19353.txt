Left invariant complex Finsler metrics on a complex Lie group
Xiyun Xu, Ming Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a left invariant complex Finsler metric F F on a complex Lie group. Using the technique of invariant frames, we prove the following properties for ( G , F ) (G,F) . First, the metric F F must be a complex Berwald metric. Second, its complex spray χ = w i ​ δ z i \chi=w^{i}\delta_{z^{i}} on T 1 , 0 ​ G \ 0 T^{1,0}G\backslash 0 can be extended to a holomorphic tangent field on T 1 , 0 ​ G T^{1,0}G . If we view χ \chi as a real tangent field on T ​ G TG , it coincides with the canonical bi-invariant spray structure on G G . Third, we prove that the strongly Kähler, Kähler, and weakly Kähler properties for F F are equivalent. More over, F F is Kähler if and only if G G has an Abelian Lie algebra. Finally, we prove that the holomorphic sectional curvature vanishes. Using above invariant frames, we prove the following main theorems.
First, we prove

A left invariant complex Finsler metric on a complex Lie group satisfies the complex Berwald property.

Theorem
1.1

Second, we consider the complex spray

Next, we consider the three different Kähler properties in complex Finsler geometry and prove

For a left invariant complex Finsler metric

Finally, we calculate the holomorphic sectional curvature and prove

A left invariant complex Finsler metric on a complex Lie group has vanishing holomorphic sectional curvature everywhere.

Theorem

Remark 1.5

C.P. Zhong and his coauthors, W. Xiao and K.K. Luo, have independently proved some main results in this paper. They first noticed the vanishing of the holomorphic sectional curvature for left invariant complex Finsler metrics on complex Lie groups. Their method is different from ours and their manuscript is now in preparation.

To summarize, complex homogeneous Finsler geometry behave quite differently from its real analog. The special situation we have considered, i.e.,
a left invariant complex Finsler metric on a complex Lie group,
is a relatively simple case. However, we still believe that the technique of invariant frame is a useful tool, which may help us explore more general complex homogeneous Finsler manifolds.

This paper is scheduled as follows. In Section 2, we collect some necessary knowledge in complex geometry and complex Finsler geometry. In Section 3, we construct the complex invariant frames on

2.1.
Complex manifold and complex vector field

Let
M
be a
complex manifold
of complex dimension
n>0
. We use
z=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})\in M
to denote a (holomorphic) local coordinate
on
M
.
For a complex function
f
, we present its real and imaginary part by
f_{\Re}

and
f_{\Im}
respectively, i.e.,
f=f_{\Re}+\sqrt{-1}f_{\Im}
. So for
a local coordinate
(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})\in M
,

(z^{1}_{\Re},z^{1}_{\Im},\cdots,z^{n}_{\Re},z^{n}_{\Im})

is the corresponding real local coordinate.

Let
J:T_{z}M\rightarrow T_{z}M
be the complex structure at
z\in M
, where
T_{z}M
is the real tangent space. Then
T_{z}M\otimes\mathbb{C}=T^{1,0}_{z}M\oplus T^{0,1}_{z}M
, in which the two summands are the eigenspaces for
\pm\sqrt{-1}
respectively.
In local coordinate,
J\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}
and
J\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}=-\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}
,
so we have

\displaystyle T^{0,1}_{z}M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{span}^{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial_{z^{i}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}-\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}),\forall i\}\quad\mbox{and}

(2.1)

\displaystyle T^{1,0}_{z}M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{span}^{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial_{\overline{z}^{i}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}),\forall i\}.

The
holomorphic tangent bundle

T^{1,0}M=\bigcup_{z\in M}T^{1,0}_{z}M
is a holomorphic bundle over
M
.
A section of
T^{1,0}M
is
complex tangent field

V

of type

(1,0)
, which can be locally presented as

V=f^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}
, in which
f^{i}
are complex function in the local chart.
In this paper, we only consider smooth tangent field of type
(1,0)
, so to avoid iteration, we will not mention the smoothness or the type in later discussion.
The smooth isomorphism
between real vector bundles,
\cdot^{\circ}:T^{1,0}M\rightarrow TM
,

\partial_{z^{i}}{}^{\circ}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}
,

(\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}})^{\circ}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}
,
provides a one-to-one correspondence between complex tangent vector fields
and real tangent vector fields, i.e., for
V=f^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}
,
V^{\circ}=V+\overline{V}=f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}+f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}
,
and conversely,
V=\tfrac{1}{2}(V^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}J(V^{\circ}))
.
In later discussion,
T^{1,0}M
is occasionally viewed as
TM

\cdot^{\circ}
, complex tangent fields are usually denoted as
U
,
V
,
W
, etc., and real tangent fields as
U^{\circ}
,
V^{\circ}
,
W^{\circ}
, etc.

For two complex

U=f^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}

V=g^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}
,
their bracket is

[U,V]=(f^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{z^{j}}
. Generally speaking,
[U,V]^{\circ}
does not coincide with

(2.2)

\displaystyle[U^{\circ},V^{\circ}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}+f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}g^{j}_{\Re}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}-g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Re})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Re}}

\displaystyle+(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}+f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}g^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Im})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Re}},

because, generally speaking,
[U^{\circ},V^{\circ}]\neq[U,V]^{\circ}
. On the other hand, when

and

are

are holomorphic,

Lemma 2.1
.

For two holomorphic tangent fields
U
and

[U^{\circ},V^{\circ}]=[U,V]^{\circ}
. Further more, we also have
[J(U^{\circ}),V^{\circ}]=[U^{\circ},J(V^{\circ})]=J([U,V]^{\circ})

Proof.
By the Cauchy-Riemann equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Im},\quad\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im}=-\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Re},\quad\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}g^{j}_{\Im},\quad\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}=-\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}g^{j}_{\Re},\quad\forall i,j,

(2.3)

\displaystyle[U^{\circ},V^{\circ}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}-f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}+g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Re}}

\displaystyle+(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}+f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Re}}.

On the other hand, the Cauchy-Riemann equation implies

\partial_{z^{i}}f^{j}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}=-\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j},\quad\partial_{z^{i}}g^{j}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}=-\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}g^{j},\quad\forall i,j.

So we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle[U,V]^{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((f^{i}_{\Re}+\sqrt{-1}f^{i}_{\Im})(\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im})-(g^{i}_{\Re}+\sqrt{-1}g^{i}_{\Im})(\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im})\partial_{z^{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}-f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}+g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Re}}

\displaystyle+(f^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Im}+f^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}g^{j}_{\Re}-g^{i}_{\Re}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im}-g^{i}_{\Im}\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re})\partial_{z^{j}_{\Im}}.

\displaystyle f^{i}A_{i}^{j}\partial_{z^{j}}+w^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}(f^{j}A^{k}_{j})\partial_{w^{k}}=f^{i}A_{i}^{j}\partial_{z^{j}}+w^{i}f^{j}\partial_{z^{i}}A^{k}_{j}\partial_{w^{k}}+w^{i}A^{k}_{j}\partial_{z^{i}}f^{j}\partial_{w^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{i}(A_{i}^{j}\partial_{z^{j}}+w^{j}\partial_{z^{j}}A^{k}_{i}\partial_{w^{k}})+v^{p}A_{p}^{i}B_{i}^{q}V_{q}(f^{j})\partial_{v^{j}}=f^{i}\widetilde{V}_{i}+v^{i}V_{i}(f^{j})\partial_{v^{j}}.

The proof of Lemma
2.4
is finished.

We end this subsection with the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any two holomorphic tangent fields
U
and
V
,
\widetilde{[U,V]}=[\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}]
.

Proof.
Using Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
5.2
, we can get

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{[U,V]})^{\circ}=\widetilde{[U,V]^{\circ}}=\widetilde{[U^{\circ},V^{\circ}]}=[\widetilde{U^{\circ}},\widetilde{V^{\circ}}].

On the other hand,
by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
,
[J(\widetilde{U}^{\circ}),J(\widetilde{V}^{\circ})]=-[\widetilde{U}^{\circ},\widetilde{V}^{\circ}]
, so the real part of

[\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}]=[\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{U}^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}J\widetilde{U}^{\circ}),\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}J\widetilde{V}^{\circ})]

coincides with

\frac{1}{4}[\widetilde{U}^{\circ},\widetilde{V}^{\circ}]-\frac{1}{4}[J\widetilde{U}^{\circ},J\widetilde{U}^{\circ}]=\frac{1}{2}[\widetilde{U}^{\circ},\widetilde{V}^{\circ}]
,
so we have
[\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}]^{\circ}=[\widetilde{U}^{\circ},\widetilde{V}^{\circ}]
.
Combine it with (
2.8
), we finish the proof of Lemma
2.5
.

2.3.
Complex Finsler metric

A
complex Finsler metric
on the complex manifold
M

is a continuous function
F:T^{1,0}M\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfying
the following properties:

(1)

F
is regular, i.e., it is positive and smooth when restricted to
T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
;

(2)

F
is 1-homogeneous, i.e.,
F(z,\lambda w)=|\lambda|F(z,w)
for any
(z,w)\in T^{1,0}M

and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

(3)

F
is pseudo-convex, i.e., for
{\mathrm{G}}=F^{2}
,
the complex Hessian matrix
({\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}})=(\partial_{w^{i}}\partial_{\overline{w}^{j}}{\mathrm{G}})

is positive definite at any
(z,w)\in T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
.

The restriction of the complex Finsler metric
F
to
each
T^{1,0}_{x}M
is called a
complex Minkowski norm
, which can be similarly defined for any finite dimensional complex linear space.

Denote by
({\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{i}j})
the inverse matrix of
({\mathrm{G}}_{\overline{i}j})
, i.e.,
{\mathrm{G}}_{k\overline{j}}{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{j}i}={\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{i}j}{\mathrm{G}}_{j\overline{k}}=\delta^{i}_{k}
. We apply the convention
abbreviating
\partial_{w^{i}}{\mathrm{G}}
,

\partial_{w^{i}}\partial_{\overline{w}^{j}}\partial_{\overline{w}^{k}}{\mathrm{G}}
,

\partial_{\overline{w}^{j}}\partial_{z^{k}}{\mathrm{G}}
, etc., as

{\mathrm{G}}_{i}
,
{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}\overline{k}}
,
{\mathrm{G}}_{\overline{j};k}
, etc., respectively.
The homogeneity property of
F
implies
{\mathrm{G}}={\mathrm{G}}_{i}w^{i}={\mathrm{G}}_{\overline{i}}\overline{w}^{i}
,

{\mathrm{G}}_{ij}w^{i}={\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}k}w^{i}={\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}\overline{k}}w^{j}=0
, etc.,
everywhere on
T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
.

The coefficients for the Chern-Finsler connection are given by

N^{i}_{k}={\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{j}i}{\mathrm{G}}_{\overline{j};k}={\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{j}i}\partial_{\overline{w}^{j}}\partial_{z^{k}}F^{2},\quad\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}=\partial_{{w}^{j}}N^{i}_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C^{i}_{jk}={\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{l}i}{\mathrm{G}}_{j{k}\overline{l}}={\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{l}i}\partial_{w^{k}}{\mathrm{G}}_{j\overline{l}}.

We call
F

complex Berwald
if all
\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}
are independent of the
w
-coordinate. We call
F

strongly Kähler
(
Kähler
or
weakly Kähler
)
if

\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j}=0\ \mbox{(}w^{k}(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j})=0\mbox{ or }w^{k}(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-{\mathrm{G}}_{i}\Gamma^{i}_{k;j}){\mathrm{G}}_{i}=0\mbox{ respectively})

is satisfied everywhere on
T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
.

Denote by
\delta_{z^{i}}=\partial_{z^{i}}-N_{i}^{j}\partial_{w^{j}}
the complex tangent fields on
T^{1,0}M
, which linearly span the horizonal distribution. Then
\chi=w^{i}\delta_{z^{i}}
is a complex tangent field, which can be globally defined on
T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
. It is the analog for the geodesic spray in real Finsler geometry, so we call it the
complex spray

for
(M,F)
.

The
holomorphic sectional curvature
can be presented as the function

K=2R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{l}}\frac{w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}w^{k}\overline{w}^{l}}{{\mathrm{G}}^{2}}

on
T^{1,0}M\backslash 0
, in which

R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{l}}=-{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j};k\overline{l}}+{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{q}p}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{q};k}{\mathrm{G}}_{p\overline{j};\overline{l}}=-\partial_{z^{k}}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}}+{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{q}p}\partial_{z^{k}}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{q}}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}{\mathrm{G}}_{p\overline{j}}
.

More details can be found in
[
1
]
.

3.
Left invariant complex Finsler metrics and invariant frames

3.1.
Left invariant complex Finsler metric on a complex Lie group

Let
G
be a

complex Lie group
. By definition it means that,
G
has a group structure with the identity element
e\in G
specified, such that the multiplication map
(g_{1},g_{2})\in G\times G\mapsto g_{1}g_{2}\in G
and the inverse map

g\in G\mapsto g^{-1}\in G
are holomorphic.
For any
g\in G
, Denote by
L_{g}(g^{\prime})=gg^{\prime}
and
R_{g}(g^{\prime})=g^{\prime}g
with
g^{\prime}\in G
the left and right translations respectively. The holomorphic property of the multiplication map implies that all left and right translations
are holomorphic diffeomorphisms on
G
.

A complex tangent field
V
on
G
is called
left invariant
(
right invariant
), if
V^{\circ}
is left invariant (right invariant), i.e.,
V^{\circ}
is preserved by the tangent maps of all left translations (all right translations respectively).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V^{\circ}
be a left or right invariant real tangent field on
G
, then
V
is holomorphic.

Proof.
We only need to verify the lemma for a left invariant
V^{\circ}
,
because the proof in other case is similar. Let
R_{g(t)}
be the one-parameter subgroup of right translations generated by
V^{\circ}
. Using a local chart,
we have a smooth map

\displaystyle(g^{\prime},t)=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n},t)=(z^{1}_{\Re},z^{1}_{\Im},\cdots,z^{n}_{\Re},z^{n}_{\Im},t)\in G\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle R_{g(t)}g^{\prime}=(f^{1}(z,t),\cdots,f^{n}(z,t))=(f^{1}_{\Re},f^{1}_{\Im},\cdots,f^{n}_{\Re},f^{n}_{\Im}),

in which all
f^{i}
are holomorphic with respect to
z
.
Then

V^{\circ}=(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{i}_{\Re})\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}+(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{i}_{\Im})\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}
and
V=(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{i})\partial_{z^{i}}
.
Apply
\partial_{t}|_{t=0}
to the Cauchy-Riemann equations

\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Re}=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Im},\quad\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}f^{j}_{\Re}=-\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}f^{j}_{\Im},\quad\forall i,j,

we get

\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{j}_{\Re})=\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{j}_{\Im}),\quad\partial_{z^{i}_{\Im}}(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{j}_{\Re})=-\partial_{z^{i}_{\Re}}(\partial_{t}|_{t=0}f^{j}_{\Im}),\quad\forall i,j.

So
V
is holomorphic with respect to
z
, which ends the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
J:TG\rightarrow TG
be the complex structure on
G
. Then for any left (right) invariant complex tangent field
V
on
G
,
\sqrt{-1}V
is also a
left (right) invariant complex tangent field, i.e.,

J(V^{\circ})=(\sqrt{-1}V)^{\circ}
is a left (right respectively) invariant real tangent field.

Proof.
We only need to verify the lemma when
V
is left invariant, because the proof for the other half is similar. The left invariance of
V^{\circ}
means

(L_{g})_{*}(V^{\circ})=V^{\circ}
for any
g\in G
.
Since
L_{g}:G\rightarrow G

is holomorphic, its tangent map
(L_{g})_{*}:TG\rightarrow TG
commutes with
J
. So we have
(L_{g})_{*}(J(V^{\circ}))=J((L_{g})_{*}(V^{\circ}))=J(V^{\circ})
for any
g\in G
, which implies
J(V^{\circ})
is left invariant. The proof of Lemma
3.2
is finished.

Let
F:T^{1,0}M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a complex Finsler metric on
G
. We call
F

left invariant
, if it is preserved by the tangent maps of all left translations
(L_{g})_{*}:T^{1,0}G=TG\rightarrow TG=T^{1,0}G
. Here we identify
T^{1,0}
with
TG
through
\cdot^{\circ}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any right invariant complex tangent field
V
on
G
, we have
\widetilde{V}{\mathrm{G}}\equiv 0
.

Proof.
Since the right invariant real tangent field
V^{\circ}
generates
left translations on
G
, the left invariance of
G
implies
\widetilde{V^{\circ}}{\mathrm{G}}\equiv 0
. By Lemma
3.2
,
J(V^{\circ})
is also right invariant. So Lemma
2.3
implies
(J(\widetilde{V}^{\circ})){\mathrm{G}}=\widetilde{J(V^{\circ})}{\mathrm{G}}\equiv 0
. To summarize,
\widetilde{V}{\mathrm{G}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}^{\circ}{\mathrm{G}}-\sqrt{-1}(J(\widetilde{V}^{\circ})){\mathrm{G}})\equiv 0
, which ends the proof.

3.2.
Technique of invariant frames

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of a complex Lie group
G

is a complex Lie algebra, which can be identified with the space of all left invariant complex tangent fields, with the canonical bracket between complex tangent fields. It can also be identified with
T^{1,0}_{e}G
.

We choose a complex basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
of

\mathfrak{g}=T^{1,0}G
, with the corresponding bracket coefficients
c^{k}_{ij}
determined by
[e_{i},e_{j}]=c_{ij}^{k}e_{k}
. Then we have left invariant complex tangent fields
\{U_{i},\forall i\}
and right invariant complex tangent fields
\{V_{i},\forall i\}
, satisfying
U_{i}(e)=V_{i}(e)=e_{i}
for each
i
. Denote
\widetilde{U}_{i}
and
\widetilde{V}_{i}
their lift to
T^{1,0}G
respectively.
Any
w\in T^{1,0}_{g}G
can be uniquely presented as
w=u^{i}U_{i}(g)=v^{i}V_{i}(g)
, so we have the complex linear coordinates
(u^{1},\cdots,u^{n})
and
(v^{1},\cdots,v^{n})
, and the corresponding
\{\partial_{u^{i}},\forall i\}
,
\{\partial_{v^{i}},\forall i\}
in
T^{1,0}_{g}G
, which can be globally defined
as holomorphic functions and tangent fields on
T^{1,0}G
. To summarize, we get the
left invariant
holomorphic frame

\{\widetilde{U}_{i},\partial_{u^{i}},\forall i\}
and the

right invariant holomorphic frame

\{\widetilde{V}_{i},\partial_{v^{i}},\forall i\}
.

The interrelation between these two frames are as follows.
For each
g\in G
, we define
\phi^{i}_{j}=\phi^{i}_{j}(g)
and
\psi^{i}_{j}=\psi^{i}_{j}(g)
, such that

\mathrm{Ad}(g)e_{j}=\phi^{i}_{j}e_{i}
and
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})e_{i}=\psi_{j}^{i}e_{i}

(so we also have
(\psi^{i}_{j})=(\phi^{i}_{j})^{-1}
, i.e.,
\psi^{i}_{j}\phi^{j}_{k}=\phi^{i}_{j}\psi^{j}_{k}=\delta^{i}_{k}
).

Lemma 3.4
.

(1)
U_{j}=\phi^{i}_{j}V_{i}
,
V_{j}=\psi^{i}_{j}U_{i}
,
u^{i}=\psi^{i}_{j}v^{j}
,

v^{i}=\phi^{i}_{j}u^{j}
,
\partial_{u^{j}}=\phi^{i}_{j}\partial_{v^{i}}
,

\partial_{v^{j}}=\psi^{i}_{j}\partial_{u^{i}}
;

(2)
[U_{i},U_{j}]=c^{k}_{ij}U_{k}
,
[V_{i},V_{j}]=-c^{k}_{ij}V_{k}
,

[\widetilde{U}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}]=0
,

[\widetilde{U}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}]=c^{k}_{ij}\widetilde{U}_{k}
,

[\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}]=-c^{k}_{ij}V_{k}
;

(3)
U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{k})=\psi_{j}^{l}c^{k}_{li}=\phi_{i}^{l}\psi_{p}^{k}c_{jl}^{p}
,

V_{i}(\phi_{j}^{k})=\phi_{j}^{l}c^{k}_{il}=\phi_{p}^{k}\psi_{i}^{l}c_{lj}^{p}
;

(4)
\widetilde{U}_{i}=\phi^{j}_{i}\widetilde{V}_{j}+c^{k}_{li}u^{l}\partial_{u^{k}}
,

\widetilde{V}_{i}=\psi^{j}_{i}\widetilde{U}_{j}-c^{k}_{li}v^{l}\partial_{v^{k}}
.

(5)
\widetilde{U}_{j}v^{i}=\widetilde{V}_{j}u^{i}=0
,

\widetilde{U}_{i}u^{j}=c_{li}^{j}u^{l}
,

\widetilde{V}_{i}v^{j}=-c_{li}^{j}v^{l}
;

(6)
[\widetilde{U}_{i},\partial_{u^{j}}]=c^{k}_{ij}\partial_{u^{k}}
,

[\widetilde{U}_{i},\partial_{v^{j}}]=0
,
[\widetilde{V}_{i},\partial_{u^{j}}]=0
,

[\widetilde{V}_{i},\partial_{v^{j}}]=-c^{k}_{ij}\partial_{v^{k}}
.

Proof.
Some statements in Lemma
3.4
can be proved as follows. The others can be proved similarly or follows easily.

(1) For any
g\in G
,

(L_{g^{-1}})_{*}(V_{j}(g))=(L_{g^{-1}})_{*}\circ(R_{g})_{*}(e_{j})=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})e_{j}=\psi_{j}^{i}e_{i},

so we have
V_{j}=\psi_{j}^{i}U_{i}
.

(2)
By Lemma
3.2
,
\{\widetilde{U^{\circ}_{i}},\widetilde{J(U^{\circ}_{i})},\partial_{u^{i}_{\Re}},\partial_{u^{i}_{\Im}},\forall i\}
and
and
\{\widetilde{V^{\circ}_{i}},\widetilde{J(V^{\circ}_{i})},\partial_{v^{i}_{\Re}},\partial_{v^{i}_{\Im}}\}
are left and right invariant frame on
TG
respectively, in the real context
[
19
]
. They are induced by the real basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n},\sqrt{-1}e_{1},\cdots
,
\sqrt{-1}e_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, with the following bracket coefficients,

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{j}]=-[\sqrt{-1}e_{i},\sqrt{-1}e_{j}]=\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}e_{k}+\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}\sqrt{-1}e_{k},

\displaystyle[\sqrt{-1}e_{i},e_{j}]=[e_{i},\sqrt{-1}e_{j}]=-\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}e_{k}+\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}\sqrt{-1}e_{k}.

So we have

\displaystyle[U^{\circ}_{i},U^{\circ}_{j}]=-[JU^{\circ}_{i},JU^{\circ}_{j}]=\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}U^{\circ}_{k}+\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}JU^{\circ}_{k},

\displaystyle[JU^{\circ}_{i},U^{\circ}_{j}]=[U^{\circ}_{i},JU^{\circ}_{j}]=-\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}U^{\circ}_{k}+\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}JU^{\circ}_{k},

\displaystyle[V^{\circ}_{i},V^{\circ}_{j}]=-[JV^{\circ}_{i},JV^{\circ}_{j}]=-\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}V^{\circ}_{k}-\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}JV^{\circ}_{k},

\displaystyle[JV^{\circ}_{i},V^{\circ}_{j}]=[V^{\circ}_{i},JV^{\circ}_{j}]=\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}V^{\circ}_{k}-\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}JV^{\circ}_{k}.

Since left invariant real tangent fields commute with right invariant real tangent fields,
U_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}(U_{i}^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}JU_{i}^{\circ})
commutes with

V_{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}JV_{j}^{\circ})
by Lemma
3.2
. More over, we have
\widetilde{U}_{i}
commutes with
\widetilde{V}_{j}
by Lemma
2.5
.

By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
,

[U_{i},U_{j}]^{\circ}=[U_{i}^{\circ},U_{j}^{\circ}]=\mathrm{Re}c_{ij}^{k}U_{k}^{\circ}+\mathrm{Im}c_{ij}^{k}JU_{k}^{\circ},

which coincides with
(c_{ij}^{k}U_{k})^{\circ}
, so we have
[U_{i},U_{j}]=c_{ij}^{k}U_{k}
, and then Lemma
2.5
provides

[\widetilde{U}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}]=c_{ij}^{k}\widetilde{U}_{k}
.

(3) Because

\displaystyle 0=[U_{i},V_{j}]=[U_{i},\psi_{j}^{k}U_{k}]=\psi_{j}^{k}[U_{i},U_{k}]+U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{k})U_{k}=(\psi_{j}^{k}c_{ik}^{l}+U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{l}))U_{l},

we get
U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{k})=\psi_{j}^{l}c^{k}_{li}
,
\forall i,j,k
.
Since
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1}):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}

is a Lie algebra isomorphism, i.e.,
\psi_{i}^{\alpha}\psi_{j}^{\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\psi_{k}^{\gamma}c_{ij}^{k}
,
U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{k})
can also be presented as
U_{i}(\psi_{j}^{k})=\psi_{j}^{l}c^{k}_{li}=\phi_{i}^{l}\psi_{p}^{k}c_{jl}^{p}
.

(4)
By Lemma
2.4
, the lift of
V_{i}=\psi_{i}^{j}U_{j}
can be presented as

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{j}\widetilde{U}_{j}+u^{j}U_{j}(\psi_{i}^{k})\partial_{u^{k}}=\psi_{i}^{j}\widetilde{U}_{j}+u^{j}\psi_{i}^{l}c^{k}_{lj}\partial_{u^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{j}\widetilde{U}_{j}+u^{j}\phi_{j}^{l}\psi_{p}^{k}c_{il}^{p}\partial_{u^{k}}=\psi_{i}^{j}\widetilde{U}_{j}-c_{li}^{k}v^{l}\partial_{v^{k}}.

(5) Since
\widetilde{V}_{j}
generates (the tangent maps of) left translations on
T^{1,0}G
, which preserves all
U_{i}
, it also preserves all functions
u_{i}
, so we have
\widetilde{V}_{j}u^{i}=0
. Meanwhile, we have

\widetilde{V}_{i}v^{j}=\widetilde{V}_{i}(\phi^{j}_{k}u^{k})=\widetilde{V}_{i}(\phi^{j}_{k})u^{k}=\phi_{k}^{l}c_{il}^{j}u^{k}=-c_{li}^{j}v^{l}.

(6) As in (5),
[\widetilde{V}_{i},\partial_{u^{k}}]=0
can also be observed from the the left invariance of
u^{k}
. Notice that
\phi_{i}^{k}
is only relevant to
z\in M
, so we have

[\widetilde{U}_{i},\partial_{u^{j}}]=[\phi_{i}^{k}\widetilde{V}_{k}+c^{k}_{li}u^{l}\partial_{u^{k}},\partial_{u^{j}}]=[c^{k}_{li}u^{l}\partial_{u^{k}},\partial_{u^{j}}]=c^{k}_{ij}\partial_{u^{k}}.

The proof of Lemma
3.4
is finished.

Alternatively, we may use local coordinate
(z,w)=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n},w^{1},\cdots,w^{n})
to present these frames.
There exist
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
-valued holomorphic functions
(A^{i}_{j})=(A^{i}_{j}(z))
,

(B^{i}_{j})=(B^{i}_{j}(z))=(A^{i}_{j})^{-1}
,
(C^{i}_{j})=(C^{i}_{j}(z))
and
(D^{i}_{j})=(D^{i}_{j}(z))=(C^{i}_{j})^{-1}
, locally defined on
G
,
such that the following are satisfied,

\displaystyle U_{j}=A^{i}_{j}\partial_{z^{i}},\ \partial_{z^{j}}=B^{i}_{j}U_{i},\ u^{i}=B^{i}_{j}w^{j},\ w^{i}=A^{i}_{j}u^{j},\ \partial_{u^{j}}=A^{i}_{j}\partial_{w^{i}},\ \partial_{w^{j}}=B^{i}_{j}\partial_{u^{i}},

\displaystyle V_{j}=C^{i}_{j}\partial_{z^{i}},\ \partial_{z^{j}}=D^{i}_{j}V_{i},\ v^{i}=D^{i}_{j}w^{j},\ w^{i}=C^{i}_{j}v^{j},\ \partial_{v^{j}}=C^{i}_{j}\partial_{w^{i}},\ \partial_{w^{j}}=D^{i}_{j}\partial_{v^{i}},

(3.9)

\displaystyle A^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{k}=B^{i}_{j}A^{j}_{k}=C^{i}_{j}D^{j}_{k}=D^{i}_{j}C^{j}_{k}=\delta^{i}_{k},\ C_{j}^{k}B_{k}^{i}=\psi_{j}^{i},\ A^{k}_{j}D_{k}^{i}=\phi_{j}^{i}.

Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
provide

Lemma 3.5
.

With respect to the local coordinate
(z,w)=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n},w^{1},\cdots,w^{n})
on
T^{1,0}G
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{j}=A^{i}_{j}\partial_{z^{i}}+w^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}A^{k}_{j}\partial_{w^{k}},

\displaystyle\partial_{z^{j}}=B^{i}_{j}\widetilde{U}_{i}+u^{i}U_{i}(B^{k}_{j})\partial_{u^{k}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{j}=C^{i}_{j}\partial_{z^{i}}+w^{i}\partial_{z^{i}}C^{k}_{j}\partial_{w^{k}},

and

\displaystyle\partial_{z^{j}}=D^{i}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{i}+v^{i}V_{i}(D^{k}_{j})\partial_{v^{k}},

Let
F=\sqrt{\mathrm{G}}

be a left invariant complex Finsler metric on
G
. We use

(\mathcal{G}_{i\overline{j}})
to denote the complex Hessian matrix

(\partial_{u^{i}}\partial_{\overline{u}^{j}}{\mathrm{G}})
and

(\mathcal{G}^{\overline{i}j})

(\mathcal{G}_{i\overline{j}})
, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{k\overline{j}}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{j}i}=\mathcal{G}^{\overline{i}j}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}=\delta^{i}_{k}

Lemma 3.6
.

With respect to the local coordinate
(z,w)=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n},w^{1},\cdots,w^{n})
on

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j}}=B_{i}^{p}\overline{B_{j}^{q}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}},\quad{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{i}j}=\overline{A^{i}_{p}}A^{j}_{q}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{p}q},

\displaystyle u^{i}\partial_{u^{i}}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}=u^{j}\partial_{u^{i}}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}=\overline{u}^{i}\partial_{\overline{u}^{i}}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}=\overline{u}^{k}\partial_{\overline{u}^{i}}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{i}\mathcal{G}_{j\overline{k}}\equiv 0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{V}_{i}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{j}{k}}\equiv 0.

4.
Proof of the main theorems

Let
G
be a complex Lie group and
F
a left invariant complex
Finsler metric on
G
. We will use the invariant frames and the notations
in the Section
3.2
to
calculate the connection coefficients and prove the main theorems.

4.1.
Berwald property and complex spray

First, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
3.4
, Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle N^{i}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{l}i}\partial_{\overline{w}^{l}}\partial_{z^{k}}{\mathrm{G}}=\overline{A^{l}_{p}}A^{i}_{q}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{p}q}\overline{B_{l}^{r}}\partial_{\overline{u}^{r}}((D_{k}^{a}\widetilde{V}_{a}+v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\partial_{v^{b}}){\mathrm{G}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{A^{l}_{p}}A^{i}_{q}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{p}q}\overline{B_{l}^{r}}\partial_{\overline{u}^{r}}((v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\psi_{b}^{c}\partial_{u^{c}}){\mathrm{G}})=A^{i}_{q}\mathcal{G}^{\overline{r}q}v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\psi_{b}^{c}\mathcal{G}_{c\overline{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{i}_{c}v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\psi_{b}^{c},

Notice that the last equality of (
3.9
) implies
A^{i}_{j}\psi^{j}_{k}=C^{i}_{k}
,
so we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle N^{i}_{k}=C^{i}_{b}v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}=\partial_{w^{j}}N^{i}_{k}=C^{i}_{b}D^{a}_{j}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b}).

From (
4.11
), we see that
\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}
only depends on
z\in M
.
So
F
is complex Berwald. The proof of Theorem
1.1

is finished.

Next, we calculate the complex spray

\chi=w^{k}\delta_{z^{k}}=w^{k}(\partial_{z^{k}}-N^{i}_{k}\partial_{w^{i}})
on
T^{1,0}G\backslash 0
and prove Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
3.5
and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle w^{k}\delta_{z^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{k}(D_{k}^{a}\widetilde{V}_{a}+v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{c})\partial_{v^{c}})-w^{k}A^{i}_{c}v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\psi_{b}^{c}\partial_{w^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{a}C^{k}_{a}(D_{k}^{b}\widetilde{V}_{b})+w^{k}v^{b}V_{b}(D_{k}^{c})\partial_{v^{c}}-w^{k}C^{i}_{b}v^{a}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})\partial_{w^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{a}\widetilde{V}_{a}-w^{k}v^{a}(V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})C_{b}^{i}-C^{i}_{b}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b}))\partial_{w^{i}}=v^{i}\widetilde{V}_{i}.

So
\chi
can be extended to a holomorphic tangent field
v^{i}\widetilde{V}_{i}
on
T^{1,0}G
.

By Lemma
2.3
, the real part of
\chi=v^{i}\widetilde{V}_{i}
is
\tfrac{1}{2}(v^{i}_{\Re}\widetilde{V_{i}}^{\circ}+v^{i}_{\Im}J(\widetilde{V_{i}}^{\circ}))=\tfrac{1}{2}(v^{i}_{\Re}\widetilde{V^{\circ}_{i}}+v^{i}_{\Im}\widetilde{J(V^{\circ})}
, we get

(v^{i}\widetilde{V}_{i})^{\circ}=v^{i}_{\Re}\widetilde{V}^{\circ}_{i}+v^{i}_{\Im}J(\widetilde{V^{\circ}})
. When
G
is viewed as a real Lie group,
v^{i}_{\Re}\widetilde{V}^{\circ}_{i}+v^{i}_{\Im}J(\widetilde{V^{\circ}})
is the canonical bi-invariant spray structure in Theorem A of
[
19
]
.
By similar calculation and
[
19
]
,
the canonical bi-invariant spray structure on
G
can also be presented as
(u^{i}\widetilde{U}_{i})^{\circ}=u^{i}_{\Re}\widetilde{U^{\circ}_{i}}+u^{i}_{\Im}\widetilde{J(U_{i}^{\circ})}
. So we have

(u^{i}\widetilde{U})^{\circ}=(v^{i}\widetilde{V})^{\circ}
, i.e.,
u^{i}\widetilde{U}=v^{i}\widetilde{V}
, which ends the proof of
Theorem
1.2
.

4.2.
Strongly Kähler, Kähler and weak Kähler properties

Without loss of generality, we assume in this section that the Lie group
G
is connected.

Notice that
F
is Kähler if and only if it is strongly Kähler
[
6
]
, which can be equivalently described by the vanishing of

(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j})\partial_{z^{i}}
everywhere. By (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j})\partial_{z^{i}}=(C^{i}_{b}D^{a}_{j}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})-C^{i}_{b}D^{a}_{k}V_{a}(D_{j}^{b}))\partial_{z^{i}}=(D^{a}_{j}V_{a}(D_{k}^{b})-D^{a}_{k}V_{a}(D_{j}^{b}))V_{b}.

On the other hand, the first line of (
3.9
) provides

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\partial_{z^{j}},\partial_{z^{k}}]=[D^{a}_{j}V_{a},D^{b}_{k}V_{b}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{p}_{j}D^{q}_{k}[V_{p},V_{q}]+(D^{a}_{j}V_{a}(D^{b}_{k})-D^{a}_{k}V_{a}(D^{b}_{j}))V_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-D^{p}_{j}D^{q}_{k}c_{pq}^{r}V_{r}+(D^{a}_{j}V_{a}(D^{b}_{k})-D^{a}_{k}V_{a}(D^{b}_{j}))V_{b}.

So we have

(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j})\partial_{z^{i}}=D^{p}_{j}D^{q}_{k}c_{pq}^{r}V_{r}
, which vanishes if and only if all bracket coefficients
c^{k}_{ij}
of
\mathfrak{g}
vanishes, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}
is Abelian. To summarize, we have proved the equivalence between
F
is Kähler and
\mathfrak{g}
is Abelian. To finish the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we only need to assume
F
is weakly Kähler and prove
\mathfrak{g}
is Abelian.

Together with (
3.9
), above calculation implies that, at
e\in G
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle w^{k}(\Gamma^{i}_{j;k}-\Gamma^{i}_{k;j}){\mathrm{G}}_{i}=w^{k}D^{p}_{j}D^{q}_{k}c_{pq}^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}{\mathrm{G}}=D^{p}_{j}v^{q}c_{pq}^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}{\mathrm{G}}=0.

If
F
is viewed as a complex Minkowski norm on the complex
\mathfrak{g}
, then (
4.12
) is equivalent
to the vanishing of the directional derivative

[e_{p},v]{\mathrm{G}}=v^{q}c_{pq}^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}{\mathrm{G}}
, for any
p
, at each point
v=v^{i}e_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\backslash\{0\}
.
Notice that
\mathfrak{g}
may be viewed as a real Lie algebra, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=T_{e}G
, and the correspondence
u^{\circ}\in T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto u=\tfrac{1}{2}(u^{\circ}-\sqrt{-1}J(u^{\circ}))\in T^{1,0}_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
is a real Lie algebra
isomorphism. So when
{\mathrm{G}}
is viewed as a function on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
, (
4.12
) implies
[e_{p}^{\circ},v^{\circ}]{\mathrm{G}}=[J(e_{p}^{\circ}),v^{\circ}]{\mathrm{G}}=0
for every
p
, at each
v^{\circ}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{0\}
. By this observation, we have verified that
F
is an
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant function on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Now we construct an
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant Euclidean norm on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
as follows. Since the indicatrix
F=1
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a sphere surrounding the origin, its convex hull
\mathcal{C}
contains the origin as an internal point. Then there exists
a real Minkowski norm
F^{\prime}
such that the indicatrix
F^{\prime}=1
coincides with the boundary sphere
\partial\mathcal{C}
.
Using the Binet-Legendre transformation, we get an Euclidean norm
F^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
. In each step of above construction, the
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariance can be preserved. To summarize, the existence of an
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant Euclidean norm implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact Lie algebra. But the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can only be compact when it is Abelian.
The proof of Theorem
1.4
is finished.

4.3.
The vanishing of holomorphic sectional curvature

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we need to use (
3.9
), Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
to translate

(4.13)

\displaystyle w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{l}}=-w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j};k\overline{l}}+w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{q}{p}}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{q};k}{\mathrm{G}}_{p\overline{j};\overline{l}}.

First, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{j};k\overline{l}}=w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}\partial_{z^{k}}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}(B_{i}^{p}\overline{B_{j}^{q}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}\partial_{z^{k}}B_{i}^{p}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}\overline{B^{q}_{j}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}}+w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}B^{p}_{i}\overline{B^{q}_{j}}\partial_{z^{k}}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}}

\displaystyle+w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}\partial_{z^{k}}B^{p}_{i}\overline{B^{q}_{j}}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}}+w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}{w}^{k}\overline{w}^{l}B^{p}_{i}\partial_{\overline{z}^{l}}\overline{B^{q}_{j}}\partial_{z^{k}}\mathcal{G}_{p\overline{q}}.

By Theorem
1.3
and

3.5
, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle w^{k}\partial_{z^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{i}\overline{w}^{j}w^{k}\overline{w}^{l}{\mathrm{G}}^{\overline{q}p}{\mathrm{G}}_{i\overline{q};k}{\mathrm{G}}_{p\overline{j};\overline{l}}

5.
Appendix: complete lift of a real tangent field

Let
M
be a smooth real manifold with
\dim M=n
and
V
a smooth real tangent field on
M
.
The local coordinate presentation for the lift
\widetilde{V}
of
V
to
M
appears in
[
22
]
and
[
19
]
. To be self contained, we present a proof here.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
V=f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}
, we have
\widetilde{V}=f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}+y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j}\partial_{y^{j}}
.

Proof.
First we briefly recall how
\widetilde{V}
is defined. The tangent field
V
generates a flow of local diffeomorphisms
\rho_{t}
on
M
. Next, the tangent maps
(\rho_{t})_{*}
provide a flow of local diffeomorphisms on
TM
. At last,
\widetilde{V}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}(\rho_{t})_{*}
.

Now we impose the local coordinate

x=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n})\in
where
V
is defined. When
t
sufficiently close to
0
, we denote by
c(x,t)=(c^{1}(x,t),\cdots,c^{n}(x,t))
the integral curve of
V
, which is the solution of

(5.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c^{i}(x,t)=f^{i}(c(x,t)),\ \forall i,\quad\mbox{and}\quad c(x,0)=x.

Replace
x
in (
5.17
) with a smooth curve
x(s)
which represents the tangent vector
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}x(0)=y=(y^{i},\cdots,y^{n})
at
x=x(0)
,
and then apply
\partial_{s}|_{s=0}
to (
5.17
), we get

(5.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}c^{\prime}{}^{i}(t)=c^{\prime}{}^{j}(t)\partial_{c^{j}}f^{i}(c(x,t)),\ \forall i,\quad\mbox{and}\quad c^{\prime}(0)=y,

with
{c}^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}c(x(s),t)
.
In particular,
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm dt}}|_{t=0}c^{\prime}{}^{i}(t)=y^{j}\partial_{x^{j}}f^{i}(x)
. So

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|_{t=0}(\rho_{t})_{*}(x,y)=(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|_{t=0}c(t),\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|_{t=0}c^{\prime}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y^{1},\cdots,y^{n},y^{j}\partial_{x^{j}}f^{1},\cdots,y^{j}\partial_{x^{j}}f^{n})

has the local presentation
\widetilde{V}=y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}+y^{j}\partial_{x^{j}}V^{i}\partial_{y^{i}}

Fundamental knowledge in Lie theory implies that lifts commute with the bracket. Here we present an explicit proof in the context.

Lemma 5.2
.

For smooth real tangent fields
U
and
V
on
M
, we have

[\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}]=\widetilde{[U,V]}
.

Proof.
Assume the following local presentations,
U=f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}
and
V=g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}
. Then the lift of
[U,V]=(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{x^{j}}

is given by

(5.19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{[U,V]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{x^{j}}+y^{k}\partial_{x^{k}}(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{y^{j}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{x^{j}}+y^{k}(\partial_{x^{k}}f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-\partial_{x^{k}}g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})

\displaystyle+y^{k}(f^{i}\partial_{x^{k}}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{k}}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{y^{j}}.

On the other hand,

(5.20)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}+y^{k}\partial_{x^{k}}f^{j}\partial_{y^{j}},g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}+y^{k}\partial_{x^{k}}g^{j}\partial_{y^{j}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{x^{j}}+y^{k}(f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}\partial_{x^{k}}g^{j}-g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}\partial_{x^{k}}f^{j})

\displaystyle+y^{k}(\partial_{x^{k}}f^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}g^{j}-\partial_{x^{k}}g^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{j})\partial_{y^{j}}.

Comparing (
5.19
) and (
5.20
), Lemma
5.2
is proved.

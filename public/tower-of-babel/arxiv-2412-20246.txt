H H -covering of a supermanifold
Fernando A.Z. Santamaria and Elizaveta Vishnyakova

Abstract
. We develop the theory of H H -graded manifolds for any finitely generated abelian group, using tools from representation theory. Furthermore, we introduce and investigate the notion of H H -graded coverings of supermanifolds in the case where H H is a finite abelian group.



1.
Introduction

The category of
A
-graded manifolds, where
A
is an abelian group or an abelian monoid, was studied by several authors; see, for instance,
[
F
,
J
,
KS
,
Vys
]
, see also
[
AKT
,
BHM
,
Ker
,
Ly
,
RW
]
. An
A
-graded manifold is a ringed space, which is locally isomorphic to a graded domain
(S,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}})
. An element
f
in the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}
of an
A
-graded domain is usually assumed to be a series in colored coordinates, that is in coordinates of weight
a\in A\setminus\{e\}
. An alternative approach, based on the use of a grading operator, is presented in
[
GG
]
, see also the references therein for further literature.

In this paper, we consider
H
-graded domains, where
H
is a finitely generated abelian group, where elements of the structure sheaf are smooth or holomorphic functions (not series) with respect to local graded coordinates. In more detail, let
G
be a finitely generated abelian group and
H=\operatorname{Ch}(G)
be its dual group, that is, the group of complex characters of
G
. First, we develop the theory of graded manifolds for a finite abelian group
G
. In this case, we have
G\simeq H
.
The case of a finite abelian group is very special due to the fact that any representation of such a group may be decomposed into a direct sum of irreducible representations. Our definition of a
H
-graded domain is as follows. Let a homomorphism
\phi:H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be fixed. We start with a representation of
G
in a vector space
V
. Then
V
is naturally
H
- and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded in the following way

V=\bigoplus_{\chi\in H}V_{\chi},\quad V_{\bar{i}}=\bigoplus_{\phi(\chi)=\bar{i}}V_{\chi}.

Using this data we define a
H
-graded domain
(U,\mathcal{O})
, where
U\subset V_{e}
, where
e\in H
is the identity, and
\mathcal{O}
is the inverse image sheaf of the sheaf of smooth or holomorphic superfunctions on
V
, see the main text for details. Using the representation theory for finite groups we decompose the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
into
H
-homogeneous components

\mathcal{O}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in H}\mathcal{O}_{\chi}.

This defines a
H
-gradation in the structure sheaf. We call a function
F\in\mathcal{O}
homogeneous of weight
\chi\in H
if

g\cdot F=\chi(g)F.

In the last section, we generalize this approach to any finitely generated abelian group. In this case, we do not have a decomposition of the structure sheaf into homogeneous components. However, since the notion of a homogeneous function is defined, we can define a homogeneous morphism of
H
-graded domains, and hence a
H
-graded manifold. Our approach is related to, but distinct from, that of
[
GG
]
; see the final section for details. Our results can also be applied to construct graded group objects for graded Lie (super)algebras; see
[
AKY
,
DEl
,
El
]
.

Another result of this paper is as follows. In
[
Vi2
]
, the notion of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded covering space for a supermanifold was introduced, and later extended in
[
Vi3
]
to the case of graded manifolds. The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded covering of a supermanifold can be seen as the space of
\mathcal{N}
-paths in a supermanifold, where
\mathcal{N}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold. Such structures also appear in classical geometry:
m
-jet space of a manifold
X
, that is
Spec(\mathbb{K}[t]/t^{m+1})
-paths in a (algebraic) manifold
X
, i.e. morphisms of
Spec(\mathbb{K}[t]/t^{m+1})
to
X
; the arc space of
X
, that is
Spec(\mathbb{K}[[t]])
-paths in a (algebraic) manifold
X
, where
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
is the ring of formal series in
t
, see
[
Ish
, Definition 2.1]
. One also considers the loop space of
X
, that is
\mathbb{K}((t))
-points in
X
, where
\mathbb{K}((t))
is the ring of formal Laurent series in
t
, where it allows finitely many negative powers of
t
, but infinitely many nonnegative ones, see
[
AM
, Proposition 1.5]
and
[
KV
]
. The jet space and arc space of
X
satisfy a similar universal property as considered in this paper, see
[
AM
, Theorem 1.1]
, see also
[
AM
, Proposition 1.5]
for the loop space in the affine case.

The
H
-covering space constructed in this paper classifies the space of
\mathcal{N}
-paths in a manifold or supermanifold, where
\mathcal{N}
is a
H
-graded manifold for a finite abelian group
H
. In other words,
H
-covering space classifies morphisms from a
H
-graded manifold to a supermanifold. In more detail, for any supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
we construct a
H
-graded manifold
\mathcal{P}
with a covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
, which satisfies the universal property: any
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded morphism of a
H
-graded manifold to the supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
can be uniquely lifted to a
H
-graded morphism
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{P}
. We have a similar universal property for a topological covering of a usual manifold. This also explains the name for the ’covering space’. For instance, a graded covering
\mathcal{P}
is unique up to isomorphism, and any morphism
\phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a
H
-graded manifold, can be factored through the covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
. We expect that important graded
H
-spaces can be interpreted as
H
-coverings of some (super)manifolds.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Characters of finite abelian groups

We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
and for
G=(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let
G
be a finite abelian group. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
the cyclic group of order
q\in\mathbb{N}
. The structure theorem for finite abelian groups states that every abelian group is isomorphic to a direct product of cyclic
groups. In other words,

G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{t}},

where the numbers
q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}
are powers of prime numbers (not necessarily distinct); see
[
KuS
, Chapter 2]
. For an introduction to the character theory of finite groups, we refer to
[
I
]
.

A group homomorphism
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
is called a
character of
G
. If
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}
are two characters of
G
, we can define their product
\chi_{1}\cdot\chi_{2}
by the following formula

[\chi_{1}\cdot\chi_{2}](g)=\chi_{1}(g)\cdot\chi_{2}(g),\quad g\in G.

The set of all characters
\operatorname{Ch}(G)
is an abelian group with respect to the introduced operation. For a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with a generator
u
we have
\chi(u)^{q}=1
, so
\chi(u)
is a root of unity. The group
\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
can be identified with the roots of unity group

\mathbb{Z}_{q}\simeq\{e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{q}}\,\,|\,\,k=0,1,\ldots,q-1\}\subset\mathbb{C}.

Indeed, for
\mathbb{Z}_{q}=\langle u\rangle
, the map
\chi\mapsto\chi(u)
defines an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q})\to\{e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{q}}\,\,|\,\,k=0,1,\ldots,q-1\}.

We also have
\mathbb{Z}_{q}\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
. Furthermore, if
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, then any character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a product of characters
\chi_{i}:G_{i}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
, where
\chi_{i}=\chi|_{G_{i}}
. In other words,

\operatorname{Ch}(G_{1}\times G_{2})\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Ch}(G_{2}).

In particular for an abelian group
G
we have

\operatorname{Ch}(G)=\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{t}})\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{q_{t}})\simeq G.

This isomorphism is not unique.

Remark 1
.

In the case
G=(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
any character is pure real.

2.2.
\operatorname{Ch}(G)
-graded vector superspaces

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and
H:=\operatorname{Ch}(G)
be its character group equipped with a surjective homomorphism

H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\},\quad\chi\mapsto|\chi|.

We will call the image of
\chi\in H
by our homomorphism the parity of
\chi
and denote this image by
|\chi|
.
Now consider any complex representation of
G
in a non-necessary finite-dimensional vector space
W
. In the case
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
we can assume that
W
is real.

Theorem 2
.

There is the following decomposition

(1)

W=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}(G)}W_{\chi},\quad W_{\chi}=\{v\in W\,\,|\,\,g\cdot v=\chi(g)v\,\}.

Proof.

If
v\in W
is any element, then for
v_{\chi}\in W
defined by the following formula

(2)

v_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)(g^{-1}\cdot v)

we have
h\cdot v_{\chi}=\chi(h)v
for any
h\in G
. In other words,
v_{\chi}\in W_{\chi}
.
Furthermore, we have

v=\sum_{\chi\in H}v_{\chi}.

Indeed, for any
g\in G\setminus\{e\}
there exists
\chi_{0}\in H
such that
\chi_{0}(g)\neq 1
. Indeed, assuming the opposite, we find that there exists
g\in G\setminus\{e\}
such that for any character
\chi\in H
we have
\chi(g)=1
. Therefore,

G\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(G)\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(G/G^{\prime})\simeq G/G^{\prime},

where
G^{\prime}
is the non-trivial subgroup generated by
g
. This is a contradiction.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in H}\chi(g)=\sum_{\chi\in H}[\chi_{0}\cdot\chi](g)=\chi_{0}(g)\sum_{\chi\in H}\chi(g).

Therefore,
\sum\limits_{\chi\in H}\chi(g)=0
, where
g\neq e
, and clearly
\sum\limits_{\chi\in H}\chi(e)=|G|
. Summing up,

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in H}v_{\chi}=\sum_{\chi\in H}\Big(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)(g^{-1}\cdot v)\Big)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\Big(\sum_{\chi\in H}\chi(g)\Big)(g^{-1}\cdot v)=v.

∎

Now we apply the result of Theorem
2
to the finite-dimensional case. Consider a finite-dimensional representation
V
of
G
. Later we will use
V
to construct a graded domain, which is assumed to be finite-dimensional.
The decomposition (
1
) leads to the following decomposition

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}(G)}V_{\chi},\quad V_{\chi}=\{v\in V\,\,|\,\,g\cdot v=\chi(g)v\,\},

and to the following decomposition

(3)

V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}},\quad V_{\overline{i}}=\bigoplus_{|\chi|=\bar{i}}V_{\chi},\quad\bar{i}\in\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}.

An element
v\in V_{\chi}
is called
homogeneous
of weight
\chi
and of parity
|\chi|
. In more detail,
v\in V
is called homogeneous of weight
\chi
if for any
g\in G
the following holds

(4)

g\cdot v=\chi(g)v,\quad g\in G.

Clearly,
\dim V_{\chi}<\infty
for any
\chi\in H
.

Remark 3
.

If
v=\sum\limits_{\chi\in H}v_{\chi}
, see (
2
), the action of
G
on
V
is also given by the formula (
4
). In other words,

g\cdot v=g\cdot\sum_{\chi\in H}v_{\chi}=\sum_{\chi\in H}\chi(g)v_{\chi}.

This means that any complex representation
V
of
G
has this form and, in particular, has a homogeneous basis.

Example 4
.

Let
K=\{e,a,b,ab\}
be the Klein group. Recall that
K
is abelian and is defined by the following relations

a^{2}=b^{2}=(ab)^{2}=e.

Let us describe the group of characters
H=\operatorname{Ch}(K)
, see Section
2.1
. Define characters
\chi_{a},\chi_{b}:K\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
by the following formulas

\displaystyle\chi_{a}(a)

\displaystyle=-1,\quad\chi_{a}(b)=1;\quad\chi_{b}(a)=1,\quad\chi_{b}(b)=-1;

\displaystyle\chi_{e}(a)=\chi_{e}(b)=1;\quad\chi_{ab}:=\chi_{a}\cdot\chi_{b}.

Assume that
|\chi_{a}|=|\chi_{b}|=\bar{1}
,
|\chi_{e}|=|\chi_{ab}|=\bar{0}
. Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
H
-graded vector superspace. That is

V=V_{\chi_{e}}\oplus V_{\chi_{a}}\oplus V_{\chi_{b}}\oplus V_{\chi_{ab}},\quad V_{\overline{0}}=V_{\chi_{e}}\oplus V_{\chi_{ab}},\quad V_{\overline{1}}=V_{\chi_{a}}\oplus V_{\chi_{b}}.

According to Remark
3
, in this case a
K
-action on
V
is given by Formula (
4
). More precisely we have

\displaystyle g\cdot v_{\chi_{c}}=\chi_{c}(g)v_{\chi_{c}},\quad g\in K,\quad v_{\chi_{c}}\in V_{\chi_{c}}.

We see that the map
c\mapsto\chi_{c}
defines a homomorphism
K\to\operatorname{Ch}(K)
. Using this homomorphism,
V
can be regarded as a
G
-graded vector space. However, such a homomorphism is clearly not unique. For example, another homomorphism is defined by
a\mapsto\chi_{b}
,
b\mapsto\chi_{a}
, which leads to another
G
-grading on
V
.

We can naturally define an action of
G
on
V^{*}
in the following way:

[g\cdot f](v):=f(g^{-1}\cdot v),\quad f\in V^{*}.

The vector space
V^{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{\chi\in H}V^{*}_{\chi}
is
H
-graded. An element
f\in V^{*}
is called homogeneous of weight
\chi\in H
, if
g\cdot f=\chi(g)f
for any
g\in G
. We can naturally identify
V^{*}_{\chi}
with
(V_{\chi^{-1}})^{*}
.

Remark 5
.

In the case
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
we can consider a real representation
V
of
G
, since all characters
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
are real in this case.

2.3.
\operatorname{Ch}(G)
-decomposition of the sheaf of smooth or holomorphic functions on
V

Let
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{t}}
be a finite abelian group that acts in a complex finite-dimensional vector space
V
. As in Section
2.2
,
H=\operatorname{Ch}(G)
is the dual group of
G
with fixed homomorphism
H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
\chi\mapsto|\chi|
, and
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is an
H
-graded vector superspace. Clearly, any homogeneous subspace
V_{\chi}
is
G
-invariant. Since
V
is a vector superspace, we can consider the sheaf of superfunctions on
V
. By definition a superfunction
F
on
V
is an element in
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the sheaf of smooth
complex-valued
or holomorphic functions on
V_{\bar{0}}
. In more detail, since
V_{\bar{0}}
is a complex vector space, we can identify it with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for some
n
. Then a smooth function
F:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is a complex-valued smooth function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. The same is true for odd variables: if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V^{*}_{\bar{1}}=m
, then a smooth function depends on
2m
odd variables. In the case
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, we also can consider a real representation
V
and real-valued even and odd smooth functions.

If
U
is a
G
-invariant open subset in
V_{\bar{0}}
we define an action on
\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
by

\displaystyle g\cdot(f\otimes\zeta)=f\circ g^{-1}\otimes g\cdot\zeta,\quad f\in\mathcal{F}(U),\,\,\,\zeta\in\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}}).

Definition 6
.

We call a superfunction
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
, where
U
is a
G
-invariant open set in
V_{\bar{0}}
, homogeneous of weight
\chi\in H=\operatorname{Ch}(G)
if the following holds

g\cdot F(v)=\chi(g)F(v),\quad v\in V

for any
g\in G
.

Let
U
be a
G
-invariant open set in
V_{\bar{0}}
and let
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
. We can decompose
F
into homogeneous components, see Theorem
2
, in the following way

(5)

F=\sum_{\chi\in H}F_{\chi},\quad F_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)[g^{-1}\cdot F].

A standard computation shows that

h\cdot F_{\chi}=\chi(h)F_{\chi}\quad\text{for any $h\in G$}.

The decomposition (
5
) leads to the following decomposition

(6)

\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})=\bigoplus_{\chi\in H}(\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}}))_{\chi}.

Note that we can define such a decomposition only for
G
-invariant sets
U
.

Definition
6
needs some comments. Indeed, now the parity of a homogeneous element
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
of weight
\chi
is induced by the parity of a character
|\chi|
and by the degree
p
of
\bigwedge^{p}(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
. In fact, both definitions coincide. It is sufficient to show the following proposition.

Proposition 7
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)
and let
\chi\in H
with
|\chi|=\bar{1}
. Then
F_{\chi}=0
.

Proof.

We need the following fact from character theory. Let
G_{0}
be any subgroup in
G
. Then we get the following two exact sequences of groups

\displaystyle e\to G_{0}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G/G_{0}\to e,\quad e\to\operatorname{Ch}(G/G_{0})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ch}(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ch}(G_{0})\to e.

And such exact sequences are in bijection. Therefore for the exact sequence of groups of characters

\displaystyle e\to H_{0}\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to e

we can find the corresponding exact sequence of subgroups

\displaystyle e\to G_{0}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G/G_{0}\to e.

such that
H_{0}=\operatorname{Ch}(G/G_{0})
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(G_{0})
. We can also describe the subgroup
G_{0}
in the following way

G_{0}=\{g\in G\,\,|\,\,\chi(g)=1\,\,\,\text{for any}\,\,\chi\in H_{0}\}.

Since
\operatorname{Ch}(G_{0})\simeq G_{0}
, we have
G_{0}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Let
G_{0}=\{e,a\}
.

Let us fix
\chi\in H
with
|\chi|=\bar{1}
. Since
|\chi|=\bar{1}
, we have
\chi(a)=-1
. (Otherwise,
\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}(G/G_{0})=H_{0}
.) The group
G_{0}
acts on
G
. Hence
G
is divided into
G_{0}
-orbits
\{g_{i},g_{i}\cdot a\}
for some
g_{i}\in G
, which do not intersect. We have

\chi(g_{i})[(g_{i})^{-1}\cdot F]+\chi(g_{i}\cdot a)[(g_{i}\cdot a)^{-1}\cdot F]=\chi(g_{i})[(g_{i})^{-1}\cdot F](1-1)=0.

Here we used the fact
a\cdot F=F
since
F
depends only on coordinates of weight
\chi_{0}\in H_{0}
. We see that the sum (
5
) is zero for such
\chi
. This implies that
F_{\chi}=0
.
∎

Remark 8
.

If
F_{i}
is a homogeneous superfunction of weight
\chi_{i}
,
i=1,2
, then
F_{1}\cdot F_{2}
is a homogeneous superfunction of weight
\chi_{1}\cdot\chi_{2}
. Furthermore, let
(x_{i}^{h_{1}})
, where
|h_{1}|=\bar{0}
, and
(\xi_{j}^{h_{2}})
, where
|h_{2}|=\bar{1}
, is a basis of
V^{*}_{h_{1}}
and
V^{*}_{h_{2}}
,
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
, respectively. By Definition
6
, any
x_{i}^{h_{1}}
is a homogeneous superfunction of weight
h_{1}\in H
and
\xi_{j}^{h_{2}}
is a homogeneous superfunction of weight
h_{2}\in H
. Hence, the weight of a monomial

x_{i_{1}}^{g_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}^{g_{s}}\cdot\xi_{j_{1}}^{h_{1}}\cdots\xi_{j_{t}}^{h_{t}}

is the product
g_{1}\cdots g_{s}\cdot h_{1}\cdots h_{t}\in H
of weights.

We finish this section with an example.

Example 9
.

K
-decomposition of the sheaf of superfunctions.

Consider the case of the Klein group
K
, see Example
4
, with a representation of
K
in
V
. Then
V
is also a
H
-graded, where
H=\operatorname{Ch(K)}
and
H
-grading is defined in Theorem
2
, see Example
4
. Since
K\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
, we can assume that
V
is real. Let us take
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
, where
U\subset V_{\bar{0}}
is
K
-invariant, and
\mathcal{F}
is the sheaf of real-valued smooth functions in
V_{\bar{0}}
.

By Remark
8
, we have for any
h\in H

[\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})]_{h}=\bigoplus_{h_{1}\cdot h_{2}=h}\mathcal{F}(U)_{h_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})_{h_{2}}.

Further elements from
\bigwedge(V^{*}_{\bar{1}})
are Grassmann polynomials, their weights are determined by weights of generators, see Remark
8
. Let us decompose an element
F\in\mathcal{F}(U)
into homogeneous components. We use notations and parity agreement from Example
4
.

Assume that
F(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}(U)
, where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p})
is a basis in
V_{\chi_{e}}
and
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{q})
is a basis in
V_{\chi_{ab}}
. (Recall that both
\chi_{e}
and
\chi_{ab}
are assumed to be even.) Applying Formula (
5
), we get

\displaystyle F_{\chi_{a}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(\chi_{a}(e)F(e\cdot x,e\cdot y)+\chi_{a}(a)F(a\cdot x,a\cdot y)+

\displaystyle\chi_{a}(b)F(b\cdot x,b\cdot y)+\chi_{a}(ab)F(ab\cdot x,ab\cdot y)\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(F(x,y)-F(x,-y)+F(x,-y)-F(x,y)\big)=0.

Here
-x:=(-x_{1},\cdots,-x_{p})
and the same notation for
y
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle F_{\chi_{e}}=\frac{1}{2}(F(x,y)+F(x,-y)),\quad F_{\chi_{b}}=0,\quad F_{\chi_{ab}}=\frac{1}{2}(F(x,y)-F(x,-y)).

Summing up, we got the following decomposition of the superfunction
F
into homogeneous components:

\displaystyle F=\frac{1}{2}(F(x,y)+F(x,-y))+\frac{1}{2}(F(x,y)-F(x,-y)).

This decomposition corresponds to the well-known decomposition of a function into even and odd parts.

2.4.
Taylor series of homogeneous components

We use the notation of Section
2.3
.
Let us study the Taylor series. We start with the following example.

Warning.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}=V_{e}\oplus V_{\chi}\oplus V_{\chi^{2}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-graded vector space. (The choice of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is not essential.) Let
\dim V_{\chi}=1
and
(x)
be the basis of
V_{\chi}
. Denote by
(y)
, where
y\in(V_{\chi})^{*}
the dual co-basis. That is
y(x)=1
.
We note that one may try to write the Taylor series in following form

\sin z=z-\frac{z^{3}}{3!}+\frac{z^{5}}{5!}+\cdots

for
z\in V_{\chi}\simeq\mathbb{C}.
This is wrong since

is not defined and since for

we get the following wrong equality

\sin(a^{-1}\cdot z)=a\cdot\sin z\neq a\cdot z-\frac{(a\cdot z)^{3}}{3!}+\frac{(a\cdot z)^{5}}{5!}+\cdots.

Consider the function
\sin\circ y:V_{\chi}\to\mathbb{C}
, given by
\sin\circ y(\alpha x)=\sin(\alpha)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Then we can write the corresponding Taylor series in the following form

\displaystyle\sin\circ y=y-\frac{y^{3}}{3!}+\frac{y^{5}}{5!}+\cdots.

We see that for any
\alpha x
the value of the function on the right-hand side and the left-hand side is the same. We also see that the Taylor series of the composition
\sin\circ y
has the usual form.

Choose a homogeneous basis
(x^{h^{-1}}_{i})
of
V_{h^{-1}}
and the dual co-basis
(y^{h}_{j})
of
V^{*}_{h}\simeq(V_{h^{-1}})^{*}
, where
h\in H_{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\phi
. Let
F\circ(y^{h}_{j})\in\mathcal{F}(U)
, where
U
is a
G
-invariant open set as above. For simplicity, let us use the following notation:
z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}
for
(y^{h}_{j})
, where
h\neq e
, and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{q}
for
(y^{e}_{j})
. In addition, we put
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q})
,
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{p})
,
n=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
and
z^{n}=z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{p}^{n_{p}}
. Since our function is smooth or holomorphic, we can consider its Taylor series with respect to
z
in a neighborhood of the point
(t,0)
. We have

\displaystyle F\circ(t,z)\mapsto T_{F}(t,z)=\sum a_{n}(t)z^{n},

where
a_{n}(t)
are smooth or holomorphic functions in

. Clearly, the
G
-action in

induces the
G
-action on Taylor series.

Remark 10
.

In the smooth category the Taylor series above is the Taylor series with respect to real variables. For example, for
F(z)
, were the weight of
z=x+iy
is not
e
, our Taylor series is in fact the Taylor series in real coordinates
(x,y)
. Recall that we also can rewrite this Taylor series with respect to variables
z,\bar{z}=x-iy
.

Lemma 11
.

We have
g\cdot T_{F}=T_{g\cdot F}
for any
g\in G
.

Proof.

First of all we have

\displaystyle[g\cdot F]\circ(t,z)(u,v)=F\circ(t,z)(g^{-1}u,g^{-1}v)=F\circ(g\cdot t,g\cdot z)(u,v),

for any point
(u,v)
in a neighborhood of the point
(t,0)
. This means that

[g\cdot F]\circ(t,z)=F\circ(g\cdot t,g\cdot z).

Hence,

\displaystyle T_{[g\cdot F]\circ(t,z)}=T_{F\circ(g\cdot t,g\cdot z)}=\sum a_{n}(t)(g\cdot z)^{n}=g\cdot T_{F\circ(t,z)}.

Note that
g\cdot t=t
for any
g\in G
.
∎

Now let
T_{F}
be the Taylor series of
F
. We apply Formula (
2
) to
T_{F}
to get its
H
-decomposition

\displaystyle T_{F}=\sum_{\chi\in H}(T_{F})_{\chi},\quad(T_{F})_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{g\in G}\chi(g)\,\,[g^{-1}\cdot T_{F}].

Lemma
11
implies the following proposition.

Proposition 12
.

We have for any
\chi\in H
.

(1)

T_{F_{\chi}}=(T_{F})_{\chi}
.

(2)

g\cdot T_{F_{\chi}}=\chi(g)T_{F_{\chi}}
for any
g\in G
.

(3)

In the notations above, let us have

T_{F_{\chi}}(t,z)=\sum a_{n}(t)z^{n}.

Then each monomial
z^{n}=z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{p}^{n_{p}}
has weight
\chi\in H
.

Proof.

(1)
First of all for functions
F_{1},F_{2}
and a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
we have

T_{F_{1}+F_{2}}=T_{F_{1}}+T_{F_{2}},\quad T_{cF}=cT_{F}.

Hence by Lemma
11
we have

\displaystyle T_{F_{\chi}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{g\in G}\chi(g)\,\,[T_{g^{-1}\cdot F}]=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{g\in G}\chi(g)\,\,[g^{-1}\cdot T_{F}]=(T_{F})_{\chi}.

(2)
The second statement is a consequence of the first one.

(3)
We have the following equality of series

\chi(g)T_{F_{\chi}}=\chi(g)\sum a_{n}(t)z^{n}=\sum a_{n}(t)(g\cdot z)^{n}=\sum a_{n}(t)c_{n}(g)z^{n},

where
c_{n}(g)\in\mathbb{C}
is a constant depending on
g
. The equality of series implies
c_{n}(g)=\chi(g)
. The result follows.
∎

Remark 13
.

Usually in the literature one considers a series as in Proposition

in homogeneous coordinates
(z_{i})
of weight defined by weights of the homogeneous coordinates. Proposition
12
implies that our definition of weights leads to the usual one for series.

Remark 18

Usually in the literature one considers another structure sheaf of a graded domain, see

,

Remark 19
.

Let us discuss another difference between our definition of the structure sheaf of a

-graded domain and the definition that is usually used in the literature; see
[

,

,
J
,

Remark 20

Using our sheaf

Proposition 21

Let

be a

Hence,
\chi(g)\neq 0

.
∎

5.4.
Examples of
H
-coverings of manifolds and supermanifolds

5.4.1.
H
-coverings for usual manifolds

The paper is devoted to the construction of
H
-coverings for supermaniolds. However, we can apply our construction to the usual manifold. In fact, consider the trivial homomorphism
\phi:H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to 0
. Let us describe the
H
-covering for
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

We fix the standard coordinates
x
and
y
for
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with transition functions

y=\frac{1}{x}.

Let us apply the covering projection morphism
\mathsf{p}
to our transition functions.

\mathsf{p}^{*}(y)=\mathsf{p}^{*}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big),\quad\text{or}\quad y^{\bar{0}}+y^{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{x^{\bar{0}}+x^{\bar{1}}},

where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
. Finally, we obtain the following
H
-graded morphism, given in local graded coordinates by

\displaystyle y^{\bar{0}}=\frac{x^{\bar{0}}}{(x^{\bar{0}})^{2}-(x^{\bar{1}})^{2}},\quad y^{\bar{1}}=\frac{-x^{\bar{1}}}{(x^{\bar{0}})^{2}-(x^{\bar{1}})^{2}}.

5.4.2.
H
-coverings for supermanifolds, example

Let us take
H=\mathbb{Z}_{4}=\{\tilde{0},\tilde{1},\tilde{2},\tilde{3}\}
and
\phi:H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defined by
\phi(\tilde{1})=\bar{1}
. We apply our the covering construction to the projective superspace
\mathbb{CP}^{1|1}
.
We fix the standard coordinates
(x,\xi)
and
(y,\eta)
for
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with transition functions

y=\frac{1}{x},\quad\eta=\frac{1}{x}\xi.

Applying the covering construction we get

\displaystyle y^{\tilde{0}}=\frac{x^{\tilde{0}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}},\quad y^{\tilde{2}}=\frac{-x^{\tilde{2}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta^{\tilde{1}}=\frac{x^{\tilde{0}}\xi^{\tilde{1}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}}-\frac{x^{\tilde{2}}\xi^{\tilde{3}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta^{\tilde{3}}=\frac{x^{\tilde{0}}\xi^{\tilde{3}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}}-\frac{x^{\tilde{2}}\xi^{\tilde{1}}}{(x^{\tilde{0}})^{2}-(x^{\tilde{2}})^{2}}.

6.
Graded manifolds and representation theory of finitely generated abelian groups

In this section, we will see that the theory of
H
-graded manifolds is closely related to the representation theory of finitely generated abelian groups
H
.

6.1.
Different approaches to the concept of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain

In this section, we give a definition of a
H
-graded domain for any finitely generated abelian group
H
and compare it with some other definitions. For example, we will see that in the case of a finite abelian group
H
, it is necessary to consider a complex version of the theory.

6.1.1.
A naive approach

Let us first consider the case of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain. A naive definition of this domain is as follows. We start with a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

V^{*}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{*}_{k},

and choose a basis
x_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{\dim V^{*}_{k}}^{k}
in any
V^{*}_{k}
. The number
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is called the
weight
of the variable
x_{i}^{k}
. Then we define the weight of a monomial in the natural way. In this case any polynomial or series in our variables
(x^{k}_{i})
can be decomposed into a (infinite or finite) sum of homogeneous components.

6.1.2.
An approach via grading operator

Another approach to the notion of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain, a weight, and a homogeneous component of a function is developed in
[
GG
]
, see also the references cited therein. The graded structure can be encoded by the following vector field, called a grading operator or a weight vector field

(16)

v=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}kx^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}.

(We have dropped the subscript here.)
We have
v(x^{p})=px^{p}.
Now a smooth or holomorphic function
F
depending on variables
(x^{k}_{i})
is called homogeneous of weight
p
if
v(F)=pF
.

Remark 38
.

The weight
p
does not need to be an integer, but any real number
p\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, even in the case where the weights of the local coordinates are integers, a smooth function may have any real weight
r\in\mathbb{R}
. For example, the function
F=(x^{k})^{\frac{r}{k}}
has weight
r
.

6.1.3.
An approach via character group

A grading operator
v
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
V^{*}
. Indeed, consider the automorphism
\Gamma:=\exp(v)
. If
F
is a homogeneous function of weight
p
, for example a local coordinate, we have

\displaystyle\exp(v)(F)=\mathrm{id}(F)+v(F)+\frac{1}{2}v\circ v(F)+\cdots=

\displaystyle F+pF+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}F+\cdots=e^{p}F.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
V
is defined as follows

\mathbb{Z}\ni 1\longmapsto\Gamma,\quad\mathbb{Z}\ni n\longmapsto\Gamma^{n},\quad\Gamma^{n}(x^{p})=e^{np}x^{p},

and by duality on
V
. Since the
\mathbb{Z}
-action in
V
is defined, it indices the
\mathbb{Z}
-action in the algebra of smooth functions on
V
.

Remark 39
.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
V^{*}
constructed above is completely reducible. The irreducible components corresponds to the real characters

\chi_{p}:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\times},\quad\mathbb{Z}\ni n\longmapsto\chi_{p}(n)=(e^{p})^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}.

Now we call a function
F
homogeneous of weight
\chi_{p}
if

g\cdot F=\chi_{p}(g)F,\quad g\in\mathbb{Z}.

In other words,
F
is homogeneous if it generate an irreducible
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Definition 40
.

The real (or complex) character group
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z})
(respectively,
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{Z})
) of
\mathbb{Z}
, also called the real (or complex) Pontryagin dual of
\mathbb{Z}
, is the group of all homomorphisms
\chi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{\times}
(respectively,
\chi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}).

We see that if a real grading operator
v
as in Section
6.1.2
is defined on
V^{*}
, then
V^{*}
is a real completely reducible
\mathbb{Z}
-representation. Conversely, if
V^{*}
is a real completely reducible
\mathbb{Z}
-module with only positive characters, that is
\chi(1)>0
, then we can construct a grading operator
v=\log(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is the image of
1\in\mathbb{Z}
. If some characters of
V^{*}
are negative, we cannot define a corresponding grading operator in general. Indeed, consider an example.

Example 41
.

Let
V^{*}
be a real completely reducible
\mathbb{Z}
-representation with characters
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{-1}
. In other words,

\chi_{1}(1)=1
, the trivial character, and
\chi_{-1}(1)=-1.

Let
x^{\chi_{1}}
and
x^{\chi_{-1}}
be graded coordinates. (Assume that
\dim V^{*}_{\chi_{1}}=\dim V^{*}_{\chi_{-1}}=1
.) In an additive picture
x^{\chi_{1}}
must have weight
0
, since
\chi_{1}\chi_{1}=\chi_{1}
, and the weights of
x^{\chi_{1}}
and
(x^{\chi_{1}})^{2}
are equal. Let
x^{\chi_{-1}}
have a certain weight
A\in\mathbb{R}
with respect to some grading operator. Since
(x^{\chi_{-1}})^{2}
has weight
\chi_{1}
, we get
A+A=0
, and hence
A=0
, which is a contradiction, because in the multiplicative picture
{\chi_{-1}}\neq{\chi_{1}}
.

In summary, the approach through completely reducible real
\mathbb{Z}
-representations is more general than via grading operators with real eigenvalues. In the next sections, we will see that in the case of a finitely generated abelian group, especially a finite abelian group, we need to work over
\mathbb{C}
.

6.2.
Some useful facts from the representation theory of finitely generated abelian groups

It is well known that any finitely generated abelian group
H
is isomorphic to

H\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}}.

Further we will need the following facts.

(1)

Any irreducible complex representation of
H
is
1
-dimensional, and corresponds to a complex character:

h\cdot v=\chi(g)v,\quad\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(H).

This is a consequence of Schur’s lemma.

(2)

Any real irreducible representation of
H
is
1
- or
2
-dimensional.
A real
1
-dimensional irreducible representation of
H
corresponds to a real-valued character
\chi:H\to\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. A real
2
-dimensional irreducible representation of
H
corresponds to a pair of complex conjugate characters
(\chi,\bar{\chi})
that are not real.

(3)

We have in both real and complex cases

\operatorname{Ch}(G_{1}\times G_{2})\simeq\operatorname{Ch}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Ch}(G_{2}).

(4)

We have

\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p},\quad\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\{\pm 1\},\quad\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{\times},\quad\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

For instance, for a finite abelian group, there are sufficient irreducibles only in the complex case.

6.3.
H
-graded domains and manifolds

In this section, we will define a
H
-graded manifold, where
H
is any finitely generated abelian group, using representation theory. Note that it is not clear how to use a grading operator in this case: we have to replace
k
in Formula (
16
) by an element
h\in H
, which is not defined.

Let
H
be a finitely generated abelian group and let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a completely reducible complex finite-dimensional representation of
H
. (We assume here that
H\cdot V_{\bar{i}}\subset V_{\bar{i}}
. We can omit this condition considering the group
\tilde{H}=H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where the factor
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defines the parity of an element.) By observations of Section
6.2
this representation always has the following form

V=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)}V_{\chi},

where
V_{\chi}
is the sum of equivalent irreducible representations corresponding to the same complex character
\chi
. Note that above we considered a different situation: now
V_{\chi}
may have non trivial even and odd part simultaneously.

If
W
is a
H
-invariant open set in
V_{\bar{0}}
, then the algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(W)
of complex-valued smooth (or holomorphic) superfunctions is a complex
H
-representation. (Note that a function
F(z_{1}+iz_{2})
is called smooth if it is smooth as a function of two variables
(z_{1},z_{2})
.) Denote by
\chi_{1}
the trivial character of
H
.

Definition 42
.

A
H
-graded domain is a ringed space
\mathcal{V}:=(U,\mathcal{O})
, where
U\subset V_{\chi_{1}}\cap V_{\bar{0}}
is an open set and
\mathcal{O}
is the sheaf generated by the presheaf

U\supset U^{\prime}\mapsto\lim\limits_{\iota(U^{\prime})\subset W}\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(W),

where
U^{\prime}\subset U
is open,
W
is a
H
-invariant open set in
V_{\bar{0}}
containing
U^{\prime}
and
\iota
is the natural inclusion.

Remark 43
.

We can replace
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(W)
by the algebra of polynomials or formal series. In the case
H=\mathbb{Z}^{p}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{q}
we may assume that
V
is pure real, since in this case we have enough elements in
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{R}}(H)
.

Remark 44
.

As we saw in Section
6.1.3
, we get the previous definition of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain if we assume that all our irreducibles are real
1
-dimensional and correspond to real-valued positive characters. In the case of a finite abelian group
H
this assumption is very restrictive, since
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\{\pm 1\}
. Thus, in the real case for
H
finite abelian, a meaningful theory is obtained only in the case of a group
H=\mathbb{Z}^{q}_{2}
. Moreover, typically, one considers the sheaf of series (or polynomials) with respect to coordinates of weight
\chi\neq\chi_{1}
. In this paper, we also develop a smooth approach.

A
morphism of graded domains
is an
H
-equivariant morphism of the corresponding ringed spaces with conditions as in Section
3.2
. In particular, in coordinates any morphism is given by
H
-homogeneous functions. Now, a
H
-graded manifold for any finitely generated abelian
H
can be defined as in Section
4.1
.

Remark 45
.

Let
H
be a finite abelian group. Consider the naive definition of a
H
-graded domain, see Section
6.1.1
. As above, let us choose a basis
x^{g_{1}}_{i_{1}},\ldots,x^{g_{k}}_{i_{k}}
of a real
H
-graded vector space
V^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}=\bigoplus_{g\in H}V^{*}_{g}
, where
g_{j}\in H
is the weight of the variable
x^{g_{j}}_{i_{j}}
. Observe that it is not clear how to define a homogeneous smooth function in the variables
(x^{g_{j}}_{i_{j}})
via a grading operator. Further, consider the complexification
V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}:=V^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Now we can define a
H
-action in
V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
by

h\cdot x^{\chi_{j}}_{i_{j}}=\chi_{i}(h)x^{\chi_{j}}_{i_{j}}

and repeat our construction of a
H
-graded domain.
(Here, we replace
g_{i}
by characters using the fact
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)\simeq H
.) We see that
V^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a choice of a real form of a graded domain. Hence, we obtain the naive definition by assuming that a real form of
V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
is chosen and we consider only real polynomials or real series in variables
x^{\chi_{i}}
,
\chi_{i}\neq\chi_{1}
. Note also that the
H
-action does not preserve this real form, and hence a definition of a homogeneous function via characters does not work either.

More general for complex-analytic graded manifold
\mathcal{M}
in our sense, if in any graded chart a real form as above is fixed and transition functions preserve these real forms, then we can define a real form
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathcal{M}
. This is a real-analytic
H
-graded manifold, which is usually considered in the literature. We note that, in general, we cannot expect that a real form of a graded manifold exists.
Conversely, if we have a real-analytic
H
-graded manifold, then the question of existence of its complexification is related to the corresponding problem in the nongraded case, see
[
BW
,
Gr
,
Kul
]
.

6.4.
About a decomposition of a graded function into homogeneous components

We can give a definition of a homogeneous function for any
H
. Namely, a function
F
is called homogeneous of weight
\chi\in\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
if

g\cdot F=\chi(g)F,\quad g\in H.

Now the question whether a function
F
can be decomposed into a (finite or infinite) sum of homogeneous components is equivalent to the question whether the subrepresentation generated by
F
is completely reducible. A
H
-representation is always completely reducible in the case where
H
is finite abelian.

On the relative cone conjecture for families of IHS manifolds
Andreas Höring, Gianluca Pacienza, Zhixin Xie

Abstract
. We study the relative cone conjecture for families of K K -trivial varieties with vanishing irregularity. As an application we prove that the relative movable and the relative nef cone conjectures hold for fibrations in projective IHS manifolds of the 4 known deformation types.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J50, 14E07, 14J42, 14D99, 14E30, 14J27, 14J28.

1.
Introduction

By the cone theorem the Mori cone of a Fano manifold is always a rational polyhedral cone.
This no longer holds for projective manifolds with trivial canonical class
which may have a nef cone with “round” shape or infinitely many extremal rays
[
Kov94
,
Bau98
]
.
The Kawamata-Morrison cone conjecture (see
[
Mor93
,
Mor96
,
Kaw97
]
)
predicts that in this case the action of the automorphism group on the cone of effective nef classes admits a rational, polyhedral fundamental domain. To state the conjecture more precisely and more generally (following Kawamata
[
Kaw97
]
and Totaro
[
Tot10
]
), we formalise the set-up.

1.1
.

Definition.

A klt K-trivial fibration is a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pair
(X,B)
,
endowed with a fibration

\pi\colon X\rightarrow S

over a quasi-projective variety
S

such that
K_{X}+B\equiv_{\pi}0
.

Remark.

Since the nonvanishing conjecture is known for klt pairs that are numerically trivial, e.g.
[
FG12
, Theorem 3.1]
, the assumption implies
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R},\pi}0

The Kawamata-Morrison cone conjecture, with the extension to pairs introduced by Totaro, is the following.

1.2
.

Conjecture.

Let
(X,B)\to S
be a klt K-trivial fibration. Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists a rational polyhedral cone
\Pi
which is a fundamental domain for the
action of the group of relative automorphisms preserving the boundary
\Aut(X/S,B)
on

\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)\coloneqq\mbox{\rm Nef}(X/S)\cap\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)

in the sense that

(a)

\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)=\cup_{g\in\Aut(X/S,B)}g^{*}\Pi
.

(b)

\Pi^{\circ}\cap g^{*}\Pi^{\circ}=\emptyset
, unless
g^{*}=\textrm{id}
in
\textrm{GL}\big(N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\big)
.

(2)

There exists a rational polyhedral cone
\Pi^{\prime}
which is a fundamental domain in the sense above for the
action of the group of relative pseudoautomorphisms preserving the boundary
\PsAut(X/S,B)
on

\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X/S)\coloneqq\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)\cap\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S).

Item (1) (resp. (2)) above will be referred to as the relative nef (resp. movable) cone conjecture for
(X,B)
over
S
.
We refer the reader to Section
2.C
for all the definitions and variants of the above conjecture whose relevance, apart from providing a unified framework for Fano and
K
-trivial varieties, is due to its consequences for the birational geometry of a
K
-trivial variety
X
(see e.g.
[
GLSW24
, Theorem 1.5]
).

Conjecture
1.2
is open even in the smooth absolute case without boundary, i.e. when
S
is a point,
X
is smooth and
B=0
. In this case we know by the celebrated Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition theorem that a projective
K
-trivial manifold is, up to a finite étale cover, a product of Calabi-Yau (CY) manifolds, of irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) manifolds and of an abelian variety.
Although Prendergast-Smith
[
PS12
]
proved the conjecture for abelian varieties and Markman
[
Mar11
]
and Amerik-Verbitsky
[
AV17
,
AV20
]
proved it for IHS manifolds, there is no general descent result for finite étale covers (see however
[
PS23
]
and
[
MQ24
]
))
and for CY manifolds, despite the effort of many people, it is wide open already in dimension 3 (cf.
[
Lut24
]
).

In the relative case the situation is even worse and no results at all are known for fibrations having arbitrarily high relative dimension. After the pioneering work of Kawamata in dimension 3
[
Kaw97
]
, only recently weak versions of the relative cone conjecture were obtained for fibrations in surfaces, see
[
LZ22
,
Li23
,
MS24
]
.

One strong motivation for studying the relative case comes from the fact that, modulo standard conjectures in the MMP, the relative cone conjecture implies for instance that the number of minimal models of any terminal projective variety with non-negative Kodaira dimension is finite up to isomorphisms, see
[
CL14
, Theorem 2.14]
. Notice however that the relative automorphism group and the relative nef effective cone of a fibration
X\rightarrow S
can be radically different from their counterparts on the very general fibre
X_{s}
. For example, an automorphism
\alpha_{s}
that exists on every general fibre
X_{s}
may not extend to
X
, even as a birational automorphism
[
Ogu24
, Example 1.3(1)]
. In Section
6
we exhibit a smooth family of Kummer surfaces,
where
\Aut(X/C)
is finite, while
\Bir(X/C)
and
\Aut(X_{s})
are infinite.
Thus the ideas and techniques used to treat the absolute case cannot be immediately transposed to tackle the relative case.

The main result of the present paper concerns the relative cone conjecture for fibrations in IHS manifolds.

1.3
.

Theorem.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective varieties
such that the total space
X
is klt. Suppose that the very general fibre of
\pi
is a projective IHS manifold of one of the 4 known deformation types.
Then the relative movable cone conjecture holds for
X
and up to isomorphisms over
S
there exist only finitely many
X^{\prime}
arising as small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications (SQM) of
X
over
S
, each of which satisfies the relative nef cone conjecture.

For the definition of SQM we refer the reader to Definition
2.9
.
Notice that the geometry of an SQM may be very different from that of the initial fibration
X\to S
and have, for instance, a relative nef cone with infinitely many effective extremal rays while
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)
is rational polyhedral (see e.g. Proposition
6.6
below). Theorem
1.3
above is an immediate consequence of the following.

1.4
.

Theorem.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective varieties
such that the total space
X
is klt. Suppose that the very general fibre of
\pi
is a projective IHS manifold such that every nef divisor is semiample. Then

(a)

the relative movable cone conjecture holds for
X
;

(b)

up to isomorphism over
S
, there are only finitely many
X^{\prime}
arising as small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications (SQM) of
X
over
S
and the
relative nef cone conjecture holds for each of them.

Amerik and Verbitsky deduced the nef cone conjecture for IHS manifolds from the movable cone conjecture proved by Markman
[
Mar11
]

by proving the boundedness of squares of primitive MBM classes

[
AV17
,
AV20
]
.
Our approach, due to
[
GLSW24
]
(see also
[
Xu24
, Theorem 13]
), provides a general
and alternative way to derive the nef cone conjecture from the movable cone conjecture
by using the so-called geography of models (cf.
[
SC11
]
). This requires
the conjectural assumption on the semiampleness of nef divisors (which was proved by
[
Mat17
,
MO22
,
MR21
]
for the 4 known deformation types of IHS manifolds, providing in passing a new
proof of the nef cone conjecture for these manifolds), but has the advantage of
working in the relative setting. In particular, Theorem
1.4
follows from a combination of results from
[
Tak21
]
and
[
LZ22
]
together with a relative version of
[
GLSW24
, Theorem 1.5]
, which is the technical core of this paper and of independent interest. To state it in full generality let us make the framework more precise.

1.5
.

Set-up.

In the rest of the text we will consider a klt
K
-trivial fibration

\pi\colon(X,B)\rightarrow S

such that

-

for a very general fibre
X_{s}
its irregularity
q(X_{s})
is zero;

-

the base
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial quasi-projective variety.

1.6
.

Remark.

In Set-up
1.5
, since
q(X_{s})=0
, the higher direct image
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is a torsion sheaf on
S
. In fact, for a klt
K
-trivial fibration we can apply
[
Men23
, Theorem 1.2]
to obtain
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=0
.
By
[
KM92
, Proposition 12.1.4]
this implies that
N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\pic(X/S)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, we know by
[
LZ22
, Proposition 4.4(4)]
that the relative movable cone
\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)
is non-degenerate. This will allow to apply Looijenga’s results
[
Loo14
]
(cf. Lemma
2.18
) in the proof of Theorem
1.7
below.

As anticipated above we build upon the recent paper
[
GLSW24
]
and give the relation between the relative movable and nef cone conjectures.

1.7
.

Theorem.

In the Set-up
1.5
, assume that

(1.1)

good minimal models exist for effective klt pairs on the fibre
X_{s}
.

Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

The action of
\PsAut(X/S)
on
\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X/S)
admits a rational polyhedral fundamental domain.

(b)

(i)

For every small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification (SQM)
X^{\prime}
of
X
over
S
, the action of
\Aut(X^{\prime}/S)
on
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X^{\prime}/S)
admits a rational polyhedral fundamental domain.

(ii)

Up to isomorphism over
S
, there are only finitely many SQMs of
X
over
S
.

When
S
is a point, this result has been proved in
[
GLSW24
, Theorem 1.5(iii)]

assuming the existence of good minimal models in dimension
\dim(X)
, and in
[
Xu24
, Theorem 13]
assuming the existence of minimal models and a non-vanishing conjecture in dimension
\dim(X)
.
Notice that the implication “(a) implies (b)(ii)” is proved in
[
LZ22
, Proposition 5.3(2)]
, again under the stronger hypothesis of the existence of good minimal models for all effective klt pairs in dimension
\dim(X)-\dim(S)
.
Building upon these inspiring papers, a crucial part of our work consists in weakening their hypotheses to (
1.1
), having in mind our application to families of IHS manifolds, for which minimal models exist by
[
LP16
]
, and, as recalled above, abundance is proved for the 4 known deformation types.

From
[
LZ22
, Theorem 1.4]
we immediately deduce the following.

1.8
.

Corollary.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
a fibration in
K3
surfaces such that
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Then the action of
\Aut(X/S)
on
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)
admits a rational polyhedral fundamental domain.

This result also appeared in the very recent preprint
[
MS24
]
more generally for any klt
K
-trivial fibration of relative dimension
2
, but with a weaker conclusion, see
[
MS24
, Conjecture 1.1(1) and (2)]
for more details.

To put this result into perspective we discuss a relevant example:
in Section
6
we will
construct a complete smooth family of K3 surfaces of Kummer type

\pi\colon X\rightarrow C

such that
\rho(X/C)=\rho(F)=17
with
F
a very general fibre
F
.
It is well known that any Kummer surface contains infinitely many smooth rational curves (see e.g.
[
BHT11
, Example 5]
), which, by Kovács’ theorem
[
Kov94
, Theorem 1]
, generate the Mori cone of
F
. In particular, the nef cone of
F
is not polyhedral.
Yet we can prove that for our family one has the following property.

1.9
.

Proposition.

The relative automorphism group
Aut(X/C)
is finite. Thus, by
Corollary
1.8
, it follows that the
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/C)
is a rational polyhedral cone.

By way of contrast we will see in Subsection
6.B

that the relative pseudoeffective cone
has infinitely many extremal rays and
\Bir(X/C)
is infinite.

Notation and basic definitions

We work over
\mathbb{C}
, for general definitions we refer to
[
Har77
]
.
We recommend Lazarsfeld’s books for notions of positivity of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors, in particular

[
Laz04a
, Section 2.2]
for pseudoeffectivity and the associated cones.
In this paper a fibration is a surjective projective morphism with connected fibres between normal quasi-projective varieties.

Notational convention:
the letter
\pi
will always be used for structural morphism, e.g.

\pi\colon X\rightarrow S,\ \pi_{Y}\colon Y\rightarrow S,\ \pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\rightarrow S,\ldots

We use the terminology of
[
KM98
]
for birational geometry. Note
however that we systematically work with boundary divisors with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficients,

3
and
4
.

2.A.
The relative setting

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal varieties.
We say that two divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
X
are
\pi
-linearly
equivalent if there exists a divisor
B
on
S
such that
D_{1}\sim D_{2}+\pi^{*}B
. In this case we will use the notation
D_{1}\sim_{\pi}D_{2}
or
D_{1}\sim_{S}D_{2}
.

We denote by
\pic(X/S)
the relative Picard group and for

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{R}
we set

\pic(X/S)_{\mathbb{K}}\coloneqq\pic(X/S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}.

2.1
.

Definition.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
be a fibration between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial varieties.
A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
is

-

\pi
-effective if
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mD)
has positive rank for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently divisible.

-

\pi
-big if for some sufficiently divisible
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the natural map

{\textrm{e}v}:\pi^{*}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mD)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mD)

induces a rational map
X\dashrightarrow Y
that is birational.

-

\pi
-movable if

\textrm{codim}({\textrm{S}upp}\big({\textrm{c}oker}({\textrm{e}v}))\big)>1.

An
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
is

\pi
-effective (resp.
\pi
-big, resp.
\pi
-movable) if
D=\sum_{j}a_{j}D_{j}
where the
D_{j}
are

\pi
-effective (resp.
\pi
-big, resp.
\pi
-movable)
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors and the

a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

One checks easily that a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor divisor is
\pi
-effective (resp.
\pi
-big) if its restriction to the generic fibre of
\pi
is effective (resp. big).

2.2
.

Lemma.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
be a fibration between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial varieties.
Let
D
an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Then the following hold:

(a)

If
D
is
\pi
-effective, there exists
D^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\pi}D
that is effective.

(b)

If
D
is
\pi
-ample, there exists
D^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\pi}D
that is ample.

(c)

If
D
is
\pi
-big, there exists
D^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\pi}D
that is big.

2.3
.

Remark.

Note that the statement does not hold for
\pi
-nef or
\pi
-pseudoeffective classes, cf.
[
KM98
, Example 1.46]
for a counterexample.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Note that the first item immediately reduces to the
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor case:
if
D=\sum_{j}a_{j}D_{j}
, where the
D_{j}
are
\pi
-effective divisors, then

D_{j}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}D_{j}^{\prime}
with
D_{j}^{\prime}\geq 0
implies that

D=\sum_{j}a_{j}D_{j}\sim_{\mathbb{R},\pi}a_{j}D_{j}^{\prime}\geq 0.

Now assume that
D
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor.
Choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that the direct image

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mD)
has rank at least one. By asymptotic Riemann-Roch (see
[
Laz04a
, Example 1.2.19]
we know that for some sufficiently ample line bundle
H
the sheaf

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mD)\otimes H
has a global section, so
D^{\prime}\coloneqq D+\pi^{*}\frac{1}{m}H
is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective.

The second item is
[
KM98
, Proposition 1.45]
.

The third item follows from the other two and from the fact that
D\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}A+E
with
A
relatively ample and
E
a
\pi
-effective divisor. Indeed, given a
\pi
-big divisor

D
and a relatively ample divisor
H
, we can find
\varepsilon>0
such that

(D-\varepsilon H)_{|F}
is effective for a general fibre
F
. Thus
D-\varepsilon H
is
\pi
-effective.
∎

2.4
.

Definition.

Let
X
be a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety, and let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
be a fibration. Two
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
X
are
\pi
-numerically equivalent
(notation
D_{1}\equiv_{\pi}D_{2}
or
D_{1}\equiv_{S}D_{2}
) if
D_{1}\cdot C=D_{2}\cdot C
for every curve
C\subset X
such that
\pi(C)
is a point.

We denote by
N^{1}(X/S)
the set of numerical equivalence classes and for

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{R}
we set

N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{K}}\coloneqq N^{1}(X/S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}.

We have a natural surjection

N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(X/S)

and a sequence

0\rightarrow\pi^{*}N^{1}(S)\rightarrow N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(X/S)\rightarrow 0

that is not necessarily exact in the middle (e.g. if
S
is an elliptic curve and
X=S\times S
).

We are now ready to define the cones inside
N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}
we will be concerned with:

-

\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)
is the cone generated by
\pi
-effective divisors,
\mbox{\rm Eff}^{\circ}(X/S)
its interior and
\overline{\textrm{Eff}}(X/S)
its closure.

-

\textrm{Mov}(X/S)
is the cone generated by
\pi
-movable divisors and
\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)
its closure. We set

\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X/S)\coloneqq\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)\cap\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)

and

{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{+}(X/S)\coloneqq\textrm{conv}\big(\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)\cap N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{Q}}\big).

-

\mbox{\rm Amp}(X/S)
is the cone generated by
\pi
-ample divisors and

\mbox{\rm Nef}(X/S)
its closure. We set

\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)\coloneqq\mbox{\rm Nef}(X/S)\cap\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)

and

\mbox{\rm Nef}^{+}(X/S)\coloneqq\textrm{conv}\big(\mbox{\rm Nef}(X/S)\cap N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{Q}}\big).

2.5
.

Remark.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration. Then
\mbox{\rm Amp}(X/S)
is the interior of
\mbox{\rm Nef}(X/S)
, and
\mbox{\rm Big}(X/S)
is the interior of
\overline{\textrm{Eff}}(X/S)
.
This is clear since given a class
[D]
in the interior of the cone,
[D]-\varepsilon[H]
with
[H]\in\mbox{\rm Amp}(X/S)
(resp. with
[H]\in\mbox{\rm Big}(X/S)
)
is still in the cone for some
\varepsilon>0
. Now we can use the results from the absolute case on the fibres.

2.B.
Rational maps

Let
\mu\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a rational map between normal projective varieties and let

\mu_{0}\colon X_{0}\rightarrow Y
be the restriction to the locus
X_{0}\subset X
, where
\mu
is well-defined.
The graph
\Gamma_{\mu}\subset X\times Y
of
\mu
is defined as the closure of the graph of
\mu_{0}
,
in particular it is a (not necessarily normal) projective variety.

Given such a rational map
\mu\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
between normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial varieties, denote by
p_{X}\colon\Gamma_{\mu}\rightarrow X

p_{Y}\colon\Gamma_{\mu}\rightarrow Y
the projections. Then we define the pull-back and push-forward

\mu_{*}\coloneqq\mbox{Cl}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mbox{Cl}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}},\ D\ \mapsto\ (p_{Y})_{*}p_{X}^{*}D

and pull-back

\mu^{*}\coloneqq\mbox{Cl}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mbox{Cl}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}},\ D\ \mapsto\ (p_{X})_{*}p_{Y}^{*}D.

In general the maps are not well-behaved with respect to the composition of rational maps.

2.6
.

Definition.

A birational map
\mu\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
between normal projective varieties is a contraction if the inverse
\mu^{-1}
does not contract a divisor.

2.7
.

Definition.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
and
\pi_{Y}\colon Y\rightarrow S
be fibrations. We say that a rational
map
\mu\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
is a map over
S
if this holds on the locus where
\mu
is well-defined.
A map
{\mu}:{X/S}\dashrightarrow{Y/S}
is a birational contraction over
S
if it is a map over
S
which is a contraction between
X
and
Y
.

2.8
.

Remark.

In the situation above, let
X_{0}\subset X
be the locus where
\mu
is well-defined. Denote by
\widetilde{X\times_{S}Y}
the unique irreducible component of
X\times_{S}Y
that contains
X_{0}\times_{S}Y
.
Then
\mu\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
is a rational map over
S
if and only if its graph

\Gamma_{\mu}\subset X\times Y
is contained in
\widetilde{X\times_{S}Y}
: this is clear on
X_{0}
, the statement follows by taking the closure.

Note that this implies that for a birational morphism over
S
, the projections
p_{X}\colon\Gamma_{\mu}\rightarrow X

and
p_{Y}\colon\Gamma_{\mu}\rightarrow Y
commute with the natural maps, i.e. we have

\pi\circ p_{X}=\pi_{Y}\circ p_{Y}.

2.9
.

Definition.

Let
(X_{i},B_{i})
with
i\in\{1,2\}
be two klt pairs.
Let
\mu\colon X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2}
be a birational contraction over
S
of normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties. We say that the birational map
\mu
is a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification (SQM) of
(X_{1},B_{1})
over
S
if
\mu
is an isomorphism in codimension one which is a morphism over
S
such that
\mu_{*}B_{1}=B_{2}
.

2.10
.

Definition.

Let
\pi\colon(X,B)\rightarrow S
be a fibration between normal quasi-projective varieties. A pseudoautomorphism of
(X,B)

is a birational map

g\colon X\dashrightarrow X

over
S
that is an isomorphism in codimension one and
g_{*}B=B
. The set of pseudoautomorphisms
forms a group which we denote by
\PsAut(X/S,B)
.

We now come a central definition of this paper:

2.11
.

Definition.

Let
{\mu}\colon{X/S}\dashrightarrow{Y/S}
be a birational contraction over
S
between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties.
The relative Mori chamber for
\mu
is defined as

\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S,\mu)\coloneqq\mu^{*}\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(Y/S)+\sum_{E\in\textrm{Exc}(\mu)}\mathbb{R}^{+}E.

Remark.

The relative Mori chamber
\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S,\mu)
is contained in the relative effective
cone
\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)
: indeed by Lemma
2.2
any class
[D]\in\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)

can be represented by an effective divisor. In view of the definition of
\mu

it is clear that
\mu^{*}D
is still effective.

We prove a relative version of a regularity criterion due to Hu and Keel
[
HK00
, Lemma 1.7]
in the absolute case (see
[
GLSW24
, Proof of Lemma 4.3]
for more details).

2.12
.

Lemma.

Let
{\mu_{i}}\colon{X/S}\dashrightarrow{Y_{i}/S}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
be two birational contractions over
S
between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties.
Suppose that there exist
\mathbb{R}
-divisors

-

D_{1}
on
Y_{1}
that is
\pi_{1}
-ample

-

D_{2}
on
Y_{2}
that is
\pi_{2}
-nef

such that

\mu_{1}^{*}D_{1}+E_{1}\equiv_{\pi}\mu_{2}^{*}D_{2}+E_{2},

where the
E_{i}
are effective
\mu_{i}
-exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisors. Then the composition

\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}^{-1}
extends to a regular birational morphism
Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}
.

Note that the statement is not a direct consequence of
[
GLSW24
, Lemma 4.3]
since, as recalled in Remark
2.3
, in general
it is not possible to replace
D_{2}
by a
D_{2}^{\prime}\equiv_{\pi_{2}}D_{2}
such that
D_{2}^{\prime}
is nef (and not just relatively nef).
However we will see that the proof adapts without too much difficulty. We start with a preliminary lemma:

2.13
.

Lemma.

In the situation of Lemma
2.12
, let
\Gamma
be the normalisation of the graph of the birational map

\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}^{-1}
, and let

p_{i}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow Y_{i},\qquad p\colon\Gamma\rightarrow X

be the projections. Then
p_{2}^{*}D_{2}
is
p_{1}
-nef and
p_{1}^{*}D_{1}
is
p_{2}
-nef.

Proof.

By symmetry it is sufficient to show the statement for
D_{2}
.
This is equivalent to showing that if
C\subset\Gamma
is an irreducible curve such that

p_{1}(C)
is a point, then
p_{2}(C)
is contained in a fibre of
\pi_{2}
. Yet this is clear:
since
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are birational maps over
S
,
so is the map
\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}^{-1}
. By Remark
2.8
we therefore
have
\pi_{1}\circ p_{1}=\pi_{2}\circ p_{2}
. Thus if
p_{1}(C)
is a point, then
so is
\pi_{2}\big(p_{2}(C)\big)
. In other words
p_{2}(C)
is contained in a
\pi_{2}
-fibre.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.12
.

Let
\Gamma
be the normalisation of the graph of the birational map

\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}^{-1}
, and let

p_{i}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow Y_{i}\text{ for }i\in\{1,2\},\qquad p\colon\Gamma\rightarrow X

be the projections. By Lemma
2.13
we know that
p_{2}^{*}D_{2}
is
p_{1}
-nef and
p_{1}^{*}D_{1}
is
p_{2}
-nef.
Now we can just copy the first part of the proof of
[
GLSW24
, Lemma 4.3]
to see that

p_{1}^{*}D_{1}\equiv_{\pi\circ p}p_{2}^{*}D_{2}
. Indeed, the key point (application of the negativity lemma) only uses the relative nefness established in Lemma
2.13
.
By Lemma
2.2
we can assume, without loss of generality, that
D_{1}
is ample. Let now

be any curve contracted by

. Then

D_{1}\cdot p_{1}(C)=p_{1}^{*}D_{1}\cdot C=p_{2}^{*}D_{2}\cdot C=0

. Thus

contracts the

-fibres, and the rigidity lemma
[

yields a morphism
\tau\colon Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}
such that

\tau\circ p_{2}=p_{1}
. By construction this morphism extends the rational map

\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}
.
∎

2.14
.

Definition.

Let
{\mu_{i}}\colon{X/S}\dashrightarrow{Y_{i}/S}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
be two birational contractions over

between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties.
We say that the two contractions are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism

\varphi\colon Y_{1}\rightarrow Y_{2}
over

such that
\mu_{2}=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}
.

2.15
.

Lemma.

Let
{\mu_{i}}\colon{X/S}\dashrightarrow{Y_{i}/S}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
be two birational contractions over
S
between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties.
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mu_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mu_{2}
;

(b)

The interiors of the Mori chambers intersect, i.e. we have

\mbox{\rm Eff}^{\circ}(X/S,\mu_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm Eff}^{\circ}(X/S,\mu_{2})\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of
[
GLSW24
, Lemma 4.2]
. It is clear that
(a)
implies
(b)
. For the other implication we observe that by Remark
2.5
and
[
Roc70
, Theorem 6.6]
we have that the relative interior
\textrm{ri}\big(\mu_{i}^{*}\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(Y_{i}/S)\big)
coincides with
\mu_{i}^{*}(\mbox{\rm Amp}(Y_{i}/S))
. Notice moreover that

\mbox{\rm Eff}^{\circ}(X/S,\mu_{i})=\textrm{ri}\big(\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S,\mu_{i})\big)
, since the convex cone
\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S,\mu_{i})
is full-dimensional in the relative effective cone
\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)
.
Then from

[
Roc70
, Corollary 6.6.2]
, it follows that

(2.1)

\mbox{\rm Eff}^{\circ}(X/S,\mu_{i})=\textrm{ri}\big(\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S,\mu_{i})\big)=\mu_{i}^{*}\mbox{\rm Amp}(Y_{i}/S)+\textrm{ri}(\sum_{E\in\textrm{Exc}(\mu_{i})}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}E).

Now from
(b)
and (
2.1
) we deduce the existence, for
i=1,2
, of relatively ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
A_{i}
on
Y_{i}
, and
\mu_{i}
-exceptional divisors on

, such that

\mu_{1}^{*}A_{1}+E_{1}=\mu_{2}^{*}A_{2}+E_{2}.

By applying twice Lemma

we obtain that
\mu_{1}\circ\mu_{2}^{-1}\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
is biregular and yields the desired isomorphism.
∎

2.16
.

Remark.

Notice that Lemma

implies in particular that as soon as

2.C.
Cones and cone conjectures

Let
V
be a real vector space of finite dimension. We suppose that
V
has a distinguished
\mathbb{Q}
-structure, i.e. there exists a rational vector space
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
V=V_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. A cone is a subset
C\subset V
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\forall x\in C,\mbox{ we have }\lambda x\in C.

A cone is called polyhedral (resp. rational polyhedral) if it is a closed
convex cone generated by a finite number of vectors (resp. rational vectors).

2.17
.

Definition.

If
C\subset V
is an open convex cone, we denote by
C_{+}
the convex hull in
V
of
\overline{C}\cap V(\mathbb{Q})
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group and
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\textrm{GL}(V)
a group homomorphism. Suppose that the action of
\Gamma
on
V
(via
\rho
) leaves a convex full-dimensional cone
C\subset V
invariant. We say that
(C_{+},V)
is of polyhedral type if there is a polyhedral cone
\Pi\subset C_{+}
such that
\Gamma\cdot\Pi\supset C
.

The following important result is
[
Loo14
, Theorem 3.8 and Application 4.14]
, see also
[
LZ22
, Lemma 3.5]
.

2.18
.

Lemma.

Under the notation
and assumptions above, suppose that
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\textrm{GL}(V)
is injective and that the cone
C
is non-degenerate. If
(C_{+},\Gamma)
is of polyhedral type, then
C_{+}
admits a rational polyhedral fundamental
domain for the action of
\Gamma
.

In the literature (and in Morrison’s original formulation when
S
is a point and without boundary divisor), the cone
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X/S)
(resp.
\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X/S)
) in Conjecture
1.2
is sometimes replaced with
\mbox{\rm Nef}^{+}(X/S)
(resp.
{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{+}(X/S)
).

The references directly related to our work are those establishing the conjecture for IHS manifolds:
[
Mar11
]
for the movable cone conjecture,
[
AV17
,
AV20
]
for deriving the nef cone conjecture from the movable one (see also
[
MY15
]
),
[
LMP24
]
for singular IHS varieties, and
[
Tak21
]
over not necessarily closed fields of characteristic zero, as well as recent and less recent works in the relative case
[
Kaw97
,
LZ22
,
Li23
,
MS24
]
. Moreover, as already mentioned, our paper is inspired by
[
GLSW24
]
(see also
[
Xu24
]
).

2.D.
Birational geometry

We will follow the terminology from
[
KM98
, Section 2.3]
.

2.19
.

Definition.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
be a fibration. Let
\Delta
be a boundary divisor on
X
such that the pair
(X,\Delta)
is log-canonical.

Let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational contraction of normal projective varieties over
S
and set
\Delta_{Y}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}\Delta
. We say that
\varphi\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(Y,\Delta_{Y})
is a minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
if

-

a(E,X,\Delta)>a(E,Y,\Delta_{Y})
for all
\varphi
-exceptional divisor,

-

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
is nef over
S
, and

-

Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

Moreover, a minimal model
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
over
S
is a good minimal model if
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
is semiample over
S
.

Note that by
[
BCHM10
, Lemma 3.6.3]
, the first condition in the definition of a minimal model is equivalent to requiring that
\varphi
is
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative.

2.20
.

Terminology.

Let
X
be a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety. We say that good minimal models
exist for effective klt pairs on
X
if for every klt pair
(X,\Delta)
such that

\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 0
, the pair
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model.

2.21
.

Remark.

The existence of good minimal models for effective klt pairs is known in the following
cases (we limit ourselves to cases relevant for klt
K
-trivial fibrations):

(a)

\dim\leq 3
,
by the “classical” work of Mori, Kawamata and many others.

(b)

klt pairs fibered in varieties admitting a good minimal model,
by
[
HX13
]
(see Theorem
2.23
below) building upon
[
Lai10
]
.

(c)

Projective IHS manifolds of the 4 known deformation types.
By
[
LP16
]
for any effective divisor
B
on any projective IHS manifolds
X
such that
(X,B)
is klt, the pair
(X,B)
admits a minimal model. Moreover the semiampleness of a square zero nef divisor (if the square is positive then the divisor is also big and one invokes the base-point-free theorem) is given by
[
Mat17
, Corollary 1.1]

in the case of
K3^{[n]}
or generalized Kummer deformation type, by
[
MO22
, Theorem 2.2]
in the case of OG10 deformation type, and by
[
MR21
, Theorem 7.2]
in the
case of OG6 deformation type.

The following result is
[

, Theorem 2.7]
.

.

Theorem.

Let
(X,B)\to S
be a klt

-trivial fibration. Let

from

Let

,

(X,B)
has a good minimal model over

.

Proof.

Li observed in
[

that the proof of

goes through by replacing

with

3.
The geography of models for Klt fibrations

We now prove a result which is stronger than
[
LZ22
, Theorem 2.4]
. Notice however that we follow closely the proof of
[
LZ22
, Theorem 2.4]
, checking that it still works with our weaker hypothesis. In its turn
[
LZ22
, Theorem 2.4]
goes back to
[
SC11
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[
BCHM10
, Lemma 7.1]
.

3.1
.

Theorem.

Let
X
be a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety and
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration. Assume that for a very general point
s\in S
, good minimal models exist for effective klt pairs on the fibre
X_{s}
.

Fix
D_{j}
effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on
X
with
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and set
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}[0,1)D_{j}
.
Suppose that
P\subset\mathcal{C}
is a rational polytope such that

(3.1)

\text{for any }\Delta\in P,\text{ the pair }(X,\Delta)\text{ is klt.}

Then there is a finite decomposition

P=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}

such that
\overline{P}_{i}
is a rational polytope for any
1\leq i\leq m
, together with finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial birational contractions

\varphi_{i}\colon X\dashrightarrow X_{i}\text{ over }S,\text{ where }1\leq i\leq m,

such that the following property holds:
if
\Delta\in P_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, then
\varphi_{i}\colon(X,\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X_{i},\Delta_{i})
with
\Delta_{i}\coloneqq(\varphi_{i})_{*}\Delta
is a good minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
over
S
.

3.2
.

Remark.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we start with the following remark on convex geometry. Let
P
be a rational polytope.

(a)

Given
\epsilon>0
, the rational polytope
P
can be decomposed into finitely many rational sub-polytopes
P_{i}
’s such that for any
i
, we have
\dim P_{i}=\dim P
and that
P_{i}
is contained in a ball of radius
\epsilon
in the affine hull of
P
. Moreover, if we denote
\textrm{ri}(\cdot)
the relative interior, for any
i\neq j
we have
\textrm{ri}(P_{i})\cap\textrm{ri}(P_{j})=\emptyset
.

(b)

Fix a decomposition
P=\bigcup_{i}P_{i}
into finitely many rational sub-polytopes
P_{i}
’s with
\dim P_{i}=\dim P
for any
i
, and
\textrm{ri}(P_{i})\cap\textrm{ri}(P_{j})=\emptyset
for any
i\neq j
.

Assume that for each
P_{i}
, there exists a finite disjoint
decomposition
P_{i}=\bigsqcup_{\ell}Q_{i,\ell}
such that

\overline{Q}_{i,\ell}
is a rational polytope for any

\ell
. Then we can construct a finite disjoint decomposition

P=\bigsqcup_{k}Q_{k}
which is compatible with
P=\bigcup_{i}P_{i}

and such that
\overline{Q}_{k}
is a rational polytope for any

k
. To prove the assertion, we fix the following terminology (see
[
SC11
, Section 3]
): let
P^{\prime}\subset P
be a polytope, we denote by
\textrm{Int}_{P}(P^{\prime})
the biggest open convex polyhedron in
P
which is contained in
P^{\prime}
; by open convex polyhedron in
P
, we mean a set defined by the intersection of
P
with finitely many hyperplanes and open halfspaces in the linear span of
P
.
Indeed, to construct the components
Q_{k}
’s, we first consider
\textrm{Int}_{P}(P_{i})
for all
i
and we take all the components in its induced disjoint decomposition as components
Q_{k}
’s. We then continue with the faces of the
P_{i}
’s for all
i
, which do not lie on the boundary of
P
: we consider these faces by decreasing dimension order, we take
\textrm{Int}_{P}(\cdot)
of each face and we add it successively as components
Q_{k}
’s.

Proof.

We first note that by assumption and Theorem
2.23
,

(3.2)

\text{ for any }\Delta\in P,\text{ the klt pair }(X,\Delta)\text{ has a good minimal model over }S.

We proceed by induction on the dimension of
P
. The case
\dim P=0
is clear by (
3.2
). We consider the case
\dim P\geq 1
.

By Remark
3.2
,
it suffices to prove the statement locally around any point
\Delta\in P
, which means to prove that for any point
\Delta\in P
, there exists a rational polytope
P^{\prime}\subset P
containing
\Delta
such that
\dim P^{\prime}=\dim P
and that
P^{\prime}
satisfies the decomposition properties in the statement of the theorem. Moreover, we can always shrink the rational polytope
P
, which means that we replace
P
by a smaller rational polytope
P^{\prime}\subset P
such that
\Delta\in P^{\prime}
and
\dim P^{\prime}=\dim P
.

Step 1. We prove the claim under the following assumption:
there exists a rational point
\Delta_{0}\in\textrm{ri}(P)
such that
K_{X}+\Delta_{0}\equiv_{S}0
.

Since
\Delta_{0}\in\textrm{ri}(P)
and
\dim P\geq 1
, we can pick a point
\Delta\in P
such that
\Delta\neq\Delta_{0}
.
Let
\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}
be the projection of
\Delta
from
\Delta_{0}
on the boundary of
P
.

Let
Q
be a face of
P
such that
\dim Q=\dim P-1
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{B}}\in Q
. Let

P^{\prime}\coloneqq\textrm{conv}(Q,\Delta_{0}).

Then
P^{\prime}
is a rational polytope such that
\dim P^{\prime}=\dim P
and
\Delta\in P^{\prime}\subset P
. By the inductive hypothesis, we can decompose
Q
into a finite disjoint union

Q=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{s}Q_{k},

such that
\overline{Q}_{k}
is a rational polytope for any
1\leq k\leq s
,
together with finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial birational contractions

\theta_{k}\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}_{k}\text{ over }S\text{ with }k\in\{1,\dots,s\}

satisfying the following property:
if
\Gamma\in Q_{k}
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, then
\theta_{k}\colon(X,\Gamma)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime}_{k},\Gamma^{\prime}_{k})
with
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}\coloneqq(\theta_{k})_{*}\Gamma
is a good minimal model of
(X,\Gamma)
over
S
.
We note that any point in
P^{\prime}\backslash\{\Delta_{0}\}
can be written as
t\Gamma+(1-t)\Delta_{0}
for some
t\in(0,1]
and some
\Gamma\in Q
. Since

K_{X}+t\Gamma+(1-t)\Delta_{0}=t(K_{X}+\Gamma)+(1-t)(K_{X}+\Delta_{0})\equiv_{S}t(K_{X}+\Gamma)\text{ for any }t\in(0,1],

setting
\Delta^{\prime}_{0,k}\coloneqq(\theta_{k})_{*}\Delta_{0}
, we infer that
(X_{k},t\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}+(1-t)\Delta^{\prime}_{0,k})
is a good minimal model of
(X,t\Gamma+(1-t)\Delta_{0})
over
S
if and only if
(X_{k},\Gamma^{\prime}_{k})
is a good minimal model of
(X,\Gamma)
over
S
.

For any
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we denote

P_{k}\coloneqq\textrm{conv}(Q_{k},\Delta_{0})\backslash\{\Delta_{0}\}.

Hence, by the above

P^{\prime}=\{\Delta_{0}\}\bigsqcup\Bigl(\big(\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{s}P_{k}\big)\Bigr)

is a decomposition satisfying the desired decomposition properties; in particular, for each
k
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial birational map is given by
\theta_{k}
.

Step 2. In this step we proceed with the general case.

Since
P
is rational, there exists always a rational point in
\textrm{ri}(P)
.
Fix a rational point
\Delta_{0}\in\textrm{ri}(P)
. Then
(X,\Delta_{0})
has a good minimal model over
S
by (
3.2
), yielding a birational map
\eta\colon(X,\Delta_{0})\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\Delta_{0}^{\prime})
over
S
, where
\Delta_{0}^{\prime}\coloneqq\eta_{*}\Delta_{0}
is semiample over
S
. Hence, there exists a contraction

\phi\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}\text{ over }S

and
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
A^{\prime}\subset Z^{\prime}
which is ample over
S
such that

(3.3)

K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta_{0}^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},S}\phi^{*}A^{\prime}.

In particular, we have

(3.4)

K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta_{0}^{\prime}\equiv_{Z^{\prime}}0.

Since
\eta
is
(K_{X}+\Delta_{0})
-negative, by shrinking
P
, we may assume that

(3.5)

\eta\text{ is }(K_{X}+\Delta)\text{-negative for any }\Delta\in P,

which implies that
P_{X^{\prime}}\coloneqq\eta_{*}(P)
satisfies the assumption (
3.1
), namely, for each divisor
\Delta^{\prime}\in P_{X^{\prime}}
the pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is klt.

By (
3.4
) and
Step 1
, the polytope
P_{X^{\prime}}
can be decomposed into a finite disjoint union

P_{X^{\prime}}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}Q^{\prime}_{i}

such that
\overline{Q^{\prime}}_{i}
is a rational polytope for any
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
and there exist finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial birational maps
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}_{i}
over
Z^{\prime}

such that for any
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}\in Q_{i}^{\prime}
, if we set
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{i}\coloneqq(\varphi_{i}^{\prime})_{*}\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
, then

(3.6)

\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\colon(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{i})\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime\prime}_{i},\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{i})\text{ is a good minimal model of }(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{i})\text{ over }Z^{\prime}.

Claim
. After shrinking
P
,
for any

Lemma.

During the proof of the statement we will show that

5.
Fibrations in IHS manifolds

In this section we treat the case of fibrations in IHS manifolds for which unconditional results can be obtained.

We record the following basic fact.

5.1
.

Lemma.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety,
\pi\colon X\to S
a fibration,
K\coloneqq\mathbb{C}(S)
the function field of the base and
j\colon X_{K}\hookrightarrow X
the natural inclusion. We have a surjective map

r\colon N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\to N^{1}(X_{K})_{\mathbb{R}},

which induces the surjective maps

\mbox{\rm Eff}(X/S)\to\mbox{\rm Eff}(X_{K})\text{ and }\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X/S)\to\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}(X_{K}).

Proof.

For any open set
U\subset S
we have a natural surjective linear map:

N^{1}(X/S)_{\mathbb{R}}\to N^{1}(X_{U}/U)_{\mathbb{R}},\ [D]\mapsto[D_{|U}].

By
[
Li23
, Lemma 3.7]
for
U
sufficiently small, we have
N^{1}(X_{U}/U)_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq N^{1}(X_{K})_{\mathbb{R}}
and thus the first statement follows. The surjections between the cones are proved in Step 1 of the proof of
[
Li23
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

Remark.

Since
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=0
(cf. Remark
1.6
) we can apply

[
Kle05
, Corollary 5.14]
to obtain
N^{1}(X_{K})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\pic(X_{K})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

5.2
.

Definition.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic 0. Let
F
be a smooth
projective variety over
K
of even dimension
2n
. We say that
F
is an irreducible symplectic manifold if

-

\pi_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny\'{e}t}}(F_{\bar{K}})=\{1\}
,

-

H^{0}(F,\Omega^{2}_{F})\simeq K
, and

-

for
0\neq\omega_{K}\in H^{0}(F,\Omega^{2}_{F})
the exterior power

\omega_{K}^{\wedge n}
does not vanish.

5.3
.

Lemma.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety,
\pi:X\to S
a fibration,
K\coloneqq\mathbb{C}(S)
the function field of the base and
j\colon X_{K}\hookrightarrow X
the natural inclusion.
If the very general fibre of
\pi
is simply connected, then
X_{\overline{K}}
is simply connected.

Proof.

Let
f_{\overline{K}}\colon Y_{\overline{K}}\to X_{\overline{K}}
be an étale morphism. By taking an open subset
U\subset S
and an étale cover of it
U^{\prime}\to U
,
the induced étale morphism
f_{U^{\prime}}\colon Y_{U^{\prime}}\to X_{U^{\prime}}
is such that
f_{\overline{K}}
is the base change of
f_{U^{\prime}}
given by an algebraic field extension of
\overline{K}
. Since
\overline{K}
is algebraically closed, the extension splits and
therefore
Y_{\overline{K}}
is a disjoint union of copies of
X_{\overline{K}}
.
∎

5.4
.

Lemma.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety,
\pi\colon X\to S
a fibration,
K\coloneqq\mathbb{C}(S)
the function field of the base and
j\colon X_{K}\hookrightarrow X
the natural inclusion.
If the very general fibre of
\pi
is a projective IHS manifold, then
X_{K}
is an irreducible symplectic variety over
K
.

Proof.

Let
s\in S
be a very general point.
Since
X_{K}\coloneqq X_{\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big)}
is the base change of the fibration

\pi\colon X\rightarrow S
to
\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big)
, we have
\Omega_{X_{K}}^{2}=j^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/S}

and

H^{0}(X_{K},\Omega^{2}_{K})=H^{0}(\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big),(\pi_{K})_{*}\Omega_{X_{K}}^{2}),

where
\pi_{K}=\pi\circ j
.
Yet
(\pi_{K})_{*}\Omega_{X_{K}}^{2}\simeq(\pi_{K})_{*}j^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/S}\simeq(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/S})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big)}
,
so
h^{0}\big(\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big),(\pi_{K})_{*}\Omega_{X_{K}}^{2}\big)
is just the rank of the direct image sheaf
\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/S}
; since
X_{s}
is an IHS manifold, the rank is equal to one.

Choose now
0\neq\omega_{K}\in H^{0}(X_{K},\Omega^{2}_{K})
, then
\omega\coloneqq j_{*}\omega_{K}
induces a symplectic form on
X_{s}
. The locus
S_{0}\subset S
(not necessarily closed) of points
p
such that
{\omega^{\wedge n}}_{|X_{p}}
does not vanish, where
n\coloneqq\frac{\dim X_{p}}{2}
, is Zariski open. Since
\omega
is symplectic on
X_{s}
, the set

is not empty and therefore contains

\mbox{Spec}\big(\mathbb{C}(S)\big)
.

Finally we know by Lemma
5.3
that
X_{\overline{K}}
is simply connected and we are done.
∎

5.5
.

Proposition.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a fibration between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial varieties with very general fibre

. Let
K\coloneqq\mathbb{C}(S)
be the function field of the base and

(a)

\mbox{\rm Nef}^{+}(X_{K})\subset\mbox{\rm Nef}^{e}(X_{K})
.

(b)

If the very general fibre of

(b1)

(b2)
{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{+}(X/S)\subset\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X/S)

(c)

If the very general fibre of
\pi

{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{+}(X_{K})\supset\overline{\mbox{\rm Mov}}^{e}(X_{K})

(a)
Consider
D_{K}\in\mbox{\rm Nef}^{+}(X_{K})

is rational. By Lemma
5.1

such that

. Let

For any integer

(D+\frac{1}{m}H)_{|X_{K}}\textrm{ is ample.}

On the other hand, we know (cf.
[
Gro67
, Corollary 9.6.4]
) that for each

(b)
We give a detailed proof of

is exactly the

D_{K}

By Lemma

such that

be an ample divisor on

. Then for any integer

the divisor

. Hence,

(c)
Consider

.

6.
A family of K3 surfaces

We consider a family of K3 surfaces obtained as a refinement of the construction in
[
BKPSB98
, Section 3]
:
let
\psi\colon A\rightarrow C
be a general complete family of abelian surfaces
given by a curve
C\subset\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
cut out by general sufficiently ample divisors. Since the boundary of the Satake compactification has codimension more than one
all the
\psi
-fibres are smooth and the very general fibre is an abelian surface with Picard number one. Up to making a base change we can assume that the family has a section
s_{1}\colon C\rightarrow A
which we consider as the zero section giving
\psi
the structure of an abelian group scheme. The two-torsion points of this abelian group scheme form an étale multisection

T_{2}\subset A
of degree 16 with
s_{1}(C)\subset T_{2}
being a connected component.
A priori the other connected components of
T_{2}
are not
\psi
-sections, but up to finite base change we can assume that

T_{2}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{16}s_{i}(C)

with
s_{i}\colon C\rightarrow A
a section for any
i\in\{1,\dots,16\}
. Again, up to finite base change, we can assume that
g(C)>1
. The automorphism
z\mapsto-z
acts on every
\psi
-fibre, so we can
apply the Kummer construction to this family to obtain a smooth family of K3 surfaces of Kummer type

(6.1)

\pi\colon X\rightarrow C

such that the very general fibre
F
has
Picard number
17
.
More precisely, we denote by

q\colon A\rightarrow A/\pm 1
the quotient map and by

\mu\colon X\rightarrow A/\pm 1

the fibrewise minimal resolution. If
\Theta\rightarrow A
is a relative principal polarisation, there exists a Cartier divisor
H\rightarrow A/\pm 1
such that

q^{*}H\simeq 2\Theta
.

Note that
q\big(s_{i}(C)\big)
is a section
of
A/\pm 1\rightarrow C
and
\mu
is the blowup along the 16 disjoint curves
q\big(s_{i}(C)\big)
.
Thus we have 16 disjoint prime divisors

E_{1},\ldots,E_{16}\subset X

that are
\mu
-exceptional and restrict to a
(-2)
-curve on every fibre.

An intersection computation now shows that the classes
\mu^{*}H,E_{1},\ldots,E_{16}

are linearly independent in
N^{1}(X/C)
, so
N_{1}(X/C)
has rank 17 and for a very general fibre the restriction morphism

N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(F)

is surjective. Note also that by the canonical bundle formula
K_{X/C}\simeq\pi^{*}M
, where
M
is an ample line bundle on
C
.

6.A.
The relative automorphism group

6.1
.

Lemma.

Let
E_{i}
be one of the exceptional divisors from the Kummer construction. Then we have
E_{i}\cdot c_{2}(X)=-2\deg M<0
.

Proof.

In order to simplify the notation we will denote
E_{i}
by
E
.
Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow T_{E}\rightarrow{T_{X}}_{|E}\rightarrow N_{E/X}\rightarrow 0.

Then we have

E\cdot c_{2}(X)=c_{2}({T_{X}}_{|E})=c_{2}(E)+c_{1}(E)\cdot c_{1}(N_{E/X}),

so we only have to compute these two terms.
For the ruled surface
E
we have

c_{2}(E)=e(E)=-2\deg K_{C}

and
K_{E/C}^{2}=0
.
By adjunction and
E^{2}\cdot F=-2
we have

K_{E/C}^{2}=(K_{X/C}+E)^{2}\cdot E=(\pi^{*}M+E)^{2}\cdot E=-4\deg M+E^{3},

therefore
E^{3}=4\deg M
. Again by adjunction we have

\displaystyle c_{1}(E)\cdot c_{1}(N_{E/X})

\displaystyle=-K_{E}\cdot E_{|E}=-(K_{X}+E)\cdot E^{2}

\displaystyle=-\big(\pi^{*}(K_{C}+M)+E\big)\cdot E^{2}

\displaystyle=2\deg K_{C}+2\deg M-E^{3}=2\deg K_{C}-2\deg M.\qed

Since the threefold
X
is not uniruled we know by Miyaoka’s theorem
[
Miy87
, Theorem 1.1]

that
c_{2}(X)\in\overline{\mbox{NE}}(X)
, so we expect its intersection with most prime divisors
to be non-negative. In our case this can be made more precise:

6.2
.

Proposition.

Let
S\subset X
be a prime divisor that is distinct from the exceptional divisors

.

The set-up of the proof requires some preparation: let

(6.2)

0\rightarrow\psi^{*}\Omega_{C}\rightarrow\Omega_{A}\rightarrow\Omega_{A/C}\rightarrow 0

be the cotangent sequence for
\psi

\Omega_{A/C}\simeq\psi^{*}V

with

a rank two vector bundle on the curve
C

theorem
[
Miy87

it is not hard to see that

is nef.
Moreover since we started the construction
with a sufficiently positive curve

,
we can deduce from the stability of the Hodge bundle
[
MVZ12

that

is semistable. Thus we obtain:

6.3
.

Proposition.

The vector bundle
V
is nef and semistable.

Let
\tilde{\mu}\colon Y\rightarrow A
be the blowup along the 16 disjoint sections
s_{i}(C)

by

the exceptional divisors. By construction of the blowup

and since

\psi

Let

\eta\colon Y\rightarrow X

is cyclic, so there exists a (non-effective) Cartier divisor class

on

6.4
.

Lemma.

We have

c_{2}(X)=-3\delta^{2}-\pi^{*}c_{1}(V)\delta.

Proof.

From the exact sequence (

) we obtain immediately that

and

Consider the cotangent sequence for the blowup
\tilde{\mu}

6.B.
An interesting SQM

6.5
.

Lemma.

Let
F
be a very general fibre of the family of Kummer surfaces as in (
6.1
).
Let
B\subset F
be any
(-2)
-curve. Then there exists a prime divisor

D\subset X
such that we have an equality of cycles
D\cdot F=B
.

It is well-known that this follows from the surjection
N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(F)
, we give the details for the convenience of the reader:

Proof.

Since
F
is very general we know by countability of the Hilbert scheme that the curve
B\subset F
deforms with
F
and it is clear that a small deformation is a
(-2)
-curve.
Thus the cycle space
\mbox{\rm Chow}(X)
is irreducible and of dimension one in the point
[B]
and the image of the universal family defines a prime divisor
D\subset X
such that

B\subset D
is an irreducible component of a fibre of
D\rightarrow C
.
We claim that the general fibre of
D\rightarrow C
is irreducible, which implies the statement. For the proof of the claim note that up to replacing
F
by some other very general fibre, we can assume without loss of generality that
D\cap F
is reduced and every irreducible component is a deformation of
B
. Now recall that
the restriction map
N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(F)
is surjective, so there are uniquely defined
\alpha,\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

[B]=(\alpha H+\sum\beta_{i}E_{i})_{|F}

in
N^{1}(F)
. Thus the class of
B
(and all its deformations) in
N_{1}(X)
is
(\alpha H+\sum\beta_{i}E_{i})\cdot F
. Yet this implies that two irreducible components

B,B^{\prime}\subset D\cap F
have the same class in
F
. Since
B
is a
(-2)
-curve, we get

B=B^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma
6.5
shows that the deformations of every
(-2)
-curve in a very general fibres cover a divisor that defines an extremal ray in the relative pseudoeffective cone
\overline{\textrm{Eff}}(X/C)
. We will now use our result and MMP to show that, after a suitably chosen SQM, they are actually extremal rays in the relative Mori cone.

6.6
.

Proposition.

Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow C
be the family of Kummer surfaces (
6.1
).
There exists an SQM
\psi:X\dashrightarrow\tilde{X}
over
C
such that

-

The variety admits infinitely divisorial contractions
\mu_{j}\colon\tilde{X}\rightarrow Y_{j}

over
C
.

-

The relative automorphism group
\Aut(\tilde{X}/C)
, and therefore

\Bir(X/C)
, is infinite.

Proof.

The second statement is immediate from the first statement and Corollary
1.8
, since
\tilde{X}
has infinitely many extremal rays in
\overline{\mbox{NE}}(X/C)
, the automorphism group must be infinite.

For the proof of the first statement, note first that by Corollary
1.3
we can choose finitely many SQM’s
\psi_{l}\colon X\dashrightarrow\tilde{X}_{l}
over
C
such that every other
SQM is isomorphic to some
\tilde{X}_{l}
. Fix now a very general fibre
F\subset X
, and
let
(B_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset F
be an infinite collection of distinct
(-2)
-curves (which are known to exist on a Kummer surface). By Lemma
6.5
there
exist prime divisor
D_{j}\subset X
such that
D_{j}\cdot F=B_{j}
as cycles. Note that the divisors
D_{j}
are distinct, since their intersections with
F
are distinct curves.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, fix
\epsilon_{j}>0
such that the pair
(X,\epsilon_{j}D_{j})
is klt.
The divisor
K_{X}+\epsilon_{j}D_{j}
is not
\pi
-nef, since the restriction to
F
is not nef. Thus we can run a
(K_{X}+\epsilon_{j}D_{j})
-MMP over
C
decomposing into a SQM

\varphi_{j}\colon X\dashrightarrow X_{j}

and a divisorial contraction
\tau_{j}\colon X_{j}\rightarrow Y_{j}
contracting
the divisor
(\varphi_{j})_{*}D_{j}
onto a curve in
Y_{j}
.

By what precedes we know that up to renumbering, there are infinitely
j\in\mathbb{N}
such
that
X_{j}\simeq\tilde{X}_{1}
over
C
. Thus we can find isomorphisms

f_{j}\colon X_{j}\rightarrow\tilde{X}\coloneqq\tilde{X}_{1}

such that
f_{j}\circ\varphi_{j}=\psi_{1}\eqcolon\psi
. We also set

\mu_{j}\coloneqq\tau_{j}\circ f_{j}^{-1}\colon\tilde{X}\rightarrow Y_{j}

and claim that the divisorial contractions
\mu_{j}
are distinct. Yet
\mu_{j}

contracts exactly the divisor
(f_{j})_{*}(\varphi_{j})_{*}D_{j}=\psi_{*}D_{j}
, so this is clear.
∎

6.7
.

Remark.

The family
\tilde{X}\rightarrow C
is still a smooth family of K3 surfaces of Kummer type, but it is no longer clear that it is globally Kummer, i.e. we have
a birational morphism

\tilde{\mu}\colon\tilde{X}\rightarrow\tilde{A}/\pm 1.

In fact the proof of Proposition
1.9
indicates that such a structure contradicts the existence of infinitely many automorphisms.

Recently Oguiso used his theory of
c_{2}
-contractions to study the relative automorphism group of fibered Calabi-Yau threefold
[
Ogu24
]
.
One might hope that a similar theory for families of K3 surfaces allows to get a better
understanding of
\Aut(\tilde{X}/C)
.

On the directional growth of the resolvent norm
Horia Cornean, Henrik Garde, Arne Jensen

Abstract
Let A A be a densely defined closed operator on a separable Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} . Its resolvent is denoted by R A ​ ( z ) = ( A − z ​ I ) − 1 R_{A}(z)=(A-zI)^{-1} . Under very general assumptions at the point z z , we construct a line segment [ z , z ′ ] [z,z^{\prime}] in the resolvent set on which the resolvent norm grows. We obtain estimates of the type ∥ R A ​ ( ζ ) ∥ ≥ ∥ R A ​ ( z ) ∥ + C ​ | ζ − z | δ \lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+C\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{\delta} for ζ ∈ [ z , z ′ ] \zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}] with either δ = 1 \delta=1 or δ = 2 \delta=2 . We give a number of examples involving both normal and non-normal operators, and an application to pseudospectra of matrices.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and let
A
be a densely defined closed operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\rho(A)
denote the resolvent set of
A
. Assume that
\rho(A)\neq\emptyset
. For
z\in\rho(A)
the resolvent is denoted by
R_{A}(z)=(A-zI)^{-1}
.
Fix
z\in\rho(A)
. Then we can always find a sequence
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi_{n}}\rVert=1
, and a
c_{0}>0
, such that

\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\frac{c_{0}}{n},\quad n\in\mathbf{N}.

(1.1)

Under additional assumptions on the sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
, we show that there exist
C>0
and
z^{\prime}\in\rho(A)
, such that the line segment

[z,z^{\prime}]=\{z+t(z^{\prime}-z)\mid t\in[0,1]\}

belongs to
\rho(A)
and such that

\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+C\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{\delta},\quad\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}],

(1.2)

where either
\delta=1
or
\delta=2
. The detailed conditions are given in Assumptions
2.2
–
2.4
, and the results are stated in Theorem
2.5
and Corollary
2.7
.

We apply the estimate to the question of (non-)existence of local extrema in the resolvent norm and the related question whether the level sets of
\lVert{R_{A}(\mkern 1.0mu\cdot\mkern 1.0mu)}\rVert
can have interior points. The main results and their proofs are given in Section
2
, which also provide sufficient criteria for a local minimum in the resolvent norm. In Section
3
we give some examples illustrating the scope of our results. In Section
4
the growth estimate (
1.2
) leads to an elementary proof, that each point in the
\varepsilon
-pseudospectrum
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
of a matrix
A
, can be connected to an eigenvalue of
A
through a finite polygonal path inside
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
.

2
Main results

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
, consisting of
z\in\rho(A)
and
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi_{n}}\rVert=1
, is called a
norm determining
pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
if there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\frac{c_{0}}{n}>0,\quad n\in\mathbf{N}.

(2.1)

Given a norm determining pair, let us define the following quantities.

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi_{n}}\rangle,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=\lVert{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi_{n}}\rVert^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}},{R_{A}(z)^{3}\psi_{n}}\rangle.

(2.4)

We introduce three separate assumptions.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair. Write (
2.2
) as
\alpha_{n}=r_{n}e^{i\varphi_{n}}
,
r_{n}\geq 0
. Assume that there exist
\theta_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi]
,
c_{1}>0
, and
\mu\in(0,\pi/2)
such that

r_{n}\geq c_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert{\varphi_{n}-\theta_{0}}\rvert\leq\mu\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbf{N}$}.

(2.5)

Assumption 2.3
.

Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair. Assume that
\alpha_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
. Write (
2.4
) as
\gamma_{n}=b_{n}e^{i\nu_{n}}
,
b_{n}\geq 0
. Assume that there exist
\theta_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi]
,
c_{2}>0
, and
\mu\in(0,\pi/2)

such that

b_{n}\geq c_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert{\nu_{n}-2\theta_{0}}\rvert\leq\mu\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbf{N}$}.

(2.6)

Assumption 2.4
.

Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair. Assume that
\alpha_{n}=0
and
\gamma_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
. Assume that there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

\beta_{n}\geq c_{3}\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbf{N}$}.

(2.7)

Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
. Then we have the following results:

(i)

Under Assumption
2.2
there exist
C_{1}>0
and
z^{\prime}\in\rho(A)
such that
[z,z^{\prime}]\subset\rho(A)
,
and for all
\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}]
we have

\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+C_{1}\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert.

(2.8)

(ii)

Under Assumption
2.3
there exist
C_{2}>0
and
z^{\prime}\in\rho(A)
such that
[z,z^{\prime}]\subset\rho(A)
, and for all
\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}]
we have

\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+C_{2}\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}.

(2.9)

(iii)

Under Assumption
2.4
there exist
C_{3}>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\zeta
satisfying
\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, we have
\zeta\in\rho(A)
and

\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+C_{3}\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}.

(2.10)

)

(1)

(2)

]

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
z\in\rho(A)
. Assume that there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi}\rVert=1
, such that

\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rVert=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert.

(2.11)

(i)

Assume
\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle\neq 0
. Then the result in Theorem
2.5
(i)
holds.

(ii)

Assume
\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle=0

and
\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{3}\psi}\rangle\neq 0
. Then the result in Theorem
2.5
(ii)
holds.

(iii)

Assume
\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle=0=\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{3}\psi}\rangle
. Then the result in Theorem
2.5
(iii)
holds.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{n}=\psi
for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
, where
\psi
satisfies (
2.11
). Then
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})

, and the corollary follows.

Remark 2.8
.

The condition (
2.11
) holds if
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}
is an eigenvalue of the self-adjoint operator
R_{A}(z)^{\ast}R_{A}(z)
. Assume this is the case and let
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi}\rVert=1
, be an eigenvector. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{3}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

We use the notation
B_{\delta}(z)=\{w\in\mathbf{C}\mid\lvert{w-z}\rvert<\delta\}
. Let
z\in\rho(A)
and choose
\delta>0
such that
B_{\delta}(z)\subset\rho(A)
. For
\zeta\in B_{\delta}(z)
, by iterating the first resolvent identity, we obtain the expansion

\displaystyle R_{A}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=R_{A}(z)+(\zeta-z)R_{A}(z)^{2}+(\zeta-z)^{2}R_{A}(z)^{3}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+(\zeta-z)^{3}R_{A}(z)^{4}\bigl(I-(\zeta-z)R_{A}(z)\bigr)^{-1}.

(2.12)

The last term is of order
\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3})
, with a constant independent of
\zeta\in B_{\delta^{\prime}}(z)
where
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\delta)
. We compute
R_{A}(\zeta)^{\ast}R_{A}(\zeta)
using (
2
):

\displaystyle R_{A}(\zeta)^{\ast}R_{A}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=R_{A}(z)^{\ast}R_{A}(z)+(\zeta-z)R_{A}(z)^{\ast}R_{A}(z)^{2}+\overline{(\zeta-z)}(R_{A}(z)^{\ast})^{2}R_{A}(z)

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}(R_{A}(z)^{\ast})^{2}R_{A}(z)^{2}+(\zeta-z)^{2}R_{A}(z)^{\ast}R_{A}(z)^{3}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\overline{(\zeta-z)}^{2}(R_{A}(z)^{\ast})^{3}R_{A}(z)+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3}).

Next we compute as follows for
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\lVert{\psi}\rVert=1
:

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)\psi}\rVert^{2}=\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(\zeta)^{\ast}R_{A}(\zeta)\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+2\re\bigl[(\zeta-z)\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}\lVert{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+2\re\bigl[(\zeta-z)^{2}\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{3}\psi}\rangle\bigr]+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3}).

(2.13)

We now prove Theorem
2.5
(i). Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
such that Assumption
2.2
holds. Using the definitions (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
), we rewrite (
2.13
), with
\psi=\psi_{n}
, as

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n}\bigr)^{2}+2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}\beta_{n}+2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3}).

(2.14)

Let
x>0
be fixed and let
\xi
be a small parameter. Then Taylor’s theorem yields

\sqrt{x^{2}+\xi}=x+\frac{1}{2x}\xi-\frac{1}{8x^{3}}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}).

(2.15)

Applying this to (
2.14
), and using that
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
, and
\gamma_{n}
all are bounded by powers of
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
, gives

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert

\displaystyle\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\frac{1}{2(\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n})}\bigl[2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}\beta_{n}+2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}-\frac{1}{8(\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n})^{3}}\bigl[2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)\bigr]^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3}).

(2.16)

Next we determine
z^{\prime}
. Let
a_{0}\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
and use
\theta_{0}
from Assumption
2.2
to define

z^{\prime}=z+a_{0}\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}},

(2.17)

such that
[z,z^{\prime}]\subset B_{\delta^{\prime}}(z)\subset\rho(A)
. Then

(z^{\prime}-z)\alpha_{n}=a_{0}r_{n}\textup{e}^{i(\varphi_{n}-\theta_{0})}.

Using (
2.5
) we get

\re\bigl((z^{\prime}-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)\geq a_{0}c_{1}\cos(\mu).

Let
\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}]
, that is
\zeta=z+t(z^{\prime}-z)
with
t\in[0,1]
, then we have

\re\bigl((\zeta-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)=t\re\bigl((z^{\prime}-z)\alpha_{n}\bigr)\geq\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert c_{1}\cos(\mu)

(2.18)

since
t=\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert/a_{0}
.

Because
\lvert{\alpha_{n}}\rvert\leq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{3}
,
\beta_{n}>0
, and
\lvert{\gamma_{n}}\rvert\leq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{4}
for all
n
, the estimates (
2.16
) and (
2.18
) ensure that we can pick a small enough
a_{0}
(which determines
z^{\prime}
) and find a
C_{1}>0
such that (
2.8
) holds.

Next we prove Theorem
2.5
(ii). Let
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
be a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
such that Assumption
2.3
holds. As above (now with
\alpha_{n}=0
), we have

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert

\displaystyle\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{={}}+\frac{1}{2(\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\tfrac{c_{0}}{n})}\bigl[\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}\beta_{n}+2\re\bigl((\zeta-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)\bigr]+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{3}).

(2.19)

We define
z^{\prime}
as in (
2.17
), but with
\theta_{0}
coming from Assumption
2.3
. Then

\re\bigl((z^{\prime}-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)=a_{0}^{2}b_{n}\cos(\nu_{n}-2\theta_{0})\geq a_{0}^{2}c_{2}\cos(\mu),

which for
\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}]
, that is
\zeta=z+t(z^{\prime}-z)
with
t\in[0,1]
, leads to

\re\bigl((\zeta-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)=t^{2}\re\bigl((z^{\prime}-z)^{2}\gamma_{n}\bigr)\geq\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}c_{2}\cos(\mu).

(2.20)

Because
\beta_{n}>0
for all
n
, the estimates (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) imply that we can pick a small enough
a_{0}
(and thereby
z^{\prime}
) and find a
C_{2}>0
such that (
2.9
) holds.

The proof of Theorem
2.5
(iii) follows in the same manner based on (
2.7
) and (
2.19
).

3
Examples

Example 3.1
.

Let
A
be a densely defined closed operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
z\in\rho(A)
and assume that
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}
is an eigenvalue of the operator
R_{A}(z)^{\ast}R_{A}(z)
. Then (
2.11
) holds for a corresponding normalized eigenvector
\psi
, and Corollary
2.7
applies to the point
z
.

If for some
z_{0}\in\rho(A)
the resolvent
R_{A}(z_{0})
is compact, then for
all

z\in\rho(A)
there exists a
\psi
such that (
2.11
) holds. In particular, Corollary
2.7
applies to all matrices
A\in\mathbf{C}^{N\times N}
and at every point in
\rho(A)
.

Example 3.2
.

Assume that
A
is a normal operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then for
z\in\rho(A)
we have

\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert=\frac{1}{\dist(z,\sigma(A))}.

Let us directly prove that the estimate (
2.8
) holds for all
z\in\rho(A)
. More precisely, let
z\in\rho(A)
and take
z_{0}\in\sigma(A)
such that

\dist(z,\sigma(A))=\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert
. Let
z^{\prime}=(z+z_{0})/2
. Then for
\zeta\in[z,z^{\prime}]
we have
|\zeta-z_{0}|=|z-z_{0}|-|\zeta-z|
and

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(\zeta)}\rVert

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lvert{\zeta-z_{0}}\rvert}=\frac{1}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert-\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert}=\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}-\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert+\frac{1}{\dist(z,\sigma(A))^{2}}\lvert{\zeta-z}\rvert.

(3.1)

We make use of the following result in the next example.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
is a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
. Then there exist
\tilde{c}_{0}>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbf{N}
such that

\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}}\rVert^{2}\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}-\tilde{c}_{0}\delta_{n}>0,\quad n\geq n_{0},

(3.2)

where
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is a decreasing sequence with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}=0
.

Conversely, assume that (
3.2
) holds. Then we may construct a subsequence of
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
which generates a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
.

Proof.

If (
2.1
) holds, then it is easy to see that (
3.2
) holds with
\delta_{n}=1/n
and
\tilde{c}_{0}=2c_{0}\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
, if
n
is large enough.

Conversely, assume that (
3.2
) holds. Passing to subsequences of
\psi_{n}
and
\delta_{n}
, we can assume that (
3.2
) holds with
\delta_{n_{k}}
replaced by
1/k
. Taking the square root and applying (
2.15
) imply

\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n_{k}}}\rVert=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\frac{\tilde{c}_{0}}{2\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert}\frac{1}{k}+\mathcal{O}(k^{-2})\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert-\frac{\tilde{c}_{0}}{\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert}\frac{1}{k}>0

for
k>k_{1}
with
k_{1}
sufficiently large. Then (
2.1
) holds for the sequence
\{\psi_{n_{k+k_{1}}}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
and
c_{0}=\frac{\tilde{c}_{0}}{\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert}
.
∎

Example 3.4
.

Related to Example
3.2
, let us check whether we can verify Assumption
2.2
for a general normal operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
z\in\rho(A)
and let
z_{0}\in\sigma(A)
such that
\dist(z,\sigma(A))=\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert
. If
z_{0}
is an isolated point in
\sigma(A)
, it is an eigenvalue of
A
, and we can find
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
such that (
2.11
) holds. This verifies Assumption
2.2
in this case.

Assume instead that
z_{0}
is an accumulation point for
\sigma(A)
. Let
E_{A}
denote the spectral resolution for the normal operator
A
. Then we have

E_{A}(B_{\delta}(z_{0})\cap\sigma(A))\neq 0\quad\text{for all $\delta>0$}.

Write
\Omega_{\delta}=B_{\delta}(z_{0})\cap\sigma(A)
and let
\lambda\in\Omega_{\delta}
. Then we have

\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert\leq\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta.

Thus there exists
c>0
such that for small
\delta>0
we have

\frac{1}{\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\lvert{z_{0}-z}\rvert^{2}}-c\delta>0.

Let
\psi_{\delta}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi_{\delta}}\rVert=1
, such that
E_{A}(\Omega_{\delta})\psi_{\delta}=\psi_{\delta}
. From the spectral theorem we have

\displaystyle\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{\delta}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\frac{1}{\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert^{2}}\,\textup{d}\langle{\psi_{\delta}},{E_{A}(\lambda)\psi_{\delta}}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lvert{z_{0}-z}\rvert^{2}}-c\delta

\displaystyle=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}-c\delta>0.

By Proposition
3.3
we can find a decreasing sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
, such that
(z,\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
with
\psi_{n}=\psi_{\delta_{n}}
is a norm determining sequence for
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
.

We estimate the location of
\alpha_{n}=\langle{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi_{n}}\rangle
. Let
\phi_{n}=R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}
. Then we have
E_{A}(\Omega_{\delta_{n}})\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}
and

\alpha_{n}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta_{n}}}\frac{1}{\lambda-z}\,\textup{d}\langle{\phi_{n}},{E_{A}(\lambda)\phi_{n}}\rangle.

(3.3)

Note that
\textup{d}\langle{\phi_{n}},{E_{A}(\lambda)\phi_{n}}\rangle
is a positive Borel measure with

\int_{\Omega_{\delta_{n}}}1\,\textup{d}\langle{\phi_{n}},{E_{A}(\lambda)\phi_{n}}\rangle=\lVert{\phi_{n}}\rVert^{2}.

(3.4)

We have
\lVert{\phi_{n}}\rVert^{2}=\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{n}}\rVert^{2}
, such that

\frac{1}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}-c\delta_{n}\leq\lVert{\phi_{n}}\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}.

(3.5)

Let
\theta_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi]
such that
z_{0}-z=\lvert{z_{0}-z}\rvert\textup{e}^{i\theta_{0}}
. For
\lambda\in\Omega_{\delta_{n}}
write
\lambda-z_{0}=t\textup{e}^{i\omega}
, where
t=\lvert{\lambda-z_{0}}\rvert\leq\delta_{n}
. Then

\re\Bigl(\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}(\lambda-z)\Bigr)=\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+t\cos(\omega-\theta_{0})

and

\frac{1}{\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}(\lambda-z)}=\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+t\cos(\omega-\theta_{0})}{\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert^{2}}-i\frac{t\sin(\omega-\theta_{0})}{\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert^{2}}.

Note that for
\lambda\in\Omega_{\delta_{n}}
we have

\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert\leq\lvert{\lambda-z}\rvert\leq\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta_{n}.

We conclude that we have

\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert-\delta_{n}}{(\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta_{n})^{2}}\leq\re\Bigl(\frac{1}{\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}(\lambda-z)}\Bigr)\leq\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta_{n}}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}

(3.6)

and

\Bigl\lvert\im\Bigl(\frac{1}{\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}(\lambda-z)}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert\leq\frac{\delta_{n}}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}.

(3.7)

Combining the estimates (
3.3
)–(
3.7
) we get

\Bigl(\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert-\delta_{n}}{(\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta_{n})^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{2}}-c\delta_{n}\Bigr)\leq\re(\textup{e}^{i\theta_{0}}\alpha_{n})\leq\frac{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert+\delta_{n}}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{4}}

(3.8)

and

\lvert\im(\textup{e}^{i\theta_{0}}\alpha_{n})\rvert\leq\frac{\delta_{n}}{\lvert{z-z_{0}}\rvert^{4}}.

(3.9)

Using (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), it follows, after a readjustment of the choice of the sequence
\delta_{n}
, that Assumption
2.2
is satisfied.

Thus for a general normal operator the estimate (
2.8
) can be proved within the framework established here, such that a growth estimate of the form (
3.1
) can be obtained from Theorem
2.5
(i).

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbf{Z})
. Let
a_{j}\in\mathbf{C}\setminus\{0\}
,
j\in\mathbf{Z}
, satisfy

\lvert{a_{0}}\rvert\geq\sup_{j\neq 0}\lvert{a_{j}}\rvert.

(3.10)

Define an operator
A
by

(Ax)_{j}=a_{j+1}^{-1}x_{j+1},\quad x\in D(A),

where
D(A)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid Ax\in\mathcal{H}\}
is the maximal domain. It is easy to verify that
A
is densely defined and closed. We have that
A
is invertible with a bounded inverse

(A^{-1}x)_{j}=a_{j}x_{j-1},\quad((A^{-1})^{*}x)_{j}=\overline{a_{j+1}}x_{j+1},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}.

A computation shows that

((A^{-1})^{*}A^{-1}x)_{j}=\lvert{a_{j+1}}\rvert^{2}x_{j},\quad(A^{-1}(A^{-1})^{\ast}x)_{j}=|a_{j}|^{2}x_{j},\quad j\in\mathbf{Z},

which shows in particular that
A
is non-normal in general. Also, a basis of eigenvectors for
(A^{-1})^{*}A^{-1}
is given by the canonical basis
\{\textup{e}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}
. We also have

\lVert{A^{-1}x}\rVert^{2}=\langle{x},{(A^{-1})^{\ast}A^{-1}x}\rangle\leq|a_{0}|^{2}\lVert{x}\rVert^{2},\quad\lVert{A^{-1}\textup{e}_{-1}}\rVert^{2}=|a_{0}|^{2}.

Thus
\lVert{A^{-1}}\rVert=|a_{0}|
and (
2.11
) is satisfied with
\psi=\textup{e}_{-1}
.

We now show that the conditions in Corollary
2.7
(iii) are also satisfied for
z=0
,
R_{A}(0)=A^{-1}
, and
\psi=\textup{e}_{-1}
. Indeed:

\displaystyle\langle{R_{A}(0)\psi},{R_{A}(0)^{2}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{(A^{-1})^{*}A^{-1}\textup{e}_{-1}},{A^{-1}\textup{e}_{-1}}\rangle=\lvert{a_{0}}\rvert^{2}\langle{\textup{e}_{-1}},{A^{-1}\textup{e}_{-1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=a_{0}\lvert{a_{0}}\rvert^{2}\langle{\textup{e}_{-1}},{\textup{e}_{0}}\rangle=0.

An analogous computation shows that
\langle{R_{A}(0)\psi},{R_{A}(0)^{3}\psi}\rangle=0
. Thus the conditions in Corollary
2.7
(iii) are satisfied, and we conclude that
0
is a local minimum of
\lVert{R_{A}(\mkern 1.0mu\cdot\mkern 1.0mu)}\rVert
.

We note that if
\lim_{\lvert{j}\rvert\to\infty}a_{j}=0
, then
A
has compact resolvent. If
\inf_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\lvert{a_{j}}\rvert>0
then
A
is bounded.

Example 3.6
.

Let
A
be as in Example
3.5
, but instead of (
3.10
) we here assume that
0<a=\sup_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}|a_{j}|<\infty
and
a<\lvert{a_{j}}\rvert
for all

j\in\mathbf{Z}
. We can construct a sequence
b_{n}=a_{j_{n}+1}
,
n\in\mathbf{N}
, such that
\lvert{b_{n}}\rvert\nearrow a
. As before we have

\lVert{A^{-1}x}\rVert^{2}=\langle{x},{(A^{-1})^{*}A^{-1}x}\rangle\leq a^{2}\lVert{x}\rVert^{2},\quad\lVert{A^{-1}\textup{e}_{j_{n}}}\rVert=\lvert{b_{n}}\rvert\nearrow a.

Thus
\lVert{A^{-1}}\rVert=a
and (up to a subsequence)
(0,\{\textup{e}_{j_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}})
is a norm determining pair for
\lVert{R_{A}(0)}\rVert
. We will determine if Assumption
2.4
is satisfied. We have

\alpha_{n}=\langle{R_{A}(0)\textup{e}_{j_{n}}},{R_{A}(0)^{2}\textup{e}_{j_{n}}}\rangle=\langle{R_{A}(0)\textup{e}_{j_{n}}},{R_{A}(0)^{3}\textup{e}_{j_{n}}}\rangle=\gamma_{n}=0,\quad n\in\mathbf{N},

while

\beta_{n}=\lVert{R_{A}(0)^{2}\textup{e}_{j_{n}}}\rVert^{2}=\lvert{a_{j_{n}+1}a_{j_{n}+2}}\rvert^{2}=\lvert{b_{n}a_{j_{n}+2}}\rvert^{2}.

Provided that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lvert{a_{j_{n}+2}}\rvert>0
, then
\beta_{n}
is bounded from below by a positive constant when
n
becomes large enough. From Theorem
2.5
(iii),
0
is a local minimum of
\lVert{R_{A}(\mkern 1.0mu\cdot\mkern 1.0mu)}\rVert
.

Remark 3.7
.

Examples
3.5
and
3.6
provide non-normal operators on an infinite dimensional Hilbert space with the property that the resolvent norm has a local minimum. For normal operators there are many examples with this property. For instance, let
U
be a unitary operator with
\sigma(U)=\mathbf{T}
, where
\mathbf{T}
denotes the unit circle. Then
\lVert{R_{U}(\mkern 1.0mu\cdot\mkern 1.0mu)}\rVert
has a local minimum at
0
. Indeed there is a “conical cusp”, because if
|\zeta|<1
we have

\lVert{R_{U}(\zeta)}\rVert=\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\geq 1+|\zeta|=\lVert{R_{U}(0)}\rVert+|\zeta|.

4
An application to pseudospectra of matrices

Let
A
be a closed operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with
\rho(A)\neq\emptyset
. For
\varepsilon>0
the
\varepsilon
-pseudospectrum
of
A
is defined as

\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)=\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert>\varepsilon^{-1}\},

with the convention that
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert=\infty
if
z\in\sigma(A)
. See
[
8
]
for further information on pseudospectra. We state a well-known result in the finite dimensional case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{C}^{N\times N}
. Then any point
z\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
can be linked to one of the eigenvalues of
A
through a finite polygonal path contained in
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
.

Note that this theorem implies the well-known result
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
that there must be at least one eigenvalue of
A
in each connected component of
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
.
Thus
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
consists of at most
J
connected components where
J
is the number of distinct eigenvalues of
A
.

Remark 4.2
.

The connected components of
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
are not necessarily simply connected. Let
A
be the diagonal matrix with
A_{jj}=j+i(-1)^{j}\sqrt{3}/2
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
, for
N\geq 2
. Elementary geometry shows that for
\varepsilon\in(1,2/\sqrt{3})
the set
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
is
N-1
connected.

Before the proof, recall from Example
3.1
that Corollary
2.7
applies to any matrix
A\in\mathbf{C}^{N\times N}
and any
z\in\rho(A)
. In particular, let
\psi
be a normalized eigenvector for the self-adjoint positive operator
R_{A}(z)^{*}R_{A}(z)
, corresponding to the largest eigenvalue
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert^{2}
. From Remark
2.6
(1), we have that the line segment
[z,z^{\prime}]
used in the growth estimates is given by
z^{\prime}=z+a_{0}\textup{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}
, for a small enough
a_{0}>0
. Here, using Remark
2.8
,
\theta_{0}
can be chosen as:

(i)

If
\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rangle\neq 0
, we may use
\theta_{0}=\Arg(\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rangle)
.

(ii)

If
\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rangle=0
and
\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle\neq 0
, we may use
\theta_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Arg(\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle).

(iii)

If
\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)\psi}\rangle=0=\langle{\psi},{R_{A}(z)^{2}\psi}\rangle
, we may use any
\theta_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi]
.

The novelty of the proof is twofold:

•

The proof only relies on Corollary
2.7
, and is therefore very elementary. In particular, we do not use the fact that the logarithm of the resolvent norm is subharmonic.

•

Although the proof is only partially constructive, one can consider an iterative method based on moving along the specific direction (mentioned above) from Corollary
2.7
, using a line-search method along each of the line segments.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We fix
\varepsilon>0
.
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
is bounded since
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert\to 0
for
\lvert{z}\rvert\to\infty
. Obviously we have

\overline{\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)}\subseteq\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert\geq 1/\varepsilon\},

but equality may fail to hold; see e.g.
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
for this result and for results on
\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert=1/\varepsilon\}
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{J}
be the distinct eigenvalues of
A
. For
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
, let
\psi_{j}
be a normalized eigenvector to
\lambda_{j}
, then
\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert\geq\lVert{R_{A}(z)\psi_{j}}\rVert=1/{\lvert{\lambda_{j}-z}\rvert}
for any
z\in\rho(A)
.
Thus

\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert\geq\frac{1}{\dist(z,\sigma(A))}.

This estimate implies
B_{\varepsilon/2}(\lambda_{j})\subset\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
for each
j
.

Define
f\colon\overline{\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)}\to[\varepsilon^{-1},\infty]
by
f(z)=\lVert{R_{A}(z)}\rVert
, and consider the compact set

K=\overline{\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)}\setminus\Bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}B_{\varepsilon/2}(\lambda_{j})\Bigr).

Then
f
is bounded on
K
and
\dist(z,\sigma(A))\geq\varepsilon/2
for any
z\in K
.

Next fix
z\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
. The remainder of the proof constructs a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
in
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
, which starts at the point
x_{1}=z
and at a finite index
m
enters
B_{\varepsilon/2}(\lambda_{j})
for some
j
. Moreover, each line segment
[x_{n},x_{n+1}]
,
1\leq n\leq m-1
, will lie inside
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
. If
z\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)\setminus K
we are done, as
z
can be connected to an eigenvalue by a single line segment. Hence we may assume
z\in K
.

Let
\delta=(f(z)-\varepsilon^{-1})/2>0
and for any
x\in\overline{\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)}\setminus\sigma(A)
define

r_{x}=\sup\{r>0\mid\exists y\in\mathbf{C},\,\forall\zeta\in[x,y]:\lvert{x-y}\rvert=r,\enskip f(x)<f(y)\text{ and }f(x)-\delta\leq f(\zeta)\}.

Note above that
y\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
but not necessarily
[x,y]\subset\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
. By Corollary
2.7
the supremum is taken over a non-empty set. Hence,
r_{x}>0
and is finite as
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
is bounded. For each
x\in\overline{\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)}\setminus\sigma(A)
there exists
y_{x}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
such that
r_{x}/2<\lvert{y_{x}-x}\rvert\leq r_{x}
,
f(x)<f(y_{x})
, and
f(x)-\delta\leq f(\zeta)
for all
\zeta\in[x,y_{x}]
. Define a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
in
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
by
x_{1}=z
and
x_{n+1}=y_{x_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbf{N}
, which implies

\varepsilon^{-1}<f(z)=f(x_{1})<f(x_{2})<\cdots.

Next we show that
x_{m}
must escape from
K
for some
m
. Assume that this is not true, such that
x_{n}\in K
for all
n
. As
K
is compact, we may assume (by passing to a subsequence) that
x_{n}
converges to
a\in K
.

From Corollary
2.7
, we know that there exists some
a^{\prime}
located at a positive distance from
a
, where
f(a)<f(\zeta)
for all
\zeta
in the line segment
(a,a^{\prime}]=[a,a^{\prime}]\setminus\{a\}
. Since
f
is uniformly continuous on
K
, then
f(x_{n})-\delta\leq f(\zeta)
for every
\zeta\in[x_{n},a^{\prime}]
if
n
is large enough. Furthermore, for large enough
n
, we may assume that

\lvert{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}\rvert\leq\lvert{a-a^{\prime}}\rvert/4\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert{a-x_{n+1}}\rvert\leq\lvert{a-a^{\prime}}\rvert/4.

Then
a^{\prime}
fulfills the criteria for
y
which we used to define
r_{x_{n}}
, but we have

\lvert{a^{\prime}-x_{n}}\rvert\geq\lvert{a^{\prime}-a}\rvert-\lvert{a-x_{n+1}}\rvert-\lvert{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}\rvert\geq 2\lvert{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}\rvert=2\lvert{y_{x_{n}}-x_{n}}\rvert>r_{x_{n}},

contradicting the definition of
r_{x_{n}}
. Thus
x_{m}\not\in K
for some
m
, and moreover
x_{m}\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(\lambda_{j})
for some
j
.

The polygonal path, defined via the points

x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\lambda_{j},

thereby connects
z
to an eigenvalue of
A
, and is contained in
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
. This follows from
[x_{m},\lambda_{j}]\subset B_{\varepsilon/2}(\lambda_{j})\subset\sigma_{\varepsilon}(A)
, and from the definition of
\delta
and the construction of
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
, since every
\zeta
on that polygonal path satisfies

f(\zeta)\geq f(z)-\delta=\frac{f(z)+\varepsilon^{-1}}{2}>\varepsilon^{-1}.\qed

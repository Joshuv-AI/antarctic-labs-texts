Vladislav Pokidkin

Abstract
. The discriminant of a multivariate polynomial with indeterminate coefficients is not necessarily a hypersurface, and the problem of characterizing its codimension remained open for an extended period. We solve this problem for the discriminants of systems of polynomials with indeterminate coefficients in which the number of equations equals the number of unknowns (square polynomial systems). Such discriminants may have several components of different dimensions. In the space of square matrices, we characterize row-generated subspaces on which the determinant is an irreducible polynomial. This allows us to prove Esterov’s conjecture that the discriminant of a nonlinear irreducible square polynomial system is an irreducible hypersurface. Building on this result, we enumerate all components and determine their dimensions and degrees for each of the three conventional formalizations of the notion of a discriminant in this setting (mixed, Cayley, and A A -discriminants), for square systems and for those overdetermined systems that do not reduce to underdetermined ones. Both the proof of the Esterov conjecture and the descriptions of the three types of discriminants are based on the theory of polymatroids.

Introduction

Let

. For a tuple of finite sets

, much attention has been paid, since the work of Gelfand, Kapranov, and Zelevinsky
[
27

, to the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
such that the system of equations
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=0
has a
degenerate root
(a point
x\in T
at which
f_{1}(x)=\cdots=f_{k}(x)=0
and
df_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{k}(x)=0
). The motivation varies from algebraic geometry, algebraic statistics
[
30
,
1
]
, and PDEs
[
28
,
26
]
to mathematical physics
[

,
35
,

]
and symbolic computation
[

A question of particular interest is the classification of the tuples
\mathscr{A}
for which the discriminant is not a hypersurface. For
k=1
, this is the classical problem of dual defective varieties (varieties whose projective dual is not a hypersurface), restricted to toric varieties; it was solved in a series of papers
[
12
,
10
,
15
,
20
,
34
,
25
,
7
]
. At the other extreme, for
n=k
, the discriminant was shown to have a hypersurface component whenever the support sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
cannot be moved by shifts and an automorphism of the lattice into an affine plane or into the tuple of standard simplices. This was proved by Esterov
[
23
]
and by Borger and Nill
[
3
]
, motivated by applications to Galois theory and to the geometry of lattice polytopes, respectively.

Two further types of discriminants can be defined. For a tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the
mixed discriminant
is the closure of the set of all polynomial systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
that have a
nondegenerate multiple root
, that is, a degenerate root
x\in T
such that no proper subtuple of
df_{1}(x),\dots,df_{k}(x)
is linearly dependent. Mixed discriminants were introduced by Cattani, Cueto, Dickenstein, Di Rocco, and Sturmfels
[
6
]
and further studied in
[

,
23
,
13
]
.

For a subset
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}
, the
Cayley trick
for the subtuple
\mathscr{B}=(A_{i},\;i\in I)
is the map sending a polynomial system
f\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
to the polynomial
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)
in the variables
x
and
\lambda
. The support of this polynomial is the
Cayley set

\cay(\mathscr{B})=\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}\times\{i\}\subset M\times\mathbb{Z}^{|I|}
. The
Cayley discriminant
of the subtuple
\mathscr{B}
is the preimage under the Cayley trick of the discriminant of the Cayley set
\cay(\mathscr{B})

[
20
,
23
]
. Cayley discriminants were introduced as intermediate objects that reduce the study of
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants of polynomial systems to that of
A
-discriminants of single polynomials, which are much simpler and better understood.

In the case
n=k
, if the Cayley discriminant is a hypersurface, then the mixed discriminant is the same hypersurface
[
6
]
. For the tuples called irreducible, Esterov showed that the three types of discriminants share a hypersurface component and conjectured that the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant has no other components
[
23
]
. We prove this conjecture; consequently, for an irreducible tuple the three types of discriminants coincide and form a hypersurface.

The following result, which is of independent interest, is crucial for the proof of Esterov’s irreducibility conjecture. Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field, equipped with the Zariski topology. A tuple of vector subspaces
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
of
V
is
irreducible
if, for
0<k<n
, no
k
of them lie in a common
k
-dimensional subspace. The product
V^{\times n}
is isomorphic to the space of
n\times n
matrices, which contains the determinant hypersurface, the set of matrices with zero determinant.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
2.5
)
.

A tuple
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
of vector subspaces in an
n
-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field is irreducible if and only if the intersection of the determinant hypersurface with the subspace
L_{1}\times\dots\times L_{n}
is irreducible in the space of
n\times n
square matrices.

The proof is based on the polymatroid partition of the dual vector
space
[
38

When

We study the case

The answer is stated in terms of the following basic quantity: the

The case of independent tuples is the essence of the matter, and we begin with irreducible tuples. A
proper
subtuple of a tuple is a subtuple that is neither the whole tuple nor the empty tuple. A tuple is

Theorem 1.3

(Esterov
[

For a nonlinear irreducible BK-tuple

, the

-discriminant is a hypersurface
in the space of polynomial systems

.

For an irreducible BK-tuple

is the mixed volume
[
23

. For reducible BK-tuples these questions remain open, and the present work provides a foundation for their solution.

The general description of the discriminants of square polynomial systems is based on Theorem
1.3
and requires the following notions.

Definition 1.4
.

A BK-tuple is
simple
if it is not a union of its proper BK-subtuples. A simple BK-subtuple is
maximal
if it is not contained in any other simple BK-subtuple.

A simple BK-tuple is

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem

)
.

For a BK-tuple

\mathscr{A}
-discriminant has the following distinct components:

(

the Cayley discriminants of the nonprelinear simple BK-subtuples, which have codimension one;

(
b
)

the Cayley discriminants of the prelinear simple BK-subtuples that are not contained in nonprelinear simple BK-subtuples, which have codimension two.

Remark 1.6
.

(1) If a prelinear BK-subtuple is contained in a nonprelinear BK-subtuple, then the Cayley discriminant of the former lies in the Cayley discriminant of the latter.

(2) The proof is based on a specific partition of the tuple
\mathscr{A}
given in Corollary
4.12

\mathscr{A}

[
38
]
.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
9.3
;
[
6
]
)
.

For a BK-tuple, the mixed discriminant is empty, a hypersurface, or a variety of codimension two according to whether the tuple is nonsimple, nonprelinear simple, or prelinear simple. For a simple BK-tuple, the mixed discriminant coincides with the Cayley discriminant.

Although Cayley discriminants are well-known

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem

For a BK-tuple, the Cayley discriminant has codimension

For a quasi-affine algebraic set

Recall that a

L_{i}
into a direct sum. Note that the preimage
q^{-1}(Y_{F})
is an open subset of
Q_{F}
and equals the intersection
E\cap(L\times Y_{F})
. By the construction of
B_{F}
, the preimage
Q_{F}
is the intersection of the set
L\times B_{F}
with the
n-|F|
quadrics
l(x_{i})=0
,
x_{i}\in L_{i}
,
i\in[n]\setminus F
,
l\in B_{F}
. Restricting these equations to the set
L\times Y_{F}
and using the decomposition
L_{i}=U_{i}\oplus\langle v_{i}\rangle
,
x_{i}=u_{i}+\lambda_{i}v_{i}
,
u_{i}\in U_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
, we obtain

l(u_{i})+\lambda_{i}l(v_{i})=0,\qquad l\in Y_{F}.

The value
l(v_{i})
is nonzero for every
l\in Y_{F}
and every
i
, so the variables
\lambda_{i}
can be eliminated using these equations. Hence the coordinate algebra of the preimage
q^{-1}(Y_{F})
is

\mathbb{K}[q^{-1}(Y_{F})]\cong\mathbb{K}\Bigl[\Bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in F}L_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\in[n]\setminus F}U_{j}\Bigr)\times Y_{F}\Bigr],

which is a polynomial algebra over the ring
\mathbb{K}[Y_{F}]
. Therefore, the preimage
q^{-1}(Y_{F})
is a variety and a trivial vector bundle over the open chart
Y_{F}
with the expected fiber. The vectors
v_{i}
and the subspaces
U_{i}
were chosen arbitrarily. Let us show that the base
B_{F}
is covered by the affine charts
Y_{F}=Y_{F}(v)
,
v=(v_{i})_{i\in[n]\setminus F}\in C_{F}=\prod_{i\in[n]\setminus F}L_{i}\setminus L_{F}
,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{v\in C_{F}}Y_{F}(v)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{v\in C_{F}}(B_{F}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[n]\setminus F}v_{i}^{\perp})=\bigcup_{v\in C_{F}}\bigcap_{i\in[n]\setminus F}B_{F}\setminus v_{i}^{\perp}=\bigcap_{i\in[n]\setminus F}(\bigcup_{v_{i}\in L_{i}\setminus L_{F}}B_{F}\setminus v_{i}^{\perp})\overset{(*)}{=}B_{F},

\displaystyle(*)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{v_{i}\in L_{i}\setminus L_{F}}B_{F}\setminus v_{i}^{\perp}=B_{F}\setminus\bigcap_{v_{i}\in L_{i}\setminus L_{F}}v_{i}^{\perp}=B_{F}\setminus L_{i}^{\perp}=B_{F}.

This computation shows that
Q_{F}
is a variety, being a vector bundle over the variety
B_{F}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The intersection of the determinant hypersurface
\mathrm{Det}
with the subspace
L
in the space of square matrices is the union

\mathrm{Det}\cap L=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{L}\setminus[n]}\overline{p(Q_{F})}.

Proof.

The intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
is defined by the single polynomial equation
\det\varphi|_{L}=0
. Hence it is closed, and all its components have codimension one in the subspace
L
.

By the polymatroid partition of the dual vector space
[
38
]
and Proposition
2.2
, the intersection
E\cap(L\times V^{\vee})=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{L}}Q_{F}
is the union of the strata
Q_{F}
indexed by the flats
F
of the polymatroid on
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
with lattice of flats
\mathcal{L}
. By definition, the zero vector is not an eigenvector, so every point
(\varphi,l)
of
E\setminus q^{-1}(0)
is a pair consisting of a degenerate matrix
\varphi
and a nonzero eigenvector
l
with eigenvalue zero. Hence the determinant hypersurface
\mathrm{Det}
is the closure of the projection
p(E\setminus q^{-1}(0))
. Observe that
B_{[n]}=L_{[n]}^{\perp}=(L_{[n]})^{\perp}=V^{\perp}=\{0\}
,

Q_{[n]}=q^{-1}(B_{[n]})\cap(L\times V^{\vee})=L\times\{0\}
, and the intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
is the union

\mathrm{Det}\cap L=\overline{p((E\setminus q^{-1}(0))\cap(L\times V^{\vee}))}=\overline{p((E\cap(L\times V^{\vee}))\setminus Q_{[n]})}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{L}\setminus[n]}\overline{p(Q_{F})},

where
\mathcal{L}\setminus[n]
denotes the lattice of flats with the maximal element
[n]
removed.
∎

Recall that a tuple of subspaces
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
is
independent
if all its subtuples have nonnegative defect. By Rado’s theorem
[
39
]
, this is equivalent to the existence of
n
linearly independent vectors
v_{i}\in L_{i}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

For a dependent tuple
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
of vector subspaces, the subspace
L
lies in the determinant hypersurface
\mathrm{Det}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

A BK-tuple
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
of vector subspaces is irreducible if and only if the intersection of the determinant hypersurface
\mathrm{Det}
with the subspace
L
is a variety.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
The equation
\det\varphi|_{L}=0
is nontrivial, because irreducible tuples of subspaces are independent. Every matrix in the intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
has at least one eigenvector with eigenvalue zero, and the dimension of the projection is bounded by
\dim p(Q_{F})\leq\dim Q_{F}-1=\dim L-\delta(F)-1
.
Since the tuple
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
is irreducible, the proper flats have positive defect, and
\dim p(Q_{F})<\dim L-1
. The inequality
\dim p(Q_{\varnothing})\leq\dim L-1
is not strict only for the empty BK-subtuple
(\varnothing)
, so no other stratum can have codimension one in
L
. Since the intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
is nonempty and has codimension one, we have
\codim_{L}p(Q_{\varnothing})=1
. Since the stratum
Q_{\varnothing}
is a variety, so is its projection
p(Q_{\varnothing})
. The closure of
p(Q_{\varnothing})
contains the projections of all other strata
Q_{F}
and equals the intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
.

(\Leftarrow)
If the BK-tuple is reducible, then, after a change of basis, a proper BK-subtuple corresponds to a proper block in the space of square matrices, the subspace
L
acquires a block-triangular shape, and the determinant
\det|_{L}
factors.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

For a BK-tuple
(L_{1},\dots,L_{n})
of vector subspaces, one can show that the number of components of the intersection
\mathrm{Det}\cap L
equals the number of elements of the Birkhoff poset of BK-subtuples
[
38
]
. This completes the description of the intersection of the determinant hypersurface with a row-generated subspace.

3.
Esterov irreducibility theorem

From now on, we work over the field of complex numbers. For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, we look for solutions of polynomial systems
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
in the algebraic torus
T(\mathscr{A})
. Denote by
\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A})
the number of sets in the tuple
\mathscr{A}
and by
\langle A\rangle
the sublattice generated by a set
A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For an irreducible BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is a variety in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

The tuple
\mathscr{A}
defines a vector subspace of matrices
\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}=\bigoplus_{A\in\mathscr{A}}(\langle A\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C})
in the space of matrices
\mathrm{Mat}_{\dim\langle\mathscr{A}\rangle,\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A})}
. Since the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
is irreducible, the intersection
Y
of the determinant hypersurface
\mathrm{Det}
with the subspace
\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}
is a variety by Theorem
2.5
.

With a finite set
A
and with the tuple
\mathscr{A}
, we associate the integer matrices
\tau_{A}=(a,\;a\in A)
and
\tau_{\mathscr{A}}=\bigoplus_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\tau_{A}
, whose ranks are
\rk\tau_{A}=\dim\langle A\rangle
and
\rk\tau_{\mathscr{A}}=\dim\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}
. The matrix
\tau_{\mathscr{A}}
defines a linear map from
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
to
\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}
by the formula
\tau_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda_{\mathscr{A}})=(\tau_{A}(\lambda_{A}),\;A\in\mathscr{A})
, where
\tau_{A}(\lambda_{A})=\sum_{a\in A}\lambda_{a}\,a
.
Consequently, the preimage
K=\tau_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(Y)
is a variety in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
, being a trivial vector bundle over
Y
of rank
\dim\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}-\dim\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Consider the following sequence of maps:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}&\overset{\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}}{\twoheadrightarrow}&\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}&\overset{\tau_{\mathscr{A}}}{\rightarrow}&\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathscr{A}}&\overset{\det}{\rightarrow}&\mathbb{C},\end{array}

where
\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}(x,c)=(c_{a}x^{a})_{a\in A\in\mathscr{A}}
is the torus action. The preimage
W=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(K)
is a variety isomorphic to
T(\mathscr{A})\times K
; indeed, the isomorphism is given by the regular maps
W\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\limits}}T(\mathscr{A})\times K
such that
\iota(x,c_{a})=(x,c_{a}x^{-a})
and
\sigma(x,c_{a})=(x,c_{a}x^{a})
for every
a\in A\in\mathscr{A}
. The composition of these maps gives the condition for a root
x
of a polynomial system
c=(c_{a})_{a\in A\in\mathscr{A}}
to be a degenerate root:

\det\circ\tau_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}(x,c)=\det\|\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}x^{a}a\|_{A\in\mathscr{A}}=0
.
This polynomial is irreducible because
W
is a variety and the determinant is multilinear in the rows.

Consider the vector subspaces
\Pi_{A}
of
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
defined by the linear equations
\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}=0
, and their intersection
\Pi=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\Pi_{A}
. Denote by
c_{A}
the coefficient corresponding to the origin of the finite set
A
. From each linear equation we can express the variable
c_{A}=-\sum_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}}c_{a}
. The determinant
\det\|\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}a\|_{A\in\mathscr{A}}
does not depend on the coefficients
c_{A}
,
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Hence the intersection
X=K\cap\Pi
of the variety
K
with the subspace
\Pi
is a variety defined by the same determinant. Indeed, the coordinate algebra
\mathbb{C}[X]
is an integral domain:

\mathbb{C}[X]=\frac{\mathbb{C}[c_{a},\,a\in A\in\mathscr{A}]}{\langle\sum_{a\in A}c_{a},\;A\in\mathscr{A};\;\det\|\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}a\|_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\rangle}=\frac{\mathbb{C}[c_{a},\,a\in A\setminus\{0\},\,A\in\mathscr{A}]}{\langle\det\|\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}a\|_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\rangle}.

Thus the preimage
Z=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(X)
is a variety, isomorphic to the direct product
T(\mathscr{A})\times X
via the same maps
\iota
and
\sigma
. Each preimage
\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(\Pi_{A})
is the zero locus of the equation
f_{A}(x)=0
in
T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Hence the variety
Z
projects onto a dense subset of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
under the map
T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
,

\pi(x,c)=c
:

\displaystyle Z=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(X)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(K\cap\Pi)=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(K)\cap\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(\Pi)=W\cap\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(\Pi_{A})

\displaystyle=V(\det\|\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}x^{a}a\|_{A\in\mathscr{A}})\cap\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{A}}V(f_{A}(x)).

Therefore, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
, being the closure of the image of a variety, is a variety.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

A linear (respectively, nonlinear) irreducible BK-tuple is called a
lir
(respectively, a
nir
).

Remark 3.3
.

Linear BK-tuples are the only tuples with unit mixed volume
[
6
,
17
]
when
\langle\mathscr{A}\rangle=M
. The discriminants of linear tuples were studied in
[
23
,
3
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

In the space
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is a variety of codimension one if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a nir, and of codimension two if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a lir.

Proof.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a nir, then the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
has a component of codimension one in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
by
[
23
]
. By Theorem
3.1
, this component is unique, and the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is a variety.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a lir, then
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
is a space of coefficients of systems of linear equations. In this case, the discriminant is the intersection of the subspace
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
with the determinantal variety of matrices of rank at most
\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A})-1
in the space of matrices of size
\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A})\times(\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A})+1)
. This intersection is irreducible by Theorem
3.1
, and it has codimension two, being an intersection of two determinantal hypersurfaces.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The set of degenerate roots of a generic polynomial system of
D_{\mathscr{A}}
has dimension zero if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a nir, and dimension one if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a lir.

Proof.

Consider the dominant map
Z\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}D_{\mathscr{A}}

from the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For a system
\Phi\in D_{\mathscr{A}}
, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\Phi)
is isomorphic to the set of degenerate roots of
\Phi
. The generic fiber
\pi^{-1}(\Phi)
has dimension
\dim Z-\dim D_{\mathscr{A}}
, which is determined by Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Corollary 3.6

(
[
6
,
23
]
)
.

For a nonlinear irreducible BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant, the Cayley discriminant, and the mixed discriminant coincide and form a hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

In this setting, the paper
[
6
]
established the equivalence between the mixed discriminant and the Cayley discriminant. Esterov proved that the three types of discriminants coincide up to codimension two
[
23
]
. Theorem
3.4
shows that there are no other components other than a single hypersurface.
∎

4.
Combinatorics behind discriminants

This section gives an overview of combinatorial results on configurations of sublattices, tuples of finite sets, and mixed volumes. For a tuple of sublattices
\mathsf{n}=(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
of a lattice
M
, the notion of irreducibility is motivated by questions of algebraic geometry. In the simplest case of a tuple of
n
sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, a reducible tuple admits a partition into subtuples corresponding to irreducible tuples. This partition of the tuple
\mathsf{n}
is determined by the realizable polymatroid on
\mathsf{n}
, and it is a combinatorial shadow of the decomposition of the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant of a polynomial system into irreducible components.

A tuple of sublattices can be regarded as a realizable polymatroid, and general polymatroids admit the same characterizations and terminology as tuples of sublattices. For simplicity, and since they are not needed here, we do not state results for general polymatroids; instead, we present the results of the general theory for tuples of sublattices. Note, however, that it was the study of discriminants that motivated these results in polymatroid theory and inspired the work
[
38
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
saturation
of a sublattice
S
is the maximal sublattice
\overline{S}
containing
S
and having the same dimension as
S
.

The
linear span

\langle\mathsf{n}\rangle
of a tuple of sublattices
\mathsf{n}
is the smallest sublattice containing all sublattices of the tuple. The
cardinality

\mathfrak{c}(\mathsf{n})
is the number of sublattices in the tuple
\mathsf{n}
, and the
defect
of
\mathsf{n}
is the difference
\delta(\mathsf{n})=\dim\langle\mathsf{n}\rangle-\mathfrak{c}(\mathsf{n})
.

A tuple is
essential
if every proper subtuple has strictly greater defect than the whole tuple. A tuple is
independent
if all its subtuples have nonnegative defect. An independent tuple is
irreducible
if all its proper subtuples have positive defect. A
BK-tuple
is an independent tuple of zero defect.

For a tuple
\mathsf{n}
in a lattice
M
and a subtuple
\mathsf{k}
of
\mathsf{n}
, the
quotient tuple
is the projection
\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}=\pi(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})
of the complementary subtuple, where
\pi\colon M\rightarrow M/\overline{\langle\mathsf{k}\rangle}
.

The notion of a BK-tuple arose from the Kouchnirenko–Bernshtein theorem
[
2
]
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
38
]
)
.

For a reducible BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
and a BK-subtuple
\mathsf{k}
,

(
a
)

the linear spans of any two distinct irreducible BK-subtuples intersect only at the origin;

(
b
)

the quotient tuple
\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}
is a BK-tuple;

(
c
)

the BK-subtuples of the quotient
\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}
are in bijection with the BK-subtuples of
\mathsf{n}
containing
\mathsf{k}
;

(
d
)

for a BK-subtuple
\mathsf{l}
of
\mathsf{k}
, there is an isomorphism of quotient tuples
\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}\cong(\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{l})/(\mathsf{k}/\mathsf{l})
.

Definition 4.3
.

An
order ideal

\mathtt{I}
of a poset
\mathtt{P}
is a subset such that
\beta\in\mathtt{I}
and
\alpha\leq\beta
imply
\alpha\in\mathtt{I}
. For an element
\alpha
of a poset, the
principal order ideal

(\alpha)
is the order ideal consisting of all elements
\beta\leq\alpha
. The dual notions, obtained by reversing all inequalities, are
order filter
and
principal order filter

[\alpha]
.

Since intersections and unions of BK-subtuples of a BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
are again BK-subtuples
[
38
]
, the set of BK-subtuples ordered by inclusion forms a distributive lattice. By the fundamental theorem of finite distributive lattices
[
40
]
, there exists a poset
\mathtt{P}
, called the
Birkhoff poset
, whose lattice of order ideals is isomorphic to this distributive lattice.

Lemma 4.4
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
and its subtuple
\mathsf{k}
, the complement
\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}
is a BK-tuple if and only if the subtuple
\mathsf{k}
corresponds to an order filter in the Birkhoff poset of the tuple
\mathsf{n}
.

Proof.

The tuple
\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}
is a BK-subtuple of
\mathsf{n}
if and only if it corresponds to an order ideal, which is equivalent to the complementary subtuple
\mathsf{k}
corresponding to an order filter of the Birkhoff poset of
\mathsf{n}
.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

A
filtration
of a tuple
\mathsf{n}
is an increasing chain of subtuples
F_{0}\mathsf{n}\hookrightarrow F_{1}\mathsf{n}\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow F_{m}\mathsf{n}=\mathsf{n}
. A filtration is a
BK-filtration
if all the quotients
F_{j}\mathsf{n}/F_{j-1}\mathsf{n}
are BK-tuples, and a BK-filtration is
maximal
if all these quotients are irreducible.

Proposition 4.6
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
, the number of subtuples in a maximal BK-filtration equals the number of elements of the Birkhoff poset. Moreover, for any two maximal BK-filtrations
F_{\bullet}\mathsf{n}
and
F^{\prime}_{\bullet}\mathsf{n}
, there exist linear isomorphisms between the successive quotients
F_{j}\mathsf{n}/F_{j-1}\mathsf{n}
and
F_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{n}/F_{j-1}^{\prime}\mathsf{n}
.

The Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}
defines a partition of the BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
: every element
\alpha
corresponds to a subtuple
\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
of
\mathsf{n}
, every principal order ideal
(\alpha)
corresponds to the simple BK-subtuple
\mathsf{k}_{(\alpha)}=\bigsqcup_{\beta\leq\alpha}\mathsf{k}_{\beta}
, and every order ideal
\mathtt{I}
of
\mathtt{P}
corresponds to the BK-subtuple
\mathsf{k}_{\mathtt{I}}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{I}}\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 4.7

(
[
38
]
)
.

A BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
admits a unique partition
\mathsf{n}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{P}}\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
such that the tuple
\hat{\mathsf{k}}_{\alpha}=\mathsf{k}_{(\alpha)}/\mathsf{k}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}
is an irreducible BK-tuple for every element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}
.

We now prove a few further statements needed for the description of Cayley discriminants. Recall that circuits always have defect
-1

[
38
]
.

Lemma 4.8
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathsf{n}
of cardinality
n
and a sublattice
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the tuple
(\mathsf{n},S)
corresponds to a circuit of the induced matroid if and only if
\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}>0
for every maximal element
\alpha
of the Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}
of the tuple
\mathsf{n}
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
For a maximal element
\alpha
, the tuple
\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
is a BK-subtuple with poset
\mathtt{P}\setminus\alpha
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there is a maximal element
\alpha\in\mathtt{P}
such that
\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}=0
. Then
S\subseteq\overline{\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}
, and the tuple
(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha},S)
is dependent. Therefore, the subtuple
\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
consists of coloops, and the tuple
(\mathsf{n},S)
contains a circuit as a proper subtuple.

(\Leftarrow)
Assume the condition holds. Supposing for a contradiction that
(\mathsf{n},S)
is not a circuit, there must exist coloops. This means there is a proper BK-subtuple
\mathsf{k}
such that
S\subseteq\langle\mathsf{k}\rangle
. Choosing an element
\alpha
corresponding to a maximal element in
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}}=\mathtt{P}\setminus\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
, yields the contradiction:
\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}\leq\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}\rangle}=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathsf{n}
be a BK-tuple with Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}
, and let
\mathsf{k}
be a BK-subtuple of
\mathsf{n}
with Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Then the Birkhoff poset of the quotient
\mathsf{n}/\mathsf{k}
is the complement
\mathtt{P}\setminus\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\mathsf{n}
be a BK-tuple with Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}
, and let
\mathsf{k}
be a BK-subtuple of
\mathsf{n}
that contains no maximal simple BK-subtuple of
\mathsf{n}
. Then the following statements hold for the sublattice
S=\langle\mathsf{k}\rangle\cap\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k}\rangle
:

(
a
)

\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}>0
for every maximal element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
of the subtuple
\mathsf{k}
;

(
b
)

the tuple
(\mathsf{k},S)
corresponds to a circuit.

Proof.

(
a
) The condition that the BK-subtuple
\mathsf{k}
contains no maximal simple BK-subtuple of
\mathsf{n}
is equivalent to the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
containing no maximal element of the poset
\mathtt{P}
.

For a maximal element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}
, the tuple
\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}
is a BK-subtuple with poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}\setminus\alpha
. Hence the quotient tuple
\mathsf{n}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})
is a BK-tuple with Birkhoff poset
\mathtt{P}\setminus(\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}\setminus\alpha)
by Lemma
4.9
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
\dim S/\overline{\langle\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}\rangle}=0
. By the definition of
S
, this is equivalent to
\dim\overline{\langle\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}\cap\overline{\langle(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}=0
. Since the tuples
\mathsf{n}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})
and
\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})
are BK-tuples, we can use the equality of dimensions

\underbrace{\dim\langle\mathsf{n}\setminus(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}_{=\mathfrak{c}(\mathsf{n}\setminus(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}))}+\underbrace{\dim\overline{\langle\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}\cap\overline{\langle(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}}_{=0}=\underbrace{\dim\langle\mathsf{k}_{\alpha}/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle}_{=\mathfrak{c}(\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})}+\dim\langle(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})\rangle

to conclude that the tuple
(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})/(\mathsf{k}\setminus\mathsf{k}_{\alpha})
has dimension
\mathfrak{c}(\mathsf{n}\setminus\mathsf{k})
and is therefore a BK-tuple. Then
\alpha
is a maximal element of the posets
\mathtt{P}\setminus(\mathtt{P}_{\mathsf{k}}\setminus\alpha)
and
\mathtt{P}
, a contradiction.

(
b
) Combine statement (
a
) with Lemma
4.8
.
∎

We use these results to construct partitions of tuples of finite sets. The
affine span
of a finite set
A
in a lattice
M
is the affine sublattice
\langle A\rangle
generated by this set. By a shift, we can always ensure that the finite set
A
contains the origin, so that its affine span is a sublattice of
M
. Then a tuple of finite sets
\mathscr{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})
, all containing the origin, generates a tuple of sublattices, and we describe a tuple of finite sets through this tuple of sublattices.

By the mixed volume
\MV_{M}(\mathscr{A})
of a tuple of finite sets
\mathscr{A}
in a lattice
M
we mean the mixed volume of the convex hulls of these sets. We stress that a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
has positive mixed volume in the sublattice
\langle\mathscr{A}\rangle
by Minkowski’s theorem (see
[
32
, Theorem 8]
). Another important result establishes the compatibility of the poset partition of a reducible BK-tuple of finite sets with the mixed volume:

Theorem 4.11
.

For tuples
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
of zero defect in a lattice
M
, the mixed volume factors as

\MV_{M}(\mathscr{A})=\MV_{\overline{\langle\mathscr{B}\rangle}}(\mathscr{B})\,\MV_{M/\overline{\langle\mathscr{B}\rangle}}(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}).

Proof.

See
[
41
, Lemma 4]
for a geometric proof,
[
16
, Lemma 3.12]
for an analytic proof, and
[
23
, Theorem 1.10]
for an algebraic proof.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

A BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
admits a unique partition
\mathscr{A}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
such that the tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}=\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}/\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}
is an irreducible BK-tuple for every element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

For a dependent tuple of finite sets, the independent subtuples are the independent sets of the matroid induced by the realizable polymatroid
[
38
]
. To describe the discriminant, we will need the subtuple that is the maximal cycle of the induced matroid.

5.
Discriminants of polynomial systems

For a lattice
N
, the
dual lattice
is
M=N^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
,
and the
algebraic torus
is
T(N)=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
The
character group
is
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T(N),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
,
and its elements
\chi_{a}
are called
characters

[
24
,
8
]
. The character group is isomorphic to the dual lattice
M
. For a tuple of finite sets
\mathscr{A}\subset M
, we have defined the space of polynomial systems
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
; the solutions of a polynomial system
\Phi
are sought in the torus
T(N)
.

A split short exact sequence of lattices
0\rightarrow N^{\prime}\rightarrow N\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}N^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
induces split short exact sequences of the dual lattices
0\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}M\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
, of the character groups, and of the tori. The projection of lattices
\pi
induces the
pushforward

\pi_{*}=\pi\otimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of tori and the
pullback

\pi^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{*},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
of character groups,
\pi^{*}(\chi_{a})=\chi_{a}\circ\pi_{*}=\chi_{i(a)}
,
for a character
\chi_{a}
of
T(N^{\prime\prime})
.

A monomorphism of dual lattices
M^{\prime\prime}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}M
yields the equality of discriminants
\pi^{*}(D_{\mathscr{A}})=D_{i(\mathscr{A})}
. In particular, an isomorphism of dual lattices
M^{\prime\prime}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}M
induces an isomorphism of discriminants
D_{\mathscr{A}}\cong D_{i(\mathscr{A})}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The discriminants
D_{\mathscr{A}}
and
D_{\mathscr{B}}
are isomorphic if one of the following conditions holds:

(
a
)

\mathscr{B}=g\mathscr{A}
for an element
g
of the affine general linear group
\mathsf{AGL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
;

(
b
)

\mathscr{B}=\lambda\mathscr{A}
for a nonzero integer
\lambda
;

(
c
)

there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the sets of the tuples
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
under which each set
A\in\mathscr{A}
is a translate of the corresponding set
B\in\mathscr{B}
.

These observations allow us to choose convenient tuples in the subsequent proofs and to use the results of Section
4
. From now on, we assume that the linear span of the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is the whole dual lattice,
\langle\mathscr{A}\rangle=M
, and we denote the torus
T(N)
by
T(\mathscr{A})
.

Consider a split short exact sequence of dual lattices,
0\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
.
Being finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, lattices are reflexive:
N\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})
. Hence the lattice
\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to a lattice
N^{\prime\prime}
with
M^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{Hom}(N^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{Z})
. Let us compute the lattice
N^{\prime\prime}
. The sublattice
M^{\prime\prime}
of the dual lattice naturally corresponds to the sublattice
N^{\prime}=M^{\prime\prime\perp}=\{n\in N\mid m(n)=0\;\forall m\in M^{\prime\prime}\}\subseteq N
. By the splitting, the lattice
N^{\prime\prime}
can be taken to be the quotient
N/N^{\prime}=N/M^{\prime\prime\perp}
.

For a subtuple
\mathscr{B}
of a tuple
\mathscr{A}
, we look for the solutions of a subsystem from
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
in the torus
T(N/\overline{\langle\mathscr{B}\rangle}^{\perp})
, which we denote by
T(\mathscr{B})
.

A
cofiltration

G_{\bullet}N
is a sequence of quotients
N=G_{i}N\twoheadrightarrow G_{i-1}N\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}N\twoheadrightarrow 0
. Cofiltrations of a lattice
N
are in bijection with filtrations of the dual lattice
M
.

Every BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
admits a maximal BK-filtration
F_{\bullet}
. The saturated linear spans of the tuples of this BK-filtration form a filtration
F_{\bullet}M
of the dual lattice,
F_{i}M=\overline{\langle F_{i}\mathscr{A}\rangle}
. This gives a cofiltration
G_{\bullet}N
and hence a cofiltration
G_{\bullet}T(N)=T(G_{\bullet}N)
of the torus. Therefore, every polynomial system
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
admits a BK-filtration
F_{\bullet}\Phi
, and the set of its solutions admits a cofiltration
G_{\bullet}V(\Phi)
. If the system
\Phi
is generic, then the projections of finite sets
G_{i}V(\Phi)\twoheadrightarrow G_{i-1}V(\Phi)
are finite covers of degree
\MV_{G_{i}N/G_{i-1}N}(F_{i}\mathscr{A}/F_{i-1}\mathscr{A})
by Theorem
4.11
and the Kouchnirenko–Bernshtein theorem. For every subsystem
F_{i}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}})=\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}
, we look for solutions in the torus
T(\mathscr{B})=T(G_{i}N)
.

6.
BK-multiplication

To compute the discriminants of BK-tuples, we introduce a specific multiplication of varieties.

Consider a split short exact sequence of dual lattices
0\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow M\overset{\tau}{\rightarrow}M^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
,
a finite set
A\subset M
, its projection
B=\tau(A)
, and the corresponding splitting
T(N)=T(N^{\prime\prime})\times T(N^{\prime})
of the algebraic torus. A point of the torus
T(N)
is a pair
(x,y)\in T(N^{\prime\prime})\times T(N^{\prime})
. The substitution
f(x,y)\overset{\ev_{x_{0}}}{\longrightarrow}f(x_{0},y)

of a fixed point
x_{0}
of the torus
T(N^{\prime\prime})
into polynomials from
\mathbb{C}_{A}
is a linear projection
\mathbb{C}_{A}\overset{\ev_{x_{0}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}_{B}
onto
\mathbb{C}_{B}
. For a tuple of finite sets
\mathscr{A}\subset M
and
\mathscr{B}=\tau(\mathscr{A})
, the substitution
\Phi(x,y)\overset{\ev_{x_{0}}}{\longrightarrow}\Phi(x_{0},y)

of the point
x_{0}
is a linear projection
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}\overset{\ev_{x_{0}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For a quasi-affine algebraic set
Y\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
, a point
x
of the torus
T(N^{\prime\prime})
, and the preimage
E_{\mathscr{A}}^{x}(Y)=\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the triple
(E_{\mathscr{A}}^{x}(Y),\ev_{x},Y)
is a trivial vector bundle over
Y
of rank
\sum_{A\in\mathscr{A}}(|A|-|\tau(A)|)
.

Definition 6.2
.

We call the total space
E_{\mathscr{A}}^{x}(Y)
the
evaluation bundle
over
Y
with respect to the evaluation at the point
x
of the torus
T(N^{\prime\prime})
.

Remark 6.3
.

For tuples of finite sets
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
and a point
x
of the torus
T(\mathscr{B})
, the evaluation bundle
E_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{x}(Y)
is a variety in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}

if and only if
Y
is a variety in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For a chain of BK-tuples
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
, a quasi-affine algebraic set
Y\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
, and a point
x\in T(\mathscr{C})
, we have

E_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{C}}^{x}\left(Y\times\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}}\right)=E_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}^{x}(Y)\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}.

Proof.

The evaluation map
\ev_{x}\colon\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}

splits as the direct sum
\ev_{x}=u_{x}\oplus v_{x}
of the two maps

u_{x}\colon\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
and
v_{x}\colon\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}}
.
Hence
\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y\times\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}})=u_{x}^{-1}(Y)\times v_{x}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}})
.
∎

Theorem 6.5

(Kouchnirenko–Bernshtein
[
2
]
)
.

For a tuple
\mathscr{A}
of
n
finite sets in an
n
-dimensional lattice
M
, there exists a nonempty open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
such that the set of solutions of every polynomial system
\Phi\in U
consists of exactly
\MV_{M}(\mathscr{A})
points.

Following Esterov
[
22
]
, we call the complement
B_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus U
the
bifurcation divisor
. The discriminants of all types lie in the bifurcation divisor.

Definition 6.6
.

For BK-tuples of finite sets
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
, the
BK-multiplication
of algebraic sets
X\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
and
Y\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}

is the constructible set

X\circ Y=\Bigl\{\Phi\times\bigcup_{x\in V(\Phi)}\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)\Bigm|\Phi\in X\Bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}},

where
V(\Phi)
is the set of zeros of the polynomial system
\Phi\in X
in the torus
T(\mathscr{B})
, and the preimage
\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)=E_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{x}(Y)

is the evaluation bundle in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
over
Y
with respect to the evaluation at a root
x\in T(\mathscr{B})
of the polynomial system
\Phi
. We denote by
X\bullet Y
the Zariski closure of the BK-multiplication
X\circ Y
.

Lemma 6.7
.

The BK-multiplication of algebraic sets is a constructible set.

Proof.

The BK-multiplication is the projection to the space of polynomial systems
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
of the following quasi-affine algebraic set:

\{(x,\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})\in T(\mathscr{B})\times X\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\mid\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}(x)=0,\;\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})\in Y\}
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

By the Kouchnirenko–Bernshtein theorem (Theorem
6.5
),
the generic fiber of the BK-multiplication over
X
is a union of
\MV_{\overline{\langle\mathscr{B}\rangle}}(\mathscr{B})
distinct trivial evaluation bundles over
Y
.

Remark 6.9
.

Denote by
Z_{\mathscr{B}}
the set of polynomial systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
without solutions. The contribution
\Phi\times\bigcup_{x\in V(\Phi)}\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)
is empty for every polynomial system
\Phi\in Z_{\mathscr{B}}
; this is why we take the Zariski closure of the multiplication.

Lemma 6.10
.

A variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{B}}
satisfies
X\circ\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}=X\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

Every system
\Phi\in X
has at least one solution, and every fiber is the same vector space
\bigcup_{x\in V(\Phi)}\ev_{x}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
. Hence the BK-multiplication is the product
X\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

In particular, we have the equality
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
, which is a shadow of the factorization of the mixed volume in Theorem
4.11
.

Lemma 6.12
.

For a tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the set of solutions
V(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(x))
is a variety in
T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

Let
\Pi=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\Pi_{A}
be the intersection of the hyperplanes
\Pi_{A}
in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
defined by the equations
\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}=0
,
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Consider the map
T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}\overset{\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}

such that
\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}(x,c)=(c_{a}x^{a})_{a\in A\in\mathscr{A}}
. The preimage
W=\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(\Pi)
is a variety isomorphic to the product
T(\mathscr{A})\times\Pi
. Indeed, the isomorphism is given by the regular maps
W\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\limits}}T(\mathscr{A})\times\Pi
such that
\iota(c_{a})=(c_{a}x^{-a})
and
\sigma(c_{a})=(c_{a}x^{a})
for every
a\in A\in\mathscr{A}
and
x\in T(\mathscr{A})
(
\iota
and
\sigma
are the identity on the remaining coordinates). Moreover, the preimage
\sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}(\Pi_{A})
of each hyperplane is the zero locus of the equation
f_{A}(x)=0
in
T(\mathscr{A})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Hence the variety
W
coincides with the set of solutions
V(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(x))
.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

For a variety
Y\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
,
the BK-multiplication
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet Y
is a variety.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.12
, for a BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}
the set of solutions
V(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}(x))
is a variety
W
in
T(\mathscr{B})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
. Consider the evaluation map

T(\mathscr{B})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}\overset{\ev}{\twoheadrightarrow}T(\mathscr{B})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}

such that
\ev(x,\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})=(x,\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}))
. The product
W\times Y
is a variety, and the preimage
\ev^{-1}(W\times Y)
is a trivial vector bundle over
W\times Y
. Indeed, for fixed
x\in T(\mathscr{B})
, the preimage is defined by the linear equations
\sum_{a\in A_{b}}c_{a}x^{a}=c_{b}
,
b\in B\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}
(every set
A
in
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}
admits the partition
A=\bigsqcup_{b\in B}A_{b}
, where
B\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}
is the corresponding set of the quotient). Hence the projection of the variety
\ev^{-1}(W\times Y)
to the space
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
is an irreducible constructible set, which equals the BK-multiplication
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\circ Y
, and its closure is a variety.
∎

Lemma 6.14
.

For quasi-affine algebraic sets
X,X^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{B}}
,
Y,Y^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
, the BK-multiplication is distributive in the following sense:

(
a
)

X\circ(Y\cup Y^{\prime})=X\circ Y\cup X\circ Y^{\prime}
,

(
b
)

(X\cup X^{\prime})\circ Y=X\circ Y\cup X^{\prime}\circ Y
,

(
c
)

(X\cap X^{\prime})\circ Y=X\circ Y\cap X^{\prime}\circ Y
.

Proof.

(
a
) By construction, both sides equal

\{\Phi\times\bigcup_{x\in V(\Phi)}\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y\cup Y^{\prime})\mid\Phi\in X\}
, because
\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y\cup Y^{\prime})=\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)\cup\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
. (
b
) The proof is the same as in (
a
). (
c
) Both sides equal
\{\Phi\times\bigcup_{x\in V(\Phi)}\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)\mid\Phi\in X\cap X^{\prime}\}
.
∎

Remark 6.15
.

We have given the explicit proof of this simple lemma in order to point out that, for varieties
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{B}}
and
Y,Y^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
, the inclusion
X\circ(Y\cap Y^{\prime})\subset X\circ Y\cap X\circ Y^{\prime}
is usually strict, and the right-hand side is in general reducible (Remark
6.18
). The reason is that for distinct roots
x
,
x^{\prime}
of a polynomial system
\Phi\in X
, the intersection
\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y)\cap\ev_{x^{\prime}}^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
can be nontrivial, whereas the left-hand side is determined by
\ev_{x}^{-1}(Y\cap Y^{\prime})
for a single root
x
. Another reason is that a resultant of resultants is reducible in general (see
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
).

If the complement
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}
is a BK-tuple, then
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}
and
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.

Lemma 6.16
.

If the complement
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}
is a BK-tuple, then the closed BK-multiplication is commutative:
X\bullet Y\cong Y\bullet X\cong\overline{X\times Y}
for quasi-affine algebraic sets
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{B}}
and
Y\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

In this case, the torus splits as
T(\mathscr{A})=T(\mathscr{B})\times T(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})
, and the evaluations
\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})=\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
and
\ev_{y}(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}})=\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}
are trivial, where
x
is a root of a polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}\in X
and
y
is a root of a polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\in Y
. Hence
X\bullet Y
is the closure of the projection of
\{(x,\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})\in T(\mathscr{B})\times X\times Y\mid\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}(x)=0\}
to the space
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
, which equals
\overline{X\times Y}
. Similarly, it is the closure of the projection
Y\circ X
of
\{(y,\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})\in T(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})\times X\times Y\mid\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}(y)=0\}
to the space
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
.
∎

Proposition 6.17

(Associativity)
.

For a chain of BK-tuples
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}

and a quasi-affine algebraic set
Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\setminus Z_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
, the BK-multiplication is associative in the following sense:

(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet(Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}).

Proof.

Let us show that both algebraic sets are defined by the same equations. By Lemma
6.10
, the left-hand side is the product
(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
. As in Theorem
6.13
, this product is the closure of the projection of the quasi-affine algebraic set
(T(\mathscr{C})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
defined by the equations
\Phi_{\mathscr{C}}(x)=0
and
\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}})\in Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
,
x\in T(\mathscr{C})
.

By Lemma
6.10
, the right-hand side has the form
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet(Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\times\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}})
. This product is the closure of the projection of the quasi-affine algebraic set
T(\mathscr{C})\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet(Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\times\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}})
defined by the equations
\Phi_{\mathscr{C}}(x)=0
and
\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{C}})\in Y_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\times\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}}
,
x\in T(\mathscr{C})
. Since the conditions
\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})\in\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})/\mathscr{C}}
are vacuous, both sides are the closure of the projection of the same quasi-affine algebraic set in
T(\mathscr{C})\times{\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}}
.
∎

Remark 6.18
.

In general, BK-multiplication is not associative, and irreducibility can fail.

Consider the eleven-dimensional space of polynomial systems
f_{1}(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}
,
f_{2}(x,y)=b_{0}+b_{1}y+b_{2}y^{2}+b_{3}xy
,
f_{3}(x,z)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+c_{3}xz
. For the chain of BK-subtuples
\mathscr{C}=(A_{1})\subset\mathscr{B}=(A_{1},A_{2})\subset\mathscr{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
, the discriminants
D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
and
D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
are irreducible, and the inclusion
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet(D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}})\subset(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
is strict; this can be checked at the point corresponding to the system
2-3x+x^{2}=1+y+y^{2}+xy=1+z^{2}+xz=0
. The right-hand side is reducible. However, by the preceding proposition, nonassociativity does not arise in this paper. The Claude AI model constructed this example.

7.
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants

The idea of decomposing a polynomial system into simpler subsystems is not new
[
43
,
5
,
32
,
4
,
13
]
; here we apply it to
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants.

Lemma 7.1
.

In a vector space
V
, consider a linearly dependent set of vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and a linearly independent subset
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in J}
,
J\subset I
. Then the set
\{\pi(v_{k})\}_{k\in I\setminus J}
is linearly dependent in
U
, where
\pi\colon V\rightarrow U=V/\langle v_{j}\rangle_{j\in J}
is the projection.

Proof.

Let
W
be the kernel of
\pi
, so that
V\cong U\oplus W
. Since the set
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is linearly dependent, there exist constants
c_{i}
, not all zero, such that
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}v_{i}=0
. Using the isomorphism, decompose each vector as
v_{i}=u_{i}+w_{i}
. The vectors
v_{j}
,
j\in J
, generate the subspace
W
, so
u_{j}=0
for
j\in J
. Therefore, the projected set is linearly dependent:
\sum_{k\in I\setminus J}c_{k}u_{k}=0
,
u_{k}=\pi(v_{k})
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

For BK-tuples
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is the union

D_{\mathscr{A}}=D_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}\cup\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}.

Proof.

For a generic system
\Phi\in D_{\mathscr{A}}
, there exists a degenerate root
(x,y)\in T(\mathscr{A})
,
x\in T(\mathscr{B})
, such that the vectors
\{df_{A}(x,y)\}_{A\in\mathscr{A}}
are linearly dependent. If the set
\{df_{B}(x)\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}

is linearly dependent, then the point
\Phi
belongs to
D_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.
Otherwise, the set
\{d(\ev_{x}f_{C})(y)\}_{C\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}

is linearly dependent by Lemma
7.1
, and substituting the root
x
into the system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
gives a system
\ev_{x}\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
lying in the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
. Hence the system
\Phi
lies in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.
∎

The theorem above splits the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
into two parts,
D_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
and
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
. Each part may consist of several components, and the theorem says nothing about inclusions between the parts; it only shows that there may be more than one component. Nevertheless, the result is significant, since it allows us to associate with every BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}
a part
C(\mathscr{B})
of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
by a sequence of splittings, using the associativity of BK-multiplication. An irreducible part is called a
stratum
.

Proposition 7.3
.

The part
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
is not contained in the part
D_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

The part
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}

contains the dense subset
(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\setminus B_{\mathscr{B}})\circ D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
,
which is not contained in the closed BK-multiplication
D_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Suppose that the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
(see Corollary
4.12
) has more than one connected component, and let
\mathtt{I}
be one of them. Then neither of the parts
C(\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{I}})
and
C(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{I}})
is contained in the other.

Proof.

Note that
\mathtt{I}
and
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\mathtt{I}
are order ideals corresponding to BK-subtuples, and apply Proposition
7.3
twice.
∎

Lemma 7.5

(Separation lemma)
.

For BK-tuples
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
, the corresponding part
C(\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C})
of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

This follows by a direct computation using Theorem
7.2
and Proposition
6.17
:

\displaystyle D_{\mathscr{A}}

\displaystyle=D_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}\cup\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet(D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C})/(\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C})}\cup\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C})/(\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C})})

\displaystyle=D_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}\cup\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}\cup\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}.

∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Every element of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
corresponds to a stratum of
D_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

Corollary
4.12
provides the unique decomposition of the tuple
\mathscr{A}
, which is encoded by the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Every element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
corresponds to a subtuple
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
such that the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is irreducible. The corresponding part of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}\bullet D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}}
by the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
). This part is irreducible, and hence a stratum, by Theorems
6.13
and
3.4
on the irreducibility of the BK-multiplication and of the discriminant
D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
.
∎

Our next goal is to determine which strata are not contained in one another.

Lemma 7.7
.

For incomparable elements
\alpha,\beta
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, neither of the strata
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
is contained in the other.

Proof.

Consider the order ideal
(\alpha)\cup(\beta)
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the union of the two principal order ideals, and the corresponding BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\cup(\beta)}
. By Proposition
7.3
, it suffices to study the strata
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
in the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{B}}
. Consider the intersection
(\alpha)\cap(\beta)
of the principal order ideals and the BK-subtuple
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\cap(\beta)}
. By Theorem
7.2
, we may further pass to the tuple
\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}
and the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
. The elements
\alpha,\beta
are not connected in the poset
(\alpha)\cup(\beta)\setminus((\alpha)\cap(\beta))
. Therefore, the corresponding strata
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}/\mathscr{C})
and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta}/\mathscr{C})
do not contain one another by Corollary
7.4
. Consequently, neither of the strata
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
is contained in the other.
∎

Definition 7.8
.

The
length

\ell(C)
of a chain
C
in a poset is one less than the number of its elements. The
height

h(\alpha)
of an element
\alpha
of a poset is the maximal length of a chain contained in the order ideal
(\alpha)
.

For comparable elements
\alpha\leq\beta
, the
closed interval

[\alpha,\beta]
is the intersection of the principal order ideal
(\beta)
with the principal order filter
[\alpha]
.

Theorem 7.9

(Height theorem)
.

If
h(\alpha)<h(\beta)
for elements
\alpha,\beta
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, then the stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
is not contained in the stratum

C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

For incomparable elements
\alpha,\beta
, we use Lemma
7.7
. For comparable elements
\alpha<\beta
, we reduce to the closed interval
[\alpha,\beta]
and the tuple
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}=\mathscr{B}_{(\beta)}/\mathscr{B}_{(\beta)\setminus[\alpha,\beta]}
by the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
). Then we apply the computation of the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
) to the tuple
\mathscr{A}=\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and its subtuples

\mathscr{C}=\tilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
,
\mathscr{B}=\tilde{\mathscr{B}}_{[\alpha,\beta]\setminus\beta}
, and use Proposition
7.3
.
∎

Lemma 7.10
.

If the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}=\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}/\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}
is a nir for some element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, then the stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
is a hypersurface component of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

By the height theorem (Theorem
7.9
) and the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
), we may assume that the element
\alpha
has height zero. The corresponding stratum is
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})=D_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
,
and it has codimension one by Theorem
3.4
. The stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
is disjoint from
(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}\setminus B_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}})\circ D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
, which is a dense open subset of
C(\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\alpha})
, a set of codimension at least one in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Therefore, the intersection
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\cap C(\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\alpha})

has codimension at least two, and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\not\subset C(\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\alpha})
.
∎

For a reducible BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
and an element
\alpha
of
the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the simple BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}
is prelinear if and only if the tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}=\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}/\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}
is linear.

Lemma 7.11
.

If the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\beta}
is a lir for every element
\beta
of the principal order filter
[\alpha]
, then the stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
is a component of codimension two of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

The complement
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus[\alpha]
of the principal order filter
[\alpha]
is an order ideal. By the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
) and Theorem
7.2
, it suffices to study the discriminant of the tuple
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}=\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus[\alpha]}
whose poset is
[\alpha]
. Hence, without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}=[\alpha]
. By the height theorem (Theorem
7.9
),
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\not\supset C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
for every

.

By the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
), the corresponding stratum is
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})=D_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
, and it has codimension two by Theorem

. The stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
is disjoint from
(\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}\setminus B_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}})\circ D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
, which is a dense open subset of
C(\mathscr{B}_{[\alpha]\setminus\alpha})
, a set of codimension at least two in

. Therefore, the intersection
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\cap C(\mathscr{B}_{[\alpha]\setminus\alpha})
has codimension at least three, and
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\not\subset C(\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\alpha})
.
∎

Lemma 7.12
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a BK-tuple with poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
such that the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is a lir for every
\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Then the set of degenerate roots of a generic polynomial system of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is a rational curve in the torus
T(\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

Recall that the set of solutions of a generic system of the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is one-dimensional by Corollary
3.5
. If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is linear, this set is an affine subspace. If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is nonlinear, then the polynomial systems
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
of
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
have the form

M_{\alpha}(x_{\beta},\;\beta<\alpha)x_{\alpha}+c_{\alpha}(x_{\beta},\;\beta<\alpha)=0,\qquad\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}},

where
x_{\alpha}=x_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
is the vector of variables of the torus
T(\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha})
,
M_{\alpha}(x_{\beta},\;\beta<\alpha)
is a square matrix of size
\mathfrak{c}(\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha})
whose entries are Laurent polynomials in the
x_{\beta}
,
\beta<\alpha
, and
c_{\alpha}(x_{\beta},\;\beta<\alpha)
is a vector of polynomials in the
x_{\beta}
. For each
\alpha
, the system is linear in
x_{\alpha}
, and a general system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
has a unique solution. This solution is a rational function of the coefficients of
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
, and it can be found by choosing a linear extension of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
and solving the system in that order.

In this case, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is reducible and has only components of codimension two. A generic system of
D_{\mathscr{A}}
corresponds to a generic system of some
D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
, whose set of solutions is a one-dimensional affine subspace. A parametrization of this subspace provides a rational parametrization of the set of solutions of the whole system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
.
∎

Recall that the BK-subtuples corresponding to principal order ideals are the simple BK-subtuples.

Lemma 7.13
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a simple BK-tuple with maximal proper BK-subtuple
\mathscr{C}
such that
\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}
is a nir and the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is a lir for every
\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{C}}
. Then the strata satisfy the inclusion
C(\mathscr{C})\subset C(\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C})
.

Proof.

Since the tuple
\mathscr{B}
is simple, the intersection of sublattices
\overline{\langle\mathscr{C}\rangle}\cap\overline{\langle\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}\rangle}
has positive dimension. This means that the polynomial systems
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}
depend nontrivially on the variables
x\in T(\mathscr{C})
.

Since the quotient tuple
\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}
is a nir, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
is defined, up to multiplicity, by a single irreducible polynomial
P(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})
by Theorem
3.4
. By Remark
6.3
, the evaluation bundle
E_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}^{x}(D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})
is also irreducible; it is defined by the nonconstant polynomial
P_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}})
obtained from
P(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}})
by replacing each coefficient
c_{b}
with the polynomial
\ev_{x}^{-1}(c_{b})
, for every
b\in C\in\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}
and a fixed point
x\in T(\mathscr{C})
.

A generic point
(\Phi_{\mathscr{C}},\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}})\in D_{\mathscr{C}}\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}
determines in the torus
T(\mathscr{C})
a generic hypersurface, given by the equation
P_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}})=0
, and a generic rational curve, the set of solutions of
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}
, by Lemma
7.12
. Their nontrivial intersections correspond to the evaluations sending the system
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}}

to the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
. Therefore,
(\Phi_{\mathscr{C}},\Phi_{\mathscr{B}\setminus\mathscr{C}})\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\circ D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
, and we obtain the inclusion of closures
D_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}\subset\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{C}}
.
∎

Theorem 7.14
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is the union
D_{\mathscr{A}}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
of the strata indexed by the elements of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
. More precisely, the discriminant
D_{\mathscr{A}}
is a union of components of codimension one or two: every hypersurface component is a stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
such that the tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is a nir, and every component of codimension two is a stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
such that the tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\beta}
is a lir for every element
\beta
of the order filter
[\alpha]
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.12
, the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
admits a partition
\mathscr{A}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
such that every tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is an irreducible BK-tuple and every subtuple
\mathscr{B}_{\mathtt{I}}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathtt{I}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
is a BK-tuple, for every order ideal
\mathtt{I}\subseteq\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

By Corollary
7.6
, every element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
corresponds to a stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
. By the separation lemma (Lemma

) and Definition
6.6
of the BK-multiplication, the codimension of the stratum

equals the codimension of the discriminant
D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}

in
\mathbb{C}_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
. By Theorem
3.4
,
the codimension of

equals one if the tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is a nir and two if it is a lir.

If an element
\alpha
with
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
a lir is covered by an element

with
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\beta}
a nir, then the strata satisfy
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})\subset C(\mathscr{B}_{\beta})
by Lemma

. Otherwise, the strata do not contain one another and are components by Lemmas

and

.
∎

Denote by
\mathtt{Q}_{\mathscr{A}}

(

8.
Cayley discriminants

The
Cayley discriminant

D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
is the discriminant of the Cayley set
\cay(\mathscr{A})
. If a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
is irreducible, then the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant coincides with the Cayley discriminant (Corollary
3.6
). The vector spaces
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
and
\mathbb{C}_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
are isomorphic.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}
be BK-tuples such that the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{B}}
is a connected component of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Then the Cayley discriminant is the product
D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}\cong D_{\cay(\mathscr{B})}\times D_{\cay(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})}
.

Proof.

Since the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{B}}
is a connected component, the tuple
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}
is a BK-tuple. Hence the linear span of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
decomposes into the direct sum
\langle\mathscr{A}\rangle=\langle\mathscr{B}\rangle\oplus\langle\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}\rangle
, and the torus splits as
T(\mathscr{A})=T(\mathscr{B})\times T(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})
. Consequently, every system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}(x,y)
in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
splits into two independent systems
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}(x)
and
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}(y)
, and every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
can be written as

f(x,y,\lambda)=\sum_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\lambda_{A}f_{A}(x,y)=\sum_{B\in\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{B}f_{B}(x)+\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}f_{C}(y).

This means that a point
(x^{\circ},y^{\circ},\lambda^{\circ})
is a singular point of the polynomial
f_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
if and only if
(x^{\circ},\lambda_{\mathscr{B}}^{\circ})
and
(y^{\circ},\lambda_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{\circ})
are singular points of the polynomials
f_{\cay(\mathscr{B})}
and
f_{\cay(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})}
, respectively.
∎

Consider a dependent tuple
\mathscr{A}
whose bases are BK-tuples. We say that a polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
and its root
x\in T(\mathscr{A})
are in
general position
if the determinant
\det(df_{B}(x))_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
is nonzero for every basis
\mathscr{B}
of the tuple
\mathscr{A}
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a tuple that is a circuit of the induced matroid. Then, for a generic polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}\in R_{\mathscr{A}}
and its root
x
, all the coefficients
\lambda_{A}
of a nontrivial linear relation
\sum_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\lambda_{A}df_{A}(x)=0
are nonzero, and the root
x
is a nondegenerate multiple root.

Proof.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a circuit, then the subtuple
\mathscr{A}\setminus A
is a BK-tuple for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Hence, for a polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
and its root
x
in general position, the determinants
\det(df_{B}(x))_{B\in\mathscr{A}\setminus A}
are nonzero, and no nontrivial linear combination
\sum_{B\in\mathscr{A}\setminus A}\lambda_{B}df_{B}(x)
vanishes, for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Therefore, in general position none of the coefficients
\lambda_{A}
,
A\in\mathscr{A}
, can be zero. In particular, the root
x
is a nondegenerate multiple root by definition.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
with poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
and a BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}
that contains no maximal (with respect to inclusion) simple BK-subtuple of
\mathscr{A}
, the Cayley discriminant satisfies
D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}\cong\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\cay(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})}
.

Proof.

We can choose a basis such that the systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
depend on the variables
x\in T(\mathscr{B})
and the systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
depend on the variables
(x,y)\in T(\mathscr{A})
. Then every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
has the form
f(x,y,\lambda)=\sum_{B\in\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{B}f_{B}(x)+\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}f_{C}(x,y)
.

(\subseteq)
A generic polynomial
f(x,y,\lambda)
in the discriminant
D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
has a singular point
(x^{\circ},y^{\circ},\lambda^{\circ})
in the torus
T(\cay(\mathscr{A}))
such that

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}f(x^{\circ},y^{\circ},\lambda^{\circ})

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}^{\circ}\,\nabla_{y}f_{C}(x^{\circ},y^{\circ})=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}^{\circ}\,\nabla_{y}(\ev_{x^{\circ}}f_{C})(y^{\circ})=0.

This means that the point
(y^{\circ},\lambda_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{\circ})
is a singular point of
f_{\cay(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})}=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}^{\circ}\,(\ev_{x^{\circ}}f_{C})(y)
, and the corresponding system
(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},f_{\cay(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})})
belongs to
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet D_{\cay(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})}
.

(\supseteq)
For a generic system
(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}},f_{\cay(\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B})})
in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\circ D_{\cay(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})}
, there exists a root
(x^{\circ},y^{\circ},\lambda_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{\circ})
in the torus
T(\mathscr{B})\times T(\cay(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}))
such that
\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}^{\circ}\,\nabla_{y}(\ev_{x^{\circ}}f_{C})(y^{\circ})=0
.

Denote by
A
the support of the polynomial
\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}^{\circ}\,(\ev_{y^{\circ}}f_{C})(x)
. Since the subtuple
\mathscr{B}
contains no maximal simple BK-subtuple, the tuple
(\mathscr{B},A)
is a circuit by Corollary
4.10
. In general position, all the coefficients
\{\mu_{B}\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
and
\mu
of a nontrivial linear relation

\sum_{B\in\mathscr{B}}\mu_{B}\,\nabla_{x}f_{B}(x^{\circ})+\mu\nabla_{x}f_{A}(x^{\circ})=0

are nonzero by Lemma
8.2
. Therefore, the corresponding Cayley polynomial
f_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
has the singular point
(x^{\circ},y^{\circ},\mu_{\mathscr{B}},\mu\lambda_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}^{\circ})
in the torus
T(\cay(\mathscr{A}))
and hence belongs to the discriminant
D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
.
∎

Some components of
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants are known to be Cayley discriminants.

Theorem 8.4

(Esterov
[
20
, Theorem 2.31]
)
.

For a reducible BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the components of codimension one of the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant are Cayley discriminants of BK-subtuples.

We extend this result to the components of codimension two of
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants.

Proposition 8.5
.

For an element
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the stratum
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
of the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant can be expressed through the Cayley discriminant of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}
:

C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}\bullet D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}}=D_{\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}}.

Proof.

By the separation lemma (Lemma
7.5
) and Corollary
7.6
, the stratum corresponding to an element
\alpha
has the form
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}\bullet D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}\bullet\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}}
. By Lemma
6.10
, we have
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}\bullet D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}\times\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}}
, so it suffices to show that
D_{\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}\bullet D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
.

The order ideal
(\alpha)
has the unique maximal element
\alpha
. We apply Proposition
8.3
iteratively, using the associativity of Proposition
6.17
. At the last step, we obtain the Cayley discriminant of the irreducible BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
, which coincides with the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant by Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Theorem 8.6
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
with poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the Cayley discriminant
D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}
equals

D_{\cay(\mathscr{A})}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet\prod_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}},

where
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
. The Cayley discriminant has codimension
\mathfrak{n}+2\mathfrak{l}
, where
\mathfrak{n}
(respectively,
\mathfrak{l}
) is the number of maximal elements
\alpha
of the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
such that the BK-tuple
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
is a nir (respectively, a lir).

Proof.

By Proposition
8.3
and the associativity of Proposition
6.17
, the Cayley discriminant of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
reduces to the Cayley discriminant of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}
,
where
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
. The poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}
is a disjoint union of
|\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}|
incomparable elements. This means that the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}
is a disjoint union of irreducible BK-subtuples, and
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}=\prod_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathbb{C}_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
. Hence the Cayley discriminant

equals the direct product
\prod_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}D_{\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}}
by Lemma

and the equality of discriminants

for irreducible BK-tuples
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Remark 8.7

By Remark

For a tuple

and an arbitrary subtuple

, we define a

of varieties by the same formula as in Definition

. We call a tuple

a

if it is a union of BK-subtuples; such a tuple may be dependent.

Theorem 8.8
.

For a BK-union
\mathscr{A}
, the Cayley discriminant

is the variety

of codimension
-\delta(\mathscr{M})+(\mathfrak{n}+2\mathfrak{l})(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{M})

is the resultant of the maximal essential subtuple

, and

(respectively,

of the poset

such that the BK-tuple

is a nir (respectively, a lir).

Following the proof of Proposition

, one shows that for a dependent tuple

the Cayley discriminant equals the multiplication

9.
Mixed discriminants

Lemma 9.1
.

If the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
of a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
has more than one connected component, then the mixed discriminant is empty.

Proof.

Every connected component corresponds to a BK-subtuple of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, and the tuple
\mathscr{A}
decomposes into a disjoint union of BK-subtuples. Hence the variables and the polynomial systems split into independent subsystems with BK-supports, and a polynomial system with support
\mathscr{A}
cannot have a nondegenerate multiple root.
∎

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, denote its mixed discriminant by
\mathring{D}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Lemma 9.2
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
and a BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}
that contains no maximal simple BK-subtuple of
\mathscr{A}
, the mixed discriminant
\mathring{D}_{\mathscr{A}}
equals
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}\bullet\mathring{D}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

(\subseteq)
If the mixed discriminant
\mathring{D}_{\mathscr{A}}
is nonempty, then its generic system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
has a nondegenerate multiple root
(x,y)\in T(\mathscr{B})\times T(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})
such that the vectors
\{df_{A}(x,y)\}_{A\in\mathscr{A}}
are linearly dependent and the vectors
\{df_{B}(x,y)\}_{B\in\mathscr{A}\setminus A}
are independent for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Then there is a nontrivial linear relation
\sum_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\lambda_{A}df_{A}(x,y)=0
in which all the coefficients
\lambda_{A}
are nonzero. Moreover, the vectors
\{df_{B}(x)\}_{B\in\mathscr{C}}
span a vector space of dimension
\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{C})
for every proper subtuple
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{A}
. For polynomial systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{B}}
, the vector space generated by
\{df_{B}(x)\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
is always the same. If the point
x
is a degenerate root of the subsystem
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}
, then the point
(x,y)
cannot be a nondegenerate multiple root of the system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
, because the vectors
\{df_{B}(x,y)\}_{B\in\mathscr{A}\setminus A}
are dependent for the sets
A\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}
. Therefore, for a nondegenerate multiple root
(x,y)
, the vectors
\{df_{B}(x)\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
are independent.

If the tuple
\mathscr{B}
contained a maximal simple BK-subtuple
\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)}
of
\mathscr{A}
, then the element
\alpha
would be maximal in both posets
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{B}}
and
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Then the tuples
\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}
and
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
would be BK-tuples, and the polynomial system
\ev_{z}(\Phi_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}})
would depend on its own set of variables
x_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
for a root
z=x_{\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)\setminus\alpha}}
. This would force the coefficients
\{\lambda_{B}\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
to vanish, a contradiction.

If the tuple
\mathscr{B}
contains no maximal simple BK-subtuple, then the tuple
(\mathscr{B},A)
is a circuit by Corollary
4.10
, where
A
is the support of the polynomial
\sum_{C\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{C}\,(\ev_{y}f_{C})(x)
. Then Lemma
8.2
guarantees that the coefficients
\{\lambda_{B}\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
are nonzero. Therefore, by Lemma
7.1
, we are reduced to the system
\ev_{x}\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}}
with linearly dependent vectors
\{d(\ev_{x}f_{C})(y)\}_{C\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
and the nondegenerate multiple root
y
. This gives the inclusion of discriminants.

(\supseteq)
If a point
y\in T(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})
is a nondegenerate multiple root of a polynomial system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
, then the vectors
\{df_{C}(y)\}_{C\in(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})\setminus A}
span the same vector space of dimension
\mathfrak{c}(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B})-1
for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}
. For a generic system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
, the vectors
\{df_{B}\}_{B\in\mathscr{B}}
are independent. This means that the point
(x,y)
is a nondegenerate multiple root of a system
\Phi_{\mathscr{A}}
such that
\ev_{x}(\Phi_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{B}})=\Phi_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{B}}
, where
x
is a root of a generic system
\Phi_{\mathscr{B}}
.
∎

Theorem 9.3
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, the mixed discriminant equals the Cayley discriminant if the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
has only one maximal element. Otherwise, the mixed discriminant is empty.

Proof.

By Lemma
9.2
and the associativity of Proposition
6.17
, the mixed discriminant of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
reduces to the mixed discriminant of the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}
, where
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
. The poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}
is a disjoint union of
|\max\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}|
incomparable elements. By Lemma
9.1
, the mixed discriminant
\mathring{D}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}
is nonempty only if the poset
\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}
consists of a single element
\alpha
. In that case, the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}
is irreducible, and the three types of discriminants coincide. Therefore, by Proposition
8.5
applied to the BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, both the mixed discriminant and the Cayley discriminant equal
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{C}}\bullet D_{\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{C}}
.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

It is shown in
[
6
]
that if the Cayley discriminant of a BK-tuple is a hypersurface, then the mixed discriminant is the same hypersurface. Theorem
9.3
(respectively, Theorem
9.5
) provides a second proof of this result for nonlinear BK-tuples (respectively, for dependent tuples).

Theorem 9.5
.

The mixed discriminant of a dependent tuple
\mathscr{A}
is nonempty if and only if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a circuit. In this case, the mixed discriminant is the resultant
R_{\mathscr{A}}
, and it is a hypersurface.

Proof.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
contains more than one circuit, then the tuple
\mathscr{A}\setminus A
is dependent for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}
. This means that the systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
cannot have a nondegenerate multiple root, and the mixed discriminant is empty. Hence the tuple
\mathscr{A}
contains exactly one circuit.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
contains a coloop
A
, then the tuple
\mathscr{A}\setminus A
is dependent, and again the systems in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathscr{A}}
cannot have a nondegenerate multiple root, so the mixed discriminant is empty. Therefore, the only remaining possibility is that the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a circuit.

If the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a circuit, then the tuple
\mathscr{A}\setminus A
is independent for every set
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Then, for a generic polynomial system in
R_{\mathscr{A}}
and its root in general position, the root is a nondegenerate multiple root by Lemma
8.2
, and the mixed discriminant coincides with the resultant
R_{\mathscr{A}}
. Since circuits of the induced matroid always have defect
-1

[
38
]
, the mixed discriminant is a hypersurface.
∎

The degree of the resultant

\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}
is essential and dependent, we use the formula with codimension
i=-\delta(\mathscr{A})
; if the tuple
\mathscr{A}
is a BK-tuple, we use it with codimension
i=2\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{n}
by Theorem
8.6
. Moreover, for a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
, Theorem
10.4
allows us to compute the degrees of the components of the
\mathscr{A}
-discriminant.

Corollary 10.5
.

For a BK-tuple
\mathscr{A}
and
\alpha\in\mathtt{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
, the component
C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})
has degree

\displaystyle\deg C(\mathscr{B}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{A\prec\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}e^{A,\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}\,(\dim\langle A\rangle+1)\Vol(A),&\text{if }\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}\text{ is a nir,}\\
\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{A\prec\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}e^{A,\cay(\mathscr{B}_{(\alpha)})}\,(\dim\langle A\rangle+1)\,(\dim\langle A\rangle-4)\Vol(A),&\text{if }\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}\text{ is a lir.}\end{cases}

Remark 10.6
.

Corollary

also gives the degrees of the mixed discriminants of BK-tuples
\mathscr{A}
whose poset

has a unique maximal element

Remark 10.7

For a nir
\hat{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}
and its face

Every face of a Cayley set is the Cayley set of a collection of faces. Esterov expressed the volumes of Cayley sets (see

Denote the mixed volume of
n
finite sets by the monomial
A_{1}\cdots A_{n}
, and the integer simplex
\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}\mid a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}=n\}

\triangle_{k}(n)

Corollary 10.8

(Danilov–Khovanskii

]

For a tuple of finite sets

Conclusion

For the reader’s convenience, the description of the components and their codimensions for
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants, Cayley discriminants, and mixed discriminants is collected in Table
1
. The description is almost complete. Our approach does not cover the explicit computation of the codimension of the Cayley discriminant for a dependent tuple
\mathscr{A}
whose quotient by the maximal cycle
\mathscr{M}
is a nontrivial independent tuple, because the tuple
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{M}
can then be an arbitrary independent tuple. However, there are at least four approaches to describing the codimension of a Cayley discriminant
[
7
]
.

Problem
.

The description of the components and degrees of mixed discriminants and
\mathscr{A}
-discriminants remains open for underdetermined polynomial systems with more than one equation.

For a BK-tuple, the Birkhoff poset of its BK-subtuples coincides with the poset of the principal partition of its polymatroid. More generally, the discriminants of underdetermined polynomial systems are conjecturally described by the principal partitions of their polymatroids
[
38
]
.

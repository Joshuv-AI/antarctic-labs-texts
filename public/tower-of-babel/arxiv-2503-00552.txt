Study of 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} P wave charmoniumlike and bottomoniumlike tetraquark spectroscopy
Zheng Zhao, Attaphon Kaewsnod, Kai Xu, Nattapat Tagsinsit, Xuyang Liu, Ayut Limphirat, Yupeng Yan

Abstract
The masses of 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} P-wave charmonium-like and bottomonium-like tetraquark states are calculated in a constituent quark model (CQM) where the Cornell-like potential and Breit-Fermi interaction are employed. All model parameters were imported from previous work, and predetermined by studying the low-lying conventional S- and P-wave light, charmed, bottom, charmonium, and bottomonium meson mass spectra. The decay widths of 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} P-wave tetraquark states are calculated for possible two-body strong decay channels within the rearrangement mechanism, including ω ​ χ c ​ J \omega\chi_{cJ} , η ​ J / ψ \eta J/\psi , and ρ ​ J / ψ \rho J/\psi for charmonium-like tetraquarks, and ω ​ χ b ​ J \omega\chi_{bJ} for bottomonium-like tetraquarks. The tetraquark theoretical results are compared with the selected exotic states, also known as Y states, and tentative assignments are suggested. This study suggests that ψ ⁡ ( 4230 ) \psi(4230) , ψ ⁡ ( 4360 ) \psi(4360) , ψ ⁡ ( 4660 ) \psi(4660) , and Υ \Upsilon (10753) may be P-wave tetraquark states and that multiple states might exist around 4.23 GeV and 4.36 GeV.



I
Introduction

The study of exotic hadrons has gained significant attention in recent years, particularly in the context of the quark model. While the conventional meson and baryon spectra are well described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD) in terms of quark-antiquark and three-quark configurations, respectively, experimental discoveries of unconventional states challenge this simple classification. Among these exotic candidates, compact tetraquarks, bound states of two quarks and two antiquarks, offer a compelling framework to explain certain resonances that do not fit within the conventional meson spectrum
Liu et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2023
.

Charmonium-like tetraquarks, which contain a charm and anticharm quark pair along with an additional light quark pair, are particularly intriguing due to their potential connection to the so-called Y states observed in electron-positron annihilation experiments. In the latest naming scheme of the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Navas et al. 2024
, the Y states have been renamed as
\psi
states because they share the same quantum number
1^{--}
, as the
J/\psi
family.

The Y(4230), an exotic particle observed and confirmed by multiple experimental collaborations, is considered part of the broader family of charmonium-like states, was first detected by BaBar collaboration in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel
Aubert et al. 2005
. The Y(4360) was later observed by Belle collaboration in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(2S)
process
Wang et al. 2007
. Subsequently, the Y(4660) was discovered by BaBar in the initial state radiation process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\text{ISR}}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(2S)

Lees et al. 2014
.
More recently, the Y(4484) was observed by BESIII collaboration in the
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
channel with a statistical significance greater than 8
\sigma

Ablikim et al. 2022a
. Additionally, the Y(4544) was reported by BESIII in the
\omega\chi_{c1}
channel
Ablikim et al. 2024
with a significance of 5.8
\sigma
, Notably, the mass of the Y(4544) is significantly higher than that of the Y(4484), suggesting possible differences in their underlying structure.

The
\Upsilon(10753)
bottomonium-like state was observed by the Belle Collaboration in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
process
Mizuk et al. 2019
. Recently, the Belle II Collaboration reported another observation that strongly supports the existence of the radiative transition process
\Upsilon(10753)\to\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)

Adachi et al. 2023
. The energy dependence of the Born cross sections for
\Upsilon(10753)
in the
\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)
channel
Adachi et al. 2023
was found to be consistent with the shape of the
\Upsilon(10753)
state in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
channel
Mizuk et al. 2019
. Belle-II concluded that the internal structure of
\Upsilon(10753)
may differ from that of
\Upsilon(10860)
, where the latter is well understood as a predominantly conventional bottomonium state.

The exotic states exhibit properties that cannot be easily accommodated with conventional quarkonium expectations, suggesting the need for alternative interpretations.
Plentiful theoretical pictures for charmonium-like exotic states, including charmonium core admixed with coupled channels

Li and Chao 2009

;

;

;

, and hidden-charm tetraquark states

;

;

;

;

II
THEORETICAL MODEL

II.1
Quark configurations of tetraquark

The construction of tetraquark states follows to the principle that a tetraquark state must be a color singlet, meaning the color wave function of the tetraquark must be a
[222]_{1}
singlet within the
SU_{c}(3)
group. In this work we consider only charmonium-like and bottomonium-like (
q_{1}Q_{2}\bar{q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4}
) states, where
q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are light quark and heavy quark, and
\bar{q}_{3}
and
\bar{Q}_{4}
are light antiquark and heavy antiquark respectively.

The permutation symmetry of the two-quark cluster (
qQ
) in tetraquark states is described by the Young tabloids
[2]_{6}
and
[11]_{\bar{3}}
of the
SU_{c}(3)
group, while the color configuration of the two-antiquark cluster (
\bar{q}\bar{Q}
) consists of a
[211]_{3}
triplet and a
[22]_{\bar{6}}
antisextet.

Thus, a
[222]_{1}
color singlet for tetraquark states demands the following configurations:
[2]_{6}(q_{1}Q_{2})\otimes[22]_{\bar{6}}(\bar{q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4})
and
[11]_{\bar{3}}(q_{1}Q_{2})\otimes[211]_{3}(\bar{q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4})
, which correspond to the color sextet-antisextet (
6_{c}\otimes\bar{6}_{c}
) and triplet-antitriplet (
\bar{3}_{c}\otimes 3_{c}
) configurations, respectively. The explicit color wave function for each tetraquark color configuration can be found in the previous work
Zhao et al. 2021
.

J^{PC}=1^{--}
Y states could be L=1, S=0 or 2 tetraquark. Thus, two possible spin combinations for tetraquarks are considered as follows:
\left[\psi_{[s=1]}^{qQ}\otimes\psi_{[s=1]}^{\bar{q}\bar{Q}}\right]_{S=0,2}
, and
\psi_{[s=0]}^{qQ}\otimes\psi_{[s=0]}^{\bar{q}\bar{Q}}
.

The complete bases is constructed by coupling among the harmonic oscillator wave functions, may take the general form,

\displaystyle\psi_{NL}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{n_{\chi_{i}},l_{\chi_{i}}}}A(n_{\chi_{1}},n_{\chi_{2}},n_{\chi_{3}},l_{\chi_{1}},l_{\chi_{2}},l_{\chi_{3}})

(1)

\displaystyle\times\psi_{n_{\chi_{1}}l_{\chi_{1}}}(\vec{\chi}_{1}\,)\otimes\psi_{n_{\chi_{2}}l_{\chi_{2}}}(\vec{\chi}_{2}\,)\otimes\psi_{n_{\chi_{3}}l_{\chi_{3}}}(\vec{\chi}_{3}),

where
\psi_{n_{\chi_{i}}l_{\chi_{i}}}
are harmonic oscillator wave functions. The sum
{n_{\chi_{i}},l_{\chi_{i}}}
is over
n_{\chi_{1}},n_{\chi_{2}},n_{\chi_{3}},l_{\chi_{1}},l_{\chi_{2}},l_{\chi_{3}}
. The relative Jacobi coordinates
\vec{\chi}_{1}
,
\vec{\chi}_{2}
and
\vec{\chi}_{3}
are defined as

\displaystyle\vec{\chi}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}),

\displaystyle\vec{\chi}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\vec{r}_{3}-\vec{r}_{4}),

\displaystyle\vec{\chi}_{3}=\frac{m_{u}\vec{r}_{1}+m_{Q}\vec{r}_{2}}{m_{u}+m_{Q}}-\frac{m_{u}\vec{r}_{3}+m_{Q}\vec{r}_{4}}{m_{u}+m_{Q}},

(2)

where
\vec{r}_{j}
are the coordinate of the jth quark. The reduced quark masses are defined as

\displaystyle u_{1}=\frac{2m_{u}m_{Q}}{m_{u}+m_{Q}},

\displaystyle u_{2}=\frac{2m_{u}m_{Q}}{m_{u}+m_{Q}},

\displaystyle u_{3}=\frac{(m_{u}+m_{Q})^{2}}{2m_{u}+2m_{Q}},

(3)

m_{u}
and
m_{Q}
are mass of the light quark and heavy quark respectively.
N
and
L
are respectively the total principle quantum number and total angular momentum, and
L=l_{\chi_{1}}+l_{\chi_{2}}+l_{\chi_{3}}=1
for P wave tetraquark and
N=(2n_{\chi_{1}}+l_{\chi_{1}})+(2n_{\chi_{2}}+l_{\chi_{2}})+(2n_{\chi_{3}}+l_{\chi_{3}})
.

II.2
Hamiltonian

We study meson and tetraquark systems within the non-relativistic quark model
Godfrey and Isgur 1985
;
Schoberl 1986
;
Barnes et al. 2005
. The Hamiltonian has been widely employed in recent years
Wang et al. 2021b
;
Wu and Zhu 2025
, taking the form,

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle H_{0}+H_{so},

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\frac{1}{2}M^{ave}_{k}+\frac{p_{k}^{2}}{2m_{k}})+\sum_{i<j}^{N}(-\frac{3}{16}\vec{\lambda}_{i}\cdot\vec{\lambda}_{j})V_{cen}(r_{ij}),

\displaystyle H_{so}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}^{N}(-\frac{3}{16}\vec{\lambda}_{i}\cdot\vec{\lambda}_{j})(V_{so}(r_{ij})),

(4)

where
M^{ave}_{k}
denotes the spin-averaged mass as
\frac{1}{4}M_{PS}+\frac{3}{4}M_{V}
.
m_{k}
are the constituent quark masses.
\vec{\lambda}_{i}
are the quark color operator in SU(3). Cornell-like potential and one-gluon-exchange potential are employed as the central potential, taking the form,

\displaystyle V_{cen}(r_{ij})=A_{ij}r_{ij}-\frac{B_{ij}}{r_{ij}}+V_{ss}(r_{ij}),

(5)

where the spin-spin interaction
V_{ss}(r_{ij})
originates from the one-gluon-exchange potential and takes the form:

\displaystyle V_{ss}=\frac{1}{6m_{i}m_{j}}\Delta V_{V}(r)\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j}=\frac{2B_{ij}\sigma_{ij}^{3}}{3m_{i}m_{j}\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\sigma_{ij}^{2}r_{ij}^{2}}\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j}.

(6)

The spin-orbital interaction
V_{so}(r_{ij})
takes the form:

\displaystyle V_{so}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\frac{dV_{V}}{d(r_{ij})}\frac{1}{4}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}+\frac{4}{m_{i}m_{j}}\right)\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{S_{ij}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\left(\vec{s_{i}}-\vec{s_{j}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\frac{dV_{S}}{d(r_{ij})}\left(\frac{\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{s_{i}}}{2m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{s_{i}}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}+\frac{4}{m_{i}m_{j}}\right)(\frac{-B_{ij}\sigma_{ij}}{2\sqrt{\pi}})\frac{e^{-\sigma_{ij}^{2}r_{ij}^{2}}}{r_{ij}^{2}}\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{S}_{ij}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{j}^{2}}+\frac{4}{m_{i}m_{j}}\right)(\frac{-B_{ij}}{4})\frac{Erf[{\sigma_{ij}r_{ij}}]}{r_{ij}^{3}}\vec{L_{ij}}\cdot\vec{S}_{ij}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{-A_{ij}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\left(\frac{\vec{L}_{ij}\cdot\vec{s}_{i}}{m_{i}^{2}}+\frac{\vec{L}_{ij}\cdot\vec{s}_{j}}{m_{j}^{2}}\right),

(7)

where
\vec{\sigma}_{i}
are the quark spin operator in SU(2). Note that we have employed
V_{V}(r)=-B\,Erf[\sigma r]/r
and
V_{S}(r)=Ar
, taken from Ref.
Schoberl 1986
.
m_{i}
and
m_{j}
are constituent quark masses of ith and jth quark.

\vec{s_{i}}
represents the spin operator for ith quark.
\vec{S_{ij}}=\vec{s_{i}}+\vec{s_{j}}
is the spin operator for (ij)th quark pair.
\vec{L_{ij}}
is relative orbital operator, taking the form,

\displaystyle\vec{L_{ij}}=\vec{r_{ij}}\times\vec{p_{ij}}=\vec{L_{ij}}\times\frac{m_{i}\vec{p_{i}}-m_{j}\vec{p_{j}}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}.

(8)

In line with the previous works,
A_{ij}
,
B_{ij}
, and
\sigma_{ij}
are proposed to be mass dependent coupling parameters, taking the form

\displaystyle A_{ij}=a+bm_{ij},\;\;B_{ij}=B_{0}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{ij}}},\;\;\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{0}{m_{ij}}.

(9)

with
m_{ij}
being the reduced mass of
i
th and
j
th quarks, defined as
\;m_{ij}=\frac{2m_{i}m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}
.
a
,
b
,
B_{0}
, and
\sigma_{0}
are constants. For more detailed discussion, one may refer to Ref.
Zhao et al. 2021
. The hyperfine coefficient
\sigma_{ij}
is also proposed to be mass dependent
Schoberl 1986
.

The central potential
V_{0}
and the spin-spin interaction
V_{ss}
are treated as the leading effects, while the remaining spin-orbital interaction
V_{so}
is considered a perturbation that shifts the mass spectrum.
The Schrödinger equation is solved for mesons and tetraquarks within the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, yielding the eigenvalue
E_{0}
and eigenstates
\psi_{0}
. The mass spectrum is then calculated by adding the diagonalized
H_{so}
matrix to the basis of the eigenstates obtained in the previous step, which takes the form,

\displaystyle E=E_{0}+\langle\psi_{0}|H_{so}|\psi_{0}\rangle.

(10)

By importing the coupling constants
a
,
b
, and
B_{0}
, as well as the constituent quark masses
m_{u}
,
m_{c}
, and
m_{b}
from previous work
Zhao et al. 2021
, the mass spectra of S- and P-wave light, charmed, bottom, charmonium, and bottomonium conventional mesons are calculated in the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
II.2
). A comparison of the theoretical results, presented in Table
1
, with experimental data from the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
, yields a model coupling parameter
\sigma_{0}=0.85
.

Two-body strong decay

T_{cs}
in the two-body strong decay channels take the form:

\displaystyle T_{cs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{f}^{cs}|\psi_{i}^{cs}\rangle

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{M_{1}M_{2}}^{c}|\psi_{Y}^{c}\rangle\langle\psi_{M_{1}M_{2}}^{s}|\psi_{Y}^{s}\rangle

where
\psi_{Y}^{c}
and
\psi_{Y}^{s}
are color and spin wave functions of the initial states corresponding to the Y tetraquark states in mass spectrum.
\psi^{c}_{M_{1}M_{2}}
and
\psi^{s}_{M_{1}M_{2}}
are the color and spin wave functions of the final states of all possible two-body strong decay channels:
\omega\chi_{c0}
,
\omega\chi_{c1}
,
\omega\chi_{c2}
,
\eta J/\psi
,
\rho J\psi
,
\omega\chi_{b0}
,
\omega\chi_{b1}
, and
\omega\chi_{b2}
.

In the work, the ratio between
|T_{cs}|^{2}
is considered to be branching ratio approximately supposing that the spatial part from different decay channels provide similar contribution.

III.2
Y(4230)

In the past two decades, the Y(4230) is an exotic particle observed and confirmed by many experimental collaborations, and is considered part of the broader family of charmonium-like states. First detected in electron-positron annihilation experiments with a mass of approximately 4.23 GeV
Aubert et al. 2005
, placing it in the region above the open-charm threshold. Unlike conventional quarkonium, which consists of a quark-antiquark pair, the Y(4230) is classified as an exotic hadron due to its unconventional properties, including anomalous decay channels and unexpected production mechanisms. In the previous work, the Y(4230) cannot be accommodated in a conventional charmonium picture including S-D mixing
Zhao et al. 2024
.

The exact internal structure of the Y(4230) remains a subject of ongoing research, with theoretical models proposing interpretations such as tetraquark states
Maiani et al. 2005
;
Ali et al. 2018
;
Wang 2019a
, molecular bound states of charmed mesons
Chiu and Hsieh 2006
;
Qin et al. 2016
, or hybrid mesons
Oncala and Soto 2017
;
Berwein et al. 2015
.

Y(4230) has been observed by many decay processes but mainly from electron positron collision, where the processes include
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi
Ablikim et al. 2020a
;
Ablikim et al. 2022b
;
Ablikim et al. 2021
,
\eta J\psi
Ablikim et al. 2020b
,
\omega\chi_{c0}
Ablikim et al. 2015
,
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
Ablikim et al. 2017
, and
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
Ablikim et al. 2022a
, which are listed in Table
5
. There are some clues about states identification appearing when the resonances are observed from different channels.

The resonance parameter of Y(4230) in the invariant mass of
\omega\chi_{c0}
Ablikim et al. 2015
is inconsistent with the line shape of the Y(4260) observed in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
Aubert et al. 2005
which is the first observed Y states in 2005 by BABAR. This Y(4260) state later superseded by a updated work
Lees et al. 2012
in PDG because two interfering resonances are not excluded. But both work were from a single-resonance fit.

Since the Y(4230) was also observed in
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
Ablikim et al. 2022a
process, and the resonance parameters is consistent with those from Y(4230) observed from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel, one may classify the Y(4230) from
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
Ablikim et al. 2022a
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
to be a same state.

Later, Y(4230) is observed in the process
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
Ablikim et al. 2017
, whose parameters are different from those of
Y(4260) observed in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel in PDG before year 2015. However, the resonance parameters of Y(4230) in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
Ablikim et al. 2017
are consistent with those of the resonance observed in
\omega\chi_{c0}

Ablikim et al. 2015
, one may support that the Y(4230) from
\omega\chi_{c0}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
are likely a same state.

The discussion above may support to separate Y(4230) into two different states. In assignment Table
6
, the one from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
and
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
might be tentatively named Y(4230) while the one observed from
\omega\chi_{c0}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
might be tentatively named Y’(4230) respectively. The Y(4230) are assigned to be E1 state of P-wave tetraquark with quantum number
1^{--}
and Y’(4230) are assigned to be E2 state due to their different decay ratio, where E2 state has much larger ratio for
\omega\chi_{c0}
channel.

III.3
Y(4360)

The Y(4360) is first observed in the process of
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(2S)

Wang et al. 2007
. With a mass of approximately 4.36 GeV, Y(4360) resides in the energy region above the open-charm threshold, similar to other Y-states. The Y(4360) is of particular interest due to its unusual decay patterns, including prominent decays into
\psi(2S)
rather than into lower charmonium states like
J/\psi
, which contrasts with expectations from conventional quarkonium models. This anomalous behavior suggests that Y(4360) could be an exotic hadron.

As a PDG established state, in PDG data listed in Table
5
, the mass of
\psi(4360)
are from 4.3 to 4.4 GeV, which might show the two different states in the large range. One is around 3.4 GeV, and another is around 3.8GeV, which are renamed as Y(4340) and Y(4380) in the assignment respectively.

The Y(4340) observed only in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
process is assigned to be E3 tetraquark state taking a small ratio to
\omega\chi_{c0}
process which is similar to E1 Y(4230) state, one may be the excitation state in same direction with Y(4230) from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
process.

Similar with the discussion for Y(4230), Y(4380) is also observed in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2021
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}

Ablikim et al. 2017
respectively, where there line shapes is inconsistent with each other
Ablikim et al. 2017
. One may support that there are two Y states around the mass 4380MeV, which are tentatively named Y(4380) for the one from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
, and Y’(4380) for the one from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
.

In the assignment Table
6
, E5 state shows similar property with the Y state from
\omega\chi_{c0}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
, where E5 state has larger ratio to
\omega\chi_{c0}
process comparing with E4 state, one may assign Y(4380) and Y’(4380) to be E4 and E5 state respectively.

III.4
Y(4660)

The Y(4660) is first discovered in the initial state radiation process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\text{ISR}}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi(2S)

Lees et al. 2014
. With a mass of approximately 4.66 GeV, Y(4660) lies well above the open-charm threshold, adding complexity to the spectrum of charmonium-like states. The Y(4660) exhibits intriguing decay patterns, most notably decaying into
\psi(2S)
rather than
J/\psi
, a feature shared with other exotic states like Y(4360).
This has led to speculation that the Y(4660) could be a tetraquark
Lebed and Polosa 2016
;
Lu et al. 2017
;
Wang 2019a
, a S-D wave mixture state
Bhavsar et al. 2018
, or a hybrid meson
Oncala and Soto 2017
;
Berwein et al. 2015
.

In the assignment Table
6
, the Y(4660) is mostly observed in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi
channel, which might be naturally grouped with Y(4230), Y(4340) and Y(4380) from the same process. One may assign it to be E15 tetraquark state which has a small decay ratio to
\omega\chi_{c0}
.

III.5
Other
Y_{c}
states

Recently, Y(4484) is observed from
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
process by BESIII for the first time with a statistical significance greater than 8
\sigma

Ablikim et al. 2022a
. Together with the Y(4230) observed in the same process, the line shape is consistent with those of established Y(4230) from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel. In the assignment, the Y(4484) is grouped with Y(4230) from
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel and assigned to be E8 tetraquark state due to mass agreement and a slightly small ratio to
\omega\chi_{c0}
.

The newly Y(4544) is observed from
\omega\chi_{c1}
channel by BESIII
Ablikim et al. 2024
. The structure of this line shape, observed for the first time with a significance of 5.8
\sigma
, and significantly higher mass compared to the Y(4484), one may support that Y(4484) and Y(4544) are different states instead of combining them to be Y(4500). In the assignment, the Y(4544) is grouped with Y’(4230) from
\omega\chi_{c0}
channel and assigned to be E11 tetraquark state due to mass agreement and a large ratio to
\omega\chi_{c0}
.

The theoretical results of branching ratio for
1^{--}
Y tetraquark state decaying to
\eta J/\psi
are all equal to zero. Thus,
the Y(4230) state and the Y(4360) state observed from
\eta J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2020b
can not be accommodated in the current tetraquark picture, and these two states could be other structure, for example, hadronic molecule states
Chiu and Hsieh 2006
;
Qin et al. 2016
;
Ding 2009
;
Close and Downum 2009
;
Close et al. 2010
, or hybrid mesons
Liu et al. 2012
;
Luo and Liu 2006
;
Zhu 2005
;
Oncala and Soto 2017
;
Berwein et al. 2015
.

III.6
Y(10753)

The Y(10753) bottomonium-like state was firstly observed by Belle in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
process
Mizuk et al. 2019
. Recently, the Belle II Collaboration reported another observation that strongly supports the existence of the radiative transition process
\Upsilon(10753)\to\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)

Adachi et al. 2023
. The energy dependence of the Born cross sections for Y(10753) in the
\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)
channel
Adachi et al. 2023
was found to be consistent with the shape of the Y(10753) state in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
channel
Mizuk et al. 2019
. Belle II concluded that the internal structure of Y(10753) may differ from that of
\Upsilon(10860)
, where the latter is well understood as a predominantly conventional bottomonium state.

In previous work, the
Y(10753)
could not be accommodated within a conventional bottomonium picture, including S-D mixing
Zhao et al. 2024
. After the experimental discovery, a P-wave hidden-bottom tetraquark interpretation was proposed in the first set of theoretical pictures
Wang 2019b
. For a tetraquark mixing interpretation, one may refer to Ref
Ali et al. 2020
.

In the assignment Table
7
, the Y(10753) observed in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
and
\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)
channel is assigned to be E5 tetraquark state due to mass agreement and a large ratio to
\omega\chi_{bJ}
.

IV
Summary

The masses of P-wave charmonium-like tetraquark states are calculated using a constituent quark model (CQM) that incorporates a Cornell-like potential and the Breit-Fermi interaction. All model parameters were predetermined by reproducing the mass spectra of low-lying S- and P-wave light, charmed, and charmonium mesons. The theoretical results for P-wave tetraquarks are compared with selected exotic states, known as Y states, and a tentative assignment is proposed.

The PDG established state around 4.23 GeV may split into two distinct states. In the assignment, the Y(4230), observed in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
and
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
channels, is assigned as E1 state of an S=0
1^{--}
P-wave tetraquark. The Y’(4230), observed in the
\omega\chi_{c0}
and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
channels, is assigned as E2 state of an S=0
1^{--}
P-wave tetraquark.

The PDG established state around 4.36 GeV may also split into three distinct states. In the assignment, the Y(4340) observed only in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
process, is assigned as E3 state of an S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark, which can be paired with the E1 Y(4230) state. The Y(4380), observed in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel, is assigned as E4 state of an S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark, while the Y’(4380), observed in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
channel, is assigned as E5 state of an S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark.

The PDG established state Y(4660), observed in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel and grouped with Y(4230), Y(4340) and Y(4380) from the same process, is assigned as the E15 S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark state.

The Y(4484), observed in the
K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi
channel by BESIII, is grouped with Y(4230) from the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
channel and tentatively assigned as the E8 S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark state.

The Y(4544), newly observed in the
\omega\chi_{c1}
channel by BESIII, is grouped with Y’(4230) from the
\omega\chi_{c0}
channel and tentatively assigned as E11 S=0
1^{--}
tetraquark state.

The Y(10753), observed in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\Upsilon(nS)
and
\omega\chi_{bJ}(1P)
channel by Belle, is assigned as an E5 S=0
1^{--}
bottomonium-like tetraquark state.

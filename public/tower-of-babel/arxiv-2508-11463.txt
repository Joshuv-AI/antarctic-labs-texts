Abstract
. We revisit the perturbative theory of infinite dimensional integrable systems developed by P. Deift and X. Zhou [ 8 ] , aiming to provide new and simpler proofs of some key L ∞ L^{\infty} bounds and L p L^{p} a priori estimates. Our proofs emphasizes a further step towards understanding focussing problems and extends the applicability to other integrable models. As a concrete application, we examine the perturbation of the one-dimensional defocussing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation by a localized higher-order term. We introduce improved estimates to control the power of the perturbative term and demonstrate that the perturbed equation exhibits the same long-time behavior as the completely integrable nonlinear Schrödinger equation.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

It is well-known that the
1
-d defocussing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)

(1.1)

iq_{t}+q_{xx}-2|q|^{2}q=0

is a canonical example of infinite dimensional integrable systems, in the sense that it is the compatibility condition for the following commutator:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}-U,\partial_{t}-W\right]=0,

where

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=iz\sigma+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
\bar{q}&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=-iz^{2}\sigma-z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
\bar{q}&0\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-i|q|^{2}&i\partial_{x}q\\
-i\partial_{x}\bar{q}&i|q|^{2}\end{array}\right).

Moving beyond integrable structures, in the seminal paper by P. Deift and X. Zhou
[
8
]
, after exploring the
L^{p}

a priori
estimates of the resolvent operator associated to the Riemann-Hilbert problem, the authors investigated the perturbation of the cubic NLS with higher order nonlinearity:

(1.3)

iq_{t}+q_{xx}-2|q|^{2}q-\varepsilon|q|^{\ell}q=0.

with
\ell>7/2
.
The authors show that for
\varepsilon\ll 1
whose size depends on the
H^{1,1}
Sobolev norm of the initial data, solutions to perturbed NLS equation behave as
t\to\infty
like solutions of the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
) which is completely integrable. Their work is the first major step towards building a rigorous perturbation theory for infinite dimensional integrable systems.
[
8
]
relies partly on the
L^{p}

a priori
estimate developed in
[
7
]
. As a byproduct, Deift and Zhou calculated the long time asymptotics for Equation (
1.1
) whose initial data belongs to the
H^{1,1}
space. Later, in
[
10
]
(see also a published version of this paper
[
11
]
), Dieng and McLaughlin re-investigated the long time asymptotics of (
1.1
) and developed the
\overline{\partial}
version of the nonlinear steepest descent method which is originally developed in
[
6
]
. We also mention that in
[
2
]
, the authors investigate the evolution of scattering data under perturbations of the Toda lattice.

In this paper, we revisit the perturbative method of
[
8
]
and give new proofs of
L^{p}

a priori
estimates and also
L^{\infty}
bounds aiming at a further step towards understanding perturbation of focussing problems and the applicability of
[
8
]
to other
integrable models. To illustrate applications of improved estimates by our new approach, we consider the long time dynamics for the following 1-d defocussing NLS with a localized nonlinearity:

(1.4)

\displaystyle iq_{t}+q_{xx}-2|q|^{2}q-\varepsilon a(x)|q|^{l}q=0,

\displaystyle q(x,t=0)=q_{0}(x)\in H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}).

Here
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
l>3
and
a(x)
is a Schwarz function (We do not aim to pursue the optimal conditions for

a(x)
here).
If we replace the RHS of (
1.2
) with
\varepsilon G(x)
where

(1.5)

G(x)=-ia(x)|q|^{l}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\
-\bar{q}&0\end{array}\right)

then it is easy to check that

(1.6)

\left[\partial_{x}-U,\partial_{t}-W\right]=\varepsilon G(x)

is the compatibility condition for (
1.4
).

In the following, we will set up problems and introduce notations based on the concrete example (
1.4
).

1.2.
The inverse scattering for the NLS

Recall that (
1.1
) generates an isospectral flow for the problem

(1.7)

\frac{d}{dx}\Psi=iz\sigma\Psi+Q(x)\Psi

where

\sigma=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}\right),\,\,\,Q(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{q(x)}\\
{\overline{q(x)}}&0\end{array}\right).

This is a standard AKNS system. If
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, Equation (
1.7
) admits bounded
solutions for
z\in\mathbb{R}
. There exist unique solutions
\Psi^{\pm}
of (
1.7
) obeying the the following space asymptotic conditions

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\Psi^{\pm}(x,z)e^{-ixz\sigma_{3}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{1}&0\\
0&{1}\end{array}\right),

and there is a matrix
S(z)
, the scattering matrix, with

\Psi^{+}(x,z)=\Psi^{-}(x,z)S(z)
.
The matrix
T(z)
takes the form

(1.8)

S(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{a(z)}&{\breve{b}(z)}\\[10.0pt]
{b(z)}&{\breve{a}(z)}\end{array}\right)

and the determinant relation gives

a(z)\breve{a}(z)-b(z)\breve{b}(z)=1

Combining this with the symmetry relations

(1.9)

\displaystyle\breve{a}(z)=\overline{a(\overline{z})},\quad\breve{b}(z)=\overline{b(\overline{z})}.

we arrive at

|a(z)|^{2}-|b(z)|^{2}=1

and conclude that
a(z)
is zero-free. By the standard inverse scattering theory, we formulate the reflection coefficient:

(1.10)

r(z)=\breve{b}(z)/a(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

In
[
14
]
, it is shown that for
k,j
integers with
k\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
, the direct scattering map
\mathcal{R}
maps
H^{k,j}(\mathbb{R})
onto
H^{j,k}_{1}=H^{j,k}(\mathbb{R})\cap\{r:\left\|r\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<1\}
where
H^{j,k}
norm is given by:

(1.11)

\left\|q\right\|_{H^{i,j}(\mathbb{R})}=\left(\left\|(1+|x|^{j})q\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|q^{(i)}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

and the map
\mathcal{R}:q\mapsto r
is Lipschitz continuous. Since we are dealing with the defocussing NLS, only the reflection coefficient
r
is needed for the reconstruction of the solution.
The long-time behavior of the solution to the NLS equation is obtained through a sequence of transformations of the following RHP:

Theorem 1.4

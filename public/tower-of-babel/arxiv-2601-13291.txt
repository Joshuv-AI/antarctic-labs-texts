Second order periodic boundary value problems with reflection and piecewise constant arguments
Alberto Cabada and Paula Cambeses-Franco

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze a second-order differential equation with a piecewise constant argument and reflection coupled to periodic boundary conditions. Our main contribution is the construction of the related Green’s function and a detailed analysis of its properties. In particular, we determine the region in which the Green’s function has constant sign, depending on the parameters m m and M M on which it depends. In some cases, we are able to characterize these parameter values in terms of the first eigenvalue related to suitable Dirichlet problems. Building in these results, we apply the Krasnosel’skii method to establish the existence of solutions for different nonlinear problems, and prove the existence of a positive solution of a perturbed Schrodinger equation.

34B05, 34B08, 34B10, 34B15, 34B18, 34B27.

Keywords:
Green’s function, equation with reflection, piecewise constant arguments, constant sign, first eigenvalue, Schrodinger equation.

Introduction

Differential equations with piecewise constant arguments and involution equations have attracted significant attention due to their diverse applications. Piecewise constant argument models are used to describe systems where variables and/or some of their derivatives are evaluated at discrete intervals. These models are particularly relevant in fields like control engineering and biomedicine. Involution equations, often involving reflection, also appear in biomedicine and physical phenomena, such as the heat equation for bent rods.

The study of involution equations date back to Silberstein’s in
1940
,
[
18
]
. Over time, these equations have been explored in numerous works
[
1
,
7
,
15
,
6
]
. Similarly, research on differential equations with piecewise constant arguments emerged in the
1980
s, blending methods from both differential and difference equations to address their challenges
[
12
,
13
]
.

Our work combines the study of these two types of equations, drawing inspiration from the methodology of
[
3
]
and employing Green’s functions. The role of Green’s functions is particularly significant: identifying regions where they maintain a constant sign enables the application of techniques such as the method of lower and upper solutions or fixed-point theorems. These tools, in turn, allow us to guarantee the existence of constant-sign solutions for a broad class of nonlinear boundary value problems involving involution and piecewise constant dependence in some of their variables.

Our primary objective is to derive the explicit form of the Green’s function for a second-order problem with involution and piecewise constant arguments, analyze its main properties and apply comparison principles to approximate numerically the region where this function maintains a constant sign. In some cases, we also provide an analytical characterization of this constant-sign region in terms of eigenvalues of related Dirichlet problems, an approach that is novelty and particularly appealing because it does not require the explicit computation of the Green’s function. Armed with this information, we will employ the Krasnosel’skii method to demonstrate the existence of solutions for a nonlinear problem. In particular, we ensure the existence of a positive solution of a perturbed Schrodinger equation.

This article is organized as follows: In Section

we present the motivation for studying this problem, introducing the Schrodinger equation to be studied at the end of the paper. Section

2
Motivation and applications

Equations of the form

v^{\prime\prime}(t)=f(t,v(t),v(-t),v([t]))

arise naturally in situations where a system exhibits continuous dynamics, discrete interventions and temporal symmetry or memory effects. These type of functional differential equations capture phenomena that cannot be modeled accurately by standard ordinary differential equations alone.

Building on this idea, it has been observed since the first experiments with Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) in optical traps that the dynamics of quantum particles in periodic media cannot be fully described by the classical linear Schrödinger equation. The need to account for inter-particle interactions, periodic structures, and nonlocal effects has motivated the formulation of more complex versions of the Schrödinger equation.

In particular:

•

Spatial reflection, incorporated via terms like
\psi(-x,t)
, allows modeling symmetries and nonlocal effects in systems with
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric potentials, which have been shown to play a crucial role in the formation and stability of solitons
[
21
]
.

•

The dependence of the potential on the cell-averaged density, represented by
|\psi([x],t)|^{2}
, naturally arises when studying BECs in optical lattices or superlattices, where the local density of atoms modifies the effective potential in each cell
[
10
]
.

Combining these effects, our model is described by the nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{p}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha\,|\psi([x],t)|^{2}\psi(x,t)+\beta\,\psi(-x,t)\quad x\in I:=[-T,T],\quad t\in[a,b],

(2.1)

where
\hbar
is the reduced Planck constant,
m_{p}
the particle’s mass and
\alpha
and
\beta
are real constants.

In the stationary case, assuming solutions of the form
\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\mu t/\hbar}\phi(x)
, the previous equation reduces to:

\mu\,\phi(x)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{p}}\frac{d^{2}\phi(x)}{dx^{2}}+\alpha|\phi([x])|^{2}\phi(x)+\beta\,\phi(-x),\quad x\in I,

(2.2)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential or eigenenergy of the stationary state.

This work represents a first step in the study of this type of equations, combining cell-based nonlinear interactions and spatial reflection effects, and opens the door to the analysis of discrete solitons, breathers, and energy bands in periodic media with nonlocal symmetry. Such models may be useful for applications in optical superlattices, BECs in lattices, and simulations of complex quantum systems.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a set containing more than one point, and let

f^{2}\equiv f\circ f=Id\textup{ on }A

If

, we refer to

as a strong involution.

If the function

represents a reflection, meaning

, then

is a particular case of an involution at any symmetric interval centered at

.

Building on the theoretical framework outlined in
[
6
, Section 3]

In addition, we will need to analyze differential problems that involve piecewise constant arguments, such as those of the form

[t]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}n,&\mbox{\rm if}&t\in[n,n+1)\\
-n,&\mbox{\rm if}&t\in(-n-1,-n].\end{array}\right.

where

. Observe that

for every

During this work, we will denote by

for all

, assuming that such definition has sense.

Remark 3.2

If

Furthermore, let

AC(I)=\Big\{\,f:I\to\mathbb{R}\ \Big|\ \exists f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(I),\,f(t)-f(s)=\int_{s}^{t}{f^{\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}r},\,\forall\,t,s\in I\Big\},

By following the procedure outlined in

\displaystyle a_{l,[-T]}

\displaystyle a_{l,[T]}

\displaystyle a_{l,0}

In the particular case where

5
Region of constant sign for the Green’s function
H_{m,M}

The aim of this section is to determine, as a function of the parameters

and
M
, the region where the Green’s function
H_{m,M}
maintains a constant sign on
I\times I
.

We begin by deriving a necessary condition for

to be either positive or negative. To this end, we present the following result:

Proposition 5.1
.

A necessary condition for the Green’s function
H_{m,M}
, related to Problem (
4.1
), to be positive on
I\times I
is that
m+M>0
. Similarly, a necessary condition for
H_{m,M}
to be negative on
I\times I
is that
m+M<0
.

Proof.

We assume that Problem (
4.1
) has a unique solution for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(I)
. The proof is a direct consequence of the fact that if
\sigma(t)=1
for all
t\in I
then
v(t)=\frac{1}{m+M}
is its unique solution.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that the curve
M=-m
is a curve of eigenvalues of problem (
4.1
).

Secondly, we will attempt to constrain the points
(t,s)\in I\times I
where the function
H_{m,M}
may attain its minimum when it is positive, or its maximum when it is negative, and approximate the region.

In this context, we present the following result:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
H_{m,M}
be the Green’s function of Problem (
4.1
), and suppose that it is of constant sign. Then:

1.

If
m\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
, the minimum, when
H_{m,M}
is positive, must be attained at the diagonal
t=s
for
s\in I
.

2.

If
m\geq 0
, the minimum, when
H_{m,M}
is positive, or the maximum, when it is negative, must be attained either at integer values of
s
with
s\in I\cap\mathbb{Z}
or at the diagonal
s=t
for
t\in I
.

Proof.

The first part follows directly from items (1), (3), (4) and (5) of Proposition
4.6
, which respectively address the regularity of
H_{m,M}(\cdot,s)
, its concavity under the assumptions of the hypotheses, the jump condition for the derivative at the diagonal, and the periodic boundary conditions.

The second statement follows from items (2), (4), (6), and (8), which respectively describe the regularity of
H_{m,M}(t,\cdot)
, the jump condition at the diagonal, the periodic boundary conditions, and the fact that
H_{m,M}
is concave when it is positive and convex when it is negative.
∎

On the other hand, due to the symmetry of the function
H_{m,M}
shown in part
7
of Proposition
4.6
, to search for the minimum or the maximum and delimit the region of constant sign, it will only be necessary to work with values of

and

Following the steps outlined in
[
3

From equation (

\displaystyle z(-T)

\displaystyle z(-T)

\displaystyle=z(T)=0.

with

We begin by defining the concept of a positive operator, a cone and a semi-non-supporting operator.

Definition 6.3
.

[
17
, Definition 2.4]

Let
E
,
F
be ordered vector spaces and let
K_{T}:E\rightarrow F
be a linear operator.
K_{T}
is called positive (in symbols:
K_{T}\geq 0
) if
K_{T}\,x>0
for all
x>0
.

Definition 6.4
.

Given a Banach space
X
,
K\subset X
is said to be a cone if it is a closed and convex subset of
X
and satisfies the following properties.

•

If
x\in K
, then
\lambda\,x\in K
for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

•

K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
.

Definition 6.5
.

[
16
, Definition 1]

Let
X
be a Banach space over
\mathbb{R}
partially ordered by a cone
K
. A linear operator
K_{T}
is a semi-non-supporting operator in
X
with respect to
K
if
K_{T}
is positive and for each nonzero
x\in K
and for each nonzero
f\in K^{*}
(the dual space of
K
) there exists a natural number
n=n(x,f)
such that
f(K_{T}^{n}x)>0
.

The next theorem provides a characterization of semi-non-supporting operators.

Theorem 6.6
.

[
16
, Theorem 2]
Denoting
\rho(K_{T})
as the spectral radius of operator
K_{T}
.
Let
K_{T}
have the resolvent
R(\lambda,K_{T})
with the point
\lambda=\rho(K_{T})
as its pole. Then, the following logical relation holds:

K_{T}\text{ is semi-non-supporting operator}\Leftrightarrow\rho(K_{T})>0\text{ and }K_{T}\text{ satisfies (A)},

where the condition
(A)
is given by

(A)
The spectral radius
\rho(K_{T})
is a simple eigenvalue of
K_{T}
with a corresponding eigenvector in the interior of the cone
K
. The adjoint operator
K_{T}^{*}
admits a strictly positive eigenfunctional associated with
\rho(K_{T})
.

Finally, we present the following theorem, which will be used in the proof.

Theorem 6.7
.

[
11
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
K\subset X
be a closed cone, and let
K_{T_{1}},K_{T_{2}}:X\to X
be positive bounded linear operators. Let
K_{T_{2}}
be a semi-non-supporting operator, let
\rho(K_{T_{2}})
be an eigenvalue of
K_{T_{2}}
and
K_{T_{2}}^{*}
with an eigenvector
x_{0}\in K
and eigenfunctional
x_{0}^{\prime}\in K^{*}
, and let
\rho(K_{T_{1}})
be an eigenvalue of the adjoint operator
K_{T_{1}}^{*}
with an eigenfunctional
x_{1}^{\prime}\in K^{*}
. If

K_{T_{2}}\geq K_{T_{1}},K_{T_{2}}\neq K_{T_{1}},

then

\rho(K_{T_{1}})<\rho(K_{T_{2}}).

Having established the above, we are now in a position to state the following result.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\lambda^{T}
denote the first (smallest) positive eigenvalue of Problem (
6.5
) posed on the interval
I
. Then the map

T\longrightarrow\lambda^{T}

is decreasing: if
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
then

\lambda^{T_{2}}<\lambda^{T_{1}}.

Proof.

Let
Gd_{T}
denote the Green operator related to the problem

-u^{\prime\prime}(t)=\sigma(t),\quad t\in I,\quad u(-T)=u(T)=0,

(6.6)

that is,
Gd_{T}:C(I)\rightarrow C(I)
denotes the operator that assigns to each
\sigma\in C(I)
the unique solution
u\in C^{2}(I)\subset C(I)
of Problem (
6.6
). Moreover, let
B:C(I)\rightarrow PC(I)
be the operator defined by
(Bu)(t):=u([t])
, where
PC(I)
denotes the space of functions that are constant on each interval of the form
[[t],[t]+1)
.

Then, the Problem (
6.5
) can be written in operator form as

u=M\,Gd_{T}(Bu).

In particular,
M
is an eigenvalue of the original problem if and only if
1/M
is a nonzero eigenvalue of the operator
K_{T}:=Gd_{T}\circ B
. Since
Gd_{T}
is compact and
B
is continuous, the composition
K_{T}
is a compact operator.

From the expression of the Green’s function
gd_{T}
(the kernel of the operator
Gd_{T}
) related to Problem (
6.6
), it is easy to see that
gd_{T}
is strictly positive for all
t,\,s\in(-T,T)
and that it is strictly increasing with respect to the parameter
T
. Specifically, if
T_{2}>T_{1}
then

gd_{T_{2}}(t,s)>gd_{T_{1}}(t,s)\textup{ for all }t,s\in(-T_{1},T_{1}).

Based on the above, we have
Gd_{T_{2}}
restricted to
(-T_{1},T_{1})
is greater than
Gd_{T_{1}}
in the usual operator order.

The operator
B
preserves the positivity of the cone of non-negative functions. Thus, each
K_{T}
is a compact and positive operator. Then, by the Krein-Rutman Theorem
[
9
]

M(T)=\frac{1}{\rho(K_{T})}.

Furthermore, it is straightforward to verify that
K_{T}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
6.6
, which implies that
K_{T}
is a semi-non-supporting operator. In addition, the hypotheses of Theorem
6.7
are fulfilled, which implies that

\rho(K_{T_{2}})>\rho(K_{T_{1}}).

As a consequence,

M(T_{2})=\frac{1}{\rho(K_{T_{2}})}<\frac{1}{\rho(K_{T_{1}})}=M(T_{1}).

This proves that
M(T)
is strictly decreasing with respect to
T
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Let

be the Green’s function related to Problem (

,

z^{\prime\prime}(t)=-mz(-t)-Mz([t]),\quad t\in I,\quad z(-T)=z(T)=0.

Proof.

According to Theorem

We calculate the Dirichlet eigenvalues

\begin{aligned} \lambda^{s_{0}}=m\left(-\frac{1}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{m}s_{0}\right)\sin\left(\sqrt{m}T\right)\text{csch}\left(\sqrt{m}T\right)\sec\left(\sqrt{m}(s_{0}-T)\right)\sinh\left(\sqrt{m}(s_{0}-T)\right)+\cos\left(\sqrt{m}s_{0}\right)-1}-1\right)\end{aligned}

By analyzing the behavior of the expression for

).

7
Existence of solution to nonlinear problems

In this section, we will present some results that allow us to deduce the existence of solutions for nonlinear problems with periodic boundary conditions. Knowing the regions where the Green’s function
H_{m,M}
has a constant sign enables the direct application of various fixed-point theorems, such as the monotone method or Krasnosel’skii’s theorem, among many others. Here, we focus on Krasnosel’skii’s method.

7.1
Krasnosel’skii’s fixed-point method

In this section, we will adapt the methods used in
[
8
]
to prove the existence of solutions to differential equations, demonstrating new existence results for Problem

v^{\prime\prime}(t)=f(t,v(t),v(-t),v([t])\quad\textup{a.e. }\quad t\in I,\quad v(-T)=v(T),\,v^{\prime}(-T)=v^{\prime}(T),

(7.1)

where
f:I\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
2\,T
-periodic in
t
and a Carathéodory function. That is,

•

For a.e.
t\in I
the function
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow f(t,x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

•

For all
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, the function
t\in I\rightarrow f(t,x,y,z)
is Lebesgue-measurable.

•

For all
R>0
, there exists
h_{R}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(I)
such that, if
|x|<R
and
|y|<R
then

|f(t,x,y,z)|\leq h_{R}(t)\textup{ a.e }t\in I.

We now state the fixed-point theorem that we will use
[
8
]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

(Krasnosel’skii) Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space, and let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{B}
be a cone in
\mathcal{B}
. Assume
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
are open subsets of
\mathcal{B}
with
0\in\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
, and let
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{P}\cap(\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
be a compact operator such that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(K_{1})

\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{T}v\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert
if
v\in\mathcal{P}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
and
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}v\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert v\right\rVert
if
v\in\mathcal{P}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}
.

(K_{2})

\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{T}v\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert v\right\rVert
if
v\in\mathcal{P}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
and
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}v\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert
if
v\in\mathcal{P}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}
.

Then,
\mathcal{T}
has, at least, one fixed-point at
\mathcal{P}\cap(\overline{\Omega_{2}}\setminus\Omega_{1})
.

From now on, we will consider
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
M\in\mathbb{R}
, and
H_{m,M}
as the Green’s function related to the Problem

\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}(t)+m\,v(-t)+Mv([t])=\sigma(t)

\displaystyle,\textup{ a.e. }t\in I

\displaystyle v(-T)=v(T),\quad v^{\prime}(T)=v^{\prime}(-T)

\displaystyle,

where
T>0
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
M\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
L=\max{\{H_{m,M}(t,s):(t,s)\in I\times I\}}
and

.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(m,M)
be a pair such that the Green’s function
H_{m,M}
is positive on
I\times I
. Let us assume that there exist
r,R\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

, such that

f(t,x,y,z)+my+Mz\geq 0,\quad\forall\,x,y,z\in\left[\frac{l}{L}r,\frac{L}{l}R\right],\textup{ a.e. }t\in I.

(7.2)

Then, if any of the following conditions is satisfied:

1.

\displaystyle f(t,x,y,z)+my+Mz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L}{2Tl^{2}}\,x,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\left[\frac{l}{L}r,r\right],\,\,\,\textup{ a.e.. }t\in I,

\displaystyle f(t,x,y,z)+my+Mz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2TL}\,x,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\left[R,\frac{L}{l}R\right],\textup{ a.e. }t\in I,

2.

\displaystyle f(t,x,y,z)+my+Mz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2TL}\,x,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\left[\frac{l}{L}r,r\right],\,\,\,\textup{ a.e. }t\in I,

\displaystyle f(t,x,y,z)+my+Mz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L}{2Tl^{2}}\,x,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\left[R,\frac{L}{l}R\right],\textup{ a.e.. }t\in I,

7.1
) has one positive solution on

.

The proof follows directly the approach of

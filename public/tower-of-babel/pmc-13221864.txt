We thank Dr Tomasi for highlighting the limitations of our study1 and the potential issues with the AVNeo—Ozaki procedure. The main purpose of our paper was to provide the available midterm outcome to inform ongoing application of the Ozaki procedure. We would like to clarify several points that are raised.

First, we have 26 patients followed more than 12 years, but the number of patients was not adequate to demonstrate more than 12 years follow-up. The low percentage of patients followed >10 years is the result of including all recent Ozaki patients to maximize the available data.

Aortic regurgitation and endocarditis (resulting in aortic regurgitation) remain the main mode of failure after Ozaki procedure. Several technical modifications to mitigate both these risks were introduced over the years such as wing extension and equal tricuspidization, and recently a new template and sizers were introduced to optimize the coaptation length.2 These efforts have not yet resulted in statistically significant improvements and further follow-up is needed. Despite using autologous tissue, the risk of endocarditis is not eliminated. The rate of endocarditis is, however, not very different from that generally reported for prosthetic aortic valve replacement—0.3% to 1.2% per patient per year.3

Interestingly, in our study, left ventricular remodeling after the Ozaki procedure continued up to 10 years, and survival following Ozaki procedure mirrored that of an age- and sex-matched Japanese population. The mirroring of the general population survival, as seen also after the Ross procedure, is the result of excellent valve hemodynamics and low rate of complication. The results are promising; however, we are careful in interpreting and extrapolating the results, as this is from a single center, predominantly Japanese patient population and the follow-up is limited. We do not suggest that the Ozaki procedure will replace well-established treatments such as Ross procedure or mechanical aortic valve replacement, but it has a role to play. The Ozaki procedure adds another good option, not all patients are suitable candidates for Ross procedure due to pulmonary valve anatomy, pathology, or function. Active patients seek alternatives to avoid lifelong anticoagulation and the Ozaki procedure is an attractive alternative option.

Apalutamide-induced drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms and Stevens-Johnson syndrome overlap successfully treated with cyclosporine and benralizumab: A case report and literature review
Authors: Alaa Erras, Zachary J Jaeger, Lena Chu, Helen H Park, Brian R Hinds, Reid Oldenburg
Published: 2026

Introduction

Drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) is a life-threatening medication-induced hypersensitivity reaction characterized by delayed onset of morbilliform rash, facial edema, eosinophilia, and multi-organ involvement.1,2 Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) exhibits full-thickness epidermal necrosis on histopathology. DRESS is clinically distinguished from SJS by longer latency, end-organ damage, eosinophilia, and human herpesvirus reactivation.

Apalutamide, an androgen receptor signaling inhibitor (ARSI) used to treat prostate cancer, has been associated with increased risk of severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR).3 We report a challenging case of DRESS with SJS-like phenotypic overlap presenting 9 days after apalutamide initiation, refractory to high-dose corticosteroids and successfully treated with cyclosporine and benralizumab.

Case report

A 68-year-old Filipino man with locally advanced prostate cancer (pT3bN1, Gleason 4 + 5) underwent radical prostatectomy in 2017, followed by adjuvant radiation and androgen deprivation therapy. He developed biochemical recurrence in 2023, prompting reinitiation of androgen deprivation therapy and subsequent addition of apalutamide for castration-resistant disease. Nine days after starting apalutamide, he developed a painful, pruritic, erythrodermic eruption with facial edema and generalized bullae without ocular involvement (Fig 1), accompanied by fever, fatigue, dysphagia, and dyspnea. Work-up revealed hypereosinophilia (peak absolute eosinophil count 7200/μL), hepatitis (alanine aminotransferase 373 U/L), and acute kidney injury (creatinine 2.1 mg/dL, blood urea nitrogen 37 mg/dL). A RegiSCAR score of 6 supported the diagnosis of DRESS.1,4

Extended laboratory evaluation revealed C-reactive protein 8.34 mg/dL, lactate 3.1 mmol/L, ferritin 2455 ng/mL, interleukin (IL) 5111 pg/mL (normal: ≤2.1), soluble IL-2 receptor/cluster of differentiation 25 5815 pg/mL (normal: 175.3-858.2), tumor necrosis factor-alpha 2.8 pg/mL (normal: ≤7.2), and chemokine(C-X-C) ligand 9 >2000 pg/mL (normal: <179). Complete human herpesvirus studies and Strongyloides IgG were unremarkable. Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) was excluded by H-score (87, <1% probability).5

Histopathology demonstrated intense interface dermatitis with apoptotic keratinocytes extending into adnexae along with tissue eosinophilia, consistent with SCAR.6,7 Marked papillary dermal edema resulted in focal detachment of the partially necrotic epidermis, correlating with bullae and desquamation suggestive of SJS-phenotypic overlap.

Apalutamide was promptly discontinued, and intravenous methylprednisolone 125 mg daily (1.5 mg/kg/day) was initiated. After 3 days, there was little improvement in bullous erythroderma, and laboratory analysis demonstrated ongoing end-organ damage despite high-dose systemic corticosteroids. Cyclosporine was initiated at 200 mg twice daily (4.8 mg/kg/day), and within 24 hours, the patient reported ∼30% improvement in the rash, along with 65% reduction in eosinophilia (Fig 2). Cyclosporine was held after 2 doses due to uptrending transaminases. Due to refractory eosinophilia and elevated IL-5 levels, benralizumab 30 mg was administered subcutaneously, with rapid normalization of eosinophilia. Liver enzymes also improved, and cyclosporine was restarted without further elevations.

As the rash resolved, the patient was discharged on oral prednisone and cyclosporine tapers. Cyclosporine was continued for 11 days after discharge then tapered to 100 mg twice daily for 10 days before discontinuation. Methylprednisolone was reduced to 100 mg daily during admission and converted to prednisone 80 mg daily upon discharge, decreasing by 10 mg every 5 days. The patient achieved full recovery without long-term sequelae after 10-m outpatient follow-up.

Discussion

DRESS pathophysiology involves complex immunological mechanisms mediated by multiple T-cell populations.8 A prominent Th2 response distinguishes DRESS from other SCARs; keratinocytes and dermal dendritic cells produce thymus and activation-regulated chemokine, recruiting Th2 cells to the skin via chemokine receptor type 4.2,9 Th2 lymphocytes release IL-5, driving eosinophil recruitment, activation, and migration into tissues.8,10 Activated eosinophils release cytotoxic granule proteins, including eosinophil cationic protein, that may contribute to tissue injury and organ involvement.2,9,11, 12, 13 Concurrently, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells and Th1 responses further amplify inflammatory injury through tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interferon-gamma, and cytotoxic mediators.2 Proteomic analysis has revealed distinct immune signatures involving type 1, type 2/eosinophilic, and type 3/innate immunity endotypes, with disease severity correlating significantly between CXCL9/10 and interferon-gamma levels.14 In recalcitrant disease, memory T-cell activation via the chemokine receptor type 8/chemokine (C-C motif) ligand 1 and chemokine receptor type 3/chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 10 axes corresponds to in vitro responsiveness to JAK inhibition.15 Serum IL-5, absolute eosinophil count, and C-reactive protein levels correlate with disease activity and severity and are useful for clinical monitoring.8

Apalutamide-induced SCARs are increasingly reported, most notably DRESS and SJS/ toxic epidermal necrolysis (Table I). In the TITAN trial, 27.1% of apalutamide-treated patients reported any rash, 6.3% grade 3 or higher.16,17 The incidence of cutaneous reactions among Japanese populations reaches 50%, with 14.7% being grade 3.17, 18, 19 In the Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System, apalutamide accounted for the majority of ARSI-associated SCAR reports (n = 77), compared to enzalutamide (n = 25) and darolutamide (n = 1), with a disproportionate number coming from Japan (n = 24).3 Mechanistic studies have suggested that apalutamide’s 2-cyanopyridine moiety may bind cysteine residues to form hapten conjugates that trigger adverse immune responses; this moiety is replaced by a 2-cyanophenyl group in enzalutamide, possibly accounting for its lower SCAR incidence.3 Our literature review suggests considering an alternative ARSI in Asian patients to reduce risk of SCAR.

Among 7 other published cases, median DRESS onset was 38 days (range 14-108) after apalutamide initiation (Table I). While 2 cases of DRESS overlapping with SJS occurred at 30 and 38 days, our case presented with an unusually short latency of 9 days. Higher-grade cutaneous reactions associated with apalutamide appear to correlate with shorter latency.24 Potential mechanisms for this shortened latency include an unidentified HLA risk allele enabling more efficient drug presentation or cancer-associated immune dysregulation with impaired regulatory T-cell function. Notably, all 3 overlapping DRESS-SJS cases occurred in patients of Asian descent.17 Among published cases, none described apalutamide-induced DRESS refractory to systemic corticosteroids.2,13

We highlight a rare and challenging steroid-refractory case successfully treated with cyclosporine and adjunctive benralizumab. In our patient, marked eosinophilia persisted despite systemic corticosteroids, and cyclosporine was held due to transaminase elevation, necessitating an alternative approach to control eosinophil-driven organ involvement. Benralizumab, an anti-IL-5 receptor alpha monoclonal antibody, prevents eosinophil activation and induces antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, achieving near-complete eosinophil depletion.2 Given the central role of Th2-driven IL-5 elevation in DRESS pathogenesis,2 benralizumab’s dual mechanism likely reduced eosinophil-mediated tissue injury, supporting the clinical response observed. Cyclosporine28 and IL-5-inhibitors such as benralizumab13,29 show promise as personalized therapies for steroid-refractory DRESS in the appropriate clinical setting.

Key teaching points

-Apalutamide may confer greater SCAR risk than other androgen receptor signaling inhibitors, particularly in patients of Asian descent.

New leap of BRD9 inhibitor: Imidazole substituents making BRD9 inhibitors excellent drug candidates for AML
Authors: Edward Zhou
Published: 2025

Bromodomain-containing protein 9 (BRD9) is a core subunit of chromatin remodeling SWI/SNF ncBAF complex. This complex, particularly the BRD9 subunit, is frequently subject to recurrent somatic mutations in various human cancers1, 2. BRD9 functions as a chromatin reader of histones, specifically recognizing and binding to acetylated or butyrylated histones through its conserved bromodomain, thereby regulating chromatin states3, 4. BRD9 participates in the pathogenesis of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) through a multi-layer regulatory network, playing a key epigenetic regulatory role in AML. By binding to and targeting the enhancer region downstream of the MYC promoter, BRD9 maintains high expression levels of the MYC gene, thereby promoting the rapid proliferation of AML cells. Additionally, BRD9 negatively regulates the expression of suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3), leading to reversible activation of the signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (STAT5) signaling pathway, which affects the proliferation and survival of AML cells. Therefore, BRD9 is considered a potential therapeutic target for AML5, 6, 7, 8.

In recent years, multiple high-affinity BRD9 bromodomain inhibitors, including compounds I-BRD9, LP99, BI-7273, and GNE-3759, have emerged as potential drug candidates for cancers. However, their limited phenotypic efficacy in disease models has hindered further development of those inhibitors into clinical trials. Furthermore, BRD9 degraders with significant phenotypic effects have been developed and several BRD9 degraders have already entered clinical trials10, 11.

In this study, Xu's group12 successfully developed a novel selective BRD9 bromodomain inhibitor Y22073 for the treatment of AML. Starting from a screen of a privileged scaffold compound library, the authors identified a hit compound (Y08284) featuring an indolizine scaffold. Based on a structure-activity relationship study, they discovered that imidazole-bearing compounds exhibited excellent anti-proliferative activities in vitro. X-ray crystal structure analysis further illustrated that the imidazole group in this inhibitor induced a flip of the Phe163 residue in the BRD9 protein and formed a stable π–π interaction with this residue. This unique mechanism of action has not been observed in other bromodomains such as BRD7, BRD4, and CECR2, demonstrating its high specificity. This molecular mechanism explains the high in vitro activity of this compound. This finding establishes Y22073 as the first BRD9 inhibitor with strong activity to disrupt disease phenotypes in AML models. Furthermore, the authors performed medicinal chemistry optimization and the resultant compounds showed significantly improved potency in vitro, further validating that the imidazole group is critical for the activity of the inhibitor.

The BRD9 inhibitor Y22073 exhibited high binding affinity to its target protein, with an IC50 value of 5.3 nmol/L at the molecular level. In the acute myeloid leukemia cell line MV4-11, Y22073 demonstrated potent inhibitory activity with an IC50 of 70 nmol/L, making it the most potent BRD9 bromodomain inhibitor against AML cell proliferation reported to date. Y22073 also exhibited more than 130-fold selectivity for BRD9 over BRD4 and CBP. Y22073 showed favorable pharmacokinetic properties, with oral bioavailability of over 50%. In the MV4-11 xenograft model, oral administration of Y22073 at a dose of 40 mg/kg significantly inhibited tumor growth, achieving over 60% tumor growth inhibition, which strongly underscores its therapeutic potential.

In conclusion, Y22073, with its excellent in vitro activity, in vivo efficacy, and drug-like properties, has become a first-in-class selective BRD9 bromodomain inhibitor, emerging as a highly promising candidate for targeted therapy of AML with significant clinical translation value.

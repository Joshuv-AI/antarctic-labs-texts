The entropic coherence is a necessary resource for non-energy preserving gates
Riccardo Castellano, Vasco Cavina, Martí Perarnau-Llobet, Vittorio Giovannetti, Pavel Sekatski

Abstract
We consider the task of implementing non-energy preserving gates (NEPG) on a finite-dimensional system S S via an energy-preserving interaction with an external battery B B . We prove that the entropic coherence of the battery (an instance of the relative entropy of resource) is a necessary resource for this task, and find a lower bound on its minimum amount that has to be present in the battery to be able to implement NEPGs with a fixed desired precision. An immediate corollary is that any finite-dimensional battery is doomed to a certain minimal error in the gate implementation task. Moreover, under assumptions on the density of energy levels in the battery Hamiltonian, our main results imply additional lower bounds on the minimal amount of energy and quantum Fisher information required to implement any gate. We show that these bounds can be stronger than the universal bounds previously established in the literature.

In contrast to the above examples, this is an entropic (unit-less) quantity unchanged by rescaling of battery’s Hamiltonian, which provides new insights on the battery requirements. Entropic coherence as a resource for gate implementation.—

For a system
B

H_{B}

\mathcal{G}_{H_{B}}
be the twirling map
\mathcal{G}_{H_{B}}[\bullet]:=\sum_{E}\Pi_{E}\bullet\Pi_{E}

C(\rho_{B},H_{B})=S\big(\mathcal{G}_{H_{B}}[\rho_{B}]\big)-S\big(\rho_{B}\big),

(3)

where
S
is the von Neumann entropy. Here we focus on energy for convenience, but the definition immediately generalizes to the entropic coherence of any additive quantity associated to an Hermitian operator. For a single system,
C(\rho_{B},H_{B})
is identical to the relative entropy of superposition
Aberg 2006
, computed with respect to energy eigenspaces of
H_{B}
. Furthermore, when
H_{B}
is non-degenerate it also coincides with the relative entropy of coherence

H_{B}
.
However, in both cases a crucial difference appears when considering composite systems. In our case, the entropic coherence
C(H_{SB},\rho_{SB})
of a joint state of two systems
S
and
B
is computed by applying Eq. (
3

H_{SB}=H_{S}+H_{B}
. In contrast, to compute the joint relative entropy of superposition/coherence one apples the same equation but for the composed twirling map
\mathcal{G}_{H_{S}}\otimes\mathcal{G}_{H_{B}}

Aberg 2006
;
Baumgratz et al. 2014a

We show in App.
B
that EC satisfies the following four conditions, that we identify as prerequisites for any resource for the gate implementation task:

(C1)

It is non-increasing under energy preserving operations:
[U_{B},{H}_{B}]=0\implies C(\beta_{B},H_{B})=C(\mathcal{U}_{B}[\rho_{B}],H_{B})
;

(C2)

It is non-increasing under partial trace:
C(\rho_{BA},H_{BA})\geq C(\rho_{B},H_{B})
, where
A
is an auxiliary system possibly correlated with
B
, and
\rho_{B}=\tr_{A}\rho_{BA}
;

(C3)

It is sub-additive on product states
C(\rho_{B}\otimes\rho_{A},H_{BA})\leq C(\rho_{B},H_{B})+C(\rho_{A},H_{A})
;

(C4)

It is regular with respect to the trace distance
D
, that is
\underset{D(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rightarrow 0}{\lim}|C(\rho_{B},H_{B})-C(\sigma_{B},H_{B})|=0
.

The conditions closely resemble those proposed in
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020
. The crucial difference is that in (C1) we explicitly define the energy preserving operations as the set of free operations. This rules out the relative entropy of coherence as a valid resource, since for composite systems the latter can increase under generic energy preserving operations and is non-increasing only under a smaller set of unitary operations, which can not exchange energy between the subsystems. Namely, those of the form
U_{AB}=\bigoplus_{a,b}e^{i\varphi_{a,b}}U_{A}^{(E_{a})}\otimes U_{B}^{(E_{b})}
, with
U_{A}^{(E_{a})}
and
U_{B}^{(E_{b})}
operating on fixed energy subspaces for both subsystems. This implies that the bound on the relative entropy of coherence discussed in
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020
is not relevant for the gate implementation task (see App.
C

Without any assumption on the Hamiltonian of the system the entropic coherence gives no informations on the content of other quantities studied for our task, like energy or quantum Fisher information. However, as we now demonstrate, once the spectrum of

Conclusions.—

We investigated the role of entropic coherence of energy as a fundamental resource for the implementation of non-energy-preserving gates (NEPGs).
We established that, to implement any NEPG with accuracy
\epsilon
, a minimum amount of entropic coherence scaling as
r\log(\epsilon^{-1})
is required in the battery, where
r\geq 1/8
depends on the specific system and gate. Under mild assumptions on the battery, this bound is strengthened by a factor of two and is tight in the case of qubits.

We then examine the implications of this bound. First, we show that the dimension of the battery system must scale at least as
d_{B}\sim\epsilon^{-\frac{r}{4}}
, thus diverging to enable perfect implementation. Furthermore, we demonstrated that the battery’s energy and Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) must scale similarly, provided the Hamiltonian has a bounded density of energy levels. These bounds can be stricter than those derived independently in
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Tajima et al. 2020
, suggesting a hierarchy among physical resources. Notably, constraints on energy and QFI do not imply corresponding limits on EC, since, by rescaling the battery’s Hamiltonian, both energy and QFI can become arbitrarily large while EC remains unchanged.
Finally, our results offer criteria for evaluating the efficiency of batteries in implementing NEPGs. In particular, for systems with multiple incommensurable energy gaps, batteries with a bounded energy level density are shown to be highly inefficient. We speculate that a battery composed of multiple harmonic oscillators—one for each energy gap—could instead prove to be efficient.

Future developments could aim to tighten the inequality by a factor of two for arbitrary systems, by extending the strategy used in the qubit case, namely selecting an appropriately entangled initial state during the resource production stage of the proof. Another promising direction is to derive upper bounds on the EC cost by explicitly constructing battery states and interactions that implement non-energy-preserving gates beyond the already explored case of equally spaced energy levels

Appendix A
Channel distances and their relationships

To evaluate how successful the setup in Fig.
1
was we need a way to measure how much the maps
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{S}\in{\tt TEP}(H_{B},\beta_{B})
and
\mathcal{V}_{S}
are close the one to the other.
This can be done by using several channel distances between two quantum channels
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
acting on the system
X
. A commonly used distance is the
diamond norm

\lVert\mathcal{E}_{1}-\mathcal{E}_{2}\rVert_{\diamond}:=\underset{\rho_{SA}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{SA})}{\text{sup}}D\Big((\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes\text{id}_{A})[{\rho}_{SA}],(\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes\text{id}_{A})[{\rho}_{SA}]\Big),

(17)

where the channels are trivially extended to act on an auxiliary system
A
(that can be taken of the same dimension as
X
without loss of generality), the supremum is taken over all states
\rho_{XA}
of the composed systems
XA
, and
D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\tr|\rho-\sigma|
is the trace distance. The diamond norm has the operational meaning of the best single-shot distinguishability between the two channels.

Similarly, the notion of fidelity between quantum states can be generalized to channels by introducing the
worst-case fidelity

F_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}):=\underset{\rho_{SA}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{SA})}{\text{inf}}F\Big((\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes\text{id}_{A})[{\rho}_{SA}],(\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes\text{id}_{A})[{\rho}_{SA}]\Big),

(18)

where use the definition of fidelity with the square
F(\rho,\sigma)=\left(\tr|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}|\right)^{2}
.
Using
F_{wc}
we can define the
worst-case infidelity

\epsilon_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}):=1-F_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}).

(19)

Notice that
\epsilon_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})
is not a distance because it fails to satisfy the triangle inequality,
however one can verify that the worst-case angle
A_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}):=\arccos(\sqrt{F_{wc}(\cE_1,\cE_2)})
is instead a distance.
The worst-case infidelity is the error quantifier used in our definition of the gate approximation task. It is related to the diamond norm by the following inequalities

1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})}\leq\lVert\mathcal{E}_{1}-\mathcal{E}_{2}\rVert_{\diamond}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})},

(20)

which is a direct consequence of the Fuchs Van-der Graaf inequality
1-\sqrt{F({\rho},{\tau})}\leq D({\rho},{\tau})\leq\sqrt{1-F({\rho},{\tau})}

Fuchs and van de Graaf 1999
.

The entropic coherence is a resource for the gate implementation

We start by introducing the set of incoherent states.

H_{A},H_{B}

\displaystyle\Delta_{AB}

\displaystyle:=\{{\rho}_{AB}:C({\rho}_{AB},{H}_{A}+{H}_{B})=0\};

(21)

\displaystyle\Delta_{B}

\displaystyle:=\{{\rho}_{B}:C({\rho}_{B},{H}_{B})=0\}.

It is immediate to verify that every incoherent state of a given Hamiltonian

It is also evident that

\rho\in\Delta\Longleftrightarrow\rho=e^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt}
for all
t
.
Further, jointly incoherent states are locally incoherent:

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Delta_{AB}
,
\Delta_{B}
be the sets of incoherent states as (
21

Tr_{A}[\Delta_{BA}]=\Delta_{B}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
Tr_{A}[\Delta_{AB}]\supseteq\Delta_{B}

\rho_{AB}=e^{-it(H_{A}+H_{B})}\rho_{AB}e^{+it(H_{A}+H_{B})}

\rho_{B}=Tr_{A}[e^{-it(H_{A}+H_{B})}\rho_{AB}e^{+it(H_{A}+H_{B})}]=e^{-itH_{B}}\rho_{B}e^{-itH_{B}}

\Delta_{B}\supseteq Tr_{A}[\Delta_{AB}]

We now prove that the entropic coherence admits other tree equivalent definitions:

\displaystyle(i)\quad C(\rho,H):=S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho))-S(\rho),

(22)

\displaystyle(ii)\quad C(\rho,H)=S(\rho||\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)),

(23)

\displaystyle(iii)\quad C(\rho,H)=\underset{\sigma\in\Delta}{\min}S(\rho||\sigma).

(24)

Proof.

We use the identity
\log(\sigma)=\log(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\sigma))=\mathcal{G}_{H}(\log(\sigma))

\mathcal{G}_{H}
is self adjoint, to prove

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)=-Tr[\rho\log(\sigma)]-S(\rho)=-Tr[\rho\mathcal{G}_{H}(\log(\sigma))]-S(\rho)

\displaystyle=Tr[\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)\log(\sigma)]-S(\rho)=S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho))-S(\rho)+S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)||\sigma).

(25)

This equality proves
(i)\iff(ii)
since for
\sigma=G_{H}(\rho)
one has
S(G_{H}(\rho)||\sigma)=0
, and
(i)\iff(iii)
because
for all other choices of
\sigma

S(G_{H}(\rho)||\sigma)>0
.
∎

Notice that in particular the expression

\Delta

HORODECKI and OPPENHEIM 2012

Baumgratz et al. 2014b

of the main text:

Proof.

First, since

C_{r}(\rho,H)\geq 0

(C1) If

\displaystyle=S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho))-S(\rho)=C(\rho,H).

(C2) From lemma

where in the first inequality we used the data processing inequality of the relative entropy

Appendix C
Comment on the bound on the relative entropy of coherence in refs
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020

In
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020
a theorem analogous to our result was derived for a similarly looking quantity, the relative entropy of coherence (REC). However, in these works the definition of REC differs for ours in a subtle way that turns out to be dramatically less relevant in this context of NEPG implementation, as we now explain.

Let
S,B
be two quantum systems with respective Hamiltonians
H_{S},H_{B}
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{{H}_{S}},\mathcal{G}_{{H}_{B}}
be the respecting dephasing channels.
Accordingly to the definition used in refs.
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020
, the REC of a composite system, that we denote
C_{loc}(\cdot)
, reads

C_{loc}(\rho_{SB}):=S(\mathcal{G}_{{H}_{S}}\circ\mathcal{G}_{{H}_{B}}(\rho_{SB}))-S(\rho_{SB}).

(34)

Notice that in this definition the knowledge of the total Hamiltonian
H_{SB}
and the joint state
\rho_{SB}
does not uniquely specify the value of
C_{loc}(\rho_{SB})
, instead the partition of the global system in subsystems must be specified. Furthermore, in refs.
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Takagi and Tajima 2020
our condition C1 is replaced with the request that a resource should be non-increasing under free operations and partial trace, while free operations are defined as being not able to increase resources. From the latter we conclude that

U_{SB}\quad\textit{is a free unitary}\quad\iff U_{SB}\ket{E_{a}}_{S}\otimes\ket{E_{b}}_{B}=e^{i\phi_{E_{a},E_{b}}}\ket{E_{a}}_{S}\otimes\ket{E_{b}}_{B},

(35)

for some arbitrary real phases
\{\phi_{E_{a},E_{b}}\}_{a,b}
, and denote with
{\tt LEP}(\beta_{B},H_{B})
the set of operations induced by such free unitaries on the system
S
, analogously to Eq. (
1
).
This set of operations is much more constrained than the set
{\tt TEP}(\beta_{B},H_{B})
, defined with the total energy preserving condition
[U_{SB},H_{S}+H_{B}]=0
. Crucially, it simply forbids interactions to exchange energy between systems
S
and
B
, i.e.

for all
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{S}\in{\tt LEP}(\beta_{B},H_{B})
and
\rho_{S}

\tr[H_{S}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{S}(\rho_{S})]=\tr[H_{S}\rho_{S}].

(36)

In view of this it shouldn’t come as a surprise that the bound found in Eq.(10) of
Chiribella et al. 2021
is exponentially higher than ours

C_{loc}(\beta_{B},H_{B})\geq\frac{f(V_{S})}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{wc}}}-o(1).

(37)

As a matter of fact any NEPG
V_{S}
cannot be approximated arbitrary well by channels in
{\tt LEP}(\beta_{B},H_{B})
irrespectivly of the chosen
\beta_{B},H_{B}
. On the other hand Eq.(
37
) is only valid in the
\epsilon_{wc}\rightarrow 0
limit, making the theorem inapplicable.

\mathcal{E}(H_{\bm{T}})
and is distributed accordingly to

{\rm Pr}(X_{\Psi}=E)=\bra{\Psi}\Pi_{E}\ket{\Psi},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\Pi_{E}=\sum_{j_{1},\dots j_{m}|\sum_{i}E_{i}^{(j_{i})}=E}\quad\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Pi_{T_{i}}^{(j_{i})}

(56)

is the projector on the subspace with total energy
E
, i.e.
H_{\bm{T}}=\underset{E\in\mathcal{E}(H_{\bm{T}})}{\sum}E\,\Pi_{E}
. Hence, by Eq. (
54
) we obtain

S\left(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{T}}}[\Psi_{\bm{T}}]\right)=S(X_{\Psi})=S\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{\Psi}^{(i)}\right).

(57)

To see that for a product initial state
\Psi_{\bm{T}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\outerproduct{\psi_i}{\psi_i}_{T_{i}}
the random variables are independent and satisfy
X_{\Psi}^{(i)}=X_{\psi_{i}}
, remark that

{\rm Pr}\Big(X_{\Psi}^{(1)}=E_{1}^{(j_{1})},\dots,X_{\Psi}^{(m)}=E_{m}^{(j_{m})}\Big)=\bra{\Psi}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\Pi_{T_{i}}^{(j_{i})}\ket{\Psi}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bra{\psi_{i}}\Pi_{T_{i}}^{(j_{i})}\ket{\psi_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\rm Pr}\left(X_{\psi_{i}}=E_{i}^{(j_{i})}\right).

(58)

∎

To proceed further we consider separately the general case and the case of a single qubit, where the derived bound is tighter. We start with the general case.

D.2.1
Resource production for general gates

For the general case of a
d
-dimensional system, we will take the initial state
\psi_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
to be a product. Specifically we now group the subsystems
S_{1},\dots S_{2m}
and
A_{1},\dots A_{2m}
into
m
subsystems

\bm{T}=\bm{S}\bm{A}=T_{1}\dots T_{m}\qquad\text{with}\qquad T_{i}=S_{2i-1}S_{2i}A_{2i-1}A_{2i},

and restrict the initial state to be a product of identical states

\rho_{\bm{T}}^{(m)}=\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\outerproduct{\varphi}{\varphi}_{T_{i}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\ket{\varphi}_{T_{i}}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{S_{2i-1}}+H_{S_{2i}}+H_{A_{2i-1}}+H_{A_{2i}}].

(59)

After the application of the gate the state becomes

\nu_{\bm{T}}^{(m)}=\mathcal{V}_{\bm{S}}^{(m)}[\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}]=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}_{T_{i}}\qquad\text{with identical}\qquad\ket{\psi}_{T}=(V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A_{1}A_{2}})\ket{\varphi}_{T}.

(60)

By Lemma
10
we conclude that the entropy of the final state after twirling is given by

S\left(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[\nu_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}]\right)=S\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{\psi}^{(i)}\right),

(61)

where
X_{\psi}^{(i)}
are iid random variables describing the energy measurement of the state
\ket{\psi}_{T}
and taking values in the set
\chi_{\psi}
. Combining with Eq. (
52
) and using the lower bound on the entropy of the sum of iid random variables (corollary
23
) derived in the dedicated Appendix
F
, we obtain the following bound on the resource production term

C\big(\nu_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{A}\big)-C\big(\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{\bm{A}}\big)=S\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{\psi}^{(i)}\right)\geq\frac{r(\chi_{\psi})}{2}\log\big(2 \pi e \,m \, \lambda(X_{\psi})\big)-o(1),

(62)

where
X_{\psi}
is the energy random variable of the definition
9
,
r(\chi_{\psi})
given in definition
20
depends on the spectrum of
X_{\psi}
,
\lambda(X_{\psi})
defined in Eq. (
173
) also depends on its distributions, and
o(1)
refers to the
m\rightarrow\infty
limit.

Next, we show that for any NEPG
V_{S}
it is possible to chose the initial state
\ket{\varphi}_{T}
such that
r(\chi_{\psi})\geq 1
and
\lambda(X_{\psi})>0
, which (by definition of these quantities) is equivalent to the random variable
X_{\psi}
taking at least two different values (
|\chi_{\psi}|\geq 2
). This is summarized by the following lemma

Lemma 11
.

If
[V_{S},H_{S}]\neq 0
then for four copies of
S
denoted
T=S_{1}S_{2}A_{1}A_{2}
with Hamiltonians
H_{T}=H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}+H_{A_{1}}+H_{A_{2}}
and
H_{S_{i}}=-H_{A_{i}}=H_{S}
, there are the states

\ket{\varphi}_{T}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{T}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ket{\psi}_{T}=V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{{\dagger}}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\ket{\varphi}_{T}

(63)

such that
|\chi_{\psi}|\geq 2
, i.e.
\ket{\psi}_{T}
is not an eigenstate of
H_{T}
.

Proof.

Let us first show that
[V_{S},H_{S}]\neq 0\implies[V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}]\neq 0
. We have

\displaystyle[V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}]

\displaystyle=[V_{S_{1}},H_{S_{1}}]\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}+V_{S_{1}}\otimes[V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{2}}]

(64)

\displaystyle=[V_{S_{1}},H_{S_{1}}]\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}-V_{S_{1}}\otimes[V_{S_{2}},H_{S_{2}}]^{\dagger}.

(65)

Since
[V_{S},H_{S}]\neq 0
, from the last identity we see that
[V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}]=0
iff
[V_{S},H_{S}]=V_{S}
. Multiplying by
V_{S}^{\dagger}
from the left, this becomes equivalent to
H_{S}-V_{S}^{\dagger}H_{S}V_{S}={\mathbb{I}}_{S}
. Finally, taking the trace on both sides of the last identity we conclude that
[V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}]
implies

0=\tr(H_{S} - V_S^\dag H_S V_S)=\tr{\mathbb{I}}_{S},

(66)

which is a contradiction. Hence,
[V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger},H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}]\neq 0
.

Next we group the systems as
S=S_{1}S_{1}
and
A=A_{1}A_{2}
, with
H_{S}=H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}
,
H_{A}=H_{A_{1}}+H_{A_{2}}
and
V_{S}=V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}
, and show that there is a state
\ket{\varphi}_{SA}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{S}+H_{A}]
such that
V_{S}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A}\ket{\varphi}_{T}
is not an energy eigenstate. Since
H_{A}=-H_{S}
we can chose the initial state of the form

\displaystyle\ket{\varphi}_{SA}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\ket{E_{1}}_{S}\otimes\ket{-E_{1}}_{A}+\ket{E_{2}}_{S}\otimes\ket{-E_{2}}_{A}}{\sqrt{2}}\in{\tt Eig}(H_{S}+H_{A}),

(67)

for any two
H_{S}\ket{E_{i}}_{S}=E_{i}\ket{E_{i}}_{S}
. Since
[V_{S},H_{S}]\neq 0
there must exist two energy eigenstates such that

\tr H_{S}\,V_{S}\outerproduct{E_1}{E_1}V_{S}^{\dagger}>E_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\tr H_{S}\,V_{S}\outerproduct{E_2}{E_2}V_{S}^{\dagger}<E_{2}.

(68)

Therefore,
V_{S}\ket{E_{1}}_{S}\otimes\ket{-E_{1}}_{A}
has positive average total energy,
V_{S}\ket{E_{2}}_{S}\otimes\ket{-E_{2}}_{A}
negative, and the energy distribution for
\ket{\psi}_{T}
is a mixture of the two. Hence, it can not be an eigenstate of the total energy, concluding the proof.
∎

The lemma guarantees that the bound (
62
) can be non-trivial for and NEPG. Nevertheless, we want to make it as tight as possible. To do so we introduce a formal maximization of the rhs of Eq. (
62
) with respect to the choice of the initial state. Since
r(\chi_{\psi})
enters linearly in the bound, and
\lambda(X_{\psi})
logarithmically, we are primarily interested in maximizing the former. This gives rise to the following definitions.

Definition 12
.

Consider an Hamiltonian
H_{S}
and a NEPG
V_{S}
. Let
T=S_{1}S_{2}A_{1}A_{2}
be composed of four copies of the system
S
with
H_{S}=H_{S_{i}}=-H_{A_{i}}
and
H_{T}=H_{S_{1}}+H_{S_{2}}+H_{A_{1}}+H_{A_{2}}
. Define

\displaystyle r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})=\max\quad

\displaystyle r(\chi_{\psi})

(69)

\displaystyle{\rm such\,that}\quad

\displaystyle\ket{\varphi}_{T}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{T}]

(70)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{T}=V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\ket{\varphi}_{T}

(71)

where
\chi_{\psi}
is the energy spectrum of the state
\ket{\psi}_{T}
(see Def.
9
), and
r(\chi_{\psi})
is given in the definition
20
. In addition, define

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})=\max\quad

\displaystyle\lambda(X_{\psi})

(72)

\displaystyle{\rm such\,that}\quad

\displaystyle\ket{\varphi}_{T}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{T}]

(73)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{T}=V_{S_{1}}\otimes V_{S_{2}}^{\dagger}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\ket{\varphi}_{T}

(74)

\displaystyle r(\chi_{\psi})=r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})

(75)

where
X_{\psi}
is the energy distribution of the state
\ket{\psi}_{T}
(see Def.
9
), and
\lambda(\chi_{\psi})
is given in Eq (
173
).

Computing
\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})
explicitly may be difficult, however lower bounds are very easy to compute for example using the state guessed in Lemma
11
. Combining everything we summarize the results of this section in the following proposition.

Result 1

(Entropic coherence production for a general gate)
.

For a
d
-dimensional quantum system with Hamiltonian
H_{S}
, and a NEPG
V_{S}
there is a choice of pure initial state
\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
, which is an eigenstate of the total energy
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
, such that the resource production term satisfies

C\big(\nu_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{\bm{A}}\big)-C\big(\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{\bm{A}}\big)\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log\big(2 \pi e \,m \, \lambda_2(V_S,H_S)\big)-o(1),

(76)

where
r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\in[1,\dots,d]
and
\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})>0
are given in the definition
12
and
o(1)
refers to the
m\rightarrow\infty
limit.

Proof.

To obtain the bound we simply combined
the definitions
12
with the bound on the entropy in Eq. (
62
). The bounds
r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\geq 1
and
\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})>0
follow from the lemma
11
, while
r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\leq d
follows from the definition of
r
.
∎

Finally we prove a simple lower bound to
r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})
that holds for
generic

V_{S},H_{S}
. We phrase the statement choosing specific measure for unitaries and Hamiltonians, but as can be readily verifies the specific choice is not crucial.

Lemma 13
.

Let
V_{S},H_{S}
be respectively sampled from the Haar measure
Mele 2024
and the Gaussian Unitary ensemble
MEHTA 1991
on the
d_{S}
dimensional Hilbert space, (or from absolutely continuous mesures with respect to them). Then, with probability one

r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\geq d_{S}-1.

(77)

Proof.

Following the Def.
12
consider the initial state

\ket{\varphi}_{T}:=\ket{E_{1},E_{1}}_{S_{1}S_{2}}\otimes\ket{E_{1},E_{1}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\in{\tt Eig}[H_{T}].

(78)

By definition we have
r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\geq r(\chi_{\psi})
where
\ket{\psi}_{T}=V_{S_{1}}\otimes V^{\dagger}_{S_{2}}\otimes{\mathbb{I}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\ket{\varphi}_{T}
and
\chi_{\psi}
is its energy spectrum. With probability one, the random gate
V_{S}
has no zeros (when written in the energy eigenbasis), implying

\bra{E_{i},E_{j}}_{S_{1}S_{2}}\bra{E_{1},E_{1}}_{A_{1}A_{2}}\ket{\psi}_{T}\neq 0\qquad\forall i,j=1,\dots,d_{S}

(79)

Hence, almost certainty the target set contains all sums of energies of the form
\chi_{\psi}=\{E_{i}+E_{j}-2E_{1}\}_{i,j}
, and

r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\geq r(\{E_{i}+E_{j}-2E_{1}\}_{i,j})=r(\{E_{i}+E_{j}\}_{i,j}),

(80)

where in the last equality we used the fact that the incommensurability rank of a set is invariant under shifts […].

Next, we recall that by Lemma
21
,
r(\{E_{i}+E_{j}\}_{i,j})\geq{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}[{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\{E_{i}+E_{j}\}_{i,j}]]-1
, where
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\chi]=\sum_{i=1}^{|\chi|}q_{i}x_{i}
with
q_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
x_{i}\in\chi
is the rational span of a set. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\{E_{i}+E_{j}\}_{i,j}]={\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\{E_{i}\}_{i}]
, leading to

r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\geq{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}[{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\{E_{i}\}_{i}]]-1.

(81)

Finally, note that for a random Hamiltonian
H_{S}
its
d_{S}
eigenergies
E_{i}
are real numbers that are
almost certainly
linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e. for
q_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
we have
\underset{i=1}{\overset{d_{S}}{\sum}}q_{i}E_{i}=0
if and only if
q_{i}=0
. Hence, with probability one
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}[{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\{E_{i}\}_{i}]]=d_{S}
, completing the proof.

∎

D.2.2
Resource production for a qubit gate

We now specifically consider the case of a qubit gate
\mathcal{V}_{S}
, with
\ket{0}_{S}
and
\ket{1}_{S}
denoting the two eigenstates of
H_{S}
with different energies. The gate can then be represented by the matrix

V_{S}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos(\theta)&\sin(\theta)e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\\
-\sin(\theta)e^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}&\cos(\theta)\end{array}\right)

(82)

in the energy basis, and for the purpose of this section we can assume that the energies are
E_{0}=0
and
E_{1}=1
without loss of generality.

Let us chose the pure initial state
\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}=\outerproduct{\psi^{(m)}}{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
to be given by

\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\ket{1,1}_{S_{i}A_{i}}\bigotimes_{i=k+1}^{2m}\ket{0,0}_{S_{i}A_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\ket{1^{\otimes k},0^{\otimes(2m-k)}}_{\bm{S}}\otimes\ket{1^{\otimes k},0^{\otimes(2m-k)}}_{\bm{A}},

(83)

where
\ket{1^{\otimes k},0^{\otimes 2m-k}}_{\bm{S}}
means that the systems
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
are in the state
\ket{1}_{S_{i}}
and the remaining systems are in the state
\ket{0}_{S_{i}}
. Note that since all
A
are copies of
S
with inverted energy scale we have
(H_{S_{i}}+H_{A_{i}})\ket{1,1}_{S_{i}A_{i}}=(H_{S_{i}}+H_{A_{i}})\ket{0,0}_{S_{i}A_{i}}=0
, and
\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is an eigenstate of
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
of total energy zero.

The
2m
-copy gate of interest can be written as
\mathcal{V}_{\bm{S}}^{(m)}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m}(\mathcal{V}_{S_{2j-1}}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{S_{2j}}^{*})=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{2m}\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{S}_{i}}
where slightly abusing the notation we introduced
\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{S_{i}}=\mathcal{V}_{S_{i}}
for odd
i
and
\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{S_{i}}=\mathcal{V}_{S_{i}}^{*}
for even
i
. After its application the state becomes

\displaystyle\ket{\nu^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{\bm{S}}^{(m)}\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\ket{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\qquad\text{with}

(84)

\displaystyle\ket{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\bar{V}_{S_{i}}\ket{1}_{S_{i}}\bigotimes_{i=k+1}^{2m}\bar{V}_{S_{i}}\ket{0}_{S_{i}}\otimes\ket{1^{\otimes k},0^{\otimes(2m-k)}}_{\bm{A}}.

(85)

Result 2
.

[Entropic coherence production for a qubit gate]
For a qubit NEPG
V_{S}
in Eq. (
82
), let
\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}
and
\nu^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
be defined as in Eqs.(
83
),(
84
)
then in the
m\rightarrow\infty
limit the following bound holds

C\big(\nu_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{A}\big)-C\big(\rho_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},H_{\bm{S}}+H_{\bm{A}}\big)\geq\log(4m\sin^2(\theta))-\delta_{1,\sin^{2}(\theta)}-o(1).

(86)

Note that this bound can be put in the form of the general Result
1
by setting

r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})=2\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})=\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta)}{\pi(1+\delta_{1,\sin^{2}(\theta))})}

(87)

in Eq. (
76
) for all qubit gates.

Proof.

By virtue of Definition
9
we have that

S\left(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[\ket{\nu^{(m)}}\bra{\nu^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}]\right)=S(X_{\nu^{(m)}}),

(88)

where
X_{\nu^{(m)}}
is the random variable describing the energy distribution of the state
\ket{\nu^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
.

Now, remark that these states satisfy
\bra{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\ket{\zeta_{k^{\prime}}^{(m)}}=0
for
k\neq k^{\prime}
since they are supported on orthogonal eigenstates for
\bm{A}
. Hence,
X_{\nu^{(m)}}
is a statistical mixture (not a sum) of the random variables
X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}
describing the energy distribution of
\ket{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
. The weigths of the mixture are uniform and equal to
\frac{1}{2m+1}
(see Eq. (
84
)).
Here and below, for a discrete respectively continuus random variable
X
we denote its probability distribution respectively density with square brakets, i.e.
X[n]:={\rm Pr}(X=n)
, the we have

X_{\nu^{(m)}}[n]=\frac{1}{2m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2m+1}X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}[n].

(89)

To compute these random variables note that
\ket{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
and therefore
X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}
is the sum random variable describing energy distribution for all systems, i.e.

X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}B^{(i)}_{\cos^{2}(\theta)}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2m}B^{(i)}_{\sin^{2}(\theta)}-k

(90)

where
B^{(i)}_{p}
are independent Bernoulli variables with

B^{(i)}_{p}[1]=p
and
B^{(i)}_{p}[0]=1-p
,
and
B^{(i)}_{\cos^{2}(\theta)}
with
B^{(i)}_{\sin^{2}(\theta)}
describe the energy distribution of the states
\bar{V}_{S_{i}}\ket{1}_{S_{i}}
and
\bar{V}_{S_{i}}\ket{0}_{S_{i}}
respectively.

In the special case
\sin^{2}(\theta)=1
the computation of the entropy is trivial, as
X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}[n]=\delta_{n,2m-2k}
and

S(X_{\nu^{(m)}})=\log(2m+1)\geq\log(4m)-1

(91)

consistently with the thesis. While the case
\sin^{2}(\theta)=0
describes the trivial gate.

Thus, from now on we will consider
\sin^{2}(\theta)\in(0,1)
,
and characterize the probability distribution of
X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}
in the
m\to\infty
limit.
X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}
is the sum of
2m
independent Bernoulli random variables (plus a constant), thus according to
Chen et al. 2011a
;
Golowich 2023
;
Chen et al. 2011b
its probability distribution converge to a discretized normal distribution of equal average and variance. More precisely, let

\displaystyle\mu_{m,k,\theta}

\displaystyle:=\mathds{E}[X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}]=2(m-k)\sin^{2}(\theta)

(92)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,\theta}^{2}

\displaystyle:={\rm Var}[X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}}]=2m\cos^{2}(\theta)\sin^{2}(\theta)

(93)

and

N_{d}(\mu,\sigma)[n]:={\rm Pr}\big(n-1/2<N(\mu,\sigma^{2})\leq n+1/2\big),

(94)

where
N(\mu,\sigma^{2})
is normal random variable with average
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Then, the total variation distance satisfies
Chen et al. 2011b

D\left(X_{\zeta_{k}^{(m)}},N_{d}\big(\mu_{m,k,\theta},\sigma_{m,\theta}\big)\right):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z\in\mathbf{Z}}\absolutevalue{X_{\zeta_k^{(m)}}[z]-N_{d}(\mu_{m,k,\theta},\sigma_{m,\theta})[z]}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{m}}\quad=\frac{7.6}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}

(95)

By joint convexity of the total variation distance this property is preserved when mixing the random variables, implying

D\left(X_{\nu^{(m)}},Z_{m}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{m}},

(96)

where
Z_{m}
is the uniform mixture of the
2m+1
random variables
\{N_{d}\big(\mu_{m,k,\theta},\sigma_{m,\theta}\big)\}_{k=0,1,..2m}
, i.e., it’s probability distribution reads

Z_{m}[n]:=\frac{1}{2m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}N_{d}\big(\mu_{m,k,\theta},\sigma_{m,\theta}\big)[n].

(97)

Notice that by construction
P(X_{\nu^{(m)}}<-2m)=P(X_{\nu^{(m)}}>2m)=0
,
implying that, by Fannes-Audenaert
Audenaert 2007
inequality

\sum_{n=-2m}^{2m}-Z_{m}[n]\log\big(Z_{m}[n] \big)-S(X_{\nu^{(m)}})\leq D\left(X_{\nu^{(m)}},Z_{m}\right)\log(4m+1)\leq\frac{C\log(4m+1)}{\sqrt{m}}=o(1)

(98)

Thus the entropy production can be approximated with negligible error by the entropy of
Z_{m}
on the
n\in[-2m,2m]
interval. Its probability distribution function is given by

\displaystyle Z_{m}[n]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\int_{n-1/2}^{n+1/2}N(\mu_{m,k,\theta},\sigma_{m,\theta})[x]\,\differential x

(99)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\int_{\frac{n-1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}}^{\frac{n+1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}}N(0,1)[x]\,\differential x

(100)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)\,N(0,1)[x]\,\differential x,

(101)

where we introduced the function

\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\int_{\frac{n-1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}}^{\frac{n+1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}}\,\delta(x-x^{\prime})\differential x^{\prime}

(102)

counting the number of intervals
\left[\frac{n-1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}},\frac{n+1/2-\mu_{m,k,\theta}}{\sigma_{m,\theta}}\right]
, labelled by
k=0,1,...,2m
, containing the value
x
. This is the case for
k
fulfilling

\displaystyle n-x\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|-\frac{1}{2}\leq 2(m-k)\sin^{2}(\theta)\leq n-x\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|+\frac{1}{2}.

(103)

We see that in the bulk, i.e. for
x
satisfying

\big|n-x\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|\big|\leq 2m\sin^{2}(\theta)-\frac{1}{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{n-2m\sin^{2}(\theta)+1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}\leq x\leq\frac{n+2m\sin^{2}(\theta)-1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|},

(104)

the value of
\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)
oscillates between

\displaystyle\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)=\left\lfloor{\frac{1}{2\sin^{2}(\theta)}}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)=\left\lfloor{\frac{1}{2\sin^{2}(\theta)}}\right\rfloor+1,

(105)

with a frequency
\frac{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}{2\sin^{2}(\theta)}

increasing with
m
. Furthermore, its average value over a period is simply
\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)\approx\frac{1}{2\sin^{2}(\theta)}
. We therefore find that

\displaystyle Z_{m}[n]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\kappa_{n,m,\theta}(x)\,N(0,1)[x]\,\differential x\geq\frac{1}{(2m+1)2\sin^{2}(\theta)}\int_{\frac{n-2m\sin^{2}(\theta)+1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}}^{\frac{n+2m\sin^{2}(\theta)-1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}}\,N(0,1)[x]\,\differential x+o(1),

(106)

where we restricted the integration to the bulk. Let us now denote
-2m\leq n:=2mr\leq 2m
with
-1\leq r\leq 1
, we see that for
|r|<\sin^{2}(\theta)
the integration boundaries satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{n-2m\sin^{2}(\theta)+1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m(r-\sin^{2}(\theta))+1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}\to-\infty

(107)

\displaystyle\frac{n+2m\sin^{2}(\theta)-1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m(r+\sin^{2}(\theta))-1/2}{\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}|\sin(2\theta)|}\to\infty

(108)

and the integral converges to one. Hence, there are
4m\sin^{2}(\theta)
values of
n
for which

Z_{m}[n]\geq\frac{1}{2(2m+1)\sin^{2}(\theta)},

implying

\displaystyle S(X_{\nu^{(m)}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=-2m}^{2m}-Z_{m}[n]\log\big(Z_{m}[n] \big)+o(1)\geq\frac{4m\sin^{2}(\theta)}{(2m+1)2\sin^{2}(\theta)}\log(2(2m+1)\sin^2(\theta))+o(1)

(109)

\displaystyle=\log(4 m \sin^2(\theta))+o(1).

(110)

∎

D.3
Step 2: Refining resource regularity

The next step is to upper bound the difference in the relative entropy produced by the ideal and approximate gates

\displaystyle C({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-C({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})

\displaystyle=S(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])-S(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])+S({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-S({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}),

(111)

as a function of the trace distances between the final states
D\left(\widetilde{\nu}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)},\nu_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}^{(m)}\right)
, which in turn is bounded by the gate error via Eq. (
46
). A key ingredient here is the following regularity condition for the von Neumann entropy.

Lemma 14

(Refined Fannes-Audenaert inequality
Audenaert et al. 2024
)
.

For density operators
\rho
and
\sigma
one has

S(\rho)-S(\sigma)\leq D(\rho,\sigma)(S(\Delta_{+})-S(\Delta_{-}))+h_{2}\big(D(\rho,\sigma)\big)

(112)

where
\Delta_{+/-}
are the unique positive, othogonal operators satisfying
D(\rho,\sigma)(\Delta_{+}-\Delta_{-})=\rho-\sigma
(Jordan-Hahn decomposition).

Since
{\rm Rank}(\rho)\geq{\rm Rank}\Delta_{+}
, we also have

S(\rho)-S(\sigma)\leq\log\big({\rm Rank}(\rho)\big)D(\rho,\sigma)+h_{2}\big(D(\rho,\sigma)\big),

(113)

Applying this bound to the entropy differences in Eq. (
111
) we obtain

\displaystyle S({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-S({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})

\displaystyle\leq\log\big({\rm Rank}({\tnu}^{(m)}_{\bm S\bm A})\big)D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})+h_{2}\big(D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\big)

(114)

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])-S(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])

\displaystyle\leq\log\big({\rm Rank}(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm S \bm A}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm S\bm A}])\big)D(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}],\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])

(115)

\displaystyle+h_{2}\big(D(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}],\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])\big)

(116)

\displaystyle\leq\log\big({\rm Rank}(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm S \bm A}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm S\bm A}])\big)D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})

(117)

\displaystyle+h_{2}\big(D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\big),

(118)

where we used the monotonicity of the trace distance
D(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}],\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])\leq D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})
, and the fact that the binary entropy
h_{2}(x)
is an increasing function for
x\leq\frac{1}{2}
(assuming
D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq\frac{1}{2}
). Combining the two bounds we obtain the following intermediate results

C({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-C({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq\log\big({\rm Rank}({\tnu}^{(m)}_{\bm S\bm A})\, {\rm Rank}(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm S \bm A}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm S\bm A}])\big)D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})+2\,h_{2}\big(D({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\big).

(119)

The rest of the section is hence devoted to bound the ranks of
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}]
.

D.3.1
Bounding the rank of
\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}]

To bound the rank of
\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}]
, notice that the state
{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is pure, therefore after twirling its rank is bounded by the number of different energies of the total Hamiltonian (number of projectors in the twirling map)

{\rm Rank}(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])\leq\absolutevalue{\mathcal{E}(H_{\bm S \bm A})}.

(120)

Recall, that the total system consists of
2m
copies of
SA
with Hamiltonian
H_{SA}=H_{S}+H_{A}
. Since
H_{S}
and
H_{A}
are identical up to the sign flip, one sees that
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{E}(H_{S A})}\leq d_{S}^{2}-(d_{S}-1)
, since the energy
0
is
d_{S}
-degenerate. Now, since all
2m
copies of
SA
have the same Hamiltonian, the total Hamiltonian
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is invariant under their permutation, and the number of different energies it admits is upper bounded by the number of different ways to pick
2m+1
symbols from an alphabet of
\#=\frac{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}{2}
symbols, and then choosing their sign i.e.

{\rm Rank}(\mathcal{G}_{H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}}[{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}])\leq\absolutevalue{\mathcal{E}(H_{\bm S \bm A})}\leq 2^{\frac{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}{2}}\frac{(2m+\#)!}{(2m+1)!(\#-1)!}=2^{\frac{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}{2}}\binom{2m+\frac{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}{2}}{2m+1}.

(121)

Notice however that for
d_{S}=2
we have
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{E}(H_{\bm S \bm A})}\leq 4m+1
.

D.3.2
Bounding the rank of
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}

Next, to bound the rank of
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
we consider three different approaches, depending on what is known about the battery system.

(i)
A straightforward but generic bound is given by the dimension of the Hilbert space on which the total system lives

{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq{\rm dim}(\mathcal{H}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})=d_{S}^{4m}.

(122)

(ii)

Now we derive a strengthened bound on the rank of
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
valid for a
proportionate
battery. For the sake of clarity we start by considering the simplified hypothesis of a fixed
d_{B}
-dimensional battery. Recall that the state of interest is obtained via
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}=\tr_{B}\mathcal{U}_{\bm{S}B}^{(m)}[\rho^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\otimes\beta_{B}]
from
\rho^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}=\outerproduct{\psi^{(m)}}{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
. Then, if the initial state of the battery is pure
\beta_{B}=\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}_{B}
, the final state
U_{\bm{S}B}^{(m)}\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\ket{\beta}_{B}
is also pure. Hence, for the bipartition
\bm{S}\bm{A}|B
it can be put in the Schmidt diagonal form with at most
d_{B}
non-zero coefficients, ensuring that
{\rm Rank}(\tr_{B}\mathcal{U}_{\bm{S}B}^{(m)}[\rho^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\otimes\beta_{B}])\leq d_{B}
. In turn, if
\beta_{B}
is not pure, it is a mixture of
{\rm Rank}(\beta_{B})\leq d_{B}
pure states. Then
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is a mixture of at most
{\rm Rank}(\beta_{B})
states with rank at most
d_{B}
, leading to the following bound

{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq{\rm Rank}(\beta_{B})\,d_{B}\leq d_{B}^{2}.

(123)

Similarly, suppose that
\beta_{B}
is supported on a subspace spanned by eigenstates of
H_{B}
with energy range
[0,E_{max}(\beta_{B})]
. Assume first that the battery
\beta_{B}=\outerproduct{\beta_B}{\beta_B}
is pure. Since
\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is an eigenstate of
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
with eigenvalue
E_{\psi}
, and
\mathcal{U}_{\bm{S}B}
is energy preserving, the total energy of the final state

\ket{\xi}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}B}=U_{\bm{S}B}^{(m)}\ket{\psi^{(m)}}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\ket{\beta}_{B}

(124)

must be smaller than
E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+E_{\psi}
. For any energy levels of the system
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\ket{E}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}=E\ket{E}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
and the battery
H_{B}\ket{E^{\prime}}_{B}=E^{\prime}\ket{E}_{B}
, we must thus have

\bra{E}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\bra{E^{\prime}}_{B}\ket{\xi}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}B}\neq 0\implies E+E^{\prime}\leq E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+E_{\psi}.

(125)

Denoting with
E^{(m)}_{\min}
the minimum eigenvalue of
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
we conclude that the final energy of the battery system must satisfy

\bra{E^{\prime}}_{B}\ket{\xi}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}B}\neq\bm{0}\implies E^{\prime}\leq E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+E_{\psi}-E_{\min}^{(m)}.

(126)

It follows that

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})={\rm Rank}(\tr_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}\outerproduct{\xi}{\xi}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}B})\leq\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+E_{\psi}-E_{\min}^{(m)}\big),

(127)

where
\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E\big)
is, by definition, the number battery level with energy no greater than
E
Recall that
H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
is a sum of
2m
operators
H_{S}
acting on different
S_{i}
and
2m
operators
H_{A}=-H_{S}
acting on different
A_{i}
, leading to
E_{\min}^{(m)}=-2m(E_{\max}-E_{\min})
, where
E_{\max(\max)}
is the minimal (maximal) eigenvalue of
H_{S}
. Introducing the pseudo-norm
\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}:=E_{\max}-E_{\min}
we obtain for pure
\beta_{B}

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+E_{\psi}+2m\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\big).

(128)

Finally, if
\beta_{B}
is not pure it is a mixture of
{\rm Rank}(\beta_{B})
terms, implying

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Rank}(\beta_{B})\,\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+4m\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\big)

(129)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})\big)\times\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B})+4m\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\big).

(130)

Now consider a family of batteries
\{H_{B}^{(\epsilon)},\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)}\}_{\epsilon}
suitable for the target gate. As we will see later, in the limit of
\epsilon\to 0
we will always chose
m
such that
4m\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\leq E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)})
. In this case we get

\displaystyle{\rm Rank}({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}(\epsilon))\big)\times\mathcal{N}_{B}\big(2E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)})\big)\leq{\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}),

(131)

for any
proportionate
family of batteries. This is the inequality that we will use to prove (ii) of the main result.

D.3.3
The final bounds

We can now combine all of the above bounds to obtain the following result

Result 3

(Regularity of the entropic coherence)
.

In the limit
m\to\infty
the difference in the relative entropy of coherence between the ideal
{\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
and the approximate
{\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}}
states is upper bounded by

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-C({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq 4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}\log(2m)+4m\log(d_S)\Big)+2\,h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big).

(132)

For a proportionate battery the following bound also holds

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle({\nu}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})-C({\widetilde{\nu}}^{(m)}_{\bm{S}\bm{A}},H_{\bm{S}\bm{A}})\leq 4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}\log(2m)+\log\left({\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})\right)\Big)+2\,h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big),

(133)

under the assumption
4m\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\leq E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)})
.

Proof.

The bounds are an immediate consequence of combining
Eqs. (
119
,

121
,
46
) with Eqs. (
122
) or (
131
) depending on weather the battery is
proportionate
. Finally, to simplify Eq. (
121
) we also used that for large enough
m

\log\left[2^{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}\binom{2m+\frac{d_{S}(d_{S}-1)}{2}}{2m}\right]\leq\log((2m)^{\frac{d_S^2}{2}})=\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}\log(2m).

(134)

∎

D.4
Step 3: Choosing the best initial state.

As discussed in the proof outline, to obtain a lower bound on
C(\beta,H_{B})
it now remains to combine the bounds on resource production (Results
1
and
2
) and resource regularity (Result
3
) derived in the last two sections, and choose the optimal scaling of
m=m(\epsilon)
to take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. To do so we consider separately the case of a general or
proportionate
battery.

D.4.1
Main result (i) i.e. any battery

For a generic battery Eq. (
48
) and the previous results gives the following bound on the entropic coherence of the battery

C(\beta,H_{B})\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log(2 \pi\; e \; m \lambda_2(V_{S},H_S))-16\log(d_S)\sqrt{\epsilon}\,m^{2}-2d_{S}^{2}\sqrt{\epsilon}\,m\log(2m)-2\,h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big)-o(1).

(135)

To obtain the strictest inequality we set the following scaling of the number of copies

m(\epsilon):=\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}}{8\sqrt{\log(d_{S})}\epsilon^{1/4}}\right\rfloor,

(136)

with
\sqrt{\epsilon}\,m\log(2m),h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big)\to 0
. This choice leads to

\displaystyle C({\beta},{H}_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{8}\log\left(\frac{(2\pi e\;\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\sqrt{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})})^{4}}{8^{4}\log^{2}(d_{S})\epsilon}\right)-\frac{r_{2}(V_{S})}{4}-o(1),

(137)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{8}\log\left(\frac{\sigma(V_{S},H_{S})}{\epsilon}\right)-o(1)

(138)

where we introduced

\sigma(V_{S},H_{S}):=\frac{\pi^{4}e^{4}}{256}\,\frac{(\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S}))^{4}(r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S}))^{2}}{\log^{2}(d_{S})}.

(139)

D.4.2
Main result (ii),
proportionate
battery

For a
proportionate
battery combining (
48
) with the previous results gives the following bound on the entropic coherence of the battery

C(\beta,H_{B})\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log(2 \pi\; e \; m \lambda_2(V_{S},H_S))-2d_{S}^{2}\sqrt{\epsilon}\,m\log(2m)-4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\log\left({\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})\right)-2\,h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big)-o(1).

(140)

Here, we will chose he number of copies as

m(\epsilon):=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\log{\epsilon^{-1}}}\right\rfloor,

(141)

where
\gamma>0
is a constant that will be determined later. First, we need to verify that this choice verifies the assumption
4m(\epsilon)\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}\leq E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)})
. This follows from the theorem 1 of
Chiribella et al. 2021
, which implies that the energy of a suitable battery must verify

E_{\rm max}(\beta_{B}^{(\epsilon)})\geq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\geq\frac{4\gamma\|H_{S}\|_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\log(\epsilon^{-1})}

(142)

in the limit of small
\epsilon
.

In Eq. (
140
) we find that
h_{2}\big(4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\big)\to 0
,

4\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}\sqrt{\epsilon}\,m\log(2m)=\,\frac{4\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}\gamma}{\log{\epsilon^{-1}}}\log\left(2\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\epsilon}\log{\epsilon^{-1}}}\right)=2d_{S}^{2}\gamma\frac{\log\epsilon^{-1/2}+2\gamma{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}-}\log\log\epsilon^{-1}}{\log{\epsilon^{-1}}}=d_{S}^{2}\gamma+o(1),

(143)

and

\displaystyle 4m\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\log\left({\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})\right)=\frac{4\gamma\log\left({\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})\right)}{\log(\epsilon^{-1})}\leq\frac{4\gamma\,\alpha\log(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})}{\log(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})}+o(1)\leq 4\gamma\,\alpha+o(1)

(144)

where
\alpha>0
is the dominant power of the polynomial, i.e. such that
{\rm poly}(\epsilon^{-1}_{wc})\leq\epsilon^{-\alpha}
in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.
Combining with the positive term we obtain

\displaystyle C({\beta},{H}_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{4}\log\left(\frac{(2\pi e\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S}))^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log( \log\epsilon^{-1})+\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log(\gamma)-\gamma(d_{S}^{2}+4\alpha)-o(1).

(145)

The rhs is maximized for the choice
\gamma=\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{4(\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}+2\alpha)}
, leading to the final bound

\displaystyle C({\beta},{H}_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{4}\log\left(\frac{(2\pi e\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S}))^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log( \log\epsilon^{-1})+\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{2}\log(\frac{r_2(V_S,H_S)}{ e (2d_S^2+8\alpha)})-o(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{4}\log\left(\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(V_{S},H_{S})}{\epsilon(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{2}}\right),

(146)

were we introduced

\sigma^{\prime}(V_{S},H_{S}):=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})\,r_{2}(V_{S},H_{S})}{\frac{d_{S}^{2}}{2}+2\alpha}\right)^{2}.

(147)

Appendix E
Proving corollaries
4
and
5

We begin by proving that at fixed energy distribution, pure states have higher entropic coherence:

Lemma 15
.

For any Hamiltonian
H
and state
\sigma

\ket{\sigma}:=\underset{\ket{E}\in\mathcal{B}[H]}{\sum}\sqrt{\bra{E}\sigma\ket{E}}\ket{E}

\mathcal{B}[H]\subseteq{\tt Eig}[H]

(148)

Proof.

Let
\Pi_{E}
be the projectors on the fixed energy subspaces of
H
, and
\sigma_{E}:=\frac{\Pi_{E}\sigma\Pi_{E}}{P_{E}}
where
P_{E}:=tr[\sigma\Pi_{E}]
, then we have

\displaystyle C(\ket{\sigma},H)-C(\sigma,H)=S(\sigma)+S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\ket{\sigma}))-S(\mathcal{G}_{H}(\sigma))

(149)

\displaystyle=S(\sigma)+S(\{P_{E}\}_{E})-S\left(\underset{E\in\mathcal{E}[H]}{\bigoplus}P_{E}\sigma_{E}\right)\geq 0

(150)

where the inequality is a consequence of the strong sub-additivity of entropy
Lieb and Ruskai 2002
, infact
Consider the system
S
in the state
\sigma_{S}
, and its purification
\Psi_{SA}
on
S
composed with an auxiliary system
A
. Consider the isometry
W_{SR}=\underset{E}{\sum}\Pi^{(E)}_{S}\otimes\outerproduct{E}{0}_{R}
(where
\braket{E}{E^{\prime}}:=\delta_{E,E^{\prime}}
), applied on
S
and a register
R
, initially in the sate
\outerproduct{0}{0}_{R}
. The isometry can be realized by a joint unitary on
SR
, hence the final state
\rho_{SR}=W_{SR}\sigma_{S}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}_{R}W_{SR}^{\dagger}
has the same entropy as the initial state of the system
S(\rho_{SR})=S(\sigma_{S}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}_{R})=S(\sigma_{S})
. Denoting the final pure state of the three systems
\Phi_{SAR}=W_{SR}\Psi_{SA}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}_{R}W_{SR}^{\dagger}
we find for this state and its marginals

\displaystyle S(SAR)

\displaystyle=S(\Phi_{SAR})=0,

(151)

\displaystyle S(SA)

\displaystyle=S(R)=S\left(\sum_{E}P_{E}\outerproduct{E}{E}_{R}\right),

(152)

\displaystyle S(SR)

\displaystyle=S(\rho_{SR})=S(\sigma_{S}),

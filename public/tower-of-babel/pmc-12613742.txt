Clinical epidemiological characteristics and treatment outcomes of Brucella spondylitis in Ningxia, China: a 12‑year single‑center retrospective study
Authors: Kun Wang, Zhangui Gu, Qiang Liu, Long Ma, Minyu Li, Hanhua Guo, Jianqiang Gao, Zongqiang Yang, Jiandang Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEpidemiological and clinical analyses of Brucellar spondylitis (BS) are crucial for public health authorities to enhance disease surveillance and optimize case management strategies. However, clinical epidemiological research on BS remains limited. This study analyzed the clinical and epidemiological characteristics of hospitalized BS patients at a hospital in Ningxia, aiming to provide evidence to support region-specific strategies for BS prevention and control.MethodsWe retrospectively collected demographic profiles, epidemiological characteristics, clinical presentations, laboratory/imaging findings, therapeutic regimens, and prognostic outcomes of BS inpatients admitted to the orthopedic department of a hospital in Ningxia from January 2012 to December 2023.ResultsA total of 821 patients with BS were included, consisting of 564 males (68.7%; 564/821) and 257 females (31.3%; 257/821), with a median age of 55 years (range: 18–88 years). Among all patients (n = 821), 466 (56.76%) were agricultural or pastoral workers, and 578 (70.4%) had a documented history of contact with cattle or sheep. Low back pain was the most common presenting symptom, reported in 779 cases (94.88%; 779/821). The combined positive rate of the Rose Bengal Plate Test (RBPT) and the Standard Agglutination Test (SAT) in serological testing was 90.99% (747/821). Blood cultures were performed in 618 patients, of whom 217 (35.11%; 217/618) yielded positive results. Imaging examinations revealed that the lumbar spine was the most frequently involved region (59.81%; 491/821), with the L4–L5 segment being the most commonly affected (20.83%; 171/821). Single-segment involvement was observed in 668 patients (81.37%; 668/821), single-vertebra involvement in 44 patients (5.36%; 44/821), and multi-segment or trans-segment involvement in 109 patients (13.28%; 109/821). Of the 465 patients who underwent surgical treatment, the most common procedure was posterior fixation combined with lesion debridement, performed in 215 cases (46.24%; 215/465). Among the 754 patients who completed follow-up, the most frequently used antibiotic regimen was doxycycline plus rifampicin (60.05%; 493/754). Relapse occurred in 26 patients during follow-up (3.45%; 26/754).ConclusionBrucellar spondylitis in Ningxia exhibits a temporal pattern aligned with regional brucellosis trends. Strengthening imaging-based diagnosis, clinical evaluation, and targeted follow-up of high-risk populations may help reduce recurrence and improve disease control.Clinical trialNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12101-z.Keywords: Brucellar spondylitis, Clinical epidemiology, Clinical features, Treatment outcomes, Management

Background

Human brucellosis (HB), a zoonosis caused by Brucella spp., has emerged as a formidable global public health challenge [1, 2]. The disease imposes dual burdens through livestock industry economic losses and chronic patient morbidity, particularly in resource-limited settings [3]. Recent epidemiological studies indicate that the global annual incidence of HB ranges from 1.6 to 2.1 million new cases, surpassing previous estimates [4, 5]. In China, the incidence rate of brucellosis has shown a significant upward trend in recent years. Data reveals that the national incidence rate increased from 0.0281 per 100,000 population in 1993 to 5.0553 per 100,000 in 2021, with cases now reported across all 31 provincial-level administrative regions [6]. Notably, over 95% of cases cluster in the northern regions, where Inner Mongolia (40.9/100,000), Xinjiang (13.6/100,000), and Ningxia (13.3/100,000) consistently ranked as the top three highest-incidence regions during 2004–2018 [7].

Brucellar spondylitis is the most severe osteoarticular complication of brucellosis, representing 2–53% of all osteoarticular infections [8]. Clinical manifestations encompass systemic symptoms (fever, night sweats, hepatosplenomegaly) and localized neurological impairments (spinal pain, sensory deficits, muscle weakness), with severe cases progressing to paralysis secondary to spinal canal abscess compression [9]. Currently, most BS research has focused on diagnosis and treatment [10, 11], while clinical epidemiological studies on BS remain relatively scarce. For instance, Kaya et al. [12]conducted a study of 343 cases of infectious discitis in Turkey, identifying 138 BS patients, and Feng et al. [13]analyzed 328 HB cases in Gansu, China, including 157 BS cases. However, neither study provided a comprehensive epidemiological analysis specific to BS. Notably, Lebre et al. [14]conducted a 25-year clinical epidemiological analysis of 54 BS patients in Portugal, revealing that chronic low back pain (98.1%) and diagnostic delay (median 5.5 months) were prominent clinical features. Their findings suggested superior efficacy of doxycycline combined with aminoglycosides compared to rifampicin-containing regimens, while the observed reduction in cases correlated with enhanced animal vector control measures, underscoring the need for tailored therapeutic strategies in endemic regions. However, this study was limited by its modest sample size and prolonged observational timeframe.

As the clinical epidemiological data of BS are lagging behind and scarce, this study analyzed the epidemiological characteristics, clinical manifestations, laboratory and imaging data, and treatment regression of 821 patients with BS at the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University retrospectively, and analyzed and summarized the data of a large sample of 12 years in our center, to reveal the epidemiological pattern of BS in Ningxia region, and to provide evidence-based evidence for the optimization of the regionalization of prevention and control strategies.

Method

Case source

Clinical data from patients diagnosed with BS and hospitalized in the Department of Orthopedics at the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University between January 2012 and December 2023 were retrospectively reviewed. Collected variables included demographic and epidemiological characteristics (e.g., sex, age, occupation, residential location—categorized as urban or rural and within or outside Ningxia, seasonal and annual distribution of disease onset, and history of contact with cattle or sheep), clinical manifestations, laboratory and imaging findings, treatment approaches, and prognostic outcomes.

Collected clinical and epidemiological data

Upon admission, all patients underwent comprehensive evaluations. Data collection included general demographic information such as sex, age, ethnicity, occupation, and place of residence (categorized as urban or rural, and within or outside the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region), as well as date of hospital admission, time from symptom onset to diagnosis, and length of hospital stay. Clinical manifestation records encompassed symptoms such as localized spinal tenderness and percussion pain, fever, back pain, sweating, and neurological deficits. Epidemiological information included the seasonal and annual distribution of disease onset, history of close contact with cattle or sheep, and consumption of unpasteurized animal products. Imaging data included the anatomical location of lesions, number of affected spinal segments, presence and distribution of abscesses, and spinal deformities such as kyphosis and scoliosis. Laboratory assessments involved white blood cell (WBC) counts, lymphocyte and platelet counts, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), the Standard Agglutination Test (SAT), blood cultures, and histopathological test results. For patients with comorbid conditions, additional imaging assessments—such as abdominal ultrasonography, echocardiography, or scrotal Doppler ultrasound—were performed as appropriate. Relapse was defined as the reappearance of clinical symptoms and signs within six months following initial treatment [15].

Treatment

In accordance with the “China Brucellosis Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines“ [16], the therapeutic regimen for brucellosis adheres to the principles of “early initiation, combination therapy, adequate dosing, and full-course treatment,” with extended therapeutic cycles when clinically warranted. Drug selection and treatment duration are tailored based on the presence and type of complications. The first-line regimen recommends doxycycline (100 mg twice daily for 6 weeks) combined with rifampicin (600–900 mg once daily for 6 weeks). Patients were categorized into two groups based on their treatment regimen: Group A: Medication-only treatment group, Group B: Surgical plus medication treatment group. All enrolled patients received brucellosis-specific antimicrobial therapy in strict accordance with clinical guidelines, consisting of oral doxycycline (0.2 g daily), rifampicin (0.6 g daily), levofloxacin (0.5 g daily), and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (two tablets twice daily). Surgical intervention was implemented for patients exhibiting antibiotic treatment failure or progressive neurological deficits. Preoperative management for surgical candidates involved a standardized 3-week anti-Brucella regimen, with operative procedures deferred until inflammatory markers demonstrated sustained improvement: ESR ≤ 30 mm/hr and CRP ≤ 45 mg/L, both showing consistent downward trajectories.Surgical indications encompass [17]: posterior spinal fixation and fusion as follows: (1) involvement of no more than 2 vertebral bodies without pedicle destruction or destruction less than 1/4; (2) the presence of a small abscess in the paravertebral or psoas muscle region; (3) small cavities or limited sequestrum formation in the bone destruction; (4) no obvious symptoms of spinal cord or nerve root compression. The indications for posterior spinal fixation plus lesion clearance through a posterior approach are: (1) severe destruction and collapse of the posterior column structure leading to spinal deformity and instability; (2) formation of abscesses within the spinal canal or intervertebral space; (3) significant destruction of the intervertebral space or intervertebral disc; (4) obvious compression of the spinal cord, nerve roots, or symptoms of cauda equina syndrome. The indications for posterior spinal fixation plus lesion clearance through an anterior approach are: (1) severe destruction of the anterior and middle column structures of the vertebral body and pedicle; (2) the presence of a large, difficult-to-absorb paravertebral or psoas muscle abscess; (3) significant destruction of the intervertebral space or intervertebral disc, without compression of the dural sac or nerve roots or protrusion into the spinal canal; (4) involvement of multiple segments of the vertebral body (≥ 3).

Clinical efficacy evaluation and complications

Clinical efficacy was categorized based on symptomatic relief, functional recovery, laboratory and imaging findings, and microbiological status. The criteria were as follows:

Cure: Normal body temperature; complete relief of lumbar pain (VAS score = 0); full recovery of daily function; Frankel Grade E neurological status; imaging showing complete resolution of vertebral inflammation and infection with stable spinal structure; normalization of ESR and CRP; and negative SAT results.

Improvement: Normal body temperature; partial relief of lumbar pain (VAS score 1–4) with ≥ 50% improvement compared to baseline; ≥50% improvement in daily activity function; imaging showing partial resolution of inflammation and bone repair; decreased ESR and CRP levels; and negative SAT results.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA). Continuous variables were first tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Variables with a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the independent samples t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Variables not conforming to a normal distribution are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR), and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical data are expressed as frequency and percentage and were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test where appropriate. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Database and data quality control

The clinical data of patients were extracted from the hospital’s electronic health record system. To ensure data completeness and accuracy, records of all hospitalized patients were retrieved by two independent researchers using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) codes M49.1 (Brucella spondylitis) and A23 (Brucellosis). After extraction, a rigorous data cleaning and validation process was implemented, including handling of missing values, screening for outliers, and consistency checks, to ensure the reliability and integrity of the dataset.

Ethical considerations

This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Ningxia Medical University General Hospital (KYLL-2025-1147) and was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Given that this research involved a retrospective review of medical records, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for obtaining informed consent from patients.

Results

Demographic and epidemiological characteristics

A total of 821 BS patients were enrolled, comprising 564 males (68.7%; 564/821) and 257 females (31.3%; 257/821), yielding a male-to-female ratio of 2.19:1. Patients’ ages ranged from 18 to 88 years, with a median age of 55 years (interquartile range: 48–63 years). The age distribution of patients from six different regions (Yinchuan, Guyuan, Wuzhong, Zhongwei, Shizuishan, and other areas) as well as the overall cohort (Total) is illustrated, showing relatively similar median ages across most regions and in the total population, with some regional variations (Fig. 1); The majority of patients (73.81%; 606/821) were aged between 41 and 65 years. Regarding ethnicity, 550 patients (66.99%; 550/821) were of Han ethnicity, while 271 (33.01%; 271/821) belonged to other ethnic groups (Table 1). Geographically, 675 patients (82.22%; 675/821) were residents of Ningxia, with their distribution across the five prefecture-level cities—Yinchuan, Shizuishan, Wuzhong, Zhongwei, and Guyuan—shown in detail in the regional map (Fig. 2). Among the remaining 146 patients from outside Ningxia, the largest proportions were from neighboring provinces, including Inner Mongolia (71 cases), Gansu (38 cases), and Shaanxi (32 cases) (Fig. 3).

Temporal trends and seasonality

The number of BS cases showed a gradual increase from 2012 to 2014, followed by a period of decline and relative stability. However, beginning in 2021, the case count rose again, peaking in 2022 with 120 reported cases (14.62%; 120/821) (Fig. 4). Regarding regional distribution, Yinchuan City consistently remained the primary reporting area, with 197 cumulative cases (29.16%; 197/675). In contrast, Shizuishan City reported only 68 cases (10.07%; 68/675). At the county level, Xiji County recorded the highest number of cases, with 62 (9.19%; 62/675), while Huinong District reported only 6 cases (0.89%; 6/675). Notably, although Yinchuan had the highest cumulative case count overall, Guyuan surpassed it between 2017 and 2019 and became the region with the highest annual case counts during that period.

Temporal distribution of 675 BS patients treated at our center (2012–2023)

To further assess seasonal patterns, monthly case counts were aggregated across the entire 12-year period (2012–2023). The results revealed a pronounced concentration of cases between March and August, which collectively accounted for 466 cases (56.74%; 466/821). August had the highest number of cases overall (91; 11.08%), followed by April (80; 9.75%) and March (77; 9.38%) (Supplementary Table 1), indicating that BS incidence peaks during spring and summer months (Fig. 5).

Clinical manifestations and comorbidities

Among the 821 patients with BS, the most commonly reported clinical symptom was localized spinal pain, observed in 779 cases (94.88%; 779/821). Other frequently observed symptoms included fever in 323 cases (39.34%), fatigue in 172 cases (20.95%), excessive sweating in 163 cases (19.85%), weight loss in 94 cases (11.45%), and loss of appetite in 33 cases (4.02%).Neurological symptoms were present in 169 patients (20.58%; 169/821), primarily manifesting as varying degrees of limb numbness, weakness, or decreased muscle strength.Comorbidities were also observed, including hepatosplenomegaly in 118 patients (14.37%; 118/821), renal cysts in 75 patients (9.14%; 75/821), orchitis or epididymitis in 46 patients (5.60%; 46/821), meningitis in 3 patients (0.37%; 3/821), and infective endocarditis in 3 patients (0.37%; 3/821) (Table 2).

Laboratory findings

Initial laboratory evaluations upon admission revealed several abnormal hematological and inflammatory markers among the 821 patients with BS (Table 4). Leukocyte count abnormalities were found in 116 patients (14.13%; 116/821), with elevated counts (> 9.5 × 10⁹/L) in 62 cases (7.55%) and decreased counts (< 3.5 × 10⁹/L) in 54 cases (6.58%). Platelet count abnormalities were identified in 140 patients (17.05%; 140/821), including 107 cases (13.03%) with thrombocytosis (> 350 × 10⁹/L) and 33 cases (4.02%) with thrombocytopenia (< 125 × 10⁹/L). Absolute lymphocyte count was abnormal in 145 patients (17.66%; 145/821), with elevated counts (> 3.2 × 10⁹/L) in 47 cases (5.72%) and reduced counts (< 1.1 × 10⁹/L) in 98 cases (11.94%). Inflammatory markers were also commonly elevated: ESR was increased in 626 patients (76.25%; 626/821), defined as > 15 mm/h in males and > 20 mm/h in females; CRP levels exceeded 10 mg/L in 602 patients (73.33%; 602/821). Among the 821 patients, screening for other infectious diseases showed positive findings in 66 patients for viral hepatitis (8.04%), 7 for syphilis (0.85%), 4 for tuberculosis (0.49%), and 1 for HIV (0.12%).

Imaging findings

All 821 patients underwent MRI evaluation upon admission. The lumbar spine was the most frequently involved region, affected in 491 cases (59.81%; 491/821), followed by the lumbosacral region in 144 cases (17.54%), cervical spine in 83 cases (10.11%), thoracic spine in 56 cases (6.82%), thoracolumbar junction in 18 cases (2.19%), cervicothoracic junction in 4 cases (0.49%), and sacral spine in 1 case (0.12%). Non-continuous multifocal vertebral lesions were identified in 24 patients (2.92%; 24/821), most commonly in the lumbar spine (9 cases; 1.10%), followed by thoracolumbar lesions (7 cases; 0.85%) (Table 5).

Regarding the pattern of vertebral involvement, single-segment lesions were observed in 668 patients (81.36%; 668/821), single-vertebra involvement in 44 patients (5.36%), and multiple or cross-segmental lesions in 109 patients (13.28%) (Fig. 7). Among patients with single-segment involvement, the most commonly affected levels were L4–L5 in 171 cases (20.83%; 171/821), C6–C7 in 25 cases (3.05%), and T8–T9 in 8 cases (0.97%).

Distribution of affected segments in 821 patients with BS

A total of 201 patients (24.48%; 201/821) had abscess formation, with the most common type being iliopsoas abscesses (76 cases; 9.26%), followed by paravertebral abscesses (74 cases; 9.01%) and epidural abscesses (51 cases; 6.21%). Spinal deformities were documented in 20 patients (2.43%; 20/821), including scoliosis in 14 cases (1.70%) and kyphosis in 6 cases (0.73%). Neurological symptoms resulting from spinal cord or nerve root compression were noted in 169 patients (20.59%; 169/821), comprising 46 cases (5.61%) of spinal cord compression and 123 cases (14.98%) of nerve root compression.

Treatment and prognosis

Among the 821 patients with BS, 465 (56.64%) received combined surgical and pharmacological treatment, while 356 (43.36%) underwent conservative medical therapy. In the surgical group (Group B), the most commonly performed procedure was posterior fixation with debridement (215 cases; 46.24%), followed by posterior fixation alone (167 cases; 35.91%), anterior debridement with bone grafting and internal fixation (44 cases; 9.46%), and combined anterior–posterior approach (39 cases; 8.39%). Among 324 cases requiring bone grafting, autologous iliac bone grafts were most frequently used (47.84%), followed by lamina and spinous process grafts (32.41%), allografts (10.18%), and artificial bone (9.57%). Between 2012 and 2023, antibiotic therapy was followed up in 754 patients (91.84%), with doxycycline plus rifampin being the most prescribed regimen (493 cases; 60.05%) (Fig. 8). Patients with concurrent meningitis received triple therapy including rifampin, doxycycline, and ceftriaxone. Among the followed patients, 738 (97.88%) adhered strictly to the prescribed regimen. Sixteen showed poor adherence due to inconsistent use, premature discontinuation after symptom relief, or switching to other treatments.

Analysis of drug combinations in 821 patients with BS

At three months post-treatment, 96.55% (728/754) of patients were either cured or improved. All 26 relapses occurred in the medication-only group (Group A), with none in the surgical group (Group B), yielding an overall relapse rate of 3.45%. Inflammatory markers (ESR and CRP) and VAS scores showed no significant difference between groups at baseline (P > 0.05). However, at 1, 3, and 6 months post-treatment, ESR and CRP levels in Group B were significantly lower than in Group A (P < 0.05) (Tables 6–8). At the final follow-up, both groups returned to normal ranges with no statistical difference. Similarly, VAS scores improved significantly in both groups within the first month (P < 0.01), but were consistently better in Group B at all follow-up points (P < 0.05).

Discussion

Brucellar spondylitis is a globally prevalent infectious disease that, in severe cases, can result in irreversible neurological impairment and spinal deformities. The Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region (hereafter referred to as Ningxia) has long been recognized as an endemic area for HB. Following the introduction of livestock vaccination programs in 1969 [18], the annual incidence rate remained below 0.45 per 100,000 population until 2004. However, a rapid resurgence began thereafter, with incidence rising to 0.441 per 100,000 in 2005 and reaching 86.83 per 100,000 by 2022 [19]. This epidemiological shift can be attributed to multiple factors. Geographically, Ningxia is located within the agro-pastoral ecotone of northern China, where economic underdevelopment and dependence on agriculture increase exposure to risk. In addition, close human-animal interactions, especially within specialized livestock farming systems, contribute to sustained transmission. For example, the region is known for its nationally protected geographical indication product, Yanchi Tan sheep, which represents a significant source of occupational exposure [20]. In the present study, we retrospectively analyzed the epidemiological patterns, clinical features, and treatment outcomes of 821 patients diagnosed with brucellar spondylitis and treated at a hospital in Ningxia. Our analysis suggests that adult and elderly males represented the largest proportion of BS cases in this hospitalized cohort, potentially indicating increased susceptibility or exposure in this demographic. This observation aligns with previous studies [21]. The increased risk may be attributed to occupational exposures, as adult males are more likely to engage in professions involving direct contact with livestock. Therefore, future preventive measures should focus on male populations, especially in regions where agriculture and animal husbandry are prevalent.

Livestock farming plays a significant role in Ningxia’s economy. However, activities such as breeding, slaughtering, processing, and selling livestock pose risks for HB infection. Our retrospective analysis of patient histories revealed that individuals engaged in livestock herding, slaughtering, raw meat handling, or consumption of unpasteurized or undercooked animal products were more frequently represented among BS cases compared to those without reported animal contact. This suggests that both direct contact with infected animals or their secretions and indirect routes, such as consumption of undercooked meat, can lead to brucellosis infection, consistent with prior research [22]. Regarding ethnic composition, the ratio of Han to other ethnic minorities is 2.03:1, reflecting differences among ethnic groups that may be attributed to variations in lifestyle, dietary habits, and occupational characteristics. Occupational stratification demonstrated predominance among agricultural/pastoral workers (56.76%, 466/821), followed by homemakers/unemployed individuals (13.89%, 114/821) and industrial laborers (13.40%, 110/821). It is noteworthy that 578 (70.4%; 578/821) cases reported a history of livestock exposure, predominantly sheep. Therefore, it was hypothesized that the most common strain in Ningxia might be B. melitensis, which was consistent with the findings of Liu et al. [19]. In our study, we observed that Xiji County reported the highest number of BS cases, differing from Liu et al.‘s findings, which identified Yanchi County as the most affected. It’s important to note that Liu et al.‘s research primarily focused on brucellosis rather than BS specifically. Nonetheless, their findings offer valuable insights. They reported an initial increase in brucellosis cases from 2012 to 2015, followed by a decline between 2015 and 2018, and a subsequent rise after 2018. This trend is generally consistent with the temporal pattern of BS case numbers observed in this hospitalized cohort in Ningxia.

In recent years, Ningxia has experienced a continuous increase in brucellosis cases, reaching a historical peak in 2022. This trend suggests that the overall epidemiological situation of brucellosis may significantly impact the incidence of BS. Therefore, conducting in-depth studies to understand the incidence characteristics of BS in Ningxia is not only crucial for enhancing local prevention and control efforts but also provides valuable insights for BS management nationwide. We conducted a retrospective analysis of the clinical features of 821 patients at initial presentation and found that back pain was the most common clinical manifestation (94.89%; 779/821), followed by fever (39.34%; 323/821), hyperhidrosis (19.85%; 163/821), fatigue (20.95%; 172/821), and neurological deficits (20.59%; 169/821). These findings align with previous literature [23, 24]. However, the proportion of patients presenting with fever in our study (39.34%) was markedly lower than the 93.3% reported by Jiang et al. [25]. Further review of patient histories revealed that some individuals had intermittent fever during the early disease stage. Due to limited disease awareness, many were misdiagnosed with common bacterial infections and treated with antipyretics or empirical antibiotics. These suboptimal interventions likely suppressed febrile symptoms without eradicating the infection, thereby facilitating the transition to chronic brucellosis. Chronic-phase BS typically presents with less specific symptoms, such as low-grade or episodic fever and atypical arthralgia, which complicates timely diagnosis.

In our cohort, 498 patients (60.66%) were afebrile upon admission, likely due to prior use of antibiotics, which may have masked early symptoms and delayed accurate diagnosis. This constitutes a methodological limitation of the present study. Future prospective research should incorporate standardized febrile monitoring protocols during early hospitalization, including continuous temperature recording and serial procalcitonin measurements, along with long-term follow-up (≥ 6 months) to better characterize the clinical course of BS across different ethnic groups and occupational exposure backgrounds.

In diagnosing BS, the primary challenges arise from the disease’s nonspecific clinical manifestations and prolonged incubation period. Patients may present with fever, fatigue, and joint pain, symptoms that overlap with numerous other conditions [11]. To enhance diagnostic accuracy, it is crucial to integrate laboratory and imaging studies. Unuvar et al. [26]identified the lumbar spine, particularly the L4–L5 level, as the most frequently affected site in BS cases. Our study concurs, with the lumbar spine being the primary site of involvement in 59.81% (491/821) of cases. Additionally, research indicates that 2–36% of BS cases exhibit multilevel vertebral involvement [24]. In our cohort, 109 patients had multisegmental involvement, with 24 cases presenting as non-contiguous BS. The ESR and CRP are effective indicators for assessing the activity and treatment response of BS [27]. Lu et al. [24] compared the inflammatory markers between HB and BS, suggesting that BS patients might exhibit higher ESR and CRP levels. However, in our study, although 76.25%(626/821) of patients had elevated ESR and 73.33%(602/821) had elevated CRP, these rates were lower compared to previous studies on HB [28, 29]. This discrepancy may be attributed to the heightened awareness of brucellosis among clinicians in Ningxia, which has led to earlier detection of the disease—often before the full activation of inflammatory responses—and consequently, a lower proportion of patients with elevated inflammatory markers. In this study, we observed anomalies in platelet counts among patients: 33 patients (4.02%; 33/821) had platelet counts below 125 × 109/L, while 107 patients (13.03%;107/821) had counts exceeding 350 × 109/L. Thrombocytopenia may be associated with splenomegaly-induced splenic hyperfunction [30]. The underlying causes of thrombocytosis are less frequently discussed in current research. Given that brucellosis is a multisystemic disease, we hypothesize that the systemic inflammatory response triggered by Brucella infection could impact hematopoietic function in the bone marrow, leading to increased platelet production. Additionally, Brucella infection might cause splenic hyperfunction, further influencing platelet generation and consumption. However, these hypotheses require validation through further studies. Furthermore, the rates of blood culture submissions and pathological examinations for surgical patients at our medical institution have increased annually since 2013, eventually stabilizing. This trend indicates a progressive enhancement in the diagnostic and therapeutic capabilities of clinicians in Ningxia concerning Brucella spondylitis. Additionally, the blood culture positivity rate rose from 18.18% (10/55) in 2016 to 59.41% (38/64) in 2023, likely reflecting reduced empirical antibiotic usage and advancements in culturing techniques.

In the management of BS, prolonged antibiotic therapy remains the cornerstone of antimicrobial intervention [31]. Surgical intervention becomes imperative in cases of antibiotic resistance, abscess formation, or progressive neurological deficits. In our study involving 821 patients, 465 received surgical treatment while 356 underwent conservative management with antibiotics. The outcomes indicated that the majority of patients in both groups achieved recovery or improvement. Notably, in this study, 51 patients exhibited negative results for both the RBT and the SAT, as well as negative blood cultures upon admission. Despite these findings, empirical treatment was initiated based on diagnoses made by two or more experienced chief physicians, leading to favorable outcomes. This finding underscores the importance of physician expertise and comprehensive clinical judgment in the diagnosis and treatment of BS. During the follow-up period, 169 patients exhibiting neurological symptoms were observed over an extended duration. However, 17 patients were lost to follow-up. Among the 101 patients who underwent surgical treatment, all experienced varying degrees of improvement in limb numbness postoperatively. In contrast, among the 51 patients who received conservative treatment due to factors such as intolerance to surgery, financial constraints, or other reasons, the majority continued to experience persistent or worsening limb numbness. This finding aligns with previous research [32], suggesting that surgical intervention not only accelerates neurological recovery but also provides more stable long-term outcomes for patients with moderate to severe conditions. While conservative treatment may maintain certain functional aspects in some patients (e.g., 18 patients in Group A remained stable at Grade D one-month post-treatment), its ability to promote significant functional improvement is limited, particularly in achieving higher functional grades (e.g., progressing from Grade D to E or from Grade C to E). By comparing the SAT test gradings before and after treatment between the two patient groups, we observed that Group B demonstrated a more favorable response than Group A in reducing SAT titers of ≥ 1:800 and ≥ 1:1600 among BS patients. In summary, Group B outperformed Group A in both the reduction of SAT titers and the improvement of Frankel grades.

Limitations

This study was a single-center retrospective design with possible selection bias, and future conclusions need to be verified by a multicenter prospective study. Although 754 of 821 patients (91.84%; 754/821) were successfully followed up, some treatment information was missing, which may affect the accuracy of the results. Future studies should increase the sample size and use a randomized controlled design to improve the broad applicability and reliability of the conclusions.

Conclusion

Brucellar spondylitis in Ningxia closely follows the regional epidemiologic trends of human brucellosis. Early diagnosis should integrate imaging examinations with clinical evaluation. To reduce disease incidence and recurrence, targeted follow-up and clinical monitoring of high-risk populations are recommended.

An extension and refinement of the theorems of Douglas and Sebestyén for unbounded operators
Yosra Barkaoui, Seppo Hassi

Abstract
. For a closed densely defined operator T T from a Hilbert space ℌ \mathfrak{H} to a Hilbert space 𝔎 , \mathfrak{K}, necessary and sufficient conditions are established for the factorization of T T with a bounded nonnegative operator X X on 𝔎 . \mathfrak{K}. This results yields a new extension and a refinement of a well-known theorem of R.G. Douglas, which shows that the operator inequality A ∗ ​ A ≤ λ 2 ​ B ∗ ​ B , λ ≥ 0 A^{*}A\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B,\lambda\geq 0 , is equivalent to the factorization A = C ​ B A=CB with ‖ C ‖ ≤ λ \|C\|\leq\lambda . The main results give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of an intermediate selfadjoint operator H ≥ 0 H\geq 0 , such that A ∗ ​ A ≤ λ ​ H ≤ λ 2 ​ B ∗ ​ B A^{*}A\leq\lambda H\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B . The key results are proved by first extending a theorem of Z. Sebestyén to the setting of unbounded operators.

1.
Introduction

Let
(\mathfrak{H},(.,.)_{\mathfrak{H}})
be a Hilbert space and denote by
\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H})
the class of bounded everywhere defined operators on
\mathfrak{H}.
For
T,B\in\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H}),
R.G. Douglas
[
6
, Theorem 1]
showed, in 1966, that the following equivalence holds for some
\lambda\geq 0:

(1.1)

TT^{*}\leq\lambda^{2}\,BB^{*}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad T=BC,\ C\in\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{ran}\,T\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,B.

Later, in 1983, Z. Sebestyén
[
10
]
established the following characterization for a related problem:

(1.2)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,T^{*}B\textrm{ for some }\,\lambda\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad T=XB,\ X\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{H}),

where
T,B\in\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H})
and
\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{H})
stands for the class of bounded nonnegative operators on
\mathfrak{H}.

In fact, the inequality in (
1.2
) is closely connected to the one in (
1.1
),
since the identity
T=XB
implies the following two inequalities:

(1.3)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda T^{*}B\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B.

Therefore, the existence of a product presentation
T=XB
, where the factor
X
is not only bounded,
but also
nonnegative
involves an intermediate nonnegative selfadjoint operator
\lambda T^{*}B

lying between
T^{*}T
and
\lambda^{2}B^{*}B
in the theorem of Douglas.

The study of such factorizations has since been extended to more general settings. For instance, in 2013, D. Popovici and Z. Sebestyén
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
generalized the second equivalence in (
1.1
) to multivalued linear operators (linear relations) and showed that
T\subseteq BC
for some liner relation
C
if and only if
\mathrm{ran}\,T\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,B.

On the other hand, the first equivalence in (
1.1
) was established by S. Hassi and H.S.V. de Snoo
[
7
]
for both unbounded linear operators and linear relations in 2015.
As to (
1.2
), its extension has been recently studied by the present authors in the context of closed unbounded densely defined operators
T,B
from the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
to another Hilbert space
\mathfrak{K}
with domains
\mathrm{dom}\,T
and
\mathrm{dom}\,B.

More precisely, it is shown in
[
3
, Theorem 2.7]
that

(1.4)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,T^{*}B\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad X\overline{B_{0}}\subseteq T,\ X\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{H}),

where
B_{0}:=B\upharpoonright\mathrm{dom}\,(T^{*}B)
and
T^{*}B\geq 0
is selfadjoint. Moreover, when in addition,
B
is nonnegative and selfadjoint, also the factorization
T=XB
is characterized in
[
3
]
by means of quasi-affinity to a nonnegative selfadjoint operator. Furthermore, such a factorization is shown to imply several local spectral properties for
T
studied further in
[
2
]
.
In the case of bounded operators Sebestyén’s result in (
1.2
) has also been studied in recent papers by M. L. Arias, G. Corach, and M. C. Gonzalez
[
1
]
and by M. Contino, M. A. Dritschel, A. Maestripieri, and S. Marcantognini
[
5
]
. In the last paper also some local spectral theoretic results for
T=XB\in\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H})
have been established.

In this paper, we improve (
1.4
) and establish a complete unbounded analog of (
1.2
) without the core condition
B=\overline{B_{0}}
and the selfadjointness assumption on
T^{*}B
. Inspired by the work of Sebestyén, our approach hinges on the construction of an auxiliary Hilbert space
by means of a sesquilinear form

\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}[f,g]:=(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}},

f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}=\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T
.
In the first step, the following equivalences are proved in
2.1
:

(1.5)

XB\subseteq T\Leftrightarrow\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}\ \Leftrightarrow\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\,{\lambda}(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}},

where the form
\tau_{T}
is defined by
\tau_{T}[f]=(Tf,Tf)_{\mathfrak{K}}
. An important further result is that the form
\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}
is closable and, therefore, its closure gives rise to a nonnegative selfadjoint operator
H
, and the inequality in (
1.5
) can be described explicitly by means of
H
,
X
and
B
as follows:

(1.6)

T^{*}T\leq\,\lambda H,\quad H=B^{*}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B};

see
2.2
. An essential difference here is that in (
1.4
) the operator
T^{*}B
is assumed to be selfadjoint,
while (
1.6
) shows that
T^{*}B
is in general just a symmetric restriction of
H=H^{*}\geq 0
. Moreover, here
\mathrm{dom}\,B
is
a core for the form
\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}
generating the operator
H
.
A further study of the inequality
T^{*}T\leq\,\lambda H
, where
H
is only assumed to be a selfadjoint operator
(i.e. without the specific formula for
H
in (
1.6
)), is carried out and yields further equivalent conditions
for (
1.5
) in
2.1
, for instance:

\displaystyle XB\subseteq T\Leftrightarrow\,T^{*}T\leq\,\lambda H,\text{ where }H\subseteq B^{*}T\text{ and }\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This not only completes the extension of Sebestyén’s result to the present unbounded framework, but the above mentioned results
motivate the investigation of the
reversed
version of the above inequalities to be studied in
Section

3
.
Namely, also in the present case of unbounded operators, the inequality
T^{*}T\leq\lambda H
implies the inequality
H\leq\lambda B^{*}B
;
see
2.1
.
This second inequality will be characterized in
3.1
with some further results, completing the study of the inequalities
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in the case of unbounded operators.

2.
An extension of Sebestyén’s theorem for unbounded operators

The main purpose of this article is in fact to solve the above problem and present analogue characterizations for the factorization of
T
as in (
1.2
). Our first approach is inspired by Sebestyén theorem
[
10
]
, which we now extend to the unbounded case.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{H},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{H}}),(\mathfrak{K},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{K}})
be two complex Hilbert spaces and let
T,B:H\rightarrow K

be closed densely defined operators. Then the following statements are equivalent for some
\lambda\geq 0:

(1)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\,\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T;

(2)

there exists
X\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K})
such that
\|X\|_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\lambda
and
XB\subseteq T
.

In this case,
X
can be selected such that
\mathrm{ran}\,X\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
.

Assume that (1) holds. Then
(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}}
,
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,B
,
defines a nonnegative sesquilinear form in the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
.
Observe, that

(2.1)

(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Tf=0,\quad f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.

By completing the quotient space
[\mathrm{dom}\,B/(\ker T\cap\mathrm{dom}\,B)]
one obtains a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{K}_{B}

whose inner product is denoted by
\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{B}}
,
\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\in\mathfrak{K}_{B}
,
such that

(2.2)

\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{B}}=(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.

Now let
V:\mathfrak{K}_{B}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be defined by

V\widetilde{f}=Tf\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.

Then
V
is a well-defined linear operator by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
and it follows from (1) that it is bounded by
\sqrt{\lambda}.

It is claimed that

V^{*}Bf=\widetilde{f}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.

To see this, let
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,B
and
\widetilde{g}\in\mathfrak{K}_{B}.
Then,

\langle\widetilde{g},V^{*}Bf\,\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{B}}=(V\widetilde{g},Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(Tg,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\langle\widetilde{g},\widetilde{f}\,\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{B}},

and therefore
V^{*}Bf=\widetilde{f},
as claimed. Consequently,
X:=VV^{*}\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K})
and one has
\|X\|_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\lambda,

\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,X\subseteq\mathrm{\overline{ran}}\,T,
by construction, and

XBf=V\widetilde{f}=Tf\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.

This proves that
XB\subseteq T.

For the converse, assume (2) and let
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.
Then,

\|Tf\|^{2}=\|XBf\|^{2}\leq\,\|X^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|^{2}\|X^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}Bf\|^{2}\leq\lambda(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}},

which completes the proof of (1).
∎

Notice that a combination of (
2.1
) and item (1) of
2.1
shows that if
T\neq 0
then
\lambda>0.
Hence, the form

(2.3)

\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}[f,g]:=(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T,

is nonnegative. The next theorem shows that
\tau_{T,B}
is closable and identifies its closure.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be closed densely defined linear operators such that condition (1) or, equivalently, (2) of
2.1
holds. Then, the form in (
2.3
) is closable and its closure is given by

(2.4)

\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}[f,g]:=(\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}f,\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}g)_{\mathfrak{K}},=(H^{\frac{1}{2}}f,H^{\frac{1}{2}}g)_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}},

where
H=B^{*}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}=H^{*}\geq 0
and
X
is as in
2.1
. Here
H
is the unique representing selfadjoint nonnegative operator of the form
\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}
with
\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}=\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Furthermore, with
\tau_{T}[f,g]:=(Tf,Tg)_{\mathfrak{K}},

f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,T,
each of the following statements is equivalent to the items (1) and (2) of
2.1
:

(1)

\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}
for some
\lambda\geq 0;

(2)

\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}
for some
\lambda\geq 0;

(3)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,H
for some
\lambda\geq 0.

Let
X
be the nonnegative operator in item (2) of
2.1
and let
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}.
Then, (
2.3
) yields

(2.5)

\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}[f,g]=(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(XBf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(X^{\frac{1}{2}}Bf,X^{\frac{1}{2}}Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}}

and therefore to prove the closability of
\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}
is equivalent to prove the closability of the associated operator
X^{\frac{1}{2}}B
to
\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B},
by
[
8
, VI, Example 1.23]
).
To see this, let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,B
such that
f_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
and
X^{\frac{1}{2}}Bf_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}g.
Since
X^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbf{B}(\mathfrak{H}),
it follows that
XBf_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}X^{\frac{1}{2}}g.
On the other hand, the inclusion
XB\subseteq T
together with the fact that
T
is closed yields that
X^{\frac{1}{2}}g=0.
Since
g\in\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,X^{\frac{1}{2}},
one concludes that
g=0.
Thus
X^{\frac{1}{2}}B
is closable. Consequently,
\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}
is a densely defined operator such that
X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}\subseteq\overline{XB}\subseteq T
and, in particular,

\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Furthermore, the operator
H:=(X^{\frac{1}{2}}B)^{*}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}\geq 0
is selfadjoint and it follows from (
2.5
) that

\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}[f,g]=(\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}f,\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}g)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(H^{\frac{1}{2}}f,H^{\frac{1}{2}}g)_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad\text{for all }f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}.

One concludes that
\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}=\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathrm{dom}\,\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.

To see the stated equivalences, observe first from (
2.3
) that for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}=\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T
and for a fixed
\lambda\geq 0,
one has

(2.6)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\,\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\tau_{T}[f]\leq\lambda\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}[f].

On the other hand, it is clear that
\tau_{T}
is closed, since
T
is closed; cf.
[
8
, VI, Example 1.13]
.
Hence, item (v) of
[
4
, Lemma 5.2.2]
gives

(2.7)

\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}\Rightarrow\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}.

Furthermore,
\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}\subseteq\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}
so again by
[
4
, Lemma 5.2.2]
one has

\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}\leq\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}.

This together with (
2.7
) implies that

(2.8)

\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\,\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}\Leftrightarrow\tau_{T}\leq\lambda\,\overline{\tau_{\scriptstyle T,B}}\Leftrightarrow T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,H,

see
[
4
, Theorem 5.2.4]
(or,
[
8
, VI, Remark 2.29]
).
One concludes the equivalences
\rm(1)-(3)
by a combination of
2.1
with (
2.6
) and (
2.8
).
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Let the operators
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
satisfy the conditions
(1)
and
(2)
in
2.1
.
Then the following statements hold for
0\leq\lambda\,(=\|X\|)
:

(1)

0\leq(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\,\lambda\,\|Bf\|^{2}
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B
;

(2)

the operator
T^{*}B
is symmetric and, moreover,

(2.9)

T^{*}B\subseteq H\subseteq B^{*}T;

(3)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda H\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B;

(4)

if
T^{*}B
is selfadjoint, then equalities hold in (
2.9
) and one has

(2.10)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,T^{*}B=\lambda\,B^{*}XB\leq\lambda^{2}\,B^{*}B.

(1) Since
XB\subseteq T
one has for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B
,

(2.11)

(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(XBf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\|X^{\frac{1}{2}}Bf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\|X^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|Bf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2},

so that the inequality holds for
\lambda=\|X\|
.

(2) Under the conditions of
2.1
one has
XB\subseteq T
for
X\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K}),
and since
\overline{XB}=\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}}
one concludes that

(2.12)

T^{*}B\subseteq B^{*}XB\subseteq B^{*}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}=H\subseteq B^{*}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}}=B^{*}\overline{XB}\subseteq B^{*}T.

Since
H
is selfadjoint,
T^{*}B
is symmetric and the proof of
(
2.9
)
is completed.

(3) Observe from
2.2
that
\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T
and let
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B.
Then,

\|H^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}=\|\overline{X^{\frac{1}{2}}B}f\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}=\|X^{\frac{1}{2}}Bf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\|X^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\|Bf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2},

which shows that
H\leq\lambda B^{*}B
with
\lambda=\|X\|.

The other inequality was proved in
2.2
.

(4) Assume that
T^{*}B
is selfadjoint. Then
B^{*}T\subseteq(T^{*}B)^{*}=T^{*}B,

so from (
2.12
) one concludes that

(2.13)

T^{*}B=B^{*}XB=H=B^{*}T.

This proves the equalities in (
2.9
) and by item (3) completes the proof.
∎

Inspired by
2.1
, a natural question arises as to whether items (1) and (3) can also be regarded as sufficient conditions.
As a first step, item (3) will be examined in the next lemma and
2.1
in a more general framework, where the nonnegative operator
H=H^{*}
is assumed to be independent from
X.
The second step deals with item (1), in particular with the question when the following implication holds for some
\lambda\geq 0:

(2.14)

0\leq(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\,\lambda\,\|Bf\|^{2}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\,\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}

for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.

This question induces the study of a reversed version of Sebestyén inequality appearing in the left-hand side of (
2.14
) and will be further studied in
Section

3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H=H^{*}\geq 0
and
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be closed densely defined operators such that

\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
H\subseteq B^{*}T.
Then the following implication holds:

(2.15)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda H\Rightarrow\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\,\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad\text{ for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Assume that

(2.16)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda\,H

and let
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Then,
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
and since
\mathrm{dom}\,H
is a core for
H^{\frac{1}{2}},
there exists
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,B^{*}T
such that
f_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}f
and
H^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}H^{\frac{1}{2}}f
. By (
2.16
) this implies that
\|T(f_{n}-f)\|_{\mathfrak{K}}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
and hence
Tf_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}Tf.
Thus

\displaystyle(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to+\infty}{lim}(Tf_{n},Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\underset{n\to+\infty}{lim}(B^{*}Tf_{n},f)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\underset{n\to+\infty}{lim}(Hf_{n},f)_{\mathfrak{K}}

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to+\infty}{lim}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{n},H^{\frac{1}{2}}f)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(H^{\frac{1}{2}}f,H^{\frac{1}{2}}f)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\|H^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}.

Combining this with (
2.16
) completes the argument.
∎

The next proposition gives some further operator theoretic criteria which are equivalent to the conditions in
2.1
. The proof is directly obtained from a combination of
2.1
,
2.1
and
2.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be closed densely defined operators. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T;

(2)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\lambda\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\,\lambda^{2}\,\|Bf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,T
;

(3)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda H\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and some
0\leq H=H^{*}\subseteq B^{*}T;

(4)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda H
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and
0\leq H=H^{*}\subseteq B^{*}T
with
\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,H^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, if
B^{*}T
is selfadjoint then
H=B^{*}T.

2.1
is, in fact, a useful tool to cover Sebestyén theorem in the general case of unbounded operators, as described in the next corollary, which is analogous to Proposition 2.10 and Corollary 2.11 in
[
3
]
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be closed densely defined operators. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

T=XB
has a solution
X\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K});

(2)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\leq\lambda(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,B=\mathrm{dom}\,T;

(3)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda B^{*}T\leq\lambda^{2}B^{*}B
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and
\mathrm{dom}\,T\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,B
;

(4)

T^{*}T\leq\lambda B^{*}T
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and
\mathrm{dom}\,T\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,B\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,(B^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3.
Characterization of the reversed inequality

The second step involving (
2.14
) is now considered. Analogously to
2.1
,
the following result characterizes a reversed inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{H},(,)_{\mathfrak{H}})
and
(\mathfrak{K},(,)_{\mathfrak{K}})
be complex Hilbert spaces and
T,B:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}
be closed densely defined operators. Then, the following statements are equivalent for some
m>0:

(1b)

\|Tf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}\geq m(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,B;

(2b)

there exists
Y\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K})
such that
YT\subseteq P_{T}B,

where
P_{T}
stands for the orthogonal projection onto
\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
.

Consider the sesquilinear form
(Tf,Bg)
,
f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
.
By assumption the quadratic form
(Tf,Bf)
is nonnegative for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
.
Therefore, it satisfies the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, i.e.,

(3.1)

|(Tg,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}|\leq(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}(Tg,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}}^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\textrm{for all }f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Hence, if
(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=0
for some
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
then by (
3.1
)

(Tg,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=0
holds for all
g\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
, i.e.,
Bf\in(\mathrm{ran}\,T)^{\perp}=\ker T^{*}
.
The converse is also true and, therefore, for
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
one has
(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=0

if and only if
f\in\ker T^{*}B
.

Next observe that
P_{T}B(\mathrm{dom}\,T)=\{0\}
if and only if
(Tf,Bf)=0
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
,
i.e.,
(Tf,Bg)
is the
0
-form on
\mathrm{dom}\,T
; cf. (
3.1
).
In this case (1b) holds trivially and
Y=0
satisfies the inclusion in (2b), and the equivalence
of (1b) and (2b) holds in this case.

\rm{(1b)}\Rightarrow\rm{(2b)}

Assume that
P_{T}B(\mathrm{dom}\,T)\neq\{0\}
or, equivalently, that for some
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
one has
(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}>0
.
In particular, in this case also
T\neq 0
.

Now introduce the Hilbert space
(\mathfrak{K}_{T},\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}})
by completing
the factor space
[\mathrm{dom}\,T/(\mathrm{dom}\,T\cap\ker T^{*}B)]
with respect to the inner product

(3.2)

\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}}:=(Tf,Bg)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad f,g\in\mathrm{dom}\,T,

where
\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}
represent the corresponding equivalence classes.

Next, let the mapping
Z:\mathrm{ran}\,T\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}_{T}
be defined by

ZTf=\widetilde{f}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Then (
3.2
) shows that
Z
is a well-defined linear operator which is bounded by
1/\sqrt{m}
,
since the assumption in (1b) implies that

\|ZTf\|_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}}^{2}=\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{f}\rangle_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}}=(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\tfrac{1}{m}\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\quad\textrm{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

By continuity
Z
can be extended to a bounded operator from
\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
to
\mathfrak{K}_{T}

and with a zero continuation to
(\mathrm{ran}\,T)^{\perp}
one gets a bounded operator
\mathfrak{K}\to\mathfrak{K}_{T}
,
which is still denoted by
Z
.
It is claimed that

Z^{*}\widetilde{f}=P_{T}Bf\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

To see this, let
h=Tt
,
t\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
and
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.
Then,

\displaystyle(h,Z^{*}\widetilde{f}\,)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(ZTt,\widetilde{f})_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}}=(\widetilde{t},\widetilde{f})_{\mathfrak{K}_{T}}=(Tt,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(h,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}},

which proves that
Z^{*}\widetilde{f}-Bf\perp\mathrm{ran}\,T
. By construction,
(\mathrm{ran}\,T)^{\perp}\subseteq\ker Z
and hence
\mathrm{ran}\,Z^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
.
Therefore,
Z^{*}\widetilde{f}=P_{T}Z^{*}\widetilde{f}=P_{T}Bf
as claimed.
Thus, for
Y:=Z^{*}Z\in\bm{B^{+}}(\mathfrak{K})
one has
\|Y\|\leq\tfrac{1}{m}
and

(3.3)

YTf=Z^{*}ZTf=Z^{*}\widetilde{f}=P_{T}Bf\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T,

which means that
YT\subseteq P_{T}B.

\rm{(2b)}\Rightarrow\rm{(1b)}

By the first part of the proof, the statement holds trivially if
Y=0
.
Now assume that
Y\neq 0
, so that
M:=\|Y\|>0
.
Then by assumption
YT\subseteq P_{T}B
and hence for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
one has

(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(Tf,P_{T}Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=(Tf,YTf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\|Y^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}Tf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq M\,\|Tf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}},

which completes the proof of (1b) with
m=1/M>0
.
∎

The proof shows that one can take
M=1/m>0
in Theorem
3.1
when the form
(T\cdot,B\cdot)
is nontrivial.

Corollary 3.1
.

The inequality (1b) in Theorem
3.1
implies also the following inequality:

(3.4)

(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\geq m\,\|P_{T}Bf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad\textrm{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

If
P_{T}B
is closable then also the form
(T\cdot,B\cdot)
on the domain
\mathrm{dom}\,T
is closable,
and this holds, in particular, if
\mathrm{ran}\,B\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
, in which case
P_{T}B=B
.

By Theorem
3.1

YT\subseteq P_{T}B
, where
\|Y\|\leq 1/m
; cf. (
3.3
).
Therefore, one obtains for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
,

\|P_{T}Bf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}=\|YTf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}\leq\frac{1}{m}\,\|Y^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}}}Tf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}=\frac{1}{m}\,(Tf,YTf)_{\mathfrak{K}}=\frac{1}{m}\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}},

which gives the inequality (
3.4
). Notice also that if
(Tf,Bf)=0
for all
f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T
then,
equivalently,
P_{T}B(\mathrm{dom}\,T)=\{0\}
(cf. the proof of Theorem
3.1
),
so that (
3.4
) remains true also in this case.

The second statement can be proved in the same way as the closability was proven in
2.2
.
By assumption in
3.1

B
is closed and hence the last statement is clear, since

\mathrm{ran}\,B\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,T
holds precisely when
P_{T}B=B
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Using
2.1
and switching the roles of
T
and
B
in
2.1
leads to the following equivalent statements
(with

):

(1)

T^{*}T\geq m\,H\geq m^{2}\,B^{*}B

(2)

(3)

Y_{2}T\subseteq B

\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,Y_{2}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{ran}}\,B.

The inclusion in

which is equivalent by
3.1
and
3.1
to

(3.5)

\|Tf\|_{\mathfrak{K}}^{2}\geq m\,(Tf,Bf)_{\mathfrak{K}}\geq m^{2}\,\|P_{T}Bf\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{K}}\quad\textrm{for all }f\in\mathrm{dom}\,T.

Another approach to the inequalities characterized in Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
is studied in
[
3
]

under the assumption that the operator

[

.
The functional analytic approach in the present paper leads to the factorization of the operator

by means of the
B

(instead of the core

Approximate controllability of a bilinear wave equation and minimum time
Karine Beauchard, Thomas Perrin, Eugenio Pozzoli

Abstract
We study the global approximate controllability (GAC) of a Klein-Gordon wave equation, posed on the torus 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} of arbitrary dimension d ∈ ℕ ∗ d\in\mathbb{N}^{*} , with bilinear control potentials supported on the first ( 2 ​ d + 1 ) (2d+1) -Fourier modes. Let Z ⁡ ( W 0 ) ⊂ 𝕋 d Z(W_{0})\subset\mathbb{T}^{d} be the set of essential zeroes of the initial state W 0 ∈ H 1 × L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 d ) W_{0}\in H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}) , and r ⁡ ( W 0 ) ≥ 0 r(W_{0})\geq 0 be the maximum radius of a ball of 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} contained in Z ⁡ ( W 0 ) Z(W_{0}) . Due to finite speed of propagation, the minimum control time starting from W 0 W_{0} is necessarily larger than or equal to r ⁡ ( W 0 ) r(W_{0}) . We prove the following three facts. In low dimensions d ∈ { 1 , 2 } d\in\{1,2\} : the minimum time for GAC from W 0 ≠ 0 W_{0}\neq 0 is equal to r ⁡ ( W 0 ) r(W_{0}) . In any dimensions d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 : the minimum time for GAC from W 0 W_{0} is zero if Z ⁡ ( W 0 ) Z(W_{0}) has zero Lebesgue measure; and the GAC in sufficiently large time from all W 0 ≠ 0 W_{0}\neq 0 . The proof strategy consists in combining Lie bracket techniques à la Agrachev-Sarychev with the propagation of well-prepared positive states.

1.4
Proof strategy

1.4.1
Approximating flows along commutators

A fundamental idea in control theory is the following:
when trajectories can approximate the flows generated by certain operators (as
A_{V}:=A+VB
and
V_{j}B
) then they could also approximate those of other operators, given by (well defined) elements of
{\rm Lie}\{A_{V},V_{j}B\}
.
In this article, we use the following commutators: for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

[fB,[fB,A_{V}]]=-2f^{2}B,

(8)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}{\rm ad}^{j}_{\log(t)F}(tA_{V})\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}B^{*},\qquad F:=[B,A_{V}]=BA_{V}-A_{V}B.

(9)

Here,
B^{*}
denotes the transpose matrix of
B
(see (
5
)). For example, when
f
is one of the control potentials
V_{j}
, then
f^{2}
is linearly independent from
f
, thus (
8
) provides a new generator.
Iterating this procedure allows to
approximate
\exp(\phi B)
for any function
\phi
in
L^{\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

e^{\phi B}\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}+\phi w_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

In this way, we realize the instantaneous transitions

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\quad\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pte^{\phi B}\hskip 8.19447pt}\quad\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}+\phi w_{0}\end{pmatrix}

(11)

that change the velocity while maintaining the profile. This argument is the main idea behind the control of the velocity, stated in Theorem
6
.
The constraint on the zeroes of the velocity
Z(w_{0})\subset Z(\dot{w}_{f}-\dot{w}_{0})
appears naturally, as the profile
w_{0}
multiplies
\phi
. For more details, we refer to the Appendix
A.3
, Propositions
20
and
29
.

Applying instead the flow generated by
B^{*}
, obtained in (
9
), gives

e^{B^{*}}\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}+\dot{w}_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix},

(12)

In this way, we prove the instantaneous transition

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\quad\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pte^{B^{*}}\hskip 8.19447pt}\quad\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}+\dot{w}_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(13)

that change the profile while maintaining the velocity. This strategy allows to control the profile (see Section
5
), once the control on the velocity is established.

In this article, the main difficulties concerning this programme are:

•

the control system (
4
) is infinite-dimensional thus we must be vigilant about the norms for which convergences (such as (
9
)) hold, and whether they can be concatenated,

•

the flows of
A_{V}
can be used only forward in time, since it is a drift, and on time intervals as small as possible, to keep track of the minimum time.

More in general, the problem of understanding (the flow of) which commutators can be approximated by the dynamics of a control system with drift is a complex question, at the root of the nonlinear control theory of ODEs and PDEs.

Propagation from well-prepared states and minimum time

The proofs of

•

For instance, we prove the first statement of

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\dot{w}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\quad\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt|Z(w_{0})|=0\hskip 8.19447pt}\quad\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\

These instantaneous transitions do not modify the zeroes set

Finally, to prove the second point of

1.5
Bibliographical comments

1.5.1
Linear control of wave equations

Intuitively, one can draw a parallel between bilinear control problems of the form
\partial_{t}^{2}w-\Delta w+uw=0
and linear control problems of the form
\partial_{t}^{2}w-\Delta w=\theta u
, where
\theta
is a function that determines the control region. There is a vast amount of literature on the linear control of wave equations, and we restrict ourselves to citing only a few references. When
\theta
is continuous and time-independent, the control region
\{\theta\neq 0\}
is open, and the celebrated condition of Bardos–Lebeau–Rauch is known to be necessary and sufficient for controllability (see
[
2
,
3
]
). Several extensions have been studied, such as the case of a subelliptic operator in
[
4
,
5
]
, or the case of a rough boundary in
[
6
,
7
]
.
Earlier results based on the multiplier method also provide controllability results in certain geometries. Some authors have addressed the case of a time-dependant open control region (see, for instance,
[
8
]
).

Recent works (see
[
9
,
10
]
) have considered the related problem of stabilization with a damping function that is not continuous. In that setting, a different geometric condition arises; however, the damping is assumed to act in a region that is a union of polyhedra and, in particular, that has nonempty interior.

Less is known about the controllability of the wave equation when the control region is merely measurable. The dual question of observability has been studied in
[
11
]
, where it is shown that observability can be obtained under a geometric condition involving the average time spent by Riemannian geodesics in the observation domain. See also the very recent article
[
12
]
which studies the controllability of the 1D wave equation with a measurable space-time control region. In contrast, controllability of the heat equation with a rough control region has been studied by several authors; see, for instance,
[
13
]
and the references therein. Finally, control problems for nonlinear wave equations has been studied in various situations; see, for instance,
[
14
,

,

,

,

,

for its vectorised version (

2.2
Generated groups

In this section, we recall the construction of groups of bounded operators on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
via the Duhamel formula. This formula will be used to prove estimates in this article.

Proposition 13
.

Let
L
be a bounded operator on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
For every
W_{0}\in H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, there exists a unique

W\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
such that, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

W(t)=e^{tA}W_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}LW(s)ds.

Moreover
W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}\times H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|W(t)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq e^{\|L\|t}\|W_{0}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{dW}{dt}(t)=(A+L)W(t),

where the last equality holds in
L^{2}\times H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Therefore, the notation
\exp(t(A+L))W_{0}:=W(t)
defines a group

\big(\exp(t(A+L))\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of bounded operators on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that

\|\exp(t(A+L))\|\leq e^{\|L\|t}.

Proof.

For the existence and uniqueness of
W
, we apply the fixed point theorem on

C^{0}([0,T],H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
with
T\|L\|<1
. Since
T
does not depend on
W_{0}
, the solution with the maximal definition interval is defined on
\mathbb{R}
. The
C^{1}
-regularity and the differential equation result from the ones of
t\mapsto e^{tA}
(see
Proposition

12
). For the bound, we apply Gronwall Lemma to the Duhamel formula.
∎

-STAR property for the operator

The composition and strong limit of

This lemma is the reason why we are interested in strong convergence criteria in the following section. We recall the following usual definition.

3
A criterium for strong convergence

The goal of this section is to prove the following abstract criterium of strong convergence. This criterium shows that it suffices to study the strong convergence
at the level of generators. It is a classical tool when dealing with skew-adjoint generators (by combining, e.g.,
[
35
, Theorem VIII.21 & Theorem VIII.25(a)]
). We show it here in a different setting. It will be applied several times in this article.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\rho
be as in (
3
) and
(m_{1}^{\tau},m_{2}^{\tau},m_{3}^{\tau})_{\tau\in[0,1]}
be bounded a family of
W^{1,\infty}\times L^{\rho}\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for which the associated operators
M_{\tau}
(see (
19
)) strongly converge towards
M_{0}
on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. We assume that
(e^{tM_{0}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group of bounded operators on
H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then the operators

\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})

strongly converge towards

\exp(M_{0})
as
\tau\to 0
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

14
, there exists
\ell>0
such that, for every
\tau\in[0,1]
,
\|M_{\tau}\|\leq\ell
.
By
Proposition

13
, the operators
\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})
are bounded on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
uniformly with respect to
\tau\in[0,1]
:
\|\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})\|\leq e^{\ell}
.
Thus it suffices to prove the convergence in
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of
\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})W_{0}
for
W_{0}
in a dense subset of
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Let
W_{0}\in H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\tau\in(0,1]
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

W(t)=\exp(t(A+\frac{M_{\tau}}{\tau}))W_{0}
and

Z(t)=\exp(t\frac{M_{0}}{\tau})W_{0}
.
By definition (see
Proposition

13
), the following equality holds in
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

W(t)=e^{tA}W_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\frac{M_{\tau}}{\tau}W(s)ds.

Since
(e^{tM_{0}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group of bounded operators on
H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
then
Z\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and satisfies the following equality in
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

Z(t)=e^{tA}W_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\left(\frac{M_{0}}{\tau}-A\right)Z(s)ds.

Thus, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(W-Z)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\left(\frac{M_{\tau}}{\tau}(W-Z)(s)+\left(\frac{M_{\tau}-M_{0}}{\tau}+A\right)Z(s)\right)ds

and, by Gronwall Lemma

\|(W-Z)(\tau)\|\leq e^{\|M_{\tau}\|}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|(M_{\tau}-M_{0})Z(s)\|ds+\int_{0}^{\tau}\|AZ(s)\|ds\right).

The first term in the right hand side is bounded by

\|(M_{\tau}-M_{0})W_{0}\|+\frac{1}{\tau}\!\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\|(M_{\tau}-M_{0})(Z(s)-W_{0})\|ds\leq\!\|(M_{\tau}-M_{0})W_{0}\|+2\ell\underset{s\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|Z(s)-W_{0}\|.

The second term in the right hand side is bounded by

\tau\underset{s\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|Z(s)\|_{H^{2}\times H^{1}}\leq C\tau\|W_{0}\|_{H^{2}\times H^{1}}.

These 2 bounds prove that

\|\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})W_{0}-\exp(M_{0})W_{0}\|\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
.
∎

4.1
Strong convergences

Proposition 20
.

Let
A_{V}:=A+VB
and
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. We have the following strong convergences of operators on

•

\exp(-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{\tau}}B)\exp(\tau A_{V})\exp(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{\tau}}B)\longrightarrow\exp(-\phi^{2}B)

We refer to
Section

A.3
for an intuition, relying on Lie brackets, of these results. The first convergence actually holds as soon as

but we will not use it. These results are also proved in
[
28
]
, but we propose here another proof strategy.

Proof.

\bullet
We apply
Proposition

19
with
M_{\tau}=(\tau V+\phi)B
. By
Proposition

14
, we have
\|M_{\tau}-M_{0}\|\leq C\|\tau V\|_{L^{\rho}}\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
, i.e.
M_{\tau}-M_{0}
converges to zero in
\mathcal{L}(H^{1}\times L^{2})

M_{\tau}
strongly converges towards
M_{0}
on

.
Moreover, by
Proposition

14
,
(e^{t\phi B})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group of bounded operators on
H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Finally, by
Proposition

19
,
\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})
strongly converges towards
\exp(M_{0})
on
H^{1}\times L^{2}
when
\tau\to 0
, which gives the conclusion.

\bullet
We first prove that, for every
\gamma\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and

\exp(-\gamma B)\exp(tA_{V})\exp(\gamma B)=\exp\big(t(A_{V}+\mathcal{M})\big)\quad\text{ where }\quad\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&0\\
-\gamma^{2}&-\gamma\end{pmatrix}

(note that the coefficients of

satisfy the assumptions of

Thanks to (

\exp(\pm\gamma B)=I\pm\gamma B

\exp(-\gamma B)\exp(tA_{V})\exp(\gamma B)W_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}w(t)\\

We have

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}w\\

which proves (

\tau V-\phi^{2}&\sqrt{\tau}\phi\end{pmatrix}.

We apply
Proposition

19
.
By
Proposition

,

\|M_{\tau}-M_{0}\|\leq C(\sqrt{\tau}\|\phi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\tau\|V\|_{L^{\rho}})\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
, i.e.
M_{\tau}-M_{0}
converges to zero in

thus
M_{\tau}
strongly converges towards
M_{0}
on
H^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Since

Proposition

,
(e^{tM_{0}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

H^{2}\times H^{1}

\exp(\tau A+M_{\tau})

on

Proposition 23

Proposition 32
.

We assume

. Let

be a non zero initial condition such that

\dot{w}_{0}\geq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

for

. Then, for every

, we have

a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

Proof.

Case
d=1
:
The following explicit expression, consequence of D’Alembert formula,

w(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}\dot{w}_{0}

(28)

proves that, for every
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
t>r(\dot{w}_{0})
then
w(t,x)>0
. Note that (
28
) defines an element of

, by continuity of translations on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and therefore it is indeed the solution of the wave equation with initial data
(0,\dot{w}_{0})
.

Case
d=2
:
For a sufficiently regular data
\dot{w}_{0}
, the following explicit expression, consequence of Poisson’s formula (see, e.g.,
[
36
]
, Section 4.2.1, equation (27) on p. 74, and Theorem 3 on p. 80)

w(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi t^{2}}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{t^{2}\dot{w}_{0}(y)}{(t^{2}-|y-x|^{2})^{1/2}}dy

(29)

proves that, if
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
t>r(\dot{w}_{0})
, then
w(t,x)>0
.

To complete the proof, we need to show that, at low regularity, i.e.
\dot{w}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, the solution
w
(given by Proposition
12
) coincides with the function at the right-hand side of (
29
), for all
t>0
and almost all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. We denote by
w
both the solution on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and its periodic extension as a function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For
t>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, set

K_{t}(y)=\mathbb{1}_{B(0,t)}(y)\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}.

Using polar coordinates, one finds

\|K_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\int_{B(0,t)}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}=2\pi\int_{0}^{t}\frac{r}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}}dr<+\infty.

Let
t>0
and

, extended by periodicity. Then Young’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|K_{t}\ast g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|K_{t}\ast\left(g\mathbb{1}_{[0,2\pi]^{2}+B(0,t)}\right)\right|^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\|K_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi]^{2}+B(0,t))}^{2}\leq C_{t}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2},

for some
C_{t}>0
, where the sum of two sets is defined by
E_{1}+E_{2}=\{x+y,x\in E_{1},y\in E_{2}\}
. Hence, the operator

\dot{w}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\longmapsto\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{t}\ast\dot{w}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

is well-defined and continuous. By density of smooth functions in

, this proves that Poisson’s formula
indeed coincides punctually with the solution given by Proposition
12
.
∎

The situation seems different along the scale of spaces
(H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}))_{s\geq 1}
. For
s>\frac{d}{2}
, the level sets are closed, because of the embedding
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R})\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R})
. For
s\leq\frac{d}{2}
, the level set may not be closed. Furthermore, within the range of
s<\frac{d}{2}
, the nature of the level sets seem to depend on
s
. We have not found a complete answer to this question in the literature, and we refer to
[
37
, Section 4.7 and 4.8]
and
[
38
]
for related results.

To prove
Theorem

9
.2.(c)
without the assumption “
Z(w_{0})
closed”, our control strategy would require the following result: for every
w_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
there exists
s>\frac{d}{2}-1
and
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that
Z(w_{0})=Z(\varphi)
. This statement is probably false.

The following example illustrates the difficulty of the question.
Let
\alpha\in(0,(d-2)/2)
and
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable and dense subset of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-i}|x-x_{i}|^{-\alpha}
defines an
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
-function which is not in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

9.2
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
-approximate control

A natural question concerns the validity of our result if we replace de
H^{1}\times L^{2}
-approximate controllability by the
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
-approximate controllability with
s>1
.
The first step consists in generalizing the small-time control of the velocity, i.e. to adapt the density criterium of
Section

4.3
.

To fix ideas, let us consider the case
s=2
and consider a non zero initial condition
W_{0}=(w_{0},\dot{w}_{0})\in H^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
Let
E:=\{\phi\in L^{\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{d});\nabla\phi\in L^{\widetilde{\rho}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\}
so that, for any
\phi\in E
the function
\phi w_{0}
belongs to
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. To formulate a result of small-time
H^{2}\times H^{1}
-approximate controllability from
W_{0}
, we need to identify the adherence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of the set
\{\phi w_{0};\phi\in E\}
.

Clearly, for every
\phi\in E
, we have

\phi w_{0}=0
a.e. on
Z(w_{0})
and
\nabla(\phi w_{0})=0
a.e. on
Z(w_{0})\cap Z(\nabla w_{0})
i.e.

\text{Adh}_{H^{1}}\{\phi w_{0};\phi\in E\}\subset\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d});Z(w_{0})\subset Z(f)\text{ and }Z(w_{0})\cap Z(\nabla w_{0})\subset Z(\nabla f)\}.

Such constraints on
\nabla f
would be much more complicated to manage in the other stages of the article.

We prove that

A.3
Formal computations of commutators

Let
A,B,F
be defined in (
5
) and (
24
). Then, formally,

[B,A]:=BA-AB=F,\qquad[B,[B,A]]=-2B,\qquad\text{ad}_{B}^{j}(A)=0\,,\forall j\geq 3,

The proof of
Proposition

20
relies on the formulas (
22
) and (
26
). For matrices
A
and
B
, these formulas can be proved in the following way

\displaystyle\exp(-\gamma B)\exp(tA)\exp(\gamma B)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\exp(-\gamma B)A\exp(\gamma B)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-\gamma)^{j}}{j!}\text{ad}_{B}^{j}(A)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\left(A-\gamma[B,A]+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\text{ad}_{B}^{2}(A)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\left(A-\gamma F-\gamma^{2}B\right)\right),

and

\displaystyle\exp(-\gamma B)\exp\big(t(A+\gamma^{2}B)\big)\exp(\gamma B)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\exp(-\gamma B)(A+\gamma^{2}B)\exp(\gamma B)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-\gamma)^{j}}{j!}\text{ad}_{B}^{j}(A+\gamma^{2}B)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\left(A+\gamma^{2}B-\gamma[B,A]+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\text{ad}_{B}^{2}(A)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t(A-\gamma F)\right).

Similarly, formal computations prove that

\text{ad}_{F}^{j}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}0&(-2)^{j}\\
2^{j}(\Delta-1)&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition

29
then can be formally proved in the following way,

\displaystyle\exp(\gamma F)\exp(tA)\exp(-\gamma F)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\exp(\gamma F)A\exp(-\gamma F)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\gamma^{j}}{j!}\text{ad}_{F}^{j}(A)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-2\gamma}\\
e^{2\gamma}(\Delta-1)&0\end{pmatrix}\right).

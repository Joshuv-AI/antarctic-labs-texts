Non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds via Malliavin-Stein method
Nguyen Tien Dung, Le Vi ∗, Pham Thi Phuong Thuy

Abstract
In this paper, we establish non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds by means of the Malliavin-Stein method. Applications to the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals and the exponential functionals of Brownian motion are given to illustrate the theory.



Keywords:
Malliavin-Stein method, non-uniform Berry-Esseen bound.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification:
60F05, 60G15, 60H07.

1
Introduction

The Malliavin-Stein method was introduced by Nourdin and Peccati in 2009. Their seminal paper
[
7
]
combines Stein’s method and Malliavin calculus to obtain quantitative central limit theorems for the functionals of a Gaussian process. Since then, many important achievements have been obtained by various authors. For a long list of papers using the Malliavin-Stein method, the reader can consult the constantly updated website

https://sites.google.com/site/malliavinstein/home.

One of main results obtained in
[
7
]
is the following uniform Berry-Esseen bound: Let
Z\sim N(0,1)
and let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
with zero mean. If the law of
F
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then we have

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}.

(1.1)

The space
\mathbb{D}^{1,2},
the operators
D
and
L^{-1}
will be defined in Section
2
below. We recall that the uniform Berry-Esseen bounds are very useful in statistical applications. However, such bounds only work well for values of
z
near the center of the distribution. For
z
sufficiently large, the difference
P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)
becomes so close to zero and the uniform bounds are too crude to be of any use.

In this paper, our aim is to obtain the non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds standing for (
1.1
). More specifically, under the same assumption as in
[
7
]
, we obtain in Theorem
3.1
below the following bound

|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}(\sqrt{P(|F|>|z|/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4})\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

In the classical central limit theorem for independent random variables, the rate of convergence with respect to
z
is of order
O(1+|z|^{3}).
Here, by using Markov’s inequality, our bound (
1.2
) also gives the same this rate whenever
E|F|^{6}
is finite. Furthermore, we can employ the theory of concentration inequalities to evaluate
P(|F|>|z|/2)
and, in many situations, we are able to obtain a much faster rate. For example, when applied to
F=I_{q}(f)
the multiple Wiener-Itô integral of order
q\geq 2,
the bound (
1.2
) gives us an exponential rate that reads

|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}(E|F|^{4}-3)}(c_{q}e^{-\frac{z^{2/q}}{2^{2+2/q}}}+2e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4}})\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R},

where
c_{q}
is a positive constant.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly recall some of the relevant elements of the Malliavin calculus. Our main results are then formulated and proved in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we apply our results to derive non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds for the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals and the exponential functionals of Brownian motion.

2
Malliavin Calculus

Let us recall some elements of Malliavin calculus that we need in order to perform our proofs (for more details see
[
8
]
). Suppose that
\mathfrak{H}
is a real separable Hilbert space with scalar product denoted by
\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}.
We denote by
W=\{W(h):h\in\mathfrak{H}\}
an isonormal Gaussian process defined in a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P),

\mathcal{F}
is the
\sigma
-field generated by
W.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all smooth cylindrical random variables of the form

F=f(W(h_{1}),...,W(h_{n})),

(2.1)

where
n\in\mathbb{N},f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of bounded and infinitely differentiable functions with bounded partial derivatives,
h_{1},...,h_{n}\in\mathfrak{H}.
If
F
has the form (
2.1
), we define its Malliavin derivative with respect to
W
as the element of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H})
given by

DF=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(W(h_{1}),...,W(h_{n}))h_{k}.

More generally, we can define the
k
th order derivative
D^{k}F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k})
by iterating the derivative operator
k
times. For any integer
k\geq 1
and any
p\geq 1,
we denote by
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\|F\|^{p}_{k,p}:=E|F|^{p}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}E\|D^{i}F\|^{p}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes i}}.

An important operator in the Malliavin calculus theory is the divergence operator
\delta,
it is the adjoint of the derivative operator
D
characterized by

E\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=E[F\delta(u)]

(2.2)

for any
F\in\mathcal{S}
and
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}).
The domain of
\delta
is the set of all processes
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H})
such that

E|\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|\leq C(u)\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
C(u)
is some positive constant depending only on
u.
Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
u\in Dom\,\delta
such that
Fu\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathfrak{H}).
Then
Fu\in Dom\,\delta
and we have the following relation

\delta(Fu)=F\delta(u)-\left\langle DF,u\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}},

provided the right-hand side is square integrable.

For any integer
q\geq 0,
we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{q}
the
q
th Wiener chaos of
W.
We recall that
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
and, for any
q\geq 1,

\mathcal{H}_{q}
is the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
generated by the family of random variables
I_{q}(f)
where
I_{q}
indicates a multiple Wiener-Itô integral of order
q
and
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}
(the
q
th symmetric tensor power of
\mathfrak{H}
). It is known that any random variable
F
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
can be expanded into an orthogonal sum of its Wiener chaos:

F=E[F]+\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}I_{q}(f_{q}),

where the series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f_{q}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\odot q}.
From this chaos expansion one may define the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
L
by
LF=\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}-qI_{q}(f_{q})
when
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
and its pseudo-inverse by
L^{-1}(F-E[F])=\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{q}I_{q}(f_{q}).
Hereafter, we write
L^{-1}F
instead of
L^{-1}(F-E[F]).
Note that, for any
F\in L^{2}(\Omega),
we have
L^{-1}F\in Dom\,L
and
LL^{-1}F=L^{-1}LF=F-E[F].
Moreover, the operators
D,\delta
and
L
satisfy the following relationship:
F\in Dom\,L
if and only if
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
and, in this case,

\delta DF=-LF.

(2.3)

3
Non-uniform Berry-Esseen bounds

Let
\Phi
denote the cumulative distribution function of standard normal random variable. We recall that, for each
z\in\mathbb{R},
the Stein equation

f^{\prime}(x)-xf(x)={\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{x\leq z\}}-\Phi(z),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}

admits a unique solution
f_{z}(x)
given by

f_{z}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{2\pi}e^{x^{2}/2}\Phi(x)(1-\Phi(z))&\hbox{if}\,\,\,x\leq z,\\
\sqrt{2\pi}e^{x^{2}/2}(1-\Phi(x))\Phi(z)&\hbox{if}\,\,\,x>z.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

The key point in our proof is to establish new estimates for
f_{z}
and
f^{\prime}_{z}
as in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
z>0
and let
f_{z}
be given by (
3.1
). Then we have

0<f_{z}(x)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4},\,\,\,|f^{\prime}_{z}(x)|\leq 1\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.2)

0<f_{z}(x)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}e^{-z^{2}/4}\,\,\forall\,|x|\leq z/2

(3.3)

and

|f^{\prime}_{z}(x)|\leq 2e^{-z^{2}/4}\,\,\forall\,|x|\leq z/2.

(3.4)

Proof.

The estimates in (
3.2
) are well known, see e.g Lemma 2.3 in
[
1
]
. Let us prove (
3.4
). When
x\leq z,
we have
f_{z}(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{x^{2}/2}\Phi(x)(1-\Phi(z))
and hence,

f^{\prime}_{z}(x)=(1-\Phi(z))\left(1+\sqrt{2\pi}xe^{x^{2}/2}\Phi(x)\right).

Fixed
z>0,
we deduce

|f^{\prime}_{z}(x)|\leq(1-\Phi(z))\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}ze^{z^{2}/8}\right),\,\,|x|\leq z/2.

We now observe that

\displaystyle 1-\Phi(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(u+z)^{2}/2}du\leq e^{-z^{2}/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}du=\frac{1}{2}e^{-z^{2}/2}.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}_{z}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-z^{2}/2}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi/2}ze^{z^{2}/8}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-z^{2}/4}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi/2}ze^{-z^{2}/8}\right),\,\,|x|\leq z/2.

Furthermore, it is easy to verify that
ze^{-z^{2}/8}\leq 2e^{-1/2}\,\forall\,z>0.
So we conclude that

|f^{\prime}_{z}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-z^{2}/4}\left(1+2\sqrt{\pi/2}e^{-1/2}\right)<2e^{-z^{2}/4},\,\,|x|\leq z/2.

This completes the proof of (
3.4
). The verification of (
3.3
) is trivial. Indeed, we have

f_{z}(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{x^{2}/2}\Phi(x)(1-\Phi(z))\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}e^{z^{2}/8}e^{-z^{2}/2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}e^{-z^{2}/4},\,\,|x|\leq z/2.

The proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

We now are in a position to state and prove the main results of the present paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Z\sim N(0,1)
and let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.
If, in addition, the law of
F
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then we have

|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\big(|E[F]|+\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\big)\big(\sqrt{P(|F|>|z|/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\big)\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.5)

Proof.

We first recall the arguments in the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
7
]
. Note that
F=E[F]-\delta DL^{-1}F.
Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{R},
by using the Stein’s equation and the relationship (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)]-E[f_{z}(F)F]

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)]-E[f_{z}(F)(F-E[F])]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F]

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)]+E[f_{z}(F)\delta DL^{-1}F]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F]

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}})]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F].

Case 1:
z\geq 0.
We rewrite the above expression as follows

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|\leq z/2\}}]+E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|>z/2\}}]

\displaystyle-E[f_{z}(F){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|\leq z/2\}}]E[F]-E[f_{z}(F){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|>z/2\}}]E[F].

Then, by Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle|P

\displaystyle(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|

\displaystyle\leq E|f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|\leq z/2\}}|+E|f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|>z/2\}}|

\displaystyle+(E[f_{z}(F){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|\leq z/2\}}]+E[f_{z}(F){\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|>z/2\}}])|E[F]|

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-z^{2}/4}E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|+E\left[|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|{\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|F|>z/2\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}e^{-z^{2}/4}+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}P(|F|>z/2)\bigg)|E[F]|.

So, by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\big(|E[F]|+\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\big)\big(\sqrt{P(|F|>z/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\big).

This finishes the proof of (
3.5
) for
z\geq 0.

Case 2:
z<0.
This case follows directly from
Case 1
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|

\displaystyle=|P(-F\geq-z)-P(-Z\geq-z)|

\displaystyle=|P(-F\geq-z)-P(Z\geq-z)|

\displaystyle=|P(-F\leq-z)-P(Z\leq-z)|

\displaystyle\leq\big(|E[-F]|+\sqrt{E|1-\langle D(-F),-DL^{-1}(-F)\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\big)\big(\sqrt{P(|-F|>-z/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(|E[F]|+\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\big)\big(\sqrt{P(|F|>-z/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\big).

The proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

When
F
can be represented as a Skorokhod integral:
F=E[F]+\delta(u)
for some stochastic process
u,
we use the relationship (
2.2
) to get

\displaystyle P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)]-E[f_{z}(F)(F-E[F])]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F]

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)]-E[f_{z}(F)\delta(u)]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F]

\displaystyle=E[f^{\prime}_{z}(F)(1-\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}})]-E[f_{z}(F)]E[F].

Hence, with the exact proof of Theorem
3.1
, we also have the following non-uniform Berry-Esseen bound

|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\big(|E[F]|+\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\big)(\sqrt{P(|F|>|z|/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4})\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.6)

4
Some applications

In this section, we provide some applications to illustrate the results obtained in the previous section.

4.1
Multiple Wiener-Itô integrals

We recall that among the applications of the bound (
1.1
) is the fourth moment theorem for the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals. Here we have the following non-uniform Berry-Esseen bound.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer, and let
F=I_{q}(f)
have variance one. Then, it holds that

|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}(E|F|^{4}-3)}(c_{q}e^{-\frac{z^{2/q}}{2^{2+2/q}}}+2e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4}})\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R},

(4.1)

where
c_{q}
is a positive constant depending only on
q.

Proof.

When
F=I_{q}(f),
it is known from
[
7
]
that

\sqrt{E|1-\langle DF,-DL^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}(E|F|^{4}-3)}.

On the other hand, for the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals with variance one, Theorem 4.1 in
[
4
]
provides us the concentration bound

P\left(|I_{q}(f)|>x\right)\leq c_{q}^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2/q}}{2}\right),\,\,\,x\geq 0,

where
c_{q}
is a positive constant depending only on
q.
So the bound (
4.1
) follows directly from (
3.5
).
∎

To our best knowledge, this is the first time that a non-uniform Berry-Esseen bound for the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals have appeared in the literature. In addition, this non-uniform bound significantly improves the uniform bound obtained in
[
7
]
:

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|P(F\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}(E|F|^{4}-3)}.

4.2
Exponential functionals

Let
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a standard Brownian motion defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P)
, where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a natural filtration generated by
B.
We consider the exponential functional of the form

F_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds,\,\,t\in[0,T],

(4.2)

where
a
is a real number. It is known that this functional plays an important role in several domains. A lot of fruitful properties of
F_{t}
can be founded in the literature, see e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
. Let us define

\tilde{F}_{t}:=\frac{F_{t}-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}},\,\,t\in[0,T],

(4.3)

where
m_{t}=E[F_{t}]
and
\sigma_{t}^{2}={\rm Var}(F_{t}).
It was proved in
[
2
]
that
\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{m_{t}}{t}=1,\,\,\,\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{3\sigma_{t}^{2}}{t^{3}}=1
and

\tilde{F}_{t}\,\,\,\text{converges in distribution to}\,\,Z\sim N(0,1)\,\,\text{as}\,\,t\to 0.

In the next theorem, we give an explicit estimate for the rate of convergence.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
Z\sim N(0,1)
and let
(\tilde{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be given by (
4.3
). Then, for each
t\in(0,T],
we have

|P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\frac{2e^{2at+4t}t^{3}\sqrt{t}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}(1+\frac{|z|\sigma_{t}}{2m_{t}})}{4t}\right)+e^{-z^{2}/16}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\right)\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

In order to see the rate of convergence, we note that

\text{$\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{2e^{2at+4t}t^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}=6$ and $\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln^{2}(1+\frac{|z|\sigma_{t}}{2m_{t}})}{4t}=\frac{z^{2}}{48}$}.

Hereafter, the Malliavin derivative operator
D
is with respect to
B.
In proofs, we use the following covariance formula: For any
F,G\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2},
we have

{\rm Cov}(F,G)=E\left[\int_{0}^{T}D_{s}FE[D_{s}G|\mathcal{F}_{s}]ds\right].

(4.4)

Before proving Theorem
4.2
, we first provide the concentration inequalities for
\tilde{F}_{t}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\tilde{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be given by (
4.3
). Then, for each
t\in(0,T],
we have

P(\tilde{F}_{t}\geq x)\leq e^{-\frac{\ln^{2}(1+x\sigma_{t}/m_{t})}{2t}},\,\,\,x\geq 0

(4.5)

and

P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq-x)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2},\,\,\,x\geq 0

(4.6)

Proof.

Fixed
t\in(0,T],
we consider the random variable

X_{t}=\ln\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds\right).

It is easy to verify that
X_{t}
is a Malliavin differentiable random variable and its derivative is given by

D_{\theta}X_{t}=\frac{\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds}{\int_{0}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds},\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t.

We have
0<D_{\theta}X_{t}\leq 1
and hence,

0<\int_{0}^{t}D_{\theta}X_{t}E[D_{\theta}X_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta\leq t\,\,a.s.

This, together with Theorem 2.4 in
[
3
]
, implies that

P\left(X_{t}-E[X_{t}]\geq y\right)\leq e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}\,\,\forall\,y\geq 0.

We now observe that
E[X_{t}]\leq\ln m_{t}
by Lyapunov’s inequality. Then, for
x\geq 0,
we deduce

\displaystyle P(\tilde{F}_{t}\geq x)

\displaystyle=P\left(X_{t}\geq\ln(x\sigma_{t}+m_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(X_{t}-E[X_{t}]\geq\ln(x\sigma_{t}+m_{t})-E[X_{t}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(X_{t}-E[X_{t}]\geq\ln(x\sigma_{t}+m_{t})-\ln m_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\ln^{2}(1+x\sigma_{t}/m_{t})}{2t}}.

This completes the proof of (
4.5
). In order to prove the relation (
4.6
), we consider the function

g(\lambda)=E[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}],\,\,\lambda\geq 0.

By using the covariance formula (
4.4
), we have

g^{\prime}(\lambda)=-E[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}\tilde{F}_{t}]=\lambda E[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}\Gamma_{t}],

where
\Gamma_{t}
is defined by

\Gamma_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta.

(4.7)

Note that
E[\Gamma_{t}]={\rm Cov}(\tilde{F}_{t},\tilde{F}_{t})=1.
Then, once again, we use (
4.4
) to get

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda E[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}]+\lambda{\rm Cov}(e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}},\Gamma_{t})

\displaystyle=\lambda g(\lambda)-\lambda^{2}E\left[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}D_{r}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{r}\Gamma_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{r}]dr\right].

We observe that
\int_{0}^{t}D_{r}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{r}\Gamma_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{r}]dr\geq 0\,\,a.s.
Indeed, we have for
0\leq r,\theta\leq t,

D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}}\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds\geq 0,\,\,\,D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}}\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}{\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{[r,t]}(s)ds\geq 0

and

D_{r}\Gamma_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta+\int_{r}^{t}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta\geq 0.

So it holds that

g^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\lambda g(\lambda),\,\,\lambda\geq 0.

This gives us

g(\lambda)\leq e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}},\,\,\lambda\geq 0.

Fixed
x\geq 0,
by Markov’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq-x)\leq e^{-\lambda x}E[e^{-\lambda\tilde{F}_{t}}]=e^{-\lambda x}g(\lambda)\leq e^{-\lambda x+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}},\,\,\lambda\geq 0.

Choosing
\lambda=x
yields
P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq-x)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2}.
So the relation (
4.6
) is verified.

The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.
We will carry out the proof in two steps.

Step 1: Moment estimates.
By using the Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle E|D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}ds\bigg|^{4}\leq\frac{t^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}\int_{\theta}^{t}E[e^{4as+4B_{s}}]ds

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{4as+8s}ds=\frac{t^{4}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}e^{4at+8t}\,\,\forall\,0\leq\theta\leq t

and

\displaystyle E|D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}E\bigg|\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{as+B_{s}}{\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{[r,t]}(s)ds\bigg|^{4}\leq\frac{t^{4}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}e^{4at+8t}\,\,\forall\,0\leq r,\theta\leq t.

Let
\Gamma_{t}
be given by (
4.7
). We use the Cauchy-Schwarz and Lyapunov inequalities to deduce

\displaystyle E|D_{r}\Gamma_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=E\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta+\int_{r}^{t}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]d\theta\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2t\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]|^{2}d\theta+2t\int_{r}^{t}E|D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}E[D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2t\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{E|D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}E|D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}}d\theta+2t\int_{r}^{t}\sqrt{E|D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}E|D_{r}D_{\theta}\tilde{F}_{t}|^{4}}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4t^{6}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}e^{4at+8t}\,\,\forall\,0\leq r\leq t.

(4.8)

Step 2: Conclusion.
By the Clark-Ocone formula, we have

\tilde{F}_{t}=E[\tilde{F}_{t}]+\int_{0}^{t}E[\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]dB_{\theta}.

Hence, by using the bound (
3.6
) with
u_{\theta}=E[\tilde{F}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}],
we obtain

|P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\sqrt{E|1-\Gamma_{t}|^{2}}\left(\sqrt{P(|\tilde{F}_{t}|>|z|/2)}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\right)\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R},

(4.9)

Thanks to Proposition
4.1
we have

\displaystyle P(|\tilde{F}_{t}|>|z|/2)

\displaystyle=P(\tilde{F}_{t}>|z|/2)+P(\tilde{F}_{t}<-|z|/2)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}(1+\frac{|z|\sigma_{t}}{2m_{t}})}{2t}\right)+e^{-z^{2}/8}\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand, recalling (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle E|1-\Gamma_{t}|^{2}={\rm Cov}(\Gamma_{t},\Gamma_{t})\leq\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{r}\Gamma_{t}|^{2}dr\leq\frac{4t^{7}}{\sigma_{t}^{4}}e^{4at+8t}.

Combining the above computations yields

|P(\tilde{F}_{t}\leq z)-P(Z\leq z)|\leq\frac{2e^{2at+4t}t^{3}\sqrt{t}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}(1+\frac{|z|\sigma_{t}}{2m_{t}})}{4t}\right)+e^{-z^{2}/16}+2e^{-z^{2}/4}\right)\,\,\,\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
\square

Global Well-Posedness and Blow-Up for the fifth order L 2 − L^{2}- critical KP-I equation
Francisc Bozgan

Abstract
In the current paper, we investigate the fifth order modified KP-I eqaution, namely ∂ t u − ∂ x 5 u − ∂ x − 1 ∂ y u + ∂ x ( u 3 ) = 0 . \partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}^{5}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u+\partial_{x}(u^{3})=0. This equation is L 2 L^{2} critical and we prove on ℝ × ℝ \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R} that it is globally well posed in the natural energy space if the L 2 L^{2} norm of the initial data is less the L 2 L^{2} norm of the ground state associated to this equation. We also find a subspace of the natural energy space associated to this equation where we have local well-posedness, nevertheless if the initial data is sufficiently localized we obtain blow-up. On ℝ × 𝕋 , \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}, we prove global well-posedness in the energy space for small data.

Introduction

We start with the equation

\begin{cases}&u_{t}-\partial_{x}^{5}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u+\partial_{x}(u^{p+1})=0\\
&u(0,x,y)=u_{0}(x,y)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\end{cases}

(1)

which is the fifth order generalized Kadomtsev-Petviashvili-I equation for
p\geq 1
and
p=\frac{m}{n}
where
(m,n)=1,

n
is an odd integer. For
p=2
, it appears as a model of sound waves in antiferromagnetic materials, see
[
FT85
]
. This is a modified version of an equation that is part of the hierarchy of KP equations, namely

\partial_{t}+(-1)^{\frac{l+1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{l}u\pm\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,

(2)

with the classical KP equations corresponding to
l=3
and depending on the sign of the weak transverse effect term, we call the equation (
2
) KP-I equation if the sign is
-
and KP-II equation if the sign is
+.
Here the operator
\partial_{x}^{-1}
is defined via Fourier transform,
\widehat{\partial_{x}^{-1}f}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{i\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta).
These equations are well-studied, especially the classical KP equations. It is a model for the propagation of weakly nonlinear dispersive long waves on the surface of a fluid, when the wave motion is essentially one-directional with weak transverse effects along the y-axis, see
[
KP70
]
,
[
AS85
]
. We refer to
[
KS21
]
to an extensive overview on these equations. More specifically, in
[
LX08
]
it was proved that the KP-I equation (
2
) for
l=5
is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see also
[
ST00
]
).

In terms of local well-posedness of (
1
), we state the best result about this which appears in
[
Esf11
]
:

Proposition 1
.

Let
u_{0}\in X_{s},s\geq 5
. Then there exists
T>0
such that (
1
) has a unique solution
u(t)
with
u(0)=u_{0}
satisfying

u\in C([0,T);X_{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T);H^{s-5}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).

Moreover, we have the conservation laws:

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\mbox{ and }E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}+(\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}](t)dxdy-\frac{1}{p+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u^{p+2}(t)=E(u_{0}).

We have both scaling and translation invariance for this equation, in particular we have that
u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{\frac{4}{p}}u(\lambda^{5}t,\lambda x,\lambda^{3}y)
solves equation (
1
), hence

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}}=\lambda^{\frac{4}{p}+s_{1}+3s_{2}-2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}},

and we notice that for
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=\lambda^{\frac{4}{p}-2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
E(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\frac{8}{p}}E(u)=\lambda^{\frac{8}{p}}E_{0}.
Thus, for
p=2
we observe that it is the
L^{2}
-critical case for the equation (
1
).

In this paper, we improve this local well-posedness result for
p=2.
We have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

We define
E^{s}=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\|(1+D_{x})^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\}.
. Then the equation (
1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in E^{s}
is locally well-posed for
s>\frac{3}{2}.

In order to prove this, we employ the energy method that was used to prove local well-posedness result for the modified third order KP-I that appeared in
[
KZ05a
]
and
[
KZ05b
]
.

By a solitary-wave solution of (
1
), we mean a traveling-wave solution of (
1
) in the form
u(t,x,y)=\varphi(x-ct,y)
with

as

x^{2}+y^{2}\rightarrow\infty
. And
\varphi_{c}
is a solution of the equation

\begin{cases}-c\varphi_{c}-\partial_{x}^{4}\varphi_{c}-\partial_{x}^{-2}\partial_{y}^{2}\varphi_{c}+\varphi_{c}^{p+1}=0\\
\varphi_{c}\in E^{2},\varphi_{c}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(3)

The existence of solitary waves for equation (
1
) was proved in
[
dBS97a
]
and
[
EL22
]
.
We define
L_{c}(u)=E(u)+\frac{c}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.
We define the ground state as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{c}
be the set of the solutions of (
3
); namely,

\Gamma_{c}=\{\phi\in E^{2}|L^{\prime}_{c}(\phi)=0,\phi\neq 0\}
, and let

be the set of the ground states of (

From (
[

]

is not empty. We know many properties of the ground state of (

). Next, we state a lemma in order to show that

Lemma 1
.

Let

Then we have the following equalities:

If

We also remind the blow-up result that we know about equation (

For the case

The initial value problem (

Well-posedness for the
L^{2}

2.1
Local Well-Posedness Result

In this section, we will deal with the following equation

\begin{cases}&u_{t}-\partial_{x}^{5}-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u+\partial_{x}(u^{3})=0\\
&u(0,x,y)=u_{0}(x,y)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\end{cases}

(4)

We begin by introducing the space, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

H_{-1}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\|u\|_{H_{-1}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}<\infty\}

where

\|u\|_{H_{-1}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\Big(\int\int(1+|\xi|^{-1})^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\mu|^{2})^{s}|\hat{u}(\xi,\mu)|^{2}d\xi d\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The proof of Theorem (
1.1
) follows from the following proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose
u_{0}\in H_{-1}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for
s_{0}
sufficiently large, is a solution to (
1
), for
t\in[0,T_{0}].
Suppose that there exists
s,\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}(\|u_{0}\|_{E^{s}})
and
C=C_{s}
such that, whenever
T_{0}\leq\tilde{T},

a)

\sup_{0<t<T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{E^{s}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{E^{s}}

and

b)

\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{T_{0}}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T_{0}}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}T_{0}^{\varepsilon}(1+\|u_{0}\|_{E^{s}}+\|u_{0}\|_{E^{s}}^{3})\|u_{0}\|_{E^{s}}.

Then equation (
4
) is locally well posed in
E^{s}.

The proof of this is standard, using a parabolic regularization and arguing as in
[
IN98
]
and the continuity of the flow map follows from the well-known Bona-Smith argument as in
[
BS75
]
. It remains to prove the a priori estimates a) and b) in the proposition. We follow the same ideas used in
[
Ken04
]
. First, we have an energy estimate.

Lemma 2
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
4
), with
u_{0}\in H_{-1}^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
for
s_{0}
sufficiently large. Then, for
s\geq 1,
we have

\partial_{t}(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}+\|D_{y}D^{-1}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2})\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2})\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}+\|D_{y}D^{-1}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}).

We define the semigroup operator associated to the equation (
4
) by

W(t)=\int\int e^{i(x\xi+y\mu)}e^{it(\xi^{5}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\xi})}d\xi d\mu.

Fix a function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that it is smooth, supported in
\{|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{5}\}
and equals to
1
for
|\xi|\leq\frac{5}{4}
.
Let
\Phi(\xi)=\eta(|\xi|/2)-\eta(|\xi|)
. For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
let
\Phi_{N}(\xi)=\Phi(\frac{\xi}{N})
and define
P_{N}
by
\widehat{P_{N}u}(\xi,\mu)=\Phi_{N}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi,\mu).

Lemma 3
.

We have

\|W(t)\phi\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}

for
\frac{5}{4q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2},
with
2\leq r<\infty,
and with
C
independent of
T.

Proof.

First we prove for any
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\|W(t)P_{N}\phi\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim N^{\beta(q,r)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}

(5)

By
TT^{*}
-method, (
5
) is equivalent to

\left\lVert\int W(t-s)P_{N}fds\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim N^{2\beta(q,r)}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{r^{\prime}}_{xy}}.

(6)

We define
W(t)P_{N}\phi=G(\cdot,\cdot,t)\star\phi,
where
\star
denotes the convolution with respect to the spatial variables and
G
is defined by

G(x,y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\mu)}e^{it(\xi^{5}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\xi}})\psi(\frac{\xi}{N})d\xi d\mu.

Integrating
\mu,
we get

G(x,y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\xi^{5}}|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{N}(\xi)e^{it(x+\frac{y^{2}}{t})},

hence

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}}|G(x,y,t)|=|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{it\xi^{5}}e^{it\xi}\psi\Big(\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big|

By
[
GPW08
]
, for
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
we have that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|e^{it\xi^{5}}e^{ix\xi}\psi\Big(\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)d\xi\Big|\lesssim|t|^{-\theta}N^{1-5\theta}.

Thus, using
|\xi|\sim N
, we obtain
\|G(\cdot,\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\theta}N^{\frac{3}{2}-5\theta}
and therefore by Young’s inequality,

\|G\star\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\theta}N^{\frac{3}{2}-5\theta}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}_{xy}}.

Also, using the semigroup properties of
W(t)
, we get

\|G\star\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}=\|W(t)P_{N}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}.

Using interpolation,

\|W(t)P_{N}\phi\|_{L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim|t|^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\theta)(1-\frac{2}{r})}N^{(\frac{3}{2}-5\theta)(1-\frac{2}{r})}\|\phi\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{xy}}.

By Hardy-Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W(t-s)P_{N}f\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim\left\lVert\int\|W(t-s)P_{N}f\|_{L^{r}_{xy}}ds\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{T}}

\lesssim\left\lVert\int|t-s|^{(-\frac{1}{2}-\theta)(1-\frac{2}{r})}N^{(\frac{3}{2}-5\theta)(1-\frac{2}{r})}\|f\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{xy}}ds\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{T}}\lesssim N^{(\frac{3}{2}-5\theta)}\|f\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{xy}},

for
\theta=\frac{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}{1-\frac{2}{r}},
which implies
(\frac{3}{2}-5\theta)(1-\frac{2}{r})=8(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{2}{q}.
Since
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],
we need

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\in\Big[0,\frac{1}{q}\Big].

We obtain

\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W(t-s)P_{N}f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\right\rVert\lesssim N^{8(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r})-\frac{2}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{r^{\prime}}_{xy}},

hence

\|W(t)P_{N}f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim N^{4(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{4q}-\frac{1}{r})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}.

Since,
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{5}{4q}+\frac{1}{r}
, we conclude that

\|W(t)P_{N}f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{xy}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}.

∎

from

Lemma 4
.

For all
\varepsilon>0,T>0

We begin by recalling Lemma 3 of

(7)

for

By Hölder’s inequality in

, (

Corollary 3

Let

\lambda\in(0,1),
in particular
L_{1}(u)>L_{1}(u_{\rho}).

Moreover, from Lemma
1
we get that
K(\varphi)=0
for
\varphi\in G_{1}.

∎

We now define an invariant set that will help us prove transverse blow-up. Denote

\mathcal{J}=\{u\in X_{s}:L_{1}(u)<d_{1},I(u)<0,K(u)>0\}.

Lemma 11
.

Let
u_{0}\in X_{s}.
Then if
u(t)\in C([0,T),X_{s})
is the solution of (
1
) with initial data
u_{0}
, with
V(u(t))=V(u_{0})
and
E(u(t))=E(u_{0}),
for
0\leq t<T.
Then, if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{J},
we have
u(t)\in\mathcal{J}
for
0\leq t<T.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{J}.
By the conservation laws, we get that
L_{1}(u(t))=L_{1}(u_{0})<d_{1}.
Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
I(u(t_{0}))>0.
By continuity, we get that there exists
t_{1}
such that
I(u(t_{1}))=0.
By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that
L_{1}(u(t_{1}))\geq d_{1},
contradiction.

Again, by contradiction, suppose that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
K(u(t_{0}))<0.
By continuity, we get that there exists
t_{1}
such that
K(u(t_{1}))=0.
By Lemma
10
, we obtain that
L_{1}(u(t_{1})\geq d_{1},
contradiction.
∎

We now prove the blow-up criterion for a certain set of initial data.

Lemma 12
.

Let
u(t)
be a solution of (
1
), with
p>0,p=\frac{m}{n}
and
m
is even,
n
is odd, in
C([0,T),X_{s})
for
s\geq 5,
having initial data
u_{0}\in X_{s}
with
u_{0}\in\mathcal{J}
and
yu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).
Then there exists a blow-up time
T_{0}<\infty
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{0}^{-}}\|\partial_{y}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\infty.

Proof.

From Lemma
9
and Lemma
11
, we observe that

\frac{1}{8}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J(t)=-K(u(t))+I(u(t))<0.

Denote, for any
\lambda>0,

u_{\lambda}(x,y)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{3}y),
and we notice that

I(u_{\lambda})\rightarrow\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0,\mbox{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0,

and

I(u_{\lambda})\rightarrow I(u)<0,\mbox{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 1.

Therefore there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
such that
I(u_{\mu})=0.

By simple computations, we have

L_{1}(u)-L_{1}(u_{\mu})=\frac{1-\mu^{4}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}+(\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}-\frac{1-\mu^{2p}}{p+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u^{p+2}

\geq\frac{1-\mu^{4}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}+(\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}-\frac{1-\mu^{2p}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u^{p+2}=I(u)-I(u_{\mu})=I(u).

By conservation laws we have that
L_{1}(u(t))=L_{1}(u_{0})
and by Theorem
3.1
since
I(u_{\mu})=0,
it implies that
L_{1}(u_{\mu})\geq d_{1}.
Thus,

0>L_{1}(u_{0})-d_{1}>I(u(t))>\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J(t),

which implies that there exists
T_{0}<\infty
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{0}^{-}}J(t)=0.
By the Weyl-Heisenberg inequality, i.e.

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim J(t)\|\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}},

it yields
\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{0}^{-}}\|\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty.

∎

Corollary 4
.

The equation (
4
) is locally well-posed in
\tilde{E}^{2}=\{u_{0}\in E^{2}:\partial_{y}u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}.
If

, then solution
u(t)
of equation (
4
) with initial data
u_{0}
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

The local-wellposedness follows from adapting the proof of Theorem
1.1
, by noting
the energy estimate

\partial_{t}\|\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\leq p\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}

by applying
\partial_{y}
of (
4
) and then multiplying it by
\partial_{y}u.
Then we replicate the proof of Proposition
5
to conclude the local-wellposedness result.

We observe that
yu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
implies
yu(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all times of existence from Theorem 3.3 in
[
Sau93
]
, with
\|yu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(T,\|yu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\|u_{0}\|_{E^{2}}),
for all

Also,
u_{0}\in\mathcal{J}
implies
u(t)\in\mathcal{J}
for all times of existence from Lemma
11
.

Then we have from Lemma
12
, that there exists
T<\infty
such that

In fact, if

then

which implies that the blow-up is given by the transverse effects of the equation.
∎

4
Global well-posedness for the fifth order modified KP-I equation on a cylinder

In this section, we prove that global-wellposedness result for the equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\partial^{5}_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u+\partial_{x}(u^{3})=0,\\
u(0,x,y)=\phi(x,y)\in Z^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

(10)

where we define the space

Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})=\Big\{g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\|g\|_{Z^{s}}=\left\lVert\Big(1+|\xi|^{2s}+\frac{|n|}{|\xi|}\Big)\hat{g}(\xi,n)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z})}\Big\}.

We begin with Strichartz estimates that sharpens the estimates found in
[
IK07
]
modifying their proofs.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
\phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}).
Then we have

i)

\|W(t)\tilde{Q}_{y}^{3j}Q_{x}^{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{2^{-2j}}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{29j}{20}}\|\tilde{Q}^{3j}_{y}Q_{x}^{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}

(11)

and

ii)

\|W(t)Q_{y}^{3j+k}Q_{x}^{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{2^{-2j-k}}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{29j}{20}-\frac{17k}{20}}\|Q^{3j+k}_{y}Q_{x}^{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}

(12)

for any integers
j\geq 0
and
k\geq 1.

Proof.

We begin with
i)
as in the proof of Theorem 9.3.1 in
[
IK07
]
. Let
a(\xi,n)=\widehat{(\tilde{Q}_{y}^{3j}Q_{x}^{j}\phi)}(\xi,n),
so
a(0,n)=0.
Let
\psi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
denote a smooth even function supported in the interval
[-2,2]
and equal to
1
in the interval
[-1,1]
and
\psi_{1}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
a smooth even function supported in the set
\{|r|\in[\frac{1}{4},4]\}
and equal to
1
in the set
\{|r|\in[\frac{1}{2},2]\}.
Then,

W(t)\tilde{Q}_{y}^{2j}Q_{y}^{j}\phi(x,y)=C\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\xi,n)\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)\psi_{0}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x+ny+F(\xi,n)t)}d\xi

where
F(\xi,n)=\xi^{5}+\frac{n^{2}}{\xi}.

Thus, it suffices to prove that

\|1_{[0,2^{-2j}]}(|t|)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(\xi,n)\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)\psi_{0}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x(t)+ny(t)+F(\xi,n)t)}d\xi\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\leq C2^{-\frac{29j}{20}}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}

for any measurable functions
x:[-2^{-j},2^{-j}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
y:[-2^{-j},2^{-j}]\rightarrow\mathbb{T}.
By duality, this is equivalent to proving that

\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)1_{[0,2^{-j}]}(|t|)\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)\psi_{0}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\Big))e^{i(\xi x(t)+ny(t)+F(\xi,n)t)}dt\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\leq C2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t}},

for any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).
By expanding the
L^{2}
norm in the left-hand side, it suffices to prove that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)g(t^{\prime})K_{j}(t,t^{\prime})dtdt^{\prime}\Big|\leq C2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t}},

where

K_{j}(t,t^{\prime})=1_{[0,2^{-2j}]}(|t|)1_{[0,2^{-2j}]}(|t^{\prime}|)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}})\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i[\xi(x(t)-x(t^{\prime}))+n(y(t)-y(t^{\prime}))+F(\xi,n)(t-t^{\prime})]}d\xi.

For integers
l\geq j
let
K_{j}^{l}(t,t^{\prime})=1_{[2^{-l},2\cdot 2^{-l}]}(|t-t^{\prime}|)K_{j}(t,t^{\prime}).

By using Young’s convolution inequality, it suffices to prove that

|K_{j}^{l}(t,t^{\prime})|\leq C2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}}

for any
l\geq 2j.

To summarize, it suffices to prove that

\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}})\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i[\xi x+ny+F(\xi,n)t]}d\xi\Big|\leq C2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R},y\in[0,2\pi),|t|\in[2^{-l},2\cdot 2^{-l}],l\geq 2j.

Let
A_{j}(\xi,t,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{0}^{2}\big(\frac{n}{2^{3j}}\big)e^{i(ny+\frac{n^{2}t}{\xi})}.
We use the Poisson summation formula

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(n)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{F}(2\pi\nu)

for any
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).
Thus,

A_{j}(\xi,t,y)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\eta(y+2\pi\nu)+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi})}d\eta.

Denote
\gamma(\eta)=\eta(y+2\pi\nu)+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi}
and
\zeta(\eta)=\psi_{0}^{2}\big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\big).

By integration by parts, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\zeta(\eta)e^{i\gamma(\eta)}d\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(i\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\{(i\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}[(i\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\zeta]^{\prime}\}^{\prime}]^{\prime}e^{i\gamma}d\eta

=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(H^{\prime})^{3}\zeta+H(H^{\prime})^{2}\zeta^{\prime}+3H^{2}H^{\prime\prime}\zeta^{\prime}+3H^{2}H^{\prime\prime\prime}\zeta+3H^{2}H^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime\prime}+H^{3}\zeta^{\prime\prime\prime}]e^{i\gamma}d\eta,

where
H=(i\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}.
We have that
\|\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\zeta\|_{L^{1}_{\eta}}\lesssim 2^{3j(1-\alpha)}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}}^{2},
for all integers
\alpha\geq 0.

Since
y\in[0,2\pi),\eta\leq 2^{3j+1},t\leq 2\cdot 2^{-2j}
and
|\xi|\geq 2^{j-1},
then
\|\gamma^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\sim|\nu|
for
|\nu|\geq 100.
Also, observe that
\|\gamma^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\sim 2^{-3j}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}=0.
Therefore, we get that

\|H\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\sim\frac{1}{|\nu|},\|H^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\sim\frac{2^{-3j}}{\nu^{2}},\|H^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\sim\frac{2^{-6j}}{|\nu|^{3}},\|H^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\sim\frac{2^{-9j}}{\nu^{4}}.

It yields that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\eta(y+2\pi\nu)+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi})}d\eta\Big|\lesssim\frac{2^{-6j}}{|\nu|^{3}}.

We conclude that

A_{j}(\xi,t,y)=\sum_{|\nu|\leq 100}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\eta(y+2\pi\nu)+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi})}d\eta+O(2^{-6j}).

By integrating in
\xi,
we get that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}O(2^{-6j})d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{-6j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)d\xi=2^{-5j}\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

hence it is definitely bounded by
2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}}
for
l\geq 2j.

Therefore, it remains to show that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\eta y^{\prime}+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi})}d\eta\Big)d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}},

for any
l\geq 2j,x\in\mathbb{R},y^{\prime}=y+2\pi\nu\in[-1000,1000],|t|\in[2^{-l},2\cdot 2^{-l}].

We use the formula that appeared in
[
IK07
]
, namely

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}^{2}\Big(\frac{\eta}{2^{3j}}\Big)e^{i(\eta y^{\prime}+\frac{\eta^{2}t}{\xi})}d\eta=C\Big|\frac{\xi}{t}\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j}})^{2}\frac{|\xi|}{|t|}}d\mu.

(13)

Hence, it suffices to show that

J=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j}})^{2}\frac{|\xi|}{|t|}}d\mu\Big)d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{\frac{l-j}{2}}2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}},

for any
x\in\mathbb{R},y^{\prime}\in[-1000,1000],|t|\sim 2^{-l}.
From now on, we fix
x,y^{\prime},t
. Define
L(\tilde{\xi})=\int_{2^{j-2}}^{\tilde{\xi}}\psi_{1}^{2}\big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\big)\big|\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}d\xi
and

G(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j}})^{2}\frac{\xi}{|t|}}d\mu,

thus
J\lesssim\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{\prime}(\xi)G(\xi)d\xi\Big|+\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{L^{\prime}(\xi)}G(\xi)d\xi\Big|,
so it suffices to show that

\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{\prime}(\xi)G(\xi)d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{\frac{l-j}{2}}2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}}.

By integration by parts and the definition of
\psi_{1}
, we get that

\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{\prime}(\xi)G(\xi)d\xi=\int_{2^{j-2}}^{2^{j+2}}L^{\prime}(\xi)G(\xi)d\xi=\Big[LG\Big]_{2^{j-2}}^{2^{j+2}}-\int_{2^{j-2}}^{2^{j+2}}L(\xi)G^{\prime}(\xi)d\xi.

(14)

We have that
L(2^{j-2})=0
and
|G(\xi)|\lesssim\|\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}\|_{L^{1}}.
By Van der Corput’s inequality (see
[
Ste93
]
, Chapter 8, Proposition 5), we have that for any
\tilde{\xi}\in[2^{j-2},2^{j+2}],

|L(\tilde{\xi})|=\Big|\int_{2^{j-2}}^{\tilde{\xi}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)\Big|\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{-\frac{l}{5}}(\||x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}).

We also notice that

|G^{\prime}(\xi)|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j}})^{2}\frac{\xi}{t}}\frac{1}{2^{3j}t}\Big(2\mu y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2^{3j}}\Big)d\mu\Big|

\approx 2^{l-3j}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j}})^{2}\frac{\xi}{t}}\Big(2\mu y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2^{3j}}\Big)d\mu\Big|\lesssim 2^{l-3j},

where we used the decay properties of the Schwartz function
\widehat{\psi_{0}^{2}}
and
|y^{\prime}|\leq 1000.

Therefore, we obtain that
\|G^{\prime}(\xi)\|_{L^{1}_{[2^{j-2},2^{j+2}]}}\leq 2^{l-2j}.

Plugging in (
14
) all these estimates, we get

J\lesssim 2^{-\frac{l}{5}}2^{l-2j}\leq 2^{\frac{l-j}{2}}2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}}2^{\frac{2j-l}{10}},

since
l\geq 2j,
which completes the proof of
i)
.

For
ii),
we proceed the same. To conclude, it suffices to prove that

\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}^{2}(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}})\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{n}{2^{3j+k}}\Big)e^{i[\xi x+ny+F(\xi,n)t]}d\xi\Big|\leq C2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}-\frac{17k}{10}}2^{\frac{2j+k-l}{10}}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R},y\in[0,2\pi),|t|\in[2^{-l},2\cdot 2^{-l}],l\geq 2j+k.

We use the Poisson summation and the formula (
13
) to reduce that we need only

\tilde{J}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big)e^{i(\xi x+\xi^{5}t)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi_{1}^{2}}(\mu)e^{-i(y^{\prime}+\frac{\mu}{2^{3j+k}})^{2}\frac{|\xi|}{|t|}}d\mu\Big)d\xi\Big|\lesssim 2^{\frac{l-j}{2}}2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}-\frac{17k}{10}}2^{\frac{2j+k-l}{10}}.

By the same reasoning as above, using Van der Corput’s inequality, we obtain that

\tilde{J}\leq 2^{-\frac{l}{5}}2^{l-2j-k}\leq 2^{\frac{l-j}{2}}2^{l}2^{-\frac{29j}{10}-\frac{17k}{10}}2^{\frac{2j+k-l}{10}},

for any
l\geq 2j+k,
which concludes the proof of
ii).

∎

Proposition 7
.

Assume
u\in C([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})),

f\in C([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})),
and

[\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{5}-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}]u=\partial_{x}f\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\times[-T,T].

Then, for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\begin{split}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{10}-2\epsilon}J_{y}^{\frac{3}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{-\frac{9}{20}+\epsilon}f\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}.\end{split}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u\in C^{1}([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
and
f\in C([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})).
It suffices to prove that if, for
\epsilon>0,

\|\widetilde{Q}^{3j}_{y}Q^{j}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon j}[\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{-\frac{9}{20}+\epsilon}f\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}]

(15)

and

\|Q^{3j+k}_{y}Q^{j}_{x}u\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon(j+k)}[\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{10}-2\epsilon}J_{y}^{\frac{3}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{-\frac{9}{20}+\epsilon}f\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}]

(16)

for any integers
j\geq 0
and
k\geq 1.

For

Corollary 5
.

Assume
u\in C^{1}([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})),

f\in C([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})),
and

[\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{5}-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}]u=\partial_{x}f\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\times[-T,T].

Then, for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\begin{split}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|J_{x}^{\frac{31}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{10}-2\epsilon}J_{y}^{\frac{3}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}f\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}.\end{split}

We state now a commutator estimate for the operators
J_{x}^{s}.

Lemma 13
.

(a)

(
[
KP88
]
, Lemma X1) Let
m\geq 0
and
f,g\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R})
. If
s\geq 1
then

\|J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}(fg)-fJ^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}g\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s}[\|J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial f\|_{L^{\infty}})\|J^{s-1}_{\mathbb{R}}g\|_{L^{2}}].

(b)

(
[
KPV93
]
, Theorem A.12) If
s\in(0,1)
then

\|J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}(fg)-fJ^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}g\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s}\|J^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(c)

Let
m\geq 0
and
f,g\in H^{m}(M)
, where
M
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}
. If
s>0
then

\|J^{s}_{M}(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{s}\|J^{s}_{M}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|J^{s}_{M}g\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We are going to bound
g(T)=\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}.
We state the eenergy estimate:

Lemma 14
.

Suppose
u\in C([-T,T]:Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
satisfies the initial value problem (
10
), with initial data
\phi\in Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. Then we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}Z^{s}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}}\exp(g(T)).

Proof.

We apply to (
10
), the operator
J^{s}_{x}
and then we multiply by
J^{s}_{x}u
. By integration by parts and applying Lemma
13
twice we get

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|J_{x}^{s}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}}&=\int J_{x}^{s}uJ^{s}_{x}(u^{2}\partial_{x}u)=\int J^{s}_{x}u[J^{s}_{x}(u^{2}\partial_{x}u)-u^{2}J^{s}_{x}\partial_{x}u]+\int u^{2}J^{s}_{x}uJ^{s}_{x}\partial_{x}u\\
&\lesssim\|J^{s}_{x}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}}(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}})\lesssim\|J^{s}_{x}u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}}(\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}})\end{split}

therefore, by Grönwall’s inequality, we get that

\|J_{x}^{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|J_{x}^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\mbox{exp}(g(T)).

(23)

Again, if we apply to (
10
) the operator
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}
and then we multiply by
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u
, we obtain integrating by parts,

\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}\partial_{x}(u^{3})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}u\partial_{x}u\leq\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2})

hence, by Grönwall’s inequality, we get

\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\exp(g(T))

which yields that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}Z^{s}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}}\mbox{exp}(g(T)).

Lemma 15
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
10
) with initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
with
\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}}
small enough. Then, for any

there exists
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}})

\|g(T)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}Z^{s}}\leq C(T,\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}}).

From Lemma

, by using AM-GM inequality we obtain that

\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}(u^{3})\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}.

Therefore, for

by using AM-GM inequality we get that

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}Z^{s}}(1+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}).

From Lemma

g(T)\leq C(1+g(T)^{2})\exp(2g(T))\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}}^{2},

and using the smallness of

We start by stating a well-known local-wellposedness result from Iorio and Nunes (see
[
IN98
]

Lemma 16
.

Assume
\phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. Then there is
T=T(\|\phi\|_{H^{5}})>0
and a solution
u\in C([-T,T]:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
of the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\partial^{5}_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u+u^{2}\partial_{x}u=0,\\
u(0,x,y)=\phi(x,y).\end{cases}

We proceed to prove the local well-posedness result.

Theorem 4.2
.

The initial value problem (
10
) is locally well-posed in
Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}),s>\frac{31}{20}.
More precisely, given
u_{0}\in Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}),s>\frac{31}{20}
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}}
small enough, there exists
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}})
and a unique solution
u
to the IVP such that
u\in C([0,T]:Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
,
u,\partial_{x}u\in L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}.
Moreover, the mapping
u_{0}\rightarrow u\in C([0,T]:Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
is continuous. Also, we have conservation of the quantities

M(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}u(t)^{2}dxdy\text{ and }E_{(s)}(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}|D_{x}^{s}u(t)|^{2}+[\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u(t)]^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}u(t)^{4}.

Proof.

Step 1.

Let
u_{0}\in Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
and fixed
u_{0,\epsilon}\in Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H^{\infty}_{-1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
such that
\|u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{H^{s,s}}\rightarrow 0
and
\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{Z^{s}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{Z^{s}}.

We know by Lemma
16
that
u_{0,\epsilon}
gives a unique solution
u_{\epsilon}.
We have by the a priori bound that
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{y}u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}\leq C_{T}
and by the previous result,
\mbox{sup}_{0<t<T}\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{s,s}}\leq C_{T}.

Henceforth,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&=\int(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\partial_{x}(\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{3}}{3}-\frac{u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{3}}{3})\\
&=\int\partial_{x}(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\cdot(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{2}+u_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{3}=\\
\\
&=\int(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})^{2}\partial_{x}[\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{2}+u_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{3}]\\
&\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}).\end{split}

and by Grönwall’s inequality and by Lemma

\|u_{\epsilon}-u_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim_{T}\|u_{0,\epsilon}-u_{0,\epsilon^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}},

hence

hence we can find
u\in C([0,T]:Z^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T]:Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
with

The fact that

Step 2.

of

&+\|J^{s}_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|J^{s+1}_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2}+\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}})\\
&+\|J^{s}_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|J^{s}_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}})\\
&+\|J^{s}_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|J^{s}_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|\partial_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}\|\partial_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}^{2})\end{split}

We are using the following variant of Grönwall’s inequality:

Lemma 17
.

If
\alpha(t),\beta(t)
are two non-negative functions, and
\frac{d}{dt}u(t)\leq u(t)\beta(t)+\alpha(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
then

u(t)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)ds}\Big(u(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)ds\Big).

Therefore, it yields

\begin{split}\|J^{s}_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}&\lesssim\exp(\frac{1}{2}g_{k,k^{\prime}}(T)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g_{k}(T)^{2})\Big[\|J^{s}_{x}v_{k,k^{\prime}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\\
&+\|J^{s+1}_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}})\\
&+\|J^{s}_{x}u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}(g_{k,k^{\prime}}(T)^{2}+g_{k}(T)^{2})\Big].\end{split}

(31)

We apply the linear estimate of Proposition
7
to the difference equation (
28
), then for
\epsilon<s-\frac{31}{20}
we obtain

\begin{split}\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}&\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|J_{x}^{\frac{11}{20}+\epsilon}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|J_{x}^{\frac{1}{10}-2\epsilon}J_{y}^{\frac{3}{20}+\epsilon}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}+\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{2}+u_{k}u_{k^{\prime}}+u_{k^{\prime}}^{2})\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\\
&\lesssim h_{\phi}(k)k^{(-1)-}+(\|J_{x}^{\frac{5}{17}+\epsilon^{\prime}}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}})^{\frac{17}{20}-\epsilon}(\|\partial^{-1}_{x}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}})^{\frac{3}{20}+\epsilon}\\
&+(g_{k}(T)^{2}+g_{k^{\prime}}(T)^{2})h_{\phi}(k)k^{-s}\\
&\lesssim h_{\phi}(k)k^{(-1)-}+(h_{\phi}(k)^{-\frac{1}{s}}k)^{(-1-\frac{1}{16})-}C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})+h_{\phi}(k)k^{-s}\lesssim k^{(-1)-},\end{split}

(32)

where we used interpolation, (
27
), (
30
) and the fact that
s>\frac{31}{20}+\epsilon
. Now plugging the decay rate (
32
) to (
31
) together with (
25
), (
27
) and (
24
), we have

\|J_{x}^{s}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\leq h_{\phi}(k)+k^{0-},

which proves continuity in time of
\|J_{s}^{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}.

We proceed with continuity of
\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}.
First, we notice by applying
\partial_{y}
to the equation (
10
) corresponding to
u_{k}
and then multiply with
\partial_{y}u_{k}
that

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{y}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\leq C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})\|\partial_{y}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}=C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}n^{2}\phi_{k}(\xi,n)^{2}d\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}k^{2}\frac{n^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\phi_{k}(\xi,n)^{2}d\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})k\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\\
&\leq C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})k,\end{split}

(33)

where in the first inequality we used the definition of
\phi_{k}.

We rewrite the equation (
28
) as

\partial_{t}v_{k,k^{\prime}}-\partial_{x}^{5}v_{k,k^{\prime}}-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}v_{k,k^{\prime}}+\partial_{x}(v_{k,k^{\prime}}[3u_{k}^{2}+3u_{k}v_{k,k^{\prime}}+v_{k,k^{\prime}}^{2}])=0

and we apply to it
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}
and then multiply it with
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}},
by integration by parts we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}&\lesssim\|\partial^{-1}_{x}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}[g_{k}(T)^{2}+g_{k^{\prime}}(T)^{2})+g_{k,k^{\prime}}(T)^{2}]\\
&+\|\partial_{y}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}})\end{split}

(34)

From the Grönwall’s inequality of Lemma
17
, we have

\begin{split}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}&\lesssim\exp(\frac{1}{2}g_{k}(T)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g_{k^{\prime}}(T)^{2})(\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\\
&+\|\partial_{y}u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}(\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}})\\
&\lesssim C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})(\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}+k^{0-}),\end{split}

(35)

where in the last inequality we used (
27
) and (
33
). Since

\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}(\phi-\phi_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty,

we get that

\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{xy}}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.
Therefore, the solution
u
belongs to
C([0,T];Z^{s}).

From the anisotropic Sobolev inequality from Proposition 3 of
[
MST04
]
(also see
[
BIN78
]
) and (
26
), we obtain that

\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{4}_{xy}}^{4}\lesssim\|v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{3-\frac{2}{s}}\|J_{x}^{s}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{\frac{2}{s}}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{k,k^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim C(\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})k^{2-3s},

which proves continuity in time of
\|u(t)\|_{L^{4}_{xy}}.
We conclude that

E_{(s)}(u(t))=E_{(s)}(\phi).

Step 3.

We conclude by proving continuity of the flow map.
We assume that
T\in[0,\infty)
and
\phi^{l}\rightarrow\phi
in
Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
as
l\rightarrow\infty.
We are going to prove that
u^{l}\rightarrow u
in
C([0,T]:Z^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
as
l\rightarrow\infty
, where
u^{l}
and
u
are solutions of the the initial value problem (
10
) corresponding to initial data
\phi^{l}
and
\phi.
We also denote for a function
f
the quantity
g_{f}(T)=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{\infty}_{xy}}.

For
k\geq 1,
let as before,
\phi^{l}_{k}=P_{k}\phi^{l}
and
u^{l}_{k}\in C([0,T]:H^{\infty})
the corresponding solutions. Denote by
\omega_{k}=u_{k}-u
. By the same estimates as in (
32
) applied to
\omega_{k}
and
\partial_{x}\omega_{k}
we get

\|u_{k}-u\|_{Z^{s}}\lesssim\mbox{exp}(\frac{1}{2}g_{\omega_{k}}(T)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g_{u_{k}}(T)^{2})(\|\phi_{k}-\phi\|_{Z^{s}}+C(T,\|\phi_{k}\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}})k^{0-}).

Following the same reasoning, we have that

\|u_{k}^{l}-u^{l}\|_{Z^{s}}\lesssim\mbox{exp}(\frac{1}{2}g_{\omega_{k}^{l}}(T)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g_{u_{k}^{l}}(T)^{2})(\|\phi_{k}^{l}-\phi^{l}\|_{Z^{s}}+C(T,\|\phi_{k}^{l}\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi^{l}\|_{Z^{s}})k^{0-}).

Now, denote
\omega_{k}^{l}=u_{k}^{l}-u_{k}.
By the same estimates from applied to
\omega_{k}^{l},
then

\|u_{k}^{l}-u_{k}\|_{Z^{s}}\lesssim\mbox{exp}(\frac{1}{2}g_{\omega_{k}^{l}}(T)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g_{u_{k}^{l}}(T)^{2})(\|\phi_{k}^{l}-\phi_{k}\|_{H^{s,s}}+C(T,\|\phi_{k}^{l}\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi_{k}\|_{Z^{s}})k^{0-}).

The boundedness of
g_{u_{k}}(T),g_{u_{k}^{l}}(T),g_{\omega_{k}}(T)
and
g_{\omega_{k}^{l}}(T)
as in (
27
) concludes that

\begin{split}\|u^{l}-u\|_{Z^{s}}&\leq\|u_{k}-u\|_{Z^{s}}+\|u_{k}^{l}-u_{k}\|_{Z^{s}}+\|u^{l}_{k}-u^{l}\|_{Z^{s}}\\
&\lesssim\|\phi_{k}-\phi\|_{Z^{s}}+\|\phi_{k}^{l}-\phi_{k}\|_{Z^{s}}+\|\phi_{k}^{l}-\phi^{l}\|_{Z^{s}}\\
&+C(T,\|\phi\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi_{k}\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi^{l}\|_{Z^{s}},\|\phi_{k}^{l}\|_{Z^{s}})k^{0-}\end{split}

which, by letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get
\|u^{l}-u\|_{Z^{s}}\lesssim\|\phi^{l}-\phi\|_{Z^{s}}
and proves the continuity of the flow map.

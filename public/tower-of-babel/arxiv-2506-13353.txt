From Graphical Lasso to Atomic Norms: High-Dimensional Pattern Recovery Thanks: For the purpose of Open Access, the authors have applied a CC-BY public copyright licence to any Author Accepted Manuscript (AAM) version arising from this submission.
Piotr Graczyk, Bartosz Kołodziejek, Hideto Nakashima, Maciej Wilczyński

Abstract
. Estimating high-dimensional precision matrices is a fundamental problem in modern statistics, with the graphical lasso and its ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -penalty being a standard approach for recovering sparsity patterns. However, many statistical models, e.g. colored graphical models, exhibit richer structures like symmetry or equality constraints, which the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm cannot adequately capture. This paper addresses the gap by extending the high-dimensional analysis of pattern recovery to a general class of atomic norm penalties, particularly those whose unit balls are polytopes, where patterns correspond to the polytope’s facial structure. We establish theoretical guarantees for recovering the true pattern induced by these general atomic norms in precision matrix estimation. Our framework builds upon and refines the primal-dual witness methodology of Ravikumar et al. (2011). Our analysis provides conditions on the deviation between sample and true covariance matrices for successful pattern recovery, given a novel, generalized irrepresentability condition applicable to any atomic norm. When specialized to the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -penalty, our results offer improved conditions—including weaker deviation requirements and a less restrictive irrepresentability condition—leading to tighter bounds and better asymptotic performance than prior work. The proposed general irrepresentability condition, based on a new thresholding concept, provides a unified perspective on model selection consistency. Numerical examples demonstrate the tightness of the derived theoretical bounds.

1.
Introduction

Estimating high-dimensional precision matrices is a central problem in modern statistics. Graphical models provide a powerful framework for uncovering conditional-independence structures.
Raskutti et al. 2008
;
Ravikumar et al. 2011
made seminal contributions by analyzing the graphical LASSO (GLASSO) estimator
(
Yuan and Lin 2007
;
Banerjee et al. 2008
;
Friedman et al. 2008
)
, focusing on its ability to recover both the support and the sign pattern of the true precision matrix. The GLASSO employs a standard
\ell_{1}
-penalty and is tailored to exploit simple sparsity.

Many statistical models, however—most notably colored graphical models—exhibit richer structure, such as symmetry or equality constraints among precision-matrix entries. Colored Gaussian graphical models, introduced by
Højsgaard and Lauritzen 2008
, combine two complementary forms of parsimony: sparsity induced by conditional independence zeros and symmetry imposed via equality constraints on entries. Recovering these structures requires penalties beyond the
\ell_{1}
-norm.

To capture more elaborate patterns, alternative regularizers have been proposed. The
\ell_{\infty}
-norm, for example, can isolate a cluster of entries with the largest magnitudes
(
Jégou et al. 2012
)
. The SLOPE/OWL norm
(
Negrinho and Martins 2014
;
Bogdan et al. 2015
;
Figueiredo and Nowak 2016
)
offers a more sophisticated approach, interpolating between
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{\infty}
properties. Its induced patterns can include sparsity (support), multiple clusters of equal-valued entries, and hierarchical relationships between these clusters
(
Schneider and Tardivel 2022
;
Bogdan et al. 2022
)
. Consequently, penalties like SLOPE appear well-suited for recovering the patterns inherent in colored graphical models. Despite this potential, theoretical guarantees of pattern recovery under such norms in the context of precision matrix estimation, particularly extending the high-dimensional framework of
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
;
Rothman et al. 2008
;
Lam and Fan 2009
;
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, remain scarce.

A powerful conceptual framework for designing such penalties comes from the theory of atomic norms
(
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
)
, which is based on the idea that many simple models can be expressed as a combination of a few elementary ‘atoms’ from a predefined set. The corresponding atomic norm, whose unit ball is the convex hull of this atomic set, then serves as a natural regularizer. This general approach provides a unified way to convert notions of simplicity into convex penalty functions, leading to convex optimization solutions for recovering structured models.

In this paper, we focus on the subclass of atomic norms whose unit ball is a polytope.
The patterns we aim to recover correspond precisely to the facial structure of the dual of this polytope, or equivalently to the structure of the subdifferential of the norm at the solution
(
Graczyk et al. 2023
)
.

1.1.
Problem Setup

Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})^{\top}
be a zero-mean
p
-dimensional random vector with finite second moments. Define the true covariance matrix and the precision matrix by

\Sigma^{\ast}=\mathbb{E}[XX^{\top}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad K^{\ast}=(\Sigma^{\ast})^{-1}.

Throughout the paper, we assume that
\Sigma^{\ast}
is positive definite.

Let
(X^{(i)})_{i=1}^{n}
be a sequence of i.i.d. copies of
X
, and define the sample covariance matrix as

\hat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X^{(i)}(X^{(i)})^{\top}.

We consider estimators for
K^{\ast}
of the form

\displaystyle\hat{K}=\argmin_{K\in\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)}\left\{\mathrm{tr}\left(\hat{\Sigma}K\right)-\log\det(K)+\lambda\,\mathrm{pen}(K)\right\},

where
\,\mathrm{pen}(K)
is a penalty term, and
\lambda\geq 0
is a regularization parameter.
We assume that

\,\mathrm{pen}(K)=2\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K)\right\|_{\diamond},

where
\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K)\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p-1)/2}
is a vectorization of strictly lower triangular entries of
K
(excluding diagonal) and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
is an atomic norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p(p-1)/2}
.

We explicitly do not penalize the diagonal entries of the precision matrix. The concept of a pattern corresponding to a given atomic norm is formally defined following
(
Graczyk et al. 2023
)
in relation to its subdifferential.

The objective function is the negative log-likelihood of a Gaussian distribution (up to constants). Moreover, one can circumvent the Gaussianity assumption via the Bregman divergence associated with the log-determinant function. Specifically, if
f(K)=-\log\det(K)
, its Bregman divergence is

D_{f}(A\mid\mid B)=-\log\det(A)+\log\det(B)+\mathrm{tr}\left(B^{-1}(A-B)\right).

The expression
\mathrm{tr}\left(\hat{\Sigma}K\right)-\log\det(K)
is equivalent to
D_{f}(K^{\ast}\mid\mid K)+\mathrm{tr}\left((\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})K\right)-\log\det(K^{\ast})+p
, when
K
is invertible. Minimizing this with respect to
K
, with
\Sigma^{\ast}
replaced by
\hat{\Sigma}
, leads to the unpenalized Bregman estimator
(
Ravikumar et al. 2011
;
Zwiernik 2023
)
.

The GLASSO uses this Gaussian negative log-likelihood with an
\ell_{1}
penalty on the off-diagonal elements

\,\mathrm{pen}(K)=2\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K)\|_{1}=\sum_{i\neq j}|K_{ij}|.

This framework, based on minimizing penalized Bregman divergence, makes it applicable to random vectors with only finite second moments, not strictly requiring Gaussianity. The support of the estimator
\hat{K}
defines the estimate of the partial correlation graph. The true partial correlation graph, under Gaussianity, fully describes the conditional independence structure of the random vector. More generally, lack of an edge between vertices
i
and
j
implies that
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
are conditionally uncorrelated given all other variables. If the underlying distribution is elliptical (or more generally, transelliptical), then a much stronger property can be read from zero partial correlation
(
Rossell and Zwiernik 2021
)
.

1.2.
Examples of Atomic Norms

Atomic norms provide a versatile family of regularizers. Notable examples include:

•

\ell_{1}
-norm: induces element-wise sparsity.

•

\ell_{\infty}
-norm: encourages clustering of the largest (in magnitude) coefficients.

•

Group LASSO with
\ell_{1}-\ell_{\infty}
mixed norm:
\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\sum_{g\in G}\|x_{g}\|_{\infty}
: selects or deselects entire predefined groups
Turlach et al. 2005
;
Yuan and Lin 2007
;
Zhao et al. 2009
.

•

Generalized LASSO:
\|Dx\|_{1}
where
D
is a specified matrix
Tibshirani and Taylor 2011
, including:

–

Fused LASSO:
\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|

Tibshirani et al. 2005
or

–

its graph-guided variants
Kim and Xing 2009
:
\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\sum_{i\sim_{G}j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|
: encourages piecewise constant structures (“fusion” of coefficients), see also Fused graphical LASSO
Danaher et al. 2014
;
Yang et al. 2015
.

Such penalties have been explored in the context of colored graphical models by
Gao and Massam 2015
.

•

SLOPE/OWL norms:
Bogdan et al. 2015
;
Figueiredo and Nowak 2016
and its special version OSCAR
Bondell and Reich 2008
:

\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}|x|_{(j)},

where
(|x|_{(j)})_{j}
are sorted absolute values of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
(w_{j})_{j}
are nonincreasing non-negative weights with
w_{1}>0
: can simultaneously encourage sparsity and clustering of coefficients of similar magnitude, leading to hierarchical patterns. A graphical version appears in
Mazza-Anthony et al. 2021
;
Riccobello et al. 2022
.

•

GOLAZO penalty:

\,\mathrm{pen}(K)=\sum_{i\neq j}\max\{L_{i,j}K_{i,j},U_{i,j}K_{i,j}\},

where
L
and
U
are symmetric matrices with entries in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
with the restriction
L_{i,j}\leq 0\leq U_{i,j}
for
i\neq j
and
L_{i,i}=U_{i,i}=0
for all
i
.
\,\mathrm{pen}(K)
is convex and positively homogeneous. It enables sparse estimates of
K
that take into account the signs of
K
, e.g., asymmetric GLASSO
or positive GLASSO

Lauritzen and Zwiernik 2022
.

•

Li et al. 2021
designed the following penalty in the context of model selection within colored graphical models

\,\mathrm{pen}(K)=\lambda_{1}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}}\|K_{i,i}-K_{i^{\prime},i^{\prime}}\|+\lambda_{2}\sum_{(i,j)}\|K_{i,j}\|+\lambda_{3}\sum_{(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}\|K_{i,j}-K_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\|.

See also
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
for more examples of atomic norms.

1.3.
Contribution of the paper

This paper extends the high-dimensional analysis of pattern recovery for precision matrix estimation to a general class of atomic norm penalties. Our main contributions are:

Generalization of pattern recovery guarantees:
We establish theoretical guarantees for the recovery of the true pattern induced by general atomic norms. This significantly generalizes the results of
Raskutti et al. 2008
and
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, which focused
on the
\ell_{1}
penalty. More precisely, for an arbitrary norm
\|\cdot\|
, under the irrepresentability condition and for a given tuning parameter
\lambda
, we determine a threshold
\delta
such that if
\|\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast}\|\leq\delta
, then
\hat{K}
recovers the true pattern.

While our proofs build upon the foundational ideas of
Ravikumar et al. 2011
(like primal-dual witness method), they incorporate key modifications. A notable change is the definition of the function used to control the fixed point of the KKT conditions, leading to tighter asymptotic bounds. Furthermore, our analysis carefully tracks the constants, and our numerical examples demonstrate that the derived theoretical bounds are quite sharp in practice, see Section
4
.
Although we do not state explicit sample size requirements; they can be easily found using general theory, see Section
3.3
.

Our novel approach can be applied to other results based on
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, e.g.,
Waghmare et al. 2023
.

Improved conditions for the
\ell_{1}
-penalty:
When specialized to the
\ell_{1}
penalty, our results offer improvements over
Ravikumar et al. 2011
. Specifically, we establish pattern recovery under weaker conditions on the deviation between the sample and true covariance matrices (
\|\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast}\|
) and a less restrictive irrepresentability condition. We stress that our bounds not only have
tighter numerical constants, but in general yield improved asymptotic behavior. Indeed, we consider an example for which theory of
Ravikumar et al. 2011
requires a bound on deviation
\|\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast}\|
which is asymptotically a factor of
p^{3}
more restrictive than our bound.

Irrepresentability condition for atomic norms:
We introduce an irrepresentability condition applicable to any atomic norm, based on a novel thresholding concept (
\tau_{\diamond}
). This framework provides a unified perspective on model selection consistency conditions and can be readily extended to other settings, such as linear regression.

Structure of the paper

The paper is structured as follows.

Section

introduces essential concepts including the definition of atomic norms and their facial structure used in this work, the precise notion of a pattern in relation to the subdifferential of these norms, pattern subspaces. \tau_{\diamond}

) and

).

and

) that establish pattern recovery guarantees for precision matrix estimators penalized by a broad class of atomic norms. We sketch how these bounds translate into finite‐sample requirements on the sample size via standard covariance‐concentration results in Section

.

, and compare asymptotic rates for a special example.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Atomic norms

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a finite set whose points are called atoms. Assume that
0\in\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{A}))
. The gauge of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\|x\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf\{t>0\colon x\in t\,\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{A})\}.

When
\mathcal{A}
is centrally symmetric about the origin (i.e.,
a\in\mathcal{A}\iff-a\in\mathcal{A}
),
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
is a norm, called the atomic norm.
We note that an atomic norm is a special case of a Minkowski functional.
Following the convention in
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
, we will refer to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
as an atomic norm even when it is a gauge that does not satisfy the symmetry property, and is therefore not strictly a norm. We note that
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
considered a more general case where
\mathcal{A}
was allowed to be a general compact set.
We note that the standard concept of a duality extends naturally to general gauges. In principle, it is clear that the dual to an atomic norm is also an atomic norm.

Since from the perspective of patterns, the dual representation often plays a more direct role, we will further build on concepts developed by
Graczyk et al. 2023
. Note that in
Graczyk et al. 2023
, such functions are generally called polyhedral gauges.
As the set
\mathcal{A}
will be fixed,
throughout the paper we will denote the atomic norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
as
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
.

Summarizing, we will assume that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
is a non-negative, positively homogeneous, convex function that vanishes at
0
, and its unit ball
B=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}\leq 1\}
is a polytope. A convex polytope is a compact set that can be described as the intersection of a finite number of half-spaces, each defined by a linear inequality.
Thus, any atomic norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
can also be expressed in the form

(2.1)

\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\max\{v_{1}^{\top}x,\ldots,v_{K}^{\top}x\}

for some vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that no
v_{i}
is redundant, meaning that for each
i
, there exists some
x
such that
\max_{j\neq i}\{v_{j}^{\top}x\}\neq\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}
. We note that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
is a norm if for every
i
, there exists
j
such that
v_{j}=-v_{i}
.

Example 2.1
.

For
w_{1}\geq w_{2}\geq\ldots\geq w_{m}\geq 0
with
w_{1}>0
, define the SLOPE norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and its dual by

\|x\|_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}|x|_{(i)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|y\|_{w}^{\ast}=\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\left\{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i}|y|_{(k)}}{\sum_{k=1}^{i}w_{k}}\right\},

where
|z|_{(k)}
is the
k
-th largest absolute value of the coordinates of
z
,
k=1,\ldots,m
.

Clearly,
\|\cdot\|_{w}
is an atomic norm. Special cases of the SLOPE norm include the
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{\infty}
-norms.

2.2.
Notion of pattern

We say that two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
have the same pattern with respect to the atomic norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
if

\partial\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\partial\left\|y\right\|_{\diamond}.

In this case, we write
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(x\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(y\right)
. Let
C_{x}
denote the pattern equivalence class of
x
.
The pattern subspace generated by
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
(referred to as the model subspace in
Vaiter et al. 2015
;
Vaiter et al. 2018
) is defined as the linear span of
C_{x}
and is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{x}
.

By definition and the fact that subdifferentials are faces of
B^{\ast}
,
there is a one-to-one correspondence between patterns and faces of
B^{\ast}
.
Let us see this correspondence in more detail.

It is well known (see, e.g.,
(
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
, Exercise 8.31)
) that if a function
f
is given by

f=\max\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{K}\},

where each
f_{i}
is a smooth function, then the subdifferential of
f
at a point
x
is

\partial f(x)=\mathrm{conv}\{\nabla f_{i}(x)\colon i\in\mathrm{I}_{x}\},

where
\mathrm{I}_{x}=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\colon f_{i}(x)=f(x)\}
is called the active set of indices. Applying this to the atomic norm
of the form (
2.1
), we obtain

\partial\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\colon i\in\mathrm{I}_{x}\},

where the active set of indices is

\mathrm{I}_{x}=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\colon v_{i}^{\top}x=\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}\}.

We see that
\partial\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}
is a face of
B^{\ast}
, and write
F_{x}=\partial\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}
.
In particular, we have

B^{\ast}=\partial\left\|0\right\|_{\diamond}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}\}.

Thus, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
its pattern equivalence class
C_{x}
is in one-to-one correspondence with
a face of
B^{\ast}
, and hence with its active set of indices
\mathrm{I}_{x}
.
The set of all patterns is then
\mathcal{I}=\{\mathrm{I}_{x}\colon x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}\subset 2^{K}
.
When
\mathrm{I}=\mathrm{I}_{x}
, we write

F_{x}=F_{\mathrm{I}},\quad C_{x}=C_{\mathrm{I}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{x}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}.

By
(
Graczyk et al. 2023
, Theorem 3.2)
,
C_{\mathrm{I}}
equals the relative interior of the normal cone
N_{F}
of the face
F=F_{\mathrm{I}}

of the dual unit ball
B^{\ast}
.
It is known from convex analysis that the relative interiors of the normal cones of the faces form a partition
{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
associated with the convex polytope
B^{\ast}
:

\mathbb{R}^{m}=\bigcup_{F\mbox{ face of }B^{\ast}}\!\!\mathrm{ri}(N_{F}).

Assigning a pattern to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
corresponds to specifying which cone in the partition
{\mathcal{P}}
the vector
x
belongs to.

For
\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{I}
, the pattern subspace is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{span}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\mathrm{I}_{y}=\mathrm{I}\}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon v_{i}^{\top}y=v_{j}^{\top}y\mbox{ for all }i,j\in\mathrm{I}\}.

In other words,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}
is a linear subspace orthogonal to the vectors spanning the face
F_{\mathrm{I}}
.
If
|\mathrm{I}|=1
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Otherwise,

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}=\ker(H_{\mathrm{I}}^{\top})
,
where, for a fixed
i_{0}\in\mathrm{I}
, the matrix
H_{\mathrm{I}}
is given by

H_{\mathrm{I}}=(v_{i}-v_{i_{0}})_{i\in\mathrm{I}\setminus\{i_{0}\}}.

Thus, the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}
is given by

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}=I_{m}-H_{\mathrm{I}}(H_{\mathrm{I}}^{\top}H_{\mathrm{I}})^{+}H_{\mathrm{I}}^{\top}.

By definition, we have
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\top}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}
.
Notably, for all
i,j\in\mathrm{I}
,

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{i}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{j}.

Since the subdifferential of the atomic norm satisfies
F_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\colon i\in\mathrm{I}\}
, and because
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{i}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{i_{0}}
for a fixed
i_{0}\in\mathrm{I}
, it follows that

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}\,\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\colon i\in\mathrm{I}\}=\{\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{i_{0}}\}.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a point
f_{\mathrm{I}}:=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{i_{0}}
,
i_{0}\in\mathrm{I}
, is the face projection of the face
F_{\mathrm{I}}
.

The condition
f_{\mathrm{I}}\in\mathrm{ri}(F_{\mathrm{I}})
will be crucial for our argument regarding pattern recovery to hold, see Lemma
2.10
.

Note that in general
f_{\mathrm{I}}
may not belong to
F_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\colon i\in\mathrm{I}\}
. The next subsection analyses this issue in detail.

Example 2.3

(
\ell_{1}
-norm)
.

Consider the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We note that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}=\|\cdot\|_{1}
holds for
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}\}=\{-1,1\}^{m}
; in particular
K=2^{m}
.
Then, the dual ball is
B^{\ast}=[-1,1]^{m}
.

The active set
\mathrm{I}_{x}
(after the identification of indices with their corresponding vectors,
i\leftrightarrow v_{i}
), for
x\neq 0
, equals

\mathrm{I}_{x}=\{v\in\{-1,1\}^{m}\colon x_{j}\neq 0\implies v_{j}=\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\}.

Each face of
B^{\ast}
can be written as

F_{x}=\left\{y\in[-1,1]^{m}\colon x_{j}\neq 0\implies y_{j}=\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\right\}.

Further, we have

C_{x}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})=\mathrm{sign}(y_{j})\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\},

where we use the convention that
\mathrm{sign}(0)=0
.
Then, the corresponding pattern subspace is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{x}

\displaystyle=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon x_{j}=0\implies y_{j}=0\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\}.

The orthogonal projection
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}
onto
\mathcal{S}_{x}
is a diagonal matrix with
(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}})_{jj}=1
if
x_{j}\neq 0
and
(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}})_{jj}=0
otherwise.

Finally, it is easy to see that if
\mathrm{I}_{x}=\mathrm{I}
, then we have
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}F_{x}=\{f_{\mathrm{I}}\}
, where the face projection is given by

f_{\mathrm{I}}=(\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})\colon j\in\{1,\ldots,m\})=\mathrm{sign}(x).

Example 2.4

(
\ell_{\infty}
-norm)
.

Consider the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\|x\|_{\infty}=\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}|x_{i}|
.
This is an atomic norm
\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}=\|x\|_{\infty}
with the set of vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}=\{e_{1},-e_{1},\ldots,e_{m},-e_{m}\}
, so
K=2m
, where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

The dual norm is
\|y\|_{\infty}^{*}=\|y\|_{1}
, and the dual unit ball is
B^{*}=\mathrm{conv}\{\pm e_{1},\ldots,\pm e_{m}\}
.

For
x\neq 0
, let
J_{x}=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\colon|x_{j}|=\|x\|_{\infty}\}
be the set of indices where the maximum absolute value is attained.
The active set of vectors is
\mathrm{I}_{x}
corresponding to
\{\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})e_{j}\colon j\in J_{x}\}
.

The active set
\mathrm{I}_{x}
can be characterized using
J_{x}
. Let
M=\|x\|_{\infty}
. An index
k
is in
\mathrm{I}_{x}
if either:

•

k=2j-1
for some
j\in J_{x}
, and
v_{2j-1}^{\top}x=e_{j}^{\top}x=x_{j}=M
. This requires
x_{j}>0
.

•

k=2j
for some
j\in J_{x}
, and
v_{2j}^{\top}x=(-e_{j})^{\top}x=-x_{j}=M
. This requires
x_{j}<0
.

So,
\mathrm{I}_{x}=\{2j-1\mid j\in J_{x},x_{j}=M\}\cup\{2j\mid j\in J_{x},x_{j}=-M\}
.

The subdifferential
\partial\|x\|_{\infty}
, which is a face
F_{x}
of
B^{*}
, is:

F_{x}=\mathrm{conv}\{\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})e_{j}\colon j\in J_{x}\}.

Let
s_{x}
be the vector with
(s_{x})_{j}=\mathrm{sign}(x_{j})
for
j\in J_{x}
and
0
otherwise. We have
|J_{x}|=\|s_{x}\|_{1}
. Let
S^{c}=\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus J_{x}
. The pattern subspace is
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}=\mathrm{span}(\{s_{x}\}\cup\{e_{j}\mid j\in S^{c}\})
.

The orthogonal projection
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}
onto
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}
is given by:

(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}z)_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{s_{x}^{\top}z}{\|s_{x}\|_{1}}\mathrm{sign}(x_{k}),&\mbox{if }k\in J_{x},\\
z_{k},&\mbox{if }k\in S^{c}.\end{cases}

The face projection
f_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}v_{i_{0}}
is
f_{\mathrm{I}_{x}}=s_{x}/\|s_{x}\|_{1}
.

2.3.
Restricted dual norm

Fix non-empty
\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{I}
. For
y\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}
, define the restricted dual gauge as

\left\|y\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}\\
\left\|x\right\|_{\diamond}\leq 1\end{subarray}}x^{\top}y.

The general properties of dual and restricted dual gauges are presented in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
B_{\mathrm{I}}^{\ast}
be the unit ball in
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}
. Then,

B_{\mathrm{I}}^{\ast}:=\{y\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}\colon\left\|y\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}\leq 1\}=\mathrm{conv}\{\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}v_{K}\}.

It is clear that if
\mathrm{I}\neq\emptyset
, then the face projection
f_{\mathrm{I}}
belongs to the boundary of the unit restricted dual norm ball so that

\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}=1
.
For any
y\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}}
, we have
\left\|y\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}\geq\left\|y\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}
, which implies that
\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}\geq 1
and, it is possible that
\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}>1
as we show in the example below.

2.4.

Let

. Let

. Let

with the trace inner product,
\left\langle\,X\,\middle|\,Y\,\right\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\left(XY\right)

•

\mathrm{vec}(X)\in\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
obtained by stacking the columns of
X
into a single column vector,

•

\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathrm{vec_{+}}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))
obtained by stacking only the strict lower triangular entries
(X_{ij})_{i>j}
, excluding diagonal elements.

For
a,b,c\in\mathrm{Mat}(p\times p)
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{vec}(abc)=(c^{\top}\otimes a)\mathrm{vec}(b),

where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product.

Let
\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p)
be the zero-diagonal subspace of
\mathrm{Sym}(p)
. Let
D
be the duplication matrix defined by

D\,\mathrm{vec_{+}}(x)=\mathrm{vec}(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p).

2.5.
First threshold

In proving our main result, we utilize the primal-dual witness method developed in
Wainwright 2009
;
Ravikumar et al. 2011
. We begin by considering a restricted version of the optimization problem in
\mathrm{vec}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))
and constructing a vector
\pi\in\mathrm{vec_{+}}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))=\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}\pi=f_{\mathrm{I}}
; in particular
\left\|\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}}\pi\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}}=1
.

The key step in the method is then to show that
\left\|\pi\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}\leq 1
. To achieve this, we control
\left\|\pi\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}
, by bounding the term
\pi^{\bot}=(I_{m}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}})\pi
.
To do so, we use an arbitrary norm
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
and measure the distance in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
via
[\mkern-1.5mu[D\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
, where

is the duplication matrix defined in the previous subsection.

.

Assume that
\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}=1
. Then,

.

\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I})>0\quad\iff\quad f_{\mathrm{I}}\in\mathrm{ri}(F_{\mathrm{I}}).

implies
\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}=1
.

2.6.
Second threshold

The second threshold specifies a sufficient condition under which an element
x
in the pattern subspace
\mathcal{S}_{y}
shares the same pattern as
y
,
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(x\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(y\right)
.
Concretely, it does so by imposing a bound on the distance between
x
and
y
.

In our setting—where
\mathbb{R}^{m}
denotes the half-vectorization of symmetric matrices—we measure this distance using an arbitrary norm
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}\supset\mathrm{vec}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))
. To streamline notation, we will write

\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(X\right)
in place of
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X)\right)
for any
X\in\mathrm{Sym}(p)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
denote the matrix-pattern subspace associated with the true concentration matrix
K^{\ast}
(see Section
3.1
for the formal definition) and
\Gamma^{\ast}=(K^{\ast})^{-1}\otimes(K^{\ast})^{-1}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Define for
X\in\mathrm{Sym}(p)
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})=\sup\{z>0\colon\forall\,K\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast},\quad[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(K-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq z\implies\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)\},

where
\Gamma^{\ast}=\Sigma^{\ast}\otimes\Sigma^{\ast}
.

We now show that this supremum can be characterized as the distance from
x
to the complement of its pattern-equivalence class in
\mathcal{S}_{x}
:

Lemma 2.12
.

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\\
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K\right)\neq\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)\end{subarray}}[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(K-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu].

Moreover, if
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
,
x^{\ast}=\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K^{\ast})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}=\mathcal{S}_{x^{\ast}}
, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})\geq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\!\!\!\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}\\
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(x\right)\neq\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(x^{\ast}\right)\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\|x-x^{\ast}\|_{\infty}.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Setting and notation

We introduce a polyhedral gauge
\,\mathrm{pen}
on
\mathrm{Sym}(p)
, defined as

\,\mathrm{pen}(X)=2\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X)\right\|_{\diamond},

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}
is an atomic norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathrm{vec_{+}}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))
,
m=p(p-1)/2
.

The dual gauge associated with
\,\mathrm{pen}
on
\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p)
is given by

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{pen}^{\ast}(Y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p)\\
\,\mathrm{pen}(X)\leq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{tr}\left(YX\right)=\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(Y)\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond},\qquad Y\in\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p).

Let
\Sigma^{\ast}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)
and
K^{\ast}=(\Sigma^{\ast})^{-1}
be the true covariance and precision matrices, respectively.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K^{\ast})}
be the pattern subspace of the true concentration matrix and let
\mathrm{I}^{\ast}
denote the corresponding active set so that
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}
. We also define the matrix pattern subspace of
\mathrm{Sym}(p)
by

\mathcal{M}^{\ast}=\{K\in\mathrm{Sym}(p)\colon\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K)\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}\}.

Define the
p^{2}\times p^{2}
matrix

\Gamma^{\ast}=\Sigma^{\ast}\otimes\Sigma^{\ast}

and its block components

\Gamma^{\ast}_{1,1}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma^{\ast}_{2,1}=(I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}})\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}},

where
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\in\mathrm{Sym}(p^{2})
is the orthogonal projection matrix from
\mathrm{vec}(\mathrm{Sym}(p))
onto
\mathrm{vec}(\mathcal{M}^{\ast})
.

We denote
(\Gamma^{\ast}_{1,1})^{+}
as the Moore-Penrose inverse of
\Gamma^{\ast}_{1,1}
and define the matrix

\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}=\Gamma^{\ast}(\Gamma_{1,1}^{\ast})^{+}.

Applying properties of the Moore-Penrose inverse yields

\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}=\Gamma_{2,1}^{\ast}(\Gamma_{1,1}^{\ast})^{+}.

Let
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
be an arbitrary norm (an actual norm)
on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
and let
\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\cdot\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}
denote the operator norm induced by
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
.

Definition 3.1

(Irrepresentability condition)
.

There exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma_{2,1}^{\ast}(\Gamma_{1,1}^{\ast})^{+}Df_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq(1-\alpha)\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I}^{\ast}),

where
f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}
is the face projection of
F_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}
and
D
is the duplication matrix.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
c_{\diamond}
be a constant for which

[\mkern-1.5mu[D\pi]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq c_{\diamond}\left\|\pi\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}^{\ast}},\quad\forall\,\pi\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}.

Additionally, let
\eta
be a constant for which

\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta\otimes I_{p}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}\leq\eta[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\Delta)]\mkern-1.5mu],\quad\forall\,\Delta\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}.

we show that if

is the

-norm, then one can take

.

3.2.
Main Theorems

We consider the following optimization problem:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\hat{K}=\argmin_{K\in\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)}\left\{\mathrm{tr}\left(\hat{\Sigma}K\right)-\log\det(K)+\lambda\,\mathrm{pen}(K)\right\}.

Although we do not explicitly assume
f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\in\mathrm{ri}(F_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}})
, the following result requires that
\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I}^{\ast})>0
, which through Lemma
2.10
implies that
f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\in\mathrm{ri}(F_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}})
. This result is supplemented with the study of the case when
\tau_{\diamond}
does not exists, see Appendix
C
.

The thresholds
\tau
and
\zeta
are defined in Definitions
2.7
and
2.11
, respectively.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that (
3.1
) holds.
Suppose that for some
(r,\lambda)\in(0,1)\times(0,\infty)
, we have
r\leq\eta\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\min\left\{\frac{r}{\eta\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}}-\lambda\,c_{\diamond},\frac{\alpha\,\lambda\,\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I}^{\ast})}{\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}}\right\}-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}.

Let
\hat{K}
be the unique solution to (
3.2
). Then,
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\hat{K}\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)
and

[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{r}{\eta}.

Below we present the optimized (i.e. for
r
and
\lambda
for which
\delta
is as large as possible) version of Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\tau_{\diamond}>0
and that there exists
\alpha>0
such that (
3.1
) holds.

Define

M=\frac{\alpha\tau_{\diamond}}{\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}(\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}c_{\diamond}+\alpha\tau_{\diamond})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad r=\min\left\{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+M}},\eta\,\zeta_{\diamond}\right\}

and

\delta=\begin{cases}\frac{\left(\sqrt{1+M}-1\right)^{2}}{\eta},&\mbox{if }r\leq\eta\zeta_{\diamond}\\
M\zeta_{\diamond}-\frac{\eta\zeta_{\diamond}^{2}}{1-\eta\,\zeta_{\diamond}},&\mbox{if }r>\eta\,\zeta_{\diamond}.\end{cases}

Let
\hat{K}
be the unique solution to (
3.2
) with

\lambda=\frac{r\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}}{\eta\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}(c_{\diamond}\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}+\alpha\tau_{\diamond})}

If

[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]<\delta

then
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\hat{K}\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)
and

[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+M}}\right).

We note that
\delta
defined in Theorem
3.4
is always positive. Moreover, one has the following asymptotic expansions:

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{1+M}-1\right)^{2}=\frac{M^{2}}{4}+O(M^{3})\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+M}}=\frac{M}{2}+O(M^{2}).

We consider three natural choices for the norm
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
.

•

Norm based on a dual norm: for
X\in\mathrm{Mat}(p\times p)
, define

[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(X)]\mkern-1.5mu]=\max\left\{\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X)\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond},\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X^{\top})\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond},\|(X_{i,i})_{i=1}^{p}\|_{\infty}\right\}.

This choice aligns the measure
\tau_{\diamond}
of the perturbation
\pi^{\bot}
using the same norm that defines the feasible region.
Consequently,
\tau_{\diamond}
becomes intrinsically linked to the geometry of the dual ball
B^{\ast}
and becomes a dimensionless quantity.

•

\ell_{\infty}
-norm: for the specific case of GLASSO, the operator norm defined above coincides with the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm. This makes the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm a particularly natural choice, as adopted by
Ravikumar et al. 2011
. To facilitate a direct comparison with GLASSO results when considering other atomic norms, using the
\ell_{\infty}
is often the most convenient approach.

•

The Mahalanobis norm: a weighted
\ell_{2}
norm as defined in Appendix
E
. For this specific norm, we have
\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}=\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}=\eta=1
, which greatly simplifies formulations of our results. However, this norm is generally not compatible with the irrepresentability condition. When formulated using the Mahalanobis norm, condition (
3.1
) tends to be much more restrictive than when expressed using the other two norms.

3.3.
Finite sample theory and concentration results

Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
establish conditions for pattern recovery that depend on a bound of the form

[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]<\delta,

where
\hat{\Sigma}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X^{(i)}(X^{(i)})^{\top}
is the sample covariance matrix based on
n
i.i.d. observations of vector
X
. Since
\delta
is independent of
n
, this condition can be satisfied with arbitrarily high probability by choosing a sufficiently large sample size. That is, for any
q\in(0,1)
, there exists a threshold
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}([\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\delta)\geq q.

The specific value of
n_{0}
and its asymptotic scaling heavily depends on the data distribution, particularly the tail behavior of
X
. For instance, a distinction is often made between sub-Gaussian distributions and those with heavier, polynomial-type tails, each leading to different concentration rates.

When
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
is the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm then the relevant concentration inequalities can be found in
(
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, Section 2.3 and Lemma 8)
. Concentration results for the spectral norm are particularly well-established in the literature, see
Adamczak et al. 2010
,
Vershynin 2012a
,
(
Vershynin 2012b
, Section 5.4.3)
,
Srivastava and Vershynin 2013
.

3.4.
GLASSO and relation to previous results

Our general result builds upon and extends foundational results, particularly Theorems 1 and 2 from
Ravikumar et al. 2011
. To make the comparison with
Ravikumar et al. 2011
more meaningful, we assume a stronger version of irrepresentability condition, see (
3.4
) below.
Recall that the
\ell_{\infty}
operator norm
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\cdot\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}
is the maximum absolute row sum of the matrix.

Let
S=\{(i,j)\colon K^{\ast}_{i,j}\neq 0\}
be the support of the true precision matrix
K^{\ast}
, and write
S^{c}
for its complement. Define
d
as the largest number of non-zero entries in any row of
K^{\ast}
, and set
\Theta_{\min}=\min_{(i,j)\in S}|K_{i,j}^{\ast}|
. Finally, denote by
\Gamma_{S,S}^{\ast}
(resp.
\Gamma_{S^{c},S}^{\ast}
) the submatrix of
\Gamma^{\ast}
obtained by selecting the rows in
S
(resp.
S^{c}
) and the columns in
S
.

Theorem 3.5

(
\ell_{1}
-norm)
.

Assume that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Gamma_{S^{c},S}^{\ast}(\Gamma_{S,S}^{\ast})^{-1}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}\leq 1-\alpha.

Let
\hat{K}
be the unique solution to (
3.2
) with

\lambda=\frac{\min\left\{\tfrac{\alpha}{4},\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min}\right\}(1-\alpha/2)}{\eta}

and define

\delta=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{16\,\eta}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{3}\right),&\mbox{if }\alpha\leq 4\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min},\\
\frac{\alpha}{6}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min},&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\eta=\min\{d\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{2},\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}\}
.
If

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta,

then
\mathrm{sign}(\hat{K})=\mathrm{sign}(K^{\ast})
and

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\alpha\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{2}}{4\,\eta}=\frac{\alpha}{4}\max\left\{\frac{1}{d\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}},\frac{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}^{2}_{\infty}}{\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}}\right\}.

For a clear comparison with our findings, we first restate key results of
Ravikumar et al. 2011
in an equivalent form. In doing so, we also identify and propose a correction for an issue in the formulation and proof of their Theorems 1/2. This correction is related to a small error in the proof of their Theorem 1, which unfortunately results in an incorrect statement.

Recall that
\Gamma^{\ast}=\Sigma^{\ast}\otimes\Sigma^{\ast}
and define the quantities

\displaystyle\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}=\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty},\quad\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}(\Gamma^{\ast}_{S,S})^{-1}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}.

Theorems 1 and 2 in
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, restated.

Assume (
3.4
) and define

(3.5)

\displaystyle\delta_{R}=\left(6\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}\max\{1,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{2}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}\}\right)^{-1}.

Let
\hat{K}
be the unique GLASSO estimator with tuning parameter
\lambda=(8/\alpha)\delta_{R}
. If

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta_{R},

then the following hold:

•

element-wise
\ell_{\infty}
-bound:

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq 2\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}\delta_{R}.

•

pattern recovery guarantees: if
K^{\ast}_{i,j}=0
, then
\hat{K}_{i,j}=0
. If additionally

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\min}>2\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}\delta_{R}.

then
\mathrm{sign}(\hat{K})=\mathrm{sign}(K^{\ast})
.

This formulation connects to the original probabilistic statement in
Ravikumar et al. 2011
by noting that for any
\tau>2
, there exists a sample size
n_{0}
(since
\delta
is independent of
n
) such that for
n\geq n_{0}
, the condition
\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta_{R}
holds with probability at least
1-1/p^{\tau-2}
. If all true non-zero edges satisfy (
3.6
), then full strong pattern recovery is achieved, which gives essentially their Theorem 2.

The derivation of
\delta
in
Ravikumar et al. 2011
involves several lemmas. Our work modifies and generalizes these to our atomic norm framework. However, we first address an issue within their original argument for the
\ell_{1}
-norm.

First we note that the term
\max\{1,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{2}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}\}
in (
3.5
) simplifies to
\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{2}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}
. This is because

\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{2}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{2}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}(\Gamma_{S,S}^{\ast})^{-1}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}\geq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Gamma^{\ast}(\mathrm{P}_{S}\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{P}_{S})^{+}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty},

where
\mathrm{P}_{S}
is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the vectorized model subspace, cf. definition of
\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}
in Section
3.1
. Since
\mathrm{Q}=\Gamma^{\ast}(\mathrm{P}_{S}\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{P}_{S})^{+}
is a projection matrix, its operator norm is at least
1
.
Thus, (
3.5
) can be rewritten as

(3.7)

\displaystyle\delta_{R}=\left(6\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}d\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)\right)^{-1}.

With
\lambda=8\delta_{R}/\alpha
, the authors of
(
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, line 11, page 963)
) state

\|\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))\|_{\infty}\leq\left\{6\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}d\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\delta_{R}\right\}\frac{\alpha\lambda}{8}.

Their objective is to show
\|\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))\|_{\infty}\leq(\alpha\lambda)/8
and then to apply their Lemma 5. However, substituting (
3.7
) into the above inequality only yields

\|\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)\frac{\alpha\lambda}{8}.

Since
\alpha
is typically small, the term
(1+8/\alpha)
can be significant. This means the condition
\|\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))\|_{\infty}\leq(\alpha\lambda)/8
is not met by their argument under (
3.7
). This invalidates the subsequent application of their Lemma 5 and leaves the proof of Theorem 1 incomplete as stated.

To rectify the proof, a different definition of
\delta_{R}
is required. Specifically, to ensure
\|\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))\|_{\infty}\leq(\alpha\lambda)/8
, one may take

(3.8)

\displaystyle\delta_{R}=\left(6\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}d\left(1+\frac{8}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

This corrected definition includes an additional factor of
(1+8/\alpha)
in the denominator compared to (
3.5
) and (
3.7
).

It is worth noting that this discrepancy may affect the results of subsequent works that directly rely on the precise formulation of
(
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, Theorem 1)
, for instance, in
(
Wainwright 2019
, Proposition 11.10)
, where one should have
n>c_{0}(1+8\alpha^{-1})^{4}m^{2}\log d
rather than
n>c_{0}(1+8\alpha^{-1})^{2}m^{2}\log d
. Interestingly,
Waghmare et al. 2023
, which extends the approach of
Ravikumar et al. 2011
to functional graphical LASSO, appears to use a condition consistent with our corrected bound (cf. (6.2) in their Theorem 5), though they do not explicitly report the error in the original paper.

Now we are ready to make a comparison, see also Section
4
for numerical experiments. For simplicity, we will only compare the terms not related to
\Theta_{\min}
. Thus, by Theorem
3.5
, we have

\delta\approx\frac{\alpha^{2}}{16\min\{d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}^{2}_{\infty},\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}\}},

while the leading term of (
3.8
) is

\delta_{R}\approx\frac{1}{384}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}}\approx\delta\cdot\frac{1}{24}\min\left\{\left(\frac{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}}{\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}}\right)^{2},\frac{\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}}{d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}}\right\}

Larger values of
\delta
lead to stronger pattern-recovery guarantees. While our bound already improves on the numerical constant reported by
Ravikumar et al. 2011
, the more consequential advantage is the superior asymptotic behavior of the term

\min\left\{\left(\frac{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}}{\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}}\right)^{2},\frac{\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}}{d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}}\right\}.

This term is upper bounded by
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}
, but in our numerical examples from Section
4
it is always less than
1
. Below, we also consider explicit example for which this term is of order
1/p^{3}
, so that our
\delta
is asymptotically
p^{3}
larger than
\delta_{R}
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
\rho\in(-1/(p-2),1)
and

K_{ij}^{\ast}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }i=j,\\
\rho,&\mbox{if }i\neq j\mbox{ and }\max\{i,j\}<p,\\
0,&\mbox{if }i\neq j\mbox{ and }\max\{i,j\}=p.\end{cases}

Then
d=p-1
and direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=(1+(p-2)\rho)^{2},\qquad\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}=1+(p-2)|\rho|,

\displaystyle\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}=\frac{1-\rho+(p-2)(\rho+|\rho|)}{(1-\rho)(1+(p-2)\rho)},\qquad\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}=p+|\rho|(p-1)(p-2).

Thus, for any
\rho\in(0,1)
(case
\rho=0
is trivial, while
\rho<0
is asymptotically forbidden), we obtain as
p\to\infty
,

\min\left\{\left(\frac{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}}{\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}}\right)^{2},\frac{\|\mathrm{vec}(K^{\ast})\|_{1}}{d\,\kappa_{\Sigma^{\ast}}^{3}\kappa_{\Gamma^{\ast}}^{2}}\right\}\sim\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2\rho}\right)^{3}\frac{1}{p^{3}}

(the first term is of order
p^{-2}
), which eventually implies that for positive
\rho
and large
p
, we have

\delta_{R}\approx\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\right)^{3}\frac{\delta}{192\,p^{3}}.

5.
Roadmap to the Proofs

5.1.
Primal-dual witness method

We consider the original and the restricted problem:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\hat{K}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{K\in\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)}\left\{\mathrm{tr}\left(\hat{\Sigma}K\right)-\log\det(K)+\lambda\,\mathrm{pen}(K)\right\},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{K}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{K\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\cap\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)}\left\{\mathrm{tr}\left(\hat{\Sigma}K\right)-\log\det(K)+\lambda\,\mathrm{pen}(K)\right\}.

Recall that
\,\mathrm{pen}
is an atomic norm on
\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p)

(see Section
2.4
for the definition of
\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
).
We define the corresponding restricted dual norm on
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}_{0}=\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\cap\mathrm{Sym}_{0}(p)
. Let
\,\mathrm{pen}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}=\,\mathrm{pen}\big|_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}
and

\,\mathrm{pen}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}^{\ast}(Y)=\sup\{\left\langle\,Y\,\middle|\,X\,\right\rangle\colon X\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}_{0},\,\mathrm{pen}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(X)\leq 1\},\quad Y\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}_{0}.

It follows that
\,\mathrm{pen}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}^{\ast}(Y)=\left\|\mathrm{vec_{+}}(Y)\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}
with
\mathrm{I}^{\ast}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{vec_{+}}(K^{\ast})}
.

Let
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(p)\to\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
.
Define also the orthogonal projection matrix
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\in\mathrm{Sym}(p^{2})
such that for all
X\in\mathrm{Sym}(p)
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathrm{vec}(\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(X))=\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathrm{vec}(X).

Note that we have
\mathrm{vec_{+}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(X))=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\mathrm{vec_{+}}(X)
(recall Section
2.2
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\hat{\Sigma}
has positive diagonal entries.

(i)

The optimization problem (
5.1
) has a unique solution
\hat{K}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)
, which is characterized by

\hat{K}^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma}=\lambda\hat{\Pi}
,
where
\hat{\Pi}\in\partial\,\mathrm{pen}(\hat{K})
.

(ii)

The constrained optimization problem (
5.2
) has a unique solution

\tilde{K}\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\cap\mathrm{Sym}_{+}(p)
, which is characterized by
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(\tilde{K}^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma})=\lambda\tilde{\Pi}
,
where
\tilde{\Pi}\in\partial\,\mathrm{pen}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(\tilde{K})
.

To establish that the original estimator
\hat{K}
belongs to the true model subspace
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
, it suffices to prove that
\hat{K}=\tilde{K}
. In Lemma
5.1
, we define

\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\hat{K}^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\Pi}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}(\tilde{K}^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma}).

Let additionally

\displaystyle\Pi=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\tilde{K}^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma}).

The next result establishes a condition under which
\Pi=\hat{\Pi}
; this, in turn, implies that
\hat{K}=\tilde{K}
, thereby ensuring that
\hat{K}
belongs to the true pattern subspace
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have
\,\mathrm{pen}^{\ast}(\Pi)\leq 1
if and only if
\hat{K}=\tilde{K}
.

5.2.
The basic bounds

Next, we find a sufficient condition under which we have
\,\mathrm{pen}^{\ast}(\Pi)\leq 1
. This condition will be formulated in terms of a projection
\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}
, recall its definition from Section
3.1
.

For
\Delta\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
such that
K^{\ast}+\Delta
is invertible, define the residual matrix

R(\Delta)=(K^{\ast}+\Delta)^{-1}-\Sigma^{\ast}+\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta\Sigma^{\ast}.

We note that
R(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast})
is the difference of
\nabla(-\log\det(\tilde{K}))=\tilde{K}^{-1}
from its first-order Taylor expansion around
K^{\ast}
.

Recall the definition of
\tau_{\diamond}
in Definition
2.7
. We obtain

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\tilde{K}\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)
.
If there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that (
3.1
) holds
and

[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(R(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}))]\mkern-1.5mu]+[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{\alpha\,\lambda\,\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I}^{\ast})}{\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}},

then
\,\mathrm{pen}^{\ast}(\Pi)\leq 1
.

While the term
[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]
,
is stochastic and cannot be directly controlled, standard concentration inequalities ensure it can be made arbitrarily small with high probability by increasing the sample size
n
. The term
[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(R(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}))]\mkern-1.5mu]
will be handled using the following result.

Lemma 5.4

(Control of the residual term)
.

If
\Delta\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
satisfies

\eta[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\Delta)]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq r<1,\qquad\mbox{then}\qquad[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(R(\Delta))]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}.

5.3.
Fixed-point argument

To obtain control over the difference
\tilde{\Delta}=\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}
, we employ a similar approach as in
Ravikumar et al. 2011
.
In this section,
we define a continuous mapping
F
that admits the unique fixed point

\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}).

Next, we will show that
F
is a self-map on a particular convex set. Consequently, Brouwer’s fixed point theorem guarantees that this fixed point lies within that convex set.

Define the function
F\colon\{\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\Delta)\colon\Delta\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast},\,\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}<1\}\to\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\mathcal{M}^{\ast})
by

F\left(\Gamma^{\ast}\,\mathrm{vec}(\Delta)\right)=\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{vec}((K^{\ast}+\Delta)^{-1}-\tilde{K}^{-1})+\Gamma^{\ast}\,\mathrm{vec}(\Delta).

We have
K^{\ast}+\Delta=K^{\ast}(I_{p}+\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta)
and since
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}<1
, the matrix
(I_{p}+\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta)
is invertible. Hence,
K^{\ast}+\Delta
remains positive definite, ensuring that
F
is well-defined. Here,
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\cdot\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}
can be arbitrary operator norm.

Lemma 5.5
.

\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{\Delta})
is a fixed point of
F
if and only if
\tilde{\Delta}=\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}
.

Lemma 5.6

(Control of the deviation
\tilde{\Delta}
)
.

Assume that
r\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
satisfy

(5.3)

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{r}{\eta\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}}-\lambda c_{\diamond}-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}.

Then,
F
has a unique fixed point
\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast})
,
\tilde{K}-K^{\ast}\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{r}{\eta}.

If additionally,
r\leq\eta\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})
,
then
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\tilde{K}\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)
.

From Lemma
5.6
, we obtain the following immediate result

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume that

x:=\eta([\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]+\lambda\,c_{\diamond})\leq\frac{0.1}{\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}^{2}}
.
Then,
r=\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}x+2\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}^{3}x^{2}
satisfies (
5.3
) and

[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{K}-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]\leq\frac{r}{\eta}\leq 1.2\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}([\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]+\lambda\,c_{\diamond}).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha^{2}(2-\alpha)}{8(4-\alpha)},&\mbox{if }\alpha\leq 4\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min},\\
\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min}-\frac{(\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min})^{2}}{1-\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min}},&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{r}{\eta}-\lambda,\frac{\alpha\lambda}{2-\alpha}\right\}-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}.

Since
r\leq\eta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{-2}\Theta_{\min}\leq\eta\zeta_{\diamond}(K^{\ast})
, by Theorem
3.3
, the condition

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta,

implies that
\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(\hat{K}\right)=\mathrm{patt}_{\diamond}\!\left(K^{\ast}\right)
(i.e.
\mathrm{sign}(\hat{K})=\mathrm{sign}(K^{\ast})
) and

\|\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{r}{\eta}\leq\frac{\alpha}{4\,\eta}.

Finally, the bound

\|\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})\|_{\infty}\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}K^{\ast}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\infty}^{2}\|\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})\|_{\infty}

completes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Dual gauges

In this appendix, we present general results on dual gauges and restricted dual gauges. These results are not new, we present them for completeness. For a comprehensive treatment of these concepts, see, e.g.,
Rockafellar 1970
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space equipped with an inner product
\left\langle\,\cdot\,\middle|\,\cdot\,\right\rangle
and consider a gauge
\|\cdot\|
on
V
; i.e.,
\|\cdot\|\colon V\to[0,\infty)
is positively homogeneous, satisfies the triangle inequality and definiteness (i.e.
\|x\|=0
implies
x=0
). A gauge becomes a norm if additionally
\|-x\|=\|x\|
for
x\in V
. The dual gauge corresponding to a gauge
\|\cdot\|
on
V
is defined as

\|y\|^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle,\qquad y\in V.

This definition relies on the identification of
V
with its dual space via the inner product.

Lemma B.1

(Characterization of the gauge subdifferential)
.

Let
V
be a vector space equipped with an inner product
\left\langle\,\cdot\,\middle|\,\cdot\,\right\rangle
, and let
\|\cdot\|
be a gauge on
V
with dual gauge
\|\cdot\|^{\ast}
. If
x\in V
, then

\displaystyle\pi\in\partial\|x\|\qquad\iff\qquad\|x\|=\left\langle\,\pi\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\pi\|^{\ast}\leq 1.

Remark B.2
.

If
x\neq 0
, then the condition
\|x\|=\left\langle\,\pi\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle
automatically implies
\|\pi\|^{\ast}\geq 1
. Indeed, by definition of the dual gauge,

\|\pi\|^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in V\\
\|y\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,\pi\,\middle|\,y\,\right\rangle\geq\langle\,\pi\,|\,\frac{x}{\|x\|}\,\rangle=1.

Thus, when
x\neq 0
, the characterization of the gauge subdifferential can be equivalently stated as

\displaystyle\pi\in\partial\|x\|\qquad\iff\qquad\|x\|=\left\langle\,\pi\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\pi\|^{\ast}=1.

For a nonzero linear subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
V
, define the restricted dual gauge on
\mathcal{S}
by

\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in S\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle,\qquad y\in\mathcal{S}.

It is clear that

\|y\|^{\ast}\geq\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}
for
y\in\mathcal{S}
,
because the supremum in
\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}
is being taken over a smaller set.

Lemma B.3
.

For
y\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}=\min_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}\|y+\xi\|^{\ast}.

Proof.

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot}
and
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle=\left\langle\,y+\xi\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle\leq\|y+\xi\|^{\ast}\|x\|.

This implies that for any
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot}
,

\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle\leq\|y+\xi\|^{\ast}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|x\|=\|y+\xi\|^{\ast}.

Fix
y\in\mathcal{S}
. For
\xi\in V
define

f(\xi)=\|y+\xi\|^{\ast}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\delta_{\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}(\xi)=\begin{cases}0,&\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot},\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly

\min_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}f(\xi)=\min_{\xi\in V}\left\{f(\xi)+\delta_{\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}(\xi)\right\}.

Because
f
is continuous, convex and coercive on a closed subspace
\mathcal{S}^{\bot}
, the minimum of
f
is attained.
At an optimal point
\xi^{\ast}
we have

0\in\partial f(\xi^{\ast})+\partial\delta_{\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}(\xi^{\ast})
. Therefore, there exist
x^{\ast}\in\partial f(\xi^{\ast})
and
w^{\ast}\in\partial\delta_{\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}(\xi^{\ast})
with
x^{\ast}+w^{\ast}=0
. It is well known that
\partial\delta_{\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}(\xi^{\ast})=\mathcal{S}
, which implies that
x^{\ast}=-w^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
x^{\ast}\in\partial f(\xi^{\ast})=\partial\|y+\xi^{\ast}\|^{\ast}
, by Lemma
B.1
, we obtain that
\|x^{\ast}\|=1
and

\left\langle\,y+\xi^{\ast}\,\middle|\,x^{\ast}\,\right\rangle=\|y+\xi^{\ast}\|^{\ast}
. Thus,

\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle\geq\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x^{\ast}\,\right\rangle=\left\langle\,y+\xi^{\ast}\,\middle|\,x^{\ast}\,\right\rangle=\min_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{\bot}}\|y+\xi\|^{\ast},

which ends the proof.
∎

Let
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\cdot\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}
denote the operator gauge induced by the gauge
\|\cdot\|
, that is, for a linear operator
A
on
V
,

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}A\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|Ax\|.

Also, let
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
be the orthogonal projection from
V
onto
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem B.4
.

We have

\|y\|^{\ast}=\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast},\qquad\forall\,y\in\mathcal{S},

if and only if
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}=1
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}=1
. Then, for any
x\in V
, we have
z=\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}x\in\mathcal{S}
and

\|z\|\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\|x\|=\|x\|.

Then, for
y\in\mathcal{S}
, by definition of
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
,

\|y\|^{\ast}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}y\,\middle|\,x\,\right\rangle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
\|x\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}x\,\right\rangle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|z\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,y\,\middle|\,z\,\right\rangle=\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}.

Since we already know that
\|y\|^{\ast}\geq\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}
, we obtain
\|y\|^{\ast}=\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}
.

Conversely, assume that
\|y\|^{\ast}=\|y\|_{S}^{\ast}
for any
y\in\mathcal{S}
.
We have

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}x\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}x\,\middle|\,y\,\right\rangle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|y\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,x\,\middle|\,y\,\right\rangle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathcal{S}\\
\|y\|^{\ast}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,x\,\middle|\,y\,\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in V\\
\|y\|^{\ast}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\,x\,\middle|\,y\,\right\rangle=\|x\|.

Thus,
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\leq 1
. Since
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
, we obtain
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}^{2}
, i.e.,
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\geq 1
.
∎

Pattern recovery for skewed gauges

In this Appendix we study the problem of pattern recovery in the setting when
\tau_{\diamond}
does not exist. In relation to Example
2.6
we call “skewed”

f_{I}\notin B^{\ast}

\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}>1

in the case when the threshold

\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}>1

\|\cdot\|
on

\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond\mid\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}<\min_{\pi^{\bot}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}^{\bot}\colon\|\pi^{\bot}\|\leq\eta}\left\|f_{\mathrm{I}^{\ast}}+\pi^{\bot}\right\|^{\ast}_{\diamond}.

\displaystyle x:=\eta([\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]+\lambda_{n}c_{\diamond})\leq\frac{r}{\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}}-\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}

Appendix D
Choice of tuning parameters in the SLOPE norm

The dual SLOPE norm is a signed permutahedron, well-studied object. Its geometry depends on the weights
w
of the SLOPE norm,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\diamond}=\|\cdot\|_{w}
. In this Appendix we will present a quantitative approach which leads to improved pattern recovery properties of the SLOPE estimator.

If
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, then
\delta
from Theorem
3.4
depends on
w
only through
c_{\diamond}
and
\tau_{\diamond}
. If
w_{1}=1
, one readily checks

\|\pi\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\left\{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i}|\pi|_{(k)}}{\sum_{k=1}^{i}w_{k}}\right\}=\|\pi\|_{w}^{\ast},

so that we may take
c_{\diamond}=1
. We write
\tau_{w}(\mathrm{I})
for
\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I})
to stress its dependence on the weights
w
. Consequently, maximizing
\delta
in Theorem
3.4
reduces to maximizing
\tau_{\diamond}(\mathrm{I}^{\ast})
provided the true pattern is known. In practice, one rarely knows
\mathrm{I}^{\ast}
exactly, but a domain expert may assume, for instance, some prior knowledge about the pattern, e.g., that there is a single non-null cluster. In such case we are interested in

w^{\ast}=\argmax_{w\colon w_{1}=1}\min_{\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{E}}\tau_{w}(\mathrm{I})

where
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{I}
is the subset of all patterns. Below, we collect several results for this tuning problem, establishing the first quantitative framework for selecting SLOPE weights that provably enhance pattern recovery performance. Note that the weights optimized for estimation or FDR control (e.g.
Bogdan et al. 2015
) will generally differ from those that maximize probability of pattern recovery.

We state the following results without proof.

Lemma D.1

(No prior knowledge)
.

If
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{I}
, then

\min_{\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{I}}\tau_{w}(\mathrm{I})=\min\left\{\min_{i=1,\ldots,m-1}\frac{w_{i}-w_{i+1}}{2},w_{m}\right\}

and
w^{\ast}
is given by

w_{i}^{\ast}=\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}-i}{m-\frac{1}{2}},\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

One has

\min_{\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{I}}\tau_{w^{\ast}}(\mathrm{I})=\frac{1}{2m-1}.

These weights coincide with the OSCAR penalty
Bondell and Reich 2008
: we have

\|x\|_{w^{\ast}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(c(i-1)+1)|x|_{(i)}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x_{j}|+c\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\min\left\{|x_{j}|,|x_{k}|\right\}

with
c=-1/(m-1/2)
.

The SLOPE pattern
\mathrm{I}_{x}\in\mathcal{I}
is conveniently identified with vector
M_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by

(M_{x})_{i}=\mathrm{sign}(x_{i})\mathrm{rank}(|x|)_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

see e.g.
[
Bogdan et al. 2022
, Definition 2.1]
. We write
\tau_{w}(M_{x})=\tau_{w}(\mathrm{I}_{x})
. By the symmetry of the SLOPE norm, one has

\tau_{w}(M)=\tau_{w}(\pm M_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\pm M_{\sigma(m)}).

Let
w_{(i,j)}
denote the average
\frac{1}{j-i+1}\sum_{k=i}^{j}w_{k}
and let
a^{(k)}
denote the vector
(a,\ldots,a)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Below we present the thresholds
\tau_{w}
calculated for all patterns with unique non-zero cluster.

Lemma D.2
.

If
M=(1^{(k)},0^{(m-k)})
, then

\tau_{w}(M)=\min\left\{h(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}),w_{(k+1,m)}\right\},

where

h(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})=\begin{cases}w_{(1,i)}-w_{(1,k)},&\mbox{ if }k=2i,\\
\min\{w_{(1,i)}-w_{(1,k)},w_{(1,k)}-w_{(2+i,k)}\},&\mbox{ if }k=2i+1.\end{cases}

In particular:

•

If
M=1^{(m)}
, then

\tau_{w}(M)=h(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}),

which is maximized at

w^{\ast}=\begin{cases}(1^{(i)},0^{(i)}),&m=2i,\\
(1^{(i)},\frac{1}{2},0^{(i)}),&m=2i+1.\end{cases}

We have
\tau_{w^{\ast}}(M)=\frac{1}{2}
.

•

If
M=(1^{(k)},0^{(m-k)})
for
k=2,\ldots,m-1
, then

\displaystyle w^{\ast}=\begin{cases}(1^{(i)},(1/3)^{(m-i)}),&k=2i,\\
(1^{(i)},2/3,(1/3)^{(m-i-1)}),&k=2i+1.\end{cases}

and
\tau_{w^{\ast}}(M)=\frac{1}{3}
.

•

If
M=(1,0^{(m-1)})
. Then,
\tau_{w}(M)=\min\{w_{1},w_{(2,m)}\}
, which is maximized for

w^{\ast}=1^{(m)}.

In this case we obtain the LASSO penalty with
\tau_{w^{\ast}}(M)=1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}=\{M\colon\|M\|_{\infty}=1\}
, which corresponds to all patterns with a single non-zero cluster. Then, with
m=2^{k}+i
, i.e.
k=\lfloor\log_{2}(m)\rfloor
, we can show that

\max_{w\colon w_{1}=1}\min_{M\in\mathcal{M}}\tau_{w}(M)=\frac{2^{k}}{2^{k}(k+2)+i}\approx\frac{1}{\log_{2}(m)+2}.

If
m=2^{k}
, then this is maximized at

w_{j}^{\ast}=\frac{\log_{2}(m)-\lfloor\log_{2}(j-1)\rfloor}{\log_{2}(m)+2},\qquad j=2,\ldots,m.

We see that incorporating prior information about the pattern can substantially increase the guaranteed recovery threshold.

Appendix E
Mahalanobis norm

There is one particularly interesting choice of
[\mkern-1.5mu[\cdot]\mkern-1.5mu]
.
We note that the matrix
\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}
is the orthogonal projection onto the space spanned by the columns of
\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}
, when the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
is given by

\langle x,y\rangle_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=x^{\top}(\Gamma^{\ast})^{-1}y.

Let
[\mkern-1.5mu[x]\mkern-1.5mu]=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{\Gamma^{\ast}}}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
, be the associated norm, known as the Mahalanobis norm. Thus, one has

\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}\mathrm{Q}_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}=\mathopen{[\mkern-1.5mu[\mkern-1.5mu[}I_{p^{2}}-Q\mathclose{]\mkern-1.5mu]\mkern-1.5mu]}=1.

We have for
X\in\mathrm{Mat}(p\times p)
,

\|\mathrm{vec}(X)\|_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{\top}K^{\ast}XK^{\ast}\right)}=\|(K^{\ast})^{1/2}X(K^{\ast})^{1/2}\|_{F},

where
\|\cdot\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm.
One can show that for a linear operator
A
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{p^{2}}
the induced operator norm is given by

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}A\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\Gamma}=\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Gamma^{-1/2}A\Gamma^{1/2}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2}
, where
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\cdot\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2}
is the spectral norm.
Since the spectral norm is bounded above by the Frobenius norm, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{\ast}\Delta\otimes I_{p}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\Gamma^{\ast}}=\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\Sigma^{1/2}\Delta\Sigma^{1/2}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2}\leq\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Delta\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{F}=\|\Gamma^{\ast}\,\mathrm{vec}(\Delta)\|_{\Gamma^{\ast}},

thus, we have
\eta=1
.

Thus, Theorem
3.4
holds with

M=\frac{\alpha\tau_{\diamond}}{c_{\diamond}+\alpha\tau_{\diamond}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda=\frac{r}{c_{\diamond}+\alpha\tau_{\diamond}}

Moreover, the norms of deviations have particularly nice expressions:

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]

\displaystyle=\|(K^{\ast})^{1/2}(\hat{\Sigma}-\Sigma^{\ast})(K^{\ast})^{1/2}\|_{F},

\displaystyle[\mkern-1.5mu[\Gamma^{\ast}\mathrm{vec}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})]\mkern-1.5mu]

\displaystyle=\|(\Sigma^{\ast})^{1/2}(\hat{K}-K^{\ast})(\Sigma^{\ast})^{1/2}\|_{F}.

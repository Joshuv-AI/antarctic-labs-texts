Moment estimates for the stochastic heat equation on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds
Fabrice Baudoin, Hongyi Chen, Cheng Ouyang

Abstract
We study the effect of curvature on the Parabolic Anderson model by posing it over a Cartan-Hadamard manifold. We first construct a family of noises white in time and colored in space parameterized by a regularity parameter α \alpha , which we use to explore regularity requirements for well-posedness. Then, we show that conditions on the heat kernel imply an exponential in time upper bound for the moments of the solution, and a lower bound for sectional curvature imply a corresponding lower bound. These results hold if the noise is strong enough, where the needed strength of the noise is affected by sectional curvature.

Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to study the effects of curvature on the parabolic Anderson model, a stochastic PDE formally expressed as

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\beta u(t,x)\cdot W(t,x),\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

(1)

The solution of (
1
) is expected to be a random field, a stochastic process
\set{u(t,x)}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},x\in M}
for some space
M
. For us,
M
will be an
n
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold, a simply connected complete Riemannian manifold with non-positive sectional curvature. We note that non-random field solutions have been constructed for various SPDEs on manifolds
[
BDFT23
,
HS23
,
MS23
]
, but study of finer properties remains difficult.
\beta>0
is a parameter known as inverse temperature, which has been known to exhibit interesting behavior
[
CSZ17
,
DG20
,
DGRZ18
,
GHL24
,
Tao22
]
.
\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator, defined by
\Delta f=\text{div}(\text{grad}f)
for smooth functions
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
. Finally,
W
is a centered space-time Gaussian noise formally satisfying

\mathbb{E}[W(t,x)W(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]=\delta(t-t^{\prime})G(x,x^{\prime})

where
\delta
is Dirac’s delta and
G:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is called the correlation function.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and graphs, the solution of (
1
) is the partition function for polymer models
[
ACQ10
,
CY04
,
CSZ20
]
. The corresponding free energy
h=\log u
solves the KPZ equation
[
ACQ10
,
BG97
]
, which also models growth of many fluctuating surfaces if
n=1
. Exploration of these topics in our setting is left for future work.

It is known that if
G(x,x^{\prime})=\delta_{x}(x^{\prime})
, (
1
) is not solvable in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
except when
n=1
. In
[
Dal01
]
, the condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\hat{G}(d\xi)}{1+|\xi|^{2}}<+\infty,

where
\hat{G}
is the Fourier transform of

was given for the well-posedness of (
1
). This is Dalang’s condition, which has been recognized as a regularity requirement for the noise
W
. Motivated by this,
[
BOTW23
]
introduced a family of noises
\set{W_{\alpha}}_{\alpha\geq 0}
, where the regularity parameter
\alpha\geq 0
is induced by making the correlation function
G_{2\alpha}
the kernel of the operator
(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}
on the Heisenberg group
H^{n}
equipped with a sub-Riemannian structure. In Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Dalang’s condition for this family of noises becomes
\alpha>\frac{n-2}{4}
. Since the topological dimension of

is

, one may initially expect Dalang’s condition to be
\alpha>\frac{2n-1}{4}
, but
[
BOTW23
]
showed that the correct condition is in fact
\alpha>\frac{n}{2}
. This is not surprising once one realizes that
\frac{n}{2}=\frac{2(n+1)-2}{4}
, where
2(n+1)
is the Hausdorff dimension of
H^{n}
with the sub-Riemannian structure. Noises of this type were also constructed in
[
BCH+24
,
COV23
]
, and Section 2 of this paper will follow a similar construction, where the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
result
\alpha>\frac{n-2}{4}
is shown to still hold.

We also investigate when the
p
-
th
moment of the solution
u(t,x)
grows exponentially in time. In
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this has been related to the intermittent behavior of the solution, which is the presence of high peaks in the graph of any realization of the solution (see
[
CM94
,
BC95
,
HHNT14
,
Mol91
]
).
[
TV02
]
showed that upper exponential in time bounds for the moments hold for compact manifolds, hinting that intermittency is a local property.
[
CCL23
,
COV23
]
gave upper and lower bounds for the moments assuming rough
u_{0}
in bounded domains and the torus, respectively. The first positive exponential in time upper and lower moment bounds in a non-compact non-Euclidean manifold appears to be
[
BOTW23
]
which as noted prior worked on a sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group. Most recently,
[
BCH+24
]
was able to show that the exponential growth rate of the moments is affected by geometric quantities on very rough domains, such as fractals. In our case,
M
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold with negative curvature and our goal is to determine how the negative curvature environment affects the
p
-
th
moment growth of the solution. For such manifolds the spectrum has a positive bottom, see
[
McK70
]
, one therefore expects that at least for small
\beta
’s the
p
-
th
moment decays exponentially fast. This heuristic is made very precise in our Theorem
14
, where we prove the following: Let
p\geq 2
, then we have for
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)\cap L^{r}(M)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)^{p}]}{t}\leq\frac{p}{2}\left(\Theta_{\alpha}(\sqrt{p-1}\beta)-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{\max(2,r)}K_{1}\right),

(2)

where
-K_{1}
is an upper bound on the sectional curvature of
M
and
\Theta_{\alpha}
is a function which is zero for
\beta
small enough and such that:

(i)

if
\alpha\in\left(\frac{n-2}{4},\frac{n}{4}\right)
,
\Theta_{\alpha}(\beta)\sim_{\beta\to\infty}(C\beta^{2})^{1-2\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}
,

(ii)

if
\alpha=\frac{n}{4}
, then
\Theta_{\alpha}(\beta)\sim_{\beta\to+\infty}\frac{C\beta^{2}}{\ln([C\beta]^2)^{2}}
,

(iii)

if
\alpha>\frac{n}{4}
, then
\Theta_{\alpha}(\beta)\sim_{\beta\to+\infty}C\beta^{2}
.

In particular, for
r=2
and
\beta

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)^{2}]}{t}\leq-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}K_{1}

which is consistent with the McKean sharp estimate
[
McK70
]
which states that the bottom of the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
-\Delta
for
M

is bounded below by
\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}K_{1}
. In our Theorem

, under the additional assumption of a lower bound on the sectional curvature, we remarkably obtain a matching lower bound to (
2
) in the asymptotic regime
\beta\to+\infty
, therefore establishing the sharpness of (
2
) in the regime
\beta\to+\infty
.

This paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we clarify some notation, state a useful elementary result about the heat kernel on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds, and define a family of noises indexed by a regularity parameter
\alpha\geq 0
, which is well defined given some conditions on the heat kernel, which is implied by a negative sectional curvature upper bound on

) for

If

Proof.

See

∎

Theorem 2

Suppose

See
[

]
.

Our main tool for the rest of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let

be a Hadamard manifold.
Suppose

. If

∎

2.2
Fractional Laplacian and Colored Noise

Definition 5
.

Suppose
M
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold satisfying
\sec_{M}\leq-K_{1}<0
.
We define a family of kernels
\{G_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha>0}
by

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha-1}P_{t}(x,y)dt,\quad x,y\in M,x\neq y.

(12)

Thanks to (
3
) the integral (
12
) is indeed convergent for every
\alpha>0
when
x\neq y
. Our situation is therefore quite different from the case of Euclidean space and Sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups, as seen in
[
BOTW23
]
. When
\alpha\leq n/2
the integral (
12
) is divergent for
x=y
. Indeed as will be seen in Lemma
18
, for
\alpha<n/2
,
G_{\alpha}(x,y)
has a singularity of order
1/d(x,y)^{n-2\alpha}
while for
\alpha=n/2
the singularity has order
|\ln d(x,y)|
. When
\alpha>n/2
the integral (
12
) is also convergent for
x=y
.

We note that
G_{\alpha}
is the kernel of the operator
(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}
, i.e. for any smooth and compactly supported
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
,

(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}f(x)=\int_{M}f(y)G_{\alpha}(x,y)dy.

We also note that
\alpha=0
corresponds to Dirac’s delta on the diagonal and for
\alpha=1
the correlation function is the Green’s function for the Laplace equation.

Definition 6

(Sobolev Space
\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)
)
.

Suppose
M
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold satisfying
\sec_{M}\leq-K_{1}<0
. For
\alpha>0
, we define the Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)
as the completion of

with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)}=\int_{M}\int_{M}f(x)g(y)G_{2\alpha}(x,y)dxdy=\int_{M}\left[(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}f\right](x)\left[(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}g\right](x)dx,

(13)

where the kernel
G_{2\alpha}
is the family in Definition
5
.

Definition 7
.

Consider
\alpha>0
and the following Hilbert space of space-time functions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)),

(14)

where
\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)
is the Sobolev space introduced in Definition
6
. On a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbf{P})
we define a centered Gaussian family

, whose covariance is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[W(\varphi)W(\psi)\right]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\ \left\langle\varphi(t,\cdot),\psi(t,\cdot)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}^{-\alpha}(M)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{M^{2}}\varphi(t,x)\psi(t,y)G_{2\alpha}(x,y)dxdydt\,,

for

,

.

2.3
L^{2}
estimate for the fractional Laplacian of the heat kernel

We will use the heat kernel bound to obtain upper estimates for
\norm{-(\Delta)^{-\alpha}P_t(x,\cdot)}_{L^{2}(M)}
which plays a central role in our study.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
M
is a Cartan-Hadamard manifold satisfying
\sec_{M}\leq-K_{1}<0
. Let
\alpha>0
. There exists
C=C_{\alpha,K_{1},n}>0
such that for every
t>0

\sup_{x\in M}\norm{(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}p_t(x,\cdot)}_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\begin{cases}Ce^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}K_{1}t}(1_{[0,1/2K_{1}]}(t)t^{\alpha-\frac{n}{4}}+1_{[1/2K_{1},+\infty)}(t)(1+K_{1}t)^{-3/4})&\alpha<\frac{n}{4}\\
Ce^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}K_{1}t}(1_{[0,1/2K_{1}]}(t)|\ln(t)|+1_{[1/2K_{1},+\infty)}(t)(1+K_{1}t)^{-3/4})&\alpha=\frac{n}{4}\\
Ce^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}K_{1}t}(1_{[0,1/2K_{1}]}(t)+1_{[1/2K_{1},+\infty)}(t)(1+K_{1}t)^{-3/4})&\alpha>\frac{n}{4}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Applying Fubini to change the order of integration, the Minknowski integral inequality, the semigroup property of the heat kernel, and our heat kernel upper bound, we have

\begin{split}\|(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}P_{t}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(M)}&\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}P_{2(s+t)}(x,x)^{1/2}ds\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}h(0,2K_{1}(t+s))^{1/2}ds\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(t+s)^{-n/4}(1+2K_{1}(t+s))^{\frac{n-3}{4}}\exp\left(-K_{1}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}(t+s)\right)ds\\
&=Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}[(2K_{1}(t+s))^{-n}(1+2K_{1}(t+s))^{n-3}]^{\frac{1}{4}}ds,\end{split}

(16)

with
b=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}
and
T=2K_{1}(t+s)
and where the second inequality used (
3
). Notice that
T<1\iff s<\frac{1}{2K_{1}}-t
. Continuing, we have

\displaystyle Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}[(1+T)^{-3}T^{-n}(1+T)^{n}]^{\frac{1}{4}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}\left[(1+T)^{-3}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}T^{j-n}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}[n!(1+T)^{-3}\max(T^{-n},1)]^{\frac{1}{4}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\left(\int_{0}^{\max(\frac{1}{2K_{1}}-t),0)}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}T^{-\frac{n}{4}}ds+\int_{\max(\frac{1}{2K_{1}}-t),0)}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}(1+T)^{-\frac{3}{4}}ds\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ce^{-bK_{1}t}\left(1_{[0,\frac{1}{2K_{1}})}(t)\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}T^{-\frac{n}{4}}ds+1_{[\frac{1}{2K_{1}},+\infty)}(t)\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}(1+T)^{-\frac{3}{4}}ds\right).

For the first integral, we treat the case
\alpha<\frac{n}{4}
; the other cases are similar or easier. Applying the substitution
s=tu
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}T^{-\frac{n}{4}}ds\leq Ct^{\alpha-\frac{n}{4}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{\alpha-1}(1+u)^{-\frac{n}{4}}e^{-bK_{1}tu}du\leq Ct^{\alpha-\frac{n}{4}}.

For the second integral, applying
(1,\infty)
Hölder with
(1+T)^{-\frac{3}{4}}=(1+2K_{1}(t+s))^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq(1+K_{1}t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}(1+T)^{-\frac{3}{4}}ds\leq(1+K_{1}t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}e^{-bK_{1}s}ds\leq C(1+K_{1}t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3

On the Cartan-Hadamard manifold
M
we consider the stochastic heat equation

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\beta u(t,x)\cdot W(t,x),\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

(17)

where
\beta>0
is a parameter and
W
is the fractional noise considered in the previous section. We consider solutions in the mild sense of Itô-Walsh, that is, one rewrites our equation of interest as

u(t,x)=J_{0}(t,x)+\beta I(t,x),

where
J_{0}(t,x)=P_{t}u_{0}(x)=\int_{M}P_{t}(x,y)u(0,y)dy
is the solution to the homogeneous heat equation and

is the Itô-Walsh the stochastic integral (see
[
Wal86
]
for a rigorous definition of this integral) given by

I(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}P_{t-s}(x,y)u(s,y)W(ds,dy).

(19)

A solution to (
18
) is then defined as below.

3.1
Definition of Solution and Chaos Expansion

Definition 9
.

A process

is called a random field solution of (

We follow the proof of Lemma 3.9 of

Step 1: Reduction to an integral recursion.
By symmetry, in order to get the desired estimates, we only need to evaluate the

Hence owing to Proposition

Step 2: A renewal procedure.

and the substitution

\displaystyle I_{1}(\rho)+I_{4}(\rho),

\displaystyle F_{2}(\rho,K):=

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho)+I_{4}(\rho),

\displaystyle F_{3}(\rho,K):=

\displaystyle I_{3}(\rho)+I_{4}(\rho).

. For the derivative,

F_{1}
and
F_{3}
large

in
\rho

F_{2}
asymptotic is slightly harder. We begin with

I_{2}(\rho)\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\rho s}\ln(s)^{2}ds

and we will compute the right hand side integral, since it is not trivial. First, observe that differentiation under the integral sign gives

\derivative[n]{a}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\rho s}s^{a}ds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\rho s}s^{a}\ln(s)^{n}ds.

We see that the integral we are interested in is the above with

which can be shown with the substitution

. It then follows that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\rho s}\ln(s)^{2}ds=\derivative[2]{a}\evaluated{}_{a=0}\frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{\rho^{a+1}}.

Taking the second derivative twice gives

\derivative[2]{a}\evaluated{}_{a=0}\frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{\rho^{a+1}}=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)-2\Gamma^{\prime}(1)\ln(\rho)+\Gamma(1)\ln(\rho)^{2}}{\rho}.

The identities

,

y

G_{\alpha}(x,y)
satisfies the lower bound

G_{\alpha}(x,y)\geq\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)

where the positive function
\overline{G}_{\alpha}
which satisfies
\derivative{\overline{G}_\alpha}{z}<0
and the asymptotics

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)\sim_{z\uparrow+\infty}e^{-z^{2}},

\displaystyle\alpha>0,

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)\sim_{z\downarrow 0}z^{2\alpha-n},

\displaystyle\alpha<\frac{n}{2},

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)\sim_{z\downarrow 0}\absolutevalue{\log z},

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{n}{2},

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)\sim_{z\downarrow 0}1,

\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{n}{2}.

Proof.

Recall that (
4
) holds for
K_{2}>0
if
\sec_{M}\geq-K_{2}>-\infty
. Using this, we have

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(x,y):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha-1}P_{t}(x,y)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(K_{2}t)^{-n/2}(1+K_{2}t+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}K_{2}t}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(K_{2}t)^{-n/2}(1+K_{2}t+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}K_{2}t}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{+\infty}(K_{2})^{1-\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}s^{-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)\frac{ds}{K_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}s^{-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds.

For
\alpha\leq\frac{n}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}s^{-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{j}s^{n-j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}{(2+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1-n/2}e^{-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}}ds=\frac{e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}{(2+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}}^{+\infty}r^{\frac{n}{2}-\alpha-1}e^{-r}dr.

If
\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
, the last integral above is equal to the upper incomplete gamma function (see Definition
26
)
\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-\alpha,\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}\right)
. If instead
\alpha=\frac{n}{2}
, we instead have equality to
-\text{Ei}(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4})
, where Ei is also given in Definition
26
.
For
\alpha>\frac{n}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}s^{-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1-n/2}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(1+s+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom{3}{j}(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{j}s^{3-j}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(3!(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4s}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\frac{e^{-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}}{(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{3/2}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{\alpha-\frac{5}{2}}e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{4}}ds=C\frac{e^{-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}}{(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{3/2}}.

Combined, these give us

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(x,y)\geq\begin{cases}C\frac{e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha)K_{2}^{\alpha}(2+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-\alpha,\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4}\right),&\alpha<\frac{n}{2},\\
C\frac{e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha)K_{2}^{\alpha}(2+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{3/2}}(-\text{Ei}(-\frac{K_{2}z^{2}}{4})),&\alpha=\frac{n}{2},\\
C\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4}-\frac{(n-1)\sqrt{K_{2}}z}{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha)K_{2}^{\alpha}(1+\sqrt{K_{2}}z)^{3/2}},&\alpha>\frac{n}{2}.\end{cases}

(37)

In the above,
C>0
is different for each
\alpha
and depends on
n,K_{2}
.
We define
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(z)
as the right-hand side of (
37
).
\derivative{\overline{G}_\alpha}{z}<0
is easily seen from the exact representation, and noting that both the upper incomplete function in the second argument and
-Ei(-z)
is strictly decreasing.

We now prove the claimed asymptotics.
The
z\uparrow+\infty
asymptotic is apparent for
\alpha>\frac{n}{2}
. For
\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
, it follows from the large
z
behavior of the upper incomplete Gamma function in the second argument. For
\alpha=\frac{n}{2}
, it follows from the large
z
asymptotic of
-\text{Ei}(-z)
.

Observe that in the
\alpha>\frac{n}{2}
case,
\overline{G}_{\alpha}
is bounded as
z\downarrow 0
, giving us the last small
z
asymptotic. For
\alpha<\frac{n}{2}
, the upper incomplete gamma function is always bounded, so the blowup at
0
is of rate
2\alpha-n
. Finally for the
\alpha=\frac{n}{2}
case, the blowup rate at
0
is given by
-\text{Ei}(-z)
, which we recall behaves like
\absolutevalue{\ln(z)}
near 0.
The proof is thus finished.
∎

Theorem 19
.

Fix
\alpha>\frac{n-2}{4},\beta>0
. Let
p\geq 2
be an integer. For any

if
u_{0}
is bounded,
u_{0}\geq\varepsilon>0
in

and

, we have for

\mathbb{E}[\absolutevalue{u(t,x)}^{p}]\geq C\varepsilon^{p}\exp\left\{\max\left[\left(Q(r),C\right)\right]pt\right\}

(38)

where

,

depend on
\alpha,n,K_{2}
and

, and

is the function defined in Lemma

(39)

Proof.

From the Feynman-Kac formula for moments, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\absolutevalue{u(t,x)}^{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}u_{0}(B^{x,j}_{t})\exp\left\{\beta^{2}\sum_{1\leq i,k\leq p,i\neq k}\int_{0}^{t}G_{2\alpha}(B^{x,i}_{s},B^{x,k}_{s})ds\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}u_{0}(B^{x,j}_{t})\exp\left\{\beta^{2}\sum_{1\leq i,k\leq p,i\neq k}\int_{0}^{t}G_{2\alpha}(B^{x,i}_{s},B^{x,k}_{s})ds\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}u_{0}(B^{x,j}_{t})\mathbb{1}_{t<T^{j}_{x,R}}\exp\left\{\beta^{2}\sum_{1\leq i,k\leq p,i\neq k}\int_{0}^{t}G_{2\alpha}(B^{x,i}_{s},B^{x,k}_{s})ds\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{p}\exp{\beta^2 p(p-1)t\overline{G}_{2\alpha}(R)}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{1}_{t<T^{j}_{x,R}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq C\varepsilon^{p}\exp{\beta^2 p(p-1)t\overline{G}_{2\alpha}(R)-\lambda(x,R)pt}.

The second to last line used the fact that
u_{0}
restricted to
B(x,R)
is bounded below by
\varepsilon>0
and applied Lemma
18
to every
G_{2\alpha}
. Replacing the Brownian motions with

is allowed due to the stopping time and the fact that
\derivative{\overline{G}_{2\alpha}}{z}<0
. The last line used the spectral decomposition of the expectation of stopping time with respect to the Dirichlet Laplace-Beltrami operator.

The formula for

emerges after applying Theorem

. The result holding for all

comes from noting that

and thus the proof above can be repeated with

∎

Definition 26

(Some non-elementary functions)
.

For
s>0
,
x>0
,
\Gamma(s,x):=\int_{x}^{+\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt
is the upper incomplete gamma function, and
\gamma(s,x):=\int_{0}^{x}t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt
is the lower incomplete gamma function. For
s>0
they satisfy
\Gamma(s)=\gamma(s,x)+\Gamma(s,x)
, where
\Gamma(s)
is the well-known gamma function with domain
s>0
. The above additive relation tells us that for
s>0
,

\sup_{x>0}\gamma(s,x)=\sup_{x>0}\Gamma(s,x)=\Gamma(s).

If
s=0,
the additive relation fails due to
\gamma(0,x)
being undefined. However, we have
\Gamma(0,x)=-\text{Ei}(-x)
, where
\text{Ei}(z):=-\int_{-z}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt
is the exponential integral function.
The following asymptotic results follow from elementary calculus arguments.

1.

For
s>0
,
\gamma(s,x)\sim_{x\downarrow 0}x^{s}.

2.

For
s\geq 0
,
\Gamma(s,x)\sim_{x\uparrow+\infty}e^{-x}
.

3.

-\text{Ei}(-x)\sim_{x\downarrow 0}-\ln(x)
.

Theorem 27

(Dirichlet Eigenvalue Estimates)
.

Suppose
M
is a
n-
dimensional complete Riemannian manifold satisfying
\sec_{M}\geq-K>-\infty
. Denote by
\lambda(x,R)
the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplace-Beltrami operator on the ball of radius
R
centered at
x
in
M
. Then,

\lambda(x,R)\leq\frac{c}{R^{2}}+C.

Here
c>0
depending on
n
and
C>0
depending on
n,K
bounded are given in
[
Ber23
]
, Theorem 3.1.

Proof.

By
[
Cha84
]
Chapter 3, Theorem 7,

\lambda(x,R)\leq\lambda^{K}(R),

where
\lambda^{K}(R)
denotes the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplace-Beltrami on any ball of radius
R
in
H^{n}_{K}
. By
[
Ber23
]
, Theorem 3.1,

\lambda^{K}(R)\leq\frac{c}{R^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}K}{4}+\left(\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sinh(R\sqrt{-K})/\sqrt{-K}}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right).

By Remark 3.2. of

]

depends on

. This finishes the proof.
∎

Oral complications during the two waves of COVID-19 pandemic in China: a prospective cross-section analysis of 510 cases using questionnaire
Authors: Bojing Qu, Honglin Liu, Wanli Zhu, Minghui Wei, Wen Yin, Qing Liu, Zhenlai Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOral complications such as parageusia, xerostomia, and oral ulcers are frequently observed in patients with COVID-19. The aim of this study was to understand the oral complications and their influencing factors during the two waves of the COVID-19 pandemic in China between December 2022 and May 2023.MethodsA total of 347 patients during the COVID-19 pandemic from December 2022 and January 2023 and 163 patients during the second COVID-19 pandemic were recruited through the Disease Prevention and Control Reporting System. Data on oral complications were collected via researcher-made questionnaires.ResultsDuring both pandemic periods, more than 50% of the subjects developed oral complications. Specifically, the incidence of parageusia, pathological tongue coating, and xerostomia all exceeded 20%, while the incidence of oral ulcers surpassed 7%. Patients with allotriosmia and xerostomia had a significantly higher likelihood of developing other oral complications. Hospitalized patients requiring supplemental oxygen had a significantly higher incidence of oral complications.ConclusionsThe results of our study underline that oral complications during the COVID-19 pandemic are prevalent, and their incidence has not decreased during the subsequent pandemic. In the context of the ongoing global spread of COVID-19, this study lays the foundation for physicians in recognizing and managing oral complications, which will improve the oral health of communities globally.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-05058-z.

Introduction

In November 2019, Coronavirus 2019 disease (COVID-19) broke out in Wuhan, China, rapidly spreading worldwide. The main symptoms of COVID-19 ranged from mild headaches to high fever and severe respiratory problems [1]. China experienced a nationwide COVID-19 outbreak from December 2022 to January 2023, driven by Omicron variants BA.5.2.48, BF.7.14, and BA.5.2.49 [2]. And then, the second nationwide wave of COVID-19 pandemic occurred from April to May 2023, caused mainly by Omicron variant XBB series [3].

Oral cells express receptors for the invasion of COVID-19, and the replication of the virus, along with the body’s immune response, leads to local inflammation. Therefore, oral damage is a common symptom associated with COVID-19 infection [4]. Various studies have reported differing incidence rates due to varied inclusion criteria and viral strains, with common manifestations including parageusia, xerostomia, and oral ulcers [5]. Oral symptoms are common complications of COVID-19 infection, but the incidence and influencing factors of oral mucosal damage during COVID-19 pandemic remain not fully understood. Considering the ongoing global impact of COVID-19 and its emerging variants, it is essential for oral healthcare professionals to understand the incidence of oral complications in COVID-19 patients and the factors influencing these complications. The present study aims to understand the incidence and patterns of new onset oral complications during the two waves of COVID-19 pandemic in China. And a survey was conducted among both non-hospitalized and hospitalized patients through researcher-made questionnaires. The key factors influencing the occurrence of oral complications were explored.

Methods

Sample size evaluation

Before initiating the formal study, we reviewed existing literature to evaluate the incidence rates of oral complications during COVID-19 infection. Moreover, we also recruited 52 patients with COVID-19 infection in December 2022 to statistically assess the occurrence rates of oral symptoms. Based on the existing literature and our preliminary survey results, we calculated the sample sizes according to the incidence rates of the symptoms.

For symptoms with a higher incidence rate, such as parageusia and xerostomia, we assumed an expected incidence rate (p) of 0.25, with an allowable error margin (d) of 0.1 and a significance level (α) of 0.05. Using the sample size calculation formula for a single proportion:

where Z2 is the Z-score corresponding to the significance level (1.96 for α = 0.05), we estimated the minimum required sample size to be 73 patients.

For symptoms with a lower incidence rate, such as oral ulcers and mucosal burning sensation, we assumed an expected incidence rate (p) of 0.05, with an allowable error margin (d) of 0.03 and a significance level (α) of 0.1. Using the same formula and a Z-score of 1.645 for α = 0.1, the calculated sample size was 143 patients.

Therefore, we set the minimum sample size for single pandemic at 143 patients. The number of cases included during two waves met this estimated sample size.

Data collection

This study was primarily conducted in Xi’an, Shaanxi Province, China. Patients were identified through the Disease Prevention and Control Reporting System, and enrollment was carried out with the informed consent of the patients or their legal guardians. For the first COVID-19 pandemic occurring between December 2022 and January 2023, both hospitalized and non-hospitalized patients were included. The inclusion criteria comprised positive nucleic acid or antigen tests for COVID-19 during this period. A total of 347 subjects participated in the survey, comprising 41 hospitalized patients and 306 non-hospitalized patients. Hospitalized patients completed the questionnaire with the assistance of doctors. For non-hospitalized patients, given the quarantine and lockdown measures at the time, we conducted remote online surveys. Researchers used contact information from the COVID-19 infection reporting system to reach out to these patients and assess the oral complications they experienced during the infection, subsequently completing the relevant questionnaires. Hospitalized cases were collected from the 986 Hospital of the Chinese People’s Liberation Army Air Force and five other hospitals. From April to May 2023, 163 non-hospitalized patients who were reinfected with COVID-19 were included. The inclusion criteria included individuals who had been previously infected and had positive nucleic acid or antigen tests for COVID-19 during this period.

Questionnaire

Subjects provided information including age, gender, and COVID-19 vaccination status. They were surveyed for new onset oral complications following COVID-19 infection, including parageusia, oral ulcers, mucosal burning sensation, mucosal roughness sensation, halitosis, xerostomia, gingival bleeding, hyperemia and pathological tongue coating. Allotriosmia was also collected. If the patient experienced pain, the subjective pain score (VAS) scale (0–10 points, with 10 being the most painful) was used to score the pain associated with oral complications. For hospitalized patients due to the COVID-19 pandemic, it was recorded whether they required supplemental oxygen, including nasal cannula oxygen therapy, mask oxygen therapy, and ventilator assistance. An additional file provides the detailed questionnaire (additional file 1).

Data analysis

Basic demographic and clinical information were statistically analyzed, and the overall incidence of oral complications was calculated, with oral complications being defined as the presence of any oral abnormality symptoms in the patients. The reporting rates of individual symptoms were analyzed, and the correlation between allotriosmia, COVID-19 vaccination status, gender, xerostomia, and oral complications was examined. The COVID-19 vaccines mainly comprised inactivated vaccines or adenovirus vector vaccines developed by China National Biotec Group (Beijing Institute) and CanSino Biologics.

Statistical Methods.

Data were analyzed and processed using R programming language (version 4.3.0), and correlation analysis was conducted using the compareGroups package (version 4.8.0). Normally distributed data were described using the mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), and non-normally distributed data were described using the median ± quartiles.

Newly emerging oral complications in non-hospitalized COVID-19 patients

Among non-hospitalized patients, 249 (53.09%) reported experiencing oral complications, with no significant difference between the two waves of COVID-19 pandemic (Table 2). For individual symptoms, the prevalence of parageusia, pathological tongue coating, and xerostomia all exceeded 20% during both waves of COVID-19 pandemic, but the rates of these symptoms did not exhibit a significant disparity between these two pandemics (Table 2). The incidence of oral ulcers exceeded 7% during both waves of COVID-19 pandemic. Compared to the first COVID-19 pandemic, the likelihood of developing halitosis after the second COVID-19 pandemic was significantly lower, and the incidence of parageusia and gingival bleeding also decreased, although not statistically significantly. Additionally, 52 subjects reported oral pain, with an average Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score of 3 out of 10.

Regarding the relationship between COVID-19 vaccination and the occurrence of oral complications, the results suggested that the proportion of oral complications among subjects who received two or fewer doses of the COVID-19 vaccine was 46.2% (54/117), while the proportion among subjects who received three or more doses of the COVID-19 vaccine was 55.4% (195/352), with a higher incidence of parageusia observed in the latter group. COVID-19 infection often leads to allotriosmia. Our results indicated that non-hospitalized patients with allotriosmia had significantly higher proportions of parageusia, xerostomia, gingival bleeding, and mucosal roughness sensation (Table 5). Additionally, among patients with xerostomia, the proportions of parageusia, pathological tongue coating, mucosal burning sensation, and mucosal roughness sensation were all significantly increased (additional file 2).

Discussion

Parageusia was noted and reported by researchers during the original strain of COVID-19 in 2020 [6], and its incidence has varied among different COVID-19 variants. During the prevalence of the original strain, a survey conducted by researchers from China, Germany, and France involving 394 COVID-19 patients [7] indicated that the overall incidence of parageusia was approximately 25%, while the incidence was 43% among a French cohort primarily consisting of severe cases. Saraf et al. [8] reported that during the Omicron variant period, the incidence of parageusia among oral healthcare workers infected with COVID-19 was 17.24%. However, other studies have reported the incidence of parageusia to be as high as 46-88% [9–12]. Our study found that the incidence of parageusia was approximately 30%, which was within the intermediate range of different studies. The variation in the incidence of parageusia may be attributed to the survey methods; studies relying on patient self-reporting tend to show higher incidence rates, while those conducted by physicians report lower rates, possibly due to patients’ broader perceptions of parageusia. Despite the potential biases introduced by different ethnicities, patient inclusion criteria, and parageusia assessment standards, parageusia remains a common symptom during COVID-19 infection. Moreover, our survey results indicate that patients with allotriosmia are approximately 3.3 times more likely to experience parageusia, further confirming their frequent co-occurrence.

Xerostomia is a high-risk factor for COVID-19 infection [13] and a common symptom post-infection [14]. COVID-19 infection can lead to impaired salivary gland function. Previous studies have confirmed that COVID-19 patients may exhibit parotid gland enlargement and reduced saliva secretion [15]. Fantozzi and colleagues summarized the incidence of xerostomia after discharge in hospitalized COVID-19 patients through a questionnaire survey [16], finding that the proportion of xerostomia was 45.9%. Chen from China [17] reported that the proportion of xerostomia in 108 hospitalized COVID-19 patients was 46.3%. We also found that the proportion of xerostomia in hospitalized COVID-19 patients requiring oxygen therapy was 57.1%. Among non-hospitalized patients, Biadsee et al. [18] reported that the incidence of xerostomia was 56.3% in 128 non-hospitalized COVID-19 patients during the early stage of the pandemic. Saraf et al. reported that the incidence of xerostomia after infection with the Omicron variant of COVID-19 was 31.03% [8]. During our survey, which mainly focused on the Omicron variant, the incidence of xerostomia was close to the proportion reported by Saraf et al. [8]. Our study revealed that among COVID-19 patients, individuals with xerostomia symptoms had a substantially higher prevalence of other oral complications. Previous studies have shown that reduced saliva secretion is a high-risk factor for oral ulcer, burning sensation, and mucosal roughness sensation [19]. Therefore, the decline in salivary gland function caused by COVID-19 infection may be an important inducer of other oral complications [20]. Recent experimental studies have demonstrated that the application of artificial saliva substantially alleviates symptoms of xerostomia in COVID-19 patients undergoing mechanical ventilation [20]. This suggests that the use of artificial saliva after infection may reduce the occurrence of oral complications.

Elderly individuals and hospitalized patients requiring oxygen therapy are at high risk for developing oral complications. A recent study found that the incidence of oral complications was significantly higher in patients admitted to the intensive care unit due to COVID-19 infection, and this was positively correlated with poor prognosis [21]. Although the number of hospitalized patients requiring oxygen therapy due to COVID-19 infection included in this survey was relatively small, the vast majority of these patients exhibited oral complications, suggesting that this population is at high risk for developing oral complications during COVID-19 infection.

Vaccination can significantly reduce the incidence of pneumonia caused by COVID-19 [22], but whether vaccination has a preventive effect on oral complications during infection remains unreported. The current study found that the association between oral complications and vaccination was insignificant. However, this study lacked an assessment of symptom severity, and it was not yet possible to analyze the severity of symptoms in relation to vaccination. Additionally, studies have reported that aerosolized COVID-19 vaccines significantly increase specific antibody titers [23], and this vaccination method may offer better protection for local oral defense against COVID-19. The preventive effect on oral complications merits further investigation.

Different strains of the COVID-19 virus can lead to varying symptoms in infected individuals, such as fever and pneumonia. There is a lack of data on the differences in the incidence of oral complications during infections caused by different COVID-19 variants. In this survey, the two waves of COVID-19 pandemic were mainly caused by the Omicron variants BA.5.2, BF.7, and XBB. Chen et al. found that antibodies generated against Omicron variants BA.5 and BF.7 can provide approximately four months of protection against strains such as XBB [24]. The results suggested that the proportion of oral complications during the two waves of COVID-19 pandemic did not differ significantly, indicating that different virus strains may mediate similar local oral immune responses. Regardless of the COVID-19 variant, oral complications are common complications following COVID-19 infection. Given the ongoing pandemic and the emergence of new variants, it is crucial to focus on symptom differences caused by different strains.

The proportion of oral complications in the general population infected with COVID-19 differs from those who actively seek treatment at oral medicine clinics. Favia et al. reported that the most common symptom in 123 patients visiting oral clinics was oral ulcers, accounting for approximately 60% [25]. Kot et al. [26] conducted a meta-analysis of 30 studies and found that most oral lesions in COVID-19 patients were also ulcers. In our survey, the proportion of oral ulcers was about 7%, similar to the 3.44% reported by Saraf et al. [8], suggesting that oral ulcers caused by COVID-19 were a major reason for patients to seek treatment at oral medicine clinics.

This survey has certain limitations. Firstly, the reported oral complications may have some subjectivity, as xerostomia reported by the patients was not confirmed by laboratory tests, which may lead to biased results. Secondly, the number of hospitalized patients was relatively small, and the proportion of elderly and pediatric subjects was low, limiting the data’s representativeness. Additionally, this study did not evaluate microbial infections such as oral candidiasis and herpes virus, which have been reported in COVID-19 patients.

COVID-19 may continue to occur intermittently in certain regions, populations, and periods in the future. Considering the large base of the infected population, the total number of patients with oral complications will still be considerable [27]. For healthcare workers, it is necessary to understand the incidence and related factors of new oral complications during COVID-19 infection. The data obtained from this survey will provide important references for diagnosing and treating patients with COVID-19.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that oral complications are prevalent among patients during COVID-19 pandemic, especially in those with allotriosmia or xerostomia. Moreover, oral complications reappear in patients during subsequent COVID-19 infections, with no significant decrease in incidence. Furthermore, for hospitalized patients requiring supplemental oxygen, the incidence of oral complications during COVID-19 infection will increase. These findings lead to a better understanding of oral complications occurrence in the context of ongoing COVID-19 infections. Dental professionals must be equipped with the knowledge to address these issues effectively as part of comprehensive patient care during the COVID-19 infections.

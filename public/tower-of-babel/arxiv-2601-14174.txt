Wavelet-Packet Content for Positive Operators
Myung-Sin Song, James Tian

Abstract
. We give a simple way to attach “content” to the nodes of a wavelet packet tree when a positive operator is given. At a fixed packet depth, the packet projections split the operator into positive pieces, and this decomposition induces a boundary measure on the packet path space, together with vector-dependent densities that show how energy is distributed across the tree. We then study a sequential extraction procedure and two depth-fixed greedy rules for choosing packet blocks, one based on trace weights and one based on Hilbert-Schmidt weights. The main results are explicit geometric decay estimates for the remainder under these greedy removals. In the Hilbert-Schmidt case we also isolate a coherence quantity that measures how close the operator is to being block-diagonal in the packet partition. We close with a concrete patch-based denoising procedure for images, where packet blocks are selected by these content weights computed from an empirical second-moment operator; the construction ensures that both the approximants and the remainders stay positive at every step.

1.
Introduction

Wavelet packets organize information along a tree. At a fixed depth
one has a finite packet partition of the ambient space into mutually
orthogonal subspaces, and this is the basic idea behind many best-basis
and subband-selection procedures in harmonic analysis and signal processing.
In this paper we use the packet tree as a tool to study positive operators,
with a simple principle: all approximations should remain inside the
positive cone at every step.

Fix a wavelet packet system
\left\{W_{w}:w\in\mathcal{T}\right\}

and the associated packet projections
\left\{P_{w}\right\}_{w\in\mathcal{T}}

satisfying the usual tree relations. Given a positive operator
R\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
the most naive way to attach information to a node
w
is by compression

P_{w}RP_{w}
, or at the scalar level by weights like
\text{tr}\left(P_{w}R\right)

and
\left\langle x,P_{w}Rx\right\rangle
. Our starting point is
that, for an operator-level theory which survives iteration, the natural
object is instead the packet content operator

C_{w}\left(R\right)=R^{1/2}P_{w}R^{1/2}\in B\left(H\right)_{+}.

This choice has two immediate advantages. First, it is positivity-preserving
by construction. Second, the packet content pieces telescope along
the tree, and at each fixed depth
n
they form a positive decomposition

R=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}C_{w}\left(R\right),

so that every partial sum is a positive approximation and the remaining
tail is also positive. This is the structure that sits behind all
of the extraction schemes studied here.

A recurrent theme is that the interest of the content operators is
not in a single trace or a single quadratic form. In particular, in
simple diagonal models (such as the Shannon multiplier example, Section
2.2
)
the numbers
\text{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)
coincide
with the naive compression weights
\text{tr}\left(P_{w}R\right)
.
This is the “scalar trap”: at the level of one statistic, the
square-root conjugation may look invisible. What is genuinely new
is what happens when one uses the packet content operators iteratively
along extraction paths, so that operator-valued interactions between
different packet blocks and different stages become visible.

To organize these scalar shadows of the operator-valued decomposition,
we attach to each positive trace-class
R
a canonical boundary measure
on the packet path space. Theorem
3.1
shows that there is
a unique finite Borel measure
\mu_{R}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}

whose cylinder masses are given by

\mu_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\text{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right),

and that for each
x\in H
there is a nonnegative density
e_{x}\in L^{1}\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\mu_{R}\right)

representing the quadratic data
\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle

as integrals over cylinders. We use this view as a convenient tool
for greedy rules: at a fixed depth, one can read the content weights
as a finite mass distribution over the depth-
n
cylinders, and extraction
becomes a deterministic rule that removes mass from the heaviest available
cylinders.

The first quantitative statement comes once we freeze a depth
n

and only allow packets at that level. In Section
4
we study
a trace-class greedy extraction scheme which, at each step, removes
the depth-
n
content block of maximal trace. Theorem
4.1

gives a geometric decay estimate for the trace of the remainder, with
a rate depending only on the number of packets at depth
n
. Thus,
at fixed depth, repeatedly removing the heaviest content block forces
the remainder to drop at a controlled rate.

In Section
5
we turn to a Hilbert-Schmidt (HS) analogue.
This is where genuinely noncommutative behavior appears. This approach
is sensitive to how packet blocks interact, not merely to their individual
traces. We introduce a depth-
n
coherence parameter
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)

which measures how far a positive HS operator is from being block-diagonal
with respect to the depth-
n
packet partition. The resulting HS-greedy
scheme admits a decay estimate (Theorem
5.3
), and the coherence
parameter provides a natural way to quantify when the HS rule improves
over the trace only picture.

Finally, in Section
6
we include a simple application to
patch-based image denoising which keeps the wavelet packet structure
explicit. One forms an empirical second-moment operator from noisy
patches, computes depth-
n
packet block weights, and denoises by
projecting onto the selected packet blocks. The main reason this fits
naturally with the rest of the paper is that the underlying approximation
mechanism is the same: the packet content decomposition ensures that
both the approximants and the remainders stay positive at every step,
while the boundary measure language from Theorem
3.1
gives
an interpretation of the block selection rule.

2.
Wavelet packet content operators

We start by fixing the packet tree and the projections. The immediate
question is how to define “content” at a node when given a positive
operator
R
. We make the choice in Definition
2.1
because
it is the one that behaves well under truncation and iteration, and
that stability is what drives the rest of the paper.

Let
H=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
, with Fourier basis
e_{k}\left(t\right)=e^{ikt}
,

k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We fix a wavelet packet system on
H
, encoded
by a rooted finite branching tree of closed subspaces
\left\{W_{w}\subset H:w\in\mathcal{T}\right\}
,
with orthogonal projections
P_{w}
, subject to the following:

For each
n\geq 0
, the subspaces
\left\{W_{w}:\left|w\right|=n\right\}

form an orthogonal decomposition of
H
, where

(2.1)

H=\bigoplus_{\left|w\right|=n}W_{w},\qquad I=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}P_{w}.

For each node
w\in\mathcal{T}
, there is a finite set
\mathrm{ch}\left(w\right)\subset\mathcal{T}

with

(2.2)

W_{w}=\bigoplus_{v\in\mathrm{ch}\left(w\right)}W_{v},\qquad P_{w}=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}\left(w\right)}P_{v},

and

P_{v}P_{v^{\prime}}=0\quad(v\neq v^{\prime},\,v,v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{ch}\left(w\right)).

Remark
.

The classical dyadic wavelet packet system is such that every node
has two children, but the setting here allows an arbitrary finite
number of children at each node.

The usual way wavelet packets are used is vector-based. One studies
packet coefficients of a function
f\in H
,

\left\{\left\langle f,\psi_{w,k}\right\rangle\right\},

and performs energy-based selection on subtrees of
\mathcal{T}
.
Our input object is a positive operator

R\in B\left(H\right)_{+},

and the goal is to use the packet tree to build a multiscale decomposition
of the “content” of
R
itself.

Definition 2.1
.

For each
w\in\mathcal{T}
and each
R\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
the packet content operator of
R
at
w
is

C_{w}\left(R\right):=R^{1/2}P_{w}R^{1/2}\in B\left(H\right)_{+}.

These operators are built from the packet tree directly. At each fixed
depth
n
, we have

R=R^{1/2}IR^{1/2}=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}R^{1/2}P_{w}R^{1/2}=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}C_{w}\left(R\right),

and for every node
w\in\mathcal{T}
, the splitting (
2.2
)
gives

(2.3)

C_{w}\left(R\right)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}\left(w\right)}C_{v}\left(R\right).

Thus the family
\left\{C_{w}\left(R\right)\right\}_{w\in\mathcal{T}}

is an operator-valued partition of
R
adapted to the packet filtration.

For each
x\in H
and each node
w
we have

\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle=\left\langle R^{1/2}x,P_{w}R^{1/2}x\right\rangle=\left\|P_{w}R^{1/2}x\right\|^{2},

so the quadratic form of
C_{w}\left(R\right)
at
x
can be interpreted
as the packet energy of the vector
R^{1/2}x
in the subspace
W_{w}
.

2.1.
Sequential content extraction

We now introduce a sequential extraction process on
B\left(H\right)_{+}

given by the packet projections.

Fix an arbitrary sequence of nodes
\left(w_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
in

\mathcal{T}
. Starting from
R^{\left(0\right)}=R\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
we define inductively for
k\geq 1

D_{k}=C_{w_{k}}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)=R^{\left(k-1\right)1/2}P_{w_{k}}R^{\left(k-1\right)1/2},

R^{\left(k\right)}=R^{\left(k-1\right)}-D_{k}=R^{\left(k-1\right)1/2}\left(I-P_{w_{k}}\right)R^{\left(k-1\right)1/2}.

Each
D_{k}
is a positive operator extracted at step
k
, and each

R^{\left(k\right)}
is also positive. The construction is nonlinear:
the operator
D_{k}
depends on the entire history
\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\right)

through
R^{\left(k-1\right)}
, and the order in which nodes are
chosen matters in general.

In particular, we have

0\leq R^{\left(k\right)}\leq R^{\left(k-1\right)}\leq\cdots\leq R^{\left(0\right)}=R

for all
k\geq 1
. Consequently there is always a limiting operator.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
R^{\left(\infty\right)}\in B\left(H\right)_{+}

such that
R^{\left(k\right)}\to R^{\left(\infty\right)}
in the
strong operator topology and
0\leq R^{\left(\infty\right)}\leq R^{\left(k\right)}

for all
k
. Moreover, for every
N\geq 1
one has

(2.4)

R=R^{\left(N\right)}+\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k},

and the series
\sum_{k\geq 1}D_{k}
converges strongly to
R-R^{\left(\infty\right)}
.

Proof.

For each
x\in H
the sequence
\left\langle x,R^{\left(k\right)}x\right\rangle

is nonincreasing and bounded below by
0
, hence convergent. Thus
the quadratic forms associated to
R^{\left(k\right)}
decrease pointwise
to a bounded nonnegative form
q
on
H
. By the standard monotone
convergence theorem for positive operators on a Hilbert space, there
is a unique
R^{\left(\infty\right)}\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
such
that
R^{\left(k\right)}\to R^{\left(\infty\right)}
strongly and

0\leq R^{\left(\infty\right)}\leq R^{\left(k\right)}
for all
k
.
The claimed identity follows by telescoping.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

We call
\left(D_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
a content extraction sequence
for
R
along the node sequence
\left(w_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
, and

R^{\left(\infty\right)}
the limit associated to that sequence.
For each
N
the partial sum
\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k}
is an
N
-term
approximation of
R
, with error
R^{\left(N\right)}
.

A central question in the remainder of the paper is how the choice
of nodes
w_{k}
affects the behavior of
\left\|R^{\left(N\right)}\right\|

(in particular the Hilbert-Schmidt norm) and the structure of the
limit
R^{\left(\infty\right)}
.

2.2.
Shannon packets and the scalar case

We recall the simplest concrete packet model and illustrate a basic
degeneracy. We work with the standard Shannon wavelet packet system
on
H=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
, indexed by a dyadic tree; here
each node has two children.

Fix a large integer
N\geq 1
. For each depth
n\geq 0
and each dyadic
word
w\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
, let
m\left(w\right)
be the
integer represented by
w
in base
2
, and set

(2.5)

I_{w}:=\left\{-2^{N-1}+m\left(w\right)2^{N-n},\dots,-2^{N-1}+\left(m\left(w\right)+1\right)2^{N-n}-1\right\}\subset\mathbb{Z}.

At fixed depth
n
, the sets
\left\{I_{w}:\left|w\right|=n\right\}

form a partition of
\mathbb{Z}
into contiguous dyadic frequency
blocks, and each parent interval
I_{w}
splits as the disjoint union

I_{w}=I_{w0}\cup I_{w1}
. The corresponding packet subspace
W_{w}

is the closed linear span of
\left\{e_{k}:k\in I_{w}\right\}
,
and the orthogonal projection
P_{w}
acts diagonally in the Fourier
basis:

(2.6)

P_{w}e_{k}=\begin{cases}e_{k},&k\in I_{w},\\[2.5pt]
0,&k\notin I_{w},\end{cases}

so
P_{w}
is the Fourier projection onto the dyadic band
I_{w}
.

We consider convolution operators on
H
, i.e., nonnegative Fourier
multipliers:

Re_{k}=r\left(k\right)e_{k},\qquad r\left(k\right)\geq 0,

with
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
(trace class). In this model
R
and
every
P_{w}
commute, so the packet content operators simplify.

Lemma 2.4
.

In the Shannon model, for every positive trace-class
multiplier
R
as above and every node
w
, one has

\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)=\sum_{k\in I_{w}}r\left(k\right).

Proof.

Both
R
and
P_{w}
are diagonal in the Fourier basis by (
2.6
),
and therefore commute. We may write

C_{w}\left(R\right)=R^{1/2}P_{w}R^{1/2}=P_{w}R.

Using the Fourier basis to compute the trace gives

\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle e_{k},P_{w}Re_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k\in I_{w}}\left\langle e_{k},Re_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k\in I_{w}}r\left(k\right),

as claimed.
∎

Thus in this model, the packet content operators recover the exact
dyadic splitting of the symbol
r
of
R
over the tree. In particular,

\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(P_{w}R\right)

for all
w
.

Note that at the scalar trace level, the square-root conjugation
R^{1/2}P_{w}R^{1/2}

is invisible in the Shannon multiplier model. It gives exactly the
same numbers as naive compression. This “scalar trap” shows that
the interest of packet content operators lies not in a single trace
or quadratic form, but in their
iterated
use along extraction
paths and in the operator-valued interaction between different scales
and branches.

3.
Content measures

In this section we package the packet content operators into a measure
on the boundary of the packet tree. At a fixed depth, the numbers

\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)
already give weights
on the depth-
n
packet blocks, but the point is that these weights
are consistent across depths because the content pieces telescope.
Theorem
3.1
formalizes this; it gives a canonical Borel measure

\mu_{R}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}
with the right cylinder masses,
as well as the associated vector-dependent densities that represent

\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle
by integration
over cylinders. This is the language we will use later when we talk
about greedy choices as “pick the heaviest cylinder at depth
n
.”

We keep the setting and notation of Section
2
. In particular,

H=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,
\left\{W_{w}:w\in\mathcal{T}\right\}

is a wavelet packet system with packet projections
\left\{P_{w}\right\}_{w\in\mathcal{T}}

satisfying (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), and for each
R\in B(H)_{+}

and
w\in\mathcal{T}
the packet content operator
C_{w}(R)\in B(H)_{+}

is defined by Definition
2.1
. Thus for each depth
n
we have

R=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}C_{w}(R)\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{w}(R)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}C_{v}(R),

cf. (
2.3
).

We write
\partial\mathcal{T}
for the set of infinite paths in
\mathcal{T}
,
and for each finite word
w\in\mathcal{T}
we denote by
\left[w\right]\subset\partial\mathcal{T}

the corresponding cylinder set

\left[w\right]=\left\{\omega\in\partial\mathcal{T}:\omega\text{ begins with }w\right\}.

Recall that for
0<q<\infty
, the Schatten class
\mathcal{S}_{q}

consists of compact operators
A
whose singular values
\{s_{j}\left(A\right)\}

satisfy
\sum_{j}s_{j}\left(A\right)^{q}<\infty
; when
A\geq 0
this
is equivalent to
\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{q}\right)<\infty
, and we write

\left\|A\right\|^{q}_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}:=\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{q}\right)
.
Thus
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is the trace class, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}

the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}

be a positive trace-class operator, and let
\left\{C_{w}\left(R\right)\right\}_{w\in\mathcal{T}}

be its packet content operators.

(1)

There exists a unique finite Borel measure
\mu_{R}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}

such that

\mu_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)

for every
w\in\mathcal{T}
. In particular
\mu_{R}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right)
.

(2)

For each
x\in H
there exists a function
e_{x}\in L^{1}\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\mu_{R}\right)

with
e_{x}\geq 0

\mu_{R}
-almost everywhere such that

\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle=\int_{\left[w\right]}e_{x}\left(\omega\right)d\mu_{R}\left(\omega\right)

for every node
w\in\mathcal{T}
. Also,

\left\langle x,Rx\right\rangle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{T}}e_{x}\left(\omega\right)d\mu_{R}\left(\omega\right).

(3)

Moreover the assignment
x\mapsto e_{x}
is quadratic in the following
sense: for all
x,y\in H
one has

e_{x+y}+e_{x-y}=2\left(e_{x}+e_{y}\right)\qquad\text{in }L^{1}\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\mu_{R}\right).

Proof.

We first construct the boundary measure
\mu_{R}
. For each node

w\in\mathcal{T}
define

\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right).

At the root we have
C_{\emptyset}\left(R\right)=R
and hence
\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)=\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[\emptyset\right]\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right)<\infty
.
The splitting identity
C_{w}\left(R\right)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}C_{v}\left(R\right)

and linearity of the trace give

\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{v}\left(R\right)\right)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[v\right]\right)

for every
w
. Thus the cylinder weights
\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)

are consistent under refinement along children.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the collection of all cylinder sets
\left[w\right]
,
and let
\Sigma
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{C}
.
Any set
A
which is a finite union of pairwise disjoint cylinders
has a well-defined weight

\mu^{0}_{R}\left(A\right)=\sum_{\left[w\right]\subset A}\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right),

and by consistency this does not depend on the particular choice of
level at which
A
is written. In this way
\mu^{0}_{R}
becomes
a finitely additive nonnegative set function on the algebra generated
by
\mathcal{C}
, with total mass
\mu^{0}_{R}(\partial\mathcal{T})=\mathrm{tr}(R)
.

By the standard Kolmogorov/Carathéodory extension there is a unique
finite Borel measure
\mu_{R}
on
\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\Sigma\right)

which extends
\mu^{0}_{R}
. This gives the first part of the statement.

We next fix
x\in H
and construct the corresponding energy measure
and density. For each node
w\in\mathcal{T}
put

\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle.

Positivity of
C_{w}\left(R\right)
implies
\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)\geq 0

for every
w
, and at the root

\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[\emptyset\right]\right)=\left\langle x,C_{\emptyset}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle=\left\langle x,Rx\right\rangle<\infty.

Using again
C_{w}(R)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}C_{v}(R)
, we obtain

\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\left\langle x,C_{w}(R)x\right\rangle=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}\left\langle x,C_{v}(R)x\right\rangle=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{ch}(w)}\nu^{0}_{x}(\left[v\right])

for every
w\in\mathcal{T}
. Thus the cylinder weights
\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)

are also consistent. Extending by finite additivity as before gives
a finitely additive set function
\nu^{0}_{x}
on the same algebra
as
\mu^{0}_{R}
, and this extends uniquely to a finite Borel measure

\nu_{x}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}
with total mass

\nu_{x}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)=\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[\emptyset\right]\right)=\left\langle x,Rx\right\rangle.

We claim that
\nu_{x}
is absolutely continuous with respect to

\mu_{R}
. It is enough to check this on cylinders. Suppose
\mu^{0}_{R}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=0

for some node
w
. Then
\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)=0
.
Since
C_{w}\left(R\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
C_{w}\left(R\right)\geq 0
,
the only way for the trace to vanish is that
C_{w}\left(R\right)\equiv 0
.
Consequently

\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle=0

for every
x
. By finite additivity the same implication holds for
any finite union of cylinders, and by passage to the limit it holds
for all sets in the
\sigma
-algebra generated by cylinders. In other
words
\nu^{0}_{x}\ll\mu^{0}_{R}
on the cylinder algebra, and therefore

\nu_{x}\ll\mu_{R}
on
\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\Sigma\right)
.

Then there exists a unique Radon-Nikodym derivative
e_{x}\in L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{T},\mu_{R})
,
with
e_{x}\geq 0

\mu_{R}
-a.e., such that

\nu_{x}\left(A\right)=\int_{A}e_{x}\left(\omega\right)d\mu_{R}\left(\omega\right)

for every Borel set
A\subset\partial\mathcal{T}
. In particular,
for each cylinder
\left[w\right]
we have

\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle=\nu_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\int_{\left[w\right]}e_{x}\left(\omega\right)d\mu_{R}\left(\omega\right),

and taking
A=\partial\mathcal{T}
gives

\left\langle x,Rx\right\rangle=\nu_{x}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{T}}e_{x}\left(\omega\right)d\mu_{R}\left(\omega\right).

It remains to verify the quadratic property. Let
x,y\in H
. For
each node
w
, linearity and selfadjointness of
C_{w}(R)
give
the familiar parallelogram identity

\left\langle x+y,C_{w}\left(R\right)\left(x+y\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle x-y,C_{w}\left(R\right)\left(x-y\right)\right\rangle\\
=2\left(\left\langle x,C_{w}\left(R\right)x\right\rangle+\left\langle y,C_{w}\left(R\right)y\right\rangle\right).

In terms of the cylinder weights of the measures
\nu^{0}_{z}
this
says

\nu^{0}_{x+y}\left(\left[w\right]\right)+\nu^{0}_{x-y}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=2\left(\nu^{0}_{x}\left(\left[w\right]\right)+\nu^{0}_{y}\left(\left[w\right]\right)\right)

for every
w\in\mathcal{T}
. By finite additivity this identity holds
for all sets in the cylinder algebra, and hence for all Borel sets
in
\partial\mathcal{T}
after passing to the measure extensions.
Thus

\nu_{x+y}\left(A\right)+\nu_{x-y}\left(A\right)=2\left(\nu_{x}\left(A\right)+\nu_{y}\left(A\right)\right)

for every Borel
A\subset\partial\mathcal{T}
.

Now write everything in terms of the Radon-Nikodym densities:

\int_{A}\left(e_{x+y}+e_{x-y}-2e_{x}-2e_{y}\right)d\mu_{R}=0

for every Borel set
A
. Since
\mu_{R}
is finite and
A
is arbitrary,
this implies

e_{x+y}+e_{x-y}=2\left(e_{x}+e_{y}\right)

in
L^{1}\left(\partial\mathcal{T},\mu_{R}\right)
, as claimed.
∎

Remark
.

Formally, the family
\{C_{w}(R)\}
may be viewed as the values of
a positive operator-valued measure on the packet boundary, obtained
by compressing a projection-valued measure
E
by
R^{1/2}
. We
will not develop this here.

Corollary 3.2
.

In the setting of the Shannon example (Lemma
2.4
),
the boundary measure
\mu_{R}
from Theorem
3.1
is given
on cylinders by

\mu_{R}(\left[w\right])=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}(R)\right)=\sum_{k\in I_{w}}r(k),

for every node
w\in\mathcal{T}
, and

\mu_{R}(\partial\mathcal{T})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(k)=\mathrm{tr}(R).

Proof.

This is immediate by construction and definitions.
∎

4.
Trace-class greedy extraction

In this section we freeze a depth
n
and look at what the trace
weights
\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R\right)\right)
do when we
repeatedly remove the largest depth-
n
block. At this point the
content measure language from Theorem
3.1
is convenient:
at each step we have a finite mass distribution on the depth-
n

cylinders, and the rule is simply “remove the heaviest one.” The
main point is that this produces a clean geometric decay estimate
for the trace of the remainder, with a rate depending only on the
number of packets at depth
n
. So, at fixed depth, the trace-greedy
rule gives a quantitative statement saying that a controlled amount
of mass must drop at each removal step.

We assume throughout this section that
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
so that all traces are finite.

For each depth
n\geq 1
we write

\mathcal{T}_{n}=\left\{w\in\mathcal{T}:\left|w\right|=n\right\},\qquad N_{n}=\#\mathcal{T}_{n}.

At depth
n
we have the orthogonal decomposition

I=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}P_{w},

and therefore for every
A\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,

A^{1/2}IA^{1/2}=A^{1/2}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}P_{w}\right)A^{1/2}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}C_{w}\left(A\right).

In particular, for all
A\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,

(4.1)

A=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}C_{w}\left(A\right).

We now fix a depth
n\geq 1
once and for all, and we look at a greedy
extraction scheme that only ever uses packets at this single level.
The idea is to start from
R
and, at each step, peel off one content
block
C_{w}\left(R^{(k-1)}\right)
at depth
n
which has maximal
trace among all packets at that depth.

We define the depth-
n
greedy extraction sequence starting from

R
by

R^{(0)}=R.

Given
R^{(k-1)}
at step
k\geq 1
, we choose a node
w_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}

such that

\mathrm{tr}(C_{w_{k}}(R^{(k-1)}))=\max_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(C_{w}(R^{(k-1)})),

define the extracted piece

D_{k}=C_{w_{k}}(R^{(k-1)}),

and update

R^{(k)}=R^{(k-1)}-D_{k}.

The maximum exists at each step because
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is finite
and each
\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(R^{(k-1)}\right)\right)
is
nonnegative. By Lemma
2.2
, the sequence
\left(R^{(k)}\right)_{k\geq 0}

decreases in the Loewner order, converges strongly to a limit
R^{(\infty)}\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
and for every
N\geq 1
, (
2.4
) holds.

At this point the construction is completely parallel to the general
sequential extraction in Section
2
, except that we are only
allowed to use packets at a fixed depth. The next theorem shows that
this additional restriction buys us a simple quantitative convergence
statement in trace norm, with a geometric rate depending only on
N_{n}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
fix a depth
n\geq 1
, and let
\left(R^{(k)},D_{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}

be the depth-
n
greedy extraction sequence defined above. Then for
every
k\geq 0
we have

\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k)})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)^{k}\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right).

In particular
R^{(k)}\to 0
in trace norm as
k\to\infty
, and

R=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}D_{k}

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. More precisely, for each
m\geq 1
,

\left\|R-\sum\nolimits^{m}_{k=1}D_{k}\right\|_{1}=\|R^{(m)}\|_{1}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)^{m}\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right).

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
. Apply (
4.1
) to
R^{(k-1)}
to get

R^{(k-1)}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}C_{w}(R^{(k-1)}).

Taking traces and using linearity of
\mathrm{tr}
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
we obtain

\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k-1)})=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(C_{w}(R^{(k-1)})).

The right-hand side is a sum of
N_{n}
nonnegative numbers. Therefore
the maximal term is at least the average, so

\mathrm{tr}\left(D_{k}\right)=\max_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(C_{w}(R^{(k-1)}))\geq\frac{1}{N_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k-1)}).

On the other hand, by definition of
R^{(k)}
and positivity,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k-1)})-\mathrm{tr}\left(D_{k}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k-1)}).

This proves the one-step inequality

\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k)})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k-1)})

for every
k\geq 1
. Iterating in
k
yields

\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k)})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)^{k}\mathrm{tr}\left(R^{(0)}\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)^{k}\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right),

as claimed.

Since each
R^{(k)}
is positive, its trace norm agrees with its
trace
\|R^{(k)}\|_{1}=\mathrm{tr}\left((R^{(k)})\right)
.
The estimate above therefore shows that
\|R^{(k)}\|_{1}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
, i.e.,
R^{(k)}\to 0
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Using (
2.4
), we get

\left\|R-\sum\nolimits^{m}_{k=1}D_{k}\right\|_{1}=\|R^{(m)}\|_{1}=\mathrm{tr}(R^{(m)})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)^{m}\mathrm{tr}\left(R\right).

The right-hand side tends to zero as
m\to\infty
, so
\sum_{k\geq 1}D_{k}

converges to
R
in trace norm.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It may be helpful to restate the depth-
n
greedy
rule using the boundary measures from Theorem
3.1
. Since
each
R^{(k)}
is positive trace-class, it has an associated finite
Borel measure
\mu_{R^{(k)}}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}
satisfying

\mu_{R^{(k)}}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\mathrm{tr}(C_{w}(R^{(k)}))\qquad\text{for every }w\in\mathcal{T}.

In particular,

\mu_{R^{(k)}}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)=\mathrm{tr}(R^{(k)}).

With this notation, the depth-
n
greedy choice at step
k
is simply

w_{k}\in\arg\max_{\left|w\right|=n}\mu_{R^{(k-1)}}\left(\left[w\right]\right),\qquad\mathrm{tr}\left(D_{k}\right)=\mu_{R^{(k-1)}}\left(\left[w_{k}\right]\right).

So Theorem
4.1
is really a statement about how fast the total
mass
\mu_{R^{(k)}}\left(\partial\mathcal{T}\right)
decays under
the deterministic rule that, at each step, removes the current heaviest
cylinder at depth
n
for the current boundary measure.

5.
HS Greedy extraction

Section
4
sees the trace weights of the depth-
n
blocks.
Here we switch to a Hilbert-Schmidt (HS) analogue, where interactions
between different packet blocks start to matter and one can distinguish
“almost block-diagonal” operators from genuinely mixed ones. The
basic score is now
\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|_{2}

rather than
\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(A\right)\right)
, and we
introduce a depth-
n
coherence parameter
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)

to quantify how far
A
is from being block-diagonal with respect
to the packet partition. With this in hand, we prove an analogue of
the geometric decay estimate for the HS-based greedy rule, and the
coherence term explains when this stronger selection criterion gives
a sharper bound than the trace-only picture. The point is that this
is the first place where genuinely noncommutative behavior shows up;
unlike traces, the HS norm is sensitive to how the packet blocks interact,
not just to their individual sizes.

Throughout we assume that
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
so in particular
R\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. We define

E_{n}\left(X\right)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}P_{w}XP_{w},

i.e., the conditional expectation onto the block-diagonal subalgebra
determined by
\left(P_{w}\right)_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}
.

For any
A\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
we set

\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)=\frac{\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}},

whenever the denominator is nonzero. This parameter will serve as
a “depth-
n
coherence” of
A
relative to the packet decomposition.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
.
Then

(5.1)

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\left(E_{n}\left(A\right)A\right).

Moreover,

(5.2)

\frac{1}{N_{n}}\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2},

and hence
1\leq\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)\leq N_{n}
.

In particular,
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)=1
if and only if
E_{n}\left(A\right)=A
,
equivalently if and only if
P_{w}A=AP_{w}
for all
w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}

(i.e.,
A
is block diagonal at depth
n
).

Proof.

Note that
C_{w}\left(A\right)=A^{1/2}P_{w}A^{1/2}
, so that

C_{w}\left(A\right)^{2}=A^{1/2}P_{w}AP_{w}A^{1/2}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}.

Thus,

\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{w}\left(A\right)^{2}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(P_{w}AP_{w}A\right).

Summing over
w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
gives (
5.1
).

View
\mathcal{S}_{2}
as a Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\left(Y^{*}X\right)
.
Then
E_{n}
is selfadjoint and idempotent on
\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
because for
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle E_{n}\left(X\right),Y\right\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(E_{n}\left(X\right)^{*}Y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w}\mathrm{tr}\left(P_{w}X^{*}P_{w}Y\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{*}E_{n}\left(Y\right)\right)=\left\langle X,E_{n}\left(Y\right)\right\rangle_{2}.

Thus
E_{n}
is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace of block-diagonal
operators, in particular

\left\|E_{n}\left(X\right)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|X\right\|_{2}

for all
X\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz and (
5.1
),
we get

(5.3)

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\left(E_{n}\left(A\right)A\right)\leq\left\|E_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{2}\left\|A\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}.

For the lower bound, use (
4.1
) in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Then

\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}=\left\|\sum\nolimits_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq N_{n}\sum\nolimits_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}

by the elementary inequality
\left\|\sum^{N}_{i=1}x_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq N\sum_{i}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}
.
This gives

(5.4)

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{N_{n}}\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Combing (
5.3
)-(
5.4
) gives (
5.2
), and
so
1\leq\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)\leq N_{n}
.

Finally, if
E_{n}\left(A\right)=A
, then

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\left(E_{n}\left(A\right)A\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{2}\right)=\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2},

so
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)=1
. Conversely, if
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)=1

then equality holds in the chain below:

\mathrm{tr}\left(E_{n}\left(A\right)A\right)\leq\left\|E_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{2}\left\|A\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Thus
\left\|E_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{2}=\left\|A\right\|_{2}
,
and since
E_{n}
is the orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
this forces
E_{n}\left(A\right)=A
. The equivalence with commuting
with every
P_{w}
at depth
n
is standard.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}

and
D\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
with
0\leq D\leq A
.
Then

\left\|A-D\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}-\left\|D\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Proof.

Write
B=A-D\geq 0
. Then,

\left\|A-D\right\|^{2}_{2}=\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}+\left\|D\right\|^{2}_{2}-2\mathrm{tr}\left(AD\right).

It suffices to show
\mathrm{tr}\left(AD\right)\geq\mathrm{tr}\left(D^{2}\right)
.
We can write

\mathrm{tr}\left(AD\right)-\mathrm{tr}\left(D^{2}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(A-D\right)D\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(BD\right).

Since
B,D\geq 0
, we have
D^{1/2}BD^{1/2}\geq 0
. Moreover,
D^{1/2}\in\mathcal{S}_{4}

and
B\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, so
D^{1/2}BD^{1/2}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}

and its trace is nonnegative:

\mathrm{tr}\left(BD\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(D^{1/2}BD^{1/2}\right)\geq 0.

This gives
\mathrm{tr}\left(AD\right)\geq\mathrm{tr}\left(D^{2}\right)
.
∎

We now describe the HS greedy algorithm and its basic convergence
property. At depth
n
, starting from
R^{\left(0\right)}=R
, we
define inductively

w_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\quad\text{such that}\quad\|C_{w_{k}}(R^{\left(k-1\right)})\|_{2}=\max_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}(R^{\left(k-1\right)})\right\|_{2},

then set

D_{k}=C_{w_{k}}(R^{\left(k-1\right)}),\qquad R^{\left(k\right)}=R^{\left(k-1\right)}-D_{k}.

As in the trace-greedy case, each
D_{k}
is positive, satisfies

0\leq D_{k}\leq R^{\left(k-1\right)}
, and is trace-class; hence all

R^{\left(k\right)}
remain in
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
and in particular in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap B\left(H\right)_{+}
,
fix
n\geq 1
, and let
\left(R^{\left(k\right)}\right)
be the depth-
n

HS greedy sequence from above. Then for every
k\geq 1
,

(5.5)

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)N_{n}}\right)\|R^{\left(k-1\right)}\|^{2}_{2}.

In particular, since
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)\leq N_{n}
,
one has the uniform estimate

(5.6)

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N^{2}_{n}}\right)^{k}\left\|R\right\|^{2}_{2},

and therefore
R^{\left(k\right)}\to 0
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Moreover,
R=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}D_{k}
with convergence in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
and for each
m\geq 1

(5.7)

\left\|R-\sum\nolimits^{m}_{k=1}D_{k}\right\|_{2}=\|R^{\left(m\right)}\|_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N^{2}_{n}}\right)^{m/2}\left\|R\right\|_{2}.

Finally, if
R^{\left(k-1\right)}
is block-diagonal at depth
n
,
i.e.
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)=1
, then the one-step
factor improves to

(5.8)

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{n}}\right)\|R^{\left(k-1\right)}\|^{2}_{2}.

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
and set
A=R^{\left(k-1\right)}
. By construction,
D_{k}=C_{w_{k}}\left(A\right)

for some
w_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, and we have
0\leq D_{k}\leq A
.
Lemma
5.2
then gives

(5.9)

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}=\left\|A-D_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}-\left\|D_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

By the choice of
w_{k}
, the block
D_{k}
has maximal HS norm
among
\left\{C_{w}\left(A\right):w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\right\}
.
Hence

\left\|D_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}=\max_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{N_{n}}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}.

By the definition of
\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)
,

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\|C_{w}\left(A\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)}.

Putting these together,

\left\|D_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{N_{n}}\cdot\frac{\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)}=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)N_{n}}\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Substituting into (
5.9
) yields

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}\left(A\right)N_{n}}\right)\left\|A\right\|^{2}_{2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)N_{n}}\right)\|R^{\left(k-1\right)}\|^{2}_{2},

which is the first assertion (
5.5
).

The uniform bound follows from
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)\leq N_{n}
,
see Lemma
5.1
. This gives

\|R^{\left(k\right)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{N^{2}_{n}}\right)\|R^{\left(k-1\right)}\|^{2}_{2}

for all
k
, and iterating yields (
5.6
).

In particular,
R^{\left(k\right)}\to 0
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Since

R^{\left(m\right)}=R-\sum^{m}_{k=1}D_{k}
by construction, this
is the same as saying that
\sum^{m}_{k=1}D_{k}\to R
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
with the remainder bound stated in (
5.7
).

The final improvement (
5.8
) in the block-diagonal case is
immediate from the proof: if
R^{\left(k-1\right)}
is block-diagonal
at depth
n
, then
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)=1

by Lemma
5.1
, and the factor becomes
1-1/N_{n}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

It is convenient to keep Theorem
3.1
in mind when thinking
about the depth-
n
HS rule. In the trace-class setting, each
R^{\left(k\right)}

carries its content measure
\mu_{R^{\left(k\right)}}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}
,
and for each
x\in H
we have the densities
e^{\left(k\right)}_{x}

with respect to
\mu_{R^{\left(k\right)}}
(see also Remark
4.2
).
The greedy rule in Section
4
just picks, at each step, the
heaviest depth-
n
cylinder for
\mu_{R^{\left(k-1\right)}}
. The
HS rule here keeps the same packet decomposition but uses the depth-
n

HS content and the parameter
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)

from Lemma
5.1
to record how unevenly the packet blocks
of
R^{\left(k-1\right)}
are arranged across those cylinders. In
particular, the case
\Gamma_{n}\left(R^{\left(k-1\right)}\right)=1

is exactly when
R^{\left(k-1\right)}
is already block-diagonal
at depth
n
, so the improved decay in Theorem
5.3
is best
viewed as the result of the fact that, at that stage, the boundary
data from Theorem
3.1
is already well aligned with the depth-
n

partition.

6.
Application: packet-content image denoising

We include a simple denoising procedure whose selection rule is exactly
the depth-
n
content rule from Sections
3
-
5
,
with the content measure from Theorem
3.1
as a convenient
language for organizing the depth-
n
weights. The point is not to
optimize denoising performance, but to give a mathematically clean
packet-block selection rule whose approximation behavior is discussed
in Theorems
4.1
and
5.3
.

Fix a 1D wavelet packet system generated by a quadrature mirror filter
pair
\left(h,g\right)
(for instance a Daubechies family), and use
the standard separable tensor-product construction to obtain a 2D
wavelet packet transform on square patches of size
m\times m
. Fix
a depth
n
. At depth
n
the packet system yields a finite family
of mutually orthogonal packet subspaces
\left\{W_{w}\right\}_{\left|w\right|=n}

whose orthogonal projections
\left\{P_{w}\right\}_{\left|w\right|=n}

satisfy
\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}P_{w}=I
. This is the concrete wavelet
packet realization of the abstract tree partition used throughout
the paper.

Given a noisy image
Y=X+\sigma Z
, we work on patches: extract
M

patches
y_{1},\dots,y_{M}\in H
, where
H\simeq\mathbb{R}^{m^{2}}
,
and form the empirical second-moment operator

\widehat{R}=\frac{1}{M}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\left|y_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle y_{i}\right|\in B\left(H\right)_{+}.

Since
H
is finite-dimensional,
\widehat{R}
is trace-class, and
Theorem
3.1
applies to
\widehat{R}
. In particular, the
associated content measure
\mu_{\widehat{R}}
on
\partial\mathcal{T}

satisfies

\mu_{\widehat{R}}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\text{tr}(C_{w}(\widehat{R}))\quad\text{for every }w\in\mathcal{T}.

For the application here, one only needs the depth-
n
cylinder weights.
Note that these weights can be computed without forming
\widehat{R}^{1/2}
,
since

\mu_{\widehat{R}}\left(\left[w\right]\right)=\text{tr}(C_{w}(\widehat{R}))=\text{tr}(\widehat{R}^{1/2}P_{w}\widehat{R}^{1/2})=\text{tr}(P_{w}\widehat{R}).

Moreover,
\text{tr}(P_{w}\widehat{R})
has a direct patch interpretation:

\text{tr}(P_{w}\widehat{R})=\frac{1}{M}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\left\langle y_{i},P_{w}y_{i}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{M}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\left\|P_{w}y_{i}\right\|^{2},

so it is literally the average packet-block energy of the observed
patches at node
w
.

With this notation, the denoising operator is simply the projection
onto the selected packet blocks. Fix a
K
, choose
\mathcal{W}_{K}\subset\left\{w:\left|w\right|=n\right\}

consisting of the
K
largest values of
\text{tr}(P_{w}\widehat{R})

(equivalently, the
K
heaviest depth-
n
cylinders for
\mu_{\widehat{R}}
),
and define

T_{K}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{K}}P_{w}.

Each noisy patch
y
is denoised by
\widehat{x}=T_{K}y
, and the
denoised image is obtained by aggregating the denoised patches by
overlap-averaging.

The selection procedure preserves positivity at the operator level.
At depth
n
we have the positive decomposition
\widehat{R}=\sum_{\left|w\right|=n}C_{w}(\widehat{R})

with each
C_{w}(\widehat{R})\in B\left(H\right)_{+}
. Hence for
any choice
\mathcal{W}_{K}\subset\left\{w:\left|w\right|=n\right\}

the truncated content operator

\widehat{R}_{K}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{K}}C_{w}(\widehat{R})

remains positive, and the term
\widehat{R}-\widehat{R}_{K}=\sum_{w\notin\mathcal{W}_{K}}C_{w}(\widehat{R})

is positive as well. This is the basic structural reason the greedy
removal estimates in Sections
4
-
5
can be read
as monotone approximation statements inside the positive cone
B\left(H\right)_{+}
.

The deterministic content-decay estimates in Section
4
can
be applied directly to justify why such a selection captures most
of the patch energy quickly. Theorem
4.1
quantifies the mass
removed when one repeatedly removes the heaviest depth-
n
cylinder
for the current content measure. If one prefers a structure-sensitive
alternative, Section
5
suggests using the Hilbert-Schmidt
scores
\|C_{w}(\widehat{R})\|_{2}
, in which case Theorem
5.3

gives the corresponding decay.

Algorithm 6.1

(packet-content denoising at depth
n
)
.

(1)

Build the separable 2D wavelet packet transform on
m\times m
patches
to depth
n
, and let
\left\{P_{w}\right\}_{\left|w\right|=n}

be the corresponding depth-
n
orthogonal projections.

(2)

Extract patches
y_{1},\dots,y_{M}\in H
from
Y
, and compute the
depth-
n
block scores

s_{w}=\frac{1}{M}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\left\|P_{w}y_{i}\right\|^{2}\quad\left(\left|w\right|=n\right).

(3)

Let
\mathcal{W}_{K}\subset\left\{w:\left|w\right|=n\right\}
be
the set of
K
nodes with largest scores
s_{w}
, and form the projection

T_{K}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{K}}P_{w}
.

(4)

For each patch
y
extracted from
Y
, form the denoised patch
\widehat{x}=T_{K}y
.
Place
\widehat{x}
back at its original location, and obtain
\widehat{X}

by averaging the values of all denoised patches that overlap at each
pixel.

Remark 6.2
.

Since the construction starts from an empirical second-moment operator

\widehat{R}
and then applies a linear projection to each patch,
it may look at first glance like a PCA-type step. The mechanism here
is different. We do not diagonalize
\widehat{R}
or learn an eigen-subspace.
Instead, we fix a wavelet packet partition at depth
n
and select
packet blocks by the content weights attached to that partition (equivalently,
by the cylinder masses of
\mu_{\widehat{R}}
from Theorem
3.1
).
In other words, the data only decide which packet blocks to keep,
not what directions define the blocks.

The key is that, unlike scalar thresholding, this selection respects
the operator structure, ensuring the retained approximation remains
in the positive cone
B\left(H\right)_{+}
. Positivity is not cosmetic
here. It is what lets one view each truncation as a second-moment
operator and compare the retained part and the discarded part in the
Loewner order, which is the order structure behind the monotone decay
estimates proved earlier.

We also emphasize that the denoising example is a one-shot depth-
n

truncation. Theorems
4.1
and
5.3
analyze genuinely
sequential updates, where the selection is repeated after each removal
step using the current operator. One can implement the same idea patchwise
by iterating the operator update itself, but we keep the present illustration
minimal and focused on making the content-based selection rule concrete.

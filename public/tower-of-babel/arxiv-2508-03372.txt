Abstract
. Making use of the fact that Hopf-Galois structures on separable extensions and skew bracoids are both intrinsically connected to transitive subgroups of the holomorph, we present an algorithm to classify and enumerate these objects in several low degree cases. Our approach allows us to greatly extend existing computational results for Hopf-Galois structures, while building on the existing algorithms presented in this area.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20-08; 12F10; 16T05; 20B05.

1.
Introduction

Hopf–Galois theory and skew bracoids are two, initially seemingly disconnected, areas of algebra which have very powerful applications across mathematics and physics.

Hopf–Galois structures, for example, first arose in a book called
Hopf Algebras and Galois Theory
by Chase and Sweedler,
[
CS69
]
, in which they investigated and generalised what it meant for a field extension
L/K
to be Galois with group
G
. One now associates to
L/K
, which need not be Galois in the classical sense, a
K
-Hopf algebra
H
which acts on
L
via
\cdot
in such a way that it behaves, morally, like a Galois group. The pair
(H,\cdot)
is termed a
Hopf–Galois structure
on
L/K
, and where there is only ever one Galois group associated to a Galois extension,
L/K
may admit several different Hopf–Galois structures. This therefore has applications for greatly expanding, for example, Galois module theory, as one now has several different lenses through which to view a single extension. One important result in the theory is that of the Hopf–Galois correspondence. Much like in the classical Galois correspondence, given a Hopf–Galois structure
(L/K,H)
, the Hopf subalgebras of
H
correspond to intermediate fields of
L/K
. This map is always injective but not necessarily surjective.

Skew bracoids are a far more recent algebraic construction developed by Martin-Lyons and Truman in
[
MLT24
]
. Some of the fundamental ideas of these objects were formulated by Rump in
[
Rum07
]
, in the form of braces, and then, through relaxing the condition that the ‘type’ of the brace need be abelian, by Guarnieri and Vendramin in
[
GV17
]
, in the form of skew braces. Skew braces yield non-degenerate solutions to the set-theoretic Yang-Baxter equation (ST-YBE). Given a set
X
and a map
r:X\times X\rightarrow X\times X
, then
(X,r)
is a solution to the ST-YBE if

(r\times\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\times r).

The idea behind understanding solutions of the ST-YBE is to shed more light on the (full/quantum) Yang-Baxter equation, which is a fundamental equation arising all over mathematics and physics. Perhaps most notably in statistical mechanics and representations of the braid group. Skew bracoids offer a far more general view of the algebraic perspective of skew braces, with the idea being to ‘decouple’ some of their internal structure. In a very recent paper by Colazzo, Koch, Martin-Lyons and Truman,
[
CKMLT24
]
, certain classes of skew bracoids have been shown to yield right (but not necessarily left) non-degenerate solutions to the ST-YBE.

Over a series of papers, the intimate connection between Hopf–Galois structures on Galois/separable extensions and skew braces/bracoids has been made known. On the one hand, given a separable extension
L/K
with Galois closure
E
, if one defines
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
and
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)
, then Greither and Pareigis showed in
[
GP87
]
that the Hopf–Galois structures on
L/K
relate to finding certain regular subgroups
N
of
\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
. The group
N
therefore has the same order as the degree of
L/K
, and is called the
type
of the associated Hopf–Galois structure. Nine years later, Byott in
[
Byo96
]
showed that finding all separable extensions admitting Hopf–Galois structures of type
N
relates to finding transitive subgroups of the holomorph of
N
,
\mathrm{Hol}(N)\cong N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. On the other hand, it was shown in
[
GV17
]
that skew braces with ‘additive group’ isomorphic to
N
correspond to regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. The connection between skew braces and Hopf–Galois structures on Galois extensions via regular subgroups of the holomorph was explored explicitly in
[
SV18
]
. This connection was then extended in
[
MLT24
]
to showing that skew bracoids correspond to transitive subgroups of the holomorph, and are therefore closely connected with Hopf–Galois structures on separable extensions.

The nature of enumerating and classifying both types of structures, along with the strong connection between them, makes the problem of doing so particularly amenable to computer calculation. The main motivation for doing so is in order to be able to produce a database of small examples which one can use for testing examples, looking for counterexamples, and producing conjectures. Computer algorithms for computing skew braces have been designed since their inception; Guarnieri and Vendramin present Algorithm 5.1 in
[
GV17
]
, which they demonstrated by computing the number of skew brace of size
n\leq 30
. This was subsequently refined by Bardakov, Neshchadim and Yadev in
[
BNY20
]
, who made the observation that two regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
are conjugate if and only if they are conjugate by an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. Their observation allowed the enumeration of skew braces of order up to 868, with some expected exceptions. A first computer-aided enumeration of Hopf–Galois structures on Galois extensions was done by Byott and Vendramin in the appendix of
[
SV18
]
. Two years later, Crespo and Salguero in
[
CS20
]
gave an algorithm to enumerate the Hopf–Galois structures on separable extensions of degree
n\leq 11
. This was shortly followed by their sequel,
[
CS21
]
, which extends these results up to degree
31
.

In this paper, we present an algorithm for classifying and enumerating both Hopf–Galois structures on separable extensions and skew bracoids. For a small integer
n>1
and a group
N
of order
n
, we utilise the Magma computational algebra system,
[
BCP97
]
, to first compute the transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, and then to sort these into the relevant classes in order to classify each corresponding structure. The method of computing these subgroups directly means that we do not rely on Magma’s knowledge of the classification of transitive permutation groups, unlike the approach of
[
CS20
,
CS21
]
. This has the upshot of being able to greatly extend these results, but also means that Magma does not always know the names of all the groups appearing in the classification. In that case, we omit this information and simply display the generators of the permutation group. We note here that our choice of Magma over alternatives is due to the efficient way it views, computes and deals with subgroups of the holomorph as permutation groups.

The code for the main algorithms can be found at
[
Dar25b
]
, and all the results (as well as all the algorithms) are given on the website
[
Dar25a
]
, which is being continuously updated. The data parser for generating the tables can be found at
[
Hod24
]
. We present a summary of results obtained up to degree 99 with some expected exceptions, noting that higher degree computations are feasible, especially for skew bracoids. As an application, we also include some conjectures based on these results.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the necessary definitions and results relating to Hopf–Galois structures, skew bracoids and transitive subgroups needed for the rest of this paper.

2.1.
Transitive subgroups of the holomorph

Unless otherwise stated, let
n
be a positive integer and let
N
be a group of order
n
.

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Perm}(N)
is
transitive
if
M
acts transitively on
N
.

If
M\leq\mathrm{Perm}(N)
is transitive, then we say it is
regular
if
|M|=|N|
, or equivalently, if
\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(\eta)=1_{M}
for all
\eta\in N
.

We may view
N
inside
\mathrm{Perm}(N)
as the image of the left translation map

\lambda:N\to\mathrm{Perm}(N)
, this then motivates the next definition:

Definition 2.2
.

The
holomorph
,
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, of
N
is the normaliser of
\lambda(N)
in
\mathrm{Perm}(N)
. That is

\mathrm{Hol}(N):=\mathrm{Norm}_{\mathrm{Perm}(N)}(\lambda(N))=\{\pi\in\mathrm{Perm}(N)\mid\pi\lambda(\eta)\pi^{-1}\in\lambda(N)\;\forall\eta\in N\}.

The following result assists with hand computations within the holomorph:

Lemma 2.3
.

\mathrm{Hol}(N)\cong N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N).

Proof.

Proposition 7.2 of
[
Chi00
]
shows that
\mathrm{Hol}(N)\cong\rho(N)\cdot\mathrm{Aut}(N)
, where

\rho:N\to\mathrm{Perm}(N)
is the right translation map. We then get a canonical isomorphism

\rho(N)\cdot\mathrm{Aut}(N)\cong N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N)

which is induced by the isomorphism
\rho(N)\cong N
and the fact that
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
normalises
N
.
∎

The group multiplication in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is given by the following: for
\eta,\mu\in N
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
, we have

(\eta,\alpha)(\mu,\beta)=(\eta\alpha(\mu),\alpha\beta)

inside
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. We therefore get an induced action
\cdot
of any subgroup
M
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
on
N
given by

(\eta,\alpha)\cdot\mu:=\eta\alpha(\mu).

The following result is a slight adaption of the key observation in
[
BNY20
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
such that
G_{1}
is transitive on
N
. Then
G_{1}
is conjugate to
G_{2}
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
if and only if they are conjugate by an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, and
[\eta,\alpha]
be an element of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. We show that there is an element
\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
such that

(1,\beta)^{-1}G_{1}(1,\beta)=(\eta,\alpha)^{-1}G_{1}(\eta,\alpha)=:G_{2}.

To this end, as
G_{1}
acts transitively on
N
, we note that there is a
(\nu,\gamma)\in G_{1}
such that
(\nu,\gamma)\cdot\eta=\nu\gamma(\eta)=1
. Therefore

(\nu,\gamma)(\eta,\alpha)=(1,\gamma\alpha).

We now let
\beta:=\gamma\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. Thus

\displaystyle(1,\beta)^{-1}G_{1}(1,\beta)

\displaystyle=((\nu,\gamma)(\eta,\alpha))^{-1}G_{1}(\nu,\gamma)(\eta,\alpha)

\displaystyle=(\eta,\alpha)^{-1}(\nu,\gamma)^{-1}G_{1}(\nu,\gamma)(\eta,\alpha)=(\eta,\alpha)^{-1}G_{1}(\eta,\alpha)=G_{2}.

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
such that
G_{1}
is transitive on
N
. If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are conjugate inside
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, then they are isomorphic as permutation groups and so, in particular,
G_{2}
is also transitive on
N
.

Proof.

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are conjugate inside
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, then Proposition
2.4
tells us that there is an element
\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
such that
\beta G_{1}\beta^{-1}=G_{2}
. Further, as
\beta(1_{N})=1_{N}
, it is clear that
\beta\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N})\beta^{-1}\leq\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N})
. If
g_{2}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N})
, then there is a
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
\beta g_{1}\beta^{-1}=g_{2}
, and so
g_{1}=\beta^{-1}g_{2}\beta
. Hence
g_{1}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N})
and
\beta\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N})\beta^{-1}=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N})
. So conjugation by
\beta
gives an isomorphism of permutation groups.
∎

2.2.
Hopf–Galois structures

Let
L/K
be a field extension and let
H
be a
K
-Hopf algebra acting on
L
with action
\cdot
such that
L
is an
H
-module algebra. Then we say that
H
together with its action on
L
gives a
Hopf–Galois structure
on
L/K
if
\cdot
induces an isomorphism between the
K
-vector spaces
L\otimes_{K}H
and
\mathrm{End}_{K}(L)
. If, in addition,
L/K
is separable, then denote by
E
its Galois closure, and define the Galois groups
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
and
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)
. Consider the left translation map
\lambda:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
,
\lambda(g)(\overline{h})=\overline{gh}
. It can be shown that
\lambda
is injective, and so we may identify
G
, via
\lambda
, as a permutation group of degree
n=[L:K]
. In this instance,
G^{\prime}
is then identified with the subgroup
\lambda(G^{\prime})=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1_{N})
. Greither and Pareigis in
[
GP87
]
showed that if
N<\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
is regular on
G/G^{\prime}
and is normalised by
\lambda(G)
, then
L/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure
H=E[N]^{G}
of type (the isomorphism class of)
N
, with
L-
action given by the following:

(1)

\left(\sum_{\eta\in N}a_{\eta}\eta\right)\cdot x=\sum_{\eta\in N}a_{\eta}(\eta^{-1}(1_{G/G^{\prime}}))(x).

In fact, this correspondence is bijective, and so every Hopf–Galois structure on
L/K
arises in this way. The problem with this approach is that
\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
can get very large very quickly, and so it might take a long time to find all the relevant subgroups. However, hope comes from the following theorem due to Byott in
[
Byo96
]
, which greatly reduces our search space. We note that the presentation here is due to Childs in chapter 2 of
[
Chi00
]
:

Theorem 2.6

(Byott’s Translation)
.

There is a bijection between the following two sets:

\mathcal{N}:=\{\alpha:N\rightarrow\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})\mid\alpha\text{ injective hom. with }\alpha(N)\text{ regular}\}

and

\mathcal{G}:=\{\beta:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Perm}(N)\mid\beta\text{ injective hom. with }\beta(G^{\prime})=\mathrm{Stab}(1_{N})\}.

Furthermore, if
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
correspond to
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
, then
\alpha(N)=\alpha^{\prime}(N)
iff
\beta(G)
and
\beta^{\prime}(G)
are conjugate by some
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
; and
\alpha(N)
is normalised by
\lambda(G)
iff
\beta(G)
is contained in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
.

We will therefore say that a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N

realises
the permutation group
G
if there is a transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
and an isomorphism
\phi:G\to M
such that
\phi(G^{\prime})=M^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})
. Theorem
2.6
leads to the following counting result:

Lemma 2.7

(
[
Byo96
]
)
.

Let
G,G^{\prime},N
be as above, let
e(G,N)
be the number of Hopf–Galois structures of type
N
which realise
G
, and
e^{\prime}(G,N)
the number of transitive subgroups
M
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
isomorphic to
G
via an isomorphism taking the stabiliser
M^{\prime}
of
1_{N}
in
M
to
G^{\prime}
. Then

e(G,N)=\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})|}{|\mathrm{Aut}(N)|}e^{\prime}(G,N),

where

\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})=\left\{\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)\mid\theta(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime}\right\},

the group of automorphisms
\theta
of
G
such that
\theta
fixes the identity coset
1_{G}G^{\prime}
of
G/G^{\prime}
.

Let
(H,\cdot)
give a Hopf–Galois structure on a field extension
L/K
and let
I
be a Hopf subalgebra of
H
. We define the subfield of
L
fixed by
I
as

L^{I}:=\{x\in L\mid h\cdot x=\varepsilon(h)x\;\forall h\in I\}.

In particular, we have that
L^{H}=K
. In
[
CS69
]
it was shown that this ‘Hopf–Galois correspondence’ is injective and inclusion reversing, but that it is not necessarily surjective, unlike the usual Galois correspondence. However, in the case that
L/K
is separable, it can be shown that certain families of extensions admit at least one Hopf–Galois structure for which the Hopf–Galois correspondence is surjective: a separable extension
L/K
is said to be
almost classically Galois
if
G^{\prime}
has a normal complement in
G
(where
G,G^{\prime}
are as above). This can further be shown to be equivalent to the notion that there is a regular subgroup
N
in
\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
normalised by
G
and contained in
G
. Therefore, if
L/K
is almost classically Galois, then
G\cong N\rtimes G^{\prime}
, and it can be shown that the Hopf algebra
H=E[N^{opp}]^{G}
gives a Hopf–Galois structure on
L/K
admitting a bijective correspondence. Here,
N^{opp}
denotes the opposite group of
N
, that is
N^{opp}
has underlying set
N
, and if
*
,
*_{opp}
are the operations on
N
and
N^{opp}
respectively, then
g*_{opp}h=h*g
for all
g,h\in N
. Although the property of being almost classically Galois is initially given to the field extension, Kohl in
[
Koh98
]
refers to the Hopf–Galois structure given by
E[N^{opp}]^{G}
as an
almost classically Galois structure
. It can therefore be asked, for example, how many almost classically Galois structures an almost classically Galois extension admits. We note that the fact that a field extension
L/K
is almost classically Galois does not necessarily guarantee that all Hopf–Galois structures on
L/K
are almost classically Galois, nor does it even guarantee that they all admit a bijective correspondence.

2.3.
Skew bracoids

A
skew bracoid
is a quintuple
(N,+,G,\circ,\odot)
such that
(N,+)
and
(G,\circ)
are (not necessarily abelian) groups with a transitive action satisfying the following relation:

g\odot(\mu+\eta)=(g\odot\mu)-(g\odot 1_{N})+(g\odot\eta)\;\;\forall g\in G,\eta,\mu\in N.

Remark 2.8

(Skew braces)
.

A skew bracoid for which
|G|=|N|
is termed
essentially a skew brace
. It is noted in
[
MLT24
]
that the structure of
G
may be transported to
N
via the rule

(g\odot 1_{N})\circ(h\odot 1_{N})=(gh)\odot 1_{N}.

Therefore
(N,+,\circ)
is now a skew brace. We note that skew braces give rise to non-degenerate solutions of the ST-YBE by defining a map
r:N\times N\rightarrow N\times N
,
r(x,y)=(\sigma_{x}(y),\rho_{y}(x)):=(-x+x\circ y,(-x+x\circ y)^{-1}\circ x\circ y)
for all
x,y\in N
. As mentioned above, it can be shown that skew bracoids may be linked to other types of solution for the ST-YBE, but we do not give the details here.

For a skew bracoid
(G,N)
, we will call
|N|
the
degree
of
(G,N)
. To our knowledge, this terminology has not yet been established, and we feel that ‘degree’ corresponds more closely with the fact that they relate to permutation groups of degree
|N|
.

In order to classify these structures, we need to understand them in some more detail. Suppose, therefore, we have two skew bracoids
(G_{1},N,\odot_{1})
and
(G_{2},N,\odot_{2})
, and let
\lambda_{\odot_{1}}:G_{1}\to\mathrm{Perm}(N)
and
\lambda_{\odot_{2}}:G_{2}\to\mathrm{Perm}(N)
be the permutation representations of
\odot_{1}
and
\odot_{2}
respectively. It was shown in Theorem 2.8 of
[
MLT24
]
that
\lambda_{\odot_{1}}(G_{1}),\lambda_{\odot_{2}}(G_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, and that they are isomorphic to a quotient of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
respectively. Now if
\lambda_{\odot_{1}}(G_{1})=\lambda_{\odot_{2}}(G_{2})
, then it is clear that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
both act on
N
in ‘essentially the same’ way. If
(G_{1},N,\odot_{1})
and
(G_{2},N,\odot_{2})
are related in precisely this way, there are therefore said to be
equivalent
. This reduces to equality in the case of skew braces.

Proposition 2.9

(Corollary 2.21 of
[
MLT24
]
)
.

Given a group
N
, there is a bijection between transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
and equivalence classes of skew bracoids
(G,N)
.

We note that every skew bracoid
(G,N,\odot)
is equivalent to a skew bracoid
(G^{\prime},N^{\prime},\odot^{\prime})
for which
G^{\prime}
acts on
N^{\prime}
faithfully (i.e. the permutation representation of
\odot^{\prime}
has trivial kernel). A skew bracoid with such a faithful transitive action is termed a
reduced
skew bracoid.

We further have a notion of isomorphism:

Definition 2.10
.

An
isomorphism
of skew bracoids
(G,N,\odot)\to(G^{\prime},N^{\prime},\odot^{\prime})
is a pair of group isomorphisms

\displaystyle\phi:G\to G^{\prime},

\displaystyle\psi:N\to N^{\prime},

such that

\psi(g\odot\eta)=\phi(g)\odot^{\prime}\psi(\eta)\;\;\forall g\in G,\eta\in N.

It turns out that, if
(\phi,\psi)
gives an isomorphism of skew bracoids, then the form of
\psi
is completely determined by
\phi
. We need one more result which again follows a similar pattern to that in skew brace theory:

Proposition 2.11

(Proposition 4.13 of
[
MLT24
]
)
.

Let
N
be a group and let
(G_{1},N,\odot_{1})
,
(G_{2},N,\odot_{2})
be reduced skew bracoids. Then
(G_{1},N,\odot_{1})\cong(G_{2},N,\odot_{2})
if and only if
\lambda_{\odot_{1}}(G_{1})
is conjugate to
\lambda_{\odot_{2}}(G_{2})
by an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
.

Thus, by Corollary
2.5
, the number of equivalence classes of skew bracoids with groups
G
and
N
, up to isomorphism, is simply equal to the number of conjugacy classes of transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
with
M\cong G
.

In
[
ML24
]
, the concept of
almost classical
skew bracoids were introduced:

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(G,N,\odot)
be a skew bracoid and
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{\odot}(1_{N})
. Then
(G,N,\odot)
is
almost classical
if
G^{\prime}
has a normal complement
H
in
G
such that the sub-skew bracoid
(H,N,\odot|_{H})
is trivial when viewed as a skew brace.

Therefore, if
(G,N,\odot)
is an almost classical skew bracoid, then
G=H\rtimes G^{\prime}
for some
H\leq G
. We note further that
(G,N,\odot)
‘contains a brace’ in the sense of
[
MLT24
]
. Hence all almost classical skew bracoids give rise to solutions of the ST-YBE by the methods presented in
[
CKMLT24
]
.

By Proposition 4.3 of
[
ML24
]
, we have that
(G,N,\odot)
is an almost classical skew bracoid if and only if
\lambda_{\odot}(G)=N\rtimes A\subseteq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for some
A\leq\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. Suppose now that
L/K
is a separable extension with Galois groups
M:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
and
M^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)
and there is an isomorphism
\phi:M\to\lambda_{\odot}(G)
such that
\phi(M^{\prime})=A
. Then
L/K
is almost classically Galois, admitting an almost classically Galois structure given by the Hopf algebra
E[N^{opp}]^{M}
.

3.
The Algorithm

Given a positive integer
n
, we outline here the algorithm we use to enumerate the Hopf–Galois structures (on separable field extensions) and skew bracoids of degree
n
, compute the relevant transitive permutation groups, and recover the actions needed to construct the respective structures.

Let
N
be a group of order
n
.

Step 1:

Construct the holomorph,
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, of
N
and compute the list
t
of transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
up to conjugation.

Step 2:

Sort
t
into equivalence classes of permutation groups. That is, for
G_{1},G_{2}\in\texttt{t}
,
G_{1}\sim G_{2}
if and only if there is an isomorphism
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
such that
\phi(\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N}))=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N})
.

Step 3:

For each equivalence class obtained in Step 2, compute the number of associated Hopf–Galois structures and skew bracoids, the number of those arising from Galois extensions / skew braces, the number of almost classical(ly Galois) structures, and the number of associated Hopf–Galois structures which admit a bijective correspondence.

We now elaborate further on Step 2 and Step 3.

3.1.
Step 2

We first note that as a separable extension may admit Hopf–Galois structures of different types the equivalence classes may in fact contain transitive subgroups of holomorphs of several groups. That is, for two (possibly distinct) groups
N_{1},N_{2}
of order
n
, what we actually check is whether two groups
G_{1}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{1})
and
G_{2}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
are isomorphic via an isomorphism taking
\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N_{1}})
to
\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N_{2}})
. Therefore given a separable extension
L/K
with Galois closure
E/K
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\cong G_{1}
and
\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)\cong\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N_{1}})
, we have that
L/K
admits Hopf–Galois structures of both types
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
, and this can be clearly seen in the data that the algorithm produces.

From the perspective of skew bracoids, this simply means that there are transitive actions
\odot_{1}:G_{1}\to N_{1}
and
\odot_{2}:G_{2}\to N_{2}
such that
(G_{1},N_{1},\odot_{1})
and
(G_{2},N_{2},\odot_{2})
are skew bracoids with
(G_{1},\odot_{1})
isomorphic to
(G_{2},\odot_{2})
as a permutation group. We note that by the discussions in Sections
2.1
and
2.3
, and the fact that the groups are being considered up to conjugacy, the permutation groups explicitly given will correspond to non-isomorphic skew bracoids.

We finally remark that in order to test whether
G_{1}\sim G_{2}
, we first ask Magma whether
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are isomorphic as abstract groups. In the affirmative, Magma provides an isomorphism
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
. However, even if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are isomorphic as permutation groups, it might be that
f
does not send
G_{1}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{1}}(1_{N_{1}})
to
G_{2}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{2}}(1_{N_{2}})
. In theory, we can then ask whether there is a
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_{2})
such that
(\psi\circ f)(G_{1}^{\prime})=G_{2}^{\prime}
, as this is equivalent to running over all the isomorphisms between
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. However, as automorphism groups are a fundamentally different object to groups in Magma, we are actually unable to loop through the elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(G_{2})
. Instead, the set of images,
Gprime
, of
G_{2}^{\prime}
under
\mathrm{Aut}(G_{2})
is computed (we say a little more about this in the following section), and hence we ask if
f(G_{1}^{\prime})
lies in
Gprime
. If so, then
f(G_{1}^{\prime})=\psi(G_{2}^{\prime})
for some
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_{2})
, and hence
\phi:=\psi^{-1}\circ f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
is the required isomorphism of permutation groups.

3.2.
Step 3

The majority of the mechanics of this step are described in
[
CS20
,
CS21
]
.

We recall that the number of (equivalence classes of) skew bracoids
(G,N)
where
G
is a transitive permutation group (i.e. with given stabiliser) is precisely the number of transitive subgroups
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
isomorphic (as a permutation group) to
G
up to conjugacy. The number of those which are essentially skew braces are those which arise from regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. To count the number of Hopf–Galois structures with group
G
of type
N
on Galois extensions of degree
n
, we have to count
all
regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
isomorphic to
G
(that is, we sum the number of subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
in the equivalence class of
G
computed in Step 2 for a fixed group
N
, accounting also for the sizes of the conjugacy classes), and multiply this count by
|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|/|\mathrm{Aut}(N)|
.

For Hopf–Galois structures (with group
G
of type
N
) on separable but non-normal extensions, we need to compute
|\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})|
. We recall that

\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})=\{\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)\mid\theta(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime}\}.

As described previously, we cannot simply loop over elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
for computations in Magma. Instead, one can essentially ‘trick’ Magma by forming the permutation representation of
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, forming the set
Gprime
described previously, and then playing with actions to eventually obtain a set corresponding to
\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})
. The function used to do this was written by Derek Holt and used in the algorithm presented in Section 2 of
[
CS21
]
(they call it ‘Automorphisms’, we call it ‘NumOfAutomorphisms’). We do not give further details here, but direct the credit to where it is due! The number of such Hopf–Galois structures is thus the (total) number of subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
in the equivalence class of
G
for a fixed
N
, multiplied by the factor
|\mathrm{Aut}(G,G^{\prime})|/|\mathrm{Aut}(N)|
.

To obtain the number of Hopf–Galois structures admitting a bijective correspondence, we need to compute the number of intermediate fields of
L/K
(this is done using the classical Galois correspondence by computing the number of subgroups of
G
containing
G^{\prime}
), and then asking how many Hopf subalgebras of
(E[N])^{G}
there are (this is done by asking how many subgroups
H
of
N
have the property that
G\leq\mathrm{Norm}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(H)
). When these numbers match, the corresponding Hopf–Galois structure admits a bijective correspondence.

Finally, of the transitive subgroups corresponding to Hopf–Galois structures admitting a bijective correspondence, we may ask if their structure is almost classical(ly Galois). That is, given a transitive subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, the corresponding structure is almost classical(ly Galois) if and only if
\mathrm{Cent}_{S_{n}}(N)\leq G
. For an explanation as to why, we refer to the discussion at the end of Section
2.2
and the fact that
N^{\text{opp}}
is precisely the centraliser of
N
in
S_{n}
.

3.3.
Recovering the action on
N
.

Let
N
be a finite group of order, say,
n
. The holomorph of
N
is viewed by Magma as a permutation group of
degree

n
. We therefore need some way of recovering the action
\cdot
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
on
N
, as Magma does not explicitly give this to us. In what follows, we let
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
be transitive on
N
via an (internally given) action
\triangleright
. We will define a (explicit) transitive action
\cdot
of
M
on
N
and use this to obtain the relevant Hopf–Galois structures (HGS) and skew bracoids (SB) as follows:

HGS:

Let
L/K
be a separable extension of degree
n
and let
E/K
be its Galois closure. Define
J:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
and
J^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)
, and suppose there is an isomorphism
\phi:J\to M
such that
\phi(J^{\prime})=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})
. Given
\cdot
, we will show how to realise
N
as a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Perm}(J/J^{\prime})
normalised by
\lambda(J)
, giving the Hopf–Galois structure
E[N]^{J}
on
L/K
.

SB:

Let
G
be a group such that there is a homomorphism
\delta:G\to\mathrm{Hol}(N)
with
\delta(G)=M
. Given
\cdot
, we can construct an action
\odot
of
G
on
N
by

g\odot\eta:=\delta(g)\cdot\eta\;\;\forall g\in G,\forall\eta\in N.

We will show that
(G,N,\odot)
is indeed a skew bracoid.

We start by considering the following two homomorphisms:

1.

The natural embedding
f:N\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hol}(N)
,

2.

The map
h:\mathrm{Hol}(N)\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
given by mapping the generators of
f(N)
to
1_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}
and the generators of
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
to themselves.

Here,
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
is the stabiliser of
1_{N}
with respect to
\triangleright
. We note also that
f
can be viewed as the left regular representation map of
N
into
\mathrm{Perm}(N)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x\in\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Then
f^{-1}(x)
is well-defined.

Proof.

We may see
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
as the internal semi-direct product
f(N)\cdot\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
. So an element
x\in\mathrm{Hol}(N)
has the form
\eta\alpha
for some
\mu\in f(N)
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
. Thus
f^{-1}(x)=f^{-1}(\mu)
with
\mu\in f(N)
.
∎

We now define the map
\cdot:M\times N\to N
as follows:

x\cdot\eta:=f^{-1}(xf(\eta)(h(x))^{-1}),\;\;\forall x\in M,\forall\eta\in N.

Note that, by Lemma
3.1
,
\cdot
is well-defined.

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
\cdot
gives a transitive action of
M
on
N
.

Proof.

It is easily checked that
\cdot
does indeed give an action of
M
on
N
. We show that this action is transitive by considering the orbit of
1_{N}
. In particular, let
x\in M
, then
x\cdot 1_{N}=f^{-1}(xh(x)^{-1})
. We again split
x
into
f(\mu)\alpha
for some
\mu\in N
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
, but then note that
\alpha
is precisely
h(x)
. So
x\cdot 1_{N}=f^{-1}(f(\mu)h(x)h(x)^{-1})=f^{-1}(f(\mu))=\mu
. So, for any
\eta\in N
, we need to show that there is an
x\in M
such that
x=f(\eta)h(x)
. To do this, recall the action
\triangleright
that Magma uses to view
M
as a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Then
x\triangleright 1_{N}=f(\mu)h(x)\triangleright 1_{N}=f(\mu)\triangleright(h(x)\triangleright 1_{N})=f(\mu)\triangleright 1_{N}
. As
M
is transitive on
N
via
\triangleright
, we must have that the orbit of
1_{N}
under
\triangleright
has size
|N|
. So as
|\{f(\mu)|x=f(\mu)h(x)\;\text{for some }x\in M\}|\leq|N|
, we must therefore have equality.
∎

Now, given a separable extension
L/K
and the isomorphism
\phi
as above, we first set
\overline{\phi}
to be given by
\overline{\phi}(gJ^{\prime})=\phi(g)\cdot(1_{N})
for all
g\in J
. We may then identify
N
with the image of the map
\alpha:N\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Perm}(X)
given by
\alpha(\eta)=\overline{\beta}\eta\overline{\beta}^{-1}
for all
\eta\in N
. This follows the bijection given by Byott in
[
Byo96
]
and shows how we may explicitly recover the related Hopf–Galois structure on
L/K
.

Remark 3.3
.

We note that, by the discussion in Section 5 of
[
MLT24
]
, we need not require
E
to be the Galois closure of
L/K
but we can simply ask for
E/K
to be any Galois extension containing
L/K
. However, we only present the Galois closure case here for simplicity.

We now recall the group
G
which is such that there is a surjective homomorphism
\delta:G\to M
, and an action
\odot:G\times N\to N
defined using
\cdot
as above by:

g\odot\eta:=f^{-1}(\delta(g)f(\eta)h(\delta(g))^{-1})\;\;\forall g\in G,\forall\eta\in N.

We note that in the case that
\delta
is bijective, the corresponding skew bracoid will be reduced.

Proposition 3.4
.

The triple
(G,N,\odot)
is a skew bracoid.

Proof.

We will give two proofs. In the first proof, view
\mathrm{Hol}(N)=N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N)
as an external semidirect product of
N
and
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. The maps
\delta,f
and
h
have well-defined interpretations which can then be used to show that
\odot
is indeed the required action.

The second proof does not rely on the explicit interpretation of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
but shows instead that
\odot
is composed of a surjective 1-cocycle from
G
to
N
and an action of
G
on
N
by automorphisms. The proof of Theorem 2.8 of
[
MLT24
]
thus completes the argument.

Proof 1:

Suppose that
\mathrm{Hol}(N)=N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. Thus the map
\delta:G\to M
takes an element
g\in G
to, say,
(\eta,\alpha)\in M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for some
\eta\in N
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. The embedding
f:N\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is thus given by
\mu\mapsto(\mu,\mathrm{id}_{N})
, and the map
h:\mathrm{Hol}(N)\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hol}(N)}(1_{N})
is given by
(\mu,\beta)\mapsto(1,\beta)
.

For
\mu,\nu\in N
, we therefore make the following computations:

\displaystyle g\odot\mu

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(g)f(\mu)h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}((\eta,\alpha)(\mu,\mathrm{id})(1,\alpha^{-1}))

\displaystyle=f^{-1}((\eta+\alpha(\mu),\mathrm{id}))

\displaystyle=\eta+\alpha(\mu)\in N.

It is therefore clear that
\odot
is transitive as
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is transitive.

\displaystyle g\odot(\mu+\nu)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}((\eta,\alpha)(\mu,\mathrm{id})(\nu,\mathrm{id})(1,\alpha^{-1}))

\displaystyle=f^{-1}((\eta+\alpha(\mu)+\alpha(\nu),\mathrm{id}))

\displaystyle=\eta+\alpha(\mu+\nu).

Finally

\displaystyle(g\odot\mu)-(g\odot 1_{N})+(g\odot\nu)

\displaystyle=(\eta+\alpha(\mu))-(\eta+\alpha(1_{N}))+(\eta+\alpha(\nu))

\displaystyle=\eta+\alpha(\mu)-\eta+\eta+\alpha(\nu)

\displaystyle=\eta+\alpha(\mu+\nu).

Therefore the skew bracoid relation is satisfied.

Proof 2:

For
\mu\in N
, we define the maps
\pi,\gamma_{\mu}:G\to N
given by:

\displaystyle\pi(g):=f^{-1}(\delta(g)h(\delta(g))^{-1}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}(g):=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))f(\mu)h(\delta(g))^{-1}).

We claim that
\pi
is a surjective 1-cocycle, and, for all
g\in G
, that the map
\gamma
given by
g\mapsto(\gamma(g)(\mu)=\gamma_{\mu}(g))
for
\mu\in N
is a homomorphism from
G
to
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
(that is
\gamma:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(N)
defines an action of
G
on
N
by automorphisms so we can think of
\gamma_{\mu}(g)
as
\gamma(g)\cdot\mu
). In what follows, let
g,k\in G
and
\eta,\mu\in N
.

We first prove our claim about
\gamma
.

Sub-claim 1:
\gamma(g)\in\mathrm{End}(N)
. We show
\gamma_{\eta+\mu}(g)=\gamma_{\eta}(g)+\gamma_{\mu}(g)
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\eta+\mu}(g)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))f(\eta+\mu)h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))(f(\eta)+f(\mu))h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))f(\eta)h(\delta(g))^{-1})+f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))f(\mu)h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\eta}(g)+\gamma_{\mu}(g)

Sub-claim 2:
\gamma(g)
is bijective. The injectivity of
\gamma(g)
follows from the injectivity of
f
. If
\eta:=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))^{-1}f(\mu)h(\delta(g)))
, then
\gamma_{\eta}(g)=\mu
and so
\gamma(g)
is surjective.

Sub-claim 3:
\gamma(g\circ k)=\gamma(g)\gamma(k)
.

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}(g\circ k)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g\circ k))f(\mu)h(\delta(g\circ k))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(h(\delta(g)\delta(k)f(\mu)h(\delta(g)\delta(k))^{-1})

\displaystyle=h(\delta(g))h(\delta(k))f(\mu)h(\delta(k))^{-1}h(\delta(g))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{h(\delta(k))f(\mu)h(\delta(k))^{-1}}(g)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\gamma_{\mu}(k)}(g)

We now show that
\pi:G\to N
is a surjective 1-cocycle.

Sub-claim 4:
\pi
is a 1-cocycle. We compute the following:

\displaystyle\pi(gk)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(gk)h(\delta(gk))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(g)\delta(k)h(\delta(k))^{-1}h(\delta(g))^{-1}).

Additionally:

\displaystyle\pi(g)(\gamma_{\pi(k)}(g))

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(g)h(\delta(g))^{-1})f^{-1}(h(\delta(g))f(\pi(k))h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(g)f(\pi(k))h(\delta(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=f^{-1}(\delta(g)\delta(k)h(\delta(k))^{-1}h(\delta(g))^{-1}.

We therefore see that
\pi(gk)=\pi(g)(\gamma_{\pi(k)}(g))
and so
\pi
is a 1-cocycle.

Sub-claim 5:
\pi
is surjective. To show this, we first consider when we have
\pi(g)=\pi(k)
, which is precisely when
\delta(k^{-1}g)=h(\delta(k^{-1}g))
. Supposing
x=\delta(g)
and
y=\delta(k)
, we have
y^{-1}x=h(y)^{-1}h(x)
, and so
\pi(g)=\pi(k)
exactly when
x
and
y
differ by an element in
h(M)=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})
. Now, as
M
is transitive on
N
, we have that
|M|=|N||\mathrm{Stab_{M}(1_{N})|}
, hence

|\{\pi(g)\mid g\in G\}|=|M|/|\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})|=|N|,

and so
\pi
is surjective.

Finally, we note that the map
\odot
is precisely the combination of these. That is

g\odot\mu=\pi(g)(\gamma_{\mu}(g)).

Thus by the proof of Theorem 2.8 of
[
MLT24
]
, we have that
\odot
indeed gives a transitive action of
G
on
N
such that
(G,N,\odot)
is a skew bracoid.
∎

4.
Enumerations and results

In this section, we present some of the Hopf–Galois structure and skew bracoid enumerations given by the algorithm described in Section
3
, in addition to proving some results about transitive subgroups of degree
2pq
for
p,q
distinct odd primes.

We note that, although there are technically no bounds to the degree of transitive subgroup our algorithm can work with, the computer appears to struggle in cases where there are large elementary abelian groups nested inside. This in particular, and perhaps unsurprisingly, includes many of the groups of order
32,48,64,80
and
96
. An approach to the enumeration of structures of these types may be possible by using techniques from linear algebra and exploiting the affine nature of the groups that arise. However, such an algorithm would be sufficiently different to our current approach that it would constitute its own project.

4.2.
Results

In this section, we give some results on Hopf–Galois structures and skew bracoids of degree
2pq
for
p,q
distinct odd primes. We will also present a conjecture involving the enumerations of Hopf–Galois structures and skew bracoids of non-abelian simple group type. These results and observations arise from the data produced by the algorithm described in Section
3
. Here, we will not focus on enumeration, but will instead focus on when a group
G
can be transitively embedded into the holomorph,
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, of another group
N
. Recalling the discussion in Section
2
, this is equivalent to the existence of a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
with group
G
and a skew bracoid of the form
(G,N)
.

To this end, a statement of the form

If
G
is a group which can be transitively embedded into
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, then
G
can also be transitively embedded into
\mathrm{Hol}(N^{\prime})

may be interpreted in the following two ways:

\bullet

Let
L/K
be a separable extension of degree
|N|
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\cong G
. If
L/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
, then it also admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N^{\prime}
.

\bullet

If the triple
(G,N,\odot)
is a skew bracoid, then there is a transitive action
\odot^{\prime}
such that
(G,N^{\prime},\odot^{\prime})
is a skew bracoid.

We will therefore focus on statements about transitive embeddings, with the idea that one may draw the relevant conclusions for both Hopf–Galois structures and skew bracoids.

4.2.1.
Transitive subgroups of degree
2pq

Let
p>q
be odd primes. If
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{q}
, then there are four groups of order
2pq
. These are
\mathrm{C}_{2pq},\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q},\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p}
and
\mathrm{D}_{pq}
. In the case that
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
, we have two further groups. These are
\mathrm{C}_{2}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})
and
\mathrm{D}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q}=C_{p}\rtimes C_{2q}
. We use the following presentations:

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{1}&:=\mathrm{C}_{2pq}=\langle x_{1}\mid x_{1}^{2pq}=1\rangle,\\
N_{2}&:=\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q}=\langle x_{2},r_{2},s_{2}\mid x_{2}^{p}=r_{2}^{q}=s_{2}^{2}=1,s_{2}r_{2}s_{2}=r_{2}^{-1},s_{2}x_{2}s_{2}=r_{2}x_{2}r_{2}^{-1}=x_{2}\rangle,\\
N_{3}&:=\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p}=\langle x_{3},r_{3},s_{3}\mid x_{3}^{q}=r_{3}^{p}=s_{3}^{2}=1,s_{3}r_{3}s_{3}=r_{3}^{-1},s_{3}x_{3}s_{3}=r_{3}x_{3}r_{3}^{-1}=x_{3}\rangle.\\
N_{4}&:=\mathrm{D}_{pq}=\langle r_{4},s_{4}\mid r_{4}^{pq}=s_{4}^{2}=1,s_{4}r_{4}s_{4}=r_{4}^{-1}\rangle.\end{split}

Now suppose that
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
and let
k
have order
q
modulo
p
. We further write:

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{5}&:=\mathrm{C}_{2}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})=\langle x_{5},r_{5},s_{5}\mid x_{5}^{2}=r_{5}^{p}=s_{5}^{q}=1,s_{5}r_{5}s_{5}^{-1}=r_{5}^{k},s_{5}x_{5}s_{5}^{-1}=r_{5}x_{5}r_{5}^{-1}=x_{5}\rangle,\\
N_{6}&:=\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{2q}=\langle r_{6},s_{6}\mid r_{6}^{p}=s_{6}^{2q}=1,s_{6}r_{6}s_{6}^{-1}=r_{6}^{-k}\rangle.\end{split}

The automorphism groups of
N_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq 6
are described below:

1)

We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{1})
with the elements
\sigma_{1}
and
\rho_{1}
, where
\sigma_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{a_{p}},\rho_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{a_{q}}
, and
a_{p}
and
a_{q}
have orders
p-1
and
q-1
respectively
\mod{2pq}
. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{1})|=(p-1)(q-1)
.

2)

We note that
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{2})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{p})\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{D}_{q})
. We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{2})
with the elements
\sigma_{2},\phi_{2},\psi_{b,2}
, where
\sigma_{2}(x_{2})=x_{2}^{a}
,
\phi_{2}(s_{2})=r_{2}s_{2}
and
\psi_{b,2}(r_{2})=r_{2}^{b}
such that
a
and
b
have orders
p-1\mod{p}
and
q-1\mod{q}
respectively, and the maps act as identity on the other generators. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{2})|=q(p-1)(q-1)
.

3)

We note that
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{3})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{q})\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{D}_{p})
. We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{3})
with the elements
\sigma_{3},\phi_{3},\psi_{b,3}
, where
\sigma_{3}(x_{3})=x_{3}^{a}
,
\phi_{3}(s_{3})=r_{3}s_{3}
and
\psi_{b,3}(r_{3})=r_{3}^{b}
such that
a
and
b
have orders
q-1\mod{q}
and
p-1\mod{p}
respectively, and the maps act as identity on the other generators. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{3})|=p(p-1)(q-1)
.

4)

We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{4})
with the elements
\phi_{4},\psi_{b_{p},4},\psi_{b_{q},4}
, where
\phi_{4}(s_{4})=r_{4}s_{4}
,
\psi_{b_{p},4}(r_{4})=r_{4}^{b_{p}}
and
\psi_{b_{q},4}(r_{4})=r_{4}^{b_{q}}
such that
b_{p}
and
b_{q}
have orders
p-1
and
q-1\mod{pq}
respectively, and the maps act as identity on the other generators. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{4})|=pq(p-1)(q-1)
.

5)

We note that
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{5})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{2})\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})
. We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{5})
with the elements
\phi_{5}
and
\psi_{b,5}
, where
\phi_{5}(s_{5})=r_{5}s_{5}
and
\psi_{b,5}(r_{5})=r_{5}^{b}
such that
b
has order
p-1\mod{p}
, and the maps act as identity on the other generators. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{5})|=p(p-1)
.

6)

We can generate
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{6})
with the elements
\phi_{6}
and
\psi_{b,6}
, where
\phi_{6}(s_{6})=r_{6}s_{6}
and
\psi_{b,6}(r_{6})=r_{6}^{b}
such that
b
has order
p-1\mod{p}
, and the maps act as identity on the other generators. So
|\mathrm{Aut}(N_{6})|=p(p-1)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group.

\bullet

If
G
transitively embeds in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
, then
G
also embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q}),\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{pq})
and
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
.

\bullet

If
G
transitively embeds in either
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q})
or
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p})
, then
G
also embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
.

Proof.

For
i=2,3,4
, we start by giving examples of regular subgroups
M_{i}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
which are isomorphic to
C_{2pq}
and whose normalisers in
\mathrm{Perm}(N_{i})
are contained in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
. In particular, we see that
|\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})|=2pq(p-1)(q-1)
, so if we find a normaliser of
M_{i}
inside
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
of this size, then we are done.

Consider the following regular subgroups
M_{i}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
such that
M_{i}\cong\mathrm{C}_{2pq}
:

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle:=\langle x_{2},r_{2},(s_{2},\psi_{-1,2})\rangle,

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle:=\langle x_{3},r_{3},(s_{3},\psi_{-1,3})\rangle,

\displaystyle M_{4}

\displaystyle:=\langle r_{4},(s_{4},\psi_{-1,4})\rangle,

where
\psi_{-1,i}
is a power of
\psi_{b,i}
such that
\psi_{-1,i}(r_{i})=r_{i}^{-1}
. It is then clear that the subgroup
\langle x_{2},r_{2},s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
normalises
M_{2}
, the subgroup
\langle x_{3},r_{3},s_{3},\sigma_{3},\psi_{b,3}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{3})
normalises
M_{3}
, and the subgroup
\langle s_{4},r_{4},\psi_{b,4}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{4})
normalises
M_{4}
. All of these normalisers have order
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
.

We now give examples of regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(D_{pq})
isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q}
and
\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p}
respectively, such that their normalisers in
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
are contained in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
. Namely:

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q}

\displaystyle\cong J_{2}:=\langle r_{4},(s_{4},\psi_{c,4})\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p}

\displaystyle\cong J_{3}:=\langle r_{4},(s_{4},\psi_{d,4})\rangle,

where
c
and
d
are powers of
b
such that
c\equiv-1\pmod{p}
and
c\equiv 1\pmod{q}
, and
d\equiv 1\pmod{p}
and
d\equiv-1\pmod{q}
. It is then clear that the subgroup
\langle r_{4},s_{4},\psi_{b,4},\phi_{4}^{p}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
of order
2pq^{2}(p-1)(q-1)
normalises
J_{2}
and the subgroup
\langle r_{4},s_{4},\psi_{b,4},\phi_{4}^{q}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq})
of order
2p^{2}q(p-1)(q-1)
normalises
J_{3}
. Noting finally that
|\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q})|=2pq^{2}(p-1)(q-1)
and
|\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p})|=2p^{2}q(p-1)(q-1)
, we are done.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group.

\bullet

If
G
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q})
but not in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
, then
G
does not embed transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p})
.

\bullet

If
G
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p})
but not in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
, then
G
does not embed transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q})
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a group which embeds transitively in both
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
and
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{3})
. We wish to show that
G
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
. Firstly, as
|\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})|=2pq^{2}(p-1)(q-1)
and
|\mathrm{Hol}(N_{3})|=2p^{2}q(p-1)(q-1)
, we must have that the order of
G
divides
2pq(p-1)(q-1)=|\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})|
. We will show that the only transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
of order
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
are isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
. Then if, say,
H
is a transitive subgroup of order dividing
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
(and hence has order divisible by
2pq
), we can extend
H
to a subgroup of order
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
by adding the appropriate generators of
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{2})
to
H
. Hence, given the uniqueness (up to isomorphism) of subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
of order
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
, any transitive subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
of order dividing
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
must be a subgroup of a group isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2pq})
, and hence the claim is proved.

Recall that

\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})=\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p})=\langle x_{2},r_{2},s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2},\phi_{2}\rangle,

where
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q})=\langle x_{2},\sigma\rangle
of order
q(q-1)
and
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{p})=\langle r_{2},s_{2},\psi_{b,2},\phi_{2}\rangle
of order
2p^{2}(p-1)
. Let
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
be a subgroup of order
2pq(p-1)(q-1)
. As
P:=\langle r_{2},\phi\rangle
is the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
,
G
must contain a subgroup of
P
of order
p
, which is hence either
\langle\phi_{2}\rangle
or of the form
\langle(r_{2},\phi_{2}^{u})\rangle
for some
0\leq u\leq p-1
. The transitivity of
G
allows us to discard
\langle\phi_{2}\rangle
and hence
G
contains
\langle(r_{2},\phi_{2}^{u})\rangle
for some
u
. Further,
G
must have a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup conjugate to
\langle x_{2},s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle
. Up to conjugacy, therefore, we have

G=\langle(r_{2},\phi_{2}^{u}),x_{2},s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle

for some
0\leq u\leq p-1
. Finally, we note that, unless
u=0
or
u=p-2
, then conjugating
(r_{2},\phi_{2}^{u})
by
s_{2}
allows us to show that
\phi_{2}\in G
and hence
p^{2}
divides
|G|
, giving a contradiction.

We conclude with the following two possibilities for
G
(up to conjugation):

(i)

G=\langle x_{2},r_{2},s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle=\langle x_{2},r_{2},(s_{2},\psi_{-1,2}),\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle\cong\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq}),
or

(ii)

G=\langle x_{2},(r_{2},\phi_{2}^{-2}),s_{2},\sigma_{2},\psi_{b,2}\rangle\cong\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq})
.

∎

Now suppose that
p\equiv 1\pmod{q}
. In this case, we recall that we have six groups of order
2pq
. Recall that
N_{5}=\mathrm{C}_{2}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})
and
N_{6}=\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{2q}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
i\in\{5,6\}
. If
G
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
, then for any
j\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\setminus\{i\}
,
G
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{j})
if and only if
|G|
divides
2pq(p-1)
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a group that embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
for
i=5
or
i=6
.

Suppose first that
G
also embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{j})
for some
j\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\setminus\{i\}
. By Proposition
4.2
, we need only assume that
j\in\{4,5,6\}
. In this case, it is clear that
|G|
divides
2p^{2}q(p-1)=\gcd(|\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})|,|\mathrm{Hol}(N_{j})|)
. In particular, we wish to show that
G
cannot contain a subgroup of order
p^{2}
. Suppose otherwise, then letting
M_{j}
be the image of the transitive embedding of
G
into
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{j})
, we must have that
P:=\langle r_{j}^{q},\phi_{j}^{q}\rangle\leq M_{j}
as it is the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup (which has order
p^{2}
) in each case.

We now make two claims:

Claim 1: Let
j,j^{\prime}\in\{4,5,6\}
. Then, for any
A_{j}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\langle r_{j}^{q}\rangle)
and
A_{j^{\prime}}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\langle r_{j^{\prime}}^{q}\rangle)
such that
|A_{j}|=|A_{j^{\prime}}|
, we have
M_{j}\rtimes A_{j}\cong M_{j^{\prime}}\rtimes A_{j^{\prime}}
.

Claim 2: There is no transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{4})
isomorphic to either
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
or
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{6})
.

In the case that
A_{5}=\mathrm{Aut}(\langle r_{5}\rangle)
and
A_{6}=\mathrm{Aut}(\langle r_{6}\rangle)
, then if
M_{5}\cong M_{6}
, claim 1 tells us that
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})=M_{5}\rtimes A_{5}\cong M_{6}\rtimes A_{6}=\mathrm{Hol}(N_{6})
, which gives a contradiction. So we must have that
M_{5}\not\cong M_{6}
. Thus both claims together show that
M_{j}
are pairwise non-isomorphic for
j\in\{4,5,6\}
.

Claim 1 follows from the fact that every element of
A_{j}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\langle r_{j}^{q}\rangle)\cong C_{p-1}
acts trivially on every element of
M_{j}/\langle r_{j}^{q},\phi_{j}^{q}\rangle
. In other words, let
\alpha:M_{j}\to M_{j^{\prime}}
and
\beta:A_{j}\to A_{j^{\prime}}
be isomorphisms. Then we can define
f:M_{j}\rtimes A_{j}\to M_{j^{\prime}}\rtimes A_{j^{\prime}}
by
f((m_{j},a_{j}))=(\alpha(m_{j}),\beta(a_{j}))
. Let
z_{j},z_{j^{\prime}}
generate
A_{j},A_{j^{\prime}}
respectively, so that
\beta(z_{j})=z_{j^{\prime}}
. To show that
f
is an isomorphism, it is enough to show that

(4)

x_{j^{\prime}}(\alpha(m_{j}))=\alpha(z_{j}(m_{j}))

for all generators
m_{j}
of
M_{j}
. Now, for
m_{j}
not of order
p
, we have that
\alpha(m_{j}),\alpha(z_{j}(m_{j}))\notin\langle r_{j}^{q},\phi_{j}^{q}\rangle
, so (
4
) holds. For
m_{j}\in P_{j}:=\langle r_{j}^{q},\phi_{j}^{q}\rangle
, we note that
z_{j}(m_{j})=m_{j}^{d}
for some
1\leq d\leq p-1
. Given that
\alpha(P_{j})=\alpha(P_{j^{\prime}})
and that
P_{j},P_{j^{\prime}}
are abelian, we again satisfy (
4
).

We now prove Claim 2. We note that
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
contains the subgroup
\langle x_{5},s_{5},\psi_{b,5}\rangle
and
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{6})
contains the subgroup
\langle s_{6},\psi_{b,6}\rangle
. Each of these subgroups is isomorphic to
C_{2q}\times C_{q}
and transitive on
N_{j}/\langle r_{j}\rangle
. We claim that
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{4})
has no such subgroup. For a contradiction, assume that
M_{4}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{4})
is transitive such that it contains a subgroup
J_{4}\cong C_{2q}\times C_{q}
which is transitive on
\langle r_{4}^{p},s_{4}\rangle\cong N_{4}/\langle r_{4}^{q}\rangle
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
r_{4}^{q},\phi_{4}^{q}\notin J_{4}
, as they are known generators of
M_{4}
of order
p
. Abusing notation then, we will denote by
r_{4}
and
\phi_{4}
the elements
r_{4}^{p}
and
\phi_{4}^{p}
. An element in
J_{4}
of order
q
has the form
(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c})
. We claim that two such elements, say
(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c}),(r_{4}^{a^{\prime}},\phi_{4}^{b^{\prime}}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c^{\prime}})
of this form commute if and only if either one is a power of the other or
c=c^{\prime}=0
. Indeed, we see that the equations

\displaystyle a(1-b_{p}^{c^{\prime}})=a^{\prime}(1-b_{p}^{c}),

\displaystyle b(1-b_{p}^{c^{\prime}})=b^{\prime}(1-b_{p}^{c})

must be satisfied. If
c=0
but
c^{\prime}\neq 0
, we have that
a=b=c=0
, which contradicts the fact that
(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c})
is an element of order
q
. A similar argument holds for
c\neq 0
and
c^{\prime}=0
. Suppose now then that
c,c^{\prime}\neq 0
, then we observe that

(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c})^{x}=(r_{4}^{aX},\phi_{4}^{bX}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{cx}),

where
X:=(1-b_{p}^{cx})/(1-b_{p}^{c})
. If we take
x=c^{\prime}/c
, then we see that
(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c})^{x}=(r_{4}^{a^{\prime}},\phi_{4}^{b^{\prime}}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{c^{\prime}})
. The subgroup of
J_{4}
isomorphic to
C_{q}\times C_{q}
must therefore be generated by
(r_{4}^{a},\phi_{4}^{b})
and
(r_{4}^{a^{\prime}},\phi_{4}^{b^{\prime}})
for some
0\leq a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\leq p-1
. Now, we consider an element
y_{4}
of order 2 in
M_{4}
. This has the form
(r_{4}^{a}s_{4}^{b},\phi_{4}^{c}\psi_{b_{p},4}^{d}\psi_{b_{q},4}^{e})
, where
a,b,c,d,e
are appropriately chosen so that
y_{4}^{2}=1_{J_{4}}
. We check the relations needed for
y_{4}
to commute with an element
(r_{4}^{u},\phi_{4}^{v})
of order
q
. This gives us the following equations:

(5)

\displaystyle u(1-(-1)^{b}b_{q}^{e})+v(1-(-1)^{b})/2=0,

(6)

\displaystyle v(1-b_{q}^{e})=0.

From (
6
) we must have either
v=0
or
e=0
. If
v=0
, then by (
5
), we get either
u=0
or
e=0
. Now if
u=0
, the element
(r_{4}^{u},\phi_{4}^{v})
is trivial and does not have order
q
, which is a contradiction. So we must have
e=0
. Given
e=0
, therefore, if
b\neq 0
(i.e.
b=1
), we obtain
2u+v=0
, but then if
y_{4}
is to commute with all elements in the subgroup
C_{q}\times C_{q}
, this relation is imposed on both generators, but then one would be a power of the other. So we must have
b=0
. However, in this case,
J_{4}
fails to be transitive on
\langle r_{4},s_{4}\rangle
. Therefore there is no transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{4})
isomorphic to either
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
or
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{6})
.

Consider now the following transitive subgroups:

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{1}&:=\langle x_{1},\sigma_{1}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2pq}),\\
M_{2}&:=\langle x_{2},r_{2},(s_{2},\psi_{-1,2}),\sigma_{2}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\times\mathrm{D}_{q}),\\
M_{3}&:=\langle x_{3},(r_{3},\phi_{3}^{-2}),s_{3},\psi_{b,3}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{q}\times\mathrm{D}_{p}),\\
M_{4}&:=\langle(r_{4},\phi_{4}^{-2}),s_{4},\psi_{b,4}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{D}_{pq}),\\
M_{5}&:=\langle x_{5},s_{5},(r_{5},\psi_{k^{-1},5}),\psi_{b,5}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{2}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{q})),\\
M_{6}&:=\langle s_{6},(r_{6},\psi_{k^{-1},6}),\psi_{b,6}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{2q}).\end{split}

Then
M_{i}\cong\mathrm{C}_{2q}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{p-1})
and is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq 6
. In each case, the stabiliser of
1_{N_{i}}
is either
\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle
or
\langle\psi_{b,i}\rangle
; it is therefore clear to see that each of the
M_{i}
are isomorphic as permutation groups. Hence if
M\leq M_{i}
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
, then
M
also embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{j})
for each
1\leq j\leq 6
.

We claim that the only transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
of order
2pq(p-1)
are isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{2q}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{p-1})
. Then if, say,
H
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
of order

(where
d
is a divisor of
(p-1)
), we can extend

to a subgroup of order
2pq(p-1)
by adding the appropriate generators of
\mathrm{Aut}(N_{5})
to

. Hence, given the uniqueness (up to isomorphism) of subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
of order
2pq(p-1)
, any transitive subgroup
H\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
with
|H|\leq 2pq(p-1)
must be a subgroup of a group isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{2q}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{p-1})
. Further, by (
7
), we then have that
H
embeds transitively in
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq 6
.

Let
M
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N_{5})
of order
2pq(p-1)
. Then any Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
M
must be conjugate to
\langle x_{5},s_{5},\psi_{b,5}\rangle
, and any Sylow
p
-subgroup of

must be contained in
\langle r_{5},\phi_{5}\rangle
. For

to be transitive, it therefore must contain
(r_{5},\phi_{5}^{u})
for some

. Up to conjugacy, therefore,

. Note then that, as
s_{5}(r_{5},\phi_{5}^{u})s_{5}^{-1}=r_{5}^{k(1+u)-1}
, unless

or

, we have that

, which gives a contradiction. Finally, we see that both
\langle r_{5},x_{5},s_{5},\psi_{b,5}\rangle
and
\langle(r_{5},\phi_{5}^{u}),s_{5},x_{5},\psi_{b,5}\rangle

are isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}_{2q}\times(\mathrm{C}_{p}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_{p-1})
.
∎

4.2.2.
Other observations

As of yet, we have not been able to observe a (non-trivial) pattern in the total number of Hopf–Galois structures on either separable or Galois extensions (or their relation to skew bracoids and skew braces). In the case that the degree
n
is a Burnside number, for example, it is clear that the number of (equivalence classes of) skew bracoids will be the same as the number of Hopf–Galois structures. This is because, as Byott shows in
[
Byo96
]
, if
N
is a group of order
n
then it is cyclic, and there is a unique transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
of any given (relevant) order. There is then a unique Hopf–Galois structure associated to that transitive subgroup, and as the number of equivalence classes of associated skew bracoids is the same as the number of conjugacy classes of isomorphic subgroups, we obtain this fairly straightforward result.

Slightly interesting patterns start to emerge, however, when one focuses on the numbers of skew bracoids. For example, the number of skew bracoids (200) of degree 44 is the same as the number of those of degree 92 (here we note that both 44 and 92 are of the form
4p
with
p\equiv 11\pmod{12}
). We also note that the number of skew bracoids of degree 30 (304) is half the number of those of degree 70 (608) (here 30 and 70 are both of the form
10p
with
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
). What makes these observations even more compelling, however, is when compared with the number of skew braces and almost classical structures. We find that the number of skew braces (29) of orders 44 and 92 are the same, as are those of orders 30 and 70 (36). We also find that the number of almost classical skew bracoids share the same relationship to their degrees’ respective total bracoid counts (70 for degrees 44 and 92, 72 for degree 30 and 144 for degree 70). Perhaps the most surprising result is that the number of almost classically Galois structures also seem to enjoy this pattern. Namely, there are 70 almost classically Galois structures of degrees 44 and 92, there are 99 of degree 30 and 198 of degree 70. For degrees 44 and 92, we also have that the number of Hopf–Galois structures giving a bijective correspondence (141) are the same. We do not yet have enough results to be able to confidently formulate these observations into a conjecture.

If one now focuses on the numbers of structures admitted by specific types, then different patterns seem to emerge. We give our next observation in the form of a conjecture:

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
N
be a non-abelian finite simple group. Then the almost classically Galois Hopf–Galois structures of type
N
are exactly the Hopf–Galois structures which admit a bijective correspondence.

One possible moral argument for this is that the outer automorphism group of a finite simple group is very small. Martin-Lyons and Truman in
[
MLT24
]
have recently linked the ideals of a skew bracoid with the image of the Hopf–Galois correspondence given by the associated Hopf–Galois structure. Therefore there should not, in general, be much room for finding Hopf–Galois structures giving a bijective correspondence which are not almost classically Galois.

Abstract
. An important implication of Rademacher’s Differentiation Theorem is that every Lipschitz curve Γ \Gamma infinitesimally looks like a line at almost all of its points in the sense that at ℋ 1 \mathcal{H}^{1} -almost every point of Γ \Gamma , the only tangent to Γ \Gamma is a straight line through the origin. In this article, we show that, in contrast, the infinitesimal structure of Hölder curves can be much more extreme. First we show that for every s > 1 s>1 there exists a ( 1 / s ) (1/s) -Hölder curve Γ s \Gamma_{s} in a Euclidean space with ℋ s ​ ( Γ s ) > 0 \mathcal{H}^{s}(\Gamma_{s})>0 such that ℋ s \mathcal{H}^{s} -almost every point of Γ s \Gamma_{s} admits infinitely many topologically distinct tangents. Second, we study the tangents of self-similar connected sets (which are canonical examples of Hölder curves) and prove that the curves Γ s \Gamma_{s} have the additional property that ℋ s \mathcal{H}^{s} -almost every point of Γ s \Gamma_{s} admits infinitely many homeomorphically distinct tangents to Γ s \Gamma_{s} which are not admitted as (not even bi-Lipschitz to) tangents to any self-similar set at typical points.

1.
Introduction

Rademacher’s Theorem, one of the most important theorems in geometric measure theory, states that every Lipschitz function defined on
[0,1]
is differentiable at
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every point of
[0,1]
. It is natural to ask whether a similar result may hold for more general functions. Calderon
[
Cal51
]
extended Rademacher’s Theorem by proving that every function in the Sobolev class
\mathcal{W}^{1,p}
with
p>1
is
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost everywhere differentiable. However, any further generalization would be futile as for each
s>1
there exists a Weierstrass function on
[0,1]
which is
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder but nowhere differentiable
[
Zyg02
]
.

A major application of Rademacher’s Theorem is towards the understanding of the “infinitesimal structure” of Lipschitz curves (i.e. Lipschitz images of
[0,1]
). To state this application, let us first define the notion of tangents. Following
[
BL15
]
, given a closed set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
x\in X
, we say that a closed set
T
is a
tangent of
X
at
x
if there exists a sequence of positive scales
r_{j}
that go to zero such that the blow-up sets
r_{j}^{-1}(X-x)
converge to the set
T
in the Attouch-Wets topology; see §
2.4
for the precise definition. Other notions of metric space convergences which produce similar tangents are known in the literature; see
[
Gro81
,
Gro99
,
DS97
]
. Another well-known notion of infinitesimal structure in geometric measure theory is that of the
tangent cone

[
Fed69
, 3.1.21]
. The tangent cone of
X
at
x
is the union of all tangents of
X
at
x
, which means that some local information is lost. We denote by
\tang(X,x)
the collection of all tangents of
X
at
x
. It is well-known that
\tang(X,x)
is nonempty and that if
T
is in
\tang(X,x)
, then
\lambda T
is in
\tang(X,x)
for every
\lambda>0
.

By Rademacher’s Theorem and by a theorem of Besicovitch
[
Bes44
]
(see also
[
Fal86
, Corollary 3.15]
), every Lipschitz curve is infinitesimally a line at
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every point. More precisely, the following is true, and we provide a proof in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is Lipschitz, then for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in f([0,1])
, there exists a straight line
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
through the origin such that
\tang(f([0,1]),x)=\{L\}
.

More generally, if
X
is the Lipschitz (or even
p
-Sobolev for
p>n
) image of
[0,1]^{n}
into some
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
m>n
, then for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in X
, the tangent space of
X
at
x
contains exactly one element which is an
n
-plane
[
BKV24
]
. Here and for the rest of this paper, given
\alpha>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(X)
the Hausdorff
\alpha
-dimensional measure of a metric space
X
.

It is worthwhile to note that while typical points of a Lipschitz curve have a simple tangent space, exceptional points may exhibit extreme behaviors. In particular, there exists a Lipschitz curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
(or in any Euclidean space of dimension at least
2
) and a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
\tang(\Gamma,x_{0})
contains
every possible tangent
; see Appendix
A
for a precise statement and the proof.

The infinitesimal structure of Lipschitz curves plays an important role in the classification of 1-rectifiable sets by Jones
[
Jon90
]
and Okikiolu
[
Oki92
]
. A feature of the proofs is the use of Jones’s
beta numbers
, which roughly measure how well a given set
E
can be approximated by lines at a given scale and location, and showing that if these values are small enough at all scales and locations then
E
can be captured in a rectifiable curve. Roughly speaking, a set
E
is contained in a Lipschitz curve if and only if at almost all points, tangent spaces are lines in a strong quantitative way. For a more complete discussion of the history of the Analyst’s Traveling Salesperson Theorem, see
[
Sch07
]
and the citations therein.

Recent years have seen great interest in obtaining a Hölder version of the Analyst’s Traveling Salesperson Theorem, that is, in characterizing all sets which are contained in a Hölder curve; see for example
[
MM93
,
Rem98
,
MM00
,
BV17
,
BNV19
,
BZ20
,
BV21
,
BS23
,
SV24
]
. This notion of “Hölder rectifiability” is greatly motivated by the fact that many fractals in analysis on metric spaces admit a Hölder parameterization but not a Lipschitz one. For example, if the attractor of an iterated function system is connected (e.g. the von Koch curve, the Sierpiński gasket, the Sierpiński carpet), then it admits a Hölder parameterization by
[0,1]
; see
[
Rem98
,
BV21
]
.

In lieu of the Lipschitz rectifiability results discussed above, a crucial step towards a comprehensive theory of Hölder rectifiable sets would be to understand the tangent spaces of Hölder curves at typical points. Unfortunately, unlike the Lipschitz case, it would be naive to expect that given
s>1
there exists one single set
T_{s}
which is the tangent of all
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder curves at typical points. For example, consider the two self-similar sets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{C}
constructed as follows. Set
S_{0}=T
where
T
is an isosceles triangle with side-lengths
1,5^{-1/4},5^{-1/4}
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
set
S_{n}=\phi_{1}(S_{n-1})\cup\phi_{2}(S_{n-1})
where
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are the two similarities in the left image of Figure
1
. The sets
S_{n}
converge to a snowflake-like curve
\mathcal{S}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
. Similarly, set
C_{0}=[0,1]^{2}
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
set
C_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{16}\psi_{i}(C_{n-1})
where
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{16}
are the similarities in the right image of Figure
1
. The sets
C_{n}
converge to a carpet-like set
\mathcal{C}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
.
Both
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{C}
are connected self-similar sets which have Hausdorff dimension equal to
s=\log{16}/\log{5}
and positive
\mathcal{H}^{s}
measure, and by a theorem of Remes
[
Rem98
]
, both are
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder curves. However, the tangents of
\mathcal{S}
at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-a.e. point are infinite snowflakes while the tangents of
\mathcal{C}
at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-a.e. point are infinite carpets; hence they are topologically different.

Figure 1.
First stages of sets
\mathcal{S}
(left) and
\mathcal{C}
(right).

The previous two examples lead us to ask whether there are infinitely many Hölder curves
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dots
and infinitely many sets
T_{1},T_{2},\dots
such that
T_{i}
are pairwise topologically different and each
T_{i}
is a tangent of
\Gamma_{i}
at typical points. In our main result, we show that not only is this true but it is even worse: this situation can arise in one single Hölder curve. Furthermore, the Hölder exponent of such a curve can be chosen arbitrarily close to 1.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
s>1
, there exists a
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder curve
\Gamma_{s}
in some Euclidean space such that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Gamma_{s})>0
and for
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost every
x\in\Gamma_{s}
, the space
\tang(\Gamma_{s},x)
contains infinitely many topologically different tangents.

We note that little is currently known about the geometry of tangents at typical points. For example, are tangents at typical points connected? If so, are they Hölder images of
\mathbb{R}
? Theorem
1.1
answers the previous two questions in the affirmative for Lipschitz curves.

1.1.
Tangents of self-similar sets

Perhaps the richest source of Hölder curves with positive and finite Hausdorff measure is that of self-similar sets. Recall that an
iterated function system
(abbv. IFS) of similarities on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a finite collection
\mathcal{F}
of contracting similarities of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that a set
K
is the
attractor
of an IFS
\mathcal{F}
if
K=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}}\phi(K)
. An IFS
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
open set condition
(abbv. OSC) if there exists a nonempty open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for all distinct
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
we have
\phi(U)\subset U
and
\phi(U)\cap\phi^{\prime}(U)=\emptyset
. In this paper, we say that a set
Q
is
self-similar
if it is the attractor of an IFS of similarities with the OSC. A well-known theorem of Hutchinson
[
Hut81
]
states that every self-similar set has finite and positive
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-measure for some
s>0
.

While there are examples of Hölder curves with positive and finite Hausdorff measure which are not self-similar sets (e.g. a bi-Lipschitz embedding of the snowflaked space
([0,1],|\cdot|^{\epsilon})

[
BH04
,
Wu15
,
RV17
]
), these examples are all bi-Lipschitz equivalent to self-similar sets. Therefore, it is natural to ask if Hölder curves have tangents possessing some form of self-similarity at typical points. Note that tangents of Lipschitz curves at typical points are straight lines which do possess local self-similarity.
This discussion motivates two questions. First, can we classify tangents of (connected) self-similar sets? Second, are tangents of general Hölder curves always bi-Lipschitz equivalent to tangents of connected self-similar sets?

In pursuit of the first of question, we investigate the tangents of self-similar sets
and we show that if
Q
is a self-similar set in a Euclidean space, then at
\mathcal{H}^{\dimh(Q)}
-almost every point
x
of
Q
, every tangent is locally made up of “big pieces of
Q
”.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\{\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be an IFS of similarities satisfying the OSC for some open set
U
with
U\cap Q\neq\emptyset
, where
Q
is the attractor, and let
s=\dimh{Q}
. Then there exists
c_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on the Lipschitz norms of
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}
such that for
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost every
x\in Q
, every tangent
T\in\tang(Q,x)
, and every
R>0
, there exist similarities
\{f_{i}^{R}:Q\to T\}_{i=1}^{M_{R}}
with
M_{R}\leq c_{0}
with Lipschitz norms in
[c_{0}^{-1}R,R]
such that

\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\cap T\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{M_{R}}f_{j}^{R}(Q)\subset\overline{B}({\bf 0},2R)\cap T,

and for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,M_{R}\}
,
f_{i}^{R}(U)\cap f_{j}^{R}(U)=\emptyset
and
f_{i}^{R}(U\cap Q)
is an open subset of
T
.

Here and throughout we denote by
{\bf 0}
the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
For the special case of self-similar
sponges
(including self-similar carpets such as the Sierpiński carpet), we show in Section
10
that at typical points of such a sponge, each tangent is
locally self-similar
, which means roughly that every open ball contains a similar copy of every other open ball as an open subset.

Regarding the second question, following the construction in Theorem
1.2
, we show in Section
10
that the curves
\Gamma_{s}
from Theorem
1.2
, infinitesimally, do not resemble self-similar sets.

Theorem 1.4
.

For each
s>1
, at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-a.e.
x\in\Gamma_{s}
there exists
T\in\tang(\Gamma_{s},x)
with the following property. If
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a self-similar set with
\dimh{Q}=s
, then the set of points
z\in Q
for which
T
is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to an element of
\tang(Q,z)
is
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-null.

To the best of our knowledge, the curves of Theorem
1.4
are the first examples of Hölder curves that
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost everywhere possess tangents which are not realizable as a tangent of a self-similar set at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost any point. However, it is worth noting that the Hölder curves of Theorem
1.4
have the property that at typical points,
some
tangent does arise as a tangent of a self-similar set at typical points. In light of this, we leave open a weakened version of the second question as a conjecture, originally due to Matthew Badger and the second named author in 2019.

Question 1.5

(Badger, Vellis)
.

If
\Gamma
is a Hölder curve in Euclidean space, then is it true that at typical points, there is some tangent to
\Gamma
which is bi-Lipschitz to a tangent of a connected self-similar set at typical points?

1.2.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.2

To simplify the exposition, we first describe the construction of
\Gamma_{s}
in the special case that
s=\log{24}/\log{6}
. The set
\Gamma_{s}
is constructed by applying two sets of iterated function systems on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in a somewhat random fashion. We start with the unit square
\mathcal{S}^{0}
. Assume now that for some
k\geq 0
we have defined
\mathcal{S}^{k}
which is the union of
24^{k}
closed squares with disjoint interiors
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}
, corresponding to strings
i_{1}\dots i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,24\}^{k}
of length
k
in
24
letters. Each such square is replaced by a copy of Model 1 or a copy of Model 2 in Figure
2
, rescaled by
6^{-k}
. After replacing each square, we obtain
\mathcal{S}^{k+1}
. We have that
\mathcal{S}^{0}\supset\mathcal{S}^{1}\supset\cdots
and the set
\Gamma_{s}
is the Hausdorff limit of these sets. The choice between Model 1 or Model 2 at every stage of the construction is encoded by a
choice function

\eta
which is defined on all finite words made from characters
\{1,\dots,24\}
and takes values in
\{1,2\}
. See Section
4
for the rigorous construction. The important detail is that Model 1 contains one
local cut-point
(i.e. a point
x
which if removed makes a neighborhood of
x
disconnected) while Model 2 has no such point.

Figure 2.
Model 1 (left) and Model 2 (right) for
s=\log{24}/\log{6}
.

In Section
5
we show that, no matter what the choice function
\eta
is, the resulting “limit set”
\Gamma_{s}
will satisfy
0<\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Gamma_{s})<\infty
. Then, in Section
6
, drawing inspiration from the techniques in parametrization results of attractors of iterated function systems (e.g. in
[
Hat85
]
and
[
BV21
]
), for each choice function
\eta
, we construct Lipschitz curves which approximate
\Gamma_{s}
and converge to a
\frac{1}{s}
-Hölder parameterization of
\Gamma_{s}
.

Note that if a choice function takes only one value (say 1), then the resulting set
\Gamma_{s}
is self-similar, and in fact, it is a self-similar
sponge
which, as we prove, implies that it has very nice tangent spaces; see §
10.1
. Thus in order to obtain a set where at typical points, the tangent space contains infinitely many topologically different elements which cannot be obtained from a self-similar set, the choice function necessarily must exhibit some form of randomness. In Section
7
, using a measure-theoretic argument on the set of choice functions, we show that almost every choice function (in terms of a suitable probability measure) yields a continuum
\Gamma_{s}
which “sees” both models in arbitrarily small neighborhoods at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost every point. With such a choice function in hand, we show in Section
8
that at
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-almost every
x\in\Gamma_{s}
, and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a tangent
T\in\tang(\Gamma_{s},x)
that contains exactly
\frac{1}{23}(24^{k}-1)
many
local cut-points
(see §
2.5
). Therefore, there are infinitely many topologically different tangents.

A construction similar to the one described above works for all values
s
of the form
\alpha_{n}=\frac{\log(5n-6)}{\log(n)}
where
n\geq 4
is an even number. To obtain Theorem
1.2
for an arbitrary
s>1
, we first choose
n\geq 4
even such that
s>\alpha_{n}
. Then in Section
9
we appropriately “snowflake” the curve
\Gamma_{\alpha_{n}}
, apply Assouad’s embedding theorem
[
Ass83
]
to bi-Lipschitz embed the snowflaked
\Gamma_{\alpha_{n}}
into some Euclidean space, and use a weak-tangent-snowflaking argument to show that the embedded image (which has dimension
s
) satisfies the conclusions of Theorem
1.2
.

Finally, in Section
10
we prove that these tangents cannot be obtained as tangents of self-similar sets at generic points.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dendrites

A
dendrite
is a Peano continuum (that is, compact, connected, and locally connected at every point) which contains no simple closed curves. The
leaves
of a dendrite
X
are exactly those points
x\in X
such that
X\setminus\{x\}
is connected.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2}
be two dendrites in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that intersect on a point. Then
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is a dendrite.

Proof.

Recall that a metric space is a dendrite if and only if any two distinct points can be separated by a third point
[
Nad92
, Theorem X.10.2]
. Suppose that
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\{x_{0}\}
. Fix distinct
x,y\in T_{1}\cup T_{2}
. If
x,y\in T_{1}
, since
T_{1}
is a dendrite, there exists a point
z\in T_{1}
that separates
x
and
y
. Similarly if
x,y\in T_{2}
. If
x\in T_{1}\setminus T_{2}
and
y\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
, then
x_{0}
separates
x
and
y
.
∎

2.2.
Words

For each even integer
n\geq 4
we denote

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{1,\dots,5n-6\}.

Given
n
as above and an integer
m\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
be the set of words of length
m
formed by characters in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, with the convention that
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}=\{\varepsilon\}
, where
\varepsilon
is the empty word. Define
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}:=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of infinitely countable words.

Given a word
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
, we denote by
|w|
the length of
w
. Also, given
w=i_{1}\cdots i_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
and
j\leq m
, we write
w(j)=i_{1}\cdots i_{j}
, and similarly given an integer
j\geq 0
and an infinite word
\tau=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
we write
\tau(j)=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{j}
. For any
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
, define the
cylinder set

\mathcal{A}_{n,w}^{\mathbb{N}}:=\{\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:\tau(m)=w\}
, that is, the set of infinite words which agree with
w
for the first
m
characters. Similarly, for
j,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq j
and for each
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{j}
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{n,w}^{m}:=\{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}:v(j)=w\}
.

Denote by
\Sigma_{n}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the cylinders
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n,w}
where
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
. Then there exists a unique probability measure
\nu_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to[0,1]
such that
\nu_{n}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n,w})=(5n-6)^{-|w|}
for all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
; see for example
[
Str93
, §3.1]
.

2.3.
Combinatorial graphs and trees

A
combinatorial graph
is a pair
G=(V,E)
of a finite or countable vertex set
V
and an edge set

E\subset\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}:v,v^{\prime}\in V\text{ and }v\neq v^{\prime}\}.

If
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
, we say that the vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
are
adjacent
in
G
.

If
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then we define the
image of
G
to be the set

\text{Im}(G):=\bigcup_{\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E}[v,v^{\prime}]

where
[v,v^{\prime}]
denotes the line segment from
v
to
v^{\prime}
. Recall that if
v\in V
is a vertex, then the
valence
of
v
in
G
is the number of vertices
u\in V\setminus\{v\}
so that
\{u,v\}\in E
.

A
simple path
in
G
is a set
\gamma=\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\dots,\{v_{n-1},v_{n}\}\}\subset E
such that for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
we have
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
; in this case we say that
\gamma
joins
v_{1}
,
v_{n}
.
A graph
T=(V,E)
is a
combinatorial tree
if for any distinct
v,v^{\prime}
there exists a unique simple path
\gamma
that joins
v
with
v^{\prime}
.

2.4.
Tangents

If
A
and
B
are nonempty subsets of a Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then we define the
excess of A over B
as

\exc(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}|a-b|;

see
[
Bee93
, §3.1]
,
[
BL15
]
, and
[
BET17
, Appendix A]
. In the next remark, we list six important properties of the excess which will be used heavily throughout this article. The first and fifth properties follow straightforwardly from the definition of excess while the other four are given in
[
BL15
, Section 2]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The excess satisfies the following six properties.

(1)

Translation invariance.
For nonempty sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\exc(A,B)=\exc(A+x,B+x).

(2)

Triangle inequality.
For nonempty sets
A,B,C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\exc(A,C)\leq\exc(A,B)+\exc(B,C).

(3)

Containment.
For nonempty sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\exc(A,B)=0
if, and only if,
A\subset\overline{B}
.

(4)

Monotonicity.
If
A\subset A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}\subset B
are all nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then

\exc(A,B)\leq\exc(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}).

(5)

Subadditivity.
If
A,B,C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
are all nonempty, then

\exc(A\cup B,C)\leq\exc(A,C)+\exc(B,C).

(6)

Closure.
If
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
are nonempty, then

\exc(A,B)=\exc(\overline{A},\overline{B}).

Let
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the collection of nonempty closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and let
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
be the collection of nonempty closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing the origin
{\bf 0}
. We consider both of these spaces equipped with the
Attouch-Wets
topology, which is defined in
[
Bee93
, Definition 3.1.2]
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Bee93
, Chapter 3]
)
.

There exists a metrizable topology, called the Attouch-Wets topology, on
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in which a sequence of sets
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
converges to a set
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if and only if for every
r>0
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\exc(X_{m}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),X)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\exc(X\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),X_{m})=0.

Moreover, the subcollection
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
is sequentially compact; that is, for any sequence
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
there exists a subsequence
(X_{i_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
and a set
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
such that
(X_{i_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges to
X
.

In the following lemma, we record a useful property of Attouch-Wets convergence in
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
, which roughly says that sequences in
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
that converge in the Attouch-Wets topology satisfy a type of Cauchy condition with respect to excess.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
(A_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
is a sequence converging to a set
A\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology, then for every
r>0
and each
\epsilon>0
, there exists an integer
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every pair of integers
j_{1},j_{2}\geq j_{0}
,
\exc(A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A_{j_{2}})<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
r>0
. Since
A_{j}\to A
in the Attouch-Wets topology as
j\to\infty
, there exists an integer
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}

\exc(A_{j}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A)<\min\{\epsilon/2,r\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\exc(A\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r),A_{j})<\min\{\epsilon/2,r\}.

Then for every pair of integers
j_{1},j_{2}\geq j_{0}
, by the triangle inequality for excess (see Remark
2.2
),

\exc(A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A_{j_{2}})\leq\exc(A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r))+\exc(A\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r),A_{j_{2}}).

Now since
j_{1}\geq j_{0}
,
\exc(A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A)<\min\{\epsilon/2,r\}
, so for every
x\in A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)
there exists a point
y\in A
so that
|x-y|<\min\{\epsilon/2,r\}
. Then it must hold that such a point
y
is contained in
\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r)\cap A
, and therefore
\exc(A_{j_{1}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),A\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r))<\epsilon/2
. Additionally, since
j_{2}\geq j_{0}
, we have also that
\exc(A\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2r),A_{j_{2}})<\epsilon/2
, and this completes the proof.
∎

Let
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let
x\in X
. Following
[
BL15
]
, we say that a set
T\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
is a
tangent set of
X
at
x
if there exists a sequence of scales
(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}>0
such that
r_{m}\to 0
and

\frac{X-x}{r_{m}}\to T
with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology. We denote by
\tang(X,x)
the set of all tangent sets to
X
at
x
.

For the next lemma, denote by
\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
the collection of all sets
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
such that every component of
X
is unbounded.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a nondegenerate continuum and
x\in X
, then
\tang(X,x)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
.

Proof.

Fix
T\in\tang(X,x)
. First, we prove that the component of
T
which contains
{\bf 0}
is unbounded. To this end, let
(r_{j})>0
be a sequence of scales converging to
0
so that
(r_{j})^{-1}(X-x)\to T
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. Given
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define the similarity
g_{j}:X\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
g_{j}(y)=(r_{j})^{-1}(y-x)
, and for
R>0
let
Q_{j,R}
be the component of
g_{j}(X)\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)
containing
{\bf 0}
. Since
X
is connected and nondegenerate, for each
R>0
there exists a
j_{R}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
Q_{j,R}\cap\partial B({\bf 0},R)\neq\emptyset
for every integer
j\geq j_{R}
. Note that for every pair of scales
R_{2}>R_{1}>0
and for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
Q_{j,R_{1}}\subset Q_{j,R_{2}}
. By compactness of
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
, for each
R>0
the sequence
(Q_{j,R})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
has an Attouch-Wets subsequential limit
K_{R}
, and for
R_{2}>R_{1}>0
we have
K_{R_{1}}\subset K_{R_{2}}
. Additionally, since
g_{j}(X)
converges to
T
in the Attouch-Wets topology, we have that
K_{R}\subset T
,
{\bf 0}\in K_{R}
, and
K_{R}\cap\partial B({\bf 0},R)\neq\emptyset
for every
R>0
. Therefore,
\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}K_{m}\subset T
and the former union is unbounded, connected, and contains
{\bf 0}
, so the component of
T
containing
{\bf 0}
is unbounded, thus
T
has an unbounded component.

Assume now for a contradiction that
T
has a bounded component
K
. Let
L
be the component of
T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\infty\}
in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
that contains
\infty
. Then clearly
K,L
are distinct connected components of
T^{\prime}
. Regarding
K
and
L
as quasi-components of
T^{\prime}
, one can choose two disjoint closed sets
Y,Z\subset T^{\prime}
satisfying
K\subset Y
,
L\subset Z
, and
T^{\prime}=Y\cup Z
. Then both
Y
and
Z
are closed in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
, and thus by normality there exists an open set
U
such that

Y\subset U\subset\overline{U}\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}\setminus Z.

Since
\infty\notin\overline{U}
, we have that
\overline{U}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Set
d=\dist(Z,Y)
.

Fix
R>0
large enough that
Y\subset\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)
and
Z\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\neq\emptyset
. By definition of Attouch-Wets convergence, there exists a
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,

\exc(r_{j}^{-1}(X-x)\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),T)+\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),r_{j}^{-1}(X-x))<d/3.

Let
y,z\in r_{j}^{-1}(X-x)
so that
\dist(y,Y)<d/3
and
\dist(z,Z)<d/3
. Since
X
is connected, there is another point
p\in r_{j}^{-1}(X-x)
so that
p\in\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)
and
\dist(p,Y)=d/3
; thus
\dist(p,Z)\geq 2d/3
.
However, it follows that
\dist(p,T)\geq d/3
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Note that since
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
is sequentially compact, we have that if
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and if
x_{0}\in X
, then the sequence of sets
(n(X-x_{0}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequential limit in
\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
. In particular, such a limit must be a tangent of
X
at
x_{0}
, so the set of tangents to a nonempty closed set at a point contained in the set is always nonempty. More simply, every nonempty closed set has tangents at every point (and every tangent contains the origin
{\bf 0}
).

2.5.
Local cut-points

Recall if
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then a point
x\in X
is called a
cut-point
if
C\setminus\{x\}
is not connected, where
C\subset X
is the component of
X
containing
x
. For a nondegenerate closed set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
in a Euclidean space, following Whyburn
[
Why35
]
, we say that a point
x_{0}\in X
is a
local cut-point of
X
if there exists some
r>0
such that
C_{X}(x_{0},r)\setminus\{x_{0}\}
is not connected, where
C_{X}(x_{0},r)
is the component of
\overline{B}(x_{0},r)\cap X
containing
x_{0}
. That is,
x_{0}
is a local cut-point if
x_{0}
is a cut-point in sufficiently small neighborhoods of itself.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
be nondegenerate closed subsets of Euclidean spaces and let
f:X\to Y
be a homeomorphism. If
p
is a local cut-point of
X
, then
f(p)
is a local cut-point of
Y
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a local cut-point of
X
and let
r>0
such that
C_{X}(p,r)\setminus\{p\}
is disconnected. As a matter of notation, throughout this proof when we write
B_{X}(p,r)
we mean
B(p,r)\cap X
, and similarly when we write
B_{Y}(f(p),r)
we mean
B(f(p),r)\cap Y
. Let
\epsilon>0
so that
\overline{B}_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\subset f(\overline{B}_{X}(p,r))
, and let
\delta\in(0,r)
such that

f(\overline{B}_{X}(p,\delta))\subset\overline{B}_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\subset f(\overline{B}_{X}(p,r)).

Then we have that

f(C_{X}(p,\delta))\subset C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\subset f(C_{X}(p,r)).

Let
A,B\subset C_{X}(p,r)\setminus\{p\}
be a disjoint pair of nonempty closed subsets of
C_{X}(p,r)\setminus\{p\}
so that
A\cup B=C_{X}(p,r)\setminus\{p\}
. Note that
A\cup\{p\}
and
B\cup\{p\}
are both closed sets in
X
. We claim that

A\cap C_{X}(p,\delta)\neq\emptyset
.

First, we show that
A\cup\{p\}
is connected. If
A\cup\{p\}
is not connected, then let
C,D
be a disjoint pair of nonempty closed subsets of
A\cup\{p\}
with
C\cup D=A\cup\{p\}
.
Then since
A\cup\{p\}
is closed in
X
, we have that
C
and
D
are both closed in
X
. Without loss of generality, assume that
p\in C
. Then the pair
B\cup C
and
D
is a disjoint pair of nonempty closed subsets of
C_{X}(p,r)
with
(B\cup C)\cup D=C_{X}(p,r)
, which is a contradiction, so
A\cup\{p\}
is connected.

Let
C_{A}
be the component of
(A\cup\{p\})\cap\overline{B}_{X}(p,\delta)
which contains
p
. Then
C_{A}=A\cup\{p\}
or
C_{A}\cap\partial B_{X}(p,\delta)\neq\emptyset
,
and in either case we have that
C_{A}\neq\{p\}
. Furthermore, since
C_{A}
is connected, we have that
C_{A}\setminus\{p\}\subset C_{X}(p,\delta)\setminus\{p\}
,
so since
C_{A}\setminus\{p\}\subset A
, we have that
A\cap C_{X}(p,\delta)\neq\emptyset
. Similarly,
B\cap C_{X}(p,\delta)\neq\emptyset
.

Finally, we have

C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\setminus\{f(p)\}\subset f(A)\cup f(B),

therefore

C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\setminus\{f(p)\}=(f(A)\cap C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon))\cup(f(B)\cap C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)).

Furthermore, the sets on the right hand side form a disjoint pair of nonempty closed subsets of
C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\setminus\{f(p)\}
with
(f(A)\cap C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon))\cup(f(B)\cap C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon))=C_{Y}(f(p),\epsilon)\setminus\{f(p)\}
, so
f(p)
is a local cut-point
of
Y
.
∎

2.6.
Self-similarity

If a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is Lipschitz continuous, then we denote by
\Lip(f)
(the
Lipschitz norm
of
f
) the smallest
L\geq 0
so that for every pair of points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L|x-y|
. If
\Lip(f)<1
, then
f
is a
contraction
. A map
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is
affine
if there exists a linear map
L:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
so that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f(x)=L(x)+f({\bf 0})
. A map
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called a
similarity
if there exists
\lambda>0
such that for every pair of points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|f(x)-f(y)|=\lambda|x-y|
. Every similarity is a composition of an orthogonal transformation, a scalar multiplication, and a translation; in particular similarity maps are affine. If
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a rotation-free and reflection-free similarity, then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f(x)=\Lip(f)x+f({\bf 0})
.

Remark 2.8
.

A map
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is affine if and only if there exists a linear transformation
L:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
so that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f(x)-f(y)=L(x-y)
. In particular, if
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a rotation-free and reflection-free similarity, then for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f(x)-f(y)=\Lip(f)(x-y)
.

An
iterated function system
(IFS for short) is a finite collection
\mathcal{F}
of contractions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By a theorem of Hutchinson
[
Hut81
]
, for each IFS
\mathcal{F}
there exists a unique nonempty compact set
K
(called the
attractor
of
\mathcal{F}
) such that
K=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}}\phi(K)
.

We say that an IFS
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
open set condition
(OSC for short) if there exists a nonempty open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for any distinct
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{F}
,

\phi(U)\subset U,\qquad\phi(U)\cap\psi(U)=\emptyset.

By a theorem of Schief
[
Sch94
, Theorem 2.2]
, the OSC is equivalent to the
strong open set condition
(SOSC): if
K
is the attractor of
\mathcal{F}
, then there exists a nonempty open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
for which the OSC holds so that
U\cap K\neq\emptyset
.

It is well-known that if
K
is the attractor of an IFS
\mathcal{F}
of similarities with the OSC, then the Hausdorff dimension, the Minkowski dimension, and the Assouad dimension are all equal to
s
, where
s
is the unique solution of the equation

\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}}(\text{Lip}(\phi))^{s}=1.

Moreover,
0<\mathcal{H}^{\dimh(K)}(K)<\infty
where
\dimh(K)
is the Hausdorff dimension of
K

[
Hut81
]
.

Henceforth, we say that a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
self-similar
if there exists an IFS
\mathcal{F}
of similarities with the OSC such that
K
is the attractor of
\mathcal{F}
.

3.
Tangents of Lipschitz curves at typical points

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
1.1
. The proof is based on two results. The first is Rademacher’s Theorem.

Lemma 3.1

(Rademacher’s Theorem)
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function, then
f
is differentiable
\mathcal{L}^{N}
-a.e., where
\mathcal{L}^{N}
is the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For a proof see for example
[
EG15
, Theorem 3.2]
. The second ingredient is a result of Falconer which roughly says that Lipschitz curves look flat around typical points. Following
[
Fal86
, §3.2]
, for a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for a line
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
through the origin, and for a number
\alpha>0
, define

C(x_{0},L,\alpha):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(x,L+x_{0})<\alpha|x-x_{0}|\}

to be the open cone centered at
x_{0}
in the direction of
L
with aperture
\alpha
. Given a closed set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we say that a point
x_{0}\in K
is
flat in
K
if there exists a line
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
through the origin such that for every
\alpha>0
, there exists
r_{\alpha}>0
with
B(x_{0},r_{\alpha})\cap X\subset C(x_{0},L,\alpha)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a continuum with
\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)<\infty
, then
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in X
is flat in
X
.

Proof.

The claim is trivially true if
\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)=0
, so we may assume that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)>0
.

By
[
Fal86
, Corollary 3.15]
, for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every point
x\in X
, there exists a unique line
L
passing through
{\bf 0}
such that for every
\alpha>0
,

(3.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}((X\cap\overline{B}(x,r))\setminus C(x,L,\alpha))}{r}=0.

Let
x_{0}\in X
be such a point, let
L
be the line given above.

Assume for a contradiction that there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a sequence of positive scales
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
going to zero, and there exists a sequence
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
X
such that

y_{j}\in(\overline{B}(x_{0},r_{j})\cap X)\setminus C(x_{0},L,\alpha).

Since
X
is a continuum, the component of
(\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\cap X)\setminus C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)
which contains
y_{j}
must intersect at least one of
\partial B(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)
and
\overline{C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)}\cap\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}\left((\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\cap X)\setminus C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\min\left\{\dist\left(y_{j},\partial B(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\right),\dist\left(y_{j},\overline{C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)}\cap\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|,\dist\left(y_{j},\overline{C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)}\cap\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\right)\right\}.

To estimate the second distance, fix
z\in\overline{C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2)}\cap\overline{B}(x_{0},\frac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)
. Since
y_{j}\notin C(x_{0},L,\alpha)
, we have
\dist(y_{j},L+x_{0})\geq\alpha|y_{j}-x_{0}|
. Moreover,
\dist(z,L+x_{0})\leq(\alpha/2)|z-x_{0}|
. Fix a point
p\in L+x_{0}
so that
|z-p|\leq\tfrac{\alpha}{2}|z-x_{0}|
, so
|z-p|\leq\tfrac{3}{4}\alpha|y_{j}-x_{0}|
. Note that
|y_{j}-p|\geq\alpha|y_{j}-x_{0}|
. Thus,
|y_{j}-z|\geq\tfrac{\alpha}{4}|y_{j}-x_{0}|
. Hence, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}((\overline{B}(x_{0},\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|)\cap X)\setminus C(x_{0},L,\alpha/2))}{\tfrac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|}\geq\frac{\alpha}{6}

which contradicts (
3.1
) as
j\to\infty
and
\frac{3}{2}|y_{j}-x_{0}|\to 0
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
1.1
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be Lipschitz and let
X=f([0,1])
. By
[
AO17
, Thoerem 4.4]
, there exists
g:[0,1]\to X
, an essentially two-to-one Lipschitz parametrization with constant speed equal to
2\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)
. By Rademacher’s Theorem, for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in X
, there exists
t\in g^{-1}(\{x\})
so that
g^{\prime}(t)
exists. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.2
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e. point
x\in X
is flat in
X
. Let
x_{0}\in X
be such that
x_{0}
is flat in
X
and there exists
t\in g^{-1}(\{x_{0}\})
such that
g^{\prime}(t)
exists. Let
T\in\tang(X,x_{0})
, and let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the line through the origin in the direction of
g^{\prime}(t)
.

First, we show that
T\subset L
. To this end, let
r_{j}\to 0
be a sequence of positive scales so that
\frac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0})\to T
in the Attouch-Wets topology. Fix
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
. By Lemma
3.2
, there is
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for every
j\geq j_{0}
,

B(x_{0},r_{j}(R+\epsilon))\cap X\subset C(x_{0},L,\tfrac{\epsilon}{2(R+\epsilon)})

and

\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),\tfrac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0}))<\epsilon/2.

Note that

\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),(\tfrac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0}))\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R+\epsilon))<\epsilon/2

as well. Then by the triangle inequality for excess from Remark
2.2
and since
x_{0}
is flat in
X
,

\displaystyle\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),L)

\displaystyle\leq\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),\tfrac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R+\epsilon))+\exc(\tfrac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R+\epsilon),L)

\displaystyle<\epsilon.

Since the latter is true for all
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
, it follows from Remark
2.2
that
T\subset L
.

Next, we show that
L\subset T
. Fix
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
. There exists
h>0
so that for every
s\in(-hR,hR)
,

\frac{1}{h}|g(t+s)-x_{0}-g^{\prime}(t)s|<\epsilon/2.

Moreover, there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every
j\geq j_{0}
and every
s\in(-r_{j}R,r_{j}R)
we get

\frac{1}{r_{j}}|g(t+s)-x_{0}-g^{\prime}(t)s|<\epsilon.

Fix
y\in L\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},\frac{R}{2\mathcal{H}^{1}(X)})
. For each
j\geq j_{0}
there exists
s_{j}\in(-r_{j}R,r_{j}R)
such that
y=g^{\prime}(t)\frac{s_{j}}{r_{j}}
. Hence,

\left|\frac{g(t+s_{j})-x_{0}}{r_{j}}-y\right|=\left|\frac{g(t+s_{j})-x_{0}}{r_{j}}-g^{\prime}(t)\frac{s_{j}}{r_{j}}\right|<\epsilon.

Noting that
\frac{g(t+s)-x_{0}}{r_{j}}\in\frac{1}{r_{j}}(X-x_{0})
, we obtain

\exc(L\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},(2\mathcal{H}^{1}(X))^{-1}R),r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0}))<\epsilon.

Therefore, for every
R>0
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists some
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq j_{0}
,

\exc(L\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0}))<\epsilon.

Proceeding to the conclusion as in the previous paragraph, we have that
L\subset T
, and therefore
L=T
as desired.
∎

4.
A “statistically self-similar” carpet

Fix for the rest of this section an even integer
n\geq 4
. Recall the alphabets
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and associated word spaces from §
2.2
. Divide the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
into
n^{2}
-many closed squares of side-length
1/n
that have disjoint interiors and let
S_{1},\dots,S_{4n-4}
be those squares that intersect with
\partial[0,1]^{2}
.

We define two iterated function systems

\mathcal{F}_{n}^{1}=\{\psi^{1}_{n,j}:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}\}_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{n}^{2}=\{\psi^{2}_{n,j}:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}\}_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}

as follows.

(1)

For
j\in\{1,\dots,4n-4\}
,
\psi_{n,j}^{1}=\psi_{n,j}^{2}
and
\psi_{n,j}^{1}
is the unique composition of a translation and a dilation that maps
[0,1]^{2}
onto
S_{j}
.

(2)

For
j\in\{4n-3,\dots,4n+\frac{n}{2}-4\}
define

\psi_{n,j}^{1}(x)=\psi_{n,j}^{2}(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}x+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{j-1}{n},\tfrac{1}{n}\right).

(3)

For
j\in\{4n+\frac{n}{2}-3,5n-6\}
define

\displaystyle\psi_{n,j}^{1}(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}x+\left(\tfrac{j-4n+\frac{n}{2}+3}{n},\tfrac{2}{n}\right),\qquad\psi_{n,j}^{2}(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}x+\left(\tfrac{j-4n+\frac{n}{2}+3}{n},\tfrac{1}{n}\right).

See Figure
2
for the first iterations of
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{2}
in the case
n=6
.

For
j\in\{1,2\}
we let
\psi_{n,\varepsilon}^{j}
be the identity map and for
w=i_{1}\dots i_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
with
m\geq 1
we let

\psi_{n,w}^{j}:=\psi_{n,i_{1}}^{j}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{n,i_{m}}^{j}.

We set

\mathscr{C}_{n}=\{\eta:\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}\to\{1,2\}\}

and each function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
will henceforth be called a
choice function
.

Fix now a choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. Set
\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\eta}
to be the identity on
[0,1]^{2}
, and for any
w=i_{1}\cdots i_{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
with
m\geq 1
, define

\phi_{w}^{\eta}:=\psi_{n,i_{1}}^{\eta(\varepsilon)}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{n,i_{m}}^{\eta(i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1})}.

Define now the “attractor” associated with the choice function
\eta
to be

K^{\eta}:=\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2}).

We list a couple of elementary facts about the set
K^{\eta}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, the set
K^{\eta}
is compact.

Proof.

This is immediate noting that each set
\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
is compact as a finite union of compact sets, and that
K^{\eta}
is the countable intersection of compact sets.
∎

Given
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
, we define

K_{w}^{\eta}:=\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap K^{\eta}.

The following version of the open set condition is satisfied.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and every pair of distinct words
w,v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
,

\phi_{w}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{v}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{w}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset(0,1)^{2}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, if
i,j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
are distinct characters, then by definition we have that
\phi_{j}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
and
\phi_{i}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
are distinct squares of side length
1/n
with disjoint interiors contained in
[0,1]^{2}
. Thus,
\phi_{i}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset(0,1)^{2}
and
\phi_{i}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{j}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
.

Fix now an
m\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that for all distinct words
v,w\in\mathcal{A}^{m}_{n}
, we have
\phi_{w}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{v}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
and
\phi_{w}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset(0,1)^{2}
. Let
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{m+1}_{n}
be distinct words. Then by the base case we have
\phi_{u}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})
, which is contained in
(0,1)^{2}
by the induction hypothesis.

The remainder of the proof falls to a case study.

Case 1.
If
u(m)\neq u^{\prime}(m)
, then we have that
\phi_{u}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})
and
\phi_{u^{\prime}}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\subset\phi_{u^{\prime}(m)}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})
by the base case. Then, since
u(m)\neq u^{\prime}(m)
, we have by the induction hypothesis that
\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{u^{\prime}(m)}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
, so
\phi_{u}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{u^{\prime}}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
.

Case 2.
If
u(m)=u^{\prime}(m)
, then there exist distinct characters
i,j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
so that
u=u(m)j
and
u^{\prime}=u^{\prime}(m)i
. By definition of
\phi_{u}^{\eta}
we then have

\phi_{u}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}\circ\psi_{j}^{\eta(u(m))}((0,1)^{2})

and

\phi_{u^{\prime}}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}\circ\psi_{i}^{\eta(u(m))}((0,1)^{2}).

Thus we can see that

\phi_{u^{\prime}}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})\cap\phi_{u}^{\eta}((0,1)^{2})=\phi_{u(m)}^{\eta}(\psi_{j}^{\eta(u(m))}((0,1)^{2})\cap\psi_{i}^{\eta(u(m))}((0,1)^{2})),

and the latter set is empty by the base case.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
,
w,v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
,
x\in K_{w}^{\eta}
, and
y\in K_{v}^{\eta}
. If
|x-y|>2\sqrt{2}\cdot n^{-m}
, then
K_{w}^{\eta}\cap K^{\eta}_{v}=\emptyset
. If
|x-y|<n^{-m}
, then
K_{w}^{\eta}\cap K_{v}^{\eta}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}=[0,1]^{2}
, and for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define
X_{m}=\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
. Recall that for
j\in\{1,\dots,4n-4\}
we have
\psi_{n,j}^{1}=\psi_{n,j}^{2}
. Therefore, for every integer
m\geq 0
, we have that

\bigcup_{j=1}^{4n-4}\psi_{n,j}^{1}(X_{m})\subset X_{m+1}

and

\partial[0,1]^{2}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{4n-4}\psi_{n,j}^{1}(\partial[0,1]^{2}).

Thus, since
\partial[0,1]^{2}\subset X_{0}
, we have that
\partial[0,1]^{2}\subset X_{m}
for every integer
m\geq 0
, and so since
K^{\eta}=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}X_{m}
, we get
\partial[0,1]^{2}\subset K^{\eta}
. Similarly, for every
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
we have that
\phi_{w}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset K_{w}^{\eta}
. Let
m\geq 1
and let
v,w\in\mathcal{A}^{m}_{n}
. If
K_{v}^{\eta}\cap K_{w}^{\eta}\neq\emptyset
, then
\diam(K_{v}^{\eta}\cup K_{w}^{\eta})\leq 2\sqrt{2}n^{-m}
. Furthermore, if
K_{v}^{\eta}\cap K_{w}^{\eta}=\emptyset
, then
\phi_{v}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{w}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})=\emptyset
, so
\dist(\phi_{v}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2}),\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2}))\geq n^{-m}
, and the result follows as
K_{v}^{\eta}\subset\phi_{v}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
.
∎

5.
Ahlfors regularity of carpets
K^{\eta}

Fix for the rest of this section an even integer
n\geq 4
and set
\alpha_{n}:=\frac{\log(5n-6)}{\log(n)}
. We show that for every choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, we have
0<\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}(K^{\eta})<\infty
. In fact, we show the following stronger statement.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
C_{n}>1
such that for every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, every
x\in K^{\eta}
, and every
r\in(0,1/n)
we have

C_{n}^{-1}r^{\alpha_{n}}\leq\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}(K^{\eta}\cap B(x,r))\leq C_{n}r^{\alpha_{n}}.

For a choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
define
\pi_{\eta}:\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}\to K^{\eta}
by

(5.1)

\pi_{\eta}(\tau):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\phi_{\tau(m)}^{\eta}({\bf 0}).

We start with an elementary topological fact.

Lemma 5.2
.

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the collection
\{K_{w}^{\eta}:w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}\}
is equal to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
K^{\eta}
.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. Let
\Sigma
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the collection
\{K^{\eta}_{w}:w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}\}
, and let
\mathcal{B}(K^{\eta})
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
K^{\eta}
. That
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{B}(K^{\eta})
is clear, as each
K_{w}^{\eta}
is a Borel set. For the reverse inclusion, we show that for any
x\in K^{\eta}
and any
r>0
,
B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}\in\Sigma
. To see this, let

W(x,r)=\{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}:K^{\eta}_{w}\subset B(x,r)\}.

Note that
W(x,r)
is a countable set since
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
is countable, and so
\bigcup_{w\in W(x,r)}K^{\eta}_{w}\in\Sigma
. Furthermore, for each point
y\in B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}
there is a word
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\pi_{\eta}(\tau)=y
. Then since
B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}
is an open subset of
K^{\eta}
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\diam(K^{\eta}_{\tau(j)})=0,
there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough that
\tau(m)\in W(x,r)
. Since
y\in K^{\eta}_{\tau(m)}
,
\bigcup_{w\in W(x,r)}K^{\eta}_{w}=B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}
, so we have that
\Sigma=\mathcal{B}(K^{\eta})
.
∎

Recall from §
2.2
the
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
and the probability measure
\nu_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to[0,1]
.
We say a word
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
is an
injective word
if for every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, we have
\pi_{\eta}^{-1}(\{\pi_{\eta}(\tau)\})=\{\tau\}
. That is,
\tau
is called an injective word if it always uniquely defines a point in
K^{\eta}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The set of non-injective words
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a
\nu_{n}
-null set.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. Recall that if
j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
satisfies
j>4n-4
, then

(\psi^{1}_{j}([0,1]^{2})\cup\psi^{2}_{j}([0,1]^{2}))\cap\partial[0,1]^{2}=\emptyset.

Thus if
\tau=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
i_{m+1}>4n-4
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\pi_{\eta}(\tau)\notin\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi^{\eta}_{v}(\partial[0,1]^{2})
. Furthermore, if
\tau,\zeta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
are distinct words with
\pi_{\eta}(\tau)=x=\pi_{\eta}(\zeta)
, then there exists some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau(N)\neq\zeta(N)
. Thus for every integer
m\geq N
, we have
\tau(m)\neq\zeta(m)
and
x\in\phi_{\tau(m)}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{\zeta(m)}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
. By Lemma
4.2
, this implies that
x\in\phi_{\tau(m)}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{\zeta(m)}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})
, so

\{x\in K^{\eta}:\pi_{\eta}^{-1}(\{x\})\text{ is not a singleton}\}\subset\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{v}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2}).

Moreover, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{v}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m+1}}\phi_{v}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2}).

Thus if a character
j\in\{4n-3,\dots,5n-6\}
appears infinitely often in
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
, then
\tau
is injective; so the set of non-injective words is contained in the set of words for which every character
j\in\{4n-3,\dots,5n-6\}
appears only finitely often. To see that this set is
\nu_{n}
-null, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
let
U_{m}:=\{\tau=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:i_{m}>4n-4\}
. We have
\nu_{n}(U_{m})=\frac{n-2}{5n-6}
for each
m
, and if
\{U_{m_{1}},\dots,U_{m_{j}}\}
is a collection of these sets, then

\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{j}U_{m_{i}}=\{\tau=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}:i_{m_{i}}>4n-4\text{ for each }i\in\{1,\dots,j\}\}

and this intersection has
\nu_{n}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}U_{m_{i}})=\left(\frac{n-2}{5n-6}\right)^{j}
.
Thus,
V_{m}:=\bigcap_{j=m}^{\infty}U_{j}
has
\nu_{n}(V_{m})=0
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Noting further that the set
F
of words for which every character
j\in\{4n-3,\dots,5n-6\}
appears only finitely often is contained in
\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{m}
, we have that
F
is
\nu_{n}
-null.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Fix
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. We claim that for some
C_{n}>1
the measure
\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}
defined on
\mathcal{B}(K^{\eta})
by Lemma
5.2
satisfies

(5.2)

C_{n}^{-1}r^{\alpha_{n}}\leq\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta})\leq C_{n}r^{\alpha_{n}}

for all
x\in K^{\eta}
and
r\in(0,1/n)
. Assuming (
5.2
), by
[
MT10
, §1.4.3]
, we have that
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }K^{\eta}
satisfies (
5.2
) (perhaps with a different constant
C_{n}
), since the measure
\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}
has the same null sets as the restricted Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }K^{\eta}
.

For any
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n,w}\subset\pi_{\eta}^{-1}(K^{\eta}_{w})\subset\mathcal{N}_{n}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n,w}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{n}
denotes the set of non-injective words in
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
. Hence by Lemma
5.3
, for any
w\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{n}
we have

\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(K^{\eta}_{w})=(5n-6)^{-|w|}=n^{-|w|\alpha_{n}}.

Fix for the rest of the proof a point
x\in K^{\eta}
, a radius
r\in(0,1/n)
, and a word
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\pi_{\eta}(\tau)=x
.

For the upper bound of (
5.2
), let
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy

n^{-m}\sqrt{2}>2r\geq n^{-m-1}\sqrt{2}.

Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
be those words with
K_{v_{i}}^{\eta}\cap K_{\tau(m)}^{\eta}\neq\emptyset
. By Lemma
4.2
,
j\leq 9
and by Lemma
4.3
, we have that
B(x,n^{-m})\cap K^{\eta}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}K^{\eta}_{v_{i}}
. We have also that
n^{-m}\geq r
, so

B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}\subset B(x,n^{-m})\cap K^{\eta}\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{j}K^{\eta}_{v_{i}},

and therefore

\displaystyle\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(K^{\eta}_{v_{i}})\leq 9\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(K^{\eta}_{\tau(m)})

\displaystyle=9n^{-m\alpha_{n}}\leq 9\left(\tfrac{8}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{n}}r^{\alpha_{n}}.

For the lower bound of (
5.2
), let
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy

n^{-m}\sqrt{2}<\frac{r}{2}\leq n^{-m+1}\sqrt{2}.

Since
r<1/n
, we have that
m
is at least
1
. If
y\in K^{\eta}_{\tau(m)}
, then

|x-y|\leq n^{-m}\sqrt{2}<\frac{r}{2},

so
K^{\eta}_{\tau(m)}\subset B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta}
. Thus

\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(B(x,r)\cap K^{\eta})\geq\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}(K^{\eta}_{\tau(m)})=n^{-m\alpha_{n}}\geq(8\sqrt{2})^{-\alpha_{n}}r^{\alpha_{n}}.\qed

6.
Hölder parametrizations of carpets
K^{\eta}

In this section, we show that sets
K^{\eta}
defined in Section
4
are Hölder curves. Recall the numbers
\alpha_{n}:=\frac{\log(5n-6)}{\log(n)}
from Section
5
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For any even integer
n\geq 4
and any
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, there exists a
(1/\alpha_{n})
-Hölder continuous surjection
f:[0,1]\to K^{\eta}
.

For the rest of this section we fix an even integer
n\geq 4
and a choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. The dependence of sets and functions on
n
and
\eta
in this section is omitted.

Define the set
T\subset[0,1]^{2}
by

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:=\left(\partial[0,1-\tfrac{1}{n}]^{2}\setminus\left(\{1-\tfrac{1}{n}\}\times[1-\tfrac{2}{n},1-\tfrac{1}{n}]\right)\right)\cup\left(\{\tfrac{1}{2}\}\times[0,\tfrac{1}{n}]\right)\cup\left([\tfrac{1}{2},1-\tfrac{2}{n}]\times\{\tfrac{1}{n}\}\right)

and the sets

T^{1}:=T\cup\left([0,\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}]\times\{\tfrac{2}{n}\}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad T^{2}:=T\cup\left([0,\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}]\times\{\tfrac{1}{n}\}\right).

See Figure
3
for
n=6
. Some elementary properties of the sets
T^{1},T^{2}
are given in the next lemma and its proof is left to the reader.

Lemma 6.2
.

Both
T^{1}
and
T^{2}
are dendrites and are contained in
[0,1)^{2}
.

Figure 3.
Dendrites
T^{1}
(left) and
T^{2}
(right) for
n=6
.

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
define combinatorial graphs
G^{i}=(V^{i},E^{i})
by

V^{i}:=\{\psi_{j}^{i}({\bf 0}):j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad E^{i}:=\{\{p,q\}:p,q\in V^{i},[p,q]\subset T^{i},|q-p|=1/n\}.

Next, we define a sequence of sets
(T_{m})_{m\geq 0}
by
T_{0}=\{{\bf 0}\}
, and for any integer
m\geq 0
,

T_{m+1}=T_{m}\cup\left(\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\phi_{w}^{\eta}(T^{\eta(w)})\right).

Define also for
m\geq 0
the combinatorial graph
G_{m}=(V_{m},E_{m})
via

V_{m}=\{\phi_{w}^{\eta}({\bf 0}):w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{m}=\{\{p,q\}:p,q\in V_{m},[p,q]\subset T_{m},|p-q|=n^{-m}\}.

Lemma 6.3
.

The sequence of sets
(T_{m})_{m=0}^{\infty}
is a nested sequence of dendrites contained in
K^{\eta}
. Moreover, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, a vertex
u\in V_{m}
is a leaf of
T_{m}
if and only if
u
has no edges emanating to the right or upward in
T_{m}
.

Proof.

By the arguments in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, for each
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
we have that
\phi_{w}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset K^{\eta}
. Therefore,

Lemma 6.4

7.
Random choice functions

Fix an even integer
n\geq 4
. Note that the set
\mathscr{C}_{n}
of choice functions
\eta:\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}\to\{1,2\}
can be identified with
\{1,2\}^{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}}
which is a countable product of finite sets. Then by
[
Str93
, §3.1]
, there exists a probability measure
\mu_{n}
on
\mathscr{C}_{n}
such that for any
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}

\mu_{n}(\{\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}:\eta(w)=1\})=\mu_{n}(\{\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}:\eta(w)=2\})=\frac{1}{2}.

We say that a choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
is a
random choice function
if
\nu_{n}
-a.e. word
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
satisfies the following property: for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
and every integer
k\geq 0
, there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
depending on
w,k,N
such that

(R1)

the restrictions

\eta\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}^{\ell+j}_{n,w(\ell)}=1\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{2N+k-1}\mathcal{A}^{\ell-N+j}_{n,w(\ell-N)}\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}^{\ell+j}_{n,w(\ell)}\right.=2,

(R2)

the character
i_{\ell-N+1}>4n-4
.

For completeness, if
k=0
, then the union
\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}^{\ell+j}_{n,w(\ell)}
is taken over an empty set thus we regard the union itself to be empty, and so we consider only the second restriction of (R1) in this case. See Figure
4
for a schematic representation of property (R1).

Figure 4.
The big triangle represents all words in
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
that start with
w(\ell-N)
and have at most
\ell+k+N
letters. Words of the same the height have the same number of letters. The part of this set where
\eta
takes the value 2 is colored by red, and the part of the set where
\eta
takes the value 1 is colored by blue.

To simplify our notation, for a word
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
, a function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and two integers
N\geq 1,k\geq 0
, we say
(\eta,w,N,k)
satisfies (R1), (R2) if there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
for which (R1) and (R2) hold. For a word
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
and a function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, we say that
(\eta,w)
satisfies (R1) and (R2) if for every pair of integers
N\geq 1,k\geq 0
, we have that
(\eta,w,N,k)
satisfies (R1) and (R2). Thus, a choice function
\eta
is random if for
\nu_{n}
-a.e.
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
,
(\eta,w)
satisfies (R1) and (R2).

The goal of this section is to show that random choice functions exist. In fact, we prove the following stronger statement.

Proposition 7.1
.

For every even integer
n\geq 4
,
\mu_{n}
-a.e.
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
is random.

Since
\pi_{\eta}\#\nu_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }K^{\eta}
have the same null sets in
K^{\eta}
for every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, Proposition
7.1
yields immediately the following corollary.

Corollary 7.2
.

For every even integer
n\geq 4
, there exists
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}
-almost every point
x\in K^{\eta}
has
\pi_{\eta}^{-1}(\{x\})=\{w\}
and
(\eta,w)
satisfies (R1) and (R2).

Before proving Proposition
7.1
, we establish some notation. For
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
define

D^{w}:=\{\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}:(\eta,w)\text{ satisfies (R1) and (R2)}\}

and for
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
define

C_{\eta}:=\{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:(\eta,w)\text{ satisfies (R1) and (R2)}\}.

Define also

\Delta_{n}:=\{(\eta,w)\in\mathscr{C}_{n}\times\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:w\in C_{\eta}\}=\{(\eta,w)\in\mathscr{C}_{n}\times\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:\eta\in D^{w}\}.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Using the notation above, we claim that
\mu_{n}(D^{w})=1
for
\nu_{n}
-almost every word
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Assuming the claim, by Fubini’s theorem we obtain

\int_{\mathscr{C}_{n}}\nu_{n}(C_{\eta})d\mu_{n}(\eta)=(\mu_{n}\times\nu_{n})(\Delta_{n})=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}}\mu_{n}(D^{w})d\nu_{n}(w)=1.

Therefore,
\nu_{n}(C_{\eta})=1
for
\mu_{n}
-a.e.
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
which implies that
\mu_{n}
-a.e.
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
is random.

For the proof of the claim, recall that

\nu_{n}(\{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}:i_{j}>4n-4\text{ infinitely often}\})=1,

so it suffices to show that the claim holds for every word
w
in the above set.

Fix
w=i_{1}i_{2}\cdots\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
i_{j}>4n-4
infinitely often. Fix also integers
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
. We construct a sequence
(M_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
in an inductive fashion. Let
M_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
i_{M_{1}+1}>4n-4
. Assuming that we have defined
M_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{m+1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M_{m+1}>M_{m}+2N+k+1
and
i_{M_{m+1}+1}>4n-4
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
let
E_{m,w}
be the set of all choice functions
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
such that
(\eta,w,N,k)
satisfies (R1) and (R2) with
\ell=M_{m}+N
. Since
i_{M_{m}+1}>4n-4
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, (R2) is satisfied for
\ell=M_{m}+N
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

B_{m}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{2N+k-1}\mathcal{A}_{n,w(M_{m})}^{M_{m}+j},

that is, the set of of all finite words
u
starting with
w(M_{m})
and having length at most
M_{m}+2N+k-1
. Define also for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\xi_{m}:B_{m}\to\{1,2\}
via

\xi_{m}\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}_{n,w(M_{m})}^{M_{m}+j}=1\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi_{m}\left|B_{m}\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}_{n,w(M_{m})}^{M_{m}+j}=2.\right.

Then
E_{m,w}=\{\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}:\eta|_{B_{m}}=\xi_{m}\}
and
|B_{m}|=\tfrac{(5n-6)^{2N+k}-1}{5n-7}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By uniformity of the measure
\mu_{n}
, we have that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu_{n}(E_{m,w})=2^{-|B_{m}|}
, in particular each set
E_{m,w}
has the same measure independent of
m
, and that measure is positive.

Next we claim that the sets
(E_{m,w})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent. To see this, let
\{E_{m_{1},w},\cdots,E_{m_{j},w}\}
be a finite collection of the sets
(E_{m,w})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
, and note that

\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{j}E_{m_{i},w}=\{\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}:\eta|_{B_{m_{i}}}=\xi_{m_{i}}\text{ for }i\in\{1,\cdots,j\}\}.

Since the sets
\{B_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are pairwise disjoint as
M_{m+1}-M_{m}>2N+k+1
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
|\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}B_{m_{i}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{j}|B_{m_{i}}|
and
\mu_{n}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}E_{m_{i},w})=2^{-|\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}B_{m_{i}}|}
. Thus,

\mu_{n}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}E_{m_{i},w})=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mu_{n}(E_{m_{i},w}),

so the sets
(E_{m,w})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent.

Furthermore, each set
E_{m,w}
has the same positive measure, so

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(E_{m,w})=\infty.

Thus, by the second Borel-Cantelli Lemma,

\mu_{n}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{m=j}^{\infty}E_{m,w})=1,

or equivalently, for
\mu_{n}
-almost every choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
,
(\eta,w,N,k)
satisfies (R1) and (R2) infinitely often. In particular, for every pair of integers
N\geq 1,k\geq 0
we have that for
\mu_{n}
-almost every
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
,
(\eta,w,N,k)
satisfies (R1) and (R2). Since there are countably many choices of
N
and
k
, we have immediately that
\mu_{n}(D^{w})=1
for
\nu_{n}
-almost every word
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
.
∎

8.
Tangents of carpets
K^{\eta}
at typical points

Fix for the rest of this section an even integer
n\geq 4
. Recall the definitions of choice functions
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
and carpets
K^{\eta}
from Section
4
, the number
\alpha_{n}=\frac{\log(5n-6)}{\log(n)}
from Section
5
, and the definition of random choice functions from Section
7
.

In this section we prove the following result about tangents of carpets
K^{\eta}
at typical points when
\eta
is random.

Proposition 8.1
.

If
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
is a random choice function, then for
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}
-a.e.
x\in K^{\eta}
there exist
T_{n,0},T_{n,1},T_{n,2},\dots\in\tang(K^{\eta},x)
such that
T_{n,k}
has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many cut points.

In §
8.1
we study the local cut-points of a certain class of “almost self-similar” carpets and in §
8.2
, we relate the tangents of
K^{\eta}
with these carpets.

8.1.
Local cut-points in a class of carpets

Define, for an integer
k\geq 0
, the set

(8.1)

K^{n,k}:=\bigcup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{k}}\psi_{n,w}^{1}\left(\bigcap\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\psi_{n,v}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\right)

where maps
\psi_{n,v}^{i}
and
\phi_{v}^{\eta}
for
v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
and
i\in\{1,2\}
are as in Section
4
.

Roughly speaking,
K^{n,k}
is obtained by first iterating
k
times the maps from the system
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{1}
, and then applying these iterates to the self-similar attractor of the IFS of similarities
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{2}
(recall the systems
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{1},\mathcal{F}_{n}^{2}
from Section
4
).

The next lemma is the main result of this subsection.

Lemma 8.2
.

For each integer
k\geq 0
,
K^{n,k}
has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many local cut-points.

The proof of Lemma
8.2
is by induction on
k
. The base case
k=0
is given in Lemma
8.6
. In the proof, we make use of the following definition.

Let
\{f_{j}(x)=Lx+b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{l}
with
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be similarities on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
where
L>0

and
l\geq 2
such that

(1)

if
i,j\in\{1,\dots,l\}
are distinct, then,
f_{i}((0,1)^{2})\cap f_{j}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
, and

(2)

\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}f_{i}([0,1]^{2})\neq\emptyset
.

We say that
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}

meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
if for every
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
, there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,l\}\setminus\{i\}
so that
f_{i}([0,1]^{2})\cap f_{j}([0,1]^{2})
is an edge of
f_{i}([0,1]^{2})
. Note that necessarily
l\in\{2,3,4\}
.

Remark 8.3
.

Recall from the proof of Lemma
4.3
that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{2}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset

\displaystyle K^{n,0}\subset\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{2}([0,1]^{2})

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{1}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset

\displaystyle K^{n,k}\subset\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{1}([0,1]^{2}).

Note also that if there exist distinct
i,j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
x\in[0,1]^{2}
with
x\in\psi_{n,j}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\cap\psi_{n,i}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, then the maps in
\{\psi_{n,l}^{2}:x\in\psi_{n,l}^{2}([0,1]^{2}),l\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\}
meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
. Note further that if there exist distinct
i,j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
x\in[0,1]^{2}
with
x\in\psi_{n,j}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\cap\psi_{n,i}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, then either
x=(1/2,2/n)
or the functions in the set
\{\psi_{n,l}^{1}:x\in\psi_{n,l}^{1}([0,1]^{2}),l\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\}
meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
. See for example Figure
2
for the case
n=6
.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
be distinct, and let

x\in\phi_{u}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{v}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{w}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2}).

Then the maps
\{\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}:x\in\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2}),\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}\}
meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that for all
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
,
\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}(x)=n^{-m}y+\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}({\bf 0})
with
\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\in\{0,\frac{1}{n^{m}},\dots,\frac{n^{m}-1}{n^{m}}\}^{2}
.

Let
E=\{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}:x\in\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\}
which, by assumption, contains at least three words. Clearly the intersection
\bigcap_{\beta\in E}\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
is nonempty as it contains
x
.

Suppose that
a,b\in E
are distinct words so that
\phi_{a}^{\eta},\phi_{b}^{\eta}
do not meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
. Then
\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{b}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\in\{-n^{-m},n^{-m}\}^{2}
. Since
\card(E)\geq 3
, there exists
c\in E\setminus\{a,b\}
with

\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\in\{-n^{-m},0,n^{-m}\}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{b}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\in\{-n^{-m},0,n^{-m}\}^{2}.

If, for example,
\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\in\{-n^{-m},n^{-m}\}^{2}
, then

\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{b}^{\eta}({\bf 0})=(\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))+(\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{b}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))\in\{-2n^{-m},0,2n^{-m}\}^{2}\cap\{-n^{-m},0,n^{-m}\}^{2}.

But then
\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})=\phi_{b}^{\eta}({\bf 0})
, and this is a contradiction. Hence,
\phi_{a}^{\eta}({\bf 0})-\phi_{c}^{\eta}({\bf 0})\notin\{-n^{-m},n^{-m}\}^{2}
, and by a simple calculation we get
\phi_{a}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{c}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
is equal to an edge of
\phi_{a}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
containing
x
, and similarly for
\phi_{b}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})\cap\phi_{c}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})
. From this we may conclude that the maps in
\{\phi_{\beta}^{\eta}:\beta\in E\}
meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u,v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
be distinct, and let
x\in K^{n,0}
with
x\in\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,v}^{2}(K^{n,0})
. Then the maps in
\{\psi_{n,w}^{2}:x\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0}),w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}\}
meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
m
. The base case
m=1
holds by Remark
8.3
.

Assume now that the claim holds for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
x\in K^{n,0}
and
u,v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m+1}
distinct so that
x\in\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,v}^{2}(K^{n,0})
. By Lemma
8.4
, we may assume that
\{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m+1}:x\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})\}=\{u,v\}
. We prove that
\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})
is an edge of
\psi_{n,u}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
.

Let
u=a_{1}\dots a_{m+1}
,
v=b_{1}\dots b_{m+1}
. If
a_{1}=b_{1}
, then applying
(\psi_{n,a_{1}}^{2})^{-1}
returns us to the claim at
m
, so by the inductive hypothesis we may assume that
a_{1}\neq b_{1}
. Next, if
x
is not a vertex of
\psi_{n,u}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, then by Lemma
10.2
the claim holds, so we may further assume that
x
is a vertex of
\psi_{n,u}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
and of
\psi_{n,v}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
. If there exists a word
w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}\setminus\{u(m),v(m)\}
so that
x\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})
, then there exists a
j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
with
x\in\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,v}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,wj}^{2}(K^{n,0})
, and this is a contradiction.

Therefore, we may further assume that
\{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}:x\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})\}=\{v(m),u(m)\}
. Then by the inductive hypothesis,
\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,v(m)}^{2}(K^{n,0})
is an edge of
\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
. The remainder of the proof is a case study on which edge of
\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
this intersection is equal to, but these cases are all essentially identical so we show only one of them and leave the remaining three to the reader. Assume that

\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,v(m)}^{2}(K^{n,0})=\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}([0,1]\times\{0\}),

and as the maps
\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}
and
\psi_{n,v(m)}^{2}
are both rotation-free and reflection-free similarity maps with Lipschitz norms
n^{-m}
, by Remark
2.8
this intersection must also be equal to the edge
\psi_{n,v(m)}^{2}([0,1]\times\{1\})
. Then

\psi_{n,u(m)}^{2}({\bf 0})-\psi_{n,v(m)}^{2}({\bf 0})=(0,n^{-m}),\quad\psi_{n,a_{m+1}}^{2}({\bf 0})=(\tfrac{k}{n},0),\quad\psi_{n,b_{m+1}}^{2}({\bf 0})=(\tfrac{k\pm 1}{n},1)

with
k,k\pm 1\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then there is some
j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
so that
\psi_{n,j}^{2}({\bf 0})=(\frac{k}{n},1)
, thus by a straightforward calculation we obtain
x\in\psi_{n,v(m)j}^{2}(K^{n,0})
. As
a_{1}\neq b_{1}
and necessarily
j\neq b_{m+1}
,
v(m)j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m+1}\setminus\{v,u\}
, and this contradicts our assumption that
\{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m+1}:x\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})\}=\{u,v\}
, concluding the proof.
∎

The next lemma is the base case for the proof of Lemma
8.2
.

Lemma 8.6
.

The set
K^{n,0}
does not contain local cut-points.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
K^{n,0}
contains no cut-points. Assuming this to be true, the proof of the lemma follows from
[
DLR+23
, Theorem 1.6]
, as Lemma
8.4
and Lemma
8.5
alongside the fact that
K^{n,0}
is a self-similar set with no cut-points are sufficient to imply the hypotheses of this theorem.

To prove that
K^{n,0}
contains no cut-points, fix
y\in K^{n,0}
and distinct
p,q\in K^{n,0}\setminus\{y\}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
so that there exist distinct
u,v,w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
for which
\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})
,
\psi_{n,v}^{2}(K^{n,0})
, and
\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})
are pairwise disjoint with
p\in\psi_{n,v}^{2}(K^{n,0})
,
q\in\psi_{n,u}^{2}(K^{n,0})
, and
y\in\psi_{n,w}^{2}(K^{n,0})
. Then by Lemma
8.5
and by
[
BV21
, Lemma 3.1]
, there exist distinct
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}
so that
p\in\psi_{n,u_{1}}^{2}(K^{n,0})
,
q\in\psi_{n,u_{k}}^{2}(K^{n,0})
, and for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
,
\psi_{n,u_{j}}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,u_{j+1}}^{2}(K^{n,0})
contains at least two points. Now let
z_{0},\dots,z_{k}\in K^{n,0}
be distinct points so that
z_{0}=p
,
z_{k}=q
, and
z_{j}\in(\psi_{n,u_{j}}^{2}(K^{n,0})\cap\psi_{n,u_{j+1}}^{2}(K^{n,0}))\setminus\{y\}
for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
. Then since
\partial[0,1]^{2}\subset K^{n,0}
by the proof of Lemma
4.3
, for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k-2\}
there is a continuous function
\gamma_{j}:[0,1]\to\psi_{n,u_{j+1}}^{2}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\setminus\{y\}
so that
\gamma_{j}(0)=z_{j}
,
\gamma_{j}(1)=z_{j+1}
and necessarily
\gamma_{j}([0,1])\subset K^{n,0}\setminus\{y\}
. By
[
BV21
, Proposition 3.2]
, there exist continuous
\gamma_{0}:[0,1]\to\psi_{n,u_{1}}^{2}(K^{n,0})
with
\gamma_{0}(0)=p
,
\gamma_{0}(1)=z_{1}
and continuous
\gamma_{k-1}:[0,1]\to\psi_{n,u_{k}}^{2}(K^{n,0})
with
\gamma_{k-1}(0)=z_{k-1}
,
\gamma_{k-1}(1)=q
. Concatenating these curves yields a path inside of
K^{n,0}
connecting
p
to
q
and avoiding
y
, so
y
cannot be a cut-point for
K^{n,0}
.
∎

As a straightforward consequence, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 8.7
.

If
f_{1},\dots,f_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
j\in\{2,3,4\}
is a collection of rotation-free and reflection-free similarities with a common Lipschitz norm which meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
, then
\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}f_{i}(K^{n,0})
does not contain local cut-points.

Proof.

We leave most of this proof to the reader. A proof follows from the observation that, up to re-scaling and translating, there are only
7
ways for such a collection of maps to meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
, and all of these arrangements are present in
\{\psi_{n,j}^{2}:j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\}
.
∎

We are now finally ready to prove Lemma
8.2
.

Proof of Lemma
8.2
.

Denote by
N_{k}
the number of local cut-points in
K^{n,k}
. By Lemma
8.6
we have
N_{0}=0
. It remains to show that
N_{k}=(5n-6)N_{k-1}+1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

By definition of sets
K^{n,k}
,

\displaystyle K^{n,k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{k}}\psi_{n,v}^{1}\left(\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\psi_{n,w}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{1}\left(\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{k-1}}\psi_{n,v}^{1}\left(\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup\limits_{w\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{m}}\psi_{n,w}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{1}(K^{n,k-1}).

By Lemma
4.2
, each
\psi_{n,j}^{1}(K^{n,k-1}\cap(0,1)^{2})
contributes
N_{k-1}
many local cut-points, and there is one additional local cut-point at
(1/2,2/n)
. Then by the last part of Remark
8.3
, every point of
K^{n,k}\setminus(\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\psi_{n,j}^{1}(K^{n,k-1}\cap(0,1)^{2})
other than
(1/2,2/n)
is either contained in exactly one image
\psi_{n,v}^{1}(K^{n,0})
for some
v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{k}
or in an intersection of at least two such images of maps which meet at edges of
[0,1]^{2}
, so there are no other local cut-points of
K^{n,k}
by Corollary
8.7
. Therefore,
N_{k}=(5n-6)N_{k-1}+1
.
∎

8.2.
Proof of Proposition
8.1

Fix for the rest of this subsection an integer
k\geq 0
, a random choice function
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and an injective word

w=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}_{n}

such that
(\eta,w)
satisfy (R1) and (R2) from the definition of a random choice function (see the paragraph before Lemma
5.3
for the definition of injective words). Set
x=\pi_{\eta}(w)
. Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\ell_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
be the integer given in properties (R1) and (R2). For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle X_{N}

\displaystyle:=n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}-x),

\displaystyle y_{N}

\displaystyle:=(\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})})^{-1}\circ\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N}+N+k-1)}({\bf 0}),

\displaystyle x_{N}

\displaystyle:=n^{\ell_{N}}(x-\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}({\bf 0})).

Intuitively,
x_{N}
are the bottom-left corners of the blow-ups of pieces of
K^{\eta}
that contain
x
, while the points
(y_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
form a sequence of points in
[0,1]^{2}
which approximate the points
x_{N}
. Additionally, the sets
X_{N}
are a sequence of blow-ups of
K^{\eta}
centered at the point

which we use to find the desired tangents in
\tang(K^{\eta},x)
.

By (R2),
i_{\ell_{N}-N+1}>(4n-4)
, so
\psi^{\eta(w(\ell_{N}-N))}_{n,i_{\ell_{N}-N+1}}([0,1]^{2})
does not intersect
\partial[0,1]^{2}
. Thus, for every
N\geq 3
we get

(8.2)

\dist\left(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})},K^{\eta}\setminus K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}\right)\geq n^{-\ell_{N}-1+N}.

The general strategy of the proof of Proposition
8.1
is to first establish estimates for the Hausdorff distance between
X_{N}
and translates of
K^{n,k}
in a region near
[0,1]^{2}
, then establish estimates for the Hausdorff distance between the rest of
X_{N}
and some blown-up and translated image of
K^{n,0}
out to some large ball containing the origin. Then, exploiting the triangle inequality for excess (as in Remark
2.2
), we show that some subsequence of
X_{N}
converges to a tangent set for which all local cut-points (see §
2.5
) lie inside a unit square near the origin, for which outside of this square the tangent “looks like” a tangent of
K^{n,0}
, and for which inside this unit square the tangent has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many local cut-points.

Lemma 8.8
.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
|y_{N}-x_{N}|\leq n^{-N-k+3}
.

Proof.

Since
x=\pi_{\eta}(w)
, the point
x_{N}
is in the set

\displaystyle n^{\ell_{N}}(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}+N+k-1)}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})

\displaystyle-\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))

\displaystyle=n^{\ell_{N}}\left(\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}\circ(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N}+N+k-1)}([0,1]^{2})-\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}({\bf 0})\right).

Now by Remark
2.8
, the latter set is equal to
(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N}+N+k-1)}([0,1]^{2})
, which has diameter no more than
n^{-N-k+2}\sqrt{2}
and contains
y_{N}
, so
|y_{N}-x_{N}|\leq n^{-N-k+3}
.
∎

In the following lemma, we show that the parts of the sets
X_{N}
inside of some unit square containing the origin become close to translates of
K^{n,k}
in terms of Hausdorff distance.

Lemma 8.9
.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the Hausdorff distance

\disth(n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-x),K^{n,k}-y_{N})\leq n^{-N-k+4}.

Proof.

Recall that if
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
are compact sets, then Hausdorff distance is given by

\disth(A,B):=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B}|x-y|,\sup_{y\in B}\inf_{x\in A}|y-x|\right\},

and recall from §
2.4
that excess is given by
\exc(A,B):=\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B}|x-y|
.
Thus to prove the lemma, it suffices to show

\exc(n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-x),K^{n,k}-y_{N})\leq n^{-N-k+4},

\exc(K^{n,k}-y_{N},n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-x))\leq n^{-N-k+4}.

For the first inequality, by Lemma
8.8
and by the triangle inequality for excess as in Remark
2.2
, it is enough to show that

\exc(n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-x),K^{n,k}-x_{N})\leq n^{-N-k+3}.

Note that since excess is translation invariant (see Remark
2.2
), this excess is equal to

\exc(n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}({\bf 0})),K^{n,k}),

which is at most
n^{-N-k+2}\sqrt{2}
by (R1) and by definition of
K^{n,k}
. Via a similar argument, we obtain the other inequality

\exc(K^{n,k}-y_{N},n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}-x))\leq n^{-N-k+4}.\qed

Now define

\displaystyle F_{N}

\displaystyle:=n^{\ell_{N}}(K^{n,0}-\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-y_{N},

\displaystyle Y_{N}

\displaystyle:=(F_{N}\setminus([0,1]^{2}-y_{N}))\cup(K^{n,k}-y_{N}).

The sets
F_{N}
are simply blown-up shifts of the self-similar set
K^{n,0}
. The sets
Y_{N}
are constructed by removing the copy of
K^{n,0}
in
F_{N}
which contains the origin, then replacing it by a shift of
K^{n,k}
. Before continuing, note that for
k=0
,
F_{N}=Y_{N}
.

Lemma 8.10
.

For every
r>0
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\exc(Y_{N}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),X_{N})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\exc(X_{N}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),Y_{N})=0.

Proof.

Fix
r>0
. By (
8.2
) there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every integer
N\geq N_{0}
,

\dist(K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})},K^{\eta}\setminus K^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N}-N)})\geq n^{-\ell_{N}}2r.

By (R2),
\dist(\partial[0,1]^{2},(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))\geq n^{-1}
. Since
y_{N}\in[0,1]^{2}
,

\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\subset n^{N}([0,1]^{2}-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-y_{N}.

Fix
y\in Y_{N}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)
. If
y\in[0,1]^{2}-y_{N}
, then by Lemma
8.9
there exists some
z\in X_{N}
so that
|y-z|\leq n^{-N-k+4}
.

Assume now that
y\in Y_{N}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\setminus([0,1]^{2}-y_{N})
. Let

Q=\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2N+k-1}}\psi_{n,v}^{2}([0,1]^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad P=\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2N+k-1}}\psi_{n,v}^{2}(\partial[0,1]^{2}).

By (R2) and since
\phi_{v}^{\eta}(\partial[0,1]^{2})\subset K^{\eta}
for every finite word
v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}
by the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we have that

n^{\ell_{N}}((\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta}(P)-x)\setminus(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})-x))\subset X_{N}.

Since the maps
\psi_{n,j}^{i}
are all similarities with Lipschitz norm
n^{-1}
, by Remark
2.8
we have

\displaystyle n^{\ell_{N}}(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta}(P)-x)

\displaystyle=n^{\ell_{N}}(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta}(P)-\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta}\circ(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=n^{N}(P-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}(x)).

Similarly, we obtain

n^{\ell_{N}}(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}([0,1]^{2})-x)=[0,1]^{2}-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta})^{-1}(x)
and
x_{N}=(\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta})^{-1}(x)
. Furthermore,
(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}(x)=(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}(x_{N})
, and so

(n^{N}(P-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-x_{N})\setminus([0,1]^{2}-x_{N})\subset X_{N}.

Since
y\in Y_{N}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\setminus([0,1]^{2}-y_{N})
, by calculations similar to those above we obtain

y\in(n^{N}(Q-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-y_{N})\setminus([0,1]^{2}-y_{N}).

Therefore, there exists
u\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2N+k-1}
so that

y\in(n^{N}(\psi_{n,u}^{2}([0,1]^{2})-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-y_{N})\setminus([0,1]^{2}-y_{N}),

which implies that there is a point

p\in(n^{N}(\psi_{n,u}^{2}(\partial[0,1]^{2})-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))-x_{N})\setminus([0,1]^{2}-x_{N}),

hence
p\in X_{N}
. Then by the triangle inequality and Lemma
8.8
we have

\displaystyle|p-y|\leq|x_{N}-y_{N}|+|(p+x_{N})-(y+y_{N})|\leq|x_{N}-y_{N}|+n^{-N-k+1}\sqrt{2}\leq n^{-N-k+4}

since
(p+x_{N}),(y+y_{N})\in n^{N}\psi_{n,u}^{2}([0,1]^{2})
, and the latter set has diameter
n^{-N-k+1}\sqrt{2}
.

The other limit can be proven in the same way, yielding the result.∎

In the following two lemmas, we prove that the sequence
(Y_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging in the Attouch-Wets topology to a set with
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
local cut-points and that the closeness of excesses from Lemma
8.10
implies that this set is also the Attouch-Wets limit of a subsequence of
(X_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, thus it is a tangent of
K^{\eta}
at
x
.

Lemma 8.11
.

The sequence
(Y_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology to a set
L_{k}\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{2};{\bf 0})
that has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many local cut-points.

Proof.

Let
r>3
, and recall from the proof of Lemma
8.10
that there exists a minimal
N_{0,r}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
N\geq N_{0,r}
,

(Y_{N}+y_{N})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)=\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\cap(n^{N}(K^{n,0}-(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi_{w(\ell_{N})}^{\eta}({\bf 0}))\setminus[0,1]^{2})\cup K^{n,k}.

Furthermore,
N_{0,r}
is nondecreasing in
r
. By (R1), for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a word
v_{N}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{N}
so that
(\phi_{w(\ell_{N}-N)}^{\eta})^{-1}\circ\phi^{\eta}_{w(\ell_{N})}=\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}
. Then by Remark
2.8
we obtain

[0,1]^{2}\cap n^{N}(K^{n,0}-\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}({\bf 0}))=n^{N}(\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}(K^{n,0})-\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}({\bf 0}))=K^{n,0}.

Thus,

(Y_{N}+y_{N})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)=\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\cap(n^{N}(((K^{n,0}\setminus\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}([0,1]^{2}))\cup(\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}(K^{n,k})))-\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}({\bf 0}))).

Since
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{2};{\bf 0})
is sequentially compact in the Attouch-Wets topology, the sequence of sets

(Y_{N}+y_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence
(Y_{N_{j}}+y_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to a limit
L
in the Attouch-Wets topology and so that
(y_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a point
y_{0}\in[0,1]^{2}
as
j\to\infty
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle D_{r,j}

\displaystyle:=\{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}:[m_{1},m_{1}+1]\times[m_{2},m_{2}+1]\subset\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),

\displaystyle\text{ and there exists }v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{N_{j}}\text{ with }(m_{1},m_{2})=n^{N_{j}}(\psi_{n,v}^{2}({\bf 0})-\psi_{n,v_{N_{j}}}^{2}({\bf 0}))\}.

If
j\in\mathbb{N}
so that
N_{j}\geq N_{0,r+2}
, then

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in D_{r,j}}((m_{1},m_{2})+K^{n,0})\cup K^{n,k}

\displaystyle\subset(Y_{N_{j}}+y_{N_{j}})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup\limits_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in D_{r+2,j}}((m_{1},m_{2})+K^{n,0})\cup K^{n,k}

and
D_{r,j}\subset D_{r+2,j}
. Then since for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, every
v\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{N}
has
n^{N}(\psi_{n,v}^{2}({\bf 0})-\psi_{n,v_{N}}^{2}({\bf 0}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and since
(Y_{N_{j}}+y_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges, there exists
j_{0,r}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0,r}
,
D_{r,j}=D_{r,j_{0,r}}
. Indeed, if
D_{r,j}\neq D_{r,j+1}
then

\exc((Y_{N_{j}}+y_{N_{j}})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r),(Y_{N_{j+1}}+y_{N_{j+1}}))\geq n^{-1}

or vice-versa, which cannot happen infinitely often by Lemma
2.4
. Consequently, for every

and every integer

, we have that
(Y_{N_{j}}+y_{N_{j}})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)=\overline{B}({\bf 0},r)\cap L
, so by Lemma
8.2
, Corollary
8.7
, and Remark
8.3
,

has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many local cut-points inside
[0,1]^{2}
and no other local cut-points. Finally,
L_{k}=L-y_{0}
is the desired limit.∎

.

The tangent

of Lemma

is a countable union

by showing that the closeness of the sequences

and

from Lemma

Lemma 8.13
.

Let
(N_{j})_{j}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
L_{k}\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{2};{\bf 0})
be the subsequence and limit set from Lemma
8.11
, respectively. Then
(X_{N_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
L_{k}
with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{2}
has the Heine-Borel property, in order to show that
\lim_{j\to\infty}X_{N_{j}}=L_{k}
with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology, by
[
Bee93
, Lemma 2.1.2]
and
[
Bee93
, Lemma 3.1.4]
it is sufficient to verify the following two conditions.

(i)

If
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an open set such that
V\cap L_{k}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
X_{N_{j}}\cap V\neq\emptyset
as well.

(ii)

If
\delta>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
B(z,\delta)\cap L_{k}=\emptyset
, then for every
\epsilon\in(0,\delta)
there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
B(z,\epsilon)\cap X_{N_{j}}=\emptyset
as well.

To verify (i), fix such an open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then there exist
z\in V
and
\epsilon>0
such that
B(z,\epsilon)\subset V
and
B(z,\epsilon/2)\cap L_{k}\neq\emptyset
. Assume for the sake of contradiction that (i) fails to hold for this
V
, and thus
X_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\epsilon)=\emptyset
infinitely often. Now by Lemma
8.10
and since
\lim_{j\to\infty}Y_{N_{j}}=L_{k}
, there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
\exc(Y_{N_{j}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},|z|+\epsilon),X_{N_{j}})<\epsilon/2
and
B(z,\epsilon/2)\cap Y_{N_{j}}\neq\emptyset
. By our assumption, there exists an integer
j\geq j_{0}
such that
X_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\epsilon)=\emptyset
and there exists a point
y\in Y_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\epsilon/2)
. Then we have

\exc(Y_{N_{j}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},|z|+\epsilon),X_{N_{j}})\geq\dist(y,X_{N_{j}})\geq\epsilon/2,

which is a contradiction.

Now to verify (ii), fix
\delta>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that
B(z,\delta)\cap L_{k}=\emptyset
and let
\epsilon\in(0,\delta)
. Since
Y_{N_{j}}\to L_{k}
as
j\to\infty
with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology, there is a
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
Y_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\frac{\epsilon+\delta}{2})=\emptyset
. Furthermore by Lemma
8.10
, choosing
j_{0}
large enough, we may assume that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
\exc(X_{N_{j}}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},|z|+\delta),Y_{N_{j}})<\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}
. Then for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
and for each
p\in X_{N_{j}}\cap B({\bf 0},|z|+\delta)
,
\dist(p,Y_{N_{j}})<\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}
. Since
Y_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\frac{\epsilon+\delta}{2})=\emptyset
, if
p\in B(z,\epsilon)
, then
\dist(p,Y_{N_{j}})\geq\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}
and
p\in B({\bf 0},|z|+\delta)
. Therefore for each integer
j\geq j_{0}
,
X_{N_{j}}\cap B(z,\epsilon)=\emptyset
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem
1.2
in the special case that
s=\alpha_{n}:=\frac{\log(5n-6)}{\log(n)}
follows from several of the propositions we have established so far. For the general case, we require the following lemma which shows that bi-Hölder equivalence is hereditary. See
[
Li21
]
for a quasisymmetric version of this lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism between two closed sets
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some
t>0
. Then for every point
x_{0}\in X
and every tangent
T\in\tang(X,x_{0})
, there exist a tangent
T^{\prime}\in\tang(Y,f(x_{0}))
and a
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism
g:T\to T^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
t\geq 1
. Let
x_{0}\in X
, let
T\in\tang(X,x_{0})
, and let
r_{j}\to 0
be a sequence of scales so that
r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})\to T
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define the function

f_{j}:r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})\to r_{j}^{-1/t}(Y-f(x_{0}))\quad\text{with}\quad f_{j}(z)=r_{j}^{-1/t}(f(r_{j}z+x_{0})-f(x_{0})).

It is easy to see that each
f_{j}
is a
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism with a bi-Hölder coefficient
H
independent of
j
. By McShane’s extension theorem
[
McS34
, Corollary 1]
, each
f_{j}
extends to a
(1/t)
-Hölder map
F_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the Hölder coefficient equal to
H
. Passing to a subsequence, by the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem, we may assume that
\{F_{j}\}
converges locally uniformly to a
(1/t)
-Hölder map
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Since the maps
F_{j}|T
are
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder with the same bi-Hölder coefficients,
F|_{T}:T\to F(T)
is a
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism. We claim that
r_{j}^{-1/t}(Y-f(x_{0}))\to F(T)
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. First note that

r_{j}^{-1/t}(Y-f(x_{0}))=F_{j}(r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})).

Let
R>0
, let
\epsilon>0
, and let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every integer
j\geq j_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|F_{j}-F\|_{\infty,\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)}

\displaystyle<\epsilon

\displaystyle\exc(\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\cap r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0}),T)

\displaystyle<\epsilon

\displaystyle\exc(\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\cap T,r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0}))

\displaystyle<\epsilon.

For an integer
j\geq j_{0}
, given
z\in\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\cap r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})
there exists
y\in T
with
|z-y|<\epsilon
, so

|F_{j}(z)-F(y)|\leq|F_{j}(y)-F_{j}(z)|+|F_{j}(z)-F(z)|<H\epsilon^{1/t}+\epsilon.

Therefore,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\exc(\overline{B}({\bf 0},HR^{1/t})\cap F_{j}(r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})),F(T))=0.

The other excess estimate follows via a similar calculation, so
r_{j}^{-1/t}(Y-f(x_{0}))\to F(T)
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. This concludes the case
t\geq 1
.

Now let
t\in(0,1)
. Then
f^{-1}:Y\to X
is a
t
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism. Fix
x_{0}\in X
, let
T\in\tang(X,x_{0})
, and let
r_{j}\to 0
be a sequence of scales so that
r_{j}^{-1}(X-x_{0})\to T
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. Then
r_{j}^{t}\to 0
is a sequence of scales, and there is a convergent subsequence
r_{j_{k}}^{-t}(Y-f(x_{0}))\to S
as
k\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology. As in the case
t\geq 1
, we have
g_{j_{k}}:r_{j_{k}}^{-t}(Y-f(x_{0}))\to r_{j_{k}}^{-1}(X-x_{0})
, a sequence of
t
-bi-Hölder homeomorphisms with uniform coefficients which extend to
t
-Hölder functions
G_{j_{k}}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the maps
G_{j_{k}}
converge locally uniformly to a
t
-Hölder map
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

G_{j_{k}}(r_{j_{k}}^{-t}(Y-f(x_{0})))\to G(S)

as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology, and since
G_{j_{k}}(r_{j_{k}}^{-t}(Y-f(x_{0})))=r_{j_{k}}^{-1}(X-x_{0})
converges, it must converge to
T
. Therefore
G(S)=T
, and by locally uniform convergence
G|_{S}
is a
t
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism onto
T
. Finally,
G^{-1}:T\to S
is a
(1/t)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism, as desired.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
s>1
. Let
n\geq 4
be an even integer such that
\alpha_{n}\in(1,s)
and let
\eta\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
be a random function. By Proposition
6.1
there exists a
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
-Hölder surjection
f:[0,1]\to K^{\eta}
and by Proposition
5.1
,
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}(K^{\eta})>0
. Finally, by Proposition
8.1
, for
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}
-a.e. point
x\in K^{\eta}
and for all integers
k\geq 0
, there exists
T_{n,k}\in\tang(K^{\eta},x)
such that
T_{n,k}
has exactly
\frac{1}{5n-7}((5n-6)^{k}-1)
many cut points. By Lemma
2.7
, it follows that tangents
T_{n,0},T_{n,1},\dots
are pairwise topologically distinct.

By Assouad’s embedding theorem
[
Ass83
]
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a bi-Lipschitz embedding of the metric space
(K^{\eta},|\cdot|^{\alpha_{n}/s})
into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and denote by
\Gamma
the embedded image. This mapping produces a
(\alpha_{n}/s)
-bi-Hölder homeomorphism
g:K^{\eta}\to\Gamma
. Therefore, there exists some
C>1
such that for every Borel
B\subset K^{\eta}
,

C^{-1}\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}(B)\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}(g(B))\leq C\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{n}}(B),

so
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Gamma)>0
. Moreover, the map
g\circ f:[0,1]\to\Gamma
is a
(1/s)
-Hölder surjection. By Lemma
9.1
, we have that for every point
x\in K^{\eta}
and every tangent
T\in\tang(K^{\eta},x)
there exists a tangent
T^{\prime}\in\tang(\Gamma,g(x))
so that
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
(\alpha_{n}/s)
-bi-Hölder equivalent; in particular,
T
and
T^{\prime}
are homeomorphic to each other. Therefore,
\tang(\Gamma,x)
contains infinitely many topologically distinct elements.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Without loss of generality, assume that

[

\mathcal{H}^{s}(Q\cap U)=\mathcal{H}^{s}(Q)

Fix

,
S\in\tang(Q,x)
, and
R>0

r_{1}<1/R
and the sets
D_{j}:=(r_{j})^{-1}(Q-x)

in the Attouch-Wets topology as
j\to\infty

j\in\mathbb{N}
the similarity
g_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

g_{j}(y)=(r_{j})^{-1}(y-x)

g_{j}(Q)=D_{j}
. Define the alphabet

and for a word

[
BV21

Note that if

(10.1)

L_{1}r_{j}R\leq L_{w}<r_{j}R.

For each

W^{R}_{j}:=\{w\in A^{*}:\phi_{w}(Q)\cap\overline{B}(x,r_{j}R)\neq\emptyset\}\quad\text{and}\quad C^{R}_{j}:=W^{R}_{j}\cap A^{*}(r_{j}R).

Note that for each
j

\overline{B}(x,r_{j}R)\cap Q\subset\bigcup_{w\in C^{R}_{j}}\phi_{w}(Q)
. Additionally, by the OSC, if

or

\frac{L_{w}}{L_{v}}\leq L_{1}
or
\frac{L_{v}}{L_{w}}\leq L_{1}

j\in\mathbb{N}
and

. Roughly speaking, each set

respects the “disjoint images” part of the open set condition for
U
. Observe that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and each
w\in C^{R}_{j}
by (
10.1
), the fact that

and the fact that

. Hence, for each

,

(10.2)

\overline{B}(x,r_{j}R)\cap Q\subset\bigcup\limits_{w\in C^{R}_{j}}\phi_{w}(Q)\subset\overline{B}(x,2r_{j}R)\cap Q.

Next, since
Q
is self-similar, it follows that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(Q)>0

[
Hut81
, §5.3]
and, in fact,

is Ahlfors
s
-regular
[
BV21
, Lemma 2.4]
. That is, there exists

so that for every

and

Moreover, by
[
Hut81
, Theorem 5.3.1]
, for every

we have
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\phi_{w}(Q))=L_{w}^{s}
.

We claim that
\card(C^{R}_{j})\leq(2/L_{1})^{s}c_{1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. To this end, fix

. By (
10.2
),

\card(C^{R}_{j})(L_{1}r_{j}R)^{s}\leq\sum_{w\in C^{R}_{j}}\mathcal{H}^{s}(\phi_{w}(Q))=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\bigcup\limits_{w\in C^{R}_{j}}\phi_{w}(Q))\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}(\overline{B}(x,2r_{j}R)\cap Q)\leq(2R)^{s}c_{1}(r_{j})^{s},

where the equality follows from the fact that sets

for

The claimed inequality follows.

By the preceding claim, the sequence

is a bounded sequence of positive integers, so passing to a subsequence, we may assume that it is constant. That is, there exists an integer

such that
\card(C^{R}_{j})=M_{R}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
We write
C_{j}^{R}=\{w_{1,j}^{R},\dots,w_{M_{R},j}^{R}\}
. Note that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and

the map
g_{j}\circ\phi_{w_{i,j}^{R}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a similarity with
L_{1}R\leq\Lip(g_{j}\circ\phi_{w_{i,j}^{R}})\leq R
. Moreover,

D_{j}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{M_{R}}(g_{j}\circ\phi_{w_{i,j}^{R}}(Q))\subset D_{j}\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},2R),

and if

are distinct, then
g_{j}\circ\phi_{w_{i_{1},j}^{R}}(U)\cap g_{j}\circ\phi_{w_{i_{2},j}^{R}}(U)=\emptyset
.

Since the similarities

i\in\{1,\dots,M_{R}\}
,

locally uniformly as

. Since

as

. Furthermore by the properties of the maps

.

To complete the proof, we show that for every

. Since

, which implies that

. By properties of functions

Furthermore, if

10.1.
Self-similar sponges

We say that a set
K\subset[0,1]^{N}
is a
self-similar sponge
if
K
is the attractor of a system

(10.3)

\{S_{i}(y)=k^{-1}y+p_{i}:[0,1]^{N}\to[0,1]^{N}\}_{i=1}^{m},

where
k\in\{2,3,\dots\}
, and points
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
are mutually distinct and contained in the set
\{0,\frac{1}{k},\dots,\frac{k-1}{k}\}^{N}
. It is easy to see that the system
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
satisfies the OSC with
U=(0,1)^{N}
.

We say that a subset
C\subset[0,1]^{N}
is a
face
of
[0,1]^{N}
if
C
is of the form
C=I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{N}
, where each
I_{j}
is either equal to
[0,1]
, to
\{0\}
, or to
\{1\}
. Additionally, we call
C=I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{N}
an
M
-face of
[0,1]^{N}
if exactly
M
-many of the
I_{1},\dots,I_{N}
are nondegenerate.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
be a system of similarities as in (
10.3
). If
i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\in\{1,\dots,m\}
are distinct indices such that
\bigcap_{j=1}^{l}S_{i_{j}}([0,1]^{N})\neq\emptyset
, then this intersection is equal to
S_{i_{1}}(C)
, where
C
is a face of
[0,1]^{N}
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, denote by
z(m)
the
m
-th coordinate of
z
.

We begin by proving the result in the case
l=2
. For simplicity, assume that
i_{1}=1
and
i_{2}=2
. Since
S_{1}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{2}([0,1]^{N})\neq\emptyset
and since
S_{1}((0,1)^{N})\cap S_{2}((0,1)^{N})=\emptyset
, we have that

e:=S_{1}({\bf 0})-S_{2}({\bf 0})\in\{-k^{-1},0,k^{-1}\}^{N}.

Write
e=(e(1),\dots,e(N))
. For
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, set
I_{m}=[0,1]
if
e(m)=0
,
I_{m}=\{0\}
if
e(m)>0
, and
I_{m}=\{1\}
if
e(m)<0
. We claim that

S_{1}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{2}([0,1]^{N})=S_{1}(I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{N}).

To see this, fix
z_{1},z_{2}\in[0,1]^{N}
such that
S_{1}(z_{1})=S_{2}(z_{2})
. Then
z_{2}-z_{1}=ke
and there are three cases to consider for
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. If
e(m)=0
, then
z_{2}(m)-z_{1}(m)=0
which means that
z_{1}(m)
can take any value in
[0,1]
. If
e(m)>0
, then
e(m)=k^{-1}
, and
z_{2}(m)-z_{1}(m)=1
which implies that
z_{1}(m)=0
. If
e(m)<0
, then
e(m)=-k^{-1}
, and
z_{2}(m)-z_{1}(m)=-1
which implies that
z_{1}(m)=1
. In either of the three cases,
z_{1}\in I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{N}
. This proves the claim and since
I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{N}
is a face of
[0,1]^{N}
, the lemma for
l=2
.

For the case
l\geq 3
, we have that

\bigcap_{j=1}^{l}S_{i_{j}}([0,1]^{N})=\bigcap_{j=2}^{l}(S_{i_{1}}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{i_{j}}([0,1]^{N})).

By the previous case, each
S_{i_{1}}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{i_{j}}([0,1]^{N})=S_{i_{1}}(C_{j})
, where
C_{j}
is a face of
[0,1]^{N}
. The nonempty intersection of faces of
[0,1]^{N}
is also a face of
[0,1]^{N}
, and the result follows.
∎

We say that a set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
locally self-similar
if for every pair of points
x,y\in F
and every pair of scales
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0
, there exists a similarity
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
g(B(x,\rho_{1})\cap F)
is a subset of
B(y,\rho_{2})\cap F
and is open relative to
F
. Note that if
F
is locally self-similar, then it cannot have a positive and finite number of local cut-points.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
K
be a self-similar sponge. Then for every
x\in(0,1)^{N}\cap K
, every tangent
T\in\tang(K,x)
is locally self-similar.

Proof.

Suppose that
K
is the attractor of
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
with
S_{i}(y)=k^{-1}y+p_{i}
where
k
and
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
are as in (
10.3
). If
m=1
, then the result is trivial, so we may assume that
m\geq 2
. Set
A=\{1,\dots,m\}
. Note that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v\in A^{n}
, and
i\in A
,

S_{iv}(y)=k^{-n-1}y+S_{iv}({\bf 0})=k^{-n-1}y+k^{-1}S_{v}({\bf 0})+S_{i}({\bf 0})

and that
S_{iv}({\bf 0})\in\{0,\frac{1}{k^{n+1}},\dots,1-\frac{1}{k^{n+1}}\}^{N}
. Thus by a simple inductive argument, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
w\in A^{n}
,
S_{w}(y)=k^{-n}y+S_{w}({\bf 0})
, where
S_{w}({\bf 0})\in\{0,\frac{1}{k^{n}},\dots,\frac{k^{n}-1}{k^{n}}\}^{N}
.

If
(0,1)^{N}\cap K=\emptyset
, then the result is trivial, so assume
(0,1)^{N}\cap K\neq\emptyset
. Fix
x\in(0,1)^{N}\cap K
,
T\in\tang(K,x)
,
p,q\in T
,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0
, and a sequence of positive scales
r_{j}\to 0
so that the sets
X_{j}:=(r_{j})^{-1}(K-x)
converge to
T
in the Attouch-Wets topology as
j\to\infty
.

We first show that there exist a set
U\subset K\cap(0,1)^{N}
open relative to
K
, and a similarity map
G:U\to B(p,\rho_{1})\cap T
which is surjective. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define
g_{j}:K\to X_{j}
by
g_{j}(y)=(r_{j})^{-1}(y-x)
, and set
B_{j}:=B(r_{j}p+x,\rho_{1}r_{j})\cap K
. Since
X_{j}\to T
as
j\to\infty
in the Attouch-Wets topology, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\exc(B(p,\rho_{1}/2)\cap T,g_{j}(B_{j}))=0,

so passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Additionally, since
\lim_{j\to\infty}\diam(B_{j})=0
, since
x\in K\cap(0,1)^{N}
, and since
\lim_{j\to\infty}\dist(x,B_{j})=0
,
again passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
B_{j}\subset K\cap(0,1)^{N}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

2\rho_{1}r_{j}<k^{-n_{j}}\leq 2\rho_{1}kr_{j}

and define

W_{j}:=\{v\in A^{n_{j}}:S_{v}([0,1]^{N})\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset\}.

The sets
W_{j}
are nonempty and a simple computation shows that

B_{j}\subset\bigcup\limits_{v\in W_{j}}S_{v}(K)\subset K\cap B(r_{j}p+x,4k\sqrt{N}\rho_{1}r_{j}).

Thus we may assume that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\bigcup_{v\in W_{j}}S_{v}(K)\subset K\cap(0,1)^{N}
. Furthermore, by the OSC, for every pair of distinct words
w,v\in W_{j}
,
S_{v}((0,1)^{N})\cap S_{w}((0,1)^{N})=\emptyset
. Therefore, by the doubling property of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\card(W_{j})\leq M^{\prime}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

We claim that
\bigcap_{w\in W_{j}}S_{w}([0,1]^{N})\neq\emptyset
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We first show the claim for two words only. Fix distinct
v,w\in W_{j}
and let
z_{v},z_{w}\in[0,1]^{N}
so that

|S_{v}(z_{v})-(r_{j}p+x)|<\rho_{1}r_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad|S_{w}(z_{w})-(r_{j}p+x)|<\rho_{1}r_{j},

so by the triangle inequality we obtain

\dist(S_{v}([0,1]^{N}),S_{w}([0,1]^{N}))<2\rho_{1}r_{j}<k^{-n_{j}}.

Therefore,
S_{w}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{v}([0,1]^{N})\neq\emptyset
and the claim in this special case holds.

For
l\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
w\in W_{j}
, denote by
S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}
the
l
-th coordinate of
S_{w}({\bf 0})
. Note that if
w,v\in W_{j}
are distinct and
S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}>0
for some
l\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, then
S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}=k^{-n_{j}}
. This further implies that if
u\in W_{j}\setminus\{w,v\}
, then
S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{u}({\bf 0})^{(l)}\geq 0
.

Fix now a word
v\in W_{j}
. For each
w\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}
and
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, let

I_{w}^{l}:=\begin{cases}[0,1],&\text{ if $S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}=0$}\\
\{0\},&\text{ if $S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}=k^{-n_{j}}$}\\
\{1\},&\text{ if $S_{v}({\bf 0})^{(l)}-S_{w}({\bf 0})^{(l)}=-k^{-n_{j}}$.}\\
\end{cases}

For each
l\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, if there exists
w\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}
with
I_{w}^{l}=\{0\}
, then for every
u\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}
either
I_{u}^{l}=\{0\}
or
I_{u}^{l}=[0,1]
, and similarly in the case that
I_{w}^{l}=\{1\}
. In particular,
I_{w}^{l}\cap I_{u}^{l}\neq\emptyset
for every pair of words
w,u\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}
. Therefore
\bigcap_{w\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}}(I_{w}^{1}\times\dots\times I_{w}^{N})\neq\emptyset
. By Lemma
10.2
,
S_{v}(I_{w}^{1}\times\dots\times I_{w}^{N})=S_{v}([0,1]^{N})\cap S_{w}([0,1]^{N})
for each
w\in W_{j}\setminus\{v\}
, and by the special case this intersection is nonempty. Thus we obtain
\bigcap_{w\in W_{j}}S_{w}([0,1]^{N})\neq\emptyset
, proving the claim.

Since the sequence
(\card(W_{j}))_{j}
is bounded, passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\card(W_{j})=M
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Write
W_{j}=\{v^{j}_{1},\dots,v^{j}_{M}\}
and note that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,M\}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
g_{j}\circ S_{v_{i}^{j}}:K\to B(p,4k\sqrt{N}\rho_{1})\cap X_{j}
is a similarity with
\Lip(g_{j}\circ S_{v_{i}^{j}})\in(2\rho_{1},2\rho_{1}k]
. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and passing to a subsequence, we may assume that that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,M\}
, the maps
g_{j}\circ S_{v_{i}^{j}}
converge uniformly to a similarity
f_{i}:K\to T
as
j\to\infty
. Moreover, for some
L\in(2\rho_{1},2\rho_{1}k]
, we have
\Lip(f_{i})=L
for all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,M\}
. Additionally,

B(p,\rho_{1})\cap T\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}f_{i}(K),

Appendix A
A Lipschitz curve and a point with extreme tangent space

Recall that
\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
denotes the collection of all sets
X\in\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
(i.e. closed sets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
that contain the origin

) such that every component of

is unbounded. In Lemma
2.5
we showed that for every non-constant Lipschitz curve in

\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})

Theorem A.1
.

For each
N\in\{2,3,\dots\}
there exists a Lipschitz curve
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

and
\tang(f([0,1]),{\bf 0})=\mathfrak{C}_{U}(\mathbb{R}^{N};{\bf 0})
.

Fix, for the rest of the appendix, an integer
N\geq 2
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
denote by
\mathcal{G}_{k}
the collection of all connected graphs
G=(V,E)
such that

(1)

\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\{2^{-k}\textbf{m}:\textbf{m}\in\partial[-2^{k},2^{k}]^{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}\subset V\subset\{2^{-k}\textbf{m}:\textbf{m}\in[-2^{k},2^{k}]^{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}

(2)

for

, if and only if

We can enumerate

and for

to be the unique positive integer with

Note that for all

we have
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\text{Im}(G_{n}))\leq(2^{k_{n}+1}+1)^{N}

Let

be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers such that

and for each

For each

let

Lemma A.2

There exists a Lipschitz surjection

First note that

\displaystyle\exc(T\cap[2^{-j}-2^{j},2^{j}-2^{-j}]^{N},2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-j}\sqrt{N}

\displaystyle\exc(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}})\cap[2^{-j}-2^{j},2^{j}-2^{-j}]^{N},T)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-j}(\sqrt{N}+1/4).

This implies that for every
R>0
,

(A.1)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))=0,\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\exc(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),T)=0.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Let
\rho_{j}=2^{j}(r_{n_{j}})^{-1}>0
. We claim that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho_{j}H=T
, with respect to the Attouch-Wets topology.

Recall that
k_{n_{j}}=2j
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, so
r_{n_{j}+1}\rho_{j}\leq 2^{-n_{j}-j+1}
and
\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}}=2^{j}
. Hence, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\rho_{j}H_{n_{j}})\setminus(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N})=(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))\setminus(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N}).

Fix
R>0
, and let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{j_{0}-1}\geq R
. For every integer
j\geq j_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{j}H\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)

\displaystyle\subset\left((\rho_{j}H_{n_{j}})\cup(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N})\right)\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R)

\displaystyle=\left(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))\cup(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N})\right)\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R).

Thus by design of
G_{n_{j}}
and by the monotonicity and subadditivity properties of excess (see Remark
2.2
) , we have

\displaystyle\exc(\rho_{j}H\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),T)

\displaystyle\leq\exc(\left(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))\cup(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N})\right)\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),T)

\displaystyle\leq\exc(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}})\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),T)+\exc(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N},T).

As
j\to\infty
, by (
A.1
) the first term in the latter sum approaches
0
. Furthermore, since
{\bf 0}\in T
and as
j\to\infty

\diam(2^{-n_{j}-j+1}[-1,1]^{N})
approaches
0
, as
j\to\infty
the second term in the latter sum approaches
0
as well.

We now show that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),\rho_{j}H)=0
. Note that

(\rho_{j}H_{n_{j}})\setminus(\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N})\subset\rho_{j}H,

so by monotonicity and triangle inequality for excess (as in Remark
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),\rho_{j}H)

\displaystyle\leq\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),(\rho_{j}H_{n_{j}})\setminus(\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N})

\displaystyle=\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))\setminus(\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N}))

\displaystyle\leq\exc(T\cap\overline{B}({\bf 0},R),2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))

\displaystyle+\exc(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}),(2^{j}\text{Im}(G_{n_{j}}))\setminus(\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N})).

As
j\to\infty
, the first term of the latter sum approaches
0
by (
A.1
) and by design of the sets
G_{n_{j}}
. In the second term of the latter sum, the sets differ only inside
\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N}
, so the excess is at most
\diam(\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}[-1,1]^{N})\leq 2\rho_{j}r_{n_{j}+1}\sqrt{N}
, which approaches
0
as
j\to\infty
. This completes the proof, by definition of convergence
\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho_{j}H
in the Attouch-Wets topology.
∎

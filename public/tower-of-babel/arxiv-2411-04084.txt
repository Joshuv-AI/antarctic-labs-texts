1.
Introduction

One of the most celebrated problems in Euclidean Harmonic analysis is the Carleson’s problem: determining the optimal regularity of the initial condition
f
of the
Schrödinger equation given by

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u\>,\>(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\>,\end{cases}

in terms of the index
\alpha
such that
f
belongs to the inhomogeneous Sobolev space
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so that the solution of the Schrödinger operator
u
converges pointwise to
f
,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0+}u(x,t)=f(x)
, almost everywhere. Such a problem was first studied by Carleson
[
Ca80
]
and Dahlberg-Kenig
[
DK82
]
in dimension
1
, followed by several other experts in the field for arbitrary dimension (see
[
Co83
,
Sj87
,
Ve88
,
Bo16
,
DGL17
,
DZ19
]
and references therein).

Recently, the first author studied the Carleson’s problem for solutions of the Schrödinger equation corresponding to the Laplace-Beltrami operator in the setting of Damek-Ricci spaces
S

[
De24
]
. They are also known as Harmonic
NA
groups. These spaces
S
are non-unimodular, solvable extensions of Heisenberg type groups
N
, obtained by letting
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
act on
N
by homogeneous dilations. In
[
De24
]
, the author proved that the solution to the Schrödinger equation with initial data
f
converges to
f
almost everwyhere, if
f
is radial and belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{\alpha}(S)
for
\alpha\geq 1/4
. It was obtained by proving a boundedness result for the local Maximal function associated to the Schrödinger equation. To explain this, we need to introduce some notations.

Let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator
on
S
corresponding to the left-invariant Riemannian metric. Its
L^{2}
-spectrum is the half line
(-\infty,-Q^{2}/4]
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
N
. The Schrödinger equation on
S
is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u\>,\>(x,t)\in S\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\>\text{ on }S\>.\end{cases}

Then for a radial function
f
belonging to the
L^{2}
-Schwartz class (see (
2.6
)),

(1.2)

S_{t}f(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\>e^{it\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

is the solution to (
1.1
), where
\varphi_{\lambda}
are the spherical functions,
\widehat{f}
is the Spherical Fourier transform of
f
and
{\bf c}(\cdot)
denotes the Harish-Chandra’s
{\bf c}
-function.

To quantify the Carleson’s problem on
S
, for
\alpha\geq 0
, we recall the fractional
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces on
S

[
APV15
]
:

(1.3)

H^{\alpha}(S):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(S):{\|f\|}_{H^{\alpha}(S)}:={\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\alpha}{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}<\infty\right\}.

We note that by the Plancherel theorem,
{\|f\|}_{H^{0}(S)}={\|f\|}_{L^{2}(S)}
.

Finally, we define the local maximal function associated to the solution (
1.2
),

(1.4)

S^{*}f(x):=\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<4/Q^{2}}\left|S_{t}f(x)\right|\>.

The main result in
[
De24
]
is the following
L^{1}_{loc}
boundedness for the maximal function: for any
R>0
, on geodesic balls
B_{R}
centered at the identity with radius
R
, there exists a constant
C>0
(depending only on the dimension and
R
) such that

{\|S^{*}f\|}_{L^{1}\left(B_{R}\right)}\leq C\>{\|f\|}_{H^{1/4}(S)}\>,

holds for every radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class function
f
on
S
.

This brings us to the natural question of obtaining the complete description of the pairs
(q,\alpha)\in[1,\infty]\times[0,\infty)
for which one has the following boundedness estimate for any
R>0
, and every radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class function
f
on
S
:

(1.5)

{\|S^{*}f\|}_{L^{q}\left(B_{R}\right)}\leq C\>{\|f\|}_{H^{\alpha}(S)}\>.

In the classical Euclidean setting, this problem was studied by Sjölin
[
Sj97
, Theorem 3]
. In the (degenerate) case of
\mathbb{H}^{3}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2)
, using the explicit expression of the spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}
, the Plancherel measure and their close similarity with their Euclidean counterparts, proceeding as in the proof of
[
De24
, Theorem 1.4]
, one can reduce matters to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to get the exact result of Sjölin. But in the general case of Damek-Ricci spaces, no explicit expression of
\varphi_{\lambda}
is known. Moreover, the weight of the Plancherel measure exhibits dichotomy in asymptotics for small and large frequencies (see
[
RS09
, Lemma 4.8]
):

(1.6)

{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\asymp\>{|\lambda|}^{2}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{n-3}\>,

(
n
is the dimension of
S
). Thus the simple-minded approach of a straightforward reduction to the Euclidean case breaks down. Nevertheless, the (degenerate) case of
\mathbb{H}^{3}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2)
, serves well to provide us with an interesting conjecture to explore in the setting of Damek-Ricci spaces, which we answer in the affirmative:

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

If
\alpha<1/4
, then (
1.5
) holds for no
q
.

(ii)

If
1/4\leq\alpha<n/2
, then (
1.5
) holds if and only if
q\leq 2n/(n-2\alpha)
.

(iii)

If
\alpha=n/2
, then (
1.5
) holds if and only if
q<\infty
.

(iv)

If
\alpha>n/2
, then (
1.5
) holds for all
q
.

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Sjölin’s proof in
[
Sj97
]
hinges heavily on the small and large argument asymptotics of the Bessel functions and the oscillation afforded by them and the multiplier corresponding to the Schrödinger equation. Unlike in the Euclidean case, in the general setting of Damek-Ricci spaces however, no explicit expression of the spherical function is known. In fact, it only admits certain series expansions (the Bessel series expansion and a series expansion similar to the classical Harish-Chandra series expansion) depending on the geodesic distance from the identity of the group. This motivates us to first impose suitable restrictions on both the frequency of the Spherical Fourier transform of the initial condition as well as on the spacial variable and then to study the boundedness of the linearized maximal function for each such piece separately. Then Theorem
1.1
is eventually obtained by means of a cumulative synthesis of these simpler components, which in a way, is much more illuminating.

More precisely, in order to study the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
, we first consider the cases when the Spherical Fourier transform of the initial condition is supported in a neighborhood of the origin and when it is supported away from the origin. In the first case, the linearized maximal function is exceptionally well-behaved and is in fact quite straightforward to control. The latter case however, is quite involved. In the large frequency regime, we decompose the geodesic ball
B_{R}
into a closed ball and an open annulus,

B_{R}=\overline{B_{R_{0}}}\sqcup A(R_{0},R)\>.

•

While studying the subproblem on the ball
\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
, we expand the spherical function in the Bessel series (see Lemma
2.2
) and decompose the linearized maximal function into two pieces. By repeated applications of the Abel transform, followed by introducing a new Schwartz multiplier corresponding to the ratio of the weights of the Plancherel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
S
, we are able to draw an Euclidean connection to bound the first piece. The second piece is taken care of by pointwise estimates of certain error terms.

•

While working on the annulus
A(R_{0},R)
, there is no Bessel series expansion at our disposal and hence the Euclidean connection mentioned above ceases to exist. In stead, we work with a variation of the classical Harish-Chandra series expansion (see (
2.7
)) which has been recently obtained by Anker-Pierfelice-Vallarino
[
APV15
]
. Although this series expansion looks similar to the classical Harish-Chandra series expansion, it has the rather remarkable property of being constituted by coefficients having decay in the spectral parameter
\lambda
(see (
2.8
)), compared to the mere boundedness in
\lambda
of its counterpart in the classical expansion. This extra decay makes our life easy in the higher regularity regime (
\alpha>1/2
). But in the lower regularity scenario (when
1/4\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
), the above mentioned pointwise decay is not sufficient. In stead, we incorporate the above expansion itself to decompose the linearized maximal function into three pieces. Two of them are taken care of by an abstract
TT^{*}
argument, followed by estimating an oscillatory integral and then applying the one dimensional Riesz potential as well as the Pitt’s inequality. The third piece is then bounded by the pointwise decay of certain error terms.

Our study of the sufficient conditions for (
1.5
) gives rise to two critical endpoints
\alpha=1/4
and
n/2
, on either sides of which the behavior of the local Schrödinger maximal function changes. This is where our ‘piecewise’ approach helps us to identify that counter-examples can only occur when the Spherical Fourier transform of the initial condition is supported away from the origin.

•

To show that no local estimate (
1.5
) holds when
\alpha<1/4
, we work with a radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class function whose Spherical Fourier transform is supported away from the origin and the divergence in the spacial estimate is done in a small annulus centered at the identity. Thus again we implement the Bessel series expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
, the Abel transform and the Schwartz multiplier ideas to form an Euclidean connection.

•

Next we give an explicit construction to establish the necessity of the condition
q\leq 2n/(n-2\alpha)
under the assumption
1/4\leq\alpha<n/2
.

•

Finally to prove the failure of the inequality (
1.5
) for
\alpha=n/2
and
q=\infty
, we weave several ideas explored in the study of the sufficient conditions for different pieces of the linearized maximal function. In the process, we obtain the failure of the delicate local borderline fractional Sobolev embedding, which can be viewed as a refinement of the corresponding global borderline fractional Sobolev embedding obtained by Cowling-Giulini-Meda
[
CGM93
, Theorem 4.7]
(see remark
5.1
).

Theorem
1.1
asserts the sharpness of
\alpha\geq 1/4
for the problem of pointwise convergence of the solution of the Schrödinger equation to its radial initial data almost everywhere, considered in
[
De24
]
and thus generalizing
[
De24
, Theorem 1.4]
to all Damek-Ricci spaces. We also note that in the degenerate case of the Real hyperbolic spaces, the arguments presented in this article can be carried out verbatim to obtain the analogue of Theorem
1.1
.

This article is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall certain aspects of Euclidean Fourier Analysis and the essential preliminaries about Damek-Ricci spaces and Spherical Fourier Analysis thereon. In section
3
, we obtain an estimate of an oscillatory integral which is useful for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The sufficient and necessary conditions of Theorem
1.1
are proved in sections
4
and
5
respectively.

Throughout, the symbols ‘c’ and ‘C’ will denote positive constants whose values may change on each occurrence.
\mathbb{N}
will denote the set of positive integers. Two non-negative functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
will be said to satisfy,

•

f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
, so that

f_{1}\leq Cf_{2}\>.

•

f_{1}\asymp f_{2}
if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
, so that

\frac{1}{C}f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq Cf_{1}\>.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminaries and fix our notations.

2.1.
Fourier Analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

In this subsection, we recall some Euclidean Fourier Analysis, most of which can be found in
[
Gr09
,
SW90
]
.
On
\mathbb{R}
, for “nice” functions
f
, the Fourier transform
\tilde{f}
is defined as

\tilde{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\>e^{-ix\xi}\>dx\>.

An important inequality in one-dimensional Fourier Analysis is the Pitt’s inequality:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
St56
, p. 489]

One has the inequality

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|\tilde{f}(\xi)\right|}^{2}\>{|\xi|}^{-2\alpha}d\xi\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|f(x)\right|}^{p}\>{|x|}^{\alpha_{1}p}dx\right)}^{1/p}\>,

where
\alpha_{1}=\alpha+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and the following two conditions are satisfied:

0\leq\alpha_{1}<1-\frac{1}{p}\>,\>\text{ and }0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{2}\>.

A
C^{\infty}
function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
is called a Schwartz class function if

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)}^{M}f(x)\right|\lesssim{(1+|x|)}^{-N}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
the class of all such functions and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
will denote the collection of all even Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re(\alpha)>0
. The Riesz potential of order
\alpha
is the operator

I_{\alpha}={(-\Delta)}^{-\alpha/2}\>.

One can also write as,

I_{\alpha}(f)(x)=C_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\>{|x-y|}^{\alpha-1}\>dy\>,

for some
C_{\alpha}>0
, whenever
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. For
\alpha>0
, the Fourier transform of the Riesz potential of
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the following identity:

(2.1)

(I_{\alpha}(f))^{\sim}(\xi)=C_{\alpha}{|\xi|}^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(\xi)\>.

For a radial function
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for
n\geq 2
), the Euclidean Spherical Fourier transform is defined as

\mathscr{F}f(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda r)\>r^{n-1}\>dr\>,

where for all
\mu\geq 0
,

\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(z)=2^{\mu}\>\pi^{1/2}\>\Gamma\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{J_{\mu}(z)}{z^{\mu}},

and
J_{\mu}
are the Bessel functions
[
SW90
, p. 154]
.

The class of radial Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, is defined to be the collection of
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
such that

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{dr}\right)}^{M}f(r)\right|\lesssim{(1+r)}^{-N}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>,

where
r=\|x\|
, is the distance of
x
from the origin.
\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}

defines a topological isomorphism.

For
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, the solution to the Schrödinger equation:

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u\>,\>(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\>,\end{cases}

is given by,

u(\|x\|,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda\|x\|)\>e^{it\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda\>.

2.2.
Damek-Ricci spaces and spherical Fourier Analysis thereon:

In this section, we will explain the notations and state relevant results on Damek-Ricci spaces. Most of these results can be found in
[
ADY96
,
APV15
,
As95
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a two-step real nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with an inner product
\langle,\rangle
. Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{v}
its orthogonal complement. We say that
\mathfrak{n}
is an
H
-type algebra if for every
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
the map
J_{Z}:\mathfrak{v}\to\mathfrak{v}
defined by

\langle J_{z}X,Y\rangle=\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle,\>\>\>\>X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}

satisfies the condition
J_{Z}^{2}=-|Z|^{2}I_{\mathfrak{v}}
,
I_{\mathfrak{v}}
being the identity operator on
\mathfrak{v}
. A connected and simply connected Lie group
N
is called an
H
-type group if its Lie algebra is
H
-type. Since
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent, the exponential map is a diffeomorphism
and hence we can parametrize the elements in
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
by
(X,Z)
, for
X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. It follows from the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula that the group law in
N
is given by

\left(X,Z\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+X^{\prime},Z+Z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[X,X^{\prime}]\right),\>\>\>\>X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z}.

The group
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
acts on an
H
-type group
N
by nonisotropic dilation:
(X,Z)\mapsto(\sqrt{a}X,aZ)
. Let
S=NA
be the semidirect product of
N
and
A
under the above action. Thus the multiplication in
S
is given by

\left(X,Z,a\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},a^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+\sqrt{a}X^{\prime},Z+aZ^{\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{a}}{2}[X,X^{\prime}],aa^{\prime}\right),

for
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z};a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Then
S
is a solvable, connected and simply connected Lie group having Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with Lie bracket

\left[\left(X,Z,l\right),\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime}\right)\right]=\left(\frac{1}{2}lX^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}l^{\prime}X,lZ^{\prime}-lZ+[X,X^{\prime}],0\right).

We write
na=(X,Z,a)
for the element
\exp(X+Z)a,X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z},a\in A
. We note
that for any
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
with
|Z|=1
,
J_{Z}^{2}=-I_{\mathfrak{v}}
; that is,
J_{Z}
defines a complex structure
on
\mathfrak{v}
and hence
\mathfrak{v}
is even dimensional.
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{z}
will denote the dimension of
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
respectively. Let
n
and
Q
denote dimension and the homogenous dimension of
S
respectively:

n=m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}+1\>\>\textit{ and }\>\>Q=\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}}{2}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}.

The group
S
is equipped with the left-invariant Riemannian metric induced by

\langle(X,Z,l),(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime})\rangle=\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle+\langle Z,Z^{\prime}\rangle+ll^{\prime}

on
\mathfrak{s}
. For
x\in S
, we denote by
s=d(e,x)
, that is, the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. Then the left Haar measure
dx
of the group
S
may be normalized so that

dx=A(s)\>ds\>d\sigma(\omega)\>,

where
A
is the density function given by,

(2.2)

A(s)=2^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\sinh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\cosh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>,

and
d\sigma
is the surface measure of the unit sphere.

A function
f:S\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be radial if, for all
x
in
S
,
f(x)
depends only on the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. If
f
is radial, then

\int_{S}f(x)~dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)~A(s)~ds\>.

We now recall the spherical functions on Damek-Ricci spaces. The spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}
on
S
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
are the radial eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
, satisfying the following normalization criterion

\begin{cases}&\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}=-\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}\\
&\varphi_{\lambda}(e)=1\>.\end{cases}

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in S
, the spherical functions satisfy

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=\varphi_{-\lambda}(s)\>.

It also satisfies for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
s\geq 0
:

(2.3)

\left|\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\right|\leq 1\>.

The spherical functions are crucial as they help us define the Spherical Fourier transform of a “nice” radial function
f
(on
S
) in the following way:

\widehat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{S}f(x)\varphi_{\lambda}(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)\varphi_{\lambda}(s)A(s)ds\>.

The Harish-Chandra
{\bf c}
-function is defined as

(2.4)

{\bf c}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{(Q-2i\lambda)}\Gamma(2i\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{Q+2i\lambda}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+4i\lambda+2}{4}\right)}\>,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we have the following derivative estimates (
[
As95
, Lemma 4.2]
):

(2.5)

\left|\frac{d^{j}}{d\lambda^{j}}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\right|\lesssim_{j}{(1+|\lambda|)}^{n-1-j}\>,\>\>\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

One has the following inversion formula (when valid) for radial functions:

f(x)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\varphi_{\lambda}(x){|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\>,

where
C
depends only on
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{z}}
. Moreover, the Spherical Fourier transform extends to an isometry from the space of radial
L^{2}
functions on
S
onto
L^{2}\left((0,\infty),C{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)
.

The class of radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class functions on
S
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, is defined to be the collection of
f\in C^{\infty}(S)_{o}
such that

(2.6)

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)}^{M}f(s)\right|\lesssim{(1+s)}^{-N}e^{-\frac{Q}{2}s}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>,

(see
[
ADY96
, p. 652]
). One has the following commutative diagram, where every map is a topological isomorphism:

•

\mathscr{A}_{S,\mathbb{R}}
is the Abel transform defined from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
to
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
.

•

\wedge
denotes the Spherical Fourier transform from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
to
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
.

•

\sim
denotes the 1-dimensional Euclidean Fourier transform from
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
to itself.

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For the purpose of our article, we will require both the Bessel series expansion as well as another series expansion (similar to the Harish-Chandra series expansion) of the spherical functions. We first see an expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
in terms of Bessel functions for points near the identity. But before that, let us define the following normalizing constant in terms of the Gamma functions,

c_{0}=2^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>\pi^{-1/2}\>\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}\>.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
As95
, Theorem 3.1]

There exist
R_{0},2<R_{0}<2R_{1}
, such that for any
0\leq s\leq R_{0}
, and any integer
M\geq 0
, and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{M}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}+E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\>,

where

a_{0}\equiv 1\>,\>|a_{l}(s)|\leq C{(4R_{1})}^{-l}\>,

and the error term has the following behaviour

\left|E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\right|\leq C_{M}\begin{cases}s^{2(M+1)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|\leq 1\\
s^{2(M+1)}{|\lambda s|}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+M+1\right)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|>1\>.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
0\leq s<2
, the series

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}\>,

is absolutely convergent.

For the asymptotic behaviour of the spherical functions when the distance from the identity is large, we look at the following series expansion
[
APV15
, pp. 735-736]
:

(2.7)

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=2^{-m_{\mathfrak{z}}/2}{A(s)}^{-1/2}\left\{{\bf c}(\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)e^{(i\lambda-\mu)s}+{\bf c}(-\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(-\lambda)e^{-(i\lambda+\mu)s}\right\}\>.

The above series converges for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, uniformly on compacts not containing the group identity, where
\Gamma_{0}\equiv 1
and for
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, one has the recursion formula,

(\mu^{2}-2i\mu\lambda)\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\omega_{\mu-j}\Gamma_{j}(\lambda)\>.

Then one has the following estimate on the coefficients
[
APV15
, Lemma 1]
, for constants
C>0,d\geq 0
:

(2.8)

\left|\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)\right|\leq C\mu^{d}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{-1}\>,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\mu\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, for any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, one has the pointwise decay of
\varphi_{\lambda}
when
\lambda>c_{1}
and
s>c_{2}
, (
[
BD23
, Proposition 3.1]
)

(2.9)

|\varphi_{\lambda}(s)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{Q}{2}s}\>.

The relevant preliminaries for the degenerate case of the Real hyperbolic spaces can be found in
[
An91
,
ST78
,
AP14
]
.

3.
Estimate of an Oscillatory integral

In this section, we prove an estimate of an oscillatory integral which will be crucial in the proof of Theorem

is the positive constant as in Lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

and

.

Proof.

By
[

, Lemma 3.1]
, there exists a constant

, depending only on the dimension

such that for all

\left|\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>,

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\frac{1}{R_{0}}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\frac{1}{R_{0}}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{1/2}}

Thus it is justified to decompose the following integral as

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\frac{1}{R_{0}}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda

\displaystyle-\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\frac{1}{R_{0}}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda\>.

Hence by (

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\frac{1}{R_{0}}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\max\left\{\frac{B(n)}{s},\frac{B(n)}{s^{\prime}}\right\}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{e^{i\left\{\lambda(s^{\prime}-s)+(t(s^{\prime})-t(s))\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}}}{{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{1/4}}d\lambda\right|

∎

4.

In this subsection, we work under the assumption that

\displaystyle\int_{N-\sqrt{N}}^{N+\sqrt{N}}s^{2}{(\lambda s)}^{-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\>\left|\widehat{g}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{{\|\phi\|}_{\infty}}{s^{\left(\frac{n-3}{2}\right)}}\int_{N-\sqrt{N}}^{N+\sqrt{N}}\lambda^{-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\>N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\>\lambda^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{N-\sqrt{N}}^{N+\sqrt{N}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\>.

Then combining (
5.2
), (d) and (
5.4
), we get that for
\varepsilon>0
, small and
N
sufficiently large, there exists
c^{\prime\prime}>0
, such that

(5.5)

\left|Tg(s)\right|\geq c^{\prime\prime}\>,

for all
\varepsilon\leq s\leq 2\varepsilon
. Then in view of (
5.3
) and (
5.5
) the result follows.

Next we prove when
1/4\leq\alpha<n/2
, the condition
q\leq 2n/(n-2\alpha)
is necessary in order to get the boundedness (
1.5
). Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
be (non-zero) non-negative with
Supp(\psi)\subset(1,2)
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, by Schwartz isomorphism theorem, there exists
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
such that
\widehat{f}(\lambda)=\psi(\lambda/N)
. Then

(5.6)

\displaystyle{\|f\|}_{H^{\alpha}(S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\alpha}\psi\left(\lambda/N\right)^{2}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle N^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}{\left(\int_{1}^{2}\psi(\lambda)^{2}\>\lambda^{2\alpha+n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}\>.

Next by Fourier inversion, we see that for some
c>0
,

\displaystyle S_{0}f(e)=f(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda/N)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle N^{n}\int_{1}^{2}\psi(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\>N^{n}\>.

Therefore, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
, such that for all
x\in B_{\varepsilon/N}
,

(5.7)

S^{*}f(x)\geq c\>N^{n}\>.

Now if the estimate (
1.5
) holds, then by (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), it follows that

{\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon/N}N^{nq}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/q}\lesssim N^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}\>,

which implies that

N^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\lesssim N^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}\>.

Then letting
N\to\infty
, it follows that

n\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\leq\alpha+\frac{n}{2}\>,

that is,
q\leq 2n/(n-2\alpha)
.

We are now left to prove the failure of

{\|S^{*}f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>,

which follows if we can show that the following inequality fails,

(5.8)

{\|f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>.

For the sake of contradiction, let us assume that for some ball
B_{R}
centered at
e
with radius
R
smaller than
R_{0}
, (
5.8
) is valid for all functions in
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
with their Spherical Fourier transform supported in
(1,\infty)
. Let us choose and fix such a function
f
. Then by Fourier inversion and the Bessel series expansion, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda s)\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{\infty}E_{1}(\lambda,s)\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(s)+f_{2}(s)\>.

Then by the proof of Lemma
4.5
, it follows that

(5.10)

{\|f_{2}\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>.

Thus by (
5.8
)-(
5.10
), we have that

(5.11)

{\|f_{1}\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>.

We next consider
g:=(\mathcal{M}\circ\mathscr{A})f
, where
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathscr{A}
are as in Lemma
4.4
and observe that
\mathcal{M}\circ\mathscr{A}
defines a bijection from the subspace of functions in
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
whose Spherical Fourier transforms are supported in
(1,\infty)
onto the subspace of functions in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
whose Euclidean Spherical Fourier transforms are supported in
(1,\infty)
. Then proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we get that

•

{\|f_{1}\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\asymp{\|g\|}_{L^{\infty}(B(o,R))}
,

•

{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\asymp{\|g\|}_{H^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,

with the implicit constant appearing in the first inequality depending only on the ball
B_{R}
. Then combining the above with (
5.11
), we get that

{\|g\|}_{L^{\infty}(B(o,R))}\lesssim{\|f_{1}\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\lesssim{\|g\|}_{H^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

with the resultant implicit constant depending only on the ball
B(o,R)
. As
f
was arbitrarily chosen, it follows that the above inequality is true for all functions in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
whose Euclidean Spherical Fourier transforms are supported in
(1,\infty)
. Now by an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
4.1
, it follows that for all
g
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
whose Euclidean Spherical Fourier transforms are supported in
[0,2)
, we also have

{\|g\|}_{L^{\infty}(B(o,R))}\lesssim{\|g\|}_{H^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>.

Then an argument as in the proof of the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
(subsection
4.3
) involving smooth resolution of identity would give,

{\|g\|}_{L^{\infty}(B(o,R))}\lesssim{\|g\|}_{H^{n/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

for all
g
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
. But this contradicts the result of Sjölin, and our proof is complete. (For an explicit counter-example in the Euclidean setting, we refer the reader to
[
Gr09
, p. 9, Exercise 6.1.4]
).
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the special case of rank one Riemannian Symmetric spaces of noncompact type
G/K
, one has the failure of the borderline fractional Sobolev embedding:

(5.12)

{\|f\|}_{L^{\infty}(G/K)}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(G/K)}\>,

by
[
CGM93
, Theorem 4.7]
, a celebrated work of Cowling-Giulini-Meda. Our proof above indicates the failure of the more delicate local borderline fractional Sobolev embedding:

{\|f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(G/K)}\>,

thus providing a refinement of (
5.12
).

Effects of different antibiotic strategies on postoperative inflammatory response and recovery in patients with acute appendicitis: a single-center, prospective, randomized controlled study
Authors: Bo-Lin He, Minhui Li, Shu-Bang Cheng, Yuming Liu, Ziwei Tu, Haozhi Xu, Min Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the impact of different antibiotic regimens on postoperative outcomes in patients with acute appendicitis and assess the predictive value of preoperative procalcitonin (PCT) for postoperative fever.MethodsThis prospective randomized controlled trial enrolled patients with acute uncomplicated appendicitis. Participants were randomly assigned to three groups: no antibiotics, single preoperative dose, or full-course antibiotics (preoperative dose plus 3 days postoperative). Inflammatory markers, pain scores, length of stay, and complications were compared. ROC analysis assessed PCT’s predictive value for fever.ResultsOf 437 randomized patients, baseline characteristics were comparable. No significant differences were observed among groups in postoperative inflammatory markers (POD1 and POD3), length of hospital stay, pain scores, or complication rates (all P > 0.05). Preoperative PCT demonstrated excellent predictive value for postoperative fever (AUC = 0.852, sensitivity 76.2%, specificity 88.5%).ConclusionOmitting perioperative antibiotics did not compromise outcomes in laparoscopic appendectomy for uncomplicated appendicitis. Preoperative PCT effectively predicts postoperative fever, aiding clinical decision-making for antibiotic use.Trial registration: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR2000032410. Introduction

Acute appendicitis is the most common cause of emergency abdominal surgery worldwide, with an annual incidence of 96.5 to 100 cases per 100,000 adults [1, 2]. Over the past four decades, laparoscopic appendectomy has become the standard surgical approach, offering advantages such as reduced postoperative pain, faster recovery, and shorter hospital stays compared to open surgery [3–5]. Once the diagnosis of appendicitis is established, antimicrobial therapy targeting aerobic Gram-negative and anaerobic bacteria is routinely initiated, following guidelines for community-acquired intra-abdominal infections [6, 7].

However, in the era of increasing antimicrobial resistance (AMR), the rational use of antibiotics is crucial to prevent the emergence and spread of resistant organisms. AMR has become a major global health concern, and judicious antibiotic stewardship is now an essential aspect of modern surgical practice. Recent evidence has challenged the necessity of routine perioperative antibiotic use for uncomplicated appendicitis [8]. Several randomized and observational studies have reported comparable outcomes between patients receiving short-course or no antibiotics and those receiving conventional prophylaxis [9, 10]. Nonetheless, most available evidence comes from Western populations [10], while data from Asian surgical settings remain limited.

To our knowledge, there is a paucity of high-quality prospective randomized studies in Asia evaluating whether laparoscopic appendectomy can be safely performed without perioperative antibiotics in uncomplicated appendicitis. Furthermore, few investigations have examined the role of preoperative biomarkers, such as procalcitonin (PCT), in guiding individualized antibiotic decisions [11]. This study therefore aimed to fill these gaps by comparing the effects of different antibiotic strategies on postoperative inflammatory responses and recovery, and by evaluating the predictive value of preoperative PCT for postoperative fever. The findings may contribute to antibiotic stewardship efforts and inform evidence-based surgical guidelines for uncomplicated appendicitis.

Methods

Study design and participants

This single-center, prospective randomized controlled trial was conducted at The People’s Hospital of Longhua Shenzhen from March 2023 to February 2024. Patients aged 18–60 years with CT-confirmed uncomplicated acute appendicitis were enrolled.

Uncomplicated appendicitis was defined on CT as an enlarged appendix (> 6 mm) with wall thickening and enhancement, without perforation, abscess, or appendicolith.

Exclusion criteria included perforation, abscess, gangrenous changes, antibiotic use before admission, significant comorbidities, and pregnancy.

Participants were randomly assigned using a computer-generated sequence into three groups:

Experimental group: No antibiotics during the perioperative period.

Experimental group: No antibiotics during the perioperative period.

Control group 1: Single preoperative antibiotic dose (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV 30 min before incision).

Control group 1: Single preoperative antibiotic dose (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV 30 min before incision).

Control group 2: Preoperative antibiotic dose (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV 30 min before incision) plus postoperative antibiotics (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV every 12 hours for 3 days).

Control group 2: Preoperative antibiotic dose (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV 30 min before incision) plus postoperative antibiotics (cefmetazole 1.0 g IV every 12 hours for 3 days).

The study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee (Approval No. KY2019187) and registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2000032410). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.The study flow is shown in Fig. 1.

CONSORT statement

This study adheres to the CONSORT guidelines for reporting randomized controlled trials.

Surgical and postoperative management

All patients underwent standardized laparoscopic appendectomy performed by an experienced surgical team. Perioperative management was identical across groups except for antibiotic use. Blood tests, including white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NEU), C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and procalcitonin (PCT), were performed preoperatively and repeated on postoperative days 1 and 3 (POD1 and POD3).

Outcome measures and statistical analysis

Primary outcomes included postoperative inflammatory markers, visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores, and length of hospital stay. Secondary outcomes included complication rates and hospital costs.

Complicated appendicitis was defined as perforation, abscess formation, or gangrenous change confirmed by CT or intraoperative findings.

Significant complications were defined as events graded ≥ II according to the Clavien–Dindo classification.

Statistical analysis was performed using Python. Continuous variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test, and categorical variables using the chi-square test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis evaluated the predictive value of preoperative markers for postoperative fever. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 437 patients were analyzed (156 no antibiotics, 143 single dose, 138 full-course). Baseline characteristics including age, sex, and BMI were comparable among groups (all P > 0.05; see Supplementary Table S1).

Preoperative laboratory findings

Preoperative laboratory and imaging findings (WBC, NEU, PCT, IL-6, appendiceal diameter) were similar among groups (all P > 0.05), and after Bonferroni correction, differences in CRP and TBil were not statistically significant (see Supplementary Table S2 for detailed data).

Intraoperative and postoperative outcomes

Intraoperative outcomes are summarized in Supplementary Table S3. No significant differences were observed among groups in postoperative day 1 and day 3 laboratory findings, except for CRP on POD1 (Table 1). Recovery outcomes including time to flatus, VAS scores, and length of stay were comparable. Hospital costs were significantly higher in the full-course antibiotic group (P = 0.000). Complication rates were low and similar across groups.

Discussion

This prospective randomized controlled trial evaluated the clinical impact of three antibiotic strategies—no antibiotics, a single preoperative dose, and perioperative antibiotics—among patients with acute uncomplicated appendicitis undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy. Overall, our findings demonstrate that withholding antibiotics does not increase postoperative inflammatory responses, fever, length of hospital stay, or infectious complications. Furthermore, preoperative PCT exhibited strong predictive performance for postoperative fever, underscoring its potential value in perioperative risk stratification.

Principal findings and clinical interpretation

The key finding of this study is that different antibiotic regimens did not translate into clinically meaningful differences in postoperative recovery or infection-related outcomes. Although certain inflammatory markers (e.g., preoperative CRP, TBil, and postoperative day 1 CRP) showed statistically significant intergroup variations, these laboratory differences did not correlate with clinical endpoints such as wound infection, intra-abdominal abscess, postoperative pain scores, or hospital stay. This distinction between statistical and clinical relevance is essential—particularly in acute appendicitis, where the inflammatory response can fluctuate widely due to individual immune variability and the degree of local peritoneal irritation. Our results suggest that these laboratory variations likely reflect physiological heterogeneity rather than true antibiotic-responsive infection.

Why perioperative antibiotics May not improve outcomes

The absence of additional benefit from perioperative antibiotics aligns with several international multicenter trials and meta-analyses showing that uncomplicated appendicitis seldom progresses to postoperative infectious complications following complete source control via laparoscopy [9, 12]. Laparoscopic appendectomy removes the infected appendix, limits bacterial contamination, and minimizes tissue trauma, collectively rendering postoperative antibiotics unnecessary for most patients. Prior studies from Europe and the United States have reported similar findings, endorsing restrictive antibiotic use for low-risk patients [13, 14]. Our results confirm that these principles are equally applicable in an Asian population, addressing a notable evidence gap in current literature.

Interpretation of inflammatory heterogeneity (addressing reviewer 2)

Inflammatory markers such as CRP, IL-6, and WBC are dynamic parameters influenced not only by bacterial load but also by surgical stress, cytokine surge, and host immune responsiveness. The significant CRP elevation observed in control group 1 on postoperative day 1 did not correspond to higher infectious complications or delayed recovery. This finding reinforces that postoperative inflammatory responses are highly variable and not linearly dependent on antibiotic administration. The heterogeneity observed in our cohort is consistent with existing literature demonstrating that postoperative CRP fluctuations commonly reflect tissue trauma rather than infection. Therefore, clinical decisions should rely on quantifiable indicators such as vital signs, imaging, and validated biomarkers rather than inflammatory markers alone.

Role of PCT in predicting postoperative fever

An important contribution of this study is the identification of preoperative PCT as a strong predictor of postoperative fever, with an AUC of 0.852. PCT has been increasingly studied as a marker of bacterial burden and systemic inflammation [11, 15]. Our findings support the incorporation of PCT into perioperative decision-making algorithms. Patients with low PCT values may be suitable candidates for restrictive antibiotic strategies, while those exceeding the identified cutoff (0.079 ng/mL) may benefit from closer monitoring or selective prophylaxis. Future trials should validate PCT-guided antibiotic protocols to further personalize appendicitis management.

Clinical implications and antibiotic stewardship

This study provides evidence supporting a more conservative approach to antibiotic use for uncomplicated appendicitis. Reducing perioperative antibiotics can meaningfully decrease healthcare costs, decrease unnecessary drug exposure, and contribute to global efforts against antimicrobial resistance. Given rising resistance rates and increasing emphasis on stewardship, the findings of this trial are timely and clinically relevant. Importantly, our results demonstrate that a no-antibiotic approach is not only safe but also cost-effective, challenging traditional assumptions regarding routine perioperative prophylaxis.

Strengths, limitations, and future directions

Major strengths of this study include its randomized design, relatively large sample size, and rigorous follow-up. However, several limitations merit consideration. First, this is a single-center study, which may limit generalizability across different healthcare systems. Second, rare complications such as deep pelvic abscess may not be fully captured due to sample size constraints. Third, the study population consisted of relatively young and immunocompetent patients, and findings may not apply to elderly or immunosuppressed individuals. Future multicenter trials incorporating microbiological profiling, cost-effectiveness analyses, and PCT-guided risk stratification frameworks will help refine antibiotic protocols and enable precision-based surgical care [16, 17].

Conclusion

Perioperative antibiotics can be safely omitted in patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy for uncomplicated acute appendicitis without compromising postoperative outcomes. Preoperative PCT demonstrates strong predictive value for postoperative fever and may guide individualized antibiotic use, contributing to rational antibiotic stewardship in surgical practice.

Model-independent constraints on Lorentz Invariance Violation with update observations of Gamma-Ray Bursts
Yu Pan 1 , Jun Tian 1,2 , Shuo Cao 3,4 , Qing-Quan Jiang 2 , Wei-Liang Qian 5,6

Abstract
Searching the possible Lorentz Invariance Violation (LIV) from astrophysical sources such as gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) is essential for finding evidences of new theories of quantum gravity. However, the effect of the underlying cosmological model is still understudied in the previous analysis. We take a novel approach using artificial neural networks to reconstruct the expansion history of the universe, thereby eliminating the influence of potential cosmological models to constrain LIV. 74 time delays from GRBs are considered to obtain stringent results on LIV, including 37 time delays measurements from GRB 160625B across various energy bands at redshift z = 1.41 z=1.41 , and 37 additional GRBs with time delays spanning redshifts 0.117 ≤ z ≤ 1.99 0.117\leq z\leq 1.99 . Our analysis yields stringent constraints on both linear and quadratic LIV, with E Q ​ G , 1 ≥ 2.63 × 10 15 E_{QG,1}\geq 2.63\times 10^{15} G ​ e ​ V GeV and E Q ​ G , 2 ≥ 1.19 × 10 10 E_{QG,2}\geq 1.19\times 10^{10} G ​ e ​ V GeV that are four orders of magnitude beneath the Planck energy scale, and shows the positive intrinsic time delay in GRBs. Our results demonstrate that such combination would significantly improve the precision and robustness of final results. Taking this into account may be an important contribution in the case of possible LIV detection in the future.



I
Introduction

The Lorentz Invariance holds crucial significance in physics and is a fundamental assumption of Relativity. However, several quantum gravity (QG) theories suggest that Lorentz Invariance Violation (LIV) may occur at the Planck energy scale,
E_{QG}\approx E_{P1}=\sqrt{\hbar c^{5}/G}\simeq 1.22\times 10^{19}

GeV

Mattingly(2005)
;
Amelino-Camelia(2013)
.
From an astronomical point of view, the observational Gamma-Ray Bursts (GRBs) can capture this microcosmic quantum gravity. The GRBs are the brightest astrophysical events in the Universe, characterized by extensive distances, brief spectral lags, and energetic emissions
Amelino-Camelia(2013)
;
Ellis(2008)
;
Jacob(2008)
. They stand out as the most promising candidates for conducting LIV tests
Amelino-Camelia(2013)
. Over the past two decades, GRBs have served as the primary source for a majority of LIV tests
Desai(2023)
;
Liao(2022)
.

GRBs are divided into two types based on their duration: Short Gamma-ray bursts (SGRBs), lasting less than two seconds, and Long Gamma-ray bursts (LGRBs), lasting more than two seconds
Kouveliotou(1993)
.
SGRBs are commonly linked to neutron star mergers
Nakar(2007)
, while LGRBs are typically associated with core-collapse supernovae
Woosley(2006)
.
LGRBs typically exhibit more pronounced positive or negative spectral lags compared to SGRBs
Chen(2005)
;
Ukwatta(2012)
;
Bernardini(2015)
, whereas SGRBs generally display smaller spectral lags
Desai(2023)
;
Bernardini(2015)
. Spectral lag, a widely recognized phenomenon in GRBs, is characterized by a time delay between low-energy photons (i.e., light curves) and high-energy photons
Xiao(2022)
. The primary approach for searching LIV with GRBs is analyzing these spectral lags
Desai(2023)
.

The lower limits of
E_{QG}
in some LIV tests have approached the Planck energy scale, determined through the observation of spectral lag in extremely high-energy photons from individual GRBs
Abdo(2009)A
;
Abdo(2009)B
;
Acciari(2020)
. However, spectral lag includes both the time delay associated with LIV and an unknown intrinsic time delay. Using a single burst to constrain LIV makes it challenging to disentangle them. Ellis et al.
Ellis(2008)
addressed this issue by stacking a sample of GRBs, primarily LGRBs, and replacing the intrinsic time delay in the rest frame with an assumed constant for all GRBs. Nevertheless, it’s unlikely that every GRB shares an identical intrinsic spectral lag. Wei et al.
Wei(2017)
employed a more realistic power-law model using multiple lags from GRB 106625B, explaining the transition in spectral lags from positive to negative. However, the data fitting in their study was not entirely satisfactory.
Agrawal et al.
Agrawal(2021)
employed a similar approach, stacking a larger number of GRBs, which improved the precision of the results but also faced challenges related to the goodness of the data fitting.

There were a lot of work that choosed the cosmological models to constrain LIV such as the
\Lambda
CDM model.
However, these models are based on the general relativity framework without considering LIV
Pan(2020)
.
In a recent study, an investigation into the effects of different cosmological models on LIV constraints revealed the complexity of this relationship, adding more uncertainty to the results
Staicova(2023)
.
To address this issue, Pan et al.
Pan(2020)
first employed a model-independent method using the Gaussian Process (GP) to reconstruct cosmic expansion history (represented by the Hubble parameter
H(z)
) for constraints on LIV, their results are more robust and conservative. For more work on the use of GP, we refer to Refs
2021MNRAS.506L…1D
;
2018ApJ…856….3Y
;
2019ApJ…886…94L
;
2024MNRAS.528.1354L
;
2023MNRAS.523.3406F
.
Nevertheless, GP comes with various disadvantages, such as overfitting and a heavy dependence on the selection of covariance functions
Mukherjee(2022)
. Recent research has also emphasized the need for caution when using GP for the reconstruction of the Hubble parameter
Zhou(2019)
.
On the other hand, the Artificial Neural Network (ANN) is a fully data-driven method that accurately captures input data distribution with a suitable network architecture
Wang(2020)
.
Recent studies have increasingly utilized this method to reconstruct the Hubble parameter
Mukherjee(2022)
;
Wang(2020)
;
Go'mez-Vargas(2023)
;
Qi(2023)
.

In this paper, we propose a novel, model-independent method by using an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to reconstruct the cosmic expansion history to constrain LIV, effectively circumvents the potential influence of cosmological models. The outline for methodology of LIV-induced time delay and the observational datasets are given in Section
II
. We show the results and discussions in Section
III
. Finally, the main conclusions are summarized in Section
IV
.

II
Methodology and Datasets

II.1
Lorentz Invariance Violation

In some QG models, the Lorentz invariance may be violated around the Planck energy scale
E_{QG}\sim 10^{19}GeV
, leading the speed of light to depend on the energy of photons
Amelino-Camelia(2013)
;
1998Natur.395Q.525A
.
This phenomenon, which can be described through a modified dispersion relation (MDR), although it does not specify a particular theoretical framework, can prompt various experimental follow-up tests
2008ApJ…689L…1K
;
2022PrPNP.12503948A
.
In our work, we use an empirical formula for the MDR of massless particles, widely recognized for its robustness across various theoretical contexts
2022PrPNP.12503948A
. This relation can be expressed as follows:

E^{2}=c^{2}p^{2}\left[1+f(E/E_{QG})\right],

(1)

Where
E_{QG}
denotes the Planck energy scale,
f
is the model dependent function of the dimensionless ratio of
E/E_{QG}
. The dispersion relation can be expanded as a Taylor series
2013PhRvD..87l2001V
:

E^{2}\simeq c^{2}p^{2}\left[1-s_{\pm}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{E}{E_{QG,n}}\right)^{n}\right],

(2)

For
E_{QG,n}\gg E
, the lowest order term in the series is expected to dominate the sum, so we keep only the lowest-order dominant term:

E^{2}\simeq c^{2}p^{2}\left[1-s_{\pm}\left(\frac{E}{E_{QG,n}}\right)^{n}\right],

(3)

Assuming the relation
v(E)=\partial E/\partial p
holds at least approximately, the energy-dependent velocity of light is
1998Natur.395Q.525A
:

v(E)=c\left[1-s_{\pm}\frac{n+1}{2}\left(\frac{E}{E_{QG}}\right)^{n}\right].

(4)

Here,
s_{\pm}=+1
(or
-1
) indicates whether the photon velocity decreases (or increases) with the growth of photon energy, and
n
is a term dependent on the model, with
n=1
(or
2
) corresponding to a linear (or quadratic) term.
Considering that the lower-energy photons are generally expected to travel faster than higher-energy ones due to LIV effects
Wei(2017)
, we only focus on
s_{\pm}=+1
in this work.

Due to the relationship between the velocity of light and energy, photons of varying energies (represented by
E
and
E_{0}
, with
E>E_{0}
) emitted by the identical origin will reach Earth at distinct moments.
In addition, based on the recent astronomical observations, particularly of the observations of type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), have shown that our universe contains a component with negative pressure that drives the accelerated expansion of the universe
1998AJ….116.1009R
. With the effects of cosmological expansion, the time delay between photons with different energy levels induced by LIV is
Jacob(2008)
;
Zhang(2015)
:

\Delta t_{LIV}=-\frac{1+n}{2H_{0}}\frac{E^{n}-E_{0}^{n}}{E_{QG,n}^{n}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z{{}^{\prime}})^{n}dz{{}^{\prime}}}{h(z{{}^{\prime}})},

(5)

where
H_{0}
stands for the Hubble constant, while
h(z)=H(z)/H_{0}
denotes the dimensionless Hubble expansion
H(z)
rate at redshift z.
The complete time delay across various energy bands should be included five terms:

\Delta t=\Delta_{LIV}+\Delta_{int}+\Delta_{spe}+\Delta_{DM}+\Delta_{gra},

(6)

where
\Delta_{LIV}
represents the potential time delay due to LIV via an energy-dependent
velocity of light,
\Delta_{int}
is the intrinsic time delay between two test photons, and this has the largest uncertainty since it is impossible to determine it based on the observational data alone,
\Delta_{spe}
is the potential time delay due to special relativistic effects when the photons have a non-zero rest mass,
\Delta_{DM}
denote the time delay contribution from the dispersion by the line-of-sight free electron content, and
\Delta_{gra}
is the gravitational potential along the propagation path of photons if the Einstein equivalence principle is violated.
However, recent works pointed that, for cosmic transient sources (like GRB), the last three terms have not significantly influence on the total time delay
\Delta t
, and can be ignored
Gao(2015)
;
Wei(2015)
. Thus, we assume the
\Delta t
is the sum of
\Delta_{LIV}
and
\Delta_{int}
.

Specifically, we still have a limited understanding of the detailed emission mechanism of GRBs, so choosing the appropriate form of intrinsic time delay is very important.
Intrinsic time delay is commonly represented by the parameter
b
, but considering the cosmological expansion
\Delta_{int}=b(1+z)

Ellis(2008)
, this form implies that GRBs exhibit a uniform intrinsic time delay.
For SGRBs, using
b
to represent intrinsic time delay may be reasonable due to small spectral lags
Xiao(2022)
.
However, LGRBs have more long spectral lags and are very likely to have different intrinsic time lags, the format of a power-law function is more reasonable
Wei(2017)
:

\Delta_{int}(E)=\tau\left[\left(\frac{E}{keV}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\frac{E_{0}}{keV}\right)^{\alpha}\right],

(7)

where
\tau
and
\alpha
are treated as free parameters and have the positive value, with
E_{0}
representing the midpoint of the lowest energy band. When both
\tau
and
\alpha
are greater than zero, the intrinsic time delay is positive.
Finally, the theoretical time delay between different energy bands is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta t_{th}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{LIV}+\Delta_{int}(1+z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+n}{2H_{0}}\frac{E^{n}-E_{0}^{n}}{E_{QG,n}^{n}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z{{}^{\prime}})^{n}dz{{}^{\prime}}}{h(z{{}^{\prime}})}+\tau\left[\left(\frac{E}{keV}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\frac{E_{0}}{keV}\right)^{\alpha}\right](1+z).

(8)

Traditionally, considerable work has chosen a specific cosmological model like the flat
\Lambda
CDM model to obtain the function
h(z)
. However, this is cosmological model-dependent. It is shown that the dimensionless Hubble parameter functions
h(z)
of different cosmological models differ significantly at high redshift, which will significantly affect our study of LIV. The test of LIV by using cosmological model independent method is particularly important.

II.3
Artificial Neural Network Method

Artificial Neural Network (ANN) is a fully data-driven approach, providing a model-independent method.
We employ an ANN to reconstruct the cosmic expansion history, represented by the Hubble parameter
H(z)
.
The Python code employed for this task is ReFANN, developed by Wang et al.
Wang(2020)
, a single hidden layer comprising a total of 4096 neurons.
ReFANN is constructed using PyTorch, a widely recognized and well-optimized open-source tensor library designed for machine learning.
To ensure the complete model independence of the
H(z)
functions, it is essential to carefully select the data, avoiding any influence from the cosmological model.
The values of
H(z)
can be acquired using two distinct methods.
The first method is the cosmic chronometer method, which calculates the age differences among galaxies undergoing passive evolution at various redshifts
Jimenez(2002)
.
The Hubble parameter of the cosmic chronometer is denoted as CC
H(z)
, and they offer model-independent measurements of
H(z)

Jimenez(2002)
.
Another one is through the detection of radial BAO features
Go'mez-Vargas(2023)
;
Blake(2012)
;
Samushia(2013)
.
However, the values acquired by this method depend on a presupposed fiducial cosmological model.
Given the requirement for model-independent
H(z)
reconstruction, we choose the CC
H(z)
data for this purpose.
We utilized the latest collection of 32 data points of CC
H(z)

Zhang(2014)
;
Stern(2010)
;
Moresco(2012)
;
Moresco(2016)
;
Ratsimbazafy(2017)

z=2
.

It’s important to note that all CC

Therefore, the dimensionless Hubble expansion rate

Results and Discussion

We have applied the ANN method to exclude the effects of the cosmological model.
Next, we employ the Monte Carlo Markov Chain method
Lewis(2002)
to determine the LIV parameters (
E_{QG}
,
\tau
, and
\alpha
) by minimizing the
\chi^{2}
objective function:

\chi^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{GRB}}\left[\frac{\Delta t_{th}\left(E_{QG},\tau,\alpha\right)-\Delta t_{obs}}{\sigma_{tot}}\right]^{2},

(9)

where
\Delta t_{th}
represents the theoretical time delay,
\Delta t_{obs}
stands for the observed time delay, and
\sigma_{tot}
denotes the total uncertainty.

\sigma_{tot}^{2}=\sigma_{t}^{2}+(\frac{\partial f}{\partial E})^{2}\sigma_{E}^{2}+(\frac{\partial f}{\partial E_{0}})^{2}\sigma_{E_{0}}^{2},

(10)

where
f
is Eq.
5
,
\sigma_{t}
is the observed time delay uncertainty, and
\sigma_{E_{0}}
and
\sigma_{E}
represent the half-width of the lower and higher energy bands, respectively.
The posterior probability distribution for each parameters and the corresponding two-dimensional confidence contours are plotted in Figures

and
3
.

In our analysis, we chose a large sample of various GRBs.
Evidently, the spectral lags of different GRBs provide more robust limits on LIV.
Compared to the earlier findings from the single special source GRB 160625B
Wei(2017)
and GRB 190114C
Du(2020)

IV
Conclusions

The search of possible Lorentz Invariance Violation (LIV) from astrophysical sources such as gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) is essential for finding evidences of new theories of quantum gravity. We analyze 74 time delays from GRBs, including 37 measurements from GRB 160625B across various energy bands at redshift
z=1.41
, and 37 additional GRBs with time delays spanning redshifts from
z=0.117
to
z=1.99
. However, the effect of the underlying cosmological model is still understudied in the previous analysis. In this work, we adopt a novel approach by utilizing an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to reconstruct the cosmic expansion history. Specially, the newest observations of cosmic chronometers acting as the training set ensures a strict model-independent methodology. Our choice of training data encompasses 32 data points of the cosmic chronometer
H(z)
(CC
H(z)
). This selection ensures that the resulting reconstruction of the cosmic expansion history remains independent of any specific theoretical model. Furthermore, we include the power-law model and discuss the searches for LIV in both linear and quadratic cases.

By using the power-law model and stacking of a large sample, we have established constraints for both linear and quadratic cases of LIV:
E_{QG,1}\geq 2.63\times 10^{15}

GeV
and
E_{QG,2}\geq 1.19\times 10^{10}

GeV
. Notably, in the quadratic case, the value of
E_{QG,2}
within the same order of magnitude as that obtained from high energy photon
Acciari(2020)
. The parameters
\tau
and
\alpha
of the power-law model are similar to the results of GRB 160625B
Wei(2017)
. Compared to using the time delays of a single special GRB, our findings present higher precision and improved constraints, different GRBs provide more robust limits on LIV. However, compared with our previous work using the GP method
Pan(2020)
, the results of ANN have a lower precision.

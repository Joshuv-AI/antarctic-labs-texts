Classical discrete multiple orthogonal polynomials: hypergeometric and integral representations
Amílcar Branquinho 1, Juan E.F. Díaz 2, Ana Foulquié-Moreno 3, Manuel Mañas 4, Thomas Wolfs 5

Abstract
. This work explores classical discrete multiple orthogonal polynomials, including Hahn, Meixner of the first and second kinds, Kravchuk, and Charlier polynomials, with an arbitrary number of weights. Explicit expressions for the recursion coefficients of Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials are derived. By leveraging the multiple Askey scheme and the recently discovered explicit hypergeometric representation of type I multiple Hahn polynomials, corresponding explicit hypergeometric representations are provided for the type I polynomials and recursion coefficients of all the aforementioned descendants within the Askey scheme. Additionally, integral representations for these families within the Hahn class in the Askey scheme are presented. The multiple Askey scheme is further completed by providing the corresponding limits for the weights, polynomials, and recurrence coefficients.

1.
Introduction

The first four coauthors embarked on an ambitious project to derive explicit hypergeometric expressions for classical multiple orthogonal polynomials in both their continuous and discrete forms. Their initial focus, detailed in
[
6
]
, involved identifying all Hahn polynomial families within the multiple Askey scheme for the simpler case of two weights, excluding the Hermite family. This effort successfully covered the Jacobi–Piñeiro, Laguerre (both first and second kinds), Meixner (both first and second kinds), Kravchuk, and Charlier polynomials. Subsequently, their research expanded to handle an arbitrary number of weights. In
[
7
]
, they presented formulas for the Jacobi–Piñeiro and Laguerre polynomials of the first kind. Further,
[
5
]
provided explicit expressions for the Laguerre polynomials of the second kind and for type I Hermite polynomials. The latter was particularly noteworthy as explicit formulas for type I Hermite polynomials, even with two weights, had previously eluded researchers. Additionally, they were able to determine the recurrence coefficients for all these cases.

Despite these advancements, finding explicit hypergeometric expressions for the Hahn polynomials of type I with an arbitrary number of weights remained unresolved by the original team. However, a recent breakthrough in
[
8
]
, achieved in collaboration with the final author, has provided the sought explicit hypergeometric formulas and contour integral representations for these Hahn polynomials. These developments build upon the work presented in earlier papers
[
15
,
16
]
.

In this paper, we derive explicit expressions for the near-neighbor recurrence coefficients of the multiple Hahn polynomials. Building on the multiple Askey scheme, we obtain hypergeometric representations for the Hahn’s descendants, specifically for the type I discrete multiple orthogonal polynomials within the Meixner of the first and second kinds, Kravchuk, and Charlier families. Additionally, we provide explicit formulas for the near-neighbor recurrence coefficients for these families. Furthermore, drawing on insights from our previous work
[
8
]
, we offer contour integral representations for all these discrete polynomial families. Additionally, the multiple Askey scheme is completed by providing the corresponding limits for the weights, polynomials, and recurrence coefficients, thereby completing the scheme and ensuring a comprehensive integration of the discrete and continuous families.

1.1.
Discrete Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials

To establish some fundamental concepts regarding discrete multiple orthogonal polynomials, see
[
10
,
11
]
, consider the system defined by
p\geqslant 2
weight functions:

w_{1},\ldots,w_{p}:\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant}.

Under certain conditions, a sequence of type II polynomials
B_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}
can be found. These polynomials are monic and have degrees
\deg B_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}\leqslant|\vec{n}|
, satisfying the orthogonality relations:

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\Delta}k^{j}B_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(k)w_{i}(k)=0,\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

In addition, there exist
p
sequences of type I polynomials, denoted
A^{(1)}_{\vec{n}},\ldots,A^{(p)}_{\vec{n}}
, each with
\deg A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}\leqslant n_{i}-1
, satisfying the orthogonality conditions:

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\in\Delta}k^{j}A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(k)w_{i}(k)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }j\in\{0,\ldots,|\vec{n}|-2\},\\
1,&\text{if }j=|\vec{n}|-1.\end{cases}

In this context, let
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{p}
with
|\vec{n}|\coloneqq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
. The notation
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
will be used. These conditions uniquely define the polynomials to fulfill the biorthogonality conditions:

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\in\Delta}B_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(k)A^{(i)}_{\vec{m}}(k)w_{i}(k)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }m_{i}\leqslant n_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,p,\\
1,&\text{if }|\vec{m}|=|\vec{n}|+1,\\
0,&\text{if }|\vec{n}|+1<|\vec{m}|.\end{cases}

Sometimes it can be convenient to rewrite the previous expressions using the type I linear form

(4)

A_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm I})}(x)\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)w_{i}(x).

1.2.
Recurrence Relations

Both type I and type II polynomials follow a recurrence relation. According to the notation in
[
10
, §23.1.4]
, let
\big(\pi(1),\pi(2),\ldots,\pi(p)\big)
represent a permutation of
(1,2,\ldots,p)
, and let
\vec{e}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
denote the
k
-th vector of the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Define the vectors:

\vec{s}_{0}\coloneqq\vec{0},\quad\vec{s}_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{j}\vec{e}_{\pi(i)},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

The type II and type I polynomials satisfy the following recurrence relations:

(5)

\displaystyle xB_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{\vec{n}+\vec{e}_{k}}^{({\rm II})}(x)+b_{\vec{n}}^{0}(k)B_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b^{j}_{\vec{n}}B_{\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j}}^{({\rm II})}(x),

\displaystyle xA^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}-\vec{e}_{k}}(x)+b_{\vec{n}-\vec{e}_{k}}^{0}(k)A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b^{j}_{\vec{n}+\vec{s}_{j-1}}A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}+\vec{s}_{j}}(x),

where
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

From the biorthogonality condition (
3
), the recurrence coefficients can be expressed as:

(6)

\displaystyle b_{\vec{n}}^{0}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Delta}xB_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x)A^{({\rm I})}_{\vec{n}+\vec{e}_{k}}(x),

(7)

\displaystyle b_{\vec{n}}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Delta}xB_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x)A^{({\rm I})}_{\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j-1}}(x),\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

Remark 1.1
.

The multi-index
\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j-1}
corresponds to subtracting
1
to the
j-1
different entries

\displaystyle\{{n}_{\pi(1)},{n}_{\pi(2)},\ldots,{n}_{\pi(j-1)}\}

of the multi-index
\vec{n}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle|\vec{n}+\vec{e}_{k}|

\displaystyle=|\vec{n}|+1,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j-1}|&=|\vec{n}|-j+1,&j&=\{1,\ldots,p\}.\end{aligned}

The condition
\left(\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j-1}\right)_{i}=n_{i}
is equivalent to write
j\leqslant\pi^{-1}(i)
while
\left(\vec{n}-\vec{s}_{j-1}\right)_{i}=n_{i}-1
is equivalent to
j>\pi^{-1}(i)
. Since these conditions will be relevant along the paper, let us define the following sets

\displaystyle S(\pi,j)

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\mid j\leqslant\pi^{-1}(i)\right\},

\displaystyle S^{\textsf{c}}(\pi,j)

\displaystyle\coloneq\{1,2,\ldots,p\}\setminus S(\pi,j).

For example, with
p=4
and the permutation

\displaystyle\pi=\begin{pNiceMatrix}1&2&3&4\\
4&2&1&3\end{pNiceMatrix}

the sets are:

\displaystyle S(\pi,1)

\displaystyle=\{1,2,3,4\},

\displaystyle S(\pi,2)

\displaystyle=\{1,2,3\},

\displaystyle S(\pi,3)

\displaystyle=\{1,3\},

\displaystyle S(\pi,4)

\displaystyle=\{3\}.

) and their recurrence coefficients (

1.3.
Hypergeometric Series

Before delving into the main results, it’s important to recall that, in many cases, these polynomials can be represented using generalized hypergeometric series
[
1
,
12
]
,

(8)

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a_1,\ldots,a_p}{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_q};x\biggr]\coloneqq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{1})_{l}\cdots(a_{p})_{l}}{(\alpha_{1})_{l}\cdots(\alpha_{q})_{l}}\dfrac{x^{l}}{l!}.

We are going to need a generalization of these previous ones; known as the multiple Kampé de Fériet series
[
13
, Chapter 1.4]

(9)

F^{n:r_{1};\cdots;r_{p}}_{q:k_{1};\cdots;k_{p}}\left[{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}):(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r_{1}});\cdots;(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r_{p}})\atop(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}):(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k_{1}});\cdots;(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k_{p}})}\Bigg|x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\right]\\
\coloneq\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{1})_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(a_{n})_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1})_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{q})_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}\dfrac{(b_{1})_{l_{1}}\cdots(b_{r_{1}})_{l_{1}}}{(\beta_{1})_{l_{1}}\cdots(\beta_{k_{1}})_{l_{1}}}\cdots\dfrac{(c_{1})_{l_{p}}\cdots(c_{r_{p}})_{l_{p}}}{(\gamma_{1})_{l_{p}}\cdots(\gamma_{k_{p}})_{l_{p}}}\dfrac{x_{1}^{l_{1}}}{l_{1}!}\cdots\dfrac{x_{p}^{l_{p}}}{l_{p}!}.

All these series are defined through the Pochhammer symbols

\displaystyle(x)_{n}\coloneqq\dfrac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x)}=\begin{cases}x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)\;\text{if}\;n\in\mathbb{N},\\
1\;\text{if}\;n=0.\end{cases}

The Chu–Vandermonde formula, that follows the umbral paradigm correspondences to Newton’s binomial formula for Pochhammer symbols, is

(10)

\displaystyle(x+y)_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom{n}{k}(x)_{k}(y)_{n-k},

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This formula can be deduced from this particular case of the celebrated Gauss’ formula

(11)

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n,x}{y};1\biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{(-n)_{k}}{k!}\dfrac{(x)_{k}}{(y)_{k}}=\dfrac{(y-x)_{n}}{(y)_{n}},

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

In order to prove the recurrence coefficients
of the multiple Hahn polynomials
we will use the following generalization of the Pfaff–Saalschütz formula, see
[
9
, Theorem 9]
:

(12)

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a,b, -n}{c+k, a+b+1-n-c};1\biggr]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(c-a)_{n}(c-b+k)_{n}}{(c+k)_{n}(c-a-b)_{n}}{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-k,b,-n}{c-a, b-c-k-n+1};1\biggr],

\displaystyle k,n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We also recall Stirling’s formula

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Gamma(z)&\sim\sqrt{2\pi}z^{z-\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{e}^{-z},&z&\rightarrow\infty,&|\arg z|<\pi,\end{aligned}

for the asymptotic behavior of the gamma function. We will use the convention that whenever an exponential function has a branch cut, we assume it is taken along the negative real line.

Sometimes it will be convenient to use the following asymptotics of a ratio of gamma functions directly

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(az+b)}{\Gamma(az+c)}

\displaystyle\sim(az)^{b-c},

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle|\arg az|

\displaystyle<\pi.

1.4.
Hahn Hypergeometric Representations

The weight functions for the Hahn family are defined as

(15)

\displaystyle w_{i}(x;\alpha_{i},\beta,N)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+x+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)\Gamma(x+1)}\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)},

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\in\{1,\ldots,p\},

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\{0,\ldots,N\},

with

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta>-1
. In order to establish an AT system, we require that
\left|\vec{n}\right|\leqslant N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i\neq j
. We will denote
\vec{\alpha}\coloneq(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
.

The corresponding type II polynomials were first found for
p=2
in
[
2
]
and then generalized for
p\geqslant 2
in
[
3
]
. One showed that

(16)

Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}C_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}\,(-x)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}},

where

(17)

C_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}\coloneq\dfrac{(-N)_{|\vec{n}|}}{(-N)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{n_{i}}}{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{i}}}\dfrac{(-n_{i})_{l_{i}}}{l_{i}!}\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\sum_{k=1}^{i}n_{k}+1)_{\sum_{j=i}^{p}l_{j}}}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{\sum_{j=i}^{p}l_{j}}}\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}+n_{i}+1)_{\sum_{j=i+1}^{p}l_{j}}}{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\sum_{k=1}^{i}n_{k}+1)_{\sum_{j=i+1}^{p}l_{j}}}.

In
[
8
]
, the following alternative representation was proven

(18)

Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)={(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\Gamma(N+\beta+1)}{(N-|\vec{n}|)!}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{n_{i}}}{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{i}}}\dfrac{\Gamma(N-x+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}\\
\times{}_{p+2}F_{p+1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-|\vec{n}|-\beta,-x,\vec{\alpha}+\vec{n}+\vec{1}_p}{-N-\beta,\vec{\alpha}+\vec{1}_p};1\biggr].

Also in
[
8
]
, the first explicit hypergeometric representation of the Hahn polynomials of type I for
p\geqslant 2
was presented as follows:

(19)

Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{k=1}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{n_{k}}}{\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}-{\alpha}_{i})_{{n}_{k}}}\\
\times{}_{p+2}F_{p+1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i+1,\alpha_i+\beta+|\vec{n}|,(\alpha_i+1)\vec{1}_{p-1}-\vec{\alpha}^{*i}-\vec{n}^{*i},x+\alpha_i+1}{\alpha_i+1,(\alpha_i+1)\vec{1}_{p-1}-\vec{\alpha}^{*i},\alpha_i+\beta+N+2};1\biggr],

or equivalently

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1}C_{\vec{n}}^{(i),l}\,(x+\alpha_{i}+1)_{l},

with

(20)

C_{\vec{n}}^{(i),l}\coloneq\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{k=1}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{n_{k}}}{\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}-{\alpha}_{i})_{{n}_{k}}}\\
\times\dfrac{(-n_{i}+1)_{l}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{l}}{l!(\alpha_{i}+1)_{l}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)_{l}}\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}-{\alpha}_{k}-{n}_{k}+1)_{l}}{(\alpha_{i}-{\alpha}_{k}+1)_{l}}.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that we used the following notation. We denoted the unit vector by

\vec{1}_{p}\coloneq(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Given a vector
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we wrote
\vec{a}^{\ast q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p-1}
for the vector obtained from
\vec{a}
after removing its
q
-th component. This notation will often appear along the rest of the text.

1.5.
Hahn
Integral Representations

In
[
8
]
, the following integral representations for the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials were obtained.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a clockwise contour in
\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Re}(t)>-1\}
enclosing
\cup_{j=1}^{p}[\alpha_{j},n_{j}+\alpha_{j}-1]
exactly once. Then the type I linear forms are given by

Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm I})}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{j}}}{\Gamma(\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}\frac{\Gamma(N-x+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)}\\
\times\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\Sigma$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\Gamma(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)}\frac{\Gamma(x+t+1)}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-t)_{n_{j}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

In particular, the type I polynomials are given by

Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{j}}}{(\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+x+1)}\\
\times\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\Sigma_{i}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\Gamma(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)}\frac{\Gamma(x+t+1)}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-t)_{n_{j}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
\Sigma_{i}
is a clockwise contour in
\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Re}(t)>-1\}
enclosing
\cup_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}\{\alpha_{i}+k\}
exactly once without enclosing any of the other points in
\cup_{j=1}^{p}\cup_{k=0}^{n_{j}-1}\{\alpha_{j}+k\}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a counterclockwise contour enclosing
[-N,0]
exactly once. Then, for
x\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
, the type II polynomials are given by

Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x;\vec{\alpha},\beta,N)=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{j}}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+\beta+1)}\\
\times\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\mathcal{C}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+\beta+N+1)}{\Gamma(s+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}+1-s)_{n_{j}}}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

2.
Hahn Recurrence Coefficients

In this section, we proceed to derive explicit expressions for the recurrence coefficients given in (
5
) for the Hahn family. For that aim we need to establish the following technical lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}
be as defined in (
17
) and
x\in\mathbb{R}
then,

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\dfrac{(-N)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}(x)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}{(x+\beta+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}C^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}_{\vec{n}}=\dfrac{(\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|}(-N)_{|\vec{n}|}}{(x+\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|}}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{q}-x+1)_{{n}_{q}}}{(\alpha_{q}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{q}}}.

Proof.

Consider the Hahn coefficients (
17
) and transform them as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}\coloneq(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\frac{(-N)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}{(-N)_{|\vec{n}|}}C_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}.

The transformed coefficients
\tilde{C}_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}
are exactly the coefficients of the monic type II polynomials for the Jacobi–Piñeiro family, as shown in
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
. Specifically, the Jacobi–Piñeiro type II polynomials can be expressed as:

\displaystyle P_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(z)=\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\tilde{C}_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}z^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}.

In
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
, we demonstrated that for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\frac{(\alpha_{i}+n_{i}+m)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{i}+m+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}\tilde{C}_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}=(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\frac{(\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|}}{(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{i}+m+1)_{|\vec{n}|}}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{q}-\alpha_{i}-n_{i}-m+1)_{n_{q}}}{(\alpha_{q}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{q}}}.

This result can be generalized by replacing
\alpha_{i}+n_{i}+m
with any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. By applying this replacement and using the coefficients
\tilde{C}_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let consider
x,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{C}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
then

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{p}\dfrac{n_{l}}{(\alpha_{l}-x)}\prod_{q=1}^{l-1}(\alpha_{q}-x+1)_{n_{q}}\prod_{q=l}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)_{n_{q}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+1)_{n_{q}}-\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)_{n_{q}}.

Proof.

By removing the common factor
\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+1)_{n_{q}-1}
from both sides of the equation, it reduces to:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{p}n_{l}\prod_{q=1}^{l-1}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})\prod_{q=l+1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)=\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})-\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x),

which is much simpler.

We will proceed by induction over the number of parameters
p
. For
p=1
, the identity is easily proven. Now, assume that it is satisfied for some
p\geq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{p+1}n_{l}\prod_{q=1}^{l-1}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})\prod_{q=l+1}^{p+1}(\alpha_{q}-x)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=(\alpha_{p+1}-x)\sum_{l=1}^{p}n_{l}\prod_{q=1}^{l-1}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})\prod_{q=l+1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)+n_{p+1}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=(\alpha_{p+1}-x)\left(\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})-\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)\right)+n_{p+1}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=(\alpha_{p+1}-x)\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})-(\alpha_{p+1}-x)\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x)+n_{p+1}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=\prod_{q=1}^{p+1}(\alpha_{q}-x+n_{q})-\prod_{q=1}^{p+1}(\alpha_{q}-x).

Hence, the identity also holds for
p+1
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C_{\vec{n}}^{l_{1},\ldots,l_{p}}

{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{i}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{p}=0}^{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p-1}}\frac{\left(-n_{i}+1\right)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{p}!}\frac{\left(-\beta-\left|\vec{n}\right|+2\right)_{k_{i}}}{(c_{i}-1)^{k_{i}}}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\frac{c_{q}^{k_{q}}(n_{q})_{k_{q}}}{\left(c_{i}-c_{q}\right)^{k_{q}}}{(x+\beta)_{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p}}}.

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}
, type II polynomials
K^{({\rm II})}_{\vec{n}}
and recurrence coefficients
b_{\vec{n}}^{(K),j}
; the following respective limiting relations from Hahn hold:

(52a)

\displaystyle K_{\vec{n}}^{(\ast)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\kappa^{(\ast)}_{\tau}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(\ast)}\left(x;\left\{\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau\right\}_{i=1}^{p},\tau,N\right),

(52b)

\displaystyle b_{\vec{n}}^{(K),j}(\vec{\uppi},N)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}b_{\vec{n}}^{j}\left(\left\{\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau\right\}_{i=1}^{p},\tau,N\right),

where

\kappa^{({\rm I})}_{\tau}=\kappa^{({\rm II})}_{\tau}=1,\quad\kappa^{(i)}_{\tau}=\frac{\tau^{N}}{\Gamma(N+1)(1-\uppi_{i})^{N}}.

5.1.
Hypergeometric Representations

The monic type II polynomials can be derived from the Hahn polynomials (
16
) by taking the limit in Corollary (
5.2
), cf.
[
3
]
:

(53)

K_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=(-N)_{|\vec{n}|}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\uppi_{i}^{n_{i}}\,F^{1:1;\cdots;1}_{1:0;\cdots;0}\left[{-x:-n_{1};\cdots;-n_{p}\atop-N:--;\cdots;--}\Bigg|\dfrac{1}{\uppi_{1}},\ldots,\dfrac{1}{\uppi_{p}}\right].

Below, we will derive a hypergeometric representation for the type I Kravchuk polynomials. For
p=2
, an alternative representation was found in
[
6
, Proposition 6.1]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

The type I Kravchuk polynomials are

(54)

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\dfrac{(-1)^{n_{i}-1}}{(n_{i}-1)!(-N)_{|\vec{n}|-n_{i}}(1-\uppi_{i})^{n_{i}-1}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\dfrac{1}{(\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i})^{n_{q}}}\\
\times F^{1:1;\cdots;1}_{1:0;\cdots;0}\left[{-n_{i}+1:-x;\;\vec{n}^{*i}\atop-N+|\vec{n}|-n_{i}:--;\cdots;--}\Bigg|\frac{1}{\uppi_{i}},\left\{\frac{1-\uppi_{q}}{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}\right\}_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\right],

for
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

Proof.

Substituting into the expression (
19
) as indicated in Corollary
5.2
, we obtain:

\displaystyle K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{N!}\dfrac{\tau^{N}}{(1-\uppi_{i})^{N}}\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\tau+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{q=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{q}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{n_{q}}}{\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i}}{(1-\uppi_{q})(1-\uppi_{i})}\tau\right)_{{n}_{q}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1}\dfrac{(-n_{i}+1)_{l}}{l!}\underbrace{\dfrac{\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+N+2\right)_{l}}}_{=\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{l}\frac{(-l)_{k_{i}}}{k_{i}!}\frac{(N-|\vec{n}|+2)_{k_{i}}}{\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+N+2\right)_{k_{i}}}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\underbrace{\dfrac{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{q})(1-\uppi_{i})}\tau-{n}_{q}+1\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{q})(1-\uppi_{i})}\tau+1\right)_{l}}}_{=\sum_{k_{q}=0}^{l}\frac{(-l)_{k_{q}}}{k_{q}!}\frac{(n_{q})_{k_{q}}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+1\right)_{k_{q}}}}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}.

We can now rewrite the fractions within the sum, as previously indicated, using Gauss’s hypergeometric formula (
11
) to simplify the expressions and obtain:

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{N!}\dfrac{\tau^{N}}{(1-\uppi_{i})^{N}}\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\tau+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{q=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{q}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{n_{q}}}{\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i}}{(1-\uppi_{q})(1-\uppi_{i})}\tau\right)_{{n}_{q}}}\\
\times\Bigg(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{i}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{p}=0}^{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p-1}}{\frac{1}{k_{i}!}\frac{(N-|\vec{n}|+2)_{k_{i}}}{\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+N+2\right)_{k_{i}}}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}{\frac{1}{k_{q}!}\frac{(n_{q})_{k_{q}}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+1\right)_{k_{q}}}}\\
\times\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1}\dfrac{(-n_{i}+1)_{l}}{l!}(-l)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{n_{i}}}\right)\Bigg).

The Pochhammer product
(-l)_{k_{1}}\cdots(-l)_{k_{p}}
is equivalent to
(-l)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}
plus lower-order Pochhammer symbols, with these contributions included in
\operatorname{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{\tau^{n_{i}}}\right)
. To further simplify the
l
-labeled sum, we will apply (
11
) once more:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1}

\displaystyle\dfrac{(-n_{i}+1)_{l}}{l!}{{(-l)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{l}}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}(-n_{i}+1)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}\\
\times\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p}}\dfrac{(-n_{i}+1+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p})_{l}}{l!}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}\right)_{l}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}\right)_{l}}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}(-n_{i}+1)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}\dfrac{\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{n_{i}-1}}{(-x)_{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p}}}.

As a result, the expression is simplified to:

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{N!}\dfrac{\tau^{N}}{(1-\uppi_{i})^{N}}\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\tau+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{q=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{q}}\tau+|\vec{n}|\right)_{n_{q}}}{\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i}}{(1-\uppi_{q})(1-\uppi_{i})}\tau\right)_{{n}_{q}}}\\
\times\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{n_{i}-1}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{i}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{p}=0}^{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p-1}}(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{p}!}\left(x+\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+1\right)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}\\
\times\frac{(N-|\vec{n}|+2)_{k_{i}}}{\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+N+2\right)_{k_{i}}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}{\frac{(n_{q})_{k_{q}}}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+1\right)_{k_{q}}}}{(-x)_{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p}}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{n_{i}}}\right)\Bigg).

For
\tau\rightarrow\infty
, the constant part behaves as follows:

\frac{1}{(n_{i}-1)!(-N)_{|\vec{n}|-1}(1-\uppi_{i})^{2(n_{i}-1)}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\frac{1}{(\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i})^{n_{q}}}\,\tau^{n_{i}-1}.

Using this expression, the limit can be applied to obtain the desired result:

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\dfrac{1}{(n_{i}-1)!(-N)_{|\vec{n}|-1}\uppi_{i}^{n_{i}-1}(1-\uppi_{i})^{n_{i}-1}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\dfrac{1}{(\uppi_{q}-\uppi_{i})^{n_{q}}}\\
\times\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{i}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{p}=0}^{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p-1}}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{p}!}{(-\uppi_{i})^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}}}{(N-|\vec{n}|+2)_{k_{i}}}\\
\times\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}(n_{q})_{k_{q}}\left(\frac{1-\uppi_{q}}{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}\right)^{k_{q}}{(-x)_{n_{i}-1-k_{1}-\cdots-k_{p}}}.

To express the limit more conveniently, we can perform index changes as follows:
k_{1}\rightarrow n_{i}-1-l_{1}
and
k_{i}\rightarrow l_{i-1}-l_{i}
for
i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}
. With these transformations, the sums can be restructured to yield the desired expression (
54
).
∎

5.2.
Integral Representations

By applying the limits in Corollary
5.2
, we can derive integral representations from those given for the multiple Hahn polynomials in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Sigma^{\ast}
be a clockwise contour in
\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Re}(t)>0\}
enclosing
\left\{\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{p}
exactly once. Then the type I linear forms
are given by

(55)

K_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm I})}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\frac{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-\uppi_{j})^{n_{j}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)}\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\Sigma^{\ast}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{(1+t)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-N-2}\,t^{x}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(t-\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

In particular, the type I polynomials are given by

K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\frac{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!}{N!\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-\uppi_{j})^{n_{j}}}\frac{1}{\uppi_{i}^{x}(1-\uppi_{i})^{N-x}}\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\Sigma^{\ast}_{i}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{(1+t)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-N-2}\,t^{x}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(t-\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
\Sigma_{i}^{\ast}
is a clockwise contour in
\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Re}(t)>0\}
enclosing
\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}
exactly once without enclosing any of the other points in
\left\{\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{p}
.

Proof.

Consider the contour integral for
Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm I})}\left(x;\left\{\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau\right\}_{i=1}^{p},\tau,N\right)
given in Theorem
1.3
. This integral involves a clockwise contour that encloses exactly once the set
\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}\left[\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau,n_{j}+\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau-1\right]
with

\operatorname{Re}(t)>-1
.
After the change of variables
t\mapsto\tau t
, the contour can be deformed to one enclosing
\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}\left[\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}},n_{j}-1+\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right]
with

\operatorname{Re}(t)>0
.
We can use (
14
) to obtain the asymptotics for the
\tau
-dependent parts of the integrand and prefactor:

(56)

\tau\frac{\Gamma(\tau+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(\tau+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}\frac{\Gamma(\tau t+\tau+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(\tau t+\tau+N+2)}\frac{\Gamma(\tau t+x+1)}{\Gamma(\tau t+1)}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau+\left|\vec{n}\right|\right)_{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau-\tau t\right)_{n_{j}}}\\
\begin{aligned} &\sim\tau^{N-x-\left|\vec{n}\right|+2}((1+t)\tau)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-N-2}(\tau t)^{x}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}-t\right)^{n_{j}}}=\frac{t^{x}}{(1+t)^{N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+2}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}-t\right)^{n_{j}}},\quad\tau\to\infty.\end{aligned}

To obtain the desired result about the type I linear forms, we use limit (
52a
) and deform the contour to
\Sigma^{\ast}
. Once these integral representations are established, one can note that only certain poles contribute to a specific type I polynomial, with the remaining poles relating to different weight functions.
∎

An application of the residue theorem leads to the following formulas for the type I polynomials.

Corollary 5.6
.

The following Rodrigues-type formula for the type I polynomials (
54
) holds:

\displaystyle K_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}}{(-N)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(n_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-\uppi_{j})^{n_{j}}}\frac{1}{\uppi_{i}^{x}(1-\uppi_{i})^{N-x}}\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n_{i}-1}}{\operatorname{d}t^{n_{i}-1}}\right|_{t=\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-N-2}\,t^{x}}{\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}\left(t-\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}\right).

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
be an counterclockwise contour enclosing the origin exactly once. Then, for
x\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the type II polynomials are given by

K_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}(x;\vec{\uppi},N)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-\piup_{j})^{n_{j}}}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\mathcal{C}^{\ast}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{(1+s)^{N-\left|\vec{n}\right|}}{s^{x+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(s-\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

Proof.

Consider the integral representation for

Q_{\vec{n}}^{({\rm II})}\left(x;\left\{\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau\right\}_{i=1}^{p},\tau,N\right)

given in Theorem
1.4
. This representation involves an counterclockwise contour enclosing the interval
[-N,0]
. After changing variables with
s\mapsto\tau s
, we can deform this contour to a contour
\mathcal{C}
enclosing
[-N,0]
. We can use (
14
) to obtain the asymptotics for the
\tau
-dependent parts of the integrand and prefactor:

\tau\frac{\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\tau+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+\tau+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\tau s+N+\tau+1)}{\Gamma(\tau s+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\tau+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\tau s)}{\Gamma(\tau s+x+1)}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau+1-\tau s\right)_{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\tau+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1\right)_{n_{j}}}\\
\begin{aligned} &\sim\tau^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-N+x+1}((1+s)\tau)^{N-\left|\vec{n}\right|}(\tau s)^{-x-1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}-s\right)^{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}}=\frac{(1+s)^{N-\left|\vec{n}\right|}}{s^{x+1}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\uppi_{j}}{1-\uppi_{j}}-s\right)^{n_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}}},\quad\tau\to\infty.\end{aligned}

Combining these results with limit (
52a
) and deforming the contour to
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
then yields the desired result.
∎

5.3.
Recurrence Coefficients

We will now present explicit expressions for the recurrence coefficients.

Theorem 5.8
.

The Kravchuk multiple orthogonal polynomials, of type I (
54
) and type II (
53
), satisfy their respective recurrence relations (
5
) with coefficients:

(57)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} b_{\vec{n}}^{(K),0}(k)&=(N-|\vec{n}|)\uppi_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}(1-\uppi_{i}),\\
b_{\vec{n}}^{(K),j}&=(N-|\vec{n}|+1)_{j}\sum_{i\in S(\pi,j)}{n_{i}\uppi_{i}(1-\uppi_{i})}\prod_{q\in S^{\textsc{c}}(\pi,j)}{(\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q})},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

Here we have to apply the limit (
52b
) to the Hahn coefficients (
2.4
). For
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, applying the limit is relatively straightforward. On the other hand, for
j=0
, additional steps are necessary. In this scenario, the expression simplifies to:

b_{\vec{n}}^{(K),0}(k)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\dfrac{(n_{k}+1)\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+N+2\right)}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|+2}\prod_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+n_{k}+1}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}+1}\\
-\dfrac{n_{k}\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+N+1\right)}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|}\prod_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+n_{k}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}}-\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\Bigg)\\
+\underbrace{\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+|\vec{n}|+n_{k}+1\right)\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{p}\dfrac{n_{i}\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+n_{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+N+n_{i}+1\right)}{\left(\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{k}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{k})}\tau-n_{k}-1+n_{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{i}}\tau+|\vec{n}|+n_{i}\right)_{2}}\prod_{q=1,q\neq i}^{p}\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau+n_{i}}{\frac{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{i})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{i}}}_{=\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{p}\frac{n_{i}\uppi_{i}(1-\uppi_{i})}{\uppi_{i}-\uppi_{k}}}.

The limit of the second term on the right-hand side is straightforward to evaluate, as previously demonstrated. The focus now shifts to the first term. To find this limit, we need to reframe the expression as follows:

\begin{multlined}(n_{k}+1)\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\left(1+\dfrac{N-|\vec{n}|}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|+2}\right)\prod_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\left(1+\dfrac{n_{q}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}+1}\right)\\
-n_{k}\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}\right)\left(1+\dfrac{N-|\vec{n}|+1}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|}\right)\prod_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\left(1+\dfrac{n_{q}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}}\right)-\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\end{multlined}\\
\begin{aligned} &=\begin{multlined}(n_{k}+1)\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\left(1+\dfrac{N-|\vec{n}|}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|+2}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\dfrac{n_{q}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}+1}+\operatorname{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{\tau^{2}}\right)\right)\\
-n_{k}\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}\right)\left(1+\dfrac{N-|\vec{n}|+1}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}\dfrac{n_{q}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{\tau^{2}}\right)\right)-\left(\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1\right)\end{multlined}\\
&=\begin{multlined}n_{k}+(n_{k}+1)\left(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|\right)\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|+2}+(n_{k}+1)\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}n_{q}\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+1}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}+1}\\
-n_{k}\left(N-|\vec{n}|+1\right)\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}}{\frac{1}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}+|\vec{n}|}-n_{k}\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{p}n_{q}\dfrac{\frac{\uppi_{k}}{1-\uppi_{k}}\tau+n_{k}}{\frac{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{q}}{(1-\uppi_{k})(1-\uppi_{q})}\tau-n_{q}+n_{k}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{\tau}\right)\end{multlined}\\
&\sim(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)\uppi_{k}+n_{k}(1-\uppi_{k})+\uppi_{k}\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{p}n_{i}\frac{1-\uppi_{i}}{\uppi_{k}-\uppi_{i}},\quad\tau\to\infty.\end{aligned}

Combining with the other term and simplifying yields the recurrence relation given in (
57
).
∎

6.
Charlier

For the multiple Charlier polynomials, the weight functions are given by

(58)

\displaystyle w_{i}^{(C)}(x;a_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{i}^{x}}{\Gamma(x+1)},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\quad\Delta=\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proposition 6.1
.

\displaystyle w_{i}^{(C)}(x;a_{i})

\displaystyle=\lim_{c\to 0}w_{i}^{(M2)}\left(x;\frac{a_{i}}{c},c\right)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}w_{i}^{(M1)}\left(x;\beta,\frac{a_{i}}{\beta}\right)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{e}^{a_{i}}w^{(K)}_{i}\left(x;\frac{a_{i}}{N},N\right),\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

This follows from the asymptotics:

\displaystyle w_{i}^{(M2)}\left(x;\frac{a_{i}}{c},c\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\frac{\Gamma\left(x+\frac{a_{i}}{c}\right)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{i}}{c}\right)}c^{x}\sim\frac{a_{i}^{x}}{\Gamma(x+1)},&c&\to 0,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle w_{i}^{(M1)}\left(x;\beta,\frac{a_{i}}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\frac{\Gamma(\beta+x)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(\beta)}\frac{a_{i}^{x}}{\beta^{x}}\sim\frac{a_{i}^{x}}{\Gamma(x+1)},&\beta&\to\infty,\end{aligned}

7.
From Discrete to Continuous Families

Recent research has uncovered explicit formulations for the type I classical multiple orthogonal polynomials and their recurrence for the continuous families of Jacobi–Piñeiro, Laguerre, and Hermite polynomials (see
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
). Then, integral representations for the linear forms and type II polynomials were found for the Jacobi–Piñeiro and Laguerre first kind families

A notable distinction is that Bleher and Kuijlaars have some additional conditions on the parameters, which simplifies the analysis for the type II polynomials and allows for the use of a simpler contour which is not applicable in the more general setting. In what follows, we will complete the Askey scheme by describing the three remaining limits. We will need the following auxiliary results.

Lemma 7.10
.

Suppose that
\beta,y,z,a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
x>0
, then

\displaystyle\left(x\beta+y\sqrt{\beta}+z\right)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\sim(x\beta)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{ay}{x}\sqrt{\beta}+\frac{az}{x}-\frac{ay^{2}}{2x^{2}}+\frac{by}{x}},\quad\beta\to\infty.

Proof.

Note that:

\displaystyle\left(x\beta+y\sqrt{\beta}+z\right)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}

\displaystyle=(x\beta)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\left(1+\frac{y}{x\sqrt{\beta}}+\frac{z}{x\beta}\right)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}

\displaystyle=(x\beta)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\exp{\left(\left({a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\right)\ln{\left(1+\frac{y}{x\sqrt{\beta}}+\frac{z}{x\beta}\right)}\right)}.

We can then use the power series of the logarithm

\ln(1+t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}t^{k}

to obtain

\displaystyle\left(x\beta+y\sqrt{\beta}+z\right)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}

\displaystyle=(x\beta)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\exp{\left(\left({a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\right)\left(\frac{y}{x\sqrt{\beta}}+\frac{z}{x\beta}-\frac{y^{2}}{2x^{2}\beta}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}^{3}}\right)\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=(x\beta)^{a\beta+b\sqrt{\beta}+c}\exp{\left(\frac{ay}{x}\sqrt{\beta}+\frac{az}{x}-\frac{ay^{2}}{2x^{2}}+\frac{by}{x}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\right)\right)}.

Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper, explicit hypergeometric and integral representations are provided for the discrete descendants of the Hahn polynomials within the Askey scheme, including the multiple Meixner polynomials of the first and second kinds, as well as the Kravchuk and Charlier polynomials. Additionally, the corresponding near-neighbor recurrence coefficients are presented. The multiple Askey scheme has been completed with corresponding limits.

A intriguing and largely unexplored area lies in mixed multiple orthogonal polynomials. These extend the classical families of multiple orthogonal polynomials discussed in this paper to incorporate mixed multiple cases. Currently, explicit hypergeometric expressions for mixed Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials are yet to be discovered, as are contour integral representations for these polynomials. This domain offers a promising avenue for future research, potentially unveiling new insights into the interactions between different orthogonal polynomial families and their applications.

Another promising line of research is to discover the explicit bidiagonal factorization for the recursion matrix on the stepline. This involves characterizing the regions of positivity and total positivity of this banded Hessenberg matrix. Additionally, constructing explicit Markov chains in terms of these multiple orthogonal polynomials is an intriguing avenue, where one can characterize their properties and factorizations in terms of pure birth and pure death chains. This could lead to a deeper understanding of the underlying stochastic processes and their connections to multiple orthogonal polynomials.

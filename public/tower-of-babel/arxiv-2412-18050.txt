Controllability and Insensitizing Controls for Some Coupled Stochastic Parabolic Systems
Abdellatif Elgrou

Abstract
In this paper, we study the null and approximate controllability, along with the existence of insensitizing controls, for a class of coupled systems described by two linear forward stochastic parabolic equations. The first objective is to identify controls that achieve the null and approximate controllability for these systems at a fixed final control time. The second goal is to find controls such that a functional of the states becomes locally insensitive to perturbations in the initial data. To achieve this, we introduce three controls: one localized in space to affect the drift term of the first equation, and two additional controls that influence the diffusion terms of both equations. Moreover, we reduce the insensitizing control problem to a null controllability problem for a cascade system of coupled forward-backward stochastic parabolic systems. By the classical duality argument, our controllability problem for the forward system (resp., forward-backward systems) is equivalent to an observability problem for the corresponding adjoint backward system (resp., backward-forward systems). The observability problems are then addressed by deriving new global Carleman estimates.

Introduction

Mathematical control theory is a branch of applied mathematics that studies the behavior of dynamic systems and how external inputs can influence or steer these systems. The central objective is to understand how to guide a system’s trajectory to achieve a desired outcome, a concept known as controllability. A comprehensive discussion of controllability in deterministic control systems can be found in
[

,
16
,
23
,
40
,
42
]
. For results related to the controllability of stochastic systems, the reader may consult
[
25
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
, as well as the references cited therein.

The insensitizing control problem is a subfield of control theory that focuses on designing control strategies to mitigate the impact of uncertainties, disturbances, or errors in system parameters. For comprehensive discussions on this topic, we refer the reader to
[
4
,

,

,

, as well as the seminal work by J.-L. Lions
[
24
]
, who first introduced the concept in the context of deterministic heat equations. In the case of stochastic systems, the insensitizing control problem remains relatively underexplored, with only a limited number of results available. Notably,
[
36
]
addresses this problem for forward stochastic heat equations,
[
25
]
considers backward stochastic parabolic equations,
[
28
]
investigates degenerate stochastic parabolic equations, and
[
3
]
explores forward stochastic parabolic equations with dynamic boundary conditions.

In this paper, we investigate two fundamental control problems for coupled stochastic parabolic equations: null/approximate controllability, and the existence of insensitizing controls. For results on the controllability of coupled stochastic parabolic equations, we refer to
[
10
,
15
,
22
]
for the case of coupled backward stochastic parabolic equations. For coupled forward-backward stochastic parabolic equations, see
[
25
,
36
]
. For coupled fourth-order backward stochastic parabolic equations, we refer to
[
39
]
. Additionally,
[
26
]
explores coupled forward fractional stochastic parabolic equations, while
[
27
]
considers a class of forward stochastic parabolic systems. In the latter two works,
[
26
,
27
]
, the coefficients are independent of the spatial variable, and the controllability results are derived using the Lebeau-Robiano strategy. In contrast, our work is the first to address the controllability and insensitizing control problems for general coupled forward stochastic parabolic systems, where the coefficients may depend on space, time, and random variables.

The primary tool in our analysis is the development of new global Carleman estimates for stochastic parabolic systems with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Introduced by Carleman in 1939 (see
[
11
]
) to prove uniqueness for second-order elliptic partial differential equations, Carleman estimates have since become essential for tackling a wide range of problems in applied mathematics, including observability, inverse problems, and controllability. These estimates provide a powerful framework for analyzing controllability and insensitizing control problems in the stochastic setting. For further details and applications of Carleman estimates for both deterministic and stochastic systems, we refer to
[
16
,
31
,
32
,
37
]
.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows: First, we derive two new global Carleman estimates for coupled stochastic parabolic systems with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Second, we apply these estimates to address key observability problems, including the observability inequality and the unique continuation property for general coupled backward stochastic parabolic systems, as well as an observability inequality for coupled backward-forward stochastic parabolic systems. Third, using these observability results, we establish both the null and approximate controllability for coupled forward stochastic parabolic systems and solve an insensitizing control problem, which aims to make the system insensitive to perturbations in the initial conditions.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to simultaneously address both the controllability and insensitizing control problems for general coupled forward stochastic parabolic systems, using the method of Carleman estimates.

1.1
Problem formulation

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
) be a nonempty open bounded domain with a
C^{4}
boundary
\Gamma=\partial G
, and let
T>0
. Denote by
G_{0}
the control region and by
\mathcal{O}
the observation domain, both of which are nonempty open subsets of
G
satisfying the geometric condition

\displaystyle G_{0}\cap\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset.

(1.1)

We denote by
\chi_{G_{0}}
and
\chi_{\mathcal{O}}
the characteristic functions of
G_{0}
and
\mathcal{O}
, respectively. The following notations are used throughout this paper:

Q=(0,T)\times G,\quad\Sigma=(0,T)\times\Gamma,\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{0}=(0,T)\times G_{0}.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a fixed complete filtered probability space, on which a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
is defined. Here,
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the natural filtration generated by
W(\cdot)
, and augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space, and let
C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
denote the Banach space of all continuous
\mathcal{X}
-valued functions defined on
[0,T]
. For some sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
, we define the Banach space
L^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega;\mathcal{X})
as the space of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\mathcal{G}
-measurable random variables
X
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[|X|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]<\infty
, with the canonical norm. Additionally, we denote by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
the Banach space consisting of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-adapted processes
X(\cdot)
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X(\cdot)\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{X})}^{2}\right]<\infty,

with the canonical norm. Similarly,
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
is the Banach space of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-adapted essentially bounded processes, with the canonical norm. The space
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathcal{X}))
is the Banach space consisting of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-adapted continuous processes
X(\cdot)
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[|X(\cdot)|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{X})}^{2}\right]<\infty,

with the canonical norm.
Similarly, one can define the space
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C^{n}([0,T];\mathcal{X}))
for any positive integer
n
. For further details on these stochastic notions, we refer to
[
31
, Chapter 2]
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the coefficients

\sigma_{ij}^{m}:\Omega\times Q\to\mathbb{R}
(
i,j=1,2,\dots,N
;
m=1,2
) satisfy the following assumptions:

1.

\sigma_{ij}^{m}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C^{1}([0,T];W^{2,\infty}(G)))
and
\sigma_{ij}^{m}=\sigma_{ji}^{m}
, for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
,
m=1,2
.

2.

There exists a constant
\sigma_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{m}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}\geq\sigma_{0}|\kappa|^{2},\qquad\text{for any }\;\,(\omega,t,x,\kappa)\in\Omega\times Q\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad m=1,2,

(1.2)

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
and
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N})
.

Define the operators
L_{m}
for
m=1,2
as follows:

\displaystyle L_{m}\textbf{y}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{m}(t,x)\frac{\partial\textbf{y}}{\partial x_{j}}\right).

(1.3)

The first main purpose of this paper is to study the null and approximate controllability of the following coupled forward stochastic parabolic system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy-L_{1}y\,dt=\left[\xi_{1}+a_{11}y+a_{12}z+B_{11}\cdot\nabla y+B_{12}\cdot\nabla z+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}\right]\,dt+u_{2}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
dz-L_{2}z\,dt=[\xi_{2}+a_{21}y+a_{22}z+B_{22}\cdot\nabla z]\,dt+u_{3}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
y=z=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0},\quad z(0)=z_{0}&\textnormal{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
are the given initial conditions,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
represent the known heat source terms, and the coefficients
a_{ij}
and
B_{ij}
are assumed to be

a_{ij}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(G))\;\,\text{for}\;\,1\leq i,j\leq 2,\quad\textnormal{and}\quad B_{ij}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{N}))\;\,\text{for}\;\,1\leq i\leq j\leq 2.

The control variable consists of the three controls:
u_{1}
, localized in space in the drift part of the first equation, and two extra controls
u_{2}
and
u_{3}
in the diffusion parts of both equations, such that

(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G)).

Here, the goal is to prove the null controllability of (
1.4
), which consists of finding controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
that drive the states
y
and
z
from any initial condition
(y_{0},z_{0})
to the state
(0,0)
at the final time
T
. We then establish the approximate controllability of (
1.4
), identifying a different set of controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
that drive the states
y
and
z
from any initial condition
(y_{0},z_{0})
to any neighborhood of any final condition
(y_{T},z_{T})
at the final time
T
.

By the classical duality argument (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Chapter 7]
), it is well known that the null and approximate controllability of (
1.4
) are equivalent, respectively, to an observability inequality and a unique continuation property for the following adjoint backward system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dh+L_{1}h\,dt=[-a_{11}h-a_{21}k+\nabla\cdot(hB_{11})]\,dt+H\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
dk+L_{2}k\,dt=[-a_{12}h-a_{22}k+\nabla\cdot(hB_{12}+kB_{22})]\,dt+K\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
h=k=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
h(T)=h_{T},\quad k(T)=k_{T}&\text{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
h_{T},k_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
are the terminal states.

The second main objective of this paper is to investigate an insensitizing control problem for the system (
1.4
), where small perturbations are introduced to the initial data
y_{0}
and
z_{0}
. Specifically, the modified system is given by

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy-L_{1}y\,dt=[\xi_{1}+a_{11}y+a_{12}z+B_{11}\cdot\nabla y+B_{12}\cdot\nabla z+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}]\,dt+u_{2}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
dz-L_{2}z\,dt=[\xi_{2}+a_{21}y+a_{22}z++B_{22}\cdot\nabla z]\,dt+u_{3}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
y=z=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0}+\tau_{1}\widehat{y}_{0},\quad z(0)=z_{0}+\tau_{2}\widehat{z}_{0}&\textnormal{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.6)

where
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are small unknown parameters. In this context,
\tau_{1}\widehat{y}_{0}
and
\tau_{2}\widehat{z}_{0}
represent small perturbations of the initial data
y_{0}
and
z_{0}
, respectively. We assume that
\widehat{y}_{0},\widehat{z}_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
are unknown functions satisfying the normalization conditions

|\widehat{y}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=|\widehat{z}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=1.

We aim to locally insensitize the following functional

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|z|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

(1.7)

where
(y,z)
is the solution of the system (
1.6
) corresponding to the parameters
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
, and the controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
. The insensitizing control problem, initially introduced by J.-L. Lions in
[
24
]
, consists of finding the control triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
such that the functional
\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
remains locally invariant under small perturbations of the initial data
y_{0}
and
z_{0}
. More precisely, the insensitizing control problem for the system (
1.6
) is formulated as follows: For any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
, a control triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
is said to insensitize the functional
\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
if

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)}{\partial\tau_{1}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)}{\partial\tau_{2}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=0,

(1.8)

for all
\widehat{y}_{0},\widehat{z}_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
satisfying

|\widehat{y}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=|\widehat{z}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=1.

In order to solve our control problems, we assume that the coupling coefficient
a_{21}
satisfies the following sign condition:

•

For the null and approximate controllability of (
1.4
):

a_{21}\geq a_{0}>0\quad\text{or}\quad-a_{21}\geq a_{0}>0,\quad\text{in }(0,T)\times\widetilde{G}_{0},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

(1.9)

for an open set
\widetilde{G}_{0}\subset G_{0}
.

•

For the insensitizing control problem (
1.8
) for (
1.6
):

a_{21}\geq a_{0}>0\quad\text{or}\quad-a_{21}\geq a_{0}>0,\quad\text{in }(0,T)\times\widetilde{G}_{0},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

(1.10)

for an open set
\widetilde{G}_{0}\subset G_{0}\cap\mathcal{O}
.

Note that the systems (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) can be rewritten as the following abstract forward system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}d\mathbf{Y}-L\mathbf{Y}\,dt=[\Xi+A\mathbf{Y}+B\cdot\nabla\textbf{Y}+D\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}]\,dt+u_{\text{ext}}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
\mathbf{Y}=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
\mathbf{Y}(0)=\mathbf{Y}_{0}&\textnormal{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.11)

where
\mathbf{Y}=(y,z)^{\top}
is the state variable,
\nabla\mathbf{Y}=(\nabla y,\nabla z)^{\top}
,
\mathbf{Y}_{0}\in L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the initial state,
\Xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})^{\top}
is the source term,
u_{\text{ext}}=(u_{2},u_{3})^{\top}
represents the extra control,
D=(1,0)^{\top}
,
A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\in L_{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}))
, and
B=(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\in L_{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2}))
(with
B_{21}\equiv 0
) are the coupling matrices. The operator
L=\text{diag}(L_{1},L_{2})
, where
L_{m}
(
m=1,2
) are the self-adjoint second-order operators defined in (
1.3
).

From
[
21
]
, it is easy to check that the system (
1.11
) is well-posed, i.e., for any
\Xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
,
\mathbf{Y}_{0}\in L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
, the system (
1.11
) admits a unique weak solution

\mathbf{Y}=(y,z)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})))\bigcap L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{Y}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})))}+\left|\mathbf{Y}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\left|\mathbf{Y}_{0}\right|_{L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})}+|\Xi|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))}+|u_{1}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))}

\displaystyle+|u_{2}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|u_{3}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\Big).

We also observe that the system (
1.5
) can be rewritten as the following abstract backward system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}d\mathbf{h}+L\mathbf{h}\,dt=[-A^{\top}\mathbf{h}+\nabla\cdot(B^{\top}\textbf{h})]\,dt+\mathbf{H}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
\mathbf{h}=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
\mathbf{h}(T)=\mathbf{h}_{T}&\textnormal{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.12)

where
(\mathbf{h};\mathbf{H})=(h,k;H,K)
is the state variable,
\mathbf{h}_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is the final state,
A^{\top}
and
B^{\top}
denote the transpose matrices of
A
and
B
, respectively. By
[
41
]
, one can show that the system (
1.12
) has a unique weak solution

(\mathbf{h};\mathbf{H})\in\left(L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})))\bigcap L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\right)\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})),

such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{h}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2})))}+\left|\mathbf{h}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))}+\left|\mathbf{H}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C\left|\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))}.

u_{2}
and
u_{3}
, which influence the diffusion terms globally. The inclusion of the controls
u_{2}
and
u_{3}
is essential due to the stochastic nature of the equations, where the Brownian motions introduce randomness in both equations. Additionally, the structure of the corresponding backward stochastic differential equations further complicates the controllability analysis. In particular, the second component of the solution, represented by the correction terms
H
and
K
in the backward system (
1.5
), plays a crucial role in ensuring the well-posedness of the system (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Chapter 4]
). However, these terms introduce additional complexity when applying Carleman estimates, which are commonly employed to prove controllability results, particularly in the context of scalar forward stochastic parabolic equations, as shown in
[
32
]
.

The assumption (
1.9
) (resp., (
1.10
)) is essential for proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
(resp., Theorem
1.3
). If these assumptions are not satisfied, the controllability results may fail to hold. In particular, we now focus on the case where the null controllability result in Theorem
1.1
does not hold. A similar reasoning can be applied to Theorem
1.2
, where we show that a unique continuation property may fail for some solutions of the adjoint equation (
1.5
). The same applies to Theorem
1.3
, as the insensitizing control problem (
1.8
) is equivalent to a classical null controllability problem (see Section
2
). Thus, the failure of the assumptions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) may lead to the non-existence of controllability results for coupled forward stochastic parabolic equations. Specifically, we present the following example:

•

If
\xi_{i}\equiv a_{ij}\equiv B_{ij}\equiv 0
and
\sigma_{ij}^{m}=\delta_{ij}
(where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta with
1\leq i,j\leq N
), the system (
1.4
) reduces to the following system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy-\Delta y\,dt=\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}\,dt+u_{2}\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
dz-\Delta z\,dt=u_{3}\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
y=z=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0},\quad z(0)=z_{0}&\text{in }G.\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.15)

We now show that the system (
1.15
) is not null controllable. To demonstrate this, we construct a particular solution to the adjoint equation which does not satisfy the appropriate observability inequality. Indeed, the adjoint equation of (
1.15
) is the following backward system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dh+\Delta h\,dt=H\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
dk+\Delta k\,dt=K\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
h=k=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
h(T)=h_{T},\quad k(T)=k_{T}&\text{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.16)

and by a duality method (see Sections
4
and
5
), we have that (
1.15
) is null controllable at time
T
if and only if the solutions of (
1.16
) satisfy the observability inequality

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |h(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+|k(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(\chi_{G_{0}}h^{2}+H^{2}+K^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

(1.17)

Let
s_{1}
be the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
(the Dirichlet Laplacian) and
e_{1}
the associated eigenfunction such that
|e_{1}|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
. Define the function
g(t):=\nu(t)e_{1}
, where
\nu\in C^{1}([0,T])
is the solution of the following differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\nu-s_{1}\nu=0,\quad\text{in }(0,T),\\
\nu(0)=1.\end{cases}

Notice that
g
solves the following backward equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}g+\Delta g=0&\text{in }Q,\\
g=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
g(T)=\nu(T)e_{1}&\text{in }G.\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.18)

By the well-posedness result for backward equations, it follows that the solution to (
1.16
) with
h_{T}=0
and
k_{T}=g(T)
is
(h,k;H,K)\equiv(0,g;0,0)
. It is then straightforward to verify that this solution does not satisfy the observability inequality (
1.17
). Therefore, the system (
1.15
) is not null controllable.

The above example illustrates that the additional control
u_{3}
is insufficient to drive the second component
z
of the system (
1.15
) to zero at the final time
T
. In the absence of the condition (
1.9
), the influence of
u_{3}
on the diffusion term in the second equation of (
1.15
) is significantly restricted. Consequently, this limitation prevents the establishment of the controllability results and highlights the necessity of the assumption (
1.9
) for the control of coupled forward stochastic parabolic systems.

Now, several remarks are in order.

Remark 1.1
.

We do not require an additional control on the drift term of the second equation in (
1.4
) and (
1.6
), thanks to the assumptions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) on the coupling term
a_{21}
, as well as the fact that
a_{21}y
can be interpreted as an indirect control for the drift part of the second equation. Using only
u_{1}
is far from sufficient to address control problems for forward stochastic parabolic equations, and this remains an open problem. For further details on partial controllability results with a single control for a class of stochastic heat equations, see
[
9
,
29
,
38
]
, and for results concerning two coupled stochastic parabolic equations, we refer to
[
26
,
27
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Since the controls
u_{2}
and
u_{3}
act over the entire space
G
, the controllability results obtained for the systems (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) can be extended to the system involving the states
y
and
z
in the diffusion terms. More precisely, the diffusion terms
u_{2}\,dW(t)
(resp.,
u_{3}\,dW(t)
) can be replaced by the general terms
[b_{11}y+b_{12}z+u_{2}]\,dW(t)
(resp.,
[b_{21}y+b_{22}z+u_{3}]\,dW(t)
), where
b_{ij}
are some bounded processes. Indeed, for instance, if (
1.4
) is null-controllable, one can find controls
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
such that

y(T)=z(T)=0\quad\textnormal{in}\;G,\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

Then, it is straightforward to verify that the new controls
(u^{\star}_{1},u^{\star}_{2},u^{\star}_{3})
so that

u^{\star}_{1}=u_{1},\quad u^{\star}_{2}=u_{2}-(b_{11}y+b_{12}z),\quad u^{\star}_{3}=u_{3}-(b_{21}y+b_{22}z),

achieve the null controllability of the extended system.

Remark 1.3
.

In this paper, the study of control problems for the systems (
1.4
) and (
1.6
), which involve the first-order term
B_{21}\cdot\nabla y
in the drift part of the second equation, remains widely open. In this context, the assumption
B_{21}\equiv 0
is crucial for proving the key Carleman estimates (
3.11
) and (
3.68
), which are essential for establishing our controllability results. Moreover, using the duality method for Carleman estimates (see
[
2
,
25
]
), the assumption on the coefficients
(B_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq j\leq 2}
is expected to be relaxed to
B_{ij}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

Remark 1.4
.

Under sign conditions similar to (
1.9
)–(
1.10
), this work can be generalized to the control of
n
forward stochastic parabolic equations (with
n\geq 2
and a cascade structure), using
n+1
controls: one localized control in the drift term of the first equation, and
n
extra controls in the diffusion terms.

Remark 1.5
.

We solve the insensitivity control problem (
1.8
) with initial data
y_{0}=z_{0}=0
, but this does not hold for non-zero initial data. In fact, the author in
[
33
]
proved that for certain initial data
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
, the functional
\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
given in (
1.7
) cannot be insensitized. Moreover, we refer to
[
35
]
, where the authors characterize the space of initial data for which insensitization is possible.

Remark 1.6
.

The geometric condition (
1.1
) is essential for establishing the global Carleman estimate in Theorem
3.2
and, consequently, the observability inequality in Proposition
4.3
, which allows us to solve the insensitivity control problem (
1.8
). However, when the assumption (
1.1
) fails, it remains unknown whether the problem (
1.8
) has a solution. For further discussion on this in the context of the heat equation, see
[
14
]
. In the case of the weaker notion of
\varepsilon
-insensitivity, where for any
\varepsilon>0
, one can find controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
that
\varepsilon
-insensitize the functional
\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
, i.e.,

\left|\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)}{\partial\tau_{1}}\bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}\right|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)}{\partial\tau_{2}}\bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}\right|\leq\varepsilon,

it has been shown that the condition (
1.1
) is not necessary in the case of deterministic heat equations, see
[
20
]
. Therefore, it is important to further study the notion of
\varepsilon
-insensitization for coupled stochastic parabolic systems, such as (
1.6
).

Remark 1.7
.

The problem of locally insensitizing the following functional

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y,z)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|y|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

involving the state
y
, can be studied in a similar manner to the functional (
1.7
), by considering the control triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, where
u_{1}
now acts on the drift term of the second equation in (
1.6
). In this case, the condition (
1.10
) must be satisfied for the coupling coefficient
a_{12}
. However, studying this problem with a functional involving both states
y
and
z
remains an open question. It is thus of particular interest to extend the insensitizing control results presented in this paper to more general control problems for the coupled system (
1.6
), involving functionals that depend on both states
y
and
z
, as well as their gradients:

•

Weighted Combination of States: One could consider the following functional

\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{\beta}(y,z)=\frac{1-\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|y|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|z|^{2}\,dx\,dt,\quad\beta\in(0,1),

which insensitizes a weighted combination of the states
y
and
z
.

•

State-Gradient Combination: Alternatively, One could work with functionals involving both the states and their gradients. For instance, the following functional

\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{\beta}(y,z)=\frac{1-\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}f(y,\nabla y)\,dx\,dt+\frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}f(z,\nabla z)\,dx\,dt,\quad\beta\in[0,1],

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{1+N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given function, combines the state values and their derivatives. This approach provides a more general and complex formulation of the control problem, introducing additional analytical depth. For some studies on deterministic heat equations, see
[
19
]
.

is devoted to solving the key observability problems. Finally, in Section

1.1

1.2
, and
1.3
.

2
From insensitizing control to null controllability

In this section, we reformulate the insensitizing control problem (
1.8
) as a null controllability problem for a coupled forward-backward stochastic parabolic systems.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
, and let
((y,z);(p,r;P,R))
be the solution of the following systems of linear forward-backward stochastic parabolic equations associated with the control triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
:

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy-L_{1}y\,dt=[\xi_{1}+a_{11}y+a_{12}z+B_{11}\cdot\nabla y+B_{12}\cdot\nabla z+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}]\,dt+u_{2}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
dz-L_{2}z\,dt=[\xi_{2}+a_{21}y+a_{22}z+B_{22}\cdot\nabla z]\,dt+u_{3}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
y=z=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0},\quad z(0)=z_{0}&\textnormal{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.1)

and

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dp+L_{1}p\,dt=[-a_{11}p-a_{21}r+\nabla\cdot(pB_{11})]\,dt+P\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
dr+L_{2}r\,dt=[-a_{12}p-a_{22}r+\nabla\cdot(pB_{12}+rB_{22})-\chi_{\mathcal{O}}z]\,dt+R\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
p=r=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
p(T)=r(T)=0&\textnormal{in }G.\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.2)

Then, the insensitizing control problem (
1.8
) holds for the control triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
if and only if the solution
((y,z);(p,r;P,R))
of systems (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) satisfies that

\displaystyle p(0)=r(0)=0\quad\textnormal{in }G.

(2.3)

Proof.

We denote by
(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})
the solution of the system (
1.6
) associated with the parameters
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and the controls
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
. Let
\widehat{y}_{0},\widehat{z}_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
be the known initial perturbations of
y_{0}
and
z_{0}
, respectively, such that
|\widehat{y}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=|\widehat{z}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
. By a direct computation of the partial derivative of
\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})}{\partial\tau_{1}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=\lim_{\tau_{1}\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}(z_{\tau_{1}}+z)\frac{z_{\tau_{1}}-z}{\tau_{1}}\,dx\,dt,

(2.4)

where
(y,z)
is the solution of (
2.1
), and
(y_{\tau_{1}},z_{\tau_{1}})
is the solution of the following system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy_{\tau_{1}}-L_{1}y_{\tau_{1}}\,dt=[\xi_{1}+a_{11}y_{\tau_{1}}+a_{12}z_{\tau_{1}}+B_{11}\cdot\nabla y_{\tau_{1}}+B_{12}\cdot\nabla z_{\tau_{1}}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}]\,dt+u_{2}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
dz_{\tau_{1}}-L_{2}z_{\tau_{1}}\,dt=[\xi_{2}+a_{21}y_{\tau_{1}}+a_{22}z_{\tau_{1}}+B_{22}\cdot\nabla z_{\tau_{1}}]\,dt+u_{3}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
y_{\tau_{1}}=z_{\tau_{1}}=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
y_{\tau_{1}}(0)=y_{0}+\tau_{1}\widehat{y}_{0},\quad z_{\tau_{1}}(0)=z_{0}&\textnormal{in }G.\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.5)

Put
\overline{y}=\frac{y_{\tau_{1}}-y}{\tau_{1}}
and
\overline{z}=\frac{z_{\tau_{1}}-z}{\tau_{1}}
, then it is easy to see that
(\overline{y},\overline{z})
is the solution of the following system

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}d\overline{y}-L_{1}\overline{y}\,dt=[a_{11}\overline{y}+a_{12}\overline{z}+B_{11}\cdot\nabla\overline{y}+B_{12}\cdot\nabla\overline{z}]\,dt&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
d\overline{z}-L_{2}\overline{z}\,dt=(a_{21}\overline{y}+a_{22}\overline{z}+B_{22}\cdot\nabla\overline{z}]\,dt&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
\overline{y}=\overline{z}=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
\overline{y}(0)=\widehat{y}_{0},\quad\overline{z}(0)=0&\textnormal{in }G.\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.6)

As a direct consequence of (
2.4
), we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})}{\partial\tau_{1}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}z\overline{z}\,dx\,dt.

(2.7)

Applying Itô’s formula to the systems (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}z\overline{z}\,dx\,dt=\int_{G}\widehat{y}_{0}p(0)\,dx.

(2.8)

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.7
), we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})}{\partial\tau_{1}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=\int_{G}\widehat{y}_{0}p(0)\,dx.

(2.9)

Similarly to
\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}}{\partial\tau_{1}}
, we derive that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})}{\partial\tau_{2}}\Bigg|_{\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0}=\int_{G}\widehat{z}_{0}r(0)\,dx.

(2.10)

From (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we conclude that the insensitivity control problem (
1.8
) is satisfied if and only if

\int_{G}\widehat{y}_{0}p(0)\,dx=\int_{G}\widehat{z}_{0}r(0)\,dx=0,

for all
\widehat{y}_{0},\widehat{z}_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
such that
|\widehat{y}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=|\widehat{z}_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
. Hence, we conclude that the insensitivity control problem (
1.8
) is equivalent to the null controllability problem (
2.3
). This completes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The systems (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) are coupled only through the state
z
in the second equation of (
2.2
). Therefore, to achieve the null controllability of (
2.2
), the controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
first act directly on the forward system (
2.1
). Subsequently, through the coupling term
z
, we control the state
r
of the backward system (
2.2
). Additionally, by the coupling term
a_{21}r
, we further influence the state
p
. Thus, the null controllability of the coupled systems (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) is achieved in a cascade-like manner. This approach ultimately relies on the interaction between the forward and backward systems (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) to establish the desired controllability property (
2.3
).

Through the classical duality argument, the null controllability condition (
2.3
) for the system of equations (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) can be equivalently reformulated as an observability inequality for the following coupled backward-forward stochastic parabolic systems

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dh+L_{1}h\,dt=[-a_{11}h-a_{21}k+\nabla\cdot(hB_{11})]\,dt+H\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
dk+L_{2}k\,dt=[-a_{12}h-a_{22}k+\nabla\cdot(hB_{12}+kB_{22})-\chi_{\mathcal{O}}v]\,dt+K\,dW(t)&\text{in }Q,\\
h=k=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
h(T)=k(T)=0&\text{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.11)

and

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dq-L_{1}q\,dt=[a_{11}q+a_{12}v+B_{11}\cdot\nabla q+B_{12}\cdot\nabla v]\,dt&\text{in }Q,\\
dv-L_{2}v\,dt=[a_{21}q+a_{22}v+B_{22}\cdot\nabla v]\,dt&\text{in }Q,\\
q=v=0&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
q(0)=q_{0}\quad v(0)=v_{0}&\text{in }G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(2.12)

where
q_{0},v_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
.

3
Global Carleman estimates

This section is devoted to deriving two global Carleman estimates: one for the coupled systems (
2.11
)–(
2.12
), and the other for the coupled system (
1.5
). These estimates will be essential for addressing the control problems considered in this paper.

3.1
Preliminary tools

In this subsection, we provide some essential tools for Carleman estimates for forward and backward stochastic parabolic equations. First, we recall the following known result (see
[
17
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any nonempty open subset
\mathcal{B}\Subset G
, there exists a function
\psi\in C^{4}(\overline{G})
such that

\psi>0\;\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,G\,;\qquad\psi=0\;\,\,\,\textnormal{on}\,\,\Gamma;\qquad|\nabla\psi|>0\;\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\overline{G\setminus\mathcal{B}}.

For some parameters
\lambda,\mu\geq 1
, we define the following weight functions

\displaystyle\,\alpha\equiv\alpha(t,x)=\frac{e^{\mu\psi(x)}-e^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}}}{t(T-t)},\qquad\gamma\equiv\gamma(t)=\frac{1}{t(T-t)},\qquad\theta\equiv\theta(t,x)=e^{\lambda\alpha}.

(3.1)

It is easy to see that there exists a constant
C=C(G,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\gamma\geq C,\quad\qquad|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq C\gamma^{2},\quad\qquad|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|\leq C\gamma^{3},\\
&|\alpha_{t}|\leq Ce^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}}\gamma^{2},\quad\qquad|\alpha_{tt}|\leq Ce^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}}\gamma^{3}.\end{aligned}

(3.2)

In what follows, for
\mathcal{B}\subset G
a nonempty open subset,
d\in\mathbb{R}
, and a process
z
, we denote by

I(d,z)=\lambda^{d}\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\theta^{2}\gamma^{d}z^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{d-2}\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\theta^{2}\gamma^{d-2}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Let us first consider the following forward stochastic parabolic equation

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dz-\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{0}(t,x)\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\,dt=F_{0}\,dt+F_{1}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
z=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
z(0)=z_{0}&\textnormal{in}\,\,G,\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.3)

where
z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
,
F_{0},F_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
\sigma_{ij}^{0}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C^{1}([0,T];W^{1,\infty}(G)))
satisfy

1.

\sigma_{ij}^{0}=\sigma_{ji}^{0}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

2.

There exists a positive constant
\sigma^{0}
so that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{0}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}\geq\sigma^{0}|\kappa|^{2}\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad(\omega,t,x,\kappa)\in\Omega\times Q\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.4)

We have the following general Carleman estimate for (
3.3
) (see
[
10
,
25
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset G
be a nonempty open subset and
d\in\mathbb{R}
. There exist a large
\mu_{1}\geq 1
such that for
\mu=\mu_{1}
, one can find a positive constant
C
and a large
\lambda_{1}
depending only on
G
,
\mathcal{B}
,
T
,
\mu_{1}
,
\sigma_{ij}^{0}
,
\sigma^{0}
, and
d
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
,
F_{0},F_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
, the associated solution
z
of equation (
3.3
) satisfies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I(d,z)\leq C\bigg[&\lambda^{d}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\theta^{2}\gamma^{d}z^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{d-3}\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\theta^{2}\gamma^{d-3}F_{0}^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\lambda^{d-1}\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\theta^{2}\gamma^{d-1}F_{1}^{2}\,dx\,dt\bigg].\end{aligned}

(3.5)

, and taking the expectation on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}d(q^{2}+v^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=-2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{1}\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx\,dt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}a_{11}q^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}a_{12}qv\,dx\,dt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}qB_{11}\cdot\nabla q\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}qB_{12}\cdot\nabla v\,dx\,dt-2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{2}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}a_{21}qv\,dx\,dt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}a_{22}v^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}vB_{22}\cdot\nabla v\,dx\,dt.

By Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}(t_{2})+v^{2}(t_{2})\right]\,dx&\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}(t_{1})+v^{2}(t_{1})\right]\,dx+C\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{G}\left(q^{2}+v^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

(4.8)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality to (
4.8
), we find that

\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}(t_{2})+v^{2}(t_{2})\right]\,dx\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}(t_{1})+v^{2}(t_{1})\right]\,dx.

Then for any
t\in(T/4,3T/4)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}\left(t+\frac{T}{4}\right)+v^{2}\left(t+\frac{T}{4}\right)\right]\,dx\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[q^{2}(t)+v^{2}(t)\right]\,dx.

(4.9)

Integrating (
4.9
) with respect to
t\in(T/4,3T/4)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\left(q^{2}+v^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{T/4}^{3T/4}\int_{G}\left(q^{2}+v^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt.

(4.10)

On the other hand, for any
t\in(0,T)
, computing
d(h^{2}+k^{2})
, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}d(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx&=2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx\,ds-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}a_{11}h^{2}\,dx\,ds\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}a_{21}hk\,dx\,ds-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}hB_{11}\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,ds\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}H^{2}\,dx\,ds+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ij}^{2}\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx\,ds\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}a_{12}hk\,dx\,ds-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}a_{22}k^{2}\,dx\,ds\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}hB_{12}\cdot\nabla k\,dx\,ds-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}kB_{22}\cdot\nabla k\,dx\,ds\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-2\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{G}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}kv\,dx\,ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}K^{2}\,dx\,ds,\end{aligned}

which provides that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{G}d(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx&\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{G}(h^{2}+k^{2}+v^{2})\,dx\,ds.\end{aligned}

(4.11)

Since
h(T)=k(T)=0
in
G
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., using Gronwall’s inequality for (
4.11
), we derive that

\mathbb{E}\int_{G}[h^{2}(t)+k^{2}(t)]\,dx\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{G}v^{2}\,dx\,ds.

Then for any
t\in(T/2,T)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}v^{2}\,dx\,dt.

(4.12)

Combining (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{T/4}^{3T/4}\int_{G}(q^{2}+v^{2})\,dx\,dt.

(4.13)

Next, it is straightforward to observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt,

which, combined with (
4.13
), yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{T/4}^{3T/4}\int_{G}\theta^{2}\gamma^{3}(q^{2}+v^{2})\,dx\,dt.

(4.14)

Applying the Carleman estimate (
3.11
) to the right-hand side of (
4.14
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(\chi_{G_{0}}h^{2}+H^{2}+K^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt.

(4.15)

On the other hand, it is easy to see that one can choose a sufficiently large constant
M
such that

\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})\leq C\theta^{2}\gamma^{3},\quad\textnormal{in}\;(0,T/2)\times\overline{G},

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\theta^{2}\gamma^{3}(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

(4.16)

Again, using the Carleman estimate (
3.11
) to the right-hand side of (
4.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\textnormal{exp}(-Mt^{-1})(h^{2}+k^{2})\,dx\,dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(\chi_{G_{0}}h^{2}+H^{2}+K^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

(4.17)

Finally, combining (
4.15
) and (
4.17
), we conclude the desired observability inequality (
4.7
). This completes the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

5
Proofs of the main results

For completeness, this section is dedicated to proving the main results of this paper, as presented in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
. We begin by establishing the null controllability for the system (
1.4
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
satisfying (
1.13
), and define the following linear subspace of the product space

L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(\chi_{G_{0}}h,H,K)\mid(h,k;H,K)\text{ is the solution of }\eqref{adback4.77firstcontro},\text{ and some }h_{T},k_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))\Big\}.

We also consider the following linear functional
\mathcal{L}_{1}
defined on
\mathcal{X}_{1}
as

\mathcal{L}_{1}(\chi_{G_{0}}h,H,K)=-\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2})\,dx\,dt-\int_{G}\left[h(0)y_{0}+k(0)z_{0}\right]\,dx.

Using the estimates (
3.68
) and (
4.1
), it is easy to see that the functional
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is bounded on
\mathcal{X}_{1}
, and we have that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{1}|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X}_{1};\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(|y_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}+|z_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}+\left|\theta^{-1}\gamma^{-3/2}\xi_{1}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+\left|\theta^{-1}\gamma^{-3/2}\xi_{2}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\right).

(5.1)

By the Hahn–Banach theorem, we can extend
\mathcal{L}_{1}
to a bounded linear functional on the whole space

L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
. For simplicity, we still denote this extension by
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.
Using the Riesz representation theorem, there exist controls

(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G)),

such that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2})\,dx\,dt-\int_{G}\left[h(0)y_{0}+k(0)z_{0}\right]\,dx=\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K)\,dx\,dt.

(5.2)

Moreover, by (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|u_{1}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))}+|u_{2}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|u_{3}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\\
&\leq C\left(|y_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}+|z_{0}|_{L^{2}(G)}+\left|\theta^{-1}\gamma^{-3/2}\xi_{1}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+\left|\theta^{-1}\gamma^{-3/2}\xi_{2}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\right).\end{aligned}

We claim that the obtained controls
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
, and
u_{3}
are the desired controls. Indeed, for any
h_{T},k_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, by applying Itô’s formula to the systems (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), we notice that

d(yh+zk)=y\,dh+h\,dy+z\,dk+k\,dz+dy\,dh+dz\,dk,

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[y(T)h_{T}+z(T)k_{T}\right]\,dx=&\,\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K\right)\,dx\,dt\\
&+\int_{G}\left[h(0)y_{0}+k(0)z_{0}\right]\,dx.\end{aligned}

(5.3)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.2
), we deduce that

\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[y(T)h_{T}+z(T)k_{T}\right]\,dx=0.

Since
h_{T}
and
k_{T}
can be chosen arbitrarily in
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, it follows that

y(T)=z(T)=0\quad\text{in }G,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Let us now prove the approximate controllability result for the system (
1.4
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
y_{0},z_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
. To establish the approximate controllability of (
1.4
), it is equivalent to prove that the following reachable state set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{T}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(y(T),z(T))\,\,|\,\,(y,z)\;\;\textnormal{is solution to }\;\eqref{ass15cont}\;\;\textnormal{with some controls}

\displaystyle\,(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\Big\}

is dense in
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
. By contradiction argument, we suppose that there exist a nonzero element
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[y(T)\eta_{1}+z(T)\eta_{2}\right]\,dx=0\quad\text{for all }\;\;(y(T),z(T))\in\mathcal{A}_{T}.

(5.4)

Using Itô’s formula for the systems (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) (with
h_{T}\equiv\eta_{1}
, and
k_{T}\equiv\eta_{2}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{G}\left[y(T)\eta_{1}+z(T)\eta_{2}\right]\,dx=&\,\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K\right)\,dx\,dt\\
&+\int_{G}\left[h(0)y_{0}+k(0)z_{0}\right]\,dx.\end{aligned}

(5.5)

Combining (
5.5
) and (
5.4
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K\right)\,dx\,dt+\int_{G}\left[h(0)y_{0}+k(0)z_{0}\right]\,dx=0.

Choosing
\xi_{1}\equiv\xi_{2}\equiv y_{0}\equiv z_{0}\equiv 0
, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K\right)\,dx\,dt=0,

which holds for all
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
. Therefore, we conclude that

(h,H,K)=(0,0,0)\quad\text{in}\;Q_{0}\times Q^{2},\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

By Proposition
4.2
, this implies that
\eta_{1}\equiv\eta_{2}\equiv 0
in
G
,
\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{A}_{T}
is dense in
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

We now proceed to prove the third main result regarding the existence of insensitizing controls for the coupled system (
1.6
).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
satisfying (
1.14
), and
consider the following linear subspace of the space

L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(\chi_{G_{0}}h,H,K)\mid\;((h,k;H,K);(q,v))\;\textnormal{is the solution of}\;\eqref{adback4.77}-\eqref{adjoforr4.8},\;\textnormal{with some}\;q_{0},v_{0}\in L^{2}(G)\Big\}.

We define the linear functional
\mathcal{L}_{2}
on
\mathcal{X}_{2}
as follows

\mathcal{L}_{2}(\chi_{G_{0}}h,H,K)=-\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2})\,dx\,dt.

By the observability inequality (
4.7
), the functional
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is bounded on
\mathcal{X}_{2}
, and we have that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{2}|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X}_{2};\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left|\textnormal{exp}\left(Mt^{-1}\right)\xi_{1}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+\left|\textnormal{exp}\left(Mt^{-1}\right)\xi_{2}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\right).

(5.6)

Using the Hahn–Banach theorem, we can extend
\mathcal{L}_{2}
to a bounded linear functional on the whole space

L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G)),
and by the Riesz representation theorem, one can find controls

(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G)),

such that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2})\,dx\,dt=\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}(\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K)\,dx\,dt.

(5.7)

Furthermore, from (
5.6
), we also have that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|u_{1}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))}+|u_{2}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|u_{3}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\\
&\leq C\left(\left|\text{exp}\left(Mt^{-1}\right)\xi_{1}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+\left|\text{exp}\left(Mt^{-1}\right)\xi_{2}\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\right).\end{aligned}

(5.8)

Applying Itô’s formula to the systems (
2.1
)-(
2.11
), we compute
d(yh+zk)
, and by integrating the resulting expression over
(0,T)
and taking the expectation on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K-\chi_{\mathcal{O}}vz\right)\,dx\,dt=0.

(5.9)

Next, applying Itô’s formula to the systems (
2.2
)-(
2.12
), we compute
d(pq+rv)
and derive that

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left[p(0)q_{0}+r(0)v_{0}\right]\,dx=\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\chi_{\mathcal{O}}zv\,dx\,dt.

(5.10)

From (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left[p(0)q_{0}+r(0)v_{0}\right]\,dx=\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\left(h\xi_{1}+k\xi_{2}+\chi_{G_{0}}u_{1}h+u_{2}H+u_{3}K\right)\,dx\,dt.

(5.11)

Combining (
5.11
) and (
5.7
), we deduce that

\int_{G}\left[p(0)q_{0}+r(0)v_{0}\right]\,dx=0.

Since
q_{0}
and
v_{0}
are arbitrary functions of
L^{2}(G)
, we obtain that

p(0)=r(0)=0\quad\text{in}\;G.

Thus, by Proposition
2.1
and the inequality (
5.8
), we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

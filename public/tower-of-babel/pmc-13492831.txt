Mapping, dynamism, thematic evolution of the scientific production on Make up or Beauty products and cancer: A scientometric study
Authors: Frank Mayta Tovalino, Berly Delgado Cumpa, Cesar Mauricio Vilchez, Arnaldo Munive Degregori, Lucia Quispe Tasayco, Julia Medina, Fran Espinoza Carhuancho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:In recent years, there has been increasing interest in understanding whether there is a relationship between the use of beauty products and the development of cancer. This study aims to map and analyze the scientific production published on this topic, identifying its evolution, collaboration dynamics, and academic impact.MATERIAL AND METHODS:To comply with transparency and quality, the guidelines defined by the RAMIBS framework when conducting scientometric studies were adhered to. A search was conducted in the Scopus database on March 21, 2025, and 1,996 articles published from 2022 to 2024 were collected. The data were then analyzed by using SciVal and the Bibliometrix package in R Studio. The analytics examined included number of papers, citations per publication, source, author, institution, country, international collaboration, and thematic analysis based on co-occurrence of keywords.RESULTS:Between 2022 and 2025, the analysis revealed an average of 3.7 citations per document, with an average of 7.4 co-authors per article and international participation in 14.8% of the cases. Notable journals included Computers in Biology and Medicine and Medical Physics, and institutions such as the Mayo Clinic and Sun Yat-Sen University. Authors such as Bruns and Wang showed high productivity and impact. The countries with the highest collaboration were the United States, China, and Germany. Thematic analysis identified relevant clusters such as “breast cancer”, “deep learning”, and “skin cancer”, reflecting the evolution of approaches in the area.CONCLUSION:The growing volume of scientific evidence reflects an increasingly collaborative and technologically advanced research landscape. This ongoing expansion underscores the importance of sustained monitoring to better understand the potential cancer-related risks associated with cosmetic product use, ultimately informing safer practices and public health strategies.

Introduction

Today, the demand for cosmetic products, including makeup, nail polish, and personal care products, has grown significantly. They play an undeniable role in people’s daily hygiene; in Europe, almost 100% of the population uses deodorant or liquid shampoo, which contain various chemical substances, mainly preservatives, colorants, metal salts, or UV protectants.[1]

Facial makeup products are commonly used, including concealers, foundations, setting powders, eye makeup, blushes, and lipsticks.[2] By prolonged application of these products, the person is exposed to the skin absorption of the particles they contain. In Europe, despite the regulations imposed on cosmetics, chemical compounds such as formaldehyde (considered a genotoxic carcinogen), phthalates, triclosan, and heavy metals (considered non-genotoxic carcinogens), among others, can be found.[2]

Aluminum, commonly found in deodorants and antiperspirants, is widely studied in literature due to its possible relationship with breast cancer. It is considered a metalloestrogen, given that it can alter the homeostasis of the organism. In addition, due to its hydrophobic nature, it can accumulate in adipose tissue, which can trigger the development of cancer.[134] Similarly, aluminum accumulation is associated with colon cancer patients, favoring the progression of the disease.[4] Despite this, the evidence and scientific studies are insufficient to closely link breast cancer with aluminum; however, it is best to avoid exposure to products containing this substance.[5]

Another substance present in cosmetics is parabens, which are considered to be a non-genotoxic carcinogen.[2] They are also classified as endocrine disrupting chemicals (EDCs), as are metalloestrogens.[6] Initially, they are intended as antimicrobial preservatives in products; however, these agents can alter established hormonal activity.[7] In addition, there are studies associating them with obesogenic effects, suggesting that exposure during critical growth stages may contribute to this condition. Despite this, there is still not enough evidence to link them directly.[89]

Regarding hair products, such as straighteners and dyes, the presence of different EDCs and chemicals has been detected, including formaldehyde or oxidized para-phenylenediamine.[1011] These substances have been associated with carcinogenic activity, especially with the development of breast, ovarian, and uterine cancer.[111213] The latter oncological disease has been associated mostly with postmenopausal women compared with premenopausal women.[13] This information is critical, considering that more than half of women in Europe and the United States report having used permanent hair dyes at least once in their lifetime.[11]

On the other hand, bibliometric studies are an approach to quantitatively analyze global or regional trends in one or several specific topics through statistical techniques.[14] This type of research can be visualized by means of graphs and tables elaborated by specialized programs, and it is possible to appreciate the analysis of citations, most used keywords, total scientific production, either by author, university or country, among others.[1415] While the biological risks of cosmetic ingredients are gaining attention, less is known about how research has evolved in response to these concerns. A bibliometric approach helps map this scientific journey—highlighting trends, key contributors, and collaborative dynamics—offering valuable insight to guide future research and public health priorities.

For all the above-mentioned, the objective of this study was to analyze the mapping, dynamism, and thematic evolution of the scientific production of makeup or beauty products and cancer through a scientometric study.

Materials and Methods

To ensure quality and transparency in the presentation of the results, this study followed the guidelines of the RAMIBS (Reporting Standards for Scientometric Research) framework.[16] This guide allowed us to systematically structure the design, analysis, and reporting of the findings, guaranteeing methodological rigor and coherence at each stage of the scientometric process.

Study design

This is a descriptive, scientometric study that analyzed the scientific production in Scopus on the relationship between makeup and beauty products and cancer. The research focuses on dynamics, mapping, and thematic evolution of publications in this field.

Search process

Data extraction was accomplished on March 21, 2025, following the utilization of the Scopus database. A total of 1,996 scientific articles were identified and their metadata exported to SciVal software for network analysis and publication impact, using the following formula [Supplementary Material S1].

Search Strategy

Criteria selection

Based on predefined eligibility criteria adapted from Study A, the research team identified articles from the Scopus database that aligned with the study’s scope. The selection included empirical primary research—studies that used investigative methods to examine relationships—published in peer-reviewed journals between January 2022 and December 2024. To ensure thematic relevance, eligible publications made explicit reference to cosmetic substances such as parabens, aluminum compounds, and formaldehyde, along with their chemical properties and potential carcinogenic mechanisms from both toxicological and epidemiological perspectives. To maintain a focused and consistent analytical framework, the team excluded review articles, editorials, conference abstracts, and publications lacking full metadata or accessible bibliographic content. Studies based solely on animal models, occupational exposures not related to consumer cosmetics, or indirect environmental contaminants were also excluded, ensuring that the dataset remained centered on human exposure through personal care and beauty product use.

Extraction and analysis

Once the search was performed and the metadata were downloaded from Scopus, the analysis process was carried out in two complementary stages. First, SciVal tools were used to explore the overall scientific output of the production found, including indicators such as volume of publications, the main institutions and countries involved, as well as international collaboration. Subsequently, the Bibliometrix package was used through R Studio to perform a more in-depth qualitative and thematic analysis. This stage included the mapping of keyword co-occurrences, analysis of co-authorship networks and thematic evolution, which made it possible to visualize emerging trends, research cores and key connections within the field. This entire process was conducted in a systematic manner, seeking to maintain methodological rigor while facilitating an accessible understanding of the scientific evolution around the use of beauty products and their possible relationship with cancer.

To maintain consistency and reduce bias in the screening process, two independent researchers (FMT, FEC) independently screened the articles against eligibility criteria described a priori. Disagreements were reconciled through discussion, or a third reviewer was consulted.

Data analysis

The data analysis included variables such as the number of documents, sources, annual growth rate, average age, citations per article, and references. The authors’ keywords, authorship patterns, international collaboration, and co-authorships per document were also evaluated. Through Bibliometrix and SciVal, the performance by journal, sector, and country was explored, along with indicators such as CiteScore, SNIP, and field-weighted citation impact. These data provided a comprehensive view of the behavior, impact, and dynamics of scientific production of beauty products and their relationship with cancer.

Results

In total, 1,996 documents were collected from 888 unique sources, indicating proactive interdisciplinary research across the space. Despite this strong amount of productivity and high level of collaboration (11,798 authors, average of 7.4 co-authors per publication), the yearly growth rate indicates a precursor of decline of -83.6%. This decline potentially indicates a sharp increase in scientific productivity and perhaps an initial interest or a shift in regulation at about the time of the burst, followed by a natural stabilization, followed by other potentially adjacent research. Nevertheless, the dataset retains a fair amount of thematic depth, as evidenced by 60,862 cited sources and a healthy amount of 5,386 author keywords. The collaboration internationally is relatively low (14.8%), and this presents opportunities for extending research partnerships more broadly and garnering global impacts in future studies [Table 1].

The bibliometric analysis based on Bradford’s law made it possible to find the distribution of scientific sources in the three productivity zones. The core zone included journals with the highest impact, with several citations per publication and notable visibility, such as Computers in Biology and Medicine and Medical Physics. In the intermediate zone, we found publications of moderate influence, in this case, Annals of Surgical Oncology and Frontiers in Oncology, in terms of productivity, establishing themselves in a constant productivity, and with a lower concentration of citations. Finally, in the peripheral zone, we find journals such as the Indian Journal of Otolaryngology and Head and Neck Surgery with lower impact, which translates into a dispersion of the scope and relevance of the topics covered. This distribution was a clear reflection of the hierarchization of sources and the unequal academic attention also anticipated by Bradford’s law [Figure 1].

The analysis of the Country Collaboration Map shows that the US, China, and Germany led the world in academic collaboration, effectively becoming nodes of international scientific exchange. The US had the greatest number of bilateral partnerships—specifically with countries including China, Germany, and the United Kingdom—allowing the US a unique position as a hub for collaborative engagement in medical research. China displayed two forms of partnership - an enduring relationship with the US, and a growing relationship with Germany and Japan - that underscored China’s emergence as a rich source of research collaboration. Germany was unique in its abiding integration with European institutions and a number of non-European institutions, while the Russian Federation was minimally incorporated internationally, resulting in insufficient global visibility and influence in research. These phenomena indicate either geopolitical or infrastructural considerations, and will still reflect leadership in the discipline under investigation. The global pre-eminence of institutions such as Mayo Clinic or MD Anderson (University of Texas)—both recognized in the global scholarly domain for their major international contribution to oncology—may explain partially why some countries lead certain collaborative networks. Their rich output and high citation impact in cancer research have garnered international co-authorship and also raised country profiles in the biomedical sciences [Figure 2].

Discussion

In this study, there were 1996 articles published in 2022–2025, which also represents an annual variation of 83.66%. There are only 37 apart from a single author, the remaining had a mean of 7.45 co-authors. “Computers in Biology and Medicine” was identified as the journal with the most scientific production, with 24 publications and 12.8 citations on average per article. In relation to other journals, “Annals of Surgical Oncology” and “Medical Physics” should be cited as excellent journals. In relation to institutions, Mayo Clinic Rochester was the institution in a field-weighted impact of 1.61, and an average of 14.3 views per publication. The author with the largest impact was Christiane Josephine, from the Faculty of Humanities, University of Cologne Bruns, with 12 publications, and an h-index of 66. Finally, the United States, China, and Germany were the countries with the largest international collaborations.

It is undeniable, and the widespread use of cosmetic products in people, as well as the possible repercussions after their application on the skin, is why it is reported that up to 78% of buyers are concerned about the possible adverse effects of these products. 17 Substances such as phthalates, found in fragrances, hair cosmetics, nail polish, and plastics, are related to infertility problems and complications in pregnancy, affecting up to 10% of the population, in addition to causing breast cancer.[1718] In recent years, cosmetic companies have decided to launch products incorporating probiotics in order to benefit the localized microbiota in the skin, which in turn can incorporate prebiotics and postbiotics.[1920]

In Feng et al.,[21] bibliometrical published works studying the use of platelet-rich plasma for cosmetic or esthetic purposes from 2001 to 2022 were assessed. This study was able to filter down what was found in the analysis to compile 306 documents. There was an ongoing tendency to increase the publications per year at a rate of approximately 15.87% per year. Next, the bibliometric analysis identified 1363 authors and 4.77 authors/paper. The three most productive authors were Saedi Nazanin, Gentile Pietro, and Sclafani Anthony P. As for the most productive countries, the USA was first with 88 documents for 27.94% of the total. Second and third were China and Italy, respectively. Similarly, for organizations, Fourth Mil Med Univ and Univ Calif San Diego were both the most productive. In terms of the journals, Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology had 34 publications, while aesthetic plastic surgery and Journal of plastic reconstructive and aesthetic surgery had 17 publications each.

A bibliometric investigation by Giles et al.[22] analyzed the dynamics of publications on the influence of diet on cancer from 1970 to 2022 in PubMed. After purging documents of no relevance to the study, 91,249 articles from 6,228 journals were found. Among the most common types of cancer, colorectal cancer was identified with almost 12,000 related publications, followed by breast, liver, and prostate cancer. In general, a trend of increasing production year after year was identified, especially in studies relating to stomach, liver, and prostate cancer to diet. Diets that included high-fat content were associated with papers on colorectal, liver, breast, prostate, and pancreatic cancer. Fruit and alcohol stood out as the most frequent dietary items over time, with more than 400 publications per year in 2021 and 2022. Similarly, the terms “phytochemicals,” “vitamins,” and “dietary supplements” were the most frequently used bioactive components.

Khoshakhlagh et al.[23] performed a bibliometric analysis of scientific knowledge on human exposure to heavy metals and cancer, using Scopus as the basis of their study. There were 2,118 published works written by 1,473 authors, published in 252 sources from 1972 to 2023. After applying several filters, there were 251 documents and 145 journals provided for analysis. Similar trends to the preceding investigations were reported in terms of annual growth in publications. The author who produced the most works was Masoudreza Sohrabi with 7 documents. Michael P. Waalkes is another prominent author. The most frequent words used were “cadmium” and “carcinogenicity”.

For their part, Du and Tang conducted an in-depth bibliometric study on the evolution of publications, patents, and drug development in natural products for cancer treatment. This study used various data sources, including the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC), PubMed, the Derwent Innovation Index, and the Cortellis database. A total of 7,598 publications were collected between 1965 and 2013, 3,010 NIH-funded projects between 1990 and 2013, 712 NSFC-funded projects between 1993 and 2013, and 641 patents from the period 1990–2013. Annual publications showed sustained growth from 1997, with an upward trend until 2008; however, they experienced a slight decline between 2008 and 2013. Regarding the countries with the highest scientific production and patents, the People’s Republic of China ranked first, followed by the United States, South Korea, Taiwan, and India. In the last 10 years of the study, MeSH terms such as “phytogenic antineoplastic agents,” “Chinese herbal drugs,” “phytotherapy,” and “traditional Chinese medicine” were the most relevant.[24]

The dominance of the United States, China, and Germany in international research collaboration could be attributed to their longstanding investment in academic infrastructure, access to competitive funding, and strategic leadership in fields such as oncology, bioinformatics, and artificial intelligence. These countries host institutions with high-impact publication records and strong collaborative cultures, which naturally attract global partnerships and visibility. However, this landscape also reveals persistent barriers for other regions. Limited access to funding, technological constraints, and lower indexing rates in major databases can reduce visibility and hinder the growth of emerging scientific communities. Bridging these gaps requires inclusive policies, resource sharing, and support for capacity-building efforts in underrepresented regions. Thematic trends within the dataset highlight a shift toward AI-driven approaches—particularly the co-occurrence of “deep learning” and “skin cancer” as dominant topics. This suggests a growing interest in applying artificial intelligence to dermatological diagnostics, with potential to enhance early detection, treatment personalization, and diagnostic accuracy. Such transitions are not merely technological—they reflect a deeper evolution in how global research communities approach complex medical challenges with innovation and collaboration.[21222324]

This study provides useful insights; however, there are some limitations. The use of only Scopus could potentially omit important publications in other major databases, and lead to underrepresentation of studies from regional or specialist journals. The limit of time for the analysis might mean that there are recent studies missing that reflect the changing trends. The inclusion of language bias could give favor to English-speaking countries, and this groups out-contributions from the world. Qualitative validation or expert assessment was not used together with the quantitative bibliometric indicators, and this may lead to there not being relevance given to context, or considered quality. To approach a more inclusive and representative scientific landscape, future analyses should include more diverse databases, multilingual sources, and potentially research methods that include qualitative methodologies.

Finally, this study offers a fresh scientometric lens to explore how research around cosmetic product exposure and cancer risk is evolving—an area often framed by regulations and epidemiology,[2526] but seldom examined through publication dynamics. By weaving together trends in productivity, influential institutions, thematic shifts, and global collaborations from 2022 to 2025, the analysis provides a deeper, more nuanced view of how scientific interest is taking shape. Notably, the intersection of “deep learning” and “skin cancer” as co-emerging themes signals a promising shift toward AI-based diagnostics in oncology, opening a window into how technology is reshaping health research in real time.

Conclusions

This study delivers a comprehensive overview of the scientific activity around the relationship between beauty products and cancer, tracking changing research themes, organizing authors, and features of collaboration from 2022 to 2025. While the declining rate of growth of publications could reflect changing research priorities or a limitation of the available data, the patterns seen in the scientometric data point to an increasing importance of international and interdisciplinary collaboration in grappling with new public health issues arising from exposure to cosmetic products. In addition to informing academic research and public health decisions, these findings will also provide a stronger evidence base for even greater expectational accountability from policymakers, public health agencies and organizations, and manufacturers of cosmetic products.

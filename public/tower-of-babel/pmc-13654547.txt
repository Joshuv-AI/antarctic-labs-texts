Xiangsha Liujunzi Tang: A promising traditional Chinese herbal formula for healing gastric mucosal injury through pyroptosis pathway modulation
Authors: Qifang Sun, Xinying Yi, Shunhua Zhou, Qing Gao, Meiyan Zeng, Xiong Cai, Houpan Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Gastric mucosal injury is a common gastrointestinal disorder characterized by a high recurrence rate, and current clinical treatments remain limited due to adverse reactions. Xiangsha Liujunzi Tang (XSLJZT) is a classic traditional Chinese medicine formula with well-documented gastroprotective efficacy in clinical practice; however, its underlying pharmacological mechanism, particularly its regulatory role in pyroptosis, remains poorly elucidated. In this study, bioinformatics and molecular docking were employed to identify targets and pathways associated with gastric mucosal injury. XSLJZT was manufactured and quality-controlled using the liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining along with scanning electron microscopy was utilized to analyze the morphology and ultrastructure of the gastric mucosal surface. Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining and ELISA were utilized to measure gastric mucosal glycoproteins and the release of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines in rats with IND-induced gastric mucosal injury. Immunofluorescence was employed to detect the expression levels of GSDMD-N, p-NF-κB, p-IKKβ, NLRP3, ASC, and cleaved caspase-1 proteins to elucidate the potential molecular mechanisms underlying XSLJZT's gastroprotective effects. Bioinformatics analysis and molecular docking simulation indicated that key molecules of the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway are closely associated with gastric mucosal injury, and the active components of XSLJZT show potential binding interactions with these target proteins. Animal experiments demonstrated that XSLJZT significantly reduced the ulcer index and improved swelling, hemorrhage, and ulcers of the gastric mucosa. Additionally, XSLJZT administration significantly increased the levels of protective cytokines (IL-4, IL-10, and IL-12) while decreasing the levels of pro-inflammatory mediators (IL-18, IL-1β, and TNF-α). Further in vivo validation confirmed that XSLJZT markedly downregulated the protein expression of GSDMD-N, p-NF-κB, p-IKKβ, NLRP3, ASC, and cleaved caspase-1. These findings suggest that XSLJZT may exert a therapeutic effect on digestive disorders associated with gastric mucosal injury by modulating the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis signaling pathway.

Introduction

The gastric mucosa, lining the inner surface of the stomach cavity, comprises epithelium, lamina propria, and muscularis mucosae. This intricate structure serves as a protective barrier, shielding the host from the acidic gastric juice and external aggressors. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are a class of medications commonly used to alleviate pain, reduce inflammation, and manage fever associated with various conditions. However, the gastric mucosal damage and the abnormal secretion of gastric acid resulting from the widespread use of NSAIDs is becoming increasingly apparent and serious [1]. Prevalent digestive disorders such as gastric ulcers, chronic atrophic gastritis, and gastroesophageal reflux are strongly connected to the corrosive effects of gastric acid on the mucosa [2,3]. These conditions, affecting 0.1%-0.3% of the population annually, significantly impair quality of life [4] and may progress to more severe complications, including perforation or cancer, if left untreated. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) have always been the drug of first choice for the treatment of gastric mucosal injury, offering satisfactory efficacy and relatively low toxicity [5]. However, long-term use of PPIs may be associated with several adverse reactions, including pneumonia, cardiovascular disease, fractures, constipation, and nutritional deficiencies [6]. Given these limitations, it is particularly important to develop treatment strategies based on ancient traditional medicines to address gastric mucosal lesions more effectively and safely.

Xiangsha Liujunzi Tang (XSLJZT) is a historic Chinese medicinal formula that has been used for centuries to maintain digestive health. Initially documented in the prestigious Ancient and Modern Famous Medical Prescriptions during the Qing Dynasty, XSLJZT comprises nine traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) herbs that can tonify Qi, benefit the spleen, and regulate the stomach. XSLJZT is widely used in the clinical treatment of chronic gastritis, gastric ulcers, functional dyspepsia, and other digestive system diseases characterized by gastrointestinal mucosal injury [[7], [8], [9]]. Recent pharmacological research has indicated that XSLJZT can regulate gastrointestinal function, boost digestive secretions, reduce inflammation, promote the repair of damaged mucosa, and strengthen the immune system [10,11]. A recent clinical investigation demonstrated that XSLJZT can repair gastric mucosal damage in patients, thereby promoting gastric ulcer healing. XSLJZT exhibited a favorable safety profile while exerting its therapeutic effects [12]. In addition, a significant number of clinical trials have shown that XSLJZT can treat various digestive system diseases, particularly those caused by gastric mucosal injury, and improve patients' clinical manifestations [[13], [14], [15]]. However, the mechanisms by which XSLJZT exerts its protective effects on the gastric mucosa in various digestive system diseases have been mainly linked to anti-inflammatory activity and the modulation of gastrointestinal motility, whereas its role in regulating cell pyroptosis remains largely undefined.

To clarify that XSLJZT offers similar efficacy with a reduced incidence of adverse effects compared to chemical drugs, it is crucial to delve deeper into the molecular pathways that are closely related to gastric mucosal damage and the key therapeutic targets associated with XSLJZT. Gastric mucosal injury results from a variety of factors, including inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, alterations in the mucosal protective barrier, Helicobacter pylori infection, and the widespread use of NSAIDs [16,17]. The NLRP3 inflammasome, which is responsible for triggering inflammatory reactions, is an intracellular complex primarily composed of the NLRP3 receptor, the adaptor protein ASC, and pro-caspase-1. Upon activation by endogenous danger signals, NLRP3 forms inflammasomes with ASC. This complex recruits and activates caspase-1 through proteolytic cleavage [18]. Activated caspase-1 facilitates the maturation of pro-interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and pro-interleukin-18 (IL-18) into their bioactive cytokine forms. Additionally, caspase-1 mediates the proteolytic cleavage of Gasdermin D (GSDMD), yielding N-terminal domains that oligomerize to create transmembrane pores. This lytic cell death process, known as pyroptosis, leads to cell swelling, rupture, and liberation of pro-inflammatory mediators [19]. NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis is a well-known pathogenic driver in multiple chronic inflammatory disorders, encompassing arthritis, peptic ulcers, and ulcerative colitis [20]. In this study, a rat model of indomethacin (IND)-induced gastric mucosal injury was employed to evaluate the pharmacological efficacy of XSLJZT. The therapeutic potential and underlying mechanisms of XSLJZT in mitigating gastric mucosal injury were assessed using multi-parametric evaluations, such as ulcer index, histological analysis, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and immunofluorescence assay. The molecular-level study focused on the dynamics of NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, activation, and the subsequent induction of pyroptosis in gastric mucosal cells.

Material and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Data acquisition and standardization

To explore the core pathogenic mechanism of gastric mucosal injury, systematic data mining was performed using the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) with the keyword "gastric mucosal injury". This search identified dataset GSE201565, which was generated on the GPL25947 platform and contains a total of 10 rat gastric mucosal samples covering multiple experimental subgroups, including GSM6067581, GSM6067582, GSM6067583, GSM6067584, GSM6067585, GSM6067586, GSM6067587, GSM6067588, GSM6067589, and GSM6067590. To identify transcriptomic alterations specifically related to IND-induced gastric mucosal injury, only samples from the normal control group and the IND-induced injury group were selected for differential expression analysis in this study. The samples included two normal control samples (GSM6067581 and GSM6067582) as well as two samples of IND-induced gastric mucosal injury (GSM6067583 and GSM6067584). The remaining six samples belonged to other experimental subgroups and were not included in this comparison because they did not meet the inclusion criteria for the normal control versus IND-induced injury comparison. This selection was based on the study design and comparison purpose, rather than data quality.

Screening of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The data were categorically stratified into two groups: a normal group and an IND-induced gastric mucosal injury group. Differential expression analysis between the two groups was conducted using the empirical Bayes method implemented in the limma package. A volcano plot was generated to visualize the overall distribution of DEGs. To screen for statistically significant DEGs, a two-tiered statistical threshold of |log2 fold change (FC)| > 1 and P < 0.05 was applied. The significant DEGs identified under these criteria were subsequently used to generate a hierarchical clustering heat-map.

Functional enrichment analysis

Utilization of the Metascape database (http://metascape.org/) facilitated the investigation of the biological functions and pathways associated with the DEGs between normal controls and rats with gastric mucosal injury. This exploration employed annotations from the Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. To identify relevant pathways, a minimum overlap of 3 genes and a maximum enrichment fold of 1.5 were used as screening criteria. Additionally, a P-value cutoff of 0.05 was applied to guarantee the statistical significance of pathway enrichment generated by DEGs. The results were then depicted through the OmicShare tool (https://www.omicshare.com/), an internet-based resource for data interpretation. Finally, the KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/) was utilized to analyze the most relevant signaling pathway map, uncovering the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated cell pyroptosis signaling pathway as the central element of this investigation.

Molecular docking

To explore the possible interactions between the active constituents of XSLJZT and the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway, the three-dimensional structures of these active ingredients were sourced from the PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP) database (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php). The structural coordinates of the signaling pathway-associated receptor protein were acquired through the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB accession available at https://www.rcsb.org). Molecular docking simulations were conducted utilizing two software programs: CB-DOCK2 (https://cadd.labshare.cn) to generate initial binding energies visualized as a heat-map, and AutoDock 4.2.6 to remove all non-essential water molecules and original ligands, add polar hydrogens, and assign Gasteiger charges to prepare the molecular structure for further analysis. Molecular visualization was subsequently conducted using PyMOL 2.5.0 to map ligand-receptor interaction patterns derived from integrated docking results.

Preparation of XSLJZT extract

A blend of nine TCM herbs, mixed in prescribed proportions, was soaked in distilled water at a 1:8 weight-to-volume ratio for half an hour. The hydrated herbal mixture was then decocted twice for 30 min each, followed by filtration. The composite filtrates were concentrated by vacuum-assisted rotary evaporation to obtain a crude drug-equivalent concentration of 3.40 g/mL, followed by comprehensive phytochemical profiling using LC-MS/MS. This validated analytical methodology enabled the precise quantification of bioactive constituents of XSLJZT while implementing pharmacopeial standards for quality assurance in TCM preparation [21].

Acute toxicity test

A standardized acute toxicity study was designed to assess the potential acute adverse effects and their severity in rats exposed to XSLJZT extract. A total of 48 Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (24 males and 24 females) weighing 180-200 g were allocated to the control and treatment groups via sex-stratified randomization to ensure balanced sex distribution in each group. The control group received oral administration of 5% Tween 80 solution at 5 mL/kg body weight, while the treatment group received XSLJZT extract orally at a dose of 68 g/kg. All animals were monitored for toxicological responses continuously during the first 6 h post-dosing and at predetermined intervals throughout the 14-day observation period.

Animals and treatment

Sixty SD rats, half male and half female, approximately 6 weeks old and each weighing between 180 and 200 g, were sourced from Hunan Slack Jingda Experimental Animal Co., Ltd. (Quality certificate No. ZS-202309200003). The rats were acclimatized for 5 days under controlled conditions with a temperature of 25 ± 0.5°C, 55% ± 5% relative humidity, and a 12-h light-dark cycle, with unlimited access to water and food. The study protocol received formal approval from the Animal Ethics Committee of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine (Ethic Approval No. LLBH-202307070001), with all operations complying strictly with Guidelines of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine for the Welfare of Laboratory Animals.

After a period of acclimation, the rats were allocated into five groups using a sex-stratified randomization strategy to ensure balanced sex distribution across all groups. Each group consisted of twelve rats, with six males and six females. The normal group served as a control and received no intervention. The remaining groups were administered IND orally at a dose of 5 mg/kg for 5 days to induce gastric mucosal injury, as described in the literature [22]. Following this, isotonic saline was administered to both the normal and IND-induced model groups. The positive control group received an oral dose of rabeprazole at 2.08 mg/kg, while the two treatment groups received oral XSLJZT extract at doses of 6.8 and 3.4 g/kg, respectively. All oral administrations, including IND, rabeprazole, XSLJZT, and isotonic saline, were performed at a fixed volume of 10 mL/kg once daily between 15:00 and 17:00. The target doses were achieved by adjusting the concentration of each solution. This treatment regimen was maintained for 3 days. Rats were included if they completed the acclimation period and the full experimental protocol and had adequate gastric tissue for analysis. Exclusion criteria were death before the end of the experiment, severe illness unrelated to the experiment, or incomplete tissue samples unsuitable for analysis. A total of 12 rats were excluded due to death or incomplete tissue samples. The investigators responsible for animal grouping and drug administration were aware of group allocation, whereas personnel responsible for tissue collection, histopathological scoring, immunofluorescence data acquisition, and statistical analysis remained blinded to group allocation. Before sample collection, rats were fasted for 24 h with free access to water. At 12 h after the final administration, rats were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (40 mg/kg, i.p.) and then euthanized by cervical dislocation. Fresh gastric tissues were collected for morphological and histopathological examination. Plasma was extracted from blood samples by centrifuging at 3000 rpm for 5 min and stored at −80°C for subsequent biochemical and inflammatory factor analysis.

Evaluation of gastric ulcer index

Following excision along its greater curvature, the gastric specimens were treated with ice-cold saline to evacuate residual contents and mucus, and photographed for macroscopic examination of gastric mucosal damage. Using the approach specified in the literature, the ulcer index (UI) and curative index (CI) were determined [2]. The UI was calculated as the average score of gastric ulcerative lesions in each group of rats. The CI was determined using the formula: CI (%) = (UI of the model group - UI of the treated group) divided by UI of the model group.

Histological analysis

The gastric mucosal specimens were harvested and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h. Following dehydration through an ascending alcohol series and PBS washes, the specimens were embedded in paraffin. Slices with a thickness of 4 μm were prepared using a pathological slicer (DAKEWE, Shenzhen, China). These sections underwent dewaxing with xylene and subsequently rehydrated with anhydrous ethanol. Afterward, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was applied to the gastric mucosal specimens, which were then mounted with neutral gum for histopathological inspection. This examination was conducted using a Nikon Eclipse E100 microscope (Japan) along with an imaging system. To assess the pathological changes induced by IND, we followed the protocol described by Yu et al. [23], which involved evaluating epithelial loss, inflammatory cell infiltration, and mucosal edema.

Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining

To assess gastric secretion and differentiate between acidic and basic glycoproteins in mucus, gastric tissue sections were subjected to PAS staining. Following dewaxing and rehydration of paraffin-embedded sections, they underwent oxidation using 0.5% periodic acid for a duration of 5 min. The sections were rinsed with distilled water, followed by staining with Schiff reagent, and then washed in sulfur-containing water and tap water for 10 min each. The PAS-stained sections, which highlighted glycoproteins in the gastric mucosa, were then inspected under a microscope. ImageJ software was utilized to perform detailed quantitative analysis of the glycoprotein distribution and density in the gastric mucosa.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

Following a PBS wash, the gastric tissue was placed in an electron microscope fixative and incubated at room temperature for 2 h. After fixation, the sample was stored at 4°C. It was then subjected to a triple washing procedure with 0.1 M PBS, with each wash lasting for 15 min. Subsequently, the sample was placed in a 1% osmium tetroxide solution, and the incubation was conducted at room temperature for 2 h. This was followed by three additional rinses with 0.1 M PBS for 15 min each. The dehydration process involved a series of treatments with anhydrous ethanol and isoamyl acetate for 15 min. Residual solvent was eliminated using critical point drying. Finally, the sample was covered with a conductive carbon layer, placed on a stage, and subjected to gold sputtering for 30 s using an ion-sputtering instrument (HITACHI, Japan) [24]. The sample was then examined and imaged using a scanning electron microscope (HITACHI, Japan).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Following centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min, ELISA was used to measure the levels of IL-18, IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-4, IL-10, and IL-12 in the supernatant of rat gastric tissue. The procedures were carried out in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions to ensure accuracy and reliability. A microplate reader (ThermoFisher Lux, Massachusetts, USA) was used to measure absorbance at 450 nm. A calibration curve was plotted with absorbance on the y-axis and cytokine concentration on the x-axis. The cytokine levels in the samples were calculated by interpolating their absorbance values onto the standard curve.

Immunofluorescence staining

Dewaxed paraffin-embedded gastric tissue sections were processed using microwave antigen retrieval in a repair buffer. After blocking with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min, the sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with all primary antibodies separately (diluted 1:400). Following three 5-min washes with PBS (pH 7.4), the secondary antibody was applied to incubate the slices for 50 min at ambient temperature in the dark. DAPI staining was performed avoiding light exposure for 10 min at room temperature. Anti-fluorescence quenching agents were used to seal the sections, which were then visualized with a fluorescence microscope (Nikon Eclipse C1, Japan). The fluorescence intensity of the images was quantified using Image Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Statistical analysis

Original data processing was conducted using SPSS (version 25) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0). Data were normalized using the mean ± standard deviation. If the data met the condition of homogeneity of variance, a one-way ANOVA was performed to compare the treated groups, the IND-induced model group, and the normal control group; otherwise, Dunnett's T3 test was employed. Statistical significance was attributed to a P-value < 0.05.

Results

NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated cell pyroptosis is involved in gastric mucosal injury

After preprocessing and quantile normalization of all 10 samples from the GSE201565 dataset, a violin plot (Fig. 1A) was generated to visualize the relative expression values and data quality of each sample. All ten data points fell within the 95% confidence interval and exhibited a linear trend, indicating high-quality sample data suitable for subsequent analysis of DEGs between gastric mucosal injury and normal tissues. Two normal samples (GSM6067581 and GSM6067582) and two IND-induced gastric mucosal injury samples (GSM6067583 and GSM6067584) were selected for differential expression analysis. A total of 14,286 genes were obtained from these four samples. The distribution pattern of DEGs was visualized using a volcano plot (Fig. 1B). A total of 130 significant DEGs were identified with |log2FC| > 1 and P < 0.05, comprising 124 upregulated genes and 6 downregulated genes. To illustrate the important and key DEGs between the two groups of samples, a clustering heat-map (Fig. 1C) was generated. Results of GO analysis and KEGG analysis

A total of 552 enriched GO terms were identified, including 489 biological processes (BP), 20 cellular components (CC), and 43 molecular functions (MF). The BP analysis indicated that the DEGs were primarily enriched in cell chemotaxis, inflammatory response, leukocyte migration, reactions to lipopolysaccharide, and the upregulation of cytokine production. In terms of CC, the DEGs interacted with the outer aspect of the plasma membrane, platelet alpha granules, secretory granules, vesicle structures, extracellular matrix components, and extracellular vesicles. The MF analysis revealed that the DEGs participated in roles such as receptor-ligand interactions, activation of signaling receptors, chemokine and cytokine activities, and binding functions associated with cell adhesion molecules.

KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that DEGs between normal and damaged gastric tissue were primarily associated with the IL-17 signaling pathway, C-type lectin receptor signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, and NOD-like receptor signaling pathway. Fig. 1D–E presents the top ten enriched components for -log2P in BP, CC, and MF categories, as well as the top nineteen pathways. The first circle of the diagram, arranged from outer to inner segments, represents distinct enrichment categories, color-coded for visual differentiation. The second circle is the gene count and P value for each enrichment category. The length of the bar represents the gene count, while the intensity of the color (redder) signifies a lower P value. The third circle indicates the overall count of foreground DEGs, whereas the fourth circle denotes the rich factor value for each category, calculated as the ratio of foreground genes to background genes within that classification. Given that the predominant pathogenesis of gastric mucosal injury involves inflammatory responses, anti-inflammatory and mucosal repair strategies are core treatment concepts. As depicted in Fig. 1F, a node diagram illustrates the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, emphasizing the NLRP3 inflammasome and its key targets in purple. The NLRP3 inflammasome plays a significant role in triggering and advancing inflammatory responses and pyroptosis in gastric mucosal epithelial cells. The enrichment of the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway suggests its potential importance in this context. Consequently, the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway was selected for molecular-level validation in subsequent animal studies.

Molecular docking

It is well-established that a molecular docking binding energy below −5.0 kcal/mol indicates favorable affinity between Chinese herbal medicine active ingredients and target proteins. Lower binding energies generally correspond to stronger molecular interactions. Analysis of the results presented in Fig. 2 revealed stable binding interactions between the active components of XSLJZT and the target proteins within the docking pocket, with ligand-receptor binding affinities ranging from −4.0 to −10.9 kcal/mol. XSLJZT induces no acute toxicity in rats

An acute oral toxicity study was conducted following OECD-423 guidelines. No animal mortality, abnormal general behavior, or significant body weight fluctuation was observed in either group throughout the 14-day observation period. These results indicate that XSLJZT exhibits favorable acute tolerance at the single high dose of 68 g/kg under the current experimental conditions, which provides a preliminary safety basis for the dose setting of subsequent pharmacodynamic studies.

XSLJZT improves gastric mucus secretion

Fig. 4C and I demonstrated that rats in the normal group exhibited robust PAS staining, indicative of adequate and abundant glycoprotein expression in the gastric mucosa. In contrast, the IND-induced model group showed a marked reduction in PAS staining, suggesting that IND suppressed the presence of glycoproteins, glycolipids, and mucins within the gastric mucosal layer. The administration of XSLJZT increased PAS staining intensity compared to the negative control group, signifying an increase in the number of mucus-secreting epithelial cells in the gastric mucosal layer and restored the integrity of the mucosal epithelium. These findings indicate that XSLJZT may protect against gastric mucosal injury induced by IND by modulating the secretion of gastric mucin.

Discussion

Chronic gastritis, gastric ulcers, gastroesophageal reflux, and other digestive disorders are often associated with gastric mucosal injury, manifesting as symptoms such as anorexia, upper abdominal discomfort, nausea, vomiting, hematemesis, or melena [25,26]. In recent years, food-medicine homologous plants and classic herbal formulas have gained increasing attention for treating functional gastrointestinal disorders and mucosal injury-related digestive diseases due to their reliable clinical efficacy and favorable safety profiles [27]. XSLJZT, a classic formula from TCM, has been widely utilized for the treatment of gastrointestinal diseases and is recognized to possess the effect of enhancing gastric mucosal healing, decreasing inflammation, and adjusting the immune response [28]. In our study, the treatment with XSLJZT significantly improved the IND-induced gastric mucosal damage and reduced the expression of proteins related to the pyroptosis induced by the NLRP3 inflammasome.

Through bioinformatics analysis of the GSE201565 dataset, which compared normal gastric tissues with IND-induced gastric mucosal injury tissues, we identified 130 significantly DEGs, comprising 124 upregulated and 6 downregulated genes. Functional enrichment analyses revealed significant differences in BP, CC, and MF between injured and normal gastric mucosal tissues. Specifically, BP enrichment highlighted pathways associated with immune activation, including chemotaxis, inflammatory responses, and leukocyte migration, suggesting a pronounced inflammatory microenvironment following IND exposure. CC analysis revealed significant enrichment in the outer plasma membrane surface, platelet α-granules, secretory granules, and particularly the extracellular matrix (ECM). The ECM is essential for preserving the integrity of the gastric mucosa and shielding it from the attack of gastric acid and digestive enzymes [29]. Moreover, ECM components can regulate inflammatory factor activity, and inflammatory cells can migrate to injury sites by following ECM degradation products [30]. According to KEGG pathway analysis, the significant DEGs were mainly concentrated in the IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway. A recent study demonstrated that LiZhong Pill effectively mitigates inflammation and oxidative stress in IND-induced gastric mucosal injury by suppressing the expression of key proteins involved in the IL-17 and TNF signaling pathways [23]. Eupatilin also exerts gastroprotective effects against ethanol-induced mucosal injury through inhibition of the NF-κB signaling pathway [31]. Previous studies have already demonstrated that the pathogenesis of NSAID-induced gastric mucosal injury involves a complex interplay of multiple programmed cell death modalities, including apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis [32]. The NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, mediated by NLRs, plays an important role in orchestrating immune activation immune and inflammatory responses. NLRP3 is the core effector molecule in the NLR family that directly mediates inflammasome assembly and pyroptotic cell death [33]. Accumulating studies have demonstrated that IND administration triggers excessive activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in gastric mucosal epithelial cells and lamina propria macrophages, which is a critical driving event for mucosal erosion, hemorrhage, and ulcer formation [34,35]. Meanwhile, the interaction between the NF-κB signaling pathway and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway results in a feedback loop that amplifies gastric immune and inflammatory responses [36]. Accordingly, we focused on the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway within the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway and identified key active components of XSLJZT for molecular docking. Molecular docking analysis further indicates that the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway is a promising potential pharmacological target of XSLJZT, which lends preliminary support to our mechanistic hypothesis.

The characteristic components identified by LC-MS/MS have well-documented gastroprotective and anti-inflammatory bioactivities. Specifically, atractylenolide I has been verified to alleviate IND-induced gastric ulcers by directly inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation [35]. Costunolide can target the NACHT domain of NLRP3 to block inflammasome assembly and attenuate gastrointestinal inflammatory injury [37]. In addition, curcumin, glycyrrhizin, and nobiletin all suppress NLRP3 inflammasome activation and downstream pyroptotic signaling, and ameliorate gastric mucosal damage through anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory pathways [[38], [39], [40]]. Lobetyolin is a recognized gastroprotective component that reduces mucosal injury by downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines [41], while L-proline and N-acetyl-glutamic acid participate in mucosal barrier repair and alleviate oxidative stress to support tissue healing [42]. The bioactivities of these components are highly consistent with the gastroprotective effects and NLRP3 pyroptosis pathway regulation observed in this study, suggesting that they may serve as the core pharmacodynamic material basis of XSLJZT. In future research, we will combine high-throughput screening studies with molecular dynamics simulations, as well as pull-down assays, to enable us to precisely determine the bioactive components of XSLJZT and its direct molecular targets [43].

IND, a common experimental chemical drug, is known to induce gastric mucosal injury characterized by edema, ulcers, bleeding, and an increased ulcer index following intragastric administration. Our histopathological and SEM analyses consistently revealed epithelial cell destruction, inflammatory cell infiltration, and hemorrhagic lesions in the rat gastric mucosa after IND administration, corroborating previous findings [44]. In contrast, XSLJZT treatment significantly alleviated these pathological changes, reducing gastric mucosal ulcers, promoting epithelial cell regeneration, and diminishing inflammatory cell infiltration, suggesting its therapeutic potential for gastric mucosal injury. Gastric mucosa is rich in glycogen, glycoproteins, and mucus, collectively contributing to the stomach's barrier function and protective mechanisms. PAS staining in our study demonstrated a notable reduction in gastric mucus secretion in the model group. However, XSLJZT treatment significantly enhanced PAS staining, indicating its protective role in maintaining gastric mucosal integrity. These results emphasize the crucial impact of XSLJZT in mitigating gastric mucosal injury, inflammation, and ulceration.

Under physiological conditions, the stability of the gastrointestinal immune system is maintained by a delicate balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory factors. However, an imbalance favoring pro-inflammatory factors can lead to persistent inflammation, a key contributor to gastric mucosal damage [45]. Pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-18, IL-1β, and TNF-α are crucial in the development of gastrointestinal diseases. Inflammasomes activate IL-18 and IL-1β, leading to the stimulation of T cells and natural killer (NK) cells to produce interferon-γ (IFN-γ), a cytokine involved in antiviral and immunomodulatory functions [46]. Studies have shown increased IL-18 and IL-1β expression in cisplatin-induced gastric mucosal inflammation, alleviated by Xiao-Ban-Xia decoction [47]. TNF-α, secreted by activated macrophages and T cells, triggers apoptosis, exacerbates inflammation, and regulates immune cell activity [48]. The pro-inflammatory cytokines are capable of initiating inflammatory cascades and magnifying each other's effects. For example, IL-18 stimulates TNF-α secretion, which enhances IL-1β activation [49]. In contrast, IL-4, IL-10, and IL-12 are anti-inflammatory cytokines that regulate excessive immune inflammation. IL-10 can suppress the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, repress macrophage activation, and prevent autoimmune diseases [50]. IL-4 promotes inflammation resolution through antibody production and Th2 immune response [51]. IL-4 drives Th2 cell differentiation, while IL-12 facilitates the growth of Th1 cells, maintaining immune balance [52]. Our results uncovered a marked elevation in the concentrations of IL-18, IL-1β, and TNF-α, accompanied by a notable decline in the levels of IL-4, IL-10, and IL-12 in the gastric tissues of the model group. Conversely, XSLJZT treatment significantly reduced the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines while boosting the levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines. These findings suggest that XSLJZT inhibits pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion (IL-18, IL-1β, and TNF-α) and promotes anti-inflammatory cytokine production (IL-4, IL-10, and IL-12), thereby mitigating IND-induced gastric mucosal injury in rats.

The NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated process of pyroptosis is associated with a range of gastrointestinal disorders, including inflammatory bowel disease, gastritis, and peptic ulcers. Research has shown elevated NLRP3 inflammasome expression in a mouse model of Crohn's disease, and IRGM can inhibit inflammasome assembly by interacting with NLRP3 and ASC to prevent oligomerization [53]. NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis has emerged as a potential treatment target for inflammatory conditions. Under normal conditions, NLRP3 inflammasome expression is low, and its activation can be suppressed by inhibiting glycolysis, modulating intracellular calcium signaling, and inducing autophagy [54]. However, upon stimulation by pathogens or cellular stress, NLRP3 inflammasome activation triggers the production of intracellular signaling molecules like NF-κB. The activation of IKKβ promotes the assembly and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, and it phosphorylates IκB, resulting in its degradation and the release of NF-κB [55]. NF-κB, an essential transcription factor, modulates the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokine genes and enhances NLRP3 inflammasome component expression, further promoting inflammasome assembly and activation [36]. Pyroptosis is a type of programmed cell death initiated by inflammasomes, which can contribute to inflammatory reactions, immune responses, pathogen defense, and the removal of mutant cells. Excessive pyroptosis is linked to autoimmune and inflammatory diseases [56,57]. Once Caspase-1 has been activated, it cleaves GSDMD and pro-IL-1β/pro-IL-18, producing their active forms IL-1β and IL-18. The oligomerization of GSDMD-N fragments leads to pore formation in the cell membrane, triggering pyroptosis in gastric mucosal cells. Mature IL-1β and IL-18 exit the gastric mucosal cells through GSDMD pores as a result of cell membrane damage [58]. Another study demonstrated that Shaoyao decoction alleviates inflammation and pyroptosis in ulcerative colitis by suppressing the MKP1/NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway [59]. Consistently, our findings showed that XSLJZT modulates inflammation and mitigates gastric mucosal damage by repressing IND-induced NF-κB signaling pathway activation, reducing inflammasome protein expression, and inhibiting pyroptosis. Collectively, these findings indicate that the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway is closely involved in the progression of gastric mucosal injury, and inhibition of this signaling axis may be an important mechanism by which XSLJZT exerts its gastroprotective effect.

Limitation

Limitations of this study: First, Western blot analysis was not performed in the present study; the modulatory effect of XSLJZT on core proteins of the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway lacks direct biochemical verification at the protein level, which will be systematically addressed in our follow-up studies. Second, all mechanistic findings are derived from in vivo experiments without corresponding in vitro cellular validation. Third, regarding the bioinformatics analysis, the GSE201565 dataset had a small sample size (n = 2 per group); therefore, the transcriptomic results should be interpreted with caution and require validation in studies with larger sample sizes. In addition, this analysis was performed solely to screen core disease pathways, and no virtual drug target prediction or experimental validation of compound-target interactions was conducted. Fourth, only qualitative chemical profiling of XSLJZT was performed, and the causal link between identified components and therapeutic effects remains unconfirmed. Fifth, safety assessment is limited to 14-day preliminary acute toxicity observation with gross indicators, lacking systematic toxicological data. All above limitations will be systematically addressed in follow-up studies.

Conclusions

In summary, our research reveals that XSLJZT exerts significant therapeutic effects on gastric mucosal lesions and inflammatory responses. Mechanistically, its protective action appears to be mediated through the inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and the modulation of downstream pyroptosis-related signaling cascades. These findings provide crucial experimental support for the therapeutic potential of the TCM prescription XSLJZT in treating inflammatory diseases associated with gastric mucosal injury.

Ferromagnetism vs. Antiferromagnetism in Narrow-Band Systems: Competition Between Quantum Geometry and Band Dispersion
Haoyu Hu, Oskar Vafek, Kristjan Haule, B. Andrei Bernevig

Abstract
Magnetism in narrow-band systems arises from the interplay between electronic correlations, quantum geometry, and band dispersion. In particular, both ferro and anti-ferro magnets are known to occur as ground states of (different) models featuring narrow bands. This poses the question of which is favored and under what conditions. In this work, we present a unified theoretical framework to investigate spin physics within narrow bands. By deriving an effective spin model, we show that the non-atomic wavefunction of the narrow bands generally favors ferromagnetic ordering, while band dispersion promotes antiferromagnetic correlations. We find that the competition between these effects gives rise to a tunable magnetic phase and rich spin phenomena. Our approach offers a systematic way to study the magnetic properties of narrow-band systems, integrating the roles of wave function, band structure, and correlation effects.

In realistic quantum materials, finite band dispersion and nontrivial quantum geometry typically coexist. Consequently, both conventional superexchange coupling and geometry-induced ferromagnetic correlation can jointly influence the magnetic ground state.
However, a unified theoretical framework that simultaneously accounts for band dispersion, quantum geometry, and electronic interactions has been lacking.
In this work, we bridge this gap by developing a comprehensive formalism that captures the interplay among these key ingredients shaping magnetism in narrow-band systems, and explains how and why certain narrow bands are ferromagnetic while others are anti-ferromagnetic.
We investigate systems with half-filled narrow bands near the Fermi energy, characterized by both finite dispersion and nontrivial quantum geometry.
We assume that the bandwidths of narrow bands are smaller than the interaction strength, placing the system in a strongly correlated regime. However, the bare hopping amplitude between atomic orbitals is not necessarily smaller than the interaction scale.
Under these conditions, we derive an effective spin-spin interaction model that captures the magnetic behavior arising from the interplay of interactions, band dispersion, and quantum geometry. Our analysis shows that the quantum geometry tends to favor ferromagnetic order, while band dispersion promotes antiferromagnetic correlations. The competition between these effects potentially gives rise to a rich magnetic phase diagram. Within our effective model, we also estimate the transition point between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic phases, providing a microscopic perspective on interaction-driven magnetism in narrow-band systems.

Finally, we also comment on the relation and difference between our work and the early study in Ref.
Anderson 1959
.
In Ref.
Anderson 1959
, hopping between Wannier orbitals was shown to generate antiferromagnetic superexchange, whereas the finite spread of atomic Wannier orbitals gave rise to a ferromagnetic coupling originating from Coulomb repulsion.
However, the early work
Anderson 1959
did not account for the role of wavefunction structure or the quantum geometry of Bloch bands. In contrast, our study starts from a tight-binding model with local Hubbard interactions and explicitly incorporates these effects.
We show that band dispersion promotes antiferromagnetism, whereas quantum geometry, which captures the non-atomic nature of the wavefunction and determines the minimal spread of real-space Wannier function of the Bloch band, favors ferromagnetism.
Thus, our study shares the same spirit as Ref.
Anderson 1959
but advances it by including the effects of the electronic wavefunction and providing a unified theory for magnetism in narrow-band systems.

Summary

In this work, we have investigated the magnetic properties of narrow-band systems by developing a unified theoretical framework that incorporates the effects of both band dispersion and quantum geometry. By expanding in powers of the non-local Green’s function, we derived an effective spin model that captures the essential magnetic correlations in the regime where electronic interactions dominate over the bandwidth of narrow bands.

Our analysis uncovers a fundamental competition: the quantum geometry of the bands tends to favor ferromagnetic ordering, whereas the band dispersion promotes antiferromagnetic correlations. This interplay determines the magnetic ground state and gives rise to a rich phase diagram. Importantly, we derive analytical expressions for the spin-spin interactions in terms of the Bloch wavefunctions and band dispersions, elucidating how both features contribute to the effective magnetic coupling. In summary, we have developed a general and physically transparent framework for magnetism in narrow-band systems, identifying quantum geometry and band dispersion as competing mechanisms governing magnetic correlations. This work provides a foundation for understanding magnetic behavior in quantum materials from more microscopic considerations.

a\neq a^{\prime}
could be sizeable unless specific symmetry enforces it to be zero.
However, we note that the separation between local and non-local Green’s functions is utilized for calculations.
Treating
G_{(\mathbf{R},a),(\mathbf{R},a^{\prime})}
(for
a\neq a^{\prime}
) as an off-diagonal component gives rise to an effective spin-spin coupling between spin operators located within the same unit cell
\mathbf{R}
but associated with different orbital indices (
a\neq a^{\prime}
).

We are interested in the case where the non-interacting electrons develop narrow bands near the Fermi energy and produce an enhanced DOS peak.
We consider the case with
n_{flat}
narrow bands near the Fermi energy. We separate the dispersion of the
n_{flat}
narrow bands into two parts

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}=\epsilon_{0}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n},\quad n=1,...,n_{flat}

(S44)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{n_{flat}}\sum_{n,\mathbf{k}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}

(S45)

represents the average energy of the narrow bands. We consider the case with

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}=0

(S46)

such that the narrow bands appear near Fermi energy. Finally, we assume the remote bands are at high energy whose contributions to the Green’s function have been ignored. The local and non-local Green’s function can then be written as

\displaystyle G_{loc,a}(i\omega_{n})\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},n=1,...,n_{flat}}\frac{|U_{\mathbf{k},an}|^{2}}{i\omega-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}}

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\omega)\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},n=1,...,n_{flat}}\frac{U_{\mathbf{k},a_{i}n}U_{\mathbf{k},a_{j}n}^{*}}{i\omega-\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{j}+\bm{r}_{a_{i}}-\bm{r}_{a_{j}})}

(S47)

S3
Effective action

We now derive the effective action by expanding the action in powers of
G^{\prime}
, which is similar to the gradient expansion.
The effective action can be written as (from Eq.
S35
)

\displaystyle S_{eff}=S_{\phi}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\left[(\tilde{G}V)^{n}\right]

(S48)

We let

\displaystyle\tilde{G}=\tilde{G}_{loc}+\tilde{G}^{\prime}

\displaystyle[\tilde{G}_{loc}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=G_{loc,a_{i}}(i\omega)\delta_{x_{i},x_{j}}\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\delta_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}e^{i\omega^{\prime}0^{+}}

\displaystyle[\tilde{G}^{\prime}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=G^{\prime}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\omega)e^{i\omega^{\prime}0^{+}}\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\delta_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}\quad\quad\text{ where }x_{i}\neq x_{j}

(S49)

Then by expanding in powers of non-local Green’s function
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
, we observe

\displaystyle S_{eff}

\displaystyle=S_{\phi}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\left[(\tilde{G}V)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle\approx S_{0}+S_{1}+S_{2}

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=S_{\phi}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\left[(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\text{Tr}\left[\tilde{G^{\prime}}V(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n}\text{Tr}\left[\tilde{G^{\prime}}V(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{k}\tilde{G^{\prime}}V(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n-2-k}\right]

(S50)

where the
S_{0},S_{1},S_{2}
correspond to the zeroth order, first order, and second order contributions (in powers of
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
), respectively.
As we discuss in
sections

S3.1
,
S3.2
and
S3.3
, the zeroth-order term generates a local on-site contribution, the first-order term vanishes, and the second-order term gives rise to two-body spin-spin interactions. Higher-order terms induce multi-spin interactions beyond the two-body level. For instance, the third-order term leads to three-body interactions of the form
n^{\mu}_{x_{1}}n^{\nu}_{x_{2}}n^{\eta}_{x_{3}}
.
Since such higher-order terms are generally less relevant in determining the magnetic ordering of the system, we truncate the expansion at second order.

S3.1
0
-th order term

In this section, we evaluate the 0-th order contribution (Eq.
S50
)

\displaystyle S_{0}=

\displaystyle\beta\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\left[(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n}\right]

(S51)

We note that

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\text{Tr}\left[(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n}\right]=\beta\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}-\text{Tr}[\log(1+\tilde{G}_{loc}V)]

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}-\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc})]-\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]

(S52)

We can again use the Gaussian integrals

\displaystyle\int D[\eta,\eta^{\dagger}]e^{\sum_{ij}\eta_{i}^{\dagger}M_{ij}\eta_{j}}=e^{\text{Tr}[\log(M)]}

(S53)

We then find

\displaystyle e^{\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\eta^{\dagger}_{x_{i}},\eta_{x_{i}}]e^{+\int_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{\sigma}\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)G^{-1}_{loc,a_{i}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}(\tau^{\prime})+\int_{\tau}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}}{2}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)\eta_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})}

(S54)

We note that the local DOS of the non-interacting system develops a peak at the Fermi energy due to the existence of the narrow bands. Approximately, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{a}(\epsilon)\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},n=1,...,n_{flat}}|U_{\mathbf{k},an}|^{2}\delta(\epsilon-\delta_{\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}})\approx A_{a}\delta(\epsilon)

(S55)

where the prefactor is defined as

\displaystyle A_{a}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n=1,...,n_{flat}}|U_{\mathbf{k},an}|^{2}

(S56)

Then the local Green’s function can be approximately written as

\displaystyle G_{loc,a}(i\omega)\approx\frac{A_{a}}{i\omega}

(S57)

Using the
eqs.

S49
,
S57
and
S54
, we find

\displaystyle e^{\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau),\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}(\tau)]\exp\left\{-\int_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}\eta_{x_{i},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)A_{a_{i}}^{-1}\left[\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\partial_{\tau}-g_{a_{i}}\bm{n}_{x_{i}}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\right]\eta_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau)\right\}

(S58)

where we have introduced

\displaystyle g_{a_{i}}=\phi_{0,a_{i}}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}/2.

(S59)

We now evaluate this term explicitly. We first introduce the following parametrization of the
n
fields

\displaystyle n_{x_{i}}^{\mu}(\tau)=\begin{bmatrix}\sin(\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau))\cos(\chi_{x_{i}}(\tau))&\sin(\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau))\sin(\chi_{x_{i}}(\tau))&\cos(\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau))\end{bmatrix}

(S60)

We introduce the fluctuation frame and define new fermionic fields
\psi
, which are standard Grassmann variables

\displaystyle R_{x_{i}}(\tau)=\begin{bmatrix}-e^{-i\chi_{x_{i}}(\tau)}\sin(\frac{\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau)}{2})&e^{-i\chi_{x_{i}}(\tau)}\cos(\frac{\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau)}{2})\\
\cos(\frac{\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau)}{2})&\sin(\frac{\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau)}{2})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\eta_{{x_{i}},\sigma}(\tau)=\sqrt{A_{a_{i}}}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}[R_{x_{i}}(\tau)]_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau)

(S61)

The action now behaves as

\displaystyle e^{\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\psi,\psi^{\dagger}]\exp\Bigg\{-\int d\tau\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\bigg\{\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{\tau}+\bigg[R_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{x_{i}}(\tau)\bigg]_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}+\sigma^{\prime}g_{a_{i}}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\}\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\Bigg\}

(S62)

We can evaluate the integral in the power series of
R_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{x_{i}}(\tau)
(which is equivalent to a gradient expansion in powers of
\partial_{\tau}
). We only keep the zeroth order and first order term

\displaystyle e^{\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\psi,\psi^{\dagger}]\exp\Bigg\{-\int d\tau\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\bigg\{\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{\tau}+\bigg[R_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{x_{i}}(\tau)\bigg]_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}+\sigma^{\prime}g_{a_{i}}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int D[\psi,\psi^{\dagger}]\bigg\{1-\int_{\tau}d\tau\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[R_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{x_{i}}(\tau)\bigg]_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg\{-\int d\tau\sum_{\sigma}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)\bigg\{\partial_{\tau}+\sigma g_{a_{i}}\bigg\}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma}(\tau)\Bigg\}

(S63)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle Z_{0,loc}-Z_{0,loc}\int_{\tau}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[R_{{x_{i}}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{{x_{i}}}(\tau)\bigg]_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\langle\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\bigg\rangle_{0,loc}

(S64)

where the partition function and the expectation value (for a given operator
O
) are defined as

\displaystyle Z_{0,loc}=

\displaystyle\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\psi,\psi^{\dagger}]\exp\Bigg\{-\int d\tau\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\bigg\{\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{\tau}+\sigma^{\prime}g_{a_{i}}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\}\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\langle O\rangle_{0,loc}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{0,loc}}\prod_{x_{i}}\int D[\psi,\psi^{\dagger}]O\exp\Bigg\{-\int d\tau\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\bigg\{\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{\tau}+\sigma^{\prime}g_{a_{i}}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\}\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\Bigg\}

(S65)

Now
Z_{0,loc,\mathbf{R}}
is just the partition function of a non-interacting system with energy

\displaystyle E_{loc,a_{i},\pm}=\pm g_{a_{i}}

(S66)

The partition function of this non-interacting system is

\displaystyle Z_{0,loc}=\prod_{x_{i}}\bigg[\bigg(1+e^{-\beta E_{loc,a_{i},+}}\bigg)\bigg(1+e^{\beta E_{loc,a_{i},-}}\bigg)\bigg]

(S67)

Moreover, the expectation values are

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\rangle_{0,loc}=\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}n_{F}(E_{loc,a_{i},\sigma^{\prime}})

(S68)

where the Fermi-Dirac function is
n_{F}(x)=1/(1+e^{\beta\epsilon})
. At the low-temperature limit, we find

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\psi_{x_{i},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\rangle_{0,loc}=\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1-\sigma^{\prime}}{2}

(S69)

Then the first-order contribution in Eq.
S65
gives

\displaystyle-Z_{0,loc}\int_{\tau}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{x_{i}}\bigg[R_{{x_{i}}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}R_{{x_{i}}}(\tau)\bigg]_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\bigg\langle\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau)\psi_{{x_{i}},\sigma^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)\bigg\rangle_{0,loc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iZ_{0,loc}\int_{\tau}\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{-\cos(\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau))-1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{x_{i}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iZ_{0,loc}\sum_{x_{i}}A[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}]

(S70)

where we define

\displaystyle A[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}]=-\int_{\tau}\frac{\cos(\theta_{x_{i}}(\tau))+1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\phi_{x_{i}}(\tau)

(S71)

which is just the conventional Berry phase term of a spin system.
Then the effective action now reads (from Eq.
S64
, Eq.
S67
, and Eq.
S70
)

\displaystyle e^{\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]}\approx e^{-\left[-\log\bigg(Z_{0,loc}(1-iA[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}])\bigg)\right]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}_{loc}^{-1}+V)]\approx-\log Z_{0,loc}+iA[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}]

(S72)

Combining Eq.
S52
and Eq.
S72
, we find

\displaystyle S_{0}=\sum_{x_{i}}\left[\beta\sum_{x_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}-\log Z_{0,loc}+iA[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}]\right]-\text{Tr}[\log\tilde{G}_{loc}]

(S73)

where
\text{Tr}[\log\tilde{G}_{loc}]
is just a constant.

We note that
S_{0}
represents the zeroth-order (in
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
) term in the action expansion (Eq. (
S50
)). It consists of two contributions: the action of the
\phi_{0,a}
field, denoted by
S_{\phi_{0}}
, and the Berry phase term,
iA[\bm{n}_{x_{i}}]
.
More explicitly, we have

\displaystyle S_{\phi_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\log(Z_{0,loc})+S_{\phi}=\sum_{x_{i}}\bigg\{\beta\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}-\log\bigg[\bigg(1+e^{-\beta(\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}/2)}\bigg)\bigg(1+e^{\beta(\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}/2)}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{i}}\bigg\{\beta\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{4}\phi_{0,a_{i}}^{2}-2\log\bigg[2\cosh\left(\frac{1}{4}\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}\right)\bigg]\bigg\}

(S74)

The value of
\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}
and be determined by the saddle-point equation

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S_{\phi_{0}}}{\delta\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}}=0\Rightarrow\beta\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}-\frac{1}{2}\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\tanh(\frac{\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}}{4})=0

(S75)

The saddle point equation has a non-zero solution only below
T_{c}
. We can determine
T_{c}
by calculating the mass term of
\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}

\displaystyle m_{a_{i}}=\frac{\delta^{2}S_{\phi_{0}}}{\delta\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}^{2}}\bigg|_{\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}=0}=\frac{\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}}{2}-\frac{\beta^{2}A_{a_{i}}^{2}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}^{2}}{8}

(S76)

The
T_{c,{a_{i}}}
corresponds to the temperature below which
m_{a_{i}}<0
. We find

\displaystyle T_{c,{a_{i}}}=\frac{A_{a_{i}}^{2}}{4}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}

(S77)

T_{c,a_{i}}
is then the temperature below which the sublattice
a_{i}
starts to develop local moment (
\phi_{0,a}\neq 0
).

We are mostly interested in the low-temperature limit. At the low-temperature limit, we can directly solve Eq.
S75
which gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\tanh(\frac{\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}}{4})=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\phi_{0,a_{i}}=A_{a_{i}}\tanh(\frac{\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}}{4})

(S78)

At low-temperature limit with
\beta\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\tanh(\frac{\beta\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}A_{a_{i}}\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}}{4})\rightarrow 1
, and then

\displaystyle\phi_{0,{a_{i}}}=A_{a_{i}}

(S79)

In other words, the size of the local moment is
A_{a_{i}}
, which is also dimensionless.

The contribution to the effective action of the
\bm{n}_{x}
fields is just a simple Berry phase term which takes the form of

\displaystyle S_{B}=i\sum_{x}A[n_{x}]

(S80)

Finally, we also comment on the phase transition suggested by the current calculation, which separates a high-temperature regime without local moment formation and a low-temperature phase with the development of local moment. Such a sharp transition could be an artifact of the current expansion. However,
T_{c,a_{i}}
can still be understood as the energy scale where the local moment behaviors start to appear.

S3.2
First-order term

We now discuss the first-order term
S_{1}
in Eq.
S50
.

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\text{Tr}\left[\tilde{G^{\prime}}V(\tilde{G}_{loc}V)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\omega_{1},...,i\omega_{n}=i\omega_{1}}\sum_{\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{1}}(-1)^{n}

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left[[\tilde{G^{\prime}}]_{(x,\sigma_{1},i\omega_{1}),(x,\sigma_{1},i\omega_{1})}\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}[V]_{(x,\sigma_{m},i\omega_{m}),(x,\sigma_{m+1},i\omega_{m+1})}[\tilde{G}_{loc}]_{(x,\sigma_{m+1},i\omega_{m+1}),(x,\sigma_{m+1},i\omega_{m+1})}\right]

(S81)

where we have use the fact that
V
and
\tilde{G}_{loc}
are diagonal with respect to the position index
x
(see Eq.
S33
and Eq.
S49
). In addition, since
\tilde{G^{\prime}}
represents the non-local component of the Green’s function,
[\tilde{G^{\prime}}]_{(x,\sigma_{1},i\omega_{1}),(x,\sigma_{1},i\omega_{1})}=0
. Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle S_{1}=0

(S82)

S3.3
Second-order term

We now discuss the second-order term
S_{2}
in Eq.
S50
. By combining Eq.
S33
, Eq.
S49
and Eq.
S50
, we observe

\displaystyle S_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}\sum_{i\omega,i\omega^{\prime},i\tilde{\omega},i\tilde{\omega^{\prime}},i\omega^{\prime}_{1},..,i\omega^{\prime}_{k+1},i\omega_{1},...,i\omega_{n-1-k}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma^{\prime}},\sigma_{1},...,i\sigma_{k+1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1},...,i\sigma^{\prime}_{n-1-k}}

\displaystyle\delta_{i\omega^{\prime},i\omega_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{i\omega^{\prime}_{k+1},i\tilde{\omega}}\delta_{i\tilde{\omega}^{\prime},i\omega_{1}}\delta_{i\omega_{n-1-k},i\omega}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma_{k+1}^{\prime},\tilde{\sigma}}\delta_{\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}\delta_{\sigma_{n-1-k},\sigma}e^{i\omega 0^{+}}

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\omega)\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\omega-i\omega^{\prime})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{k}[G_{loc,a_{j}}(i\omega^{\prime}_{m})]\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\omega^{\prime}_{m}-i\omega^{\prime}_{m+1})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{m},\sigma^{\prime}_{m+1}}}{2\beta}\right)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{x_{j},x_{i}}(i\tilde{\omega})\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\tilde{\omega}-i\tilde{\omega^{\prime}})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma^{\prime}}}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{n-2-k}[G_{loc,a_{i}}(i\omega_{s})]\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\omega_{s}-i\omega_{s+1})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{s+1}}}{2\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}\sum_{i\omega,i\Omega,i\Omega^{\prime},i\Omega_{1}^{\prime},...,i\Omega_{k}^{\prime},i\Omega_{1},..,i\Omega_{n-2-k}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma^{\prime}},\sigma_{1},...,i\sigma_{k+1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1},...,i\sigma^{\prime}_{n-1-k}}

\displaystyle\delta_{i\Omega+i\Omega^{\prime}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}i\Omega^{\prime}_{m}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-2-k}i\Omega_{s},0}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\sigma_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma_{k+1}^{\prime},\tilde{\sigma}}\delta_{\tilde{\sigma}^{\prime},\sigma_{1}}\delta_{\sigma_{n-1-k},\sigma}e^{i\omega 0^{+}}

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\omega)\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\Omega)\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{k}[G_{loc,a_{j}}(i\omega-i\Omega-\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}i\Omega^{\prime}_{t})]\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\Omega^{\prime}_{m})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{m},\sigma^{\prime}_{m+1}}}{2\beta}\right)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{x_{j},x_{i}}(i\omega-i\Omega-\sum_{m=1}^{k}i\Omega^{\prime}_{m})\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\Omega^{\prime})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma^{\prime}}}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{n-2-k}[G_{loc,a_{i}}(i\omega-i\Omega-\sum_{m=1}^{k}i\Omega^{\prime}_{m}-i\Omega^{\prime}-\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}i\Omega_{t})]\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\Omega_{s})\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{s+1}}}{2\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}F_{x_{i},x-j}(i\Omega,i\Omega_{1}^{\prime},..,i\Omega_{k}^{\prime},i\Omega^{\prime},i\Omega_{1},...,i\Omega_{n-2-k})\delta_{i\Omega+i\Omega^{\prime}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}i\Omega^{\prime}_{m}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-2-k}i\Omega_{s},0}

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left[\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\Omega)\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{k}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{j}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{j}}(i\Omega^{\prime}_{m})\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{2\beta}\right)\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\Omega)\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{2\beta}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{n-2-k}\frac{\mathcal{U}_{a_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}(i\Omega_{s})\cdot\bm{\sigma}}{2\beta}\right)\right]

(S83)

where the interaction vertex is defined as

\displaystyle F_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\Omega,i\Omega_{1}^{\prime},..,i\Omega_{k}^{\prime},i\Omega^{\prime},i\Omega_{1},...,i\Omega_{n-2-k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\omega}G^{\prime}_{x_{i},x_{j}}(i\omega)\left(\prod_{m=1}^{k}[G_{loc,a_{j}}(i\omega-i\Omega-\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}i\Omega^{\prime}_{t})]\right)G^{\prime}_{x_{j},x_{i}}(i\omega-i\Omega-\sum_{m=1}^{k}i\Omega^{\prime}_{m})

It is also useful to investigate the spin-spin coupling in the momentum space, which is defined as

\displaystyle J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{i},\mathbf{R}_{j}}J_{(\mathbf{R}_{i},a),(\mathbf{R}_{j},b)}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{j}+\mathbf{r}_{a}-\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{b})}

\displaystyle U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a1}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},b1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}

(S102)

S4
Effective spin model at the single-orbital atomic limit

We discuss our effective spin theory at the single-orbital limit. We consider a single atomic orbital at a square lattice with dispersions

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=-2t(\cos(k_{x})+\cos(k_{y}))

(S103)

We consider the limit of
|t|\ll\mathcal{U}
where we have a single narrow band near the Fermi energy. We have

\displaystyle U_{\mathbf{k},11}=1,\quad A=1,\quad B=0,\quad C=4t^{2},\quad\quad\phi_{0}=1

(S104)

From Eq.
S100
, we find

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j}}=0,\quad B_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(-2t)(\cos(k_{x})+\cos(k_{y}))e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{j})}=-t\sum_{\bm{e}\in\{(\pm 1,0),(0,\pm 1)\}}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j},\bm{e}}

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j}}=0

(S105)

for
\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\mathbf{x}_{j}
.
Then the effective spin-spin interactions are

\displaystyle J_{\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j}}=\frac{t^{2}}{2\mathcal{U}}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\{(\pm 1,0),(0,\pm 1)\}}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j},\bm{e}}

(S106)

We thus conclude that our approach successfully recovers the conventional antiferromagnetic superexchange coupling.

S5
Effective spin model in the flat-band limit

We study the flat-band limit, where the system develops an isolated flat band at the Fermi energy with

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},n}=0,\quad B=C=0

(S107)

With
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{x_{i},x_{j}}=0,\quad C_{x_{i},x_{j}}=0

(S108)

and

\displaystyle J_{x_{i},x_{j}}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}A_{x_{i},x_{j}}A_{x_{j},x_{i}}

(S109)

We further assume the flat-band is non-atomic such that the wavefunction or quantum geometry of the band generates a non-zero
A_{x_{i},x_{j}}
.
At this limit, since (from Eq.
S100
)

\displaystyle A_{x_{i},x_{j}}=A_{x_{j},x_{i}}^{*},

(S110)

the spin-spin interaction is purely ferromagnetic

\displaystyle J_{x_{i},x_{j}}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}A_{x_{i},x_{j}}A_{x_{j},x_{i}}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}|A_{x_{i},x_{j}}|^{2}\leq 0

(S111)

Therefore, the ground state is ferromagnetic, which is consistent with the exact solution for the flat band system with finite quantum geometry.

We also note that, in the atomic limit with a single orbital, we have

U_{\mathbf{k},(a_{i}=1,n=1)}=1,

(S112)

and consequently,

A_{x_{i},x_{j}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k},(a_{i}=1,n=1)}U_{\mathbf{k},(a_{j}=1,n=1)}^{*}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{j})}=\delta_{\mathbf{R}_{i},\mathbf{R}_{j}}.

(S113)

Since the spin-spin interaction is induced by
A_{x_{i},x_{j}}
with
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
, we find

J_{x_{i},x_{j}}=0,\quad\text{for }x_{i}\neq x_{j}.

(S114)

Therefore, for atomic orbitals that form an exactly flat band, the effective spin-spin coupling vanishes.

S6
FM-AFM transition

We study the energy competition between the FM and AFM states. We focus on the case where a single-flat band appears near the Fermi-energy, leading to the effective spin-spin coupling given in
section

S3.4
. We observe that in the classical limit (where we have ignored the
\tau
-dependency of
\b{,}{n}_{x_{i}}
fields), the energy of the system and then be effectively written as

\displaystyle E=\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}J_{x_{i},x_{j}}\bm{n}_{x_{i}}\cdot\bm{n}_{x_{j}}

(S115)

with
\bm{n}_{x_{i}}
the unit vector characterizing the spin orientation.
The configuration
\bm{n}_{x_{i}}
that minimizes the energy
E
will be the ground state energy in the classical limit. It is then also useful to introduce the momentum space formula

\displaystyle\bm{n}_{x_{i}=(\mathbf{R}_{i},a_{i})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}\bm{n}_{\mathbf{q},a_{i}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}+\mathbf{r}_{a_{i}})}

(S116)

We have

\displaystyle E=\sum_{\mathbf{q},ab}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bm{n}_{-\mathbf{q},a}\cdot\bm{n}_{\mathbf{q},b}

(S117)

where the momentum-space coupling is defined as

\displaystyle J_{a_{i}b_{j}}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(\mathbf{R}_{i},a_{i}),(\mathbf{R}_{j},a_{j})}J_{(\mathbf{R}_{i},a_{i}),(\mathbf{R}_{j},a_{j})}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{j}+\mathbf{r}_{a_{j}}-\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{a_{i}})}

(S118)

To study the magnetic order of the system, it is useful to study the eigenvalue of the matrix
J_{a_{i},b_{j}}(\mathbf{q})
. The momentum where the smallest eigenvalue is realized corresponds to the magnetic order that the interaction favors.

We first investigate the flat-band limit, where
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}=0
. We have (see
eq.

S100
)

\displaystyle J_{x_{i}x_{j}}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}|A_{x_{i},x_{j}}A_{x_{j},x_{i}}|^{2}\leq 0

(S119)

We observe that
J_{x_{i},x_{j}}
is always ferromagnetic (
\mathbf{q}=0
order).
We then investigate the spin-spin coupling matrix at
\mathbf{q}=0
. We find

\displaystyle J_{ab}(\mathbf{q}=0)=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}|U_{\mathbf{k},b1}|^{2}

(S120)

Since all the spin-spin couplings are ferromagnetic, the eigenvector of
J_{ab}(\mathbf{q}=0)
with the lowest eigenvalue is
[v]_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{sub}}}
with
n_{sub}
the number of sublattices.
The corresponding eigenvalue is

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q}=0,lowest}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8n_{sub}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},ab}|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}|U_{\mathbf{k},b1}|^{2}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8n_{sub}}=-\frac{\mathcal{U}A}{8}

(S121)

We now discuss the effect of finite dispersion.
When the band dispersion is finite (
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\neq 0
), the spin-spin couplings are no longer purely ferromagnetic. The emergence of antiferromagnetic coupling implies that the lowest eigenvalue of
J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})
may occur at a finite momentum
\mathbf{q}
, indicating a tendency toward antiferromagnetic ordering.
To investigate this potential instability, it is useful to expand
E_{\mathbf{q},\text{lowest}}
in powers of
\mathbf{q}
around
\mathbf{q}=0
, in the presence of finite dispersion.

To perform such expansion, we first consider the effect of finite dispersion at
\mathbf{q}=0
.
From
section

S3.4
, we have

\displaystyle J_{ab}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\mathcal{U}\bigg[\frac{-1}{8}+\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg]|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}|U_{\mathbf{k},b1}|^{2}

(S122)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{3(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})^{2}}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}^{2}}-\frac{3C-2B^{2}/A}{2A^{5}\mathcal{U}^{2}}-\frac{3B\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{A^{5}\mathcal{U}^{2}}

(S123)

Since
C\sim\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}^{2},B\sim\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
(
eq.

S100
), we note that
\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}\sim\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}/\mathcal{U}^{2}
.

As long as we remain in the narrow-band limit where
|\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}|/\mathcal{U}\ll 1
, we expect the wavefunction associated with the lowest eigenvalue of
J_{ab}(\mathbf{q}=0)
remain

\displaystyle[v]_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{sub}}}

(S124)

We now examine

at finite
\mathbf{q}
.
At small
\mathbf{q}
, we project to the eigenstates at
\mathbf{q}=0
(
eq.

S124

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q},lowest}\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}[v^{*}]_{a}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})[v]_{b}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q}=0,lowest}+\sum_{\mu}\sum_{a,b}\frac{1}{n_{sub}}q^{\mu}\partial_{q^{\mu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\sum_{a,b}\frac{1}{n_{sub}}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}\partial_{q^{\mu}}\partial_{q^{\nu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}

We now show that the first-order term vanishes

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}\partial_{q^{\mu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},ab}\bigg[-\frac{\mathcal{U}}{8}+\mathcal{U}\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg]\bigg[\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}+U_{\mathbf{k},a1}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},ab}\bigg[+\frac{3(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})\partial_{k^{\mu}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}-3B\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}A}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}}\bigg]|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}|U_{\mathbf{k},b1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},ab}\bigg[+\frac{3(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}-3B\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}A}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}}\bigg]|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}|U_{\mathbf{k},b1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[\frac{3(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}-3B\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}A}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{6(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})\partial_{k^{\mu}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}}

We notice that for an isolated flat band, the dispersion
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}
is an analytical function of
\mathbf{k}
with the following periodicity

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\sum_{\mu}n^{\mu}\bm{b}_{\mu},1}=\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1},\quad n^{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}

(S127)

where
\{\bm{b}_{\mu}\}_{\mu}
are the reciprocal lattice vectors. Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k},1})^{n}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1})^{n},\quad n\in\{1,2\}

(S128)

and then

\displaystyle 0=\partial_{k^{\mu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k},1})^{n}]|_{\mathbf{k}=0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}2\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}\partial_{k^{\prime\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1},\quad\text{and},\quad 0=\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\partial_{k^{\prime\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}

(S129)

Therefore, combining
sections

S6
and
S6
, we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}\partial_{q^{\mu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})=0

(S130)

We next consider the second-order contributions of
section

S6
.
We find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}\partial_{q^{\mu}}\partial_{q^{\nu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}(-\sum_{a}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}+\sum_{a,b}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*})(-\frac{1}{8}+\alpha_{\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2A^{4}\mathcal{U}N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\frac{\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{2}

where we use

\displaystyle 0=[\partial_{k^{\mu}}\partial_{k^{\nu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k},1}^{2}]|_{k^{\mu}=0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}2\partial_{k^{\prime\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}\partial_{k^{\prime\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}+2\partial_{k^{\prime\mu}}\partial_{k^{\prime\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},1}

(S132)

We then find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}\partial_{q^{\mu}}\partial_{q^{\nu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}=

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathcal{U}}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}(-\sum_{a}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}+\sum_{a,b}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},b1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*})(-\frac{1}{8}+\alpha_{\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4A^{4}\mathcal{U}N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}

(S133)

We introduce

\displaystyle Q_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{a,b}\partial_{k^{\mu}}U_{\mathbf{k},a}^{*}\bigg(\delta_{a,b}-U_{\mathbf{k},a}U_{\mathbf{k},b}^{*}\bigg)\partial_{k^{\nu}}U_{\mathbf{k},b},\quad\quad Q=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}Q_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle m_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})=\partial_{k^{\mu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{k^{\nu}}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1},\quad\quad M_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}m_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})

(S134)

and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}\partial_{q^{\mu}}\partial_{q^{\nu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}=\mathcal{U}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg\{Q_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})\bigg(\frac{1}{8}-\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg)-\frac{m_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})}{4A^{4}\mathcal{U}^{2}}\bigg\}

In practice, we assume that
Q_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})
and

are of the same order, so that the effects of quantum geometry and band dispersion can compete.
Consequently, the term
Q_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}
with
\alpha_{\mathbf{k}}\sim|\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}/\mathcal{U}^{2}
is expected to be much smaller.
By dropping

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ab}\partial_{q^{\mu}}\partial_{q^{\nu}}J_{ab}(\mathbf{q})\bigg|_{\mathbf{q}=0}\approx\mathcal{U}\bigg\{\frac{Q_{\mu\nu}}{8}-\frac{M_{\mu\nu}}{4A^{4}\mathcal{U}^{2}}\bigg\}

(S136)

Then we finally have (from

S6

S136

\displaystyle E_{lowest,\mathbf{q}}\approx

\displaystyle E_{lowest,\mathbf{q}=0}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n_{sub}}\mathcal{U}\sum_{\mu\nu}\bigg\{\frac{Q_{\mu\nu}}{8}-\frac{M_{\mu\nu}}{4A^{4}\mathcal{U}^{2}}\bigg\}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}

(S137)

Therefore,
E_{lowest,\mathbf{q}}
no longer reach its minimum at
\mathbf{q}=0
when the determinant of the matrix

\displaystyle H_{\mu\nu}=A\mathcal{U}\bigg[\frac{Q_{\mu\nu}}{8}-\frac{M_{\mu\nu}}{4A^{4}\mathcal{U}^{2}}\bigg]

(S138)

becomes negative. This then indicates the instability of the FM phase.

S7
Spin stiffness

In this section, we also provide detailed calculations of the spin stiffness of the ferromagnetic state to check the stability of the flat-band ferromagnetism
Peotta and Törmä 2015
;
Julku et al. 2016
;
Törmä et al. 2022
;
Liang et al. 2017
;
Huhtinen et al. 2022
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2022b
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2022c
;
Yu et al. 2025
.
For convenience, we consider a simple situation with only one flat band appearing near the Fermi energy, and all the sublattices are equivalent/symmetry-related.
In this section, we provide the calculation of stiffness from the original action given in
section

S1
.
We also note that the spin model derived in
eq.

S99
captures only two-body interaction terms, neglecting higher-order spin interactions such as
n^{\mu}{x_{i}}n^{\nu}{x_{j}}n^{\gamma}_{x_{m}}
. As a result, it cannot fully reproduce the exact spin stiffness of the ferromagnetic state.
To recover the exact result, one can perform the calculation using the exact action presented in
section

S1
.

The exact action (
section

S1
) takes the form of

\displaystyle S=S_{\phi}-\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}^{-1}+V)]

(S139)

At low-temperature limits, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{x_{i}}=\phi_{0}\bm{n}_{x_{i}},\quad\phi_{0}=A

(S140)

Since all the sublattices are equivalent, we also have

\displaystyle A=A_{a}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}|U_{\mathbf{k},a1}|^{2}=\frac{1}{n_{sub}}

(S141)

We use the Holstein-Primakoff boson to calculate the stiffness of the ferromagnetic state

\displaystyle{n}^{z}_{x_{i}}=1-2a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}a_{x_{i}},\quad n^{x}_{x_{i}}=(a_{x_{i}}+a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}),\quad n^{y}_{x_{i}}=\frac{1}{i}(a_{x_{i}}-a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger})

(S142)

Then we could expand the action
S
to a quadratic action of the
a,a^{\dagger}
fields which describe the spin fluctuations of the system.
We first separate interaction vertex
V
(
eq.

S33
) into three parts

\displaystyle V=v_{0}+\delta v_{1}+\delta v_{2}

\displaystyle[v_{0}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=\delta_{x_{i},x_{j}}\frac{\mathcal{U}\phi_{0}}{2\beta}\sigma^{z}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}e^{i\omega^{\prime}0^{+}}

\displaystyle[\delta v_{1}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=\delta_{x_{i},x_{j}}\frac{\mathcal{U}{A}}{\beta}e^{i\omega^{\prime}0^{+}}\begin{bmatrix}0&a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(-i\omega+i\omega^{\prime})\\
a_{x_{i}}(i\omega-i\omega^{\prime})&0\end{bmatrix}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle[\delta v_{2}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=-\delta_{x_{i},x_{j}}\sigma\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{U}{A}}{\beta}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega^{\prime\prime}}a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\omega^{\prime\prime})a_{x_{i}}(i\omega-i\omega^{\prime}+i\omega^{\prime\prime})

(S143)

where we combine
eqs.

S33
and
S142
and expand
V
in powers of
a,a^{\dagger}
fields. In addition, the operator in Matsubara frequency is defined as

\displaystyle a_{x_{i}}(i\omega)=\int_{\tau}a_{x_{i}}(\tau)e^{i\omega\tau}

(S144)

Since
S_{\phi}
does not depend on
\bm{n}_{x_{i}}
, only the trace part in
eq.

S139
contributes to the spin stiffness and can be written as

\displaystyle S_{stiff}=-\text{Tr}[\log(\tilde{G}^{-1}+v_{0}+\delta v_{1}+\delta v_{2})]

(S145)

We expand the above action to quadratic order in
a,a^{\dagger}
and find

\displaystyle S_{stiff}\approx S_{const}-\text{Tr}[(\tilde{G}^{-1}+v_{0})^{-1}\delta v_{2}]+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\tilde{G}^{-1}+v_{0})^{-1}\delta v_{1}(\tilde{G}^{-1}+v_{0})^{-1}\delta v_{1}]

(S146)

where
S_{const}
denotes the term that does not depend on
a,a^{\dagger}
fields.
We evaluate the remaining two terms. For later convenience, we also introduce

\displaystyle G_{v}=[\tilde{G}^{-1}+v_{0}]^{-1}\,.

(S147)

We find

\displaystyle[G_{v}]_{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(\mathbf{x}_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}=\delta_{i\sigma,i\omega^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[i\omega\mathbb{I}-t_{\mathbf{k}}+\frac{\mathcal{U}{A}}{2}\sigma\mathbb{I}\bigg]^{-1}_{a_{i},a_{j}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}

(S148)

We focus on the contributions from the flat band. By projecting to the flat band, we have

\displaystyle[G_{v}]_{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(\mathbf{x}_{j},\sigma^{\prime},i\omega^{\prime})}\approx\delta_{i\omega,i\omega^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}e^{i\omega^{\prime}0^{+}}\frac{1}{{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{i\omega-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}+\sigma\frac{\mathcal{U}{A}}{2}}U_{\mathbf{k},a_{i}1}U_{\mathbf{k},a_{j}1}^{*}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}

(S149)

We now evaluate
S_{stiff}
.
The second term in the
S_{stiff}
(
eq.

S146
) reads

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}[G_{v}\delta v_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{i},i\omega,\sigma}\frac{\mathcal{U}\phi_{0}}{\beta}[G_{v}]_{(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega),(x_{i},\sigma,i\omega)}\frac{\sigma}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega^{\prime}}a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\omega^{\prime})a_{x_{i}}(i\omega^{\prime})e^{i\omega 0^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{x_{i},i\omega,\sigma}\mathcal{U}{A}a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\omega)a_{x_{i}}(i\omega)\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}|U_{\mathbf{k},a_{i}1}|^{2}\frac{\sigma+1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{x_{i},i\omega}\mathcal{U}{A}^{2}a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\omega)a_{x_{i}}(i\omega)

(S150)

The third term in the
S_{stiff}
(
eq.

S146
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[G_{v}\delta v_{1}G_{v}\delta v_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\omega_{1},i\omega_{2},x_{i},x_{j},\sigma_{1},i\sigma_{2}}\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{\beta^{2}N^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}}\frac{U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{i}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{j}1}^{*}}{i\omega_{1}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}+\sigma_{1}\mathcal{U}{A}/2}\frac{U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{j}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{i}1}^{*}}{i\omega_{2}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}+\sigma_{2}\mathcal{U}{A}/2}e^{i(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\mathbf{k}_{2})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}&a_{x_{j}}^{\dagger}(-i\omega_{1}+i\omega_{2})\\
a_{x_{j}}(i\omega_{1}-i\omega_{2})\end{bmatrix}_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}\begin{bmatrix}&a_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}(-i\omega_{2}+i\omega_{1})\\
a_{x_{i}}(i\omega_{2}-i\omega_{1})\end{bmatrix}_{\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{2\beta N^{2}}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}}U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{i}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{j}1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{j}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{i}1}^{*}e^{i(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\mathbf{k}_{2})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{1}{i\Omega-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}-\mathcal{U}{A}}a_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)+\frac{1}{i\Omega-\delta_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}-\mathcal{U}{A}}a_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}(i\Omega)\bigg]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{2\beta N^{2}}\sum_{x_{i},x_{j}}\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}}U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{i}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{1},a_{j}1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{j}1}U_{\mathbf{k}_{2},a_{i}1}^{*}e^{i(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\mathbf{k}_{2})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}

\displaystyle\bigg[-\frac{1+\frac{i\Omega}{\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}}}{\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}}a_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)-\frac{1+\frac{i\Omega}{\mathcal{U}{A}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}}}{\mathcal{U}{A}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{1},1}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}_{2},1}}a_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}(i\Omega)\bigg]

(S151)

where in the final line we have expanded in powers of frequency
\Omega
.
Since we are interested in the low-energy behavior, corresponding to the low-frequency regime, we retain terms up to first order in
i\Omega
. Higher-order terms, while in principle capable of further renormalizing the dispersion of the
a
fields, are expected to be less relevant in the low-energy limit.

It is useful to work in the momentum space. We perform the following Fourier transformation with

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}(i\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}a_{\mathbf{k},a_{i}}(i\omega)e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{i}}

(S152)

Then the low-energy effective theory of
a,a^{\dagger}
reads

\displaystyle S_{stiff}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\Omega,\mathbf{q},a,a^{\prime}}\bigg[\mathcal{U}{A}^{2}\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a1}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a^{\prime}1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},a^{\prime}1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}\bigg(1+\frac{i\Omega}{\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}\bigg)\bigg]a_{\mathbf{q},a}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{q},a^{\prime}}(i\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\Omega,\mathbf{q}}\bigg[M_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}-N_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}i\Omega\bigg]a_{\mathbf{q},a}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)a_{\mathbf{q},a^{\prime}}(i\Omega)

(S153)

where

\displaystyle N_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{(\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1})^{2}}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a1}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a^{\prime}1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},a^{\prime}1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}

\displaystyle M_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}={\mathcal{U}{A}^{2}}{}\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\mathcal{U}^{2}{A}^{2}}{\mathcal{U}{A}-\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},1}+\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a1}U_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},a^{\prime}1}^{*}U_{\mathbf{k},a^{\prime}1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}

(S154)

We now investigate the gapless mode.
The Goldstone mode can be written as

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)=\sum_{a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{sub}}}a_{\mathbf{q},a}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)

(S155)

As we show later,
A_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)
develops a gapless mode at
\mathbf{q}=0
. Projecting to the Goldstone mode, we take
a_{\mathbf{q},a}^{\dagger}(i\Omega)\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{sub}}}A_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)
. The effective theory of Goldstone mode reads

\displaystyle S_{sitff,G}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\Omega,\mathbf{q}}\frac{1}{n_{sub}}\sum_{aa^{\prime}}\bigg[M_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}-N_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}i\Omega\bigg]A^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)A_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\Omega,\mathbf{q}}\frac{N_{\mathbf{q}}}{n_{sub}}\bigg[\frac{M_{\mathbf{q}}}{N_{\mathbf{q}}}-i\Omega\bigg]A^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)A_{\mathbf{q}}(i\Omega)

(S156)

where we have introduced
M_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{aa^{\prime}}M_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}},N_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{aa^{\prime}}N_{\mathbf{q},aa^{\prime}}
.
Therefore, the Green’s function of Goldstone mode is proportional to
1/(i\Omega-M_{\mathbf{q}}/N_{\mathbf{q}})
. Then the dispersion of Goldstone mode is

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{M_{\mathbf{q}}}{N_{\mathbf{q}}}

(S157)

To illustrate the stiffness of the Goldstone mode, and also its gapless nature, we perform a small
|\mathbf{q}|
expansion and find

\displaystyle M_{\mathbf{q}}\approx\mathcal{U}{A}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\bigg[Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}+\mathcal{U}{A}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{({A}\mathcal{U})^{2}}\bigg]q^{\mu}q^{\nu}

\displaystyle N_{\mathbf{q}}\approx 1+O(|\mathbf{q}|^{2})

(S158)

where

\displaystyle Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{aa^{\prime}}\partial_{\nu}U^{*}_{\mathbf{k},a^{\prime}1}\bigg[\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}-U_{\mathbf{k},a^{\prime}1}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}^{*}\bigg]\partial_{\mu}U_{\mathbf{k},a1}

(S159)

We now show that for an isolated band,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}=0

(S160)

For an isolated narrow band, its dispersion
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}
is a smooth and periodic function of
\mathbf{k}
. This allows us to rewrite
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}
as

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}T(\mathbf{R})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}}

(S161)

with
T(\mathbf{R})
the Fourier transformation of
\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}
.

This indicates

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}}T(\mathbf{R})(-R^{\mu}R^{\nu})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}}=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}T(\mathbf{R})(-R^{\mu}R^{\nu})N\delta_{\mathbf{R},0}=0

(S162)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle M_{\mathbf{q}}\approx\mathcal{U}{A}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\bigg[Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}+\mathcal{U}{A}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{({A}\mathcal{U})^{2}}\bigg]q^{\mu}q^{\nu}=\mathcal{U}{A}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\bigg[Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{({A}\mathcal{U})^{2}}\bigg]q^{\mu}q^{\nu}

(S163)

Therefore we conclude the dispersion of the Goldstone mode is

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{M_{\mathbf{q}}}{N_{\mathbf{q}}}\approx\mathcal{U}{A}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mu\nu}\bigg[Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}}{({A}\mathcal{U})^{2}}\bigg]q^{\mu}q^{\nu}

(S164)

We let

\displaystyle Q^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}Q^{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k}),\quad M^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\partial_{\mu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}\partial_{\nu}\delta\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},1}

(S165)

where
Q^{\mu\nu}
denotes the quantum geometry of the system, and
M^{\mu\nu}
characterizes the dispersion of the narrow band. Then we find

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{q}}\approx\mathcal{U}{A}\sum_{\mu\nu}\bigg[Q^{\mu\nu}-\frac{M^{\mu\nu}}{({A}\mathcal{U})^{2}}\bigg]q^{\mu}q^{\nu}

(S166)

We find that the dispersion of the Goldstone mode is quadratic. We now show it is consistent with the counting rule discussed in Refs.
Nielsen and Chadha 1976
;
Watanabe and Murayama 2012
.
The original system possesses an SU(2) spin symmetry with three generators. After the development of ferromagnetic order, this symmetry is spontaneously broken down to a U(1) spin symmetry with only one generator. Since the translational symmetry remains unbroken, the number of spontaneously broken generators is
n_{{BG}}=2
.
According to the counting rule of the Goldstone modes
Nielsen and Chadha 1976
;
Watanabe and Murayama 2012
, the number of Goldstone modes must satisfy the inequality

n_{I}+2n_{II}\geq n_{\mathrm{BG}},

(S167)

where
n_{I}
is the number of type-I Goldstone modes with dispersion proportional to odd powers of momentum, and
n_{II}
is the number of type-II Goldstone modes with dispersion proportional to even powers of momentum.
From our Holstein–Primakoff boson analysis, we find that there is only one Goldstone mode. Therefore, in order for
eq.

S167
to be satisfied, this mode must be type-II. Consequently, its dispersion must be proportional to even powers of momentum. Indeed, our calculation confirms that the Goldstone mode exhibits a quadratic dispersion.

We observe that the quantum geometry tends to stabilize the ferromagnetic state, while the dispersions of the narrow bands tend to destabilize it. The instability of ferromagnetic states is characterized by the parameter values where
Q^{\mu\nu}-\frac{M^{\mu\nu}}{(A\mathcal{U})^{2}}
has a negative eigenvalue.
However, the transition between antiferromagnetism and ferromagnetism estimated from stiffness (
eq.

S166
) is different from the transition point estimated from the effective spin-spin coupling (
section

S6
).
Specifically, we consider the case
Q^{\mu\nu}=Q\delta_{\mu\nu}
and
M^{\mu\nu}=M\delta_{\mu\nu}
. From the stiffness calculation in
eq.

S166
, the transition point is
Q=M/(A\mathcal{U})^{2}
, whereas the effective spin model in
section

S6
yields
Q=2M/(A^{2}\mathcal{U})^{2}
.
The deviation comes from the fact that while computing spin stiffness, the high-order spin-spin interaction terms are also included.
This can be seen from the perfect flat-band limit, where the stiffness is the velocity of the Goldstone mode and can be obtained exactly from the expansion we have performed in
eq.

S166
. However, while we are calculating the spin-spin couplings, we truncated to the two-body interactions. The higher-order interactions, such as interactions that take the form of

n^{\mu}_{x_{i}}n^{\nu}_{x_{j}}n^{\delta}_{x_{m}}n^{\gamma}_{x_{n}}
are not included.
This high-order term also contributes to the spin stiffness and has been implicitly included in our calculation of
eq.

S166
discussed in this section, yielding the exact result in the perfect flat-band limit.

Finally, we note that although our effective spin-spin couplings neglect higher-order terms, they still capture essential information about the magnetic orderings favored by the wavefunctions and dispersions of the system.

S8
Toy model

To test our theory, we discuss the magnetism of a simple toy model using both our analytic results as well as numerical analysis. We show that a transition between a ferromagnetic phase and an antiferromagnetic phase can be realized by tuning the quantum geometry and the bandwidth of the narrow band.

We take a system formed by two layers of square lattices, with each atom located at

S9
Matsubara summations

We aim to evaluate the following summations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega=(2n+1)\pi/\beta}\frac{(i\omega)^{n}}{(\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}}e^{i\omega 0^{+}}

(S199)

for an integer number
n\geq 0
and a real number
\omega_{0}>0
.

We define

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{z^{n}}{(-z^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}}\frac{1}{z^{n}}e^{z0^{+}}

(S200)

We could use the contour integral which indicates

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i\omega}f(i\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi i\beta}\oint f(z)h(z)dz=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{z_{0}\in Poles}\text{Res}[f(z_{0})]h(z_{0})

(S201)

where

\displaystyle h(z)=-\beta n_{F}(z)

(S202)

f(z)
has two poles at
\omega_{0},-\omega_{0}
. The corresponding residues are

\displaystyle\text{Res}[f(\omega_{0})]=\frac{d}{dz}[(z-\omega_{0})^{2}f(z)]\bigg|_{z=\omega_{0}}=\frac{n-1}{4}\omega_{0}^{n-3}

\displaystyle\text{Res}[f(-\omega_{0})]=\frac{d}{dz}[(z+\omega_{0})^{2}f(z)]\bigg|_{z=-\omega_{0}}=\frac{n-1}{4}(-\omega_{0})^{n-3}

(S203)

In the low-temperature limit, we have

\displaystyle h(\omega_{0})\approx 0,\quad h(-\omega_{0})\approx-\beta

(S204)

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega=(2n+1)\pi/\beta}\frac{(i\omega)^{n}}{(\omega^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}}e^{i\omega 0^{+}}=\frac{n-1}{4}(-\omega_{0})^{n-3}

(S205)

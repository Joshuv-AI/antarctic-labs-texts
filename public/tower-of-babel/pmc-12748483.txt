Charting the growth of programmed cell death studies in uveal melanoma: a bibliometric evidence
Authors: Jie Zhang, Li Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveUveal melanoma is a malignant intraocular tumor with a high propensity for metastasis, and the significance of programmed cell death (PCD) in this disease has attracted growing attention in recent decades. However, few studies have systematically investigated the global publication trends, collaboration networks, and thematic focus areas in this domain. The current study utilized a bibliometric approach to examine how PCD research in uveal melanoma has evolved from 1999 to 2024, highlighting key contributors, citation patterns, and emerging research topics.MethodsData were collected from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for publications dated January 1, 1999–December 31, 2024. We restricted our search to original articles and reviews in English, employing a comprehensive keyword strategy to identify publications specifically addressing PCD mechanisms—such as apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis—in the context of uveal melanoma. After removing duplicates and screening for relevance, 536 publications were included. WoSCC was selected for its standardized indexing and cited-reference data; potential omissions from Scopus, PubMed, or Embase are addressed in Limitations. Bibliometric indicators, including annual output, institutional productivity, citation counts, and keyword co-occurrence, were analyzed using R-bibliometrix and VOSviewer. Data were last retrieved on May 1, 2025; analyses were restricted to the WoSCC ‘Publication Year’ 1999–2024 to avoid post-hoc reassignment. References published in 2025 are cited only for context and were not counted in the bibliometric dataset.ResultsIn total, 536 articles in 242 distinct sources were identified, reflecting an annual growth rate of 11.05%. The United States and China emerged as the leading producers of relevant publications, exhibiting robust and often international collaborative networks. Top institutions included major Chinese universities (e.g., Sun Yat-sen University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University) and renowned Western research centers such as Harvard University and Leiden University, many of which formed high-density clusters in collaboration maps. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science was the most prolific journal in this area, while Cancer Research and Nature ranked among the top in local citations. Keyword co-occurrence analysis revealed an evolving focus over time—from foundational topics like apoptosis and tumor proliferation to more recent interests in ferroptosis, immunotherapy, and metastatic pathways. These findings suggest that PCD research in uveal melanoma has matured into a dynamic, multidisciplinary field that intersects with precision oncology and immuno-oncology.ConclusionThis bibliometric study established a view of PCD research trends in uveal melanoma, demonstrating the field’s rapid expansion, strong international collaborations, and an increasing emphasis on emerging cell-death pathways. These results underscore that deeper mechanistic insights into PCD processes have the potential to inform more effective therapies and prognostic tools, particularly in light of the limited treatment options once metastatic disease occurs. Early integration of PCD-focused hypotheses may support precision-medicine study design; however, bibliometrics cannot establish treatment benefit, and clinical trials are required to determine whether PCD-targeted strategies improve outcomes.Keywords: Uveal melanoma, Programmed cell death, Autophagy, Precision oncology, Ocular oncology, Necroptosis

Introduction

Uveal melanoma is a malignant intraocular tumor with multiple dimensions, characterized by a high propensity for metastasis and associated with a range of adverse clinical outcomes [1–4]. Uveal melanoma remains the most common primary intraocular malignancy in adults, and its incidence varies across different populations. In many regions, the burden of this disease has prompted growing attention from both clinicians and researchers, as effective treatments are limited once metastasis occurs [5–10]. With the persistent challenges of early detection and the heterogeneity of disease progression, uveal melanoma has emerged as one pressing concern for ocular oncology, posing a substantial burden to healthcare systems worldwide [11–16]. Relevant literature has extensively documented a large number of risk factors for uveal melanoma, such as certain genetic mutations, and environmental exposures, underscoring its multifaceted etiology [17–24]. Concurrently, there is a need to explore pivotal biological pathways—particularly those involving programmed cell death (PCD)—that may govern the transition from primary ocular lesions to metastatic disease and ultimately affect overall patient prognosis. Programmed cell death represents a broad spectrum of regulated processes, including apoptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, and several emerging modalities [25–31]. These mechanisms are widely recognized as reliable indicators of cancer cell viability and therapeutic responses, given their integral role in shaping tumor behavior. The adverse outcomes of dysfunctional PCD pathways in uveal melanoma include increased tumor aggressiveness, elevated metastatic potential, and diminished treatment efficacy. Multiple preclinical and clinical observations suggest that aberrations in cell-death signaling contribute to immune evasion, resistance to conventional therapies, and, in some instances, rapid disease progression [32–38]. Despite the growth of studies addressing these mechanisms, most research has traditionally focused on particular PCD pathways in isolation, leaving an incomplete picture of how the dynamic interplay among different forms of cell death might influence therapeutic interventions. It remains crucial, therefore, to systematically investigate the current state of global research and identify the underexplored aspects of programmed cell death in uveal melanoma. To our knowledge, this is the first bibliometric analysis focused specifically on PCD modalities within uveal melanoma, extending beyond melanoma-wide or ophthalmic oncology surveys to map how discrete PCD themes, institutions, and collaborations coalesce in this disease.

Some studies have examined single pathways such as apoptosis in various tumors, yet only a subset has explored more complex interactions—such as the role of autophagy in immune modulation or ferroptosis in targeted therapy resistance—in the specific context of uveal melanoma [39–41]. A better understanding of these multifactorial processes holds considerable promise for novel therapeutic avenues, including combination regimens that merge immunotherapy with cell-death pathway inhibitors [42–48]. However, the existing literature has been scattered across disciplines, limiting the ability of researchers and clinicians to form cohesive, evidence-based strategies. In view of these limitations, our study aims to capture a comprehensive overview of global research trends in the domain of programmed cell death in uveal melanoma.

In the present study, we employed a bibliometric methodology to synthesize data from January 1, 1999, to December 31, 2024, using a standardized search strategy within the Web of Science Core Collection. Our objectives were to identify publication patterns, assess major contributing institutions and countries, and elucidate the thematic focus areas within uveal melanoma-related PCD research. Through this approach, we sought to reveal how the field has evolved over time, highlight prominent clusters of collaborative networks, and discern emerging topics that are poised to reshape clinical practice. We anticipated that bibliometric indicators—such as citation counts and keyword co-occurrence—would underscore critical knowledge gaps, thus guiding future research priorities and offering a roadmap for multi-institutional, interdisciplinary collaborations. Based on these considerations, the current article endeavors to provide a panoramic perspective on how this area of inquiry has matured into a recognized field, reflecting the shifting landscape of uveal melanoma research from isolated mechanistic reports to increasingly integrative explorations of cell-death pathways. We aim to facilitate a more cohesive and targeted research framework that can expedite the development of new treatment paradigms for patients facing a disease known for its clinical complexity and high metastatic risk. We further outline how citation bursts, keyword co-occurrence clusters, and cross-institutional hubs can be operationalized to generate testable biomarkers and combinatorial targets, and to inform pragmatic trial design elements such as site selection and accrual networks.

Method

Data sources and search strategies

Literature retrieval was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). We limited to English-language original articles and reviews to ensure consistent metadata and reproducible export; the implications of this restriction are addressed in Limitations, searching for publications dated from January 1, 1999 to December 31, 2024. The language was limited to English, and the document types were restricted to original articles and reviews. The search formula applied was:

TS = ((“Uveal Melanoma” OR “Ocular Melanoma” OR “Choroidal Melanoma” OR “Intraocular Melanoma” OR “Iris Melanoma” OR “Ciliary Body Melanoma”) AND (“Programmed Cell Death” OR “Apoptosis” OR “Autophagy” OR “Ferroptosis” OR “Necroptosis” OR “Pyroptosis” OR “Anoikis” OR “Cuproptosis” OR “Parthanatos” OR “Entosis” OR “Mitotic Catastrophe” OR “NETosis”)).

We piloted additional synonyms (e.g., ‘regulated cell death,’ ‘immunogenic cell death,’ ‘programmed necrosis,’ ‘cell death modalities’) and consulted MeSH terms to harmonize terminology. The final string balanced recall and precision by minimizing off-topic retrieval After deduplication, two reviewers (JZ, LL) independently screened titles/abstracts against predefined criteria, with full-text review as needed. Inter-rater agreement was quantified using Cohen’s κ, and disagreements were resolved by consensus with third-party adjudication to exclude articles irrelevant to the field of programmed cell death (PCD) in uveal melanoma (UM). Additional exclusions were made by reading the full text if articles failed to address relevant concepts of PCD mechanisms or UM pathology. Ultimately, 536 publications specifically pertaining to PCD in UM were identified and subjected to further analysis. Records were exported on May 1, 2025. Items tagged ‘Early Access’ with WoSCC ‘Publication Year’ = 2024 were retained; items assigned to 2025 were excluded from counts to maintain reproducibility. As a completeness check, we re-ran the core query in Scopus and PubMed to assess qualitative overlap; because licensing and indexing differences preclude seamless merging, these sources were not combined with the primary WoSCC dataset.

Statistics analysis

All included publications with their complete records (author, title, abstract, references, etc.) were downloaded from WoSCC in a text format suitable for bibliometric analysis. The R-bibliometrix package (R version 4.3.0) was employed to synthesize descriptive data and produce comparative indicators, such as annual productivity and growth rates. VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) was used to construct visualization networks, including co-authorship, keyword co-occurrence, and citation clusters. Keyword co-occurrence used full counting with a minimum occurrence threshold of 5. Clusters were detected via the Louvain algorithm (resolution = 1.0), yielding early-mechanistic, immuno-oncology, and emerging-modalities themes. Country-level outputs are presented as absolute counts; we additionally discuss interpretation when normalizing by population size and gross domestic expenditure on R&D (GERD), recognizing that such normalizations can reorder apparent productivity. Each node in the visualization maps represented a distinct analytical unit (e.g., authors, institutions, journals), while the thickness of the connecting lines reflected the intensity of collaboration or co-occurrence.

Result

Overall analysis of the development of the field of programmed cell death in uveal melanoma

From 1999 to 2024, scholarly interest in programmed cell death in uveal melanoma has continued to expand, as evidenced by the steady rise in publications and a notable 11.05% annual growth rate (Fig. 1). During this period, 536 documents appeared in 242 distinct sources, indicating a substantial body of work for a specialized area. A total of 2,819 authors contributed to these documents, with only 11 papers being single-authored, suggesting the prevalence of collaborative efforts. Indeed, each manuscript had on average 7.09 co-authors, and nearly one quarter (24.81%) of the collaborations were international, reflecting the global scope of research and the recognition that diverse teams can offer broader insights into disease mechanisms. The field’s robust citation record, averaging 29.17 citations per document, underlines the scientific community’s engagement with these findings. Although the document average age of 8.49 years suggests that some foundational studies are relatively well established, the continued influx of new references—totaling 20,071—demonstrates an evolving research landscape. Moreover, the presence of 1,126 distinct author keywords highlights the multifaceted nature of programmed cell death in uveal melanoma and points to the varied experimental approaches, therapeutic strategies, and mechanistic explorations. Taken together, these indicators illustrate a maturing but still rapidly advancing research domain that increasingly benefits from international and interdisciplinary collaborations.

Institutional analysis

As shown in Fig. 8, the institutional collaboration network features several high-density clusters, reflecting both regional and global partnerships in studying programmed cell death in uveal melanoma. Prominent nodes include major Chinese universities—such as Sun Yat-sen University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University—connected closely with each other and with other large research hubs. European and North American centers, for example Leiden University, Harvard University, and the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, also occupy central positions, forming extensive cross-border linkages. Figure 9 highlights the most prolific affiliations by publication count, with Harvard University Medical Affiliates leading at 40 articles, closely followed by Institut Curie and Leiden University (each with 38) and Université PSL at 37. Harvard University itself and Leiden University Medical Center both appear in the mid-30s, suggesting that the Harvard and Leiden networks are particularly influential when their multiple branches are considered collectively. Shanghai Jiao Tong University, although part of the robust Chinese cluster observed in Fig. 8, ranks somewhat lower in total articles at 28, indicating that while Chinese institutions are forging strong intra-national collaborations, some Western centers continue to dominate global citation and publication metrics. The temporal trends in Fig. 10 reveal that these top institutions have steadily increased their research output since the early 2000s, with a pronounced acceleration around 2010. This surge coincided with a broader shift toward precision oncology and immunotherapeutic research; causal attribution is beyond the scope of bibliometrics, areas where mechanisms of cell death play a pivotal role.

Analysis of publication volume development

As shown in Fig. 15; Table 1, publications on programmed cell death in uveal melanoma began at a low base in 1999, with only four articles (0.75% of the total), followed by modest growth and occasional dips through the early 2000s. For example, after reaching seven publications in 2001 (1.31%), the output dropped back down to four the very next year, suggesting a nascent field still testing research avenues and funding streams. Around 2003–2004, the volume steadily climbed (to 10 and then 11 publications), marking a transition from the embryonic stage to a more recognized area of inquiry. This pattern persisted through 2005 and 2006, when publication counts and growth rates oscillated (14 publications in 2005, then down to seven in 2006). From 2007 onward, fluctuations became less pronounced, and by 2012 the number of publications rose significantly to 22 (4.10%), reflecting heightened interest and potentially increased investment in translational or immuno-oncology approaches. Although the growth rate continued to vary year by year—spiking by 118.75% in 2017 and then contracting or leveling off in certain intervals—the overall trajectory remained upward. Notably, 2019 to 2022 shows consecutive high counts (38, 39, 41, then 53 publications), a sign of a maturing field with more researchers, funding channels, and institutional collaborations focusing on cell death pathways in uveal melanoma. By 2024, the annual output reached 55 articles, representing 10.26% of the total publications over the study period. Taken together, these swings and sustained expansions reflect the dynamism of an emerging scientific domain: early studies laid foundational knowledge, mid-phase investigations refined research methods, and later surges coincided with increased attention to targeted and immunomodulatory strategies; we do not infer causality.

Analysis of keywords and programmed cell death in uveal melanoma field essentials

As shown in Fig. 16, the distribution of keywords highlights several prominent clusters that reflect the evolving research landscape of programmed cell death in uveal melanoma. Using Louvain clustering on the keyword co-occurrence network (min occurrence = 5), we identified three themes—early mechanistic biology (e.g., apoptosis/autophagy), immuno-oncology, and emerging modalities (e.g., ferroptosis/cuproptosis). Terms like “uveal melanoma,” “apoptosis,” “expression,” and “cancer” occupy central positions, suggesting foundational themes around tumor biology and cell-death mechanisms. In the left-hand region, keywords such as “mutations,” “GNAQ,” “BRAF mutations,” and “autophagy” form a network linked to molecular pathways and targeted therapy strategies, indicating that early studies focused on identifying key genetic drivers and exploring cell-death signaling. By contrast, the right side features “invasion,” “migration,” and “proliferation,” capturing the metastatic and invasive potential of uveal melanoma—a recurring challenge in clinical management. Meanwhile, Fig. 17 shows a broader network in which “apoptosis” and “expression” serve as bridges between clusters, reflecting how multiple facets of programmed cell death converge to shape tumor progression, treatment resistance, and therapeutic innovation. Notably, immunotherapy-related keywords, such as “ipilimumab” and “immune microenvironment,” appear toward the periphery, indicating a temporal shift in topic prominence; this descriptive pattern does not imply clinical effectiveness. Figure 18 provides a temporal overlay, revealing that terms tied to immunomodulation and novel cell-death modalities—e.g., “ferroptosis” and “tumor microenvironment”—have become more prominent in recent years, signaling a move beyond classical apoptosis-focused approaches. Together, these keyword distributions illustrate how the field has transitioned from fundamental molecular discoveries to increasingly sophisticated, multi-pronged strategies that address metastasis, therapy resistance, and the crosstalk between tumor cells and their immune milieu. This trajectory underscores the complexity of uveal melanoma and the need for integrated approaches that unite molecular insights with immunological and clinical dimensions.

Discussion

The present bibliometric study, grounded in a broad review of publications from 1999 to 2024, reveals that interest in programmed cell death (PCD) in uveal melanoma has experienced a notable expansion, mirroring the dynamic evolution seen in other oncological research fields. The descriptive data suggest that this domain, once represented by sporadic articles in the early 2000s, has now solidified into a major area of inquiry characterized by high-impact publications, an increasing number of collaborative teams, and the widespread exploration of multiple cell-death pathways. In particular, the steep rise in publications after 2012 highlights the emergence of immuno-oncology and targeted molecular therapies as driving forces that have increased attention to PCD in uveal melanoma; our mapping remains descriptive rather than inferential. At the same time, the shift toward diversified experimental designs and global research alliances appears to have fostered more integrative investigations into apoptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, and other regulated cell-death processes. Several aspects of this analysis warrant attention. First, the exploration of apoptosis and immunomodulation in uveal melanoma has been enriched by the co-occurrence of terms such as “ipilimumab,” “immune microenvironment,” and “invasion,” reflecting a progressive alignment with evolving cancer therapies that emphasize the synergy of immune checkpoint blockade and tumor-specific cell-death pathways. Second, our data underscore that countries like the United States and China have moved rapidly from an early experimental phase to a more advanced, collaborative stage of research, fueling a steady increase in high-quality publications. Comparable to prior bibliometric examinations in other malignancies, these trends point to the role of shared funding initiatives and the establishment of specialized research centers as pivotal catalysts for the field’s acceleration. Moreover, institutional clusters observed in our co-authorship networks suggest that both intra-national and multi-national partnerships have become increasingly vital, allowing investigators to harness novel technologies—from genomic profiling to advanced imaging—for dissecting the complexities of tumor biology.

One of the central themes to emerge from our keyword analysis is that programmed cell death in uveal melanoma is not merely an academic curiosity but rather a critical entry point for understanding metastatic progression and therapy resistance. By mapping transitions from mechanistic to targeted topics, we identify where clinical hypotheses concentrate; bibliometric signals cannot determine whether modulating specific PCD pathways alters disease course or prognosis, immune tolerance, and patient prognosis. Cuproptosis and parthanatos appeared in the query but yielded few UM-specific publications, suggesting either nascent inquiry or inconsistent terminology; harmonized nomenclature may improve retrieval and synthesis. Such a trajectory parallels observations in cutaneous melanoma, where the development of targeted agents and immunotherapies triggered a similar leap in publication outputs and global collaborations.Beyond cutaneous melanoma, patterns mirror other rare cancers (e.g., Merkel cell carcinoma, retinoblastoma, select sarcomas), where bursts around immuno-oncology coincide with collaboration growth; such analogies contextualize UM within broader rare-cancer research ecosystems. At the same time, the findings underscore persisting gaps: although clinical trials and translational projects are increasingly robust, variability in the standard of care across different regions and the challenges in effectively treating metastatic uveal melanoma remain significant obstacles. Notwithstanding the strengths of this bibliometric approach—particularly its comprehensive inclusion of global publications and its visualization of collaboration patterns—certain limitations should be recognized. For instance, our reliance on the Web of Science Core Collection may have omitted relevant literature from other platforms or regional databases. Moreover, while we provide broad insights into publication patterns, the actual quality and clinical impact of individual studies require a more nuanced appraisal involving randomized controlled trials and long-term follow-up data. Bibliometric outputs can inform translation by: (i) prioritizing candidate biomarkers from keyword/citation bursts for prospective validation, (ii) selecting trial endpoints aligned with emerging PCD modalities, (iii) leveraging high-betweenness institutions/countries to optimize multi-site accrual, and (iv) aligning funding calls with underrepresented, high-promise PCD themes. China’s higher single-country output likely reflects strong domestic funding schemes, rapidly expanding ocular oncology centers, and language/indexing patterns; how these structures shape long-term impact warrants continued monitoring. Early foundational work established its genetic and mechanistic underpinnings, while later surges in publications integrated immunotherapeutic strategies, thereby enhancing our collective understanding of this rare but formidable malignancy. Considering the relatively recent incorporation of ferroptosis, immunomodulation, and emerging cell-death modalities into the conversation, it is plausible that further breakthroughs, particularly in combination therapies and biomarker discovery, will continue to shape the field. Consequently, ongoing international collaboration, cross-disciplinary partnerships, and supportive research infrastructures remain indispensable to unlocking effective treatment strategies that can improve patient survival and quality of life in the coming years.

Conclusion

This study established a comprehensive view of global research trends in programmed cell death in uveal melanoma using a bibliometric approach spanning 1999 to 2024. This study suggests that intensified investigations into multiple cell-death modalities—encompassing apoptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, and beyond—have coalesced to drive a rapidly expanding body of work and foster increasingly collaborative international networks. They may create a reinforcing cycle, in which mechanistic insights and clinical applications feed back into one another. This bibliometric mapping (1999–2024) charts rapid growth, evolving themes, and collaboration clusters in PCD research for uveal melanoma. We recommend: (1) integrating bibliometric intelligence into grant and portfolio planning; (2) normalizing multinational outputs by population and GERD when benchmarking; (3) prospectively testing combinatorial PCD-immuno hypotheses prioritized by citation/keyword bursts; (4) harmonizing PCD terminology to improve discoverability and evidence synthesis. Clinical benefit requires prospective studies. In the context of advancing immuno-oncology and precision medicine, the interplay among distinct programmed cell-death mechanisms should be explored to avert treatment resistance and accelerate progress toward more effective management strategies.

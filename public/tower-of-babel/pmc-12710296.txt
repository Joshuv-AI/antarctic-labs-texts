Effectiveness of Transcatheter Arterial Embolization in Locally Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer Resistant to Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy: A Case Report
Authors: Seo Ha Park, Keong Won Yun, Ga Young Yoon, Youngjong Cho, Seungeun Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 74-year-old woman was diagnosed with locally advanced invasive ductal carcinoma of the right breast, which was confirmed as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) by immunohisto-chemical analysis. Multiple ipsilateral axillary lymph node metastases were identified, but no distant metastases were detected. The patient underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which was ineffective, and subsequently developed tumor-related wound bleeding. To control the bleeding and reduce tumor vascularity, transcatheter arterial embolization (TAE) was performed. Permanent embolization using Embosphere microspheres and glue induced in complete ischemia, leading to a significant reduction in tumor and lymph node size, thereby enabling surgical resection. A follow-up examination one year later revealed no evidence of recurrence or metastasis. This case report highlights the potential therapeutic efficacy of TAE in the management of locally advanced TNBC resistant to neoadjuvant chemotherapy.Keywords: Triple-Negative Breast Neoplasms; Embolization, Therapeutic; Microsphere

INTRODUCTION

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a subtype of breast cancer characterized by the lack of expression of estrogen receptors, progesterone receptors, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2. It accounts for approximately 10%–20% of all breast cancer cases and is associated with an aggressive clinical course, rapid tumor progression, poor prognosis, and a high recurrence rate (1). Owing to its limited therapeutic options, treatment remains challenging and generally involves a combination of surgery and chemotherapy. Standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy typically includes a regimen of adriamycin, cyclophosphamide, and paclitaxel (2). Despite ongoing efforts to develop targeted therapies, effective treatment strategies for TNBC remain an area of active research. Although not yet widely established, some researchers have proposed transcatheter arterial embolization (TAE) as a potential adjunct treatment for TNBC. This case report describes the successful use of TAE as a neoadjuvant approach in a patient with locally advanced TNBC that was refractory to conventional chemotherapy. Initially performed to control tumor-related bleeding, TAE unexpectedly induced a significant tumor response, ultimately enabling surgical resection.

CASE REPORT

A 74-year-old female patient with a known diagnosis of breast cancer presented to our hospital’s emergency room with persistent bleeding at the tumor site for the past four days (Fig. 1A). She had been diagnosed with TNBC through biopsy at another hospital in 2019 but had declined treatment at that time. Imaging studies, including chest CT and breast MRI performed at our hospital, revealed a large tumor measuring approximately 11 × 10 cm involving the entire right breast, with evidence of skin invasion (Fig. 1B). Skin thickening and edematous changes were observed along the right breast and anterior chest wall. Metastatic lymph nodes were identified in the right axillary levels I and II (Fig. 1C, left image) as well as small but prominent nodes along the right internal mammary lymphatic chain. However, PET-CT revealed no signs of distant metastases (not shown). Based on these findings, the cancer was classified as cT4N2M0 (Stage IIIb). The patient was administered four cycles of neoadjuvant chemotherapy consisting of adriamycin and cyclophosphamide, followed by two cycles of docetaxel, which were initiated 4 months prior to the procedure. The patient subsequently underwent adjuvant radiotherapy for one month. However, the treatment response was minimal, with only a 1 cm reduction in tumor size. Despite the daily application of compression dressings, bleeding persisted, and her hemoglobin level decreased from 10 to 8 g/dL. Given the ongoing hemorrhage and lack of response to conservative management, TAE was considered for bleeding control.

Under local anesthesia and direct ultrasound guidance, vascular access was obtained via the right common femoral artery using a micropuncture kit (Micro-stick, Medcomp, Harleysville, PA, USA). A 5-Fr long sheath (Radiofocus; Terumo, Tokyo, Japan) was introduced, followed by catheterization with a 5-Fr angiographic headhunter catheter (Terumo) to perform right brachiocephalic artery angiography. The angiogram revealed a hypervascular right breast tumor that received blood supply from multiple feeding arteries originating from the right subclavian artery. The right axillary artery supplied the lateral aspect of the tumor, whereas the right internal mammary artery supplied the medial portion (Fig. 1C, right image).

Selective catheterization with contrast infusion was performed using a 0.016-inch microwire (Meister, Asahi, Aichi, Japan) and a 2-Fr microcatheter (Progreat, Terumo) to visualize the right thoracoacromial and right lateral thoracic arteries. These vessels exhibited a prominent tumor blush and multiple branching arteries supplying both the tumor and metastatic lymph nodes (Fig. 1D). Although no active extravasation was observed, the identified feeding arteries were strongly suspected as the source of bleeding. Consequently, embolization was performed on all identified arteries. Each vessel was embolized using Embosphere microspheres (300–500 µm, Merit Medical, South Jordan, UT, USA) followed by selective catheterization of the right internal mammary artery, which was embolized with Histoacryl glue (B.Braun, Melsungen, Germany) mixed with lipiodol (Guerbet, Villepinte, France) in a 1:2 ratio.

The procedure was well-tolerated without severe complications. Although tumor lysis syndrome is considered a potential risk due to extensive embolization, serum potassium and uric acid levels increased only mildly to 4.5 mmol/L and 6 mg/dL, respectively, without reaching critical levels. Fever and pain, characteristic of post-embolization syndrome (3), appeared within a week but gradually resolved with conservative management.

Three weeks later, most exophytic tumors were devascularized, detached, and spontaneously exfoliated (Fig. 1E, left image). In the pathology report, 10% necrosis was still observed in the remnant breast cancer. Additionally, the metastatic lymph nodes became necrotic with air formation (Fig. 1E, right image). This enabled surgical intervention in contrast to the initial treatment plan. As a result, modified radical mastectomy with axillary lymph node dissection was successfully performed (Fig. 1F). Three years after the procedure, the patient returned for follow-up, and MRI, CT, and bone scans showed no abnormal findings in the right breast or evidence of metastasis.

This study obtained Institutional Review Board approval from the institution and the need for informed consent was waived (IRB No. GNAH 2025-01-002).

DISCUSSION

TNBC is a subtype of breast cancer characterized by the lack of three target receptors, which poses significant treatment challenges. Consequently, various therapeutic strategies are being explored. Several case reports have documented the use of transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) for locally advanced breast cancer, demonstrating consistent efficacy in reducing both primary tumor size and metastatic lymph nodes, with minimal severe adverse events and a lower incidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis (4,5).

In this case, TAE was initially performed to control wound bleeding. However, the resulting ischemic effects led to a significant reduction in tumor vascularity, inducing a partial tumor response. Additionally, by reducing the size of both the tumor and metastatic lymph nodes, TAE improved the feasibility of surgical resection. Unlike previous studies that employed TACE (5) or aimed for a complete tumor response (4), our approach was distinct in that TAE was not intended to achieve a direct oncological response. Despite the use of TAE instead of TACE, favorable treatment outcomes were observed. Consequently, for inoperable or chemoresistant breast cancer, TAE may serve as a bridge to surgery by inducing a partial response, thereby potentially improving patient outcomes.

Moreover, TAE improved surgical precision by reducing intraoperative bleeding and facilitating the determination of the resection margins. Controlled vascular occlusion during TAE effectively minimized bleeding, while selective embolization of feeding arteries helped delineate tumor boundaries, enabling the surgeons to plan and execute a more precise resection.

Previous studies have employed drug-loaded microspheres, such as docetaxel- or epirubicin- loaded beads (HepaSphere, Merit Medical), in TACE procedures (4,5). However, in this case, TAE was chosen over TACE, which resulted in significant tumor shrinkage. Given the initial chemoresistance of the tumor, it was anticipated that additional chemotherapy via TACE would have limited efficacy, leading to the decision to proceed with TAE alone. Indeed, a marked reduction in tumor size was observed after embolization. Consequently, tumor necrosis was achieved through ischemic effects in this patient. Another study reported mass shrinkage in inflammatory breast cancer following TAE, similar to our case (6). While this case raised the question of whether the tumor reduction was primarily due to the chemotherapeutic drugs themselves or embolization-induced ischemia, the findings from a single case are insufficient to conclude the superiority of ischemia at this stage.

Achieving complete ischemia in breast cancer is inherently difficult due to the presence of multiple collateral vessels. However, our approach may have been successful because of the extensive embolization of multiple feeding arteries. Additionally, axillary lymph node embolization was performed, which resulted in a partial response. This aligns with previous studies demonstrating the positive effect of transarterial embolization on metastatic lymph nodes in breast cancer.

Concerns regarding potential skin necrosis were mitigated by using relatively large embolic particles (≥300 µm), which prevented the occlusion of small dermal blood vessels (7). Importantly, as no anticancer drugs were used in this approach, the risk of chemotherapy-induced systemic side effects was eliminated.

In conclusion, TAE may be considered a viable treatment option for select breast cancer cases, particularly for large, inoperable tumors or those causing significant symptoms such as pain or hemorrhage. When performed before surgery, TAE has the potential to reduce tumor size and vascularity, making subsequent surgical resection more feasible and improving overall treatment outcomes.

Global dynamics above the ground state for the energy-critical Hartree equation with radial data
Xuemei Li, Chenxi Liu, Guixiang Xu

Abstract
. Based on the concentration-compactness-rigidity argument in [ 15 , 16 ] and the non-degeneracy of the ground state in [ 28 , 29 , 30 ] , long time dynamics for the focusing energy-critical Hartree equation with radial data have been classified when the energy E ⁡ ( u 0 ) ≤ E ⁡ ( W ) E(u_{0})\leq E(W) in [ 24 , 29 , 34 , 37 ] , where W W is the ground state. In this paper, we continue the study on the dynamics of the radial solutions with the energy E ⁡ ( u 0 ) E(u_{0}) at most slightly larger than that of the ground states. This is an extension of the results [ 22 , 23 , 43 , 44 , 45 , 46 , 47 , 49 ] on NLS, NLW and NLKG, which were pioneered by K. Nakanishi and W. Schlag in [ 44 , 45 ] in the study of nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation in the subcritical case. The argument is an adaptation of the works in [ 22 , 23 , 43 , 49 ] , the proof uses an analysis of the hyperbolic dynamics near the ground state and the variational structure far from them. The key components that allow to classify the solutions are the hyperbolic (ejection) dynamical behavior near the ground state and the one-pass lemma.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the focusing, energy-critical Hartree equation

The symplectic form

\omega(u,v):=\langle iu,v\rangle.

\displaystyle E(W)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle-C(v),

(2.5)

\displaystyle K(W+v)=

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla W,\nabla v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}),

where

C(v):=\frac{1}{4}\iint\frac{\big|v(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|v(t,y)\big|^{2}+4W(x)v_{1}(t,x)\big|v(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}).

Proof.

\displaystyle E(W+v)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(W+v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\iint\frac{\big|(W+v)(x)\big|^{2}\big|(W+v)(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(W)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\iint\frac{\big|v(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|v(t,y)\big|^{2}+4W(x)v_{1}(t,x)\big|v(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(W)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}).

\displaystyle K(W+v)=

\displaystyle\|\nabla(W+v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\iint\frac{\big|(W+v)(x)\big|^{2}\big|(W+v)(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\langle\nabla W,\nabla v\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\iint\frac{2|W(x)|^{2}|v(y)|^{2}+|v(x)|^{2}|v(y)|^{2}+4W(y)v_{1}(y)|v(x)|^{2}+4W(x)W(y)v_{1}(x)v_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla W,\nabla v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}).

∎

3.
Proof of main theorem.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
relies upon some propositions stated below.

3.1.
Orthogonal decomposition.

The following proposition gives a decomposition of a vector
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}
close to the ground states
\mathcal{W}
, taking account of two parameters (the rotation parameter and the scaling parameter) and a constraint (the so-called orthogonality condition).

Proposition 3.1
.

(Orthogonal decomposition of
\varphi
). There exist an absolute constant
0<\delta_{E}\ll 1
and a
C^{1}
function
\left(\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{\sigma}\right):B_{\delta_{E}}(\mathcal{W})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mathbb{R}
with the following properties. For any
\varphi\in B_{\delta_{E}}(\mathcal{W})
, putting

\varphi=e^{i\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi)}S_{-1}^{\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)}(W+v),

(3.1)

we have

\displaystyle(v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=(v,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0,\;d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Moreover,
\left(\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi),\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)\right)\in(\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mathbb{R}
is unique for the above property.

Furthermore, if
\|\varphi-W_{\theta,\sigma}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\ll 1
for some
(\theta,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

\big|\left(e^{i\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi)}-e^{i\theta},\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)-\sigma\right)\big|\lesssim\|\varphi-W_{\theta,\sigma}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Remark 3.2
.

We use the null eigenfunctions
iW,\widetilde{W}
here. While the authors in
[
43
]
uses an alternative function
\chi
instead of zero resonance functions for the energy-critical NLS in dimension three.

Proof.

(v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=(v,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0
and the uniqueness of
(\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi),\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi))
have been proved in
[
34
, Lemma 4.1]
. For the sake of completeness, we give the details.

Now consider the functional

\displaystyle J(\theta,\sigma,\psi)=

\displaystyle\left(J_{0}(\theta,\sigma,\psi),J_{1}(\theta,\sigma,\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((e^{-i\theta}S_{-1}^{-\sigma}(W+\psi)-W,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}},\;\;(e^{-i\theta}S_{-1}^{-\sigma}(W+\psi)-W,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

Hence, by simple computations, we have

\displaystyle J(0,0,0)=0,\;\;\left|\frac{\partial J}{\partial(\theta,\sigma)}(0,0,0)\right|=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}(-iW,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}&0\\
0&(-\widetilde{W},\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}\end{array}\right|\not=0.

Thus by the implicit function theorem, there exist
\delta>0
and a
C^{1}
function
\left(\theta,\sigma\right):\;B_{\delta}(0)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

J(\theta(\psi),\sigma(\psi),\psi)=0,\;\;\theta(0)=\sigma(0)=0,

which is unique in
B_{\delta}(0)
and a neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By modulation decomposition, we know that for any
\varphi\in B_{\delta}(\mathcal{W})
, there exists
(\alpha,\beta,\psi)\in(\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mathbb{R}\times B_{\delta}(0)
such that
\varphi=e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}(W+\psi)
. Then we put

\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi):=\theta(\psi)+\alpha\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}},\;\;\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi):=\sigma(\psi)+\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

Then defining
v
by (
3.1
), we have
v=e^{-i\theta(\psi)}S_{-1}^{-\sigma(\psi)}(W+\psi)-W
and so

\displaystyle 0=J(\theta(\psi),\sigma(\psi),\psi)=\left((v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}},(v,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right),

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim|\theta(\psi)|+|\sigma(\psi)|+\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

This implies

\big|\left(e^{i\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi)}-e^{i\theta},\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)-\sigma\right)\big|\lesssim\|\varphi-W_{\theta,\sigma}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Choosing (
\alpha
,
\beta
) such that
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\sim\|\varphi-e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, then
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\gtrsim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
. Meanwhile,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}}\|e^{i\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi)}S_{-1}^{\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)}(W+v)-W_{\theta,\sigma}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi),\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)}+e^{i\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi)}S_{-1}^{\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)}v-W_{\widetilde{\theta}(\varphi),\widetilde{\sigma}(\varphi)}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

This proves
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.

∎

3.2.
Evolution around the ground states.

The following proposition describes more precisely the decomposition in Proposition
3.1
, taking into account the spectral properties of
i\mathcal{L}
. This decomposition does not use the radial symmetry.

Proposition 3.3
.

(Spectral decomposition of
v
). For any
v\in\dot{H}^{1}
, there exists a unique decomposition

v=\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}+\gamma,\;\;\lambda_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R},\;\;\gamma\in\dot{H}^{1},

(3.2)

such that
\omega(g_{\pm},\gamma)=0
(This implies that
\gamma\in G_{\bot}
).

After normalizing
g_{\pm}
(or
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
) such that

\omega(g_{+},g_{-})=2\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle=1,\;\pm\omega(W,g_{\mp})=\langle W,g_{2}\rangle>0,

(3.3)

the above decomposition is given by

\lambda_{\pm}=\pm\omega(v,g_{\mp}).

Putting
\lambda_{1}:=(\lambda_{+}+\lambda_{-})/2
and
\lambda_{2}:=(\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-})/2
, it can also be written as

v=2\lambda_{1}g_{1}-2i\lambda_{2}g_{2}+\gamma,\;\lambda_{1}=(v_{1}|g_{2}),\;\lambda_{2}=-(v_{2}|g_{1}),

(3.4)

with
(\gamma_{1}|g_{2})=(\gamma_{2}|g_{1})=0
.

Proof.

We only show that we can normalize
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
such that (
3.3
) holds. The remaining can be obtained by immediate computations.

Since
L_{-}\geq 0
with Ker
(L_{-})
=span
\{W\}
, we have

0<c:=\langle L_{-}g_{2},g_{2}\rangle=-\langle L_{+}g_{1},g_{1}\rangle=\mu\omega(g_{+},g_{-})/2
. By normalizing
g_{+}
and
g_{-}
, we can get
\omega(g_{+},g_{-})=1
.

Now we show that
\langle W,g_{2}\rangle\neq 0
. We prove it by contradiction. Assume that
\langle W,g_{2}\rangle=0
, then
0=\langle W,-\mu g_{2}\rangle=\langle L_{+}W,g_{1}\rangle=2\langle\Delta W,g_{1}\rangle
. Let
0<\delta\ll 1
and
v=\alpha W+\delta g_{1}
with
\alpha=O(\delta^{2})
to be chosen shortly. By expansion of
E,K
in (
2.5
) and

\frac{1}{2}\langle L_{+}v,v\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle-\iint\frac{W(x)W(y)v_{2}(x)v_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}),

we can get

\displaystyle E(W+v)=E(W)+\frac{1}{2}\langle L_{+}v,v\rangle-O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3})<E(W)-\frac{c}{2}\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3}),

K(W+v)=-2\langle\nabla W,\nabla v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})=-2\alpha\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+O(\delta^{2}).

Thus one can find
\alpha=O(\delta^{2})
such that
K(W+v)=0
, which contradicts
(
2.3
)
. Hence
\langle W,g_{2}\rangle\neq 0
.

Thus we prove that we can normalize
g_{\pm}
such that
(
3.3
)
holds.
∎

The following proposition aims at describing the dynamics of the solution near the ground states, using the decomposition in Proposition
3.1
. Again, this does not use the radial symmetry.

Proposition 3.4
.

(Linearization and parametrization around
\mathcal{W}
). Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
) on an interval I in the form (
3.1
), i.e.
(\theta,\sigma,v):I\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mathbb{R}\times\dot{H}^{1}
is defined by

u(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}S_{-1}^{\sigma(t)}\left(W+v(t)\right),\;\theta(t):=\widetilde{\theta}(u(t)),\;\sigma(t):=\widetilde{\sigma}(u(t)).

(3.5)

Then, letting
\tau:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\tau^{\prime}(t):=e^{2\sigma(t)}
, we have

\partial_{\tau}v=i\mathcal{L}v-\left(i\theta_{\tau}+\sigma_{\tau}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)(W+v)-iN(v),

(3.6)

where
\theta_{\tau}=\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tau}
etc., and

\displaystyle N(v):=

\displaystyle(W+v)\left(|x|^{-4}*|W+v|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle W\left(|x|^{-4}*|W|^{2}\right)-v\left(|x|^{-4}*|W|^{2}\right)-2\left(|x|^{-4}*(Wv_{1})\right)W.

Furthermore,
\partial_{\tau}(\theta,\sigma)=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})
and, decomposing v by Proposition 3.2,

\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{\pm}=\pm\mu\lambda_{\pm}+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}),

(3.7)

or equivalently,

\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{1}=\mu\lambda_{2}+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}),\;\;\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{2}=\mu\lambda_{1}+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}).

(3.8)

Proof.

Injecting the decomposition (
3.5
) into the equation (
1.1
), we obtain

v_{t}=ie^{2\sigma}\mathcal{L}v-\left(i\theta_{t}+\sigma_{t}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)(W+v)-ie^{2\sigma}N(v).

Applying the change of variable
t\mapsto\tau
with
\dot{\tau}=e^{2\sigma}
to the above yields (
3.6
).

Next we consider the equations for the parameters. Using (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}=\omega(\partial_{\tau}v,g_{-})=

\displaystyle\omega(i\mathcal{L}v,g_{-})-\omega\left(\left(i\theta_{\tau}+\sigma_{\tau}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)W,g_{-}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle N(v),g_{-}\rangle-\omega\left(\left(i\theta_{\tau}+\sigma_{\tau}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)v,g_{-}\right).

Using
\mathcal{L}^{*}=\mathcal{L}
and
\left(S_{a}^{\prime}\right)^{*}=-S_{-a}^{\prime}
, we see that

\displaystyle\omega(i\mathcal{L}v,g_{-})=\omega(i\mathcal{L}g_{-},v)=\mu\lambda_{+},\;\;\omega\left(\left(i\theta_{\tau}+\sigma_{\tau}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)W,g_{-}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\omega\left(\left(i\theta_{\tau}+\sigma_{\tau}S_{-1}^{\prime}\right)v,g_{-}\right)=-\theta_{\tau}\langle v,g_{-}\rangle-\sigma_{\tau}\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{-}\rangle.

Hence

\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}=\mu\lambda_{+}+\theta_{\tau}\langle v,g_{-}\rangle+\sigma_{\tau}\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{-}\rangle+\langle N(v),g_{-}\rangle.

(3.9)

Similarly, we have

\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}=-\mu\lambda_{-}-\theta_{\tau}\langle v,g_{+}\rangle-\sigma_{\tau}\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{+}\rangle-\langle N(v),g_{+}\rangle.

(3.10)

We differentiate the orthogonality condition
(v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0
with respect to
\tau
to obtain the parameter estimate. Using (
3.6
), we get

0=(v_{\tau},iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=(i\mathcal{L}v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}-\theta_{\tau}(iW,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}-\sigma_{\tau}(S_{-1}^{\prime}v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}-(iN(v),iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Combined with
(i\mathcal{L}v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})
and
(iN(v),iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})
, we obtain

\partial_{\tau}(\theta,\sigma)=O\left(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

(3.11)

Thus, we have
\theta_{\tau}\langle v,g_{\pm}\rangle=\sigma_{\tau}\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{\pm}\rangle=\langle N(v),g_{\pm}\rangle=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})
. Now plugging the above into (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we see that (
3.7
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Control by the linearized energy.

The following proposition shows that the orthogonal direction
\gamma

Proposition 3.5
.

(Control of orthogonal direction). For function
\gamma

\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\sim\langle\mathcal{L}\gamma,\gamma\rangle.

Proof.

By the defination of
\Phi

\Phi(\gamma)\gtrsim\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}

Hence in the subspace

using the decomposition of Proposition

\|v\|_{E}^{2}:=\mu(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}\gamma,\gamma\rangle\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}.

(3.12)

In particular, in the decomposition of Proposition

Henceforth, we assume that whenever a solution u of (

\displaystyle e^{-i\theta}S_{-1}^{-\sigma}u-W=v=\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}+\gamma=2\lambda_{1}g_{1}-2i\lambda_{2}g_{2}+\gamma,

\displaystyle 0=(v,iW)_{\dot{H}^{1}}=(v,\widetilde{W})_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\omega(g_{\pm},\gamma)=\langle g_{1},\gamma_{2}\rangle=\langle g_{2},\gamma_{1}\rangle,

\displaystyle\delta_{E}>d_{\mathcal{W}}(u)\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\sim\|v\|_{E},\;(\theta,\sigma)=\left(\widetilde{\theta}(u),\widetilde{\sigma}(u)\right),\;\dot{\tau}=e^{2\sigma}.

3.4.
Uniform local existence.

The following proposition ensures the existence of a solution
u
of (
1.1
) in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{W}
as long as the scaling parameter
\sigma
is bounded from above.

Proposition 3.6
.

(Uniform local existence in
\tau
). There exists an absolute constant
\delta_{L}\in(0,\delta_{E}/2)
such that for any solution
u
of (
1.1
) with
d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0))=:\delta\in\left[0,2\delta_{L}\right]
, we have
T_{\pm}(u)>3e^{-2\sigma(0)}=:T_{0}
,
\pm\left(\tau(\pm T_{0})-\tau(0)\right)>2
, and for
|t|\leq T_{0}
,

\delta_{E}>d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))\sim\delta,\;\;\sigma(t)=\sigma(0)+O(\delta).

Proof.

Let
u(0)\in B_{\delta}(\mathcal{W})
for some constant
\delta\in(0,\delta_{E})
, whose smallness will be required in the following.

By rotation and scaling, we may reduce to the case
\theta(0)=\sigma(0)=0
. Using the stability theory, we have: If
\|u(0)-W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0))=:\delta
small enough, then for
|t|\leq 3
,
u
exists and remains in
O(\delta)
neighborhood of
W
. This implies that
d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))\sim d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0))=\delta
for
|t|\leq 3
. Then by Proposition
3.4
, we have, for
|t|\leq 3

\dot{\tau}=e^{2\sigma},\;\;\dot{\sigma}=\dot{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}=e^{2\sigma}O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})=e^{2\sigma}O(d_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t)))=e^{2\sigma}O(\delta).

Integrating the second identity and using
\sigma(0)=0
, we have, for
|t|\leq 3
,

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma}=1+O(\delta),

which leads to

\tau(t)=\tau(0)+\left(1+O(\delta)\right)t,\;\;\text{for}\;\;|t|\leq 3.

In particular, if
\delta>0
is small enough,
\tau(t)
reaches
\tau(0)\pm 2
within
I(u)
.
∎

Now we are ready to define the nonlinear distance
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
. Let
\varphi\in B_{\delta_{E}}(\mathcal{W})

3.1
) of
\varphi
. Then we define a local distance

d_{0}(\varphi)^{2}:=E(\varphi)-E(W)+2\mu\lambda_{1}^{2}.

(3.14)

As observed in
[

]
, this is close to be convex in
\tau
when the solution is ejected out of a small neighborhood of
\mathcal{W}
, but it may have small oscillation around minima in
\tau
. This is a difference for the Schrödinger equation from the Klein-Gordon equation, for which

is strictly convex (see
[

Here instead, we introduce a dynamical mollification of

, which yields a strictly convex function in
\tau
. The same argument works in the subcritical setting as in
[
46
]
. Let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
) with intial data
u(0):=\varphi\in B_{2\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})

d_{1}(u(t))^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\tau)d_{0}^{2}(u(t-\tau))d\tau=\phi*d_{0}^{2}(u)(t),

where

\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi):=\phi_{\delta_{L}}\left({d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\right)d_{1}(\varphi)+\phi_{\delta_{L}}^{C}\left({d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\right)d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi).

(3.16)

3.5.
Nonlinear distance function.

The following proposition gives the main static properties of the distance function.

Proposition 3.7
.

(Nonlinear distance function). The function
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
on
\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}
is invariant for the rotation and scaling, and equivalent to
d_{\mathcal{W}}
. Precisely, there exists an absolute constant
C\in\left(1,\infty\right)
such that for all
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}
and
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)/C\leq\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)=\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}\varphi)\leq Cd_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi).

Moreover, there exists an absolute constant
c_{D}\in\left(0,1\right)
such that putting

\check{\mathcal{H}}:=\{\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}|E(\varphi)<E(W)+(c_{D}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi))^{2}\},

we have

\varphi\in B_{\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}\Longrightarrow\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\sim|\lambda_{1}|.

(3.17)

Proof.

First we proof
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}\sim d_{\mathcal{W}}
. If
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\geq 2\delta_{L}
, then
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}=d_{\mathcal{W}}
by the defination of
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)
. It remains to consider the case
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\leq 2\delta_{L}
. Since
\varphi\in B_{2\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})\subset B_{\delta_{E}}(\mathcal{W})
, we can decompose
\varphi
by Proposition
3.1
and
3.3
. By (
2.5
) and the scale invariance of

, we have

E(\varphi)-E(W)=E(W+v)-E(W)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle-C(v)=-\mu\lambda_{+}\lambda_{-}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}\gamma,\gamma\rangle-C(v).

(3.18)

Hence, using Proposition
3.5
and (
3.14
),

d_{0}(\varphi)^{2}=\|v\|_{E}^{2}-C(v)=\|v\|_{E}^{2}+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3})\sim d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)^{2}.

Then by Proposition
3.6
, we have
d_{1}(\varphi)\sim d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)
, and so
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\sim d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)
.

Next we prove (
3.17
). On the one hand, since
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)<\delta_{L}
and
E(\varphi)-E(W)<(c_{D}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi))^{2}
, we have

\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)^{2}\sim d_{0}(\varphi)^{2}=E(\varphi)-E(W)+2\mu\lambda_{1}^{2},

and so
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)^{2}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{2}
. On the other hand, we see that
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)^{2}\sim\|v\|_{E}^{2}\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}
by (
3.12

Finally, we check the invariance for the rotation and scaling. Let
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\varphi\in B_{\delta_{E}}(\mathcal{W})
and let
u
and

be the solutions of (
1.1
) with the initial data

u(0)=\varphi,\;\;u^{\prime}(0)=e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}\varphi,

with the decompositions by Proposition
3.1
and the rescaled time functions

\displaystyle u=

\displaystyle e^{i\theta}S_{-1}^{\sigma}(W+v),\;\;u^{\prime}=e^{i\theta^{\prime}}S_{-1}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(W+v^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\dot{\tau}=

\displaystyle e^{2\sigma},\;\;\dot{\tau}^{\prime}=e^{2\sigma^{\prime}},\;\;\tau(0)=0=\tau^{\prime}(0).

Then

e^{i\theta^{\prime}(0)}S_{-1}^{\sigma^{\prime}(0)}(W+v^{\prime}(0))=e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}\varphi=e^{i\alpha}e^{i\theta(0)}S_{-1}^{\beta}S_{-1}^{\sigma(0)}(W+v(0)),

that is

\theta^{\prime}(0)=\theta(0)+\alpha,\;\;\sigma^{\prime}(0)=\sigma(0)+\beta.

By the uniqueness of
\left(\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{\sigma}\right)
in Proposition
3.1
, we have
\left(\theta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\right)=\left(\theta,\sigma\right)+(\alpha,\beta)
.
Integrating the following identity

\dot{\tau}^{\prime}=e^{2\sigma^{\prime}}=e^{2\sigma+2\beta}=e^{2\beta}\dot{\tau},

we have
\tau^{\prime}=\tau e^{2\beta}
, while the invariance of the equation (
1.1
) implies
u^{\prime}(t)=e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}u(e^{2\beta}t)
. Hence
v
is invariant in the rescaled time, namely

\tau(t)=\tau^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\Longrightarrow v(t)=v^{\prime}(t^{\prime}),

which is inherited by
\lambda_{\pm},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
and
\gamma
. Therefore
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
are invariant, so is
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
.
∎

Hence we can use
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)
to measure the distance to
\mathcal{W}
, instead of the standard
d_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)
. The
\delta
neighborhood with respect to this distance function is denoted by

\widetilde{B}_{\delta}(\mathcal{W}):=\{\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}\;|\;\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)<\delta\}.

3.6.
Dynamics in the ejection mode.

The following proposition describes the dynamics close to the ground states in the ejection mode.

In order to verify that: the dynamics can be ruled by that of its unstable eigenmode of the linearized operator around
\mathcal{W}
just as the dynamics of solutions of linear differential equations. We have to control the orthogonal component
\gamma
of the spectral decomposition of the remainder resulting from the linearization around
\mathcal{W}
. We would like to control this component by using the quadratic terms resulting from the Taylor expansion of the energy around
\mathcal{W}
. This can be done if and only if the remainder satisfies two orthogonality conditions: see Proposition
3.5
. In order to satisfy these conditions, we have to give two degrees of freedom to the decomposition of the solution around
\mathcal{W}
: a rotation parameter (this was done in
[
46
]
) and a scaling parameter: see Propositions
3.1
and
3.4
. Then, we also have to control the evolution of these two parameters (see (
3.22
)). We prove in Proposition
3.8
that we can close the argument. More precisely, the dynamic of the solution close to
\mathcal{W}
and in the exit mode is dominated by the exponential growth of the unstable eigenmode. Moreover, a relevant functional
K
grows exponentially and its sign eventually becomes opposite to that of the eigenmode( see (
3.23
)).

Proposition 3.8
.

(Dynamics in the ejection mode). There is an absolute constant
\delta_{X}\in(0,1)
such that
\widetilde{B}_{\delta_{X}}(\mathcal{W})\subset B_{\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})
, and that for any solution
u
of (
1.1
) with

u(t_{0})\in\widetilde{B}_{\delta_{X}}(\mathcal{W})\cap\check{\mathcal{H}},\;\partial_{t}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{0}))\geq 0,

(3.19)

at some
t_{0}\in I(u)
, we have the following.

\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))
is increasing strictly until it reaches
\delta_{X}
at some
t_{X}\in(t_{0},T_{+}(u))
.

For all
t\in\left[t_{0},t_{X}\right]
, we have

\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))\sim|\lambda_{1}(t)|\sim e^{\mu(\tau(t)-\tau(t_{0}))}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{0})),

(3.20)

\|\gamma(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{0}))+\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))^{\frac{3}{2}},

(3.21)

\Big|\left(e^{i\theta(t)}-e^{i\theta(t_{0})},\sigma(t)-\sigma(t_{0})\right)\Big|\lesssim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t)),

(3.22)

sign(\lambda_{1}(t))
is a constant, and there exists an absolute constant
C_{K}>0
such that

-sign(\lambda_{1}(t))K(u(t))\gtrsim\left(e^{\mu(\tau(t)-\tau(t_{0}))}-C_{K}\right)\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{0}))

(3.23)

Proof.

Let
u
be a solution in the ejection mode (
3.19
) at
t=t_{0}\in I(u)
. Choosing
\delta_{X}
small enough ensures that
\widetilde{B}_{\delta_{X}}(\mathcal{W})\subset B_{\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})
. Then there are only two cases:

(1)

There exists a minimal
\tau_{X}>\tau_{0}
, such that
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)=\delta_{X}
at
\tau=\tau_{X}
.

(2)

For all
\tau\in(\tau_{0},\infty)
, we have
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)<\delta_{X}
.

Let
\tau_{X}:=\infty
in the second case, then in the both cases, we have
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)<\delta_{X}
for
\tau\in(\tau_{0},\tau_{X})
. Then
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)=d_{1}(u)\sim|\lambda_{1}|
on
\tau\in(\tau_{0},\tau_{X})
by (
3.16
). Hence, using (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and the equations (
3.8
) of
\lambda_{j}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)^{2}=

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}d_{1}(u)^{2}=\phi*\partial_{\tau}(d_{0}(u)^{2})=\phi*\partial_{\tau}(2\mu\lambda_{1}^{2})=\phi*[4\mu^{2}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+O(\|v\|_{E}^{3})],

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)^{2}=

\displaystyle\phi*[4\mu^{3}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})+O(\|v\|_{E}^{3})]+\phi^{\prime}*O(\|v\|_{E}^{3})\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}\sim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)^{2},

where we also used Proposition
3.6
to remove the convolution in the last step. Since
\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{0}))\geq 0
, the last estimate implies that
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)
is strictly increasing for
\tau\in(\tau_{0},\tau_{X})
. It also implies exponential growth in
\tau
of
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
, so it is impossible to have the the case
\tau_{X}=\infty
above. In other words, there exists
T_{X}<T_{+}(u)
such that
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)
reaches
\delta_{X}
at
\tau=\tau_{X}=\tau(T_{X})
.

Since
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}\sim|\lambda_{1}|
is positive continuous on
(\tau_{0},\tau_{X})
,
\lambda_{1}(\tau)
cannot change the sign. Let
\mathfrak{s}:=sign\lambda_{1}(\tau)\in\{\pm\}
be its sign.

Next we show the more precise exponential behavior. Since
\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)^{2}\geq 0
at
\tau=\tau_{0}
, there exists
\tau\in(\tau_{0}-2,\tau_{0}+2)
where
\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{1}^{2}\geq 0
. Combined with
\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\sim|\lambda_{1}|
and (
3.8
), we have
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\gtrsim-|\lambda_{1}|^{3}
. Since
\partial_{\tau}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}\sim\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})^{2}
for
|\tau-\tau_{0}|<2
, there exists
\tau_{1}\in(\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+2)
such that
\lambda_{1}(\tau_{1})\lambda_{2}(\tau_{1})\gtrsim-|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{1})|^{3}
, or equivalently
\mathfrak{s}\lambda_{2}(\tau_{1})\gtrsim-\lambda_{1}(\tau_{1})^{2}
. Then
\mathfrak{s}\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})\geq|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{1})|/2
and
\mathfrak{s}\lambda_{-}(\tau_{1})\geq 0
. Let
\widetilde{R}:=|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{1})|
, and suppose that for some
\tau_{2}\in(\tau_{1},\tau_{X})

\tau_{1}<\tau<\tau_{2}\Longrightarrow\;\;|\lambda_{1}(\tau)|\leq 2\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\lesssim\delta_{X}.

Then the equations (
3.7
) of
\lambda_{\pm}
together with
\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\sim|\lambda_{1}|
imply for
\tau\in(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
,

\lambda_{+}(\tau)e^{-\mu\tau}-\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})e^{-\mu\tau_{1}}=C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau}e^{-\mu s}\lambda_{1}^{2}(s)ds,

(3.24)

which implies

\displaystyle|\lambda_{+}(\tau)-e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})|=

\displaystyle Ce^{\mu\tau}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau}e^{-\mu s}\lambda_{1}^{2}(s)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{\mu\tau-2\mu\tau_{1}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau}e^{\mu s}\widetilde{R}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}^{2}e^{2\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\lesssim\delta_{X}\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}.

Similarly, we have

|\lambda_{-}(\tau)-e^{-\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\lambda_{-}(\tau_{1})|\lesssim\widetilde{R}^{2}e^{2\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\lesssim\delta_{X}\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}.

On the one hand, we have

|\lambda_{1}(\tau)|\sim|\lambda_{+}(\tau)|\lesssim\delta_{X}\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}+|\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})|e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\\
\lesssim\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}(1+\delta_{X}).

On the other hand, since

|\lambda_{+}(\tau)|\geq|\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})|e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}-C\delta_{X}\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})},\;\;|\lambda_{-}(\tau)|\leq e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\left(|\lambda_{-}(\tau_{1})|+C\widetilde{R}\delta_{X}\right),

thus we have

|\lambda_{1}(\tau)|\gtrsim|\lambda_{+}(\tau)|-|\lambda_{-}(\tau)|\gtrsim e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}\left(|\lambda_{+}(\tau_{1})|-C\widetilde{R}\delta_{X}-|\lambda_{-}(\tau_{1})|-C\widetilde{R}\delta_{X}\right)\gtrsim\widetilde{R}e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{1})}.

Hence the continuity in
\tau
allows us to take
\tau_{2}=\tau_{X}
. Moreover the above estimates together with
|\lambda_{1}|\sim\widetilde{R}
on
(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})
implies that, with
R:=\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(\tau_{0}))
,

\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq\tau_{X}\Longrightarrow\;\;\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}\sim\mathfrak{s}\lambda_{1}\sim Re^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}.

In order to estimate
\gamma
, consider the expansion of the energy (
3.18
) without the
\gamma
terms. We denote this expansion by
E_{\gamma^{\bot}}
:

E_{\gamma^{\perp}}(u):=-\mu\lambda_{+}\lambda_{-}-C(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}).

Notice that
C^{\prime}(f)=N(f)
. By this observation, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we see that

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u)=

\displaystyle\langle N(v)-N(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}),g_{+}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}

\displaystyle+\langle N(v)-N(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}),g_{-}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}

\displaystyle+\theta_{\tau}\left(\langle v,g_{-}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}+\langle v,g_{+}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}\right)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{\tau}\left(\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{-}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}+\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{+}\rangle\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}\right).

Since

\langle N(v)-N(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-}),g_{\pm}\rangle\lesssim\lambda_{1}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},\;\;\langle v,g_{\pm}\rangle=\langle iv,S_{1}^{\prime}g_{\pm}\rangle=O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}),

this together with
|\lambda_{1}|\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, (
3.7
) and (
3.11
) implies that, for
\tau\in\left[\tau_{0},\tau_{X}\right]
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\tau}\left(E(u)-E(W)-E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u)\right)|=

\displaystyle|\partial_{\tau}E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u)|

(3.25)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\left(\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}+\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}\right)+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{+}+\partial_{\tau}\lambda_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\lambda_{1}^{3}.

Moreover, using Sobolev’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we see that

|C(v)-C(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-})|\lesssim|\lambda_{1}|^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

By (
2.5
) and Proposition
3.5
,

\displaystyle E(u)-E(W)-E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}\gamma,\gamma\rangle+C(\lambda_{+}g_{+}+\lambda_{-}g_{-})-C(v)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+O(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}).

Combined with (
3.20
) and (
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}O(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\lambda_{1}^{3})=

\displaystyle E(u)-E(W)-E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u)-\left(E(u(\tau_{0}))-E(W)-E_{\gamma^{\bot}}(u(\tau_{0}))\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+O(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})-\|\gamma(\tau_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-O(|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|^{2}\|\gamma(\tau_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}).

By continuity method, we have

\displaystyle\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{3}+\|\gamma(\tau_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-O(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})+O(|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|^{2}\|\gamma(\tau_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{3}+|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|^{2}+|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{3}+|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|^{2}.

Thus we have

\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{3/2}+|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|\sim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(\tau))^{3/2}+\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(\tau_{0})),

this proves (
3.21
).

Using (
3.11
) and
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)\sim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, we obtain
\pm\theta_{\tau}=O(\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u))
. Integrating in
\tau
, we have

|\theta(\tau)-\theta(\tau_{0})|\sim\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}Re^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}\lesssim Re^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}\sim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u).

Similarly, we get
|\sigma(\tau)-\sigma(\tau_{0})|\lesssim\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u)
. Thus we obtain (
3.22
).

Next we show (
3.23
). Using
L_{+}W=2\Delta W
, (
2.5
) and Proposition
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle K(W+v)=

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla W,\nabla v\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})

(3.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+\langle 2\Delta W,\gamma\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}).

This together with Proposition
3.1
,
\langle W,g_{2}\rangle>0
(see (
3.3
)),
|\langle\Delta W,\gamma\rangle|\lesssim\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
as well as the above estimates on
\lambda_{1},\gamma
,

\displaystyle-sign(\lambda_{1}(t))K(u(t))=

\displaystyle-sign(\lambda_{1}(t))K(W+v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-sign(\lambda_{1}(t))\left(-2\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+\langle 2\Delta W,\gamma\rangle+O(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|-\|\gamma\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+O(\lambda_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|-|\lambda_{1}(\tau_{0})|

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\left(e^{\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}-C_{K}\right)\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(\tau_{0})).

Thus (
3.23
) is proved.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The following proposition gives a variational estimate away from the ground states.

Proposition 3.10
.

(Variational estimates). There exist two increasing function
\epsilon_{V}
and
\kappa
from

to
(0,1)
, and an absolute constant
c_{V}>0
, such that for any

satisfying

, we have

, where

This is proved in the same way as

.

3.7.
Sign functional.

The following proposition defines a functional
\Theta
that decides the fate of the solution around
t=T_{\pm}(u)
, as well as at the exit time
t=t_{X}
in the above proposition. The continuity of
\Theta
implies that for any solution
u
in
\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}
,
\Theta(u)\in\{\pm 1\}
can change along
t\in I(u)
only if
u
goes through the small neighborhood
\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon}\setminus\check{\mathcal{H}}\subset\widetilde{B}_{\epsilon/{c_{D}}}(\mathcal{W})
.

Proposition 3.11
.

(Sign functional). There exist an absolute constant
\epsilon_{S}\in(0,1)
and a continuous function
\Theta:\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
, such that for some
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\delta_{X}
and for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
, with the convention
sign0=+1
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\geq\delta_{1}\Longrightarrow\Theta(\varphi)=signK(\varphi),\\
&\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\leq\delta_{2}\Longrightarrow\Theta(\varphi)=-sign\lambda_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.28)

with the following properties.

(1)

For all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\Theta(e^{i\alpha}S_{-1}^{\beta}\varphi)=\Theta(\varphi)
.

(2)

Moreover, if
E(\varphi)<E(W)
, then
\Theta(\varphi)=signK(\varphi)
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
\Theta
is well-defined and continous on
\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
. We have the following assertions. On the one hand, Proposition
3.10
implies that: if
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{V}(\delta)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon}\setminus\widetilde{B}_{\delta}(\mathcal{W})
, then
signK(\varphi)
is a constant. On the other hand, Proposition
3.7
implies that: if
\varphi\in B_{\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
, then
sign\lambda_{1}
is a constant.

Hence, after fixing
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\epsilon_{S}
such that
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}\ll\delta_{X}
and
\epsilon_{S}\leq\epsilon_{V}(\delta_{1})
, the functional
\Theta
is well-defined and continuous on
\check{\mathcal{H}}^{\epsilon_{S}}:=\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
by (
3.28
), once we prove that
-sign\lambda_{1}=signK
on

Y:=\{\varphi\in\check{\mathcal{H}}^{\epsilon_{S}}|\;\delta_{1}\leq\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\leq\delta_{2}\}.

To this end, take any solution
u
of (
1.1
) with initial data
u(0)\in Y
. By applying Proposition
3.8
either forward or backward in time, there exists
t_{X}\in I(u)
such that
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{X}))=\delta_{X}
and
\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))\in[\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0)),\delta_{X}]\subset[\delta_{1},\delta_{X}]
when
t\in[0,t_{X}]
. Thus
u(t)\in\check{\mathcal{H}}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap B_{\delta_{L}}(\mathcal{W})\setminus\widetilde{B}_{\delta_{1}}(\mathcal{W})
when
t\in[0,t_{X}]
. Hence
sign\lambda_{1}(u(t))
and
signK(u(t))
are unchanged when
t\in[0,t_{X}]
using the initial assertion. Combined (
3.23
) with
\delta_{2}\ll\delta_{X}
, we have

\displaystyle-sign(\lambda_{1}(t_{X}))K(u(t_{X}))\gtrsim

\displaystyle\left(e^{\mu(\tau(t_{X})-\tau(0))}-C_{K}\right)\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0))

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t_{X}))-C_{K}\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(0))\geq\delta_{X}-\delta_{2}C_{K}>0.

Thus
-sign\lambda_{1}(u(t_{X}))=signK(u(t_{X}))
. Consequently,
-sign\lambda_{1}=signK
on
Y
.

Next, we proved that
\Theta=signK
on
\mathcal{H}^{0}
. Since
0\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
, we have
\Theta(0)=signK(0)=+1
. It suffices to consider: if
E(u)<E(W)
and
u\neq 0
, then
\Theta(u)=signK(u)
. By (
2.3
), we have
K(u)\neq 0
. By

\lambda\partial_{\lambda}E(\lambda u)=\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\lambda^{4}\iint\frac{\big|u(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|u(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy=K(\lambda u),

there is a unique
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for
0<\lambda_{-}<\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{+}<\infty

K(\lambda_{-}u)>0=K(\lambda_{0}u)>K(\lambda_{+}u).

If
K(u)>0
, then
\partial_{\lambda}E(\lambda u)\geq\lambda\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\lambda\|\left(|x|^{-4}*|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}\geq 0
if
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Thus we have
E(\lambda u)\leq E(u)<E(W)
, which implies that
\{\lambda u\}_{0\leq\lambda\leq 1}
is a
C^{0}
curve in
\mathcal{H}^{0}\subset\check{\mathcal{H}}
connecting
u
and 0. Hence by continuity
\Theta(u)=+1=signK(u)
.

If
K(u)<0
, then
\partial_{\lambda}E(\lambda u)<\lambda\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\lambda^{3}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq 0
if
1\leq\lambda<\infty
. Thus we have
E(\lambda u)\leq E(u)<E(W)
, which implies that
\{\lambda u\}_{1\leq\lambda<\infty}
is a
C^{0}
curve in
\mathcal{H}^{0}\subset\check{\mathcal{H}}
connecting
u
with the region
E(u)<0
, where
\mathcal{W}
is far and so
\Theta=signK=-1
. Hence by continuity
\Theta(u)=-1=signK(u)
.

Finally, the invariance of
\Theta
for the rotation and scaling follows from that of
K
and
\lambda_{1}
. The latter being proved in the proof of Proposition
3.7
, and the former is obviously easy to prove.
∎

Note that the region
\{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}|\;\Theta(\varphi)=+1\}
is bounded in
\dot{H}^{1}
, because

\left\{\begin{aligned} &K(\varphi)\geq 0\Longrightarrow\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq 4E(\varphi)\leq 4(E(W)+\epsilon_{S}^{2}),\\
&\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(\varphi)\leq\delta_{X}\Longrightarrow\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+C\delta_{X},\end{aligned}\right.

3.8.
One-pass lemma.

The one-pass lemma implies that every solution can enter and exit a small neighborhood of the ground states
W
at most one time, which is the most crucial step in proving Theorem

. It precludes “almost homoclinic orbits”, i.e., those solutions starting in, the moving away from, and finally returning to, a small neighborhood of the ground states. Indeed, this part of analysis is the main part of this paper.

Proposition 3.12
.

(One-pass). There exist an absolute constant
\delta_{B}\in(0,\delta_{X})
and an increasing function

for

\exists t_{+}(\delta)\in\left(t_{0},T_{+}(u)\right],s.t.\left\{\begin{aligned} &t_{0}\leq t<t_{+}(\delta)\Longrightarrow\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))<\delta,\\
&t_{+}(\delta)<t<T_{+}(u)\Longrightarrow\widetilde{d}_{\mathcal{W}}(u(t))>\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.30)

In the case where

after ejection, we introduce a threshold

, which allows us to compare

with the main part of the virial identity. After integrating the virial identity, we can prove that this threshold must be very large; By proving a decay estimate, we can show that this threshold is not so large. This leads to a contradiction.

In the case where

) and the hyperbolic parameter (see definition of

This proposition will be proved in Section

t\in I_{H}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;K\left(\phi_{R}u\right)=-2\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right).

(4.7)

Secondly, using the decomposition (
3.5
) and Hardy’s inequality, we have: for
t\in I_{H}
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varOmega_{1}}\frac{\big|u(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|u(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|x|\geq R)}^{2}+\|\frac{u}{|x|}\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|W+v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|y|\geq\widetilde{R})}^{2}+\|W+v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|y|\geq\widetilde{R})}^{4}\lesssim\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-1}+\lambda_{1}^{2}.

Finally, putting (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) into (
4.4
), we have

t\in I_{H}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\ddot{V}_{R}(t)=-16\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right).

Then using the hyperbolic dynamics of
\lambda_{1}
and
\dot{\tau}=e^{2\sigma}\sim e^{2\sigma(s)}
, we obtain, with some absolute constant
C_{H}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{}_{\partial I[s]}=

\displaystyle\int_{I[s]}16\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle-O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\int_{I[s]}\lambda_{1}-O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\left(\delta_{M}-C_{H}\langle\widetilde{R}(s)\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\left(\delta_{M}-C_{H}\left(R_{H}/R\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right).

Thus if

R>R_{X}:=\frac{4C_{H}^{2}}{\delta_{M}^{2}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{\delta_{M}}{\delta}\right)\right)^{2}R_{H},

then for any
s\in\mathscr{L},t\in I[s]
,

\int_{I[s]}-\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt=[-\dot{V}_{R}(t)]_{\partial I[s]}\gtrsim e^{-2\sigma(s)}\delta_{M}/2.

Then by the defination of (
4.2
), we have

\int_{I_{H}}-\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt\gtrsim R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}.

(4.9)

Next, we consider
\ddot{V}_{R}
on
I_{V}
. Since
\widetilde{d}(t)\geq\delta_{V}
on
I_{V}
and
u(t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
, Proposition
3.11
implies
-1=\Theta(u(t))=signK(u(t))
. Thus
K(u(t))<0
.

In order to estimate
\ddot{V}_{R}
with
K(u)
on
I_{V}
, the optimal cut-off radius is given by

R_{V}^{-}:=\sup\{m>0\;\Big|\;\int_{I_{V}}\left(\int_{|x|>m}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varOmega^{\prime}_{1}}\frac{\big|u(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|u(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy\right)dt\leq 0\},

where
\varOmega^{\prime}_{1}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;|x|\leq m,|y|\leq m\}
.
K(u)<0
on
I_{V}
implies
R_{V}^{-}>0
, while the Sobolev inequality implies that
R_{V}^{-}<\infty
.

In order to bound
R_{V}^{-}
, we use the equation
\partial_{t}|u|^{2}=2\mathrm{Im}\left(\nabla\left(\nabla u\cdot\bar{u}\right)\right)
.

First, we give the estimate on
\sup_{t\in I_{V}}\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}
. On the one hand, for any interval
J\subset I_{V},R\geq\mathcal{I}_{V}^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{\partial J}=

\displaystyle\int\int_{J}\frac{\phi_{R/2}^{C}}{r^{2}}2\mathrm{Im}\left(\nabla\left(\nabla u\cdot\bar{u}\right)\right)dtdx=-2\mathrm{Im}\int_{|x|>\frac{R}{2}}\int_{J}\left(\nabla u\bar{u}\right)\nabla\left(\frac{\phi_{R/2}^{C}}{r^{2}}\right)dtdx

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{J}\int_{|x|>\frac{R}{2}}\frac{|uu_{r}|}{r^{3}}dxdt\leq\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|>\frac{R}{2}}\frac{R^{2}}{\mathcal{I}_{V}r^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\left(|u_{r}|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}\right)dxdt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{V}}\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|>\frac{R}{2}}\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(|u_{r}|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}\right)dxdt.

On the other hand, we have from (
4.8
),

t\in\partial I_{V}\Longrightarrow\int_{|x|>R/2}|\frac{u}{r}|^{2}dx\lesssim\langle\frac{Re^{\sigma(t)}}{2}\rangle^{-1}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\frac{R_{H}}{R}+\delta_{M}^{2}.

Hence, by (
4.6
), there exists
R\sim\max(R_{H},\mathcal{I}_{V}^{1/2})
such that

\sup_{t\in I_{V}}\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}\ll 1.

(4.11)

Next, it is obvious that

\|r^{\frac{d-2}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|>R)}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}.

For any
R\geq 0
and
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}
, we have

\int_{|x|>R}|u|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}dx\lesssim\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{2}\|r^{\frac{d-2}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\lesssim\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{2d-2}{d-2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{2}{d-2}}.

Using (
4.11
), we have: for
t\in I_{V}
,

\displaystyle\iint_{|x|>R}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{2}|u(t,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\cdot>R}u\|_{L^{2d/(d-2)}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{2d-2}{d}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{2}{d}+2}\ll\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{2}{d}+2},

which implies

\int_{|x|>R}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varOmega_{1}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{2}|u(t,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy\gg 0.

(4.12)

By the definition of
R_{V}^{-}
,

R_{V}^{-}\leq R\sim\max(R_{H},\mathcal{I}_{V}^{1/2}).

(4.13)

Since
u(t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon},\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{V}(\delta_{V})
and
\widetilde{d}(t)\geq\delta_{V}
on
I_{V}
, Proposition
3.10
implies

t\in I_{V}\Longrightarrow-K(u)\geq\kappa(\delta_{V})\geq c_{0}(\delta_{V})\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},

(4.14)

for some constant
c_{0}(\delta_{V})>0
.
For any
R\geq R_{V}^{-},t\in I_{V}
, we have from (
4.4
) and (
4.14
),

\displaystyle-\ddot{V}_{R}(t)=

\displaystyle-8\int_{|x|\leq R}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+8\iint_{\varOmega_{1}}\frac{\big|u(t,x)\big|^{2}\big|u(t,y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy+O(E_{R})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-8K(u)+O(E_{R})\geq 8c_{0}(\delta_{V})\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+O(E_{R}).

Since

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}E_{R}(t)dt=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varOmega_{1}}\frac{\big|u(x)\big|^{2}\big|u(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}dxdy\right)dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\left(\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(R\leq|x|\leq 2R)}^{2}+\|\frac{u}{|x|}\|_{L^{2}(R\leq|x|\leq 2R)}^{2}\right)dt=:A+B,

then by (
4.6
), (
4.12
) and
\mathcal{I}_{V}\sim\int_{I_{V}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}dt
, we have

\displaystyle A\ll

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|x|>R)}^{2}dt\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{V},

\displaystyle B\sim

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 2R}\frac{R}{r}\left(|\frac{u}{|x|}|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)dxdt\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{I}_{V}}{\ln(R_{1}/R_{0})}.

Hence for any
R_{0}>R_{V}^{-}
, there exists
R\in\left(R_{0},C_{1}(\delta_{V})R_{0}\right)
for some constant
C_{1}(\delta_{V})>1
such that

\int_{I_{V}}-\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt\geq 8c_{0}(\delta_{V})\int_{I_{V}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}dt+\int_{I_{V}}O(E_{R})dt\gtrsim c_{0}(\delta_{V})\mathcal{I}_{V}.

The minimal
R
satisfying this and (
4.9
) satisfies

R\leq C_{1}(\delta_{V})\max\left(R_{V}^{-},R_{X}\right),

for which (
4.5
) with
[t_{a},t_{b}]=I_{H}\cup I_{V}
implies

R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}+c_{0}(\delta_{V})\mathcal{I}_{V}\lesssim[-\dot{V}_{R}]_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}\lesssim\frac{R\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}\leq\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{1}(\delta_{V})\left(R_{V}^{-}+R_{X}\right).

(4.15)

Now we impose an upper bound on
\delta
by the condition

\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{1}(\delta_{V})R_{X}\ll R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}.

(4.16)

Then the last term in (
4.15
) is absorbed by the first one, hence

R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}+c_{0}(\delta_{V})\mathcal{I}_{V}\lesssim\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{1}(\delta_{V})R_{V}^{-}.

(4.17)

Imposing another upper bound on
\delta
by the condition

\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{1}(\delta_{V})\ll c_{0}(\delta_{V})\mathcal{I}_{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.18)

Combined with (
4.13
), we see that (
4.18
) contradicts (
4.17
).

In conclusion, the smallness conditions on
\delta,\epsilon
in the case
\Theta=-1
are (
4.1
), (
4.16
) and (
4.18
), which determine
\delta_{B}
and
\epsilon_{B}
.

4.5.
Scattering region.

Now we consider the case
\Theta(u)=+1
, where the solution will scatter. The argument is similar to that in the previous case, but more involved. In particular, we need several smallness conditions on
\delta_{M}
.

It can be observed that there exists an absolute constant
C_{E}\sim 1
such that

1/C_{E}\leq\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{E}

(4.19)

for all
t\in I(u)
.

Indeed, it is obvious that
E(u)\sim E(W)\sim 1
. On the one hand, by (
2.4
), we have
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq 4E(u)\lesssim 1
. On the other hand,
E(\varphi)\sim\|\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}
for small
\varphi\in\dot{H}^{1}
, then
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\geq 1/C_{E}
.

First, we consider
\ddot{V}_{R}
on
I_{H}
. The proof is exactly the same as before, that is,

t\in I_{H}\;\Longrightarrow\;\ddot{V}_{R}(t)=-16\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right).

Then using the hyperbolic dynamics of
\lambda_{1}
and
\dot{\tau}=e^{2\sigma}\sim e^{2\sigma(s)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{\partial I[s]}=

\displaystyle\int_{I[s]}-16\mu\lambda_{1}\langle W,g_{2}\rangle+O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\int_{I[s]}-\lambda_{1}+O\left(\widetilde{d}(s)+\langle\widetilde{R}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\left(\delta_{M}-C_{H}\langle\widetilde{R}(s)\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma(s)}\left(\delta_{M}-C_{H}\left(R_{H}/R\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right).

Thus if
R>R_{X}
, then for any
s\in\mathscr{L},t\in I[s]
,

\int_{I[s]}\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt=[\dot{V}_{R}(t)]_{\partial I[s]}\gtrsim e^{-2\sigma(s)}\delta_{M}/2.

Then by the defination of (
4.2
), we have

R>R_{X}\Longrightarrow\int_{I_{H}}\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt\gtrsim R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}.

(4.20)

Next, we consider
\ddot{V}_{R}
on
I_{V}
. Since
\widetilde{d}(t)\geq\delta_{V}
on
I_{V}
and
u(t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon_{S}}\cap\check{\mathcal{H}}
, Proposition
3.11
implies
+1=\Theta(u(t))=signK(u(t))
. Thus
K(u(t))\geq 0
.

In order to estimate
\ddot{V}_{R}
with
K\left(\phi_{R}u\right)
on
I_{V}
. Since
u(t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\epsilon},\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{V}(\delta_{V})
and
\widetilde{d}(t)\geq\delta_{V}
on
I_{V}
, Proposition
3.10
implies

K(u(t))\geq\min\{\kappa(\widetilde{d}(t)),c_{V}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\}\geq\min\{\kappa(\delta_{V}),c_{V}/C_{E}\}=:\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V}).

By (
4.19
), if
t\in I_{V},4\epsilon^{2}\leq\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})
, then

\displaystyle I\left(\phi_{R}u\right)\leq

\displaystyle I(u)<E(W)+\epsilon^{2}-K(u)/2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(W)+\epsilon^{2}-\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})/2\leq I(W)-\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})/4.

By Proposition
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle K(\phi_{R}u)\geq

\displaystyle\|\phi_{R}u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|\left(|x|^{-4}*|\phi_{R}u|^{2}\right)|\phi_{R}u|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}\|\left(|x|^{-4}*|W|^{2}\right)|W|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}}{\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}}\right)

(4.21)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\phi_{R}u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\left(\|\left(|x|^{-4}*|W|^{2}\right)|W|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}-\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\right)^{1/2}}{\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\|\phi_{R}u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\geq\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|x|<R)}^{2}.

In order to decide the cut-off for
I_{V}
, we introduce

R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M}):=\inf\{m>0\Big|\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|<m}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt\geq\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|\}\in(0,\infty).

As above,

\int_{I_{V}}E_{R}(t)dt=A+B,\;\;A\ll\mathcal{I}_{V},\;\;B\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{I}_{V}}{\ln(R_{1}/R_{0})}.

Using (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and (
4.21
), for any
R_{0}\geq R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})
, there exists
R\in\left(R_{0},C_{2}(\delta_{V})R_{0}\right)
for some constant
C_{2}(\delta_{V})>1
such that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\ddot{V}_{R}(t)dt=\int_{I_{V}}8K(\phi_{R}u)+O(E_{R})dt\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{I_{V}}\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|x|<R)}^{2}+E_{R}\;dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\int_{I_{V}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(|x|<R)}^{2}dt\geq\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

Note that
\delta_{V}
depends on
\delta_{M}
through the condition
\delta_{V}\ll\delta_{M}
in (
4.1
), which allows us to determine
C_{2}
in terms of
\delta_{V}
only.

The minimal
R>R_{0}
satisfying both this and (
4.20
) must satisfy

R\leq C_{2}(\delta_{V})\max\left(R_{V}^{+}(\delta),R_{X}\right),

for which (
4.5
) implies

R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}+\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|\lesssim[\dot{V}_{R}(t)]_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}\lesssim\frac{R\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}\leq\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{2}(\delta_{V})\left(R_{V}^{+}(\delta)+R_{X}\right).

(4.22)

Now we impose an upper bound on
\delta
by the condition

\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{2}(\delta_{V})R_{X}\ll R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M},

which is equivalent to

\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{2}(\delta_{V})\left(C_{H}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right)^{2}\ll R_{H}\delta_{M}^{3}.

(4.23)

Then the last term in (
4.22
) is absorbed by the first one, combined with (
4.23
), we have

R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}+\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|\lesssim\frac{\delta}{e^{\sigma(t)}}C_{2}(\delta_{V})R_{V}^{+}(\delta)\ll R_{H}\delta_{M}^{3}\left(\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right)^{-2}R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M}).

Imposing another upper bound on
\delta
by

\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})^{1/2}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\gg e^{1/\delta_{M}^{3}},

(4.24)

yields

R_{H}^{2}+|I_{V}|\leq\frac{R_{H}^{2}}{\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})\delta_{M}^{2}}+|I_{V}|\ll\frac{R_{H}R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})}{\left(\widetilde{\kappa}(\delta_{V})^{1/2}\ln\left(\delta_{M}/\delta\right)\right)^{2}}\ll R_{H}R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})e^{-2/\delta_{M}^{3}},

thus

R_{H}+|I_{V}|\ll e^{-2/\delta_{M}^{3}}R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M}).

(4.25)

First, we compare
R_{H}
with
|I_{V}|
. By
\mathcal{I}_{V}\sim\int_{I_{V}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}dt
, (
4.19
), (
4.25
) and (
4.6
) with
R_{0}:=R_{H}+|I_{V}|,R_{1}:=R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})/2
, then there exists
R\in(R_{0},R_{1})
such that

\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|>R}\frac{R}{r}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}+\left(|x|^{-4}*|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq\frac{\mathcal{I}_{V}}{\ln(R_{1}/R_{0})}\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

(4.26)

Using Hardy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|<2R)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\phi_{R}u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(R<|x|<2R)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(R<|x|<2R)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|<2R)}.

By the defination of
R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})
and
R_{V}^{+}(\delta_{M})>2R
, we have
\int_{I_{V}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|<2R)}^{2}dt<\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|
. Using (
4.26
), we have

\int_{I_{V}}\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(R<|x|<2R)}^{2}\sim\int_{I_{V}}\int_{R<|x|<2R}\frac{R}{r}|\frac{u}{r}|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

Combining the above estimates yieds

\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|<2R}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}\right)dxdt\lesssim\int_{I_{V}}\|\frac{u}{r}\|_{L^{2}(R<|x|<2R)}^{2}dt+\int_{I_{V}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|<2R)}^{2}dt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

By
E(u)\gtrsim 0
, we have

\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|<2R}\left(|x|^{-4}*|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\int_{I_{V}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|<2R)}^{2}dt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

Thus we have

\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|<2R}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}+\left(|x|^{-4}*|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}\right)dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|.

(4.27)

If
R_{H}^{2}\gg\delta_{M}^{2}|I_{V}|
, then by (
4.4
), (
4.27
) and
R_{H}<R
, we have

\int_{I_{V}}|\ddot{V}_{R_{H}}|dt\lesssim\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|\leq 2R}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}+\left(|x|^{-4}*|u|^{2}\right)|u|^{2}\right)dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}|I_{V}|\ll R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M}.

Noting that
R_{X}\ll R_{H}
by (
4.24
), using the above estimate, (
4.5
) and (
4.20
), we have

\frac{\delta R_{H}}{e^{\sigma(t)}}\gtrsim[\dot{V}_{R_{H}}]_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}=\int_{I_{H}}\ddot{V}_{R_{H}}dt+\int_{I_{V}}\ddot{V}_{R_{H}}dt\gtrsim R_{H}^{2}\delta_{M},

which contradicts
\delta\ll\delta_{M}
. Therefore
R_{H}^{2}\lesssim\delta_{M}^{2}|I_{V}|
.

Next, we estimate
\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(I_{V}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. On the one hand, for any interval
J\subset I_{V},R>R_{0}>|I_{V}|^{1/2}
, using (
4.26
) and the same argument as (
4.10
) , we have

[\langle|u/r|^{2},\phi_{R}^{C}\rangle]_{\partial J}\lesssim\frac{1}{|I_{V}|}\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|>R}\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(|u_{r}|^{2}+|u/r|^{2}\right)dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}^{3}.

On the other hand, we have from (
4.8
) and
R_{H}^{2}\lesssim\delta_{M}^{2}|I_{V}|
,

t\in\partial I_{V}\Longrightarrow\int_{|x|>R}|u/r|^{2}dx\lesssim\frac{R_{H}}{R}+\delta_{M}^{2}<\frac{R_{H}}{|I_{V}|^{1/2}}+\delta_{M}^{2}\lesssim\delta_{M}.

Combining the above two estimates yields

\sup_{t\in I_{V}}\|u/r\|_{L^{2}(|x|>2R)}^{2}\lesssim\delta_{M}.

Using above and (
4.27
), we obtain

\int_{I_{V}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u/r|^{2}dxdt=\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|<2R}|u/r|^{2}dxdt+\int_{I_{V}}\int_{|x|>2R}|u/r|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}|I_{V}|.

Decomposing
I_{V}
into its connected components, we obtain an interval
I\subset I_{V}
such that
\partial I\subset\partial I_{V}
and

\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u/r|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\delta_{M}|I|.

(4.28)

Using (
4.28
), we can achieve Bourgain’s energy induction method
[

Lemma 5.5

We divide the proof into three steps:

Step 1:

exists for all time

We prove it by contradiction. Using the local well-posedness theory and Lemma
5.4

\sigma_{c}(t)\to\infty
as

[
37
]
for a proof. For any

y_{m}(t):=\langle|U_{c}(t)|^{2},\phi_{m}\rangle.

Since

is precompact in

e^{-2\sigma_{c}}\phi_{me^{\sigma_{c}}}\to 0

, we have

y_{m}(t)=\langle|S_{-1}^{-\sigma_{c}}U_{c}|^{2},e^{-2\sigma_{c}}\phi_{me^{\sigma_{c}}}\rangle\to 0,\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\nearrow T_{+}(U_{c}).

Using
\partial_{t}|u|^{2}=2\mathrm{Im}\left(\nabla\left(\nabla u\cdot\bar{u}\right)\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{m}(t)=

\displaystyle\int\partial_{t}|U_{c}(t)|^{2}\phi_{m}dx=2\mathrm{Im}\int\phi_{m}\nabla\left(\nabla U_{c}\cdot\bar{U_{c}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mathrm{Im}\int\nabla\phi_{m}\nabla U_{c}\bar{U_{c}}dx=-2\langle\nabla U_{c},iU_{c}\nabla\phi_{m}\rangle.

By Hardy’s inequality, we have

-\dot{y}_{m}(t)\leq|\dot{y}_{m}(t)|\leq 2\int|\nabla U_{c}||U_{c}||\nabla\phi_{m}|dx\lesssim\|U_{c}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\lesssim 1.

Integrating it on

and using

, we have

\int|U_{c}(t)|^{2}\phi_{m}dx=y_{m}(t)\lesssim|T_{+}(U_{c})-t|.

Sending

, we obtain

\|U_{c}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim|T_{+}(U_{c})-t|,

which implies that

\displaystyle-

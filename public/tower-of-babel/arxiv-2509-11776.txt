A Poisson representation of the positive sojourn time of Lévy processes
Helmut H. Pitters

Abstract
We study the distribution of the positive sojourn time A t ≔ ∫ 0 t 𝟏 { X s > 0 } d s A_{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}\{X_{s}>0\}ds of an arbitrary Lévy process X ≔ ( X t ) t ≥ 0 X\coloneqq(X_{t})_{t\geq 0} . For an exponential random variable E ( q ) E^{(q)} of rate q > 0 q>0 independent of X X we show the representation in law A E ( q ) = ∑ T ∈ Π d ⁡ T \displaystyle A_{E^{(q)}}=_{d}\sum_{T\in\Pi}T as the sum of points of a Poisson process Π \Pi with intensity given explicitely in terms of the positivity t ↦ ℙ { X t > 0 } t\mapsto\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\} . This representation raises some fundamental questions, not least because Π \Pi turns out to be intimately connected to the celebrated Poisson-Dirichlet distribution. Moreover, we characterise A t A_{t} by working out its double Laplace transform, and thus complement a recent result in which the distribution of A t A_{t} was characterised via its higher moments. As a Corollary of the Poisson representation, in the special cases where X X is Brownian motion, a symmetric stable process, a Lévy process with constant positivity, we obtain an extension and new derivation of classical (generalised) arcsine laws going back to Lévy (1939), Kac (1951), and Getoor and Sharpe (1994), respectively. Even in these cases the Poisson representation is new. As an application, if X X is the ( 1 / 2 ) (1/2) -stable subordinator with drift, we obtain both the Laplace transform of A t A_{t} and the density of its distribution. This is the second example of a Lévy process whose occupation time distribution is known explicitely but is not generalized arcsine, the first example being Brownian motion with drift that was studied earlier in the context of option prizing.

2.1
Characterization of positive sojourn time of Lévy processes

Our first result is a characterization of the positive sojourn time

for abritrary Lévy processes

Proposition 1
.

Consider the positive sojourn time
A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}\{X_{s}>0\}ds
up until time

The Laplace transform of

is uniquely determined by

\displaystyle G(q,\lambda)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\exp(-\lambda A_t)dt\right]

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}t^{-1}\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}(1-e^{-\lambda t})dt\right);\hskip 18.49988ptq,\lambda>0.

(8)

As a Corollary we recover the generalised arcsine law for Lévy processes with constant positivity due to Getoor and Sharpe
[
5

. We say that a real random variable is gamma distributed with scale parameter

Corollary 1
.

Consider the setting of Proposition
1

(i)

\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}=c

t>0
.

(ii)

There exists

A_{t}/t\sim\Arcsin(c)

(iii)

(iv)

(v)

Remark 1

If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[A_{E^{(q)}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}e^{-qt}dt\leq q^{-1},\hskip 18.49988pt\Var\left(A_{E^{(q)}}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}te^{-qt}dt\leq 1.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\exp(-\lambda A_t^{(-\mu)})dt\right]

(17)

respectively, where

and

\displaystyle g(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(2bu+1)(\erf\sqrt{bu}-1)+2e^{-bu}\sqrt{\frac{bu}{\pi}},

\displaystyle h(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\left(b(\erf\sqrt{bu}+1)+\sqrt{\frac{b}{\pi u}}e^{-bu}\right).

Before we turn to the proofs of our results, we provide an overview of the ideas and tools that we employ.

Preliminaries

One idea guiding our approach is what we call the sampling of the occupation time as introduced in
[
2
]
. The simplest instance of this method may be phrased as follows.

Proposition 2

(Sampling the occupation time)
.

Consider a real stochastic process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. The distribution of its occupation time
A_{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}\{X_{s}>0\}ds
may be characterised via its moments by sampling the process
(X_{t})
at times
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,
which are i.i.d. with
U_{1}\sim\uniform(0,t),
namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{A_{t}}{t}\right)^{m}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{U_{k}}>0,1\leq k\leq m\right\};\hskip 18.49988ptm\geq 1.

(18)

Proof.

Write
F_{A_{t}/t}
for the cumulative distribution function of
A_{t}/t
defined by
F_{A_{t}/t}(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left\{A_{t}/t\leq x\right\},

x\in\mathbb{R}
. Conditionally given
A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}\{X_{s}>0\}ds,

(\mathbf{1}\{X_{U_{k}}>0\})_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials with success probability
A_{t}/t.
Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{U_{k}}>0,1\leq k\leq m\right\}=\int_{0}^{1}p^{m}F_{A_{t}/t}(dp)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{A_{t}}{t}\right)^{m}\right];\hskip 18.49988ptm\geq 1.

Since
A_{t}
is bounded, its distribution is uniquely determined by its moment sequence according to the Hausdorff’s moment problem, cf.
[
23
, Chapter III]
.
∎

If
(X_{t})
is a Lévy process,
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
shows that the sampling of the occupation time (
18
) leads to the integral formula (
6
) with integrands that are various convolutions of functions of the form
t\mapsto t^{b}\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}
. Though this formula holds in full generality, it may or may not be tractable depending on the specific form of the positivity function
\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{t}>0\right\}
at hand. In working on specific examples of positivity functions we found that considering the Laplace transform of
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}[(-\lambda A_{t})]
may be a viable alternative to characterising the distribution of
A_{t}
. Interestingly, it turns out that this Laplace transform may again be represented in terms of the survival probabilities
\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{U_{k}}>0,1\leq k\leq m\right\}
. Moreover, if
(X_{t})
is a Lévy process, these survival probabilities lead to fluctuations of random walks and Spitzer’s combinatorial Lemma more specifically. As we have shown earlier, in our setting it is convenient to rephrase Spitzer’s combinatorial Lemma in terms of (complete) Bell polynomials.

Let us now recall the notion of (complete and partial) Bell polynomials and some basic facts about their generating functions taken from the exposition in
[
17
, Chapter 1]
. For any two fixed sequences
v_{\bullet}\coloneqq(v_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
w_{\bullet}\coloneqq(w_{k})_{k\geq 1}
define the complete Bell polynomials associated with
(v_{\bullet},w_{\bullet})
by

\displaystyle B_{k}(v_{\bullet},w_{\bullet})\coloneqq\sum_{l=1}^{k}v_{l}B_{kl}(w_{\bullet});\hskip 18.49988ptk\in\mathbb{N},

(19)

where

\displaystyle B_{kl}(w_{\bullet})\coloneqq\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr{P}_{kl}}\prod_{B\in\rho}w_{\mathtt{\#}B};\hskip 18.49988pt1\leq l\leq k,

(20)

denotes the
(k,l)
th partial Bell polynomial (associated with
w_{\bullet}
), and
\mathscr{P}_{kl}
is the set of all partitions of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
containing
l
blocks. In addition, we write
\mathscr{P}_{k}
for the set of all partitions of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Recall that for some sequence
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of real numbers its exponential generating function is defined as the formal power series
\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}x^{k}/k!
. In what follows we work with the exponential generating function of the complete Bell polynomial which is well known to be given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}B_{k}(v_{\bullet},w_{\bullet})\frac{x^{k}}{k!}=v(w(x)),

(21)

whenever either of these quantities is well-defined, with
v,w
the exponential generating functions of
v_{\bullet},w_{\bullet},
respectively. We may now recast a variant of Spitzer’s combinatorial Lemma, a cornerstone result in the fluctuation theory of random walks, in terms of Bell polynomials as follows.

Proposition 3

(cf. Corollary 3 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
denote a random walk defined by
S_{n}\coloneqq\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n},
where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
is a sequence of real-valued i.i.d. random variables. The survival probability of
(S_{n})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{k}\geq 0,1\leq k\leq m\right\}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr{P}_{k}}\prod_{B\in\rho}(\mathtt{\#}B-1)!\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{\mathtt{\#}B}\geq 0\right\}.

(22)

The identity in (
22
) still holds if the weak inequalities are replaced by their strict versions.

Notice that the right hand side in (
22
) is a complete Bell polynomial.

Multiplying (

are the persistence probabilities. By Proposition

\displaystyle p_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=k!^{-1}B_{k}\left(1^{\bullet},(\bullet-1)!\mathbb{P}\left\{X^{(q)}_{T_{\bullet}}>0\right\}\right).

(25)

Consequently,

is the exponential generating function of a complete Bell polynomial. More specifically,

\displaystyle G(q,\lambda)

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\left(v\left(w\left(-\frac{\lambda}{q}\right)\right)+1\right),

(26)

where

denotes the exponential generating function of

and

respectively. In particular,

Since

we find

\displaystyle w(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(k-1)!\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{T_{k}^{(q)}}>0\right\}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}t^{k-1}e^{-qt}p_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\frac{p_{t}}{t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(\zeta qt)^{k}}{k!}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\frac{p_{t}}{t}(e^{\zeta qt}-1)dt,

where in the penultimate step we applied the dominated convergence theorem. Putting everything together we obtain the claim.

Theorem 6

(Campbell’s Theorem)

\displaystyle\int_{S}\min(\absolutevalue{f(x)},1)\mu(dx)<\infty.

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp(\theta\Sigma)]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{S}(e^{\theta f(x)}-1)\mu(dx)\right)

(29)

for any complex
\theta

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Sigma]=\int_{S}f(x)\mu(dx)

(30)

\displaystyle\Var(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}f(x)^{2}\mu(dx),

(31)

finite or infinite.

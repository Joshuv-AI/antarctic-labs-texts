Machine learning-based analysis of heat and mass transfer in casson hybrid nanofluid flow over a variable porous riga plate
Authors: Shajar Abbas, Syeda Farzeen Fatima Gilani, Imran Siddique, Rashid Jan, Mohammad Farooq Hakimi, Aiedh Mrisi Alharthi, Mamurakhon Toshpulatova, Ibrahim Mahariq
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study uses an artificial neural network (ANN) architecture in combination with the Levenberg-Marquardt backpropagation (LMBP) method to investigate the impact of heat radiation on the hybrid nanofluid flow through a variable porous medium beneath a stretchy Riga plate. The purpose of this work is driven by the increasing demand for accurately modeling complex nonlinear thermal systems involving electromagnetic control, porous media, and nanofluids, which are not sufficiently covered in the existing literature. Single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) and multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) are suspended in propylene glycol, which is regarded as the base fluid. The Riga plate is a plate composed of alternating electrodes as well as permanent magnets to form electromagnetic forces, which act significantly on the flow and the heat transfer properties. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) are transformed into ordinary differential equations (ODEs) via similarity transformations in order to provide datasets for ANN training. The finite difference technique (FDM) is then used to numerically solve these ODEs. Then the successive over relaxation method, executed through custom MATLAB code, is used to obtain the solutions of these equations. The novelty of the work is in the integrated evaluation of hybrid nanofluid flow over a Riga plate in a variable permeable media with radiative heat transfer, as well as the use of an ANN-LMBP predictive model, which is trained on high-fidelity numerical data. The numerical data show that the thermal radiation increases the temperature distribution by about 21% in the case of SWCNT-based nanofluid and 18% in the case of MWCNT-based nanofluid. The porosity parameter decreases the velocity of fluid by approximately 12%, whereas the modified Hartmann number increases the fluid velocity by 15–17%. Additionally, SWCNTs exhibit 8–10% higher thermal conductivity compared to MWCNTs. The ANN-LMBP model demonstrates excellent predictive performance with a coefficient of determination R2 > 0.998 and a mean squared error on the order of 10−4. These results confirm that the proposed ANN-based framework is a fast, accurate, and computationally efficient tool for modeling nonlinear thermal fluid systems, with significant applications in thermal management, cooling technologies, energy systems, and industrial fluid flow processes.Clinical trial number not applicable.Keywords: Artificial neural networking, Levenberg-Marquardt backpropagation algorithm, Thermal radiation, Riga plate, Variable porous medium, Finite difference

Introduction

When two different nanomaterials diffuse into base fluid, they create hybrid nanofluids. By using special qualities of nanoparticles, hybrid nanofluids are employed in fluid dynamics to enhance rheological, thermal, and stability aspects of the base fluid. Because of their improved thermal and rheological characteristics, hybrid nanofluids may be used in a variety of fields, such as energy storage, industrial processes, biomedical engineering, automotive cooling systems, heat transfer improvement, solar thermal systems, and electronics cooling. Kumar et al. [1] investigated enhancement rate of the heat transmission in a non-linear media by covering a porous surface with carbon nanotubes. In addition, the heat source and heat transport mechanism are analyzed. Using a complicated heat source and a variable viscosity, Darcy Forchheimer channel was used to study the hydromagnetic convective dispersion of materials by Pal and Mondal [2].

The term variable porous space is used to describe an area or substance whose porosity varies, or how much liquids or gasses are allowed to flow through its design. The idea of the transformation of porous places are crucial in material science, heat transport, fluid dynamics, among other scientific and technical domains. Although the porosity of such formations may be influenced by the presence of different types of soil layers, weathering, compaction, and other variables. These variations in porosity can have a significant influence on the ways in which fluids move through the medium, and how pollutants and nutrients are distributed in the soil. Applications including as environmental engineering, filtration and gas and oil extraction, system design require an understanding of the ability to simulate changeable porous spaces. The research conducted by Moreno et al. [3] is on a porous medium that has different porosity. For a magnetizing nanofluid passes across a curved porous sheet that is sensitive to both exothermic and endothermic processes and has varying porosity, Ullah et al. [4] conducted a theoretical analysis of the entropy generating process. A fluid will come across regions with fluctuating flow resistance as it passes through the surface having varying porosity, like a layer of earth or a composite substance with variable porosity. On a permeable region, Elkoumy et al. [5] examined how magnetic and Hall fields affected peristaltic Maxwell fluid flow on peristaltic Maxwell fluid flow. This leads to irregular surface velocity distributions and flow patterns. In their simulation of flow model, Shimizu et al. [6] included effects of the saturated and unsaturated permeable media on fluid flowing on a flexible porous surface. Arafa et al. [7] discussed homogenous nanofluid flow across a porous surface with varying porosity. Whereas regions with lower porosity hinder fluid movement, leading to flow slowing and the creation of boundary layers, areas with higher porosity generally provide less flow resistance, enabling more fluid penetration and higher velocities. Through the disk cone gap, Khan et al. [8] investigated Double diffusions in Maxwell flow of fluid on a variable permeability region. Another similar study was conducted by Abdelsalam et al. [9], which discussed the flow of Maxwell flow across a long porous area with effects of rheological factors. Due to higher fluid velocities and heat conduction, more porous areas tend to be cooler. The latter can cause temperature gradients across the porous surface.

The magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) and boundary layer flow applications are studied in the phenomenon of nanofluid flow over the Riga Plate. Well and laminar flow characteristics are evaluated using the Riga Plate. Finding the properties of flow of the fluid over a Riga surface is essential for several uses, including heat exchange, manufacturing, and aerodynamics. In order to improve drag and lift characteristics on aerodynamic surfaces and get a better understanding of boundary layer management, flow over Riga Plate was investigated in aerodynamics [10]. For the construction of airplanes that are both structurally robust and aerodynamically efficient, such information is essential. Understanding heat transfer mechanisms under electromagnetic stimulation is improved for thermal transfer applications by looking at fluid movement across a Riga sheet. Electrical equipment thermal management, cooling systems, and heat exchanger design all require this understanding. To enhance performance of the pumps, compressors, and turbines, flow of fluid across the Riga sheet is employed in the manufacturing processes. Engineers may analyze flow parameters across the Riga plate [11] to improve system performance, save energy consumption, and boost performance. The results of many investigations of fluid flow over the Riga sheet were published. Using the MATLAB software BVP4c and HAM, Eswaramoorthi et al. [12] described Williamson nanofluid flow in a Darcy medium that is suctioned or injected across a Riga plate. With changes in the porosity factor, the fluid flow rate dropped. Depending on reaction speeds and diffusivity, the fluid content was influenced by the chemical reaction component. Some further recent studies are found in [13–15].

In the modern world, neural networks also known as artificial neural networks are essential for solving problems in all fields of research and engineering. An ANN, is a learning system that mimics the human brain by utilizing a layered structure of linked neurons or nodes. Neural Network is able to learning from its input data, which enables them to be trained to identify patterns, arrange information, and forecast future occurrences. These days, in the realm of nanofluids, ANNs are extensively utilized. Using a Darcy-Forchheimer medium in electromagnetic hydrodynamics, he importance of machine learning techniques in examining the flow of water, ethylene glycol, and graphene oxide nanofluids was investigated by Shafiq et al. [16]. A neural network model was developed by Shafiq et al. [17] to investigate convective thermal transfer in nanofluids using a moving needle. To forecast heat transfer performance, an very precise ANN model was created, and the influence of Soret and Dufour coefficients were considered. To investigate the impacts of solid fluid interfacial layers and nanoparticle flow of ethylene glycol nanofluids and single-walled carbon nanotubes via thin, narrow needles, Abbas et al. [18] developed an ANN model. According to the study, the ANN model accurately predicted the nanofluid temperature transfer capabilities and flow properties. In order to build effective temperature transfer devices and fluid flow capabilities were accurately predicted by the ANN model. Analyzing COVID-19 data [19], examination of dependability using a mix of Lindley distributions [20], and electrical component dependability [21] are some excellent uses for ANNs.

Recent advances in nanofluid dynamics have attracted significant attention due to their enhanced thermal performance and wide range of engineering applications, particularly in heat transfer systems, renewable energy technologies, and biomedical processes. In this context, hybrid nanofluids based on carbon nanotubes (CNTs) have shown superior thermal conductivity and improved energy transport characteristics compared to conventional fluids. The behaviour of CNT-based nanofluids under different physical effects, such as magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), porous media, radiation, and chemical reactions have been investigated in several studies. Shahbaz et al. [22] used intelligent neural network techniques to study radiative Casson nanofluid flow on curved stretchy surfaces, emphasizing the function of CNTs in improving heat transmission. Shahbaz et al. [23] have employed clever predictive modeling methodology in order to examine MHD nanofluid flow in porous media, which have realized better thermal control and optimization capabilities. Other numerical studies have examined the Darcy-Forchheimer flow of CNT nanofluids based on propylene glycol under the impact of activation and magnetic energy, and as a result, they have discovered significant effects on the fluid behavior employing the nanofluids [24]. Three dimensional non-Newtonian flows of nanofluid over stretching sheets have also been analyzed using advanced computational paradigms [25]. Additionally, hybrid nanofluid systems have been thoroughly studied in HVAC and solar energy applications, and it has been demonstrated that they have improved thermal efficiency when exposed to magnetic fields and porous materials [26–28]. In addition, rotation of CNT nanofluid flows has been radiated, ohmically heated, and reacted with chemical species numerically simulated to gain insight into the multifaceted nature of thermal transport processes by Eswaramoorthi et al. [29]. All these studies have shown that CNT-based hybrid nanofluids under magnetic, porous and radiative conditions are very important in the present heat transfer enhancement technology.

This work investigates the hybrid nanofluid flow dynamics across an elastic Riga plate placed in a changeable porous media. The radiation effect, porosity, modified Hartmann number, magnet and electrode width parameters are incorporated in the model. A system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) is created by applying suitable similarity transformations to the controlling nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs).

Novelty

This work is unique in that it examines hybrid nanofluid flow over a flexible Riga plate in a medium with variable porosity while accounting for electromagnetic actuation and radiative heat transfer. In addition, a hybrid computing architecture grounded on Artificial Neural Networks with LevenbergMarquardt Backpropagation (ANNs-LMBP), trained on data created with the FDM are utilized to effectively approximate the solution of the nonlinear system.

Research gap

Although there is a vast literature on the flow of nanofluids and the Riga plate configurations, limited studies have been conducted on the combined effects of variable porous media, electromagnetic control parameters, and machine learning-based solution techniques within a single framework. This gap will inspire the current study.

Practical applications

The results of this research are applicable in thermal engineering systems where there is a great need to make the heat transfer more effective. Potential uses are electronic cooling systems, energy conversion devices, biomedical thermal therapies, microfluidic systems and electromagnetic flow control systems. The results can be used to optimize the thermal efficiency of heat transfer systems based on porous media.

Artificial neural network analysis

Based on the biological neural system, the artificial neural network (ANN) is a computational and mathematical model that can effectively handle complicated nonlinear data. It is made up of interconnected neurons that are organized in layers forming a parallel processing structure that is capable of learning patterns and approximating nonlinear relationships. This paper uses ANN model to estimate the output response of the nonlinear governing equations at different physical parameters i.e. radiation parameter and porosity parameter. ANN architecture consists of three major layers which include the hidden layer(s), input layer, and the output layer. The external data is received in the input layer, and the predicted results are the ones generated in the output layer. The hidden layer carries out nonlinear transformations of the data.

In the current work, a single hidden layer, feedforward backpropagation neural network is used. The hidden layer’s neurons number ten, which was chosen after some trial simulations to obtain the best performance with the least error as revealed in Fig. 3. The governing parameters (radiation parameter, modified Hartmann number), and porosity parameter are the input layer and the predicted flow and thermal profiles are the output layer. ANN models are trained using the Levenberg-Marquardt backpropagation (LMBP) approach because it converges rapidly and is excellent at solving nonlinear issues.

Neural network design

The output layer utilizes the linear (purelin) activation function, which is necessary to provide continuous output prediction, while the hidden layer uses the tangent sigmoid (tansig) activation function, which is necessary to capture nonlinear behavior.

Velocity profile

Figure 14 shows that the velocity profile increases with an increase in the modified Hartmann number γ. Quantitatively, the velocity enhances by approximately 15–17% as γ increases. This phenomenon may be explained physically by the Lorentz force produced by the electromagnetic field, which accelerates the conducting fluid by acting along the flow direction. This process is in line with the theory of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) where electromagnetic forcing can promote momentum transport under specific configurations. Figure 15 shows that the velocity goes down by almost 12% as the porosity parameter, denoted by the symbol λ, is increased. This decrease is attributed to the fact the porous medium provides a greater resistance to fluid movement. This action is in line with the law of higher porosity resistance that is associated with the inhibition of velocity by higher drag forces in the medium. Figure 16 shows that the larger the magnet and electrode width parameter, denoted as B the smaller the velocity becomes. The reason is that, the greater the electromagnetic field, the greater the resistive (Lorentz) forces that act against this fluid motion thereby slowing down flow. This effect is the balance between the electromagnetic braking force and fluid inertia. Moreover, with the growth of the nanoparticles concentrations (φ1, φ2), the velocities drop significantly, as seen in Fig. 17. This is mainly because there is a rise in the hybrid nanofluid viscosity, which increases the internal friction and resists the movement of the fluid. This is in line with classical fluid mechanics, with an increase in viscosity decreasing the flow velocity.

Temperature profile θ(η)

As shown in Fig. 18, the temperature rises by a large percentage with the radiation parameter Rd showing an improvement of about 18–21%. This has been enhanced by the augmented radiative heat flux that raises the internal energy of the fluid. This is the behavior in accordance with radiative heat transfer theory in that, the greater the radiation, the greater the absorption of thermal energy in the fluid. As Fig. 19 demonstrates, the temperature rises with porosity parameter, or rather the parameter that is denoted by the symbol λ. This is because the fluid motion in the porous medium is reduced, and reduces convective dissipation caused by fluid motion and improves thermal energy storage. The border belt’s temperature will rise as a result of convectional transit being inhibited. Figure 20 show the higher width parameter of the magnet and electrode B will lead to an increase in temperature. This can be attributed to the fact that it is most likely to increase the intensity of electromagnetic interaction, which produces more internal heat due to the induced currents and energy dissipation. One of the causes of the apparent increase in temperature is the conversion of electromagnetic energy into thermal energy. Moreover, Fig. 21 indicates that temperature increases with the increased nanoparticle concentrations (φ1, φ2). This can be attributed to the fact that the hybrid nanofluid has a better thermal conductivity that improves heat diffusion and energy transportation in the system. The existence of nanoparticles leads to more effective heat transfer causing a higher temperature profile.

Conclusion

This work examines the hybrid nanofluid flow over a nonlinear stretchy Riga plate embedded in a variable porous medium under the impact of heat radiation and a changing Hartmann number. The governing model is numerically solved and the performance is assessed using the ANN-LMBP technique to make accurate predictions of the flow characteristics.

Future work

1.
Introduction

Study of moments of
L
-functions is a recurring theme in modern number theory. Given a family of
L
-functions
\{L(s,F)\}
associated to an automorphic form
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we define the
2k
-th moment at the central point
s=1/2

M_{2k}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|L(1/2,F)|^{2k}.

These moments
are the natural objects to study as they illuminate
structure of the family and display beautiful symmetries in the family. A broad goal is to get an asymptotic formula of the form

M_{2k}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{Main\ Term}+o(\mathrm{Main\ Term}),

as
|\mathcal{F}|\rightarrow\infty
, where
\mathrm{Main\ Term}\asymp|\mathcal{F}|
.
Such estimates often yield interesting arithmetic applications, for example, non-vanishing of
L
-values, subconvexity of
L
-functions etc.
Unfortunately, there are only a handful of known asymptotic formulae in the literature.

For GL(1) family

(1)

\displaystyle M_{2k}(T,q):=\sum_{\chi^{\star}(q)}\int_{-T}^{T}|L(1/2+it,\chi)|^{2k}\,\mathrm{d}t,

asymptotic formulae are known due to Hardy-Littlewood (see
[
13
]
) for
k=1,q=1
and due to Ingham (see
[
13
]
) for
k=2,q=1
, and due to Heath-Brown
[
12
]
for
k=1,T=0
and Young
[
14
]
for
k=2,T=0
. There is also some recent progress for
k=3
and
4
for a large family (also including the sum over the moduli
q
) by Chandee et. al. in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
.

This problem gets challenging for large
k
and for ‘higher rank’ family. Even a slightly weaker problem of getting the Lindelöf–consistent upper bound

M_{2k}(\mathcal{F})\ll_{\epsilon}|\mathcal{F}|^{1+\epsilon},\ |\mathcal{F}|\rightarrow\infty

is mostly out of reach.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{2}(q,T)
denote the set of Maass cusp forms on GL(2) of level
q
and Laplace eigenvalue
1/4+t_{f}^{2},

T\leq t_{f}\leq 2T
. For this family
some notable known results are due to Kowalski-Michel-Vanderkam
[
9
]
in the level aspect and due to Jutila-Motohashi
[
8
]
in the spectral aspect for the fourth moment.
The sixth moment was first studied by Young
[
15
]
who proved

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(1,T)}|L(1/2+it_{f},f)|^{6}\ll_{\epsilon}T^{2+\epsilon},

In the same paper he also estimated the second moment

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(1,T)}|L(1/2+it_{f},\pi\times f)|^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}T^{2+\epsilon},

of Rankin–Selberg
L
-function associated to (GL(3), GL(2)) pair of automorphic forms
(\pi,f)
. This may be thought of as the cuspidal analog of (
2
). In a companion paper
[
16
]
, Young proved

\int_{-T^{1-\epsilon}}^{T^{1-\epsilon}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(1,T)}|L(1/2+it,\pi\times f)|^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}T^{3+\epsilon}.

Motivated by
[
16
]
, Djanković
[
5
]
proved a ‘non-archimedian’ analog of (
2

M
prime and
k\geq 3
odd integer

\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi(M)\\
\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}}\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H_{k}^{\star}(M,\psi)}|L(1/2,f)|^{6}\ll M^{1+\epsilon},

where for any integer
M
,
H_{k}^{\star}(M,\psi)
denote the set
of primitive (normalized such that the first coefficient is
1
) newforms which forms an orthogonal basis of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(M,\psi)
. A key ingredient of
[
5
]
is the asymptotic large sieve of Iwaniec-Li
[
7
]
followed by Voronoi summation formula.

Recently Qi
[
11
]
extended Young’s result
[
15
]
to the short-interval range
T<t_{f}\leq T+\sqrt{T}
and obtained Lindelöf consistent bound in the coveted family range (

and

.

Let

.
Then we have

2.3.
Duality principle and large sieve inequality

Lemma 1

(Duality principle)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For any complex numbers
a_{m}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\left|\sum_{m}a_{m}\phi(m,n)\right|^{2}\ll\left(\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}\right)\sup_{\|\beta\|_{2}=1}\sum_{m}\left|\sum_{n}\beta(n)\phi(m,n)\right|^{2},

where the supremum is taken over all sequences of complex numbers
\beta(n)
such that

\|\beta\|_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{n}|\beta(n)|^{2}}=1.

Lemma 2
.

Let
a_{m}
be any complex numbers and
D>0
be a fixed integer. Then we have

(4)

\displaystyle S(N;D):=\sum_{\gamma(D)}\Big|\sum_{N\leq m<2N}a_{m}e\left(\frac{\gamma m}{D}\right)\Big|^{2}\leq(N+D)\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}.

Proof.

We open the absolute valued square to arrive at

\displaystyle S(N;D)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}a_{m_{1}}\overline{a}_{m_{2}}\sum_{\gamma(D)}e\left(\frac{(m_{1}-m_{2})\gamma}{D}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D\mathop{\sum\sum}_{m_{1}=m_{2}(D)}|a_{m_{1}}\overline{a}_{m_{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq(D+N)\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
a_{m}
be any complex numbers and
D>0
be a fixed integer. Let
1\leq M\leq N
. Then we have

(5)

\displaystyle S(C,N,M;D):=\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim C\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma(cD)}\Big|\sum_{N\leq m<N+M}a_{m}e\left(\frac{\gamma m}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}\ll C^{\epsilon}(M+DC^{2})\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}.

Proof.

By a change of variable, we see that

\displaystyle S(C,N,M;D)=\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim C\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma(cD)}\Big|\sum_{1\leq m<M+1}a({N-1+m})e\left(\frac{\gamma m}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}.

We apply the duality principle (see Lemma
1
) to analyse
S(C,N,M;D)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle S(C,N,M;D)\leq\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}\sup_{\|\beta\|_{2}=1}\sum_{1\leq m<M+1}\Big|\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim{C}\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{cD}\end{subarray}}\beta(\gamma,d)e\left(\frac{m\gamma}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}.

Next we extend the sum over
m
to
-2MQ\leq m\leq 2MQ
, where
Q>2
is a parameter such that

Q=C^{5\epsilon}+\frac{C^{2+\epsilon}D}{M}.

To this end, we introduce
a smooth bump function
w(x)
such that
0\leq w(x)\leq 1
,
w(x)=1
for
x\in[-1,1]
and
w(x)=0
, for
x\notin(-2,2)
, and
x^{j}w^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
. Thus we have

\displaystyle S(c,N;D)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}\sup_{\|\beta\|_{2}=1}\sum_{m}w\left(\frac{m}{MQ}\right)\Big|\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim{C}\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{cD}\end{subarray}}\beta(\gamma,c)e\left(\frac{m\gamma}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}.

Opening the absolute valued square, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathop{\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,c^{\prime}\sim{C}\end{subarray}}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{cD}\end{subarray}}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{c^{\prime}D}\end{subarray}}\beta(\cdots)\overline{\beta}(\cdots)\sum_{m}w(m/MQ)e\left(\frac{m(\gamma c^{\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}c)}{Dcc^{\prime}}\right).

We proceed to apply the Poisson summation formula to the
m
-sum. The dual
m
-sum is given by

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\cdots={MQ}{}\sum_{m}\delta(\gamma c^{\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}c\equiv-m\,\mathrm{mod}\,Dcc^{\prime})I_{1}(\cdots),

where

(7)

\displaystyle I_{1}(\cdots)=\int w(z)e\left(\frac{-mMQz}{Dcc^{\prime}}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

An integration by parts argument shows that
I_{1}(\cdots)
is negligibly small (
\ll D^{-2025}
)unless

|m|\leq N_{2}:=C^{\epsilon}\frac{DC^{2}}{MQ}.

Note that
N_{2}<1
as

DC^{2}C^{\epsilon}<MQ=MC^{\epsilon}+C^{2+\epsilon}D.
Thus only the zero–frequency survives, i.e., we have
m=0
.
Using the congruence, we conclude that
c=c^{\prime}
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}(Dc)
. Hence

\displaystyle S(C,N;D)\ll\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}MQ

\displaystyle\mathop{\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim{C}{}\end{subarray}}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{cD}\end{subarray}}|\beta(\gamma,c)\overline{\beta}(\gamma,c)|\ll MQ\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}.

Hence the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4

Let

be any complex numbers and

\displaystyle S_{T}(C,N;D):=\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim C\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma(cD)}\int_{-T}^{T}\Big|\sum_{N\leq m<2N}a_{m}e(tf(m))e\left(\frac{\gamma m}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\ll(C^{2}DT+X)\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}.

Let
W
be a non-negative smooth bump function supported on
[1/2,3/2]
such that

[1,2]
. Thus we have

\displaystyle S_{T}(C,N;D)\leq\mathop{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\sim C\\
(c,D)=1\end{subarray}}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\gamma(cD)}\int_{0}^{\infty}W(t/T)\Big|\sum_{N\leq m<2N}a_{m}e(tf(m))e\left(\frac{\gamma m}{cD}\right)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

For any sequence of complex numbers
(b_{m})
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\Big|\sum_{N\leq m<2N}b_{m}e(tf(m))\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}b_{m_{1}}\overline{b}_{m_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}W\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)e\left(t(f(m_{1})-f(m_{2})\right)\mathrm{d}t

By a change of variable, we see that the

By the mean-value theorem, we have

|m_{2}-m_{1}|\leq{|f(m_{1})-f(m_{2})|X}\leq{C^{\epsilon}X}/{T}.

Let

. We dissect the sum over

and

Thus the right side of (

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}W\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\sum_{k}\Big|\sum_{m_{1}\in I_{k}}b_{m_{1}}e\left(t(f(m_{1})\right)\Big|^{2}+\Big|\sum_{m_{2}\in J_{k}}\bar{b}_{m_{2}}e\left(-tf(m_{2})\right)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

As a first step, we apply approximate functional equation (see Th. 5.3 and Prop. 5.4 in

]

\displaystyle V_{\ell,N}^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1,\ \ j\geq 0.

and

\ell

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\pi}(M)\ll\sup_{\ell}\sup_{N\ell^{2}\ll M^{3/2+\epsilon}}\sum_{\psi(M_{1})}\,\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H_{k}^{\star}(M,\psi)}\frac{|S_{\ell}(N)|^{2}}{N}+M^{-A}

7.
Conclusion

We plug in estimates from Lemma
10
,
13
,
15
into (
26
) to get

(61)

\displaystyle\mathrm{OD}_{\pi}^{(2,+)}(\cdots)

\displaystyle\ll\sup_{A\ll C}\sum_{a\sim A}\sum_{b_{1},b_{2}\ll\log C}G^{b_{1},b_{2}}(\ell,A,C,N)

(62)

\displaystyle\ll M^{\epsilon}C^{2}\left(N+\frac{N^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}}\right)+{NK^{2}{\ell^{2}}C^{2}M_{2}}{}\left(1+\frac{CM_{2}^{2}}{N}\right)+\frac{N^{2}M_{2}}{\sqrt{M}}.

Same estimates hold for
\mathrm{OD}_{\pi}^{(2,-)}(\cdots)
and
\mathrm{OD}_{\pi}^{(1,\pm)}(\cdots)
. Thus from (
25
) we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{OD}_{\pi}(\ell,C,N)

\displaystyle\ll M^{\epsilon}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{{N}},\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}\right\}\left(C^{2}\left(N+\frac{N^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}}\right)+{NK^{2}{\ell^{2}}C^{2}M_{2}}{}\left(1+\frac{CM_{2}^{2}}{N}\right)+\frac{N^{2}M_{2}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)

We estimate the first term as follows:

\displaystyle M^{\epsilon}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{{N}},\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}\right\}C^{2}(N+N^{2}/M_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle M^{\epsilon}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{{N}},\frac{M_{1}N}{C^{2}M^{2}}\right\}C^{2}(N+N^{2}/M_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle M^{\epsilon}\frac{M_{1}N}{C^{2}M^{2}}C^{2}(N+N^{2}/M_{1}^{2})\ll M^{\epsilon}M_{1}N.

The second term is

(63)

\displaystyle M^{\epsilon}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{{N}},\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}\right\}{NK^{2}{\ell^{2}}C^{2}M_{2}}{}\left(1+\frac{CM_{2}^{2}}{N}\right).

For
C\ll M^{-\epsilon}N/M
, (
63
) is bounded by (using
N\ell^{2}\ll M^{3/2+\epsilon}
)

M^{\epsilon}\frac{M_{1}}{N}N\frac{N^{2}}{C^{2}M^{2}}\ell^{2}C^{2}M_{2}\ll M^{\epsilon}M_{1}N.

For
C\gg M^{-\epsilon}N/M
, we have
K\ll M^{\epsilon}
. Thus (
63
) is bounded by

M^{\epsilon}\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}N\ell^{2}C^{2}M_{2}+M^{\epsilon}\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}\ell^{2}C^{3}M_{2}^{3}\ll M^{\epsilon}M_{1}N.

Similarly the third term

M^{\epsilon}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}}{{N}},\frac{M_{1}N^{2}}{C^{3}M^{3}}\right\}\frac{N^{2}M_{2}}{\sqrt{M}}\ll M^{\epsilon}\frac{M_{1}}{{N}}\frac{N^{2}M_{2}}{\sqrt{M}}\ll M^{\epsilon}M_{1}N.

On plugging in the above bound in (
17
), we get

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\pi}(M)\ll\frac{M_{1}-1}{2}+\sup_{\ell}\sup_{N\ell^{2}\ll M^{3/2+\epsilon}}\sup_{C}\frac{|\mathrm{OD}_{\pi}(\ell,C,N)|}{N}\ll M^{\epsilon}M_{1}.

This proves Theorem
1
.

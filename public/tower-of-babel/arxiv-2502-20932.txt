Restricted weak type endpoint estimate for the spherical maximal operators on the Heisenberg group
Hyunwoo Jeon, Joonil Kim

Abstract
Let ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} denote the Heisenberg group, identified with ℝ d × ℝ \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R} , where d = 2 ​ n d=2n and n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} . We consider the spherical maximal operator ℳ \mathcal{M} associated with the sphere S d − 1 S^{d-1} embedded in the horizontal subspace ℝ d × { 0 } \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\} of ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} . It is known that ℳ \mathcal{M} is bounded on L p ​ ( ℍ n ) L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}) if and only if p ∈ ( d d − 1 , ∞ ] p\in\bigl(\tfrac{d}{d-1},\infty\bigr] . In this paper, we establish a restricted weak type ( p , p ) (p,p) estimate at the endpoint p = d d − 1 p=\tfrac{d}{d-1} for ℳ \mathcal{M} , provided d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 .

Introduction

In this paper, we shall establish a restricted weak type estimate at the endpoint
p=\frac{d}{d-1}
for the spherical maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. To set the stage, let
\left(\mathbb{H}^{n},\cdot\right)
denote the Heisenberg group, identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
d=2n\in\mathbb{N}
. We write a general point in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as
\bar{x}=\left(x,x_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, and the group law
\cdot
is given by

\bar{x}\cdot\bar{y}=\left(x,x_{d+1}\right)\cdot\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)=\left(x+y,x_{d+1}+y_{d+1}+\langle x,Jy\rangle\right),

where
J
is an invertible skew-symmetric
d\times d
matrix.

Throughout the paper, the averaging operators we consider are defined via the noncommutaitve convolution

(1.1)

\displaystyle f\ast_{\mathbb{H}}K\left(\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\int f\left(\bar{y}^{-1}\cdot\bar{x}\right)K\left(\bar{y}\right)d\bar{y}

\displaystyle=\int f\left(x-y,x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle x,Jy\rangle\right)K\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)dydy_{d+1}.

Let
d\sigma
be the normalized surface measure on the
\left(d-1\right)
-dimensional unit sphere in the hoizontal subspace
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\left\{0\right\}
, centered at the origin. For
t>0
, define the dilate
d\sigma_{t}
by

\left\langle f,d\sigma_{t}\right\rangle=\int f\left(tx,0\right)d\sigma\left(x\right).

Given a Schwartz function
f
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define the spherical averages by

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x},t\right):=f\ast_{\mathbb{H}}d\sigma_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)=\int_{S^{d-1}}f\left(x-ty,x_{d+1}-t\langle x,Jy\rangle\right)d\sigma\left(y\right),

where
S^{d-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The corresponding maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
is then given by

(1.3)

\mathcal{M}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)=\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x},t\right)\right\rvert.

In
[
11
]
, Nevo and Thangavelu initiated the study of the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, with
d=2n\geq 4
. They proved that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
p>\tfrac{d-1}{d-2}
. An optimal
L^{p}
boundedness result for
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(with
n\geq 2
), namely that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L^{p}
if and only if
p>\tfrac{d}{d-1}
was proved by Müller and Seeger
[
9
]
and, independently and by a different method, by Narayanan and Thangavelu
[
10
]
. In
[
9
]
, they actually cover a larger class of groups, namely the Métivier groups, which strictly contains the class of groups of Heisenberg-type. For the case of
n=1
, the
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
for
p>2
remains open; however, positive results have been obtained for Heisenberg radial functions (see
[
8
]
and
[
1
]
). On the other hand, an interesting phenomenon arises when
J
is not a skew-symmetric matrix. In
[
7
]
, the second author investigated more general matrices according to the multiplicities of their eigenvalues.

More recently, Ryu and Seeger
[
12
]
established the sharp range
p>\tfrac{d}{d-1}
for a broader class of groups, namely two-step nilpotent Lie groups of the form
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
d\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
. They also obtained a restricted weak type inequality at the endpoint
p=\tfrac{d}{d-1}
for the
local
maximal operator

\mathcal{M}_{loc}\left(f\right)\left(\tilde{x}\right)=\sup_{t\in I}\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\left(f\right)\left(\tilde{x},t\right)\right\rvert,

where
I\subset[0,\infty)
is a compact interval and
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In the same paper, they posed questions regarding the case of
global

t
. To date, restricted weak type estimates at
p=\tfrac{d}{d-1}
for global
t
remain unproved, even for the Heisenberg group. The aim of this paper is to prove a restricted weak type estimate at the endpoint
p=\frac{d}{d-1}
for the global maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
defined in (

) on the Heisenberg group

.

Main Theorem
.

Let
d>2
and let
\mathcal{M}
be the spherical maximal operator defined in (
1.3
). Then
\mathcal{M}
is of restricted weak type
(p,p)
for
p=\frac{d}{d-1}
:
for every finite measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
(with characteristic function
1_{E}
), there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
E
, such that

\left\|\mathcal{M}(1_{E})\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq C\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

The main difficulty in obtaining the endpoint restricted weak type estimate for the global
t
arises from the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate for each individual piece of the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
. More precisely, in
[
12
]
and
[
9
]
, the last two frequency variables in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
are decomposed according to their size
2^{k}
, and a
k2^{k}
bound is obtained for the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate for each piece of the global maximal operator. Due to the additional
k
loss in these weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimates, the Bourgain interpolation trick cannot be applied to obtain the endpoint restricted weak type estimate. In contrast, in this paper we decompose the full frequency space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
instead of only a partial set and employ a variant of the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition to eliminate these losses for the maximal averages. In particular, we use the parameter

j
instead of
k
. To achieve a
2^{j}
bound for the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate, we consider an application of the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition with respect to different heights and different maximal functions.

Organization of this paper

In Section 2, we decompose the frequency space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
into regions where
\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\sim 2^{j}
and
\left\lvert\xi_{d+1}\right\rvert\sim 2^{j+\ell}
. Moreover, we reduce the Main Theorem to obtaining a
2^{j}
bound for the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate and a
2^{-j\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}
decay rate for the
L^{2}
estimate via the Bourgain interpolation trick. Then, in Section 3, we introduce a new ball and a new scaled maximal operator
\mathfrak{M}
to construct a variant of the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition. Using this decomposition, we remove the worst part of the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate—that is, we eliminate the portion of the domain that fails to satisfy the Hörmander type condition. Once this problematic part is removed, we demonstrate that the Hörmander type condition holds for the remaining portion, thereby establishing the desired
2^{j}
bound for the weak type
\left(1,1\right)
estimate. For the
L^{2}
estimate, we provide several reductions in Section 4, including the asymptotics of the Fourier transform of the spherical measure, and we obtain the
2^{-j\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}
decay rate in Section 5 by employing an almost orthogonality argument. Finally, in the Appendix, we detail minor calculations concerning the small frequency term and the tail term in the
L^{2}
estimate, utilizing classical results for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal functions on the Heisenberg group.

Frequency Decomposition and Bourgain Interpolation

Utilizing the Fourier transform, we can express
\mathcal{A}\left(f\right)
as follows:

\mathcal{A}\left(f\right)\left(x,x_{d+1},t\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi+x_{d+1}\xi_{d+1})}\widehat{d\sigma}\left(t\left(\xi+\xi_{d+1}Jx\right)\right)\widehat{f}\left(\xi,\xi_{d+1}\right)d\xi d\xi_{d+1}.

Decompose
\widehat{d\sigma}\left(\xi\right)
as

\widehat{d\sigma}\left(\xi\right)=\widehat{d\sigma}_{1}\left(\xi\right)+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\widehat{d\sigma_{j}}\left(\xi\right)

where

\widehat{d\sigma_{1}}\left(\xi\right)=\widehat{d\sigma}\left(\xi\right)\psi\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\ \text{and}\ \widehat{d\sigma_{j}}\left(\xi\right)=\widehat{d\sigma}\left(\xi\right)\chi\left(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\right)\ \text{for}\ j\geq 2.

For
j\geq 1
, we define the corresponding average and its maximal function by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x},t\right)

\displaystyle=f\ast_{\mathbb{H}}\left(d\sigma_{j}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi+x_{d+1}\xi_{d+1})}\widehat{d\sigma_{j}}\left(t\left(\xi+\xi_{d+1}Jx\right)\right)\widehat{f}\left(\xi,\xi_{d+1}\right)d\xi d\xi_{d+1}\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{j}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{j}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x},t\right)\right\rvert.

Then, we have the pointwise inequality

\mathcal{M}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)\leq\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{j}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right).

We shall frequently use the following well-known estimates for

:

(2.2)

\left\lvert d\sigma_{j}\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq 2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\right\rvert d\sigma\left(u\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{C_{N}2^{jd}}{\left(1+2^{j}\left\lvert x-u\right\rvert\right)^{N}}d\sigma\left(u\right)\leq\frac{C_{M}2^{j}}{\left(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert\right)^{M}}

for every
N>M>d
. When we use (
2.2
), we frequently take
N,M>100d
. By using (
2.2
) for the case of

, we show that
\left\lVert\mathcal{M}_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim 1
for

To handle the second term (when

), we decompose

into the inner term

\displaystyle d\sigma_{j}^{I}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2^{jd}\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\cdot\right)\psi\left(\cdot\right)\ast d\sigma\right)\left(x\right)=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\psi\left(x-u\right)d\sigma\left(u\right),

(2.4)

\displaystyle d\sigma_{j}^{O}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2^{jd}\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\cdot\right)\psi^{c}\left(\cdot\right)\ast d\sigma\right)\left(x\right)=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\psi^{c}\left(x-u\right)d\sigma\left(u\right),

where

is a smooth function supported in

and
*
denotes the usual Euclidean convolution as in (

and

,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{d}}d\sigma_{j}^{I}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\frac{1}{t^{2}}\varphi_{j,\ell}^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}}{t^{2}}\right).

Let

\left(1,1\right)
with a
2^{j}
bound. That is, for any
f\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)
and
\alpha>0
, it holds that

(3.1)

\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}_{j,\ell}^{I}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\alpha\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}

where the positive constant
C
is independent of
j
and
\ell
.

3.1
Preliminaries

Fix
j\geq 2
and
\ell\geq 0
. To establish (
3.1
), we define a new ball
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)
for
r>0
by

(3.2)

B\left(\bar{x},r\right)=\left\{\bar{y}=\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}\ \text{and}\ \left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}\right\}.

We shall show that
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)
satisfies the doubling property and that the associated Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is of weak type
\left(1,1\right)
.

Lemma 3.1

(Doubling property)
.

Let
0<s\leq r
.

For any
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)\cap B\left(\bar{y},s\right)\neq\emptyset
implies that
B\left(\bar{y},s\right)\subseteq B\left(\bar{x},3r\right)
,

\left\lvert B\left(\bar{x},3r\right)\right\rvert=3^{d+2}\left\lvert B\left(\bar{x},r\right)\right\rvert
.

Proof.

(1): Let
\bar{z}\in B\left(\bar{x},r\right)\cap B\left(\bar{y},s\right)
. Then, we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert,\ \left\lvert y-z\right\rvert

\displaystyle<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}},

\displaystyle\left\lvert x_{d+1}-z_{d+1}+\langle Jx,z\rangle\right\rvert,\ \left\lvert y_{d+1}-z_{d+1}+\langle Jy,z\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}.

Given
\bar{u}\in B\left(\bar{y},s\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert u-x\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert u-y\right\rvert+\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert+\left\lvert z-x\right\rvert<\frac{3r}{2^{j+\ell}},

\displaystyle\left\lvert x_{d+1}-u_{d+1}+\langle Jx,u\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert x_{d+1}-z_{d+1}+\langle Jx,z\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert z_{d+1}-y_{d+1}-\langle Jy,z\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\left\lvert y_{d+1}-u_{d+1}+\langle Jy,u\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\langle J\left(x-y\right),u-z\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}+\left\lvert\langle J\left(x-y\right),u-z\rangle\right\rvert.

Note that
\left\lvert\langle J\left(x-y\right),u-z\rangle\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\left\lvert u-z\right\rvert\leq\frac{2r}{2^{j+\ell}}\times\frac{2s}{2^{j+\ell}}\leq\frac{4r^{2}}{2^{2\left(j+\ell\right)}}<\frac{4r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}
from
j,\ell\geq 0
. Thus
\left\lvert x_{d+1}-u_{d+1}+\langle Jx,u\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{9r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}
and so
\bar{u}\in B\left(\bar{x},3r\right)
.

(2): Consider the Euclidean measure of
B\left(\bar{x},3r\right)
for an arbitrary
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

\displaystyle\left\lvert B\left(\bar{x},3r\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert<\frac{3r}{2^{j+\ell}}}\int_{\left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{9r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}}dy_{d+1}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert<\frac{3r}{2^{j+\ell}}}\int_{\left\lvert y_{d+1}\right\rvert<\frac{9r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}}dy_{d+1}dy

\displaystyle=3^{d+2}\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}}\int_{\left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}}dy_{d+1}dy=3^{d+2}\left\lvert B\left(\bar{x},r\right)\right\rvert.

Therefore, the new ball
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)
in (
3.2
) has the doubling property.
∎

In fact, our new ball
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)
is thicker than the original Carnot–Carathéodory ball with dimensions
\left(\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}\right)^{d}\times\left(\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}\right)^{2}
, thereby satisfying the doubling property. Now, using standard techniques associated with the doubling property, we can construct both the Whitney-type decomposition and the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition required for our analysis. As a preliminary step, we establish a Vitali covering lemma for coverings composed of
B\left(\bar{x},r\right)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Vitali Covering Lemma)
.

Let
S
be a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
S\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B\left(\bar{x}_{i},r_{i}\right)
for some
\bar{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
r_{i}>0
. Then there exists a disjoint subcollection
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of
\{B\left(\bar{x}_{i},r_{i}\right)\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lvert B_{i}\right\rvert\geq 3^{-\left(d+2\right)}\left\lvert S\right\rvert.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 1,p. 12]
. In that proof, only the doubling property of the balls
B(\bar{x},r)
is required. By combining this fact with our Lemma
3.1
, we can follow the same argument to obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Now, we define a maximal function
\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}\left(f\right)
by

(3.3)

\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r>0,\\
\bar{x}\in B\left(\bar{c},r\right),\\
\bar{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert B\left(\bar{c},t\right)\right\rvert}\int_{B\left(\bar{c},r\right)}\left\lvert f\left(\bar{y}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{y}.

Lemma 3.3
.

The maximal function
\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}
defined above is of weak type
(1,1)
. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
j
and
\ell
, such that for all
\alpha>0
and for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

(3.4)

\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\alpha\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Proof.

Arguing similarly as in Lemma
3.2
, we see that the claim follows directly from the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 1, p. 13]
.
∎

3.2
Proof of Theorem
3.1

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
3.1
by focusing on the two sets:

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}_{j,\ell}^{I}(f)(\bar{x})>\alpha\right\},

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}(f)(\bar{x})>\alpha\right\},

where the target set
F_{\alpha}
corresponds to the left-hand side of (
3.1
) and the exceptional set
E_{\alpha}
corresponds to the left-hand side of (
3.4
).

In the traditional approach to proving the weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of a maximal function via the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition, the target maximal function in
F_{\alpha}
and the maximal function in
E_{\alpha}
used in the decomposition (which defines the exceptional set
E_{\alpha}
) share the same form. Also, the corresponding target set
F_{\alpha}
and the exceptional set
E_{\alpha}
share the same height
\alpha
. For further details on more advanced applications of the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
and
[
13
]
. However, these approaches are not suitable for establishing the weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of our maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{j,\ell}^{I}
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we develop a variant of the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for the exceptional set
E_{\lambda(\alpha)}
, where we employ a different height
\lambda(\alpha)\neq\alpha
and the different maximal function
\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}\neq\mathcal{M}_{j,\ell}^{I}
as defined in (
3.3
).

Proposition 3.1

(Caldeón-Zygmund Decomposition)
.

Given
f\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)
and
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)>0
, let
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathfrak{M}_{j,\ell}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\right\}
. Then there exist disjoint collections
\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
where
B_{k}=B\left(\bar{c}_{k},r_{k}\right)
is defined in (
3.2
) such that

(3.5)

B_{k}\subseteq Q_{k}\subseteq B^{*}_{k}:=B\left(\bar{c}_{k},c_{1}r_{k}\right)\ \text{and}\ E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}B^{*}_{k}

where we set
c_{1}=6^{2}
with respect to the doubling constant
3
in Lemma
3.1
. With the above decomposition for
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}
, there exists also a decomposition of
f=g+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}
so that

(1)

g=f1_{E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}^{c}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{Q_{k}}1_{Q_{k}}
satisfies
\left\lvert g\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert\leq C\lambda\left(\alpha\right)
for almost everywhere
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
where
f_{Q_{k}}=\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q_{k}\right\rvert}\int_{Q_{k}}f\left(\bar{y}\right)d\bar{y}
,

(2)

each
b_{k}=\left(f-f_{Q_{k}}\right)1_{Q_{k}}
is supported in
Q_{k}\subseteq B^{*}_{k}
,

\int\left\lvert b_{k}\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C\lambda\left\lvert B^{*}_{k}\right\rvert\ \text{and}\ \int b_{k}\left(\bar{x}\right)d\bar{x}=0,

(3)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert B^{*}_{k}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}
.

Proof.

See the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 2, p. 15]
and
[
15
, Theorem 2, p. 17]
with
\lambda=\lambda\left(\alpha\right)
.
∎

Now, we shall prove Theorem
3.1
. To establish (
3.1
), we employ the Calderón–Zygmund decomposition introduced above with

(3.6)

\lambda\left(\alpha\right)=2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha.

Then, our exceptional set
E_{\lambda(\alpha)}
is given by

\displaystyle E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:M_{R}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:M_{R}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha\right\}.

The set

E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=E_{2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha}

is the key component of this section. We can replace
\alpha
in
E_{\alpha}
by
2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha
thanks to the good
L^{2}
-decay rate

2^{-j\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}2^{-\ell\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}

of
\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}
in (
2.16
). With this choice of height
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)=2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha
, we first control a good portion of
F_{\alpha}
as indicated in (
3.7
) below. On the other hand, note that this new exceptional set
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}
is smaller than the traditional exceptional set
E_{\alpha}
since
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)=2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha>\alpha
. Hence, we can enlarge
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}
to
\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}
as needed. Using this enlarged set
\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}
, we remove the worst part in the remaining bad portion of
F_{\alpha}
, where the Hörmander type condition (
3.14
) below cannot be controlled. After this elimination, we conclude (
3.1
) by verifying the Hörmander type condition (
3.14
) in Subsection
3.3
.

Let
f=g+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}
be the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition of
f
with
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=E_{2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha}
as defined in Proposition
3.1
. Then, to prove (
3.1
), it suffices to show the following two inequalities:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(g\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Proof of (
3.7
)
.

By applying the Chebyshev inequality to the left-hand side of (
3.7
) and using the
L^{2}
decay
2^{-j\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}2^{-\ell\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}
of
\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}
in (
2.16
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(g\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(g\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}\leq\frac{C2^{-j\left(d-2\right)}2^{-\ell d}}{\alpha^{2}}\int\left\lvert g\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert^{2}d\bar{x}.

From (1) of Proposition
3.1
with
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)=2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert g\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert\leq C2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha\ \text{for almost everywhere}\ \bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}.

Substituting this into the right-hand side of (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(g\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\alpha\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\int\left\lvert g\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)},

which implies (
3.7
).
∎

Proof of (
3.8
)
.

Now, we turn our attention to proving (
3.8
). First, we enlarge the exceptional set

E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=E_{2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha}

by anisotropic dilation. Recall the definition of our new ball
B\left(\bar{c},r\right)
in (
3.2
):

\displaystyle B\left(\bar{c},r\right)=\left\{\bar{y}=\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\left\lvert c-y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}\ \text{and}\ \left\lvert c_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jc,y\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}\right\}.

We then define the enlarged ball
\widetilde{B}\left(\bar{c},r\right)
by

(3.10)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}\left(\bar{c},r\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{y}=\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\left\lvert c-y\right\rvert<30r\ \text{and}\ \left\lvert c_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jc,y\rangle\right\rvert<\frac{(30r)^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}\right\}.

Accordingly, we enlarge
E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}B^{*}_{k}
(as in (
3.5
)) to

(3.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{B^{*}_{k}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}\left(\bar{c}_{k},c_{1}r_{k}\right).

To prove (
3.8
), we decompose the set
\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\left(\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{c}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right\rvert.

From (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{B^{*}_{k}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\left(j+\ell\right)d}\left\lvert\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}B^{*}_{k}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=C2^{\left(j+\ell\right)d}\left\lvert E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right\rvert.

By Lemma
3.3
with
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)=2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert E_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}=\frac{C}{2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C2^{\left(j+\ell\right)d}}{2^{j\left(d-1\right)}2^{\ell d}\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}=\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Thus, to show (
3.8
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\bar{x}\in\left(\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{c}:\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)>\frac{\alpha}{4}\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\alpha}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Rather than proving the above inequality, we shall establish the following estimate:

(3.12)

\left\lVert\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left(\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{c}\right)}\leq C2^{j}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Recall the definitions of our spherical maximal operator
\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}
in (
2.10
) and its kernel in (
2.8
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(f\right)\left(\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert f\ast_{\mathbb{H}}\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert\ \text{where}

\displaystyle\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(x,x_{d+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{t^{d}}d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\frac{2^{j}}{t^{2}}\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}}{t^{2}/2^{j}}\right)\ \text{if}\ \ell=0,\\
\frac{1}{t^{d}}d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\chi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)\ \text{if}\ \ell\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Since
\int b_{k}\left(\bar{x}\right)d\bar{x}=0
(see (2) in Proposition
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle b_{k}\ast_{\mathbb{H}}\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}b_{k}\left(\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\cdot\bar{x}\right)\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{y}\right)d\bar{y}=\int_{Q_{k}}\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)b_{k}\left(\bar{y}\right)d\bar{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{k}}\left[\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{c}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)\right]b_{k}\left(\bar{y}\right)d\bar{y}

where
\bar{c}_{k}
is the center of
B_{k}=B\left(\bar{c}_{k},r_{k}\right)\subseteq Q_{k}\subseteq B^{*}_{k}
. Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(b_{k}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left(\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{c}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{k}}\left(\int_{\left(\widetilde{B^{*}_{k}}\right)^{c}}\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{c}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left\lvert b_{k}\left(\bar{y}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{y}.

Now, if we can show that the following Hörmander type condition

(3.13)

\sup_{r>0}\sup_{\bar{y}\in B\left(\bar{c}_{k},r\right)}\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(\bar{c}_{k},r\right)\right)^{c}}\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{c}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C2^{j},

holds, then we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{M}^{I}_{j,\ell}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left(\widetilde{E}_{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{c}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{k}}\left\lvert b_{k}\left(\bar{y}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{y}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert B^{*}_{k}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\frac{1}{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}

\displaystyle=C2^{j}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)}.

Thus, (
3.8
) follows once we establish (
3.13
). We complete the proof of Theorem 3.1 by proving (
3.13
) in the following subsection.
∎

3.3
Hörmander Type Condition

Rather than proving (
3.13
) directly, by translating the center
\bar{c}_{k}
of
B_{k}
and
\widetilde{B^{*}_{k}}
to
0
, we shall prove the following Hörmander type condition:

(3.14)

\sup_{r>0}\sup_{\bar{y}\in B\left(0,r\right)}\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}}\sup_{t>0}\left\lvert\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C2^{j}.

To show (
3.14
), we consider the regions of
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
in (
3.14
). Put
\bar{c}=0
to (
3.2
) and (
3.10
). Then we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle B\left(0,r\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{y}=\left(y,y_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\left\lvert y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}\ \text{and}\ \left\lvert y_{d+1}\right\rvert<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}\right\},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)^{c}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bar{x}=\left(x,x_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\left\lvert x\right\rvert>30r\ \text{or}\ \left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert>\frac{(30r)^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}\right\}.

From now on, we fix
r>0
,
\left\lvert y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{j+\ell}}
, and
\left\lvert y_{d+1}\right\rvert<\frac{r^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}
.

In this subsection, we do not distinguish between
\psi
and
\chi
and hence use a smooth compact supported function
\psi
for all cases
\ell\geq 0
. Recall the definition of
\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}
in (
2.8
):

\displaystyle\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(x,x_{d+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{d}}d\sigma_{j}^{I}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right).

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\cdot\left(\bar{y}\right)^{-1}\right)-\left(K_{j,\ell}\right)_{t}\left(\bar{x}\right)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert+\left\lvert H\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert

where the Euclidean difference part
E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)
and the Heisenberg difference part
H\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)
are defined by

(3.17)

\displaystyle E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)=\frac{1}{t^{d}}\left\lvert d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)-d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right\rvert\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)\right\rvert,

(3.18)

\displaystyle H\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)=\frac{1}{t^{d}}\left\lvert d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right\rvert\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)-\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)\right\rvert.

To show (
3.14
), it suffices to show that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}}\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C2^{j},

(3.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}}\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert H\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C2^{j}.

Before proving the above two estimates, we recall the following well-known Mean-value Lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F\left(x\right)
be a function in Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 1
. Then, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\lvert F\left(x-y\right)-F\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C_{N}\left\lvert y\right\rvert}{\left(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert\right)^{N}}.

Proof of (
3.19
)
.

Decompose the sum over
n
as

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}.

Case I: the 1st sum
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}

To treat the integrand
\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert
, recall the definition of
d\sigma^{I}_{j}
in (
2.3
):

d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(x\right)=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\psi\left(x-u\right)d\sigma\left(u\right).

Then, together with
\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)-d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)\right)-\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)\right)\right\rvert d\sigma\left(u\right)

\displaystyle+2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\right\rvert\left\lvert\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)\right\rvert d\sigma\left(u\right).

Since
2^{n-1}\leq t\leq 2^{n}
and
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<2^{n-1}
, it holds that
\left\lvert 2^{j}\frac{y}{t}\right\rvert<1
. Thus, by applying Lemma
3.4
to the function
\chi^{\vee}
, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)\right)-\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)\right)\right\rvert\leq 2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{t}\right\rvert\frac{C_{N}}{\left(1+2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}-u\right\rvert\right)^{N}}.

Hence, by the above inequality and applying (
2.2
) with
N
sufficiently large, we deduce that

\displaystyle 2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)\right)-\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)\right)\right\rvert d\sigma\left(u\right)\leq 2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{t}\right\rvert\frac{C2^{j}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}.

Similarly, by applying the Mean-value theorem to
\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)
and using (
2.2
), one obtains

2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left\lvert\chi^{\vee}\left(2^{j}\left(x-u\right)\right)\right\rvert\left\lvert\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u-\frac{y}{t}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}-u\right)\right\rvert d\sigma\left(u\right)\leq 2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{t}\right\rvert\frac{C2^{j}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}.

Therefore, we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\lvert d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)-d\sigma^{I}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right\rvert\leq 2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{t}\right\rvert\frac{C2^{j}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}.

Also, since
\psi^{\vee}
belongs to the Schwartz class, we have

(3.23)

\left\lvert\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C_{N}}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{N}}

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, applying (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) to (
3.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert\leq C2^{j}\cdot 2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\frac{\left(1/2^{n}\right)^{d}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}\frac{\left(2^{j+\ell}/2^{2n}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{2^{2n}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{2}}.

By inserting this estimate to (
3.19
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}}\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert d\bar{x}\leq C2^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\lesssim 2^{j}.

Case II: the 2nd sum
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}

Combining the condition
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}
with

from (
3.15
), we have

(3.24)

2^{n-1}\leq 2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert<\frac{r}{2^{\ell}}\leq r\ \text{and}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
r\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}.

On the other hand, according to (
3.16
), the integral over
\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}
can be decomposed as

\int_{\left(\widetilde{B}\left(0,r\right)\right)^{c}}d\bar{x}=\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert>30r}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx_{d+1}dx+\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 30r}\int_{\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert>\frac{\left(30r\right)^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}}dx_{d+1}dx.

Case II-1: the 1st integral

From the range of
y
in (
3.24
) and the fact that
\left\lvert x\right\rvert>30r
in the first integral, we observe that
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert x\right\rvert
. Combining this with the upper bound of

in (
2.2
) and using

\displaystyle\left\lvert d\sigma_{j}^{I}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right\rvert,\left\lvert d\sigma_{j}^{I}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C2^{j}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert\right)}.

Also, note that
\left\lvert\frac{x}{t}\right\rvert>\frac{r}{t}\geq\frac{r}{2^{n}}
for

. Together with the above and (
3.23
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert\leq C2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{2^{n}}{r}\right\rvert\frac{\left(1/2^{n}\right)^{d}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}\frac{\left(2^{j+\ell}/2^{2n}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{2^{2n}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{2}}.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert>30r}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert dx_{d+1}dx\lesssim 2^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
r\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{2^{n}}{r}\right\rvert\lesssim 2^{j}.

Case II-2: the 2nd integral

From the range of
\bar{y}
in (
3.15
) and the conditions

and

Using this estimate and (
3.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\psi^{\vee}\left(\frac{x_{d+1}-y_{d+1}+\langle Jx,y\rangle}{t^{2}/2^{j+\ell}}\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert\right)}.

Also, since
\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert>\frac{\left(30r\right)^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}
, for
t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]
, it holds that
\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{t^{2}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert\geq\frac{r^{2}}{2^{2n}}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{2^{2n}}{r^{2}}\right\rvert\frac{\left(1/2^{n}\right)^{d}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x-y}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}\frac{\left(2^{j+\ell}/2^{2n}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{2^{2n}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+C2^{j}\left\lvert\frac{2^{2n}}{r^{2}}\right\rvert\frac{\left(1/2^{n}\right)^{d}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}\frac{\left(2^{j+\ell}/2^{2n}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{2^{j+\ell}}{2^{2n}}\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert\right)^{2}}.

Since
y
is fixed and
\int\frac{\left(1/2^{n}\right)^{d}}{\left(1+\left\lvert\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right\rvert\right)^{d+1}}dx\lesssim 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
2^{j}\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq 2^{n-1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 30r}\int_{\left\lvert x_{d+1}\right\rvert>\frac{\left(30r\right)^{2}}{2^{j+\ell}}}\sup_{t\in[2^{n-1},2^{n}]}\left\lvert E\left(\bar{x},\bar{y},t\right)\right\rvert dx_{d+1}dx\lesssim 2^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\

Therefore, we conclude that (
3.19
) holds.
∎

)
.

4
Some Reductions for the
L^{2}
Estimate for
\mathcal{M}_{j,\ell}^{I}

Now, we shall show that (

(4.1)

4.3

Recall that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)}\lesssim\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)}.

(4.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\ell\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\ \text{for}\ \ell>0,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert T_{j,0,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.

The following dilation invariant property helps us to treat the operator norm of single pieces.

Lemma 4.1

(Dilation)
.

For every
\left(j,\ell,k\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\right\rVert_{op}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T_{j,\ell,0}^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\right\rVert_{op}

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{op}
denotes the operaotor norm from
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
to
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)

Proof.

Express the phase
\psi^{\lambda}
and the amplitude
\Phi^{\lambda}
of

in (
4.22
) as

\displaystyle\phi^{\lambda}\left(x,\xi,t\right)

\displaystyle=x\cdot\xi+t\left\lvert\xi+\lambda Jx\right\rvert\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\Psi^{\lambda}\left(x,\xi,t\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{t\left\lvert\xi+\lambda Jx\right\rvert}{2^{j}}\right)\widetilde{\chi}_{j}\left(t\left(\xi+\lambda Jx\right)\right)\varphi_{j,\ell}\left(t^{2}\lambda\right).

Then

T_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\left(x,t\right)=2^{\frac{k}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}\left(x,\xi,t\right)}\chi\left(2^{k}t\right)\Psi^{\lambda}\left(x,\xi,t\right)\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)d\xi.

Using the substitution
\left(x,\xi,t\right)\mapsto\left(2^{-k}x,2^{k}\xi,2^{-k}t\right)

\displaystyle\phi^{\lambda}\left(2^{-k}x,2^{k}\xi,2^{-k}t\right)

\displaystyle=x\cdot\xi+t\left\lvert\xi+\lambda 2^{-2k}Jx\right\rvert=\phi^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\left(x,\xi,t\right)\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\Psi^{\lambda}\left(2^{-k}x,2^{k}\xi,2^{-k}t\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{t\left\lvert\xi+\lambda 2^{-2k}Jx\right\rvert}{2^{j}}\right)\widetilde{\chi}_{j}\left(t\left\lvert\xi+\lambda 2^{-2k}Jx\right\rvert\right)\varphi_{j,\ell}\left(t^{2}\lambda 2^{-2k}\right)

Hence it holds that

\displaystyle T_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\left(2^{-k}x,2^{-k}t\right)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{k}{2}}2^{k\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\left(x,\xi,t\right)}\chi\left(t\right)\Psi^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\left(x,\xi,t\right)\left(2^{k\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}\widehat{f}\left(2^{k}\xi\right)\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{k}{2}}2^{k\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}T_{j,\ell,0}^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\left(2^{-k\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}{f}\left(2^{-k}\cdot\right)\right)\left(x,t\right).

Then, by applying this to the second equation below, we see that

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert T_{j,\ell,k}^{\lambda}\left(f\right)\left(2^{-k}x,2^{-k}t\right)\right\rvert^{2}2^{-kd}2^{-k}dxdt

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T_{j,\ell,0}^{\lambda 2^{-2k}}\left(2^{-\frac{d}{2}k}{f}\left(2^{-k}\cdot\right)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)}^{2}.

Thus, together with
\left\lVert 2^{-\frac{d}{2}k}{f}\left(2^{-k}\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
, we obain (
4.25

∎

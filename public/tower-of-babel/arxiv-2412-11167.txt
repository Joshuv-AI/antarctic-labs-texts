Cultural Palette: Pluralising Culture Alignment via Multi-agent Palette
Jiahao Yuan, Zixiang Di, Shangzixin Zhao, Usman Naseem

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) face challenges in aligning with diverse cultural values despite their remarkable performance in generation, which stems from inherent monocultural biases and difficulties in capturing nuanced cultural semantics. Existing methods struggle to adapt to unkown culture after fine-tuning. Inspired by cultural geography across five continents, we propose Cultural Palette , a multi-agent framework that redefines cultural alignment as an adaptive "color-blending" process for country-specific adaptation. Our approach harnesses cultural geography across five continents (Africa, America, Asia, Europe, Oceania) through three key steps: First, we synthesize the Pentachromatic Cultural Palette Dataset using GPT-4o , refining continental-level dialogues with Hofstede’s cultural dimensions to establish foundational cultural representations. Second, five continent-level alignment agents form specialized cultural communities that generate region-specific draft responses. Third, a Meta Agent employs Cultural MoErges to dynamically blend these cultural "colors" through attention-gated parameter merging, akin to mixing pigments on a palette, resolving conflicts while preserving cultural nuances to produce the final culturally-aligned response. Extensive experiments across various countries demonstrate that Cultural Palette surpasses existing baselines in cultural alignment.

4.1
Cultural Palette for Meta Agent

Joint/Merging Alignment.

After constructing the Pentachromatic Cultural Palette dataset, we leverage it to perform continent-level alignment for LLMs as cultural decision-maker judger aligned with users, ensuring culturally relevant outputs tailored to diverse regions. Inspired by
Li et al. 2024c
, we introduce two alignment strategies to highlight the benefits of model merging for overall cultural pluralism. The first strategy, joint alignment, integrates all cultural dialogue data into a single LLM. The second strategy involves creating separate models for each culture, which are then merged into a unified LLM using separately various merging techniques including Ties
Yadav et al. 2024
, Model Stock
Jang et al. 2025
and MoErges
Goddard et al. 2024
;
Tang et al. 2024
;
Jiang et al. 2024
to demonstrate that MoErges exhibits the greatest potential for cultural alignment (see Section
5.2
).

Cultural MoErges (CM) for Meta Agent.

Inspired by MoErges
Goddard et al. 2024
;
Tang et al. 2024
;
Pourreza et al. 2024
, we propose Cultural MoErges to integrate culturally diverse knowledge. We first train five continent-specific LLMs, denoted as
\mathcal{E}_{Group}=\{E_{Africa},E_{Asia},E_{Europe},E_{America},E_{Oceania}\}
, each specializing in the cultural contexts of its respective region. These models are then merged into a unified MoErges model
LLM_{meta}
by selectively integrating the expert-specific feed-forward network (FFN) layers into a single basic LLM architecture and initializing a gating mechanism
Jiang et al. 2024

G
with weight matrix
W_{g}
utilizing hidden representations from the self-attention sub-modules corresponding to continent names, facilitating a weighted sum of each continent-aligned expert’s outputs. Consequently, Cultural MoErges leverages diverse cultural knowledge by dynamically activating the relevant continent-level expert layers aligned with the user prompt without retraining. Specifically, given user prompts
P
, the basic LLM generates
hidden_{P}
, the output of
LLM_{meta}
, denoted as
Output_{meta}
, is formally defined as:

\displaystyle G(P)=\mathrm{Softmax}(hidden_{P}\cdot W_{g}),

(1)

\displaystyle Output_{\text{meta}}=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{Group}}}G(P)\cdot E_{C}(P).

where
E_{C}(P)
are intermediate representations of continent-level expert.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce the Cultural Palette framework, a novel approach designed to enhance cultural alignment of LLMs conceptualizing the cultures of five continents as primary colors. We present the Pentachromatic Cultural Palette Dataset, which provides a comprehensive and diverse set of cultural dialogues, laying a solid foundation for training culturally aware LLMs. By integrating five continent-level agents for cultural drafts with a Meta Agent through
Cultural MoErges
mechanism for self-regulate aggregation, our framework dynamically adapts to diverse cultural contexts, significantly outperforming existing alignment techniques on continent-level cultural alignment and better alignment with real-world, country-level cultural distribution. Our research may inspire future studies on modeling diverse cultural alignment by leveraging continent-level agents to adapt to specific countries and their unique cultural contexts.

Limitations

Our Cultural Palette integrates the core cultural "colors" of five continents with a Meta Agent that blends these base colors into a unified multi-agent framework, symbolizing global cultural diversity
Crang 2013
. Extensive experiments and case studies show that our multi-agent framework enhances the cultural sensitivity and adaptability of LLMs. While the multi-agent setup introduces some computational complexity, as noted in
Feng et al. 2024
, we have addressed this by implementing optimization techniques such as efficient alignment
Dao
;
Zhao et al. 2024
and inference
Kwon et al. 2023
, effectively reducing computational burdens. As a result, our framework remains competitive with other cultural alignment techniques while ensuring better scalability.
Additionally, We envision ongoing efforts to distill the framework’s capabilities into smaller LLM variants, enabling more efficient deployment across a broader range of applications and devices.

Finally, as highlighted by
Xu et al. 2024
;
Feng et al. 2024
, culture definition and cultural map division are inherently diverse and multifaceted. We validate our Cultural Palette with continent-to-country alignment based on continental cultural geography
Crang 2013
, offering a flexible framework that can be adapted to other cultural maps
Lechner and Boli 2008
;
Inglehart and Welzel 2010
and encouraging exploration of alternative cultural divisions to expand the applicability of our Cultural Palette
.

Ethical Considerations

In this study, we use the PRISM
Kirk et al. 2024
as the source dataset for constructing Pentachromatic Cultural Palette Dataset, which is central to our analysis of cultural alignment in LLMs. Our utilization of this data adheres to established protocols and aligns with its intended purpose. While we aim to improve cultural alignment and reduce biases in LLMs, we acknowledge the potential risks of reinforcing stereotypes or misrepresenting cultures. To mitigate these risks, our dataset undergoes human ethics review after data synthesis, ensuring cultural fairness and ethical safety. Our goal is to promote a fair, inclusive, and pluralistic representation of cultural perspectives, while continuously addressing biases to enhance cultural fairness through our multi-agent review.

(6)

\displaystyle\lambda\underbrace{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left[-\log\sigma\left(\log\frac{p_{\theta}(Response_{p}^{(i)}|Query^{(i)})}{p_{\theta}(Response_{r_{k}}^{(i)}|Query^{(i)})}\right)\right]}_{\text{(b) Cross-continent Contrastive Alignment}}\Biggr]

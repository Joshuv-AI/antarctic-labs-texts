Abstract
. The three dimensional primitive equations with full viscosity are considered in a horizontally periodic box Ω \Omega , which are subject to either the homogeneous Neumann or Dirichlet conditions on the upper and bottom parts of the boundary. For a strong solution v v with initial data a a , we establish a priori bounds in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , ∞ , H 1 ​ ( Ω ) ) ∩ L 2 ​ ( 0 , ∞ , H ˙ 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{2}(\Omega)) , the exponential part of which is exp ⁡ ( C ​ ‖ a ‖ L 2 ​ ( Ω ) 2 ) \exp(C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}) . This is in contrast to the upper bounds reported in the existing literature that are double exponential. Furthermore, the uniform-in-time estimate for the Neumann condition case, in which the Poincaré inequality is unavailable for v v , seems to be new.

1.
Introduction

The primitive equations are known to be governing equations for geophysical flow of large-scale atmosphere and ocean.
We refer to the review articles
[
11
,
12
]
for more details on physical backgrounds of the primitive equations.
In a simplified setting where variations of temperature and salinity are neglected, they are described as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v-\Delta v+\nabla_{H}\pi

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{ in }\;\Omega\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\pi

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{ in }\;\Omega\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,u

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{ in }\;\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\Omega=G\times(-h,0)
is a box domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
G=(0,1)^{2}
, and
h,T
are positive constants.
Let the coordinate system be
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}=(x,y,z)^{\top}
.
The 3D velocity field is denoted by
u=(v,w)^{\top}=(v_{1},v_{2},w)^{\top}
, whose horizontal (in
x
- and
y
-directions) and vertical (in
z
-direction) components are
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
The pressure field, which is independent of
z
, is represented by
\pi:G\to\mathbb{R}
, and it is enforced that
\int_{G}\pi\,dxdy=0
to ensure its uniqueness.
The 3D and 2D gradient operators are written as
\nabla=(\nabla_{H},\partial_{z})^{\top}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y},\partial_{z})^{\top}
; accordingly, the 3D and 2D divergence operators, as well as the laplacians, are denoted by
\operatorname{div}u=\operatorname{div}_{H}v+\partial_{z}w
,
\operatorname{div}_{H}v=\partial_{x}v_{1}+\partial_{y}v_{2}
,
\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla
, and
\Delta_{H}=\operatorname{div}_{H}\nabla_{H}
.
In particular, the convection term means
u\cdot\nabla v=(u\cdot\nabla)v=v\cdot\nabla_{H}v+w\partial_{z}v
.

This is supplemented with the initial condition

(1.2)

v|_{t=0}=a\qquad\text{ in }\;\Omega\times\{0\},

and with one of the following sets of boundary conditions:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}v=0,\quad w=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\;(\Gamma_{u}\cup\Gamma_{b})\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\text{$u$, $\pi$ are periodic}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\;\Gamma_{l}\times(0,T),

which we refer to as
the Neumann BC case
, and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}v=0,\quad w=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\;\Gamma_{u}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle v=0,\quad w=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\;\Gamma_{b}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\text{$u$, $\pi$ are periodic}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\;\Gamma_{l}\times(0,T),

which we refer to as
the Dirichlet BC case
.
Here,
\Gamma_{u}=G\times\{0\}
,
\Gamma_{b}=G\times\{-h\}
, and
\Gamma_{l}=\partial G\times[-h,0]
denote the upper, bottom, and lateral parts of the boundary of
\Omega
, respectively.
We could call (
1.4
) mixed Dirichlet–Neumann boundary conditions as in
[
4
]
; nevertheless, we employ the above terminology since a suitable reflection principle (cf.
[
4
, proof of Lemma 4.3]
) can reduce (
1.4
) to a pure Dirichlet problem.

It is well known that the original system (
1.1
) can be reformulated, in terms of the prognostic variable
v
, as

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla_{H}v+w\partial_{z}v-\Delta v+\nabla_{H}\pi=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\;\Omega\times(0,T),

\displaystyle w=-\int_{-h}^{z}\operatorname{div}_{H}v\,d\zeta\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\;\Omega\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{H}\bar{v}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\;G\times(0,T),

where
\bar{v}=\bar{v}(x,y):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{-h}^{0}v(x,y,z)\,dz
denotes the vertical average of
v
in the last equality (it results from the 3D divergence-free condition
\operatorname{div}u=0
combined with the impermeability condition
w=0
on
\Gamma_{u}\cup\Gamma_{b}
).
Based on this representation,
[
6
]
proved local-in-time existence and uniqueness of a strong solution in the maximal
L^{2}
-regularity class, that is,
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
by the Galerkin method (one can also show
v\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
).
Later, in the cerebrated work by Cao and Titi
[
3
]
, this solution was extended globally in time by establishing an
a priori
estimate in the same class.
More details on earlier and recent developments in mathematical analysis of the primitive equations can be found in
[
11
,
12
]
.

The present work focuses on the structure of the key
a priori
estimate of
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
, which we call
the
H^{1}
-upper bound for the primitive equations
.
To be more specific, the
H^{1}
-upper bound, designated as
K_{V}(t)
in
[
3
]
, is triple exponential with respect to
a
and double exponentially growing in
t
.
In fact, from equations (78), (76), (67), and (58) of
[
3
]
we see that
K_{V}(t)\lesssim e^{K_{1}(t)K_{z}(t)}
,
K_{1}(t)\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
,
K_{z}(t)\lesssim e^{K_{6}(t)^{2/3}t}
, and
K_{6}(t)\lesssim e^{K_{1}(t)^{2}}
(we consider only the principal part and assume for simplicity that the temperature equals
0
), so that

K_{V}(t)\lesssim\exp\big[\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\exp\big(\exp(2/3\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4})t\big)\big].

In
[
9
]
, thanks to the uniform Gronwall inequality, a uniform-in-time
H^{1}
-upper bound is obtained, thus eliminating the dependence on
t
from above.
We remark that the Poincaré inequality
\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is available in the setting of
[
3
,
9
]
due to the slip boundary condition
v\cdot n_{\Gamma_{l}}=0
and
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{\Gamma_{l}}}\times n_{\Gamma_{l}}=0
on
\Gamma_{l}
(
n_{\Gamma_{l}}
is the outer unit normal vector to
\Gamma_{l}
), which enables the use of the uniform Gronwall inequality.
However, the
H^{1}
-bound of
[
9
]
is still at least double exponential since its
a priori
estimates follow those of
[
3
]
.

For the Dirichlet BC case,
[
10
]
established global well-posedness by combining the
L^{6}
-estimate for
v
and
L^{2}
-estimate for
v_{z}:=\partial_{z}v
.
One of the key arguments in
[
10
]
is introduction of
\delta
(see
[
10
, (2.12)]
) such that

\int_{t}^{t+2\delta}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq(\text{sufficiently small constant})\qquad\forall t\in[0,T_{\max}].

However, because the way such
\delta
depends on
T_{\max}
or on initial data
a
is
a priori
unknown, the final
H^{1}
-upper bound obtained in
[
10
, p. 2748]
, the principal part of which reads
O(e^{\delta^{-2}})
, can grow larger than single exponential functions of
T_{\max}
and
a
.
A different strategy was shown by
[
7
]
, which combines the
L^{4}
-estimate for
v
(in more precise, for
\tilde{v}:=v-\bar{v}
) and
L^{2}
-estimate for
v_{z}
.
As in
[
7
, p. 1108]
, the obtained
H^{1}
-upper bound is double exponential and exponentially depends on
T
, whereas exponential decay is proved for the long time behavior as
t\to\infty
by utilizing
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|v(s)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds<\infty
and a small-data-global-existence theory of the primitive equations (the Poincaré inequality is also essential in this result).

More refined
a priori
estimates for the primitive equations including temperature are presented in
[
1
]
, where only the horizontal viscosity (and diffusivity) is considered.
Namely, a more difficult problem than (
1.1
) is treated there (see also
[
2
,
8
]
).
However, as far as the structure of
H^{1}
-upper bounds is concerned, the bounds obtained in
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
are at least double exponential because of the use of the logarithmic Sobolev and Gronwall inequalities (see
[
1
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]
).
Recently, long time behavior of solutions with some smallness assumptions on initial data is examined in
[
13
]
.

In this paper, we consider the primitive equations (
1.1
) with the full viscosity and establish
H^{1}
-upper bounds that are only single exponential with respect to
a
and are independent of
t
for both of the Neumann and Dirichlet BC cases.
Namely, the following is our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the initial data satisfies
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
a_{z}:=\partial_{z}a\in L^{3}(\Omega)
, as well as the compatibility conditions:
\operatorname{div}_{H}\bar{a}=0
, the horizontal periodicity, and
a=0
on
\Gamma_{b}
in case of (
1.4
).
Then the solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with (
1.3
) or (
1.4
) satisfies

\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq B(a)e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

where
B(a)
is a polynomial function of
\|a\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\|a_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}
, and
C
is a positive constant dependent only on
\Omega
.

Remark 1
.

(i) Since the above
H^{1}
-bound is independent of
T
, one can even have

\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla^{2}v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq B(a)e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty).

(ii)
B(a)
actually depends on
\|a\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
and
\|a\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}
(see e.g. (
3.5
) below), which can be bounded by
\|a\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
in virtue of the Sobolev embedding theorem.

(iii) For the Neumann BC case, since
\int_{\Omega}a\,dxdydz\neq 0
in general, we do not expect for
v
to satisfy the Poincaré inequality as in
[
12
, (3.174) and (3.279)]
.
To the best of our knowledge, a uniform-in-time
H^{1}
-upper bound for such a situation was not shown in the existing literature.

(iv) For the Dirichlet BC case, if the Poincaré inequality is taken into account, an exponential decaying bound as
t\to\infty
could be derived.
Nevertheless it is not directly addressed in this paper.

As a building block to prove the theorem above, we first obtain in Section
2
the following result for the Neumann BC case.

Proposition 1
.

If
a\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{8}(\Omega)
,
a_{z}\in L^{4}(\Omega)
,
\operatorname{div}_{H}\bar{a}=0
, and the horizontal periodicity of
a
hold, then the solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with (
1.3
) satisfies

\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq B(a,t)e^{C(\sqrt{t}+1)\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

where
B(a,t)
is a polynomial functions of
\|a\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\|a\|_{L^{8}(\Omega)},\|a_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)},\sqrt{t}
, and
C
is a positive constant dependent only on
\Omega
.

The proof of Proposition
1
, in which we utilize the
L^{4}(\Omega)
- and
L^{8}(\Omega)
-estimates for
v
as well as
L^{2}(\Omega)
- and
L^{4}(\Omega)
-estimates for
v_{z}
, is more straightforward than that of Theorem
1
.
In fact, the dissipative parts associated with those estimates are nicely consistent in dealing with the “most difficult nonlinearity” of the primitive equations, i.e.,
wv_{z}
(see (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) below).
However, there are two drawbacks with this approach: 1) the technique is not directly applicable to the Dirichlet BC case, and 2) the bound depends on
t
.

In Section
3
we overcome the first difficulty and prove Theorem
1
for the Dirichlet BC case.
The idea is to make use of the
L^{6}(\Omega)
-estimate for
v
and
L^{3}(\Omega)
-estimate for
v_{z}
(instead of
L^{8}
- and
L^{4}
-estimates).
Employing a similar strategy, in Section
4
we eliminate time dependency from the
H^{1}
-upper bound and prove Theorem
1
for the Neumann BC case.
Since the main issue here is treatment of “lower order terms” (see e.g. (
4.2
)), a Poincaré-type inequality is a key ingredient for this observation.
Thus we are led to consider
L^{4}(\Omega)
- and
L^{6}(\Omega)
-estimates for
\tilde{v}=v-\bar{v}
in place of
v
, which satisfies
\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq h\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
(cf.
[
5
, (6.4)]
).

We will exploit the standard notation of the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(D)
and the Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(D)
in a Lipschitz domain
D
.
Norms of a function
f
in isotropic spaces are denoted as

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{z}L^{q}_{x,y}}=\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\|f(z)\|_{L^{q}(G)}^{p}\,dz\Big)^{1/p}=\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\Big(\int_{G}|f(x,y,z)|^{q}\,dxdy\Big)^{p/q}\,dz\Big)^{1/p},

with appropriate modifications for the cases
p,q=\infty
.
For integration in
\Omega
and
G
, we will omit the symbols of volume elements, i.e.,
dxdydz
and
dxdy
.
The
L^{2}
-inner products in
\Omega
and
G
are denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{G}
, respectively.

Finally, we recall the Gronwall inequality of the following form: if nonnegative functions
y(t),z(t),\alpha(t)
, and
\beta(t)
satisfy
\frac{dy}{dt}(t)+z(t)\leq\alpha(t)y(t)+\beta(t)
for
t>0
, then for all
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(y(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\,ds\Big)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\,ds},

\displaystyle y(t)+\int_{0}^{t}z(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\Big(y(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\,ds\Big)\Big(1+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\,ds\,e^{\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\,ds}\Big).

2.
Proof of Proposition
1

To prove Proposition
1
(and Theorem
1
as well) we may assume that a sufficiently smooth solution for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with (
1.3
) or (
1.4
) uniquely exists; in fact, this is already known by
[
5
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3]
.
In the following,
C
denotes a generic positive constant which may depend only on
\Omega
unless otherwise stated.

2.1.
Step 1: estimates for
v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{q}(\Omega)

Although this step is essentially known (see
[
1
, Proposition 3.1(iii)]
), we present the calculation for completeness.
First we multiply (
1.5
) by
v
and integrate over
\Omega\times[0,t]
to obtain

(2.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next, to get an expression of
\pi
in terms of
v
, we apply
\frac{1}{h}\operatorname{div}_{H}\int_{-h}^{0}(\cdot)\,dz
to (
1.5
)
1
.
It follows that, since
\int_{-h}^{0}wv_{z}\,dz=\int_{-h}^{0}(\operatorname{div}_{H}v)v\,dz
,

(2.2)

-\Delta_{H}\pi=\frac{1}{h}\operatorname{div}_{H}\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)\,dz\Big),\quad\nabla_{H}\pi=\frac{1}{h}\big[\nabla_{H}(-\Delta_{H})^{-1}\operatorname{div}_{H}\big]\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)\,dz,

where
\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)
means the vector whose
i
-th component is
\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{j}}(v_{i}v_{j})
for
i=1,2
.
We then apply the Calderon–Zygmund inequality (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
) and the Minkowski inequality to deduce that

(2.3)

\|\nabla_{H}\pi\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\Big\|\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)\,dz\Big\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{q}(G)}\,dz\quad(q\in(1,\infty)),

where the constant
C
may depend on
q
.

Let us multiply (
1.5
)
1
by
|v|^{q-2}v\,(q>2)
and use the cancelation property of the convection term, that is,
(u\cdot\nabla v,|v|^{q-2}v)_{\Omega}=0
for the divergence-free 3D vector
u
with the vanishing (or periodic) normal components on the boundary.
It follows from integration by parts that

(2.4)

\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{4(q-2)}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=-\Big(\nabla_{H}\pi,\int_{-h}^{0}|v|^{q-2}v\,dz\Big)_{G}.

Substituting (
2.3
) and using the Hölder inequality with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q-2}{2q}=1
, we see that the right-hand side above is bounded by

(2.5)

\|\nabla_{H}\pi\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+2}}(G)}\Big\|\int_{-h}^{0}|v|^{q-2}v\,dz\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(G)}\leq\int_{-h}^{0}\|v\|_{L^{q}(G)}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(G)}\,dz\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v|^{q-1}\big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(G)}\,dz.

The first integral is bounded by
\|1\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}_{z}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
To estimate the second one, by the embedding
W^{1,\frac{q}{q-1}}(G)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(G)
as well as the Hölder inequality with
\frac{q-2}{2q}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{q-1}{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\big\||v|^{q-1}\big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\big\||v|^{q-1}\big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(G)}+\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{q-1}\big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(G)})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v\|_{L^{q}(G)}^{q-1}+\big\||v|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\nabla_{H}|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(G)})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|v\|_{L^{q}(G)}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{L^{q}(G)}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}).

Consequently,

\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v|^{q-1}\big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(G)}\,dz\leq C\|1\|_{L^{q}_{z}}(\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Since the right-hand side of (
2.5
) is bounded by

C(\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{q-2}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

we obtain, for all
t>0
,

(2.6)

\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{3(q-2)}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla|v|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Now the Gronwall inequality and
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq\frac{\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2}
(recall (
2.1
)) conclude, for all
t\geq 0
and
q\in(2,\infty)
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\||v|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|a\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+(\sqrt{t}+1)\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\big(1+(\sqrt{t}+1)\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}e^{C(\sqrt{t}+1)\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big)=:A_{1}(q,a,t),

provided that the initial data satisfy
a\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.
In particular, we will make use of these estimates for
q=4
and
q=8
below.

2.2.
Step 2: estimates for
v_{z}:=\partial_{z}v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{r}(\Omega)\,(r=2,4)

Although we will restrict to the cases
r=2
and
r=4
in the end, we first multiply (
1.5
)
1
by
-\partial_{z}(|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})
and perform integration by parts in
\Omega
with general
r\geq 2
.
Observing that no boundary terms appear and using the cancelation property for the convection term, we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{4(r-2)}{r^{2}}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}-1}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(u_{z}\cdot\nabla v,|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-(v_{z}\cdot\nabla_{H}v,|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Omega}+(\operatorname{div}_{H}v,|v_{z}|^{r})_{\Omega}.

By further integration by parts with respect to horizontal derivatives, the right-hand side terms are bounded as

(2.9)

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|v||v_{z}|^{r-1}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\||v||v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}-1}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{\frac{r}{2}-1}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||v||v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where the first term can be absorbed into the left-hand side.

To treat the second term, we consider either
r=2
or
r=4
.
If
r=2
, we find immediately from (
2.7
) for
q=4
that
\int_{0}^{t}\big\||v||v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq A_{1}(4,a,t)
.
Therefore, just integration of (
2.8
) with respect to
t
gives us, if
a_{z}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

(2.10)

\frac{1}{2}\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{9}{10}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\|a_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+CA_{1}(4,a,t).

Next, for
r=4
we observe from integration by parts that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\big\||v||v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}(v_{z}\cdot v_{z})|v_{z}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z}(|v|^{2})\cdot\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}(|v|^{2})|v_{z}^{2}|}_{\leq 0}-\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}(v\cdot\partial_{z}v_{z})|v_{z}|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}(v\cdot v_{z})2(v_{z}\cdot\partial_{z}v_{z})

\displaystyle\leq 3(|v|^{3}|v_{z}|,|v_{z}||\partial_{z}v_{z}|)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v_{z}||\partial_{z}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||v|^{3}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore, if
a_{z}\in L^{4}(\Omega)
, then (
2.7
) for
q=8
leads to, for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{8}{10}\big\||v_{z}||\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|a_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+CA_{1}(8,a,t)=:A_{2}(a,t).

2.3.
Step 3: estimate for
\nabla_{H}v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\Omega)

Multiply (
1.5
)
1
by
-\Delta_{H}v
and integrate by parts over
\Omega
to obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(v\cdot\nabla_{H}v,\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}+(wv_{z},\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla_{H}^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|wv_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\nabla_{H}^{2}v
means the Hessian tensor of rank
3
for a vector-valued function
v
.
We apply an anisotropic estimate (cf.
[
7
, p. 1108]
) to handle the last term as

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|wv_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{4}_{xy}}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\operatorname{div}_{H}v\|_{L^{2}_{z}L^{4}_{xy}}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)},

so that

\displaystyle\|wv_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}(\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3/2}+\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\Delta_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+1).

Therefore, just integration of (
2.13
) with respect to

, combined with (
2.7
) for
q=4
and with (
2.12
) yields, for all

,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{H}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{8}{10}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{H}a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+CA_{1}(4,a,t)+C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(A_{2}(a,t)+1).

This together with (
2.10
) concludes the desired upper bound for
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1
for Dirichlet BC case

Let us turn our attention to the boundary condition (
1.4
), in which case the pressure
\pi
involves an additional component.
In fact, the Poisson equation in (
2.2
) is replaced as

-\Delta_{H}\pi=\frac{1}{h}\operatorname{div}_{H}\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)\,dz\Big)-\frac{1}{h}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v_{z}|_{\Gamma_{b}}),

that is,

\nabla_{H}\pi=\frac{1}{h}\big[\nabla_{H}(-\Delta_{H})^{-1}\operatorname{div}_{H}\big]\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(v\otimes v)\,dz\Big)-\frac{1}{h}\big[\nabla_{H}(-\Delta_{H})^{-1}\operatorname{div}_{H}\big](v_{z}|_{\Gamma_{b}})=:\nabla_{H}\pi_{1}+\nabla_{H}\pi_{2}.

The Calderon–Zygmund inequality implies that, for
q\in(0,\infty)
,

\|\nabla_{H}\pi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{q}(G)}\,dz,\qquad\|\nabla_{H}\pi_{2}\|_{L^{q}(G)}\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{q}(\Gamma_{b})}.

On the one hand, since the appearance of
\pi_{2}
—especially
(\nabla_{H}\pi_{2},|v|^{q-2}v)_{\Omega}
—prevents the
L^{q}(\Omega)
-estimate for
v
from being closed solely by itself, we need to combine it with
L^{r}(\Omega)
-estimate for
v_{z}
.
On the other hand, in the latter estimate we encounter
(\nabla_{H}\pi_{1},|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Gamma_{b}}
, which need be taken care by the former one.
Below we show that choosing the two combinations
(q,r)=(4,2),(6,3)
enables us to resolve this delicate interdependence between the two estimates, which was absent in the Neumann BC case.

3.1.
Step 1: estimates for
v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{4}(\Omega)
and
v_{z}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\Omega)

The energy equality (
2.1
) remains the same, and we also have
\int_{0}^{t}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,(\forall t\geq 0)
as a result of the Poincaré inequality.
Addressing
\pi_{1}
in the same way as before, in place of (
2.4
) for
q=4
we obtain

(3.1)

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{3}{8}\big\|\nabla|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v||\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}-\Big(\nabla_{H}\pi_{2},\int_{-h}^{0}|v|^{2}v\,dz\Big)_{G}.

By the estimate of
\nabla_{H}\pi_{2}
mentioned above and a trace inequality, we estimate the last term as

(3.2)

C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v|^{3}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}dz\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{3}(G)}^{3/2}\,dz.

Since
H^{1/3}(G)\hookrightarrow L^{3}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\big\||v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{3}(G)}^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\||v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}\big(\big\||v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{4}(G)}^{2}\big(\|v\|_{L^{4}(G)}+\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{1/2}\big).

Hence the right-hand side of (
3.2
) is bounded by

\displaystyle C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\cdot\|1\|_{L^{4}_{z}}C\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\big(\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\big\|\nabla_{H}|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2/3}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}.

This together with (
3.1
), with
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
, and with
\alpha^{2}\beta^{3}\leq\frac{3}{4}\alpha^{2}\beta^{4}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{2}
leads to

(3.3)

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{2}{8}\big\|\nabla|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v||\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+1)+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next, in place of (
2.8
) for
r=2
we have

(3.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{9}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\big\||v||v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{h}(\nabla_{H}\pi,v_{z}|_{\Gamma_{b}})_{G},

where we see that

\displaystyle|(\nabla_{H}\pi_{1},v_{z}|_{\Gamma_{b}})_{G}|\leq C\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle|(\nabla_{H}\pi_{2},v_{z}|_{\Gamma_{b}})_{G}|\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Now, addition of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) deduces

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{4}\big\|\nabla|v|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{9}{10}\big\||v||\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{7}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+1).

Thus we conclude from the Gronwall inequality that, for all
t\geq 0
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\big\||v||\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|a\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|a_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\big(1+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big)=:B_{1}(a).

3.2.
Step 2: estimates for
v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{6}(\Omega)
and
v_{z}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{3}(\Omega)

In place of (
2.4
) for
q=6
we have

(3.6)

\frac{1}{6}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{6}+\frac{4}{9}\|\nabla|v|^{3}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v|^{2}|\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{6}+1)-\Big(\nabla_{H}\pi_{2},\int_{-h}^{0}|v|^{4}v\,dz\Big)_{G}.

In virtue of the trace inequality
\|v_{z}\|_{L^{6}(\Gamma_{b})}=\big\||v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}^{2/3}\leq C\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2/3}
, the last term is estimated, as a result of the Young and Hölder inequalities with
\frac{1}{6}+\frac{5}{6}=1
and the embedding

, by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\pi_{2}\|_{L^{6}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v|^{5}\big\|_{L^{6/5}(G)}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{6}(\Gamma_{b})}\int_{-h}^{0}\|v\|_{L^{6}(G)}^{5}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}\|v\|_{L^{6}(G)}^{5}\,dz\Big)^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|1\|_{L^{6}_{z}}^{3/2}\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{15/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{3/2}\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{6}.

Since
\alpha^{3/2}\leq(\alpha^{2}+\alpha)/2
, the contribution of the last term above is the same as that of the first term in the right-hand side of (

).
Consequently,

(3.7)

\frac{1}{6}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{6}+\big\||v|^{2}|\nabla v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{6}+1)+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next, in place of (
2.8
) for
r=3
we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+\frac{4}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(|v||v_{z}|^{3/2},|v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big)_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{h}\big(\nabla_{H}\pi,|v_{z}|v_{z}\big)_{\Gamma_{b}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||v||v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{h}\big(\nabla_{H}\pi_{1},|v_{z}|v_{z}\big)_{\Gamma_{b}}+\frac{1}{h}\big(\nabla_{H}\pi_{2},|v_{z}|v_{z}\big)_{\Gamma_{b}}.

The second term in the right-hand side is estimated, in virtue of
H^{3/8}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{8/3}(\Omega)
and
H^{1/2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{3}(\Omega)
, by

(3.9)

\displaystyle C\big(|v|^{2}|v_{z}|,|v_{z}|^{2}\big)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}=\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{8/3}(\Omega)}^{8/3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\underbrace{\Big[\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{8}}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{8}}\Big]^{\frac{8}{3}}}_{=\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{5/2}\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\underbrace{C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{4}}_{\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\hskip-17.07164pt\cdot\hskip 5.69054pt\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}.

Let us address the remaining two pressure terms in (
3.8
).
The term for
\pi_{1}
is bounded, in virtue of the trace inequality
\|f\|_{L^{8/3}(\Gamma_{b})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/4}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3/4}
if

on
\Gamma_{u}
(cf.
[
7
, p. 1109]
), by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\pi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\big\||v_{z}|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}\,dz\,\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}(\Gamma_{b})}^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|1\|_{L^{2}_{z}}\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\underbrace{\Big[\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{4}}\Big]^{\frac{4}{3}}}_{=\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The term for
\pi_{2}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\pi_{2}\|_{L^{3}(G)}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Gamma_{b})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Gamma_{b})}^{3}=C\big\||v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}^{2}\leq C\big\||v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}}_{\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\hskip-17.07164pt\cdot\hskip 5.69054pt\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore, we deduce that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{9}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{10}\big\||v|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We are now in a position to add (
3.7
) and (
3.10
) to arrive at

\frac{dY}{dt}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}Y+C\big(\big\||v||\nabla_{H}v|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)Y^{1/3},

where we set

.
After division by

,

Y(t)^{2/3}\leq e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big(Y(0)^{2/3}+CB_{1}(a)+CB_{1}(a)^{2}+C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big).

We thus obtain

\|v(t)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq Ce^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big(\|a\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|a_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+B_{1}(a)+B_{1}(a)^{2}\big)=:B_{2}(a),

which involves only single exponential functions.

3.3.
Step 3: estimate for
\nabla_{H}v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\Omega)

This part is almost the same as in Subsection

, except that we replace (

), using

\|wv_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{6}_{xy}}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\operatorname{div}_{H}v\|_{L^{2}_{z}L^{6}_{xy}}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/3}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2/3}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{6}+\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2})+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Now, integration of (
2.13
) in

as before yields, for all

,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{H}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{8}{10}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{H}a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+CB_{1}(a)+C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(B_{2}(a)^{3}+B_{2}(a)),

.

4.
Proof of Theorem
3
for Neumann BC case

Let us return to the Neumann BC case (
1.3
) and eliminate the factor
e^{\sqrt{t}}
from the upper bound of Proposition
1
.
The cause of this factor is the lower-order term
\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}
in (

).
To address it we desire a Poincaré-type inequality and an embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
.
For this reason we employ an
L^{6}(\Omega)
-estimate as in Section
3
for
\tilde{v}=v-\bar{v}
, instead of

itself, which satisfies

(4.1)

\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\quad(q\in[1,\infty]).

The above inequality follows from integration in
G
of the one-dimensional Wirtinger inequality

\int_{-h}^{0}|\tilde{v}(x,y,z)|^{q}\,dz\leq h^{q}\int_{-h}^{0}|v_{z}(x,y,z)|^{q}\,dz\quad\text{a.e.}\;(x,y)\in G.

We also observe that
\tilde{v}
satisfies (cf.
[
7
, (6.4)]
)

\partial_{t}\tilde{v}-\Delta\tilde{v}+u\cdot\nabla\tilde{v}=-\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v}+\frac{1}{h}\int_{-h}^{0}\operatorname{div}_{H}(\tilde{v}\otimes\tilde{v})\,dz\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T).

4.1.
Step 1: estimate for
\tilde{v}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{q}(\Omega)\,(q\in(2,6])

We multiply the above equation by
|\tilde{v}|^{q-2}\tilde{v}
and integrate over
\Omega
to obtain

\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{4(q-2)}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla\tilde{v}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\Big(\int_{-h}^{0}|\tilde{v}||\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}|\,dz,|\tilde{v}|^{q-1}\Big)_{\Omega}-(\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v},|\tilde{v}|^{q-2}\tilde{v})_{\Omega}.

The first term in the right-hand side is bounded, thanks to calculation similar to Subsection
2.1
, by

(4.2)

\displaystyle C(\|\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{q-2}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla_{H}|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+1)+\frac{q-2}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla_{H}|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we have used
\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\tilde{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
(recall (
4.1
)) for
q\leq 6
to address the lower-order term.
The second term is bounded, again by calculation similar to Subsection
2.1
, by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||\tilde{v}|^{q}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)})\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{q-2}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla_{H}|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}(\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+1)+\frac{q-2}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla_{H}|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore, it follows that

\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{2(q-2)}{q^{2}}\big\|\nabla|\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla\tilde{v}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+1),

which yields, by the Gronwall inequality, for all
t\geq 0
,

\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\||\tilde{v}|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|\nabla\tilde{v}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq C\big(\|\tilde{a}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\big(1+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big)=:E_{1}(q,a).

4.2.
Step 2: estimates for
v_{z}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{r}(\Omega)\,(r=2,3)

We find from (
2.8
) that, since
\operatorname{div}_{H}v=\operatorname{div}_{H}\tilde{v}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{4(r-2)}{r^{2}}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{r}{2}-1}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v_{z}\cdot\nabla_{H}v,|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Omega}+(\operatorname{div}_{H}v,|v_{z}|^{r})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v_{z}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v},|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Omega}-(v_{z}\cdot\nabla_{H}\tilde{v},|v_{z}|^{r-2}v_{z})_{\Omega}+(\operatorname{div}_{H}\tilde{v},|v_{z}|^{r})_{\Omega}.

If
r=2
, then the first term and the last two terms in the right-hand side are bounded by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(G)}^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\|v_{z}\|_{H^{1}(G)}\,dz\leq\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and by

C\big\||\tilde{v}||v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla_{H}v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

respectively. Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{8}{10}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\big\||\tilde{v}|\,|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

which implies (note that
v_{z}=\tilde{v}_{z}
), for all
t\geq 0
,

\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds\leq C\big(\|a_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+E_{1}(4,a)\big)\big(1+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big)=:E_{2}(a),

by the Gronwall inequality.

If
r=3
, then the first term and the last two terms of (
4.3
) are bounded by

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}(G)}^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\int_{-h}^{0}\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(G)}\big\||v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{H^{1}(G)}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and by (recall (
3.9
) for similar calculation)

\displaystyle C\big(|\tilde{v}||v_{z}|^{3/2},|v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\||\tilde{v}||v_{z}|^{3/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla_{H}v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

respectively. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+\frac{2}{9}\big\|\nabla|v_{z}|^{3/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{8}{10}\big\||v_{z}|^{1/2}|\nabla v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+C\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

in particular,

\frac{d}{dt}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+1)\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+1)+C\Big(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla v_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\||\tilde{v}|^{2}|v_{z}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\big(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+1\big)^{1/3}.

Dividing the both sides by
\big(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+1\big)^{1/3}
and applying the Gronwall inequality, we obtain, for all
t\geq 0
,

\|v_{z}(t)\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq Ce^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\big(\|a_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+E_{2}(a)^{2}+E_{1}(6,a)\big)=:E_{3}(a).

4.3.
Step 3: estimate for
\nabla_{H}v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\Omega)

In place of (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\bar{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}v+\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v},\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}+(\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}+wv_{z},\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(\bar{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}v+\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v},\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}+C\big\||\tilde{v}||\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{6}+\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2})+\frac{2}{10}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we have addressed
\|wv_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in the same way as in Subsection
3.3
.
From
\operatorname{div}_{H}\bar{v}=0
and
\alpha^{3}\leq(\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{2})/2
, we find that

\displaystyle(\bar{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}v,\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{H}\bar{v})(\nabla_{H}v)(\nabla_{H}v)^{\top}-\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}(\bar{v}\cdot\nabla_{H})|\nabla_{H}v|^{2}}_{=0}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\int_{-h}^{0}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{4}(G)}^{2}\,dz\leq C\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}_{z}H^{1}_{xy}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{1}{10}\|\nabla_{H}^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Finally, observe that

\displaystyle(\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{H}\bar{v},\Delta_{H}v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{4}(G)}\|\Delta_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{1/2}\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{H^{1}(G)}^{1/2}\|\Delta_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}(\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2})+\frac{2}{10}\|\nabla_{H}^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Collecting the estimates above, together with
\alpha^{2}\leq(\alpha^{6}+2)/3
and
\alpha^{2}\leq(\alpha^{4}+1)/2
, leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{5}{10}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+C\big\||\tilde{v}||\nabla_{H}\tilde{v}|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\|v_{z}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{6}+\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}+1).

This combined with the Gronwall inequality implies that, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{H}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\nabla_{H}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle C(1+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}e^{C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}})\big(\|\nabla_{H}a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+E_{1}(4,a)+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(E_{3}(a)^{3}+E_{1}(4,a)+1)\big),

which concludes the desired estimate for
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
.

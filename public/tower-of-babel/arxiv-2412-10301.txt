Complex quaternionic manifolds and c-projective structures
Aleksandra Borówka

Abstract
. We discuss complex quaternionic manifolds, i.e., those that have holonomy U ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) U^{*}(2n) , which naturally arise via quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction. We show that for a fixed c-projective class, any real analytic connection with type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) curvature induces, via quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction, an S 1 S^{1} -invariant connection with holonomy contained in U ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) U^{*}(2n) . As an application, we characterize in this setting the distinguished U ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) U^{*}(2n) connection studied in Battaglia [ 3 ] and Hitchin [ 9 ] .

1.
Introduction

A quaternionic manifold is a smooth manifold of dimension
4n
,
n>1
such that it is equipped with a rank
3

subbbundle
Q
of the endomorphisms of
TM
generated locally by three almost complex structures satisfying quaternionic relations and such that there exists a torsion-free connection
D
with
DQ\subset Q
. Such connections, called quaternionic connections, form an affine space modeled on
1
-forms.

A natural question that one could ask is whether there always exists an integrable complex structure that is a section of
Q
. Although globally this may not be the case (e.g., on
\mathbb{HP}^{n}
for topological reasons there is no global almost complex structure), as shown by Alekseevsky et al.
[
2
]
, locally such complex structures do exist and we call a quaternionic manifold equipped with a distinguished complex structure a quaternionic complex manifold (see Joyce,
[
10
]
).

On the other hand, for a fixed almost complex structure
I
, one could ask when there exists a quaternionic connection with
DI=0
. As was shown by Joyce
[
10
]
the holonomy group of such a connection is contained in
U^{*}(2n)
, which is one of the possible Riemannian holonomy groups on the Berger list
[
4
]
that was later excluded. In Alekseevsky et al.
[
2
]
it is shown that such a connection, necessarily unique, exists exactly when the almost complex structure
I
is integrable. However, it seems that this result has been somewhat overlooked (for example, Thurman
[
12
]
remarks that we do not know whether the condition that the complex structure
I
is integrable is a sufficient condition). On the other hand, the paper by Hitchin
[
9
]
focuses more on a complex than a quaternionic aspect of the geometry and therefore fixes a complex structure first. In particular, it studies a distinguished
S^{1}
-invariant
U^{*}(2n)
connection on quaternion-Kähler manifolds showing that after fixing
J
it is unique. However, it does not study the problem of the existence of different
S^{1}
-invariant complex structures, and therefore different
S^{1}
-invariant
U^{*}(2n)
connections in the class. The main aim of this note is to describe an interesting class of examples of such structures that naturally arise from quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction
[
6
]
. More precisely, we show

Theorem
.

Let
(S,J,[D])
be a real analytic c-projective manifold with c-projective curvature of type
(1,1)
. Let
(\mathfrak{L},\nabla)
be a holomorphic line bundle with a holomorphic connection with real analytic type
(1,1)
curvature. Then locally near
S
, the quaternionic manifold
M
obtained by quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction from
(S,J,[D],\mathfrak{L},\nabla)
admits a family
J_{D}
of compatible
S^{1}
-invariant complex structures parametrized by real analytic connections
D
in the c-projective class with curvature of type
(1,1)
and such that
J_{D}|_{S}=J
.

As a corollary, we deduce that quaternionic manifolds near maximal fixed point sets of a rotating quaternionic
S^{1}
-action admit a distinguished family of connections with holonomy contained in
U^{*}(2n)
parametrized by the connections in c-projective class of the right type.
In particular, we characterize the distinguished complex structure considered by Battaglia
[
3
]
and Hitchin
[
9
]
in the case when the
S^{1}
-action admits a fixed point set of maximal dimension. Note that the obtained results show that this complex structure is unique only in quaternion-Kähler setting - if we forget the metric (and therefore a distinguished quaternionic connection), we may get other distinguished
S^{1}
-invariant complex structures.

Background

In this section we give necessary definitions and a very brief description of quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction
[
6
]
. For more detailed background, see
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[

]
and
[

]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
. A
4n
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
is called quaternionic if it is equipped with a subbundle
Q\subset End(TM)
with
rk(Q)=3
such that
Q
is locally generated by three anti-commuting almost complex structures
I,J,K
satisfying

I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=IJK=-Id,

and such that there exists a torsion-free connection
D
, called a quaternionic connection, with
D_{X}Q\subset Q
for all
X
.

For a fixed quaternionic manifold the class of all quaternionic connections
[D]_{q}
is given by an equivalence relation

(1)

D\sim_{q}D^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\exists_{\gamma}:\ \forall Y,Z\in TM\quad D^{\prime}_{Y}Z=D_{Y}Z+[\mkern-3.0mu[Y,\gamma]\mkern-3.0mu]_{q}Z,

where
\gamma\in T^{*}M
and

[\mkern-3.0mu[Y,\gamma]\mkern-3.0mu]_{q}(Z)=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma(Y)Z+\gamma(Z)Y-\Sigma_{i=1}^{3}(\gamma(I_{i}Y)I_{i}Z+\gamma(I_{i}Z)I_{i}Y))

where
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
is a local frame of almost complex structures (see
[
1
]
).

A quaternionic manifold admits many
Q
-hermitian metrics. However, usually the Levi-Civita connection of such metric is not quaternionic. In a rare case when it is quaternionic we call the metric
quaternion-Kähler
and in fact, it is one of the special holonomy cases from the Berger list
[
4
]
, where the group is
Sp(n)\cdot Sp(1)
.

Another special case of quaternionic manifold is when the bundle
Q
is trivial and generated by three complex structures
I,J,K
satisfying quaternionic relation. In this case the manifold is called
hypercomplex
and it admits unique quaternionic connection with
DI=DJ=DK=0
called the
Obata connection
. In the case where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection of a metric which is Kähler with respect to all
(I,J,K)
we call the manifold
hyperkähler
.

The main object of this paper is
complex quaternionic structure
, i.e., a quaternionic manifold with a fixed compatible integrable complex structure. Now, we will recall some facts about them due to D. Alekseevsky, S. Marchiafava and M. Pontecorvo.

Proposition 2.1

(Alekseevsky et al.
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold of dimension
4n>4
. Then locally on
M
there exists an integrable complex structure.

Theorem 2.1

(Alekseevsky at al.
[
2
]
, Proposition 3.6 and Theorem 4.1)
.

Let
I
be an integrable complex structure on some quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
. Then, there exists a unique quaternionic connection
D
preserving
I
, i.e., satisfying
DI=0
.

As shown by Joyce
[
10
]
, quaternionic connections preserving some integrable complex structure have holonomy inside
SL(n,\mathbb{H})U(1)
which in
[
9
]
is denoted by
U^{*}(2n)
to emphasize its analogy with
U(2n)
as a real form of
GL(2n,\mathbb{C})
.

For any quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
over each point of
M
we have a sphere (i.e.,
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
) of anti-commuting complex structures, i.e. those elements of
Q
that square to
-Id
. The
twistor space

Z
of
M
is defined to be the total space of this sphere bundle. Salamon
[
13
]
showed that
Z
admits an integrable complex structure and that the family of projective lines given by fibres of the sphere bundle over

have normal bundle isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.

Moreover, the following theorem is true:

Theorem 2.2

(Pedersen–Poon,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
Z
be a complex manifold of dimension
2n+1
such that:

(i)

There is a family of holomorphic projective lines
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
each with normal bundle isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(1)
,

(ii)

Z has a real structure which on lines from the family which are invariant under this real structure induces the antipodal map of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Then the parameter space of projective lines invariant under the real structure, called real twistor lines, is a
4n
-manifold with natural quaternionic structure for which
Z
is the twistor space.

Many additional structures and properties of quaternionic structures can be encoded in the underlying twistor spaces. In particular, the existence of quaternion-Kähler metric corresponds to the existence of a holomorphic contact distribution on
Z
and the existence of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on
M
corresponds to a local holomorphic
C^{*}
-action on
Z
. Both the distribution and the action need to be compatible with the real structure and transversal to real twistor lines.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
x\in M
is a fixed point set of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action. Consider the
C^{*}
-action on the twistor line corresponding to
x
in
Z
.

•

If the action on the twistor line is trivial then we call
x
a tri-holomorphic point,

•

If the action rotates the twistor line then we call
x
a rotating point.

Note that any rotation of
CP^{1}
fixes exactly two antipodal points.

Recall that a
projective structure
on a smooth manifold is an equivalence class of connections that have the same geodesics. It can be also considered in a holomorphic setting, where we consider only holomorphic connections (i.e. connections on complex manifolds with holomorphic Chistoffel symbols). If such a holomorphic projective structure on a complex manifold
S
is flat (which for complex dimension
n>1
means that the invariant part of the curvature - called projective Weyl curvature - vanishes) then there exists unique embedding of
S
into the model
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
such that the projective structures are compatible.

Definition 2.3
.

A
2n
-manifold
(M,J,[D]_{c})
,
n>1
, is called a
c-projective
manifold if
J
is an integrable complex structure and
[D]_{c}
is an c-projective structure, i.e., an equivalence class of complex
(DJ=0)
torsion-free connections under the equivalence

D\sim_{c}D^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\exists_{\gamma}:\ \forall Y,Z\in TM\quad D^{\prime}_{Y}Z=D_{Y}Z+[\mkern-3.0mu[Y,\gamma]\mkern-3.0mu]_{c}Z,

where
\gamma\in T^{*}M
and
[\mkern-3.0mu[Y,\gamma]\mkern-3.0mu]_{c}(Z)=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma(Y)Z+\gamma(Z)Y-\gamma(JY)JZ-\gamma(JZ)JY)
.

Note that in older literature, the c-projective structure was called h-projective structure.

Projective, c-projective and quaternionic structures are examples of projective parabolic Cartan geometries. In such geometries an important role is played by density bundles. In case of projective structures in holomorphic setting, this is the holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
over a projective manifold
S
, where

(2)

\mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes(n+1)}=\bigwedge^{n}TS

Note that in general the bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
is defined only locally on
S
, as roots of bundles may exist only locally. However, when
S
is equal to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with the standard projective structure, the bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
is defined globally and is the dual of the tautological line bundle.

Although the projective structure is a class of compatible connections on
TS
, we can encode it by a single connection on a bigger bundle called the tractor bundle. For holomorphic setting this bundle is
J^{1}\mathcal{O}(1)
the
1
-jet bundle of
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Fixing a connection
D
in the projective class induces an isomorphism of
J^{1}\mathcal{O}(1)
with
[T^{*}S\otimes\mathcal{O}(1)]\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
and then the tractor connection
\mathcal{D}
on
J^{1}\mathcal{O}(1)
is equal

(3)

\mathcal{D}^{D}_{Y}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
\alpha\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}D_{Y}l-\alpha(Y)\\
D_{Y}\alpha+(r_{p}^{D})_{Y}l\end{array}\right),

where
r_{p}^{D}
is some normalization of the Ricci tensor.
One can show that this connection when considered on
J^{1}\mathcal{O}(1)
does not depend on the choice of the connection in the class and that
\mathcal{D}
is flat iff the projective structure is flat.

2.1.
Quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction

Now we will give a very brief description of quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction
[
6
]
. As the construction is quite complicated, we limit only to the facts that are needed to construct examples in Section
3
.

The idea behind quaternionic Feix–Kaledin is to fully characterize quaternionic structures on a neighbourhood of a component
S
of the fixed point set of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action provided it is rotating and of maximal dimension (which is half of the dimension of the quaternionic manifold) in terms of a structure on
S
. It turns out that such
S
is a complex submanifold and is equipped with a c-projective structure with c-projective Weyl curvature (i.e. invariant part of the curvature) of type
(1,1)
together with a holomorphic line bundle with a holomorphic connection with curvature of type
(1,1)
. Moreover, these data fully characterize (locally near
S
) the underlying quaternionic manifold and therefore can be used to reconstruct it.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
6
]

Let
(S,J,[D]_{c})
be a real analytic c-projective manifold with c-projective curvature of type
(1,1)
. Let
(\mathfrak{L},\nabla)
be a holomorphic line bundle with a holomorphic connection with real-analytic type
(1,1)
curvature. Then the quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction from
[
6
]
gives a twistor space
Z
of a quaternionic manifold
M
equipped with a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action having
S
as a component of its fixed points. Furthermore,
S
is a totally complex submanifold of
M
, with induced c-projective structure
[D]_{c}
, and a neighbourhood of
S
in
M
is

S^{1}
-equivariantly diffeomorphic to a neighbourhood of the zero section in

TS\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{1,0}\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{0,1})^{*})|_{S}
. Moreover, any quaternionic

4n
-manifold with a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action whose fixed point set has a
connected component
S
which is a submanifold of real dimension
2n
with no
triholomorphic points arises from the induced c-projective structure on
S
via the
quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction, for some holomorphic line bundle
\mathfrak{L}
on

S
.

The bundles
\mathcal{L}_{1,0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0,1}
are some line bundles depending on
\mathfrak{L}
which will be defined in Section
2.1.2
.

In particular, the construction gives a precise description of the twistor space of quaternionic manifolds with
S^{1}
-action of the required type in a neighborhood of the twistor lines corresponding to the fixed point set
S
. To be able to construct the example, we need to understand the properties of these twistor spaces.

2.1.1.
Overview

Let
Z
be a twistor space obtained by the quaternionic Feix–Kaledin construction, i.e. it is a twistor space of a quaternionic manifold
M
with an
S^{1}
-action which preserves the quaternionic structure and it admits a fixed point set
S\subset M
of the
S^{1}
-action which is a complex manifold of complex dimension

. Then there exist two holomorphic vector bundles
V^{+}
and
V^{-}
of rank

over

and

such that

is obtained as a gluing of
U^{+}
and

. Moreover, the

-action is induced on

from the scalar multiplication in the fibres of
(V^{+})^{*}
and the inverse of scalar multiplication in the fibres of
(V^{-})^{*}
. The general idea is, that the gluing is given by two blow-down maps to

and
U^{-}
from some bundle

of projective lines over neighborhood of the diagonal of the complex manifold

. The two zero sections of

and

correspond to the submanifold

with two distinguished complex structures

and
-J
. Below we discuss necessary technical details.

2.1.2.
The bundle
\mathcal{P}

Observe that

is a model of complexification of the complex manifold

and the diagonal is the real submanifold isomorphic with

(for details concerning complexification see

]
). The projections on the first and second factor are holomorphic and define on

two transverse holomorphic foliations by manifolds isomorphic with

Definition 2.4
.

The foliation given by equation

(1,0)
-foliation and the foliation given by equation

(1,0)

(0,1)

For the holomorphic bundles

(0,1)
-foliation. Similarly, the bundles
\overline{\mathfrak{L}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
are holomorphic over
\overline{S}
(see

(1,0)

and
\mathcal{L}_{0,1}

Flat structures along the leaves

On the other hand, one can show that the complexification of c-projective structure and of the connection

gives flat holomorphic projective structures along each leaf of the two foliations defined in

.

Note that as the projective tractor bundle is defined on

Remark 3.1

The equations used to construct hyperspaces

Proposition 3.2

The

Proof.

Let

a

]

[

]
studied by Hitchin
[
9
]
(and given by the zero set of the moment map on the twistor space). In Proposition 2 of
[
5

coming from the quaternion-Kähler connection is tangent to

and

along the zero sections. Therefore along the zero sections of

and

In Proposition 2 of

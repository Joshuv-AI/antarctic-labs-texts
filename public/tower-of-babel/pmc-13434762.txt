Determinants of mobile fitness app continuance intention using PLS SEM NCA and fsQCA
Authors: Yifan Zheng, Kun Li, Qianjin Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines how users’ continuance intention toward mobile fitness apps is formed, using an analytical framework of “value creation–satisfaction evaluation–platform lock-in.” Based on 704 valid survey responses, the study applies PLS-SEM, NCA, and fsQCA. The PLS-SEM results show that entertainment attributes and service attributes significantly improve user satisfaction, which in turn increases continuance intention. Switching costs also have a significant positive effect on continuance intention, whereas the direct effect of social attributes on satisfaction is not significant. The NCA results indicate that entertainment attributes, service attributes, and satisfaction are moderate necessary conditions for high continuance intention. The fsQCA results further reveal multiple configurations leading to high continuance intention, which can be grouped into three patterns: “multidimensional value–high satisfaction,” “function–lock-in driven,” and “social–entertainment synergy.” Overall, the findings suggest that users’ continuance intention toward mobile fitness apps is not determined by a single factor in a linear way. Instead, it is jointly shaped by service experience, entertainment experience, satisfaction, accumulated platform value, and different combinations of conditions. This study extends the explanatory framework for continuance behavior in mobile fitness apps and offers practical implications for improving product design, implementing user segmentation strategies, and enhancing voluntary user retention.Keywords: PLE-SEM, NCA, fsQCA, Fitness app, Integrated approachSubject terms: Information systems and information technology, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As digital transformation accelerates, fitness apps have rapidly gained popularity worldwide and have become important tools for everyday health management1. Continuance intention refers to users’ willingness or tendency to keep using a product or service over time2. In the context of mobile fitness apps, continued use is especially important. Fitness activities are long-term and repetitive, so one-time or short-term use is unlikely to produce stable health benefits. At the same time, these apps depend on sustained user engagement, accumulated data, and habit formation to provide personalized feedback, improve training plans, and support health promotion. Prior research also shows that acquiring a new user can cost five times more than retaining an existing one3. However, many users stop using fitness apps soon after their first experience, making it difficult for firms to achieve long-term profitability and user retention4. Expectation confirmation theory suggests that users’ intentions and behaviors change dynamically as their experience deepens5. Initial adoption is only the starting point of an app’s success, whereas continued use is crucial to its long-term viability6. Therefore, identifying the factors that shape users’ continuance intention is important for strengthening user engagement and improving product design.

An increasing body of research has begun to emphasize the critical role of continued usage intention, especially in the analysis of user behavior within the context of mobile applications7,8. However, the continued use of mobile fitness apps faces specific challenges. Compared with general mobile applications, fitness apps must not only provide reliable functions but also sustain users’ motivation, maintain a sense of novelty, and help them develop stable exercise habits over time. Keep, a representative app in China’s mobile fitness market, illustrates this shift. By offering professional fitness content, AI-assisted personalized courses, live and recorded classes, smart fitness devices, sports equipment, and health foods, Keep seeks to cover the full fitness journey, from goal setting and training to data tracking and fitness-related consumption. Mobile fitness apps, therefore, are no longer simple tools for recording exercise. Rather, they have developed into integrated fitness ecosystems that combine online courses, personalized training plans, activity tracking, smart devices, and related consumer products. This suggests that user retention is shaped not by a single function, but by service quality, entertainment experience, social interaction, and platform investment. In practice, however, fitness apps often face problems such as similar features, high user churn, and low engagement with social functions9. Accordingly, research on continuance intention in mobile fitness apps should consider users’ functional needs, emotional experiences, social interactions, and platform-related investments, rather than focusing only on isolated variables or linear relationships.

Building on this, this study views continuance intention toward mobile fitness apps as a behavioral mechanism shaped by “value creation–satisfaction evaluation–platform lock-in,” incorporating social attributes, entertainment attributes, service attributes, satisfaction, and switching costs. The theoretical contributions are threefold. First, the study develops a “value creation–satisfaction evaluation–platform lock-in” framework for explaining continuance intention in mobile fitness apps. Second, by combining PLS-SEM, NCA, and fsQCA, it shows that different methods reveal different layers of causal logic. PLS-SEM identifies which variables have significant average effects; NCA identifies which conditions must not be absent for high continuance intention to occur; and fsQCA identifies which combinations of conditions can jointly lead to high continuance intention. Together, these methods provide a more comprehensive understanding of user retention mechanisms. Third, this study clarifies the boundary conditions of social attributes. Although social attributes have no significant average net effect on satisfaction, the fsQCA results show that they can still contribute to high continuance intention when combined with entertainment attributes. This suggests that social functions are more likely to work through mechanisms such as socialized entertainment, group challenges, and leaderboard feedback within specific configurations.

Literature review and research hypothesis

Physical experience and satisfaction level

Social attributes refer to the interactive, sharing, comparison, and support functions provided by mobile fitness apps, reflecting the platform’s social value. Through features such as workout sharing, friend activity feeds, community discussions, and challenge activities, users can obtain emotional support, engage in social comparison, and develop a sense of group belonging10. For example, fitness app users can share their experiences and monitor progress within the app11. Furthermore, numerous studies have supported the positive relationship between social characteristics and user satisfaction. These characteristics can strengthen user loyalty and satisfaction by fostering emotional connections12. Specifically, in the context of fitness apps, research suggests that a higher level of social interaction significantly enhances user satisfaction13. Based on the above analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed. Its boundary conditions will be further explained in the later discussion in light of the empirical results.

H1

Social characteristics have a positive effect on user satisfaction.

Entertainment attributes reflect the hedonic value created by mobile fitness apps through engaging content, gamified design, challenge tasks, reward mechanisms, and immersive experiences. Exercise behavior typically requires long-term commitment and is often affected by fatigue, repetition, and the lack of immediate rewards. Prior research has shown that entertainment attributes are a major driver of user satisfaction14. In the realm of fitness apps, Choe and Noh15 found that the enjoyment offered by these apps boosts user satisfaction and enhances the overall user experience. Additionally, studies suggest that immersive designs, particularly those involving game-based elements, tend to have a greater appeal to users16. Based on these findings, we propose the following hypothesis:

H2

Entertainment attributes have a positive impact on user satisfaction.

Service attributes refer to the utilitarian service experience provided by mobile fitness apps in areas such as functional support, personalized recommendations, data feedback, privacy protection, customer responsiveness, and system maintenance17. Research suggests that real-time, efficient services play a critical role in enhancing user retention and satisfaction18. Moreover, the convenience and reliability of services are also found to significantly influence user satisfaction19. Additionally, personalized experiences, a key component of service attributes, are essential for boosting user satisfaction20. For instance, users often expect fitness apps to provide tailored, personalized experiences21,22. Based on this analysis, we propose the following hypothesis:

H3

Service attributes positively affect user satisfaction.

Switching costs and willingness to continue to use

Switching costs refer to the economic, time, learning, emotional, and relational investments that users perceive when moving from their current platform to similar products or services23. In mobile fitness apps, switching costs arise not only from membership benefits or paid content, but also from accumulated exercise data, personalized training profiles, platform familiarity, habit formation, and connections with other users. Higher switching costs increase the psychological and behavioral burden of leaving the current platform, thereby strengthening users’ continuance intention24. Based on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H4

Switching costs positively influence users’ willingness to continue using the service.

The mediating effect of satisfaction

Satisfaction is widely acknowledged as a crucial determinant of consumer behavior outcomes in fitness centers25. Previous research has shown that when users perceive mobile fitness apps as capable of meeting their needs for exercise management, health improvement, and enjoyable experiences, their satisfaction increases, which in turn strengthens continuance intention26. In the framework of this study, social attributes, entertainment attributes, and service attributes represent different forms of value creation, while satisfaction reflects users’ overall evaluation of these value experiences. Therefore, satisfaction may not only directly influence continuance intention, but also mediate the relationship between platform attributes and continuance intention. Based on this analysis, we propose the following hypothesis:

H5

Satisfaction positively influences users’ willingness to continue using the service.

H5a

Social characteristics positively affect users’ willingness to continue using the service through the mediation of satisfaction.

H5b

Entertainment attributes positively influence users’ willingness to continue using the service through the mediation of satisfaction.

H5c

Service attributes positively influence users’ willingness to continue using the service through the mediation of satisfaction.

Methodology

Measures

The survey was designed to include questions pertaining to demographics, social characteristics, entertainment attributes, service attributes, switching costs, satisfaction level with fitness apps, and willingness to continue using these apps. Respondents were instructed to answer the questions sequentially, beginning with demographic questions, followed by factors influencing their willingness to continue using fitness apps. To ensure both the reliability and validity of the questionnaire, established scales from relevant literature were utilized to develop the survey items. Specifically, the measurement of social characteristics was based on the work of H.-M. Kim (2022)27; entertainment attributes were adapted from28; service attributes were derived from Lee et al., (2021)29; switching costs followed the framework of K. Kim & Lee, (2024)30; satisfaction level was referenced from Min & Noh, (2015)31; and willingness to continue using the app was based on Lee et al., (2013). All survey items were assessed using a 5-point Likert scale (Table 2).

Data collection

As the world’s largest mobile internet market, China had 1.125 billion internet users by December 2025. In light of this, Chinese users were selected as the research sample. Following the creation of the initial questionnaire, a pre-survey was conducted with 40 participants. Items that exhibited a CR value below 0.7 or were semantically unclear were removed, resulting in the final version of the questionnaire. The survey was administered via Questionnaire Star over the span of one month, collecting a total of 856 responses. After performing manual screening and data cleaning, 704 valid responses were retained, leading to an effective response rate of 82.24%. (Table 1).

A hybrid method combining PLS-SEM, NCA, and fsQCA

This study adopts a combined methodological approach integrating PLS-SEM, NCA, and fsQCA. First, PLS-SEM is used to examine the net effects among social attributes, entertainment attributes, service attributes, satisfaction, switching costs, and continuance intention, as well as to test the mediating role of satisfaction. This method addresses whether a particular variable has a significant average effect after controlling for other variables. Second, NCA is employed to identify the necessary conditions and minimum threshold levels required for high continuance intention. It addresses whether high continuance intention is unlikely to occur when a certain condition is absent or fails to reach a required level. Finally, fsQCA is used to analyze the sufficiency of multiple combinations of conditions, identifying which configurations jointly lead to high continuance intention. Therefore, the three methods are not used for simple cross-validation. Instead, they correspond to net-effect logic, necessity logic, and configurational sufficiency logic, respectively, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of the complex mechanisms underlying continuance intention toward mobile fitness apps32,33.

Results

PLS-SEM results

Model credibility validation

We performed reliability and validity assessments on the indicators of the model, utilizing 5,000 bootstrap samples to derive the results. Cronbach’s α (CA) and composite reliability (CR) were employed to evaluate the reliability of the questionnaire. The findings indicated that the CA values for all variables exceeded 0.8 (ranging from 0.811 to 0.952), CR values were also above 0.8 (ranging from 0.833 to 0.952), and the factor loadings were consistently above 0.8 (ranging from 0.899 to 0.977), demonstrating a high level of reliability for the questionnaire (Table 2). The AVE values listed in Tables 2 and 3 all surpassed 0.5, and based on the Fornell-Larcker criterion, the square root of the average variance extracted (AVE) for each variable was greater than its correlation with other variables. This suggests that while the variables exhibit correlations, they remain distinct, indicating strong discriminant validity (Table 3). Furthermore, we employed the Gaussian Copula approach to examine potential endogeneity, and the results revealed no significant findings, with p-values ranging from 0.068 to 0.948. Consequently, the model in this study does not experience endogeneity issues.

NCA results

Before conducting the NCA analysis, it is crucial to ensure the completeness of the data. Following this, a Cartesian coordinate system is used, where the condition variables are plotted on the horizontal X-axis, and the outcome variables are plotted on the vertical Y-axis. The ceiling line is then fitted using either the CE-FDH algorithm, based on non-parametric envelope methods, or the CR-FDH algorithm, which uses regression analysis (Fig. 2). In this study, the CE-FDH algorithm, which relies on non-parametric envelope methods, was selected to fit the ceiling line. This algorithm was chosen because it more accurately reflects the strength of necessary conditions and demonstrates robustness in capturing boundary effects, thereby providing more reliable results. This approach will be particularly valuable in exploring the necessary relationships between influencing factors and willingness to continue using the service, ultimately identifying the critical factors that are indispensable for sustaining willingness to continue using.

Analysis of necessity between various factors and sustainable usage intention.

To examine the effect sizes of the variables, a bootstrapping procedure with 1,000 resamples was conducted to ensure the stability and reliability of the relationships between the predictor and outcome variables. The results show that the effect sizes of SO and SC are zero, indicating that they cannot be regarded as necessary conditions for WT. In contrast, the effect sizes of EA, SA, and SL are 0.125, 0.125, and 0.188, respectively, all of which are statistically significant. However, because these values fall within the range of medium effect sizes (0.1 ≤ d ≤ 0.3)32, this study cautiously interprets them as “moderately necessary conditions.”

To further identify the necessary condition requirements at different outcome levels, this study constructs a bottleneck table (Table 7). The bottleneck level (%) indicates the minimum level that a given condition must reach to achieve a specific level of the outcome. The results show that, to achieve 80% of WT, EA and SA must each reach at least 10%, while SL must reach at least 30%. This suggests that even when users have high switching costs or some degree of social interaction, they are unlikely to develop a high level of continuance intention without a basic level of entertainment experience, service experience, and satisfaction.

fsQCA results

Data processing

Before conducting the fsQCA analysis, it is essential to process the data by converting the values of all variables into fuzzy set membership scores. In this study, a 5-point Likert scale was utilized. Some scholars suggest using 95%, 50%, and 5% as crossover anchors33,34, while others prefer using the upper and lower quartiles for calibratio35. Additionally, certain researchers recommend that the choice of crossover anchors should be tailored to the characteristics of the data36. Adopting the approach of relevant scholars37–39, we selected the calibration method based on the mean ± standard deviation (Table 8). In this method, the full membership point corresponds to the mean plus the standard deviation, the crossover point is set at the mean value, and the non-membership point corresponds to the mean minus the standard deviation. This calibration method was chosen for two reasons. First, the study includes 704 valid responses, providing a sufficiently large sample to capture the central tendency and dispersion of the variables. Second, all variables were measured using five-point Likert scales and calculated from multiple items. As a result, the mean and standard deviation can clearly distinguish users with high, medium, and low levels of each variable, while the influence of extreme outliers on the calibration anchors is relatively limited.

Sufficiency analysis of condition configurations

Unlike the analysis of necessary conditions, configurational analysis aims to reveal the sufficiency of outcomes generated by different configurations composed of multiple conditions. Consistency is the criterion used to assess sufficiency. Schneider and Wagemann, (2013) suggest that the consistency level for determining sufficiency should not be lower than 0.75, although some scholars set a different consistency threshold, such as 0.838. The frequency threshold should be determined based on the sample size40. For small to medium-sized samples with fewer observations, the frequency threshold should be set to 1, while for larger samples, the frequency threshold should be greater than 1. Based on this, in this study, the consistency level is set to 0.8 to maintain analytical rigor while allowing some flexibility to capture more patterns and variations. The frequency threshold is set to 2. Using these steps, the configurational analysis results (Table 9) were obtained. The results show that the overall coverage rate for triggering a high willingness to continue using is 0.923, with an overall consistency of 0.854, indicating a good explanatory power of the model. Based on the core conditions, six configurations were classified into three models.

Model 1: Multidimensional Value–High Satisfaction Pattern. This model includes Configuration 1, Configuration 2, and Configuration 3. The core conditions across these configurations are high EA, SA, SL, and SC, with SL consistently appearing as a core condition in all three configurations. This pathway suggests that users are more likely to develop high continuance intention when mobile fitness apps simultaneously provide enjoyable experiences, reliable services, high satisfaction, and a certain level of platform investment. The findings are consistent with the PLS-SEM results and further highlight the critical role of satisfaction in linking value experience to continuance intention.

Model 2: Function–Lock-in Driven Pattern. This model includes Configuration 4 and Configuration 5. The core conditions are high EA, SA, and SC, while the absence of SO is also a defining feature. Among these conditions, EA consistently appears as a core condition in both configurations, indicating that strong entertainment attributes are a key driver of continuance intention. These findings suggest that, for some users, continued use does not depend on social interaction but is instead driven by enjoyable experiences, reliable services, and platform-related investments. This pathway further explains why social attributes are not significant in the PLS-SEM results. Specifically, continuance intention toward mobile fitness apps can be achieved through non-social pathways, particularly when users place greater emphasis on training efficiency, functional services, and accumulated platform data, making social interaction unnecessary.

Model 3: Social–Entertainment Synergy Pattern. This model includes Configuration 6. Its core conditions are SO and EA, while SA and SC are absent. This pathway shows that although social attributes do not significantly improve satisfaction on their own, they may still lead to high continuance intention when combined with entertainment attributes. Social interaction can strengthen user engagement through challenges, sharing, comparison, and group feedback, while entertainment attributes enhance enjoyment through gamification and immersive experiences. Together, they create a “socialized entertainment” pathway, through which users may maintain high continuance intention even when service attributes or switching costs are relatively weak.

Robustness testing and predictive analysis

In fsQCA analysis, robustness testing is essential to ensure the reliability and validity of the results. First, this study slightly adjusted the thresholds for full membership, the crossover point, and full non-membership, and then repeated the fsQCA analysis38. The results show that overall consistency changed from 0.854 to 0.892, while overall coverage changed from 0.923 to 0.871. However, the core interpretation remained substantively unchanged. Second, following prior research41, the raw consistency threshold was adjusted to 0.85. The results were similar to the initial findings, further confirming the robustness of the analysis.

First, service attributes have the strongest effect on satisfaction. User satisfaction depends mainly on whether the app can support fitness goals in a stable, accurate, and personalized way, such as by providing reliable data tracking, scientific training plans, clear feedback, and proper privacy protection. Service quality theory suggests that reliability, responsiveness, and personalization are key bases of user evaluation17. In fitness apps, service quality is fundamental. If basic services are unstable, gamification or community features alone are unlikely to sustain continued use.

Second, entertainment attributes also have a significant positive effect on satisfaction, indicating that fitness apps must be not only useful but also enjoyable. This finding is consistent with prior studies15,16. Gamified design and achievement badges can break long-term fitness goals into shorter and more visible rewards, reducing boredom and increasing immediate enjoyment. Keep’s running medal campaign is a useful example. Users select themed or IP-branded medals, register for the event, complete the required running distance through the Keep App within a set period, and then receive a physical medal. Through the process of “task completion–instant feedback–physical reward,” this mechanism turns repetitive running into an engaging challenge and encourages continued participation.

Third, social attributes do not significantly affect satisfaction in the PLS-SEM results, but they contribute to high continuance intention when combined with entertainment attributes in the fsQCA analysis. This suggests that many users primarily use fitness apps for personal training, exercise tracking, or personalized guidance rather than as social platforms. Prior studies show that social comparison in fitness contexts has dual effects: moderate comparison may motivate exercise persistence, while excessive comparison can create pressure, frustration, or privacy concerns42. Therefore, simply adding communities, rankings, or friend feeds does not necessarily improve satisfaction. However, when social functions are combined with entertainment features, such as fun check-ins, shared goals, or lightweight interaction feedback, social attributes become a form of “socialized entertainment” that can strengthen continuance intention.

Fourth, switching costs play a retention role. The results show that switching costs significantly increase continuance intention, consistent with prior studies43. When users accumulate exercise data, training habits, and membership benefits on a fitness app, leaving the platform creates time, learning, financial, and emotional costs. However, the NCA results indicate that switching costs are not a necessary condition. This means switching costs cannot replace core user value. If users report poor entertainment experience, service experience, or satisfaction, high switching costs may lead only to passive retention rather than active continued use.

Fifth, the NCA bottleneck analysis provides minimum standards for fitness app management. To achieve 80% continuance intention, entertainment and service attributes must each reach at least 10%, while satisfaction must reach at least 30%. Because this study used a 1–5 Likert scale, 80% continuance intention corresponds to approximately 4.20 points, while 30% satisfaction corresponds to about 2.20 points. Importantly, this threshold does not mean that a satisfaction score of 2.20 will necessarily produce high continuance intention. Rather, it indicates that high continuance intention is unlikely when satisfaction falls below this minimum level.

Sixth, the fsQCA results reveal multiple pathways to high continuance intention. fsQCA emphasizes that outcomes are usually produced by combinations of conditions rather than by a single factor, and different combinations may lead to the same outcome33. The three identified pathways suggest that fitness app users are not homogeneous. “Multidimensional value–high satisfaction” users rely on the combined effects of entertainment, service, satisfaction, and switching costs. “Function–lock-in driven” users depend more on service value, entertainment experience, and platform investment, without necessarily requiring strong social interaction. “Social–entertainment synergy” users develop continuance intention through enjoyable interaction and group participation. These findings indicate that fitness apps should avoid one-size-fits-all strategies and instead adopt segmented design and refined user management based on different user needs.

Practical implications

Regarding user satisfaction, the research results highlight its pivotal role as a mediator in promoting continued use of mobile fitness apps. Therefore, user satisfaction should be a central focus in the development and operation of these apps. First, developers should consistently enhance and update the app’s features, ensuring that its content is of high quality, both in terms of professionalism and practicality, to fully meet users’ fitness needs. Second, for long-term active users, offering incentives and rewards (such as point exchanges and promotional activities) can significantly boost user satisfaction while also increasing user engagement and platform loyalty. Lastly, through refined data analysis, developers can deliver personalized service experiences tailored to individual users’ fitness goals, habits, and preferences. This could include custom training plans and precise exercise data tracking and analysis. These personalized services will not only strengthen users’ trust in the app but will also enhance their overall satisfaction, encouraging continued use.

Regarding entertainment attributes and service attributes, the study reveals that both factors have a significant positive impact on user satisfaction and their willingness to continue using the app. First, enhancing user privacy protection is crucial. This can be achieved by collecting only the minimal necessary data required to enable the app’s features and minimizing the frequency of push notifications to avoid overwhelming users with excessive information. Second, increasing service transparency can help build trust and improve service quality. Using visual tools, such as progress bars and status indicators, can allow users to track the progress of services and issue resolution in real time, thereby enhancing their understanding and trust in the service process. Furthermore, integrating gamification elements into fitness courses or challenge tasks can significantly boost user engagement and interactivity. The essence of gamification lies in the satisfaction and positive feedback mechanisms it creates. As users become engaged in fitness activities and receive timely encouragement, they are likely to experience positive reinforcement, which helps them enter a “flow” state. The "game + fitness" model not only enhances the entertainment value of the fitness experience but also indirectly increases user satisfaction with the app, further promoting their willingness to continue using it. Additionally, NCA research highlights the minimum thresholds for entertainment and service attributes. Consequently, developers should ensure that these elements consistently meet or surpass the minimum required standards to improve overall user retention.

Switching costs can be divided into positive and negative forms. Positive switching costs arise from the long-term value users accumulate on a platform, whereas negative switching costs stem from complicated cancellation procedures, non-transferable user data, opaque pricing rules, or deliberately increased migration barriers. Although the latter may reduce user churn in the short term, it can damage user trust and platform reputation, ultimately undermining long-term continuance. Therefore, mobile fitness apps should avoid coercive lock-in strategies and instead strengthen voluntary retention through value accumulation. For example, platforms can introduce in-app reward systems, such as point redemption and achievement badges, to encourage long-term engagement and increase users’ sense of participation and belonging.

Finally, social functions should not be designed as mandatory core features for all users. Instead, fitness apps should integrate social interaction with entertainment mechanisms, such as group challenges, friend check-ins, periodic leaderboards, and shared goal tasks, allowing social attributes to function effectively for specific user groups and usage contexts.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. First, the sample is limited, as it focuses mainly on Chinese users and thus reflects a relatively narrow geographic and cultural context. Future studies could include users from different countries and regions to examine how cultural differences shape continuance intention. Second, this study uses cross-sectional self-reported survey data. Although PLS-SEM, NCA, and fsQCA provide complementary explanations from different causal perspectives, they cannot strictly identify temporal order or causality. Future research could use longitudinal or experimental designs to examine the dynamic changes in satisfaction, habit formation, and continued use. Finally, this study measures continuance intention rather than actual long-term behavior. Although continuance intention can predict future behavior, it is not the same as real usage frequency, active days, course completion, or subscription renewal. Future research could combine survey data with objective behavioral data to improve external validity.

Rate independent capillary motion on a narrow Wilhelmy plate
Carson Collins, William M Feldman

Abstract
. We study a rate independent energetic model of the Wilhelmy plate experiment in capillarity. The evolution is driven by vertical motions of the plate. We show stability of energy solutions to the evolution, in the sense used in the rate-independent systems literature, as the ratio between container width and plate width goes to infinity. In particular, we show that the volume-constraint for the finite-ratio problem disappears in the limit. This leads to a volume-unconstrained Dirichlet-forced evolution, a setting where monotonicity, uniqueness, and contact line regularity properties have been established in previous literature. Our result is based on using comparison principle techniques for the prescribed mean curvature equation with capillary contact angle condition that characterizes the liquid surface at equilibrium. Through barrier arguments, we are able to develop asymptotics for the energy which give us control independent of the container-to-plate ratio.

Introduction

In this paper we study a model for the evolution of a capillary surface in the scenario of the Wilhelmy plate experiment. In this experiment a solid plate is dipped into a liquid bath and then slowly lifted in order to measure dynamic contact angles. See Figure
1
. We will work with a rate-independent energetic evolution incorporating the effects of contact angle hysteresis on the plate. Our main result is a limit theorem in the case where the ratio
R>1
of the container radius to the plate radius becomes very large. We focus on the evolution of the contact line on the plate, and show how a monotone evolution arises in the limit from volume constrained evolutions as
R\to\infty
.

From a mathematical perspective one of the issues that we want to explore in this paper is the connection between volume-forced evolutions, as studied in
[
1
]
, and Dirichlet-forced evolutions as studied in
[
13
,
12
,
6
]
. The volume constrained case is more relevant to typical experimental setups; however, its spatial regularity of solutions and uniqueness properties are very challenging to address. On the other hand, with monotonically varying Dirichlet data, there has been progress toward describing the motion law of arbitrary energy solutions
[
13
]
, and showing regularity and uniqueness of solutions constructed by discrete-time schemes
[
6
]
. Intuitively speaking one may expect Dirichlet driven evolutions to arise in a setting where the length scale defined by the volume constraint is quite large relative to the length scale of displacements of the contact line. Our main result will make this idea rigorous in a model of the Wilhelmy plate experiment. We show that the Dirichlet-forced evolution can be derived as a suitable scaling limit of the volume-forced evolution.

1.2.
Energy formulation

Our energy is composed of contributions from each liquid-vapor and liquid-solid interface, as well as gravity:

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]=|\Sigma^{f}_{L}|-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\lvert{\Sigma^{p}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},p}-\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\lvert{\Sigma^{o}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},o}+\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|.

Here
\theta^{p}_{Y}
and
\theta^{o}_{Y}
are the Young contact angles for the plate and outer container respectively. The quantity

g=\frac{\rho\mathfrak{g}r^{2}}{\sigma_{\textup{LV}}}

is the non-dimensional Bond number, where
r
is the characteristic length scale,
\mathfrak{g}
is the gravitational constant,
\rho
is the density different between the liquid and vapor phases, and
\sigma_{\textup{LV}}
is the surface tension between liquid and vapor phases. Since we take
g
as an
O(1)
constant and the width of the plate is normalized to

, we are essentially assuming that the Bond number for plate radius
r
is of unit order. This can be checked to be physically reasonable for, for example, water and air where the Bond number will be of unit size around
2.7
millimeters, for example see
[
19
]
*Section 21.2.

For minimization purposes, we impose the volume constraint inside
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}
:

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}_{R,F}(L):=\int_{L\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}1-\int_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus L}1=0

We remark that as part of this constraint, we require both integrals to be finite.

To consider the container limit as

, we also define

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L]=\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}(1-e_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}_{L})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\lvert{\Sigma^{p}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},p}+\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{\infty,F}}g|z-F|

2.
Basic properties of globally stable profiles

In this section we establish several basic regularity properties of globally stable states, along with some important calculus formulas which are useful in computing the time derivative of the energy along the evolution. We accomplish:

(1)

A local coercivity property controlling the contact surface area by the free surface area up to a lower order term. As corollaries, we obtain a lower bound on the energy, lower semicontinuity of the energy, and existence of globally stable profiles.

(2)

A derivation of the Euler-Lagrange equation for globally stable profiles.

(3)

Taylor expansion of the energy of a profile under perturbations which modify the forcing
F
, or the volume. The introduction of the pressure.

2.1.
Energy coercivity and existence of globally stable states

We first recall the following estimate, which can be viewed as a trace inequality for functions of bounded variation. Our proof is similar in spirit to standard averaging techniques as in
[

]

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma

surface with unit normal

. Write

for the one-sided neighborhood

Let

(2.1)

|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L)|\leq|\partial^{*}L\cap\Gamma_{\beta}|+\frac{C}{\beta}\operatorname{vol}(L\cap\Gamma_{\beta})

In particular, both
C,r
depend only on the

\Gamma

Proof.

We prove the corresponding estimate for smooth

. Write

Using divergence theorem on

\int_{\partial\Gamma_{t}}f(\nabla d\cdot\nu_{\Gamma_{t}})=-\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla\cdot(f\nabla d)

with

the inward unit normal, coinciding with

on

. Now, observe that
\partial\Gamma_{t}=\{y+s\nu_{\Gamma}(y):y\in\partial\Gamma\hbox{ or }s\in\{0,t\}\}

Then, we average in
t
to get

\int_{\Gamma}fd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\{y+t\nu_{\Gamma}(y):y\in\Gamma\}}fd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\,dt-\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla\cdot(f\nabla d)\,dxdt

To bound the right-hand-side, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\{y+t\nu_{\Gamma}(y):y\in\Gamma\}}fd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J}{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{\beta}}|f|dx

where
J
bounds the weight for the change of measure. For the next term, we have

\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla d\cdot\nabla f\leq\int_{\Gamma_{\beta}}|\nabla f|

since
|\nabla d|=1
and
\Gamma_{t}\subset\Gamma_{\beta}
for each
t<\beta
. For the last term, we use

\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}f\Delta d\leq\frac{C}{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{\beta}}|f|

where
C
is a bound for
|\Delta d|
. Altogether,

\int_{\Gamma}f\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq\int_{\Gamma_{\beta}}|\nabla f|\,dx+\frac{C+J}{\beta}\int_{\Gamma_{\beta}}|f|\,dx

Then, it is straightforward to replace
f
by
|f|
on the left hand side and extend to general
BV
functions by regularization.
∎

A variant of Lemma
2.1
which we will need concludes that given
L^{1}
-convergence of profiles, we have
L^{1}
-convergence of contact sets, provided that the perimeter of the free surface does not accumulate near the fixed boundary. We state this below:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
L_{n},L_{\infty}\in\mathrm{Cacc}(\Gamma_{r})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{vol}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})=0

and

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Per}(L_{n};\Gamma_{t})=0

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{n})\Delta\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{\infty})|=0

Proof.

It is straightforward that

|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{n})\Delta\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{\infty})|=|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})|

By Lemma
2.1
, we have for all
t\in(0,r)
that:

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Per}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty};\Gamma_{t})+\frac{C}{t}\operatorname{vol}((L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})\cap\Gamma_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Per}(L_{n};\Gamma_{t})+\mathrm{Per}(L_{\infty};\Gamma_{t})+\frac{C}{t}\operatorname{vol}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})

Sending
n\to\infty
, the volume term disappears and we are left with

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty})|\leq\mathrm{Per}(L_{\infty};\Gamma_{t})+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Per}(L_{n};\Gamma_{t})

Sending
t\to 0^{+}
, we have that
\Gamma_{t}
shrinks to the empty set, so the first term on the right side goes to
0
. The second term on the right goes to
0
by the hypothesis, and we conclude.
∎

Now we return to the Wilhelmy plate setting. We will apply Lemma
2.1
to conclude that the global energy
\mathcal{E}_{R}
is bounded below.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
L\in X^{R}_{F}
such that
|\Sigma^{p}_{L}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0}|,|\Sigma^{o}_{L}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}|<\infty
, the contact surface terms in the energy are controlled by the free surface and gravity terms, up to an additive constant:

\displaystyle|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}||\lvert{\Sigma^{p}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},p}|+|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}||\lvert{\Sigma^{o}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},o}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\vee|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\right)\left(|\Sigma^{f}_{L}|+\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|\right)

\displaystyle\quad\dots+C|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|(1+|F|)+C|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|R^{d-2}.

It follows that the energy is bounded from below

\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]\geq-C|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|(1+|F|)-C|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|R^{d-2}.

Moreover, since
|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|,|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|<1
with strict inequality, the energy controls the area of the free surface and the gravitational energy up to additive and multiplicative constants:

\displaystyle|\Sigma^{f}_{L}|+\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-(|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\vee|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|)}\left(\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]+C|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|(1+|F|)+C|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|R^{d-2}\right).

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we can locally estimate the area of the surfaces
\Sigma^{p}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0},\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L},\Sigma^{o}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}
and
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus\Sigma^{o}_{L}
in the same way. We cover these surfaces by nonoverlapping open sets
B_{i}
, chosen as uniform open cubes in tubular coordinates with the property that
B_{i}\cap(\partial B_{1}\times\{z=0\})=\emptyset
and
B_{i}\cap\partial B_{R}\times\{z=F\})=\emptyset
for each
i
. For future use, we write
r
for the coordinate side length of the
B_{i}
in the normal direction.

Now, each
B_{i}
only hits one of the four surfaces we need to estimate, and we can apply the lemma for each
B_{i}
with a fixed constant
C
. The resulting inequalities we obtain are:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}|(\Sigma^{p}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0})\cap B_{i}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap B_{i}|+C\operatorname{vol}(L\cap B_{i})\\
|(\Sigma^{o}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R,F})\cap B_{i}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap B_{i}|+C\operatorname{vol}(L\cap B_{i})\\
|(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L})\cap B_{i}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap B_{i}|+C\operatorname{vol}(L^{c}\cap B_{i})\\
|(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R,F}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L})\cap B_{i}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap B_{i}|+C\operatorname{vol}(L^{c}\cap B_{i})\end{cases}

Now, we can sum these over the covering. For the free surface terms, we have

\sum|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap B_{i}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L}|

since the covering is nonoverlapping. For the volume terms, let us consider for a moment just the
B_{i}
which cover
\Sigma^{p}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0}
. For any
h_{0}>0
, we can choose the covering so that it splits exactly into cubes contained in
\{z-F\geq h_{0}\}
and cubes contained in
\{z-F<h_{0}\}
. In the former region, we have

\int_{(L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0})\cap\{|z-F|\geq h_{0}\}}g|z-F|\geq gh_{0}\operatorname{vol}((L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0})\cap\{|z-F|\geq h_{0}\})

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{B_{i}\cap(\Sigma^{p}_{L}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0})\cap\{|z-F|\geq h_{0}\}\neq\emptyset}C\operatorname{vol}(L\cap B_{i})

\displaystyle\leq C\operatorname{vol}((L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0})\cap\{|z-F|\geq h_{0}\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{gh_{0}}\int_{(L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F})\cap\{z-F\geq h_{0}\}}g|z-F|

If we choose
h_{0}>\frac{C}{g}
, then we obtain that the left hand side is controlled by the gravitational energy. The remaining region is
\{0<z<F+h_{0}\}
. Assuming we have chosen uniform cubes of length
r
in the normal direction, the volume of this region is bounded by
Cr\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})(F+h_{0})_{+}
, for a constant
C
that can be estimated in terms of the tubular parametrization.

The volume terms for the other three contact surfaces are estimated similarly, using gravity for balls far from
z=F
and using the naïve estimate for the volume near
z=F
. The latter scales like
Cr\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R})h_{0}
for the two outer contact surfaces with the same justification as before (the
F
disappears since we work with respect to
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R,F}
rather than
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0}
). We note for emphasis that

are uniformly bounded with respect to
R
.

Finally, we sum (
2.2
) with weights
|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|
and
|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|
for the inner and outer contact terms, to obtain:

\displaystyle|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}||\lvert{\Sigma^{p}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},p}|+|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|||\lvert{\Sigma^{o}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},o}|

\displaystyle\leq|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}||\Sigma^{p}_{L}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1,0}|+|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}||\Sigma^{o}_{L}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R,F}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\vee|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\right)\left(|\Sigma^{f}_{L}|+\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|\right)

\displaystyle\quad\dots+C|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|(|F|+1)+C|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|R^{d-2}

With this, the other conclusions in the statement follow directly.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Local lower semicontinuity of energy)
.

Fix
R\in(1,\infty]
. If
L_{n}\to L_{\infty}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
with
L_{n},L_{\infty}\in X^{R}_{F}
, then in any bounded set
U
we have

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};U]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{n};U]

and for any competitor
L_{0}\in X^{R}_{F}
, it holds that

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};U]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L_{\infty};U]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{n};U]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L_{n};U]

Proof.

We split into cases. Away from the container and plate, the convergence of the
L_{n}
implies that the integrand
|z-F|\mathbf{1}_{L_{n}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}
converges locally in
L^{1}
to
|z-F|\mathbf{1}_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}
. Thus, we get lower semicontinuity of gravity from Fatou (which only requires local convergence in measure). The lower semicontinuity of the free surface energy follows from lower semicontinuity of relative perimeter.

The situations near the container and near the plate are similar, so we focus on the latter (in the case with dissipation), following the approach in
[
4
]
*Lemma 2. We will apply Lemma
2.1
with
\Gamma=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1}}(U)
. Up to subdivision and covering, it then suffices to prove the lower semicontinuity with
U
replaced by the one-sided tubular neighborhood
\Gamma_{r}
of
\Gamma
.

Suppose that lower semicontinuity fails in
\Gamma_{r}
. Then we may pass to a subsequence along which

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};\Gamma_{r}]>\mathcal{E}[L_{n};\Gamma_{r}]+\delta

for all
n
for some
\delta>0
. Ignoring gravity, which converges just by
|L_{n}\Delta L_{\infty}|\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|+\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|

\displaystyle\dots+\operatorname{Diss}(L_{0},L_{\infty};\Gamma_{r})-\operatorname{Diss}(L_{0},L_{n};\Gamma_{r})

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|+\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|

\displaystyle\dots+\operatorname{Diss}(L_{\infty},L_{n};\Gamma_{r})

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|+|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}|

Here, in the second line we use the dissipation distance triangle inequality (
1.4
), and in the third line we use the ellipticity hypotheses in Section
1.4
to bound all contact surface terms. Now, from (
2.1
), we have for all sufficiently small
t
that

|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}|\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{t}|+|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}\cap\Gamma_{t}|+C(t)\operatorname{vol}((L_{\infty}\Delta L_{n})\cap\Gamma_{t})

Putting this with
t<r
into the previous inequality, we use lower semicontinuity of perimeter to obtain:

\displaystyle|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap(\Gamma_{r}\setminus\Gamma_{t})|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}\cap(\Gamma_{r}\setminus\Gamma_{t})|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{r}|+|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{t}|

\displaystyle\dots+\liminf_{n\to\infty}C(t)\operatorname{vol}((L_{\infty}\Delta L_{n})\cap\Gamma_{t})

Since
L_{n}\to L_{\infty}
, we may drop the volume term. Thus, we reduce to

\delta\leq 2|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}\cap\Gamma_{t}|

As
t\to 0
, the right side decreases to 0, being a finite measure evaluated on a family of sets shrinking to empty. Then
\delta\leq 0
is a contradiction, so we conclude.

∎

Lemma 2.5

(Lower semicontinuity of energy)
.

If
L_{n}\to L_{\infty}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
with
L_{n},L_{\infty}\in X^{R}_{F}
, then we have

-\infty<\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\infty};F]\leq\liminf_{n}\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{n};F]\leq+\infty

For any
L_{0}\in\mathrm{Cacc}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, we have

-\infty<\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\infty};F]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L_{\infty}]\leq\liminf_{n}\left(\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{n};F]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L_{n}]\right)\leq+\infty

Proof.

We cover
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}
by countably many disjoint bounded sets
U_{n}
. Near the plate and (if
R<\infty
) the container, we choose these as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
and write
C(U_{n})
for the additive constant such that
\mathcal{E}[L;U_{n}]+C(U_{n})
is the contribution of
U_{n}
to the relativized energy
\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]
. From the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have:

•

For all but finitely many
n
, for all
L\in X^{R}_{F}
,
\mathcal{E}[L;U_{n}]+C(U_{n})\geq 0
.

•

On the finitely many remaining
n
,
\mathcal{E}[L;U_{n}]+C(U_{n})
is bounded below by a negative constant over all
L\in X^{R}_{F}
.

Thus, we apply the local lower semicontinuity of the energy from Lemma
2.4
in each set. We have

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};U_{k}]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{n};U_{k}]

and

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};U_{k}]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L_{\infty};U_{k}]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{n};U_{k}]+\operatorname{Diss}(L_{0},L_{n};U_{k})

In both cases, we relativize the energies and sum in
k
, using the aforementioned bounds from Lemma
2.3
to ensure that this sum is well-defined in
(-\infty,\infty]
. Subsequently, we bring the summation inside the liminf to conclude.
∎

With the lower semicontinuity of the energy augmented by dissipation, it is straightforward to show existence of globally stable profiles by the direct method:

Lemma 2.6

(Existence of globally stable profiles)
.

Let
R\in(1,\infty]
. For any
L_{0}\in\mathrm{Cacc}_{\mathrm{loc}}(C_{R})
, there exists a minimizer of

(2.3)

X^{R}_{F}\ni L\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{0},L]

Any minimizing
L
is a globally stable profile.

Proof.

We restrict to the case
R<\infty
. The case
R=\infty
is similar, omitting the subproof to verify the volume constraint.

Let
(L_{n})
be a minimizing sequence for (
2.3
). Then
\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{n};F]
is uniformly bounded, so by Lemma
2.3
, we have
|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}|=\mathrm{Per}(L_{n};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R})
is uniformly bounded, and
\int_{L_{n}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|
is uniformly bounded. From compactness of sets of finite perimeter, we can restrict to a subsequence (without relabeling) which converges in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R})
and pointwise a.e. to some
L\in\mathrm{Cacc}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R})
. Using the pointwise convergence with Fatou, we have

\int_{L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{L_{n}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}g|z-F|

As a corollary,
|L\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}|
and
|\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus L^{c}|
are both finite. Furthermore, we observe that for any
h>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}|L_{n}\Delta L|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n}\bigg[|(L_{n}\Delta L)\cap\{|z-F|\leq h\}|+|(L_{n}\Delta L)\cap\{|z-F|>h\}|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{h}

where we use the
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence on the portion in
|z-F|\leq h
, and the uniform bound on gravitational energy of
L_{n}
and
L
to control the portion in
|z-F|\geq h
. Sending
h\to\infty
, we get that
|L_{n}\Delta L|\to 0
, and thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{F}[L]|

\displaystyle=||L\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}|-|\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus L||

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}||L_{n}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}|-|\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus L_{n}||+|L\Delta L_{n}|

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n}|\mathcal{C}_{F}[L_{n}]|+|L\Delta L_{n}|

\displaystyle=0

so
L
satisfies the volume constraint, and
L\in X^{R}_{F}
. Using Lemma
2.5
, we conclude that
L
minimizes (
2.3
). Global stability of
L
follows directly from the dissipation triangle inequality (
1.4
).
∎

2.3.
Perturbations of globally stable states

In this section, we estimate the energy cost of perturbing a state to satisfy a perturbed constraint. This estimate will be key in obtaining the energy-dissipation balance for limits of discrete-time schemes. The ideas in this section are more or less following
[
1
]
*Proposition 5.3, though due to our different setting and constraint we must use a different family of perturbations.

Fix
L_{0},F_{0}
such that
L_{0}
is an admissible volume-constrained state for forcing
F_{0}
. Fix
R_{0}
such that
1<R_{0}<R
. Let
V\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{1}})
, such that

(2.5)

\begin{cases}V\equiv 1&\hbox{ on }B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}\\
V\equiv 0&\hbox{ near }\partial B_{1}\\
\int_{B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}}V=|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|\end{cases}

We form the vector field
\mathcal{V}(x,z)=V(x)e_{z}
in the vertical direction, and introduce the flow
\Phi_{s}=I+s\mathcal{V}

L_{s}=\Phi_{s}(L_{0}),\;F_{s}=F_{0}+s

Note that if we take
V\equiv 1
on

V
is admissible for every

The main result of this section is a Taylor expansion at

with second order remainder for the energy along the flow
\Phi_{s}
. The pressure appears as the linear term.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
L_{0}
be admissible for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F_{0}]
with
R\in(R_{0},\infty]
with finite energy, and satisfy that
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}
is bounded above and below in the vertical direction: there exists

such that
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}\subset B_{R}\times[F_{0}-h_{0},F_{0}+h_{0}]
. We have the pressure formula

(2.6)

P^{*}_{R}[L_{0},F_{0}]:=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{s},F_{s}]=\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}\cap\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}}\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}\cdot D\mathcal{V}\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}+g(z-F_{0})(\mathcal{V}-e_{z})\cdot\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}

Moreover, we have the Taylor estimate

\left|\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{s};F_{s}]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{0};F_{0}]-P^{*}[L_{0},F_{0}]s\right|\leq C|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}\cap\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}|s^{2}

which holds for all
|s|\leq\frac{1}{\|\nabla_{x}V\|_{C_{0}}}
and for a
C=C(\|V\|_{C^{1}})(1+h_{0})
.

Remark 2.9
.

We will later show in Lemma
2.13
that globally stable profiles satisfy the assumption that
\Sigma^{f}
is bounded in the vertical direction, and in Lemma

that

can be controlled uniformly for globally stable profiles.

We will also later show the local perimeter bound Lemma

for globally stable profiles. With that, both the pressure and second derivative bound are uniformly bounded for fixed
R_{0}
, over all
R\in(R_{0},\infty]
, all

, and all
L_{0}
globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F_{0}]
.

We will establish this Taylor expansion by explicit computations involving the constraint function and the energy. First we show that the constraint is preserved under the flow.

Lemma 2.10
.

We have
\mathcal{C}_{F_{s}}[L_{s}]=\mathcal{C}_{F_{0}}[L_{0}]=0
.

Proof.

First, with regards to the constraint, we have

\frac{d}{dF}\mathcal{C}_{F}(L)=-\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}1dx

and

\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{C}_{F}(L_{s})=\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{s}}}Ve_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}_{L_{s}}dS

An integration by parts which we will use several times in the following gives that

(2.7)

\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{s}}}Ve_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}_{L_{s}}dS=\int_{L_{s}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,0}}\nabla\cdot(V(x)|z|e_{z})+\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}V(x)\ dx=\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}V(x)\,dx

Note that this does not use any property of

or

only depends on the horizontal variable. Combining the above computations,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{C}_{F_{0}+s}[L_{s}]=\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}V(x)-1\,dx=0.

∎

Next we write the energy, which involves surface integrals on
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{s}}
, using the change of variables theorem as surface integrals over
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

For all

\mathcal{E}[L_{s};F_{s};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]=\mathcal{E}[L_{0};F_{0};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[L_{s};F_{s};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{L_{0}}\cap\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}}|\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}-s(\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}\cdot e_{z})(\nabla_{x}V,0)|

3.
Viscosity solution properties and height bound

In this section we introduce a notion of viscosity solution for the Euler-Lagrange equation satisfied by globally stable states

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-H_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}(x,z)+g(z-F)=\lambda_{L}&x\in\Sigma^{f}_{L}\\
\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}-\mu_{+}\leq\cos\theta_{\gamma^{p}_{L}}(x)\leq\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{-}&x\in\gamma^{p}_{L}\\
\cos\theta_{\gamma^{o}_{L}}(x)=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}&x\in\gamma^{o}_{L}.\end{cases}

We show that globally stable states indeed satisfy (
3.1
) in the viscosity sense. No higher regularity beyond the
BV
bounds and qualitative height bound is needed to justify the viscosity solution properties. In fact the pinned contact angle condition appearing in (
3.1
), by itself, likely does not admit
C^{1}
-estimates for the contact line.

With the viscosity theory, we gain access to comparison and barrier arguments to characterize globally stable profiles. As an application, we use barrier arguments to obtain a quantitative height bound for globally stable profiles. This height bound, in conjunction with the local length bound of the contact line, implies a quantitative global length bound for the contact line. The length bound will be a key estimate for our analysis of the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
in (
3.1
) in Section
7.1
.

Proof.

For a subgraph

3.4.
Lagrange multiplier and energy bound

As a corollary of the height bound Lemma
3.8
, we obtain bounds on the Lagrange multiplier and the energy of globally stable states. Note the bound on
\lambda_{L}
will be used to obtain improved regularity of
L
, and is itself subsequently improved to
O(R^{-1})
later in Lemma
7.1
.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
L
is globally stable with height
F
then

|\lambda_{L}|\leq C_{0}g\ \hbox{ and }\ \mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]\leq|B_{R}|+C_{1}(\mu_{+}\vee\mu_{-})

Moreover, for any open
U\Subset B_{R}\setminus\overline{B_{1}}
, we have the local perimeter bound

|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap(U\times\mathbb{R})|\leq C_{2}(|U|+\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial U))

Here,
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
both depend only on
d,1-|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\mp\mu_{\pm}|,1-|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|
.

Proof.

First we bound the Lagrange multiplier. The idea is that the width bound Lemma
3.8
shows that
L
stays constant order distance from height
F+\frac{\lambda}{g}
, this will contradict the volume constraint, which centers
L
around height
F
, if
\lambda
is too large. Since
L\in X^{R}_{F}
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{R,F}(L)=0
, which is to say:

|L\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}|=|\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus L|

in particular there exists
z_{+}\in\partial L
with
z_{+}\geq F
and there exists
z_{-}\in\partial L
with
z_{-}\leq F
. Then the width bound Lemma
3.8
implies that

F\geq z_{-}\geq F+\frac{\lambda}{g}-C\ \hbox{ and }\ F\leq z_{+}\leq F+\frac{\lambda}{g}+C

and rearranging these we get
-gC\leq\lambda\leq gC
. Here
C\geq 1
depends only on
d
,
1-|\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\mp\mu_{\pm}|
, and
1-|\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|
.

To prove the global energy bound, take
L^{\prime}:=\{z\leq F\}
as a competitor. Due to Lemma
3.8
and using the Lagrange multiplier bound from above

\textup{Diss}(L,L^{\prime})\leq(C+\frac{|\lambda|}{g})(\mu_{+}\vee\mu_{-})\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})\leq C(\mu_{+}\vee\mu_{-})

for some
C\geq 1
with the same dependence as before as long as
R\geq 2
. Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L^{\prime};F]+\operatorname{Diss}(L,L^{\prime})\leq|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|+C(\mu_{+}\vee\mu_{-}).

To prove the local energy bound, take

L^{\prime}:=(L\setminus(U\times\mathbb{R}))\cup(U\times(-\infty,a])

where

is chosen so that
\mathcal{C}_{F}[L^{\prime}]=0
. Due to Lemma
3.8
and the Lagrange multiplier bound, we have
|z-F|\leq C+\frac{|\lambda|}{g}\leq C
for
(x,z)\in\Sigma^{f}_{L}
, so we have
|a-F|\leq C
. Then global stability gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L^{\prime};F]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]

\displaystyle\leq|U|+|\mathrm{tr}_{\partial U\times(a,\infty)}(L)|+|\mathrm{tr}_{\partial U\times(-\infty,a]}(L^{c})|-|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap(U\times\mathbb{R})|

\displaystyle\dots+g|U|\frac{(a-F)^{2}}{2}-\int_{(L\cap(U\times\mathbb{R}))\Delta(U\times(-\infty,F])}g|z-F|

\displaystyle\leq|U|+2C\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial U)-|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap(U\times\mathbb{R})|

\displaystyle\dots+g|U|\frac{(a-F)^{2}}{2}

We remark that the second line is a strict inequality when

.

We bound the two trace terms using Lemma
3.8
and rearrange to obtain:

\displaystyle|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap(U\times\mathbb{R})|+\int_{(L\cap(U\times\mathbb{R}))\Delta(U\times(-\infty,F])}g|z-F|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+g\frac{(a-F)^{2}}{2}\right)|U|+2C\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial U)

∎

3.5.
Comparison principle for nonstrict super/subsolutions

We finish the section with an application of the width bound to compare solutions of (
3.1
) away from the pinned contact line. Since we will now compare two viscosity solutions instead of a viscosity solution with a smooth test function, this requires justification. For this, it is standard that this type of comparison principle can be obtained by sliding arguments. We recall the viscosity theory for time-evolving level set functions in full detail in Appendix
B
, based on results by Barles
[
3
]
and Ishii and Sato
[
15
]
. The application of this theory will be in the following result:

Proposition 3.10

(Sliding comparison)
.

Fix
R_{0}\in(1,R)
. Let
L_{0},L_{1}
be viscosity solutions of

(3.3)

\begin{cases}-H_{L}+g(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L}}{g})=0&\hbox{on }\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}})\\
\cos\theta_{\gamma^{o}_{L}}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}&\hbox{on }\gamma^{o}_{L}\\
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0},F+\lambda_{L}/g-h_{0}}\subset\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}}(L)\subset\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0},F+\lambda_{L}/g+h_{0}}\end{cases}

for a constant
h_{0}>0
(chosen, for example, using Lemma
3.8
). Abbreviating
\tilde{L}_{i}=L_{i}\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}})
, we also assume that
\tilde{L}_{i}\Delta(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0},F})
is a bounded set for each
i=0,1
.

We then have

(\tilde{L}_{1}-te_{z})^{*}\subset(\tilde{L}_{0})_{*}\subset(\tilde{L}_{0})^{*}\subset(\tilde{L}_{1}+te_{z})_{*}

for any
t>\max(\frac{|\lambda_{L_{0}}-\lambda_{L_{1}}|}{g},2h_{0})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
B.2
, the stationary indicator
u_{0}(t,x,z)=\mathbf{1}_{L_{0}^{*}}(x,z)
is a viscosity solution of

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{0}-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla u_{0}\otimes\nabla u_{0}}{|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}})D^{2}u_{0}\right)+g(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L_{0}}}{g})|\nabla u_{0}|\geq 0&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\\
\partial_{r}u_{0}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\nabla u_{0}|&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}\\
\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0},F+\lambda_{L}/g-h_{0}}}\leq u_{0}&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}\end{cases}

and the sliding indicator
u_{1}(t,x,z)=\mathbf{1}_{L_{1}^{*}}(x,z-f(t))
with
f
a smooth increasing function is a viscosity solution of

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{1}-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla u_{1}\otimes\nabla u_{1}}{|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}})D^{2}u_{1}\right)+g(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L_{1}}+gf(t)+f^{\prime}(t)}{g})|\nabla u_{1}|\leq 0&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\\
\partial_{r}u_{1}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\nabla u_{1}|&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}\\
u_{1}\leq\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0},F+\lambda_{L}/g+h_{0}+f(t)}}&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}.\end{cases}

We apply Lemma
3.8
, and choose
f(0)\ll 0
so that
L_{1}-f(0)e_{z}\subset L_{0}
. We can achieve this, and reduce to a comparison in a bounded domain, by the hypothesis that
\tilde{L}_{i}\Delta(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0},F})
are bounded. Subsequently, we would like to increase
f
as much as possible while maintaining the comparison principle given by Lemma
B.1
, which preserves
u_{1}(t)\leq u_{0}(t)
from the ordering of initial data. The capillary boundary condition on
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}
is invariant under sliding, so this is a question of the interior equation and the Dirichlet ordering on
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}
. For the interior equation, we have

-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla u_{1}\otimes\nabla u_{1}}{|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}})D^{2}u_{1}\right)+g(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L_{0}}}{g})|\nabla u_{1}|\leq(\lambda_{L_{1}}-\lambda_{L_{0}}+gf(t)+f^{\prime}(t))|\nabla u_{1}|

so the right side is nonpositive as long as

gf(t)<\lambda_{L_{0}}-\lambda_{L_{1}}

and
f^{\prime}
is taken sufficiently small.

On
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}
, we have inclusion of boundary data as long as

f(t)+h_{0}\leq-h_{0}

Combining the two estimates, we conclude the comparison can be extended to any

f(t)<\min(-2h_{0},\tfrac{\lambda_{L_{0}}-\lambda_{L_{1}}}{g})

The reverse bound is similar, so we conclude.
∎

4.
Regularity of globally stable profiles

In this section we establish basic regularity of globally stable profiles. As the main tool in doing so, we show that these profiles satisfy an
almost-minimality property
involving the dissipation. We use this property to obtain nondegeneracy of globally stable profiles near their contact sets, and consequently we get length bounds on the contact lines
\gamma^{p},\gamma^{o}
. Together these regularity estimates imply a pre-compactness property of globally stable states under which many important quantities, including the energy and the pressure, converge.

Throughout this section, we focus on the local regularity of globally stable states, and write for a profile
L
and a bounded set

\mathcal{E}[L;U]:=|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap U|-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}|\Sigma^{p}_{L}\cap U|-\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\Sigma^{o}_{L}\cap U|+\int_{(L\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F})\cap U}g|z-F|

Note that we are suppressing the dependence on
R
and
F
from our notation, but both are still present in the energy. Throughout, we allow
R\in(1+\varepsilon_{0},\infty]
, and our universal results depend on
\varepsilon_{0}
.

4.1.

2.4
to handle the left side, we obtain (
4.2
) in the limit.

It remains only to discuss the convergence of free surface area and of energy. Proceeding as in
[
17
]
*Theorem 21.14, one can show that
|\nabla\mathbf{1}_{L_{n}}|\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}|\nabla\mathbf{1}_{L_{\infty}}|
inside
U
. This implies convergence of area of the free surface, again using the estimate from Lemma
D.2
to prevent accumulation of the perimeter near the fixed boundary.
∎

As an application of Lemma
4.4
, we state an improved compactness result for sequences of globally stable profiles. Here, the main point is the stability of the limiting profile.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose we have a sequence of profiles
(L_{n})
and forcing
F_{n}\to F_{\infty}
such that the
L_{n}
are globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F_{n}]
for some
R\in(1,\infty]
and such that the plate contact sets of the
L_{n}
converge: i.e. there exists
\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}|=0

Then the
L_{n}
are precompact in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. All subsequential limits
L_{\infty}
are in
X^{R}_{F_{\infty}}
with
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}=\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}
, are globally stable (with volume constraint for
F_{\infty}
, if
R<\infty
), and have the same energy
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, the
L_{n}
are uniform almost minimizers. Thus, we may apply Lemma
4.4
to extract a subsequence
L_{n_{k}}
and a subsequential limit
L_{\infty}
. This lemma gives local convergence of the
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n_{k}}}
to
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}
, so we conclude that
\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}=\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}}
. Also, in the case
R<\infty
, we can combine with Lemma
3.8
to conclude that the
L_{n_{k}}
converge in volume, and hence
L_{\infty}
solves the volume constraint for
F_{\infty}
.

The lemma also gives local convergence of energy. If
R<\infty
, this implies global convergence, by using Lemma
3.8
and the boundedness of the
F_{n}
to trap the free surfaces in a compact region. In the case
R=\infty
, a modified proof of Lemma
3.8
shows that the free surfaces can be trapped in a region whose height shrinks exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
. Combining this with local estimates from almost-minimality, we can interchange limits by dominated convergence.

Finally, it remains to show stability of
L_{\infty}
. As soon as we have stability, the fact that all limiting profiles have the same energy follows from the fact that they share
\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}
. Let us consider a competitor
L^{\prime}\in X^{R}_{F_{\infty}}
. Then we can take

L_{n}^{\prime}:=L^{\prime}+(F_{n}-F_{\infty})e_{z}\in X^{R}_{F_{n}}

as a competitor to
L_{n}
(satisfying the volume constraint if
R<\infty
). From stability of
L_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{n};F_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{n}^{\prime};F_{n}]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{n},L_{n}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L^{\prime};F_{\infty}]-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}(F_{n}-F_{\infty})\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{n},L_{n}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L^{\prime};F_{\infty}]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\infty},L^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\dots+C|F_{n}-F_{\infty}|+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{n},L_{\infty}]

Here, in the last step we have used the dissipation triangle inequality (
1.4
) twice to replace
\operatorname{Diss}[L_{n},L_{n}^{\prime}]
, absorbing the resulting
\operatorname{Diss}[L^{\prime},L_{n}^{\prime}]
into the
|F_{n}-F_{\infty}|
term. Both terms in the last line disappear as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, the left side converges to
\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\infty};F_{\infty}]
as already shown, so we obtain the stability inequality for
L_{\infty}
in the limit.
∎

4.3.
Continuity of the pressure

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
(L_{n})
are globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[\cdot;F_{n}]
with
R_{n}\in(1,\infty]
, such that
R_{n}\to R_{\infty}\in(1,\infty]
and
F_{n}\to F_{\infty}
. If
L_{n}\to L_{\infty}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, then
P^{*}[L_{n};F_{n}]\to P^{*}[L_{\infty};F_{\infty}]
, where
P^{*}
is the pressure from Proposition
2.8
for a fixed parameter
R_{0}
and vector field
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

We recall

P^{*}[L;F]=\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap C_{R_{0}}}\nu^{f}_{L}\cdot D\mathcal{V}\nu^{f}_{L}+g(z-F)(\mathcal{V}-e_{z})\cdot\nu^{f}_{L}

where the support of
\mathcal{V}
avoids
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1}
. Let us observe that the integral is uniformly bounded given a bound for
F
, by using Lemma
3.9
to control the measure of the free surface and Lemma
3.8
to ensure that the integrand is bounded.

Write
\mu_{n}
(resp.
\mu_{\infty}
) for the vector measure
\mathbf{1}_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}\cap\mathrm{spt}(\mathcal{V}-e_{z})}\nu^{f}_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{n}}}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
. By Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
3.8
, these measures have support trapped in a compact region, and the free surfaces are uniform perimeter almost-minimizers in the standard sense. Therefore, we can apply standard closure properties for
L^{1}
-convergent almost-minimizers
[
17
]
*Theorem 21.14 to have
\mu_{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu_{\infty}
and
|\mu_{n}|\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}|\mu_{\infty}|
.

The convergence of the gravity term in the pressure follows immediately from the weak* convergence of vector measures. The convergence of the
\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}\cdot D\mathcal{V}\nu^{f}_{L_{0}}
term comes from a theorem of Reshetnyak (
[
20
]
, see also the appendix in
[
16
]
), which relies on the fact that we also have
|\mu_{n}|\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}|\mu_{\infty}|
and thus
|\mu_{n}|(U)\to|\mu_{\infty}|(U)
for any
U
with
|\partial U\cap\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}|=0
.
∎

5.
Limits of rate-independent systems

In this section we introduce some of the foundational elements which are used in studying the limits of rate independent systems. We recall for the convenience of the reader that we work with energetic rate-independent systems which satisfy the properties of Definition
1.4
(
R<\infty
) or Definition
1.5
(
R=\infty
). The results in this section will form the backbone of the arguments for later sections. In particular, in Section
6
we will discuss precompactness of discrete minimizing movements schemes which approximate energy solutions to the evolution. In Section
7
, we will discuss the precompactness as
R\to\infty
of energy solutions.

This section is mainly recalling results from the literature, especially
[
1
]
, and adapting them to our setting. We summarize the framework in
[
1
]
condensing it into a clear list of criteria for convergence in Lemma
5.5
. The ideas presented here should also be closely related to general evolutionary
\Gamma
convergence type frameworks for limits of rate independent systems, see the
[
18
]
for further description and citations. However due to our nonlinear geometric setting where the forcing acts through a constraint we are unable to conveniently apply those abstract results set in linear spaces.

This section is organized as follows. First, we discuss the energy dissipation upper bound, the opposite inequality to (
1.6
). This is a fact which depends only on global stability, and plays a key role in proving convergence results for the energy dissipation lower bound. Next, we discuss the time compactness of evolutions given by dissipation bounds. Hand-in-hand with this, we address lack of evolutionary compactness for the nondissipative part of the evolution, and the measurable selection techniques that we employ to work around this. Finally, the main result of this section is a proof of the energy dissipation lower bound for a limit of approximate solutions satisfying appropriate assumptions.

5.1.
Energy dissipation upper bound

Here we show that general paths of globally stable states satisfy an upper bound on the energy dissipated.

Lemma 5.1

(Reverse EDI)
.

Fix
R\in(1,\infty]
, and suppose
L(t)
is a Borel measurable map
[0,T]\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
L(t)
is
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F(t)]
-globally stable for each
t
. Then we have the energy-dissipation upper bound:

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(0);F(0)]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(0);F(T)]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R}[L(t);F(t)]\dot{F}(t)\,dt\leq\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}[L;[0,T]]

where we recall that
\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}
refers to the total variation of the specified path with respect to the dissipation distance. Here,
P^{*}_{R}
is the pressure defined in Proposition
2.8
.

Proof.

We follow the approach in
[
1
]
*Lemma 4.4.

Let
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{N}=T
be a
\delta
-fine and
\delta/2
-separated partition of
[0,T]
, where
\delta
is sufficiently small to apply Proposition
2.8
for each pair of adjacent times. Let
\Phi_{s}
be the volume altering flow as in the proof of Proposition
2.8
.

Then using global stability of
L(t_{n})
and the Taylor expansion about
L(t_{n+1})
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[\Phi_{F(t_{n})-F(t_{n+1})}(L(t_{n+1}));F(t_{n})]+\operatorname{Diss}[L(t_{n}),L(t_{n+1})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{n});F(t_{n})]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{n+1});F(t_{n+1})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{n});F(t_{n})]

\displaystyle\quad\dots+P^{*}_{R}[L(t_{n+1});F(t_{n+1})]\dot{F}(t_{n+1})(t_{n}-t_{n+1})+O(|t_{n}-t_{n+1}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad\dots+\operatorname{Diss}[L(t_{n}),L(t_{n+1})]

We note that the quadratic remainder term is controlled independent of
L(t)
using the perimeter bound from Lemma
3.9
and the height bound from Lemma
3.8
. Summing, we get

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}[L;[0,T]]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Diss}[L(t_{n}),L(t_{n+1})]

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(0);F(0)]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(T);F(T)]

\displaystyle\quad\dots+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}P^{*}_{R}[L(t_{n+1});F(t_{n+1})]\dot{F}(t_{n+1})(t_{n+1}-t_{n})

\displaystyle\quad\dots+NO(\delta^{2})

Note that only the last two lines depend on the choice of partition. The term
P^{*}_{R}[L(t);F(t)]\dot{F}
is a measurable function of
t
, which can be bounded pointwise by another application of Lemma
3.9
, so the theory of Lebesgue integration provides that we can choose a family of partitions parametrized by
\delta
such that

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}P^{*}_{R}[L(t_{n+1});F(t_{n+1})]\dot{F}(t_{n+1})(t_{n+1}-t_{n})\to\int_{0}^{T}P^{*}[L(t);F(t)]\dot{F}dt

On the other hand, the quadratic remainder term vanishes as
\delta\to 0
since
N\leq 2/\delta
by assumption. Thus, we obtain the inequality in the limit.
∎

5.2.
Time compactness of contact sets and measurable selection of profiles for contact sets

We recall a version of Helly’s selection principle, the basic compactness result for bounded variation paths into a metric space, adapted to our setting:

Lemma 5.2

(Time compactness of contact sets / Helly’s selection)
.

Let
\Sigma^{p}_{n}(t)
be a sequence of paths of contact sets with corresponding contact lines
\gamma^{p}_{n}(t)
. If

\sup_{n}\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(\Sigma^{p}_{n};[t_{0},t_{1}])<\infty

and

\sup_{n}\sup_{t}\sup_{(x,z)\in\gamma^{p}_{n}(t)}|z|<+\infty\ \hbox{ and }\ \sup_{n}\sup_{t}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\gamma^{p}_{n}(t))<+\infty

then there exists a subsequence along which
\Sigma^{p}_{n_{k}}(t)
converges pointwise on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
in the
\operatorname{Diss}
quasimetric.

Here, the notation
\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}
refers to the total dissipation of a path

(5.2)

\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(\Sigma^{p}(t);[t_{0},t_{1}])=\sup_{\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Diss}(\Sigma^{p}(\tau_{i}),\Sigma^{p}(\tau_{i+1}))

where the supremum is over all partitions of
[t_{0},t_{1}]
. The assumed height and length bounds restrict us to a space which is compact with respect to the dissipation distance, by
L^{1}
pre-compactness of sets of bounded perimeter in a bounded region. We will almost always verify these bounds using Lemma
3.8
and Theorem
4.3
respectively.

We will use Helly’s selection principle to get evolutionary compactness of energy solutions, or discrete approximations thereof. However, this leads to an important technical issue which must be addressed:

Remark 5.3
.

The natural mode of convergence of energy solutions is pointwise in time with respect to the dissipation distance. As a result, this gives an evolutionary notion of convergence for the contact set, but not for the profile itself. This is possible due to the lack of unique extension of a given contact set to a globally stable free surface, and the lack of frictional dissipation associated with motions of the free surface. We can identify a limiting profile by choosing a convergent subsequence at any given time, but it is not necessarily possible to choose a subsequence for which the profile converges for every time. This kind of issue has been discussed before in the literature, in particular it also appears in
[
1
]
and we can follow a similar approach. We ensure that the selection of profiles along a time-dependent subsequence is measurable, and so we will treat this selection as the limiting evolution.

The measurable-selection result we quote to justify Remark
5.3
is:

Lemma 5.4

(Measurable selection, (
[
22
]
Srivastava 5.2.5))
.

Let
T
be a measurable space, and
Y
be a separable metric space. If
F:T\to Y
is a measurable, compact-valued multifunction, then there exists a measurable selection of
F
: a measurable function
f:T\to Y
such that
f(t)\in F(t)
for each
t\in T
.

Here, the preimage of the multifunction, for purposes of measurability, is

F^{-1}(A):=\{t\in T:F(t)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}

We present a corollary of Lemma
5.4
in the appendix as Lemma
C.2
, to justify the claim that measurable selection of subsequential limits (or alternatively of subsequential limits extremizing a measurable functional) is possible.

Thus, it is possible in principle to select an evolution of profiles, corresponding to the evolution of contact sets. The final and most involved proof in this section will be to show that the selection can be made to satisfy the energy dissipation balance.

5.3.
Energy dissipation lower bound

Here we show that limits of sequences of paths which satisfy an approximately optimal energy dissipation lower bound (EDI) will also satisfy the EDI (and therefore the full dissipation balance as well). This is technically complicated by the fact that motions of the free surface carry no dissipation and therefore there is very little time compactness. As discussed already in Section
5.2
the set of globally stable states
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{p})
with a given contact set
\Sigma^{p}
may be nontrivial and so a measurable selection needs to occur at the limit level. This is additionally complicated by the fact that the pressure may not be constant on the set
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{p})
, so it is not clear that all measurable selections of the path actually do satisfy the EDI. Instead we show that, by performing a selection of a restricted subset of the minimizers attained along good subsequences, there is at least one selection which does satisfy the EDI.

We state the assumptions of the next lemma in a very general setting, to allow us to apply it both to the discrete scheme and to limits of continuous-time solutions.

Lemma 5.5

(Stability of the energy-dissipation balance)
.

Let
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto L_{n}(t)\in X^{R_{n}}_{F_{n}(t)}
be a family of Borel-measurable paths, such that

(i)

(Convergence of
R_{n}
)
R_{n},R_{\infty}\in(1,\infty]
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{n}=R_{\infty}
.

(ii)

(Convergence of
F_{n}
) The
F_{n}(t)
are bounded in
BV[t_{0},t_{1}]
and converge uniformly to a
C^{1}
limit
F_{\infty}(t)
(but
F_{n}
may be discontinuous).

(iii)

(Convergence of
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}}
) The family of paths
\Sigma_{L_{n}}^{c}(t)
converges pointwise in time as
n\to\infty
and has uniformly bounded total dissipation

\sup_{n}\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L_{n};[0,T])<\infty.

(iv)

(Pointwise compactness of
L_{n}
) For each
t
, the sequence
(L_{n}(t))
is compact in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, with all subsequential limits contained in
X^{R_{\infty}}_{F_{\infty}(t)}
and globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[\cdot,F_{\infty}(t)]
.

(v)

(Relative energy convergence) For any
t_{0},t_{1}
, and any subsequential limits
L_{\infty}(t_{i})
of the
L_{n}(t_{i})
for
i=0,1
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{n}(t_{0});F_{n}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{n}(t_{1});F_{n}(t_{1})]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[L_{\infty}(t_{0});F_{\infty}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[L_{\infty}(t_{1});F_{\infty}(t_{1})]

Note that the right-hand-side is independent of the choice of subsequential limit, since all subsequential limits are globally stable for the same contact set, hence have the same energy.

(vi)

(Dominated approximation of pressure-forcing term) There exist functions
Q_{n}
on
[0,T]
which are dominated by an integrable
\hat{Q}
and satisfy the next two properties. For any
t
and for any subsequence
L_{n_{k}}(t)
which converges in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to some
L_{\infty}(t)
, it holds that

\lim_{k\to\infty}Q_{n_{k}}(t)=P^{*}_{R_{\infty}}[L_{\infty}(t),F_{\infty}(t)]\dot{F}_{\infty}(t)

Moreover, the energy dissipation inequality holds in a limiting sense with the
Q_{n}
replacing the pressure-forcing term: for any
(t_{0},t_{1})\subset[0,T]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{E}_{R_{n_{k}}}[L_{n_{k}}(t_{0});F_{n_{k}}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n_{k}}}[L_{n_{k}}(t_{1});F_{n_{k}}(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{n_{k}}(t)dt\right]

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L_{n_{k}};[t_{0},t_{1}])

Under these assumptions, there exists a Borel-measurable selection
L(t)
of subsequential limits of the
L_{n}(t)
such that
L(t)
is an energy solution as per Definition
1.4
.

Proof.

For any selection of subsequential limits
L
, we have that
L(t)\in X^{R_{\infty}}_{F_{\infty}(t)}
and
L(t)
is globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[\cdot,F_{\infty}(t)]
by assumption. Thus, our task is to show that a measurable selection exists which satisfies the energy dissipation balance

\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{0});F(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{1});F(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R}[L(t);F(t)]\dot{F}=\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L;[t_{0},t_{1}]).

We check this following the proof of
[
1
]
*Theorem 4.3(viii). For each time
t
, we restrict to a subsequence along which
\limsup_{n}Q_{n}(t)
is achieved and use the compactness assumption to identify a corresponding limiting profile
\overline{L}(t)
. By Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
C.2
, the path
\overline{L}(t)
can be selected to be Borel measurable. From assumption
(vi)
, we conclude
\limsup_{n}Q_{n}(t)=P^{*}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t);F_{\infty}(t)]\dot{F}_{\infty}
.

By Lemma
5.1
, the reverse energy dissipation inequality for measurable paths of globally stable states, we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{R}[\overline{L}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[\overline{L}(t_{1});F(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R}[\overline{L}(t);F(t)]\dot{F}\leq\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(\overline{L};[t_{0},t_{1}]).

We deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}

\displaystyle[\overline{L}(t_{0});F_{\infty}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t_{1});F_{\infty}(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t);F_{\infty}(t)]\dot{F}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(\overline{L};[t_{0},t_{1}])

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L_{n},[t_{0},t_{1}])

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}\left[\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{n}(t_{0});F_{n}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{n}(t_{1});F_{n}(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{n}(t)dt\right]

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t_{0});F_{\infty}(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t_{1});F_{\infty}(t_{1})]+\liminf_{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{n}(t)

Here, the first inequality comes from the previous step, the second inequality uses lower semicontinuity of the total dissipation and assumption
(iii)
, the third inequality uses assumption
(vi)
, and the fourth inequality uses assumption
(v)
. We obtain

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t);F_{\infty}(t)]\dot{F}_{\infty}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\limsup_{n}Q_{n}(t)\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{n}(t)

Consequently, we can apply the elementary result Lemma
C.1
to get that
Q_{N}\to\limsup_{n}Q_{n}
in measure as
N\to\infty
, since assumption
(vi)
provides that the
Q_{N}
are dominated. The energy dissipation balance for
\overline{L}(t)
then follows from (
5.3
), after replacing
\liminf_{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{n}
by its value
\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}P^{*}_{R_{\infty}}[\overline{L}(t);F_{\infty}(t)]\dot{F}_{\infty}
by dominated convergence theorem.

∎

6.
Existence of minimizing movements energy solutions

We now consider a discrete-time scheme for the evolution. Fix
\delta>0
, and let
P_{\delta}=\{t_{0},\dots,t_{N}\}
be a partition of times with
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{N}=T
and
0<t_{i+1}-t_{i}<\delta
for each
i
. Given the initial shape
L(t_{0})
, we choose the subsequent elements of the discrete scheme for partition
P_{\delta}
by:

(6.1)

L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{L\in X^{R}_{F(t_{i+1})}}\bigg\{\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F(t_{i+1})]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L]\bigg\}

Recall that we have shown existence of minimizers for this functional in Lemma
2.6
and that minimizers are automatically globally stable profiles. We extend this to a function of time via discontinuous interpolation:

(6.2)

L_{\delta}(t)=L_{\delta}(t_{i})\hbox{ for }t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1})

We will derive some basic estimates for the discrete scheme which show that we can send
\delta\to 0
and obtain a limiting evolution which satisfies Definition
1.4
. Our main result is:

Corollary 6.1
.

Up to a subsequence
\delta_{n}\to 0
, we have that
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\delta_{n}}(t)}
converges in
L^{1}(d\mathcal{H}^{d-1})
for each
t
, to a limit which we denote
\Sigma^{p}_{L(t)}
. For
L(t)
the minimal energy profile associated to the contact set
\Sigma^{p}_{L(t)}
, we have that
L(t)
is an energy solution as in Definition
1.4
.

To prove this, we use the results of the previous section to verify global stability and energy dissipation balance for the limit. This requires the Taylor expansion of Proposition
2.8
to show that the discrete scheme obeys a discrete version of the energy-dissipation balance, which converges to the expected balance as
\delta\to 0
.

Lemma 6.2
.

We have the total dissipation bound

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}

\displaystyle[L(t_{N_{1}});F(t_{N_{1}})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{0});F(t_{0})]+\sum_{i=0}^{N_{1}}\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}[F]_{BV[t_{0},t_{N_{1}}]}e^{C[F]_{BV[t_{0},t_{N_{1}}]}}

for constants
A_{0},C
depending on
L_{\delta}(t_{0})
and
\mathop{\textup{osc}}F
.

In particular, we conclude the energy bound

(6.4)

\sup_{0\leq i\leq N_{1}}(C_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(t_{i});F(t_{i})])\leq A_{0}e^{C[F]_{BV[t_{0},t_{N_{1}}]}}

where
C_{0}
is an additive constant similar to the one appearing in Lemma
2.3
, such that
\mathcal{E}_{R}+C_{0}>0
.

Proof.

For
L_{\delta}(t)
as in (
6.1
), (
6.2
), write

d_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i+1});F(t_{i+1})]+\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})]

We use Proposition
2.8
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{N});F(t_{N})]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}d_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{N});F(t_{N})]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}P_{R}^{*}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})](F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))

\displaystyle+C(C_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})])(F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))^{2}

Here,
C
is the constant in front of the remainder term in the proposition, which is uniformly bounded as explained in the remark after the proposition. The energy is being used with an additive constant
C_{0}
from the coercivity result Lemma
2.3
to control the term
|\Sigma^{f}_{L_{0}}\cap C_{R_{0}}|
from the proposition. For conciseness, we write
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L;F]:=C_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]
for the rest of the proof.

The pressure
P^{*}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})]
can also be bounded by
C\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})])
for a different
C
, so we merge together to get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{N});F(t_{N})]-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]

(6.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})]|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|

From here, we use Gronwall-type arguments to bound the energy and the total dissipation separately. First, for the energy, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{N});F(t_{N})]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})](F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))

Let
f(n)=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{n});F(t_{n})]
. We have shown
f>0
with

f(n)\leq f(0)+C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(i)|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|

We recall the discrete-time version of Grönwall’s inequality: we have
f(0)\leq f(0)
, and inductively, if

f(n)\leq f(0)\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1+C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|\right)

then

\displaystyle f(n+1)

\displaystyle\leq f(0)+C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)\sum_{k=0}^{n}f(k)|F(t_{k+1})-F(t_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq f(0)\left(1+\sum_{k=0}^{n}|F(t_{k+1})-F(t_{k})|\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(1+C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq f(0)\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n}\left(1+C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)|F(t_{k+1})-F(t_{k})|\right)\right)

where the final inequality follows directly from matching terms. Using that
\prod(1+a_{k})\leq e^{\sum a_{k}}
for
a_{k}\geq 0
, we conclude

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle\leq f(0)\exp\left(C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq f(0)\exp\left(C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)[F]_{BV[t_{0},t_{n}]}\right)

This proves (
6.4
). To prove (
6.2
), we then substitute the energy bound into the right-hand-side of (
6.5
) to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{N});F(t_{N})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]

\displaystyle\quad\leq(C_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})])\exp\left(C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)[F]_{BV[t_{0},t_{N}]}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}C(1+\mathop{\textup{osc}}F)|F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})|

which is in the desired form.

∎

In particular, as a refinement of the Taylor expansions in the previous lemma, we have:

Lemma 6.3

(Discrete approximation of pressure-forcing)
.

For any
0<m<n<N
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{m});F(t_{m})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{n});F(t_{n})]+\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}P^{*}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})](F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\delta}(t_{i}),L_{\delta}(t_{i+1})]-C\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}(F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))^{2}

In particular, setting

Q_{\delta}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}P^{*}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{i});F(t_{i})]\frac{F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i})}{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(t)

we have for any fixed
t_{0},t_{1}
that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left[\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{0});F(t_{0})]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{\delta}(t_{1});F(t_{1})]+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}Q_{\delta}(t)dt\right]

\displaystyle\quad\geq\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L_{\delta},[t_{0},t_{1}])

Moreover, for any
t
, along any subsequence
\delta_{k}\to 0
such that
L_{\delta_{k}}(t)
converges in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to some
L
, we have
Q_{\delta_{k}}(t)\to P^{*}_{R}[L;F(t)]\dot{F}(t)
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows directly from the computation in Lemma
6.2
. The inequality of liminfs then follows from the fact that
\sum(F(t_{i+1})-F(t_{i}))^{2}\to 0
as partition fineness goes to 0 for any continuous BV function.

Fix a time
t
and restrict to a subsequence along which
L_{\delta_{k}}(t)
converges in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to some
L
. We assume enough regularity for
F
that
F(t_{k})\to F(t)
, where
t_{k}
is the actual time step of
L_{\delta_{k}}(t)
. Thus, Lemma
4.6
gives

P^{*}_{R}[L_{\delta_{k}}(t);F(t_{k})]\to P^{*}_{R}[L;F(t)]

The convergence
Q_{\delta_{k}}(t)\to P^{*}_{R}[L;F(t)]\dot{F}(t)
then follows.
∎

Finally, we prove the main result of this section: compactness of the discrete scheme as
\delta\to 0^{+}
, with all subsequential limits satisfying the properties of an energy solution as per Definition

.

.

We use the version of Helly’s section principle stated in Lemma

such that

for each

This selects the contact sets of the limiting path, but not the profiles

The existence of an energy solution

7.
Limit as
R\to\infty

We now turn toward establishing compactness of energy solutions as the outer container radius
R
is allowed to increase to
\infty
. This is challenging due to the fact that the absolute energy (
1.1
) diverges with
R
, so an appropriate relativization must be found. To deal with this issue, we first show in Section
7.1
that the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
for a globally stable state has a decomposition
\lambda=a_{0}R^{-1}+O(R^{1-d})
, where crucially the coefficient of the first term is an explicit constant. In Section
7.2
this allows us to use our viscosity theory to trap the globally stable state between two barriers that are very close, with the large term in the Lagrange multiplier only acting as a constant vertical shift.

Finally in Section
7.3
we apply this to establish asymptotics for the diverging part of the energy with respect to
R
. These are accurate to high enough order to allow us to relativize the energy, send
R\to\infty
, and obtain a limiting evolution with finite relative energy. The last part of the section then verifies that the limiting object is an energy solution for the expected limiting energy (
1.3
) in our main result Corollary
7.6
.

7.1.
An important decomposition of the Lagrange multiplier

The decomposition of the Lagrange multiplier can be understood through the following formal computation:

\displaystyle\lambda_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}\lambda_{L}e_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}(-H_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}+g(z-F))e_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}\left(\int_{\gamma^{p}_{L}}\eta^{p}\cdot e_{z}+\int_{\gamma^{o}_{L}}\eta^{o}\cdot e_{z}\right)

where
\eta^{p},\eta^{o}
are conormal vectors, normal to the contact lines and tangent to the free surface. These integrals can then be estimated in terms of the contact angles, and in particular the integral over
\gamma^{o}_{L}
can be computed explicitly, using Stokes’ theorem along with the fact that the contact angle is constant on
\gamma^{o}_{L}
.

To avoid the technical issues that arise with integrating by parts near the contact line, we instead we use an energy perturbation argument to capture the same idea.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
L
be globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot,F]
,
R\in(1,\infty)
. We have the following formula for the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{L}=-H_{\Sigma^{f}_{L}}+g(z-F)
:

(7.1)

\lambda_{L}=-\frac{\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{L}}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}

where

(7.2)

\tilde{\lambda}_{L}\in-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})+(\mu_{-}\vee\mu_{+})\mathrm{Per}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1}}(\Sigma^{p}_{L})[-1,1]

By Theorem
4.3
, we conclude that
\lambda_{L}=a_{0}R^{-1}+a_{1}(R,L)R^{1-d}
, where
a_{0}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\frac{\omega_{d-2}}{\omega_{d-1}}
is a constant independent of
L
and
R
, and
a_{1}
is uniformly bounded in
L,R
.

Proof.

We consider a test velocity
\mathcal{V}=-\frac{e_{z}}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}+\mathcal{V}_{0}
, for
\mathcal{V}_{0}
as in Corollary
2.12
. Let
L_{s}
be the result of flowing
L_{0}:=L
under this velocity for time
s
.

We note that
\mathcal{C}_{F}[L_{s}]=\mathcal{C}_{F}[L_{0}]=0
by repeating the argument of Lemma
2.10
. The energy change due to flowing by
\mathcal{V}_{0}
for time
s
is
\lambda_{L_{0}}s+O(s^{2})
by Corollary
2.12
. We subsequently must compute the energy change due to the constant vertical shift explicitly. The area of the free surface does not change, while the relative areas of the contact surfaces change by exactly
-s\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{r})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}
, for
r=1,R
respectively. The change in gravity can also be computed (we assume
s>0
for demonstration, and write
c_{R}=|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|^{-1}
):

\displaystyle\int_{L_{s}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}|z-F|-\int_{(L_{s}+c_{R}se_{z})\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}|z-F|

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{s}\cap\{z\geq F\}}-c_{R}s+\int_{\{z\leq F-c_{R}s\}\setminus L_{s}}c_{R}s

\displaystyle\dots-\int_{L_{s}\cap\{F-c_{R}s<z<F\}}z+c_{R}s-F-\int_{\{F-c_{R}s<z<F\}\setminus L_{s}}z-F

\displaystyle=-c_{R}s\int_{L_{s}\Delta\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}}\mathrm{sign}(z-F)-\int_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F-c_{R}s}}z+c_{R}s-F

Here, the first term in the last line disappears since
\mathcal{C}_{F}[L_{s}]=0
, while the second term is clearly
O(s^{2})
. Thus, we absorb this into the other quadratic remainder term to obtain:

(7.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[L_{s};F]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}[L_{0};F]+\lambda_{L}s+O(s^{2})

\displaystyle\dots+s\frac{\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}+s\frac{\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}.

To estimate the dissipation for this flow, we will use the perimeter bound on
\Sigma^{p}_{L}
. With an argument essentially identical to that of
[
17
]
*Lemma 17.9, one can show with sharp constant that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Diss}(L_{0},L_{s})

\displaystyle\leq(\mu_{-}\vee\mu_{+})|(\Sigma^{p}_{L_{0}}-|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|^{-1}se_{z})\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{L_{0}}|

(7.4)

\displaystyle\leq|s|\frac{(\mu_{-}\vee\mu_{+})\mathrm{Per}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}}(\Sigma^{p}_{L_{0}})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}

We combine (
7.3
) and (
7.1
) with the global stability of
L_{0}
to obtain:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{L_{0}}s+s\frac{\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}+s\frac{\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}

\displaystyle\quad\dots+|s|\frac{(\mu_{-}\vee\mu_{+})\mathrm{Per}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1}}(\Sigma^{p}_{L_{0}})}{|B_{R}\setminus B_{1}|}+O(s^{2})

Sending
s\to 0
, we obtain (
7.1
) and (
7.2
).
∎

7.2.
Outer approximation of globally stable profiles

In this section, we develop approximations of the energy in the region
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}
, where
R_{0}\in(1,R)
is a parameter. To do so, we show that globally stable profiles can generally be trapped between translates of a fixed
R
-dependent reference configuration using our viscosity theory, particularly Proposition
3.10
. Subsequently, we show that approximating with the reference configuration yields good estimates for the energy.

Write
\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}
for the unconstrained minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;0]
in
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}
. We have the Euler-Lagrange equation:

(7.5)

\begin{cases}-H_{\Sigma^{f}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}}+gz=0&\hbox{ on }\Sigma^{f}_{\mathsf{L}^{0}_{R}}\\
\nu^{f}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}\cdot\nu^{c}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}=-\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}&\hbox{ on }\gamma^{o}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}\\
\nu^{f}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}\cdot e_{r}=-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}&\hbox{ on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{1}\cap\Sigma^{f}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}}\end{cases}

If we consider vertical translations
\mathsf{L}^{0}_{R}+te_{z}
, the right-hand side of the first equation is replaced by
gt
, while the other two equations are unchanged.

We show that the decomposition for the Lagrange multiplier (
7.1
) implies that shifts of
\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}
closely approximate general globally stable profiles far from the plate:

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
L
be globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F]
. Then there exist constants
C,c
independent of
L,R,r
such that

(7.6)

L\Delta\left(\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}+(F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR})e_{z}\right)\subset\Sigma^{f}_{\mathsf{L}^{0}_{R}+(F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR})e_{z}}+[-h(r),h(r)]\,e_{z}\ \hbox{ in }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{r}

for
h(r)=C\left(R^{1-d}+e^{-cr}+e^{-c(R-r)}\right)
. Here
a_{0}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}\frac{\omega_{d-2}}{\omega_{d-1}}
is the same constant from Lemma
7.1
.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
3.10
with
r
serving as the
R_{0}
in the statement of that result. This yields that the inclusion holds for
h>\max(\frac{|\lambda_{L}-\lambda_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}+(F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR})e_{z}}|}{g},2h_{0})
where
h_{0}
bounds the oscillation in height of
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{r}}(\Sigma^{f}_{L})
and
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{r}}(\Sigma^{f}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}})
. We have
\lambda_{\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}+(F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR})e_{z}}=\frac{a_{0}}{R}
, so the first term in the maximum is
O(R^{1-d})
by Lemma
7.1
. We bound the second term in the maximum by exponentials as a direct application of Lemma
3.8
.
∎

We will subsequently write

(7.7)

\mathsf{L}_{R}:=\mathsf{L}_{R}^{0}+(F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR})e_{z},

where the dependence on
F
is not displayed explicitly to reduce notation.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
L
be globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F]
, and
\mathsf{L}_{R}
be as in (
7.7
). Fix
R_{0}\in(1,R)
. We have estimates comparing the two profiles in the region
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}
, as follows. All constants
C,c
will be independent of
R,R_{0},L,F
, but may depend on all other parameters.

The difference in volume is bounded as:

(7.8)

\operatorname{vol}((L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R})\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}))\leq C

The difference in container contact surface area is bounded as:

(7.9)

\left|\lvert{\Sigma^{o}_{L}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},o}-\lvert{\Sigma^{o}_{\mathsf{L}_{R}}}\rvert_{\mathrm{rel},o}\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}

The difference in gravitational energy is bounded as:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{L\Delta(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus C_{R_{0},F})}|z-F|-\int_{\mathsf{L}_{R}\Delta(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R,F}\setminus C_{R_{0},F})}|z-F|\right|

(7.10)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\frac{\log R}{R}+e^{-cR_{0}}\right)

The difference in free surface area is bounded as:

(7.11)

|\mathrm{Per}(L,\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}})-\mathrm{Per}(L_{R,R_{0}},\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}})|\leq\;C\left(\frac{\log R}{R}+e^{-cR_{0}}\right)

Proof.

First, we estimate the volume difference. We use Lemma
7.2
, noting that the vertical oscillation of the symmetric difference
L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R}
inside the thin shell
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{r}
can be bounded by

\min_{s\leq r}h(s)=\begin{cases}h(R/2)&r\geq R/2\\
h(r)&r<R/2\end{cases}

It follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}((L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R})\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}))

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{R_{0}}^{R}(R^{1-d}+e^{-c(r\wedge R/2)})r^{d-2}dr

\displaystyle=C\frac{R^{d-1}-R_{0}^{d-1}}{R^{d-1}}+Ce^{-cR/2}\frac{R^{d-1}-(R_{0}\vee R/2)^{d-1}}{R^{d-1}}

(7.12)

\displaystyle\quad\dots+C\mathrm{sgn}_{+}(R/2-R_{0})\int_{R_{0}}^{R/2}e^{-cr}r^{d-2}dr

The last integral is bounded by
\Gamma(d-1)
up to a constant factor, so the entire expression is bounded uniformly in
R_{0},R
, giving (
7.8
).

Next, we estimate the container contact surface. Once again, we use that the vertical oscillation of the symmetric difference
L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R}
inside
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}
is bounded by
h(R/2)=R^{1-d}+e^{-cR/2}
. Combining with
\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R})\sim R^{d-2}
gives (
7.9
).

Next, we estimate the difference in gravity. To improve on the estimate for the difference in volume, we must take advantage of the fact that the gravitational potential,
|z-F|
, is small far from the container. Thus, we split into cases. Let
R_{1}=R_{0}\vee(R-c^{-1}\log R)
, where
c
is the same constant appearing in the term
e^{-c(R-R_{1})}
in the width bound from Lemma
3.8
.

In the outer region
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{1}}
, we use the width bound to have
|z-F|=O(1)
. Since
R-R_{1}=O(\log R)
, we are able to improve the estimate of the volume difference in (
7.12
) to give
\operatorname{vol}((L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R})\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{1}}))=O(\tfrac{\log R}{R})
. Thus, the gravity difference in the outer region is
O(\tfrac{\log R}{R})
. In the inner region
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{1}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}
, we use the width bound to get
|z-F|=O(R^{-1}+e^{-cR_{0}})
. We combine this with the
O(1)
estimate for the volume of
L\Delta\mathsf{L}_{R}
to estimate the gravity difference in the inner region as
O(R^{-1}+e^{-cR_{0}})
. Combining the estimates for the two regions, we get the
O(\tfrac{\log R}{R}+e^{-cR_{0}})
estimate for the difference of gravitational energies.

Finally, to derive the free surface estimate, we use the stability properties of each profile. First, the unconstrained minimality of
\mathsf{L}_{R}
for
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR}]
implies:

\mathcal{E}_{R}[\mathsf{L}_{R};F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR}]\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[((L\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}))\cup(\mathsf{L}_{R}\cap\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}));F+\frac{a_{0}}{gR}]

7.3.
Relative energy and the
R\to\infty
limit

We continue to use
R_{0}
to refer to a parameter in
(1,R)
and
\mathsf{L}_{R}
to refer to the reference configuration defined in (
7.7
). We introduce the relative energy

(7.15)

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R,R_{0}}[L;F]:=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]-\mathcal{E}[{\mathsf{L}_{R}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|

To understand the relative energy, let us observe that the issues with sending
R\to\infty
come in the following parts: (i) the energy of
L
near the container, where one expects the area of the free surface, the gravitational energy, and the area of the container contact surface to diverge with
R
and (ii) the energy of
L
away from the container, where all terms have already been relativized in the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\cdot;F]
except for the area of the free surface. Subtracting
|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|
handles (ii) and in a manner consistent with our definition for the
R=\infty
energy (
1.3
). To handle (i), we subtract
\mathcal{E}[{\mathsf{L}_{R}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]
, relying on our proof in Lemma
7.3
that this closely approximates the energy of
L
in the same region.

With this definition, we now show that we can send
R\to\infty
and
R_{0}\to\infty
in that order and obtain a meaningful limit for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R,R_{0}}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Suppose for a sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
, we have profiles
L_{R_{n}}
which are globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[\cdot,F]
and which converge in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to a set
L_{\infty}
. Then
L_{\infty}
is globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[\cdot,F]
, with finite energy satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]

\displaystyle=\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]

\displaystyle=\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F].

Moreover, if
L_{R_{n}}^{\prime}
is another sequence, globally stable for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[\cdot,F^{\prime}]
and converging to
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]-\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]

Proof.

First, we observe that for any
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\setminus B_{1}
bounded, the height bound on globally stable states ensures that
(L_{R_{n}}\cap(U\times\mathbb{R}))\Delta(U\times\{z\leq 0\})
is contained in a compact set independent of
R_{n}
. Then by Lemma
4.4
, the uniform-in-
R
almost-minimality of globally stable states implies that we have energy convergence
\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};U\times\mathbb{R}]\to\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};U\times\mathbb{R}]
and convergence of contact sets in
U
(under the assumption that the limiting free surface intersects
\partial U\times\mathbb{R}
in a set of zero
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
measure, which holds for generic
U
).

Now, considering
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]
, we split into the inner and outer parts of the energy

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdots+\bigg(\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[\mathsf{L}_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]\bigg).

For the inner part, the first term in parenthesis above, the above local energy convergence ensures that as
n\to\infty
, we have for a.e.
R_{0}
that:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|

On the other hand for the outer energy, the energy asymptotics in Lemma
7.3
ensure

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[\mathsf{L}_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\frac{\log R_{n}}{R_{n}}+e^{-cR_{0}}\right)

so

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[\mathsf{L}_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]|\leq Ce^{-cR_{0}}.

Sending
R_{0}\to\infty
, this last error term disappears. Simultaneously, we have

\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-|B_{R_{0}}\setminus B_{1}|=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]

since we defined
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
in (
1.3
) by replacing the area of the free surface with the relativization
\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}}1-e_{z}\cdot\nu^{f}
. Thus, we conclude that

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]=\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]

The same computations hold with the limsup replaced by liminf.

To verify that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]
is indeed finite, we can use the width bound Lemma
3.8
. This implies that
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}}
is exponentially close to
\{z=F\}
as
|x|\to\infty
. Consequently, we can use almost-minimality to show that the local energy in an
r
-ball centered at
(x,F)
also decays exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
, from which we conclude that the energy is summable.

Next, we show global stability. Supposing stability fails, there must be some globally stable
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]-\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};F]-\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\infty},L_{\infty}^{\prime}]=:\varepsilon>0

By a similar argument to that of the previous paragraph, we can show that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
decays exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
for
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
as well. In particular, this means that for all sufficiently large
R_{0}
, we have

\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty};F,\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};F,\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\operatorname{Diss}[L_{\infty},L_{\infty}^{\prime}]\geq\varepsilon/2

Then, from convergence of local energy and dissipation, we have

(7.16)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};F,\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};F,\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\operatorname{Diss}[L_{n},L_{\infty}^{\prime}]\geq\varepsilon/2

We modify
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
to make a competitor to
L_{R_{n}}
. More precisely, we will keep the data of
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
up to a vertical shift on an annular region which scales with
R_{0}
and avoids the container and plate, while replacing it elsewhere by
L_{R_{n}}
. Thus, we define:

L_{R_{n}}^{\prime}:=(L_{R_{n}}\cap((\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}})\cup\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{\frac{R_{0}-1}{2}})\cup((L_{\infty}^{\prime}\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{\frac{R_{0}-1}{2}})+he_{z})

where
h
is a parameter chosen to ensure the volume constraint
\mathcal{C}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime}]=0
. Let us note that for fixed
R_{0}
, we can employ the local convergence of the
L_{R_{n}}
to conclude that
|h|(|B_{R_{0}}|-|B_{\frac{R_{0}-1}{2}}|)\leq 1+\operatorname{vol}((L_{\infty}\Delta L_{\infty}^{\prime})\cap(\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}\setminus\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{\frac{R_{0}-1}{2}})
for all sufficiently large
n
. This volume term is itself bounded independently of
R_{0}\gg 1
, again using the width bounds for
L_{\infty}
and
L_{\infty}^{\prime}
which decay exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
, so we in fact have
|h|\leq CR_{0}^{1-d}
for a uniform
C
and all
n
sufficiently large depending on
R_{0}
.

Now, global stability of
L_{R_{n}}
for
\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[\cdot;F]
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty}^{\prime}+he_{z};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]+\operatorname{Diss}(L_{R_{n}},L_{\infty}^{\prime}+he_{z})

\displaystyle\quad\dots+|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}}(L_{R_{n}}\Delta(L_{\infty}^{\prime}+he_{z}))|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]+\operatorname{Diss}(L_{R_{n}},L_{\infty}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\dots+\delta(R_{n},R_{0})

where the remainder
\delta
satisfies

(7.17)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\delta(R_{n},R_{0})\leq CR_{0}^{-1}

We achieve (
7.17
) by straightforward estimates for the energy terms using the decay of
|h|
. The replacement produces free surface terms
|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{r}}(L_{R_{n}}\Delta(L_{\infty}^{\prime}+he_{z}))|
for
r=R_{0},\frac{R_{0}-1}{2}
. We bound these by using Lemma
3.8
to ensure that
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{R_{n}}}
and
\Sigma^{f}_{L_{\infty}^{\prime}}
are within distance
C(R_{n}^{-1}+e^{-cR_{0}}+e^{-c(R_{n}-R_{0})})
of
\{z=F\}
; combining then with
|h|\leq CR_{0}^{1-d}
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial B_{R_{0}})=CR_{0}^{d-2}
, we conclude that the areas of the free surface terms are at most
CR_{0}^{-1}
, provided
n
is sufficiently large depending on
R_{0}
. For the change in gravity due to the shift, we estimate similarly to Lemma
7.3
, using that the change in volume due to the replacement is
O(1)
, while the gravitational potential
|z-F|
in the modified region is controlled by
C(|h|+R_{n}^{-1}+e^{-cR_{0}}+e^{-c(R_{n}-R_{0})})\leq CR_{0}^{1-d}
(again, for
n
sufficiently large depending on
R_{0}
).

Then from (
7.17
), it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{R_{0}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[L_{R_{n}};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\mathcal{E}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}]-\operatorname{Diss}(L_{R_{n}},L_{\infty}^{\prime})=0

This contradicts (
7.16
), so we conclude that
L_{\infty}
is globally stable.

Now, we check the last part of the statement, regarding convergence of differences of nonrelativized energies. For any
R_{0}\in(1,R_{n})
, we observe that

\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]

since the relative energy simply subtracts a constant depending only on
R_{n},R_{0}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\mathcal{E}_{R_{n}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lim_{R_{0}\to\infty}\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}};F]-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{R_{n},R_{0}}[L_{R_{n}}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty};F]-\mathcal{E}_{\infty}[L_{\infty}^{\prime};F^{\prime}]

The
\liminf
is bounded below in the same manner and by the same value, so the limit in
n
exists.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
L_{R}(t)
be an energy solution in
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}
for forcing
F(t)
. Then it holds

\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L_{R}(t);[0,T])\leq C(1+\|\dot{F}\|_{L^{1}[0,T]})

for a constant
C
independent of
L,R,F
.

Proof.

By the energy dissipation inequality, we have

\overline{\operatorname{Diss}}(L(t);[0,T])\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(0);F(0)]-\mathcal{E}_{R}[L(T);F(T)]+\int_{0}^{T}P^{*}_{R}[L(t);F(t)]\dot{F}(t)dt

Using Lemma
7.3
, we take
R_{0}=2
and bound the energy difference outside
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{0}}
by a constant. Inside, we use the local perimeter bounds implied by Lemma
4.2
and the height bound from Lemma
3.8
to bound the energy. Similarly,
P^{*}_{R}[L(t);F(t)]
is bounded independently of
R
by combining the height and local perimeter bounds. Thus, we conclude that the entire right side is bounded as
C(1+\|\dot{F}\|_{L^{1}})
.
∎

Corollary 7.6
.

If
L_{R_{n}}(t)
are energy solutions in
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R_{n}}
for forcing
F(t)
, with
R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, then there exists a subsequence
(R_{n_{k}})
and an energy solution
L_{\infty}(t)
in
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{\infty}
such that
|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{R_{n_{k}}}(t)}\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}(t)}|\to 0
pointwise in
t
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

By the total dissipation bound in Lemma
7.5
, the height bound in Lemma
3.8
, and the perimeter bound in Theorem
4.3
, we have the compactness to apply Helly’s selection principle as in Lemma
5.2
for the
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}(t)}
. Without relabeling, we pass to a subsequence and identify a limit
\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}(t)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\Sigma^{p}_{L_{n}(t)}\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}|=0\hbox{ for all }t\in[0,T].

Next, we select the limit profiles
L_{\infty}(t)
for which
\Sigma^{p}_{L_{\infty}(t)}=\Sigma^{p}_{\infty}(t)
. As discussed in Remark
5.3
, the profiles need not converge in an evolutionary sense, so we instead apply Lemma
5.5
to extract a measurable selection of subsequential limits which satisfy the properties of an energy solution. Thus, we must verify the six assumptions of this lemma.

The assumption
(i)
that the
R_{n}
converge is a hypothesis, while the forcing assumption
(ii)
is trivially satisfied since we do not vary
F
with
n
. Our use of Helly’s selection principle guarantees the dissipation bound and convergence of contact sets for assumption
(iii)
. The compactness of the profiles, closure of global stability, and convergence of the relative energy needed for
(iv)
and
(v)
has been verified in Lemma
7.4
. Finally, for assumption
(vi)
on the pressure-forcing term, we take
Q_{n}(t)=P^{*}_{R_{n}}[L_{n}(t);F(t)]\dot{F}(t)
. Note that
Q_{n}
is uniformly bounded from the proof in Lemma
7.5
, and
Q_{n}
converges given convergence of profiles by Lemma
4.6
. Therefore, we satisfiy all assumptions needed to conclude the result.
∎

]
*Proposition 5.4ii, only relies on Lemma

as in Corollary

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}[\widetilde{L}_{h};F]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{h};F]+\mathcal{C}_{F}[L_{h}]\int_{\Sigma^{f}_{L_{h}}}\nu^{f}\cdot D\mathcal{V}_{0}\nu^{f}+g(z-F)\mathcal{V}_{0}\cdot\nu^{f}\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

We now plug this into the global stability inequality, and expand

, using that

is defined in terms of set operations on

. This yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[\widetilde{L}_{h};F]+\operatorname{Diss}(L,\widetilde{L}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L_{h};F]+\operatorname{Diss}(L,L_{h})+Ca(h)(1+h)(1-a^{\prime}(h))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[L;F]+Ca(h)(1+h)(1-a^{\prime}(h))

\displaystyle\quad\dots+|S_{h}|-|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap\{|z|>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots-g(h-F)\operatorname{vol}(L\cap\{z>h\})

\displaystyle\quad\dots-g(h+F)\operatorname{vol}(\{-z>h\}\setminus L)

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{-})|\Sigma^{p}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{+})|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}-\mu_{-})|\Sigma^{o}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(-\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}+\mu_{+})|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{o}_{L}|

As a first step to deal with the proliferation of boundary terms on the right side, we use the isoperimetric inequality. Note that

\displaystyle|\partial(L\Delta L_{h})|

\displaystyle=|S_{h}|+|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap\{|z|>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+|\Sigma^{p}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|+|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+|\Sigma^{o}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|+|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{o}_{L}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\operatorname{vol}(L\Delta L_{h})^{(d-1)/d}

Let
\varepsilon>0
to be determined later. We add the nonnegative quantity
\varepsilon(|\partial(L\Delta L_{h})|-\frac{1}{C}\operatorname{vol}(L\Delta L_{h})^{(d-1)/d})
to the right-hand side of the global stability inequality to obtain:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq Ca(h)(1+h)(1-a^{\prime}(h))-\frac{\varepsilon}{C}a(h)^{(d-1)/d}

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(1+\varepsilon)|S_{h}|-(1-\varepsilon)|\Sigma^{f}_{L}\cap\{|z|>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots-g(h-F)\operatorname{vol}(L\cap\{z>h\})

\displaystyle\quad\dots-g(h+F)\operatorname{vol}(\{-z>h\}\setminus L)

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{-}+\varepsilon)|\Sigma^{p}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(-\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{+}+\varepsilon)|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{p}_{L}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}-\mu_{-}+\varepsilon)|\Sigma^{o}_{L}\cap\{z>h\}|

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(-\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}+\mu_{+}+\varepsilon)|\{-z>h\}\setminus\Sigma^{o}_{L}|

Note that again
|S_{h}|=-a^{\prime}(h)
for a.e.
h
, so we can combine
|S_{h}|
with the first term.

Next, we use Lemma
2.1
to bound the four contact surface terms and absorb the result into the gravity and free surface coefficients. To ensure the sign of the free surface coeffcient does not change, we require that each of the coefficients
\cos\theta^{p}_{Y}+\mu_{-}+\varepsilon
and so on to be strictly smaller than
1-\varepsilon
, which we can guarantee by choosing
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.
In the application of the lemma, we of course must choose the parameter
r
in the lemma sufficiently small that the inner and outer contact surface estimates do not interact. To ensure the sign of both gravity coefficients are negative, we require that
h
is sufficiently large to absorb the constant from the lemma. We apply this, and discard the free surface and gravity terms with negative coefficients, obtaining as a result:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-(1+\varepsilon)a(h)+Ca(h)(1+h)(1-a^{\prime}(h))-\frac{\varepsilon}{C}a(h)^{(d-1)/d}

which we rearrange to write as

((1+\varepsilon)+Ca(h)(1+h))a^{\prime}(h)\leq-Ca(h)(1+h)-\frac{\varepsilon}{C}a(h)^{(d-1)/d}

Recall that by definition
a
is decreasing in
h
, so
a^{\prime}
is nonpositive and all other variables are nonnegative. Since
L
has finite energy, and Lemma
2.3
ensures that energy controls gravity up to an additive constant, it follows that
(1+h)a(h)\to 0
as
h\to\infty
. Thus, by choosing
h
sufficiently large and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we may assume that the coefficient of
a^{\prime}
is at most
2
and absorb the first term on the right into the second, giving

a^{\prime}\leq-\frac{1}{C}a(h)^{(d-1)/d}

Finally, we observe that solutions of this differential inequality with positive initial data become 0 in after finite time, and conclude that
\Sigma^{f}_{L}
is vertically bounded.
∎

Appendix B
Sliding comparison for general viscosity solutions

In this appendix we recall a comparison principle for level set viscosity solutions of the mean curvature flow with capillary boundary conditions due Barles
[
3
]
and independently Ishii and Sato
[
15
]
. We then explain how to translate that result to our setting.

Comparison for curvature flows with capillary condition

In
[
3
,
15
]
they formulate the mean curvature flow with capillary contact angle condition in the level set form. For our purposes we just consider a level set PDE related, as we will explain below, to (
3.1
). Let
\Omega
be a
C^{1}
bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for us
n=d+1
. Split
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{N}\cup\Gamma_{D}
into two disjoint pieces, the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
and the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
. We will take
\Gamma_{N}
to be relatively open in
\partial\Omega
and
\Gamma_{D}
to be relatively closed. Then we will be considering level set PDE in the class

(B.1)

u_{t}-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla u\otimes\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{2}})D^{2}u\right)+h(x,t)|\nabla u|=0\ \hbox{ in }\ \Omega

with a continuous forcing
h
and the capillary boundary condition

(B.2)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\nabla u|\ \hbox{ on }\ \Gamma_{N}.

Lemma B.1

(
[
15
]
*Theorem 2.1, comparison principle for singular degenerate parabolic equations)
.

If
u
and
v
are respectively an upper semicontinuous subsolution and lower semicontinuous supersolution of (
B.1
) and (
B.2
) on
(0,T)\times\Omega
with
u(x,0)\leq v(x,0)
on
\overline{\Omega}
and
u(x,t)\leq v(x,t)
on
\Gamma_{D}\times[0,T]
then
u(x,t)\leq v(x,t)
on
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T)
.

Note that the Dirichlet boundary is not present in
[
15
]
*Theorem 2.1, but it is standard and does not add any difficulty to the proof there, since a positive maximum of
u-v
on the Dirichlet boundary can be immediately ruled out by the assumed boundary ordering.

We recall the standard definition of viscosity subsolution of (
B.1
): whenever
\varphi
a smooth test function touches
u
from above at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega\times(0,T)
then

\left[\varphi_{t}-\textup{tr}^{*}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)+h(x,t)|\nabla\varphi|\right](x_{0},t_{0})\leq 0

where we adopted the shorthand notation

\textup{tr}^{*}\left((I-\frac{p\otimes p}{|p|^{2}})M\right):=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\to p\\
q\neq 0\end{subarray}}\textup{tr}^{*}\left((I-\frac{q\otimes q}{|q|^{2}})M\right)

The supersolutions are defined analogously with
\textup{tr}_{*}
. See
[
7
]
*Section 9 for further discussion about the case of touching with degenerate slope.

Viscosity property for sliding level sets

Now we explain that viscosity solutions of (
3.2
), in the sense of Definitions
3.2
and
3.3
, can be interpreted in level-set form. We also add in a sliding factor to take advantage of the parabolic comparison principle Lemma
B.1
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
f
be a smooth increasing function of
t
, and
L
be a subsolution of (
3.2
). Then the sliding level set function

u(t,x,z):={\bf 1}_{L^{*}}(x,z-f(t))

is a subsolution on
\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\times(0,T]
of

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla u\otimes\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{2}})D^{2}u\right)+g\big(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L}+gf(t)+f^{\prime}(t)}{g}\big)|\nabla u|\leq 0&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d}_{R}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\cos\theta^{o}_{Y}|\nabla u|&\hbox{on }\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}\end{cases}

Similarly, for
f
decreasing and
L
a supersolution,
\mathbf{1}_{L_{*}}(x,z-f(t))
is a supersolution.

Proof.

We verify the claim for the subsolution property. Suppose
\varphi
is a smooth test function, such that
u-\varphi
has a strict maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
. Then
\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}-\varphi(t,x,z+f(t))
has the same at
(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))
, and we may split into four cases, depending on whether
(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))\in\partial L^{*}
and whether
(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))\in\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}
.

Starting with the trivial case
(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))\notin\partial L^{*}\cup\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}
, at
(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0})
we have

\partial_{t}\varphi=f^{\prime}\partial_{z}\varphi,\quad\nabla\varphi=0,\quad D^{2}\varphi\geq 0

The curvature term in (
B.1
) is interpreted in the sense of upper semicontinuous envelopes, as

\sup_{|\nu|=1}\mathrm{tr}\left((I-\nu\otimes\nu)D^{2}\varphi\right)

Therefore, we directly obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\mathrm{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)+g(z_{0}-f(t_{0})-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L}}{g})|\nabla\varphi|

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0

In the trivial boundary case,
(x_{0},z_{0})\in\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}\setminus\partial L^{*}
, we similarly have that for
\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}-\varphi(t,x,z-f(t))
to have a boundary maximum, we must have

\partial_{r}\varphi\leq 0,\quad\nabla_{\mathrm{Cyl}^{d-1}_{R}}\varphi=0,\quad\partial_{t}\varphi=0

Hence, from
\cos\alpha\in(-1,1)
we conclude that

\partial_{r}\varphi\leq\cos\alpha|\nabla\varphi|=\cos\alpha|\partial_{r}\varphi|

Proceeding to the nontrivial interior case
(x_{0},z_{0})\in\partial(L^{*}+f(t_{0})e_{z})
, we assume that
\nabla\varphi(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0})\neq 0
. Letting
\Phi:=\{(x,z-f(t_{0})):\varphi(t_{0},x,z)\geq 1\}
, we note that
\Phi
has a smooth parametrization near
(x_{0},z_{0})
, and can be redefined elsewhere if necessary to be a smooth test region which touches
L^{*}
from outside at
(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))
. Applying Definition
3.2
, we obtain that either
-H_{\Phi}(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))+g(z_{0}-f(t_{0})-F)\leq\lambda_{L}
or
(x_{0},z_{0})\in\gamma_{\Phi}
and
\theta_{\gamma_{\Phi}}(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))\geq\alpha
.

We recall that the outward normal to
\Phi
is
-\frac{\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|}
, and with our sign convention the mean curvature is negative where
\Phi
is convex (e.g. where
\varphi
is concave). Therefore,

\displaystyle-H_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(-\tfrac{\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|})

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{|\nabla\varphi|}\left(I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}\right):D^{2}\varphi

from which we conclude that in the first case, we have at
(x_{0},z_{0})
that

-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)+g(z_{0}-f(t_{0})-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L}}{g})|\nabla\varphi|\leq 0

Since
\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}(x,z)-\varphi(t,x,z-f(t))
has a local maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0})
, we also obtain that
\partial_{t}\varphi=f^{\prime}\partial_{z}\varphi
at
(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0})
. Bounding
\partial_{z}\varphi\leq|\nabla\varphi|
, we conclude that

\partial_{t}\varphi-\textup{tr}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)+g(z_{0}-f(t_{0})-F-\tfrac{\lambda_{L}}{g})|\nabla\varphi|\leq f^{\prime}(t_{0})|\nabla\varphi|

In the second case, we can compute the contact angle with our sign convention as

\cos\theta_{\gamma_{\Phi}}(x_{0},z_{0}-f(t_{0}))=-(-\tfrac{\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|}\cdot\tfrac{x_{0}}{|x_{0}|})\leq\cos\alpha

Therefore, we have the boundary inequality

\partial_{r}\varphi(x_{0},z_{0})\leq|\nabla\varphi(x_{0},z_{0})|\cos\alpha

where
r
is the cylindrical radius coordinate.

The degenerate case
\nabla\varphi(t_{0},x_{0},z_{0})=0
can be handled by a standard tilting argument, since we only need to check, for interior touching,

\left[-\textup{tr}^{*}\left((I-\frac{\nabla\varphi\otimes\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}})D^{2}\varphi\right)+g(z-F-\tfrac{\lambda}{g})|\nabla\varphi|\right]_{(x_{0},z_{0})}\leq 0,

and a similar condition for boundary touching. Thus, we conclude.

Appendix C
Some measure theory results

Lemma C.1
.

Let
Q_{n}
be a sequence of functions on a finite-measure set
I
which are dominated by an integrable function
\hat{Q}
. If

\int_{I}\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{I}Q_{n}

then the
Q_{n}
converge in measure as
n\to\infty
to
\limsup_{n}Q_{n}
.

Proof.

First note that

\lim_{N}\mathbf{1}_{\{Q_{N}>\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}}=0

pointwise in time for any
\varepsilon>0
by definition. Bounded convergence implies

|\{Q_{N}>\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}|\to 0

On the other hand, we have

Q_{N}+\varepsilon\mathbf{1}_{\{Q_{N}<-\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}}\leq Q_{N}\vee\limsup_{n}Q_{n}

We integrate both sides in time and take the limsup in
N
, to get

\displaystyle\liminf_{N}

\displaystyle\int_{I}Q_{N}dt+\limsup_{N}\varepsilon|\{Q_{N}<-\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N}\left(\int_{I}Q_{N}dt+\varepsilon|\{Q_{N}<-\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N}\int_{I}Q_{N}\vee\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\,dt

(Reverse Fatou)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\limsup_{N}(Q_{N}\vee\limsup_{n}Q_{n})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\limsup_{N}Q_{N}dt

(Hypothesis)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{N}\int_{I}Q_{N}dt

The only step that requires justification is the reverse Fatou inequality. This holds since
Q_{N}\vee\limsup_{n}Q_{n}
is dominated by
\hat{Q}
(since
\hat{Q}
dominates the
Q_{N}
).

With this, we cancel
\liminf_{N}\int_{I}Q_{N}dt
from both sides of the inequality and conclude that

\limsup_{N}|\{Q_{N}<-\varepsilon+\limsup_{n}Q_{n}\}|=0

so the convergence in measure holds.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Let
Y
be a separable metric space (equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra),
T
a measurable space, and
L_{k}(t)
a sequence of measurable functions

. Let
\mathcal{M}:T\to 2^{Y}

.

Let

denote the set of subsequential limits

Proof.

Note that

We then have for any

\mathrm{Per}(\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(U);\Gamma):=\sup_{\varphi}\int_{\Gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(U)}\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\varphi\leq C(\Lambda_{0},\mu_{\pm},r_{0},s_{0})

where the supremum is taken over (tangent) vector fields on
\Gamma

\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\gamma_{U})=\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial^{*}_{\Gamma}\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(U))\leq C(\Lambda_{0},\mu_{\pm},r_{0},s_{0}).

Here,
\partial^{*}_{\Gamma}
is the reduced boundary operator on
\Gamma
, which selects boundary points where
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(U)
has a measure-theoretic normal inside
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Write the tubular coordinate functions as
Y(x)=\mathrm{argmin}_{y\in\Gamma}|x-y|
and
N(x)=|x-Y(x)|
. Set
U_{s}=Y(U\cap N^{-1}(\{s\}))
, the slice of
U
at distance
s
from
\Gamma
, projected to
\Gamma
. We identify
U_{0}
with
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma}(U)
.

The slices of a
BV
function with respect to a one-dimensional coordinate are
L^{1}
-continuous, so we have that
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}|U_{s}\Delta U_{0}|=0
. It follows by lower semicontinuity of perimeter, that we have

\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{0})\leq\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{s})

where we write
\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}
to emphasize that the perimeter of a subset of
\Gamma
is computed by testing against compactly supported tangent vector fields to
\Gamma
.

The coarea formula
[
17
]
*Theorem 18.8 gives for
s_{1}<s_{2}
that

\displaystyle\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{s})ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\partial^{*}U\cap N^{-1}(\{s\}))ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{\partial^{*}U\cap(\Gamma_{s_{2}}\setminus\Gamma_{s_{1}})}|\nabla^{\partial^{*}U}N|d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{Per}(U;\Gamma_{s_{2}}\setminus\Gamma_{s_{1}})

where here the implicit constant in the first line appears from estimating the Jacobian for the change of variables, and is modified in the last line from estimating
N
in
C^{1}
. Applying Lemma
D.2
, with a suitable covering argument, we have

\mathrm{Per}(U;\Gamma_{s_{2}})\leq Cs_{2}

By sending
s_{1}\to 0
, we conclude that for any
s_{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{s_{2}}\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{s})ds\leq C

and hence there exists
s\in(0,s_{2})
such that
\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{s})\leq C
. It follows that

\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{0})\leq\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\mathrm{Per}_{\Gamma}(U_{s})\leq C

∎

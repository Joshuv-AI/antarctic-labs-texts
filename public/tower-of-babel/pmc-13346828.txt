Axial power distribution control in pressurized water reactors based on flow rate regulation based on distributed parameters method
Authors: Qizhi Duan, Minghan Yang, Shuai Chen, Huijuan Li, Hongyun Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe control of axial power distribution is a key challenge in the operation of Pressurized Water Reactors (PWRs). Traditionally, this control has been achieved by adjusting the concentration of soluble boron and moving a select number of control rod banks. In this work, an innovative axial power distribution control method based on flow rate regulation is proposed. This method can reduce the dependence on boron concentration regulation and control rod banks movement, so that the treatment for lots of liquid waste can be avoided, and the structure of reactor cores may become more compact. Different with the lumped parameter system control method, this research introduces a unique PWR core exponential stability condition based on SDLMI from the distributed parameter control theory. Moreover, a distributed parameter state observer is established to provide the feedback of power density, delayed neutron precursor density and concentration which cannot be measured by sensors directly in PWRs. The numerical experiment shows that the axial power distribution control method based on flow rate regulation can inhibit the power density peak and eliminate hot spots efficiently.Keywords: Nuclear reactor, PWR, Power distribution control, SDLMISubject terms: Nuclear energy, Energy infrastructure, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Nuclear fission energy is a promising sustainable energy source, and capable of meeting more than 14% of the energy demand during the world1, where, the most of them is generated by Pressurized Water Reactors (PWRs). To meet the increasing demand of nuclear power plants (NPPs) operation, tremendous research efforts have been focused on the automatic control of reactor core power, because the reactor core is the key part as the hot source with the radioactive nuclear fuel, which possesses security risks and economic potential2. However, the neutron transportation, neutron-thermal coupling, and in PWRs can bring complex power control problems. Actually, the power distribution in the nuclear reactor is nonuniform3. As the power distribution changes, the reactivity of nuclear fission can be affected because of control rod banks movement, doppler effect, and poisoning4. If power distribution is left uncontrolled, the power density and heat flux at some points in fuel assemblies and coolant may exceed the permissible limit, result into increased risk of fuel failure and coolant departure from nucleate boiling (DNB)5. Hence, in PWRs, it becomes necessary to employ power distribution control systems.

In the power distribution control system of PWRs, the axial power distribution becomes the main controlled object6,7. Generally, early power distribution control systems were based on constant axial offset control strategies mainly, such as ‘Mode-A’, ‘Mode-G’ and ‘Mode-T’8. However, the introduction of regulating boron concentrations produced lots of liquid waste. Moreover, the low automation and high complexity of these strategies brought high requirement for operators, which may increase risk of core failure2.

For the purpose of power distribution control without adjustment of boron concentration, the control strategy ‘MSHIM’(Mechanical Shim) was presented by Westinghouse9,10. MSHIM is performed through control rod banks movement only, and maintains power peaking factors within the acceptable range. Under the framework of MSHIM, some advanced closed loop control methods were developed continuously in recent years. In these study of advanced control methods, the first question is that, how to develop a suitable control-oriented model11. Generally, the average power density of nuclear reactors is adequately represented by the point kinetic model. However, this model is insufficient to capture the shape of the power distribution. Hence, the multi-point kinetic model based on the spatial discretization method was proposed12–14. Essentially, based on the multi-point kinetic model, power distribution control can be classified as the control problem of multi-variable systems. Accordingly, some Lumped Parameters Control (LPC) methods were presented gradually, including adaptive control15, model predictive control16, sliding mode control17, LQG/LTR (Linear Quadratic Gaussian/Loop Transfer Recovery)18, fuzzy logic control19 and robust control20, which are also referred to as the “Reduce-Then-Design” (RTD) approach.

Although MSHIM increased the level of automation, it still needed a lot of space in reactor cores for control rod banks. for example, In AP1000, 9 control rod banks with relatively high worth are used for axial power distribution control, and move independently with DNBR and Peak Linear Heat Rate (PLHR)21. Obviously, the use of fewer control rod banks can reduce more construction, operation and maintenance costs of PWRs, and make the structure more compact. Moreover, As the theoretical basis of MSHIM, LPC method based on lots of control rod banks may also need more complex multi-variables control design process. Therefore, it is necessary to develop an axial power control method that can reduce the dependence on boron concentration regulation and control rod banks movement.

It is well-known that the temperature Doppler effect in nuclear reactors can affect the absorption cross section significantly.Given that current Pressurized Water Reactors (PWRs) generally display a negative temperature feedback coefficient, adjusting the absorption cross-section distribution based on coolant temperature distribution allows for axial power flattening. Similarly, because coolant flow rate can exhibit a significant moderating effect on temperature and can be modulated by the main pump without requiring additional actuators, it is physically feasible to control axial power distribution in PWRs via coolant flow rate adjustment. To this end, from a control theory viewpoint, establishing a flow rate controller that guarantees the asymptotic stability of the axial power distribution control system becomes crucial.

Considering the distributed parameters characteristic of coolant temperature, the control oriented model for axial power distribution control based on flow rate adjustment is suitable to be expressed by Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) than Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) which is often used for LPC method. Accordingly, distributed parameters control (PDC) method, which allows designers to take advantage of all natural features and understand the system structure much more completely, will be used for the control system analysis. During recent decades, the DPC method based on PDEs were gradually studied, including the linear-quadratic optimal control based on the spectral factorization method22; the sliding model control for nonlinear chemical processes23; the geometric control for the flow system24; the stabilization method based on pseudo-spectral25; the robust sampled-data control26. In the previous work, we developed a DPC method for axial power distribution control based on rotating drums in fast nuclear reactors27. However, besides on the difference of actuators, axial power distribution control in PWRs still needs to be developed further for the following reasons in this work: first, as a kind of thermal nuclear reactors, PWRs has complex kinetics, which may affect the stability of the axial power distribution control system; second, as control feedbacks, distributions of power density, delay neutron precursor density, concentration and concentration cannot be measured directly, which was not discussed in previous work.

The control of axial power distribution in pressurized water reactors (PWRs) via primary coolant flow rate adjustment is an area of ongoing research. While previous works, such as, have demonstrated this concept in a lumped parameter system setting, this paper introduces a fundamentally different approach grounded in distributed parameter system (DPS) theory. The core novelty of this work is the development of a complete DPC framework. Specifically, the contributions include: (1) a control law derived directly from a distributed parameter model of the reactor core; (2) a novel asymptotic distributed parameter state observer that provides essential feedback for the controller based on measurable states (e.g., fuel assembly and coolant temperatures); and (3) a rigorous stability analysis for the resulting closed-loop system using spatial differential linear matrix inequalities (SDLMIs), which guarantees system performance.

Verification

Reference design and experimental setup

In this work, the structure size, cross sections, and thermal-hydraulic parameters of Chinese Pressurized Water Reactor (CPR1000) was used as the reference involved control system verification. CPR1000, built and operated by China General Nuclear Power Group, is one of the main reactor types in China38. In the original design, it employs two control rod banks to obtain fast and accurate reactor power automatic control, and also requires manually changing the soluble boron concentration to compensate the reactivity during load-follow maneuvers.

For verifying the effect of the DPC method presented in this work, it is assumed that the flow rate of primary loop can be regulated automatically, meanwhile the regulation of boron concentration and control rod banks displacement are cut off. The purpose of this is to test whether the flow rate adjustment based on DPC method can independently flatten the power distribution and suppress the appearance of hot spots. Conclusion

Axial power distribution control is one of the critical problem for PWRs operation. If the power distribution is left uncontrolled, the power density, and heat flux at some points in fuel assemblies and coolant may exceed the permissible limit, result into the increased risks of fault and failure. In the most of PWRs, the axial power distribution control often depends on boron concentration regulation and control rod banks movement. while, The introduction of regulating boron concentrations produced lots of liquid waste, and the high complexity of these strategies brought high requirement for operators. Meanwhile, control rod banks dedicated to axial power distribution control also increase the structural complexity of reactor core.

In this study, we propose an alternative approach for controlling axial power distribution in PWRs through flow rate regulation. This approach offers the advantage of using existing nuclear reactor equipment and does not necessitate supplementary dedicated actuators, thereby avoiding increased structural complexity. Based on the DPC method, the primary pump delivers satisfactory control performance due to the continuous Doppler effect of temperatures in the axial direction.

During the process of flow rate regulation based on DPC method, two key problems need to be discussed further. Firstly, an asymptotic distributed parameter observer is built for provide state feedback, because the state variables , , and can not be measured directly. For this purpose, we designed an asymptotically distributed parameters stable observer. Secondary, The stability of axial power control system based on flow rate regulation with the observer needs to be proved Under the framework of DPC theory. According to the Lyapunov function with axial integral form, we derive SDLMIs that can ensure the system’s exponential stability through the integration by parts.

For verifying the effectiveness of the axial power control system based on flow rate regulation, a uncertain disturbance, inadvertent control rod withdrawal, is inserted in a numerical experiment based on a custom simulator developed for this study. The simulation result shows that the asymptotic distributed parameter observer can reconstruct the axial distribution of system states, And, on this basis, the axial power distribution is quickly flattened in a short time. By comparing with the response without any control action, we can also find some advantages of the DPC method for axial power control. Firstly, and have the same distribution because is is almost flattened, which means local overheating is difficult to occur in fuel assemblies; Then, as the power density is quickly flattened, the fluctuation range of can be greatly reduced, which can ensure the safety of the reactor core; The distribution of can also be fattened rapidly due to the fast convergence of , and , which means that the risk of xenon oscillations is reduced. Finally, we briefly discussed responses of coolant flow rate and core outlet temperature under some extreme disturbances. The results show that during the process of DPC suppressing extreme disturbances, the pressure level of the coolant must be well maintained. In summary, the preliminary analysis and calculation results in this work show that, the flow rate regulation based on DPC method can theoretically be a feasible scheme for axial power distribution control in PWRs.

In the future, some problems on below need to be studied further:

(i)Potential of Flow Rate RegulationDespite the feasibility of the flow rate regulation scheme based on the DPC method for axial power distribution control in PWRs, its potential has not been scrutinized in detail. Specifically, the capability of the flow regulation scheme to handle cross-sectional disturbances, its effectiveness in reducing the number of control rod banks to simplify reactor structure, and its capacity to lessen the use of the boron adjustment system thereby reducing waste liquid production remain unexplored.

Kitaev model in regular hyperbolic tilings
Julien Vidal, Rémy Mosseri

Abstract
We study the Kitaev model on regular hyperbolic trivalent tilings. Depending on the length p p of the elementary polygons, we examine two distinct tri-colorings of the tiling. Using a recent conjecture on the ground-state flux sector, we compute the phase diagram via exact diagonalizations and derive analytical expressions for the effective Hamiltonians in the isolated-dimer limit which are valid for all values of p p . Our results interpolates between the Euclidean honeycomb lattice and the trivalent Bethe lattice ( p = ∞ p=\infty ) for which we derive the exact solution of the phase boundaries.



I
Introduction

Following the discovery of the fractional quantum Hall effect
Tsui et al. 1982
;
Stormer et al. 1999
, much attention has been paid to two-dimensional systems hosting anyons
Leinaas and Myrheim 1977
;
Wilczek 1982a
;
Wilczek 1982b
;
Goldin et al. 1985
. These collective excitations are known to obey nontrivial braiding statistics which have been probed experimentally in recent years
Bartolomei et al. 2020
;
Nakamura et al. 2020
. Classifying and understanding anyonic (topological) quantum phases of matter is one of the most challenging problems in condensed matter physics
Wen 2017
, notably because of their potential use in topological quantum computation
Nayak et al. 2008
.

An important step has been achieved in this direction by Kitaev
Kitaev 2006
who proposed an exactly solvable spin model and the celebrated sixteenfold way which describes all possible phases of Majorana fermions coupled to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field. A remarkable feature of this model is its ability to generate both Abelian (toric code) and non-Abelian (Ising) anyons when varying the Hamiltonian parameters.
Many variations of this model have been proposed by changing, e.g., the geometry, the space dimension, the Hilbert space dimension, the interactions…(see, e.g., Refs.
Trebst and Hickey 2022
;
Matsuda et al.
for recent reviews). However, to ensure the exact solvability of this model based on a free Majorana-fermion representation of the Hamiltonian, one important constraint is to consider trivalent structures.

Recently, a new extension of the Kitaev model has emerged by considering tilings of the hyperbolic plane, i.e., a space with constant negative curvature. Latest works on trivalent tilings made of
7
-gons
Mosseri et al. 2025
,
8
-gons
Lenggenhager et al.
, and
9
-gons
Dusel et al.
motivated us to explore the Kitaev model on any regular hyperbolic tilings
\{p,3\}
, i.e., on regular tesselations of the hyperbolic plane by a trivalent tiling made of
p
-gons. The honeycomb lattice
(p=6)
corresponds to the limiting Euclidean (zero curvature) case
Kitaev 2006
whereas tilings with
p<6
(not considered here) correspond to trivalent Platonic solids [tetrahedron
(p=3)
, cube
(p=4)
, and dodecahedron
(p=5)
]. Our main goal is to connect these recent results
Mosseri et al. 2025
;
Lenggenhager et al.
;
Dusel et al.
, with the
p=\infty
limit, which corresponds to the trivalent Bethe lattice
Mosseri and Sadoc 1982
. As we will see, this limit can be reached in various ways depending on the coloring considered. Using numerical exact diagonalizations (ED) of the Hamiltonian, we compute the phase diagram of the Kitaev model for finite values of
p
(up to 16). We also derive the effective low-energy Hamiltonian for arbitrary
p
in the isolated-dimer limit, in which the system is gapped and in a toric code phase. Finally, we present the exact phase diagram for the Bethe lattice by revisiting (and correcting) the results given in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
.

This paper is organized as follows: in Sec.
II
, we introduce the Kitaev model and the two colorings (coupling patterns) considered in this study, namely, the Kekulé coloring and the Kitaev coloring. The former allows us to study
\{p,3\}
tilings with
p\mod 2=0
and whereas the latter is only relevant for when
p\mod 3=0
. Section
III
discusses the conjecture proposed in Refs.
Lieb and Loss 1993
;
G. Cassella and P. d’Ornellas and T. Hodson and W.
M. H. Natori and J. Knolle 2023
that determines the ground-state sector, and Sec.
IV
provides some remarks about the gauge choice and Aharonov-Bohm (AB) fluxes. Section
V
is dedicated to the numerical study of the fermion gap for the aforementioned families of tilings. In Sec.
VI
, we use the formalism given in Ref.
Petrova et al. 2014
to derive the low-energy effective Hamiltonians in the isolated-dimer limit allowing for a direct calculation of the vison gap in this limit.
The
p=\infty

where we compute the exact phase diagram within the full parameter range. We conclude and provide some perspectives in Sec.
VIII
. Technical details are given in Appendix
A

In Appendix
B
, we present an alternative approach towards the Bethe lattice limit by examining the Kitaev model on certain polygonal tree structures. These tilings are related to the Hecke group Cayley graphs and allow for an exact solution that matches that of the Bethe lattice one in the case of infinite-length polygons.

II
Model

For any three-edge colorable tiling embedded on an orientable surface, the Kitaev model Hamiltonian is defined as

H=-\sum_{(j,k)}J_{\alpha}\,\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}\sigma_{k}^{\alpha},

(1)

where the
\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}
’s are the Pauli matrices acting on the spin located at site
i
. In Eq. (
1
), the sum runs over all links
(j,k)
of the tiling, and the type
\alpha=x,y,
or
z
is given by the corresponding edge color. For any plaquette
n
, the operator

W_{n}=\prod_{(j,k)\in{\partial n}}\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}\sigma_{k}^{\alpha},

(2)

is a conserved quantity, i.e.,
[H,W_{n}]=0

Kitaev 2006
. Here, the product runs over all links
(j,k)
of the oriented
plaquette

n
(e.g., clockwise). Additionally,
[W_{n},W_{n}^{\prime}]=0
, which allows one to block-diagonalize the Hamiltonian according to each map of the
W_{n}
’s.

Figure 1:
A piece of the
\{8,3\}
tiling in the Poincaré disk conformal representation with Kekulé coloring, in which each polygon is only made up of two colors. The red, green, and blue links are associated with
J_{x}
,
J_{y}
, and
J_{z}
couplings, respectively.

For a given configuration of the
W_{n}
’s, the Hamiltonian (
1
) can be mapped onto a quadratic Majorana fermion Hamiltonian

H=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}c_{j}c_{k},

(3)

where the sum runs over all sites of the tiling, and where
c_{j}
is a Majorana operator acting on site
j
. As explained by Kitaev, this mapping leads to an extended Hilbert space and a projection onto the physical subspace must be performed
Kitaev 2006
.
The matrix
A
is a real skew-symmetric matrix and its elements
A_{jk}=2\,J_{\alpha}\,u_{jk}
depend on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
variables
u_{jk}=-u_{kj}=\pm 1
, which are defined on each link of the lattice
Kitaev 2006
. In this language, the eigenvalue of the operator
W_{n}
is given by

w_{n}=(-{\rm i})^{p}\prod_{(j,k)\in{\partial n}}u_{jk},

(4)

where
p
is the number of edges of the plaquette
n
, and the product again runs over all links
(j,k)
of the oriented plaquette
n

Kitaev 2006
;
Petrova et al. 2014
.
Thus, for each configuration of the gauge variables
u_{jk}
’s, the problem reduces to a quadratic Majorana fermion Hamiltonian. Note that on a closed surface, one always has
\prod_{n}W_{n}=\mathds{1}
, or equivalently
\prod_{n}w_{n}=1
.
Importantly, the Hamiltonian (
1
) is time-reversal symmetric. However, if the tiling contains plaquettes of odd length, time-reversal symmetry (TRS) is spontaneously broken
Kitaev 2006
and the ground state is doubly degenerate (Kramers pairs)
Kramers 1930
. This phenomenon was first analyzed in the decorated honeycomb lattice
Yao and Kivelson 2007
;
Dusuel et al. 2008
.

The properties of the Kitaev model for a given tiling strongly depends on the coloring considered (see Ref.
Kam
for a discussion in the honeycomb lattice). In particular, when
p\mod 2=0
, one can use a “Kekulé coloring” (see Fig.
1
) where each
p
-gon has only two types of links (see Refs.
Kam
for
p=6
, and Ref.
Lenggenhager et al.
for
p=8
). When
p\mod 3=0
, one can use the “Kitaev coloring”
Kitaev 2006
(see Fig.
2
) which consists of alternating
x
,
y
, and
z
couplings around each
p
-gon. This coloring was recently analyzed in the
\{9,3\}
tiling
Dusel et al.
.
In the following, we will focus on these two families of tilings, studying how, when
p
goes to infinity, the corresponding phase diagrams converge to that of the trivalent Bethe lattice (see Fig.
3
) for which the coloring is irrelevant. Note that other values of
p
may be of interest, e.g.,
p=7

Mosseri et al. 2025
but they do not allow for either of the two colorings mentioned above. Additionally, when periodic boundary conditions are considered (closed surface), frustration effects may occur preventing the aforementioned coloring from being realized.

III
Ground-state sector(s)

Among all possible gauge choices, the ground-state manifold has recently been conjectured to be obtained when

w_{n}^{{\rm g.s.}}=-(\pm{\rm i})^{p},

(5)

for each elementary plaquette
n
with
p
edges, provided the system is not too small
G. Cassella and P. d’Ornellas and T. Hodson and W.
M. H. Natori and J. Knolle 2023
. First proposed by Lieb and Loss
Lieb and Loss 1993
, this conjecture is in agreement with recent works on disordered tilings systems (see also Ref.
Grushin and Repellin 2023
), and is supposed to hold for any coloring and nonnegative couplings
J_{\alpha}
. As an aside, let us stress that when one of the couplings dominates (e.g., when
J_{z}\gg J_{x},J_{y}
), the perturbative results derived in Ref.
Petrova et al. 2014
corroborate this conjecture.

For even
p
, the ground-state configuration is thus given by
w_{n}^{{\rm g.s.}}=+1
when
p\mod 4=2
, and by
w_{n}^{{\rm g.s.}}=-1
when
p\mod 4=0
. For odd
p
, this conjecture predicts two ground-state sectors (Kramers pair
Kramers 1930
) associated with
w_{n}^{{\rm g.s.}}=\pm{\rm i}
) reminiscent of the spontaneous TRS breaking expected in this case (see the above discussion).

In the trivalent Bethe lattice (infinite-
p
limit) where loops are absent, it is important to underline that all gauge configurations and all colorings lead to the same spectrum. This issue was originally addressed in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
but, as we will see below, some solutions were overlooked.

.

IV
Closed surfaces and Aharonov-Bohm fluxes

To determine the phase diagram of the Kitaev model on a given
\{p,3\}
tiling, we performed exact diagonalizations (ED) of the Hamiltonian (
3
) in the ground-state sector(s) given by the condition (
5
). To limit finite-size effects effects, for a fixed
p
, we considered the cluster with the largest number of exact moments of its adjacency matrix (as compared to the infinite tiling)
R. Mosseri and J. Vidal 2023
in the list of trivalent graphs with up to
N_{\rm v}=10000
vertices (see Ref.
Conder
). Each cluster corresponds to a closed surface (with periodic boundary conditions) of genus
g=1+\frac{p-6}{4p}N_{\rm v}
so that no boundary effects are present.
Consequently, the results depend not only on the flux
\phi_{n}
in each plaquette (
w_{n}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi_{n}}
) but also on the AB fluxes in each noncontractible loop. Once these quantities are fixed, the spectrum of the original spin Hamiltonian is ultimately found by selecting the physical states from the extended Majorana fermion Hamiltonian. Moreover, this spectrum is also sensitive to finite-size effects which must be analyzed carefully (see Refs.
Stegmaier et al. 2022
;
Mosseri et al. 2025
for related discussions).

For a given tiling on a closed surface, the exact fermion gap
\Delta_{\rm f}
defined as the smallest nonnegative (physical) eigenvalue of the matrix
{\rm i}A
, is obtained by minimizing over all possible AB fluxes. In practice, computing the fermion gap is impossible because of the large number AB fluxes to be sampled (
g
grows as
N_{\rm v}
). Therefore, for a given gauge choice [fixing both local (
w_{n}
) and nonlocal (AB) fluxes], one can only obtain an upper bound for the fermion gap.

V
Phase diagrams

V.1
Kekulé-colored tilings
(p\mod 2=0)

As explained in Sec.
II
, when
p\mod 2=0
, one can used the Kekulé coloring to three-color the corresponding
\{p,3\}
tiling. For
p=6
, the phase diagram was exactly computed in Ref.
Kam
consisting of a single gapless point found for
J_{x}=J_{y}=J_{z}
. For any other values of the
J_{\alpha}
’s, the system is gapped. The phase diagram for
p=8
has been analyzed in Ref.
Lenggenhager et al.
and the authors conclude that the phase diagram may be the same as for
p=6
using various approaches. Given that when
p
goes infinity, one should recover the diagram of the trivalent Bethe lattice (see Sec.
VII
), this result seems a bit surprising. Therefore, we revisited this issue by means of ED and we observed a small gapless phase near the isotropic point. This is most evident plotting the gap along the line parametrized by
J_{x}+J_{y}+J_{z}=1
and
J_{x}=J_{y}
(see the dashed line in Fig.
4
) where the gap clearly vanishes in a finite region. As mentioned earlier, the boundaries of this gapless phase are a bit imprecise (about a few percents) because we cannot sample all possible AB fluxes. However, for any fixed gauge choice, we always observe a finite gapless phase. As
p
increases, the gapless phase grows and converges to that of the Bethe lattice (see Fig.
4
and Sec.
VII
).

When one of the couplings vanishes (e.g.,
J_{z}=0
), the system consists of a set of isolated
p
-gons made of of alternating couplings (
J_{x},J_{y}
). When
J_{x}=J_{y}=1/2
, the fermion gap is then given by

\Delta_{\rm f}=\min\{|2\cos(2\pi j/p+\phi)|,j=0,\dots,p-1\}.

(6)

The phase shift (
\phi=\pi/p
, if
p\mod 4=0
, and
0
otherwise) comes from the flux constraint [see Eq. (
5
)]. As can be seen in Fig.
4
, this gap is always finite but as
p
approaches infinity
\Delta_{\rm f}=0
, which is in agreement with the Bethe lattice results discussed in Sec.
VII
.

VI
The isolated-dimer limit

To analyze the nature of the gapped phase, we adopt the original approach proposed by Kitaev and examine the isolated-dimer limit in which one of coupling dominates. In this limit, one can derive an effective low-energy Hamiltonian that describes weakly-coupled dimers. Using the results from Petrova
et al.

Petrova et al. 2014
for Kekulé colored tilings (
p\mod 2=0
) and assuming
J_{z}\gg J_{x},J_{y}
, one gets at leading order
p/2
:

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm const}+\frac{(-1)^{p/2}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{p-1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\>\Gamma\left(\tfrac{p}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{J_{z}^{p/2-1}}\times

(7)

\displaystyle{\Bigg(}J_{x}^{p/2}\sum_{n,(xz)}W_{n}+J_{y}^{p/2}\sum_{n,(yz)}W_{n}{\Bigg)},

where the first (second) sum runs over all plaquettes
n
made of
x
and
z
(
y
and
z
) links (see Fig.
1
). The standard Euler-Gamma function is denoted by
\Gamma
. It is easy to check that expression (
7
) reproduces the results obtained in Ref.
Kam
for
p=6
, and in Ref.
Lenggenhager et al.
for
p=8
.

For tilings with a Kitaev coloring (
p\mod 3=0
), one must distinguish between two cases depending on the parity of
p
. When
p
is even (
p\mod 6=0
), the effective Hamiltonian at leading order
2p/3
is given by:

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm const}+\frac{(-1)^{p/2}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{p-1}{2}\right)\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1+p/3}{2}\big)}{\pi\>\Gamma\left(\tfrac{2p}{3}\right)}\frac{J_{x}^{p/3}J_{y}^{p/3}}{J_{z}^{2p/3-1}}\times

(8)

\displaystyle{\Bigg(}\sum_{n}W_{n}{\Bigg)},

where the sum runs over all plaquettes
n
. For
p=6
, this expression matches the one found by Kitaev
Kitaev 2006
(see also Ref.
Vidal et al. 2008
for higher-order terms). By contrast, when
p
is odd (
p\mod 6=3
), one gets the following form at leading order
4p/3-2

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm const}+\frac{4\>\Gamma\left(p-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\Gamma\big(\tfrac{p}{3}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}{\pi\>\Gamma\left(\tfrac{4p}{3}-1\right)}\frac{J_{x}^{2p/3-2}J_{y}^{2p/3-2}}{J_{z}^{4p/3-3}}\times

(9)

\displaystyle{\Bigg(}J_{x}^{2}\sum_{(n,n^{\prime})_{y}}W_{n}W_{n^{\prime}}+J_{y}^{2}\sum_{(n,n^{\prime})_{x}}W_{n}W_{n^{\prime}}{\Bigg)},

where the first (second) sum runs over all pairs plaquettes
(n,n^{\prime})
sharing a
y
-link (
x
-link) (see Fig.
2
). For
p=9
, this result coincides with the one given in Ref.
Dusel et al.
, as expected. Notably, we observe that the leading-order terms involve two neighboring plaquettes. This is reminiscent from the spontaneous TRS breaking that occurs for odd
p
, where the two ground-state sectors correspond to uniform flux maps (
w_{n}=\pm{\rm i}
, for all
n
).

These effective Hamiltonians corroborate the conjecture given in Eq. (
5
). They also allow to straightforwardly compute the vison gap,
\Delta_{\rm v}
, obtained by exciting plaquettes (see Refs.
Mosseri et al. 2025
;
Dusel et al.
for recent discussions of this gap). Note that for
J_{x}=J_{y}=0
, the fermion gap, denoted by
\Delta_{\rm f}
, is obtained by exciting a
z
-dimer and is equal to
2J_{z}
.
The gapped phase adiabatically connected to the isolated-dimer limit has a Chern number
\nu=0
(see Ref.
Kitaev 2006
). According to the sixteenfold-way classification, this corresponds to a toric code phase
Kitaev 2006
.

VII
The trivalent Bethe lattice

As the size
p
of elementary plaquettes approaches infinity, the tiling becomes equivalent to the trivalent Bethe lattice. Each site is threefold coordinated and there are no closed loops of finite length in the system. In this limit, the spectrum of the Kitaev model is independent of the coloring and independent of the
u_{jk}
’s. In fact, the notion of
w_{n}
’s becomes irrelevant. This problem was originally studied in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
as a large-loop limit of a three-dimensional hyperhoneycomb lattice (see also Ref.
Mandal and Surendran 2009
).

To determine the phase boundaries of the gapless phase, we follow Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
(see Appendix D) and consider the following set of equations derived in Appendix
A
:

1=\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-J_{\alpha}^{2}r^{2}},

(10)

where
r=2\pi\rho(0)
[
\rho(E)
being the normalized density of states of the matrix
{\rm i}A/2
at energy
E
], and
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=\pm 1
. Actually, in Appendix D of Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
, the authors only consider the case
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
, for all
\alpha
, and overlooked the other cases which are relevant to determine the exact phase boundaries.
Indeed, let us seek for a solution of Eq. (
10
) with infinitesimal
r
. We can then expand Eq. (
10
) at small
r
and obtain:

1=\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{J_{\alpha}^{2}r^{2}}{2}\right)+O(r^{4}).

(11)

Determining the region where the gap vanishes is equivalent to finding the set of parameters
J_{\alpha}
for which
r=0
. As discussed in Appendix D of Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
, this never happens with the choice
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
, for all
\alpha
, but this is possible with other sign choices as we shall now discuss.

.

Now, let us assume that we selected, in Eq. (
11
), the solution corresponding to, e.g.,
\varepsilon_{x}=\varepsilon_{y}=-\varepsilon_{z}=+1
. Then, it is straightforward to see that Eq. (
11
) is satisfied when

J_{z}^{2}=J_{x}^{2}+J_{y}^{2}.

(12)

This equation and its counterparts which are obtained by permuting the indices, define the exact critical lines that separate the gapless phase (
r\neq 0
) from the gapped phase (
r=0
) (see Fig.
6
) for the Kitaev model on the trivalent Bethe lattice. Surprisingly, the corresponding phase diagram shown in Fig.
6
is exactly the same as the one the Kitaev model on the honeycomb lattice in the vortex-full sector
Pachos 2007
!

For completeness, we also determine the boundaries of the gapless phase obtained by considering only the solution of Eq. (
10
) with
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
, for all
\alpha
. Indeed, the corresponding solution only holds up to a set of parameters beyond which one must consider a solution where one of the
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
is negative. Equating the two corresponding Eq. (
10
) immediately leads to the following condition:

J_{z}^{2}=J_{x}^{2}+J_{y}^{2}-2\sqrt{(J_{z}^{2}-J_{x}^{2})(J_{z}^{2}-J_{y}^{2})}.

(13)

This is in agreement with the results found in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
(see Appendix D). The corresponding lines shown in Fig.
6
clearly illustrate the inability of the solution
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
, for all
\alpha
, to determine the exact boundaries of the gapless phase.

VIII
Conclusions

In this work, we examined the zero-temperature phase diagram of the Kitaev model defined on
\{p,3\}
hyperbolic tilings. For finite
p>6
, we computed the fermion gap using ED on finite-size clusters embedded on closed surfaces to avoid boundary effects. We focused on two families of tilings that can accommodate either Kekulé (
p\mod 2=0
) or Kitaev (
p\mod 3=0
) three-edge-colorings. In the former case, the gapless phase behaves like a bubble that grows as
p
increases (from a single point at
p=6
to an amoeba-like shape at
p=\infty
). In contrast, in the latter case, the gapless phase evolves from a triangular shape at
p=6
to its asymptotic form at
p=\infty
, which corresponds to the trivalent Bethe lattice.
We also obtained the exact boundaries of the phase diagram for the trivalent Bethe lattice by revisiting (and correcting) the results given in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
. In this limiting case, the phase diagram is independent of the coloring and, since there are no loops, it does not depend on the flux map. When one of the couplings dominates (isolated-dimer limit), we derived the low-energy effective Hamiltonians that describe a toric code phase.

An interesting open question concerns the nature of the gapless phase. For
p=6
(honeycomb lattice), Kitaev showed that this phase hosts non-Abelian (Ising) anyons when gapped by a three-spin term
Kitaev 2006
. Interestingly, this is also the case for
p=8

Lenggenhager et al.
and
p=9

Dusel et al.
, for which a Chern number
\nu=\pm 1
has been found around the isotropic point. It would be nice to investigate this problem for other values of
p
, including
p=\infty
.
Finally, we note that our focus here was on regular colorings. Considering random (three-edge) colorings could lead to new interesting physics related to the recently studied Anderson localization in hyperbolic lattices
Li et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
.

Appendix A
Trivalent three-coloured Bethe lattice

To compute the density of states of the trivalent three-colored Bethe lattice, we consider the Green’s function

G(E)=\frac{1}{E-H},

(14)

which satisfies the Dyson equation

G(E)=\frac{1}{E}+\frac{1}{E}\>H\>G(E).

(15)

Then, we analyze the resulting hierarchy of equations starting from the
G_{00}
matrix element where
0
denotes an (arbitrary) given site
0
. We apply the standard method for tree-like structures which amounts to studying an effective Hamiltonian limited to a central cluster dressed with energy-dependent diagonal self-energies to take account for all neglected contributions. This procedure is known to be exact for tree graphs, provided that self-consistent equations are considered for the self-energies. This ensures that the local Green function is indeed site-independent. When only the central site
0
is kept, this leads to (see Appendix D in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
),

G_{00}=\frac{1}{E-s_{x}-s_{y}-s_{z}}=\frac{1}{E-S},

(16)

where
s_{\alpha}
is the self-energy associated with couplings
J_{\alpha}
, and where
S=s_{x}+s_{y}+s_{z}
. From here on out, we will omit the dependence with
E
of the Green’s function.

Figure 7:
Local cluster of the trivalent Bethe lattice. Links are associated with hopping terms whereas circles represent self-energies (see text).

If we consider a larger cluster consisting of a central site and its three neighbors (see Fig.
7
), one gets

E\>G_{00}=1+J_{x}G_{10}+J_{y}G_{20}+J_{z}G_{30},

(17)

and

\displaystyle(E-s_{y}-s_{z})G_{10}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{x}G_{00},

(18)

\displaystyle(E-s_{z}-s_{x})G_{20}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{y}G_{00},

(19)

\displaystyle(E-s_{x}-s_{y})G_{30}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{z}G_{00},

(20)

which yields:

G_{00}=\frac{1}{E-\frac{J_{x}^{2}}{E-S+s_{x}}-\frac{J_{y}^{2}}{E-S+s_{y}}-\frac{J_{z}^{2}}{E-S+s_{z}}}.

(21)

Note that, in Eqs. (
17
)-(
20
), we consider the matrix elements of
{\rm i}A/2
with
J_{\alpha}
’s as hopping terms.
Equating Eq. (
16
) and Eq. (
21
) leads to the self-consistent equation:

\displaystyle s_{\alpha}=\frac{J_{\alpha}^{2}}{E-S+s_{\alpha}}.

(22)

The three self-energies
s_{\alpha}
are then given by one of the two solutions

s_{\alpha}=\frac{-E+S+\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{(E-S)^{2}+4J_{\alpha}^{2}}}{2},

(23)

with
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=\pm 1
. By summing over Eq. (
23
) over
\alpha=x,y,z
, one obtains:

S=3E-\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{(E-S)^{2}+4J_{\alpha}^{2}},

(24)

Using Eq. (
16
), one then gets the following expression for the local Green’s function:

G_{00}^{-1}=-2E+\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{G_{00}^{-2}+4J_{\alpha}^{2}}.

(25)

Recall that, since all sites are equivalent, the above equation applies to any
G_{ii}
. In Appendix D of Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
, the authors only consider the solution to Eq. (
25
) corresponding to
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
. However, a close inspection of the entire set of equations reveals that other solutions are also relevant.

Indeed, determining the region where the gap vanishes at
E=0
is equivalent to finding the set of parameters
J_{\alpha}
for which the local density of states at site
i
vanishes, i.e.,
\rho_{i}(E=0)=-\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}G_{ii}(0)=0
. As discussed in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
, this never happens with the choice
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=+1
, for all
\alpha
. Nevertheless, this is possible with other sign choices as we shall now discuss. Indeed, setting
E=0
and assuming
\rho_{i}(0)\neq 0
(gapless phase), Eq. (
25
) can be recasted as:

1=\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-J_{\alpha}^{2}r^{2}},

(26)

where, following Appendix D in Ref.
Kimchi et al. 2014
, we introduced
r=2\pi\rho_{i}(0)
. Here, we also used the fact that
{\rm Re}\>\>G_{ii}(0)=0
, which is always the case for a Majorana-fermion spectrum since
\rho_{i}(E)=\rho_{i}(-E)
.

Equation (
26
) allows one to obtain the exact boundary between gapped and gapless phases as explained in Sec.
VII
.

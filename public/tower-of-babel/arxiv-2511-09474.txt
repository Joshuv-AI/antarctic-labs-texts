Outage Probability Analysis of MRC-Based Fluid Antenna Systems under Rician Fading
Tummi Ganesh, Soumya P. Dash, Italo Atzeni

Abstract
This paper investigates a fluid antenna system (FAS) where a single-antenna transmitter communicates with a receiver equipped with a fluid antenna (FA) over a Rician fading channel. Considering that multiple ports among the M M available FA ports can be activated, the receiver selects the best K K with the highest instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and combines the received signals at the selected ports using maximum ratio combining. The statistics of the post-combining SNR are derived using a Laplace transform-based approach, which allows to analyze the outage probability (OP) of the FAS. Additional closed-form expressions for a lower bound on the OP and the asymptotic OP at high SNR are presented. Numerical results validate the analytical framework and demonstrate the interplay of key system parameters on the performance of the considered MRC-based FAS.



I
Introduction

The evolution toward sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks necessitates communication architectures capable of achieving ultra-reliable and large-scale connectivity. Although massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems have been instrumental to the advancements of fifth-generation (5G) networks, their conventional implementations employing fixed, uniformly spaced antenna arrays hinder the efficient exploitation of spatial diversity
[
7
]
. Consequently, 6G research is increasingly exploring the feasibility of flexible and reconfigurable array architectures that can adapt their geometry and electromagnetic response in real time to optimize performance across varying environments. Moreover, indefinitely increasing antenna counts is unsustainable due to the proportional rise in power consumption and hardware complexity
[
2
]
.

Fluid antenna systems (FASs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm to overcome the spatial and hardware constraints of conventional massive MIMO systems
[
9
]
.
Typically, by enabling a single radiating element to dynamically switch among multiple predefined ports within a limited region, FASs achieve spatial diversity without requiring multiple radio-frequency (RF) chains. This positional reconfigurability enables the efficient exploitation of spatial channel variations, thereby enhancing reliability and spectral efficiency
[
8
]
. This has inspired several studies in recent years on the performance analysis of FAS-assisted wireless systems. For instance,
[
5
]
evaluated the outage probability (OP) of a single-user FAS by employing elliptical copula models to characterize the dependence structure among the fluid antenna (FA) ports. The asymptotic OP at high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the diversity order of FASs over Rayleigh fading channels were studied in
[
4
]
. Similar studies providing insights into the OP and ergodic capacity of FASs were presented in
[
11
]
, with results obtained using elementary functions. Furthermore, the OP of FASs under correlated Nakagami-
m
and Rician fading was investigated in
[
6
]
and
[
1
]
, respectively.

All the above-mentioned studies, along with many others, restrict their focus to the activation of a single FA port. However, the performance of FASs can be greatly enhanced by activating multiple ports through the utilization of a subset of the numerous RF chains already available in next-generation wireless terminals. In addition to enabling multi-user communications, this capability represents a step towards realizing the concept of continuous-aperture MIMO, which requires an extremely large number of densely deployed discrete antenna elements
[
10
]
. To this end,
[
3
]
studied the OP performance of a FAS over Rayleigh fading channels, where a few best ports were selected and combined using maximum ratio combining (MRC). However, to the best of our knowledge, no prior study has considered the impact of a line-of-sight (LoS) component, prominent in ultra-dense 6G, indoor, and vehicular networks, on the performance of multi-port FASs.

To address this research gap, this paper investigates a FAS where the receiver is equipped with
M
FA ports and selects the best
K
, and where the wireless channels are subjected to Rician fading. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

Considering the best
K
among
M
ports with the highest instantaneous SNR combined using MRC, the OP of the FAS is derived using an approach based on the Laplace transform (LT).

•

A closed-form expression for a lower bound on the OP is obtained, from which the asymptotic OP at high SNR is derived, revealing the diversity order of the FAS.

•

Numerical results validate the analytical framework and illustrate the performance trends of the considered MRC-based FAS for varying system parameters.

II
System Model

We consider a transmitter equipped with a single antenna communicating with a receiver that utilizes a single one-dimensional FA for data reception. The FA consists of
M
ports, which are equispaced and spread across a linear distance of
W\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the wavelength of the RF signal. Denoting the transmitted symbol by
s\in\mathbb{C}
, the received signal at the
m
-th port of the FA is expressed as

y_{m}=\sqrt{\alpha(d)}h_{m}s+w_{m}\,,\ m=1,\ldots,M\,,

(1)

where
\mathbb{E}\left[\lvert s\rvert^{2}\right]=P_{s}
,
P_{s}
denotes the transmit symbol power,
\alpha(d)=\left(\lambda/4\pi d\right)^{2}
represents the large-scale fading coefficient arising due to the free-space path-loss,
d
is the distance between the transmitter and the receiver, and
w_{m}\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\right)
is the additive white Gaussian noise at the
m
-th port. Moreover,
h_{m}
represents the small-scale fading coefficient at the
m
-th port, which is modeled to capture the effect of both LoS and non-LoS propagation, implying that
h_{m}\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left(\mu_{h},\sigma_{h}^{2}\right)
,
\forall m=\left\{1,\ldots,M\right\}
. Thus, each
\left|h_{m}\right|
follows a Rician distribution with probability density function (p.d.f.) given by

f_{|h_{m}|}(r)=\frac{2r}{\sigma_{h}^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{r^{2}+\mu_{h}^{2}}{\sigma_{h}^{2}}\right\}I_{0}\left(\frac{2\mu_{h}r}{\sigma_{h}^{2}}\right),\ r\geq 0\,,

(2)

with Rician factor defined as
\kappa=\mu_{h}^{2}/\sigma_{h}^{2}
. Without loss of generality, we consider
\mathbb{E}\left[|h_{m}|^{2}\right]=1
, which results in the expressions of the powers of the LoS and the non-LoS components to be obtained as

\mu_{h}^{2}=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}\,,\qquad\sigma_{h}^{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa+1}\,,

(3)

respectively. Owing to the proximity of the ports in the FA, the small-scale fading channels are statistically correlated among the ports and can be expressed using their distributions as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!h_{1}=\sigma_{h}x_{1}+\jmath\sigma_{h}y_{1}+\mu_{h}\,,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!h_{m}=\sigma_{h}\left(\sqrt{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}\,x_{m}+\rho_{m}x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\jmath\sigma_{h}\left(\sqrt{1-\rho_{m}^{2}}\,y_{m}+\rho_{m}y_{1}\right)+\mu_{h}\,,\ m=2,\ldots,M\,,

(4)

where
x_{m}
,
y_{m}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,1/2\right)
and are all independent of each other, and

\rho_{m}=J_{0}\left(\frac{2\pi(m-1)}{M-1}W\right)\,,\ m=1,\ldots,M\,.

(5)

Considering (
1
) along with the statistics of the small-scale fading coefficients and the additive noise, the instantaneous SNR at the
m
-th port,
\gamma_{m}
, and the average SNR of the FAS,
\bar{\gamma}
, are defined as

\gamma_{m}=\frac{\alpha(d)\left|h_{m}\right|^{2}P_{s}}{\sigma_{w}^{2}}\,,\ m=1,\ldots,M\,,\quad\bar{\gamma}=\frac{\alpha(d)P_{s}}{\sigma_{w}^{2}}\,,

(6)

respectively, implying that
\gamma_{m}=\bar{\gamma}\left|h_{m}\right|^{2}
. From (
4
), it is observed that
\left|h_{1}\right|^{2}
follows a non-central chi-squared distribution with two degrees of freedom. This results in the p.d.f. of
\gamma_{1}
to be obtained using (
2
) as

\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{1}}(v_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa+1}{\bar{\gamma}}\exp\left\{-\kappa-\frac{\kappa+1}{\bar{\gamma}}v_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle\times I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{\frac{\kappa(\kappa+1)}{\bar{\gamma}}v_{1}}\right),\ v_{1}\geq 0\,.

(7)

Furthermore, from (
4
),
h_{m}\big|h_{1}
for
m\in\left\{2,\ldots,M\right\}
follows a non-zero mean complex Gaussian distribution, implying that

h_{m}\big|h_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}\left(\rho_{m}h_{1}+\mu_{h}\left(1-\rho_{m}\right),\sigma_{h}^{2}\left(1-\rho_{m}^{2}\right)\right)\,,

(8)

which results in the p.d.f. of
\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1}
expressed as

\displaystyle\!\!\!f_{\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1}}\left(v_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{m}e^{-\tau_{m}\kappa}e^{-\beta_{m}\left(v_{m}+\rho_{m}^{2}v_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\times I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{\beta_{m}v_{m}\left(\tau_{m}\kappa+\beta_{m}\rho_{m}^{2}v_{1}\right)}\right)\,,\ v_{m}\geq 0\,,

(9)

where

\beta_{m}=\frac{\kappa+1}{(1-\rho_{m}^{2})\bar{\gamma}}\,,\quad\tau_{m}=\frac{1-\rho_{m}}{1+\rho_{m}}\,,\ m=2,\ldots,M\,.

(10)

Utilizing the series expansion
I_{0}(u)=

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2k}}{2^{2k}k!\,\Gamma(k+1)}

in (
9
), followed by some algebraic simplifications, results in the p.d.f. of
\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1}
for

to be alternatively expressed as

f_{\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1}}\left(v_{m}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{m,n}(v_{1})v_{m}^{n}e^{-\beta_{m}v_{m}}\,,\ v_{m}\geq 0\,,

(11)

where

\displaystyle d_{m,n}(v_{1})=\beta_{m}^{n+1}e^{-\tau_{m}\kappa}e^{-\beta_{m}\rho_{m}^{2}v_{1}}\frac{\left(\tau_{m}\kappa+\beta_{m}\rho_{m}^{2}v_{1}\right)^{n}}{\left(n!\right)^{2}}\,.

(12)

Additionally, using (
9
), the conditional cumulative distribution function (c.d.f.) of
\gamma_{m}
for
m\in\left\{2,\ldots,M\right\}
conditioned on
\gamma_{1}
is obtained as

F_{\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1}}\left(v_{m}\right)=1-Q_{1}\left(\sqrt{2\tau_{m}\kappa+2\beta_{m}\rho_{m}^{2}v_{1}},\sqrt{2\beta_{m}v_{m}}\right)\,.

(13)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{LB}}\left(\gamma_{\text{th}},\nu,v_{1}\right)=e^{-\nu\beta_{\mathcal{S}}}\left(\prod_{m\in\mathcal{S}}\beta_{m}^{-1}d_{m,0}(v_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\left(1-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{S}\\
\ell\neq j\end{subarray}}\frac{\beta_{\ell}}{\beta_{\ell}-\beta_{j}}\right)e^{-\beta_{j}\left(\gamma_{\text{th}}-K\nu\right)}\right)\,.

(31)

) and (

) into (

), followed by some algebraic simplifications, results in the closed-form lower bound on the OP given in (

) at the top of the page, where
\tau_{[M]}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\tau_{m}
,
\beta_{\rho}^{[M]}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\beta_{m}\rho_{m}^{2}

\tilde{\beta}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}=\beta_{\mathcal{S}}+\beta_{K+1}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\beta_{m_{q}}

Consider now the regime of high average SNR, i.e., for
\bar{\gamma}\gg 1
. From the expressions of
\beta_{m}
in (

), we have
e^{-\nu\beta_{\mathcal{S}}}\approx 1
. Furthermore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left.\prod_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}\left(1-e^{-\beta_{m}\nu}\right)\right|_{\bar{\gamma}\gg 1}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\prod_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}\beta_{m}\right)\nu^{M-K-1}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle F_{\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathcal{S}}}\gamma_{m}\big|\gamma_{1},\bar{\gamma}\gg 1}(u)

\displaystyle\approx\left(\prod_{m\in{\mathcal{S}}}\beta_{m}\right)\frac{u^{K}}{K!}\,.

Utilizing (

V
Conclusions

We considered a FAS with a single-antenna transmitter and a one-dimensional FA equipped with
M
ports for data reception. The receiver selects the best
K
ports among the
M
available FA ports based on their instantaneous SNR and combines them using MRC. For such a system, a LT-based approach was utilized to obtain the statistics of the post-combining SNR, using which an integral-form expression for the OP was derived. Additionally, a closed-form expression for a tight lower bound on the OP was derived, using which the asymptotic OP at high average SNR of the system was obtained. The analysis and the numerical results showed that the considered MRC-based FAS achieves a diversity order of
M
and that the OP sharply reduces with increasing values of
K
and
M
. Future directions of this work will consider the effect of imperfect channel state information on the port selection and performance of FASs.

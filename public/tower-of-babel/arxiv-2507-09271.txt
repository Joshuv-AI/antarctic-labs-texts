1.
Introduction

1.1.
Set-up and motivation

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of characteristic
p>3
given by the Weierstrass equation as following, with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, define the
n
th division polynomial
\psi_{n}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x,y]
as follows.

\displaystyle\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\psi_{1}=1,\quad\psi_{2}=2y,

\displaystyle\psi_{3}

\displaystyle=3x^{4}+6ax^{2}+12bx-a^{2},

\displaystyle\psi_{4}

\displaystyle=4y(x^{6}+5ax^{4}+20bx^{3}-5a^{2}x^{2}-4abx-8b^{2}-a^{3}),

for more details, see
[
8
, Exercise 3.7]
, combined with the discussion in
[
8
, Chapter III.1]
.

Denote by
E({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
the group of points on
E
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. We can interpret each
\psi_{n}
as rational functions on
E({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. The multiplication of
P\in E
by
n
is given as a rational map by

\displaystyle[n](P)

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{x(P)\psi_{n}^{2}(P)-\psi_{n-1}(P)\psi_{n+1}(P)}{\psi_{n}(P)^{2}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\psi_{n-1}(P)^{2}\psi_{n+2}(P)-\psi_{n-2}(P)\psi_{n+1}(P)^{2}}{4y(P)\psi_{n}(P)^{3}}\biggr).

It is well known that

\displaystyle\psi_{m+n}(P)

\displaystyle\psi_{m-n}(P)\psi_{r}^{2}(P)

\displaystyle=\psi_{m+r}(P)\psi_{m-r}(P)\psi_{n}^{2}(P)-\psi_{n+r}(P)\psi_{n-r}(P)\psi_{m}(P)^{2},

for any integers
m,n,r
, see, for example,
[
8
, Exercise 3.7(g)]
.
It turns out that
\psi_{n}(P)
is periodic, and the period
T
, which could be as large as
(p-1)\operatorname{ord}P
,
where
\operatorname{ord}P
is the order of
P
in the group
E({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, see
[
7
, Corollary 9]
.

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
. We consider the sequence
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
, where of course we set
\chi(0)=0
. Then,
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
is periodic with a much smaller period (see Section
1.3
), compared to the period of
\psi_{n}(P)
. For points
P\in E({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
of large orders, Shparlinski and Stange
[
6
]
have studied the character sums of the form

S_{\chi,P}(N)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)),

for the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and optained a nontrivial bound provided
\operatorname{ord}P\geq N\geq p^{1/2+\varepsilon}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
.

Here, we study a related but more difficult question on the correlation of
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
with its shifts, that is, about bounding the sums

S_{\chi,P}(N,h)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))

with an integer
h\neq 0
.
More precisely, we ask the following:

Question 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime,
P\in E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a point and
\chi
be a multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
. When do we have

S_{\chi,P}(N,h)=o(N)?

Due to the limitations of our techniques, we do not have an affirmative answer to the question above. However, we prove an average version. For instance, we estimate the following sum

\sum_{h=1}^{H}|S_{\chi,P}(N,h)|=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|.

Question
1.1
itself, as well as our results, are motivated by the progress towards the
Chowla conjecture for
\lambda(n)
and
\mu(n)
, that is,
for the Liouville and Möbius functions, respectively.

It is well-known that the prime number theorem is equivalent to any of the following
statements

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda(n)=o(N)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(n)=o(N),\qquad\text{as}\ N\to\infty,

Chowla
[
1
]
has conjectured that for any integer
h>0
, the following holds

(1.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h)=o(N)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(n)\mu(n+h)=o(N),

as
N\to\infty
.

Despite several striking results towards these conjectures, both remain widely open,
see
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and references therein. An averaged version of this conjecture has been studied in
[
4
]
, in fact for a more general class of multiplicative functions. Our function
\chi\left(\psi_{n}(P)\right)
is not multiplicative, although it satisfies an
almost multiplicative
property, as stated in Lemma
2.1
. Thus, Question
1.1
essentially studies an elliptic analogue of Chowla’s conjectures (
1.1
), for the function
\chi\left(\psi_{n}(P)\right)
, and also remains widely open.

1.2.
Notation

Throughout the whole article, as usual, the notations
U=O(V)
,
U\ll V
, and
V\gg U
are equivalent to
|U|\leq cV
for some positive constant
c
, which may depend on the the order

d
of the multiplicative character
\chi
and on the dimension
m
.

We use
\omega(n)
and
\tau(n)
to denote the number of distinct prime and positive integer factors of an integer
n\neq 0
and use
\varphi(n)
to denote the Euler function.

We also denote
{\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(n)=\exp(2\pi in/R)
.

Everywhere, we use
p
to denote a prime, and
h,k,m,n
are reserved for integers.

1.3.
Main result

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
of order
d
, and
P\in E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a point. Denote
\operatorname{ord}P
be the order of
P
in
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and set

(1.2)

R=d\operatorname{ord}P.

Applying
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
for
s=d
, the sequences
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
is periodic with a period dividing
R
, as long as
\operatorname{ord}P\geq 3
.

Here, given
m
integers
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}
, we consider the following average values of multidimensional correlations
between the values of
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
:

\displaystyle U_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)=\frac{1}{H^{m-1}}\sum_{h_{2},\ldots,h_{m}=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\prod_{j=2}^{m}\chi(\psi_{n+h_{j}}(P))\right|,

\displaystyle V_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)=\frac{1}{H^{m}}\sum_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\chi(\psi_{n+h_{j}}(P))\right|.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any integer
m\geq 2
, and integers
1\leq H,N\leq R
,
we have

(1.3)

\begin{split}U_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)&,V_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)\\
&\ll H^{-1/8}R\exp\left(O\left((\log R)^{1/2}/\log\log R\right)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+H^{1/2}R^{3/4}p^{1/8}\log R,\end{split}

and if

R\geq p^{1/2}\exp\left(2.1\log p/\log\log p\right)
,
then we also have

(1.4)

\begin{split}U_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)&,V_{m,\chi,P}(H,N)\\
&\ll H^{-1/4}R^{7/6}p^{1/24}(\log R)(\log\log R)^{1/6}.\end{split}

Remark 1.3
.

It is easy to see from the proof of Theorem
1.2
that for complete sums
N=R
, one can remove
\log R
from the second term of the bound (
1.3
) and from
the bound (
1.4
).

To understand the strength of Theorem
1.2
we consider the extreme case of very
long sums with
R\geq N\gg R
. Then the bound (
1.3
) is nontrivial in the range

\exp\left(C(\log R)^{1/2}/\log\log R\right)\leq H\leq R^{1/2}p^{-1/4}(\log R)^{-2}f(p)^{-1}

for some absolute constant
C>0
and any function
f(p)\to 0

as
p\to\infty
. In fact, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, Remark
3.2
allows us to have a non-trivial bound in the extended lower range

H\geq\exp\left(C_{\varepsilon}(\log R)^{\varepsilon}\right),

where
C_{\varepsilon}>0
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon
.

On the other hand, the bound (
1.4
) is nontrivial only when
H
is quite large,
namely
R\geq H\geq R^{2/3}p^{1/6}(\log\log R)^{2/3}F(p)
with any function
F(p)\to\infty

as
p\to\infty
.

Unfortunately there is a wide gap between this ranges as

R^{2/3}p^{1/6}/R^{1/2}p^{-1/4}\geq p^{5/12}R^{1/6}\gg p^{1/2}.

(since the we have
R\geq p^{1/2}
for either of the
bounds (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) to be nontrivial). Closing this gap is certainly an interesting open question.

Another interesting regime is
H=N
. In this case the bound (
1.3
) is always trivial, while (
1.4
) requires

R\geq H=N\geq R^{14/15}p^{1/30}(\log R)^{4/5}(\log\log R)^{2/15}F(p)

with
F(p)\to\infty
as
p\to\infty
.

In a response to Question
1.1
, we have the following simple application of Theorem
1.2
.
Namely, taking
m=2
and applying (
1.4
) we immediately derive the following result.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Delta>0
be an arbitrary real number. If
R\geq p^{1/2}\exp\left(2.1\log p/\log\log p\right)
then, we have

\displaystyle\sharp\,\left\{1\leq h\leq H:\penalty\ \left|S_{\chi,P,h}(N)\right|\geq\Delta N\right\}

\displaystyle\ll\Delta^{-1}H^{3/4}N^{-1}

\displaystyle R^{7/6}p^{1/24}(\log\log R)^{1/6}.

We note that, ignoring logarithmic terms, Corollary
1.4
produces a meaningful
result if
H^{1/4}N\geq R^{7/6}p^{1/24+\varepsilon}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Some properties of division polynomials

We have an
almost multiplicative
nature of
\chi(\psi_{n}(P))
, which is given in
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character. Then for any integers
m,n
we have

\chi(\psi_{mn}(P))=\chi(\psi_{m}(nP))\chi(\psi_{n}(P))^{m^{2}}.

As usual, for any
\Psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)
, we define

\deg\Psi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in E(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\\
\nu_{P}(\Psi)>0\end{subarray}}\nu_{P}(\Psi),

where
\nu_{P}(\Psi)
is the multiplicity of
P
as a zero of
\Psi
(and thus this is a finite sum over
all zeros
P
of
\Psi
).

Moreover, we also need the following results to show that a certain class of functions satisfies the requirement of Lemma
2.3
below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m
be any integer such that
\mathrm{ord}(P)\not\mid m
. For positive integers
k\neq\ell
with
\gcd(k\ell,R)=1
, we consider the function

\Psi_{P}(Q)=\psi_{k}(Q)\cdot\psi_{k}^{-1}(Q+mP)\cdot\psi^{-1}_{\ell}(Q)\cdot\psi_{\ell}(Q+mP).

Then,

(i)

\deg\Psi_{P}<2(k^{2}+\ell^{2})
;

(ii)

\Psi_{P}
is not a non-trivial power of any function in the function field
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)}
.

Proof.

Proof of (i) is clear, as each
\psi_{h}
has
h^{2}-1
simple zeroes,
see
[
8
, Exercise III.3.7]
.

To prove (ii), let us assume that
\Psi_{P}=G^{\nu}
for some
\nu\geq 2
and
G\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}(E)}
. In particular, the function

\widetilde{\Psi}_{P}(Q)=\Psi_{P}(Q)\cdot\psi^{2}_{\ell}(Q)\cdot\psi^{2}_{k}(Q+mP)

has the property that each zero of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{P}
has multiplicity at least two.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that
k>\ell
. Since, as we have mentioned, the polynomial
\psi_{k}
has
k^{2}-1>1
simple roots in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, each root
Q_{0}
of
\psi_{k}
, is also a root at least one of the
\psi_{k}(Q_{0}+mP)
,
\psi_{\ell}(Q_{0})
or
\psi_{\ell}(Q_{0}+mP)
is zero.

Of course, we have
k^{2}-\ell^{2}\geq 3
. Therefore, we can choose
Q_{0}
so that
\psi_{\ell}(Q_{0})\neq 0
. Now, if either of
\psi_{k}(Q_{0}+mP)=0
or
\psi_{\ell}(Q_{0}+mP)=0
is zero and thus
k\ell mP=0
. However, since
\operatorname{ord}P\not\mid m
this contradicts our assumption
\gcd(k\ell,\operatorname{ord}P)=1
and
concludes the proof.

2.2.
Some character sums with division polynomials

We now derive from
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\chi
be any non-principal multiplicative character on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
,
R
be as in (
1.2
), and
\Psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)
be of degree
\deg\Psi
. Assume that
\Psi
is not a non-trivial power of a function in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)}
. Then, we have the following estimate for any integer
a

\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\Psi(nP)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(an)\ll\deg\Psi\cdot\sqrt{p}.

Proof.

Since
R=dr
, where
r=\operatorname{ord}P
, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\Psi(nP)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(an)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\sum_{n=1}^{r}\chi(\Psi((nd-k)P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(a(nd-k))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(-ak)\sum_{n=1}^{r}\chi(\Psi((nd-k)P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{r}(an).

The result now follows by applying
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
to each of the inner sums over
n
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Consequently, we deduce the following estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
R\geq p^{1/2}\exp\left(2.1\log p/\log\log p\right)
. For any integer
a
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(an)\ll p^{1/12}R^{5/6}(\log\log R)^{1/3}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in the same manner as that of
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
; for completeness, we briefly sketch the argument. The only modification involves the choice of the set of integers

{\mathcal{R}}_{d}=\left\{1\leq r\leq L:\penalty\ r\equiv 1\bmod{d},\ \gcd(r,R)=1\right\},

for a suitably chosen parameter
L
.
Let
Q
be the largest factor of
R
with
\gcd(d,Q)=1
.
Clearly for
r\equiv 1\bmod{d}
the condition
\gcd(r,R)=1
is equivalent to

\gcd(r,Q)=1
. Writing
r=du+1
, a simple inclusion–exclusion argument gives

\sharp{\mathcal{R}}_{d}=\sum_{e\mid Q}\mu(e)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u\leq(L-1)/d\\
du+1\equiv 0\bmod e\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{e\mid Q}\mu(e)\left(\frac{L-1}{de}+\vartheta_{e}\right)

with some
\vartheta_{e}\in[-1,1]
,
e\mid Q
. Hence, for some
\vartheta\in[-1,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\sharp{\mathcal{R}}_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\mid Q}\mu(e)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u\leq(L-1)/d\\
du+1\equiv 0\bmod e\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{e\mid Q}\mu(e)\left(\frac{L-1}{de}+\vartheta_{e}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{L-1}{d}\sum_{e\mid Q}\frac{\mu(e)}{e}+\vartheta\tau(Q)=\frac{L-1}{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid Q\\
\ell\penalty\ \text{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)+\vartheta\tau(Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(Q)(L-1)}{dQ}+\vartheta\tau(Q),

see
[
2
, Theorem 62]
.

Thus, using the elementary observations

\frac{\varphi(Q)}{Q}\geq\frac{\varphi(R)}{R}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau(Q)\leq\tau(R)

and the following well-known estimates

\tau(R)\leq\exp\left(\left(\log 2+o(1)\right)\log R/\log\log R\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(R)\gg\frac{R}{\log\log R},

as
R\to\infty
,
see
[
2
, Theorems 317 and 328]
, we see that

\sharp\,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\geq\frac{\varphi(R)(L-1)}{dR}-\tau(R)\gg\frac{L}{\log\log R}

provided that

(2.1)

L\geq\exp\left(0.7\log p/\log\log p\right).

The desired sum is then bounded by
|W|
, where

W=\frac{1}{\sharp\,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{nr}(P))\,{\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(anr).

We interchange the sums and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, as exactly done in
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
. Thanks to Lemma
2.1
, we have the following identity for any
r\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}

\chi(\psi_{nr}(P))=\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\chi(\psi_{r}(nP)).

Applying the identity, we have

\displaystyle W^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{\left(\sharp\,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\right)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{r\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}\chi(\psi_{r}(nP))\,{\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(anr)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{\left(\sharp\,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\right)^{2}}\sum_{r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}\left|\ \sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\varPsi_{r_{1},r_{2}}(nP)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(a(r_{1}-r_{2})n)\right|,

where
\varPsi_{r_{1},r_{2}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)
is given by the following

\varPsi_{r_{1},r_{2}}(Q)=\psi_{r_{1}}(Q)\cdot\psi_{r_{2}}^{-1}(Q).

For any
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
, it is clear that
\varPsi_{r_{1},r_{2}}
is not power of a function in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(E)}

(since zeros of
\psi_{r}(Q)
in the function field of
E
are exactly distinct torsion points of order dividing
r
, see also the proof of Lemma
2.2
).

Estimating the contributions from the integers
r_{1}=r_{2}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
trivially, and applying Lemma
2.3
for each such
r_{1}\neq r_{2}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
, we have

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle\ll R\left(\sharp\,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\right)^{-1/2}+R^{1/2}Lp^{1/4}

\displaystyle\ll RL^{-1/2}(\log\log R)^{1/2}+R^{1/2}Lp^{1/4}.

The result follows by choosing
L=R^{1/3}p^{-1/6}(\log\log p)^{1/3}
, as clearly it satisfies (
2.1
) due to the assumption on
R
.

3.
Bounds of some double character sums with division polynomials

3.1.
Small
H

We denote
\overline{\chi}
to be the complex conjugate character of a multiplicative character
\chi
of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, that is,
\chi\overline{\chi}=1
. To prove Theorem
1.2
, we first need the following estimate regarding the correlation between
\chi
and
\overline{\chi}
.

Denote

T_{\chi,P}(H)=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be as in (
1.2
). For any integer
1\leq H\leq R
, we have

T_{\chi,P}(H)\ll H^{1/2}R^{2}\exp\left(O\left((\log R)^{1/2}/\log\log R\right)\right)+H^{3}Rp^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the set of positive integers
e\leq H
which factors into primes which are also prime divisors of
R
.
For each
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
we consider the following set of integers

{\mathcal{K}}_{e}=\{1\leq k\leq H/e:\penalty\ \gcd(k,R)=1\}.

Clearly we have the following partition

\{1,\ldots,H\}=\bigsqcup_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}\left(e{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\right)

in disjointed sets
e{\mathcal{K}}_{e}=\{ek:\penalty\ k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\}
. Hence

(3.1)

T_{\chi,P}(H,N)\leq\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}S_{e},

where

S_{e}=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+ek}(P))\right|^{2}.

Clearly, we can replace
n
in the inner sums with
kn
for any
k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}
, hence

S_{e}=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{kn}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{k(n+e)}(P))\right|^{2}.

For any
k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}
, by Lemma
2.1
we have the following identity

\displaystyle\chi(\psi_{kn}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{k(n+e)}(P))

\displaystyle\qquad=\chi\left(\psi_{k}\left(nP\right)\psi_{k}^{-1}\left((n+e)P\right)\right)\chi\left(\psi_{n}\left(P\right)\psi^{-1}_{n+e}\left(P\right)\right)^{2k^{2}},

with the convention that if
\psi_{k}\left((n+e)P\right)=0
or
\psi_{n+e}\left(P\right)=0
then the whole expression
is equal to zero. We follow a similar convention below as well.

Therefore,

(3.2)

S_{e}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}W_{j,e},

with

\displaystyle W_{j,e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\biggl|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi\left(\psi_{k}\left(nP\right)\psi_{k}^{-1}\left((n+e)P\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\chi\left(\psi_{n}(P)\psi^{-1}_{n+e}\left(P\right)\right)^{2k^{2}}\biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\chi\left(\psi_{m}(P)\cdot\psi^{-1}_{m+e}(P)\cdot\psi^{-1}_{n}(P)\cdot\psi_{n+e}(P)\right)^{2j^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\varXi_{k,m,n},

where we denote

\varXi_{k,m,n}=\chi\left(\psi_{k}\left(mP\right)\psi_{k}^{-1}\left((m+e)P\right)\right)\overline{\chi}\left(\psi_{k}\left(nP\right)\psi_{k}^{-1}\left((n+e)P\right)\right).

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and changing the order of summation, we see that

\displaystyle W_{j,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{2}\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\varXi_{k,m,n}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv\ell\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\varXi_{k,m,n}\overline{\varXi_{\ell,m,n}}.

Denoting

\varPsi_{k,\ell}(Q)=\psi_{k}(Q)\cdot\psi_{k}^{-1}(Q+eP)\cdot\psi_{\ell}^{-1}(Q)\cdot\psi_{\ell}(Q+eP),

we see that

\displaystyle W_{j,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv\ell\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\chi\left(\varPsi_{k,\ell}(nP)\right)\overline{\chi\left(\varPsi_{k,\ell}(mP)\right)}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\\
k\equiv\ell\equiv j\bmod d\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\varPsi_{k,\ell}(nP))\right|^{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
we have

\deg\varPsi_{k,\ell}\ll k^{2}+\ell^{2}\ll(H/e)^{2}.

In particular, using Lemma
2.3
for any distinct integers
k,\ell\in{\mathcal{K}}_{e}
and
estimating the contribution from
k=\ell
trivially, since the implied constants
may depend on
d
, we obtain

W_{j,e}^{2}\ll R^{2}\left(\sharp\,{\mathcal{K}}_{e}R^{2}+\left(\sharp\,{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\right)^{2}\left(\left(He^{-1}\right)^{2}p^{1/2}\right)^{2}\right).

Since
\sharp\,{\mathcal{K}}_{e}\leq H/e
, we see that

W_{j,e}\ll H^{1/2}R^{2}e^{-1/2}+\ H^{3}Rp^{1/2}e^{-3}.

Substituting this bound in (
3.2
), we derive

\displaystyle S_{e}

\displaystyle\ll H^{1/2}R^{2}e^{-1/2}+H^{3}Rp^{1/2}e^{-3}.

Recalling (
3.1
), we see that

(3.3)

\begin{split}T_{\chi,P}(H)&\ll\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}\left(H^{1/2}R^{2}e^{-1/2}+H^{3}Rp^{1/2}e^{-3}\right)\\
&\ll H^{1/2}R^{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}e^{-1/2}+H^{3}Rp^{1/2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}e^{-3}\\
&\ll H^{1/2}R^{2}\rho+H^{3}Rp^{1/2},\end{split}

where

\rho=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}e^{-1/2}.

Let
\nu=\omega(R)
and let
p_{i}
denote the
i
th prime. Then

(3.4)

\begin{split}\rho&\leq\prod_{p\mid R}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{-j/2}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}p_{i}^{-j/2}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(1+p_{i}^{-1/2}+O\left(p_{i}^{-1}\right)\right)=\exp\lambda,\end{split}

where

(3.5)

\begin{split}\lambda&=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\log\left(1+p_{i}^{-1/2}+O(p_{i}^{-1})\right)\ll\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}p_{i}^{-1/2}\\
&\ll\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(i\log i\right)^{-1/2}\ll\nu^{1/2}\left(\log\nu\right)^{-1/2}.\end{split}

Since obviously
\nu!\leq R
, from the Stirling formula, we see that

\nu\ll\frac{\log R}{\log\log R}

and thus
\rho\leq\exp\left(O\left((\log R)^{1/2}/\log\log R\right)\right)
. Substituting this bound on
\rho
in (
3.3
), we conclude the proof.

Remark 3.2
.

Using the so-called Rankin method, that is, using that for any
\gamma>0
, similarly to (
3.4
)
and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}}e^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
p\mid e\,\Rightarrow\,p\mid R\end{subarray}}e^{-1/2}\left(\frac{H}{e}\right)^{\gamma}\ll H^{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\left(i\log i\right)^{-1/2-\gamma}

\displaystyle=H^{\gamma}\nu^{1/2-\gamma}\left(\log\nu\right)^{-1/2-\gamma}

\displaystyle=H^{\gamma}\exp\left(O\left((\log R)^{1/2-\gamma}/\log\log R\right)\right),

and optimising the choice of
\gamma
one can obtain a slightly more precise bound when
H
is

3.2.
Large

In this section, we obtain a similar estimate for the correlation between
\chi

\overline{\chi}
as in Lemma
3.1
, for a large
H
.

We first estimate
T_{\chi,P}(H)
for

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
R\geq p^{1/2}\exp\left(2.1\log p/\log\log p\right)
. Then, we have

T_{\chi,P}(R)\ll R^{8/3}p^{1/6}(\log\log R)^{2/3}.

Proof.

Using that
\chi(\psi_{k}(P))
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, is periodic with period
R
, we can derive

\displaystyle T_{\chi,P}(R)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{h=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{m}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n}(P))\sum_{h=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{m+h}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2},n_{1},n_{2}=1\\
m_{1}-m_{2}\equiv n_{1}-n_{2}\bmod R\end{subarray}}^{R}\chi(\psi_{m_{1}}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n_{1}}(P)\chi(\psi_{m_{2}}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n_{2}}(P)).

Using the orthogonality of exponential functions, we write

\displaystyle T_{\chi,P}(R)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},n_{1},n_{2}=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{m_{1}}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n_{1}}(P)\chi(\psi_{m_{2}}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n_{2}}(P))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\frac{1}{R}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{R}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}\left(\lambda\left(m_{1}-m_{2}-n_{1}+n_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(\lambda n)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(-\lambda n)\right|^{2}.

Applying Lemma
2.4
and the orthogonality of exponential functions to the
remaining sum
we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{R}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(R^{5/6}p^{1/12}(\log\log R)^{1/3}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{R}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)){\mathbf{\,e}}_{R}(\lambda n)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(R^{5/6}p^{1/12}(\log\log R)^{1/3}\right)^{2}R=R^{8/3}p^{1/6}(\log\log R)^{2/3},

which concludes the proof.

4.1.
Preliminaries

We start with the following bound on weighted sums.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be as in (
1.2
), and let
\alpha_{n}
,
n=1,\ldots,R
, be a sequence of weights with
|\alpha_{n}|\leq 1
. Then, we have

\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\alpha_{n}\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|\ll H^{-1/4}\left(R^{1/2}\left|T_{\chi,P}(H)\right|^{1/4}+R\right).

Proof.

Denote

S=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\alpha_{n}\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|S|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq H\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\alpha_{n}\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq H\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\overline{\alpha_{m}}\alpha_{n}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\chi(\psi_{m+h}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P)).

In particular, we have

|S|^{2}\leq H\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{h=1}^{H}\chi(\psi_{m+h}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again, we get

\displaystyle|S|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq H^{2}R^{2}\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\,\left|\sum_{h=1}^{H}\chi(\psi_{m+h}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=H^{2}R^{2}\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\,\sum_{h,k=1}^{H}\chi(\psi_{m+h}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\overline{\chi}(\psi_{m+k}(P))\chi(\psi_{n+k}(P)).

By periodicity, we now have

\displaystyle|S|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq H^{3}R^{2}\sum_{|h|\leq H}\left|\sum_{m,n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{m}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n}(P))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{m+h}(P))\chi(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|

\displaystyle=H^{3}R^{2}\sum_{|h|\leq H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2H^{3}R^{2}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{R}\chi(\psi_{n}(P)))\overline{\chi}(\psi_{n+h}(P))\right|^{2}+H^{3}R^{4}

\displaystyle=2H^{3}R^{2}T_{\chi,P}(H)+H^{3}R^{4},

and the result follows.

Graph C*-algebras are singly generated
Jakub Curda, Julian Gonzales, Victor Wu

Abstract
. We show that the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra of a countable directed graph is singly generated. As a consequence, any C ∗ C^{*} -algebra generated by a countable family of projections and partial isometries satisfying Cuntz-Krieger relations is singly generated.



Introduction

The question of which operator algebras are generated by a single element, or equivalently two self-adjoint elements, goes at least back to Kadison’s list of problems from 1967 (see
[
9
]
). He asked the question for separably acting
\mathrm{II}_{1}\,
factors, or more generally, separably acting von Neumann algebras. Kadison’s problem has been solved for all factors not of type
\mathrm{II}_{1}\,
(
[
16
,
26
]
), and many classes of
\mathrm{II}_{1}\,
factors are also known to be singly generated (see for example
[
17
,
8
,
7
]
). The problem remains open in its full generality - for example, it is open for the free group factors
L(\mathbb{F}_{n})
when
n\geq 3
.

In contrast to the von Neumann algebraic setting, it is too naive to ask if all separable
C^{*}
-algebras are singly generated - one does not need to look far to find counterexamples. For unital commutative
C^{*}
-algebras, it is known that
C(X)
is singly generated if and only if
X
embeds into the complex plane. Noncommutative counterexamples are also abundant. Algebras of the form
C(X)\otimes M_{n}
are not singly generated provided the matrix dimension
n
is sufficiently small compared to the dimension of
X
(see
[
14
]
for instance). Since the quotient of a singly generated
C^{*}
-algebra remains singly generated, more counterexamples can be constructed from extensions of these. For example, the full group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is not singly generated since it quotients onto
C(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

It is therefore unsurprising that the generator problem for
C^{*}
-algebras is often restricted to algebras that lack finite-dimensional representations (see
[
22
, Question 1.3]
for instance), or sometimes even to simple algebras. To the best of our knowledge, the only known obstruction to single generation is the presence of homogeneous quotients that are not singly generated.

A natural class of
C^{*}
-algebras that do not admit problematic quotients is the class of graph
C^{*}
-algebras; see the decomposition described below. In this paper we show that for any countable directed graph
E
, its graph
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(E)
is singly generated.

Theorem A
.

Let
E
be a countable directed graph.
Then
C^{*}(E)
is singly generated.

First introduced in
[
11
]
(see also
[
6
]
), graph
C^{*}
-algebras have proven to be a class of algebras which often allow for explicit calculations, direct study of
C^{*}
-algebraic properties, and testing of conjectures (see for example
[
20
,
21
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
).
Even though these algebras are rarely simple, loosely speaking, they arise as iterated extensions of AF algebras, Kirchberg algebras and algebras of the form
C(\mathbb{T})\otimes M_{n}
(
[
1
]
), all of which are known to be singly generated (see the paragraph below). Since single generation does not pass to extensions (see
[
23
]
), Theorem
A
cannot be deduced directly from this fact. Nevertheless, this suggests that graph
C^{*}
-algebras are a natural class on which to consider the generator problem.
Observe that Theorem
A
does not extend to
C^{*}
-algebras of higher-rank graphs in full generality - it is known that a
k
-graph can have
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphic to
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})
(see
[
12
, Example 1.7 (iii)]
).

Let us give a brief overview of known single generation results in the
C^{*}
-setting.
Early results (
[
25
,
15
,
13
]
) focused on algebras with suitable decomposition properties. This culminated with the work
[
22
]
which showed that, in particular, any unital separable and
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebra is singly generated. We also note that stabilisations of unital separable
C^{*}
-algebras are singly generated (
[
15
, Theorem 8]
) and so are all AF algebras (
[
23
]
). In a different direction, an unpublished result, due to Kirchberg, states that any unital separable
C^{*}
-algebra containing two isometries with orthogonal ranges is singly generated (see
[
14
, p. 138]
for the statement and
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
for a sketch of the proof in a special case).

The class of graph
C^{*}
-algebras contains algebras that are stable and/or
\mathcal{Z}
-stable (see
[
24
,
5
]
). For the graph algebras falling into the classes above, our methods give an alternative proof for their single generation. The class also contains algebras covered by Kirchberg’s result, although those are necessarily unital. Note that the generator problem is often restricted to unital
C^{*}
-algebras, since a
C^{*}
-algebra is singly generated if and only if its minimal unitization is. We do not make any unitality assumptions in this paper - indeed, a graph has unital
C^{*}
-algebra if and only if it has finitely many vertices.

Let us now illustrate the basic idea of our proof in the special case of a finite graph with no sinks. Let
E
be such a graph with edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
. Writing
s_{i}
for the corresponding partial isometries, note that
C^{*}(E)=C^{*}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
. It turns out that if
E
contains no sinks, the element
d=\left(\sum s_{i}\right)\zeta
is a generator for
C^{*}(E)
for a suitable choice of weight
\zeta
belonging to the commutative subalgebra generated by all of the vertex projections and range projections of edge partial isometries. Using orthogonality of the ranges of the
s_{i}
, one finds that
d^{*}d
is a weighted sum of the range projections, and hence they all belong to
C^{*}(d)
. Finally, multiplying
d
on the left by
s_{i}s_{i}^{*}
shows that
s_{i}
also belongs to
C^{*}(d)
. We note that the idea of summing scalar multiples of isometries already appeared in the proof sketch of Kirchberg’s result in
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
.

It turns out that the strategy described above makes no use of the unit and so extending to countable graphs without sinks is straightforward (see Corollary
8
). The main obstacles are the sinks in
E
. More specifically, in the presence of sinks receiving infinitely many edges, the graph
C^{*}
-algebra is no longer generated by the edge partial isometries. We adjust the generator to be a sum of three terms, each designed to handle a different portion of the graph. The first captures the sinks and the edges terminating at them, the second captures all the edges “sufficiently far” from the sinks, and the third takes care of the remaining edges and helps glue the first two terms together. A careful double induction argument lets us recover each edge partial isometry and vertex projection from the generator.

The paper is organised as follows: Section
1
recalls some basic facts about spectral theory and graph
C^{*}
-algebras, while Section
2
treats the special case of a graph
E
with no sinks. In Section
3
we construct the candidate generator for
C^{*}(E)
and then verify that it is indeed a generator in Section
4
.

2.
Graphs with no sinks

In this section we present a short proof that the
C^{*}
-algebra of a graph with no sinks is singly generated. As in Section
4
, a key feature of graph
C^{*}
-algebras we use is that edge partial isometries have mutually orthogonal range projections. As remarked on in the introduction, a similar idea is used in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
.

The generator
d
in this section is of the form
\left(\sum\epsilon_{e}{s_{e}}\right)\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\sum\epsilon_{e}{s_{e}}
is a weighted sum of all edge partial isometries, and
\xi
is a positive element belonging to the (commutative)
C^{*}
-subalgebra generated by all vertex and range projections. The element
\xi
is chosen so that the
C^{*}
-subalgebra generated by
d^{*}d
contains all range projections
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
. Range projections are pairwise orthogonal, so multiplying the generator
d
on the left by
s_{e}s_{e}^{*}
shows that
s_{e}
also belongs to
C^{*}(d)
. Since the
C^{*}
-algebra of a graph with no sinks is generated by its edge partial isometries, it follows that
d
generates
C^{*}(E)
.

The following lemma describes the construction of
\xi
, and will also be used in Section
3
. Roughly speaking, the existence of an appropriate positive element
\xi
is guaranteed because any commutative
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a family of commuting projections has 0-dimensional spectrum and, in particular, is therefore singly generated.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S\subseteq E^{1}
be a set of edges in a directed graph
E
. Let
V\coloneq s(S)\subseteq E^{0}
be the set of sources of edges in
S
, and let
T\coloneq r^{-1}(V)\subseteq E^{1}
be the set of all edges that have range in
V
. Define

(2.1)

A\coloneq C^{*}(p_{v},s_{e}s_{e}^{*}\colon v\in V,e\in T)\subseteq C^{*}(E)\,.

Let
(\epsilon_{e})_{e\in S}
be positive real numbers satisfying
\sum_{e\in S}\epsilon_{e}<\infty
. There exists a positive element
\xi\in A
of norm at most 1 for which

(2.2)

d\coloneq\left(\sum_{e\in S}\epsilon_{e}s_{e}\right)\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}

satisfies the following:

(i)

A=C^{*}(d^{*}d)
;

(ii)

for any

, there exists

such that

.

Moreover, given any countable compact set

has spectrum disjoint from

.

Set
\mu_{v}\coloneq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in S\cap s^{-1}(v)\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{e}^{2}

is non-empty for every

be countable and compact, so that for any

\xi\in A

[\frac{\theta_{v}}{\mu_{v}},\frac{\theta_{v}^{\prime}}{\mu_{v}}]

4

\xi

Set

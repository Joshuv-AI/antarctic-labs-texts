SFEM for the Lagrangian formulation of the surface Stokes problem
Charles M. Elliott, Achilleas Mavrakis

Abstract
. We consider the surface Stokes equation with Lagrange multiplier and approach it numerically. Using a Taylor-Hood surface finite element method, along with an appropriate estimate for the additional Lagrange multiplier, we derive a new inf-sup condition to help with the stability and convergence results. We establish optimal velocity convergence both in energy and tangential L 2 L^{2} norms, along with optimal L 2 L^{2} norm convergence for the two pressures, in the case of super-parametric finite elements. Furthermore, if the approximation order of the velocities matches that of the extra Lagrange multiplier, we achieve optimal order convergence even in the standard iso-parametric case. In this case, we also establish some new estimates for the normal L 2 L^{2} velocity norm. In addition, we provide numerical simulations that confirm the established error bounds and also perform a comparative analysis against the penalty approach.



1.
Introduction

For a stationary two dimensional surface
\Gamma
the
(steady) generalized surface Stokes
problem is to: Find the velocity field
{\bm{u}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
, surface pressure
p:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\Gamma}pds=0
and Lagrange multiplier
\lambda:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that solves on
\Gamma

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(E({\bm{u}}))+{\bm{u}}+\lambda{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}+\nabla_{\Gamma}p={\bm{f}},\\
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}=g,\\
{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}=0,\end{cases}

where
\mu
is the viscosity coefficient,
{\bm{f}}
is a given force field
{\bm{f}}\in(L^{2}(\Gamma))^{3}
,

\lambda
is a normal force field to be determined and
g
is a given source term
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, with
\int_{\Gamma}gds=0
. Essentially we view
\lambda
as a Lagrange multiplier for the constraint that the normal component of
{\bm{u}}
is zero. Here the rate-of-strain (deformation) tensor
E(\cdot)
,
[
32
]
, is defined for an arbitrary vector field
{\bm{v}}
, by

(1.2)

\displaystyle E({\bm{v}})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}+\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov,t}{\bm{v}}).

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}:={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}
(with adjoint
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov,t}{\bm{v}}=(\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}})^{t}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
) and
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
denotes the projection to the tangent space of
\Gamma
.

Writing
{\bm{u}}\equiv{\bm{u}}_{T}+{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}
and
{\bm{u}}_{T}:={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{u}}
,
this may also be written as: Find a tangential velocity
{\bm{u}}_{T}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with
{\bm{u}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{\Gamma}=0
and surface pressure
p:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\Gamma}pds=0
that solves on
\Gamma

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\mu{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(E({\bm{u}}_{T}))+\nabla_{\Gamma}p+{\bm{u}}_{T}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{f}},\\
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}_{T}=g.\end{cases}

The well-posedness of both surface Stokes systems has been studied in
[
26
,
31
,
39
,
32
]
, where the authors do not consider the zero-order term, but instead filter out the
Killing fields
, that is, the non-zero tangential vectors
{\bm{v}}_{K}
satisfying
E({\bm{v}}_{K})=0
. In our case, we follow
[
27
,
36
,
34
,
38
,
9
,
24
]
and add a zero-order term
{\bm{u}}
to ensure well-posedness (uniqueness) and avoid the resulting technical difficulties in the numerical analysis.
[
1
]
have studied the effect this zero-order term has on the solution and also the error bounds.

In this work, we consider the discretization of a variational form of the problem (
1.3
) using
H^{1}
-conforming
Taylor-Hood
surface finite elements. Surface finite elements methods (SFEM) have been studied extensively in
[
10
,
11
,
14
,
13
]
for elliptic and parabolic problems on a surface
\Gamma
, including their evolving surface counterpart
[
16
,
12
,
15
,
29
]
. There is also work for the vector- or tensor-valued corresponding problem using SFEM
[
22
,
23
]
.

The tangential Stokes problem has been studied using TraceFem in
[
27
,
36
,
34
]
, and more recently
[
1
,
38
,
9
,
24
,
2
]
with the help of SFEM. There have also been discretizations of the stream function formulation using SFEM in
[
3
,
37
,
33
]
. The issue with analyzing a discretization of (
1.3
) is that trying to enforce the tangential constraint in the discrete space would not lead to
H^{1}
-conforming finite elements, due to the discontinuity across the edges of the mesh. Some papers enforce the tangentiality condition exactly, see
[
1
]
where an
H(\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma})
conforming finite element method with an interior penalty approach to enforce
H^{1}-
continuity quickly is analyzed, and
[
9
]
again using a
H(\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma})
conforming finite element method where no penalization is needed due to the use of the Piola transform. There are also such methods, using the Piola transform to approximate the tangential condition exactly for the Darcy problem
[
18
]
as well. These methods, though, more often than not are more difficult to implement numerically.

An easier approach is to use
H^{1}-
conforming
\emph{Taylor-Hood}
surface finite element methods, where one enforces the tangential constrain weakly via penalization or Lagrange multiplier. Several papers have studied the penalization approach using TraceFEM Stokes problem
[
27
,
36
,
34
]
and SFEM
[
24
,
38
]
. However, there appears to be none employing the Lagrange multiplier method, except for
[
28
]
, where the authors use Tracefem to approximate the vector-Laplace equation.

In
[
24
]

using a penalty approach for the tangentiality condition, with penalty scaling
\eta=h^{-1}
, they are, with the help of the macro-element technique, able to prove optimal tangential
L^{2}
-norm velocity error bounds. With this technique, it is possible to prove well-posedness for different known pairs of stable Stokes elements from the flat case, for example, the
Taylor-Hood
MINI element. In comparison, in
[
38
]
the author enforces the tangential condition again via a penalty with a penalty parameter
\eta=h^{-2}
, and achieves optimal
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
error bounds for the velocity field and pressure respectively, in the case where a higher-order approximation of normal
\tilde{{\bm{n}}}_{h}
is considered. Both authors have to approximate the strain tensor
E({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{v}})
, for which the mean curvature tensor was evaluated as the gradient derivative of the geometry normal.

In our work, as mentioned above, we use
H^{1}-
conforming
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{u}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{pr}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{\lambda}}
extended
Taylor-Hood
finite elements, where we enforce the tangential constraint weakly via an extra Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{h}
, to approximate (
1.3
). We consider and treat this new Lagrange multiplier as an extra pressure and, therefore, show a combined discrete
inf-sup condition
involving both
\{p_{h},\lambda_{h}\}
. In particular, we establish two different discrete
inf-sup conditions
depending on the choice of norm for
\lambda_{h}
. One concerning the
L^{2}\times L^{2}
-norm of the pressures with respect to the energy norm of the velocity, and another involving the
L^{2}\times H_{h}^{-1}
-norm with respect to the
H^{1}
-norm of the velocity instead, where
H_{h}^{-1}
is the dual of the finite element space for
\lambda_{h}
.
For the error bounds, we consider two different scenarios depending on the choice of approximation of the extra Lagrange multiplier, i.e.
k_{\lambda}
. First, in the case where
k_{\lambda}=k_{u}-1
we show optimal order discrete error estimates in energy and tangential
L^{2}
-norms for the velocity and
L^{2}\times L^{2}
-norm for the pressures, only under sufficiently accurate geometry approximation; e.g. we have
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{k_{g}-1})
convergence for the geometric part of the error in the energy norm. Therefore, we use
super-parametric finite elements
, where the order of the geometric approximation of the discrete surface is one higher than the order of the velocity approximation,
k_{u}=k_{g}+1
and
k_{g}\geq 2
. On the other hand, for the
k_{\lambda}=k_{u}
case,
we establish optimal error bounds with respect to the geometric part of the error even with
iso-parametric finite elements
; e.g. we show
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{k_{g}})
convergence with respect to the geometric approximation in the energy norm. The reason for the improvement in the geometric error is a new improved
H^{1}
coercivity result for weakly discretely tangential divergence-free functions
Lemma

6.10
(this result is obtained by better controlling the normal part of such functions due to the richer F.E. space for
\lambda_{h}
) and the
L^{2}\times H_{h}^{-1}

discrete inf-sup condition
. In this case, we also establish new error estimates for the
L^{2}
-norm of the normal part of the velocity, which are half-order sub-optimal compared to the tangential
L^{2}
-norm, unless an improved normal
\tilde{{\bm{n}}}_{h}
is used; see
Remark

6.17
. Moreover, these new improved estimates hold even for planar finite elements, i.e. for
k_{g}=1
, which was not the case for
k_{\lambda}=k_{u}-1
. We also want to notice that these results are important for the study of the
time-dependent Navier-Stokes
on stationary or evolving surfaces. Regarding the numerical implementation of our method, we notice that it is simple and straightforward. Experiments are provided in
Section

7
, where we test our theoretical results. Lastly, we notice that the choice of
k_{\lambda}
plays an important role in the numerical implementation, since, despite the good results we observe for
k_{\lambda}=k_{u}
, this choice leads to a worse conditioned system compared to the other case.

2.
Differential geometry on surfaces

In this section, we recall some fundamental notions and tools concerning surface vector-PDEs necessary for the study of the surface Stokes problem. We mainly follow definitions in
[
22
]
and
[
26
]
.

2.1.
The closed smooth surface

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a closed, oriented, compact
C^{m}
two-dimensional hypersurface embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We will see later that
m\geq 4
is required in the error analysis. Note that since
\Gamma
is the boundary of an open set we choose the orientation by setting
{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}
to be the unit outward pointing normal to
\Gamma
. Let
d(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the signed distance function, defined in
[
14
]
, and
for
\delta>0
, let
U_{\delta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the tubular neighborhood
U_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ :|d(x)|<\delta\}
. Then we may define the closest point projection mapping to
\Gamma
as
\pi(x)=x-d(x){\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}(\pi(x))\in\Gamma
for each
x\in U_{\delta}
for
\delta>0
small enough, see
[
20
,
14
]
. Moreover,
\nabla d(x)={\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}(\pi(x))
for
x\in U_{\delta}
. We can see that
d(\cdot)
and
\pi(\cdot)
are of class
C^{m}
and
C^{m-1}
on
\overline{U_{\delta}}
.

The extended normal allows us to define the extended Weingarten map on the surface and in
U_{\delta}
, by
\bm{H}:=\nabla{\bm{n}}^{e}=\nabla^{2}d
, which is of class
C^{m-2}
. The mean curvature of
\Gamma
is
{\bm{\kappa}}:=tr(\bm{H})
. We also define the orthogonal projection operator onto the tangent plane
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}(x)={\bm{I}}-{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}(x)\otimes{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}(x)
for
x\in\Gamma
that satisfies
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}^{T}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}^{2}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
,
|{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}|_{Fr}=2
, and
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}=0
.

Using the projection
\pi
we may extend functions
{\bm{v}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
on
\Gamma
to the tubular neighbourhood
U
, by

(2.1)

{\bm{v}}^{e}(x)={\bm{v}}(\pi(x)),\ \ \ x\in U_{\delta}.

This extension is constant in the normal direction of
\Gamma
and thus contains certain useful additional properties in comparison to other regular extensions. The extension of
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
to the
\delta-
strip
U_{\delta}
, is defined as

(2.2)

{\bm{P}}={\bm{I}}-{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}^{e}(x)\otimes{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}^{e}(x)={\bm{I}}-{\bm{n}}(x)\otimes{\bm{n}}(x),\ \text{ for }x\in U_{\delta},

where we have used
{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}^{e}={\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}\circ\pi={\bm{n}}
. We can then easily see that
{\bm{P}}|_{\Gamma}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
.

2.2.
Scalar functions and vector fields

We start by defining useful quantities for
scalar
functions following
[
14
]
. For a function
f\in C^{1}(\Gamma)
we define the
tangential (surface) derivative
of
f
as

(2.3)

\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}(x)\nabla f^{e}(x),\ \ \text{with }\ \underline{D}_{i}f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}P_{ij}\partial_{j}f^{e}(x),\ \ \ x\in\Gamma,

where
(\nabla f^{e})_{i}=\partial_{i}(f^{e})
,
1\leq i\leq 3
is the Euclidean derivative and
\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)=(\underline{D}_{1}f(x),

\underline{D}_{2}f(x),

\underline{D}_{3}f(x))^{t}
is a column vector. Here
f^{e}
is the extension to
U_{\delta}
where
f^{e}|_{\Gamma}=f
as in (
2.1
). Now, since
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
is the orthogonal projection, which is symmetric, we have that

(2.4)

\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}(x)\nabla f^{e}(x)=\nabla^{t}f^{e}(x){\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}(x),\ \ \ \ {\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}f=\nabla_{\Gamma}f\ \ \text{on }\Gamma.

As noted in
[
14
, Lemma 2.4]
the definition of the tangential gradient is independent of the extension. We now introduce derivatives of surface
vector fields
. We begin by defining tangential derivatives, analogous to the scalar case, as Euclidean derivatives of extended quantities (this time-vector fields) and proceed to establish the covariant derivatives.

Definition 2.1

(Tangential derivative)
.

Let
{\bm{v}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{T}
be a smooth vector field, and
{\bm{v}}^{e}
the smooth extension in (
2.1
), the tangential gradient of this vector field is defined by

(2.5)

(\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}})_{ij}=(\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}})_{ij}=\sum_{l=1}^{3}\partial_{l}v^{e}_{i}P_{jl},

where
(\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}})_{ij}=\underline{D}_{j}v_{i}
and
\underline{D}_{j}v=({\mathbf{p}}_{j}\cdot\nabla)v^{e}=(\nabla v^{e}){\mathbf{p}}_{j}
. This derivative is a
3\times 3
matrix, which may be presented as

(2.6)

\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
v_{3}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{D}_{1}v_{1}&\underline{D}_{2}v_{1}&\underline{D}_{3}v_{1}\\
\underline{D}_{1}v_{2}&\underline{D}_{2}v_{2}&\underline{D}_{3}v_{2}\\
\underline{D}_{1}v_{3}&\underline{D}_{2}v_{3}&\underline{D}_{3}v_{3}\end{bmatrix}.

We note again that due to the extension
{\bm{v}}^{e}={\bm{v}}\circ\pi
we find that the tangential derivative turns out to be the Euclidean derivative corresponding to this extended vector field
i.e.

\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}=\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}|_{\Gamma}
. With the help of the tangential derivatives for vector fields we may define the Weingarten map
\bm{H}
on the
\delta-
strip
U_{\delta}
again

(2.7)

\bm{H}:=\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{n}},\ \text{ with }\ \bm{H}{\bm{n}}=0,\ \ \ \bm{H}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{H}=\bm{H},

where we observe that it is tangent to
\Gamma
. Note that
\bm{H}
is of class
C^{m-2}
and thus bounded on
\Gamma
, see
[
14
,
20
]
.

We continue by providing an extended definition of covariant derivatives for general vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, as seen in
[
4
]
and
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.2

(Covariant derivatives)
.

Let
{\bm{v}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{T}
be a smooth vector field, and
{\bm{v}}^{e}
the smooth extension in (
2.1
), the covariant derivative
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}
is then defined by

(2.8)

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}|_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}P_{jk}\partial_{k}v^{e}_{l}P_{li},

where the derivative is a
3\times 3
matrix.

Notice that the covariant derivative, in comparison to the tangential derivative of a smooth vector field defined in (
2.5
), is a tangential tensor field, i.e. matrix.
Now, for a smooth vector-valued function
{\bm{v}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the surface divergence is given by

(2.9)

\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}({\bm{v}})=tr(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}})=tr({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}})=tr({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e})=tr(\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}})=tr(\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}})=tr(\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}),

and thus we can easily see that

(2.10)

||\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq 3||\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

We may split the arbitrary smooth vector field
{\bm{v}}
into
{\bm{v}}={\bm{v}}_{T}+v_{n}{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}
with
{\bm{v}}_{T}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{v}}
and
v_{n}={\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}
such that
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}=\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}_{T}+\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}(v_{n}{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}})
, and get the following useful formulae

(2.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}=\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\bm{v}}_{T}+\bm{H}v_{n}.

We also define with the help of (
2.9
) the surface divergence of a tensor function
{\mathbf{F}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{F}}):=\begin{bmatrix}\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{F}}_{1,j})\\
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{F}}_{2,j})\\
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}({\mathbf{F}}_{3,j})\end{bmatrix},\quad j=1,2,3.

2.3.
Function spaces

We now define function spaces on the surface
\Gamma
. By
(\cdot,\cdot)
we denote the usual
L^{2}(\Gamma)
inner product with norm
||\cdot||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=(\cdot,\cdot)^{1/2}
and by
||\cdot||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}
we denote the
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
norm. By
<\cdot,\cdot>_{H^{-1},H^{1}}
we denote the duality pairing. Also
(H^{k}(\Gamma))^{n}
denotes the standard Sobolev spaces as presented in
[
14
]
of scalar
(n=1)
or vector valued functions
(n=3)
for
{\mathbf{\eta}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the corresponding norm being given by

(2.13)

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{\eta}}||_{H^{k}(\Gamma)}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}||\nabla_{\Gamma}^{j}{\mathbf{\eta}}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{j}
denote all the weak tangential derivative of order
j
, i.e.
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{j}{\mathbf{\eta}}=\underbrace{\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\cdot\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}}_{\text{ j times}}{\mathbf{\eta}}
. For
k=0
we get the usual
L^{2}-
norm
||\cdot||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
for scalar or vector-valued functions. Now more specifically for a vector fields
{\bm{v}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
the space
\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)=(H^{1}(\Gamma))^{3}
is equipped with the following norm

(2.14)

\displaystyle||{\bm{v}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}=||{\bm{v}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+||\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

We set

L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma):=\{\eta\in L^{2}(\Gamma)|\ \int_{\Gamma}\eta\,d\sigma=0\},

equipped with the standard
L^{2}
-norm and the subspace of tangential vector fields:

\bm{H}^{1}_{T}:=\{{\bm{u}}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)^{3}\>|\>{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}=0\},

endowed with the
H^{1}
-norm (
2.14
).
For any vector field
{\bm{v}}
we use the decomposition
{\bm{v}}:={\bm{v}}_{T}+v_{n}{\bm{n}}_{\Gamma}
, where
{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{v}}={\bm{v}}_{T}

and
v_{n}:={\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{\Gamma}
. We may also define the space

(2.15)

{\bm{V}}:=\{{\bm{v}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)^{n}:{\bm{v}}_{T}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T},\,v_{n}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\},\ \ \text{ with }\ \ ||{\bm{v}}||_{{\bm{V}}}:=||{\bm{v}}_{T}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+||v_{n}||_{L^{2}({\Gamma})},

where we can interpret
{\bm{V}}\sim\bm{H}^{1}_{T}\oplus L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

The following inequality known as
surface Korn’s inequality
was established in
[
26
]
. There exists a constant
c_{K}>0
such that

(2.16)

c_{K}||{\bm{v}}_{T}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq||E({\bm{v}}_{T})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+||{\bm{v}}_{T}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}~~\text{ for all }{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T}.

3.
Variational formulation

3.1.
Bilinear forms

In order to set up the variational formulations we define bilinear forms.

Definition 3.1
.

For
{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\{q,\xi\}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle a({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma}E({\bm{u}}):E({\bm{v}})+{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,d\sigma.

(3.2)

\displaystyle b^{L}({\bm{u}},\{q,\xi\})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Gamma}q\ \textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}_{T}\,d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma}\xi u_{n}\,d\sigma:=b({\bm{u}},q)+\int_{\Gamma}\xi u_{n}\,d\sigma.

In the case where
{\bm{u}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\{q,\xi\}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1}(\Gamma)
we define

(3.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{b}({\bm{u}},\{q,\xi\}):=b({\bm{u}},q)+<\xi,\,u_{n}>_{H^{-1},H^{1}},

where by
<\cdot,\cdot>_{H^{-1},H^{1}}
we mean the duality pairing.

3.2.
Lagrange multiplier variational formulations

Consider (
1.1
) with
g=0
. By applying integration by parts, we want to look for a solution for the following problem,

(LP)
: Given
{\bm{f}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)^{3}

determine
({\bm{u}},\{p,\lambda\})\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)\times(L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma))
such that,

(LP)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})\ +\!\!\!\!&b^{L}({\bm{v}},\{p,\lambda\})=({\bm{f}},{\bm{v}})_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma),\\

The surface Stokes problem (

(LP-aux)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})\ +\!\!\!\!&\tilde{b}({\bm{v}},\{p,\lambda\})=({\bm{f}},{\bm{v}})_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma),\\
&\tilde{b}({\bm{u}},\{q,\xi\})=0\ \ \ \text{ for all }\{q,\xi\}\in(L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1}(\Gamma)).\end{cases}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

and compact. Then there exists a constant

(3.4)

\displaystyle\inf_{\{p,\lambda\}\in(L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1}(\Gamma))}\ \sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)}\frac{\tilde{b}({\bm{v}},\{p,\lambda\})}{||{\bm{v}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}||\{p,\lambda\}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1}(\Gamma)}}\geq c_{b}>0,

Proof.

For any

and

we may set

, where,

may be chosen such that

due to

Thus the following holds

\displaystyle||{\bm{v}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}=||{\bm{v}}_{T}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+||v_{n}{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c(||p||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+||\mathcal{R}_{H^{1}}\lambda||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)})

\displaystyle\leq c(||p||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+||\lambda||_{H^{-1}(\Gamma)}),

and thus

\displaystyle\tilde{b}({\bm{v}},\{p,\lambda\})=b({\bm{v}}_{T},p)+||\lambda||_{H^{-1}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c(||p||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+||\lambda||_{H^{-1}(\Gamma)}^{2})^{1/2}||{\bm{v}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Surface finite elements

4.1.
Triangulated surfaces

4.1.1.
Piecewise linear triangulation
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}

To approximate the continuous surface
\Gamma
we start by constructing a polyhedral approximation
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
contained in the tubular neighborhood
U_{\delta}
i.e.
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}\subset U_{\delta}
, whose vertices lie on
\Gamma
and which is equipped with an admissible and conforming subdivision of triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
, see
[
16
, Section 6.2]
, so that

\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}=\cup_{\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}}\tilde{T}.

We denote by
h_{\tilde{T}}
the diameter of a simplex
\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
and set
h:=\text{max}\{\text{diam}(\tilde{T})\,:\,\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}\}
. We assume that the triangulation is
shape-regular
, hence the following inequality holds

(4.1)

c_{1}h_{\tilde{T}_{i}}\leq h_{\tilde{T}}\leq c_{2}h_{\tilde{T}_{i}},\quad\text{for all }\tilde{T}_{i}\in\omega_{\tilde{T}}.

for fixed constants
c_{1},\,c_{2}

[
6
]
, where
\omega_{\tilde{T}}
denotes the neighbouring elements to
\tilde{T}
assumed to bounded by a fixed constant.
We further assume that the family of triangulations is also
quasi-uniform

[
16
, Definition 6.22]
, hence associated with a quasi-uniform constant
\sigma
such that

(4.2)

{\max_{\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}}h_{\tilde{T}}}/{\min_{\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}}h_{\tilde{T}}}\leq\sigma.

The set of all the edges of the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
, is denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{(1)}
. For each edge we may also define the outward pointing unit co-normals
{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{(1),\pm}
w.r.t. the two adjacent triangles
\tilde{T}^{\pm}
with a common edge. Notice that on
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
,

•

{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{(1),+}\neq-{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{(1),-}
(unless the adjacent triangles are in the same plane),

•

{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{(1),\pm}
is tangential to the planar triangles
\tilde{T}^{\pm}
, respectively.

4.1.2.
Higher order triangulation
\Gamma_{h}

Let us consider a reference element
\hat{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then for every
\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
there exists an affine invertible map

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{x}):\hat{T}\rightarrow\tilde{T},

that maps the reference triangle
\hat{T}
to every
\tilde{T}
. Now let us describe the higher-order surface approximation
\Gamma_{h}^{k_{g}}=\Gamma_{h}
with again
quasi uniform
triangulation
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Using the previous polyhedral approximation
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
with triangulation
\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
we consider the map

(4.4)

\displaystyle F_{T}(\tilde{x}):\tilde{T}\rightarrow T,

which may be constructed in the following way: Let
\{\phi_{1},...,\phi_{n_{k_{g}}}\}
be the Lagrange basis functions of degree
k_{g}
of a planar element
\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}
corresponding to a set of nodal points on that triangle
\{\tilde{a}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{k_{g}}}
. Then with the help of the
k_{g}-
th order Lagrange interpolant
[
14
]
on the planar simplex we define

\displaystyle\pi_{k_{g}}(\tilde{x})=\mathcal{I}_{h}^{k_{g}}\pi(\tilde{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k_{g}}}\pi(\tilde{a}_{j})\phi_{i}(\tilde{x}),\qquad\text{ for }\tilde{x}\in\tilde{T}.

And by setting
F_{T}(\tilde{x})=\pi_{k_{g}}(\tilde{x})
for
\tilde{x}\in\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
we have constructed element-wise a continuous polynomial map on
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
. The high-order triangulation can be written as
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\cup\{F_{T}(\tilde{T})\ |\ \tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(1)}\}
and thus the high-order surface approximation can then be defined as
\Gamma_{h}=\cup_{\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{F_{T}(\tilde{x})\ |\ \tilde{x}\in\tilde{T}\}
. We now denote element-wise outward unit normal to
\Gamma_{h}
by
{\bm{n}}_{h}
and define the discrete projection
{\bm{P}}_{h}
onto the tangent space of
\Gamma_{h}
by

{\bm{P}_{h}}(x)={\bm{I}}-{\bm{n}}_{h}(x)\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}(x),\quad x\in T,\text{ where }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

Also, the corresponding discrete Weingarten map is
\bm{H}_{h}:=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}{\bm{n}}_{h}.
For the curved triangulation, we may also consider the co-dimension one set of all the edges of the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
. For each edge we may also define the outward pointing unit co-normals
{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{\pm}
with respect to the two adjacent triangles
T^{\pm}
. Notice that on discrete surfaces in general

[{\mathbf{m}}_{h}]|_{E}={\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{+}+{\mathbf{m}}_{h}^{-},

is not necessarily zero. Now returning back to the reference map (
4.3
) we may introduce a new map

(4.5)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{T}:\hat{T}\rightarrow T,

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{T}(\hat{x})=F_{T}(\hat{F}_{\tilde{T}}(\hat{x}))\quad\text{ for }\hat{x}\in\hat{T},

which maps the reference triangle
\hat{T}
to every triangle
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Note that this map agrees with the map constructed in
[
16
, Definition 6.1, Section 9.5]
.

The approximation of the smooth surface

Lemma 4.1

(Geometric Errors)

For
\Gamma_{h}
and

(4.6)

(4.7)

Proof.

The estimates in (

) are already known, see
[

7
]

∎

Next, we consider the local area deformation transforming

to

]

\displaystyle||1-\mu_{h}^{(1)}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(1)}_{h})}\leq ch^{2},

from which we get the bounds

and

Similarly for the deformation area

\displaystyle||1-\mu_{h}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq ch^{k_{g}+1},

from which we get yet again the bounds

and

We may now, also define

\displaystyle\mu_{h,1}^{k}d\sigma_{h}^{(1)}=d\sigma_{h}(F_{T}(\tilde{x})),\quad\text{ for }\tilde{x}\in\Gamma_{h}.

4.2.
Finite element function spaces

Since certain quantities on the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
, e.g. projections and their derivatives, are only defined elementwise on each surface finite element
T
, it is convenient to introduce broken surface Sobolev spaces together with their respective norms,
[
16
]
. The norm
||\cdot||_{H^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
related to the
broken Sobolev space

\bm{H}^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=(H^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))^{3}
is defined by

(4.12)

\displaystyle||{\bm{v}}||_{H^{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}||{\bm{v}}||^{2}_{H^{m}(T)}.

Given a triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Gamma_{h}
, as described above, we define the
H^{1}-
conforming Lagrange finite element space on
\Gamma_{h}
, with finite elements of degree
k\geq 0
as:

(4.13)

S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}:=\{v_{h}\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{h}):v_{h}|_{T}=\hat{v}_{h}\circ\hat{F}_{T}^{-1}\text{ for some }\hat{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\hat{T}),\ \text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}^{k}(\hat{T})
is the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
k_{u}
on the reference element
\hat{T}
. Due to the mapping
\hat{F}_{T}^{-1}
, (
4.5
), we observe that the space does not necessarily consists of polynomials over each triangle
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, unless we consider affine finite elements
[
16
, Example 6.7]
, consisting of piecewise linear triangulation and first-order polynomial functions. Note as well that
S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}\subset C^{0}(\Gamma_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})
. An alternate and equivalent way to define the parametric space of polynomial order
k
and surface approximation of order
k_{g}
is the following:

(4.14)

S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}:=\{v_{h}\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{h}):v_{h}|_{T}=\tilde{v}_{h}\circ F_{T}^{-1}\text{ for some }\tilde{v}_{h}\in S_{h,1}^{k},\ \text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

This definition is equivalent to the previous one since
\tilde{v}_{h}(\tilde{x})=\hat{v}_{h}\circ\hat{F}_{\tilde{T}}^{-1}(\tilde{x})
, for
\hat{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\hat{T})
and thus
\tilde{v}_{h}\circ F_{T}^{-1}=\hat{v}_{h}\circ\hat{F}_{\tilde{T}}^{-1}\circ F_{T}^{-1}
which according to the map
(
4.5
) becomes
\tilde{v}_{h}\circ F_{T}^{-1}=\hat{v}_{h}\circ\hat{F}_{T}^{-1}
. This definition will be useful when we want to relate function on
\Gamma^{(1)}_{h}
to functions on
\Gamma_{h}
.

All the above can be easily expanded for vector valued functions where now the finite element space takes the form:

(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k})^{3}=S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}\times S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}\times S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}\subset\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{h})=(H^{1}(\Gamma_{h}))^{3}.

We denote the velocity approximation order by
k_{u}
, and for the pressures
p
and
\lambda
, we represent their approximation orders as
k_{pr}
and
k_{\lambda}
, respectively. Writing

\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{u}}:=(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k_{u}})^{3},\quad\mathcal{P}_{k_{pr}}:=S_{h,k_{g}}^{k_{pr}},\quad\mathcal{P}_{k_{\lambda}}:=S_{h,k_{g}}^{k_{\lambda}},

we use the notation
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{u}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{pr}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{\lambda}}
to denote
Taylor-Hood
finite elements, where
k_{pr}=k_{u}-1
.

4.3.
Surface lifting

4.3.1.
Lifting to the exact surface

Our finite element approximation is defined for functions on the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
, and therefore the solution will also belong to this approximated surface, while the solution of (
1.3
) lies on
\Gamma
. To analyze and compare the two solutions we need mappings between functions on
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{h}
. We accomplish this by using an extension/lifting procedure; see
[
14
,
7
]
.

We already assumed that
h
is small enough such that
\Gamma_{h}\subset U_{\delta}
, hence the projection
\pi(\cdot)
is well-defined and a bijection. Therefore, for every triangle in the triangulation
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
there exists an induced curvilinear triangle
T^{\ell}=\pi(T)\subset\Gamma
by the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Gamma_{h}
. We may then define the conforming quasi-uniform triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ell}
of
\Gamma
:

\Gamma=\cup_{T^{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\ell}}T^{\ell}.

We will define the lift operator on
\Gamma_{h}
using the closest point projection operator
\pi(\cdot)
. For any finite element function
v_{h}:\Gamma_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
lift
onto
\Gamma
by

(4.15)

v_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x)):=v_{h}(x),\quad\text{for }x\in\Gamma_{h}.

Similar to the extension (
2.1
), we also define the inverse lift operator for a function
v:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

(4.16)

v^{-\ell}(x):=v(\pi(x)),\quad\text{for }x\in\Gamma_{h}.

Notice that the two extensions (
2.1
) and (
4.16
) agree on the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
i.e.
v^{-\ell}=v^{e}|_{\Gamma_{h}}
since we assumed
\Gamma_{h}\subset U_{\delta}
.
Lastly, we also introduce the space of lifted finite element functions given by

(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k})^{\ell}=\{v_{h}^{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Gamma):v_{h}\in S_{h,k_{g}}^{k}\}\subset H^{1}(\Gamma).

We now compare the functions defined on the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
and the exact surface
\Gamma
. We relate their tangential derivatives with the help of the lift extension defined above. We start with the standard lifts regarding scalar functions and then extend these results component-wise to vector-valued functions. Set

(4.17)

{\bm{B}_{h}}={\bm{P}_{h}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H}){\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}.

Scalar Functions :: :

Considering the extension of a scalar function
v_{h}:\Gamma_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, (
4.15
) and the chain rule we have that the discrete tangent gradient of
v_{h}\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})
, for
x\in\Gamma_{h}
is

(4.18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v_{h}(x)={\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla v_{h}(x)={\bm{P}_{h}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H}){\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla v_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x))={\bm{B}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x)),

where we see later on that
{\bm{B}_{h}}:T_{\pi(x)}(\Gamma)\mapsto T_{x}(\Gamma_{h})
is invertible, i.e. we have that the map
{\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}
on the tangent space
T_{x}(\Gamma_{h})
is given by

{\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}|_{T_{x}(\Gamma_{h})}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H})^{-1}({\bm{I}}-\frac{{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}}{{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}}){\bm{P}_{h}},

thus we have for
u_{h}:\Gamma_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and for
x\in\Gamma_{h}
, see
[
8
]
,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x))={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H})^{-1}({\bm{I}}-\frac{{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}}{{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}})\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v_{h}(x)={\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}v_{h}(x).

Noticing that the matrix
{\mathbf{G}}=({\bm{I}}-\frac{{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}}{{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}})|_{T_{x}(\Gamma_{h})}=({\bm{I}}-\frac{{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}}{{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}}){\bm{P}_{h}}
we can easily calculate that
{\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}{\bm{B}_{h}}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}
and
{\bm{B}_{h}}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}={\bm{P}_{h}}
.

Vector Valued Functions :: :

Considering the tangential gradients of vector valued function (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) we may apply the lift extension component-wise, for each row, and with similar calculations to the scalar case we may get, after factoring out the common map
{\bm{B}_{h}}
, that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{h}(x)=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x))=\nabla\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x)){\bm{P}_{h}}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x)){\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}.

Likewise for the inverse transformation we get

(4.21)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}(\pi(x))=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{h}(x)({\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1})^{t}.

Recalling the results from
[
25
,
14
,
22
,
28
]
we have the following lemma, which also shows that
{\mathbf{B}}_{h}
is invertible.

Lemma 4.2

(Estimates for
{\mathbf{B}}_{h}
)
.

For sufficiently small

we have the following bounds

(4.22)

\displaystyle||{\bm{B}_{h}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c,\qquad\qquad\ ||{\bm{B}_{h}}^{-1}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq 1,

(4.23)

\displaystyle||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{B}_{h}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k_{g}},\qquad\quad||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}{\bm{B}_{h}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq ch^{k_{g}+1},

where the constant
c
is independent of

.

Proof.

The first two bounds on
{\bm{B}_{h}}
and its inverse follow directly from the bounds on the projection and normals, as well as the geometric errors (
4.6
). For the third estimate we have the following calculations

\displaystyle||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{B}_{h}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{P}_{h}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H}){\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq||({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{P}_{h}}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}})+{\bm{P}_{h}}d\bm{H}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}+||{\bm{P}_{h}}d\bm{H}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq ch^{k_{g}},

where the last inequality holds again due to the geometric errors (
4.6
) and the uniform bound of the curvature tensor
\bm{H}
. For the fourth estimate we have similarly

\displaystyle||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}{\bm{B}_{h}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H}){\bm{P}_{h}}({\bm{I}}-d\bm{H}){\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}-{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{P}_{h}}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}+\mathcal{O}(h^{k_{g}+1})

\displaystyle\leq||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}{\bm{n}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}+\mathcal{O}(h^{k_{g}+1})

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k_{g}+1}.

which completes the proof.
∎

4.4.

is a projection.

Now following
[
22
]
,
[
7
]
, with the help of the maps in Section

\Gamma_{h}
by the interpolant
\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}=\widetilde{I}_{h,k_{g}}^{z}:(L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}))^{3}\rightarrow(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k})^{3}

{\bm{v}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})^{3}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

Lemma 4.6

(4.33)

\displaystyle|(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell},\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h},\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}|\leq ch^{k_{g}}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})},

(4.34)

\displaystyle|(E_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}),E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h}))_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}-(E({\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}),E(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}))_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}|\leq ch^{k_{g}}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}

(4.35)

\displaystyle||E({\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell})^{-\ell}-E_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq ch^{k_{g}}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})},

where the constant
c
are independent of the mesh parameter

Proof.

Let us start by proving (
4.33
), which can be estimated similarly to
[
25
,
14
]
. Considering that
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}(\cdot)={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
and
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}(\cdot)={\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}(\cdot)
the calculations are as followed,

\displaystyle|(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell},\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h},\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}:\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h}\,d\sigma_{h}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:\nabla_{\Gamma}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\mu_{h}}(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}{\mathbf{w}}_{h})^{\ell}:(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h})^{\ell}\,d\sigma\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}:{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}\,d\sigma

The second term is once again immediate from the deformation estimate (
4.9
) and the bounds of
{\bm{P}_{h}}
and
{\bm{B}_{h}}
(
4.22
) so that

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\Gamma}(1-\frac{1}{\mu_{h}}){\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}:{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}\,d\sigma\leq ch^{k_{g}+1}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\big|.

Regarding the first term, using the properties of the Frobenius inner product we have that

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}:{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}\,d\sigma\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,d\sigma-\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{P}_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{\ell}:\nabla_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{B}_{h}}^{t}{\bm{B}_{h}}\,d\sigma\big|

by using (
4.7

Now, to prove the estimate (

4.6.
Discrete Korn’s inequality

Lemma 4.7

(Discrete Korn’s inequality)
.

For h small enough it holds that

(4.36)

||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq c(||E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+h^{-1}||\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}),\quad

\text{ for all }\bm{v}_{h}\in(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k})^{3}
.

Proof.

Let us start from the continuous relation, similar to
[
28
]
. We know in the continuous tangent space
\bm{H}_{T}^{1}(\Gamma)
, Korn’s inequality
(
2.16
) holds so the bound for the tangential term
||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}
reads as

(4.37)

\displaystyle||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c(||E({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}).

On the other hand,
E(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})=E({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})+(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}})\bm{H}
for all
\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma)
so we may obtain, due to norm equivalence, that

(4.38)

\displaystyle||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq c||\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq c||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+c||\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

We now try and bound the two terms appearing on the right hand-side appropriately. In regards to the second term we have for
{\bm{n}}={\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}^{-\ell}
that

\displaystyle||\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c||\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+c||\nabla_{\Gamma}(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+c||\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}.

We cannot directly apply an inverse-type estimate to the second term. For that we want to utilize the super-approximation (
4.28
) and the fact that the Scott-Zhang interpolant (
4.26
) maps onto our finite element space
(S_{h,k_{g}}^{k})^{3}
. To do so we have the following

\displaystyle||\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}(I-\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z})(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+||\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq c||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{-1}||\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

where we used the inverse estimate
[
25
, Lemma 4.5]
for the second right-hand side term. So, combining this result we get

(4.39)

||\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq c||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{-1}||\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

It remains to bound the tangent term
||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}
,

\displaystyle||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c||E({\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+c||{\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c||E(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+c||(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}_{\Gamma}})\bm{H}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+||{\bm{P}}\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq c||E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+c||E(\bm{v}_{h}^{\ell})-E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+c||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq c||E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{k_{g}}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{k_{g}}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+c||\bm{v}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

where
{\bm{P}}={\bm{P}_{\Gamma}}^{-\ell}
and where we have used geometric perturbation result (
4.35
) in Section
4.5
and the geometric estimates in (
4.7
). Now combining the two estimate and absorbing the appropriate term to the left-hand side we get the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Note that, the needed regularity of the normal

{\mathbf{m}}_{h}

.

On the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}

(4.40)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}q_{h}\,d\sigma_{h}

5.
The discrete Lagrange formulation

We consider the
Taylor-Hood
surface finite elements
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{k_{u}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{pr}}/\mathcal{P}_{k_{\lambda}}
for a
k_{g}
-order approximation of the surface, (c.f. Section

), and set

Let us define the following finite element spaces

(5.1)

We also define the subspace of weakly discretely tangential divergence-free velocity fields

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}_{h}^{div}:=\{{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}:b_{h}^{L}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\})=0,~~\forall\,\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\}\in Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}\}.

||\ell_{h}||_{H_{h}^{-1}(\Gamma_{h})}=\sup_{\xi_{h}\in\Lambda_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{<\ell_{h},\xi_{h}>_{H^{-1},H^{1}}}{||\xi_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}}.

5.1.
The discrete bilinear forms

The finite element method relies on the discrete bilinear forms
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot),\,b_{h}^{L}(\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
defined by,

(5.4)

\displaystyle a_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}E_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}):E_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})d\sigma_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\,d\sigma_{h},

(5.5)

\displaystyle b_{h}^{L}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}q_{h}\,d\sigma_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}\,\xi_{h}\,d\sigma_{h},

(5.6)

\displaystyle b_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}q_{h}\,d\sigma_{h}.

In our stability analysis we use the energy norm

(5.7)

||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{a_{h}}=||E_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h})||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

for a velocity vector
{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{h})
.

Lemma 5.1

(Bounds and coercivity)
.

The following bounds and coercivity estimates hold:

(5.8)

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h})+h^{-2}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}\leq ch^{-2}a_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h})\quad\ \,\textnormal{ for all }{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},

(5.9)

\displaystyle a_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{a_{h}}||\bm{v}_{h}||_{a_{h}}\ \ \ \qquad\quad\ \ \textnormal{ for all }{\mathbf{w}}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},

(5.10)

\displaystyle b_{h}^{L}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\})

\displaystyle\leq c||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{a_{h}}||\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\ \textnormal{ for all }{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},\,(q_{h},\xi_{h})\in Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}.

Proof.

The
H^{1}
coercivity w.r.t. energy norm (

) follows immediately from the discrete Korn’s Inequality (

), while the bound (
5.9
) is derived with simple use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. For the last inequality, as previously, we need to use the integration by parts formula (

). This, coupled with the bound for the co-normal
|[{\mathbf{m}}_{h}]|\leq ch^{k_{g}}
(c.f.
[

) and
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma_{h}}(\bm{v}_{h})=tr(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{h})=tr(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{cov}\bm{v}_{h})

completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Improved
H^{1}
coercivity bound)
.

If
\underline{k_{\lambda}=k_{u}}
, that is
\Lambda_{h}=V_{h}
, and
{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}^{div}
then the following holds

(5.11)

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}({\mathbf{w}}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h}).

Proof.

From (
5.8
) we see that we need to find a bound for

of the form

so that the

improved coercivity bound (

, since we allow

for any

. More specifically,

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}))({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\underbrace{\big({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})-\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})\big)}_{\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})=0}({\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})+ch^{k_{g}}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

where we have used the geometric errors (

\displaystyle||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}\leq ch||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}||{\mathbf{w}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

which gives our desired bound.
∎

5.2.
The discrete Lagrange approximation
(LhP)

The Lagrange finite element approximation is based on the continuous homogeneous Problem (
LP
), where the tangentiality condition is enforced weakly via the use of a Lagrange multiplier. This formulation has the advantage of being really easy to implement.
The discrete Lagrange finite element method reads as:

(LhP)
Given
{\bm{f}}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})
, determine
(\bm{u}_{h},\{p_{h},\lambda_{h}\})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}
such that,

(LhP)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h}^{L}(\bm{v}_{h},\{p_{h},\lambda_{h}\})&=({\bm{f}}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\ \ \text{for all }\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},\\
b_{h}^{L}(\bm{u}_{h},\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\})&=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ for all }\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\}\in Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}.\end{cases}

Theorem 5.5

(Well-posedness of the discrete Lagrange problem)
.

There exists a unique solution
(\bm{u}_{h},\{p_{h},\lambda_{h}\})
that satisfies (
LhP
) for all
(\bm{v}_{h},\{q_{h},\xi_{h}\})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}
. Moreover, we have the following a priori estimates:

(5.16)

||\bm{u}_{h}||_{a_{h}}+||\{p_{h},\lambda_{h}\}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq c||{\bm{f}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the inf-sup condition Lemma
5.6
presented in the following
Section

5.3
, the bounds in
Lemma

5.1
, and simple calculations.
∎

5.3.
The
L^{2}\times L^{2}
inf-sup condition

Lemma 5.6

(Discrete Lagrange inf-sup condition)
.

Assume a quasi-uniform triangulation of
\Gamma_{h}
, then there exists a constant
\beta>0
independent of the mesh parameter
h
such that

(5.17)

c||\{q_{h},\lambda_{h}\}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}}\frac{b_{h}^{L}(\bm{v}_{h},\{q_{h},\lambda_{h}\})}{||\bm{v}_{h}||_{a_{h}}}\ \ \ \forall\{q_{h},\lambda_{h}\}\in Q_{h}\times\Lambda_{h}.

The proof of

5.6

First
, we bound below the bilinear form

by choosing an appropriate discrete velocity with the help of the continuous problem,

we try to control the pressure gradient term that arises in the first step by once again choosing another appropriate velocity, this time using the geometry of the problem, and finally in the
third
step we combine the previous two to get our

C=||{\bm{u}}||_{H^{k_{u}+1}(\Gamma)}+||p||_{H^{k_{pr}+1}(\Gamma)}+||\lambda||_{H^{k_{\lambda}+1}(\Gamma)}+||{\bm{f}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
. Let us bound the last term
||(\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell})-\bm{u}_{h})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}=||\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}
:

\displaystyle||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})(\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}})d\sigma_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})(\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}))d\sigma_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\big(\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})-\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}\big)\,d\sigma_{h}+ch^{k_{g}}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

(6.69)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}+ch||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}.

where in the last inequality we have used the super-approximation property (
4.29
) and the fact that

\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\bm{u}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}=0
with
\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\cdot)\in S^{k_{u}}_{h,k_{g}}
, since
\bm{u}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}^{div}
and therefore
\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{u}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}\xi_{h}\,d\sigma_{h}=0
for any
\xi_{h}\in\Lambda_{h}=S^{k_{\lambda}}_{h,k_{g}}=S^{k_{u}}_{h,k_{g}}
.
Now, with the help of the geometric errors (
4.6
),
{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
, the
L^{2}
-stability (
4.30
) and the non-standard geometric estimate (
6.7
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\big((\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell})-{\bm{u}}^{-\ell})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}\big)\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h})\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}(\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}})\,d\sigma_{h}

(6.70)

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k_{u}+1}||{\bm{u}}||_{H^{k_{u}+1}(\Gamma)}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{k_{g}+1}||{\bm{u}}||_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k_{u}+1}||{\bm{u}}||_{H^{k_{u}+1}(\Gamma)}||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+ch^{k_{g}+1}||{\bm{u}}||_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\big(||{\bm{u}}^{-\ell}-\widetilde{I}_{h}^{z}({\bm{u}}^{-\ell})||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}+||{\bm{u}}^{-\ell}-\bm{u}_{h}||_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}\big).

Combining (
6.69
) and (
6.70
), using
Theorem

6.11
and the
H^{1}
velocity error estimate (
6.58
) we see that, after applying young’s inequality and a kickback argument

(6.71)

\displaystyle||\theta_{{\bm{u}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{k_{u}+k_{g}+1}+h^{\widehat{m}+k_{g}+1})\leq Ch^{2\widehat{m}+1}.

Substituting this back to (
6.68
) we get our desired result.
∎

(About the

In the above proof of

FM - OFDM: Unifying High - Mobility Communications and Sensing
AMIR BOUZIANE 1 , AND HÜSEYIN ARSLAN 1

Abstract
— ISAC ( ISAC ) is foundational to future 6G ( 6G ) systems, demanding waveform co-design that supports both high-throughput data transmission and accurate environmental perception. While OFDM ( OFDM ) offers flexibility and backward compatibility, its high PAPR ( PAPR ) poses significant challenges at higher frequency bands. To address this, we investigate FM-OFDM ( FM-OFDM )—a constant-envelope waveform that facilitates robust joint sensing and communication under highly mobile, doubly dispersive channel conditions. We derive a comprehensive input–output relationship for FM-OFDM in time-varying multipath channels, including analytical expressions for ICI ( ICI ), Doppler effects, and effective channel gains. Extensive simulations comparing FM-OFDM with conventional CP-OFDM ( CP-OFDM ) and CE-OFDM ( CE-OFDM ) demonstrate superior range and velocity estimation accuracy of FM-OFDM , particularly at high SNR ( SNR )s and under high mobility, highlighting its suitability as a unified waveform for high-frequency ISAC applications in 6G .

INTRODUCTION

The upcoming
6G
wireless networks promise a transformation of global connectivity, targeting ultra-high data rates up to terabits per second (Tbps), ultra-low latency as low as 0.1 ms, massive connectivity density (millions of devices per km
2
), and sustainable energy usage
[
1
]
. These capabilities are fundamental for supporting next-generation applications such as immersive holography, real-time digital twins, remote telesurgery, autonomous mobility systems, and massive-scale intelligent infrastructure
[
2
]
. In order to meet these extreme performance requirements, a fundamental redesign of physical-layer protocols, signal processing, and spectrum usage is necessary
[
3
,
4
]
.

One of the most promising paradigms enabling this evolution is
ISAC
, where the radio interface is co-designed to simultaneously support data exchange and environmental perception. Unlike traditional systems that treat sensing and communication as separate tasks often using orthogonal resources like time, frequency, or antennas
ISAC
enables both functionalities to coexist on the same waveform, hardware, and spectrum. This shared architecture reduces hardware redundancy, increases spectrum efficiency, improves latency through tighter integration, and provides situational awareness that benefits beamforming, handover, and interference management
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Recent standardization efforts underscore the growing importance of
ISAC
. IEEE 802.11bf introduces protocols for Wi-Fi based sensing, enabling gesture detection, respiration monitoring, and device-free localization
[
8
]
. Simultaneously,
3GPP
(
3GPP
) has incorporated
ISAC
primitives into Releases 18 and 19, supporting sidelink positioning, radar-aided beamforming, and joint vehicular sensing in NR-V2X scenarios. ETSI’s Industry Specification Group on
ISAC
is actively defining requirements, key performance indicators (KPIs), and interoperability frameworks to facilitate deployment in industrial automation and smart transportation networks
[
9
,
10
]
.

Historically, radar and communication systems evolved along separate paths: radar prioritized precise range and velocity estimation using wideband chirps and pulse–Doppler techniques, while communication systems emphasized spectral efficiency and capacity. With the advent of software-defined radio (SDR) platforms, massive MIMO, and mmWave front-ends, the technical boundary between these domains has blurred, enabling physical-layer reuse. This convergence demands that the waveform-design community revisit long-standing metrics such as ambiguity functions, Cramér–Rao bounds (CRBs), spectral containment, and
PAPR
trade-offs
[
11
,
12
]
.

A central enabler of
ISAC
is waveform design. For
ISAC
to be effective, the waveform must simultaneously meet multiple, often conflicting requirements. These include high time–frequency resolution for accurate sensing, low
BER
(
BER
) and robustness for reliable communication, low
PAPR
to ensure power efficiency, and tunable spectral occupation to comply with regulatory constraints
[
13
]
.

Existing waveform-design strategies for
ISAC
generally fall into three main categories. The first category, sensing-centric designs, includes waveforms such as
PMCW
(
PMCW
) and
FMCW
(
FMCW
). These waveforms are optimized for sensing and provide excellent range–Doppler performance; however, they typically perform poorly in terms of data transmission efficiency.
The second category is communication-centric designs. These use traditional communication waveforms such as
OFDM
or single-carrier systems. While such waveforms are effective for data transmission, they suffer from limitations in sensing resolution and exhibit high
PAPR
, which reduces power efficiency.
The third and most promising category comprises joint designs. These aim to co-optimize sensing and communication within a unified waveform framework. Examples include
CE-OFDM
,
DFT
(
DFT
)-spread-
OFDM
, and other hybrid strategies designed to balance dual-function performance
[
14
,
15
]
.

OFDM
, as used in
LTE
(
LTE
),
5G-NR
(
5G-NR
), DVB, and Wi-Fi, remains the dominant communication waveform due to its flexible multicarrier structure, cyclic prefixing for
ISI
(
ISI
) mitigation, and
FFT
(
FFT
)-based signal processing. Its frequency-domain representation facilitates sensing through delay–Doppler analysis using 2D FFT. However,
OFDM
suffers from critical drawbacks: most notably, its inherently high
PAPR
—often in excess of 12 dB—requires operating the power amplifier in a back-off region, substantially degrading energy efficiency. This inefficiency is particularly problematic in mmWave and THz bands where hardware linearity and thermal constraints limit achievable output power
[
16
]
.

To address these limitations, constant envelope waveforms have gained significant attention.
CE-OFDM
modulates the phase of
OFDM
signals to achieve 0 dB
PAPR
, permitting fully saturated
PA
(
PA
) operation and improving energy efficiency. However,
CE-OFDM
is sensitive to phase-unwrapping errors, especially under low
SNR
, which hinders accurate Doppler estimation. Similarly,
PMCW
and DFT-s-
OFDM
exhibit restricted flexibility:
PMCW
struggles with Doppler spread, while DFT-s-
OFDM
limits dynamic spectrum access due to its fixed transform structure
[
17
,
18

.

A novel approach that overcomes many of these limitations is
FM-OFDM

[
28
]
. FM-
OFDM
employs instantaneous frequency modulation of a baseband
OFDM
signal to generate a constant-envelope waveform. It preserves the data-bearing subcarrier structure of
OFDM
while ensuring phase continuity across symbols and achieving 0 dB
PAPR
. Importantly, FM-
OFDM
introduces a tunable modulation index
m
, which controls the frequency deviation and thus influences autocorrelation peak width, resolution, and Doppler robustness. It offers a spectrum of operational trade-offs, enabling designers to prioritize communication quality or sensing precision depending on scenario requirements. Furthermore, FM-
OFDM
’s compatibility with existing
OFDM
-based receivers simplifies integration and reduces implementation cost, especially for dual-mode hardware.

Despite these theoretical advantages, FM-
OFDM
remains relatively underexplored in the literature compared to other
ISAC
waveforms. While some recent works have analyzed its fundamental properties and potential benefits, comprehensive studies addressing practical challenges such as Doppler-induced
ICI
, spectral leakage, hardware implementation, and performance under realistic channel conditions are still limited. In particular, there is a lack of detailed quantitative evaluations of FM-
OFDM
’s
ISAC
trade-offs. This paper aims to address these gaps by providing both theoretical analysis and simulation results for FM-
OFDM
in representative
ISAC
contexts.

Beyond wireless terrestrial networks, FM-
OFDM
holds promise in high-mobility and power-constrained environments such as low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite communications, high-speed trains, and drone swarms, where waveform resilience and
PA
efficiency are paramount. Its low
PAPR
enables saturation-mode transmission even in miniature platforms, while its frequency agility permits flexible spectrum coexistence. Additionally, FM-
OFDM
aligns with emerging machine-learning-based receiver architectures, offering smooth, differentiable phase trajectories suitable for neural demodulators
[
29
]
.

From a theoretical standpoint, the smooth phase evolution of FM-
OFDM
minimizes the ambiguity-function sidelobes in both range and Doppler, thereby enhancing resolution. This property makes FM-
OFDM
compatible with matched-filtering approaches and supports derivation of tighter Cramér–Rao bounds (CRBs) for sensing accuracy. Moreover, the modulation index
m
acts as a physical hyperparameter, enabling dynamic sensing adaptation in reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS)-assisted or cell-free MIMO systems
[
30
]
.

Furthermore, FM-
OFDM
’s constant-envelope nature and phase continuity simplify analog implementations and relax linearity requirements in
DAC
(
DAC
)s and mixers. This leads to reduced hardware cost and better scalability for dense deployments. In high-speed scenarios, where channel coherence time is limited, FM-
OFDM
’s robustness to Doppler shift makes it suitable for reliable tracking and prediction.

Relative to recent literature, FM-
OFDM
combines a
PA
-friendly constant envelope (as in
FMCW
/
PMCW
) with an
OFDM
-compatible data plane; exposes a tunable sensing knob via the modulation index
m
; and mitigates the phase-unwrapping fragility of CE-
OFDM
through phase continuity across symbols, while aligning with emerging standardization in IEEE 802.11bf and 3GPP Rel-19 for
ISAC

[

,

CONTRIBUTIONS

This work undertakes an end-to-end treatment of FM-
OFDM
for
ISAC
, covering waveform synthesis, interaction with doubly dispersive channels, sensing-receiver design in the discriminator domain, and rigorous benchmarking against CP-
OFDM
and CE-
OFDM
. We establish an analytical framework that ties channel time–frequency selectivity to estimator behavior, quantify CFO de-meaning bias/variance and

i.

We present a comprehensive analytical framework for FM-
OFDM
waveform generation and its propagation characteristics in doubly dispersive wireless channels, rigorously modeling both time and frequency selectivity effects relevant to
ISAC
scenarios.

ii.

We develop a unified sensing receiver in the discriminator domain and quantify CFO de-meaning bias/variance and
ICI
bounds under weight dynamics.

iii.

We conduct an extensive comparative performance evaluation of FM-
OFDM
, CP-
OFDM
, and CE-
OFDM
waveforms, analyzing key metrics such as

,
RMSE
(

iv.

We provide detailed visualizations and interpretations of range–Doppler maps, systematically investigating how the modulation index
m
in FM-
OFDM
influences sensing resolution, target separability, and the trade-off between spectral efficiency and estimation accuracy.

IV-A

BER PERFORMANCE IN FLAT FADING

Figure

CP-OFDM achieves the lowest BER across the SNR range.
We expected this since CP-OFDM is a linear modulation scheme with perfectly orthogonal subcarriers assuming no Doppler, so there is no distortion from nonlinear frequency modulation or phase unwrapping.
Its detection can be implemented with simple FFT demodulation, which is optimal under Gaussian noise.

In contrast, CE-OFDM and FM-OFDM use constant-envelope frequency modulation, which requires nonlinear demodulation.
This process is more sensitive to noise: additive Gaussian noise perturbs the phase trajectory, and errors in phase unwrapping can propagate, especially at high SNR where residual phase errors dominate.
FM-OFDM slightly outperforms CE-OFDM here due to the continuous phase evolution across
OFDM
symbols, which reduces phase discontinuities and improves correlation stability.

Future work could explore adaptive modulation index strategies that dynamically balance sensing and communication requirements based on real-time SNR, regulatory constraints, and application priorities. Additionally, investigating hardware-aware waveform optimization and joint resource allocation for ISAC systems remains an open research direction.

V
CONCLUSION

In this paper we presented a detailed theoretical and simulation-based investigation of FM-OFDM as a unified waveform candidate for 6G Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC). By deriving rigorous analytical input-output relationships suitable for realistic mobility scenarios and doubly dispersive channel conditions, we demonstrated that FM-OFDM effectively addresses key limitations of conventional CP-OFDM and CE-OFDM waveforms, particularly in Doppler-dominant environments. Simulation results confirmed that the constant-envelope characteristic and frequency-modulation structure inherent to FM-OFDM significantly enhance resilience against phase distortions, enabling precise and robust range and velocity estimation. The modulation index
m
emerged as a crucial tunable parameter, with higher modulation indices significantly improving sensing accuracy while introducing manageable spectral efficiency trade-offs.

Future research directions will further explore adaptive modulation index strategies, dynamically optimizing FM-OFDM waveforms according to real-time sensing and communication requirements, spectral constraints, and channel conditions. Practical implementations will also require addressing hardware constraints and interference management to realize the full potential of FM-OFDM in next-generation high-frequency ISAC applications.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\!\{z^{*}\dot{z}\}

\dot{\Theta}(t)=\sum_{p=0}^{P-1}\beta_{p}(t)\,\dot{\theta}_{p}(t),\qquad\beta_{p}(t)\triangleq\frac{S_{p}}{S},\quad\sum_{p}\beta_{p}(t)=1.

(43)

When the phasors are not spread over angles exceeding
\pm 90^{\circ}
(dominant-path/weak-sidelobe regime),
\beta_{p}(t)\geq 0
.

For FM-OFDM, each path phase is

\displaystyle\theta_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=\arg a_{p}+2\pi\nu_{p}t+\phi(t-\tau_{p}),

(44)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=2\pi\big(\nu_{p}+mf_{\Delta}\,x(t-\tau_{p})\,\big)

(45)

where
x(t)
denotes the continuous-time counterpart of the discrete-time sequence
x[n]
in (
1
).
Hence (
43
) yields

\dot{\Theta}(t)=2\pi\sum_{p}\beta_{p}(t)\big(\nu_{p}+mf_{\Delta}x(t-\tau_{p})\big).

(46)

\widehat{f}[n]=\frac{1}{2\pi T_{s}}\angle\!\big(z[n]z^{*}[n-1]\big)

<\pi
(Assumption A2). Replacing

by

\widehat{f}[n]\approx\sum_{p}\beta_{p}[n]\big(\nu_{p}+mf_{\Delta}x[n-\ell_{p}]\big).

(47)

Multiplying by
K_{V}
gives (
11

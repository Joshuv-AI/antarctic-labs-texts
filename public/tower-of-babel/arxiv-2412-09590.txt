Dissipative measure-valued solutions and weak-strong uniqueness for the Euler alignment system
A. Chaudhary, U. Koley, E. Wiedemann

Abstract
We introduce the concept of a dissipative measure-valued solution to the Euler alignment system. This approach incorporates a modified total energy balance, utilizing a binary tensor Young measure. The central finding is a weak (measure-valued)–strong uniqueness principle: if both a dissipative measure-valued solution and a classical smooth solution originate from the same initial data, they will be identical as long as the classical solution exists.



Key words:
Euler alignment system; Measure-valued solution; Weak–strong uniqueness; Binary tensor Young measure; Density dependent viscosity.

1
Introduction

The study of hydrodynamic models originating from the field of self-organization has garnered significant attention in recent years, leading to new challenges in partial differential equations that pose intriguing questions for the mathematical community. In this paper, we are interested in the following Euler alignment system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho)=\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}[\varrho,{\bf u}]\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
(\varrho(0),{\bf u}(0))=(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0})\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\lambda\in(0,1)
is a fixed number,
\varrho
is the density,
{\bf u}
is the velocity,
p(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma}
is the given pressure function with
\gamma\geq 2
. Moreover,
\mathbb{T}^{d}
denotes the
d
-dimensional periodic torus
[0,2\pi]^{d}
, with endpoints identified for
d=1,2,3
. Finally, the commutator
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}[\varrho,{\bf u}]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}[\varrho,{\bf u}](t,x):=\varrho(t,x)\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}[\varrho(t,\cdot){\bf u}(t,\cdot)](x)-\varrho(t,x){\bf u}(t,x)\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}[\varrho(t,\cdot)](x),

(1.2)

where the fractional Laplacian
\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}[\cdot]
of order
\lambda\in(0,1)
defined on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}[\varphi](x)=[(-\Delta)^{\lambda}{\mathbf{\varphi}}](x):=-\text{P.V}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}({\mathbf{\varphi}}(x)-{\mathbf{\varphi}}(y))\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y)dy,

for a sufficiently regular function
\varphi
. Here the singular kernel of the fractional Laplacian given by

\kappa_{\lambda}(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-2n\pi|^{d+2\lambda}},\qquad 0<\lambda<1.

The Euler alignment system (
1.1
) is a model that effectively captures the collective behavior observed in animal swarms. In the past decade, this system has garnered increasing attention in the literature, particularly concerning its global well-posedness and the long-term flocking behavior, see
[
28
]
.

The commutator (
1.2
) with the singular kernel contributes to the system’s dissipative nature. Specifically, when enforcing
\varrho\equiv 1
, the alignment term transforms into a fractional Laplacian, which induces a regularizing effect on the solution. The corresponding system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf u}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}=(-\Delta)^{\lambda}{\bf u}

(1.3)

is recognized as the fractal Burgers equation. This equation has been comprehensively analyzed in
[
20
]
, where global well-posedness is demonstrated for
\lambda\in[1/2,1)
. In contrast, for
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
, solutions are prone to forming shocks. The regularization effect of
(
1.2
)
was investigated in
[
25
,
26
,
14
]
for a one-dimensional periodic domain
\mathbb{T}
, assuming the absence of pressure. In
[
14
]
, a notable finding is the global well-posedness of the system for any

\lambda\in(0,1)
. Specifically, when
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
, unlike in
(
1.3
)
, the regularization effect of
(
1.2
)
is sufficiently strong to guarantee global regularity for all smooth initial data.

The theory of the pressureless Euler alignment system becomes increasingly complex in higher dimensions. Global well-posedness has predominantly been established for small initial data that are perturbed around
\rho\equiv 1
. Notable recent results include Shvydkoy’s work
[
27
]
, which addresses the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and the study by Danchin et al.
[
11
]
, which focuses on the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
. Global regularity for general initial data is proven for uni-directional flows
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
with
\lambda\in[1/2,1)
.

The global well-posedness theory for the Euler-alignment system (
1.1
) with pressure is much less understood compared to the pressureless system. For the one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
, Constantin et al.
[
10
]
established a global well-posedness theory for sufficiently large
\lambda\in(5/6,1)
. Their result necessitates the inclusion of an additional strong local dissipation term of the form
(\mu(\rho)u_{x})_{x}
in the system. In higher dimensions, existing global regularity results typically depend on smallness conditions applied to the initial data. For
\lambda\in(0,1)
, Chen et al.
[
9
]
demonstrated global well-posedness for smooth initial data under smallness assumptions in the spatial domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

For the Euler-alignment system (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the authors in
[
2
]
investigated global well-posedness for small initial data within a suitable critical Besov space, specifically when
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
. Recently, in
[
3
]
the authors studied the Cauchy problem of the compressible Euler system with strongly singular velocity alignment. They established a global well-posedness theory for the system with small smooth initial data for
\lambda\in(0,1/2]
. Global regularity for general large initial data remains a challenging open problem.

Březina and Mácha analyzed the equations governing a viscous approximation of the generalized compressible Euler system in
[
6
]
. They demonstrated that, as viscosity approaches zero, the dissipative measure-valued solutions converge to a strong solution of the Euler system, provided such a strong solution exists. In recent work
[
7
]
, Carrillo et al. studied several pressureless variants of the compressible Euler equation driven by nonlocal repulsion-attraction and alignment forces with Poisson interaction. Under an energy admissibility criterion, they established the existence of global measure-valued solutions.

1.1
Scope and outline of the paper

The concept of measure-valued solutions to partial differential equations was introduced by DiPerna in the context of conservation laws (see
[
12
]
). By employing Young measures, he successfully handled the passage to the artificial viscosity limit. In specific cases, such as the scalar setting, he also demonstrated a posteriori that these measure-valued solutions are atomic, implying they coincide with distributional solutions. For general systems of conservation laws, obtaining distributional (or entropy) solutions is often impractical or impossible. Therefore, measure-valued solutions or related frameworks become essential. In fluid dynamics, the existence of measure-valued solutions has been established for various models (
[
13
,
18
,
24
,
15
]
), highlighting their significance in addressing complex systems.

We aim to investigate the Euler alignment system (
1.1
) with pressure for generic initial data. We develop a suitable mathematical framework for
dissipative measure-valued
solutions to the Euler alignment system (
1.1
), with the goal of establishing a weak (measure-valued)–strong uniqueness principle. Notably, our solution framework relies solely on natural energy bounds and a uniform lower bound on density related to approximate solutions.

In this work, we focus on measure-valued solutions because they can capture a broad class of approximate problems, including systems with higher-order viscosities and specific numerical schemes. These approaches naturally produce measure-valued solutions, even in cases where proving convergence to a weak solution is difficult or remains unresolved. Motivated by this, we introduce the notion of a (dissipative) measure-valued solution for equation (
1.1
).

The key novelty of our work is the successful identification, in the limit, of the non-local term (
1.2
), which is crucial for the framework of
dissipative measure-valued
solutions (see Definition
2.3
) and proving the weak (measure-valued)–strong uniqueness principle (see Theorem
2.2
). In the case of a non-local term (
1.2
), the standard approach of identifying non-linear terms using a Young measure is not directly effective. To address this, we used the concept of a binary tensor Young measure, see Definition
2.2
, which ensures the accurate identification of the non-local term in the limit. It turns out that establishing a compatibility condition (see (
2.11
)) and the core proprieties of a binary tensor Young measure, which are inherently satisfied by any measure derived from a well-behaved sequence of approximate solutions to (
1.1
) (see Theorem
2.1
), is enough to ensure weak-strong uniqueness. As a result, we bypass the cumbersome notation used in the framework of Alibert and Bouchitté
[
1
]
and provide a more comprehensive definition of
dissipative measure-valued
solutions that still adheres to the weak-strong uniqueness principle.

This paper’s central objective is to establish weak-strong uniqueness for dissipative measure-valued solutions to (
1.1
) as defined in Definition
2.3
. Weak-strong uniqueness guarantees that classical solutions are stable within the more general framework of dissipative measure-valued solutions. Notably, we present the first proof of weak-strong uniqueness for measure-valued solutions of the Euler alignment model (
1.1
).

We also identify a wide range of problems that generate dissipative measure-valued solutions, including density-dependent viscosity equations, which are outside the scope of current weak solution theory. In addition, we observe that certain numerical schemes are consistent with this approach, aligning with the viewpoint of Fjordholm et al.
[
16
]
. They contend that for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws, dissipative measure-valued solutions provide a more suitable framework than weak entropy solutions. This is because dissipative measure-valued solutions can be derived as limits of typical numerical approximations, a feat not possible with weak entropy solutions.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
presents the key mathematical and technical frameworks, followed by a discussion of solution concepts and the main results. Section
3
demonstrates the convergence of approximate solutions and proves the existence of
dissipative measure-valued
solutions to (
1.1
). Finally, in Section
4
, we establish a weak (measure-valued)-strong uniqueness principle for (
1.1
), based on a suitable relative energy inequality for the Euler alignment system.

2
Mathematical framework and main results

We start with a brief review of the key mathematical tools essential for the subsequent analysis, followed by a presentation of the paper’s main results. Throughout, the symbol C denotes generic constants that may vary from one step to another in the proofs. While it is theoretically possible to track these constants explicitly, this would be overly cumbersome, so we omit such details for clarity.

2.1
Weak solution to the regularized system

We will approximate a
dissipative measure-valued
solution to (
1.1
) as a limit of viscous dissipative weak solutions to the following regularized system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho_{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})=0\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T],\\
\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}[\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}]-\varepsilon\Delta^{2m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,T],\\
(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(0),{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(0))=(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{0,\varepsilon})\qquad&\text{in}\,\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{0,\varepsilon})
is given by
\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}=\varrho_{0}*g_{\varepsilon}
,
{\bf u}_{0,\varepsilon}={\bf u}*{g}_{\varepsilon}
, and
g_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}g(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
with a standard mollifier
g
on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

For sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
\varepsilon>0
, modulo cosmetic changes, the existence of a unique smooth solution to the regularized system (
2.1
) can be established; see, for example,
[
19
,
8
]
for a detailed discussion. Next, we introduce the weak formulation associated with the regularized system (
2.1
). This is essential because, in the final steps of proving the existence of a dissipative measure-valued solution, we will need to pass to the limit in this weak formulation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0})
be an initial data such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+P(\varrho_{0})\right){\rm d}{x}\,\textless\,\infty
. We call
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\times L^{2}([0,T];H^{2m}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
a dissipative weak solution to (
2.1
) if
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})
satisfies the following conditions:

•

Equation of continuity.

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\psi(\tau,x){\rm d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\psi(0,x){\rm d}{x}\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\Big[\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x)\Big]}{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\end{split}

(2.2)

holds for every
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\tau\in[0,T]
.

•

Momentum equation.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)\cdot{\mathbf{\varphi}}(\tau,x){\rm d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}(x){\bf u}_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\cdot{\mathbf{\varphi}}(0,x){\rm d}{x}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta^{m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\Delta^{m}\varphi(t,x){\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\Big[\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}+\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)):\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}+p(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)){\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}\Big]}{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,y){({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,y))({\mathbf{\varphi}}(t,x)-{\mathbf{\varphi}}(t,y))}\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y){\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t,

(2.3)

holds for every
{\mathbf{\varphi}}\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{2m}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mbox{\F R}^{d}))
and
\tau\in[0,T]
.

•

Energy inequality.

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}+P(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x))\right){\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta^{m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,y){|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t,y)|^{2}}\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y){\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}(x)|{\bf u}_{0,\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}+\ P(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}(x))\right){\rm d}{x},\end{split}

(2.4)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
, where the pressure potential
P
is given by
P(\varrho)=\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}
.

2.2
Binary tensor Young measure

Given the absence of well-posedness results for weak solutions to (
1.1
), there is significant potential for developing alternative solution frameworks for equation (
1.1
). One such framework involves measure-valued solutions, where the solutions sought are not functions but rather space-time parameterized probability measures on the state space. Motivated by the concept of statistical solutions
[
16
,
17
]
, we introduce the notion of a binary tensor Young measure. Given a topological space
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}(X)
the space of all probability measures on the Borel sigma-algebra of
X
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}(X)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}^{+}(X)
) the space of all measures (resp. positive measures) on the Borel sigma-algebra of
X
. For convenience we set
U=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
.

Definition 2.2

(Binary tensor Young measure)
.

Let
\nu:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathcal{P}(U)
be a weak*-measurable mapping, meaning that for every
f\in C_{0}(U)
, the function
(t,x)\mapsto\langle\nu_{t,x},f\rangle
, mapping
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
into
\mathbb{R}
, is Borel measurable. In other words,
\nu
is a Young measure. The binary tensor Young measure, denoted by
\nu^{2}_{t,x,y}
, is defined as the product measure
\nu_{t,x}\otimes\nu_{t,y}
.

It is easy to see that, if
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mbox{\F R}^{k})
generates the Young measure
\nu
, then the sequence given by
(x,y)\mapsto(u_{n}(x),u_{n}(y))\in(\mbox{\F R}^{k})^{2}
generates the tensorized Young measure
\nu^{2}_{x,y}(dz_{1}dz_{2})=\nu_{x}(dz_{1})\nu_{y}(dz_{2})
. We now collect some useful properties of binary tensor Young measure.

Remark 2.1

(Properties of binary tensor Young measure)
.

A binary tensor Young measure
\nu_{t,x,y}^{2}=\nu_{t,x}\otimes\nu_{t,y}
satisfies the following properties:

1.

Symmetry:
If
f\in C_{0}(U^{2})
, then

\displaystyle\left<\nu_{t,x,y}^{2};f(s,{\bf v},s^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime})\right>=\left<\nu_{t,y,x}^{2};f(s^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime},s,{\bf v})\right>

for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

2.

Consistency:
If
f\in C_{0}(U^{2})
is of the form
f(s,{\bf v},s^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime})=g(s,{\bf v})
, for some
g\in C_{0}(U)
, then

\displaystyle\left<\nu_{t,x,y}^{2};f\right>=\left<\nu_{t,x};g\right>

for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

In view of the above properties it is clear that
\nu_{t,x,y}^{2}
contains all informations about
\nu_{t,x}
, but not vice-versa.

2.3
Dissipative measure-valued solution

Now we are in a position to introduce the concept of a
dissipative measure-valued solution
of the Euler alignment system (
1.1
).

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a pair
[\nu,\mathcal{D}]

is a dissipative measure-valued solution of the Euler alignment system (
1.1
), in
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
with the given initial data
\nu_{0}
, if the following conditions hold:

•

Young measure.

\nu:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathcal{P}(U)
is a weak*-measurable mapping, meaning that for every
f\in C_{0}(U)
, the function
(t,x)\mapsto\langle\nu_{t,x},f\rangle
, mapping
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
into
\mathbb{R}
, is Borel measurable. In other words,
\nu
is a Young measure.

•

L^{\infty}
-boundedness.

\nu_{t,x}
satisfies the following bounds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x};|s|^{\gamma}+s|{\bf v}|^{2}\right>dx\,<\,\infty.

(2.5)

•

Dissipation defect.
The dissipation defect satisfies

\mathcal{D}\in L^{\infty}(0,T),\ \mathcal{D}\geq 0.

•

Equation of continuity.

There exists a measure
r^{C}\in L^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
such that for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
and for every
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\left|\langle r^{C}(\tau);\nabla_{x}\psi\rangle\right|\leq\chi(\tau)\mathcal{D}(\tau)\|\psi\|_{C^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}

(2.6)

and

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\langle\nu_{\tau,x};s\rangle\psi(\tau,\cdot)}{\rm d}x&-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\langle(\nu_{0})_{x};s\rangle\psi(0,\cdot)}{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\Big[\langle\nu_{t,x};s\rangle\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+\langle\nu_{t,x};s{\bf v}\rangle\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x)\Big]}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle r^{C};\nabla_{x}\psi\rangle\ {\rm d}t.\end{split}

(2.7)

•

Momentum equation.

There exists a measure
r^{M}\in L^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
such that for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
and for every
{\mathbf{\varphi}}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left|\langle r^{M}(\tau);\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle\right|\leq\xi(\tau)\mathcal{D}(\tau)\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}

(2.8)

and

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}{\langle\nu_{\tau,x};s{\bf v}\rangle\cdot{\mathbf{\varphi}}(\tau,\cdot)}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\langle(\nu_{0})_{x};s{\bf v}\rangle\cdot{\mathbf{\varphi}}(0,\cdot)}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\Big[\langle\nu_{t,x};s{\bf v}\rangle\cdot\partial_{t}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}+\langle\nu_{t,x};{s{\bf v}\otimes{\bf v}}\rangle:\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}+\langle\nu_{t,x};p(s)\rangle{\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{\varphi}(t,x)}\Big]}dx\ {\rm d}t\\
&-\frac{1}{2}{\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x,y}^{2};ss^{\prime}{({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{\prime})({\mathbf{\varphi}}(t,x)-{\mathbf{\varphi}}(t,y))}{\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y)}\right>{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\left<{r}^{M};\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{\varphi}}\right>{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(2.9)

•

Energy inequality.
The following energy inequality holds:

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left<\nu_{\tau,x};\left(\frac{1}{2}{s|{\bf v}|^{2}}+P(s)\right)\right>}{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}&+\frac{1}{2}{\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x,y}^{2};{ss^{\prime}|{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{\prime}|^{2}}\right>\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y){\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t}\\
+\mathcal{D}(\tau)&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left<(\nu_{0})_{x};\left(\frac{1}{2}{s|{\bf v}|^{2}}+P(s)\right)\right>}{\rm d}{x}\end{split}

(2.10)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

•

Compatibility conditions.
If
\mathbf{U}\in C([0,T];C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, then the following condition holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x};|\big({\bf v}-\bar{{\bf v}}(t)\big)-\big(\mathbf{U}(t,x)-\bar{{\bf U}}(t)\big)|^{2}\right>{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x,y}^{2};ss^{\prime}|({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{\prime})-(\mathbf{U}(t,x)-\mathbf{U}(t,y))|^{2}\kappa_{\lambda}(x-y)\right>{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}{y}{\rm d}t+C_{\lambda}\mathcal{D}(\tau)

(2.11)

for a.a.
\tau\in[0,T]
, for some positive constant
C_{\lambda}
, where
\bar{{\bf v}}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x},{\bf v}\right>{\rm d}x
and
\bar{{\bf U}}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\bf U}(t,x){\rm d}x
.

•

Support of
\nu
.
There exists a constant
c_{\varrho}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{supp}(\nu_{t,x,y}^{2})\subset\{(s,{\bf v},s^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime}):s,s^{\prime}\geq c_{\varrho}>0\,\&\,{\bf v},{\bf v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

(2.12)

and

\displaystyle\mbox{supp}(\nu_{t,x})\subset\{(s,{\bf v}):s\geq c_{\varrho}>0\,\&\,{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

(2.13)

for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that (
2.5
) and (
2.13
) give that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left<\nu_{t,x};|{\bf v}|^{2}\right>{\rm d}x<\infty.

(2.14)

2.4
Main results

We now present the main results of this paper. First, concerning the convergence of the regularized system (
2.1
) to a dissipative weak solution to (
1.1
), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
Convergence to a
dissipative measure-valued
solution
)
.

Let
(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0})
be an initial data such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+P(\varrho_{0})\right){\rm d}{x}\,\textless\,\infty
. Let
\{(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of viscous dissipative weak solutions to (
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
and assume that there exists a
\underline{\varrho}>0
such that

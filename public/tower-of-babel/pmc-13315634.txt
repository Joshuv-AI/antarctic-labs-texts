Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is one of the leading causes of visual impairment worldwide. Although repackaged intravenous formulation of bevacizumab is widely used off-label as a cost-effective treatment for nAMD, concerns persist regarding sterility, potency variability, dosing inaccuracies and contamination risks.This phase I study was designed to evaluate the safety and biological activity of GBL1204, an ophthalmic-grade bevacizumab formulation supplied in a sterile, single-use prefilled syringe (PFS), in patients with nAMD. What was learned from the study? Introduction

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) remains one of the leading causes of visual impairment among the elderly population [1]. The intravitreal administration of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) agents has become the standard of care for managing nAMD [2, 3]. Among these agents, bevacizumab, a humanized, full-length monoclonal antibody targeting VEGF-A, has demonstrated efficacy and safety comparable to ranibizumab in multiple randomized clinical trials for nAMD [4–11]. As a result, bevacizumab (Avastin®, Genentech Roche, Switzerland) is widely employed off-label in routine clinical practice, despite lacking formal regulatory approval, alongside approved anti-VEGF therapies such as ranibizumab and aflibercept [12].

Despite its widespread use, concerns remain about the use of repackaged intravenous formulation of bevacizumab, originally approved for oncologic indications, in retinal disorders [13]. Typically, multiple single-dose plastic syringes are compounded from a single vial, a process that may not consistently meet stringent quality standards for repackaged products [14]. Furthermore, the United States Pharmacopeia (USP) < 789 > outlines strict specifications for particulate matter in ophthalmic solutions, requiring that such formulations contain no more than 50 particles/mL measuring ≥ 10 µm, no more than 5 particles/mL measuring ≥ 25 µm, and not more than 2 particles/mL measuring ≥ 50 µm [15].

Current use of repackaged bevacizumab for ophthalmic administration is associated with significant challenges, including variable potency, increased sub-visible particulate matter, and risk of contamination [16–18]. Improper handling or storage of compounded bevacizumab can lead to adverse events (AEs) such as infectious endophthalmitis and elevated intraocular pressure [19]. Moreover, silicone oil droplets may be introduced during repackaging into the plastic syringes posing further ocular risks [20, 21]. These limitations highlight an unmet need for a single-use ophthalmic bevacizumab to minimize contamination risk, reduce dosing errors, enhance safety and align with best practices for ophthalmic drug delivery [22].

GBL1204 (Gennova Biopharmaceuticals Limited) addresses this clinical need and represents a key innovation: a single-use, ophthalmic-grade bevacizumab formulation manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) and supplied in a specialised glass prefilled syringe (PFS). The objectives of this study were to (1) evaluate sub-visible particulate matter in GBL1204 in accordance with the USP < 789 > acceptance criteria (2) assess the safety and biological activity of a single intravitreal injection of GBL1204 in patients with nAMD.

Methods

Device Description

The GBL1204 PFS is a sterile, single-use delivery system designed specifically for intravitreal administration. It features a syringe cap, a Luer lock for secure needle attachment, a 0.05-mL dose mark, a grey bromobutyl rubber stopper, a finger grip, and a plunger rod (Fig. 1). Each PFS contains 0.140 mL of solution to facilitate air removal and enable delivery of a fixed dose of 1.25 mg in 0.05 mL when the top rib of rubber stopper is aligned with the dose mark. For administration, ophthalmologists or trained healthcare professionals must attach a sterile 30-gauge × 1/2-inch injection needle to the Luer lock prior to use. The PFS features an internal cross-linked silicone oil coating bonded to the glass. This coating minimizes drug–container interactions and reduces the potential of migration of inorganic extractable components into the formulation. The coating also ensures low break-loose force and minimal friction (glide force), facilitating smooth and consistent plunger movement during intravitreal injection.

Sub-visible Particulate and Aggregate Assessment

Sub-visible particulate matter in GBL1204 was measured using light obscuration in accordance with USP < 787 > [23], with acceptance thresholds applied based on the USP < 789 > guidance [15]. Protein aggregate content was assessed using size-exclusion chromatography. Samples were analysed on a TSK-Gel SuperSW column with a potassium phosphate buffer (pH 6.2) serving as the mobile phase. Monomer and aggregate species were separated based on hydrodynamic size and detected at 280 nm.

Study Design and Participants

This was a prospective, multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase I study conducted from March to June 2024 at five centres across India. The study evaluated the ocular safety and biological activity of a single intravitreal injection of GBL1204 in participants with nAMD. The study comprised a screening period (up to 10 days), enrolment (and treatment) and a 28-day follow-up. The trial enrolled patients of either sex aged ≥ 50 years with confirmed diagnosis of nAMD in the study eye and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) score using Early Treatment of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) charts between 20/40 and 20/320 (Snellen equivalent). The diagnosis of nAMD was confirmed by fundus fluorescein angiography. A complete list of eligibility criteria is provided in the Supplementary Material (Table S1). The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki 1964 and its later amendments, International Conference on Harmonization- Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP), as well as applicable regulatory requirements. The central licensing authority (Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation) and institutional ethics committee at each site approved the study prior to its initiation. A list of study sites and their respective ethics committees is provided in the Supplementary Material (Table S2). All participants provided written informed consent before enrolment in the study.

Study Treatment

GBL1204 was administered as a single intravitreal injection at a dose of 1.25 mg in 0.05 mL using a PFS in one eye, consistent with routine clinical practice. Vital signs were monitored at 30 min post injection. Patients were observed for at least 2 h, ocular examination was performed, intraocular pressure (IOP) recorded, and patients were then discharged at the discretion of the investigator. Participants received diaries to record AEs and concomitant medications. The follow-up assessments were performed on days 7 and 28, with a check by telephone on day 14. Patients self-administered ophthalmic broad-spectrum antimicrobial drops as prescribed by the investigators. At study completion, patients were transitioned to the standard of care as per the assessment by the investigator.

Study Objectives and Assessments

The primary objective was to evaluate the ocular safety of an intravitreal injection of GBL1204 using PFS at day 7. The secondary objectives included assessment of safety parameters, changes in BCVA score, and central macular thickness (CMT) at days 7 and 28. An exploratory objective comprised of measurement of the serum VEGF levels at enrolment and days 7 and 28.

The safety evaluation included the incidence and severity of ocular and non-ocular treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), and serious adverse events (SAEs). Adverse event severity was graded according to the National Cancer Institute—Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE, version 5.0). Other safety assessments included clinical laboratory findings (e.g. hematology, serum chemistry and urinalysis), vital signs (blood pressure, pulse rate, body temperature and respiratory rate), electrocardiograms, concomitant medications and comprehensive ocular examination [IOP measurement, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, assessment using spectral domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) and fundus photography]. AEs were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, version 27) and identified by using their preferred terms. AEs were assessed for their relatedness by the study investigator. Secondary endpoints involved assessment of BCVA using ETDRS charts and changes in CMT by OCT. Serum VEGF levels were measured using a validated sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, Vector Best, Russian Federation) with detection limits of 51 to 2000 pg/mL.

Statistical Analysis

No formal calculations were performed for determining the sample size. Sample size was deemed sufficient to evaluate the safety profile and biological activity of GBL1204 in patients with nAMD. All enrolled patients who received GBL1024 were included in the safety analysis set. The biological activity (efficacy) was analysed in participants with available post-baseline data. All safety, secondary and exploratory parameters were reported using descriptive/inferential statistics. Data following a normal distribution were analysed using the paired t test, whereas non-normally distributed (asymmetrical) data were assessed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The reported P values should be interpreted in an exploratory manner only. No imputations were performed for the missing data. The data analyses were performed using R software version 4.3.2 and Graphpad Prism version 10.5.

Results

Sub-visible Particulates and Protein Aggregates

Across four independently manufactured batches, GBL1204 demonstrated sub-visible particulate levels (≥ 10, ≥ 25 and ≥ 50 µm) well below the acceptance criteria specified in USP < 789 > (Fig. 2). The mean (standard deviation, SD) levels were 12 (9.83) and 1 (0) for ≥ 10 µm and ≥ 25 µm particulates respectively. No particulates ≥ 50 µm were detected in the analysed batches. In terms of protein aggregates, GBL1204 demonstrated markedly lower levels [mean (SD) 1.20% (0.21)] compared with the reference bevacizumab (Avastin®), which exhibited higher aggregate content [mean (SD) 3.16% (0.45)] (Fig. 3).

Patient Population and Baseline Characteristics

Between March and May 2024, 16 patients with nAMD were enrolled across five centres in India. Safety follow-up through 4 weeks was completed for 15 patients; one participant withdrew consent because of inability to attend study site visit. Each patient received a single injection of GBL1204 in one eye (hereafter referred as study eye). The baseline demographics and ocular characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age of the patients was 77 years (range 58–82), mean (SD) BCVA score was 41.81 letters (11.35), mean (SD) CMT was 343.69 µm (139.56), and the mean (SD) IOP was 13.62 mmHg (2.39). Ten patients were treatment-naïve, while six had received anti-VEGF treatment at ≥ 3 months prior to enrolment.

Safety and Tolerability

GBL1204 was safe and well tolerated by the patients. No SAEs (ocular or systemic), ocular inflammation or unexpected TEAEs related to the intravitreal injection were observed. A total of 14 AEs (13 ocular AEs and 1 non-ocular AE-headache) occurred in 9 (56.25%) patients over 4 weeks. Six patients (37.50%) experienced one AE, 1 (6.25%) and 2 (12.50%) patients experienced two, and three AEs respectively. The most common ocular AEs were eye pain and lacrimation, observed in 5 patients each (31.25%, 95% CI 11.02–58.66), followed by vitreous floaters in 2 (12.5%, 95% CI 1.55–38.35) and photopsia in 1 (6.25%, 95% CI 0.16–30.23) patient (Table 2). Of the 14 AEs, 8 AEs (57.14%) were classified as mild whereas 6 AEs (42.86%) were considered as moderate; all resolved without sequelae.

No clinically relevant increase in IOP was observed at 2 h post injection. Best-Corrected Visual Activity

Mean BCVA ETDRS letter score improved from baseline at both day 7 [mean (range) 44.73 letters (30–70)] and day 28 [mean (range) 45.47 letters (30–74)]. The mean (SEM) improvement from baseline was + 1.80 (0.76) letters at day 7 and + 3.0 (1.10) letters at day 28 (P = 0.016, paired t test, Fig. 4), with 93% patients (14/15) experiencing stable or improved vision. A reduction in CMT was observed at both day 7 [mean (range) 294.53 µm (139–603)] and day 28 [mean (range) 283.60 µm (range 127–566)] from baseline. The mean (SEM) reductions from baseline were − 41.67 µm (40.35) and − 64.67 µm (42.74) at days 7 and 28, respectively (Fig. 5). Mean (SD) serum VEGF decreased from baseline [382.92 (256.94) pg/mL] to day 7 [128.52 (100.58) pg/mL] and day 28 [223.60 (187.44) pg/mL] (Fig. S2).

Discussion

This phase I study demonstrated that GBL1204, an ophthalmic-grade bevacizumab formulation delivered in a sterile, single-use PFS, is safe and well tolerated in patients with nAMD. No cases of intraocular inflammation, clinically significant changes in vital signs or laboratory values, systemic or ocular serious adverse events, were observed. As expected, transient ocular events (such as eye pain) were noted and were consistent with procedural effects of intravitreal anti-VEGF injections. GBL1204 administration resulted in clinically meaningful improvements in BCVA score and reductions in CMT in patients. The mean BCVA gain (+ 3 letters) was comparable to the outcomes reported in the bevacizumab arms of GEFAL, MANTA and BRAMD trials at 4 weeks [7, 8, 10]. Importantly, GBL1204 met USP < 789 > standards for sub-visible particulates and exhibited lower aggregate content compared to commercially available bevacizumab, emphasizing its quality. The systemic VEGF suppression observed at days 7 and 28 was consistent with the earlier reported pharmacokinetic profile of bevacizumab in patients with nAMD [24, 25].

Off-label use of repackaged, unlicensed intravenous formulation of bevacizumab remains widespread as a cost-effective step therapy for nAMD, despite concerns regarding potency, sterility and safety. A study conducted using bevacizumab from five licensed compounding pharmacies in the UK reported an increase in sub-visible particle density over time in the repackaged syringes and the particle distribution beyond the limits specified by the USP < 789 > for ophthalmic injectable products [16]. In another study, 81% of the bevacizumab samples (17/21 repackaged syringes) collected from 11 compounding pharmacies in the USA had lower drug concentrations in compounded formulations than source bevacizumab obtained directly from the manufacturer [17]. The syringe materials used for bevacizumab storage can also impact drug quality by leading to protein aggregations and contamination with silicone oil microdroplets [18, 26, 27], potentially leading to sub-therapeutic outcomes and increased adverse event risk. Over the years, outbreaks of multiple individual and cluster endophthalmitis cases linked to compounded bevacizumab have been reported from many countries, leading to varying degrees of vision loss [28–33]. Gonzalez et al. [28] described lack of adherence to USP < 797 > sterile-compounding standards [14] and poor aseptic technique during bevacizumab preparation as likely causes for these vision threatening catastrophic events.

From a regulatory perspective, the development of ophthalmic-grade bevacizumab aligns with global efforts to standardize anti-VEGF therapy. Regulatory authorities have emphasized stringent compliance with USP < 789 > particulate standards, sterility assurance and container-closure integrity for ophthalmic biologics. These concerns have necessitated product recalls and led to the suspension of compounded bevacizumab preparation by supplier [34]. Moreover, the Summary of Product Characteristics for bevacizumab (Avastin®) explicitly states that the product is not formulated for intravitreal administration [35], emphasising the regulatory and safety challenges associated with its off-label intravitreal use.

GBL1204 addresses these challenges by providing a ready-to-use, ophthalmic-grade formulation in a sterile PFS, eliminating the need for compounding, reducing contamination risk, allowing for easy administration and addressing the critical safety gaps in current practice. This innovation is particularly relevant for low- and middle-income countries, where infrastructure for sterile compounding is often inadequate. GBL1204 manufactured in a cGMP-adherent facility ensures standardize care, improvement in patient safety and compliance to pharmacopeial standards. The PFS configuration offers practical advantages for intravitreal delivery of ophthalmic injections. A systemic review reported that PFS use was associated with increased procedural efficiency and safer handling versus vial-based intravitreal injections [36]. The reduction in the steps required for dose preparation with PFS may help alleviate the burden of intravitreal therapy administration in busy clinics and potentially decrease risk of endophthalmitis caused by poor aseptic technique [37, 38].

This study had several limitations. The study duration was short and the number of patients enrolled was small, reflecting the phase I design focused on evaluating the safety and biological activity. The study did not include a control arm and was not powered to make statistical comparisons. Nevertheless, the favourable safety profile and biological activity observed supports further clinical development. The study received approval from Indian regulatory authority and aimed to move to pivotal phase III trial. A phase III comparative trial of GBL1204 versus ranibizumab in patients with nAMD has been completed and is expected to provide definitive data on comparative efficacy and safety.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that GBL1204, an ophthalmic-grade bevacizumab formulation presented in a PFS, is safe, well tolerated and biologically active in patients with nAMD. GBL1204 met the stringent USP < 789 > acceptance criteria for sub-visible particulate matter and showed low aggregate levels, highlighting its pharmaceutical quality and suitability for intravitreal administration.

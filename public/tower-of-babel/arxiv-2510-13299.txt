1.
Introduction

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can write
z=(x,y)
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
m
and
n
being two non-negative integers such that
N=m+n\geq 3
. Now, considering the Grushin operator
\Delta\gamma
(
\gamma\geq 0
) defined by
[
15
]

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}u(z)=\Delta_{x}u(z)+(1+\gamma)^{2}|x|^{2\gamma}\Delta_{y}u(z),

where
\Delta_{x}
and
\Delta_{y}
are, respectively, the Laplace operators with respect to the variables
x
and
y
, we observe that it reduces to the Laplace operator for
\gamma=0
. The Grushin operator is a generalization of the degenerate differential operator
[
30
]

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+x\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}
.
\Delta_{\gamma}
belongs to the class of subelliptic operators, intermediate between elliptic and hyperbolic operators (see
[
11
]
).
One of the key properties of this operator is its lack of uniform ellipticity in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, as it is degenerate on the subspace
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The homogeneous norm
d(\cdot)
related to the operator is defined by

d(z)=(|x|^{2(\gamma+1)}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2(\gamma+1)}}

and the anisotropic dilations naturally associated with the Grushin operator are defined as

\tau_{t}(x,y)=\left(tx,\,t^{\gamma+1}y\right)\quad\text{for all}\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0.

It is well known that the operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
is homogeneous of degree two with respect to these dilations, while the norm
d(z)
is homogeneous and of degree one. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\gamma
and the homogeneous dimension
N_{\gamma}=m+(1+\gamma)n
, such that

\Phi(z)=\frac{C}{d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}

is the fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\gamma}
with a singularity at the origin. Throughout this paper we use the following notation,
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
is a ball of radius
r>0
, centered at the origin, respect to the homogeneous norm
d(z)
, i.e.,

B_{r}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,d(z)<r\right\}.

Motivated by the seminal work of Brezis and Nirenberg
[
9
]
, the following critical problem has been extensively studied:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta&u=\lambda u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u&=0&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent,
\Omega
is an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 3
), and
\lambda>0
is a positive parameter. The bifurcation and multiplicity of problem (
1.1
) were first studied by Cerami, Fortunato, and Struwe
[
10
]
. In their work, the authors proved that there is a bifurcation from any eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\gamma}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Moreover, they showed that in a suitable left neighborhood of any such eigenvalue, the number of solutions is at least twice the multiplicity of the corresponding eigenvalue. They also provided an explicit estimate for the size of this neighborhood, which depends on the best constant in the Sobolev inequality, the Lebesgue measure of the domain, and the dimension. Following this seminal contribution, similar results have been obtained for a variety of variational operators. For instance, Fiscella, Bisci, and Servadei
[
12
]
extended these ideas to the nonlocal framework by considering a general class of fractional operators. Their work generalizes the bifurcation and multiplicity results of Cerami, Fortunato, and Struwe
[
10
]
from classical elliptic equations to the setting of nonlocal fractional operators. We refer to
[
28
]
for quasilinear results, where the authors extended the bifurcation and multiplicity theory for the critical fractional
p
-Laplacian from the semilinear case

p=2
to all
p\in(1,\infty)
by using a new abstract critical point theorem.

On the other hand, Choquard-type problems have gained significant attention in recent years. The equation

(1.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(w)|^{p}}{|z-w|^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

with
N=3
,
\mu=1
,
p=2
and
V
as a continuous potential, was first introduced by
Pekar
[
27
]
in 1954 in the context of quantum field theory to describe a polaron at rest. Later, P. Choquard
[
17
]
also studied (
1.2
) as an approximation to the Hartree-Fock theory for one component plasma, modeling an electron trapped in its hole. Since then, several authors have investigated Choquard-type problems with
N\geq 3,0<\mu<N,p\geq 2
as seen in the works of
[
1
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein. In particular, Gao and Yang
[
14
]
established the existence of solutions for the Brézis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) with Choquard-type nonlinearity

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta&u=\lambda u+\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\mu}}}{|z-w|^{\mu}}dw\right)|u|^{2^{*}_{\mu}-2}u\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&&\text{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
2^{*}_{\mu}
is the critical exponent in the sense of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality. For the fractional Laplacian counterpart of this problem, similar results were obtained by Mukherjee and Sreenadh
[
26
]
.

Now, coming to the Grushin operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
, we observe that it is contained in a family of operators of the form

\Delta_{\lambda}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\lambda_{i}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}\right),\quad\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}),

which was introduced and studied by Franchi and Lanconelli
[
13
]
and later discussed by Kogoj and Lanconelli
[
16
]
. Problems involving the Grushin operator have been widely studied in the last few years. For interested readers, we refer
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
along with the references therein. Recently, Bisci, Malanchini, and Secchi
[
6
]
established a bifurcation and multiplicity results for the Brézis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) involving the Grushin operator

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{\gamma}&u=\lambda u+|u|^{2^{*}_{\gamma}-2}u\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u&=0&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
2^{*}_{\gamma}=\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-2}
is the associated Sobolev critical exponent. Their results extend those obtained in
[
10
]
for classical elliptic problems and in
[
12
]
for nonlocal fractional operators. For related results concerning the

p
-Grushin operator, see
[
7
]
.

Motivated by the above results for classical elliptic operators, fractional operators, and the Grushin framework, our aim is to establish bifurcation and multiplicity results for the Brézis-Nirenberg type Choquard problem involving the Grushin operator

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{\gamma}&u=\lambda u+\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&&\text{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is an open, bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 3
,
\lambda>0
is a parameter and
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
. Here,
2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}=\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-2}
is the Sobolev critical exponent in the Grushin Hardy-Littlewood context.

Now, we define the function space and variational setup for our problem.

1.1.
Function space and variational formulation

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{\gamma}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where the Grushin gradient
\nabla_{\gamma}
is defined as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma}u(z)=\left(\nabla_{x}u(z),(1+\gamma)|x|^{\gamma}\nabla_{y}u(z)\right).

The space
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle_{\gamma}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\gamma}u\nabla_{\gamma}v\ dz
. For
\Omega
bounded, the continuous embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
holds with
p\in[1,2^{*}_{\gamma}]
and it is compact with
p\in[1,2^{*}_{\gamma})
(see
[
2
,
16
]
).

Definition 1
.

We say that a function
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
to be a (weak) solution of (
1.3
) if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\gamma}u\nabla_{\gamma}\phi\ dz=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\phi\ dz+\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u\phi\ dz,\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ \phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega).

The energy functional
J_{\gamma}:\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to problem (
1.3
) is given by

J_{\gamma}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz.

1.2.
Spectral properties of the Grushin operator

We denote
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{j}\leq\cdots
the sequence of eigenvalues of the problem

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\gamma}u=\lambda u\ \text{ in }\ \Omega,\ u=0\ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega.

with
\lambda_{j}\rightarrow\infty
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
The eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
corresponding to each
\lambda_{j}
forms an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
and an orthogonal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
(see
[
31
]
). The characterization of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
is given by

\lambda_{1}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dz}.

Furthermore, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the eigenvalues can be characterized as follows:

\lambda_{j+1}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\perp}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dz},

where
\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\perp}(\Omega)=\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega):\langle u,\phi_{i}\rangle_{\gamma},\text{ for }i=1,2,\cdots,j\}
. We denote
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,j}(\Omega)=span\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{j}\},
then
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,j}(\Omega)\bigoplus\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\perp}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,j}(\Omega)
is finite dimensional.

1.3.
Main result

To prove the multiplicity of solutions for (
1.3
), we use the abstract critical point theorem due to P. Bartolo, V. Benci, and D. Fortunato
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
functional on a Hilbert space
(H,\|\cdot\|)
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

I(u)=I(-u)
and
I(0)=0
;

(ii)

there exists a positive constant
\beta
such that
I
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition in
(0,\beta)
;

(iii)

there exist two closed subspaces
V
and
W
of
H
and three positive constants
\rho,\delta,\beta^{\prime}
with
\delta<\beta^{\prime}<\beta
such that

(a)

I(u)\leq\beta^{\prime}
for all
u\in W
,

(b)

I(u)\geq\delta
for any
u\in V
with
\|u\|=\rho
;

(c)

codim
V<+\infty
and dim
W\geq codimV
.

Then, there exist at least
dimW-codimV
pairs of critical points of
I
, with critical values belonging to the interval
[\delta,\beta^{\prime}]
.

Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 3
with smooth boundary. Let
\lambda>0
and
\lambda^{*}
be the eigenvalue of the operator
(-\Delta_{\gamma},\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega))
given by

\lambda^{*}=\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{\lambda_{k}:\lambda<\lambda_{k}\}

and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be its multiplicity. Assume that

\lambda^{*}-\lambda<\frac{S_{\Omega}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{\mu}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}},

where
\operatorname{diam}\Omega
is the diameter of the domain
\Omega
with respect to the Grushin norm

and

is defined in (

). Then, problem (
1.3
) admits at least

pairs of nontrivial solutions
\{-u_{\gamma,i},u_{\gamma,i}\}
,

such that

as

.

2.
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

In order to address Choquard-type problems, the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality plays a crucial role. As far as we know, a complete proof of this inequality in the context of the Grushin operator does not appear in the literature. Therefore, we generalize the classical results of
[
29
, Chapter V, Section 1]
to the setting of the Grushin operator. We include a detailed outline of its proof for completeness.

Define the following integral operator:

I_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(w)}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\ \text{ with }\mu\in(0,N_{\gamma}).

In order to prove the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev (HLS) inequality, we first need to understand when the operator
I_{\gamma,\mu}
is bounded between Lebesgue spaces.

For this reason, we consider the following question. Given
\mu
with
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
, for which pairs
p
and
q
is the operator
f\mapsto I_{\gamma,\mu}(f)
bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
?
That is, when does the inequality

(2.1)

\|I_{\gamma,\mu}(f)\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p}

hold for some constant
C>0
?

There is a basic necessary condition for the boundedness of the operator
I_{\gamma,\mu}
, which comes from the fact that its kernel
d(z)^{-\mu}
is homogeneous. To understand this, we consider how both sides of the equation (
2.1
) behave under scaling. For each
t>0
, define the dilation
\tau_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

\tau_{t}f(z):=f(tx,t^{\gamma+1}y).

By change of variable, we get

\|\tau_{t}f(z)\|_{p}=t^{-\frac{N_{\gamma}}{p}}\|f\|_{p}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|I_{\gamma,\mu}(\tau_{t}f)\|_{q}=t^{\mu-N_{\gamma}-\frac{N_{\gamma}}{q}}.

Hence, for the inequality (
2.1
) to hold, a necessary relation between the exponents
p
and
q
is

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1.

In the next result, we show that this condition is also sufficient for the inequality (
2.1
) to hold.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
, and let
1\leq p<q<\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1.

Then the following hold:

(a)

If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then the integral
I_{\gamma,\mu}(f)(z)
converges absolutely for almost every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(b)

If
1<p
, then the operator
I_{\gamma,\mu}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, i.e.,

\|I_{\gamma,\mu}(f)\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p},

where
C
is a constant independent of
f
.

(c)

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
q=\frac{N_{\gamma}}{\mu}
, then for all
\kappa>0
,

\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I_{\gamma,\mu}(f)(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|\leq\left(\frac{C\|f\|_{1}}{\kappa}\right)^{q}.

That is, the operator
I_{\gamma,\mu}
is of weak-type
(1,\frac{N_{\gamma}}{\mu})
.

Proof.

For any
\alpha>0
, we decompose the operator
I_{\gamma,\mu}
as
I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}+I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}
, where

I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)=\int_{d(z-w)<\alpha}\frac{f(w)}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw,

and

I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)=\int_{d(z-w)\geq\alpha}\frac{f(w)}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw.

At this stage, let
\alpha>0
be a fixed constant. Its precise value is not essential for the analysis that follows.

The first integral
I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)

is the convolution of the function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the kernel
\frac{1}{d(z)^{\mu}}
, which is in
L^{1}(B_{\alpha})
because
\mu<2
. Therefore, this integral converges absolutely for almost every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Similarly, the second integral
I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)

is the convolution of
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the kernel
\frac{1}{d(z)^{\mu}}
. Note that, this kernel belongs to
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\alpha})
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Indeed, the integral

\int_{d(z)\geq\alpha}d(z)^{-\mu p^{\prime}}\ dz<\infty,

provided
-\mu p^{\prime}>N_{\gamma}
, which is equivalent to
q<\infty
, where
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1
.

Thus, both integrals are absolutely convergent a.e., and this establishes part
(a)
of the theorem.

Next, we aim to show that the operator
f\mapsto I_{\gamma,\mu}f
is of weak type
(p,q)
, that is,

(2.2)

\left|\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\}\right|\leq\left(\frac{C\|f\|_{p}}{\kappa}\right)^{q},\ f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and for all }\ \kappa>0.

Thus, for any
\kappa>0
,

\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>2\kappa\right\}\right|\leq\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|+\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|.

We note that it is sufficient to prove the inequality (
2.2
) with
2\kappa
in place of
\kappa
and
\|f\|_{p}=1
.

By Young’s inequality
[
18
]
, we have

\|I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{p}\leq C\left(\int_{d(w)<\alpha}\frac{1}{d(w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)\|f\|_{p}=C_{1}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\rho^{N_{\gamma}-\mu-1}\ d\rho=C_{1}\alpha^{N_{\gamma}-\mu}.

Hence,

\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|\leq\frac{\|I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{p}^{p}}{\kappa^{p}}\leq C_{1}\alpha^{p(N_{\gamma}-\mu)}\kappa^{-p}.

On the other hand, by Young’s inequality, we obtain

\|I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{\infty}\leq C\left(\int_{d(w)\geq\alpha}\frac{1}{d(w)^{\mu p^{\prime}}}\ dw\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{p}=C_{2}\alpha^{-N_{\gamma}/q}.

Choose
\alpha
such that
C_{2}\alpha^{-N_{\gamma}/q}=\kappa
. Then

\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I^{2}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|=0,

and

\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|I^{1}_{\gamma,\mu}f(z)|>\kappa\right\}\right|\leq C_{1}\alpha^{p(N_{\gamma}-\mu)}\kappa^{-p}=C_{3}\kappa^{-q}.

This establishes inequality (
2.2
), which shows that the mapping
f\mapsto I_{\gamma,\mu}f
is of weak type
(p,q)
.

Now, in the special case
p=1
, we have
q=\frac{N_{\gamma}}{\mu}
, and the above argument implies that
I_{\gamma,\mu}
is of weak type
(1,q)
with
q=\frac{N_{\gamma}}{\mu}
. This establishes part
(c)
of the theorem.

To conclude part
(b)
, i.e., the strong-type boundedness condition

\|I_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p},

follows from the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, which interpolates between the weak-type bounds at
(p_{0}=1,q_{0}=\frac{N_{\gamma}}{\mu})
and another point
(p_{1},q_{1})
with
1<p_{1}<\frac{N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-\mu}
and
\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1
, for which
I_{\gamma,\mu}
is also of weak type.
∎

The following form of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in the Grushin setting is a direct consequence of the previous theorem combined with an application of Hölder’s inequality.

Proposition 2.2

(Grushin HLS inequality)
.

Let
p,r>1
and
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}+\frac{1}{r}=2
. Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exists a sharp constant
C(p,N_{\gamma},\mu,r)
, independent of
f,h
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(z)h(w)}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dzdw\leq C(p,N_{\gamma},\mu,r)\|f\|_{p}\|h\|_{r}.

Proof.

Applying Fubini’s theorem, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(z)h(w)}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dzdw=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(w)I_{\gamma,\mu}f(w)\ dw.

By Theorem
2.1
(b), if
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1
, then

\|I_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p}.

Now apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
q
and
r
, where
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Then, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(w)I_{\gamma,\mu}f(w)\ dw\leq\|h\|_{r}\|I_{\gamma,\mu}f\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p}\|h\|_{r}.

The condition
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}-1
together give

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}+\frac{1}{r}=2.

Hence, the HLS inequality holds with constant
C=C(p,N_{\gamma},\mu,r)
, completing the proof.
∎

We observe that, by Proposition
2.2
, the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(w)|^{q}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{q}dz

is well-defined provided that
|u|^{q}\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
t>1
satisfying
\frac{2}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N_{\gamma}}=2
.
Now, if
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then by the embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
p\in[2,2^{*}_{\gamma}]
, we must have

2\leq tq\leq\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-2},

equivalently,

\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}}\leq q\leq\ \frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-2}.

Therefore,
\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}}
is called the lower critical exponent, and
2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}:=\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-2}
is called the upper critical exponent in the setting of the Grushin HLS inequality. To the best of our knowledge, neither the lower nor the upper critical case has been previously studied for Choquard-type nonlocal problems in the Grushin setting. In this work, we consider for the first time the upper critical exponent case.

From the Proposition
2.2
, for all
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\leq C(N,\mu)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2^{*}_{\gamma}},

where
C(N_{\gamma},\mu)>0
is a constant. Now, we define the best constant
S_{H,L,\gamma}
in the Hardy-Littlewood sense as

S_{H,L,\gamma}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz}{\Bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}},

where
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.
For an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the best constant associated with the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in the Grushin setting is defined as

(2.3)

S_{\Omega}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u|^{2}dz}{\Bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}}.

From Proposition
2.2
, we conclude that
J_{\gamma}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
. It is easy to show that
J_{\gamma}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega))
by standard techniques. The critical points of
J_{\gamma}
coincide with the weak solutions of equation (
1.3
).

Remark 1
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we have assumed that

\lambda^{*}-\lambda<\frac{S_{\Omega}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{\mu}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}}.

Note that the term

\frac{S_{\Omega}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{\mu}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}}

depends on the domain
\Omega
. It is not guaranteed that

\lambda^{*}>\frac{S_{\Omega}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{\mu}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}}.

If we take
\lambda<0
, then the above assumption is violated.
Hence,
\lambda>0
condition is considered throughout this article.

3.
Palais-Smale sequence analysis

In this section, we prove the compactness condition for the Palais-Smale sequence. We begin by introducing a few preliminary lemmas essential for proving compactness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
, and
\{u_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2^{*}_{\gamma}}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a. e. in
\Omega
as
n\to\infty
. Then the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)-u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|(u_{n}(z)-u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle\to\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

Since
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{2^{*}_{\gamma}}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a. e. in
\Omega
, the Brézis-Lieb Lemma
[
8
]
implies that

(3.1)

|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\to|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\quad\text{ in }L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}(\Omega).

We now apply Theorem
2.1
(b) with

p=\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu},\quad q=\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{\mu}\quad\text{ and }\quad f=|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}},

and using equation (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right\|_{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{\mu}}\leq C\left\||u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right\|_{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}\to 0,

i.e.

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\to\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\quad\text{ in }\quad L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{\mu}}(\Omega).

Now, by Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)-u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(w)-u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}(w)-u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)\left(|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}(z)-u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)dz

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}-|u_{n}(w)-u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(z)-u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we know that the first term in (
3
) converges to

\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz.

For the second term, we note that
|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}(\Omega)
, and combining this with (
3.2
), we conclude that, the second term tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
, and
\lambda>0
. If
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
is a Palais-Smale sequence of
J_{\gamma}
, then
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
is the weak limit of
\{u_{n}\}
, then
u_{0}
is a weak solution of the problem (
1.3
).

Proof.

By the definition of the Palais-Smale sequence, it is easy to observe that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle|J_{\gamma}(u_{n})|\leq C_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|\left\langle J^{\prime}_{\gamma}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle_{\gamma}\right|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}.

From (
3.4
), we can obtain a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle J_{\gamma}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle J_{\gamma}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle_{\gamma}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\leq C_{2}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}),

that is

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\leq C_{3}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}),

for some
C_{3}>0
. Let
\operatorname{diam}\Omega
denotes the diameter of
\Omega
with respect to the Grushin norm

d(\cdot)
, defined by

\operatorname{diam}\Omega=\sup\{d(z-w):z,w\in\Omega\}.

From (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle C_{2}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz\right)^{2}.

Thus, there exists a positive constant
C_{4}
such that

(3.6)

\|u_{n}\|_{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\leq C_{4}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}).

Since
2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}>2
, using Hölder’s inequality and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\|u_{n}\|_{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}^{2}\leq C_{4}^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}.

It follows that we can find a positive constant
C_{5}
such that

(3.7)

\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{5}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}.

From (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.7
)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle\geq J_{\gamma}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\frac{\lambda C_{5}}{2}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}-\frac{C_{3}}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}(1+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}).

Hence, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
which implies that there exists a function
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
and

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
L^{2^{*}_{\gamma}}(\Omega)
. Then, we have

|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\rightharpoonup|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\text{ in }L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}(\Omega)\quad\text{ and }\quad|u_{n}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u_{n}\rightharpoonup|u_{0}|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}(\Omega)

as
n\to+\infty
. By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw
defines a linear continuous map from
L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}(\Omega)
to
L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{\mu}}(\Omega)
. This implies that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\rightharpoonup\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{\mu}}(\Omega)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

Combining all these, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u_{n}(z)\rightharpoonup\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u_{0}(z)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}}{N_{\gamma}+2}}(\Omega)\quad

as
n\to+\infty
. Next, we know that
J_{\gamma}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Thus, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\gamma}u_{0}\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi\,dz-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\varphi\,dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}u_{0}(z)\varphi(z)\ dz=0,

which implies that
u_{0}
is a weak solution of equation (
1.3
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Let
u_{0}
be the weak solution as proved in the previous lemma, taking
\varphi=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
as a test function in (
1.3
), we get

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{0}|^{2}\ dz=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}\ dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz.

This implies that

J_{\gamma}(u_{0})=\frac{N_{\gamma}+2-\mu}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\,dz\,dw\geq 0.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
0<\mu<N_{\gamma}
, and
\lambda>0
. If
\{u_{n}\}
is a Palais-Smale sequence of
J_{\gamma}
at level
c
with

c<\frac{N_{\gamma}+2-\mu}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}S^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}+2-\mu}}_{\Omega},

then the functional
J_{\gamma}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.2
, there exists
u_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
, and let us define
v_{n}:=u_{n}-u_{0}
. It follows that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
and
v_{n}\to 0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Additionally, by the Brezis-Lieb lemma
[
8
]
, it is easy to see that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{n}|^{2}\,dz=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,dz+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{0}|^{2}\,dz+o_{n}(1).

From Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1).

Since
J_{\gamma}(u_{n})\to c
as
n\to\infty
and by the above equations, we have

\displaystyle c+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=J_{\gamma}(u_{n})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{n}|^{2}\ dz-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{2}\,dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\ dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{0}|^{2}\ dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{n}^{2}dz-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=J_{\gamma}(u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}dz-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{n}^{2}dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dw+o_{n}(1)

By the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have
\int_{\Omega}v_{n}^{2}\ dz\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and since
J_{\gamma}(u_{0})\geq 0
, therefore

(3.8)

\displaystyle c+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,dz-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1).

As
u_{0}
is a weak solution this implies that
\langle J^{\prime}_{\gamma}(u_{0}),u_{0}\rangle_{\gamma}=0
with
\int_{\Omega}v_{n}^{2}\ dz\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\langle J^{\prime}_{\gamma}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{n}|^{2}\ dz-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{2}\ dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\ dz+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}u_{0}|^{2}\ dz-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}\ dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{0}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\langle J^{\prime}_{\gamma}(u_{0}),u_{0}\rangle_{\gamma}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\ dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,dz-\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz+o_{n}(1).

This implies that there must exist a non-negative constant
b
such that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,dz\to b\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz\to b,

as
n\to+\infty
. Thus, from (
3.8
), we get

(3.9)

c\geq\frac{N_{\gamma}+2-\mu}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}b.

Recall the definition of the best constant
S_{\Omega}
in (
2.3
), we have

S_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|v_{n}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz\right)^{\frac{N_{\gamma}-2}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\gamma}v_{n}|^{2}\,dz,

which gives that

b\geq S_{\Omega}b^{\frac{N_{\gamma}-2}{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}}.

From this we conclude that

\textrm{ either }b=0\ \ \textrm{ or}\ \ b\geq S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}.

If
b=0
, the proof is completed. If

b\geq S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}},

then from (
3.9
), we deduce that

\frac{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}\leq\frac{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}b\leq c,

which contradicts the hypothesis that

c<\frac{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}.

Hence,
b=0
which further implies that
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\gamma}\to 0\text{ as }n\to+\infty
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now prepared to start the proof of Theorem (
1.2
). The following lemma is very crucial proving the main result. Before starting, we fix some notations. Since
\lambda^{*}=\min\{\lambda_{k}:\lambda<\lambda_{k}\}
defined in Theorem
1.2
, we assume that
\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{k}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It is known that
\lambda^{*}
has multiplicity
m\in\mathbb{N}
by assumption. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2},\ \ \text{ if }k=1

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{k-1}<\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{k}=\cdots=\lambda_{k+m-1}<\lambda_{k+m},\ \ \text{if }k\geq 2

With the notation from Theorem
1.1
, we define

W=\text{span}\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k+m-1}\}

\text{ and }\quad V=\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)&\text{if}\ k=1,\\
\left\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega):\langle u,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\gamma}=0\ \forall j=1,\cdots,k-1\right\}&\text{if}\ k\geq 2.\end{cases}

Note that both
W
and
V
are closed subsets of
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
, with

\dim W=k+m-1\quad\text{and}\quad\text{codim}\ V=k-1,

hence condition (iii)(c) of Theorem
1.1
is satisfied. In the next lemma, we verify the remaining conditions of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\lambda^{*}
is defined as Theorem
1.2
with multiplicity
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there exist positive constants
\beta^{\prime},\delta
and
\rho
such that

(1)

J_{\gamma}(u)\leq\beta^{\prime}
, for all
u\in W
;

(2)

J_{\gamma}(u)\geq\delta
, for any
u\in V
with
\|u\|_{\gamma}=\rho
;

where
V
and
W
are given as above.

Proof.

(1) Let
u\in W
, we can write

u(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{k+m-1}u_{j}\phi_{j}(z)

with
u_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,2,\cdots,k+m-1
.

Since
\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
and an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega)
, we have

\|u\|^{2}_{\gamma}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+m-1}u_{j}^{2}\|\phi_{j}\|^{2}_{\gamma}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+m-1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k+m-1}u_{j}^{2}=\lambda_{k}\|u\|^{2}_{2}=\lambda^{*}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

Let
\operatorname{diam}\Omega
denotes the diameter of the domain
\Omega
with respect to the Grushin norm
d(\cdot)
, then for
u\in W

\displaystyle J_{\gamma}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\gamma}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dw\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\|u\|_{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\|u\|_{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}.

Let

g(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\tau^{2}-\frac{1}{2(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\tau^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}},\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ \tau\geq 0.

Then
g(\tau)
has a maximum at

\tau_{\max}=\Big((\lambda^{*}-\lambda)|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-1)}}.

Therefore, by simple computations, we have

\displaystyle g(\tau_{max})=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\left[(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}|\Omega|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-1}}.

Hence, we choose

\beta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\left[(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}|\Omega|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-1}}.

Then, from (
4.1
) we have

J_{\gamma}(u)\leq\beta^{\prime}\text{ for all }u\in W.

(2) For every
u\in V
, it follows from the variational characterization of
\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{k}
given by

\lambda_{k}=\min_{u\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{\gamma}}{\|u\|^{2}_{2}},

the following inequality holds

\|u\|^{2}_{\gamma}\geq\lambda^{*}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

Therefore, by the definition of
S_{\Omega}
and noting that
\lambda>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\gamma}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)\|u\|^{2}_{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(w)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}dw\right)|u(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}dz

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{\gamma}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}S_{\Omega}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\|u\|_{\gamma}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}\right)

Now, let
u\in V
such that
\|u\|_{\gamma}=\rho>0
. Since
2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}>2
, we can choose
\rho
sufficiently small, say
\rho\leq\overline{\rho}
with
\overline{\rho}>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}S_{\Omega}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\|u\|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}_{\gamma}>0,

and

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}S_{\Omega}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\rho^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}\right)<\rho^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)\right)<\beta^{\prime}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

By Lemma
3.3
, the functional
J_{\gamma}
satisfies compactness assumption (iii) of Theorem
1.1
with

\beta=\frac{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}.

From the Lemma
4.1
,
J_{\gamma}
verifies geometric properties required by the assumption (iii)-(a) and (b) of Theorem
1.1
with

\rho=\overline{\rho}

\beta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\left[(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}|\Omega|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-1}}

\delta=\rho^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{*}}\right)-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}S_{\Omega}^{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}\rho^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}\right)

By our assumption

\lambda^{*}-\lambda<\frac{S_{\Omega}}{|\Omega|^{\frac{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\frac{\mu}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}},

it is easy to show that

\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\left[(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}|\Omega|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-2}(\operatorname{diam}\Omega)^{\mu}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}-1}}<\frac{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}{4N_{\gamma}-2\mu}S_{\Omega}^{\frac{2N_{\gamma}-\mu}{N_{\gamma}-\mu+2}}.

Therefore, assumption (iii) of Theorem
1.1
holds with
\delta<\beta^{\prime}<\beta
. We conclude that
J_{\gamma}
has
m
pairs of critical points
\{-u_{\gamma,i},u_{\gamma,i}\}

(4.2)

0<\delta\leq J_{\gamma}(\pm u_{\gamma,i})\leq\beta^{\prime}.

Fix an index

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq J_{\gamma}(u_{\gamma,i})

\displaystyle=J_{\gamma}(u_{\gamma,i})-\frac{1}{2}\langle J^{\prime}_{\gamma}(u_{\gamma,i}),u_{\gamma,i}\rangle_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{\gamma,i}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}}{d(z-w)^{\mu}}\ dw\right)|u_{\gamma,i}(z)|^{2^{*}_{\gamma,\mu}}\ dz

Taking the limit as
\lambda\to\lambda^{*}

(4.4)

Since the embedding

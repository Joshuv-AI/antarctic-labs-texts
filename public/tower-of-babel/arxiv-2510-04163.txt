White’s Conjecture for Paving Matroids
Yu-Chuan Yu, Chi Ho Yuen

Abstract
. White’s conjecture asserts that any two tuples of matroid bases that have the same multi-set union can be transformed from one to another by symmetric exchanges; it also implies that the toric ideals of matroids are generated by the binomials encoding these exchanges. We prove White’s conjecture for the class of paving matroids. Our strategy is to generalize the inductive argument using circuit-hyperplane relaxations in the recent work of Han et al. to stressed hyperplane relaxations.

Primary: 05B35; Secondary: 13F65

1.
Introduction

Matroids are combinatorial structures that capture the notion of independence in various mathematical areas from graph theory to algebraic geometry. A standard characterization of matroids is the
symmetric exchange property
of bases: given two distinct bases
A,B
of a matroid
M
and
a\in A\setminus B
, there exists
b\in B\setminus A
such that both
(A\cup\{b\})\setminus\{a\},(B\cup\{a\})\setminus\{b\}
are bases. A natural question, first due to White
[
Whi80
]
, is a multi-basis, multi-step generalization of the said property.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Given two ordered tuples of bases
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})
and
(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{n})
with equal multi-set union, there exists an exchange sequence
(B_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(0)})=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}),(B_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(1)}),\ldots,(B_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(\ell)})=(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{n})
of tuples of bases such that, within each step indexed by
0<t\leq\ell
, some pair
B_{i}^{(t)},B_{j}^{(t)}
is obtained from
B_{i}^{(t-1)},B_{j}^{(t-1)}
by a symmetric exchange (and other bases remain the same).

Conjecture
1.1
is one of the most fundamental and well-known problems in matroid theory. Despite its elementary description, it is of interest to combinatorial commutative algebra and algebraic geometry: the conjecture describes the generators of the
toric ideals of matroids
, which include the ideals defining the
torus orbit closures
in Grassmannians
[
MS21
, Section 13.2]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
M
be a matroid on ground set
E
with set of bases
{\mathcal{B}}
. Consider the kernel
I_{M}
of the map
f_{M}:\mathbb{K}[y_{B}:B\in{\mathcal{B}}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[x_{e}:e\in E]
given by
y_{B}\mapsto\prod_{e\in B}x_{e}
. Then
I_{M}
is generated by binomials of the form
y_{A}y_{B}-y_{A^{\prime}}y_{B^{\prime}}
, where
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
are obtained from
A,B
by a symmetric exchange.

This formulation leads to several algebraic variants of White’s conjecture
[
Haw09
,
HH02
]
, and establishes further connections with the polyhedral geometry of
matroid polytopes

[
BL23
]
. For example, the weak White’s conjecture asserts that
I_{M}
is generated by quadrics, but we refer to Conjecture
1.1
as White’s conjecture here, and say “White’s conjecture in degree
n
” for Conjecture
1.1
with a fixed
n
.

White’s conjecture and almost all of its variants remain open in general
1
1

1

Two notable variants that are proven for all matroids are (1) Conjecture
1.2
up to saturation
[
LM14
]
and (2) the existence of regular unimodular triangulations of matroid polytopes
[
BL23
]
.
, but several special cases have been proven. We refer the reader to the recent paper by Han, Michałek, and Weigert
[
HMW25
]
(which we discuss in more details in the next subsection) for a list of related works, and only highlight the works by Bonin and Bérczi–Schwarcz:

Theorem 1.3
.

[
Bon13
]

White’s conjecture is true for sparse paving matroids.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
BS24
]

White’s conjecture in degree 2 is true for split matroids. Moreover,
\ell
can be chosen to be
\leq\min\{r,r-|B_{1}\cap B^{\prime}_{1}|+1\}
.

The main contribution of our paper is to settle White’s conjecture for the class of
paving matroids
, which are matroids whose circuits are all of size
r
or
r+1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

White’s conjecture is true for paving matroids.

Every sparse paving matroid is paving whereas every paving matroid is split, so our result extends Theorem
1.3
and makes a step towards solving White’s conjecture for split matroids (and more) completely.

The class of paving matroids, together with sparse paving matroids and split matroids, are interesting in the context of White’s conjecture for several reasons. On one hand, it has been conjectured that asymptotically almost all matroids are paving (respectively, sparse paving or split), see
[
MNWW11
, Conjecture 1.6]
and
[
Oxl11
, Conjecture 15.5.8]
. Therefore, assuming this conjecture, our result confirms White’s conjecture for a very large class of matroids (that properly contains all sparse paving matroids and Steiner systems). On the other hand, paving matroids can be characterized using their matroid polytopes
[
JS17
]
, which connects to the algebraic/geometric aspect of White’s conjecture, and more generally, the modern trends in matroid theory.

1.1.
Overview of the Strategy

Our strategy is inspired by the aforementioned work by Han et al. They proved that the validity of White’s conjecture is preserved by
circuit-hyperplane relaxation
and its inverse operation.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
HMW25
]

Let
M
be a matroid with a circuit-hyperplane
H
and let
\widetilde{M}
be the relaxation of
M
at
H
. Then White’s conjecture is true for
M
if and only if it is true for
\widetilde{M}
.

Since every sparse paving matroid is related to a uniform matroid by a sequence of circuit-hyperplane relaxations, and White’s conjecture is trivially true for the latter, they gave an alternative proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The operation of circuit-hyperplane relaxation can be generalized to
stressed hyperplane relaxation
(relaxation for short), which was first considered by Bonin and de Mier
[
BdM08
]
before further developed by Ferroni, Nasr, and Vecchi
[
FNV23
]
. Every paving matroid is related to a uniform matroid by a sequence of relaxations, and our argument is by induction on the length of such a relaxation sequence. Part of our results remain true for arbitrary relaxations (regardless of
M
is paving or not), particularly for higher degree. In the next theorem, we consider the condition

(*):
Any two tuples (of bases of
M
) of degree 2​ or​ 3 that are connected by a 2-step exchange sequence in
\widetilde{M}
can be connected by an exchange sequence in
M
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M
be a matroid with a stressed hyperplane
H
and let
\widetilde{M}
be the relaxation of
M
at
H
. Then the followings are true:

(1)

if White’s conjecture holds for
M
, then White’s conjecture holds for
\widetilde{M}
;

(2)

if White’s conjecture holds for
\widetilde{M}
and
(*)
holds for
M
, then White’s conjecture holds for
M
.

This theorem extends
[
HMW25
, Theorem 2.13]
, but the proof is more complicated as there are now many bases of
\widetilde{M}
not in
M
, so the original induction (on the number of occurrences of these bases in an exchange sequence) and degree reduction procedure there are not enough.
Instead, we apply a more careful induction and modification procedure to the exchange sequence so to create the 2-step sequences that (*) can be applied on; we put no effort on optimizing
\ell
as in Theorem
1.4
.

We prove the validity of (*) for paving matroids
2

2

As mentioned above, the degree 2 case is true more generally for split matroids, but we include our own proof here as a simpler illustration of our argument.
in Proposition
3.1
and
4.1
. Same as the above, their proofs are more difficult than their counterpart in
[
HMW25
]
, as we need substantial work to make sure a basis of
\widetilde{M}
we consider is also a basis of
M
(e.g., in a circuit-hyperplane relaxation, performing an arbitrary basis exchange in
\widetilde{M}
on the unique new basis produces a basis of
M
, but in our case more careful choice is needed). We sketch our high level strategy here.

(1)

We may assume
E
is the union of the bases, and by the 2-step exchange sequence in the relaxation, at most
4
elements are being exchanged;

(2)

we may assume that various candidate exchange sequences only exchanging these elements are not valid in the original matroid
M
, which implies that certain subsets considered in these sequences are circuits and they span distinct (stressed) hyperplanes

’s of
M
;

(3)

we may assume certain pairs of these hyperplanes each covers

, or else we may use an uncovered element to produce a valid exchange sequence in
M
;

(4)

the sum of
|H\cap H^{\prime}|
’s over all pairs is now simultaneously small because of the property of stressed hyperplanes (Proposition

) and large because of the covering assumption, which yields a contradiction.

2.
Background

We assume basic familiarity of matroid theory from the reader, a standard reference on the subject is
[
Oxl11
]
.
Unless otherwise specified,
M
denotes a matroid of rank
r
on ground set
E
. The set of bases of
M
is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}
, and the closure operation is denoted by
\cl
. The fundamental circuit of
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
with respect to an element
e\not\in B
is denoted by
C(B,e)
; we always take fundamental circuit in
M
.

Definition 2.1
.

A matroid
M
is paving if every circuit of
M
is of size
r
or
r+1
. Equivalently, any subset of size
\leq r-1
is independent.

The following is straightforward from the definition; in fact, it is not difficult to see that
M
is paving if and only if it is
U_{2,2}\oplus U_{0,1}
-free.

Proposition 2.2
.

The class of paving matroids is minor-closed.

Definition 2.3
.

A hyperplane
H
of
M
is stressed if every subset of size
r
is circuit.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
BdM08
,
FNV23
]

Let
H
be a stressed hyperplane of
M
. Then

{\mathcal{B}}\cup\{S:S\subset H,|S|=r\}

is the collection of bases of a matroid
\widetilde{M}
, known as the relaxation of
M
at
H
.

A circuit-hyperplane relaxation is thus a special case of this operation where the stressed hyperplane
H
is just a circuit. We only consider relaxation at a stressed hyperplane of size
\geq r
, so
M\neq\widetilde{M}
(in particular,
M
is not uniform).

The importance of stressed hyperplanes in the study of paving matroids is the following crucial theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
FNV23
]

Every hyperplane of a paving matroid
M
is stressed. Moreover, the relaxation of
M
at any of its stressed hyperplanes is again paving. Hence, every paving matroid can be related to the uniform matroid
U_{r,E}
by a sequence of relaxations.

We collect a few simple properties of stressed hyperplanes and paving matroids.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be two distinct hyperplanes in a paving matroid
M
. Then
|H\cap H^{\prime}|\leq r-2
.

Proof.

If
|H\cap H^{\prime}|\geq r-1
, then
H\cap H^{\prime}
is of rank at least (indeed, exactly)
r-1
, which uniquely determines its closure as a hyperplane.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
H
be a stressed hyperplane,
S\subset H
be a subset of size
r-1
and
x\not\in H
. Then
S\cup\{x\}
is a basis of
M
.

Proof.

S
is an independent set of rank
r-1
, and
x
is an element not in its closure
H
, so their union is a rank
r
independent set, that is, a basis.
∎

2.1.
Convention of Notation

We often write
X+x_{1}\ldots x_{k}-y_{1}\ldots y_{l}
or
X-y_{1}\ldots y_{l}+x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
for
(X\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\})\setminus\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}\}
. Here
k,l
are always small enough that we can list out the elements explicitly; moreover, we guarantee
y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}
are elements of
X
whereas
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
are not, with the exception that
x_{i}=y_{j}
(which we indicate the possibility explicitly). We use
\uplus
for multi-set union.
A symmetric exchange from
(A,B)
to
(A^{\prime}:=A-a+b,B^{\prime}:=B-b+a)
is written as

\begin{array}[]{c c c}A&\xleftrightarrow{a\hskip 8.19447ptb}&A^{\prime}\\
B&\xleftrightarrow{b\hskip 8.19447pta}&B^{\prime}\end{array}.

We denote by
{\mathcal{B}}(H,x):=\{S\cup\{x\}:S\subset H,|S|=r-1\}
the collection of bases in Proposition
2.7
. We often verify a subset
B
in an exchange sequence is a basis by simply stating “
B\in{\mathcal{B}}(H,x)
”, if we believe it is clear to the reader that
B,H,x
satisfy the assumption in the proposition.

When we work with a matroid with a single distinguished stressed hyperplane
H
, we often further omit
H
and write
B\in{\mathcal{B}}(x)
. In which case, we say a size
r
subset
S
is of type
|S\setminus H|
. By definition and Proposition
2.7
, a type 0 subset is a basis of the relaxation
\widetilde{M}
but not
M
, a type 1 subset is necessarily a basis of both, and we only consider type
\geq 2
subsets that are bases of
M
(hence
\widetilde{M}
).

2.2.
Two Lemmas on Types

We state a lemma on the exchange property of type 0 and type 1 subsets. As a bonus, the mild modification where we take
H=E
in the proof justifies the claim that White’s conjecture is trivial for uniform matroids.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M
be a matroid with a stressed hyperplane
H
.
Let
(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
and
(B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{n})
be two tuples of type
\leq 1
subsets with the same multi-set union.
Then there exists a sequence of symmetric exchanges transforming
(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
into
(B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{n})
such that all intermediate subsets are of type
\leq 1
.

Proof.

First, we align all elements not in
H
by applying symmetric exchanges. Suppose by induction,
B_{i}\setminus H=B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus H
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
thus

(1)

\biguplus_{i\geq m}(B_{i}\setminus H)=\biguplus_{i\geq m}(B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus H),

but
B_{m}\setminus H\neq B^{\prime}_{m}\setminus H
, say
a\in B_{m}\setminus H
(reverse the role of
B_{m},B^{\prime}_{m}
if needed). If
B^{\prime}_{m}
is of type 0 (respectively, contains
b\not\in H
), then there must exist some
k>m
such that
B_{k}
is also of type 0 (respectively, contains
b
) by (
1
), and we can exchange
a\in B_{m}
with an arbitrary element from
B_{k}\setminus B_{m}
(respectively,
b\in B_{k}
), which is non-empty as
a\in B_{m}\setminus B_{k}
. Such an operation preserves the property that every subset is of type
\leq 1
.

Next, suppose by induction,
B_{i}\cap H=B^{\prime}_{i}\cap H
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
thus

(2)

\biguplus_{i\geq m}(B_{i}\cap H)=\biguplus_{i\geq m}(B^{\prime}_{i}\cap H).

We then induct on
|B_{m}\triangle B^{\prime}_{m}|
. If we are in the base case
B_{m}=B^{\prime}_{m}
, then we can move on to the next pair. So suppose there exists
t\in(B^{\prime}_{m}\setminus B_{m})\cap H
. By (
2
), there exists
k>m
such that
t\in B_{k}
, and if
((B_{m}\setminus B^{\prime}_{m})\setminus B_{k})\cap H\neq\emptyset
, we can pick
s
from it and exchange
s,t
between
B_{m},B_{k}
, which reduces
|B_{m}\triangle B^{\prime}_{m}|
without modifying subsets whose indices are
<m
, nor changing the type of any subset.

Otherwise, we still pick some
s\in(B_{m}\setminus B^{\prime}_{m})\cap H
. The fact that
s\not\in B^{\prime}_{m}
and (
2
) imply there exists
l>m,l\neq k
such that
s\not\in B_{l}
, and we may assume
t\not\in B_{l}
or else replace
k
by
l
in the above. Since
|B_{k}\cap H|,|B_{l}\cap H|
differ by at most 1, while
s,t\in B_{k},s,t\not\in B_{l}
, there exists
p\in(B_{l}\setminus B_{k})\cap H
. Now we first exchange
t,p
between
B_{k},B_{l}
before exchanging
s,t
between
B_{m}
and the updated
B_{l}
. This again reduces
|B_{m}\triangle B^{\prime}_{m}|
without changing other hypotheses.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
2.8
, if all
B_{i}
’s and
B^{\prime}_{i}
’s are type 1
bases
of
M
, then there exists a sequence of symmetric exchanges transforming
(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
into
(B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{n})
such that all intermediate subsets are type 1 bases.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.8
to obtain an exchange sequence whose intermediate subsets are of type
\leq 1
, they must be all of type 1 by considering total type.
∎

We also use the following easy lemma multiple times in this paper.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
M
be a matroid with a stressed hyperplane
H
.
Let
X
be a type 0 subset and
Y
a basis of
M
of type
\geq 2
. Let
a\in Y\setminus H\subset Y\setminus X
. Then there exists
s\in X\setminus Y\subset H
such that
X-s+a,Y-a+s
are bases of

.

Proof.

Since both

are bases of
\widetilde{M}
, we can pick

such that
X-s+a,Y-a+s
are bases of
\widetilde{M}
. But the former is of type 1 and the latter is of type

, so they are bases of
M
.
∎

3.
Degree 2

We show the degree 2 part of (*) for any paving matroid
M
, and the degree 2 part of Theorem

(2) in this section. Together we prove White’s conjecture in degree 2 for paving matroids.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a paving matroid and fix a hyperplane
H
of size
\geq r
. Let
\widetilde{M}
be the relaxation of
M
at
H
. Let
X,Y,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}
be 4 bases of
M
(hence
\widetilde{M}
) such that there exists an exchange sequence from
(X,Y)
to
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
in
\widetilde{M}
as

(3)

\begin{array}[]{c c c c c}X&\xleftrightarrow{a\hskip 8.19447pts}&X^{\prime\prime}&\xleftrightarrow{t\hskip 8.19447ptb}&X^{\prime}\\

Then there exists an exchange sequence from
(X,Y)
to
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})

Proof.

We are done if
X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}
are bases of

By induction on

We write

X^{\prime}=\widetilde{X}+bs,Y^{\prime}=\widetilde{Y}+at

\widetilde{Y}+bq,\widetilde{Y}+tq\in{\mathcal{B}}(H_{Y,bt},q)

\widetilde{X}-q+ast=(\widetilde{X}-q+st)+a\in{\mathcal{B}}(H_{X,st},a),\widetilde{X}-q+abs=(\widetilde{X}-q+ab)+s\in{\mathcal{B}}(H_{X,ab},s)

Step 4
: For each pair of distinct hyperplanes chosen from

, we consider the size of their intersection

and sum over the

terms. It can be seen that

(1)

each of

appears in exactly 2 hyperplanes and contributes

to the sum;

(2)

each element in

appears in exactly 3 hyperplanes and contributes

thus

Therefore the sum is

, a contradiction.
∎

Now we deal with the second task.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

be a (not necessarily paving) matroid and let

be a stressed hyperplane of

at

Let

be subsets of type 0, and

\geq 2

Suppose

and
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
differ by a single symmetric exchange in

that is proper (

\begin{array}[]{c c c}X&\xleftrightarrow{s\hskip 8.19447ptt}&X^{\prime}\\

to

Proof.

Consider the fundamental circuit

Theorem 3.3

(Degree 2 case of Theorem

Hence, we may assume the total type is at least 4 and

for the rest of the proof.

Case I

y,z\not\in H
. By Lemma

\begin{array}[]{c c c c c c c}X&\xleftrightarrow{p\hskip 8.19447pty}&X-p+y&\xleftrightarrow{y\hskip 8.19447ptz}&X-p+z&\xleftrightarrow{z\hskip 8.19447ptp}&X\\

Z&&Z&\xleftrightarrow{z\hskip 8.19447pty}&Z^{\prime}&&Z^{\prime}\end{array}.

Case II

(and by symmetry, the case

\begin{array}[]{c c c c c c c c c}X&\xleftrightarrow{p\hskip 8.19447pty}&X-p+y&\xleftrightarrow{q\hskip 8.19447ptz}&X-pq+yz&\xleftrightarrow{z\hskip 8.19447ptp}&X-q+y&\xleftrightarrow{y\hskip 8.19447ptq}&X\\

here

. If

, then we take

in the above sequence and omit the second step (as well as the third step if

).

Case III
:

. Again applying Lemma
2.10
, we pick some

, which cannot be

, and

If

If

5.
Higher Degree

We prove Theorem
1.7
in this section, which also wraps up the proof of Theorem
1.5
. We assume
H
is a (size
\geq r
) stressed hyperplane of a (not necessarily paving) matroid
M
and let
\widetilde{M}
be its relaxation at

.

5.1.
White’s Conjecture for
M
Implies White’s Conjecture for
\widetilde{M}

Proof of Theorem
1.7
(1).

Suppose
(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
and
(B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{n})
are two tuples of bases of
\widetilde{M}
such that they have the same multi-set union.
We aim to show that there exists a sequence of symmetric exchanges transforming
(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
into
(B^{\prime}_{1},\dots,B^{\prime}_{n})
.

By repeatedly applying Lemma
2.10
, we may assume each tuple either does not contain any type 0 bases or does not contain any type
\geq 2
bases. Since the total type is preserved by symmetric exchanges, the two tuples belong to the same case.

Case I
: There are no type 0 bases. In this case, all
B_{i}
’s and
B^{\prime}_{i}
’s are bases of
M
, and there exists an exchange sequence using bases of
M
(hence
\widetilde{M}
) by assumption.

Case II
: There are no type
\geq 2
bases. This is Lemma
2.8
.
∎

5.2.
Setup for Induction in the Opposite Direction

Suppose White’s conjecture is true for
\widetilde{M}
, and (*) holds for
M
. By Section
3
, we know White’s conjecture in degree 2 is true for
M
. For
n\geq 3
, fix a pair of tuples
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}),(B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{n})
of bases of
M
with equal multi-set union. As an easy observation, the total type of
B_{j}
’s (respectively,
B^{\prime}_{j}
’s) is at least
n
as each basis is of type
\geq 1
.

By hypothesis, there exists a valid exchange sequence in
\widetilde{M}
that connects the tuples:

\mathfrak{B}=((B_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(0)}),(B_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(1)}),\ldots,(B_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,B_{n}^{(\ell)})),

in which we assume there are no repeated tuples.
Define
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}(\mathfrak{B}):=\{1\leq j\leq\ell:B_{i}^{(j)}\subset H\}
for each

and
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B}):=\{1\leq i\leq n:{\mathcal{H}}_{i}(\mathfrak{B})\neq\emptyset\}
. By definition, when
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B})=\emptyset
,
\mathfrak{B}
itself is a valid exchange sequence only using bases in
M
. So by induction on
|{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B})|
, if
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B})\neq\emptyset
, it suffices to fix an arbitrary

and construct a new exchange sequence
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
such that
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B}^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{R}}(\mathfrak{B})\setminus\{i\}
. By permuting indices if necessary, we assume

for the rest of the proof.

5.3.

contains no consecutive indices. Suppose

-th exchange step by a longer exchange sequence in

with the same starting and ending tuples (and re-index the steps if necessary), such that

(1)

of the internal steps is either in

, or is at least 2 steps apart from another instance of

of type 0 (in all cases except Case II(d), the former is guaranteed), so in the updated

(2)

for every other position

Case I

\begin{array}[]{c c c}X:=B_{1}^{(l)}&\xleftrightarrow{s\hskip 8.19447ptt}&X^{\prime}:=B_{1}^{(l+1)}\\

Case I(b)

6.
Open Problems

We list a few research directions that could be follow-up of our work.

As mentioned in the introduction, paving matroids are special cases of the class of split matroids, and these matroids are closely related to the notion of
stressed subset

[
FS24
]
. A subset
A
(of rank
r^{\prime}
) of a matroid
M
is
stressed
if
M|_{A},M/A
are both uniform, and we can
relax

M
at
A
by declaring every subset
S
of size
r
satisfying
|S\cap A|\geq r^{\prime}+1
to be a basis (together with the original bases of
M
); a stressed hyperplane is thus a stressed subset of rank
r-1
. A(n elementary) split matroid can be characterized as a matroid that becomes uniform after relaxing all of its (non-trivial) stressed subsets
[
FS24
, Theorem 4.8]
. It may be possible to prove White’s conjecture for split matroids following an inductive argument on the length of such a relaxation sequence.

More generally, as suggested by Mateusz Michałek, one may look for other matroidal operations (besides the trivial ones such as direct sum) that preserve the validity of White’s conjecture.

With our inductive component (Theorem
1.7
) works beyond paving matroids, it is also interesting to look for other families of matroids related by stressed hyperplane relaxations, where (*) can be verified for all of their members while White’s conjecture can be verified for any of their members.

Another direction is to strengthen Theorem
1.5
to other versions of White’s conjecture. On the combinatorial side, we ask what is the minimum
\ell
that guarantees an exchange sequence exist? On the algebraic/geometric side, whether any of our techniques can be applied to prove some of the stronger variants, for example, whether one can produce suitable monomial order (perhaps using the induction process) so that the symmetric exchange binomials form a Gröbner basis of
I_{M}
?

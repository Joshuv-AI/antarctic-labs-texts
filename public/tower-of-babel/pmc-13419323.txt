SETD7-mediated epigenetic regulation of LncRNA XIST promotes trophoblast pyroptosis: a study in preeclampsia mouse and cell models
Authors: Xiaolan Zhao, Lingling Li, Yujue Wang, Zhenrong Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPre-eclampsia (PE) is a severe pregnancy-specific hypertensive disorder that poses significant risks to both maternal and fetal health. This study seeks to elucidate the mechanism by which SETD7 regulates trophoblast cell pyroptosis in PE through epigenetic mechanisms.MethodsPE mouse model was developed, and cell model was constructed using H/R-treated HTR-8/SVneo cells. SETD7, LncRNA XIST, FOXA1, TMBIM4, GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, and NLRP3 were detected via qRT-PCR or Western blot. ELISA was employed to measure IL-1β and IL-18. ChIP was employed to assess H3K4me3 and SETD7 enrichments on the promoter of LncRNA XIST. RIP assay was applied to verify interaction of YTHDF2/m6A with FOXA1 mRNA. The stability of FOXA1 mRNA was detected. Dual-luciferase reporter and ChIP were applied to validate the interaction between FOXA1 and TMBIM4.ResultsSETD7 exhibited high expression in placental tissues in PE. SETD7 knockdown attenuated IL-1β, IL-18, GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, and NLRP3 levels. SETD7 suppression ameliorated PE symptoms and inhibited H/R-induced pyroptosis. Mechanistically, SETD7 may upregulate LncRNA XIST expression by increasing H3K4me3 enrichment at the LncRNA XIST promoter. LncRNA XIST recruited YTHDF2 to FOXA1 mRNA, promoted the degradation of FOXA1 mRNA and inhibited FOXA1 expression through m6A modification, thereby reducing TMBIM4 transcription and inducing trophoblast cell pyroptosis via NLRP3 inflammasome activation.ConclusionIn the sFlt-1-induced PE mouse model and H/R-treated HTR-8/SVneo cells, SETD7 promotes placental trophoblast cell pyroptosis and exacerbates PE-like symptoms through the LncRNA XIST/FOXA1/TMBIM4 axis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13062-026-00831-2.

Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE) is a pregnancy-specific hypertensive disorder characterized by new-onset hypertension and proteinuria after 20 weeks of gestation, affecting approximately 2%–8% of pregnancies worldwide [1, 2]. Risk factors include primiparity, obesity, pregestational hypertension, and family history of PE, but the precise molecular regulatory network remains elusive [3]. PE pathogenesis centers on abnormal placentation, characterized by insufficient trophoblast invasion and incomplete remodeling of uterine spiral arteries, resulting in placental ischemia-hypoxia, excessive release of anti-angiogenic factors and pro-inflammatory cytokines, and ultimately systemic endothelial dysfunction [4]. Trophoblast cells are the functional core of the placenta, mediating nutrient exchange, hormone secretion, and immune tolerance at the maternal-fetal interface [5]. Dysregulated trophoblast death is a hallmark of PE. Pyroptosis, an inflammatory programmed cell death governed by the NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome and gasdermin family proteins, has recently emerged as a key contributor [6]. Current clinical interventions rely on antihypertensive medications and timely termination of pregnancy, which fail to target the root cause of placental dysfunction and often increase the risk of iatrogenic preterm birth, fetal growth restriction, and long-term metabolic disorders in offspring [7]. Thus, deciphering the mechanisms underlying placental injury in PE is critical for developing targeted therapeutic strategies.

SET domain-containing lysine methyltransferase 7 (SETD7) is a histone methyltransferase that specifically catalyzes histone H3 lysine 4 trimethylation (H3K4me3) modification to promote gene transcription and is widely involved in inflammation, cell differentiation, and oxidative stress [8, 9]. Dysregulated SETD7 expression is recognized as a contributing factor to PE pathophysiology [10]. Notably, SETD7 exhibits high expression in placental tissues under PE conditions and accelerates pyroptosis of trophoblast cells [11]. However, the precise mechanisms by which SETD7 regulates trophoblast pyroptosis and contributes to PE progression remain unclear. Therefore, we investigated the role of SETD7 as an epigenetic regulator in PE pathogenesis.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), as key regulatory molecules, orchestrate gene expression through epigenetic, chromatin remodeling, transcriptional, and post-transcriptional mechanisms, and play crucial roles in the regulatory networks underlying human diseases [12]. X-inactive specific transcript (XIST), a canonical lncRNA essential for X-chromosome inactivation, has emerged as a regulator of cell migration, invasion, and death in various pathological contexts [13]. XIST expression is augmented in patients with gestational diabetes mellitus and high glucose-induced trophoblast cells, where it impairs cell viability [14]. Importantly, XIST upregulation in pre-eclamptic tissues suppresses trophoblast cell proliferation and invasion [15]. Mechanistically, XIST transcription is tightly regulated by H3K4me3 modification [16], suggesting a potential interaction with SETD7. However, whether SETD7 mediates XIST expression via H3K4me3 to regulate trophoblast pyroptosis in PE has not been explored.

Forkhead box protein A1 (FOXA1) belongs to forkhead transcription factor family and exerts a pivotal role in cellular development [17]. FOXA1 is downregulated in PE placental tissues, correlating with impaired migration and invasion of trophoblast cells [18]. FOXA1 expression regulates trophoblast function and is modulated by the m6A reader YTH domain family member 2 (YTHDF2) [19]. In our early prediction, the Starbase and RPISeq databases predicted the interactions between LncRNA XIST and YTHDF2, as well as between FOXA1 and YTHDF2. As a transcription factor, FOXA1 enhances the proliferation, migration and invasion of gastric cancer cells by activating KRT7 [20]. JASPAR database analysis showed that FOXA1 has binding sites with transmembrane BAX inhibitor motif-containing protein 4 (TMBIM4). Additionally, TMBIM4 expression is downregulated in PE placental tissues, and TMBIM4 deficiency promotes trophoblast cell pyroptosis through NLRP3 inflammasome activation [21]. Therefore, we hypothesized that FOXA1 participates in trophoblast cell pyroptosis by transcriptionally promoting TMBIM4 expression.

Using soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1)-induced PE mouse models and hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-induced HTR-8/SVneo cells, this study aims to verify the role of the SETD7/LncRNA XIST/FOXA1/TMBIM4 axis in PE, providing new insights into the epigenetic regulation of PE.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All animal experimental protocols were confirmed by the Animal Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval Number: Lunshen (Research) No. 518, 2025) and implemented in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animal Facilities [22].

Establishment of the PE mouse model

Adult ICR mice (26–30 g) (Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Center, Shanghai, China) were acclimatized for at least 1 week before experiments, with free access to food and water, and maintained under a 12-hour light/dark cycle. Male and female mice (2:1) were housed together in a temperature-controlled room (18–22℃), and vaginal plugs of female mice were examined every morning. The presence of a vaginal plug confirmed successful mating, and that day was designated as gestational day 0.5 (E0.5). Female mice without vaginal plugs remained housed with males. Pregnant mice were randomly allocated to 6 experimental groups: sham operation group, PE group, PE + sh-NC group, PE + sh-SETD7 group, PE + sh-SETD7 + Ad-NC group, and PE + sh-SETD7 + Ad-XIST group, with 12 mice in each group. The PE mouse model was constructed as previously described [23]: pregnant ICR mice received a tail vein injection of 6 × 10⁸ pfu of adenovirus carrying mouse sFlt-1 gene at E8.5 to generate a well-validated sFlt-1 overexpression model. Control mice were administered an equal volume of adenovirus encoding mouse Fc protein (Ad-Fc). Adenovirus-encapsulated Ad-XIST or sh-SETD7 were co-administered with sFlt-1 adenovirus into mice at E8.5 for gene intervention. Hanbio (Shanghai, China) provided all aforementioned vectors and adenovirus packaging. Blood, urine, and tissues were procured at the experimental endpoint. Mice were euthanized at E18.5, and fetuses and placentas were retrieved and weighed for further analysis. Prior to euthanasia, all mice underwent blood pressure measurement and proteinuria assessment. Following euthanasia, 6 mice were randomly chosen from each group for pathological staining, with the remaining 6 mice for tissue homogenization.

Blood pressure measurement

Following the acclimation period, the systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of mice were monitored daily by a CODA non-invasive plethysmography blood pressure transducer (Kent Scientific Corporation, Torrington, CT, USA) before cohousing, and these values were recorded as the baseline blood pressure of mice prior to pregnancy. After confirmation of pregnancy, blood pressure was monitored in each pregnant mouse at E7.5, E9.5, E11.5, E13.5, E15.5, and E17.5. For each measurement, each pregnant mouse underwent 10 repeated measurements; three measurement results with a SBP difference of ≤ 6 mmHg were selected from these measurements, and the average value was calculated and documented as the blood pressure at that time point.

Albumin/creatinine ratio

To measure proteinuria, urine samples were collected from mice at E17.5 using metabolic cages. Urinary albumin levels were determined using the Albuwell M ELISA Kit (Exocell Inc., Philadelphia, PA, USA), and urinary creatinine concentrations were measured via a creatinine assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio was calculated as the proteinuria index.

Tissue staining

Placental and renal tissues of mice were fixed in 10% neutral formalin at 4 °C for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, and cut into 4 μm thick sections. Placental tissues were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) using an H&E staining kit (Vector Labs, Burlingame, CA, USA), while renal tissues were subjected to periodic acid-Schiff staining. All stained sections were examined under a microscope (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Baden-Württemberg, Germany).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Isolation of total RNA from placental tissues or HTR-8/SVneo cells was conducted via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and the protein concentration was assayed via a UV spectrophotometer (NanoDrop 2000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). Next, RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA via the PrimeScript RT Kit (Takara, Beijing, China). PCR amplification was executed on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using the SYBR Premix EX Taq kit (Takara). With β-actin as the internal reference, the relative expression levels of genes were computed via the 2−ΔΔCt method. The PCR primers are exhibited in Table 1.

Western blot

Total protein extraction from placental tissues and HTR-8/SVneo cells was executed via radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) supplemented with a phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride protease inhibitor and phosphatase inhibitors. The protein concentration was assessed via a bicinchoninic acid kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Protein (30 µg) was separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. The membranes underwent blockade in 5% non-fat milk dissolved in tris-buffered saline with ‌tween 20 (TBST) and incubated with primary antibodies against SETD7 (1:3000, 24840-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, Hubei, China), NLRP3 (1:2000, 30109-1-AP, Proteintech), cleaved Caspase-1 (1:1000, #89332 / #4199, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), Gasdermin D N-terminal fragment (GSDMD-N) (1:5000, ab215203, Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), ‌YTHDF2 (1:1000, ab220163, Abcam), FOXA1 (1:1000, ab170933, Abcam), TMBIM4 (1:1000, abs148245, Absin, Shanghai, China), and β-actin (1:2000, ab8227, Abcam) at 4 °C overnight. Next day, the membranes were rinsed three times with TBST and underwent incubation with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:7000, SA00001-2, Proteintech) for 1 h. Finally, the immunoreactive signals were measured using a Bio-Rad Gel Imaging System with enhanced chemiluminescence reagent, and the gray values were analyzed using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software to calculate the relative protein levels.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18 levels in tissue homogenates and cells were quantified using ELISA kits (JYM0083Hu, JYM0092Hu, Wuhan, Hubei, China). The absorbance (optical density, OD) was measured at 450 nm. A standard curve was plotted with standard substance concentration as the abscissa and OD value as the ordinate to generate a linear regression equation. The correlation coefficient (r) between linear regression and the expected concentration was above 0.99 for all assays. Interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18 concentrations were calculated according to the standard curve. All experiments were repeated three times.

Cell culture

The human villous trophoblast cell line HTR-8/SVneo (ProCell, Wuhan, Hubei, China) was maintained in RPMI 1640 medium (Invitrogen) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Invitrogen) at 37 °C under a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. To simulate hypoxic conditions in placenta, the cells were transferred to a hypoxic workstation (AW200SG, Electrotek, UK) with 2% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 93% N₂ at 37 °C. For the normoxic group, the cells were cultured in 20% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 75% N₂. The culture medium was changed every 2–3 days.

Cell treatment

Overexpression vectors of SETD7, XIST, YTHDF2, and FOXA1, their corresponding short hairpin RNAs (sh-SETD7, sh-FOXA1, sh-TMBIM4), empty vector (oe-NC), and shRNA negative control (sh-NC) were purchased from GenePharma (Shanghai, China) and transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). Briefly, cells were seeded into 12-well plates at 1 × 10⁵ cells/well. The transfection reagent was mixed with the specified plasmids and applied to wells, followed by 6-hour incubation (37 °C, 5% CO₂). Subsequently, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium, and cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

HTR-8/SVneo cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 5 × 10³ cells per well overnight and underwent 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime) treatment for 2 h at 37 °C. The absorbance of HTR-8/SVneo cells at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Transwell assay

Cell invasion assay was performed using BD BioCoat Matrigel Invasion Chambers (pore size: 8 μm, BD, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Briefly, cells (1 × 10⁵ cells per well) were seeded into the upper chambers precoated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, Bedford, MA, USA). Complete medium containing 10% FBS was applied to the lower chambers. After 24 h, the invading cells attached to the membrane of the lower chambers were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet working solution. The number of invading cells was counted under a microscope (IX51; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

ChIP assay was executed via the EpiQuik ChIP kit (Epigentek, Farmingdale, NY, USA). Cells or tissues were treated with 1% formaldehyde and glycine solution, followed by chromatin fragmentation. Subsequently, fragmented DNA was incubated with 2 µg of H3K4me3 antibody (ab8580, Abcam), SETD7 antibody (24840-1-AP, Proteintech), or immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody (ab172730, Abcam). The beads were washed with wash buffer and then eluted to purify chromosomal DNA. The enrichment of the LncRNA XIST promoter region was detected by qPCR. The primers used for qPCR are listed in Table 1.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RIP assay was performed using the EZ Magna RIP RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation kit (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). Cells or tissue homogenates were treated with RIP lysis buffer, and then incubated with anti-YTHDF2 (1:1000, ab220163, Abcam) or IgG (1:50, ab172730, Abcam) conjugated to magnetic beads to capture immunoprecipitated magnetic beads. The magnetic beads underwent incubation with cell lysates to immunoprecipitate RNA-binding protein-RNA complexes, followed by elution of RNA from the complexes. The enrichment of XIST and FOXA1 in the immunoprecipitated RNA was assessed by RT-PCR.

RNA pull-down assay

XIST and FOXA1 were transcribed via T7 RNA polymerase (Roche, Basel, Switzerland), purified utilizing the RNeasy plus mini kit (QIAGEN, Germantown, MD, USA), and subjected to biotin labeling with the Biotin RNA Labeling Mix (Roche Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN, USA). Biotinylated RNA was incubated with HTR-8/SVneo cell lysates. Streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads (Invitrogen) were introduced, and the eluted fractions underwent Western blot analysis.

RNA stability assay

Cells underwent actinomycin D treatment (5 µg/mL, MedChem Express, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) for 0, 3, 6, and 9 h to block transcription. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The transcriptional levels of FOXA1 mRNA were measured via qPCR.

Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP)-PCR

The relative levels of m6A in total RNA were quantified using the EpiQuik m6A RNA methylation quantification kit (Epigentek). Extracted total RNA was fragmented with the Ambion RNA fragmentation reagent (Ambion, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Subsequently, the fragmented RNA was incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-m6A antibody or IgG conjugated to Dynabeads (Invitrogen). The fragmented RNA with m6A was eluted and purified via an RNA purification kit (Qiagen). Enriched fragments were analysed via RT-qPCR.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Wild-type (WT) and mutant-type (MUT) sequences containing FOXA1 binding sites were cloned into the pGL3-Basic reporter vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). These vectors, together with the FOXA1 overexpression vector or its negative control (NC), were co-transfected into HTR-8/SVneo cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). Luciferase activity was then measured 48 h later using the dual-luciferase reporter assay kit (Promega).

Bioinformatics analysis

The interactions between LncRNA XIST and YTHDF2, as well as FOXA1 and YTHDF2, were predicted using Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) [24] and the RPISeq database (http://pridb.gdcb.iastate.edu/RPISeq/index.html) [25]. The presence of m6A modification sites on FOXA1 was predicted utilizing the SRAMP website (http://www.cuilab.cn/m6asiteapp/old) [25]. The binding sites of transcription factor FOXA1 with the TMBIM4 promoter sequence were predicted using the JASPAR database (http://jaspar.genereg.net/) [26].

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed via GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). The normality of the distribution of measurement data was tested by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and the data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. The t-test was used for comparisons between two groups, while one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for multiple group comparisons, followed by the Tukey honestly significant difference (HSD) post‑hoc test for multiple comparison correction. All cell experiments were independently repeated at least three times with technical replicates set in each experiment. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

SETD7 inhibition ameliorates PE symptoms and relieves pyroptosis in placental tissues

PE mice exhibited significantly increased SBP and DBP (both P < 0.001, Fig. 1A), a marked elevation in proteinuria (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B), and lowered fetal and placental weights (both P < 0.01, Fig. 1C and D). Placental tissues exhibited immature villous blood vessels and disordered distribution of trophoblast cells (Fig. 1E), while the kidneys presented abnormal glomerular vascular branching and mild swelling of glomerular capillary endothelial cells (Fig. 1E). SETD7 showed high expression in placental tissues (P < 0.001, Fig. 1F and G). We hypothesized that SETD7 contributed to PE progression. Thus, we silenced SETD7 expression in the placenta via sh-SETD7 adenovirus (P < 0.01, Fig. 1F and G). SETD7 inhibition reduced blood pressure and proteinuria (both P < 0.01, Fig. 1A and B), increased fetal and placental weights (both P < 0.05, Fig. 1C and D), and ameliorated pathological damage in placental and renal tissues (Fig. 1E). Additionally, the protein levels of GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, and NLRP3 were elevated in placental tissues (all P < 0.01, Fig. 1G), and IL-1β and IL-18 levels were markedly augmented (both P < 0.01, Fig. 1H). These findings indicated that sFlt-1 adenovirus induced pyroptosis in mouse placental tissues, whereas treatment with sh-SETD7 adenovirus decreased the expression of GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, NLRP3, IL-1β, and IL-18 (P < 0.01, Fig. 1G and H). Collectively, SETD7 silencing relieves PE symptoms and pyroptosis in placental tissues.

SETD7 inhibits proliferation and invasion and induces pyroptosis of H/R-treated HTR-8/SVneo cells

To investigate the role of SETD7 in proliferation, invasion, and pyroptosis of trophoblast cells during PE progression, we silenced or overexpressed SETD7 using sh-SETD7 or oe-SETD7 (all P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). Transfection with sh-SETD7 effectively reduced SETD7 expression in PE cell model, while transfection with oe-SETD7 increased SETD7 expression (all P < 0.001, Fig. 2B and E). Under H/R conditions, the proliferation and invasion capacities of HTR-8/SVneo cells were lowered, while inhibition of SETD7 markedly enhanced the proliferation and invasion capacities of H/R-treated HTR-8/SVneo cells, whereas upregulation of SETD7 further inhibited these capacities (all P < 0.05, Fig. 2C and D). Next, we found that inhibition of SETD7 decreased the protein levels of GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, and NLRP3 (all P < 0.05, Fig. 2E), as well as IL-1β and IL-18 (all P < 0.05, Fig. 2F), while upregulation of SETD7 promoted their expression (all P < 0.05, Fig. 2E and F). Collectively, SETD7 inhibits the proliferation and invasion of PE cell model and promotes cellular pyroptosis.

SETD7 promotes LncRNA XIST expression via H3K4me3 modification and LncRNA XIST recruits YTHDF2 to degrade FOXA1

Previous studies have shown that SETD7 regulates tumor growth and metastasis by promoting H3K4me3 modification on the promoter of lncRNAs [27], and LncRNA XIST expression is upregulated in the placental tissues of PE patients [15]. However, whether SETD7 participates in PE progression by regulating XIST expression through an epigenetic mechanism remains unclear. We detected the enrichment of SETD7 and H3K4me3 in the lncRNA XIST promoter region using ChIP assays and found that SETD7 and H3K4me3 were enriched at the LncRNA XIST promoter in HTR-8/SVneo cells, and SETD7 knockdown reduced the enrichment of SETD7 in the XIST promoter region, accompanied by a prominent decline in H3K4me3 enrichment levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A and B). The expression of LncRNA XIST was elevated, which was markedly decreased after SETD7 knockdown (P < 0.01, Fig. 3C and D). These results suggest that SETD7 may participate in the upregulation of XIST by promoting H3K4me3 enrichment at the XIST promoter region. It has been reported that FOXA1 enhances the proliferation, migration, and invasion by inhibiting IL-8 transcription. The Starbase and RPISeq databases predicted interactions among LncRNA XIST, YTHDF2, and FOXA1 (Supplementary Figs. 1A and B). RIP assay results showed that YTHDF2 bound to both LncRNA XIST and FOXA1 mRNA in HTR-8/SVneo cells (both P < 0.001, Fig. 3E). RNA pull-down assay further confirmed that LncRNA XIST and FOXA1 mRNA could directly bind to YTHDF2 (Fig. 3F). Overexpression of LncRNA XIST caused an upregulation in LncRNA XIST levels (P < 0.001) and a marked downregulation of FOXA1 mRNA and protein levels (both P < 0.05) in HTR-8/SVneo cells, while the mRNA and protein expression of YTHDF2 remained unaffected (Supplementary Fig. 1C, Fig. 3G). Upregulation of YTHDF2 in HTR-8/SVneo cells (P < 0.001) led to downregulation of FOXA1 mRNA and protein levels (P < 0.01, Supplementary Fig. 1D, Fig. 3H), without affecting LncRNA XIST expression (Supplementary Fig. 1D). These findings demonstrated that YTHDF2 may modulate FOXA1 expression by forming a complex with LncRNA XIST. Following actinomycin D treatment, overexpression of YTHDF2 and LncRNA XIST accelerated the degradation of FOXA1 mRNA (P < 0.01, Fig. 3I), indicating that LncRNA XIST and YTHDF2 modulate FOXA1 expression by affecting the stability of FOXA1 mRNA.

YTHDF2 is a key m6A reader that typically regulates mRNA stability via m6A modification. Multiple m6A modification sites in FOXA1 mRNA were predicted using the SRAMP database (Supplementary Fig. 1E). As shown by MeRIP assay results, the level of m6A modification was increased in tissues and cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 3J and K). YTHDF2 enrichment was attenuated after SETD7 inhibition, while it was enhanced after SETD7 overexpression (P < 0.001, Fig. 3L and M). After cell treatment with actinomycin D, we found that inhibition of SETD7 slowed down the degradation of FOXA1 mRNA, whereas SETD7 overexpression exerted the opposite effect (P < 0.01, Fig. 3N). Additionally, FOXA1 expression was decreased in tissues and cells (both P < 0.01), and was upregulated after SETD7 inhibition (P < 0.05, Fig. 3O and R). Collectively, SETD7 promotes LncRNA XIST expression by enhancing H3K4me3 modification; LncRNA XIST recruits YTHDF2 to FOXA1 mRNA, and facilitates FOXA1 mRNA degradation via m6A modification, thereby inhibiting FOXA1 expression.

FOXA1 promotes TMBIM4 expression and participates in trophoblast cell pyroptosis

The transcription factor FOXA1 binds to gene promoter regions to increase their expression [28]. Additionally, TMBIM4 expression is downregulated in PE placental tissues, and TMBIM4 deficiency promotes trophoblast cell pyroptosis induced by NLRP3 inflammasome activation [21]. Prediction using the JASPAR database revealed that FOXA1 has binding sites on the TMBIM4 promoter (Fig. 6A). Dual-luciferase reporter assay demonstrated that transfection with the FOXA1 overexpression plasmid (oe-FOXA1) led to a significant increase in luciferase activity of the WT-promoter plasmid (P < 0.01), while the luciferase activity of the MUT-promoter plasmid remained unchanged (P > 0.05, Fig. 6B). Subsequently, FOXA1 enrichment on the TMBIM4 promoter was reduced in tissues and cells (P < 0.01); FOXA1 enrichment levels were increased after sh-SETD7 treatment (P < 0.01), whereas SETD7 upregulation decreased FOXA1 enrichment on the TMBIM4 promoter (P < 0.01, Fig. 6C and D). Furthermore, TMBIM4 expression was decreased in tissues and cells (P < 0.01); TMBIM4 expression was increased after sh-SETD7 treatment (P < 0.01), while upregulation of SETD7 reduced TMBIM4 expression (P < 0.01, Fig. 6E and H). Collectively, FOXA1 binds to the TMBIM4 promoter to promote the transcription and expression of TMBIM4.

Collectively, SETD7 promotes trophoblast pyroptosis and exacerbates PE symptoms via the XIST/FOXA1/TMBIM4 axis.

Discussion

PE remains one of the leading causes of maternal and perinatal morbidity worldwide, with its pathogenesis closely associated with abnormal placentation and excessive trophoblast cell death [1]. Epigenetic modifications and lncRNAs have emerged as key regulators of PE progression [29, 30]. Specifically, the mechanism of SETD7 in PE has not been elucidated. The present study fills this gap by identifying a novel regulatory axis that SETD7 promotes lncRNA XIST expression via H3K4me3 modification, and XIST recruits the m6A reader YTHDF2 to degrade FOXA1 mRNA, thereby downregulating TMBIM4 and activating NLRP3-dependent trophoblast pyroptosis (Fig. 9).

SETD7 is a histone methyltransferase that specifically catalyzes H3K4me3 to activate gene transcription and is increasingly recognized as a critical modulator of inflammatory responses [31, 32]. For instance, SETD7 mediates H3K4me3 to promote the expression of p65/RelA, thereby activating the NF-κB signaling pathway and inflammatory responses [33]. Specifically, inhibition of SETD7 can suppress isoflurane-induced neuroinflammation and NLRP3 activation in mice, and alleviate cellular pyroptosis [34]. SETD7 promotes the binding of Cbx1 to H3K9me2/3 and inhibits the transcription of Sirt5, which in turn promotes glutaminase, induces excitotoxicity, and exacerbates cerebral ischemic injury [35]. Our findings demonstrate that SETD7 exhibited high expression in placental tissues of PE mice, and SETD7 inhibition reduced blood pressure, proteinuria, and placental/renal pathological damage, concomitant with decreased NLRP3, GSDMD-N, and IL-1β and restored fetal/placental weights. Another study has found that after SETD7 overexpression, the migration and invasion of H/R-treated HTR-8/SVneo cells were impaired, and pyroptosis was accelerated [11]. Extending these observations, we show that under H/R conditions, SETD7 knockdown enhanced cell proliferation and invasion, while SETD7 overexpression exacerbated pyroptosis. These results establish SETD7 as a pro-pathogenic factor in PE, functioning as an epigenetic switch to drive trophoblast pyroptosis.

Extensive evidence indicates that aberrant lncRNA expression serves as a potential biomarker for PE and is associated with the pathogenesis of PE, involving the migration and invasion of trophoblast cells [36]. Our study advances this knowledge by identifying SETD7-mediated H3K4me3 modification as a key upstream regulator of XIST in PE. ChIP assays confirmed that SETD7 bound to the XIST promoter and enhanced H3K4me3 enrichment, leading to XIST upregulation in PE placentas and H/R-treated cells. Consistently, lncRNA XIST expression is upregulated in PE patients and interacts with miR-340-5p/KCNJ16 via the ceRNA mechanism, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation and invasion and inducing cell apoptosis [15]. Inhibition of XIST alleviates the impairment of trophoblast viability induced by high glucose via rescuing miR-497-5p [14]. Moreover, knockout of XIST can suppress NLRP3/caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells and alleviate diabetic nephropathy [37]. Interestingly, our results uncover that rather than acting as a ceRNA, XIST served as a scaffold to recruit YTHDF2 to target mRNAs, bridging histone modification and m6A RNA modification. We validated the direct interaction between XIST, YTHDF2, and FOXA1 mRNA, and confirmed that this complex accelerated FOXA1 mRNA degradation. In summary, LncRNA XIST inhibits FOXA1 expression, thereby leading to impaired migration and proliferation of trophoblast cells.

Subsequently, we found that after FOXA1 knockdown, the proliferative and invasive capacities of trophoblast cells were decreased, and pyroptosis was exacerbated. Previous studies have reported that the protein level of FOXA1 is downregulated in trophoblast cells from patients with recurrent miscarriage, and that FOXA1 can prevent trophoblast dysfunction and miscarriage by maintaining homologous recombination repair [38]. FOXA1 expression is negatively correlated with SBP and DBP, and FOXA1 silencing can impair the migration and invasion of HTR-8/SVneo cells and predict poor prognosis of early-onset PE [18]. Mechanistically, FOXA1 is identified as a transcriptional regulator of the NNMT gene in villous tissue, and exerts a potential protective effect against trophoblast dysfunction [39]. Our study found that FOXA1 transcriptionally promoted TMBIM4 expression in the pathology of PE. TMBIM4 has been identified as a novel PE-associated protein, and its deficiency enhances NLRP3 activity and induces pyroptosis [21]. TMBIM4 gene deficiency may increase embryonic damage in a toxic environment [40]. TMBIM4 deletion leads to cardiac asymmetry defects and causes embryonic depolarization [41]. These evidences highlight the critical role of TMBIM4 in embryonic development, and its potential mechanism may involve regulatory effects on processes such as trophoblast pyroptosis, autophagy, and apoptosis.

We acknowledge certain unresolved limitations of this study. First, the sFlt-1 adenovirus tail vein injection PE model in this study (injection at E8.5, tissue collection at E18.5) has been widely employed to simulate key late-onset phenotypes of PE, including anti-angiogenic imbalance, hypertension, and proteinuria [23]. It should be noted that this model primarily induces systemic endothelial dysfunction through elevated circulating sFlt-1, and therefore cannot fully recapitulate the early-onset human PE characteristics of inadequate trophoblast invasion and impaired spiral artery remodeling. Nevertheless, this study focuses on placental trophoblast pyroptosis. Overexpression of sFlt-1 can induce placental oxidative stress and an inflammatory microenvironment, directly activating the NLRP3/GSDMD-N pyroptosis pathway, which is highly consistent with the pathological process of increased placental sFlt-1 and enhanced trophoblast pyroptosis in clinical PE patients. Furthermore, systemic delivery via tail vein injection results in widespread gene manipulation across multiple maternal organs, precluding the distinction between local placental and systemic effects. Additionally, as this model primarily recapitulates late-stage PE phenotypes, it is limited in its ability to reproduce early trophoblast invasion defects. Future studies may consider employing gene vectors driven by placenta-specific promoters (e.g., Cyp19, Tpbpa) in combination with local uterine artery injection or placenta-tropic AAV9, to achieve placenta-restricted expression, and to quantify the contribution of systemic effects by detecting relevant molecular markers in maternal liver and kidney tissues. Second, only the immortalized HTR-8/SVneo trophoblast cell line was used in this study, and no validation was performed in primary trophoblasts or other trophoblast cell lines including JEG-3 and BeWo. In follow-up studies, we will isolate primary human trophoblasts and repeat key experiments using the above cell lines. Third, the expression levels of SETD7, XIST, FOXA1, TMBIM4 and pyroptosis-related proteins in placental tissues from PE patients were not verified, and their correlations with clinical parameters were not analyzed. In the future, placental samples from PE patients and normal controls will be collected for subsequent validation via qRT-PCR, Western blot and IHC. Fourth, SETD7 is classically defined as an H3K4me1 histone methyltransferase. The mechanism by which SETD7 promotes H3K4me3 enrichment at the XIST promoter in our study (direct catalysis versus indirect regulation) remains unclear. Further ChIP rescue experiments using a SETD7 enzymatic activity-deficient mutant (H297A) will be conducted to distinguish direct from indirect effects. Fifth, pharmacological rescue experiments with the GSDMD inhibitor disulfiram or the Caspase-1 inhibitor VX-765 were not conducted. The inhibitors will be applied in combination in cellular and animal models to verify whether pyroptosis acts as an essential downstream effector of the SETD7 regulatory axis. Sixth, the cause of SETD7 upregulation in preeclampsia was not explored. Accumulated evidence has demonstrated that oxidative stress [42] and HIF-1α [43] serve as potential upstream regulators. Subsequently, NAC (ROS inhibitor) and YC-1 (HIF-1α inhibitor) will be utilized in H/R-stimulated trophoblasts to validate this regulatory mechanism.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study identifies the SETD7/XIST/FOXA1/TMBIM4 axis as a novel epigenetic regulatory pathway governing trophoblast pyroptosis in PE mouse and cell models. By uncovering the crosstalk between H3K4me3 and m6A modifications, we provide new insights into PE pathogenesis. Our findings may improve knowledge of epigenetic regulation in pregnancy disorders and lay the groundwork for developing mechanism-based treatments for PE.

Radiation reaction force for scalar-tensor theories in effective-one-body formalism
Tamanna Jain 1,2, Piero Rettegno 3

Abstract
Whilst most of the binary configurations in modified theories of gravity are studied under quasi-circular orbit limit, eccentricity effects could play a significant role in future gravitational wave detections. We derive the gravitational radiation-reaction force for nonspinning eccentric orbits within the effective-one-body (EOB) description for the massless scalar-tensor theories up to 1.5 post-Newtonian (PN) order. The effects in such theories start at 1 / c 3 1/c^{3} and interestingly, the 1.5PN order effect is due to the radiation reaction square effects which is a conservative effect. The results derived here can be implemented in the quasi-circular EOB-based waveform models to construct waveform templates for generic orbit binaries within massless scalar-tensor theories of gravity.

II
Brief Overview: Scalar-tensor

In this work, we consider the massless scalar-tensor theories for which the action is described by the coupling of the usual Einstein-Hilbert action with the scalar field
\varphi
as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}(R-2g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+S_{m}[\Psi,{\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}^{2}g_{\mu\nu}]~.

(1)

Here,
g_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein metric,
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\Psi
collectively denotes the matter fields,
g\equiv\det(g_{\mu\nu})
and
G
is the bare Newton’s constant.
The above action is expressed in the Einstein frame, which is obtained by a conformal transformation of the physical frame (Jordan frame) metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
as

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}={\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}^{2}g_{\mu\nu}\ ,

(2)

to simplify the calculations, where
\mathcal{A}(\varphi)
is called the coupling function. The scalar field couples minimally to Einstein frame metric and non-minimally to the matter fields, however it is non-minimally coupled to the metric in the Jordan frame.
The scalar-tensor theory is uniquely fixed when the function
{\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}
is defined,
General Relativity is recovered when
{\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}=cst
.
We adopt the conventions and notations of Damour and Esposito-Farese (DEF, hereafter)
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1992
;
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1996b
.

The field equations for the scalar-tensor theories in Einstein frame are derived in
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1992
and the coupling between the matter and the scalar field in the equations of motion is measured by the parameter

\alpha(\varphi)=\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal{A}}{\partial\varphi}~.

(3)

The effective gravitational constant in scalar-tensor theories depends on the scalar-field so that the compact object size and its internal gravity vary with the scalar field.
Therefore, as suggested by
Eardley 1975
, the compact, self-gravitating objects in scalar-tensor theories can be considered as point particles described by the mass function
m_{J}(\phi)
, dependent on the value of scalar field at the position of particle.
The matter action is then given by

S_{m}=-\sum_{J=A,B}\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\lambda}}{m}_{J}(\varphi)~,

(4)

where
{m}_{J}(\varphi)
is the Einstein frame mass of body
J
, and
\lambda

is an affine parameter.
Since
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}={\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
, the Jordan frame mass is then defined as

\tilde{m}_{J}(\varphi)=\frac{{m}_{J}(\varphi)}{\mathcal{A}(\varphi)}~.

(5)

The dimensionless body-dependent parameters that describe the scalar field effect in the Einstein-frame up to the 2PN order are defined using the Einstein frame mass function
m_{J}(\varphi)
following Refs.
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1992
;
Damour and Esposito-Farese 1996b
i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha_{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\ln m_{J}(\varphi)}{d\varphi},

\displaystyle\beta_{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\alpha_{J}}{d\varphi}~.

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\beta_{J}}{d\varphi}~.

(6)

The energy flux in Refs.
Bernard et al. 2022
;
Lange et al. 2017
;
Lange et al. 2018
is expressed in Jordan frame equivalents of the above parameters.
As we work in DEF conventions, we will make use of the relations given in Table I of Ref.
Jain et al. 2023
to perform the conversion of Jordan frame parameters to DEF ones.

III
Brief Overview: EOB & radiation reaction force

In this section, before proceeding to the computations of the radiation-reaction force in ST theory, we briefly review the EOB formalism proposed in Refs.
Buonanno and Damour 1999
;
Buonanno and Damour 2000
as a way to extend the validity of PN results beyond the weak-field and slow-motion regime by resumming the PN results. The three main features of the EOB approach are: description of the conservative (Hamiltonian) dynamics of a two-body system, expression for the radiation-reaction effects, and the description of GWs emitted by the coalescence of a compact binary.

The EOB approach maps the relative motion of two bodies with masses
m_{A}
and
m_{B}
into the motion of an effective particle of mass
\mu=m_{A}m_{B}/M
, where
M=m_{A}+m_{B}
, moving in an external metric. Throughout this paper, we describe the motion of the effective particle with rescaled polar coordinates
\left(r,\,\theta,\,\phi\right)
.

The conservative EOB dynamics is completely described by the following effective metric up to 2PN order

ds^{2}_{\rm eff}=-A(r)c^{2}dt^{2}_{\rm eff}+B(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin(\theta)^{2}d\phi^{2}\right)

(7)

where
A(r)
and
B(r)
are the two EOB potentials,
t_{\rm eff}
is the coordinate time of the effective EOB metric, and
r
is the radial separation in EOB coordinates.
In this work, we specialise to nonspinning systems and equatorial motions, i.e. set
\theta={\pi}/{2}
.
The generalisation of the EOB formalism beyond 2PN order is attained by introducing the non-geodesic contributions to the effective metric (see, e.g., Ref.
Damour et al. 2000
for details).

The metric above, Eq. (
7
), can be used to obtain an EOB Hamiltonian, hereafter denoted by
H
, describing the conservative dynamics of generic binary orbits, both bound and unbound.
The EOB Hamiltonian in the scalar-tensor theories for non-spinning binary is known up to 3PN order
Julié and Deruelle 2017
;
Julié 2018
;
Jain et al. 2023
;
Jain 2023
;
Julié et al. 2023
.
However, the current EOB realization of the radiation reaction force and the emitted waveform in scalar-tensor theories is limited to the case of quasi-circular orbits.
The main aim of our study is to extend the radiation reaction force along general orbits for nonspinning compact objects.

In GR, different approaches have been employed to study the gravitational radiation reaction. In this work, we will follow the
balance
approach developed in Refs.
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
, i.e. we relate the energy and angular momentum losses of the system to the corresponding gravitational radiation at infinity.
This approach was already used in Refs.
Bini and Damour 2012
;
Khalil et al. 2021
to derive the radiation reaction force for GR within the EOB-formalism.

For nonprecessing binaries, when the motion is planar, Hamilton’s equations read

\displaystyle\dot{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial p_{r}}~,\quad\dot{p}_{r}=-\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial r}+\mathcal{F}_{r}~,

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial p_{\phi}}~,\quad\dot{p}_{\phi}=-\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial\phi}+\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\,,

(8)

where ‘dot’ denotes differentiation with respect to time and radiation reaction forces (
\mathcal{F}_{r},\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}
)
Buonanno and Damour 2000
;
Buonanno et al. 2006
have been added to the conservative equations.

The force components
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{F}_{r}
can be determined by computing the variation in the energy

\displaystyle E_{\rm{system}}=H(r,p_{r},p_{\phi})-(m_{A}+m_{B})c^{2}~,

(9)

and the angular momentum

\displaystyle J_{\rm{system}}=p_{\phi}~,

(10)

of the binary system using Hamilton’s equations, Eq. (
III
) above, and comparing them with the corresponding energy
\Phi_{E}
and angular momentum
\Phi_{J}
fluxes emitted in the form of gravitational waves at infinity.
However, we can not simply equate the energy and angular momentum losses of the system to
-\Phi_{E}
and
-\Phi_{J}
, respectively, as the energy and angular momentum of the system have additional terms called the Schott terms
Schott 1915
, say
E_{\rm Schott}
and
J_{\rm Schott}
, due to the system’s interaction with the radiation field.

Using Hamilton’s equations, and remembering
H
is independent of
\phi
, we obtain that the time derivatives of the energy and angular momentum (radiation losses) of the system are

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm{system}}

\displaystyle=\dot{r}\,\mathcal{F}_{r}+\dot{\phi}\,\mathcal{F}_{\phi}~,

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{\rm{system}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\phi}~.

(11)

The balance equation after including the Schott terms read

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm{system}}+\dot{E}_{\rm{Schott}}+\Phi_{E}

\displaystyle=0~,

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{\rm{system}}+\dot{J}_{\rm{Schott}}+\Phi_{J}

\displaystyle=0~.

(12)

Combining Eqs. (
III
) and (
III
), we obtain the equations to solve for the two components of the force are

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}\dot{r}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\dot{\phi}-\dot{E}_{\rm{Schott}}-\Phi_{E}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\dot{J}_{\rm{Schott}}-\Phi_{J}~.

(13)

The far zone energy and angular momentum fluxes are gauge invariant but the radiation reaction forces
\mathcal{F}_{r}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
, and the Schott terms could be gauge dependent quantities. The gauge freedom in radiation reaction force at the leading order in GR, i.e. 2.5PN order, was first discussed by Refs.
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
where they found that this gauge freedom is related to the freedom to change the coordinate system. Following this, Ref.
Bini and Damour 2012
showed that the gauge freedom of radiation reaction term at 2.5PN in GR is also related to gauge freedom in defining Schott terms, i.e., we can consider a vanishing
{J}_{\rm{Schott}}
.

However, due to the presence of a non-vanishing dipole in scalar-tensor theories, in addition to the quadrupolar radiation, there is also a scalar dipolar radiation in scalar-tensor theories starting at a lower PN order. In Sec.
IV.0.1
, we study the presence (or lack of) gauge freedom at 1.5PN order.

\displaystyle+\Big(4+\nu-\frac{17}{2}\nu^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm AB}-7\gamma_{\rm AB}\nu\Big)\tilde{p}_{e}^{2}\tilde{p}_{r,e}^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+2\langle\beta\rangle-\beta_{+}\Big)\tilde{p}_{e}^{2}+\Big(7+\frac{29}{2}\nu+\frac{9}{4}\nu^{2}+\gamma_{\rm AB}[10+7\nu]+3\gamma_{\rm AB}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle\beta\rangle[\nu-2]+2\beta_{+}\nu+\langle\delta\rangle\Big)\tilde{p}_{r,e}^{2}\Bigg]\Bigg\}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{5}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{e}}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\Bigg[-\frac{\nu}{2r_{e}}\tilde{p}_{e}^{2}+\frac{G_{\rm AB}M}{r_{e}^{2}}\left(1+\gamma_{\rm AB}+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\nu}{r_{e}}\tilde{p}_{r,e}^{2}\Bigg]+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\Bigg[(\nu+3\nu^{2})\frac{\tilde{p}_{e}^{4}}{8r_{e}}+\nu\frac{\tilde{p}_{e}^{2}\tilde{p}_{r,e}^{2}}{2r_{e}}+3\nu^{2}\frac{\tilde{p}_{r,e}^{4}}{2r_{e}}

\displaystyle+\langle\beta\rangle+\beta_{+}\nu-\frac{\langle\delta\rangle}{2}\Big)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{5}}\right).

. The energy flux in EOB coordinates is expressed as

\displaystyle\phi_{E}^{e}=G_{\rm AB}c^{3}\phi_{E}~,

(29)

where
\phi_{E}^{e}
is the energy flux in EOB coordinate system and
\phi_{E}
is the physical energy flux expressed in harmonic coordinates. Similarly, the angular momentum flux in EOB coordinates can be expressed as

\displaystyle\phi_{J}^{e}=c^{3}\frac{\phi_{J}}{M}~,

(30)

where
\phi_{J}^{e}
is the angular momentum flux in EOB coordinate system and
\phi_{J}
is the physical angular momentum flux expressed in harmonic coordinates. The explicit expression of EOB coordinate energy and angular momentum flux up to 2PN is given in the supplementary file attached.

V
Radiation-Reaction

In this section, we use the EOB coordinate energy and angular momentum flux to derive the generic orbit radiation reaction force within EOB formalism for non-spinning systems.
Since from now on we will only work in EOB coordinates, we will drop the
e
subscript and tilde to define the dimensionless variables and denote EOB phase space coordinates with
(r,\varphi,p_{r},p_{\varphi})
.

Now, let us remember that there are two contributions to the fluxes in scalar-tensor theory, i.e. a scalar contribution in addition to the usual tensorial one. In this work, we will compute the radiation reaction force due to both the scalar and tensorial contributions up to 1.5PN order.

Numerically, in most regions of the parameter space the scalar fluxes are small contributions to the total energy or angular momentum fluxes.

Radiation reaction in the EOB framework was first computed in Ref.
Buonanno and Damour 2000
for quasi-circular inspirals in GR.
The arbitrary gauge freedom discussed in Refs.
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
in the radiation reaction force was utilised in
Buonanno and Damour 2000
to find a freedom
in relating the angular (
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
) and radial component (
\mathcal{F}_{r}
) of the radiation reaction force.
However, as shown above, there is no gauge freedom at 1.5PN order in scalar-tensor theories which implies that the quasi-circular orbit relations of GR do not hold in ST theories at 1.5PN order.

GR relations were later used to compute the generic orbit radiation reaction force in Ref.
Bini and Damour 2012
(see also
Khalil et al. 2021
).
We here start with the approach of Ref.
Khalil et al. 2021
in GR to derive radiation reaction (RR) force that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}^{\rm qc}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Phi_{E}^{\rm qc}}{\Omega}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm qc}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\phi}^{\rm qc}\frac{p_{r}}{p_{\phi}}=-\frac{\Phi_{E}^{\rm qc}p_{r}}{\Omega p_{\phi}}~,

(31)

for circular orbits, where
\Omega
is the orbital frequency.
This formulation then allows the generic orbit force to be factorised as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}=\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm qc}\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm ecc}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}=\mathcal{F}_{\phi}^{\rm qc}\mathcal{F}_{\phi}^{\rm ecc}~,

(32)

where
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\rm ecc}
now denote the eccentric fluxes.

To derive the radiation reaction force in the EOB coordinates using the above balance equations, we use the energy flux and angular momentum flux in the EOB coordinates and write a generic ansatz with the unknown coefficients for the Schott terms. Then, we calculate the unknown coefficients in the Schott terms by requiring that the force be reduced to the radiation reaction force in the limit of quasi-circular orbits,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{J}+\mathcal{O}\left(p_{r}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dot{p}_{r}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}_{r}p_{\phi}}{\mathcal{F}_{\phi}p_{r}}

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{O}\left(p_{r}^{2}\right),

(33)

where the radial momenta
p_{r}
and its first derivative
\dot{p}_{r}
are zero for circular orbits.
As discussed previously, we will show that Eqs. (
V
), which are valid in General Relativity (GR), cannot be upheld in scalar-tensor (ST) theories and therefore must be relaxed.

V.1
Ansatz for Schott terms

The general ansatz for the Schott terms is dictated by the fact that these terms enter due to the interaction with the radiation field, i.e., these are radiation effects. Therefore, we follow the postulates of constructing the radiation reaction acceleration given in Eq. (
19
) and additionally requiring that the angular momentum remains a pseudo-vector to construct the general ansatz.
This implies that the Schott energy and angular momentum must be proportional to the odd powers in
p_{r}
,
m/r
for the non-linear effects and
\nu^{2}
for vanishing radiation effects in the test mass limit.

However, before expressing the general form of the ansatz for Schott terms, we recall that due to the presence of the scalar field (and hence, the dipolar radiation due to the scalar field) in scalar-tensor theories, the contribution to flux starts one PN order before their GR equivalent, i.e., at -1PN order.
In order to account of this information for the construction of general ansatz, we first explicitly compute both the energy and the angular momentum Schott terms at -1PN order.
To do this, we consider an analogy with the dipolar radiation due to an electric charge in electrodynamics.
The radiation-reaction force (Abraham-Lorentz Force) for a slowly moving point particle with charge
e
is
Landau and Lifshitz 1975
,

F_{\rm rad}^{\rm ED}=\frac{2e^{2}}{3c^{3}}\dot{a}~,

(34)

where
a
is the acceleration and the superscript ‘ED’ is to specify electrodynamics. In terms of the multipole moments the above equation can then be expressed as

F_{i}^{\rm rad,ED}=\frac{2e}{3c^{3}}I^{(3)}_{i}~,

(35)

where
I^{(3)}_{i}
is the electric dipole moment. This implies that the radiation reaction force for a point particle moving in a scalar field must also be proportional to the third-time derivative of the scalar dipole moment,

F_{i}^{\rm rad}=C_{s}I^{s^{(3)}}_{i}~,

(36)

where
I^{s}_{i}=\frac{4\alpha_{\rm AB}(\alpha_{B}^{0}-\alpha_{A}^{0})\zeta M\nu}{(1-\zeta)}r_{i}=S_{\rm AB}r_{i}
is the scalar dipole moment and
C_{s}
is some constant of proportionality. Therefore, the energy and angular momentum radiated from the system due to the scalar dipolar radiation are,

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=F_{i}^{\rm rad}v^{i}~,

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ijk}r_{i}F_{j}^{\rm rad}~,

(37)

where
r_{i}
and
v_{i}
are the position and the velocity of the particle, respectively. Using Eq. (
36
) in the above equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=C_{s}I^{s^{(3)}}_{i}v^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}I^{s^{(3)}}_{i}\dot{I}_{s}^{i}~,

(38)

and

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ijk}C_{s}I^{s^{(3)}}_{j}r_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}\epsilon^{ijk}I^{s}_{i}I^{s^{(3)}}_{j}~.

(39)

The radiated energy given in Eq. (
V.1
) can be expressed in terms of the total time derivative term as

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}\left[\frac{d}{dt}\left(\ddot{I}^{s}_{i}\dot{I}^{s}_{i}\right)-\ddot{I}^{s}_{i}\ddot{I}^{s}_{i}\right]~.

(40)

Now, using the above equation and the balance law equation given in Eq. (
III
), we find that the leading order energy Schott term,
E_{(\rm schott)}
, due to the scalar dipolar radiation is

\displaystyle E_{(\rm schott)}=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}\ddot{I}^{s}_{i}\dot{I}^{s}_{i}~.

(41)

Similarly, for the radiated angular momentum given in Eq. (
V.1
) can be expressed as,

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{\rm system}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}\epsilon^{ijk}\left[\frac{d}{dt}\left(I^{s}_{i}\ddot{I}^{s}_{j}\right)-\dot{I}^{s}_{i}\ddot{I}^{s}_{j}\right]~,

(42)

and the leading order angular momentum Schott term,
J_{(\rm schott)}
, due to the scalar dipolar radiation is,

\displaystyle J_{(\rm schott)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{s}}{S_{\rm AB}}\epsilon^{ijk}I^{s}_{i}\ddot{I}^{s}_{j}~.

(43)

Using the definition of the scalar dipole moment and the Newtonian order equations of motion in Eqs. (
41
) and (
43
), we obtain that for the dipolar radiation there is no angular momentum contribution to the Schott term, i.e.
J_{(\rm schott)}=0
, whereas the energy Schott term is non-zero.

Furthermore, we note that the energy and the angular momentum flux at 0.5PN order (the radiation-reaction square effects) is a total derivative term, implying that these can be used to define a new conserved quantity. Therefore, these terms must not contribute in the radiation-reaction force.

After considering the above two observations, the ansatz for the Schott terms we use is,

\displaystyle\text{J}_{\rm Schott}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}p_{\phi}p_{r}}{r^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\xi_{1}}{c^{2}}+\frac{1}{c^{3}}\frac{\xi_{2}}{p_{r}r}\Bigg]\,,

(44)

\displaystyle E_{\rm Schott}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}p_{r}}{r^{2}}\Bigg[\psi_{0}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left(\psi_{1}p_{r}^{2}+\psi_{2}p^{2}+\frac{\psi_{3}}{r}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c^{3}}\frac{\psi_{4}}{p_{r}r^{2}}\Bigg]\,.

(45)

V.2
Generic orbit RR force

Using the fluxes and Schott terms, the radiation reaction force is calculated using the balance equation and the radiation reaction condition in the circular-orbit limit.

V.2.1
-1PN order

As mentioned previously, scalar-tensor contributions start one PN order before their GR equivalents.
We also noted how there is no gauge freedom in the radiation reaction term at 1.5PN order between Harmonic coordinate system and ADM coordinate system.

At this order, the RR fluxes read

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,p_{\varphi}\,u^{3}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\nu^{2}u^{3}\Bigg[\frac{3}{2}\psi_{0}\,p_{r}+\left(\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\psi_{0}\right)\frac{p_{\varphi}^{2}u^{2}}{p_{r}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\psi_{0}\right)\frac{u}{p_{r}}\Bigg]+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,,

(46)

where we defined
u\equiv 1/r
and
p_{\varphi}
is such that
p^{2}=p_{r}^{2}+p_{\varphi}^{2}u^{2}
.

As mentioned previously, the unknown parameters entering the ansatz of the Schott terms are determined by satisfying the circular orbit limit, which requires that the radial flux in the above equation must not contain negative powers of the radial momentum
p_{r}
. This leads to the solution,

\psi_{0}=\frac{4}{3}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,,

(47)

so that

\mathcal{F}_{r}=\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}p_{r}u^{3}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right)\,.

(48)

At this level, it is easy to see we cannot impose
(\mathcal{F}_{r}p_{\phi})/(\mathcal{F}_{\phi}p_{r})=1
. Instead we get

\frac{\mathcal{F}_{r}p_{\phi}}{\mathcal{F}_{\phi}p_{r}}=-2\,.

(49)

From here on, we will use the conditions

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{J}+\mathcal{O}\left(p_{r}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dot{p}_{r}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}_{r}p_{\phi}}{\mathcal{F}_{\phi}p_{r}}

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{O}\left(p_{r}^{2}\right),

(50)

in the limit of quasi-circular orbits for scalar-tensor theories.

V.2.2
Newtonian Order

At Newtonian order, we follow the same steps as above, i.e. impose that the radial momentum contains only positive powers of
p_{r}
, in addition to Eq. (
V.2.1
) to determine all four free parameters, as

\displaystyle\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{15}\left(2+\gamma_{\rm AB}\right)+\frac{4}{15}\zeta^{\prime}\Bigg[14\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-\mathcal{S}_{+}(3\mathcal{S}_{-}X_{\rm AB}+80\theta_{+})

\displaystyle+10\mathcal{S}_{-}(\theta_{-}+X_{\rm AB}\theta_{+})-\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(6-5\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle+5\gamma_{\rm AB}+\nu)

\displaystyle+120\theta_{+}^{2}\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}\left(2+\gamma_{\rm AB}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\zeta^{\prime}\Bigg[6\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-32\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+8\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(3-2\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle+2\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})+40\theta_{+}^{2}\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{32}{15}\left(2+\gamma_{\rm AB}\right)+\frac{2}{15}\Bigg[32\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-160\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+40\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(2-10\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle+15\nu)+240\theta_{+}^{2}\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{15}\mathcal{S}_{-}\zeta^{\prime}\Bigg[4\mathcal{S}_{+}X_{\rm AB}+\mathcal{S}_{-}\Bigg(31+10\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle+10\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB}-7\nu\Bigg)\Bigg]\,.

(51)

At this order, the final forms for the radiation reaction forces are

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,p_{\varphi}\,u^{3}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}^{\rm N}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{3}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,p_{r}\,u^{3}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm N}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{3}}\right)\,,

(52)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}^{\rm N}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{15}\nu^{2}p_{\varphi}u^{3}\Bigg\{\Bigg[-16(2+\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})-\zeta^{\prime}\big(32\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-160\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}

\displaystyle+40\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}-\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(2-10\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle+15\nu)+240\theta_{+}^{2}\big)\Bigg]p^{2}

\displaystyle+15\Bigg[2(2+\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})+\zeta^{\prime}\big(6\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-32\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+8\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}

\displaystyle-\zeta^{\prime}\big(24\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-160\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+40X_{\rm AB}\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{-}+\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(41+30\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle-10\nu)

\displaystyle+240\theta_{+}^{2}\big)\Bigg]u\Bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm N}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{15}\nu^{2}p_{r}u^{3}\Bigg\{\Bigg[16(2+\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})+\zeta^{\prime}\big(32\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}-160\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+40\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(2-10\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle+15\nu)+240\theta_{+}^{2}\big)\Bigg]p^{2}-5\Bigg[2(2+\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})+\zeta^{\prime}\big(6\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle-32\theta_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+}+8\theta_{-}\mathcal{S}_{-}+\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}(-3+2\langle\bar{\beta}\rangle-2\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB})+48\theta_{+}^{2}\big)\Bigg]p_{r}^{2}

V.2.3
0.5PN

Again following the same steps as previously to determine the unknowns entering at 0.5PN order. However, instead of requiring that
1/p_{r}
term in
\mathcal{F}_{r}
vanishes , we now impose that the
\mathcal{O}(p_{r}^{0})
vanishes (as
\mathcal{F}_{r}
vanishes in the quasi circular orbit limit). This is because, at the 0.5PN order, the leading contribution is of order
\mathcal{O}(p_{r}^{0})
unlike -1PN and Newtonian order where such terms disappear and leading contribution is
\mathcal{O}(1/p_{r})
. This along with the condition
(\mathcal{F}_{r}p_{\phi})/(\mathcal{F}_{\phi}p_{r})=-2
, leads to the solution,

\displaystyle\psi_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{9}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\Big[4\mathcal{S}_{-}(\mathcal{S}_{-}+X_{\rm AB}\mathcal{S}_{+})\zeta^{\prime}-\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{9}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\Big[4\mathcal{S}_{-}(\mathcal{S}_{-}+X_{\rm AB}\mathcal{S}_{+})\zeta^{\prime}-\bar{\gamma}_{\rm AB}\Big]\,.

(54)

At this order, the radiation reaction force vanishes, consistent with the fact that at 0.5PN order the energy and the angular momentum flux are total derivatives, implying that these can be used to define a new conserved quantity. Hence, the radiation-reaction force up to 0.5PN reads,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,p_{\varphi}\,u^{3}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}^{\rm N}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\nu^{2}\zeta^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\,p_{r}\,u^{3}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm N}+O\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,,

(55)

with
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}^{\rm N}
and
\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\rm N}
are given in Eq. (
53
).

VI
Discussions

Recently there has been a significant effort to extend EOB waveform models to incorporate eccentricity effects in GR
Bini and Damour 2012
;
Hinderer and Babak 2017
;
Gamboa et al. 2024
;
Albanesi et al. 2025

In this paper, we extend the quasi-circular orbit approximation results of the radiation reaction force in massless scalar-tensor theories to eccentric orbits for nonspinning black hole binaries within the EOB framework.

In particular, we first derive the coordinate transformations required to map the energy and angular momentum fluxes from harmonic coordinates to EOB coordinates up to 2PN order by deriving the transformation from harmonic to ADM coordinate system and from ADM to EOB coordinate system. As in scalar-tensor theories, the radiation reaction effects start 1PN order before (1.5PN order) in contrast to GR, we also discuss the coordinate transformation from harmonic coordinates to ADM-like coordinates by investigating the gauge freedom in the radiation reaction term following Ref.
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
. Unlike in GR
Iyer and Will 1993
;
Iyer and Will 1995
, we find that there is no gauge freedom in the radiation reaction effects at 1.5PN order for scalar-tensor theories, implying that there is no coordinate transformation between the harmonic and ADM-like coordinate systems at 1.5PN order. We also verified that the coordinate transformation between harmonic, ADM and EOB coordinates systems are in agreement with existing PN-based results in the GR-limit.

We hence made use of 2PN-order coordinate transformations between harmonic, ADM and EOB frames to translate the harmonic coordinate energy and angular momentum fluxes derived in Refs.
Bernard et al. 2022
;
Jain and Rettegno 2024
into the EOB coordinate system. Next, we construct the general ansatz of the Schott terms by considering an analogy of the scalar dipolar radiation with the dipolar radiation due to an electric charge in electrodynamics and found that there is no angular momentum contribution to the Schott term, i.e.
J_{\rm(schott)}=0
at the leading order (-1PN order with respect to GR) in scalar-tensor theories.
Using these results, we derive the radiation reaction force for generic orbits up to relative 1.5PN order, i.e. -1PN, Newtonian and 0.5PN order, where we find that at the 0.5PN order the energy and the angular momentum flux are total derivatives, suggesting that the radiation reaction force vanishes at 0.5PN order.

The results presented in this paper will be useful when building accurate GW models that take into account multiple effects, such as eccentricity and ST corrections.
Such models could then be employed in tests of GR in order to avoid possible biases due to the omission of one such effect.

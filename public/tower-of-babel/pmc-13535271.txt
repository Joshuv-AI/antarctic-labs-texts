Calprotectin levels in Crohn's perianal fistula scraping are associated with fistula outcomes after surgical closure
Authors: A J M Pronk, K J Beek, M A J Becker, J Stoker, M S Vlug, W A Bemelman, M E Wildenberg, C J Buskens
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsSo far, there are no diagnostic tools to identify Crohn's disease (CD) patients at risk of failure or recurrence after perianal fistula closure surgery. This study aims to analyse if fistula scrapings calprotectin levels in CD patients undergoing surgical fistula closure can be predictive of postoperative outcomes.MethodsIn this prospective study, fistula scrapings were collected from 47 TOpCLASS 2a patients who underwent a surgical closure procedure with or without mesenchymal stem cell (MST) between November 2019 and February 2024. Scraping calprotectin was determined by ELISA. The primary outcome parameter was the correlation between scraping calprotectin levels and clinical fistula closure and radiological remission (MAGNIFI score ≤ 6).ResultsEighteen patients underwent primary surgical closure and 29 patients underwent surgical closure with MST. Scraping calprotectin levels were significantly lower in the primary closure group when compared to MST (822 μg/g (IQR 134–3285) vs. 2698 μg/g (IQR 1042–6000), p = 0.041). After a median follow‐up of 18 months (IQR 14–51), overall, 68% clinical closure with 40% radiological remission was seen. In the primary surgical closure group, scraping calprotectin levels were significantly lower in patients with clinical fistula closure (586 μg/g (IQR 84–1454) vs. 5855 μg/g (IQR 3291–6000), p = 0.003). In contrast, in the MST group no significant differences were found for clinical closure, whereas higher scraping calprotectin levels were found in patients who reached radiological remission (4827 μg/g(IQR 2698–6000) vs. 1967 μg/g (IQR 428–3937), p = 0.033).ConclusionsThese results suggest that scraping calprotectin can be used to identify patients amenable for primary surgical closure and select patients most likely to need additional treatment like MST.Keywords: calprotectine, Crohn's disease, perianal fistula

What does this paper add to the literature?

This paper highlights the potential of scraping calprotectin levels as a biomarker to predict outcomes in patients undergoing primary surgical closure versus surgical closure with MST. It suggests that calprotectin can help identify patients more likely to benefit from primary closure and those needing additional treatments, enhancing personalized care strategies.

INTRODUCTION

A common complication of Crohn's disease (CD) is a perianal fistula, which develops in up to one third of patients with CD [1]. The recently developed TOpCLASS classification system subdivides these perianal fistulas according to disease severity and outcomes [2]. Fistulas classified as 2a are suitable for repair, 2b are unsuitable for repair but the goal is symptom control and patients in class 2c are heading towards defunctioning. In class 2a patients, various surgical interventions are available aiming at fistula closure. Simple intersphincteric or low transsphincteric fistulas can be treated with fistulotomy with high success rates [3, 4]. More complex perianal fistulas are mostly treated with the endorectal advancement flap (AF) or ligation of the intersphincteric tract (LIFT). In addition, allogenic and autologous mesenchymal stem cell treatment (MST) has been developed as an adjunct to surgical closure of the internal opening [5, 6]. Although recently the European Medicines Agency has withdrawn the commercially available Darvadstrocel from the European market, in the Netherlands this product has been used up to December 2024 for therapy refractory CD fistula patients that had previously failed both anti‐TNF therapy and surgical closure. Unfortunately, so far, all surgical options for complex fistulas suffer from poor long‐term outcomes with around 70% clinical closure and 40% radiological closure [7, 8]. So far, there are no diagnostic tools to predict when to use which technique to improve outcomes. In a previous study, our group has shown that there is a strong correlation between local fistula calprotectin scraping levels and the TOpCLASS classification, suggesting a relationship with fistula prognosis [9]. The aim of this study was to analyse if fistula scraping calprotectin levels in TOpCLASS 2a CD patients undergoing surgical fistula closure are associated with postoperative outcomes.

METHODS

Study design

This study was a prospective single centre study conducted at the Amsterdam University Medical Centre. TOpCLASS 2a CD patients with perianal fistulas who underwent a surgical closure procedure aimed at fistula closure with or without MST between November 2019 and February 2024 were included. Patients without previous surgical closure were counselled for fistulotomy, AF or LIFT, depending on fistula anatomy, whereas patients with previous surgical closure were offered closure of the internal opening with allogenic MSTs according to Dutch guidelines. Patients in whom no fistula scraping was collected were excluded. The Local Institutional Review Board approved the study protocol (International Clinical Trial Registry Platform ID: NL7673). All participants provided written informed consent.

Patient demographics and outcome variables

Patient demographics, previous surgical (perianal) interventions, type of fistula closure procedure and clinical fistula outcomes were collected from medical notes. Patient demographics included sex, age, smoking history, duration of CD, location of CD according to the Montreal classification and fistula characteristics.

The primary outcome parameters were the correlation between fistula scraping calprotectin levels and clinical fistula closure and radiological remission (MAGNIFI score ≤6). Secondary outcomes were clinical closure, radiological remission and recurrence rates. Clinical closure was defined as closure of the external fistula opening(s) without discharge of faeces and/or pus upon gentle pressure. Radiological remission was determined blinded for clinical information by an abdominal radiologist [JS] with over 30 years of experience in MRI of Crohn's disease. Scoring items consisted of activity items according to the MAGNIFI‐CD index [10]. Radiological remission was defined as a hypointense, fibrotic fistula tract; absence of hyperintensity on T2‐weighted sequences regardless of fat suppression and no enhancement of the tract after contrast administration on a fat suppressed T1‐weighted sequence (MAGNIFI ≤6) [11]. Recurrence was defined as recurrent discharge of faeces and/or pus of the fistula tract after previous clinical closure.

Sample collection and processing

During surgery, fistula tract scraping was collected. With use of a curette surgical instrument, tissue/debris was scraped from within the fistula tract to create a fresh tract. Scraping calprotectin was measured by a quantitative enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, Calprotectin 2 test, Phadia AB, Freiburg, Germany), with an upper detection limit of 6000 mcg/g.

Statistical analysis

Non‐parametric data were presented as median and interquartile range [IQR] and categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages to summarize patient characteristics. Differences in patient characteristics between both study groups were analysed using chi‐squared test, Fisher's exact test and the Mann–Whitney U test. The primary outcome was analysed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Secondary outcomes were presented using frequencies and percentages. A p‐value of less than 0.05 (two‐tailed) was considered significant. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 28.0.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

In total, 47 patients were included, 18 patients in the primary surgical closure group and 29 patients in the MST group. Four patients (22.2%) in the primary surgical closure group underwent an AF, six patients (33.3%) underwent a LIFT and eight patients (44.4%) underwent a fistulotomy. Overall, median age at surgery was 34.0 years (IQR 28.0–45.0) and more than half of the patients were male (51.0%) (Table 1). Patients in the MST group had fistula complaints for a median of 6.0 years (IQR 2.0–10.5), which was significantly longer when compared to patients who underwent primary surgical closure (3.0 years (IQR 2.0–4.8) p = 0.036). In addition, the median number of perianal procedures was significantly higher in the MST group (5.0 (IQR 3.0–7.0) vs. 2.5 (IQR 1.8–5.3); p = 0.028, respectively). The number of internal‐ and external openings was comparable between groups. The median scraping calprotectin level was 822 μg/g (IQR 134–3285) in the surgical closure group, which was significantly lower than the median scraping calprotectin level in the MST group (2698 μg/g (IQR 1042–6000)), p = 0.041. A median follow‐up of 18.4 months (IQR 14.7–50.9) was available for the entire study group, which was significantly longer in the primary closure group compared to the MST group (52.1 months (IQR 39.8–57.4) vs. 15.3 months (IQR 11.8–19.5), respectively, p = <0.001).

Fistula closure and scraping calprotectin levels

A total of 32 patients (68.1%) had a clinically closed fistula after a median of 2.0 months (IQR 1.0–4.5); 13/18 (72.2%) in the primary surgical closure group and 19/29 (65.5%) in the group treated with MST. In total, 19 patients (40.4%) reached MRI remission with MAGNIFI ≤ 6 after a median of 5.0 months (IQR 4.0–9.5); 8/18 (44.4%) in the primary surgical closure group and 11/29 (37.9%) in the MST group.

The median scraping calprotectin level of patients with clinical closure in the primary surgical closure group was 586 μg/g (IQR 84–1454), which was significantly lower compared to patients without clinical closure (5855 μg/g (IQR 3291–6000); p = 0.003) (Table 2, Figure 1). Among patients with radiological remission in this group, the median scraping calprotectin levels were numerically lower compared to those without remission (375 μg/g (IQR 41–2164) vs. 2161 μg/g (IQR 476–5891), respectively, p = 0.091). In contrast, in the MST group, no difference in scraping calprotectin levels was seen between patients with and without clinical closure (3213 μg/g (IQR 1670–6000) vs. 2097 μg/g (378–3937), respectively, p = 0.211). Interestingly, in this group significantly higher scraping calprotectin levels were found in patients who reached radiological remission (4827 μg/g (IQR 2698–6000) vs. 1970 μg/g (IQR 428–3937) respectively, p = 0.033).

Recurrences

During follow‐up, 2/32 patients (6.3%) with clinical closure had a recurrent fistula after 8 and 24 months, respectively. Neither of these patients had a fistula tract that had reached radiological remission. Both patients were part of the primary surgical closure group and underwent multiple reinterventions (3 and 2, respectively) without achieving clinical closure of the fistula tract. One patient required a defunctioning ostomy due to ongoing fistula complaints.

DISCUSSION

In this prospective study, fistula scraping calprotectin levels were associated with postoperative outcomes and may help guide treatment selection. Surgical closure is more effective in patients with low calprotectin levels, while high levels may identify those who require and respond to MST. Patients who achieved clinical fistula closure after primary surgical closure had significantly lower scraping calprotectin levels compared to patients without clinical closure, suggesting that low local inflammation is associated with a higher chance of successful closure without adjunctive therapy. In addition, patients who achieved radiological remission, scraping calprotectin levels were numerically lower compared to patients without radiological remission. In contrast, patients treated with MST who achieved radiological remission had significant higher scraping calprotectin levels, with the median scraping calprotectin level (4827 μg/g) comparable to patients who did not achieve clinical closure in the primary surgical closure group (5855 μg/g). This finding suggests that using additional MST is predominantly beneficial in the patient group with more inflammatory fistula characteristics that are less likely to close after a primary surgical procedure. Based on this, it could be hypothesized that patients may be stratified based on scraping calprotectin levels for a surgical intervention with or without additional MST. Patients with low scraping calprotectin levels had a higher a priori chance of a successful closure procedure, while patients with high scraping calprotectin levels in their fistula tract were less likely to reach closure by surgical intervention alone. Especially interesting in this context is that the current MST group was characterized by longer disease duration and more previous perianal interventions, emphasizing the more refractory nature of these fistulas, while the overall clinical closure and radiological remission rates were comparable to the primary surgical closure group. These findings suggest that using MST in selected patients with therapy refractory perianal fistulas, could lead to improved outcomes and a better quality of life. During follow‐up, only patient in the primary closure group had a recurrent fistula. This could be explained by the significantly longer median follow‐up in this patient group.

The impact of these findings gains further importance in light of the recent withdrawal of Darvadstrocel from the European market following the ADMIRE‐2 trial. This randomized controlled trial showed no significant difference in combined remission between MST and placebo in CD fistulas [12]. In the ADMIRE‐II trial, 283 patients received Darvadstrocel and 285 received placebo. Despite these substantial numbers, the trial found no statistically significant difference in the primary endpoint of combined remission at week 24 (48.8% vs. 46.3%; p = 0.571). The most striking finding of the ADMIRE‐II trial was the unexpectedly high remission rate in the placebo group, rather than the lack of efficacy of Darvadstrocel. The inclusion criteria for patients in ADMIRE‐II were limited to an inadequate response to immunosuppressive agents or biologics. This may have resulted in the inclusion of many patients with less refractory fistulas, who were probably more likely to respond well to surgery and medical therapy. This could have diluted a potential benefit of Darvastrocel in patients with more complex, therapy refractory fistulas, including those in our study.

In our MST group, consisting solely of patients who failed both anti‐TNF and prior surgical closure, a 66% clinical closure rate was achieved, a surprisingly high outcome in this difficult‐to‐treat population. So, based on this, we believe that MST actually is a promising treatment option for patients with therapy‐refractory Crohn's perianal fistulas and persistent inflammatory characteristics as demonstrated by scraping calprotectin levels. Moreover, our data suggest that patients with a high scraping calprotectin level were more likely to reach closure after MST than those with a low scraping calprotectin level. While this seems counterintuitive, one hypothesis is related to the mechanism of action of MST. It has been shown that activation of MST by a variety of inflammatory mediators increases their function [13] Therefore, administration of MST in a highly inflammatory milieu may result in better activation of the MST, with subsequently improved efficacy and thus increased closure.

With the commercially allogenic mesenchymal stem cells no longer available, it would obviously be interesting to analyse if autologous MST with or without platelet rich plasma (PRS) could induce a comparable effect. The study by Bak et al. [6] investigated the efficacy of PRS in CD patients with therapy refractory perianal fistulas and found a clinical closure rate of 88%, a radiological healing rate of 75% and a recurrence rate of 8% (none in patients with radiological healing) after a mean follow‐up of 3.7 years. Several studies investigated treatment with autologous‐derived stem cells (including adipose‐ and bone marrow‐derived) and reported clinical closure rates of approximately 65% and radiological healing rates around 40% [14, 15, 16, 17] However, radiological outcomes after autologous stem cell treatments are scarce and the previously reported healing rates are based on small clinical trials involving up to 25 patients. Further research is required to better understand the therapeutic potential and radiological outcomes of autologous stem cell treatments.

Previous research showed a strong correlation between local fistula scraping levels and the TOpCLASS classification system [9]. The median scraping calprotectin level of class 2a in this study was 140 μg/g. Although the absolute scraping calprotectin level was slightly higher in the current primary surgical closure group with clinical closure (586 μg/g), the interquartile range (84–1453) indicated that patients with successful closure generally had lower scraping calprotectin levels than those with unsuccessful procedures (3291–6000). This suggests scraping calprotectin has the potential to stratify patients suitable for repair by identifying less inflammatory and more epithelialized tracts.

A few limitations need to be considered. First, the small number of included patients with 47 patients included in 5 years, of which only 29 patients were treated with additional MST. However, this reflects clinical reality in the Netherlands, where MST is only available for patients with prior failure of anti‐TNF and surgical therapy. Secondly, this prospective study was conducted in a tertiary referral centre, which could lead to a more therapy‐refractory/severe patient group. Thirdly, the quantitative enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay used has an upper detection limit of 6000 mcg/g. This could have influenced the scraping calprotectin levels and thereby the strength of correlations or significance found in this study negatively. To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the scraping calprotectin level in fistula scrapings and its association with clinical fistula outcomes, which is the strength of this study.

In conclusion, fistula scraping calprotectin levels are associated with outcomes following surgical closure in perianal CD. Low levels may be associated with more successful closure after primary closure surgery, while higher levels may be used to identify a patient group more likely to benefit from MST. These results suggest that scraping calprotectin can be used to identify patients amenable to primary surgical closure and select patients most likely to need additional treatment like MST. Further research is needed to validate these results in a large prospective study.

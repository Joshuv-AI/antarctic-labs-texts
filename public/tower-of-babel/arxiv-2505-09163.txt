Introduction

Let
\mathbb{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}~|~\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\}
be the complex upper half-plane.
A congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
acting on
\mathbb{H}
via fractional linear transformations,
defines the modular curve
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}=\{[\tau]:=\Gamma\tau~|~\tau\in\mathbb{H}\}
.
In particular, for each positive integer
N
, we consider the modular curves

Y(N)=\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}\quad\textrm{and}\quad Y_{1}(N)=\Gamma_{1}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}

which parametrize isomorphism classes of complex elliptic curves equipped,
respectively, with
full level
N
structure and with a point of order
N

(see
[
2
, Theorem 1.5.1]
). Here,

\Gamma(N)
denotes the principal congruence subgroup of level
N
, and

\Gamma_{1}(N)=\left\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})~\bigg|~\gamma\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&\mathrm{*}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\ (\textup{mod}\ N\cdot M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\right\}.

There exists a natural forgetful map

Y(N)\twoheadrightarrow Y_{1}(N)
.
These modular curves can be viewed as Riemann surfaces,
which compactify into smooth projective algebraic curves
X(N)
and

X_{1}(N)
, respectively.
It is well known that
X(N)
and
X_{1}(N)

are defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{N})
and
\mathbb{Q}
, respectively,
where
\mu_{N}
denotes the group of
N
th roots of unity (see
[
2
, Chapters 2 and 7]
).

Let
(\mathbb{N},\,\preccurlyeq)
be the directed partially ordered set of
positive integers ordered by divisibility : for
n,\,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
we write
n\preccurlyeq m
if and only if
n\,|\,m
.
In their seminal work
[
1
]
, Daw and Zilber used the theory of canonical models of Shimura varieties to
describe the projective limit
\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}Y(N)
and its automorphism group.
A key result of their work is the realization of the Galois group

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{CM})/\mathbb{Q})

as an open subgroup of the automorphism group of a certain adelic structure,
where
\mathrm{CM}
denotes the union of all CM points on the modular curves
Y(N)
.

In contrast, our approach is more explicit.
Let
\mathbb{H}^{-}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}~|~\mathrm{Im}(\tau)<0\}
be the lower half-plane.
Rather than considering the full inverse system
of modular curves
Y(N)
, we restrict our attention to the
inverse systems consisting of certain CM points on Shimura varieties

Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}:=Y_{1}(N)\sqcup Y_{1}(N)^{-}\quad\textrm{and}\quad Y(N)^{\pm}:=Y(N)\sqcup Y(N)^{-},

where
Y_{1}(N)^{-}:=\Gamma_{1}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}^{-}
and

Y(N)^{-}:=\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}^{-}
.
Define

\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm}:=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm}:=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}Y(N)^{\pm}.

)

Let

or

and let

Definite form class groups

We shall present an explicit approach to class field theory
via definite form class groups, as recently developed by
Jung et al. in
[
6
]
.

Throughout this paper, let
N
be a positive integer and

D
be a negative integer such that
D\equiv 0
or
1\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)
.
Define the set
\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)
of primitive positive definite binary quadratic forms
of discriminant
D
, subject to an additional condition, as follows :

\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N):=\left\{Q\left(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\right)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,\,y]~\Bigg|~\begin{array}[]{l}\gcd(a,\,b,\,c)=1,\\
b^{2}-4ac=D,~a>0,\\
\gcd(a,\,N)=1\end{array}\right\}.

For each
Q(x,\,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)
, let
\omega_{Q}\in\mathbb{H}
denote the root of the
quadratic polynomial
Q(x,\,1)
, that is,

\omega_{Q}=\frac{-b+\sqrt{D}}{2a}.

We extend this set by defining

\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}:=\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)\sqcup\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-},~~\textrm{where}~\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}:=\{-Q~|~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)\}.

Let
\Gamma
be either
\Gamma_{1}(N)
or
\Gamma(N)
.
Then
\Gamma
acts on
\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}
from the right by

Q\left(\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\right)^{\gamma}=Q\left(\gamma\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\right)\quad(Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm},~\gamma\in\Gamma),

which induces an equivalence relation
\sim_{\Gamma}
defined by :
for
Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}
,

Q\sim_{\Gamma}Q^{\prime}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q^{\prime}=Q^{\gamma}~\textrm{for some}~\gamma\in\Gamma.

Note that the sets
\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)
and

\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}

are invariant under this action.

Unless stated otherwise, let
K
be the imaginary quadratic field given by
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})

and
\mathcal{O}
be the order in
K
of discriminant
D
.
Let
K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}
denote the ray class field of
\mathcal{O}

N\mathcal{O}

[

[

More precisely, define the subgroups of proper fractional
\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{O},\,N)

\displaystyle=\langle\textrm{nonzero proper $\mathcal{O}$-ideals prime to $N$}\rangle,

\displaystyle P_{1}(\mathcal{O},\,N)

\displaystyle=\langle\nu\mathcal{O}~|~\textrm{$\nu$ is a nonzero element of $\mathcal{O}$
such that}~\nu\equiv 1\ (\textup{mod}\ N\mathcal{O})\rangle.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the maximal order of
K
, and let
\ell=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}]
denote the conductor of
\mathcal{O}
.
Then, by the existence theorem of class field theory,
there exists a unique abelian extension

(i)

Every nonzero prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
that ramifies in

(ii)

The Artin reciprocity map for the modulus

3
Families of CM points on certain Shimura varieties

Note that the disjoint union
Y(N)^{\pm}=Y(N)\sqcup Y(N)^{-}
is isomorphic to the Shimura variety

\mathrm{Sh}(G,\,X)_{\mathcal{K}(N)}

associated with the Shimura datum
(G,\,X)
, where

G=\mathrm{SL}_{2},\quad X=\mathbb{H}^{\pm}:=\mathbb{H}\sqcup\mathbb{H}^{-},\quad\mathcal{K}(N)=\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}))

(cf.
[
1
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2]
or
[
7
, Definition 5.5]
). More precisely,
if
\mathbb{A}_{f}
denotes the ring
of finite adeles of
\mathbb{Q}
with restricted topology, then
we have the isomorphism of complex manifolds

\mathrm{Sh}(G,\,X)_{\mathcal{K}(N)}=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/\mathcal{K}(N)\simeq Y(N)^{\pm}.

Similarly, the space
Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}=Y_{1}(N)\sqcup Y_{1}(N)^{-}
can also be regarded as a Shimura variety
associated with the same Shimura datum
(G,\,X)
.
In this section, we shall construct two inverse systems

\{\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\{\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y(N)^{\pm}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}},

where each object consists of certain CM points on
the Shimura varieties
Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}
and
Y(N)^{\pm}
, respectively.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}
be a CM point (i.e.,
\tau
is imaginary quadratic), and let

ax^{2}+bx+c\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a primitive quadratic polynomial (namely,
\gcd(a,\,b,\,c)=1
)
having
\tau
as a root.

(i)

The
discriminant

D_{\tau}
of
\tau
is defined to be the discriminant of
the polynomial
ax^{2}+bx+c
, namely,
D_{\tau}=b^{2}-4ac
.

(ii)

We say that
\tau
is
primitive modulo
N
if
\gcd(a,\,N)=1
.

(iii)

Let
\mathbb{H}(D,\,N)^{\pm}
denote the set of all CM points
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}

such that
D_{\tau}=D
and
\tau
is primitive modulo
N
.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle:=\{[\tau]\in Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}~|~\tau\in\mathbb{H}(D,\,N)^{\pm}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle:=\{[\tau]\in Y(N)^{\pm}~|~\tau\in\mathbb{H}(D,\,N)^{\pm}\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be either
\Gamma_{1}(N)
or
\Gamma(N)
. Then the map

\rho:\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}/\sim_{\Gamma}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{\pm}),\quad[Q]\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\omega_{Q}]&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
\mathrm{[}\overline{\omega}_{-Q}]&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}\end{array}\right.

(3)

is a well-defined bijection.

Proof..

Let
Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}
such that
[Q]=[Q^{\prime}]

in
\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}/\sim_{\Gamma}
, and so

Q^{\prime}=Q^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then we see that
-Q^{\prime}=-(Q^{\gamma})=(-Q)^{\gamma}
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega_{Q^{\prime}}=\omega_{Q^{\gamma}}=\gamma^{-1}(\omega_{Q})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
\overline{\omega}_{-Q^{\prime}}=\overline{\omega}_{(-Q)^{\gamma}}=\overline{\gamma^{-1}(\omega_{-Q})}=\gamma^{-1}(\overline{\omega}_{-Q})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}.\end{array}\right.

This observation implies that
\rho
is well defined.

We deduce that for
Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rho([Q])=\rho([Q^{\prime}])

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\omega_{Q}]=[\omega_{Q^{\prime}}]&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
\mathrm{[}\overline{\omega}_{-Q}]=[\overline{\omega}_{-Q^{\prime}}]&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega_{Q}=\gamma(\omega_{Q^{\prime}})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
\overline{\omega}_{-Q}=\gamma(\overline{\omega}_{-Q^{\prime}})=\overline{\gamma(\omega_{-Q^{\prime}})}&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}\end{array}\right.\quad\textrm{for some}~\gamma\in\Gamma

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega_{Q}=\gamma(\omega_{Q^{\prime}})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
\omega_{-Q}=\gamma(\omega_{-Q^{\prime}})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}\end{array}\right.\quad\textrm{for some}~\gamma\in\Gamma

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Q^{\prime}=Q^{\gamma}&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N),\\
-Q^{\prime}=(-Q)^{\gamma}=-(Q^{\gamma})&\textrm{if}~Q\in\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{-}\end{array}\right.\quad\textrm{for some}~\gamma\in\Gamma

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow Q^{\prime}=Q^{\gamma}~\textrm{for some}~\gamma\in\Gamma

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow[Q]=[Q^{\prime}].

This shows that
\rho
is injective.

For surjectivity of
\rho
, let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}(D,\,N)^{\pm}
.
Let
Q
be the quadratic form in
\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)
having
\tau
as a zero, and set

Q_{\tau}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Q&\textrm{if}~\tau\in\mathbb{H},\\
-Q&\textrm{if}~\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{-}.\end{array}\right.

We then find by the definition (
3
) that

\rho([Q_{\tau}])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho([Q])=[\omega_{Q}]=[\tau]&\textrm{if}~\tau\in\mathbb{H},\\
\rho([-Q])=[\overline{\omega}_{Q}]=[\tau]&\textrm{if}~\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,
\rho
is surjective and hence bijective.
∎

(ii)

Inverse limits of CM points as Galois groups

We shall prove our main theorem by interpreting the inverse limits

\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm})

(iii)

\lx@sectionsign

(ii)

See

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
D\equiv 0
or
1\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)
be a negative integer,
and let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be the
corresponding imaginary quadratic field.
Let
t
be a transcendental number.

(i)

The subset
\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm})

of
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm}
admits a group structure isomorphic to

\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})
.

(ii)

Define

K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/\infty}):=\bigcup_{N\geq 1}K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/N}).

The subset

\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm})
of

\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm}
can be regarded as a group isomorphic to

\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/\infty})/\mathbb{Q}(t))
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm})

can be identified with a quotient group
of
\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm})

via the natural forgetful maps.

Proof..

(i)

As sets, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}/\sim_{\Gamma_{1}(N)}\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{formpoint}}

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{by Corollary \ref{GalFormCor} (i)}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(\bigcup_{N\geq 1}K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{KONinclusion} (i)
(cf. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{I-M}{}{}, Theorem 164]} and \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Neukirch}{}{}, $\lx@sectionsign$I.2]})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{KONinclusion} (iii)}.

Thus,
\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm})
inherits a natural group
structure isomorphic to
\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})
.

(ii)

Similarly, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}(D,\,\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathcal{Q}(D,\,N)^{\pm}/\sim_{\Gamma(N)}\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{formpoint}}

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}(t^{1/N})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{by Corollary \ref{GalFormCor} (ii)}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(\bigcup_{N\geq 1}K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}(t^{1/N})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{KONinclusion} (i)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/\infty})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{KONinclusion} (iii)}.

The second assertion follows from (i) together with the natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t)/\mathbb{Q}(t))\\
\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/\infty})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\,/\,\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t^{1/\infty})/K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t))

and the commutative diagram in Figure
1
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\{[\tau]\in Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}~|~\textrm{$\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}$ is a CM point primitive modulo $N$}\}~(\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\pm}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\{[\tau]\in Y(N)^{\pm}~|~\textrm{$\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}$ is a CM point primitive modulo $N$}\}~(\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\pm}).

Then, as sets, we have the decompositions

\mathcal{CM}_{1}^{\pm}\simeq\bigsqcup_{K}\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{CM}^{\pm}\simeq\bigsqcup_{K}\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(t_{K,\,\ell}^{1/\infty})/\mathbb{Q}(t_{K,\,\ell})),

where
K
ranges over all imaginary quadratic fields,
\ell
indicates
the conductor of an arbitrary order in
K
, and
\{t_{K,\,\ell}\}_{K,\,\ell}
is a collection of algebraically independent
transcendental numbers.

Proof..

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a partition

\{[\tau]\in Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}~|~\textrm{$\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}$ is a CM point primitive modulo $N$}\}=\bigsqcup_{D}\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm}),

where
D
runs over all negative integers such that
D\equiv 0
or
1\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)
.
Taking the inverse limit yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{CM}_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigsqcup_{D}\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle\simeq\bigsqcup_{D}\varprojlim_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y_{1}(N)^{\pm})

\displaystyle\simeq\bigsqcup_{D}\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{by Theorem \ref{main} (i)}

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{K}\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell^{2}d_{K}})^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textrm{with}~D=\ell^{2}d_{K}

where
K
runs over all imaginary quadratic fields of discriminant
d_{K}

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{K}\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q}).

The second decomposition follows analogously from Theorem
4.2
(ii).
∎

5
An explicit description of some Galois groups

Let
p
be an odd prime. In this final section, we shall provide
an explicit realization of the Galois group

\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{\infty}}(t^{1/p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}(t))

under the assumption
D\neq-3,\,-4
, where
t
is a transcendental number and

K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{\infty}}(t^{1/p^{\infty}}):=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{n}}(t^{1/p^{n}}).

(4)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 1}
be sequences in

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
satisfying
:

(i)

\gamma_{n+1}\equiv\gamma_{n}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
and

\gamma_{n+1}^{\prime}\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

for all
n\geq 1

;

(ii)

\gamma_{1}\equiv\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

;

(iii)

For each
n\geq 1
,
\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
or

\gamma_{n}\equiv-\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
.

Then their
p
-adic limits

\gamma:=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{n}
and

\gamma^{\prime}:=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
coincide, that is,

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.

Proof..

We see from (i) and (ii) that

\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\quad\textrm{for all}~n\geq 1.

(5)

Let
S=\{n\geq 1~|~\gamma_{n}\not\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\}
, and then
1\not\in S
.
If
S\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
m\geq 2
such that

\gamma_{m}\equiv-\gamma_{m}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{m}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

by (iii). This contradicts (
5
), since
p\geq 3
.

Thus we conclude that
\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
for all
n\geq 1
, and hence

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}
be a CM point. If
D_{\tau}\neq-3,\,-4
,
then the isotropy group of
\tau
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is

\{I_{2},\,-I_{2}\}
, namely,

\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})~|~\gamma(\tau)=\gamma\}=\{I_{2},\,-I_{2}\}
.

Proof..

See
[
9
, Proposition 1.5 (c) in Chapter I]
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

With the same notations as in
Theorem
4.2
, let
p
be an odd prime, and
let
R\subset\mathbb{H}(D,\,p)^{\pm}

be a set of representatives for the elements of
\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y(p)^{\pm})
.
If
D\neq-3,\,-4
, then there is a one-to-one correspondence between

\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{\infty}}(t^{1/p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{and}\quad R\times\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~|~\gamma\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\}.

Proof..

For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{\pm}
, let
[\tau]_{n}
denote its image in
Y(p^{n})^{\pm}
. We derive that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{\infty}}(t^{1/p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gal}(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{n}}(t^{1/p^{n}})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{by the definition (\ref{defKp}) }

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\,\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,p^{n}}(t^{1/p^{n}})/\mathbb{Q}(t))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{KONinclusion} (i)}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\,\mathcal{CM}(D,\,Y(p^{n})^{\pm})\quad\textrm{by Corollary \ref{GalFormCor} (ii)}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\left\{([\tau]_{1},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}}]_{2},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}]_{3},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}]_{4},\,\ldots)~|~\tau\in\mathbb{H}(D,\,p)^{\pm}~\textrm{and}~\alpha_{n}\in\Gamma(p^{n})~(n\geq 1)\right\}.

Define a map

\displaystyle\phi:R\times\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~|~\gamma\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\rightarrow\left\{([\tau]_{1},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}}]_{2},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}]_{3},\,[\tau^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}]_{4},\,\ldots)~|~\tau\in\mathbb{H}(D,\,p)^{\pm}~\textrm{and}~\alpha_{n}\in\Gamma(p^{n})~(n\geq 1)\right\}

by

\phi(\tau,\,\gamma)=([\tau^{\gamma_{1}}]_{1},\,[\tau^{\gamma_{2}}]_{2},\,[\tau^{\gamma_{3}}]_{3},\,\ldots)\quad(\tau\in R,~\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~\textrm{satisfying}~\gamma\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))),

where
\gamma_{n}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is any lift such that

\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\quad(n\geq 1).

It is straightforward to verify that this map
is a well-defined surjection, independent of the choice of lifts

\gamma_{n}
.
To prove injectivity, suppose that

\phi(\tau,\,\gamma)=\phi(\tau^{\prime},\,\gamma^{\prime})\quad\textrm{for some}~(\tau,\,\gamma),\,(\tau^{\prime},\,\gamma^{\prime})\in R\times\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~|~\gamma\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\}.

Then we have

([\tau^{\gamma_{1}}]_{1},\,[\tau^{\gamma_{2}}]_{2},\,[\tau^{\gamma_{3}}]_{3},\,\ldots)=([\tau^{\prime\,\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}]_{1},\,[\tau^{\prime\,\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}]_{2},\,[\tau^{\prime\,\gamma_{3}^{\prime}}]_{3},\,\ldots)

(6)

where
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{n}
are elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma^{\prime}_{n}\equiv\gamma^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\quad(n\geq 1),

respectively.
Since
\gamma_{1}\equiv\gamma\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))

and
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\equiv\gamma^{\prime}\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ pM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
,
we attain
\gamma_{1},\,\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(p)
, and so

[\tau]_{1}=[\tau^{\gamma_{1}}]_{1}=[\tau^{\prime\,\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}]_{1}=[\tau^{\prime}]_{1}.

It follows by the definition of
R
that
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
, and we obtain by (
6
) that

\tau^{\gamma_{n}}=\tau^{\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\beta_{n}}\quad\textrm{for some}~\beta_{n}\in\Gamma(p^{n})\quad(n\geq 1).

(7)

Since
D_{\tau}=D\neq-3,\,-4
, the isotropy group of
\tau

in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is
\{I_{2},\,-I_{2}\}
by Lemma
5.2
. Thus we deduce by (
7
) that
for each
n\geq 1

\gamma_{n}\equiv\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\quad\textrm{or}\quad\gamma_{n}\equiv-\gamma_{n}^{\prime}\ (\textup{mod}\ p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

Therefore we achieve by Lemma
5.1
that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
, which proves that
\phi
is injective and hence bijective.
∎

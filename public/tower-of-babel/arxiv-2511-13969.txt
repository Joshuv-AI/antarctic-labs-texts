Proportion of Atkin-Lehner sign patterns and Hecke Eigenvalue Equidistribution
Erick Ross, Alexandre van Lidth, Martha Rose Wolf, Hui Xue

Abstract
. Let N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 , k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 even, and σ \sigma denote a sign pattern for N N . In this paper, we first determine the exact proportion of forms in S k ​ ( N ) S_{k}(N) and S k new ​ ( N ) S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N) with a given Atkin-Lehner sign pattern σ \sigma . Then we study the asymptotic behavior of the Hecke operators T p T_{p} over the subspaces of S k ​ ( N ) S_{k}(N) and S k new ​ ( N ) S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N) with Atkin-Lehner sign pattern σ \sigma . In particular, for the p p -adic Plancherel measure μ p \mu_{p} , we show that the Hecke eigenvalues for T p T_{p} over these subspaces are μ p \mu_{p} -equidistributed as N + k → ∞ N+k\to\infty .

1.
Introduction

For
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
even, let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cusp forms of weight
k
and modular group
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and let
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\subseteq S_{k}(N)
denote its new subspace.
For
p^{r}\|N
, let
W_{p^{r}}
denote the Atkin-Lehner involutions on these spaces, and recall that these
W_{p^{r}}
can be extended multiplicatively to
W_{Q}
for all
Q\|N
(i.e.
Q|N
where
(Q,N/Q)=1
).

Now, recall that the
W_{Q}
involutions diagonalize
S_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
with eigenvalues
\pm 1
.
So, we define the Atkin-Lehner sign pattern spaces by specifying these signs for all
p^{r}\|N
. To be precise, define a sign pattern
\sigma
for
N
to be a multiplicative function on the exact divisors of
N
such that
\sigma(p^{r})=\pm 1
for all
p^{r}\|N
. Then we have the decomposition of
S_{k}(N)

\displaystyle S_{k}(N)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma}S_{k}^{\sigma}(N),\quad S_{k}^{\sigma}(N):=\{f\in S_{k}(N):W_{p^{r}}f=\sigma(p^{r})f\ \text{ for each }p^{r}\|N\}

(1.1)

(and similarly for
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
).
Note that these sign pattern spaces
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
are a finer decomposition of the more well-known Fricke sign spaces
S_{k}^{\pm}(N)
, which only specify the “global” sign (i.e. the sign of the Fricke involution
W_{N}
).

Now, a natural question one might ask is how all the possible sign patterns are distributed over the spaces
S_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. For both global signs and local sign patterns, we determine the exact proportion of signs / sign patterns appearing.

In the following theorem,
\mathbbm{1}_{\textit{condition}}
denotes the indicator function for
condition
, and the notation “
A(N,k)\sim B(N)
as
N+k\to\infty
” means that
A(N,k)/B(N)\to 1
as
N+k\to\infty
. We also take the convention that
0\sim 0
, because as discussed shortly, both sides of (
1.5
) are identically zero in one particular case (see Theorem
1.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
even. Then as
N+k\to\infty
, the proportion of cusp forms with global sign
+1
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\dim S_{k}^{+}(N)}{\dim S_{k}(N)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{N=1}}{2},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},+}(N)}{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{N=\textnormal{cubefree square}}}{2}\prod_{p|N}\frac{-1}{p^{2}-p-1}.

(1.3)

Also, consider sign patterns
\sigma
for
N
. Then as
N+k\to\infty
, the proportion of cusp forms with sign pattern
\sigma
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\dim S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}{\dim S_{k}(N)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\prod_{p^{r}||N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right).

(1.5)

We note that this theorem predicts an equal proportion of both signs (and more generally, all sign patterns) in many cases. Over the full space, both signs (respectively, all sign patterns) will have equal proportion in
S_{k}(N)
for all
N\geq 2
, by (
1.2
) and (
1.4
). Over the newspace, both global signs (respectively, all sign patterns) will have equal proportion in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
for squarefree
N\geq 2
, by (
1.3
) and (
1.5
). This equiproportion property for the case of newspaces of squarefree level was also shown by Iwaniec-Luo-Sarnak
[
9
, Equation (2.73)]
(respectively, Martin
[
11
, Corollary 3.4]
).

The paper
[
13
]
recently investigated small biases in global sign, and claimed that each global sign would appear for
50\%
of newforms as
N+k\to\infty

[
13
, Section 1.3]
. However, as one can see from (
1.3
), this is not quite accurate; the global sign in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
will actually be asymptotically biased towards
(-1)^{\omega(N)}
whenever
N
is a cubefree square. One can see this bias numerically from the data calculated in
[
18
]
.

We would also like to point out that Martin
[
14
]
recently investigated small biases in the sign of
W_{p^{r}}
for a single fixed prime power
p^{r}
, and observed a stronger bias at
r=2
. Here, Theorem
1.1
quantifies this observation by establishing the exact proportion of each sign pattern.

Now, observe that the proportions (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are always nonzero, except when
4\|N
with
\sigma(4)=+1
over the newspace
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. For this reason, we define the notion of “admissible” sign patterns, where all sign patterns are admissible except for the one case of
4\|N
with
\sigma(4)=+1
over the newspace.
It is worth noting here that the admissible sign patterns
\sigma
all have proportion within a factor of
3
of
1/2^{\omega(N)}
. In other words, the admissible sign patterns are not too far off from being equally proportioned. This “factor of
3
” comes from the lower and upper bounds on the
\prod_{p^{r}||N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)
factor from (
1.5
) for admissible
\sigma
:

\displaystyle 0.71546\leq\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)

(1.6)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)\leq 2.67412.

(1.7)

It turns out that for inadmissible sign patterns
\sigma
, there are no newforms at all with sign pattern
\sigma
.
This is because
W_{4}
always has Atkin-Lehner sign
-1
over
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. This result was originally shown by Atkin and Lehner in
[
2
, Theorem 7]
. We give an alternative proof here based on the trace formula.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
with
4\|N
. Then
W_{4}f=-f
for every
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
.

Now, recall that the Hecke operators
T_{m}
for
(m,N)=1
commute with the Atkin-Lehner involutions
W_{Q}

[
6
, Proposition 13.2.6]
(see
[
6
]
for the precise definition of these Hecke operators
T_{m}
). This means that the Hecke operators preserve the sign pattern spaces
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
. So for the admissible sign patterns
\sigma
, we can then investigate how the Hecke eigenvalues over
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
are distributed. For
m\geq 1
, let
T_{m}^{\prime}:=m^{-(k-1)/2}T_{m}
denote the normalized Hecke operators (over both
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
). Then for a fixed prime
p
, let
\displaystyle\mu_{p}(x):=\frac{p+1}{\pi}\frac{(1-x^{2}/4)^{1/2}}{(p^{1/2}+p^{-1/2})^{2}-x^{2}}dx
denote the
p
-adic Plancherel measure. We will show that the eigenvalues of
T_{p}^{\prime}
(over both
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
) are
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
p
prime. Consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
p
,
\sigma
sign patterns for
N
, and
k\geq 2
even. Then the Hecke eigenvalues of
T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}
are
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
. In other words, for any sequence

\{(N_{i},k_{i},\sigma_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}

with
N_{i}+k_{i}\to\infty
, the collection of Hecke eigenvalues
\textnormal{eigv}(T^{\prime}_{m}|_{S_{k_{i}}^{\sigma_{i}}(N_{i})})
is
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
i\to\infty
.
Moreover, if we assume it is not the case that
4\|N
with
\sigma(4)=+1
, then the Hecke eigenvalues of
T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}
are also
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
.

Note here that similar
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution results have been shown before in different settings. For example, Conrey-Duke-Farmer showed
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution over
S_{k}(1)

[
7
]
and Serre showed
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution over
S_{k}(N)
,
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, and
S_{k}(N,\chi)

[
19
]
. More recently, the first author showed
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution over
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N,\chi)
for admissible characters
\chi

[
17
]
. Theorem
1.3
can be interpreted as strengthening Serre’s result over
S_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, showing that
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution still holds over the much finer sign pattern decompositions
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
. In fact, this sign pattern decomposition is, in some sense, the finest (natural) decomposition possible; the
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
seem to be the smallest subspaces of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
that still respect the Hecke operators and Galois conjugation
[
4
, Section 5]
.

Lastly, in Section
6
, we discuss several applications of Theorem
1.3
, similar to those in
[
19
, §6]
. The
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution shown in Theorem
1.3
can be used to obtain information about the Galois orbits of Hecke eigenvalues, or equivalently, about the coefficient fields
\mathbb{Q}(f)
of newforms
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
. In particular, we show that the degrees of these coefficient fields are unbounded as
N\to\infty
. This can be interpreted in terms of a weak form of the generalized Maeda conjecture. We are also able to obtain information about the simple
\mathbb{Q}
-factors of the Jacobian of the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
. See Section
6
for precise details.

[

]
to estimate

over both

and

. Then in Section

to obtain a trace formula for

over the sign pattern spaces.
Next, in Section

, we prove Theorems

and

, computing the exact proportion of how often each sign pattern can appear.
Then, in Section

, we use the
\mathrm{Tr}\,T_{m}^{\prime}
estimates of Section

to prove the
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution of the Hecke eigenvalues over
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
. In this section, we use the same general strategy used by Serre
[
19
]
. Finally, in Section

, we discuss applications of Theorem
1.3
.

.

Fix

Proof.

Note that
\sigma_{0}(Q)\leq\sigma_{0}(N)=O(N^{\varepsilon})

\sigma_{1}(m)=O(1)

\displaystyle\left(B(Q),m^{\prime}+\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}\right)\leq 2m,\qquad\left(B\left(\frac{N}{Q}\right),m^{\prime}-\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{N}{Q}}\leq\sqrt{N},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2m^{(k-1)/2}}\sum_{m^{\prime}|m}\min\left(m^{\prime},\frac{m}{m^{\prime}}\right)^{k-1}(2m)\sqrt{N}\leq\frac{(2m)\sqrt{N}}{{2}}\sum_{m^{\prime}|m}1=O(\sqrt{N}).

\displaystyle\left|p_{k}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{Q^{\prime}}},m\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2m^{(k-1)/2}}{\sqrt{\left|\frac{s^{2}}{Q^{\prime}}-4m\right|}}=\frac{2m^{(k-1)/2}}{\sqrt{\left\lvert\Delta\right\lvert/Q^{\prime}}}.

Using

\displaystyle H(D)\leq\frac{\sqrt{D}(\log D+2)}{\pi}.

So letting

\displaystyle\left|H_{\frac{N}{Q}}(\Delta)\right|

\displaystyle\left|p_{k}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{Q^{\prime}}},m\right)H_{\frac{N}{Q}}(\Delta)\right|

, and
\eta
are the nonzero multiplicative functions defined on prime powers via

\displaystyle\psi(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=p^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right),

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=p^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}-\left\llbracket\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right\rrbracket_{r\geq 2}+\left\llbracket\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right\rrbracket_{r\geq 3}\right),

\displaystyle\eta(p^{r})

\displaystyle:=\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}.

Note in particular that when
N
is squarefree,
\eta(Q)=\mathbbm{1}_{Q=1}
.

Proof.

Define the multiplicative function
\theta_{M}(d):=\frac{d}{(M,d)}
. Observe that
\mu{\circ}\theta_{M}
is also multiplicative since
\theta_{M}(d_{1}),\theta_{M}(d_{2})
are coprime whenever
d_{1},d_{2}
are coprime.

To show the first desired identity, it suffices (by Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.1
) to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N^{\prime}\mid N\\
N/N^{\prime}\mathrm{squarefree}\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{Q}{(Q,N^{\prime})}\right)\frac{N^{\prime}}{(Q,N^{\prime})}=\psi(N)\mathbbm{1}_{Q=1}.

(2.9)

Observe that
\mathbbm{1}_{N/N^{\prime}\ \text{squarefree}}=\mu^{2}(N/N^{\prime})
,
\mu\left(\frac{Q}{(Q,N^{\prime})}\right)=\mu\left(\frac{N/N^{\prime}}{(N/N^{\prime},N/Q)}\right)=\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}(N/N^{\prime})
, and
\frac{N^{\prime}}{(Q,N^{\prime})}=\theta_{Q}(N^{\prime})
. This means that

LHS of (
2.9
)

\displaystyle=[[\mu^{2}\cdot\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}]*\theta_{Q}](N)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}[[\mu^{2}\cdot\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}]*\theta_{Q}](p^{r})

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\mu^{2}(p^{i})\,\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}(p^{i})\,\theta_{Q}(p^{r-i})

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\Big(\theta_{Q}(p^{r})+\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}(p)\,\theta_{Q}(p^{r-1})\Big)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\begin{cases}p^{r}+p^{r-1}&\text{if }p\nmid Q\\
0&\text{if }p\mid Q\end{cases}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\psi(N)\mathbbm{1}_{Q=1}.

(2.15)

Note that here, the second-to-last equality comes from the facts that

\displaystyle\theta_{Q}(p^{s})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p^{s}&\text{if }p\nmid Q\\
1&\text{if }p|Q\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu{\circ}\theta_{N/Q}(p)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }p\nmid Q\\
-1&\text{if }p|Q.\end{cases}

(2.16)

Similarly, to show the second desired identity, it suffices (by Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.1
) to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{N^{\prime}|N}\alpha\left(\frac{N}{N^{\prime}}\right)\frac{N^{\prime}}{(Q,N^{\prime})}=\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\eta(Q).

(2.17)

And here,

LHS of (
2.17
)

\displaystyle=[\alpha*\theta_{Q}](N)

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}[\alpha*\theta_{Q}](p^{r})

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\alpha(p^{i})\theta_{Q}(p^{r-i})

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\parallel N}\Big(\theta_{Q}(p^{r})-\theta_{Q}(p^{r-1})-\left\llbracket\theta_{Q}(p^{r-2})\right\rrbracket_{r\geq 2}+\left\llbracket\theta_{Q}(p^{r-3})\right\rrbracket_{r\geq 3}\Big)

(2.21)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\parallel N}\begin{cases}\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(p^{r})&\text{if }p\nmid Q\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}&\text{if }p\mid Q\end{cases}

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\prod_{p\mid Q}\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{v_{p}(N)=2}}{\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(p^{2})}

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\prod_{p^{r}\|Q}\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\eta(Q),

(2.25)

as desired.
∎

3.
Trace Formula for
T^{\prime}_{m}
over the sign pattern spaces

In
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
, Martin derived a formula for
\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
in terms of the traces
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}W_{Q}
. Here, we prove a similar formula for
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}
in terms of the traces
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}
, respectively.

We note that the identity (
3.4
) underlying this lemma can also be interpreted as a special case of orthogonality for characters over a finite group. (The set of all exact divisors of
N
forms a group via the binary operation
Q_{1}*Q_{2}:=\frac{Q_{1}Q_{2}}{(Q_{1},Q_{2})^{2}}
. And sign patterns for
N
make up the characters on this group.)

Lemma 3.1
.

For
N\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}(N)}\,T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}\,T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}.

(3.2)

Proof.

We show this result just for the full space
S_{k}(N)
, and the argument for the newspace
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
is identical.

First, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\sigma^{\prime}(Q)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\sum_{Q\|p^{r}}\sigma(Q)\sigma^{\prime}(Q)=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\sigma^{\prime}(p^{r})\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{r}\|N}2\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma(p^{r})=\sigma^{\prime}(p^{r})}=2^{\omega(N)}\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}}.

(3.4)

Then, proceeding by splitting
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}
into a sum of
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}
for all sign patterns
\sigma^{\prime}
of
N
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}(N)}\,T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}{\circ}W_{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma(Q)\sigma^{\prime}(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}2^{\omega(N)}\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}}\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(N)}T_{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m},

as desired.
∎

We can then estimate
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{m}
by applying Proposition
2.3
to Lemma
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Fix
m\geq 1
.
Then for
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}\,T^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\frac{\psi(N)}{2^{\omega(N)}}+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}\,T^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\frac{\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}{2^{\omega(N)}}\prod_{p^{r}||N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N).\qquad

(3.6)

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.1
and then Proposition
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}T_{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}(N)}T_{m}^{\prime}{\circ}W_{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\|N}\sigma(Q)\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)\mathbbm{1}_{Q=1}+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\frac{\psi(N)}{2^{\omega(N)}}+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T_{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\mid\mid N}\sigma(Q)\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}T_{m}^{\prime}{\circ}W_{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\omega(N)}}\sum_{Q\|N}\sigma(Q)\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\eta(Q)+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{m=\square}}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{k-1}{12}\frac{\psi^{\mathrm{new}}(N)}{2^{\omega(N)}}\prod_{p^{r}||N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)+O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N),

as desired.
∎

We point out here that the main term of (
3.5
) always grows faster than the error term

O(N^{1/2}\sigma_{0}(N)^{2}\log N)=O(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})
. Furthermore, the main term of (
3.6
) grows faster than the error term, assuming it is not the case that
4\|N
with
\sigma(4)=+1
(because after excluding this one particular case, the
\prod_{p^{r}||N}\left(1+\sigma(p^{r})\frac{-\mathbbm{1}_{r=2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)
factor in (
3.6
) is
\geq 1/2
by (
1.7
)). In other words, the main terms of this corollary grow faster than the corresponding error terms for all admissible sign patterns
\sigma
.

4.

In this section, we show Theorems

and

5.
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution of Hecke eigenvalues

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
.
For a fixed prime
p
, let
\mu_{p}
denote the
p
-adic Plancherel measure
\displaystyle\mu_{p}(x)=\frac{p+1}{\pi}\frac{(1-x^{2}/4)^{1/2}}{(p^{1/2}+p^{-1/2})^{2}-x^{2}}dx
.
Then over the spaces
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
(for admissible sign patterns
\sigma
),
we show that the Hecke eigenvalues
for
T_{p}^{\prime}
are
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
.

Theorem
1.3
.

Fix
p
prime. Consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
p
,
\sigma
sign patterns for
N
, and
k\geq 2
even. Then the Hecke eigenvalues of
T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}
are
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
. In other words, for any sequence

\{(N_{i},k_{i},\sigma_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}

with
N_{i}+k_{i}\to\infty
, the collection of Hecke eigenvalues
\textnormal{eigv}(T^{\prime}_{m}|_{S_{k_{i}}^{\sigma_{i}}(N_{i})})
is
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
i\to\infty
.
Moreover, if we assume it is not the case that
4\|N
with
\sigma(4)=+1
, then the Hecke eigenvalues of
T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}
are also
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed as
N+k\to\infty
.

Proof.

We just state the argument here for
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
, and an identical argument works for the full space
S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)
.

To show asymptotic
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution of the Hecke eigenvalues, it suffices (e.g. by Kuipers-Niederreiter
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, Serre
[
19
, Proposition 2]
) to show that

\frac{1}{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}\sum_{\lambda\in\text{eigv}\left(T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}\right)}X_{n}(\lambda)\longrightarrow\int_{-2}^{2}X_{n}(x)\mu_{p}(x)\quad\text{as }N+k\to\infty

(5.1)

for the Chebyshev polynomials
X_{n}(x):=U_{n}(x/2)
for
n\geq 0
, since they span the space of all polynomials, which is a dense subspace of all continuous functions on
[-2,2]
.

The integral on the right hand side of (
5.1
) has value
p^{-n/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\text{$n$ even}}
, by Serre
[
19
, Equation (20)]
.

Additionally, it is well-known (see
[
19
, Lemme 1]
, for example) that

T_{p^{n}}^{\prime}=X_{n}(T_{p}^{\prime}).

This means that

LHS of (
5.1
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}\sum_{\lambda\in\text{eigv}\left(T^{\prime}_{p}|_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}\right)}X_{n}(\lambda)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}X_{n}(T^{\prime}_{p})}{\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{1}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{p^{n}}}{\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}T^{\prime}_{1}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\to p^{-n/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\text{$n$ even}}\quad\text{as }N+k\to\infty,

(5.5)

where for the last step, we used Corollary
3.2
at
m=p^{n}
and
m=1
(since
\sigma
is an admissible sign pattern). This verifies (
5.1
), completing the proof.
∎

analogous to those in Serre

.

For each
N\geq 1
and
\sigma
admissible sign patterns for

For each

Similarly for

Corollary 6.1
.

Fix

. Then for

\sigma
admissible sign patterns for

\displaystyle\frac{s(N,k,\sigma)_{r}}{\dim S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}

\displaystyle\to 0\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{s(N,k,\sigma)^{\mathrm{new}}_{r}}{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}

\displaystyle\to 0\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Proof.

We show the proof only over the full space, as the proof over the newspace is identical. For
N
coprime to
p
, let
s(N,k,\sigma,p)_{r}
denote the number of indices
i
such that
[\mathbb{Q}(x_{i,p}):\mathbb{Q}]\leq r
. Note that
s(N,k,\sigma,p)_{r}\geq s(N,k,\sigma)_{r}
, so it suffices to show

\frac{s(N,k,\sigma,p)_{r}}{\dim S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
while
(N,p)=1
.
Note that if
x=x_{i,p}
satisfies
[\mathbb{Q}(x_{i,p}):\mathbb{Q}]\leq r,
then
x
also satisfies:

(1)

x
is a totally real algebraic integer of degree
\leq r
;

(2)

For
\tau\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})

of

Let

be the set of all

must have degree
\leq r
and bounded integer coefficients. Let

be the dilation of

under the mapping

The set

is finite, so it has

(and hence

) tends to

as

(e.g. by Serre
[

). This is equivalent to saying that
\frac{s(N,k,\sigma,p)_{r}}{\dim S_{k}^{\sigma}(N)}\to 0
as

completing the proof.

We note here that over the sign pattern newspaces

, where
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)}

From this perspective, Corollary
6.1
can be interpreted as a weak form of the Maeda Conjecture for general level.

Recall that the classical Maeda Conjecture states that
\deg\mathbb{Q}(f)=\dim S_{k}(1)
(i.e. is maximum possible) for each normalized Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{k}(1)
. For squarefree levels
N
, a corresponding conjecture has also been stated for the newspaces. Since the Hecke operators restrict to the
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma}(N)
; for newforms
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, the maximum possible degree of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
is
\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma_{f}}(N)
, where
\sigma_{f}
denotes the sign pattern for
f
. In particular, Tsaknias and Chow-Ghitza posited a version of the Maeda Conjecture for squarefree level: that for newforms
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
,
\deg\mathbb{Q}(f)=\dim S_{k}^{\mathrm{new},\sigma_{f}}(N)
for sufficiently large weight
k
(see
[
21
, Conjecture 2.4]
and
[
4
, Conjecture 5.2]
).

For general level
N
, however, the picture is not as clear. In this case, there seem to be plenty of examples where
\deg\mathbb{Q}(f)
is not maximal. However, weaker forms of the Maeda Conjecture have still been investigated. For example, Roberts conjectured that for any fixed weight
k\geq 6
,
\deg\mathbb{Q}(f)=1
for only finitely many newforms
f

[
16
, Conjecture 1.1]
. Later, Martin made the same conjecture for arbitrary fixed degree

[
12
, Question 3]
. Here, Corollary

can be interpreted as a density
1
version of these conjectures. In particular, for any fixed

, Corollary
6.1
shows that the proportion of newforms

with

tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
(along
N

p
).

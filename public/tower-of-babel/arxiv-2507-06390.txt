Introduction

Write
K=K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
for the complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph (henceforth
r
-graph) with parts of size
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\cdots\leq s_{r}
. More precisely, the vertex set of

S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}
, where
|S_{i}|=s_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, and the edge set of

\{\{x_{i},\ldots,x_{r}\}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{r}\}.

Given
K
as above, write
{\rm ex}(n,K)
for the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
r
-graph that contains no copy of
K
as a subhypergraph. Similarly, write
z(n,K)
for the maximum number of edges in an
r
-partite
r
-graph

with parts
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
, each of size
n
, such that there is no copy of
K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
in
H
with
S_{i}\subset X_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
(there could be copies of
K
in
H
, where for some
i

S_{i}\not\subset X_{i}
). Determining
{\rm ex}(n,K)={\rm ex}_{r}(n,K)
is usually called the Turán problem, while determining
z(n,K)=z_{r}(n,K)
is called the Zarankiewicz problem (we will omit the subscript
r
if it is obvious from context). These are fundamental questions in combinatorics with applications in analysis
[
1
,
7
]
, number theory
[
16
]
, group theory
[
13
]
, geometry
[
10
]
, and computer science
[

]

A basic result in extremal hypergraph theory, due to Erdős
[
9
]
, is the upper bound

{\rm ex}(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}))=O(n^{r-1/s}),

(1)

where
s=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}
(and, as before
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\cdots\leq s_{r-1}\leq s_{r}
). Here
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
are fixed and the asymptotic notation is taken as
n\to\infty
. As

is fixed, and
z(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}))\leq{\rm ex}(rn,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}))
, the same upper bound as (
1
) holds for
z(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}))
.

A major problem in extremal (hyper)graph theory is to obtain corresponding lower bounds to (
1
) (or prove that no such lower bounds exist). In fact, it was conjectured in
[
15
]
that the exponent
r-1/s
in (
1
) is optimal. This question has been studied for graphs since the 1930s, and results of Erdős-Rényi and Brown
[
5
]
gave optimal (in the exponent) lower bounds for

and
K(3,t)
. The first breakthrough for arbitrary

occurred in the mid 1990’s by Kollar-Ronyai-Szabo
[
11
]
and then Alon-Ronyai-Szabo
[
2
]
, who proved that
{\rm ex}(n,K(s_{1},s_{2}))=\Omega(n^{2-1/s_{1}})
as long as
s_{2}>(s_{1}-1)!
. More recently, in another significant advance,
Bukh
[
6
]
has proved the same lower bound as long as
s_{2}>9^{s_{1}+o(s_{1})}
.

For

, the first nontrivial constructions that were superior to the bound given by the probabilistic deletion method were provided in the cases

by the current author

]

Later, optimal bounds for both

and

were provided by Ma, Yuan, Zhang
[
14
]
(and independently by Verstraëte) by extending the method of Bukh, however, the threshold for

for which the bound holds was not even explicitly calculated. More recently,

Theorem 1

Fix

z_{r}(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-1},t))>n^{1-o(1)}\cdot z_{r-1}(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-3},s_{r-2}s_{r-1},t)).

z_{r}(n,K(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-1},t))>n^{r-2-o(1)}\cdot z_{2}(n,K(s,t))

Proof

Write
e(H)=|E(H)|
for a hypergraph
H
.
To prove Theorem

, we need the following well-known consequence of Behrend’s construction

]
of a subset of

contains no path with three edges. Additionally,

e(G)=\sum_{i}|M_{i}|=n^{2-o(1)}
.

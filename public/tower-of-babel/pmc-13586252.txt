A decade-long retrospective analysis of cell line authentication in China using over 10,000 samples
Authors: Cuijie Chen, Xiankun Zhao, Yan He, Xuekun Chen, Yuzhen Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Since the 1951 establishment of the HeLa cell line, immortalized cell lines have served as indispensable in vitro models for basic and clinical biomedical research. However, misidentification and contamination continue to undermine experimental reproducibility and validity. Here, we authenticate 11,087 cell lines from 6,947 Chinese institutions between 2016 and 2025 using STR profiling. Decade-long analysis reveals steady annual increases in testing volume and accuracy, identifying HeLa, T24, K-562, HEK293T, and A-549 as the most frequent contaminants. We observe that mutation rates at core STR loci are two to three orders of magnitude higher than those of germline mutations. Given the apparent variability in genomic stability across these loci, we develop a novel weighted algorithm based on locus-specific mutation frequencies of the eight core loci. Re-analysis demonstrates that this algorithm outperforms the classical Tanabe method by reducing false positives and negatives while increasing overall stringency. Additionally, the rising use of murine models highlights the urgent need for more comprehensive reference databases. Our large-scale analysis reflects a growing commitment within China’s biomedical community to combat misidentification, offering critical insights for preventing contamination and providing conceptual avenues for next-generation authentication strategies.

Introduction

Continuous cell lines represent essential biological platforms that underpin biomedical discovery and streamline the translation of therapeutic innovation. However, the scientific validity of cell-based research is frequently threatened by systemic issues of cross-contamination and misidentification, with estimates suggesting that 20% to 30% of all cell lines currently in use may be compromised1–3. This phenomenon has led to a “contaminated literature” exceeding 30,000 primary articles, which in turn have been cited by over half a million subsequent studies4,5. Despite the long-standing recognition of “super-contaminants” like HeLa displacing hundreds of unique cultures across diverse tissue types, the absence of universal, rigorous authentication mandates at the bench level remains a significant vulnerability in the global biomedical research community6,7. Currently, Short Tandem Repeat (STR) profiling stands as the international gold standard for verifying human cell line identity, providing a robust framework for genetic fingerprinting that is increasingly required by top-tier journals and funding agencies8–12. However, the utility of standard STR profiling is occasionally confounded by the inherent genomic instability of immortalized lines, particularly in high-passage cultures or specialized cancer subtypes13,14. The occurrence of de novo mutations and loss of heterozygosity (LOH) can complicate the interpretation of genetic fingerprints, raising questions about whether a fixed matching threshold (e.g., 80%) is universally applicable across all loci without considering their specific stability.

In the Chinese research ecosystem, the rapid expansion of biomedical output has been accompanied by complex challenges regarding reagent integrity15. Early preliminary evaluations revealed alarming misidentification rates, with investigations into hundreds of widely used tumor lines finding that nearly half were cross-contaminated16,17. This alarming discovery prompted a strategic shift toward more rigorous oversight and standardized cell line authentication. While longitudinal assessments over the past decade have identified a “silver lining” of improving authentication practices, persistent concerns remain, such as the perennial misuse of the L-02 and SMMC-7721 lines-both of which have been confirmed as HeLa derivatives-as “normal” or “hepatocellular carcinoma” models18,19. The legacy of these ‘zombie’ cell lines extends beyond mere nomenclature; for instance, using the HeLa-derived L-02 as a ‘normal liver’ model in toxicological assays potentially generates misleading safety profiles, thereby compromising the downstream development of hepatoprotective drugs and polluting the biological big-data landscape20,21. Previous assessments have often lacked the necessary breadth-in terms of sample volume, time span, and institutional diversity-to fully capture the dynamics of immortalized cell line use in China. There is, therefore, an urgent need for a comprehensive census to resolve the ambiguity surrounding cell line integrity across the country’s expanding scientific ecosystem.

To address these longstanding challenges, we compiled a comprehensive dataset of 11,087 human cell lines submitted for authentication from 6947 distinct institutions, including universities, research institutes, hospitals, and biopharmaceutical enterprises, across 31 provinces in China between 2016 and 2025. Through this extensive cohort, we systematically identified the primary sources of cross-contamination within the Chinese research community and, for the first time, calculated the specific mutation rates of core STR loci across a massive sample size of immortalized lines. Building upon these mutation rates, we developed a locus-stability weighted scoring system that assigns higher weights to stable loci (e.g., TH01) and lower weights to highly mutable loci (e.g., CSF1PO). By utilizing the largest and most spatially diverse sample set reported to date, this investigation establishes a comprehensive perspective on cell line usage trends in China over the past decade, offering critical insights into the prevention of future contamination and providing conceptual avenues for next-generation authentication strategies.

Trends in scale, accuracy, and geographical coverage of cell line authentication in China

The analysis from 2016 to 2025 demonstrates that both the volume of authentication submissions and the accuracy of cell line identification have increased dramatically over the decade. Regarding authentication scale (Fig. 2a), the total annual cell count exhibited a robust upward trajectory, rising from approximately 500 samples in 2016 to a peak of nearly 3000 by 2025. Linear regression analysis demonstrated a statistically significant increase in the number of submissions over the years (P = 0.0007). This expansion was primarily driven by identity-disclosed cell lines. As shown in Fig. 2b, the overall accuracy improved from a lower baseline in the first five-year period (2016–2020), with a mean of approximately 0.70, to a higher level in the subsequent period (2021–2025), where the mean reached approximately 0.85. The Cochran-Armitage trend test revealed a statistically significant increasing trend in accuracy over the years (P < 2.2 × 10−16). Notably, the accuracy for identity-undisclosed cell lines showed the most substantial gains, exceeding 0.90 by 2025. Beyond the temporal trends, the geographical analysis reveals that cell line authentication requests covered 31 out of 34 provincial-level administrative regions in China (Fig. 2c). Discussions

This study represents the largest decadal census of cell line integrity in China, analyzing 11,087 samples across 31 provinces from 2016 to 2025. Our findings reveal a significant upward trajectory in both authentication volume and accuracy, reflecting a growing national commitment to research integrity. Despite this progress, we identified persistent threats from “super-contaminants” like HeLa, which remains the primary source of cross-contamination, particularly in liver and stomach models. Considering the significant variability in mutation rates across core STR loci, we propose a locus-stability weighted scoring system designed based on the observed mutation rates of each STR locus. This system prioritizes stable loci (e.g., TH01) over more mutable ones (e.g., CSF1PO), thereby increasing algorithm robustness against culturing-induced artifacts. Collectively, these data provide a vital roadmap for preventing contamination and implementing next-generation authentication strategies to safeguard the validity of global biomedical research.

Our decade-long analysis revealed that the submission volumes rose markedly, interrupted only briefly by pandemic disruptions, while authentication accuracy improved from ~70% in the early period to a sustained ~85% post-2020. These trends likely reflect a maturing awareness and institutional commitment to cell line authentication within the Chinese biomedical community. The consistent increase in submission volumes evidences the progressive integration of cell line authentication into standard laboratory protocols as a core element of quality assurance. This trend is likely attributable to a confluence of factors, including progressively stringent journal mandates, heightened requirements from funding bodies, and targeted educational programs that prioritize experimental reproducibility22–24. However, these encouraging trends must be interpreted with caution due to the inherent self-selection bias present in voluntary authentication datasets. Because researchers proactively submitting samples for STR profiling are often already more “integrity-conscious,” our data likely reflects the best-case scenario within the scientific community. This leaves a vast “dark matter” of the research ecosystem, laboratories that never authenticate their lines, entirely unmonitored and excluded from these performance gains.

Notably, liver and gallbladder as well as stomach and esophagus cell lines demonstrated the lowest authentication accuracy (approximately 0.65 and 0.75, respectively), whereas cell lines derived from the cecum, colon, and rectum exhibited the highest accuracy. This tissue-specific pattern is largely driven by the markedly lower prevalence of HeLa-derived contamination, the predominant contaminant observed in this study, in colorectal-derived cell lines within our large cohort25. While differences in intrinsic cellular properties (e.g., proliferation rate or culture robustness) cannot be entirely excluded, the observed disparity aligns more closely with historical usage patterns and contamination dissemination of specific liver-derived lines in Chinese research laboratories. Further mechanistic studies are warranted to fully elucidate these differences.

Although the ANSI/ATCC ASN-0002-2022 standard has strengthened human cell line authentication by standardizing the use of 13 core STR loci26, our analysis highlights persistent heterogeneity in mutation rates across these loci in cultured cell lines. This variability can produce partial mismatches or extra alleles, complicating traditional equal-weight matching algorithms and risking ambiguous authentication results. Among the STR loci analyzed, CSF1PO exhibited the highest mutation frequency, whereas TH01 showed the lowest. This observation is consistent with their sequence characteristics. CSF1PO is a simple tetranucleotide repeat (AGAT)n located in intron 6 of the CSF1R gene and tends to have longer uninterrupted repeat stretches, making it more prone to slippage during DNA replication. In contrast, TH01 is a tetranucleotide repeat (AATG)n or (TCAT)n located in intron 1 of the tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) gene and typically possesses shorter, more interrupted repeat structures, conferring greater stability27,28. These intrinsic sequence features likely contribute to the observed differences in mutation frequency during long-term cell line culture. In addition, we observed that certain core STR loci were more prone to loss of heterozygosity (LOH), while others tended toward gain of heterozygosity. This locus-specific pattern likely stems from distinct mutational mechanisms. For example, CSF1PO frequently showed gains of alleles (e.g., transition from 11/12 to 11/12/13), consistent with its longer uninterrupted AGAT repeats that are highly susceptible to slipped-strand mispairing during DNA replication. In contrast, TH01 was more stable and occasionally exhibited LOH. LOH is commonly associated with chromosomal-scale events, including deletions, mitotic recombination, or loss of entire chromosomes, processes frequently observed in genomically unstable cancer cell lines under prolonged culture11. Conversely, gains of alleles are predominantly driven by slipped-strand mispairing during DNA replication, which is more prevalent at loci with longer uninterrupted repeat tracts27. These findings underscore the complex interplay between sequence characteristics, genomic instability, and selective pressures in vitro. Loci with high mutation rates tend to undergo culture-induced genetic drift with relatively minor functional impact, whereas low-mutation-rate loci are more likely to experience LOH that may reflect larger genomic alterations. Consequently, the classic Tanabe algorithm’s equal-weight treatment of all loci is suboptimal and requires improvement.

To mitigate this, we propose a locus-stability weighted scoring system designed based on the observed mutation rates of each STR locus. This system prioritizes stable loci (e.g., TH01) over more mutable ones (e.g., CSF1PO), thereby increasing algorithm robustness against culturing-induced artifacts. Evaluation on 28 representative samples showed that the weighted algorithm improved matching scores in 79.3% of cases. It successfully rescued 5 false positives, samples previously classified as no match by the classic Tanabe algorithm, thereby enabling stricter and more accurate authentication by better accounting for culture-induced genetic drift. Meanwhile, the identification of only 1 false negative demonstrates that the new algorithm can also correct misclassifications made by the classic method. The majority of samples exhibited a modest decrease in matching scores, further indicating a stricter yet more biologically relevant screening criterion. Overall, integrating stability weights yields a more biologically informed and statistically robust matching score, effectively balancing sensitivity and specificity while better accommodating in vitro genetic evolution during long-term cell line culture.

The marked increase in non-human submissions over recent years-predominantly murine lines-underscores the growing reliance on model organism systems in contemporary studies. A pioneering multiplex PCR method was developed and validated in an interlaboratory study by Almeida et al.29, incorporating 18 mouse-specific STR markers along with two human-specific STR markers to simultaneously detect potential human cross-contamination. However, reference databases for mouse STR profiles, such as the Expasy Cellosaurus database30, remain relatively small and fragmented compared to human counterparts, often covering only a few hundred well-characterized lines. This lag in database development has not kept pace with rising utilization, highlighting the pressing need for coordinated international efforts to build more comprehensive and substantially larger repositories that incorporate standardized panels. Additionally, accurate species identification is equally critical to exclude interspecies contamination, routinely performed via DNA barcoding, which provides definitive taxonomic resolution across mammalian cell lines31. Recently, parallel progress has emerged for rat cell lines with the establishment of a validated 16-locus STR multiplexing method, offering a promising framework to extend reliable authentication to this additional widely used rodent model32. Integrating these complementary techniques into routine practice will ensure robust quality control as reliance on diverse model organism systems continues to grow.

Although the scale and temporal scope of our dataset represent a substantial advance in characterizing cell line authentication practices in China, several important limitations should be noted. A fundamental constraint arises from the voluntary nature of submissions to the authentication service. Inherent biases in the dataset must be acknowledged, as submissions likely overrepresent institutions with heightened awareness and resources, potentially leading to an overestimation of national authentication accuracy. The representativeness of the data is limited by the sparse distribution of samples; although the study encompasses 11,087 samples, the average per institution (1.6 across 6947 institutions) is low. This sparsity is most evident in inland provinces with few to no submissions, highlighting a geographic gap that future broader institutional engagement must bridge. Additionally, the study does not track downstream research outcomes for submitting clients, such as whether authenticated cell lines were subsequently used in successful grant applications, high-impact publications, or reproducible studies. This absence limits evaluation of the practical value of authentication, whether proactive testing correlates with enhanced funding success, publication rates, or research credibility, and prevents assessment of whether low-submission regions face compounded disadvantages in competitive scientific outputs. Meanwhile, the development of our weighted algorithm is currently limited by the available sample sources. To further validate its performance, larger cohorts with sufficient positive (contaminated) and negative (authentic) samples will be required to evaluate accuracy, false-positive and false-negative rates using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves33 and F1 scores (the harmonic mean of precision and recall)34.

As the largest decadal census to date, our study reveals a steady upward trajectory in both authentication volumes and accuracy, reflecting a maturing commitment to research integrity within China’s biomedical community. However, persistent threats from “super-contaminants” like HeLa, particularly in liver and gastric models, continue to challenge the field. By developing a locus-stability weighted scoring system (SW-Tanabe) based on observed mutation rates of core STR loci, we propose a more biologically relevant approach for cell line authentication. This algorithm prioritizes stable loci and better accommodates culture-induced genetic drift. We hope that the SW-Tanabe system will provide a practical framework and inspire future improvements in STR-based authentication guidelines, ultimately contributing to higher standards of research reproducibility in the biomedical community. Furthermore, the rising utilization of murine models underscores an urgent need for expanded non-human reference repositories. These insights offer a strategic roadmap for safeguarding experimental reproducibility and inform next-generation authentication strategies.

Methods

Data resources

The summary-level cell line authentication data were initially retrieved from the service portal of Genetic Testing Biotechnology (Suzhou) Co., Ltd., one of China’s largest providers of cell line authentication services. During the extraction process, all personally identifiable information and client-specific metadata were anonymized or redacted to ensure data privacy. The dataset is firstly curated into two primary categories: human cell lines and non-human cell lines.

STR profiling and DNA barcode species identification

The genomic DNA of the submitted cell lines were extracted using a Chelex-100 method35. For human cell lines, genotyping was performed in accordance with the ANSI/ATCC ASN-0002-2022 international standards, utilizing the SiFaSTRTM 23plex kit to target 21 short tandem repeat (STR) loci alongside the Amelogenin marker. While the MusSTR 18 plex kit was used to perform the genotyping of mouse cell lines. One additional marker (human TH01) was used to screen for the presence of human species. Subsequent fragment analysis and data processing were conducted on an ABI Prism® 3130 XL Genetic Analyzer via GeneMapper® ID v3.2 software. To ensure technical reliability, validated positive and negative controls accompanied every sample run. Species identification was conducted via DNA barcoding, utilizing PCR amplification of the 12S rRNA, 16S rRNA, or COI markers36. The amplicons were sequenced using the Sanger method and cross-referenced against global repositories through BLAST to verify consistency between the genetic profile and the claimed species.

Data comparison

The generated STR profiles were systematically cross-referenced against major international reference repositories, primarily Expasy Cellosaurus, ATCC, and DSMZ. This comparison focused on a core set of 8 or 13 highly polymorphic STR loci to ensure cross-platform/cell line compatibility. The degree of matching was quantified using the Tanabe Algorithm to derive an Evaluation Value (EV), defined as follows: EV = [(2×No. shared alleles)/(No. query alleles+ No. reference alleles)] × 100%. According to established consensus guidelines37, an EV > 80% was utilized as the threshold to confirm a “match” while profiles falling below this value were flagged “no match”.

Data curation

During the data curation of 10,503 human cell line cases, samples were categorized into two primary analytical streams based on whether the client provided the cell identity at the time of submission: identity-disclosed submissions and identity-undisclosed (blind) submissions. Within the identity-disclosed cohort, the vast majority of commercial cell lines were curated as authenticated (T) if the EV was > 80% compared with the disclosed reference profile. Conversely, they were flagged as misidentified (F) if the EV exceeded 80% against a different reference, if the sample was identified as non-human, or if no match was found despite an available reference in the database. For primary cell lines, cases were curated as T if no matching commercial profile was detected, while they were labeled F if they matched an existing commercial line (EV > 80%) or were of non-human origin. A specialized subset of tissue-versus-cell-line comparisons was curated using a likelihood ratio (LR) threshold, where LR > 1 × 1015 confirmed authentication (T).

For the identity-undisclosed (blind) submissions, the curation followed a more complex verification logic: profiles with no database matches were curated as T, whereas those showing an EV > 80% match against any commercial cell line required mandatory secondary confirmation with the client. These were ultimately curated as T only if the match was verified to be consistent with the client’s internal records, whereas inconsistent matches were marked as F, and cases where clients were unable or unwilling to confirm the identity were excluded from the final dataset.

The curation of non-human cell line cases followed a specialized verification framework. For mouse cell lines, the system only accepted identity-disclosed submissions, as blind testing for murine samples was not supported; these cases were authenticated and categorized using the same EV thresholds applied to human cell lines. For general species identification, curation remained focused on DNA barcode analysis (16S/12S/COI sequencing) to verify consistency between the genetic sequence and the claimed species.

Statistics and Reproducibility

The study analyzed 10,503 human cell line cases together with non-human cell line submissions obtained through routine authentication services. Samples were excluded only when the identity of identity-undisclosed submissions could not be verified because the submitting client was unable or unwilling to provide confirmation of the matched cell line identity. All other samples meeting quality-control requirements were included in the analyses. STR profiling was performed using validated laboratory workflows with positive and negative controls included in each analytical batch. Descriptive statistics were employed to summarize the trends in cell line authentication and the prevalence of contamination across the ten-year study period. All categorical variables, including the status of cell line authenticity, were expressed as frequencies and percentages. The temporal trend in annual submission volumes was assessed using simple linear regression, with the year of testing treated as the independent variable. To determine whether the proportion of correctly identified cell lines exhibited a significant monotonic trend over time, the Cochran-Armitage trend test was performed. A one-sided P-value was calculated to test the hypothesis of a steady yearly increase in authentication accuracy. For the comparative analysis of misidentification rates across different cell types, the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was utilized where appropriate. All statistical computations were performed using R (version 2024.12.0) and ggplot2 for visualization. Unless otherwise stated, a two-tailed P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

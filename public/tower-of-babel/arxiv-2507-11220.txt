Abstract
We say that a Tychonoff space X X is a κ \kappa -space if it is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of C p ​ ( Y ) C_{p}(Y) for some locally compact space Y Y . The class of κ \kappa -spaces is strictly between the class of Dieudonné complete spaces and the class of μ \mu -spaces. We show that the class of κ \kappa -spaces has nice stability properties, that allows us to define the κ \kappa -completion κ ​ X \kappa X of X X as the smallest κ \kappa -space in the Stone–Čech compactification β ​ X \beta X of X X containing X X . For a point z ∈ β ​ X z\in\beta X , we show that (1) if z ∈ υ ​ X z\in\upsilon X , then the Dirac measure δ z \delta_{z} at z z is bounded on each compact subset of C p ​ ( X ) C_{p}(X) , (2) z ∈ κ ​ X z\in\kappa X iff δ z \delta_{z} is continuous on each compact subset of C p ​ ( X ) C_{p}(X) iff δ z \delta_{z} is continuous on each compact subset of C p b ​ ( X ) C_{p}^{b}(X) , (3) z ∈ υ ​ X z\in\upsilon X iff δ z \delta_{z} is bounded on each compact subset of C p b ​ ( X ) C_{p}^{b}(X) . It is proved that κ ​ X \kappa X is the largest subspace Y Y of β ​ X \beta X containing X X for which C p ​ ( Y ) C_{p}(Y) and C p ​ ( X ) C_{p}(X) have the same compact subsets, this result essentially generalizes a known result of R. Haydon.

Introduction

We denote by
C_{p}(X)
the space
C(X)
of all continuous real-valued functions on a Tychonoff space
X
endowed with the pointwise topology. It is well-known that each Tychonoff space
X
is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of the space
C_{p}(Y)
for some Tychonoff space
Y
(one can take
Y=C_{p}(X)
). The classical characterization of realcompact spaces states that
X
is realcompact if, and only if,
X
is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of
C_{p}(D)
for some discrete (the same sequential, see Proposition
3.5
below) space
D
. These results motivate us to consider the following general problem.

Problem 1.1

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a family of Tychonoff spaces. Characterize those spaces
X
which are homeomorphic to a closed subspace of
C_{p}(Y)
for some
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
.

In this article we solve Problem
1.1
for one of the most important classes of Tychonoff spaces, namely, the class
\mathcal{LCS}
of all locally compact spaces. We shall say that a Tychonoff space
X
is a
\kappa
-space
if it is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of
C_{p}(Y)
for some locally compact space
Y
. In Theorem
3.1
we give several characterizations of
\kappa
-spaces, in which we show in particular that
X
is a
\kappa
-space if, and only if, it is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of
C_{p}(Y)
for the topological sum
Y
of a family of compact spaces if, and only if,
X
is homeomorphic to a closed subspace of
C_{p}(Y)
for some
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
Y
. The condition of being a
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space cannot be replaced by the strictly weaker condition of being an Ascoli space, see Remark
3.4
(for numerous results concerning
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-spaces and Ascoli spaces, see
[
11
]
). In Proposition
3.3
we show that the class of all
\kappa
-spaces consists of all Dieudonné complete spaces and is contained in the class of all
\mu
-spaces. However, there are
\kappa
-spaces which are not Dieudonné complete (Example
4.8
) as well as there are
\mu
-spaces which are not
\kappa
-spaces (Example
4.9
). It turns out that the class of all
\kappa
-spaces has nice stability properties (Proposition
3.3
) which allow us to define the
\kappa
-completion
of
X
as the smallest subspace of the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta X
of
X
which is a
\kappa
-space. We characterize the
\kappa
-completion
\kappa X
of
X
and the realcompactification
\upsilon X
of
X
as follows, where
\delta_{z}
is the Dirac measure at a point
z
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space, and let
z\in\upsilon X
.

(i)

\delta_{z}
is bounded on each compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
.

(ii)

z\in\kappa X
if, and only if,
\delta_{z}
is continuous on each compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
.

For the space
C_{p}^{b}(X)\subseteq C_{p}(X)
of all
bounded
functions on a Tychonoff space
X
, we have the following analogue of Theorem
1.2
(in which we consider the points of
\beta X
, but not only of
\upsilon X
).

Theorem 1.3

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space, and let
z\in\beta X
.

(i)

z\in\upsilon X
if, and only if,
\delta_{z}
is bounded on each compact subset of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
.

(ii)

z\in\kappa X
if, and only if,
\delta_{z}
is continuous on each compact subset of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
.

We denote by
\mu X
the
\mu
-completion
of a Tychonoff space
X
(i.e.,
\mu X
is the smallest subspace of
\beta X
containing

which is a
\mu
-space). A famous result of Haydon

and

In the following theorem we essentially strengthen Haydon’s assertion.

Theorem 1.4

Let

be a subspace of a Tychonoff space

. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

C_{p}(Y)
and

(ii)

(iii)

Y\subseteq\kappa X
.

Consequently,
\kappa X
can be characterized as the largest subspace

\beta X

Let

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

an

of

Theorem 2.1

For every Tychonoff space

We shall use the following topological result.

Lemma 2.2

Let
T:X\to Y
and
R:X\to Z
be embeddings. Then
T{\bigtriangleup}R:X\to Y\times Z
,
T{\bigtriangleup}R(x):=\big(T(x),R(x)\big)
, is an embedding. If, moreover,
T(X)
is a closed subset of
Y
, then
T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)
is a closed subset of
Y\times Z
.

Proof 1

The mapping
T{\bigtriangleup}R
is an embedding by Theorem 2.3.20 of
[
5
]
. Assume that
T(X)
is a closed subset of
Y
. To show that
T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)
is a closed subset of
Y\times Z
, let
(y,z)\not\in T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)
.

Assume that
y\not\in T(X)
. Since
T(X)
is closed, there is a neighborhood
U
of
y
such that
U\cap T(X)=\emptyset
. Then
U\times Z
is a neighborhood of
(y,z)
such that
(U\times Z)\cap\big(T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)\big)=\emptyset
.

Assume that
y\in T(X)
. Since
T
is an embedding, there is a unique
x\in X
such that
T(x)=y
. As
(y,z)\not\in T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)
, we have
z\not=R(x)
. Choose a neighborhood
V\subseteq Z
of
z
and a neighborhood
W\subseteq Z
of
R(x)
such that
V\cap W=\emptyset
. Choose a neighborhood
Q\subseteq Y
of
y
such that
Q\cap T(X)\subseteq T\big(R^{-1}(W)\big)
. Then
Q\times V
is a neighborhood of
(y,z)
. We claim that
(Q\times V)\cap\big(T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)\big)=\emptyset
. Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that there is
(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in(Q\times V)\cap\big(T{\bigtriangleup}R(X)\big)
. Then there is
x^{\prime}\in X
such that
T(x^{\prime})=y^{\prime}\in Q
and
R(x^{\prime})=z^{\prime}\in V
. Therefore
T(x^{\prime})\in Q\cap T(X)\subseteq T\big(R^{-1}(W)\big)
. Since
T
and

are bijective, we obtain

. Therefore
z^{\prime}\in V\cap W=\emptyset
, which is impossible. This contradiction finishes the proof.∎

The following result is of independent interest (possibly the existence of an embedding is known but hard to find explicitly stated, so we give also a detailed proof of this fact).

Proposition 2.3

Each complete metrizable space
(X,\rho)
is closely embedded into
C_{p}(K)
for some compact space
K
.

Proof 2

Consider the following subset of
C_{p}(X)

K:=\big\{f\in C_{p}(X):\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\mbox{ and }|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\rho(x,y)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in X\big\}.

It is easy to see that
K
is a closed subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{X}
, which is equicontinuous and contained in
C(X)
.
Being also pointwise bounded,
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{X}
and hence of
C_{p}(X)
.
It remains to prove that the mapping
\varphi:X\to C_{p}(K)
defined by

\varphi(x)(f):=f(x),\quad\mbox{ where }\;x\in X\;\mbox{ and }\;f\in K,

is an embedding with closed image. Clearly,
\varphi
is continuous.

To show that
\varphi
is injective, fix
x_{0}\in X
and observe that the function
g(x):=\tfrac{\rho(x,x_{0})}{\rho(x,x_{0})+1}
belongs to
K
by the triangle inequality. Since
g(x)\not=g(x_{0})=0
,
\varphi
is injective.

We claim that
\varphi:X\to\varphi(X)
is open. Indeed, since
K
is closed in
C_{p}(X)
, the restriction mapping
\pi_{K}:C_{p}(C_{p}(X))\to C_{p}(K)
is open onto its image by Proposition 0.4.1(2) of
[
1
]
. Recall also (see Corollary 0.5.5 of
[
1
]
) that the mapping
\psi:X\to C_{p}(C_{p}(X))
,
\psi(x)(f)=f(x)
, is an embedding. Since
\varphi=\pi_{K}\circ\psi
, we obtain that
\varphi:X\to\varphi(X)
is open.

It remains to prove that
\varphi(X)
is closed in
C_{p}(K)
. Let
F\in\overline{\varphi(X)}
. Take a net
\{\varphi(x_{i})\}_{i\in I}
in
\varphi(X)
converging to
F
. Then
\{\varphi(x_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a Cauchy net in
\varphi(X)
. Since
\varphi
is an embedding,
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a Cauchy net in
X
considering as a uniform space. Taking into account that
X
is complete also as a uniform space (see Proposition 8.3.5 of
[
5
]
), Theorem 8.3.20 of
[
5
]
implies that
x_{i}\to x
for some
x\in X
. Clearly,
\varphi(x)=F
. Thus
\varphi(X)
is closed in
C_{p}(K)
.∎

Proposition 2.4

Let
X
be a normal space, and let
M\subseteq C_{p}(C_{p}(X))
.
Then there exists
L\subseteq X
such that
|L|\leq|M|
and
\overline{M}
is closely embedded in
C_{p}(C_{p}(\overline{L}))
.

Proof 3

The case of finite
M
is trivial. We assume that
M
is infinite.
For
Q\subseteq X
and
h\in C_{p}(X)
, we put
\pi_{Q}(h):=h{\restriction}_{Q}
and
C_{p}(Q|X):=\pi_{Q}(C_{p}(X))\subseteq C_{p}(Q)
. By Proposition 0.4.1 of
[
1
]
, the mapping
\pi_{Q}:C_{p}(X)\to C_{p}(Q|X)
is continuous and it is open provided that
Q
is closed in
X
. For
f\in M
, by Factorization Lemma 0.2.3 of
[
1
]
, there are a countable
L_{f}\subseteq X
and a continuous function
g_{f}:C_{p}(L_{f}|X)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f=g_{f}\circ\pi_{L_{f}}
. Let
L=\bigcup_{f\in M}L_{f}
. Clearly,
|L|\leq|M|
.

We will prove that
\overline{M}
is closely embedded in
C_{p}(C_{p}(\overline{L}))
. Since
X
is a normal space, then, by the Tietze–Urysohn theorem,
C_{p}(\overline{L}|X)=C_{p}(\overline{L})
. As the mapping
\pi_{\overline{L}}:C_{p}(X)\to C_{p}(\overline{L})
is continuous and open, the mapping
\pi_{\overline{L}}
is a quotient mapping. Therefore, by Proposition 0.4.8 of
[
1
]
, the mapping

\pi_{\overline{L}}^{\#}:C_{p}(C_{p}(\overline{L}))\to C_{p}(C_{p}(X)),\quad r\mapsto r\circ\pi_{\overline{L}},

is a closed embedding of
C_{p}(C_{p}(\overline{L}))
into
C_{p}(C_{p}(X))
. It remains to prove that
M\subseteq C_{p}(C_{p}(\overline{L}))
. Let
f\in M
. Put

\tilde{f}:C_{p}(\overline{L})\to\mathbb{R},\ r\mapsto g_{f}(r{\restriction}_{L_{f}}).

Since
L_{f}\subseteq\overline{L}
, for every
r\in C_{p}(X)
, we have

\pi_{\overline{L}}^{\#}(\tilde{f})(r)=\tilde{f}\circ\pi_{\overline{L}}(r)=g_{f}\big(r\circ\pi_{\overline{L}}{\restriction}_{L_{f}}\big)=g_{f}\big(r{\restriction}_{L_{f}}\big)=g_{f}\circ\pi_{L_{f}}(r)=f(r).

Thus
\pi_{\overline{L}}^{\#}(\tilde{f})=f
.∎

Theorem 3.1

For a Tychonoff space

, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

is closely embedded into
\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{p}(K_{\alpha})
for some family
\{K_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of compact spaces;

(ii)

is closely embedded into

, where

is the topological sum of a family of compact spaces;

(iii)

X
is closely embedded into
C_{p}(Y)
for some locally compact paracompact space

;

(iv)

X
is closely embedded into
C_{p}(Y)
for some locally compact space
Y
;

(v)

X
is closely embedded into
C_{p}(Y)
for some
k
-space
Y
;

(vi)

X
is closely embedded into
C_{p}(Y)
for some
k_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
Y
;

(vii)

the diagonal mapping

\psi:=\bigtriangleup_{K\in\mathcal{K}\big(C_{p}(X)\big)}\phi_{K}:X\to\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}\big(C_{p}(X)\big)}C_{p}(K)

is an embedding with closed image, where
\phi_{K}:X\to C_{p}(K)
is defined by
\phi_{K}(x)(s):=s(x)

(s\in K)
.

Proof 4

The implications (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(v)
\Rightarrow
(vi) and (vii)
\Rightarrow
(i) are evident.

(vi)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let

be a

-space for which

is closely embedded into
C_{p}(Y)
. Observe that, by definition, a space is a

-space if, and only if, it is strongly functionally generated by the family of all its compact subsets. It follows from Theorem 2(1) of
[
4
]
that
C_{p}(Y)
is closely embedded in
\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}(Y)}C_{p}(K)
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(vii) Let
T
be an embedding of
X
into
\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{p}(D_{\alpha})
for some family
\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of compact spaces. For every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, let
\pi_{\alpha}
denote the
\alpha

\pi_{\alpha}\circ T:X\to C_{p}(D_{\alpha})
is continuous. Denote by
f_{\alpha}
the restriction onto

of the adjoint mapping

\big(\pi_{\alpha}\circ T\big)^{\#}:C_{p}\big(C_{p}(D_{\alpha})\big)\to C_{p}(X).

Set
Q_{\alpha}:=f_{\alpha}(D_{\alpha})
. Then
Q_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}\big(C_{p}(X)\big)
and

is a quotient map. Therefore, by Proposition 0.4.6(2) and Corollary 0.4.8(2) of
[
1
]
, the adjoint mapping

is an embedding with closed image. Whence, by Proposition 2.3.2 and Corollary 2.3.4 of
[
5
]
, the product mapping

is also an embedding with closed image. Let

\xi:=\bigtriangleup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\;\phi_{Q_{\alpha}}:X\to\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{p}(Q_{\alpha})

be the diagonal map. For every

Therefore

. Since

Set

, and let

be the diagonal map. Then
\psi=\xi{\bigtriangleup}\chi
. Therefore, by Theorem 2.3.20 of
[

,
\psi
is also an embedding. Applying Lemma

Proposition 4.6

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space, and let
z\in\beta X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

z\in\kappa X
;

(ii)

for each compact space
K
, any continuous mapping
\phi:X\to C_{p}(K)
can be extended to a continuous mapping
\hat{\phi}:X\cup\{z\}\to C_{p}(K)
;

(iii)

z\in\upsilon X
and
\delta_{z}
is
k
-continuous on
C_{p}(X)
;

(iv)

z\in\upsilon X
and
\delta_{z}
is
k
-continuous on
C_{p}^{b}(X)
;

(v)

z\in\upsilon X
and
\delta_{z}
is
k
-continuous on every uniformly bounded compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
.

Proof 18

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follows from Corollary
4.4
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Since
\kappa X
is a
\kappa
-space, Theorem
3.1
implies that for some family
\{K_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of compact spaces, there is an embedding

T:\kappa X\to P:=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{p}(K_{\alpha})

with closed image. For simplicity of notations we set
Y:=X\cup\{z\}
. For every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, let

i_{\alpha}:\pi_{\alpha}\circ T{\restriction}_{X},\;\;\mbox{ where }\;\pi_{\alpha}:P\to C_{p}(K_{\alpha})\;\mbox{ is the $\alpha$-th projection}.

By (ii),
i_{\alpha}
is extended to a continuous mapping
\hat{i}_{\alpha}:Y\to C_{p}(K_{\alpha})
. Consider the continuous mapping
\hat{i}:Y\to P
defined by

\hat{i}={\bigtriangleup}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\,\hat{i}_{\alpha}:Y\to P,\quad\hat{i}(y):=\big(\hat{i}_{\alpha}(y)\big)_{\alpha}.

Let
\kappa:\kappa X\to\beta X
and
R:Y\to\beta X
be embeddings. Then also
T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa:\kappa X\to P\times\beta X
,
T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa(x):=\big(T(x),\kappa(x)\big)
, is an embedding. Moreover, since
T
is an embedding with closed image, Lemma
2.2
guarantees that the embedding
T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa
also has closed image.

Since
T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa\big(\kappa X\big)
is closed in
P\times\beta X
,
X
is dense in
Y
, and the mappings
T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa
and
\hat{i}{\bigtriangleup}R
coincide on
X
, we obtain
\hat{i}{\bigtriangleup}R(Y)\subseteq\big(T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa\big)\big(\kappa X\big)
. Therefore there is some
y\in\kappa X
such that
\hat{i}{\bigtriangleup}R(z)=\big(T{\bigtriangleup}\kappa\big)(y)
. In particular,
R(z)=\kappa(y)
. Since
R
and
\kappa
are bijective, it follows that
z=y\in\kappa X
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Since
\upsilon X
is a
\kappa
-space by (iii) of Proposition
3.3
, the inclusion
z\in\upsilon X
follows from the definition of
\kappa X
. To show that
\delta_{z}
is
k
-continuous on
C_{p}(X)
, let
K
be a compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
. Define a mapping
\psi:X\to C_{p}(K)
by
\psi(x)(f):=f(x)
for
f\in K
. Then
\psi
is continuous, and hence, by (ii),
\psi
is extended to a continuous mapping
\hat{\psi}:X\cup\{z\}\to C_{p}(K)
. Observe that
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{K}=\hat{\psi}(z)
. Indeed, if
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq X
is a net converging to
z
and
f\in K
, then

\delta_{z}(f)=\bar{f}(z)=\lim_{i}f(x_{i})=\lim_{i}\psi(x_{i})(f)=\hat{\psi}(z)(f)

and hence
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{K}=\hat{\psi}(z)
. Thus the mapping
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{K}
is continuous.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Let
K
be a compact space, and let
\phi:X\to C_{p}(K)
be a continuous mapping. Consider the mapping
\psi:K\to C_{p}(X)
defined by
\psi(s)(x):=\phi(x)(s)
, where
s\in K
and
x\in X
. Then
\psi
is continuous as the restriction mapping onto
K
of the adjoint map
C_{p}(C_{p}(K))\to C_{p}(X)
. Then
\psi(K)
is a compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
, and hence, by (iii), the function
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{\psi(K)}
is continuous. Therefore the mapping
\hat{\phi}:X\cup\{z\}\to C_{p}(K)
defined by

\hat{\phi}(x)(s):=\delta_{x}(\psi(s))\quad(x\in X\cup\{z\}\mbox{ and }s\in K)

is well-defined because if
x\in X
and
s\in K
, we have

\hat{\phi}(x)(s)=\delta_{x}\big(\psi(s)\big)=\psi(s)(x)=\phi(x)(s).

In particular,
\hat{\phi}
extends
\phi
and hence
\hat{\phi}{\restriction}_{X}
is continuous. It remains to show that
\hat{\phi}
is continuous at
z
. To this end, let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq X
be a net converging to
z
. Then for every
s\in K
, we have (recall that
\overline{\psi(s)}
denotes the unique extension of
\psi(s)
onto
\upsilon X
)

\hat{\phi}(x_{i})(s)=\delta_{x_{i}}\big(\psi(s)\big)=\delta_{x_{i}}\big(\overline{\psi(s)}\big)=\overline{\psi(s)}(x_{i})\to\overline{\psi(s)}(z)=\delta_{z}\big(\psi(s)\big)=\hat{\phi}(z)(s).

Thus
\hat{\phi}
is continuous also at
z
, and hence
\hat{\phi}
is a desired extension of
\phi
.

The implications (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(v) are clear.

(v)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Let
K
be a compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
. It follows from Proposition
4.5
that
\delta_{z}
is bounded on
K
. Choose
n\in\omega
such that

|\delta_{z}(f)|=|\bar{f}(z)|\leq n\quad\mbox{ for every }\;f\in K.

Then for every
g\in K
, we obtain

\delta_{z}(g)=\bar{g}(z)=(Q_{n}\circ\bar{g})(z)=\overline{Q_{n}\circ g}(z)=\delta_{z}(Q_{n}\circ g).

(4.2)

To prove that
\delta_{z}
is continuous on
K
, we observe first that the continuity of
Q_{n}
implies that
Q_{n}(K)
is a uniformly bounded compact subset of
C_{p}(X)
. By assumption
\delta_{z}
is continuous on
Q_{n}(K)
. Since, by (
4.2
),
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{K}=\delta_{z}\circ Q_{n}{\restriction}_{K}
it follows that
\delta_{z}{\restriction}_{K}
is continuous.∎

Proposition 4.7

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space, and let
z\in\beta X
. If
\delta_{z}
is bounded on each convergent sequence in
C_{p}^{b}(X)
, then
z\in\upsilon X
.

Proof 19

Suppose for a contradiction that
z\not\in\upsilon X
. Then, by Theorem 3.11.10 of
[
5
]
, there is a continuous function
h:\beta X\to[0,1]
such that
h(z)=0
and
h(x)>0
for every
x\in\upsilon X
. Choose a null-sequence
0<a_{n+1}<a_{n}\leq 1
such that
the set
A_{n}:=\big\{x\in\beta X:h(x)\in[a_{n+1},a_{n}]\big\}\not=\emptyset
for every
n\in\omega
. For every
n>0
, set
B_{n}:=\big\{x\in\beta X:a_{n}\leq h(x)\big\}
and choose a continuous function
g_{n}:\beta X\to[a_{n},\tfrac{1}{a_{n}}]
such that

g_{n}{\restriction}_{A_{n+1}\cup\{z\}}=a_{n}\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;g_{n}{\restriction}_{B_{n}}=\tfrac{1}{a_{n}}.

Finally, for every
n\in\omega
, we define
f_{n}:=\tfrac{1}{g_{n}}{\restriction}_{X}\in C^{b}(X)
. The choice of
h
implies that for every
x\in X
, there is
n_{x}\in\omega
such that
x\in B_{n}
for every
n\geq n_{x}
. Therefore
f_{n}\to\mathbf{0}
in
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Whence
S:=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}\cup\{\mathbf{0}\}
is a null-sequence in
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. However,
\delta_{z}(f_{n})=\tfrac{1}{a_{n}}\to\infty
, and hence
\delta_{z}
is unbounded on
S
. This contradiction shows that
z\in\upsilon X
.∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
.

Proof 20
(Proof of Theorem
1.2
)

(i) This is Proposition
4.5
.

(ii) follows from Proposition
4.6
.∎

Proof 21
(Proof of Theorem
1.3
)

(i) Let
z\in\beta X
. If
z\in\upsilon X
, then, by (i) of Theorem
1.2
,
\delta_{z}
is bounded on all compact subsets of
C_{p}(X)
and hence also on all compact subsets of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Conversely, if
\delta_{z}
is bounded on all compact subsets of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
, then, by Proposition
4.7
,
z\in\upsilon X
.

(ii) Let
z\in\kappa X
. Then, by (ii) of Theorem
1.2
,
\delta_{z}
is continuous on all compact subsets of
C_{p}(X)
and hence also on all compact subsets of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Conversely, assume that
z\in\beta X
is such that
\delta_{z}
is continuous on all compact subsets of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Then, by Proposition
4.7
,
z\in\upsilon X
. Thus, by Proposition
4.6
,
z\in\kappa X
.∎

Proof 22
(Proof of Theorem
1.4
)

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Assume that
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
and
C_{p}^{b}(X)
have the same compact subsets. To show that
Y\subseteq\kappa X
, fix an arbitrary
y\in Y
. Then
\delta_{y}
is continuous on
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
. Therefore
\delta_{y}
is continuous on all compact subsets of
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
and hence of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Hence, by Theorem
1.2
,
y\in\kappa X
. Thus
Y\subseteq\kappa X
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Assume that
Y\subseteq\kappa X
. Since the topology of
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
is defined by the family
\{\delta_{y}:y\in Y\}
,
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
and
C_{p}^{b}(X)
have the same compact subsets by Proposition
4.6
.

It is useful to give a totally different proof of the sufficiency. Since the restriction map
I:C_{p}^{b}(Y)\to C_{p}^{b}(X)
is continuous, we always have the inclusion
\mathcal{K}\big(C_{p}^{b}(Y)\big)\subseteq\mathcal{K}\big(C_{p}^{b}(X)\big)
. To prove the inverse inclusion, let
K
be a compact subset of
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Since
Y\subseteq\kappa X
, Theorem
1.2
implies that each
\delta_{y}
is continuous and hence bounded on
K
. Therefore
K
is a precompact subset of
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
. It remains to prove that
K
is complete in
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
. To this end, let
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
be a Cauchy net in
K
with respect to
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
. As
I
is continuous,
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
is a Cauchy net in
K
with respect to
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Since
K
is compact in
C_{p}^{b}(X)
, there is
f\in K
such that
f_{\alpha}\to f
in
C_{p}^{b}(X)
. Since for every
y\in Y
,
\delta_{y}
is continuous on
K
, we have
f_{\alpha}(y)\to f(y)
. But this means that
f_{\alpha}\to f\in K
also in
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
. Thus
K
is complete and hence compact in
C_{p}^{b}(Y)
.∎

Accelerated Mirror Descent Method through Variable and Operator Splitting Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Long Chen, Hao Luo, Jingrong Wei, Zeyi Xu, Yuan Yao

Abstract
Mirror descent uses the mirror function to encode geometry and constraints, improving convergence while preserving feasibility. Accelerated Mirror Descent Methods (Acc-MD) are derived from a discretization of an accelerated mirror ODE system using a variable–operator splitting framework. A geometric assumption, termed the Generalized Cauchy-Schwarz (GCS) condition, is introduced to quantify the compatibility between the objective and the mirror geometry, under which the first accelerated linear convergence for Acc-MD on a broad class of problems is established. Numerical experiments on smooth and composite optimization tasks demonstrate that Acc-MD exhibits superior stability and competitive acceleration compared to existing variants.

Introduction

Consider the unconstrained convex optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x),

where
f
is convex and differentiable. Mirror descent
[
25
]
updates as

x_{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x\rangle+\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}D_{\phi}(x,x_{k})\right\},

(1)

where
\alpha_{k}>0
is the step size,
\phi
is a smooth, strictly convex
mirror function
of Legendre type, and
D_{\phi}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the associated Bregman divergence. In
[
26
]
, Nesterov proposed an accelerated version that later inspired the mirror–prox method
[
24
]
.

The mirror function encodes both geometry and constraints, with applications in imaging, phase transition, signal processing, and machine learning
[
27
,
6
,
15
,
21
,
2
,
19
]
.
One use of mirror descent is as a nonlinear preconditioner. Relative smoothness is defined by
D_{f}(x,y)\leq LD_{\phi}(x,y)
. The Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f
is
L_{f}
. Mirror descent improves convergence when
L\ll L_{f}
, especially if
L_{f}=\infty
(i.e., when
f
is not smooth in the Euclidean sense).

Another use of mirror descent is to impose constraints through the mirror function. For example, the entropic mirror descent method uses Shannon’s entropy
\phi(x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\log x_{i}
, which ensures that
x_{i}
stay in the unit simplex. In
[
5
]
, mirror descent is seen as a nonlinear projected subgradient-type method for constrained optimization.

While acceleration is well-understood in Euclidean spaces, extending it to general Bregman geometries remains challenging due to the lack of a natural coupling between the primal and dual metrics. Recently, acceleration has been studied by viewing optimization algorithms as discretizations of continuous-time ODE dynamics. Krichene, Bayen, and Bartlett
[
18
]
extended the ODE framework for Nesterov acceleration
[
30
]
to accelerated mirror descent, showing that suitable discretizations yield a
\mathcal{O}(L_{f}/k^{2})
rate. Wibisono, Wilson, and Jordan
[
33
]
derived a Bregman Lagrangian that captures accelerated flows. Yuan and Zhang
[
35
]
developed high-resolution ODEs and recovered the optimal
\mathcal{O}(L_{f}/k^{2})
rate for accelerated mirror descent methods in
[
26
]
. Also starting from Nesterov acceleration, Yu et al.
[
34
]
extended the method to work under generalized smoothness
[
20
]
and established the
O(1/k^{2})
rate. Other variants are in
[
1
,
14
]
.

However, these analyses
[
18
,
26
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
1
,
14
]
do not use relative smoothness,
D_{f}(x,y)\leq LD_{\phi}(x,y)
. Thus, convergence guarantees can be loose outside the Euclidean setting, especially when
L
is small but
L_{f}
is large or infinite. Birnbaum, Devanur, and Xiao
[
8
]
showed that mirror descent achieves a
\mathcal{O}(L/k)
rate under relative smoothness. For composite problems
F(x)=f(x)+g(x)
, where
g
is convex but possibly non-smooth, the Bregman Proximal Gradient (BPG) method
[
31
]
achieves a
\mathcal{O}(L/k)
rate; see also
[
3
,
22
,
36
]
. In
[
22
]
, Lu, Freund, and Nesterov introduced relative strong convexity,
\mu D_{\phi}(x,y)\leq D_{f}(x,y)
, and proved a linear convergence rate of
1-\mu/L
for mirror descent. Later, Hanzely and Richtárik
[
17
]
extended the linear convergence to stochastic mirror descent .

Without extra structure, mirror descent methods are limited to
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
complexity
[
13
]
. Extra conditions are needed for acceleration under relative smoothness. Under the gradient dominated condition and the
\theta
-uniform condition, the Bregman gradient method achieved linear convergence
[
4
]
. The Accelerated Bregman Proximal Gradient (ABPG) method
[
16
]
obtains a rate
\mathcal{O}(L/k^{\gamma})
based on the triangle scaling exponent (TSE)
\gamma\leq 2
. Full acceleration
\mathcal{O}(L/k^{2})
requires
\gamma=2
. Notably, no accelerated linear rate is known under relative strong convexity.

In this work, we develop accelerated mirror descent methods. Using the variable and operator splitting (VOS) framework from
[
10
]
, we propose a new accelerated mirror descent (Acc-MD) flow:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{\prime}&=y-x,\\
\left(\nabla\phi(y)\right)^{\prime}&=-\mu^{-1}\nabla f(x)+\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(y),\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

with
x(0)=x_{0}
and
y(0)=y_{0}
. The variable is split into
x
and
y
. At equilibrium,
y^{\star}=x^{\star}
and
\nabla f(x^{\star})=0
. The variable
y
allows operator splitting
\nabla f(x)
and
\nabla\phi(y)
.

From discretizations of (
2
), we design Acc-MD methods:
algorithm

1
and
algorithm

2
. We introduce a new assumption (
A2
), a generalized Cauchy-Schwarz (GCS) inequality with constant
C_{f,\phi}
. Based on GCS, We establish the first accelerated linear convergence result for mirror descent, with rate
(1+\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}})^{-1}
. We show cases where GCS (
A2
) holds and
C_{f,\phi}
is explicit.

As seen in Figure
1
(Section
4.4
), GCS covers important geometries, such as Example
9
, where ABPG
[
16
]
based on TSE fails to accelerate. We do not claim GCS is more general than TSE, or that Acc-MD is always better than ABPG. Instead, they address
different structure regimes
. See Section
4.4
for discussion.

For the convex case
\mu=0
, we use a perturbation-homotopy argument
[
10
]
to derive the optimal accelerated rate
\mathcal{O}(C_{f,\phi}/k^{2})
under GCS. For composite problems
\min_{x}f(x)+g(x)
, our method leads to a Bregman proximal gradient variant that handles non-smooth
g
using variable splitting in the gradient term.

We test the Acc-MD methods on smooth, non-smooth, and constrained convex problems. Results show benefits over existing mirror descent methods.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
gives preliminaries. Section
3

4

5
and Section
6
extend the methods to convex and composite cases. Section
7
reports numerical results.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present useful results in convex optimization and duality theory; see standard textbooks, e.g.,
[
7
,
28
]
, for details. Let
V
be a normed vector space and
V^{*}
be its dual space. The duality pairing between
V^{\ast}
and
V
is denoted by
\langle\chi,x\rangle
for
\chi\in V^{\ast}
and
x\in V
. When
V
is a Hilbert space, the Riesz representation theorem allows us to identify
V
with
V^{*}
, in which case the duality pairing coincides with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

For a continuously differentiable function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(V)
, the
Bregman divergence
is

D_{f}(y,x):=f(y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle.

For
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(V)
,
f
is convex if and only if
D_{f}(y,x)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in V
. If
f
is strictly convex, then
D_{f}(y,x)=0
if and only if
x=y
. In general, the Bregman divergence is not symmetric. Its symmetrization is

D_{f}(y,x)+D_{f}(x,y)=\langle\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle.

A key tool in the convergence analysis is the three-point identity of Bregman divergence
[
9
]
:

\langle\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x),y-z\rangle=D_{f}(y,x)+D_{f}(z,y)-D_{f}(z,x).

(3)

We fix a smooth, strictly convex function of Legendre type
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(V)
, known as the
mirror function
. Let
\phi^{*}
be the convex conjugate of
\phi
. The mappings between primal and dual variables are

\chi=\nabla\phi(x)\Leftrightarrow x=\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi),\qquad\eta=\nabla\phi(y)\Leftrightarrow y=\nabla\phi^{*}(\eta),

where
(x,y)
are primal variables and
(\chi,\eta)
are dual variables. The maps
\nabla\phi:V\to V^{*}
and
\nabla\phi^{*}:V^{*}\to V
are assumed to be one-to-one and easy to compute. We refer to
\phi
as the mirror function and
\nabla\phi
as the mirror map.

A symmetry relation connects the Bregman divergences of
\phi
and
\phi^{*}
:

D_{\phi}(x,y)=D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi),

(4)

with reversed arguments. Moreover, the gradient of the Bregman divergence with respect to the first variable satisfies

\nabla D_{f}(\cdot,x)=\nabla f(\cdot)-\nabla f(x),\qquad\nabla D_{\phi^{*}}(\cdot,\chi)=\nabla\phi^{*}(\cdot)-\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi).

(5)

Let
A
be a self-adjoint, positive definite operator on a Hilbert space
V
with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then
(x,y)_{A}:=(Ax,y)
defines a new inner product with norm
\|\cdot\|_{A}
. The dual norm is
\|\cdot\|_{A^{-1}}
. The convexity and smoothness constants of a differentiable function
f
relative to
\|\cdot\|_{A}
satisfy

\mu_{f}(A)\,\|x-y\|_{A}^{2}\leq\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle\leq L_{f}(A)\,\|x-y\|_{A}^{2}\quad\forall\,x,y\in V.

When
A
is the identity,
L_{f}
and
\mu_{f}
are standard constants in the Euclidean norm. A proper choice of
A
may reduce the condition number
\kappa_{A}(f):=L_{f}(A)/\mu_{f}(A)
; in this case
A
is a
preconditioner
. Similarly, the mirror function
\phi
acts as a
nonlinear preconditioner
. This is useful when
L_{f}
is large but the relative smoothness
L
is small.

For two symmetric operators
D
and
A
, we write
A<D
if
D-A
is positive definite and
A\leq D
if
D-A
is positive semi-definite. Note that
A\leq D
is equivalent to
\lambda_{\max}(D^{-1}A)\leq 1
for non-singular symmetric operators.

3
Mirror Descent Methods

Given an initial condition
x(0)=x_{0}
, the mirror descent flow is defined by the evolution of the mirror map:

\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\nabla\phi(x(t))=-\nabla f(x(t)).

(6)

The flow (
6
) can be expressed in the dual form
[
18
]
:

\chi^{\prime}=-\nabla f(x),

(7)

where
\chi=\nabla\phi(x)
represents the dual variable and
x=\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi)
. This formulation is closely related to the Bregman Inverse Scale Space dynamics studied in
[
27
]
.

Discretizing (
6
) or (
7
) using the explicit Euler method with step size
\alpha_{k}>0
yields the iteration

\nabla\phi(x_{k+1})-\nabla\phi(x_{k})=-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(8)

By the definition of the Bregman divergence, this update is equivalent to the standard mirror descent step:

x_{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in V}\left\{\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x\rangle+\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}D_{\phi}(x,x_{k})\right\}.

The following three-point identity simplifies the convergence analysis by connecting the geometries of the mirror function
\phi
and the objective
f
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by the mirror descent method (
8
). Then

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})-D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})+D_{\phi}(x_{k+1},x_{k})

(9)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\Big[-D_{f}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})-D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})+D_{f}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\Big].

Proof.

Using the three-point identity (
3
), the optimality condition
\nabla f(x^{\star})=0
, and the update (
8
), we have:

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})-D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})+D_{\phi}(x_{k+1},x_{k})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi(x_{k+1})-\nabla\phi(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x^{\star}),x_{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\left[-D_{f}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})-D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})+D_{f}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\right].

To establish linear convergence without assuming Euclidean Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f
, we utilize the notions of relative smoothness and relative strong convexity
[
22
]
.

Assumption 1
.

The function
f
is
L
-relatively smooth and
\mu
-relatively convex with respect to
\phi
if there exist
\mu\geq 0
and
L\geq\mu
such that

\mu D_{\phi}(x,y)\leq D_{f}(x,y)\leq LD_{\phi}(x,y)\quad\forall\,x,y\in V.

(A1)

Equivalently,
1
holds if and only if
L\phi-f
and
f-\mu\phi
are convex.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
f
satisfies
1
with
L\geq\mu>0
. Let
\{x_{k}\}
be the sequence from (
8
). For any step size
\alpha_{k}\leq 1/L
, the Bregman divergence satisfies the following decay:

D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})-D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})\geq\alpha_{k}\big[D_{f}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})+D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})\big].

(10)

Specifically, if
\alpha_{k}=1/L
, mirror descent achieves a linear convergence rate:

D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{k}D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{0}).

(11)

Proof.

Combining (
9
) with the upper bounds in (
A1
), we obtain:

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})-D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k}\big[D_{f}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})+D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})\big]+\left[\alpha_{k}D_{f}(x_{k+1},x_{k})-D_{\phi}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k}\big[D_{f}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})+D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})\big].

Applying the relative strong convexity
D_{f}(x^{\star},x_{k})\geq\mu D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k})
and setting
\alpha_{k}=1/L
yields:

D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})\leq(1-\mu/L)D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k}).

Iterating the inequality completes the proof.

4
Accelerated Mirror Descent Methods

We use the variable and operator splitting (VOS) framework
[
10
]
to develop accelerated mirror descent methods. Throughout this section, we assume
f
satisfies
1
with
L\geq\mu>0
.

4.1
Flow and stability

By introducing dual variables
\chi=\nabla\phi(x)
and
\eta=\nabla\phi(y)
, we write the accelerated mirror descent flow (
2
) as

x^{\prime}=y-x,\quad\eta^{\prime}=-\mu^{-1}\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\eta,

(12)

where the
\mu
-shifted function is defined as

f_{-\mu}:=f-\mu\phi.

Under
1
,
f_{-\mu}
is convex. We define a Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{E}(x,\eta):=D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,x^{\star})+\mu D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi^{\star}),

(13)

where
x^{\star}
is a minimizer of
f
and
\chi^{\star}=\nabla\phi(x^{\star})
. This energy couples primal and dual Bregman divergences to capture the geometry of the mirror map. We show exponential stability by verifying a strong Lyapunov condition
[
11
]
.

Strong Lyapunov condition for linear cases

We illustrate the flow with a linear system
Ax^{*}=b
. Let
A
and
B
be two symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices satisfying
A\geq\mu B^{-1}
. Define

\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)=A_{-\mu}x-b:=(A-\mu B^{-1})x-b,\quad\nabla\phi(x)=B^{-1}x.

The flow simplifies to

x^{\prime}=y-x,\qquad y^{\prime}=-\mu^{-1}BA_{-\mu}x-y=-\mu^{-1}B(Ax-b)+x-y.

(14)

Let
\boldsymbol{z}=(x,y)^{\top}
and introduce the matrices

\mathcal{D}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{-\mu}&0\\
0&\mu B^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{G}=\begin{pmatrix}-I&I\\
-\mu^{-1}BA_{-\mu}&-I\end{pmatrix}.

The product
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}
is

\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}=\begin{pmatrix}-A_{-\mu}&A_{-\mu}\\
-A_{-\mu}&-\mu B^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

We write the Lyapunov function and the flow as

\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2},\quad\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}),

where
\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}=(x^{\star},x^{\star})^{\top}
is the equilibrium point. Then

\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z})\cdot\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z})=(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star})^{\top}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}).

Since
x^{\top}Mx=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}(M+M^{\top})x
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}+(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G})^{\top}\bigr)=-\mathcal{D}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z})\cdot\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z})=2\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}).

Strong Lyapunov condition of nonlinear cases

We now consider the nonlinear case. Due to the non-symmetry of the Bregman divergence, the factor
2
becomes
1
.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
f
is
\mu
-relative convex with respect to
\phi
with
\mu>0
. Let
\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)
be defined by (
13
), and define the vector field
\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)=(y-x,\,-\mu^{-1}\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\eta)
, where the dual variables satisfy
\chi=\nabla\phi(x)
and
\eta=\nabla\phi(y)
. Then the following strong Lyapunov property holds:

-\nabla\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)\cdot\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)=\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)+D_{f_{-\mu}}(x^{\star},x)+\mu D_{\phi}(y,x^{\star}).

(15)

Consequently, any solution
(x(t),y(t))
of the flow (
2
) satisfies the exponential decay bound

\mathcal{E}(x(t),\eta(t))\leq e^{-t}\mathcal{E}(x(0),\eta(0)).

(16)

Proof.

Using (
5
), we compute the gradients of the Lyapunov function:

\partial_{x}\mathcal{E}=\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),\quad\partial_{\eta}\mathcal{E}=\mu\left(\nabla\phi^{*}(\eta)-\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi^{\star})\right)=\mu(y-x^{\star}).

By direct calculation and the symmetry relation (
4
), we have

\displaystyle-\nabla\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)\cdot\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),x-y\rangle

\displaystyle+\mu\left\langle y-x^{\star},\,\mu^{-1}(\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}))+\eta-\chi^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle+\mu\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\eta)-\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi^{\star}),\eta-\chi^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle=D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,x^{\star})+D_{f_{-\mu}}(x^{\star},x)+\mu D_{\phi^{*}}(\chi^{\star},\eta)+\mu D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi^{\star})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)+D_{f_{-\mu}}(x^{\star},x)+\mu D_{\phi}(y,x^{\star}).

Since
D_{f_{-\mu}}(x^{\star},x)\geq 0
and
D_{\phi}(y,x^{\star})\geq 0
follow from the convexity of
f_{-\mu}
and
\phi
, we obtain the inequality

\nabla\mathcal{E}(x,\eta)\cdot\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)\leq-\mathcal{E}(x,\eta).

The exponential decay (
16
) follows from Grönwall’s inequality.

As a result, we have
x(t),y(t)\in B(x^{\star},R)
for all
t\geq 0
, where
B(x^{\star},R)
is the ball of radius
R
centered at
x^{\star}
.

4.2
Accelerated Mirror Descent Methods

We propose an accelerated mirror descent (Acc-MD) method via an implicit–explicit discretization of (
2
):

\displaystyle\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\alpha}=y_{k}-x_{k+1},

(17a)

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla\phi(y_{k+1})-\nabla\phi(y_{k})}{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{\mu}\bigl(2\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k})\bigr)-\nabla\phi(y_{k+1}).

(17b)

In (
17a
),
y
is discretized explicitly as
y_{k}
to update
x_{k+1}
, whereas in (
17b
) the term
-\nabla\phi(y)
is implicit. The extrapolation

\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)\;\approx\;2\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k})

acts as an accelerated over-relaxation (AOR), symmetrizing the error equation. This mechanism was used in
[
32
]
to develop the first globally convergent accelerated Heavy-Ball method (AOR-HB).

The VOS framework
[
10
]
is not naturally built for mirror geometry. To extend it beyond the Euclidean setting, we introduce a new geometric assumption.

Assumption 2
.

For
f,\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(V)
, there exists a constant
C_{f,\phi}>0
such that the following generalized Cauchy–Schwarz (GCS) inequality holds:

\bigl|\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),\,y-\hat{y}\rangle\bigr|\leq 2\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}}\,D_{f_{-\mu}}^{1/2}(x,\hat{x})\,D_{\phi}^{1/2}(\hat{y},y),\quad\forall x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y}\in V.

(A2)

Briefly,
1
controls distances, while
2
imposes an angle condition. Using dual variables
\zeta=\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)
and the identity
D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})=D_{f_{-\mu}^{*}}(\hat{\zeta},\zeta)
, we rewrite the inequality as

\frac{\bigl|\langle\zeta-\hat{\zeta},\,y-\hat{y}\rangle\bigr|}{\sqrt{2}\,D_{f_{-\mu}^{*}}^{1/2}(\hat{\zeta},\zeta)\,\sqrt{2}\,D_{\phi}^{1/2}(\hat{y},y)}\leq\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}},\qquad\forall x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y}\in V.

(18)

In the quadratic case,
\sqrt{2}\,D_{\phi}^{1/2}(\hat{y},y)=\|\hat{y}-y\|_{\nabla^{2}\phi}
. Inequality (
18
) therefore measures the angle between vectors under different geometries.

We summarize (
17
) in
algorithm

1
and call it the
forward Acc-MD
method.

Algorithm 1

Forward Accelerated Mirror Descent (Acc-MD)

1:

Input:

x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, parameters
\mu>0
,
C_{f,\phi}>0

2:

Set
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}

3:

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

4:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\bigl(x_{k}+\alpha y_{k}\bigr)

5:

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha)\phi(y)-\left\langle-\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\bigl(2\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k})\bigr)+\nabla\phi(y_{k}),\,y\right\rangle

6:

end

for

We also consider an alternative discretization:

\displaystyle\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\alpha}=2y_{k+1}-y_{k}-x_{k+1},

(19a)

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla\phi(y_{k+1})-\nabla\phi(y_{k})}{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla f(x_{k})+\nabla\phi(x_{k})-\nabla\phi(y_{k+1}).

(19b)

In this scheme, the gradient term
\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)
is explicit in (
19b
), while
y
in (
19a
) uses AOR. Since the update computes
y_{k+1}
then
x_{k+1}
, we call this the
backward Acc-MD
method; see
algorithm

2
.

Algorithm 2

Backward Accelerated Mirror Descent

1:

Input:

x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, parameters
\mu>0
,
C_{f,\phi}>0

2:

Set
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}

3:

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

4:

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha)\phi(y)-\left\langle-\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\nabla f(x_{k})+\alpha\nabla\phi(x_{k})+\nabla\phi(y_{k}),\,y\right\rangle

5:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\bigl[x_{k}+\alpha\bigl(2y_{k+1}-y_{k}\bigr)\bigr]

6:

end

for

One benefit of the forward Acc-MD scheme is that
x_{k+1}
is a convex combination of
x_{k}
and
y_{k}
. Thus, if
x_{k},y_{k}
stay in a convex set
K
, then
x_{k+1}
does too. This property is useful when
K
is a constraint set.

4.3
Convergence analysis

We establish the accelerated linear convergence result for the Acc-MD methods under relative strong convexity
\mu>0
. We first use linear cases to illustrate the proof.

Convergence analysis of linear cases

Introduce the matrices

\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_{-\mu}\\
A_{-\mu}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2},\quad\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{D}+\alpha\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}.

Spectral analysis shows that
\mathcal{D}+\alpha\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}>0
if

|\alpha|\leq\sqrt{\mu/\lambda_{\max}\!\bigl(BA_{-\mu}\bigr)}<\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{L-\mu}}.

We take
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/L}
, so that
\mathcal{D}\pm\alpha\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}
are symmetric positive definite (SPD). Since
\mathcal{E}
is quadratic, we have

\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k})=\langle\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}),\,\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}.

(20)

The Acc-MD method corrects the implicit Euler discretization. Let
\Delta\boldsymbol{z}_{k}=\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}
. We write

\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}=\alpha\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\alpha\mathcal{C}\,\Delta\boldsymbol{z}_{k}:=\alpha\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\alpha\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\mu^{-1}BA_{-\mu}&0\end{pmatrix}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}).

Expanding the cross term gives

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}),\,\mathcal{C}\Delta\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star},\,\Delta\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}\mathcal{C}}=\langle\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star},\,\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{k}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}.

Using the strong Lyapunov property
\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})\cdot\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})=-2\,\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k})=-\alpha\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal{D}-\alpha\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}+\alpha\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}}^{2}.

(21)

Dropping negative terms yields linear convergence:

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k})\leq-\alpha\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})\leq(1+\alpha)^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k}).

The AOR term associates the cross term with
\mathcal{A}^{\text{sym}}
, allowing the identity of squares to control the cross term.

Convergence analysis of nonlinear cases

For
(x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the cross term

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x,\hat{x},\hat{y},y)=D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})+\mu D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y)+\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),\,y-\hat{y}\rangle.

We define the modified Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x,y):=\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x,x^{\star},x^{\star},y)=\mathcal{E}(x,\eta(y))+\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),y-x^{\star}\rangle.

(22)

We now generalize identity (
21
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
(x_{k},y_{k})
be the sequence from the Acc-MD method (
17
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k},y_{k})={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})

(23)

\displaystyle-\alpha\,\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha}(x^{\star},x_{k+1},y_{k+1},x^{\star})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x_{k},x_{k+1},y_{k+1},y_{k}).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{z}=(x,\eta)
. We expand the difference
\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z})
at
\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k})={}

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}),\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\rangle-D_{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha\langle\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}),\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})\rangle-D_{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})

\displaystyle-\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),y_{k+1}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-\alpha\langle y_{k+1}-x^{\star},\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k})\rangle.

The difference from the implicit Euler discretization is symmetrized as it can be written as

\displaystyle-\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),y_{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x^{\star}),y_{k}-x^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle-\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k}),y_{k+1}-y_{k}\rangle.

Using identity (
15
) to expand
\langle\nabla\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}),\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})\rangle
and rearranging gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k},y_{k})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\mathcal{E}(x_{k+1},\eta_{k+1})-\alpha D_{f_{-\mu}}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})-\mu\alpha D_{\phi}(y_{k+1},x^{\star})

\displaystyle-D_{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{z}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1})-\alpha\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f_{-\mu}(x_{k}),y_{k+1}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-\alpha\,\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha}(x^{\star},x_{k+1},y_{k+1},x^{\star})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x_{k},x_{k+1},y_{k+1},y_{k}).

2
is essential both for defining the algorithm and for establishing its convergence.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
2
holds. Then for
|\alpha|\leq\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}
, we have
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x,\hat{x},\hat{y},y)\geq 0.

Proof.

By (
A2
) and the inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha|\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),y-\hat{y}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}}\,D_{f_{-\mu}}^{1/2}(x,\hat{x})D_{\phi}^{1/2}(\hat{y},y)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}/\mu}\left(D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})+\mu D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y)\right).

Therefore
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x,\hat{x},\hat{y},y)\geq 0
if
\alpha\leq\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}
.

As shown in
lemma

5
, when
\alpha\leq\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}
, the modified energy
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x,y)\geq 0
is a valid Lyapunov function.

Theorem 6
(Convergence of forward Acc-MD method).

Suppose
f
is
\mu
-relatively convex with respect to
\phi
with
\mu>0
and
2
holds with constant
C_{f,\phi}
. Let
(x_{k},y_{k})
be the sequence from scheme (
17
) with initial value
(x_{0},y_{0})
,
\eta_{k}=\nabla\phi(y_{k})
, and step size
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}
such that the following accelerated linear convergence holds:

D_{f_{-\mu}}(x_{k+1},x^{\star})+\mu D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta_{k+1},\chi^{\star})\leq C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}}\right)^{k},\quad k\geq 1.

(24)

Proof.

By
lemma

5
, for
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}
, we drop non-positive terms from identity (
23
) to obtain

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})\leq\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k},y_{k})\leq\left(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}}\right)^{k+1}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{0},y_{0}).

(25)

From the proof of
lemma

4
, we have

\alpha\mathcal{E}(x_{k+1},\eta_{k+1})\leq\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k},y_{k})-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})\leq\left(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\mu/C_{f,\phi}}}\right)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{0},y_{0}),

which yields (
24
) with
C_{0}=\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(x_{0},y_{0})\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}/\mu}
.

The convergence of the backward Acc-MD scheme (
19
) can be proved by changing the sign of the Lyapunov function,
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}\to\mathcal{E}^{-\alpha}
, and repeating the procedure.

If
C_{f,\phi}=cL
, then one exactly recovers the standard accelerated convergence rate like
1-\sqrt{1/(c\kappa)}
but the condition number is relative to the mirror function showing the preconditioning effect.

4.4
Discussion on the generalized Cauchy–Schwarz condition

Without extra structural conditions, mirror descent methods under
1
are limited to
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
convergence; see
[
13
]
. Consequently, accelerated mirror descent is generally impossible without assumptions beyond (
A1
). Additional structure, such as the triangle scaling exponent (TSE), the generalized Cauchy–Schwarz (GCS) condition (
A2
), or related variants
[
4
,
23
]
, is necessary for theoretical guarantees and algorithmic acceleration.

We first show that the GCS angle condition implies relative smoothness.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose (
A2
) holds with constant
C_{f,\phi}
. Then
f
is relatively smooth with respect to
\phi
with
L\leq 4C_{f,\phi}+\mu
.

Proof.

Set
y=x
and
\hat{y}=\hat{x}
in (
A2
). Using the property
\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),x-\hat{x}\rangle=D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})+D_{f_{-\mu}}(\hat{x},x)\geq D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})
, the GCS inequality becomes:

D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})\leq 2\sqrt{C_{f,\phi}}\,D_{f_{-\mu}}^{1/2}(x,\hat{x})D_{\phi}^{1/2}(\hat{x},x).

The desired result then follows through simple algebraic manipulation.

The constant
4C_{f,\phi}
in the global bound is a consequence of the potential asymmetry of the Bregman divergence. However, this can be sharpened locally for
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions. By observing that
\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),x-\hat{x}\rangle=2D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})+(D_{f_{-\mu}}(\hat{x},x)-D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x}))
, and noting that the difference
D_{f_{-\mu}}(\hat{x},x)-D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})=o(\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2})
vanishes faster than the divergence itself, we find that:

D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})\leq(C_{f,\phi}+o(1))D_{\phi}(\hat{x},x)\quad\text{as }x\to\hat{x}.

This implies that locally,
L\approx C_{f,\phi}+\mu
, which is consistent with the condition number recovered in the quadratic case in
theorem

8
.

We next verify
2
in several common settings to show it is a natural and practical condition.

Theorem 8
.

Let
A
be a self-adjoint, positive definite operator. Suppose
\phi
is
\mu_{\phi}
-strongly convex and
f_{-\mu}:=f-\mu\phi
is
L_{f_{-\mu}}
-smooth. Then

C_{f,\phi}\leq\inf_{\text{SPD }A}\frac{L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)}{\mu_{\phi}(A)}\leq\frac{L_{f_{-\mu}}}{\mu_{\phi}}.

Proof.

For any SPD
A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),\,y-\hat{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A^{-1/2}(\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})),\,A^{1/2}(y-\hat{y})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})\|_{A^{-1}}\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A}.

By the strong convexity of
\phi
in the
\|\cdot\|_{A}
norm and the co-convexity of
f_{-\mu}
in the
\|\cdot\|_{A^{-1}}
norm, we obtain

2D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y)\geq\mu_{\phi}(A)\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A}^{2},\quad 2D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})\geq\frac{1}{L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)}\|\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})\|_{A^{-1}}^{2}.

Combining these inequalities yields the first estimate. Setting
A=I
gives the second.

The smoothness of
f_{-\mu}=f-\mu\phi
is less restrictive than the smoothness of
f
. We decompose the objective as

f=f_{-\mu}+\mu\phi.

The non-Lipschitz part of
\nabla f
is absorbed into the mirror function, so the remaining term
\nabla f_{-\mu}
is smooth.

Example 9
(Log-linear model).

Consider the log-linear dual model in machine learning
[
12
]
:

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\log x_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\,x^{\top}(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top})x,\quad\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\log x_{i},

where
x=(x_{i})\in\Delta^{d-1}
is the dual variable on the
(d-1)
-dimensional simplex and
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The mirror function
\phi
is the Shannon entropy. Setting
\mu=1
, we have
f_{-\mu}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,x^{\top}(\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top})x
. Since
\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top}
is positive semidefinite, we regularize it by

A=\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top}+\varepsilon I,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Under this regularization, the smoothness constant is
L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)=1
. Collins et al.
[
12
, Lemma 7]
proved that
f
is
1
-relatively strongly convex and
(1+|A|_{\infty})
-relatively smooth with respect to
\phi
, where
|\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top}|_{\infty}
is the largest entry of
\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top}
. Thus,
1
holds with
\mu=1
and
L=|\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\top}|_{\infty}
.

The Bregman divergence
D_{\phi}(x,z)
is the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between two discrete probability measures:

D_{\phi}(x,z)={\rm KL}(x,z):=\sum_{i}x_{i}\log\left(\frac{x_{i}}{z_{i}}\right).

By Pinsker’s inequality,
\lVert y-\hat{y}\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{KL}(\hat{y},y)}
, which implies

\displaystyle\lVert y-\hat{y}\rVert^{2}_{A}\leq{}

\displaystyle(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert^{2}_{2}+\epsilon)\lVert y-\hat{y}\rVert^{2}_{2}\leq(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert^{2}_{2}+\epsilon)\lVert y-\hat{y}\rVert^{2}_{1}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 2(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert^{2}_{2}+\epsilon)\mathrm{KL}(\hat{y},y)=2(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert^{2}_{2}+\epsilon)D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y).

By
theorem

8
,
2
holds with constant

C_{f,\phi}\leq\frac{L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)}{\mu_{\phi}(A)}=\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\epsilon\to\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we take the limit
\epsilon\to 0+
to get a tighter upper bound.
\Box

Choosing a mirror function
\phi
that enforces feasibility allows the method to handle constrained optimization naturally.

Example 10
(Max-margin model).

We consider the max-margin dual model from
[
12
]
:

\min_{x\in\Delta^{d}}f(x)=b^{\top}x+\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax,

where
\Delta^{d}
is the probability simplex,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
A
is SPD. We use Shannon entropy
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\log x_{i}
as the mirror function to keep iterates
y_{k}
in
\Delta^{d}
. In the relatively convex case
\mu=0
, we have
L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)=L_{f}(A)=1
. Similar to (
26
),
\mu_{\phi}(A)=1/\|A\|_{2}
, so

C_{f,\phi}=\|A\|_{2}=\lambda_{\max}(A),

which the power method can estimate. We extend Acc-MD to the case
\mu=0
in
algorithm

4
.
\Box

In Examples
9
and
10
,
f_{-\mu}
is quadratic and admits a global metric
A
. For
\mathcal{C}^{2}
mirror functions, the Hessians
\nabla^{2}\phi(x)
and
\nabla^{2}\phi(y)
usually differ. We use continuity to bound this difference.

We assume
\phi
has a Lipschitz continuous Hessian in the ball
B(x^{\star},R/2)
with constant
L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}(R)
. Specifically,
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\theta}(B(x^{\star},R/2))
if the Hessian is Hölder continuous with parameter
\theta\in[0,1]
:

\|\nabla^{2}\phi(x)-\nabla^{2}\phi(y)\|_{*}\leq L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}\|x-y\|^{\theta},\quad\forall x,y\in V.

Theorem 11
.

Suppose
\phi
is
1
-strongly convex and
1
holds. If
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\theta}(B(x^{\star},R/2))
for
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, then
2
holds locally for
(x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y})\in B(x^{\star},R/2)
with

C_{f,\phi}\leq(L-\mu)\bigl(1+L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}(R)R^{\theta}\bigr),

where
L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}
is the Hölder constant of
\nabla^{2}\phi
on
B(x^{\star},R/2)
. If
\phi
is quadratic, then
L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}=0
and
C_{f,\phi}\leq L-\mu
.

Proof.

Fix
(x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y})\in B(x^{\star},R/2)
. By Taylor’s theorem, there exist points

\xi_{y}
on the segment
[\hat{y},y]
and
\xi_{x}
on
[\hat{x},x]
such that

2D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y)=\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A}^{2},\qquad\langle\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(\hat{x}),\,x-\hat{x}\rangle=\|x-\hat{x}\|_{H_{x}}^{2},

where

A:=\nabla^{2}\phi(\xi_{y}),\qquad H_{x}:=\nabla^{2}\phi(\xi_{x}).

Since
\phi
is
1
-strongly convex, we have
A\geq I
, and therefore

\|v\|\leq\|v\|_{A}
for all
v
.
The Hölder continuity of
\nabla^{2}\phi
on
B(x^{\star},R/2)
ensures

\|H_{x}-A\|\leq L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}\|\xi_{x}-\xi_{y}\|^{\theta}\leq L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}R^{\theta}.

It follows that

\|x-\hat{x}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}\leq\|x-\hat{x}\|_{A}^{2}+\|H_{x}-A\|\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2}\leq(1+L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}R^{\theta})\|x-\hat{x}\|_{A}^{2}.

By relative smoothness,
\langle\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),x-\hat{x}\rangle\leq(L-\mu)\|x-\hat{x}\|_{H_{x}}^{2}
. Combining these bounds implies
f_{-\mu}
is
L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)
-smooth in the
\|\cdot\|_{A}
norm with

L_{f_{-\mu}}(A)\leq(L-\mu)(1+L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}R^{\theta}).

The result then follows from co-convexity and the Cauchy–Schwarz argument used in
theorem

8
.

In practice, the intermediate points
\xi_{x}
and
\xi_{y}
from the Taylor expansion are unknown. To operationalize
theorem

11
, one can use a practical adaptive estimate for the GCS constant:

C_{f,\phi}\approx(L-\mu)\bigl(1+L_{\nabla^{2}\phi}(R)\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{\theta}\bigr).

(27)

Alternatively, using
L
as a global estimate for
C_{f,\phi}
, i.e.,

C_{f,\phi}\approx L.

(28)

provides a robust and conservative upper bound that ensures algorithmic stability by bypassing the need to compute sensitive local Hessian curvature parameters or Hölder constants, which is particularly advantageous during the transient phase when iterates are far from the optimum; see Numerical Example
7.1
.

A similar result can be obtained by assuming that
f_{-\mu}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\theta}(B(x^{\star},R/2))
. The proof is slightly involved as
f_{-\mu}
may not be strongly convex.

Theorem 12
.

Suppose
\phi
is
1
-strongly convex and
1
holds. If
f-\mu\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\theta}(B(x^{\star},R/2))
, then
2
holds locally for any
(x,\hat{x},y,\hat{y})\in B(x^{\star},R/2)
with

C_{f,\phi}\leq 3\bigl((L-\mu)+2L_{\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}}(R)R^{\theta}\bigr).

Proof.

By Taylor’s theorem, there exist points
\xi,\zeta
on the line segment

[\hat{x},x]
and a point
\eta
on
[\hat{y},y]
such that

\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})=A_{1}(x-\hat{x}),\ 2D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x})=\|x-\hat{x}\|_{A_{2}}^{2},\ 2D_{f_{-\mu}}(y,\hat{y})=\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A_{3}}^{2},

where
A_{1}=\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}(\xi)
,
A_{2}=\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}(\zeta)
, and

A_{3}=\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}(\eta)
.

Setting
A=A_{2}+\varepsilon I
for
\varepsilon>0
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies

\left\langle{\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x}),y-\hat{y}}\right\rangle\leq\|\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})\|_{A^{-1}}\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A}.

Hölder continuity on
B(x^{\star},R/2)
ensures
\|A-A_{3}\|\leq L_{\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}}R^{\theta}+\varepsilon
. Using relative smoothness and the strong convexity of
\phi
, we have

\|y-\hat{y}\|_{A}^{2}\leq 2((L-\mu)+L_{\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}}R^{\theta}+\varepsilon)D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y).

Next, co-coercivity of
f_{-\mu}
in the
\|\cdot\|_{A^{-1}}
norm gives

\|\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)-\nabla f_{-\mu}(\hat{x})\|_{A^{-1}}^{2}\leq 2(2+\varepsilon^{-1}L_{\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}}R^{\theta})D_{f_{-\mu}}(x,\hat{x}).

Combining these two bounds and setting the regularization parameter
\varepsilon=L_{\nabla^{2}f_{-\mu}}R^{\theta}
yields the desired constant
C_{f,\phi}
.

Extension to convex optimization

For convex objectives (
\mu=0
), we consider the following perturbed flow:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{\prime}&=y-x,\\
(\nabla\phi(y))^{\prime}&=-\epsilon^{-1}\nabla f(x)+\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(y),\end{aligned}\right.

(30)

for a fixed perturbation level
\epsilon>0
. In dual variables, this system becomes

x^{\prime}=y-x,\quad\eta^{\prime}=-\epsilon^{-1}\nabla f(x)+\chi-\eta,

where
\chi=\nabla\phi(x)
and
\eta=\nabla\phi(y)
. Due to the variable splitting, the perturbation does not change the stational points of the original system.

Consider the Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\eta):=D_{f}(x,x^{\star})+\epsilon\,D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi^{\star}),

(31)

where
\epsilon>0
is a fixed parameter. Taking derivatives with respect to
x
and
\eta
gives a modified energy dissipation identity.

Lemma 14
(Perturbed strong Lyapunov property).

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
be the Lyapunov function in (
31
), and let
\mathcal{G}
be the vector field of the perturbed accelerated mirror descent flow (
30
). Then:

-\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\eta)\cdot\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\eta)+D_{f}(x^{\star},x)+\epsilon D_{\phi^{*}}(\chi,\eta)-\epsilon D_{\phi^{*}}(\chi,\chi^{\star}).

(32)

Proof.

Direct calculation gives:

\displaystyle-\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\eta)\cdot\mathcal{G}(x,\eta)={}

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\star}),\,x-x^{\star}\rangle+\epsilon\,\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\eta)-\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi^{\star}),\,\eta-\chi\rangle.

The first term is the symmetric Bregman divergence of
f
,

\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\star}),\,x-x^{\star}\rangle=D_{f}(x,x^{\star})+D_{f}(x^{\star},x).

Using the three-point identity, the second term expands to

\epsilon\,\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\eta)-\nabla\phi^{*}(\chi^{\star}),\,\eta-\chi\rangle=\epsilon(D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi^{\star})+D_{\phi^{*}}(\chi,\eta)-D_{\phi^{*}}(\chi,\chi^{\star})).

Combining these with the definition (
31
) of
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
yields (
32
).

The perturbed forward Acc-MD method is stated in
algorithm

3
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=D_{f}(x,x^{\star})+\epsilon D_{\phi^{*}}(\eta,\chi^{\star})+\alpha\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\star}),\,y-x^{\star}\rangle,

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x,\hat{x},\hat{y},y)

\displaystyle:=D_{f}(x,\hat{x})+\epsilon D_{\phi}(\hat{y},y)+\alpha\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(\hat{x}),\,y-\hat{y}\rangle.

(34)

Using
lemma

14
, we obtain the following identity.

Lemma 15
.

Let
(x_{k},y_{k})
be the sequence from Algorithm

. Then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x_{k},y_{k})={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\,\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-\alpha\epsilon D_{\phi}(y_{k+1},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle+\alpha\epsilon D_{\phi}(x^{\star},y_{k+1})+\alpha\epsilon D_{\phi}(x^{\star},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle-\alpha\,\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha}(x^{\star},x_{k+1},y_{k+1},x^{\star})-\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}(x_{k},x_{k+1},y_{k},y_{k+1}).

Theorem 16
(Convergence of Perturbed Acc-MD).

Assume
f
is convex and
2
holds with
\mu=0
and
C_{f,\phi}>0
. For any
\epsilon>0
, let
(x_{k},y_{k})
be the sequence generated by Algorithm

with
\alpha=\sqrt{\epsilon/C_{f,\phi}}

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{\epsilon}(x_{k},y_{k})\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\epsilon/C_{f,\phi}}\right)^{-k}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{\epsilon}(x_{0},y_{0})+\epsilon R.

For

Iterating this inequality and summing the geometric series yields (

Extension to Composite Optimization

Consider the composite optimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad F(x):=f(x)+g(x),

(36)

where
f
satisfies
1
and
g
is convex but possibly non-smooth. This formulation encompasses constrained optimization by letting
g
be the indicator function of a convex set
K
. We assume
g
admits a tractable proximal operator with respect to the mirror function
\phi
. Crucially, (
A1
) and (
A2
) are required only for
f
.

The composite accelerated mirror descent flow is defined as:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{\prime}&=y-x,\\
-\mu(\nabla\phi(y))^{\prime}+\mu(\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(y))-\nabla f(x)&\in\partial g(y).\end{aligned}\right.

(37)

In dual variables
\eta=\nabla\phi(y)
, this system simplifies to:

x^{\prime}=y-x,\qquad\eta^{\prime}=-\mu^{-1}(\nabla f_{-\mu}(x)+q(y))-\eta,

where
q(y)\in\partial g(y)
is a subgradient of
g
. We employ the Lyapunov function (
13
) used in the smooth case; the strong Lyapunov property follows by an analogous argument.

The backward Acc-MD scheme for composite optimization is summarized in Algorithm
5
, and the forward version can be obtained similarly.

Algorithm 5

1:

Input:

x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, parameters
\mu>0
,
C_{f,\phi}>0

Set
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu/{C}_{f,\phi}}.

3:

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

4:

y_{k+1}=\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\alpha)\,\phi(y)+\frac{\alpha}{\mu}g(y)-\left\langle\alpha\nabla\phi(x_{k})+\nabla\phi(y_{k})-\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\nabla f(x_{k}),\,y\right\rangle.

5:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=\tfrac{1}{1+\alpha}[x_{k}+\alpha(2y_{k+1}-y_{k})]
.

6:

end

for

As subgradients of
g
are evaluated implicitly, the non-smooth term
g
does not contribute to the cross-term error, and convergence follows.

(Convergence in the Composite Setting).

Let
g
be convex and
f
be
\mu
-relatively convex with respect to
\phi
(

2

Numerical Examples

We evaluate the performance of Acc-MD on a variety of convex optimization problems, ensuring reproducibility by fixing all random seeds. We compare Acc-MD against several state-of-the-art first-order methods from the literature. Numerical experiment demonstrate that Acc-MD exhibits superior stability and competitive acceleration compared to existing variants.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced accelerated mirror descent (Acc-MD) methods that achieve improved convergence rates under relative smoothness and relative convexity settings. By leveraging relative strong convexity and a new generalized Cauchy-Schwarz (GCS) inequality, we established the first accelerated linear convergence rate for mirror descent methods. In the standard convex setting, we utilized a perturbation–homotopy argument to recover the optimal
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
rate. Our proposed framework naturally extends to composite and constrained optimization problems while maintaining these accelerated guarantees.

We emphasize that
2
is not intended to be universally verifiable or strictly stronger than existing assumptions. Relaxing this condition, or adaptively updating the constant
C_{f,\phi}
, represents an important direction for future research. Another promising path is the extension of this framework to stochastic settings, which is highly relevant for large-scale applications such as neural network training.

Real-World Deployment of Cloud Autonomous Mobility System Using 5G Networks for Outdoor and Indoor Environments
Yufeng Yang, Minghao Ning, Keqi Shu, Aladdin Saleh, Ehsan Hashemi, and Amir Khajepour

Abstract
The growing complexity of both outdoor and indoor mobility systems demands scalable, cost-effective, and reliable perception and communication frameworks. This work presents the real-world deployment and evaluation of a Cloud Autonomous Mobility (CAM) system that leverages distributed sensor nodes connected via 5G networks, which integrates LiDAR- and camera-based perception at infrastructure units, cloud computing for global information fusion, and Ultra-Reliable Low Latency Communications (URLLC) to enable real-time situational awareness and autonomous operation. The CAM system is deployed in two distinct environments: a dense urban roundabout and a narrow indoor hospital corridor. Field experiments show improved traffic monitoring, hazard detection, and asset management capabilities. The paper also discusses practical deployment challenges and shares key insights for scaling CAM systems. The results highlight the potential of cloud-based infrastructure perception to advance both outdoor and indoor intelligent transportation systems.

As Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) evolved beyond onboard sensing, the development of communication technologies became a key enabler. This led to the integration of Roadside Units (RSUs) into transportation systems

However, this setup still faced several shortcomings. DSRC was designed for limited communication ranges, requiring RSUs to be deployed closely together, which reduced cost efficiency. Maintenance was also difficult due to the lack of standardized protocols and the reliance on custom-made equipment. Furthermore, the computational power of these systems was limited, and they provided minimal support for cybersecurity. These limitations underscored the need for more scalable, long-range, and standardized communication infrastructures to meet the growing demands of ITS.

In this recently proposed CAM system, each end unit is connected through the cloud and the system is managed in a centralized manner. Thanks to the remarkable 5G communication technology, the CAM system is able to achieve low-latency, high-bandwidth, and stable connections between vehicles, RSUs, and the cloud.

The nature of the cloud-based architecture offers more flexibility to the system. Given the cloud-based architecture, the system is more modularized, allowing more RSUs to integrate comparatively easily and seamlessly. As the transportation network increases and the size of urban areas increases, the system is more feasible to expand

Due to the advanced features of the CAM system, and to the best of our knowledge, there are limited large-scale real-world applications of the CAM system. In this paper, a CAM system that is equipped with multiple roadside units (also known as sensor nodes), implemented in the real world for both outdoor and indoor environments, is presented. This system overcomes the challenges of implementing CAM systems in the real world, such as smart device and data management, high-efficiency communication configuration, multi-input perception integration, and energy and power management.

II
CAM Framework Overview

Fig. 2:
Information flow of the Cloud Autonomous Mobility System

II-A

System Architecture

The proposed CAM system is composed of two primary layers:

•

A
distributed infrastructure sensor node network
, and

•

A
centralized cloud computing unit
.

An overview of the system architecture is illustrated in Fig.
1
. The infrastructure sensor nodes—deployed in both outdoor urban environments and indoor facilities—collect local perception data using LiDAR and camera sensors. Each node processes this data locally and transmits compact perception outputs via 5G communication to a central cloud server.

The cloud server fuses information from multiple nodes to create a unified global perception, supports real-time trajectory planning, and disseminates control and safety messages back to connected entities such as autonomous vehicles and robotic platforms over 5G network.

II-B

Infrastructure Sensor Nodes

Each infrastructure sensor node is designed as a modular and self-contained unit comprising three main components:

•

Sensing Module
: A combination of LiDAR and cameras that capture 3D spatial and visual information of the environment.

•

Compute Module
: An onboard processor responsible for sensor synchronization, multi-modal data fusion, object detection, and metadata generation.

•

Communication Module
: A 5G modem that transmits processed perception data to the cloud with minimal latency.

As shown in Fig.
2
, each node performs local sensor fusion—aligning LiDAR point clouds and camera images through time synchronization and extrinsic calibration. Then the global perception information is send to the computing cloud center.
This distributed edge processing approach reduces the burden on cloud bandwidth, lowers system latency, and enhances overall scalability and robustness.

II-C

Cloud Computing and Information Fusion

The cloud server receives perception data streams from all connected nodes and performs centralized information fusion. A global fusion module merges multi-source sensor (LiDARs and cameras) inputs into a coherent spatial map, enabling accurate tracking and forecasting of object trajectories across a broad area. As presented in Fig.
2
, for outdoor applications, perception and hazard warning information is sent to human-driven vehicles (NV), and supporting planning information is sent to autonomous vehicles (AV). For indoor applications, planning and asset management information is sent to mobile robots for advanced autonomous mobility.

The cloud unit performs several key functions:

•

Global-level data fusion and consistency maintenance

•

Multi-agent tracking and prediction

•

Detection of critical traffic events or anomalies

•

Generation of planning commands for connected vehicles and robots

The processed outputs are relayed back to end-users—vehicles, hospital beds, or facility managers—via 5G networks, ensuring timely delivery of insights and instructions.

II-D

Advantages of the CAM System

The CAM framework offers several unique advantages over conventional perception architectures that rely solely on onboard sensors or isolated roadside units:

•

Low-Latency Communication:
Leveraging 5G connectivity, the system achieves fast, reliable data transmission between infrastructure and vehicles. This enables real-time response with minimal delay across large areas.

•

Extended Perception Coverage:
High-mounted infrastructure sensors provide unobstructed fields of view, minimizing occlusion effects and enhancing object detection accuracy—particularly at intersections, corners, or indoor corridors.

•

Modularity and Scalability:
The plug-and-play nature of sensor nodes and distributed computing allows for easy system expansion. Additional nodes can be integrated without overloading the cloud, and regional processing ensures scalable operation across large deployments.

•

Real-Time Safety and Navigation Support:
The system supports both CAVs and human-driven vehicles by transmitting enhanced decision-making data to CAVs and hazard warnings to human drivers. Infrastructure-based sensing reduces the need for onboard sensors, lowering costs and enabling broader deployment.

In summary, the CAM system bridges local sensing, high-speed networking, and cloud-level intelligence to support cooperative, safe, and efficient mobility in a wide range of transportation scenarios.

III
Outdoor application

The proposed framework has shown promising results in urban traffic scenarios. The CAM system was implemented at a roundabout that is part of a local city road. In the four-way roundabout setup, 14 sensor nodes were installed on light poles along the roadway. Each sensor node is equipped with advanced sensors and connected via the 5G network to the cloud.

III-A

Connected autonomous vehicle fleet management

As the local sensor-equipped infrastructure units are connected to the cloud through 5G network, and global perception information is obtained, a connected vehicle—also connected to the system via 5G network—is able to navigate through the urban area safely and efficiently. Thanks to the height of the deployed sensors, the global perception has minimal occlusion and a wide range, allowing the connected vehicle to have a more comprehensive and unbiased understanding of the environment. The implementation of 5G network enables very low-latency communication; thus, the movement of surrounding traffic participants, as well as decision-making commands to the connected vehicle, can be transmitted in near real time. This ensures the responsiveness not only of a single connected vehicle but also enables coordinated behavior across an entire connected vehicle fleet. Such fleet-level coordination can lead to more stable traffic flow, improved routing, and collaborative maneuvers—particularly in dense urban scenarios.

III-B

Road hazard and critical scenario warning

With this enhanced perception, the system is also capable of supporting additional safety-critical functions, such as issuing warnings for potential collisions, road blockages, or traffic rule violations.
For example, at a roundabout, if a vehicle is entering while a pedestrian is attempting to cross the road—creating a potential collision scenario—the CAM system can provide a timely alert to any traffic participant connected to the 5G network. This could include an alert sent to a smartphone app with 5G connectivity or directly to a connected vehicle.
These proactive responses further enhance overall traffic safety and system reliability.

Indoor application

The proposed CAM system was implemented and evaluated at the University of Waterloo to simulate the hospital environment. A total of four sensor nodes were installed across two separate areas for different experimental objectives. Specifically, Sensor Nodes 1 to 3 were deployed along a hallway to evaluate asset management capabilities, whereas Sensor Node 4 was positioned to assess autonomous driving functionalities, including human-robot interaction and control. Asset Management Results

For the asset tracking evaluation, the medical bed was tele-operated, starting from the position of Sensor Node 3. It will travel through the hallway, enter the room, and stop at the location of Sensor Node 1. As the medical bed moved through the hallway, real-time position estimates were generated through the cooperative sensing framework, enabling accurate tracking as the bed transitioned between the coverage zones of adjacent nodes. The primary objective of this test was to demonstrate the capability of the distributed sensor network to detect, localize, and track the medical bed accurately and reliably in real time. In addition, the test aimed to evaluate system performance metrics, including localization accuracy, communication latency, and tracking robustness during transitions between sensor coverage areas. These results provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of the proposed CAM framework for asset management applications.

Fig.
6
presents sample images captured by Sensor Nodes 1 and 2 at selected time instances during the asset tracking evaluation. As shown, the onboard cameras successfully detect and identify both the medical bed and nearby individuals using trained neural networks. Based on these detections, the corresponding positions of the bed and person are then estimated by fusing the camera outputs with LiDAR point cloud data. In this particular example, the system focuses on tracking the medical bed; however, the same framework can be extended to recognize and track other types of medical equipment, such as medication carts, surgical carts, and similar assets commonly found in healthcare environments.

IV-B

Autonomous Driving Results

For autonomous driving evaluation, the medical bed was controlled by the developed social-aware planning algorithm that considers the nearby human’s personal space defined from psychology studies
[
17
]
. Three testing scenarios were given to evaluate the performance:
Single Person Interaction
,
Yielding
, and
Group Interaction
.

In the
Single Person Interaction
scenario, the medical bed was required to navigate around an individual standing or walking within its path while maintaining a socially acceptable distance. In the
Yielding
scenario, the bed needed to yield fast-walking person approaching from behind and stop in front of a standing person, demonstrating appropriate social compliance. Finally, in the
Group Interaction
scenario, the medical bed navigated around a group of people. These scenarios were designed to simulate realistic human-robot interaction environments and evaluate the planner’s ability to generate smooth, socially appropriate trajectories while ensuring safe and efficient autonomous navigation.

, respectively. For each instance, the top image displays the RViz-based visualization, and the bottom images show the real-world scenes captured by the two cameras mounted on Sensor Node 4.

In the RViz visualizations, the green rectangle denotes the medical bed, while the green circles represent nearby individuals. The blue shaded regions correspond to the defined personal spaces around each person, based on proxemic theory. The red solid line indicates the operating boundary within which the medical bed must remain, and the red dashed line marks the reference path that the bed should follow. The yellow arrow illustrates the planned trajectory, which is dynamically adjusted to respect personal space constraints and ensure safe, socially-aware navigation.

In all the scenarios, the sensor node four was able to detect the human and the bed and provide accurate estimated positions; the social-aware planning algorithm was able to navigate around a single person while maintaining a socially acceptable distance. These results qualitatively demonstrate the effectiveness of the social-aware planning framework in managing complex human-robot interaction scenarios.

V
Conclusion

This article presented a Cloud Autonomous Mobility (CAM) system through cooperation between multiple sensor nodes via 5G networks. Each sensor node integrates two cameras and a LiDAR sensor, allowing it to correctly identify object types and provide accurate position estimates. By leveraging the low latency, high bandwidth, and reliable communication capabilities of 5G network, global perception information can be processed in a real-time manner using cloud computing resources.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed system, the CAM framework was deployed and tested in real-world environments in two distinct scenarios: outdoor and indoor. In the outdoor scenario, the CAM system demonstrated its potential for applications such as connected autonomous fleet management and traffic collision monitoring. In the indoor scenario, the system demonstrated its capabilities for asset management and socially aware autonomous navigation. These experimental results highlight the versatility and scalability of the proposed CAM approach across diverse mobility and monitoring applications.

Future work will focus on further developing the potential applications of the proposed CAM system in both outdoor and indoor scenarios. For example, in outdoor environments, the CAM system could be utilized for traffic flow monitoring, while in indoor environments, it could be extended to support additional functions such as patient fall detection and infrastructure anomaly monitoring. To enable even more instantaneous decision-making in real time with added security, ongoing efforts are focused on enhancing 5G connectivity through the integration of mmWave bands and the deployment of a Multi-access Edge Computing (MEC) platform to further reduce latency.

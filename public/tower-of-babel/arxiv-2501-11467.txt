Abstract
The possibility of errors in human-engineered formal verification software, such as model checkers, poses a serious threat to the purpose of these tools. An established approach to mitigate this problem are certificates —lightweight, easy-to-check proofs of the verification results. In this paper, we develop novel certificates for model checking of Markov decision processes (MDPs) with quantitative reachability and expected reward properties. Our approach is conceptually simple and relies almost exclusively on elementary fixed point theory. Our certificates work for arbitrary finite MDPs and can be readily computed with little overhead using standard algorithms. We formalize the soundness of our certificates in Isabelle/HOL and provide a formally verified certificate checker . Moreover, we augment existing algorithms in the probabilistic model checker Storm with the ability to produce certificates and demonstrate practical applicability by conducting the first formal certification of the reference results in the Quantitative Verification Benchmark Set.

Fixed Point Theory.

A
partial order
on a set

is a binary relation
\preceq
that is reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric;
in this case, the tuple
(X,\preceq)
is called a

.
Given a poset

If an upper bound

(or least upper bound) and denoted

.

Lower bounds
and

(or greatest lower bounds) are defined analogously.

The poset
(X,\preceq)
is a

if
\sup Y
and
\inf Y
exist for every
Y\subseteq X
.
Every complete lattice has a least and greatest element
\sup\emptyset
and
\inf\emptyset
, respectively.
The following complete lattices are of interest in this paper:

•

({\overline{\mathbb{N}}},\leq)
where
{\overline{\mathbb{N}}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
are the
extended natural numbers
and
\leq
is the usual order on
\mathbb{N}
extended by
a\leq\infty
for all
a\in{\overline{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Notice that for every
Y\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
\sup Y=\infty
iff
Y
is infinite.

•

Similarly,
(\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0},\leq)
, with
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
the
extended non-negative reals

For every
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\sup Y=\infty
iff
Y
is unbounded.

•

([0,1],\leq)
, the totally ordered set of real probabilities.

•

If
(X,\preceq)
is an arbitrary complete lattice, then for all sets

(X^{S},\mathrel{\ddot{\preceq}})

X^{S}=\{f\mid f\colon S\to X\}
is the set of functions from
S
to

f\mathrel{\ddot{\preceq}}g\iff\forall s\in S\colon f(s)\preceq g(s)

In the following, we overload notation and write

For example, if

Let
(X,\preceq)
be a poset.
A function
\mathcal{F}\colon X\to X
is called

\forall a,b\in X\colon a\preceq b\implies\mathcal{F}(a)\preceq\mathcal{F}(b)
.
The following is
the
key tool of this paper:

Theorem 2.1
(Knaster-Tarski)

Let
(X,\preceq)
be a complete lattice and
\mathcal{F}\colon X\to X
be monotone.
Then, the set of
fixed points

(\{a\in X\mid\mathcal{F}(a)=a\},\preceq)
is also a complete lattice.
In particular,
\mathcal{F}
has a
least
and a
greatest
fixed point given by
\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{F}}=\inf{\{a\in X\mid\mathcal{F}(a)\preceq a\}}
and, dually,
\operatorname{gfp}{\mathcal{F}}=\sup{\{a\in X\mid a\preceq\mathcal{F}(a)\}}
.
As a consequence, the following
fixed point induction rules
are sound:

\forall a\in X\colon

•

\mathcal{F}(a)\preceq a\implies\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{F}}\preceq a

(fixed point induction)

•

a\preceq\mathcal{F}(a)\implies a\preceq\operatorname{gfp}{\mathcal{F}}

(fixed point co-induction)

Elements
a\in X
with
\mathcal{F}(a)\preceq a
(or
a\preceq\mathcal{F}(a)
) are called
(co-)
inductive
.

The iteration can be stopped when the difference is as small as desired.

However, as we demonstrate experimentally (

, as was already reported in

in a similar setting.
More precisely, let

be a

of
\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}

the (exact) result of each operation is rounded

This the default rounding mode in IEEE 754.
Let

does

might not be co-inductive.
We propose two ways to mitigate this problem:
Safe rounding

[
27
]
and

.

First, safe rounding amounts to configuring the IEEE754 rounding mode so that results of floating point computations are always rounded towards
0
when iterating from below, and towards
\infty
when iterating from above.
While safe rounding provably yields sound bounds
[
27
]
, it may slow down or even prevent convergence of II.
Nonetheless, in practice,
II with safe rounding finds significantly more certificates than II with default rounding (
Section

7
).

Second, for Smooth II we define the
\gamma

(
\gamma\in[0,1)

\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}(x)\penalty\ =\penalty\ \gamma\cdot x+(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x)\penalty\ ,

where scalar multiplication and addition are component-wise.

\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}
have the same fixed points, and every
(co-)

\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}

(co-)

\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}

).
The key property of

compared to
\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}
is that the former enforces
ultimately strictly monotonic VI sequences
.
This mitigates the floating point rounding issues.
Notice, however, that smoothing slows down convergence.
Smoothing and safe rounding may be combined.

Implementation.

We implemented certificate computation as discussed in
Section

6
in
Storm

[
33
]
.
Given a higher-level model description (
PRISM

[
42
]
or
Jani

[
14
]
), and a reachability probability or expected reward query, our implementation proceeds in three steps:
First,
Storm
builds an explicit MDP from the description.
Second, it computes a certificate for both lower
and
upper bounds, such that the relative difference between the two values is at most
\varepsilon=10^{-6}
for each MDP state.
Finally,
Storm
checks the validity of the certificate.

Following the discussion in
Section

6
, we consider the following algorithms:
Regarding exact computation, we use PI with
exact
LU decomposition, called PI
X
.
For approximate computation with floating point arithmetic, we employ II.
Further, to investigate the impact of the rounding error mitigation techniques from
Section

6
, we complement II with either safe rounding (denoted II
rnd
), smoothing with parameter
\gamma
(denoted II
↻γ
; we consider
\gamma\in\{0.05,0.8,0.9,0.95\}
), or a combination of both (denoted II
{}^{\circlearrowright\gamma}_{\mathrm{rnd}}
).
Overall, we compare PI
X
and seven variants of II.
We employ additional standard modifications of the algorithms, namely:
We eliminate end components whenever possible, apply topological optimizations for PI
X
and II, and apply Gauß-Seidl Bellman updates for II
[
8
,
29
]
.

In all three steps of the implementation, we represent numbers as arbitrary precision rationals implemented in GMP
[
22
]
—except when running II in the second step (in which case we convert rationals to their nearest floats, potentially yielding invalid certificates).
We thus certify reachability probabilities and expected rewards with respect to the
exact
MDP without rounding errors.

The MDP and the certificate computed with
Storm
can be exported and checked by an independent
formally verified certificate checker
.
To construct the latter, we verified the correctness of the certificate checking algorithms in the interactive proof assistant
Isabelle/HOL

[
46
]
, extending previous work on MDPs
[
34
,
50
]
by total rewards and qualitative reachability properties.
Based on this library, we proved correct the soundness of the certificates described in
Sections

3
,
4
and
5
.
We used
Isabelle/HOL

[
24
]
to obtain a verified, executable Standard ML implementation that employs exact rational arithmetic.
The construction of the MDP from a
PRISM
or
Jani

Conclusion and Future Work

We proposed
fixed point certificates
as a new standard for certified model checking of reachability and expected reward properties in MDPs.
The soundness of these certificates was formalized in
Isabelle/HOL
, increasing their trustworthiness and enabling us to generate a formally verified certificate checker, applicable to non-trivial practically relevant instances.
Our certificates can be generated with moderate overhead via minor, yet careful, modifications of established algorithms like II or PI.
This allows tool developers and competitions
[
15
]
—for which our certificates provide formally verified reference results—to adopt our proposal with relatively low effort.
Future work is to develop a more efficient certificate format.
Further, we plan to extend our theory to other quantitative verification settings
[
3
]
, e.g., stochastic games and
\omega
-regular properties, and make it amenable to techniques such as
symbolic model checking
and
partial exploration
.

Appendix 0.B
More Details on Preliminaries

So far, (in
Section

2
) we only defined reachability probabilities and expected rewards w.r.t. probability measures on infinite paths. For some proofs however, we also use reachability probabilities w.r.t. all paths of length
\leq n
for a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
and expected rewards w.r.t. all paths of length exactly
n
.
To be precise, these so called step-bounded reachability probabilities are used in the proof of
Lemma

2
as well as in the proof of
Lemma

7
, and the step-bounded cumulative rewards are used in the proof of
Lemma

7
as well as in the proof of
Theorem

0.F.1
.

Fix a DTMC
(S,{Act},P)
and a
target
(or goal) set
T\in S
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider a finite path
s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{k}
of length
k\leq n
. We define the random variable

\lozenge^{\leq n}T(s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{k})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\exists i\colon s_{i}\in T\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

For a state
s\in S
, we define the step-bounded reachability probabilities
\mathbb{P}_{s}(\lozenge^{\leq n}T)
from
s
towards
T
as the expected value (Lebesgue integral) of
\lozenge^{\leq n}T
.

Now fix a
reward function

\textnormal{rew}\colon S\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and a finite path
s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{n-1}\in S^{n}
. We define the random variable

\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T}(s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{n-1})=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{j\mid s_{j}\in T\}}\textnormal{rew}(s_{i})&\text{if }\exists j\colon s_{j}\in T\\
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\textnormal{rew}(s_{i})&\text{else,}\end{cases}

for both, the
“
*=\infty
”
and the
“
*=\rho
”
semantics of expected rewards. For a state
s\in S
, we define the step-bounded cumulative rewards
\mathbb{E}_{s}(\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T})
from
s
towards
T
as the expected value of
\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T}
. We often write
\mathbb{E}_{s}(\lozenge^{=n}T)
instead of
\mathbb{E}_{s}(\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T})
when

is clear from the context.

Finally, given an MDP
\mathcal{M}=(S,{Act},P)
, a state
s\in S
, a target set
T\subseteq S
, a reward function
\textnormal{rew}\colon S\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and

we define the

\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{s}(\lozenge^{\leq n}T)=\operatorname{opt}_{\sigma}\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}_{s}(\lozenge^{\leq n}T)
and the
optimal step-bounded cumulative expected reward

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{s}(\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T})=\operatorname{opt}_{\sigma}\mathbb{E}^{\sigma}_{s}(\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T})
, where
\mathbb{P}^{\sigma}_{s}(\lozenge^{\leq n}T)
and
\mathbb{E}^{\sigma}_{s}(\textnormal{rew}^{\lozenge^{=n}T})
are the step-bounded reachability probabilities and step-bounded cumulative expected rewards in the induced DTMC
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}
.

With the
“
*=\rho
”
semantics of expected rewards, we have for all strategies

. With the
“

\mathit{Post}(q,a)\nsubseteq X^{*}
.
For every such leaving state-action pair, we have that there exists a
q^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(q,a)
with
r(q^{\prime})\leq n
: The rank
r(q^{\prime})
cannot be greater than
n+1
, as otherwise
r(q)
would be greater than
n+1
, and it cannot be equal to
n+1
, because otherwise
q^{\prime}
would have been added to
X^{*}
.
Thus, every strategy using a leaving state-action pair reaches a state with
r
less than or equal to
n
, and by Induction Hypothesis, then this strategy has positive probability to reach
Z
.

Finally, note that every strategy remaining in
X^{*}
forever gets value 0, as
X^{*}
does not contain any target state.
Consequently, an optimal strategy has to use a leaving state-action pair, and thus decrease the rank.

Overall, we have shown that for
\operatorname{opt}=\max
, if a rank is finite, then an optimal strategy for reaching
T
will reach
Z
(constrained to
S\setminus T
) with positive probability (avoiding to be stuck in a cycle and not reaching the target set at all).

Together, we have shown that for
\operatorname{opt}
being
\min
or
\max
, if the rank is finite, then the sink states are reached with positive probability under strategies optimal for reaching
T
.

Lemma 11
(Completeness)

Let
\operatorname{opt}\in\{\min,\max\}
and let
r=\operatorname{lfp}{\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}}
.
Let
\sigma
be an optimal strategy for reaching
T
according to
\operatorname{opt}
.
Let

for
s\in S
,
where
\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\sigma}(S\setminus T\mathbin{\mathsf{U}}Z)
denotes the probability that the play reaches
Z
before reaching
T
under strategy
\sigma
(constrained reachability problem).
It holds for all
s\in S
that
r(s)<\infty\impliedby z(s)>0
.

Proof.

We start with the assumption that
z(s)>0
and assume for contradiction
r(s)=\infty
.
We make a case distinction on whether
\operatorname{opt}
used by
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}
is
\max
or
\min
.

Case
\operatorname{opt}=\min
:

In this case, since

is a fixpoint of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\min}
, using

2

r(q)=\infty
implies that for all
a\in{Act}(q),s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(q,a)
we have
r(s^{\prime})=\infty
.

As in
Lemma

10
, we inductively construct a set

, defined as the least fixpoint of:

X_{0}=\{s\}
and
X_{i+1}=X_{i}\cup\{p\in\mathit{Post}(q,a)\mid\exists q\in X_{i}\setminus T,a\in{Act}(q)\}
.
The set
X^{*}
is well defined, since there are finitely many states, and every iteration adds a state or terminates.
Moreover, by the above argument, all successors of states not in
T
have rank infinity and hence we have for all
q\in X^{*}
that
r(q)=\infty
.

However, since
z(s)>0
, we also have
Z\cap X^{*}\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma

9
shows that
r(q)=0
for all
q\in Z
when
r
is the least fixed point of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}
.
This is a contradiction to the rank being infinite for all states in
X^{*}
, concluding the case.

Case
\operatorname{opt}=\max
:

In this case, since
r
is a fixpoint of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\max}
, using
Definition

2
we get that for all states
q\in S
,
r(q)=\infty
implies that there exists
a\in{Act}(q)
such that for all
s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(q,a)
we have

.
We construct

as the least fixpoint of:

Note that this

These actions need not be those that are used by the optimal strategy

Thus, we cannot simply conclude that

We now show that there have to be some states in

that should have finite rank, thus contradicting the assumption

Intuitively, we show that if

As the proof is quite involved, we adopt a proof style of providing the major claims, and then indenting the necessary definitions and reasoning.
This way, the overall idea can be obtained by reading only the major items.

Further Details on Smooth Interval Iteration

In

6

\displaystyle x\leq\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}(x)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x\leq\gamma\cdot x+(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(1-\gamma)\cdot x\leq(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x\leq\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x)

For Value Iteration from below this means that the sequence the
\gamma
-smooth Bellman operator creates constantly lies below the sequence that the normal Bellman operator creates, i.e., if you let

and

, then it holds for all

. We prove this by an induction:

•

i=0

.

•

i>0
: For states

it surely holds that

For states

the induction hypothesis yields

and by that

\displaystyle x_{\gamma}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\gamma\cdot x_{\gamma}^{(i-1)}+(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x_{\gamma}^{(i-1)})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\cdot x^{(i-1)}+(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x^{(i-1)})\leq\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(x^{(i-1)})=x^{(i)}.

By the principle of induction, we conclude that
x_{\gamma}^{(i)}\leq x^{(i)}
for all

. Consequently, this also implies that
\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}}\leq\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}}
. However, we show that the least fixed points of

and

even coincide. To do so, it remains to prove that

\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}}\leq\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}(\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}})

\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{\gamma}(\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}})=\gamma\cdot\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}}+(1-\gamma)\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}(\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}})=\operatorname{lfp}{\mathcal{B}^{\operatorname{opt}}},

we obtain the desired.

Computation of the least fixed point of the modified distance operator

Proposition 16
(Computing the Least Fixed Point of the Complementary Distance Operator)

Algorithm

2
terminates and computes
r\in{\overline{\mathbb{N}}}^{S}
with
r=\operatorname{lfp}{\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}{}}
.

Proof.

Since the algorithm only updates
r
values by applying the
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}{}
function and
\operatorname{lfp}{\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}{}}=\sup{\{{\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\operatorname{opt}}{}}^{n}(0)\penalty\ |\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}}
, it is an invariant that
\hat{r}(s)\leq r(s)
holds for all vectors
\hat{r}
during the execution of the algorithm and all
s\in S
. (
\dagger
)

According to
Lemma

3
, we have that
r(s)=\infty
, if and only if
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{s}(\lozenge T)=1
. Because of (
\dagger
) and the fact that the algorithm initializes the rank of exactly all
s
with
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{s}(\lozenge T)=1
by
\infty
, we get that the algorithm correctly outputs
\infty
for exactly those
s
with
r(s)=\infty.

Now, we show by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
, that also the ranks of all states
s
with
r(s)=n
are correctly computed by the algorithm.

•

Induction start:
n=0
. Follows immediately from (
\dagger
) and the fact that the algorithm initializes the ranks of all
s
with
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{opt}}_{s}(\lozenge T)<1
by
0
.

•

Induction Step:
n>0
. Let
s\in S
with
r(s)=n>0
. Since
r
is a fixed point, it holds that

\underset{a\in{Act}(s)}{\operatorname{opt}}\penalty\ \Big(\penalty\ \underset{s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a)}{\min}r(s^{\prime})+[\exists s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a):r(s^{\prime})\neq r(s^{\prime\prime})]\Big)=n.

If
\underset{a\in{Act}(s)}{\operatorname{opt}}\penalty\ \underset{s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a)}{\min}r(s^{\prime})=n
, then the algorithm considers
s
at a time when the current vector
\hat{r}
satisfies

\underset{a\in{Act}(s)}{\operatorname{opt}}\penalty\ \Big(\penalty\ \underset{s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a)}{\min}\hat{r}(s^{\prime})+[\exists s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a):\hat{r}(s^{\prime})\neq\hat{r}(s^{\prime\prime})]\Big)\geq n.

Because of (
\dagger
), the algorithm correctly sets
r(s)=n
.

If
\underset{a\in{Act}(s)}{\operatorname{opt}}\penalty\ \underset{s^{\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a)}{\min}r(s^{\prime})=n-1
, then there exists a successor
s^{\prime}
of
s
such that
r(s^{\prime})=n-1
and by induction hypothesis the algorithm correctly outputs
r(s^{\prime})=n-1
too. Since
s
is a predecessor of
s^{\prime}
,
s
is surely considered by the algorithm at a time when
\hat{r(s^{\prime})}=n-1
is already correctly set. Then, either
[\exists s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a):\hat{r}(s^{\prime})\neq\hat{r}(s^{\prime\prime})]=1
and
r(s)=n
is correctly set by the algorithm or
[\exists s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a):\hat{r}(s^{\prime})\neq\hat{r}(s^{\prime\prime})]=0
, but then some successor
s^{\prime\prime}
will get a rank update at a later time by the algorithm, and thereafter
[\exists s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathit{Post}(s,a):\hat{r}(s^{\prime})\neq\hat{r}(s^{\prime\prime})]=1
holds and
r(s)=n
is correctly set by the algorithm.

Termination follows from the fact that states are inserted into the queue only a finite amount of times. This is because each state only gets inserted into the queue after its rank increased, and the rank of all states
s
with
r(s)=\infty
is already correctly set on initialization.

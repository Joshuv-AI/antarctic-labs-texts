Orbital angular momentum of entangled photons as a probe for relativistic effects
Fazilah Nothlawala, Kiki Dekkers, Moslem Mahdavifar, Jonathan Leach, Andrew Forbes, Isaac Nape

Abstract
Orbital angular momentum (OAM) as both classical and quantum states of light has proven essential in numerous applications, from high-capacity information transfer to enhanced precision and accuracy in metrology. Here, we extend OAM metrology to relativistic scenarios to determine the Lorentz factor of a moving reference frame, exploiting the fact that OAM is not Lorentz invariant. We show that the joint OAM spectrum from entangled states is modified by length contraction when measured by two observers moving relative to the entanglement source. This relative motion rescales the spatial dimensions, thus breaking the orthogonality of the OAM measurement process and resulting in a broadening of the joint OAM spectrum that can precisely determine the Lorentz factor. We experimentally simulate velocities up to 0.99 ​ c 0.99c , confirm the predicted broadening, and use the measurement outcomes to extract the Lorentz factor. Our work provides a pathway for novel measurement techniques suitable for relativistic conditions that leverage OAM structured light as a resource.

Here, we demonstrate that the broadening in OAM due to Lorentz boosts can determine physical parameters from special relativity. We show that the technique can be used to estimate the Lorentz factor,
\gamma
, from a pair of synchronised relativistic inertial reference frames. In such frames, length contraction rescales spatial coordinates of detectors and distorts the orthogonality of measured entangled photons, producing a measurable broadening in detected OAM correlations. For two photons entangled in OAM, this effect modifies their joint correlations, enabling direct inference of
\gamma
from the altered distribution. To validate our approach, we generate entangled photon pairs via SPDC and emulate relativistic length contraction with spatial light modulators. By comparing the measured OAM spectra against theoretical predictions, we demonstrate quantitative agreement and establish structured light as a novel metrology tool for probing relativistic effects.

Consider Alice (A) and Bob (B) moving at relativistic speeds with respect to Charlie (C), who is in a rest frame, as depicted in Fig.

The aim is to use the entangled photons to investigate the relativistic motion of Alice and Bob via their correlated measurements. To begin, we assume that the photons are entangled in the orbital angular momentum (OAM) degree of freedom and are described by the two-photon state,
\ket{\Psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\ell}\ket{\ell}_{A}\ket{-\ell}_{B}
, where
N
is the number of modes contributing to the quantum state. The OAM eigenstates
\ket{\ell}\propto\int\exp(i \ell\phi)\ket{\phi}d\phi
are characterised by an azimuth dependent (
\phi=\arctan(y/x)
) phase profile,
\exp(i \ell\phi)
, with
\ell
corresponding to an OAM of
\ell\hbar
per photon. Alternatively, the two-photon state can be rewritten in the azimuthal coordinate basis as
\ket{\Psi}\propto\int\ket{\phi}\ket{\phi}d\phi
, where the inner product relation
\langle\phi|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\delta(\phi^{\prime}-\phi)
holds. By defining the two-photon states in this way, we see that the entangled state acts as a projector that maps Alice’s and Bob’s states onto wavefunctions described in terms of the
\phi
coordinate as seen from the rest frame. This means that the projection states used by Alice and Bob take the form
\langle\psi_{A,B}|\phi\rangle=\psi^{*}_{A(B)}(\phi)
, defining the conjugate of the wavefunctions of Alice or Bob measurements in terms an observer at rest (Charlie). For this reason, we will describe the projection states using the rest frame coordinates from here on.

To describe Alice and Bob’s measurement states while moving at relativistic speeds, we use the Lorentz factor
\gamma=1/{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}}
, where the velocity vector aligns with one of the coordinate axes of the measurement system (i.e., the
x
-direction). As a result, Alice and Bob’s measurements are altered so that when viewed from a rest reference frame, their transverse coordinates are mapped via
(x,y)\rightarrow(\frac{x}{\gamma},y)
, scaling the
x
-coordinate by a factor
\gamma
due to length contraction (see Supplementary Material). However, we emphasise that the detection probabilities are equivalent to this when evaluated in the boosted reference frames of Alice and Bob, who observe the incoming photons as sheared in the
x
-direction (see Supplementary Material). Further, because Alice and Bob experience the same time dilation, we assume that they remain synchronised.

The OAM projections that Alice and Bob perform are described using length-contracted azimuthal coordinates that are transformed as
\phi\rightarrow\phi^{\prime}(\phi)=\arctan(\gamma\tan(\phi))
, resulting in measurements corresponding to
\ket{\ell_{A, B}}\propto\int\exp(i \ell_{A,B} \phi'(\phi))\ket{\phi}d\phi.

In conclusion, we have predicted the implications of relativistic length contraction on the joint OAM correlations measured by two spatially separated detectors and harnessed OAM dispersion as a tool to quantify the Lorentz factor. From these observations, we established that a one-to-one map exists between the Lorentz factor and the properties of the modified OAM spectrum. We tested this for different Lorentz factors, starting with the rest frame (
\gamma=1
) up to a moving frame of
\gamma=20
. Our results agreed well with the theoretical predictions.

Importantly, while this work only considered the motion of the detectors in inertial reference frames, both moving at the same velocities, it is easily extended to probe richer scenarios, such as accelerated reference frames or detectors moving at disparate speeds

and there we can now simplify the integral as

\displaystyle\bra{\ell_A}\innerproduct{\ell_B |\psi}{\ell_B |\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\innerproduct{\ell_A|\phi_1}{\ell_A|\phi_1}\innerproduct{\ell_B|\phi_1}{\ell_B|\phi_1}\ d\phi_{1},

(S21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(-i\ell_{A}\phi^{\prime}\left(\phi_{1}\right)\right)\exp\left(-i\ell_{B}\phi^{\prime}\left(\phi_{1}\right)\right)\ d\phi_{1}

(S22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(-i\left(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\phi_{1}\right)\right)\ d\phi_{1}.

(S23)

Hence, the joint probability,
P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}
, for Alice and Bob projecting the
\ket{\psi}
state onto
\ket{\ell_A}
and
\ket{\ell_B}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}

\displaystyle=\left|\bra{\ell_A}\innerproduct{\ell_B |\psi}{\ell_B |\psi}\right|^{2}

(S24)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(-i\left(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\phi_{1}\right)\right)\ d\phi_{1}\right|^{2}.

(S25)

These joint probabilities will be proportional to the coincidence counts that they obtain experimentally. Charlie describes his reference frame by spatial coordinates (
x
,
y
), and so observes his azimuthal coordinate
\phi
as
\tan\phi=\frac{x}{y}
. For another reference frame experiencing a Lorentz boost along
x
, Charlie observes that reference frame’s coordinates as length contracted, namely (
x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}
) = (
\frac{x}{\gamma}
,
y
), and observes that reference frame’s azimuthal coordinate
\phi^{\prime}
as
\tan\phi^{\prime}=\frac{y}{\frac{x}{\gamma}}=\gamma\tan\phi
.
Hence

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\arctan(\frac{1}{\gamma}\tan\phi'),

(S26)

To calculate the Jacobian of this coordinate transformation, substitute
u(\phi^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\gamma}\tan\phi^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{d\phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\arctan u}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{d\phi^{\prime}}

(S27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+u^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}}

(S28)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\tan^{2}\phi^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}}

(S29)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\gamma^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}}

(S30)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma^{2}\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}+\sin^{2}\phi^{\prime}}

(S31)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma^{2}\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}+1-\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}}

(S32)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma^{2}-1)\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}+1^{\prime}}.

(S33)

Therefore

d\phi=\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma^{2}-1)\cos^{2}\phi^{\prime}+1^{\prime}}d\phi^{\prime}.

(S34)

The joint probabilities can now be mapped onto

P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\gamma e^{-i(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})\phi^{\prime}(\phi)}}{(\gamma^{2}-1)\cos^{2}(\phi^{\prime})+1}d\phi^{\prime}\right|^{2}.

(S35)

Subsequently, we apply the residue theorem to the integral after performing the mapping
z=\exp(-i \phi')
,
\cos(2\phi')=(z^{2}+z^{-2})/2
, and
d\phi^{\prime}=-1/(iz)\ dz
so that

P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}=\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{|z|=1}\frac{2i\gamma z^{\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}+1}dz}{\pi\left(\gamma+\gamma z^{2}-z^{2}+1\right)\left(\gamma+\gamma z^{2}+z^{2}-1\right)}\right|^{2}.

(S36)

Noting that the new function mapping has poles at
z_{\pm}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{\sqrt{\gamma+1}}
that are within the boundary
|z|=1
, we can evaluate the integral using the residue theorem, i.e.
\int_{|z|=1}f(z)dz=2\pi i\sum_{k}\text{Res}(f,z_{k})
. Evaluation using the above-mentioned poles (
z_{\pm}
), results in the closed form elementary solution

P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right)^{|\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}|}\cos^{2}((\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})\frac{\pi}{2}),

(S37)

showing that the spectra is defined when
\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}
is even.

Further, the function
\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right)^{|\ell|}
(with
\ell=\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}
) is a simple geometric sequence resulting in the convergent series
\sum^{\infty}_{l=-\infty}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right)^{|l|}=\gamma
.

Importantly, equivalent results are obtained when considering the perspective from Alice and Bob’s reference frames, where Alice and Bob observe the photons from their reference frames, and it appears as though the photon states now experience the distortion. In that case, one solves the integral by applying a Lorentz boost to the Cartesian coordinate system of the joint two-photon wavefunction. This transforms the coordinates as,
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rightarrow(\gamma x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, assuming we have set
t^{\prime}=0
, resulting in

\displaystyle P_{\ell_{A}\ell_{B}}

\displaystyle\propto\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\underbrace{\exp\left(-r^{\prime 2}(\gamma^{2}\cos(\phi')^{2}+\sin(\phi')^{2})\right)}_{\text{entangled state}}\underbrace{\exp(-i\ell_A \phi')}_{\text{Alice}}\underbrace{\exp(-i\ell_B \phi')}_{\text{Bob}}r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}d\phi^{\prime},

(S38)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-r^{\prime 2}\left((\gamma^{2}-1)\cos(\phi')^{2}+1\right)\right)\exp\left(-i(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})\phi^{\prime}\right)r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}d\phi^{\prime}.

(S39)

This is an adaptation of the two-photon integral for SPDC, assuming that a Gaussian function defines the joint state in the near field,
\propto\exp(-r^2)
, written in normalised radial coordinates ”r” in Charlies reference frame. From Alice and Bob’s perspective, the two-photon state appears to be distorted due to their motion, such that the radial coordinate of the photons is mapped as
r=\sqrt{r^{\prime 2}(\gamma^{2}\cos(\phi')^{2}+\sin(\phi')^{2})}=r^{\prime}\sqrt{((\gamma^{2}-1)\cos(\phi')^{2}+1)}
. Next, we use the fact that

\frac{dr}{dr^{\prime}}=\sqrt{(\gamma^{2}-1)\cos(\phi')^{2}+1},

(S40)

so that the integral now becomes,

P_{\ell_{A},\ell_{B}}\propto\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-r\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}\exp\left(-i(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})\phi^{\prime}\right)}{(\gamma^{2}-1)\cos(\phi')^{2}+1}rdrd\phi^{\prime}

(S41)

The radial part of the integral evaluates to a constant, while the azimuthal part can be solved using the same means as before and will result in the same solution.

III
Number of contributing modes

Since the detection probabilities are a function of
\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}
, we study the spread given a Lorentz factor by fixing
\ell_{A}
and studying the detection probabilities with respect to
\ell_{B}
. Therefore, the conditional probabilities become

P_{\ell_{B}|\ell_{A}}=\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right)^{|\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}|}\cos^{2}(\left(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B}\right)\frac{\pi}{2}).

(S42)

Therefore, the modal spread is quantified using the formula

\displaystyle\Omega_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sum_{\ell_{B}}P_{\ell_{B}|\ell_{A}}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{\ell_{B}}P^{2}_{\ell_{B}|\ell_{A}}},

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\gamma^{2})^{3}}{\gamma(1+6\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{4})},

(S43)

and similarly for
\Omega_{A}.

This is commonly used to estimate the number of effective modes in a given distribution
[
46
]
. For example, when
\gamma=1
, the expression yields
\Omega_{B}=1
, meaning that Bob detects a spectrum that effectively has one contributing mode when Alice projects onto any given mode. However, for
\gamma=10
, Bob will see a spectrum that, on average, has
\Omega_{A,B}\approx 10
modes.

Alternatively, the spread can be characterised via the standard definition of uncertainty,
\sqrt{\langle\ell^{2}_{B}\rangle-\langle\ell_{B}\rangle^{2}}
, yielding the expression

\frac{\gamma-\gamma^{-1}}{\sqrt{2}}
, when the measurement detection events are normalised, capturing the width observed by Bob when Alice projects onto a single OAM mode.

IV
Experimental setup details

We emulated the experiment using photons generated through SPDC. A pump beam of wavelength
\lambda_{p}=532
nm was used to generate collinear, vertically polarised photon pairs at wavelengths
\lambda_{1}=810
nm and
\lambda_{2}=1550
nm using a temperature‐controlled ppKTP crystal. The down-converted photons were imaged onto PLUTO-NIR and PLUTO-TELCO SLMs for OAM projections, and thereafter coupled into single‐mode fibers and subsequently detected using APDs at each wavelength, enabling coincidence measurements. The joint probability spectra were obtained by performing OAM projections using the SLMs and single-mode fibers that together served as our effective, contracted detectors.

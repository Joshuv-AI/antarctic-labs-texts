Influence of thickness on magnetic properties of RF-sputtered amorphous CoNbZr thin films
Indujan Sivanesarajah, Leon Abelmann, Uwe Hartmann

Abstract
Amorphous sputtered Co-based thin films are widely used as soft magnetic materials in applications such as sensors, inductors and magnetic flux concentrators. The magnetic properties of these films can be controlled by deposition parameters like film thickness, argon pressure, deposition rate and others. In this study, we present a detailed investigation of the magnetic properties of RF-sputtered Co 91 Nb 7 Zr 2 films with thicknesses ranging from 52 nm 52\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} to 1040 nm 1040\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} . These amorphous films exhibit an average saturation magnetisation of 1.01 ​ ( 4 ) MA / m 1.01(4)\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m} . As the film thickness increases, there is a significant decrease in coercivity, remanent-to-saturation magnetisation ratio M r / M s M_{\mathrm{r}}/M_{\mathrm{s}} , and maximum permeability. The change in macroscopic magnetic properties is also reflected by the domain structure. At a thickness of 52 nm 52\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} , the remanent domain state shows irregular domains, while films thicknesses above 208 nm 208\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} exhibit flux-closure domain structures instead. The thickness-dependent modifications are attributed to the transition between Néel and Bloch type domain walls, which is expected to occur at approximately 84 nm 84\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} .

1
Introduction

Amorphous alloys have gained substantial prominence as soft magnetic materials
Shimada and Kojima [1982]
,
Sakakima [1983]
,
Herzer [2013]
. These materials find versatile applications, ranging from magnetic sensors
Meydan [1994]
,
Treutler [2001]
,
Morón et al. [2015]
and inductors
Korenivski [2000]
,
Ryłko et al. [2009]
,
Hsiang et al. [2022]
to motors
Hasegawa [2004]
,
Chai et al. [2020]
,
Enomoto et al. [2023]
. Of particular interest is CoNbZr, a specific variant of amorphous Co-based alloys, known for its high susceptibility, large saturation magnetisation, and low coercive field
de Wit et al. [1987]
,
Żuberek et al. [1992]
. These properties make CoNbZr an ideal choice for integrated magnetic flux concentrators, establishing it as a material of paramount importance in this area
Marinho et al. [2011]
,
He et al. [2018]
,
Maspero et al. [2021]
.

CoNbZr films are typically prepared by sputter deposition. However, the magnetic properties can be considerably influenced by details of the deposition process. For instance, different argon pressures can induce changes in the magnetic properties due to residual stress and morphology alterations, leading to the emergence of perpendicular magnetic anisotropy
Peng et al. [2013]
. Furthermore, Takahashi et al. revealed intriguing insights into how a compressed magnetic field magnetron sputtering technique affects key magnetic parameters
Takahashi et al. [1989]
.

Of particular interest is the pivotal role that the film thickness plays in determining the magnetic performance of CoNbZr films. Observations involve an inverse relationship between film thickness and coercive field
Li et al. [2007]
,
Cao and Zhou [2012]
,
Takahashi et al. [1989]
and an increasing anisotropy field with increasing film thickness
Katada et al. [2000]
. An increasing film thickness also leads to an intriguing in-plane uniaxial magnetic anisotropy
Cao and Zhou [2012]
.

However, despite extensive research in the field, a comprehensive understanding of how fine film thickness influences the magnetic properties in samples prepared through RF-magnetron sputtering is not yet existing. In particular, the intricate relationship between the film thickness and soft magnetic properties, such as permeability has not been thoroughly explored. This present study conducts an examination of the magnetic properties of RF-sputtered CoNbZr films at varying film thickness.

Materials and methods

CoNbZr films were deposited on Si - SiO
2

3

For magnetic domain structure analysis via magnetic force microscopy (MFM), CoNbZr films were patterned into

Magnetic characterisation

As evident from the hysteresis loops in Fig.

N_{x}

Aharoni [1998]

with increasing thickness. This rise in the demagnetisation field counteracts the external field, requiring thicker films to experience a higher external field to achieve the same level of magnetisation. The variation in the demagnetisation field also affects the remanent magnetisation
M_{\mathrm{r}}

Additionally, the contrast observed in the domain structure of Fig.

In thin films, surface effects strongly influence the total anisotropy, but as the film thickness increases, the volume anisotropy becomes dominant, reducing the overall impact of surface contributions.

4
Conclusion

We systematically investigated as-prepared amorphous CoNbZr thin films deposited on Si-SiO
2
wafers, varying in thickness from
52\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
to
1040\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
.

XRD spectra and TEM diffraction patterns confirmed that the CoNbZr films maintain their amorphous structure across all thicknesses. EDX analyses indicated consistent stoichiometry in the sputtered films, with compositions of Co
91±1
Nb
7±1
Zr
2±1
. The saturation magnetisation was found to be constant, with a mean value of
1.01(4)\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}
. Based on this saturation magnetisation and the film composition, we estimated that the magnetic moment per Co atom is approximately
60\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
lower than that of pure cobalt. This reduction is attributed to the amorphous nature of the Co matrix and the incorporation of Nb and Zr.

In contrast to the saturation magnetisation, the coercive field exhibits a significant reduction with increasing film thickness, decreasing by a factor of 10 to a value of
10\text{\,}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}
. Similarly, the ratio of remanent-to-saturation magnetisation decreases by a factor of 16, reaching a minimum value of 0.05. The maximum permeability number also decreases by a factor of 5 down to
4\text{⋅}{10}^{4}
.

MFM measurements revealed an irregular domain pattern with cross-tie walls at a thickness of
52\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
, while
208\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
thickness has shown a regular flux-closure diamond state in both, as-deposited and saturated films. This behaviour is attributed to the transition between Néel and Bloch type domain walls, which is expected to occur at approximately
84\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
.

These results offer valuable insights for the advancement of magnetic sensors based on the giant magnetoimpedance (GMI) effect. The observed trends indicate that sputtering a multilayer system of CoNbZr, with optimised interlayer and film thicknesses, can significantly enhance both the GMI ratio and sensitivity compared to a single-layer CoNbZr film of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
thickness, where the total magnetic thickness remains equivalent. This suggests that multilayer CoNbZr/interlayer thin films may outperform their single-layer counterparts in sensor applications.

Mean-field approach to finite-size fluctuations in the Kuramoto-Sakaguchi model
Oleh E. Omel’chenko, Georg A. Gottwald

Abstract
We develop an ab initio approach to describe the statistical behavior of finite-size fluctuations in the Kuramoto-Sakaguchi model. We obtain explicit expressions for the covariance function of fluctuations of the complex order parameter and determine the variance of its magnitude entirely in terms of the equation parameters. Our results rely on an explicit complex-valued formula for solutions of the Adler equation. We present analytical results for both the sub- and the super-critical case. Moreover, our framework does not require any prior knowledge about the structure of the partially synchronized state. We corroborate our results with numerical simulations of the full Kuramoto-Sakaguchi model. The proposed methodology is sufficiently general such that it can be applied to other interacting particle systems.

Synchronization is a generic phenomenon inherent in interacting oscillators

;

.
The main features of synchronization are described
by the paradigmatic Kuramoto-Sakaguchi (KS) model
40

\dot{\psi}_{i}=\omega_{i}+\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\psi_{j}-\psi_{i}-\lambda)

(1)

for the collective behavior of a system of
N
all-to-all coupled phase oscillators.
In this model,
\omega_{i}
are natural frequencies drawn randomly and independently
from a given distribution
g(\omega)
and
K
and
\lambda
are real parameters
quantifying the coupling strength and the phase lag, respectively.
It is often convenient to rewrite (
1
)
in the corotating frame
\theta_{i}=\psi_{i}-\Omega t
with a global rotation frequency
\Omega
as

\dot{\theta}_{i}=\omega_{i}-\Omega+K\>\mathrm{Im}\left(Z(t)e^{-i\theta_{i}}e^{-i\lambda}\right),

(2)

which reveals that each oscillator interacts
with the other oscillators only via the mean field.
Importantly, the degree of synchronization is quantified
by the mean field, also coined the complex order parameter,

Z(t)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}.

(3)

A fully synchronized state with
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=\dots=\theta_{N}
is characterized by
r(t)=|Z(t)|=1
,
whereas a completely disordered (incoherent) state is characterized by
r(t)\sim\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
. It is well known that for sufficiently large values of the coupling strength
K
the system becomes fully synchronized, whereas for sufficiently small values of
K
the oscillators behave independently and form an incoherent state. For intermediate values of the coupling strength, partially synchronized states are observed in which
r(t)
fluctuates around a constant value between
0
and
1
. On a microscopic level the oscillator population splits into two groups: coherent oscillators
\mathcal{C}
, which are phase locked, and rogue oscillators
\mathcal{R}
, which drift with respect to the mean field.

In the thermodynamic limit of infinitely many oscillators each oscillator interacts with the other oscillators via a constant collective mean. This limit is well understood and described by mean-field theory
40
;
31
;
32
;
33
. In particular, the rotation frequency of the coherent oscillators
\Omega_{\infty}
and the constant order parameter
r_{\infty}
are determined by a self-consistency relation

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle K}e^{i\lambda}=i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\Omega_{\infty}+Kr_{\infty}s)h(s)ds,

(4)

where

h(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(1-\sqrt{1-s^{-2}})s&\mbox{ for }&|s|>1\\[2.84526pt]
s-i\sqrt{1-s^{2}}&\mbox{ for }&|s|\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

(5)

;

In this Letter we will capture finite-size effects in a first principle way and provide explicit expressions for the covariance function and the variance of the order parameter. Our focus will be the hitherto unexplored parameter range away from criticality.

Recently, it was numerically recognized that fluctuations

\zeta(t)=\sqrt{N}(Z(t)-\langle Z(t)\rangle)

(6)

around the thermodynamic mean
\langle Z(t)\rangle
, where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes time averaging, approximately obey a Gaussian process
48
;
23
. Fluctuations in
Z(t)
, with a variance that decays as
1/N
and vanishes in the thermodynamic limit, lead to fluctuations in other macroscopic variables, including the order parameter with fluctuations

\delta(t)=\sqrt{N}(|Z(t)|-\langle|Z(t)|\rangle)=\sqrt{N}(r(t)-\langle r(t)\rangle).

(7)

Gaussian processes are entirely determined by their mean, accessible via classical mean-field theory, and their covariance function

R(\tau)=\langle\zeta(t)\overline{\zeta(t+\tau)}\rangle,

(8)

where the bar denotes the complex-conjugate. Once determined, this suggests to approximate the deterministic dynamics of the synchronized oscillators
\theta_{i}
, which are deterministically driven by
Z(t)
, by an effective stochastic equation for
\theta_{i}
, which is driven by a Gaussian process
16
. Previous work
48
;
23
has shown the efficacy of such an approach, however, these works relied on approximating the general Gaussian process by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process the parameters of which were estimated only numerically and needed to be recalculated for each set of equation parameters. Moreover, a precise a priori knowledge of which oscillators are synchronized and which are not was required.

In this Letter, we find explicit analytical expressions for the covariance function (
8
) and the variance of finite-size fluctuations of the order parameter
V=\langle\delta^{2}(t)\rangle
, given entirely in terms of equation parameters
K
,
\lambda
and

. We present expressions for both the partially synchronized and the completely incoherent state. Remarkably, our expressions do not require any a priori knowledge about which oscillators partake in the synchronized cluster and which do not.

Our main results can be formulated as follows. For partially synchronized states we predict

R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=Kr_{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\displaystyle(\overline{h^{2}(s)}-1)(1-|h^{2}(s)|)g(\Omega_{\infty}+Kr_{\infty}s)}{\displaystyle\overline{h^{2}(s)}-\exp(iKr_{\infty}s\sqrt{1-s^{-2}}\>\tau)}ds

(9)

and

V_{\mathrm{ps}}=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}R_{\mathrm{ps}}(0)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\quad N\gg 1.

(10)

For completely incoherent states we find

R_{\mathrm{incoh}}(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\omega)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\omega

(11)

and

\sqrt{N}\langle r(t)\rangle=\frac{\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}}{\displaystyle 2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad V_{\mathrm{incoh}}=1-\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle 4}\quad\mbox{for}\quad N\gg 1.

(12)

Covariance function for a partially synchronized state.

Our approach is based on the following prerequisites.
(i) Although seeking to quantify fluctuations of
Z(t)
,
we replace in Eq. (
2
) the mean-field
Z(t)

by its constant thermodynamic value
Z_{\infty}
,
and we move into the corotating frame

\theta_{i}(t)=\psi_{i}(t)-\Omega_{\infty}t+\phi\quad\mbox{with some}\quad\phi\in\mathbb{R}.

(13)

Moreover, we assume
\langle Z(t)\rangle=Z_{\infty}
.
(ii) We use the well-known fact (see Appendix for details)
that the probability density of the solution to Eq. (
2
) with
Z(t)=Z_{\infty}

is given by the Ott-Antonson ansatz
33

f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega)=\frac{\displaystyle g(\omega)}{\displaystyle 2\pi}\left\{1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\overline{z}_{\phi}^{n}(\omega)e^{in\theta}+z_{\phi}^{n}(\omega)e^{-in\theta}\right]\right\},

(14)

where

z_{\phi}(\omega)=e^{i\phi}h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\omega-\Omega_{\infty}}{\displaystyle Kr_{\infty}}\right).

(15)

Then, for every non-negative integer
n
we have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega)}{\displaystyle g(\omega)}e^{in\theta}d\theta=z_{\phi}^{n}(\omega),

(16)

which, upon employing (
3
) and (
4
), implies

Z_{\infty}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\int_{0}^{2\pi}f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega)e^{i\theta}d\theta=-r_{\infty}ie^{i\lambda}e^{i\phi}.

Moreover, due to the ergodicity property, for any
2\pi
-periodic function
F(\theta)
we have

\langle F(\theta_{j}(t))\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega_{j})}{\displaystyle g(\omega_{j})}F(\theta)d\theta.

(17)

(iii) Finally, we establish an explicit solution of the thermodynamic limit of (
2
)
with constant driver
Z(t)=Z_{\infty}
, the so called Adler equation.
Introducing
\Delta_{j}=\omega_{j}-\Omega_{\infty}
and
H_{\infty}=KZ_{\infty}e^{-i\lambda}
,
solutions of the Adler equation
\theta_{j}(t)
with initial conditions
e^{i\theta_{j}(0)}

are given for
|\Delta_{j}|\neq|H_{\infty}|
by the explicit formula

e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}=W(t,H_{\infty},\Delta_{j},e^{i\theta_{j}(0)}),

(18)

with

W(t,H,\Delta,\sigma)=Ha_{1}(t)+\frac{\displaystyle a_{2}(t)\sigma}{\displaystyle 1+\overline{H}a_{1}(t)\sigma},

(19)

where

a_{1}(t)=i(e^{i\xi t}-1)/\chi_{1}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{2}(t)=4\xi^{2}e^{i\xi t}/\chi_{1}^{2}(t)

with
\chi_{1}(t)=(\Delta-\xi)e^{i\xi t}-(\Delta+\xi)
and

\xi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}-i\sqrt{|H|^{2}-\Delta^{2}}&\mbox{ for }&|\Delta|\leq|H|,\\[5.69054pt]
\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-|H|^{2}}&\mbox{ for }&|\Delta|>|H|.\end{array}\right.

(20)

Note that for
|\Delta_{j}|<|H_{\infty}|
, the
j
th oscillator is coherent
and the dynamics of
\theta_{j}(t)
converges to a fixed point as
t\to\infty
,
while for
|\Delta_{j}|>|H_{\infty}|
, the oscillator is incoherent with underlying periodic dynamics.
Details on the derivation of (
18
), (
19
) are given in the Appendix.

The covariance function (
8
) can be expressed in terms of the fluctuations (
6
) as

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\langle Z(t)\overline{Z(t+\tau)}\rangle-N|Z_{\infty}|^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{k}(t+\tau)}\rangle-N|Z_{\infty}|^{2}.

It is easy to see that if
j\in\mathcal{C}
or
k\in\mathcal{C}
, the summands can be written as

\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{k}(t+\tau)}\rangle=\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle\langle e^{-i\theta_{k}(t)}\rangle.

(22)

Moreover, assuming that the variables
e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}
and
e^{i\theta_{k}(t)}

with
j,k\in\mathcal{R}
and
j\neq k
are independent,
and therefore identity (
22
) holds,
the sum in (
21
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{k}(t+\tau)}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle\langle e^{-i\theta_{k}(t)}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}\rangle-\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\left|\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle\right|^{2}.

Substitution in (
21
) yields, upon using (
6
),

R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}\rangle-\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\left|\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle\right|^{2}.

(23)

Now, using (
17
) to replace temporal averages by averages over the phases and the explicit formula (
18
) for
e^{i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{\phi}(\omega_{j}),

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{-i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\theta}\overline{W}(\tau,H_{\infty},\Delta_{j},e^{i\theta})\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega_{j})}{\displaystyle g(\omega_{j})}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\overline{Ha_{1}(\tau)}}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})}}(|z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})|^{2}-1)+1.

(Note that for the second correlation, we used identity (
16
)
after expanding the solution (
18
)–(
19
) in a geometric series.
Details of the calculations can be found in the Appendix.)
Altogether, this yields

R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\overline{Ha_{1}(\tau)}}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})}}-1\right]\left(|z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})|^{2}-1\right).

Finally, in the thermodynamic limit, averaging over indices
j

can be replaced by averaging over the distribution
g(\omega)
,
so we can write

R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\overline{Ha_{1}(\tau)}}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega)}}-1\right]\left(|z_{\phi}(\omega)|^{2}-1\right)g(\omega)d\omega.

(24)

After some algebraic manipulations (see Appendix), the covariance function (
24
) simplifies to our expression (
9
). Note that if
|\omega-\Omega|\leq|H_{\infty}|=Kr_{\infty}
,
|z_{\phi}|=1
. Therefore such values
\omega
do not contribute to the integral
R_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)
. This has the desirable consequence that in order to evaluate the covariance function, no prior knowledge is required about which oscillators are and which are not synchronized.

Covariance function for a completely incoherent state.

Our analysis of the completely incoherent state is based on the following prerequisites.
(i) In this state, the phases
\psi_{j}
tend to be uniformly distributed on the interval
[0,2\pi]
,
and their probability density in the thermodynamic limit is given by

f_{\mathrm{incoh}}(\psi,\omega,t)=\frac{\displaystyle g(\omega)}{\displaystyle 2\pi}.

(25)

(ii) The global order parameter shows no collective oscillations, and
Z(t)=0
as
N\to\infty
. This implies that the dynamics of each oscillator is approximately described by
\dot{\psi}_{i}=\omega_{i}
,
and therefore
\psi_{i}(t)=\omega_{i}t+\phi
with
\phi=\psi_{i}(0)
.

The covariance function for fluctuations
\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)
of this incoherent state is then given by

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{incoh}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t+\tau)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\left\langle e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}e^{-i\psi_{k}(t+\tau)}\right\rangle-N|\langle Z(t)\rangle|^{2}.

For randomly chosen initial conditions
\psi_{i}(0)
the variables
e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}
and
e^{i\psi_{k}(t)}
with
j\neq k
are independent, which implies again an identity of the form (
22
), and we obtain

R_{\mathrm{incoh}}(\tau)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\langle e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}e^{-i\psi_{j}(t+\tau)}\right\rangle-\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left|\langle e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}\rangle\right|^{2}.

Moreover, using the ergodicity property and the stationary density for incoherent oscillators (
25
), we find

\langle e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\psi}\frac{\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{incoh}}(\psi,\omega,t)}{\displaystyle g(\omega)}d\psi=0

(26)

and

\langle e^{i\psi_{j}(t)}e^{-i\psi_{j}(t+\tau)}\rangle=e^{-i\omega_{j}\tau}.

Finally, in the large-
N
limit we can replace averaging over indices
j

by averaging over the distribution
g(\omega)

and arrive at our expression for the covariance function
R_{\rm{incoh}}
(
11
).
Note that due to the normalization condition for
g(\omega)
, we have

\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|^{2}\rangle=R_{\mathrm{incoh}}(0)=1
.

Variance of the order parameter for a partially synchronized state.

Rewriting (
6
) as

Z(t)=\langle Z(t)\rangle+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\zeta(t)=Z_{\infty}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\zeta(t)

implies

|Z(t)|^{2}=|Z_{\infty}|^{2}+\frac{\displaystyle 2}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\mathrm{Re}\>\left(\overline{Z_{\infty}}\zeta(t)\right)+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}|\zeta(t)|^{2},

(27)

which upon expansion for
N\gg 1
becomes

|Z(t)|=|Z_{\infty}|+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}|Z_{\infty}|}\mathrm{Re}\>\left(\overline{Z_{\infty}}\zeta(t)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\right).

This allows to express the fluctuations of the order parameter (
7
) as

\delta(t)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle|Z_{\infty}|}\mathrm{Re}\>\left(\overline{Z_{\infty}}\zeta(t)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\right),

and the variance of the order parameter becomes

V_{\mathrm{ps}}=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\mathrm{Re}\>\left(\frac{\displaystyle\overline{Z_{\infty}^{2}}}{\displaystyle|Z_{\infty}|^{2}}\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ps}}(0)\right)+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}R_{\mathrm{ps}}(0)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\right),

where
\tilde{R}_{\rm{ps}}(t)
denotes the pseudo-covariance
\tilde{R}_{\rm{ps}}(t)=\langle\zeta(t)\zeta(t+\tau)\rangle
. Employing calculations similar to those for
R_{\mathrm{ps}}(t)
we can show that
\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=0
for all
\tau\geq 0
. The vanishing of the pseudo-covariance can be intuitively understood by evoking a symmetry between the real and imaginary parts of the fluctuations
\zeta
and their independence. Using
\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=0
for all
\tau\geq 0
, leads to our result (
10
).

Variance of the order parameter for a completely incoherent state.

Consider fluctuations
\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)
of the complex order parameter in the incoherent state.
In the large-
N
limit, for a completely incoherent state we have
\langle Z(t)\rangle=0
,
and (
27
) reduces to

|Z(t)|=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|.

Using the definition of fluctuations (
6
), we write

\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\cos\psi_{j}(t)+\frac{\displaystyle i}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sin\psi_{j}(t).

In the completely incoherent state all phases
\psi_{j}(t)
are uniformly distributed in
[0,2\pi]
. This implies that in the large-
N
limit fluctuations satisfy a central limit theorem, and
\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)
is a two-dimensional random vector
distributed according to a bivariate normal distribution. The mean and variances are readily determined. For each
t\geq 0
the function
\cos\psi_{j}(t)
is a random variable with zero expected value
\langle\cos\psi_{j}\rangle_{\mathrm{ens}}=0
and variance

\left\langle\cos^{2}\psi_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{ens}}=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}\psi\>d\psi=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{ens}}
denotes the ensemble average. Similarly,
\sin\psi_{j}(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
. Moreover, it is easy to verify that the random variables
\cos\psi_{j}(t)
and
\sin\psi_{j}(t)
are uncorrelated, since

\langle\cos\psi_{j}\sin\psi_{j}\rangle_{\mathrm{ens}}=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\psi\sin\psi\>d\psi=0.

Hence
\mathrm{Re}\>\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
and
\mathrm{Im}\>\>\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
, and
\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|
has a Rayleigh distribution with expected value

\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|\rangle=\sqrt{\pi}/2

and variance

V_{\mathrm{incoh}}=\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|^{2}\rangle-\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|\rangle^{2}=1-\pi/4.

Note that the value
\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|^{2}\rangle=1
, which follows from the last two identities, agrees with the value
\langle\left|\zeta_{\mathrm{incoh}}(t)\right|^{2}\rangle=R_{\mathrm{incoh}}(0)
obtained above.

Discussion and outlook.

In this Letter, we have proposed a fully analytical approach
to capture the statistical behavior of finite-size fluctuations
in the KS model (
1
).
In particular, our approach provides

(i) expressions for the covariance function of fluctuations of the complex order parameter
and the variance of the order parameter entirely in terms of the KS model parameters,

(ii) expressions for both sub- and super-critical coupling strengths,

(iii) expressions that do not require prior knowledge
about which oscillators are synchronized and which are not.

The results obtained were verified with simulations for the full KS model
and were shown to perform well away from criticality.
On the other hand, our approximations were less accurate
when approaching the critical coupling strength
K_{\rm{crit}}\approx 1.92

demarcating the transition from a desynchronized to a partially synchronized state.
It seems that near criticality, long-range correlations cannot be neglected
and our independence assumptions cease to be valid.
We envisage extensions of our framework to the near critical case
using a perturbative approach around the expressions derived in this work.

Our approach is sufficiently general to allow for further applications
to other interacting particle systems such as
\theta
-neuron models
and more complex network topologies going beyond the all-to-all coupling considered in this Letter.
The mathematical tools developed here can potentially be adapted
to study fluctuations in coupled oscillator systems
in the presence of noise
29
;
27
;
18
;
38
;
11
;
49

and finite-size corrections of Lyapunov exponents
5
.

In summary, our results fully specify the statistical behavior
of finite-size fluctuations in the KS model
which have been numerically shown to be Gaussian processes.
This opens up the way to perform systematic stochastic model reductions
for such interacting particle systems from first principles
and to construct reduced stochastic equations
for designated collective variables
17
;
41
.

Appendix: Derivations of equations and additional results

Thermodynamic limit for the KS model (
1
).

Most of the dynamical states found in the KS model (
1
) with finite
N
, have complex chaotic behavior. However, in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
, they admit a simple analytic representation. If
N\gg 1
, the state of the phase oscillators
\{\psi_{i}(t)\}
can be described by a probability density function
f(\psi,\omega,t)
, which obeys the continuity equation

\frac{\displaystyle\partial f}{\displaystyle\partial t}+\frac{\displaystyle\partial}{\displaystyle\partial\psi}(fv)=0,

(28)

where

v(\psi,\omega,t)=\omega+\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2i}\left[e^{-i\lambda}\mathcal{Z}(t)e^{-i\psi}-e^{i\lambda}\overline{\mathcal{Z}}(t)e^{i\psi}\right]

is the continuum version of the velocity field in Eq. (
1
), and

\mathcal{Z}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\psi,\omega,t)e^{i\psi}d\psi

(29)

is the continuum version of the complex order parameter (see formula (
3
) with
\Omega=0
).

It is well-known
33
;
34
that the long-term dynamics of the continuity equation (
28
) asymptotically approaches the Ott-Antonsen manifold for
t\to\infty
, consisting of distributions of the form

f(\psi,\omega,t)=\frac{\displaystyle g(\omega)}{\displaystyle 2\pi}\left\{1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\overline{z}^{n}(\omega,t)e^{in\psi}+z^{n}(\omega,t)e^{-in\psi}\right]\right\},

where
z(\omega,t)
satisfies the inequality
|z|\leq 1

and solves the integro-differential equation

\frac{\displaystyle\partial z}{\displaystyle\partial t}=i\omega z(\omega,t)+\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}\mathcal{G}z-\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{i\lambda}z^{2}(\omega,t)\mathcal{G}\overline{z}

(30)

with the integral operator

(\mathcal{G}z)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\omega)z(\omega,t)d\omega.

Remarkably, all statistically stationary states of system (
1
) lie on the Ott-Antonsen manifold
31
;
32
. In particular, the zero solution
z(\omega,t)=0
of Eq. (
30
) corresponds to the completely incoherent state in (
1
), while all stationary partially synchronized states in (
1
) are represented by rotating waves

z(\omega,t)=h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\omega-\Omega_{\infty}}{\displaystyle Kr_{\infty}}\right)e^{i\Omega_{\infty}t}

(31)

where
h(s)
is defined by (
5
) and
(r_{\infty},\Omega_{\infty})\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
is a pair of numbers satisfying the self-consistency equation (
4
).

The physical meaning of the parameters
r_{\infty}
and
\Omega_{\infty}
can be understood by inserting the probability density
f(\psi,\omega,t)
corresponding to (
31
) into Eq. (
29
). This yields

\mathcal{Z}(t)=-r_{\infty}ie^{i\lambda}e^{i\Omega_{\infty}t},

(32)

and therefore

|\mathcal{Z}(t)|=r_{\infty}

(33)

is the magnitude of the order parameter, while
\Omega_{\infty}
is the angular speed of its phase.

If in the KS model (
1
) we move into the corotating frame of reference (
13
), the probability density function in the new frame will take on the time-independent form (
14
), which is used for the calculations in the main text.

Explicit solutions of the Adler equation.

Let us consider the Adler equation written in complex form,

\dot{\theta}=\Delta+\mathrm{Im}\>\left(He^{-i\theta}\right)=\Delta+\frac{\displaystyle He^{-i\theta}-\overline{H}e^{i\theta}}{\displaystyle 2i}

(34)

with arbitrary
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
H\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 1

If
|\Delta|\neq|H|
, the solution of the Adler equation (
34
) is of the form

e^{i\theta(t)}=W(t,H,\Delta,e^{i\theta(0)}):=\frac{\displaystyle iH(e^{i\xi t}-1)-\chi_{2}(t)e^{i\theta(0)}}{\displaystyle i\overline{H}(e^{i\xi t}-1)e^{i\theta(0)}+\chi_{1}(t)},

where
\xi
is defined in Eq. (
20
),
and

\chi_{1}(t)=(\Delta-\xi)e^{i\xi t}-(\Delta+\xi),\quad\chi_{2}(t)=(\Delta+\xi)e^{i\xi t}-(\Delta-\xi).

In addition, in the degenerate case
|\Delta|=|H|
, the solution of the Adler equation (
34
) is given by

e^{i\theta(t)}=W_{0}(t,H,\Delta,e^{i\theta(0)}):=z_{*}+\frac{\displaystyle e^{i\theta(0)}-z_{*}}{\displaystyle 1+(e^{i\theta(0)}-z_{*})\overline{H}t/2},

where
z_{*}=i\Delta/H
. (For brevity of notations, we do not specify that
\xi
,
\chi_{1}(t)
,
\chi_{2}(t)
and
z_{*}
depend on
\Delta
and
H
.)

Proof:

Multiplying Eq. (
34
) by
ie^{i\theta}
,
we obtain a complex differential equation for
z=e^{i\theta}
:

\dot{z}=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}H+i\Delta z-\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\overline{H}z^{2}.

(35)

For
|\Delta|\neq|H|
, the right-hand side of this equation can be written as

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}H+i\Delta z-\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\overline{H}z^{2}=-\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\overline{H}(z-z_{+})(z-z_{-}),

where
z_{\pm}=i(\Delta\pm\xi)/\overline{H}
.
Using the method of separation of variables and the initial condition
z(0)=\sigma
, we obtain

z(t)=\frac{\displaystyle z_{+}(\sigma-z_{-})e^{i\xi t}-z_{-}(\sigma-z_{+})}{\displaystyle(\sigma-z_{-})e^{i\xi t}-(\sigma-z_{+})},

or equivalently
e^{i\theta(t)}=W(t,H,\Delta,e^{i\theta(0)})
.

In the degenerate case
|\Delta|=|H|
, we have
z_{+}=z_{-}=z_{*}
. Integrating Eq. (
35
), we obtain
e^{i\theta(t)}=W_{0}(t,H,\Delta,e^{i\theta(0)})
.

Remark 1

In the case
|\Delta|\neq|H|
, we can write

W(t,H,\Delta,\sigma)=Ha_{1}(t)+\frac{\displaystyle a_{2}(t)\sigma}{\displaystyle 1+\overline{H}a_{1}(t)\sigma},

with

a_{1}(t)=\frac{\displaystyle i(e^{i\xi t}-1)}{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{2}(t)=\frac{\displaystyle 4\xi^{2}e^{i\xi t}}{\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{2}(t)}.

Moreover, in this case, we have
|Ha_{1}(t)|<1
, and hence

W(t,H,\Delta,\sigma)=Ha_{1}(t)+a_{2}(t)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\overline{H}^{n}a_{1}^{n}(t)\sigma^{n+1}

is an absolutely convergent series for
|\sigma|\leq 1
.

Remark 2

If
(r_{\infty},\Omega_{\infty})\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy
the self-consistency equation (
4
), it can be easily verified
that
z_{\varphi}(\omega)
defined by (
15
) is a fixed point
of the Adler equation (
34
) with
\Delta=\omega-\Omega_{\infty}

and
H=-iKr_{\infty}e^{i\phi}
. Therefore,

W\left(t,-iKr_{\infty}e^{i\phi},\omega-\Omega_{\infty},z_{\phi}(\omega)\right)=z_{\phi}(\omega)

for all
t,\omega,\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 3

Under the assumptions of Remark
2
,
using Remark
1
and formula (
16
), we can show

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\theta}\overline{W}(t,H,\Delta,e^{i\theta})\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega)}{\displaystyle g(\omega)}d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[\overline{Ha_{1}(t)}e^{i\theta}\vphantom{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\overline{a_{2}(t)}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}H^{n}\overline{a_{1}^{n}(t)}e^{-in\theta}\right]\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega)}{\displaystyle g(\omega)}d\theta

\displaystyle=\overline{Ha_{1}(t)}z_{\phi}(\omega)+\overline{a_{2}(t)}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}H^{n}\overline{a_{1}^{n}(t)}\overline{z}_{\phi}^{n}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\overline{Ha_{1}(t)}\left(z_{\phi}(\omega)-\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega)}}\right)+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega)}}\overline{W(t,H,\Delta,z_{\phi}(\omega))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\overline{Ha_{1}(t)}}{\displaystyle\overline{z_{\phi}(\omega)}}(|z_{\phi}(\omega)|^{2}-1)+1.

Derivation of Eq. (
9
) from Eq. (
24
).

Using (
5
) it is easy to verify that

|H|h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\Delta}{\displaystyle|H|}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Delta-\xi\>\mathrm{sgn}(\Delta)&\mbox{ for }&|\Delta|>|H|,\\[5.69054pt]
\Delta+\xi&\mbox{ for }&|\Delta|\leq|H|,\end{array}\right.

(36)

where
\xi
is given by (
20
).
Note that formula (
36
) holds for all
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
H\in\mathbb{C}
.

Now, we consider the integrand of Eq. (
24
)
recalling that
H=KZ_{\infty}e^{-i\lambda}=-iKr_{\infty}e^{i\phi}
.
Recognizing that in this case

\frac{\displaystyle H}{\displaystyle z_{\phi}(\omega)}=-\frac{\displaystyle iKr_{\infty}e^{i\phi}}{\displaystyle e^{i\phi}h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\omega-\Omega_{\infty}}{\displaystyle Kr_{\infty}}\right)}=-\frac{\displaystyle i|H|}{\displaystyle h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\Delta}{\displaystyle|H|}\right)}

does not depend on
\phi

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle Ha_{1}(\tau)}{\displaystyle z_{\phi}(\omega)}-1=-\frac{\displaystyle i|H|}{\displaystyle h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\Delta}{\displaystyle|H|}\right)}\frac{\displaystyle i(e^{i\xi t}-1)}{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t)}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle|H|^{2}}{\displaystyle|H|h\left(\frac{\displaystyle\Delta}{\displaystyle|H|}\right)}\frac{\displaystyle e^{i\xi t}-1}{\displaystyle(\Delta-\xi)e^{i\xi t}-(\Delta+\xi)}-1.

(37)

For
\Delta>|H|

\Delta^{2}-\xi^{2}=|H|^{2}

(

\frac{\displaystyle Ha_{1}(\tau)}{\displaystyle z_{\phi}(\omega)}-1=\frac{\displaystyle 1-h^{2}(\Delta/|H|)}{\displaystyle h^{2}(\Delta/|H|)-e^{-i\Delta\sqrt{1-|H|^{2}/\Delta^{2}}t}}.

Substituting into Eq. (

Pseudo-covariance function for a partially synchronized state.

Using the definition of the pseudo-covariance function
\tilde{R}_{\rm{ps}}(t)=\langle\zeta(t)\zeta(t+\tau)\rangle

and assuming that the variables
e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}
and
e^{i\theta_{k}(t)}
are independent for
j\neq k
,
by analogy with (
23

\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}\rangle-\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{R}}\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle^{2}.

Using the ergodicity property (
17
)
and performing calculations as in Remark
3
, we find

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}e^{i\theta_{j}(t+\tau)}\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\theta}W(\tau,H_{\infty},\Delta_{j},e^{i\theta})\frac{\displaystyle f_{\phi}(\theta,\omega_{j})}{\displaystyle g(\omega_{j})}d\theta

\displaystyle=z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})W(\tau,H_{\infty},\Delta_{j},z_{\phi}(\omega_{j}))=z_{\phi}^{2}(\omega_{j}).

Recalling that
\langle e^{i\theta_{j}(t)}\rangle=z_{\phi}(\omega_{j})
,
we obtain
\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\tau)=0
.
Note that this relation is valid regardless of the choice of
\phi

in the definition of the corotating frame (

Details of the numerical setup.

Given a distribution
g(\omega)
, we generate a set of
N
natural frequencies
\omega_{j}
according to

\int_{-\infty}^{\omega_{j}}g(\omega)d\omega=\frac{\displaystyle j}{\displaystyle N+1},\qquad j=1,\dots,N.

This equiprobable sampling avoids finite-size effects such as randomly clustered natural frequencies leading to small synchronized clusters with non-zero mean frequencies and their respective interactions
36
;
13
. For each pair of parameters
K
and
\lambda
, we perform simulations using a standard Runge-Kutta solver with constant time step

. We start from initial conditions chosen randomly from the interval
[0,2\pi]
, discard a transient period of
T_{\mathrm{transient}}=10^{5}
time units and use the subsequent interval of length
T_{\mathrm{max}}=10^{5}
time units to calculate statistical quantities such as means, variances, and covariances.

Daido’s theory.

In
7
, H. Daido proposed an analytical approach to approximate finite-size fluctuations
in the KS model (
1
) with
\lambda=0
.
In particular, for the subcritical case he obtained explicit expressions
for the covariance function and the variance of the complex order parameter.
We follow here his approach and determine expressions for
\lambda\neq 0

in the subcritical case, and show that unlike for a Lorentz distribution of the natural frequencies
this method is not well suited for a Gaussian frequency distribution.

Let us assume that the dynamics of the
i
th oscillator can be represented as

\psi_{i}(t)=\Theta_{i}(t)+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\vartheta_{i}(t),

(38)

where
\Theta_{i},\vartheta_{i}=\mathcal{O}(1)
for
N\to\infty
.
Upon substitution into Eq. (
2
) with
\Omega=0
in the subcritical case, we obtain

\dot{\Theta}_{i}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\dot{\vartheta}_{i}=\omega_{i}+K\>\mathrm{Im}\left(Z(t)e^{-i(\Theta_{i}+\vartheta_{i}/\sqrt{N})}e^{-i\lambda}\right).

Next, assuming that
\langle Z(t)\rangle=0
and hence
Z(t)=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
,
and equating separately the terms of order
\mathcal{O}(1)

and the terms of order
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
, we obtain

\dot{\Theta}_{i}=\omega_{i}

(39)

and

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\dot{\vartheta}_{i}=K\>\mathrm{Im}\left(Z(t)e^{-i\Theta_{i}}e^{-i\lambda}\right).

(40)

Eq. (
39
) is solved by
\Theta_{i}(t)=\omega_{i}t+\Theta_{i}(0)
.
Substituting this into Eq. (
40
), we obtain

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle\sqrt{N}}\dot{\vartheta}_{i}(t)=K\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Im}\left(Z(t^{\prime})e^{-i(\omega_{i}t^{\prime}+\Theta_{i}(0))}e^{-i\lambda}\right)dt^{\prime}

with
\vartheta_{i}(0)=0
.
Finally, substituting the expressions for the solution to Eqs. (
39
)
and (
40
) into (
38
)
and then substituting
\theta_{i}(t)=\psi_{i}(t)

into the definition of the complex order parameter (
3
),
we obtain a self-consistency equation for
Z(t)
:

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i(\omega_{i}t+\Theta_{i}(0))}\times

(41)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(1+iK\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Im}\left(Z(t^{\prime})e^{-i(\omega_{i}t^{\prime}+\Theta_{i}(0))}e^{-i\lambda}\right)dt^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i(\omega_{j}t+\Theta_{j}(0))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}Z(t^{\prime})\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{i\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{Z}(t^{\prime})\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i(\omega_{j}(t+t^{\prime})+2\Theta_{j}(0))}dt^{\prime},

(42)

where we employed a Taylor expansion of the exponential function for
|Z(t)|\ll 1
.

In the next step, we will obtain an asymptotic formula for
Z(t)
as
t\to\infty
.
For this, we first notice that the function

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i(\omega_{j}(t+t^{\prime})+2\Theta_{j}(0))}

is vanishing as
t\to\infty
and hence can be neglected.
Furthermore, for
N\gg 1
we have

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\omega_{j}t}\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\omega)e^{i\omega t}d\omega=\hat{g}(t).

Thus, the complex order parameter
Z(t)
given in (
42
)
is the solution of the following Volterra integral equation

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}e^{i(\omega_{j}t+\Theta_{j}(0))}

(43)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{g}(t-t^{\prime})Z(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

This equation can be solved using the Laplace transformation

\mathcal{L}\>:\>f(t)\mapsto F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}f(t)dt.

In particular, due to the convolution theorem, Eq. (
43
) we write

(\mathcal{L}Z)(s)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\displaystyle e^{i\Theta_{j}(0)}}{\displaystyle s-i\omega_{j}}+\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\hat{g})(s)(\mathcal{L}Z)(s),

and therefore obtain

(\mathcal{L}Z)(s)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\hat{g})(s)\right)^{-1}\frac{\displaystyle e^{i\Theta_{j}(0)}}{\displaystyle s-i\omega_{j}}.

Now, assuming that

Q(s):=1-\frac{\displaystyle K}{\displaystyle 2}e^{-i\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\hat{g})(s)\neq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 0

and that
1/Q(s)
is a meromorphic function in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)<0
that satisfies

\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\max\limits_{\pi/2\leq|\phi|\leq\pi}|1/Q(pe^{i\phi})|=0,

(44)

we apply the inverse Laplace transformation and find that the long-term asymptotics of
Z(t)
is given by

Z(t)=\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\displaystyle e^{i(\omega_{j}t+\Theta_{j}(0))}}{\displaystyle Q(i\omega_{j})}.

The covariance function of the complex order parameter
Z(t)
is now evaluated as

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{Daido}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\langle Z(t)\overline{Z(t+\tau)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\displaystyle e^{-i(\omega_{k}\tau+\Theta_{k}(0)-\Theta_{j}(0))}}{\displaystyle Q(i\omega_{j})\overline{Q(i\omega_{k})}}\langle e^{i(\omega_{j}-\omega_{k})t}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\displaystyle e^{-i\omega_{j}\tau}}{\displaystyle|Q(i\omega_{j})|^{2}}\approx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\omega)\frac{\displaystyle e^{-i\omega\tau}}{\displaystyle|Q(i\omega)|^{2}}d\omega,

where we used
\langle e^{i(\omega_{j}-\omega_{k})t}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
.
The variance of the order parameter
r(t)=|Z(t)|
in the subcritical case is then given by

V_{\mathrm{Daido}}=N\langle|Z(t)|^{2}\rangle-N\langle|Z(t)|\rangle^{2}=R_{\mathrm{Daido}}(0)-\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle 4},

(45)

where we used our result (
12
) for
\langle|Z(t)|\rangle
.

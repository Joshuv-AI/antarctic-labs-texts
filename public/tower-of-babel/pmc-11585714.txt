A comprehensive dataset for Bangladeshi dessert classification
Authors: Mushfiqur Rahman, Jahid Hasan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In the realm of culinary culture, classifying desserts presents an interesting challenge. Due to the complexity as well as the variety of dessert types across various regions. This study present a comprehensive dataset of dessert images that have been specially selected for Bangladeshi dessert classification. In this research, there is a thorough collection of high-quality pictures showing the vast range of traditional sweets from Bangladesh. The collection comprises a varied assortment of traditional Bangladeshi desserts, demonstrating the richness and diversity of the countryʼs culinary traditions. This study seeks to develop reliable disease recognition models through the inspection of different image processing techniques and deep learning algorithms such as MobileNet. Specifically, these models' performance is subjected to a rigorous evaluation process using a set of standardized evaluation metrics to achieve a 98 % overall test accuracy. This paper presents a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners interested in the domain of culinary image classification, particularly focusing on Bangladeshi desserts. The availability of such a dataset will foster advancements in machine learning techniques tailored to culinary applications, ultimately enriching culinary heritage preservation and promoting cultural diversity through technology.

Background

Creating a Bangladeshi dessert classification dataset involves gathering and arranging many high-quality pictures of various types of desserts available in Bangladesh. The cultural heritage of Bangladeshi desserts is a reflection of a rich history that has been influenced by regional diversity, trade, and colonial influences, resulting in distinct delicacies that represent community and tradition. Local ingredients and traditional recipes that have been handed down through generations are prominently featured in these delicacies, which are a significant part of social and religious celebrations [1,5]. The dataset of Bangladeshi delicacies can be effectively used to perform image classification and recognition tasks using machine learning and deep learning techniques [[2], [3], [4]]. This method not only improves automated food classification systems but also contributes to applications in cultural preservation and culinary education. This is important to know the diversity of Bangladesh's dessert culture and its rich culinary traditions. Like early in the fifteenth century Jilapi was created. A few decades ago, it was introduced to the residents of Dhakaiya people [6], The Bengali regionʼs Narsingdi District is where Nokshi Pitha first make. In British period, the people living in a the village near the Meghna River in that region used to make Nokshi Pitha using rice powder [7]. The Ghosh community of Tripura state, or Comilla, invented kshirbhog, a caramel confection, in the early 1800s. They did this by thickening milk with fire, then soaking small, dry “bhog” or rasagolla in it. This milkshake eventually gained the name Rasmalai [8]. Desserts are integral parts of Bangladeshi life. However, studying existing frameworks for dessert categorization can help organize datasets and identify types of desserts. Professionals in cookery, food historians as well as gastronomy literature therefore may be essential for this [9]. Moreover, the detection of diseases is made difficult by cultural diversity and variability, which present unique challenges. As such, the current investigation aims to create computer vision algorithms that specifically identify and classify desserts with precision [10]. The purpose of this research is to help preserve and promote the Bangladeshi culinary legacy. It preserves cultural identity by exposing traditional dessert recipes, ingredients used, and how they are prepared. The study attempts to be an important reference for researchers, historians, or educators who want to know about Bangladesh's cuisine. It allows for uncovering regional differences, tracing historical influences, and investigating socio-cultural values associated with Bangladeshi sweet dishes.

Data Description

This dataset contains a comprehensive collection of structured information on a variety of traditional Bangladeshi dessert. This collection contains specific information, such as the names of several desserts. This dataset contains a comprehensive collection of high-quality photographs that capture the diverse range of traditional desserts from Bangladesh. The collection contains a wide array of conventional Bangladeshi sweets, which highlight the opulence and diversity of the countryʼs culinary legacy [11,12]. This dataset has been collected in two parts: the first part consists of traditional Bangladeshi sweets, while the second part focuses on pithas. The sweets samples were been collected from local sweet shops and the pithas were collected from Nabanna festival of Bangladeshi village. The Nabanna festival is a traditional Bengali harvest celebration honoring the arrival of the new rice crop, usually celebrated in late November or early December. During this festival, a variety of handmade pithas are produced, utilizing newly harvested rice, date palm jaggery, and other seasonal ingredients. It is a time for community eating, celebrating agricultural customs, and sharing seasonal delicacies with family and neighbours [13].

In total, 4118 photos of Bangladeshi dessert leaves have been captured from local Sweet shop and house. There are 27 folders in dataset for 27 categories of Bangladeshi dessert. Each folder is named after a Bangladeshi dessert and that dessert image dataset is kept in that folder like Balosai(258), Cream Jam(138), Doi(150), Dudh Puli PItha (103), Faluda(140), Golap Jam (168), Hafsi Sondesh (144), Jilapi (103),Kach Chana(156), Kacha Golla(150), Kalojam(216), Kasmiri Chomchom (222), Lemon Barfi(198), Malaikari(216), Maoyaa Laddo(186), Nokshi Pitha(115), Pakon Pitha(100), Patishapta(100), Payesh(116), Rajvog(210), Ravogsai(174), Roshmalai (174), S.P.Chomchom(138), Sada Chomchom(119), Sajer Pitha(117), Shamok Pitha(100),Shemai(107).

In this dataset, there are no duplicate photos and low-quality or superfluous photos, and making sure the photos are accurately labelled and categorized. Classification methodology

Apply augmentation to the actual dataset and then split it into three parts, validation, testing, and training. The proportions of divisions are 80 %, 10 % and 10 % respectively in the order mentioned. In those parts, images are chosen randomly. These sections are chosen at random. It is worth mentioning that figurative 2 brings out the process of classification for better understanding of our task. For instance, we trained DenseNet, MobileNet, VGG16, VGG19 and Xception using the training dataset. After that evaluate the model performance through validation data set. Lastly, a testing dataset is used to test our model's accuracy after which fresh or unseen data can be tested by utilizing an independent test sample whose size we have varied throughout this study in order to assess how well the model adapts to such situations.

The proposed model is trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate 0.0001 and an input shape of (120, 120, 3). The batch size is set to 32 with 10 epochs, and a dropout rate 0.7 is applied. The loss function remains sparse categorical cross-entropy, while the activation function for the output layer is SoftMax. The weight initialization is Glorot uniform, and the ReLU activation function is used for the hidden layers.

Limitations

•Limited coverage of regional variations in a Bangladeshi dessert classification dataset can lead to inaccurate models. The dataset's comprehensiveness may be compromised by an underrepresentation of regional diversity in ingredients, nomenclature, and preparation approaches. This can impede the model's capacity to generalize to various regions.•Limitations in the quality and consistency of images for Bangladeshi dessert classification can result in poor model accuracy. It is more difficult for the model to extract essential features when the image is low-resolution, inadequately illuminated, or from inconsistent angles. This may result in the misclassification of certain image types or bias to them. In order to enhance the model's reliability and performance, it is imperative to guarantee consistent image quality.•Limited access to high-quality images, computational capacity, and data labeling expertise are among the resource constraints that must be addressed when developing a dataset for the classification of Bangladeshi desserts. Gathering disparate, well-annotated images from various regions can be expensive and time-consuming. Furthermore, the training of intricate models on extensive datasets may necessitate substantial computational resources, which may not be readily accessible. These constraints can impact the dataset's diversity and the model's overall efficacy.

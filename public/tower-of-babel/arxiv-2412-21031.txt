Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. The concept of the
homological shift ideal
was introduced in 2020 by Herzog
et al.

[
30
]
, although it first appeared implicitly in 2005 in the book by Miller and Sturmfels
[
37
, Theorem 2.18]
. In that theorem, using the language that we will explain shortly after, the authors proved that the first homological shift ideal of an equigenerated Borel ideal has a linear resolution.

Given
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we set
{\bf x^{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. The
i
th
homological shift ideal

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
of
I
is defined as the monomial ideal generated by the monomials
{\bf x^{a}}
such that
{\bf a}
is an
i
th
multigraded shift
of
I
. That is,

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}\ :\ \beta_{i,{\bf a}}(I)\neq 0),

where
\beta_{i,{\bf a}}(I)=\dim_{K}\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I)_{\bf a}
. It is clear that
\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
and
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=0
if
i\notin\{0,\dots,\textup{proj\,dim}(I)\}
. See also
[
4
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
31
,
35
,
38
]
.

The ideals
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
encode some of the information provided by the multigraded syzygies of
I
. It should be noted, however, that not all the multigraded shifts of
I
correspond to minimal generators of
I
. This fact does not hold if
I
has a linear resolution. This observation suggests that the algebraic properties of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
are closer to those enjoyed by
I
when
I
has a linear resolution. We say that
I
has
homological linear resolution
if
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
has linear resolution for all
i
.

In this vein, it was conjectured in 2012 by Bandari, Bayati and Herzog that the homological shift ideals of polymatroidal ideals are polymatroidal. Since polymatroidal ideals have a linear resolution, these ideals would constitute a family of monomial ideals with homological linear resolution. This conjecture is still open, however, it is supported by several partial results
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
19
,
30
]
.

In
[
12
,
13
]
, Crupi and the first author conjectured that the homological shift ideals of each power of the cover ideal of a Cohen-Macaulay very well-covered graph have a linear resolution, and they proved this in some special cases.

For a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
, let
\alpha(I)
denote the
initial degree
of
I
, that is, the smallest degree of a generator of
I
. It is known that the powers of polymatroidal ideals are polymatroidal
[
27
, Theorem 12.6.3]
. Thus, if the Bandari-Bayati-Herzog conjecture, or the Crupi-Ficarra conjecture is true, then for the monomial ideals
I\subset S
considered in these conjectures,
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
would have linear resolution for all
i
and
k\geq 1
. In particular, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
would be a linear function of the form
\alpha(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))=\alpha(I)k+i
, for all
k\gg 0
.

Thus, we were led to ask whether
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
becomes a linear function for
k\gg 0
for a monomial ideal
I\subset S
with
linear powers
. Recall that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
has
linear powers
if
I^{k}
has a linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
. In this paper, we show that this is indeed the case. This phenomenon arises due to the fact that the
K
-algebra

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

is a finitely generated bigraded module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)
of
I
, provided that
I
has linear powers. We call
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
the
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
. In this paper, we deeply study the homological shift algebras of a monomial ideal.

In Section
1
, we introduce the
i
th homological shift algebra of a monomial ideal
I
and investigate when this algebra has the structure of a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. For this aim, one must require that
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. We find examples of monomial ideals such that
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\not\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. On the other hand, we show in Theorem
1.2
that the opposite inclusion
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
holds for all
k\gg 0
. Under the assumption that
I
has linear powers, we prove in Theorem
1.4
that
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
it is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. In Proposition
1.5
, we determine some conditions on
I
that ensure that
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. We apply this result to edge ideals
I(G)
of graphs
G
whose induced odd cycles satisfy certain distance conditions. Hence, if
G
is bipartite or unicyclic, then
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Next, in Section
2
, we address the main question that motivated this paper in the first place. Namely, we prove in Theorem
2.1
that
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a linear function for all
k\gg 0
, provided that
I
has linear powers. Under the same assumption on
I
, we also show that the depth and the associated primes of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
stabilize and that the
v
-number of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is an eventually linear function for all
k\gg 0
. The
v
-number of a homogeneous ideal
I
is a new invariant introduced firstly in
[
11
]
which measures the homogeneous complexity of the primary decomposition of
I
. See also
[
10
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
and the references therein. It is proved in
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\textup{v}(I^{k})/k=\alpha(I)
for any graded ideal. Thus one could expect that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))/k=\alpha(I)
if
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\neq 0
for all
k\gg 0
. This equation holds for
i=0
or when
I
is equigenerated by
[
24
, Theorem 1.9(2)]
. To demonstrate our theory, we compute the asymptotic invariants of the homological shift algebras of some distinguished families of monomial ideals.

In Section
3
, we turn to the monomial ideals whose all powers have linear quotients. A powerful technique to show that an equigenerated monomial ideal
I\subset S
has linear resolution is to show that
I
has linear quotients
[
27
, Theorem 8.2.15]
. Herzog and the first author proved that if
I
is an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear quotients, then
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
has linear quotients, and so a linear resolution. It is an open question whether this result remains true if we only assume that
I
has linear resolution. Supporting this expectation, we prove in Theorem
3.8
that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
has linear relations if
I
has linear relations. This result cannot be extended to the higher homological shift ideals. Indeed, in Example
3.4
we present an equigenerated strongly stable ideal
I=B(x_{1}x_{2}x_{5},x_{3}^{3})
such that
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
has no linear relations, and therefore no linear resolution and no linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
. This ideal has two Borel generators and is generated in degree three. This example also shows that Proposition
2.4
, as well as the result of Miller and Sturmfels
[
37
, Theorem 2.18]
, cannot be extended to all strongly stable ideals. We say that
I
has
eventually homological linear powers
if
I
has linear powers and
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
has linear resolution for all
k\gg 0
. Not all monomial ideals with linear powers have eventually homological linear powers as Example
3.4
shows. Theorem
3.9
presents several classes of ideals with eventually homological linear powers.

Finally, in Section
4
, we consider the asymptotic Golodness of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, when
I
has linear powers. By an influential result of Herzog and Huneke
[
29
]
, the powers
I^{k}
of the graded ideal
I\subset S
are Golod from
k\geq 2
on. In view of this result, it is natural to expect that if
I
has linear powers, then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a Golod ideal for all
k\gg 0
. In Theorem
4.2
, we show that this is indeed the case. This result is a consequence of the more general Theorem
4.3
, which in turn is a generalization of a result of Herzog, Welker and Yassemi
[
34
]
.

1.
The homological shift algebra

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal and let
\mathcal{G}(I)
be its minimal monomial generating set. For systematic reasons, we set
I^{0}=S
. We want to investigate the asymptotic behavior of the homological invariants of the ideals
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for a fixed
i
and all
k\gg 0
. For this aim, we consider the following natural
K
-algebra. The
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
is the
K
-algebra defined as

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}).

Notice that
S\oplus\textup{HS}_{0}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is simply the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
of
I
. Our goal is to investigate under which conditions the
K
-algebra
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
has a natural structure of a bigraded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

We regard
\mathcal{R}(I)
as a bigraded ring with
\mathcal{R}(I)_{(d,k)}=(I^{k})_{d}
as
(d,k)
th bigraded component, and we put
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))_{(d,k)}=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})_{d}
.

For a bigraded
R
-module
M=\bigoplus_{d,k\geq 0}M_{(d,k)}
over a graded ring
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
, we set
M_{(*,k)}=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}M_{(d,k)}
.

Notice that
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module if and only if

\mathcal{R}(I)_{1}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))_{k}\ \subseteq\ \textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))_{k+1}

for all
k\geq 1
. So, we should have
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. Of course, this is not the case in general. In the next example, we present a monomial ideal
I
such that
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\not\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
and some
i
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Recall that the
socle
of
I\subset S
is the ideal

\textup{soc}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}\ :\ ((I:\mathfrak{m})/I)_{\bf a}\neq 0).

By
[
30
, Proposition 2.13]
we have
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I)=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\cdot\textup{soc}(I)
. In particular,
\textup{soc}(I)
is generated by all monomials
u\notin I
such that
x_{j}u\in I
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
I=(x^{2},y^{2},xyz)\subset K[x,y,z]
. Then
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})\not\subseteq\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
. Indeed,
u_{k}=x^{3}y^{2k+1}z^{2}\in(I\cdot\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k}))\setminus\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})=xyz\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
, it follows that
u_{k}\in I\cdot\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
if and only if

g_{k}=u_{k}/(xyz)=x^{2}y^{2k}z\in I\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
. Since
xyz\in I
, it is enough to show that
v_{k}=xy^{2k-1}\in\textup{soc}(I^{k})
. The only generator of
I
which divides
v_{k}
is
y^{2}
. Since
\deg_{y}(v_{k})=2k-1
, it follows that
v_{k}\notin I^{k}
. Now we show that
xv_{k},yv_{k},zv_{k}\in I^{k}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle xv_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ x^{2}y^{2(k-1)}\cdot y\in I^{k},

\displaystyle yv_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ y^{2k}\cdot x\in I^{k},

\displaystyle zv_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ (xyz)y^{2(k-1)}\in I^{k}.

Hence
v_{k}\in\textup{soc}(I^{k})
and so
u_{k}\in I\cdot\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
.

It remains to show that
u_{k}\notin\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k+1})
. Since
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k+1})=xyz\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})
, we must show that
g_{k}=x^{2}y^{2k}z\notin\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})
. For this aim, it is enough to show that

\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})\ =\ (x^{p}y^{q}\ :\ p+q=2(k+1),\ \textit{and $p$, $q$ are odd}).

(1)

We have
I:(x)=(x,y^{2},yz)
,
I:(y)=(x^{2},y,xz)
and
I:(z)=(x^{2},y^{2},xy)
. Hence
I:(z)
is contained in
I:(x)
and
I:(y)
. Recall that for a monomial ideal
J
and a variable
x_{i}
, we have
J^{k}:(x_{i})=J^{k-1}(J:(x_{i}))
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus
I^{k}:(z)
is a subset of
I^{k}:(x)
and
I^{k}:(y)
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,z)
. Then

I^{k}:\mathfrak{m}=(I^{k}:(x))\cap(I^{k}:(y))\cap(I^{k}:(z))=I^{k}:(z)=I^{k-1}(I:(z))=I^{k-1}(x,y)^{2}.

Notice that
I^{k}:\mathfrak{m}=I^{k-1}(x,y)^{2}
contains all monomials of degree
2k
in the variables
x,y
, and for any monomial
u\in(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})
with
z\in\textup{supp}(u)
, we have
\deg_{x}(u)+\deg_{y}(u)
at least
2k
. Hence any monomial of
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})
with
z\in\textup{supp}(u)
is not a minimal generator. Since
\alpha(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})=\alpha(I^{k-1}(x,y)^{2})=2k
, we see that
I^{k}:\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)^{2k}
and

(I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{k+1}=(x^{p}y^{q}+I^{k+1}\ :\ p+q=2(k+1),\ \textit{and p,q are odd}).

This shows that equation (
1
) indeed holds and so
g_{k}\notin\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})
.

∎

On the other hand, the opposite inclusion
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
holds for all
k\gg 0
, as we show next.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then,

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

Firstly, we notice that
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated bigraded module over
\mathcal{R}(I)\otimes_{S}K=\mathcal{R}(I)\otimes_{S}S/\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathcal{R}(I)/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{R}(I)
, with
(*,k)
th graded component

\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,\mathcal{R}(I))_{(*,k)}\cong\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,\mathcal{R}(I)_{(*,k)})\cong\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k}).

Since the ring
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\mathcal{R}(I)/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/\mathfrak{m}I^{k})
is standard graded, then

\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})\ =\ \mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)_{(*,1)}\cdot\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k})

(2)

for all
k\gg 0
(see also
[
21
, Proposition 2.8]
). Equation (
2
) implies that

\{{\bf a}\ :\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})_{\bf a}\neq 0\}\ \subseteq\ \{{\bf b+c}\ :\ (I/\mathfrak{m}I)_{\bf b}\neq 0,\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k})_{\bf c}\neq 0\}

for all
k\gg 0
. Since
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=({\bf x^{a}}:\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})_{\bf a}\neq 0)
, the above inclusion implies that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
, as desired.

∎

Example
1.1
shows that, in general, the inclusion
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is not an equality for all
k\gg 0
.

Nonetheless, if
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal with
eventually linear powers
, that is, if
I
is generated in a single degree and
I^{k}
has linear resolution for all
k\gg 0
, then

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with eventually linear powers. Then

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. To show the opposite inclusion, let
\ell
large enough such that
I^{k}
has linear resolution for all
k\geq\ell
, and let
{\bf x^{b}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
,
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
. We will show that
{\bf x^{a+b}}\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
.

The short exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\bf x^{b}}I^{k}\rightarrow I^{k+1}\rightarrow I^{k+1}/({\bf x^{b}}I^{k})\rightarrow 0

induces the exact sequence

\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i+1}(K,I^{k+1}/({\bf x^{b}}I^{k}))_{\bf a+b}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,{\bf x^{b}}I^{k})_{\bf a+b}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})_{\bf a+b}.

Let
d=\alpha(I)
. By assumption
I^{k}
and
I^{k+1}
have
dk
and
d(k+1)
-linear resolutions, respectively. In particular,
\alpha(I^{k+1})=d(k+1)
and so
\alpha(I^{k+1}/({\bf x^{b}}I^{k}))\geq d(k+1)
. It follows that
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i+1}(K,I^{k+1}/({\bf x^{b}}I^{k}))_{j}=0
for
j\leq d(k+1)+i
. Since
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
we have
|{\bf a}|=dk+i
. Moreover
|{\bf b}|=d
because
I
is equigenerated in degree
d
. Therefore
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i+1}(K,I^{k+1}/({\bf x^{b}}I^{k}))_{\bf a+b}=0
because
|{\bf a+b}|=d(k+1)+i
. Hence, we have an injective map

0\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,{\bf x^{b}}I^{k})_{\bf a+b}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})_{\bf a+b}.

Notice that
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,{\bf x^{b}}I^{k})_{\bf a+b}\cong\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k})_{\bf a}(-{\bf b})\neq 0
since
{\bf x^{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
. Hence
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I^{k+1})_{\bf a+b}\neq 0
as well, and this implies that
{\bf x^{a+b}}\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
.

∎

If
I
has linear powers, then the previous result implies that
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subseteq\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
, and by Theorem
1.2
equality holds for all
k\gg 0
. Hence, as an immediate consequence we have

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

We close this section by describing another situation which ensures that the equality
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
holds for all
k\gg 0
and some
i
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree
d
. Assume that the socle of
I^{k}
,
\textup{soc}(I^{k})
, is generated in degree
dk-1
for all
k\gg 0
. Then
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

Since, as we recalled before,
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k})=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
, it is enough to show that
\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. By Theorem
1.2
we have
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Hence
\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})\subseteq I\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. To show the opposite inclusion, let
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
v\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{soc}(I^{k}))
where
k
is large enough. Our assumptions ensure that
\deg(v)=dk-1
. Now
uv\in I(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})\subseteq(I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})
. Since
\deg(uv)=d(k+1)-1
, it follows that
uv\notin I^{k+1}
because
I^{k+1}
is equigenerated in degree
d(k+1)
. Hence
uv\in\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})
. This shows that
\textup{soc}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{soc}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
, as desired.

∎

We apply this result to edge ideals. Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the monomial ideal
I(G)\subset S
generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
for which
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on the vertex set
[n]
such that any two induced odd cycles of
G
have distance at most one, that is, there is an edge between two vertices of the odd cycles, or the odd cycles share a common vertex. Then
\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I(G)^{k+1})=I(G)\cdot\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

It is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 2.2]
that
\textup{soc}(I(G)^{k})
is equigenerated in degree
2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence, the assertion follows from Proposition
1.5
.

∎

A basic result in graph theory says that a graph is bipartite if and only if it does not contain any induced odd cycle. Therefore, the previous result applies to all bipartite graphs, as well as to all unicyclic graphs. That is, graphs containing only one induced cycle.

2.
Asymptotic behavior of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
be an associated prime ideal of
I
. Recall that the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
I
is

\textup{reg}(I)\ =\ \max\{j\ :\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I)_{i+j}\neq 0\},

the
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-number
of
I
is

\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \min\{\deg(f)\ :\ f\in S_{d},\ (I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\},

and the
v
-number
of
I
is

\textup{v}(I)\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)}\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. The following statements hold.

(a)

The set
\textup{Ass}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
stabilizes:
\textup{Ass}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}))=\textup{Ass}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
for
k\gg 0
. We denote the common sets
\textup{Ass}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
for
k\gg 0
by
\textup{Ass}^{\infty}_{i}(I)
.

(b)

For all
k\gg 0
, we have
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
.

(c)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
.

(d)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

(e)

For all
k\gg 0
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}_{i}(I)
,
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.4
,
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated bigraded module over the standard graded ring
\mathcal{R}(I)
with
\mathcal{R}(I)_{0}=S
. Thus, statement (a) is a consequence of
[
36
, Proposition 2]
. By
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\textup{depth}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))_{(*,k)}=\textup{depth}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is constant for all
k\gg 0
. Since
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=\textup{depth}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})-1
for all
k
, statement (b) follows. For the proof of the statements (c), (d) and (e), we recall that by Theorem
1.4
we have that

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ \mathcal{R}(I)_{(*,k-k_{0})}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})\ =\ I^{k-k_{0}}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})

(3)

for some
k_{0}>0
and all
k\geq k_{0}
. Set
M=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})
. Since
M
is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, then
[
39
, Theorem 3.2]
implies that
\textup{reg}(I^{k}M)
is an eventually linear function in
k
. From equation (
3
) we conclude that
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is an eventually linear function in
k
as well, and (c) follows. By
[
24
, Theorem 2.8 (2)-(3)]
it follows that
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k}M)
, for any prime
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I^{k}M)
for all
k\gg 0
, and
\textup{v}(I^{k}M)
, are eventually linear functions in
k
for all
k\gg 0
. Using again equation (
3
) we see that the statements (d) and (e) indeed hold.

∎

At the moment, we do not know whether Theorem
2.1
holds for any monomial ideal
I\subset S
.

The following result complements Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\textup{depth}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ \leq\ \dim\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))-\dim\frac{\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))}{\mathcal{R}(I)_{(*,1)}*\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))},

and equality holds if
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
1.2
and
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
.

∎

To illustrate the theory as developed thus far, we investigate the homological shift ideals of powers of three special families of monomial ideals.

2.1.
Monomial ideals in two variables

Let
S=K[x,y]
. The set of monomial ideals
I\subset S
is in bijection with the set of all pairs
({\bf a},{\bf b})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
satisfying

{\bf a}:a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m}\geq 0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\textup{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\bf b}:0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{m}.

(4)

Indeed, if
I
is a monomial ideal of
S
, then
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}},x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}},\dots,x^{a_{m}}y^{b_{m}}\}
for two sequences
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
as above. Conversely, if
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
are as in (
4
), then
x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}},x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}},\dots,x^{a_{m}}y^{b_{m}}

I\subset S
.

Hereafter, we set
I_{\bf a,b}=(x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}},x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}},\dots,x^{a_{m}}y^{b_{m}})
, where
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
are as in (
4
). Moreover, we put
\mathfrak{p}_{x}=(x)
,
\mathfrak{p}_{y}=(y)
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}
be a monomial ideal in two variables. Then,

(a)

\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
,
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=(x^{a_{j}}y^{b_{j+1}}:1\leq j\leq m-1)
and
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=0

.

(b)

We have

\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ \begin{cases}1&\textit{for all}\,\ k\geq 1,\,\ \textit{if}\ m=1,\\
0&\textit{for all}\,\ k\geq 2,\,\ \textit{if}\ m=2,\\
0&\textit{for all}\,\ k\geq 1,\,\ \textit{if}\ m\geq 3.\end{cases}

(c)

(c1)
For
i=0,1
, we have
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\textup{Ass}_{i}^{\infty}(I)
if and only if
a_{m}>0
.

(c2)
For
i=0,1
, we have
\mathfrak{p}_{y}\in\textup{Ass}_{i}^{\infty}(I)

.

(c3)
For

Proof.

Part (a) is known, for instance, see

∎

To compute

one can use
[

, Theorem 5.6]
.

2.2.
Principal Borel ideals

Recall that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
strongly stable
if for all
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and all
i<j
with
x_{j}
dividing
u
we have
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in I
. The smallest strongly stable containing
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
is denoted by
I=B(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
and the monomials
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
are called the
Borel generators
of
I
. If
I=B(u)
has only one Borel generator, then
I
is called a
principal Borel ideal
.

Given monomials
u,v\in S
, we set
u:v=u/\textup{gcd}(u,v)
. Thus
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):(u_{j})
is generated by the monomials
u_{i}:u_{j}
for
i=1,\dots,j-1
.

Recall that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
has
linear quotients
if there exists an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
such that the ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
are generated by variables for all
j=2,\dots,m
. We put
\textup{set}(u_{j})=\{i\ :\ x_{i}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}\}
for all
j
. If
I\subset S
is equigenerated and it has linear quotients, then it has linear resolution.

For a subset
F\subseteq[n]
we set
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
. In particular, we have
{\bf x}_{\emptyset}=1
. Then, by
[
33
, Lemma 1.5]
we have
\textup{proj\,dim}\,I=\max\{|\textup{set}(u)|:u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\}
and

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{F}u\ :\ u\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ F\subseteq\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=i).

It is not difficult to see that any strongly stable ideal
I
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order induced by
x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots>x_{n}
, and that for each
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
we have
\textup{set}(u)=\{1,\dots,\max(u)-1\}
, where
\max(u)=\max\{i\ :\ x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
I=B(u)
be a principal Borel ideal. For all
k\geq 1
, we have

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ (x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{i}}v\ :\ v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k}),\,1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}<\max(v)).

Moreover, for all
0\leq i<\max(u)
we have
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=\deg(u)k+i
for all
k\geq 1
, and
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=n-\max(u)
for all
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

It is easily seen that
B(u)^{k}=B(u^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus, the formula given for
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
follows from this observation and the previous discussion. In view of this fact, it is enough to consider the case
k=1
. In
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
, Bayati
et al.
proved that
\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u))
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order. From this observation, the formula for the regularity follows. To prove the formula for the depth, it is enough to show that
\textup{proj\,dim}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k}))=\max(u^{k})-1=\max(u)-1
for all
0\leq i<\max(u)
and all
k\geq 2
. Since
\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k}))
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order,
[
14
, Lemma 2]
gives
\textup{set}(v)\subseteq\{1,\dots,\max(v)-1\}
for all
v\in\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k}))
. This shows that
\textup{proj\,dim}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k}))\leq\max(u^{k})-1
. To prove the opposite inequality, we let
v=(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}x_{\max(u)-\ell})u^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k})))
and show that
\textup{set}(v)=\{1,\dots,\max(u)-1\}
. Let
j\in\textup{set}(v)
. If
j<\max(u)-i
, then
w=x_{j}(v/x_{\max(u)-1})=x_{j}(\prod_{\ell=2}^{i}x_{\max(u)-\ell})u^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k})))
is greater than
v
with respect to lex order. Thus,
w:v=x_{j}
and
j\in\textup{set}(v)
. Otherwise, if
j\geq\max(u)-i
, then
w=x_{j}(v/x_{\max(u)})=(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}x_{\max(u)-\ell})(x_{j}(u^{k}/x_{\max(u)}))\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(B(u^{k})))
since
k\geq 2
and so
x_{j}(u^{k}/x_{\max(u)})\in\mathcal{G}(B(u^{k}))
has maximum equal to
\max(u)
. Since
w
is greater than
v
with respect to lex order and
w:v=x_{j}
, we have
j\in\textup{set}(v)
.

∎

Given a monomial ideal
I
and an integer
j
, let
I_{\geq j}=(u\in\mathcal{G}(I):|\textup{supp}(u)|\geq j)
. Here, the
support
of a monomial
u\in S
is the set
\textup{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}\ :\ x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{k})=(\mathfrak{m}^{k+i})_{\geq i+1}
for all
k\geq 1
and all
0\leq i\leq n-1
. In particular,
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{k})=k+i
and
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{k})=0
.

Proof.

The assertion is immediate for
k=1
. For
k\geq 2
, notice that
\mathfrak{m}^{k}=B(x_{n}^{k})
and then apply Proposition
2.4
.

∎

Let
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. A monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
is called
{\bf c}
-bounded
if
a_{i}\leq c_{i}
for all
i
. A monomial ideal
I
is called
{\bf c}
-bounded strongly stable
, if each monomial in
\mathcal{G}(I)
is
{\bf c}
-bounded and for all
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and all integers
i<j
for which
x_{i}
divides
u
and
x_{i}(u/x_{j})
is
{\bf c}
-bounded, it follows that
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in I
. The smallest
{\bf c}
-bounded strongly stable ideal containing a
{\bf c}
-bounded monomial
u\in S
is called a
{\bf c}
-bounded principal Borel ideal
and is denoted by
B^{{\bf c}}(u)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any
{\bf c}
-bounded monomial
u
and
k\geq 1
, the ideal
\textup{HS}_{i}(B^{{\bf c}}(u)^{k})
has linear quotients.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
,
(B^{{\bf c}}(u))^{k}=B^{k{\bf c}}(u^{k})
. Moreover, by
[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
, we know that
\textup{HS}_{j}(B^{k{\bf c}}(u^{k}))
has linear quotients for all
j
, as required.

∎

2.3.
Monomial complete intersections

Recall that a sequence
{\bf f}:f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
in
S
is called a
regular sequence
if
f_{i}
is a non-zero divisor on
S/(f_{1},\dots,f_{i-1})
for all
i
.

Let
{\bf u}:u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
be a sequence of monomials of
S
. It is easily seen that
{\bf u}
is a regular sequence (for any order) if and only if
\textup{supp}(u_{i})\cap\textup{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

We put
{\bf u^{b}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}^{b_{i}}
and
|{\bf b}|=b_{1}+\dots+b_{m}
, for
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
. Moreover, we set
\max{\bf u^{b}}=\max\{i:b_{i}\neq 0\}
if
{\bf b}\neq{\bf 0}=(0,\dots,0)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
{\bf u}:u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
be a monomial regular sequence in
S
. Let
I=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
and
d_{i}=\deg(u_{i})
for all
i
, and assume that
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\dots\leq d_{m}
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
we have

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ (u_{j_{1}}\cdots u_{j_{i}}{\bf u^{b}}\ :\ |{\bf b}|=k,\,1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}<\max{\bf u^{b}}).

(5)

In particular, for all
k\geq 1
we have
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=n-m
and for all
k\geq 2

\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ d_{m}k+\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}d_{m-j}-(m-1).

Proof.

Let
R=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be the non-standard graded polynomial ring with
\deg(y_{i})=d_{i}
for all
i
. The
K
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:R\rightarrow S
defined by setting
\varphi(y_{i})=u_{i}
is flat, because
{\bf u}
is a regular sequence. Let
\mathfrak{n}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
, then
\varphi(\mathfrak{n})=I
. Therefore, if
\mathbb{F}
is the minimal free
R
-resolution of
\mathfrak{n}^{k}
, then
\mathbb{F}\otimes_{R}S
is the minimal free
S
-resolution of
I^{k}
. Hence
\varphi(\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k}))=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
i
and
k
.

To compute
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k})
we may employ the Eagon-Northcott complex. Alternatively, observe that
\mathfrak{n}^{k}
is equal to the principal Borel ideal
B(y_{m}^{k})
. So, by Proposition
2.4
,

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k})\ =\ (y_{j_{1}}\cdots y_{j_{i}}{\bf y^{b}}\ :\ {\bf b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m},\,|{\bf b}|=k,\,1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}<\max{\bf y^{b}}).

Applying the map
\varphi
, the desired formula (
5
) follows.

By Corollary
2.5
, we have
\textup{proj\,dim}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k})=m
for
0\leq i<m
and
k\geq 1
. Thus
\textup{proj\,dim}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=m
, and so
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\,=n-m
by applying the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula.

By the proof of Proposition
2.4
,
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k})
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order. Applying the flat map
\varphi
we then see that the
j
th multigraded free module in the minimal resolution of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
has basis
\mathcal{B}_{j}
given by the elements

{\bf u}_{F}(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}u_{j_{\ell}}){\bf u^{b}}

satisfying the side conditions

|{\bf b}|=k,\ \ \ \ 1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}<\max{\bf u^{b}},\ \ \ \ F\subseteq\textup{set}((\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}u_{j_{\ell}}){\bf u^{b}}),\ \ \ \ |F|=j,

where
{\bf u}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}u_{i}
for
F\neq\emptyset
, and
{\bf u}_{\emptyset}=1
. Hence,

\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ \max_{\bigcup_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j}}\,\{\deg({\bf u}_{F}(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}u_{j_{\ell}}){\bf u^{b}})-j\}.

(6)

Consider the monomial
w=(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}y_{m-\ell})y_{m}^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathfrak{n}^{k}))
where
k\geq 2
. As in the proof of Proposition
2.4
we see that
\textup{set}(w)=\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. Applying the map
\varphi
we see that the elements

{\bf b}_{j}\ =\ (\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}u_{m-\ell})(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}u_{m-\ell})u_{m}^{k}

are basis elements of
\mathcal{B}_{j}
for all
j=0,\dots,m-1=\textup{proj\,dim}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, with

\deg{\bf b}_{j}-j\ =\ d_{m}k+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}d_{m-\ell}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}d_{m-\ell}-j.

It is clear that
\deg{\bf b}_{m-1}-(m-1)
maximizes (
6
), and this proves the desired formula for the regularity.

∎

3.
Monomial ideals with homological linear powers

It was expected for a brief time that if a monomial ideal has linear resolution, or it is equigenerated and has linear quotients, then the same would be true for each of its homological shift ideals. However, it was shown that this is not the case in general
[
19
]
. One then could ask the following

Question 3.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear powers or whose all powers have linear quotients. Does
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
have linear resolution or linear quotients for all
i
and all
k\gg 0
?

In this regard, we have the following consequence of
[
19
, Theorem 1.3]
proved by Herzog and the first author.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be an equigenerated monomial ideal whose all powers have linear quotients. Then,
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
, in particular it has a linear resolution.

Next, we show with an example that Question
3.1
has a negative answer in general. For this aim, we first recall the following consequence of
[
19
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear resolution, and let
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. Then

(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\dots,u_{m}):u_{i}

is generated by variables for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

Now, we are ready to present our example.

Example 3.4
.

Let
I=B(x_{1}x_{2}x_{5},x_{3}^{3})
be a strongly stable ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{5}]
with two Borel generators. Since
I^{k}
is also a strongly stable ideal for all
k\geq 1
, it follows that
I^{k}
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order, in particular it has a
3k
-linear resolution. Therefore,
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
is equigenerated in degree
3k+2
. Notice that the monomial
u=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}^{3k}
is a minimal generator of
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
. Indeed
x_{3}^{3k}
is a minimal generator of
I^{k}
and
\textup{set}(x_{3}^{3k})=\{1,2\}
. Let
J
be the monomial ideal generated by the set
\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k}))\setminus\{u\}
. If
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
were to have linear resolution, then by Corollary
3.2
the ideal
J:u
would be generated by variables. Notice that the monomial
(x_{1}x_{2}x_{5})x_{3}^{3(k-1)}
is a minimal generator of
I^{k}
. Thus
v=x_{3}x_{4}(x_{1}x_{2}x_{5})x_{3}^{3(k-1)}
is a minimal generator of
\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{k})
. Then

. We claim that neither

, so that

is a minimal generator of

is a minimal generator of

. Since

.

.

Let

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree, and suppose that

has linear relations. Then

with

Let

The following result was shown in

Let

be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree. Then

The next result shows that if

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

be a linearly related monomial ideal generated in degree

. Then
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)

Proof.

Let

and

\alpha
and

are connected by a path in

. Using
[

, Proposition 2.3]
, for some integers

and

and

4.
Asymptotic Golodness of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be a Noetherian local ring, or a standard graded
K
-algebra with graded maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and let
{\bf x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be a minimal (homogeneous) system of generators of
\mathfrak{m}
. We denote by
(K(R),\partial)
the Koszul complex of
R
with respect to
{\bf x}
. Let
Z(R)
,
B(R)
and
H(R)
denote the module of cycles, boundaries and the homology of
K(R)
.

The formal power series
P_{R}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim_{K}\textup{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{m},R/\mathfrak{m})t^{i}
is called the
Poincaré series
of
R
. Serre showed that
P_{R}(t)
is coefficientwise bounded above by the rational series

\frac{(1+t)^{n}}{1-t\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim_{K}H_{i}({\bf x};R)t^{i}},

where
n=\dim R
. We say that
R
is a
Golod
ring, if the Poincaré series
P_{R}(t)
of
R
coincides with this upper bound given by Serre.

It was shown by Golod (see
[
25
]
or also
[
2
, Def. 5.5 and 5.6]
) that
R
is a
Golod
ring if and only if for each subset
\mathcal{S}
of homogeneous elements of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}(R)
there exists a function
\gamma
, which is defined on the set of finite
sequences of elements from
\mathcal{S}
with values in
\mathfrak{m}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}(R)
, subject to the following conditions:

(G1)

if
h\in\mathcal{S}
, then
\gamma(h)\in Z(R)
and
h=[\gamma(h)]
,

(G2)

if
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{S}
with
m>1
, then

\partial\gamma(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\overline{\gamma(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell})}\gamma(h_{\ell+1},\ldots,h_{m}),

where
\bar{a}=(-1)^{i+1}a
for
a\in K_{i}(R)
.

A function
\gamma
satisfying the properties (G1)-(G2) is called a
Massey operation
on
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a Massey operation on
\mathcal{S}
. Then for each sequence
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}
of elements from
\mathcal{S}
we have
\gamma(h_{1},\dots,h_{m})\in K_{a}(R)
for some
a\geq 2m-1
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
, with the base case
m=1
being trivial. Now, let
m>1
. For each
j<m
we may assume that
\gamma(h_{1},\dots,h_{j})\in K_{b}(R)
for some
b\geq 2j-1
, for each
1\leq j\leq m-1
. Then, by the property (G2) we have that

\partial\gamma(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\overline{\gamma(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell})}\gamma(h_{\ell+1},\ldots,h_{m}).

belongs to
K_{b}(R)K_{c}(R)=K_{b+c}(R)
for some
b\geq 2\ell-1
,
c\geq 2(m-\ell)-1
with
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
. Hence
\gamma(h_{1},\dots,h_{m})\in K_{a}(R)
with
a=b+c+1\geq 2m-1
.

∎

We say that an ideal
I\subset R
is
Golod
if the quotient ring
R/I
is Golod. Our aim in this section is to prove the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
having linear powers. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a Golod ideal for all
i
and all
k\gg 0
.

This result will be a consequence of the following more general statement which is a generalization of
[
34
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring or the polynomial ring
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
I\subset R
be an ideal, and
M=\bigoplus_{k>0}M_{k}
be a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module such that
M_{k}
is a proper ideal of
R
for all
k>0
. Then
M_{k}
is a Golod ideal for all
k\gg 0
.

For the proof of this result, we need some preparation.

As in the statement of the theorem, let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
be a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module such that
M_{k}\subset R
is a proper ideal for all
k>0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}M_{k}=I^{\ell}M_{k}\subseteq M_{k+\ell}
for all
k
and
\ell
. We claim that there is a natural graded
R
-module homomorphism

\ast\ :\ \mathcal{R}(I)\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}(\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}K_{i}(R/M_{k}))\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}(\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}K_{i}(R/M_{k})).

To prove this claim, it suffices to describe the map
\ast
for all
\ell
and
k

\ast\ :\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}\otimes K_{i}(R/M_{k})&\rightarrow&K_{i}(R/M_{k+\ell})\\
a\otimes v&\mapsto&a\ast v.\end{array}\right.

For this aim, note that
K_{i}(R/M_{k})=K_{i}(R)/M_{k}K_{i}(R)
, and let
K_{i}(R)\to K_{i}(R/M_{k})
be the canonical epimorphism which assigns to any
u\in K_{i}(R)
the residue class
u+M_{k}K_{i}(R)
. Then, if
a\in\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}=I^{\ell}
and
v=u+M_{k}K_{i}(R)\in K_{i}(R/M_{k})
, we
set

a\ast v:=au+I^{\ell}M_{k}K_{i}(R)\subseteq M_{k+\ell}K_{i}(R).

It is routine to check that map is well defined, in the sense that it is independent from the particular choice of the representative
u
of
v
. It is also trivial to show that for
a\in\mathcal{R}(I)_{j}=I^{j}
,
b\in\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}=I^{\ell}
and
v\in K_{i}(R/M_{k})
we have
(ab)\ast v=a\ast(b\ast v)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\ast
be the map introduced before. Then

(i)

For all
\ell
and
k
,
\ast
induced a map

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}\otimes H_{i}(R/M_{k})\

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ H_{i}(R/M_{k+\ell}),

\displaystyle\quad a\otimes[z]\

\displaystyle\mapsto\ a\ast[z]:=[a\ast z].

We will denote the image of this map by
I^{\ell}\ast H_{i}(R/M_{k})\subseteq H_{i}(R/M_{k+\ell})
.

(ii)

There exists an integer
s
such that for all
k\geq s
,
i\geq 1
, and
\ell\geq 0
, we have

\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}\ast H_{i}(R/M_{k})=I^{\ell}\ast H_{i}(R/M_{k})=H_{i}(R/M_{k+\ell}).

Proof.

(i) Let
v=u+M_{k}K_{i}(R)\in K_{i}(R/M_{k})
and
a\in\mathcal{R}(I)_{\ell}=I^{\ell}
. It is enough to show that
a*v
is a cycle whenever
v
is a cycle and that it is a boundary whenever
v
is a boundary. For both claims, notice that
\partial(au)=a\partial(u)
.

If
v\in Z_{i}(R/M_{k})
is a cycle, then
\partial(v)=0
. Hence
\partial(u)\in M_{k}K_{i-1}(R)
. Thus
\partial(au)=a\partial(u)\in I^{\ell}M_{k}K_{i-1}(R)\subseteq M_{k+\ell}K_{i-1}(R)
, so that
\partial(a\ast v)\in Z_{i}(R/M_{k+\ell})
is again a cycle.

Similarly, if
v\in M_{k}B_{i}(R)
is a boundary, then
v=\partial(w)
for some
w\in M_{k}K_{i+1}(R)
. As before,
\partial(a*w)=a*v
, and since
a*w\in M_{k+\ell}K_{i+1}(R)
, it then follows that
a*v\in M_{k+\ell}B_{i}(R)
is a boundary, as desired.

(ii) The assumptions guarantee that
M_{k+1}=IM_{k}
for all
k\gg 0
. Since
M
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, the Koszul homology module of
M
,
H_{i}(M)=H_{i}(R)\otimes M
, is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module with
k
th graded component
H_{i}(M)_{k}=H_{i}(M_{k})
. Since
M_{k}
is an ideal and
H_{i}(R/M_{k})\cong H_{i-1}(M_{k})
, it follows that there exists an integer
s_{i}
such that
H_{i}(R/M_{k})=I^{k-s_{i}}\ast H_{i}(R/M_{s_{i}})
for all
k\geq s_{i}
, where the multiplication
\ast
is described in Lemma
4.4
(i). Therefore, setting
s=\max\{s_{0},\dots,s_{n-1}\}
, the assertion (ii) follows.

is a boundary of
K(R/M_{k})
. To this end, observe that
b
is a linear combination of expressions of the form

b^{\prime}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\overline{\gamma(g_{k_{1}},\ldots,g_{k_{\ell}})}\gamma(g_{k_{\ell+1}},\ldots,g_{k_{r+1}})

with coefficients in
I^{(r+1)(k-s_{r})}\subset I^{k-s_{r}}
. Let
a
be the coefficient of
b^{\prime}
. Since
b^{\prime}
is a boundary in
K(R/M_{s_{r}})
and
a\in I^{k-s_{r}}
, it follows that
ab^{\prime}
is a boundary in
K(R/M_{k})
. Thus
b\in B(R/M_{k})
, as desired.

∎

Finally, we can prove our final result in the paper.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

By Theorem
1.4
, the
i
th homological shift algebra of
I
is a finitely generated (bi)graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module for which
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))_{k}=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
a proper ideal of
S
for all
k>0
. So, the assertion follows from Theorem
4.3
.

∎

In
[
29
]
, Herzog and Huneke introduced the concept of
strongly Golod
ideal. Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. We denote by
\partial I
the graded ideal generated by all partial derivatives of the generators of
I
. The ideal
I
is called
strongly Golod
if
(\partial I)^{2}\subseteq I
.

By
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
any strongly Golod ideal is Golod. It is easy to see that
I^{k}
is strongly Golod for all
k\geq 2

[
29
, Theorem 2.1.(d)]
. Therefore, one is led to ask whether
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is strongly Golod as well for all monomial ideals
I\subset S
, all
k\gg 0
and all
i
. Next, we show that this is not the case in general.

Let
S=K[x,y]
and
I=(x,y)
. Using Proposition
2.3
we obtain that

\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})=(x^{k}y,x^{k-1}y^{2},\dots,xy^{k}).

Then
\partial_{y}(x^{k}y)^{2}=x^{2k}\notin\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
. So
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
is not strongly Golod for all
k\geq 1
. Nonetheless, since
I
has linear powers, by Theorem
4.2
we have that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
is Golod for all
k\gg 0
. Indeed, in this example the ideal
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
is Golod for all
k\geq 1
, because
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})=xy(x,y)^{k-1}
has linear resolution, and ideals with linear resolutions are Golod
[
32
, Theorem 4]
.

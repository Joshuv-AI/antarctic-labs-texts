Effectiveness of tolvaptan on renal replacement therapy in patients with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease: a retrospective cohort study from the TriNetX global collaborative network
Authors: Ming-Ju Wu, Cheng-Hsu Chen, Shang-Feng Tsai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and hypothesisAutosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD) is a major genetic contributor to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD). Current evidence on tolvaptan primarily focuses on slowing estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline and kidney volume growth. However, direct confirmation of its effectiveness in reducing the need for hemodialysis in ESKD remains limited.MethodsWe included ADPKD patients aged ≥18 years using TriNetx data from Sep 2, 2018, to Sep 3, 2023. Propensity score matching (PSM) ensured baseline comparability (standardized mean difference (SMD) <0.1). Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) evaluated outcomes, and subgroup analyses were performed.ResultsAfter 1:1 PSM, both groups comprised 673 patients. The average age was 45, with generally good health (3–5% diabetes, 2-3% ischemic heart disease). Baseline eGFR averaged ∼55 ml/min/1.732m2. Post-matching, all SMDs were <0.1, indicating successful matching. Tolvaptan users exhibited lower eGFR (51.45 ± 30.09 vs. 57.37 ± 33.65, p < 0.001) and higher risk of stage 4-CKD (HR: 2.436, 95% CI:1.649, 3.599) compared to non-users. However, tolvaptan users showed significantly reduced chances of initiating hemodialysis (HR:0.362, 95%CI:0.176, 0.745), experiencing urinary tract infections (HR:0.581, 95%CI:0.354, 0.956), and all-cause mortality (HR:0.355, 95% CI:0.180, 0.700). Kaplan-Meier curves for hemodialysis initiation indicated higher survival rates among tolvaptan users across age and number of medication refill subgroups.ConclusionsThis real-world study, employing precise matching, reveals tolvaptan’s role in reducing hemodialysis initiation risk in ADPKD, despite initial hemodynamic-induced lower eGFR.Keywords: Tolvaptan, autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD), end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), hemodialysis, mortality

Introduction

Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD) is the most common genetic cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) [1,2]. Many patients progress to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) requiring renal replacement therapy (RRT) [1]. Despite aggressive symptomatic treatment targeting hypertension and pain, ADPKD patients have not seen improved outcomes in decades [3]. The standard of care for ADPKD involves managing blood pressure (BP) with an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor or an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) [4], and adhering to a strict low-sodium diet with a target intake of 2 grams per day [5]. However, the above treatment primarily addresses the symptoms of ADPKD and does not target its pathogenesis.

A study from European Renal Association-European Dialysis and Transplant Association (ERA-EDTA) Registry showed that conventional treatments for CKD do not reduce the need for RRT in ADPKD [3]. Vasopressin initiates the activation of the V2-receptor (V2R), triggering a series of intracellular processes that ultimately lead to cell growth and fluid release, thereby promoting cyst formation [6]. Tolvaptan, a V2R antagonist, is the first and only treatment proven to slow the progression of ADPKD. In April 2018, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved tolvaptan for ‘slowing kidney function decline in adults at risk of rapidly progressing ADPKD’ [7]. The landmark studies for tolvaptan were the TEMPO 3:4 [8,9] and REPRISE studies ((NCT02160145) [10]. The results from the aforementioned studies demonstrated a reduced annual decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and a decreased rate of total kidney volume (TKV) growth compared to conventional standard care. Nevertheless, published studies currently lack a definitive assessment of hard outcomes (such as the initiation of dialysis) regarding the risks and benefits associated with tolvaptan. Current evidence, based on only extrapolations of data from the REPRISE trial and TEMPO trial, suggests that tolvaptan may extend the time until stage 5 CKD from 6 to 9 years [7,10–13]. Hence, there is a lack of studies providing direct evidence supporting the notion that the use of tolvaptan in ADPKD can delay the initiation of dialysis. Even though a follow-up study extended up to 11.2 years (with an average duration of 4.6 years), it also focused on eGFR delineation [12].

Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to explore the impact of tolvaptan on a definitive renal outcome (the initiation of RRT) among patients diagnosed with ADPKD.

Materials and methods

Database of TriNetx

We utilized TriNetX’s extensive dataset, comprising data from 104 healthcare organizations (HCOs) and over 122 million individuals across 15 countries. TriNetX has contributed to over 400 articles in PubMed. Notably, a study within the dialysis-dependent cohort, defined by an initial Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) code for dialysis, has been previously published [14]. This robust dataset enabled us to explore and analyze various aspects of our research comprehensively. The collaboration among these organizations proved invaluable, allowing us to derive insightful findings and draw meaningful conclusions. The dataset covers demographic information, diagnoses (encoded using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification, ICD-10-CM), procedures (using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Procedure Coding System, ICD-10-PCS, or CPT), supplementary procedures (using the Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine Clinical Terms, SNOMED CT), medications (using the Veterans Affairs National Formulary), and laboratory tests (using the Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes, LOINC). The wealth and diversity within this dataset provide a unique opportunity to delve into our study with exceptional precision and depth.

The collaborative network encompasses a variety of HCOs, including hospitals, primary care units, and specialized medical facilities, offering data from both insured and uninsured patients. TriNetX, our data source, functions as a global health research platform that collects real-time electronic medical information from these HCOs. For our study, we curated a cohort from 2018/9/2 to 2023/9/1, utilizing TriNetX’s expansive global network. Upon obtaining positive findings, we will submit a request to TriNetX for access to the original database, enabling a more detailed analysis.

Ethics statement

As the data is anonymized, the necessity for informed consent was considered redundant. It is essential to underscore that TriNetX diligently adheres to the principles delineated in the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act and the General Data Protection Regulation. Furthermore, the Western Institutional Review Board has granted TriNetX the authority to waive the informed consent requirement, given that the platform exclusively aggregates counts and statistical summaries of de-identified data. Additionally, our meticulous utilization of the TriNetX platform within the scope of this study received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at Taichung Veterans General Hospital (Approval number: SE22220A-1).

Study design

The study design delineating the tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan groups is illustrated in Figure 1B and 1C, respectively. In the design of the tolvaptan group (Figure 1B), the index event corresponds to the initiation of tolvaptan usage, with the date of tolvaptan commencement serving as the index date. It is essential that any diagnosis of ADPKD predates the index event by at least 1 day. In contrast, for the non-tolvaptan group (Figure 1C), the index event is defined as the diagnosis of ADPKD. Patients in this group should not have received any tolvaptan throughout the study duration.

Flowchart of cohort construction and study designs for tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan groups. (A) Algorithm for participants recruitment. (B) Study design for tolvaptan group. (C) Study design for non-tolvaptan group.

For both groups, the time window for collecting baseline data extends from 1 year to 1 day before the index date, capturing the most recent available data. Concerning the data collection timeframe for outcomes, to mitigate reverse causality, data collection commenced 1 month after the index event and continued until the end of the follow-up period.

Definition of population

The study population was defined based on the ICD-10-CM code Q61.2 for ADPKD. Individuals under the age of 18 were excluded in accordance with the regulations established by our institute’s IRB. Since tolvaptan received approval from the US FDA in April 2018, we restricted the duration of this study to within 5 years from that date (2018/9/2-2023/9/1). The criteria were established in TriNetx: any ADPKD instance within 5 years and 1 day before any tolvaptan instance. The administration of tolvaptan was documented in accordance with the ATC code C03XA01.

For subgroup analysis, we divided the study population into: male vs. female, ages 20-55 (young) vs. 55 and above (elderly), and prescription frequency (medication refill times, which reflected the cumulative dose of tolvaptan) (at least 1, 3, 6, and 9 refills). We chose 55 as the cutoff age based on observations from the REPRISE trial, where participants over 55 showed no renal benefits.

Patients who had previously experienced any outcomes were excluded, ensuring that all outcomes observed were incidences.

Statistical analyses

In this study, we used propensity score matching (PSM) via the TriNetX platform to create 1:1 matched groups with similar baseline characteristics. We conducted greedy nearest neighbor matching with a caliper of 0.1 pooled standard deviations (SDs). PSM was performed exclusively for complete and available covariates, separately for subgroup analysis, and with an adequate number of covariates. Variables used in matching included demographic characteristics (age, gender, race), medical conditions (diabetes mellitus, ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, urolithiasis, hypertension), medications (ACEi, ARB, NSAID, SGLT2i), and blood-related laboratory data (eGFR calculated via CKD-EPI formula).

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD, and categorical variables as n (%). We assessed baseline characteristics’ comparability using standardized mean difference (SMD), aiming for SMD < 0.1 to indicate successful matching.

Next, we calculated hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) for all outcomes comparing tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan groups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were used to assess outcomes, and the assumption of proportional hazards was checked using scaled Schoenfeld residuals within TriNetX. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 for all analyses.

Results

Patient selection algorithms

Initially, we identified 26,215 ADPKD patients in the TriNetX database (Figure 1A). The study designs were in Figure 1B for tolvaptan group and Figure 1C for non-tolvaptan group. We excluded individuals aged <18 years and included patients within a 5-year timeframe. Subsequently, we had a total of 14,901 ADPKD patients, among whom 699 patients had received tolvaptan, while 14,111 patients had never taken tolvaptan. After performing 1:1 PSM (for 16 variables), we ultimately retained 673 patients in each group for further analysis.

Baseline characteristics of this cohort before and after matching

Table 1 outlines the baseline characteristics before and after PSM. The cohort had an average age of 45 years, with Asians comprising 3% of the population. Concurrent health conditions were low: 3.7-5.0% had diabetes, 2.7-3.9% had ischemic heart disease, 4.8-6.7% had cerebrovascular disease, 9.8-11.0% had urolithiasis, and 68.0% had hypertension. Baseline eGFR-CKD-EPI averaged around 58.5 and 48.8 mL/min/1.732m2 in tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan groups. About 35% used ACEi, while 24.0-25.7% used ARBs. After 1:1 PSM, all SMDs were <0.1, indicating favorable alignment (see Supplementary Figure 1). The median follow-up duration was 987.5 days for the tolvaptan group and 1123.6 days for the non-tolvaptan group. Of the patients, 93% were from U.S. regions, 5% were from non-U.S. regions, and 2% had unknown regional origins (Supplementary Figure 2).

Outcomes between tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan group

The incidence of outcomes post-PSM is delineated in Table 2. The tolvaptan-treated groups exhibited significantly lower subsequent eGFR compared to the non-tolvaptan group (51.45 ± 30.09 vs. 57.37 ± 33.65 mL/min/1.732m2, p < 0.001). Similarly, the tolvaptan group displayed a heightened risk of being diagnosed with stage 4 (HR: 2.436, 95% CI: 1.649-3.599) and stage 5 CKD (HR: 1.906, 95% CI: 1.278-2.822). However, the diagnosis of ESKD did not differ between the tolvaptan and non-tolvaptan groups (p = 0.516). Conversely, in comparison to the non-tolvaptan group, the tolvaptan group exhibited a reduced risk of initiating hemodialysis (HR: 0.362, 95% CI: 0.176-0.745) and experiencing AKI (HR: 0.663, 95% CI: 0.460-0.957). Additionally, the tolvaptan group demonstrated a lower risk of UTI (HR: 0.581, 95% CI: 0.354-0.956) and all-cause mortality (HR: 0.355, 95% CI: 0.180-0.700).

Discussion

This study represents the first direct evidence employing a real-world, well-matched methodology to investigate the impact of tolvaptan on the initiation of RRT among patients with ADPKD. Moreover, our findings reconfirm the acute hemodynamic eGFR changes with tolvaptan. Initially, the tolvaptan-treated groups exhibited lower eGFR and a heightened risk of stage 4-CKD, a pattern observed across all subgroups except for the subgroup with medication refill time ≥ 9. Similarly, as renal outcomes worsened, tolvaptan users only demonstrated a higher risk for stage 5-CKD in select subgroups, while exhibiting a comparable risk of ESKD across all subgroups. Furthermore, in nearly all subgroups, tolvaptan users displayed significantly lower risks of initiating hemodialysis.

The current benefits of tolvaptan on renal function are primarily derived from its ability to reduce the TKV growth rate and the rate of eGFR deterioration (annual eGFR decline slope), as demonstrated in previous studies [8–10,15]. Our study is the first to provide direct evidence regarding the risk of the initiation of RRT. Prior to this study, numerous investigations focused on using surrogate markers to predict ESKD or the initiation of RRT. The rate of eGFR decline (Δ eGFR) has been reported as an indicator associated with ESKD [16], and image classification based on TKV has also been used to predict ESKD outcomes [17]. Researchers have developed cohort models to estimate long-term disease progression in patients with ADPKD [18–20]. However, these models often assumed a uniform rate of disease progression for all patients, which may not be realistic. Recently, a study attempted to create a predictive model to assess the effect of tolvaptan on ESKD [21]. This model, based on the Irazabal equation coefficients, suggested that tolvaptan usage could potentially delay ESRD by 3.1 years [21]. However, all the aforementioned surrogate markers and predictive models relied on indirect exploratory evidence. In contrast, our study provides direct evidence demonstrating the positive impact of tolvaptan in reducing the risk of initiating RRT.

In our study, we observed acute hemodynamic changes in eGFR among patients using tolvaptan. Despite this acute effect, the final tracked renal function remained superior in individuals using tolvaptan. Previous studies have also reported similar acute hemodynamic effects of tolvaptan in patients with ADPKD [11,22,23]. These effects might be attributed to the influence of vasopressin [24,25]. Vasopressin could potentially increase GFR through the activation of tubuloglomerular feedback, causing afferent vasodilation and efferent vasoconstriction due to the release of renin [24,25]. Consequently, tolvaptan, as a vasopressin inhibitor, leads to a reduction in eGFR. This initial eGFR decrease is likely due to the pharmacological effects of tolvaptan, potentially by reducing renal plasma flow and causing volume depletion [26]. This phenomenon may serve as a clinical indicator for predicting renal prognosis in patients undergoing tolvaptan treatment [26]. According to the ERA Working Group consensus statement [27], tolvaptan has been shown to cause an initial 3–9% decrease in measured GFR [10], likely due to treatment-associated hemodynamic effects, which are reversible upon withdrawal. Our findings are consistent with previous studies, including two landmark trials of tolvaptan in ADPKD [9,10]. This is why, when patients are taking tolvaptan but their eGFR continues to decline and they approach the need for renal replacement therapy, the ERA-EDTA suggests that discontinuing tolvaptan may be reasonable for those nearing RRT initiation (e.g., reaching an eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m2), as they might benefit from a small predicted increase in GFR upon withdrawal [27]

However, not all clinical studies align with the observation of acute hemodynamic changes in eGFR following tolvaptan usage [9,28]. These discrepancies may stem from several factors. Firstly, in situations of preserved renal function, there exists well-documented autoregulation to maintain GFR and renal plasma flow [29]. Hence, in previous studies where eGFR remained high (e.g., 81.6 ± 29.6 mL/min/1.732m2 in the TEMPO 3:4 trial [9]), there was no initial decrease in eGFR among tolvaptan users. Our study, characterized by moderately reduced baseline eGFR (55-57 mL/min/1.732m2), may lack such autoregulation. Our study population resembles that of the REPRISE trial [10] with an eGFR of 41.0 ± 11.1 mL/min/1.732m2, where acute hemodynamic changes in eGFR were also noted. Secondly, differences in study designs could account for these disparities. Some studies commenced outcome collection 1 month after treatment initiation to avoid potential hemodynamic effects [28]. Thirdly, the potential impact of varying tolvaptan doses cannot be discounted. Previous studies supporting acute hemodynamic changes in eGFR with tolvaptan had very limited case numbers: Boertien et al. studied 27 ADPKD [23], Irazabal et al. studied 20 patients [22], and Therwani et al. studied 18 patients [30]. Conversely, our study involved more than 600 patients, establishing evidence for acute hemodynamic changes in eGFR at the initiation of tolvaptan usage. In essence, the initial decrease in eGFR upon initiating tolvaptan does not warrant discontinuation of this medication to achieve long-term renal outcomes.

In addition to the direct effects of tolvaptan on eGFR and dialysis outcomes in ADPKD, we also observed a reduced incidence of UTIs in tolvaptan users. UTIs are one of the most common early complications of ADPKD. In the TEMPO 3:4 trial, a reduction in UTI incidence was also demonstrated following the use of tolvaptan [9]. A meta-analysis [31] showed that tolvaptan reduced the incidence of UTIs (OR 0.69, 95% CI 0.54–0.89, p < 0.01). This effect may be attributed to tolvaptan-induced polyuria and polydipsia, both of which contribute to a lower risk of UTIs. Similarly, the polydipsia may also have contributed to the reduced incidence of AKI observed in our study.

This study has limitations worth noting. Firstly, it’s retrospective, yet it’s the largest of its kind in terms of patient volume. However, there’s a lack of ongoing prospective trials on tolvaptan’s impact on dialysis initiation in ADPKD. Secondly, other uses of tolvaptan, like in cirrhosis and heart failure, weren’t excluded, though they also affect polycystic kidneys. Notably, tolvaptan for heart failure isn’t common due to off-label use, and its approval for cirrhosis is limited to Japan, where only about 3% of our cohort was of Asian descent. Patients diagnosed with heart failure (n = 13, 1.93%) and cirrhosis (n = 10, 1.49%) were very small. Thus, these patients weren’t excluded. Fourthly, TKV information in ADPKD was lacking. Lastly, specific tolvaptan doses administered weren’t documented. Besides, we did not have data of drug adherence. However, despite these limitations, our study robustly shows that tolvaptan use significantly delays hemodialysis initiation in real-world ADPKD practice.

Conclusion

This study presents novel evidence through a real-world, closely matched methodology, establishing that tolvaptan usage correlates with a decreased likelihood of necessitating hemodialysis in ADPKD. In the cohort characterized by moderately decreased eGFR, initial lower eGFR is observed among tolvaptan users.

Unleashing the Cognitive Potential of Chess: A View of the Contribution of Neurofeedback Training to the Enrichment of High-level Cognitive Abilities
Authors: Saranya TS, Elayaraja Masilamani, Nafih Cherappurath
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChess is a cognitively demanding activity requiring sustained attention, working memory, rapid decision-making, and higher-order reasoning. Conventional chess training enhances knowledge but does not directly strengthen the neural mechanisms underlying cognition. Neurofeedback training (NFT) has emerged as a promising neuroscience-based approach for cognitive enhancement.SummaryThis narrative review synthesizes evidence from approximately 50 peer-reviewed studies examining the effects of NFT on cognitive domains relevant to chess performance, including attention, memory, reaction time, decision-making, and reasoning. Literature was identified from PsycINFO, PubMed, Scopus, and related databases. Findings indicate that NFT improves attentional control, working memory, executive functioning, cognitive flexibility, and reasoning, although direct empirical studies involving chess players remain limited.Key MessageNFT represents a promising adjunct to conventional chess training by enhancing the neural mechanisms underlying expert performance. While current evidence supports its potential for improving chess-related cognitive abilities, well-designed chess-specific experimental studies are needed to establish standardized protocols and confirm long-term effectiveness.Keywords: Chess, neurofeedback training, cognition, attention, working memory, decision making, reasoning, cognitive enhancement

Introduction

Chess as a Cognitive Battlefield

Chess has also been referred to as the ‘gymnasium of the mind’, where the players are constantly challenged to participate in complex cognitive operations. Fundamentally, the game is simplicity itself: 32 pieces slide about a 64-square board. However, beneath this simplicity, there is a staggering cognitive challenge. Every move involves considering dozens of options, predicting the opponent’s plan and balancing short-term tactical needs and long-term strategic thinking. Such requirements make chess a perfect candidate for the examination of expertise, problem-solving and human cognition.1, 2

Decades of psychological research have confirmed that expert chess players differ from novices not only in terms of accumulated knowledge but also in the efficiency of their cognitive processing. Concepts such as chunking theory and pattern recognition explain how grandmasters can recall board positions with remarkable accuracy and retrieve thousands of tactical motifs from long-term memory within seconds. These findings have positioned chess as a central paradigm in cognitive science.

However, while classic training methods, opening repertoires memorised by heart, tactical problems solved and endgames worked out have largely determined expert performance, they have no direct impact on reinforcing neural substrates for optimal cognitive performance. They support knowledge structures instead, without optimising brain activity patterns driving attention, working memory and decision making. This shortcoming prompted researchers to consider new approaches, including neurofeedback training (NFT), specifically aimed at influencing neural mechanisms.

Cognitive Science Applications of Neurofeedback Training

NFT is an application of biofeedback that gives people direct feedback on their brainwave activity, normally in the form of electroencephalographic (EEG) measurements. People learn to control their neural oscillations through feedback—commonly visual or auditory—whenever desired brainwave states are attained. Through regular exercise, NFT allows operant conditioning of the brain, which can help people self-manage alertness, attention, relaxation or imagination. 3

For instance, enhancing sensorimotor rhythm (SMR, 12–15 Hz) has been implicated in better attention and motor accuracy, while theta/beta ratio reduction has been implicated in better sustained attention and vigilance. Likewise, alpha and theta synchronisation have been linked to creative and divergent thinking. These discoveries have led NFT to be a promising intervention for many domains, from clinical therapies of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and epilepsy to sports performance enhancement in athletes, musicians and surgeons.

With its proven influence on cognitive abilities directly applicable to chess, NFT shows strong promise for chess training. The high level of mental exertion required in extended competition games, often spanning hours, serves to maximise constant focus, speedy recall and effective decision making under pressure. NFT, as it fortifies the underlying cognitive architecture of the brain, can endow chess players with an exceptional edge that conventional training cannot possibly match.

Why a Narrative Review?

The literature on NFT and cognitive abilities is extensive but diverse. Experiments differ in their protocols, populations of participants and cognitive outcomes targeted. Many have not been carried out specifically among chess players, although several include abilities that are very generalisable to the chess environment.

A narrative review is most appropriate for integrating this literature for three reasons:

Flexibility in integration: While systematic reviews are limited to narrowly defined inclusion criteria, a narrative framework enables inclusion of a variety of studies across education, clinical neuroscience and sports science.

Conceptual focus: Chess performance is based on the combination of several interacting cognitive skills, and therefore, adopting a systems-oriented framework instead of isolating individual outcomes is essential.

Identification of gaps: Through the synthesis of findings across fields, a narrative review can emphasise both positive trends and where evidence is limited.

Therefore, this review endeavours to chart the cognitive chess landscape based on NFT, assessing how implications of findings from general NFT research can be transferred to chess training and where additional research is warranted.

Methods

Justification for a Systematic Review with Narrative Synthesis

In examining the literature on NFT and chess, methodology selection is important. A systematic review with narrative synthesis or meta-analysis would typically expect a large volume of homogeneous studies carried out under similar protocols. However, NFT research is presently heterogeneous and scattered across clinical settings (e.g., ADHD, epilepsy), educational intervention, sporting performance and cognitive performance. In addition, very few studies have specifically examined NFT in terms of chess or other board games.

With this heterogeneity, a narrative review was considered the best approach. While systematic reviews are meant to generate statistical generalisations, narrative reviews do not pursue statistical generalisations but aim at interpreting, synthesising and putting findings into context from heterogeneous fields. This provides the reviewer with the liberty to make conceptual links between studies of NFT from neighbouring areas and the cognitive abilities required in chess.

Selection and Synthesis Process

The initial search retrieved about 350 records. Following the screening of abstracts and the elimination of duplicates, around 120 studies were deemed potentially relevant. Of these, around 50 studies were thoroughly analysed and synthesised for this narrative review, selected on the basis of direct applicability to cognitive domains relevant to chess.

The synthesis process was thematic in nature. Findings were clustered by cognitive domains: attention, memory, reaction time, decision making and reasoning—and subsequently interpreted against the demands of chess—this enabled depth (within individual cognitive domains) as well as breadth (across several domains).

Limitations

While systematic reviews with a narrative method enable richness of interpretation, they also have limitations:

Risk of selection bias: Without strict inclusion criteria, there is a risk of over-representation of studies that are favourable to NFT efficacy. Steps were taken to mitigate this by including those reporting mixed or null findings.

Lack of meta-analytic quantification: In contrast to systematic reviews, narrative reviews are unable to provide aggregate effect sizes. This implies that strength in conclusions relies more on conceptual synthesis than statistical pooling.

Extrapolation to chess: As few studies have directly tested NFT in chess populations, much of this review extrapolates from neighbouring fields. Although this offers informative insights, it also adds some measure of speculative interpretation.

Ethical Considerations

All sources used in this review are publicly accessible via academic databases. Unpublished personal data and confidential records were not utilised. In the process of synthesising findings, attention was given to noting both the potential advantages and the existing limitations of NFT, in an attempt to avoid exaggerating efficacy claims.

Summary of the Review Method

In short, this narrative review builds upon a guided but adaptive strategy for literature selection and integration. Through the combination of evidence from around half a dozen studies in neuroscience, psychology and performance sciences, the review seeks to present a unified account of how NFT can facilitate cognitive processes related to chess. The narrative approach enables findings to be translated across fields, making NFT a potentially revolutionary tool in the cognitive training of chess players.

Neuroscientific and Theoretical Bases of Neurofeedback Training

Brain Self-regulation and Operant Conditioning

NFT is founded upon operant conditioning in which subjects are provided with instant feedback about their brainwave activity (by way of EEG) and are reinforced for regulating their brain states in a desired direction. For instance, when volunteers increase SMR activity or reduce theta/beta ratios, they may be rewarded with auditory tones, visual feedback or reward games. Through repetition, the loop of feedback facilitates self-regulation of brain activity and leads to measurable gains in cognition.

Unlike pharmacological or behavioural interventions, NFT does not expose the individual to outside changes but trains the brain from within, capitalising on its own neuroplasticity. This makes it very attractive for the enhancement of performance in non-clinical groups such as athletes, musicians and perhaps chess players.

Processes of Neurofeedback Training’s Neuroplasticity

NFT draws on experience-dependent plasticity, where the repeated modulation of neural oscillations increases functional connectivity between subcortical and cortical regions. Over time, this leads to long-term changes in synaptic efficacy, integrity of white matter and network coherence. 4

Historical Development of Neurofeedback Training

In the 1990s and 2000s, NFT expanded to encompass cognitive enhancement. Sporting competitors, musical performers and high-level professionals (e.g., pilots, surgeons) discovered more universal applications of NFT protocols for boosting concentration, stress management and coordination of fine motor skills. Today, NFT is recognised not only as a treatment intervention but also as a peak performance training method. 3

The development from clinical use to optimal performance improvement follows in line with the overall shift in neuroscience from the repair of deficits to the enhancement of healthy function. Chess, as one of the most cognitively challenging activities, naturally progresses along the way.

Comparison with Other Brain Training Techniques

NFT can be compared with other methods of cognitive improvement:

Mindfulness meditation: Supports attentional control and emotion regulation but lacks the immediate neurophysiological feedback cycle of NFT.

Cognitive training (working memory tasks, puzzles): Supports task-specific skills but often does not generalise to daily tasks.

Pharmacological treatments (stimulants, nootropics): Produce short-term performance gains but carry risks for dependency and side effects.

NFT is unique because it explicitly manipulates neural oscillations but gives the subject active control over the activity. For chess players, this would imply they might condition their brains in ways that augment standard preparation, supplementing without replacing study of openings, tactics and endgames.

Cognitive Domains Applicable to Chess and Neurofeedback Training

Attention

Attention is the foundation of chess skill. Each chess move demands continuous attention, tracking one’s own plan and one’s opponent’s plan, as well as quick attention-shifting between tactical specifics and strategic objectives. Even momentary lapses lead to disastrous mistakes—interspersed by players as ‘blunders’.

NFT has been found to improve attentional control in various populations. A seminal study by Gevensleben et al. showed that theta/beta training enhanced sustained and selective attention in children with ADHD and healthy controls. 5 Theta activity (linking drowsiness and distractibility) was reduced, while beta activity (associating vigilance and focus) was increased. These findings are particularly applicable to chess, where individuals tend to sit for 4–6 h during gruelling tournaments, where concentration is needed without mental exhaustion.

Additional support is provided by sports psychology. Performers trained on SMR protocols exhibited superior focus under pressure. 6 As relaxed attentiveness, SMR would seem to assist performers in staying attentive while calm, a condition frequently characterised by chess players as being ‘in the zone’. Steadfast attention, possibly avoiding impulsive action in time-pressured situations, may especially counteract during swift or blitz affairs.

Notably, NFT seems to enhance not only vigilance but also attentional flexibility. This is particularly important in chess, where constant switches between tactical micro-analysis and overall strategic thinking are required. Enhanced attentional switching might enable players to manage more than one candidate move better, which lessens cognitive overload.

In short, there is strong evidence for NFT’s effectiveness in improving attentional processes directly translatable to chess. Although chess-specific studies are still scarce, attentional gains seen in sport populations, students and clinical populations indicate obvious advantages for sustaining mental endurance under elite-level play.

Memory

Performance at chess relies significantly on working memory (ability to remember and manipulate positions in mind) and long-term memory (memory for opening theory, patterns and strategic principles). Grandmasters possess enormous chess-specific memory that allows them to identify intricate setups and look several moves ahead. 7

NFT has shown an effective impact on both long-term and working memory. Zoefel et al. found that coherence training of the frontal-parietal network enhanced memory encoding and retrieval in normal adults. 8 The subjects who trained to improve theta synchronisation had better recall in word and picture recognition tests. In chess, this means better retrieval of memorised opening lines and faster mental simulation of variations at play.

Alpha coherence training has also been linked to memory consolidation. By enabling relaxed but concentrated states, alpha enhancement enables players to move from encoding new patterns to storing them for long-term recall. This is especially useful in chess instruction, where hours spent memorising tactical motifs or grandmaster games need to be consolidated effectively.

Additionally, improvements in working memory have been reported in NFT experiments of executive function. Enriquez-Geppert et al. 9 reported that NFT enhanced n-back task performance, a recognised test of working memory. As chess involves large amounts of mentally retaining lists of moves and calculating multiple steps ahead, increased working memory capacity might lead to direct improvement in accuracy when calculating deeply.

Therefore, NFT seems to enhance short-term calculation ability and long-term pattern memory, two principal cornerstones of chess expertise. These findings are consistent with previous evidence demonstrating that upper alpha neurofeedback improves working memory performance.10

Reaction Time

Within competitive chess structures like blitz (5 min per player) and bullet (1 min per player), reaction time is as important as tactical knowledge. Even in traditional time controls, time-troubled players are frequently called upon to make instantaneous decisions at the closing stages of the game.

Studies in NFT consistently demonstrate gains in the speed of response. Egner and Gruzelier11, 12 discovered that SMR and beta training decreased reaction time among sports participants and video game players as a result of increasing thalamocortical regulation and sensory-motor integration. This neural efficiency can potentially be applied to quicker perception of tactical dangers and faster movement execution on the board.

Improvements in reaction time are not just about speed—they are also linked to accuracy under stress. NFT seems to dampen cognitive ‘noise’ by refining neural oscillations, enabling quicker but more trustworthy responses. For chess, this would mean that players do not just move fast but also avoid mistakes in high-stress situations.

In addition, NFT-enhanced reaction time is consistent with evidence from e-sports. NFT-trained professional gamers exhibited faster and more accurate actions in stressful conditions. 4 As e-sports and blitz chess have similar requirements—maintained attention, fast perception and instant response—such findings support the argument for NFT-based chess training.

Decision Making

Chess decision-making entails considering several candidate moves, predicting the opponent’s moves, and balancing risk against long-term positional advantages. All of these processes have close links to executive functions within the prefrontal cortex, such as planning, inhibition and cognitive flexibility.

NFT has been demonstrated to support these executive functions. Hammond indicated that NFT of frontal alpha and beta activity enhanced the accuracy of decision-making and cut back on impulsivity.13, 14 Those trained to maintain higher frontal activation showed better cognitive control and less susceptibility to hasty judgments. In chess, this might translate into avoiding the temptation of appearing tempting moves with strategically unsound consequences.

NFT also boosts cognitive flexibility, so that people can switch strategies better. This is crucial in chess, where successful players need to be flexible—they need to drop preconceived notions when faced with unanticipated opponent reactions. With its generation of prefrontal efficiency, NFT could help players stay flexible without losing strategic coherence.

Besides, decision-making performance under pressure has been proven to be enhanced after NFT. Experiments in military training environments demonstrated that NFT minimised decision mistakes throughout high-stress simulations. 3 Considering the psychological pressure of competitive chess, particularly in endgame tournament matches, these results are highly relevant.

Generally, NFT seems to enhance both the precision and the robustness of decision-making—two characteristics distinguishing grandmasters from lower-level players.

Reasoning Abilities

Reasoning is the highest-order thinking process in chess. Although much everyday play is guided by pattern recognition and memory, real mastery involves abstract reasoning, logical deduction and creative problem-solving.

NFT studies indicate an advantage on several reasoning dimensions:

Mathematical/logical reasoning: NFT to parietal areas has been associated with enhanced symbolic manipulation and sequential logic. 9 For chess, this facilitates calculating complex forced variations and exact calculation of consequences.

Abstract reasoning: Alpha-theta protocols encourage creativity and divergent thinking. 3 Trainees in such states can find unorthodox moves or fresh sacrifices that catch their opponents.

Deductive reasoning: NFT has correlated with increased frontal lobe coherence, enhancing the skills to use broad principles on particular board positions. 6 This aids systematic positional play.

Inductive reasoning: Cross-hemispheric NFT enhances pattern recognition and intuitive generalisation. 4 In chess, this enables recognition of emerging motifs and adjustment to novel structures.

Perhaps most significantly, NFT seems to encourage synergistic reasoning, in which more than one kind of thinking comes together. For example, a player might use deductive thinking (‘my opponent’s poor pawn structure needs to be attacked’) and abstract visualisation (‘if I exchange bishops here, the resulting position could be winning’). This kind of complex reasoning under time pressure is characteristic of high-level play, and NFT might supply the neural economy required for bringing these together.

Discussion

Critical Synthesis of Evidence

The reviewed evidence points to the potential of NFT in augmenting cognitive abilities pertinent to chess. Studies in clinical, educational and sports psychology all emphasise gains in attention, working memory and executive functions after NFT interventions. All these areas are important for performing chess, where success is based to a large extent on maintaining concentration, remembering patterns and reasoning adaptively. 2 Early studies in sports performance also demonstrated that neurofeedback contributed to improvements in elite athletic performance, supporting its potential application in cognitively demanding domains such as chess. 15

However, evidence of strength varies between cognitive capacities. Enhancement of attention and working memory is strongest, underpinned by controlled experiments that yield replicable findings. 12 In enhancements in decision making, gains have been noted but are currently confined to small trials with relatively small samples. 3 Reasoning and creativity outcomes, while theoretically concomitant with chess requirements, are the least supported, frequently deduced by proxy measures instead of specifically chess-related tasks. 14

Implications for Chess-specific Performance

Though no large-scale studies have specifically examined NFT in high-competitive chess populations, inferences can be made from analogous high-demand areas. For instance, sports participants in accuracy sports like golf and archery have shown enhanced concentration and lower anxiety after NFT. 16 The same has been recorded among musicians, where NFT supported states of creative flow and error minimisation. 3 In e-sports, NFT has been associated with quicker reaction time and superior attentional switching, reflecting the rapid cognitive requirements in blitz and quick chess. These analogies propose reasonable transferability, although empirical support in chess is an overdue research priority.

Controversies and Debates in Neurofeedback Training Research

Despite its potential, NFT is a contentious field. One of the significant issues is placebo effects; according to some researchers, improvements seen might be due to expectancy bias and not true neurophysiological change. 14 While sham-controlled designs have tried to overcome this, results are inconclusive. Protocol heterogeneity also makes replication difficult, as studies tend to greatly vary in terms of training frequency, feedback modalities and reinforcement criteria (Hammond, 2011). 17 Individual differences are also at play; some participants are ‘non-responders’, indicating minimal or no measurable gain. 13 Last, there are concerns regarding the sustainability of NFT effects—short-term improvement is well established, but long-term maintenance of gains is less certain.

Contributions to Theories of Expertise

Apart from its applied utility, NFT research also makes a contribution to more general theories of expertise. Theories like chunking theory 7 and template theory 10 focus on the performance of long-term memory and recognition of patterns in becoming a master chess player. NFT, on the other hand, points to the importance of neural efficiency and cognitive plasticity, indicating that exceptional performance is not merely a result of prior knowledge, but also of the brain’s ability for attentional control, distraction suppression and flexible strategy shifting (Neubauer & Fink, 2009). 18 It brings neuroscience into expertise research by blending cognitive theories with biological processes of learning and performance.

Conclusion

With this narrative review, an attempt was made to investigate whether NFT, a neuroscience-driven technique of self-regulating brainwave activity, might improve the high-level cognitive skills underpinning chess mastery. Integrating the outcomes of about 50 studies conducted in psychology, neuroscience, sports performance and education, the review underlines that NFT uniformly enhances attention, working memory, reaction time, decision making and reasoning, skills, all vital to chess mastery.

The integration of results indicates that NFT does not merely enhance isolated abilities but enables network-level optimisation of cognition. Enhanced attentional regulation allows more effective memory retrieval; better decision making is enhanced by more powerful executive control; accelerated reaction times permit players to stay accurate under pressure; and reasoning capacities, ranging from deductive to creative, are honed by improved frontal and cross-hemispheric communication. For chess players, who have to coordinate these skills simultaneously over the duration of long, high-stakes games, such integrated cognitive enhancement is especially pertinent.

Meanwhile, the review pinpoints significant limitations and gaps. There is limited research directly connecting NFT to chess, with much of the evidence drawn from the neighbouring disciplines of sports and clinical psychology. Methodological shortcomings such as small sample sizes, varied protocols and variable control conditions constrain the conclusions’ generalisability. There are still questions regarding the long-term durability of improvements brought about by NFT, the predictors of individual sensitivity to NFT, and the ethical considerations for the delivery of NFT in competition settings.

However, the potential of NFT for chess is indubitable. As physical conditioning, psychological toughness and nutritional planning have become as much a part of contemporary chess preparation as study and analysis, NFT might become part of the next generation of mental training. Future studies should focus on chess-specific research, marrying NFT to computer platforms, creating standard protocols and measuring both short- and long-term impacts on performance measures like Elo ratings and accuracy of moves. Just as significant is the requirement for interdisciplinary collaboration between neuroscientists, psychologists and chess trainers so that NFT interventions are scientifically rigorous, ethically responsible and practically accessible.

In more general terms, the review also advances the theory of expertise. Classical models of cognitive chess place strong reliance on memory, chunking and pattern recognition, but NFT research highlights neural efficiency and adaptability. Expertise, then, is not merely a matter of knowledge gained but the capacity of the brain to self-regulate for peak performance under changing and challenging conditions.

In summary, NFT represents a promising avenue to improving chess performance through the development of cognitive underpinnings of expertise. Though evidence is thus far preliminary and further research is necessary, NFT can potentially revolutionise the manner in which players exercise their minds, moving away from sole external study of openings and tactics and towards internal growth of neural efficiency. The future of chess is perhaps not so much about memorising grandmaster games but about conditioning the brain itself—unleashing cognitive potential through the collaboration of neuroscience and one of the world’s most intellectually deep games.

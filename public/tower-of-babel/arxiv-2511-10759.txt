Vertex-transitive graphs with uniformly bisecting quasi-geodesics
Joseph Paul MacManus

Abstract
. Suppose that X X is an infinite, connected, locally finite, quasi-transitive graph with the property that every bi-infinite quasi-geodesic uniformly coarsely separates X X into exactly two deep pieces. We show that such an X X is quasi-isometric to either the Euclidean plane or the hyperbolic plane. In particular, if X X is a Cayley graph of a finitely generated group G G with the above property, then G G is a virtual surface group. This can be interpreted as an extension of the well-known fact that a hyperbolic group with circular boundary is virtually Fuchsian. Our theorem positively resolves Problem 14.98 of the Kourovka Notebook, posed by V. A. Churkin in 1999. The proof uses an isoperimetric inequality of Varopoulos to show that if such a graph has the above property, then either it is hyperbolic or has quadratic growth.



1.
Introduction

Planarity is known to be among the most rigid properties of finitely generated groups and, more generally, of locally finite, quasi-transitive graphs. Perhaps one of the most important examples of this phenomenon is a theorem rooted in work of Mess on the Seifert conjecture
[
15
]
, which states that every finitely generated group that is quasi-isometric to a complete Riemannian plane is a virtual surface group. Mess dealt directly with the non-hyperbolic case using an inequality of Varopoulos to bound the growth of the group, while the hyperbolic case was later completed with the celebrated convergence group theorem of Tukia
[
20
]
, Gabai
[
7
]
, and Casson–Jungreis
[
4
]
.
Bowditch later gave a new proof and several remarkable algebraic extensions of this result, making use of a certain kind of ‘winding number’ on the Cayley complex
[
3
]
. Around the same time, another coarse characterisation of virtual surface groups was given by Papasoglu in
[
17
]
, which describes virtual surface groups as, roughly speaking, those one-ended finitely presented groups whose Cayley graphs have the property that any two sufficiently far apart points can be separated by a quasi-line.
More recently, virtually planar groups were characterised by the present author among finitely presented groups as those which are asymptotically minor excluded
[
14
]
, and among finitely generated groups as those which are quasi-isometric to a planar graph
[
12
]
. An interesting extension of the latter was recently presented by Davies in
[
6
]
.

We now return our attention to the convergence group theorem, mentioned above. This asserts that if a group
G
admits a discrete uniform convergence action on the circle, then this action is topologically conjugate to one which is induced by some geometric action of
G
on the hyperbolic plane. In particular, this implies that any hyperbolic group with circular Gromov boundary is virtually Fuchsian.
The goal of this paper is to present an extension of this result applicable to all locally finite, quasi-transitive graphs. Of course, if the graph in question is not hyperbolic then we do not necessarily have a natural or helpful notion of a ‘boundary at infinity’. Thus, to clarify the situation, it makes sense to reframe our problem in terms of the intrinsic geometry of the Cayley graph.
To this end, we invite the reader to consider the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a geodesic metric space. A connected subspace
Z\subset X
is called
narrow
if it is quasi-isometric to a subset of
\mathbf{R}
, with respect to the ambient metric. If
Z
is not narrow then it is said to be
wide
. We define the
uniformly bisecting quasi-geodesics
property, denoted (

UBQ

), as follows:

(
\rm{UBQ}
)

There exists
\sigma>0
such that for any connected, bi-infinite quasi-geodesic
\rho\subset X
, we have that
X\setminus N_{\sigma}(\rho)
contains exactly two wide connected components.

We call
\sigma
the
separation constant
.

The property (

UBQ

) is easily checked to be a quasi-isometry invariant among geodesic spaces.
Thus, it makes sense to say that a finitely generated group has this property. The Euclidean and hyperbolic planes obviously have (

UBQ

), by the Jordan curve theorem. It is an exercise in hyperbolic geometry to show that if an infinite hyperbolic group satisfies (

UBQ

) then its Gromov boundary is circular. Hence, the convergence group theorem implies the following.

Theorem

(Corollary of the convergence group theorem)
.

Let
G
be an infinite hyperbolic group with (

UBQ

). Then
G
is virtually Fuchsian.

In this form, the hypotheses now make sense in any finitely generated group, or indeed in any locally finite, quasi-transitive graph.
It is therefore natural to ask whether this theorem extends to non-hyperbolic groups or to arbitrary locally finite, quasi-transitive graphs. This question appears as Problem 14.98 of the Kourovka Notebook
[
11
]
, posed by V. A. Churkin in 1999.
In this paper, we answer Churkin’s question in the affirmative and prove the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be an infinite, connected, locally finite, quasi-transitive graph with (

UBQ

). Then
X
is quasi-isometric to either the Euclidean plane or the hyperbolic plane.

Since finitely generated abelian groups and Fuchsian groups are known to be quasi-isometrically rigid, this has the consequence that if
G
is a finitely generated group with (

UBQ

) then
G
is a virtual surface group. Thus we provide a new, purely geometric characterisation of virtual surface groups among all finitely generated groups.

We remark that Theorem
A
can be compared to Bing’s characterisation of the 2-sphere
[
2
]
. This states that any compact, connected, locally connected space without cut-points or cut-pairs, in which every simple closed curve separates, is homeomorphic to the 2-sphere. Morally, our theorem is a coarse analogue of this statement for locally finite, quasi-transitive graphs.
We also point out that our characterisation is similar in spirit to Papasoglu’s theorem mentioned above, in the sense that they both characterise virtual surface groups through the separation properties of their quasi-lines. One key difference between the two results is that Papasoglu requires the group to be
a priori
finitely presented, whereas we do away with this assumption altogether.

Outline

Before we begin, we describe a rough outline of this paper and the proof therein.
In order to prove Theorem
A
, we consider separately the cases where
X
is hyperbolic and where
X
is not hyperbolic. The hyperbolic case follows from the convergence group theorem as discussed above.

Our attention is thus entirely focused on the case where

The ultimate goal is to prove that non-hyperbolicity plus (

, building on work of Gromov

, plus the Bass–Guivarc’h formula for the degrees of growth of nilpotent groups

, allows us to conclude that

is quasi-isometric to the Euclidean plane.

We will study this problem through the eyes of
witnesses
, which are introduced in §

. These are pairs of rays which probe deep into the two wide components bound by a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic. A key tool here will be the
witness protection lemma
, which asserts that if we perturb a bi-infinite geodesic in a bounded way, then the original witnesses still witness two distinct deep components bound by the new quasi-geodesic. Following this, in §
4
and §

we construct and consider a large figure called a
cross-examiner
. Attaching paths to the cross-examiner and applying the witness protection lemma allows us to construct closed loops which coarsely bound regions of

in a controlled way. These loops should be compared to Jordan curves in the plane. Combining this with an inequality of Varopoulos,
in §

we establish a useful criterion for showing that
X
has quadratic growth.

Following this, we study the separation properties of
quasi-circles
and, more generally,
truncated quasi-circles
in §
7
. We apply our new criterion for quadratic growth and see that either all (truncated) quasi-circles coarsely bound regions of at most uniformly bounded depth, regardless of length, or
X
has quadratic growth. In the language of this paper, we prove that truncated quasi-circles have
limited jurisdiction
. In the final section, §
8
, we apply the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem to construct a sequence of ‘nested’ truncated quasi-circles lying along a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic. We then observe that this ‘nesting’ taken together with the witness protection lemma means the truncated quasi-circles cannot possibly have limited jurisdiction. This enables us to conclude that
X
has quadratic growth, and deliver our final verdict.

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper, every graph is assumed to be simplicial. We will often conflate a graph with its ‘geometric realisation’, and view a graph as a CW-complex, metrised in the obvious way with edges having unit length. Given a graph
X
, let
\dist_{X}
denote this metric. We write
V(X)
for its vertex set and
E(X)
for its set of (unoriented) edges, respectively.

For us, a path
p
is a sequence of vertices
p=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
such that
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are connected by an edge for every
0\leq i<n
. The vertices
v_{0}
and
v_{n}
are called the
initial
and
terminal
vertices of
p
. We also allow bi-infinite and one-way infinite paths. Given a finite path
p
as above, we define
\length(p):=n
. The path
p
is said to be
simple
if every
v_{i}
appears exactly once. A finite path is called a
cycle
or
loop
if
v_{0}=v_{n}
, and is said to be a
simple loop
if every
v_{i}
appears exactly once, except for the endpoints which appear twice.
We will often abuse notation and parametrise paths as maps
p:I\to X
. Paths will
always
be parametrised at unit speed. Given two paths
p
,
q
such that the terminal vertex of
p
is equal to the initial vertex of
q
, we write
p\cdot q
to mean their concatenation. We also write
p^{-1}
to mean the reversal of the path
p
.
Let
\lambda\geq 1
,
c\geq 0
. Given a path
p:I\to X
, we call
p
a
(\lambda,c)
-quasi-geodesic
if

\dist_{X}(p(s),p(t))\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}|s-t|-c,

for all
s,t\in I
.
Throughout the rest of this paper, we will adhere to the following conventions.

Convention 2.1
.

Unless otherwise stated, we fix
X
and
\sigma>0
throughout this paper where
X
is an infinite, connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graph which satisfies (

UBQ

) with separation constant
\sigma
.

Convention 2.2
.

When we talk of a quasi-geodesic, we will always mean a connected path parametrised at unit speed.

On that note, we may now begin proceedings.

3.
Witness protection

The purpose of this section is to motivate and prove a key lemma, which we call the
witness protection lemma
. This is Lemma
3.6
below. Before this, we need to make a few basic observations.

Proposition 3.1
.

X
is one-ended.

Proof.

Note we assumed
X
to be infinite, and clearly
X
cannot be two-ended. Suppose
X
is infinite-ended. In this case, it is easy to find a quasi-geodesic
\rho
and
R>0
such that
X\setminus N_{R}(\rho)
has infinitely many wide connected components. Indeed, we may simply take
\rho
to be a geodesic connecting any two distinct ends. It follows that
X
must be one-ended.
∎

The following remark is also worth noting.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\rho
is a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic, then tubular neighbourhoods of
\rho
also ‘bisect’
X
. That is, given
r\geq\sigma
, if we let
Z=N_{r}(\rho)
, then
X\setminus Z
also contains exactly two wide connected components. This is because there exists a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic
\rho^{\prime}
whose vertex set is exactly
Z
, obtained by travelling along
\rho
, visiting every vertex in the
r
-ball around a given vertex, then travelling to the next vertex along
\rho
, and so on.

We now prove a sequence of basic lemmata, the first of which presents a way of recognising wide components.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\rho\subset X
be a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic. Let
U
be a connected component of
X\setminus N_{\sigma}(\rho)
. Suppose that there is a one-way infinite simple path
p
contained in
U
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dist(p(t),\rho)=\infty
. Then
U
is wide.

Proof.

Suppose
U
is narrow, so that
U
is quasi-isometric to some connected—and necessarily infinite—subset of
\mathbf{R}
. We therefore have two cases, either
U
is quasi-isometric to the full-line
\mathbf{R}
, or to the half-line
\mathbf{R}^{+}
.

First, let us assume that
U
is quasi-isometric to the half-line
\mathbf{R}^{+}
. Then there exists a one-way infinite quasi-geodesic ray
\alpha\subset U
such that
\dHaus(U,\alpha)
is finite. We now note that
\alpha
must diverge from
\rho
. That is,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dist(\alpha(t),\rho)=\infty
. Indeed, if this were not the case then
p
itself could not diverge from
\rho
.
In particular, this implies that
X
has multiple ends which contradicts Proposition
3.1
.

The case where
U
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbf{R}
follows identically, as we need only notice that
p
will spend an infinite amount of time in exactly one ‘half’ of
U
. The corresponding ‘half’ of
\alpha
will then diverge from
\rho
and we conclude similarly.
∎

The next lemma provides a converse to the above.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\rho\subset X
be a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic, and let
U
be a wide component of
X\setminus N_{\sigma}(\rho)
. Then there exists a path
p\subset U
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dist_{X}(p(t),\rho)=\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose not, then there exists
R>0
such that every component of
U\setminus N_{R}(\rho)
is finite. But then
X\setminus N_{R}(\rho)
has at most one wide component. This contradicts Remark
3.2
.
∎

In light of Lemmata
3.3
and
3.4
, we introduce the following terminology.

Definition 3.5

(Witnesses)
.

Let
\rho\subset X
be a bi-infinite quasi-geodesic. We say a pair
(w_{1},w_{2})
of one-way infinite simple paths in
X
is a
pair of witnesses for
\rho
if the following hold:

(1)

w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are contained in distinct wide components of
X\setminus N_{\sigma}(\rho)
, and

(2)

For both
i=1,2
, we have
\dist(w_{i}(t),\rho)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.

We now have the following key lemma, which will be employed throughout this paper. It says that pairs of witnesses are preserved by bounded perturbations of the quasi-geodesic.

Lemma 3.6

(The witness protection lemma)
.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\subset X

\dHaus(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

(w_{1},w_{2})
is a pair of witnesses for
\rho_{1}
. Then there are infinite subpaths

Proof.

Fix a pair of witnesses
(w_{1},w_{2})
for
\rho_{1}

\rho_{2}
.
Write

4.
Cross-examination

In this section we construct a certain gadget, called a

Their utility will lie in their ability to help us build separating loops with controlled interiors.

Definition 4.1

6.
Jurisdiction

We introduce some more terminology.

Definition 6.1

(Depth)
.

Let

be a connected subgraph. We denote by
\partial A
the set of vertices at distance exactly 1 from
A
. The

of

, denoted
\depth(A)
, is defined as

\depth(A):=\inf\{r\geq 0:A\subset N_{r}(\partial A)\}.

Definition 6.2

(Jurisdiction)
.

Given

, let
S
be a finite subgraph of
X
. Define the
\delta

of
S
, denoted
\operatorname{Jur}_{\delta}(S)
, as

\operatorname{Jur}_{\delta}(S):=\max_{A}\ \depth(A),

where
A
ranges over all finite connected components of
X\setminus N_{\delta}(S)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a collection of finite subgraphs of
X
.
We say that
\Lambda
has

if for all
\delta\geq 0
there exists
C\geq 0
so that for all

\operatorname{Jur}_{\delta}(S)<C
.

We will show that, in a vertex-transitive graph with (

UBQ

), not of quadratic growth, certain nice collections of subgraphs must have limited jurisdiction.
To prove this, we will use an isoperimetric inequality of Varopoulos to bound the growth of our graph. More precisely, we make use of a certain extension of this inequality which applies to all connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graphs, due to Saloff-Coste.

We need to set up some notation.
Given a connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graph
X
, let
\mathfrak{G}_{X}(n)
denote the size of the closed
n

\mathfrak{G}_{X}:\mathbf{N}\to\mathbf{N}
is the
growth function
of
X
.
We say that
X
has
quadratic growth
if
\mathfrak{G}_{X}(n)
grows quadratically.
Given a subset
A\subset V(X)
, we denote by
\partial A
the set of vertices which lie at a distance of exactly 1 from
A
.

Theorem 6.3

(
[
18
, Thm. 2.1]
)
.

Every infinite, connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graph
X
satisfies the following isoperimetric inequality.
For all finite
A\subset V(X)
, we have that

\frac{|A|}{\phi(2|A|)}\leq 4|\partial A|,

where
\phi(\lambda)=\inf\{n\in\mathbf{N}:\mathfrak{G}_{X}(n)>\lambda\}
.
2
2

2

This function
\phi
is sometimes referred to as the
inverse growth function
in the literature.

This inequality has the consequence that if there exists

such that

for all

, then there exists

for all finite

.
If we consider the contrapositive of this statement, it says that if there exists ‘large’ subsets with suitably ‘small’ boundaries, then this, in turn, presents us with a bound on the growth of

Corollary 6.4

Suppose that there exists

and a sequence of finite subsets

such that

(1)

|\partial A_{n}|\to\infty

(2)

\depth(A_{n})>\frac{1}{K}|\partial A_{n}|-K

has quadratic growth.

9.
Closing remarks

We close this paper with a question. In the introduction, we remarked that the convergence group theorem implies that hyperbolic groups with (

UBQ

) are virtually Fuchsian. One might protest that the consequences here are actually stronger.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
X
be a geodesic metric space. We define the
uniformly bisecting geodesics
property, denoted (

UBG

), as follows:

(
\rm{UBG}
)

There exists
\sigma>0
such that for any connected, bi-infinite
geodesic

\rho\subset X
, we have that
X\setminus N_{\sigma}(\rho)
contains exactly two wide connected components.

In other words, the definition of (

UBG

) is obtained by simply taking Definition
1.1
and replacing the word ‘quasi-geodesic’ with ‘geodesic’.
Note that (

UBG

) is
a priori
much weaker than (

UBQ

). However, it follows from the convergence group theorem and standard properties of hyperbolic groups that an infinite hyperbolic group is virtually Fuchsian if and only if its Cayley graphs have (

UBG

). This begs the natural question.

Question 9.2
.

Let
X
be an infinite, connected, locally finite, quasi-transitive graph satisfying (

UBG

). Then, is
X
quasi-isometric to either the Euclidean plane or the hyperbolic plane?

I expect the answer to be negative in general, but perhaps positive under the right additional hypotheses. For example, it is not obvious that (

UBG

) should be invariant under quasi-isometry. Very little is known about the structure of geodesic rays in an arbitrary locally finite, quasi-transitive graph, and the outlook is relatively bleak. It could be that there is a counterexample to the above question which just contains ‘very few’ bi-infinite geodesics, by some measure. This would be very interesting to see.

We could also ask a slightly weaker question. Perhaps instead of considering
all
quasi-geodesics like we did in Definition
1.1
, we just consider
(\lambda,c)
-quasi-geodesics where
\lambda
and
c
are some fixed universal constants. It is plausible to imagine that if, say, all
(100,100)
-quasi-geodesics uniformly coarsely bisect a locally finite, quasi-transitive graph
X
, then
X
is quasi-isometric to a plane. The existence of such universal constants do not follow from our proof of Theorem
A
.

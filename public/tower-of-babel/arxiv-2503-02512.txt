LTL Verification of Memoryful Neural Agents
Mehran Hosseini, Alessio Lomuscio, Nicola Paoletti

Abstract
. We present a framework for verifying Memoryful Neural Multi-Agent Systems (MN-MAS) against full Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications. In MN-MAS, agents interact with a non-deterministic, partially observable environment. Examples of MN-MAS include multi-agent systems based on feed-forward and recurrent neural networks or state-space models. Different from previous approaches, we support the verification of both bounded and unbounded LTL specifications. We leverage well-established bounded model checking techniques, including lasso search and invariant synthesis, to reduce the verification problem to that of constraint solving. To solve these constraints, we develop efficient methods based on bound propagation, mixed-integer linear programming, and adaptive splitting. We evaluate the effectiveness of our algorithms in single and multi-agent environments from the Gymnasium and PettingZoo libraries, verifying unbounded specifications for the first time and improving the verification time for bounded specifications by an order of magnitude compared to the SoA.

1.
Introduction

With the rise of machine learning, we are increasingly witnessing applications of neural networks (NNs) to autonomous systems. While
neural agents
may solve significantly more complex tasks than traditionally programmed
counterparts
(
Silver et al., 2017
;
Vinyals et al., 2019
)
, NNs suffer from fragilities
(
Szegedy et al., 2014
)
, which is a concern in safety-critical applications. Thus, they require rigorous guarantees, as those offered by formal verification.

In this paper, we develop the methodology for verifying systems comprising multiple memoryful neural
agents that interact with an uncertain environment over time in a
closed loop. We refer to such systems as
Memoryful Neural Multi-Agent System

(
MN-MAS
).

The verification of MN-MAS is more challenging than the
verification of individual predictions made by NNs, due to the
presence of multiple networks, multiple time steps, and temporal
dependencies between predictions. Several methods for the verification
of systems with NNs “in the loop” have been recently
proposed
(
Hosseini and
Lomuscio, 2023
;
Fan
et al., 2020
)
. Nevertheless, these are limited by at least two of the following: they only consider single-agent systems; are limited to the analysis of (time-)bounded reachability; do not
account for uncertainty in the environment; or the agents are
implemented by feedforward models, thus are stateless/memoryless.

We propose a verification approach for MN-MAS that addresses all the
above limitations. The approach presented here supports
verification of MN-MAS against full
linear
temporal logic

(
Pnueli, 1977
)
. Thus, the method presented here goes beyond simple
bounded reachability analysis and, unlike existing methods, can verify both bounded and
unbounded
specification, as well as nested properties. These include (unbounded) safety (e.g.,
will an agent always remain in a safe state region?
) or stability (e.g.,
will an agent reach its target and remain there long enough thereafter?
).

Although we show that the verification of unbounded specifications is
undecidable in general, we develop sound or complete procedures based
on Bounded Model Checking (BMC)
(
Biere et al., 2009
)
, which allows us to
verify unbounded properties of the MN-MAS using finite paths. In
particular, we develop three BMC procedures, based on, respectively,
simple path unrolling, lasso search, and inductive invariants.
Thus, when the presented method establishes that an MN-MAS
S

satisfies an LTL specification
\phi
, written
S\models\phi
, then

\phi
must hold for all the paths of the system induced by the
uncertain (non-deterministic) environment dynamics and initial states.

With BMC, we reduce the verification problem to solving a set of
logical constraints over the MN-MAS states. In particular, we
target linearly definable systems, i.e., systems that can be
formulated by
a finite set of piecewise-linear (PWL) constraints. This class of
systems remains quite expressive, as it includes, among others, neural
agents with ReLU-based activations.

We introduce two efficient algorithms to solve the resulting PWL
constraints. The first is
Bound Propagation through
Time (BPT)
, an extension of interval and linear bound
propagation
(
Gowal et al., 2018
;
Wang
et al., 2018
)
to support memoryful models, such
as those based on
Recurrent Neural Networks
(
RNNs
). BPT
is highly efficient but results in an over-approximation of the
model’s outputs, meaning that it cannot be used in all verification
instances (see
Section

3.2
). The second is a

Mixed-Integer Linear Programming
(
MILP
)-based algorithm,
called
Recursive MILP
(
RMILP
), which allows encoding the
constraints and variables during multiple time steps in MILP. Unlike
BPT, the latter is precise (i.e., does not overapproximate) and is
more efficient than past MILP approaches for recurrent models

(
Hosseini and
Lomuscio, 2023
;
Akintunde et al., 2019
)
(see
Section

5.3
).
We also employ
adaptive splitting

(
Kouvaros and
Lomuscio, 2021
)
,
which exploits the information about the reachable sets computed by
BPT to resolve
a priori
some of the discrete variables of the
MILP instances, thereby considerably simplifying the MILP encoding.

In summary, we make the following contributions:
(1)
we introduce the first framework for verifying memoryful,
neural multi-agent systems against full LTL specifications under
uncertainty;

(2)
we characterize the decidability of the verification problem;

(3)
we develop efficient solutions based on bounded model checking,
bound propagation, and constraint solving; and

(4)
we evaluate the approach on deep Reinforcement Learning (RL)
benchmarks with single and multiple agents, demonstrating its
efficiency compared to the SoA (being
strictly faster in all instances, up to one order of magnitude)
and its versatility in verifying, for the first time, both time-bounded
and unbounded specifications.

Related Work.

Following several seminal works exposing the
vulnerability of machine learning models to adversarial
perturbations
(
Szegedy et al., 2014
;
Biggio and Roli, 2018
)
, significant attention has
been paid to the verification of neural networks against input-output
relations. A special class of these consists of the verification of
local robustness including output reachability.
Most solution techniques are based on (a combination of) bound
propagation
(
Gowal et al., 2018
;
Wang
et al., 2018
)
and
constraint solving

(
Ehlers, 2017
;
Lomuscio and
Maganti, 2017
;
Tjeng
et al., 2019
;
Katz
et al., 2019
;
Botoeva et al., 2020
)
.

While this line of research focuses on open-loop systems, we here consider
closed-loop systems with NN components, i.e., sequential processes
characterised by multiple interdependent NN predictions over time. In
recent years, several methods have been proposed to verify this kind
of systems

(
Hosseini and
Lomuscio, 2023
;
Akintunde et al., 2020a
;
Akintunde et al., 2020b
;
Tran et al., 2020
;
Sidrane et al., 2022
;
Fan
et al., 2020
;
Bacci and Parker, 2020
;
Ivanov et al., 2021
;
Wicker et al., 2021
;
Wicker et al., 2024
;
Giacomarra et al., 2025
)
.

Among the above, the work of
Akintunde et al. (2020b)

focuses on temporal logic verification of multi-agent systems but
considers a bounded fragment of the logic and agents implemented via
feedforward NNs (FFNN), i.e., memoryless models. Closer to this
contribution is the method of
Akintunde et al. (2019)
, which supports RNN-based agents
but is limited to single-agent systems and bounded LTL properties, and
its recent extension by
Hosseini and
Lomuscio (2023)
, which introduces an inductive
invariant algorithm for unbounded safety.
Our work considerably
expands on these previous proposals in that it is the first to support multi-agent systems comprising neural memoryful
agents and partially-observable uncertain environments, as well as arbitrary LTL
specifications, including unbounded and nested formulas.

Verifying recurrent models on the other hand is significantly more
challenging—even for recurrent linear models w.r.t.
time-unbounded linear specifications
(
Hosseini, 2021
;
Hosseini
et al., 2019
;
Almagor et al., 2018
;
Ouaknine and
Worrell, 2014
;
Braverman, 2006
;
Tiwari, 2004
)
.

To verify models with recurrent layers, one approach is to explicitly
unroll the RNN to derive an equivalent
FFNN
(
Akintunde et al., 2019
)
. However, the size of the resulting network
blows up with the unrolling depth (i.e., the time bound). This problem
is mitigated in
(
Hosseini and
Lomuscio, 2023
)
by introducing a (more efficient)
direct MILP encoding of the RNN constraints, but still results in
often unfeasible queries. Other related RNN verification methods, but
applied to open-loop systems, include
(
Ko
et al., 2019
)
, based on linear
bound propagation, and
(
Jacoby
et al., 2020
)
, which derives a
time-invariant overapproximation of the RNN. Our solution extends the MILP encoding of
(
Hosseini and
Lomuscio, 2023
)
to allow for multiple agents and composite
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
formulae, and further enhances it with bound
propagation techniques and adaptive splitting
(
Kouvaros and
Lomuscio, 2021
;
Henriksen and
Lomuscio, 2020
)
, as we discuss in
Section

3.2
.

A related line of work concerns the verification of multi-agent
reinforcement learning (MARL) systems with neural agents
(
Mqirmi
et al., 2021
;
Riley et al., 2021
;
Yan et al., 2022b
;
Yan et al., 2022a
)
. The main
difference is that in MARL the environment is stochastic while in
MN-MASit is non-deterministic.

We denote the
n

\mathbb{R}

denote the set of all finite sequences over a set

(\bm{x}^{\langle i\rangle})_{i=0}^{n}
and

(\bm{x}^{\langle i\rangle})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to indicate a sequence in

(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\omega}
, respectively. The
t
-th
element of such sequence denoted by
\bm{x}^{\langle t\rangle}
. For a function

t
consecutive applications is denoted by

. For the sake of brevity, we treat sets like vectors and

Next, we define PWL
constraints, and then, in the rest of the section, we lay out the

Definition 0.

A
Piecewise-Linear
(
PWL
)
constraint
is a
constraint of the form

(1)

A_{\bm{x}}\bm{x}+A_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}+A_{\bm{\delta}}\bm{\delta}\leq\bm{d},

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\bm{x}}},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\bm{y}}}
, and

\bm{\delta}\in\bm{\Delta}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n_{\bm{\delta}}}
are vector
variables (or simply variables), and

A_{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n_{c}\times n_{\bm{x}}},A_{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n_{c}\times n_{\bm{y}}}
,
A_{\bm{\delta}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n_{c}\times n_{\bm{\delta}}}
, and

\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n_{c}}
are real-valued matrices. The variable

\bm{\delta}
is called the
discrete variable
and often

\bm{\Delta}=\{0,1\}^{n_{\bm{\delta}}}
, i.e.,
\bm{\delta}
is a
Boolean variable. Variables
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
are the
free

and
pivot
variables, respectively. In other words,

Eq.

1
defines
\bm{y}
linearly in terms of

\bm{x}
, in a piecewise fashion because of the discrete variable

\bm{\delta}
. We say that
\bm{y}
as a PWL functions of
\bm{x}
if
for each
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\bm{y}}}
, there exists at most one

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\bm{x}}}
that satisfies
Eq.

1
.

We consider systems comprising a non-deterministic and partially
observable environment with multiple agents, controlled by
arbitrary PWL neural policies. Such policies can be memoryful,
e.g., RNN-based, or without memory, e.g., FFNN-based. In this paper,
we focus on the verification of specifications expressed in

Linear Temporal Logic
over the
reals
,
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
, an extension
of LTL
(
Pnueli, 1977
)
with atomic predicates involving real
variables, as defined in

4
.

Assumption 1
.

We will focus on MN-MAS that are linearly definable or can be

et al., 2010
)
.

In the experiments, we implement memoryful policies in

We can now proceed to introduce the
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
specifications.

Definition 0 (Specifications).

The
Linear Temporal Logic
over the
reals
,
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
, is
defined by following BNF.

\displaystyle\phi::={\bm{c}}^{\intercal}\!\cdot\bm{x}\leq d\,|\,\neg\phi\,|\,\phi\wedge\phi\,|\,\phi\vee\phi\,|\,\bigcirc\phi\,|\,\phi\,\mathcal{U}\phi\,|\,\phi\mathcal{R}\phi\,|\,\square\phi\,|\,\lozenge\phi,

where
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
atoms are linear inequalities of the form

{\bm{c}}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{x}\leq d
involving the environment state

\bm{x}
. If a formula
contains any of the temporal operators

\,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{R},\square,\lozenge
, it is an

unbounded formula
; otherwise, a
bounded formula
.

Remark 2
.

\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
atoms are defined over the environment’s states as we are not
interested in reasoning about the hidden states of agents, although these can be trivially included in the logic if required.

The formula
\bigcirc\phi
is read as “
\phi
holds at the next
time step”;
\psi\,\mathcal{U}\phi
is read as “
\psi
holds at least
until
\phi
becomes true, which must hold at the current or a
future position”;
\psi\mathcal{R}\phi
is read as “
\phi
has to
be true until and including the point where
\psi
first becomes
true; if
\psi
never becomes true,
\phi
must remain true
forever”;
\square\phi
is read as “
\phi
always holds”; and

\lozenge\mathcal{C}
is read as “
\mathcal{C}
holds at some point in the
future”.

For the sake of brevity, we use
\bigcirc^{k}\phi
, to indicate
k

consecutive applications of
\bigcirc
, i.e., to state “
\phi
holds
after
k
steps”. For an unbounded operator, such as
\,\mathcal{U}
, we denote with
\,\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}
its bounded version; e.g.,
\psi\,\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}\phi
reads “
\phi

holds within
k
time steps and
\psi
holds up to then.” Note
that
\psi\,\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}\phi
is, in fact, a bounded formula,
which can be expressed using Boolean operators and finitely many

\bigcirc
; specifically,
\psi\,\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}\phi=\bigvee_{i=0}^{k}((\bigwedge_{j=0}^{i-1}\bigcirc^{j}\phi)\wedge\bigcirc^{i}\psi)
.

Next, the
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
satisfaction relation is defined as follows.

Definition 0 (Satisfaction).

The satisfaction relation
\models
for a path
\pi\in\Pi_{S}

of an MN-MAS
S
, time step
t
, and
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
formulae
\phi

and
\psi
is defined as follows.

\begin{array}[]{rlcl}(\pi,t)\models&{\bm{c}}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{x}\leq d&\text{iff}&{\bm{c}}^{\intercal}\cdot\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\langle t\rangle}\leq d;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\psi\vee\phi&\text{iff}&(\pi,t)\models\psi\text{ or }(\pi,t)\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\psi\wedge\phi&\text{iff}&(\pi,t)\models\psi\text{ and }(\pi,t)\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\lnot\phi&\text{iff}&(\pi,t)\not\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\bigcirc^{k}\phi&\text{iff}&(\pi,t+k)\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\square\phi&\text{iff}&\forall i\geq 0,\ (\pi,t+i)\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\lozenge\phi&\text{iff}&\exists i\geq 0,\ (\pi,t+i)\models\phi;\\[2.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\psi\,\mathcal{U}\phi&\text{iff}&\exists i\geq 0,\ (\pi,t+i)\models\phi\ \wedge\\
&&&\bigwedge_{j=0}^{i-1}(\pi,t+j)\!\models\psi;\\[4.0pt]
(\pi,t)\models&\psi\mathcal{R}\phi&\text{iff}&\forall i\geq 0,\ (\pi,t+i)\!\models\phi;\vee\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\exists i\geq 0,\ (\pi,t+i)\models\psi\wedge\bigwedge_{j=0}^{i}(\pi,t+j)\!\models\phi.\lx@intercol\end{array}

We say that a set of environment states
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and a
set of hidden states
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
satisfy
\phi
, written

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\models\phi
, if
(\pi,0)\models\phi
for all
paths
\pi\in\Pi_{S}
where
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\langle 0\rangle}\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

One limitation of RMILP is its high computational complexity, as we prove in

4.3.

Despite the versatility of the lasso approach, it cannot be used to

in
non-deterministic environments. This is because to verify

. If there exists an inductive invariant

for
S
such that

for a formula

\phi\in

\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}

since all
initial and future states of

\phi
. This allows us to

Conclusions

We introduced the first framework for verifying full LTL specifications
of multi-agent systems with memoryful neural agents under
uncertainty by adapting well-established verification techniques.
We evaluated the introduced algorithms on
various widely used deep RL environments with single and multiple
agents and verified bounded and unbounded
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
specifications. Compared to
the SoA that consider fragments of
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we improved the verification time by an order of magnitude. This work demonstrates how foundational verification techniques can be used for verifying the safety of MN-MAS, thus paving the way for the adoption of other advanced verification methods for the safety verification of MAS.

In this section, we prove that the

1
.

, identity observation function

, and transition function

\tau:(\bm{x},\bm{a})\mapsto\bm{a}
. Now, by
(
Chen
et al., 2018
, Theorem 8)
,
deciding whether
S\models\lozenge(a_{1}>c)
for a given

is undecidable.

Appendix B
Future Research

This paper aims to introduce the wealth of tools available in formal methods literature to the MAS domain. We have adapted several well-studied verification techniques to verify MAS safety with respect to
\text{LTL}_{\mathbb{R}}
specifications. Similar extensions of other logics, such as LTL[F] and LTL with discounting
(
Almagor
et al., 2016
)
or Alternating-Time Temporal Logic (ATL) and ATL*
(
Alur
et al., 2002
)
, may be also considered. Similarly, there are numerous additional tools developed by the verification community that can be adapted for verifying MAS safety. Perhaps the most notable among these is IC3
(
Bradley, 2011
)
. Other examples of literature that could potentially be exploited for verifying MAS safety, particularly to the problems considered here, are
(
Bradley and Manna, 2008
;
Dillig
et al., 2013
;
Padon et al., 2022
)
.

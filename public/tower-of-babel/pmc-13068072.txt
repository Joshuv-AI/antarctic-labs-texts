Electronic-phononic decoupling and Fermi-level tuning enable high thermoelectric performance in Ag8SnSe6
Authors: Mengyao Li, Xueke Zhao, Yu Zhang, Jing Yu, Xuyang Liu, Mochen Jia, Hongzhang Song, Dongyang Wang, Jordi Arbiol, Maria Ibáñez, Chongxin Shan, Andreu Cabot, Ziyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Liquid-like superionic conductors, with highly mobile ions in a rigid framework, offer intrinsically low lattice thermal conductivity without compromising electronic transport. Argyrodite-type Ag8SnSe6 exhibits a melt-like Ag sublattice that drives lattice thermal conductivity (κL) below 0.2 watts per meter per kelvin, yet its low carrier concentration limits the power factor. Here, interstitial Ag atoms raise the Fermi level into the conduction band, substantially increasing the electron concentration. Simultaneously, the formation of a secondary Ag2Se phase generates lattice distortions that enhance phonon scattering. A pronounced mismatch between electronic (~200 nanometers) and phononic (~0.22 nanometers) mean free paths decouples charge and heat transport, enabling concurrent suppression of κL and retention of high electrical conductivity. This coupled electronic-phononic modulation yields a record ZT of 0.72 at ambient temperature and a peak ZT of 1.1 at 735 kelvins, with an average ZTavg of 0.72 over 320 to 735 kelvins. A unicouple device achieves 6.3% efficiency under a 357-kelvin gradient, highlighting a practical strategy for high-performance midtemperature thermoelectrics.

INTRODUCTION

Thermoelectric (TE) devices, which enable direct conversion between thermal and electrical energy via the Seebeck and Peltier effects, offer a promising solution for sustainable energy harvesting and solid-state refrigeration (1–6). Their efficiency is governed by the dimensionless figure of merit, ZT = σS2T/κ, where σ is the electrical conductivity, S is the Seebeck coefficient, T is the absolute temperature, and κ is the total thermal conductivity, comprising both electronic (κe), lattice (κL), and bipolar diffusion (κb) components. Achieving a high ZT requires maximizing the power factor (PF = σS2) while simultaneously minimizing κ to suppress parasitic heat conduction (7–9). However, the intrinsic coupling among these parameters presents a fundamental challenge: Strategies aimed at enhancing σ (e.g., carrier doping) often increase carrier scattering or elevate κe, while methods to reduce κL (e.g., nanostructuring) may adversely affect charge transport (10–16). Decoupling these interdependent factors is therefore essential for the development of high-performance TE materials.

Argyrodite-type Ag8SnSe6 has emerged as a compelling candidate for high-efficiency TE applications, owing to its intrinsic phonon-liquid-electron-crystal behavior (17). In this compound, the rigid Se-Sn sublattice maintains electrical conductivity, while the loosely bonded Ag+ ions exhibit liquid-like mobility, leading to extreme phonon anharmonicity and ultralow lattice thermal conductivity (κL ≈ 0.15 to 0.20 W m−1 K−1 at 300 K), comparable to wood and only three times higher than air (18, 19). This unique structure results in record-low sound velocity and phonon cutoff frequency, enabling suppression of κL without a major compromising of the electronic conduction. Furthermore, Ag8SnSe6 comprises earth-abundant, environmentally benign elements, aligning with scalability and eco-friendly applications. Its exceptionally low κL positions Ag8SnSe6 as a paradigm for overcoming the ZT optimization bottleneck in midtemperature n-type TE materials (20).

Various strategies have been developed to modulate the band structure, carrier concentration, and microstructure of Ag8SnSe6, leading to substantial improvements in its TE performance (21–30). Composition tuning, such as introducing a slight excess of Sn (Ag8Sn1.005Se6), enhances carrier concentration and mobility (24). Cation substitution and lattice engineering further contribute to performance optimization (26). For example, Ta doping at the Sn site enables precise control over electron concentration. Partial substitution of Ag with Cu (Ag7.95Cu0.05SnSe6) lowers the lattice thermal conductivity from ~0.17 to ~0.12 W m−1 K−1 and slightly enhances the PF, yielding a ZT of ~0.85 at 645 K (18). In addition, incorporating mixed cations such as Ga, Cu, or Nb intensifies phonon scattering, further suppressing thermal conductivity and improving TE performance (21). While Ag8SnSe6 exhibits a peak ZT value now exceeding 1 at mid-to-high temperatures, primarily due to its intrinsically low thermal conductivity, its relatively low PF and ZT value at lower temperatures retain substantial limitations to overall device efficiency. Therefore, further efforts are required to concurrently enhance both the average ZT and PF to fully realize the practical potential of Ag8SnSe6-based TE devices.

Here, we demonstrate a strategy to enhance the electrical transport properties and TE performance of n-type Ag8SnSe6 through interstitial doping with Ag, enabling a record ZT value at ambient temperature. The introduction of excess Ag atoms at interstitial sites shifts the Fermi level upward into the conduction band, substantially improving electron transport. This interstitial doping approach leads to a pronounced enhancement in the PF, particularly in the low-temperature regime. Furthermore, a unicouple TE device based on the optimized material is fabricated and evaluated, confirming its potential for practical energy conversion applications.

RESULTS

Intrinsic Ag8SnSe6 behaves as a nondegenerate semiconductor with a low carrier concentration, where charge transport is dominated by single-band, near-band-edge states, resulting in a limited PF. Increasing the carrier concentration and driving the system toward a degenerate regime are essential for improving its electrical transport. To achieve this, excess Ag atoms were introduced at interstitial sites (Fig. 1A). Density functional theory (DFT) calculations confirm that these interstitial Ag atoms act as electron donors, shifting the Fermi level upward toward the conduction band edge at the Γ point (Fig. 1B). This Fermi-level upshift substantially increases the electron concentration and transforms the transport behavior from nondegenerate to degenerate. Under these weakly degenerate conditions, the population of conduction band electrons is enhanced, leading to a pronounced increase in electrical conductivity and PF. As a result, both the PF and average ZTavg value are substantially enhanced (Fig. 1C).

We further evaluated the electronic mean free path in Ag-doped Ag8SnSe6 at 300 K on the basis of effective mass and scattering rate calculations. The directional effective masses along the Γ-Z, Γ-X, and Γ-Y directions are 0.136, 0.170, and 0.132 me, respectively, yielding a density-of-states (DOS) effective mass of md ≈ 0.15 me (table S1). The corresponding carrier velocity was evaluated as vth=8kBT/(πmd∗) ≈ 2.83 × 105 m/s. By combining impurity, acoustic deformation potential, and polar optical phonon scattering through Matthiessen’s rule, a total scattering rate Γtot = 1.45 × 1012 S−1 was obtained (table S2), corresponding to a relaxation time of τ ≈ 6.9 × 10−13 s and a mean free path of le ≈ 200 nm (Fig. 1D). The phonon mean free path was estimated from the kinetic formula κL = 1/3Cvvsl. On the basis of a lattice thermal conductivity of 0.15 W m−1 K−1, a sound velocity of 1387 m s−1, and a typical volumetric specific heat of 1.5 × 106 J m−3 K−1 at room temperature, the phonon mean free path was calculated to be lph ≈ 0.22 nm.

This stark contrast between the electronic (~200 nm) and phononic (~0.22 nm) mean free paths illustrates that electrons can travel nearly three orders of magnitude farther than phonons before scattering. The ultrashort phonon mean free path, comparable to the lattice constant, signifies strong phonon scattering and underlies the ultralow lattice thermal conductivity, approaching the phonon-glass limit. Meanwhile, the long electronic mean free path ensures efficient charge transport, maintaining high electrical conductivity. Such decoupled transport of electrons and phonons is highly advantageous for TE performance, enabling simultaneous suppression of thermal conductivity and preservation of electrical transport. As a result, the Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag sample achieves a record ZT of 0.72 at ambient temperature, underscoring its potential for practical TE applications (Fig. 1E) (18, 21–24, 26, 29, 31, 32).

Structure characterization and underlying mechanism

Polycrystalline Ag8SnSe6 nanocrystals were synthesized via a colloidal route (see Materials and Methods for details). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (fig. S1A) confirm the formation of the cubic Ag8SnSe6 phase (JCPDS no. 019-1133), while transmission electron microscopy (TEM) reveals an average particle size of ~15 ± 5 nm (figs. S1B and S2A). Figure S2B presents a representative high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image, from which the corresponding power spectrum identifies an orthorhombic Ag8SnSe6 phase (space group Pmn21, a = 7.9168 Å, b = 7.8219 Å, c = 11.0453 Å). Lattice fringes with spacings of 0.30, 0.33, and 0.22 nm at angles of 49.2° and 83.67° further corroborate this assignment, viewed along the [−120] zone axis. The observed phase transition from cubic to orthorhombic is attributed to electron beam–induced structural transformation during TEM analysis (29). Electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) confirms the uniform distribution of Ag, Sn, and Se throughout the nanocrystals (fig. S2C).

The XRD patterns of all the Ag-doped pellets reveal a highly crystalline orthorhombic structure (ICSD no. 95093). This structural evolution can be attributed to the combined effects of high temperature and external pressure during the hot-pressing process, which provide sufficient driving force for the phase transition from the metastable cubic phase to the thermodynamically stable orthorhombic phase. With increasing Ag content in the Ag8SnSe6-x wt % Ag samples, a systematic shift of the diffraction peaks toward lower angles is observed (Fig. 2A and fig. S3), indicating an expansion of the lattice parameters. This lattice expansion provides structural evidence for the incorporation of interstitial Ag in Ag8SnSe6. No secondary phases are detected, likely due to their low concentration being below the detection limit of XRD.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were conducted on both pristine Ag8SnSe6 and Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag samples (Fig. 2B). Upon doping, the Ag 3d peak exhibits a noticeable shift toward higher binding energy. This shift can be attributed to the incorporation of Ag+ ions into interstitial sites, which increases the overall Ag concentration. A portion of the interstitial Ag+ does not participate in chemical bonding and thus exhibits a weaker electronic shielding effect, leading to a reduced local electron density and, consequently, a higher binding energy for Ag. The Sn 3d peak position remains essentially unchanged, as Sn4+ resides within the robust [SnSe4]4− tetrahedral framework, where the strong Sn─Se bonding renders the local chemical environment insensitive to the presence of interstitial Ag+. For Se, the interstitial Ag+ ions act as local positive charge centers, drawing valence electrons away from neighboring Se atoms. This electron redistribution reduces the local electron density around Se, enhances the binding strength of its core electrons, and leads to an increase in the Se 3d binding energy (33–37).

Scanning electron microscopy images of the Ag8SnSe6-x wt % Ag samples reveal that the nanocrystalline grains undergo substantial growth during high-temperature consolidation (fig. S4). In addition, the incorporation of excess Ag induces the formation of nanopores, likely resulting from partial volatilization of Ag during the sintering process (38). These nanopores can act as phonon-scattering centers, contributing to a reduction in lattice thermal conductivity. Scanning electron microscopy-energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDX) analysis confirms that the elemental distribution remains homogeneous throughout the samples, both before and after Ag incorporation (figs. S5 and S6).

HRTEM analysis was conducted on the Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag pellet, revealing well-defined lattice fringes indicative of high crystallinity (Fig. 2C). The magnified region shows the atomic arrangement along the [011] zone axis of Ag8SnSe6, with a simulated structure model overlaid for comparison (Fig. 2D). The corresponding fast Fourier transform pattern (Fig. 2E) confirms the orthorhombic crystal structure of Ag8SnSe6 (ICSD no. 95093), while the presence of an additional diffraction pattern indexed to Ag2Se suggests the formation of a secondary Ag-rich phase (Fig. 2, F and G).

Atomic-resolution high-angle annular dark field (HAADF)-STEM imaging acquired along the [011] zone axis of Ag8SnSe6 provides direct structural evidence for the incorporation of interstitial Ag. As shown in Fig. 3, the pristine argyrodite framework viewed along the [011] direction exhibits a well-ordered orthorhombic lattice with periodically arranged atomic columns. In most regions, the projected intensities of the atomic columns are uniform and consistent with the fully occupied intrinsic cation sites of Ag8SnSe6. Notably, localized extra intensity maxima are observed at positions that do not correspond to any crystallographic Ag, Sn, or Se sites in the pristine structure. Line intensity profiles extracted from representative regions (Fig. 3C) reveal an additional weakly but distinct peak located between two adjacent host atomic columns. Given the strong Z-contrast nature of HAADF-STEM imaging, this extra contrast cannot be attributed to imaging noise or thickness variation and is instead indicative of the presence of additional heavy atoms.

Atomic-scale evidence of interstitial Ag in Ag8SnSe6.

(A) HAADF-STEM image of Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag acquired along the [011] zone axis. (B and C) Intensity images of atoms marked with rectangular frames in (A). (D) Atomic-resolution EDX maps of Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag.

Considering that all intrinsic cation sites in Ag8SnSe6 are fully occupied, the most plausible origin of this extra intensity is the incorporation of Ag atoms into intrinsic tetrahedral voids of the argyrodite lattice, i.e., interstitial Ag. Complementary atomic-resolution EDX mapping confirms the homogeneous distribution of Ag, Sn, and Se at the nanoscale. Together with the systematic lattice expansion observed by XRD, these results provide compelling and mutually consistent evidence for interstitial Ag incorporation in Ag8SnSe6.

To further investigate the impact of Ag incorporation on the local lattice environment, geometric phase analysis was performed on the HRTEM image (Fig. 2H and fig. S7). Both normal strain components (εxx, εyy) and shear strain (εxy) show concentrated regions of tensile and compressive strain. The derivative strain map (Dxy) and lattice rotation map (Rxy) indicate the presence of dislocations or grain boundary–induced distortions, which may serve as effective phonon scattering centers to reduce lattice thermal conductivity. EELS confirms the elemental distribution of Sn, Ag, and Se (Fig. 2I). The result shows several Ag- and Se-enriched nanoparticles within the matrix, some of which display distinct Ag-Se–rich regions indicative of localized Ag2Se precipitates.

Given that Ag2Se is an n-type conductor with intrinsically high electron carrier concentration and low electrical resistivity, its presence as a minor secondary phase can play a beneficial role in the electrical transport behavior of n-type Ag8SnSe6. As experimentally determined (fig. S8), Ag8SnSe6 exhibits a work function of 4.58 eV, which is higher than that of Ag2Se (~4.33 eV). This work function difference establishes a favorable band alignment at the Ag2Se/Ag8SnSe6 interface, driving spontaneous electron transfer from Ag2Se into the conduction band of Ag8SnSe6. Consequently, the electron carrier concentration in the Ag8SnSe6 matrix is substantially increased, contributing to the enhanced electrical conductivity (39).

Electrical transport characterization

To investigate the impact of Ag interstitial doping on the TE properties of Ag8SnSe6, we systematically measured the electrical conductivity (σ) of Ag8SnSe6-x wt % Ag pellets over a broad temperature range (320 to 735 K), as shown in Fig. 4A. The pristine Ag8SnSe6 exhibits relatively low σ values across the entire temperature range, with a gradual increase at elevated temperatures, consistent with thermally activated carrier excitation. Upon Ag incorporation, σ is markedly enhanced throughout the measured range. This enhancement is primarily attributed to the interstitial incorporation of Ag+ ions, which act as electron donors and substantially increase the carrier concentration (n). Furthermore, the presence of trace amounts of Ag2Se as a secondary phase may also contribute to the enhanced σ. Because of its relatively low work function, Ag2Se can facilitate electron injection into the conduction band of the Ag8SnSe6 matrix, thereby increasing the carrier concentration of the host material (40). In addition, when Ag2Se is finely dispersed within the Ag8SnSe6 matrix at the nanoscale, the Ag2Se/Ag8SnSe6 interfaces may form continuous electron transport pathways, effectively reducing the overall interfacial resistance. Such heterogeneous interfaces, when favorable band alignment is present, facilitate efficient cross-interface electron transfer, which in turn enhances the overall electrical conductivity of the composite material (41).

A subtle peak in σ appears near 356 K for all Ag-doped samples, which corresponds to a structural phase transition from the orthorhombic to cubic phase. To verify this, temperature-dependent XRD measurements were conducted (fig. S9), confirming that the phase transition occurs between 348 and 373 K, and the cubic phase remains stable at higher temperatures for both undoped and Ag-doped samples. At higher temperatures, σ reaches a maximum near 500 K and subsequently declines with further temperature increase. This behavior is attributed to the onset of notable bipolar conduction, wherein thermally excited minority carriers contribute to enhanced carrier scattering and reduced electrical transport efficiency (42). A negative Seebeck coefficient (S) was measured across the entire temperature range (Fig. 4B), indicating n-type conduction characteristics. The introduction of Ag resulted in a decrease in S, attributed to the increase in n.

To elucidate the impact of excess Ag incorporation on the electronic structure of Ag8SnSe6, DFT calculations of the electronic band structure and projected DOS (PDOS) before and after Ag interstitial doping were performed (Fig. 5). A supercell model was constructed to describe interstitial incorporation, and several possible configurations were systematically compared (fig. S10). The energetically most favorable configuration, corresponding to an interstitial concentration of 6.25 at %, was adopted for the electronic structure analysis. Although slightly higher than the experimental concentration, this model reliably captures the essential modifications induced by interstitial Ag, including the Fermi level shift and the evolution of conduction bands.

The valence band maximum and conduction band minimum (CBM) are both located at the Γ point, indicating that the material exhibits a direct bandgap semiconductor character. The calculated bandgap is ~0.70 eV based on the Tran-Blaha modified Becke-Johnson (TB-mBJ) potential, which is comparable to the experimentally measured value, thereby confirming the accuracy of our computational approach. The dispersion of the conduction bands is more pronounced than that of the valence bands, suggesting a lower effective mass of electrons and potentially high electron carrier mobility. Besides, the hybrid Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof (HSE06) functional results closely reproduce the overall band dispersion and, in particular, the conduction-band features near the band edge obtained from the TB-mBJ calculations (fig. S11). The excellent consistency between the two methods confirms that the TB-mBJ approach provides a reliable description of the electronic structure of Ag8SnSe6.

For the pristine Ag8SnSe6, the valence band maximum is composed predominantly of heavy bands with multiple degenerate valleys, originating mainly from hybridized Ag 4d and Se 4p orbitals. The CBM, in contrast, is formed primarily by the antibonding interactions between Se 4p and Sn 5s orbitals, exhibiting a strong dispersive band character that is favorable for high carrier mobility. Upon the introduction of excess Ag atoms, occupying interstitial sites, several notable changes in the band structure are observed. Most prominently, the Fermi level shifts upward toward the conduction band, indicating a transition from nondegenerate to degenerate semiconductor behavior. The observed nonmonotonic σ-T behavior (Fig. 4A) does not contradict the degeneracy induced by interstitial Ag. Ag8SnSe6-based materials undergo Ag ion ordering/disordering transitions, and the mobility is strongly temperature-dependent. At low temperatures, reduced Ag ion hopping decreases scattering and increases mobility, raising σ. At higher temperatures, phonon scattering dominates and σ decreases, which is typical for degenerate semiconductors. Therefore, the observed σ(T) trend results from the interplay between mobility changes and the intrinsic phase transition, not from a lack of degeneracy.

Although the calculated band structure suggests a potential trend toward multiband conduction at sufficiently high carrier concentrations, the actual transport regime depends on the experimentally achieved carrier concentrations. To clarify this point, we calculated the Fermi-level positions corresponding to different carrier concentrations (fig. S12) and compared them with the measured values. The results show that, at the experimental carrier concentration, the Fermi level is only shifted to the conduction band edge, rather than being deep enough to activate multiple conduction band valleys. Therefore, the enhanced electrical transport arises primarily from the transition to a degenerate regime with an increased population of conduction band electrons, while transport remains dominated by near-band-edge states. This Fermi-level tuning substantially improves the electrical conductivity and PF, leading to the observed enhancement in TE performance.

Hall effect measurements were conducted to evaluate the charge carrier concentration (nH) and mobility (μH) under different magnetic field strengths at room temperature (Fig. 4, C and D). The pristine Ag8SnSe6 exhibits a relatively low nH of ~5 × 1016 cm−3. Upon Ag incorporation, nH increases notably by nearly two orders of magnitude, reaching ~1018 cm−3. This substantial enhancement in carrier concentration aligns well with our theoretical predictions and is attributed to Fermi-level tuning, where the upward shift of the Fermi level toward the conduction band drives the transition from nondegenerate to degenerate semiconductor behavior.

Unlike conventional extrinsic atomic doping, which often introduces ionized impurity scattering and degrades carrier mobility, the incorporation of excess Ag as an intrinsic dopant not only increases charge carrier concentration but also preserves, and even enhance, carrier mobility (Fig. 4D). This anomalous behavior is likely associated with the formation of Ag2Se at grain boundaries. Given its relatively low work function, Ag2Se can effectively lower the intergranular potential barriers, thereby facilitating charge transport across grains and contributing to the observed mobility enhancement (29). To further elucidate the impact of Ag incorporation on charge transport, we estimated the grain boundary potential barriers of the samples. The relationship between σ and energy barrier (Eb) can be expressed as σ ∝ T1/2 exp(−Eb/kBT) (40, 43). As shown in Fig. 4I, a linear relationship is observed between ln(σT1/2) and (kBT)−1, allowing extraction of Eb from the slope. The Eb is found to be 76 meV for pristine Ag8SnSe6, which decreases to 58 meV upon the addition of 1.5 wt % Ag. This reduction in Eb suggests that the formation of Ag2Se at grain boundaries effectively lowers the potential barrier for carrier transport, thereby enhancing the effective carrier mobility.

The effective mass (m*) values were estimated using a single parabolic band (SPB) model, which provides an averaged representation of m* in systems with complex valence band structures (Fig. 4E). Ag incorporation leads to a clear increase in m*, which is consistent with theoretical predictions. The minor discrepancy between experimental and theoretical m* values (table S1) is reasonable, as the experimental transport effective mass reflects band nonparabolicity, scattering mechanisms, partially localized carriers, and possible incomplete dopant ionization, whereas the theoretical value is derived from an ideal parabolic approximation of the CBM. Overall, the close agreement between experiment and theory supports the reliability of our electronic structure analysis.

Figure 4F presents the Pisarenko plot, showing the relationship between S and nH for all samples at room temperature. The S values of the Ag-doped samples lie systematically above the theoretical SPB curve on the basis of the m* of pristine Ag8SnSe6, further validating the presence of enhanced m* induced by Fermi-level tuning. These results are in strong agreement with theoretical calculations.

Across the entire temperature range, Ag-doped samples exhibit markedly enhanced PFs compared to pristine Ag8SnSe6. This enhancement is particularly pronounced at room temperature, where the PF increases from 0.05 to 0.56 mW m−1 K−2 at 320 K (Fig. 4G). To gain deeper insight into the origin of the improved electronic transport, the weighted mobility (μW) was calculated from the measured σ and S, reflecting the average mobility across all contributing electronic bands. The detailed calculation procedure is provided in the Supplementary Materials. As shown in Fig. 4H, μW increases notably with Ag incorporation, reaching up to 55 cm2 V−1 s−1 at 320 K. This improvement in transport is primarily attributed to the reduced grain boundary potential barriers, facilitated by the formation of the Ag2Se matrix phase, which promotes more efficient charge carrier movement.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed to assess the effect of the Ag excess on the thermal stability of Ag8SnSe6 (fig. S13). Ag-doped samples exhibit improved thermal stability, with reduced weight loss and delayed onset of thermal degradation compared to pristine Ag8SnSe6. This improvement arises from the synergistic effects of the Ag2Se secondary phase. Thermodynamically, Ag2Se stabilizes the Ag8SnSe6 lattice by forming solid solutions and shifting the phase equilibrium while chemically anchoring selenium within a robust Ag-Se framework (44). In addition, Ag2Se precipitates at grain boundaries serve as diffusion barriers, hindering the migration of Se and Ag species (41). Together, these mechanisms effectively suppress the formation and release of volatile components, thereby enhancing the overall thermal robustness of the composite.

Thermal transport characterization

The total thermal conductivity (κtotal) of all samples consistently exhibits low values across the entire temperature range (Fig. 6A). The κtotal consists of three components: the electronic thermal conductivity (κele), lattice thermal conductivity (κL), and bipolar thermal conductivity (κb): κtotal = κele + κL + κb. The κele values for all samples were estimated using the Wiedemann-Franz law, κele = L0σT, where L0 is the Lorenz number calculated on the basis of the experimentally measured S values (fig. S14). By subtracting κele from κtotal, we observe κL to reach extremely low values, especially with the introduction of excess amounts of Ag (Fig. 6B). κL + κb initially decreases with increasing temperature but exhibits an upturn at higher temperatures. This high-temperature increase is primarily attributed to the onset of bipolar thermal conduction.

To elucidate the microscopic origin of this low κL, we performed first-principles calculations of the phonon spectrum (Fig. 6D). The absence of imaginary frequencies in the calculated phonon dispersion confirms the dynamical stability of the material. The three low-lying acoustic branches exhibit small slopes near the Γ point, corresponding to low phonon group velocities and a low acoustic cutoff frequency, both of which play a dominate role in the heat transport in solids. The optical phonon branches are relatively flat, further indicating low group velocities for optical modes. As shown in Fig. 6E, the calculated phonon group velocities are mainly distributed in the range of 100 to 1000 m/s, notably lower than those of typical crystalline semiconductors. These low group velocities reflect the sluggish propagation of phonons, leading to inherently poor heat conduction. To further elucidate the phonon transport characteristics, the phonon group velocities of pristine Ag8SnSe6 were calculated along the principal crystallographic directions (Γ-X, Γ-Y, and Γ-Z), including the transverse acoustic (TA and TA′), the longitudinal acoustic (LA) modes, and the averaged sound velocity (table S3), presenting good constituency with the measure results.

In addition, Fig. 6F presents the temperature-dependent anisotropic mean square displacements (MSDs) of atoms along the crystallographic axes a, b, and c, denoted as U11, U22, and U33, respectively. The MSDs of Ag_12, Ag_15, and Se_11 are markedly higher than those of other atoms, especially at elevated temperatures, suggesting large vibrational amplitudes and weaker local bonding environments. Large MSDs are indicative of pronounced anharmonicity and dynamic disorder, which can serve as localized vibrational centers that strongly scatter phonons. Figure S15 shows the atomic displacement ellipsoid diagram at 700 K, where the length of each principal axis represents the magnitude of atomic displacement. Ag_12, Ag_15, and Se_11 display notable anisotropic displacements, consistent with their large MSDs. Furthermore, the projected phonon spectrum reveals that Ag_12 and Ag_15 contribute predominantly to low-frequency modes, while Se_11 contributes mainly to high-frequency vibrations (Fig. 6, G to I). These soft and strongly anharmonic vibrational modes effectively suppress phonon propagation and lead to a flattened phonon dispersion, thereby further reducing the phonon group velocity and resulting in ultralow κL.

Ag doping effectively reduces both the κtotal and κL of Ag8SnSe6 materials. The pronounced decrease in κL can be primarily attributed to two synergistic mechanisms: the interstitial incorporation of Ag+ ions and the formation of a secondary Ag2Se phase. First, interstitial Ag+ ions introduce local lattice distortions and increase structural disorder, thereby enhancing phonon scattering through point-defect mechanisms. Moreover, these loosely bonded Ag+ ions behave as localized “rattlers,” exhibiting strong anharmonic vibrations that further scatter phonons via resonant processes. Second, the dispersed Ag2Se nanophase provides additional phonon scattering centers, owing to interfacial mismatches and elastic discontinuities between the host matrix and the secondary phase. The combined effect of these mechanisms notably suppresses κL, reaching values as low as ~0.1 W m−1 K−1 in the Ag-doped samples.

As shown in Fig. 6C, the measured longitudinal sound velocities at 300 K remain consistently low for all samples, in good agreement with theoretical predictions, validating the reliability of the phonon dispersion analysis. A slight decrease in sound velocity is observed upon Ag doping, which correlates with the reduction in κL. Furthermore, the phonon mean free path (lph) was estimated on the basis of the kinetic theory of lattice thermal conductivity and sound velocity (fig. S16). The results reveal that Ag doping further shortens lph to values comparable to the lattice constant. Such extremely short mean free paths indicate that phonon transport is dominated by intense scattering processes, driving the lattice thermal conductivity toward the amorphous or “phonon-glass” limit. This behavior is characteristic of complex chalcogenide thermoelectrics with strong lattice anharmonicity and structural disorder.

ZT performance and mechanical hardness characterization

The simultaneous enhancement of electrical transport and suppression of thermal conductivity results in a substantial improvement in the ZT value across the entire temperature range of 320 to 735 K (Fig. 7A). A maximum ZT value of 1.1 is achieved at 735 K. The room-temperature ZT increases substantially from 0.08 to 0.72, and the average ZT value over the measured temperature range improves from 0.15 to 0.72 (Fig. 7B). This notable enhancement of ZT, especially near room temperature, highlights the strong potential of the Ag-doped Ag8SnSe6 material for practical TE applications. In addition, the Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag sample demonstrates excellent thermal and structural stability under prolonged heating conditions, which is essential for the long-term reliability of TE devices (fig. S17).

The mechanical properties of the pellets were evaluated via nanoindentation using the Nano-Blitz three-dimensional (3D) technique (Fig. 7, C and D, and fig. S18). Analysis of 3D hardness mapping reveals a substantial enhancement in mechanical strength upon Ag doping. Specifically, the average hardness increases by ~45%, from 0.93 GPa for pristine Ag8SnSe6 to 1.34 GPa for the Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag sample. This improvement in hardness can be attributed to two synergistic mechanisms. First, the incorporation of Ag+ ions into interstitial sites induces local lattice distortion and increases atomic packing density. These interstitial dopants generate strain fields and act as effective barriers to dislocation motion, thereby enhancing the material’s resistance to plastic deformation. In addition, the increased bond entanglement and localized stress fields associated with the interstitial Ag+ further strengthen the lattice mechanically. Second, the formation of a minor Ag2Se secondary phase contributes to hardness enhancement through a particle-reinforcement mechanism. The dispersed Ag2Se nanophase introduces heterogeneous interfaces within the matrix, which impede dislocation propagation via interface-pinning and crack-deflection effects. These nanoscale second-phase particles act as mechanical reinforcements that increase the composite’s resistance to indentation and fracture (40, 45). Collectively, interstitial solid solution strengthening from Ag+ doping and dispersion strengthening from the Ag2Se nanophase collectively contribute to the observed enhancement in mechanical hardness, improving the structural robustness of the material for practical TE device applications.

Prototype TE device characterization

To validate the practical potential of Ag8SnSe6-based materials for TE applications, we proceeded with the fabrication and characterization of prototype TE devices. Initially, a single-leg module based on Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag was assembled (fig. S19). When the cold-side temperature was fixed at ~294 K, the open-circuit voltage (Voc) increased linearly with the applied temperature gradient (ΔT), confirming the presence of low interfacial contact resistance (fig. S20). The device output power (Pout) increased from ~1.4 mW at ΔT = 201 K to 12.1 mW at ΔT = 369 K (Fig. 8A), corresponding to a peak power density of 0.3 W cm−2 (fig. S21). On the basis of these measurements, an energy conversion efficiency (η) of 3.7% was achieved at ΔT = 369 K (Fig. 8B). Notably, excellent device-to-device reproducibility was demonstrated across three identically fabricated single-leg modules (Fig. 8C and figs. S20 and S21). Furthermore, the single-leg TE module maintains a highly stable open-circuit voltage over 500 operating cycles, providing additional evidence of its excellent long-term operational stability (fig. S22).

To further enhance the performance and explore device integration, we fabricated a unicouple module consisting of a 2.3-mm-long p-n TE leg pair, where n-type Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag was combined with a p-type AgSbTe2-based material (fig. The module was evaluated by varying the hot-side temperature from 494 to 651 K while maintaining the cold-side temperature at ~294 K. The measured Voc again exhibited a linear dependence on ΔT (fig. S25), reaffirming the low contact resistance of the device. The Pout increased from 6.7 mW at ΔT = 200 K to 43.8 mW at ΔT = 357 K (Fig. 8D), yielding a maximum power density of 0.6 W cm−2 (fig. S26). Impressively, a peak η of 6.3% was attained at ΔT = 357 K (Fig. 8E). The measurements from three separate unicouple modules consistently reproduced these performance metrics, underscoring the reliability and robustness of the device fabrication process (Fig. 8F). This high efficiency is attributed to the optimized ZT across the entire operational temperature range.

DISCUSSION

In summary, we demonstrated that the TE performance of polycrystalline Ag8SnSe6 was substantially enhanced through Ag doping. Interstitial incorporation of Ag atoms into the lattice increased the electron concentration and shifted the Fermi level upward to the conduction band, indicating a transition from nondegenerate to degenerate semiconductor behavior and markedly improving the electrical transport properties. In addition, the presence of excess Ag in the form of a Ag2Se secondary phase further contributed to the enhancement of n-type conductivity. As a result, the Ag8SnSe6-1.5 wt % Ag composition exhibited a high average PF of up to 0.55 mW m−1 K−2 across a broad temperature range of 320 to 735 K. Moreover, Ag doping induced local lattice distortions, which effectively suppressed lattice thermal conductivity to a minimum value of 0.1 W m−1 K−1. Benefiting from these synergistic effects, a record ZT value of 0.72 at ambient temperature, a peak ZT of 1.1 at 735 K, and a ZTavg of 0.72 were achieved over the 320 to 735 K range. Building on this material optimization, we further designed and fabricated a unicouple TE module with minimized interfacial contact resistance, which demonstrated an outstanding energy conversion efficiency of 6.3% and a maximum power density of 0.6 W cm−2 under a temperature gradient of ΔT = 357 K. Together, this work offers valuable insights into the design and integration of high-performance n-type TE materials and devices operating in the medium-temperature regime, holding promise for efficient waste heat harvesting in practical applications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Chemicals

Selenium powder (99.5%), dodecanethiol (98%), oleylamine (OLA; 80 to 90%), ammonium thiocyanate, and oleic acid (99%) were purchased from Aladdin (China). Silver nitrate (≥98%) and analytical-grade acetone, hexane, ethanol, and chloroform were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Tin(IV) chloride pentahydrate (99%) and silver powder (≥99%) were supplied by Macklin and Kermel, respectively. All reagents were used as received without further purification. All synthetic procedures were conducted under an inert atmosphere using standard air-free techniques. Nanocrystal synthesis was performed on a Schlenk line, and air- and moisture-sensitive chemicals were handled and stored in a nitrogen glovebox.

Synthesis of Ag8SnSe6 nanocrystals

The synthesis was carried out in a 500-ml three-necked flask equipped with a 29-cm double-wall reflux condenser and a side-mounted thermometer. A 0.5 M selenium precursor solution was prepared at room temperature by dissolving selenium powder (13.5 mmol, 1.07 g) in a mixture of 13.5 ml of OLA and 13.5 ml of dodecanethiol in a 30-ml centrifuge tube using ultrasonication until it was fully dissolved. In parallel, silver nitrate (18 mmol, 3.12 g), tin(IV) chloride pentahydrate (2.25 mmol, 0.797 g), OLA (200 ml), and oleic acid (30 ml) were loaded into the three-necked flask and stirred under vacuum at room temperature for 30 min to remove volatile impurities. The mixture was subsequently heated to 80°C under vacuum for 1 hour, followed by further heating to 120°C for 30 min for additional purification. After purification, the system was switched to a nitrogen atmosphere and rapidly heated to 220°C. The selenium precursor solution was then injected into the flask, resulting in an immediate color change from dark brown to black, indicating the nucleation of nanocrystals. The reaction was maintained at 220°C for 30 min to allow for nanocrystal growth and then cooled to room temperature. The resulting black colloidal solution was purified by centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 5 min using chloroform as the solvent and ethanol as the nonsolvent. This washing step was repeated twice, and the purified nanocrystals were redispersed in chloroform for further use. This optimized procedure yields more than 3.0 g of Ag8SnSe6 nanocrystals per batch (29).

Nanocrystal surface treatment

Ag8SnSe6 nanocrystals from a single batch were divided into six centrifuge tubes, each containing ~500 mg of nanocrystals dispersed in 15 ml of chloroform. To each tube, 3 ml of a 50 mM NH4SCN solution in acetone was added, followed by shaking for 1 to 2 min to displace surface organic ligands. The nanocrystals were then precipitated by centrifugation. This ligand-exchange and precipitation process was repeated three times, with fresh chloroform and NH4SCN solution added at each step. Last, the nanocrystals were collected and dried under vacuum (46).

Synthesis of Ag8SnSe6-x wt % Ag nanopowders

The Ag8SnSe6-x wt % Ag nanopowders were prepared by proportionally weighing and thoroughly mixing the Ag8SnSe6 nanocrystals and Ag nanopowders in an ethanol solution using ultrasonic waves. This was followed by consistent drying and grinding steps to produce the final powder.

Bulk nanomaterial consolidation

The dried powders were loaded into a 10 by 10–mm graphite die and hot-pressed at 650°C and 65 MPa for 15 min using a custom-made hot press. The relative density of the pellets is in the range of 92 to 97%, as measured by the Archimedes method (table S4).

Structural and chemical characterization

XRD patterns were recorded on a Bruker AXS D8 Advance diffractometer using Cu-Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). High-temperature XRD was performed in an Anton Paar XRK 900 reactor chamber with a CCU 750 control unit under N2 flow (0.9 liter min−1). Measurements were conducted on squared pellets with height correction to minimize thickness errors. The morphology and particle size were examined using a Zeiss Auriga field-emission scanning electron microscope operated at 5.0 kV. The composition was determined by EDX (Oxford) at 20.0 kV. TEM and HRTEM were carried out on a Tecnai F20 microscope (200 keV) equipped with a Gatan quantum image filter. XPS was performed on a Specs system using a Mg anode XR50 source (250 W) and a Phoibos 150 MCD-9 detector under a pressure below 10−7 Pa. Data were analyzed with CasaXPS software. TGA was performed on a LINSEIS TGA 1000 instrument. Mechanical properties were evaluated by nanoindentation using the Nano-Blitz 3D method (HXD-1000TMC/LCD).

TE property measurements

The Seebeck coefficient and electrical resistivity were simultaneously measured under a helium atmosphere using an LSR-3 Linseis system. Each sample was tested over at least three heating-cooling cycles. The measurement uncertainty for both electrical conductivity and Seebeck coefficient was estimated to be ~4%. Thermal conductivity (κtotal) was calculated from κtotal = λCpρ, where λ is the thermal diffusivity, Cp is the heat capacity, and ρ is the density. Thermal diffusivities were determined using Xenon Flash (XFA 600) and Laser Flash (LFA 1000) analyzers (Linseis) with an estimated error of ~5%. Cp was estimated from the Dulong-Petit law. Hall carrier concentrations (nH) and mobilities (μH) were measured at room temperature using the van der Pauw method under a 0.6-T magnetic field on an ezHEMS (NanoMagnetics) system. Reported values are the average of six measurements with an estimated uncertainty of ~10%.

Module fabrication

The synthesized Ag8SnSe6 bulk pellet was polished and ultrasonically cleaned to remove surface contaminants. A uniform Ni barrier layer was then deposited by electroless plating. The coated pellet was diced into TE legs with dimensions of 2 mm by 2 mm by 2 mm (L × W × H). A AgSbTe2 leg (2.3 mm by 1.5 mm by 2 mm) was used as the p-type counterpart. Both n-type Ag8SnSe6 and p-type AgSbTe2 legs, precoated with Ni, were soldered onto Cu substrates using a Sn/Pb solder to assemble a single-leg TE module (11, 42).

Power generation performance measurement

The power generation performance of the TE modules (TE generators) was evaluated using a custom-built testing system calibrated with commercial modules of known performance. The overall measurement accuracy is within ~10%, ensuring the reliability of the observed performance trends and comparative analysis. Measurements were conducted under vacuum (~10−4 torr) from room temperature to 400°C. The hot-side temperature was controlled by a heater supplying a constant heat flow (Qin), while the cold side was maintained at ~21°C using a water-cooled heat sink. The TE generator converted part of Qin into electrical power (Pout), with the remainder (Qout) transferred to a Q-meter. Open-circuit voltage (Voc) and device voltage (Vd) at different currents were recorded using a KEITHLEY voltmeter and power supply. Internal resistance (Ri) and Pout were calculated accordingly. Hot- and cold-side temperatures were monitored by K-type thermocouples. The system was carefully calibrated to minimize heat loss. DFT calculations

First-principles calculations were performed within the framework of DFT using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) (48, 49). The PBEsol (Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof revised for solids) (50) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (51) was used to describe exchange-correlation effects. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 450 eV was used. The Brillouin zone was sampled with a Monkhorst-Pack k-point spacing of 0.02 Å−1, generated using VASPKIT (52). Lattice parameters and atomic positions were fully relaxed with energy and force convergence criteria of 10−8 eV and 10−4 eV Å−1, respectively. The optimized lattice parameters (a = 7.97 Å, b = 7.67 Å, c = 10.86 Å) are in good agreement with experimental values (a = 7.92 Å, b = 7.82 Å, c = 11.04 Å) for Ag8SnSe6 crystallized in the Pmn21 space group.

Electronic band structures were calculated using the TB-mBJ potential (53), which offers accuracy comparable to hybrid (54, 55) and GW (56, 57) methods for semiconductors and insulators. Seven possible interstitial Ag sites were examined, and the lowest-energy configuration was adopted for subsequent calculations.

Lattice dynamics were investigated using the finite displacement method implemented in Phonopy (58). A 2-by-2-by-1 supercell was constructed, and small displacements of 0.02 Å were applied according to crystal symmetry. Hellmann-Feynman forces were computed for each displaced structure. To account for LO-TO (longitudinal optical-transverse optical) splitting, Born effective charges and the dielectric tensor were calculated using density functional perturbation theory (59). On the basis of the force constants, phonon dispersion relations, phonon group velocities, and mean-squared atomic displacements were obtained.

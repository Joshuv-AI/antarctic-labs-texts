Melatonin mitigates intervertebral disc degeneration by suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome activation via the EGR1/DDX3X pathway
Authors: Kangcheng Zhao, Yukun Zhang, Zhiwei Liao, Weifeng Zhang, Gaocai Li, Pengzhi Shi, Zhangrong Cheng, Yuhang Chen, Shuai Li, Kun Wang, Yu Song, Xiaobo Feng, Ran An, Cao Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD), is one of the leading causes of low back pain. Inflammation is considered to be the main pathophysiological process of IVDD. The nucleotide‐binding domain and leucine‐rich pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome‐mediated inflammatory responses are critically involved in the progression of IVDD. Melatonin is known for its anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant effects. However, little is known about the potential effects of melatonin in the pathological process of IVDD. We found that the expression of EGR1, DDX3X, and NLRP3 inflammasome increased and extracellular matrix (ECM) degraded in IVDD. With the application of EGR1 siRNA, the expression of DDX3X and the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome were inhibited in stress‐induced NP cells. DDX3X/NLRP3 was regulated on dependence of EGR1. Besides, the utility of melatonin mitigated the EGR1‐induced overproduction of DDX3X and activation of NLRP3 inflammasome, thus protecting cells from pyroptosis and ECM degradation. In vivo, in a rat IVDD model, melatonin was found to be able to delay the development of IVDD by imageological and histological evaluation. In conclusion, our study demonstrated that melatonin prevented IVDD progression by regulating EGR1/DDX3X/NLRP3 axis. Our study provides insight into melatonin as a new target for therapeutic approaches for IVDD.

INTRODUCTION

Intervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IVDD) is an age‐related disease which is the leading cause of low back pain. 1 IVDD is identified to be a chronic inflammatory disease. 2 Remarkably, inflammation is considered to be one of the important pathogenic factor contributing to dysfunction of nucleus pulposus (NP) cells which is closely related to the function of IVD. 3 Amount evidence has demonstrated that inflammatory cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF)‐α and interleukin (IL)‐1β are strongly correlated with extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation and NP cell survival. 4 Currently, the exact mechanisms of IVDD are largely unknown. Therefore, a more profound understanding of the mechanisms underlying inflammation might provide new insights for IVDD.

As pattern recognition receptor (PRR), inflammasomes are cellular protein complexes that is an important part of innate immunity. 5 Nucleotide‐binding oligomerization domain‐like receptor pyrin domain‐containing 3 (NLRP3), a characterized PRR, which interacts with adapter apoptosis‐associated speck‐like protein containing a C‐terminal caspase recruitment domain (ASC) to form NLRPS inflammasome, is a cytoplasmic receptor responding to a variety of inflammation‐associated stimuli. 6 Abnormal activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome contributes to the development of inflammatory diseases, including diabetes, rheumatic arthritis, hypertension, and cancer. 7 Increasing evidence has confirmed the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in the progression of IVDD. 8 , 9 Notably, the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome could promote the production and release of proinflammatory cytokines, such as IL‐1β and IL‐18, strongly correlated with NP cell survival and ECM degradation, which may further participate in the pathogenesis of IVDD. 10 DDX3X (the DEAD‐box helicase family member) participates in RNA metabolism and plays an important role in many diseases, including inflammation, cancer, and intellectual disabilities. 11 Moreover, a further role for DDX3X as a positive regulator of NLRP3 inflammasome activation was reported. 12 , 13 DDX3X is able to promote the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and determine the cell fate by competitively combining it with stress granules. 12 Considering the correlation between NLRP3 and IVDD, 9 therefore, we hypothesized that DDX3X/NLRP3 axis activation might be associated with IVDD development and that blocking the activation of the DDX3X/NLRP3 pathway could effectively alleviate the IVDD process. Endothelial growth factor (EGR1) is a member of the early growth response proteins, associated with inflammatory responses in different diseases, including vascular, cancer, and retinopathy. 14 , 15 EGR1 has been reported to be responsible for metabolic regulation of NP cells. 16 However, the relationship among EGR1, DDX3X and NLRP3 inflammasome have not been experimentally investigated.

Melatonin, known as N‐acetyl‐5‐methoxytryptamine, is synthesized mainly in the pineal gland and participates in a wide range of physiological functions. 17 Numerous studies have confirmed that melatonin plays an important role in the regulation of various organs by scavenging free radicals, repressing inflammatory responses, and inhibiting oxidative stress and aging. 18 , 19 Recently, the protective signaling pathways of melatonin have attracted more and more attention. Melatonin could inhibit atherosclerosis progression by reducing inflammation by galectin‐3 downregulation. 20 Melatonin alleviated rheumatoid arthritis by inhibiting TNF‐αand IL‐βproduction via the downregulation of the PI3K/AKT, ERK, and NF‐kB signaling pathways, as well as the elevated expression of miR‐3150a‐3p. 21 Besides, recent studies have demonstrated that melatonin participates in the IVDD process by regulating NP cell proliferation, remodeling the extracellular matrix, and inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation against apoptosis. 22 , 23 , 24 However, the underlying mechanisms of melatonin against IVDD are not fully understood. Thus, it is of great importance to demonstrate the protective action of melatonin for its clinical application.

In this study, we aimed to decipher the protective effects of melatonin on IVDD mediated by its ability to inhibit the activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes via the EGR1/DDX3X pathway. Our research distinctly highlights melatonin's effectiveness as a novel therapeutic target treatment for IVDD through its specific targeting of DDX3X.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Clinical specimens

This study protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. Human nucleus pulposus (NP) specimens were obtained from surgical patients at our hospital. All patients participating in this study had provided informed consent. The NP tissue samples were categorized into two groups: the normal group (comprising 6 males and 12 females, with ages ranging from 10 to 48 years, mean age = 26 years; Pfirrmann level ≤ III) and the degenerative group (comprising 10 males and 8 females, with ages ranging from 36 to 78 years, mean age = 56 years; Pfirrmann level > III).

Human NP cell isolation and culture

Mildly degenerated human nucleus pulposus (NP) specimens were utilized for the isolation and culture of NP cells, following established procedures. 8 These NP cells were then seeded and expanded for 3 weeks at 37°C and 5% CO2 in Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Invitrogen). The culture medium was refreshed twice weekly, and cells from the second passage were employed in subsequent experiments. For the identification of NP cell markers (CD24, 311117; KRT18, 628404; Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), fluorescent‐activated cell sorting was employed using cells from the second passage. In the in vitro experiments, NP cells were subjected to 1 MPa pressure for a duration of 72 h. To achieve knockdown of DDX3X and EGR1, NP cells were transfected with DDX3X siRNA (100 nM) and EGR1 siRNA (100 nM) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) for 48 h.

Quantitative Real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‐PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from NP specimens or cultured NP cells using TRIzol reagent (Aidlab) and reverse transcribed following the manufacturer's instructions. A 1 μL total RNA reverse transcription was performed using the All‐in‐one First‐Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (GeneCopoeia). Subsequently, 1 μL of cDNA was subjected to PCR amplification using 200 μM specific primers and 10 μL of 2X SYBR Green/Fluorescein qPCR Master Mix. The PCR tubes were subjected to an initial incubation at 50°C for 2 min, followed by denaturation at 95°C for 10 min, and then 40 cycles at 95°C for 30 s and 60°C for 30 s. For data normalization, β‐actin was used following the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences employed in this experiment are provided in Table 1.

Western blotting

For cell lysis, we employed lysis buffer (Beyotime) and extracted NP tissues for 20 min. Subsequently, protein quantification and analysis were conducted via Western blotting, following established procedures. 8 The proteins were separated using 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate‐polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane, which was then blocked with Tris‐buffered saline containing 0.1% nonfat milk for 2 h at room temperature. Following the blocking step, the membrane was incubated overnight at 4°C with specific antibodies. Primary antibodies against collagen II (Abcam, ab34712), aggrecan (Abcam, ab3778), GSDMD‐N (Abcam, ab215203), NLRP3 (Abcam, ab214185), cleaved caspase‐1 (CST, Asp297), IL‐1β (Abcam, ab2105), and EGR1 (Abcam, ab216964) were employed in this experiment. As for the secondary antibody, we used a goat anti‐rabbit IgG antibody, and β‐actin served as the protein loading control.

IL‐1β release assay

The IL‐1β concentration in the cell culture supernatants was assessed utilizing ELISA kits sourced from R&D Systems, following the manufacturer's guidelines. Absorbances at 450 nm were measured using a SpectraMax M2 fluorescence microplate reader, and data were analyzed with SoftMax Pro version 5 Software (Molecular Devices). A correction wavelength of 610 nm was applied during the measurements.

Flow cytometry

Human NP cells from the second generation were subjected to incubation with a red caspase‐1 detection probe (FLICA 660‐YVAD‐FMK) within a shaded environment at 37°C for 60 min. Upon completion of the incubation period, the dissociative FLICA reagent was removed through a cell wash buffer. Subsequently, these NP cells were stained with 1 μM SYTOX Green at 37°C for 10 min in darkness. Finally, these cells were analyzed using a flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA). NP cells that exhibited dual labeling with FLICA 660‐YVAD‐FMK and SYTOX Green were designated as pyroptosis cells.

Histochemical staining

Human NP cells were initially seeded in a 24‐well plate and allowed to adhere for 24 h. Following this, appropriate staining procedures were performed. Meanwhile, the tail discs of rats were collected, fixed using 4% PFA, demineralized, dehydrated, and subsequently paraffin‐embedded. Subsequent to paraffin embedding, these tail discs were sectioned along the sagittal plane. The sections underwent deparaffinization and rehydration before being subjected to staining. It is important to note that the human NP specimens were treated in a manner similar to the rat tail disc tissues.

Immunofluorescence staining

The harvested NP cells underwent three rinses with PBS and were subsequently fixed using 4% formaldehyde for 15 min. Following fixation, NP cells were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. Finally, after thorough rinsing, these NP cells were labeled with secondary antibodies at 37°C for 1 h in a dark environment.

Animal experiment

Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats (3 months old) were sourced from the Animal Experiment Center of Huazhong University of Science and Technology. To replicate pressure‐induced disc degeneration, a rat‐tail compression device was employed. In this procedure, two sterilized 0.8 mm Kirschner wires were securely inserted into the vertebrae of the upper and lower segments of the rat tail, positioned at a 45° angle. Subsequently, a spring device was used to apply pressure to these segments. A microsyringe with a 32G needle was used to inject melatonin (2 μmmol/L) into the rat tail molding segment of the melatonin group, once a week in the first 4 weeks. After 6 weeks of continuous pressure application, the pressure device was removed, and the rat tail segment subjected to intervention was examined via MRI. Following MRI examination, the rats were euthanized, and disc tissues were collected, and then sliced into 4 μm sections. Histological evaluation of rat tail disc degeneration and inflammation was performed using HE staining and histochemical methods.

Statistical analysis

Data from these experiments are presented as the mean ± SEM (standard error of the mean). GraphPad Prism 8.0 was utilized for data visualization and statistical analysis. Student's t‐test was employed to assess significant differences between the two groups, while two‐way ANOVA was used to analyze significant differences among multiple groups. Significance levels are denoted as follows: *p < .05 and # p < .05.

RESULTS

The expression of EGR1, NLRP3, and DDX3X upregulated and synthesis of extracellular matrix downregulated in degenerated human NP tissues

Based on the Pfirrmann degeneration grade of IVDD, human NP tissues were categorized into normal (grade < III) and degenerative (grade ≥ III) groups shown as representative MRI images (Figure 1A). RT‐PCR results revealed a significant increase in EGR1 mRNA in degenerative NP specimens (Figure 1B). ELISA detected a notable elevation in IL‐1β concentration in degenerative NP specimens (Figure 1C). Histochemical staining indicated the expression of collagen II and aggrecan was decreased significantly in degenerative NP specimens (Figure 1D). Additionally, the expression of NLRP3, IL‐1β, GSDMD‐N, DDX3X, and EGR1 was increased remarkably in the severely degenerated human discs expression compared with that in normal human discs (Figure 1E,F).

Mechanical stress‐activated NLRP3 inflammasome and increased the expression of DDX3X and EGR1 of NP cells in vitro

To investigate the effects of mechanical stress on NP cell degeneration, we subjected NP cells to mechanical stress in vitro. The findings revealed that the increasing mechanical stress (0.2 and 0.6 MPa) did not show any harmful impact on NP cells. However, 1 MPa mechanical stress increased the extracellular IL‐1β and the expression of EGR1 significantly (Figure 2A,B). Moreover, the expression of NLRP3, IL‐1β, cleaved caspase‐1, and GSDMD significantly elevated in a dose‐dependent manner, especially under 1 MPa stimulation (Figure 2C–G). Similarly, the expression of DDX3X and EGR1 significantly increased in degenerative NP cells along with the increasing stress (Figure 2H–J).

DDX3X emerged as a pivotal factor in NLRP3 inflammasome activation in degenerated NP cells

To investigate the function of DDX3X in stress‐exposed NP cell degeneration, DDX3X siRNA was designed to knock down DDX3X in NP cells. The effectiveness of DDX3X siRNA was confirmed by RT‐PCR (Figure 3A). After NP cells were transfected with DDX3X siRNA, we assessed the mRNA levels of DDX3X, IL‐1β, NLRP3, caspase‐1, and GSDMD by RT‐PCR to examine the effect of DDX3X siRNA. The results indicated that DDX3X siRNA effectively reduced DDX3X mRNA levels but no significant difference was observed in the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome activation‐related proteins (Figure 3B–F). However, we examined the levels of DDX3X and IL‐1β in NP cells transfected with DDX3X siRNA using Western blotting (Figure 3G). The findings showed that DDX3X siRNA significantly reduced DDX3X and IL‐1β levels under pressure stimulation (Figure 3H,I). Additionally, the concentration of IL‐1β decreased notably with the application of DDX3X siRNA (Figure 3J). Furthermore, although DDX3X knockdown did not affect NLRP3 protein levels significantly in degenerated NP cells, it led to a notable reduction in cleaved caspase‐1 and GSDMD‐N levels (Figure 3K–N). Histochemical staining provided a more direct observation of protein levels, revealing that DDX3X siRNA significantly reduced the expression of DDX3X, cleaved caspase‐1, GSDMD‐N, and IL‐1β (Figure 3O,Q–T). Immunofluorescence staining was utilized to assess GSDMD‐N levels, which are closely related to NLRP3 inflammasome activation. DDX3X siRNA markedly decreased GSDMD‐N (Figure 3P).

EGR1 promoted NLRP3 inflammasome activation in NP cells by regulating DDX3X expression

To investigate the role of EGR1 in NLRP3 inflammasome activation in stress‐exposed NP cells, we designed EGR1 siRNA and confirmed its efficacy (Figure 4A). RT‐PCR results demonstrated that EGR1 knowdown effectively reduced EGR1 and DDX3X mRNA levels in degenerated NP cells significantly (Figure 4B,C). However, no significant difference was observed in the mRNA levels of GSDMD, caspase‐1, and IL‐1β (Figure 4D–F). In addition, EGR1 knockdown significantly decreased the expression of both EGR1 and DDX3X (Figure 4G,I,J). Notably, NLRP3 inflammasome activation‐related proteins, including NLRP3, cleaved caspase‐1, GSDMD‐N, and IL‐1β, exhibited a significant reduction in the EGR1 siRNA group (Figure 4K–M). Immunofluorescence staining revealed that EGR1 siRNA could inhibit the expression of both DDX3X and GSDMD‐N in NP cells (Figure 4O,P).

Melatonin inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation and attenuated degeneration of NP cells through EGR1/DDX3X pathway in vitro

In this study, melatonin was found to reduce extracellular IL‐1β concentrations in NP cells in a dose‐dependent manner (Figure 5B). The expression of collagen II and aggrecan was decreased in degenerated NP cells compared with that of the control group, while melatonin reversed the effect with the highest concentration of melatonin showing the strongest protection (Figure 5C–E). Similarly, melatonin treatment reversed the oversecretion of cleaved caspase‐1, GSDMD‐N, EGR1, and DDX3X significantly (Figure 5F–I). Whereas, there was no difference observed among the used concentrations of melatonin. To further confirm the role of EGR1 in melatonin's inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation, we constructed an EGR1 overexpression vector. Overexpression of EGR1 in NP cells increased NLRP3 inflammasome activation, DDX3X, and IL‐1β expression (Figure 5J–O). Flow cytometric analysis revealed that EGR1 could counteract the protective effect of melatonin (Figure 5P,Q). In addition, immunofluorescence staining was performed to observe the DDX3X and GSDMD‐N in NP cells vividly. As shown in Figure 5R,S, melatonin significantly decreased DDX3X and GSDMD‐N expression in NP cells under stress stimulation, and EGR1 overexpression weakened this effect.

Melatonin effectively alleviated the progression of IVDD and inflammation in vivo

We established a rat IVDD model in which SD rat tail was exposed to stress using a specialized tool to mimic IVDD in vivo (Figure 6A). After 6 weeks, MRI images were obtained. IVDs from the rats treated with melatonin displayed a significantly higher signal intensity and increased cartilage endplate height than those without melatonin treatment (Figure 6B). Histologically, H&E staining showed that the amounts of fissures formation and disc height in the melatonin‐treated rats were larger than those in the stress‐induced rats. The IHC results showed that expression of collagen II and aggrecan was decreased significantly in the stress‐induced rat, while the utility of melatonin partially reversed these negative effects. 25 The expression level of NLRP3, cleaved caspase‐1, IL‐1β, GSDMD‐N, EGR1, and DDX3X was higher than that of the control group, and melatonin decreased significantly the expression of these factors to some extent (Figure 6C).

DISCUSSION

IVDD is an age‐related degenerative disease characterized by cell dysfunction and death, ECM degeneration, and inflammatory response. 9 , 26 , 27 Etiologies of IVDD, such as genetics, mechanical loading, senescence smoking, and other factors, have been reported, and the role of pressure cannot be ignored. 9 The pathological magnitude of mechanical stress can induce metabolic events in NP cells. 28 The intensity of mechanical stress applied to cells should be considered. Previous studies have reported that the metabolism of disc is not altered at a threshold magnitude of 0.2. 28 In our study, 1 MPa was chosen to mimic physical condition caused by mechanical stress for the further study, in which, the inflammation‐associated factors were upregulated statistically comparing the low‐to‐moderate degree of mechanical stress.

Inflammation plays a crucial role in the pathological progression of IVDD, 1 in which the overexpression of inflammatory cytokines, mainly IL‐1β, leading NP cell apoptosis, downregulating matrix gene expression or upregulating the expression of collagen‐ and aggrecan‐cleaving enzymes. 29 Searching an anti‐inflammatory strategies for IVDD underscores the importance of a mechanistic understanding of IVDD. NLRP3 inflammasome, a multiple protein complex, triggers the cleavage of caspase‐1 and GSDMD upon intracellular activation. 30 NLRP3 inflammation activation is an important source of IL‐1β. 9 Cleaved caspase‐1 subsequently facilitates the maturation of IL‐1β precursor, while GSDMD‐N drills channels on the cell membrane to release IL‐1β. 31 The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome has been demonstrated to participate in neurological disorders, Alzheimer's disease, diabetes, and cerebrovascular diseases, 32 inhibition of which is beneficial. 27 , 33 Consistently, our study revealed that the expression of NLRP3 and IL‐1β was elevated in IVDD model. Besides, in human disc samples, we found that NLRP3 inflammasome priming and activation marker expression were significantly increased, amplifying the inflammatory response by releasing IL‐1β, indicating the important role of NLRP3 inflammasome in IVDD. Moreover, high levels of inflammatory factors were released on the NP cells leading to the rapid degeneration of IVDD. Hence, these findings suggest a potential therapy by inhibiting NLRP3 to prevent and reverse IVDD.

Next, we explored the underlying mechanisms that regulate NLRP3 inflammasome activation. A bulk of studies concerning NLRP3 inflammasome regulation under various pathways, including the Smad, NF‐kB, and MAP kinase pathways. 34 , 35 Notably, the crosstalk between NLRP3 and DDX3X has been widely reported. 13 Previous study provided evidence that DDX3X plays a key role in driving NLRP3 inflammasome activation under stress conditions. 36 In accordance, our research demonstrated that DDX3X induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation. This conclusion was determined by the application of DDX3X siRNA accompanied by the inhibitory effect of NLRP3 inflammasome in stress‐induced NP cells. Furthermore, we investigated the mechanism in depth by which DDX3X induces NLRP3 inflammasome priming and activation.

EGR1 is a transcription factor that could modulate cell or tissue function in response to diverse stimuli. 37 It has been demonstrated that EGR1 plays important roles in multiple diseases, such as malignant tumors, atherosclerosis, and diabetes mellitus, involving the regulation of oxidative stress, inflammation, and synthesis of extracellular and fibrous process. 38 EGR1 was reported to be enriched in the vessel wall of apoE−/− mice, accompanied by an increased expression of inflammatory mediators, including monocyte chemotactic protein‐1, IL‐1β, and intercellular adhesion factor 1. 39 In addition, EGR1 in retinal endothelial cells stimulated with UV‐B, resulted in the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome. 14 EGR1/TXNIP/NLRP3 signaling pathway contributed to SAHH inhibition‐aggravated diabetic nephropathy. 38 However, the precise mechanism, especially involved in IVDD, has remained elusive. In this study, we found that EGR1 was significantly upregulated in IVDD tissues. The level of DDX3X was concomitantly regulated by EGR1, indicating that the upregulation of DDX3X and its downstream NLRP3 inflammasome in IVDD was EGR1‐dependent. These disruptive effects were mitigated after EGR1 knockdown. With melatonin treatment, the expression level of EGR1 was declined, accompanied by the downregulation of DDX3X. Notably, the knockout of EGR1 abolished the effect of DDX3X‐induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation. In addition, the overexpression of ERG1 weakened the protective effects of melatonin against mechanical stress‐induced activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in IVDD. The graphical representation of the underlying mechanism is shown in Figure 7. To the best of our knowledge, few studies have focused on the relationship between EGR1 and DDX3X. We are the first to decipher that melatonin modulated EGR1/DDX3X signaling pathway to inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome activation and attenuated IVDD progression.

Melatonin is known for multiple cell‐protective functions, such as antioxidant, anti‐inflammatory, and antiapoptotic properties in various pathological process. 40 Recently, the protective signaling pathways of melatonin have drawn more and more attention. Melatonin has been reported to suppress NLRP3 inflammasome activation, ASC, cleaved caspase‐1, IL‐1β, and IL‐6 expression and upregulate the expression of collagen and aggrecan. 25 , 41 , 42 , 43 Notably, Chen et al. showed that melatonin alleviated IVDD by disrupting IL‐1β/NF‐kB‐NLRP3 inflammasome positive feedback loop. 24 Consistent with previous reports, in our study, all destructive alterations induced by stress stimulation were markedly reversed by pretreatment with melatonin. Melatonin is observed to be protective against IVDD, as shown by markedly suppressed EGR1 expression, further, leading to disrupt DDX3X production to inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome activation and the subsequent production of IL‐1β. Furthermore, we confirmed our discovery in vivo and found that melatonin delayed the development of IVDD at both the imaging and histological levels, which was in accordance with our in vitro results. After being treated with melatonin, no evidence of endplate injury was noted by MRI imaging. However, in the stress group, the border of the endplate appeared to be blurred. These findings were confirmed by histological staining, where lower levels of EGR1, DDX3X, NLRP3, and IL‐1β were detected in the melatonin group, indicating the potent anti‐inflammatory effect of melatonin.

In conclusion, we found for the first time that EGR1/DDX3X forms a positive feedback loop through NLRP3 inflammasome activation in IVDD and that melatonin disrupts this vicious cycle. Our study provided a new insight into which EGR1/DDX3X promotes the inflammatory response in IVDD, and melatonin may be used as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of inflammation‐associated IVDD.

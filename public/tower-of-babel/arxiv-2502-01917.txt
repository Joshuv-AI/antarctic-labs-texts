Blowup Algebras of n n –dimensional Ferrers Diagrams Thanks: August 24, 2026 Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13A30 13F65 05E40 Secondary 14M25 Thanks: Keyword: Multi-Rees algebra, Ferrers diagram, Cohen–Macaulay, normality, singularity
Kuei-Nuan Lin and Yi-Huang Shen

Abstract
. We demonstrate that the direct sum of ideals satisfying the strong ℓ \ell -exchange property is of fiber type. Furthermore, we provide Gröbner bases of the presentation ideals of multi-Rees algebras and the corresponding special fibers, when they are associated with an n n -dimensional Ferrers diagram that is standardizable. In particular, we show that these blowup algebras are Koszul Cohen–Macaulay normal domains and classify their singularities.



1.
Introduction

Rees algebras play a central and pivotal role in the realms of commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, as extensively discussed in
[
24
]
. They have a wide range of applications in various fields such as elimination theory, geometric modeling, and chemical reaction networks, as explained in
[
4
,
8
,
9
]
.

Generalizing the classical investigation of the Rees algebra and the special fiber ring of a single ideal, we study the multi-Rees algebra associated with a collection of ideals.
Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, and

I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
be homogeneous ideals in
R
.
The
multi-Rees algebra
of this collection of ideals encapsulates the homogeneous coordinate ring of the blowup along the subschemes defined by these ideals. It is also identified as the Rees algebra of the module
I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r}
, defined as the following multi-graded
R
-algebra:

\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}I_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots I_{r}^{a_{r}}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{a_{r}}\subseteq R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}].

Here, auxiliary variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
are introduced. A related concept is the
special fiber ring
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
. It corresponds to the image resulting from the blowup map, and can be expressed by
\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\otimes_{R}\mathbb{K}
.
An important approach to studying the multi-Rees algebra and its special fiber is to represent them as quotients of polynomial rings respectively:

\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\cong S[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{J}

and

\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\cong\mathbb{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{K},

where
\mu
is the total number of minimal generators of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
.

A fundamental problem in the study of multi-Rees algebras is to find the implicit equations of the presentation ideals
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
. Cox, Lin, and Sosa demonstrated that an explicit description of
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
provides insight into the equilibrium solutions of a chemical reaction network, as discussed in
[
9
]
.
Furthermore, when each of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
is generated by monomials of the same degree, the presentation ideals
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
are generated by multigraded binomials.
Consequently, the isomorphisms
\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\cong R[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\cong\mathbb{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{K}
yield coordinate rings of toric varieties, given that
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
are prime ideals.
Nevertheless, this implicit-equation problem remains open in general.

In addition to determining the presentation equations of the multi-Rees algebras, our interest lies in identifying instances where these algebras exhibit Koszul properties. A graded ring over a field
\mathbb{K}
is
Koszul
if the residue field
\mathbb{K}
possesses a linear resolution over this ring. Koszul algebras are well-studied in classical commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, due to their favorable homological characteristics.
For example, it has been established that if
\mathcal{R}(I)
is Koszul, then linear powers exist for
I
, meaning that every power of
I
has a linear resolution
[
1
, Corollary 3.6]
. Bruns and Conca extended this result to the multigraded setting in
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
, showing that when
\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
is Koszul, products of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
exhibit linear resolutions.

To establish the Koszul property of an algebra
A
, an effective strategy is to demonstrate its
G
-quadratic
nature. This involves expressing
A
via an isomorphism
A\cong S/Q
, where
S
is a polynomial ring and
Q\subset S
is an ideal possessing a quadratic Gröbner basis with respect to a certain monomial order (refer to
[
12
, Theorem 6.7]
or
[
13
]
). Using this method, Lin and Polini established the Koszul property for multi-Rees algebras of powers of maximal ideals
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
. DiPasquale and Jabbar Nezhad confirmed the validity of Bruns and Conca’s conjecture for principal strongly stable ideals
[
11
, Corollary 6.4]
. Later, Kara, Lin, and Sosa demonstrated the Koszul nature of the multi-Rees algebra associated with two-generated strongly stable ideals
[
19
, Theorems 3.2 and 6.11]
.

In this work, we begin by extending the findings of Herzog, Hibi, and Vladoiu
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
to the realm of multi-Rees algebras. Specifically, we introduce the concept of the
strong
\ell
-exchange property
for a collection of ideals, and demonstrate that the direct sum of those ideals is of fiber-type. This means that the presentation ideal of the multi-Rees algebras is the sum of linear relationships and the presentation ideal of the fiber ring (
Theorem

2.5
). This result also generalizes

[
19
, Theorem 3.2]

of Kara, Lin and Sosa, since any collection of strongly stable ideals satisfies the strong
\ell
-exchange property.

Our subsequent focus narrows down to a monomial ideal,
I_{\mathcal{D}}
, associated with an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram,
\mathcal{D}
, chosen for their relevance to statistics and coding theory (
[
6
,
14
]
). Specifically, exploring the presentation ideals of their blowup algebras offers a solution for Maximum Likelihood Estimation problems (
[
18
]
). Previous studies (
[
7
,
6
]
) have addressed the presentation ideals of
\mathcal{R}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
and
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
for
n=2
,
proving these algebras to be Koszul. However, for
n=3
, the minimal generating sets of the presentation ideals for
\mathcal{R}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
and
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
could involve cubic generators in general, making these algebras non-Koszul (
[
21
, Example 2.4]
). Due to this reason, Lin and Shen introduced the
projection property
for the three-dimensional Ferrers diagram, and established the Koszul property for
\mathcal{R}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
and
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
under this condition (refer to
Theorem

3.3
).
However, the monomial order, naturally extended from
n=3
to
n=4
, does not lead to a
G
-quadratic quality under the projection property assumption.
This leads us to the notion of
standardizable diagrams
(
Definition

3.5
), which is a generalization of the
strong projection property
in
[
22
]
for investigating the special fiber rings of a pair of three-dimensional Ferrers diagrams.

In the final section of our work, we prove that both the multi-Rees algebra and the special fiber are
G
-quadratic (
Theorems

5.10
and
5.7
) when the associated
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram is
standardizable
. Consequently, these algebras are normal Cohen–Macaulay domains, and their singularities are classified (
Corollary

5.8
and
Theorem

5.10
). It is worth noting that our proof strategy is notably concise and efficient compared to the
n=2
case in
[
6
]
and the
n=3
case in
[
21
]
.

2.
Multi-Rees Algebras and its special fibers

Throughout this work, we will use the following notation convention.

Notation 2.1
.

(1)

To avoid confusion, the set of positive integers is denoted by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and the set of non-negative integers is denoted by
{\mathbb{N}}
.

(2)

If
p
is a positive integer, we denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,p\}
by
[p]
.

(3)

Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[{\bm{x}}]={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, endowed with the lexicographic order defined by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
. Furthermore, let
r
be a positive integer and
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
be a collection of monomial ideals in
R
. For each
i
, suppose that
\{f_{i,1},\dots,f_{i,\mu_{i}}\}
is the minimal monomial generating set of
I_{i}
, such that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{i,j})=d_{i}
for all
j\in[\mu_{i}]
and
f_{i,1}>_{lex}f_{i,2}>_{lex}\dots>_{lex}f_{i,\mu_{i}}
.

(4)

Additionally, we frequently consider the polynomial rings
S=R[\mathbf{T}]=R[T_{i,j}:i\in[r],j\in[\mu_{i}]]
and
T={\mathbb{K}}[{\bm{T}}]={\mathbb{K}}[T_{i,j}:i\in[r],j\in[\mu_{i}]]
.

To describe the presentations of the
symmetric algebra

\mathcal{S}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
, the
multi-Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
, and the
special fiber ring

\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
, we introduce the following homomorphisms:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle:S\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r}),

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle:S\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r}),

and

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle:T\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r}).

Here, for each
i
and
j
, we require that
\rho(x_{i})=x_{i}=\phi(x_{i})
,
\rho(T_{i,j})=f_{i,j}=\psi(T_{i,j})
, and
\phi(T_{i,j})=f_{i,j}t_{i}
.

Definition 2.2
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\rho)
,
\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\phi)
, and
\mathcal{K}\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\psi)
.
They are known as the
presentation ideals
of
\mathcal{S}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
,
\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
, and
\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
, respectively. Alternatively, they are sometimes referred to as the
symmetric ideal
, the
Rees ideal
, and the
special fiber ideal
, respectively.

(2)

If
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{K}S
, then
I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r}
is
of fiber type
.

Remark 2.3
.

Since
\mathcal{R}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})
is a subring of the integral domain
R[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]
, the Rees ideal
\mathcal{J}
is prime. Similarly,
\mathcal{F}(I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{r})\cong{\mathbb{K}}[f_{i,j}t_{i}:i\in[r],j\in[\mu_{j}]]
is a semigroup ring. Hence, the special fiber ideal
\mathcal{K}
is also prime.

The notion of
\ell
-exchange property was introduced in
[
15
]
. It is a valuable tool for demonstrating the fiber-type property of ideals that are close to strongly stable ideals, when considering their Rees algebras. In this work, we adapt this concept to handle the multi-Rees algebras in a similar manner.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
be the ideals in
Notation

2.1
.
They are said to satisfy the
strong
\ell
-exchange property
, if the
following condition holds: for arbitrary
r
-tuple of non-negative integers

(w_{1},\dots,w_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, and any monomials

u=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{w_{i}}f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}
,

v=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{w_{i}}f_{i,\beta_{i,j}}
satisfying

(1)

\operatorname{deg}_{x_{t}}(u)=\operatorname{deg}_{x_{t}}(v)
for
t=1,\dots,q-1
with
q\leq n-1
,

(2)

\operatorname{deg}_{x_{q}}(u)<\operatorname{deg}_{x_{q}}(v)
,

there exists a factor
f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}
of
u
, and an integer
q^{\prime}
with

q<q^{\prime}\leq n
such that
x_{q}f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}/x_{q^{\prime}}\in I_{i}
.

The theorem presented below is a straightforward generalization of
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
to the case of multi-Rees algebras.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
are ideals as in
Notation

2.1
which satisfy the strong
\ell
-exchange property. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the collection of binomials
x_{k_{1}}T_{i,j}-x_{k_{2}}T_{i,j^{\prime}}
where
x_{k_{1}}f_{i,j}=x_{k_{2}}f_{i,j^{\prime}}
,
k_{1}<k_{2}
, and
k_{2}
is the largest for which
x_{k_{1}}f_{i,j}/x_{k_{2}}
belongs to
I_{i}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis for the special fiber ideal
\mathcal{K}
with respect to a monomial order
\tau
on
T
. Then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis for the Rees ideal
\mathcal{J}
with respect to an extended monomial order
\tau^{\prime}
on
S
such that
\tau^{\prime}|_{T}=\tau
. In addition, if
\mathcal{G}
is a reduced Gröbner basis, then so is
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Given two monomials
{\bm{x}}^{\bm{a}}{\bm{T}}^{\bm{b}}
and
{\bm{x}}^{\bm{c}}{\bm{T}}^{\bm{d}}
in
S
, we set
{\bm{x}}^{\bm{a}}{\bm{T}}^{\bm{b}}>_{\tau^{\prime}}{\bm{x}}^{\bm{c}}{\bm{T}}^{\bm{d}}
if and only if (i)
{\bm{x}}^{\bm{a}}>_{lex}{\bm{x}}^{\bm{c}}
or (ii)
{\bm{x}}^{\bm{a}}={\bm{x}}^{\bm{c}}
and
{\bm{T}}^{\bm{b}}>_{\tau}{\bm{T}}^{\bm{d}}
. It is the product order on
S
of the lexicographic order on
R
and the order
\tau
on
T
in the sense of
[
12
, page 17]
.

Take any
g\in\mathcal{J}
. Since the prime ideal
\mathcal{J}
has a binomial generating set,
we can assume that
g
is an irreducible binomial. We have two cases.

(1)

Suppose that
g\in\mathcal{K}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(g)=\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(g)
is divisible by
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(h)=\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(h)
for some
h\in\mathcal{G}
.

(2)

Suppose that
g\notin\mathcal{K}
. Therefore, we may assume that
g=x_{q}{\bm{x}}^{{\bm{a}}}{\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}}-x_{q^{\prime}}{\bm{x}}^{{\bm{a}}^{\prime}}{\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}^{\prime}}
where
q
is the smallest such that
x_{q}
appears in the binomial
g
. Since
g
is irreducible,
q\neq q^{\prime}
and
q\notin\operatorname{supp}({\bm{x}}^{{\bm{a}}^{\prime}})
. Thus,
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{q}}(u)<\operatorname{deg}_{x_{q}}(v)
, where
u\coloneqq\psi({\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}})
and
v\coloneqq\psi({\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}^{\prime}})
. Whence, by the strong
\ell
-exchange property of
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
, we can find
T_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}
dividing
{\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}}
and
q^{\prime\prime}>q
such that
x_{q}f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}/x_{q^{\prime\prime}}\in I_{i}
. Let
q^{\prime\prime}
be the largest such that
x_{q}f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}/x_{q^{\prime\prime}}\in I_{i}
and write
f_{i,s}=x_{q}f_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}/x_{q^{\prime\prime}}
. Whence,
h\coloneqq x_{q}T_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}-x_{q^{\prime\prime}}T_{i,s}\in\mathcal{J}
with
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(h)=x_{q}T_{i,\alpha_{i,j}}
dividing
\operatorname{in}_{\tau^{\prime}}(g)=x_{q}{\bm{x}}^{{\bm{a}}}{\bm{T}}^{{\bm{b}}}
.

By the above discussion, it is clear now that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{J}
with respect to
\tau^{\prime}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{G}
is a reduced Gröbner basis, then it is clear that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is also a reduced Gröbner basis.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

A simple adaption of the argument presented in
[
15
, Example 4.2]
shows that any set of equigenerated strongly stable ideals satisfies the strong
\ell
-exchange property. Thus,
Theorem

2.5
also offers a succinct proof of the recent finding in
[
19
, Theorem 3.12]
by Kara, Lin and Sosa.

3.
Ferrers diagrams

The final goal of this paper is to discuss the multi-Rees algebra linked to an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram. To begin with, we introduce essential concepts related to this combinatorial object.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

An
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram
is a nonempty set
\mathcal{D}
of finite lattice points in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with the property that, if
{\bm{j}}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
, and
{\bm{i}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with
i_{k}\leq j_{k}
for each
k\in[n]
, then
{\bm{i}}\in\mathcal{D}
. We say
\mathcal{D}
is
rectangular
if there exits a point
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\mathcal{D}=\Set{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}):1\leq i_{k}\leq a_{k},1\leq k\leq n}.

Whence, it is the Cartesian product
[a_{1}]\times[a_{2}]\times\cdots\times[a_{n}]
.

(2)

Given an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
, let
m
be a fixed positive integer such that

m\geq\max\Set{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}:(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\mathcal{D}}.

We will consider the associated polynomial ring

R=R_{\mathcal{D}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[m]].

For each
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
, let
{\bm{x}}_{\bm{a}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i,a_{i}}\in R
. Then, we have the
Ferrers ideal

I_{\mathcal{D}}\coloneqq\left({\bm{x}}_{{\bm{a}}}:{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}\right)\subset R.

The candidates for the presentation equations of the blowup algebras of
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagrams are similar to those of the three-dimensional case.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram.

(1)

Suppose that
H
is a subset of
[n]
. For every
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),{\bm{b}}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
in
\mathcal{D}
, we define
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
and
{\bm{q}}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
with
p_{i}=a_{i}
and
q_{i}=b_{i}
if
i\notin H
, and
p_{i}=b_{i}
and
q_{i}=a_{i}
if
i\in H
. Then, we introduce the
interchange
of
{\bm{a}}
and
{\bm{b}}
with respect to
H
:

\mathbf{I}_{H}({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})\coloneqq\begin{cases}T_{{\bm{a}}}T_{{\bm{b}}}-T_{{\bm{p}}}T_{{\bm{q}}},&\text{if
${\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}\in\mathcal{D}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which is considered as an element in
{\mathbb{K}}[{\bm{T}}]={\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}}}:{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}]
. Notice that
{\bm{a}}={\bm{b}}
if and only if
{\bm{p}}={\bm{q}}
, which will imply that
\mathbf{I}_{H}({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})=0
.

(2)

Furthermore, let

\mathbf{I}(\mathcal{D})\coloneqq\Set{\mathbf{I}_{H}({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}):H\subseteq[n],{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D},\mathbf{I}_{H}({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})\neq 0}.

This is a collection of binomials with squarefree terms.

In the next section, we are going to generalize the following result to higher dimensional case.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
21
, Theorem 6.1 and 6.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a three-dimensional Ferrers diagram that satisfies the
projection property
in the sense of
[
21
, Definition 2.5]
. The set
\mathbf{I}(\mathcal{D})
is a quadratic Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{K}
with respect to a lexicographic order and
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
is a Koszul Cohen–Macaulay normal domain. Moreover, the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
is Koszul and the ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}}
is of fiber type.

However, for higher dimensional case, the projection property condition as in
Theorem

3.3
is not enough; see
Example

3.4
below.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the minimal four-dimensional Ferrers diagram containing the following lattice points:

(1,3,4,4),\;(1,4,3,3),\;(2,1,4,4),\;(2,2,3,3),\;(3,1,3,3),\;(3,2,2,2).

It is not difficult to verify that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies the projection property. However, the reduced Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
\mathcal{K}
for the special fiber ring
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}})
contains
25
additional binomials of degree
3
when using the natural lexicographic order.

Therefore, we introduce the
standardizability
,
which can be seen as a generalization of the
strong projection property
introduced in
[
22
]
for studying the special fiber rings of a pair of three-dimensional Ferrers diagrams.
The rest of the paper is devoted to the computation of a Gröbner basis for the presentation ideal of the multi-Rees algebra associated with a standardizable Ferrers diagram.

Definition 3.5
.

An
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
is called

if for every

a_{i}=b_{i}
for

, there exists a lattice point

{\bm{p}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\max\{a_{k+1},b_{k+1}\},\ldots,\max\{a_{n},b_{n}\})\in\mathcal{D}.

4.
Ordering of tableau

The aim of this paper is to extend
Theorem

3.3
to multi-Rees algebras of arbitrary dimensional Ferrers diagrams.
In this section, we introduce a useful total order on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
, inspired by the work of De Negri
[
10
]
. We will use this order in the following section when examining the presentation ideals of the blowup algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.

(1)

For vectors
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
, we say
{\bm{a}}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}
if the first nonzero entry of
{\bm{a}}-{\bm{b}}
is negative. We will denote this total order by
\sigma
.

(2)

In this paper, by
tableau
we mean a matrix with entries in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. The tableau can be visualized as a rectangular collection of boxes filled with positive integers, similar to the
Young tableau
in the literature. If
\mathbf{A}=[a_{i,j}]
is a
p\times n
tableau with row vectors
{\bm{a}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}
, it is written as
\mathbf{A}=[{\bm{a}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}]
by abuse of notation. It is called
semi-standard
if the row vectors satisfy

(Cond-1)

{\bm{a}}^{1}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{a}}^{2}\geq_{\sigma}\dots\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{a}}^{p}.

(3)

Given two
p\times n
semi-standard tableaux
\mathbf{A}=[{\bm{a}}^{1},\ldots,{\bm{a}}^{p}]
and
\mathbf{B}=[{\bm{b}}^{1},\ldots,{\bm{b}}^{p}]
, we say
\mathbf{A}>_{\sigma}\mathbf{B}
if there is an integer
k\in[p]
such that
{\bm{a}}^{i}={\bm{b}}^{i}
for
i\in[k-1]
and
{\bm{a}}^{k}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{k}
.

(4)

Given a
p\times n
tableau
\mathbf{A}=[a_{i,j}]
, for each
j\in[n]
, we will denote the multiset
\{a_{1,j},a_{2,j},\dots,a_{p,j}\}
by
\operatorname{supp}_{j}(\mathbf{A})
. Suppose that
\mathbf{B}
is another tableau of the same size. We say that
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})
if
\operatorname{supp}_{j}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}_{j}(\mathbf{B})
for all
j\in[n]
.

(5)

A semi-standard tableaux
\mathbf{A}
is called
standard
if for every semi-standard tableau
\mathbf{B}
with
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})
and
\mathbf{A}\neq\mathbf{B}
, we have
\mathbf{B}>_{\sigma}\mathbf{A}
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

If
\mathbf{A}
is a tableau, we can turn it into a semi-standard tableau by swapping the rows.

(2)

Any subtableau obtained from a semi-standard tableau by removing rows or rightmost columns remains semi-standard.

(3)

If
\mathbf{A}
is a semi-standard tableau, the set

\Set{\mathbf{B}:\text{$\mathbf{B}$ is semi-standard with $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})$}}

is finite. Since
\sigma
is a total order on this set, there exists a unique standard tableau
\mathbf{B}
in it.

Next, we present an algorithm for finding the standard tableau from a given tableau. It is based on the following simple fact:

Observation 4.3
.

Given a chain of elements
{\bm{a}}^{1}\succeq{\bm{a}}^{2}\succeq\cdots\succeq{\bm{a}}^{p}
in some poset and a multiset of integers
B=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{p}\}
, the elements in
B
can be uniquely sorted so that the following conditions holds:

•

if
{\bm{a}}^{i}\succ{\bm{a}}^{j}
, then
b_{i}\geq b_{j}
, and

•

if
{\bm{a}}^{i}={\bm{a}}^{j}
with
i<j
, then
b_{i}\leq b_{j}
.

Algorithm 4.4
.

Given a
p\times n
semi-standard tableau
\mathbf{A}=[a_{i,j}]
, we use the following method to find a
p\times n
standard tableaux
\mathbf{B}=[b_{i,j}]
such that
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathbf{B})
.

(1)

Firstly, since
\mathbf{A}
is semi-standard, we have
(a_{1,1})\geq_{\sigma}(a_{2,1})\geq_{\sigma}\cdots\geq_{\sigma}(a_{p,1})
, i.e.,
a_{1,1}\leq a_{2,1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{p,1}
. Whence, we set
b_{i,1}=a_{i,1}
for
i\in[p]
.

(2)

Secondly, for
j=1,2,\dots,n
, suppose that the first
j
columns of
\mathbf{B}
have been determined. For
1\leq i\leq p
, let
{\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}
be the vector
(b_{i,1},b_{i,2},\dots,b_{i,j})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{j}
. Whence, we have
{\bm{b}}^{1}_{j}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{2}_{j}\geq_{\sigma}\cdots\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{p}_{j}
.

Observation

4.3
implies that the multiset
\{a_{1,j+1},a_{2,j+1},\dots,a_{p,j+1}\}
can be rearranged to
\{b_{1,j+1},b_{2,j+1},\dots,b_{p,j+1}\}
such that

•

if
{\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{i^{\prime}}_{j}
, then
b_{i,j+1}\geq b_{i^{\prime},j+1}
, and
(Cond-2)

•

if
{\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}={\bm{b}}^{i^{\prime}}_{j}
with
i<i^{\prime}
, then
b_{i,j+1}\leq b_{i^{\prime},j+1}
.
(Cond-3)

Consequently, the
(j+1)
-th column of
{\bm{B}}

is determined. Furthermore, we have vectors
{\bm{b}}^{1}_{j+1},\dots,{\bm{b}}^{p}_{j+1}
, where each
{\bm{b}}^{i}_{j+1}=(b_{i,1},\dots,b_{i,j},b_{i,j+1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{j+1}
.
It is clear from
(Cond-2)
and
(Cond-3)
that
{\bm{b}}^{1}_{j+1}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{2}_{j+1}\geq_{\sigma}\cdots\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{p}_{j+1}
.

After all these computations, we get a semi-standard tableau
\mathbf{B}
with
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

The tableau
\mathbf{B}
constructed in

4.4
is standard.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{C}=[c_{i,j}]
be an arbitrary semi-standard tableau with
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{C})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})
. If
\mathbf{C}\neq\mathbf{B}
, we will prove that
\mathbf{C}>_{\sigma}\mathbf{B}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{B}
is standard.

Let
j_{0}=\min\{j:c_{i,j}\neq b_{i,j}\text{ for some $i\in[p]$ and $j\in[n]$}\}
and
i_{0}=\min\{i\in[p]:c_{i,j_{0}}\neq b_{i,j_{0}}\}
.
Based on
Remark

4.2
2
, we can assume that
j_{0}=n
and
i_{0}=1
.

For each
i
and
j
, let
{\bm{c}}^{i}_{j}
be the vector
(c_{i,1},c_{i,2},\dots,c_{i,j})
. Whence,
{\bm{c}}^{i}_{j}={\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}
for
i\in[p]
and
j\in[n-1]
by the choice of
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
. Let
i_{1}\coloneqq\max\{i:{\bm{b}}^{i}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{1}_{n-1}\}
. Consequently,
{\bm{b}}^{1}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{2}_{n-1}=\cdots={\bm{b}}^{i_{1}}_{n-1}
.
It follows from (
Cond-1
) that
c_{1,n}\leq c_{2,n}\leq\cdots\leq c_{i_{1},n}
and
b_{1,n}\leq b_{2,n}\leq\cdots\leq b_{i_{1},n}
.
Furthermore, (Cond-2) implies that the multiset
\{b_{1,n},b_{2,n},\dots,b_{i_{1},n}\}
is the
i_{1}
-subset of
\operatorname{supp}_{n}(\mathbf{A})
with the largest sum. Since
c_{1,n}\neq b_{1,n}
by the choice of
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
, we have
\{b_{1,n},b_{2,n},\dots,b_{i_{1},n}\}\neq\{c_{1,n},c_{2,n},\dots,c_{i_{1},n}\}
. This implies that
[{\bm{c}}^{1}_{n},\ldots,{\bm{c}}^{i_{1}}_{n}]>_{\sigma}[{\bm{b}}^{1}_{n},\ldots,{\bm{b}}^{i_{1}}_{n}]
. Since
{\bm{c}}^{i}_{j}={\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}
for
i\in[p]
and
j\in[n-1]
, and

{\bm{b}}^{1}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{2}_{n-1}=\cdots={\bm{b}}^{i_{1}}_{n-1}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{i_{1}+1}_{n-1}\geq_{\sigma}\cdots\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}^{p}_{n-1},

this implies that
[{\bm{c}}^{1}_{n},\ldots,{\bm{c}}^{p}_{n}]>_{\sigma}[{\bm{b}}^{1}_{n},\ldots,{\bm{b}}_{n}^{p}]
, i.e.,
\mathbf{C}>_{\sigma}\mathbf{B}
.
∎

Next, we present a useful tool for verifying the
standardness
of a given semi-standard tableau.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{\bm{a}}
and
{\bm{b}}
be two tuples in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
such that
{\bm{a}}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}
. Then, the semi-standard tableau
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is standard if and only if
{\bm{a}}={\bm{b}}
, or there exists a
k\in[n]
such that
a_{i}=b_{i}
for all
i<k
, and
a_{k}<b_{k}
, but
a_{j}\geq b_{j}
for all
k<j\leq n
.

Proof.

If
{\bm{a}}={\bm{b}}
, then for any semi-standard tableau
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
with
\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}])=\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}])
, we must have
{\bm{p}}={\bm{q}}={\bm{a}}={\bm{b}}
. Therefore,
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is standard. Thus, in the following, we will assume
{\bm{a}}\neq{\bm{b}}
.

Suppose that there exists a value
k
such that
a_{i}=b_{i}
for all
i<k
, and
a_{k}<b_{k}
but
a_{j}\geq b_{j}
for all
k<j\leq n
. Then, for any semi-standard tableau
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
with
\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}])=\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}])
, we have
p_{i}=a_{i}=b_{i}=q_{i}
for all
i<k
. It is clear that
a_{k}=\min\{a_{k},b_{k}\}=\min\{p_{k},q_{k}\}=p_{k}
by (
Cond-1
). Additionally,
a_{j}=\max\{a_{j},b_{j}\}=\max\{p_{j},q_{j}\}\geq p_{j}
for all
k<j\leq n
, by the assumptions on
k
. Therefore,
{\bm{p}}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{a}}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}
. Moreover,
{\bm{p}}={\bm{a}}
if and only if
{\bm{q}}={\bm{b}}
. Thus,
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]\geq_{\sigma}[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
, implying that
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is standard.

Conversely, suppose that no such
k
exists.
Since
{\bm{a}}\neq{\bm{b}}
and
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is semi-standard, we have
a_{k^{\prime}}<b_{k^{\prime}}
from (
Cond-1
) for
k^{\prime}\coloneqq\min\{j\in[n]:a_{j}\neq b_{j}\}
. Due to the non-existence of such
k
and the choice of
k^{\prime}
, we can find
j>k^{\prime}
such that
a_{j}<b_{j}
. Then, we consider the tuples
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
and
{\bm{q}}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
such that

p_{\ell}=\begin{cases}a_{\ell},&\text{if $\ell\neq j$,}\\
b_{j},&\text{if $\ell=j$,}\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{\ell}=\begin{cases}b_{\ell},&\text{if $\ell\neq j$,}\\
a_{j},&\text{if $\ell=j$.}\end{cases}

It is clear that
{\bm{a}}>_{\sigma}{\bm{p}}\geq_{\sigma}{\bm{q}}
and
\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}])=\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}])
.
This indicates that
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is not standard.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

A semi-standard
p\times n
tableau
\mathbf{A}=[{\bm{a}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}]
is standard if and only if
[{\bm{a}}^{h},{\bm{a}}^{k}]
is standard for every
1\leq h<k\leq p
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{B}=[{\bm{b}}^{1},\ldots,{\bm{b}}^{p}]
be the tableau constructed in
Algorithm

4.4
from
\mathbf{A}
. According to
Lemma

4.6
,
\mathbf{B}
is standard. Furthermore, it follows from the construction of
\mathbf{B}
in
Algorithm

4.4
and
Lemma

4.7
that
[{\bm{b}}^{h},{\bm{b}}^{k}]
is standard for every
1\leq h<k\leq p
.

(1)

Suppose that
\mathbf{A}
is standard. It follows from the uniqueness of standard tableau that
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}
. Consequently,
[{\bm{a}}^{h},{\bm{a}}^{k}]
is standard for every
1\leq h<k\leq p
.

(2)

Conversely, suppose that
[{\bm{a}}^{h},{\bm{a}}^{k}]
is standard for every
1\leq h<k\leq p
. We prove that
\mathbf{A}
is standard. For this purpose, in the following, we prove by induction on
n
that
{\bm{a}}^{i}={\bm{b}}^{i}
for each
i
. Suppose that the
(i,j)
-entries of the semi-standard tableaux
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are
a_{i,j}
and
b_{i,j}
, respectively.

Notice that the base where
n=1
is clear, since
a_{1,1}\leq a_{2,1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{p,1}
and
b_{1,1}\leq b_{2,1}\leq\cdots\leq b_{p,1}

from (
Cond-1
).
Now, consider the general case. For

i\in[p]
and
j\in[n]
,
let
{{\bm{a}}}^{i}_{j}=({a}_{i,1},\dots,{a}_{i,j})
and
{\bm{b}}^{i}_{j}=(b_{i,1},\dots,b_{i,j})
, by abuse of notation. It follows from
Lemma

4.7
that both
[{\bm{a}}^{h}_{n-1},{\bm{a}}^{k}_{n-1}]
and
[{\bm{b}}^{h}_{n-1},{\bm{b}}^{k}_{n-1}]
are standard for
1\leq h\leq k\leq p
. Furthermore, it is clear that
\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{a}}^{1}_{n-1},{\bm{a}}^{2}_{n-1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}_{n-1}])=\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{b}}^{1}_{n-1},{\bm{b}}^{2}_{n-1},\dots,{\bm{b}}^{p}_{n-1}])
. Hence, by the induction hypothesis for the
n-1
case, we know that
{\bm{a}}^{i}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{i}_{n-1}
for each
i
.

Moreover, for
1\leq h<k\leq p
, since
[{{\bm{a}}}^{h}_{n}={{\bm{a}}}^{h},{{\bm{a}}}^{k}_{n}={{\bm{a}}}^{k}]
and
[{{\bm{b}}}^{h}_{n}={{\bm{b}}}^{h},{{\bm{b}}}^{k}_{n}={{\bm{b}}}^{k}]
are standard, we have the following observations from
Lemma

4.7
:

•

if
{\bm{a}}^{h}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{h}_{n-1}>_{\sigma}{\bm{a}}^{k}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{k}_{n-1}
, then
{a}_{h,n}\geq{a}_{k,n}
and
b_{h,n}\geq b_{k,n}
;

•

if
{\bm{a}}^{h}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{h}_{n-1}={\bm{a}}^{k}_{n-1}={\bm{b}}^{k}_{n-1}
, then
{a}_{h,n}\leq{a}_{k,n}
and
b_{h,n}\leq b_{k,n}
.

Since
\sigma
is a total order, we deduce readily from
Observation

4.3
that the ordered multiset
\{a_{1,n},a_{2,n},\dots,a_{p,n}\}
and
\{b_{1,n},b_{2,n},\dots,b_{p,n}\}
are uniquely determined. Since
\operatorname{supp}_{n}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}_{n}(\mathbf{B})
, we have
a_{i,n}=b_{i,n}
for each
i
. In short,
{\bm{a}}^{i}_{n}={\bm{b}}^{i}_{n}
for each
i
.

Consequently,
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}
. In particular,
\mathbf{A}
is standard.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Any subtableau obtained from a standard tableau by removing rows or rightmost columns remains standard.

5.
Multi-Rees algebras of standardizable Ferrers diagrams

Since we have gathered all the necessary tools, we establish the Gröbner bases of the presentation ideals of multi-Rees algebras and their special fibers in this section.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram as in
Definition

3.1
. We begin by defining a monomial order on the base rings
R={\mathbb{K}}[{\bm{x}}]={\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[m]]
,
T={\mathbb{K}}[{\bm{T}}]={\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}}}:{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}
, and
S=R[{\bm{T}}]=R[T_{\bm{a}}:{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}]
.

Definition 5.1
.

(1)

The variables in
R
are naturally ordered:
x_{i,j}>x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
if and only if
i<i^{\prime}
or
i=i^{\prime}
and
j<j^{\prime}
. We apply the lexicographic order to
R
based on this natural order.

(2)

For any vectors
{\bm{a}}
and
{\bm{b}}
in
\mathcal{D}
, we define
T_{{\bm{a}}}>T_{{\bm{b}}}
if
{\bm{a}}>_{\sigma}{\bm{b}}
according to
Definition

4.1
.
The induced lexicographic order on the polynomial ring
T
from this order of the indeterminates is also denoted by
{\sigma}
, by abuse of notation.

(3)

As in the proof of
Theorem

2.5
, let
\sigma^{\prime}
denote the product order of the lexicographic order
R
, with the order
\sigma
on
T
.

(4)

When working with a
p\times n
semi-standard tableau
\mathbf{A}=[{\bm{a}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}]
with row vectors in
\mathcal{D}
, the monomial
T_{{\bm{a}}^{1}}T_{{\bm{a}}^{2}}\cdots T_{{\bm{a}}^{p}}
in the polynomial ring
T
is denoted by
{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}
.

Remark 5.2
.

If two
p\times n
semi-standard tableaux,
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, have row vectors in
\mathcal{D}
, then
{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}>_{\sigma}{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{B}}
if and only if
\mathbf{A}>_{\sigma}\mathbf{B}
. This implies that if
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})
and
\mathbf{B}
is standard, then the leading term of
{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}-{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{B}}
is
{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}
.

Notation 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram and
r
be a positive integer. By the loose restriction on the integer
m
in
Definition

3.1
, without loss of generality, we may assume that
r\leq m
. We will denote the Cartesian product
\mathcal{D}\times[r]
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
. It is clear that this is an
(n+1)
-dimensional Ferrers diagram. Furthermore, if
R_{\mathcal{D}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[m]]
is the base ring for considering the Ferrers ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}}
, then we may choose
R_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}}\coloneqq R_{\mathcal{D}}[x_{n+1,1},\dots,x_{n+1,m}]
for considering the Ferrers ideal
I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}}
.

Remark 5.4
.

We have a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})\cong\mathcal{F}(I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}})
of special fiber rings, which is clear from the
equality of the ideal
I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}}
with the product ideal
(x_{n+1,1},\dots,x_{n+1,r})I_{\mathcal{D}}
in
R_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}}
.

The proposition below is analogous to Proposition 2.11 in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a rectangular
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram,
r
be a positive integer, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
be the
(n+1)
-dimensional Ferrers diagram associated with
\mathcal{D}
. Then the set
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\mathbf{I}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}})
defined in
Definition

3.2
is a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
\mathcal{K}
of the special fiber
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
with respect to the monomial order
\sigma
defined in
Definition

5.1
.

Proof.

By
Remark

5.4
, we need to show
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\mathbf{I}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}})
is a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
\mathcal{K}
of the special fiber ring
\mathcal{F}(I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}})
. By
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
, the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\coloneqq\{{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}-{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle:\text{ $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are two semi-standard tableaux with}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ row vectors in $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}$ and $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{B})$}\}

is a generating set of the presentation ideal of
\mathcal{F}(I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}})
as a
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space. Since
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f)\in(\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(g):g\in\mathcal{G})
for every
f\in\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
f={\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}-{\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{B}}
with
\text{supp}(\mathbf{A})=\text{supp}(\mathbf{B})
and
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f)={\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathbf{A}=[{\bm{a}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{p}]
.

(1)

If
\mathbf{A}
is not standard, by
Proposition

4.8
, there exist
1\leq h<k\leq p
such that the
2
-row semi-standard tableau
[{\bm{a}}^{h},{\bm{a}}^{k}]
is not standard. Applying
Algorithm

4.4
, we can find a standard tableau
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
from
[{\bm{a}}^{h},{\bm{a}}^{k}]
. Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
is also a rectangular diagram,
{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
. Whence, the binomial
f^{\prime}\coloneqq T_{{\bm{a}}^{h}}T_{{\bm{a}}^{k}}-T_{{\bm{p}}}T_{{\bm{q}}}
belongs to
\mathcal{G}
. Notice that
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f^{\prime})=T_{{\bm{a}}^{h}}T_{{\bm{a}}^{k}}
, which divides
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f)={\bm{T}}_{\mathbf{A}}
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f)\in(\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(g):g\in\mathcal{G})
.

(2)

If
\mathbf{A}
is standard, then
\mathbf{B}\geq_{\sigma}\mathbf{A}
. Since
\operatorname{in}_{\sigma}(f)=T_{\mathbf{A}}
, this implies that
T_{\mathbf{B}}=T_{\mathbf{A}}
and
f=0
. This case is trivial. ∎

The previous proof relies heavily on the existence of the standard tableau in the case of rectangular diagrams. The following lemma demonstrates that this existence can be extended to a standardizable Ferrers diagram.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram that is standardizable.

(1)

Let
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
and
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
be two distinct
2\times n
semi-standard tableaux such that
\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}])=\operatorname{supp}([{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}])
. If
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
is standard and
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D}
, then
{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}\in\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

Let
[\tilde{{\bm{a}}},\tilde{{\bm{b}}}]
and
[\tilde{{\bm{p}}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}}]
be two distinct
2\times(n+1)
semi-standard tableaux such that
\operatorname{supp}([\tilde{{\bm{p}}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}}])=\operatorname{supp}([\tilde{{\bm{a}}},\tilde{{\bm{b}}}])
. If
[\tilde{{\bm{p}}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}}]
is standard and
\tilde{{\bm{a}}},\tilde{{\bm{b}}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
, then
\tilde{{\bm{p}}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
.

Proof.

(1)

By the uniqueness of standard tableau, we know
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is not standard. In particular,
{\bm{a}}\neq{\bm{b}}
. But since
[{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}]
is semi-standard, there is a
k
such that
a_{i}=b_{i}
for
i<k
, and
a_{k}<b_{k}
. This implies that
a_{i}=p_{i}=q_{i}=b_{i}
for
i<k
and
a_{k}=p_{k}<q_{k}=b_{k}
. Since
[{\bm{p}},{\bm{q}}]
is standard, by
Lemma

4.7
, we must have
p_{j}=\max\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\geq q_{j}=\min\{a_{j},b_{j}\}
for
j>k
.
Since
\mathcal{D}
is standardizable and
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D}
, we obtain
{\bm{p}}\in\mathcal{D}
by
Definition

3.5
. Notice that
q_{j}\leq b_{j}
for each
j
.
Since
{\bm{b}}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
, we can conclude that
{\bm{q}}
is also an element of
\mathcal{D}
due to the Ferrers property of
\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

Since
\mathcal{D}
is standardizable, it is clear that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}

is also standardizable. Thus, it remains to apply the previous part with respect to
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
.
∎

We are now ready to state one of the main theorems of this work.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a standardizable
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram,
I_{\mathcal{D}}
be the ideal associated with
\mathcal{D}
, and
r
be a positive integer. Then the set
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\mathbf{I}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}})
defined in
Definition

3.2
is a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
\mathcal{K}
of the special fiber
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
. In particular,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is Koszul.

Proof.

By
Remark

5.4
,
Lemma

5.6
,
[
23
, Proposition 4.13]
, and
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
, it suffices to assume that
\mathcal{D}
is a rectangular Ferrers diagram. Meanwhile, the corresponding result for this special case has already been given in
Proposition

5.5
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a standardizable
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram and
I_{\mathcal{D}}
be the ideal associated with
\mathcal{D}
. Then the special fiber
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is a Cohen–Macaulay normal domain.
Furthermore, if the field
{\mathbb{K}}
has characteristic zero,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
has rational singularities. If
{\mathbb{K}}
has positive characteristic,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is
F
-rational .

Proof.

Notice that
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is isomorphic to the semigroup ring
{\mathbb{K}}[I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}}}]
.
By
Theorem

5.7
and
Definition

3.2
, the presentation ideal
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
has a squarefree initial ideal. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is normal by
[
23
, Proposition 13.15]
, and it is Cohen–Macaulay by

[
16
, Theorem 1]
.

The following argument from
[
5
, Corollary 2.3]
proves the statements regarding the singularities. We cite it here for readers’ convenience.
By Hochster
[
16
, Proposition 1]
, since
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is normal, it is a direct summand of a polynomial ring. Thus by a theorem of Boutot
[
2
]
,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
has rational singularities if the characteristic of
{\mathbb{K}}
is zero.
Furthermore, by a theorem of Hochster and Huneke
[
17
]
,
\mathcal{F}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is strongly
F
-regular. In particular, it is
F
-rational, if
{\mathbb{K}}
has positive characteristic.
∎

In the last part of this work, we investigate the multi-Rees algebras. The following lemma shows that
any collection of Ferrers ideals
satisfies the strong
\ell
-exchange property as defined in
Definition

2.4
. It paves the way for finding the presentation equations of the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{r}
be a collection of Ferrers diagrams, where
\mathcal{D}_{k}
is
n_{k}
-dimensional for each
k
. Set
n\coloneqq\max\{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\}
. Let
m
be large enough so that we have the Ferrers ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}_{k}}\subset R_{k}\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j},i\in[n_{k}],j\in[m]]\subseteq R\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,m},x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,m},\dots,x_{n,1},\dots,x_{n,m}]
, for each
k
. Then, the collection of ideals
I_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\dots,I_{\mathcal{D}_{r}}
satisfies the strong
\ell
-exchange property.

Proof.

For notational simplicity, we denote the variables

x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,m},x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,m},\dots,x_{n,1},\dots,x_{n,m}

in
R
by
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{nm}
. Suppose that
\{f_{k,1},\dots,f_{k,\mu_{i}}\}
is a minimal monomial generating set of
I_{\mathcal{D}_{k}}
, for
k\in[r]
. Furthermore, suppose that
(w_{1},\dots,w_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, and
u=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{w_{k}}f_{k,\alpha_{k,\ell}}
,
v=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{w_{k}}f_{k,\beta_{k,\ell}}
satisfy

(1)

\operatorname{deg}_{z_{t}}(u)=\operatorname{deg}_{z_{t}}(v)
for
t=1,\dots,q-1
with
q\leq nm-1
,

(2)

\operatorname{deg}_{z_{q}}(u)<\operatorname{deg}_{z_{q}}(v)
.

Suppose that
z_{q}=x_{i_{0},j_{0}}
in the above notation.
Then,

\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}-1}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}-1}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(v)\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(u)<\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(v).

Since

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in[r],\\
n_{k}\geq i_{0}\end{subarray}}w_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j}}(v),

we must have
j_{0}\leq m-1
.
Furthermore, we can find
j_{1}
with
j_{0}<j_{1}\leq m
such that
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i_{0},j_{1}}}(u)\geq 1
. Let
z_{q^{\prime}}=x_{i_{0},j_{1}}
. It is clear that
q^{\prime}>q
.
Whence, we can find
k,\ell
such that
f_{k,\alpha_{k,\ell}}
divides
u
and
\operatorname{deg}_{z_{q^{\prime}}}(f_{k,\alpha_{k,\ell}})=1
. Since
{\mathcal{D}_{k}}
is a Ferrers diagram, it is clear that
z_{q}f_{k,\alpha_{k,\ell}}/z_{q^{\prime}}\in I_{\mathcal{D}_{k}}
.
∎

We conclude this work with the following result on the multi-Rees algebras
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
. As announced, it generalizes the previous
Theorem

3.3
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a standardizable
n
-dimensional Ferrers diagram,
I_{\mathcal{D}}
be the ideal associated with
\mathcal{D}
, and
r
be a positive integer. Furthermore, let

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{x_{k,k_{1}}T_{({\bm{a}},i)}-x_{k,k_{2}}T_{({\bm{b}},i)}:i\in[r],{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D},\text{ and }x_{k,k_{1}}{\bm{x}}_{{\bm{a}}}=x_{k,k_{2}}{\bm{x}}_{\bm{b}}\}.

Then,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathbf{I}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{r}})\cup\mathcal{H}
is a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
\mathcal{J}
of the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
.
In particular,
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is a Koszul Cohen–Macaulay normal domain.
Furthermore, if the field
{\mathbb{K}}
has characteristic zero,
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
has rational singularities. If
{\mathbb{K}}
has positive characteristic,
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
is
F
-rational.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
constitutes a Gröbner basis for the presentation ideal of
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I_{\mathcal{D}})
with respect to the order
\sigma^{\prime}
defined in
Definition

5.1
is a direct outcome of
Theorem

5.7
,
Lemma

5.9
, and
Theorem

2.5
. The remaining assertions can be established through a comparable argument employed in the proof of
Corollary

5.8
.
∎

We conclude this work with a simple example that demonstrates that the same proving strategy cannot be applied to the case of multi-Rees algebras associated with distinct Ferrers diagrams. A new order must be found to apply similar proving steps.

Example 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
be the minimal
2
-dimensional Ferrers diagram that contains the lattice points
(4,1)
and
(2,4)

\mathcal{D}_{2}
be the similar diagram that contains the lattice points
(1,3)
and
(3,1)

\mathcal{K}
of the special fiber ring
\mathcal{F}(I_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\oplus I_{\mathcal{D}_{2}})
with respect to the natural lexicographic order, there exist
4
extra binomials of degree
3
.

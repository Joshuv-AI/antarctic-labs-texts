Discrete Poincaré inequalities and universal approximators for random graphs Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 05C12, 05C48, 05C50, 05C80, 46B85. Thanks: Key words : expander graphs, random regular graphs, nonlinear Poincaré inequalities.
Dylan J. Altschuler, Pandelis Dodos, Konstantin Tikhomirov and Konstantinos Tyros

Abstract
. Nonlinear Poincaré inequalities are indispensable tools in the study of dimension reduction and low-distortion embeddings of graphs into metric spaces, and have found remarkable algorithmic applications. A basic open problem, posed by Jon Kleinberg (2013), asks whether the optimal nonlinear Poincaré constant for maps between two independent 3 3 -regular random graphs is dimension-free, i.e., independent of vertex-set sizes. We give a complete and affirmative resolution to Kleinberg’s problem, also allowing for arbitrary graph degrees. As a corollary, we obtain a stochastic construction of O ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ -universal O(1)\text{-universal} approximators for random graphs, answering a question of Mendel and Naor.

1.4.
Proof overview

We now briefly describe the key ideas of the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
. Let
G
and
H
be two independent random regular graphs on
[n]
and
[m]
with degrees
d
and
\Delta
, respectively. Our goal is to prove that with high probability, for any
f\colon[n]\to[m]
,

(1.10)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in[n]}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant O_{d}\bigg(\frac{1}{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\bigg),

where the implied constant is independent of
n,m,\Delta
, and
f
.
As the starting point, let us discuss the extreme situations when
f
is nearly injective, and when
f
is nearly constant. Upon considering these cases, three natural mechanisms emerge as candidates for enabling the proof of (
1.10
).

The first mechanism is spectral expansion.
Consider the toy situation in which there are two points (“clusters”) in the image of
f
, each of which has pre-image of size linear in
n
. As a consequence of spectral expansion of random graphs, with high probability there will be a linear number of edges in
G
connecting the pre-images of the clusters. This implies (
1.10
); however, the viability of this strategy rapidly deteriorates as the number of clusters begins to grow.

Second, we can leverage concentration of measure (namely, the fact that edges are nearly equidistributed) for random graphs. Consider the toy example in which
m\asymp|\mathrm{Image}(f)|\leqslant n
. As a deterministic consequence of the
\Delta
-regularity of
H
, for any fixed
x\in V_{H}
, there are at most order
m^{o(1)}
vertices
y\in V_{H}
with
{\rm dist}_{H}(x,y)=o(\log m)
. Then with very high probability (in particular, high enough to union bound over all possible choices of
f
), the graph
G
has order
n
edges between vertices which are mapped to logarithmic distance in
H
, yielding the validity of (
1.10
). (A similar computation appears in
[
MN15
, Proposition 8.1]
to obtain (
1.10
) in the specific case that
m=n
and
f
is a bijection.)

Third, towards bridging the gap between these regimes, we utilize the locally tree-like behaviour of random graphs. A strong form of this property—developed by Arora et al.
[
ALNRRV12
]
and Mendel–Naor
[
MN15
]
—is that any sublinear-sized neighborhood of a random graph admits a
low-distortion
embedding into
L_{1}
. This will be formalized in Definition
3.2
as property
\mathcal{R(\varepsilon)}
, which asserts that the subgraph of
H
induced by any subset of size at most
m^{1-\varepsilon}
—where
\varepsilon
is an arbitrarily small constant—admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
L_{1}
. Property
\mathcal{R(\varepsilon)}
is used in conjunction with a classical result of Matoušek asserting that random graphs satisfy a Poincaré inequality with respect to
L_{1}
. Together, these facts eventually yield (
1.10
) for any
f
with
|\mathrm{Image}(f)|\leqslant m^{1-\varepsilon}
.

In view of these tools, the critical regime for proving (
1.10
) is
m^{1-o(1)}\leqslant|\mathrm{Image}(f)|\leqslant o(m)
, where none of these mechanisms directly apply.
We shall highlight three of the key ideas employed in this setting.
First, we develop a random compression argument to “reduce” the image of
f
, thereby enabling the usage of property
\mathcal{R(\varepsilon)}
.
Second, we adopt a “cluster-level” view of
f
, in which vertices
v\in V_{G}
are sorted by
\big|f^{-1}\big(f(v)\big)\big|
. This enables us to decompose the domain of
f
into regions on which the function
f
is nearly injective, or nearly constant. Third,
our proof heavily utilizes a strong form of vertex expansion for random regular graphs—see Definition
3.1
—introduced in our previous work
[
ADTT24
]
. This property is used in Lemma
5.8
as the key ingredient for understanding the statistical behaviour of the random compression scheme.

(B)

We have
\lambda_{2}(G)\leqslant 2.1\sqrt{d-1}
.

Part (
B
) of property
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
is a standard spectral property related to edge expansion.
On the other hand, note that part (
A
) quantifies vertex expansion; it was recently introduced in

[
ADTT24
, Definition 1.5, part (
A
)]
.

The second property concerns the “range” graph
H
.

Definition 3.2

(Property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
)
.

Let
m\geqslant\Delta\geqslant 3
be integers, and let
H
be a
\Delta
-regular graph on
[m]
.
Given
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
, we say that
H
satisfies
property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)

if the following are satisfied.

(A)

For every nonempty subset
S
of
[m]
with
|S|\leqslant m^{1-\varepsilon}
,

c_{L_{1}}\big(H[S]\big)\leqslant\frac{216}{\varepsilon}.

(B)

We have that
H
is connected and
{\rm diam}(H)\leqslant 3\log_{\Delta-1}m
.

Notice that part (
B
) of property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
—just like
part (
B
) of property
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
—is also a standard property of random regular graphs.
Part (
A
) is more subtle; it was introduced by Mendel and Naor
[
MN15
, Section 6]
,
though closely related versions have been studied earlier (see, e.g.,
[
ALNRRV12
]
).

It is crucial for our analysis that both properties hold with high probability for random graphs.

Proposition 3.3

(Properties
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
are typical)
.

The following hold.

(i)

Let
d\geqslant 3
be an integer, and set

(3.1)

\alpha=\alpha(d):=exp\big(-10^{11}\,\log^{2}d\big).

There exists a constant
\tau_{1}=\tau_{1}(d)>0
such that

(3.2)

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,d)}\big[G\text{ satisfies property }\mathcal{D}(\alpha)\big]\geqslant 1-O_{d}\Big(\frac{1}{n^{\tau_{1}}}\Big).

(ii)

Let
\Delta\geqslant 3
be an integer, and let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
.
There exists a constant
\tau_{2}=\tau_{2}(\Delta,\varepsilon)>0
such that

(3.3)

\mathbb{P}_{G(m,\Delta)}\big[H\text{ satisfies property }\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)\big]\geqslant 1-O_{\Delta,\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{m^{\tau_{2}}}\Big).

Proof.

First we argue for property
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
. The fact that a uniformly random
d
-regular graph
satisfies part (
A
) of property
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
with the desired probability
follows from
[
ADTT24
, Proposition 1.9]
. Part (
B
) is a direct consequence of
Friedman’s second eigenvalue theorem
[
Fr08
]
.

Towards property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
, it is a classical observation—see, e.g.,
[
ADTT24
, Proposition 1.9]
—that
part (
B
) of property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
is satisfied with the desired probability.
So, it remains to verify that a uniformly random
\Delta
-regular graph satisfies part (
A
)
of property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
. To this end, fix
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
, and set

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:=\Big\{H\in G(m,\Delta)\colon|E_{H[S]}|\leqslant\Big(1+\frac{7}{\varepsilon\log_{\Delta}m}\Big)|S|\text{ for every }\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[m]\text{ with }|S|\leqslant m^{1-\varepsilon}\Big\},

where
E_{H[S]}
denotes the edge-set of the induced on
S
subgraph of
H
. By
[
MN15
, Lemma 7.3]
,
we have
\mathbb{P}_{G(m,\Delta)}\big[\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}\big]\geqslant 1-O_{\Delta,\varepsilon}(m^{-1+\varepsilon})
.
On the other hand, by part (i) of the proposition,

\mathbb{P}_{G(m,\Delta)}[H\text{ is connected and }{\rm diam}(H)\leqslant 3\log_{\Delta-1}m]\geqslant 1-O_{\Delta}(m^{-\tau})

for some
\tau=\tau(\Delta)>0
. Finally, if
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
is connected with
{\rm diam}(H)\leqslant 3\log_{\Delta-1}m
,
then, by
[
MN15
, Corollary 6.6]
and after keeping track the constants in its proof,
for every nonempty subset
S
of
[m]
with
|S|\leqslant m^{1-\varepsilon}
,

c_{L_{1}}\big(H[S]\big)\leqslant\frac{4e}{e-1}\,\bigg(1+\frac{7}{\varepsilon\log_{\Delta}m}\cdot 3\log_{\Delta-1}m\bigg)\leqslant\frac{216}{\varepsilon}.\qed

3.2.
Decomposition, and main propositions

Let
G\in G(n,d)
and
H\in G(m,\Delta)
be two regular graphs, and let
f\colon[n]\to[m]
be a function from the
vertex set of
G
into the vertex set of
H
. The proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
depend
on the behavior of the function
f
, especially in regard to whether
f
is injective, constant, or somewhere in between. The following definition quantifies this behavior.

Definition 3.4

(Decomposition)
.

For every
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
and every
f\colon[n]\to[m]
, where
n,m
are positive integers, we set

(3.4)

\displaystyle M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v\in[n]\colon\big|f^{-1}\big(f(v)\big)\big|\leqslant\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)m^{2\varepsilon}\Big\},

(3.5)

\displaystyle M_{1}(f,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v\in[n]\colon\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)m^{2\varepsilon}<\big|f^{-1}\big(f(v)\big)\big|\leqslant\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)m^{4\varepsilon}\Big\},

(3.6)

\displaystyle M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v\in[n]\colon\big|f^{-1}\big(f(v)\big)\big|>\Big(\frac{n}{m}\Big)m^{4\varepsilon}\Big\}.

The reader can roughly think of the set
M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)
as the part of the domain of
f
on
which the function
f
is essentially injective, while
M_{1}(f,\varepsilon)
and
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)

as the parts of the domain of
f
on which the function
f
in nearly constant
(in the sense that the image of
M_{1}(f,\varepsilon)
and
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is a “thin” subset of
[m]
).

The following proposition treats the case when the set
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is small.

Proposition 3.5

(Validity of Poincaré inequality if
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is small)
.

Let
d,\Delta\geqslant 3
be integers, let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
, and set

(3.7)

\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}(\varepsilon):=\frac{18}{\varepsilon}.

There exists a positive integer
m_{0}=m_{0}(d)
with the following property.
Let
m\geqslant m_{0}
be an integer, and let
H\in G(m,\Delta)
be connected with
{\rm diam}(H)\leqslant 3\log_{\Delta-1}m
.
Then, for any integer
n\geqslant 4d
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G(n,d)}\bigg[G\colon\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in[n]}{\rm dist}_{H}

\displaystyle\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant\Gamma_{1}\,\frac{1}{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big),

\displaystyle\text{for every }f\colon[n]\to[m]\text{ with }|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\leqslant\frac{n}{8}\bigg]\geqslant 1-O_{d}\Big(\frac{1}{m^{0.05\,n}}\Big).

The proof of Proposition
3.5
is given in Section
4
, and it relies on strong concentration
bounds that are available if
|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\leqslant\frac{n}{8}
.

The next proposition complements Proposition
3.5
and treats the case
when the set
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is large.

Proposition 3.6

(Validity of Poincaré inequality if
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is large)
.

Let
d,\Delta\geqslant 3
be integers, let
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
, let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
, and set

(3.9)

\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{2}(d,\alpha,\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{\alpha\cdot\varepsilon^{2}}\,\exp\big(3632\cdot\log^{2}d\big).

There exists a positive integer
m_{1}=m_{1}(d,\alpha,\varepsilon)
with the following property.
Let
n\geqslant d
and
m\geqslant m_{1}
be integers, and let
G\in G(n,d)
and
H\in G(m,\Delta)
. Assume that
G
satisfies
property
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
, and that
H
satisfies property
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
.
Then, for any
f\colon[n]\to[m]
with
|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\geqslant\frac{n}{8}
,
where
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
is as in (
3.6
), we have

(3.10)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in[n]}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant\Gamma_{2}\,\frac{1}{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big).

The proof of Proposition
3.6
is given in Section
5
, and it is significantly more demanding.
It relies on properties
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
, a dyadic decomposition
of the set
M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)
, and a randomized “compression” argument.

4.
Proof of Proposition
3.5

Fix
d,\Delta,\varepsilon,m,H
and
n
; clearly, we may assume that
dn
is even.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all maps
f\colon[n]\to[m]

with
|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\leqslant\frac{n}{8}
. For every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, set

\mathcal{G}_{f}:=\bigg\{G\in G(n,d)\colon\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in[n]}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant\Gamma_{1}\,\frac{1}{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\bigg\}.

Since
|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant m^{n}
, by a union bound, it is enough to show that for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
,

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,d)}\big[\mathcal{G}_{f}\big]\geqslant 1-O_{d}\Big(\frac{1}{m^{1.05n}}\Big).

So, fix
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and set
R:=[n]\setminus M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
and

\mathcal{A}:=\bigg\{\{v,u\}\in\binom{R}{2}\colon\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant 4\varepsilon\log_{\Delta-1}m\bigg\}.

Notice that by the
\Delta
-regularity of

,
the definition of
M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)
and the fact that

, we have

(4.2)

|\mathcal{A}|\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\,|R|\,n\,m^{8\varepsilon-1}.

Also observe that, since
|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\leqslant\frac{n}{8}
, for every
G\in G(n,d)
,

(4.3)

|E_{G[R]}|\geqslant\frac{dn}{2}-d|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\geqslant\frac{3dn}{8},

where
E_{G[R]}
denotes the edge-set of the induced on
R
subgraph of
G
.
For every
k\in[\frac{dn}{2}]
with
k\geqslant\frac{3dn}{8}
, set

\mathcal{E}_{k}:=\big\{G\in G(n)\colon|E_{G[R]}|=k\big\},

where

denotes the set of all graphs on

. Since
{\rm diam}(H)\leqslant 3\log_{\Delta-1}m

for every

with

and every

\frac{1}{|E_{G[R]}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G[R]}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant\frac{4}{3|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}{\rm dist}_{H}\big(f(v),f(u)\big)\leqslant\frac{4}{\Gamma_{1}}\log_{\Delta-1}m.

Therefore, by Markov’s inequality, the definition of
\mathcal{A}
, and the choice of
\Gamma_{1}

in (
3.7
), we see that
\big|E_{G[R]}\cap\big(\binom{R}{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}\big)\big|\leqslant k/18
.

p:=d/(n-1)
, and let
\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}
denote the law of the classical (independent) Erdős–Rényi random graph.
Then the previous observations imply, after conditioning on the number of edges contained in
\binom{[n]}{2}\setminus\binom{R}{2}
,
that for every
k\in[\frac{dn}{2}]
with
k\geqslant\frac{3dn}{8}
,

(4.4)

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}\big[G(n,d)\setminus\mathcal{G}_{f}\,\big|\,\mathcal{E}_{k}\big]\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}\big[|\mathcal{A}\cap E_{\mathbf{G}[R]}|\geqslant 17k/18\,\big|\,\mathcal{E}_{k}\big]\leqslant\sum_{j=\lceil 17k/18\rceil}^{k}\frac{\binom{|\mathcal{A}|}{j}\binom{|\binom{R}{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}|}{k-j}}{\binom{{\binom{|R|}{2}}}{k}},

where
\mathbf{G}\sim\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}
.
Since
n\geqslant 4d
,
\varepsilon\leqslant 10^{-4}
,
d\geqslant 3
and
|R|\geqslant\frac{7n}{8}
,
by (

), we see that, for every
m\geqslant 28^{1000}
, the right-hand side of (

) is upper bounded by

(4.5)

\frac{k\binom{|\mathcal{A}|}{\lceil 17k/18\rceil}\binom{|\binom{R}{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}|}{k-\lceil 17k/18\rceil}}{18\binom{{\binom{|R|}{2}}}{k}}\leqslant\Bigg(\frac{4|\mathcal{A}|}{\binom{|R|}{2}-k}\Bigg)^{17k/18}\leqslant\big(28m^{8\varepsilon-1}\big)^{\frac{3\cdot 17}{8\cdot 18}dn}\leqslant m^{-1.059\cdot\frac{d}{3}n}.

To complete the proof, we will use the following asymptotic enumeration of regular graphs
[

]
.

Fact 4.1
.

If
n\geqslant d\geqslant 3
are integers with

even, then the cardinality of

is of order

\Theta_{d}\bigg(\frac{(dn)!}{(dn/2)!2^{dn/2}(d!)^{n}}\bigg);

in particular, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}\big[G(n,d)\big]\geqslant\exp(-C_{1}n),

where

denotes the law of the Erdős–Rényi random graph.

Since
d\geqslant 3

4.3
)–(
4.5
) and Fact

,
there exists a positive integer
m_{0}=m_{0}(d)\geqslant 28^{1000}
such that for every

and every

,

(4.6)

\mathbb{P}_{G(n,d)}\big[G(n,d)\setminus\mathcal{G}_{f}\big]=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}\big[G(n,d)\setminus\mathcal{G}_{f}\big]}{\mathbb{P}_{G(n,p)}\big[G(n,d)\big]}\leqslant m^{-1.05\cdot\frac{d}{3}n}\leqslant m^{-1.05\,n}.

M_{0,r}:=\Big\{v\in M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)\colon 2^{r-1}\leqslant\big|f^{-1}\big(f(v)\big)\big|<2^{r}\Big\};

We also set

(5.11)

r^{*}:=\max\big\{r\colon M_{0,r}\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Finally, define

(5.12)

M_{0}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{*}-2\log_{2}m\leqslant r\leqslant r^{*}\\
|f(M_{0,r})|\geqslant m^{1-2\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}M_{0,r}\ \ \ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \ \ M_{0}^{\prime\prime}:=\bigcup_{r<r^{*}-2\log_{2}m}M_{0,r}.

Observe that
M_{0}^{\prime}\cup M_{0}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)=M_{0,1}\cup\cdots\cup M_{0,r^{*}}
.

We also introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.6

(Random compression)
.

We define the (deterministic) function
\hat{f}\colon[n]\to[m]
by

(5.13)

\hat{f}(v):=\begin{cases}1&v\in M_{0}^{\prime\prime},\\
f(v)&v\in[n]\setminus M_{0}^{\prime\prime}.\end{cases}

Notice that
\hat{f}=f
if
M_{0}^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
. We also define the

random compression

\boldsymbol{f}\colon[n]\to[m]
of
f
as follows.
We select uniformly at random a vertex
\mathbf{v}
from
M_{0}^{\prime}
, and we set

(5.14)

\boldsymbol{f}(v):=\begin{cases}\hat{f}(\mathbf{v})&v\in M_{0}^{\prime},\\
\hat{f}(v)&v\in[n]\setminus M_{0}^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Again observe that if
M_{0}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, then
\boldsymbol{f}
deterministically equals to
\hat{f}
.

In other words, the deterministic function
\hat{f}
is obtained by compressing the image

f(M_{0}^{\prime\prime})
of the set
M_{0}^{\prime\prime}
into the singleton
\{1\}
. Subsequently, the random compression
\boldsymbol{f}
is defined
by selecting a vertex
\mathbf{v}\in M_{0}^{\prime}
uniformly at random, and then compressing the image

f(M_{0}^{\prime})
of the set
M_{0}^{\prime}
into the singleton
\{f(\mathbf{v})\}
. Crucially, this construction allows us to leverage the following.

Fact 5.7
.

There exists a positive integer
m_{2}=m_{2}(\varepsilon)
such that for every integer
m\geqslant m_{2}
,
we have, almost everywhere,

(5.15)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\boldsymbol{f}(v),\boldsymbol{f}(u)\big)\leqslant\frac{10746}{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{|E_{G}|}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E_{G}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\boldsymbol{f}(v),\boldsymbol{f}(u)\big).

Proof.

Set
m_{2}(\varepsilon):=\big\lceil(4+2\log_{2}m)^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big\rceil
.
If
m\geqslant m_{2}(\varepsilon)
, then, since
\Delta\geqslant 3
, it holds almost everywhere,

\displaystyle\big|\mathrm{Im}(\boldsymbol{f})\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2+\big|f\big(M_{0}(f,\varepsilon)\setminus(M_{0}^{\prime\prime}\cup M_{0}^{\prime})\big)\big|+\big|f\big(M_{1}(f,\varepsilon)\cup M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2+(2\log_{2}m+1)m^{1-2\varepsilon}+m^{1-2\varepsilon}\leqslant m^{1-\varepsilon}.

Therefore, inequality (
5.15
) follows from Fact
5.1
.

therefore,

\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\boldsymbol{f}(v),\boldsymbol{f}(u)\big)\big]=\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)-\sum_{v,u\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big).

Since
n-|M_{0}^{\prime}|\geqslant|M_{2}(f,\varepsilon)|\geqslant\frac{n}{8}
,
we see that
\frac{|M_{0}^{\prime}|}{n-|M_{0}^{\prime}|}\leqslant 7
. Thus, by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{v,u\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n-|M_{0}^{\prime}|}\sum_{v,u\in M_{0}^{\prime},w\not\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\Big(\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(w)\big)+\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(w),\hat{f}(u)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\,\frac{|M_{0}^{\prime}|}{n-|M_{0}^{\prime}|}\sum_{v\in M_{0}^{\prime},u\not\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant 7\Big(\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)-\sum_{v,u\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)\Big),

that implies that,

(5.38)

\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)\leqslant 8\Big(\sum_{v,u\in[n]}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)-\sum_{v,u\in M_{0}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}_{H}\big(\hat{f}(v),\hat{f}(u)\big)\Big).

The desired estimate (
5.18
) follows from (
5.36
)–(
5.38
) for every integer
m\geqslant m_{6}
.
The proof of Lemma
5.8
is completed.
∎

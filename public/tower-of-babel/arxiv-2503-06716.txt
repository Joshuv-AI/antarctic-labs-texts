1.
Introduction

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the sharp quantitative stability of various functional and geometric inequalities and their applications in fields such as the calculus of variations, geometric analysis and diffusion flows, among others. Notable examples include the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality, Sobolev inequality and its nonlocal variant, Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality and the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in hyperbolic space, along with many others from a wide range of studies
[
6
,
11
,
9
,
12
,
5
,
7
,
19
,
30
]
.

This paper examines the sharp quantitative stability of the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, beginning with an approach inspired by the work of Bianchi and Egnell
[
6
]
and subsequently extending the analysis to quantify also the sharp stability for the associated Euler-Lagrange equation by following the footsteps of Ciraolo, Figalli, and Maggi
[
13
]
.

Our starting point is the following fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality:

(1.1)

\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 8.53581pt;\text{ for all }u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
0<t<2s<N
and
2^{*}_{s}(t)\coloneqq\frac{2(N-t)}{N-2s}
. Clearly,
2<2^{*}_{s}(t)<2^{*}_{s}\coloneqq\frac{2N}{N-2s}
.
The best constant
\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})>0
in (
1.1
) is defined by

(1.2)

\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\coloneqq\mu_{s,t}=\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}^{2}}{\left\||x|^{-\frac{t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}u\right\|_{2_{s}^{*}(t)}^{2}}

and is attained by a non-negative, radially symmetric, strictly decreasing function
U_{s,t}
(see e.g.,
[
31
,
22
]
) such that
\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}U_{s,t}\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}=1
. Clearly then
U_{s,t}
is a weak solution to the Euler-Lagrange (fractional Hardy-Sobolev) equation

(1.3)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\mu_{s,t}\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}}\quad;u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\vskip 2.84526pt\\
u>0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\end{cases}

associated with (
1.1
).

Let us fix this minimizer (bubble)
U_{s,t}
of (
1.2
) from now on. By a direct computation, it’s easy to see that for any
\lambda>0
, the radial function

U^{\lambda}_{s,t}(x)\coloneqq\lambda^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}U_{s,t}\left(\lambda x\right)

and its scalar multiples achieve the best constant
\mu_{s,t}
. Furthermore, for any
\lambda>0
, according to our normalization, it holds that

\|U^{\lambda}_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|U_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\sqrt{\mu_{s,t}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}U_{s,t}^{\lambda}\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}=\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}U_{s,t}\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}=1.

In
[
23
]
, Musina and Nazarov provided a complete classification of the first two eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalue problem for the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{s,t}^{\lambda}
(see Appendix
A
for details) around a positive weak solution
U^{\lambda}_{s,t}
of the equation (
1.3
). Additionally, they established various regularity, uniqueness, and decay properties of these weak solutions. Since these results will be frequently used in this paper, for the reader’s convenience, we paraphrase them here in the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.1

(Non-degeneracy
[
23
, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 4.2]
)
.

If
v\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is a weak solution of

(-\Delta)^{s}v=\mu_{s,t}(2_{s}^{*}(t)-1)\frac{\left(U_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}}\right)^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}{|x|^{t}}v\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

then
v=c\partial_{\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}U_{s,t}^{\lambda}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover, the first two eigenvalues of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}}

(
see (
A.1
) for definition
)
are
\eta_{1}=1,\,\eta_{2}=2^{*}_{s}(t)-1
respectively.

Theorem 1.2

(Regularity, decay estimates and uniqueness
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

If
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is a solution to (
4.2
) then
u=V^{\lambda}_{s,t}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}U^{\lambda}_{s,t}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Furthermore,
V_{s,t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
for any
\alpha\in[0,2s-t)
; also there exist
c,\,C>0

(
depending on
V_{s,t})
such that

\frac{c}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}\leq V_{s,t}(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, we consider the deficit functional corresponding to the inequality (
1.1
)

(1.4)

\delta(u)\coloneqq\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}^{2}-\mu_{s,t}\left\||x|^{-\frac{t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}u\right\|_{2_{s}^{*}(t)}^{2}.

Now from the above discussion, it is immediate that the set where the equality holds for (
1.1
), i.e., when
\delta(u)=0
, is precisely the two-dimensional smooth manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
defined as

(1.5)

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{cU_{s,t}^{\lambda}\,\colon\,(c,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\,U_{s,t}^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}U_{s,t}(\lambda x)\right\}.

The question of stability asks whether, for a given
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, a sufficiently small
\delta(u)
implies that
u
is close to
\mathcal{M}
in the
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
topology. Moreover, can one determine how
\delta(u)
controls the
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
distance between
u
and
\mathcal{M}
in a quantitative manner? Brézis and Lieb
[
8
]
originally posed this question in the context of the classical Sobolev inequality, where the extremal functions were characterized by those so-called Aubin-Talenti bubbles
[
2
,
28
]
. Now for the fractional Sobolev inequality (i.e., when
0<2s<N
), the minimizers were classified by Cotsiolis et al.
[
14
]
. The first in-depth study on the sharp stability on the classical Sobolev inequality was conducted in the seminal work of Bianchi and Egnell
[
6
]
, as well as by Rey
[
25
]
. Later, the stability result established by Bianchi and Egnell was extended to biharmonic operators in
[
21
]
and further generalized to poly-harmonic operators by Bartsch et al.
[
3
]
. The sharp stability for the fractional Sobolev inequality, in the case where
0<2s<N
, was subsequently investigated by Chen et al.
[
11
]
.

Turning our attention from inequality to the derivation of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation, we examine the first variation of the deficit functional
\delta(\cdot)
in the vicinity of a minimizer
u
of
\mu_{s,t}
. i.e.,

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\delta(u+t\phi)=0

for all
\phi\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, under the constraint
\left\||x|^{-\frac{t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}u\right\|_{2_{s}^{*}(t)}=1
. As a result, we obtain (
1.3
) without imposing any sign restriction on
u
. In other words, the set of all critical points of the energy functional in (
3.7
) includes all minimizers of
\mu_{s,t}
. However, Theorem
1.2
, which characterizes all solutions of (
1.3
) that do not change sign, guarantees that the sub-manifold
\mathcal{M}_{1}\coloneqq\{cU_{s,t}^{\lambda}\mid(c,\lambda)\in\{\pm 1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\}
precisely corresponds to these non-sign-changing critical points.

For
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, consider the deficit functional corresponding to (
4.2
) as

(1.6)

\Gamma(u)\coloneqq\left\|(-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}}\right\|_{\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}}=\left\|I_{s,t}^{\prime}(u)\right\|_{\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}},

where
I_{s,t}
is defined in (
4.1
).

We begin by exploring the qualitative stability question in this context, specifically, whether a sufficiently small value of
\Gamma(u)
for some non-sign-changing
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
implies that
u
is close to the sub-manifold
\mathcal{M}_{1}
of non-sign-changing critical points of
I_{s,t}
. The answer to this question is negative because of the occurrence of the bubbling phenomenon. Specifically, it is observed that if the energy of a non-negative function
u
is close to that of
\nu
bubbles, then as
\Gamma(u)
is very close to
0
, the function
u
is almost a sum of
\nu
bubbles (see Theorem
1.4
).

We provide a brief overview of the existing literature related to the stability of Euler-Lagrange PDEs corresponding to the aforementioned functional inequalities. The local one-bubble case
(s=1,\,t=0,\,\nu=1)
, which corresponds to the critical exponent problem for the negative Laplacian
(-\Delta)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, was first examined in all dimensions
N\geq 3
by Ciraolo, Figalli, and Maggi
[
13
]
. The multi-bubble case
(s=1,\,t=0,\,\nu\geq 2)
was later investigated by Figalli and Glaudo
[
18
]
, who discovered sharp, dimension-dependent linear stability results in low dimensions
N=3,\,4,\,5
. However, they also constructed a counterexample demonstrating the failure of linear stability in higher dimensions
N\geq 6
. This linear instability is largely attributed to strong interactions between Aubin-Talenti bubbles in dimensions
N\geq 6
as then
2^{*}-2<1
. These challenges were addressed by Deng, Sun, and Wei
[
16
]
, who resolved a conjecture posed by Figalli and Glaudo and established sharp nonlinear stability results in all dimensions
N\geq 6
.

The nonlocal counterpart of the critical exponent problem for the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
was studied in various settings. The one-bubble case
(0<s<1,\,t=0,\,\nu=1)
in dimensions
N>2s
was analyzed by De Netti and König
[
15
]
, while Aryan
[
1
]
investigated the multi-bubble case
(0<s<1,\,t=0,\,\nu\geq 2)
for
N>2s
. More recently, Chen, Kim, and Wei
[
10
]
extended these stability results to the entire range
(0<2s<N,\,t=0,\,\nu\geq 1)
.

It is important to emphasize that, unlike the classical or fractional Sobolev inequality studied in
[
2
,
28
,
14
,
20
]
, in our case
(0<s<1,\,0<t<2s<N)
, the explicit form of the minimizers for
\mu_{s,t}
remains unknown. Furthermore, while previous research on the associated Euler-Lagrange equation has focused exclusively on the case
t=0
, our work extends the stability analysis to the full range
0<t<2s
and proves the sharp linear rate when
0<s<1\,\mbox{ and }\,\nu=1
. The case involving multiple bubbles i.e.,
\nu\geq 2
will be addressed in our upcoming paper.

1.1.
Main Results

Our first result gives a Bianchi-Egnell-type stability estimate for the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.3

(Quantitative stability near a minimizer)
.

There is a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,s\mbox{ and }t
such that for all
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, one has

(1.7)

\delta(u)\coloneqq\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}^{2}-\mu_{s,t}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t})}\geq\alpha d(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}.

Moreover, this result is sharp in the sense that it becomes false if the left-hand side is replaced by

d(u,\mathcal{M})^{-2\epsilon}{\delta(u)}^{1+\epsilon}

for any
\epsilon>0
.

Moving on to the Euler-Lagrange equation (
4.2
) associated to the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.1
), the next theorem depicts a Struwe-type decomposition for a non-negative
(PS)
sequence.

Theorem 1.4

(Qualitative stability)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
0<t<2s<N
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence of non-negative functions such that

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\leq\|u_{k}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}},

where
\mu_{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as in (
1.2
), and suppose the deficit functional

\Gamma(u_{k})\to 0\hskip 4.2679pt\text{in}\hskip 4.2679pt\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}\hskip 4.2679pt\text{as}\hskip 4.2679ptk\to\infty.

Then, up to a subsequence, one of the following holds:

•

Nonzero weak limit case:
If
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\neq 0
weakly in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists a sequence of
\nu
-tuples of positive real numbers

(\lambda_{k}^{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{\nu})_{k\geq 1}

such that

(1.8)

\left\|u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{k}^{i}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,

with

\lambda_{k}^{1}=\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

•

Zero weak limit case:
If
u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists such a sequence
(\lambda_{k}^{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{\nu})_{k\geq 1}
satisfying (
1.8
) but with no fixed parameter.

Moreover, in all cases except the fixed parameter,

\text{for each }1\leq i\leq\nu,\quad\lambda_{k}^{i}\to 0\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda_{k}^{i}\to\infty,

and the asymptotic orthogonality condition
(c.f.
2.1
)

\text{for }i\neq j,\quad\left|\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}^{i}}{\lambda_{k}^{j}}\right)\right|\to\infty\quad\text{as }k\to\infty

holds.

We also have the following sharp linear stability estimate that quantifies the above theorem when
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is a non-negative function and its energy is strictly less than that of two bubbles.

Theorem 1.5

(Quantitative stability - one bubble)
.

Let
N>2s
be an integer, then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
N,s\mbox{ and }t
such that for any non-negative
u\in\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
satisfying

(1.9)

\frac{1}{2}\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\leq\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}},

there exists
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that
u=V_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}}+\rho
and
V_{s,t}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}}U_{s,t}
with

(1.10)

\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\rho\right\|_{2}\leq C\,\Gamma(u).

Furthermore, this result is optimal in the sense that it becomes false if the left-hand side is replaced by
\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{1-\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and its properties

For
N>2s
,
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined on the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

(2.1)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle C_{N,s}\,\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(x,\epsilon)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}y=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)\right)(x).

The homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is defined as the completion of
{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the Gagliardo semi-norm
[\cdot]_{s}
, which is given below via several equivalent forms as

(2.2)

[u]_{s}^{2}=\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\coloneqq\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Here the constant
C_{N,s}>0
is normalized in such a way (e.g.,
[
17
, Proposition 3.4]
) that the relations (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) hold. Namely,

C_{N,s}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1-\cos(\xi_{1})}{|\xi|^{N+2s}}{\rm d}\xi\right)^{-1}.

\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
becomes a Hilbert space with the following inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)\left(v(x)-v(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,\text{ for all }u,\,v\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Throughout the paper, any of the above expressions will be used to represent the
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
-norm as needed.

For
N>2s
, the continuous fractional Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\xhookrightarrow{}L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
holds, meaning there exists a positive constant
S=S(N,s)>0
such that

(2.3)

S\|u\|_{2_{s}^{*}}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\,;\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ for all }u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

We also have the following fractional Hardy inequality

(2.4)

\gamma_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2},\quad\gamma_{N,s}=2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N+2s}{4})}{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N-2s}{4})}.

Here,
\gamma_{N,s}
represents the best fractional Hardy constant. It is important to note that
\lim\limits_{s\to 1}\gamma_{N,s}=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
, which corresponds to the best Hardy constant in the local case.

By applying the two inequalities above in conjunction with Hölder’s inequality, we can conclude that for all
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{\frac{t}{s}}}{|x|^{t}}\cdot|u(x)|^{\frac{N(2s-t)}{s(N-2s)}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{t}{2s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2s-t}{2s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S^{-\frac{N(2s-t)}{2s(N-2s)}}\gamma_{N,s}^{-\frac{t}{2s}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}.

Thus one gets the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) by interpolating the fractional Sobolev inequality (
2.3
) and the fractional Hardy inequality (
2.4
).

For more details on the fractional Sobolev spaces, we refer the interested reader to
[
17
,
24
]
and the references therein.

2.2.
Symmetries of the Problem

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, let
D_{\lambda}\colon\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\longrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be the operator induced by a dilation with parameter
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is defined as

D_{\lambda}u(x)\coloneqq u^{\lambda}(x)\coloneqq\lambda^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}u\left(\lambda x\right).

This operator
D_{\lambda}
satisfies a plethora of important properties. We list out the ones that will be used frequently throughout this paper.

(1)

For all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+};\,D_{\lambda}
is a unitary operator. Clearly on
{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, its inverse is given by
D_{\lambda}^{-1}=D_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}
and more over it satisfies for any
u\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\|D_{\lambda}u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Now by density one has this isometry in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. This implies
D_{\lambda}
is a surjective isometry and therefore unitary.

(2)

For any two functions
u,v\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

D_{\lambda}(uv)=D_{\lambda}u\cdot D_{\lambda}v.

(3)

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that for any choices of positive exponents
\left(e_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq k}
with
e_{1}+e_{2}+\cdots+e_{k}=2^{*}_{s}(t)
and for any choice of non-negative functions
u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left(D_{\lambda}u_{1}\right)^{e_{1}}\cdots\left(D_{\lambda}u_{k}\right)^{e_{k}}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots u_{k}^{e_{k}}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

and in particular

\|D_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)}

for any
u\in L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)
by density.

(4)

For any pair of functions
u,v\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, it holds by the Plancherel product and a change of variable

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{\lambda}(x)\cdot\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v^{\lambda}(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{\lambda}}(\xi)\cdot\overline{\widehat{\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v^{\lambda}}(\xi)}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u^{\lambda}}(\xi)\cdot\overline{\widehat{v^{\lambda}}(\xi)}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)\cdot\overline{\widehat{v}(\xi)}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(x)\cdot\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v(x)\,{\rm d}x

i.e.,
\left\langle u^{\lambda},v^{\lambda}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}
. Now again by density, the result extends to
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
functions.

The transformations
\left(D_{\lambda}\right)_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}
are crucial in the analysis of the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality because they leave the quantities
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)}
unchanged. Specifically, we will frequently use these symmetries to simplify our approach, reducing the problem to the case where, instead of analyzing a general bubble
U_{s,t}^{\lambda}
, we can focus on the bubble
U_{s,t}
.

2.3.
Action of dilations on
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and asymptotic orthogonality

Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{U}\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)
be the group of all unitary operators on
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
induced by dilations on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left\{D_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{U}\left(\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)\colon D_{\lambda}u=u^{\lambda}\quad\forall u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N});\,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\right\}.

We observe below in Lemma
2.1
, that the map
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\ni\lambda\longrightarrow D_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{U}\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)
defined above is continuous in the strong topology of
\mathcal{U}\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)
. Now focusing on the group of dilates
G=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{+},\cdot\right)
, we endow
G
with the natural metric

d(\lambda,\delta)\coloneqq\left|\log\left(\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\right)\right|

which makes it a complete metric space. It’s easy to check that it induces the ususal Euclidean topology with the usual notion of convergent sequences.

The important characterization of how the dilations of a given fixed function become mutually asymptotically orthogonal in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
under the actions of sequences of dilations going to infinity with respect to the metric
d
on the group
G
is due to Palatucci-Pisante
[
24
, Lemma 3 and Lemma 4]
. Below, we give a simplified combined proof of these lemmata specifically tailored to our situation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(\delta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be sequences of positive real numbers. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\forall u,v\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right);\quad\left\langle u^{\lambda_{k}},v^{\delta_{k}}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad k\to\infty
.

(ii)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left|\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)\right|=\infty
.

Proof
.

We first observe that for any
\lambda,\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle u^{\lambda},v^{\delta}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u^{\lambda}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}v^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u^{\lambda}}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{v^{\delta}}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda\delta\right)^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}\left(\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\right)\,\overline{\widehat{v}\left(\frac{\xi}{\delta}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\delta}\right)^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}\left(\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\xi\right)\,\overline{\widehat{v}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\left\langle u^{\frac{\lambda}{\delta}},v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Therefore it’s enough to consider the case when
\delta_{k}=1
for all
k\geq 1
. To deduce
(i)
implies
(ii)
, we argue as follows.

Suppose
(ii)
is false. This implies
\sup_{k\geq 1}|\log\lambda_{k}|<\infty
, i.e., there exists
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that up to a subsequence still denoted by
\lambda_{k}
, it converges to
\lambda
. Let us fix
u\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that the measure of the set where
u\neq 0
is strictly positive. It’s easy to see that
u^{\lambda}(\cdot)=\lambda^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}u(\lambda\cdot)\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u^{\lambda_{k}}\to u^{\lambda}
point-wise as
k\to\infty
.

Then by
(i)
, Dominated convergence theorem and norm invariance, we obtain

0=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\langle u^{\lambda_{k}},u^{\lambda}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left\langle u^{\lambda},u^{\lambda}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left\|u^{\lambda}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

This implies
u=0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by (
1.1
) and this is a contradiction.

Conversely,
(ii)
implies
\lambda_{k}\to 0
or
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Let
u,v\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
then
\widehat{u},\widehat{v}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover a change of variable shows that
\widehat{u^{\lambda}}(\xi)=\lambda^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\widehat{u}\left(\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\right)
for any
\lambda>0
.

Then by the Plancherel product and Hölder’s inequality with
p\neq 2
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle u^{\lambda_{k}},v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{s}\left|\widehat{v}(\xi)\right||\xi|^{s}\left|\widehat{u^{\lambda_{k}}}(\xi)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\lambda_{k}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}}\left\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{v}\right\|_{p}\left\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{u}\right\|_{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

Now one can observe that as
k\to\infty
, if
\lambda_{k}\to 0
(or
\infty
), choosing
p>2
(or
<2
) in (
2.6
) entails
(i)
and the lemma is proved for all
{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
functions.

To extend this for all
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
functions, we argue via density. Let
u,v\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
\epsilon>0
, choose
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\epsilon
.

Then

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\left\langle\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\left\langle\tilde{u},{v}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right|+\left|\left\langle\tilde{u},v\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\left\langle\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle<\epsilon\left(\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)+\epsilon^{2}.

Finally letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in above gives the lemma in its full generality. ∎

Lemma 2.2

(Action of dilations on weak convergence in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
)
.

Suppose
\left(v_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be such that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup\,0
as
n\to\infty
. For a sequence
\left(\lambda_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
consider the corresponding sequence
w_{n}=D_{\lambda_{n}}v_{n}
. If all the weak limits of
\left(w_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
are nonzero
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
functions then
|\log\lambda_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof
.

Since
v_{n}
weakly converges to zero, it is weakly bounded and by uniform boundedness principle, it is bounded in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. From the beginning of the proof of Proposition
A.1
in Appendix
A
, one has the compact embedding
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and this allows us to have
v_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and thus up to a subsequence
v_{n}\to 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover by the scale invariance property of the norm under
D_{\lambda}
, we have
\|v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|D_{\lambda_{n}}v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|w_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
. Therefore,
w_{n}
is also a bounded sequence in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and again arguing as above, we infer that there exists
w\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that up to the same subsequence (corr. to
v_{n}
)
w_{n}\to w
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
w_{n}\to w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By our hypothesis
w\neq 0
.

On the contrary to the hypothesis of our lemma, we suppose that
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Evidently then, for any
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we observe that when
n
is large enough,
\lambda_{n}B_{R}\subset(\lambda_{0}+1)B_{R}=B_{(\lambda_{0}+1)R}
which is a fixed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Therefore,

\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}=\|w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)\leq\lambda_{n}^{-s}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{(\lambda_{0}+1)R})}+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)=\left(1+\lambda_{0}^{-s}\right)\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)

and this implies
w=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is a contradiction.

Now, given a subsequence
(\lambda_{n_{m}})
of
(\lambda_{n})
, we could find a further subsequence
\left(\lambda_{n_{m_{k}}}\right)
such that
\left|\log\lambda_{n_{m_{k}}}\right|\to\infty
and thus
|\log\lambda_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In
[
22
]
, Musina-Nazarov showed that up to a change of sign any minimizer of
\mu_{s,t}
is strictly positive, decreasing and radially symmetric moreover combining with Theorem
1.2
also due to the same authors (see e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
), one achieves equality in (
1.1
)
if and only if
there are constants
c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\},\lambda>0
such that
u=cU^{\lambda}_{s,t}
. Therefore the minimizers of (
1.1
) along with
0\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
constitute a two dimensional smooth sub-manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
defined in (
1.5
). We define the distance of a function
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
from this manifold
\mathcal{M}
as

d\left(u,\mathcal{M}\right)\coloneqq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}}\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(u-v)\right\|_{2}=\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{R}},\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(u-cU^{\lambda}_{s,t}\right)\right\|_{2}.

The following lemmata describe the sharp local stability of
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
functions that are close to
{\mathcal{M}}
, and this is pivotal in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
\alpha=\alpha(N,s,t)\in(0,1)
such that

(3.1)

\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\right\|_{2}^{2}-\mu_{s,t}\left\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}u\right\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}^{2}\geq\alpha d\left(u,\mathcal{M}\right)^{2}+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}\left(d\left(u,\mathcal{M}\right)^{2}\right)

for all
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
d\left(u,\mathcal{M}\right)<\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}
.

Proof
.

First we claim that there exists
c_{0}\neq 0\mbox{ and }0<\lambda_{0}<\infty
such that

(3.2)

d\left(u,\mathcal{M}\right)=\left\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\right)\right\|_{2}=\left\|u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

holds.

To see this, we consider a sequence
(c_{k},\lambda_{k})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that
\|u-c_{k}U^{\lambda_{k}}_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to d(u,\mathcal{M})
as
k\to\infty
(this exists from the definition of infimum). In the expansion of the fractional norm, using the norm invariance of
U^{\lambda}_{s,t}
and an application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we see,

\displaystyle\|u-c_{k}U^{\lambda_{k}}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+|c_{k}|^{2}\|U^{\lambda_{k}}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}-2c_{k}\langle u,U^{\lambda_{k}}_{s,t}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+|c_{k}|^{2}\|U_{s,t}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}-2|c_{k}|\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|U_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}-|c_{k}|\|U_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)^{2}.

Taking limit as
k\to\infty
above, one observes

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}>d(u,\mathcal{M})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left|\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}-|c_{k}|\|U_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right|.

This immediately shows that
|c_{k}|
is a bounded sequence of positive real numbers, thus up to a subsequence still denoted by
c_{k}
, it converges to some
c_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover,
c_{0}=0
contradicts (
3.3
), hence
c_{0}\neq 0
.
Again from the fractional norm expansion above, we see by taking limit along the subsequence
c_{k}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle 2c_{0}\cdot\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\langle u,U^{\lambda_{k}}_{s,t}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+|c_{0}|^{2}\|U_{s,t}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}-d(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}>|c_{0}|^{2}\|U_{s,t}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}>0.

Now invoking Lemma
2.1
, one can easily deduce that, (
3.4
) forces the existence of a
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that up to a subsequence
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
as
k\to\infty
and the claim follows.

As we have seen above that for a
u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the distance of
u
from the smooth sub-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is achieved at a point
c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\{0\}
under the condition
d(u,\mathcal{M})<\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
, we then must have
(u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t})\perp{\bf T}_{c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, the tangent space is given as

{\bf T}_{c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{span}\left\{U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\,,\,\partial_{\lambda}{U^{\lambda}_{s,t}}\big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}\right\}.

From the discussions in Appendix
A
, we see, Proposition
A.1
and Theorem
A.2
together give us that
i\circ\left(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\right)^{-1}=i\circ\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}}{w}\right)^{-1}\mbox{ ; where }w=\mu_{s,t}\frac{(U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t})^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}{|x|^{t}}
from
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to itself is a compact self-adjoint operator and thus its spectrum is discrete and consequently the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}
is also discrete. Combining Theorem
1.1
with this, we see that the first and the second eigenspace of the operator
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}
are spanned by
U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}
and
\partial_{\lambda}U^{\lambda}_{s,t}\big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}
respectively. Moreover, we have the Rayleigh quotient characterisation for the third eigenvalue as

(3.5)

\eta_{3}\leq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x)|\phi(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\hskip 8.53581pt\text{for all }\phi\perp{\bf T}_{c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\mathcal{M},

with equality
if and only if

\phi
is the third eigenfunction. We also notice
\eta_{1}=1,\,\eta_{2}=2^{*}_{s}(t)-1
and all other eigenvalues are independent of the scaling
\lambda_{0}
.

Since
(u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t})\perp{\bf T}_{c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\mathcal{M}
, there exists a vector
\phi\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
perpendicular to the tangent plane
{\bf T}_{c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\mathcal{M}
such that
\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=1\mbox{ and }u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}=d\phi
. Certainly, we can choose
d
to be positive and then
d=\|u-c_{0}U^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=d(u,\mathcal{M})
.

Now a Taylor’s expansion in
d
around
0
for the following yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}\left\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}u\right\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}^{2}

Since the eigenvalues of

) with

∎

.

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\|u_{k}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\mu_{s,t}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)}^{2}\to 1

\left(\text{up to a subsequence, still denoted by }u_{k}\right)

(

.

From the hypothesis of this lemma, one sees that

, therefore by employing the Ekeland’s variational principle, we can extract a Palais-Smale
(PS)

\displaystyle I_{s,t}(u)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{4}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y-\frac{\mu_{s,t}}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\frac{\mu_{s,t}}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left\||x|^{-\frac{t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}u\right\|_{2_{s}^{*}(t)}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}.

Now the lemma is an obvious consequence of Proposition

We are now ready to present the proof of the Bianchi-Egnell type stability for the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality (

).

Proof of Theorem

Clearly the sharpness of the result follows from the Lemma

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\mu_{s,t}\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}u_{k}\|^{2}_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{d(u_{k},\mathcal{M})^{2}}=0.

4.
Palais-Smale decomposition

Although the energy functional associated with our problem (
1.3
) is given as in (
3.7
), for purely technical reasons, we consider the following normalized functional

(4.1)

I_{s,t}(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left\||x|^{-\frac{t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}u\right\|_{2_{s}^{*}(t)}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the critical points of (
4.1
) and the critical points of (
3.7
). Specifically, one has that
u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is a weak solution to (
1.3
) (
equivalently
a non-negative critical point of (
3.7
))
if and only if

\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}}u
is a weak solution to the normalized Euler-Lagrange equation

(4.2)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\frac{|u|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u}{|x|^{t}};\quad u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\vskip 2.84526pt\\
u>0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

(
equivalently
a non-negative critical point of (
4.1
)). Due to this one-to-one correspondence, all the crucial properties of the energy functional are unchanged, therefore we only analyze the normalized functional moving onward.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
I_{s,t}
at level
\beta

\left((PS)_{\beta}\mbox{ condition in-short }\right)
, if
I_{s,t}(u_{n})\to\beta
and
(I_{s,t})^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
in
\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}
as
n\to\infty
. The functional
I_{s,t}
is said to satisfy
(PS)_{\beta}
condition if every
(PS)_{\beta}
sequence has a convergent subsequence in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

The weak limit of a
(PS)
sequence of
I_{s,t}
satisfies (
4.2
), except for positivity. The main challenge is that
(PS)
sequences may not converge strongly, and the weak limit can be zero even if
\beta>0
. Global compactness results for
(PS)
sequences have been widely studied in
[
4
,
24
,
26
,
27
]
and the references therein. For an abstract framework on profile decompositions, see
[
29
]
. This section classifies
(PS)
sequences of
I_{s,t}
, as stated in the next proposition, with a proof analogous to
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be a Palais-Smale sequence for
I_{s,t}

(c.f. (
4.1
))
at level
\beta
. Then up to a subsequence
(
still denoted by
u_{n})
the following properties hold:

There exist
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m
sequences
\left(R_{n}^{k}\right)_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}

(
for
1\leq k\leq m)
and functions
u_{0},\left(w_{k}\right)_{1\leq k\leq m}\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\,u_{n}=u_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}D_{\frac{1}{R_{n}^{k}}}w_{k}+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1);\qquad\,\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)\to 0\,\mbox{ in }\,\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,\mbox{ as }\,n\to\infty.

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\,I^{\prime}_{s,t}(u_{0})=0,\,I^{\prime}_{s,t}(w_{k})=0;\quad\forall\,1\leq k\leq m.

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\,\forall\,1\leq k\leq m;\quad R_{n}^{k}\to 0\,\mbox{ or }\,\infty\mbox{ and for }(l\neq k),\,\left|\log\left(\frac{R_{n}^{l}}{R_{n}^{k}}\right)\right|\to\infty\,\text{ as }\,n\to\infty.

\displaystyle(iv)

\displaystyle\,\beta=I_{s,t}(u_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}I_{s,t}\left(w_{k}\right).

Proof
.

We prove the result in several steps.

Step 1.
In this step we prove that every
(PS)_{\beta}
sequence is uniformly bounded in
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, i.e., there exists an
C>0
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C.

Indeed, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\beta+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle I_{s,t}(u_{n})\,-\,\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\prescript{}{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}{\left\langle I^{\prime}_{s,t}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

As
2_{s}^{*}(t)>2
, the above inequality concludes that
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Consequently, there exists
u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that, up to a subsequence (still denoted by
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
)
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover,
\prescript{}{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}{\left\langle I^{\prime}_{s,t}(u_{n}),v\right\rangle}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
implies

(4.3)

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}-\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{t}}\to 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}.

Step 2.

In this step, we prove that the weak limit
u_{0}
of our
(PS)_{\beta}
sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)
is a weak solution for (
4.2
). From (
4.3
), letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

(4.4)

\langle u_{n},v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x\,{\to}\,0,\qquad\text{for all }v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows from the definition of weak convergence that
\langle u_{n},v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}
for all
v\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Next we claim the following:

Claim 1.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{0}v}{|x|^{t}}{\rm d}x
for all

.

Indeed,
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x=\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x.

On
B_{R}
we are going to use Vitali’s convergence theorem. For that, given any
\epsilon>0
, we choose
\Omega\subset B_{R}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}<\frac{\epsilon}{\left(M\mu_{s,t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}}
. Since
\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}
is in

, the above choice makes sense. Therefore using Hölder’s inequality and fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality we obtain,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}|v|}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}<\epsilon.

Thus
\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{t}}
is a uniformly integrable sequence in

. Therefore, using Vitali’s convergence theorem we can pass to the limit in the integral on
B_{R}
of (
4.5

To estimate the integral on

, we first set

.
Then
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
It is not difficult to see that for every

there exists

C(\epsilon)>0
such that

\left|\frac{|v_{n}+u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}(v_{n}+u_{0})}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{t}}\right|<\epsilon\frac{|v_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}+C(\epsilon)\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\left\{\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{t}}\right\}v\;{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left[\epsilon\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}|v|}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x+C(\epsilon)\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}|v|}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg[\epsilon\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+C(\epsilon)\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\epsilon\|v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}+C(\epsilon)\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-1}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\right].

Since
\left(\|v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly bounded and
\frac{|v|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, given
\epsilon>0
, we can choose

large enough such that

\left|\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\left(\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{t}}\right)v\;{\rm d}x\right|<\epsilon.

This completes the proof of Claim 1.

Hence (
4.4
) yields that
u_{0}
is a solution of (
4.2
).

Step 3.
Here we show that
\left(u_{n}-u_{0}\right)_{n\geq 1}
is a
(PS)
sequence for
I_{s,t}
at the level
\beta-I_{s,t}(u_{0})
.
To see this, first, we observe that as

\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1),

and by the Brézis-Lieb lemma as

Therefore, as

\displaystyle I_{s,t}(u_{n}-u_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\;{\rm d}x\right)+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)

\displaystyle=I_{s,t}(u_{n})-I_{s,t}(u_{0})+\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)

\displaystyle\to\beta-I_{s,t}(u_{0}).

Further, as
\prescript{}{(\dot{H}^{s})^{\prime}}{\left\langle I_{s,t}^{\prime}(u_{0}),v\right\rangle}_{\dot{H}^{s}}=0

we obtain, as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{t}}-\frac{|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)-2}(u_{n}-u_{0})}{|x|^{t}}\right)v\,{\rm d}x=\scalebox{.5}[.5]{$\mathcal{O}$}(1)

Step 4.
In this step we rescale our
(PS)
sequence to get a nonzero weak limit. Define

Therefore,

and the
(PS)

If not, let

, which we analyze below in steps 5 and 6 separately.

Step 5.

Define

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\leq\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}\rho_{0}\|^{3}_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}c_{0}V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\|^{{2^{*}_{s}(t)}-3}_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\leq c_{0}^{p-2}\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{-3}{2}+\frac{2^{*}_{s}(t)(2^{*}_{s}(t)-3)}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}\|\rho_{0}\|^{3}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\quad\mbox{for }2s<N<6s-2t\,\,(\mbox{i.e., }p>2).

Now (
5.4
),(
5.6
),(
5.7
),(
5.8
),
\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\epsilon,\,|c_{0}-1|<\epsilon
and the spectral properties of the operator
\mu_{s,t}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}
together imply that

\displaystyle{\forall N>2s};\quad\left(1-\frac{c_{0}^{p-1}\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}\right)\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(u)+C(N,s,t)\left(c_{0}^{p-2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(u)+\left((1+\epsilon)^{p-2}\epsilon+\epsilon^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}\right)C(N,s,t)\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(u)+\epsilon^{\gamma}C(N,s,t)\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\hskip 8.53581pt;\left(\gamma=\min\{1,2^{*}_{s}(t)-2\}\right).

Choose
\epsilon>0
small enough such that

1-\frac{c_{0}^{p-1}\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}>1-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}-\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)>0\mbox{ and }1-\frac{c_{0}^{p-1}\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}-\epsilon^{\gamma}C(N,s,t)>0.

For this choice, (
5.9
) at once gives that

(5.10)

\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C(N,s,t)\,\Gamma(u).

Testing
(-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}
against
u
, we obtain,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left((-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}\right)u\,{\rm d}x.

From (
5.11
), we get by using
\left\langle\rho_{0},V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}}=0
,
\left\|V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\right\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)}=\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}
,
|c_{0}-1|<\epsilon
, triangle inequality, (
5.10
) and (
1.1
), that

\displaystyle\Gamma(u)\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left((-\Delta)^{s}u-\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-1}}{|x|^{t}}\right)u\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|c_{0}^{2}\,\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}(t)}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}+\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}-\left\|\left({\rho_{0}+c_{0}V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}}\right){|x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}}\right\|^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}(t)}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}\left|c_{0}^{2}-c_{0}^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\right|-\left|\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\mp\mathcal{O}\left(\Gamma(u)^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\right)\right|\right|

thus,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\left|c_{0}^{2}-c_{0}^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\Gamma(u)\right)\implies|c_{0}-1|=\mathcal{O}\left(\Gamma(u)\right).

Therefore, by virtue of (
5.10
) and (
5.12
), one observes

\|\rho\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|u-c_{0}V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}+(c_{0}-1)V^{\lambda_{0}}_{s,t}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+|c_{0}-1|\,\mu_{s,t}^{\frac{N-t}{2(2s-t)}}\leq C(N,s,t)\,\Gamma(u).

∎

Appendix A

In this section, we collect some important results that guarantee the discreteness of the spectrum of the linearized operator

(A.1)

\boxed{\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{s,t}\coloneqq\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}}{\frac{\mu_{s,t}(U_{s,t}^{\lambda})^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}{|x|^{t}}}.}

The following are appropriate modifications of the results due to Figalli-Glaudo (see
[
18
, Proposition A.1 and Theorem A.2]
). For completeness, we present the details tailored to our setup.

A.1.
Compact embedding of
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
into
L^{2}_{w}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

Proposition A.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
0<t<2s<N
. For any positive weight
w
with
|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\in L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the following embedding is compact:

i\colon\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Proof
.

Let us fix
R>0
. Consider the chain of continuous embeddings

\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{R}).

Hence we obtain the continuous embedding
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(B_{R})
. By the fractional Rellich–Kondrachov theorem
[
17
, Theorem 7.1]
, the embedding
H^{s}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{R})
is compact, which in turn implies that the embedding

\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{R})

is compact.

Now define
w_{R}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

w_{R}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}w(x)\quad\text{ if }w(x)<R,\\
0\,\qquad\,\,\,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

From the definition of
w_{R}
it is evident that
w_{R}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for every
R>0
. Moreover, we have,

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{w_{R}}(B_{R})}\leq\sqrt{R}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})},\text{ for all }u\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Thus we have the compact embedding:

(A.2)

\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{w_{R}}(B_{R}).

Now for every
f\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and any Borel set
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
one has,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{w}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}}\frac{|f|^{2}}{|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}}|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-t})}^{2}\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\|_{L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}^{-1}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\|_{L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)}.

Now fix a bounded sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\subset\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, i.e.,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C
. Then up to a subsequence (still denoted by
u_{n}
), we have the following

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\,u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\text{ in }\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\,u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{2}_{\rm loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\,u_{n}\to u\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By the compact embedding (
A.2
) and
(i)
above, we can ensure that for any
R>0
,
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}_{w_{R}}(B_{R})
as
n\to\infty
. We want to show that,
u_{n}\to u
in the stronger
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm.

Now we compute,

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{2}_{w}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}^{2}\\
=&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left[\int\limits_{\{|x|<R\}\,\cap\,\{w<R\}}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w_{R}\,{\rm d}x+\int\limits_{\{|x|\geq R\}\,\cap\,\{w<R\}}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w_{R}\,{\rm d}x\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 113.81102pt+\int\limits_{\left\{w\geq R\right\}}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w\,{\rm d}x\vphantom{\int\limits_{|x|<R\,\land\,|x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w<R}}\right].\end{split}

From the compact embedding
\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{w_{R}}(B_{R})
the first integral converges to
0
. For the second integral, we observe by using (
A.3
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\left\{|x|\geq R\right\}\,\cap\,\left\{w<R\right\}}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w_{R}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}^{-1}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\left\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\right\|_{L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\left(B_{R}^{c}\right)}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.

Since
\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|u_{n}-u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C
and
|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\in L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, indeed the above limit tends to
0
as
R\to\infty
.

For the last integral, we partition the region
\{w\geq R\}=\mathcal{A}_{R}\cup\left(\{w\geq R\}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{R}\right)
, where

\mathcal{A}_{R}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,\colon\,w(x)\geq R\right\}\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,\colon\,|x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w(x)<R\right\}.

Observe that, for every
R>0
,

x\in\mathcal{A}_{R}\implies R|x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\leq|x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w(x)<R\implies x\in B_{1}.

Thus for
\Omega\subset B_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}-u|^{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}\left(\int_{\Omega}||x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w|^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2s-t}{N-t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}^{-1}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}-u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}||x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w|^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2s-t}{N-t}}<\epsilon,

whenever
|\Omega|<\delta(\epsilon)
, which says the sequence
\left(|u_{n}-u|^{2}w\right)_{n\geq 1}
is tight and hence uniformly integrable in
L^{1}\left(B_{1}\right)
. Since
u_{n}\to u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, using Vitali’s convergence theorem and the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{R}\subset B_{1}
for every
R>0
, we get,

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}|u_{n}-u|^{2}w\,{\rm d}x\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty.

For the remaining case, i.e., the integral on the region
\{w\geq R\}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{R}
, using Chebyshev’s inequality we see

\left|\left\{w\geq R\right\}\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,\colon\,|x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w\geq R\right\}\right|\leq\frac{{\left\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2_{s}^{*}(t)}}w\right\|}^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}_{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}}{R^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.

Thus the final integral also goes to
0
as
R\to\infty
, which concludes the result. ∎

A.2.
Spectrum of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{s,t}

Theorem A.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
0<t<2s<N
. For any positive weight
w
with
|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\in L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the inverse operator

T_{s,w}\coloneqq\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}}{w}\right)^{-1}\colon L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

is well-defined and continuous. Hence, using Proposition
A.1
,
i\circ T_{s,w}
is a compact self-adjoint operator from
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into itself.

Proof
.

Define
\Phi\colon L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}
by
\Phi(f)(g)\coloneqq\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{w}}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}fgw\,{\rm d}x
, for all
g\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

Using Hölder’s inequality and fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality we obtain,

\displaystyle|\Phi(f)(g)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(fw^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\frac{g}{|x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|^{2}w\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\frac{2t(N-t)}{2^{*}_{s}(t)(2s-t)}}|w|^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2s-t}{2(N-t)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|g|^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{N-2s}{2(N-t)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{s,t}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\|_{L^{\frac{N-t}{2s-t}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Thus,
\Phi
is continuous and injective on
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Again
\Phi(f)\in\left(\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\right)^{\prime}
. By the Riesz Representation theorem, there exists a unique
v_{f}\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(f)(g)=\langle v_{f},g\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}},\,\text{ for all }g\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\frac{(v_{f}(x)-v_{f}(y))(g(x)-g(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}fwg\,{\rm d}x\quad;\forall g\in\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v_{f}=fw\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ in the sense of distributions.}

As a consequence, there is a unique continuous map
T_{s,w}\colon L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
defined by
T_{s,w}(f)\coloneqq v_{f}
such that

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{f}=fw\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

Thus,
T_{s,w}=\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}}{w}\right)^{-1}
and this completes the proof. ∎

Remark A.3
.

We consider the positive weight
w=\mu_{s,t}\frac{(U_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}}{|x|^{t}}
, where
U_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}}
weakly solves the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.3
). Now, denoting
\kappa\coloneqq\frac{N-t}{2s-t}=\frac{2^{*}_{s}(t)}{2^{*}_{s}(t)-2}
, we see that

\left\||x|^{\frac{2t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}w\right\|_{\kappa}^{\kappa}=\mu_{s,t}^{\kappa}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(U_{s,t}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}{|x|^{t}}\,{\rm d}x\right)=\mu_{s,t}^{\kappa}\left\||x|^{\frac{-t}{2^{*}_{s}(t)}}U_{s,t}\right\|_{2^{*}_{s}(t)}^{2^{*}_{s}(t)}=\mu_{s,t}^{\kappa}<\infty.

Clearly, for this
w
, Theorem
A.2
now guarantees that
i\circ T_{s,w}
is a compact self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to itself and consequently has a discrete spectrum.

Abstract
We analyze a static recommendation problem in which an expert with a continuous private signal advises a risky or a safe action and is evaluated through belief-based reputation. With MLRP signals and public observation of recommendations and outcomes, equilibrium advice is characterized by a signal cutoff. A mild relative-diagnosticity condition—failures are (weakly) more revealing than successes at high standing—implies reputational conservatism: higher reputation raises the cutoff, so risky advice becomes rarer yet more accurate. Comparative statics are transparent: greater signal informativeness or a higher success prior lowers the cutoff, whereas stronger career concerns raise it. A success-only bonus provides a one-to-one map to the experimentation rate, yielding an implementable design lever. Extensions show how implementation intensity (gatekeeping) shifts selection on risk; a Gaussian benchmark gives closed forms.

1
Introduction

Experts often face a tradeoff between recommending a
risky
action that can visibly fail and a
safe
action whose outcome reveals little. Because recommendations and realized outcomes are publicly observed and interpreted as evidence about ability, reputational incentives can tilt advice toward caution: avoiding a visible failure can be attractive even when the private information is favorable. This paper develops a tractable model of risky advice with reputational payoffs and shows when such incentives generate
reputational conservatism
—higher–standing experts become more selective and recommend risk less often.

We study a static recommendation problem. Before advising, the public holds a reputation index about the expert’s competence. The expert privately observes a continuous signal about a binary payoff and recommends either a risky or a safe action. If risky is recommended (and implemented), a success or failure is realized and publicly observed; the public then updates reputation. The expert’s payoff is increasing in the resulting posterior reputation; we also allow for a success–contingent transfer that pays only after risky advice succeeds. The analysis is cast directly in signal space, which yields simple characterizations and comparative statics without solving a dynamic program.

Our first result is a sharp behavioral characterization: for any prior reputation, equilibrium advice follows a
cutoff rule
in the private signal (Theorem
1
). The second result identifies a mild
relative–diagnosticity
condition under which the cutoff is (weakly) increasing in reputation (Theorem
2
). Intuitively, at high standing an unexpected failure is especially informative and hence especially costly in reputational terms, while the incremental informational gain from a success does not rise commensurately; this makes risk taking less attractive at the top. The same framework delivers transparent comparative statics: greater signal informativeness or a higher prior success probability lowers the cutoff, whereas stronger career concerns raise it (Proposition
1
). Finally, a simple success–contingent bonus rotates incentives and induces a one–to–one mapping from the bonus to the experimentation rate (Proposition
2
), furnishing implementable levers for committees and monitoring regimes that operate through implementation intensity.

The primitives are intentionally minimal: a continuous private signal satisfying a monotone likelihood–ratio property (single–crossing), public observation of recommendations and outcomes, and a monotone reputational payoff. The analysis is distribution–free under these assumptions; a Gaussian benchmark provides intuition but is not required. The baseline treats implementation as exogenous (equal to one); an extension allows implementation intensity to vary (e.g., due to gatekeeping or monitoring) and shows how this amplifies conservatism and strengthens the design levers.

This paper contributes to the literatures on career concerns and reputation, expert advice and strategic communication, delegated experimentation, and contract design, with an emphasis on theory.

The interaction of selection on the private signal with the relative informativeness of successes and failures at different standing generates the conservatism effect and supports simple design results.

Delegated experimentation links action-generated information to incentives and organizational rules. Our static formulation abstracts from intertemporal tradeoffs but is connected to dynamic reputational learning and bandit-style models

Model

We study a one–shot advisory interaction in which an expert recommends a
risky
action (
a=1
) or a

3.1
Information and outcome primitives

The expert’s (ability) type is
\theta\in\{H,L\}
with public prior
\pi=\Pr(\theta=H)\in(0,1)
. There is a payoff state
\omega\in\{0,1\}
with prior
\Pr(\omega=1)=\alpha\in(0,1)
. Before making a recommendation, the expert privately observes a continuous signal
s\in S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
about
\omega
. Conditional on
(\theta,\omega)
,

has density
f_{\theta}(s\mid\omega)
with the monotone likelihood ratio property (MLRP) in

\ell_{\theta}(s)\;\equiv\;\frac{f_{\theta}(s\mid\omega=1)}{f_{\theta}(s\mid\omega=0)}\quad\text{is strictly increasing in $s$.}

Moreover,
H
is (weakly) more informative than
L
in the Blackwell sense. A convenient benchmark (used for illustrations only) is Gaussian signals

s\mid(\omega,\theta)\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\omega},\sigma_{\theta}^{2}),\qquad\mu_{1}>\mu_{0},\quad\sigma_{L}^{2}>\sigma_{H}^{2},

which satisfy both MLRP and the informativeness ordering.

If
a=0
the safe action produces a non-informative baseline outcome

. If
a=1
, the risky action succeeds (
y=1
) if and only if
\omega=1
; otherwise

(a,\omega)

\begin{split}\Pr(y=1\mid a,\omega)&=\mathbf{1}\{a=1\}\,\mathbf{1}\{\omega=1\},\\
\Pr(y=0\mid a,\omega)&=1-\Pr(y=1\mid a,\omega).\end{split}

Extensions that allow baseline risk under
a=0
, misclassification of

, or partial observability of
a
are collected in Section
6

Before the episode the market holds a public belief
\pi=\Pr(\theta=H)\in(0,1)
. After observing the public history
h=(a,y)
, the market forms a posterior
\pi^{\prime}=\Pr(\theta=H\mid h)
using Bayes’ rule. The only role of the private signal
s
in public updating is through the expert’s strategy; the market integrates over
s
using equilibrium behavior.

Let
r_{\theta}(a\mid\omega)
denote the
recommendation frequency
: the probability (under type
\theta
and state
\omega
) that the expert’s strategy prescribes
a
. Given public
\pi
and prior
\alpha

\begin{split}\Pr(a\mid\theta)&=(1-\alpha)\,r_{\theta}(a\mid 0)+\alpha\,r_{\theta}(a\mid 1),\\
\Pr(y\mid a,\theta)&=\begin{cases}1,&a=0,\ y=0,\\
\alpha,&a=1,\ y=1,\\
1-\alpha,&a=1,\ y=0,\end{cases}\end{split}

and the joint likelihood of
(a,y)
under
\theta
is
\Pr(a\mid\theta)\Pr(y\mid a,\theta)
. The posterior odds satisfy

\frac{\pi^{\prime}}{1-\pi^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{\Pr(a\mid\theta=H)}{\Pr(a\mid\theta=L)}}_{\text{recommendation LLR}}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{\Pr(y\mid a,\theta=H)}{\Pr(y\mid a,\theta=L)}}_{\text{outcome LLR}},

(1)

where the outcome LLR equals
1
in the baseline (because
\Pr(y\mid a,\theta)
does not depend on
\theta
); recommendations remain informative about
\theta
via
r_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\cdot)
.

Beliefs and reputation updates

Given a strategy profile, (

\pi^{\prime}
after each publicly observed event
h\in\{(0,0),(1,1),(1,0)\}

3.3
Expert’s objective and strategies

The expert’s payoff from the episode is

u\;=\;\phi\,a\;+\;V(\pi^{\prime}),

where
V:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is increasing and convex, and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
is a reduced-form flow payoff from advocating risk (capturing, e.g., taste or direct incentives; we allow
\phi=0
). Convexity captures the standard force that the marginal value of reputation rises with standing; we keep
V
reduced-form as is common in career-concerns models.

We also allow simple transfers tied to realized outcomes under
a=1

affine
transfer takes the form
T(a,y)=\mathbf{1}\{a=1\}(\beta_{1}\mathbf{1}\{y=1\}-\beta_{0}\mathbf{1}\{y=0\})
, where
\beta_{1},\beta_{0}\geq 0
. The success-only bonus used later is the special case
\beta_{0}=0
,
\beta_{1}=b

A (pure) strategy for type
\theta
is a measurable mapping
s\mapsto a(s)\in\{0,1\}
. We focus on

\theta

\pi

such that

3.4
Equilibrium

An equilibrium consists of cutoff strategies
\{s^{*}_{\theta}(\pi):\theta\in\{H,L\}\}
, recommendation frequencies

implied by those cutoffs and the primitives, and Bayes-consistent posteriors

such that (i) given
\pi

, and (ii)
\pi^{\prime}
is obtained from (

The results above provide a compact economic summary. For fixed primitives, reputational payoffs tilt the recommendation margin through the relative informativeness of success and failure at the prevailing standing. When failures are the more revealing outcome at high reputation, conservatism follows; otherwise, the effect can be neutral or reversed on lower parts of the reputation range. These patterns are portable and testable across advisory domains.

6
Discussion and Extensions

This section records a few minimal variants that leave the single–crossing logic and the cutoff characterization intact. We keep the static, continuous–signal environment and Assumptions (A1)–(A4) unless noted. The variants cover (i) implementation/gatekeeping (committees and aggregation), (ii) partial observability and noisy outcomes, and (iii) the success–contingent–bonus mapping collected in a standalone statement.

6.1
Implementation intensity and gatekeeping

Suppose that when the expert recommends the risky action, it is implemented with probability
\lambda\in(0,1]
(exogenous in this subsection). If the outcome is realized only upon implementation, the risky–safe advantage at signal
s
shifts from
\Delta_{H}(s;\pi)
to

\Delta^{\lambda}_{H}(s;\pi)\;=\;\phi\;+\;\lambda\,\underbrace{\Big(\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid a=1,H,s]-\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid a=0,H,s]\Big)}_{\text{reputational return at }s}\!,

and the same algebra applies when a success bonus
\beta_{1}
is present (the bonus term is multiplied by
\lambda
). Because
\partial_{s}\Delta^{\lambda}_{H}(s;\pi)=\lambda\,\partial_{s}\Delta_{H}(s;\pi)
and
\partial_{s}\Delta_{H}>0
by Lemma
1
, single–crossing is preserved. The indifference condition at the cutoff then implies a comparative static:

Proposition 5
.

Fix
\pi\in(0,1)
and Assumptions (A1)–(A4). Let
s^{*}(\pi;\lambda)
be the unique cutoff under implementation probability
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Then
s^{*}(\pi;\lambda)
is (strictly) decreasing in
\lambda
; equivalently, the experimentation rate
1-F_{S}(s^{*}(\pi;\lambda))
is (strictly) increasing in
\lambda
.

Sketch.
Differentiate the indifference condition
\Delta^{\lambda}_{H}(s^{*}(\pi;\lambda);\pi)=0
with respect to
\lambda
:

\frac{\partial s^{*}}{\partial\lambda}(\pi;\lambda)\;=\;-\,\frac{\partial_{\lambda}\Delta^{\lambda}_{H}}{\partial_{s}\Delta^{\lambda}_{H}}\;=\;-\,\frac{\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid a=1,H,s^{*}]-\mathbb{E}[V(\pi^{\prime})\mid a=0,H,s^{*}]}{\lambda\,\partial_{s}\Delta_{H}(s^{*};\pi)}\;<\;0,

since the numerator is positive at the cutoff and
\partial_{s}\Delta_{H}>0
by Lemma
1
. ∎

A parsimonious reduced form for committee thresholds and gatekeeping is
\lambda=\lambda(T)
with
\lambda^{\prime}(T)\leq 0
. By the chain rule, stricter gatekeeping (higher
T
) raises the cutoff and lowers experimentation. If
\lambda
(weakly) increases with prior reputation
\pi
(e.g., high–reputation experts face looser gatekeeping), the reputational conservatism in Section
4
is amplified.

6.2
Partial observability and noisy outcomes

Two measurement frictions are natural and straightforward to incorporate.

(i) Baseline outcome noise.
If
a=0
produces
y=1
with small probability
\eta\in(0,1)
(baseline risk), or if the realized
y
is misclassified with probability
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, the three public histories become
(0,0)
,
(1,1)
, and
(1,0)
with perturbed likelihoods. Because these perturbations leave the likelihood–ratio order in
s
intact, single–crossing and the cutoff characterization remain unchanged. All comparative statics carry through with
\alpha
replaced by the appropriate effective success probabilities.

(ii) Unobserved implementation.
If the recommendation
a=1
is public but the outcome is observed only when implemented (probability
\lambda
), the public history splits into
(1,1)
,
(1,0)
, and
(1,\varnothing)
, where
\varnothing
denotes “no observed outcome”. The posterior updates weight the recommendation–only event
(1,\varnothing)
proportionally to
1-\lambda
. As above, the risky–safe advantage is scaled by
\lambda
, and Proposition
5
applies.

6.3
Success–contingent bonus: implementation theorem

For completeness, we collect the one–to–one mapping from a success–only bonus to the experimentation rate. Let the affine transfer after a risky recommendation be

T(1,1)=\beta_{1}\geq 0,\qquad T(1,0)=-\beta_{0}\leq 0,\qquad T(0,0)=0,

and write the high–type’s on–path risky frequency as

\rho(\pi;\beta_{1},\beta_{0})\;=\;(1-\alpha)\,r_{H}(1\mid 0;s^{*})\;+\;\alpha\,r_{H}(1\mid 1;s^{*}),

where
s^{*}=s^{*}(\pi;\beta_{1},\beta_{0})
is the unique cutoff under
(\beta_{1},\beta_{0})
.

Proposition 6
.

Fix
\pi\in(0,1)
and Assumptions (A1)–(A4).

1.

Limited liability.
With
\beta_{0}=0
, the map
\beta_{1}\mapsto\rho(\pi;\beta_{1},0)
is continuous and strictly increasing, with
\lim_{\beta_{1}\downarrow 0}\rho(\pi;\beta_{1},0)=\rho(\pi;0,0)\in(0,1)
and
\lim_{\beta_{1}\uparrow\infty}\rho(\pi;\beta_{1},0)=1
. Hence for any target
\rho^{\star}\in\big(\rho(\pi;0,0),1\big)
there exists a unique
\beta_{1}^{\star}(\pi,\rho^{\star})\geq 0
implementing
\rho^{\star}
.

2.

General affine contract.
Allowing
\beta_{0}\geq 0
, any
\rho^{\star}\in(0,1)
is implementable. The set of implementers is the affine line

\alpha\,\beta_{1}-(1-\alpha)\,\beta_{0}\;=\;-\,\Delta_{H}(\hat{s};\pi),

where
\hat{s}
is the unique cutoff that delivers
\rho^{\star}
without transfers, and
\Delta_{H}(\cdot;\pi)
is the risky–safe advantage without transfers.

Moreover, when the cutoff is interior and densities are positive at
\hat{s}
, the local sensitivities are

\frac{\partial s^{*}}{\partial\beta_{1}}\;=\;-\,\frac{\alpha}{\partial_{s}\Delta_{H}(s^{*};\pi)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{d\rho}{d\beta_{1}}\;=\;\Big[(1-\alpha)f_{H}(s^{*}\!\mid 0)+\alpha\,f_{H}(s^{*}\!\mid 1)\Big]\Big(-\frac{\partial s^{*}}{\partial\beta_{1}}\Big)\;>\;0.

The proposition restates, in a self–contained way, the design result used in Section
5
. In the Gaussian benchmark, the closed–form back–out
\beta_{1}(c;\pi)
in (
4
) provides a direct calibration route; Table
2
illustrates.

These extensions show that the core mechanism—single–crossing in the signal and belief–based evaluation—survives implementation frictions and measurement noise, and that simple bonuses retain a one–to–one link to experimentation while gatekeeping shifts selection on risk in predictable ways.

7
Conclusion

We analyzed a one–shot advisory problem in which an expert with a continuous private signal recommends a risky or a safe action and is evaluated through a belief–based reputational payoff. Under standard single–crossing primitives (MLRP) and public observability of recommendations and outcomes, advice is characterized by a cutoff in the signal (Theorem
1
). A mild relative–diagnosticity condition then delivers
reputational conservatism
: the cutoff (weakly) increases with prior reputation (Theorem
2
). The structure yields transparent comparative statics—greater signal informativeness and a higher success prior lower the cutoff, while stronger career concerns raise it (Proposition
1
)—and an implementability result: a simple success–contingent transfer provides a one–to–one map to the experimentation rate (Proposition
2
; see also Proposition
6
). Extensions show that implementation intensity (gatekeeping) shifts the cutoff in predictable ways (Proposition
5
) and that the results are robust to basic measurement frictions; a Gaussian benchmark offers closed forms and an easy calibration route.

The main message is that selection on private information, together with asymmetric diagnosticity of successes and failures at different standings, suffices to generate a disciplined link between reputation and risk taking. Because the primitives are minimal and the characterization is global, the framework is portable and can serve as a building block for organizational design with committees, monitoring, or simple compensation rules. Natural next steps include richer contracts (e.g., failure penalties under limited liability), multiple interacting experts, and environments with correlated opportunities or multi–dimensional signals; our cutoff characterization and design formulas provide tractable starting points for these extensions.

OA6.1
Loss aversion around a reputational benchmark

Fix
\bar{\pi}\in(0,1)
and parameters
b>0
,
\lambda\geq 1
,
\kappa_{\pm}\geq 0
; let

V(\pi)=v_{0}+b\!\left[(\pi-\bar{\pi})_{+}-\lambda\,(\bar{\pi}-\pi)_{+}\right]+\frac{\kappa_{+}}{2}(\pi-\bar{\pi})_{+}^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{-}}{2}(\bar{\pi}-\pi)_{+}^{2}.

Then
V^{\prime}_{+}(\bar{\pi})=b
and
V^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{\pi})=\lambda b
with
\lambda>1
. Writing
p_{H}(s)=\Pr(\omega=1\mid H,s)
and
\tilde{\pi}
the posterior after
a=0
,

\frac{\partial\Delta_{H}(s;\pi)}{\partial\lambda}=b\Big[(\bar{\pi}-\tilde{\pi})_{+}-\mathbb{E}\big[(\bar{\pi}-\pi^{\prime})_{+}\mid a=1,H,s,\pi\big]\Big]

so increasing loss aversion (higher
\lambda
) lowers
\Delta_{H}
when
\tilde{\pi}
sits near
\bar{\pi}
, raising the cutoff locally.

OA6.2
Overconfidence as distorted informativeness

If the expert is overconfident about signal precision (perceives
\hat{\sigma}_{H}<\sigma_{H}
), her perceived
p_{H}(s)
is steeper, which lowers the perceived cutoff; with correct market updating, this yields an observational wedge between perceived and implemented experimentation. The comparative static in
\sigma_{H}
from Proposition
10
quantifies the direction.

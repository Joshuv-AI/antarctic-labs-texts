Concurrent immunoradiotherapy with Toripalimab versus concurrent chemoradiotherapy with S-1 in older patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yixin Huang, Chao Zhou, Jian Zhu, Haijian Jia, Feng Zhao, Haihua Yang, Suna Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•CIRT vs CCRT in older ESCC: similar response, shorter survival, improved tolerability.•Distant recurrences prevailed post-CIRT, while CCRT failures were mainly locoregional.•Early-treatment lymphopenia linked to poorer OS, particularly in CIRT group.Keywords: Concurrent chemoradiotherapy, Concurrent immunoradiotherapy, Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Immuno-inflammatory biomarker, Toripalimab, S-1

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors globally and became the seventh cause of cancer death in 2022 [1]. China accounts for 43.8% of global cases and 42.1% deaths of EC, with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) being the predominant histological subtype [2]. The median age at diagnosis is 69 years, with over 40% of patients being 70 years or older [3,4]. Older adults represent a distinct population group, typically afflicted with a multitude of underlying health conditions, and thus, they exhibit a relatively low tolerance to the toxicity and side effects associated with treatment modalities. Concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) was the standard treatment regimen for patients with locally advanced ESCC [5,6]. Aged patients cannot tolerate the toxicity and side effects associated with dual-agent chemotherapy. A study of older patients with EC demonstrated that CCRT with S-1 was well-tolerated and offered substantial benefits compared to radiotherapy (RT) alone [7]. In recent years, immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has demonstrated encouraging success in various tumors [8]. Although RT combined with immunotherapy is widely used in oncology, the effect of RT on further enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapy in ESCC remains unclear [9]. Clinical data on immunoradiotherapy in ESCC are accumulating: a retrospective cohort showed survival benefit with postoperative immunotherapy and RT [10]; a prospective phase II trial established its safety and efficacy with concurrent CCRT [11]. These findings are being further evaluated in ongoing trials such as iCROSS (NCT04973306), which is comparing CCRT with versus without immunotherapy. The accumulating efficacy evidence must be balanced against significant safety signals. High rates of grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) have been observed with toripalimab-based combinations (73.2% with chemotherapy; 82% with added radiotherapy) [11,12]. Whether this risk-benefit profile is acceptable for older, potentially more frail patients is a key unanswered question, currently being explored in a dedicated trial (NCT06061146) for inoperable locally advanced ESCC [13]. Notably, despite comprising over 40% of the ESCC population, patients aged 70 years or older remain significantly underrepresented in large clinical trials. Consequently, there is a critical gap in high-level evidence to inform clinical decision-making for this demographic. Thus, it is crucial to investigate if a chemotherapy-free regimen of combined radiotherapy and immunotherapy can reduce toxicity without compromising efficacy. A direct comparison between toripalimab-based CIRT and S-1-based CCRT may directly addresses this unanswered question and provides a critical evidence base for optimizing treatment in the elderly population. Moreover, no prior studies have directly compared a novel toripalimab-based concurrent immunoradiotherapy (CIRT) regimen against the standard S-1-based CCRT exclusively in an elderly cohort. Toripalimab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody that binds to PD-1, can reactivate the innate immune system, promote the proliferation and differentiation of T cells, and suppress tumor growth [14]. Toripalimab combined with chemoradiotherapy has demonstrated promising efficacy and manageable toxicity in patients with locally advanced ESCC [11,15,16]. Although the combination of immunotherapy and chemoradiotherapy improves survival, not all patients benefit from this combination treatment, and some responders experience disease progression after a certain period of remission. Therefore, it is essential to identify effective biomarkers to predict sustained responses to the combined treatment with immunotherapy. Immune and inflammatory status are relevant to tumor response and clinical outcome [17]. An increasing number of studies have shown that immuno-inflammatory biomarkers (IBs) can predict the prognosis of patients diagnosed with tumors, such as absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), pan-immune-inflammation value (PIV), platelet lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte monocyte ratio (LMR), neutrophil lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) [[18], [19], [20]]. Furthermore, these IBs based on routine blood tests are easily accessible in clinical practice and convenient for clinical management.

This retrospective study was designed to test our hypothesis and fill a critical evidence gap by directly comparing CIRT to CCRT in older patients with locally advanced ESCC. Additionally, biomarker analyses were conducted to elucidate potential mechanisms underlying differential treatment outcomes.

Materials and methods

Patients

The Institutional Review Board of Taizhou Hospital approved this retrospective cohort study (approval number K20250912 (EZ)), waiving the requirement for written informed consent. The study complied with the amended Declaration of Helsinki. A total of 93 patients with ESCC aged 70 years or older who underwent CIRT or CCRT between October 2021 and June 2024 at the hospital were enrolled in the study and screened based on the following criteria. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed ESCC staged as Ⅱ-Ⅳa according to the eighth edition TNM staging system of the American Joint Committee on Cancer; (2) received chest RT combined with anti-PD-1 toripalimab or S-1; (3) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score of 0–1; (4) accessible blood tests before, during and after concurrent treatment; (5) patients aged 70 years or older; (6) completing RT with 50–60 Gy in 25–30 fractions to planning gross tumor volume (PGTV), with treatment interruptions not exceeding 2 weeks. The exclusion criteria were: (1) non-ESCC patients or those with staged I or Ⅳb; (2) inadequate clinical information and follow-up; (3) receiving concurrent treatment for other tumors; (4) previous treatment for esophageal cancer with RT or PD-1/PD-L1 blocker. Finally, 22 patients with ESCC treated with CIRT were enrolled, alongside 25 patients with ESCC treated with CCRT.

Treatment and follow-up

Treatment was delivered via a linear accelerator using 6-MV photons under an intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) technique. All diagnostic modalities—such as physical examination, endoscopy, and radiographic assessments—were utilized to define the gross tumor volume (GTV), covering the primary lesion and any metastatic lymph nodes. The clinical target volume (CTV) for the primary tumor was derived by adding a 3-cm longitudinal and a 0.5-cm radial margin to the GTV. Similarly, a 0.5–1.0 cm expansion was applied to involved nodes to create the nodal CTV. Additionally, elective nodal irradiation was performed in all cases. A total prescription dose was 50–60 Gy/25–30 fractions to PGTV, and 45–50 Gy/25–30 fractions to PTV. Dose constraints for the main organs at risk were defined in accordance with Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) guidelines (0126∼0822) as follows: for the lungs, mean dose < 20 Gy and V20 < 25%; for the heart, V30 < 40% and V40 < 30%; and for the spinal cord, maximum point dose < 45 Gy with the dose to 1 cm³ < 50 Gy. The CIRT cohort data originate from a clinical trial (NCT05817201) whose results have not yet been publicly reported. In the CIRT group, toripalimab was administered at a dose of 240 mg by intravenous injection. The first cycle of concurrent toripalimab was administered within 5 calendar days following the initiation of RT. The second cycle was delivered on day 25 of RT (±3 days). For patients proceeding to consolidation therapy, the first maintenance cycle was initiated within a predefined window of 14 to 28 days after the completion of RT. Patients received up to 15 additional cycles of toripalimab (240 mg every 3 weeks), resulting in a cumulative maximum of 17 cycles including the initial treatment. Therapy for the CCRT cohort was administered in accordance with the protocol of a clinical trial (NCT02813967) conducted with the participation of our institution[7]. In the CCRT group, S-1 was given 40–60 mg/m2 for 14 days, followed by a 7-day break, and this cycle was repeated twice during the RT course. One month after completing RT, the first follow-up evaluation was performed, followed by assessments once every 3 months for the first 2 years, then biannual evaluations starting from the third year. Progression-free survival (PFS) was calculated as the time from treatment initiation to tumor progression or death. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death or the censoring date at the last follow-up.

Data collection

The following baseline characteristics were obtained from cases: age, sex, smoking history, alcohol history, body mass index, ECOG PS, primary tumor length, tumor location, clinical TNM stage, and clinical stage. Tumor response was evaluated one month after the completion of RT. Assessments were conducted in accordance with Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. Two experienced radiologists, blinded to clinical data, independently reviewed the chest computed tomography scans to determine the response. The objective response rate was calculated as the sum of complete response (%) and partial response (%), whereas the disease control rate was defined as the proportion of patients with complete response, partial response, or stable disease. Adverse events (AEs) were assessed using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. The data of routine blood test (RBT) was collected at four timepoints (pre-: within 7 days prior to the initiation of RT; during1-: at the end of the first week of RT (Day 7 ± 3 days); during2-: at the end of the fifth week of RT (Week 5 ± 7 days); post-: Day 21 ± 7 days from the end of RT) as illustrated in Fig. 1. Particularly, RBT was collected at specific timepoints: during1- prior to the first cycle of immunotherapy infusion throughout the concurrent RT phase, and post- before initiating the first cycle of toripalimab maintenance therapy after RT. The concerned biomarkers were as follows: ALC; PIV, absolute neutrophil count × absolute monocyte count × platelet count/ALC; PLR, platelet count/ALC; LMR, ALC/absolute monocyte count; NLR, absolute neutrophil count/ALC; SIRI, absolute neutrophil count × absolute monocyte count/ALC. Moreover, IBs at four timepoints were respectively named as pre-IB, during1-IB, during2-IB, and post-IB.

Statistical analysis

Independent sample t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests were used to compare differences between groups for continuous variables, based on normality calculated using Shapiro-Wilk test. For binary variables, the Chi-square test was used, and for ordinal categorical variables, the Mann-Whitney U test was employed. Differences in IBs between timepoints were compared with a paired samples t-test or a paired samples Wilcoxon signed-rank test. We used the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test to determine PFS and OS curves, as well as the significance of the difference. The relevance between IBs (as continuous variables) and PFS or OS in all enrolled patients was explored using univariate Cox proportional hazards regression. Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the correlations among significant variables before multivariate Cox regression. Variables with P < 0.05 on univariate Cox analysis were entered into multivariable Cox analysis for OS with the backward likelihood ratio method. And, to avoid collinearity, only one representative variable from each cluster of highly correlated variables (defined as Spearman's rho > 0.9) was retained for the multivariable Cox models. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using the Schoenfeld residual test and plot. Patients were divided into groups using the median value as the cut-off for Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Significant difference was considered as P values < 0.05. SPSS (version 27.0; IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) and R software (version 4.5.0; R Core Team, Vienna, Austria) and Origin (Pro) (Version 2024; OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA.) were used for statistical analysis and graphing.

Results

Patient characteristics

Fig. 1 presents the flowchart of this study. Finally, a total of 47 patients were enrolled into further analysis, including the CIRT group (n = 22) and the CCRT group (n = 25). There were no statistically significant differences in clinical characteristics or major organ functions between groups (Table 1 and Table S1). The median age was 77 years (interquartile range, 76–80.5) for the CIRT group and 80 years (interquartile range, 76–83) for the CCRT group. The CIRT group consisted of 19 (86.4%) men, and the CCRT group consisted of 16 (64.0%) men. >95% of the CIRT and CCRT patients had thoracic esophageal cancer, with the majority being at local advanced stages (Ⅲ–Ⅳa). The counts of patients at stage Ⅱ, Ⅲ–Ⅳa were 5 (22.7%) and 17 (77.3%) in the CIRT group, while 4 (16.0%) and 21 (84.0%) in the CCRT group, respectively.

Efficacy and adverse events

Of the enrolled 22 CIRT patients, 17 (77.3%) achieved partial response, 3 (13.6%) had stable disease, 2 (9.1%) experienced progressive disease, while all 25 (100%) in the CCRT group reached partial response (Table 2). The objective response and disease control were achieved in 17 (77.3%) and 20 (90.9%) patients, respectively, in the CIRT group, whereas both were achieved in 25 (100%) patients in the CCRT group. The waterfall plot displays the degree of tumor shrinkage for each individual patient in the two treatment groups (Figure S1). Kaplan-Meier curves analysis showed that the CIRT group exhibited a shorter median PFS of 9.67 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 3.10–16.23 months) compared to the CCRT group, which had a median PFS of 20.63 months (95% CI, 16.96–24.31 months; P = 0.042) (Fig. 2). Also, the median OS of the CIRT group (13.67 months; 95% CI, 11.18–16.15 months) was significantly shorter than that of the CCRT group (23.73 months; 95% CI, 18.38–29.10 months; P = 0.045).

Table 3 summarizes treatment-related AEs during therapy. In the CIRT group, 22 (100%) patients reported AEs of any grade, and 16 (72.7%) sustained grade 3 or higher AEs. In the CCRT group, 25 (100%) patients experienced AEs of any grade, and 24 (96.0%) patients had grade 3 or higher AEs. Lymphopenia was the most prevalent AE in both groups, occurring in 21 (95.5%) in CIRT patients and 25 (100%) in CCRT patients. A similar atlas of AEs was found between the CIRT group and the CCRT group. The common treatment-related AEs in the CIRT group were esophagitis (15 [68.2%] patients), leukopenia (10 [45.5%] patients), and anemia (17 [77.3%] patients), while grade 3 or higher AEs, such as lymphopenia, occurred in 16 (72.7%) patients. The common treatment-related AEs in the CCRT group were esophagitis (18 [72.0%] patients), leukopenia (18 [72.0%] patients), and anemia (21 [84.0%] patients). Grade 3 or higher lymphopenia occurred in 23 (92.0%) patients of the CCRT group. The CCRT group showed a trend toward increased leukopenia frequency compared to the CIRT group (18 [72.0%] patients vs 10 [45.5%] patients). Notably, the CIRT group developed 1 (4.5%) patient of grade 2 immune-related dermatitis. The only grade 5 AE occurred in the CIRT group, in which 1 (4.5%) patient died of immune-related pneumonia.

Variation in biomarkers during therapy

To uncover the dynamic changes in these potential biomarkers, we collected data from RBT at four timepoints. The results showed that in the CIRT group, ALC and LMR decreased sharply during CIRT and recovered after 1 month, although they did not return to the baseline level (Fig. 3). Inversely, PIV, PLR, NLR, and SIRI increased during CIRT and decreased 1 month after the end of concurrent therapy. The CCRT group showed a similar dynamic change (Fig. 4). We then compared the differences in IBs between groups and found differences only in post-LMR (P = 0.016); no differences were detected in the remaining IBs (Table S2). Additionally, the ALC decline (P = 0.615) and recovery (P = 0.698) patterns showed no significant intergroup differences. Therefore, we further analyzed the differences in monocyte count between the two groups. We discovered that the absolute monocyte count showed significant differences between groups for during1- (P = 0.006) and during2- (P = 0.006) (Fig. 5). In the CIRT group, the monocytes of during2- increased following the initiation of anti-PD-1 treatment. While in the CCRT group, the monocytes of during1- declined but recovered by the timepoint of during2-.

Predictors for survival prognosis

We conducted univariate Cox regression analyses to investigate the predictive efficacy of IBs in PFS and OS prognosis. Univariate Cox regression analyses indicated that treatment group (Hazard Ratio [HR], 0.49; P = 0.045), during1-ALC (HR, 0.24; P = 0.016), pre-PIV (HR, 1.00; P = 0.040), pre-NLR (HR, 1.16; P = 0.034), pre-SIRI (HR, 1.60; P = 0.027), during1-PIV (HR, 1.00; P = 0.030), during1-PLR (HR, 1.01; P = 0.012), during1-NLR (HR, 1.09; P = 0.006) and during1-SIRI (HR, 1.19; P = 0.020) were risk factors for PFS (Table 5). Meanwhile, the risk factors for OS were treatment group (HR, 0.47; P = 0.049), clinical stage Ⅳa (HR, 3.49; P = 0.047), during1-ALC (HR, 0.26; P = 0.029), pre-PIV (HR, 1.00; P = 0.044), pre-NLR (HR, 1.19; P = 0.033), pre-SIRI (HR, 1.58; P = 0.043), during1-PIV (HR, 1.00; P = 0.008), during1-PLR (HR, 1.00; P = 0.039), during1-NLR (HR, 1.12; P = 0.003) and during1-SIRI (HR, 1.27; P = 0.004). According to Spearman correlation analysis, we excluded several variables with correlation coefficient > 0.9 (Figure S2). We added the remaining variables to multivariate Cox analysis. We used the Schoenfeld residual test to verify the proportional hazard assumption. The assumption was true for global (P = 0.218) and covariate-specific (treatment group, P = 0.204; during1-ALC, P = 0.256; pre-SIRI, P = 0.378) tests (Figure S3). Treatment group (HR, 0.32; P = 0.008), during1-ALC (HR, 0.20; P = 0.018) and pre-SIRI (HR, 1.66; P = 0.036) were independently associated with OS (Table 6).

Discussion

Antitumor treatment guidelines primarily rely on evidence-based medicine derived from clinical trials involving patients under 75 years old. Significantly, patients with esophageal cancer aged 70 years or older constitute over 40% of cases[4]. Therefore, ESCC patients aged 70 years or older should be considered for further exploration of treatment strategies. A diminished tolerance to chemotherapy and multimodal antitumor regimens is frequently observed in older adults relative to younger patients. Despite its efficacy, immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy carries a high burden of grade ≥ 3 AEs in ESCC patients under 70, which poses a potentially greater challenge for the older population [11,12]. Therefore, evaluating a potentially less toxic, chemotherapy-free approach is of paramount importance. Hence, this study was motivated to compare the CIRT regimen against standard CCRT, aiming to generate preliminary evidence for a de-escalation strategy tailored to the elderly. The median PFS and OS of patients with EC in NCT02813967, 99.3% of whom were ESCC, treated with concurrent RT and S-1 were 18.7 months and 24.9 months, respectively [7]. Compared with NCT02813967, although our enrolled patients were of advanced age and had more aggressive stages, they also experienced comparable survival benefits from concurrent RT and S-1, with a median PFS of 20.63 months and a median OS of 23.73 months. In patients with stage IV ESCC (median age 59 years), the addition of radiotherapy to first-line toripalimab plus chemotherapy (ChiCTR2100046715) achieved a median PFS of 9.8 months and a median OS of 16.5 months, with a tolerable safety profile [11]. A phase Ⅰb study evaluating RT plus anti-PD-1 camrelizumab as a first-line treatment for locally advanced ESCC, with a median age of 64 years, reported a median OS of 16.7 months and a PFS of 11.7 months, respectively [21]. In the KEYNOTE-590 trial investigating first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, the median OS was 12.6 months and the median PFS was 6.3 months for ESCC[22]. In our older cohort (median age 77 years), toripalimab-based CIRT yielded a median PFS of 9.67 months and OS of 13.67 months. These cross-trial comparisons, while informative, are limited by differences in study designs and patient populations. The potential efficacy differences between various immunotherapy combinations remain an open question in the absence of prospective head-to-head trials. Compared internally to CCRT, the CIRT group exhibited inferior survival outcomes in our study. Despite the observed survival difference, it should be emphasized that the adverse event profiles were similar between groups. Moreover, in this retrospective, non-randomized comparison, the survival disparity may partially reflect confounding factors and selection bias. Any grade lymphopenia was observed in 95.5% of CIRT patients, and the incidence of ≥ grade 3 lymphopenia was 72.7%. Lymphopenia is a common adverse event following thoracic RT and serves as a significant prognostic marker for patients with cancer, such as lung cancer and breast cancer, undergoing this treatment [23,24]. Lymphopenia can affect either all or just specific subsets of the T or B lymphocyte [25]. The T and B lymphocytes play a central role in engaging the immune system to combat cancer, and their abundances and activities are important bases for responding to cancer immunotherapies [26,27]. A more plausible explanation for the survival difference is that lymphopenia had a greater detrimental effect in the CIRT group. Additionally, despite comparable ALC between groups, the two therapies may have induced distinct changes in lymphocyte subsets, which could differentially affect clinical outcomes. Furthermore, a persistent decline in the absolute lymphocyte count was observed throughout RT. This suggests that the RT period itself may represent a suboptimal window for administering immunotherapy. The absolute lymphocyte count began to recover 2 to 4 weeks after the completion of RT. Therefore, initiating immunotherapy following this recovery period—rather than concurrently with RT—may thus represent a more optimal treatment strategy. This hypothesis finds indirect support in the divergent survival outcomes of the PACIFIC trials: consolidative durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy was beneficial, whereas its concurrent use was not [28,29]. Currently, there is no clear recommendation regarding the optimal timing for integrating immunotherapy with definitive RT in ESCC, a question that awaits validation in large prospective trials. Alternatively, adopting involved-field irradiation with reduced radiation volumes to attenuate the decrease in radiotherapy-induced absolute lymphocyte count may help minimize radiation-induced lymphopenia, thereby preserving immune function for subsequent immunotherapy. Given the focus on an older population, it is crucial to acknowledge that comorbidities and other unmeasured factors may confound outcomes, and that the non-randomized design cannot be overlooked.

Moreover, within the constraints of our non-randomized study with limited sample size, additional exploratory analyses were performed. These suggested that during1-ALC and pre-SIRI might be candidate prognostic factors for OS, while “pre-PIV”, “during1-PLR”, and “during1-NLR” could be potential risk factors. Supportive trends were noted in Kaplan-Meier analyses. Critically, all these biomarker findings should be viewed as preliminary and must be interpreted cautiously. They were based on median-derived cut-offs in a retrospective setting and are prone to confounding. Their primary value was to identify candidates for definitive evaluation in appropriately powered, prospective studies. Thoracic RT could expose substantial circulating blood volume, resulting in more severe lymphopenia compared to brain, extremity, or abdominal irradiation [30,31]. Dynamic ALC changes showed a temporary reduction in lymphocytes during treatment, followed by recovery after RT. Numerous studies have demonstrated that lymphopenia adversely affected survival outcomes in solid tumors [32,33]. These external observations could offer a partial explanation for our findings. NLR and PLR are inflammatory markers reported relevant to survival outcomes of esophageal cancer in numerous clinical studies [34,35]. Neutrophils, as the component of NLR, could drive oncogenesis and metastatic progression through tumor microenvironment remodeling [36]. Platelets could aid tumor cells in evading immune surveillance and facilitating metastasis [37]. Thenceforth, platelets enable tumor cells to adhere to blood vessels and therewith promote extravasation to form metastatic foci. Platelets can also promote angiogenesis to enhance tumor vascularization and metastasis. PIV, which integrates neutrophils, platelets, monocytes, and lymphocytes, represents the systemic immune and inflammatory status to some extent. Similarly, SIRI has components of neutrophils, monocytes, and lymphocytes. Monocytes can modulate the tumor microenvironment through mechanisms that promote immune tolerance, angiogenesis, and metastatic dissemination, while enhancing antitumor effectors and promoting efficient antigen presentation [38]. By upregulating PD-L1, monocytes can suppress T-cell cytotoxicity via the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, as demonstrated in several studies [39,40]. In our study, an increase in monocyte count was observed after immunotherapy, which may contribute to the reduced efficacy of this treatment. Several clinical studies suggested that high PIV was associated with inferior survival outcomes of patients receiving neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy, immunotherapy, or radical resection [[41], [42], [43]]. We found that high pre-PIV and high during1-PIV was associated with shorter OS in exploratory analyses. Although LMR was reported as a predictive factor of prognosis for solid tumors [44,45], this IB was excluded from the univariate Cox model in our study. This may be attributed to the heterogeneity of the older demographic enrolled and the therapy they received. Moreover, significant IBs found in this study were classified as pre-IB and during1-IB. Neither during2-IB nor post-IB was significant in Cox regression analysis. This phenomenon may result from temporary treatment-induced interference, wherein immune and inflammatory status showed a recovery trend but failed to fully regain pre-treatment baseline levels.

This study has several limitations. The exploratory status of CIRT for ESCC (especially in those ≥ 70 years), combined with our study's retrospective, single-center design, small sample size, and absence of detailed comorbidity data, may introduce selection bias and constrain the generalizability of the results. Owing to concerns about model stability with our limited sample size and the fundamental inability of propensity score methods to address unmeasured confounders in retrospective data, they were not employed. Although no statistically significant differences in clinical characteristics were observed between the two treatment groups, confounding remains a primary limitation of this non-randomized comparison. Consequently, future prospective, blinded studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to definitively compare survival outcomes and to enable meaningful subgroup analyses. External validation through multicenter collaborations will be essential. Moreover, the interpretation of immune patterns, particularly lymphopenia, is constrained by potential differences in radiotherapy parameters between cohorts, which may have independently affected cell counts and confounded the regimen comparison. Our findings in this regard should therefore be interpreted with caution. Future research must employ more granular analyses of immune cell subpopulations to clarify their distinct roles, coupled with mechanistic studies to define the specific pathways through which immune-inflammatory markers influence survival after CIRT or CCRT.

Conclusions

This retrospective analysis of older ESCC patients demonstrated that while toripalimab-based CIRT was tolerable, it was associated with poorer survival outcomes than S-1–based CCRT—an observation potentially influenced by residual confounding. The observed association between lymphocyte/monocyte dynamics and response provides a framework for future studies, prompting the critical question of whether mitigating lymphopenia can specifically enhance the efficacy of CIRT.

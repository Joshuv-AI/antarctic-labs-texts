Reconstructing SLE-decorated Liouville quantum gravity surfaces from random permutons
Jacopo Borga MIT Ewain Gwynne University of Chicago

Abstract
Permutons constructed from a Liouville quantum gravity surface and a pair of space-filling Schramm-Loewner evolutions (SLEs) have been shown – or are conjectured – to describe the scaling limit of various natural models of random constrained permutations. We prove that, in two distinct and natural settings, these permutons uniquely determine, modulo rotation, scaling, translation and reflection, both the Liouville quantum gravity surface and the pair of space-filling SLEs used in their construction. In other words, the Liouville quantum gravity surface and the pair of space-filling SLEs can be deterministically reconstructed from the permuton. Our results cover the cases of the skew Brownian permutons , the universal limits of pattern-avoiding permutations, and the meandric permuton , which is the conjectural permuton limit of permutations obtained from uniform meanders. In the course of the proof, we give a detailed description of how the support of the permuton relates to the multiple points of the two space-filling SLEs.

We now explain another important consequence of our
Theorem

1.1

[

]

,
GKMW18

,
BHS23
]

This notion of convergence has two important features. First, it allows one to extract properties of the discrete models from the continuum and vice versa, as demonstrated, for instance, in
[
GHS20
]
. Second, it constitutes the first step towards establishing stronger types of convergence
[
GMS21
,
HS23
]

.

Our
Theorem

1.1
admits a similar interpretation. Several models of curve-decorated random planar maps are bijectively encoded by corresponding models of random constrained permutations (e.g., bipolar orientations and Baxter permutations
[

]
, and meanders and meandric permutations
[

]

Thus, permuton convergence for such permutation models can be reinterpreted as a new notion of convergence for the corresponding models of curve-decorated random planar maps towards SLE-decorated Liouville quantum gravity spheres. Crucially, this new notion of convergence is not restricted to the case
\gamma^{2}=16/\kappa

\gamma^{2}\neq 16/\kappa

In a similar vein to the mating-of-trees/peanosphere theory, we anticipate that this new notion of convergence will facilitate the transfer of results between the continuum and the discrete, and will serve as a foundational step towards establishing stronger results in the future.

In this subsection we recall some key definitions related to the theory of Liouville quantum gravity (LQG). We refer to

Let

Definition 2.1

A

•

\phi:\widetilde{U}\rightarrow U

\widetilde{h}=h\circ\phi+Q\log|\phi^{\prime}|\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(\widetilde{z}_{j})=z_{j},\quad\forall j=1,\dots k,

All of the LQG surfaces we consider will be random, and the generalized function

•

is a generalized function on
U
(which we will always take to be some variant of the GFF);

•

\eta_{1}:[a_{1},b_{1}]\rightarrow U\cup\partial U
and
\eta_{2}:[a_{2},b_{2}]\rightarrow U\cup\partial U
are curves in
U\cup\partial U
;

•

(U,h,z,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
and
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\eta}_{1},\widetilde{\eta}_{2})
are declared to be equivalent if there is a conformal map
\phi:\widetilde{U}\rightarrow U
such that

\widetilde{h}=h\circ\phi+Q\log|\phi^{\prime}|,\quad\phi(\widetilde{z})=z,\quad\phi\circ\widetilde{\eta}_{1}=\eta_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\circ\widetilde{\eta}_{2}=\eta_{2}

where

2.3

Quite often we will refer to a
\gamma
-LQG surface with a single marked point, decorated by two curves, simply as curve-decorated quantum surface.

Furthermore, a curve-decorated quantum surface
(U,h,\infty,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}

up to complex conjugation
is the same equivalence class above, where the conformal map
\phi:\widetilde{U}\rightarrow U
is replaced by a conformal map composed with a complex conjugation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be the first hitting times of

Proof.

Note that since

(then

must be on the boundary of

, so
\eta
(which is space-filling) will have to hit
z
again before time
\tau_{w}
, since we assume that
\tau_{z}+\varepsilon<\tau_{w}
. This contradicts our assumption that

is a double point of
\eta
.
∎

∎

Proof of
Corollary

1.3
.

Given the support of the permuton
{\bm{\pi}}
, we can recover
(\mu_{h},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
modulo scaling, translation, rotation and reflection via the first three steps described above, which we recall do not depend either on whether Assumption
1
or
2
is adopted, or on the choice of the parameters
(\gamma,\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
in Assumption
1
or
(\gamma,\kappa,\theta)
in Assumption
2
.

In the case of Assumption
1
, the law of the equivalence classes
(\mu_{h},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}
for different vales of
(\gamma,\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
are mutually singular, for instance for the following two reasons:

•

Recall from
Section

2.2
that the boundary of a space-filling SLE
κ
curve stopped at any given time is an SLE
16/κ
-type curve, which has Hausdorff dimension
1+\frac{16/\kappa}{8}
by
[
Bef08
]
. This gives the mutual singularity for different values of
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
.

•

The
\gamma
-LQG measure is supported on the
\gamma
-thick points of
h
, which is a set of Hausdorff dimension
2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
BP24
, Theorem 2.4]
). This gives the mutual singularity for different values of
\gamma
.

In the case of Assumption
2
, the law of the equivalence classes
(\mu_{h},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}
for different vales of
(\gamma,\kappa,\theta)
are mutually singular, for instance for the following reason (note that we only need to argue for
\theta
, since the mutual singularity for different values of
\gamma,\kappa
is already proved above).

Let
\beta^{L}_{1}
and
\beta^{L}_{2}
be the
\pi/2
-angle and
\theta
-angle flow lines of the same whole-plane GFF
\hat{h}
started at a point
Z
sampled uniformly from the LQG-measure
\mu_{h}
. In this way,
\beta^{L}_{1}
and
\beta^{L}_{2}
are the left outer boundaries of
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, respectively, stopped upon hitting
Z
.

If
\theta
is such that
\beta^{L}_{1}
and
\beta^{L}_{2}
intersect somewhere besides at their starting point, then the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection depends on
\theta
by
[
MW17
, Theorem 1.5]
. This gives the mutual singularity in this case.

If
\theta
is such that
\beta^{L}_{1}
and
\beta^{L}_{2}
do not intersect, let
I
be the set of angles
\phi
for which the
\phi
-angle flow lines started from
Z
intersects
\beta^{L}_{1}
but not
\beta^{L}_{2}
, and let
J
be the set of angles
\phi
for which the
\phi
-angle flow line started from
Z
intersects neither
\beta^{L}_{1}
nor
\beta^{L}_{2}
(note that the
\phi

, which is determined by
\eta_{1}
by
[
MS16
, Theorem 1.16]
). Then the pair of sets

depends on on
\theta
(e.g., by the same argument as in
Lemma

4.12
). This gives the mutual singularity in this case.

Finally, for the statement on the skew Brownian permutons

Proof of
Corollary

1.6
.

Suppose that we are in the setting of Theorem
1.1
and that the permuton
{\bm{\pi}}
satisfies the re-rooting invariance
{\bm{\pi}}_{T}\overset{d}{=}{\bm{\pi}}
when
T
is sampled uniformly from
[0,1]
, independently from
{\bm{\pi}}
. We need to show that
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=8
.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, we define the re-rooting of
\eta_{i}
at
z\in\mathbbm{C}
to be the curve
\eta_{i}^{z}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}
obtained by concatenating
\eta_{i}|_{[\tau_{i}^{z},1]}
followed by
\eta_{i}|_{[0,\tau_{i}^{z}]}
where
\tau_{i}^{z}
is the first hitting time of
z
. That is,

\eta_{i}^{z}(t):=\begin{cases}\eta_{i}(t+\tau_{i}^{z}),\quad&\text{if $t\in[0,1-\tau_{i}^{z}]$},\\
\eta_{i}(t-(1-\tau_{i}^{z})),\quad&\text{if $t\in[1-\tau_{i}^{z},1]$}.\end{cases}

It is shown in
[
BGS22
, Proposition 5.2]
that if
\kappa_{i}=8
, then for any
z\in\mathbbm{C}
and any fractional linear transformation
\phi_{z}:\mathbbm{C}\cup\{\infty\}\to\mathbbm{C}\cup\{\infty\}
taking
z
to
\infty
, we have
\phi_{z}\circ\eta_{i}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{i}
modulo time parametrization; and that this property is not true for any
z\in\mathbbm{C}
if
\kappa_{i}\not=8
. The proof will follow easily from this fact together with Theorem
1.1
.

Let us now give the details. To avoid complications with choices of conformal maps, it is convenient to consider a triply marked quantum sphere
(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,\infty)
(recall Definition
2.2
). Note that the single marked quantum sphere
(\mathbbm{C},h,\infty)
used to construct
{\bm{\pi}}
can be recovered from the triply marked quantum sphere
(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,\infty)
by forgetting two of the three marked points.

Conditional on
(h,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
, let
Z\in\mathbbm{C}
be sampled from the probability measure
\mu_{h}
. Consider the curve-decorated quantum surface
(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,Z,\eta_{1}^{Z},\eta_{2}^{Z})
obtained from
(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,\infty,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
by re-rooting at
Z
. Let
\widetilde{\bm{\pi}}
be the permuton associated with
(\eta_{1}^{Z},\eta_{2}^{Z})
as in (
1.2
).

Since
\eta_{1}
is parametrized by
\mu_{h}
-mass, the time
\tau_{1}^{Z}
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
and is independent from
(h,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
, and hence also from
{\bm{\pi}}
.
By definition of
{\bm{\pi}}_{t}
(recall (
1.6
)) and Assertion 1 in
Lemma

4.1
we have that, almost surely, for each rectangle
[a,b]\times[c,d]\subset[0,1]^{2}
,

{\bm{\pi}}_{\tau_{1}^{Z}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left([a,b]\times[c,d]}}\right)=\mu_{h}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\eta_{1}^{Z}([a,b])\cap\eta_{2}^{Z}([c,d])}}\right),

where we used that
\psi(\tau_{Z}^{i})=\tau_{Z}^{2}
is the a.s. unique time at which
\eta_{2}
hits
Z
(
[
BGS22
, Lemma 2.10]
). By construction of
\widetilde{\bm{\pi}}
, this implies that almost surely
\widetilde{\bm{\pi}}={\bm{\pi}}_{\tau_{1}^{Z}}
.
Since
\tau_{1}^{Z}
is independent from
{\bm{\pi}}
, our re-rooting invariance assumption implies that

\widetilde{\bm{\pi}}\overset{d}{=}{\bm{\pi}}.

Now, by
Theorem

1.1
and

1.4
, we have that

(\mathbbm{C},h,\infty,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}=F(\operatorname{supp}{\bm{\pi}})\quad\text{ and }\quad(\mathbbm{C},h,Z,\eta_{1}^{Z},\eta_{2}^{Z})_{\mathrm{cc}}=F(\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{\bm{\pi}}).

Note that we can use the same function

for both permutons thanks to the description of

at the beginning of

. Hence
(\mathbbm{C},h,\infty,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}\overset{d}{=}(\mathbbm{C},h,Z,\eta_{1}^{Z},\eta_{2}^{Z})
, and so also

(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,\infty,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{\mathrm{cc}}\overset{d}{=}(\mathbbm{C},h,0,1,Z,\eta_{1}^{Z},\eta_{2}^{Z})_{\mathrm{cc}}.

To convert this to a statement about
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, let
\phi_{Z}(w)=w(1-Z)/(w-Z)
be the conformal which takes
Z
to
\infty
and fixes 0 and 1. Let
\xi,\widetilde{\xi}
be random functions which are each equal to the identity with probability
1/2
, and equal to
w\mapsto\overline{w}
with probability
1/2
, chosen independently from each other and everything else. The previous paragraph implies that for each
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\widetilde{\xi}\circ\phi_{Z}\circ\eta_{i}^{Z}\overset{d}{=}\xi\circ\eta_{i}.

Since the law of SLE
κ
is invariant under complex conjugation, this implies that
\phi_{Z}\circ\eta_{i}^{Z}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{i}
. Since
Z
is sampled independently from
\eta_{i}
, viewed modulo time parametrization, we get from
[
BGS22
, Proposition 5.2]
that
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=8
.
∎

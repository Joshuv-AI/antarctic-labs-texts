The Pros and Cons of Dental Laser Therapy in Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics—A Systematic Review
Authors: Priyanka Sarangi, Shradha Suman, Sukanta Kumar Satapathy, Rinki Meher, Abhisek Das, Divya M Pradeep
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe objective of this systematic review was to investigate the clinical evidence on the utilization of lasers in endodontics and evaluate the advantages and disadvantages associated with its use. The search engines, such as PubMed, Cochrane, and Scopus, were utilized to locate human clinical trials pertaining to the application of lasers in endodontic therapy. By utilizing keywords, additional filters, and inclusion and exclusion criteria, the initial pool of 387 articles was narrowed down to 12. Laser reduces endodontic postoperative discomfort and improves patient satisfaction, according to research.

INTRODUCTION

Lasers with various wavelengths and technologies have been used in dentistry for decades to debride, ablate, manage inflammation, and expedite healing.[1] Laser therapy has improved significantly in maxillofacial, oral implant, periodontics, and endodontics. Its use in periodontics began in the 1980s.[2] It is now a standard periodontal surgical device. Even though various research works have examined laser treatment in endodontics, a full investigation is needed. This systematic review aimed to analyze research on lasers as an auxiliary technique in nonsurgical endodontic treatment.

METHODOLOGY

Subsequently, suitable databases and sources of literature were determined. The primary databases used for this review were PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMBASE, Scopus, and Web of Science. In addition, a search was conducted in specialized databases. An exhaustive search strategy was established by selecting appropriate keywords and MeSH terms.

DISCUSSION

Photodynamic therapy (PDT) and photobiomodulation (PBM) use laser photonic energy nonphotothermally. PDT is employed with photosensitizers at 450–810 nm. Chiniforush et al.[15] recommended using PDT with traditional chemo-mechanical methods to reduce bacteria and change their pathogenicity, minimizing biofilm formation.

It was also clear that most research, whether conventional, PDT, or PBM, focused on laser pain control in endodontic therapy. However, pain intensity studies were heterogeneous due to pain perception’s subjectivity.[16]

de Miranda et al.[10] found a significant difference in the periapical index score (PAI) after six months using pain, bacterial count, and radiographic healing criteria in their PDT research. Additionally, laser photonic energy scattered and diffused beyond the target tissue’s strict limitations, photobiomodulating the area.[17]

In endodontics, low-level laser therapy (LLLT) uses lasers from 810 to 2940 nm. A single-visit RCT found that this therapy reduced postoperative pain.[8] LLLT after endodontic surgery yielded similar results.[18]

Laser-activated irrigation (LAI) with saline as a NaOCl replacement failed in vitro.[19] Kreisler et al.[20] showed that an 809-nm diode laser alone did not perform better than with NaOCl. They concluded that the diode laser has potential but should not replace traditional treatment. This should be explored as a possible supplemental treatment.

CONCLUSION

Laser treatment may reduce postoperative pain and improve endodontic patient satisfaction, according to research. The decrease in discomfort shows that laser reduces inflammation and germs, which are essential to endodontic therapy. Laser is a complement, not a standalone treatment.

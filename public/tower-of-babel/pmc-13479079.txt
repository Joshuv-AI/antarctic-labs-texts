Clinical Performance of an Isofocal Toric Intraocular Lens with Double C-Loop Haptics: A Multicenter Retrospective Study
Authors: Sheraz Daya, Takayuki Akahoshi, Camille Bosc, Alessandra Di Maria, Marcela Espinosa Lagana, Noemi Sasso, Alessandro Gaeta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate clinical outcomes in eyes with pre-existing astigmatism undergoing cataract surgery or refractive lens exchange with the implantation of an isofocal optic-design toric intraocular lens (IOL) with double C-loop haptics.MethodThis multicenter retrospective study comprised 100 eyes (65 patients) implanted with the Isopure Serenity toric IOL (BVI, Inc.) conducted across four sites. Postoperative evaluation at between 2 and 3 months included manifest refraction, monocular uncorrected and distance corrected visual acuity (UDVA and CDVA), monocular uncorrected and distance corrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA and DCIVA, at 80 and 66 cm), monocular uncorrected and distance corrected near visual acuity (UNVA and DCNVA, at 40 cm), binocular defocus curve and rotational stability.ResultsManifest refraction spherical equivalent and refractive astigmatism were −0.24±0.41D and −0.36±0.36D, respectively. About 79% and 97% of eyes were within ±0.50D and ±1.00D, respectively, and 80% and 96% of eyes were ≤0.50D and ≤1.00D of refractive cylinder, respectively. At distance vision, the mean monocular CDVA was −0.02±0.06 logMAR and 92.93% of eyes showed a CDVA of ≥20/20. At intermediate vision, the mean monocular DCIVA values were 0.10±0.07 and 0.17±0.10 logMAR at 80 cm and 66 cm, respectively; and 96.92% and 88.33% of eyes showed a DCIVA of ≥20/32 at 80 cm and 66 cm, respectively. At near vision, the mean monocular DCNVA was 0.31±0.08 logMAR and 75.44% of eyes showed a DCNVA of ≥20/40. The binocular depth of focus range was approximately 1.50D. The mean absolute IOL rotation was 2.73±2.58 degrees with all eyes having a rotation of <10 degrees.ConclusionsEyes with pre-existing astigmatism implanted with the Isopure Serenity toric IOL showed good distance visual acuity and functional intermediate vision, with high refractive accuracy and good rotational stability following cataract and refractive lens exchange surgery.

Introduction

Cataracts are the leading cause of visual impairment and blindness worldwide (among adults aged 50 years or older), accounting for an estimated 15 million cases of blindness and an additional 79 million cases of moderate to severe visual impairment globally in 2020.1 In addition to improving quality of vision, cataract surgery can offer our patients the possibility of reducing eyeglasses dependence. The use of high-performance intraocular lenses (IOLs) provides good visual acuity at different distances, and the use of different technologies and surgical strategies may be considered by surgeons for presbyopia correction.2 Trifocal IOLs improve uncorrected near visual acuity when compared to extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) IOLs but no difference has been reported for distance and intermediate visual acuity.3 Mechanisms of action of EDOF IOLs differ, many creating a single elongated focal point, rather than several foci, in order to enhance the depth-of-focus, aiming to reduce photic phenomena, glare and halos.4 It has also been shown that EDOF and enhanced monofocal IOLs have improved visual quality at intermediate distances. Lens selection ideally should be based on the lens characteristics and patient’s needs.5

The recently introduced Isopure Serenity IOL (BVI, Inc., Waltham, USA) is an aspheric refractive optic design, based on an isofocal concept,6 which aims to extend the depth-of-focus compared to monofocal IOLs. Two recent studies analyzing the outcomes of this lens have reported good outcomes in terms of refractive accuracy and excellent visual performance for distance vision and functional intermediate vision.7,8 Uncorrected refractive astigmatism degrades visual acuity and toric IOLs corrects astigmatism contributing to spectacle-free vision.9 Pre-existing astigmatism of ≥1D has been shown to present in up to 47% of cataract eyes,10 and in patients with corneal astigmatism, toric IOLs provide more precise astigmatic correction and better uncorrected distance vision.11 The Isopure Serenity toric IOL is based on its spherical counterpart, incorporating a toric design in order to correct astigmatism and provide patients with a consistent range of vision. The purpose of the current multicenter study was to assess refractive accuracy and vision at different distances in eyes with pre-existing astigmatism implanted with the Isopure Serenity toric IOL following cataract or refractive lens exchange surgery.

Methods

This was a multicenter retrospective open-label clinical study conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by each of the different local Review Boards (IRB) of the four centers involved in the study: Centre for Sight (United Kingdom, CFS-IST/2025-1), Nihonbashi Cataract-Clinic (Japan, NCC/IST/2025/1), Clinique Jules Verne (France, CJV_IST_2026_1), and IRCCS Humanitas Research Hospital (Italy; IRCSSHRH-IST-2025-1). Patients signed informed consent to participate in the study.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria considered patients aged 50 years or older on the treatment day that were bilaterally implanted with Isopure Serenity IOLs, with at least one eye being implanted with the toric model and available for a 2- to 3-month follow-up visit. Exclusion criteria included the presence of ocular comorbidity that could compromise postoperative visual outcomes, patients for whom in-the-bag implantation was not possible, and patients who experienced surgical complications such as posterior capsule rupture.

Isopure Serenity Toric Intraocular Lens and Surgery

All the eyes analyzed in this study were implanted with the posterior chamber hydrophobic acrylic Isopure Serenity toric IOL (PODST49P, Figure 1). This IOL is manufactured with GFY hydrophobic acrylic material with an overall diameter of 11.10 and optic diameter of 6.00 mm. The haptic design is double C-loop (POD) with a square edge (RidgeTech) and posteriorly angulated, incorporating UV and blue light filters (refractive index of 1.53 and an Abbe number of 42). The spherical power ranges from +10.00D to +30.00D in 0.50D steps, and from +31.00D to +35.00D in 1.00D steps. Specifically, for this toric model, the cylinder power at the IOL plane (at the corneal plane) is available for 1.00 (0.68), 1.50 (1.03), 2.25 (1.55), 3.00 (2.06), 3.75 (2.57), 4.50 (3.08), 5.25 (3.60) and 6.00D (4.11). Optical biometry was performed using the IOL Master (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany), the Anterion (Heidelberg Engineering GmbH, Heidelberg, Germany) or the TOMEY OA-200 (Tomey Corporation, Nagoya, Japan) devices, and IOL power was calculated using the Barrett, Holladay 2 or BVI calculator. Postoperative Measurements and Analysis

All patients underwent a complete ophthalmological examination both before and after the surgery. At the 2- to 3-month follow-up visit, the following metrics were measured: subjective refraction (sphere, cylinder, and axis); monocular uncorrected and distance corrected visual acuity (UDVA and CDVA); uncorrected and distance corrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA and DCIVA) both at 80 cm and 66 cm; uncorrected and distance-corrected near acuity (UNVA and DCNVA) at 40 cm; and binocular photopic distance-corrected defocus curves (from +1.00D to −4.00D in 0.50D steps). All patients were implanted with at least one Isopure Serenity toric IOL, and, in the case of no bilateral implantation with this IOL, the spherical model was used in one eye. Therefore, binocular defocus curves considered patients implanted with both toric and non-toric models. Adverse events were monitored and registered. The rotational stability of the lenses after implantation was evaluated by obtaining the mean absolute rotation value, comparing the IOL orientation at baseline (from the surgical position or at the end of surgery) with its orientation at the postoperative follow-up visit using a slit-lamp 6–12 weeks. The information recorded from all the eyes was registered into an Excel spreadsheet (Microsoft Corporation, Redmont, USA) to provide the mean, standard deviation, and ranges for all the continuous measurements studied (age, visual acuity, refraction, biometry parameters, IOL powers and IOL rotation). Standard graphs showing refractive and visual acuity outcomes post-surgery were generated.12 Vector analysis was performed using the double-angle plot tool,13 considering preoperative corneal astigmatism and the postoperative refractive astigmatism data.

Seventy‑nine percent of eyes were within ±0.50D, and 97% of eyes were within ±1.00D of the target spherical equivalent, and 80% and 96% of eyes had a refractive cylinder of ≤0.50D and ≤1.00D, respectively. Monocular visual acuity outcomes are summarized in Table 2 (it should be noted that some values were not recorded for the whole sample). At intermediate distances, monocular DCIVA was ≥20/25 in 83.08% of eyes and ≥20/32 in 96.92% at 80 cm and 35% and 88.33% at 66 cm, respectively (Figure 4B). Defocus Curve and IOL Rotation

Figure 5 plots the binocular distance-corrected defocus curve, from +1.00D to −3.50D in the whole cohort, showing a peak of best visual acuity at 0.00D of defocus corresponding to distance vision (−0.03±0.08 logMAR). Visual acuity reduces with higher defocus values, representing closer distances. The binocular defocus curve was measured in 47 of the 65 patients and, therefore, the mean defocus curve includes patients with eyes with toric and non-toric models. The depth-of-focus, defined as the range of lens powers from 0D to the maximum negative defocus with visual acuity of ≥20/32 (0.2 logMAR)14 in our cohort was approximately 1.50D. This figure also shows binocular values from the multicenter study by Daya et al8 for comparative purposes. In relation to the stability of the lens when implanted, the mean absolute IOL rotation at the final postoperative visit was 2.73±2.58 degrees, ranging from 0 to 9 degrees. Discussion

With the increasing demand for intermediate visual tasks, high-performance IOLs are widely used among cataract and refractive surgeons in order to increase the range of vision and reduce patients’ spectacle dependence. The benefits associated with the use of toric IOLs during cataract surgery have been reviewed, and there is high-quality evidence that UDVA is better when toric IOLs are used providing greater spectacle independence, and lower amounts of residual refractive astigmatism than non-toric IOLs.15 It is recommended that patients with regular corneal astigmatism receive a toric IOL if they are to undergo cataract surgery and seek to be spectacle independent for distance viewing.15 Two previous studies analyzing the Isopure Serenity IOL7,8 reported good visual acuity at far distance with functional intermediate vision and accurate refractive outcomes. This IOL is considered an effective option for extending the range of focus following cataract surgery.7 The purpose of the current multicenter study was to assess the refractive accuracy and visual outcomes at different distances in eyes with pre-existing astigmatism implanted with the Isopure Serenity toric IOL after cataract or refractive lens exchange surgeries. This analysis is restricted to eyes implanted with the Isopure Serenity toric lenses.

The refractive outcomes in this study demonstrate high accuracy obtaining a mean spherical equivalent of −0.24±0.41D, a mean refractive cylinder of −0.36±0.36D, and a higher percentage of eyes with spherical equivalent ±1.00D (97%) and refractive cylinder ≤1.00D (96%). Specifically, the reduction of refractive astigmatism is demonstrated by the scatter plot in Figure 3, with a postoperative centroid mean of 0.06±0.51D. Comparative data illustrated in Table 3 summarizes refractive and visual acuity outcomes reported in the two previous studies on the Isopure Serenity IOL. In terms of refractive accuracy, the mean spherical equivalent and cylinder values for Akahoshi7 and Daya et al8 were close to emmetropia and about a quarter of a diopter, respectively, with almost all eyes within ±1.00D and ≤1.00D. Our results align with these studies, showing the high accuracy of the procedure. It should be noted that the refractive accuracy is highly important in relation to the outcomes found using high-performance IOLs. Cumulative visual plots (Figure 3) show the percentages of eyes with a given value of visual acuity at distance, intermediate, and near vision. The mean values for the different distances are shown in Table 2. These findings support earlier outcomes found by Akahoshi7 and Daya et al8 in their respective studies. For example, as shown in Table 3, the mean values for visual acuities at different distances (far, intermediate, and near vision) are similar across the three studies. The cumulative percentages of eyes with corrected distance visual acuity of ≥20/20, ≥20/32 and ≥20/40 at far, intermediate and near vision, respectively, across the three groups show slight differences, which could be related to the different cohorts assessed. Analysis of the defocus curve enables visual acuity at different distances to be evaluated. The Isopure Serenity IOL performs well across a wide range of distances (Figure 5). Distance visual acuity was excellent (−0.03±0.08 logMAR) (0.00D of defocus). This gradually decreases for intermediate and near distances. As indicated in the results section, the American Academy of Ophthalmology Task Force for EDOF IOLs defines depth-of-focus as the range of lens powers from 0.00D to the maximum negative defocus with visual acuity ≥20/32.15 In our cohort, this value was approximately 1.50D. This range is of practical benefit for patients, who should be able to comfortably carry out visual tasks such as viewing a car dashboard or computer monitor without the need for spectacles. In this figure, the defocus curve obtained by Daya et al8 in their multicenter study has been included, showing similar outcomes. Therefore, the performance of the Isopure Serenity IOL can be considered to provide good visual acuity at distance with functional intermediate vision.

Rotational stability is important in current cataract and refractive lens exchange surgeries. Developments in material, and, specifically, in haptic designs, have significantly improved the stability of these lenses, reducing rotation and improving refractive outcomes. Rotation of the IOL reduces the astigmatic correcting effect of toric lenses and is eliminated by a rotation of 30 degrees.17 Our findings support the good rotational stability of the Isopure Serenity toric IOL when implanted, with a mean absolute rotation of 2.73±2.58 degrees and all eyes exhibiting a rotation of ≤10 degrees. A recent systematic review and single-arm meta-analysis study carried out considering 4863 eyes in 51 studies found that the pooled mean absolute rotation for different toric IOLs available on the market was 2.36 degrees.18 Our value broadly agrees with this value. It is important to note that the ISO11979-7:2024 indicates that the absolute rotation value in toric IOL must be less than 10 degrees in 90% of the eyes implanted and less than 20 degrees in 95% of the eyes implanted.19 The outcomes reported here comply with this standard. As previously mentioned, the POD platform has a double C-loop with a symmetrical quadripod design with two-by-two oppositely oriented haptics, allowing four contact points in order to fix the IOL in the capsular bag. A recent review has summarized the rotational stability outcomes reported in different clinical studies using this platform.20 This study, considering monofocal and trifocal optical design IOLs made of both hydrophobic or hydrophilic material, based on 19 publications (1428 eyes), found that the mean rotation was 2.61 degrees and concluded that this platform provides good rotational stability when used with different IOL models (optical design and material). The Isopure Serenity toric IOL also uses the POD platform and similar outcomes are expected. Specifically, the monofocal PODEYE toric IOL (BVI, Inc., Waltham, USA), which uses the same haptic design and material as the Isopure Serenity toric IOL, has shown excellent rotational stability values at 4–6 months (1.22±2.21 degrees with 97.87% of eyes with <10 degrees)21 and up to 12 months (2.52±2.59 degrees with 98.56% of eyes with <10 degrees).22 Our findings are consistent with these studies demonstrating good lens stability of this platform when implanted in the capsular bag. In relation to the timing of toric IOLs, it has been reported that the capsular bag gradually decreases after cataract surgery, suggesting that IOL stability increases as the capsular bag contracts.23 One study found that the rotation changes between 1 and 3 months after surgery were significantly smaller than at other time points.24 The rotation of the toric IOL may decrease when the anterior and posterior capsules fuse, which typically begins at approximately 2 weeks, and minimal IOL rotation is observed after 1 month.25 Therefore, the examinations in our cohort, at between 2 and 3 months post-surgery, were conducted in an adequate timeframe to assess its stability. It has also been suggested that the axial length of the eye is a principal factor contributing to early postoperative rotation, with correlation between longer axial values with wider capsule width. Furthermore, large capsular bags may reduce friction with the haptic and, therefore, decrease IOL stability.26–30 However, He et al31 examined rotational stability in patients with an axial length >25 mm and did not find a significant correlation between axial length and rotation. This is in agreement with a recent study which assessed the rotational stability of the PODEYE toric IOL in normal and axial lengths and concluded that this model shows excellent rotational stability without significant differences between eyes with normal (≤23.9 mm; mean 2.93±2.48 degrees) and high (≥24 mm; mean 2.17±1.73 degrees) axial lengths at 11–13 months post-surgery (P>0.05).32 There is no evidence that axial length plays a role in rotational stability with this IOL model. Based on the outcomes previously reported with the POD platform and current findings using the Isofocal optic design, this haptic platform is its rotationally stable in spite of capsular bag size and axial length.

This study has limitations including being non-comparative. A comparative analysis was made with other lenses using published literature. The short follow-up period of 3 months is a limitation, however it provides fairly reliable information on lens performance. Undoubtedly a longer follow-up is always desirable to ensure longer term stability. Patient-reported outcomes questionnaires (PROMS) would add information about patient satisfaction and spectacle independence. Future prospective studies should ideally be carried out to assess the stability and performance of the lens.

Conclusions

This multicenter study demonstrates that Isopure Serenity toric IOL implantation provides good distance visual acuity with functional intermediate vision. The Isopure Serenity toric IOL has high refractive accuracy and good rotational stability. Future clinical studies with larger samples and longer follow-ups should be conducted to support these preliminary findings. Current outcomes support the use of the Isopure Serenity toric IOL in eyes with pre-existing astigmatism following cataract and refractive lens exchange surgery.

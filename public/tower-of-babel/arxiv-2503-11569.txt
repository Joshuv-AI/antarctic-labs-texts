Improvement of Sinc-collocation methods for Volterra-Fredholm integral equations of the second kind and their theoretical analysis Note: This study was partially supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C) JP23K03218.
Tomoaki Okayama

Abstract
Sinc-collocation methods for Volterra-Fredholm integral equations of the second kind were proposed independently by multiple authors: by Shamloo et al. in 2012 and by Mesgarani and Mollapourasl in 2013. Their theoretical analyses and numerical experiments suggest that the presented methods can attain root-exponential convergence. However, their convergence has not been strictly proved. This study improves these methods to facilitate implementation, and provides a convergence theorem for the improved method. For the same equations, another Sinc-collocation method was proposed in 2016 by John and Ogbonna, which is regarded as an improvement to the variable transformation employed by Shamloo et al. It may attain a higher rate than the previous methods, but its convergence has not yet been proved. Therefore, this study improves it to facilitate implementation, and provides a convergence theorem for the improved method.

Introduction and summary

This study focuses on Sinc-collocation methods
for Volterra-Fredholm integral equations of the second kind of the form

u(t)-\int_{a}^{t}k_{1}(t,s)u(s)\mathrm{d}s-\int_{a}^{b}k_{2}(t,s)u(s)\mathrm{d}s=g(t),\quad a\leq t\leq b,

(1.1)

where
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
, and
g
are given continuous functions,
and
u
is the solution to be determined.
This type of equations arises in application of various
fields
[
6
]
, and for this reason,
several numerical methods for the equations have been
developed
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
21
]
.
Most numerical methods in the literature
assume that the given functions are continuously differentiable
on the whole interval
[a,b]
,
and consequently such methods exhibit reduced efficiency
when any one of given functions has derivative singularity at the endpoints.
To address this problem,
Shamloo et al.
[
18
]
proposed a Sinc-collocation method,
which does not require differentiability at the endpoints.
Their method was derived by a typical collocation procedure
based on the Sinc approximation

F(x)\approx\sum_{j=-N}^{N}F(jh)S(j,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2)

where
h
is a mesh size selected appropriately depending on
a positive integer
N
, and

S(j,h)
is the so-called Sinc function defined by

S(j,h)(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin[\piup(x-jh)/h]}{\piup(x-jh)/h}&(x\neq jh),\\
1&(x=jh).\end{cases}

The Sinc approximation is known as a highly efficient
formula
[
19
]
,
but two issues need to be addressed.
The first issue is that the given interval
(a,b)
in (
1.1
)
is finite, whereas the variable
x
in (
1.2
)
should be able to move over the entire real axis
\mathbb{R}
.
A standard way to resolve this issue is
to employ the tanh transformation

t=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(x)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\frac{b+a}{2},

which maps
\mathbb{R}
onto
(a,b)
.
In this paper, this is referred to as
the single-exponential (SE) transformation.
Combining the Sinc approximation with the SE transformation,
we obtain the following formula

f(t)\approx\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.3)

The second issue is that the right-hand side of (
1.3
)
tends to
0
as
t\to a
or
t\to b
.
This implies that the right-hand side cannot give an accurate approximation
near the endpoints if
f(a)\neq 0
or
f(b)\neq 0
.
A standard way to resolve this issue is to introduce auxiliary basis functions

\omega_{a}(t)=\frac{b-t}{b-a},\quad\omega_{b}(t)=\frac{t-a}{b-a}.

Based on the two standard ways,
Shamloo et al.
[
18
]
set their approximate solution
u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
as

u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)=c_{-N}\omega_{a}(t)+\sum_{j=-N+1}^{N-1}c_{j}S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))+c_{N}\omega_{b}(t),

(1.4)

and applied a usual collocation procedure, setting
collocation points at
t=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih)
(
i=-N,\,\ldots,\,N
)
to obtain the unknown coefficients
c_{-N},\,\ldots,\,c_{N}
.
They conducted an error analysis of the method and showed that
there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}|u(t)-u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)|\leq C\mu_{N}\sqrt{N}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}},

(1.5)

where
d
and
\alpha
are positive constants,
and
\mu_{N}
denotes the 2-norm of the inverse of the coefficient matrix of
the resulting system.
The error estimate and their numerical examples
suggest that their method may attain root-exponential convergence.

However, three points remain to be discussed concerning their results.
The first point is that
u(a)=u(b)=0
is assumed to derive the
error estimate, which is not generally satisfied.
The second point is that even if
u(a)=u(b)=0
is satisfied,
its convergence is not rigorously proved
because
\mu_{N}
is not estimated with respect to
N
.
The third point is that the implementation of their method is rather
complicated. This is mainly because

u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))=c_{i}
does not hold.
This defect was taken over in extension
to nonlinear equations
[
22
]
.

Another Sinc-collocation method was proposed by
Mesgarani and Mollapourasl
[
5
]
. Their approximate
solution
\tilde{u}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
is simply set
based on the formula (
1.3
) as

\tilde{u}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}c_{j}S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t)).

Their resulting system of linear equations is simple because

\tilde{u}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))=c_{i}

does
hold.
However, as pointed out for (
1.3
),
their solution generally produces poor results near the endpoints
except when
u(a)=u(b)=0
.
Therefore, although they conducted an error analysis on their method,
their result is difficult to justify.

To address the aforementioned challenges,
this study proposes an easy-to-implement Sinc-collocation method,
where the approximate solution
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
is set as

v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)=c_{-N}\omega_{a}(t)+\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left\{c_{j}-c_{-N}\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))-c_{N}\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\right\}S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))+c_{N}\omega_{b}(t),

(1.6)

which satisfies
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))=c_{i}
.
This concept has already been utilized for several
equations
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
19
]
,
and may work well for the equation (
1.1
).
In fact, this study theoretically shows that
the error of
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
is bounded as

\max_{t\in[a,b]}|u(t)-v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)|\leq C\sqrt{N}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}},

which rigorously proves root-exponential convergence of
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
.

Furthermore, this study extends this result to
the Sinc-collocation method proposed by
John and Ogbonna
[
4
]
.
Their method can be approximately derived
from the Sinc-collocation method of Shamloo et al.
[
18
]

by replacing the tanh transformation with the
so-called double-exponential (DE) transformation

t=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(x)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{\piup}{2}\sinh x\right)+\frac{b+a}{2}.

During recent decades, researchers have indicated that
such replacement generally enhances the convergence rate
of the numerical methods based on the Sinc
approximation
[
7
,
20
]
,
which may have motivated the authors to employ the DE transformation
in the present case.
However, their variable transformation is not exactly
the original DE transformation,
and an inconsistency exists in the collocation points
(the details are discussed later).
By appropriately modifying their method,
this study derives a further improved Sinc-collocation method
attaining almost exponential convergence;
the error of the approximate solution
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}
is bounded as

\max_{t\in[a,b]}|u(t)-v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(t)|\leq C\E^{-\piup dN/\log(2dN/\alpha)},

which is proved theoretically.
The convergence rate is significantly higher than that of
v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
As a preliminary, in Sect.
2
,
we describe basic approximation formulas for
functions, definite integrals, and indefinite integrals,
along with their convergence theorems.
Existing Sinc-collocation methods by Shamloo et al.
[
18
]

and John–Ogbonna
[
4
]
are
described in Sect.
3
.
New Sinc-collocation methods by this study and their
convergence theorems are described in Sect.
4
.
Numerical examples are presented in Sect.
5
.
The proofs of the new theorems are provided in Sect.
6
.

2
Sinc approximation and Sinc indefinite integration

In this section, approximation formulas
for functions, definite integrals, and indefinite integrals, based on
the Sinc approximation (
1.2
) are described.
The associated convergence theorems are also stated.

2.1
Generalized SE-Sinc and DE-Sinc approximations

As explained in Sect.
1
,
a naive combination of the Sinc approximation and
the SE transformation (
1.3
)
does not give an accurate approximation near the endpoints
if
f(a)\neq 0
or
f(b)\neq 0
.
For a general boundary condition,
we introduce the approximation operator
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)

\displaystyle=f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))\omega_{a}(t)+f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))\omega_{b}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left\{f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))-f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))-f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\right\}S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t)).

(2.1)

The approximation
f\approx\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}f
is referred to as
the generalized SE-Sinc approximation in this paper.
Similarly, replacing the SE transformation with the DE transformation,
we introduce the approximation operator
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}[f](t)

\displaystyle=f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(-Nh))\omega_{a}(t)+f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(Nh))\omega_{b}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left\{f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(jh))-f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(-Nh))\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(jh))-f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(Nh))\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(jh))\right\}S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}\}^{-1}(t)).

(2.2)

The approximation
f\approx\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}f
is referred to as
the generalized DE-Sinc approximation in this paper.
The following function spaces are important in stating the
convergence theorems of the two approximations.

Definition 2.1

Let
\mathscr{D}
be a bounded and simply-connected complex domain
(or Riemann surface).
Then,
\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
denotes the family of all functions
f

that are analytic in
\mathscr{D}
and continuous on
\overline{\mathscr{D}}
.
This function space is complete with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})}

defined by
\|f\|_{\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})}=\max_{z\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}}|f(z)|
.

Definition 2.2

Let
\alpha
be a positive constant with
\alpha\leq 1
,
and let
\mathscr{D}
be a simply-connected complex domain
(or Riemann surface) that satisfies
(a,b)\subset\mathscr{D}
.
Then,
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{D})
denotes the family of all functions

f\in\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
for which there exists a constant
C
such that
for all
z\in\mathscr{D}
,

\displaystyle|f(z)-f(a)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|z-a\right|^{\alpha},

\displaystyle|f(b)-f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|b-z\right|^{\alpha}.

Here,
\mathscr{D}
is supposed to be a domain translated by
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
or
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}

from the strip complex domain

\mathscr{D}_{d}=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d\right\}

for a positive constant
d
. To be more precise, we suppose either

\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})=\left\{z=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\right\}

or

\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})=\left\{z=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\right\}.

For functions belonging to
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{D})
,
convergence theorems of the generalized SE/DE-Sinc approximations
were provided as follows.
Here,
\|\cdot\|_{C([a,b])}
denotes the usual uniform norm over
[a,b]
.

Theorem 2.1
(Okayama
[
10
, Theorem 3]
)

Assume that
f\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))
for
d
with
0<d<\piup
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula

h=\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\alpha N}}.

(2.3)

Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\|f-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}f\|_{C([a,b])}\leq C\sqrt{N}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}.

Theorem 2.2
(Okayama
[
10
, Theorem 6]
)

Assume that
f\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))
for
d
with
0<d<\piup/2
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula

h=\frac{\log(2dN/\alpha)}{N}.

(2.4)

Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\|f-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}f\|_{C([a,b])}\leq C\E^{-\piup dN/\log(2dN/\alpha)}.

Remark 2.1

In view of several studies
[
18
,
22
]
,
some researchers may labor under the false assumption that

f
is approximated as

f(t)\approx f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))\omega_{a}(t)+\sum_{j=-N+1}^{N-1}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))S(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))+f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))\omega_{b}(t).

In reality, this is not true.
We should note that
“
f
is approximated by a linear combination of

\omega_{a}
,
S(-N+1,h),\,\ldots,\,S(N-1,h)
,
\omega_{b}
”
does not necessarily imply that
“the coefficients of the linear combination are

f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))
(
j=-N,\,\ldots,\,N
).”

2.2
SE-Sinc quadrature and DE-Sinc quadrature

The SE-Sinc and DE-Sinc quadratures are approximation formulas
for definite integrals,
and their convergence theorems can be stated as follows.
The dependency of the constants is explicitly expressed
following Okayama et al.
[
15
]
,
which is effectively used in a later analysis.

Corollary 2.3
(Okayama et al.
[
14
, Corollary 2.1]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d
with
0<d<\piup
,
and there exists constants
L
and
\alpha
such that

|f(z)|\leq L|z-a|^{\alpha-1}|b-z|^{\alpha-1}

(2.5)

holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula (
2.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}

depending only on
\alpha
and
d
such that

\left|\int_{a}^{b}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-h\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)\right|\leq L(b-a)^{2\alpha-1}C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}.

Corollary 2.4
(Okayama et al.
[
14
, Corollary 2.3]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d
with
0<d<\piup/2
,
and there exist constants
L
and
\alpha
such that (
2.5
)
holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula (
2.4
).
Then, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}

depending only on
\alpha
and
d
such that

\left|\int_{a}^{b}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-h\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)\right|\leq L(b-a)^{2\alpha-1}C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}\E^{-2\piup dN/\log(2dN/\alpha)}.

We note that the selection formula of
h
is not optimal here;
if
h
is selected optimally, then the convergence rate becomes higher.
However, the optimal selection formula cannot be employed here because
the formula of
h
should be unified in all approximations of
functions, definite integrals, and indefinite integrals.

2.3
SE-Sinc indefinite integration and DE-Sinc indefinite integration

The SE-Sinc and DE-Sinc indefinite integrations are approximation formulas
for indefinite integrals,
and their convergence theorems were provided as follows.
Here,
J(j,h)
is defined by

J(j,h)(x)=h\left\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\piup}\Si\left[\frac{\piup(x-jh)}{h}\right]\right\},

where
\Si(x)
is the sine integral defined by

\Si(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\{(\sin t)/t\}\,\mathrm{d}{t}
.

Theorem 2.5
(Okayama et al.
[
15
, Theorem 2.9]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d
with
0<d<\piup
,
and there exist constants
L
and
\alpha
such that (
2.5
)
holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula (
2.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\max_{t\in[a,b]}\left|\int_{a}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}.

Theorem 2.6
(Okayama et al.
[
15
, Theorem 2.16]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d
with
0<d<\piup/2
,
and there exist constants
L
and
\alpha
such that (
2.5
)
holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
N
be a positive integer,
and let
h
be selected by the formula (
2.4
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\max_{t\in[a,b]}\left|\int_{a}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\frac{\log(2dN/\alpha)}{N}\E^{-\piup dN/\log(2dN/\alpha)}.

3
Existing Sinc-collocation methods

In this section, we describe two Sinc-collocation methods
proposed by Shamloo et al.
[
18
]

and John–Ogbonna
[
4
]
.
The first method employs the SE transformation,
whereas the second method employs the DE transformation.

3.1
Sinc-collocation method by Shamloo et al.

First, we describe the Sinc-collocation method by Shamloo et al.
As outlined in Sect.
1
,
after setting their approximate solution
u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
as (
1.4
),
they substitute
u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
into the given equation (
1.1
),
where the definite and indefinite integrals
are approximated by the SE-Sinc quadrature and
SE-Sinc indefinite integration, respectively.
For the sake of convenience,
we introduce the approximated integral operator

\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])

and
\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}k_{1}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)

\displaystyle=h\sum_{j=-N}^{N}k_{2}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh).

Using these notations, we obtain an equation expressed as

u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(t)-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}](t)-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[u_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}](t)=g(t).

Discretizing this equation at
n=2N+1
collocation points
t=\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih)

(
i=-N,\,\ldots,\,N
), we obtain the system of equations
with respect to
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}=[c_{-N},\,\ldots,\,c_{N}]^{\mathrm{T}}
as

(\tilde{E}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-\tilde{V}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-\tilde{K}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\boldsymbol{c}_{n}=\boldsymbol{g}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}},

(3.1)

where
\tilde{E}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
,
\tilde{V}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}

and
\tilde{K}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
are
n\times n
matrices defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}l|ccc|l@{\,}}\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))&0&\cdots&0&\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))\\
\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-(N-1)h))&1&&\OO&\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-(N-1)h))\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\vdots\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol&&\ddots&&\lx@intercol\hfil\vdots\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}((N-1)h))&\OO&&1&\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}((N-1)h))\\
\omega_{a}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))&0&\cdots&0&\omega_{b}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(Nh))\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}l|clc|l@{\,}}\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[\omega_{a}](\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))&\cdots&k(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh),\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(-Nh)&\cdots&\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[\omega_{b}](\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(-Nh))\\

Numerical examples

This section presents numerical results.
All the programs were written in C with double-precision floating-point
arithmetic.
The source code is available at

https://github.com/okayamat/sinc-colloc-vol-fred
.

First, we consider the following equation,
which is relatively easy to solve numerically.

Example 5.1
(Shamloo et al.
[
18
, Example 5.1]
)

Consider the following equation

u(t)-\int_{0}^{t}(ts)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\int_{0}^{1}(ts)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}=\frac{2}{3}t-\frac{1}{3}t^{4},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1,

whose solution is
u(t)=t
.

In this case, the assumptions of Theorem
4.1

are fulfilled with
\alpha=1
and
d=3.14
(slightly less than
\piup
),
and those of Theorem
4.2

are fulfilled with
\alpha=1
and
d=1.57
(slightly less than
\piup/2
).
Therefore, these values were used for the selection formulas
of
h
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), respectively.
For the original Sinc-collocation methods,
Shamloo et al.
[
18
]
fixed
the formula of
h
as
h=\piup/\sqrt{N}
,
and John–Ogbonna
[
4
]
fixed
the formula of
h
as
h=\log(\piup N)/N
.
Therefore, these formulas were used for computation
in the original Sinc-collocation methods.
The error was examined at 4096 equally spaced points
over the given interval, and the maximum error was plotted
on the graph.
The result is shown in Fig.
1
.
In the top graph, we observe that
the Sinc-collocation methods with the DE transformation
converge much faster than those with the SE transformation.
The bottom graph shows the condition number (with the infinity norm)
of the resulting linear system.
The result indicates that the system is well-conditioned
for all methods.

Next, we consider the following equation,
which is not easy to solve numerically, particularly
for Gaussian quadrature-based methods.
This is due to the derivative singularity at the endpoints.

Example 5.2

Consider the following equation

u(t)-\int_{0}^{t}s^{t+1/2}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{t}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}=\sqrt{t}-\frac{t^{t+2}}{t+2}-\Beta\left(\frac{3}{2},t+1\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq 1,

whose solution is
u(t)=\sqrt{t}
.
Here,
\Beta(\alpha,\beta)
is the beta function.

In this case, the assumptions of Theorem
4.1

are fulfilled with
\alpha=1/2
and
d=3.14
,
and those of Theorem
4.2

are fulfilled with
\alpha=1/2
and
d=1.11
.
Therefore, these values were used for the selection formulas
of
h
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), respectively.
The error was examined at 4096 equally spaced points
over the given interval, and the maximum error was plotted
on the graph.
The result is shown in Fig.
2
,
which exhibits a pattern similar to Example
5.1
.
It should be emphasized that
the convergence of the original Sinc-collocation methods is not proved,
whereas that of the new Sinc-collocation methods is proved
by Theorems
4.1

and
4.2
.
Furthermore,
implementation of the original Sinc-collocation methods
is rather complicated because there exists an
inconsistency in the coefficient matrices.

Finally, we consider the following equation
from a recent paper
[
3
, Example 1]
,
where the kernel
k_{1}(t,s)
is slightly modified
so that it has derivative singularity at
s=-1

Consider the following equation

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+t}-\frac{t}{2\piup}\left\{(t+3)\E^{-t}+\E(t-1)\right\}+\frac{16\sqrt{2}}{105\piup}(7t^{2}+1),\quad-1\leq t\leq 1,

whose solution is
u(t)=\sqrt{1+t}
.

In this case, the assumptions of Theorem
4.1

are fulfilled with
\alpha=1/2
and

,
and those of Theorem
4.2

are fulfilled with

and

.
Therefore, these values were used for the selection formulas
of
h
in (
2.3
) and (

), respectively.
The error was examined at 4096 equally spaced points

In this figure, the result of an existing method based on

6
Proofs

In this section,
proofs of the new theorems stated in Sect.
4
are provided.

6.1
Sketch of the proof of Theorem
4.1

Here, for readers’ convenience, we present
the organization of the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
In this proof, we consider the following three equations:

\displaystyle(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K})u

\displaystyle=g,

\displaystyle(I-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})w

\displaystyle=g,

(6.1)

\displaystyle(I-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})v

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}g.

(6.2)

In Sect.
6.2
,
we analyze the first equation,
which is nothing but (
1.1
),
and show that the equation has
a unique solution
u\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))
.
In Sect.
6.3
,
we analyze the second equation,
and show that (
6.1
) has
a unique solution
w\in C([a,b])
, and there exists a
constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\|u-w\|_{C([a,b])}\leq C\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}.

(6.3)

In Sect.
6.4
,
we analyze the third equation,
and show that (
6.2
) has
a unique solution
v\in C([a,b])
,
which means that the system (
4.1
) is
uniquely solvable, i.e.,
the coefficient matrix
(I_{n}-V_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-K_{n}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})

is invertible.
Furthermore, we show that
v=v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}=\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}w
holds.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|u-v_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{C([a,b])}\leq\|u-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}u\|_{C([a,b])}+\|\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}u-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}w\|_{C([a,b])}\leq\|u-\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}u\|_{C([a,b])}+\|\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\|u-w\|_{C([a,b])}.

(6.4)

Because
u\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))
,
we can use Theorem
2.1
for the first term.
For the second term, we can use (
6.3
)
and the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
(Okayama
[
12
, Lemma 7.2]
)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
be defined by (
2.1
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that

\|\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\leq C\log(N+1).

Thus, we obtain the desired error bound
of Theorem
4.1
.

6.2
On the first equation (
1.1
)

Here, we prove
u\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{D})

(
\mathscr{D}
is either
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
or
\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{DE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
).
The important tool here is the Fredholm alternative theorem.
We start with showing the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2

Assume that
k_{1}(z,\cdot)
and
k_{2}(z,\cdot)

belong to
\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
for all
z\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
,
and
k_{1}(\cdot,w)
and
k_{2}(\cdot,w)

belong to
\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
for all
w\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
.
Then, the operator
(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K}):\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})\to\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
is compact.

Proof 1

It is easy to show that
the operator
(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K})
maps the set
\{f:\|f\|_{\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})}\leq 1\}

onto a uniformly bounded and equicontinuous set.
Thus, the claim follows from the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem
for complex functions (cf. Rudin
[
17
, Theorem 11.28]
).

Therefore, we obtain the following result
by the Fredholm alternative theorem.

Theorem 6.3

Assume that all the assumptions of Lemma
6.2
are fulfilled.
Furthermore, assume that
the homogeneous equation
(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K})f=0

has only the trivial solution
f\equiv 0
.
Then, the operator
(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K}):\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})\to\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})

has a bounded inverse,

(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K})^{-1}:\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})\to\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
.
Furthermore, if
g\in\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
,
then (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
.

For
u\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{D})
,
we must further show the Hölder continuity at the endpoints.
This is done in the proof of the next theorem.

Theorem 6.4

Assume that all the assumptions of Theorem
6.3
are fulfilled.
Furthermore, assume that

g
,
k_{1}(\cdot,w)
, and
k_{2}(\cdot,w)

belong to
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))

uniformly for
w\in\overline{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})}
.
Then, (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{D})
.

Proof 2

From Theorem
6.3
, we have
u\in\mathbf{HC}(\mathscr{D})
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
C
such that

|u(z)-u(a)|\leq C|z-a|^{\alpha}

and
|u(b)-u(z)|\leq C|b-z|^{\alpha}
hold
for all
z\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
.
From
u=g+\mathcal{V}u+\mathcal{K}u
, we have

\displaystyle|u(b)-u(z)|

\displaystyle\leq|g(b)-g(z)|+\int_{z}^{b}|k_{1}(b,w)||u(w)||\mathrm{d}{w}|+\int_{a}^{z}|k_{1}(b,w)-k_{1}(z,w)||u(w)||\mathrm{d}{w}|+\int_{a}^{b}|k_{2}(b,w)-k_{2}(z,w)||u(w)||\mathrm{d}{w}|.

Thus, noting
\alpha\leq 1
, we obtain
|u(b)-u(z)|\leq C_{1}|b-z|^{\alpha}

by the Hölder continuity of
g
,
k_{1}(\cdot,w)
and
k_{2}(\cdot,w)
,
and by the boundedness of
\mathscr{D}
,
k_{1}(b,\cdot)
and
u
.
In the same manner,
we obtain
|u(z)-u(a)|\leq C_{2}|z-a|^{\alpha}
.
Setting
C=\max\{C_{1},\,C_{2}\}
, we obtain the desired inequalities.

In the same manner as Theorem
6.3
,

u\in C([a,b])
is also established as follows.
The proof is omitted.

Theorem 6.5

Assume that
k_{1}(t,\cdot)
and
k_{2}(t,\cdot)

belong to
C([a,b])
for all
t\in[a,b]
,
and
k_{1}(\cdot,s)
and
k_{2}(\cdot,s)

belong to
C([a,b])
for all
s\in[a,b]
.
Furthermore, assume that
the homogeneous equation
(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K})f=0

has only the trivial solution
f\equiv 0
.
Then, the operator
(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K}):C([a,b])\to C([a,b])

has a bounded inverse,

(I-\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{K})^{-1}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])
.
Furthermore, if
g\in C([a,b])
,
then (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in C([a,b])
.

6.3
On the second equation (
6.1
)

Next, we show that (
6.1
) has
a unique solution
w\in C([a,b])
, and estimate the term

\|u-w\|_{C([a,b])}
.
For this purpose,
the following perturbation theorem is useful.

Theorem 6.6
(Atkinson
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
)

Assume the following conditions:

1.

Operators
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}_{n}

are bounded operators on
C([a,b])
to
C([a,b])
.

2.

Operator
(I-\mathcal{X}):C([a,b])\to C([a,b])

has a bounded inverse

(I-\mathcal{X})^{-1}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])
.

3.

Operator
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is compact on
C([a,b])
.

4.

The following inequality holds:

\|(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{n})\mathcal{X}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}<\frac{1}{\|(I-\mathcal{X})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}}.

(6.5)

Then,
(I-\mathcal{X}_{n})^{-1}
exists as a bounded operator
on
C([a,b])
to
C([a,b])
, with

\|(I-\mathcal{X}_{n})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\leq\frac{1+\|(I-\mathcal{X})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\|\mathcal{X}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}}{1-\|(I-\mathcal{X})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\|(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{n})\mathcal{X}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}}.

(6.6)

Furthermore, if
(I-\mathcal{X})u=g
and

(I-\mathcal{X}_{n})w=g
, then

\|u-w\|_{C([a,b])}\leq\|(I-\mathcal{X}_{n})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\|(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{n})u\|_{C([a,b])}.

In what follows,
we show that the four conditions of Theorem
6.6

are fulfilled with
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K}

and
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}
,
under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
.
Condition 1 clearly holds.
Condition 2 is well known, as stated by Theorem
6.5
.
Condition 3 immediately follows from the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem.
For condition 4, in the following lemma, we show

\|\{(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K})-(\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\}(\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\to 0\quad(N\to\infty),

(6.7)

which implies that
there exists a positive integer
N_{0}

such that (
6.5
) holds for all

N\geq N_{0}
.

Lemma 6.7

Assume that
k_{1}(z,\cdot)
and
k_{2}(z,\cdot)

belong to
\mathbf{HC}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))

uniformly for
z\in\overline{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})}
,
and
k_{1}(\cdot,w)
and
k_{2}(\cdot,w)

belong to
\mathbf{HC}(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))

uniformly for
w\in\overline{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})}
.
Let
h
be selected by the formula (
2.3
).
Then, (
6.7
) holds.

Proof 3

Expanding and arranging the terms, we have

\displaystyle\|\{(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K})-(\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\}(\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}+\|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}+\|(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}.

(6.8)

In an existing study
[
16
, Lemma 6.5]
,

\|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\to 0

is already proved,
and in another existing study
[
14
]
,

\|(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\to 0

is also proved.
The remaining terms to be estimated in (
6.8
)
are the second and third terms.
For the second term, setting
F_{i}(t,s)=k_{1}(t,s)k_{2}(s,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))
,
we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)

\displaystyle=h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)\left\{\int_{a}^{t}F_{i}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\sum_{j=-N}^{N}F_{i}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))\right\}.

Because
F_{i}(t,\cdot)
satisfies the assumptions of
Theorem
2.5

uniformly for
t\in[a,b]
and
i=-N,\,\ldots,\,N
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}|f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))|\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)\left|\int_{a}^{t}F_{i}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-\sum_{j=-N}^{N}F_{i}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{C([a,b])}\left(h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)\right)C_{0}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}

for some constant
C_{0}
. Furthermore,
because
h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)
converges to
(b-a)
as
N\to\infty
,
we have

\|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\leq C_{0}C_{1}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}

for some constant
C_{1}
.
This proves that the second term in (
6.8
)
tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
.
Finally, we investigate the third term in (
6.8
).
Setting
G_{j}(t,s)=k_{2}(t,s)k_{1}(s,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))J(j,h)(\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{-1}(s))
,
we have

(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}[f](t)=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}f(\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(jh))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)\left\{\int_{a}^{b}G_{j}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}G_{j}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)\right\}.

Here, we use Corollary
2.3
,
focusing on
G_{j}(t,\cdot)
.
This includes
J(j,h)
, which diverges exponentially as

|J(j,h)(x+\I y)|\leq\frac{5h}{\piup}\cdot\frac{\sinh(\piup y/h)}{\piup y/h},

(6.9)

as shown in an existing study
[
16
, Lemma 6.4]
.
Taking it into account,
setting
K_{1}=\max_{z,w\in\overline{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})}}|k_{1}(z,w)|

and
K_{2}=\max_{z,w\in\overline{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})}}|k_{2}(z,w)|
,
Corollary
2.3
yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{a}^{b}G_{j}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}{s}-h\sum_{i=-N}^{N}G_{j}(t,\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}(ih))\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(ih)\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}K_{1}\frac{5h}{\piup}\cdot\frac{\sinh(\piup d/h)}{\piup d/h}(b-a)^{2\alpha-1}C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\alpha N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{5K_{1}K_{2}(b-a)^{2\alpha-1}C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}}{\piup^{2}d}h^{2}\left[\sinh(\piup d/h)\E^{-\piup d/h}\right].

Furthermore, using
\sinh(\piup d/h)\E^{-\piup d/h}\leq 1/2

and
h\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\{\psi^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\}^{\prime}(jh)\leq C_{1}
as previously estimated,
we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\leq\frac{5K_{1}K_{2}(b-a)^{2\alpha-1}C_{\alpha,d}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}C_{1}}{2\piup^{2}d}h,

which proves that the third term in (
6.8
)
tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
.
This completes the proof.

Thus, all conditions 1 through 4 in Theorem
6.6

are fulfilled, and the next theorem is established.

Theorem 6.8

Assume that all the assumptions of Lemma
6.7

are fulfilled.
Then, there exists a positive integer
N_{0}
such that
for all
N\geq N_{0}
, (
6.1
) has a unique
solution
w\in C([a,b])
.
Furthermore, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that
for all
N\geq N_{0}
, the inequality (
6.3
) holds.

Proof 4

From Theorem
6.6
, we have

\|u-w\|_{C([a,b])}\leq\|(I-\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}-\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}\|(\mathcal{V}+\mathcal{K})u-(\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}})u\|_{C([a,b])}.

If we show that
\|\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}+\mathcal{K}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{\rm{SE}}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([a,b]),C([a,b]))}

is uniformly bounded,

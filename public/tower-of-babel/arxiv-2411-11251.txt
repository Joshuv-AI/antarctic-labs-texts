Black Hole Scaling Relations in the Dwarf-galaxy Regime with Gaia -Sausage/Enceladus and ω \omega Centauri
Guilherme Limberg

Abstract
The discovery of fast moving stars in Milky Way’s most massive globular cluster, ω \omega Centauri ( ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} ), has provided strong evidence for an intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) inside of it. However, ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} is known to be the stripped nuclear star cluster (NSC) of an ancient, now-destroyed, dwarf galaxy. The best candidate to be the original host progenitor of ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} is the tidally disrupted dwarf Gaia -Sausage/Enceladus (GSE), a former Milky Way satellite as massive as the Large Magellanic Cloud. I compare ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} /GSE with other central BH hosts and place it within the broader context of BH-galaxy (co)evolution. The IMBH of ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} /GSE follows the scaling relation between central BH mass and host stellar mass ( M BH {\rm M_{\rm BH}} – M ⋆ {\rm M_{\star}} ) extrapolated from local massive galaxies ( M ⋆ ≳ 10 10 ​ M ⊙ {\rm M_{\star}}\gtrsim 10^{10}\,{\rm M_{\odot}} ). Therefore, the IMBH of ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} /GSE suggests that this relation extends to the dwarf-galaxy regime. I verify that ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} (GSE), as well as other NSCs with candidate IMBHs and ultracompact dwarf galaxies, also follow the M BH {\rm M_{\rm BH}} – σ ⋆ \sigma_{\star} relation with stellar velocity dispersion. Under the assumption of a direct collapse BH, ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} /GSE’s IMBH would require a low initial mass ( ≲ \lesssim 10,000 M ⊙ {\rm M_{\odot}} ) and almost no accretion over ∼ \sim 3 Gyr, which could be the extreme opposite of high- z z galaxies with overmassive BHs such as GN-z11. If ω ​ Cen \omega{\rm Cen} /GSE’s IMBH formed from a Population III supernova remnant, then it could indicate that both light and heavy seeding mechanisms of central BH formation are at play. Other stripped NSCs and dwarf galaxies could help further populate the M BH {\rm M_{\rm BH}} – M ⋆ {\rm M_{\star}} and M BH {\rm M_{\rm BH}} – σ ⋆ \sigma_{\star} relations in the low-mass regime and constraint IMBH demographics and their formation channels.

Introduction

Understanding the early assembly of supermassive black holes (BHs) and their coevolution with their host galaxies is a major goal in Astrophysics

,
39
,
22

[

Just recently,

However, it has been known for more than 20 years that

With the advent of

In this

{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
and stellar velocity dispersion

III
Discussion

III.1
The IMBH of GSE inside
\omega{\rm Cen}
extends the local
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation

The main takeaway from Figure
1
is that the IMBH of
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE resides on top of the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
scaling relations from
Greene et al. [30]
; see text below for possible variations. Since GSE analogs in cosmological hydrodynamical simulations are found to be ubiquitously star-forming systems leading to gas-rich mergers with their MW-like hosts
[
15
,
29
, e.g.,]
, we consider
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relations for both all galaxies and late-type only samples. Different possible
{\rm M_{\star}}
and/or
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
values for
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE would not change this qualitative conclusion given the scatters (shaded areas around the black and blue relation lines). The consequence is that S/LMC-mass galaxies (
10^{8}<{\rm M_{\star}}/{\rm M_{\odot}}\lesssim 10^{9}
, similar to GSE) might follow a natural extension of high-mass systems (
{\rm M_{\star}}\gtrsim 10^{10}\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
) in the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation; note the claimed IMBHs in NGC 205
[
61
]
and NGC 4395
[
82
]
also plotted in Figure
1
. Therefore, other MW and/or M31 satellites and stripped NSCs could be promising targets to further expand IMBH demographics in the dwarf-galaxy regime. Observational challenges are many
[
69
, see]
, but this approach would allow us to further contextualize IMBHs within the broader picture of galaxy-BH coevolution.

JWST
has revealed a surprising excess of overmassive BHs in faint AGNs at
z\gtrsim 4
(red symbols in Figures
1
and
3
), far exceeding the number density expected from canonical quasar luminosity functions
[
43
, e.g.,]
. These AGNs host central BHs where
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}/{\rm M_{\star}}\gtrsim 0.01
, well above the expected from the local
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation
[
64
, see]
. Many of these high-
z
AGNs have similar
{\rm M_{\star}}
to GSE, but central BHs of order 100–1000
\times
more massive than
\omega{\rm Cen}
’s IMBH. To reconcile the fast growth of these overmassive BHs in the early universe with the IMBH of
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE, similar host
{\rm M_{\star}}
at different
z
, might be challenging for BH–galaxy coevolution theory, but useful for supermassive BH growth and seeding models (Section
III.3
).

Many previous works, including some aforementioned ones, have derived the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
scaling relation from various samples and found discrepant shapes for it
[
44
,
70
,
30
, and
Pacucci et al. 64
for very high
z
only]
. These empirical relations basically all converge in the high-mass regime (
10^{10}<{\rm M_{\star}}/{\rm M_{\odot}}\lesssim 10^{12}
), which is expected given that all of them are derived from data for massive galaxies. However, their slopes can be dramatically different, ranging from
{\sim}{\rm M_{\rm BH}}\propto{\rm M_{\star}}
to
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}\propto{\rm M_{\star}}^{3}

[
74
, see]
.
Hence, when approaching the IMBH/dwarf-galaxy regime, some of these fits severally under predict, by factors of
\sim
1000
\times
, the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
of
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE. These might simply mean that these relations should not be extrapolated below
{\rm M_{\star}}\approx 10^{10}\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
. Having said that, we basically have constraints neither on the scatter around
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
nor on the BH occupation fraction for GSE-mass (S/LMC-mass) galaxies with so few IMBH detections: NGC 205
[
61
]
, NGC 4395
[
82
]
, and now
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE
[
31
]
. Therefore, although the exact choice of
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation might change the exact statement that
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE follows an extrapolation from massive galaxies, we can
now
extend it to the IMBH/dwarf-galaxy regime.

Apart from the broader implications to the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation for dwarf galaxies, the formation/evolution of a GSE-mass system with its central IMBH is also interesting to explore. It would be important to understand how the central dynamics of dwarf galaxies and star formation in them respond to the presence of an IMBH within the specific
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}/{\rm M_{\star}}
ratios allowed for
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE
[
45
, e.g.,]
. Likewise, the evolutionary pathway of GSE-mass galaxies with central (IM)BHs could be useful for refining feedback models since AGN activity is expected to regulate the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation itself
[
19
]
, although that might not be the case for dwarf galaxies
[
75
]
. The accretion history of the IMBH in
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE could even tell us about how stellar feedback regulates the early growth of central BHs in dwarf galaxies
[
5
,
32
]
. Moreover,
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE’s IMBH might hold clues to the role of environment (isolated versus satellite dwarf) since merger activity could play a role in providing the necessary conditions for BH growth
[
79
]
.
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE could even be explored as a laboratory for IMBH “incubation” in NSCs, where the host would provide the necessary gas supply for a stellar mass BHs to grow to the intermediate-mass status
[
58
]
.

III.3
Implications for BH seeding

The main classes of BH seeding prescriptions are “light seeds” (Population III supernovae remnants) and “heavy seeds” (direct collapse); see
Volonteri [78]
. The latter might be favored by the high-
z
population of overmassive BHs discovered by
JWST
. For example, galaxy GN-z11
[
63
]
, similar
\log{({\rm M_{\star}}/{\rm M_{\odot}})}=8.9
to GSE, has a central BH of
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}=1.5\times 10^{6}\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
already at
z=10.6

[
52
]
. On the other hand, the IMBH of
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE has an estimated
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
quite close to the minimum value expected by heavy seeding models
[e.g.,
79
,
{\approx}10^{4}\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
]
. Hence, the IMBH in
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE could, in principle, establish a lower limit to the mass distribution of heavy seeds
[
69
]
.

\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE’s IMBH would have a low initial mass and experience almost no accretion.

If Population III supernovae seeding is allowed, one interpretation could be that both mechanisms are at play, light and heavy seeding simultaneously, hence creating a true physical distinction between supermassive and intermediate-mass central BHs. This reinforces the need to hunt for additional IMBHs to constrain both their properties and their host dwarf galaxies’. Sagittarius dSph might be interesting for testing that hypothesis since the local
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation predicts that the mass of a central BH in that dwarf galaxy would be of
\lesssim
1000
{\rm M_{\odot}}

[
30
, Figure
1
]
.

III.4
Other accreted NSCs in the MW

If the discovery of an IMBH in
\omega{\rm Cen}
is confirmed, the next step would be to extend the search to other NSCs with the obvious candidate being NGC 6751 (M54), the NSC of Sagittarius dSph galaxy
[
13
]
. However, M54 is
{\sim}5\times
farther from the Sun than
\omega{\rm Cen}
and is located behind the Galactic bulge, so stars are fainter, more difficult to resolve, and the field is plagued with MW foreground. Moreover, M54 still resides within the dark-matter halo of Sagittarius and this galaxy is undergoing severe tidal stripping by the MW
[
53
]
, which might affect its central dynamics.

There are other candidate NSCs in the MW. NGC 6273 (M19) and NGC 6934 have been proposed to be NSCs of Kraken/Heracles
[
46
,
38
]
and Helmi streams
[
37
]
disrupted dwarfs, respectively, by
Pfeffer et al. [67]
. However, unlike
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE and M54/Sagittarius, both NGC 6273 and NGC 6934 would be significant outliers in the
{\rm M_{\rm NSC}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation if really related to these satellites (Figure
4
). These associations could be incorrect, but these candidate NSCs might have also experienced substantial mass loss through tides. Future studies on the stripping history of NSCs in dwarf galaxies merging with massive hosts would be of interest to test if these systems would really be expected to be outliers in the
{\rm M_{\rm NSC}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation.

With respect to the NSC candidates themselves, there is strong evidence from high-resolution stellar spectroscopy that NGC 6273 is a genuine NSC
[
41
]
. Hence, there might be a low-mass (
{\rm M_{\star}}\lesssim 10^{7}\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
) dwarf-galaxy host for NGC 6273 still undiscovered in the MW. On the other hand, confirmation of NGC 6934 as a true NSC is still needed; the best spectroscopic sample available is from
Marino et al. [54]
with only 13 stars. Other aforementioned candidate stripped NSCs in M31 with claims of IMBHs are G1
[
26
,
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}\sim 20{,}000\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
]
and B023-G078
[
65
,
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}\sim 100{,}000\,{\rm M_{\odot}}
]
. In Figure
1
, I only provide lower limits on
{\rm M_{\star}}
for these clusters since their original hosts are unknown.

IV
SUMMARY

I have demonstrated that the proposed central IMBH inside
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE follows the local (redshift
z\sim 0
)
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation. This suggests that this scaling relation might extend to GSE-mass, i.e., S/LMC-mass galaxies. Hence, since there are many Local Group satellites that could host central IMBHs, this result might encourage new observational programs to hunt for them. Sagittarius dSph could be a compelling target since it is relatively close and it also hosts the NSC M54. Further theoretical studies could also be promising such as on the dynamical response of dwarf galaxies to central IMBHs and the AGN feedback mechanism for regulating the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation in low-mass systems.

I have also shown that the IMBH in
\omega{\rm Cen}
agrees with the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
\sigma_{\star}
relation. Other stellar clusters with candidate IMBHs in M31, namely G1 and B023-G078, as well as UCDs also reside on top of this relation, perhaps corroborating the connection between stripped NSCs and these compact galaxies. Moreover, NSCs and UCDs might be comparable to bulges of local massive galaxies in the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
\sigma_{\star}
relation. Other wandering accreted NSCs in the MW might also offer the opportunity to push further the
{\rm M_{\rm BH}}
–
{\rm M_{\star}}
relation into the low-mass regime and inform about IMBH demographics. Confirming the host galaxy of NGC 6273 (M19) and if NGC 6934 is a genuine NSC are of particular urgency.

I also performed an initial exploration of BH growth histories for
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE. This analysis suggest that, if this IMBH formed from direct collapse, its initial mass must have been quite low compared to the expected distribution from heavy seeding prescriptions. The accretion rate of this IMBH must have also stayed very low throughout its
\sim
3 Gyr lifetime until the GSE merger with the MW. This behavior could be the complete opposite of high-
z
AGNs similar to GN-z11 identified to host overmassive BHs that require both high initial masses and accretion rates. Another possibility is that
\omega{\rm Cen}
/GSE’s IMBH formed from light seeding from a Population III supernova remnant. One implication could be that both heavy and light seeding mechanisms are at play to form central BHs, further reinforcing the importance of finding additional IMBHs.

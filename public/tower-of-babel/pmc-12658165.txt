Low complexity hybrid algorithm for improving PAPR BER and PSD in OTFS under diverse channel conditions
Authors: Arun Kumar, Aziz Nanthaamornphong, Nishant Gaur, Mehedi Masud
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, the Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR) and Bit Error Rate (BER) performance in Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (OTFS) modulation with Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) over Rayleigh fading channels were investigated. Simulations with 256-QAM and 512-QAM modulation schemes on 256 subcarriers evaluated several PAPR reduction techniques, including the Vandermonde matrix–based Partial Transmit Sequence with Companding (Vm-PTS+Companding), Vm-SLM+Companding, conventional PTS, Selective Mapping (SLM), Companding, and Clipping & Filtering (C&F). The proposed Vm-PTS+Companding achieves up to 9 dB PAPR reduction for 256-QAM and 8.9 dB for 512-QAM at a Complementary Cumulative Distribution Function (CCDF) of , outperforming existing methods. Under a channel estimation error, the PAPR performance degrades, yet the proposed scheme maintains a 2.9–9.9 dB gain over conventional approaches. BER analysis showed Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) improvements of 3.5–13.7 dB in ideal channels and up to 16 dB under error conditions. Power Spectral Density (PSD) evaluation further confirmed the reduced spectral leakage, with the method achieving a level of dB. These results demonstrate that Vm-PTS+Companding effectively optimizes both the PAPR and BER in OTFS-NOMA systems, supporting high data rates in error-prone wireless environments.Keywords: OTFS, PAPR, Vandermonde matrix, PTS, SLMSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (OTFS) is a high-mobility wireless communication scheme for modulating signals in the delay–Doppler domain. In contrast to Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), OTFS offers resistance against multipath fading and Doppler shifts, increasing vehicular and satellite network reliability. Its importance arises from its ability to increase spectral efficiency and reliability under changing conditions.

The Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR) is the ratio of the peak power to the average power in a signal. A high PAPR leads to nonlinear power amplifier distortions, which degrade the signal, lower the efficiency, and increase the Bit Error Rate (BER). In OTFS, working in the delay–Doppler domain, a high PAPR continues to be an issue as it impacts signal transmission, especially in high-mobility cases1.

The intrinsic multiplexing capability of OTFS can exacerbate the PAPR challenges, as is the case for OFDM. Reducing techniques such as clipping, companding, and selective mapping are necessary to improve OTFS performance, guaranteeing effective power efficiency and enhanced system reliability in practical implementations. The PAPR problem in OTFS remains a significant challenge, as OTFS has an inherently high PAPR characteristic compared to OFDM because of the superposition of multicarrier signals.

Researchers are actively developing hybrid PAPR reduction methods to address this issue. However, most solutions add complexity and computational burden or introduce distortion into the signal2. Existing work aims to achieve a balance between PAPR reduction and system performance in terms of BER and spectral efficiency. Although OTFS is gaining popularity in 6G and high-mobility contexts, effective PAPR reduction is still an open issue for practical implementation and optimization3.

Traditional PAPR reduction techniques, including clipping, companding, selective mapping (SLM), and partial transmit sequence (PTS), alleviate high-PAPR problems by altering the waveform structure. Clipping restricts the peak amplitude and minimizes nonlinear distortions but causes in-band distortion and out-of-band emissions. Companding alters signal dynamics to compress high peaks and enhance amplifier efficiency, but may compromise signal quality. SLM and PTS optimize the signal phase structures to create low-PAPR alternatives, requiring side information transmission and adding computational complexity4.

These techniques are effective in OFDM but encounter limitations in OTFS owing to its unique delay–Doppler domain representation.

In contrast to OFDM, OTFS transforms symbols in both time and frequency, thereby distributing information across several subcarriers. This leads to complex interdependencies, rendering the conventional PAPR reduction techniques less efficient. Clipping and companding can distort the delay–Doppler domain representation, impairing system performance. SLM and PTS require phase adjustments, which disrupt the OTFS Doppler robustness. Additionally, the two-dimensional spreading of OTFS adds a computational burden to these algorithms.

Because of these issues, traditional PAPR reduction methods cannot yield optimal solutions for OTFS. Instead, hybrid and adaptive techniques specific to OTFS, including Doppler-aware precoding and advanced signal processing methods, are required to efficiently minimize the PAPR without degrading overall system performance5.

The proposed Vm-based PTS+Companding (Vm-PTS+C) and Vm-SLM+Companding schemes significantly mitigate PAPR in OTFS by exploiting structured phase optimization and signal transformation. In Vm-PTS+Companding, the input OTFS symbols are divided into sub-blocks, and phase rotation via the Vandermonde matrix (Vm) reduces the PAPR prior to companding, which further smooths peak oscillations. Similarly, Vm-SLM+Companding generates several candidate signals using Vm-based phase sequences and selects the one with the lowest PAPR before applying companding. This organized phase rotation enables more effective peak suppression without compromising the integrity of the delay–Doppler domain representation.

Compared with traditional PAPR methods, the proposed scheme has several benefits6. The contributions of this study are as follows.

The Vm-PTS+Companding method significantly reduces the PAPR in OTFS signals over Rayleigh fading channels, achieving excellent efficiency for 256-QAM with 256 subcarriers. Background and terminology

Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (OTFS) is a doubly dispersive channel modulation strategy designed to improve wireless communication performance under both time- and frequency-selective fading. Unlike conventional Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), which operates in the frequency domain, OTFS transmits data in the delay–Doppler domain, providing strong resistance against multipath fading and Doppler shifts.

The OTFS waveform is constructed by first mapping modulated symbols to a delay–Doppler grid. The transmitted symbols are represented as X[k, l], where k denotes the Doppler index and l represents the delay index. This two-dimensional representation provides a stable characterization of the wireless channel, as it remains resilient to time and frequency variations. By mapping data into this domain, OTFS exploits channel diversity more effectively and improves system performance in high-mobility scenarios.

These factors contribute to the high PAPR in OTFS, requiring effective reduction methods for better transmission performance.

Vandermonde matrix

The Vandermonde structure is particularly suitable for OTFS because it inherently captures the delay-Doppler characteristics of time-varying channels, making it a natural fit for the OTFS modulation framework. Unlike random or other structured matrices, the Vandermonde matrix provides deterministic orthogonality and preserves the shift-invariance property, which is crucial for efficient spreading of symbols across the delay and Doppler domains. This ensures improved resilience against multipath fading and Doppler shifts, leading to better diversity gains and lower intersymbol interference. Additionally, its structured nature simplifies the implementation of PAPR reduction techniques such as PTS or SLM by allowing predictable transformations with reduced computational complexity. Hence, the Vandermonde structure offers both mathematical elegance and practical efficiency in OTFS systems.

In Partial Transmit Sequence (PTS), the OTFS signal is split into a number of sub-blocks, each with independent phase rotations to achieve peak power reduction.

By incorporating a transform, the sub-blocks then experience structured phase shifts, maximizing orthogonality and ideal phase alignment with additional reduction of high PAPR occurrence chances. Furthermore, companding transformation is applied subsequent to PTS to re-form the signal amplitude, inhibiting peak values and preserving average power. guarantees that phase-rotated sub-blocks have a low correlation, inhibiting constructive interference that contributes to high peaks. The improvement in BER performance primarily arises from the combined effects of reduced nonlinear distortion and enhanced diversity in the OTFS system. High PAPR signals often push the power amplifier into its nonlinear region, introducing distortions that shift the constellation points and increase the likelihood of detection errors. By lowering the PAPR, the proposed approach ensures that the transmitted waveform remains within the linear range of the amplifier, thereby maintaining the accuracy of symbol mapping and improving signal fidelity. In addition, the Vandermonde matrix introduces structured diversity in the delay-Doppler domain, which strengthens resilience against multipath fading and helps preserve the symbol orthogonality under varying channel conditions. The inclusion of companding further balances the power distribution by compressing the high-amplitude peaks and amplifying the weaker signals, leading to a more uniform and reliable signal structure at the receiver. These combined effects reduce symbol misinterpretation and improve error resilience, allowing the system to achieve a better BER performance. Ultimately, the method enhances signal quality, ensuring reliable transmission in challenging wireless environments.

Future work

The promising simulation results of the proposed methods invite several concrete extensions that move the technique from simulation toward practical deployment and adaptive intelligence. Future research can be extended along three key directions: hardware prototyping, AI-driven optimization, and adaptive Vandermonde matrix (Vm) design, supported by a rigorous integration and evaluation plan.

Hardware implementation

Translating the proposed algorithm into real hardware is the most direct path to validate real-world gains in PAPR, BER, PSD, and complexity while also exposing practical constraints such as latency, numerical precision, and power consumption. An FPGA/SoC prototype (e.g., Xilinx/Intel FPGA with ARM host) serves as an ideal platform for rapid iteration. The IFFT/FFT cores can be pipelined and parallelized, while Vandermonde transforms and companding can be mapped as fixed-point streaming blocks with careful reuse of FFT IP to reduce logic cost. Key challenges include optimal word-length selection to balance quantization effects against performance, memory and bandwidth planning (BRAM/DMA), and implementing low-latency phase-search kernels for SLM/PTS variants. Integration with an SDR/USRP and RF front-end will enable measurement of nonlinear effects and ACPR/EVM under realistic conditions, possibly including a digital predistortion (DPD) stage. For final deployment, an ASIC implementation can be explored for energy-efficient operation, but FPGA validation should precede it. Evaluation should jointly consider communication metrics (BER, PSD, CCDF) and hardware efficiency (LUTs, DSPs, BRAM, throughput, latency, energy/bit).

AI-driven PAPR reduction

Machine learning offers powerful tools for reducing runtime search complexity and improving adaptability. One approach is to use lightweight neural predictors to rank or generate candidate phase vectors, ensuring only the top-k are tested instead of exhaustive U or P trials. Alternatively, autoencoder-inspired companding functions can be trained to minimize multi-objective losses combining PAPR, BER, and PSD leakage. Reinforcement learning (RL) can dynamically allocate computation budgets (e.g., number of candidates tested) based on channel state and QoS requirements. Practical deployment will require compact models trained with quantization-aware techniques, pruning, and distillation for real-time inference on FPGA/SoC or embedded CPUs. Further, online or federated learning can enable continual adaptation across base stations without centralized raw data collection. Emphasis must also be placed on robustness against channel mismatches, adversarial perturbations, and ensuring interpretability so that failing modes can be detected and managed.

Adaptive Vm design

Instead of relying on a fixed Vandermonde matrix, the Vm can be parameterized and adapted to instantaneous channel statistics. For example, columns defined as , with , can be dynamically tuned to match dominant delay–Doppler clusters while minimizing surrogate objectives such as PAPR, cross-correlation, or BER proxies. Optimization strategies may include gradient-based updates, alternating optimization, or manifold optimization on the torus of angle parameters. Adaptive Vm design improves effective diversity in the delay–Doppler domain, reduces redundancy in candidate search (U, P), and enhances numerical stability. Regularization constraints are needed to preserve orthogonality and conditioning. AI can further accelerate this adaptation by learning mappings from channel state information (CSI) to Vm parameters for near–real-time operation without iterative optimization.

Integration, evaluation, and challenges

A staged evaluation plan should begin with offline simulations using realistic channel models (Doppler spreads, measured delay profiles) to quantify PAPR–BER–PSD trade-offs across modulation orders and subcarrier counts. Next, FPGA/SoC proof-of-concept systems with SDR measurements should be developed, followed by field trials assessing coexistence and interference. Evaluation metrics should span both communication performance (CCDF, BER vs. SNR, PSD leakage, ACPR, EVM) and hardware efficiency (resource utilization, throughput, latency, energy/bit). For AI-based variants, additional metrics such as model size, inference latency, and online adaptation speed are required.

The main challenges include ensuring that learned models generalize across diverse channel conditions, mitigating quantization losses in fixed-point hardware, and avoiding ill-conditioning of aggressively tuned Vm structures. These can be addressed with robust training, quantization-aware design, conservative stability regularization, and fallback deterministic modes in low-confidence situations. Furthermore, co-design with the power amplifier and digital predistortion is critical, as analog nonlinearities strongly impact achievable performance.

Conclusion

The research thoroughly examines the PAPR, BER, and PSD performance of OTFS waveforms under Rayleigh fading channels for both 256-QAM and 512-QAM modulations, with and without channel errors. The proposed -PTS+Companding scheme significantly outperforms existing standard PAPR reduction methods, achieving PAPR reduction of up to 9 dB with 256-QAM and up to 8.9 dB with 512-QAM.

Performance analysis under a 10% channel error further validates the robustness of the technique, efficiently mitigating the increased PAPR resulting from higher-order QAM and channel distortions. Furthermore, BER analysis demonstrates that the proposed technique achieves substantial SNR gains of up to 16 dB in error-prone conditions, affirming its efficacy in enhancing signal reliability. The PSD evaluation also shows that -PTS+Companding introduces minimal spectral leakage, ensuring optimal spectrum utilization and reduced interference.

Despite these advantages, the computational complexity of the -PTS+Companding algorithm is higher compared to traditional approaches, which may limit its practical applicability in real-time systems. Additionally, while this study focuses on single-user OTFS, future research should explore its adaptability to multi-user and MIMO-OTFS scenarios to assess scalability. Finally, the impact of different power amplifier nonlinearities on the proposed scheme should be examined in greater depth to fully optimize its performance in real-world deployments.

Overall, the -PTS+Companding technique is a highly effective method for PAPR reduction in OTFS systems, with promising potential for future wireless communication applications.

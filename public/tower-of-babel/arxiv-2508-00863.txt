A Note on the Solution of Symmetric Circulant Real Linear Systems
Alessandro Guazzini, Enrico Caricchio

Abstract
Employing the Fast Fourier Transform we propose a ready-to-use solution to symmetric circulant real linear systems of equations, particularly useful when a broader theoretical analysis is involved. Key words. Symmetric circulant matrix, Real linear system of equations, Circulant structure, FFT MSC codes. 15A06, 15A09



1
Introduction

Several fields within physics, engineering, statistics, economics involve circulant linear systems of equations and their solutions.

A considerable body of earlier literature has addressed this topic. See for instance, (Berg 1975) [1], (Chen 1987) [3], (Chao 1988) [2] and (Rojo 1990) [10]. In recent years, new methods have been presented by (El-Sayed 2005) [6], (Corless and Fillion 2013) [5], (Lin 2013) [8] and (Lin 2014) [9].

The above works have not reached a ready-to-use solution, convenient when a deeper theoretical analysis is entailed.

Utilizing the Fast Fourier Transform we try to fill this gap by providing an easily intelligible solution to symmetric circulant real linear systems, both for the case of a non-constant and a constant vector of known values.

An ideal application of the result is in the well-known (Salop 1979) [10] or (Chen and Riordan 2007) [4] economics models where the equilibrium allocations are found solving symmetric circulant real systems of linear equations, to which a straightforward solution is of great benefit for their economic interpretations.

2
The Solution to a Symmetric Circulant Real Linear System of Equations

In this section we derive our proposed solution to a symmetric circulant real linear system.

We first introduce the following.

Definition 1
.

A symmetric circulant real linear system is

Ax=b

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the non-singular matrix of coefficients such that the
(k,j)
entry of
A
is
a_{k,j}=a_{\left(j-k\right)\mod n}
with
a_{n-l}=a_{l}
. While
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector of solutions and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector of known values.

Proposition 1
.

A=F\Psi F^{*}

where
F\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) matrix of
A
, or the matrix of
A
’s eigenvectors where the
j^{\text{th}}
element of the
k^{\text{th}}
eigenvector is the
j^{\text{th}}
of
n
distinct complex roots of unity
\frac{\omega_{jk}}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}e^{\frac{-2\pi ijk}{n}}
,
F^{*}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is instead
A
’s FFT conjugate matrix and eventually
\Psi\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is the diagonal matrix of
A
’s eigenvalues with the
k^{\text{th}}
element being
\psi_{k}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}a_{m}e^{\frac{-2\pi imk}{n}}
.

Proof.

See (Gray 2006, 32-34) [7].
∎

Corollary 1
.

A^{-1}=F\Psi^{-1}F^{*}

Proof.

See (Gray 2006, 35) [7].
∎

Proposition 2
.

\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that

\psi_{k}=a_{0}+2\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}a_{m}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi mk}{n}\right)+\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}a_{\frac{n}{2}}&\text{ if }n\text{ even }\\
0&\text{ if }n\text{ odd }\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\psi_{k}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}S_{m}
with
S_{m}=a_{m}e^{\frac{-2\pi imk}{n}}
.

Noting that for any
m=1,\dots,n-1
,

with
1
and
2
following by
definition

1
and from Euler identity,
e^{\pi i}+1=0
, respectively, it follows that
S_{m}+S_{n-m}=S_{m}+\overline{S_{m}}=2\mathfrak{R}(S_{m})=2a_{m}\cos(\frac{2\pi mk}{n})
as
e^{\frac{-2\pi imk}{n}}=\cos\left(\frac{2\pi imk}{n}\right)-i\sin\left(\frac{2\pi imk}{n}\right)
.

Moreover, since there are
\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
unique couples
\left(m,n-m\right)
, with a distinguished
m=\frac{n}{2}
if
n
is even, we might rewrite
\psi_{k}
as

\psi_{k}=S_{0}+2\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}a_{m}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi mk}{n}\right)+\begin{cases}S_{\frac{n}{2}}&\text{ if }n\text{ even }\\
0&\text{ if }n\text{ odd }\end{cases}

Eventually, noting that
S_{0}=a_{0}
and
S_{\frac{n}{2}}=a_{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\pi ik}=(-1)^{k}a_{\frac{n}{2}}
the proof is completed.
∎

We now introduce the main result.

Theorem 1
.

For any
l=0,\dots,n-1
the
l^{\text{th}}
element of the solution vector
x
is

\displaystyle x_{l}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\psi_{0}^{-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}+\frac{2}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\psi_{k}^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k\left(j-l\right)}{n}\right)+\begin{cases}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l}}{n}\psi_{\frac{n}{2}}^{-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(-1\right)^{j}b_{j}&\text{ if n even}\\
0&\text{ if n odd}\end{cases}

Proof.

We first note that
x=A^{-1}b=F\Psi^{-1}F^{*}b
by
definition

1
and
corollary

1
.

Then, using
proposition

1
and
corollary

1
we can readily observe that for any
l=0,\dots,n-1

x_{l}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{lk}\omega_{kj}^{*}\psi_{k}^{-1}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k}^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{n}}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}

or

x_{l}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k}^{-1}T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}

with
T_{k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{n}}
.

Furthermore, noting that

T_{n-k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi i(n-k)j}{n}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi inj}{n}}e^{\frac{-2\pi ikj}{n}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}(-1)^{2j}e^{\frac{-2\pi ikj}{n}}=\overline{T_{k}}

and similarly, since by
proposition

2
eigenvalues are real, that

\psi_{n-k}=\overline{\psi_{k}}=\psi_{k}

it follows that
\psi_{k}^{-1}T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}+\psi_{n-k}^{-1}T_{n-k}e^{\frac{-2\pi il\left(n-k\right)}{n}}=\psi_{k}^{-1}T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}+\psi_{k}^{-1}\overline{T_{k}}e^{\frac{-2\pi iln}{n}}e^{\frac{2\pi ilk}{n}}=\psi_{k}^{-1}T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}+\overline{\psi_{k}^{-1}A_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}}=2\psi_{k}^{-1}\mathfrak{R}\left(T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}\right)
.

Eventually, since there are
\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
unique couples
\left(k,n-k\right)
with a distinguished
k=\frac{n}{2}
if
n
is even,
x_{l}
might be rewritten as

x_{l}=\psi_{0}^{-1}T_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}2\psi_{k}^{-1}\mathfrak{R}\left(T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}\right)+\begin{cases}\psi_{\frac{n}{2}}^{-1}T_{\frac{n}{2}}\left(-1\right)^{l}&\text{ if n even}\\
0&\text{ if n odd}\end{cases}

Finally, noting that
T_{0}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}
,
T_{\frac{n}{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi i\frac{n}{2}j}{n}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\left(-1\right)^{j}
and

\mathfrak{R}\left(T_{k}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\left[\left(\cos\left(\frac{2\pi jk}{n}\right)+i\sin\left(\frac{2\pi jk}{n}\right)\right)\left(\cos\left(\frac{2\pi kl}{n}\right)-i\sin\left(\frac{2\pi kl}{n}\right)\right)\right]\right]\ =\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\left[\cos\left(\frac{2\pi jk}{n}\right)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi kl}{n}\right)+\sin\left(\frac{2\pi jk}{n}\right)\sin\left(\frac{2\pi kl}{n}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k\left(j-l\right)}{n}\right)
the proof is completed.
∎

We eventually introduce a second result.

Proposition 3
.

If the vector of known values
b
is such that for any
j

b_{j}=\beta
, the
l^{\text{th}}
element of the solution vector
x
is

x_{l}=\beta\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}\right)^{-1}

Proof.

Noting again that
x=A^{-1}b=F\Psi^{-1}F^{*}b
by
definition

1
and
corollary

1
, it is easily verified that

x_{l}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\beta\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{lk}\omega_{kj}^{*}\psi_{k}^{-1}=\frac{\beta}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{\frac{-2\pi ilk}{n}}\psi_{k}^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{n}}

by proposition 1.

Then, by the orthogonality of the complex exponential

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{n}}=\begin{cases}n&\text{if $k\mod n=0$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

since for any
k
,
k<n
it follows that

x_{l}=\beta\psi_{0}^{-1}

Eventually, for
\psi_{0}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}
by
proposition

2
, the proof is completed.
∎

3
Conclusion

Capitalizing on the properties of the Fast Fourier Transform of symmetric circulant real matrices we proposed a readily applicable solution to symmetric circulant real linear systems of equations, particularly functional when a broader theoretical analysis is needed.

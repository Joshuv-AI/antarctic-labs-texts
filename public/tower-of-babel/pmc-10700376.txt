Deployment of fine and coarse grain rice cultivars in time and space for increased productivity
Authors: Md Parvez Anwar, Abrarul Hoque Shayokh, Probir Kumer Sarkar, Sabina Yeasmin, Md Harun Rashid, Afroza Sultana, Sinthia Afsana Kheya, A K M Mominul Islam
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo examine the impact of mixture ratio and temporal deployment of fine and coarse grain cultivars on rice productivity and profitability, two experiments were carried out at Bangladesh Agricultural University. In both investigations, two monsoon rice cultivars viz., Binadhan-13 (a tall, late-maturing, fine-grained) and Dhani Gold (a semi-dwarf, mid-maturing, coarse-grained) were utilized. In the 1st experiment, rice cultivars were planted in different mixture ratios viz., sole Binadhan-13, sole Dhani Gold, 1:1, 2:3, 3:2, 2:4 and 4:2 ratio of Binadhan-13 to Dhani Gold. The second study included the introducing time of Dhani Gold viz., seven days before or after or same day of Binadhan-13, sole Binadhan-13 and sole Dhani Gold. Three replications of the randomized complete block design were used for both trials. In the first experiment, both cultivars produced more when grown in mixtures as opposed to solitary cultures. Even though Dhani Gold's sole culture had the greatest yield, it was statistically equivalent to combined yields when Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold were grown together in mixtures at 2:1, 3:1, or 4:1 ratios. The maximum net return and benefit cost ratio (BCR) were achieved when Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold were planted in mixture following a 4:2 ratio as opposed to when Binadhan-13 was grown solely. In the second experiment, a sole cultivar generated less yield than cultivars that were transplanted on the same day, before, and after another cultivar. Cultivation of sole Binadhan-13 produced the lowest net return and minimum BCR (1.37), while the highest net return and BCR (1.81) was recorded when Dhani Gold was planted seven days before Binadhan-13 in mixture. A clear economic advantage of mixed culture with temporal deployment over the sole culture of either cultivar was evident. Therefore, transplanting Dhani Gold in a 1:1 ratio seven days before Binadhan-13 may be advised for greater output and economic return.Keywords: Monsoon rice, Fine rice, Coarse rice, Cultivar mixture, Spatial deployment, Temporal deployment

Introduction

Rice is the primary food source for about two billion Asians and 400 million Africans and Latin Americans [1]. Among the major food crops of the world, rice is one, and the importance of rice needs no over-emphasis. Rice is widely grown in Bangladesh due to its favorable geographic and agronomic conditions. In Bangladesh, rice is farmed in the 'Aus' (March to June), 'Aman' (June to November), and 'Boro' seasons (November to May). In terms of production volume, Aman is the second-largest rice harvest in the nation, while Boro remains the top. With a production of 14.4 million metric tons, Aman rice occupies an area of 5.62 million hectares (estimated data of husked Aman rice, 2020–2021 by BBS). The estimated average yield of Aman rice for the fiscal year 2020–2021 is 2.57 metric tons per hectare, 0.46 % higher than the fiscal year 2019–2020 [2]. Between 2001 and 2025, rice demand must rise by 25 % to keep up with population growth [3]. Every year, the extent of arable land shrinks. Therefore, it's a great challenge to feed this large population with a decreasing area of land. To fulfill the rising demand, Bangladesh must significantly enhance rice yield and production in a sustainable way.

Cultivar mixture refers to growing more than one cultivar simultaneously on the same piece of land with no attempt to breed [4]. The component cultivars may differ in some aspects, must be sufficiently similar to one another to be grown together. A cultivar mixture might consist of cultivars from the same species “intra specific mixtures” or be made up of "multi-lines," which are combinations of genetically identical lines from different crop species that only differ in one particular trait. Compared to monoculture of the component crop, inter-planting cultivars gives each cultivar a greater ability to use resources more effectively and helps them adapt to various stress conditions, resulting in higher yields. Three benefits of cultivar mixtures were highlighted by Ref. [5]: disease control, compensatory impacts (a vigorous variety makes up for a weak or damaged variety), and stabilization of yield. Additionally, inter-planting cultivars provides a special chance for on-farm genetic resource conservation by enabling farmers to use commonly used conventional rice cultivars. Variety is a genetic trait that greatly influences a crop's yield and yield-related characteristics. The variety and the surrounding settings where it grows directly impact on the yield components.

Temporal deployment is a crucial element since it significantly affects the growth and development, tiller formation, grain development and other rice yield-contributing traits [6]. Temporal deployment during transplanting of a variety at one season may not be suited for other kinds at other seasons for best output. Utilizing over-aged seedlings negatively impacts the crop's overall performance and significantly lowers crop production. Therefore, it is crucial to determine the seedlings' ideal variety age for a certain season. Enhancing the compatibility of mixes and improving system efficiency can be accomplished by modifying the temporal deployment of associated crops. This tactic's effectiveness in reducing insect, disease, lodging, and weed has been supported by numerous reports. By increasing the likelihood of beneficial interactions between cultivars, mixing cultivars, can improve functional diversity and increase production [7]. Farmers with little resources can readily implement the cultivar mixture method since they only need to combine currently available cultivars with varying agronomic features and performance. It also opens up opportunities for genetic resource conservation on farms by enabling farmers to grow widely adapted traditional cultivars. Although a number of studies have been documented on interplanting cultivars in different crops for weed suppression, disease management and yield advantages [[8], [9], [10]], not a single work have been carried out to date to examine the performance cultivar mixture of the same species of crops and their temporal deployment in a specific season to enhance crop productivity. However, growing two or more cultivars of same crop species in mixture reduces intra-specific competition for natural resources and increases competitive ability of crops against weeds and thus enhances crop yield. Therefore, the present study was initiated to assess the potentiality of cultivar mixture and temporal deployment strategy as a tool in increasing productivity and profitability of monsoon rice under Bangladesh condition.

Methodology

Experimental duration and site

During the monsoon season of 2018, two studies were carried out between July and December. The area is located at latitude 24° 75′ N, longitude 90° 50′ E and 19 m above mean sea level. The testing site is in the Old Brahmaputra Floodplain Agro-ecological Zone (AEZ-9) characterized by sediments from the Brahmaputra River that were deposited before the river-shifted into its Jamuna channel about 200 years ago [11].

The Sonatola series of non-calcareous dark gray floodplain soil type makes up the soil of the experimental site and it is part of the Old Brahmaputra Alluvial Tract. The test area was a medium-high piece of land with a moderate drainage system. The soil had a silty loam texture, a pH of 6.42, and a moderate amount of organic matter. The climate of the experimental site is under sub-tropical in nature and is characterized by high temperature, high humidity and heavy precipitation with occasional gusty winds during Kharif (April to September) season and scanty rainfall associated with moderately low temperatures and plenty of sunshine during rabi season (October to March). At the experimental site during the study period, the maximum, minimum, and average monthly temperatures (°C), relative humidity (%), monthly total rainfall (mm), and sunshine (hour) were all reported as 30.6 °C, 18.3 °C, 24.4 °C, 84.6 %, 190.2 mm, and 141.2 h, respectively.

Treatments and design for the experiment

Two experiments were carried out in this research. In experiment 1, the treatments included five different mixture ratios of Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold along with their sole culture viz., (i) Sole Binadhan-13 (inbred variety), (ii) Sole Dhani Gold (hybrid variety), (iii) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold in 1:1 row ratio, (iv) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold in 2:3 row ratio, (v) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold in 3:2 row ratio, (vi) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold in 2:4 row ratio, (vii) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold in 4:2 row ratio.

In experiment 2, the treatments included different transplanting time of Dhani-Gold variety concerning Binadhan-13 transplanting viz., (i) Sole Binadha-13, (ii)_Sole Dhani Gold, (iii) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold transplanted on same days, (iv) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold transplanted 7 days after Binadhan-13, (v) Binadhan-13 + Dhani Gold transplanted 7 days before Binadhan-13. With three replications, both studies were set up using a randomized complete block design (RCBD). The first experiment had 21 plots and the second experiment had 15, with each plot measuring 4.0 m × 2.5 m. (10 m2).

Plant materials

In this study, plant materials included two high-yielding transplant varieties of monsoon rice. Here is a short description of those.

Binadhan-13

The Bangladesh Institute of Nuclear Agriculture (BINA) released the mutant KD5-18-150 as Binadhan-13. It is a type of aromatic fine-grained rice that is excellent for cultivation in Bangladesh during the monsoon season. It was created using the native Kalizira fine grain fragrant rice cultivar along with gamma radiation and Datura extract. This cultivar has a medium level of lodging resistance. It is a tall variety with a 160 cm average plant height and a 13.2 g average 1000-grain weight. It matures by 138–142 days. It yields 3.5 t ha−1 of grain on average.

Dhani Gold

This is a coarse grain hybrid rice variety introduced and marketed by ACI Company in Bangladesh. With a short growing season of 125–130 days, the cultivar produces average 6–7 t ha−1 of grain. It possesses qualities that make it resistant to bacterial leaf blight and blast disease. This cultivar is small, with an average plant height of 105 cm and a 1000-grain weight of 24.0 g.

Crop management

Ten days before transplantation, the experimental field was prepared with a power tiller, following puddling with country plough and laddering to get the soil suited for transplanting. The experimental field was fertilized with urea, triple super phosphate, muriate of potash, gypsum, and zinc sulphate @ 150, 100, 70, 60, and 10 kg ha−1, respectively. Before final tillage, whole amount of TSP, MoP, gypsum, and zinc sulphate were applied. Urea was applied in three equal splits, at 15, 30, and 45 DAT of Binadhan-13. Thirty days old seedlings were transplanted into the well-prepared puddled field maintaining a row and hill distance of 25 cm and 15 cm, respectively. The cultivar mixture ratio for experiment 1 was maintained in accordance with following the experimental treatments during transplantation (Fig. 1). In case of experiment 2, seedlings of Binadhan-13 were transplanted on 3rd September 2018; seedlings of Dhani Gold were transplanted on 28th August, 3rd and 10th September as per treatment requirement following mixture ratio of 1:1 where Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold were planted in alternate row. It needed four manual weedings through hand pulling to maintain weed-free condition. As it rained frequently while the crop was growing, irrigation was not necessary, and the water was drained out before the crop reached maturity. Neither notable disease outbreaks nor insect infestations affected the crop during this time. In light of this, no plant protection actions were implemented.

Data collection and harvesting

Data on plant height, tillering ability, yield-contributing components, and rice yield were recorded at harvest. Except for rice yield, all information was gathered from five randomly chosen hills on each plot. To record grain and straw yields, central one square meter from each plot was manually cut at ground level when 90 % of the grains had turned golden yellow. Each plot's harvested crops was individually packed, appropriately labelled, and taken to the threshing floor. The harvested crop was manually threshed. After being cleaned, the grains were dried to a moisture content of 14 %. Straws were appropriately dried in the sun. The final yields of grain and straw from plot−1 were noted and adjusted to t ha−1.

Statistical analysis of data

Data recorded for various parameters were properly collated, tabulated and statistically analyzed. Using the MSTAT-C software tool, the analysis of variance was carried out. Duncan's Multiple Range Test was used to adjudge the mean differences between the treatments [12].

Discussion

Spatial deployment of different mixture ratios of fine and coarse grain varieties on rice productivity and profitability

The term "cultivar mixtures" refers to combinations of cultivated varieties that are grown concurrently on the same plot of land without an effort to breed for phenotypic consistency [4]. Farmers with limited resources can readily implement the cultivar mixture method because all they need to do is combine existing cultivars with different agronomic features and performance. As a result, cultivar mixing can increase functional diversity and boost output by giving cultivars more opportunities for fruitful interactions.

The purpose of this study was to compare the combined yield performance and profitability of Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold, which were interplanted in various row ratios. In this research, the interplanting of Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold in various row ratios was done to evaluate the combined yield performance and profitability. Binadhan-13 is a tall, late-maturing, fine-grained fragrant rice cultivar with a high market price, whereas Dhani Gold is a semi-dwarf, mid-maturing, coarse-grained rice cultivar with a low market price. The study clearly demonstrates the advantages of mixed culture of rice cultivars over solitary culture in terms of growth and yield.

In case of both cultivars, mixed culture produced more effective tillers hill−1, grains panicle−1, and grain weight hill−1 than either cultivar's solitary culture did. The difference in agronomic traits of both of the cultivars may have complementary effect on each other which improved their yield contributing characters.

Result reveals that the solo cultivation of Dhani Gold produced the maximum grain yield, which was followed by mixture ratios of 2:3, 2:4, and 4:2. According to many experts [9,13], mixing ratio affects component cultivars' competitiveness and, eventually, yield.

The cost-benefit analysis revealed that solitary rice farming had lower total variable costs than intercrop combinations. The differences were mostly due to variable seed price of the cultivars and labor requirements for transplanting and harvesting operations. Dhani Gold is a coarse grain hybrid rice, so it yielded higher than Binadhan-13. On the other hand, Binadhan-13 yielded lower than Dhani Gold due to small grain size, but its fine and aromatic grains have much higher market price than that of Dhani Gold. Sole culture of Dhani Gold and mixed culture produced higher BCR than sole culture of Binadhan-13. Mixed cultures clearly outperformed solitary cultures of both cultivars when grown in a 2:3, 2:4, or 4:2 ratio (Binadhan-13: Dhani Gold).

Therefore, it can be said that both cultivars yielded more when grown in a mixture as opposed to a sole culture since they grew better in the mixture. While Dhani Gold's solo culture had the highest yield, it was statistically equivalent to mixed yields when Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold were cultivated in mixtures at 2:1, 3:1, and 4:1 ratios. The maximum net return and BCR were reported when Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold were grown in mixture following a 4:2 ratio as opposed to solitary Binadhan-13 cultivation, which provided the lowest net return and BCR. Thus, a clear advantage of mixed culture than sole culture of either cultivar is evident from this study. So, it is advised to plant fine grain rice cultivar Binadhan-13 in a 2:4 ratio with coarse grain hybrid cultivar Dhani Gold for a better yield and greater financial return.

Temporal deployment effect of fine and coarse grain varietal mixture on rice productivity

To maximize the benefits of cultivar combination in terms of rice growth, yield, and weed suppression, two different rice cultivars, Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold, were interplanted in the current study at a 1:1 row ratio in different temporal dimensions. It was predicted that the two cultivars' opposing characteristics would assure maximum resource use, which would lead to less weed growth and greater production. In terms of growth, yield, and economic gain, mixed rice cultivar cultures are superior than single cultures in the temporal dimension.

In the present research, growth of both rice cultivars was observed in terms of plant height and tillering capacity. During the growing season, the temporal deployment of rice cultivars boosted plant height and capacity for tillering of both cultivars; although transplanting of Dhani Gold in temporal dimension resulted in similar effect. However, compared to monocultures of either variety, planting both cultivars on the same day had no favorable impact on plant height or tillering ability, indicating that there is no benefit to spatial deployment. According to Ref. [14], this was mostly caused by the cultivars' complimentary utilization of resources. Because the component cultivars have different patterns for using resources, when more than one cultivar is grown together, both above- and below-ground resources are used more effectively than in a single culture [15]. Only when component cultivars have different plant architectures and development rates, then the complementary effect can take action. Dhani Gold matured within 110 days, but Binadhan-13 took 140 days to reach maturity. Additionally, Binadhan-13 was significantly taller than Dhani Gold, ensuring superior resource use in mixed culture, which finally resulted in increased plant height and tillering capacity.

The temporal deployment of cultivar combination benefited the number of effective tillers hill−1, grains panicle−1, and grain weight hill−1 among the yield components, but did not favor sterile spikelets panicle−1 and 1000-grain weight. There are no advantages to spatial deployment of cultivars on rice yield components, as shown by the identical effects on yield parameters achieved by planting both cultivars in solo cultures of either cultivar. According to Refs. [9,10], this was mostly caused by the cultivars' complimentary utilization of resources. When two cultivars are cultivated in combination, both of the component cultivars get a greater capacity to adapt under varied biotic and abiotic stressors and different limiting resources, leading to higher efficiency than solitary culture [16].

Although planting Dhani Gold on the same day as Binadhan-13 and before or after Binadhan-13 resulted in identical grain weight hill−1, the timing of the deployment of the rice cultivar combination has a noticeable benefit in this scenario. In terms of grain weight hill−1, solo cultures of Binadhan-13 and Dhani Gold fared equally well. This demonstrates that unless and until temporal deployment is mandated, spatial layout alone is insufficient to achieve the benefits of cultivar mixing. Ref. [16] found similar findings and claimed that the spatial layout of the rice had no effect on the system's production.

Total yield in the combined culture was higher than the yield from Binadhan-13 alone, but it was equivalent to the yield from Dhani Gold alone. As Dhani Gold was a coarse grain cultivar (Weight of 1000 grains = 23 g), its production potential was substantially larger than that of a fine cultivar (Weight of 1000 grains = 14 g) Binadhan-13. In comparison to each cultivar's sole culture, yield of both cultivars rose when they were interplanted with temporal deployment. As a result of the facilitation effect [17], one component cultivar aids another component by reducing lodging, improving the microclimate, and minimizing various biotic and abiotic stressors [18]. The climatic variations brought on by the cultivar mixing were not monitored here, nor was lodging. The facilitation effect may be applicable here because the cultivar mixing lowered weed infestation. Additionally, decreased lodging of tall cultivar Binadhan-13 owing to physical assistance from short cultivar Dhani Gold may also promote to higher system yield.

The solo rice cultivars Dhani Gold (6.25 t ha−1) and Binadhan-13 (3.07 t ha−1) produced the best and lowest grain yields, respectively, however the mixed culture of rice cultivars produced statistically identical results, as indicated in Table 3. Variations in rice yield may be related to genetic characteristics, the frequency of diseases and pests, and crop response to the environment. According to Ref. [19], a crop's genetic composition determines its development and output.

This study recorded the cost of total variable, gross return, net return, and benefit cost ratio (BCR) of several treatments. Total variable cost was the lowest (1,11,200 BDT ha−1) in sole culture of Binadhan-13, while in sole Dhani Gold, it was calculated the highest (1,11,600 BDT ha−1). The differences were mostly due to variable seed price of the cultivars. The gross return varied widely (1,53,375 -2,09,175 BDT ha−1) among the treatments mostly due to the huge differences in yield and grain price between the cultivars. Dhani Gold is a coarse grain hybrid rice, so it yielded higher than Binadhan-13. On the other hand, Binadhan-13 yielded lower than Dhani Gold due to small grain size, but its fine and aromatic grains have much higher market price than that of Dhani Gold. The net return varied between 42,175 and 93,775 BDT ha−1 among the treatments. Cultivation of sole Binadhan-13 produced the lowest net return, while the highest net return was recorded when Dhani Gold was planted 7 days before Binadhan-13 in mixture. Sole culture of Binadhan-13 produced the lowest BCR of 1.37. A clear economic advantage of mixed culture with temporal deployment than sole culture of either cultivar was evident. But, the highest BCR (1.81) was obtained when Dhani Gold was planted 7 days before Binadhan-13 in mixture.

Findings of the present study confirm that merely spatial organization did not generate any advantages over sole culture of the rice cultivar. Therefore, Dhani Gold may be transplanted in a 1:1 ratio seven days prior to Binadhan-13 for increased production and economic benefits.

Limitations

Present study was carried out only for one year in a research farm. The practical issues and anticipated difficulties in crop establishment, water-nutrient-pest management, harvesting, threshing and milling and marketing were not analyzed in this study while designing the system for mixing rice cultivars. The socioeconomic need of the farmers was not considered at all while selecting component cultivars.

Future research

Present study was conducted stressing only the agronomic aspect of cultivar mixture strategy. Further studies are needed emphasizing on i) selecting criteria of component cultivars ii) elucidating planting proportion and pattern effect on weed competitiveness and insect/disease resistance iii) understanding ecological and genetic bases of yield advantages. Farmer participatory methods can be pragmatic to develop a cultivar mixture strategy that suits the socioeconomic and cultural needs of the farmers of a locality.

Policy implications

Increasing rice yield and productivity in a sustainable way through adopting cultivar mixture strategy appears to be more promising in near future. Recent advancement in genomics and phenotyping allows more options to select the right combination and spatio-temporal deployment of rice cultivars now than before. Moreover, selection of component rice cultivars functionally diverse in terms of weed competitiveness, insect and disease resistance, stress tolerance and agronomic traits will help grow rice more successfully and profitably in the coming days with changed climate. Thus, the findings of the present study have huge impact on formulating national or regional policy for rice production to ensure global food security.

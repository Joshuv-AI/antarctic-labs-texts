I. K. Kozlov

Abstract

. We can "split" the nondegenerate pencils
\mathcal{P}^{z}
using
[
12
, Lemma 2]
. Alternatively, one can use the splitting theorem for Nijenhuis operators (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
). We get coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{k},u_{1},\dots u_{s}
such that the matrices of the pencils
\mathcal{P}^{z}
are block-diagonal:

\mathcal{P}^{z}=\left(\begin{matrix}C^{1}_{\lambda}(x_{1},z)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&D^{s}_{\lambda}(u_{s},z)\end{matrix}\right).

Since
z_{i}
are Casimir function, the pencil
\mathcal{P}
takes the form (
13
). Theorem
4.3
is proved. ∎

5
Standard integrals

Standard integrals were defined in Definition
1.2
. The next statement is proved similar to
[
9
, Lemma 5.1, Lemma 5.3]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
v=\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}dH_{\lambda}
be a system that is bi-Hamiltonian w.r.t. a pencil
\mathcal{P}=\left\{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\right\}
. The family of standard integrals
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is an admissible family of functions in bi-involution. The standard integrals
\mathcal{F}
on
M
are first integrals of the bi-Hamiltonian system.

In the proof of Lemma
5.1
we should replace the following statement (which is
[
9
, Lemma 5.2]
) with its analog Lemma
5.3
for complex conjugate eigenvalues.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\left\{\mathcal{A}+\lambda\mathcal{B}\right\}
be a Poisson pencil on a manifold
M
. For any JK-regular point
x\in(M,\mathcal{P})
and any finite eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}(x)<\infty
we have

(\mathcal{A}-\lambda_{i}(x)\mathcal{B})d\lambda_{i}(x)=0.

(14)

The next statement follows from Theorem
4.3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\left\{\mathcal{A}+\lambda\mathcal{B}\right\}
be a Poisson pencil on real manifold
M
and
\lambda(x)=\alpha(x)+i\beta(x)
be its complex eigenvalue on
M
. Then almost everywhere on
M
we have

d\lambda(x)=d\alpha(x)+i\cdot d\beta(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}_{-\lambda(x)}^{\mathbb{C}}+\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{C}}.

(15)

Here at each point
x\in M
we complexify the cotangent space
T^{*}M
and extend
\mathcal{A}(x)
and
\mathcal{B}(x)
to the skew-symmetric forms
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{C}}(x)
and
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(x)
on
(T^{*}M)^{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of the core distribution
\mathcal{K}
and

\operatorname{Ker}_{-\lambda(x)}^{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{C}}(x)-\lambda(x)\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(x)\right).

6
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof is in several steps:

1.

Perform bi-Poisson reduction w.r.t.
d\mathcal{F}
(see Section
4
). Note that
d\mathcal{F}
satisfies conditions of Theorem
4.2
by Lemma
5.1
(also,
\mathcal{K}\subseteq d\mathcal{F}
by Definition
1.3
). The next statement easily follows from Theorem
4.2
and Assertion
4.1
.

Assertion 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
denote the Poisson pencil that results from performing bi-Poisson reduction w.r.t. the family of standard integrals
\mathcal{F}
. The following properties hold:

(a)

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a Poisson pencil with common Casimirs. In other words, in some local coordinates
(f,q)=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n_{1}},q_{1},\dots,q_{n_{2}})
the pencil has the form

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\left(\begin{matrix}C_{\lambda}(f,q)&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right),

(16)

where the pencils
\left\{C_{\lambda}(f,q)\right\}
are nondegenerate for fixed
z
.

(b)

All eigenvalues of the pencil
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are its common Casimir functions. For complex eigenvalues
\alpha_{j}(x)+i\beta_{j}(x)
the real part
\alpha_{j}(x)
and the imaginary part
\beta_{j}(x)
are common Casimir functions.

(c)

All eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are also eigenvalues of the original pencil
\mathcal{P}
.

As we proceed, we substitute the pencil
\mathcal{P}
with the reduced pencil
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.

2.

Reduction to the case of one real eigenvalue or a pair of complex conjugate complex eigenvalues
. After the bi-Poisson reductions, the pencil
\mathcal{P}
has the form (
16
). Hence, we can use Theorem
4.3
. By selecting the appropriate coordinates
x_{j},z
or
u_{j},z
we can effectively reduce the general case to a simpler scenario involving either a single eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}(x)
or a pair of complex conjugate eigenvalues
\alpha_{j}(x)\pm i\beta_{j}(x)
.

3.

Case of a single real eigenvalue
. Assume that after the previous step there is only one eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(x)
and it is a common Casimir function for all brackets

\lambda_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{C}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\right),\qquad\forall\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{C}}.

Without the loss of generality,
\lambda_{1}(x)=0
, since by Assertion
3.2
we can replace the pencil
\mathcal{P}
with the new pencil

\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\left\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}+\lambda\mathcal{B}\right\},\qquad\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}-\lambda_{1}(x)\mathcal{B}.

By Theorem
3.1
the considered Poisson pencil
\left\{\mathcal{A}+\lambda\mathcal{B}\right\}
is locally flat. Thus, we can take local coordinates in which the pencil has constant coefficients

\mathcal{A}^{ij}(x)=\operatorname{const},\qquad\mathcal{B}^{ij}(x)=\operatorname{const}.

The family of functions
\mathcal{F}
can be easily extended to a complete family of functions
\mathcal{G}
that are in bi-involution. This can be accomplished by incorporating the coordinate functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}
that define the bi-Lagrangian subspace
L
.

4.

Case of a pair of complex conjugate complex eigenvalues
. Assume that after Step
2
there is a pair of complex conjugate eigenvalues
\alpha(x)\pm i\beta(x)
. Without loss of generality, the pencil
\mathcal{P}
has the form (
16
). Complexify each symplectic leaf
\left\{q_{j}=\operatorname{const}\right\}
similarly to how it is done in
[
12
, Section 6]
or how it is done for Nijenhuis operators in
[
2
, Section 3.3]
. In short, the semi-simple part of the recursion operator defines a complex structure. Take the function

f(z)=\begin{cases}i,\quad\operatorname{Im}z>0,\\
-i,\quad\operatorname{Im}z<0.\end{cases}

By
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]

J=f(P)
, where
P
is the recursion operator, is a complex structure
J^{2}=-\operatorname{id}
. After complexification on each symplectic leaf
\left\{q_{j}=\operatorname{const}\right\}
we get a nondegenerate Poisson pencil with one complex eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}(x)=\alpha(x)+i\beta(x)
. We use the following statement.

Assertion 6.2
.

The Poisson bracket

\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}-\lambda_{0}(x)\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}-\alpha(x)\mathcal{B}-\beta(x)J\circ\mathcal{B}

is compatible with
\mathcal{B}
. The pencil
\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\left\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}+\lambda\mathcal{B}\right\}
has one zero eigenvalue and, therefore, it is flat.

Proof of Assertion
6.2
.

By Assertion
3.1
, it suffices to prove that
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{B}
are compatible on each symplectic leaf
S_{q}=\left\{q_{j}=\operatorname{const}\right\}
. By Assertion
6.1
, the functions
\alpha(x)
and
\beta(x)
are constants on each symplectic leaf
S_{q}
. Hence, on each leaf the complexified
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}-\lambda_{0}(x)\mathcal{B}
is a linear combination with constant coefficients and it is compatible with
\mathcal{B}
. The pencil
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
is flat by Theorem
3.1
. Assertion
6.2
is proved. ∎

By Corollary
2.1
the bi-Lagrangian subspace
L
is also bi-Lagrangian for
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
. In coordinates where
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
has constant coefficients, we construct a complete family of functions
\mathcal{G}
for both
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}
by selecting coordinate functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}
, similar to the case of one real eigenvalue.

Theorem
1.2
is proved.

Remark 6.1
.

Note that after bi-Poisson reduction w.r.t. standard integrals the induced pencil
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
becomes flat when restricted to each common symplectic leaf (i.e.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is
leaf-wise flat
). By applying Turiel’s local coordinates from
[
12
]
to each leaf, we can locally bring
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to the form as in the Jordan-Kronecker theorem, but with eigenvalues
\lambda(z)
depending on the common Casimir functions.

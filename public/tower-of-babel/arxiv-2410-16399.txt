Abstract
. For any given submersion π : X → B \pi:X\to B with closed, oriented and spin c fibers of even dimension, equipped with a Riemannian and differential spin c structure, we apply the Atiyah–Singer–Gorokhovsky–Lott approach to the local family index theorem without the kernel bundle assumption to construct an analytic index ind k a \ind^{a}_{k} in odd Z / k ​ Z \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z} K K -theory at the cocycle level. This is achieved by associating to every cocycle ( 𝐄 , 𝐅 , α ) (\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F},\alpha) of the odd Z / k ​ Z \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z} K K -theory group of X X a cocycle ind k a ⁡ ( 𝐄 , 𝐅 , α ) \ind^{a}_{k}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F},\alpha) of the odd Z / k ​ Z \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z} K K -theory group of B B . We also prove a Riemann–Roch–Grothendieck-type formula in odd Z / k ​ Z \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z} K K -theory, which expresses the Cheeger–Chern–Simons form of ind k a ⁡ ( 𝐄 , 𝐅 , α ) \ind^{a}_{k}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F},\alpha) in terms of that of ( 𝐄 , 𝐅 , α ) (\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F},\alpha) . Furthermore, we show that the analytic index ind k a \ind^{a}_{k} and the Riemann–Roch–Grothendieck-type formula in odd Z / k ​ Z \mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z} K K -theory refine the underlying geometric bundle of the analytic index and the Riemann–Roch–Grothendieck theorem in R / Z \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} K K -theory, respectively.

cannot be made trivial to begin with.

If our goal was just to obtain the extended variational formula for the Bismut–Cheeger eta form in the ASGL approach, we could have applied Proposition

) along the lines similar to those in

2.1.
Background material

In this paper,
X
and
B
are closed manifolds and
I
is the closed interval
[0,1]
. Given a manifold
X
, define
{\widetilde{X}}=X\times I
. Given
t\in I
, define a map
i_{X,t}:X\to{\widetilde{X}}
by
i_{X,t}(x)=(x,t)
. Denote by
p_{X}:{\widetilde{X}}\to X
the standard projection map. For any differential forms
\omega
and
\eta
, we write
\omega\equiv\eta
if
\omega-\eta\in\im(d)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
\pi:M\to B
a smooth fiber bundle with compact fibers of dimension
n
which satisfies certain orientability assumptions. Then

\intop\nolimits_{M/B}\pi^{*}\alpha\wedge\beta=\alpha\wedge\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{M/B}\beta\bigg)

for any
\alpha\in{}^{\bullet}(B)
and
\beta\in{}^{\bullet}(M)
. If
M
has nonempty boundary, then Stokes’ theorem for integration along the fibers
[
12
, Problem 4 of Chapter VII]
states that for any
\omega\in{}^{k}(M)
,

(-1)^{k-n+1}\intop\nolimits_{\partial M/B}i^{*}\omega=\intop\nolimits_{M/B}d^{M}\omega-d^{B}\intop\nolimits_{M/B}\omega,

(2.1)

where
i:\partial M\hookrightarrow M
is the inclusion map.

Let
\bun_{\nabla}(X)
be the category of geometric bundles
\mathbf{E}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
, where
E\to X
is a complex vector bundle equipped with a Hermitian metric
g^{E}
and a unitary connection
\nabla^{E}
. Given any two geometric bundles
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{F}
, a morphism
\alpha:\mathbf{E}\to\mathbf{F}
is an isometric isomorphism
\alpha:(E,g^{E})\to(F,g^{F})
of the underlying Hermitian bundles. Throughout this paper, a morphism
T:\mathbf{E}\to\mathbf{F}
between geometric bundles always means the one in
\bun_{\nabla}(X)
, but not the morphism between the underlying bundles in
\bun(X)
. Since every morphism in
\bun_{\nabla}(X)
is an isomorphism,
\bun_{\nabla}(X)
is a groupoid. But we will not use this fact. A geometric bundle
\mathbf{E}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
is said to be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded if
E\to X
,
g^{E}
and
\nabla^{E}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle
\mathbf{E}
, denote by
\mathbf{E}^{\pm}
the even and the odd part of
\mathbf{E}
, respectively, and by
\mathbf{E}^{\op}
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle whose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading is opposite to that of
\mathbf{E}
. Given a geometric bundle
\mathbf{E}
, denote by
{\widehat{\mathbf{E}}}
the associated
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle defined by
{\widehat{\mathbf{E}}}^{\pm}=\mathbf{E}
. Such
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundles are said to be elementary. For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, write
\mathbf{C}^{\ell}=(\mathbb{C}^{\ell},g^{\ell},d^{\ell})
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\to X
is the trivial complex vector bundle of rank
\ell
, and
g^{\ell}
and
d^{\ell}
are the standard metric and the standard connection on
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\to X
, respectively.

We now recall the definition of a split quadruple
[
7
, Definition 2.9]
. Let
\mathbf{W}
be a geometric bundle over
B
. The map
s_{{\widehat{W}}}:{\widehat{W}}\to{\widehat{W}}
defined by

s_{{\widehat{W}}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\id\\
\id&0\end{pmatrix}

(2.2)

is odd self-adjoint. Then
({\widehat{\mathbf{W}}},s_{{\widehat{W}}})
is an example of split quadruple. Fix
a\in(0,1)
. Let
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to I
be a smooth function that satisfies
\alpha(t)=0
for all
t\leq a
and
\alpha(t)=1
for all
t\geq 1
. Define a rescaled superconnection on
{\widehat{W}}\to B
by

\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t}=\sqrt{t}\alpha(t)s_{{\widehat{W}}}+\nabla^{{\widehat{W}}}.

(2.3)

Define the Bismut–Cheeger eta form associated to
\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t}
by

{\widehat{\eta}}^{{\widehat{W}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\intop\nolimits^{\infty}_{0}\str\bigg(\frac{d\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t}}{dt}e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi i}(\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t})^{2}}\bigg)dt.

Since

\str\bigg(\frac{d\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t}}{dt}e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi i}(\mathbb{A}^{{\widehat{W}}}_{t})^{2}}\bigg)=0

by
[
7
, (2.24)]
, it follows that

{\widehat{\eta}}^{{\widehat{W}}}=0.

(2.4)

We now recall a morphism between geometric bundles defined by parallel transport. Let
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{E}}=(\mathscr{E},g^{\mathscr{E}},\nabla^{\mathscr{E}})
be a geometric bundle over
{\widetilde{X}}
. Let
j\in\{0,1\}
and define a geometric bundle
\mathbf{E}_{j}
over
X
by
\mathbf{E}_{j}=i_{X,j}^{*}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{E}}
. Note that for each
p\in X
,

(E_{j})_{p}=(i_{X,j}^{*}\mathscr{E})_{p}=\mathscr{E}_{(p,j)}.

For each fixed
p\in X
, define a smooth curve
c_{p}:I\to{\widetilde{X}}
by
c_{p}(t)=(p,t)
. The linear map
P_{c_{p}}:\mathscr{E}_{(p,0)}\to\mathscr{E}_{(p,1)}
given by the parallel transport associated to
\nabla^{\mathscr{E}}
along
c_{p}
is independent of the choices of reparameterizations of
c_{p}
and the representation of
c_{p}
as a concatenation of piecewise smooth curves. Write
\pi:E_{0}\to X
for the bundle projection. The map

P_{01}:\mathbf{E}_{0}\to\mathbf{E}_{1}

(2.5)

defined by

P_{01}(e):=P_{c_{\pi(e)}}(e)

is a morphism (see, for example,
[
1

) and is uniquely determined by
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{E}}
and
c_{p}
.

2.2.
Chern–Simons form

Let
\mathbf{E}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
be a geometric bundle over
X
. Denote by
R^{E}
the curvature of
\nabla^{E}
. The Chern character form of
\nabla^{E}
is defined to be

\ch(\nabla^{E})=\tr(e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi i}R^{E}}),

and the first Chern form of
\nabla^{E}
is defined to be

c_{1}(\nabla^{E})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\tr(R^{E}).

Let
\mathbf{E}_{0}=(E,g^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{0})
and
\mathbf{E}_{1}=(E,g^{E}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{1})
be geometric bundles over
X
. By
[
15
, Theorem 8.8 of Chapter I]
, there exists a unique
f\in\Aut(E)
such that
g^{E}_{0}=f^{*}g^{E}_{1}
. Thus
f^{*}\nabla^{E}_{1}
is unitary with respect to
g^{E}_{0}
. For each
t\in I
, let
f_{t}=(1-t)\id_{E}+tf^{-1}
. Then

g^{E}_{t}=f_{t}^{*}g^{E}_{0}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\nabla^{E}_{t}=f_{t}^{*}\big((1-t)\nabla^{E}_{0}+tf^{*}\nabla^{E}_{1}\big)

(2.6)

are smooth paths of Hermitian metrics and unitary connections from
g^{E}_{0}
to
g^{E}_{1}
and from
\nabla^{E}_{0}
to
\nabla^{E}_{1}
, respectively. Note that
\nabla^{E}_{t}
is unitary with respect to
g^{E}_{t}
for each
t\in I
. Define a geometric bundle
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{E}}=(\mathscr{E},g^{\mathscr{E}},\nabla^{\mathscr{E}})
over
{\widetilde{X}}
by
\mathscr{E}=p_{X}^{*}E
,

g^{\mathscr{E}}=p_{X}^{*}g^{E}_{t}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\nabla^{\mathscr{E}}=dt\wedge\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}(g^{E}_{t})^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{E}_{t}\bigg)+\nabla^{E}_{t}.

(2.7)

Note that
i_{X,j}^{*}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbf{E}_{j}
for
j\in\{0,1\}
. The Chern–Simons form
\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1})\in\frac{{}^{\odd}(X)}{\im(d)}
is defined by

\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1}):=-\intop\nolimits_{{\widetilde{X}}/X}\ch(\nabla^{\mathscr{E}})\mod\im(d).

Note that
\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1})
does not depend on the choice of
(g^{\mathscr{E}},\nabla^{\mathscr{E}})
satisfying
i_{X,j}^{*}(g^{\mathscr{E}},\nabla^{\mathscr{E}})=(g^{E}_{j},\nabla^{E}_{j})
, and satisfies the following transgression formula:

d\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1})=\ch(\nabla^{E}_{1})-\ch(\nabla^{E}_{0}).

Equivalently, the Chern–Simons form can be defined as

\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits^{1}_{0}\tr\bigg(\frac{d\nabla^{E}_{t}}{dt}e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi i}R^{E}_{t}}\bigg)dt.

(2.8)

The choices of 0 and 1 are immaterial. If
t<T
are two fixed positive real numbers, then one can replace 0 by
t
and 1 by
T
in (
2.8
).

The Chern–Simons form satisfies the following properties:

\displaystyle\CS(\nabla^{E}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv-\CS(\nabla^{E}_{0},\nabla^{E}_{1}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\CS(\nabla^{E}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv\CS(\nabla^{E}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{2})+\CS(\nabla^{E}_{2},\nabla^{E}_{0}),

(2.10)

\displaystyle\CS(\nabla^{E}_{1}\oplus\nabla^{F}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{0}\oplus\nabla^{F}_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv\CS(\nabla^{E}_{1},\nabla^{E}_{0})+\CS(\nabla^{F}_{1},\nabla^{F}_{0}),

(2.11)

where
\mathbf{F}_{0}=(F,g^{F}_{0},\nabla^{F}_{0})
and
\mathbf{F}_{1}=(F,g^{F}_{1},\nabla^{F}_{1})
are geometric bundles over
X
. In particular, (
2.9
) implies

\CS(\nabla^{E},\nabla^{E})\equiv 0.

(2.12)

If
\mathbf{E}_{j}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded, then

\CS(\nabla_{0}^{E},\nabla^{E}_{1})\equiv\CS(\nabla_{0}^{E,+},\nabla_{1}^{E,+})-\CS(\nabla_{0}^{E,-},\nabla_{1}^{E,-}).

(2.13)

Now suppose
g^{F}_{0}=g^{F}_{1}
. Let
\alpha:\mathbf{E}\to\mathbf{F}_{j}
be a morphism. The naturality of Chern–Simons form implies

\CS(\alpha^{*}\nabla^{F}_{0},\alpha^{*}\nabla^{F}_{1})\equiv\CS(\nabla^{F}_{0},\nabla^{F}_{1}).

(2.14)

Let
(H,g^{H},\nabla^{H})
be a Riemannian bundle with a metric connection. Denote by
R^{H}
the curvature of
\nabla^{H}
. The
{\widehat{A}}
-genus form of
\nabla^{H}
is defined to be

{\widehat{A}}(\nabla^{H})=\sqrt{\det\bigg(\frac{-\frac{1}{4\pi i}R^{H}}{\sinh(-\frac{1}{4\pi i}R^{H})}\bigg)}\in{}^{4\bullet}(X).

(3,0)

(3.2)

. Since

3.2.
The Atiyah–Singer–Gorokhovsky–Lott approach

For the details of the ASGL approach to the local FIT without the kernel bundle assumption, we refer the readers to
[
11
, §4, 5]
for the details.

Atiyah–Singer prove that there exist a finite rank complex vector bundle
H\to B
and a linear map
s:H\to(\pi_{*}E)^{-}
such that

\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}+s:(\pi_{*}E)^{+}\oplus H\to(\pi_{*}E)^{-}

is surjective
[
4
, Proposition 2.2]
. Here,
\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}+s
is understood to be
(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}\oplus 0)+(0\oplus s)
. Note that
H\to B
can always be taken as a trivial bundle. Such a pair
(H,s)
is said to satisfy the AS property for
\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}
.

Define maps
\Delta_{\pm}:(\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}})^{\pm}\to(\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}})^{\mp}
by

\Delta_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}0&s\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\Delta_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The map
\Delta:\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}\to\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}
defined by

\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Delta_{-}\\
\Delta_{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.3)

is odd self-adjoint, and the same is true for the map

\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}:=(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}\oplus 0)+\Delta:\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}\to\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}.

With respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}\to B
,
\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}
can be written as

\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{-}&0\\
0&0&s^{*}&0\\
\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}&s&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

In particular,

\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}&s\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{-}&0\\
s^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}+s
is surjective, the vector bundle
\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}+s\big)\to B
exists and is a finite rank subbundle of
(\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}})^{+}\to B
. Thus the vector bundle
\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{+}\big)\to B
exists and

\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{+}\big)=\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E}_{+}+s\big).

(3.4)

On the other hand, the vector bundle
\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{-}\big)\to B
exists and

\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{-}\big)\cong\cok\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{+}\big)=\frac{(\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}})^{-}}{\im\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}_{+}\big)}\cong{\widehat{H}}^{-}.

(3.5)

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), the vector bundle

\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big)\to B

exists and is a finite rank
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded subbundle of
\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}\to B
. There is an orthogonal decomposition

\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}=\im\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big)\oplus\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big).

(3.6)

The analytic index
\ind^{a}([E])
in
K
-theory is given by

\ind^{a}([E])=\big[\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big)^{+}\big]-\big[\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big)^{-}\big].

By putting a Hermitian metric
g^{H}
and a unitary connection
\nabla^{H}
on
H\to B
, we write
\mathbf{H}
for the geometric bundle
(H,g^{H},\nabla^{H})
. Denote by
g^{\ker(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta})}
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hermitian metric on
\ker\big(\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}\big)\to B
inherited from
g^{\pi_{*}E}\oplus g^{{\widehat{H}}}
.

Define a rescaled Bismut superconnection on
\pi_{*}E\oplus{\widehat{H}}\to B
by

{\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}}^{E;\Delta}_{t}=\sqrt{t}\mathsf{D}^{S\otimes E;\Delta}+\big(\nabla^{\pi_{*}E,u}\oplus\nabla^{{\widehat{H}}}\big)-\frac{c(T)}{4\sqrt{t}},

(3.7)

where
T
is given by (
3.2
) and the last term on the right-hand side of (
3.7

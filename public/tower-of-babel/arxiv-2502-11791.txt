Forbidden configurations and dominating bicliques in undirected 2-quasi best match graphs Journal: Discrete Applied Mathematics
Annachiara Korchmaros, Peter F. Stadler

Abstract
2-quasi best match graphs (2-qBMGs) are directed graphs that capture a notion of close relatedness in phylogenetics. Here, we investigate the undirected underlying graph of a 2-qBMG (un-2qBMG) and show that they contain neither a path P l P_{l} nor a cycle C l C_{l} of length l ≥ 6 l\geq 6 as an induced subgraph. This property guarantees the existence of specific vertex decompositions with dominating bicliques that provide further insights into their structure.

Introduction

Relationships between genes that are of key interest in evolutionary
biology give rise to vertex colored graphs that are defined in terms of
phylogenetic trees that model evolutionary history. This binary relationship can be inferred from data, at least in principle, prompting the
development of a method to infer the trees from the relational data, see
e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
A common starting point to infer pairs of orthologous genes are Best Match
Graphs (BMGs). The basic idea is to capture pairs of
genes
a
and
b
from species
A
and
B
, respectively, that are
evolutionarily most closely related.
They are directed vertex colored graphs and several characterizations of
BMGs have been established in

[
2
,
5
]
. In
particular, several subgraphs in BMGs give rise to binary trees on three
leaves that must or must not be displayed by any tree that explains these
BMGs. This and additional properties yield a classical characterization of
BMGs
[
2
, Theorem 9]
. Equivalently, BMGs are
precisely those properly colored graphs that are so-called color-sink-free
quasi-best match graphs (qBMGs)
[
4
, Theorem 4.3]
, i.e. no vertex has empty out-neighborhood.
In particular, quasi-best match graphs form a natural generalization of
BMGs used to keep only the best matches that are evolutionary not
too far in the past
[
4
]
.
Two-colored qBMGs (2-qBMG) arise not only as a special case of
qBMGs. Importantly, every subgraph of a qBMG induced by a pair of colors
is a 2-qBMG. While all known characterizations of qBMGs explicitly
involve the tree structure on which the best matches are defined,
tree-free characterizations have been derived for the two-colored case:

Proposition 1.1
.

[
2
,
4
]

A two-colored quasi best match graph (2-qBMG) is a bipartite directed
simple graph
\overrightarrow{G}

that satisfied the following three properties:

if

and

A class of colored graphs related to BMGs is the class of reciprocal BMGs,
defined as the subgraphs of a BMG comprising all its symmetric edges, which
are of key interest in studying gene family

[

undirected graphs of BMGs and qBMGs, aiming to exploit profound results on

3
Dominating sets in 2-qBMGs

Theorem
2.2
, together with previous results, yields new
insights into vertex decompositions involving dominating biclique sets.

Definition 3.1
.

A connected 2-qBMG is of type (A) if its undirected underlying graph
has a vertex decomposition into a biclique and a stable set.

It may be observed that such a biclique is necessarily a dominating set.

Theorem 3.1

Every connected 2-qBMG has a vertex decomposition into connected 2-qBMGs

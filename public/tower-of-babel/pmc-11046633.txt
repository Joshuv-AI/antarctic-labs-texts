Unusual case of infertility reversal in a woman with atypical undiagnosed coeliac disease
Authors: Maisam Akroush, Farah Alsa'di, Myassar Al Zoubi
Published: 2024

Coeliac disease is a systematic autoimmune disorder, in which the precipitant is known to be gluten, that presents with extraintestinal and intestinal manifestations and affects almost 1% of the population.1, 2 In multiple cases, it is linked to several obstetric and gynaecological complications, particularly, infertility in females. The objective of this clinical report is to highlight the vitality for screening for coeliac disease in female patients with fertility of unknown cause in the presence of any gastrointestinal symptoms.

A 35-year-old female nurse, working at an IVF clinic, who has been trying to conceive for 10 years, presented to the clinic and demonstrated multiple non-specific gastrointestinal symptoms: abdominal distention and post-prandial bloating of 12 years duration, associated with intermittent vomiting, excessive belching and persistent iron deficiency anemia, that is non-responsive to oral supplementation. She also had irregular menstrual cycles, hypomenorrhea, anxiety, palpitations and myalgias. Past medical history showed hypothyroidism – patient on levothyroxine – and, notably, six failed IVF trials. Her symptoms raised the suspicion of coeliac disease, upon further investigations and retrieval of duodenal biopsies – according to the guidelines–the diagnosis was confirmed as a case of coeliac disease and the patient was instructed to adhere to a gluten free diet and was prescribed iron supplements; in addition, she was screened for associated health conditions and appropriate measures were taken.3, 4, 5 Eight months after her adherence to a strict gluten-free diet, the patient was capable of conceiving naturally and delivered a healthy baby. Notably so, it had been concluded that the patient's obstinate infertility had been a manifestation of an underlying coeliac disease condition.

It has been quite commonly reported in the literature the prevalence of cases of infertility of unknown cause associated with coeliac disease, although the exact pathophysiological route through which gluten intake in coeliac disease patients results in infertility is not known, two hypotheses have been proposed.6, 7 Thus, more studies should be executed to find the pathophysiological route in which infertility may occur secondary to coeliac disease to reach conclusions to assess the cost and risk-related factors of subjecting female patients with infertility of unknown cause to coeliac disease screening, which may pose a solution to a modifiable chronic issue that usually causes immense physical and psychological burdens on the patient.8, 9, 10

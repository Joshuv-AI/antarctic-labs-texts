Real Eigenvalues of Asymmetric Wishart Matrices: Expected Number, Global Density and Integrable Structure
Sung-Soo Byun, and, Kohei Noda

Abstract
We investigate the real eigenvalues of asymmetric Wishart matrices of size N N , indexed by the rectangular parameter ν ∈ ℕ \nu\in\mathbb{N} and the non-Hermiticity parameter τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \tau\in[0,1] . The rectangular parameter ν \nu is either fixed or proportional to N N . The non-Hermiticity parameter τ \tau is either fixed or τ = 1 − O ⁡ ( 1 / N ) \tau=1-O(1/N) , corresponding to the strongly and weakly non-Hermitian regimes, respectively. We establish a decomposition structure for the finite- N N correlation kernel of the real eigenvalues, which form Pfaffian point processes. Taking the symmetric limit τ = 1 \tau=1 , where the model reduces to the Laguerre orthogonal ensemble, this decomposition structure reduces to the known rank-one perturbation structure established by Adler, Forrester, Nagao, and van Moerbeke, as well as by Widom. Using the decomposition structure, we show that the expected number of real eigenvalues is proportional to N \sqrt{N} in the strongly non-Hermitian regime and to N N in the weakly non-Hermitian regime, providing explicit leading coefficients in both cases. Furthermore, we derive the limiting real eigenvalue densities, which recovers the Marchenko-Pastur distribution in the symmetric limit.

We now present the limiting density of the real eigenvalues.
First, recall that in the extremal symmetric case when
\tau=1

\rho_{\textup{MP},\varrho}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\sqrt{(\lambda_{+}-x)(x-\lambda_{-})}}{x},\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{\pm}=(\sqrt{\varrho+1}\pm 1)^{2}.

For a fixed

Remark 1.5

(Square root relation between complex and real eigenvalue densities at strong non-Hermiticity)
.

In
[
73

, Tarnowski introduced an interesting relation for general asymmetric random matrices at strong non-Hermiticity: the continuous part of the limiting density of real eigenvalues is proportional to the
square root
of the limiting density of complex eigenvalues evaluated on the real line. Furthermore, it was observed that this relation holds for certain known real and complex eigenvalue densities, such as those of product matrices.
In Theorem
1.2
(i), one can observe that such a square root relation also holds for asymmetric Wishart matrix at strong non-Hermiticity. Namely, up to a multiplicative constant, the density
\rho_{\tau,\varrho}^{s}
is the square root of that of the equilibrium measure (
1.17
).

Remark 1.6

(Interpolating properties)
.

By using the well-known asymptotic behaviours of the error function
[
64
, Eqs. (7.6.1), (7.12.1)]
and (
1.26
), it follows that the function
c(\alpha)
satisfies

c(\alpha)\sim\begin{cases}1&\text{as}\quad\alpha\to 0\,,\\
\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}&\text{as}\quad\alpha\to\infty\,.\end{cases}

(1.27)

These asymptotic behaviours allow us to observe the interpolating properties.
In the symmetric limit
\alpha\to 0
, it is obvious that the expected number of real eigenvalues equals the total number of eigenvalues, which is consistent with
Nc(\alpha)\sim N
as
\alpha\to 0
.
Conversely, in the non-Hermitian limit
\alpha\to\infty
, the asymptotic behaviour aligns with the result of Theorem
1.1
(i) at strong non-Hermiticity, since for
\tau
given in (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big(\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}c(\tau,\varrho)N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\sim\Big(\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda_{+}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{-}})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\frac{2N}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}.\end{split}

(1.28)

Similarly, one can also observe that

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\rho_{\alpha,\varrho}^{w}(x)=\rho_{\textup{MP},\varrho}(x),\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\rho_{\alpha,\varrho}^{w}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{4\sqrt{x}}.

(1.29)

Here, the latter limit can be interpreted as
\rho_{\tau,\varrho}^{s}(x)\big|_{\tau=1}
. Therefore, one can see that the density
\rho_{\alpha,\varrho}^{w}
also exhibits a natural interpolating property, recovering the Marchenko-Pastur law in the symmetric limit.

Remark 1.7

(Comparison with the elliptic Ginibre matrix)
.

The asymmetric Wishart matrix is often referred to as the chiral version of the elliptic Ginibre matrices.
As previously mentioned, analogous results to Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for the real eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre matrices were obtained in
[
36
,
45
,
46
,
24
]
.
In general, the analysis of asymmetric Wishart matrices is more technical and involved than that of the elliptic Ginibre matrices due to the additional rectangular parameter. Compared to
[
46
,
24
]
, determining the expected number of real eigenvalues is particularly more challenging. For the elliptic Ginibre matrices, the symmetry with respect to the origin leads to significant cancellations, particularly canceling the integral term in the
1
-point function (see
[
21
, Appendix A]
for more details). Furthermore, this simplification, along with the evaluation of the integral
[
21
, Eq. (A.15)]
involving the Hermite polynomial, allows the use of several properties of the hypergeometric function. However, such a simplification is no longer valid in our present case due to the absence of both the cancellations and an integral evaluation. In addition, contrary to the elliptic Ginibre matrices, where the associated potential is given by an exponential, the potential for the asymmetric Wishart matrices involves the modified Bessel function. This makes it impossible to utilise many advantageous properties, particularly the factorisation of exponentials.

We conclude this section with a comment on our result on the integrable structure, Theorem
2.1
below, which we regard as one of the most significant contributions of our work.
In the study of correlation kernels of random matrices with real entries, one of the few successful approaches for asymptotic analysis involves establishing a relationship with their complex counterparts, for which more analytical techniques are available. This idea was first applied to the classical orthogonal ensembles
[
2
,
76
]
, where it was shown that the correlation kernel of real matrices can be decomposed into two components: one corresponding to the correlation kernel of their complex counterpart and the other expressed in terms of certain integrals involving the associated (skew-)orthogonal polynomials; cf. (
2.20
).
This relationship, often referred to as the
rank-one perturbation
of the correlation kernel, has proven to be highly useful in studying universality for Hermitian random matrices
[
34
]
, as well as in various applications, such as establishing connections between Toda and Pfaff lattices
[
3
]
.
However, beyond the classical ensembles, such a rank-one perturbation structure has been less extensively developed. (Nonetheless, recent progress has been made in
[
42
,
59
]
on the
q
-deformed models.)

For the real eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre matrices, such a decomposition structure was established by Forrester and Nagao in
[
45
,
46
]
. This result arises from remarkable manipulations of the correlation kernel, utilising various properties of Hermite polynomials. This decomposition has proven to be highly useful, as it facilitates the derivation of many important properties of the elliptic Ginibre matrices, including the expected number of real eigenvalues and their densities
[
46
,
24
,
8
]
, scaling limits
[
20
,
46
,
45
,
13
]
, finite-size corrections
[
25
,
17
]
, spectral moments
[
23
,
21
]
, fluctuations/large deviations in the number of real eigenvalues
[
26
,
41
,
57
]
, and eigenvector statistics
[
49
,
31
,
54
]
.

Beyond the elliptic Ginibre matrices, the decomposition structure has not been established for any other class of real asymmetric matrices, to the best of our knowledge. In the following section, we derive it for asymmetric Wishart matrices, which will serve as a crucial tool for the subsequent asymptotic analysis.
The statement in Theorem
2.1
requires introducing additional notation, so it is not included in this section. Nevertheless, from a technical perspective and considering its potential applications in future studies, we regard this as one of the most significant contributions of our work.
In addition, we stress that the decomposition structure in Theorem
2.1
is even new for the
\tau=0
, the product of rectangular GinOE matrices, while it recovers the previous findings in
[
2
,
76
]
for the
\tau=1
, the Laguerre orthogonal ensemble; see Remarks
2.1
and
2.2
for further details.

2.1
). Section

4

1.1
and

2
Integrable structure of real eigenvalues

In this section, we introduce the integrable structure of real eigenvalue and prove Theorem
2.1
.

As in the classical Laguerre orthogonal ensemble, the integrable properties of real eigenvalues of asymmtric Wishart matrices can be effectively described using
the generalised Laguerre polynomial

L_{j}^{(\nu)}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}{(j-k)!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k!}.

(2.1)

Following
[
12
]
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{2j}(x)&:=\tau^{2j}(2j)!\,L_{2j}^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr),\\
p_{2j+1}(x)&:=-\tau^{2j+1}(2j+1)!\,L_{2j+1}^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)+\tau^{2j-1}(2j)!(2j+\nu)\,L_{2j-1}^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr).\end{split}

(2.2)

The modified Bessel function
K_{\nu}
of the second kind (see e.g.
[
64
, Chapter 10]
) is defined by

K_{\nu}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(z)-I_{\nu}(z)}{\sin(\nu\pi)},

(2.3)

where
I_{\nu}
is given by (
1.7
).
We write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{j}(y)&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{sgn}(y-v)|v|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|v|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{\tau\,v}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)p_{j}(v)\,dv,\end{split}

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle r_{j}:=2\pi(1-\tau^{2})\Gamma(2j+1)\Gamma(2j+1+\nu).

(2.5)

These are building blocks to define

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N}(x,y)&:=|x|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{\tau\,x}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{p_{2j+1}(x)\Phi_{2j}(y)-\Phi_{2j+1}(x)p_{2j}(y)}{r_{j}}.\end{split}

(2.6)

It is well known that the real eigenvalues of an asymmetric Wishart matrix form a Pfaffian point process. To be more precise, the
k
-th correlation function
\mathbf{R}_{N,k}
can be expressed as

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})={\textup{Pf}}\Big[\mathbf{K}_{N}(x_{j},x_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

(2.7)

where
\mathbf{K}_{N}
is a
2\times 2
correlation kernel of the form

\mathbf{K}_{N}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{N}(x,y)&\mathbf{S}_{N}(x,y)\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
-\mathbf{S}_{N}(y,x)&\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{N}(x,y)\end{pmatrix}.

(2.8)

Here,
\mathbf{S}_{N}
is expressed in terms of
S_{N}
given in (
2.6
) as

\mathbf{S}_{N}(x,y)=NS_{N}(Nx,Ny);

(2.9)

see
[
12
,
14
]
.
The other functions
\mathbf{D}_{N}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{N}
are given in terms of
\mathbf{S}_{N}
as

\mathbf{D}_{N}(x,y):=-\frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbf{S}_{N}(x,y),\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{N}(x,y):=\int_{x}^{y}\mathbf{S}_{N}(t,y)\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(x-y).

(2.10)

In particular, the
1
-point function is written as

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)=\mathbf{S}_{N}(x,x)=NR_{N}(Nx),\hskip 20.00003ptR_{N}(x):=S_{N}(x,x).

(2.11)

As previously mentioned, in order to establish the decomposition structure, we define

\mathcal{K}_{N}(x,y)\equiv\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(\nu)}(x,y):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{y}{\tau}\Bigr).

(2.12)

This corresponds to the kernel of the complex non-Hermitian Wishart ensemble, which forms a determinantal point process; see e.g.
[
27
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
Decomposition structure of the correlation kernel
)
.

For any even integer
N
,
\tau\in(0,1)
and
\nu>-1
, we have

S_{N}(x,y)=S_{N,1}(x,y)+\widetilde{S}_{N,1}(x,y)+S_{N,2}(x,y),

(2.13)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N,1}(x,y)&:=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}|xy|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}\\
&\quad\times\bigg[\tau yK_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|y|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)+|y|K_{\nu/2+1}\Bigl(\frac{|y|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\bigg]e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}y}\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(x,y),\end{split}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{N,1}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\pi}y|xy|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|y|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(x+y)}\partial_{y}\mathcal{K}_{N-1}^{(\nu)}(x,y),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N,2}(x,y)&=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\tau^{2N-3}}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{(N-1)!}{\Gamma(N+\nu-1)}|x|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)\\
&\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,y)}(t)\Big)|t|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)\,dt.\end{split}

(2.16)

Theorem
2.1
also holds for
\tau=0
, where one needs to take the proper limits of the Laguerre polynomials. This results in the replacement of
L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z/\tau)
with monic polynomials that have the same leading coefficient.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.1
, the 1-point function
R_{N}
can be written as

R_{N}(x)=R_{N,1}(x)+\widetilde{R}_{N,1}(x)+R_{N,2}(x),

(2.17)

where

R_{N,1}(x):=S_{N,1}(x,x),\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{R}_{N,1}(x):=\widetilde{S}_{N,1}(x,x),\hskip 20.00003ptR_{N,2}(x):=S_{N,2}(x,x).

(2.18)

Before proceeding with the proof, we first discuss the extremal cases of Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.1

(Products of two rectangular GinOEs;
\tau=0
)
.

As mentioned in Remark
1.2
, if
\tau=0
, the model (
1.1
) becomes the two products of rectangular GinOEs.
In this case, the skew-orthogonal polynomials (
2.2
) becomes simplified as

p_{2k}(z)=z^{2k},\hskip 20.00003ptp_{2k+1}(z)=z^{2k+1}-2j(2j+\nu)z^{2k-1};

see also
[
22
, Eqs. (9.46), (9.47)]
.
Note that in this case,

R_{N}(x)\Big|_{\tau=0}=\frac{|x|^{\nu/2}}{2\pi}K_{\nu/2}(|x|)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{sgn}(x-y)(x-y)|y|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}(|y|)f_{N-2}^{(\nu)}(xy)\,dy,

where

f_{N-2}^{(\nu)}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{x^{j}}{j!\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}.

Then it follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N}(x)\Big|_{\tau=0}&=\frac{|x|^{\nu}K_{\nu/2}(|x|)}{2\pi}\Bigl(xK_{\nu/2}(|x|)\partial_{x}f_{N-2}^{(\nu)}(x^{2})+2|x|K_{\nu/2+1}(|x|)f_{N-2}^{(\nu)}(x^{2})\Bigr)\\
&\quad+\frac{x^{N-1}|x|^{\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}(|x|)}{\pi(N-2)!(N-2+\nu)!}\int_{0}^{x}y^{N-2+\nu/2}K_{\nu/2}(y)\,dy.\end{split}

(2.19)

Even for the
\tau=0
case, the relation (
2.19
) is new to our knowledge.

Remark 2.2

(Wishart matrices;
\tau=1
)
.

We now discuss how the result in
[
2
]
can be recovered from Theorem
2.1
.
The eigenvalue density of the Laguerre unitary ensemble with the weight
x^{\nu}e^{-x}
is given by

\rho(x;\mathrm{UE}_{N-1}(x^{\nu}e^{-x}))=x^{\nu}e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{k!}{\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}L_{k}^{\nu}(x)^{2}.

Its counterpart for the Laguerre orthogonal ensemble with weight
x^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-x/2}
is given by

\rho(x;\mathrm{OE}_{N}(x^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-x/2}))=\frac{x^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-x/2}}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Phi_{2k}(x)q_{2k+1}(x)-\Phi_{2k+1}(x)q_{2k}(x)}{(2k)!(2k+\nu)!}\bigg|_{\tau=1}.

Then, by
[
2
, Proposition 4.1]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(x;\mathrm{OE}_{N}(x^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-x/2}))=\rho(x;\mathrm{UE}_{N-1}(x^{\nu}e^{-x}))+\rho_{2}(x),\end{split}

(2.20)

where

\rho_{2}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(N-1)!}{\Gamma(\nu+N-1)}x^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-x/2}L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mathbbm{1}_{(0,x)}(t)\Big)t^{(\nu-1)/2}e^{-t/2}L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}(t)\,dt.

One can observe that the relation (
2.20
) can be recovered from Theorem
2.1
by taking
\tau\to 1
limit.
For this, recall that the modified Bessel function satisfies the asymptotic behaviour

K_{\nu/2}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}\Bigl(1+\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{8z}+O(z^{-2})\Bigl),\hskip 20.00003ptz\to\infty,

(2.21)

see e.g.
[
64
, Eq. (10.40.2)]
.
Then as
\tau\to 1
, we have

K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}{2|x|}}\times\begin{cases}\displaystyle e^{-x/2}&\text{if $x\geq 0$},\\
e^{x/(1-\tau)}&\text{if $x<0$}.\end{cases}

Therefore for
x\geq 0
, one can observe that

R_{N,1}(x)\to\rho(x;\mathrm{UE}_{N-1}(x^{\nu}e^{-x})),\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{R}_{N,1}(x)\to 0,\hskip 20.00003ptR_{N,2}(x)\to\rho_{2}(x),

as
\tau\to 1
. This gives (
2.20
).

We now prove Theorem
2.1
. The following identities of the generalised Laguerre polynomials will be used in the proof below.

•

Recurrence relations:

\displaystyle L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)

\displaystyle=L_{k}^{(\nu+1)}(z)-L_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z),

(2.22)

\displaystyle kL_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)

\displaystyle=(k+\nu)L_{k-1}^{(\nu)}(z)-zL_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z).

(2.23)

•

Differentiation rules:

\displaystyle\partial_{z}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)

\displaystyle=-L_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z),

(2.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\bigl(z^{\nu}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(k+\nu)z^{\nu-1}L_{k}^{(\nu-1)}(z).

(2.25)

We also make use of some properties of the modified Bessel function.

•

Asymptotic behaviours:

K_{\nu}(z)\sim\begin{cases}-\log z&\text{if $\nu=0$},\\
(2/z)^{\nu}\,\Gamma(\nu)/2&\text{if $\operatorname{Re}\nu>0$},\end{cases}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $z\to 0$}.

(2.26)

•

Differentiation rules:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Bigl[t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(at)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl[(\nu+1)K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(at)-atK_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}(at)\Bigr],

(2.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Bigl[t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}(at)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=-at^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(at).

(2.28)

Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{N}
is defined in (
2.12
).
Let us write

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w):=e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}z}\mathcal{K}_{N}(z,w).

(2.29)

We first show the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,y)=\int_{y}^{\infty}|t|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)(x-t)\,\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt.

(2.30)

Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,y)&=-(1-\tau^{2})^{2}\,y\,|y|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{y}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(y,x)\\
&\quad-(1-\tau^{2})|y|^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}\Bigl(\frac{y}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(y,x)-\mathrm{III}_{N}(x,y),\end{split}

(2.31)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{III}_{N}(x,y)&:=\frac{(N-1)!\,\tau^{2N-3}}{\Gamma(N+\nu-1)}\int_{y}^{\infty}|t|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}\\
&\quad\times\bigg[L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}\,L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg]\,dt.\end{split}

(2.32)

In particular, we have

\mathrm{J}_{N}(x):=\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,-\infty)=-\mathrm{III}_{N}(x,-\infty).

(2.33)

Proof.

One of the key ingredients of our proof is the following generalised Christoffel-Darboux formula recently established in
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
: for any
\tau\in[0,1]
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\bigg[(1-\tau^{2})\,z\,\partial_{z}^{2}+\Big((1-\tau^{2})(\nu+1)+2\tau z\Big)\partial_{z}+\frac{\tau^{2}z-w}{1-\tau^{2}}+(\nu+1)\tau\bigg]\mathcal{K}_{N}(z,w)\\
&=\frac{N!}{\Gamma(N+\nu)}\frac{\tau^{2N-1}}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg[L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

(2.34)

By using (
2.29
) and (
2.34
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}x\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)&=(1-\tau^{2})^{2}\bigl[t\,\partial_{t}^{2}+(\nu+1)\partial_{t}\bigr]\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\\
&\quad-e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}\frac{(N-1)!\,\tau^{2N-3}}{\Gamma(N+\nu-1)}\bigg[L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}\,L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

(2.35)

We first assume that
y\geq 0
.
Note that by (
2.35
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)x\,\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt+\mathrm{III}_{N}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(1-\tau^{2})^{2}\bigg[\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt+(\nu+1)\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt\bigg].

Here, it follows from (
2.27
) and integration by parts that

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt+y^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\bigg[(\nu+1)K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Big(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big)-\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}\Big(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big)\bigg]\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt,

where we have used (
2.21
).
Combining all of the above, it follows that for
y\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,y)&=-(1-\tau^{2})^{2}y^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{y}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(y,x)\\
&\quad+(1-\tau^{2})\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt\\
&\quad-\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt-\mathrm{III}_{N}(x,y).\end{split}

Furthermore, by using (
2.28
) and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt(1-\tau^{2})\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt

\displaystyle=-(1-\tau^{2})y^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}\Bigl(\frac{y}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(y,x)+\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\nu}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt.

Therefore, we obtain the desired expression (
2.31
).

For the case
y<0
, we consider the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,0)+\int_{y}^{0}(-t)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{-t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)x\,\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt+\int_{y}^{0}(-t)^{\nu/2+1}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{-t}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt.

Then the desired expression (
2.31
) follows from similar computations as above together with (
2.26
).
∎

Now, we are ready to show Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Recall that
S_{N}
is given by (
2.6
).
It was shown in
[
14
, Subsection 4.2]
that
S_{N}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N}(x,y)=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{sgn}(y-t)|xt|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(x+t)\Bigr)\\
&\quad\times\Bigl(t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{t-x}{\tau}\Bigr)\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(t,x)\,dt.\end{split}

(2.36)

By using (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\Bigl(t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{t-x}{\tau}\Bigr)\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{x-t}{\tau}\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(x,t)+\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{(N-1)!\tau^{2N-2}}{\Gamma(N+\nu-1)}\bigg(L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)-L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg).

Therefore (
2.36
) can be written as

S_{N}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}|x|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x\Bigr)\Big[\mathrm{I}_{N}(x,y)+\mathrm{II}_{N}(x,y)\Big],

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{I}_{N}(x,y)&:=\mathrm{J}_{N}(x)-2\,\mathrm{J}_{N}(x,y),\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{II}_{N}(x,y)&:=\frac{2(N-1)!\tau^{2N-1}}{\Gamma(N+\nu-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,y)}(t)\Big)|t|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}\\
&\quad\times\Bigl[L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)-L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{x}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\tau}\Bigr)\Bigr]\,dt.\end{split}

Here,
\mathrm{J}_{N}
is given by (
2.30
).
Then by combining Lemma
2.2
with

\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N-1}(t,x)=\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(t,x)+e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}_{N-1}(t,x),

straightforward computations yield the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Negative non-Hermiticity parameter

.

We have primarily focused on the case where the non-Hermiticity parameter
\tau
is nonnegative, but the model (

) is naturally well-defined for

as well.
Indeed, for
-1<\tau<0
, by (

) and the change of variables

,

) for

3
Preliminary analysis for the correlation kernel

In this section, we gather some preliminary results needed to prove our main results. While this requires some book-keeping, it is otherwise quite elementary.

3.1
Plancherel-Rotach asymptotis of the Laguerre polynomials

We present the Plancherel-Rotach asymptotics of the Laguerre polynomials.
There are several methods for obtaining the asymptotic behaviour of classical orthogonal polynomials with complex arguments, such as the Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) approximation (see e.g.
[
58
, Appendix B]
) or the Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method for Riemann–Hilbert problems (see e.g.
[
35
,
32
]
).

In this framework, the conformal map associated with the droplet (
1.18
) plays a crucial role.
For
\varrho\geq 0
, we define

\psi_{\varrho}(z):=\frac{z-\tau(2+\varrho)+\sqrt{(z-\tau(2+\varrho))^{2}-4(1+\varrho)\tau^{2}}}{2\sqrt{1+\varrho}},\hskip 20.00003ptz\in\mathbb{C}\backslash[F_{-}^{(\varrho)},F_{+}^{(\varrho)}],

(3.1)

where

F_{\pm}\equiv F_{\pm}^{(\varrho)}:=\tau(\sqrt{1+\varrho}\pm 1)^{2}

(3.2)

are the foci of the ellipse (
1.18
). In particular, we write
\psi(z)\equiv\psi_{0}(z)
.
The function
\psi_{\varrho}
defines a conformal map from the exterior of the droplet (
1.18
) to the exterior of the unit disc; see
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(
Exponential regime
)
.

Fix
\tau\in[0,1)
, and let
m
and
r
be fixed integers.

(i)

(Large parameter
\nu=\varrho\,N
with
\varrho>0
)
Assume
\nu=\varrho N
with
\varrho>0
.
As
N\to\infty
, we have

L_{N+r}^{(\nu+m)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N}}\frac{(-1)^{N+r}}{\tau^{N+r}}\sqrt{\varrho+1}^{\frac{2r+1}{2}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}_{\varrho}(z)}\psi_{\varrho}(z)^{r}\varpi(z)^{m}e^{Ng_{\tau}^{(\varrho)}(z)}\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),

(3.3)

uniformly over any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\backslash[F_{-}^{(\varrho)},F_{+}^{(\varrho)}]
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{\tau}^{(\varrho)}(z)&:=\frac{z-\sqrt{(z-(2+\varrho)\tau)^{2}-4(\varrho+1)\tau^{2}}}{2\tau}\\
&\quad+(1+\varrho)\log\Bigl(z-\tau(2+\varrho)+\sqrt{(z-\tau(2+\varrho))^{2}-4(\varrho+1)\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\\
&\quad-\varrho\log\Bigl(z-\tau\varrho+\sqrt{(z-\tau(2+\varrho))^{2}-4(\varrho+1)\tau^{2}}\Bigr)-\log 2-\frac{\varrho}{2}\end{split}

(3.4)

and

\varpi(z):=\frac{2\tau(1+\varrho)}{z+\varrho\tau-\sqrt{(z-\tau(2+\varrho))^{2}-4(\varrho+1)\tau^{2}}}.

(3.5)

(ii)

(Fixed parameter
\nu>-1
)

Assume
\nu>-1
is fixed. As
N\to\infty
, we have

L_{N+r}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N}}\frac{(-1)^{N+r}}{\tau^{N+r}}\frac{\psi(z)^{r+\frac{\nu}{2}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{\nu/2}}e^{Ng_{\tau}(z)}\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),

(3.6)

uniformly over any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\backslash[0,4\tau]
, where
g_{\tau}\equiv g_{\tau}^{(0)}
.

Lemma
3.1
for
\nu=\varrho\,N
with
\varrho>0
can be derived from
[
32
, Theorem 1 (d)]
. For fixed
\nu>-1
, it can be found in
[
74
]
.

As a consequence of Lemma
3.1
and
[
32
, Theorem 1 (a), (b), and (c)]
, we obtain the following estimate, which will be useful in the subsequent asymptotic analysis.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
\tau\in(0,1)
, and we assume
\nu=\varrho N
with
\varrho>0
. Let
r
be a fixed integer. Then we have

\Bigl|e^{-Ng_{\tau}^{(\varrho)}(z)}L_{N+r}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)\Bigr|\leq\frac{1}{\tau^{N+r}}\cdot O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}),

(3.7)

where the
O(N^{-1/2})
-term is uniform for
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Furthermore, (
3.7
) also holds for fixed
\nu>-1
including
\varrho=0
over
\mathbb{C}\backslash B_{\delta}(0)
with a small
\delta>0
.

For the weakly non-Hermitian case, we require the asymptotic behaviour of the Laguerre polynomials in both the oscillatory and critical regimes. The following result, stated in for instance
[

Lemma 3.3

(
Oscillatory regime
)
.

Let
\tau=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2N}
with fixed
\alpha>0
, and let
r
and
m
be fixed integers.

(i)

(Large parameter
\nu=\varrho\,N
with
\varrho>0
)
Assume
\nu=\varrho N
with
\varrho>0
. Given
\delta>0
small, as
N\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{N+r}^{(\nu+m)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)&=\frac{\sqrt{2}(-1)^{N+r}(1+\varrho)^{\frac{N(1+\varrho)+r+m}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{2N+\alpha^{2}}{4}(z-\varrho)}\cos(\Psi_{N+r,m}(z))}{\sqrt{\pi N}(z-\lambda_{-})^{\frac{1}{4}}(\lambda_{+}-z)^{\frac{1}{4}}z^{\frac{N\varrho+m}{2}}}\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),\end{split}

(3.8)

uniformly for
z\in[\lambda_{-}+\delta,\lambda_{+}-\delta]
.
Here,

\Psi_{N+r,m}(z):=\boldsymbol{\psi}_{N}(z)-\frac{2r+1+m}{2}\arccos\boldsymbol{p}(z)+m\arccos\boldsymbol{q}(z)+\frac{\pi}{4},

(3.9)

where

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\psi}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle:=N\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(1)}(z)+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(2)}(z),\hskip 20.00003pt\boldsymbol{p}(z):=\frac{z-(\varrho+2)}{2\sqrt{1+\varrho}},\hskip 20.00003pt\boldsymbol{q}(z):=\frac{(2+\varrho)z-\varrho^{2}}{2\sqrt{1+\varrho}z},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(1)}(z)&:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\sqrt{(z-\lambda_{-})(\lambda_{+}-z)}+\varrho\arccos\Bigl(\frac{(2+\varrho)z-\varrho^{2}}{2\sqrt{1+\varrho}z}\Bigr)-(2+\varrho)\arccos\Bigl(\frac{z-(\varrho+2)}{2\sqrt{1+\varrho}}\Bigr)\Bigr],\end{split}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(2)}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{c^{2}}{2}\sqrt{(z-\lambda_{-})(\lambda_{+}-z)}.

(3.12)

(ii)

(Fixed parameter
\nu>-1
)

Assume
\nu>-1
is fixed. Given
\delta,\delta^{\prime}>0
small, as
N\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle L_{N+r}^{(\nu+m)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N+r}N^{N+r}}{(N+r)!}\frac{2\,e^{(\frac{x}{2}-1)N+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}x}}{x^{1/4}(4-x)^{1/4}x^{(\nu+m)/2}}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(m,r)}(x)\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),

(3.13)

uniformly for
x\in[\delta^{\prime},4-\delta]
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(m,r)}(x)

\displaystyle:=\cos\Bigl(b_{-1}(x)N+b_{0}^{(\nu,m,\alpha,r)}(x)\Bigr).

Here,

\displaystyle b_{-1}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x(4-x)}-2\arccos\Big(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2}\Big),

\displaystyle b_{0}^{(\nu,m,\alpha,r)}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\sqrt{(4-x)x}-(2r+\nu+m+1)\arccos\Big(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2}\Big)+\frac{\pi}{4}.

Recall that the Airy function of the first kind is given by

\operatorname{Ai}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\Big(\frac{t^{3}}{3}+xt\Big)\,dt;

(3.14)

see e.g.
[
64
, Chapter 9]
.
The following can be derived from
[
32
, Theorem 1 (a) and (b)]
for the large parameter
\nu=\varrho\,N
with
\varrho>0
and
[

, Theorem 2.4]
or
[

(
Airy regime
)
.

Let
\tau=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2N}
with fixed
\alpha>0
, and let

and

be fixed integers.

(Large parameter

with

Given

small, as

uniformly for

, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{N+r}^{(\nu+m)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}}\frac{(1+\varrho)^{\frac{(1+\varrho)N}{2}+\frac{r+m}{2}+\frac{1}{24}}}{(\sqrt{1+\varrho}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}z^{\frac{N\varrho+m}{2}}}(\lambda_{+}-z)^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp\Bigl(\frac{2N+\alpha^{2}}{4}(z-\varrho)\Bigr)\\

(Fixed parameter
\nu>-1

\nu>-1
is fixed. Given

small, as

L_{N+r}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}u\Bigr)=(-1)^{N+r}2^{-\nu-\frac{1}{3}}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}e^{\frac{N}{2}u+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}u}\Bigl[\operatorname{Ai}\Bigl(\frac{N^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2^{\frac{4}{3}}}(u-4)\Bigr)+O(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{3}}})\Bigr],

3.2
Complex correlation kernel and rescaling

Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{N}
is given by (
2.12
). In order to discuss the rescaled correlation kernels, it is convenient to introduce

S_{N}^{\rm c}(x,y):=\frac{2N^{\nu+2}}{1-\tau^{2}}\mathcal{K}_{N}(Nx,Ny)=\frac{2N^{\nu+2}}{1-\tau^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}y\Bigr)

(3.20)

and
R_{N}^{\rm c}(x):=S_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x,x).

For
x\not=0
, we denote by

\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(x):=|x|^{\nu}K_{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{2N\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x\Bigr)

(3.21)

the weight function associated with the complex non-Hermitian Wishart ensemble. Here, by using the asymptotic behaviour of
K_{\nu}
near the origin (see e.g.
[
64
, Eq. (10.30.2)]
), we set

\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(0)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{2}\Big(\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big)^{-\nu}.

(3.22)

Then we define

\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x,y):=\sqrt{\omega_{N}^{\text{c}}(x)\omega_{N}^{\text{c}}(y)}S_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x,y),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x):=\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x,x).

(3.23)

This corresponds to the weighted kernel for the complex non-Hermitian Wishart ensemble; see e.g.
[
9
,
27
]
.

We also define the weight functions

\displaystyle\omega_{N,1}(x)

\displaystyle:=K_{\nu/2}\Big(\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)^{2}K_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)^{-1},

(3.24)

\displaystyle\omega_{N,2}(x)

\displaystyle:=K_{\nu/2}\Big(\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)K_{\nu/2+1}\Big(\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)K_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)^{-1},

(3.25)

\displaystyle\omega_{N,3}(x)

\displaystyle:=K_{\nu-1}\Big(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)K_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\Big)^{-1}.

(3.26)

Recall that the one-point density
\mathbf{R}_{N}
of the real eigenvalues is given by (
2.11
).

Lemma 3.6

(
Decomposition structure of the
1
-point function
)
.

We have

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)=\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,1}(x)+\mathbf{R}_{N,2}(x),

(3.27)

where

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,1}(x):=\frac{|x|}{2\pi}\Big(\omega_{N,1}(x)\,\omega_{N,3}(x)+\omega_{N,2}(x)\Big)\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{2N}x\,\omega_{N,1}(x)\partial_{x}\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x),

(3.28)

and

\mathbf{R}_{N,2}(x):=\frac{N^{3}}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{(1)}(x)\cdot\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{(2)}(x).

(3.29)

Here,

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\frac{N^{\nu-1}(N-1)!}{\Gamma(N-1+\nu)}}\tau^{N-1}|x|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{N|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{N\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr),

(3.30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{(2)}(x)

\displaystyle:=2\sqrt{\frac{N^{\nu-1}(N-1)!}{\Gamma(N-1+\nu)}}\tau^{N-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,x)}(t)\Big)|t|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}K_{\nu/2}\Bigl(\frac{N|t|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{N\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}t}L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}t\Bigr)\,dt.

(3.31)

Proof.

This immediately follows from Theorem
2.1
, together with definitions (
2.11
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
).
∎

3.3
Differential equation for complex counterpart of kernel

In this subsection, we formulate the limiting differential equations, which play an important role in the subsequent asymptotic analysis. To this end, we introduce a further change of the weight functions, which gives rise to limiting differential equations that are convenient to analyse.

Recall that
S_{N}^{\rm c}
is given by (
3.20
) and
R_{N}^{\rm c}(x)=S_{N}^{\rm c}(x,x)
. We define

\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x):=\sqrt{x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}}W_{N}^{\rm c}(x)R_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(x),

(3.32)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{N}^{\rm c}(x)&:=\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}{4N}}\Bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}}+\tfrac{\varrho(1-\tau^{2})}{2}\Bigr)^{\varrho N}\Bigl(x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}\Bigr)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\\
&\quad\times\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}}-\tau x\Bigr)\Bigr).\end{split}

(3.33)

Lemma 3.7
.

The 1-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\rm c}
in (
3.23
) is related to
\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}
in (
3.32
) as

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\rm c}(x)=\Big(x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{|x|^{\nu}K_{\nu}\bigl(\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}|x|\bigr)e^{\frac{2N\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}}{W_{N}^{\rm c}(x)}\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{\rm c}(x).

(3.34)

Furthermore, for
\tau\in[0,1)
which may depend on
N
, as
N\to\infty,
we have

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{\rm c}(x)=\Big(x^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{\rm c}(x)\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),

(3.35)

uniformly for
0<|x|<\infty.

Proof.

The first assertion immediately follows from the definitions (
3.23
) and (
3.32
).

By
[
64
, Eq. (10.41.4)]
, for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{\frac{m\nu}{2}+r}\Bigl(\frac{mN}{1-\tau^{2}}|t|\Bigr)&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}{2mN}}\Bigl(|t|^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}\Bigr)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{mN}{1-\tau^{2}}\sqrt{|t|^{2}+\tfrac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}}\Bigr)\\
&\quad\times\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{|t|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{2}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}{4}}+\frac{\varrho(1-\tau^{2})}{2}}{|t|}\Bigr)^{\frac{m\varrho N}{2}+r}\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),\end{split}

(3.36)

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly for
0<|t|<\infty
.
On the other hand, for a fixed
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
, we need the following asymptotic expansion of the modified Bessel functions that for fixed
\nu>-1
and fixed
m,r\in\mathbb{Z}
, by
[
64
, Eq. (10.40.2)]

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{\frac{m\nu}{2}+r}\Bigl(\frac{mN|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}{2mN|x|}}e^{-\frac{mN|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}}\Bigl[1+\frac{(1-\tau^{2})(4(\frac{m\nu}{2}+r)^{2}-1)}{8mN|z|}+O(\frac{1}{N^{2}})\Bigr],\end{split}

(3.37)

uniformly for

|x|^{\nu}K_{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{2N|x|}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigr)e^{\frac{2N\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}x}=W_{N}^{\rm c}(x)\Bigl(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Bigr),

(3.38)

as
N\to\infty
, uniformly for

∎

in (

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{I}_{N,1}(x)\cdot\mathcal{I}_{N,2}(x),

(3.41)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N,2}(x)

\displaystyle:=L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr)L_{N-2}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr)+L_{N-2}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu-1)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}x\Bigr).

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Omega_{\varrho}(x)&:=\frac{2}{1-\tau^{2}}\Bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+\tfrac{(1-\tau^{2})^{2}\varrho^{2}}{4}}-\tau x\Bigr)\\
&\quad-\varrho\log\Big(\sqrt{x^{2}+\tfrac{(1-\tau^{2})^{2}\varrho^{2}}{4}}+\tfrac{(1-\tau^{2})\varrho}{2}\Big)-\varrho+(1+\varrho)\log(1+\varrho)-2g_{\tau}^{(\varrho)}(x),\end{split}

The following lemma can be established using the same strategy as in
[

Lemma A.2

\displaystyle J_{r}[\mathcal{U}_{N,4}]

ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionWhat are the prognostically relevant echocardiographic tricuspid regurgitation thresholds in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, and do they outperform current guideline definitions?FindingsIn this multicenter cohort study of 1124 patients with newly diagnosed transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, spline-derived thresholds for vena contracta width, effective regurgitant orifice area, and regurgitant volume were substantially lower than guideline cutoffs. A 4-grade risk-based framework classified nearly 3-fold more patients as having a prognostically relevant degree of tricuspid regurgitation than guideline or Tricuspid Valve Academic Research Consortium (TVARC) criteria and remained independently associated with mortality and heart failure hospitalization after full adjustment.MeaningDisease-specific quantitative echocardiographic thresholds for tricuspid regurgitation better reflect the restrictive low-flow physiology of transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy and improve prognostic stratification at diagnosis compared to other grading schemes.

Introduction

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is characterized by progressive myocardial infiltration, restrictive physiology, and fixed low stroke volumes, resulting in limited hemodynamic reserve and adverse clinical outcomes despite expanding disease-modifying therapies. Beyond infiltration of the myocardium, contemporary data have reshaped our understanding of ATTR-CM as a disorder with frequent and clinically relevant valvular involvement. The intersection of cardiac amyloidosis with aortic stenosis has provided a key proof of concept that valve disease in amyloidosis is associated with adverse prognosis and is clinically actionable within contemporary care pathways, including valve replacement and subsequent implementation of ATTR-specific therapy. Against this background, tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is highly prevalent in ATTR-CM and likely reflects a convergence of atrial fibrillation, right atrial and annular dilatation, pulmonary hypertension, and progressive right ventricular dysfunction, representing hallmarks of advanced restrictive heart failure. Prior studies in cardiac amyloidosis have linked TR to mortality, and recent multicenter data further highlight the burden and prognostic relevance of combined mitral and tricuspid regurgitation. However, TR severity in ATTR-CM is typically graded using guideline thresholds derived from nonamyloid populations, despite a physiology in which even modest regurgitant volumes may represent a meaningful proportion of effective forward flow. This raises concern that conventional grading may underestimate clinically relevant TR in ATTR-CM and may fail to reliably identify patients at elevated risk.

This international, multicenter cohort study aimed to systematically evaluate the prognostic relevance of TR-specific (semi-)quantitative echocardiographic criteria in ATTR-CM, derive and externally validate disease-specific risk thresholds, and compare their prognostic performance with established guideline definitions and the Tricuspid Valve Academic Research Consortium (TVARC) 5-grade extension.

Methods

Study Population

The present analysis is based on an ongoing international, multicenter ATTR-CM registry that includes 8 high-volume tertiary referral centers in 4 European countries (Austria, Italy, Germany, and the Netherlands). Consecutive adult patients with ATTR-CM were enrolled at the time they established care at the participating centers. ATTR-CM was diagnosed either noninvasively, based on Perugini grade 2 or higher myocardial tracer uptake on bone scintigraphy with exclusion of light-chain amyloidosis by serum protein electrophoresis, serum free light-chain quantification, and serum and urine immunofixation; or invasively by endomyocardial biopsy demonstrating transthyretin amyloid, in line with current recommendations. The transthoracic echocardiogram performed at the baseline evaluation was considered the baseline study date for the present analysis. Exclusion criteria were prior tricuspid valve intervention and severe aortic stenosis or aortic regurgitation. The study was conducted in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline and the Declaration of Helsinki, and local ethics approval was obtained at each participating center. Participants provided written consent. The study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06836011).

Clinical Measures and Follow-Up

Baseline demographics, medical history, comorbidities, New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class, electrocardiographic findings, routine laboratory parameters, and cardiovascular medication were systematically recorded at the time of diagnosis. ATTR subtype (wild-type vs variant) and the use of ATTR-specific therapy at baseline and during follow-up were documented. Follow-up was performed through regular clinical visits according to local practice, and the performance of any tricuspid valve intervention (surgical or transcatheter) during follow-up was captured. The primary end point was all-cause mortality, and time to first heart failure hospitalization (HFH) served as the key secondary end point.

Echocardiographic Core Laboratory Assessment

Comprehensive transthoracic echocardiography was performed at baseline at all centers using commercially available ultrasound systems and in accordance with current American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI) recommendations. Tricuspid regurgitation was assessed using a multiparametric approach combining a semiquantitative measure (vena contracta width [VCW]) with quantitative, proximal isovelocity surface area (PISA)–based measures (effective regurgitant orifice area [EROA] and regurgitant volume [RegVol]).

VCW was measured on 2-dimensional color Doppler images in the apical 4-chamber view, which was feasible in all cases; no alternative view was used for this measurement. All echocardiographic studies underwent centralized, blinded core laboratory assessment at the Medical University of Vienna according to a prespecified standard operating procedure (eTable 1 in Supplement 1).

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are reported as median and interquartile range, and categorical variables as absolute numbers and percentages. Differences between groups were assessed using the χ2 test for categorical variables and the Kruskal-Wallis test for continuous variables, as appropriate. The derivation cohort included patients recruited in Austria and Germany, while patients from Italy and the Netherlands constituted the validation cohort to provide external validation and evaluate generalizability. Unless otherwise indicated, all reported analyses pertain to the overall cohort; results specific to the derivation and validation cohorts are presented in Supplement 1 and labeled accordingly.

To derive novel TR severity thresholds in ATTR-CM, we explored the continuous association between quantitative TR metrics and mortality using restricted cubic spline analyses. Risk strata were defined based on the lower 95% confidence interval of the relative hazard estimates of the continuous range of TR measures, in analogy to prior spline-based approaches (eMethods in Supplement 1). Prognostic cutpoints were derived in the derivation cohort and subsequently applied to the validation cohort. Separation of the resulting risk strata was examined using Kaplan-Meier estimates with log-rank testing and further quantified by univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards models.

The proposed framework defined TR severity according to spline-derived thresholds: patients were classified as intermediate if at least 1 TR parameter fell into the intermediate-risk range (relative risk, 1.0-1.5), as high if any parameter exceeded the high-risk threshold (relative risk, >1.5), and as extreme if any parameter exceeded the relative risk threshold of 2.0. To enable comparison with established standards, TR severity was additionally classified according to guideline criteria from the American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology (2020 AHA/ACC), the European Society of Cardiology/European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (2025 ESC/EACTS), the recommendations for evaluation of valvular regurgitation from the ASE, and the expanded TVARC 5-grade scheme.

Cox proportional hazards regression was used to examine the association between TR severity and clinical outcomes. For all time-to-event analyses, patients were censored at the time of any tricuspid valve intervention. To assess the independent prognostic value of TR, we performed a prespecified stepwise confounder expansion. Model 1 was univariable. The clinical confounder model (clinical + comorbidity) comprised ATTR subtype (wild-type vs variant), National Amyloidosis Centre (NAC) stage, log-transformed troponin, NYHA functional class, ATTR-specific therapy (entered as a time-dependent covariate), coronary artery disease, arterial hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and diabetes. The full confounder model additionally included the following echocardiographic confounders: systolic pulmonary artery pressure, left ventricular (LV) global longitudinal strain, LV ejection fraction, LV end-diastolic volume index, LV mass index, right ventricular (RV) end-diastolic diameter, right atrial volume index, tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), tissue Doppler-derived tricuspid annular systolic velocity, forward stroke volume index, diastolic dysfunction grade, and significant (≥moderate) mitral regurgitation. All P values are 2-sided, with α = .05. Statistical analyses were conducted in R version 4.5.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 1124 patients with ATTR-CM and baseline echocardiographic assessment at the time of diagnosis were recruited in 8 centers across 4 countries. The median (IQR) patient age was 80 (75-84) years, and 260 of 1124 patients (23.1%) were female (Table 1). Most had wild-type ATTR (1030 of 1124 [91.6%]), whereas variant ATTR was present in 94 patients (8.4%). The echocardiographic phenotype is described in Table 1 and was consistent with ATTR-CM. On average, patients had small to normal LV cavity size, with considerable LV thickening (median [IQR] LV mass index, 162 [134-195] g/m2) and marked left atrial (LA) enlargement (median [IQR] LA volume index, 44 [33-56] mL/m2). LV ejection fraction was borderline (median [IQR], 52% [44%-59%]), with impaired median (IQR) global longitudinal strain (−11% [−14% to −9%]) and low stroke volumes (median [IQR] stroke volume index, 29 [23-36] mL/m2). Diastolic dysfunction was predominantly grade II (749 of 1124 [66.6%]), and grade III was present in 183 of 1124 patients (16.3%).

(Semi-)quantitative TR assessment according to core laboratory analysis yielded a median (IQR) VCW of 3 (1-4) mm, median (IQR) EROA of 0.11 (0.08-0.21) cm2, and median (IQR) RegVol of 8 (3-18) mL. Mitral regurgitation was absent or trace in 263 patients (23.4%), mild in 394 (35.1%), moderate in 362 (32.2%), and severe in 105 (9.3%); aortic stenosis was absent in 1008 patients (89.7%), mild in 59 (5.2%), and moderate in 57 (5.1%); and aortic regurgitation was absent or mild in 1046 patients (93.1%) and moderate in 78 (6.9%). Interobserver reproducibility was good to excellent across all TR parameters, with intraclass correlation coefficient values between 0.935 and 0.955, minimal biases on Bland-Altman analysis, and coefficients of variability ranging from 19% to 23% (eTable 3 in Supplement 1). Feasibility of each quantitative TR parameter was 84.0% to 88.5% overall and 96.5% or higher in patients with mild to moderate TR or greater according to local grading (eTable 4 in Supplement 1). Patients without measurable quantitative TR had a lower-risk profile than those with measurable TR (lower N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide [NT-proBNP] and NAC stage and lower mortality), consistent with earlier-stage disease.

Risk Group Stratification

Based on the proposed conceptual framework, patients in the overall cohort were stratified into the following 4 risk groups: low (413 [36.7%]), intermediate (377 [33.5%]), high (234 [20.8%]), and extreme (100 [8.9%]). Patients in higher risk categories were older (median [IQR] ages of 79 [72-83], 80 [76-84], 81 [77-84], and 81 [78-85] years for low through extreme risk, respectively; P < .001), with no differences in sex (female sex: 86 of 413 [20.8%], 88 of 377 [23.3%], 67 of 234 [28.6%], and 19 of 100 [19.0%] for low through extreme risk; P = .10) or ATTR subtype (P = .46). Atrial fibrillation prevalence increased progressively from 134 of 413 (32.4%) to 179 of 377 (47.5%), 146 of 234 (62.4%), and 67 of 100 (67.0%) across risk strata (P < .001).

Prescription of ATTR-specific therapy at baseline decreased with increasing risk stage (233 of 413 [56.4%], 184 of 377 [48.8%], 90 of 234 [38.5%], and 32 of 100 [32.0%] for low through extreme risk; P < .001). Baseline characteristics of both the derivation cohort (745 of 1124 [66%]) and validation cohort (379 of 1124 [34%]) are depicted in eTables 5 and 6 in Supplement 1.

Quantitative TR Assessment and Outcome

Over a median (IQR) follow-up of 25.2 (12.2-43.2) months, 324 patients (28.8%) died, and 251 (22.3%) experienced at least 1 HFH. By univariable Cox regression, higher values of all continuous quantitative TR surrogates were associated with higher hazard of mortality, including VCW (hazard ratio [HR], 1.64; 95% CI, 1.50-1.79), EROA (HR, 1.35; 95% CI, 1.28-1.43), and RegVol (HR, 1.40; 95% CI, 1.31-1.49). Statistical significance was retained for all parameters after multivariate adjustment for the clinical confounder model (eTable 7 in Supplement 1). Similarly, TR surrogates were significantly associated with time to first HFH, including VCW (HR, 1.41; 95% CI, 1.28-1.55), EROA (HR, 1.37; 95% CI, 1.28-1.47), and RegVol (HR, 1.51; 95% CI, 1.40-1.64).

Spline Analysis

Spline-derived analyses defined prognostic cutpoints according to prespecified relative-hazard thresholds across all quantitative TR measures in the derivation cohort (eFigure 1 in Supplement 1). For VCW, thresholds at 3 mm, 5 mm, and 8 mm delineated transitions from low to intermediate, intermediate to high, and high to extreme risk, respectively. For EROA, corresponding thresholds were 0.15 cm2, 0.25 cm2, and 0.50 cm2, and for RegVol, thresholds were 10 mL, 20 mL, and 40 mL, each identifying stepwise increases in risk across strata (Table 2). Independent validation confirmed the prognostic significance of the proposed cutoffs (eTable 8 in Supplement 1).

Comparison of Framework With Existing Definitions

With the proposed conceptual framework, 413 patients (36.7%) were classified as low risk, 377 (33.5%) as intermediate risk, 234 (20.8%) as high risk, and 100 (8.9%) as extreme risk. Within the high-risk stratum, classification was most frequently driven by RegVol (182 of 234 [77.8%]) and VCW (179 of 234 [76.5%]), whereas EROA contributed less often (84 of 234 [35.9%]). Within the extreme-risk stratum, classification was based on VCW in 73 of 100 patients (73.0%), RegVol in 56 patients (56.0%), and EROA in 45 patients (45.0%) (eFigure 6 in Supplement 1).

In the overall cohort, the framework-based stratification yielded a clear, stepwise increase in mortality risk from low- to intermediate-, high-, and extreme-risk groups, with corresponding separation of Kaplan-Meier survival curves (Figure 1); similar stepwise separation was confirmed in both the derivation and validation cohorts (eFigure 7 in Supplement 1).

Prognostication

In a prespecified stepwise confounder expansion in the overall cohort, all 4 TR grading algorithms were significantly associated with both end points univariably. After full adjustment, the proposed framework and the TVARC scheme remained independently associated with both mortality (framework: HR, 1.41; 95% CI, 1.23-1.62; TVARC: HR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.10-1.37) and HFH (framework: HR, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.12-1.54; TVARC: HR, 1.24; 95% CI, 1.09-1.42), whereas the ESC/EACTS and AHA/ACC guidelines lost significance for both end points, and the ASE scheme for HFH (Table 3). A complete-case analysis restricted to patients with availability of all 3 quantitative parameters yielded similar results to the overall cohort (eTable 9 in Supplement 1). In the overall and derivation cohorts, the framework yielded the highest time-dependent area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) at all time points, with significantly improved discrimination compared to the ESC/EACTS and AHA/ACC guidelines at all time points and compared to the ASE and TVARC schemes from 36 months onward (Figure 2; eTables 10 and 11 and eFigure 9 in Supplement 1). In the validation cohort, the framework remained significantly superior to the ESC/EACTS and AHA/ACC guidelines (from 24 months onward); its advantage over the TVARC and ASE schemes was numerically consistent, yet did not reach statistical significance (eTable 12 and eFigure 9 in Supplement 1), consistent with the smaller validation sample.

Discussion

In this large-scale, multicenter international cohort study of more than 1100 patients with newly diagnosed ATTR-CM, we demonstrate a consistent and continuous association between (semi-)quantitative echocardiographic TR severity measures and clinical outcomes. Risk-based prognostic thresholds across TR parameters were substantially lower than established grading schemes. When integrated into a conceptual framework, established thresholds identified approximately 3-fold more patients with high-risk TR and provided superior prognostic discrimination compared to conventional grading criteria. Collectively, these findings provide the first systematic evaluation of quantitative TR parameters in ATTR-CM to our knowledge and support prognostication based on disease-specific TR grading to account for its distinctive restrictive, low-flow hemodynamics.

TR in ATTR-CM

In ATTR-CM, TR is pathophysiologically distinct to TR in other cardiac conditions and in most cases arises through multiple converging mechanisms. First, direct amyloid fibril deposition has been described within the tricuspid valve apparatus, including leaflet tissue, chordae tendineae, and the annular and subannular myocardium, impairing leaflet coaptation and annular geometry, analogous to changes described for the mitral valve in ATTR-CM. Infiltration of the RV myocardium progressively impairs RV contractility and promotes annular dilatation, thereby perpetuating leaflet malcoaptation independently of hemodynamic loading conditions. Second, the restrictive diastolic phenotype of ATTR-CM inevitably leads to chronically elevated left-sided filling pressures, pulmonary venous hypertension, and upstream pulmonary hypertension, each of which promotes progressive RV pressure overload and functional TR. Third, atrial dilatation, driven by chronically elevated atrial pressures, leads to progressive right atrial and tricuspid annular enlargement. Fourth, atrial fibrillation, present in approximately half of patients in the present cohort, further drives atrial remodeling and worsens functional TR through annular dilatation.

Quantitative TR Assessment and Disease-Specific TR Thresholds in ATTR-CM

Although TR has been recognized as common and prognostically relevant in cardiac amyloidosis, systematic quantitative assessment in a large ATTR-CM population has never been performed. All 3 quantitative metrics were independently associated with mortality and HFH. Importantly, prognostic TR thresholds in ATTR-CM are substantially lower than the conventional cutoffs in current guideline and TVARC definitions. Translating these parameter-specific thresholds into a multiparametric conceptual framework identifies a considerable proportion of patients with ATTR-CM with disease deemed nonsevere TR by conventional criteria who already carry a risk profile consistent with advanced disease. Such patients may possibly benefit from intensified care.

The derivation of a separate extreme-risk stratum represents a further contribution of the present analysis. Although extended grading schemes including massive and torrential categories have been proposed to capture advanced TR in interventional contexts, these were not empirically derived as disease-specific prognostic thresholds in amyloidosis. Our conceptual framework defines an extreme-risk group that experiences a disproportionate excess hazard for adverse outcomes.

The framework also demonstrated consistent prognostic power in the subgroup of patients with RV lead presence, where its time-dependent AUROC exceeded that of the other grading schemes at all time points, reaching statistical significance against the guideline-based definition at 24 and 48 months (eTables 13 and 14 and eFigures 10 and 11 in Supplement 1). Even though the relatively low number of patients in this group renders these analyses exploratory, the results are reinforcing. With the increasing implementation of leadless pacemakers, lead-associated TR becomes a potentially modifiable risk factor, even though the small anatomy involving RV concentric thickening may pose technical challenges in ATTR-CM.

Clinical Implications

The present findings have several practical implications for the management of ATTR-CM. First, disease-specific TR grading enables more accurate phenotyping and risk stratification at the time of diagnosis, identifying patients at elevated risk who may warrant closer surveillance. We emphasize that the proposed thresholds are intended for prognostic risk stratification and not as criteria for tricuspid valve intervention. Second, the recognition of ATTR-CM as a disorder with frequent and clinically relevant valvular involvement has progressively extended from the aortic to the atrioventricular valves. Recent multicenter data have underscored the prognostic relevance of combined mitral and tricuspid regurgitation in cardiac amyloidosis, and our group previously demonstrated that ATTR-CM is prevalent among patients undergoing transcatheter mitral edge-to-edge repair, carrying a distinct phenotype and prognosis after successful intervention. In parallel, emerging data support the feasibility of transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair in ATTR-CM, with reported improvements in NYHA functional class and TR reduction.

Importantly, our data do not justify new thresholds for tricuspid valve intervention. As thresholds derived in clinical scenarios with lower absolute TR measures identify patients with smaller regurgitant volumes, any absolute benefit of intervention at these lower thresholds is uncertain and may be smaller than at higher regurgitant volumes. Moreover, transcatheter TR interventions have to date improved symptoms and quality of life but have not yet been shown to confer a survival benefit. Whether management guided by these prognostic thresholds improves outcomes will require dedicated clinical trials.

Limitations

Several limitations merit discussion. First, the observational design precludes causal inference, and residual confounding remains possible. Second, PISA-derived quantification and related measures rely on geometric assumptions and may be affected by complex jet morphology and rhythm irregularity, although the consistency of prognostic signals across parameters mitigates this concern. Third, TR severity was assessed at baseline only; longitudinal trajectories and treatment-related changes were not evaluated. Fourth, RV free wall longitudinal strain was not uniformly available and therefore could not be incorporated into the models. Finally, these findings derive from high-volume tertiary referral centers: the high feasibility of quantitative TR assessment (>80%) reflects standardized core laboratory acquisition and measurement that may be less reproducible in routine practice, and referral patterns may enrich the cohort for more advanced or selected disease, potentially influencing the observed distribution of TR severity and outcomes. Interobserver reproducibility was nonetheless good to excellent (eTable 3 in Supplement 1), and risk thresholds remained discriminatory in external validation. The proposed classification should therefore be regarded as a decision-support tool for expert follow-up centers, with broader adoption requiring further external validation.

Conclusions

In this cohort study among patients with newly diagnosed ATTR-CM, quantitative echocardiographic TR metrics were independently associated with mortality and HFH. A pragmatic, multiparametric conceptual framework identified nearly 3-fold more high-risk patients and provides superior prognostic discrimination compared with established TR grading schemes. These findings support disease-specific TR grading for prognostic assessment in ATTR-CM and provide a foundation for future studies testing whether targeted TR interventions can improve outcomes in this population.

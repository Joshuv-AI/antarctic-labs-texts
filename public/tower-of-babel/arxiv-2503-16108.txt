Inclusive D 0 \mathrm{D}^{0} Photoproduction in Ultraperipheral Collisions
Patricia Gimeno-Estivill, Tuomas Lappi, Heikki Mäntysaari

Abstract
We compute the differential cross section for inclusive D 0 \mathrm{D}^{0} production in ultraperipheral collisions within the Color Glass Condensate framework. Our predictions are found to be in relatively good agreement with the CMS data at small transverse momentum, which is the region of validity of our approach. Furthermore, we quantify saturation effects by a nuclear modification ratio R p ​ A R_{pA} for D 0 \mathrm{D}^{0} photoproduction and examine both analytically and numerically the collinear factorization limit of the D 0 \mathrm{D}^{0} differential photoproduction cross section.

II
Theoretical framework

In this section we will derive the differential cross section for inclusive hadron photoproduction in UPCs using the CGC formalism and including quark masses. As illustrated in Fig.
1
, UPCs occur when the distance
B_{\perp}
between the centers of the two nuclei is greater than twice their radius
R=(1.2A^{1/3}-0.86A^{-1/3})\;\mathrm{fm}^{2}
, where
A
is the atomic number. At this separation, hadronic interactions are suppressed and the interaction is mediated by a photon.
We will start by writing down the expression of the photon flux that mediates the interaction. Then, using Light-Cone Perturbation Theory and the Color Glass Condensate
[
18
]
, we will calculate the partonic cross section representing the photon splitting into a quark pair and the subsequent interaction with the target nucleus. Finally, we will convolute our expression with a fragmentation function to obtain the
\mathrm{D}^{0}
differential cross section. The final result is written in Eq. (
26
).

II.1
Photon flux

At high energies, the electromagnetic field emitted by a point-like particle is Lorentz contracted in the direction parallel to its motion. As a result, the interaction time between the transversely boosted electromagnetic field and a target becomes very short:
\Delta t=B_{\perp}/\gamma
, where
B_{\perp}
is the impact parameter and
\gamma
the Lorentz factor. This interaction can be approximated as a real pulse of light incident on the target in the Weizsäcker-Williams method (one-photon exchange)
[
19
,
20
]
. In this approximation, the Fourier transform of the Lorentz-contracted electromagnetic fields emitted by a high-energy charged particle is equivalent to a flux of photons
N(\omega)
with energy
\omega

[
1
,
21
]
:

\displaystyle N(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\perp}>2R}\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{B}_{\perp}\frac{Z^{2}\alpha_{\mathrm{e.m.}}\omega^{2}}{\pi^{2}\gamma^{2}}K_{1}^{2}\bigg(\frac{\omega B_{\perp}}{\gamma}\bigg)\;.

(1)

Here
Z
is the ion charge,
\alpha_{\mathrm{e.m.}}
=1/137 the fine structure constant and
\gamma=A\sqrt{s}/(2M_{A})
is the boost factor of the nucleus, with
\sqrt{s}
the nucleon-nucleon center-of-mass collision energy. We consider lead nuclei with charge
Z=82
, atomic number
A=208
and mass
M_{A}=193.64
GeV. The modified Bessel function
K_{1}
gives the flux for transversely polarized photons.
The photon flux is large in the regime when they are coherently emitted from the whole nucleus, which leads to the coherence condition
[
22
]
:

Q^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\;.

(2)

Because the nuclear radius is large,
the photons can be considered as quasi-real, with a maximum value of
Q^{2}_{\mathrm{max}}\approx 0.001\;\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
for a lead nucleus.

Thus, the UPC is mediated by a quasi-real photon from one of the swarms of equivalent photons that ‘‘interacts directly’’

1

1
, we concretely choose our coordinate system such that the right-moving nucleus is the photon source and the left-moving nucleus the target.
We will want to contrast our calculation with CMS data taken with an “Xn0n” event selection, referring to at least one neutron (“Xn”) being detected on one side and no neutrons (“0n”) on the other side by zero-degree calorimeters
[
23

Since we are looking at inclusive photon-target scattering with no rapidity gap between the produced system and the target, the target nucleus will practically always break up and emit neutrons, and we do not need to specifically include the “Xn” condition in our calculation. The photon-emitting nucleus, on the other hand, can break up due to Coulomb dissociation, emit neutrons and fall outside of the “0n” category.
To incorporate this condition in our predictions, we include in the cross section a factor
P(B_{\perp})
, which represents the probability that the projectile nucleus does not emit any neutrons (0n) using the parametrization introduced in Ref.
[

P(B_{\perp})=\mathrm{exp}(-S/B_{\perp}^{2})\;,

(3)

where

S=5.45\cdot 10^{-5}\frac{Z^{3}(A-Z)}{A^{2/3}}\;.

(4)

For lead nucleus
S=(10.4\;\mathrm{fm})^{2}

In our numerical analysis, we also use the parameter

obtained in Refs.
[
24

25

II.2
Partonic cross section in Light-Cone Perturbation Theory

In this subsection, we first derive the partonic cross section in LCPT and CGC formalism for the leading order quark-pair production process:

\gamma_{\lambda}+A\rightarrow q\bar{q}+X\;.

(6)

Subsequently, we will integrate over the momentum of the antiquark to obtain the inclusive charm production cross section in the CGC formalism. Our expressions are consistent with the results for massless and also heavy quarks obtained in Refs.
[
14
,
16
,
15
]
. The mathematical conventions used for the normalization of the states and the Fourier transforms are given in Appendix
A
.

We work in light-cone coordinates where
v^{\pm}=(v^{0}\pm v^{3})/\sqrt{2}
and
\bm{v}_{\perp}=(v^{1},v^{2})
for any 4-vector
v^{\mu}
. In this notation, the momentum of the incoming photon is expressed as
q^{\mu}=(q^{+},0^{-},\bm{0}_{\perp})
and its transverse polarization by
\epsilon^{\mu}_{\lambda}=(0^{+},0^{-},\bm{\varepsilon}_{\lambda})
. Here
\bm{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
are circular polarization vectors, written in Eq. (
43
). Before the interaction, the photon splits into a quark and antiquark with respectively momenta
k^{\mu}_{0}=(zq^{+},k_{2}^{-},\bm{k}_{0\perp})
and
k^{\mu}_{1}=((1-z)q^{+},k_{1}^{-},\bm{k}_{1\perp})
, where
\bm{k}_{0\perp}=-\bm{k}_{1\perp}
. The
z=k_{0}^{+}/q^{+}
is the fraction of longitudinal momentum transferred from the photon to the quark. The colored partons then interact with the target nucleus which is described as a classical gluon field
A^{-}(x)
in the Color Glass Condensate. As illustrated in Fig.
2
and discussed later in this subsection, the target does not contribute any
k^{+}
momentum in the interaction.

To obtain the invariant scattering amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{\gamma\rightarrow q\bar{q}}
for the process in Eq. (
6

momentum

[
27

:

\displaystyle\big|\gamma_{\lambda}(q^{+},0^{-},\bm{0}_{\perp})\big\rangle_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Z_{\gamma_{\lambda}}}\Bigg\{\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}\Psi^{\gamma_{\lambda}\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}|q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}\rangle_{0}

(7)

\displaystyle+\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}g_{2}}\Psi^{\gamma\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}g_{2}}|q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}g_{2}\rangle_{0}+\dots\Bigg\}\;

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}\Psi^{\gamma_{\lambda}\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}b^{\dagger}_{0}d^{\dagger}_{1}|0\rangle+\dots\;.

Here
|\gamma_{\lambda}(q^{+},0^{-},\bm{0}_{\perp})\rangle_{\mathrm{D}}
is the physical (dressed) state in the interaction picture, while the free (bare) states are denoted by the subscript
0
. We work here at leading order in both the strong and electromagnetic couplings. This implies that we use a one-photon exchange approximation and neglect higher order contributions in the renormalization factor
Z_{\gamma_{\lambda}^{*}}=1+\mathcal{O}(e^{2})
. Additionally, at leading order in the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
, we neglect higher order bare states that involve additional gluons. The notation
\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}
represents a sum over the quantum numbers of each parton in the Fock state and it includes a phase-space integration
[
28
]
:

\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}:=\sum_{h_{0},\alpha_{0},f_{0}}\sum_{h_{1},\alpha_{1},f_{1}}\prod_{i=0}^{1}\bigg[\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{i}^{+}}{(2\pi)}\frac{\theta(k_{i}^{+})}{2k_{i}^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{k}_{i\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bigg]\;.

(8)

Here
\alpha_{0}(\alpha_{1})
represents the color of the quark(antiquark) and
h_{0}(h_{1})
their helicity (
h_{i}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
). For the quark and antiquark creation operators:
b_{0}^{\dagger}
and
d_{1}^{\dagger}
, we use the shorthand notation
b^{\dagger}_{0}\coloneqq b(k_{0}^{+},\bm{k}_{0\perp},h_{0},\alpha_{0})
and
d^{\dagger}_{1}\coloneqq d(k_{1}^{+},\bm{k}_{1\perp},h_{1},\alpha_{1})
. The light-cone wave functions (LCWFs)
\Psi
in Eq. (
7
) can be computed perturbatively from interaction vertices. These conserve the spatial momentum
\underline{p}\equiv(p^{+},\bm{p}_{\perp})
but not the light-cone energy
p^{-}=(m^{2}+\bm{p}_{\perp}^{2})/2p^{+}
. This is because we are working in “old-fashioned” perturbation theory, where the perturbative expression is expressed in terms of on-shell energies of intermediate particles, but interactions cause transitions between these different energy bare states.

At the lowest order in
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}
, the LCWF in Eq. (
7
) is

\displaystyle\Psi^{\gamma_{\lambda}\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}(\bm{k}_{0\perp},\bm{k}_{1\perp},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle 0|d_{1}b_{0}\hat{V}_{I}(0)a_{\gamma}^{\dagger}|0\rangle}{\mathrm{ED}}\;,

(9)

where
\hat{V}_{I}(0)
is the interaction operator and ED is the energy denominator that contain the light-front energies of the incoming and outgoing states:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ED}

\displaystyle=q_{\gamma}^{-}-k_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}^{-}

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{q}_{\perp}^{2}-Q^{2}}{2q^{+}}-\frac{\bm{k}_{0\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}{2k_{0}^{+}}-\frac{\bm{k}_{1\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}{2k_{1}^{+}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\bm{k}_{0\perp}^{2}+m^{2})}{2q^{+}z(1-z)}\;.

Here
Q^{2}=0
and
\bm{q}_{\perp}=0
for the quasi-real photon. We have also used the momentum conservation
q^{+}=k_{0}^{+}+k_{1}^{+}
and expressed longitudinal momenta in terms of the momentum fraction
z=k_{0}^{+}/q^{+}
.
These light-cone energy denominators, calculated with on-shell single particle energies, represent the off-shellness of the whole multiparticle intermediate state. They correspond to propagators, which in covariant theory are forced to be off-shell by energy conservation at the vertices.

The expression for the interaction operator
\hat{V}_{I}
and the calculation of the numerator in Eq. (
9

[
29
,
30

28
]

\displaystyle\Psi(\bm{k}_{0\perp},\bm{k}_{1\perp},z)

\displaystyle\quad=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\underline{q}-\underline{k_{1}}-\underline{k_{0}})\delta_{\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}}\frac{ee_{f}\bar{u}_{0}\not{\epsilon}_{\lambda}(q^{\mu})v_{1}}{\mathrm{ED}}

(11)

\displaystyle\quad=-2q^{+}(2\pi)\delta(q^{+}-k_{1}^{+}-k_{0}^{+})\psi(\bm{k}_{0\perp},\bm{k}_{1\perp},z)\>.

In this expression
e
is the electromagnetic constant and
e_{f}
the fractional charge of the quark:
e_{c}=2/3
for charm and
e_{b}=-1/3
for bottom quarks. The longitudinal momentum
2q^{+}
in the second line arises from the energy denominator in Eq. (
10
). By factorizing out the longitudinal momentum conservation, we introduced the
reduced wave function

\psi(\bm{k}_{0\perp},\bm{k}_{1\perp},z)
.

The matrix elements between the spinors in Eq. (
11

[
31

28
]
. For transversally polarized photons they take the form:

\bar{u}_{0}\not{\epsilon}_{\lambda}(q^{\mu})v_{1}=\bigg[\delta_{h_{0},-h_{1}}\frac{2}{\sqrt{z(1-z)}}\Big(z\delta_{\lambda,2h_{0}}-(1-z)\delta_{\lambda,-2h_{0}}\Big)\varepsilon_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{k}_{0\perp}+\delta_{h_{0},h_{1}}\delta_{\lambda,2h_{1}}\frac{\sqrt{2}m}{\sqrt{z(1-z)}}\bigg]\;.

(12)

This expression separates the helicity-conserving vertex, which is independent of the mass, from the helicity-flip matrix element, which is proportional to the mass.

With the explicit form of the physical incoming state in Eq. (
7
), we can now calculate the invariant scattering amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{\gamma\rightarrow q\bar{q}}
from the matrix elements of the incoming and outgoing states
[
32
,
27
,
33
]
:

2q^{+}(2\pi)\delta(q^{+}-{k^{\prime}_{0}}^{+}-{k^{\prime}_{1}}^{+})i\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{q_{\perp}}=0\rightarrow{\bm{k}_{0\perp}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}_{1\perp}}^{\prime})\\
\equiv\big\langle q({\bm{k}_{0\perp}}^{\prime},{k^{\prime}_{0}}^{+})\bar{q}({\bm{k}_{1\perp}}^{\prime},{k^{\prime}_{1}}^{+})\big|(\hat{S}-\mathds{1})\big|\gamma(q^{+},0^{-},\bm{0}_{\perp})\big\rangle\;.

(13)

Here we subtract the identity case where there is no scattering. The operator
\hat{S}
represents the
eikonal
scattering of the incoming quark-pair with the gluon field in the target nucleus. In this eikonal interaction, the partons undergo a color rotation and transverse momentum is transferred from the target, but their helicities and transverse positions are conserved. The scattering operator
\hat{S}
operates on the bare states in
mixed
space as:

\hat{S}\big|q(q^{+},\bm{x}_{\perp},i)\big\rangle=\sum_{j}[U_{F}(\bm{x}_{\perp})]_{ji}\big|(q^{+},\bm{x}_{\perp},j)\big\rangle\>,

(14)

where
i,j
denote the color indices of the quark and
\hat{S}|0\rangle=|0\rangle
.
The Wilson line operator
U_{F}(\bm{x}_{\perp})
in the fundamental representation represents the quark propagator in the gluon color field
A_{a}^{-}(x)
of the target nucleus. It is expressed as
[
28
]

U_{F}(\bm{x}_{\perp})=\mathcal{P}\;\mathrm{exp}\Bigg(-ig\int\mathrm{d}x^{+}A^{-}_{a}(x^{+},\bm{x}_{\perp})t^{a}\Bigg)\;,

(15)

where
\mathcal{P}
is a path-ordering operator and
t^{a}
fundamental color matrices.

Since the scattering operator
\hat{S}
acts in coordinate space, we Fourier transform the scattering matrix element in Eq. (
13
) to mixed space:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\langle q(\bm{k}_{0\perp}^{\prime},k^{\prime+}_{0},\beta^{\prime}_{0})\bar{q}(\bm{k}_{1\perp}^{\prime},k^{\prime+}_{1},\beta^{\prime}_{1})|\hat{S}-\mathds{1}|q(\bm{k}_{0\perp},k_{0}^{+},\alpha_{0})\bar{q}(\bm{k}_{1\perp},k_{1}^{+},\alpha_{1})\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp}}e^{i\bm{k}_{0\perp}\bm{x}_{0\perp}+i\bm{k}_{1\perp}\bm{x}_{1\perp}-i\bm{k}_{0\perp}^{\prime}\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime}+i\bm{k}_{1\perp}^{\prime}\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta_{0}\beta_{1}}\sum_{q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}\Big[U_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp})_{\alpha_{0}\beta_{0}}U^{\dagger}_{F}(\bm{x}_{1\perp})_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}-\delta_{\beta_{0}\alpha_{0}}\delta_{\beta_{1}\alpha_{1}}\Big]

\displaystyle\crossproduct\Psi(\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp},z)\big\langle q(\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},k^{\prime+}_{0},\beta^{\prime}_{0})\bar{q}(\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime},k^{\prime+}_{1},\beta^{\prime}_{1})|q(\bm{x}_{0\perp},k_{0}^{+},\beta_{0})\bar{q}(\bm{x}_{1\perp},k_{1}^{+},\beta_{1})\big\rangle\;.\vphantom{\int}

In the above expression, we have used the short-hand notation in Eq. (
8
) for the phase space integration in momentum space as well as
\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{x}_{\perp}=\int_{\bm{x}_{\perp}}
for the Fourier transform integrals. We also omit helicity indices for simplicity, but they are easy to restore when needed since helicity is conserved in the eikonal interaction with the color field. The
U_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp})
and
U_{F}^{\dagger}(\bm{x}_{1\perp})

\hat{S}

\Psi^{\gamma\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}(\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp},z)

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma\rightarrow q\bar{q}}=\int_{\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime}}e^{i\bm{k}_{0\perp}\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime}}e^{i\bm{k}_{1\perp}\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime}}\psi(\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime},z)\\
\crossproduct\bigg(\Big[U_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime})U^{\dagger}_{F}(\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime})\Big]_{\beta^{\prime}_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{1}}-\delta_{\beta^{\prime}_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{1}}\bigg)\;.

(17)

\big\langle|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\big\rangle=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp}\\
\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!e^{i\bm{k}_{0\perp}(\bm{x}_{0\perp}-\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime})}e^{i\bm{k}_{1\perp}(\bm{x}_{1\perp}-\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime})}\big|\psi^{\gamma\rightarrow q_{0}\bar{q}_{1}}\big|^{2}\\
\times\Big(Q_{\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp},\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime}}-D_{\bm{x}_{0\perp},\bm{x}_{1\perp}}-D_{\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime}}+1\Big)\;.

(18)

In the above expression, we introduced the dipoles
(D)

and quadrupoles
(Q)
which are averaged over the color configurations of the target:

\displaystyle D=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\big\langle\mathrm{Tr}\big[U_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp})U^{\dagger}_{F}(\bm{x}_{1\perp})\big]\big\rangle_{x}\;,

(19)

\displaystyle Q=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\big\langle\mathrm{Tr}\big[U_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp})U^{\dagger}_{F}(\bm{x}_{1\perp})U_{F}(\bm{x}_{1\perp}^{\prime})U^{\dagger}_{F}(\bm{x}_{0\perp}^{\prime})\big]\big\rangle_{x}\;.

,

The

For simplicity, we also approximate the rapidity of the

Additionally, in our numerical analysis in Sec.

II.4
The collinear factorization limit

Before moving to our numerical analysis, let us first analyze our result (
26
) in more detail. In particular we want to demonstrate how to take the collinear limit of our calculation. We will see that the cross section reduces to what can clearly be identified as the hard factor and the collinear gluon PDF as they would appear in the corresponding collinearly factorized expression.

In the actual calculation we will take the dipole operator from a parametrization satisfying the Balitsky-Kovchegov evolution equation
[
34
,
35
]
with initial conditions fit to HERA data. However, for the purposes of this discussion it is better to use a more tractable analytical parametrization. For this purpose we will here take the dipole in Eq. (
19
) to be given by the explicit solution in the McLerran-Venugopalan (MV) model
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
:

D_{\mathrm{MV}}(r=|\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{y}_{\perp}|)=\mathrm{exp}\Bigg(-\frac{Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}r^{2}}{4}\mathrm{ln}\frac{1}{r\Lambda}\Bigg)\;,

(28)

where
Q_{\mathrm{s}}
is known as the saturation scale and the parameter
\Lambda
is an infrared cutoff.

The transverse momentum dependence of the cross section originates from the
\bm{\ell}_{\perp}
–integral in Eq. (
26
):

\mathrm{I}_{\ell}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\ell}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}D(\ell_{\perp})\{\dots\}\;.

(29)

The dipole
D(\ell_{\perp})
in momentum space (Fourier transform (
44
) of Eq. (
28
)) follows the approximate scaling behavior:

D(\ell_{\perp})\sim\begin{cases}\mathrm{const.},&\ell_{\perp}\ll Q_{\mathrm{s}}\>\mathrm{(saturated\;regime)}\\
1/\ell_{\perp}^{4},&\ell_{\perp}\gg Q_{\mathrm{s}}\>\mathrm{(dilute\;regime)}\;.\end{cases}

(30)

The saturation scale
Q_{\mathrm{s}}
separates the perturbative QCD regime, governed by linear evolution equations (DGLAP and BFKL), from the non-linear saturation regime described by the BK equation
[
28
]
.
For charm quarks at LHC energies, in practice
2
2

2

We refer the reader to Ref.
[
40
]
for the definition of the saturation scale
Q_{\mathrm{s}}
used in this work.

Q_{\mathrm{s}}\sim m\approx 1.5
GeV, making charm observables sensitive probes of gluon saturation.

The dipole amplitude is multiplied in Eq. (
26
) by the expression inside the braces, which is a kind of “hard factor” for charm production. In both terms of this expression the term inside the
square brackets
[\dots]
vanishes linearly in
\ell_{\perp}
in the limit
\ell_{\perp}\rightarrow 0
. On the other hand, for
\ell_{\perp}\gg k_{0\perp}=k_{D\perp}/z_{f}
it approaches a constant. This means that the term inside the braces follows the approximate scaling:

\{\dots\}\sim\begin{cases}\ell_{\perp}^{2},&\ell_{\perp}\ll k_{0\perp}\;,\\
\mathrm{const.},&\ell_{\perp}\gg k_{0\perp}\;.\end{cases}

(31)

We expect that for large momenta,
k_{0\perp}\gg Q_{\mathrm{s}}
, the CGC expressions should exhibit a behavior corresponding to a collinear factorization picture of the same process. Let us therefore analyze how the partonic cross section in Eq. (
24
) behaves in the limit
k_{0\perp}\gg Q_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Combining the estimates (
30
) and (
31
) we see that the integral
\mathrm{I}_{\ell_{\perp}}
in Eq. (
29
) exhibits a different behavior in three distinct kinematic regimes:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(A)}\ \ \ell_{\perp}\ll Q_{\mathrm{s}}\ll k_{0\perp}:\int^{Q_{\mathrm{s}}}_{0}\mathrm{d}\ell_{\perp}\ell_{\perp}^{3}

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{(B)}\ \ Q_{\mathrm{s}}\ll\ell_{\perp}\ll k_{0\perp}:\int^{k_{0\perp}}_{Q_{\mathrm{s}}}\mathrm{d}\ell_{\perp}\frac{1}{\ell_{\perp}}

(33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{(C)}\ \ Q_{\mathrm{s}}\ll k_{0\perp}\ll\ell_{\perp}:\int_{k_{0\perp}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\ell_{\perp}\frac{1}{\ell_{\perp}^{3}}\;.

(34)

We observe that the integral from region (B) is logarithmic. This logarithm is the dominant contribution in the regime of validity of collinear factorization
k_{0\perp}\gg Q_{\mathrm{s}}
.
The usual way of digging this limit out of the full expression (
24
) is the following. We first start from saying that typically
\ell_{\perp}\sim Q_{\mathrm{s}}\ll k_{0\perp}
, and develop the term inside braces in the limit
\ell_{\perp}\ll k_{0\perp}
to get

\mathrm{I}_{\ell_{\perp}}^{\mathrm{collinear}}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\ell}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}D(\ell_{\perp})\bm{\ell}_{\perp}^{2}\\
\crossproduct\Bigg\{2z\frac{2m^{2}k_{0\perp}^{2}+[z^{2}+(1-z)^{2}](k_{0\perp}^{4}+m^{4})}{(k_{0\perp}^{2}+m^{2})^{4}}\Bigg\}\;.

(35)

Now, in principle, the upper integration limit of
\ell_{\perp}
is infinity, and with the behavior (
30
) the integral does not actually converge. However, this divergence is just an artefact from incorrectly using the
\ell_{\perp}\ll k_{0\perp}
behavior of the hard factor in regime C, which does not give a logarithmic contribution. It can therefore be fixed by setting an upper limit
\ell_{\perp}\leq k_{0\perp}
or
\ell_{\perp}\leq k_{D\perp}
(the choice of which one does not affect the leading logarithm). This turns the cross section into a product of a hard factor which is independent of
\ell_{\perp}
, and an integrated gluon distribution

xg(x,k_{0\perp}^{2})\propto\int^{k_{0\perp}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{\ell}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}D(\ell_{\perp})\bm{\ell}_{\perp}^{2}\;,

(36)

which depends on the hard scale of the process
k_{0\perp}^{2}
. With the
D(\ell_{\perp})\sim 1/\ell_{\perp}^{4}
behavior in the MV model (
28
), the integrated gluon distribution behaves logarithmically in the hard scale, which corresponds to the DGLAP evolution of the gluon distribution.
This procedure can also be thought of as the limit being
\ell_{\perp}\ll m
, if we assume the quark mass to be the large scale, and the collinear factorization regime as
m\gg Q_{\mathrm{s}}
.

In the next section, we will numerically compare the full integral
\mathrm{I}_{\ell_{\perp}}
in Eq. (
29
) to the collinear approximation
\mathrm{I}_{\ell_{\perp}}^{\mathrm{coll.}}
in Eq. (
35
) for a fixed photon energy
\omega
.
We believe this is a more informative apples-to-apples comparison of the CGC formalism and collinear factorization than a full calculation with state-of-the art DGLAP evolve parton distribution. By comparing the CGC calculation with its own collinear limit, we can better assess the effect of the
Q_{\mathrm{s}}
–scale transverse momentum
\ell_{\perp}
on observables. The comparison we make in the next section focuses on the transition region from the saturation regime
k_{0\perp}\sim Q_{\mathrm{s}}
to the collinear regime
k_{0\perp}\gtrsim Q_{\mathrm{s}}
, which is more interesting for saturation physics. This differs from the correct resummation of the logarithms
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\ln k_{0\perp}^{2}/Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}
, which is, at least parametrically, only relevant for larger values of
k_{0\perp}
, and can only be performed correctly by a proper DGLAP evolution of the integrated gluon distribution.

IV
Summary

In this paper we have derived in Eq. (
26
) the differential cross section for inclusive
\mathrm{D}^{0}
photoproduction in lead-lead UPCs in the CGC formalism, at lowest order in the strong coupling constant. Our intermediate result for quark pair production in Eq. (
24
) agrees with Ref.
[
14
]
, and our final expression for single inclusive charm photoproduction is consistent with Refs.
[
16
,
15
]
.

Concretely, in this work we apply this framework in a comparison to CMS data for the “Xn0n” event class where the projectile nucleus remains intact after the interaction. This constraint is imposed in the cross section by a probability distribution, Eq. (
3
), for the “0n” emission in the projectile. In contrast, the target nucleus always dissociates in inclusive
\mathrm{D}^{0}
production, emitting “X” neutrons in its forward region. We leave for a future publication a more refined calculation of this breakup survival factor along the lines in Ref.
[
47
]
, where it is calculated using the optical Glauber model.
The authors of Ref.
[
47
]
found that modifying the photon flux to account for the nuclei as extended charge distributions significantly affects the highest-energy photons
(\omega=10^{-2}\sqrt{s}/2)
. This is not significant in the kinematical domain considered in this work.

The CGC description of the dense target is constrained by HERA DIS data
[
40
]
, and the hadronization process is described with a lowest-order fragmentation function
[
26
]
. Thus, there are no free parameters in our setup and we calculate
\mathrm{D}^{0}
photoproduction consistently with the DIS data.
A relatively good description of the CMS data is obtained especially at low
\mathrm{D}^{0}
transverse momenta, although the data would prefer a more steeply falling spectrum.

We also compare the collinear factorization limit of our calculation with the full result in Figs.
6
and
7
. This comparison first of all shows that there is no fundamental disagreement, the two approaches are perfectly consistent qualitatively in the region where both are applicable. At
k_{\mathrm{D}\perp}\lesssim 2\;
GeV the transverse momentum dependence starts to be strongly affected by gluon saturation, which the collinear factorization limit does not treat correctly. On the other hand, at high
k_{\mathrm{D}\perp}
large logarithms of transverse momenta need to be resummed with a DGLAP evolution of the collinear gluon distribution, which we do not do here.

We expect that including heavy quarks into the DIS fits for the initial condition parametrization of the dipole amplitude in Eq. (
28
) could improve the normalization of the cross section. This would lead to a better agreement with the CMS data at large transverse momentum. Furthermore, we emphasize the lack of experimental data for
k_{\mathrm{D}\perp}<2\;\mathrm{GeV}
, which is a region of most interest for CGC calculations where a strong saturation effect (nuclear modification factor
\sim 0.7
) is predicted. Unfortunately, at very small
k_{\mathrm{D}\perp}
the collinear fragmentation approach that we use for hadronization might become more questionable. Overall, the smooth matching of CGC calculations to DGLAP evolution in terms of either integrated or transverse momentum dependent PDFs has been actively discussed recently
[
48
,
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
]
. We believe the inclusive hadron production in
\gamma+A
or
\gamma+p
collisions is an interesting process both experimentally and theoretically for understanding this physics.

Appendix A
General conventions

The transversely polarized photon 4-vector in the light-cone gauge is

\epsilon^{\mu}_{\lambda=\pm}(q)=\left(0,\frac{\mathbf{q_{\perp}}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}}{q^{+}},\bm{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}\right)\;.

(41)

This is orthonormal and complete:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu}(q,\lambda)\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(q,\lambda^{\prime})=-\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\;,

\displaystyle q^{\mu}\epsilon_{\mu}(q,\lambda)=0\;.

(42)

The circular polarization
\bm{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}
can be written as

\bm{\varepsilon}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mp 1\\
-i\end{pmatrix}\>.

(43)

In subsection
II.2
, we use the following convention for the two-dimensional Fourier transforms between mixed and momentum space:

\displaystyle f(\bm{p}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\int\differential[]\bm{x}_{\perp}e^{i\bm{p}_{\perp}\bm{x}_{\perp}}f(\bm{x}_{\perp})\;,

(44)

\displaystyle f(\bm{x}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential[]\bm{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i\bm{p}_{\perp}\bm{x}_{\perp}}f(\bm{p}_{\perp})\;.

(45)

In mixed space, quark eigenstates with light-front helicity
h
and color
c
in the fundamental representation are normalized as

\displaystyle\big\langle q(p^{+},\bm{x}_{\perp},h_{p},c_{p})\big|q(q^{+},\bm{y}_{\perp},h_{q},c_{q})\big\rangle

(46)

\displaystyle=2p^{+}(2\pi)\delta(p^{+}-q^{+})\delta^{(2)}(\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{y}_{\perp})\delta_{h_{p},h_{q}}\delta_{c_{p},c_{q}}\;.

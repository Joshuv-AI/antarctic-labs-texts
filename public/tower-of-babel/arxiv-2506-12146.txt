Structural results on the Weak Commutativity construction
R. Bastos, R. de Oliveira, G. Ortega

Abstract
. The weak commutativity group χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) is generated by two isomorphic groups G G and G φ G^{\varphi} subject to the relations [ g , g φ ] = 1 [g,g^{\varphi}]=1 for all g ∈ G g\in G . We obtain new expressions for the terms of the derived series and the lower central series of χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) . We also present new bounds for the exponent of some sections of χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) . 1.
Introduction

Let
G^{\varphi}
be a isomorphic copy of the group
G
, via
\varphi:G\rightarrow G^{\varphi}
, given by
g\mapsto g^{\varphi}
. In
[
29
]
, Sidki introduces and analyzes the
weak commutativity group
,
\chi(G)
, which is defined as follows

\chi(G)=\langle G\cup G^{\varphi}\mid[g,g^{\varphi}]=1,\forall g\in G\rangle.

The group
\chi(G)
is also known as the double Sidki of
G
. Now, we recall some homomorphisms associated with the group
\chi(G)
to describe some of its normal subgroups and sections. First of all
\chi(G)
maps onto
G
by
g\mapsto g
,
g^{\varphi}\mapsto g

with kernel
L(G)=\left\langle g^{-1}g^{\varphi}\mid g\;\in G\right\rangle
and it
maps onto
G\times G
by
g\mapsto\left(g,1\right),g^{\varphi}\mapsto\left(1,g\right)
with kernel
D(G)=[G,G^{\varphi}]=\langle[g,h^{\varphi}]\mid g,h\in G\rangle
. As a consequence of the defining relation of the group
\chi(G)
, the subgroups
L(G)
and
D(G)
commute. Furthermore, if we denote by
T(G)
the
subgroup of
G\times G\times G
generated by
\{(g,g,1),(1,g,g)\mid g\in G\}
, then
\chi(G)
maps onto
T(G)
by
g\mapsto\left(g,g,1\right)
,
g^{\varphi}\mapsto\left(1,g,g\right)
, with kernel
W(G)=L(G)\cap D(G)
. The subgroup
W(G)
is central in
L(G)D(G)
and
\chi(G)/W(G)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
G\times G\times G
. Another normal subgroup of
\chi(G)
is
R(G)=[G,L(G),G^{\varphi}]\leq W(G)
. The weak commutativity group has some links with homology. It is worth pointing out that the section
W(G)/R(G)
is isomorphic to the Schur multiplier
M(G)
(cf.
[
29
, Lemma 4.1.11]
and
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
). See also
[
24
]
. Moreover, the section
D(G)/R(G)
is isomorphic to the non-abelian exterior square
G\wedge G
. See
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[

,
[

[

[
23
]
,
[
26
]
and
[

]
for a rich source of results regarding this construction.

In the present article we give more information on the structure of the group
\chi(G)
. The key approach was to describe a certain subgroup of
R(G)
.

The structure of the subgroup
R(G)
is not yet completely understood, making it an obstacle to understanding the group
\chi(G)
. In
[
29
]
, Sidki described
R(G)
for cases where
G
is either an abelian or a perfect group. Specifically, he showed that if
G
is an abelian group, then
R(G)^{2}=1
. Later, in
[
19
]
, Kochloukova and Mendonça proved that if
G
is a
2
-generated group, then
R(G)=1
(see also
[
7
, Remark 3.2 (ii)]
). We obtain the following related results.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a group.

(a)

If
G
is periodic, then
R(G)
is periodic.

(b)

If
G
has finite exponent, then
\exp(R(G))
divides
\exp(G)\cdot\exp(G^{\prime})
.

We do not know whether the bound obtained in Theorem A (b) is sharp. At this moment, we have no examples that attain the bound in Theorem A (b). See Remark
4.6
for more details. It is worth to mentioning that in the proof of Theorem A was crucial to understand the subgroup
L_{1,2}(G):=[L_{1}(G),L_{2}(G)]\leq R(G)
(see Theorem
4.1
, below). In the next result, we present more information on the subgroup

.

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated periodic group. Then
L_{1,2}(G)
is finite.

It is well-known that there are finitely generated periodic groups which are infinite. Some examples with this property it has been constructed in

In this direction, we also deduce new descriptions of the lower central and derived series of the group

Theorem C
.

Let
D=D(G)
,

(b)

2.
Preliminaries

We follow the notations from
[
29
]
and review some basic properties of
the group
\chi(G)
. Let
G^{\varphi}
be an isomorphic copy of
G
. By definition,

\chi(G)=\langle G\cup G^{\varphi}\mid[g,g^{\varphi}]=1,\forall g\in G\rangle.

Additionally set

L=L(G)
,
W=W(G)
,
R=R(G)
,
L_{1}=L_{1}(G)=[L,G]
,
L_{2}=L_{2}(G)=[L,G^{\varphi}]
, and
L_{1,2}=[L_{1},L_{2}]
, we will omit the
G
from these symbols whenever the
context allows it.

As usual, for arbitrary elements
x,y\in G
we write
x^{y}=y^{-1}xy
for the conjugate of
x
by
y
; the commutator of
x
and
y
is then
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
and our commutators are left normed:
[x,y,z]=[[x,y],z]
. To simplify notation, we write
[g,\varphi]
instead of
g^{-1}g^{\varphi}
.

The following basic properties are consequences of
the defining relations of
\chi(G)
and the commutator rules (see
[
29
, Proposition 4.1.13]
and
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
for more details).

Lemma 2.1
.

The following relations hold in
\chi(G)
, for all

x,y,y_{i},z,z_{i}\in G
.

(i)

[x,y^{\varphi}]=[x^{\varphi},y]
;

(ii)

[x,y^{\varphi}]^{z^{\varphi}}=[x,y^{\varphi}]^{z}
;

(iii)

[x,y^{\varphi}]^{\omega(z_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}},\ldots,z_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}})}=[x,y^{\varphi}]^{\omega(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})}
for
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{1,\varphi\}
and any word
\omega(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in G
;

(iv)

[x^{\varphi},y,x]=[x,y,x^{\varphi}]
;

(v)

[x^{\varphi},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x]=[x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x^{\varphi}]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
29
, Lemma 4.1.6]

Let
a,b,g,h,m\in G
, then

(i)

[h,g^{-\varphi}g]=[g^{\varphi},h][h,g]=[h,g][g^{\varphi},h]
;

(ii)

[h^{-1}h^{\varphi},g^{\varphi}]=[g^{\varphi},h][h^{\varphi},g^{\varphi}]=[h^{\varphi},g^{\varphi}][g^{\varphi},h]
.

The subgroup
L_{1,2}
plays a central role in this work. To investigate some of its properties, we use
[
29
, Lemma 4.1.10]
, which states that
[L_{1},L_{2}]\leq L_{1}\cap L_{2}=W
. Therefore,
[L_{1},L_{2}]
centralizes both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.

The next lemma shows that commutators are bilinear in their inputs and will be essential for the proof of Theorem C.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in L_{1}
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in L_{2}
. Then we have

[\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}]=[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}][\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}].

Proof.

Once
L_{1,2}
centralizes
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, we have that,

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}]=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}]^{\alpha_{2}}[\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}]^{\beta_{2}}[\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}]^{\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}][\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}][\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}].\qed

The following results will be used on Theorem
4.4
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
g,h,a,b,c\in G
. Then, the following properties hold.

(i)

[\,[g,\varphi][h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]=[[g,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}][[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]\ \mod(L_{1,2})
;

(ii)

[\,[g,\varphi,a][h,\varphi,b],c^{\varphi}]=[g,\varphi,a,c^{\varphi}][h,\varphi,b,c^{\varphi}]

Proof.

Let
g,h,a,b,c\in G
. For
(i)
consider
x=[[g,\varphi][h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]
. Then, module
L_{1,2}
, we have that

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=[[[g,\varphi],a]^{[h,\varphi]}[[h,\varphi],a],b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[[g,\varphi],a]^{[h,\varphi]},b^{\varphi}]^{[[h,\varphi],a]}[[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[[g,\varphi],a]^{[h,\varphi]},b^{\varphi}][[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]\ \ \ \ \big([[[g,\varphi],a]^{[h,\varphi]},b^{\varphi}]\in R\big)

\displaystyle=[[[g,\varphi],a],(b^{\varphi})^{[h,\varphi]^{-1}}]^{[h,\varphi]}[[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[[g,\varphi],a],b^{\varphi}[b^{\varphi},{[h,\varphi]^{-1}}]]^{[h,\varphi]}[[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=\left([[[g,\varphi],a],[b^{\varphi},{[h,\varphi]^{-1}}]][[g,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]^{[b^{\varphi},{[h,\varphi]^{-1}}]}\right)^{[h,\varphi]}[[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[g,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]^{[h,\varphi]}[[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]\ \ \ \ \big([[[g,\varphi],a],[b^{\varphi},{[h,\varphi]^{-1}}]]\in L_{1,2}\big)

\displaystyle=[[g,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}][[h,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \big([[g,\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}]\in R\big)

Now, to prove
(ii)
, observe that

\displaystyle[[[g,\varphi],a][[h,\varphi],b],c^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[g,\varphi],a,c^{\varphi}]^{[[h,\varphi],b]}[[h,\varphi],b,c^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[g,\varphi],a,c^{\varphi}][[h,\varphi],b,c^{\varphi}],

since
[[g,\varphi],a,c^{\varphi}]\in R
.
∎

The next is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a group. Then,

\frac{R}{L_{1,2}}=\big\langle{[g,\varphi,a,b^{\varphi}]}L_{1,2}\mid g,a,b\in G\big\rangle.

Proof.

Let
g,a,b,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in L_{1}
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
item (ii)

\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i},b^{\varphi}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\alpha_{i},b^{\varphi}].

And, module
L_{1,2}
we have that by Lemma
2.4
item (i)

\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}[g_{i},\varphi],a,b^{\varphi}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left[g_{i},\varphi,a,b^{\varphi}\right].

Therefore, every element of
R/L_{1,2}
splits in a product of elements of the form
[g,\varphi,a,b^{\varphi}]L_{1,2}
.
∎

3.
The group
\nu(G)

The non-abelian tensor square
G\otimes G
of a group
G
, as introduced by Brown and Loday
[
11
]
, is defined to be the group generated by all symbols
\;\,g\otimes h,\;g,h\in G
, subject to the relations

gg_{1}\otimes h=(g^{g_{1}}\otimes h^{g_{1}})(g_{1}\otimes h)\quad\mbox{and}\quad g\otimes hh_{1}=(g\otimes h_{1})(g^{h_{1}}\otimes h^{h_{1}})

for all
g,g_{1},h,h_{1}\in G
, where we write
x^{y}
for the conjugate
y^{-1}xy
of
x
by
y
, for any elements
x,y\in G
. In
[
11
]
, Brown and Loday show that the third homotopy group of the suspension of an Eilenberg-MacLane space
K(G,1)
satisfies
\pi_{3}(SK(G,1))\cong\mu(G),
where
\mu(G)
denotes the kernel of the derived map
\rho^{\prime}:G\otimes G\to G^{\prime}
, given by
g\otimes h\mapsto[g,h]
.

In
[
27
]
, Rocco considered the following construction. Let
G
be a group and let
\varphi:G\rightarrow G^{\varphi}
be an isomorphism (
G^{\varphi}
is a copy of
G
, where
g\mapsto g^{\varphi}
, for all
g\in G
). Define the group
\nu(G)
to be

\nu(G):=\langle G\cup G^{\varphi}\ |\ [g_{1},{g_{2}}^{\varphi}]^{g_{3}}=[{g_{1}}^{g_{3}},({g_{2}}^{g_{3}})^{\varphi}]=[g_{1},{g_{2}}^{\varphi}]^{{g_{3}}^{\varphi}},\;\ g_{i}\in G\rangle.

The motivation for studying
\nu(G)
is the commutator connection: indeed, the map
\Phi:G\otimes G\rightarrow[G,G^{\varphi}]
, defined by
g\otimes h\mapsto[g,h^{\varphi}]
is an isomorphism
[
27
, Proposition 2.6]
. By
[
28
, Remark 4, pags. 1984–1985]
,

\frac{\chi(G)}{R}\cong\frac{\nu(G)}{\Delta(G)},

where
\Delta(G)=\langle[g,g^{\varphi}]\,|\,g\in G\rangle\leq\nu(G)
. In the group
\nu(G)
consider the following three subgroups:

•

\Upsilon_{1}(G)=\langle[g,h^{\varphi}]\ \mid\ g,h\in G\rangle=[G,G^{\varphi}]
.

•

\Upsilon_{2}(G)=\langle[h,g][g,h^{\varphi}]\ \mid\ g,h\in G\rangle
.

•

\Upsilon_{3}(G)=\langle[h^{\varphi},g^{\varphi}][g,h^{\varphi}]\ \mid\ g,h\in G\rangle
.

In
[
8
]
, the authors obtain the following description to the derived subgroup
\nu(G)^{\prime}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

[
8
, Theorem A]

Let
G
be a group.

(i)

Then
\nu(G)^{\prime}
is a central product of
\Upsilon_{1}(G)
,
\Upsilon_{1}(G)
and
\Upsilon_{3}(G)
.

(ii)

\Upsilon_{i}(G)/\Delta(G)
is isomorphic to the non-abelian exterior product
G\wedge G
.

Now, we can rephrase the previous result in the context of
\chi(G)/R
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group.

(i)

The group
\chi(G)^{\prime}/R
is a central product of
D/R
,
L_{1}/R
and
L_{2}/R
.

(ii)

The subgroups
D/R
,
L_{1}/R
and
L_{2}/R
are isomorphic to the non-abelian exterior product
G\wedge G
.

Proof.

(1) and (2). According to
[
28
, Theorem 2.1 (ii)]
, we deduce that:

\dfrac{\nu(G)}{\Delta(G)}\cong\dfrac{\chi(G)}{R}.

By
[
28
, Section 2]
,
D/R
is isomorphic to the exterior square
G\wedge G
. By
[
8
, Theorem A (a)]
, the subgroups
L_{1}/R
and
L_{2}
are isomorphic to
G\wedge G
.

Moreover, according to Lemma
3.1
(i), we deduce that the derived group
\nu(G)^{\prime}/\Delta(G)
is a central product of
D/R
,
L_{1}/R
and
L_{2}/R
.
∎

4.

\exp(R(G))

4.1.
Proof of Theorem A

First we present some information on the subgroup
L_{1,2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group.

(a)

L_{1,2}\leq R
.

(b)

Suppose that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are
d
-generated, then
L_{1,2}
has
d
-bounded rank.

Proof.

(a)

By Lemma
3.2
item (i), we have that
L_{1}/R
commutes with
L_{2}/R
. Therefore,
L_{1,2}=[L_{1},L_{2}]\leq R
.

(b)

Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
are finitely generated, it follows that the non-abelian exterior square is finitely generated (cf.
[
13
, Proposition 5.1]
). Set
D/R=\langle\,[g_{1},h_{1}^{\varphi}]R,\ \ldots\ ,[g_{n},h_{n}^{\varphi}]R\,\rangle
. Our aim is to prove that

L_{1,2}=\left\langle\big[[g_{i},\varphi,h_{i}],[g_{j},\varphi,h_{j}^{\varphi}]\big]\ \big|\ 1\leq i,j\leq n\right\rangle.

According to Lemma
3.2
item (ii), we deduce that

\frac{D}{R}\cong\frac{L_{1}}{R}\cong\frac{L_{2}}{R}.

Therefore we can write
L_{1}={\langle[g_{i},h_{i}][h_{i},g_{i}^{\varphi}]\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\rangle R}
and
L_{2}={\langle[g_{i}^{\varphi},h_{i}^{\varphi}][h_{i}^{\varphi},g_{i}]\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\rangle R}
. Moreover, Lemma
2.3
gives us

\displaystyle L_{1,2}=

\displaystyle\left[\left\langle[g_{i},h_{i}][h_{i},g_{i}^{\varphi}]\mid 1\leq i\leq n\right\rangle R,\left\langle[g_{j}^{\varphi},h_{j}^{\varphi}][h_{j}^{\varphi},g_{j}]\mid 1\leq j\leq n\right\rangle R\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left\langle[g_{i},h_{i}][h_{i},g_{i}^{\varphi}]\mid 1\leq i\leq n\right\rangle,\left\langle[g_{j}^{\varphi},h_{j}^{\varphi}][h_{j}^{\varphi},g_{j}]\mid 1\leq j\leq n\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\big[[g_{i},\varphi,h_{i}],[g_{j},\varphi,h_{j}^{\varphi}]\big]\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n\right\rangle,

as required. ∎

The proof of Theorem A will be divided into the following two results.

Theorem 4.2

Let
G

(a)

If

(b)

By Lemma

Then, it is sufficient to consider the elements:

[g,\varphi,a,b^{\varphi}]^{n}L_{1,2}=[[g,\varphi]^{n},a,b^{\varphi}]L_{1,2}=[g^{n},\varphi,a,b^{\varphi}]L_{1,2},

(a) and (b). Now, assume that

m
). Arguing as in the previous paragraph, we deduce that

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G
be a group.

(a)

Suppose that for every
x,y\in G

m=m(x,y)

(b)

\exp(L_{1,2})

Proof.

Choose arbitrarily
a,b,g,h\in G
. Set

.
First, we prove that
x^{n}=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]^{n}\big]
. We use induction on

, we have

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,\varphi,h^{\varphi}]\big]

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}][h,g^{\varphi}]\big]\ \ \ \ (Lemma\ \ref{l1}\ item\ (iv))

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[h,g^{\varphi}]\big]\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]\big]^{[h,g^{\varphi}]}

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]\big]^{[h,g^{\varphi}]}\ \ \ \ \big(\big[[a,\varphi,b],[h,g^{\varphi}]\big]\in[D,L]=1\big)

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]\big]\ \ \ \big(\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]\big]\in R\big).

Now, let us suppose that the identity holds for
n
.
Then,

\displaystyle x^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,{\varphi},h]\big]^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,{\varphi},h]\big]^{n}\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,{\varphi},h]\big]

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,{\varphi},h]^{n}\big]\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g,{\varphi},h]\big]

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]^{n}[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]\big]\ \ \ (Lemma\ \ref{lem:L12prod})

\displaystyle=\big[[a,\varphi,b],[g^{\varphi},h^{\varphi}]^{n+1}\big]

Consequently, the order of an element
x
divides
m
, where
m
is the order of the commutator
[g,h]
.

(a) Suppose that for every
x,y\in G
there exists a positive integer
m=m(x,y)
such that
[x,y]^{m}=1
. Since
L_{1,2}
is abelian and

L_{1,2}

(b) Suppose that

for every

. Arguing as in the previous paragraph the exponent

divides

.
∎

4.2.
Finiteness properties of commutators

In this subsection we derived some information on the subgroup
L_{1,2}
, when
G
is a group subject to restrictions on its set of commutators. More precisely, Theorem
4.4
reveals a connection between the set of commutators (of
G
) and the subgroup
L_{1,2}\leq\chi(G)
. The groups that appear in this context are related to the famous Burnside problem (and MacDonald’s question
[
22
, Question 13.34]
).

Let
m,n
be positive integers. Let
F_{m}
be the free group of rank
m
. The Burnside group

is given by the factor group
F_{m}/F^{n}_{m}
. In
[
2
]
, Adjan and Novikov prove that

is infinite for

odd,
n\geq 4381
(see also, Ol’shanskii
[
25
]
). We obtain the following related result.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
G=B(m,n)
the Burnside group. Then
L_{1,2}
is finite.

We can refine the previous result as follows.

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated periodic group. Then
L_{1,2}
is finite.

Proof.

As
G
is periodic, we have
G/G^{\prime}
is finite. From this we deduce that the derived subgroup
G^{\prime}
is also finitely generated. Consequently, by Theorem
4.1
(b),
L_{1,2}
is finitely generated. Theorem
4.4
now shows that
L_{1,2}
has finite exponent. Since
L_{1,2}
is abelian, we conclude that
L_{1,2}
is finite, as required.
∎

It is possible the exponent
\exp(L_{1,2})
may be finite while the derived subgroup
G^{\prime}
is non-periodic.

Remark 4.9
.

In
[
12
]
, Deryabina and Kozhevnikov shows that there exist a positive integer

and a group

in which

for every

and the derived subgroup is non-periodic (see also, Adjan

5.
Some proprieties of the derived and lower central series of
\chi(G)

5.1.
Derived series of
\chi(G)

According to
[
29
, Proposition 4.1.4]
, we deduce that the derived subgroup of
\chi(G)
satisfies
\chi(G)^{\prime}\leqslant D(G)L(G)
. In this subsection we describe the derived series of
\chi(G)
as the central product of the corresponding terms of the derived series of the subgroups
D
,
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
(and
L_{1,2}
). For reader’s convenience we restate Theorem C.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a group.

(a)

The derived subgroup
\chi(G)^{\prime}
is the central product of
D
and
L_{1}L_{2}
.

(b)

The second derived subgroup
\chi(G)^{(2)}
is given by the product of subgroups
D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime}L_{1,2}
.

(c)

If
k\geq 2
, then
\chi(G)^{(k+1)}
is the central product of
D^{(k)}
,
L_{1}^{(k)}
and
L_{2}^{(k)}
.

Proof.

(a)

By
[
29
, Proposition 4.1.4]
, we can argue that
\chi(G)^{\prime}=DL_{1}L_{2}
and
D\cap L_{1}L_{2}\leq D\cap L=W\leq C_{\chi(G)}(\chi(G)^{\prime})
. Therefore,
\chi(G)^{\prime}
is the central product of
D
and
L_{1}L_{2}
.

(b)

Using commutator calculus and the previous item, we obtain that

\chi(G)^{(2)}=[DL_{1}L_{2},DL_{1}L_{2}]=D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime}L_{1,2}.

(c)

We argue by induction on
k\geq 3
. Using commutator calculus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\chi(G)^{(3)}

\displaystyle=[D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime}L_{1,2},D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime}L_{1,2}]=[D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime},D^{\prime}L_{1}^{\prime}L_{2}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=D^{(2)}L_{1}^{(2)}L_{2}^{(2)}[L^{\prime}_{1},L^{\prime}_{2}].

By Lemma
2.3
we have that
[L^{\prime}_{1},L^{\prime}_{2}]=1
. Moreover,

D^{(2)}\cap L_{1}^{(2)},D^{(2)}\cap L_{2}^{(2)},L_{1}^{(2)}\cap L_{2}^{(2)}\leq W\leq C_{\chi(G)}(\chi(G)^{(3)}).

Suppose that for
k\geq 3
,

\chi(G)^{(k)}=D^{(k-1)}L_{1}^{(k-1)}L_{2}^{(k-1)}.

Consequently,

\chi(G)^{(k+1)}=D^{(k)}L_{1}^{(k)}L_{2}^{(k)}[L_{1}^{(k-1)},L_{2}^{(k-1)}].

Observe that
[L_{1}^{(k-1)},L_{2}^{(k-1)}]\leq[L^{\prime}_{1},L^{\prime}_{2}]=1
and

D^{(k)}\cap L_{1}^{(k)},D^{(k)}\cap L_{2}^{(k)},L_{2}^{(k)}\cap L_{1}^{(k)}\leq W\leq C_{\chi(G)}(\chi(G)^{(k+1)}).

Therefore, if
k\geq 3
,

\chi(G)^{(k)}=D^{(k-1)}L_{1}^{(k-1)}L_{2}^{(k-1)}.

And it is a central product as required. ∎

5.2.
Lower central series of
\chi(G)

We start with the following lemma related to the lower central series
\gamma_{n}(\chi(G))
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
g,a,b\in G
and
\alpha\in L
. Then

(i)

[\alpha,a,g,b^{\varphi}]=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi},g]
;

(ii)

[\alpha,a^{\varphi},g^{\varphi},b]=[\alpha,a^{\varphi},b,g^{\varphi}].

Proof.

We will prove only the first item, as the second follows similarly.

\displaystyle[\alpha,a,g,b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[\alpha,a]^{-1}[\alpha,a]^{g},b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[\alpha,a]^{-1},b^{\varphi}]^{[\alpha,a]^{g}}[[\alpha,a]^{g},b^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[\alpha,a]^{-1},b^{\varphi}][[\alpha,a]^{g},b^{\varphi}]\ \ \ \ ([[\alpha,a]^{-1},b^{\varphi}]\in R)

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-[\alpha,a]^{-1}}[\alpha,a,(b^{\varphi})^{g^{-1}}]^{g}

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}[\alpha,a,(b^{\varphi})^{g^{-1}}]^{g}\ \ \ \ \ ([\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}\in R)

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}[b^{\varphi},{g^{-1}}]]^{g}

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}\big([\alpha,a,[b^{\varphi},{g^{-1}}]][\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{[b^{\varphi},{g^{-1}}]}\big)^{g}

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{g}\ \ \ \ ([\alpha,a,[b^{\varphi},{g^{-1}}]]\in[L,D];[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]\in R)

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}]^{-1}[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi}][\alpha,a,b^{\varphi},g]

\displaystyle=[\alpha,a,b^{\varphi},g].\qed

The statement below includes Theorem D (a) from the Introduction.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a group and let
n
be a positive integer. Then the subgroups
[D,_{n}G]
,
[L_{1},_{n}G]

[L_{2},_{n}G^{\varphi}]
are normal subgroups
\chi(G)
.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, we have that

\displaystyle[L_{1},G]^{\chi(G)}

\displaystyle=[L_{1},G]^{DGG^{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=[L_{1},G]^{G^{\varphi}}

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},G][L_{1},G,G^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},G][L_{1},G^{\varphi},G]\ \ \ \ (Lemma\ \ref{Lm:Rcontas})

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},G][R,G]

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},G].

Therefore,
[L_{1},G]\lhd\chi(G)
. Analogously,
[L_{2},G^{\varphi}]\lhd\chi(G)
. Now, suppose that
[L_{1},_{n}G],[L_{2},_{n}G^{\varphi}]\lhd\chi(G)
. Then, is enough to prove that
[L_{1},_{n+1}G]^{G^{\varphi}}\leq[L_{1},_{n+1}G]
and
[L_{2},_{n+1}G^{\varphi}]^{G}\leq[L_{2},_{n+1}G^{\varphi}]
. By Lemma
5.1
,

\displaystyle[L_{1},_{n+1}G]^{G^{\varphi}}

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},_{n+1}G][[L_{1},_{n}G],G,{G^{\varphi}}]

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},_{n+1}G][[L_{1},_{n}G],{G^{\varphi}},G]

\displaystyle\leq[L_{1},_{n+1}G].

We can also prove the case of
[L_{2},_{n+1}G^{\varphi}]
analogously.

Now, we prove that
[D,_{n}G]
is a normal subgroup of
\chi(G)
. We proceed by induction. First, by
[
29
, Lemma 4.1.3]
, the subgroup
D
is normal in
\chi(G)
, then

[D,G]^{\chi(G)}=[D,G]^{LG}=[D,G]^{G}=[D^{G},G]\leq[D,G].

Suppose that it goes for
n
, then

\displaystyle[D,_{n+1}G]^{G^{\varphi}}=[D,_{n+1}G]^{G}=\left[[D,_{n}G]^{G},G^{G}\right]=[D,_{n+1}G],

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a group and and let
n
be a positive integer. Then

[R,_{n}G]\leq[D,_{n+1}G]\cap[L_{1},_{n+1}G]\cap[L_{2},_{n+1}G^{\varphi}].

Proof.

Observe that is enough to prove that

R\leq[D,G]\cap[L_{1},G]\cap[L_{2},G^{\varphi}].

Let
g,h,a\in G
. We want to prove that
[\varphi,g,h,a^{\varphi}]
belongs to each subgroup of the intersection. In fact

\displaystyle[\varphi,g,h,a^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[g,h^{\varphi}][h,g],a^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[g,h^{\varphi},a^{\varphi}]^{[h,g]}[h,g,a^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[g,h^{\varphi},a]^{[h,g]}[h,g,a^{\varphi}].

Once
[D,G]
is a normal subgroup of
\chi(G)
and by Hall-Witt Identity, we have that
[h,g,a^{\varphi}]\in[D,G]
. Therefore,
R\leq[D,G]
.

Now, without loss of generality, we will prove the result for
[L_{1},G]
as the proof for
[L_{2},G^{\varphi}]
follows analogously.

\displaystyle[\varphi,g,h,a^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=[[g,h^{\varphi}][h,g],a^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{a^{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}[g,h^{\varphi}]^{a^{\varphi}}[h,g]^{a^{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}[g,h^{\varphi}]^{a}[h,g]^{a(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})}

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}[g,h^{\varphi}]^{a}[h,g]^{a}\big[[h,g]^{a},(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})\big]

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{a}\big[[h,g]^{a},(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})\big]

\displaystyle=([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{-1}([g,h^{\varphi}][h,g])^{a}\big[[h,g]^{a},(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})\big]

\displaystyle=[\varphi,g,h,a]\big[h^{a},g^{a},(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})\big].

Again, by Hall-Witt Identity and by Lemma
5.2
,
\big[h^{a},g^{a},(a^{-1}a^{\varphi})\big]\in[L_{1},G]
. Consequently,
R\leq[D,G]{\cap[L_{1},G]\cap[L_{2},G^{\varphi}]}.

∎

The following statement includes Theorem D (b) from the Introduction.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a group. Then

\gamma_{n}(\chi(G))=[D,_{n-2}G][L_{1},_{n-2}G][L_{2},_{n-2}G^{\varphi}]

if
n\geq 3
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n\geq 3
. For
n=3
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(\chi(G))

\displaystyle=[DL_{1}L_{2},GL]

\displaystyle=[D,G][L_{1},G][L_{2},G][L_{1},L][L_{2},L]

\displaystyle=[D,G][L_{1},G]R[L_{1},L][L_{2},L]

Observe that
[L_{1},L]\leq[L_{1},G][L_{1},G^{\varphi}]
and
[L_{2},L]\leq[L_{2},G][L_{2},G^{\varphi}]
. Then,

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}(\chi(G))

\displaystyle=[D,G][L_{1},G]R[L_{1},L][L_{2},L]

\displaystyle\leq[D,G][L_{1},G]R[L_{1},G][L_{1},G^{\varphi}][L_{2},G][L_{2},G^{\varphi}]

\displaystyle\leq[D,G][L_{1},G][L_{2},G^{\varphi}]R

\displaystyle\leq[D,G][L_{1},G][L_{2},G^{\varphi}].\ \ \ (Lemma\ \ref{RnaIntersessao})

This is enough to conclude that
\gamma_{3}(\chi(G))=[D,G][L_{1},G][L_{2},G^{\varphi}].

Now, suppose that holds for
n\geq 3
, then

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+1}(\chi(G))=

\displaystyle\big[[D,_{n-1}G]\,[L_{1},_{n-1}G]\,[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi}],GL\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D,_{n}G]\,[L_{1},_{n}G][[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi}],G]

\displaystyle[[L_{1},_{n-1}G],L]\,[[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi}],L].

But,

[L_{1},_{n-1}G,L]\leq[L_{1},_{n}G]\,[[L_{1},_{n-1}G],G^{\varphi}],

and

[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi},L]\leq[L_{2},_{n}G^{\varphi}]\,[[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi}],G].

Additionally, by Lemma
5.1
,
[L_{1},_{n-1}G,G^{\varphi}]
and
[L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi},G]
are subgroups of
[R,_{n-1}G]
. Finally,
by Lemma
5.3
,
[R,_{n-1}G]\leq[D,_{n}G]
, and we can conclude that

\gamma_{n+1}(\chi(G))=[D,_{n-1}G][L_{1},_{n-1}G][L_{2},_{n-1}G^{\varphi}].\qed

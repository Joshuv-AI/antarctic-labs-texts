Necessary and Sufficient Characterization of Absolutely Continuous functions defined over unbounded intervals.
Gourav Banerjee

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate and find a necessary and sufficient condition for a function to be absolutely continuous over ℝ \mathbb{R} (denoted by A ​ C ​ ( ℝ ) AC(\mathbb{R}) ) or any unbounded interval in ℝ \mathbb{R} . Note that the Lebesgue’s Fundamental theorem of Calculus gives us a necessary and sufficient condition [ 2 ] for a function defined over a closed interval [a,b] to be absolutely continuous ,and the condition is that the derivative of the function should be in L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( [ a , b ] ) L^{1}_{loc}([a,b]) . However, we don’t have any such sufficient condition on the derivative of a function that is absolutely continuous over unbounded intervals. One necessary condition is that the function must be locally absolutely continuous (denoted by A ​ C l ​ o ​ c ​ ( ℝ ) AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R}) ), but it may not be globally absolutely continuous despite being locally absolutely continuous(we give an explicit example of this). The theorem 1 in this paper gives us a necessary and sufficient condition for a function belonging in A ​ C l ​ o ​ c ​ ( ℝ ) AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R}) to belong in A ​ C ​ ( ℝ ) AC(\mathbb{R}) in terms of its derivative and identifies the space to which the derivative of an A ​ C ​ ( ℝ ) AC(\mathbb{R}) function must belong to as L G 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R}) (a strict subspace of L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) ). Moreover, we define a new space of functions called L H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{R}) , and in theorem 2 we show that L G 1 ⊂ L H 1 L^{1}_{G}\subset L^{1}_{H} , which helps us to find an easier criteria to check whether a function belonging to A ​ C l ​ o ​ c ​ ( ℝ ) AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R}) , belongs to A ​ C ​ ( ℝ ) AC(\mathbb{R}) or not. Finally, we provide a Venn diagram to explicitly show the relation of the newly defined spaces L G 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R}) and L H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{R}) with respect to the spaces L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}) , L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) and L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) .



Definitions:

L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{I})=\{\text{space of measurable functions }f:\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }\int_{K}|f|<\infty,\quad\quad

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ where K is a lebesgue measurable subset of I and with finite measure.}\}

AC(\mathbb{I})=\{\text{space of measurable functions }\text{ that are absolutely continuous over }\mathbb{I}\}.

AC_{loc}(\mathbb{I})=\{\text{space of measurable functions }\text{ that are absolutely continuous over any compact subset of }\mathbb{I}\}.

L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{I})=\left\{\text{set of all measurable functions }f:\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{R}|\text{ for a given }f\text{ there exists }M>0:\mu\{x\in\mathbb{I}\mid|f(x)|\geq M\}<\infty\right\}.

\mathbb{I}
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}

Introduction:

A function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
absolutely continuous
on
[a,b]
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for any finite collection of pairwise disjoint subintervals
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}_{k=1}^{n}
of
[a,b]
satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(y_{k}-x_{k})<\delta,

it follows that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}|f(y_{k})-f(x_{k})|<\varepsilon.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be an absolutely continuous function. Then:

1.

f
is differentiable almost everywhere on
[a,b]
,

2.

its derivative
f^{\prime}
is Lebesgue integrable on
[a,b]
, and

3.

for all
x\in[a,b]
,

f(x)=f(a)+\int_{a}^{x}f^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Conversely, if
g\in L^{1}([a,b])
and we define

F(x)=\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,dt,

then
F
is absolutely continuous on
[a,b]
,
F^{\prime}=g
almost everywhere, and

F(x)=F(a)+\int_{a}^{x}F^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

The theorem shows that
a function is absolutely continuous on
[a,b]
(bounded interval) if and only if it is the indefinite integral of some Lebesgue integrable function.
Thus, absolute continuity provides the exact class of functions for which the classical Fundamental Theorem of Calculus extends to the Lebesgue integral setting.

However, we don’t have any such sufficient condition on the derivative of a function which is in
AC(\mathbb{R})
. For example,

f(x)=x\in AC(\mathbb{R})
but
f^{\prime}(x)\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. One necessary condition on the derivative of a function
f
belonging to
AC(\mathbb{R})
can be easily noted by noting that such a function must belong to
AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
, and hence
f^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
but the converse is not true. We give an example of such a function in the next section to stress this point.
Hence, the derivative of a function
f\in AC_{(}\mathbb{R})
must belong to a smaller subset of
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
. It turns out that this subset is rather a strict subspace of
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
and we call it
L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus, theorem 1 gives us a necessary and sufficient condition for a function belonging in
AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
to belong in
AC(\mathbb{R})
in terms of its derivative.
Moreover, we define a new space of functions called
L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{R})
, and in theorem 2 we show that
L^{1}_{G}\subset L^{1}_{H}
, which helps us to find an easier criteria to check whether a function belonging to
AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
, belongs to
AC(\mathbb{R})
or not.

Finally, we provide a Venn diagram to explicitly show the relation of the newly defined spaces
L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the spaces
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
,
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 1: If
f\in AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
and
f^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R})\iff f\in AC(\mathbb{R})

Proof

Let us assume that
f\notin AC(\mathbb{R})
,but
f\in AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
and
f^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{R})
,

Then,
\exists\,\epsilon>0
, such that for any
\delta>0
, there exists a sequence of disjoint intervals
(a_{i},b_{i}),\,i\in I
such that:

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-a_{i})<\delta\quad\text{but}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|f(b_{i})-f(a_{i})\right|\geq\epsilon.

(1)

Consider a positive sequence
(r_{n})

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}<\infty.\quad\text{(In particular, let }r_{n}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\text{.)}

Now, there exists a sequence of intervals
(a_{i},b_{i})
such that:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|f(b_{i})-f(a_{i})\right|>\epsilon\quad\text{but}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-a_{i})<1.

take
S_{1}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(a_{i},b_{i})
,

Now there exist a closed bounded interval
L_{1}

such that
S_{1}\subset L_{1}

Note that
\int_{L_{1}}|f^{\prime}|<\infty
since
f^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{G}

hence by continuity of measure , given

\epsilon_{1}=\frac{\epsilon}{2^{2}}\,,\,\exists\,\delta_{1}\text{ such that }\int_{E\cap L_{1}}|f^{\prime}|<\epsilon_{1}\text{ whenever }\mu(E\cap L_{1})<\delta_{1}

(2)

let
m_{1}=\min(\delta_{1},r_{1})
.

Then from (
1
), there exists
S_{2}
(a finite collection of disjoint intervals)

such that

m(S_{2})<m_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\left|f(b_{i_{2}})-f(a_{i_{2}})\right|>\epsilon.

since
f\in AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})\implies\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}f^{\prime}d\mu=f(b_{i_{2}})-f(a_{i_{2}})

due to Lebesgue’s fundamental theorem of calculus

\implies\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\left|\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}f^{\prime}\,d\mu\right|>\epsilon.\implies\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu>\epsilon.\implies\int_{S_{2}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|>\epsilon

(3)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}\setminus L_{1}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu+\int_{S_{2}\cap L_{1}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle>\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}\setminus L_{1}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle>\epsilon-\int_{S_{2}\cap L_{1}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle>\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{2^{2}}\quad(\because\mu(S_{2}\cap L_{1}<\delta_{1})\text{ from }\text{(\ref{r2}))}

then consider
S_{2}\setminus L_{1}=A_{2}
and
S_{1}=A_{1}

In particular, after having chosen
A_{n}
, consider a closed bounded interval
L_{n}
such that
\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}\subset L_{n}

\mu(L_{n})<\infty

\int_{L_{n}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu<\infty
, hence by continuity of measure , given
\epsilon_{n}=\frac{\epsilon}{(n+1)^{2}}
,there exists a
\delta_{n}
such that

\int_{E\cap L_{n}}\left|f^{\prime}\right|\,d\mu<\epsilon_{n}=\frac{\epsilon}{(n+1)^{2}}\quad\forall\mu(E\cap L_{n})<\delta_{n}

(4)

set
m_{n}=\min(\delta_{n},r_{n})

now from

,there exists a set

Theorem 2 :
L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{I})\subset L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{I})

Proof: We show that
\left(L^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{I})\right)^{c}\subset\left(L^{1}_{G}(\mathbb{I})\right)^{c}

Given

Now

Define:

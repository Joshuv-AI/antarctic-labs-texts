Wall crossing in Iwasawa theory
Shilin Lai

Abstract
. This paper sets up a framework to organize anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory in the context of the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for unitary groups. We propose multiple main conjectures depending on archimedean weight interlacing conditions, generalizing phenomena in the anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves. We also prove an abstract theorem in Galois cohomology which relates the conjectures.



1.
Introduction

In studying the arithmetic of elliptic curves, Heegner points play many important role. In the ordinary case, the Heegner points fit into a
p
-adic family over the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathcal{K}
. This family has been used to deduce two kinds of results:

–

rank 1: If the Heegner point is non-torsion, then the seminal work of Kolyvagin
[
Kol88
]
proves that the Selmer group has rank 1 using an Euler system argument. This is extended to prove one divisibility of Perrin-Riou’s Heegner point main conjecture
[
PR87
]
by Howard
[
How04a
]
, leading to results on the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula in rank 1.

–

rank 0: By
p
-adically deforming the family and using a special value formula proven by Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna
[
BDP13
]
, Castella–Hsieh proved the vanishing of a Selmer group in the region where the specialization is non-geometric
[
CH18
]
. They also proved one divisibility of the corresponding Greenberg–Iwasawa main conjecture.

In other words, a special point controls two different flavours of Iwasawa theory. From an automorphic point of view, the setting is conjugate self-dual, and the two different regions arise because of a change in the global root number from
-1
to
+1
. Their interplay continues to play an important role in the arithmetic of elliptic curves, for example in the works of Skinner and Jetchev–Skinner–Wan towards rank 1 cases of the BSD conjecture
[
Ski20
,
JSW17
]
.

The goal of this article is to propose a framework which generalizes this phenomenon to a higher dimensional setting, motivated by automorphic considerations arising from the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture and its arithmetic counterpart
[
GGP12
]
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{F}
be a CM extension, and let
\Pi
be an regular algebraic, conjugate self-dual, cuspidal (RACSDC) automorphic representation on
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})
. Let
p
be a prime which splits completely in
\mathcal{K}
. Suppose
\Pi
is ordinary at
p
. We are interested in the Iwasawa theory as
\Pi
varies in the full Hida family of
2n-1
variables. Let
\boldsymbol{T}
be the associated
p
-adic family of Galois representations. It is a projective module over the ordinary Hecke algebra
\mathbb{I}
attached to the Hida family. Its properties are axiomatized in §
5.2
.

At each archimedean place of
\mathcal{F}
, the weight of
\Pi
there can be labelled by two tuples of half-integers

(a_{1}>\cdots>a_{n})\in\Big(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)^{n},\quad(b_{1}>\cdots>b_{n-1})\in\Big(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{n}{2}\Big)^{n-1}

The relative position of these weights plays an important role. We describe them using a weight interlacing string
\square
(Definition
5.1
). Each
\square
gives rise to a Selmer condition at primes above
p
. When
n=2
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{Q}
, the three possible interlacing conditions are

AAB:a_{1}>a_{2}>b_{1},\quad ABA:a_{1}>b_{1}>a_{2},\quad BAA:b_{1}>a_{1}>a_{2}

In the middle case
ABA
, one example for
\Pi
is just an elliptic curve which is ordinary at
p
, and the Selmer condition is the ordinary one
[
Ski18
, §3.2.1]
. If we twist the elliptic curve by an infinite order anticyclotomic Hecke character, then we are in one of the other two cases. The corresponding Selmer condition is strict at one place above
p
, and relaxed at the other place, leading to the BDP-Selmer group defined in
[
Ski18
, §3.2.3]
.

Now consider the
p
-adic family
\boldsymbol{T}
. For each string
\square
, its Selmer condition interpolates to give one for
\boldsymbol{T}
, and we can define a Greenberg-style Selmer group (more precisely the cohomology group of a Selmer complex, cf. §
5.3
)

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T}).

When specialized to a classical point
x
whose weight interlacing string is actually
\square
, this recovers the Bloch–Kato Selmer group. However, in a Hida family, the weight interlacing relation is not fixed, so we should consider all such Selmer groups, one for each choice of
\square
. As
\square
varies, the only change in the Selmer condition is at the primes above
p
.

Each
\square
should lead to its own Iwasawa main conjecture, but the nature of the conjecture differs depending on
\square
. In fact, we expect that the rank of
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})
could be either 0 or 1 depending on
\square
. To explain this, note that the global root number of a specialization depends only on its weight interlacing string (Lemma
5.4
). As a result, for “half” of the strings
\square
, the global root number is
-1
, and the Bloch–Kato conjecture predicts that the Selmer groups for those specializations have rank at least 1. In this case,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})
is expected to have rank 1 over
\mathbb{I}
. This is in contrast to non-self-dual settings such as cyclotomic deformation
[
Gre94
]
, where the rank is expected to be 0.

To formalize the above discussions, we propose the following framework.

Conjecture 1.1

(Conjecture
5.5
)
.

Suppose
p
splits completely in
\mathcal{K}
and
\Pi
is ordinary at
p
. Given a
p
-adic family of Galois representations
\boldsymbol{T}
as above, there are
\binom{2n-1}{n}^{[\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{Q}]}
main conjectures indexed by weight interlacing strings
\square
. They are divided into two types depending only on a sign attached to
\square
:

–

Coherent case
The Selmer group
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})
is torsion over
\mathbb{I}
, and

\Char_{\mathbb{I}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})=(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\square})^{2},

where
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\square}
is a
p
-adic
L
-function whose square interpolates central critical values of classical points when their archimedean weights lie in the region defined by
\square
.

–

Incoherent case
The Selmer group
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})
has rank 1 over
\mathbb{I}
, and there is a special class

\boldsymbol{z}_{\square}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})

such that

\Char_{\mathbb{I}}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})_{\mathrm{tors}}=\Char_{\mathbb{I}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\square}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})}{\mathbb{I}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}_{\square}}\right)^{2}

Remark 1.2
.

In the related orthogonal GGP setting
\mathrm{SO}_{4}\times\mathrm{SO}_{5}
, analogous conjectures have been formulated by Loeffler–Zerbes
[
LZ21
]
.

The special class should play an important role for both the incoherent and coherent main conjecture. We explain this as a wall crossing phenomenon. In the
n=2
case, if we change the weight interlacing relation from
(a_{1}>a_{2}>b_{1})
to
(a_{1}>b_{1}>a_{2})
, then the local root number at the archimedean place changes, so we transition between a coherent and an incoherent region. This is an example of a pair of
nearby
interlacing relations. In general, there is a relation between the main conjectures for nearby words.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.9
)
.

Assume
\mathbb{I}
is Gorenstein. Suppose
\square
and
\triangle
are nearby (Definition
5.6
), and
\triangle
is incoherent, then there is a regulator map

\mathrm{reg}:\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\triangle}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})\to\mathbb{I}

If
\mathrm{reg}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\triangle})\neq 0
for some
\boldsymbol{z}_{\triangle}\in\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\triangle}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T})
, then the incoherent main conjecture for
\triangle
and special cycle
\boldsymbol{z}_{\triangle}
is equivalent to the coherent main conjecture for
\square
, with the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\square}
replaced by the motivic
p
-adic
L
-function defined by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mot}}^{\square}:=\mathrm{reg}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\triangle})
.

The main input to this theorem is the construction of the regulator map, which comes down to the one-dimensional Coleman map. The proof of the equivalence then involves a series of applications of global duality theorems, which we organize using Selmer complexes.

1.2.
Related and future works

We know describe some cases of the conjecture which have been studied in the literature. Along the way, we indicate a few questions for future investigations.

1.2.1.
Diagonal condition

We first consider the “diagonal” interlacing condition

\mathtt{diag}:a_{1}>b_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>b_{n-1}>a_{n}.

For a single representation
\Pi
with this interlacing condition, the paper
[
LTX+22
]
made significant progresses towards rank 0 and 1 cases of the Bloch–Kato conjecture. For the partial family of
\Pi
twisted by Hecke characters, the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathtt{diag}}
in the coherent case and the special class
\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathtt{diag}}
in the incoherent case were both constructed by Yifeng Liu
[
Liu23
]
. Under some technical hypotheses, the divisibility (LHS)
|
(RHS) of both the coherent and the incoherent main conjecture was recently established by Liu–Tian–Xiao
[
LTX24
]
.

The theory of split anticyclotomic Euler systems by Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner
[
JNS24
]
offers an alternative approach to establishing cases of the incoherent main conjecture. The necessary Euler system input was recently constructed by the author and Skinner
[
LS24
]
, giving a different proof the incoherent divisibility of Liu–Tian–Xiao. It is likely one make this approach work over the full Hida family using the method described in
[
LRZ24
]
. We plan to investigate this in a future work.

When
n=3
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{Q}
, the full 5-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathtt{diag}}
has been constructed by Hsieh–Yamana
[
HY23
]
. It would be interesting to study if the method of
[
LTX24
]
can be extended to prove one divisibility of the 5-variable coherent main conjecture.

1.2.2.
Other conditions

Starting with
\mathtt{diag}
, there are
2(n-1)
nearby words, one example being

\square:b_{1}>a_{1}>a_{2}>b_{2}>\cdots>b_{n-1}>a_{n},

which we obtained from
\mathtt{diag}
by switching the order of
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
. In the case where
\mathtt{diag}
is incoherent, all such nearby words are coherent. Once the full Hida family deformation
\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathtt{diag}}
is constructed, our theorem relates the incoherent main conjecture for
\mathtt{diag}
to coherent main conjectures for all
2(n-1)
nearby words.

To exploit this relation, we need to relate the regulator of
\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathtt{diag}}
to a
p
-adic
L
-function obtained by interpolation, or in other words, prove an “explicit reciprocity law”. In the case of
\square
given above, a 1-variable
p
-adic
L
-function has been constructed by Harris
[
Har21
]
. As explained in Example
5.11
, this is exactly the variable needed to deform the word
\square
into
\mathtt{diag}
. The explicit reciprocity law in this case should be within reach of current methods.

On the other hand, when the word
\mathtt{diag}
is coherent, its
p
-adic
L
-function should be related to special classes for nearby words such as
\square
. However, currently there is no construction of these special classes except for
n=2
, which is a recent work of Castella–Do
[
CD23
]
. One way to replicate their construction in our setting is to allow non-cuspidal
\Pi
. In the product
\Pi=\Pi_{n-1}\times\Pi_{n}
, we can take
\Pi_{n}
to be an isobaric sum of the form
\Pi_{n-2}\boxplus\chi_{1}\boxplus\chi_{2}
for two auxiliary characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
. Then the Galois representation for
\Pi
decomposes as a direct sum, where one piece is associated to the lower rank case
\Pi_{n-2}\times\Pi_{n-1}
. It is a subtle and interesting question to understand this process in family.

Finally, some computations in §
5.4
raises a possibility that there may be further connections between the regions of interpolation. This already appears in the
n=3
case, and we hope this can be clarified in the near future.

1.3.
Notations and conventions

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a totally real field of degree
g
, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a CM extension of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\mathtt{c}
be a fixed element in
\Gal_{\mathcal{F}}
which is non-trivial in
\Gal(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{F})
. Fix a CM type
\Sigma^{+}_{\infty}
for
\mathcal{K}
. We will typically use it to index the archimedean places of
\mathcal{F}
, so for each archimedean place, we have a distinguished embedding
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

Let
p>2
be a rational prime such that

(spl)

p\text{ splits completely in }\mathcal{K}

Fix an isomorphism
\iota:\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Let
\Sigma_{p}^{+}=\{\iota\circ\sigma\,|\,\sigma\in\Sigma^{+}_{\infty}\}
. This is a
p
-adic CM type, and we may identify
\Sigma_{p}^{+}
with a set of
p
-adic places of
\mathcal{K}
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{p}^{+}\cap\Sigma_{p}^{+}\mathtt{c}=\emptyset
, so for each
p
-adic place
v
of
\mathcal{F}
, the CM type distinguishes one of the two places above
v
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
denote the ring of adeles of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}}
denote the ring of adeles of
\mathcal{K}
. We will systematically use the following convention: a superscript consisting of places indicates omitting those places, and a subscript consisting of places indicates only considering those places. For example,
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K},f}
is the ring of finite adeles of
\mathcal{K}
, and
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}=\mathbb{A}^{p\infty}
is the ring of finite adeles of
\mathcal{F}
omitting the places above
p
.

Class field theory is always normalized geometrically. We will implicitly use it to pass between characters of the Galois group and algebraic Hecke characters. Hodge–Tate weights are normalized so that the cyclotomic character has weight
-1
.

2.
p
-adic family of Galois representations

In this section, we set up some notations and definitions for Iwasawa theory. In particular, we construct a Coleman map in our setting. Fix
E
, a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be its ring of integers. They will serve as coefficients for the representations we consider.

2.1.
Weight space

Let
\mathbf{T}=\Res_{\mathcal{F}/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{G}_{m}^{r}
. Let
\underline{\Lambda}=\mathcal{O}[[\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})]]
be its completed group ring. This is a semi-local regular Noetherian ring. Let
\mathbf{T}^{0}
be the pro-p part of the abelian group
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, then we have a factorization

\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\mathbf{T}^{0}\times\Delta

for some finite abelian group
\Delta
. Correspondingly, we can write
\underline{\Lambda}=\Lambda[\Delta]
, where
\Lambda
is the completed group ring of
\mathbf{T}^{0}
. Given a character
\psi:\Delta\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, we can pair with it to get a surjection
\mathtt{pr}_{\psi}:\underline{\Lambda}\to\Lambda
. This presents
\Lambda
as a
\underline{\Lambda}
-algebra, and we will write
\Lambda^{\psi}
if we want to emphasize this structure.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{W}}
be the rigid analytic space attached to
\spf\underline{\Lambda}
, then it is a disjoint union of open unit discs labelled by characters of
\Delta
. Let
\mathcal{W}_{\psi}
denote the component corresponding to
\psi
. We will view
\underline{\Lambda}
as the bounded functions in the space of rigid functions
\mathcal{O}(\underline{\mathcal{W}})
.

Given a cocharacter
\epsilon:\Res_{\mathcal{F}/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{G}_{m}\to\mathbf{T}
, we can define the tautological character

(1)

\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}:\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}\twoheadrightarrow\Gal(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{ac}}/\mathcal{K})\to\Res_{\mathcal{F}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\underline{\Lambda}^{\times},

where the final step sends
z
to the group element
[\epsilon(z)]
. We say a specialization
x:\underline{\Lambda}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is
classical
if
x\circ\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}
is Hodge–Tate for all
\epsilon
. In this case, we define the
\epsilon
-weight
w_{\epsilon}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
to be negative of the Hodge–Tate weight of
x\circ\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}
at the places of
\mathcal{K}
lying in the CM type
\Sigma_{p}^{+}
. In particular, by taking
\varepsilon
to be the
r
standard cocharacters, we get a tuple of weights

(w_{1}^{\sigma},\cdots,w_{r}^{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}^{+}}\in\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}^{+}}\mathbb{Z}^{r}

attached to
x
. The subset of classical points of
\underline{\mathcal{W}}
will be denoted by
\underline{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

2.2.
p
-adic family

Let
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
be a ring with a finite inclusion
\underline{\Lambda}\to\underline{\mathbb{I}}
. Let
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
denote the rigid space attached to
\spf\underline{\mathbb{I}}
, so we have a finite morphism
\underline{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{\mathcal{W}}
. We say a point of
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
is classical if its image in
\underline{\mathcal{W}}
is classical. In the intended applications,
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
occurs as an irreducible component of the ordinary eigenvariety.

Definition 2.1
.

A
p
-adic family of Galois representations is a finitely generated
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
-module
\boldsymbol{T}
with an action of
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}
such that

(1)

The
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}
-action is admissible in the sense of
[
Nek06
, Section 3.2]
.

(2)

As a
\underline{\Lambda}
-module,
\boldsymbol{T}
is projective.

The first condition is a version of continuity. In the cases we will consider, it holds for a finite rank
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
-module if the action is continuous with respect to the topology induced from each of the maximal ideal of
\underline{\Lambda}
. The notion is also stable under Pontryagin duality and extensions, so we will usually not need to explicitly check it in practice.

If
\boldsymbol{\chi}
is a character valued in
\underline{\mathbb{I}}^{\times}
, then we write
\underline{\mathbb{I}}(\boldsymbol{\chi})
to mean
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
with the Galois group acting through
\boldsymbol{\chi}
. This is admissible if and only if
\boldsymbol{\chi}
is continuous. In that case it defines a
p
-adic family of characters. In particular, for any
\alpha\in\underline{\mathbb{I}}^{\times}
, we can form the unramified character

\boldsymbol{\alpha}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\underline{\mathbb{I}}^{\times}

sending a geometric Frobenius to
\alpha
. Moreover, using the inclusion
\underline{\Lambda}\to\underline{\mathbb{I}}
, the universal characters
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}
defined in equation (
1
) are also characters on
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
.

2.3.
Commutative algebra

The notation
(-)^{\vee}
will always mean taking the (underived) dual with respect to a dualizing module over the implied base ring. This is usually applied with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or
\Lambda
, depending on the context. Both of them are Gorenstein, so they are their own dualizing module.

Definition 2.2
.

A
p
-adic family
\boldsymbol{T}
is
conjugate self-dual
if there exists a
\Gal_{K}
-invariant bilinear pairing
\boldsymbol{T}\otimes_{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{T}^{\mathtt{c}}\to\Lambda(1)
which induces an isomorphism
\boldsymbol{T}^{\mathtt{c}}\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{T},\Lambda)(1)
.

Now suppose
\boldsymbol{T}
is conjugate self-dual. There are two types of duality pairing we want to consider for
\boldsymbol{T}
. For the ring
R=\Lambda
, the representations
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{\mathtt{c}}
satisfy condition 5.2.3(B) of
[
Nek06
]
, so we get a good theory of duality for their Galois cohomology complexes viewed as
\Lambda
-modules. Let
(-)^{*}
denote the Pontryagin dual, then we have
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}\simeq\boldsymbol{T}^{\vee}\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda^{*}
as
\Lambda
-modules. The pair
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{\mathtt{c}}\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda^{*}
therefore satisfies condition 5.2.3(A) of
[
Nek06
]
.

Finally, we define notations surrounding the characteristic ideal. Let
P
be a height one prime ideal of a complete local ring
R
(either
\Lambda
or
\mathbb{I}
in applications). It is associated to a valuation
v_{P}
on
\Lambda
which can be computed as
v_{P}(x)=\mathrm{length}_{R_{P}}(R_{P}/(x))
. The characteristic ideal of a torsion
R
-module
X
is defined to be

\Char_{R}(X):=\{x\in\Lambda\,|\,v_{P}(x)\geq\mathrm{length}_{R_{P}}(X_{P})\text{ for all $P$ of height 1}\}

It is clear that the characteristic ideal is multiplicative in exact sequences. A torsion module is pseudo-null if its characteristic ideal is the unit ideal. They form a Serre subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules. We use the notation
\mathbf{psMod}_{R}
to denote the quotient abelian category. Though we will not use it, we remark that all torsion
\Lambda
-modules are isomorphic to a direct sum of the form
\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda/(f_{i})
in
\mathbf{psMod}_{\Lambda}
.

2.4.
Coleman map

We want to generalize the classical Coleman logarithm
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\Lambda(1))\simeq\Lambda
to our setting. Our result will follow from a slight modification of the constructions in
[
Och03
]
. We first recall some notations. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}
denote the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. There is a canonical isomorphism
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\to\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}
sending
1
to the geometric Frobenius
\sigma
.

Let
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
be the cyclotomic character. Let
\epsilon:\mathbf{G}_{m}\to\mathbf{T}
be a standard cocharacter, so we have a universal character
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\underline{\Lambda}^{\times}
defined by equation (
1
). Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\underline{\mathbb{I}}^{\times}
, which gives an unramified character
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
. We are interested in the character

\boldsymbol{\chi}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\underline{\mathbb{I}}^{\times}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that the specialization of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
at the trivial character is not equal to 1. Then for any finitely generated
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
-module
M
which is projective over
\Lambda
, there is an isomorphism of
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
-modules

\mathrm{Col}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\boldsymbol{\chi}))\to(M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}})^{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}}

Moreover, the right hand side is non-canonically isomorphic to
M
.

Proof.

We will work over each component of
\underline{\mathbb{I}}
separately, so fix a tame character
\psi
and suppress it from the notation. The group
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}
has
p
-cohomological dimension 1, so we have the following exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}},M(\boldsymbol{\chi})^{\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\boldsymbol{\chi}))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}},M(\boldsymbol{\chi}))^{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}}\to 0

By assumption,
\epsilon
is non-trivial and
M
is
p
-torsion-free, so the
\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}}
-invariant is trivial. Moreover, over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}
, we can factor out the unramified character
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
. Therefore,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\boldsymbol{\chi}))\simeq\big(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}},\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon}))\otimes_{\Lambda}M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\big)^{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ur}}}

Fix an isomorphism
\mathbf{T}\simeq\mathbf{G}_{m}^{r}
such that
\epsilon
is the first component, then as a Galois module,

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\epsilon})=\Lambda_{1}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{r-1}

where
\Lambda_{1}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\mathbb{Z}_{p}]]
, and the Galois action on the second term is trivial.

We now recall the Coleman map, in the form stated as
[
Och03
, Proposition 5.10]
. It gives a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}},\underline{\Lambda}_{1}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\xrightarrow{\,\Log\,}\underline{\Lambda}_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\mathrm{ur}}\to 0

such that for any classical point

3.

3.3.
Equivalence of main conjectures

In classical Iwasawa theory, we want to study the compact
\mathbb{I}
-modules

and

since they interpolate Bloch–Kato Selmer groups if
\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner}
and

are chosen appropriately. They are singled out in parts because their local conditions are self-dual. We might expect the central values of the associated

would be torsion, and we have an Iwasawa main conjecture equating its characteristic ideal to a

[

In the case which inspired the current axiomatization, there is a further feature, namely the

-functions are self-dual, and the sign of the function equation is constant in family. In the sign

[

In our setting,

and

are mutual annihilators, so
\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner}+\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\boldsymbol{T}^{c,\llcorner}=\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\boldsymbol{T}
. Again by ordinarity,
\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner}-\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner}=1
. Therefore,

\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}-\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}=1

It follows that
\rank_{\mathbb{I}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}=1
. By applying the argument in the previous paragraph to the exact triangle
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\llcorner,{\llcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}\to U_{w}\to[1]
, it remains to show that
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{w},\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner}/\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner})
has non-torsion image. This again follows from assumption (2) since the regulator map factors through
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}
.

We now verify the equivalence in the second part of the statement. First observe that by the rank calculation above and Proposition
3.7
, we must have
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}
is pseudo-null. Let
C=\coker(\mathrm{reg})
. It is torsion since
\boldsymbol{M}
has
\mathbb{I}
-rank 1 and
\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{reg})
contains a non-torsion element by part (2) of the hypothesis. The long exact sequence attached to the exact triangle
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\to U_{w}\to[1]
gives

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\boldsymbol{M}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,ad}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(U_{w})

The two end terms are pseudo-null. In the middle, the map
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\boldsymbol{M}
is exactly the regulator map by definition. Therefore, we can split it into the following two short exact sequences:

(pseudo-null)

\displaystyle\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\boldsymbol{M}\to C\to 0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to C\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\text{(pseudo-null)}

In the first sequence, after quotienting out by
\mathbb{I}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}
, we get the relation

\Char_{\Lambda}(C)\Char_{\Lambda}\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}}{\mathbb{I}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}}=\Char_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{reg}(\boldsymbol{z}))

In the second sequence, since
C
is torsion, we can take the torsion subgroup without affecting the pseudo-null cokernel, so

\Char_{\Lambda}\big(\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\big)_{\mathrm{tors}}=\Char_{\Lambda}C\Char_{\Lambda}\big(\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}\big)_{\mathrm{tors}}

Again by Proposition
3.7
, the module
C
is also the cokernel of localization from
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}
, so by the same argument as before, we have a third exact sequence

0\to C\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\llcorner}}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}\to\text{(pseudo-null)}

This gives another equality of characteristic ideals. Combining it with the previous two, we see that it remains to show that
\Char_{\Lambda}\big(\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\big)_{\mathrm{tors}}=\Char_{\Lambda}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}
.

This is now a consequence of global duality. We work one prime at a time. Let
P
be a height one prime in
\Lambda
, and let
R=\Lambda_{P}
. It is a discrete valuation ring, and we need to prove
(\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}})_{\mathrm{tors}}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}
have the same lengths after localizing at
P
. Since localization is exact, equation (
\vee
) still holds with
\Lambda

R
everywhere. We apply it to get the following exact triangle

\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}\to\mathbf{R}\!\Hom_{R}(\widetilde{\mathbf{R}\Gamma}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}},R)[3]\to\mathrm{Err}\to[1]

Let
A^{\bullet}
denote the cohomology of the middle term. It can be computed by the spectral sequence

\Ext^{i}_{R}(\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{j}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}},R)\Rightarrow A^{i-j+3}

Since

, and
\Ext^{1}(-,R)
computes the torsion module. Moreover, we have computed that
\rank_{R}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{j}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}=1
if

and is
0
otherwise. The assumption (no-pole) implies
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}=0
, and by global duality (

. Finally, Proposition

A^{i}=\begin{cases}R\oplus\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}&i=2\\
0&i\neq 2\end{cases}

Plugging this into the long exact sequence and splitting it gives the following two short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{Err}^{1}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\llcorner,{\urcorner}}

\displaystyle\to N\to 0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to N\to R\oplus\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\urcorner,{\llcorner}}\to\mathrm{Err}^{2}\to 0

Remark 3.10
.

(1)

We can interchange
\llcorner
with
\urcorner
throughout the entire theorem. This requires localizing at
\bar{w}
instead of

, and the proof goes through with this change.

(2)

If we know that
\mathbb{I}
is Gorenstein and
\boldsymbol{T}

\mathbb{I}
, then we can do the above computations over
\mathbb{I}

(3)

(4)

3.4.
Applications

In this subsection, we give two applications of the above formalism. The first will recover one main theorem of
[
CH18
]
without the rank 0 Kolyvagin system argument. The second allows one to deduce the rank 0 results in
[
CD23
]
using the Euler systems they constructed. This section also hints at the wall crossing phenomena we will explain in general in the following section.

3.4.1.
Set-up

We specialize to
F=\mathbb{Q}
, though many of the results stated here extend (or should extend) to more general totally real fields.

Let
N
be a square-free natural number. Let
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a modular form which is ordinary at
p
. The modular form
f
has an attached
p
-adic Galois representation
T_{f}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We assume that
T_{f}
is residually irreducible. Let
D_{p}
be a decomposition group of
\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}
at
p
, then
T_{f}
has the following
D_{p}
-stable filtration

0\to T_{f}^{-}\to T_{f}\to T_{f}^{+}\to 0

where
D_{p}
acts on
T_{f}^{-}
as
\alpha_{p}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}
and on
T_{f}^{+}
as
\beta_{p}
, for some units
\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, identified as the unramified character sending Frobenius to that value. Both
T_{f}^{-}
and
T_{f}^{+}
are 1-dimensional, and there is a perfect pairing on
T_{f}
exchanging
T_{f}^{-}
and
T_{f}^{+}
.

We are interested in the anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory of
f
. Fix an anticyclotomic character
\chi
of our imaginary quadratic field
\mathcal{K}
. For simplicity, we assume the numbers
N\ ,\mathrm{disc}(\mathcal{K}),\ \mathrm{cond}(\chi),\ p
are pairwise coprime. Let
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{ac}}
be the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathcal{K}
,
\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}=\Gal(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{ac}}/\mathcal{K})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}=\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}]]
, and
\Psi:\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}\to\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}
be the canonical character. Our
p
-adic family of Galois representation will be

\boldsymbol{T}=T_{f}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}(\chi\Psi)

with
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}
acting diagonally. This certainly satisfies (no-pole) by residual irreducibility. For the Selmer condition defined by either
\boldsymbol{T}
or
T_{f}^{-}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}(\chi\Psi)
, the condition (no-triv-zero) is easily checked. Moreover, the pairing on
T_{f}
induces a perfect pairing on
\boldsymbol{T}
.

3.4.2.
Heegner points

The theory in this setting was very clearly set up in
[
BCK21
]
, and this was the model on which we built our abstract framework. Suppose the generalized Heegner condition holds, namely the number of primes
\ell|N
which is inert in
\mathcal{K}
is even. Let
\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner}=0
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner}=T_{f}^{-}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}(\chi\Psi)
. This satisfies (ord) since
\chi
is unramified at
p
. The dual spaces are
\boldsymbol{T}^{c,{\urcorner}}=\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner}
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{c,{\llcorner}}=\boldsymbol{T}
.

The special class here is the Howard’s big Heegner point
[
How04b
]
.

\boldsymbol{z}_{c}\in\varprojlim_{n}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{K}[cp^{n}],T_{f}^{-})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{K}[c],T_{f}^{-}\otimes\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}(\Psi))

where we have used the notation
\mathcal{K}[c]
to denote the ring class field of
\mathcal{K}
with conductor
c
, and the equality is by a standard application of Shapiro’s lemma. If
c=\mathrm{cond}(\chi)
, then we can further project to the
\chi
-isotypic part to produce an element
\boldsymbol{z}^{\chi}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{K},\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner})
. Since the dual condition is also defined by the same space, we have
\boldsymbol{z}^{\chi}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}}
.

To verify the hypothesis (2) of Theorem
3.9
, we need to compute
\mathrm{reg}(\boldsymbol{z}^{\chi})
, where
\boldsymbol{z}^{\chi}\in\varprojlim_{n}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{K}[p^{n}]_{w},T_{f}^{-})
. This is the explicit reciprocity law proven in
[
BCK21
, Theorem 4.4]
. It now follows from
[
Bur17
]
that the localization at
w
is non-torsion.

Hypothesis (1) as well as one divisibility of the Perrin-Riou main conjecture are both proven in
[
How04b
]
using a bipartite Euler system argument. Using our theorem, this implies

\Char_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}}X_{\llcorner,{\llcorner}}|(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BDP}})^{2}

In classical terms, the Selmer group
X_{\llcorner,{\llcorner}}
is the Greenberg Selmer group with the strict and relaxed conditions at
w
and
\bar{w}
respectively. The other side (with the square) is the
p
-adic
L
-function first constructed by Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna
[
BDP13
]
interpolating the algebraic parts of the central values
L(\frac{1}{2},f\times\chi^{\prime})
where the weights of
\chi^{\prime}
are greater than those of
f
. It follows by a standard application of the control theorem that
L(\frac{1}{2},f\times\chi^{\prime})\neq 0
implies
\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(V_{f}\otimes\chi^{\prime})=0
under the previous assumptions on
f
.

Remark 3.11
.

By applying complex conjugation to our argument, we can prove a similar rank-0 results for the relaxed-strict Selmer group, corresponding to the case where the weight of
\chi^{\prime}
is less than 0. In this case, the result is just the conjugate of the one for positive weight. However, we will see that in general, one special cycle can control several rank 0 regions which appear to be fundamentally different.

3.4.3.
No Heegner points

We now consider the opposite of the above setting, so the number of prime
\ell|N
which is inert in
\mathcal{K}
is odd. In this case, we know that
L(\frac{1}{2},f\times\chi)\neq 0
for a generic finite order
\chi
by the main result of
[
Vat03
]
. They can be interpolated into a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BD}}
(cf.
[
CD23
, Theorem 5.1.1]
), and we have an Iwasawa main conjecture of the form

X_{\mathrm{ord}}(E/\mathcal{K})|(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BD}})

which implies a generic rank 0 result. This conjecture was established by Bertolini–Darmon
[
BD05
]
using level-raising methods under mild technical hypotheses.

In
[
CD23
]
, Castella-Tuan constructed an Euler system for the representation
V_{f}\otimes\chi^{\prime}
, where
\chi^{\prime}
has large weight. In short, they specialized the Euler system constructed by Bertolini–Seveso–Venerucci
[
BSV22
]
in the balanced triple product setting to the case where two of the forms have CM by
\mathcal{K}
. As was further shown in
[
CD23
]
, the explicit reciprocity law computes the big logarithm of their class as a
p
-adic
L
-function with certain interpolation properties coming from an easy factorization of the unbalanced triple product
p
-adic
L
-function.

In our formalism, let
\boldsymbol{T}^{\llcorner}=T_{f}^{-}\otimes\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}(\chi\Psi)
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{\urcorner}=\boldsymbol{T}
, so we start with information about the Greenberg Selmer group and want to derive a rank-0 result for the usual Selmer group. The above construction gives rise to a class
\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathrm{CT}}
satisfying the following properties

\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathrm{CT}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\urcorner,{\urcorner}},\quad\mathrm{reg}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\mathrm{CT}})^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BD}}

The interpolation property as well as the result of Vatsal cited earlier imply that
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BD}}
is non-torsion, so Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner’s Euler system argument
[
JNS24
]
implies one divisibility of the Perrin-Riou main conjecture. Therefore, by Theorem
3.9
,

\Char_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{ac}}}X_{\mathrm{ord}}|\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{BD}}

The usual control theorem argument implies a rank 0 result. This was deduced without appealing to cyclotomic constructions such as the Rankin–Eisenstein elements of
[
KLZ17
]
. While this is not known at the moment, the generalization of the results in the triple product setting to the totally real field case appears to be within reach, so this argument could lead to rank-0 results in those cases as well, where the cyclotomic constructions are not known.

4.
Automorphic background

Before presenting our proposed framework, we use this expository section to recall certain aspects of the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture. In the discrete series case, we will also explain the combinatorial recipe relating the distinguished signatures and the weight interlacing conditions.

4.1.
Discrete series

This section recalls some facts about the discrete series on real unitary groups. Throughout this section, the group
\mathrm{U}(p,q)
is the unitary group of the Hermitian space
V_{p,q}
defined using the Hermitian form
\mathbf{1}_{p,q}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{p}&\\
&-\mathbf{1}_{q}\end{smallmatrix}\big)
. We view it as a real Lie group embedded in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, where
n=p+q
.

4.1.1.
Root data

Let
T
be the diagonal torus in
\mathrm{U}(p,q)
, then
T\simeq\mathrm{U}(1)^{n}
is a compact maximal torus. Define the characters
t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}
, so that
z\in T
is equal to
\diag(t_{1}(z),\cdots,t_{n}(z))
. We will label a general character
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}t_{i}
by the tuple
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We pick
\Delta^{+}=\{t_{i}-t_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}
to be the set of positive roots, and we let

\rho=\left(\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n-3}{2},\cdots,\frac{1-n}{2}\right)

be the half sum of positive roots.

Let
K_{p,q}\subseteq\mathrm{U}(p,q)
be the maximal compact subgroup consisting of matrices of the form
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\\
&h\end{smallmatrix}\big)
, where
g\in\mathrm{U}(p)
and
h\in\mathrm{U}(q)
. The torus
T
is also a maximal torus of
K_{p,q}
.

4.1.2.
Harish-Chandra classification

The discrete series of the group
G=\mathrm{U}(p,q)
can be indexed its Harish-Chandra parameter, which we combinatorially represent by

–

a regular infinitesimal character
\underline{a}=(a_{1}>\cdots>a_{n})\in\big(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{n-1}{2}\big)^{n}
and

–

a binary string
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with exactly
p
0s.

We call the binary string its
Harish-Chandra code
. Let
i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}
be the location of the 0s in
\epsilon
and
j_{1},\cdots,j_{q}
be the location of the 1s, then
(\underline{a},\epsilon)
represents

(a_{i_{1}},\cdots,a_{i_{p}};a_{j_{1}},\cdots,a_{j_{q}})\in X^{*}(T)+\rho

in the usual notations for the Harish-Chandra parameter. In particular, the holomorphic discrete series whose minimal
K
-type is
\underline{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{p};\kappa_{p+1}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n})
is represented by the pair

(\underline{\kappa}+\rho,\underbrace{1\cdots 1}_{q}\underbrace{0\cdots 0}_{p})

Such a representation exists if and only if
\kappa_{n}\geq\kappa_{1}+n
. This will be generalized in §
4.1.4
.

4.1.3.
Generic datum

We now specify the choices needed to call a representation “generic”. This will be used in the following section to label elements of the Vogan
L
-packet.

Let
V
be a split Hermitian space over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
G=\mathrm{U}(V)
, then
G
is quasi-split. Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
with Levi decomposition
B=T\ltimes N
. Let
\lambda:N\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a generic character, then a representation
\pi
is
(N,\lambda)
-generic if

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Hom_{N}(\pi,\lambda)=1

The torus
T
acts on
\lambda
, and the above condition is invariant under such action. It is now useful to split into two cases, depending on the parity of
n=\dim V
.

n
odd:

The action of
T
on the space of generic characters is transitive, and there is a unique generic discrete series representation for each infinitesimal character
\underline{a}
. If
n=2m+1
, then it is the representation on
\mathrm{U}(m+1,m)
with Harish-Chandra code
01\cdots 010
.

n
even:

The action of
T
on the space of generic characters has two orbits, which correspond to the two
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
-orbits on additive characters
\psi:\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(cf.
[
GGP12
, Section 12]
). A set of representative characters is

\psi_{\mathbb{C}}:z\mapsto\exp(2\pi(\bar{z}-z)),\quad\overline{\psi_{\mathbb{C}}}:z\mapsto\exp(2\pi(z-\bar{z}))

Let
n=2m
, then the Harish-Chandra code for the
\overline{\psi_{\mathbb{C}}}
-generic (resp.
\psi_{\mathbb{C}}
-generic) representation is
01\cdots 01
(resp.
10\cdots 10
).

Proofs of the above claims can be found in
[
Ato20
, §A.3]
.

4.1.4.
Coherent cohomology

Let
V
be a Hermitian space defined over the extension
\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{F}
. At the archimedean places, write its signature as
\{(a_{\sigma},b_{\sigma})\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}}
. Let
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{U}(V)
be its unitary group, then we fix an isomorphism

\mathbf{G}_{\infty}\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}}\mathrm{U}(a_{\sigma},b_{\sigma}).

Let
K_{\infty}\subseteq\mathbf{G}_{\infty}
be the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathbf{G}_{\infty}
which corresponds to
\prod_{\sigma}K_{a_{\sigma},b_{\sigma}}
under this isomorphism. The Shimura datum for
\mathbf{G}
defined by

z\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z/\bar{z}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{a_{\sigma}}&\\
&\mathbf{1}_{b_{\sigma}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}}

gives rise to a Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathbf{G}}
whose reflex field is contained in
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\xi
be an algebraic representation of
K_{\infty}
, then it gives rise to an automorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{\xi}
over
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathbf{G}}
.

Let
\pi
be a cusp form on
\mathbf{G}
whose archimedean components lie in the discrete series. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
, we have a pair
(\underline{a}_{\sigma},\epsilon_{\sigma})
as described in §
4.1.2
. Let
q_{\sigma}
denotes the number of times where 0 occurs before 1 in
\epsilon_{\sigma}
, so
0\leq q_{\sigma}\leq a_{\sigma}b_{\sigma}
. Let
q_{\pi}=\sum_{\sigma}q_{\sigma}
, then
0\leq q_{\pi}\leq\dim\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathbf{G}}
.

Suppose
\underline{a}_{\sigma}
is sufficiently far from walls for all
\sigma
, then by theorems B and D of
[
Har90
]
,
\pi
contributes to the coherent cohomology of
\mathcal{V}_{\xi}
for a unique
\xi
, in which case its contribution is concentrated in the degree
q_{\pi}
. We will use this to check some dimension compatibility for
p
-adic
L
-functions.

4.2.
Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture

Let
\Pi=\Pi_{n-1}\otimes\Pi_{n}
be a RACSDC automorphic representation on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})
. We are interested in the arithmetic of
\Pi
, in particular its central
L
-value
L\big(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\big)
. This value depends on the endoscopic transfer of
\Pi
to a specific pair of unitary groups, predicted by a local restriction problem. This section will briefly recall the key points of this picture.

4.2.1.
Vogan packets

Let
v
be a place of
\mathcal{F}
which does not split, and let
w
be the place of
\mathcal{K}
above
v
. For
i=n-1,n
, the local Langlands correspondence attaches to
\Pi_{i}
an
L
-parameter

\phi_{i,w}:\mathrm{WD}_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{i}(\mathbb{C}).

It is conjugate self-dual. The algebraicity requirement forces the sign to be
(-1)^{i-1}
(cf. §
4.2.2
). It follows from
[
GGP12
, Theorem 8.1]
that
\phi_{i}
descends to an
L
-parameter

\phi_{i,v}:\mathrm{WD}_{\mathcal{F}_{v}}\to{}^{L}\mathrm{U}_{i}.

We now explain some aspects of endoscopic classification following
[
GGP12
, Section 9]
. By definition, the Vogan packet
\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}
attached to
\phi_{i,v}
is a disjoint union

\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}=\bigsqcup_{V}\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}^{V},

where
V
runs over all isomorphism classes of Hermitian spaces over
\mathcal{K}_{w}/\mathcal{F}_{v}
of dimension
i
, and each
\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}^{V}
is a collection of irreducible admissible representations of
\mathrm{U}(V)
. Given
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}
, let
V^{(\pi)}
be the Hermitian space such that
\pi
is a member of
\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}^{V^{(\pi)}}
. For any representation
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}
, its base change to
\mathcal{K}_{w}
is
\Pi_{i,w}
.

Let
A_{\phi_{i},v}
be the component group of the centralizer of
\phi_{i,v}
. By the discussion in
[
GGP12
, Section 4]
, it is an elementary abelian 2-group. There is a bijection

J_{v}:A_{\phi_{i},v}^{\vee}\simeq\Pi_{\phi_{i},v}.

More precisely, this depends on the following auxiliary data.

i
odd:

There are exactly two split Hermitian spaces of dimension

up to isomorphism. They are distinguished by their discriminants
\delta\in\mathcal{F}_{v}^{\times}/\mathbf{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{w}/\mathcal{F}_{v}}\mathcal{K}_{w}^{\times}

i
even:

The action of
\mathbf{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{w}/\mathcal{F}_{v}}\mathcal{K}_{w}^{\times}
on the space of additive characters

has two orbits. We need to choose one of the orbits.

Following

4.2.2.
Archimedean packets

Let
v
be an archimedean place of
\mathcal{F}
, and let

be the place of
\mathcal{K}
above

. The CM type
\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
selected in the beginning gives an identification
\mathcal{K}_{w}\simeq\mathbb{C}

Let

\Pi_{\underline{a}}=\bigsqcup_{p=0}^{n}\Pi_{\underline{a}}^{(p)}

where

whose infinitesimal character is

\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{HC}}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. The association

The component group of
\varphi_{\underline{a}}
is

A_{n}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})e_{i}.

Therefore, a character
\chi:A_{n}\to\{\pm 1\}
can be represented by the binary string
\epsilon_{\chi}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that
(\epsilon_{\chi})_{i}=1
if and only if

. From the previous subsection, we have a bijection

Given

, let

denote the binary string representing

. The relation between
\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{HC}}
and

is very concrete. It is useful to split into two cases depending on the parity of

i
odd:

Let

. For the split Hermitian space of dimension
2m+1
, we choose

, which has discriminant 1. In this case,

\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{HC}}=\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{End}}+01\cdots 010

i
even:

Let
i=2m
. The two orbits are represented by
\psi_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\overline{\psi_{\mathbb{C}}}
. We choose the character
\overline{\psi_{\mathbb{C}}}
(this will explained in the next subsection). In this case

\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{HC}}=\epsilon_{\pi}^{\mathrm{End}}+01\cdots 01

4.2.3.
Local conjecture

From the point of view of this paper, the local GGP conjecture identifies a specific element of the product
\Pi_{\phi_{n-1},v}\times\Pi_{\phi_{n},v}
which is important to the arithmetic of
\Pi
. We will recall this construction and make it explicit in the archimedean case.

Let
v
be a place of
\mathcal{F}
which does not split in
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\psi_{v}:\mathcal{K}_{w}/\mathcal{F}_{v}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be an additive character. If
M
is any representation of the Weil–Deligne group
\mathrm{WD}_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}
, then we have a local root number

\varepsilon(M,\psi_{v})\in\{\pm 1\}

defined in
[
GGP12
, Section 5]
. Using it, we form two distinguished characters as follows. If
a\in A_{\phi_{n},v}
, then the subspace
\phi_{n,v}^{a=-1}
where
a
acts by
-1
is stable under
\mathrm{WD}_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}
. Define

\chi_{n}:A_{\phi_{n}}\to\{\pm 1\},\ a\mapsto\varepsilon(\phi_{n-1,v}\otimes\phi_{n,v}^{a=-1},\psi_{v})

and similarly define
\chi_{n-1}:A_{\phi_{n-1}}\to\{\pm 1\}
. We can now state the local GGP conjecture, which was proven by Beuzart-Plessis
[
BP16
,
BP15
]
in general.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathtt{V}_{n-1}\subseteq\mathtt{V}_{n}
be split Hermitian spaces over
\mathcal{K}_{w}/\mathcal{F}_{v}
of dimensions
n-1
and
n
respectively. There is a unique pair
(\pi_{n-1.v},\pi_{n,v})
in
\Pi_{\phi_{n-1},v}\times\Pi_{\phi_{n},v}
satisfying the conditions

(1)

Relevancy:
V^{(\pi_{n-1,v})}\subseteq V^{(\pi_{n,v})}
, and the quotient is isomorphic to
\mathtt{V}_{n}/\mathtt{V}_{n-1}
;

(2)

Distinguished: the Hom-space

\Hom_{\mathrm{U}(V^{(\pi_{n-1,v})})}(\pi_{n}\otimes\pi_{n-1},\mathbb{C})

is non-trivial, which implies it is one dimensional.

Let
\delta
be the discriminant of the odd Hermitian space used to label the Vogan
L
-packet on the odd group. Let
\psi_{v}^{\prime}(x)=\psi_{v}(-2\delta x)
. If we label the Vogan
L
-packet for the even space using
\psi_{v}^{\prime}
, then
\pi_{i,v}=J_{v}(\chi_{i})
for
i=n-1,n
.

Now suppose
v|\infty
. We describe the distinguished representation explicitly in terms of their Harish-Chandra codes. In this case, the local conjecture was first proven by H. He, who gave a different combinatorial recipe for describing the distinguished representation
[
He17
]
. Choose
\psi_{\mathbb{C}}(z)=e^{2\pi(\bar{z}-z)}
, and suppose the parameter
\phi
corresponds to discrete series with infinitesimal character

\phi_{\underline{a}}\leftrightarrow(a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{n}),\ \phi_{\underline{b}}\leftrightarrow(b_{1}>b_{2}>\cdots>b_{n-1})

We consider the pair of parameters
(\phi_{\underline{b}}^{\vee},\phi_{\underline{a}})
. This is the point of view of a restriction problem, which we find to be clearer. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\phi_{\underline{a}}^{e_{i}=-1}=\mathbb{C}((z/\bar{z})^{a_{i}})
, so

\chi_{n}(e_{i})=\varepsilon(\mathbb{C}((z/\bar{z})^{a_{i}}\otimes\phi_{\underline{b}}^{\vee},\psi_{\mathbb{C}})=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(\mathbb{C}((z/\bar{z})^{a_{i}-b_{j}}),\psi_{\mathbb{C}})=(-1)^{\#\{j\,|\,a_{i}>b_{j}\}}

where the final equality follows from
[
Tat79
, (3.2.5)]
. Similarly, we have

\chi_{n-1}(f_{j})=(-1)^{\#\{i\,|\,a_{i}>b_{n-j}\}}

where the appearance of
n-j
instead of
j
is due to the contragredient on
\phi_{\underline{b}}
. The Harish-Chandra codes of the distinguished representations are obtained by adding
0101\cdots
to the codes for
\chi_{n}
and
\chi_{n-1}
.

Since we are always labelling the odd unitary group using the space
V_{m,m-1}
of discriminant 1, our starting pair is one of the two pairs

(3)

\mathrm{U}(m-1,m-1)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(m,m-1)\text{ or }\mathrm{U}(m,m-1)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(m,m),

depending on the parity of
n
. The twist by
-2\delta
in the statement of the theorem explains our previous choice of labelling the Vogan packets on the even unitary group using
\overline{\psi_{\mathbb{C}}}
. As a sanity check, we prove an easy combinatorial lemma which shows that the resulting Harish-Chandra codes always give a relevant pair of Hermitian spaces.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\epsilon_{n}
and
\epsilon_{n-1}
be the binary strings obtained from the above procedure. Let
p_{n}
(resp.
p_{n-1}
) be the number of 1s in
\epsilon_{n}
(resp.
\epsilon_{n-1}
). If
n
is even, then
p_{n}-p_{n-1}=1
, otherwise
p_{n}-p_{n-1}=0
.

Proof.

We first show that if
a_{i}<b_{j}
is a part of the weight interlacing condition, then
(\epsilon_{n})_{i}=(\epsilon_{n-1})_{j}
. We will check that this holds for the classical branching law in the next example, and it’s easy to see that changing the order of two consecutive weights preserve the property, so this is proven. It follows that if we change
a_{i}<b_{j}
to
b_{j}<a_{i}
, then only
(\epsilon_{n})_{i}
and
(\epsilon_{n-1})_{j}
gets flipped, so
p_{n}-p_{n-1}
does not change. Again by the next example,
p_{n}-p_{n-1}
takes the prescribed value in the case of the classical branching law, so we are done.
∎

Finally, we give two examples to illustrate the above combinatorial recipe.

Example 4.3
.

This is compatible with the classical branching law for compact groups. Recall that if
\pi_{n}
is the representation of the compact Lie group
\mathrm{U}(n)
with highest weight
(w_{1}\geq w_{2}\geq\cdots\geq w_{n})
, then
\pi_{n}|_{\mathrm{U}(n-1)}
is a multiplicity-free direct sum of representations with highest weights
(v_{1}\geq v_{2}\geq\cdots\geq v_{n-1})
satisfying the perfect interlacing condition

w_{1}\geq v_{1}\geq w_{2}\geq v_{2}\cdots\geq v_{n-1}\geq w_{n}

The infinitesimal character
\underline{a}
is equal to
\underline{w}+\rho_{n}
, where
\rho_{n}=\big(\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n-3}{2},\cdots,\frac{1-n}{2}\big)
is half of the sum of positive roots. Similarly for
\underline{b}
. Therefore, the interlacing condition in terms of infinitesimal characters is

a_{1}>b_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{n-1}>b_{n-1}>a_{n}

The distinguished characters have binary words

\chi_{n}:\cdots 1010,\quad\chi_{n-1}:\cdots 0101

If
n
is even, then the Harish-Chandra codes for both groups are entirely 1, corresponding to the inner forms
\mathrm{U}(0,n-1)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(0,n)
. If
n
is odd, then they are all 0, corresponding to the inner forms
\mathrm{U}(n-1,0)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(n,0)
. Note that in both cases, the pairs are relevant.

Example 4.4
.

This is also compatible with Blattner’s formula in the case
\mathrm{U}(n,0)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(n,1)

[
HS75
]
. We consider the case
n
is odd for simplicity, otherwise the relevant signature is
\mathrm{U}(0,n)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(1,n)
, but the discussion is identical if we had chosen a different odd Hermitian space as our base space.

We use the notations in
loc. cit.
to state the formula. Suppose the Harish-Chandra parameter is

\lambda=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k-1},a_{k+1},\cdots,a_{n+1},a_{k})

The positive roots associated with this system are

R_{c}^{+}=\{t_{i}-t_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\},\ R_{n}^{+}=\{t_{1}-t_{n+1},\cdots,t_{k-1}-t_{n+1},-t_{k}+t_{n+1},\cdots,-t_{n}+t_{n+1}\}

Let
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n+1})
be a character whose components sum to 0, then
Q(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n+1})
is 1 if and only if
c_{1},\cdots,c_{k-1}\geq 0
and
c_{k},\cdots,c_{n}\leq 0
. Since
(b_{1}>\cdots>b_{n})
is the infinitesimal character on
\mathrm{U}(n)
, we have
\mu+\rho_{c}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n},b_{n+1})
, where
b_{n+1}
is chosen so that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}
and correspond to the character on
\mathrm{U}(1)
. Moreover, we can compute that

\lambda+\rho_{n}=\Big(a_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\cdots,a_{k-1}+\frac{1}{2},a_{k+1}-\frac{1}{2},\cdots,a_{n+1}-\frac{1}{2},a_{k}+\frac{n-2k+2}{2}\Big)

If the weight interlacing condition is

b_{1}>a_{1}>\cdots>b_{k-1}>a_{k-1}>a_{k}>a_{k+1}>b_{k}\cdots>a_{n+1}>b_{n}

then it’s easy to check that the multiplicity given by Blattner’s formula is 1. Moreover, in a weight interlacing condition with
b_{i}>b_{i+1}
and no
a_{i^{\prime}}
in between, the transposition
(i\ i+1)
does not change the value of

, but it changes the sign, so the total sum is 0. Therefore, we have described the exact weight interlacing condition distinguished by
\mathrm{U}(n,0)\subseteq\mathrm{U}(n,1)
.

The distinguished representation predicted by the local GGP conjecture satisfy
\chi_{n}=01\cdots 010
and
\chi_{n+1}
differs from
10\cdots 1010
at the
k
-th bit. It is easy to see that they correspond exactly to the weight interlacing condition computed above.

4.2.4.
Global conjecture

Going back to the RACSDC global representation
\Pi=\Pi_{n-1}\otimes\Pi_{n}
. Fix a global additive character
\psi:\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}}/\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Using it, we can define the global root number

\varepsilon(\Pi)=\prod_{v}\varepsilon(\phi_{n-1,v}\otimes\phi_{n,v},\psi_{v})\in\{\pm 1\}

It is independent of the choice of
\psi
, and it is the sign of the functional equation of
L(s,\Pi)
at the centre
s=\frac{1}{2}
. We have two cases.

Coherent case:

\varepsilon(\Pi)=1
. There is a pair of Hermitian spaces
V_{n-1}\subseteq V_{n}
and a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
on
\mathrm{U}(V_{n-1})\times\mathrm{U}(V_{n})
such that

(1):

The base change of
\pi
to
\mathcal{K}
is
\Pi
.

(2):

For each non-split place
v
, the local component
\pi_{v}
is the distinguished element of the Vogan packet specified by the local conjecture.

(3):

For any
\varphi\in\pi
, there is a formula of the form

\left|\int_{[\mathrm{U}(V_{n-1})]}\varphi(h)\,dh\right|^{2}=(\ast)\cdot L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\Big)\cdot L(1,\Pi,\mathrm{ad})^{-1}

where the term
(\ast)
consists of an elementary term and an explicit product of local terms depending on
\varphi
. It is non-zero for some choice of
\varphi
.

By the discussion in
[
GGP12
, Section 25]
, items (1) and (2) follows from the endoscopic classification, which is known by
[
KMSW14
]
since our
L
-parameters are generic. Item (3) is the Gan–Gross–Prasad formula, fully proven in our case by
[
BPLZZ21
]
(since we are starting with a cusp form
\Pi
, our parameters are stable).

Incoherent case:

\varepsilon(\Pi)=-1
. In contrast to the previous case,

(1):

The collection of local Hermitian spaces distinguished by the local conjecture no longer fits together into a Hermitian space defined over
\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{F}
.

(2):

L\big(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\big)=0
trivially by the functional equation.

By the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture, this vanishing of the central value should be explained by the presence of certain algebraic cycles. In the “minimal weight” case, where the infinitesimal character of
\Pi
at each archimedean place is given by the pair

\Pi_{n-1}:\big(-\frac{n-2}{2},-\frac{n-4}{2},\cdots,\frac{n-2}{2}\big),\quad\Pi_{n}:\big(-\frac{n-1}{2},-\frac{n-3}{2},\cdots,\frac{n-1}{2}\big),

such an algebraic cycle is conjecturally constructed in
[
GGP12
, §27]
and
[
Zha12
]
. In the more general case where the archimedean weights are perfectly interlacing (as in Example
4.3
), one may construct the realizations of such cycles in various cohomology theories using coefficients.

As will be explained later in this article, such a dichotomy is also present in Iwasawa theory, producing what we will call coherent and incoherent Iwasawa main conjectures. The incoherent case appears to be considerably deeper, and it actually implies a version of the coherent main conjecture.

4.3.
Galois representations

4.3.1.
General properties

Let
\Pi
be an RACSDC automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})
. By the work of many people (cf.
[
CH13
,
Car12
]
), there is a geometric Galois representation

\rho_{\Pi}:\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

attached to
\Pi
in the sense that

(4)

L(s,\Pi)=L\Big(\iota^{-1}\rho_{\Pi},s+\frac{n-1}{2}\Big).

Recall that
\iota:\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}_{p}
was a fixed isomorphism. Since
\Pi
is conjugate self-dual of sign
(-1)^{n-1}
, the same is true for
\rho_{\Pi}
up to a twist, namely we have an isomorphism

\rho_{\Pi}^{\mathtt{c}}\simeq\rho_{\Pi}^{\vee}(1-n).

Moreover,
\rho_{\Pi}
is pure of weight
n-1
.

We recall the Hodge–Tate weights of
\rho_{\Pi}
at places above
p
. This requires some bookkeeping. Let
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
be an embedding
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. The associated archimedean places of
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{F}
will also be denoted by
\sigma
. Let
(a_{i}^{\sigma})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{n-1}{2})^{n}
be the infinitesimal character of
\Pi_{\sigma}
. The composition
\sigma_{p}:=\iota\circ\sigma:\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
induces a
p
-adic place
w
of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
v
be the place of
\mathcal{F}
below
w
, then
v=w\bar{w}
since
p
splits completely in
\mathcal{K}
. The Hodge–Tate weights of
\rho_{\Pi}
are

\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}-a_{i}^{\sigma}\Big)_{1\leq i\leq n}\text{ at }w,\quad\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}+a_{i}^{\sigma}\Big)_{1\leq i\leq n}\text{ at }\bar{w}.

Recall that by our convention, the cyclotomic character has Hodge–Tate weight
-1
.

4.3.2.
Ordinary representations

Let
\sigma,\sigma_{p},v,w
be as in the previous subsection. Suppose
\Pi
is unramified at
w
(equivalently
\bar{w}
), then
\Pi_{w}
is an unramified representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{v})
. Write its Satake parameters in the form

p^{-a_{1}^{\sigma}}\iota^{-1}(\alpha_{1}^{\sigma}),\cdots p^{-a_{n}^{\sigma}}\iota^{-1}(\alpha_{n}^{\sigma})\in\mathbb{C}

so we have a list of
p
-adic numbers
\alpha_{1}^{\sigma},\cdots,\alpha_{n}^{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
. The representation
\rho_{\Pi}
is crystalline at
w

[
CH18
]
, and its Frobenius eigenvalues are

\Big(p^{\frac{n-1}{2}-a_{i}^{\sigma}}\alpha_{i}^{\sigma}\Big)_{1\leq i\leq n},

which can be read off from equation (
4
).

We say
\Pi
is
ordinary
(at
p
) if

(ord)

\begin{cases}\text{(i) $\Pi$ is unramified at all places above }p\\
\text{(ii) }|\alpha_{i}^{\sigma}|_{v}=1\text{ for all $i$ and all $\sigma$}\end{cases}

Equivalently, the Hodge polygon and the Newton polygon coincide for
D_{\mathrm{cris}}(\rho_{\pi})
. It follows that the local Galois representation
\rho_{\Pi}|_{\Gal_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}}
is upper triangular for each
p
-adic place
w
(cf.
[
SU14
, Lemma 7.2]
). More precisely, if

, then there exists a
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}
-stable increasing filtration

0=\mathrm{Fil}_{0}\rho_{\Pi}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{Fil}_{n}\rho_{\Pi}=\rho_{\Pi}

such that for
i=1,\cdots,n
, the graded pieces are

(\mathrm{Fil}_{i}/\mathrm{Fil}_{i-1})\rho_{\Pi}\simeq\alpha_{i}^{\sigma}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{a_{i}^{\sigma}-\frac{n-1}{2}},

where we also use
\alpha_{i}
to denote the unramified character such that
\alpha_{i}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w})=\alpha_{i}
.

Choose a basis
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\}
for
\rho_{\pi}
so that
\mathrm{Fil}_{i}\rho_{\pi}=\langle v_{1},\cdots,v_{i}\rangle
. Let
\{v_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,v_{n}^{\vee}\}
be the dual basis on
\rho_{\Pi}^{\vee}
. Using the identification
\rho_{\Pi}^{\vee}\simeq\rho_{\Pi}^{\mathtt{c}}(n-1)
, we view
\rho_{\Pi}^{\vee}
as supported on the same space as
\rho_{\Pi}
. This induces a
decreasing

\Gal_{\mathcal{K}_{\bar{w}}}
-stable filtration

\rho_{\Pi}=\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\rho_{\Pi}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathrm{Fil}^{n}\rho_{\Pi}=0

with
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\rho_{\Pi}=\langle v_{i+1}^{\vee},\cdots,v_{n}^{\vee}\rangle
. The graded pieces hare are

(\mathrm{Fil}^{i-1}/\mathrm{Fil}^{i})\rho_{\Pi}\simeq\alpha_{i}^{-1}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-a_{i}+\frac{n-1}{2}}

as
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}_{\bar{w}}}
-representations.

5.
Framework and further speculations

Let
\Pi=\Pi_{n-1}^{\vee}\otimes\Pi_{n}
be an RACSDC representation of the group
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{K}})
which is ordinary at
p
. The contragredient is inserted, since we are taking the point of view of a restriction problem. The various numerology will also be simpler.

In this section, we propose a framework to understand Iwasawa theory for
\Pi
. Recall that for each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
, we also denote its induced places on
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{K}
by
\sigma
. In addition, let

(a_{1}^{\sigma}>\cdots>a_{n}^{\sigma}),\quad(b_{1}^{\sigma}>\cdots>b_{n-1}^{\sigma})

be the infinitesimal characters of
\Pi_{n,\sigma}
and
\Pi_{n-1,\sigma}
respectively. Their relative position plays a key role in our discussion.

5.1.
Interlacing and Selmer conditions

By applying the constructions in §
4.3
to
\Pi_{n-1}
and
\Pi_{n}
, we can define an
n(n-1)
-dimensional geometric Galois representation

\rho_{\Pi}:=\rho_{\Pi_{n-1}^{\vee}}\otimes\rho_{\Pi_{n}}(n):\Gal_{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n(n-1)}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

which is attached to
\Pi
in the sense that

L\Big(s+\frac{1}{2},\Pi\Big)=L(\rho_{\Pi},s)

It is arithmetically conjugate-symplectic. As a result,
L(\rho_{\Pi},0)
is always a critical value. More precisely, the
L
-factor at the archimedean place
\sigma
is

L_{\sigma}(\rho_{\Pi},s)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\bigg(s+\left|b_{j}^{\sigma}-a_{i}^{\sigma}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\bigg).

It follows that the critical values are those
s
such that
\left|s\right|\leq\left|b_{j}^{\sigma}-a_{i}^{\sigma}\right|+\frac{1}{2}
for all
i,j,\sigma
.

The Bloch–Kato conjecture for
\Pi
states that

\ord_{s=\frac{1}{2}}L(s,\Pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\dim_{\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathcal{K},\rho_{\Pi})

Here, the subscript
f
is the Bloch–Kato local condition. It is the unramified condition away from
p
, but the conditions at the two primes above
p
are more subtle. However, it is easy to describe if
\rho_{\Pi}
satisfies the Panchishkin condition

(P)

At each
p
-adic place
w
of
\mathcal{K}
, the representation
\rho_{\Pi}
contains a
\Gal_{\mathcal{K}_{w}}
-stable subspace
\rho_{\Pi}^{-}
such that the Hodge–Tate weights of
\rho_{\Pi}^{-}
are non-positive and the Hodge–Tate weights of
\rho_{\Pi}/\rho_{\Pi}^{-}
are positive.

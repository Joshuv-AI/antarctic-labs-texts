An epidemiological investigation of the prevalence of digestive diseases in the older prisoner population in Taiwan
Authors: Zhu Liduzi Jiesisibieke, Ping Tao, Yen-Chun Wang, Ching-Wen Chien, Tao-Hsin Tung
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe prison population has been increasing globally, and prisoner health is an important issue of global health. However, it has not received sufficient attention, especially in developing countries and regions. Among all the prisoners, the health status of older prisoners requires special attention due to their declining health and energy. This study aims to assess the prevalence of digestive diseases among older prisoners in Taiwan.MethodsData on older prisoners from the National Health Insurance Research Database from January 1 to December 31, 2013, were used in this study. Digestive diseases were assessed in 2,215 older prisoners using the International Classification of Diseases, 9th revision Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM). We identified the most common digestive diseases and explored any sex-specific differences in their prevalence.ResultsThe estimated prevalence of digestive system diseases among the prisoners was 51.56%. Older women prisoners had a higher prevalence (62.68%) compared to men prisoners in general (50.80%). Diseases with a prevalence > 10% include esophageal, gastric, and duodenal diseases; diseases of the oral cavity, salivary glands, and jaws; and other intestinal and peritoneal diseases. Women prisoners showed a significantly higher prevalence of diseases of the oral cavity, salivary glands, jaws, esophagus, stomach, duodenum, non-infectious enteritis, and colitis.ConclusionOur findings indicate that a considerable number of older prisoners in Taiwan experienced digestive diseases, which may be attributable to general deterioration of physical function and poor resistance. Early detection and treatment are crucial for these conditions, and it is important to maintain human rights and justice in prison systems.Keywords: Older prisoners, Prevalence, Taiwan, Digestive diseases

Introduction

According to the World Prison Population List published in 2021, the global prison population currently stands at approximately 11.5 million, with 51,673 individuals incarcerated in Taiwan [1]. Based on information from 2013, around 2.7% of all prisoners in Taiwan were older people [2, 3]. Prisons serve various purposes, including protecting society from risks, punishment, correction, and reintegration into society [4]. However, they are not often concerned with healthcare. More than three decades ago, the United Nations stated that prisoners also have the right to equal health services [5]. Prisoners experience higher rates of physical and psychiatric disorders than the general population, largely due to limited healthcare access and unsanitary conditions [6]. Older prisoners, in particular, face even greater health challenges due to declining energy and health status with age. However, most current research on prisoner health comes from developed countries [6–8], leaving gaps in understanding the health needs of prisoners—a critical issue as healthcare costs rise with age. Lack of information from developing countries and regions may lead to oversight when making relevant health-related policies. Failure to meet older prisoners’ healthcare needs can lead to unnecessary suffering for this medically vulnerable group and violate the World Health Organization’s guidelines for providing timely and appropriate care during incarceration [9, 10]. Given that most prisoners (> 95%) are eventually released back into society [11], addressing their health needs is essential for public health and achieving health equity. Untreated health conditions may ultimately become community burdens when prisoners reintegrate into society [12].

Digestive disorders are often overlooked despite their significance to the healthcare system, while mental health tends to receive more attention [13, 14]. Globally, the prevalence of inflammatory bowel disease has increased from 3.32 million in 1990 to 4.90 million in 2019. The prevalence was reported to be high in Western Europe and East Asia (0.93 million) [15]. Although the prevalence of digestive diseases is not the highest among older people, the death rate associated with these diseases has increased in this age group [15]. The timely identification and treatment of digestive diseases are crucial for disease management. In 2019, women globally had a higher prevalence of inflammatory bowel disease, although the sex difference was not significant [15]. Therefore, we also considered examining the sex-specific prevalence.

Descriptive studies identifying prevalence and incidence are essential for understanding the disease burden. However, the prevalence of digestive diseases among older prisoners in Taiwan remains unknown. To address this research gap, this descriptive study investigated the prevalence of digestive diseases in this population.

Methods

Data source

The Taiwan National Health Insurance (NHI) and National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) have been extensively described in previous publications [2, 16]. The Ministry of Health and Welfare oversees the healthcare of inmates, ensuringthat they undergo a thorough initial evaluation of their mental and physical health upon admission. This assessment, conducted by healthcare professionals, includes a review of their medical history, current health status, and previous medical conditions. The NHI system provides comprehensive coverage for almost the entire population in Taiwan. The NHIRD collects data on NHI beneficiaries [17], making it a valuable resource for numerous epidemiological studies [18–20]. It uses the International Classification of Diseases, 9th Revision Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM), the accuracy and completeness of which, including ICD-9-CM codes and medication records, have been validated in previous studies [21, 22]. The NHIRD has been collecting and curating health information on prisoners in Taiwan since 2013 [16]. For this study, we accessed this extensive administrative dataset to identify prisoners who were incarcerated in 2013. The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Cheng-Hsin General Hospital (CHGH-IRB: (471)104-07) granted approval for this study, ensuring compliance with the guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Patient identities were protected to guarantee data anonymity.

Study population

This study focused exclusively on participants aged 65 years and over. The primary assessment of digestive diseases was based on the ICD-9 code range 520–579, which was further classified into seven distinct groups for ease of reference. These groups included diseases related to the oral cavity, salivary glands, and jaws (ICD_520–529); diseases of the esophagus, stomach, and duodenum (530–539); appendicitis (540–543); hernias of the abdominal cavity (550–553); non-infectious enteritis and colitis (555–558); other diseases of the intestines and peritoneum (560–569); and other diseases of the digestive system (570–579). To ensure data reliability, prisoners with three or more diagnoses within any of these groups were also considered to potentially suffer from mental health disorders.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.1 software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Categorical variables were described as absolute frequencies and percentages. Prevalence is presented as both absolute frequency and percentage. It was calculated by dividing the number of detained older individuals diagnosed with each type of digestive disease by the total number of older detained individuals in the study sample. Sex-specific prevalence was determined by dividing the number of diagnosed detained men and women with each type of digestive disease by the total number of older detained men and women, respectively. The χ2 test was used to establish whether there was a significant difference in the percentage of categorical variables during our univariate analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Diseases of the oral cavity, salivary glands, and jaws were the second most prevalent, affecting 19.05%. Other diseases of the intestines and peritoneum were also notable, accounting for 15.21% of cases. Table 3 presents the results of the study. Women had a significantly higher prevalence of diseases in the oral cavity, salivary glands, jaws, esophagus, stomach, duodenum and non-infectious enteritis and colitis compared to men.

Discussion

The estimated prevalence of digestive system diseases among older prisoners was 51.56%. Older women prisoners had a higher prevalence than men. We also observed sex-related differences in some subtypes of digestive diseases.

Given the potential impact of the prison living environment on overall health [23, 24], examining the conditions in prisons in Taiwan is crucial. However, research on specific environmental conditions within prisons in Taiwan is limited. For comparison, a study conducted in the UK revealed that prisoners there had low rates of vegetable intake, excessive salt consumption, high rates of smoking, and decreased levels of physical activity [25], influenced by the restrictive prison environment. Limited access to nutritious food options, confined spaces, and stress-inducing conditions may indirectly affect their health-related behaviors [26]. Although living conditions in prisons in Taiwan may differ, studies indicate that overcrowding remains a prominent challenge in prisons in Taiwan, which could similarly impact health and lifestyle factors within this environment [27]. Furthermore, hygiene concerns are evident in many prisons [28]. Indicators of poor hygiene conditions, such as loose teeth and the reliance on unpurified water, suggest inadequate sanitation practices, which can contribute to the prevalence of certain digestive diseases [29]. Additionally, the lack of proper sanitation facilities and limited access to clean water as well as overcrowding, increase prisoners’ susceptibility to communicable diseases [30, 31].

Globally, prisoners are generally acknowledged as a vulnerable population. One meta-analysis demonstrated that obesity rates are rising in low-income countries/regions and that sex-based disparities are evident in the health status of incarcerated individuals. Genetic and lifestyle factors (such as diet) play an important role in the development of digestive diseases [32, 33]. Women, in particular, are more prone to being overweight than their peers of the same age and sex in the general population, partly due to the standardized food supply in prisons, which often mirrors the provisions for men [34]. This suggests that prison facilities may primarily cater to men’s needs without fully considering the healthcare requirements of women [34, 35]. Due to physiological differences, women typically need different nutrient distributions to support hormonal and metabolic health [36]. The lack of nutrient-specific meals for women means they may not receive adequate levels of certain nutrients essential for their health. In our study, we observed a higher prevalence of digestive diseases among women prisoners compared to men, further underscoring the need for sex-specific healthcare services that address the unique needs of older women inmates.

Strengths and limitations

This descriptive study has the following strengths worth mentioning. First, the inclusion of data from the NHIRD in Taiwan provides a comprehensive representation of all older prisoners across Taiwan. Second, our focus on descriptive research addressing the prevalence of digestive diseases among older prisoners in Taiwan fills a crucial knowledge gap in the field. Furthermore, our study highlights the importance of preventing and treating digestive diseases in the prison population. However, this study has some key limitations as well. First, the generalizability of our findings to other regions may be limited because we only studied data from prisoners in Taiwan. Nonetheless, our study can hopefully still provide a call to prioritize the health of older prisoners worldwide. Second, we observed a high prevalence of digestive disorders among older inmates in Taiwan, the factors associated with these conditions were not examined. Future studies should aim to identify the factors associated with these digestive diseases, such as dietary qualities, hygiene standards, and history of diseases, to develop more effective prevention strategies for prisoners. It is also important to note that the NHIRD used in this study does not include information on socioeconomic status and biochemical variables. Investigating these factors in subsequent studies using alternative data sources would be valuable. Third, we used ICD codes to classify the diseases; however, ICD codes may not reflect the actual disease state and can sometimes provide wrong diagnoses. Fourth, it is possible that some prisoners, even if they have digestive diseases, may refuse or be afraid to seek diagnosis, leading to a potential underestimation of the prevalence. Lastly, the anonymization of data in the NHIRD resulted in certain clinical tests being unavailable for this analysis.

Conclusion

The findings of this descriptive study reveal a notable prevalent digestive diseases among older prisoners, with a higher prevalence among older women prisoners compared to their men counterparts. Prioritizing routine health screenings for this population is essential to ensure early detection and timely management, alongside other necessary interventions.

Abstract
This paper explores the maximization of the harvested power efficiency (HPE) in a modular extremely large multiple-input multiple-output (XL-MIMO) system, which supports energy harvesting (EH) for near-field users. These users are located in spatially distinct visibility regions (VRs) with non-stationary channel characteristics. We propose to determine which sub-arrays are switched on or off as well the power control coefficients at the sub-arrays to maximize the HPE. The design can be processed via a multi-tier joint optimization framework based on fractional programming. The numerical results showcase that the HPE performance of the proposed algorithm is nearly optimal, comparable to that of exhaustive search. As a matter of fact, it achieves up to a 120 % 120\% gain over the benchmark scheme which uses the entire XL-MIMO array with equal power allocation (PA) across sub-arrays, while significantly reducing the computational time.



I
Introduction

Extremely large multiple-input multiple-output (XL-MIMO) antenna arrays are one of the key technologies for future 6G mobile networks.
In addition to the hardware structure differences with conventional massive MIMO (mMIMO), XL-MIMO entails several new features and opportunities that are not present in conventional mMIMO. First, the substantial increase in the array aperture has motivated the modeling of the electromagnetic (EM) radiation field by near-field spherical waves, in contrast to the planar-wave-based radiation model of mMIMO
[
1
]
. Spherical wavefronts stimulate the generation of radiation patterns that focus the beam at a specific location, referred to as beamfocusing
[
2
]
.
Focused beams facilitate the interference mitigation in multi-user communications and have been envisioned as a method to enable efficient wireless power transfer (WPT) with minimal energy dissipation
[
2
]
. Secondly, the spatial non-stationarity (SnS) of the channel, where only part of the array is visible to the users (known as the visibility region, VR)
[
3
]
, must be also considered during the system design.

Recent near-field WPT studies promote energy focusing for efficient EH. For example,
[
2
]
used dynamic metasurface antennas to direct power and reduce energy leakage, while
[
4
,
5
]
explored joint beam scheduling, power allocation, and hybrid beamforming for SWIPT. However, these works overlook the SnS effects, like spherical propagation and blockage. By leveraging SnS-induced VRs, where users mainly receive power from specific sub-arrays, this letter investigates strategic sub-array activation to enhance the harvested power efficiency (HPE).

The main contributions of the paper are two-fold: 1) we develop an optimization framework to maximize the HPE of WPT-based XL-MIMO, subject to a joint PA and sub-array activation (SA). By leveraging the multi-layered optimization approach from
[
6
]
, we split the primary complicated objective into two sub-problems. 2) For the PA sub-problem, the Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting method
[
7
]
is applied alongside the Dinkelbach’s transform based fractional programming
[
8
]
. The SA is parameterized using a surrogate auxiliary function of the optimized power coefficients, similar to the model proposed in
[
9
]
. Our simulation results demonstrate that the HPE performance of the proposed algorithm closely matches that of the optimized PA achieved through exhaustive search (ES) across the favorable active sub-arrays, while significantly reducing the computational time.

III
Optimization Problem Formulation

In this section, we propose a joint optimization framework for the considered XL-MIMO system that determines which sub-arrays are switched on/off (
a_{s}=1
or 0) and selects the power coefficients
{\Omega_{s,m}}
to maximize the HPE.
1
1

1

The prospective practical applications of our proposed joint PA-SA optimization framework for XL-MIMO systems include the concentrated energy beamfocusing and VR association for SA by exploiting the SnS effects in near-field communication scenarios.
Hence, the optimization problem can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}:

\displaystyle\max_{\big\{{\Omega_{s,m}},{a_{s}}\big\}}\,\,{\Gamma({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{a_{s}})}

(10a)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\text{s.t.}\hskip 10.00002pt{\sum\nolimits^{S}_{s=1}}{a_{s}}{\sum\nolimits^{M}_{m=1}}{\Omega_{s,m}}\leq P_{t},

(10b)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\sum\nolimits^{M}_{m=1}}{\Omega_{s,m}}\leq P_{s},\,\,\forall\,\,s=1,\ldots,S,

(10c)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\Omega_{s,m}}\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,\,s=1,\ldots,S,m=1,\ldots,M,

(10d)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{a_{s}}\in\{0,1\},\,\,\forall\,\,s=1,\ldots,S.

(10e)

In

III-A

Power Allocation Update (PA procedure)

For a particular SA choice (
{\tilde{a}_{s}}
) as specified in the next section, it can be ascertained that
{\Gamma({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{\tilde{a}_{s}})}
is a quasi-concave ratio of a concave function
{{I}^{E}_{M}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{\tilde{a}_{s}})}

{P_{c}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{\tilde{a}_{s}})}

This mathematical transformation converts the ratio of these two functions into their linear combination
\phi({\Omega_{s,m}},{\tilde{a}_{s}},\lambda)

\lambda

{\Omega_{s,m}}
for a fixed
\lambda

{\tilde{a}_{s}}

\phi({\Omega_{s,m}},{\tilde{a}_{s}},\lambda)={{I}^{E}_{M}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{\tilde{a}_{s}})}-\lambda{P_{c}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{\tilde{a}_{s}})}.

(11)

The conic representation of the transformed function with inversion operation in (
11

\displaystyle~\Omega^{t}_{s,m}=\argmin\nolimits_{\big\{{\Omega_{s,m}}\big\}}\,\,\big(-\phi(\Omega^{t-1}_{s,m},\tilde{{a_{s}}},\lambda_{t})\big).

(12)

To this point, we can observe that the transformed objective is the sum of two monotone functions, which can be maximized using the DR splitting method. From any initial

u

\displaystyle x^{u+1}

\displaystyle=\text{prox}_{\{\lambda_{t}P_{c}({\Omega_{s,m}},\tilde{a}_{s}\}}(z^{u}),

\displaystyle z^{u+1}

\displaystyle\text{prox}_{\Phi}(z)=\argmin_{x}\Big\{\Phi(x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\lVert x-z\rVert^{2}\Big\}.

III-B

Sub-array Activation Update (SA procedure)

In this subsection, we explain the iterative SA procedure utilizing
{\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}}
estimated by the PA procedure discussed in Section

. For a specific iteration
i
of the SA procedure, the PA procedure is completed for a particular SA

(i-1)

\displaystyle~{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}=\dfrac{\,{\sum\nolimits^{M}_{m=1}}{\tilde{\Omega}^{i-1}_{s,m}}}{{\sum\nolimits^{S}_{s=1}}{\sum\nolimits^{M}_{m=1}}{\tilde{\Omega}^{i-1}_{s,m}}}.

(16)

Here,
{g_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}
characterizes the ratio of the transmitted power by each sub-array relative to the total transmission power using the optimized PA from the PA procedure. This pertains to the fact that the sub-array which has higher
{g_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}
value offers stronger contribution to the harvested power
{{I}^{E}_{M}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{a_{s}})}
with higher numerator values in the evaluation of the overall HPE in (
16
) in comparison to the dormant sub-arrays. Utilizing the function
{g_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}
with a mean threshold
{\mathbb{E}}\{{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}\}
, we can iteratively update the binary SA variables as given below:

\displaystyle{a^{i}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1\quad\quad{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}\geq{\mathbb{E}}\{{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}\},\\
0\quad\quad{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}<{\mathbb{E}}\{{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}\}.\end{cases}

(17)

The choice of this baseline
{\mathbb{E}}\{{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}\}(1/S)
ensures that only those sub-arrays, which have higher relative optimized PA coefficients than the mean value of the function
{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}

{P_{c}({\Omega_{s,m}},\!{a_{s}})}
in (

{a^{i}_{s}}
are scaled once again with the same continuous auxiliary function
{g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}
to define a parameterized SA variable
\tilde{a}_{s}^{i}
, which preserves the relative scaling within the activated sub-array subset during the next iteration of the PA procedure:

\tilde{a}_{s}^{i}={g^{i}_{s}({\tilde{\Omega}_{s,m}})}{a^{i}_{s}}.

(18)

The use of this continuous variable
\tilde{a}_{s}^{i}
evaluated in (

\tilde{a}_{s}^{i}
is substituted in (

of PA procedure. Although the users’ association to a particular VR can be performed using the estimation techniques presented in the recent literature

V
Conclusion

In this letter, we have developed an optimization framework to enhance the HPE of an XL-MIMO system in near-field communication, addressing challenges such as spatial non-stationarities. The proposed joint optimization algorithm consolidates the PA and SA using useful techniques, such as the Dinkelbach’s transform based fractional programming and the DR splitting method. The simulation results show that our proposed method achieves near-optimal sub-array selection, while significantly reducing the computational requirements compared to the ES method. Future research could investigate XL-MIMO for SWIPT using appropriate channel estimation along with more energy-efficient hybrid beamforming techniques. Joint optimization of analog phase shifters and digital precoding can reduce the required number of RF chains, thereby mitigating the high power consumption associated with fully digital precoding.

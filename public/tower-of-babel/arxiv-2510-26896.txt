Admissible perturbations of a multivalued Picard operator: Ćirić contraction condition; fixed point and stability results
Cristina Gheorghe 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification]47H10, 54H25

Abstract
This paper studies strict fixed point and stability results for multivalued operators which does not satisfy a Ćirić type contraction condition, but their admissible perturbation does. We focus on the conditions imposed on the admissible perturbation T G T_{G} of a Picard operator T : X → P ⁡ ( X ) T:X\rightarrow P(X) such that the strict fixed point and stability results still hold for T. The results obtained are reformulated in terms of admissible perturbations in the sense of Takahashi and illustrated with some examples.

Introduction

The concept of admissible perturbation of an operator was introduced by I.A. Rus in 2012, in
Rus2012
, where iterative algorithms in terms of admissible perturbations of a single-valued operator were formulated and some data dependence and stability results were given in the context of these algorithms. Recently, in
Rus
, some data dependence and stability results related to retraction-displacement conditions and admissible perturbations of a single-valued weakly Picard mapping were given.

Lately, in
art1
, some strict fixed point results related to multi-valued Picard operators were given, satisfying contraction conditions of Ćirić and Ćirić-Reich-Rus type. The present work extends, for the case of admissible perturbations, the results related to multivalued Picard operators for whom a contraction condition of Ćirić type holds. The aim of this paper is to analyze the conditions that should be imposed on the admissible perturbation, so that the following conclusions hold true: the set of strict fixed points coincides with the set of fixed points and both have a unique element, the sequence of Picard iterations converges to that unique strict fixed point, and the hypotheses under which a retraction-displacement condition occurs for the multivalued operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
. Then, based on the contraction principle formulated in terms of admissible perturbations, the data dependence and stability results are studied. We give similar results for the particular case of admissible perturbations in the sense of Takahashi and illustrate the results with relevant examples.

Terminology and notations

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Throughout this paper,
P(X)
represents the family of all non-empty subsets of X,
P_{cp}
defines the family of all non-empty compact subsets of X, and
P_{cl}
is the family of all non-empty closed subsets of X. Also, for a multivalued operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
, the following notations are used :

•

the fixed point set of T:

\displaystyle Fix(T):=\{x\in X:x\in T(x)\}

•

the strict fixed point set of T:

\displaystyle SFix(T):=\{x\in X:\{x\}=T(x)\}

•

the graphic of T:

\displaystyle Graph(T):=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in T(x)\}

We also recall, in the context of a metric space, the definitions of the following functionals:

•

the gap functional generated by
d
:

\displaystyle D:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},~D(A,B):=\inf\{d(a,b):a\in A,b\in B\}

•

the excess functional of A over B induced by d:

\displaystyle e:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},~e(A,B):=\sup\{D(a,B):a\in A\};

•

the Pompeiu-Hausdorff functional generated by
d
:

\displaystyle H:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},~H(A,B):=\max\{e(A,B),e(B,A)\}.

.

(see
AGPetruselSomeVariants

SFix(T)=Fix(T)=\{x^{*}\}

T^{n}(x)\xrightarrow{H}\{x^{*}\}
as

, for each

(see

\Psi:\mathbb{R_{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}

Theorem 1.7
.

(see

)
Let (X,d) be a complete metric space, and let

be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Assume there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
such that T satisfies the following condition:

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

(1.2)

Then, the following relations hold:

i)

Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
;

ii)

For each

converges to
\{x^{*}\}
(with respect to the Pompeiu-Hausdorff generalized metric H);

iii)

For all

and for some

,

d(x,x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(x,T(x)).

Admissible perturbation of a multivalued Ćirić type operator

We aim to obtain strict fixed point and stability results for admissible perturbations presented in Definition
1.1

1.7

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
a multivalued operator. Let
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
and
T_{G}:X\rightarrow P(X)
be an admissible perturbation of T corresponding to G. Then, for all
x\in X
,

\displaystyle T_{G}^{n}(x)=T_{G}(T_{G}^{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{y\in T_{G}^{n-1}(x)}T_{G}(y)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{y\in T_{G}^{n-1}(x)}G(y,T(y)).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d)
represent a complete metric space, and let
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
be a mapping such that the following conditions hold true:

i)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

ii)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Assume that
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
is a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and consider
T_{G}
is an admissible perturbation of T with respect to
T_{G}
. Provide there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),x^{*}),\text{ for all }x\in X.

(2.1)

Then,

H(T(Y),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(Y),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }Y\in P_{cl}(X).

Proof.

Suppose
Y\in P_{cl}(X)
and arbitrarily choose an element
v\in T(Y)
. Then there exists
y\in Y
such that
v\in T(y).
Hence, using (
2.1
), we get

d(v,x^{*})\leq H(T(y),x^{*})\leq lH(T_{G}(y),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(Y),\{x^{*}\}).

By taking the supremum of v over the set
T(Y)
, we obtain

H(T(Y),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(Y),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }Y\in P_{cl}(X).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
be a mapping satisfying the conditions:

i)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

ii)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Let
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and let
T_{G}
be an admissible perturbation of T with respect to G. Suppose that there exists
k\in(0,1)
such that for all
x\in X
:

H(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq kd(x,x^{*}).

(2.2)

Then

H(T_{G}(Y),\{x^{*}\})\leq kH(Y,\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }Y\in P_{cl}(X).

Proof.

Assume
Y\in P_{cl}(X)
and let a
v\in T_{G}(Y)
be an arbitrary element. Then, there exists
y\in Y
such that
v\in T_{G}(y).

Moreover, for some
k\in(0,1)
, using (
2.2
), we get

d(v,x^{*})\leq H(T_{G}(y),\{x^{*}\})\leq kd(y,\{x^{*}\})\leq H(Y,\{x^{*}\}).

Taking the supremum of v over the set
T(Y)
, we obtain

H(T_{G}(Y),\{x^{*}\})\leq kH(Y,\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }Y\in P(X).

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
be a mapping satisfying the conditions:

a)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

b)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Let
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and
T_{G}
be an admissible perturbation of T with respect to G. Suppose that there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
and

H(T_{G}(x),T_{G}(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T_{G}(y))+\gamma D(y,T_{G}(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

(2.3)

Then the following statements hold true:

i)

Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
;

ii)

Additionally, assume there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }x\in X.

(2.4)

Then the sequence of successive approximations
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\{x^{*}\};

iii)

Suppose there exists
L>0
such that the following inequality holds true for all
x\in X
:

D(x,T_{G}(x))\leq LD(x,T(x)).

(2.5)

Then one has the retraction-displacement condition:

d(x,x^{*})\leq\frac{(1+\gamma)L}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(x,T(x)),\text{ for all }x\in X\text{ and for some }\xi\in(0,1).

Proof.

From Lemma
1.4
and Theorem
1.7
, affirmation i) follows immediately.

ii) Let
x\in X
be an arbitrary element.

From Corollary
1.9
(applied for the multivalued
T_{G}
having the unique strict fixed point
x^{*}
), we obtain that:

H(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq kd(x,x^{*}),

(2.6)

where
k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}\in(0,1).

Let us consider the sequence
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
induced by the multivalued operator T. We aim to prove by mathematical induction the proposition:

P(n):H(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq(lk)^{n}d(x,x^{*}),\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1.

From the hypothesis of ii) and from (
2.6
), P(1) holds true. We suppose P(n) holds true and prove that
P(n)
implies
P(n+1).
Using Lemma
2.2
ii), Lemma
2.3
and condition (
2.6
), we obtain:

\displaystyle H(T^{n+1}(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle=H(T(T^{n}(x)),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\leq lH(T_{G}(T^{n}(x)),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\leq l\cdot kH(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\overset{P(n)}{\leq}(lk)^{n+1}d(x,x^{*}).

Therefore, for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1,

H(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq(lk)^{n}d(x,x^{*}),

which converges to 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty.

iii) From Theorem
1.7
, for all
x\in X
, and some
\xi\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle d(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(x,T_{G}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}LD(x,T(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\gamma)L}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(x,T(x)).

∎

2.1
Data dependence of the strict fixed point

Definition 2.6
.

(see
article
)
Let
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
a multivalued operator and let
F:X\rightarrow P(X)
be another multivalued operator satisfying the following conditions:

i)

SFix(F)\neq\varnothing
;

ii)

there exists
\eta>0
such that
H(T(x),F(x))\leq\eta,
for all
x\in X.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the data dependence property if for each multivalued operator F satisfying conditions i) and ii) and for each strict fixed point
x^{*}
of T, there exists a strict fixed point
u^{*}\in SFix(F)
with the following property:

\text{ there exists some }c>0\text{ such that }d(x^{*},u^{*})\leq c\eta.

.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and let
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
be a mapping satisfying the conditions :

for all

x,y\in X

.

.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space,

Let

}x\in X,\text{ for some }\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}.

;

for all

2.2
Ulam-Hyers stability property of the strict fixed point problem

Definition 2.9
.

(see
Carte55Rus
)
The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is said to be Ulam-Hyers stable if there exists
c\in\mathbb{R^{*}}
such that for any solution
y^{*}
of the given equation

D(y,T(y))\leq\varepsilon,

there exists a strict fixed point of T, namely
x^{*}\in SFix(T)
, satisfying the inequality

d(y^{*},x^{*})\leq\varepsilon.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose that
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
is a mapping satisfying the conditions:

i)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

ii)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
,

Consider
T_{G}
as the admissible perturbation of T with respect to G. We denote, for some
\varepsilon>0
, by
y^{*}
the solution of the inequation

D(y,T(y))\leq\varepsilon.

Assume
T_{G}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
and the following additional condition holds:

\text{iii) there exists }L>0\text{ such that }D(x,T_{G}(x))\leq LD(x,T(x)),\text{ for every }L>0.

Then the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is Ulam-Hyers stable.

Proof.

Let
y^{*}
be a solution of the inequation
D(y,T_{G}(y))\leq\varepsilon.
From Theorem
1.7
, we have that
SFix(T_{G})=\{x^{*}\}.
Then,

\displaystyle d(y^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(y^{*},T_{G}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(y^{*},T_{G}(y^{*}))+H(T_{G}(y^{*}),T_{G}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq LD(y^{*},T(y^{*}))+\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T_{G}(y^{*}))+\gamma D(y^{*},T_{G}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq L\varepsilon+(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta LD(y^{*},T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq L\varepsilon(1+\beta)+(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)d(x^{*},y^{*}).

Therefore,

d(x^{*},y^{*})\leq c\varepsilon,

where

c:=\dfrac{L(1+\beta)}{1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma}\in\mathbb{R^{*}_{+}}.

∎

2.3
Well-posedness of the strict fixed point problem

Definition 2.11
.

(see, e.g.,
article
,
artprec
)
The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is well-posed in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski (see
Reich2005ANO
) if T has a unique strict fixed point
x^{*}
and the following implication holds true:

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X\text{ and }D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\text{ implies }u_{n}\rightarrow x^{*}\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let (X,d) be a metric space and let
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose that
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
is a mapping that satisfies the following conditions:

a)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

b)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Consider
T_{G}
as the admissible perturbation of T with respect to G.
Assume
T_{G}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
, including the additional conditions ii) and iii), namely:

ii)

there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }x\in X;

(2.7)

iii)

there exists
L>0
such that, for all
x\in X
, the following inequality holds:

D(x,T_{G}(x))\leq LD(x,T(x)).

(2.8)

Then the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is well-posed.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.4
, we have
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
. Moreover, from condition iii), for all
x\in X,
there exists
\eta\in(0,1)
satisfying

d(u_{n},x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\eta}D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Alternatively, we infer

\displaystyle d(u_{n},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(u_{n},T_{G}(u_{n}))+H(T_{G}(u_{n}),T_{G}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(u_{n},T_{G}(u_{n}))+\alpha d(u_{n},x^{*})+\beta[d(x^{*},u_{n})+D(u_{n},T_{G}(u_{n}))]+\gamma D(u_{n},x^{*}).

Consequently,

\displaystyle d(u_{n},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma}D(u_{n},T_{G}(u_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq L\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma}D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

∎

2.4
Ostrowski stability property of strict fixed point problem

Definition 2.13
.

(see
art9
,
Rus
)
The fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is said to have the Ostrowski stability property if T has a unique strict fixed point
x^{*}
and the following implication holds:

(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X,D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))\rightarrow 0\Rightarrow v_{n}\rightarrow x^{*}\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Lemma 2.14
.

(Cauchy-Toeplitz lemma; see, e.g.,
CauchyToeplitz
) We consider
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of positive real numbers such that the series
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
is convergent. Let
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be another sequence of nonnegative numbers convergent to zero. Then, we have:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n-k}b_{k}\Bigg)=0.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let (X, d) represent a complete metric space and let
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Assume that
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
is a mapping satisfying the conditions:

a)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

b)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Suppose
T_{G}
is an admissible perturbation of T with respect to G. We assume true the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
related to
T_{G}
, including the supplementary conditions ii) and iii), namely:

ii)

there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }x\in X;

(2.9)

iii)

there exists
L>0
such that, for all
x\in X
, the following inequality holds:

D(x,T_{G}(x))\leq LD(x,T(x)).

(2.10)

Then the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is Ostrowski stable.

Proof.

We deduce, from Theorem
1.7
applied to
T_{G}
, that
SFix(T_{G})=\{x^{*}\}.
Successively, we get

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+H(T_{G}(v_{n}),T_{G}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+\alpha d(v_{n},x^{*})+\beta D(v_{n},T_{G}(x^{*}))+\gamma D(T_{G}(v_{n}),x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+(\alpha+\beta)d(v_{n},x^{*})+\gamma[d(v_{n+1},x^{*})+D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))].

With the notation

k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\alpha},\text{ where }0<k<1;

and using condition iii) from Theorem
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+kd(v_{n},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+k\Big[\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n},T_{G}(v_{n-1}))+kd(v_{n-1},x^{*})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Big[D(v_{n+1},T_{G}(v_{n}))+kD(v_{n},T_{G}(v_{n-1}))+\cdots+k^{n}D(v_{1},T_{G}(v_{0}))\Big]

\displaystyle\leq L\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Big[D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+

\displaystyle+kD(v_{n},T(v_{n-1}))+\cdots+k^{n}D(v_{1},T(v_{0}))\Big].

From Cauchy-Toeplitz Lemma, having
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k^{n}
convergent and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))=0
, one deduces that
d(v_{n+1},x^{*})\rightarrow 0
and therefore, the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=x
is Ostrowski stable.
∎

2.5
Quasi-contraction condition for the multivalued operator T

Definition 2.16
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space. We call quasi-contraction an operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and for whom there exists
l\in(0,1)
satisfying

H(T(x),x^{*})\leq ld(x,x^{*})\text{ for all }x^{*}\in SFix(T)\text{ and for all }x\in X.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
(X,d)
represent a complete metric space, let
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be a multivalued operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and consider
G:X\times X\rightarrow X
be a mapping satisfying the conditions:

a)

G(x,x)=x,
for all
x\in X
;

b)

x,y\in X
and
G(x,y)=x
imply
y=x
.

Suppose
T_{G}
is an admissible perturbation of T with respect to G. Assume
T_{G}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
and the following supplementary condition holds:

\text{there exists $l\in(0,1)$ such that }H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\}),\text{ for all }x\in X.

(2.11)

Then the multivalued operator T is a quasi-contraction.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.7
, we deduce
SFix(T_{G})=\{x^{*}\}
. One has:

\displaystyle D(T_{G}(x),x^{*})\leq H(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T_{G}(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T_{G}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T_{G}(x^{*}))+\gamma H(x^{*},T_{G}(x))

Therefore,

(1-\gamma)H(T_{G}(x),x^{*})\leq(\alpha+\beta)d(x,x^{*}).

From condition (
2.11
), there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq lH(T_{G}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq l\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}d(x,x^{*}).

Since
l\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}\in(0,1)
, it follows that T is a quasi-contraction.
∎

.

T_{W}(x)=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)T(x).

Proof.

For any
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle T_{W}(x)=W(x,T(x),\lambda)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\bigcup_{y\in T(x)}W(x,y,\lambda)=\bigcup_{y\in T(x)}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

\displaystyle=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)\bigcup_{y\in T(x)}y

\displaystyle=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)T(x).

∎

Example 3.12
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space, where
X=\Bigg[\dfrac{1}{4},4\Bigg]
, and let
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be a multivalued operator, defined as

\displaystyle T(x)=\begin{cases}\Bigg[1,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\Bigg],&\text{ for }x\in\Bigg[\dfrac{1}{4},1\Bigg),\\
~\\
[1,\sqrt{x}],&\text{ for }x\in[1,4].\end{cases}

Let
T_{W}:X\times X\times\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{2},1\Bigg)\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be an admissible perturbation of T in the sense of Takahashi,

\displaystyle T_{W}(x)=\begin{cases}\Bigg[\lambda x+(1-\lambda),~~~~~~~~\lambda x+(1-\lambda)\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\Bigg],&\text{ for }x\in\Bigg[\dfrac{1}{4},1\Bigg),\\
~\\
\Bigg[\lambda x+(1-\lambda),~~~~~~~~\lambda x+(1-\lambda)\sqrt{x}\Bigg],&\text{ for }x\in[1,4].\end{cases}

For all
x,y\in X,
there exists
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
, with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<0
, such that
T_{W}
satisfies the contraction condition of Ćirić type, as follows:

H(T_{W}(x),T_{W}(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T_{W}(y))+\gamma D(y,T_{W}(x)).

Then the upcoming statements hold true:

i)

SFix(T)=Fix(T)=\{1\}
;

ii)

There exists
l\in\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{2\lambda},1\Bigg)
such that

H(T(x),\{1\})\leq lH(T_{W}(x),\{1\}),\text{ for all }x\in X.

Therefore,
(T^{n}(x))\overset{H}{\rightarrow}\{1\}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

iii)

There exists
L\geq 1-\lambda
such that

D(x,T_{W}(x))\leq LD(x,T(x)),\text{ for all }x\in X.

Then the retraction-displacement condition for T is

|x-1|\leq\dfrac{(1+\gamma)L}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}|x-1|,\text{ for all }x\in X\text{ which is trivial for some }\xi\in(0,1).

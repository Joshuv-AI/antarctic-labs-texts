Durability of Three Monthly Loading Doses with Faricimab in Treatment-Naïve Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration
Authors: Leyla Turan, Jakob Bjerager, Yousif Subhi, Oliver Niels Klefter, Javad Nouri Hajari, Miklos Schneider
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionUnderstanding the durability of faricimab with three monthly loading injections in a real-world clinical setting is important for optimizing treatment intervals and reducing the treatment burden. The aim of this study was to evaluate the durability and visual outcomes of a loading dose regimen consisting of three consecutive monthly faricimab injections in treatment-naïve patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).MethodsThis is a retrospective, single-center cohort study using chart review and an electronic injection database, including treatment-naïve patients with nAMD aged 50 years or older who received three monthly faricimab injections between 1 November 2023 and 31 August 2024.ResultsOverall, 827 eyes of 742 patients were included in the study. Following the loading dose, the median injection-free interval was 15.0 weeks (95% confidence interval [CI] 13.1 to 16.6). The estimated injection-free survival probabilities at 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, and 24 weeks were 90.9%, 81.1%, 79.1%, 75.5%, 57.1%, 51.1%, 48.6%, and 29.4%, respectively. At 4 weeks post-loading, 42.6% of eyes showed improved best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), 46.3% were stable, and 11.1% declined. Treatment after loading can be paused for an extended period in a substantial subset of patients. Introduction

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is a leading cause of irreversible central vision loss among the elderly population worldwide. The introduction of intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapies since the mid-2000s has substantially transformed the management and visual outcomes in nAMD; however, the need for frequent injections poses a treatment burden on patients, caregivers, and healthcare systems [1–3].

Faricimab (Vabysmo®, Roche, Basel, Switzerland), a novel humanized bispecific monoclonal antibody directed against both vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) and angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2), has been developed with the aim of extending treatment intervals while maintaining disease control. Blockade of VEGF-A is known to suppress neovascularization and vascular leakage, while Ang-2 inhibition has shown to restore vascular stability, reduce inflammation, and limit pericyte dropout and fibrosis [4, 5]. Preclinical and clinical data have suggested that this dual mechanism results in more durable control of exudation, neovascularization, and vascular leakage compared with previous anti-VEGF monotherapy [5, 6]. Randomized, double-masked phase 3 trials such as TENAYA and LUCERNE demonstrated that faricimab 6 mg administered intravitreally, with dosing intervals up to every 16 weeks, was noninferior to aflibercept 2 mg administered every 8 weeks regarding mean change in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) at 1 and 2 years in patients with nAMD. Most patients achieved extended dosing intervals (≥ 12 weeks), indicating a reduced treatment burden with sustained efficacy [6–8]. While faricimab in pivotal clinical trials was recommended to be initiated with four monthly loading doses, this recommendation was changed to three in subsequent editions of the prescribing information [9].

Understanding the durability of this regimen in a real-world clinical setting is important for optimizing treatment intervals and reducing the treatment burden.

This study aimed to assess the durability of a loading regimen with three monthly faricimab injections and to evaluate changes in BCVA after such a loading dose in treatment-naïve patients with nAMD under real-world conditions.

Methods

Study Design, Ethical Approval, and Consent

This retrospective, single-center cohort study was carried out at the Department of Ophthalmology, Rigshospitalet, Glostrup, Denmark, a large tertiary center.

Clinical and treatment-related data were extracted from the department’s internal intravitreal injection database. The data were cross-checked against the patient’s electronic medical records. The study and data handling were conducted in compliance with local laws and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments. Ethical approval for the project was waived by the Regional Health Research Ethics Committees in Denmark (no. F-24044334). The Capital Region’s Research and Health Legal Department approved the data protocol and waived informed consent requirements (approval no. p-2024-16973, 1 November 2024). The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06890026, 16 March 2025) and reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [10].

Setting, Procedures, and Follow-Ups

In Denmark, all anti-VEGF injections and follow‑up assessments are performed exclusively within the public healthcare system at tertiary centers. At our center, patients received intravitreal faricimab injections by trained nurses or ophthalmology residents. The procedures followed a local standard protocol [11]. The loading dose consisted of three consecutive monthly intravitreal faricimab 6-mg injections followed by a follow-up visit 4 weeks after the third injection. The treatment strategy followed an observe-and-plan regimen [12], which allows for pausing the treatment after loading if the retina is dry, in which case patients will be followed every 6 weeks until recurrence of the fluid or until 180 days of follow-up is reached. Patients not requiring treatment for 180 days or longer are discharged from hospital‑based care to community-based practicing ophthalmologists in primary care for routine monitoring.

Patient Selection and Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Patients were eligible if they were 50 years of age or older, were diagnosed with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), were previously treatment naïve, and had received an initial loading dose during the inclusion period of 1 November 2023 to 31 August 2024. Additionally, inclusion required follow-up data available until the next (fourth) subsequent injection or for a minimum of 6 months (180 days) after the third loading injection in case no retreatment was needed after the loading phase.

Patients were excluded if they had a history of previous anti-VEGF treatment, diagnosis of neovascular retinal diseases other than nAMD, or intraocular surgery during the loading phase or before the first post-loading follow-up. Additional exclusion criteria were poor baseline BCVA defined as < 0.1 Snellen (> 1.0 logMAR). Furthermore, eyes with a deviation of ±2 weeks from the planned loading dose interval of 4 weeks were also excluded. This window was chosen to ensure comparable pharmacologic exposure across eyes and to limit heterogeneity introduced by substantial deviations from the prescribed loading regimen.

Data Collection

Data were collected from the center’s electronic medical records, injection database, and patient chart reviews. Patients were identified on the basis of International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) diagnosis codes and relevant procedure codes within the inclusion period. Patient demographics (age and sex), BCVA at baseline and at 4 weeks after completion of the loading dose, and interval to the next intravitreal injection after the loading regimen (weeks) were retrieved.

BCVA was initially documented in Snellen decimal notation and subsequently converted to logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) units for analysis.

Low BCVA values post loading dose were converted according to Bach et al. [13] as counting fingers (CF) = 1.9 logMAR, hand motion (HM) = 2.3 logMAR, and light perception (LP) = 2.7 logMAR.

An improvement in BCVA was defined as a decrease in the logMAR score of more than 0.1, stability corresponded to a change within ± 0.1 logMAR, and a decline was indicated by an increase of more than 0.1 in logMAR.

Outcome Measures

The primary endpoint was durability of the three-dose loading regimen, defined as time from the third loading injection to reinjection within a fixed 180-day horizon, analyzed using time-to-event methods. Secondary endpoints were (1) injection-free survival probabilities at prespecified time points and (2) change in BCVA from baseline to the first post-loading assessment.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed in R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) using RStudio version 2024.12.1. Two-sided p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

For the durability analysis, time to reinjection was defined as the number of days between the third and fourth faricimab injection and expressed in weeks. Follow-up began at the third injection. Reinjection within 180 days was defined as the event. Eyes without reinjection were administratively censored at 180 days.

Injection-free survival was estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods. Median injection-free survival and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were derived from the survival function. Survival probabilities were calculated at prespecified time points up to 180 days. Restricted mean survival time within the 180-day horizon was also estimated.

Associations between baseline best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA; logMAR) and time to reinjection were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression. Hazard ratios (HRs) were reported per 0.1-logMAR increase. Robust standard errors clustered by patient were used to account for within-patient correlation in bilateral cases. Multivariable models adjusted for age and sex were used. Nonlinearity was assessed using restricted cubic splines, and the proportional hazards assumption was evaluated using Schoenfeld residuals.

Changes in BCVA from baseline to the first post-loading assessment were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models with time as a fixed effect and random intercepts for eye nested within patient to account for repeated measurements and bilateral cases. Adjusted models included age and sex as covariates. Results are reported as β coefficients with 95% CIs.

Results

Study Population

During the initial data extraction, 6632 eyes with nAMD were identified in the injection database, of which 1250 were treatment-naïve. Of these, 941 received at least three consecutive faricimab injections during the inclusion period. A total of 114 eyes were excluded owing to poor baseline BCVA, irregular loading intervals, cataract surgery, misdiagnosis, or missing follow-up data. Of the 235 eyes without reinjection within 180 days, 179 (21.6% of the cohort) were discharged from hospital-based care to community ophthalmologists.

Predictors of Time to Reinjection

In univariable Cox regression with robust standard errors clustered by patient, baseline BCVA was not associated with time to reinjection (HR per 0.1-logMAR increase 1.00, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.03; p = 0.94). There was no evidence of nonlinearity (p = 0.23).

In the adjusted model including age and sex, baseline BCVA remained nonsignificant (HR per 0.1 logMAR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.04; p = 0.81). Increasing age showed a trend toward a lower hazard of reinjection (HR per year 0.99, 95% CI 0.98 to 1.00; p = 0.079). Sex was not associated with reinjection risk (HR 1.01 for male versus female patients, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.20; p = 0.95).

Assessment of Schoenfeld residuals indicated deviation from the proportional hazards assumption in the adjusted model (global test p = 0.012), primarily attributable to age (p = 0.008), suggesting possible time-varying effects of age.

Mean logMAR BCVA improved from 0.382 (standard deviation [SD]: 0.265) to 0.299 (SD: 0.293), with corresponding median improvement from 0.301 (interquartile range [IQR]: 0.155–0.523) to 0.222 (IQR: 0.097–0.398).

Linear mixed-effects modeling demonstrated a mean improvement of −0.082 logMAR (95% CI −0.097 to −0.067; p < 0.001), corresponding to approximately four Early Treatment of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters. The magnitude of improvement was unchanged after adjustment for age and sex.

Increasing age was independently associated with worse visual acuity (β = 0.0048 logMAR per year, 95% CI 0.0023 to 0.0073; p < 0.001). Sex was not associated with visual acuity (β = 0.012 logMAR for male versus female patients, 95% CI −0.025 to 0.049; p = 0.52).

Variance component analysis demonstrated clustering at both patient and eye levels (patient-level intraclass correlation coefficient [ICC] 0.13; combined ICC 0.67), supporting the use of mixed-effects modeling.

Discussion

Strategies to reduce treatment burden are increasingly important, given the chronic nature of nAMD, the ageing global population, and the pressure on healthcare systems. This real-world cohort of 827 eyes demonstrates that three monthly faricimab loading injections provide favorable early durability in treatment-naïve nAMD.

Approximately 91% of our patients did not require a reinjection at 4 weeks after completing the loading phase with faricimab. From a clinical perspective, this suggests that a follow-up visit at 4 weeks may be unnecessary as most reinjections are still captured at the 8-week mark. Moving the first post-loading follow-up from 4 to 8 weeks could mean an early and already meaningful reduction of burden on patients, caregivers, and the healthcare system. Modern treatment algorithms, such as the TANGO protocol [14], already benefit from an extended post-loading follow-up scheduling recommendation.

Approximately half of eyes required reinjection by 16 weeks, and 29% remained injection-free at 24 weeks within the prespecified 180-day horizon. More than one-fifth of eyes achieved ≥ 180 days without reinjection and were discharged, indicating that a clinically meaningful subset achieves sustained early disease quiescence after the loading injections alone. These findings support the rationale for extended dosing intervals beyond 16 weeks with the potential to pause the treatment with faricimab post-loading in patients who show good treatment response with dry macula and resume the therapy only when disease activity recurs. With the lack of a comparator group, however, these results should be interpreted cautiously as evidence of favorable durability beyond the specific treatment strategy and healthcare setting used in this study.

Visual acuity improved significantly following loading, with a mean gain of approximately four ETDRS letters. However, since BCVA was recorded in Snellen and subsequently converted to logMAR, this ETDRS equivalent needs to be interpreted with caution. The magnitude of improvement aligns directionally with pivotal trials such as TENAYA and LUCERNE[7], though slightly smaller than gains reported in those controlled settings. Substantial clustering at both patient and eye levels underscores the importance of hierarchical modeling in bilateral injection cohorts. The majority of the patients in this cohort—89%—showed either improvement in BCVA or stability after loading, while the remaining 11% had declining vision.

Baseline BCVA was not associated with retreatment risk, even after adjustment and formal testing for nonlinearity. Increasing age showed a trend toward longer injection-free intervals; however, proportional hazards diagnostics suggest that the age effect may vary over time and should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

A study by Matsumoto et al. compared three-dose and four-dose faricimab loading regimens, including 24 eyes in the three-dose group and 25 eyes in the four-dose group. Improvements in BCVA were observed in both cohorts at month 2. Following the loading phase, treatment intervals were comparable between the two groups. Anatomical and functional gains were maintained with no significant differences in the magnitude of change in BCVA between the three-dose and four-dose groups at 1 year. Overall, the findings indicate that visual outcomes over 1 year were equivalent between the two faricimab loading regimens, suggesting that a three-dose loading phase may provide efficacy comparable to that of four initial doses [15]. Another study by Doi et al. demonstrated similar results, finding that four- and three-dose faricimab loading regimens provided comparable morphological and functional outcomes over 1 year [16].

A network meta-analysis including 49 randomized controlled trials with 23,257 eyes with nAMD found that no anti-VEGF agent or dosing regimen achieved superior 12-month BCVA improvements compared with monthly ranibizumab; however, newer regimens demonstrated comparable visual outcomes with extended dosing intervals, suggesting reduced treatment burden. Notably, faricimab 6 mg (loading dose followed by treat and extend) and aflibercept 8 mg (loading dose followed by 12- or 16-week dosing) maintained BCVA gains similar to monthly ranibizumab while requiring fewer injections [17].

Limitations of this study need to be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design and the single-center setting come with inherent biases and may limit generalizability of the results.

Additionally, our study focused on the evaluation of the treatment durability as well as the visual outcomes following loading, while no anatomical data were collected. Therefore, structural outcomes could not be evaluated, and stratification by lesion type was not performed, limiting comparisons across nAMD subtypes. In addition, the ±0.1 logMAR threshold used to categorize BCVA outcomes was selected as a pragmatic cutoff widely used in the nAMD literature, chosen as a more sensitive alternative to the 15-letter threshold used in some pivotal trials. It nonetheless lies close to the reported test–retest variability of ETDRS BCVA and does not carry fully uniform clinical meaning across the baseline acuity range. Categorical outcomes should therefore be interpreted with caution. Moreover, the Danish system with the observe-and-plan regimen that allows for pausing the treatment for a prolonged period might differ from treatment strategies employed in other countries, also limiting the generalizability of the results.

The strengths of the study include structured and verified data collection and a large sample size, allowing for precise estimates with narrow confidence intervals.

Conclusions

Our study indicates that a loading strategy with three monthly injections of faricimab achieves favorable durability as well as improvement and stability in BCVA in the majority of cases of treatment-naïve nAMD already early on. These findings suggest that a routine follow-up at 4 weeks after loading may be unnecessary in many patients, as most reinjections were still captured at the 8-week visit. Our data show that treatment after loading can be paused for an extended period in a significant subset of patients.

The real-world outcomes presented here extend the evidence from controlled trials, confirming that meaningful BCVA improvement and stabilization can be achieved and sustained while reducing treatment frequency and burden for a substantial proportion of patients. This may improve patient quality of life and reduce healthcare burden.

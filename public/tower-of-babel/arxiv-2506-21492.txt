We show that if a subspace A A of a coarse P ​ D ​ ( n ) PD(n) metric space X X coarsely separates it then it must have asymptotic dimension at least n − 1 n-1 .

Introduction

A subspace
A
of a topological space
X
is said to separate
X
if the complement
X-A
consists of more than one connected component. The classical Jordan curve theorem states that every simple closed curve separates the Euclidean plane and the complement consists of two connected components.
The analogous notion in the coarse category of metric spaces is that of coarse separation. Informally, a subspace
A
of a metric space is said to coarsely separates
X
if there exist two disjoint sets
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
such that neither
B_{1}
nor
B_{2}
are contained in a finite neighborhood of
A
and for every
R>0
there exists a
R^{\prime}>0
such that any two points
x_{1}\in B_{1}
and
x_{2}\in B_{2}
that lie within a distance of
R
from each other are always contained in the
R^{\prime}
neighborhood of
A
.
Recently, the authors of
[
2
]
proved various necessary conditions for separating subsets of a variety of spaces including symmetric spaces of non-compact type, horocyclic product of trees, and Bourdon’s hyperbolic buildings.
In his recent preprint
[
6
]
,
Tselekidis makes the following conjecture:

\mathbb{R}^{n}
cannot be separated by a subspace of asymptotic dimension strictly less than
n-1
.

We give a solution to this conjecture and prove it for the larger collection of Coarse
PD(n)
spaces:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a coarse
PD(n)
space and let
A
be a subspace of
X
such that
A
separates
X
. Then, the asymptotic dimension of

must be at least
n-1
.

Coarse
PD(n)
spaces were introduced first by Kapovich and Kleiner
[
4
]
as metric simplicial complexes whose large scale geometric properties are similar to that of a universal cover of a compact manifold. Banerjee and Okun
[
1
]
recast it in the language of coarse cohomology and and gave a more general definition in the coarse category of metric spaces.

The main tool used in the proof of Theorem

[

]
.

We obtain the following corollary as an immediate consequence

Corollary 1.2

Let

be a

Definition 2.1

(Coarse Separation)

2.4
Direct system of groups and chain complexes

We recall the definition of a direct system of groups and its direct limit.

Definition 2.4

(Direct system of groups)
.

Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of groups and let
\{f^{i,j}\}_{i\leq j}
denote a collection of maps such that

1.

f^{i,j}
is a homomorphism from
A_{i}
to
A_{j}
for all
i\leq j
,

2.

f^{i,i}
is the identity map for all
i
, and

3.

for all
i\leq j\leq k
,
f^{i,k}=f^{j,k}\circ f^{i,j}
.

Then, the pair
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
is said to be a direct system of groups.

Definition 2.5

(Direct limit of groups)
.

Let
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
denote a direct system of groups. Then the direct limit of
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
, denoted by
\ulalim_{i}A_{i}=(A,(\phi_{i})_{i})
, is a group
A
together with a collection of homomorphisms
\phi_{i}:A_{i}\to A
such that:

1.

\phi_{j}=\phi_{i}\circ f^{i,j}
for all
i\leq j
,

2.

For any other group
B
with homomorphisms
\psi_{i}:A_{i}\to B
satisfying
\psi_{j}=\psi_{i}\circ f^{i,j}
for all
i\leq j
, there exists a unique homomorphism
u:A\to B
such that
\psi_{i}=u\circ\phi_{i}
for all
i
.

The homomorphisms
\{\phi_{i}\}
are called the
canonical homomorphisms
associated to the direct limit.

A direct limit always exists whenever the direct system consists of abelian groups and it is unique up to isomorphism. We recall the standard construction of the direct limit of a directed system of abelian groups.

Let
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
denote a direct system of abelian groups. Let
G=\bigoplus A_{i}
. Let
N
be the subgroup of
G
generated by the set
\bigcup_{i}\{x-f^{i,j}(x)|x\in A_{i},j\geq i\}
. Define
A:=G/N
. Let
\phi_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow A
denote the map obtained by composing the canonical inclusion
\mathfrak{q}:A_{i}\rightarrow G
by the quotient map
G\rightarrow G/N
. Then, the pair
(A,\phi_{i})
is a direct limit for the system
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\langle A_{i},f^{i,j}\rangle
be a direct system of abelian groups and let
(A,\phi_{i})
denote its direct limit. Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
be finitely many elements of
\bigoplus_{i}A_{i}
such that, for each
1\leq i\leq k
,
g_{i}
belongs to
A_{m_{i}}
for some
m_{i}
.
Let
g=g_{1}+g_{2}+\dots+g_{k}\in\bigoplus_{i}A_{i}
.

g
maps to the trivial element under the quotient
G\rightarrow A
if and only if there exists
M
such that
M>m_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}f^{m_{i},M}(g_{i})=0
.

Proof.

The ’if’ part of the statement is straightforward. Indeed, if such a
M
exists then
\mathfrak{q}(g)=(g_{1}-f^{m_{1},N}(g_{1}))+\dots+(g_{k}-f^{m_{k},N}(g_{k}))\in N
.

For the converse, we show that the statement holds for all the elements in
N
.
An arbitrary element
g\in N
is a sum of finitely many elements of the form
x-f^{i,j}(x)
,

g=(x_{1}-f^{i_{1},j_{1}}(x_{1}))+\dots+(x_{t}-f^{i_{t},j_{t}}(x_{t}))
. Let
M>i_{p},j_{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq t
.
Then,

\displaystyle\sum f^{i_{1},M}(x_{1})-f^{j_{1},M}(f^{i_{1},j_{1}}(x_{1}))+\dots+f^{i_{t},M}(x_{t})-f^{j_{t},M}(f^{i_{t},j_{t}}(x_{t}))

\displaystyle=\sum f^{i_{1},M}(x_{1})-f^{i_{1},M}(x_{1})+\dots+f^{i_{t},M}(x_{t})-f^{i_{t},M}(x_{t})

\displaystyle=0.

∎

We also recall the definition of a direct system of chain complexes and that of its direct limit.

Definition 2.7

(Direct system of chain complexes)
.

Let
(A^{i}_{\ast})_{i}
be a sequence of chain complexes and let
(f^{i,j})_{i\leq j}
be a collection of maps such that

1.

f^{i,j}
is a chain map from
A_{\ast}^{i}
to

,

2.

f^{i,i}

, and

3.

for all

,
f^{i,k}=f^{j,k}\circ f^{i,j}
.

Then, the sequence
(A^{i}_{\ast})_{i}
together with
\{f^{i,j}\}
is said to form a direct system of chain complexes. It is denote as
\langle A^{i}_{\ast},f^{i,j}\rangle
.

Given a chain map
f:A_{\ast}\rightarrow B_{\ast}
, let
f^{k}
denote its restriction to the
k
-th chain group
f^{k}:A_{k}\rightarrow B_{k}
. Given a direct system of chain complexes
\langle(A^{i}_{\ast}),f^{i,j}\rangle

, taking the

-th chain groups
\{A^{i}_{k}\}_{i}

\langle(A^{i}_{k})_{i},f_{k}^{i,j}\rangle

\partial:A_{k}\rightarrow A_{k-1}

s. A more precise description of

\phi_{i}:A^{i}_{k-1}\rightarrow A_{k-1}

.
In this way, we obtain a chain complex

Let

Theorem 2.8

Let
\langle(A^{i}_{\ast}),f^{i,j}\rangle

2.5
Anti-Čech systems

Let
X
be a metric space. A cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
by uniformly bounded subsets is said to be a
good cover
if it is locally finite i.e. for each
x\in X
the set
\{U\in\mathcal{U}|x\in U\}
is finite.
A cover
\mathcal{V}
is said to be a
refinement
of another cover
\mathcal{V}
if for every element
V\in\mathcal{V}
is contained in some element
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 2.9

(Anti-Čech system)
.

A
Lebesgue number
for a cover
\mathcal{U}
of a metric space is a number
r>0
such that any set of diameter
r
is included in some member of the covering. If every
x\in X
is contained in at most
k
elements of
\mathcal{U}
then we say that the
multiplicity
of
\mathcal{U}
is at most
k
.
We recall the definition of asymptotic dimension.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
X
be a metric space. We say that the asymptotic dimension
asdim(X)\leq n
if the following condition is satisfied:

for every
\lambda>0
, there exists a cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
by uniformly bounded sets with Lebesgue number greater than
\lambda
and multiplicity at most
n+1
.

We say that
asdim(X)=k
if the above condition holds for
n=k
but not for
n=k-1
.

A sequence
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
of good covers of a metric space
X
. We will say that they form an Anti-Čech system if there is a sequence of real numbers
R_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that for all
n
,

1.

Each set
U
in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
has diameter less than or equal to
R_{n}
.

2.

The cover
\mathcal{U}_{n+1}
has a Lebesgue number greater than
R_{n}
.

The two conditions in the above definition imply that for each
i<j
,
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is a refinement of
\mathcal{U}_{j}
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
X
be a metric space such that
asdim(X)=k
. Then
X
admits an anti-Čech system
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
such that each cover
\mathcal{U}_{i}
has multiplicity at most
k+1
.

Proof.

We inductively construct a sequence
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
of covers with the required properties. Let
R_{0}=1
and let
\mathcal{U}_{1}
be a uniformly bounded cover of multiplicity at most
k+1
such that the Lebesgue number of
\mathcal{U}_{1}
is at least
R_{0}
. Let
R_{1}=sup\{diam(U)|U\in\mathcal{U}_{1}\}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{2}
be a uniformly bounded cover of multiplicity at most
k+1
and Lebesgue number at least
R_{1}
. Proceeding inductively one can obtain a sequence of covers
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
such that each
\mathcal{U}_{i}
has multiplicity at most
k+1
and there is a sequence
R_{n}
of real numbers tending to infinity such that, for each
i
, every set in
\mathcal{U}_{i}
has diameter less than or equal to
R_{i}
and the cover
\mathcal{U}_{i+1}
has a Lebesgue number greater than or equal to
R_{i}
.
∎

(Nerve of a cover)
.

Let
X

\mathcal{U}
be a cover of

Let

X
be a metric space and let
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
be a anti-Čech system for
X
. Then there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{HX}_{k}(X)\cong\ulalim_{i}H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}))

Proof.

In view of Proposition
2.13
, it suffices to exhibit an isomorphism between
\mathrm{HX}_{k}(X)
and
\varinjlim H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}))
for any one anti-Čech system
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
.
The proof consists of two steps. In the first step, we express the chain complex
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}(X)
as the direct limit of a direct system
\langle\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X),c_{i.j}\rangle
of subcomplexes. In the second step, we show that the direct system
\langle\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X),c_{i.j}\rangle
is isomorphic to the direct system
\langle C^{lf}_{\ast}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i})),p^{j}_{i}\rangle
for a particular Anti-Čech system
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
.

Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}
be a monotonically increasing sequence of positive reals such that
\lambda_{i}\to\infty
. Let
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}(X)
be as defined in Section 2.2, and let
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)
denote the subcomplex of
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}(X)
consisting of chains
\alpha=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in X^{(n+1)}}a_{\sigma}\sigma
such that:

1.

For any bounded subset
B
of
X
, there are only finitely many simplices
\sigma
such that
a_{\sigma}\neq 0
and
\sigma
has all of its vertices in
B
, and

2.

For any
\sigma=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
, there exists
y\in X
such that
y\in B_{\lambda_{i}}(x_{i})
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

We denote the boundary map of
\mathrm{CX}^{\lambda_{i}}_{\ast}(X)
by
\partial^{\lambda_{i}}
. It should be clear that
\partial^{\lambda_{i}}
is simply the restriction of the boundary map
\partial
of
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}(X)
to the subcomplex
\mathrm{CX}^{\lambda_{i}}_{\ast}(X)
.
Note that there is a natural inclusion map
c^{k}_{i,j}:\mathrm{CX}_{k}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{CX}_{k}^{\lambda_{j}}(X)
for all
i<j
and for all
k
. It is not hard to see that
c^{k}_{i,j}
commutes with the boundary operator and extends to a chain map
c_{i,j}:\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{j}}(X)
. This gives rise to
a direct system of chain complexes
\langle\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X),c_{i,j}\rangle
. Additionally, for each
k
, the collection
\langle\mathrm{CX}^{\lambda_{i}}_{k}(X),c^{k}_{i,j}\rangle
is a direct system of abelian groups and the direct limit of this system is naturally isomorphic to
\mathrm{CX}_{k}(X)
. The direct limit of the system
\langle\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X),c_{i,j}\rangle
is the chain complex obtained by taking the abelian groups
\mathrm{CX}_{k}(X)
together with boundary map obtained by taking the direct limit of the boundary maps
\partial^{\lambda_{i}}
,
\delta:=\varinjlim_{i}(\partial^{\lambda_{i}})
. It can be easily verified that the map
\delta
coincides with the original boundary map
\partial
.

The chain map
c_{i,j}
induces a map
H_{k}(c_{i,j})
on the corresponding homology groups
H_{k}(c_{i,j}):H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))\rightarrow H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{j}}(X))
. For each
k
, the collection of groups

\langle H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)),H_{k}(c_{i,j})\rangle
forms a direct system. By Theorem
2.8
, the direct limit
\varinjlim_{i}H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))
is isomorphic to the
k
-th homology group of the complex
\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}(X)
, i.e.
\mathrm{HX}_{k}(X)\cong\varinjlim_{i}H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
denote the cover of
X
consisting of balls of radius
\lambda_{i}
. Then
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
forms an anti-Čech system. Let
C^{lf}_{\ast}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}))
denote the chain complex of locally finite chains on
N(\mathcal{U}_{i})
. For all
n
, there is a natural bijection between
n
-simplices in
N(\mathcal{U}_{i})
and simplices
\sigma\in X^{(n+1)}
, which sends
(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
to the simplex
(B_{\lambda_{i}}(x_{0}),B_{\lambda_{i}}(x_{1}),\dots,B_{\lambda_{i}}(x_{n}))
. This bijection induces an isomorphism

\theta^{i}_{n}:\mathrm{CX}_{n}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)\rightarrow C^{lf}_{n}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}))

for all
n
. The isomorphisms
p_{n}
extend to a chain map

\theta^{i}:\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X)\rightarrow C^{lf}_{\ast}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i})).

p
is easily seen to be a chain homotopy equivalence by considering the map induced by the inverse of the bijection between
n
-simplices in
N(\mathcal{U}_{i})
and simplices
\sigma\in X^{(n+1)}
.
As a result, the corresponding homology groups are isomorphic:

H_{k}(\theta^{i}):H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i})).

It remains to show that the isomorphisms
H_{k}(p)
extend to an isomorphism between direct systems
\langle H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X),H_{k}(c_{i,j})\rangle
and
\langle H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}),H_{k}(p^{j}_{i})\rangle
.
It can be easily verified that the following diagram commutes for all
i\leq j
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{j}}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{j}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(\theta^{i})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(c_{i,j})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(p^{j}_{i})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}(\theta^{j})}

It follows that the corresponding direct limits as isomorphic,

\mathrm{HX}_{k}(X)\cong\varinjlim_{i}H_{k}(\mathrm{CX}_{\ast}^{\lambda_{i}}(X))\cong\ulalim_{i}H^{lf}_{k}(N(\mathcal{U}_{i})).

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a metric space such that
X
has finite asymptotic dimension. Then,

asdim(X)\geq\textrm{sup}\{k|\mathrm{HX}_{k}(X)\neq 0\}.

Proof.

Let
asdim(A)=n
. Then, by Corollary
2.11
, there exists a anti-Čech system
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
for
X
such that each cover
\mathcal{U}_{i}
has multiplicity
n+1
. Thus, the corresponding nerves
N(\mathcal{U}_{i})
all have dimension at most
n
. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathrm{HX}_{i}(X)
vanishes for all
i>n
.
∎

Finally, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Proof.

Let
X
be a coarse
PD(n)
space. Let
A
be a subspace of
X
such that
A
separates
X
. By Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
,
\mathrm{HX}_{n-1}(A)\cong\mathrm{HX}^{1}(X-A)\neq 0
. An application of Corollary
3.2
tells us that the asymptotic dimension of
A
is at least
n-1
.
∎

The second moment of Ramanujan sums
Hong Ziwei, Zheng Zhiyong

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze C ⁡ ( x , y ) C(x,y) , the second moment of Ramanujan sums. Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, we derive an asymptotic formula for C ⁡ ( x , y ) C(x,y) with improved error term precision. The key feature of our approach is that it allows x x and y y to be arbitrarily close.

11L40

Key words:
Ramanujan sum, Riemann zeta function, Mellin transform

1.
Introduction

The Ramanujan sum is a classical topic in number theory, defined as

(1.1)

\displaystyle c_{q}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\ mod\ q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(\frac{na}{q}),

where
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
.

In
[
1
]
, T.H. Chan and A.V. Kumchev examined the second moment of the average of Ramanujan sums given by

(1.2)

C_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{n\leq y}\left(\sum_{q\leq x}c_{q}(n)\right)^{2}.

We introduce a more concise approach to extend Chan and Kumchev’s result under the assumption of Riemann Hypothesis(RH).
Our primary interest is the asymptotic behavior of
C_{2}(x,y)
when
x^{1+\varepsilon}<y<x^{2+\varepsilon}
. Chan and Kumchev established the following theorem

Theorem 1.1

(T.H. Chan and A.V. Kumchev)
.

Let
x
be a large real number,
y\geq x
, and
B>10
be fixed.

(1)

If
y\geq x^{2}(\log x)^{B}
, then

C_{2}(x,y)=\frac{yx^{2}}{2\zeta(2)}+O(yx^{2}(\log x)^{10}(x^{-1/2}+x^{2}y^{-1})).

(2)

If
x(\log x)^{2B}\leq y\leq x^{2}(\log x)^{B}
, then

C_{2}(x,y)=\frac{yx^{2}}{2\zeta(2)}(1+2\kappa(u))+O(yx^{2}(\log x)^{10}(x^{-1/2}+(y/x)^{-1/2})),

where
u=\log(y/x^{2})
, and
\kappa(u)
is defined in
[
1
]
.

It is important to note that Chan and Kumchev’s result imposes a restriction on the sizes of
x
and
y
, specifically
x(\log x)^{2B}\leq y\leq x^{2}(\log x)^{B}
. Motivated by this, we establish a new asymptotic which allows the sizes of
x
and
y
arbitrarily approximated.

Initially, we express
C_{2}(x,y)
as a smooth summation. Specifically, we introduce a smooth, rapidly decaying function
W
, supported within the interval
(0,1)
. This function satisfies
W\left(t\right)=1
for
t\in(0,1)
, and its higher derivatives
W\left(t\right)^{(j)}
are bounded by
U^{j}
outside this interval. Our focus lies on the refined smooth sum

(1.3)

C(x,y)=C_{2}(x,y;W)=\sum_{n}\left(\sum_{q}c_{q}(n)W\left(\frac{q}{x}\right)\right)^{2}W\left(\frac{n}{y}\right).

Notably, we substitute
W
for the cutoff function used in
[
1
]
. For our analysis, we assume the truth of the Riemann Hypothesis, which guarantees all non-trivial zeros of the zeta function
\zeta(s)
lie on the critical line
Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
.

Our main result is presented in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume the RH. Let
\varepsilon>0
,
c_{2}\in(1,2)
, large real numbers
y>x
, and
W
be fixed.

(1)

If
x<y\leq x^{2}
, then

(1.4)

\displaystyle C(x,y)=\frac{yx^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\widehat{W}(1)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{2}+O(y^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{7}{2}-\varepsilon})+O(y^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}x^{1+2\varepsilon});

(2)

If
y\geq x^{2}
, then

(1.5)

\displaystyle C(x,y)=\frac{yx^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\widehat{W}(1)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{2}+O(yx^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon})+O(y^{\varepsilon}x^{4-2\varepsilon}).

The Mellin transform of
W
is defined by
\widehat{W}(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W(t)t^{s-1}dt
.
\widehat{W}
is analytic in the right half-plane
\sigma>0
, which allows coefficient
\widehat{W}(1)
to be a computable constant. The integral in the equations has a magnitude of
\frac{1}{2}
.

There are two main differences between Chan’s result and ours: the distance between
x
and
y
, and the size of error terms.

First, we remove the precondition
x(\log x)^{2B}\leq y\leq x^{2}(\log x)^{B}
, allowing the sizes of
x
and
y
to be arbitrarily close.

Second, our method provides sharper bounds. In particular, the case
y=x^{2}
. IN this case, we obtain the same main term as in Chan’s result, along with a secondary main term that doesn’t appear in Chan’s result. As a result, the error term in our work is of size
x^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}
; while Chan’s result has an error term of size
x^{4}
. Details can be found in
Case 1
in Section
3.3
.

Additionally, our method is more concise.

Chan and Kumchev utilized Perron’s formula to express
C_{2}(x,y)
as an integral along the line
Re(s)=1+1/\log x
in their paper. Their analysis focused on the integral over the contour
\Gamma(\alpha,\beta,T)
as defined in
[
1
]
(equation (3.8)). The unsmooth cutoff function used by Chan and Kumchev imposes a restriction
yx^{-1}\geq(\log x)^{2B}
, which we resolve by replacing it with a smooth cutoff function
W
.

Furthermore, Chan’s method relies on the computation of integrals over a zero-free region of
\zeta(s)
. Under RH, all zeros of
\zeta(s)
are on the line
Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
which allows larger zero-free region and sharper bounds of
C(c,y)
.

Recently, there has been growing interest in investigating the mean values of Ramanujan sums across number fields, leading to significant findings (see
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
2
]
). Our method can be extended to improve results in the number field case.
For illustration, we build upon the work of Chan and Kumchev (as referenced in
[
1
]
), focusing on the integer ring—a common illustrative case.

2.
preliminary

2.1.
Mellin inversion

Let W be a smooth, rapidly decaying function supported within the interval
(0,1)
, Using Mellin inversion, we express
W\left(t\right)
as

(2.1)

W\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\widehat{W}(s)t^{-s}ds,

where
\widehat{W}(s)
is the Mellin transform of
W
, defined by

(2.2)

\widehat{W}(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}W(t)t^{s-1}dt.

Here,
c
is a constant with
c>1
and
\widehat{W}(s)
is analytic for
\sigma>0
. We also have the following estimate for (
2.2
)

(2.3)

\widehat{W}(s)\ll\frac{1}{|s|(1+|s|)^{E}},

where E is any positive integer. For further details on
W
and
\widehat{W}
see
[
3
]
. For our purposes, the explicit form of
W
is of minor concern. Our primary concern is the rapid decay of
\widehat{W}
.

2.2.
Ramanujan’s identity

By applying Mellin inversion (
2.1
) and Ramanujan’s identity (3.1) from
[
1
]
, we can express
C(x,y)
as

\displaystyle C(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\sum_{q}\frac{c_{q}(n)}{q^{s}}x^{s}\widehat{W}(s)ds\right)^{2}W\left(\frac{n}{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\frac{\sigma_{1-s}(n)}{\zeta(s)}x^{s}\widehat{W}(s)ds\right)^{2}W\left(\frac{n}{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\sigma_{1-s_{1}}(n)\sigma_{1-s_{2}}(n)}{\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})}x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}W\left(\frac{n}{y}\right)

where
2>c_{2}>c_{1}>1
are some real numbers close to
1
. Further, combining with equation (3.6) from
[
1
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle C(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\sum\limits_{n}\sigma_{1-s_{1}}(n)\sigma_{1-s_{2}}(n)W\left(\frac{n}{y}\right)}{\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})}x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}H(s_{1},s_{2};y)\frac{x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}{\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2},

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle H(s_{1},s_{2};y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{3})}\frac{\zeta(s_{3})\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)}{\zeta(2s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)}\widehat{W}(s_{3})y^{s_{3}}ds_{3}

with
c_{3}>\max\{1,2-Re(s_{1}),2-Re(s_{2}),3-Re(s_{1}+s_{2})\}
. In our context, we may choose
c_{3}=3/2
. More information about the convergence region for
\zeta(s)
can be found in
[
4
]
.

3.
The second moment

3.1.
Expression for
C(x,y)

The integrand in (
2.5
) has poles at
s_{3}=1
,
s_{3}=2-s_{1}
,
s_{3}=2-s_{1}
and
s_{3}=3-s_{1}-s_{2}
with corresponding residues

\frac{\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)}{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})}y\widehat{W}(1),

\frac{\zeta(2-s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2}+s_{1})\zeta(s_{1})}{\zeta(2+s_{1}-s_{2})}y^{2-s_{2}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2}),

\frac{\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(1-s_{1}+s_{2})\zeta(s_{2})}{\zeta(2+s_{2}-s_{1})}y^{2-s_{1}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{1})

and

\frac{\zeta(3-s_{1}-s_{2})\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(2-s_{2})}{\zeta(4-(s_{1}+s_{2}))}y^{3-s_{1}-s_{2}}\widehat{W}(3-s_{1}-s_{2}).

Assuming the RH and shifting
(c_{3})
to
(c_{3}^{\prime})
, we can express
H(s_{1},s_{2};y)
as

\displaystyle H(s_{1},s_{2};y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)}{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})}y\widehat{W}(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{\zeta(2-s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2}+s_{1})\zeta(s_{1})}{\zeta(2+s_{1}-s_{2})}y^{2-s_{2}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(1-s_{1}+s_{2})\zeta(s_{2})}{\zeta(2+s_{2}-s_{1})}y^{2-s_{1}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{1})

\displaystyle+\frac{\zeta(3-s_{1}-s_{2})\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(2-s_{2})}{\zeta(4-(s_{1}+s_{2}))}y^{3-s_{1}-s_{2}}\widehat{W}(3-s_{1}-s_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{3}^{\prime})}\frac{\zeta(s_{3})\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)}{\zeta(2s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)}\widehat{W}(s_{3})y^{s_{3}}ds_{3},

where
c_{3}^{\prime}<1
is a parameter to be chosen later.
Then, we can express
C(x,y)
as

(3.1)

\displaystyle C(x,y)=C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}+C_{4}+R,

where

\displaystyle C_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)}{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})}y\widehat{W}(1)x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2},

\displaystyle C_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\zeta(2-s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2}+s_{1})}{\zeta(2+s_{1}-s_{2})\zeta(s_{2})}y^{2-s_{2}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2},

\displaystyle C_{3}=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(1-s_{1}+s_{2})}{\zeta(2+s_{2}-s_{1})\zeta(s_{1})}y^{2-s_{1}}\widehat{W}(2-s_{1})x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2},

\displaystyle C_{4}=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\frac{\zeta(3-s_{1}-s_{2})\zeta(2-s_{1})\zeta(2-s_{2})}{\zeta(4-(s_{1}+s_{2}))\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})}y^{3-s_{2}-s_{1}}x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\widehat{W}(3-s_{1}-s_{2})\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}

and

\displaystyle R=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{3}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\int\limits_{(c_{3}^{\prime})}\frac{\zeta(s_{3})\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)\zeta(s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)}{\zeta(2s_{3}+s_{1}+s_{2}-2)\zeta(s_{1})\zeta(s_{2})}y^{s_{3}}x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\widehat{W}(s_{3})\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}ds_{3}.

3.2.
The first main term in
C(x,y)

Let’s proceed with analyzing the term
C_{1}
. It’s easy to see that
\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)
has a simple pole at
s_{2}=2-s_{1}
with residue 1. Assuming the RH and shifting
(c_{2})
to
(c_{2}^{\prime})
with
c_{1}+c_{2}^{\prime}-1<1
, we compute the residue at
s_{2}=2-s_{1}
which yields the main term. Then we deduce the following expression

\displaystyle C_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{yx^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\widehat{W}(1)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\widehat{W}(2-s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{1})ds_{1}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}y\widehat{W}(1)\int\limits_{(c_{1})}\int\limits_{(c_{2}^{\prime})}\frac{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)}{\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})}x^{s_{1}+s_{2}}\widehat{W}(s_{1})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}.

The second term in the expression for
C_{1}
involves an integral over and its size is determined by the
x^{c_{1}+c_{2}^{\prime}}
. It’s easy to find proper
c_{1}
and
c_{2}^{\prime}
such that
c_{1}+c_{2}^{\prime}
is as small as possible, with restrictions
c_{1}+c_{2}^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}
and
c_{1},c_{2}^{\prime}>0
. We may take
c_{1}=c_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, then the second term in
C_{1}
becomes

\displaystyle\ll yx^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{|\zeta(-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+(t_{1}+t_{2})i)|}{|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+(t_{1}+t_{2})i)|}\widehat{W}(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+t_{1}i)\widehat{W}(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+t_{2}i)dt_{1}dt_{2}.

For
|\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}|
, we use the bound
(14.2.6)
in
[
7
]
. Using functional equation and Stirling’s formula, we obtain a standard bound for
\zeta(s)

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\zeta(s)|\ll\left(\frac{|t|}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\left(1+\frac{1}{|t|}\right)|\zeta(1-\sigma)|

for
-1\leq\sigma\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then the integration is convergent by using the rapid decay of
\widehat{W}
. Hence, we know that the integration in above equation is a computable constants, independent of
x
and
y
.
Consequently,
C_{1}
becomes

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{yx^{2}}{\zeta(2)}\widehat{W}(1)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{2}+O(yx^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}).

It’s worth noting that (
2.2
) implies
\widehat{W}(1)=1+O(\frac{1}{U})
and
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c_{2})}\widehat{W}(2-s_{2})\widehat{W}(s_{2})ds_{2}=\frac{1}{2}+O(\frac{1}{U})
.

3.4.
Error terms

We process with the analysis of the terms

,
C_{4}
, and
